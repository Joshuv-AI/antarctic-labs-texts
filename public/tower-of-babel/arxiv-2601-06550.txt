LLMTrack: Semantic Multi-Object Tracking with Multimodal Large Models
Pan Liao, Feng Yang, Di Wu, Jinwen Yu, Yuhua Zhu, Wenhui Zhao

Abstract
This version only covers certain sections, and the complete version will also include a dataset. At present, since the authors have incorporated this part into their graduation thesis (which is subject to anonymous review), we have pre-uploaded it to arXiv to avoid potential disputes that might arise later. Traditional Multi-Object Tracking (MOT) systems have achieved remarkable precision in localization and association, effectively answering where and who . However, they often function as autistic observers, capable of tracing geometric paths but blind to the semantic what and why behind object behaviors. To bridge the gap between geometric perception and cognitive reasoning, we propose LLMTrack , a novel end-to-end framework for Semantic Multi-Object Tracking (SMOT). We adopt a bionic design philosophy that decouples strong localization from deep understanding, utilizing Grounding DINO as the eyes and the LLaVA-OneVision multimodal large model as the brain. We introduce a Spatio-Temporal Fusion Module that aggregates instance-level interaction features and video-level contexts, enabling the Large Language Model (LLM) to comprehend complex trajectories. Furthermore, we design a progressive three-stage training strategy, Visual Alignment, Temporal Fine-tuning, and Semantic Injection via LoRA to efficiently adapt the massive model to the tracking domain. Extensive experiments on the BenSMOT benchmark demonstrate that LLMTrack achieves state-of-the-art performance, significantly outperforming existing methods in instance description, interaction recognition, and video summarization while maintaining robust tracking stability.

Introduction

In the preceding evolution of computer vision, the Multi-Object Tracking (MOT) task has primarily focused on the questions of "Where" (localization) and "Who" (association). Through sophisticated designs like MOTR
[
13
]
and MOTIP
[
3
]
, modern trackers have successfully bridged the gap from pixel-level features to geometric trajectories, achieving high precision in single-view tracking. However, the ultimate goal of intelligent perception extends beyond mere geometric digitization toward semantic cognition. Traditional MOT systems, regardless of their high metrics, essentially function as "autistic observers." They can meticulously trace the movement path of a target but remain completely ignorant of "what the target is doing," "what their intentions are," or "how they interact with the environment." This absence of the semantic dimension constitutes a fundamental barrier preventing current perception systems from advancing to higher-order cognitive intelligence.

With the generational evolution of Artificial Intelligence, particularly the explosion of Generative AI represented by Large Language Models (LLMs), the field is undergoing a paradigm shift from "perception" to "reasoning." If CNNs and Transformers endowed machines with the ability to "see," then LLMs have equipped them with a "brain" to understand. Against this backdrop, the task of Semantic Multi-Object Tracking (SMOT)
[
5
]
has emerged. SMOT requires systems to not only output spatiotemporal trajectories but also generate natural language descriptions concerning object behaviors, interaction relationships, and scene dynamics. This demand for interpretability and logical reasoning is critical for downstream applications such as autonomous driving, embodied AI, and intelligent surveillance.

However, integrating Large Models into tracking systems is not a trivial process of module stacking. It faces three significant challenges. First, there is the alignment problem: how to effectively align highly structured, abstract trajectory data with unstructured visual semantics so that the LLM can "read" the trajectory remains an unsolved puzzle. Second, the efficiency paradox: the massive parameter size of LLMs imposes huge computational costs, conflicting with the inherent requirement for timeliness in tracking tasks. Third, the temporal sensitivity gap: existing Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) are mostly trained on static images or short clips and lack inherent sensitivity to long-term trajectory logic. Directly applying them to complex SMOT tasks often results in hallucinations or spatiotemporal mismatches.

To address these challenges, we propose
LLMTrack
, a novel framework designed to dismantle the barriers between traditional trackers and multimodal large models, constructing an end-to-end intelligent perception loop with deep cognitive capabilities. The core philosophy of LLMTrack lies in the decoupling and synergy of strong localization and deep understanding. Specifically, we employ a bionic architecture:

1.

The Eyes (Perception):
We introduce Grounding DINO
[
8
]
, renowned for its open-world detection capabilities, as the frontend perceiver. It provides precise object candidates and features, ensuring that no potential target is overlooked.

2.

The Brain (Reasoning):
For the core inference engine, we adopt the LLaVA-OneVision
[
4
]
architecture. Leveraging its unique "AnyRes" strategy and video instruction tuning, it enables unified modeling of dynamic video streams, allowing the model to capture fine-grained visual details without losing global context.

To balance performance and efficiency, LLMTrack incorporates a scalable design. We utilize a lightweight version based on Qwen2-VL-0.5B
[
10
]
for mechanism verification and a heavyweight version based on Qwen3-VL-4B
[
12
]
for deep interaction analysis. By combining this "Brain + Eyes" architecture with a novel Spatio-Temporal Fusion Module and a progressive training strategy, LLMTrack achieves qualitative breakthroughs in three core dimensions: instance description, interaction recognition, and video summarization. Our approach not only answers where the targets are but also articulates what is happening using natural language, proving the immense potential of LLM-empowered tracking systems.

2
Related Work

2.1
From Geometric Perception to Semantic Cognition

The history of MOT research is essentially a pursuit of spatiotemporal continuity. From early Kalman filters to Deep Learning-based ReID and Transformer-based association, the focus has been on locking physical positions. However, the physical world is semantic. Humans do not just see pixel displacements; they construct causal chains (e.g., "The taxi stopped
because
the person waved"). SMOT
[
5
]
formalizes this by requiring an enhanced trajectory output
T_{sem}=\{(b_{i},S_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
, covering instance description, interaction recognition, and scene summarization (Fig.
). While the pioneering SMOTer framework
[
5
]
introduced semantic descriptions, it relied on BERT architectures, which lack the generative reasoning and world knowledge of modern LLMs.

2.2
Vision-Language Models and LLaVA-OneVision

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) bridge visual features with text space. The LLaVA series
[
7
,
6
]
has become a dominant paradigm by treating visual encoders as sensory inputs for LLMs. For LLMTrack, we select the LLaVA-OneVision architecture
[
4
]
due to its unified treatment of visual signals. Unlike traditional video LMMs that aggressively downsample video frames (losing critical motion details), LLaVA-OneVision employs the AnyRes mechanism to process video as a set of high-fidelity images, preserving fine-grained cues. Coupled with the Qwen language base
[
10
,
12
]
, it provides the long-context window and logical reasoning necessary to infer complex interactions like "chasing" vs. "following."

3
Method

3.1
Architecture Overview

LLMTrack treats the SMOT task as a sequence generation problem. As shown in Fig.
1
, given a video sequence
\mathcal{V}
, the model utilizes a shared backbone and a Grounding DINO-based detector to generate trajectory embeddings. These embeddings are processed by our core Fusion Module to aggregate inter-instance and temporal information. Finally, the fused features are mapped to the token space of the LLaVA-OneVision Large Language Model to generate structured text output
S
, covering instance descriptions, interactions, and summaries.

:
Overall architecture of LLMTrack. It consists of a visual frontend (Grounding DINO), a Spatio-Temporal Fusion Module, and a reasoning backend (LLaVA-OneVision).

3.2
Spatio-Temporal Fusion Module

Raw trajectory coordinates are too sparse for deep semantic reasoning. We introduce a dual-stream fusion module (Fig.
2
) to bridge the gap between geometry and semantics.

Figure 2
:
Detailed structure of the Spatio-Temporal Fusion Module, featuring instance-level interaction modeling and video-level context recursion.

Instance Fusion: Adaptive Temporal Attention.

Interaction recognition (e.g., "following") is dynamic. Given the variable length of trajectories due to occlusion, we employ an adaptive temporal attention mechanism. For a target
i
with a feature sequence
\mathcal{Q}^{i}
, the attention weight
\alpha^{i}_{k}
for the
k
-th frame is calculated as:

\alpha^{i}_{k}=\frac{\exp\left(W_{2}\cdot\tanh(W_{1}f^{i}_{k}+b_{1})\right)}{\sum_{m=1}^{N_{i}}\exp\left(W_{2}\cdot\tanh(W_{1}f^{i}_{m}+b_{1})\right)}

(1)

This allows the model to prioritize keyframes with clear motion patterns. The aggregated feature
\tilde{f}^{i}=\sum\alpha^{i}_{k}f^{i}_{k}
provides a robust representation. To model interaction between target
i
and
j
, we concatenate their features and pass them through a fusion MLP:

h_{rel}^{ij}=W_{f2}\cdot\sigma\left(W_{f1}\cdot[\tilde{f}^{i};\tilde{f}^{j}]+b_{f1}\right)+b_{f2}

(2)

This generates a relation query
h_{rel}^{ij}
that implicitly encodes the dynamic interplay between targets.

Video Fusion: Recursive Context Aggregation.

To generate video summaries, the model requires global memory. We model video processing recursively. The fused feature
F_{i}
at time
i
is updated using the previous state
F_{i-1}
as the Query, and the current frame feature
f_{i}
as Key and Value in a Multi-Head Attention (MHA) block:

F_{i}=\text{Norm}(F_{i-1}+\text{MHA}(Q_{v}=W_{vQ}F_{i-1},K_{v}=W_{vK}f_{i},V_{v}=W_{vV}f_{i}))

(3)

This effectively creates an implicit RNN structure, accumulating context over time to support long-term reasoning.

3.3
Progressive Three-Stage Training

Directly training the entire heterogeneous architecture is unstable. We propose a progressive strategy (Fig.
3
):

1.

Stage 1: Visual-Semantic Alignment.
We train the backbone and detection network using image-text pairs. The LLM is frozen. This establishes the "vocabulary" of visual concepts.

2.

Stage 2: Temporal Association.
We freeze the backbone and train the detection network on video data. The focus is on the Transformer’s query-passing mechanism to ensure identity consistency across frames.

3.

Stage 3: Semantic Injection via LoRA.
We freeze the entire visual frontend and only fine-tune the LLM using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA). The objective is Causal Language Modeling (CLM):

\mathcal{L}_{stage3}=-\sum_{t=1}^{L}\log P(w_{t}|w_{<t},T_{emb},I_{context})

(4)

This stage teaches the LLM to translate aligned visual features into rich natural language descriptions.

Figure 3
:
The progressive three-stage training pipeline: (1) Visual Alignment, (2) Temporal Fine-tuning, and (3) Semantic Injection.

Experiments

4.1
Setup and Metrics

We evaluate LLMTrack on the
BenSMOT
benchmark
[
5
]
, which contains 3,292 videos with dense semantic annotations.

•

Tracking Metrics:
HOTA, MOTA, and IDF1.

•

Semantic Metrics:
BLEU-4, ROUGE-L, METEOR, and CIDEr for text generation quality.

•

Interaction Metrics:
Precision, Recall, and F1 Score for multi-label interaction classification.

Implementation relies on a Swin-Tiny backbone and Qwen-VL based LLaVA-OneVision. We utilize LoRA (rank=64) for efficient fine-tuning. Training is split across NVIDIA RTX 4090 (Stages 1 & 2) and RTX 5880 Ada (Stage 3).

5
Conclusion

This paper presented LLMTrack, a novel framework that integrates Grounding DINO and LLaVA-OneVision to solve the Semantic Multi-Object Tracking task. By decoupling localization and reasoning, and bridging them with a specialized Spatio-Temporal Fusion Module, we successfully enabled the system to "read" trajectories and generate coherent semantic descriptions. Our progressive three-stage training strategy ensures efficient alignment and adaptation. Experimental results on the BenSMOT benchmark validate that LLMTrack not only accurately locates objects but also profoundly understands their behaviors, paving the way for next-generation cognitive perception systems.

Introduction

The goal of this paper is to compute the essential
2
-dimension of the linear groups when the defining prime is odd. Fix a field
k
. The essential dimension of a finite group
G
, denoted
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(G)
, is the smallest number of algebraically independent parameters needed to define a Galois
G
-algebra over any field extension
F/k
(or equivalently
G\text{-torsors over }\text{Spec}F
). In other words, the essential dimension of a finite group
G
is the supremum taken over all field extensions
F/k
of the smallest number of algebraically independent parameters needed to define a Galois
G
-algebra over
F
. The essential
2
-dimension of a finite group, denoted
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(G,2)
, is similar except that before taking the supremum, we allow finite extensions of
F
of odd degree and take the minimum of the number of parameters needed. In other words, the essential
2
-dimension of a finite group is the supremum taken over all fields
F/k
of the smallest number of algebraically independent parameters needed to define a Galois
G
-algebra over a field extension of
F
of odd degree. See Section
2
for more formal definitions. See also
[
3
]
and
[
7
]
for more detailed discussions. For a discussion of some interesting applications of essential dimension and essential
p
-dimension, see
[
14
]
.

What is the essential dimension of the finite simple groups? This question is quite difficult to answer. A few results for small groups (not necessarily simple) have been proven. For example, it is known that
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(S_{5})=2
,
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(S_{6})=3
for

of characteristic not
2

[

in characteristic

[
5

0
containing all roots of unity,
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(G)=1
if and only if
G
is isomorphic to a cyclic group

Essential Dimension and Representation Theory Background

For completeness, we recall the relevant background. Fix a field
k
. Let
G
be a finite group,
p
a prime.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
T:\text{Fields}/k\to\text{Sets}
be a functor. Let
F/k
be a field extension, and
t\in T(F)
. The
essential dimension of

\mathit{t}
is

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(t)=\min_{F^{\prime}\subset F\text{ s.t. }t\in Im(T(F^{\prime})\to T(F))}\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(F^{\prime}).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
T:\text{Fields}/k\to\text{Sets}
be a functor. The
essential dimension of

\mathit{T}
is

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(T)=\sup_{t\in T(F),F/k\in\text{Fields}/k}\operatorname{ed}_{k}(t).

Definition 2.3
.

For
G
be a finite group, let

H^{1}(-;G):\text{Fields}/k\to\text{Sets}

be defined by

H^{1}(-;G)(F/k)=\{\text{the isomorphism classes of }G\text{-torsors over }\text{Spec}F\}.

Definition 2.4
.

The
essential dimension of

\mathit{G}
is

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(G)=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(H^{1}(-;G)).

Definition 2.5
.

Let
T:\text{Fields}/k\to\text{Sets}
be a functor. Let
F/k
be a field extension, and
t\in T(F)
. The
essential

\mathit{p}
-dimension of

\mathit{t}
is

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(t,p)=\min\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(F^{\prime\prime})

where the minimum is taken over all

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}\subset F^{\prime}\text{ a finite extension},\text{ with }F\subset F^{\prime}

\displaystyle[F^{\prime}:F]\text{ finite }\text{ s.t. }p\nmid[F^{\prime}:F]\text{ and }

\displaystyle\text{the image of }t\text{ in }T(F^{\prime})\text{ is in }\text{Im}(T(F^{\prime\prime})\to T(F^{\prime}))

Note
.

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(t,p)=\min_{F\subset F^{\prime},p\nmid[F^{\prime}:F]}\operatorname{ed}_{k}(t|_{F^{\prime}}).

Definition 2.6
.

Let
T:\text{Fields}/k\to\text{Sets}
be a functor. The

\mathit{p}
-dimension of

is

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(T,p)=\sup_{t\in T(F),F/k\in\text{Fields}/k}\operatorname{ed}_{k}(t,p).

Definition 2.7
.

The

\mathit{p}
-dimension of

\mathbf{x}=(x,\dots,x)
,
\psi_{\mathbf{a}}(\mathbf{x})=x^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}}
. So
\Psi_{\mathbf{a}}|_{T}\in\widehat{T}/\Gamma
will be trivial on
\{(x,\dots,x):x^{n^{\prime}}=1\}
if and only if
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}=0\mod 2^{v}
, where
v=\min(v_{2}(n^{\prime}),s)
. So
\lambda_{1}\cong\Psi_{\mathbf{a}}|_{T}
for some
\mathbf{a}\in H/\Gamma
with
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}=\mod 2^{v}
.

Also, since
\lambda_{1}\cong\Psi_{\mathbf{a}}|_{T}
is non-trivial on
T_{j}\cap C[2]=\langle\mathbf{b}^{j}\rangle
, where
(\mathbf{b}^{j})_{i}=\begin{cases}\zeta_{2},&i\in I_{j}\\
1,&i\notin I_{j}\end{cases},

we must have

1\neq\prod_{i\in I_{j}}\zeta_{2}^{a_{i}}=\zeta_{2}^{\sum_{i\in I_{j}}a_{i}}=\zeta_{2}^{A_{j}}.

Thus
2\nmid A_{j}
and so
A_{j}
is invertible. So by Corollary
10.9
, since we are assuming that
\Gamma
is trivial, the orbit of
\lambda_{i}=\Psi_{\mathbf{a}}|_{T}
under the action of
P_{2}(S_{n})
has size at least
2^{k_{j}+v_{l}(n)}
. So
c\geq 2^{k_{j}+v_{l}(n)}
. Thus for
\varphi=\varphi_{j}
,

\dim(\varphi)\geq 2^{k_{j}+v_{l}(n)}.

Hence

\displaystyle\dim(\rho)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\xi_{2}(n)-1}\dim(\varphi_{j})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\xi_{2}(n)-1}2^{k_{j}+v_{l}(n)}

\displaystyle=2^{v_{l}(n)}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\xi_{2}(n)-1}2^{k_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=2^{v_{l}(n)}(n-2^{v_{l}(n)})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(PGL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),2)

Also, since
PSL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\subset PGL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
,

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(PSL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),2)\leq\operatorname{ed}_{k}(PGL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),2).

Therefore for
\Gamma=\text{Gal}(k(\zeta_{2^{s}})/k)
trivial,
n\neq 2^{t}
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{ed}_{k}(PSL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),2)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(PGL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q}),2)=2^{v_{2}(n)}(n-2^{v_{2}(n)}).\qed

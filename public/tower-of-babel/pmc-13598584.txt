A semi-analytical physics-informed neural network framework with hessian analysis for singularly perturbed fractal convection-dominated anomalous diffusion problems
Authors: Navnit Jha, Mon Bahadur Rai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe present study introduces the concept of physics-informed neural networks to develop a numerical approach for solving singularly perturbed convection-dominated anomalous diffusion problems. A key focus of this research is to analyse the boundary layers that arise in convection-dominated anomalous diffusion problems using an artificial neural network, namely a physics-informed neural network. We explore solutions to convection-dominated anomalous diffusion problems using neural-network corrector functions derived from underlying auxiliary conditions. The thin layers represent regions with rapid variations in the solution, exhibiting intricate behaviour and rendering existing numerical methods less effective. The proposed method is a semi-analytic method that captures the minute behaviour of the solution in the boundary layer and resolves the singularity that naturally arises in fractal boundary-value problems. An in-depth discussion will be provided, supported by numerical examples demonstrating promising, uncompromised results related to linear and nonlinear stationary anomalous diffusion equations.•PINN framework is proposed for singularly perturbed anomalous diffusion problems.•Corrector functions effectively capture thin boundary layers and singular behaviour.Hessian-based analysis is employed to investigate convergence and solution sensitivity.•The proposed method demonstrates strong performance on both linear and nonlinear problems.Keywords: Artificial neural network, Physics-informed neural network, Boundary layers, Fractal derivative, Singularly perturbed problems

Background

Singularly perturbed problems (SPPs) arise when a significantly small parameter is multiplied to the highest derivative term in a boundary value problems. In the practical sense, the perturbation parameter allegedly causes layers, not necessarily in the boundaries, in the interior of domain. The layer is called the boundary layer if it is formed on the boundaries; otherwise, it is interior layer or the shocked [1]. It occurs when the solution curve changes rapidly, forming the layer, which gives great hindrance in the uniform approximations of the solution in the interior of the domain. The SPPs commonly occur in aerodynamics, fluid mechanics, viscous flows, electromagnetism, modeling of semiconductor devices, rotating flows, geophysical flows, acoustics, mathematical biology, and control problems [[2], [3], [4], [5]] where the edge effects need to be taken care of. It is always a challenging task, particularly in boundary layers, to claim the proper solutions for second-order ordinary or partial differential equations with singular perturbation. Many physical phenomenon occurs in the boundary layers, it is essential to study the behavior of the solution. In the past, approximate solutions of various types of SPPs using the finite element, finite difference and the fitted operator method, have been obtained [6,7]. Similarly anomalous diffusion-advection model by FDM is presented in [[8], [9], [10]]. The approximation of the solution in the single boundary layer that develops the corrector function is analysed in [11]. Alongside these evolutions, SPPs are also examined by Artificial Neural Nework (ANN) approach [[12], [13], [14]]. Deep neural network based prediction for viscosity of an oil based hybrid nanofluid and the modeling of time series disease prediction confronted by spatiotemporal dependencies is presented in [15,16]. The concept of capturing boundary layer by corrector function using Physic-informed neural networks (PINNs) is reported in [16].

Many physical phenomena often exhibit irregular structures. Problems related to these irregularities are discussed in [17]. In the Lebesgue sense, the classical derivative of the Lebesgue Cantor staircase function is nearly zero along the domain. Such domains having fractal structures are, therefore, taken care of with the aid of fractals and fractional derivatives. When we consider the geometric structures with fine details at every scale, we encounter fractal profiles. We use this concept to model creep behavior in any materials. Analysis of the fractal concept is seemingly useful for the treatment of diffusion, heat conduction, and waves [18]. In this work, we generalize the classical derivative, and we will study the SPPs of fractal order.

Furthermore, an ANN-based scheme has also been proposed for solving fractal-fractional differential equations, where the differentiation is considered in the generalized fractional order derivative. This method offers a more comprehensive framework for managing fractional-order derivatives, which is essential for precisely simulating intricate engineering and physical processes [27,28] provides a thorough discussion of the application and efficacy of ANN-based methods in the context of fractal-fractional differential equations. These studies highlight the advantages of artificial intelligence in solving differential equations that are otherwise challenging to address using traditional numerical approaches.

Neural network architecture

In the analysis of SPPs, the solution domain is often divided into two distinct components: the outer layer and the boundary layer. The outer layer represents the region away from the boundaries where the solution remains relatively smooth and can be approximated using standard perturbation techniques. In this region, the effects of the small parameter are minimal, and the solution behaves according to the leading-order differential equation. Conversely, the boundary layer is a thin region adjacent to the boundary where rapid changes in the solution occur due to the influence of viscous effects or other boundary conditions. Here, traditional methods may fail to capture the behavior accurately, necessitating a more refined approach such as matched asymptotic expansions to reconcile the inner and outer solutions. Together, these two components provide a comprehensive understanding of the solution's behavior across the entire domain, highlighting how local phenomena near boundaries can significantly impact the overall dynamics of the system. Method validation

The numerical solutions to some linear and nonlinear convection-dominated anomalous diffusion reaction equations by optimizing the loss term using a two-layer fully connected neural network with 5000 neurons per layer is obtained. The computations are performed for various values of fractal parameter α. Each problem is tested with different numbers of hidden layer nodes and learning rates for gradient descent. Across all simulations, the gradient descent process runs for 2000 iterations with a fixed learning rate of 10−4. For the numerical simulation, the weight values wr=1 and wr=0.01 have been used. The implementations are carried out in Python 3.0 utilizing the DeepXDE and TensorFlow libraries. To enhance the network's performance, a scalable hyperparameter a is incorporated into the activation function, improving learning efficiency and significantly accelerating convergence, particularly during initial training phases. Adjusting this parameter modifies the slope of the activation function. In our experiments, we employ the Swish activation function, a scalable sigmoidal function defined as σ(x)=x/(1+eax). Conclusion

In this study, we have successfully demonstrated the application of physics-informed neural networks to solve singularly perturbed convection dominated anomalous diffusion problems. By wielding in hand the concepts of artificial neural networks, we effectively analyzed the boundary layers, which are critical regions exhibiting rapid variations in the solution by developing corrector functions. The numerical experiments presented in this work highlight the effectiveness of our approach in capturing the minute variations of SPPs along the boundaries. Our findings suggest that integrating neural network-based methods with established analytical techniques can significantly enhance the accuracy of solutions in complex differential equations. This work paves the way for future exploration into applying other approaches of deep learning and extending this methodology to higher-dimensional problems through optimized neural network architectures.

Limitations

The PINNS applies to a wide class of differential models; however, it has been observed that singularity resolution requires significant computational time. A properly designed neural network may help reduce computation time near the singularity of the differential mode.

Fast Model Selection and Stable Optimization for Softmax-Gated Multinomial-Logistic Mixture of Experts Models
TrungKhang Tran, TrungTin Nguyen, Md Abul Bashar, Nhat Ho, Richi Nayak, Christopher Drovandi, TrungKhang Tran, TrungTin Nguyen, Md Abul Bashar, Nhat Ho, Richi Nayak, Christopher Drovandi

Abstract
Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures combine specialized predictors through a learned gate and are effective across regression and classification, but for classification with softmax multinomial-logistic gating, rigorous guarantees for stable maximum-likelihood training and principled model selection remain limited. We address both issues in the full-data (batch) regime. First, we derive a batch minorization-maximization (MM) algorithm for softmax-gated multinomial-logistic MoE using an explicit quadratic minorizer, yielding coordinate-wise closed-form updates that guarantee monotone ascent of the objective and global convergence to a stationary point (in the standard MM sense), avoiding approximate M-steps common in EM-type implementations. Second, we prove finite-sample rates for conditional density estimation and parameter recovery, and we adapt dendrograms of mixing measures to the classification setting to obtain a sweep-free selector of the number of experts that achieves near-parametric optimal rates after merging redundant fitted atoms. Experiments on biological protein–protein interaction prediction validate the full pipeline, delivering improved accuracy and better-calibrated probabilities than strong statistical and machine-learning baselines.

Preliminaries

SGMLMoE Models.
We refer to the output
{\textnormal{y}}\in{\mathbb{Y}}
as the response variable and the input
\mathbf{{\mathbf{x}}}\in{\mathbb{X}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{P}
,
P\in{\mathbb{N}}
, as the predictor variable. We consider a batch dataset of size
N
, denoted by
\{({\mathbf{x}}_{n},{\textnormal{y}}_{n}):n\in[N]\}
, consisting of i.i.d. copies of
({\mathbf{x}},{\textnormal{y}})
, where the conditional law of
y
given
{\mathbf{x}}={\bm{x}}
is characterized by an unknown conditional probability mass function (or density)
\pi(\cdot\mid{\mathbf{x}}={\bm{x}})
. The corresponding observed values are written as
({\bm{x}},{y})
. Motivated by universal approximation results for MoE models, we approximate
\pi(\cdot\mid{\mathbf{x}}={\bm{x}})
by a SGMLMoE of the form

\displaystyle s_{{\bm{\theta}}}({y}\mid{\bm{x}})=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathsf{g}_{k}\!\big({\bm{w}}({\bm{x}})\big)\,\mathsf{e}_{k}\!\big({y};{\bm{v}}({\bm{x}})\big),

(1)

where
{\bm{\theta}}
collects all unknown parameters and
K
is the number of experts. The gating probabilities are defined by the softmax map
\mathsf{g}_{k}\!({\bm{w}}({\bm{x}}))=\exp\!\big(w_{k}({\bm{x}})\big)/(\sum_{l=1}^{K}\exp\!\big(w_{l}({\bm{x}})\big)),

\forall k\in[K],

with gating score functions
{\bm{w}}({\bm{x}})=(w_{1}({\bm{x}}),\ldots,w_{K}({\bm{x}}))
. In the discrete-output setting, we take
{\mathbb{Y}}=[M]
,
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and specify each expert as a multinomial-logistic model

\mathsf{e}_{k}\!\big({y}=m;{\bm{v}}({\bm{x}};{\bm{\upsilon}})\big)=\exp\!\big({v}_{m,k}({\bm{x}})\big)/\sum_{l=1}^{M}\exp\!\big({v}_{l,k}({\bm{x}})\big),

\forall k\in[K],\ \forall m\in[M],

where the expert scores
{\bm{v}}({\bm{x}})=({v}_{m,k}({\bm{x}}))_{m\in[M],k\in[K]}
are modeled as polynomial functions of
{\bm{x}}
. Concretely, for degrees
D_{W},D_{V}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

w_{k}({\bm{x}})=\sum_{d=0}^{D_{W}}{\bm{\omega}}_{k,d}^{\top}{\bm{x}}^{d}=\sum_{d=0}^{D_{W}}\Big(\sum_{p=1}^{P}\omega_{k,d,p}{x}_{p}^{d}\Big)
,

{v}_{m,k}({\bm{x}})=\sum_{d=0}^{D_{V}}{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m,k,d}^{\top}{\bm{x}}^{d}=\sum_{d=0}^{D_{V}}\Big(\sum_{p=1}^{P}\upsilon_{m,k,d,p}{x}_{p}^{d}\Big)

with coefficient collections
{\bm{\omega}}=(\omega_{k,d,p})_{k\in[K],\,d\in\{0,\ldots,D_{W}\},\,p\in[P]}
and

{\bm{\upsilon}}=(\upsilon_{m,k,d,p})_{m\in[M],\,k\in[K],\,d\in\{0,\ldots,D_{V}\},\,p\in[P]}
. The full parameter vector is denoted by

{\bm{\theta}}=\big({\bm{\omega}},{\bm{\upsilon}}\big)
. For simplify, we take
D_{W}=D_{V}=D
and leave the general case
D_{W}\neq D_{V}
for future work.

Maximum Log-likelihood Estimator.
We denote by
{\bm{\theta}}^{0}
a maximizer of the population objective

{\bm{\theta}}\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{{\textnormal{y}}\mid{\mathbf{x}}\sim\mathbb{P}}\left[\log s_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\textnormal{y}}\mid{\mathbf{x}})\right]
.
Given the batch dataset
\{({\bm{x}}_{n},{y}_{n})\}_{n=1}^{N}
, we estimate
{\bm{\theta}}
by maximum likelihood,

\widehat{{\bm{\theta}}}_{N}\in\arg\max_{{\bm{\theta}}\in{\mathbb{T}}}\;\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}}),

where the observed-data log-likelihood is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}})=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\log\!\left[\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathsf{g}_{k}\!\big({\bm{w}}({\bm{x}}_{n})\big)\,\mathsf{e}_{k}\!\big({y}_{n};{\bm{v}}({\bm{x}}_{n})\big)\right].

(2)

In this paper we work in the full-data (batch) regime, focusing on stable maximum-likelihood fitting for
Equation

1
–
Equation

2
without invoking stochastic-approximation devices used in incremental or mini-batch variants.

Identifiability of SGMLMoE Models.

Because the softmax gate is invariant to additive shifts, the gating parameters are not fully identifiable. In particular, if we translate each coefficient vector as
{\bm{\omega}}_{k,d}\mapsto{\bm{\omega}}_{k,d}+{\bm{t}}
for some
{\bm{t}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{P}
, then the resulting gating probabilities
\mathsf{g}_{k}({\bm{w}}({\bm{x}}))
remain unchanged, so
\{{\bm{\omega}}_{k,d}\}
are identifiable only up to a common translation. This phenomenon, and standard remedies for it, are discussed in the context of establishing convergence rates for maximum-likelihood estimation of softmax-gated Gaussian MoE models in
(
71
)
. Following
(
35
;
43
)
, we enforce identifiability by fixing a reference expert and imposing, without loss of generality, the constraint
\{{\bm{\omega}}_{K,d,p}\}_{d\in\{0,\ldots,D\},\,p\in[P]}=\mathbf{0}
, which yields the equivalent parameterization
\mathsf{g}_{K}({\bm{x}};{\bm{\omega}})=1-\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\mathsf{g}_{k}({\bm{x}};{\bm{\omega}})
and
\mathsf{g}_{k}({\bm{x}};{\bm{\omega}})=\exp(w_{k}({\bm{x}};{\bm{\omega}}_{k}))\big/\!\left(1+\sum_{l=1}^{K-1}\exp(w_{l}({\bm{x}};{\bm{\omega}}_{l}))\right)
for all
k\in[K-1]
. In addition to the gating identifiability constraint, we also impose an identifiability convention for the multinomial-logistic expert networks, as considered in the convergence-rate analysis for softmax-gated multinomial logistic MoE models in
(
70
)
. Specifically, we fix a reference class and set, without loss of generality,
\{\upsilon_{M,k,d,p}\}_{d\in\{0,\ldots,D\},\,p\in[P]}=\mathbf{0}
for all
k\in[K]
, so that the last class probability is determined by the remaining
M-1
logits. Equivalently, for each expert
k
, we have
\mathsf{e}_{k}({\bm{v}}_{M,k}({\bm{x}}))=1-\sum_{m=1}^{M-1}\mathsf{e}_{k}({\bm{v}}_{m,k}({\bm{x}}))
and
\mathsf{e}_{k}({\bm{v}}_{m,k}({\bm{x}}))=\exp({v}_{m,k}({\bm{x}}))\big/\!\left(1+\sum_{l=1}^{M-1}\exp({v}_{l,k}({\bm{x}}))\right)
for all
m\in[M-1]
.

3
MM Algorithm for SGMLMoE Models

We now specialize the MM framework to the full-data (batch) maximum-likelihood problem for the SGMLMoE model in
Equation

1
with discrete output
{\textnormal{y}}\in{\mathbb{Y}}=[M]
. Let
\{({\bm{x}}_{n},{\textnormal{y}}_{n})\}_{n\in[N]}
be i.i.d. copies of
({\mathbf{x}},{\textnormal{y}})
and recall the observed-data log-likelihood
\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}})
in
Equation

2
. For each datum
n\in[N]
and expert
k\in[K]
, define the usual posterior responsibility at the current iterate
{\bm{\theta}}^{(t)}
by

\displaystyle\tau_{n,k}^{(t)}=\frac{\mathsf{g}_{k}\!\left({\bm{w}}^{(t)}({\bm{x}}_{n})\right)\,\mathsf{e}_{k}\!\left({\bm{v}}^{(t)}_{{\textnormal{y}}_{n}}({\bm{x}}_{n})\right)}{\sum_{l=1}^{K}\mathsf{g}_{l}\!\left({\bm{w}}^{(t)}({\bm{x}}_{n})\right)\,\mathsf{e}_{l}\!\left({\bm{v}}^{(t)}_{{\textnormal{y}}_{n}}({\bm{x}}_{n})\right)}.

(3)

To simplify notation for polynomial features, let

\displaystyle\hat{{\bm{x}}}_{n}=\left[{x}_{n,1}^{0},\dots,{x}_{n,1}^{D},\dots,{x}_{n,P}^{0},\dots,{x}_{n,P}^{D}\right]^{\top}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{P(D+1)}.

Next, we use an indicator polynomial
\mathbbm{1}(z,l)
to encode class labels:

\mathbbm{1}(z,l)=\frac{\prod_{q=1}^{M}(z-q)}{(z-l)(z-1)!(M-z)!(-1)^{M-z}},\forall z,l\in[M],

so that
\mathbbm{1}(z,l)\in\{0,1\}
. This representation is convenient for coding and yields the identity
{v}_{m,k}({\bm{x}})=\sum_{l=1}^{M}\mathbbm{1}(m,l)\,{v}_{l,k}({\bm{x}}),

which allows recovering any class logit from the vector of logits.

Batch Sufficient-statistics Notation.

For each
n\in[N]
, define the gating-design vector in
{\mathbb{R}}^{(K-1)P(D+1)}
:

\displaystyle{\bm{s}}_{n}^{(t)}=\big[\tau_{n,1}^{(t)}{x}_{n,1}^{0},\dots,\tau_{n,1}^{(t)}{x}_{n,1}^{D},\dots,\tau_{n,K-1}^{(t)}{x}_{n,P}^{D}\big]^{\top},

(4)

and define the expert-design vector
{\bm{r}}_{n}^{(t)}
by stacking
r_{n,d,l,p,k}^{(t)}=\mathbbm{1}({\textnormal{y}}_{n},l)\,\tau_{n,k}^{(t)}\,{x}_{n,p}^{d}
,
\penalty\ d\in\{0,\ldots,D\},\penalty\ l\in[M],\penalty\ p\in[P],\penalty\ k\in[K],

into

{\bm{r}}_{n}^{(t)}=\big[r_{n,0,1,1,1}^{(t)},\dots,r_{n,D,1,1,1}^{(t)},\dots,r_{n,D,M,1,1}^{(t)},\dots,r_{n,D,M,P,K}^{(t)}\big]^{\top}.

We also define the class-parameter blocks for each expert
k
by

{\bm{c}}_{k}=\big[\upsilon_{1,k,0,1},\dots,\upsilon_{1,k,D,1},\dots,{\bm{\upsilon}}_{M,k,D,1},\dots,\upsilon_{M,k,D,P}\big]^{\top}
,
{\bm{\upsilon}}=\vect([{\bm{c}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{c}}_{K}]).

Finally, we aggregate the batch statistics as

{\bm{s}}^{(t)}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\bm{s}}_{n}^{(t)}
,
{\bm{r}}^{(t)}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\bm{r}}_{n}^{(t)}.

Quadratic Majorizers for the Gate and Experts.

For
k\in[K-1]
, let
w_{k}({\bm{x}})=\sum_{d=0}^{D}{\bm{\omega}}_{k,d}^{\top}{\bm{x}}^{d}
and define the gate log-sum-exp term

g_{n}({\bm{w}})=\log\!\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\exp\!\left(w_{k}({\bm{x}}_{n})\right)\right).

For experts, define for each
k\in[K]
the multinomial log-sum-exp term

e_{n}({\bm{c}}_{k})=\log\!\left(1+\sum_{m=1}^{M-1}\exp\!\left({v}_{m,k}({\bm{x}}_{n})\right)\right).

For any
K\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we use the quadratic majorizers based on the matrices

\displaystyle{\bm{B}}_{n,K}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{3}{4}{\bm{I}}_{K-1}-\frac{\bm{1}_{K-1}\bm{1}_{K-1}^{\top}}{2(K-1)}\right)\otimes\hat{{\bm{x}}}_{n}\hat{{\bm{x}}}_{n}^{\top}.

(5)

For compactness, introduce the centered increments
\bar{{\bm{w}}}_{t}={\bm{w}}-{\bm{w}}^{(t)}
and
\bar{{\bm{c}}}_{k,t}={\bm{c}}_{k}-{\bm{c}}_{k}^{(t)}
.

4
Fast-rate-aware Aggregation and Sweep-free Model Selection for SGMLMoE

4.1
The “Rate Gap” under Overfitting

In the SGMLMoE classification model
Equation

1
–
Equation

2
, likelihood-based estimation is
challenging for the same structural reasons that arise in softmax-gated Gaussian experts
(
34
)
, but with multinomial-logistic
experts:
(i)
softmax translation invariance
makes gate parameters identifiable only up to a common shift;
(ii)
gate–expert coupling
yields nontrivial interactions in local expansions of
s_{{\bm{\theta}}}(\cdot\mid{\bm{x}})
;
(iii)
softmax numerator–denominator coupling
creates cancellation phenomena that can make densities close even
when individual components are not.

These effects become most visible when the fitted model is
over-specified
(
K>K_{0}
): several fitted atoms can
cluster around a single true expert. In this regime, the conditional density
s_{\widehat{G}_{N}}(\cdot\mid{\bm{x}})
can
converge at near-parametric rates in total variation, while componentwise parameters converge more slowly along
near-nonidentifiable directions. The goal of this section is to (a) introduce a Voronoi-type loss aligned with the
gate-induced partition geometry that resolves these slow directions, and (b) leverage it to build a
hierarchical
aggregation path
(a dendrogram of mixing measures) that yields a
sweep-free
and
consistent
selector of the
number of experts.

Practical Implication (Single-fit Workflow).

Fit one over-specified SGMLMoE (moderate
K\geq K_{0}
) using the batch MM procedure in
Algorithm

2
,
construct its dendrogram by iterative merging, and select
\widehat{K}
via a height–likelihood dendrogram selection criterion (DSC). This avoids
training multiple models across candidate sizes.

4.2
Mixing-measure Representation for SGMLMoE

Lifted Features and Intercept/Slope Decomposition.

With the polynomial degree
D
fixed, define the non-constant lifted feature map

\phi_{D}({\bm{x}}):=\vect\!\big([{\bm{x}}^{1},{\bm{x}}^{2},\ldots,{\bm{x}}^{D}]\big)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{PD}
,
\widehat{{\mathbb{X}}}:=\phi_{D}({\mathbb{X}})\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{PD}.

(Since
{\mathbb{X}}
is compact and
\phi_{D}
is continuous,
\widehat{{\mathbb{X}}}
is compact via using Heine-Borel theorem).
For each gate
k\in[K-1]
, decompose

w_{k}({\bm{x}})=\bar{\omega}_{k}+\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{k}^{\top}\hat{\bm{x}}
,
\hat{\bm{x}}:=\phi_{D}({\bm{x}}),

where
\bar{\omega}_{k}:=\sum_{p=1}^{P}\omega_{k,0,p}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and

\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{k}:=\vect([{\bm{\omega}}_{k,1},\ldots,{\bm{\omega}}_{k,D}])\in{\mathbb{R}}^{PD}
.
For each expert
k\in[K]
and class
m\in[M-1]
, similarly write

{v}_{m,k}({\bm{x}})=\bar{\upsilon}_{m,k}+\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m,k}^{\top}\hat{\bm{x}},

with
\bar{\upsilon}_{m,k}:=\sum_{p=1}^{P}\upsilon_{m,k,0,p}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and

\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m,k}:=\vect([{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m,k,1},\ldots,{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m,k,D}])\in{\mathbb{R}}^{PD}
.

Identifiability Conventions (as in
Section

2
).

We fix the reference expert and the reference class:
(\bar{\omega}_{K},\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{K})=(0,\mathbf{0}),

(\bar{\upsilon}_{M,k},\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{M,k})=(0,\mathbf{0}),\ \ \forall k\in[K].

Mixing Measures.

Let
{\mathbb{T}}
denote the identifiable parameter space for one SGMLMoE atom:

\eta=\Big(\hat{\bm{\omega}},\bar{\omega},\ \{(\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m},\bar{\upsilon}_{m})\}_{m=1}^{M-1}\Big)\in{\mathbb{T}},

and let
\mathcal{O}_{K}({\mathbb{T}})
be the set of finite mixing measures with at most

From “Density Closeness” to “Parameter Closeness”.

A key step in overfitted mixture/MoE analysis is an

and

should imply small discrepancy between
G
and
G_{0}
in a geometry-adapted loss. For softmax-gated

Voronoi Partition.

Write
G=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\pi_{i}\delta_{\eta_{i}}
and
G_{0}=\sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}\pi_{k}^{0}\delta_{\eta_{k}^{0}}
.
When

\eta_{i}

\eta_{k(i)}^{0}
under the local metric on
{\mathbb{T}}

{\mathbb{V}}_{k}:=\{\,i\in[K]:\ k(i)=k\,\}
,

For

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(G,G_{0})\>=\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{E}}(G,G_{0})

4.4
Why Merge Experts? The Dendrogram Viewpoint

The Overfitted Picture.

When
K>K_{0}
, some Voronoi cell
{\mathbb{V}}_{k}
may contain multiple fitted atoms. These atoms are “near duplicates”
from the perspective of the conditional density, and they create slow/flat directions in the likelihood geometry.
The dendrogram mechanism collapses each such cluster by repeated merging, producing a hierarchy of mixing measures

\widehat{G}_{N}^{(K)}=\widehat{G}_{N}\ \rightsquigarrow\ \widehat{G}_{N}^{(K-1)}\ \rightsquigarrow\ \cdots\ \rightsquigarrow\ \widehat{G}_{N}^{(2)}.

The key facts we exploit are:
(i) each merge reduces redundancy, and
(ii) the Voronoi loss becomes easier (monotonically) along the merge chain.

Atom Dissimilarity (Merge Criterion).

For two atoms
\pi_{i}\delta_{\eta_{i}}
and
\pi_{j}\delta_{\eta_{j}}
, define

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\!\big(\pi_{i}\delta_{\eta_{i}},\pi_{j}\delta_{\eta_{j}}\big):=\frac{\pi_{i}\pi_{j}}{\pi_{i}+\pi_{j}}\big(\|\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{i}-\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{m=1}^{M-1}\big(\|\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m,i}-\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m,j}\|^{2}+|\bar{\upsilon}_{m,i}-\bar{\upsilon}_{m,j}|^{2}\big)\big).

(9)

Merge Operator (Weight-averaged Atom).

Merging atoms
i
and
j
produces a new atom
(\pi_{*},{\bm{\eta}}_{*})
via parameter barycenters for
{\bm{\eta}}_{*}
as follows: given any
m\in[M-1]
,

\displaystyle\pi_{*}=\pi_{i}+\pi_{j},\penalty\ \bar{\omega}_{*}=\log\pi_{*},\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m,*}=\frac{\pi_{i}}{\pi_{*}}\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m,i}+\frac{\pi_{j}}{\pi_{*}}\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m,j},

\displaystyle\bar{\upsilon}_{m,*}=\frac{\pi_{i}}{\pi_{*}}\bar{\upsilon}_{m,i}+\frac{\pi_{j}}{\pi_{*}}\bar{\upsilon}_{m,j},\penalty\ \hat{\bm{\omega}}_{*}=\frac{\pi_{i}}{\pi_{*}}\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{i}+\frac{\pi_{j}}{\pi_{*}}\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{j}.

(10)

Theorem 4

(Voronoi monotonicity along the merge chain)
.

Let
G^{(K)},G^{(K-1)},\ldots,G^{(K_{0})}
be obtained by repeatedly merging the closest pair of atoms according to

, starting from
G^{(K)}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}({\mathbb{T}})
.
For
G^{(K)}
sufficiently close to
G_{0}

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}\!\big(G^{(K)},G_{0}\big)\ \gtrsim\ \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}\!\big(G^{(K-1)},G_{0}\big)\ \gtrsim\ \cdots\ \gtrsim\ \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}\!\big(G^{(K_{0})},G_{0}\big).

For

Condition K (local lower bound).

Interpretation of Why DSC Beats Pure Likelihood Penalties under Overfit.

Heights detect structural redundancy: small
h_{N}^{(\kappa)}
indicates that the
\kappa
-atom model contains a pair of
nearly indistinguishable atoms (often created by over-specification), which standard criteria (AIC/BIC/ICL) do not
directly penalize. DSC combines this structural signal with fit, yielding a sweep-free selector aligned with the Voronoi
geometry.

5
Numerical Experiment

In this section, we conduct experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets to systematically evaluate the effectiveness, stability, and practical utility of the proposed pipeline: batch MM training, Voronoi-loss diagnostics, and dendrogram-based selection/merging via DSC.
On
synthetic datasets
, we first demonstrate that the proposed Batch MM algorithm converges reliably to the global optimum, while classical optimization methods, including EM and popular gradient-based optimizers, often become trapped at suboptimal stationary points, in both well-specified and over-parameterized regimes.
We then empirically verify that the decay of the Voronoi loss aligns with the finite-sample convergence rates established in
Theorem

5
, under both exact-fit and over-fit settings.
Finally, we show that the proposed DSC-based merging strategy consistently outperforms standard model selection criteria (AIC, BIC, and ICL) in recovering the true number of experts, especially in small-sample regimes, while retaining consistency as sample size grows.
On
real-world datasets
, we show that SGMLMoE trained via Batch MM yields superior predictive performance compared to strong baselines such as SVM and Random Forest. Moreover, DSC selects an appropriate number of experts that achieves an attractive trade-off between predictive accuracy and memory efficiency, while the resulting dendrogram provides an interpretable hierarchical summary of the fitted mixing measure via

Conclusion and Perspectives.

In summary, our experiments confirm the paper’s main message: Batch MM yields stable maximum-likelihood training for SGMLMoE in the full-data regime, while the Voronoi-loss/dendrogram view provides a principled remedy for over-parameterization. Empirically, Batch MM converges reliably, the Voronoi loss decays in line with the predicted finite-sample rates, and DSC consistently selects a compact expert count that preserves accuracy and improves calibration on protein–protein interaction prediction. Future work includes extending the MM surrogates to incremental/mini-batch settings, broadening the dendrogram theory to richer expert parameterizations and structured regularization, and developing robustness guarantees under contamination and covariate shift.

Empirically, we observe (i) monotone and repeatable likelihood improvement with Batch MM across well-specified and over-specified settings, (ii) decay of the Voronoi loss consistent with the finite-sample theory in both exact-fit and over-fit regimes, and (iii) robust recovery of the effective number of experts via DSC, which translates into compact models with competitive or improved predictive performance and probability calibration on protein–protein interaction data.

Looking forward, several extensions are natural. First, it would be valuable to couple the present batch-MM stability guarantees with incremental or mini-batch variants to handle larger-scale datasets while preserving the monotone-surrogate structure. Second, extending the dendrogram/DSC analysis to richer expert parameterizations (e.g., deeper feature maps, structured sparsity, or context-dependent expert sharing) could broaden applicability while maintaining interpretability through the merging hierarchy. Third, the robustness observed under mild misspecification suggests studying contamination and covariate-shift regimes theoretically, with DSC-style structural penalties as a principled alternative to purely likelihood-based criteria. Finally, developing open-source, production-grade implementations and broader biological benchmarks (beyond PPI) would further clarify when and how hierarchical aggregation best trades off accuracy, calibration, and memory/compute in heterogeneous classification problems.

Monotonicity of Maximum Loglikelihood Estimator.
Classical optimization methods such as SGD and Adam often lead to unstable MLE behavior in mixture or Mixture-of-Experts models. This instability arises from the highly non-convex likelihood landscape, where inappropriate step sizes can cause oscillations or divergence, making performance sensitive to careful learning-rate tuning. Such tuning is problem-dependent and can be computationally costly, limiting robustness and reproducibility. In contrast, our proposed method avoids these issues by adopting an MM-based optimization scheme that guarantees monotonic improvement of the MLE objective, as established in
Theorem

\bar{{\bm{c}}}_{k,t}={\bm{c}}_{k}-{\bm{c}}_{k}^{(t)}
as in
Section

D.2
.
Since the surrogate is the sum of (i) a gate quadratic term in
{\bm{w}}
and (ii)
K
independent expert quadratics in
\{{\bm{c}}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
,
it decomposes as

\mathcal{S}({\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\theta}}^{(t)})=C^{(t)}+\mathcal{S}_{g}({\bm{w}};{\bm{\theta}}^{(t)})+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{S}_{e,k}({\bm{c}}_{k};{\bm{\theta}}^{(t)}),

where
C^{(t)}
does not depend on
{\bm{\theta}}
, and (up to additive constants)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{g}({\bm{w}};{\bm{\theta}}^{(t)})

\displaystyle=-{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{s}}^{(t)}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Big\{\nabla g_{n}({\bm{w}}^{(t)})^{\top}\bar{{\bm{w}}}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\,\bar{{\bm{w}}}_{t}^{\top}{\bm{B}}_{n,K}\bar{{\bm{w}}}_{t}\Big\},

(22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{e,k}({\bm{c}}_{k};{\bm{\theta}}^{(t)})

\displaystyle=-{\bm{c}}_{k}^{\top}{\bm{r}}_{k}^{(t)}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau_{n,k}^{(t)}\Big\{\nabla e_{n}({\bm{c}}_{k}^{(t)})^{\top}\bar{{\bm{c}}}_{k,t}+\frac{1}{2}\,\bar{{\bm{c}}}_{k,t}^{\top}{\bm{B}}_{n,M}\bar{{\bm{c}}}_{k,t}\Big\}.

(23)

Here
{\bm{s}}^{(t)}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\bm{s}}_{n}^{(t)}
,
{\bm{r}}^{(t)}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\bm{r}}_{n}^{(t)}
, and
{\bm{r}}_{k}^{(t)}
is the block of
{\bm{r}}^{(t)}

corresponding to expert
k
(under the stacking convention defining
{\bm{\upsilon}}=\vect([{\bm{c}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{c}}_{K}])
).

Introduce the aggregated curvatures and gradients

\nabla g({\bm{w}}^{(t)}):=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\nabla g_{n}({\bm{w}}^{(t)}),\qquad\nabla e_{k}^{(t)}:=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau_{n,k}^{(t)}\,\nabla e_{n}({\bm{c}}_{k}^{(t)}).

Then

is a (strictly) convex quadratic in
{\bm{w}}
whenever

, and differentiating yields

Setting this to
\mathbf{0}
gives the unique minimizer

{\bm{w}}^{(t+1)}={\bm{w}}^{(t)}+{\bm{B}}_{K-1}^{-1}\big({\bm{s}}^{(t)}-\nabla g({\bm{w}}^{(t)})\big),

(24)

which matches the gate update in
Algorithm

1
(up to the same aggregation notation).

Similarly, for each

,

is a convex quadratic in

and

\nabla_{{\bm{c}}_{k}}\mathcal{S}_{e,k}({\bm{c}}_{k};{\bm{\theta}}^{(t)})=-{\bm{r}}_{k}^{(t)}+\nabla e_{k}^{(t)}+{\bm{B}}_{M-1,k}^{(t)}({\bm{c}}_{k}-{\bm{c}}_{k}^{(t)}).

Setting this to
\mathbf{0}
yields

{\bm{c}}_{k}^{(t+1)}={\bm{c}}_{k}^{(t)}+\big({\bm{B}}_{M-1,k}^{(t)}\big)^{-1}\big({\bm{r}}_{k}^{(t)}-\nabla e_{k}^{(t)}\big),\qquad k\in[K],

(25)

equivalent to the expert update in
Algorithm

1
(the algorithm writes this in a vectorized form).
Since
\mathcal{S}
decomposes across the gate block and expert blocks, the concatenation

{\bm{\theta}}^{(t+1)}=({\bm{w}}^{(t+1)},{\bm{\upsilon}}^{(t+1)})
satisfies

{\bm{\theta}}^{(t+1)}\in\arg\min_{{\bm{\theta}}}\ \mathcal{S}({\bm{\theta}},{\bm{\theta}}^{(t)}).

(26)

If some curvature matrix is singular, the same first-order conditions hold using the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse,
or by adding a small ridge
\lambda{\bm{I}}
; in either case the MM validity in
Equation

21
is unchanged.

Step 3: Monotonicity (MM Ascent).

By exact minimization
Equation

26
,

\mathcal{S}({\bm{\theta}}^{(t+1)},{\bm{\theta}}^{(t)})\leq\mathcal{S}({\bm{\theta}}^{(t)},{\bm{\theta}}^{(t)}).

By tangency in
Equation

21
,

\mathcal{S}({\bm{\theta}}^{(t)},{\bm{\theta}}^{(t)})=-\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}}^{(t)})
.
By majorization in
Equation

21
evaluated at
{\bm{\theta}}={\bm{\theta}}^{(t+1)}
,

-\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}}^{(t+1)})\leq\mathcal{S}({\bm{\theta}}^{(t+1)},{\bm{\theta}}^{(t)})
.
Combining the three displays yields

-\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}}^{(t+1)})\leq-\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}}^{(t)})\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}}^{(t+1)})\geq\mathcal{L}({\bm{\theta}}^{(t)}),

which proves
Theorem

2
.

Proof of
Theorem

This result extends the inverse-inequality technique developed in
70
to our
SGMLMoE parameterization with polynomial score functions. Throughout, we use the lifted feature
\hat{\bm{x}}=\phi_{D}({\bm{x}})\in\widehat{{\mathbb{X}}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{PD}
and the identifiable

4.2

Section

4.3

Recall that for

We prove the local and global parts separately.

Local Structure.

We show the following local inverse inequality:

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\ \inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\in\mathcal{O}_{K}({\mathbb{T}}):\\
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(G,G_{0})\leq\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{TV}}(s_{G},s_{G_{0}})}{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(G,G_{0})}\ >\ 0.

(27)

Assume by contradiction that

27
fails. Then there exists a sequence

G^{(r)}=\sum_{l=1}^{K}\pi_{l}^{(r)}\delta_{\eta_{l}^{(r)}}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}({\mathbb{T}})
such that

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(G^{(r)},G_{0})\to 0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{TV}}(s_{G^{(r)}},s_{G_{0}})}{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(G^{(r)},G_{0})}\to 0\qquad(r\to\infty).

(28)

For
r
large, the Voronoi partition
\{{\mathbb{V}}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K_{0}}
is well-defined as in

Section

4.3
. For
l\in{\mathbb{V}}_{k}
, write the local differences

\Delta\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{lk}^{(r)}=\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{l}^{(r)}-\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{k}^{0}
,

\Delta\bar{\upsilon}_{lkm}^{(r)}=\bar{\upsilon}_{m,l}^{(r)}-\bar{\upsilon}_{m,k}^{0}
,

\Delta\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{lkm}^{(r)}=\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m,l}^{(r)}-\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m,k}^{0}

and
\Delta\pi_{k}^{(r)}=\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{V}}_{k}}\pi_{l}^{(r)}-\pi_{k}^{0}

By

7
and
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(G^{(r)},G_{0})\to 0

Step 1: Finite Taylor Representation.

Define the true gate normalizer

For each class
m\in[M]

(29)

Using the softmax form for

a Taylor expansion around each true atom

(30)

where:
(i)

);
(ii) the coefficients

are explicit finite sums over

of monomials in

\Delta\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{lk}^{(r)}
,
\Delta\bar{\upsilon}_{lkm}^{(r)}
,
\Delta\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{lkm}^{(r)}
, and
\Delta\pi_{k}^{(r)}
,
weighted by
\pi_{l}^{(r)}
;
(iii) the remainder satisfies

\sup_{m\in[M]}\ \mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}}\!\big[\,|R_{r}(m,\phi_{D}({\mathbf{x}}))|\,\big]\ =\ o\!\big(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(G^{(r)},G_{0})\big),\qquad(r\to\infty).

(31)

Step 2: Some Normalized Coefficient Must be Non-vanishing.

Let

m_{r}:=\max_{k,\xi}\ \frac{|A_{k,\xi}^{(r)}|}{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(G^{(r)},G_{0})}.

We claim
\liminf_{r\to\infty}m_{r}>0
.
If instead
m_{r}\to 0
, then all coefficients in
Equation

30
would satisfy

|A_{k,\xi}^{(r)}|=o(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(G^{(r)},G_{0}))
.
But the collection of coefficients
\{A_{k,\xi}^{(r)}\}
contains, by construction, the same first- and second-order
weighted increments that appear in
Equation

7
:
for cells
|{\mathbb{V}}_{k}|=1
it contains the weighted linear terms

\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{V}}_{k}}\pi_{l}^{(r)}\|\Delta\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{lk}^{(r)}\|

\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{V}}_{k}}\pi_{l}^{(r)}|\Delta\bar{\upsilon}_{lkm}^{(r)}|
,

\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{V}}_{k}}\pi_{l}^{(r)}\|\Delta\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{lkm}^{(r)}\|

and for cells

it contains the weighted quadratic terms

\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{V}}_{k}}\pi_{l}^{(r)}\|\Delta\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{lk}^{(r)}\|^{2}

\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{V}}_{k}}\pi_{l}^{(r)}|\Delta\bar{\upsilon}_{lkm}^{(r)}|^{2}

\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{V}}_{k}}\pi_{l}^{(r)}\|\Delta\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{lkm}^{(r)}\|^{2}

Step 3: Fatou and Linear Independence Gives a Contradiction.

Divide
Equation

30

and take covariate expectation.
Using
Equation

28

Equation

29
, and
Equation

31
,
we obtain

\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}}\!\Bigg[\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{|T_{r}(m,\phi_{D}({\mathbf{x}}))|}{m_{r}\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(G^{(r)},G_{0})}\Bigg]\ \longrightarrow\ 0.

By Fatou’s lemma, for each
m\in[M]
we can extract a subsequence (not relabeled) such that

\frac{T_{r}(m,\hat{\bm{x}})}{m_{r}\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(G^{(r)},G_{0})}\ \longrightarrow\ 0\qquad\text{for a.e. }\hat{\bm{x}}\in\widehat{{\mathbb{X}}}.

Along this subsequence, the normalized coefficients converge (by boundedness) to limits

a_{k,\xi}=\lim_{r\to\infty}A_{k,\xi}^{(r)}/(m_{r}\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(G^{(r)},G_{0}))
,
with at least one
a_{k,\xi}\neq 0
because
\liminf m_{r}>0
.
Passing to the limit in
Equation

30
yields, for each
m\in[M]
and a.e.

,

\sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}\ \sum_{\xi\in\Xi_{k}}a_{k,\xi}\ \Psi_{k,\xi}(m,\hat{\bm{x}})\ =\ 0.

(32)

Finally, by identifiability of the SGMLMoE and the fact that

are distinct, the family of analytic functions

is linearly independent on
\widehat{{\mathbb{X}}}
.
Thus

32
forces all

, contradicting that at least one is nonzero.
This contradiction establishes the local bound

27
.

Global Structure.

By the local result, there exists
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{TV}}(s_{G},s_{G_{0}})\ \geq\ c^{\prime}\,\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(G,G_{0})\qquad\text{whenever }\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(G,G_{0})\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}.

It remains to prove the complementary bound on the set

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\in\mathcal{O}_{K}({\mathbb{T}}):\\
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(G,G_{0})\geq\varepsilon^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{TV}}(s_{G},s_{G_{0}})}{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(G,G_{0})}\ >\ 0.

(33)

Assume

33
fails. Then there exists

such that

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(G^{\prime(r)},G_{0})\geq\varepsilon^{\prime}

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{TV}}(s_{G^{\prime(r)}},s_{G_{0}})\to 0

Under the standing compactness assumption on

(hence on

for some

Therefore

Proof of

We prove the first link of the chain,

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}\!\big(G^{(K)},G_{0}\big)\gtrsim\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}\!\big(G^{(K-1)},G_{0}\big),

since the remaining inequalities follow by repeating the same argument at each merge step.

Throughout, we write a generic
K
-atom measure as

G=\sum_{l=1}^{K}\pi_{l}\,\delta_{\eta_{l}},\qquad\pi_{l}=\exp(\bar{\omega}_{l}),\qquad\eta_{l}=\Big(\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{l},\bar{\omega}_{l},\{(\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m,l},\bar{\upsilon}_{m,l})\}_{m=1}^{M-1}\Big)\in{\mathbb{T}},

and we use the Voronoi partition
\{{\mathbb{V}}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K_{0}}
induced by
G_{0}
as in
Section

4.3
.
For
l\in{\mathbb{V}}_{k}
, recall the parameter gaps

\Delta\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{lk}=\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{l}-\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{k}^{0},\ \Delta\bar{\upsilon}_{lkm}=\bar{\upsilon}_{m,l}-\bar{\upsilon}_{m,k}^{0},\ \Delta\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{lkm}=\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m,l}-\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m,k}^{0}

for

Step 1: the Closest Pair Lies in a Common Voronoi Cell.

Let
G^{(K)}
be sufficiently close to
G_{0}
so that the Voronoi cells
{\mathbb{V}}_{k}
are well-defined and stable.
Since the merge rule in
Section

4.4
uses squared Euclidean dissimilarities in

\hat{\bm{\omega}}

and

(\bar{\upsilon}_{m},\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m})_{m\in[M-1]},

any pair of atoms belonging to different Voronoi cells stays separated by a fixed positive amount (depending only on
G_{0}

and the local metric on
{\mathbb{T}}
), whereas within-cell pairs can be arbitrarily close when over-fitting occurs.
Consequently, for
G^{(K)}
close enough to
G_{0}
, the minimizing pair in

h^{(K)}=\min_{l\neq l^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}(\pi_{l}\delta_{\eta_{l}},\pi_{l^{\prime}}\delta_{\eta_{l^{\prime}}})

must belong to the same cell
{\mathbb{V}}_{k}
.
Fix such a cell and denote it by
{\mathbb{V}}_{k^{\star}}
, and assume the merged indices are
(l_{1},l_{2})\subset{\mathbb{V}}_{k^{\star}}
.

Step 2: Notation for the Merge.

Let
G^{(K-1)}
be obtained from
G^{(K)}
by merging atoms
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
according to
Section

4.4
.
Write the merged atom as
(\pi_{\ast},\eta_{\ast})
, where

\pi_{\ast}=\pi_{l_{1}}+\pi_{l_{2}},\qquad\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{\ast}=\frac{\pi_{l_{1}}}{\pi_{\ast}}\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{l_{1}}+\frac{\pi_{l_{2}}}{\pi_{\ast}}\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{l_{2}},

and, for each

\bar{\upsilon}_{m,\ast}=\frac{\pi_{l_{1}}}{\pi_{\ast}}\bar{\upsilon}_{m,l_{1}}+\frac{\pi_{l_{2}}}{\pi_{\ast}}\bar{\upsilon}_{m,l_{2}},\qquad\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m,\ast}=\frac{\pi_{l_{1}}}{\pi_{\ast}}\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m,l_{1}}+\frac{\pi_{l_{2}}}{\pi_{\ast}}\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m,l_{2}}.

All other atoms are unchanged.
Since
l_{1},l_{2}\in{\mathbb{V}}_{k^{\star}}
and
G^{(K)}
is close to
G_{0}
, the merged atom also remains assigned to the same cell

{\mathbb{V}}_{k^{\star}}
.

Step 3: the Voronoi Weight-mismatch Term is Preserved.

For each
k\in[K_{0}]
, the weight-mismatch contribution

\big|\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{V}}_{k}}\pi_{l}-\pi_{k}^{0}\big|

is unchanged by merging within
{\mathbb{V}}_{k}
:
indeed, for

\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{V}}_{k^{\star}}}\pi_{l}=\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{V}}_{k^{\star}}\setminus\{l_{1},l_{2}\}}\pi_{l}+\pi_{l_{1}}+\pi_{l_{2}}=\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{V}}_{k^{\star}}\setminus\{l_{1},l_{2}\}}\pi_{l}+\pi_{\ast},

so the absolute deviation from
\pi_{k^{\star}}^{0}
is identical before and after merging.

Step 4: Within-cell Quadratic Terms Decrease under Barycentric Merging.

Consider first the gate-slope quadratic term on the over-covered cell
|{\mathbb{V}}_{k^{\star}}|>1
:

\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{V}}_{k^{\star}}}\pi_{l}\|\Delta\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{lk^{\star}}\|^{2}=\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{V}}_{k^{\star}}\setminus\{l_{1},l_{2}\}}\pi_{l}\|\Delta\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{lk^{\star}}\|^{2}+\pi_{l_{1}}\|\Delta\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{l_{1}k^{\star}}\|^{2}+\pi_{l_{2}}\|\Delta\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{l_{2}k^{\star}}\|^{2}.

By convexity of
u\mapsto\|u\|^{2}
and the definition

\Delta\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{\ast k^{\star}}=\frac{\pi_{l_{1}}}{\pi_{\ast}}\Delta\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{l_{1}k^{\star}}+\frac{\pi_{l_{2}}}{\pi_{\ast}}\Delta\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{l_{2}k^{\star}},

we have the Jensen inequality

\pi_{l_{1}}\|\Delta\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{l_{1}k^{\star}}\|^{2}+\pi_{l_{2}}\|\Delta\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{l_{2}k^{\star}}\|^{2}\geq\pi_{\ast}\|\Delta\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{\ast k^{\star}}\|^{2}.

Hence,

\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{V}}_{k^{\star}}}\pi_{l}\|\Delta\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{lk^{\star}}\|^{2}\geq\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{V}}_{k^{\star}}\setminus\{l_{1},l_{2}\}}\pi_{l}\|\Delta\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{lk^{\star}}\|^{2}+\pi_{\ast}\|\Delta\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{\ast k^{\star}}\|^{2},

which is exactly the corresponding contribution after merging
l_{1},l_{2}
into
\ast
.

The same argument applies to each expert block, for every
m\in[M-1]
:
since
u\mapsto|u|^{2}
and
u\mapsto\|u\|^{2}
are convex,

\pi_{l_{1}}|\Delta\bar{\upsilon}_{l_{1}k^{\star}m}|^{2}+\pi_{l_{2}}|\Delta\bar{\upsilon}_{l_{2}k^{\star}m}|^{2}\geq\pi_{\ast}|\Delta\bar{\upsilon}_{\ast k^{\star}m}|^{2},

\pi_{l_{1}}\|\Delta\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{l_{1}k^{\star}m}\|^{2}+\pi_{l_{2}}\|\Delta\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{l_{2}k^{\star}m}\|^{2}\geq\pi_{\ast}\|\Delta\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{\ast k^{\star}m}\|^{2},

and therefore the entire within-cell quadratic contribution in
Equation

7
for cell
{\mathbb{V}}_{k^{\star}}

does not increase after merging.

Step 5: Conclusion.

All other cells are unaffected, and the linear (single-covered) terms in
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{E}}(G,G_{0})
are unchanged
because they depend only on weights and on singletons. Hence,

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}\!\big(G^{(K)},G_{0}\big)\ \gtrsim\ \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}\!\big(G^{(K-1)},G_{0}\big),

with an absolute constant (in particular, one may take the implicit constant to be
1
in the within-cell comparison).
Iterating the same argument along the successive merges yields the full chain claimed in
Theorem

4
.

Wasserstein Distance for Finite Mixing Measures.

For two mixing measures

G=\sum_{l=1}^{K}\pi_{l}\,\delta_{\eta_{l}},\qquad G^{\prime}=\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{K^{\prime}}\pi^{\prime}_{l^{\prime}}\,\delta_{\eta^{\prime}_{l^{\prime}}},

\Pi(\pi,\pi^{\prime})=\Big\{Q\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{K\times K^{\prime}}:\ \sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{K^{\prime}}Q_{ll^{\prime}}=\pi_{l},\ \sum_{l=1}^{K}Q_{ll^{\prime}}=\pi^{\prime}_{l^{\prime}},\ \forall l\in[K],\,l^{\prime}\in[K^{\prime}]\Big\}.

A Useful Lower Bound (Wasserstein Controls Componentwise Discrepancy).

.
Then (see, e.g.,
(
39
)
) there exists a Voronoi assignment

and cells

{\mathbb{V}}_{k}=\{l\in[K]:k(l)=k\}
such that

W_{r}^{r}(G,G_{0})\ \gtrsim\ \sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}\Bigg(\Big|\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{V}}_{k}}\pi_{l}-\pi_{k}^{0}\Big|+\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{V}}_{k}}\pi_{l}\,\|\eta_{l}-\eta_{k}^{0}\|^{r}\Bigg).

(34)

Part 1: Voronoi and Height Rates on Over-fitted Levels
\kappa\in\{K_{0}+1,\ldots,K\}
.

Let

A_{N}:=\Big\{\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{TV}}(s_{\widehat{G}_{N}},s_{G_{0}})\leq C\sqrt{\log N/N}\Big\},

where
C>0
is the constant from
1
. By
1
,

\mathbb{P}(A_{N})\geq 1-c_{1}N^{-c_{2}}.

On
A_{N}
, combining
Theorem

3
with the density bound yields

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(\widehat{G}_{N},G_{0})\ \lesssim\ \sqrt{\log N/N}.

(35)

Next, by
Theorem

4
, the Voronoi loss is monotone (up to constants) along the merge chain, hence for each

\kappa\in\{K_{0}+1,\ldots,K\}
,

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)},G_{0})\ \lesssim\ \sqrt{\log N/N}.

(36)

We now bound the dendrogram height
h_{N}^{(\kappa)}
for
\kappa\geq K_{0}+1
.
Since
\kappa>K_{0}
, at level
\kappa
there exists at least one Voronoi cell containing two distinct atoms of

\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)}
. Fix such a cell
{\mathbb{V}}_{k^{\star}}
and pick two distinct indices

l_{1}\neq l_{2}\in{\mathbb{V}}_{k^{\star}}
.
Write
\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)}=\sum_{l=1}^{\kappa}\pi_{l}^{(\kappa)}\delta_{\eta_{l}^{(\kappa)}}
and abbreviate

\pi_{l}:=\pi_{l}^{(\kappa)},\ \eta_{l}:=\eta_{l}^{(\kappa)}.

From the definition of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}
in
Equation

7
and
Equation

36
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\pi_{l_{1}}\Big(\|\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{l_{1}}-\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{k^{\star}}^{0}\|^{2}+\sum_{m=1}^{M-1}\big(|\bar{\upsilon}_{m,l_{1}}-\bar{\upsilon}_{m,k^{\star}}^{0}|^{2}+\|\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m,l_{1}}-\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m,k^{\star}}^{0}\|^{2}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\pi_{l_{2}}\Big(\|\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{l_{2}}-\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{k^{\star}}^{0}\|^{2}+\sum_{m=1}^{M-1}\big(|\bar{\upsilon}_{m,l_{2}}-\bar{\upsilon}_{m,k^{\star}}^{0}|^{2}+\|\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m,l_{2}}-\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m,k^{\star}}^{0}\|^{2}\big)\Big)\ \lesssim\ \sqrt{\log N/N}.

(37)

Using the elementary inequality

\min\{\pi_{l_{1}},\pi_{l_{2}}\}\ \geq\ \frac{\pi_{l_{1}}\pi_{l_{2}}}{\pi_{l_{1}}+\pi_{l_{2}}},

and the triangle inequality

\|\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{l_{1}}-\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{l_{2}}\|\leq\|\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{l_{1}}-\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{k^{\star}}^{0}\|+\|\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{l_{2}}-\hat{\bm{\omega}}_{k^{\star}}^{0}\|

(and similarly for expert parameters), we can lower bound the left-hand side of
Section

D.6
by a constant multiple of the dissimilarity

\mathrm{d}(\pi_{l_{1}}\delta_{\eta_{l_{1}}},\pi_{l_{2}}\delta_{\eta_{l_{2}}})

defined in
Section

4.4
. Concretely, there exists a universal
c>0
such that

\mathrm{d}\!\big(\pi_{l_{1}}\delta_{\eta_{l_{1}}},\pi_{l_{2}}\delta_{\eta_{l_{2}}}\big)\ \leq\ c\cdot\text{LHS of \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq_two_atom_quadratic_small}}\ \lesssim\ \sqrt{\log N/N}.

Since
h_{N}^{(\kappa)}
is the minimum dissimilarity over all pairs of atoms at level
\kappa
,

h_{N}^{(\kappa)}\ \lesssim\ \sqrt{\log N/N},\qquad\forall\kappa\in\{K_{0}+1,\ldots,K\}.

(38)

Part 2: Under-fitted Levels
\kappa^{\prime}\in[K_{0}]
.

At the exact level
K_{0}
, each Voronoi cell is a singleton, and by inspection of
Equation

7
,

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(\widehat{G}_{N}^{(K_{0})},G_{0})

is equivalent (up to constants) to a
W_{1}
-type discrepancy between the two
K_{0}
-atom measures. In particular,

W_{1}(\widehat{G}_{N}^{(K_{0})},G_{0})\ \lesssim\ \sqrt{\log N/N}.

(39)

Write

\widehat{G}_{N}^{(K_{0})}=\sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}\pi_{k,N}\,\delta_{\eta_{k,N}},\qquad G_{0}=\sum_{k=1}^{K_{0}}\pi_{k}^{0}\,\delta_{\eta_{k}^{0}},

under the natural matching implied by the Voronoi construction (valid for
N
large enough on
A_{N}
).
Then
Equation

39
implies componentwise control, e.g.,

|\pi_{k,N}-\pi_{k}^{0}|\lesssim\sqrt{\log N/N},\qquad\|\eta_{k,N}-\eta_{k}^{0}\|\lesssim\sqrt{\log N/N},\qquad k\in[K_{0}],

and hence, for any fixed pair
(k_{1},k_{2})
, the pairwise dissimilarity at level
K_{0}
satisfies the perturbation bound

\Big|\mathrm{d}\!\big(\pi_{k_{1},N}\delta_{\eta_{k_{1},N}},\pi_{k_{2},N}\delta_{\eta_{k_{2},N}}\big)-\mathrm{d}\!\big(\pi_{k_{1}}^{0}\delta_{\eta_{k_{1}}^{0}},\pi_{k_{2}}^{0}\delta_{\eta_{k_{2}}^{0}}\big)\Big|\ \lesssim\ \sqrt{\log N/N},

(40)

where we used that all relevant parameters are uniformly bounded (and
\pi_{k}^{0}
are bounded away from
0
).

Now let
(k_{1}^{\star},k_{2}^{\star})
be the (unique, for simplicity) minimizing pair for the true height

h_{0}^{(K_{0})}=\min_{k_{1}\neq k_{2}}\mathrm{d}(\pi_{k_{1}}^{0}\delta_{\eta_{k_{1}}^{0}},\pi_{k_{2}}^{0}\delta_{\eta_{k_{2}}^{0}}).

By
Equation

40
, for
N
large enough the minimizing pair for the empirical dissimilarity at level
K_{0}
coincides with
(k_{1}^{\star},k_{2}^{\star})
, and therefore

|h_{N}^{(K_{0})}-h_{0}^{(K_{0})}|\ \lesssim\ \sqrt{\log N/N}.

After merging this pair once, the merged atom parameters are barycenters as in
Section

4.4
, so the same Lipschitz-type perturbation argument (using
Equation

39
and boundedness of parameters/weights away from
0
) yields

W_{1}\!\big(\widehat{G}_{N}^{(K_{0}-1)},G_{0}^{(K_{0}-1)}\big)\ \lesssim\ \sqrt{\log N/N},\qquad|h_{N}^{(K_{0}-1)}-h_{0}^{(K_{0}-1)}|\ \lesssim\ \sqrt{\log N/N}.

Iterating this argument inductively down the chain proves that for every
\kappa^{\prime}\in[K_{0}]
,

|h_{N}^{(\kappa^{\prime})}-h_{0}^{(\kappa^{\prime})}|\ \lesssim\ \sqrt{\log N/N}.

(41)

Conclusion.

Combining
Equation

36
,
Equation

38
, and
Equation

41
,
and recalling
\mathbb{P}(A_{N})\geq 1-c_{1}N^{-c_{2}}
, completes the proof of
Theorem

5
.
∎

D.7
Proof of
Theorem

6

Suppose
({\mathbf{x}}_{1},{\textnormal{y}}_{1}),\ldots,({\mathbf{x}}_{N},{\textnormal{y}}_{N})
are i.i.d. from
G_{0}
and write the empirical measure

P_{N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\delta_{({\bm{x}}_{n},{y}_{n})}
(with
({\bm{x}}_{n},{y}_{n})
the observed sample).
For any mixing measure
G\in\mathcal{O}_{K}({\mathbb{T}})
, define the empirical process

\nu_{N}(G):=\sqrt{N}\,(P_{N}-P_{G_{0}})\log\frac{s_{G}}{s_{G_{0}}}.

We will repeatedly use the following exponential inequality.

Fact 2

(Theorem 5.11 from
(
96
)
)
.

Let
R,C,C_{1},a>0
satisfy

a\leq C_{1}\sqrt{N}\,R^{2}\ \wedge\ 8\sqrt{N}\,R,

and

a\geq\sqrt{C^{2}(C_{1}+1)}\left(\int_{a/(2^{6}\sqrt{N})}^{R}H_{B}^{1/2}\!\left(\frac{u}{\sqrt{2}},\ \{\,s_{G}:\ h(s_{G},s_{G_{0}})\leq R\,\},\ \nu\right)\,du\ \vee\ R\right).

Then

\mathbb{P}_{G_{0}}\!\left(\sup_{h(s_{G},s_{G_{0}})\leq R}\big|\nu_{N}(G)\big|\geq a\right)\leq C\exp\!\left(-\frac{a^{2}}{C^{2}(C_{1}+1)R^{2}}\right).

Case 1:
\kappa\geq K_{0}
.

For
\kappa\in\{K_{0},\ldots,K\}
, let
\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)}
denote the
\kappa
-atom mixing measure along the dendrogram path and write

\bar{\ell}_{N}^{(\kappa)}=\bar{\ell}_{N}(\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\log s_{\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)}}({y}_{n}\mid{\bm{x}}_{n})
.

Step 1 (a concavity bound).

Using concavity of
\log
, for any two densities
p,q
,

\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{p}{q}\leq\log\frac{p+q}{2q}.

Applying this with
p=s_{\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)}}({y}\mid{\bm{x}})
and
q=s_{G_{0}}({y}\mid{\bm{x}})
gives

\frac{1}{2}\,P_{N}\log\frac{s_{\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)}}}{s_{G_{0}}}\leq P_{N}\log\frac{\bar{s}_{\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)}}}{s_{G_{0}}},\qquad\bar{s}_{\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)}}:=\frac{s_{\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)}}+s_{G_{0}}}{2}.

Moreover,

P_{N}\log\frac{\bar{s}_{\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)}}}{s_{G_{0}}}=(P_{N}-P_{G_{0}})\log\frac{\bar{s}_{\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)}}}{s_{G_{0}}}-D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(s_{G_{0}}\,\|\,\bar{s}_{\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)}}\right)\leq(P_{N}-P_{G_{0}})\log\frac{\bar{s}_{\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)}}}{s_{G_{0}}}.

Hence,

P_{N}\log s_{\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)}}-\mathcal{L}(G_{0})=P_{N}\log\frac{s_{\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)}}}{s_{G_{0}}}+(P_{N}-P_{G_{0}})\log s_{G_{0}}\leq 2(P_{N}-P_{G_{0}})\log\frac{s_{\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)}}}{s_{G_{0}}}+(P_{N}-P_{G_{0}})\log s_{G_{0}}.

(42)

Step 2 (Hellinger radius at over-fitted levels).

By
Theorem

5
, with probability at least
1-c_{1}N^{-c_{2}}
,

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}\!\big(\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)},G_{0}\big)\ \lesssim\ \sqrt{\log N/N},\qquad\kappa\in\{K_{0},\ldots,K\}.

Since
W_{2}^{2}(\cdot,\cdot)\lesssim\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(\cdot,\cdot)
(for measures on bounded parameter sets with the Voronoi construction),
we obtain on the same event

W_{2}\!\big(\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)},G_{0}\big)\ \lesssim\ (\log N/N)^{1/4}.

Using the standard inequality
h(s_{G},s_{G_{0}})\lesssim W_{2}(G,G_{0})
for these finite-mixture/MoE families on bounded domains (see, e.g.,
(
82
)
),
we deduce that there exists
R_{N}\asymp(\log N/N)^{1/4}
and an event
A_{N}
with
\mathbb{P}_{G_{0}}(A_{N})\geq 1-c_{1}N^{-c_{2}}
such that

h\!\left(s_{\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)}},s_{G_{0}}\right)\leq R_{N},\qquad\forall\kappa\in\{K_{0},\ldots,K\}.

(43)

Step 3 (empirical-process control).

Set
R:=R_{N}
in
2
and choose

a:=\sqrt{N}\,R_{N}\,\log(1/R_{N})\ \asymp\ N^{1/4}\log^{1/4}N\cdot\log N

(which is of the same order as
\sqrt{N}R_{N}\log(1/R_{N})
).
Using the entropy bound
H_{B}^{1/2}(u,\{s_{G}:\ h(s_{G},s_{G_{0}})\leq R\},\nu)\lesssim\log(1/u)
(e.g. Lemma 4 from
(
72
)
),
the conditions of
2
hold for
N
large enough, yielding

\mathbb{P}_{G_{0}}\!\left(\sup_{h(s_{G},s_{G_{0}})\leq R_{N}}\big|\nu_{N}(G)\big|\ \gtrsim\ a\right)\lesssim N^{-c}

for some
c>0
. Equivalently, dividing by
\sqrt{N}
,

\mathbb{P}_{G_{0}}\!\left(\sup_{h(s_{G},s_{G_{0}})\leq R_{N}}\big|(P_{N}-P_{G_{0}})\log(s_{G}/s_{G_{0}})\big|\ \gtrsim\ R_{N}\log(1/R_{N})\right)\lesssim N^{-c}.

(44)

Combining
Equation

43
with
Equation

44
yields that, for each fixed
\kappa\in\{K_{0},\ldots,K\}
,

\big|(P_{N}-P_{G_{0}})\log(s_{\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)}}/s_{G_{0}})\big|\ \lesssim\ R_{N}\log(1/R_{N})\ \lesssim\ (\log N/N)^{1/4}

(45)

with probability at least
1-c_{1}^{\prime}N^{-c_{2}^{\prime}}
.

Step 4 (control of
(P_{N}-P_{G_{0}})\log s_{G_{0}}
).

By Chebyshev’s inequality,

\mathbb{P}_{G_{0}}\!\left(\big|(P_{N}-P_{G_{0}})\log s_{G_{0}}\big|\geq t\right)\leq\frac{\mathrm{Var}(\log s_{G_{0}})}{Nt^{2}}.

(46)

Taking
t\asymp(\log N/N)^{1/4}
gives

\big|(P_{N}-P_{G_{0}})\log s_{G_{0}}\big|\ \lesssim\ (\log N/N)^{1/4}

(47)

with probability at least
1-\mathrm{Var}(\log s_{G_{0}})/\sqrt{\log N}
.

Step 5 (conclusion for
\kappa\geq K_{0}
).

Plugging
Equation

45
and
Equation

47
into
Equation

42
yields, with probability tending to
1
,

\bar{\ell}_{N}^{(\kappa)}-\mathcal{L}(G_{0})=P_{N}\log s_{\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)}}-\mathcal{L}(G_{0})\ \lesssim\ (\log N/N)^{1/4},\qquad\kappa\in\{K_{0},\ldots,K\}.

This proves the first claim of
Theorem

6
.

Case 2:
\kappa=K_{0}
(a Matching Lower Bound using Condition K).

At the exact-fitted level,
Theorem

5
implies

W_{1}(\widehat{G}_{N}^{(K_{0})},G_{0})\ \lesssim\ \sqrt{\log N/N}.

Let
\epsilon_{N}:=\sqrt{\log N/N}
. By the definition of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{V}}
at singleton Voronoi cells (exact-fit),
this implies a componentwise bound
\|\eta_{k,N}-\eta_{k}^{0}\|\lesssim\epsilon_{N}
and
|\pi_{k,N}-\pi_{k}^{0}|\lesssim\epsilon_{N}
(under the canonical matching),
hence Condition K yields the pointwise inequality

\log s_{\widehat{G}_{N}^{(K_{0})}}({\textnormal{y}}\mid{\mathbf{x}})\geq(1+c_{\beta}\epsilon_{N})\log s_{G_{0}}({\textnormal{y}}\mid{\mathbf{x}})-c_{\alpha}\epsilon_{N},\qquad({\mathbf{x}},{\textnormal{y}})\in{\mathbb{X}}\times{\mathbb{Y}},

for some constants
c_{\alpha},c_{\beta}>0
.
Averaging over
P_{N}
and subtracting
\mathcal{L}(G_{0})
gives

\bar{\ell}_{N}^{(K_{0})}-\mathcal{L}(G_{0})\geq-c_{\alpha}\epsilon_{N}+c_{\beta}\epsilon_{N}\,P_{N}\log s_{G_{0}}+(1+c_{\beta}\epsilon_{N})(P_{N}-P_{G_{0}})\log s_{G_{0}}.

Using
Equation

46
with
t=\epsilon_{N}
implies

(P_{N}-P_{G_{0}})\log s_{G_{0}}=O_{\mathbb{P}}(\epsilon_{N}),

and since
P_{N}\log s_{G_{0}}=P_{G_{0}}\log s_{G_{0}}+O_{\mathbb{P}}(\epsilon_{N})
, we conclude that

\bar{\ell}_{N}^{(K_{0})}-\mathcal{L}(G_{0})\ \geq\ -C\epsilon_{N}

in
\mathbb{P}_{G_{0}}
-probability. Together with the upper bound from Case 1 at
\kappa=K_{0}
, this yields

\big|\bar{\ell}_{N}^{(K_{0})}-\mathcal{L}(G_{0})\big|\ \lesssim\ (\log N/N)^{1/4}

(up to logs), which is consistent with the statement in
Theorem

6
.

Case 3:
\kappa<K_{0}
.

Fix
\kappa\in\{2,\ldots,K_{0}-1\}
. By boundedness of
{\mathbb{X}}
and the parameter space and the existence of an integrable envelope

m({\bm{x}},{\textnormal{y}})
such that
\sup_{G\in\mathcal{O}_{\kappa}}|\log s_{G}({\textnormal{y}}\mid{\bm{x}})|\leq m({\bm{x}},{\textnormal{y}})
,
the class
\{\log s_{G}:\ G\in\mathcal{O}_{\kappa}\}
is Glivenko–Cantelli. Hence (e.g.
48
, Theorem 9.2
),

\sup_{G\in\mathcal{O}_{\kappa}}\big|\bar{\ell}_{N}(G)-\mathcal{L}(G)\big|\ \to\ 0\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{P}_{G_{0}}\text{-probability}.

Since
\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)}
maximizes
\bar{\ell}_{N}(\cdot)
over
\mathcal{O}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{T}})
, it follows that

\bar{\ell}_{N}(\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)})\ \to\ \sup_{G\in\mathcal{O}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{T}})}\mathcal{L}(G)\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{P}_{G_{0}}\text{-probability}.

Denoting by
G_{0}^{(\kappa)}
a maximizer of
\mathcal{L}(\cdot)
over
\mathcal{O}_{\kappa}({\mathbb{T}})
, we obtain the second claim in
Theorem

6
:

\bar{\ell}_{N}(\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)})\ \to\ \mathcal{L}(G_{0}^{(\kappa)})\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{P}_{G_{0}}\text{-probability}.

∎

D.8
Proof of Condition K in
Section

4.6

Fix
{\bm{\theta}}^{0}\in{\mathbb{T}}
and suppose
\|{\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}^{0}\|\leq\epsilon
for
\epsilon>0
small.
By norm equivalence on finite-dimensional spaces, it suffices to assume that for all
m\in[M-1]
,

\|\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m}-\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m}^{0}\|\leq\epsilon,\qquad|\bar{\upsilon}_{m}-\bar{\upsilon}_{m}^{0}|\leq\epsilon,

(and the reference class
m=M
is fixed at
(\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{M},\bar{\upsilon}_{M})=(\mathbf{0},0)
).
We show that there exist constants
c_{\alpha},c_{\beta}>0
such that for all
({\mathbf{x}},{\textnormal{y}})\in{\mathbb{X}}\times{\mathbb{Y}}
,

\log s_{{\bm{\theta}}}({\textnormal{y}}\mid{\mathbf{x}})\geq(1+c_{\beta}\epsilon)\log s_{{\bm{\theta}}^{0}}({\textnormal{y}}\mid{\mathbf{x}})-c_{\alpha}\epsilon.

Since
{\mathbb{X}}
is compact and the parameter space is bounded, the score map

(\hat{\bm{\upsilon}},\bar{\upsilon},{\bm{x}})\mapsto\log\mathsf{e}({\textnormal{y}}\mid{\bm{x}};\hat{\bm{\upsilon}},\bar{\upsilon})

is locally Lipschitz uniformly over
{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{X}}
.
Thus there exists
L>0
such that, for all
m\in[M-1]
and all
{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{X}}
,

\big|(\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m}-\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{m}^{0})^{\top}{\bm{x}}\big|\leq L\epsilon,\qquad|\bar{\upsilon}_{m}-\bar{\upsilon}_{m}^{0}|\leq\epsilon.

Moreover, the map

(\{\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{\ell},\bar{\upsilon}_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{M-1},{\bm{x}})\mapsto\log\!\big(1+\sum_{\ell=1}^{M-1}\exp(\bar{\upsilon}_{\ell}+\hat{\bm{\upsilon}}_{\ell}^{\top}{\bm{x}})\big)

is smooth, hence locally Lipschitz, and therefore its perturbation is
O(\epsilon)
uniformly over
{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{X}}
.
Combining these uniform Lipschitz bounds yields

\log\mathsf{e}({\textnormal{y}}\mid{\bm{x}};{\bm{\theta}})\geq\log\mathsf{e}({\textnormal{y}}\mid{\bm{x}};{\bm{\theta}}^{0})-C\epsilon

for some
C>0
and all
({\bm{x}},{\textnormal{y}})
.
Finally, since
\log\mathsf{e}({\textnormal{y}}\mid{\bm{x}};{\bm{\theta}}^{0})\leq 0
and is bounded below on
{\mathbb{X}}\times{\mathbb{Y}}
, we may choose

c_{\beta}>0
small enough and
c_{\alpha}>0
large enough so that

\log\mathsf{e}({\textnormal{y}}\mid{\bm{x}};{\bm{\theta}})\geq(1+c_{\beta}\epsilon)\log\mathsf{e}({\textnormal{y}}\mid{\bm{x}};{\bm{\theta}}^{0})-c_{\alpha}\epsilon.

Applying the same reasoning to the gating part and summing over experts inside
s_{\bm{\theta}}
gives Condition K.
∎

Recall
\bar{\ell}_{N}^{(\kappa)}:=\bar{\ell}_{N}(\widehat{G}_{N}^{(\kappa)})
and

\mathrm{DSC}_{N}^{(\kappa)}:=-\big(h_{N}^{(\kappa)}+\omega_{N}\bar{\ell}_{N}^{(\kappa)}\big)
.
From
Theorem

5
,

h_{N}^{(\kappa)}=\begin{cases}O_{\mathbb{P}}\!\big((\log N/N)^{1/2}\big),&\kappa>K_{0},\\[2.58334pt]
h_{0}^{(\kappa)}+O_{\mathbb{P}}\!\big((\log N/N)^{1/2}\big),&\kappa\leq K_{0}.\end{cases}

From
Theorem

6
,

\bar{\ell}_{N}^{(\kappa)}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}(G_{0})+O_{\mathbb{P}}\!\big((\log N/N)^{1/4}\big),&\kappa\geq K_{0},\\[2.58334pt]
\mathcal{L}(G_{0}^{(\kappa)})+o_{\mathbb{P}}(1),&\kappa<K_{0},\end{cases}

and
\mathcal{L}(G_{0}^{(\kappa)})<\mathcal{L}(G_{0})
for
\kappa<K_{0}
(equivalently
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(s_{G_{0}}\|s_{G_{0}}^{(\kappa)})>0

Therefore,

\mathrm{DSC}_{N}^{(\kappa)}=\begin{cases}-\omega_{N}\mathcal{L}(G_{0})+O_{\mathbb{P}}\!\big(\omega_{N}(\log N/N)^{1/4}\big),&\kappa>K_{0},\\[3.44444pt]
-\omega_{N}\mathcal{L}(G_{0})-h_{0}^{(K_{0})}+O_{\mathbb{P}}\!\big(\omega_{N}(\log N/N)^{1/4}\big),&\kappa=K_{0},\\[3.44444pt]
-\omega_{N}\mathcal{L}(G_{0}^{(\kappa)})-h_{0}^{(\kappa)}+o_{\mathbb{P}}(\omega_{N}),&\kappa<K_{0}.\end{cases}

Since
\omega_{N}\to\infty
and
\omega_{N}(\log N/N)^{1/4}\to 0
, while
\mathcal{L}(G_{0})-\mathcal{L}(G_{0}^{(\kappa)})>0
for
\kappa<K_{0}
,
we have that for
N
large enough,

\mathrm{DSC}_{N}^{(K_{0})}<\min_{\kappa\neq K_{0}}\mathrm{DSC}_{N}^{(\kappa)}

with
\mathbb{P}_{G_{0}}
-probability tending to
1
. Hence
\widehat{K}_{N}=\arg\min_{\kappa\in\{2,\ldots,K\}}\mathrm{DSC}_{N}^{(\kappa)}

satisfies
\widehat{K}_{N}\to K_{0}
in
\mathbb{P}_{G_{0}}
-probability.
∎

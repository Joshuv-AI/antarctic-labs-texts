Breakthrough energy storage in medium-entropy superparaelectrics by triple synergy strategy
Authors: Yang Liu, Jiarui Wang, Lulu Liu, Jigong Hao, Peng Li, Zhongbin Pan, Jianli Wang, Min Zhang, Limei Zheng, Hairui Bai, Song Chen, Wei Li, Zhenxiang Cheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
High-performance dielectric ceramic capacitors hold immerse potential for advanced electronics and high-power electrical systems. However, achieving both high recoverable energy density (Wrec) and high energy efficiency (η) remains a critical challenge in bulk dielectric ceramics. Herein, we propose a triple-synergistic strategy that integrates medium-entropy superparaelectric design, local structural distortion, and plate-like secondary bismuth titanate phase construction in SrTiO3-based dielectric ceramics. This approach effectively promotes the formation of polymorphic polar nano-regions with short-range polar vectors, enhancing dynamic polarization response. Simultaneously, the introduction of a plate-like secondary bismuth titanate phase within the perovskite matrix significantly increases the breakdown field strength (Eb) by inhibiting breakdown-path propagation. The resulting medium-entropy superparaelectric exhibits a nearly hysteresis-free P-E loop, combined with an ultrahigh Eb and high polarization. Notably, the material achieves a remarkable Wrec of 10.31 J/cm3 along with an ultrahigh η of 90.9%. Furthermore, it demonstrates outstanding charge/discharge performance, excellent thermal stability and robust cycling stability, making it a highly promising candidate for energy storage applications. This study provides a feasible and innovative design strategy for high-performance bulk dielectrics, paving the way for the development of eco-friendly dielectric capacitors for next-generation energy storage systems.

Introduction

Dielectric energy storage capacitors have emerged as a compelling solution for advanced energy storage applications, owing to their exceptional attributes such as high-power density, rapid charging/discharging capabilities, and cost-effective manufacturing. These unique properties have established them as critical components across a range of cutting-edge technologies, from automotive electronics and aerospace systems to next-generation communications infrastructure1. However, despite their remarkable potential, dielectric capacitors face a critical limitation: their energy storage density remains significantly lower than that of lithium-ion batteries and chemical capacitors. This poses a substantial barrier to their widespread adoption, particularly in miniaturized and integrated circuit systems, where space efficiency and performance are paramount. Addressing this challenge through enhanced energy storage performance has thus become a pressing priority in materials science and engineering.

The performance of energy storage ceramics is primarily characterized by two metrics: recoverable energy storage density (Wrec) and efficiency (η) as illustrated in Fig. S1. Meanwhile, the charge-discharge performance of the energy storage ceramics is crucial for evaluating their potential for practical applications (Fig. S2). Extensive research has been conducted on ferroelectric and anti-ferroelectric compositions, including BaTiO3 (BT), (K0.5Na0.5)NbO3 (KNN), (Bi0.5Na0.5)TiO3 (BNT), BiFeO3 (BF), AgNbO3 (AN) and NaNbO3 (NN). Significant advancements have been achieved through chemical design, microstructure control, and process optimization2–15. In contrast to these nonlinear dielectric ceramics, SrTiO3 (ST)-based linear dielectric ceramic offers distinct advantages, such as low dielectric loss and high breakdown field strength (Eb), which enable superior energy storage efficiency and ultrafast charging/discharging rates1–3. However, the inherent limitation of ST ceramics results in a notably low maximum polarization strength (Pmax = 7.95 µC/cm2) and, consequently, a modest Wrec (<2.0 J/cm3)16. This performance gap has spurred extensive efforts to enhance the energy storage capabilities of ST-based ceramics through chemical modification and process optimization, yet their Wrec remains inferior to that of other lead-free systems, particularly when η exceeds 90%17–25.

The fundamental issue lies in the highly symmetric cubic structure of ST, which inherently lacks spontaneous polarization, leading to a low Pmax. To overcome this limitation, inducing non-cubic structural distortion through chemical design has emerged as a promising strategy. Recent breakthrough in lead-free relaxors have demonstrated the efficacy of balancing structural distortion (δ) and tolerance factor (t) in perovskites, achieving high Wrec and η through the integration of paraelectric and ferroelectric components with large δ 26. Furthermore, the development of superparaelectrics (SPEs) has introduced a new paradigm in dielectric energy storage27,28, where highly dynamic polar nanoregions (PNRs) enable low-energy domain switching and minimal hysteresis. Beyond polarization optimization, maximization Eb has been identified as a critical factor in enhancing energy storage performance. Strategies such as increasing configurational entropy to induce atomic disorder and lattice distortion5,29, as well as microstructural engineering through grain refinement and secondary phase construction19,30, have shown significant potential in improving resistivity and Eb.

Building on these insights, we innovatively propose a triple-synergistic strategy to revolutionize the energy storage performance of ST-based ceramics. This approach integrates medium-entropy SPE design, local structural distortion, and the construction of plate-like secondary bismuth titanate (BiTO) phases within a perovskite matrix. The tailored composition (Sr0.49Na0.15Bi0.29-xErx)TiO3 (SNBT-xEr) leverages advanced chemical design to achieve medium entropy (ΔS = 1.02-1.07 R, Fig. 1a), local structural distortions (Fig. 1b), and a wide-temperature-range SPE state (Fig. 1c). Moreover, the novel composition promotes the formation of plate-like BiTO phases within the ceramic matrix, creating a heterogeneous microstructure (Fig. 1d). This unique microstructure not only facilitates ultrafast polarization response with minimal hysteresis through the generation of an intrinsic flipping field (EF)30 (Fig. 1e) but also enhances the Eb by impeding breakdown propagation (Fig. 1f). The resulting medium-entropy SPE exhibits exceptional energy storage performance, achieving a superior Wrec of 10.31 J/cm3 along with an ultrahigh η of 90.9% under an applied electric field of 810 kV/cm. This breakthrough marks a significant milestone in the development of ST-based energy storage ceramics (Fig. 1g, Table S1), offering a transformative approach to achieving high energy density and efficiency in dielectric materials. Results and discussion

Phase structure and microstructural analysis

The phase structure and microstructural characteristics of the SNBT-xEr (x = 0-0.02) ceramics were thoroughly examined to elucidate the impact of Er3+ doping on the material’s crystallographic and structural properties. The XRD patterns (Fig. S3) reveal that the undoped SNBT ceramic crystallizes into a pure perovskite structure without detectable secondary phase. However, the introduction of Er3+ ions induces the emergence of additional diffraction peaks corresponding to the secondary BiTO phase (Bi2Ti2O7, PDF#00-032-0118; Bi2Ti4O11, PDF#01-072-1820), highlighting the structural modifications induced by Er doping. SEM-EDS, SEM-EBSD, TEM-EDS and Raman mapping analysis (Figs. S4–S9) further confirm the localized distribution of the secondary BiTO phase within the ceramic matrix. Specifically, the plate-like BiTO phase is identified primarily as Bi2Ti4O11, as corroborated by SAED pattern and atomic-resolution EDS mapping (Fig. S8). Despite these secondary phase contributions, the primary diffraction features remain consistent with a cubic perovskite structure, indicating the preservation of the dominant phase. Moreover, all samples exhibit high relative densities ( > 97%, Table S2), demonstrating that densification is not hindered by the emergence of the second BiTO phase.

The temperature-dependent dielectric behaviour of SNBT-0.005Er ceramics were systemically examined (Fig. 2d), revealing the variation of dielectric constant (εr) and loss tangent (tanδ) with temperature. Complementary data for other compositions are provided in Fig. S11. The broad εr -T peak, accompanied by diffuse phase transition and frequency dispersion, is characteristic of relaxor ferroelectrics, confirming the material’s intrinsic relaxor nature. For the x = 0.005 sample, the temperature corresponding to the maximum permittivity (Tm) is 15 oC (@100 kHz), while the Burns temperature (TB) determined from the 1/εr -T curve follows the Curie-Weiss law, is 216 oC (@100 kHz). This delineates an exceptionally broad superparaelectric state window (15 oC <T < 216 oC), extending from below room temperature to well above 200 °C. Within this temperature range, highly dynamic PNRs emerges, a phenomenon critical for achieving superior energy storage performance27. Furthermore, the relaxation kinetics of the compositions was further analyzed using the Vogel-Fulcher (V-F) model (Fig. S12), revealing an elevated activation energy (Ea) and a lower freezing temperature (Tf) in Er-doped compositions. These changes indicate that Er3+ doping effectively strengthens the dynamic response of PNRs and weakens the long-range coupling through local structural modifications.

To corroborate the existence of these dynamic PNRs and to gain deeper insights into their microstructure, transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and piezoelectric force microscopy (PFM) were employed. Bright-field and high-resolution TEM images of the SNBT-0.005Er sample (Fig. 2e,f) reveals no discernible macrodomains, consistent with the room temperature superparaelectric state. However, localized polar regions exhibiting lattice distortions are evident in inverse fast Fourier transform patterns derived from high-resolution TEM (Fig. 2g), providing direct evidence of nanoscale PNRs19,32.

The stability and dynamic response of the PNRs were further probed using PFM, as shown in Figs. 2h, i and S13. Out-of-plane PFM phase and amplitude images (Fig. 2h, i) of the SNBT-0.005Er sample after poling treatment with varying voltages and relaxation durations show minimal domain contrast in unpoled state, aligning with TEM results and confirming the strong relaxor behavior and room temperature SPE state. Localized poling experiments (5, 10, 20 and 30 V, applied to 6 × 6 µm2 areas) revealed pronounced PFM signals post-poling, which rapidly diminished upon relaxation due to the swift polarization response of the PNRs. Regions polarized at 5 V, 10 V, and 20 V/30 V returned to their original state after 5, 10 and 15 minutes of relaxation, respectively, highlight the dynamic reversibility of PNRs upon poling voltage removal. The behavior is pivotal for enhancing the Wrec and η22,23.

To delve deeper into the local structure and spatial distribution of PNRs, scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM) with atomic-level resolution was employed (Fig. 3). High-angle annular dark-field (HAADF) STEM images, combined with atomic displacement vector mapping along the [100]c crystal axis (Fig. 3a), reveal polarization vectors (yellow arrows) originating from B site cations and extending toward neighboring A-site cations, reflecting relative cation displacement. Quantitative analysis of polarization magnitude and angle distribution (Fig. 3b, c) reveals an average polarization magnitude of ~5.4 pm (normally distributed) and a broad range of polarization angles, resulting in weak net polarization. This disordered arrangement of polarization vectors facilitates the nearly hysteresis-free P-E loop6. The observed short-range polar vectors and disrupted polar order provide a structural basis for the SPE behavior, elucidating the underlying mechanism behind the material’s superior Wrec and η under high Eb.

Furthermore, the formation of plate-like BiTO phase also contributes to the local structural distortion and polarization enhancement of the SNBT-0.005Er. Local structure analysis at the perovskite-BiTO phase boundary provides direct evidence of the chemical segregation (Fig. S17) and reveals severe local lattice distortion (Fig. S18). This distortion gives rise to a distinct strain field at the interface, which serves to enhance the local dipole moments while disrupting their long-range ordering. Notably, within a transition zone of 5-8 atomic layers from the perovskite matrix to the phase boundary, the polarization vectors exhibit an ordering trend. This zone, identified as a region of coexisting R- and T-phase PNRs, directly contributes to the enhanced polarization in Er-doped samples. These results experimentally validate our chemical design strategy for high-performance ST-based SPEs, highlighting the critical role of lattice distortion and local structural modification in optimizing energy storage properties.

Energy storage, charge-discharge performance and stability

The synergistic integration of room temperature SPE structures and local lattice distortions in SNBT-xEr ceramics yield exceptionally slim P-E loops (Figs. 4a and S19). The optimal performance is achieved at x = 0.005, delivering an exceptional Wrec of 10.31 J/cm3 and a high η of 90.9%, with good reproducibility across different tested samples. The Weibull distribution analysis (sample size N = 8) shows a high Eb of 851 kV/cm (95% confidence interval CI: 806.02-868.98 kV/cm), accompanied by a large shape parameter (β) of 15.49 (95%CI: 11.15-31.57) (Fig. S20). Furthermore, this ceramic demonstrates good batch-to-batch reproducibility, highlighted by the consistent formation of the plate-like phase and stable energy storage performance across different batches (Fig. S21). This performance surpasses that of the undoped SNBT ceramic, driven by two complementary mechanisms: enhanced ΔP (Pmax-Pr) resulting from R-T phases coexistence, and substantially improved Eb enabled by Er3+-driven entropy enhancement and the formation of plate-like BiTO phases. This breakthrough Wrec significantly exceeds the ~8.0 J/cm3 ceiling typical of ST-based ceramic systems18–22,36–40 (Fig. 4b, Table S3) and outperforms state-of-the-art high-efficiency (η ≥ 90%) dielectrics, including BNT-, BKT-, BT-, KNN-, NN-, AN-, BF-, and CT-based bulk ceramics11,41–54. Moreover, the material demonstrates robust scalability, maintaining high performance (Eb ≥ 615 kV/cm, Wrec > 7.2 J/cm3, η > 90%) even with increased sample thickness or electrode size (Fig. S22, Table S4). Based on the Wrec and η vaules shown in Fig. 4a, the figure of merit (WF = Wrec/(1−η)) reaches an ultrahigh value of 113, eclipsing benchmark materials17–21,24,25,36–38 (Fig. 4c, Table S5). Remarkably, the material demonstrates robust operational stability under harsh conditions: variations in Wrec and η are limited to ≤ 6.3% and ≤ 3.9%, respectively, across a wide temperature range (25-200 °C), frequency span (1-200 Hz), and 104 charge-discharge cycles (Figs. 4d and S23). Additionally, the ceramic exhibit superior thermal endurance, quantified as ((Tmax–Tmin)/ΔWrec(%) = 27.8), which leads reported ceramics under thermal stress (Fig. 4e, Table S5)7,9,11,21,22,24,25,27,33,36,39,40,43,44,46,48–55. Practical viability is further demonstrated through ultrafast discharge characteristics. At an applied field of 550 kV/cm, the capacitor delivers a discharge energy density (Wd) of 5.08 J/cm3, a current density (CD) of 1310.38 A/cm2, and a power density (PD) of 360.35 MW/cm3 with a discharge time (t0.9) of 42 ns (Fig. 4f,g). Compared with the existing energy storage systems18,19,40,52–54,56,60–64, the sample exhibits significantly enhanced charge-discharge performance at elevated temperatures and during repeated cycling. In-situ XRD confirms the material’s structural integrity during heating (Fig. S26), with only lattice expansion-induced peak shifts observed. This stability is attributed to the ultra-wide SPE window (15–216 °C), where persistent dynamics of polar nanoregions enable sustained high-temperature performance. The comprehensive performance metrics of the optimal composition (x = 0.005), as summarized in Table S7, highlight its superior combination of high energy density, efficiency, and stability.

Origin of enhanced energy storage induced by Er-doping

Er3+ doping significantly enhances energy storage performance of SNBT ceramics through complementary atomic and microstructural mechanisms. To elucidate these effects, we systematically investigated how erbium incorporation optimizes both polarization and Eb in ST-based ceramics. Using the constructed structural models for the undoped and Er-doped components (Fig. S27), we analyzed the electronic structure of the materials by employing first-principles calculations. During the calculations, we also performed a sensitivity analysis to confirm that the observed electronic structure effects are robust and reliable (Fig. S28). Density functional theory (DFT) calculations reveal atomic-scale polarization mechanisms underlying this enhancement. Differential charge density distributions in the Sr-Bi plane (Fig. 5a, b) show similar electron accumulation and deficiency patterns around Er3+ and Bi3+ ions, indicating analogous ionic interactions. Crucially, electron localization function (ELF) analysis (Fig. 5c, d) demonstrates significantly reduced electron density at Er-O bonds compared to Bi-O bonds (denoted by green lines), revealing greater lattice displacement freedom for Er3+. This enhanced mobility directly boosts ionic polarization. Furthermore, density of states (DOS) spectra (Fig. S29) confirm Er3+ doping strengthens existing O 2p-Bi 6s hybridization while creating new O 2p-Er 6s couplings. Concurrently, the Eb improvement stems from two reinforcing phenomena. First, the increased entropy enhances local lattice distortion, refining the PNRs. These highly dynamic PNRs suppress polarization-rotation heating and carrier migration27, thereby contributing to the enhanced Eb. Second, Er3+-induced plate-like BiTO phases play a pronounced role in enhancing Eb (Fig. S30, Table S8), as they dramatically improve the ceramic’s insulation and provide a blocking effect that impedes breakdown propagation. Electrical characterization confirms a significant increase in resistivity and a corresponding decrease in conductivity (Fig. 5e–g), while the activation energy rises from 1.33 eV to 1.48 eV (Fig. 5h), providing clear evidence of inhibited charge transport66. In addition, the enhanced Eb also correlates with a wider band gap (Eg) (Fig. S31). This correlation is mechanistically explained by the known role of a wider Eg in suppressing intrinsic carrier excitation under high fields47. Finite element simulations visually demonstrate the reinforcement arising from the plate-like BiTO phase (Figs. 5i, j and S32–34). In pure SNBT, electrical tree penetration occurs at 775 kV/cm (Figs. 5i and S33a), whereas the plate-like BiTO-embedded SNBT-0.005Er ceramic forces tortuous propagation pathways that circumvent insulating plates (Figs. 5j and S33b). This morphological obstruction delays complete breakdown until 900 kV/cm (Fig. S34), attributable to BiTO phase’s low dielectric loss and low dielectric constant, which physically impede charge progression. In the present work, finite element simulations and macroscopic impedance data provide strong correlative evidence for the breakdown-blocking role of the BiTO phase. However, direct experimental evidence linking the BiTO plates to breakdown suppression is still needed to be more conclusive. Thus, future work utilizing local electrical probes (e.g., Kelvin probe force microscopy) will be essential to provide such direct verification.

Thus, Er3+ doping orchestrates dual advantages: atomic-scale polarization optimization through enhanced ionic mobility and orbital hybridization, coupled with microstructural breakdown resistance via strategically dispersed plate-like BiTO phases. Importantly, this beneficial plate-like morphology has been successfully replicated in preliminary multilayer architectures (Fig. S35), providing crucial evidence for the scalability and future development of multilayer capacitors (MLCCs) based on this material system.

In summary, a high-performance medium-entropy SPE material based on SrTiO3 ceramics has been successfully developed through a triple-synergistic strategy, achieving an exceptional comprehensive energy storage performance. The material delivers a remarkable comprehensive performance with Wrec ~ 10.31 J/cm3, η ~ 90.9% and WF ~ 113 under 810 kV/cm. Moreover, the medium-entropy SPE exhibits remarkable temperature stability (25–200 °C), frequency stability (1–200 Hz), cycling stability (104 cycles), and good charge-discharge performance (Wd ~ 5.08 J/cm3, t0.9 ~ 42 ns). The impressive performance is attributed to the synergistic effect including formation of polymorphic PNRs with short-range polar vectors and the constructed plate-like BiTO phase that effectively hinder the breakdown path propagation. This work establishes an effective method for designing high-performance bulk dielectrics through the medium-entropy SPE design, which synergistically combines local distortion engineering and plate-like BiTO phase construction.

Methods

Materials preparation

SNBT-xEr (x = 0-0.02) samples were prepared by solid-state synthesis using SrCO3 (99%, Alfa Aesar), Bi2O3 (99.975%, Alfa Aesar), TiO2 (99.6%, Alfa Aesar), Na2CO3 (99.95–100.05%, Alfa Aesar) and Er2O3 (99.9%, Sinopharm Co. Ltd, China) as the raw materials. The raw materials were weighed according to the nominal composition and then ball-milled for 24 h using ethanol and zirconia balls as the grinding media. After drying, the powders were calcined at 950 oC for 4 h, followed by a second ball-milling process for 24 h. The powders were mixed well with appropriate amount of binder and then were cast into specimens via a tape-casting method. After removing binder at 600 °C, samples were sintered at 1150 °C–1200 °C for 2 h. To minimize the volatilization of Bi and Na during sintering, samples were embedded in sacrificial powder of the same composition and sintered within a sealed crucible.

Structure characterization

The phase structure of the samples was identified by X-ray powder diffraction (D8 advanced, Bruker, Germany). To determine the phase composition, high-resolution synchrotron X-ray diffraction (SXRD) measurements were carried out at the Australian Synchrotron Beamline (λ = 0.590340 Å). The software for SXRD Rietveld refinements is Fullprof. The microstructure of the samples was observed using field emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM; Carl Zeiss, Germany). The phase distribution and grain morphology of the ceramics were characterized using electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) on an FEI Apreo ChemiSEM S instrument with a TruePix detector. Raman mapping analysis on the localized microstructure of the sample was performed using a LabRAM HR800 Raman microspectrometer (Horiba Scientific, France). The domain morphologies and local point group symmetries of the samples were analyzed by the transmission electron microscopy (TEM) studied using an FEI Talos F200X transmission electron microscope. The TEM results including bright-field scanning transmission electron microscopy (BF-TEM) images, high-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HR-TEM) images and selected area electron diffraction (SAED) patterns. The response of domain structure to electric fields of the samples was observed by the piezoelectric force microscopy (PFM) measurements using an MFP-3D Origin Atomic Force Microscope (Asylum Research, Oxford instruments). For the detailed analysis of local structure, an advanced scanning transmission electron microscope (STEM, Thermofisher Spectra 300) equipped with a double aberration corrector was used to obtain atomic-level images, including high-angle annular dark field (HAADF)-STEM and annular bright field (ABF) images. The analysis of polarization vectors, along with their amplitudes and angles, was performed by determining atomic positions via a two-dimensional elliptic Gaussian distribution. εxx and εyy strain components were mapped using geometric phase analysis (GPA).

Electrical measurements

For dielectric properties, the ceramics were coated with silver electrodes and characterized by the precise impedance analyzer (Wayne Kerr 6500B, China). The impedance properties of the samples were characterized using a broadband dielectric spectrometer (Concept 80, Novocontrol Inc., Germany). For the energy storage performance and its dependence on sample thickness and electrode size, samples with varying thicknesses (30 ± 3, 40 ± 3, 60 ± 5, and 100 ± 5 μm) were prepared, and gold electrodes with diameters of 1, 2, 3 and 5 mm were then sputtered on their surfaces. The P-E hysteresis loops of the ceramics were analyzed by a ferroelectric tester (Poly K, USA). The charge–discharge performances were analyzed using a pulsed charge–discharge system (model CFD-003 plus, Tongguo Technology Co., China). The tests were performed on samples with silver electrodes. Detailed information regarding the electrical measurements is provided in the Supporting Information.

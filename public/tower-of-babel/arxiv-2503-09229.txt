Improved a priori error estimates for a space-time finite element method for parabolic problems
Thi Thanh Mai Ta, Quang Huy Nguyen, Phi Hung Pham

Abstract
In this paper, we employ a space-time finite element method to discretize the parabolic initial-boundary value problem and extend its error analysis with refined estimates on unstructured space-time meshes. We establish higher-order estimates in three different norms, thereby supplementing existing research. Moreover, we obtain an optimal estimate in a norm stronger than that of the trial space. Finally, we present numerical examples to illustrate our theoretical results.

Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded, Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \left(n=1,2,3\right)
with boundary
\partial\Omega
and let
\left(0,T\right)
be a bounded time interval, where
T>0
. The space-time cylinder is denoted by
\Omega_{T}:=\Omega\times\left(0,T\right)
. This paper considers the following parabolic initial-boundary value problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\nabla\cdot\left(\boldsymbol{A}\nabla u\right)=f\qquad\quad&\text{in}\quad\Omega_{T},\\
u=0\qquad\quad&\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega\times\left(0,T\right),\\
u(\cdot,0)=0\qquad\quad&\text{on}\quad\Omega,\end{cases}

(1)

where
\boldsymbol{A}=\boldsymbol{A}^{\top}\in\mathbb{L}^{\infty}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
is a given uniformly positive definite matrix, and
f\in\Ls^{2}\left(\left(0,T\right),\Hs^{-1}\left(\Omega\right)\right)
represents the source term. Problems of this form arise in various physical and industrial applications, including heat conduction, population dispersion, and polymer thermal diffusivity.

In the past 50 years, space-time finite element methods have emerged as one of the most efficient approaches for solving Problem (
1
) on fully unstructured space-time meshes
Hughes and Hulbert (1988)
;
Neumüller (2013)
;
Bank et al. (2017)
. Unlike the time-stepping schemes or the time-discontinuous Galerkin methods, this approach treats the time variable as an additional spatial variable and the temporal derivative of the solution as a convection term along the time direction. In this way, space and time can be discretized simultaneously, allowing us to enhance the rapid development of parallel computations for tackling complex problems, such as optimal control
Langer et al. (2021)
, electromagnetics
Gangl et al. (2024)
, and moving-subdomains problems
Nguyen et al. (2025)
.

Regarding the error analysis of space-time finite element methods, let us mention some recent works
Steinbach (2015)
;
Langer et al. (2016)
;
Moore (2018)
;
Stevenson and Westerdiep (2021)
. In
Steinbach (2015)
, Steinbach proposed a conforming space-time Galerkin approximation for Problem (
1
) and established an error estimate in a discrete trial space norm. Subsequently, the theoretical analysis of an upwind-stabilized space-time method for the heat equation was studied in
Langer et al. (2016)
;
Moore (2018)
. The authors ended up with a priori error estimates with respect to discrete test space norms. Moreover, utilizing the least square formulation of parabolic initial-boundary value problems, Stevenson and Westerdiep arrived at a stable approximation and a quasi-optimal error estimate in the trial space norm
Stevenson and Westerdiep (2021)
. However, these studies primarily focused on errors measured in specific norms on the trial or test space, lacking results with other miscellaneous norms of higher order as in the literature on classical time discretizations
Thomée (2006)
.

Supplementing the work of Steinbach
Steinbach (2015)
, this paper analyzes the error of the space-time finite element method in four different norms. To improve the convergence order of this method and derive the desired error estimates, we extend the application of duality arguments from conforming approximations of elliptic problems
Nitsche (1968)
to that of Problem (
1
). We obtain higher-order estimates in the
\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
-norm at
t=T
and the
\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
-norm, which is weaker than the norm considered in
Steinbach (2015)
. Moreover, with respect to a norm stronger than that of the trial space, specifically the
\Hs^{1}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
-norm, we establish an optimal error estimate. The convergence order can be further improved when employing a negative-order norm. To the best of our knowledge, these error estimates have not yet been explored in the numerical analysis literature on space-time finite element methods.

The manuscript is structured into four sections. In the
next
section, we present the Petrov-Galerkin variational formulation of Problem (
1
) and its space-time discretization, along with the approximability of the
\Ls^{2}
-orthogonal projection. In Section
3
, we establish and prove the improved error estimates. Subsequently, Section
4
presents numerical experiments that validate the theoretical findings. Finally, we discuss perspectives and potential directions for future research.

2
Variational formulation and its discretization

Throughout this work, we use standard notations for Lebesgue spaces, Sobolev spaces, and Bochner spaces, along with their associated norms. For further details, see
(
Ern and Guermond, 2004
, Appendix B.1)
and
(
Ern and Guermond, 2021a
, Section 64.1)
, for example. The symbol
C>0
denotes a generic constant that is independent of the solution
u
and the mesh size
h
but may depend on the space-time cylinder
\Omega_{T}
and the matrix
\boldsymbol{A}
. Its value may vary across different contexts.

Define
\Vs:=\Ls^{2}\left(\left(0,T\right),\Hs_{0}^{1}\left(\Omega\right)\right)
for convenience and endow this space with an equivalent norm

\left\lVert{y}\right\rVert_{\Vs}^{2}:=\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\boldsymbol{A}\nabla y\right)\cdot\nabla y\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\qquad\forall y\in\Vs.

This equivalence follows from the uniformly positive definiteness of
\boldsymbol{A}
and the Poincaré–Steklov inequality

\left\lVert{y}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\leq C\left\lVert{\nabla y}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\qquad\qquad\forall y\in\Vs,

(2)

see
(
Ern and Guermond, 2004
, Lemma B.61)
. The notation
\Vs^{\prime}
stands for the dual space of
\Vs
. We denote by
\left\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\right\rangle_{\Vs^{\prime}\times\Vs}
the duality pairing between
\Vs^{\prime}
and
\Vs
. Let us introduce the spaces

\Us:=\left\{y\in\Vs\mid\partial_{t}y\in\Vs^{\prime}\right\}\qquad\quad\text{and}\qquad\quad\Us_{0}:=\left\{y\in\Us\mid y(\cdot,0)=0\right\},

equipped with the graph norm

\left\lVert{y}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Us}:=\left\lVert{y}\right\rVert_{\Vs}^{2}+\left\lVert{\partial_{t}y}\right\rVert_{\Vs^{\prime}}^{2}\qquad\qquad\forall y\in\Us.

In
\Us
, the trace operator
y\in\Us\rightarrow y\left(\cdot,t\right)\in\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
is bounded for almost every
t\in\left[0,T\right]
. Moreover, we recall from
(
Ern and Guermond, 2021a
, Lemma 64.40)
the following inequality

\sup_{t\in\left[0,T\right]}\left\lVert{y\left(\cdot,t\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\leq C\left\lVert{y}\right\rVert_{\Us}\qquad\qquad\forall y\in\Us.

(3)

Given a source term
f\in\Vs^{\prime}
, the Petrov-Galerkin variational formulation of Problem (
1
) is stated as follows: Find
u\in\Us_{0}
such that

a\left(u,v\right)=\left\langle{f,v}\right\rangle_{\Vs^{\prime}\times\Vs}\qquad\qquad\forall v\in\Vs,

(4)

where the bilinear form
a:\Us_{0}\times\Vs\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as

a\left(u,v\right):=\left\langle{\partial_{t}u,v}\right\rangle_{\Vs^{\prime}\times\Vs}+\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\boldsymbol{A}\nabla u\right)\cdot\nabla v\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\qquad\forall\left(u,v\right)\in\Us_{0}\times\Vs.

By the Banach-Nečas-Babuška theorem
(
Ern and Guermond, 2004
, Theorem 2.6)
, the well-posedness of this problem follows from the boundedness of
a\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
, the inf-sup condition
(
Steinbach, 2015
, Theorem 2.1)
, as well as a result in
(
Langer et al., 2021
, Section 2)
.

Let
\Omega
be a polyhedron in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \left(n=1,2,3\right)
. Consider a family of quasi-uniform meshes
\left\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\}_{h\in\left(0,h^{\ast}\right)}
of the cylinder
\Omega_{T}=\Omega\times\left(0,T\right)
, with mesh size
h\in\left(0,h^{\ast}\right)
, where
h^{\ast}>0
is a fixed constant. We define the finite element space as

\Us_{h}:=\left\{\varphi_{h}\in\Cs\left(\overline{\Omega_{T}}\right)\mid\varphi_{h\,\mid\,K}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}\left(K\right)\text{ for all }K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\}\cap\Us_{0}.

Here, for
k\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
and
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, let
\mathbb{P}_{k}\left(K\right)
denote the space of polynomials of degree
k
on
K
. The discrete counterpart of Problem (
4
) is given by: Find
u_{h}\in\Us_{h}
such that

a\left(u_{h},v_{h}\right)=\left\langle{f,v_{h}}\right\rangle_{\Vs^{\prime}\times\Vs}\qquad\qquad\forall v_{h}\in\Us_{h}.

(5)

From
(
Steinbach, 2015
, Theorem 3.1)
, it follows that the bilinear form
a\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
satisfies the discrete stability condition

\sup_{v_{h}\in\Us_{h}\setminus\{0\}}\dfrac{a\left(u_{h},v_{h}\right)}{\left\lVert{v_{h}}\right\rVert_{\Vs}}\geq\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\left\lVert{u_{h}}\right\rVert_{h}\qquad\qquad\forall u_{h}\in\Us_{h},

(6)

where
\left\lVert{\cdot}\right\rVert_{h}
is a mesh-dependent norm on
\Us_{0}
, defined as

\left\lVert{y}\right\rVert_{h}^{2}:=\left\lVert{y}\right\rVert_{\Vs}^{2}+\left\lVert{q_{h}\left(y\right)}\right\rVert_{\Vs}^{2}\qquad\qquad\forall y\in\Us_{0},

and
q_{h}\left(y\right)\in\Us_{h}
is the unique solution to the problem

\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left[\boldsymbol{A}\nabla q_{h}\left(y\right)\right]\cdot\nabla\phi_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t=\left\langle{\partial_{t}y,\phi_{h}}\right\rangle_{\Vs^{\prime}\times\Vs}\qquad\qquad\forall\phi_{h}\in\Us_{h}.

Using (
6
) and the discrete Banach-Nečas-Babuška theorem
(
Ern and Guermond, 2004
, Theorem 2.22)
, we conclude the well-posedness of Problem (
5
). Moreover, the following Galerkin orthogonality holds

a\left(u-u_{h},v_{h}\right)=0\qquad\qquad\forall v_{h}\in\Us_{h}.

(7)

We end this section by discussing the approximability of the projection operator
\Pi_{h}:\Ls^{1}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)\to\Us_{h}
, which is defined by

\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\Pi_{h}y-y\right)\varphi_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t=0\qquad\qquad\forall\varphi_{h}\in\Us_{h},

for all
y\in\Ls^{1}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
. The following lemma follows from
(
Ern and Guermond, 2021b
, Remark 12.17, Section 22.5)
.

Lemma 1
.

For all
y\in\Hs^{1}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
, the operator
\Pi_{h}
satisfies the inequality

\left\lVert{\left(y-y_{h}\right)\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\leq C\sqrt{h}\left\lVert{\Ds y}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}.

Moreover, for all
\eta\in\left[1,k+1\right]
and
y\in\Hs^{\eta}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
, the following estimate holds

\left\lVert{y-\Pi_{h}y}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}+h\left\lVert{\Ds\left(y-\Pi_{h}y\right)}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\leq Ch^{\eta}\left\lVert{y}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{\eta}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}.

Here,
\Ds:=\left(\nabla,\partial_{t}\right)^{\top}
denotes the space-time gradient operator.

3
A priori error estimates

Let
u\in\Us_{0}
and
u_{h}\in\Us_{h}
be the solutions to Problems (
4
) and (
5
), respectively. In this section, we estimate the error
u-u_{h}
in various norms. We begin by recalling an error estimate in the norm
\left\lVert{\cdot}\right\rVert_{h}
. For the proof when
k=1
, see
(
Steinbach, 2015
, Theorem 3.3, Corollary 3.4)
. The result for
k>1
follows similarly.

Lemma 2
.

Let
u\in\Us_{0}
and
u_{h}\in\Us_{h}
be the solutions to Problems (
4
) and (
5
), respectively. For
s\in\left[1,k+1\right]
, if
u\in\Hs^{s}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
, then we have

\left\lVert{u-u_{h}}\right\rVert_{h}\leq Ch^{s-1}\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}.

The primary objective of this work is to establish higher-order error estimates beyond the bound in Lemma
2
. To achieve this, we employ duality arguments
Nitsche (1968)
. For any
g\in\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
and
z_{T}\in\Hs^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
, it follows from
(
Steinbach, 2015
, Corollary 2.3)
and
(
Manohar and Sinha, 2020
, Proposition 1)
that the following problem

\begin{cases}-\left\langle{\partial_{t}z,w}\right\rangle_{\Vs^{\prime}\times\Vs}+\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\boldsymbol{A}\nabla z\right)\cdot\nabla w\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t=\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}gw\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\quad\forall w\in\Vs,\\
z\left(\cdot,T\right)=z_{T}\end{cases}

(8)

admits a solution
z\in\Us
, which further satisfies
z\in\Hs^{1}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
along with the stability estimate

\left\lVert{z}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{1}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\leq C\left(\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{z_{T}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\right).

(9)

Next, we choose
w=u-u_{h}\in\Us_{0}
in (
8
), integrate by parts with
z\in\Us
and
u-u_{h}\in\Us_{0}
, and apply (
7
) to get

\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}g\left(u-u_{h}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int\limits_{\Omega}z_{T}\left(u-u_{h}\right)\left(\boldsymbol{x},T\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}=a\left(u-u_{h},z\right)=a\left(u-u_{h},e\right),

where we define
e:=z-\Pi_{h}z
. Integrating by parts once more with
u-u_{h}\in\Us_{0}
and
e\in\Hs^{1}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
, and subsequently applying (
2
), we obtain

\displaystyle a\left(u-u_{h},e\right)=

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(u-u_{h}\right)\left(\boldsymbol{x},T\right)e\left(\boldsymbol{x},T\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}+\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}-\left(u-u_{h}\right)\left(\partial_{t}e\right)+\left[\boldsymbol{A}\nabla\left(u-u_{h}\right)\right]\cdot\nabla e\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{\left(u-u_{h}\right)\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\left\lVert{e\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}+C\left\lVert{\nabla\left(u-u_{h}\right)}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\left\lVert{\Ds e}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}.

Thus, we end up with the following inequality

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}g\left(u-u_{h}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int\limits_{\Omega}z_{T}\left(u-u_{h}\right)\left(\boldsymbol{x},T\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\leq

(10)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{\left(u-u_{h}\right)\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\left\lVert{e\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}+C\left\lVert{\nabla\left(u-u_{h}\right)}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\left\lVert{\Ds e}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)},

for all
g\in\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
and
z_{T}\in\Hs^{1}_{0}\left(\Omega\right)
. This inequality plays a fundamental role in the subsequent error analysis. To bound the right-hand side of (
10
), we introduce the following assumption:

Assumption 1
.

Assume that the solution
z\in\Us
to Problem (
8
) possesses additional regularity, namely
z\in\Hs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
.

This assumption was previously employed in
Langer et al. (2016)
;
Moore (2018)
and validated in
(
Langer et al., 2021
, Remark 3.3)
. We are now ready to establish our main theoretical results. First, we derive the
\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
-norm error estimate at
t=T
. In the following theorem, let
z_{1}
be the solution to Problem (
8
) when
g=0
and
z_{T}=\left\lVert{\left(u-u_{h}\right)\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert^{-1}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\left(u-u_{h}\right)\left(\cdot,T\right)
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
u\in\Us_{0}
and
u_{h}\in\Us_{h}
be the solutions to Problems (
4
) and (
5
), respectively. Suppose that
u\in\Hs^{s}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
for some
s\in\left[1,k+1\right]
and that Assumption
1
is fullfilled. Then, we can find
h^{\ast}>0
such that for all
h\in\left(0,h^{\ast}\right)
, the following estimate holds

\left\lVert{\left(u-u_{h}\right)\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\leq Ch^{s}\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\left\lVert{z_{1}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}.

Proof.

By substituting
g=0
and
z_{T}=\left\lVert{\left(u-u_{h}\right)\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert^{-1}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\left(u-u_{h}\right)\left(\cdot,T\right)
into (
10
), and then invoking Lemmas
1
and
2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\left(u-u_{h}\right)\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\leq

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{\left(u-u_{h}\right)\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\left\lVert{e_{1}\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}+Ch^{s-1}\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}h\left\lVert{z_{1}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)},

where
e_{1}=z_{1}-\Pi_{h}z_{1}
. Next, we apply Lemma
1
once more and use (
9
) to get

\left\lVert{e_{1}\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\leq C\sqrt{h}\left\lVert{\Ds z_{1}}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\leq C\sqrt{h}.

Thus, we arrive at the inequality

\left(1-C\sqrt{h}\right)\left\lVert{\left(u-u_{h}\right)\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\leq Ch^{s}\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\left\lVert{z_{1}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}.

Since
h\in\left(0,h^{\ast}\right)
for some
h^{\ast}>0
, we complete the proof by choosing
h^{\ast}
such that
1-C\sqrt{h}>1-C\sqrt{h^{\ast}}\geq\frac{1}{2}
.
∎

We now derive a higher-order error estimate in the
\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
-norm. From (
2
), we observe that this norm is weaker than
\left\lVert{\cdot}\right\rVert_{\Vs}
, and consequently, weaker than
\left\lVert{\cdot}\right\rVert_{h}
. Such an estimate is essential for the error analysis of optimal control and eigenvalue problems. In the following theorem, we consider Problem (
8
) with
g=\left\lVert{u-u_{h}}\right\rVert^{-1}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\left(u-u_{h}\right)
and
z_{T}=0
, and denote by
z_{2}
its corresponding solution.

Theorem 2
.

Let
u\in\Us_{0}
and
u_{h}\in\Us_{h}
be the solutions to Problems (
4
) and (
5
), respectively. Suppose that
u\in\Hs^{s}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
for some
s\in\left[1,k+1\right]
and that Assumption
1
is satisfied. Then, there exists
h^{\ast}>0
such that for all
h\in\left(0,h^{\ast}\right)
, we have the following estimate

\left\lVert{u-u_{h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\leq Ch^{s}\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\left(\left\lVert{z_{1}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{z_{2}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\right).

Proof.

When
g=\left\lVert{u-u_{h}}\right\rVert^{-1}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\left(u-u_{h}\right)
and
z_{T}=0
, it follows from (
10
) that

\displaystyle\left\lVert{u-u_{h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\leq

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{\left(u-u_{h}\right)\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\left\lVert{\left(\Pi_{h}z_{2}\right)\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}+C\left\lVert{\nabla\left(u-u_{h}\right)}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\left\lVert{\Ds e_{2}}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)},

where
e_{2}=z_{2}-\Pi_{h}z_{2}
. Now, by invoking (
3
), (
2
), the
\Hs^{1}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
-seminorm stability of
\Pi_{h}

(
Ern and Guermond, 2021b
, Proposition 22.21)
, and finally (
9
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\left(\Pi_{h}z_{2}\right)\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert{\Pi_{h}z_{2}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\Us}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert{\nabla\left(\Pi_{h}z_{2}\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\LLs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{\Ds\left(\Pi_{h}z_{2}\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\LLs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert{\Ds\left(\Pi_{h}z_{2}\right)}\right\rVert^{2}_{\LLs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert{\Ds z_{2}}\right\rVert^{2}_{\LLs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\leq C.

Together with Theorem
1
, as well as Lemmas
1
and
2
, we thus derive the desired result.
∎

We proceed with estimating the error in the
\Hs^{1}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
-norm. This norm is stronger than
\left\lVert{\cdot}\right\rVert_{h}
due to the inclusion of
\left\lVert{\partial_{t}\cdot}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}
. Unlike Lemma
2
, this estimate cannot be derived directly from (
6
). Following classical arguments for elliptic problems, we combine Lemmas
1
and
2
with Theorem
2
to establish the desired result.

Theorem 3
.

Let
u\in\Us_{0}
and
u_{h}\in\Us_{h}
be the solutions to Problems (
4
) and (
5
), respectively. Assume that
u\in\Hs^{s}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
for some
s\in\left[1,k+1\right]
and that Assumption
1
holds. Then, we can find
h^{\ast}>0
such that for all
h\in\left(0,h^{\ast}\right)
, their holds the following estimate

\left\lVert{u-u_{h}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{1}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\leq Ch^{s-1}\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\left(1+\left\lVert{z_{1}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{z_{2}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\right).

(11)

Proof.

Using the triangle inequality, we have

\left\lVert{u-u_{h}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{1}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\leq\left\lVert{u-\Pi_{h}u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{1}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{\Pi_{h}u-u_{h}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{1}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}.

On the one hand, it follows from Lemma
1
that

\left\lVert{u-\Pi_{h}u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{1}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\leq Ch^{s-1}\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}.

On the other hand, we apply the global inverse inequality
(
Ern and Guermond, 2004
, Corollary 1.141)
, Lemma
1
, and Theorem
2
to obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert{\Pi_{h}u-u_{h}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{1}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-1}\left\lVert{\Pi_{h}u-u_{h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-1}\left(\left\lVert{\Pi_{h}u-u}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{u-u_{h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{s-1}\left\lVert{u}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{s}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\left(1+\left\lVert{z_{1}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}+\left\lVert{z_{2}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\right),

for all
h\in\left(0,h^{\ast}\right)
, where
h^{\ast}>0
is given. The conclusion follows.
∎

From Lemma
2
and Theorem
3
, we observe that the error estimates in the two norms
\left\lVert{\cdot}\right\rVert_{h}
and
\left\lVert{\cdot}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{1}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}
are formally of the same order. The latter is preferable, as it evaluates the error with respect to
\left\lVert{\partial_{t}\cdot}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}
, thereby making more effective use of the condition
u\in\Hs^{s}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
, where
s\in\left[1,k+1\right]
.

However, it is worth noting that we can only conclude the convergence order from Theorem
3
if there exists an
h
-independent constant
C
such that

\left\lVert{z_{1}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\leq C\qquad\quad\text{and}\qquad\quad\left\lVert{z_{2}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\leq C.

(12)

In that case, we also obtain the convergence order with respect to the two
\Ls^{2}
-norms. Specifically, we have the following result:

Corollary 1
.

Let
u\in\Us_{0}
and
u_{h}\in\Us_{h}
be the solutions to Problems (
4
) and (
5
), respectively. Assume that the conditions of Theorem
3
and (
12
) hold. Then, as
h\to 0
, the following higher-order estimates hold

\left\lVert{\left(u-u_{h}\right)\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}=\mathcal{O}\left(h^{s}\right)\qquad\quad\text{and}\qquad\quad\left\lVert{u-u_{h}}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}=\mathcal{O}\left(h^{s}\right),

along with the optimal estimate

\left\lVert{u-u_{h}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{1}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}=\mathcal{O}\left(h^{s-1}\right).

Finally, we improve the estimate in Theorem
2
by incorporating a negative-order norm. This refined estimate is particularly relevant to the study of superconvergence. On the space
\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
, we define the following norm

\left\lVert{y}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{-r}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}:=\sup_{\xi\in\Hs^{r}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\dfrac{1}{\left\lVert{\xi}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{r}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\xi y\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\qquad\forall y\in\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right),

for any fixed
r\geq 1
. Note that this norm is weaker than
\left\lVert{\cdot}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}
and should not be confused with the norm of the dual space
\left(\Hs_{0}^{r}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)\right)^{\prime}
. To achieve the desired result, we employ duality arguments once again. Let us introduce another assumption:

Assumption 2
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
r\in\left[1,k-1\right]
. For any
g\in\Hs^{r}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
and
z_{T}=0
, suppose that the solution
z\in\Us
to Problem (
8
) has an improved regularity
z\in\Hs^{r+2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
, along with the stability estimate
\left\lVert{z}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{r+2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\leq C\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{r}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
u\in\Us_{0}
and
u_{h}\in\Us_{h}
(with
k\geq 2
) be the solutions to Problems (
4
) and (
5
), respectively. For
r\in\left[1,k-1\right]
, if Assumption
2
holds, then we have the following inequality

\left\lVert{u-u_{h}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{-r}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\leq Ch^{r+1}\left(\left\lVert{\left(u-u_{h}\right)\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}+\left\lVert{u-u_{h}}\right\rVert_{h}\right).

Proof.

First, we choose
g\in\Hs^{r}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)\setminus\left\{0\right\}
and
z_{T}=0
in (
10
) to get

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}g\left(u-u_{h}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert{\left(u-u_{h}\right)\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\left\lVert{e\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}+C\left\lVert{\nabla\left(u-u_{h}\right)}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\left\lVert{\Ds e}\right\rVert_{\LLs^{2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}.

By applying (
3
) and (
2
), we have

\left\lVert{e\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\leq C\left\lVert{e}\right\rVert_{\Us}\leq C\left\lVert{e}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{1}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}.

Together with Lemma
1
, this implies

\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}g\left(u-u_{h}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C\left(\left\lVert{\left(u-u_{h}\right)\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}+\left\lVert{u-u_{h}}\right\rVert_{h}\right)h^{r+1}\left\lVert{z}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{r+2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)},

which leads to

\displaystyle\left\lVert{u-u_{h}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{-r}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{g\in\Hs^{r}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}\dfrac{1}{\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{r}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}}\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}g\left(u-u_{h}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{C\left(\left\lVert{\left(u-u_{h}\right)\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}+\left\lVert{u-u_{h}}\right\rVert_{h}\right)h^{r+1}\left\lVert{z}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{r+2}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}}{\left\lVert{g}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{r}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert{\left(u-u_{h}\right)\left(\cdot,T\right)}\right\rVert_{\Ls^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}+\left\lVert{u-u_{h}}\right\rVert_{h}\right)h^{r+1},

using Assumption
2
. This completes the proof.
∎

By combining Corollary
1
with Lemmas
2
and
3
, we obtain the following
\Hs^{-r}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)
-norm convergence result:

Theorem 4
.

Let
u\in\Us_{0}
and
u_{h}\in\Us_{h}
(with
k\geq 2
) be the solutions to Problems (
4
) and (
5
), respectively. Suppose that the assumptions of Corollary
1
and Lemma
3
hold. Then, as
h\to 0
, we achieve the following convergence order

\left\lVert{u-u_{h}}\right\rVert_{\Hs^{-r}\left(\Omega_{T}\right)}=\mathcal{O}\left(h^{r+s}\right).

5
Conclusions

Using duality arguments, we derived new error estimates for the space-time finite element method. Compared to the trial space norm, employing three weaker norms led to higher-order estimates under appropriate conditions. Conversely, for a stronger norm, the convergence order remained consistent with the mesh-dependent norm in
Steinbach (2015)
. Finally, we provided numerical examples in one and two spatial dimensions to illustrate these findings.

For future work, one possible direction is to investigate superconvergence based on the established negative-order norm error estimate. Another potential direction is to extend the proposed approach to enhance error estimates for other space-time methods, such as isogeometric analysis and the mixed finite element method.

Renormalized and entropy solutions to the general nonlinear parabolic equations in Musielak-Orlicz spaces Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author. Thanks: Keywords : Renormalized solutions, entropy solutions, existence, uniqueness, L 1 L^{1} -data, Musielak-Orlicz spaces Thanks: MSC 2020 : 35K55, 35D99
Ying Li, Chao Zhang ∗

Abstract
. We study the well-posedness of solutions to the general nonlinear parabolic equations with merely integrable data in time-dependent Musielak-Orlicz spaces. With the help of a density argument, we establish the existence and uniqueness of both renormalized and entropy solutions. Moreover, we conclude that the entropy and renormalized solutions for this equation are equivalent. Our results cover a variety of problems, including those with Orlicz growth, variable exponents, and double-phase growth.



1.
Introduction

Suppose that
\Omega
is a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
, and
T
is a positive number. Denote
\Omega_{T}=\Omega\times(0,T]
,
\Sigma=\partial\Omega\times(0,T)
. This manuscript aims to establish the existence and uniqueness of both renormalized and entropy solutions for a general parabolic equation with merely integrable data in time-dependent spaces. Namely, we consider the following parabolic problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\partial_{t}u-\text{div}~{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u)=f(t,x)&\mbox{in}&\Omega_{T}:=(0,T]\times\Omega,\\
u(t,x)=0&\mbox{on}&\Sigma:=(0,T]\times\partial\Omega,\\
u(\cdot,0)=u_{0}(\cdot)&\mbox{in}&\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
f\in L^{1}(\Omega_{T})
and
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. We assume the following hypotheses:

(A1)

{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\xi):(0,T)\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a Carathéodory function (i.e., measurable in
t
and
x
for fixed
\xi
and continuous with respect to
\xi
for fixed
(t,x)
).

(A2)

{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,0)=0
for almost every
(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}
and there exist an
N
-function
M
(see Definition
2.1
) and constants
c_{1}\in(0,1)
, such that for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
we have

c_{1}\left(M(t,x,|\xi|)+M^{*}(t,x,|{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\xi)|)\right)\leq{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\xi)\cdot\xi,

where
M^{*}
is the complementary function of
M
(see Definition
2.2
).

(A3)

\left[{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\xi)-{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\eta)\right]\cdot(\xi-\eta)>0
for all
\xi,\eta
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\xi\neq\eta
and a.e.
(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}
.

Motivated by fluids of nonstandard rheology, we focus on the general form of growth conditions for the leading term of the operator, which makes Musielak-Orlicz spaces a suitable function space for the considered problem. We do not assume any growth condition of doubling type on the function
M
. Instead, we impose a condition that balances the behavior of
M
with respect to its variable, ensuring that smooth functions are modularly dense in the related Sobolev-type space.

Musielak-Orlicz spaces, which generalize Orlicz, variable exponent and double-phase spaces, have received significant attention since the seminal contributions of Marcellini
[

[

[

]
. Subsequent advancements were made by Gossez
[

,

[

Recently, Chlebicka, Gwiazda and Zatorska-Goldstein
[
15
]
showed the existence and uniqueness of renormalized solutions of the following equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\partial_{t}u-\text{div}~\mathcal{A}(t,x,\nabla u)=f(t,x)&\mbox{in}&\Omega_{T},\\
u(t,x)=0&\mbox{on}&\Sigma,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)&\mbox{in}&\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
f\in L^{1}(\Omega_{T})
,
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, and
\mathcal{A}
was assumed to be controlled by a
N
-function. The authors therein employed a delicate time approximation method to achieve smoothness in the time direction. Unlike the proof in
[
15
]
, we do not require separate approximations for time and space variables. We assume that the regularity of the modular function is strong enough to ensure the density of smooth functions in the related Sobolev-type space. In fact, this density can be guaranteed by the balance condition (B) in the isotropic Musielak-Orlicz space, see Lemma
2.6
. Utilizing this density result, we provide direct proof of the existence and uniqueness of renormalized solutions to equation (
1
), demonstrate that the renormalized solution is also an entropy solution, and establish the uniqueness of the entropy solution, thereby showing the equivalence between entropy and renormalized solutions. It is worth noting that when

M(t,x,\xi)=|\xi|^{p(t,x)}
, the existence and uniqueness of entropy solutions for equation (
1
) was discussed by Bulíček and Woźnicki in
[
10
]
. The results of this work extend the results presented in
[
10
,
15
,
35
,
46
]
.

We rely on the density of smooth functions in a relevant function space to study problem (
1
), namely

\textbf{W}(\Omega_{T}):=\bigg\{u\in W^{1,x}_{0}L_{M}(\Omega_{T})\cap L^{2}(\Omega_{T}),\partial_{t}u\in W^{-1,x}L_{M^{*}}(\Omega_{T})+L^{2}(\Omega_{T})\bigg\},

where
W^{1,x}_{0}L_{M}(\Omega_{T})
and
W^{-1,x}L_{M^{*}}(\Omega_{T})
are defined in Section 2.

To ensure the density, one may assume the regularity of
M
. Note that the smooth functions are dense in
\textbf{W}(\Omega_{T})
in the modular topology if the following balance condition holds, see Lemma
2.6
.

Balance condition
\mathsf{(B)}
.
If there exists a function
\varrho:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
which is non-decreasing with respect to each of the variables such that for
(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}
and
(\tau,y)\in\Omega_{T}
,

M(t,x,s)\leq\varrho(|t-\tau|+c|x-y|,s)M(\tau,y,s)\quad\text{with}\quad\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\varrho(\epsilon,\epsilon^{-N})<\infty.

We point out that the Balanced condition is only used to ensure the density of smooth functions in our proof. In addition, throughout the paper, we assume that the
N
-functions
M(t,x,s)
satisfies the following Y-condition.

\mathsf{(Y)}
-condition.
A
N
-function
M
is said to satisfy the
Y
-condition on a segment
[a,b]
of the real line
\mathbb{R}
, if either

(Y1)

: there exist
q_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
1\leq i\leq N
such that
x_{i}\in[a,b]\to M(t,x,s)
is increasing when
x_{i}\geq q_{0}
and decreasing when
x_{i}\leq q_{0}
, or vise versa,

or

(Y2)

: there exists
1\leq i\leq N
such that for all
s\geq 0
, the partial function
x_{i}\in[a,b]\to M(t,x,s)
is monotone on
[a,b]
.

Here
x_{i}
stands for the
i^{th}
component of
x\in\Omega
.

In Musielak-Orlicz spaces, the norm Poincaré inequality is no longer true in general.
We remark that the
\mathsf{(Y)}
-condition is only used as a sufficient condition to obtain the norm Poincaré inequality (see Lemma
2.5
below), which is crucial in the proof of Lemma
2.8
. This condition covers the assumption given by Maeda
[
37
]
to provide the Poincaré integral form for variable exponents. See
[
4
]
for more information about this condition.

In
[
15
]
, the authors used different approximation methods for spatial and time directions, making it unnecessary to assume
\mathsf{(Y)}
-condition to obtain information about the
L_{M}
norm of the solution itself. In our situation, we do not separate space and time approximations. We rely on the density argument of Lemma
2.6
, which shows that for any test function
\phi
, it is necessary that
\phi\in L_{M}(\Omega_{T})
and
\nabla\phi\in L_{M}(\Omega_{T})
. However, when making a priori estimates for the solutions to the approximation problem, we can only obtain gradient information about the approximate solutions due to the growth condition (A2). Thus, we need to impose that
N
-function
M
satisfies
\mathsf{(Y)}
-condition. Nevertheless, we provide a more straightforward approach that reveals the intrinsic connection between renormalized and entropy solutions for such equations. In our future work, we will explore more general conditions that ensure smooth functions are dense in the anisotropic Musielak-Orilcz space. We believe that our method, with slight modifications, will be applicable to anisotropic Musielak-Orlicz spaces.

Before we proceed to define the renormalized and entropy solution to (
1
). We first introduce the truncation operator
T_{k}(r)
as follows:

T_{k}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}r&\text{if}~|r|\leq k,\\
k\frac{r}{|r|}&\text{if}~|r|>k.\end{array}\right.

Its primitive
\Theta_{k}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
defined by

\displaystyle\Theta_{k}(r):=\int^{r}_{0}T_{k}(s)ds=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}&\!\!\!\!\!\!\frac{r^{2}}{2}&\mbox{if}\quad~~|r|\leq k,\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!k|r|-\frac{k^{2}}{2}&\mbox{if}\quad~~|r|>k.\end{array}\right.

It is obvious that
\Theta_{k}(r)\geq 0
and
\Theta_{k}(r)\leq k|r|
.

The definitions of entropy and renormalized solutions for problem (
1
) are as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

A function
u\in C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))
is an entropy solution to problem (
1
) if
u
satisfies the following two conditions:

(E1)

u
is a measurable function satisfying

T_{k}(u)\in W_{0}^{1,x}L_{M}(\Omega_{T})\quad\text{for each}~k>0~\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla T_{k}(u))\in L_{M^{*}}(\Omega_{T});

(E2)

For every
k>0
and every
\phi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}_{T})
with
\phi|_{\Sigma}=0
, the inequality

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{k}(u-\phi)(T)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{k}(u_{0}-\phi(0))\,dx+\int^{T}_{0}\langle\phi_{t},T_{k}(u-\phi)\rangle\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla T_{k}(u-\phi)\,dxdt=\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}fT_{k}(u-\phi)\,dxdt

(1.6)

holds.

Definition 1.2
.

A function
u\in C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))
is a renormalized solution to problem (
1
) if
u
satisfies the following two conditions:

(R1)

u
is a measurable function satisfying

T_{k}(u)\in W_{0}^{1,x}L_{M}(\Omega_{T})\quad\text{for each}~k>0,\qquad{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla T_{k}(u))\in L_{M^{*}}(\Omega_{T})

and

\int_{\{l<|u|<l+1\}}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla u\,dxdt\rightarrow 0\quad\quad\mbox{as}\quad l\rightarrow+\infty;

(R2)

For every
\phi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}_{T})
with
\phi(\cdot,T)=0
and
S\in W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with

S^{\prime}
has compact support, we have

\begin{split}&-\int_{\Omega}S(u_{0})\phi(x,0)\,dx-\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}S(u)\partial_{t}\phi\,dxdt\\
&\quad+\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla\left(S^{\prime}(u)\phi\right)\,dxdt=\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}fS^{\prime}(u)\phi\,dxdt.\end{split}

(1.7)

Now, let us state the main results of this work.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
f\in L^{1}(\Omega_{T})
,
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
,
{\mathcal{A}}
satisfies the conditions
(A1)
–
(A3)
,

-function

is regular enough so that the set of smooth functions is dense in
\textbf{W}(\Omega_{T})
in the modular topology, and

satisfies
\mathsf{(Y)}
-condition. Then there exists a unique renormalized solution to problem (
1
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume that
f\in L^{1}(\Omega_{T})
,
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
,
{\mathcal{A}}
satisfies the conditions
(A1)
–
(A3)
,

-function

is regular enough so that the set of smooth functions is dense in
\textbf{W}(\Omega_{T})
in the modular topology, and

satisfies
\mathsf{(Y)}
-condition. Then the renormalized solution for problem (

) is also an entropy solution to problem (

Remark 1.5
.

The entropy solution obtained in Theorem

).

Example 1.1
.

We will provide examples of time and space dependent Musielak-type spaces that are admissible in our investigation.

(1)

If
M(t,x,s)=|s|^{p}
with
1<p<\infty
, then the space
W^{1,x}L_{M}(\Omega_{T})
is the classical Bochner space
L^{p}(0,T;W^{1,p}(\Omega))
defined as

L^{p}(0,T;W^{1,p}(\Omega)):=\left\{u:(0,T)\to W^{1,p}(\Omega):\int^{T}_{0}\|u(t)\|^{p}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\,dt<+\infty\right\}.

(2)

If

, then we obtain the Orlicz-Sobolev space.

(3)

If

,

,
where

is log-Hölder continuous and

stands for the

component of

.

(4)

If

, with

,
\alpha\in(0,1]
,

\frac{q}{p}\leq 1+\frac{\alpha}{n}
, and

is monotone on a compact subset of the real line

, then we obtain the double-phase spaces. Here
x_{i}
stands for the
i^{th}
component of
x\in\Omega
.

2.
Functional setting and main tools

In this section, we give some functional settings that will be used below. For a more complete discussion on this subject, we refer the readers to
[

,
2
]
.
We begin with the definition of
N
-functions.

Definition 2.1
.

A function
M(\cdot,s):\Omega_{T}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is called an
N
-function if
M(\cdot,s)
is a measurable function for every
s\geq 0
,
M(t,x,\cdot)
is strictly increasing with respect to last variable, and
M(t,x,\cdot)
is a convex function for almost every
(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}
with
M(t,x,0)=0
,
M(t,x,s)\to+\infty
as
s\to+\infty
and

\lim\limits_{s\to 0}\frac{M(t,x,s)}{s}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim\limits_{s\to+\infty}\frac{M(t,x,s)}{s}=+\infty.

Definition 2.2
.

The complementary function
M^{*}
to an
N
-function
M
in the sense of Young defined by

M^{*}(t,x,\xi_{1}):=\displaystyle\sup_{\xi_{2}\geq 0}\left[\xi_{1}\xi_{2}-M(t,x,\xi_{2})\right]

(2.1)

for any
\xi_{1}\geq 0
and a.e.
(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}
.

For an
N
-function, we define the general Musielak-Orlicz class
\mathcal{L}_{M}(\Omega_{T})
as the set of all measurable functions
u(t,x):\Omega_{T}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}M(t,x,|u(t,x)|)\,dxdt<\infty.

The Musielak-Orlicz space
L_{M}(\Omega_{T})
(resp.
E_{M}(\Omega_{T})
) is defined as the set of all measurable functions
u:\Omega_{T}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}M\left(t,x,\frac{|u(t,x)|}{\lambda}\right)\,dxdt<+\infty

for some
\lambda>0
(resp. for all
\lambda>0
). Equipped with Luxemburg norm

\|u\|_{L_{M}(\Omega_{T})}=\inf\bigg\{\lambda>0:\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}M\bigg(t,x,\frac{|u(t,x)|}{\lambda}\bigg)\,dxdt\leq 1\bigg\}.

Then
L_{M}(\Omega_{T})
is a Banach space and
E_{M}(\Omega_{T})
is its closed subset.

An
N
-function
M
is called locally integrable on
\Omega_{T}
, if for any constant number
c>0
and for any compact
\Omega_{T}^{\prime}
of
\Omega_{T}
, the following holds

\int_{\Omega_{T}^{\prime}}M(t,x,c)\,dxdt<+\infty.

We remark here that if an
N
-function
M
satisfies the balanced condition (B), then the function is naturally locally integrable, see
[
3
,
5
]
. It is shown in
[
6
, Lemma 2.1]
that the continuous embedding
L_{M}(\Omega_{T})\hookrightarrow L^{1}(\Omega_{T})
holds if either
M^{*}
is locally integrable, or
M
satisfies
\essinf\limits_{(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}}M(t,x,1)\geq c>0
.

Definition 2.3
.

Suppose that the complementary
N
-function
M^{*}
of
M
is locally integrable on
\Omega_{T}
, we define

\displaystyle W^{1,x}L_{M}(\Omega_{T}):=\{u:\Omega_{T}\to\mathbb{R}:u\in L_{M}(\Omega_{T}),|\nabla u|\in L_{M}(\Omega_{T})\}

(2.2)

and

\displaystyle W^{1,x}E_{M}(\Omega_{T}):=\{u:\Omega_{T}\to\mathbb{R}:u\in E_{M}(\Omega_{T}),|\nabla u|\in E_{M}(\Omega_{T})\}.

(2.3)

We denote
\nabla u
the vector gradient with respect to the space variable. These spaces are normed by
\|u\|_{W^{1,x}L_{M}(\Omega_{T})}:=\|u\|_{L_{M}(\Omega_{T})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L_{M}(\Omega_{T})}
, and then
W^{1,x}L_{M}(\Omega_{T})
is a Banach space.

Let
X
and
Y
be subsets of
L^{1}(\Omega_{T})
not necessarily related by duality. We say
f_{n}\to f
for
\sigma(X,Y)
if

\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}f_{n}g\,dxdt\xrightarrow{n\to+\infty}\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}fg\,dxdt

for all
g\in Y
. If
X=L_{M}(\Omega_{T})
and
Y=E_{M^{\ast}}(\Omega_{T})
, we recover the weak-
\ast
convergence and can also denote
f_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}f
.

We define
W^{1}L_{M}(\Omega)
(resp.
W^{1}E_{M}(\Omega)
) as the set of all measurable function
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, such that for all
|\alpha|\leq 1
, the function
|D^{\alpha}u|
belong to
L_{M}(\Omega)
(resp.
E_{M}(\Omega)
), that is,

\int_{\Omega}M\left(x,\frac{|D^{\alpha}u|}{\lambda}\right)\,dx<+\infty\quad\text{for some $\lambda>0$ (resp. for all $\lambda>0$)}.

Note that if a
N
-function
M
is locally integrable, then the set of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
-functions is contained in
W^{1}E_{M}(\Omega)
. Therefore, the norm closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
-functions in
W^{1}E_{M}(\Omega)
, denoted by
W_{0}^{1}E_{M}(\Omega)
, is well defined. Moreover, if the pair of complementary
N
-functions
(M,M^{*})
are both locally integrable, then the space
W_{0}^{1}L_{M}(\Omega)
, defined as the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
-functions with respect to the weak-* topology
\sigma(L_{M},E_{M^{*}})
, is also well defined.

Definition 2.4
.

Suppose that the complementary
N
-function
M^{*}
of
M
is locally integrable on
\Omega_{T}
, we define

\displaystyle W_{0}^{1,x}L_{M}(\Omega_{T}):=\{u:(0,T)\to W_{0}^{1}L_{M}(\Omega):u\in L_{M}(\Omega_{T}),|\nabla u|\in L_{M}(\Omega_{T})\}

(2.4)

and

\displaystyle W_{0}^{1,x}E_{M}(\Omega_{T}):=\{u:(0,T)\to W_{0}^{1}E_{M}(\Omega):u\in E_{M}(\Omega_{T}),|\nabla u|\in E_{M}(\Omega_{T})\}.

(2.5)

These spaces are equipped with the norm
\|u\|_{W^{1,x}L_{M}(\Omega_{T})}
.

Lemma 2.5

(Theorem 1.1,
[
4
]
)
.

Assume that the pair of complementary
N
-functions
M
and
M^{*}
satisfy both balance condition
(B)
, and
M
satisfies
\mathsf{(Y)}
-condition. Then there exists a constant
C
depending only on
\Omega_{T}
such that for every
u\in W^{1,x}_{0}L_{M}(\Omega_{T})
it holds

\|u\|_{L_{M}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L_{M}(\Omega_{T})}.

From Lemma
2.5
, the two norms
\|\cdot\|_{L_{M}(\Omega_{T})}+\|\nabla\cdot\|_{L_{M}(\Omega_{T})}
and
\|\nabla\cdot\|_{L_{M}(\Omega_{T})}
are equivalent on
W^{1,x}_{0}L_{M}(\Omega_{T})
. In addition, it follows from Definition
2.2
that

\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}|u_{1}u_{2}|\,dxdt\leq 2\|u_{1}\|_{L_{M}(\Omega_{T})}\|u_{2}\|_{L_{M^{*}}(\Omega_{T})}

(2.6)

for all
u_{1}\in L_{M}(\Omega_{T})
and
u_{2}\in L_{M^{*}}(\Omega_{T})
.
We say that a sequence
\{v_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges modularly to
v
in
L_{M}(\Omega_{T})
, if there exists
\lambda>0
such that

\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}M\bigg(t,x,\frac{|v_{n}-v|}{\lambda}\bigg)\,dxdt\to 0\ \ \ \text{as}\ \ \ n\to+\infty.

For the notion of this convergence, we write
v_{n}\xrightarrow{M}v
.

Lemma 2.6

(Theorem 5.2,
[
6
]
)
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{A}}
satisfies the conditions
(A1)
–
(A3)
, and the pair of complementary
N
-functions
M
and
M^{*}
satisfy both balance condition
(B)
. Then, for every
\phi\in\textbf{W}(\Omega_{T})
there exists a sequence
\{\phi_{\delta}\}_{\delta}\subset C_{0}^{\infty}((0,T];C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega))
such that

\begin{split}\phi_{\delta}\xrightarrow{}\phi\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{in}\ \ &L^{2}(\Omega_{T}),\\
\partial_{t}\phi_{\delta}\xrightarrow{M}\partial_{t}\phi\ \ \ \ \text{in}\ \ \ &W^{-1,x}L_{M^{*}}(\Omega_{T})+L^{2}(\Omega_{T}),\\
D^{\alpha}\phi_{\delta}\xrightarrow{M}D^{\alpha}\phi,|\alpha|\leq 1\ \ \ \text{in}\ \ &L_{M}(\Omega_{T}),\\
\end{split}

where

is defined as

\begin{split}W^{-1,x}L_{M^{*}}(\Omega_{T}):&=\left\{u:(0,T)\to W^{-1}L_{M^{*}}(\Omega):u=\tilde{u}-\text{\rm div}U,\right.\\
&\quad\left.~\textrm{with}~\tilde{u}\in L_{M^{*}}(\Omega_{T})~\text{and}~U\in L_{M^{*}}(\Omega_{T})\right\}.\end{split}

The following fact is a consequence of modular topology.

Lemma 2.7

(Lemma 2,

]
)
.

Let

be an

. If

modularly, then

in
\sigma(L_{M},L_{M^{*}})
.

Using a proof strategy analogous to that in

–

. Furthermore, for all

for every

Then, we will present some preliminary lemmas that will be used later.

3.
The proofs of main results

We are now in a position to give the proofs of our main results. Some of the reasoning is based on the ideas developed in
[
13
,
28
,
46
]
.

We first consider the following approximate problems

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\partial_{t}u_{n}-\text{div}~{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})=f_{n}&\mbox{in}&\quad\Omega_{T},\\
u_{n}=0&\mbox{on}&\quad\Sigma,\\
u_{n}(0,x)=u_{0n}&\mbox{in}&\quad\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where the two sequences of functions
\{f_{n}\}\subset C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega_{T})
and
\{u_{0n}\}\subset C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
strongly convergent respectively to
f
in
L^{1}(\Omega_{T})
and to
u_{0}
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\|f_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})},\quad\quad~~~~~\|u_{0n}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

(3.5)

It follows from Lemma
2.8
that there exists a distributional solution
u_{n}\in\textbf{W}(\Omega_{T})
for problem (
3
), such that

\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}u_{n}\phi\,dxdt+\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})\cdot\nabla\phi\,dxdt=\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}f_{n}\phi\,dxdt

(3.6)

for every
\phi\in\textbf{W}(\Omega_{T})
.

Taking the test function as
T_{k}(u_{n})\chi_{(0,\tau)}
with
\tau\in(0,T]
in (
3.6
), we have

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{k}(u_{n})(\tau)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{k}(u_{0n})\,dx\\
&\quad+\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla T_{k}(u_{n}))\cdot\nabla T_{k}(u_{n})\,dxdt=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}f_{n}T_{k}(u_{n})\,dxdt.\end{split}

(3.7)

According to the definition of
\Theta_{k}(r)
and (
3.5
), we deduce

\begin{split}&\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla T_{k}(u_{n}))\cdot\nabla T_{k}(u_{n})\,dxdt+\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{k}(u_{n})(\tau)\,dx\\
&\quad\leq k\left(\|f_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+\|u_{0n}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\right)\leq k\left(\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\right).\end{split}

(3.8)

Recalling condition (A2), we have

\displaystyle c_{1}\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}M(t,x,|\nabla T_{k}(u_{n})|)\,dxdt\leq\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla T_{k}(u_{n}))\cdot\nabla T_{k}(u_{n})\,dxdt\leq Ck

(3.9)

and

\displaystyle c_{1}\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}M^{*}(t,x,|{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla T_{k}(u_{n})|)\,dxdt\leq Ck.

(3.10)

Since
L_{M}(\Omega_{T})\hookrightarrow L^{1}(\Omega_{T})
, we know

\displaystyle\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla T_{k}(u_{n})|\,dxdt\leq C\left(k+1\right),

(3.11)

that is,
T_{k}(u_{n})
is bounded in
L^{1}(0,T;W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega))
.

Choosing
k=1
in the inequality (
3.8
), we find that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{1}(u_{n}(\tau))dx\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

for a.e.
\tau\in(0.T]
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}(\tau)|dx\leq\text{meas}(\Omega)+\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))}\leq C.

(3.12)

Now, we are ready to prove the existence and uniqueness of renormalized solutions.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

(1). Existence of renormalized solutions.

We divide our proof into several steps.

Step 1
. Prove the convergence of
\{u_{n}\}
in
C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))
ans finds its subsequence which is almost everywhere convergent in
\Omega_{T}
.

Due to (
3.6
), we can write the weak form as

\begin{split}&\int^{T}_{0}\langle\partial_{t}(u_{n}-u_{m}),\phi\rangle\,dt+\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}[{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})-{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{m})]\cdot\nabla\phi\,dxdt\\
&\quad=\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}(f_{n}-f_{m})\phi\,dxdt\end{split}

(3.13)

for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\phi\in\textbf{W}(\Omega_{T})
.
It follows from the Fenchel’s Young inequality and (
3.8
) that

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})\cdot\nabla u_{m}|\leq\left(M(t,x,|\nabla u_{n}|)+M^{*}\left(t,x,|{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{m})|\right)\right)\in L^{1}(\Omega_{T}).

Define

\displaystyle\alpha_{n,m}:=\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}|f_{n}-f_{m}|\,dxdt+\int_{\Omega}|u_{0n}-u_{0m}|dx.

(3.14)

Since
\{f_{n}\}
and
\{u_{0n}\}
are convergent in
L^{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n,m\to+\infty}\alpha_{n,m}=0.

Taking
w=T_{1}(u_{n}-u_{m})\chi_{(0,\tau)}
with
\tau\leq T
as a test function in (
3.13
), and discarding the positive term we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{1}(u_{n}-u_{m})(\tau)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{1}(u_{0n}-u_{0m})dx+\|f_{n}-f_{m}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u_{0n}-u_{0m}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|f_{n}-f_{m}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}=\alpha_{n,m}.

Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\{|u_{n}-u_{m}|<1\}}\frac{|u_{n}-u_{m}|^{2}(\tau)}{2}\,dx+\int_{\{|u_{n}-u_{m}|\geq 1\}}\frac{|u_{n}-u_{m}|(\tau)}{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}[\Theta_{1}(u_{n}-u_{m})](\tau)\,dx\leq\alpha_{n,m}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}-u_{m}|(\tau)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{|u_{n}-u_{m}|<1\}}|u_{n}-u_{m}|(\tau)\,dx+\int_{\{|u_{n}-u_{m}|\geq 1\}}|u_{n}-u_{m}|(\tau)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\{|u_{n}-u_{m}|<1\}}|u_{n}-u_{m}|^{2}(\tau)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{meas}(\Omega)^{\frac{1}{2}}+2\alpha_{n,m}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(2\text{meas}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}_{n,m}+2\alpha_{n,m}.

Thus, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|u_{n}-u_{m}\|_{C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))}\rightarrow 0~~~~~\quad\mbox{as}~~n,~m\rightarrow+\infty,

which implies that
\{u_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))
. Then
u_{n}
converges to
u
in
C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))
. We find an a.e. convergent subsequence (still denoted by
\{u_{n}\})
in
\Omega_{T}
such that

\displaystyle u_{n}\rightarrow u~~~\quad\mbox{a.e.~~~in ~~~~~}~~~~~\Omega_{T}\qquad~~~~~~\mbox{as}~~~~~n\rightarrow+\infty.

(3.15)

Step 2.
Show that the sequence
\{\nabla u_{n}\}
converges almost everywhere in
\Omega_{T}
to
\nabla u
(up to a subsequence). Let us first set
\delta>0
and denote

\displaystyle A_{1}:=\{(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}:|\nabla u_{n}|>h\}\cup\{(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}:|\nabla u_{m}|>h\},

\displaystyle A_{2}:=\{(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}:|u_{n}-u_{m}|>1\}

and

\displaystyle A_{3}:=\{(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}:|\nabla u_{n}|\leq h,|\nabla u_{m}|\leq h,|u_{n}-u_{m}|\leq 1,|\nabla u_{n}-\nabla u_{m}|>\delta\},

where
h
will be chosen later. Next, we shall show that
\{\nabla u_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in measure.
It is easy to check that

\displaystyle\{(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}:|\nabla u_{n}-\nabla u_{m}|>\delta\}\subset A_{1}\cup A_{2}\cup A_{3}.

Firstly, we notice that

\displaystyle\{(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}:|\nabla u_{n}|\geq h\}\subset\{(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}:|u_{n}|\geq k\}\cup\{(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}:|\nabla T_{k}(u_{n})|\geq h\}

for all
k>0
.
Thus, using (
3.12
) and (
3.11
), we know that there exist constants
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\mbox{meas}\{(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}:|\nabla u_{n}|\geq h\}\leq\frac{C}{k}+\frac{C(k+1)}{h},

when
h
is large appropriately. By choosing
k=Ch^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\mbox{meas}\{(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}:|\nabla u_{n}|\geq h\}\leq Ch^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. We may let
h=h(\varepsilon)
large enough such that

\displaystyle\mbox{meas}(A_{1})\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\quad\mbox{for~~ all}~n,m>0.

(3.16)

Secondly, by Step 1 we know that
\{u_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in measure. Then there exists
N_{1}(\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\mbox{meas}(A_{2})\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\qquad\mbox{for all}~n,m\geq N_{1}(\varepsilon).

(3.17)

Finally, from condition
(A3)
, we know that there exists a real-valued function
m(h,\delta)>0
such that

\displaystyle[{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla\eta)-{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla\zeta)]\cdot({\nabla\eta-\nabla\zeta})\geq m(h,\delta)>0

for all
\eta,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
|\eta|,|\zeta|\leq h,\delta\leq|\eta-\zeta|
.
By taking
T_{1}(u_{n}-u_{m})
as a test function in (
3.13
) and integrate on
A_{3}
, we obtain

\begin{split}m(h,\delta)\mbox{meas}(A_{3})&\leq\int_{A_{3}}[{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})-{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{m})]\cdot(\nabla u_{n}-\nabla u_{m})\,dxdt\\
&\leq\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}[{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})-{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{m})]\cdot(\nabla u_{n}-\nabla u_{m})\,dxdt\\
&\leq\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}|f_{n}-f_{m}|\,dxdt+\int_{\Omega}|u_{0n}-u_{0m}|dx=\alpha(n,m),\end{split}

which implies that

\displaystyle\mbox{meas}(A_{3})\leq\frac{\alpha(n,m)}{m(h,\delta)}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\qquad\mbox{for all}~n,m\geq N_{2}(\varepsilon,\delta).

(3.18)

Combining the estimates (
3.16
)–(
3.18
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mbox{meas}\{(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}:|\nabla u_{n}-\nabla u_{m}|>\delta\}\leq\varepsilon\qquad\mbox{for all}~~n,m\geq\rm{max}\{N_{1},N_{2}\},

that is
\{\nabla u_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in measure. Therefore, we obtain a subsequence of
\{\nabla u_{n}\}
which is almost everywhere convergent in
\Omega_{T}
. Moreover, a priori estimate (
3.9
) and weak lower semi-continuity of a convex functional give that

\displaystyle T_{k}(u_{n})\rightharpoonup T_{k}(u)~~~~~~~~~\quad\mbox{ weakly ~in}~~~L^{1}(0,T;W^{1,1}_{0}(\Omega)).

(3.19)

Therefore, we deduce from Lemma
2.9
that

\displaystyle\nabla u_{n}\rightarrow\nabla u\qquad\mbox{a.e. in}~\Omega_{T}~\mbox{as}~~~~~n\rightarrow+\infty.

(3.20)

Step 3.
Prove a decay condition for
u_{n}
. In this step, we aim to prove that

\lim\limits_{l\to+\infty}\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\int_{\{l\leq|u_{n}|\leq l+1\}}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})\cdot\nabla u_{n}\,dxdt=0.

(3.21)

Define the function
T_{l,a}(s)=T_{a}(s-T_{l}(s))
as

\displaystyle T_{l,a}(s):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}s-l\rm{sign}(s)&\mbox{if}&~~l\leq|s|<l+a,\\
a\rm{sign}(s)&\mbox{if}&~~l+a\leq|s|,\\
0&\mbox{if}&~~|s|<l.\end{array}\right.

Using
T_{l,a}(u_{n})=T_{a}\left(u_{n}-T_{l}(u_{n})\right)
as a test function in (
3.6
), we find

\begin{split}&\int_{\{|u_{n}|>l\}}\Theta_{a}(u_{n}\mp l)(T)\,dx-\int_{\{|u_{0n}|>l\}}\Theta_{a}(u_{0n}\mp l)\,dx\\
&\quad+\int_{\{l\leq|u_{n}|\leq l+a\}}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})\cdot\nabla u_{n}\,dxdt\\
&\leq\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}f_{n}T_{l,a}(u_{n})\,dxdt,\end{split}

which yields that

\displaystyle\int_{\{l\leq|u_{n}|\leq l+a\}}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})\cdot\nabla u_{n}\,dxdt\leq a(\int_{\{|u_{n}|>l\}}|f_{n}|\,dxdt+\int_{\{|u_{0n}|>l\}}|u_{0n}|\,dx).

(3.22)

Recalling the convergence of
\{u_{n}\}
in
C\left([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega)\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\text{meas}\{(t,x)\in\Omega_{T}:|u_{n}|>l\}=0\quad\quad\mbox{uniformly with respect to}~n.

Therefore, passing to the limit first in
n
then in
l
, we conclude from (
3.22
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{l\to+\infty}\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\{l\leq|u_{n}|\leq l+a\}}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})\cdot\nabla u_{n}\,dxdt=0.

Choosing

, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{l\to+\infty}\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\{l\leq|u_{n}|\leq l+1\}}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})\cdot\nabla u_{n}\,dxdt=0.

(3.23)

Step 4.
Establish the convergence of
{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla T_{k}(u_{n}))\cdot\nabla T_{k}(u_{n})
.

We are going to show that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla T_{k}(u_{n}))\nabla T_{k}(u_{n})\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla T_{k}(u))\nabla T_{k}(u)~~~~~\quad~\quad~~\mbox{strongly in }~~~~~~~L^{1}(\Omega_{T}).

(3.24)

In fact, according to (
3.10
) and condition (A2), we obtain

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}M^{*}(t,x,|{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla T_{k}(u_{n}))|)\,dxdt\leq C.

Therefore, there exists a subsequence of
{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla T_{k}(u_{n})
such that as
n\to+\infty
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla T_{k}(u_{n}))\rightharpoonup{\mathcal{A}}_{k}\quad\quad\text{weakly-$*$~in}~L_{M^{*}}(\Omega_{T}).

(3.25)

Recalling the fact that
\nabla u_{n}\to\nabla u
a.e. in
\Omega_{T}
and
{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla T_{k}(u_{n}))
is continuous with respect to
\nabla T_{k}(u_{n})
, we deduced that

{\mathcal{A}}_{k}={\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla T_{k}(u)).

To prove (
3.24
), we first show

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla T_{k}(u_{n}))\cdot\nabla T_{k}(u_{n})\,dxdt=\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla T_{k}(u))\cdot\nabla T_{k}(u)\,dxdt.

Since
T_{k}(u)\in\textbf{W}(\Omega_{T})
, it follows from Lemma
2.6
that there exists a sequence
\{T_{k}(u)\}_{\delta}\subset C_{0}^{\infty}((0,T];C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega))
such that

\begin{split}\{T_{k}(u)\}_{\delta}\xrightarrow{M}T_{k}(u)&\ \ \ \text{in}\ \ L_{M}(\Omega_{T}),\\
\nabla\{T_{k}(u_{n})\}_{\delta}\xrightarrow{M}\nabla T_{k}(u)&\ \ \ \text{in}\ \ L_{M}(\Omega_{T}),\\
\partial_{t}\{T_{k}(u)\}_{\delta}\xrightarrow{M}\partial_{t}T_{k}(u)&\ \ \ \text{in}\ \ W^{-1,x}L_{M^{*}}(\Omega_{T})+L^{2}(\Omega_{T}).\end{split}

Define
\psi_{l}(r)=\min\{(l+1-|r|)^{+},1\}
. Taking
\psi_{l}(u_{n})\{T_{k}(u)\}_{\delta}\chi_{(0,\tau)}
as a test function for problem (
3
), we have

\begin{split}&\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}u_{n}\psi_{l}(u_{n})(T_{k}(u))_{\delta}\,dxdt+\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,u_{n})\cdot\nabla\left(\psi_{l}(u_{n})(T_{k}(u))_{\delta}\right)\,dxdt\\
&\quad=\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}f_{n}\psi_{l}(u_{n})(T_{k}(u))_{\delta}\,dxdt.\end{split}

(3.26)

For the first term on the left-hand side of equation (
3.26
), recalling the fact that
u_{n}\to u
a.e in
\Omega_{T}
as
n\to+\infty
, we deduced from Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem that

\begin{split}&\lim\limits_{\delta\to 0}\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}u_{n}\psi_{l}(u_{n})(T_{k}(u))_{\delta}\,dxdt\\
&=\lim\limits_{\delta\to 0}\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}\int^{u_{n}}_{0}\psi_{l}(r)\,dr(T_{k}(u))_{\delta}\,dxdt\\
&=\lim\limits_{\delta\to 0}\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\bigg[\int_{\Omega}\int^{u_{n}}_{0}\psi_{l}(r)\,dr(T_{k}(u))_{\delta}\,dx\bigg|^{\tau}_{0}-\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}\int^{u_{n}}_{0}\psi_{l}(r)\,dr\partial_{t}(T_{k}(u))_{\delta}\,dxdt\bigg]\\
&=\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}u\psi_{l}(u)T_{k}(u)\,dxdt.\end{split}

Taking

, it is obvious that

\lim\limits_{l\to+\infty}\lim\limits_{\delta\to 0}\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}u_{n}\psi_{l}(u_{n})(T_{k}(u))_{\delta}\,dxdt=\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}uT_{k}(u)\,dxdt.

(3.27)

For the second term on the left-hand side of equation (

), we know that

\begin{split}&\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})\cdot\nabla\left(\psi_{l}(u_{n})(T_{k}(u))_{\delta}\right)\,dxdt\\
&\quad=\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})\cdot\nabla T_{l+1}(u_{n})\psi_{l}^{\prime}(u_{n})(T_{k}(u))_{\delta}\,dxdt\\
&\quad\quad+\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})\cdot\nabla(T_{k}(u))_{\delta}\psi_{l}(u_{n})\,dxdt\\
&\quad:=Z_{1}+Z_{2}.\end{split}

For
Z_{1}
, it follows from (
3.23
) that

\begin{split}&\lim\limits_{l\to+\infty}\lim\limits_{\delta\to 0}\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})\cdot\nabla T_{l+1}(u_{n})\psi_{l}^{\prime}(u_{n})(T_{k}(u))_{\delta}\,dxdt\\
&\quad\leq\lim\limits_{l\to+\infty}\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}C\int_{\{l\leq|u_{n}|\leq l+1\}}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})\cdot\nabla T_{l+1}(u_{n})\,dxdt\to 0.\end{split}

For
Z_{2}
, we deduced from (
3.25
) that

{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla T_{l+1}(u_{n}))\psi_{l}(u_{n})\rightharpoonup{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,T_{l+1}(\nabla u))\psi_{l}(u)~\text{weakly in}~L^{1}(\Omega_{T})~\text{as}~n\to+\infty,

(3.28)

which gives that

\begin{split}&\lim\limits_{l\to+\infty}\lim\limits_{\delta\to 0}\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})\cdot\nabla(T_{k}(u))_{\delta}\psi_{l}(u_{n})\,dxdt\\

The limit as

Therefore, we have

For the term on the right-hand side of equation (

(3.30)

Thus, it follows from (

(3.31)

In addition, testing the approximate problem (

Combining with (

(2). Uniqueness of renormalized solutions.

Let
u
and
v
be two renormalized solutions to problem (
1
). For
\sigma>0
, define the function
S_{\sigma}
as follows:

\displaystyle S_{\sigma}(r):=\begin{cases}r&\text{if }|r|<\sigma,\\
\left(\sigma+\frac{1}{2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(r-(\sigma+1)\right)^{2}&\text{if }\sigma\leq r\leq\sigma+1,\\
-\left(\sigma+\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(r+(\sigma+1)\right)^{2}&\text{if }-\sigma-1\leq r\leq-\sigma,\\
\left(\sigma+\frac{1}{2}\right)&\text{if }r>\sigma+1,\\
-\left(\sigma+\frac{1}{2}\right)&\text{if }r<-\sigma-1.\end{cases}

It is obvious that

\displaystyle S_{\sigma}^{\prime}(r)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }|r|<\sigma,\\
\sigma+1-|r|&\text{if }\sigma\leq|r|\leq\sigma+1,\\
0&\text{if }|r|>\sigma+1,\end{cases}

S_{\sigma}\in W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
\text{supp}\,S_{\sigma}^{\prime}\subset[-\sigma-1,\sigma+1]
and
\text{supp}\,S_{\sigma}^{\prime\prime}\subset[\sigma,\sigma+1]\cup[-\sigma-1,-\sigma]
.

We take
S=S_{\sigma}
to obtain

\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial S_{\sigma}(u)}{\partial t}\phi\,dxdt+\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla(S^{\prime}(u)\phi)\,dxdt=\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}fS^{\prime}_{\sigma}(u)\phi\,dxdt

(3.37)

and

\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial S_{\sigma}(v)}{\partial t}\phi\,dxdt+\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla v)\cdot\nabla(S^{\prime}(v)\phi)\,dxdt=\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}fS^{\prime}_{\sigma}(v)\phi\,dxdt.

(3.38)

For fixed
k>0
, taking
\phi=T_{k}(S_{\sigma}(u)-S_{\sigma}(v))
and subtracting the two above equations, we deduce that

\begin{split}&\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\partial S_{\sigma}(u)}{\partial t}-\frac{\partial S_{\sigma}(v)}{\partial t}\right)T_{k}(S_{\sigma}(u)-S_{\sigma}(v))\,dxdt\\
&\quad+\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla uS^{\prime\prime}(u)T_{k}(S_{\sigma}(u)-S_{\sigma}(v))\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla v)\cdot\nabla vS^{\prime\prime}(v)T_{k}(S_{\sigma}(u)-S_{\sigma}(v))\,dxdt\\
&\quad+\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}S^{\prime}(u){\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla T_{k}(S_{\sigma}(u)-S_{\sigma}(v))\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-S^{\prime}(v){\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla v)\cdot\nabla T_{k}(S_{\sigma}(u)-S_{\sigma}(v))\,dxdt\\
&=\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}(fS^{\prime}_{\sigma}(u)-fS^{\prime}_{\sigma}(v))T_{k}(S_{\sigma}(u)-S_{\sigma}(v))\,dxdt.\end{split}

We set

\displaystyle J_{0}:=\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\partial S_{\sigma}(u)}{\partial t}-\frac{\partial S_{\sigma}(v)}{\partial t}\right)T_{k}(S_{\sigma}(u)-S_{\sigma}(v))\,dxdt,

\displaystyle J_{1}:=\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla uS_{\sigma}^{\prime\prime}(u)T_{k}(S_{\sigma}(u)-S_{\sigma}(v))

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla v)\cdot\nabla vS_{\sigma}^{\prime\prime}(v)T_{k}(S_{\sigma}(u)-S_{\sigma}(v))\,dxdt,

\displaystyle J_{2}:=\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}S_{\sigma}^{\prime}(u){\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla T_{k}(S_{\sigma}(u)-S_{\sigma}(v))

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-S_{\sigma}^{\prime}(v){\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla v)\cdot\nabla T_{k}(S_{\sigma}(u)-S_{\sigma}(v))\,dxdt,

\displaystyle J_{3}:=\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}(fS^{\prime}_{\sigma}(u)-fS^{\prime}_{\sigma}(v))T_{k}(S_{\sigma}(u)-S_{\sigma}(v))\,dxdt.

Thus, we have

Next, we shall estimate

one by one.

Estimates of
J_{0}
.
Due to the same initial condition for

and

Estimates of

According to the definition of renormalized solutions, we have

) to conclude

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{k}(u-\phi)(T)dx-\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{k}(u_{0}-\phi(0))dx+\int^{T}_{0}\langle\partial_{t}\phi,T_{k}(u-\phi)\rangle\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla T_{k}(u-\phi)\,dxdt=\int^{T}_{0}\int_{\Omega}fT_{k}(u-\phi)\,dxdt

(3.44)

for all
k>0
and
\phi\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}_{T})
with
\phi|_{\Sigma}=0
. Hence, our solution
u
satisfies condition
(E2)
. Since
u_{n}
is the distributional solution of problem (
3
), then its limit
u
satisfies condition
(E1)
naturally. This completes the proof.

We have established the existence of the entropy solution. Now, we further show its uniqueness.

(2). Uniqueness of entropy solutions.

Suppose that
v
is another entropy solution of problem (
1
), we will show that
u=v
a.e. in
\Omega_{T}
. For
\sigma>0,0<\varepsilon\leq 1
, define the function
S_{\sigma,\varepsilon}
in
W^{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
by

\displaystyle S_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(r):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}r&\mbox{if}&|r|\leq\sigma,\\
\left(\sigma+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)-\frac{r}{|r|}\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\left(r-\frac{r}{|r|}(\sigma+\varepsilon)\right)^{2}&\mbox{if}&\sigma<|r|<\sigma+\varepsilon,\\
\frac{r}{|r|}\left(\sigma+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)&\mbox{if}&|r|\geq\sigma+\varepsilon.\end{array}\right.

Clearly,

\displaystyle S^{\prime}_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&\mbox{if}&|r|\leq\sigma,\\
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left(\sigma+\varepsilon-|r|\right)&\mbox{if}&\sigma<|r|<\sigma+\varepsilon,\\
0&\mbox{if}&|r|\geq\sigma+\varepsilon.\end{array}\right.

Choosing
\phi=S_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})\chi_{(0,\tau)}
as a test function in (
1.6
) for entropy solution
v
, we have

\displaystyle H_{0}+H_{1}+H_{2}=H_{3},

(3.45)

where

\displaystyle H_{0}:=\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{k}(v-S_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n}))(\tau)dx-\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{k}(u_{0}-S_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{0n}))\,dx,

\displaystyle H_{1}:=\int^{\tau}_{0}\langle\partial_{t}u_{n},S^{\prime}_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})T_{k}\left(v-S_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})\right)\rangle\,dt,

\displaystyle H_{2}:=\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla v)\cdot\nabla T_{k}\left(v-S_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})\right)\,dxdt,

\displaystyle H_{3}:=\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}fT_{k}\left(v-S_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})\right)\,dxdt.

Taking
S^{\prime}_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})T_{k}\left(v-S_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})\right)\chi_{(0,\tau)}
as a test function for problem (
3
) we get

\displaystyle H_{1}=\int^{\tau}_{0}\langle\partial_{t}u_{n},S^{\prime}_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})T_{k}(v-S_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n}))\rangle\,dt

\displaystyle\quad=\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}f_{n}S^{\prime}_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})T_{k}\left(v-S_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})\right)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}S^{\prime\prime}_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})T_{k}\left(v-S_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})\right){\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})\cdot\nabla u_{n}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}S^{\prime}_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n}){\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})\cdot\nabla T_{k}\left(v-S_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})\right)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad=:H_{11}+H_{12}+H_{13}.

(3.46)

Combining with (
3.45
) and (
3.46
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{k}\left(v-S_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})\right)(\tau)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{k}\left(u_{0}-S_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{0n})\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\quad-\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}S^{\prime}_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n}){\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})\cdot\nabla T_{k}\left(v-S_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})\right)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla v)\cdot\nabla T_{k}\left(v-S_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})\right)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}fT_{k}\left(v-S_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})\right)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad-\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}f_{n}S^{\prime}_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})T_{k}\left(v-S_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})\right)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}S^{\prime\prime}_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})T_{k}\left(v-S_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})\right){\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})\cdot\nabla u_{n}\,dxdt\right|.

That is,

\displaystyle H_{0}+H_{13}+H_{2}\leq H_{3}-H_{11}+|H_{12}|.

(3.47)

We will pass to the limits as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
,
n\to+\infty
and
\sigma\to+\infty
successively.

We begin with
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. Since
\left|\Theta_{k}\left(v-S_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})\right)(\tau)\right|\leq k\left(|v|+|T_{\sigma+1}(u_{n})|\right)(\tau)
,
S^{\prime}_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(r)\leq T^{\prime}_{\sigma+1}(r)
and
\left|\nabla T_{k}\left(v-S_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})\right)\right|\leq|\nabla T_{\sigma+k+1}(v)|+|\nabla T_{\sigma+1}(u_{n})|
, the three terms of the left-hand side and the two terms of the right-hand side in (
3.47
) pass to the limit for
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem. Now we estimate
|H_{12}|
. Let
R_{\sigma,\varepsilon}
be an even function such that
R_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(r)=r-S_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(r)
for
r\geq 0
. Then we choose
R^{\prime}_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})\chi_{(0,\tau)}
as a test function in (
3
) to have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}R_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})(\tau)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}R_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{0n})\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}R^{\prime\prime}_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n}){\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})\cdot\nabla u_{n}\,dxdt=\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}f_{n}R^{\prime}_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})\,dxdt.

Since
R_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(r)\geq 0
,
R_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(r)\leq|r|
on the set
\{|r|>\sigma\}
and
|S^{\prime\prime}_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(r)|=R^{\prime\prime}_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(r)
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}|S^{\prime\prime}_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n})|{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})\cdot\nabla u_{n}\,dxdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}R^{\prime\prime}_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(u_{n}){\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})\cdot\nabla u_{n}\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\{|u_{n}|>\sigma\}}|f_{n}|\,dxdt+\int_{\{|u_{0n}|>\sigma\}}|u_{0n}|\,dx.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|H_{12}|\leq k\left(\int_{\{|u_{n}|>\sigma\}}|f_{n}|\,dxdt+\int_{\{|u_{0n}|>\sigma\}}|u_{0n}|dx\right).

Recalling that
{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,0)=0
,
T^{\prime}_{\sigma}(u_{n}){\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u_{n})={\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla T_{\sigma}(u_{n}))
, and letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
in (
3.47
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{k}\left(v-T_{\sigma}(u_{n})\right)(\tau)dx-\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{k}\left(u_{0}-T_{\sigma}(u_{0n})\right)dx

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}\left({\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla v)-{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla T_{\sigma}(u_{n}))\right)\cdot\nabla T_{k}(v-T_{\sigma}(u_{n}))\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}\left(f-f_{n}T^{\prime}_{\sigma}(u_{n})\right)T_{k}(v-T_{\sigma}(u_{n}))\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad\quad+k\left(\int_{\{|u_{n}|>\sigma\}}|f_{n}|\,dxdt+\int_{\{|u_{0n}|>\sigma\}}|u_{0n}|dx\right).

Next, we take
n\rightarrow+\infty
. Due to the fact that
\nabla T_{\sigma}(u_{n})\rightarrow\nabla T_{\sigma}(u)
a.e. in
\Omega_{T}
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
, Fatou’s lemma and the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, sending
n\rightarrow+\infty
in the above inequality we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{k}\left(v-T_{\sigma}(u)\right)(\tau)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{k}\left(u_{0}-T_{\sigma}(u_{0})\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}\left({\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla v)-{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla T_{\sigma}(u))\right)\cdot\nabla T_{k}(v-T_{\sigma}(u))\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}f\left(1-T^{\prime}_{\sigma}(u)\right)T_{k}(v-T_{\sigma}(u))\,dxdt

\displaystyle\quad\quad+k\left(\int_{\{|u|>\sigma\}}|f|\,dxdt+\int_{\{|u_{0}|>\sigma\}}|u_{0}|dx\right).

Now we let
\sigma\to+\infty
. Since

\displaystyle\left|\Theta_{k}(v-T_{\sigma}(u))(\tau)\right|\leq k\left(|v(\tau)|+|u(\tau)|\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\left|\Theta_{k}\left(u_{0}-T_{\sigma}(u_{0})\right)\right|\leq k|u_{0}|,

by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{k}(v-T_{\sigma}(u))(\tau)\,dx\rightarrow\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{k}(v-u)(\tau)\,dx,\quad\quad\quad\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{k}\left(u_{0}-T_{\sigma}(u_{0})\right)dx\rightarrow 0

and

\displaystyle\int_{\{|u|>\sigma\}}|f|\,dxdt+\int_{\{|u_{0}|>\sigma\}}|u_{0}|\,dx\rightarrow 0.

Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{k}(v-u)(\tau)dx+\int^{\tau}_{0}\int_{\Omega}\left({\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla v)-{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u)\right)\cdot\nabla T_{k}(v-u)\,dxdt\leq 0,

which implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{k}(v-u)(\tau)dx+\int_{\{|u|\leq\frac{k}{2},|v|\leq\frac{k}{2}\}}\left({\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla v)-{\mathcal{A}}(t,x,\nabla u)\right)\cdot\nabla(v-u)\,dxdt\leq 0.

Using the nonnegativity of the two terms in the above inequality, we conclude that
u=v
a.e. in
\Omega_{T}
. Therefore, we obtain the uniqueness of entropy solutions.
\Box.

Patient-Zero: A Unified Framework for Real-Record-Free Patient Agent Generation
Yunghwei Lai, Weizhi Ma, Yang Liu

Abstract
Synthetic data generation using large language models (LLMs) has emerged as a promising solution across various domains, particularly in medical field, to mitigate data collection challenges. However, existing studies mainly utilize LLMs to rewrite and complete existing medical records, where the limitations in data privacy, accuracy, and diversity still exist, and additionally lack the ability to interact like real patients. To address these issues, we propose a realistic patient generation framework, Patient-Zero , which requires no real medical records. Patient-Zero first introduces a medically-aligned multi-step generation architecture, which builds comprehensive patient records through hierarchical medical knowledge injection without real medical records. Then, to optimize the virtual patient’s interaction abilities with humans, Patient-Zero designs a dynamic updating mechanism to improve the consistency and conversational performance. Our framework enables the generation of contextually diverse patient records while maintaining strict medical coherence, supported by adaptive dialogue strategies and real-time clinical plausibility verification. Experimental results demonstrate that our model achieves good performance in accuracy, diversity, and consistency. After training with our generated virtual patients, existing models show significant improvements on the MedQA dataset.

Introduction

Figure 1:
The proposed framework consists of two main modules: the Patient Record Construction module and the Patient Agent Interaction Simulation module. Patient records are generated through a multi-step process with atomic fact decomposition, using a knowledge base and stored in patient agent memory. In the Patient Agent Interaction Simulation module, these patient records are utilized to simulate dialogues between patient agents and doctors. Patient responses are evaluated using a triplet evaluation mechanism, where consistent responses are replied directly, neutral statements dynamically update the patient agent memory, and contradictory responses trigger regeneration.

Large Language Models (LLMs) have shown powerful content generation capabilities in various challenging language tasks. Beyond using them to answer questions, recent studies also use them to generate synthetic data across various domains for various purposes, e.g., model training and data construction. The high-quality data generated by LLMs can alleviate the time, labor, and financial costs associated with collecting real-world data. Due to privacy issues in the medical domain, using LLMs to generate medical-related data has become a promising direction.

Existing patient record generation approaches employ diverse methodologies to balance privacy protection with clinical utility.
Synthetic EHR generation is formulated as a probabilistic graphical model framework to generate high-quality medical reports
Sun et al. 2023
.
Retrieval-Reasoning few-shot framework leverages LLMs to generate synthetic clinical trial data by retrieving relevant trials
Xu et al. 2025
. This approach generates new trials that mimic real-world data while preserving patient privacy.
Historical diagnostic information is used to guide LLMs in generating accurate radiology reports that reflect disease progression over time
Liu et al. 2024
.
Reinforcement learning
Das et al. 2024
and text-to-tabular
Tornqvist et al. 2024
approaches are used to generate synthetic clinical trial data, improving the utility for downstream tasks and overcoming privacy concerns in clinical data sharing.

However, existing studies on medical data generation still rely on real medical records, which may cause the data privacy, accuracy, and diversity issues.
Firstly, existing studies have utilized real medical records as references in record generation. Although prompts may be designed to exclude personal patient information during the generation process, the uniqueness of the generated content may still pose a risk of privacy leakage and may also lead to false positives, meaning the generated data may not always fully adhere to medical principles
Sun et al. 2023
. Secondly, the diversity and quality of synthetic medical data remain insufficient to fully capture the complexity and richness of real-world data, leading to biases or inaccuracies
Huang et al. 2024
.
Furthermore, how to enable synthetic patient records to possess conversation abilities like real patients remains a challenge.

To address these challenges, we propose a medically-aligned multi-step generation framework, which is named
Patient-Zero
due to no real-world medical records is used in patient generation and playing.
Patient-Zero
improves the quality of synthetic patient records and integrates them into realistic patient agent interactions using diverse conversational styles and real-time triplet evaluation mechanisms. This approach mimics the real-world hospital workflow, ensuring that generated data aligns with the step-by-step processes followed during patient diagnosis and treatment.

To be more specific, by incorporating domain-specific knowledge, epidemiological considerations, and standardized examination protocols,
Patient-Zero
not only improves the diagnostic accuracy of generated data but also ensures alignment with established medical theories and practices.
The multi-step process generates an outline of core medical concepts, followed by patient details such as symptoms and demographics, and completes with detailed examination results aligned with medical guidelines.
The triplet evaluation mechanism was designed to ensure that the patient agent’s responses remain consistent with its underlying patient record, dynamically updating the patient agent memory or regenerating responses when contradictions arise.
We synthesized tens of thousands of medical records across multiple clinical departments and nearly a hundred diseases. Evaluation results indicate that the synthetic medical records exhibit high accuracy and consistency. In external validation, the generated generated medical records has been shown to enhance the model’s medical decision-making ability, demonstrating promising application potential.

In summary, our key contributions include:

•

A multi-step generation framework aligns with medical principles is proposed in
Patient-Zero
to improve the accuracy and diversity of synthetic patient records.

•

Patient-Zero
designed a patient-agent interaction framework with real-time agent memory management, which enable the virtual patients provide consistent conversation ability.

•

Comprehensive internal and external experiments demonstrated the prior performance of the generated medical records on accuracy, diversity, and consistency.

Medically-Aligned Multi-step Generation

Step 1: Disease Outline Selection.

Before generating a specific synthetic medical record, we first select a target disease through a two-step process: choosing a medical department, then selecting a specific disease.
To avoid inconsistencies from varied formats and irrelevant information in direct disease knowledge, we human-verify and reconstruct the disease knowledge into an outline for generating diverse patient records.
As shown in Figure
2
Step 1, the outline includes demographic and disease-specific contexts.

Step 2: Basic Information Generation.

Directly prompting LLMs for complete records can result in overly long, inconsistent, and less diverse outputs. To address this, we use a multi-step approach based on medical logic, first generating basic patient inforamtion, and then clinical exam results.

Patient-Zero
generates patient’s basic information by a one-shot prompt with disease outline, as indicated in Figure
2
. Symptom trajectories are modeled via temporal probability distributions, capturing onset patterns, exacerbation triggers, and severity dynamics. These fundamental data are designed to reflect diverse demographics, ensuring that the synthetic patient records represent a wide range of patient populations. The full features can be seen in Appendix
A

Step 3: Detailed Information Generation.

In the final phase, clinical examination results are generated based on the patient’s symptoms and disease outline. A carefully selected one-shot prompt guides the generation of complex medical data while maintaining diversity. Each output is cross-referenced with prior information to ensure coherence throughout the synthetic patient record.
After the mentioned steps, we can obtain a full synthetic patient record, as shown in Appendix

3.2
Patient Agent Interaction Simulation

Generated medical records can serve multiple purposes. We propose designing them to enhance the simulation of real patient interactions, enabling conversations with doctors or other roles (see Appendix
C
). Thus, we introduce a novel patient agent interaction strategy with virtual memory.

The patient agent interaction framework simulates dynamic clinical dialogues through context-aware exchanges. The generated patient record serves as basic information for the patient role, stored as private memory. We propose dynamically updating the patient agent’s memory during conversations, integrating new, non-conflicting information while maintaining alignment with clinical details.

Patient Agent Memory Construction.

Inspired by MediQ
Li et al. 2024b
, we decompose the complete patient record into atomic statements stored in personal memory. These atomic statements are the smallest information units extracted from the complex record, standardizing the information into a simple, unified structure. This enables LLMs to process and reason over complex medical data more accurately and efficiently, as shown in Figure
3
. The modular structure of atomic statements also allows seamless integration of new data into the patient agent memory.

To replicate human patient variability, our framework includes six conversational styles: plain, upset, verbose, reserved, tangent, and pleasing. These styles follow the taxonomy in PATIENT-
\psi

Wang et al. 2024
, allowing the generated patient agent to exhibit diverse communication behaviors. For instance, reserved patients give brief answers, while verbose ones provide excessive details. This variety enhances simulation depth, helping medical trainees adapt to different patient behaviors and better prepare for real clinical encounters
Lizée et al. 2024
;
Yu et al. 2024
;
Du et al. 2024
.

Triplets Evaluation for Memory Updating.

Our framework further implements a triplet evaluation mechanism to ensure the accuracy and coherence of the patient agent’s responses. The system cross-references each response against the patient’s record to identify and address inconsistencies. Contradictions trigger iterative regeneration until alignment is achieved. Furthermore, the system dynamically updates patient records by integrating new clinical information while ensuring conflict resolution. This adaptive memory mechanism mirrors the evolution of real clinical dialogues while preventing data corruption.

4.5
Case Study: Human Expert Evaluation

To evaluate the clinical realism of our synthetic patient data, we conducted human annotation to verify the quality of the generated medical cases. Physicians at a Class III (tertiary-level) hospital in Beijing China, which works in General Surgery department, to review a randomly selected subset of the synthetic patient records. The feedback from the human experts indicated that the synthetic patient records largely met expectations for clinical realism.
However, they observed that some records occasionally lacked minor but important elements of a comprehensive clinical evaluation, such as detailed physical examination findings and conclusions.
This feedback is valuable for future refinements of our framework to enhance the depth of clinical detail in the synthetic data.

5
Conclusion and Future Work

Our study presents a multi-step generation framework that systematically enhances synthetic patient records through three core innovations: 1) a multi-step outline generation process guided by medical guidelines, 2) a real-time triplet evaluation mechanism with a memory update process for consistency checks, and 3) an adaptive conversational agent design supporting diverse interaction styles. Evaluations across medical specialties demonstrate measurable improvements in data accuracy, diversity, and MedQA accuracy compared to baseline methods. The system’s capacity to simulate context-aware dialogues provides new potentials for the usage of synthetic data in medical AI tasks.

Our future research will focus on the integration of multimodal data to enable comprehensive medical record generation. Optimizing distribution-aware generation to help maintain statistical alignment between synthetic data and real-world distributions and demographic variations.

Limitations

Clinical Validation Gap

A limitation of this framework lies in its reliance on synthetic validation metrics rather than real-world clinical trials. While the system demonstrates improved MedQA accuracy and interaction plausibility in controlled evaluations, its performance remains unproven in organic healthcare settings. This raises concerns about external validity, particularly regarding the system’s ability to handle atypical symptom presentations, cultural communication differences, and complex medical complications that often challenge human clinicians.

Distribution Misalignment

The generated data exhibits statistical deviations from real-world clinical distributions due to limitations in modeling regional epidemiological variations and disparities in healthcare access. The framework does not account for factors such as seasonal disease patterns and age-dependent clinical manifestations in disease prevalence and symptom presentation. These limitations may lead to an artificially homogenized synthetic patient cohort, potentially reducing the utility of the data for training models intended for deployment in diverse clinical environments.

Unable to Generate Multi-modal Patient Data

Currently, our model can only generate textual features of patients, including imaging examination results, which are limited to textual conclusions without corresponding image content. Although these data can still be used for training doctor agents and other applications, they lack the completeness of real medical records. In the future, we plan to enhance and refine the relevant modules to improve the quality of synthetic patient data.

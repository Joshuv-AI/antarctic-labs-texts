1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the space-time nonlocal equation

(1.1)

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}u=u^{\frac{n+2s}{n-2s}}\quad\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T).

For
0<s<1
, the fractional heat operator
H^{s}u(x,t)=(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}u(x,t)
is defined for functions
u=u(x,t)
in the parabolic Hölder space
C_{x,t}^{2s+\epsilon,s+\epsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
with
\epsilon>0
by the pointwise integro-differential expression

(1.2)

H^{s}u(x,t)=(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}u(x,t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u(x,t)-u(x-z,t-\tau))K_{s}(\tau,z)dzd\tau,

where the kernel is given by

K_{s}(\tau,z)=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}\left|\Gamma(-s)\right|}\frac{e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4\tau}}}{\tau^{\frac{n}{2}+1+s}},\quad\tau>0,~z\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

and
\Gamma
denotes the Gamma function. We focus specifically on the case
s=\frac{1}{2}
,
n=3
in (
1.1
).

Problem (
1.1
) is a special case of the general nonlocal fractional space-time equation.

(1.3)

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}u(x,t)=f(x,t),\quad 0<s<1\quad\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty).

Equation (
1.3
) serves as a fundamental example of a space-time nonlocal equation with order
s
in time and
2s
in space, analogous to the role of the fractional Laplacian in purely spatial nonlocal problems.

The nonlocality of the operator
(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}
rules out the direct application of classical localization techniques, such as multiplication by compactly supported test functions and integration by parts. This challenge is effectively resolved by the extension technique introduced by Stinga and Torrea
[
48
]
. They established a regularity theory for solutions to the fractional nonlocal equation (
1.3
). Using the parabolic semigroups method, they rigorously justified the pointwise intefro-differential formula (
1.2
) for
(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}
and derived an associated maximum principle, and characterized the nonlocal operator via a parabolic extension equation (see
[
48
, Theorem 1.7]
). This extension transforms the
n
-dimensional nonlocal problem into a local boundary value problem in the
(n+1)
-dimensional upper half-space.
The key advantage lies in the applicability of numerous numerical and analytical methods for partial differential equations to this extension equation, thereby enabling us to derive estimates and properties of the solution
u(x,t)
to the original problem. Additionally, they established parabolic interior and boundary Harnack inequalities, an Almgren-type monotonicity formula, and fractional parabolic Hölder ad Schauder estimates. We refer readers to
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
37
]
,
[
39
]
and the references therein for regularity, singularity as well as unique continuity property for the fractional heat operator
(\partial_{t}-\Delta)^{s}
.

It is worth noting that in equation (
1.1
), the random jumps are coupled with the random waiting times. To address this coupling, we reformulate the fractional nonlocal problem (
1.1
) as a local extension problem (
1.4
) in one higher spatial dimension.
Indeed,
s=\frac{1}{2}
and
n=3
, the parabolic extension technique established in
[
48
, Theorem 1.7]
shows that (
1.1
) is equivalent to

(1.4)

\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta u&\text{ in }\ \mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}\times(0,T),\\
-\frac{du}{dx_{4}}(\tilde{x},0,t)\ ={u}^{2}(\tilde{x},0,t)&\text{ in }\ \mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T),\end{cases}

where
x_{4}>0
is an additional spatial variable. This corresponds to a critical heat equation with Neumann boundary conditions.

In this paper, we establish the existence and explicit form of a finite-time blow-up solution to (
1.4
). A smooth solution of (
1.4
) blows up at time
T
if

\lim_{t\to T}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4})}=+\infty.

Recall that all positive solutions of the fractional Yamabe problem

(-\Delta)^{s}u=u^{\frac{n+2s}{n-2s}}(y)\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n},

are given by

(1.5)

\widetilde{U}_{\mu_{j}(t),\tilde{\xi}_{j}(t)}=\mu_{j}^{-\frac{n-2s}{2}}(t)\widetilde{U}\left(\frac{x-\tilde{\xi}_{j}(t)}{\mu_{j}(t)}\right)=\alpha_{n,s}\left(\frac{\mu_{j}(t)}{\mu_{j}^{2}(t)+|\tilde{x}-\tilde{\xi}_{j}(t)|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n-2s}{2}},

where
\alpha_{n,s}
is a constant depending only on
n
and
s
,
\mu_{j}(t)
and
\tilde{\xi}_{j}(t)
are the corresponding scaling parameters. Here, the fractional Laplace operator
(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined as

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x):=C(n,s)P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}dy,

where
C(n,s)
is a positive normalizing constant,
P.V.
denotes convergence in the sense of the Cauchy principal value. For further details on the fractional Laplace operator, we refer to
[
17
]
and
[
25
]
.

Substituting
s=\frac{1}{2}
and
n=3
into (
1.5
), we obtain

\widetilde{U}_{\mu(t),\tilde{\xi}(t)}(\tilde{x})=\mu^{-1}(t)\widetilde{U}\left(\frac{\tilde{x}-\tilde{\xi}(t)}{\mu(t)}\right)=\alpha_{0}\frac{\mu(t)}{\mu^{2}(t)+|\tilde{x}-\tilde{\xi}(t)|^{2}},

which solves the
\frac{1}{2}
-order Yamabe problem

(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\widetilde{U}=\widetilde{U}^{2}\quad\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3},

with
\alpha_{0}
a constant. It is well known that the harmonic extension of
\widetilde{U}
to
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}
is given by

U\left(\tilde{y},y_{4}\right):=U(y)=(P_{y_{4}}*\widetilde{U})(\tilde{y}),

where
P_{y_{4}}\left(\tilde{y}\right)
is the Poisson kernel for
(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, defined as

(1.6)

P_{y_{4}}\left(\tilde{y}\right)=\frac{\Gamma(2)}{\pi^{2}}\frac{y_{4}}{\left(|\tilde{y}|^{2}+\left|y_{4}\right|^{2}\right)^{2}},\quad\tilde{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},~y_{4}>0,

and
*
denotes the convolution operator. Moreover,
U\left(\tilde{y},y_{4}\right)
solves

\begin{cases}\Delta U=0&\text{ in }\ \mathbb{R}_{+}^{4},\\
-\frac{dU}{dy_{4}}(\tilde{y},0)\ =U^{2}(\tilde{y},0)&\text{ in }\ \mathbb{R}^{3}.\end{cases}

As shown in
[
38
, Section 2.2]
, the bubble profile takes the form

(1.7)

\displaystyle U(y):=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\left|\tilde{y}\right|^{2}+\left(1+y_{4}\right)^{2}},\quad y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4},

where
\alpha_{0}=2
. Writing
\xi(t)=(\tilde{\xi}(t),0)
with
\tilde{\xi}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we define the rescaled bubble as

(1.8)

U_{\mu(t),\xi(t)}:=\mu^{-1}(t)U\left(\frac{x-\xi(t)}{\mu(t)}\right)=\mu^{-1}(t)\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\left|\frac{\tilde{x}-\tilde{\xi}(t)}{\mu(t)}\right|^{2}+\left(1+\frac{x_{4}}{\mu(t)}\right)^{2}},\quad x=(\tilde{x},x_{4})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}.

Throughout the paper, for
k
distinct points
q_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\ j=1,\dots,k
, where
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we assume that a function
Z_{0}^{*}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4})
is chosen such that

Z_{0}^{*}(q_{j},0)<0\quad\text{for all}\ j=1,\dots,k.

The main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any sufficiently small
T>0
, there exists an initial datum
u_{0}
such that the solution
u(x,t)
to problem (
1.4
) blows up at the
k
points
q_{1},\dots,q_{k}
as
t\nearrow T
. Specifically, the solution admits the form

(1.9)

u(x,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}U_{\mu_{j}(t),\xi_{j}(t)}(x)+Z_{0}^{*}(x)+\Theta(x,t),

where
U_{\mu_{j}(t),\xi_{j}(t)}(x)
is the function defined in (
1.8
), and the parameters satisfy

\mu_{j}(t)\to 0,\quad\xi_{j}(t)=(\tilde{\xi}_{j}(t),0)\to\hat{q}_{j}=(q_{j},0)\quad\text{as}\quad t\nearrow T.

Moreover, there exist constants
c>0
and
\beta_{j}>0,j=1,\dots,k
such that

\|\Theta\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq T^{c},\quad\mu_{j}(t)=\beta_{j}\frac{|\log 2T|(T-t)}{|\log(T-t)|^{2}}(1+o(1)).

In recent years, the fractional heat equation with the Sobolev critical exponent

(1.10)

\begin{cases}u_{t}=-(-\Delta)^{s}u+u^{\frac{n+2s}{n-2s}}&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T),\\
u(\cdot,0)=u_{0}&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\end{cases}

for
0<s<1
and
0<T\leq\infty
has attracted much attention.
Extensive research has focused on equation (
1.10
) and related problems, see
[
7
,
8
,

,

,

,

,
14
,

,

,

,

,

,

and references therein.

When
s=1
, equation (
1.10
) reduces to the classical critical parabolic equation

(1.11)

\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta u+u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T),\\
u(\cdot,0)=u_{0}&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

For a smooth positive initial datum
u_{0}
, problem (
1.11
) admits a unique classical solution
u(x,t)
. If
(0,T)
is the maximum existence interval of
u(x,t)
with
T<\infty
, then
u(x,t)
blows up at time
T
. Finite-time blow-up is commonly classified into two types. Letting
p=\frac{n+2}{n-2}
, we define

\text{Type}~I:\lim\sup_{t\to T}(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty,

\text{Type}~II:\lim\sup_{t\to T}(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\infty.

Type I behaves similarly to the ODE
u_{t}=u^{p}
, where nonlinearity dominates.
Type II blow up is more intricate and challenging to analyze mathematically, owing to the delicate interplay of diffusion, nonlinearity, and domain geometry.

Singularity formation, especially the blow-up phenomenon for
n\geq 3
in problem (
1.11
) and related problems, for instance in smooth bounded domain
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, has been extensively studied in
[
16
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
30
,
40
,
44
]
and references therein.
It is usually expected that under suitable conditions, a blow-up solution admits the asymptotic profile

(1.12)

u(x,t)\sim\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}(t)W\left(\frac{x-\xi_{j}(t)}{\mu_{j}(t)}\right),

where
\mu_{j}(t)
and
\xi_{j}(t)
are scaling and translation parameters with
\mu_{j}(t)\to 0
as
t\to T
, and
W
is the positive solution of the Yamabe problem

\Delta W+W^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}=0\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{n}.

For
n\geq 3
, Filippas, Herrero and Velázquez
[
31
]
showed that Type II blow up can occur in the class of positive, radially symmetric and monotonically decreasing solutions.
Matano and Merle
[

]
later extended this result without monotonicity assumptions.
Furthermore, in
[
31
]
, the authors formally obtain a Type II blow-up to (

by using the matched

On the other hand, Galaktionov and Vázquez

showed that any radial solution

of (

must blow up in infinite time, i.e.,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}(B(0,1))}=\infty.

Galaktionov and King
[

constructed positive, radially symmetric infinite-time blow up solutions in

that satisfy the asymptotic form (
1.12
) as

with

and
\mu_{1}(t)\sim t^{-\frac{1}{n-4}}

In the non-radial setting, Cortázar del Pino and Musso

constructed positive infinite-time blow up solutions to problem (

Fila and King
[

studied problem (

) for positive, radially symmetric

with exact power decay rate, formally deriving growth and decay rates via matched asymptotics.
They further conjectured that infinite-time blow up occurs only in dimensions

and

del Pino, Musso, Wei, and Zhou
[

]
establish the existence of Type II finite-time blow-up solutions for (
1.11
) in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Their result guarantees that for any prescribed finite set of points and sufficiently small blow-up time
T>0
, there exists an initial datum leading to a solution that blows up exactly at those points with the sharp rate
\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\sim(T-t)^{-1}|\log(T-t)|^{2}
, the same blow-up rate as that observed for the 2-dimensional harmonic map flow
[

,
20

. The solution exhibits a multi-bubbling profile, asymptotically described by a sum of rescaled Aubin–Talenti bubbles with scaling parameters
\mu_{j}(t)\sim(T-t)|\log(T-t)|^{-2}
. The construction relies on a rigorous inner–outer gluing method and deals with a nonlocal effective dynamics for the scaling parameters. Theorem
1.1
can be viewed as counterpart of
[

]
to problem (
1.4

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on the parabolic inner-outer gluing method, as developed in
[
16
]
and
[

]
. This method has proven to be a powerful tool for constructing solutions to various parabolic problems, such as singularity formation in harmonic map flow

The main difficulty in studying singularity formation for energy-critical heat equations with Neumann boundary conditions is the lack of symmetry, which renders the ODE techniques inapplicable. Inspired by
[

, we develop a linear theory that is independent of symmetry, thus completing the proof of Theorem

This paper is organized as follows. In Section

, and carefully estimate its error. Section

introduces the inner–outer gluing scheme, decomposing the problem into an inner parabolic linearized equation and an outer heat equation with Neumann conditions. In Section

and

via a reduction procedure, deriving the blow-up rate
\mu(t)\sim(T-t)|\log(T-t)|^{-2}
. Section
5
establishes a linear theory for the outer problem, providing weighted estimates for the heat equation. In Section

by combining methods from

2.
Approximate solutions and error estimates

In this section, we construct an approximate solution to (
1.4
) and carefully estimate its residual error in both the interior and the boundary sense. For simplicity, we focus primarily on the one bubble case, modifications for multi-bubble case will be addressed at the end of this paper.

Define the error operators as

\displaystyle S^{in}(u):=-u_{t}+\Delta u\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \text{ in }\ \mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}\times(0,T),

\displaystyle S^{b}(u):=\frac{du}{dx_{4}}(\tilde{x},0,t)+u^{2}(\tilde{x},0,t)\quad\text{ in }\ \mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T).

Solving system (
1.4
) is equivalent to finding
u
satisfying

S^{in}(u)=0\quad\text{and}\quad S^{b}(u)=0.

It is a well-established result that the linearized operator around the bubble

L_{0}[\phi]:=-(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi+2\widetilde{U}(\tilde{y})\phi

is non-degenerate, in the sense that all bounded solutions to
L_{0}[\phi]=0

in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
are linear combinations of the functions
\widetilde{Z}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{Z}_{4}
, given by

\widetilde{Z}_{i}(\tilde{y})=\partial_{\tilde{y}_{i}}\widetilde{U}(\tilde{y}),\quad i=1,2,3,\quad\widetilde{Z}_{4}(y)=\widetilde{U}(\tilde{y})+\tilde{y}\cdot\nabla\widetilde{U}(\tilde{y}).

We now define the extension operator
L_{U}
in the half-space as follows

(2.1)

\displaystyle L_{U}[\phi]:=\begin{cases}\Delta_{y}\phi&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}\times(0,T),\\
-\frac{d\phi}{dy_{4}}(\tilde{y},0,t)-2U(\tilde{y},0)\phi(\tilde{y},0,t)&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T).\end{cases}

By the Caffarelli-Silvestre extension technique
[
10
]
, the Poisson convolution

Z_{i}(y)=(P_{y_{4}}*\widetilde{Z}_{i})(\tilde{y}),\quad i=1,\dots,4,

with
P_{y_{4}}
as the Poisson kernel for
(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, as defined in (
1.6
), yields a basis of the bounded solution space to
L_{U}[\phi]=0
.
These functions can be alternatively expressed as

(2.2)

\displaystyle Z_{i}(y)=\partial_{y_{i}}U(y),\ i=1,2,3,\quad Z_{4}(y)=U(y)+y\cdot\nabla U(y),

and they satisfy
L_{U}[Z_{i}]=0
in the sense of the system (
2.1
).

Our first approximation is the rescaled bubble

U_{\mu(t),\xi(t)}=\mu^{-1}(t)U\left(\frac{x-\xi(t)}{\mu(t)}\right)=\mu^{-1}(t)\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\left|\frac{\tilde{x}-\tilde{\xi}(t)}{\mu(t)}\right|^{2}+\left(1+\frac{x_{4}}{\mu(t)}\right)^{2}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4},

where
\mu(t)>0
and
\xi(t)=(\tilde{\xi}(t),0)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}
denote scaling and translation parameters to be determined later.
Direct computations yield

\displaystyle S^{in}(U_{\mu(t),\xi(t)})

\displaystyle=-\partial_{t}U_{\mu(t),\xi(t)}

\displaystyle=\mu^{-2}(t)\dot{\mu}(t)\left[U(y)+y\cdot\nabla U(y)\right]+\mu^{-2}(t)\dot{\xi}(t)\cdot\nabla U(y)

\displaystyle=\mu^{-2}(t)\dot{\mu}(t)\left(-\frac{\alpha_{0}}{|\tilde{y}|^{2}+(1+y_{4})^{2}}+\frac{\alpha_{0}(2+2y_{4})}{\left(|\tilde{y}|^{2}+(1+y_{4})^{2}\right)^{2}}\right)

(2.3)

\displaystyle\quad+\mu^{-2}(t)\nabla_{y}U(y)\cdot\dot{\xi}(t),

and

\displaystyle S^{b}(U_{\mu(t),\xi(t)})=\frac{dU_{\mu(t),\xi(t)}}{dx_{4}}(\tilde{x},0)+U^{2}_{\mu(t),\xi(t)}(\tilde{x},0)=0,

where
\tilde{y}=\frac{\tilde{x}-\tilde{\xi}(t)}{\mu(t)}
,
y_{4}=\frac{x_{4}}{\mu(t)}
. The slow-decaying error component in (
2
) is

\mathcal{E}_{0}=-\frac{\alpha_{0}\dot{\mu}(t)}{\left|\tilde{x}-\tilde{\xi}(t)\right|^{2}+\left(\mu(t)+x_{4}\right)^{2}}\approx-\frac{\alpha_{0}\dot{\mu}(t)}{\rho^{2}},

where
\rho:=|x-\xi(t)|
. To refine the approximation, we introduce the correction equation

(2.4)

\partial_{t}u_{1}=\Delta u_{1}+\mathcal{E}_{0}\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{4}\times(0,T).

Following the analysis in
[
19
]
, an explicit solution to (
2.4
) is given by

u_{1}=-\alpha_{0}\int_{-T}^{t}\dot{\mu}(s)k(\rho,t-s)ds,

where

(2.5)

k(\rho,t):=\frac{1-e^{-\frac{\rho^{2}}{4t}}}{\rho^{2}}.

Note that
u_{1}
is radially symmetric about
\xi(t)=(\tilde{\xi}(t),0)
, we have
u_{1}(\tilde{x},x_{4})=u_{1}(\tilde{x},-x_{4})
for
x_{4}\geq 0
, it follows that
u_{1}
solves

(2.6)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{1}=\Delta u_{1}+\mathcal{E}_{0}&~\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}\times(0,T),\\
-\frac{du_{1}}{dx_{4}}=0&\text{ in }\ \mathbb{R}^{3}.\end{cases}

To further regularize
u_{1}
, we choose a refined correction term
\Psi_{0}
to be

(2.7)

\Psi_{0}(x,t)=-\alpha_{0}\int_{-T}^{t}\dot{\mu}(s)k(\zeta(\rho,t),t-s)ds,

where

\zeta(\rho,t)=\sqrt{\rho^{2}+\mu^{2}(t)}.

We now analyze the error introduced by
\Psi_{0}
. A direct computation gives

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Psi_{0}-\Delta\Psi_{0}-\mathcal{E}_{0}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{0}\left[\frac{\mu y\cdot\dot{\xi}-\mu(t)\dot{\mu}(t)}{\zeta}\right]\int_{-T}^{t}\dot{\mu}(s)k_{\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)ds

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha_{0}\int_{-T}^{t}\dot{\mu}(s)\bigg[{-k_{t}(\zeta,t-s)}+\frac{\rho^{2}}{\zeta^{2}}k_{\zeta\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)+\frac{3}{\zeta}k_{\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)+\frac{\mu^{2}(t)}{\zeta^{3}}k_{\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)\bigg]ds.

Using the identity
-k_{t}+k_{\zeta\zeta}+\frac{3}{\zeta}k_{\zeta}=0
, which follows from (
2.5
), the expression simplifies to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Psi_{0}-\Delta\Psi_{0}-\mathcal{E}_{0}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{0}\left[\frac{y\cdot\dot{\xi}-\dot{\mu}(t)}{(1+|y|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right]\int_{-T}^{t}\dot{\mu}(s)k_{\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)ds

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\mu(t)(1+|y|^{2})^{3/2}}\int_{-T}^{t}\dot{\mu}(s)\left[-\zeta k_{\zeta\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)+k_{\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)\right]ds

(2.8)

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{R}[\mu].

The corrected approximate solution is therefore

u^{*}(x,t)=U_{\mu(t),\xi(t)}(x)+\Psi_{0}(x,t)\quad\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}\times(0,T),

with residual error

\displaystyle S^{in}(u^{*})

\displaystyle=S^{in}(U_{\mu(t),\xi(t)})-\mathcal{E}_{0}-\mathcal{R}[\mu]=:\mathcal{K}[\mu,\xi],

\displaystyle S^{b}(u^{*})

\displaystyle=\left(U_{\mu(t),\xi(t)}+\Psi_{0}\right)^{2}-U_{\mu(t),\xi(t)}^{2}

where

(2.9)

\mathcal{K}[\mu,\xi]:=\frac{\mu^{-2}(t)\dot{\mu}(t)\alpha_{0}(2+2y_{4})}{\left(|\tilde{y}|^{2}+(1+y_{4})^{2}\right)^{2}}+\mu^{-2}(t)\nabla U(y)\cdot\dot{\xi}(t)-\mathcal{R}[\mu],

and
\mathcal{R}[\mu]
is as defined in (
2
).

3.
The inner-outer gluing scheme

This section introduces the inner-outer gluing scheme, which decomposes the full problem into a system of coupled equations for an inner and an outer component. We then derive the precise form of this coupled system.

We seek a solution of the form

u(x,t)=u^{*}(x,t)+w\quad\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}\times(0,T),

where
w
is a small perturbation comprising inner and outer components

w=\varphi_{in}+\varphi_{out},\quad\varphi_{in}=\mu^{-1}(t)\eta_{R}\phi(y,t),\quad\varphi_{out}=\psi(x,t)+Z^{*}(x,t).

Here, the cut-off function

\eta_{R(t)}(y)=\eta_{0}\left(\frac{|y|}{R(t)}\right)\quad\text{for}~R(t)>0,

is defined via a smooth function
\eta_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
satisfying

(3.1)

\eta_{0}(s)=\begin{cases}1,&s<1,\\
0,&s>2.\end{cases}

And
Z^{*}(x,t)
is the unique solution to the initial-boundary value problem

\begin{cases}Z_{t}^{*}=\Delta Z^{*}&\text{ in }\ \mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}\times(0,T),\\
-\frac{dZ^{*}}{dx_{4}}(\tilde{x},0,t)=0&\text{ in }\ \mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T),\\
Z^{*}(x,0)=Z_{0}^{*}(x)&\text{ in }\ \mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}.\end{cases}

Direct computation yields

(3.2)

\displaystyle S^{in}\left(u^{*}+w\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{K}[\mu,\xi]-\mu^{-2}\dot{\mu}\eta_{R}\phi-\mu^{-1}\phi\partial_{t}\eta_{R}-\mu^{-1}\eta_{R}\phi_{t}+\mu^{-2}\eta_{R}\nabla_{y}\phi\left(\dot{\xi}+\dot{\mu}y\right)

\displaystyle+\mu^{-3}\phi\Delta_{y}\eta_{R}+\mu^{-3}\eta_{R}\Delta\phi+2\mu^{-3}\nabla_{y}\phi\nabla_{y}\eta_{R}-\psi_{t}+\Delta\psi,

and

(3.3)

\displaystyle S^{b}\left(u^{*}+w\right)

\displaystyle=\mu^{-2}\eta_{R}\frac{d\phi}{dy_{4}}(\tilde{y},0,t)+\mu^{-2}\phi(\tilde{y},0,t)\frac{d\eta_{R}}{dy_{4}}(\tilde{y},0,t)+\frac{d\psi}{dx_{4}}(\tilde{x},0,t)

\displaystyle+2\mu^{-2}U(\tilde{y},0)\eta_{R}\phi(\tilde{y},0,t)+2\mu^{-1}U(\tilde{y},0)\left(\Psi_{0}+\psi+Z^{*}\right)(\tilde{x},0,t).

Thus,
u=u^{*}+w
solves the original equation (
1.4
) if
(\phi(y,t),\psi(x,t))
satisfies the following inner-outer gluing system

(3.4)

\begin{cases}\mu^{2}\phi_{t}=\Delta_{y}\phi+\mathcal{H}_{1}[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi]&\text{ in }\ B_{2R}^{+}\times(0,T),\\
-\frac{d\phi}{dy_{4}}(\tilde{y},0,t)=2U(\tilde{y},0)\phi(\tilde{y},0,t)+\mathcal{H}_{2}[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi]&\text{ in }\ (\partial B_{2R}^{+}\cap\mathbb{R}^{3})\times(0,T),\end{cases}

and

(3.5)

\begin{cases}\psi_{t}=\Delta_{x}\psi+\mathcal{G}_{1}\left[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi\right]&\text{ in }\ \mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}\times(0,T),\\
-\frac{d\psi}{dx_{4}}\left(\tilde{x},0,t\right)=\mathcal{G}_{2}\left[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi\right]&\text{ in }\ \mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T),\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle B_{2R}^{+}

\displaystyle:=\left\{y=(\tilde{y},y_{4})\mid\tilde{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\ y_{4}\geq 0,\ |y|\leq 2R(t)\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{1}[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi](y,t)

\displaystyle:=\mu\left[\dot{\mu}\left(\nabla_{y}\phi\cdot y+\phi\right)+\nabla_{y}\phi\cdot\dot{\xi}\right]+\mu^{3}\mathcal{K}[\mu,\xi],

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{2}[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi](\tilde{y},0,t)

\displaystyle:=2\mu U(\tilde{y},0)\left(\Psi_{0}+\psi+Z^{*}\right)(\tilde{x},0,t),

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1}\left[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi\right](y,t):=

\displaystyle~\mu^{-3}\left[\phi\Delta_{y}\eta_{R}+2\nabla_{y}\phi\nabla_{y}\eta_{R}-\mu^{2}\phi\partial_{t}\eta_{R}\right]+(1-\eta_{R})\mathcal{K}[\mu,\xi],

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{2}\left[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi\right](\tilde{y},0,t):=

\displaystyle~2\mu^{-1}(1-\eta_{R})U(\tilde{y},0)\left(\Psi_{0}+\psi+Z^{*}\right)(\tilde{x},0,t)

\displaystyle~+\mu^{-2}\phi(\tilde{y},0,t)\frac{d\eta_{R}}{dy_{4}}(\tilde{y},0,t).

4.
Formal derivation of
\mu_{0}(t)
and
\xi_{0}(t)

In this section, we formally derive the leading-order behavior of the scaling parameter
\mu(t)
and the translation parameter
\xi(t)
as
t\nearrow T
. This is achieved by imposing orthogonality conditions against the kernel of the linearized operator.

At this point, we consider the linear system

(4.1)

\begin{cases}\Delta_{y}\phi=-\mathcal{H}_{1}[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi](y,t)&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}\times(0,T),\\
-\frac{d\phi}{dy_{4}}(\tilde{y},0,t)-2U(\tilde{y},0)\phi(\tilde{y},0,t)=\mathcal{H}_{2}[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi](\tilde{y},0,t)&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T).\end{cases}

Testing equation (
4.1
) against the kernel functions
Z_{i}(y)
and integrating over
\mathbb{R}{+}^{4}
gives

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}}\left(\Delta\phi+\mathcal{H}_{1}[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi](y,t)\right)Z_{i}(y)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}}\phi\Delta Z_{i}(y)dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}Z_{i}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\nu}d\tilde{y}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi\frac{\partial Z_{i}}{\partial\nu}d\tilde{y}+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}}\mathcal{H}_{1}[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi](y,t)Z_{i}(y)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}}\mathcal{H}_{1}[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi](y,t)Z_{i}(y)dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{H}_{2}[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi](\tilde{y},0,t)Z_{i}(\tilde{y},0)d\tilde{y}.

Therefore, the inner problem must satisfy the orthogonality conditions

(4.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}}\mathcal{H}_{1}[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi](y,t)Z_{i}(y)dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{H}_{2}[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi](\tilde{y},0,t)Z_{i}(\tilde{y},0)d\tilde{y}=0,\quad i=1,\dots,4,

where
Z_{i}
are the kernel functions of the linearized operator
L_{U}
defined in (
2.1
).

We consider functions
\xi(t)=(\tilde{\xi}(t),0)\to\hat{q}=(q,0)
and parameters
\mu(t)\to 0
as
t\nearrow T
, decomposed as

(4.3)

\mu(t)=\mu_{0}(t)+\mu_{1}(t),\quad\xi(t)=\xi_{0}(t)+\xi^{1}(t),

where
\mu_{0}(t)
and
\xi_{0}(t)
are the leading-order terms of
\mu(t)
and
\xi(t)
, respectively, while
\mu_{1}(t)
and
\xi^{1}(t)
are lower-order remainders satisfying
\mu_{1}(t)\ll\mu_{0}(t)
and
\xi^{1}(t)\ll\xi_{0}(t)
.
To determine the leading-order parameters
\mu_{0}(t)
and
\xi_{0}(t)
from (
4.2
), we extract the dominant terms in (
3.4
),

(4.4)

\begin{cases}\mu_{0}^{2}\phi_{t}=\Delta_{y}\phi+\mathcal{H}_{1}^{*}[\mu,\xi]&\text{ in }\ B_{2R}^{+}\times(0,T),\\
-\frac{d\phi}{dy_{4}}(\tilde{y},0,t)=pU^{p-1}(\tilde{y},0)\phi(\tilde{y},0,t)+\mathcal{H}_{2}^{*}[\mu,\xi,\psi,Z^{*}]&\text{ in }\ (\partial B_{2R}^{+}\cap\mathbb{R}^{3})\times(0,T)\end{cases}

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{1}^{*}[\mu,\xi](y,t):=

\displaystyle~\frac{\mu(t)\dot{\mu}(t)\alpha_{0}(2+2y_{4})}{\left(1+|y|^{2}\right)^{2}}+\mu(t)\nabla U(y)\cdot\dot{\xi}(t)-\mu^{3}(t)\mathcal{R}[\mu],

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{2}^{*}[\mu,\xi,\psi,Z^{*}](\tilde{y},0,t):=

\displaystyle~2\mu U(\tilde{y},0)\left(\Psi_{0}+\psi+Z^{*}\right)(\tilde{x},0,t).

The contributions from the residual terms
\mathcal{H}_{i}-\mathcal{H}_{i}^{*}
, (
i=1,2
) to the orthogonality conditions are negligible compared to those of the leading-order terms
\mathcal{H}_{i}
.

We thus approximate solutions to (
4.4
) by requiring that
\mu_{0}(t)
and
\xi_{0}(t)
to satisfy

(4.5)

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{*}[\mu,\xi](y,t)Z_{i}(y)dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{*}[\mu,\xi,\psi,Z^{*}](\tilde{y},0,t)Z_{i}(\tilde{y},0)d\tilde{y}=0,\quad i=1,\dots,4.

For
i=1,2,3
, (
4.5
) imply

\dot{\xi}_{0}(t)=o(1),

where
o(1)\to 0
as
t\nearrow T
. Hence,

(4.6)

\xi_{0}(t)=\hat{q}+o(1)\vec{v}

for some vector
\vec{v}
, where
\hat{q}=(q,0)
is a prescribed point in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}
.

For
i=4
, we expand
Z^{*}(\mu y+\xi,t)
and
\psi(\mu y+\xi,t)
at the point
\hat{q}
,

Z^{*}(\mu y+\xi,t)=Z_{0}^{*}(\hat{q})+o(1),\quad\psi(\mu y+\xi,t)=\psi(\hat{q},0)+o(1).

Substituting into the orthogonality condition

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{*}[\mu,\xi](y,t)Z_{4}(y)dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{*}[\mu,\xi,\psi,Z^{*}](\tilde{y},0,t)Z_{4}(\tilde{y},0)d\tilde{y}=0,

we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}}\left[\frac{\dot{\mu}(t)\alpha_{0}(2+2y_{4})}{\left(1+|y|^{2}\right)^{2}}+\nabla U(y)\cdot\dot{\xi}(t)-\mu^{2}(t)\mathcal{R}[\mu]\right]Z_{4}(y)dy

(4.7)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}2U(\tilde{y},0)\left[\Psi_{0}(x,t)+\psi(\hat{q},0)+Z_{0}^{*}(\hat{q})\right]Z_{4}(\tilde{y},0)d\tilde{y}+o(1)=0.

We first calculate

(4.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}}\left[\frac{\dot{\mu}(t)\alpha_{0}(2+2y_{4})}{\left(1+|y|^{2}\right)^{2}}+\nabla U(y)\cdot\dot{\xi}(t)\right]Z_{4}(y)dy=\alpha_{0}\dot{\mu}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}}\frac{2+2y_{4}}{\left(1+|y|^{2}\right)^{2}}Z_{4}(y)dy.

Next, we examine the
\mathcal{R}[\mu]
term

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}}\mathcal{R}[\mu]Z_{4}(y)dy

\displaystyle=-\alpha_{0}\dot{\mu}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}}\frac{Z_{4}(y)}{(1+|y|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{-T}^{t}\dot{\mu}(s)k_{\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)dsdy

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\mu(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}}\frac{Z_{4}(y)}{(1+|y|^{2})^{3/2}}\int_{-T}^{t}\dot{\mu}(s)[k_{\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)-\zeta k_{\zeta\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)]dsdy

Let

(4.9)

\Upsilon=\frac{\zeta^{2}}{t-s}=\frac{\mu^{2}(t)(1+|y|^{2})}{t-s},\quad\tau=\frac{\mu^{2}(t)}{t-s}

and define
K(\Upsilon)=\frac{1-e^{-\frac{\Upsilon}{4}}}{\Upsilon}
. From (
2.5
), we derive

\displaystyle k_{\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)-\zeta k_{\zeta\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)

\displaystyle=-4\left(\frac{\Upsilon}{t-s}\right)^{3/2}K_{\Upsilon\Upsilon}(\Upsilon)

and also

\displaystyle k_{\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)=-\frac{2}{\zeta^{3}}+\frac{e^{-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4(t-s)}}}{2\zeta(t-s)}+\frac{2e^{-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4(t-s)}}}{\zeta^{3}}=\frac{2\sqrt{\Upsilon}}{(t-s)^{3/2}}K_{\Upsilon}(\Upsilon).

Substituting these yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\mathcal{R}[\mu]Z_{4}(y)dy

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\alpha_{0}\dot{\mu}(t)}{\mu(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}}\frac{Z_{4}(y)}{1+|y|^{2}}\left(\int_{-T}^{t}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}\Upsilon K_{\Upsilon}(\Upsilon)ds\right)dy

(4.10)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{4\alpha_{0}}{\mu^{2}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}}\frac{Z_{4}(y)}{(1+|y|^{2})^{2}}\left(\int_{-T}^{t}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}\Upsilon^{2}K_{\Upsilon\Upsilon}(\Upsilon)ds\right)dy.

On the other hand, from (
2.7
) and (
4.9
), we find

(4.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}2U(\tilde{y},0)\Psi_{0}(\tilde{x},0,t)Z_{4}(\tilde{y},0)d\tilde{y}=-2\alpha_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}U^{2}(\tilde{y},0)Z_{4}(\tilde{y},0)\int_{-T}^{t}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}K(\Upsilon)dsd\tilde{y}.

Combining (
4
), (
4.8
) (
4
) and (
4.11
) yields

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}\dot{\mu}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}}\frac{2+2y_{4}}{\left(1+|y|^{2}\right)^{2}}Z_{4}(y)dy+2\alpha_{0}\dot{\mu}(t)\mu(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}}\frac{Z_{4}(y)}{1+|y|^{2}}\left(\int_{-T}^{t}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}\Upsilon K_{\Upsilon}(\Upsilon)ds\right)dy

\displaystyle+4\alpha_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}}\frac{Z_{4}(y)}{(1+|y|^{2})^{2}}\left(\int_{-T}^{t}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}\Upsilon^{2}K_{\Upsilon\Upsilon}(\Upsilon)ds\right)dy

\displaystyle-2\alpha_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}U^{2}(\tilde{y},0)Z_{4}(\tilde{y},0)\int_{-T}^{t}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}K(\Upsilon)dsd\tilde{y}

(4.12)

\displaystyle+2\left[\psi(\hat{q},0)+Z_{0}^{*}(\hat{q})\right]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}U(\tilde{y},0)Z_{4}(\tilde{y},0)d\tilde{y}+o(1)=0.

For the construction of a blow-up solution, the scaling parameter
\mu(t)
must tend to
0
as
t\nearrow T
. We therefore impose the condition

\dot{\mu}(t)=o(1)\quad\text{as}\quad t\nearrow T.

Thus, (
4
) simplifies to

\displaystyle 4\alpha_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}}\frac{Z_{4}(y)}{(1+|y|^{2})^{2}}\left(\int_{-T}^{t}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}\Upsilon^{2}K_{\Upsilon\Upsilon}(\Upsilon)ds\right)dy

\displaystyle-2\alpha_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}U^{2}(\tilde{y},0)Z_{4}(\tilde{y},0)\int_{-T}^{t}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}K(\Upsilon)dsd\tilde{y}

(4.13)

\displaystyle=-2\left[\psi(\hat{q},0)+Z_{0}^{*}(\hat{q})\right]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}U(\tilde{y},0)Z_{4}(\tilde{y},0)d\tilde{y}+o(1).

Now define the function

\displaystyle\Gamma(\tau):=4\alpha_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}}\frac{Z_{4}(y)}{(1+|y|^{2})^{2}}\Upsilon^{2}K_{\Upsilon\Upsilon}(\Upsilon)dy-2\alpha_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}U^{2}(\tilde{y},0)Z_{4}(\tilde{y},0)K(\Upsilon)d\tilde{y},

where
\Upsilon=\tau(1+|y|^{2})
. Then (
4
) becomes

(4.14)

\displaystyle\int_{-T}^{t}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}\Gamma\left(\frac{\mu^{2}(t)}{t-s}\right)ds=-2\left[\psi(\hat{q},0)+Z_{0}^{*}(\hat{q})\right]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}U(\tilde{y},0)Z_{4}(\tilde{y},0)d\tilde{y}+o(1).

Using the explicit forms of
U(\tilde{y},0)
and
Z_{4}(\tilde{y},0)
from (
1.7
) and (
2.2
), we compute the asymptotic behavior

\Gamma(\tau)=\begin{cases}c_{*}+O\left(\tau\right)&\text{for }\tau<1,\\
O\left(\frac{1}{\tau}\right)&\text{for }\tau>1,\end{cases}

with
c_{*}>0
is a constant. Thus, (
4.14
) reduces to

(4.15)

c_{*}\int_{-T}^{t-\mu^{2}(t)}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}ds=-2c_{0}[Z^{*}_{0}(\hat{q})+\psi(\hat{q},0)]+o(1),

where

c_{0}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}U(\tilde{y},0)Z_{4}(\tilde{y},0)d\tilde{y}<0.

Since
\mu(t)
decreases to
0
as
t\nearrow T
, we require

a_{*}:=Z^{*}_{0}(\hat{q})+\psi(\hat{q},0)<0.

A natural choice for the leading-order behavior of
\mu(t)
is given by

(4.16)

\dot{\mu}(t)=-\frac{c}{|\log(T-t)|^{2}},

where
c>0
is a constant that will be determined below. Indeed, substituting into the integral yields

\displaystyle\int_{-T}^{t-\mu^{2}(t)}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{-T}^{t-(T-t)}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}ds+\int_{t-(T-t)}^{t-\mu^{2}(t)}\frac{\dot{\mu}(t)}{t-s}ds-\int_{t-(T-t)}^{t-\mu^{2}(t)}\frac{\dot{\mu}(t)-\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{-T}^{t-(T-t)}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}ds+\dot{\mu}(t)(\log(T-t)-2\log\mu(t))

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{t-(T-t)}^{t-\mu^{2}(t)}\frac{\dot{\mu}(t)-\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{-T}^{t}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{T-s}ds-\dot{\mu}(t)\log(T-t)+o(1).

Define
\beta(t):=\int_{-T}^{t}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{T-s}ds-\dot{\mu}(t)\log(T-t)
. From (
4.16
), we compute

\log(T-t)\frac{d\beta}{dt}(t)=\frac{d}{dt}\left(-\log^{2}(T-t)\dot{\mu}(t)\right)=0,

which implies
\beta(t)
is a constant. Thus, (
4.15
) can be approximately solved by
(
4.16
) with
c
chosen as

-c\int_{-T}^{T}\frac{ds}{(T-s)|\log(T-s)|^{2}}=-\frac{3c_{0}\,a_{*}}{c_{*}}:=\kappa_{*}.

At main order, we thus obtain

\dot{\mu}(t)=\kappa_{*}\dot{\mu}_{0}(t)\quad\text{with}\quad\dot{\mu}_{0}(t)=-\frac{|\log 2T|}{|\log(T-t)|^{2}}.

Imposing the condition
\mu_{0}(T)=0
, we obtain

(4.17)

\displaystyle\mu_{0}(t)=\frac{|\log 2T|(T-t)}{|\log(T-t)|^{2}}(1+o(1))\quad\text{as}\quad t\nearrow T.

5.
The linear theory of the outer problem

In this section, we establish a priori estimates of the outer problem. We first consider the corresponding linear problem

(5.1)

\begin{cases}\psi_{t}=\Delta\psi+g_{1}(x,t)&\text{in}~\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}\times(0,T),\\
-\frac{d\psi}{dx_{4}}(\tilde{x},0,t)=g_{2}(\tilde{x},t)&\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T).\end{cases}

We decompose problem (
5.1
) into

(5.2)

\begin{cases}\psi_{t}=\Delta\psi+g_{1}(x,t)&\text{in}~\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}\times(0,T),\\
-\frac{d\psi}{dx_{4}}(\tilde{x},0,t)=0&\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T)\end{cases}

and

(5.3)

\begin{cases}\psi_{t}=\Delta\psi&\text{in}~\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}\times(0,T),\\
-\frac{d\psi}{dx_{4}}(\tilde{x},0,t)=g_{2}(\tilde{x},t)&\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T).\\
\end{cases}

5.1.
Estimates for problem (
5.2
)

We begin by estimating solutions to (
5.2
). From
[
34
]
, the heat kernel of problem (
5.2
) is given by

(5.4)

K_{t}(x,y):=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{2}}\left(e^{\frac{-|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}+e^{\frac{-|x-\bar{y}|^{2}}{4t}}\right),

where
\bar{y}=(\tilde{y},-y_{4})
denotes the reflection of
y
across the hyperplane
\{y_{4}=0\}
.
A solution
\psi
to (
5.2
) can then be represented as

(5.5)

\displaystyle\psi(x,t):

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}}K_{t-s}(x,z)g_{1}(z,s)dzds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{2}}\left(e^{\frac{-|x-z|^{2}}{4(t-s)}}+e^{\frac{-|x-\bar{z}|^{2}}{4(t-s)}}\right)g_{1}(z,s)dzds.

We introduce the following weight functions

(5.6)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\varrho_{1}:=\mu_{0}^{\nu-3}(t)R^{-2-\alpha}(t)\chi_{\{|x-\xi(t)|\leq 2\mu_{0}R\}}\\

6.
The linear theory of the inner problem

In this section, we develop a linear theory for the inner problem (
3.4
). To analyze (
3.4
), we first consider the linearized equation

(6.1)

\begin{cases}\mu^{2}\phi_{t}=\Delta_{y}\phi+g(y,t)&\text{ in }\ B_{2R}^{+}\times(0,T),\\
-\frac{d\phi}{dy_{4}}(\tilde{y},0,t)=2U(\tilde{y},0)\phi(\tilde{y},0,t)+h(\tilde{y},0,t)&\text{ in }\ (\partial B_{2R}^{+}\cap\mathbb{R}^{3})\times(0,T),\end{cases}

where

B_{2R}^{+}:=\{y=(\tilde{y},y_{4})\mid\tilde{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\ y_{4}\geq 0,\ |y|\leq 2R(t)\}.

We shall construct a solution to the following initial value problem

(6.2)

\begin{cases}\mu^{2}\phi_{t}=\Delta_{y}\phi+g(y,t)&\text{ in }\ B_{2R}^{+}\times(0,T),\\
-\frac{d\phi}{dy_{4}}(\tilde{y},0,t)=2U(\tilde{y},0)\phi(\tilde{y},0,t)+h(\tilde{y},0,t)&\text{ in }\ (\partial B_{2R}^{+}\cap\mathbb{R}^{3})\times(0,T).\end{cases}

Our goal is to construct a solution to (
6.2
) that defines a bounded linear operator of
g
and
h
, satisfying favorable bounds in appropriate weighted norms.
For any
0<\sigma<1
and
a>0
, we introduce the weighted norms

\displaystyle\|g\|_{a+\sigma,\nu}:=\sup_{(y,t)\in B_{2R}^{+}\times(0,T)}\mu_{0}^{-\nu}(t)(1+|y|^{a+\sigma})|g(y,t)|,

(6.3)

\displaystyle\|h\|_{a+\sigma,\nu}:=\sup_{(\tilde{y},t)\in(\partial B_{2R}^{+}\cap\mathbb{R}^{3})\times(0,T)}\mu_{0}^{-\nu}(t)(1+|\tilde{y}|^{a+\sigma})|h(\tilde{y},t)|.

Moreover, orthogonality conditions are imposed on
g
and
h
. Specifically, we seek a solution
\phi
to (
6.2
) that satisfies

\int_{B_{2R}^{+}}g(y,t)Z_{i}(y)dy+\int_{\partial B_{2R}^{+}\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}h(\tilde{y},0,t)Z_{i}(\tilde{y},0)d\tilde{y}=0\quad\text{for}~i=1,\dots,4,~t\in(0,T).

As detailed below, our construction involves decomposing the equation into distinct spherical harmonic modes. Let
\{\Theta_{m}\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
be an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
consisting of spherical harmonics, which satisfy the eigenvalue problem

\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\Theta_{m}+\tilde{\lambda}_{m}\Theta_{m}=0\quad\text{in}\ \mathbb{S}^{2},

where the eigenvalues are ordered as

0=\tilde{\lambda}_{0}<\tilde{\lambda}_{1}=\cdots=\tilde{\lambda}_{3}=2<\tilde{\lambda}_{4}\leq\cdots.

Specifically,

\Theta_{0}(\tilde{y})=a_{0},\quad\Theta_{j}(\tilde{y})=a_{1}\tilde{y}_{j},~j=1,2,3,

for constants
a_{0}
and
a_{1}
. In general, all eigenvalues eigenvalues take the form
\tilde{\lambda}_{m}=l(1+l)
for some nonnegative integer
l
.

Given
g\in L^{2}(B_{2R}^{+})
and
h\in L^{2}(\partial B_{2R}^{+}\cap\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we decompose them into spherical harmonic components,

\displaystyle g(\tilde{y},y_{4},t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}g_{j}(r,y_{4},t)\Theta_{j}\left(\frac{\tilde{y}}{r}\right),\quad r=|\tilde{y}|,

\displaystyle g_{j}(r,y_{4},t)

Lemma 6.2

We will utilize a fundamental lemma concerning the quadratic form associated with the linear operator

:

\displaystyle B(\phi,\phi)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}}\phi\Delta\phi dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}}|\nabla\phi|^{2}dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{d\phi}{dy_{4}}\phi d\tilde{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}}|\nabla\phi|^{2}dy-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}2U(\tilde{y},0)\phi^{2}(\tilde{y},0,\tau)d\tilde{y}.

The following lemma provides an estimate for the quadratic form
B(\phi,\phi)
associated with the second
L^{2}
-eigenvalue in the half-ball

under zero boundary conditions for large radius

Define the radial Sobolev space

H_{radial}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}):=\left\{\phi(y)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+})\mid\phi(y)\ \text{is radially symmetric in the variable}\ \tilde{y}\right\}.

And the function space

H_{R}:=\left\{\phi\in H_{radial}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+})~\Big|~\phi|_{\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}\setminus B_{2R}^{+}}=0,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}}\phi Z_{0}dy=0\right\}.

Lemma 6.3
.

There exists a constant
\gamma>0
such that for every

, there holds

\frac{\gamma}{R^{2}}\int_{B_{2R}^{+}}|\phi|^{2}dy\leq B(\phi,\phi).

Proof.

Define

\lambda_{R}:=\inf\left\{B(\phi,\phi)~\Big|~\phi\in H_{R},\ \int_{B_{2R}^{+}}|\phi|^{2}dy=1\right\}.

By standard compactness arguments
[
26

,
\lambda_{R}
is achieved by a radial minimizer
\phi_{R}\in H_{R}
with
\int_{B_{2R}^{+}}|\phi_{R}|^{2}dy=1
, satisfying

(6.16)

\begin{cases}\Delta\phi_{R}+\lambda_{R}\phi_{R}=c_{R}Z_{0}(y)&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}_{+}^{4},\\
-\frac{d\phi_{R}}{dy_{4}}(\tilde{y},0)-2U(\tilde{y},0)\phi_{R}(\tilde{y},0)=0&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3},\end{cases}

for a suitable Lagrange multiplier

.
Combining the

-orthogonality condition of
\phi_{R}
with respect to
\widetilde{Z}_{0}
, it follows that

B(\phi,\phi)\geq 0\quad\text{for all}~\phi\in H_{radial}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}),

and thus
\lambda_{R}\geq 0
.
Indeed, if
\lambda_{R}<0
, then the condition
\phi_{R}|_{\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}\backslash B_{2R}^{+}}=0
implies
B(\phi_{R},\phi_{R})<0
. However, this would force
\phi_{R}
to satisfy

\phi_{R}(y)=kZ_{0}(y)\quad\text{for some constant }k\neq 0.

Combining this with the orthogonality condition
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+}}\phi Z_{0}dy=0
, we deduce
\phi_{R}=0
, contradicting
\int_{B_{2R}^{+}}|\phi_{R}|^{2}dy=1
.

6.15

\lambda_{R}=o(R^{-2})\quad\text{as}\ R\to\infty.

Testing (

Since

|c_{R}|\lesssim\lambda_{R}
.

On the other hand, again since

Moreover, testing (
6.16
) against
Z_{4}

It then follows that

Recall that

implying

From (

On the other hand, multiplying

\bar{\phi}_{\tau}

Hence,

Then by

Solving the inner-outer gluing system

In this section, we construct a solution to the inner-outer gluing system (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) by formulating it as a fixed-point problem for the tuple
\vec{p}=(\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi)
within a carefully chosen function space. The construction relies on the linear theories developed in Sections
5
and
6
, and is completed via the Schauder fixed-point theorem.

7.1.
Function spaces and parameter decomposition

To set up the fixed-point argument, we first define the function spaces for the inner and outer corrections, as well as the modulation parameters. The choice of these spaces and their associated weighted norms is crucial, they are meticulously designed to:

(1)

capture the expected spatial decay and temporal blow-up rates of the solutions,

(2)

accommodate the singular behavior near the blow-up point,

(3)

be compatible with the linear theories established in Sections
5
and
6
, ensuring that the solution operators are bounded.

We therefore introduce the following decompositions and Banach spaces.
The scaling and translation parameters are decomposed as

\mu(t)=\mu_{0}(t)+\mu_{1}(t),\quad\xi(t)=\xi_{0}(t)+\xi^{1}(t),\quad t\in[0,T),

where
\mu_{0}(t)
and
\xi_{0}(t)
are defined in (
4.17
) and (
4.6
), respectively, with
\mu_{1}(T)=0
and
\xi^{1}(T)=0
. Furthermore, we assume that the time derivatives satisfy the comparative bound

c_{1}|\dot{\mu}_{0}(t)|\leq|\dot{\mu}(t)|\leq c_{2}|\dot{\mu}_{0}(t)|\quad\text{for all}~t\in(0,T),

for some constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
.

The inner correction
\phi
and the outer correction
\psi
are decomposed according to their spectral properties and roles in the boundary conditions:

\phi=\phi^{0}+\phi^{\perp},\qquad\psi=\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}.

Here,
\phi^{0}
corresponds to the mode associated with
Z_{4}
(the scaling instability), while
\phi^{\perp}
spans the higher modes related to translations (
Z_{\ell}
,
\ell=1,2,3
). The outer correction
\psi_{1}
solves a homogeneous Neumann problem, and
\psi_{2}
handles the inhomogeneous boundary data.

The solution will be sought in the following Banach spaces, defined by their respective weighted norms.

\displaystyle X_{\phi^{0}}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\phi^{0}\in L^{\infty}(B_{2R}^{+}\times(0,T))\mid\nabla_{y}\phi^{0}\in L^{\infty}(B_{2R}^{+}\times(0,T)),\ \|\phi^{0}\|_{\sigma_{1},\nu}<+\infty\right\},

\displaystyle X_{\phi^{\perp}}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\phi^{\perp}\in L^{\infty}(B_{2R}^{+}\times(0,T))\mid\nabla_{y}\phi^{\perp}\in L^{\infty}(B_{2R}^{+}\times(0,T)),\ \|\phi^{\perp}\|_{1+\sigma_{2},\nu}<+\infty\right\},

\displaystyle X_{\psi_{1}}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\psi_{1}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}\times(0,T))\mid\|\psi_{1}\|_{*}^{(1)}<+\infty\right\},

\displaystyle X_{\psi_{2}}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\psi_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}\times(0,T))\mid\|\psi_{2}\|_{*}^{(2)}<+\infty\right\}.

The norms referenced here are defined in (
6
), (
5.8
) and (
5.19
).

The modulation parameter
\mu(t)
will be determined by a non-local integral equation arising from the orthogonality condition
c^{0}=0
(to be detailed in Subsection
7.3.2
). The analysis of this equation, following the methodology of
[
19
]
, dictates a specific decomposition of the solution. We therefore define the parameter space accordingly.

We decompose
\mu(t)
as

\mu(t)=\mu_{0,\kappa}(t)+\mu_{1}(t),

where the leading-order term is defined for some
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
by

(7.1)

\displaystyle\mu_{0,\kappa}:=\kappa|\log T|\int_{t}^{T}\frac{1}{|\log(T-s)|^{2}}ds,\quad t\leq T,

and the perturbation
\mu_{1}
is assumed to lie in the space

X_{\mu}:=\left\{\mu_{1}\in C^{1}([-T,T]),0<\iota<1\mid\mu_{1}(T)=0,\ \|\mu_{1}\|_{*,3-\iota}<+\infty\right\}.

Here, the norm for the perturbation is given by

\|f\|_{*,k}:=\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}|\log(T-t)|^{k}|\dot{f}(t)|.

The full parameter
\mu
is then equipped with the norm

(7.2)

\displaystyle\|\mu\|_{F}=|\kappa|+\|\mu_{1}\|_{*,3-\iota}.

The motivation for this specific decomposition and the associated norms will become clear when we analyze the governing equation for
\mu(t)
in Subsection
7.3.2
.

Writing
\xi(t)=\hat{q}+\xi^{1}(t)
, we define the space for
\xi^{1}
as

X_{\xi}=\left\{\xi\in C^{1}((0,T);\mathbb{R}^{4}_{+})\mid\dot{\xi}(T)=0,\ \|\xi\|_{G}<+\infty\right\}

where the norm is given by

(7.3)

\displaystyle\|\xi\|_{G}=\|\xi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}+\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\mu_{0}^{-\nu_{1}}(t)|\dot{\xi}(t)|

for some fixed
\nu_{1}\in(0,\nu)
.

The full solution space is the product space

\mathcal{X}=X_{\phi^{0}}\times X_{\phi^{\perp}}\times X_{\psi_{1}}\times X_{\psi_{2}}\times\mathbb{R}\times X_{\mu}\times X_{\xi}.

where the
\mathbb{R}
corresponds to the parameter
\kappa
. We will seek a fixed point within a closed ball
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{X}
defined by the bounds in (
7.55
).

Next, we will estimate the nonlinear terms using the appropriate norms. This ensures that the linear theories can be applied and enables the definition of a compact fixed-point map.

7.2.
Estimates of the nonlinear terms

This subsection is devoted to estimating the nonlinear terms
\mathcal{G}_{1}
,
\mathcal{G}_{2}
and
\mathcal{H}_{1}
,
\mathcal{H}_{2}
in the appropriate dual norms. These estimates are crucial for ensuring the boundedness of the fixed-point map to be defined later. We proceed by examining each term systematically, using the support properties of the cut-off function
\eta_{R}
and the asymptotic behavior of the bubble
U(y)
.

7.2.1.
Estimates for the outer problem:
\mathcal{G}_{1}
and
\mathcal{G}_{2}

We begin by analyzing the terms
\mathcal{G}_{1}[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi]
and
\mathcal{G}_{2}[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi]
in the norms
\|\cdot\|_{**}^{(1)}
and
\|\cdot\|_{**}^{(2)}
, defined in (
5.7
) and (
5.20
), respectively.

Recall that

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1}\left[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi\right](y,t)=\mu^{-3}\left[\phi\Delta_{y}\eta_{R}+2\nabla_{y}\phi\nabla_{y}\eta_{R}-\mu^{2}\phi\partial_{t}\eta_{R}\right]+(1-\eta_{R})\mathcal{K}[\mu,\xi],

where
\mathcal{K}[\mu,\xi]
is defined in (
2.9
).

We proceed term by term. Due to the cut-off function
\eta_{R}
, the term

\mu^{-3}\left[\phi\Delta_{y}\eta_{R}+2\nabla_{y}\phi\nabla_{y}\eta_{R}-\mu^{2}\phi\partial_{t}\eta_{R}\right]

is supported in the region

\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}\times(0,T)\mid\mu_{0}R\leq|x-\xi(t)|\leq 2\mu_{0}R\right\}.

It follows directly that

\displaystyle\mu^{-3}\left[\phi\Delta_{y}\eta_{R}+2\nabla_{y}\phi\nabla_{y}\eta_{R}-\mu^{2}\phi\partial_{t}\eta_{R}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu_{0}^{\nu-3}R^{-2-\alpha}\chi_{\{|x-\xi(t)|\sim\mu_{0}R\}}\left(R^{\alpha-\sigma_{1}}\|\phi^{0}\|_{\sigma_{1},\nu}+R^{\alpha-1-\sigma_{2}}\|\phi^{\perp}\|_{1+\sigma_{2},\nu}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(1+\mu_{0}^{\nu_{1}+1}R\|\xi\|_{G}+\mu_{0}R^{2}\|\dot{\mu}\|_{\infty}+\mu_{0}^{2}R\dot{R}\right)

(7.4)

\displaystyle\lesssim\rho_{1}\left(R^{\alpha-\sigma_{1}}\|\phi^{0}\|_{\sigma_{1},\nu}+R^{\alpha-1-\sigma_{2}}\|\phi^{\perp}\|_{1+\sigma_{2},\nu}\right),

where we used the relation
R(t)=\mu_{0}^{-\beta}(t)
with
\beta\in(0,1/2)
and the norm
\|\xi\|_{G}
is defined in (
7.3
).

Next, we estimate the term
(1-\eta_{R})\mathcal{K}[\mu,\xi]
. Recalling from (
2.9
) that

\mathcal{K}[\mu,\xi]=\frac{\mu^{-2}(t)\dot{\mu}(t)\alpha_{0}(2+2y_{4})}{\left(|\tilde{y}|^{2}+(1+y_{4})^{2}\right)^{2}}+\mu^{-2}(t)\nabla U(y)\cdot\dot{\xi}(t)-\mathcal{R}[\mu].

Then

\displaystyle(1-\eta_{R})\left[\frac{\mu^{-2}(t)\dot{\mu}(t)\alpha_{0}(2+2y_{4})}{\left(|\tilde{y}|^{2}+(1+y_{4})^{2}\right)^{2}}+\mu^{-2}(t)\nabla U(y)\cdot\dot{\xi}(t)\right]

(7.5)

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-1}\mu_{0}^{-\nu_{2}}\rho_{2}\|\dot{\mu}\|_{\infty}+R^{-1}\mu_{0}^{\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}}\rho_{2}\|\xi\|_{G}.

Recall from (
2
) that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}[\mu]

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\Psi_{0}-\Delta\Psi_{0}-\mathcal{E}_{0}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{0}\frac{y\cdot\dot{\xi}-\dot{\mu}(t)}{(1+|y|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{-T}^{t}\dot{\mu}(s)k_{\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)ds

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\mu(t)(1+|y|^{2})^{3/2}}\int_{-T}^{t}\dot{\mu}(s)\left[-\zeta k_{\zeta\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)+k_{\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)\right]ds.

Following computations similar to those in
[
24
, Section 6.1]
, we decompose the integral over
s
into two intervals

\displaystyle\int_{-T}^{t}\dot{\mu}(s)k_{\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)ds=\left(\int_{-T}^{t-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4}}+\int_{t-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4}}^{t}\right)\dot{\mu}(s)k_{\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)ds.

We estimate the first integral as follows.

•

For
T-t>\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4}
, we further split this interval into

\displaystyle\int_{-T}^{t-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4}}\dot{\mu}(s)k_{\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)ds=\left(\int_{-T}^{t-(T-t)}+\int_{t-(T-t)}^{t-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4}}\right)\dot{\mu}(s)k_{\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)ds.

Using the expression

k_{\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)=-\frac{2}{\zeta^{3}}+\frac{e^{-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4(t-s)}}}{2\zeta(t-s)}+\frac{2e^{-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4(t-s)}}}{\zeta^{3}},

and noting that
\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4(t-s)}<1
and
T-s<2(t-s)
for
s\in(-T,t-(T-t))
, we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{-T}^{t-(T-t)}\dot{\mu}(s)\left[-\frac{2}{\zeta^{3}}+\frac{e^{-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4(t-s)}}}{2\zeta(t-s)}+\frac{2e^{-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4(t-s)}}}{\zeta^{3}}\right]ds

(7.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{-T}^{t-(T-t)}\frac{|\dot{\mu}(s)|\zeta}{(t-s)^{2}}ds\lesssim\|\dot{\mu}\|_{\infty}\int_{-T}^{t-(T-t)}\frac{1}{(T-s)^{\frac{3}{2}}}ds\lesssim\|\dot{\mu}\|_{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(T-t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Similarly, for the second integral

\displaystyle\int_{t-(T-t)}^{t-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4}}\dot{\mu}(s)\left[-\frac{2}{\zeta^{3}}+\frac{e^{-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4(t-s)}}}{2\zeta(t-s)}+\frac{2e^{-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4(t-s)}}}{\zeta^{3}}\right]ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\dot{\mu}\|_{\infty}\int_{t-(T-t)}^{t-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4}}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{3}{2}}}ds

(7.7)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\dot{\mu}\|_{\infty}\left|\frac{1}{\zeta}-\frac{1}{\left(T-t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right|

•

For
T-t<\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4}
, since
s<t-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4}<t-(T-t)
, a direct estimation gives

(7.8)

\displaystyle\int_{-T}^{t-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4}}\frac{|\dot{\mu}(s)|\zeta}{(t-s)^{2}}ds\lesssim\int_{-T}^{t-(T-t)}\frac{|\dot{\mu}(s)|\zeta}{(t-s)^{2}}dsds\lesssim\|\dot{\mu}\|_{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(T-t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}

Next, we estimate the integral near
s=t
,

(7.9)

\displaystyle\int_{t-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4}}^{t}\dot{\mu}(s)k_{\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)ds\lesssim\frac{1}{\zeta^{3}}\int_{t-\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4}}^{t}|\dot{\mu}(s)|ds\lesssim\|\dot{\mu}\|_{\infty}\frac{1}{\zeta}.

Combining estimates (
•
‣
7.2.1
)-(
7.9
) and recalling that
\zeta=\mu(t)\sqrt{1+|y|^{2}}
, we conclude that

(7.10)

\displaystyle\left|\alpha_{0}\frac{y\cdot\dot{\xi}-\dot{\mu}(t)}{(1+|y|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{-T}^{t}\dot{\mu}(s)k_{\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)ds\right|\lesssim\|\dot{\mu}\|_{\infty}\left[\frac{|y\cdot\dot{\xi}+\dot{\mu}|}{\left(T-t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+|y|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{|y\cdot\dot{\xi}+\dot{\mu}|}{\mu(1+|y|^{2})}\right].

For the term involving
-\zeta k_{\zeta\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)+k_{\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)
, note the asymptotic behaviors:

\left|-\zeta k_{\zeta\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)+k_{\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)\right|\sim\frac{\zeta}{(t-s)^{2}}\quad\text{for}~~\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4(t-s)}<1

and

\left|-\zeta k_{\zeta\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)+k_{\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)\right|\sim\frac{1}{\zeta^{3}}~\quad\text{for}~~\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4(t-s)}>1.

By analogous estimates to those above, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{0}}{\mu(t)(1+|y|^{2})^{3/2}}\int_{-T}^{t}\dot{\mu}(s)\left[-\zeta k_{\zeta\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)+k_{\zeta}(\zeta,t-s)\right]ds

(7.11)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\dot{\mu}\|_{\infty}\left[\frac{1}{\left(T-t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu(t)(1+|y|^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{1}{\mu^{2}(1+|y|^{2})^{2}}\right].

Combining all contributions, we summarize

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}[\mu]\lesssim\|\dot{\mu}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\left[\frac{|y\cdot\dot{\xi}+\dot{\mu}|}{\left(T-t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+|y|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{|y\cdot\dot{\xi}+\dot{\mu}|}{\mu(1+|y|^{2})}\right.

(7.12)

\displaystyle\left.\quad+\frac{1}{\left(T-t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu(t)(1+|y|^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{1}{\mu^{2}(1+|y|^{2})^{2}}\right].

Consequently, the contribution of
(1-\eta_{R})\mathcal{R}[\mu]
is bounded by

\displaystyle(1-\eta_{R})\mathcal{R}[\mu]\lesssim\|\dot{\mu}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\left[\mu_{0}^{\nu_{1}-\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\|_{G}+\mu_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}-\nu_{2}}\rho_{2}+\mu_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{3}+\mu_{0}^{\nu_{1}-1}R^{-1}\|\xi\|_{G}\right.

(7.13)

\displaystyle\left.\quad+\mu_{0}^{1-\nu_{2}}\rho_{2}+\mu_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}-\nu_{2}}R^{-1}\rho_{2}+R^{-2}\mu_{0}^{-\nu_{2}}\rho_{2}\right].

Combining the estimates from (
7.2.1
), (
7.2.1
), and (
7.2.1
), we conclude that

(7.14)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{G}_{1}\right\|_{**}^{(1)}\lesssim T^{\varepsilon_{0}}\left(\|\phi^{0}\|_{\sigma,\nu}+\|\phi^{\perp}\|_{1+\sigma,\nu}+\|\dot{\mu}\|_{\infty}+\|\xi\|_{G}+1\right),

provided the following parameter conditions hold:

(7.15)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\sigma_{1}-\alpha>0,\\
1+\sigma_{2}-\alpha>0,\\
\beta-\nu_{2}>0,\\
\nu_{1}+\beta-\nu_{2}>0,\\
\nu_{1}-\frac{1}{2}>0,\\
\frac{1}{2}-\nu_{2}>0,\\
\nu_{1}-1+\beta>0,\\
2\beta-\nu_{2}>0,\end{cases}

where
\varepsilon_{0}
is a small positive number.

Next, we proceed to estimate
\mathcal{G}_{2}\left[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi\right]
. Recall that

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{2}\left[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi\right](\tilde{y},0,t)=

\displaystyle~2\mu^{-1}(1-\eta_{R})U(\tilde{y},0)\left(\Psi_{0}+\psi+Z^{*}\right)(\tilde{x},0,t)

\displaystyle~+\mu^{-2}\phi(\tilde{y},0,t)\frac{d\eta_{R}}{dy_{4}}(\tilde{y},0,t)+f_{1}(\tilde{x},t),

where

f_{1}(\tilde{x},t)=-\mu^{-2}(t)\eta_{R}(y)f(\tilde{y},t).

By direct computations analogous to those employed in estimating
\mathcal{R}[\mu]
, see also
[
24
, Section 6.1]
, we establish the bound

(7.16)

\displaystyle|\Psi_{0}|\lesssim|\dot{\mu}|\left[\log\left(\rho^{2}+\mu^{2}\right)+1\right].

Substituting this into the first term of
\mathcal{G}_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle 2\mu^{-1}(1-\eta_{R})U(\tilde{y},0)\left(\Psi_{0}+\psi+Z^{*}\right)(\tilde{x},0,t)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\mu_{0}^{\nu_{2}}(t)}{|\tilde{x}-\tilde{\xi}(t)|}\chi_{\{|\tilde{x}-\tilde{\xi}(t)|\geq\mu_{0}R\}}R^{-1}(t)\mu_{0}^{-\nu_{2}}(t)\Big[|\log(T-t)|\|\dot{\mu}\|_{\infty}+\|Z^{*}\|_{*}

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\mu_{0}^{\nu-1}(0)R^{-\alpha}(0)+T^{\nu_{2}}|\log T|^{1-\nu_{2}}+T\right)\left(\|\psi\|_{*}^{(1)}+\|\psi\|_{*}^{(2)}\right)\Big].

Moreover,

\displaystyle\left|\mu^{-2}\phi(\tilde{y},0,t)\frac{d\eta_{R}}{dy_{4}}(\tilde{y},0,t)\right|=0.

Recall that
f(\tilde{y},t)
corresponds to
-c(\tau)\widetilde{Z}_{0}(\tilde{y})
under a suitable change of variables
\tau
. Combining this with the fact that
\widetilde{Z}_{0}(\tilde{y})\sim|\tilde{y}|^{-4}
, we get

\left|f_{1}(\tilde{x},t)\right|=\left|-\mu^{-2}(t)\eta_{R}(y)f(\tilde{y},t)\right|\lesssim\mu_{0}^{\nu-2}(t)R^{-1-\alpha}(t)\chi_{\{|\tilde{x}-\tilde{\xi}(t)|\leq 2\mu_{0}R\}}R^{\alpha-3}(t).

Combining these estimates, we conclude the bound for
\mathcal{G}_{2}
in the norm
\|\cdot\|_{**}^{(2)}
, defined in (
5.20
),

(7.17)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{G}_{2}\|_{**}^{(2)}\lesssim T^{\varepsilon_{0}}\left(\|\dot{\mu}\|_{\infty}+\|\psi\|_{\infty}+\|Z^{*}\|_{\infty}+1\right)

provided

(7.18)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\beta-\nu_{2}>0,\\
\nu-1-\nu_{2}+\beta(1+\alpha)>0,\\
\alpha-3<0.\end{cases}

7.2.2.
Estimates for the inner problem:
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}

We now consider the inner problem (
3.4
). The linear theory developed in Section
6
guarantees that for
\mathcal{H}_{1}=\mathcal{H}_{1}^{0}+\mathcal{H}_{1}^{\perp}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}=\mathcal{H}_{2}^{0}+\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\perp}
satisfying

(7.19)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}_{1}^{0}\|_{2+\sigma_{1},\nu},\ \|\mathcal{H}_{1}^{\perp}\|_{3+\sigma_{2},\nu},~\|\mathcal{H}_{2}^{0}\|_{1+\sigma_{1},\nu},\ \|\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\perp}\|_{2+\sigma_{2},\nu}<+\infty,

there exists a solution
(\phi^{0},\phi^{\perp},c^{0},c^{\ell})
(
\ell=1,2,3
) to the projected inner problems

(7.20)

\begin{cases}\mu^{2}\phi^{0}_{t}=\Delta_{y}\phi^{0}+\mathcal{H}^{0}_{1}[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi]&\text{ in }~B_{2R}^{+}\times(0,T),\\
-\frac{d\phi^{0}}{dy_{4}}(\tilde{y},0,t)=2U(\tilde{y},0)\phi^{0}(\tilde{y},0,t)+\mathcal{H}^{0}_{2}[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi]\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+c^{0}Z_{4}(\tilde{y},0)&\text{ in }~(\partial B_{2R}^{+}\cap\mathbb{R}^{3})\times(0,T),\\
\phi^{0}(\cdot,0)=0&\ \text{in }~B_{2R}^{+},\end{cases}

and

(7.21)

\begin{cases}\mu^{2}\phi^{\perp}_{t}=\Delta_{y}\phi^{\perp}+\mathcal{H}^{\perp}_{1}[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi]&\text{ in }~B_{2R}^{+}\times(0,T),\\

\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{3}c^{\ell}Z_{\ell}(\tilde{y},0)&\text{ in }~(\partial B_{2R}^{+}\cap\mathbb{R}^{3})\times(0,T),\\
\phi^{\perp}(\cdot,0)=0&\ \text{in }~B_{2R}^{+}.\end{cases}

Under these conditions, the inner solution
\phi[\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}]=\phi^{0}[\mathcal{H}_{1}^{0},\mathcal{H}_{2}^{0}]+\phi^{\perp}[\mathcal{H}_{1}^{\perp},\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\perp}]
with appropriate space-time decay can then be constructed to carry out the inner-outer gluing procedure. Our first step is to ensure that the boundedness conditions (

) hold by appropriately selecting constants.

Recall that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{1}[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi](y,t)

\displaystyle=\mu\left[\dot{\mu}\left(\nabla_{y}\phi\cdot y+\phi\right)+\nabla_{y}\phi\cdot\dot{\xi}\right]+\mu^{3}\mathcal{K}[\mu,\xi],

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{2}[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi](\tilde{y},0,t)

\displaystyle=2\mu U(\tilde{y},0)\left(\Psi_{0}+\psi+Z^{*}\right)(\tilde{x},0,t).

Direct computation gives

\displaystyle\left|\mu\left[\dot{\mu}\left(\nabla_{y}\phi\cdot y+\phi\right)+\nabla_{y}\phi\cdot\dot{\xi}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu_{0}|\dot{\mu}_{0}|\left[\frac{\mu_{0}^{\nu}}{1+|y|^{\sigma_{1}}}\|\phi^{0}\|_{\sigma_{1},\nu}+\frac{\mu_{0}^{\nu}}{1+|y|^{1+\sigma_{2}}}\|\phi^{\perp}\|_{1+\sigma_{2},\nu}\right]

(7.22)

\displaystyle\quad+\mu_{0}|\dot{\xi}|\left[\frac{\mu_{0}^{\nu}}{1+|y|^{1+\sigma_{1}}}\|\phi^{0}\|_{\sigma_{1},\nu}+\frac{\mu_{0}^{\nu}}{1+|y|^{2+\sigma_{2}}}\|\phi^{\perp}\|_{1+\sigma_{2},\nu}\right].

Using the bound for
\mathcal{R}[\mu]
from (
7.2.1
), we estimate the second term in
\mathcal{H}_{1}

\displaystyle\left|\mu^{3}\mathcal{K}[\mu,\xi]\right|\lesssim

\displaystyle~\frac{\mu_{0}\left|\dot{\mu}_{0}\right|+\mu_{0}|\dot{\xi}|}{1+|y|^{3}}+\|\dot{\mu}\|_{\infty}\left[\frac{\mu_{0}^{3}|y\cdot\dot{\xi}+\dot{\mu}|}{\left(T-t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+|y|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}|y\cdot\dot{\xi}+\dot{\mu}|}{1+|y|^{2}}\right]

(7.23)

\displaystyle~+\|\dot{\mu}\|_{\infty}\left[\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}}{\left(T-t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+|y|^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{\mu_{0}}{(1+|y|^{2})^{2}}\right].

Combining (
7.2.2
), (
7.2.2
) and applying the relevant norms, we obtain the bound for
\mathcal{H}_{1}^{0}

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}_{1}^{0}\|_{2+\sigma_{1},\nu}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu_{0}\|\dot{\mu}\|_{\infty}\left(R^{2}\|\phi^{0}\|_{\sigma_{1},\nu}+R^{1+\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}}\|\phi^{\perp}\|_{1+\sigma_{2},\nu}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\mu_{0}^{1+\nu_{1}}\|\xi\|_{G}\left(R\|\phi^{0}\|_{\sigma_{1},\nu}+R^{\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{2}}\|\phi^{\perp}\|_{1+\sigma_{2},\nu}\right)+\left(\mu_{0}^{1-\nu}\|\dot{\mu}\|_{\infty}+\mu_{0}^{1+\nu_{1}-\nu}\|\xi\|_{G}\right)R^{-1+\sigma_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\dot{\mu}\|_{\infty}\left[\frac{\mu_{0}^{3-\nu}R^{1+\sigma_{1}}|R\cdot\dot{\xi}+\dot{\mu}|+\mu_{0}^{2-\nu}R^{-1+\sigma_{1}}}{\left(T-t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\mu_{0}^{2-\nu}R^{\sigma_{1}}|R\cdot\dot{\xi}+\dot{\mu}|+\mu_{0}^{1-\nu}R^{-2+\sigma_{1}}\right]

(7.24)

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\varepsilon_{0}}\left(\|\phi^{0}\|_{\sigma_{1},\nu}+\|\phi^{\perp}\|_{1+\sigma_{2},\nu}+\|\dot{\mu}\|_{\infty}+\|\xi\|_{G}+1\right),

provided that the following parameter conditions are satisfied

\displaystyle\begin{cases}1-2\beta>0,\\

7.3.
The parameter problems

The parameters
\mu(t)
and
\xi(t)
are not prescribed in advance but are determined as part of the solution. Their evolution is governed by the conditions
c^{0}=0
and
c^{\ell}=0
. Specifically, to complete the system defined by (

) and (

), we must adjust the parameters
\mu(t)

(7.34)

\displaystyle c^{0}[\mu,\xi,\psi,Z^{*}]=0,\quad c^{\ell}[\mu,\xi,\psi,Z^{*}]=0,\quad\ell=1,2,3,\ \forall~t\in(0,T),

where the coefficients are given by

(7.35)

\displaystyle c^{0}[\mu,\xi,\psi,Z^{*}]=\frac{\int_{B_{2R}^{+}}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{0}(y,t)Z_{4}(y)dy+\int_{\partial{B}_{2R}^{+}\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{0}(\tilde{y},t)\widetilde{Z}_{4}(\tilde{y})d\tilde{y}}{\int_{\partial{B}_{2R}^{+}\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\widetilde{Z}_{4}|^{2}d\tilde{y}},

\displaystyle c^{\ell}[\mu,\xi,\psi,Z^{*}]=\frac{\int_{B_{2R}^{+}}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{\perp}(y,t)Z_{\ell}(y)dy+\int_{\partial{B}_{2R}^{+}\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\perp}(\tilde{y},t)\widetilde{Z}_{\ell}(\tilde{y})d\tilde{y}}{\int_{\partial{B}_{2R}^{+}\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\widetilde{Z}_{\ell}|^{2}d\tilde{y}}\quad\text{for}~\ell=1,2,3.

The equation for
\xi(t)
controls the location of the blow-up point by counteracting translation instabilities, while the non-local integro-differential equation for

determines the blow-up rate by balancing the dominant scaling instability. The subsequent analysis translates conditions (
7.34
) into solvable equations for
\dot{\xi}
and
\dot{\mu}
.

7.3.1.
The reduced equation for
\xi(t)

We begin by considering the reduced equation for the parameter
\xi(t)
. Observing that condition (
7.36
) is equivalent to

\int_{B_{2R}^{+}}\mathcal{H}_{1}^{\perp}(y,t)Z_{\ell}(y)dy+\int_{\partial{B}_{2R}^{+}\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\perp}(\tilde{y},t)\widetilde{Z}_{\ell}(\tilde{y})d\tilde{y}=0,\quad\ell=1,2,3,

which implies

(7.37)

\dot{\xi}_{\ell}=b_{\ell}[\mu,\xi,\phi,\psi,Z^{*}],

where

\displaystyle b_{\ell}[\mu,\xi,\phi,\psi,Z^{*}]=\frac{\int_{B_{2R}^{+}}\left[\mathcal{H}_{1}^{\perp}(y,t)-\mu\partial_{y_{l}}U(y)\dot{\xi}_{l}\right]Z_{\ell}(y)dy+\int_{\partial{B}_{2R}^{+}\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\perp}(\tilde{y},t)\widetilde{Z}_{\ell}(\tilde{y})d\tilde{y}}{\mu(t)\int_{B_{2R}^{+}}\left|Z_{\ell}(y)\right|^{2}dy}.

A direct estimate yields

\displaystyle\left|\int_{B_{2R}^{+}}\mu\left[\dot{\mu}\left(\nabla_{y}\phi\cdot y+\phi\right)+\nabla_{y}\phi\cdot\dot{\xi}\right]Z_{\ell}(y)dy\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu_{0}^{1+\nu}|\dot{\mu}_{0}|\left(R^{1-\sigma_{1}}\|\phi^{0}\|_{\sigma_{1},\nu}+\|\phi^{\perp}\|_{1+\sigma_{2},\nu}\right)+\mu_{0}^{1+\nu}|\dot{\xi}|\left(\|\phi^{0}\|_{\sigma_{1},\nu}+\|\phi^{\perp}\|_{1+\sigma_{2},\nu}\right).

Symmetry considerations play a crucial role. From (
2
) and (
2.9
), the term
\mu^{3}\mathcal{K}[\mu,\xi]-\mu\partial_{y_{\ell}}U(y)\dot{\xi}_{\ell}
is even in
y_{\ell}
. Since
Z_{\ell}(y)
is odd in
y_{\ell}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2R}^{+}}\left[\mu^{3}\mathcal{K}[\mu,\xi]-\mu\partial_{y_{l}}U(y)\dot{\xi}_{l}\right]Z_{\ell}(y)dy=0.

Similarly, by (
2.7
),
\Psi_{0}
is even in
y_{\ell}
. Furthermore, recalling that

corresponds to

under a suitable change of variables

, and noting that
\widetilde{Z}_{0}(\tilde{y})
is even in
\tilde{y}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\partial{B}_{2R}^{+}\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left[2\mu U(\tilde{y},0)\Psi_{0}(\tilde{x},0,t)+f(\tilde{y},t)\right]\widetilde{Z}_{\ell}(\tilde{y})d\tilde{y}=0.

For the remaining boundary term involving
\psi
and
Z^{*}
, we expand

\displaystyle\mu\int_{\partial{B}_{2R}^{+}\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}U(\tilde{y},0)\left(\psi+Z^{*}\right)(\tilde{x},0,t)\widetilde{Z}_{\ell}(\tilde{y})d\tilde{y}

\displaystyle=\mu\int_{\partial{B}_{2R}^{+}\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}U(\tilde{y},0)\left[\psi(\tilde{\xi},0,t)+Z^{*}(\tilde{\xi},0,t)+\mu y_{l}\left(\frac{{\partial}\psi}{{\partial}x_{l}}+\frac{{\partial}Z^{*}}{{\partial}x_{l}}\right)(\bar{\xi},0,t)\right]\widetilde{Z}_{\ell}(\tilde{y})d\tilde{y},

where
\bar{\xi}=\tilde{\xi}+\theta(\tilde{x}-\tilde{\xi})
for some
\theta\in[0,1]
. Using the evenness of

in
\tilde{y}
and the oddness of
\widetilde{Z}_{\ell}(\tilde{y})
in
\tilde{y}
for

, we estimate

\displaystyle\left|2\mu\int_{\partial{B}_{2R}^{+}\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}U(\tilde{y},0)\left(\psi+Z^{*}\right)(\tilde{x},0,t)\widetilde{Z}_{\ell}(\tilde{y})d\tilde{y}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu_{0}^{2}(t)\left[\left(\mu_{0}^{\nu-1}(0)R^{-\alpha}(0)+T^{\nu_{2}}|\log T|^{1-\nu_{2}}+T\right)\left(\|\psi\|_{*}^{(1)}+\|\psi\|_{*}^{(2)}\right)+\|Z^{*}\|_{*}\right].

Thus, the size of

is controlled by

\displaystyle|b_{\ell}[\mu,\xi,\phi,\psi,Z^{*}]|

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu_{0}^{\nu}|\dot{\mu}_{0}|\left(R^{1-\sigma_{1}}\|\phi^{0}\|_{\sigma_{1},\nu}+\|\phi^{\perp}\|_{1+\sigma_{2},\nu}\right)+\mu_{0}^{\nu}|\dot{\xi}|\left(\|\phi^{0}\|_{\sigma_{1},\nu}+\|\phi^{\perp}\|_{1+\sigma_{2},\nu}\right)

(7.38)

\displaystyle\quad+\mu_{0}(t)\left[\left(\mu_{0}^{\nu-1}(0)R^{-\alpha}(0)+T^{\nu_{2}}|\log T|^{1-\nu_{2}}+T\right)\left(\|\psi\|_{*}^{(1)}+\|\psi\|_{*}^{(2)}\right)+\|Z^{*}\|_{*}\right].

We now analyze the reduced problem (
7.37
), which defines solution operators
\Xi_{\ell}
(
\ell=1,2,3
) for the components
\xi_{\ell}
. Writing

\Xi=(\Xi_{1},\Xi_{2},\Xi_{3}),

we express

, where

is a prescribed point in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}
. We seek solutions for

within the function space equipped with the norm

for some fixed

Consequently, we have

Combining (

) and (

\displaystyle\|\Xi_{\ell}\|_{G}\leq

\displaystyle~|q_{\ell}|+C(T-t)^{-\nu_{1}}\mu_{0}^{\nu}|\dot{\mu}_{0}|\left(R^{1-\sigma_{1}}\|\phi^{0}\|_{\sigma_{1},\nu}+\|\phi^{\perp}\|_{1+\sigma_{2},\nu}\right)

The reduced problem for the scaling parameter

is fundamentally analogous to that treated in

Direct computations show that condition (

(7.42)

\int_{-T}^{t}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}\Gamma\left(\frac{\mu^{2}(t)}{t-s}\right)ds+\mathbf{c}_{0}\dot{\mu}=a[\mu,\xi,\psi,Z^{*}](t)+\mathbf{a}_{r}[\mu,\xi,\phi,\psi,Z^{*}](t),

and the remainder term

\displaystyle|\mathbf{a}_{r}[\mu,\xi,\phi,\psi,Z^{*}](t)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu_{0}|\dot{\mu}_{0}|+\mu_{0}^{\nu}|\dot{\mu}_{0}|\left(R^{2-\sigma_{1}}\|\phi^{0}\|_{\sigma_{1},\nu}+R^{1-\sigma_{2}}\|\phi^{\perp}\|_{1+\sigma_{2},\nu}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\mu_{0}^{\nu}|\dot{\xi}|\left(R^{1-\sigma_{1}}\|\phi^{0}\|_{\sigma_{1},\nu}+\|\phi^{\perp}\|_{1+\sigma_{2},\nu}\right)+|\dot{\mu}_{0}|R^{-1}.

To solve

, we introduce the following functional norms.

•

\|\cdot\|_{\Theta,l}

,

\|f\|_{\Theta,l}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\frac{|\log(T-t)|^{l}}{(T-t)^{\Theta}}|f(t)|,

where

and
l\in\mathbb{R}
.

•

[\cdot]_{\gamma,m,l}
-seminorm: for

with

,

[g]_{\gamma,m,l}:=\sup_{I_{T}}\frac{|\log(T-t)|^{l}}{(T-t)^{m}(t-s)^{\gamma}}|g(t)-g(s)|,

where

,

,

and

.

We define the principal operator as

(7.44)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{0}[\mu](t):=\int_{-T}^{t}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}\Gamma\left(\frac{\mu^{2}(t)}{t-s}\right)ds+\mathbf{c}_{0}\dot{\mu},

and express the orthogonality coefficient as

\displaystyle c^{0}[\mathcal{H}^{0}_{1},\mathcal{H}^{0}_{2}]=\frac{\mathcal{B}_{0}[\mu]-\left(a[\mu,\xi,\psi,Z^{*}]+\mathbf{a}_{r}[\mu,\xi,\phi,\psi,Z^{*}]\right)}{\int_{\partial{B}_{2R}^{+}\cap{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left|\widetilde{Z}_{4}\right|^{2}d\tilde{y}}.

The solvability of
\mu(t)
relies on the following key proposition from
[
19
]
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
\omega,\Theta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
m\leq\Theta-\gamma
and
l\in\mathbb{R}
. Suppose
a(t)
satisfies
a(T)<0
with
1/C\leq|a(T)|\leq C
for some constant
C>1
, and

(7.45)

\displaystyle T^{\Theta}|\log T|^{1+c-l}\|a(\cdot)-a(T)\|_{\Theta,l-1}+[a]_{\gamma,m,l-1}\leq C_{1}

for some
c>0
. Then there exist operators
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{R}_{0}
such that

satisfies

(7.46)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{0}[\mu](t)=a(t)+\mathcal{R}_{0}[a](t)

with the remainder estimate

|\mathcal{R}_{0}[a](t)|\lesssim\left(T^{\frac{1}{2}+c}+T^{\Theta}\frac{\log|\log T|}{|\log T|}\|a(\cdot)-a(T)\|_{\Theta,l-1}+[a]_{\gamma,m,l-1}\right)\frac{(T-t)^{m+(1+\omega)\gamma}}{|\log(T-t)|^{l}}.

Recall from (

) that the operator
\mathcal{B}_{0}
satisfies the asymptotic expansion

\mathcal{B}_{0}[\mu]=\int_{-T}^{t-\mu^{2}_{0}(t)}\frac{\dot{\mu}(s)}{t-s}ds+O(\|\dot{\mu}\|_{\infty}).

Proposition

establishes an approximate inverse operator
\mathcal{P}
for
\mathcal{B}_{0}
. Specifically, if the source term

satisfies (

), then the function

satisfies

\mathcal{B}_{0}[\mu]=a+\mathcal{R}_{0}[a]\quad\text{in }~[-T,T],

where
\mathcal{R}_{0}[a]
is a negligible remainder. Moreover, the analysis in
[
19
]
yields the decomposition

(7.47)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}[a]=\mu_{0,\kappa}+\mathcal{P}_{1}[a],

in which

is given by (

) with

,
and the perturbation

satisfies the norm estimate

(7.48)

\displaystyle\|\mu_{1}\|_{*,3-\iota}\lesssim|\log T|^{1-\iota}\log^{2}(|\log T|),\quad 0<\iota<1.

This analysis thereby clarifies the motivation behind the functional framework introduced in Subsection
7.1
for the parameter
\mu

7.4.
Proof of Theorem

The proof of Theorem

, building on the preparatory work of Subsections

,

7.4.1.
The inner-outer gluing system and parameter constraints

Following the analysis of the modulation parameters in Subsection
7.3
, we transform the inner-outer problems (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) into finding a tuple
(\psi,\phi^{0},\phi^{\perp},\mu,\xi)
that satisfy the following

-\frac{d\psi_{1}}{dx_{4}}\left(\tilde{x},0,t\right)=0&\text{ in }~\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T),\end{cases}

(7.50)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\psi_{2}=\Delta_{x}\psi_{2}&\text{ in }~\mathbb{R}_{+}^{4}\times(0,T),\\

\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+c^{0}[\mathcal{H}^{0}_{1},\mathcal{H}^{0}_{2}]Z_{4}(\tilde{y},0)&\text{ in }~(\partial B_{2R}^{+}\cap\mathbb{R}^{3})\times(0,T),\\

(7.52)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mu^{2}\phi^{\perp}_{t}=\Delta_{y}\phi^{\perp}+\mathcal{H}^{\perp}_{1}[\phi,\psi,\mu,\xi]&\text{ in }~B_{2R}^{+}\times(0,T),\\

\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{3}c^{\ell}[\mathcal{H}^{\perp}_{1},\mathcal{H}^{\perp}_{2}]Z_{\ell}(\tilde{y},0)&\text{ in }~(\partial B_{2R}^{+}\cap\mathbb{R}^{3})\times(0,T),\\

(7.53)

\displaystyle c^{0}[\mathcal{H}^{0}_{1},\mathcal{H}^{0}_{2}](t)=0\quad\text{for all}\ t\in(0,T),

Here,

7.4.2.
Construction of the fixed-point operator

We aim to solve the inner-outer gluing system within a closed ball
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{X}
, where elements
(\phi^{0},\phi^{\perp},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\kappa,\mu_{1},\xi^{1})
satisfy

(7.55)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\|\phi^{0}\|_{\sigma_{1},\nu}+\|\phi^{\perp}\|_{1+\sigma_{2},\nu}\leq 1,\\
\|\psi_{1}\|_{*}^{(1)}+\|\psi_{2}\|_{*}^{(2)}\leq 1,\\
|\kappa-\kappa_{0}|\leq|\log T|^{-1/2},\\
\|\mu_{1}\|_{\ast,3-\iota}\leq C|\log T|^{1-\iota}\log^{2}(|\log T|),\\
\|\xi\|_{G}\leq 1,\end{cases}

with
\kappa_{0}=Z^{*}_{0}(0)
and
C>0
is a large and fixed constant.

We now construct the fixed-point operator
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
for the entire inner-outer gluing system. The main strategy is to apply the linear solution operators from the previous sections to define a map whose fixed point satisfies the nonlinear system (
7.49
)–(
7.54
). Specifically, for a given tuple of approximate solutions and parameters, we

(1)

solve the inner problems (
7.51
) and (
7.52
) for
\phi^{0}
and
\phi^{\perp}
using the linear theory in Proposition
6.1
,

(2)

solve the outer problems (
7.49
) and (
7.50
) for
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
using the linear theories in Propositions
5.1
and
5.5
,

(3)

update the parameters
\mu
and
\xi
by solving the reduced equations (
7.42
) and (
7.37
) derived from the orthogonality conditions.

This procedure defines the components of our fixed-point operator
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
as follows:

(7.56)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(v)=(\mathcal{F}_{\phi^{0}}(v),\mathcal{F}_{\phi^{\perp}}(v),\mathcal{F}_{\psi_{1}}(v),\mathcal{F}_{\psi_{2}}(v),\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}(v),\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{1}}(v),\mathcal{F}_{\xi}(v)),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\phi^{0}}(\phi^{0},\phi^{\perp},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\kappa,\mu_{1},\xi^{1})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{0}(\mathcal{H}^{0}_{1}[\mu,\xi,\psi_{1},\psi_{2},Z^{*}],\mathcal{H}^{0}_{2}[\mu,\xi,\psi_{1},\psi_{2},Z^{*}]+c^{0}[\mathcal{H}^{0}_{1},\mathcal{H}^{0}_{2}]Z_{4}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\phi^{\perp}}(\phi^{0},\phi^{\perp},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\kappa,\mu_{1},\xi^{1})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{\perp}\left(\mathcal{H}_{1}^{\perp}[\mu,\xi,\psi_{1},\psi_{2},Z^{*}],\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\perp}[\mu,\xi,\psi_{1},\psi_{2},Z^{*}]+\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{3}c^{\ell}[\mathcal{H}^{\perp}_{1},\mathcal{H}^{\perp}_{2}]Z_{\ell}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\psi_{1}}(\phi^{0},\phi^{\perp},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\kappa,\mu_{1},\xi^{1})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{\psi_{1}}\left(\mathcal{G}_{1}(\phi^{0}+\phi^{\perp},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},Z^{*},\mu,\xi)\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\psi_{2}}(\phi^{0},\phi^{\perp},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\kappa,\mu_{1},\xi^{1})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{\psi_{2}}\left(\mathcal{G}_{2}(\phi^{0}+\phi^{\perp},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},Z^{*},\mu,\xi)\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}(\phi^{0},\phi^{\perp},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\kappa,\mu_{1},\xi^{1})

\displaystyle=\kappa\left[a^{0}[\mu,\xi,\psi_{1},\psi_{2},Z^{*}]\right],

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{1}}(\phi^{0},\phi^{\perp},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\kappa,\mu_{1},\xi^{1})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{1}\left[a^{0}[\mu,\xi,\psi_{1},\psi_{2},Z^{*}]\right],

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\xi}(\phi^{0},\phi^{\perp},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\kappa,\mu_{1},\xi^{1})

\displaystyle=\Xi(\phi^{0},\phi^{\perp},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\mu,\xi).

Here,
\mathcal{T}_{0}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\perp}
are the solution operators for the inner problems (Proposition
6.1
),
\mathcal{T}_{\psi_{1}}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\psi_{2}}
solve the outer problems (Propositions
5.1
and
5.5
), while
\kappa[a]
,
\mathcal{P}_{1}
, and
\Xi
are the parameter-updating operators (which determine the new values of
\mu
and
\xi
from the orthogonality conditions) defined in Proposition
7.1
, (
7.47
), and (
7.39
), respectively.

A fixed point of
\mathcal{F}
, i.e., a point
v
such that
v=\mathcal{F}(v)
, corresponds to a solution of the coupled system (
7.49
)-(
7.54
).
We now verify that the operator
\mathcal{F}
defined in (
7.56
) with components

(7.57)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\left(\mathcal{F}_{\phi^{0}},\mathcal{F}_{\phi^{\perp}},\mathcal{F}_{\psi_{1}},\mathcal{F}_{\psi_{2}},\mathcal{F}_{\kappa},\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{1}},\mathcal{F}_{\xi}\right)

satisfies the conditions of Schauder’s fixed-point theorem in the closed ball
\mathcal{B}
defined by (
7.55
).

Combining the estimates (
7.14
), (
7.17
), (
7.2.2
), (
7.2.2
), (
7.2.2
), (
7.2.2
), (

) and (

,
5.1
,

, and
7.1
, we obtain for any
(\phi^{0},\phi^{\perp},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\kappa,\mu_{1},\xi^{1})\in\mathcal{B}
,

(7.58)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\|\mathcal{F}_{\phi^{0}}(\phi^{0},\phi^{\perp},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\kappa,\mu_{1},\xi^{1})\|_{\sigma_{1},\nu}\leq CT^{\epsilon},\\
\|\mathcal{F}_{\phi^{\perp}}(\phi^{0},\phi^{\perp},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\kappa,\mu_{1},\xi^{1})\|_{1+\sigma_{2},\nu}\leq CT^{\epsilon},\\
\|\mathcal{F}_{\psi_{1}}(\phi^{0},\phi^{\perp},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\kappa,\mu_{1},\xi^{1})\|_{*}^{(1)}\leq CT^{\epsilon},\\
\|\mathcal{F}_{\psi_{2}}(\phi^{0},\phi^{\perp},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\kappa,\mu_{1},\xi^{1})\|_{*}^{(2)}\leq CT^{\epsilon},\\
|\mathcal{F}_{\kappa}(\phi^{0},\phi^{\perp},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\kappa,\mu_{1},\xi^{1})-\kappa_{0}|\leq C|\log T|^{-1},\\
\|\mathcal{F}_{\mu_{1}}(\phi^{0},\phi^{\perp},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\kappa,\mu_{1},\xi^{1})\|_{\ast,3-\epsilon}\leq C|\log T|^{1-\epsilon}\log^{2}(|\log T|),\\
\|\mathcal{F}_{\xi}(\phi^{0},\phi^{\perp},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\kappa,\mu_{1},\xi^{1})\|_{G}\leq CT^{\epsilon},\end{cases}

where
C>0
is independent of
T
, and
\epsilon>0
is sufficiently small. This establishes that
\mathcal{F}
maps
\mathcal{B}
into itself.

The compactness of
\mathcal{F}
follows from continuity and the Arzel
\grave{a}
-Ascoli theorem. Indeed, for parameters
\beta,\alpha,\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\nu,\nu_{1},\nu_{2}
slightly perturbed while maintaining (
7.15
), (
7.18
), (
7.25
), (

7.58

\mathcal{B}
remains unchanged. More precisely, for fixed
\nu^{\prime},\sigma_{1}^{\prime}
close to
\nu,\sigma_{1}
, one may show that if
(\phi^{0},\phi^{\perp},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\kappa,\mu_{1},\xi^{1})\in\mathcal{B}

\|\mathcal{F}_{\phi^{0}}(\phi^{0},\phi^{\perp},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\kappa,\mu_{1},\xi^{1})\|_{\sigma_{1}^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}}\leq CT^{\epsilon^{\prime}}.

Moreover, for
\nu^{\prime}>\nu

\nu^{\prime}-\beta\left(2-\frac{\sigma_{1}^{\prime}}{2}\right)>\nu-\beta\left(2-\frac{\sigma_{1}}{2}\right),

the embedding
\|\cdot\|_{\sigma_{1}^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}}\hookrightarrow\|\cdot\|_{\sigma_{1},\nu}
is compact. Since any bounded sequence in the
\|\cdot\|_{\sigma_{1}^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}}
-norm admits a subsequence converging in the
\|\cdot\|_{\sigma_{1},\nu}
-norm, a standard diagonal argument using Arzelà-Ascoli theorem establishes the compactness of
\mathcal{F}
. Similar reasoning applies to the remaining components.

7.4.3.
Completion of the proof

Since
\mathcal{B}
is a nonempty, closed, bounded, and convex subset of
\mathcal{X}
, and
\mathcal{F}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}
is compact, Schauder’s fixed-point theorem guarantees the existence of a fixed point
v^{*}=\mathcal{F}(v^{*})
. This corresponds to a solution of the system (
7.49
)-(
7.54
), which yields the desired blow-up solution for the case
k=1
via the decompositions
\psi=\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}
and
\phi=\phi^{0}+\phi^{\perp}
.

The multi-bubble case follows an analogous construction. We consider the modified ansatz

u(x,t)=u^{*}(x,t)+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}^{-1}(t)\eta_{R(t)}(y_{j})\phi_{j}(y_{j},t)+Z^{*}(x,t)+\psi(x,t),~y_{j}=\frac{x-\xi_{j}(t)}{\mu_{j}(t)},

where the leading profile is given by

u^{*}(x,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}U_{\mu_{j}(t),\xi_{j}(t)}+\Psi_{0j}(x,t)

with
\Psi_{0j}
defined as in (
2.7
) under the replacements
\mu
to
\mu_{j}
,
\xi
to
\xi_{j}
.
This leads to one outer problem and
k
inner problems with estimates identical to the single-bubble case. The corresponding system of fixed-point problems can be solved in the same manner, and we omit the details.

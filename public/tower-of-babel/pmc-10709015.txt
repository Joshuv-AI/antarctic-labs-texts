Taurine alleviates high-fat-high-glucose-induced pancreatic islet β-cell oxidative stress and apoptosis in rat
Authors: Dongdong Zhao, Deqi Yin, Xinxin Wang, Yanxi Li, Miao He, Jianmin Hu, Lin Shumei, Jiancheng Yang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe effect of taurine (TAU) as a specific regulatory mediator on pancreatic function in obese rats induced by a high-fat-high-glucose (HFHG) diet was investigated. We fed male Sprague-Dawley rats under different conditions, namely the control, HFHG, TAU, and HFHG + TAU treatment groups for 4 months. Compared with the HFHG group, TAU supplementation significantly reduced malondialdehyde levels and increased superoxide dismutase, total antioxidant capacity, and glutathione levels in the rat pancreas. In addition, TAU significantly decreased the level of reactive oxygen species, and markedly increased the activity of heme oxygenase 1 (HO-1), Kelch-like ECH-associated protein 1 (KEAP-1), and nuclear factor erythrocyte-2-related factor 2 (Nrf2) in the rat pancreas. Notably, HFHG diet could induce pancreatic injury in the rats through the Nrf2/HO-1 signaling pathway and activate the mitochondrial channel-mediated apoptotic signaling pathway. The addition of TAU significantly improved the pancreatic tissue injury induced by the HFHG diet in the rats and reduced the protein expression of Caspase-3, Cleaved-caspase-3, Caspase-9 and Bcl-2 associated protein X (BAX), and increased the protein expression of B-cell lymphoma-2 (Bcl-2). In conclusion, this experiment confirmed that TAU could alleviate the oxidative stress and apoptosis induced by the HFHG diet in rat pancreatic β-cells.

Introduction

According to the International Diabetes Federation statistics, more than 500 million people worldwide have diabetes, which represents more than 10.5 % of the world's adult population [1]. It is estimated to cost 850 billion dollars annually [2], and is a risk factor in triggering other diseases (including cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, and stroke) [3]. Diabetes mellitus can be categorized into type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM), which is insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus, mainly characterized by autoimmune-related β-cell destruction, and type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), which is insulin-nondependent diabetes mellitus, and mainly manifests as a β-cell dysfunction. The clinical manifestation of diabetes is hyperglycemia [4], mainly caused by insufficient insulin release from the pancreatic β-cells [5]. The root cause of insulin deficiency lies in β-cell loss. Patients with T1DM experience a 70–85 % reduction in β-cell abundance relative to patients without diabetes [[6], [7], [8]], and patients with T2DM experience a 30–63 % reduction in β-cell volume [[9], [10], [11], [12]].

T2DM is a progressive metabolic disease syndrome, which begins with peripheral insulin resistance and ends with the exhaustion of the islet β-cells; it accounts for approximately 90 % of all diabetes. Both insulin resistance and destruction of the β-cells are related to genetic and environmental factors, especially the excessive intake of high-fat-high-glucose (HFHG) foods. HFHG diet is a risk factor for obesity and insulin resistance; in addition, a chronic HFHG diet will also produce “glucotoxicity” and “lipotoxicity” for islet β-cells, which leads to the loss of β-cells. A long-term study using rhesus monkeys found that compared with a regular diet, energy-restricted diets reduced the prevalence of age-related diseases, which included diabetes [13]. In the final stage of the study, 40 % of the rhesus monkeys with regular diets developed diabetes or prediabetes. Surprisingly, none of the rhesus monkeys with the restricted diet had abnormal glucose tolerance. Previous research results have shown that prolonged HFHG diets can cause obesity, elevated blood glucose levels, and insulin resistance in rats. Promoting pancreatic islets β cells undergo endoplasmic reticulum stress, which leads to pancreatic islets β-cells apoptosis. Taurine can reduce pancreatic islets by alleviating Endoplasmic reticulum stress β-cells apoptosis [14]. The pathogenesis of T2DM is complex and closely related to endoplasmic reticulum stress, mitochondrial oxidative stress, and cell apoptosis, but the specific mechanism is not yet clear. Especially mitochondria, which are pancreatic islets β-cells Key Organelle for cell survival and function [15]. In short, obesity, overnutrition, inactivity, mental stress, and genetic factors are all risk factors for T2DM, while oxidative stress may be the various common pathways of T2DM caused by the risk factors.

Cells can obtain oxygen through respiration, 98 % of which is combined with glucose and fat in the organelles and converted into energy for cellular activity, and the other 2 % is converted into reactive oxygen species (ROS). Because this substance is very active, it can interact with various substances, causing a series of destructive chain reactions in cells. Normally, cells are not destroyed by this molecular killer because cells have a large number of ROS antioxidants, such as superoxide dismutase (SOD), glutathione peroxidase (GSH-PX), and catalase (CAT), which can react with ROS to eliminate it. In previous studies, the protein levels of antioxidant enzymes, such as CAT, SOD, and GSH-PX, were approximately 30 % of that of the liver in the islet tissue [16,17]. This evidence suggested that β-cells are especially sensitive to the damage caused by ROS and that the protection of β-cells from oxidative damage is to inhibit ROS caused by hyperglycemia or hyperlipidemia.

Taurine (TAU), one of the body's richest amino acids, exists in almost all excitable tissue cells. It has a wide range of biological functions, including antioxidation, osmotic adjustment, membrane stability, bile acid binding, neuromodulation, detoxification, and regulation of calcium homeostasis. Taurine is an conditionally essential amino acid that possesses an anti-oxidative effect, which may contribute to the reduction of aging [10]. Prevention and therapeutic TAU supplementation have shown beneficial effects on oxidative stress-induced diseases, including hepatotoxicity and liver injury chaos, kidney disease, retinal degeneration, epilepsy, cardiomyopathy, growth retardation, Alzheimer's disease, and diabetes [18]. The main objective of this project is to study the antioxidant effects of taurine and its inhibitory effect on pancreatic β-cell apoptosis induced by HFHG food. Through long-term induction of HFHG diet in animal models to simulate the conditions of HFHG levels in the human body, we observed the impact of taurine on β-cell apoptosis and studied its modulation of antioxidant stress. This research will contribute to a deeper understanding of the pathogenesis of diabetes and explore new treatment strategies.

Materials and methods

Chemicals and animals

SPF grade male Sprague Dawley (SD) rats (140–160g) were obtained from Liaoning Changsheng Biotechnology Co. Ltd. The light cycle was 12 h per day, the room was ventilated, and the temperature was 22 °C with 50 % humidity. The food formulations for the HFHG diet are shown in Supplementary Table 1. Purchased from SYSE Bio-Tec. Co., Ltd. (Changzhou, China). TAU (purity >99 %) (Huaheng, Hebei, China) was obtained.

Experimental design

We divided the male SD rats into four groups (six rats in each group), namely the control, HFHG, TAU, and HFHG + TAU groups, and the duration of the experiment was 4 months, the control group was fed a basal diet with free access to water, the TAU group was fed a basal diet with free access to 2 % (2 g taurine/100 mL water) taurine water, the HFHG group was fed a HFHG diet with free access to water and The HFHG + TAU group was fed a HFHG diet with free access to 2 % taurine water [19]. All SD rats were fasted from 9 p.m. to 9 a.m. the following day [20]. Fasting blood glucose (FBG) levels and body weights were measured. Detailed data on body weight, blood glucose and glucose tolerance during the survey period can be found in our previous publication [14].

Sample preparation and detection of oxidative stress indicators

Anaesthesia with ethyl ether before dissecting rats, the pancreatic tissues were removed and placed on ice, cut into pieces with ophthalmic scissors, and then tissue homogenization was performed. The fresh rat pancreas was tested according to the instructions of the Total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC) assay kit (cat. No. A015-3-1), Malondialdehyde (MDA) assay kit (cat. No. A003-1), Reduced glutathione (GSH) assay kit (cat. No. A006-2-1), and Superoxide Dismutase (SOD) assay kit (cat. No. A001-3) from the Nanjing Jiancheng Institute of Biological Engineering.

Histopathological observation

The paraffin section were rewarmed at room temperature and circles were drawn around the tissues using a histochemical pen. Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining was performed as described previously [21]. The samples were observed under a microscope (Leica, Cologne, Germany) and photographed for storage. The histological score was graded according to the standard described in a previous report [22].

Fluorescence detection assay for oxidative stress-related protein

The fresh pancreatic tissues were washed with saline and placed in a 4 % formaldehyde fixative solution. Pancreas from six rats in each group were completely fixed for 24 h, dehydrated, made transparent, wax-dipped and embedded, and then cut into 4-μm-thick sections according to the instructions of the Nanjing Jiancheng Institute of Bioengineering ROS assay kit (cat. No. E004-1-1). A Leica DM750 microscope (Leica, Beijing, China) was used to observe the changes in the oxidative stress indicators.

Detection of terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end labeling (TUNEL) assay

Paraffin slices from the pancreatic tissues were collected from the six rats in each group. Apoptosis was detected according to the instructions of the TUNEL Assay Kit (No. A111-01, Vazyme, Nanjing, China). Apoptosis was analyzed using the Aperio ImageScope software (Leica Biosystems Imaging, Inc. California, USA).

Western blotting

Tissue homogenates were obtained after homogenization of the fresh pancreatic tissues, and total pancreatic proteins were obtained using centrifugation at 4 °C. The primary antibodies include anti-Nrf2 (cat. No. BS6286, Bioworld, USA), anti–HO–1 (cat. No. Ab68477, Abcam, USA), anti-KEAP-1 (cat. No. A00514-3, Boster Bioengineering Co. Ltd., Wuhan, China), anti-Caspase-9 (cat. No. Ab25758, Abcam, USA), anti-Caspase-3 (cat. No. Ab44976, Abcam, USA), anti-Cleaved-caspase-3 (cat. No. 9661S, CST, USA), anti-BAX (cat. No. Ab32503, Abcam, USA), anti-Bcl-2 (cat. No. Ab59348, Abcam, USA), and anti-Actin (excellent Ningwei, Shanghai, China). The secondary antibody was used with HRP-labeled goat anti-rabbit IgG (cat. No.7074P2, CST, USA). The intensity of the bands was quantified using Image J software. β-Actin was standardized for the analysis.

Statistical analysis

The data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (mean ± SD). The data were analyzed using SPSS 13.0 software and histograms were plotted using GraphPad Prism v7.0 software. Differences in the data between different groups were analyzed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) for pairwise comparisons, and the differences were considered significant at P < 0.05.

Results

Biochemical index test results of pancreatic tissue

ROS are capable of mediating the physiological functions of organisms, and are closely associated with a variety of diseases [23]. In comparison with the control group, the pancreatic ROS concentration was significantly higher in the HFHG group rats (Fig. 1A). In addition, fluorescence diminished significantly in the HFHG + TAU group compared with the HFHG group.

Test results of oxidative stress indicators in pancreatic tissue. A) Results of ROS staining in rat pancreas. B.C.D.E) Determination of malondialdehyde (MDA), superoxide dismutase (SOD), total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC) and glutathione peroxidase (GSH-PX) content. The data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) from six independent experiments (*P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001) (n = 6).

Relative to the control group, the MDA activity of the pancreatic tissue in the HFHG group increased significantly (P < 0.01). In addition, the MDA activity was significantly lower in the HFHG + TAU group than in the HFHG group (P < 0.01). This result confirmed that adding TAU significantly reduced the elevation of MDA activity induced by the HFHG diet in rats (Fig. 1B). The SOD activity of the pancreatic tissue was significantly lower in the HFHG group than in the control group. In addition, the SOD activity of the pancreatic tissue was significantly higher in the HFHG + TAU group than in the HFHG group (P < 0.01) (Fig. 1C). T-AOC and GSH activities were significantly lower in the HFHG group than in the control group. In addition, T-AOC and GSH activities in the pancreas of the HFHG + TAU group were significantly higher than those in the HFHG group (Fig. 1D and E). The results showed that TAU could alleviate the oxidative stress of the rat pancreas induced by the HFHG diet.

Pathological observation of rat pancreatic tissue

As shown in Fig. 2A, the pancreatic tissue structure of the average rat pancreatic acini in the endocrine and exocrine parts is visible. The connective tissue between the islet cells and between the pancreatic lobules in the pancreatic tissue of HFHG rats has an accumulation of adipocytes. In addition, some acinar cells showed steatosis, the volume of the acinar cells increased, the cytoplasmic structure was unclear, and many small vacuoles were visible.

The morphological changes in the islets greatly improved in the HFHG + TAU group compared with those in the HFHG group. Therefore, TAU can reduce the damage of pancreatic tissues in rats by the HFHG diet. Projection electron microscope showed that the number of mitochondrial cristae and mitochondrial swelling in the islet β-cells of the HFHG group decreased in comparison with that in the control group. In addition, compared with the HFHG group, the mitochondrial cristae of the HFHG + TAU group were significantly improved. The results of the electron microscopy showed that TAU could enhance the damage of rat mitochondria caused by the HFHG diet (Fig. 2B).

Results of tests related to oxidative stress levels

KEAP 1, HO-1, and Nrf 2 are essential regulators in the cellular oxidative stress response and are important indicators of the oxidative stress status of the body [24]. The results of the fluorescence detection experiments of oxidative stress-related proteins showed that the fluorescence intensity of HO-1, KEAP-1, and Nrf2 was significantly higher in the pancreas of the HFHG + TAU group than in the HFHG group (Fig. 3A–C). These results indicated that TAU could reduce the damage to the pancreas caused by HFHG diet in rats.

As illustrated in Fig. 3D–F, the protein level of KEAP-1 in the HFHG + TAU group was substantially higher than that in the HFHG group (P < 0.01). We also found that the levels of HO-1 and Nrf2 proteins increased significantly (P < 0.01). The results showed that TAU administration attenuated oxidative damage to the rat pancreas caused by the HFHG diet via the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway.

Apoptosis signaling pathway test results

The apoptosis rate of the pancreatic cells increased significantly in the HFHG group compared with that of the control group (P < 0.01) (Fig. 4A). A significantly lower number of apoptotic cells occurred in the pancreas in the HFHG + TAU and TAU groups than in the HFHG group (P < 0.01). The results suggest that the HFHG diet induced severe apoptosis in rat islet β-cells, while TAU ameliorated this phenomenon of HFHG-induced apoptosis in the pancreatic cells.

Analysis of TUNEL apoptosis assay and expression levels of proteins associated with apoptotic pathways in rat pancreas. A) Detection of TUNEL in rat pancreatic cells. B) Protein expression level analysis of the BAX and Bcl-2 proteins. C) Western-blot results for Cleaved-caspase-3 and Caspase-9 proteins. The uncropped versions of figures are shown in Supplementary Fig. 2, Values are expressed as mean ± SD (n = 3).

The proapoptotic Bcl-2-associated X protein (BAX) and the antiapoptotic protein β-cell lymphoma-2 (Bcl-2) play key roles in regulating cellular commitment to mitochondrial apoptosis [25]. Moreover, the levels of the Bcl-2 and BAX proteins increased in the pancreatic tissue of the rats in the HFHG group compared with those of the control group (Fig. 4B). The protein level of the Cleaved-caspase-3 protein increased significantly (P < 0.05); Caspase-9 protein level also increased significantly (P < 0.01). Compared with HFHG, the protein level of Bcl-2/BAX in the pancreas of rats in the HFHG + TAU group increased significantly (P < 0.01). The protein level of the Cleaved-caspase-3 reduced significantly (P < 0.05); the Caspase-9 protein level also decreased significantly (P < 0.01) (Fig. 4C). Therefore, the HFHG diet can induce pancreatic apoptosis through the mitochondrial apoptosis pathway, and TAU administration can effectively reduce pancreatic apoptosis in the HFHG diet.

Discussion

The pathological mechanism of T2DM is complex, and the molecular mechanism of cell damage has not been clarified, resulting in the lack of effective treatment strategies. The activity of the TAU transporter reduces under hyperglycemia and hyperinsulinemia, which means that the antioxidant capacity of the cells decreases [26]. Various studies have reported the relation between oxidative stress and the prognosis of diabetes and diabetes complications for both experimental models and human diabetes [[27], [28], [29]]. Although the development of T2DM is also associated with genetics and the environment, it is more closely related to lifestyle and diet, and is particularly associated with obesity. A crucial pathological mechanism for developing T2DM is insulin resistance and islet β-cell damage. One of the major causes of islet β-cell injury and insulin resistance in the peripheral tissues is oxidative stress [2]. Under conditions of obesity or persistent hyperglycemia, the production of superoxide products in the body increases rapidly and oxidative stress occurs when the speed of production of superoxide products exceeds the rate of their removal. Persistent hyperglycemia causes increased oxidative stress in the body and accelerates the production of free radicals, which further damage the pancreatic β-cells. Persistent hyperglycemia and oxidative stress both affect each other and are mutually causal.

The islet β-cell damage is one of the most important features of T2DM, but the mechanisms of islet β-cells damage have not been elucidated, leading to a lack of effective therapeutic strategies [30,31]. Studies have shown that glucolipotoxicity is the primary cause of islet β-cell injury in T2DM [[31], [32], [33]]. When pancreatic β-cells become dysfunctional, there is a decrease in cell numbers, apoptosis, and increased inflammatory response, resulting in impaired insulin secretion and consequently high glucose concentrations. When sustained elevated glucose concentrations exceed the physiological range, they lead to hyperglycemia [34]. The constant development of hyperglycemia and hyperinsulinemia can lead to islet β-cell failure [35]. T2DM is an impaired glucose regulation caused by insulin resistance and progressive damage to pancreatic islet β-cells and accounts for approximately 90 % of all diabetes. It is closely associated with obesity, with persistent hyperglycemia leading to complete islet β-cells failure. Evidence from prospective epidemiological studies, human genetics, and experimental validation suggest that glucolipotoxicity is the primary cause of islet β-cell damage in T2DM, but the underlying mechanisms remain unclear. Environmental factors are linked to the onset of T2DM, including obesity, hypertension, alcohol, smoking, and caffeine. T2DM is associated with a reduction in symptoms in 80 % of subjects after a 15-kg weight loss by restricting caloric intake [36]. Previous study has shown that TAU is a free β-amino acid containing sulfur with antioxidant and therapeutic value against several illnesses [37]. HFHG diet can inactivate antioxidant enzymes in the body, leading to the accumulation of peroxides and its effects, such as oxidative damage to cells and tissues when the ROS produced exceed the cellular scavenging capacity [38,39]. Our results confirmed that the HFHG diet induces oxidative stress damage in rat pancreatic β-cells, while TAU attenuates the HFHG diet-induced oxidative damage (Fig. 1A).

Oxidative stress is an imbalance between the cellular antioxidant and oxidative systems, producing an excess of ROS that constantly accumulates in the cells. Oxidative stress leads to an increase in the body's ROS content, such as hydroxyl radicals and superoxide anions. Too much ROS can damage nucleic acids and proteins and produce large amounts of MDA and other substances that induce tissue damage, leading to the development of disease [40]. With the excessive accumulation of oxygen free radicals in the cell, the cell membrane and mitochondria are damaged after breaking the homeostatic system of the cell, causing the cell to lose its energy support. The cell loses its energy support and eventually moves towards autolysis. The levels of MDA, SOD, and GSH-Px are important indicators of the body's oxidative stress response [41]. We found that TAU significantly reduced MDA production in rat pancreas and greatly increased the activities of T-AOC, SOD, and GSH-Px in this study. Combined with previously published data [14,42], we confirmed that TAU increased the antioxidant capacity of the pancreas and antagonized HFHG diet-induced oxidative stress in rats. KEAP-1, Nrf2, and HO-1 proteins are the main regulators of the KEAP-1-Nrf2/HO-1 signaling pathway, which are importantly linked to oxidative stress in organisms [43,44]. Oxygen stress-inducible HO-1 is an organism-protective antioxidant enzyme that effectively regulates the systemic inflammatory response under stress by inhibiting cytokines, such as interleukin 10 and tumor necrosis factor, thereby reducing liver damage during pancreatitis. Nrf2 is a key factor in the cellular oxidative stress response and a major regulator of downstream antioxidant gene expression. However, this protective effect fails when the damage exceeds the antioxidant capacity of endogenous Nrf2-ARE. In this study, we further observed that the levels of Nrf2 and KEAP-1 proteins reduced significantly in the pancreatic tissue of the HFHG group. This result confirmed that TAU can inhibit oxidative stress by inhibiting the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway, thereby attenuating the damage to the animal organism. Our results showed that the apoptosis rate of the pancreatic islet β-cells decreased significantly in HFHG rats fed TAU. These results suggest that adding TAU could attenuate HFHG-induced apoptosis in pancreatic islet β-cells. We further confirmed this result using western blotting.

In our previous research, we found that endoplasmic reticulum lacunae became swollen and enlarged Under conditions of HFHG diets stimulation. While observing this phenomenon, we also observed that the mitochondria of islet β cells swelled and the number of mitochondrial cristae in the islet β cells of the HFHG group decreased in comparison with that in the control group through transmission electron. HFHG diets cause endoplasmic reticulum-mitochondria stress in pancreatic β-cells, which may also simultaneously cause endoplasmic reticulum mitochondrial crosstalk-mediated apoptosis, ultimately leading to T2DM. HFHG can lead to the production of highly reactive oxygen radical molecules in rats to induce oxidative damage, which in turn leads to apoptosis in the animals. By giving TAU treatment to rats of the HFHG group, we found a significant decrease in the rate of apoptosis in the pancreas. Therefore, TAU improved HFHG-induced apoptosis in the rat pancreatic tissue. We further analyzed the proteins associated with the apoptosis pathways using western blotting. The Bcl-2 and BAX in the PI3K/AKT pathway are important regulators of the development of apoptosis [45,46]. In addition, Caspase-3 and Caspase-9 regulate biological processes through protein hydrolysis and have important links to mitochondrial channel-mediated apoptosis [47,48]. The addition of TAU significantly increased the protein levels of Bcl-2/BAX and decreased the protein levels of the Cleaved-caspase-3 and Caspase-9 in the rat pancreas. Thus, our experimental results confirmed that the HFHG diet causes apoptotic phenomena via the PI3K/AKT pathway and the mitochondria-mediated apoptotic signaling pathway (Fig. 5). Although islet β-cells make up only a small part of the entire pancreas, an improvement in the overall oxidative stress status of the animal is bound to have some protective effects on the islet β-cells. However, further studies are required to confirm whether 2 % TAU can be mediated through the signaling pathways mentioned above.

Taurine alleviates oxidative stress and apoptosis in pancreatic islet β cells induced by HFHG diet in rats. Taurine can alleviate mitochondrial oxidative stress induced by HFHG by acting on multiple targets, and reduce the apoptosis rate of pancreatic islet cells.

The limitation of this experiment is that only the oxidative stress-mediated partial islet β-cell apoptosis induced by feeding a HFHG diet for 4 consecutive months was examined, but its effect on the incidence of T2DM was not investigated. Another limitation of this study was the absence of a placebo group. Therefore, in future studies, we could determine the effect of taurine on the incidence of T2DM and its mechanism of action by prolonging the feeding of the HFHG diet, in addition to adding a placebo group to exclude the effect of the trial intervention on the results.

Conclusion

In summary, our study discusses the effects of a HFHG diet on oxidative stress and apoptosis in rat pancreatic β-cells, as well as the potential protective role of TAU. The HFHG diet is shown to inactivate antioxidant enzymes, leading to the accumulation of ROS and oxidative damage to cells and tissues. TAU is found to attenuate the HFHG diet-induced oxidative damage and decrease apoptosis in pancreatic islet β-cells. TAU is also observed to enhance antioxidant capacity, inhibit the Nrf2/HO-1 pathway, and regulate the PI3K/AKT and mitochondria-mediated apoptotic signaling pathways. Overall, the study suggested that TAU may serve as a feed additive by ameliorating oxidative stress and reducing pancreatic β-cell apoptosis in rats fed an HFHG diet. Further research is needed to investigate the exact molecular mechanisms involved.

Future stability of solutions of the Einstein-nonlinear scalar field system with decelerated expansion
Louie Bernhardt

Abstract
We study solutions to the Einstein equations coupled to a nonlinear scalar field with exponential potential. This system admits Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker solutions undergoing decelerated expansion, with 𝕋 3 \mathbb{T}^{3} spatial topology and scale factor a ⁡ ( t ) = t p a(t)=t^{p} for 1 / 3 < p < 1 1/3<p<1 . For each p ∈ ( 2 / 3 , 1 ) p\in(2/3,1) , we prove that the corresponding FLRW spacetime is future-stable as a solution to the Einstein-nonlinear scalar field system. Given initial data on a spacelike hypersurface that is sufficiently close to the FLRW data, we show the resulting solution is future-causal geodesically complete, and remains close to the FLRW solution for all time. Moreover, we show the perturbed metric components and scalar field converge to spatially homogeneous functions as t → ∞ t\rightarrow\infty . A key feature of our analysis is the decomposition of the metric and scalar field perturbations into their spatial averages and oscillatory remainders with zero average.

The Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) spacetimes are a family of Lorentzian manifolds that are fundamental objects in the study of cosmology

The Einstein-nonlinear scalar field system with exponential potential has thus far been studied in the regime of

A key difference between the regimes of accelerated and decelerated expansion is that their causal structures are very different. For spacetimes undergoing accelerated expansion, two late-time observers can eventually become causally disconnected, with their future domains of communication becoming disjoint after sufficient time.

This can also be interpreted as the existence of a

These properties assist in the analysis of such spacetimes, and in some cases turn global problems into entirely local ones
[

In contrast, such localisation does not occur in spacetimes with decelerated expansion. In the above FLRW solutions with

This all raises the question of whether decelerated FLRW spacetimes are stable. In this paper, we show that these decelerated spacetimes are indeed stable in the range of expansion exponents

Theorem 1.3

(Future nonlinear stability of decelerated solutions of the Einstein-nonlinear scalar field system)
.

Fix the spacetime exponent
2/3<p<1

((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3},\widetilde{g}_{b},\phi_{b})

1.5
) that solves the Einstein-nonlinear scalar field system (
1.2

1.4

(\mathcal{M},\widetilde{g},\phi)

(\widetilde{g}_{b},\phi_{b})
has a maximal globally hyperbolic development which is future-causal geodesically complete.

The metric components
\widetilde{g}_{tt}

\widetilde{g}_{it}:i=1,2,3

|\widetilde{g}_{tt}+1|\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{3p-2}},\hskip 20.00003pt\frac{1}{t^{p}}|\widetilde{g}_{it}|\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{2p-1-\delta}},\hskip 20.00003pt\frac{1}{\log t}|\phi-\phi_{b}|\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{3p-2+\delta}},

(1.6)

where

\widetilde{g}_{ij}:i,j=1,2,3
,
there exists a symmetric, positive-definite
3\times 3

that is close to the identity matrix, and satisfies

|t^{-2p}\widetilde{g}_{ij}-(g_{\infty})_{ij}|\lesssim\frac{1}{t^{2p-1-\delta}}.

(1.7)

Remark 1.5

(Leading-order asymptotics)

In the asymptotic estimates (

2

This is not immediately clear for the metric shift components

\widetilde{g}_{tt}

\widetilde{g}_{it}

Remark 1.6

(Higher-order asymptotics)

Theorem

In fact, we do not expect such an expansion to exists, as an aspect of the decelerated setting is higher-order oscillatory behaviour. This behaviour plays an important role in our analysis which we will explain further in Section
1.2

1.1
Related work

In the following we discuss some of the previous results in the literature, and how they relate to the present work.

Spacetimes undergoing decelerated expansion.

Theorem
1.3
is the first stability statement for decelerated spacetimes without additional symmetry assumptions. The recent work
[
Tay24
]
studies the decelerated solutions to the Einstein-massless Vlasov system in spherical symmetry. The Einstein-massless Vlasov system describes the dynamics of an ensemble of massless, gravitationally attractive particles, and admits spatially homogeneous FLRW solutions with scale factor
a(t)=t^{1/2}
. In
[
Tay24
]
spherically symmetric perturbations of these FLRW spacetimes (with
\mathbb{R}^{3}
spatial topology) are shown to be future-stable. We note that the noncompact spatial topology leads to additional decay via dispersion, and so we expect the spatially non-compact setting to have improved stability in comparison to the spatially compact setting.

The stability of spacetimes undergoing decelerated expansion has also been investigated numerically, in the context of the Einstein-Euler system
[
Mar25
]
. This system models a self-gravitating perfect fluid, and admits an FLRW solution with
\mathbb{T}^{3}
spatial topology undergoing decelerated expansion, provided the fluid satisfies a linear equation of state
P=c_{s}^{2}\rho
, where
P
is the fluid pressure,
\rho
is the fluid density, and
c_{s}
is the fluid sound speed. For a given sound speed
c_{s}\in[0,1]
, the corresponding FLRW solution has a fixed expansion exponent in the range
1/3\leq p\leq 2/3
.
3
3

3

The precise relation between the sound speed
c_{s}
and expansion exponent
p
is
p=\frac{2}{3}(1+c_{s}^{2})^{-1}
, from which one can see that if
c_{s}^{2}\in[0,1]
then
p\in[1/3,2/3]
. Some important equations of state are (i) the
dust
model with
c_{s}=0
,
p=2/3
, the
radiation
model with
c_{s}=1/\sqrt{3}
,
p=1/2
, and the
stiff fluid
model with
c_{s}=1
,
p=1/3
.
Numerical simulations carried out in
[
Mar25
]
indicate that for all sound speeds
c_{s}\in[0,1]
, the FLRW solutions with
\mathbb{T}^{3}
-spatial topology are
unstable
as solutions to the Einstein-Euler system, with shocks forming in finite time for arbitrarily small perturbations of the FLRW solutions. We discuss this result and how it relates to the present paper in more detail in Section
1.3
.

Related hyperbolic equations have been studied on
fixed
decelerated FLRW backgrounds. The linear wave equation on FLRW spacetimes undergoing decelerated expansion has been studied in the works
[
KS81
,
NR23
,
Hag25
]
. The relativistic Euler equations on a fixed spacetime undergoing decelerated expansion have been considered in
[
Spe13
,
FOOW25
]
.

Stability for linearly expanding spacetimes.

Our result does not include the endpoint
p=1
case corresponding to
linear
expansion, meaning we do not prove the stability of linearly expanding FLRW solutions to the ENSF system. This specific problem is open, although linearly expanding solutions to the Einstein equations have been shown to be stable outside of symmetry. The first such result was
[
AM11
]
, in which Andersson and Moncrief proved that the
Milne universe
, a linearly expanding spacetime with negative spatial Einstein geometry, is stable as a solution to the Einstein-vacuum equations. This has been extended in
[
AF20
]
to include perturbations of Milne that contain Vlasov-type matter. We also mention
[
FOW24
]
which demonstrates stability of a class of linearly expanding FLRW solutions to the Einstein-dust system. Like the Milne model, these spacetimes also possess negative spatial curvature, and indeed this property is used in a fundamental way in all three papers. The spacetimes for which we prove stability do not have negative spatial geometry, and so the methods of proof here are different to those in
[
AM11
,
AF20
,
FOW24
]
.

Stability in the accelerated expansion regime.

In the setting of accelerated expansion, much more is understood regarding stability. We have already mentioned the works
[
HR07
,
Rin09b
]
which consider the stability of accelerated FLRW spacetimes to the ENSF system. We also mention Luo and Isenberg’s
[
LI13
]
, in which Ringström’s stability result is generalised by coupling the ENSF system to an electromagnetic field satisfying Maxwell’s equations. Stability of the FLRW solution is then proved for the Einstein-nonlinear scalar field-Maxwell system.

Another mechanism for accelerated expansion is the inclusion of a positive cosmological constant in Einstein’s vacuum equations, which produces spacetimes which expand at an
exponential
rate. There is a rich and growing literature concerning the stability of solutions to the Einstein equations with positive cosmological constant, for an incomplete list see
[
Fri91
,
And04
,
LVK13
,
RS13
,
Spe12
,
HS15
,
Oli16
,
HV18
,
Fou22
,
Fan25
,
FMO24
,
FS24
,
HV24
,
Cic24
]
.

Study of the nonlinear stability of solutions to the Einstein vacuum equations with positive cosmological constant began with Friedrich’s proof of stability of
3+1
-de Sitter in
[
Fri86
]
. In that work, Friedrich introduced a formalism of Einstein’s equations known as the conformal field equations, which reduces the global problem to an entirely local one. We also mention
[
Rin08
]
, in which Ringström proved stability of de Sitter in general spacetime dimensions as a solution to the Einstein-nonlinear scalar field equations with a positive well-like potential. This work introduced the idea of fixing a choice of generalised wave coordinates with gauge source functions that capture the expansion of the spacetime; we will discuss this in more detail in Section
1.2
.

1.2
Outline of the proof of global existence

The key components of the proof of Theorem
1.3
are (i) a reduction of the Einstein-nonlinear scalar field system into a system of quasilinear wave equations by fixing a certain choice of wave coordinates, (ii) a decomposition of the metric components and scalar field into their spatial averages and oscillatory remainders, along with a derivation of decoupled systems for these quantities, (iii) ODE estimates for the spatial averages, and (iv) energy estimates for the oscillatory remainders. We discuss these steps in more detail below.

Wave coordinates.

We use wave coordinates (also known as
harmonic gauge
or
de Donder gauge
) in this paper
[
CB52
]
. This breaks the diffeomorphism invariance of the Einstein-equations and transforms them into a system of quasilinear wave equations for the metric components. The “standard” choice of wave coordinates is to fix coordinates
\{x^{\mu}\}_{\mu=0,1,2,3}
such that
\square_{\widetilde{g}}x^{\mu}=0
. This is equivalent to setting
\Gamma^{\mu}=0
,
\mu=0,1,2,3
, where
\Gamma^{\mu}=\widetilde{g}^{\alpha\beta}\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{\mu}(\widetilde{g})
are contracted Christoffel symbols. Wave coordinates were used most prominently in Choquet-Bruhat’s proof of the local well-posedness of the Einstein vacuum equations
[
CB52
]
. A key observation made by Choquet-Bruhat was that the wave coordinate condition
\Gamma^{\mu}=0
is propagated by the gauge-fixed Einstein vacuum equations, so that this condition is preserved provided it is satisfied by the initial data.

Wave coordinates have also proved useful in understanding the global theory of Einstein’s equations. This was exemplified by Lindblad and Rodnianski’s
[
LR10
]
, where they proved stability of Minkowski in wave coordinates, see also Christodoulou and Klainerman’s original proof
[
CK93
]
.

Gauge source functions.

It has been shown that in the setting of spacetime expansion, a more general form of wave coordinates can be useful. These amount to adding “gauge source functions” and fixing
\Gamma^{\mu}=\mathcal{Y}^{\mu}
. The source functions
\mathcal{Y}^{\mu}
can depend on the coordinate functions
x^{\mu}
, and the metric components
\widetilde{g}
, but
not
on any derivatives of the metric. Gauge source functions can capture the spacetime’s expansion, in a certain sense, and induce decay in the resulting system. Gauge source functions were used to this effect in Ringström’s
[
Rin08
]
, in which asymptotically de Sitter spacetimes were shown to be future-stable as solutions to the Einstein-nonlinear scalar field system with a positive well-type potential. Ringström’s proof of stability of FLRW spacetimes undergoing accelerated expansion built on this, and also used a generalized form of wave coordinates
[
Rin09b
]
. Numerous works since have applied this method in the cosmological setting; see for example
[
RS13
,
Spe12
,
HS15
,
Oli16
,
HV24
]
.

The conformal metric
g
.

Before fixing wave coordinates, we reformulate the Einstein equations as equations for a conformally rescaled metric. To justify this, we consider a background FLRW spacetime
\widetilde{g}_{b}
of the form given in (
1.5a
), writing

\widetilde{g}_{b}=t^{2p}\Big[-(t^{-p}\mathrm{d}t)^{2}+\delta_{ij}\mathrm{d}x^{i}\mathrm{d}x^{j}\Big].

(1.8)

Then we change to a conformal time coordinate
\tau
so that
\mathrm{d}\tau=(1-p)t^{-p}\mathrm{d}t
. Integrating, it follows that

\tau(t)=t^{1-p},

(1.9)

and so
\tau\in(0,\infty)
. The background metric then takes the form

\widetilde{g}_{b}=\tau^{2p/(1-p)}\big[-(1-p)^{-2}\mathrm{d}\tau^{2}+\delta_{ij}\mathrm{d}x^{i}\mathrm{d}x^{j}\big].

(1.10)

Thus the conformal metric
m=\tau^{-2p/(1-p)}\widetilde{g}_{b}
is the flat Minkowski metric. We take advantage of this structure and derive equations for the conformal metric

g:=\tau^{-2p/(1-p)}\widetilde{g}

(1.11)

in
(\tau,x)
coordinates. This rescales the leading-order asymptotics of the perturbed metric components so that they are small perturbations of their Minkowski counterparts.

Remark 1.7
.

We point out that the conformal coordinate
\tau
and associated conformal transformation do not compactify the spacetime. In particular we have
\tau(t=0)=0
and
\tau(t=\infty)=\infty
, so intervals of the form
[t_{0},\infty)
do not become compact intervals in
\tau
. This stands in contrast to spacetimes undergoing accelerated expansion, where the same process results in a
conformal compactification
of the spacetime.
4
4

4

Since conformal transformations map null curves to null curves, this is another way one can show that causal observers on accelerated spacetimes can become causally disconnected, while observers on decelerated (or linearly expanding) spacetimes cannot.

On a related note, while our setup reduces the ENSF system to equations for a conformal metric, we emphasise that our analysis does not appeal to the conformal field equations of Friedrich
[
Fri86
]
. The conformal factor is a fixed function of the time coordinate, not an evolution variable. This approach can also be found in
[
Oli16
]
.

Additionally, we formulate the conformal system so that they constitute equations for the
inverse
metric components
g^{\mu\nu}:=(g^{-1})^{\mu\nu}
. This is conceptually equivalent to analysing the lowered metric components, and simplifies the form of the resulting equations. We denote the metric and scalar field perturbations by

h^{\mu\nu}:=g^{\mu\nu}-m^{\mu\nu},\hskip 20.00003pt\psi:=\phi-\phi_{b}.

(1.12)

The gauge-fixed system.

We derive the gauge-fixed system for the conformal metric and scalar field perturbations
(h^{\mu\nu},\psi)
in
Section
3
. We fix wave coordinates with a particular choice of gauge source functions
\mathcal{Y}^{\mu}
(see (
3.29
) for the specific choice). The resulting equations for the
(h^{\mu\nu},\psi)
are quasilinear wave equations, schematically of the form

\square_{g(u)}u=-\frac{a}{\tau}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}u-\frac{b}{\tau^{2}}g^{00}u+\mathcal{N},

(1.13)

for constants
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
and semilinear error term
\mathcal{N}
. The first and second term on the right hand side of the above equation are dissipative terms. Depending on their signs, they can have a damping effect, inducing decay in solutions and/or their derivatives. We emphasise though that the signs and sizes of
a,b
are different for
h^{00}
,
h^{0i}
,
h^{ij}
and
\psi
. See (
3.33
) for the full system. Moreover, the values of the coefficients in the full system depend on the choice of gauge source functions, and the motivation for our particular choice is discussed in Remark
3.8
.

For the purpose of exposition, in this section we will relate our analysis of the gauge-fixed system to the “toy model”

\square u=\frac{a}{\tau}\partial_{\tau}u+\frac{b}{\tau^{2}}u+F,

(1.14)

where
\square=-\partial_{\tau}^{2}+\Delta
is the flat wave operator and
F
is an inhomogeneous source term. The dynamics of the two equations (
1.13
), (
1.14
) are essentially the same, particularly because the perturbed metric
g
remains globally close to the Minkowski metric for all time.

Remark 1.8

(The linear wave equation on a fixed FLRW background)
.

A related problem in this setting is the dynamics of solutions to the linear wave equation

\square_{\widetilde{g}_{b}}\phi=0

(1.15)

on a fixed decelerated FLRW background. A short computation reveals that (
1.15
) is equivalent to the equation (
1.14
) with vanishing
F
and coefficients of the dissipative terms taking the values

(a,b)=\Big(\frac{2p}{1-p},0\Big).

(1.16)

In
[
Hag25
]
, the linear wave equation is studied on a fixed decelerated FLRW spacetime with topology
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and all expansion exponents
p\in(0,1)
. In that paper boundedness and decay of solutions is shown using energy methods which capture the
dispersion
of waves on non-compact manifolds. Wave equations with dissipative terms like (
1.14
) have also been studied on
\mathbb{R}^{3+1}
-Minkowski, see for example
[
Mat77
,
Ues80
]
. We emphasise that for spatially compact spacetimes, this decay via dispersion does not occur, and so the only source of decay present is from from spacetime expansion.

Estimating the spatial averages.

Our analysis of the perturbed quantities
(h^{\mu\nu},\psi)
is based on a decomposition of these functions into their spatial averages, which are obtained by integrating these scalar functions over the torus, and oscillatory remainders which have zero average. We write this decomposition like
u=u_{\mathrm{av}}+u_{\mathrm{osc}}
, where

u_{\mathrm{av}}(\tau)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}u(\tau,x)\mathrm{d}^{3}x

(1.17)

is the spatial average of
u
over
\mathbb{T}^{3}
. A key observation in our analysis is that the gauge-fixed system (
3.33
) decouples (up to some decaying error) into an ODE system for the averaged quantities
(h_{\mathrm{av}}^{\mu\nu},\psi_{\mathrm{av}})
and a system for the remainders
(h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{\mu\nu},\psi_{\mathrm{osc}})
. This allows us to analyse the behaviour of the averages and the remainders separately.

Remark 1.9

(Spatial average decomposition for the relativistic Euler equations)
.

A similar decomposition of the evolution variables into average contributions and oscillations was used in
[
FOOW25
]

Remark 1.10

Remark 1.11

(Obstructions to decay of the spatial averages)
.

The decay one can obtain for all the averaged quantities
(h_{\mathrm{av}}^{\mu\nu},\psi_{\mathrm{av}})
obviously depend on the decay of the various nonlinear terms in the equations. This error depend on all of the functions
(h^{\mu\nu},\psi)
, including the oscillatory remainders
(h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{\mu\nu},\psi_{\mathrm{osc}})
. It turns out that the principle equations for the averages imply more decay than the principle equations for the remainders. Due to the nonlinear coupling of the systems, the decay of the remainders “bottleneck” the decay of the averages, reducing the amount of decay of the averaged quantities. We discuss this more in Remark
6.5
. An additional complication (one that is also true of the equations for the oscillatory quantities
(h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{\mu\nu},\psi_{\mathrm{osc}})
) is that the system for the averages does not diagonalise entirely. There are mixed linear terms in the equations for
h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}

h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ij}
, see (
6.35
).

Estimates for the oscillatory remainders.

The functions
h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{\mu\nu}=h^{\mu\nu}-h_{\mathrm{av}}^{\mu\nu}
,
\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}=\psi-\psi_{\mathrm{av}}
capture the higher-order oscillations of the perturbations, and have zero spatial average. In line with these observations, we estimate these terms using energy estimates and the Poincaré inequality
6
6

6

See Section
2.1
for a description of the notation we use for differential operators and Sobolev spaces in this paper.

\|u_{\mathrm{osc}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq C\|\overline{\partial}u_{\mathrm{osc}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}.

(1.26)

In brief, we use energy estimates to control the top-order derivatives in the system, while the Poincaré inequality allows us to control lower-order terms.

The oscillatory remainders
(h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{\mu\nu},\psi_{\mathrm{osc}})
satisfy the same wave equation as the full functions
(h^{\mu\nu},\psi)
, at least up to a nonlinear error. One can simply subtract off the equations satisfied by the spatial averages, which are estimated separately. For the toy model (
1.14
), this yields an equation of the form

\square u_{\mathrm{osc}}=\frac{a}{\tau}\partial_{\tau}u_{\mathrm{osc}}+\frac{b}{\tau^{2}}u_{\mathrm{osc}}+F_{\mathrm{osc}}.

(1.27)

As previously mentioned, the first two terms on the right hand side of the above equation have a damping effect. The signs already play a role in determining the behaviour of the spatial averages. This is also true for the oscillating remainders, although it turns out only the sign of
a
plays a role. If
a>0
, a short calculation shows that the rescaled function
v=\tau^{a/2}u_{\mathrm{osc}}
satisfies the equation

\square v=\frac{c}{\tau^{2}}v+\tau^{a/2}F_{\mathrm{osc}},

(1.28)

where
c=b-a(a-2)/4
. We let
E_{0}[v]
denote the standard wave equation energy on the torus

E_{0}[v](\tau)=\frac{1}{2}(\|\partial_{\tau}v(\tau)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}+\|\overline{\partial}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}).

(1.29)

Then
E_{0}[v]
satisfies the differential inequality

\partial_{\tau}E_{0}[v]\leq\Big(\frac{|c|}{\tau^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}+\tau^{a/2}\|F\|_{L^{2}}\Big)(E_{0}[v])^{1/2}.

(1.30)

The
\tau^{-2}\|v\|_{L^{2}}
term could form an obstruction to this argument, since
c=b-a(a-2)/4
may be nonzero,
7
7

7

This is the case for all equations in the system for
(h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{\mu\nu},\psi_{\mathrm{osc}})
.
However,

has zero spatial average, and so we use the aforementioned Poincaré inequality (
1.26
), and bound
\tau^{-2}\|v\|_{L^{2}}\leq\tau^{-2}(E_{0}[v])^{1/2}
. This turns the lower-order term into an error term in terms of decay, and can be controlled in a straightforward manner via a Grönwall-type inequality.

The upshot is that the decay of the oscillatory remainders are dictated by the
\tau^{-1}\partial_{\tau}u_{\mathrm{osc}}
term (as well as the error term

), and not by the
\tau^{-2}u_{\mathrm{osc}}
term. For the actual system (
3.33
), we derive the relevant energy inequalities in

Remark 1.12
.

Our analysis of the system for
(h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{\mu\nu},\psi_{\mathrm{osc}})
is less straight-forward than rescaling the quantities and deriving standard energy estimates for them. We instead define a family of weighted energies which have the same effect, but allow for more freedom in estimating mixed linear terms which appear in the system. See Section

Remark 1.13

(Analysis of linear wave equations)
.

The decomposition of solutions that we apply to the toy model (
1.14
) can also be used to analyse several linear wave equations of note.

The flat wave equation on Minkowski with
\mathbb{T}^{3}
spatial topology has
a=b=0
, and the averaged equation is
\partial_{\tau}^{2}u_{\mathrm{av}}=0
, which admits solutions
u_{\mathrm{av}}(\tau)=c_{1}+c_{2}\tau
, for
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. It follows that generic solutions grow linearly in time.
8
8

8

In the setting of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
-spatial topology, we exclude these solutions from consideration, as they are not finite-energy.
This destroys any chance of stability for nonlinear versions of this equation, an obvious example of which is the Einstein-vacuum equations with
\mathbb{T}^{3}
spatial topology.

We compare this to the linear wave equation on a flat FLRW background (see Remark
1.9
). The equation for the average is

\partial_{\tau}^{2}u_{\mathrm{av}}=-\frac{2p}{1-p}\frac{1}{\tau}\partial_{\tau}u_{\mathrm{av}},

which has solutions

(1.32)

If

, then

, and so solutions are bounded in this case. Moreover, one can show using the methods discussed above that
\|u_{\mathrm{osc}}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\tau^{-p/(1-p)}

(1.33)

where

is an oscillatory function that does not have a limit as

smooths out and becomes spatially homogeneous as

, but this homogenisation does not occur at higher order.

1.3
Breakdown of the proof for
1/3<p\leq 2/3

It is interesting to note that the numerical simulations in
[
Mar25
]
indicate instability of the FLRW solutions of the Einstein-Euler system with expansion exponent
1/3\leq p\leq 2/3
. We recall that the ENSF system admits FLRW solutions with expansion exponent

. While we do not prove instability for the ENSF system in the range
1/3<p\leq 2/3
of expansion exponents, the proof of Theorem

Remark 1.14

(Issues with gauge)
.

Given that the lapse and shift equations are intrinsically tied to the choice of wave coordinates (more so than the equations for the horizontal components and scalar field), there is the possibility that this is an artefact of the gauge we have set. We do not know whether other choices of gauge source functions, for example, could resolve this issue.

A model problem which captures the obstruction in the gauge-fixed system is the system of semilinear wave equations

\displaystyle\square u_{1}

\displaystyle=\Big[\frac{2p}{1-p}-2\Big]\frac{1}{\tau}\partial_{\tau}u_{1}+\mathcal{N}(\partial u_{1},\partial u_{1}),

(1.34a)

\displaystyle\square u_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2p}{1-p}\frac{1}{\tau}\partial_{\tau}u_{2}+\mathcal{N}(\partial u_{2},\partial u_{2}),

(1.34b)

where
\mathcal{N}
is arbitrary quadratic form.
9
9

9

\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}(\partial u_{1},\partial u_{2})
. With this system one would see that not only does the equation (
1.34a
) for
u_{1}
form the primary obstruction to stability if
p\leq 2/3

u_{2}
.

) for

models the equation for the remainder quantities of the lapse perturbation

3.33a

10

10

2
Preliminaries

This section contains various preliminaries for the global stability theorem. First we introduce notation that we use throughout this paper. Then we state several important Sobolev space embeddings and inequalities. Finally we show that the FLRW spacetime
(\widetilde{g}_{b},\phi_{b})
is indeed a solution to the Einstein-nonlinear scalar field system.

2.1
Notation

Index notation.

We employ abstract index notation in this paper. Greek letters
\mu,\nu,\alpha,\beta,\dots
represent spacetime coordinates that range over values
\{0,1,2,3\}
, and lowercase Latin letters
a,b,,\dots
represent spatial coordinates that range over
\{1,2,3\}
. Pairs of repeated indices in a product are summed. Unless stated otherwise, we raise and lower indices with respect to the conformal metric
g
introduced in Section
3
.

Coordinates.

We will work in a standard local coordinate system
(x^{1},x^{2},x^{3})
on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
. We remark that this coordinate system is not globally well-defined on all of
\mathbb{T}^{3}
; however the coordinate vector-fields

\partial_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}},\hskip 20.00003pti=1,2,3

(2.1)

are globally well-defined.
11
11

11

One could equivalently take
(x^{1},x^{2},x^{3})
to be periodic coordinates on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
.
This coordinate system extends to a coordinate system
(x^{0},x^{1},x^{2},x^{3})
on an
(n+1)
-dimensional manifold with boundary
\mathcal{M}=[\tau_{0},T)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}
. For the time coordinate, we often write
x^{0}=\tau
.

Differential operators.

We denote the coordinate vector-fields

\partial_{\mu}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}},

(2.2)

and we often write
\partial_{0}=\partial_{\tau}
for the time derivative. We perform all computations with respect to the frame
\{\partial_{\mu}\}_{\mu=01,2,3}
.

Given a multi-index
I=(I_{1},I_{2},I_{3})\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
, the operator
\overline{\partial}{}^{I}
denotes the spatial derivative

\overline{\partial}{}^{I}=\partial_{1}^{I_{1}}\cdot\partial_{2}^{I_{2}}\cdot\partial_{3}^{I_{3}}.

(2.3)

For a spacetime function
u

\partial u
to denote any/all spacetime derivatives of
u
, and
\overline{\partial}u
to denote any/all purely spatial derivatives of
u
.

Norms and spatial averages.

Let
u
be a function on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
. We often write the integral of
u
over
\mathbb{T}^{3}
as

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}u:=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}u(x)\,\mathrm{d}^{3}x,

(2.4)

omitting the volume form
\mathrm{d}^{3}x=\mathrm{d}x^{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{2}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{3}
.
We define the standard inhomogeneous Sobolev norm of order
K
on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
by

\|u\|_{H^{K}}^{2}:=\sum_{|I|\leq K}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(\overline{\partial}{}^{I}u)^{2}\mathrm{d}^{3}x,

(2.5)

and write

. Similarly, we define the

\|u\|_{W^{K,\infty}}:=\sum_{|I|\leq K}\mathrm{ess}\,\sup_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\overline{\partial}{}^{I}u|,

(2.6)

and write
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}):=W^{0,\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
. We denote the spatial average of
u
over
\mathbb{T}^{3}
by

u_{\mathrm{av}}:=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}u(x)\mathrm{d}^{3}x.

(2.7)

We denote the zero-average remainders by
u_{\mathrm{osc}}=u-u_{\mathrm{av}}
.

Running constants.

We use a running constant in this paper, which we denote by
C
. This constant can vary from line to line, but is generally used in the context where
C
must be chosen to be sufficiently large for the inequality to hold. The constant
C
can depend on the regularity
K

that determine the size of the initial data, but are independent of
(g^{\mu\nu},\phi)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
, we have the following inequality:

\|u_{\mathrm{osc}}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\overline{\partial}u\|_{L^{2}}.

(2.12)

2.3
The background FLRW solution

The Einstein-nonlinear scalar field system (
1.2
) can equivalently be written in trace-reversed form as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}_{\mu\nu}[\widetilde{g}]

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi+\widetilde{g}_{\mu\nu}V(\phi),

(2.13a)

\displaystyle\square_{g}\phi-V^{\prime}(\phi)

\displaystyle=0.

(2.13b)

We recall from (
1.5a
), (
1.5b
) the background solution
((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3},\widetilde{g}_{b},\phi_{b})
, given by

\widetilde{g}_{b}=-\mathrm{d}t^{2}+t^{2p}\delta_{ij}\,\mathrm{d}x^{i}\mathrm{d}x^{j},\hskip 20.00003pt\phi_{b}=(2p)^{1/2}\log t+(p/2)^{1/2}\log\Big[\frac{V_{0}}{p(3p-1)}\Big].

(2.14)

In the next lemma, we prove that the above spacetime is in fact a solution to the ENSF system. This was also proved in Lemma 1 of
[
Rin09b
]
, albeit for expansion exponents restricted to
p>1
.

Lemma 2.5

(Background solution to the Einstein-nonlinear scalar field system)
.

The triple
((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3},\widetilde{g}_{b},\phi_{b})
is a solution to the Einstein nonlinear scalar field system (
2.13a
), (
2.13b
).

Proof.

The nonzero Christoffel symbols of the metric
\widetilde{g}_{b}
are

\Gamma_{ij}^{t}(\widetilde{g}_{b})=pt^{2p-1}\delta_{ij},\hskip 20.00003pt\Gamma_{ti}^{j}(\widetilde{g}_{b})=\frac{p}{t}\delta_{i}^{j},\hskip 10.00002pti,j=1,2,3.

(2.15)

The components of the Ricci tensor for an arbitrary metric
\widetilde{g}
satisfies the identity

\mathrm{Ric}_{\mu\nu}[\widetilde{g}]=\partial_{\lambda}\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{g})-\partial_{(\mu}\Gamma_{\nu)\lambda}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{g})+\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{g})\Gamma_{\lambda\delta}^{\delta}(\widetilde{g})-\Gamma_{\mu\lambda}^{\delta}(\widetilde{g})\Gamma_{\nu\delta}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{g}).

(2.16)

The off-diagonal components of
\mathrm{Ric}[\widetilde{g}_{b}]
are identically zero, and the diagonal components are

\displaystyle R_{tt}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\partial_{t}\Gamma_{ta}^{a}(\widetilde{g}_{b})-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\big(\Gamma_{ta}^{a}(\widetilde{g}_{b})\big)^{2}=\frac{3p(1-p)}{t^{2}},

(2.17a)

\displaystyle R_{ij}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\Gamma_{ij}^{t}(\widetilde{g}_{b})+\Gamma_{ij}^{t}(\widetilde{g}_{b})\sum_{j=1}^{3}\Gamma_{ta}^{a}(\widetilde{g}_{b})-2\sum_{a=1}^{3}\Gamma_{ia}^{t}(\widetilde{g}_{b})\Gamma_{tj}^{a}(\widetilde{g}_{b})=\frac{p(3p-1)}{t^{2-2p}}\delta_{ij},\hskip 10.00002pti,j=1,2,3.

(2.17b)

For the background scalar field, we compute

V(\phi_{b})=\frac{p(3p-1)}{t^{2}},\hskip 20.00003pt\partial_{\mu}\phi_{b}\partial_{\nu}\phi_{b}=\frac{2p}{t^{2}}\delta_{\mu}^{0}\delta_{\nu}^{0}.

(2.18)

The trace-reversed stress-energy-momentum tensor has the formula
T_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}T\widetilde{g}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi+V(\phi)\widetilde{g}_{\mu\nu}
. Clearly the off-diagonal components are identically zero, and the diagonal components are

T_{tt}-\frac{1}{2}T(\widetilde{g}_{b})_{tt}=\frac{3p(1-p)}{t^{2}},\hskip 20.00003ptT_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}T(\widetilde{g}_{b})_{ij}=\frac{p(3p-1)}{t^{2-2p}}\delta_{ij},\hskip 10.00002pti,j=1,2,3,

(2.19)

matching those of
\mathrm{Ric}_{\mu\nu}[\widetilde{g}]
. Hence the Einstein equations (
2.13a
) are satisfied. For the scalar field equations (
2.13b
) we have

\displaystyle\square_{\widetilde{g}_{b}}\phi_{b}-V^{\prime}(\phi_{b})

\displaystyle=-\partial_{t}^{2}\phi_{b}-\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}g^{ab}\Gamma_{ab}^{t}(\widetilde{g}_{b})\partial_{t}\phi_{b}+(2/p)^{1/2}V(\phi_{b})

\displaystyle=\frac{(2p)^{1/2}}{t^{2}}-\frac{3p(2p)^{1/2}}{t^{2}}+\frac{(2p)^{1/2}(3p-1)}{t^{2}}=0.

(2.20)

This completes the proof.
∎

The FLRW solution in conformal coordinates.

We recall that the FLRW metric
\widetilde{g}_{b}
is conformally flat. We change time coordinate

\tau=\frac{1}{1-p}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t^{p}}=t^{1-p}.

(2.21)

In the coordinates
(\tau,x)
, the metric and scalar field for the background solution take the form

\widetilde{g}_{b}=\tau^{2p/(1-p)}\big(-(1-p)^{-2}\mathrm{d}\tau^{2}+\delta_{ij}\,\mathrm{d}x^{i}\mathrm{d}x^{j}\big),\hskip 20.00003pt\phi_{b}=\frac{(2p)^{1/2}}{1-p}\log\tau+(p/2)^{1/2}\log\Big|\frac{V_{0}}{p(3p-1)}\Big|.

(2.22)

So that the conformal metric
m:=\tau^{-2p/(1-p)}\widetilde{g}_{b}
is the flat Minkowski metric. We will henceforth refer to the pair
(m,\phi_{b})
as the background solution. We note that the future boundary
\Sigma^{+}=\{t=\infty\}
is the level set
\{\tau=\infty\}
, while the past singularity
\Sigma^{-}=\{t=0\}
is the level set
\{\tau=0\}
.

3
The gauge-fixed Einstein-nonlinear scalar field system

This section is dedicated to deriving the gauge-fixed Einstein-nonlinear scalar field system (
3.33
) which is central to our global existence argument. First we restate the Einstein-nonlinear scalar field system as a system of equations on a conformally transformed metric. Then we reformulate these equations as a system for the metric and scalar field perturbations, with the background FLRW spacetime constituting the “zero solution”. We introduce the particular choice of wave coordinates that we make for this paper, and and derive the resulting gauge-fixed system.

3.1
The Einstein-nonlinear scalar field system on a conformal metric

The gauge-fixed system we derive amounts to a system for a conformal metric
g=\Omega^{2}\widetilde{g}
(and the the original scalar field
\phi
), where
(\widetilde{g},\phi)
satisfy the Einstein-nonlinear scalar field system. Due to the particularly simple nature of our choice of conformal factor (see (
3.5
)), this is completely equivalent to the ENSF system, and also rescales the leading order asymptotics of the metric components, simplifying the analysis. We will solve for the inverse metric components
g^{\mu\nu}
:
\mu,\nu=0,1,2,3
, rather than the (lowered) metric components
g_{\mu\nu}
.

We give two preliminary lemmas which amount to Ricci curvature identities for a Lorentzian metric. These are fairly standard identities, and so we will delay their proof to Appendix
B
. The first lemma is an identity satisfied by the Ricci tensors of two metrics which are related by a conformal transformation.

Lemma 3.1

(Ricci curvatures for conformal metrics)
.

Let
\widetilde{g}
be a Lorentzian metric, and introduce the conformally transformed metric
g=\Omega^{2}\widetilde{g}
, where
\Omega\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R})
is a scalar function that is smooth and positive everywhere. Then the Ricci curvature of the metrics
g
and
\widetilde{g}
are related by the identity

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}^{\mu\nu}[g]=\>

\displaystyle g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}\mathrm{Ric}_{\alpha\beta}[\widetilde{g}]+g^{\delta\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\delta}(\log\Omega)-2g^{\lambda(\mu}\partial_{\lambda}g^{\nu)\delta}\partial_{\delta}(\log\Omega)

\displaystyle-g^{\mu\nu}\Big[g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}(\log\Omega)-2g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}(\log\Omega)\partial_{\beta}(\log\Omega)-\Gamma^{\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}(\log\Omega)\Big]

\displaystyle-2g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}\Big[\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}(\log\Omega)+\partial_{\alpha}(\log\Omega)\partial_{\beta}(\log\Omega)\Big],

(3.1)

where
\Gamma^{\lambda}:=g^{\alpha\beta}\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}(g)
.

The second lemma is a decomposition of the Ricci curvature into a scalar wave operator, non-hyperbolic principal terms which vanish upon fixing wave coordinates, and semilinear terms.

Lemma 3.2

(Ricci curvature wave decomposition)
.

Let
g
be a Lorentzian metric, and let
\widehat{\square}_{g}
denote the reduced wave operator

\widehat{\square}_{g}:=g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}.

(3.2)

Then the Ricci curvature of
g
satisfies the identity

2\mathrm{Ric}^{\mu\nu}[g]=\widehat{\square}_{g}g^{\mu\nu}+2g^{\lambda(\mu}\partial_{\lambda}\Gamma^{\nu)}-\mathcal{F}^{\mu\nu}(g,\partial g),

(3.3)

where the
\mathcal{F}^{\mu\nu}
are the functions

\mathcal{F}^{\mu\nu}(g,\partial g)=g^{\alpha\beta}g^{\lambda\delta}\partial_{\lambda}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{(\alpha}g_{\beta)\delta}-\frac{1}{2}g^{\alpha\beta}g^{\lambda\delta}\partial_{\lambda}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\delta}g_{\alpha\beta}+2g^{\lambda(\mu}g^{\nu)\delta}\partial_{\delta}g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}g_{\beta\lambda}\\
-\frac{1}{2}g^{\lambda(\mu}g^{\nu)\delta}\partial_{\lambda}g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\delta}g_{\alpha\beta}-g^{\alpha\beta}g^{\lambda(\mu|}\partial_{\alpha}g^{|\nu)\delta}\partial_{\delta}g_{\beta\lambda}-g^{\alpha\beta}g^{\lambda(\mu|}\partial_{\alpha}g^{|\nu)\delta}\partial_{\beta}g_{\lambda\delta}.

(3.4)

We will now fix the conformal factor to be

\Omega=\tau^{-\frac{p}{1-p}},

(3.5)

which is precisely the conformal factor which transforms the background FLRW metric
\widetilde{g}_{b}
into the Minkowski metric
m
(cf. the discussion of regarding the conformal metric in Section
1.2
). This means that instead of solving for a metric
\widetilde{g}
which is initially a small perturbation of the FLRW solution
\widetilde{g}_{b}
, we will instead derive a system of equations for the (inverse) conformal metric components
g^{\mu\nu}
, which are initially small perturbations of Minkowski.

We combine the previous two lemmas to derive equations for the components of the inverse conformal metric components
g^{\mu\nu}
and scalar field
\phi
. As we have not yet fixed a gauge, the equations for the
g^{\mu\nu}
are not wave equations, but the structure of the non-hyperbolic principle terms are made clear.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
(\widetilde{g},\phi)
satisfy the Einstein-nonlinear scalar field system (
2.13a
), (
2.13b
). Let
(\tau=x^{0},x^{1},x^{2},x^{3})
be coordinates on
\mathcal{M}
, and let
g=\Omega^{2}\widetilde{g}
be the conformally related metric with conformal factor given by (
3.5
). Then the quantities
\widehat{\square}_{g}g^{\mu\nu}
,
\widehat{\square}_{g}\phi
satisfy the identities

\displaystyle\widehat{\square}_{g}g^{\mu\nu}=\>

\displaystyle-\frac{2p}{1-p}\frac{1}{\tau}g^{0\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}g^{\mu\nu}+\frac{4p}{1-p}\frac{1}{\tau}g^{\lambda(\mu}\partial_{\lambda}g^{\nu)0}-2g^{\lambda(\mu}\partial_{\lambda}\Gamma^{\nu)}(g)

\displaystyle-\frac{2p}{1-p}\frac{1}{\tau}\Gamma^{0}(g)g^{\mu\nu}+\frac{2p(3p-1)}{(1-p)^{2}}\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}g^{00}g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{4p}{(1-p)^{2}}\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}g^{0\mu}g^{0\nu}

\displaystyle+\mathcal{F}^{\mu\nu}(g,\partial g)+2g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\mu\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\beta}\phi+2\tau^{2p/(1-p)}g^{\mu\nu}V(\phi),

(3.6a)

\displaystyle\widehat{\square}_{g}\phi=

\displaystyle-\frac{2p}{1-p}\frac{1}{\tau}g^{0\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}\phi+\Gamma^{\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}\phi+\tau^{2p/(1-p)}V^{\prime}(\phi).

(3.6b)

Proof.

The equation (
3.6b
) follows from (
2.13b
) with the computation

\displaystyle\widehat{\square}_{g}\phi

\displaystyle=\tau^{2p/(1-p)}\widetilde{g}^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\phi

\displaystyle=\tau^{2p/(1-p)}\big[\square_{\widetilde{g}}\phi+\widetilde{g}^{\alpha\beta}\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{g})\partial_{\lambda}\phi\big].

(3.7)

We expand the contracted Christoffel symbol
\widetilde{g}^{\alpha\beta}\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{g})
:

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}^{\alpha\beta}\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{g})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\widetilde{g}^{\alpha\beta}\widetilde{g}^{\lambda\delta}(2\partial_{(\alpha}\widetilde{g}_{\beta)\delta}-\partial_{\delta}\widetilde{g}_{\alpha\beta})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\tau^{-4p/(1-p)}g^{\alpha\beta}g^{\lambda\delta}\big[2\partial_{(\alpha|}(\tau^{2p/(1-p)}g_{|\beta)\delta})-\partial_{\delta}(\tau^{2p/(1-p)}g_{\alpha\beta})\big]

\displaystyle=\tau^{-2p/(1-p)}\Big[-\frac{2p}{1-p}\frac{1}{\tau}g^{0\lambda}+\Gamma^{\lambda}(g)\Big],

(3.8)

combining this with (
3.7
) yields (
3.6b
).

Next we derive (
3.6a
). We compute for the terms on the right hand side of (
3.1
):

\displaystyle g^{\delta\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\delta}(\log\Omega)-2g^{\lambda(\mu}\partial_{\lambda}g^{\nu)\delta}\partial_{\delta}(\log\Omega)=-\frac{p}{1-p}\frac{1}{\tau}g^{0\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}g^{\mu\nu}+\frac{2p}{1-p}\frac{1}{\tau}g^{\lambda(\mu}\partial_{\lambda}g^{\nu)0},

(3.9a)

\displaystyle-g^{\mu\nu}\Big[g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}(\log\Omega)-2g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}(\log\Omega)\partial_{\beta}(\log\Omega)-\Gamma^{\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}(\log\Omega)\Big]=g^{\mu\nu}\Big[\frac{p(3p-1)}{(1-p)^{2}}\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}g^{00}-\frac{p}{1-p}\frac{1}{\tau}\Gamma^{0}\Big],

(3.9b)

\displaystyle-2g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}\Big[\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}(\log\Omega)+\partial_{\alpha}(\log\Omega)\partial_{\beta}(\log\Omega)\Big]=-\frac{2p}{(1-p)^{2}}\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}g^{0\mu}g^{0\nu}.

(3.9c)

Therefore by Lemma
3.1
the Ricci curvature of
g
obeys the identity

2\mathrm{Ric}^{\mu\nu}[g]=2g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}\mathrm{Ric}_{\alpha\beta}[\widetilde{g}]-\frac{2p}{1-p}\frac{1}{\tau}g^{0\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}g^{\mu\nu}+\frac{4p}{1-p}\frac{1}{\tau}g^{\lambda(\mu}\partial_{\lambda}g^{\nu)0}\\
-\frac{2p}{1-p}\frac{1}{\tau}\Gamma^{0}(g)g^{\mu\nu}+\frac{2p(3p-1)}{(1-p)^{2}}\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}g^{00}g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{4p}{(1-p)^{2}}\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}g^{0\mu}g^{0\nu}.

(3.10)

The Einstein equations imply that the Ricci curvature of
\widetilde{g}
satisfies

\displaystyle g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}\mathrm{Ric}_{\alpha\beta}[\widetilde{g}]

\displaystyle=g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\beta}\phi+g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}\widetilde{g}_{\alpha\beta}V(\phi)

\displaystyle=g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\beta}\phi+\tau^{2p/(1-p)}g^{\mu\nu}V(\phi).

(3.11)

Combining (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) with the decomposition of the Ricci curvature from Lemma
3.2
, we obtain (
3.6a
).
∎

3.2

We wish to solve the system (
2.13a
), (
2.13b

h^{\mu\nu}:=g^{\mu\nu}-m^{\mu\nu},\hskip 20.00003pt\psi:=\phi-\phi_{b},

where
(m,\phi_{b})

1.5

Note that the background components

\partial g^{\mu\nu}=\partial h^{\mu\nu}

In the following Lemma we derive equations for the perturbations

V(\phi)=V_{0}\exp(-(2/p)^{1/2}\phi).

(3.13)

(Equations for metric and scalar field perturbations)

The system of equations for the

). We group terms appropriately to obtain

\widehat{\square}_{g}h^{\mu\nu}=-\frac{2p}{1-p}\frac{1}{\tau}g^{0\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}h^{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{\tau}\Big[\frac{4p}{1-p}g^{\lambda(\mu}\partial_{\lambda}h^{\nu)0}-2g^{\lambda(\mu}\partial_{\lambda}(\tau\Gamma^{\nu)})\Big]\\
+\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\Big[2\tau g^{0(\mu}\Gamma^{\nu)}-\frac{2p}{1-p}\tau\Gamma^{0}g^{\mu\nu}+\frac{2p(3p-1)}{(1-p)^{2}}h^{00}g^{\mu\nu}\Big]+\mathcal{F}^{\mu\nu}+\mathcal{G}^{\mu\nu}.

(3.26)

The result now follows after considering the
00
-,
0i
-, and
ij
-components individually.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

We recall the cancellation of the terms

\frac{2p(3p-1)}{(1-p)^{2}}\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}g^{00}g^{\mu\nu},\hskip 20.00003pt-\frac{4p}{(1-p)^{2}}\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}g^{0\mu}g^{0\nu},

(3.27)

present in the system (
3.6a
) with the corresponding terms in (
3.24a
) - (
3.24b
). While important for our argument, such a cancellation is expected because the background metric (the Minkowski metric) must also solve the system (
3.6a
). The terms (
3.27
) are the only ones present in (
3.6a
) that do not depend on any derivatives of the metric. Since all terms containing derivatives vanish for the Minkowski metric, these are the only potentially nonzero terms present in the system, and so their cancellation with the terms arising from
g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\beta}\phi
and
\tau^{2p/(1-p)}V(\phi)g^{\mu\nu}
is necessary.

3.3
Wave coordinates

We fix wave coordinates

\Gamma^{\mu}(g)=\mathcal{Y}^{\mu}(g),\hskip 20.00003pt\mu=0,1,2,3,

(3.28)

where
\mathcal{Y}^{\mu}
are the gauge source functions

\mathcal{Y}^{0}(g)=\frac{3p-1}{1-p}\frac{1}{\tau}h^{00},\hskip 20.00003pt\mathcal{Y}^{i}(g)=\frac{2p}{1-p}\frac{1}{\tau}h^{0i},\hskip 20.00003pti=1,2,3.

(3.29)

Remark 3.8

(Motivation for choice of gauge source functions)
.

Our motivation for this choice of the functions
\mathcal{Y}^{\mu}
is that they simplify the system more than any other choice. We highlight the boxed terms in the equations (
3.14c
) for the horizontal metric perturbations and (
3.14d
) for the scalar field perturbations. These are all mixed linear terms, which complicate the structure of the equations. Fixing wave coordinates (
3.28
) with source functions (
3.29
) ensures that these terms vanish completely. Despite this, there are still mixed linear terms present in the system, see the terms
\mathcal{L}^{\mu\nu}
,
\mathcal{L}^{(\psi)}
in the gauge-fixed system (
3.33
).

Remark 3.9

(Propagation of wave coordinate condition)
.

It is a standard property of the Einstein equations that wave coordinate conditions of the form (
3.28
) are propagated by the reduced Einstein equations. One can prove that if (
3.28
) are satisfied on some initial Cauchy surface, then they are satisfied for all future time such that the solution to the reduced system exists. See Chapter VII of
[
CB15
]
for details.

In other works, wave coordinates are usually fixed with respect to the original metric
\widetilde{g}
that solves the Einstein equations, rather than to a conformal metric. In the present setting these two formulations are equivalent due to the choice of conformal factor (
3.5
). Indeed, by (
3.8
), the contracted Christoffel symbols of
g
and
\widetilde{g}
satisfy the relation

\widetilde{g}^{\alpha\beta}\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{\mu}(\widetilde{g})=\tau^{-2p/(1-p)}\Big(g^{\alpha\beta}\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{\mu}(g)-\frac{2p}{1-p}\frac{1}{\tau}g^{0\mu}\Big),

(3.30)

and so fixing
\Gamma^{\lambda}(g)=\mathcal{Y}^{\lambda}(g)
is equivalent to setting

\widetilde{g}^{\alpha\beta}\Gamma_{\alpha\beta}^{\mu}(\widetilde{g})=\tau^{-2p/(1-p)}\mathcal{Y}^{\mu}\big(\tau^{2p/(1-p)}\widetilde{g}\big)-\frac{2p}{1-p}\frac{1}{\tau}\widetilde{g}^{0\mu}.

(3.31)

Hence fixing wave coordinates for the conformal metric
g
are equivalent to fixing (different) wave coordinates for
\widetilde{g}
.

3.4
The gauge-fixed system

We now state the gauge-fixed system, which is equivalent to the ENSF system provided the wave coordinate condition (
3.28
) holds. We introduce the parameter

q:=\frac{p}{1-p}-1,

(3.32)

and observe that for
p\in(2/3,1)
,
q>1
.
The gauge-fixed Einstein-nonlinear scalar field system is

\displaystyle\widehat{\square}_{g}h^{00}+\frac{2q}{\tau}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}h^{00}-\frac{4q+2}{\tau^{2}}g^{00}h^{00}=\mathcal{L}^{00}+\mathcal{N}^{00},

(3.33a)

\displaystyle\widehat{\square}_{g}h^{0i}+\frac{2q+2}{\tau}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}h^{0i}-\frac{2q+2}{\tau^{2}}g^{00}h^{0i}=\mathcal{L}^{0i}+\mathcal{N}^{0i},

(3.33b)

\displaystyle\widehat{\square}_{g}h^{ij}+\frac{2q+2}{\tau}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}h^{ij}=\mathcal{L}^{ij}+\mathcal{N}^{ij},

(3.33c)

\displaystyle\widehat{\square}_{g}\psi+\frac{2q+2}{\tau}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}\psi+\frac{2(2q+1)(q+2)}{\tau^{2}}g^{00}\psi=\mathcal{N}^{(\psi)},

(3.33d)

where the
\mathcal{L}^{00}
,
\mathcal{L}^{0i}
,
\mathcal{L}^{ij}
are the linear terms

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{00}

\displaystyle:=\frac{(32p)^{1/2}}{1-p}\frac{1}{\tau}(g^{00})^{2}\partial_{\tau}\psi-\frac{(8p)^{1/2}(3p-1)}{\tau^{2}}g^{00}\psi,

(3.34a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{0i}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\tau}g^{ia}\partial_{a}h^{00}+\frac{(8p)^{1/2}}{1-p}\frac{1}{\tau}g^{00}g^{ia}\partial_{a}\psi,

(3.34b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{ij}

\displaystyle:=-\frac{(8p)^{1/2}(3p-1)}{\tau}g^{ij}\psi,

(3.34c)

and the
\mathcal{N}^{00}
,
\mathcal{N}^{0i}
,
\mathcal{N}^{ij}
,
\mathcal{N}^{(\psi)}
are the following error terms:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{N}_{0}^{\mu\nu}+\mathcal{N}_{1}^{\mu\nu}+\mathcal{N}_{2}^{\mu\nu},

(3.35a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{(\psi)}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{N}_{0}^{(\psi)}+\mathcal{N}_{1}^{(\psi)},

(3.35b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{0}^{00}:=

\displaystyle\>\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\Big[2pg^{00}\mathcal{W}(\psi)\Big],

(3.36a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{0}^{0i}:=

\displaystyle\>\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\Big[\frac{3p-1}{1-p}h^{0i}h^{00}-(8p)^{1/2}(3p-1)h^{0i}\psi+2ph^{0i}\mathcal{W}(\psi)\Big],

(3.36b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{0}^{ij}:=

\displaystyle\>\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\Big[\frac{4p}{1-p}h^{0i}h^{0j}+2pg^{ij}\mathcal{W}(\psi)\Big],

(3.36c)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{0}^{(\psi)}:=

\displaystyle\>\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\Big[\frac{2(3p-1)}{(1-p)^{2}}h^{00}\psi-(2p)^{1/2}\mathcal{W}(\psi)\Big],

(3.36d)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{1}^{00}:=

\displaystyle\>\frac{1}{\tau}\Big[-\frac{2(2p-1)}{1-p}h^{0a}\partial_{a}h^{00}+\frac{(32p)^{1/2}}{1-p}g^{00}h^{0a}\partial_{a}\psi\Big],

(3.37a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{1}^{0i}:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\Big[-\frac{2p}{1-p}h^{0a}\partial_{a}h^{0i}+h^{0i}\partial_{\tau}h^{00}+\frac{(32p)^{1/2}}{1-p}g^{00}h^{0i}\partial_{\tau}\psi+\frac{(8p)^{1/2}}{1-p}h^{0a}h^{0i}\partial_{a}\psi\Big],

(3.37b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{1}^{ij}:=

\displaystyle\>\frac{1}{\tau}\Big[-\frac{2p}{1-p}\frac{1}{\tau}h^{0a}\partial_{a}h^{ij}+\frac{(32p)^{1/2}}{1-p}g^{\lambda(i}h^{j)0}\partial_{\lambda}\psi\Big],

(3.37c)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{1}^{(\psi)}:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau}\Big[\frac{3p-1}{1-p}h^{00}\partial_{\tau}\psi\Big],

(3.37d)

\mathcal{N}_{2}^{\mu\nu}:=\>2g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\psi\partial_{\beta}\psi+\mathcal{F}^{\mu\nu}.

(3.38)

Remark 3.10
.

The error terms are broken up into the terms in
\mathcal{N}_{0}
that are quadratic in
(\tau^{-1}h^{00},\tau^{-1}h^{0i},\tau^{-1}\psi)
, terms in
\mathcal{N}_{2}
that are quadratic in
(\partial h^{\mu\nu},\partial\psi)
, and the
\mathcal{N}_{1}
are comprised of “mixed” error terms that are quadratic in one each of
(\tau^{-1}h^{00},\tau^{-1}h^{0i},\tau^{-1}\psi)
and
(\partial h^{\mu\nu},\partial\psi)
.

We point out that none of the error terms depend on the undifferentiated horizontal metric perturbations
h^{ij}
. This is important for later estimates of these error terms, as the
h^{ij}
do not decay at leading order, while the other perturbed quantities do decay.

4
The global existence theorem

In this section we give the full statement of the main theorem of this paper, Theorem
4.2
, and begin its proof.

Norms for global existence.

There are several norms that are central to the global existence theorem. We fix an integer
K\geq 3
, and fix a parameter
\delta>0
sufficiently small that
5\delta\leq q-1
. Since
q>1
for all
p>2/3
, such a choice can always be achieved.

We define the following norms for the metric and scalar field perturbations:

\displaystyle S_{h^{00},K}

\displaystyle:=\tau^{q-1}\|h^{00}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+\tau^{q-\delta}\|\partial h^{00}\|_{H^{K}(\mathbb{T}^{3})},

(4.1a)

\displaystyle S_{h^{0*},K}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{a=1}^{3}\Big\{\tau^{q-\delta}\|h^{0a}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+\tau^{q-\delta}\|\partial h^{0a}\|_{H^{K}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\Big\},

(4.1b)

\displaystyle S_{h^{**},K}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}\Big\{\|h^{ab}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+\tau^{q-\delta}\|\partial h^{ab}\|_{H^{K}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\Big\},

(4.1c)

\displaystyle S_{\psi,K}

\displaystyle:=\tau^{q-1+\delta}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}+\tau^{q}\|\partial\psi\|_{H^{K}(\mathbb{T}^{3})},

(4.1d)

\displaystyle S_{K}

\displaystyle:=S_{h^{00},K}+S_{h^{0*},K}+S_{h^{**},K}+S_{\psi,K}.

(4.1e)

Central to the global existence argument is proving that the norm
S_{K}
is small for all time.

Remark 4.1
.

In our argument, we fix
\delta
for the rest of the paper so that
0<5\delta<q-1
. The parameter
\delta
can in fact be arbitrarily small, and assuming the initial data still satisfies the smallness condition (
4.7
), the global existence part of Theorem
4.2
goes through unchanged. In this way one may obtain (slightly) improved asymptotics for the resulting perturbations.

We now state the main theorem.

Theorem 4.2

(Global existence of the Einstein-nonlinear scalar field system)
.

Fix an exponent
p\in(2/3,1)
, an integer
K\geq 3
, and an initial time
\tau_{0}>0
. Consider initial data
\widetilde{g}_{0}^{\mu\nu},\phi_{0}\in H^{K+1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
,
\widetilde{g}_{1}^{\mu\nu},\phi_{1}\in H^{K}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
for
\mu,\nu=0,1,2,3
so that

\big(\widetilde{g}^{\mu\nu},\partial_{\tau}\widetilde{g}^{\mu\nu},\phi,\partial_{\tau}\phi\big)\Big|_{\tau=\tau_{0}}=(\widetilde{g}_{0}^{\mu\nu},\widetilde{g}_{1}^{\mu\nu},\phi_{0},\phi_{1}).

(4.2)

Assume that
\widetilde{g}_{0}^{00}<0
and that
(\widetilde{g}_{0}^{ij})_{i,j=1,2,3}
is the inverse of a Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
. Suppose additionally that
(\widetilde{g}_{0}^{\mu\nu},\widetilde{g}_{1}^{\mu\nu},\phi_{0},\phi_{1})
satisfy the Hamiltonian and momentum constraint equations

\displaystyle\Big(\mathrm{Ric}_{00}[\widetilde{g}]-\frac{1}{2}R\widetilde{g}_{00}-T_{00}[\widetilde{g},\phi]\Big)\Big|_{\tau=\tau_{0}}

\displaystyle=0,

(4.3a)

\displaystyle\Big(\mathrm{Ric}_{0i}[\widetilde{g}]-\frac{1}{2}R\widetilde{g}_{0i}-T_{0i}[\widetilde{g},\phi]\Big)\Big|_{\tau=\tau_{0}}

\displaystyle=0,\hskip 10.00002pti=1,2,3,

(4.3b)

as well as the initial wave coordinate condition

\big(\Gamma^{\mu}-\mathcal{Y}^{\mu}\big)\Big|_{\tau=\tau_{0}}=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\mu=0,1,2,3.

(4.4)

(I)

Then for some
T>\tau_{0}
, there exists a unique classical solution
([\tau_{0},T)\times\mathbb{T}^{3},g,\phi)
to the Einstein nonlinear scalar field system (
3.33
) with initial data (
4.2
) and regularity properties

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}^{\mu\nu},\phi\in C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{0}\big([\tau_{0},T),H^{K+1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\big),\hskip 20.00003pt\partial_{\tau}\widetilde{g}^{\mu\nu},\partial_{\tau}\phi\in C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{0}\big([\tau_{0},T),H^{K}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\big).

(4.5)

The wave coordinate condition

\Gamma^{\mu}-\mathcal{Y}^{\mu}=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\mu=0,1,2,3

(4.6)

is satisfied for all
(\tau,x)\in[\tau_{0},T)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}
and so, letting
g^{\mu\nu}=\tau^{2p/(1-p)}\widetilde{g}^{\mu\nu}
, the functions

h^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}-m^{\mu\nu}
,
\psi=\phi-\phi_{b}
satisfy the gauge-fixed equations (
3.33
) on
[\tau_{0},T)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}
.

(II)

There exists a constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
sufficiently small such that if
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
and the solution
(\widetilde{g},\phi)
satisfies the initial bound

S_{K}(\tau_{0})\leq\varepsilon^{2},

(4.7)

then the time interval on which the solution exists is
[\tau_{0},\infty)
, i.e. the solution to the ENSF system exists for all time. Additionally, the bound

S_{K}(\tau)\leq\varepsilon

(4.8)

holds for all
\tau\geq\tau_{0}
.

(III)

The spacetime
(\mathcal{M},\widetilde{g})
is future-causally geodesically complete, where
\mathcal{M}=[\tau_{0},\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}
. That is, all future-causal inextendible geodesics
\gamma:(s_{-},s_{+})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
are future-complete, i.e.
s_{+}=\infty
.

(IV)

The future-global perturbed solution
(\widetilde{g},\phi)
from above has the following asymptotics. The quantities
g^{00}
,
g^{0i}
,
\phi
decay to leading order to their background counterparts, so that

\displaystyle\tau^{2p/(1-p)}\|\widetilde{g}^{00}-\widetilde{g}_{b}^{00}\|_{W^{K-1,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\varepsilon}{\tau^{p/(1-p)-2}},

(4.9a)

\displaystyle\tau^{2p/(1-p)}\|\widetilde{g}^{0i}\|_{W^{K-1,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\varepsilon}{\tau^{p/(1-p)-1-\delta}},\hskip 20.00003pti=1,2,3,

(4.9b)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\log\tau}\|\phi-\phi_{b}\|_{W^{K-1,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\varepsilon}{\tau^{p/(1-p)-2+\delta}\log\tau}.

(4.9c)

For the horizontal metric components, there exists a symmetric, positive definite
3\times 3
matrix
(g_{\infty}^{ij})_{i,j=1,2,3}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
such that

\|\tau^{2p/(1-p)}\widetilde{g}^{ij}-g_{\infty}^{ij}\|_{W^{K-2,\infty}}\leq\frac{C\varepsilon}{\tau^{p/(1-p)-2}},\hskip 20.00003pti,j=1,2,3,

(4.10)

moreover
g_{\infty}^{ij}
satisfies
|g_{\infty}^{ij}-\delta^{ij}|\leq C\varepsilon
for
i,j=1,2,3
.

Remark 4.3

(Local theory)
.

Part (I) of Theorem
4.2
is a local existence statement that is implicitly used in our global existence argument. The local theory for the Einstein equations is by now standard, and we refer the reader to the texts
[
Rin09a
,
CB15
]
for thorough treatments of this topic. The propagation of the wave coordinate condition
\mathcal{D}^{\mu}=0
(which implies the equivalence of the Einstein equations and the gauge-fixed equations) is central to the local theory, and were used in the foundational works
[
CB52
]
by Choquet-Bruhat and
[
CBG69
]
by Choquet-Bruhat/Geroch to prove local well-posedness of the Einstein equations.

Remark 4.4

(Asymptotics)
.

The asymptotics described in part (IV) of Theorem
4.2
are expressed in terms of the inverse metric
\widetilde{g}^{\mu\nu}
in the coordinates
(\tau,x)
fixed by the wave coordinate condition (
3.28
). We note that since
5\delta<p/(1-p)-2
, the right hand side of the inequalities (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) all decay in
\tau
.

We can also present the asymptotics in coordinates
(t,x)
, where
t=[(1-p)\tau]^{1/1-p}
is the time coordinate with respect to which the background metric
\widetilde{g}_{b}
takes the usual form
\widetilde{g}_{b}=-\mathrm{d}t^{2}+t^{2p}\delta_{ij}\mathrm{d}x^{i}\mathrm{d}x^{j}
. The metric components in coordinates
(t,x)
then satisfy

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}^{tt}(t,x)=\Big(\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\Big)^{2}\widetilde{g}^{00}(\tau(t),x),\hskip 10.00002pt\widetilde{g}^{it}(t,x)=\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\widetilde{g}^{0i}(\tau(t),x),\hskip 20.00003pt\widetilde{g}^{ij}(t,x)=\widetilde{g}^{ij}(\tau(t),x).

(4.11)

Since
t^{\prime}(\tau)=t^{p}
, the bounds (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) imply the following bounds for the components
\widetilde{g}^{tt}
,
\widetilde{g}^{it}
,
\widetilde{g}^{ij}
,
\phi
:

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{g}^{tt}+1\|_{W^{K-1,\infty}}\leq\frac{C\varepsilon}{t^{3p-2}},\hskip 20.00003ptt^{p}\|\widetilde{g}^{it}\|_{W^{K-1,\infty}}\leq\frac{C\varepsilon}{t^{2p-1-\delta^{\prime}}},\hskip 20.00003pt\|t^{2p}\widetilde{g}^{ij}-g_{\infty}^{ij}\|_{W^{K-2,\infty}}\leq\frac{C\varepsilon}{t^{3p-2}},

(4.12)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\log t}\|\phi-\phi_{b}\|_{W^{K-1,\infty}}\leq\frac{C\varepsilon}{t^{3p-2+\delta^{\prime}}\log t},

(4.13)

where
\delta^{\prime}=(1-p)\delta
. Using algebraic estimates like those found in Lemma 2 and Lemma 7 in
[
Rin08

, one can also derive the following asymptotics for the lowered metric components in

coordinates:

\|\widetilde{g}_{tt}+1\|_{W^{K-1,\infty}}\leq\frac{C\varepsilon}{t^{3p-2}},\hskip 20.00003ptt^{-p}\|\widetilde{g}_{it}\|_{W^{K-1,\infty}}\leq\frac{C\varepsilon}{t^{2p-1-\delta^{\prime}}},\hskip 20.00003pt\|t^{-2p}\widetilde{g}_{ij}-(g_{\infty})_{ij}\|_{W^{K-2,\infty}}\leq\frac{C\varepsilon}{t^{3p-2}},

(4.14)

where
(g_{\infty})_{ij}
is the inverse matrix of
(g_{\infty}^{ij})_{i,j=1,2,3}
. This yields the asymptotic description of
\widetilde{g}
presented in Theorem
1.3
in the introduction.

Proof of parts (II), (III), and (IV) of Theorem
4.2
.

Part (II) of Theorem
4.2
comprises a global existence statement, part (III) states that the perturbed spacetimes are geodesically complete, and part (IV) describes the asymptotics of these spacetimes. Before beginning the proof of global existence (which comprises the majority of the rest of the paper), we mention that the geodesic completeness statement from part (III) is proven in Proposition
9.5
, while the asymptotics of part (IV) are proven in Proposition
9.6
.

We now begin the global existence part of the proof. Due to the propagation of the wave coordinate condition (
4.6
), the Einstein-nonlinear scalar field system is equivalent to the gauge-fixed system (
3.33
). Thus it suffices to prove global existence of the solutions
(h^{\mu\nu},\psi)
of the gauged-fixed system. We do this by a bootstrap argument. Let
I\subset[\tau_{0},\infty)
denote the connected set where
T\in I
if and only if the unique solution to the gauge-fixed system (
3.33
) exists for all
\tau_{0}\leq\tau\leq T
and obeys the bound

S_{K}(\tau)\leq\varepsilon

(4.15)

for all
\tau_{0}\leq\tau\leq T
. We will henceforth call (
4.15
) the bootstrap assumption. Clearly
I
is nonempty, as there exists a unique solution in a neighbourhood of
\tau_{0}
that obeys (
4.15
), this follows by the local existence part of Theorem
4.2
. Moreover,
I
is closed by standard continuity properties of the equations. If we can show that
I
is open in
[\tau_{0},\infty)
, that would then imply that
I=[\tau_{0},\infty)
, and so the solution
(g^{\mu\nu},\phi)
exists for all time. Suppose
T\in I
, so that the bootstrap assumption holds for all
\tau_{0}\leq\tau\leq T
. By part (I) of Theorem
4.2
, the solution can be extended to a small neighbourhood in time of
T
, and so it remains to improve the bootstrap assumption (
4.15
). This is proven in Theorem
9.1
, using the various estimates derived in Sections
5
-
9
.
∎

5
Bounds from the bootstrap assumption

From this section until Theorem
9.1
, we assume the hypotheses of Theorem
4.2
, and we assume that the bootstrap assumption (
4.15
) holds on the interval
[\tau_{0},T]
. In this section we prove an array of estimates directly from the bootstrap assumption. These include bounds for the lowered metric components
g_{\mu\nu}
, and for the nonlinear terms
\mathcal{N}^{\mu\nu}
,
\mathcal{N}^{(\psi)}
present in the gauge-fixed system (
3.33
). We make extensive use of the Sobolev embedding theorems from Section
2.2
.

First we derive bounds for several miscellaneous quantities which appear later in the global existence argument.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assuming the bootstrap assumption (
4.15
), the following estimates hold:

\displaystyle\|(g^{00})^{-1}-(m^{00})^{-1}\|_{H^{K}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\tau^{-(q-1-\delta)},

(5.1)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{W}(\psi)\|_{H^{K}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-1-\delta)}.

(5.2)

Proof.

Both inequalities follow from the estimate (
2.11
) from Lemma
2.3
, as well as the bootstrap assumption. We introduce the function
F(x)=(m^{00}+x)^{-1}-(m^{00})^{-1}
. Since
F(0)=0
and
h^{00}
is contained in a small ball around
0
, we apply Lemma
2.3
with
l=1
to
F\circ h^{00}
:

\|(g^{00})^{-1}-(m^{00})^{-1}\|_{H^{K}}=\|F\circ h^{00}\|_{H^{K}}\leq C\|h^{00}\|_{H^{K}}\leq C\varepsilon\tau^{-(q-1-\delta)},

(5.3)

where the last inequality follows from the bootstrap assumption (
4.15
). Similarly for
\mathcal{W}(\psi)
, we have
\mathcal{W}(0)=\mathcal{W}^{\prime}(0)=0
. We apply Lemma
2.3
with
l=2
to
\mathcal{W}\circ\psi
, along with the bootstrap assumption (
4.15
):

\|\mathcal{W}(\psi)\|_{H^{K}}\leq C\|\psi\|_{H^{K}}^{2}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-1-\delta)},

(5.4)

and we are done.
∎

5.1
The lowered metric components

Our strategy for proving global existence revolves around the gauge-fixed system (
3.33
), which we have written as a system for the inverse metric perturbations
h^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}-m^{\mu\nu}
(as well as the scalar field perturbation
\psi
). While we are primarily estimating the raised metric perturbations, we do need to derive bounds for the lowered metric components
g_{\mu\nu}
, as they appear in the gauge-fixed system in the nonlinear terms
\mathcal{N}^{\mu\nu}
.

Lemma 5.2

(Estimates for the lowered metric components)
.

Suppose the bootstrap assumption (
4.15
) holds. Let
g_{\mu\nu}
denote the (lowered) metric components of
g
, with inverse
(g^{-1})^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}
, and let
m_{\mu\nu}
denote the (lowered) components of the Minkowski metric

m=-(1-p)^{-2}\mathrm{d}\tau^{2}+\delta_{ij}\mathrm{d}x^{i}\mathrm{d}x^{j}.

(5.5)

Then the components
g_{\mu\nu}
satisfy the following estimates:

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{3}\|g_{\alpha\beta}-m_{\alpha\beta}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon,

(5.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{3}\|\partial g_{\alpha\beta}\|_{H^{K}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\tau^{-(q-\delta)}.

(5.7)

Proof.

The components of the lowered metric
g_{\mu\nu}
are rational functions of the inverse metric components
g^{\mu\nu}
, in the sense that

g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{P_{\mu\nu}(g^{-1})}{Q(g^{-1})},

(5.8)

where
P
,
Q
are polynomials over
\mathbb{R}^{4\times 4}
. Then the functions

F_{\mu\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{4\times 4}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\hskip 20.00003ptF_{\mu\nu}(A)=\frac{P_{\mu\nu}(A+m^{-1})}{Q(A+m^{-1})}-\frac{P_{\mu\nu}(m^{-1})}{Q(m^{-1})}

(5.9)

satisfy
F_{\mu\nu}(A=0)=0
. Since the components of
g^{-1}
lie in a small ball around the components of
m^{-1}
by the bootstrap assumption we may apply Lemma
2.3
to
F_{\mu\nu}\circ h
to bound

\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{3}\|g_{\alpha\beta}-m_{\alpha\beta}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\sum_{\alpha\beta=0}^{3}\|h^{\alpha\beta}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\varepsilon,

(5.10)

yielding (
5.6
). To prove the estimate (
5.7
) we use the identity

\partial_{\mu}g_{\nu\lambda}=-g_{\nu\alpha}g_{\lambda\beta}\partial_{\mu}g^{\alpha\beta}=-g_{\nu\alpha}g_{\lambda\beta}\partial_{\mu}h^{\alpha\beta}.

(5.11)

Applying the Sobolev product estimate from Lemma
2.2
, the previous estimate (
5.6
), and the bootstrap assumption (
4.15
) then implies the bound

\displaystyle\|\partial g_{\mu\nu}\|_{H^{K}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{3}\|g_{\alpha\beta}\|_{H^{K}}^{2}\Big)\sum_{\lambda,\delta=0}^{3}\|\partial h^{\lambda\delta}\|_{H^{K}},

(5.12)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\tau^{-(q-\delta)},

(5.13)

hence we have (
5.7
).
∎

Remark 5.3
.

The above estimates are not particularly sharp. We could, for example, bound the lowered lapse
g_{00}
in terms of
g^{00}
like
\|g_{00}-m_{00}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|g^{00}-m^{00}\|_{L^{2}}
, and exploit the extra decay of
h^{00}
(this also applies to the lowered shift). However this is not necessary as all lowered metric components in the gauge-fixed system (
3.33
) are differentiated at least once (see Proposition
5.4
). Hence we may use the estimate (
5.7
) instead.

5.2
Estimates of the nonlinear terms from the gauge-fixed system

In the last part of this section, we derive estimates for the nonlinear error terms present in the gauge-fixed system (
3.33
).

Proposition 5.4

(Error term estimates)
.

Suppose the bootstrap assumption (
4.15
) holds. Then we have the following error estimates:

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}^{\mu\nu}\|_{H^{K}}\leq

\displaystyle\>C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-\delta)},\hskip 10.00002pt\mu,\nu=0,1,2,3,

(5.14a)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}^{(\psi)}\|_{H^{K}}\leq

\displaystyle\>C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-\delta)}.

(5.14b)

Proof.

We write schematic forms of the error, letting
\cdot
denote any possible contraction or regular product of the given factors. We will explicitly write the components of the undifferentiated components of
h
which appear, as the
h^{00}
and
h^{0i}
terms decay, while the
h^{ij}
terms do not. We have:

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{00}

\displaystyle=g^{-1}\cdot g^{-1}\cdot(\partial h\cdot\partial g+\partial\psi\cdot\partial\psi)+\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}g^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{W}(\psi),

(5.15a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{0i}

\displaystyle=g^{-1}\cdot g^{-1}\cdot(\partial h\cdot\partial g+\partial\psi\cdot\partial\psi)+\frac{1}{\tau}g^{-1}\cdot h^{0i}\cdot\partial\psi+\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}h^{0i}\cdot(h^{00}+\psi+\mathcal{W}(\psi)),

(5.15b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{ij}

\displaystyle=g^{-1}\cdot g^{-1}\cdot(\partial h\cdot\partial g+\partial\psi\cdot\partial\psi)+\frac{1}{\tau}g^{-1}\cdot h^{0i}\cdot\partial\psi+\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}g^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{W}(\psi),

(5.15c)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{(\psi)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}(h^{0i}\cdot\partial\psi+h^{00}\cdot\partial\psi)+\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}(h^{00}\cdot\psi+\mathcal{W}(\psi)).

(5.15d)

The
\mathcal{W}(\psi)
terms can be estimated by the Sobolev product estimate from Lemma
2.2
, Lemma
5.1
and the bootstrap assumption:

\tau^{-2}\|g^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{W}(\psi)\|_{H^{K}}\leq C\tau^{-2}\|g^{\mu\nu}\|_{H^{K}}\|\mathcal{W}(\psi)|\|_{H^{K}}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-\delta)}.

(5.16)

There are no undifferentiated
h^{ij}
components present in any of the error terms, thus all remaining terms in
\mathcal{N}^{\mu\nu}
depend quadratically on
(\tau^{-1}h^{00},\tau^{-1}h^{0i},\tau^{-1}\psi,\partial h^{\mu\nu},\partial g_{\mu\nu},\partial\psi)
, which by the bootstrap assumption and Lemma
5.2
obey the bound

\frac{1}{\tau}\Big(\|h^{00}\|_{H^{K}}+\sum_{a=1}^{3}\|h^{0a}\|_{H^{K}}+\|\psi\|_{H^{K}}\Big)+\sum_{\mu,\nu=0}^{3}\|\partial h^{\mu\nu}\|_{H^{K}}+\sum_{\mu,\nu=0}^{3}\|\partial g_{\mu\nu}\|_{H^{K}}+\|\partial\psi\|_{H^{K}}\leq C\varepsilon\tau^{-(q-\delta)}.

(5.17)

The estimates (
5.14a
), (
5.14b
) now follow after repeated applications of the Sobolev product Lemma
2.2
, the bootstrap assumption, and the estimates for the lowered metric components from Lemma
5.2
.
∎

6
Estimates for the spatially averaged perturbations
h_{\mathrm{av}}^{\mu\nu}
,
\psi_{\mathrm{av}}

The goal of this section is to derive a system of equations satisfied by the spatial averages of the quantities
(h^{\mu\nu},\psi)
. Recall that we denote the spatial average of a scalar function
u
on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
by

u_{\mathrm{av}}:=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}u(x)\mathrm{d}^{3}x,

(6.1)

and we denote the oscillatory functions with zero average by
u_{\mathrm{osc}}=u-u_{\mathrm{av}}
.
The two functions
u_{\mathrm{osc}}
,
u_{\mathrm{av}}
satisfy the bounds

|u_{\mathrm{av}}|\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\|u_{\mathrm{osc}}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}},

(6.2)

which follow from the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
.

We analyse the spatial averages of
h_{\mathrm{av}}^{\mu\nu}
,
\psi_{\mathrm{av}}
and the functions
h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{\mu\nu}
,
\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}
separately, as the equations for these quantities decouple (up to some decaying error), and their principle systems yield substantially different asymptotics. On the one hand, the equations satisfied by the averages
h_{\mathrm{av}}^{\mu\nu}
,
\psi_{\mathrm{av}}
do not possess terms which depend linearly on spatial derivatives, which can limit decay. On the other hand, the
L^{2}
norm of the oscillating remainders
h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{\mu\nu}
,
\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}
can be estimated directly by the following Poincaré inequality.

For a given scalar function
u
, we have the following trivial commutator identities

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}(u_{\mathrm{av}})=(\partial_{\tau}u)_{\mathrm{av}},\hskip 20.00003pt\partial_{\mu}(u_{\mathrm{osc}})=(\partial_{\mu}u)_{\mathrm{osc}},\hskip 10.00002pt\mu=0,1,2,3,

(6.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}(u_{\mathrm{av}})=(\partial_{i}u)_{\mathrm{av}}=0,\hskip 10.00002pti=1,2,3.

(6.4)

Due to the first two equalities, we will unambiguously write
\partial_{\tau}u_{\mathrm{av}}
,
\partial_{\tau}u_{\mathrm{osc}}
. Moreover, by the last line we have
\partial_{i}u=\partial_{i}u_{\mathrm{osc}}
. We also observe that since the background solution is spatially homogeneous, the remainder functions satisfy
g^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{osc}}=h^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{osc}}
and
\phi_{\mathrm{osc}}=\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}
.

We will derive the equations for the averaged horizontal metric components
h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ij}
and the averaged scalar field
\psi_{\mathrm{av}}
from the evolution equations. Then we will derive equations for the averaged lapse and shift perturbations
h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}
,
h_{\mathrm{av}}^{0i}
directly from the gauge equations

\Gamma^{\mu}(g)=\mathcal{Y}^{\mu}(g).

(6.5)

We remind the reader that we continue to assume the bootstrap assumption (
4.15
) throughout this section.

6.1
ODE for the averaged horizontal metric and scalar field perturbations

We recall the equations for satisfied by the horizontal metric perturbations and scalar field perturbation:

\displaystyle\widehat{\square}_{g}h^{ij}+\frac{2q+2}{\tau}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}h^{ij}-\mathcal{L}^{ij}-\mathcal{N}^{ij}=0,

(6.6)

\displaystyle\widehat{\square}_{g}\psi+\frac{2q+2}{\tau}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}\psi+\frac{2(2q+1)(q+2)}{\tau^{2}}g^{00}\psi-\mathcal{N}^{(\psi)}=0,

(6.7)

where the functions
\mathcal{L}^{ij}
are

\mathcal{L}^{ij}=-\frac{(8p)^{1/2}(3p-1)}{\tau^{2}}g^{ij}\psi.

(6.8)

We derive ordinary differential equations for
h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ij}
,
\psi_{\mathrm{av}}
using these equations. These will be second order ODE, due to the wave equations from which they are derived.

Lemma 6.1

(ODE for the averaged horizontal metric and scalar field perturbations)
.

The spatial averages of the horizontal metric and scalar field perturbations satisfy the differential inequalities

\displaystyle\Big|\partial_{\tau}^{2}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ij}(\tau)+\frac{2q+2}{\tau}\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ij}(\tau)\Big|\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}|\psi_{\mathrm{av}}(\tau)|+\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-\delta)}\Big),\hskip 10.00002pti,j=1,2,3,

(6.9a)

\displaystyle\Big|\partial_{\tau}^{2}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}(\tau)+\frac{2q+2}{\tau}\partial_{\tau}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}(\tau)+\frac{2(2q+1)(q+2)}{\tau^{2}}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}(\tau)\Big|\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-\delta)}.

(6.9b)

Proof.

First we derive (
6.9b
) by multiplying the equation (
6.7
) by
(g^{00})^{-1}
and integrating over
\mathbb{T}^{3}
, which yields

\displaystyle 0=\>

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(g^{00})^{-1}\Big[\widehat{\square}_{g}\psi+\frac{2q+2}{\tau}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}\psi+\frac{2(2q+1)(q+2)}{\tau^{2}}g^{00}\psi-\mathcal{N}^{(\psi)}\Big]

\displaystyle=\>

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}^{2}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}+\frac{2q+2}{\tau}\partial_{\tau}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}+\frac{2(2q+1)(q+2)}{\tau^{2}}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(g^{00})^{-1}\Big[2h^{0a}\partial_{a}\partial_{\tau}\psi+g^{ab}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}\psi-\mathcal{N}^{(\psi)}\Big].

(6.10)

Then we estimate the integral in the last line. The bound for
\mathcal{N}^{(\psi)}
from Proposition
5.4
, the bound for
(g^{00})^{-1}
from Lemma
5.1
, and Cauchy-Schwartz give

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(g^{00})^{-1}\mathcal{N}^{(\psi)}\Big|\leq\|(g^{00})^{-1}\|_{L^{2}}\|\mathcal{N}^{(\psi)}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-\delta)}.

(6.11)

For the other terms in the integral in (
6.10
), we integrate-by parts and estimate via Cauchy-Schwartz, the Sobolev embedding, Lemma
5.1
to bound
(g^{00})^{-1})
, and the bootstrap assumption:

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(g^{00})^{-1}h^{0a}\partial_{a}\partial_{\tau}\psi\Big|\leq

\displaystyle\>\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(g^{00})^{-2}h^{0a}\partial_{a}h^{00}\partial_{\tau}\psi\Big|+\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(g^{00})^{-1}\partial_{a}h^{0a}\partial_{\tau}\psi\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\>\sum_{a=1}^{3}\big(\|(g^{00})^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|h^{0a}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{a}h^{00}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{\tau}\psi\|_{L^{2}}+\|(g^{00})^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{a}h^{0a}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{\tau}\psi\|_{L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\>C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-\delta)},

(6.12a)

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(g^{00})^{-1}g^{ab}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}\psi\Big|\leq

\displaystyle\>\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(g^{00})^{-2}g^{ab}\partial_{a}h^{00}\partial_{b}\psi\Big|+\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(g^{00})^{-1}\partial_{a}g^{ab}\partial_{b}\psi\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\>\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}\big(\|(g^{00})^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|g^{ab}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{a}h^{00}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{b}\psi\|_{L^{2}}+\|(g^{00})^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{a}h^{ab}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{b}\psi\|_{L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\>C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-\delta)}.

(6.12b)

Plugging the estimates (
6.11
) - (
6.12b
) into the equation (
6.10
), we obtain (
6.9b
). We proceed analogously for (
6.9a
), multiplying (
6.6
) by
(g^{00})^{-1}
and integrating over the torus:

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\>\partial_{\tau}^{2}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ij}+\frac{2(q+1)}{\tau}\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ij}+\frac{(8p)^{1/2}(3p-1)}{(2\pi)^{3}\tau^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(g^{00})^{-1}g^{ij}\psi

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(g^{00})^{-1}\Big[2h^{0a}\partial_{a}\partial_{\tau}h^{ij}+g^{ab}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}h^{ij}-\mathcal{N}^{ij}\Big].

(6.13)

We estimate the latter integral in (

) the same way as we did the for the last integral in (
6.10
) (using Lemmas
5.4
and
5.1
) and calculate a similar bound:

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(g^{00})^{-1}\Big[2h^{0a}\partial_{a}\partial_{\tau}h^{ij}+g^{ab}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}h^{ij}-\mathcal{N}^{ij}\Big]\Big|\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-\delta)}.

(6.14)

For the former integral in (

), we decompose the integrand

\int(g^{00})^{-1}g^{ij}\psi=\>\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(g^{00})^{-1}g^{ij}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(g^{00})^{-1}g^{ij}\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}.

) using Cauchy-Schwartz, the Sobolev embedding, Lemma
5.1
and the bootstrap assumption:

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(g^{00})^{-1}g^{ij}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}\Big|\leq C\|(g^{00})^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|g^{ij}\|_{L^{2}}|\psi_{\mathrm{av}}|\leq C|\psi_{\mathrm{av}}|.

(6.16)

The second integral on the RHS of (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(g^{00})^{-1}g^{ij}\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}=

\displaystyle\>\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}\Big[((g^{00})^{-1})_{\mathrm{av}}g_{\mathrm{av}}^{ij}+((g^{00})^{-1})_{\mathrm{av}}h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{ij}+((g^{00})^{-1})_{\mathrm{osc}}g_{\mathrm{av}}^{ij}+((g^{00})^{-1})_{\mathrm{osc}}h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{ij}\Big]

\displaystyle\>\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}\Big[((g^{00})^{-1})_{\mathrm{av}}h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{ij}+((g^{00})^{-1})_{\mathrm{osc}}g_{\mathrm{av}}^{ij}+((g^{00})^{-1})_{\mathrm{osc}}h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{ij}\Big].

\displaystyle\Big|\int(g^{00})^{-1}g^{ij}\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}\Big|\leq

\displaystyle\>\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(g^{00})^{-1}h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{ij}\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}\Big|+|g_{\mathrm{av}}^{ij}|\cdot\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}((g^{00})^{-1})_{\mathrm{osc}}\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}\Big|

\displaystyle\>C\big(\|(g^{00})^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{ij}\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}\|_{L^{2}}+|g_{\mathrm{av}}^{ij}|\cdot\|((g^{00})^{-1})_{\mathrm{osc}}\|_{L^{2}}\|\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}\|_{L^{2}}\big)

6.2
ODE for the averaged lapse and shift perturbations

Next we derive a system of first-order ODE for the spatial averages of the lapse and shift metric
h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}
,
h_{\mathrm{av}}^{0i}
. We derive these from the gauge constraint equations
D^{\mu}=\Gamma^{\mu}(g)-\mathcal{Y}^{\mu}=0
, as they have better structure than the evolution equations for
h^{00}
,
h^{0i}
, at the cost of a loss of derivatives. Since we’re only using these to estimate the

norms of the functions
h^{00}
,
h^{0i}
,
\partial_{\tau}h^{00}
,
\partial_{\tau}h^{0i}
, this is not an issue.

Using the identity

\partial_{\lambda}g^{\mu\nu}=-g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}\partial_{\lambda}g_{\alpha\beta},

(6.22)

The contracted Christoffel symbols satisfy

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mu}

\displaystyle=g^{\alpha\beta}g^{\mu\lambda}\big(\partial_{(\alpha}g_{\beta)\lambda}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\lambda}g_{\alpha\beta}\big)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\lambda}g^{\lambda\mu}+\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}g^{\mu\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}g^{\alpha\beta}.

(6.23)

Keeping in mind that the components of
m
are constant and
m^{0i}=0
, this implies that

\displaystyle\Gamma^{0}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\tau}h^{00}-\partial_{a}h^{0a}+\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}h^{\alpha\beta}+\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}h^{0a}\partial_{a}h^{\alpha\beta},

(6.24a)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{i}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\tau}h^{0i}-\partial_{a}h^{ia}+\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}h^{0i}\partial_{\tau}h^{\alpha\beta}+\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}g^{ia}\partial_{a}h^{\alpha\beta},

(6.24b)

moreover we expand the term

\displaystyle g_{\alpha\beta}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}h^{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=g_{00}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}h^{00}+2h_{0i}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}h^{0i}+g_{ab}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}h^{ab}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\tau}h^{00}-h_{0a}h^{0a}\partial_{\tau}h^{00}+2h_{0i}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}h^{0i}+g_{ab}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}h^{ab}.

(6.25)

We assume the wave constraint equations (
3.28
) are satisfied, so that

\Gamma^{0}(g)=\frac{2q+1}{\tau}h^{00},\hskip 20.00003pt\Gamma^{i}(g)=\frac{2q+2}{\tau}h^{0i},\hskip 10.00002pti=1,2,3.

(6.26)

These are therefore equivalent to the following transport equations:

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}h^{00}+\frac{4q+2}{\tau}h^{00}=\>

\displaystyle g_{ab}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}h^{ab}-2\partial_{a}h^{0a}+h_{0a}h^{0a}\partial_{\tau}h^{00}+2h_{0a}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}h^{0a}+g_{\alpha\beta}h^{0a}\partial_{a}h^{\alpha\beta},

(6.27a)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}h^{0i}+\frac{2q+2}{\tau}h^{0i}=\>

\displaystyle-\partial_{a}h^{ab}+\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}h^{0i}\partial_{\tau}h^{\alpha\beta}+\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}g^{ia}\partial_{a}h^{\alpha\beta}.

(6.27b)

We use equations (
6.27a
), (
6.27b
) to derive differential equations satisfied by the averaged lapse and shift.

Lemma 6.3

(ODE for the spatial averages of the lapse and shift)
.

Suppose
\mathcal{D}^{\mu}=0
for
\mu=0,1,2,3
. The averaged lapse and shift perturbations
h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}
,
h_{\mathrm{av}}^{0i}
satisfy the following differential inequalities:

\displaystyle\Big|\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}(\tau)+\frac{4q+2}{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}(\tau)\Big|\leq

\displaystyle\>C\Big(\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}|\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ab}(\tau)|+\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-\delta)}\Big),

(6.28a)

\displaystyle\Big|\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{0i}(\tau)+\frac{2q+2}{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{0i}(\tau)\Big|\leq

\displaystyle\>C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-\delta)},\hskip 10.00002pti=1,2,3.

(6.28b)

Proof.

We integrate the equations (
6.27a
), (
6.27b
) over the torus. Any terms which are solely spatial derivatives have zero average, giving

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}+\frac{4q+2}{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}g_{ab}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}h^{ab}+\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big[h_{0a}h^{0a}\partial_{\tau}h^{00}+2h_{0a}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}h^{0a}+g_{\alpha\beta}h^{0a}\partial_{a}h^{\alpha\beta}\Big],

(6.29a)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{0i}+\frac{2q+2}{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{0i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(2\pi)^{3}}\int[g_{\alpha\beta}g^{ia}\partial_{a}h^{\alpha\beta}+g_{\alpha\beta}h^{0i}\partial_{\tau}h^{\alpha\beta}\Big],

(6.29b)

We treat the equation (
6.29a
) for
h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}
first. Estimating the second integral using Cauchy-Schwartz and the bootstrap assumption:

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big[h_{0a}h^{0a}\partial_{\tau}h^{00}+

\displaystyle 2h_{0a}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}h^{0a}+g_{\alpha\beta}h^{0a}\partial_{a}h^{\alpha\beta}\Big]\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\>C\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{4}\sum_{a=1}^{3}\Big[\|h_{0a}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|h^{0a}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial h^{00}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|h_{0a}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|g^{00}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial h^{0a}\|_{L^{2}}+\|g_{\alpha\beta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|h^{0a}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial g^{\alpha\beta}\|_{L^{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\>C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-\delta)}.

(6.30)

For the first integral term on the right hand side of (
6.29a
) we decompose into average and oscillating factors. Keeping in mind that all terms which contain a single oscillating factor have zero average, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}g_{ab}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}h^{ab}=

\displaystyle\>\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}\Big(\>\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}g_{ab}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ab}+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}g_{ab}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{ab}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\>\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}\Big(\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ab}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}g_{ab}g^{00}+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big[g_{ab}h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{00}\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{ab}+(g_{ab})_{\mathrm{osc}}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{ab}\Big]\Big).

(6.31)

Then we estimate

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}g_{ab}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}h^{ab}\Big|\leq

\displaystyle\>C\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}\Big(|\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ab}|\cdot\|g_{ab}\|_{L^{2}}\|g^{00}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|g_{ab}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{00}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{ab}\|_{L^{2}}+|h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}|\cdot\|(g_{ab})_{\mathrm{osc}}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{ab}\|\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\>C\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}\Big(|\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ab}|\cdot\|g_{ab}\|_{L^{2}}\|g^{00}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|g_{ab}\|_{H^{2}}\|\overline{\partial}h^{00}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{\tau}h^{ab}\|_{L^{2}}+\|h^{00}\|_{L^{2}}\|\overline{\partial}g_{ab}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{\tau}h^{ab}\|_{L^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\>C\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}|\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ab}(\tau)|+C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-\delta)},

(6.32)

with the first inequality following from Cauchy-Schwartz, the second from a combination of the Sobolev embedding, the Poincaré inequality, (
6.2
), and the final inequality from the bootstrap assumption and Lemma
5.2
to bound the lowered metric components.
The equation (
6.29a
) combined with the error bounds (
6.30
), (
6.32
), yields the ODE (
6.28a
).

For the equation (
6.29b
) we estimate one error term with Cauchy-Schwartz and the bootstrap assumption:

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}g_{\alpha\beta}h^{0i}\partial_{\tau}h^{\alpha\beta}\Big|\lesssim\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{3}\|g_{\alpha\beta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|h^{0i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial h^{\alpha\beta}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-\delta)},

(6.33)

while we decompose the other term into averaged and oscillatory factors and estimate in an analogous manner to (
6.32
):

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}g_{\alpha\beta}g^{ia}\partial_{a}h^{\alpha\beta}\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big[(g_{\mathrm{av}})_{\alpha\beta}g_{\mathrm{osc}}^{ia}\partial_{a}h^{\alpha\beta}+(g_{\mathrm{osc}})_{\alpha\beta}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ia}\partial_{a}h^{\alpha\beta}+(g_{\mathrm{osc}})_{\alpha\beta}h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{ia}\partial_{a}h^{\alpha\beta}\Big]\mathrm{d}^{3}x\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{4}\sum_{a=1}^{3}\Big(|(g_{\mathrm{av}})_{\alpha\beta}|\cdot\|g_{\mathrm{osc}}^{ia}\|_{L^{2}}\|\overline{\partial}h^{\alpha\beta}\|_{L^{2}}+\|(g_{\mathrm{osc}})_{\alpha\beta}\|_{L^{2}}\|g^{ia}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\overline{\partial}h^{\alpha\beta}\|_{L^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{4}\sum_{a=1}^{3}\Big(\|g_{\alpha\beta}\|_{L^{2}}\|\overline{\partial}g^{ia}\|_{L^{2}}\|\overline{\partial}h^{\alpha\beta}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\overline{\partial}g_{\alpha\beta}\|_{L^{2}}\|g^{ia}\|_{H^{2}}\|\overline{\partial}h^{\alpha\beta}\|_{L^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-\delta)}.

(6.34)

Plugging the estimates (
6.33
), (
6.34
) into (
6.29b
) we obtain the desired inequality (
6.28b
).
∎

6.3
Estimates for the averaged quantities

Combining Lemmas
6.1
and
6.3
we arrive at the following system of ordinary differential inequalities:

\displaystyle\Big|\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}(\tau)+\frac{4q+2}{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}(\tau)\Big|\leq\>C\Big(\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}|\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ab}(\tau)|+\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-\delta)}\Big),

(6.35a)

\displaystyle\Big|\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{0i}(\tau)+\frac{2q+2}{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{0i}(\tau)\Big|\leq\>C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-\delta)},

(6.35b)

\displaystyle\Big|\partial_{\tau}^{2}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ij}(\tau)+\frac{2q+2}{\tau}\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ij}(\tau)\Big|\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}|\psi_{\mathrm{av}}(\tau)|+\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-\delta)}\Big),

(6.35c)

\displaystyle\Big|\partial_{\tau}^{2}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}(\tau)+\frac{2q+2}{\tau}\partial_{\tau}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}(\tau)+\frac{2(2q+1)(q+2)}{\tau^{2}}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}(\tau)\Big|\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-\delta)}.

(6.35d)

We introduce the norm
S_{\mathrm{av}}
for the averaged quantities
(h_{\mathrm{av}}^{\mu\nu},\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{\mu\nu},\psi_{\mathrm{av}},\partial_{\tau}\psi_{\mathrm{av}})
:

S_{\mathrm{av}}(\tau):=\tau^{q-1}|h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}(\tau)|+\tau^{q}|\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}(\tau)|+\sum_{a=1}^{3}\Big\{\tau^{q-1}|h_{\mathrm{av}}^{0a}(\tau)|+\tau^{q}|\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{0a}(\tau)|\Big\}\\
+\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}\Big\{|h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ab}(\tau)|+\tau^{q}|\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ab}(\tau)|\Big\}+\tau^{q+\delta-1}|\psi_{\mathrm{av}}(\tau)|+\tau^{q+\delta}|\partial_{\tau}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}(\tau)|.

(6.36)

This norm satisfies the bound

S_{\mathrm{av}}(\tau)\leq C\varepsilon\tau^{\delta}

(6.37)

as an immediate consequence of the bound (
6.2
) and the bootstrap assumption. In the next proposition we use the system (
6.35
) to improve this bound.

Proposition 6.4

(Estimate for the spatial averages)
.

Suppose the bootstrap assumption (
4.15
) holds on the interval
I=[\tau_{0},T]
, and suppose the initial data satisfies the bound (
4.7
). Then the norm
S_{\mathrm{av}}
satisfies the following estimate for all
\tau\in I
:

S_{\mathrm{av}}(\tau)\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

(6.38)

Remark 6.5

(Reduced decay of averages from nonlinear coupling)
.

The weights in
\tau
in the various terms that make up the norm
S_{\mathrm{av}}
rescale the leading-order behaviour of the quantities
(h_{\mathrm{av}}^{\mu\nu},\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{\mu\nu},\psi_{\mathrm{av}},\partial_{\tau}\psi_{\mathrm{av}})
. Consequently, the improved bound (
6.38
) on
S_{\mathrm{av}}
implies the corresponding amount of decay of these quantities. These decay rates are weaker than the principle ODE (the left hand side of the system (
6.35
)) predict. For example, the principle terms in the ODE (
6.35b
) for the
h_{\mathrm{av}}^{0i}
imply that
h_{\mathrm{av}}^{0i}=O(\tau^{-(q+1)})
, not
O(\tau^{-q})
as implied by (
6.38
). Ultimately the weaker decay is due to the
\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-\delta)}
inhomogeneous terms present on the right hand side of the system (
6.35
), which arise from the nonlinear coupling of the system to the oscillating terms
(h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{\mu\nu},\psi_{\mathrm{osc}})
. The decay of the oscillating terms limit or bottleneck the decay available from the principle system for the averaged quantities.

Proof.

Let us define the following rescaled functions

\displaystyle\mathfrak{u}^{00}:=\tau^{q-1}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00},\hskip 20.00003pt\mathfrak{u}^{0i}:=\tau^{q}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{0i},\hskip 20.00003pt\mathfrak{u}^{ij}:=h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ij},\hskip 20.00003pt\mathfrak{v}^{ij}:=\tau^{q}\partial_{\tau}\mathfrak{u}^{ij},

(6.39a)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{r}:=\tau^{q+\delta-1}\psi_{\mathrm{av}},\hskip 20.00003pt\mathfrak{s}:=\tau\partial_{\tau}\mathfrak{r}.

(6.39b)

We claim that the system (
6.35
) implies the following differential equalities/inequalities:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\tau}(\mathfrak{u}^{00})^{2}\leq-\frac{3q+3}{\tau}(\mathfrak{u}^{00})^{2}+\frac{C}{\tau}\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}|\mathfrak{u}^{00}||\mathfrak{v}^{ab}|+C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-(q+1-2\delta)}|\mathfrak{u}^{00}|,

(6.40a)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\tau}(\mathfrak{u}^{0i})^{2}\leq-\frac{q+2}{\tau}(\mathfrak{u}^{0i})^{2}+C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-(q-2\delta)}|\mathfrak{u}^{0i}|,

(6.40b)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\tau}(\mathfrak{u}^{ij})^{2}=\frac{1}{\tau^{q}}\mathfrak{u}^{ij}\mathfrak{v}^{ij},\hskip 20.00003pt\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\tau}\mathfrak{r}^{2}=\frac{1}{\tau}\mathfrak{r}\mathfrak{s}

(6.40c)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\tau}(\mathfrak{v}^{ij})^{2}\leq-\frac{q+2}{\tau}(\mathfrak{v}^{ij})^{2}+\frac{C}{\tau^{1+\delta}}|\mathfrak{v}^{ij}||\mathfrak{r}|+C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-(q-2\delta)}|\mathfrak{v}^{ij}|,

(6.40d)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\tau}\mathfrak{s}^{2}\leq-\frac{3-2\delta}{\tau}\mathfrak{s}^{2}-\frac{c_{1}}{\tau}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{r}+C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-(q-3\delta)}|\mathfrak{s}|,

(6.40e)

where
c_{1}
is the positive constant

c_{1}=3q^{2}+9q+6-3\delta+\delta^{2}.

(6.41)

The identities in (
6.40c
), are true by the definitions of the
\mathfrak{v}^{ij}
and
\mathfrak{s}
respectively. The inequality (
6.40a
) follows from the computation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\tau}(\mathfrak{u}^{00})^{2}

\displaystyle=\tau^{q-1}\mathfrak{u}^{00}\big(\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}+\frac{q-1}{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}\big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{3q+3}{\tau}(\mathfrak{u}^{00})^{2}+\tau^{q-1}\mathfrak{u}^{00}\big(\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}+\frac{4q+2}{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}\big)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{3q+3}{\tau}(\mathfrak{u}^{00})^{2}+\frac{C}{\tau}\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}|\mathfrak{u}^{00}||\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ab}|+C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-(q+1-2\delta)}|\mathfrak{u}^{00}|,

(6.42)

where in the last line we used (
6.35a
). The inequality (
6.40b
) follows similarly from (
6.35b
). For (
6.40d
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\tau}(\mathfrak{v}^{ij})^{2}=

\displaystyle\>\tau^{q}\mathfrak{v}^{ij}\big(\partial_{\tau}^{2}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ij}+\frac{q}{\tau}\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ij}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\>\tau^{q}\mathfrak{v}^{ij}\big(\partial_{\tau}^{2}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ij}+\frac{2q+2}{\tau}\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ij}\big)-\frac{q+2}{\tau}(\mathfrak{v}^{ij})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\>\frac{C}{\tau}|\mathfrak{v}^{ij}||\mathfrak{r}|+C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-(q-2\delta)}|\mathfrak{v}^{ij}|-\frac{q+2}{\tau}(\mathfrak{v}^{ij})^{2},

(6.43)

where in the last line we used (
6.35c
). Finally for (
6.40e
), we compute that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\tau}\mathfrak{s}^{2}=

\displaystyle\>\mathfrak{s}\partial_{\tau}(\tau^{q+\delta}\partial_{\tau}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}+(q+\delta-1)\tau^{q+\delta-1}\psi_{\mathrm{av}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\>\tau^{q+\delta}\mathfrak{s}\Big(\partial_{\tau}^{2}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}+\frac{2q+2\delta-1}{\tau}\partial_{\tau}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}+\frac{(q+\delta-1)^{2}}{\tau^{2}}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\>\tau^{q+\delta}\mathfrak{s}\Big(\partial_{\tau}^{2}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}+\frac{2q+2}{\tau}\partial_{\tau}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}+\frac{2(2q+1)(q+2)}{\tau^{2}}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}\Big)

\displaystyle-(3-2\delta)\tau^{q+\delta-1}\mathfrak{s}\partial_{\tau}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}-\big(3q^{2}+(12-2\delta)q+(3+2\delta-\delta^{2})\big)\tau^{q+\delta-2}\mathfrak{s}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}.

(6.44)

We estimate by (
6.35d

\tau^{q+\delta}\mathfrak{s}\Big(\partial_{\tau}^{2}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}+\frac{2q+2}{\tau}\partial_{\tau}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}+\frac{2(2q+1)(q+2)}{\tau^{2}}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}\Big)\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-(q-3\delta)}|\mathfrak{s}|,

(6.45)

moreover

-(3-2\delta)\tau^{q+\delta-1}\mathfrak{s}\partial_{\tau}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}-\Big[3q^{2}+(12-2\delta)q+(3+2\delta-\delta^{2})\Big]\tau^{q+\delta-2}\mathfrak{s}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}\\
=-\frac{3-2\delta}{\tau}\mathfrak{s}^{2}-\frac{3q^{2}+9q+6-3\delta+\delta^{2}}{\tau^{2}}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{r}.

(6.46)

The constant
c_{1}
is clearly positive for

sufficiently small. Combining the above computations, (
6.40e
) follows.

Next we derive estimates for the system
(\mathfrak{u}^{\mu\nu},\mathfrak{v}^{ij},\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{s})
using the system (
6.40a
) - (
6.40e
). The estimate

(\mathfrak{u}^{0i}(\tau))^{2}\leq C\big((\mathfrak{u}^{0i}(\tau_{0}))^{2}+\varepsilon^{4}\tau^{-\delta}\big)

follows from (

) by a straightforward application of Cauchy-Schwartz and Grönwall’s inequality. For the remaining quantities, we estimate the
\mathfrak{r}
,
\mathfrak{s}
(which are derived from
\psi_{\mathrm{av}}

h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}

\psi_{\mathrm{av}}

) and (

(6.48)

Note the cancellation of the mixed

terms. This implies by a Grönwall-type inequality that

\frac{c_{1}}{2}\mathfrak{r}^{2}(\tau)+\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{s}^{2}(\tau)\leq C(\mathfrak{r}^{2}(\tau_{0})+\mathfrak{s}^{2}(\tau_{0})+\varepsilon^{4}\tau^{-\delta}),

(6.49)

as long as

. We must estimate the
\mathfrak{u}^{00}
and
\mathfrak{v}^{ij}
jointly, due to the mixed

term present in (

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\Big[\frac{1}{2}(\mathfrak{u}^{00})^{2}+\frac{c_{2}}{2}\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}(\mathfrak{v}^{ab})^{2}\Big]\leq

\displaystyle\>-\frac{3q+3}{\tau}(\mathfrak{u}^{00})^{2}+\frac{C}{\tau}\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}|\mathfrak{u}^{00}||\mathfrak{v}^{ab}|-\frac{c_{2}(q+2)}{\tau}\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}(\mathfrak{v}^{ab})^{2}

\displaystyle+C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-(q+1-2\delta)}|\mathfrak{u}^{00}|+C\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}\Big(\frac{1}{\tau^{1+\delta}}|\mathfrak{r}||\mathfrak{v}^{ab}|+\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-(q-2\delta)}|\mathfrak{v}^{ab}|\Big).

(6.50)

We fix

to be sufficiently large that the quadratic terms on the RHS of the above equation are non-positive, i.e. we have

-\frac{3q+3}{\tau}(\mathfrak{u}^{00})^{2}+\frac{C}{\tau}\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}|\mathfrak{u}^{00}||\mathfrak{v}^{ab}|-\frac{c_{2}(q+2)}{\tau}\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}(\mathfrak{v}^{ab})^{2}\leq 0.

(6.51)

Then we apply the inequality (
6.49
) to estimate
|\mathfrak{r}|
and apply Cauchy-Schwartz to obtain

\partial_{\tau}\Big(\frac{1}{2}(\mathfrak{u}^{00})^{2}+\frac{c_{2}}{2}\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}(\mathfrak{v}^{ab})^{2}\Big)\leq\frac{C}{\tau^{1+\delta}}\Big((\mathfrak{u}^{00})^{2}+\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}(\mathfrak{v}^{ab})^{2}\Big)+\frac{C}{\tau^{1+\delta}}(\mathfrak{r}^{2}(\tau_{0})+\mathfrak{s}^{2}(\tau_{0})+\varepsilon^{4}),

by Grönwall. We then derive the bound

S_{\mathrm{av}}(\tau)

\displaystyle\tau^{q-1-\delta}|h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}|\sim\tau^{-\delta}|\mathfrak{u}^{00}|,

(6.55a)

\displaystyle\tau^{q-\delta}|h_{\mathrm{av}}^{0i}|\sim|\mathfrak{u}^{0i}|,

(6.55b)

\displaystyle|h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ij}|+\tau^{q}|\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{ij}|\sim|\mathfrak{u}^{ij}|+|\mathfrak{v}^{ij}|,

(6.55d)

and applying the estimates (

) - (

). The quantities

and

6.35a
), (

6.47

) and hence the initial data bound (

(6.57)

This completes the proof of Proposition
6.4
.

7
Energy estimates

In this section we introduce a family of weighted energies, then we derive differential inequalities for them, provided the bootstrap assumption (
4.15
) holds. We derive higher-order versions of these energies, and prove analogous differential inequalities for those. These weighted energies will be used to derive estimates for the oscillatory functions
(h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{\mu\nu},\psi_{\mathrm{osc}})
in the next section. For this section however, we will derive inequalities that hold for any (sufficiently regular) scalar function
u
with zero spatial average.

We introduce the parameters
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and define the following energy:

E_{\alpha,\beta}[u](\tau):=\frac{1}{2\tau^{2\beta}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big[-g^{00}(\partial_{\tau}(\tau^{\alpha}u))^{2}+\tau^{2\alpha}g^{ab}\partial_{a}u\partial_{b}u\Big]\mathrm{d}^{3}x.

(7.1)

In the following lemma we demonstrate that the family of energies
E_{\alpha,\beta}
are coercive for functions
u
with zero spatial average.

Lemma 7.1
.

Suppose
u
is a function with zero spatial average, so that
u=u_{\mathrm{osc}}
. Then
E_{\alpha,\beta}[u]
satisfies the following inequalities:

\frac{1}{C}\tau^{2(\alpha-\beta)}(\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial u\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\leq E_{\alpha,\beta}[u]\leq C\tau^{2(\alpha-\beta)}(\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

(7.2)

Proof.

This is a straightforward application of the bootstrap assumption and the Poincaré inequality. We will prove the first inequality, the second follows by a similar argument. The bootstrap assumption implies

1\leq-Cg^{00},\hskip 20.00003pt\delta^{ij}\leq Cg^{ij},\hskip 10.00002pti,j=1,2,3,

(7.3)

and therefore

\|\partial_{\tau}(\tau^{\alpha}u)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\tau^{2\alpha}\|\overline{\partial}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big[-g^{00}(\partial_{\tau}(\tau^{\alpha}u))^{2}+\tau^{2\alpha}g^{ij}\partial_{i}u\partial_{j}u].

(7.4)

As
u
has zero spatial average, we apply the Poincaré inequality to bound

\displaystyle\tau^{2(\alpha-\beta)}(\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial u\|_{L^{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\tau^{2\beta}}\big(\tau^{2\alpha}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{\tau}(\tau^{\alpha}u)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\tau^{2\alpha}\|\overline{\partial}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\tau^{2\beta}}\big(\|\partial_{\tau}(\tau^{\alpha}u)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\tau^{2\alpha}\|\overline{\partial}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big).

(7.5)

Combining (
7.4
) and (
7.5
) yields (
7.2
).
∎

7.1
Energy differential inequality

We now derive the basic energy inequality for the family of energies
E_{\alpha,\beta}
.

Lemma 7.2

(Energy inequality)
.

Suppose
u
is a function with zero spatial average, and let
\beta,\alpha\geq 0
. The energy
E_{\alpha,\beta}[u]
satisfies the differential inequality

\partial_{\tau}E_{\alpha,\beta}[u](\tau)\leq-\frac{2\beta}{\tau}E_{\alpha,\beta}[u](\tau)+C\Big(\frac{1}{\tau^{1+\delta}}E_{\alpha,\beta}[u](\tau)+\tau^{2(\alpha-\beta)+1}\|P_{\alpha}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big),

(7.6a)

where
P_{\alpha}
is the wave operator

P_{\alpha}:=\widehat{\square}_{g}+\frac{2\alpha}{\tau}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}.

(7.7)

Remark 7.3

(Justification of weights in the energy
E_{\alpha,\beta}
)
.

The introduction of the weights in time for the energy
E_{\alpha,\beta}[u]
rescale the leading-order behaviour of the function
u
, so that (using Lemma
7.1
) one can prove decay of
u
. The first parameter
\alpha
introduces the principal damping term
2\alpha\tau^{-1}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}
(contained in
P_{\alpha}
) to the differential inequality for
E_{\alpha,\beta}
. Such damping terms are present in the gauge-fixed system (
3.33
) (and therefore in the equations for the oscillatory remainders
(h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{\mu\nu},\psi_{\mathrm{osc}})
). More specifically,
\alpha=q
for the damping term in the equation (
3.33a
) for
h^{00}
, while
\alpha=q+1
for the damping term in all the other equations. We can fix
\alpha
in the energies for these quantities to exactly cancel with the damping terms in the gauge-fixed system. The actual decay of the quantities
(h^{\mu\nu},\psi)
are less than what the damping term
2\alpha\tau^{-1}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}
predicts, however. This is due to the linear and nonlinear coupling of the equations for the
h^{00}
,
h^{0i}
,
h^{ij}
,
\psi
. The weight
\tau^{-2\beta}
in
E_{\alpha,\beta}
are introduced to compensate for this reduced decay. See (
8.1
) for the specific choice of energies we make for the quantities
(h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{\mu\nu},\psi_{\mathrm{osc}})
.

Proof.

Let
v=\tau^{\alpha}u
, so that

E_{\alpha,\beta}[u]=\frac{1}{2\tau^{2\beta}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big[-g^{00}(\partial_{\tau}v)^{2}+g^{ij}\partial_{i}v\partial_{j}v\Big].

(7.8)

We differentiate
E_{\alpha,\beta}[u]
in time:

\partial_{\tau}E_{\alpha,\beta}[u]=-\frac{2\beta}{\tau}E_{\alpha,\beta}+\frac{1}{\tau^{2\beta}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big[-g^{00}\partial_{\tau}v\partial_{\tau}^{2}v+g^{ab}\partial_{a}v\partial_{b}\partial_{\tau}v\Big]\\
-\frac{1}{2\tau^{2\beta}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big[(\partial_{\tau}h^{00})(\partial_{\tau}v)^{2}-(\partial_{\tau}h^{ab})\partial_{a}v\partial_{b}v\Big].

(7.9)

The product rule implies

\displaystyle\partial_{b}(g^{ab}\partial_{a}v\partial_{\tau}v)=

\displaystyle\>\partial_{b}h^{ab}\partial_{a}v\partial_{\tau}v+g^{ab}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}v\partial_{\tau}v+g^{ab}\partial_{a}v\partial_{\tau}\partial_{b}v,

(7.10a)

\displaystyle\partial_{a}(g^{0a}(\partial_{\tau}v)^{2})=

\displaystyle\>\partial_{b}g^{0a}(\partial_{\tau}v)^{2}+2g^{0a}\partial_{\tau}\partial_{a}v\partial_{\tau}v,

(7.10b)

and so we integrate by parts and compute

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big[-\partial_{\tau}vg^{00}\partial_{\tau}^{2}v+g^{ab}\partial_{a}v\partial_{b}\partial_{\tau}v\Big]=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(\partial_{\tau}v)\Big[g^{00}\partial_{\tau}^{2}v+2g^{0a}\partial_{\tau}\partial_{a}v+g^{ab}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}v\Big]\mathrm{d}^{3}x

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\partial_{a}h^{0a}(\partial_{\tau}v)^{2}+\partial_{b}h^{ab}\partial_{a}v\partial_{\tau}v\Big]\mathrm{d}^{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(\partial_{\tau}v)(\widehat{\square}_{g}v)\mathrm{d}^{3}x-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\partial_{a}h^{0a}(\partial_{\tau}v)^{2}+\partial_{b}h^{ab}\partial_{a}v\partial_{\tau}v\Big]\mathrm{d}^{3}x.

(7.11)

Thus we obtain the following differential equation for
E_{\alpha,\beta}[u]
:

\partial_{\tau}E_{\alpha,\beta}[u]=-\frac{2\beta}{\tau}E_{\alpha,\beta}[u]-\frac{1}{\tau^{2\beta}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(\partial_{\tau}v)(\widehat{\square}_{g}v)\\
-\frac{1}{2\tau^{2\beta}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big[(\partial_{\tau}h^{00})(\partial_{\tau}v)^{2}-(\partial_{\tau}h^{ab})\partial_{a}v\partial_{b}v+(\partial_{a}h^{0a})(\partial_{\tau}v)^{2}+2(\partial_{b}h^{ab})\partial_{a}v\partial_{\tau}v\Big].

(7.12)

The final integral in (
7.12
) is an error term. We estimate

\frac{1}{\tau^{2\beta}}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\Big[(\partial_{\tau}h^{00})(\partial_{\tau}v)^{2}-(\partial_{\tau}h^{ab})\partial_{a}v\partial_{b}v+(\partial_{a}h^{0a})(\partial_{\tau}v)^{2}+2(\partial_{b}h^{ab})\partial_{a}v\partial_{\tau}v\Big]\mathrm{d}^{3}x\Big|\leq\\
\leq\frac{C}{\tau^{2\beta}}\sum_{\mu,\nu=0}^{3}\|\partial h^{\mu\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big(\|\partial v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)\leq C\sum_{\mu,\nu=0}^{3}\|\partial h^{\mu\nu}\|_{H^{2}}E_{\alpha,\beta}[u]\leq C\varepsilon\tau^{-(q-\delta)}E_{\alpha,\beta}[u],

(7.13)

where the first inequality is due to Cauchy-Schwartz, the second uses the Sobolev embedding and Lemma
7.1
, and the last inequality follows from the bootstrap assumption.

Next we estimate the integral of
(\partial_{\tau}v)(\widehat{\square}_{g}v)
in (
7.12
). We calculate

\displaystyle\widehat{\square}_{g}v=

\displaystyle\>\tau^{\alpha}\Big(\widehat{\square}_{g}u+\frac{2\alpha}{\tau}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}u+\frac{2\alpha}{\tau}h^{0a}\partial_{a}u+\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)}{\tau^{2}}g^{00}u\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\>\tau^{\alpha}P_{\alpha}u+\frac{2\alpha}{\tau}h^{0a}\partial_{a}v+\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)}{\tau^{2}}g^{00}v,

(7.14)

so that

-\frac{1}{\tau^{2\beta}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(\partial_{\tau}v)(\square_{g}v)=-\tau^{\alpha-2\beta}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(\partial_{\tau}v)(P_{\alpha}u)\\
-\frac{2\alpha}{\tau^{2\beta+1}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}h^{0a}\partial_{a}v\partial_{\tau}v+\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)}{\tau^{2\beta+2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}g^{00}v\partial_{\tau}v.

(7.15)

We estimate the three integrals on the RHS of the above equation using a combination of Cauchy-Schwartz, Lemma
7.1
, and the bootstrap assumption:

\displaystyle\tau^{\alpha-2\beta}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}(\partial_{\tau}v)(P_{\alpha}u)\Big|\leq C\tau^{\alpha-2\beta}\|\partial_{\tau}v\|_{L^{2}}\|P_{\beta}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{\beta}{\tau}E_{\alpha,\beta}[u](\tau)+C\tau^{2(\alpha-\beta)+1}\|P_{\alpha}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(7.16a)

\displaystyle\tau^{-(1+2\beta)}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}h^{0a}\partial_{\tau}v\partial_{a}v\>\mathrm{d}^{3}x\Big|\leq C\tau^{-(1+2\beta)}\sum_{a=1}^{3}\|h^{0a}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{\tau}v\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{a}v\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\tau^{-(q+1-\delta)}E_{\alpha,\beta}[u],

(7.16b)

\displaystyle\tau^{-(2+2\beta)}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}g^{00}v\partial_{\tau}v\Big|\leq C\tau^{-(2+2\beta)}\|g^{00}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{\tau}v\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{C}{\tau^{2}}E_{\alpha,\beta}[u].

(7.16c)

Plugging these bounds into (
7.15
), then combining with (
7.12
), (
7.13
) implies the final estimate (
7.6a
).
∎

7.2
Higher order energy

Next we derive a higher-order version of the energy inequality from Lemma
7.2
which controls all higher derivatives in the bootstrap norm
S_{K}(\tau)
. We introduce the energies

E_{\alpha,\beta}^{(K)}[u](\tau):=\sum_{|I|\leq K}E_{\alpha,\beta}[\overline{\partial}{}^{I}u](\tau).

(7.17)

As an immediate consequence of Lemma
7.1
, this energy obeys the inequalities

\frac{1}{C}\tau^{2(\alpha-\beta)}(\|u\|_{H^{K}}^{2}+\|\partial u\|_{H^{K}}^{2})\leq E_{\alpha,\beta}^{(K)}[u](\tau)\leq C\tau^{2(\alpha-\beta)}(\|u\|_{H^{K}}^{2}+\|\partial u\|_{H^{K}}^{2}).

(7.18)

In order to derive an inequality for the higher-order energy
E_{\alpha,\beta}^{(K)}[u]
we first derive an estimate for the commutator
[P_{\alpha},\overline{\partial}{}^{I}]
.

Lemma 7.4

(Wave operator commutator estimate)
.

The wave operators
P_{\alpha}
obeys the estimate

\sum_{|I|\leq K}\|[P_{\alpha},\overline{\partial}{}^{I}]u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\tau^{-(q-\delta)}\Big(\|P_{\alpha}u\|_{H^{K-1}}+\|\partial u\|_{H^{K}}\Big).

(7.19)

Proof.

The operator
P_{\alpha}
satisfies the commutator identity

[P_{\alpha},\overline{\partial}{}^{I}]u=[\widehat{\square}_{g},\overline{\partial}{}^{I}]u+\frac{2\alpha}{\tau}[g^{00}\partial_{\tau},\overline{\partial}{}^{I}]u=[\widehat{\square}_{g},\overline{\partial}{}^{I}]u+\frac{2\alpha}{\tau}[g^{00},\overline{\partial}{}^{I}]\partial_{\tau}u.

(7.20)

We estimate the
[\widehat{\square}_{g},\overline{\partial}{}^{I}]u
term first.
For each multi-index
I
:
|I|\leq K
, the commutator
[\widehat{\square}_{g},\overline{\partial}{}^{I}]u
satisfies the identity

[\widehat{\square}_{g},\overline{\partial}{}^{I}]u=\sum_{0<J\leq I}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{3}c_{I,J}(\overline{\partial}{}^{J}h^{\alpha\beta})(\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\overline{\partial}{}^{I-J}u),

(7.21)

for some constants
c_{I,J}
. Observe that all terms on the right hand side of the above equation contain at least one spatial derivative of the metric components
g^{\mu\nu}
, and at most
|I|+1
spacetime derivatives of
u
, at most
2
of which are purely time derivatives. We sum over all
|I|\leq K
and estimate the factors with fewer than
\lfloor K/2\rfloor
derivatives in
L^{\infty}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{|I|\leq K}\Big(\sum_{0<J\leq I}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{3}\|(\overline{\partial}{}^{J}h^{\alpha\beta})(\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}{}^{I-J}u)\|_{L^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\>\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{3}\Big\{\sum_{\lfloor K/2\rfloor+1\leq|I|\leq K}\Big(\sum_{|J|\leq\lfloor K/2\rfloor-1}\|\overline{\partial}{}^{I}h^{\alpha\beta}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\overline{\partial}{}^{J}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq|I|\leq\lfloor K/2\rfloor+1}\Big(\sum_{\lfloor K/2\rfloor-1\leq|J|\leq K-1}\|\overline{\partial}{}^{I}h^{\alpha\beta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\overline{\partial}{}^{J}u\|_{L^{2}}\Big)\Big\}.

(7.22)

Since
K\geq 3
, we have
\lfloor K/2\rfloor\leq K-2
, and so the Sobolev embedding implies the inequalities

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{3}\sum_{|J|\leq\lfloor K/2\rfloor-1}\|\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\overline{\partial}{}^{J}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq

\displaystyle\>C\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{3}\sum_{|J|\leq K-1}\|\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\overline{\partial}{}^{J}u\|_{L^{2}},

(7.23a)

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{3}\sum_{1\leq|I|\leq\lfloor K/2\rfloor+1}\|\overline{\partial}{}^{I}h^{\alpha\beta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq

\displaystyle\>C\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{3}\|\overline{\partial}h^{\alpha\beta}\|_{H^{K}},

(7.23b)

therefore by the bootstrap assumption we obtain the estimate

\sum_{|I|\leq K}\|[\widehat{\square}_{g},\overline{\partial}{}^{I}]u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{3}\sum_{|I|\leq K-1}\|\overline{\partial}h^{\alpha\beta}\|_{H^{K}}\|\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\overline{\partial}{}^{I}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\varepsilon\tau^{-(q-\delta)}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{3}\sum_{|I|\leq K-1}\|\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\overline{\partial}{}^{I}u\|_{L^{2}}.

(7.24)

By a similar argument, for the commutator
\frac{2\alpha}{\tau}[g^{00},\overline{\partial}{}^{I}]\partial_{\tau}u
we have

\frac{2\alpha}{\tau}\sum_{|I|\leq K}\|[g^{00},\overline{\partial}{}^{I}]\partial_{\tau}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{C}{\tau}\|\overline{\partial}h^{00}\|_{H^{K}}\|\partial_{\tau}u\|_{H^{K}}\leq C\varepsilon\tau^{-(q+1-\delta)}\|\partial_{\tau}u\|_{H^{K}}.

(7.25)

Combining the estimates (
7.24
) and (
7.25
) yields the bound

\displaystyle\sum_{|I|\leq K}\big\|[P_{\alpha},\overline{\partial}{}^{I}]u\|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\>C\varepsilon\tau^{-(q-\delta)}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{3}\sum_{|I|\leq K-1}\|\partial_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\overline{\partial}^{I}u\|_{L^{2}}+C\varepsilon\tau^{-(q+1-\delta)}\|\partial_{\tau}u\|_{H^{K}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\>C\varepsilon\tau^{-(q-\delta)}\Big(\sum_{|I|\leq K-1}\|\partial_{\tau}^{2}\overline{\partial}{}^{I}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial u\|_{H^{K}}\Big).

(7.26)

It remains to estimate the
\partial_{\tau}^{2}\overline{\partial}{}^{I}u
terms. We compute

\partial_{\tau}^{2}\overline{\partial}{}^{I}u=(g^{00})^{-1}\Big(P_{\alpha}\overline{\partial}{}^{I}u-2h^{0a}\partial_{a}\tau\partial_{\tau}\overline{\partial}{}^{I}u-g^{ab}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}\overline{\partial}{}^{I}u-\frac{2\alpha}{\tau}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}\overline{\partial}{}^{I}u\Big),

(7.27)

and estimate via Cauchy-Schwartz, the Sobolev inequality, and the bootstrap assumption:

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha,\beta=0}^{3}\sum_{|I|\leq K-1}\|\partial_{\tau}^{2}\overline{\partial}{}^{I}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C

\displaystyle\sum_{|I|\leq K-1}\Big\{\|P_{\alpha}\overline{\partial}{}^{I}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|g^{0a}\partial_{a}\overline{\partial}{}^{I}\partial_{\tau}u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|g^{ab}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}\overline{\partial}{}^{I}u\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{\tau}\|g^{00}\partial_{\tau}\overline{\partial}{}^{I}u\|_{L^{2}}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq C

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{|I|\leq K-1}\|P_{\alpha}\overline{\partial}{}^{I}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial u\|_{H^{K}}\Big).

(7.28)

Finally, we bound

\sum_{|J|\leq K-1}\|P_{\alpha}\overline{\partial}{}^{J}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|P_{\alpha}u\|_{H^{K-1}}+\sum_{|J|\leq K-1}\|[P_{\alpha},\overline{\partial}{}^{J}]u\|_{L^{2}},

(7.29)

which implies

\sum_{|I|\leq K}\big\|[P_{\alpha},\overline{\partial}{}^{I}]u\|_{L^{2}}\leq\>C\varepsilon\tau^{-(q-\delta)}\Big(\|P_{\alpha}u\|_{H^{K-1}}+\|\partial u\|_{H^{K}}\Big)\\
+C\varepsilon\tau^{-(q-\delta)}\sum_{|J|\leq K-1}\|[P_{\alpha},\overline{\partial}^{J}]u\|_{L^{2}}.

(7.30)

The last term can be absorbed into the left hand side, hence we obtain (
7.19
).
∎

We now state the differential inequalities satisfied by the higher order energy
E_{\alpha,\beta}^{(K)}
defined in (
7.17
).

Proposition 7.5

(Higher-order energy inequality)
.

Let
u
be a sufficiently regular function with zero spatial average. The energy
E_{\alpha,\beta}^{(K)}
satisfies the following differential inequality:

\partial_{\tau}E_{\alpha,\beta}^{(K)}[u](\tau)\leq-\frac{\beta}{\tau}E_{\alpha,\beta}^{(K)}[u](\tau)+C\Big(\frac{1}{\tau^{1+\delta}}E_{\alpha,\beta}^{(K)}[u](\tau)+\tau^{2(\alpha-\beta)+1}\|P_{\alpha}u\|_{H^{K}}^{2}\Big).

(7.31)

Proof.

Lemma
7.2
implies the bound

\partial_{\tau}E_{\alpha,\beta}^{(K)}[u]\leq-\frac{2\beta}{\tau}E_{\alpha,\beta}^{(K)}[u](\tau)+\frac{C}{\tau^{1+\delta}}E_{\alpha,\beta}^{(K)}[u](\tau)+C\tau^{2(\alpha-\beta)+1+\delta}\sum_{|I|\leq K}\|P_{\alpha}(\overline{\partial}{}^{I}u)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(7.32)

Then we apply the wave commutator Lemma
7.4
to estimate the final term:

\displaystyle C\tau^{2(\alpha-\beta)+1+\delta}\sum_{|I|\leq K}\|P_{\alpha}(\overline{\partial}{}^{I}u)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

\displaystyle\leq C\tau^{2(\alpha-\beta)+1+\delta}\Big(\|P_{\alpha}u\|_{H^{K}}^{2}+\sum_{|I|\leq K}\|[P_{\alpha},\overline{\partial}{}^{I}]u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\tau^{2(\alpha-\beta)+1+\delta}\big(\|P_{\alpha}u\|_{H^{K}}^{2}+\varepsilon\tau^{-2(q-\delta)}\|\partial u\|_{H^{K}}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\tau^{2(\alpha-\beta)+1+\delta}\|P_{\alpha}u\|_{H^{K}}^{2}+\frac{C}{\tau^{1+\delta}}E_{\alpha,\beta}^{K},

(7.33)

where the last line holds due to (
7.18
). The result now follows.
∎

8
Estimates for the oscillatory remainders
h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{\mu\nu}
,
\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}

In this section we will derive energy estimates for the oscillatory remainders
h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{\mu\nu}
,
\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}
using the higher-order energy inequality of Proposition
7.5
. We will choose different parameter values
\alpha,\beta
for the energies
E_{\alpha,\beta}^{(K)}
for each of
h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{00}
,
h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{0i}
,
h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{ij}
,
\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}
, specifically we consider the following energies:

E^{00}:=E_{q,\delta}^{(K)}[h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{00}],\hskip 10.00002ptE^{0i}:=E_{q+1,1+\delta}^{(K)}[h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{0i}],\hskip 10.00002ptE^{ij}:=E_{q+1,1+\delta}^{(K)}[h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{ij}],\hskip 10.00002ptE^{(\psi)}:=E_{q+1,1}^{(K)}[\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}].

(8.1)

A short computation using the bound (
6.2
) and the energy coercivity inequalities (
7.18
) shows that these energies are controlled by the bootstrap norm
S_{K}^{2}(\tau)
, from which one immediately obtains the bound

E^{00}+\sum_{a=1}^{3}E^{0a}+\sum_{a,b=1}^{3}E^{ab}+E^{(\psi)}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

(8.2)

The goal of this section is to improve this bound. First we derive
H^{K}
-Sobolev norm bounds on the quantities

P_{q}h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{00},\hskip 10.00002ptP_{q+1}h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{0i},\hskip 10.00002ptP_{q+1}h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{ij},\hskip 10.00002ptP_{q+1}\psi_{\mathrm{osc}},

(8.3)

which appear in the differential inequalities for the energies (
8.1
).

Lemma 8.1
.

Suppose the gauge-fixed equations (
3.33
) hold for
(h^{\mu\nu},\psi)
. Then the quantities (
8.3
) satisfy the following norm bounds:

\displaystyle\|P_{q}h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{00}\|_{H^{K}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{\tau}\|\partial\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}\|_{H^{K}}+\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\|\partial h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{00}\|_{H^{K}}+\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-(q+1+\delta)}\Big),

(8.4a)

\displaystyle\|P_{q+1}h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{0i}\|_{H^{K}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{\tau}\|\partial h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{00}\|_{H^{K}}+\frac{1}{\tau}\|\partial\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}\|_{H^{K}}+\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\|\partial h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{0i}\|_{H^{K}}+\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-\delta)}\Big),

(8.4b)

\displaystyle\|P_{q+1}h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{ij}\|_{H^{K}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\|\partial\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}\|_{H^{K}}+\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-(q+1+\delta)}\Big),

(8.4c)

\displaystyle\|P_{q+1}\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}\|_{H^{K}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\|\partial\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}\|_{H^{K}}+\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-2(q-\delta)}\Big).

(8.4d)

Proof.

We will prove (
8.4a
) first. Since
P_{q}h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{00}=P_{q}h^{00}-P_{q}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}
, we see from the wave equation (
3.33a
) for
h^{00}
that

P_{q}h^{00}=\frac{4q+2}{\tau}g^{00}h^{00}+\mathcal{L}^{00}+\mathcal{N}^{00},

(8.5)

moreover we have

\displaystyle P_{q}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}

\displaystyle=g^{00}\partial_{\tau}^{2}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}+\frac{2q}{\tau}g^{00}\partial_{\tau}h^{00}

\displaystyle=g^{00}\Big(\partial_{\tau}^{2}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}+\frac{2q}{\tau}\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}-\frac{4q+2}{\tau^{2}}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}\Big)+\frac{4q+2}{\tau^{2}}g^{00}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}.

(8.6)

Combining these yields the identity

P_{q}h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{00}=\frac{4q+2}{\tau^{2}}g^{00}h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{00}-g^{00}\Big(\partial_{\tau}^{2}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}+\frac{2q}{\tau}\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}-\frac{4q+2}{\tau^{2}}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}\Big)+\mathcal{L}^{00}+\mathcal{N}^{00}.

(8.7)

It follows from the Sobolev product lemma and the bootstrap assumption that

\displaystyle\|P_{q}h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{00}\|_{H^{K}}\leq

\displaystyle\>\frac{4q+2}{\tau^{2}}\|g^{00}h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{00}\|_{H^{K}}+\Big|\partial_{\tau}^{2}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}+\frac{2q}{\tau}\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}-\frac{4q+2}{\tau^{2}}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}\Big|\cdot\|g^{00}\|_{H^{K}}+\|\mathcal{L}^{00}\|_{H^{K}}+\|\mathcal{N}^{00}\|_{H^{K}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\>C\Big(\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\|h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{00}\|_{H^{K}}+\Big|\partial_{\tau}^{2}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}+\frac{2q}{\tau}\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}-\frac{4q+2}{\tau^{2}}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}\Big|+\|\mathcal{L}^{00}\|_{H^{K}}+\|\mathcal{N}^{00}\|_{H^{K}}\Big).

(8.8)

Now we estimate the right hand side terms. The differential inequality (
6.19a
) for
h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}
(see Remark
6.2
after Lemma
6.1
) and the estimate from Proposition
6.4
implies

\Big|\partial_{\tau}^{2}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}+\frac{2q}{\tau}\partial_{\tau}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}-\frac{4q+2}{\tau^{2}}h_{\mathrm{av}}^{00}\Big|\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}|\psi_{\mathrm{av}}|+\frac{1}{\tau}|\partial_{\tau}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}|\Big)\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-(q+1+\delta)},

(8.9)

while the bootstrap assumption, Proposition
6.4
and the Poincaré inequality gives

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}^{00}\|_{H^{K}}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\tau}\|g^{00}\|_{H^{K}}^{2}\|\partial_{\tau}\psi\|_{H^{K}}+\frac{C}{\tau^{2}}\|g^{00}\|_{H^{K}}\|\psi\|_{H^{K}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\tau}(\|\partial_{\tau}\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}\|_{H^{K}}+|\partial_{\tau}\psi_{\mathrm{av}}|)+\frac{C}{\tau^{2}}(\|\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}\|_{H^{K}}+|\psi_{\mathrm{av}}|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\tau}\|\partial\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}\|_{H^{K}}+C\varepsilon^{2}\tau^{-(q+1+\delta)}.

(8.10)

The error estimate from Proposition

9.2

We have demonstrated that the perturbed solution exists globally. Next we demonstrate that the spacetime

Proposition 9.5

(Completeness of future-causal inextendible geodesics)
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\widetilde{g})
be a Lorentzian manifold constructed in Theorem
4.2
, and let
\gamma:[s_{0},s_{\max})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
be a future-causal inextendible geodesic. Then
\lim_{s\nearrow s_{\max}}\gamma^{0}(s)=\infty
. Moreover,
s_{\max}=\infty
, hence
\gamma
is complete.

Proof.

First we show that
\lim_{s\nearrow s_{\max}}\gamma^{0}(s)=\infty
. As
\dot{\gamma}^{0}(s)>0
for all
s\in[s_{0},s_{\max})
,
\gamma^{0}
is monotonically increasing, and so the only other possibility is that
\gamma^{0}
has a finite limit which is approached from below. Suppose
\lim_{s\nearrow s_{\max}}\gamma^{0}(s)=L<\infty
. Then we have the two cases
s_{\max}=\infty
or
s_{\max}<\infty
. If
s_{\max}=\infty
, let
s_{n}
be a sequence
s_{n}\rightarrow\infty
. For all
s\in[s_{0},s_{\max})
,
\gamma^{0}(s)\leq L
, and so the bound from Lemma
9.4
implies

\dot{\gamma}^{0}(s_{n})\geq\frac{c_{1}}{L^{4p/(1-p)}}.

(9.27)

Integrating this we obtain the lower bound

\gamma^{0}(s_{n})\geq\gamma^{0}(s_{0})+c(s_{n}-s_{0}),

(9.28)

which is unbounded. For the second case
s_{\max}<\infty
, we note that
\gamma^{0}
has a limit, and therefore
\lim_{s\nearrow s_{\max}}\gamma^{i}(s)
does not exist for one of
i=1,2,3
.
\gamma
is future causal, so
\dot{\gamma}^{0}
controls all other components of
\dot{\gamma}
, and therefore by Lemma
9.4
we have

|\dot{\gamma}^{i}(s)|\leq\frac{c_{2}}{(\gamma^{0}(s))^{p/(2p(1-p))}},\hskip 20.00003pti=1,2,3.

(9.29)

Let
s_{n}
be a sequence such that
s_{n}\nearrow s_{\max}<\infty
. Then there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\gamma^{0}(s_{n})\geq L/2
for all
n\geq N
. The above bound then implies that

|\dot{\gamma}^{i}(s_{n})|\leq C

(9.30)

for all
n\geq N
, and so integrating over
[s_{m},s_{n}]
implies

|\gamma^{i}(s_{n})-\gamma^{i}(s_{m})|\leq C|s_{m}-s_{n}|,

(9.31)

implying that
\gamma^{i}(s_{n})

, contradicting the fact that
\lim_{s\nearrow s_{\max}}\gamma(s)

\lim_{s\nearrow s_{\max}}\gamma^{0}(s)=\infty
.

Next we prove that

(9.34)

Remark 9.7

(Higher-order asymptotics)
.

We derive only leading order asymptotics, which are determined primarily from the boundedness of the norm
S_{K}(\tau)
and the weights in
\tau
contained therein. We do not derive higher-order asymptotics, (such as asymptotic expansions) because of the oscillatory nature of the higher derivatives, which ultimately stems from the setting of decelerated expansion.

The oscillating remainders
(h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{\mu\nu},\psi_{\mathrm{osc}})
have a leading-order decay due to spacetime expansion, but once rescaled are purely oscillatory. For example,
|h_{\mathrm{osc}}^{00}|=O(\tau^{-(q-\delta)})
, so take
v=\tau^{q-\delta}\psi_{\mathrm{osc}}
. By the arguments in Sections
7
and
8
, the energy of
v
is bounded (and small), but
v
is oscillatory in nature, and does not possess an asymptotic expansion. This is analogous to the flat homogeneous wave equation
\square u=0
on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{3}
. The remainder
u_{\mathrm{osc}}
has finite energy, but is an infinite sum of the oscillating modes
\exp(ik\cdot x\pm i|k|t)
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\backslash\{0\}
.

We contrast this with the regime of accelerated expansion, where one can derive asymptotic expansions of solutions to the Einstein equations in various settings (see for example
[
HV24
]
). This is possible to do because of the strong localisation that occurs within the spacetimes. Points in spacetime interact with a smaller and smaller portion of the spatial geometry over time, resulting in perturbations which “smooth out” in time (but not necessarily in space).

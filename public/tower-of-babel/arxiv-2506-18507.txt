Mld versus lct near zero
Florin Ambro

Abstract
. We study the equivalence of approaching zero for two invariants of a singularity: the minimal log discrepancy and the log canonical threshold of the general hyperplane section.

1.
Intro

We consider in this note the following question: let
f\colon X\to Y\ni P
be the germ
of a Fano contraction, with positive dimensional base, near the fiber over a special point.
Let
a=\operatorname{mld}_{f^{-1}P}X
be the minimal log discrepancy of
X

in the geometric valuations centered inside the central fiber. For a hyperplane section

P\in H\subset Y
, let
\gamma(H):=\operatorname{lct}(X,f^{*}H)
be the log canonical threshold of
its pullback, that is
\gamma\geq 0
is maximal such that
(X,\gamma f^{*}H)
has log canonical singularities.
If
\dim X
is fixed, is
a
bounded away from zero if and only if
\gamma:=\max_{H}\gamma(H)
is bounded away from zero?

The converse implication follows from the inequality

a\geq\gamma,

which holds since the Cartier closure of
f^{*}H
has multiplicity at least one in each
geometric valuation of
X
centered inside
f^{-1}P
. The direct implication is non-trivial,
but there exists toric evidence.

The question emerged from our study of toric singularities (case
f
isomorphism), while
trying 20 years ago to understand McKernan-Shokurov’s conjecture on the singularities
of Fano fibrations (solved
[
6
]
in the meantime). Our hope was that
Borisov’s classification of toric
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial singularities into finitely many series
[
7
]

would imply McKernan-Shokurov’s conjecture, after finding a geometric interpretation for the
series, which extends to the non-toric case.
Let
P\in X
be a toric singularity of dimension
d
. If
d=2
,
Borisov’s classification has the following geometric
interpretation: the minimal log discrepancy is determined by the log canonical threshold of (one or
two linearly independent) invariant hyperplane sections (see
[
4
]
, which is in fact a preprint
from 2006). In particular,
\gamma\geq\frac{a}{2}
. If
d=3
, invariant hyperplane sections no longer
determine the minimal log discrepancy. For example, the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial toric singularity

0\in\{z_{4}^{2}=z_{1}z_{2}z_{3}\}\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{4}
satisfies
K\sim 0
and

\operatorname{mld}_{0}X=2>\frac{3}{2}=\max\{\sum_{i}t_{i}|\operatorname{mld}(X,\sum_{i}t_{i}H_{i})\geq 0,t_{i}\geq 0,H_{i}\text{ invariant hyperplane section}\}

However, it is still true that
\gamma
is bounded away from zero if so is
a
, as can be deduced
from Borisov’s classification (after replacing
\operatorname{mld}X
by
\operatorname{mld}_{P}X
, and assuming
X
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial).
Borisov’s classification extends to the non-
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial case, but only after enlarging the category of
toric singularities back to the category of germs of toric Fano fibrations. In particular, our question has a positive answer
in the toric case
[
3
, Theorem 3.12]
. Here we also provide an effective estimate.
An effective estimate also exists for (non-toric) surface singularities
[
5
, Theorem 1.7]
.

For
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
and
a\in(0,+\infty)
, define recursively
\gamma(d,a)
by

\gamma(1,a)=a
and
\gamma(d,a)=\gamma(d-1,\frac{a^{2}}{d^{2}})
for
d\geq 2
.
One computes

\gamma(d,a)=\frac{a^{2^{d-1}}}{\prod_{i=1}^{d}i^{2^{i-1}}}.

In particular,
\gamma(d,a)
is increasing in
a
. Recall that for a toric variety
X
,
the complement
\Sigma_{X}
of the open dense torus inside
X
satisfies

K+\Sigma_{X}\sim 0,\operatorname{mld}(X,\Sigma_{X})=0
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y\ni P
be a toric contraction, where
Y
is affine of positive
dimension, containing an invariant point
P
. Suppose
0\leq B\leq\Sigma_{X}
is an
invariant
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor on
X
such that
-(K+B)
is
f
-nef and

\operatorname{mld}_{f^{-1}P}(X,B)=a>0
.
Then there exists an invariant hyperplane section
P\in H\subset Y
such that

(X,B+\gamma(d,a)f^{*}H)
has log canonical singularities, where
d=\dim X
.

Example 1.2
.

Let
f\colon X\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
be a toric contraction. Let
X_{0}=f^{*}(0)
be the special fiber,
let
a>0
such that
B=\Sigma_{X}-aX_{0}
is effective.
Then
K+B\sim_{\mathbb{R}}0
,
\operatorname{mld}_{X_{0}}(X,B)=a
,
\operatorname{mld}_{X_{0}}(X,B+aX_{0})=0
.

Let
n
be a positive integer such that
n\gamma(d,a)\geq 1
. Set
B_{n}=\Sigma_{X}-\frac{1}{n}f^{*}H
.
Then

B\leq B_{n},n(K+B_{n})\sim 0,\operatorname{mld}_{f^{-1}P}(X,B_{n})\geq\frac{1}{n}.

Therefore the toric case of Shokurov’s conjecture
[
9
]
on the existence of klt complements of
bounded index follows from Theorem
1.1
. Moreover, the coefficients of
B
are arbitrary.

Theorem
1.1
holds in fact in a more general form (see Theorem
1.3
),
allowing a log Calabi-Yau structure with general boundary on
X
, and an immediate
application is an effective version of the toric case of McKernan’s conjecture:
if moreover
Y
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial, then
\operatorname{mld}_{P}Y\geq\gamma(d,a)
.

McKernan’s conjecture was solved in the toric case by Alexeev and Borisov
[
1
]
.
They use global minimal log discrepancies, but their method extends to minimal log
discrepancies over proper closed subsets
Z\subseteq Y
, to give an
effective lower bound for
\operatorname{mld}_{Z}Y
in terms of
\operatorname{mld}_{f^{-1}Z}X
, the dimension and the

\alpha
-invariant of the
general fiber of
f
. Our method is different, since we ignore the
general fiber of
f
. In Theorem
1.3
we work exclusively with the
special fiber, also allowing log canonical centers which are not contained in the special fiber.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is combinatorial, with the following geometric interpretation.
Let
d=\dim X
and assume
Y
is affine.
We first construct a toric rational contraction
\phi\colon X\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{1}

induced by an invariant pencil
\Lambda_{1}\subset|-n_{1}K-n_{1}B|
, such that
1\leq n_{1}\leq\frac{d^{2}}{t}

and for a general member
D_{1}\in\Lambda_{1}
, the log Calabi-Yau structure of index
n_{1}

K+B+\frac{1}{n_{1}}D_{1}\sim_{n_{1}}0

has log canonical singularities, and every lc center dominate
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
via
\phi
.
There are two possibilities.

a) Case
\operatorname{mld}_{f^{-1}P}(X,B+\frac{1}{n_{1}}D_{1})>0
. This corresponds to an invariant
hyperplane section
P\in H\subset Y
such that
(X,B+\frac{1}{n_{1}}f^{*}H)
has log canonical singularities.
Here
\gamma\geq\frac{1}{n_{1}}\geq\frac{a}{d^{2}}
.

b) Case
\operatorname{mld}_{f^{-1}P}(X,B+\frac{1}{n_{1}}D_{1})=0
. Let
\mu\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be the normalization
of the graph of
\phi
, let
\mu^{*}(K+B)=K_{X^{\prime}}+B_{X^{\prime}}
be the log pullback. Let
X^{\prime}_{1}
be the
general fiber of
\phi^{\prime}\colon X^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, let
(K_{X^{\prime}}+B_{X^{\prime}})|_{X^{\prime}_{1}}=K_{X^{\prime}_{1}}+B_{X^{\prime}_{1}}

be the adjunction formula. The Stein factorization of the composition
X^{\prime}_{1}\subset X^{\prime}\to X\to Y
induces a
germ of toric contraction
f_{1}\colon X^{\prime}_{1}\to Y_{1}\ni P_{1}
, with
P_{1}
mapped onto
P
and

\operatorname{mld}_{f_{1}^{-1}P_{1}}(X^{\prime}_{1},B_{X^{\prime}_{1}})\geq\operatorname{mld}_{f^{-1}P}(X,B)
.
We have
\dim X^{\prime}_{1}=d-1
and the claim lifts from
X^{\prime}_{1}
to
X
, provided

B_{X^{\prime}_{1}}
is effective. If not, the boundedness of
n_{1}
allows to make

B_{X^{\prime}_{1}}
effective after replacing it with a convex combination with the toric log canonical
complement
\Sigma_{X^{\prime}_{1}}\in|-K_{X^{\prime}_{1}}|
, at the expense of scaling down

\operatorname{mld}_{f_{1}^{-1}P_{1}}(X^{\prime}_{1},B_{X^{\prime}_{1}})
by a bounded factor.

The natural category for the above argument is that of log varieties
(X,B)
, with
X

toric and
B=\sum_{i}b_{i}D_{i}
where
b_{i}\in[0,1]
and
D_{i}
a general member
of an invariant linear system
\Lambda_{i}
on
X
. However, here we are concerned only
with the singularities of a general member of a linear system, not with the member itself.
Therefore it is more convenient to work with
toric generalized log varieties
(X,B+{\mathbf{D}}_{X})
, where
X
is toric,

B
is effective invariant, and
{\mathbf{D}}
is an invariant
{\mathbb{R}}
-free
{\mathbb{R}}
-b-divisor.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y\ni P
be a toric contraction, where
Y
is affine of positive
dimension, containing an invariant point
P
. Let
(X,B+{\mathbf{D}}_{X})
be a toric
g-log variety structure such that
-(K+B+{\mathbf{D}}_{X})
is
f
-nef and

\operatorname{g-mld}_{f^{-1}P}(X,B+{\mathbf{D}}_{X})=a>0
. Then there exists an invariant hyperplane
section
P\in H\subset Y
such that
(X,B+\gamma(d,a)f^{*}H+{\mathbf{D}}_{X})
has g-lc singularities,
where
d=\dim X
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
X=T_{N}\operatorname{emb}(\Delta)
be a toric variety. We use standard notation on toric varieties
(see
[
8
]
). We always choose the anticanonical class to be
-K=\Sigma_{X}
,
the complement of the open dense torus sitting inside
X
.

Support functions

Let
M=N^{*}
be the dual lattice. A subset
A\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}
defines
a support function
h_{A}\colon N_{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{-\infty\}
by
h_{A}(e)=\inf_{a\in A}\langle a,e\rangle
.
If
A
has finite cardinality, the infimum is a minimum.
Let
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
. Then
tA=\{ta|a\in A\}\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies
h_{tA}=t\cdot h_{A}
.
The sum of two sets
A,A^{\prime}\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}
is
A+A^{\prime}:=\{a+a^{\prime}|a\in A,a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}\}\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}
,
and
h_{A+A^{\prime}}=h_{A}+h_{A^{\prime}}
.

Toric b-divisors

A
toric valuation
of
X
is the valuation
induced by an invariant prime divisor on a toric birational contraction
X^{\prime}\to X
.
Toric valuations of
X
are in bijection with the primitive lattice points contained
in the support of the fan defining
X
. Denote by
E_{e}
the toric valuation corresponding to

e\in N^{prim}\cap|\Delta|
. The center of
E_{e}
on
X
is the orbit
O_{\sigma}
, where

\sigma\in\Delta
is minimal with the property
e\in\sigma
.

Let
R\in\{{\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{Q}},{\mathbb{R}}\}
. A
toric
R
-b-divisor
of
X
is a formal sum

{\mathbf{D}}=\sum_{e\in N^{prim}\cap|\Delta|}d_{e}E_{e}\ (d_{e}\in R).

The
trace of
{\mathbf{D}}
on a proper toric birational modification

X^{\prime}\dashrightarrow X

is the invariant
R
-Weil divisor
{\mathbf{D}}_{X}=\sum_{e\in\Delta_{X^{\prime}}(1)}d_{e}V(e)
.
Traces are compatible with pushforwards. Conversely, a toric
R
-b-divisor is the same
as a pushforward compatible collection of invariant
R
-Weil divisors on the proper toric
birational modifications of
X
.

If
{\mathbf{D}}_{i}
is a finite collection of toric
R
-b-divisors of
X
, then

\min_{i}{\mathbf{D}}_{i}:=\sum_{e}(\min_{i}d_{i,e})E_{e}
is a toric
R
-b-divisor of
X
.

An element
m\in M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R
induces the toric
R
-b-divisor
(\chi^{m})=\sum_{e}\langle m,e\rangle E_{e}

whose trace on
X^{\prime}
is the principal
R
-Weil divisor

(\chi^{m})_{X^{\prime}}=\sum_{e\in\Delta_{X^{\prime}}(1)}\langle m,e\rangle V(e)
.

A finite set
A\subset M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R
induces a toric
R
-b-divisor

{\mathbf{D}}_{A}:=-\min_{a\in A}(\chi^{a})=\sum_{e}-h_{A}(e)E_{e}
.

Let
L
be an
R
-Cartier invariant
R
-Weil divisor on
X^{\prime}
. The pullbacks of
L

on higher models induce the
R
-b-divisor
\overline{L}
of
X
, called the

Cartier closure of

L
.

Let
{\mathbf{D}}
be a toric
{\mathbb{R}}
-b-divisor of
X
.
We say that
{\mathbf{D}}

descends to
X^{\prime}
if
{\mathbf{D}}_{X^{\prime}}
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier and
{\mathbf{D}}=\overline{{\mathbf{D}}_{X^{\prime}}}
.
We say that
{\mathbf{D}}
is
R
-free
if
{\mathbf{D}}={\mathbf{D}}_{A}
for some finite set
A\subset M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R
.
Suppose
X\to Z
is a proper toric morphism, with
Z
affine. Then
{\mathbf{D}}
is
R
-free if and only if

{\mathbf{D}}
descends to a toric birational modification
X^{\prime}\to X
and
{\mathbf{D}}_{X^{\prime}}
is
R
-Cartier and
nef over
Z
.

Invariant linear systems

Let
L
be an invariant
{\mathbb{R}}
-Weil divisor on
X
, let
V\subset\Gamma(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(L))

be an invariant
k
-vector space of finite positive dimension. Being invariant,

V=\oplus_{a\in A}k\cdot\chi^{m}
for a unique finite set
A\subset M\cap\square_{L}
.
The invariant linear system induced by
V\subset\Gamma(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(L))
is

\Lambda=\{(f)_{X}+L|f\in V\setminus 0\}.

Since
\Lambda
is invariant, so is the
fixed part

\operatorname{Fix}\Lambda=\min_{D\in\Lambda}D
. One computes

\operatorname{Fix}\Lambda=\min_{a\in A}(\chi^{a})_{X}+L.

The
mobile part

\operatorname{Mov}\Lambda=\Lambda-\operatorname{Fix}\Lambda

coincides with the invariant mobile linear system on
X
induced by
V=\sum_{a\in A}k\cdot\chi^{m}\subset\Gamma(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(L-\operatorname{Fix}\Lambda))
, where
L-\operatorname{Fix}\Lambda=({\mathbf{D}}_{A})_{X}
. Therefore

\operatorname{Mov}\Lambda
depends only on
A
, not on
L
.

The vector space
V
induces its
mobile b-divisor

\operatorname{{\mathbf{M}ov}}V=-\min_{f\in V\setminus 0}(f)
,
whose restriction to toric modifications coincides with the b-free toric b-divisor
{\mathbf{D}}_{A}
.

Let
m\in M
, let
A^{\prime}=A-m
and
L^{\prime}=L+(\chi^{m})_{X}
. Then

V^{\prime}=V\cdot\chi^{-m}
,
V^{\prime}\subset\Gamma(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(L^{\prime}))
induces the same linear system
\Lambda
,
and
\operatorname{{\mathbf{M}ov}}V^{\prime}=\operatorname{{\mathbf{M}ov}}V+(\chi^{m})
. When discussing properties invariant under linear equivalence,
we abuse notation and call
\operatorname{{\mathbf{M}ov}}V
the
mobile b-divisor of

\Lambda
, denoted

\operatorname{{\mathbf{M}ov}}\Lambda
.

A finite set
A\subset M
induces a pushforward compatible family of invariant mobile
linear systems
\Lambda_{X^{\prime}}
for all toric birational contractions
X^{\prime}\to X
.
Indeed, on
X^{\prime}
it is induced by
L_{X^{\prime}}=({\mathbf{D}}_{A})_{X^{\prime}}
and

V=\sum_{a\in A}k\cdot\chi^{a}\subset\Gamma(X^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}_{X^{\prime}}(L_{X^{\prime}}))
.
The linear system
\Lambda_{X^{\prime}}
has no base points if and only if the support function

h_{A}
is linear on each cone of
\Delta_{X^{\prime}}
, and over such
X^{\prime}
it is also compatible
with pullbacks.

Example 2.1
.

Let
D
be an effective invariant
{\mathbb{R}}
-Weil divisor on
X
. Set
A=\{0\}
and
L=D
.
Then
A\subset M\cap\square_{L}
induces the fixed linear system
\Lambda=\{D\}
,
with
\operatorname{Fix}\Lambda=D
and
\operatorname{{\mathbf{M}ov}}V=0
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
\Lambda_{i}
be the invariant linear system on
X
induced by

A_{i}\subset M\cap\square_{L_{i}}
, for
i=1,2
. The sum of linear systems

\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}
is again invariant, induced by
A_{1}+A_{2}\subset M\cap\square_{L_{1}+L_{2}}
.
Then
\operatorname{Fix}(\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2})=\operatorname{Fix}\Lambda_{1}+\operatorname{Fix}\Lambda_{2}
and

\operatorname{{\mathbf{M}ov}}(V_{1}\cdot V_{2})=\operatorname{{\mathbf{M}ov}}V_{1}+\operatorname{{\mathbf{M}ov}}V_{2}
.

Length estimates for extension of non-negative linear maps

Let
\varphi\colon\Lambda\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be a surjective homomorphism, where
\Lambda

is a lattice of dimension at least two. Let
\sigma\subset\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
be a rational polyhedral convex cone.
Let
C\subset\sigma
be a convex set such that
\Lambda\cap C
contains the origin and a set of generators of the cone
\sigma
. In particular,

\sigma=\cup_{t>0}tC.

Suppose
\varphi(C)\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is a compact interval of positive length
w
,
containing
0
in its interior.
Denote
\Lambda_{0}=\Lambda\cap\varphi^{\perp}
and
C_{0}=C\cap\varphi^{\perp}\subset\Lambda_{0,{\mathbb{R}}}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\varphi_{0}\colon\Lambda_{0}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be a lattice homomorphism such that

\varphi_{0}(C_{0})=[0,l_{0}]
for some
l_{0}>0
. Then there exists a lattice
homomorphism
\varphi^{\prime}\colon\Lambda\to{\mathbb{Z}}
such that:

•

\varphi^{\prime}|_{\Lambda_{0}}=q\varphi_{0}
for some integer
1\leq q<w
.

•

\varphi^{\prime}(C)=[0,l^{\prime}]
for some
0<l^{\prime}\leq wl_{0}
.

Proof.

The inclusion of lattices
j\colon\Lambda_{0}\subset\Lambda
is a direct
summand. It dualizes to a lattice projection
j^{*}\colon\Lambda^{*}\to\Lambda_{0}^{*}
,
which identifies
\Lambda_{0}^{*}
with the quotient
\Lambda^{*}/{\mathbb{Z}}\varphi
.
Let
C^{*}\subset\Lambda^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{R}}
be the convex set polar dual to
C\subset\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
C^{*}_{0}\subset\Lambda_{0}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{R}}
be the
j^{*}
-image of

C\subset\Lambda^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{R}}
. Then
C_{0}\subset\Lambda_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{R}}
is polar
dual to
C_{0}^{*}\subset\Lambda^{*}_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{R}}
.

Let
\varphi(C)=[-w_{-},w_{+}]
, with
w_{-},w_{+}>0
and
w_{-}+w_{+}=w
.
Denote
\varphi_{1}=\varphi
. Let
\varphi_{2}\colon\Lambda\to{\mathbb{Z}}
be an arbitrary extension of

\varphi_{0}
. Then
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\Lambda^{*}
are linearly independent in
\Lambda^{*}_{\mathbb{R}}
.

By assumption,
\varphi_{0}
is non-negative
on
\cup_{t>0}tC_{0}=\sigma\cap\varphi^{\perp}
. Equivalently, there exists
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

c\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}\in\sigma^{\vee}.

By assumption, there exist finitely many elements
e_{i}\in\Lambda^{prim}\cap C
such that

\sigma=\sum_{i}{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}e_{i}\subset\Lambda_{\mathbb{R}}
. The condition for
c\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}\in\sigma^{\vee}

becomes

\max_{\varphi_{1}(e_{i})>0}\frac{-\varphi_{2}(e_{i})}{\varphi_{1}(e_{i})}\leq c\leq\min_{\varphi_{1}(e_{i})<0}\frac{\varphi_{2}(e_{i})}{-\varphi_{1}(e_{i})}.

Denote
\gamma^{\prime}=\frac{1}{l_{0}}
.
Since
-\gamma^{\prime}\varphi_{0}\in C^{*}_{0}
, there exists
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

t\varphi_{1}-\gamma^{\prime}\varphi_{2}\in C^{*}
. Denote
c^{\prime}=\gamma^{\prime}c+t
. Then

c^{\prime}\varphi_{1}-\gamma^{\prime}(c\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})\in C^{*}.

Step 1
: We claim that
-\frac{1}{w_{+}}\leq c^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{w_{-}}
. Indeed, from
\sigma^{\vee}\subseteq C^{*}

we deduce

c^{\prime}\varphi_{1}=(c^{\prime}\varphi_{1}-\gamma^{\prime}(c\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}))+\gamma^{\prime}(c\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})\in C^{*}.

The assumption
\varphi(C)=[-w_{-},w_{+}]
is polar dual to

\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}|x\varphi_{1}\in C^{*}\}=[-\frac{1}{w_{+}},\frac{1}{w_{-}}]
. Therefore the claim holds.

Step 2
: We claim that
-\gamma^{\prime}\frac{\min(w_{-},w_{+})}{w_{-}+w_{+}}\cdot(c\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})\in C^{*}
.

Indeed, suppose
c^{\prime}\leq 0
. Then

-\epsilon\gamma^{\prime}(c\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})=\epsilon(c^{\prime}\varphi_{1}-\gamma^{\prime}(c\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}))+(1-\epsilon)\frac{\varphi_{1}}{w_{-}}\in C^{*},

where
\epsilon=\frac{1}{1-c^{\prime}w_{-}}\in(0,1]
. Since
c^{\prime}\geq-\frac{1}{w_{+}}
, we deduce
\epsilon\geq\frac{w_{+}}{w_{-}+w_{+}}
.

Suppose
c^{\prime}\geq 0
. Then

-\epsilon\gamma^{\prime}(c\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})=\epsilon(c^{\prime}\varphi_{1}-\gamma^{\prime}(c\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}))+(1-\epsilon)\frac{-\varphi_{1}}{w_{-}}\in C^{*},

where
\epsilon=\frac{1}{1+c^{\prime}w_{+}}\in(0,1]
.
Since
c^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{w_{-}}
, we deduce
\epsilon\geq\frac{w_{-}}{w_{-}+w_{+}}
.

Step 3
:
Suppose
w_{-}\leq w_{+}
. Choose
c=\min_{\varphi_{1}(e_{i})<0}\frac{\varphi_{2}(e_{i})}{-\varphi_{1}(e_{i})}
.
From Step 2 we have

-\gamma^{\prime}\frac{w_{-}}{w_{-}+w_{+}}\cdot(c\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})\in C^{*}.

There exists
e_{i}
such that
\varphi_{1}(e_{i})<0
and
c=\frac{\varphi_{2}(e_{i})}{-\varphi_{1}(e_{i})}
.
Here
\varphi_{1}(e_{i})=-q
for some positive integer
q
. Set

\varphi^{\prime}=\varphi_{2}(e_{i})\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{1}(e_{i})\varphi_{2}\in\Lambda\setminus 0
.
Then
\varphi^{\prime}=q(c\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})\in\sigma^{\vee}
,
j^{*}(\varphi^{\prime})=q\varphi_{0}
and

-\gamma^{\prime}\frac{w_{-}}{w_{-}+w_{+}}\frac{1}{q}\varphi^{\prime}\in C^{*}.

Since
e_{i}\in C
and
\varphi_{1}(C)\subset[-w_{-},w_{+}]
,
we obtain
q\leq w_{-}
. Therefore
q<w
and

\frac{w_{-}}{w_{-}+w_{+}}\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{1}{w}.

We obtain
-\frac{\gamma^{\prime}}{w}\varphi^{\prime}\in C^{*}
. Then
\varphi^{\prime}(C)
is contained
in
[0,\frac{w}{\gamma^{\prime}}]=[0,wl_{0}]
.

If

we choose

to be the other extremal point, and argue similarly.
∎

3.
General(-ized) log pair structures on toric varieties

Let
X=T_{N}\operatorname{emb}(\Delta)
be a toric variety. We consider (generalized) log pair structures
(X,B)

which can be resolved by toric modifications, and therefore log discrepancies
and minimal log discrepancies can be computed combinatorially. Only the
invariant part of
B
may have negative coefficients.
If
B
is effective, we say
(X,B)
is a (generalized) log variety.

Invariant boundary

Let
B
be an invariant
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor
such that
K+B
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier. The latter is equivalent to: for every

\sigma\in\Delta(top)
, there exists
\psi_{\sigma}\in M_{\mathbb{R}}
such that

(\chi^{\psi_{\sigma}})+K+B=0
on
U_{\sigma}
, that is

\langle\psi_{\sigma},e_{i}\rangle=1-\operatorname{mult}_{V(e_{i})}B
for every
e_{i}\in\sigma(1)
.

Let
\mu\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be a toric birational contraction such that
X^{\prime}
is smooth.
Then
(X^{\prime},\Sigma_{X^{\prime}})
is log smooth. Let
\mu^{*}(K+B)=K_{X^{\prime}}+B_{X^{\prime}}
be the
log pullback. Then
B_{X^{\prime}}
is an invariant
{\mathbb{R}}
-divisor, with coefficients

\operatorname{mult}_{V(e)}B_{X^{\prime}}=1-\langle\psi_{\sigma},e\rangle\ (e\in\Delta_{X^{\prime}}(1),e\in\sigma).

In other words, the log discrepancy of
(X,B)
in a toric valuation
E_{e}\ (e\in N^{prim}\cap|\Delta|)

of
X
, is computed as follows:

a_{E_{e}}(X,B)=\langle\psi_{\sigma},e\rangle\ (e\in\sigma\in\Delta(top)).

In particular,
(X,B)
has log canonical singularities if and only if
\psi_{\sigma}\in\sigma^{\vee}
for every

\sigma\in\Delta(top)
.

General boundary (Alexeev)

Let
X=T_{N}\operatorname{emb}(\Delta)
be a toric variety, let
B=B^{inv}+\sum_{j}b_{j}S_{j}
,
where
B^{inv}
is an invariant
{\mathbb{R}}
-Weil divisor on
X
,
b_{j}\geq 0
and

S_{j}
is a general member of an invariant mobile linear system
\Lambda_{j}
on
X
.
Let
\Lambda_{j}
be defined by a finite set
A_{j}\subset M
.
The condition that
K+B
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier is equivalent to:
for every
\sigma\in\Delta(top)
, there exists
\psi_{\sigma}\in M_{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\langle\psi_{\sigma},e\rangle-h_{\sum_{j}b_{j}A_{j}}(e)=1-\operatorname{mult}_{V(e)}B^{inv}\ \forall e\in\sigma(1).

Let
\mu\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be a toric birational contraction such that
X^{\prime}
is smooth,
and
\operatorname{{\mathbf{M}ov}}\Lambda_{j}
descends to
X^{\prime}
for all
j
.
Let
S^{\prime}_{j}
be a general member of the base point free linear system induced by
\Lambda_{j}

on
X^{\prime}
. Then
S^{\prime}_{j}
is smooth and
(X^{\prime},\Sigma_{X^{\prime}}+\sum_{j}S^{\prime}_{j})
is log smooth.
We may take
S_{j}=\mu_{*}(S^{\prime}_{j})
. Then

\mu^{*}(K+B)=K_{X^{\prime}}+\sum_{i}(1-a_{i})E_{i}+\sum_{j}b_{j}S^{\prime}_{j},

where
E_{i}=V(e_{i})
are the invariant prime divisors of
X^{\prime}
and

a_{i}=\langle\psi_{\sigma},e_{i}\rangle-h_{\sum_{j}b_{j}A_{j}}(e_{i})\ (e_{i}\in\sigma).

In other words, the log discrepancy of
(X,B)
in a toric valuation
E_{e}\ (e\in N^{prim}\cap|\Delta|)

of
X
is computed as follows:

a_{E_{e}}(X,B)=\langle\psi_{\sigma},e\rangle-h_{\sum_{j}b_{j}A_{j}}(e)\ (e\in\sigma\in\Delta(top)).

In particular,
(X,B)
has log canonical singularities if and only if
b_{j}\leq 1
for all
j
, and

\psi_{\sigma}\in\operatorname{Conv}(\sum_{j}b_{j}A_{j})+\sigma^{\vee}
for every
\sigma\in\Delta(top)
.

The definition covers the case when the invariant linear systems have fixed parts.
Let
B=B^{inv}+\sum_{j}b_{j}D_{j}
where
b_{j}\geq 0
and
D_{j}
is a general member of
an invariant linear system of
{\mathbb{R}}
-Weil divisors on
X
.
Let
\Lambda_{j}=F_{j}+\Lambda^{m}_{j}
be the fixed-mobile decomposition.
Then
D_{j}=F_{j}+S_{j}
, where
S_{j}
is a general member of the invariant mobile
linear system
\Lambda^{m}_{j}
(zero if
\Lambda_{j}
is fixed). Then

K+B^{inv}+\sum_{j}b_{j}D_{j}=K+(B^{inv}+\sum_{j}b_{j}F_{j})+\sum_{j}b_{j}S_{j}.

For each
j
, choose a positive integer
n_{j}
. Let

\tilde{\Lambda}_{j}=\Lambda_{j}+\cdots+\Lambda_{j}

(n_{j}
-times), let
\tilde{D}_{j}

be the general member of
\tilde{\Lambda}_{j}
. Then

K+B^{inv}+\sum_{j}b_{j}D_{j}
and
K+B^{inv}+\sum_{j}\frac{b_{j}}{n_{j}}\tilde{D}_{j}

have the same log discrepancies in toric valuations.

Generalized boundary (Birkar-Zhang)

Let
B=B^{inv}+\sum_{j}b_{j}S_{j}
where

B^{inv}
is an invariant
{\mathbb{R}}
-Weil divisor on
X
,
b_{j}\geq 0
and
S_{j}
is a general
member of an invariant mobile linear system
\Lambda_{j}
on
X
.
Let
{\mathbf{D}}
be an
{\mathbb{R}}
-free invariant
{\mathbb{R}}
-b-divisor of
X
, induced by a finite set

A\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}
. Thus

{\mathbf{D}}=\sum_{e\in N^{prim}\cap|\Delta|}-h_{A}(e)E_{e}.

Let
\Lambda_{j}
be induced by the finite set
A_{j}\subset M
.
We assume that
K+B+{\mathbf{D}}_{X}
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-Cartier. Equivalently, for every

\sigma\in\Delta(top)
there exists
\psi_{\sigma}\in M_{\mathbb{R}}
such that

(\chi^{\psi_{\sigma}})+K+B+{\mathbf{D}}_{X}=0
on
U_{\sigma}
, i.e.

\langle\psi_{\sigma},e_{i}\rangle-h_{A+\sum_{j}b_{j}A_{j}}(e_{i})=1-\operatorname{mult}_{V(e_{i})}B^{inv}\ \forall e_{i}\in\sigma(1).

Let
\mu\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be a toric birational contraction such that
X^{\prime}
is smooth,

{\mathbf{D}}
and all
\operatorname{{\mathbf{M}ov}}\Lambda_{j}
descend to
X^{\prime}
. Then

\mu^{*}(K+B+{\mathbf{D}}_{X})=K_{X^{\prime}}+\sum_{i}(1-a_{i})E_{i}+\sum_{j}b_{j}S^{\prime}_{j}+{\mathbf{D}}_{X^{\prime}},

where

a_{i}=\langle\psi_{\sigma},e_{i}\rangle-h_{A+\sum_{j}b_{j}A_{j}}(e_{i})\ (e_{i}\in\sigma).

In particular,
(X,B+{\mathbf{D}}_{X})
has g-log canonical singularities if and only if

\psi_{\sigma}\in\operatorname{Conv}(A+\sum_{j}b_{j}A_{j})+\sigma^{\vee}
for every
\sigma
.

For
m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}
, denote
{\mathbf{D}}^{\prime}={\mathbf{D}}+(\chi^{m})
. Then

(X,B+{\mathbf{D}}_{X})
and
(X,B+{\mathbf{D}}^{\prime}_{X})
have the same g-log discrepancies in toric valuations.
Indeed, g-log discrepancies depend only on
{\mathbf{D}}-\overline{{\mathbf{D}}_{X}}
, which does not change
after replacing
{\mathbf{D}}
by
{\mathbf{D}}^{\prime}
.

Let
K+B^{inv}+\sum_{j}b_{j}D_{j}
be a log pair structure with general boundary
induced by invariant linear systems
\Lambda_{j}
on
X
.
Let
{\mathbf{D}}=\sum_{j}b_{j}\operatorname{{\mathbf{M}ov}}\Lambda_{j}
.
Then
K+B^{inv}+\sum_{j}b_{j}\operatorname{Fix}\Lambda_{j}+{\mathbf{D}}_{X}
is a generalized log pair
structure, having the same log discrepancies in toric valuations as

K+B^{inv}+\sum_{j}b_{j}D_{j}
. In particular, for an invariant closed subset
Z\subseteq\Sigma_{X}

we have

\operatorname{mld}_{Z}(X,B^{inv}+\sum_{j}b_{j}D_{j})=\operatorname{g-mld}_{Z}(X,B^{inv}+\sum_{j}b_{j}\operatorname{Fix}\Lambda_{j}+{\mathbf{D}}_{X}).

Due to this property, when focusing only on the singularities of general members
of linear systems, it is convenient to replace general boundaries by generalized ones.
The same holds for a combination of general and generalized pair:

(X,B^{inv}+\sum_{j}b_{j}\Lambda_{j}^{gen}+{\mathbf{D}}_{X})\leadsto(X,B^{inv}+\sum_{j}b_{j}\operatorname{Fix}\Lambda_{j}+(\sum_{j}b_{j}\operatorname{{\mathbf{M}ov}}\Lambda_{j}+{\mathbf{D}})_{X}).

4.
Anti-minimal models

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a proper toric contraction, with
Y
affine and containing an invariant point
P
.
The contraction
f\colon X=T_{N}\operatorname{emb}(\Delta)\to Y=T_{\bar{N}}\operatorname{emb}(\bar{\sigma})
corresponds to
a lattice projection
\pi\colon N\to\bar{N}
such that
|\Delta|=\pi^{-1}(\bar{\sigma})
.
In particular,
|\Delta|
is a rational polyhedral convex cone.
Note that
Y
contains an invariant point

only if
\dim\bar{\sigma}=\dim\bar{N}
,
which is equivalent to
\dim|\Delta|=\dim N
. Moreover,
N
and
\Delta
determine

f
, since
\bar{N}
is the quotient of

modulo
|\Delta|\cap(-|\Delta|)
, and

\bar{\sigma}
is the image of
|\Delta|
.

Let
(X,B+{\mathbf{D}}_{X})
be a g-log pair structure such that
-(K+B+{\mathbf{D}}_{X})

is
f
-nef. Since
f
is toric and
Y
is affine, this is equivalent to
-(K+B+{\mathbf{D}}_{X})

{\mathbb{R}}
-free. In other words, for every
\sigma\in\Delta(top)

\psi_{\sigma}\in M_{\mathbb{R}}

is effective on
X
,
and zero on
U_{\sigma}
. In particular,

\square_{-K-B-{\mathbf{D}}_{X}}=\cap_{\sigma\in\Delta(top)}-\psi_{\sigma}+\sigma^{\vee}
.

Let
X=T_{N}\operatorname{emb}(\Delta)
, let
{\mathbf{D}}
be defined by a finite set
A\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}
.
The polyhedral convex set

.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

. The g-log discrepancy of
(X,B+{\mathbf{D}}_{X})

in the toric valuation
E_{e}
equals
-h_{\square}(e)
.

Proof.

Let
\mu\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be a toric birational contraction such that
{\mathbf{D}}
descends to

X^{\prime}
. Let

\sigma^{\prime}\subset\sigma\in\Delta(top)

(\chi^{\psi_{\sigma}})+K+B+{\mathbf{D}}_{X}=0
on
U_{\sigma}
.
Then
(\chi^{\psi_{\sigma}})_{X^{\prime}}+K_{X^{\prime}}+B_{X^{\prime}}+{\mathbf{D}}_{X^{\prime}}=0
on
U_{\sigma^{\prime}}
.
There exists
a\in A
such that
(\chi^{a})+{\mathbf{D}}_{X^{\prime}}=0

U_{\sigma^{\prime}}
.
That is,

Since

descends to
X^{\prime}

E_{e}

We have
(\chi^{\psi_{\sigma}-a})_{X^{\prime}}+K_{X^{\prime}}+B_{X^{\prime}}=0

We have
\operatorname{Conv}(A)\subset a+\sigma^{\prime\vee}

{\mathbb{R}}

\square_{-(K+B+{\mathbf{D}}_{X})}\subset-\psi_{\sigma}+\sigma^{\vee}

Therefore

∎

Proposition 4.2
.

(X,B+{\mathbf{D}}_{X})
has g-lc singularities if and only if
\square
contains the origin.

Since
{\mathbf{D}}
descends to a toric modification of

for all

,
which is equivalent to the same property for all

. The latter
is equivalent to
0\in\square
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

\square=\{m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}|(X,B+(\chi^{-m})_{X}+{\mathbf{D}}_{X})\text{ has g-lc singularities}\}
.

We have
\square_{-(K+B+(\chi^{-m})_{X}+{\mathbf{D}}_{X})}=\square_{-(K+B+{\mathbf{D}}_{X})}-m
.
Since
{\mathbf{D}}
is the same, we conclude from Proposition

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mu\colon X^{\prime}\to X

5.
A construction

Let
f\colon(X,B+{\mathbf{D}}_{X})\to Y\ni P
be an anti-minimal model as in Section 4.
Suppose moreover that
\dim Y>0
and
\operatorname{g-mld}_{f^{-1}(P)}(X,B+{\mathbf{D}}_{X})\geq t>0
. In particular,

(X,B+{\mathbf{D}}_{X})
has g-lc singularities.

Let
\varphi\colon N\to{\mathbb{Z}}
a surjective linear homomorphism such that
\varphi(U)\subset{\mathbb{R}}

is compact. Then
\varphi(U)
is an interval of finite length
w>0
. Since
U
contains the origin,
so does
\varphi(U)
. Modulo replacing
\varphi
by
-\varphi
, we have exactly two
cases: 1)
\varphi(U)=[0,w]
for some
w>0
, or 2)
\varphi(U)=[-a,b]
, with
a,b>0
and

a+b=w
.

Case 1):
The condition
\varphi(U)=[0,w]
is dual equivalent to

\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}\ |x\varphi\in\square\}=[-\frac{1}{w},+\infty).

We have
\varphi\in|\Delta|^{\vee}
.
Therefore there exists
\bar{\varphi}\in\bar{M}^{prim}\cap{\bar{\sigma}}^{\vee}
such that

\pi^{*}(\bar{\varphi})=\varphi
. Then

is an invariant hyperplane section
of

such that

. Therefore

has g-lc singularities (and
\frac{1}{w}
is maximal with this property).

Case 2):
The condition
\varphi_{1}(U)=[-a,b]
is dual equivalent to

\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}\ |x\varphi\in\square\}=[-\frac{1}{b},\frac{1}{a}].

In particular,
\pm\frac{1}{w}\varphi\in\square

-h_{\square}(e)\geq\frac{|\langle\varphi,e\rangle|}{w}

for all

.
It follows that every

with

must satisfy
\varphi(e)=0
.

Let
M_{0}=M/{\mathbb{Z}}\varphi
be the quotient lattice, dual to

.
Let
\square_{0}
be the image of
\square
under the projection

. The polar dual convex set

equals

.
The assumption
\operatorname{g-mld}_{f^{-1}(P)}(X,B+{\mathbf{D}}_{X})\geq t
is equivalent to

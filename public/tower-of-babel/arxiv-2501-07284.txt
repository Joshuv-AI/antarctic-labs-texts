Free energy of spherical Coulomb gases with point charges
Sung-Soo Byun, Nam-Gyu Kang, Seong-Mi Seo, Meng Yang

Abstract
. We consider two-dimensional Coulomb gases on the Riemann sphere with determinantal or Pfaffian structures, under external potentials that are invariant under rotations around the axis connecting the north and south poles, and with microscopic point charges inserted at the poles. These models can be interpreted as Coulomb gases on the complex plane with weakly confining potentials, where the associated droplet is the entire complex plane. For these models, we derive precise asymptotic expansions of the free energies, including the constant terms.



1.
Introduction and main results

In the theory of two-dimensional Coulomb gases
[
28
]
, the asymptotic expansion of the free energy, as the system size
N
increases, is a central and longstanding problem. In this expansion, the coefficients are believed to provide essential information about the model, including potential theoretical quantities, topological characteristics, and conformal geometric properties
[
17
,
54
,
33
,
50
]
. In addition, the expansion of the free energy plays a crucial role in establishing fluctuations and the Gaussian free field convergence of Coulomb gases, see e.g.
[
49
]
for more details.

There has been remarkable progress in obtaining such expansions for general Coulomb gases, with the first three terms up to the
O(N)
term, including the energy and entropy terms, being derived in
[
39
]
, see also
[
9
,
48
]
. On the one hand, early conjectures
[
17
,
54
,
33
,
50
]
suggest that the expansion beyond the entropy term begins to depend on the topological properties of the Coulomb gas. For general inverse temperature
\beta>0
, obtaining a complete expansion still seems a highly challenging problem. On the other hand, for a certain class of ensembles with
\beta=2
, where one can utilise their notable integrable structures, combining cumulative knowledge from orthogonal polynomial and random matrix theory, has recently led to precise expansions up to the conjectural
O(1)
-terms
[
14
,
5
,
16
,
13
]
.

Recent work has primarily focused on Coulomb gases on the plane. However, as discussed in
[
33
]
with a prototypical example, models on the Riemann sphere are expected to exhibit different behaviour in their asymptotic expansions. In this work, we aim to systematically investigate and contribute to the free energy expansion of such spherical Coulomb gases. Furthermore, by inserting microscopic point charges, we investigate their effects on the expansions, which go beyond the conjectural forms in the previous literature.

1.1.
Spherical Coulomb gases and weakly confining potential

We first introduce the models, the spherical Coulomb gases.
Let
\mathbb{S}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\|x\|=1\}
be the Riemann sphere equipped with the Euclidean metric
\|\cdot\|
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
We investigate two different types of Coulomb gases
\bm{x}=\{x_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
on
\mathbb{S}
, known as the determinantal and Pfaffian Coulomb gases due to their respective integrable structures. Their joint probability distribution functions are of the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle d\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(Q_{\mathbb{S}})}\prod_{j>k=1}^{N}\|x_{j}-x_{k}\|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{N}e^{-NQ_{\mathbb{S}}(x_{j})}\,dA_{\mathbb{S}}(x_{j}),

(1.2)

\displaystyle d\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(Q_{\mathbb{S}})}\prod_{j>k=1}^{N}\|x_{j}-x_{k}\|^{2}\|x_{j}-\overline{x}_{k}\|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\|x_{j}-\overline{x}_{j}\|^{2}e^{-2NQ_{\mathbb{S}}(x_{j})}\,dA_{\mathbb{S}}(x_{j}),

where
dA_{\mathbb{S}}
is the area measure on the sphere, and
\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(Q_{\mathbb{S}})
and
\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(Q_{\mathbb{S}})
are partition functions that make (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) probability measures.
Here,
\overline{u}=(u_{1},u_{2},-u_{3})
for
u=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
, and
Q_{\mathbb{S}}:\mathbb{S}\to\mathbb{R}
is the external potential, which may depend on
N
.
The prototypical example of the potential is of the form

(1.3)

Q_{\mathbb{S}}^{\rm sp}(x):=-\frac{2\alpha}{N}\log\|x-{\rm{N}}\|-\frac{2c}{N}\log\|x-{\rm{S}}\|,\qquad\begin{cases}{\rm{N}}=(0,0,1),\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
{\rm{S}}=(0,0,-1),\end{cases}

where
\alpha,c\geq 0
are point charges.
Notice that the potentials can be defined up to additive constants. Namely, if
Q_{\mathbb{S}}
is replaced by
Q_{\mathbb{S}}+\mathfrak{c}
for some constant
\mathfrak{c}
, the models (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) remain unchanged.

It is convenient to consider equivalent models in the complex plane. The determinantal and Pfaffian Coulomb gas ensembles
\bm{z}=\{z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
in the complex plane
z_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
are given by

(1.4)

\displaystyle d\mathbf{P}_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(\bm{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(Q)}\prod_{j>k=1}^{N}|z_{j}-z_{k}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{N}e^{-NQ(z_{j})}\,dA(z_{j}),

(1.5)

\displaystyle d\mathbf{P}_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(Q)}\prod_{j>k=1}^{N}|z_{j}-z_{k}|^{2}|z_{j}-\bar{z}_{k}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{N}|z_{j}-\bar{z}_{j}|^{2}e^{-2NQ(z_{j})}\,dA(z_{j}),

where
dA(z)=d^{2}z/\pi
is the area measure.
Here, the potential
Q_{\mathbb{S}}
is related to the potential
Q
as

(1.6)

Q_{\mathbb{S}}=Q\circ\phi-\frac{N+1}{N}\log(1+|\phi|^{2}),

up to an additive constant, where
\phi:\mathbb{S}\to\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
is the stereographic projection

(1.7)

\phi(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})=\begin{cases}\dfrac{u_{1}+iu_{2}}{1-u_{3}},&\textup{for }(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\in\mathbb{S}^{2},u_{3}\not=1,\\
\infty,&\textup{for }(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})={\rm{N}}.\end{cases}

The prototypical example (
1.3
) then reads as

(1.8)

Q^{\rm sp}(z):=\frac{N+\alpha+c+1}{N}\log(1+|z|^{2})-\frac{2c}{N}\log|z|.

In this context, it is crucial that the potential is
N
-dependent. We also write

(1.9)

Q^{\rm sp}_{\infty}(z):=\log(1+|z|^{2})

for the large-
N
limit of (
1.8
).
The ensembles (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) with the potential
Q^{\rm sp}
correspond to the eigenvalues of complex
[
38
,
26
,
30
]
and symplectic
[
31
,
44
,
45
,
11
]
spherical induced ensembles, respectively.
See
[
12
, Sections 2.5 and 11.3]
for comprehensive reviews.

The partition functions
\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(Q_{\mathbb{S}})
and
\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(Q_{\mathbb{S}})
in (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) are related to
Z_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(Q)
and
Z_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(Q)
via the following property.

Lemma 1.1
.

We have

(1.10)

\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(Q_{\mathbb{S}})=2^{N(N-1)}Z_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(Q),\qquad\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(Q_{\mathbb{S}})=2^{2N^{2}}Z_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(Q).

This lemma easily follows by the change of variables under the stereographic projections, see Subsection
2.1
.

In general, there are two equivalent but slightly different perspectives on defining the Coulomb gases.
The first approach is to consider a given potential
Q
and define the model according to (
1.4
) and (
1.5
). Together with the classical equilibrium convergence
[
49
]
, the geometric properties of the models then follow from logarithmic potential theory
[
47
]
. Specifically, the system tends to be distributed according to Frostman’s equilibrium measure associated with the potential
Q
. The limiting support of the system consequently depends on the potential
Q
. This approach is straightforward for defining the model; however, it is generally difficult to determine the geometry of the droplet a priori.
The second approach is somewhat the opposite. We begin with a certain probability measure supported on the complex plane, which coincides with the equilibrium measure. Using this measure, we then define the external potential via a logarithmic potential. While this approach is somewhat less explicit in defining the external potential, it has the advantage of allowing us to determine the geometry of the droplet a priori, as it is given by the support of the initial probability measure.

For our purpose of investigating Coulomb gases supported on the whole Riemann sphere, we take this second approach. This is closely related to the notion of weakly confining potential or the planar jellium model.
We first collect our assumptions on the background measure.

Definition 1.2

(
Background measure
)
.

Let
\mu
be an
N
-independent probability measure supported on the whole complex plane. We assume the following.

•

\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to the area measure:

(1.11)

d\mu(z)={\bm{\rho}}(z)\,dA(z),

where
\bm{\rho}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is smooth.

•

\bm{\rho}
is radially symmetric with
\bm{\rho}(z)=\rho(|z|)
, where
\rho:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

•

The pull-back of the density on the Riemann sphere

(1.12)

\bm{\rho}_{\mathbb{S}}(x)=(1+|\phi(x)|^{2})^{2}\bm{\rho}(\phi(x))

does not vanish at the north pole
\rm N
.
Equivalently, there exists a positive constant
A>0
such that

(1.13)

\bm{\rho}(z)\sim\frac{A}{|z|^{4}},\qquad z\to\infty.

We write

(1.14)

\tilde{\rho}(s):=s^{-4}\rho(s^{-1}),\qquad s>0.

Then
\tilde{\rho}
has the unique continuous extension to
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
\tilde{\rho}(0)=A.
We further assume that
\tilde{\rho}
is smooth at
0
.

For a probability measure
\mu
supported on the complex plane, we write

(1.15)

U_{\mu}(z)=\int\log\frac{1}{|z-w|}\,d\mu(w)

for the associated logarithmic potential.
For a given probability measure
\mu
and positive real numbers
\alpha,c\geq 0
, we define the external potential

(1.16)

Q(z)\equiv Q_{N,\alpha,c}(z):=-2\frac{n}{N}U_{\mu}(z)-\frac{2c}{N}\log|z|,\qquad n:=N+\alpha+c+1.

Let us also write

(1.17)

Q_{\infty}(z):=-2U_{\mu}(z).

Note that since

(1.18)

U_{\mu}(z)=-\log|z|+o(1),\qquad z\to\infty,

we have

(1.19)

Q_{N,\alpha,c}(z)\sim 2\frac{N+\alpha+1}{N}\log|z|,\qquad z\to\infty.

This growth property is indeed a characteristic feature of the spherical ensemble. For a potential with a sufficiently strong growth condition
Q(z)\gg 2\log|z|
as
z\to\infty
, it is well known that the associated droplet is a compact subset of the complex plane. In contrast, for a potential with the growth condition (
1.19
), the confining energy is weak enough that the system (
1.4
) lie in the whole complex plane as
N\to\infty
. A potential with the growth condition (
1.19
) is referred to as a weakly confining potential, see e.g.
[
10
]
and references therein.
Let us also mention that for
c=0
, our definition of the models correspond to the definition of the planar jellium
[
18
]
.
Thus, our model can be regarded as the jellium model in the plane with total background charge
2(N+\alpha+c+1)
and background distribution
\mu
with an insertion of a point charge
c
at the origin.

Example 1
.

The simplest and fundamental example is the case where
\bm{\rho}_{\mathbb{S}}
is uniform on the Riemann sphere. In the complex plane, it corresponds to the spherical density

(1.20)

\bm{\rho}^{\rm sp}_{\infty}(z):=\frac{1}{(1+|z|^{2})^{2}}.

In this case, we have

U_{\mu}(z)\Big|_{\bm{\rho}=\bm{\rho}^{\rm sp}_{\infty}}=-\frac{1}{2}\log(1+|z|^{2})=-\frac{1}{2}\,Q^{\rm sp}_{\infty}(z),

where
Q^{\rm sp}_{\infty}
is given by (
1.9
).
Then associated external potential is given by
Q^{\rm sp}
in (
1.8
).

1.2.
Main results

For a given probability measure
\mu
in Definition
1.2
, we write

(1.21)

I[\mu]:=\int\log|z-w|\,d\mu(z)\,d\mu(w)=-\int U_{\mu}\,d\mu

for the (unweighted) logarithmic energy. By using (
1.17
), it can also be interpreted as a weighted logarithmic energy associated with the potential
Q_{\infty}
, namely,

(1.22)

I[\mu]\equiv I_{Q_{\infty}}[\mu]=-\int\log|z-w|\,d\mu(z)\,d\mu(w)+\int Q_{\infty}\,d\mu.

We also write

(1.23)

E[\mu]:=\int\log\bm{\rho}\,d\mu

for the entropy.

In the presence of local point charges, the expansions of the free energies are expressed in terms of the Barnes
G
-function, which is defined recursively by

(1.24)

G(z+1)=\Gamma(z)G(z),\qquad G(1)=1,

see
[
46
, Section 5.17]
.
We then have the following.

Theorem 1.3

(
Free energy expansion of the determinantal Coulomb gas on the sphere
)
.

Suppose that a probability measure
\mu
satisfies the assumptions in Definition
1.2
. Let
Q\equiv Q_{N,\alpha,c}
be given in (
1.16
).
Then as
N\to\infty
, the partition function
Z_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(Q)
in (
1.4
) satisfies the asymptotic expansion

(1.25)

\log Z_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(Q)=C_{1}N^{2}+C_{2}N\log N+C_{3}N+C_{4}\log N+C_{5}+O(N^{-\frac{1}{12}}(\log N)^{d}),

for some constant
d>0
, where

(1.26)

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle=-I[\mu],

(1.27)

\displaystyle C_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2},

(1.28)

\displaystyle C_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{\log(2\pi)}{2}-1-2(\alpha+c+1)I[\mu]-\frac{1}{2}E[\mu]-2c\,U_{\mu}(0),

(1.29)

\displaystyle C_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{2}+c^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{3},

(1.30)

\displaystyle\begin{split}C_{5}&=-(\alpha+c+1)^{2}I[\mu]-(\alpha+c+1)\Big(\frac{1}{2}E[\mu]+2c\,U_{\mu}(0)\Big)\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\Big(c^{2}+c+\frac{1}{3}\Big)\log\rho(0)+\frac{1}{2}\Big(\alpha^{2}+\alpha+\frac{1}{3}\Big)\log\tilde{\rho}(0)\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}(\alpha+c+1)(\log(2\pi)-1)+2\zeta^{\prime}(-1)-\log\Big(G(c+1)G(\alpha+1)\Big)\\
&\quad-\frac{5}{12}-\frac{1}{6}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}-\frac{5}{4}\Big(\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}\Big)^{2}\Big)t\,dt.\end{split}

Here
\tilde{\rho}
is given by (
1.14
),
\zeta
is the Riemann zeta function, and
G
is the Barnes
G
-function.

In contrast to the usual asymptotic formulas found in the literature (see also (
1.45
) below), the formula for
C_{3}
contains the energy term, while
C_{5}
contains both the energy and entropy terms. This arises from the
N
-dependence of the potential (
1.16
). Consequently, it is also natural to express the expansion in terms of the total background charge
n=N+\alpha+c+1
as

(1.31)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\log Z_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(Q)&\sim-I[\mu]n^{2}+\frac{1}{2}N\log N-\Big(\frac{1}{2}E[\mu]+2c\,U_{\mu}(0)\Big)n+\Big(\frac{\log(2\pi)}{2}-1\Big)N+C_{4}\log N\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\Big(c^{2}+c+\frac{1}{3}\Big)\log\rho(0)+\frac{1}{2}\Big(\alpha^{2}+\alpha+\frac{1}{3}\Big)\log\tilde{\rho}(0)+\frac{1}{2}(\alpha+c+1)\log(2\pi)\\
&\quad+2\zeta^{\prime}(-1)-\log(G(c+1)G(\alpha+1))-\frac{5}{12}-\frac{1}{6}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}-\frac{5}{4}\Big(\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}\Big)^{2}\Big)t\,dt.\end{split}

Remark 1
.

It is intuitively clear that the term
2c\,U_{\mu}(0)
in
C_{3}
arises from the microscopic insertion of a point charge at the origin. Notice however that the symmetric counterpart of
2c\,U_{\mu}(0)
does not appear in
C_{3}
. This omission arises from our specific normalisation when defining the potential. To be more precise, we first write

(1.32)

Q_{\mathbb{S,\infty}}:=Q_{\infty}\circ\phi-\log(1+|\phi|^{2})

for the large-
N
counterpart of the potential on the sphere geometry.
Note that by (
1.18
) and (
1.17
),

Q_{\infty}(z)=2\log|z|+o(1),\qquad Q_{\mathbb{S,\infty}}({\rm{N}})=0.

Therefore the seemingly asymmetric term
-2c\,U_{\mu}(0)
can actually be written in a more symmetric manner

(1.33)

-2c\,U_{\mu}(0)=c\,Q_{\mathbb{S,\infty}}({\rm{S}})=c\,Q_{\mathbb{S,\infty}}({\rm{S}})+\alpha\,Q_{\mathbb{S,\infty}}({\rm{N}}).

As a counterpart for the Pfaffian Coulomb gases, we have the following.

Theorem 1.4

(
Free energy expansion of the Pfaffian Coulomb gas on the sphere
)
.

Under the same assumptions in Theorem
1.3
, as
N\to\infty
, the partition function
Z_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(Q)
in (
1.5
) satisfies

(1.34)

\log Z_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(Q)=D_{1}N^{2}+D_{2}N\log N+D_{3}N+D_{4}\log N+D_{5}+O(N^{-\frac{1}{12}}(\log N)^{d}),

for some constant
d>0
, where

(1.35)

\displaystyle D_{1}

\displaystyle=-2I[\mu],

(1.36)

\displaystyle D_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2},

(1.37)

\displaystyle D_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{\log(4\pi)}{2}-1-4(\alpha+c+1)I[\mu]-\frac{1}{2}E[\mu]-(4c+1)U_{\mu}(0),

(1.38)

\displaystyle D_{4}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}+c^{2}+\frac{c}{2}+\frac{5}{12},

(1.39)

\displaystyle\begin{split}D_{5}&=-2(\alpha+c+1)^{2}I[\mu]-(\alpha+c+1)\Big(\frac{1}{2}E[\mu]+(4c+1)\,U_{\mu}(0)\Big)\\
&\quad+\Big(c^{2}+c+\frac{5}{24}\Big)\log\rho(0)+\Big(\alpha^{2}+\alpha+\frac{5}{24}\Big)\log\tilde{\rho}(0)\\
&\quad+(\alpha+c+1)\Big(\log(2\pi)-\frac{1}{2}\Big)+4\zeta^{\prime}(-1)-\log\Big(G(c+1)G(c+\tfrac{3}{2})G(\alpha+1)G(\alpha+\tfrac{3}{2})\Big)\\
&\quad-\frac{5}{24}-\frac{1}{12}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}-\frac{5}{4}\Big(\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}\Big)^{2}\Big)t\,dt.\end{split}

Here
\zeta
is the Riemann zeta function and
G
is the Barnes
G
-function.

By using the scaling parameter
n
, we can again rewrite the expansion so that the energy and entropy terms appear only in the coefficients of
n^{2}
and
n
, respectively:

(1.40)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\log Z_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(Q)&\sim-2I[\mu]\,n^{2}+\frac{1}{2}N\log N-\Big(\frac{1}{2}E[\mu]+(4c+1)U_{\mu}(0)\Big)n+\Big(\frac{\log(4\pi)}{2}-1\Big)N+D_{4}\log N\\
&\quad+\Big(c^{2}+c+\frac{5}{24}\Big)\log\rho(0)+\Big(\alpha^{2}+\alpha+\frac{5}{24}\Big)\log\tilde{\rho}(0)+(\alpha+c+1)\log(2\pi)+4\zeta^{\prime}(-1)\\
&\quad-\log\Big(G(c+1)G(c+\tfrac{3}{2})G(\alpha+1)G(\alpha+\tfrac{3}{2})\Big)-\frac{5}{24}-\frac{1}{12}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}-\frac{5}{4}\Big(\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}\Big)^{2}\Big)t\,dt.\end{split}

Remark 2
.

Notice that the term
D_{3}
contains
(4c+1)U_{\mu}(0).
As explained in
[
14
]
, due to the radial symmetry,
U_{\mu}(0)
can also be rewritten as

U_{\mu}(0)=-\int\log|z-\overline{z}|\,d\mu(z),

which leads to

(1.41)

(4c+1)U_{\mu}(0)=4c\,U_{\mu}(0)-\int\log|z-\overline{z}|\,d\mu(z).

Albeit equivalent, the expression on the right-hand side captures more of the model’s statistical meaning, as the first term arises from the insertion of a point charge, while the second term reflects the local repulsion along the real axis in (
1.5
).

Due to the well-known evaluation of
G(\tfrac{1}{2})
(see e.g.
[
8
]
) and the recursive relation (
1.24
), we have

(1.42)

\log G(\tfrac{3}{2})=\frac{1}{24}\log 2+\frac{3}{2}\zeta^{\prime}(-1)+\frac{1}{4}\log\pi.

Then as an immediate consequence of the above theorems, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.5
.

Suppose that
\alpha=c=0
. Then under the same assumptions in Theorem
1.3
, as
N\to\infty
, we have

(1.43)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\log Z_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(Q)&=-I[\mu]N^{2}+\frac{1}{2}N\log N+\Big(\frac{\log(2\pi)}{2}-1-2I[\mu]-\frac{1}{2}E[\mu]\Big)N+\frac{1}{3}\log N\\
&\quad+\frac{\log(2\pi)}{2}+2\zeta^{\prime}(-1)-I[\mu]-\frac{1}{2}E[\mu]+\frac{1}{6}\log(\rho(0)\tilde{\rho}(0))\\
&\quad-\frac{11}{12}-\frac{1}{6}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}-\frac{5}{4}\Big(\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}\Big)^{2}\Big)t\,dt+O(N^{-\frac{1}{12}}(\log N)^{d}),\end{split}

and

(1.44)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\log Z_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(Q)&=-2I[\mu]N^{2}+\frac{1}{2}N\log N+\Big(\frac{\log(4\pi)}{2}-1-4I[\mu]-\frac{1}{2}E[\mu]-U_{\mu}(0)\Big)N+\frac{5}{12}\log N\\
&\quad+\frac{\log(2\pi)}{2}+\frac{5\log 2}{12}+\zeta^{\prime}(-1)-2I[\mu]-\frac{1}{2}E[\mu]-\,U_{\mu}(0)+\frac{5}{24}\log(\rho(0)\tilde{\rho}(0))\\
&\quad-\frac{17}{24}-\frac{1}{12}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}-\frac{5}{4}\Big(\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}\Big)^{2}\Big)t\,dt+O(N^{-\frac{1}{12}}(\log N)^{d}).\end{split}

Remark 4
.

The growth condition (
1.13
) of
\bm{\rho}
guarantees that the integrand in

and

vanishes at infinity with the following decay rate

\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}-\frac{5}{4}\Big(\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}\Big)^{2}=o(\frac{1}{t^{2}}),\qquad t\to\infty,

and thus it is integrable over

.
Let

(1.47)

\kappa(r)=-\frac{2\Delta\log\bm{\rho}(z)}{\bm{\rho}(z)}=-\frac{1}{2\rho(r)}\Big(\frac{\rho^{\prime}(r)}{\rho(r)}\Big)^{\prime},\qquad(r=|z|)

be the curvature.
Then

\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}-\frac{5}{4}\Big(\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}\Big)^{2}=\Big(\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}\Big)^{\prime}-\frac{1}{4}\Big(\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}\Big)^{2}=-2\kappa(t)\rho(t)-\Big(\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t)}{\rho(t)}\Big)^{2}.

This term appears in the Polyakov–Alvarez formula for exterior determinants in

.

Plan of the paper

The rest of this paper is organised as follows. In the next section, we provide preliminaries such as the integrable structures of partition functions and an outline of the proof of our main results. Section
3
is devoted to the asymptotic behaviours of orthogonal and skew-orthogonal norms. These are crucially used in Section
4
, where we complete the proofs of the main theorems.

2.
Preliminaries and outline of the proof

In this section, we provide the preliminaries and outline our main results.

2.1.
Stereographic projection

We first provide a proof of Lemma
1.1
, which involves a simple change of variables under the stereographic projection.

Proof of Lemma
1.1
.

Recall that the stereographic projection
\phi
is given by (
1.7
). It has the inverse

(2.1)

\phi^{-1}(z)=\Big(\frac{2\operatorname{Re}z}{1+|z|^{2}},\frac{2\operatorname{Im}z}{1+|z|^{2}},\frac{-1+|z|^{2}}{1+|z|^{2}}\Big).

Let us write
z=\phi(x),w=\phi(y)
for
x,y\in\mathbb{S}
. Then the chordal distance on the Riemann sphere is given by

\|x-y\|=\frac{2|z-w|}{\sqrt{(1+|z|^{2})(1+|w|^{2})}}.

On the one hand, since

dA_{\mathbb{S}}(x)=\frac{dA(z)}{(1+|z|^{2})^{2}},

we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\prod_{j>k=1}^{N}\|x_{j}-x_{k}\|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{N}e^{-NQ_{\mathbb{S}}(x_{j})}\,dA_{\mathbb{S}}(x_{j})=2^{N(N-1)}\prod_{j>k=1}^{N}|z_{j}-z_{k}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{N}e^{-NQ(z_{j})}\,dA(z_{j}),\end{split}

and

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\prod_{j>k=1}^{N}\|x_{j}-x_{k}\|^{2}\|x_{j}-\overline{x}_{k}\|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\|x_{j}-\overline{x}_{j}\|^{2}e^{-2NQ_{\mathbb{S}}(x_{j})}\,dA_{\mathbb{S}}(x_{j})\\
&=2^{2N^{2}}\prod_{j>k=1}^{N}|z_{j}-z_{k}|^{2}|z_{j}-\bar{z}_{k}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{N}|z_{j}-\bar{z}_{j}|^{2}e^{-NQ(z_{j})}\,dA(z_{j}).\end{split}

This completes the proof.
∎

2.2.
Partition functions and planar (skew)-orthogonal polynomials

We now discuss the integrable structures of the partition functions.
The statistical properties of (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) are effectively analysed using their integrable structures and the associated planar (skew)-orthogonal polynomials, see e.g.
[
12
]
.
For a given potential
Q
, let
(p_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a family of monic orthogonal polynomial:

(2.4)

\int_{\mathbb{C}}p_{j}(z)\overline{p_{k}(z)}e^{-NQ(z)}\,dA(z)=h_{k}\,\delta_{j,k},

where
h_{k}
is the squared norm and
\delta
is the Kronecker delta. We refer to
[
32
,
40
,
35
]
and references therein for recent progress on planar orthogonal polynomials.
On the other hand, a family of polynomials
(q_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is called planar skew-orthogonal polynomials
[
29
,
34
]
if it satisfies

(2.5)

\langle q_{2k},q_{2\ell}\rangle_{s}=\langle q_{2k+1},q_{2\ell+1}\rangle_{s}=0,\qquad\langle q_{2k},q_{2\ell+1}\rangle_{s}=-\langle q_{2\ell+1},q_{2k}\rangle_{s}=r_{k}\,\delta_{k,\ell},

where

(2.6)

\langle f,g\rangle_{s}:=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\Big(f(z)\overline{g(z)}-g(z)\overline{f(z)}\Big)(z-\overline{z})\,e^{-2NQ(z)}\,dA(z)

and
r_{k}
is their skew-norm.
Then, it follows from Andréief’s and de Bruijn’s integration formulas that the partition functions can be expressed as

(2.7)

Z_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(Q)=N!\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}h_{j},\qquad Z_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(Q)=N!\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}r_{j}.

For the radially symmetric potential
Q
, it is obvious that the associated orthogonal polynomial is monomials. Therefore, the squared norm is given by
h_{j,N}
, where

(2.8)

h_{j,m}:=\int_{\mathbb{C}}|z|^{2j}\,e^{-mQ(z)}\,dA(z).

In general, there is no comprehensive theory for constructing skew-orthogonal polynomials. Nonetheless, a recent work
[
3
]
introduced a method to construct them under the assumption that the associated orthogonal polynomial satisfies the three-term recurrence relation, see also a recent work
[
4
]
providing an alternative and more general construction. In particular, for a radially symmetric potential
Q
, it follows from
[
3
, Corollary 4.3]
that

(2.9)

q_{2k+1}(z)=z^{2k+1},\qquad q_{2k}(z)=z^{2k}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}z^{2\ell}\prod_{j=0}^{k-\ell-1}\frac{h_{2\ell+2j+2}}{h_{2\ell+2j+1}},\qquad r_{k}=2\,h_{2k+1,2N}.

Combining the above, for a radially symmetric
Q
, we have the expression

(2.10)

\log Z_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}=\log N!+\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\log h_{j,N},\qquad\log Z_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}=\log N!+\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\log(2h_{2j+1,2N}).

The rest of this paper is devoted to analyse the summations in (
2.10
).

In order to obtain the asymptotic expansions of the orthogonal norms
h_{j,N}
(resp.,
h_{j,2N}
), we distinguish the following three cases. Let
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
be the large numbers with
m_{1}
,
m_{2}\in\Theta(N^{\epsilon})
with some
\epsilon>0
.

•

Case 1:
m_{1}\leq j<N-m_{2}
(resp.,
2m_{1}\leq j<2(N-m_{2})
). Applying the Laplace’s method, we obtain the asymptotic expansions of the integral
h_{j,N}
which mainly contribute the large
N
expansion of the partition function.

•

Case 2:
0\leq j<m_{1}
(resp.,
0\leq j<2m_{1}
). In this case, the asymptotic expansion contains the gamma function
\Gamma(c+1)
where
c
is the point charge at the origin (or the south pole).

•

Case 3:
N-m_{2}\leq j<N
(resp.,
2(N-m_{2})\leq j<2N
). Symmetrically to Case 2, the asymptotic expansion contains
\Gamma(\alpha+1)
where
\alpha
is the point charge at infinity (or the north pole).

Asymptotic behaviours of the orthogonal norms in each case are given in Lemmas
3.1
,
3.2
, and

, respectively.

In addition to the asymptotic behaviours in each case, to analyse the summations

(2.11)

\sum_{j=m_{1}}^{N-m_{2}-1}\log h_{j,N},\qquad\sum_{j=0}^{m_{1}-1}\log h_{j,N},\qquad\sum_{j=N-m_{2}}^{N-1}\log h_{j,N},

respectively, we will make use of the Euler-Maclaurin formula (see e.g.

\displaystyle\sum_{j=m}^{n}f(j)=\int_{m}^{n}f(x)\,dx+\frac{f(m)+f(n)}{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{l-1}\frac{B_{2k}}{(2k)!}\big(f^{(2k-1)}(n)-f^{(2k-1)}(m)\big)+R_{l},

where

is the Bernoulli number defined by the generating function

\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}B_{k}\frac{t^{k}}{k!},\qquad(|t|<2\pi).

Here, the error term

satisfies

|R_{l}|\leq C_{l}\int_{m}^{n}|f^{(2l)}(x)|\,dx

for some constant

4.5

2.3.
Logarithmic potential and critical points

In this subsection, we set up some notations that will be used in the asymptotic behaviours of the orthogonal norms.

We first define a function

(2.13)

F(t)=\int_{t}^{\infty}2r\rho(r)\,dr=1-\mu(D(0,t)),\qquad t\in[0,\infty).

It follows from (
1.13
) that

(2.14)

F(t)\sim\frac{A}{t^{2}},\qquad t\to\infty.

We express the potential
U_{\mu}
in (
1.15
) in terms of the function
F
in (
2.13
).

Lemma 2.1
.

For each
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
, we have

(2.15)

U_{\mu}(z)=-\log|z|-\int_{|z|}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r}F(r)\,dr.

Proof.

Recall the well-known Jensen’s formula: for
r>0
,

(2.16)

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log|z-re^{i\theta}|\,d\theta=\begin{cases}\log r&\text{if }r>|z|,\\
\log|z|&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

By using this, the definition (
1.15
), and integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle U_{\mu}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\bigg(\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\frac{1}{|z-re^{i\theta}|}\,d\theta\bigg)\rho(r)r\,dr=-2\log|z|\int_{0}^{|z|}\rho(r)r\,dr-2\int_{|z|}^{\infty}r\rho(r)\log r\,dr

\displaystyle=-\big(1-F(|z|)\big)\log|z|+\Big[F(r)\log r\Big]_{r=|z|}^{\infty}-\int_{|z|}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r}F(r)\,dr.

Then the desired identity follows from the fact that
F(r)\log r\to 0
as
r\to\infty
.
∎

For
r>0
, we define the function

(2.17)

V_{j}(r):=\frac{N}{n}\Big(Q_{\alpha,c}(r)-\frac{2j+1}{N}\log r\Big)=-2U_{\mu}(r)-\frac{2j+2c+1}{n}\log r,

where
Q_{\alpha,c}
is given by (
1.16
).
It is convenient to use
V_{j}
to analyse the orthogonal norm since

(2.18)

h_{j,N}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}|z|^{2j}e^{-NQ_{\alpha,c}(z)}\,dA(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}2e^{-nV_{j}(r)}\,dr.

For each
\tau
with
0<\tau<1
, let
r_{\tau}
be the number satisfying

(2.19)

F(r_{\tau})=1-\tau.

Since
F
is strictly decreasing in the range between
0
and
1
,
r_{0}=0
,
r_{1}=\infty
, and
r_{\tau}
is uniquely determined for each
0<\tau<\infty
.
For each
\alpha,c\geq 0
, we write

(2.20)

\tau_{c}(j)=\frac{2(j+c)+1}{2n},\qquad\tau_{\alpha}(j)=\frac{2(j+\alpha)+1}{2n}.

Then it follows from the numerology
n=N+\alpha+c+1
that
\tau_{c}
and
\tau_{\alpha}
have the relation

(2.21)

\tau_{c}(j)+\tau_{\alpha}(N-j)=1.

On the other hand, by Lemma
2.1
, one can rewrite
V_{j}
as

(2.22)

V_{j}(r)=2\Big(\log r+\int_{r}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t}F(t)\,dt-\tau_{c}(j)\log r\Big).

This gives

(2.23)

V_{j}^{\prime}(r)=\frac{2}{r}(1-F(r)-\tau_{c}(j)).

Observe that there exists a unique critical point
t_{j}
of
V_{j}
such that
V_{j}^{\prime}(t_{j})=0
, and

(2.24)

F(t_{j})=1-\tau_{c}(j).

This implies that

(2.25)

t_{j}=r_{\tau_{c}(j)},

where
r_{\tau}
is defined in (
2.19
).
Differentiating (
2.23
), we have

(2.26)

\displaystyle\begin{split}V_{j}^{\prime\prime}(t_{j})&=\Big(-\frac{1}{r}V_{j}^{\prime}(r)+4\rho(r)\Big)\Big|_{r=t_{j}}=4\rho(t_{j}),\\
V_{j}^{(3)}(t_{j})&=\Big(\frac{2}{r^{2}}V_{j}^{\prime}(r)-\frac{4}{r}\rho(r)+4\rho^{\prime}(r)\Big)\Big|_{r=t_{j}}=-\frac{4}{t_{j}}\rho(t_{j})+4\rho^{\prime}(t_{j}),\\
V_{j}^{(4)}(t_{j})&=\Big(-\frac{6}{r^{3}}V_{j}^{\prime}(r)+\frac{12}{r^{2}}\rho(r)-\frac{4}{r}\rho^{\prime}(r)+4\rho^{\prime\prime}(r)\Big)\Big|_{r=t_{j}}=\frac{12}{t_{j}^{2}}\rho(t_{j})-\frac{4}{t_{j}}\rho^{\prime}(t_{j})+4\rho^{\prime\prime}(t_{j}).\end{split}

The following lemma describes the asymptotic behaviour of the critical point.

Lemma 2.2
.

For small
j\ll N
, the critical point
t_{j}
satisfies

(2.27)

t_{j}=\Big(\frac{\tau_{c}(j)}{\rho(0)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+O(\frac{1}{N}),\qquad N\to\infty.

On the other hand, for
j=N-k
with small
k\ll N
, it satisfies

(2.28)

t_{j}^{-1}=\Big(\frac{\tau_{\alpha}(k)}{\tilde{\rho}(0)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+O(\frac{1}{N}),\qquad N\to\infty.

Proof.

For small
t>0
, we have

\displaystyle 1-F(t)=\int_{0}^{t}2r\rho(r)\,dr=\rho(0)t^{2}+O(t^{3}),\qquad t\to 0.

Combining this with (
2.24
), we have the asymptotic expansion (
2.27
) for the critical point
t_{j}
.
On the other hand, for large
t
near the infinity, by using the inversion, we obtain

\displaystyle F(t)=\int_{t}^{\infty}2r\rho(r)\,dr=\int_{0}^{t^{-1}}2s^{-3}\rho(s^{-1})\,ds=\tilde{\rho}(0)t^{-2}+O(t^{-3}),\qquad t\to\infty.

Here, we have used
\rho(s)=\tilde{\rho}(0)s^{-4}+O(s^{-5})
as
s\to\infty
, cf. (
1.13
). Combining with the relation (
2.21
), this gives the desired result.
∎

For the symplectic counterpart, we also define

(2.29)

\widetilde{V}_{j}(r):=-2U_{\mu}(r)-\frac{2j+4c+1}{2n}\log r

so that

h_{j,2N}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}|z|^{2j}e^{-2NQ_{\alpha,c}(z)}\,dA(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}2e^{-2n\widetilde{V}_{j}(r)}\,dr.

The unique critical point
\tilde{t}_{j}
of
\widetilde{V}_{j}
then satisfies

(2.30)

F(\tilde{t}_{j})=1-\tilde{\tau}_{c}(j),\qquad\tilde{\tau}_{c}(j)=\frac{2j+4c+1}{4n}.

Here, we note that the following relation holds:

(2.31)

\tilde{\tau}_{c}(2(N-j)+1)=\frac{4(N-j)+4c+3}{4n}=1-\frac{4j+4\alpha+1}{4n}=1-\tilde{\tau}_{\alpha}(2j).

By applying the same argument as in Lemma
2.2
, we obtain the following asymptotic expansion for
\tilde{t}_{j}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For small
j\ll 2N
, the critical point
\tilde{t}_{j}
satisfies

(2.32)

\tilde{t}_{j}=\Big(\frac{\tilde{\tau}_{c}(j)}{\rho(0)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+O(\tilde{\tau}_{c}(j)),\quad N\to\infty.

On the other hand, for
j=2N-k
with small
k\ll 2N
, it satisfies

(2.33)

\tilde{t}_{j}^{-1}=\Big(\frac{\tilde{\tau}_{\alpha}(k+1)}{\tilde{\rho}(0)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+O(\tilde{\tau}_{\alpha}(k+1)),\quad N\to\infty.

3.
Asymptotic analysis of (skew)-orthogonal norms

In this section, we derive the asymptotic behaviours of the (skew)-orthogonal norms for Cases 1, 2, and 3, as described in Subsection
2.2
.

3.1.
Asymptotics of orthogonal norms: Case 1

For the formulation of the asymptotic expansion of the orthogonal norm, we define the functional

(3.1)

\mathfrak{B}_{1}(r):=-\frac{1}{24}\frac{1}{r^{2}\rho(r)}-\frac{7}{96}\frac{\rho^{\prime}(r)}{r\rho(r)^{2}}-\frac{1}{32}\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}(r)}{\rho(r)^{2}}+\frac{5}{96}\frac{(\rho^{\prime}(r))^{2}}{\rho(r)^{3}}.

Lemma 3.1
.

As
N\to\infty
,we have the following.

•

For
j
with
m_{1}\leq j<N-m_{2}
, we have

(3.2)

\log h_{j,N}=-nV_{j}(t_{j})+\frac{1}{2}\left(\log 2\pi-\log n-\log\rho(t_{j})\right)+\frac{1}{n}\mathfrak{B}_{1}(t_{j})+\varepsilon_{N,1}(j),

where
\varepsilon_{N,1}(j)=O(j^{-2}(\log N)^{d})+O((N-j)^{-2}(\log N)^{d})
for some
d>0
.

•

For
j
with
2m_{1}\leq j<2N-2m_{2}
, we have

(3.3)

\log h_{j,2N}=-2n\widetilde{V}_{j}(\tilde{t}_{j})+\frac{1}{2}\big(\log(2\pi)-\log(2n)-\log\rho(\tilde{t}_{j})\big)+\frac{1}{2n}\mathfrak{B}_{1}(\tilde{t}_{j})+\tilde{\varepsilon}_{N,1}(j),

where
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{N,1}(j)=O(j^{-2}(\log N)^{d})+O((2N-j)^{-2}(\log N)^{d})
for some
d>0
.

Proof.

In the sequel, it is convenient to write

(3.4)

\delta_{N}=\log N/\sqrt{N}.

Recall that the orthogonal norm
h_{j,N}
can be written as (
2.18
).

We first consider the case when
m_{1}\leq j\leq aN
for some constant
0<a<1
so that the critical point
t_{j}
given by (
2.25
) is contained in a compact set. Then, by Lemma
2.2
, we have

V_{j}^{(k)}(t_{j})=O(t_{j}^{-k+2})=O((N/j)^{k/2-1})

and the Taylor series expansion at the critical point
t_{j}
deduces

\displaystyle\int_{|r-t_{j}|<\delta_{N}}2\,e^{-nV_{j}(r)}\,dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}e^{-nV_{j}(t_{j})}\int_{-\sqrt{n}\delta_{N}}^{\sqrt{n}\delta_{N}}e^{-\big(\frac{1}{2}V_{j}^{\prime\prime}(t_{j})s^{2}+\frac{1}{6\sqrt{n}}V_{j}^{(3)}(t_{j})s^{3}+\frac{1}{24n}V_{j}^{(4)}(t_{j})s^{4}+\frac{1}{120n\sqrt{n}}V_{j}^{(5)}(t_{j})s^{5}+O(j^{-2}|s|^{6})\big)}\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\sqrt{n}}e^{-nV_{j}(t_{j})}\int_{-\sqrt{n}\delta_{N}}^{\sqrt{n}\delta_{N}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}V_{j}^{\prime\prime}(t_{j})s^{2}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{24n}V_{j}^{(4)}(t_{j})s^{4}+\frac{1}{72n}(V_{j}^{(3)}(t_{j}))^{2}s^{6}+O(j^{-2}(\log N)^{8})\Big)\,ds.

Computing the Gaussian integrals, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{|r-t_{j}|<\delta_{N}}2\,e^{-nV_{j}(r)}\,dr\\
=&\frac{2\sqrt{2\pi}}{\sqrt{nV_{j}^{\prime\prime}(t_{j})}}e^{-nV_{j}(t_{j})}\Big(1-\frac{1}{8n}\frac{V_{j}^{(4)}(t_{j})}{(V_{j}^{\prime\prime}(t_{j}))^{2}}+\frac{5}{24n}\frac{(V_{j}^{(3)}(t_{j}))^{2}}{(V_{j}^{\prime\prime}(t_{j}))^{3}}+\epsilon_{N,1}(j)\Big)\\
=&\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{\sqrt{n\rho(t_{j})}}e^{-nV_{j}(t_{j})}\Big(1+\frac{1}{n}\Big(-\frac{1}{24}\frac{1}{\rho(t_{j})t_{j}^{2}}-\frac{7}{96}\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t_{j})}{\rho(t_{j})^{2}t_{j}}-\frac{1}{32}\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}(t_{j})}{\rho(t_{j})^{2}}+\frac{5}{96}\frac{(\rho^{\prime}(t_{j}))^{2}}{\rho(t_{j})^{3}}\Big)+\epsilon_{N,1}(j)\Big),\end{split}

where
\epsilon_{N,1}(j)=O(j^{-2}(\log N)^{d})
for some
d>0
and the
O
-terms are uniform for all
m_{1}\leq j\leq aN
.

On the other hand, it follows from Lemma
2.2
that for
aN\leq j<N-m_{2}
,

V_{j}^{(k)}(t_{j})=O(t_{j}^{-k-2})=O(((N-j)/N)^{k/2+1}).

By using calculations similar to those in the case
m_{1}\leq j\leq aN
, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{|r-t_{j}|<\delta_{N}}2\,e^{-nV_{j}(r)}\,dr\\
=&\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{\sqrt{n\rho(t_{j})}}e^{-nV_{j}(t_{j})}\Big(1+\frac{1}{n}\Big(-\frac{1}{24}\frac{1}{\rho(t_{j})t_{j}^{2}}-\frac{7}{96}\frac{\rho^{\prime}(t_{j})}{\rho(t_{j})^{2}t_{j}}-\frac{1}{32}\frac{\rho^{\prime\prime}(t_{j})}{\rho(t_{j})^{2}}+\frac{5}{96}\frac{(\rho^{\prime}(t_{j}))^{2}}{\rho(t_{j})^{3}}\Big)+\epsilon^{\prime}_{N,1}(j)\Big),\end{split}

where
\epsilon^{\prime}_{N,1}(j)=O((N-j)^{-2}(\log N)^{d})
for some
d>0
and the
O
-terms are uniform for all
aN\leq j<N-m_{2}
.

Next, we show that the integral over the outer region
|r-t_{j}|>\delta_{N}
is negligible. For this, notice that there exists
c>0
such that for
r
with
|r-t_{j}|>\delta_{N}
, the estimate

|V_{j}(r)-V_{j}(t_{j})|\geq cV_{j}^{\prime\prime}(t_{j})\delta_{N}^{2}

holds. Then it follows that

\begin{split}\int_{|r-t_{j}|>\delta_{N}}e^{-nV_{j}(r)}\,dr=e^{-nV_{j}(t_{j})}\int_{|r-t_{j}|>\delta_{N}}e^{-n(V_{j}(r)-V_{j}(t_{j}))}\,dr\leq e^{-nV_{j}(t_{j})}\epsilon_{N},\end{split}

where
\epsilon_{N}=O(e^{-c^{\prime}(\log N)^{2}})
for some
c^{\prime}>0
and the
O
-constant can be taken uniformly for all
j
. This completes the proof of the first assertion. The second assertion follows from similar computations with minor modifications.
∎

3.2.
Asymptotics of orthogonal norms: Case 2 and Case 3

We begin with the following lemma about the asymptotic expansion of orthogonal norms of Case 2.

Lemma 3.2
.

As
N\to\infty
we have the following.

•

For
j
with
0\leq j<m_{1}
, we have

(3.5)

\log h_{j,N}=2nU_{\mu}(0)-(j+c+1)\log(n\rho(0))+\log\Gamma(j+c+1)+\varepsilon_{N,2}(j),

where
\varepsilon_{N,2}(j)=O(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}(j+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}(\log N)^{3})
.

•

For
0\leq j<2m_{1}
, we have

(3.6)

\log h_{j,2N}=4nU_{\mu}(0)-(j+2c+1)\log(2n\rho(0))+\log\Gamma(j+2c+1)+\tilde{\varepsilon}_{N,2}(j),

where
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{N,2}(j)=O(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}(j+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}(\log N)^{3})
.

Proof.

Recall that
t_{j}
is the critical point of
V_{j}
given by (
2.25
).
Let
t_{j}^{*}=t_{j}\cdot\log N
. We split the integral for
h_{j,N}
by

(3.7)

\displaystyle h_{j,N}=\int_{0}^{t_{j}^{*}}2r^{2j+1}e^{-NQ_{\alpha,c}(r)}\,dr+\int_{t_{j}^{*}}^{\infty}2r^{2j+1}e^{-NQ_{\alpha,c}(r)}\,dr.

Recall that
Q_{\alpha,c}
is given by (
1.16
).
Note that by (
2.15
), we have
{\partial}_{r}U_{\mu}(0)=0
and
{\partial}^{2}_{r}U_{\mu}(0)=-\rho(0)
.
This gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t_{j}^{*}}2r^{2j+1}e^{-NQ_{\alpha,c}(r)}\,dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t_{j}^{*}}2r^{2j+2c+1}e^{2n(U_{\mu}(0)-\frac{1}{2}\rho(0)r^{2}+O(r^{3}))}\,dr

\displaystyle=e^{2nU_{\mu}(0)}\int_{0}^{t_{j}^{*}}2r^{2(j+c)+1}e^{-n\rho(0)r^{2}}(1+O(nr^{3}))\,dr.

By using (
2.25
) and (
2.27
), we have

{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}t_{j}^{*}=\Big(\frac{2j+2c+1}{2\rho(0)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\log N+O((j+1)N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log N).

Consequently, it follows that

(3.8)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t_{j}^{*}}2r^{2j+1}e^{-NQ_{\alpha,c}(r)}\,dr&=e^{2nU_{\mu}(0)}n^{-(j+c+1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}2r^{2j+2c+1}e^{-\rho(0)r^{2}}\,dr\cdot(1+O(n\cdot{t_{j}^{*}}^{3}))\\
&=e^{2nU_{\mu}(0)}(n\rho(0))^{-(j+c+1)}\Gamma(j+c+1)\cdot(1+O(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}(j+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}(\log N)^{3})).\end{split}

Next, we show that the remaining integral in (
3.7
) is negligible.
Notice that by (
1.19
) and (
2.17
), the function
r\mapsto e^{-V_{j}(r)}
is integrable.
Since
V_{j}
has a global minimum at
t_{j}
and increases in
(t_{j},\infty)
, for
r>t_{j}^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle V_{j}(r)\geq V_{j}(t_{j}^{*})

\displaystyle=V_{j}(t_{j})+2\rho(t_{j})(t_{j}^{*}-t_{j})^{2}+O(t_{j}^{*}-t_{j})^{3}

\displaystyle=-2U_{\mu}(t_{j})-\frac{2(j+c)+1}{n}\log t_{j}+2\rho(t_{j})(t_{j}^{*}-t_{j})^{2}+O(t_{j}^{*}-t_{j})^{3}\geq-2U_{\mu}(0)+c_{1}{t_{j}^{*}}^{2},

where
c_{1}>0
can be taken to be independent for all
j
with
j<m_{N}
.
Then it follows that

(3.9)

\begin{split}\int_{t_{j}^{*}}^{\infty}2e^{-nV_{j}(r)}\,dr\leq e^{-(n-1)(-2U_{\mu}(0)+c_{1}{t_{j}^{*}}^{2})}\int_{t_{j}^{*}}^{\infty}e^{-V_{j}(r)}\,dr\leq Ce^{2nU_{\mu}(0)}e^{-c_{1}(n-1){t_{j}}^{2}(\log N)^{2}}\end{split}

for some
C>0
. Therefore we conclude that the integral in (
3.9
) is negligible. ∎

We use the inversion to obtain the estimate of the orthogonal norm
h_{j,N}
for
N-m_{2}\leq j\leq N-1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

As
N\to\infty
, we have the following.

•

For
j
with
N-m_{2}\leq j<N
, we have

(3.10)

\log h_{j,N}=-(N-j+\alpha)\log(n\tilde{\rho}(0))+\log\Gamma(N-j+\alpha)+\varepsilon_{N,3}(j),

where
\varepsilon_{N,3}(j)=O(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}(N-j)^{\frac{3}{2}}(\log N)^{3})
.

•

For
2N-2m_{2}\leq j<2N
, we have

(3.11)

\log h_{j,2N}=-(2N-j+2\alpha+1)\log(2n\tilde{\rho}(0))+\log\Gamma(2N-j+2\alpha+1)+\tilde{\varepsilon}_{N,3}(j),

where
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{N,3}(j)=O(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}(2N-j)^{\frac{3}{2}}(\log N)^{3}).

Proof.

Using the inversion, we write

h_{j,N}=\int_{0}^{\infty}2e^{-nW_{j}(s)}\,ds,\qquad W_{j}(s):=V_{j}(s^{-1})+\frac{2}{n}\log s.

Note that by (
2.21
) and (
2.22
), we have

\begin{split}W_{j}(s)=2\int_{0}^{s}u^{-1}F(u^{-1})\,du-2\tau_{\alpha}(N-1-j)\log s,\qquad W_{j}^{\prime}(s)=2s^{-1}\Big(F(s^{-1})-\tau_{\alpha}(N-1-j)\Big),\end{split}

where
F
is given by (
2.13
).
Notice that there exists a unique number
s_{j}
such that
W_{j}^{\prime}(s_{j})=0
. Then, similar to the proof of Lemma
2.2
, we can see that
for
j
with
N-m_{2}\leq j\leq N-1,

(3.12)

s_{j}=\Big(\frac{\tau_{\alpha}(N-1-j)}{\tilde{\rho}(0)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+O(\tau_{\alpha}(N-1-j)),\quad N\to\infty,

where
\tilde{\rho}
is defined by (
1.14
). Note that by (
1.13
),
\tilde{\rho}
has the asymptotic expansion

(3.13)

\tilde{\rho}(s)=\tilde{\rho}(0)+\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}(0)s+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\rho}^{\prime\prime}(0)s^{2}+O(s^{3}),\qquad s\to 0.

By using the change of variables, we have

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{s}u^{-1}F(u^{-1})\,du&=\int_{0}^{s}u^{-1}\int_{u^{-1}}^{\infty}2r\rho(r)\,dr\,du=\int_{0}^{s}u^{-1}\int_{0}^{u}2w\tilde{\rho}(w)\,dw\,du=\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\rho}(0)s^{2}+O(s^{3}),\end{split}

as
s\to 0
.
Let
s_{j}^{*}=s_{j}\log N
. Then it follows from (
3.12
) that

n^{\frac{1}{2}}s_{j}^{*}=\Big(\frac{2(N-j+\alpha)-1}{2\tilde{\rho}(0)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\log N+O(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}(N-j)\log N).

By using this asymptotic behaviour, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s_{j}^{*}}2e^{-nW_{j}(s)}\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s_{j}^{*}}2s^{2(N-j+\alpha)-1}e^{-n\tilde{\rho}(0)s^{2}}(1+O(ns^{3}))\,ds

\displaystyle=(n\tilde{\rho}(0))^{-(N-j+\alpha)}\,\Gamma(N-j+\alpha)\cdot(1+O(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}(N-j)^{\frac{3}{2}}(\log N)^{3})).

Furthermore, as in the proof of Lemma
3.2
, the integral over
(s_{j}^{*},\infty)
is negligible. Hence, the desired result follows.
∎

Proof of main results

In this section, we prove our main results, Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
.

4.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this subsection, we show Theorem
1.3
.
For this, we need to derive the asymptotic expansion of the summations (
2.11
) using Lemmas
3.1
,
3.2
and
3.3
.

We begin with the summation
\sum_{j=m_{1}}^{N-m_{2}-1}\log h_{j,N}
.
Note that by Lemma
3.1
, it suffices to derive asymptotic behaviours of the summations

(4.1)

-n\sum_{j=m_{1}}^{N-m_{2}-1}V_{j}(t_{j}),\qquad\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=m_{1}}^{N-m_{2}-1}\log\rho(t_{j}),\qquad\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=m_{1}}^{N-m_{2}-1}\mathfrak{B}_{1}(t_{j}).

The asymptotic behaviour of the first term is given by (
4.3
) together with Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
, whereas the asymptotic behaviours of the second and third terms are given by Lemmas
4.3
and
4.4
, respectively. Combining all of the above, the final result for the asymptotic behaviour of
\sum_{j=m_{1}}^{N-m_{2}-1}\log h_{j,N}
is provided in Lemma
4.5
.

Recall that
V_{j}
is given by (
2.17
). We observe that

V_{j}(t_{j})=-2U_{\mu}(t_{j})-2\tau_{c}(j)\log t_{j}=-2U_{\mu}(t_{j})-2(1-F(t_{j}))\log t_{j},

where
t_{j}
is given by (
2.25
).
Note that the definition of
t_{j}
can be extended from integer values
j
to positive real numbers
s
. Since
F^{\prime}(t)=-2t\rho(t)
, we also have

(4.2)

\frac{dt_{s}}{ds}=-\frac{1}{nF^{\prime}(t_{s})}=\frac{1}{2nt_{s}\rho(t_{s})}.

Then by the Euler-Maclaurin formula (
2.12
), we have

(4.3)

\begin{split}\sum_{j=m_{1}}^{N-m_{2}-1}V_{j}(t_{j})&=-n\int_{t_{m_{1}}}^{t_{N-m_{2}}}\Big(2U_{\mu}(t)+2(1-F(t))\log t\Big)\cdot 2t\rho(t)\,dt\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\Big(V_{m_{1}}(t_{m_{1}})-V_{N-m_{2}}(t_{m_{2}})\Big)+\frac{1}{12}\Big({\partial}_{s}(V_{s}(t_{s}))\big|_{s={N-m_{2}}}-{\partial}_{s}(V_{s}(t_{s}))\big|_{s={m_{1}}}\Big)+\epsilon_{N}.\end{split}

Here, the error term satisfies

\epsilon_{N}=O(N^{-3}\tau_{c}(m_{1})^{-2})+O(N^{-3}\tau_{\alpha}(m_{2})^{-2})=O(N^{-1}m_{1}^{-2})+O(N^{-1}m_{2}^{-2})

since
{\partial}_{s}^{3}\,V_{s}(t_{s})\big|_{s=m_{1}}=O(N^{-3}t_{m_{1}}^{-4})
and
{\partial}_{s}^{3}\,V_{s}(t_{s})|_{s=N-m_{2}}=O(N^{-3}t_{N-m_{2}}^{4})
, cf. (
2.26
).

We first derive the asymptotic behaviour of the first term in the left-hand side of (
4.3
).

Lemma 4.1
.

As
N\to\infty
, we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad-n\int_{t_{m_{1}}}^{t_{N-m_{2}}}\Big(2U_{\mu}(t)+2(1-F(t))\log t\Big)\cdot 2t\rho(t)\,dt\\
&=nI[\mu]+n\Big(2\tau_{c}(m_{1})U_{\mu}(0)-\frac{3}{4}\tau_{c}(m_{1})^{2}+\tau_{c}(m_{1})^{2}\log t_{m_{1}}\Big)-n\Big(\frac{3}{4}\tau_{\alpha}(m_{2})^{2}+\tau_{\alpha}(m_{2})^{2}\log t_{N-m_{2}}\Big)+\epsilon_{N},\end{split}

where
\epsilon_{N}=O\big(N^{-\frac{3}{2}}(m_{1}^{\frac{5}{2}}+m_{2}^{\frac{5}{2}})\big)
.

Proof.

By using
I[\mu]=-\int U_{\mu}\,d\mu
, we first write

\begin{split}-\int_{t_{m_{1}}}^{t_{N-m_{2}}}U_{\mu}(t)\,2t\rho(t)\,dt=I[\mu]+\int_{0}^{t_{m_{1}}}U_{\mu}(t)\,2t\rho(t)\,dt+\int_{t_{N-m_{2}}}^{\infty}U_{\mu}(t)\,2t\rho(t)\,dt.\end{split}

Note that by Lemma
2.1
and (
2.13
), we have

U_{\mu}^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{1}{t}(1-F(t)),\qquad 2t\rho(t)=(1-F(t))^{\prime}.

Here, by the Taylor series expansion at
t=0
together with (
2.25
), we have

\begin{split}&\quad\int_{0}^{t_{m_{1}}}U_{\mu}(t)\cdot 2t\rho(t)\,dt=\Big[(1-F(t))U_{\mu}(t)\Big]_{t=0}^{t_{m_{1}}}-\int_{0}^{t_{m_{1}}}(1-F(t))U_{\mu}^{\prime}(t)\,dt\\
&=\tau_{c}(m_{1})U_{\mu}(t_{m_{1}})+\int_{0}^{t_{m_{1}}}t\,U_{\mu}^{\prime}(t)U_{\mu}^{\prime}(t)\,dt=\tau_{c}(m_{1})U_{\mu}(t_{m_{1}})+\int_{0}^{t_{m_{1}}}t\Big(U_{\mu}^{\prime\prime}(0)t+O(t^{2})\Big)^{2}\,dt\\
&=\tau_{c}(m_{1})U_{\mu}(t_{m_{1}})+U_{\mu}^{\prime\prime}(0)^{2}\frac{1}{4}t_{m_{1}}^{4}+O(t_{m_{1}}^{5}).\end{split}

Similarly, we have

\begin{split}\int_{t_{N-m_{2}}}^{\infty}U_{\mu}(t)\cdot 2t\rho(t)\,dt&=-\Big[U_{\mu}(t)F(t)\Big]_{t=t_{N-m_{2}}}^{\infty}\\
&\quad+\int_{t_{N-m_{2}}}^{\infty}U_{\mu}^{\prime}(t)F(t)\,dt=\tau_{\alpha}(m_{2})U_{\mu}(t_{N-m_{2}})-\int_{t_{N-m_{2}}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t}(1-F(t))F(t)\,dt.\end{split}

Note that by (
1.13
), we have

\begin{split}F(s^{-1})&=\tilde{\rho}(0)s^{2}+\frac{2}{3}\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}(0)s^{3}+\frac{1}{4}\tilde{\rho}^{\prime\prime}(0)s^{4}+O(s^{5}),\\
U_{\mu}(s^{-1})&=\log s-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\rho}(0)s^{2}-\frac{2}{9}\tilde{\rho}(0)s^{3}-\frac{1}{16}\tilde{\rho}^{\prime\prime}(0)s^{4}+O(s^{5}),\end{split}

as
s\to 0
.
This gives rise to

\begin{split}\int_{t_{N-m_{2}}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t}(1-F(t))F(t)\,dt&=\int_{0}^{t_{N-m_{2}}^{-1}}\frac{1}{s}(1-F(s^{-1}))F(s^{-1})\,ds\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\rho}(0)t_{N-{m_{2}}}^{-2}+\frac{2}{9}\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}(0)t_{N-{m_{2}}}^{-3}-\Big(\tilde{\rho}(0)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\tilde{\rho}^{\prime\prime}(0)\Big)\frac{1}{4}t_{N-{m_{2}}}^{-4}+O(t_{N-{m_{2}}}^{-5}).\end{split}

We also have

\begin{split}\int_{t_{m_{1}}}^{t_{N-m_{2}}}(U_{\mu}(t)+2(1-F(t))\log t)\cdot 2t\rho(t)\,dt=\Big[(1-F(t))^{2}\log t+(1-F(t))U_{\mu}(t)\Big]_{t=t_{m_{1}}}^{t_{N-m_{2}}}.\end{split}

Combining all of the asymptotic expansions, we have

\begin{split}-n\int_{t_{m_{1}}}^{t_{N-m_{2}}}(2U_{\mu}(t)+2(1-F(t))\log t)\cdot 2t\rho(t)\,dt=nI[\mu]+n\mathcal{E}_{1,1}(m_{1})+n\mathcal{E}_{1,2}(m_{2})+O(nt_{m_{1}}^{5}+nt_{N-m_{2}}^{-5}),\end{split}

where

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{1,1}(m_{1})&=2\tau_{c}(m_{1})U_{\mu}(t_{m_{1}})+U_{\mu}^{\prime\prime}(0)^{2}\frac{1}{4}t_{m_{1}}^{4}+\tau_{c}(m_{1})^{2}\log t_{m_{1}},\\
\mathcal{E}_{1,2}(m_{2})&=2\tau_{\alpha}(m_{2})U_{\mu}(t_{N-m_{2}})-U_{\mu}(t_{N-m_{2}})-\tau_{c}(N-m_{2})^{2}\log t_{N-m_{2}}\\
&\quad-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\rho}(0)t_{N-m_{2}}^{-2}-\frac{2}{9}\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}(0)t_{N-m_{2}}^{-3}+\Big(\tilde{\rho}(0)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\tilde{\rho}^{\prime\prime}(0)\Big)\frac{1}{4}t_{N-m_{2}}^{-4}.\end{split}

Furthermore, by straightforward computations, we obtain

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{1,1}(m_{1})&=2\tau_{c}(m_{1})U_{\mu}(0)-\frac{3}{4}\tau_{c}(m_{1})^{2}+\tau_{c}(m_{1})^{2}\log t_{m_{1}}+O(\tau_{c}(m_{1})^{\frac{5}{2}}),\\
\mathcal{E}_{1,2}(m_{2})&=-\tau_{\alpha}(m_{2})^{2}\log t_{N-m_{2}}-\frac{3}{4}\tau_{\alpha}(m_{2})^{2}+O(\tau_{\alpha}(m_{2})^{\frac{5}{2}}),\end{split}

which completes the proof.
∎

Next, we turn to asymptotic behaviours the remaining terms in (
4.3
).

Lemma 4.2
.

As
N\to\infty
, we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{1}{2}\Big(V_{m_{1}}(t_{m_{1}})-V_{N-m_{2}}(t_{N-m_{2}})\Big)\\
&=-U_{\mu}(0)+\frac{1}{2}\tau_{c}(m_{1})-\tau_{c}(m_{1})\log t_{m_{1}}-\frac{1}{2}\tau_{\alpha}(m_{2})-\tau_{\alpha}(m_{2})\log t_{N-m_{2}}+O\big(N^{-\frac{3}{2}}(m_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}+m_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}})\big)\end{split}

and

(4.6)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{12}\Big({\partial}_{s}(V_{s}(t_{s}))\big|_{s=N-m_{2}}-{\partial}_{s}(V_{s}(t_{s}))\big|_{s=m_{1}}\Big)=\frac{1}{6n}\Big(\log t_{m_{1}}-\log t_{N-m_{2}}\Big).\end{split}

Proof.

The first assertion follows from

\begin{split}&\quad\frac{1}{2}(V_{m_{1}}(t_{m_{1}})-V_{N-m_{2}}(t_{N-m_{2}}))\\
&=-U_{\mu}(t_{m_{1}})-\tau_{c}(m_{1})\log t_{m_{1}}+U_{\mu}(t_{N-m_{2}})+\tau_{c}(N-m_{2})\log t_{N-m_{2}}\\
&=-U_{\mu}(0)+\frac{1}{2}\tau_{c}(m_{1})-\tau_{c}(m_{1})\log t_{m_{1}}-\tau_{\alpha}(m_{2})\log t_{N-m_{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\tau_{\alpha}(m_{2})+O(\tau_{c}(m_{1})^{\frac{3}{2}}+\tau_{\alpha}(m_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}),\end{split}

where we use the relation (
2.21
) between
\tau_{c}
and
\tau_{\alpha}
.
Applying the chain rule,

{\partial}_{s}(V_{s}(t_{s}))={\partial}_{s}(-2U_{\mu}(t_{s})-2\tau_{c}(s)\log t_{s})=-V^{\prime}_{s}(t_{s})\frac{dt_{s}}{ds}-\frac{2}{n}\log t_{s}=-\frac{2}{n}\log t_{s},

which yields the second assertion.
∎

Next, we derive the asymptotic behaviour of the second summation in (
4.1
).

Lemma 4.3
.

As
N\to\infty
, we have

(4.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{j=m_{1}}^{N-m_{2}-1}\frac{1}{2}\log\rho(t_{j})&=\frac{1}{2}nE[\mu]-\frac{1}{2}n\tau_{c}(m_{1})\log\rho(0)-\frac{1}{2}n\tau_{\alpha}(m_{2})(-2+\log\tilde{\rho}(0)-4\log t_{N-m_{2}})\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{4}(\log\rho(t_{m_{1}})-\log\rho(t_{N-m_{2}}))+O(m_{1}^{-1}+m_{2}^{-1})+O(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}(m_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}+m_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}})\log N).\end{split}

Proof.

By using the Euler-Maclaurin formula (
2.12
) and (
4.2
), we have

\begin{split}\sum_{j=m_{1}}^{N-m_{2}-1}\frac{1}{2}\log\rho(t_{j})=n\int_{t_{m_{1}}}^{t_{N-m_{2}}}\frac{1}{2}\log\rho(t)\cdot 2t\rho(t)\,dt+\frac{1}{4}(\log\rho(t_{m_{1}})-\log\rho(t_{N-m_{2}}))+O(N^{-1}(t_{m_{1}}^{-2}+t_{N-m_{2}}^{2})).\end{split}

Using the Taylor expansion at
0

2.27
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t_{m_{1}}}\log\rho(t)\cdot 2t\rho(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t_{m_{1}}}\left(\rho(0)\log\rho(0)+O(t)\right)2t\,dt

\displaystyle=\rho(0)\log\rho(0)t_{m_{1}}^{2}+O(t_{m_{1}}^{3})=\log\rho(0)\tau_{c}(m_{1})+O(t_{m_{1}}^{3}).

On the other hand, it follows from (
3.13
) and (
2.28
) that

\displaystyle\int_{t_{N-m_{2}}}^{\infty}\log\rho(t)\cdot 2t\rho(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t_{N-m_{2}}^{-1}}\log(s^{4}\tilde{\rho}(s))\cdot 2s\tilde{\rho}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t_{N-m_{2}}^{-1}}\left(4\log s+\log\tilde{\rho}(0)+O(s)\right)\cdot(2\tilde{\rho}(0)s+O(s^{2}))\,ds

\displaystyle=\tilde{\rho(0)}t_{N-m_{2}}^{-2}(-2+\log\tilde{\rho}(0)-4\log t_{N-m_{2}})+O(t_{N-m_{2}}^{-3}\log t_{N-m_{2}})

\displaystyle=\tau_{\alpha}(m_{2})(-2+\log\tilde{\rho}(0)-4\log t_{N-m_{2}})+O(t_{N-m_{2}}^{-3}\log t_{N-m_{2}}).

Since the error terms satisfy
O(N^{-1}(t_{m_{1}}^{-2}+t_{N-m_{2}}^{2}))=O(N^{-1}(\tau_{c}(m_{1})^{-1}+\tau_{\alpha}(m_{2})^{-1}))

O(t_{m_{1}}^{3})=O(\tau_{c}(m_{1})^{\frac{3}{2}})
and
O(t_{N-m_{2}}^{-3}\log t_{N-m_{2}})=O(\tau_{\alpha}(m_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}\log N)
, the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 4.4

\begin{split}&\quad\sum_{j=m_{1}}^{N-m_{2}-1}\frac{1}{n}\mathfrak{B}_{1}(t_{j})=\int_{t_{m_{1}}}^{t_{N-m_{2}}}\mathfrak{B}_{1}(t)2t\rho(t)\,dt+O(n^{-1}(t_{m_{1}}^{-2}+t_{N-m_{2}}^{2}))\\

&\quad+\Big(\alpha+\frac{1}{4}\Big)\log 2\pi+2\zeta^{\prime}(-1)+O(m_{2}^{-2})+O(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}m_{2}^{\frac{5}{2}}(\log N)^{3}).\end{split}

Proof.

Modifying similar computations used in Lemmas
4.6
and
4.7
, we have

\begin{split}\sum_{j=0}^{m_{1}-1}\log h_{2j+1,2N}&=4m_{1}nU_{\mu}(0)-m_{1}(m_{1}+2c+1)\log(2n\rho(0))+m_{1}(m_{1}+2c)\log 2-\frac{1}{2}m_{1}\log\pi\\
&\quad+\log\Big(\frac{G(m_{1}+c+1)G(m_{1}+c+3/2)}{G(c+1)G(c+3/2)}\Big)+O(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}m_{1}^{\frac{5}{2}}(\log N)^{3}),\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\sum_{j=N-m_{2}}^{N-1}\log{h}_{2j+1,2N}&=-m_{2}(m_{2}+2\alpha+1)\log(2n\tilde{\rho}(0))+m_{2}(m_{2}+2\alpha)\log 2-\frac{1}{2}m_{2}\log\pi\\
&\quad+\log\Big(\frac{G(m_{2}+\alpha+1)G(m_{2}+\alpha+3/2)}{G(\alpha+1)G(\alpha+3/2)}\Big)+O(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}m_{2}^{\frac{5}{2}}(\log N)^{3})\end{split}

as

By using (
1.50
), we have

\begin{split}&\quad\log(G(m_{1}+c+1)G(m_{1}+c+3/2))\\
&=m_{1}^{2}\log m_{1}-\frac{3}{2}m_{1}^{2}+\Big(2c+\frac{1}{2}\Big)m_{1}\log m_{1}+\log(2\pi)m_{1}-\Big(2c+\frac{1}{2}\Big)m_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\Big(2c^{2}+c-\frac{1}{12}\Big)\log m_{1}\\
&\quad+\Big(c+\frac{1}{4}\Big)\log 2\pi+2\zeta^{\prime}(-1)+O(m_{1}^{-2})\end{split}

as

, and similar asymptotic formula holds for

.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

By combining the previous lemmas and straightforward computations, including the cancellations of the terms
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
as in the proof of Theorem
1.3
in the previous section, we obtain

\begin{split}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}h_{2j+1,2N}&=-2n^{2}I[\mu]-\frac{N}{2}\log\frac{n}{\pi}-\frac{n}{2}E[\mu]-n(4c+1)U_{\mu}(0)+\Big(c^{2}+\alpha^{2}+\frac{c}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{1}{12}\Big)\log n\\

as

. Now, the theorem follows from (
2.10
) and (

).

Dimension and topology in transserial tame pairs
Nigel Pynn-Coates

Abstract
. Every maximal Hardy field has a proper elementary differential subfield that is Dedekind complete in the maximal Hardy field. This pair of Hardy fields is a transserial tame pair, shown to have a complete and model complete elementary theory in [ Pyn24 ] . This paper introduces a dimension in transserial tame pairs and shows that it equals the (naive) dimension coming from the order topology. In particular, the dimension is definable in a certain sense, and is the unique definable dimension in a transserial tame pair. Further, transserial tame pairs are locally o-minimal and d-minimal. These properties and the dimension are used to establish topological properties of definable sets in transserial tame pairs, including a definable Baire category theorem.



1.
Introduction

Each semialgebraic subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
has a dimension in
\{-\infty,0,1,\dots,n\}
, which can be defined as the supremum of
m\leqslant n
such that
\pi(S)
has interior in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
for some coordinate projection
\pi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
This “naive topological dimension” generalizes to sets definable in any o-minimal expansion
\mathcal{R}
of
\mathbb{R}
, and can alternatively be defined via cell decomposition
[
KPS86
]
(see also
[
Dri98
, Chapter 4]
)
or as the dimension coming from the definable closure pregeometry
[
Pil86
,
Pil88
]
.
The germs at
+\infty
of functions
\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
definable in
\mathcal{R}
form a
Hardy field
, which is a field of germs at
+\infty
of eventually differentiable functions
\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
that is closed under differentiation.
Hardy fields, whether or not they come from o-minimal structures, are ordered differential fields, so the naive topological dimension makes sense.
By a recent remarkable theorem
[
ADH24
]
, all maximal Hardy fields (i.e., maximal with respect to inclusion) are elementarily equivalent as differential fields and satisfy an effectively axiomatized model complete theory as ordered valued differential fields.
The naive topological dimension
\dim
of sets definable in maximal Hardy fields has good topological and definability properties, and comes from the differential-algebraic closure pregeometry
[
ADH17a
]
.

Every maximal Hardy field
K
can be equipped with a proper elementary differential subfield
L
such that
L
is Dedekind complete in
K
, meaning that if
f\in K
defines a proper nonempty subset
(-\infty,f)\cap L
of
L
, then there is
g\in L
with
(-\infty,f)\cap L=(-\infty,g)\cap L
.
We call this pair
(K,L)
a
transserial tame pair
and study it in
[
Pyn24
]
.
In particular, the theory of transserial tame pairs is complete and model complete in an appropriate language.
This paper focuses on the naive topological dimension of sets definable in transserial tame pairs.
More correctly, we modify the differential-algebraic closure pregeometry in
(K,L)
to define a dimension
\dim_{2}
, along the lines of
[
BV10
,
For11
,
ÁD16
]
for various pairs, and show:

Theorem 1
.

If
S\subseteq K^{n}
is definable in
(K,L)
, then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\dim_{2}S<n
;

(ii)

S
has empty interior in
K^{n}
;

(iii)

S
is nowhere dense in
K^{n}
;

(iv)

S
is definably meagre in
K^{n}
.

In particular, the equivalence of
(i)
and
(ii)
yields the coincidence of
\dim_{2}
with naive topological dimension, and hence
\dim_{2}S=\dim S
for subsets
S\subseteq K^{n}
definable in
K
.
It also shows that
\dim_{2}
is
definable
in a certain sense, and so is a dimension function in the sense of
[
Dri89
]
, leading to Theorem
2
below.
By
[
For11
]
,
\dim_{2}
is the unique (definable) dimension function on transserial tame pairs.

Moreover, Theorem
1
includes a strong definable Baire category theorem for transserial tame pairs, since every definably meagre set is nowhere dense; a definably meagre set is one covered by the union of an increasing definable family of nowhere dense sets.
Every definably complete ordered field satisfies a definable Baire category theorem by
[
Hie13
]
(see also
[
FS10
]
for consequences), but the structures in this paper are not definably complete.
By the above remarks, the definable Baire category theorem also applies to sets definable in maximal Hardy fields, which is new.

As a consequence of Theorem
1
and generalities on pregeometric dimensions from
[
ÁD16
,
For11
]
, we show that
\dim_{2}
is preserved by definable bijections.
In particular, no space-filling function is definable in a transserial tame pair.
More generally:

Theorem 2
.

If
S\subseteq K^{m}
and
f\colon S\to K^{n}
are definable in
(K,L)
, then

(i)

\dim_{2}S\geqslant\dim_{2}f(S)
, so
\dim_{2}S=\dim_{2}f(S)
if
f
is injective;

(ii)

B_{i}\coloneqq\{b\in K^{n}:\dim_{2}f^{-1}(b)=i\}
is definable and
\dim_{2}f^{-1}(B_{i})=i+\dim_{2}B_{i}
for
i=0,\dots,m
.

If
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is semialgebraic (or definable in an o-minimal expansion of
\mathbb{R}
), then
S
has positive dimension if and only if
S
is infinite.
This fails for definable sets in
K
, since
\mathbb{R}
is definable (without parameters) in
K
but
\dim\mathbb{R}=0
.
In
(K,L)
,
L\supseteq\mathbb{R}
also satisfies
\dim_{2}L=0
.
Moreover,
L
is discrete in
K
(the reader should keep in mind that the underlying ordered fields here are highly non-archimedean), and discrete is the right analogue of finite.

Theorem 3
.

If
S\subseteq K^{n}
is definable in
(K,L)
, then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\dim_{2}S\leqslant 0
;

(ii)

S
is discrete in
K^{n}
;

(iii)

S
is coanalyzable relative to
L
.

Coanalyzability in item
(iii)
of Theorem
3
is a model-theoretic notion from
[
HHM03
]
.
More common for differential fields is to consider coanalyzability relative to the field of constants, which for
K
and
L
is
\mathbb{R}
.
Although for
S\subseteq K^{n}
definable in
K
,
\dim S=0
if and only if
S
is coanalyzable relative to
\mathbb{R}

[
ADH17a
, Proposition 6.2]
, this fails for sets definable in
(K,L)
, since
L
itself is not coanalyzable relative to
\mathbb{R}
but satisfies
\dim_{2}L=0
.

The key step in proving Theorem
3
is to establish the equivalences for
n=1
.
To do this, we show that transserial tame pairs are locally o-minimal in the sense of
[
TV09
]
and d-minimal in the sense of
[
For11
]
.
Additionally, we use Theorems
1
,
2
, and
3
to derive further topological consequences for sets definable in transserial tame pairs.
First, any discrete
S\subseteq K^{n}
definable in
(K,L)
is also closed in
K^{n}
.
More difficult is the following.

Theorem 4
.

If
S\subseteq K^{n}
is definable in a transserial tame pair
(K,L)
and
\overline{S}
is its topological closure in
K^{n}
, then
\dim_{2}(\overline{S}\setminus S)<\dim_{2}S
.
Hence,
\dim_{2}S=\dim_{2}\overline{S}
.

At this point we mention connections to other work.
First, parts of the first three theorems have analogues for maximal Hardy fields (and elementarily equivalent differential fields) in
[
ADH17a
]
.
The analogue of Theorem
4
is also stated but not proved there.
This paper is thus a generalization and continuation of those results.
As stated above, transserial tame pairs are not o-minimal, but they are locally o-minimal and d-minimal, which we use in establishing Theorems
3
and
4
.
However, much of the general theory for such structures is developed under the assumption that they are definably complete.
Hence, the results here require different arguments and in particular cannot appeal to dimension theorems for definably complete locally o-minimal structures from
[
For13
,
Fuj23
,
FKK22
]
.
Additionally, transserial tame pairs are not
t-minimal
in the sense of
[
Mat95
]
, so in particular not
visceral
in the sense of
[
DG22
]
(see also
[
Joh24
]
).

The paper
[
FHW17
]
considers expansions of
\mathbb{R}
that are not o-minimal but satisfy a weaker tameness condition.
Among their results are
[
FHW17
, Theorem D]
and the consequent strong definable Baire category theorem, which are analogous to Theorem
1
but only apply to so-called
D_{\Sigma}
sets (a certain definable analogue of
F_{\sigma}
sets).
Also,
[
FHW17
, Theorem E]
is analogous to Theorem
2
and the last part of Theorem
4
.
It is stated for small inductive dimension, which they show agrees with naive topological dimension on
D_{\Sigma}
sets, which suggests that it could be interesting to explore connections between the work here and other topological dimensions of sets definable in transserial tame pairs.

Although the introduction has focused on transserial tame pairs, throughout the paper, we actually work in the more general setting of pre-
H
-fields that are differential-henselian and Liouville closed, equipped with a lift of their differential residue field.
In that sense, this is a continuation of
[
Pyn20
,
Pyn25
,
Pyn24
]
and indeed relies on the results of those papers.
We also consider the case of existentially closed pre-
H
-fields with gap
0
, with and without such a lift, and the results proved here in that case were announced in
[
Pyn25a
]
.

2.
Preliminaries and notation

We let
\ell
,
m
, and
n
range over
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
.

2.1.
Valued differential fields and differential-henselianity

Let
K
be a differential field (which here always has characteristic
0
), equipped with the derivation
\der\colon K\to K
.
The
constant field
of
K
is
C\coloneqq\{f\in K:\der(f)=0\}
; to indicate the dependence on
K
, we write
C_{K}
.
For
f\in K
, we often write
f^{\prime}
for
\der(f)
if the derivation is clear from context and set
f^{\dagger}\coloneqq f^{\prime}/f
if
f\neq 0
, the logarithmic derivative of
f
.
We say that
K
is
closed under integration
if
\der K=K

and
closed under exponential integration
if
(K^{\times})^{\dagger}=K
.
We let
K\{Y\}\coloneqq K[Y,Y^{\prime},Y^{\prime\prime},\dots]
be the ring of differential polynomials over
K
and set
K\{Y\}^{\neq}\coloneqq K\{Y\}\setminus\{0\}
.
For
P\in K\{Y\}^{\neq}
, the
order
of
P
is the smallest
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
P\in K[Y,Y^{\prime},\dots,Y^{(m)}]
and
P_{n}
is the homogeneous part of
P
of degree
n
, which we will only need for
n=0
and
n=1
; degree for differential polynomials means total degree.
We call
K

linearly surjective
if for all
a_{0},\dots,a_{n}\in K
with
a_{n}\neq 0
, the equation
1+a_{0}y+a_{1}y^{\prime}+\dots+a_{n}y^{(n)}=0
has a solution in
K
.
If
L
is a differential field extension of
K
and
a\in L
, then
K\langle a\rangle
denotes the differential subfield of
L
generated by
a
over
K
.
Throughout,
\operatorname{d}
-algebraic
and
\operatorname{d}
-transcendental
abbreviate “differentially algebraic” and “differentially transcendental”.

Now let
(K,\mathcal{O})
be a
valued differential field
in the sense of
[
ADH17
, Section 4.4]
, which simply means that
K
is a differential field and
\mathcal{O}\supseteq\mathbb{Q}
is a valuation ring of
K
, i.e.,
\mathcal{O}
is a subring of
K
that contains
a
or
a^{-1}
for every
a\in K^{\times}
.
In this paper, there will often be two valuation rings on a field equipped with a single derivation, so in the notation, we specify
\mathcal{O}
but leave
\der
implicit.
With
\mathcal{O}^{\times}=\{a\in K^{\times}:a,a^{-1}\in\mathcal{O}\}
, the ring
\mathcal{O}
has a unique maximal ideal
\cao\coloneqq\mathcal{O}\setminus\mathcal{O}^{\times}
.
We introduce the following binary relations, for
f,g\in K
:

\displaystyle f\preccurlyeq g\

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\ f\in\mathcal{O}g,

\displaystyle f\asymp g\

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\ f\preccurlyeq g\ \text{and}\ g\preccurlyeq f,

\displaystyle f\prec g\

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\ f\in\cao g\ \text{and}\ g\neq 0,

\displaystyle f\sim g\

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\ f-g\prec g.

The relation
\asymp
is an equivalence relation on
K
, the relation
\sim
is an equivalence relation on
K^{\times}
, and they satisfy: if
f\sim g
, then
f\asymp g
.
When there is a second valuation ring of
K
denoted by
\dot{\mathcal{O}}
, we distinguish the above as
\dot{\cao}
,
\mathrel{\dot{\preccurlyeq}}
, etc.
The
residue field
of
(K,\mathcal{O})
is
\res(K,\mathcal{O})\coloneqq\mathcal{O}/\cao
.
By our assumption that
\mathbb{Q}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
, the characteristics of
K
and
\res(K,\mathcal{O})
are
0
.

To define differential-henselianity below, we extend the relations displayed above to
K\{Y\}
, for which it is convenient to work with valuations instead of valuation rings.
It is well-known that to
(K,\mathcal{O})
is associated a surjective
valuation

v\colon K^{\times}\to\Gamma
, where
\Gamma\coloneqq K^{\times}/\mathcal{O}^{\times}
is an ordered abelian group called the
value group
of
K
(and conversely, from such a valuation one gets a valuation ring of
K
).
Adding a new symbol
\infty
to the value group
\Gamma
and extending the addition and ordering to
\Gamma_{\infty}\coloneqq\Gamma\cup\{\infty\}
by
\infty+\gamma=\gamma+\infty=\infty
and
\infty>\gamma
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
allows us to extend
v
to
K
by setting
v(0)\coloneqq\infty
.
Then for all
f,g\in K
,

f\preccurlyeq g\ \Leftrightarrow\ vf\geqslant vg\ \qquad\ \text{and}\ \qquad\ f\prec g\ \Leftrightarrow\ vf>vg.

Thus setting
v(P)
to be the minimum valuation of the coefficients of
P\in K\{Y\}
, we can extend the relations
\preccurlyeq
,
\prec
,
\asymp
, and
\sim
to
K\{Y\}
.

One basic condition relating the valuation and the derivation is called
small derivation
, which means for
(K,\mathcal{O})
that
\der\cao\subseteq\cao
.
In this case,
\der\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{O}

[
ADH17
, Lemma 4.4.2]
, so
\der
induces a derivation on
\res(K,\mathcal{O})
, and we always construe
\res(K,\mathcal{O})
as a differential field with this induced derivation.
(Small derivation is a strong form of continuity of the derivation with respect to the valuation topology, for which see
[
ADH17
, Lemma 4.4.7]
.)
An analogue of henselianity of a valued field for a valued differential field with small derivation is differential-henselianity.

Definition 2.1
.

We call
(K,\mathcal{O})

differential-henselian
(
\operatorname{d}
-henselian
for short) if
(K,\mathcal{O})
has small derivation and

(DH1)

the differential field
\res(K,\mathcal{O})
is linearly surjective;

(DH2)

if
P\in K\{Y\}
satisfies
P_{0}\prec 1
and
P\asymp P_{1}\asymp 1
, there is
y\prec 1
in
K
with
P(y)=0
.

Note that
P_{0}=P(0)
and
P_{1}\asymp 1
means
\frac{\partial P}{\partial Y^{(n)}}(0)\asymp 1
for some
n
at most the order of
P
.
Differential-henselianity was introduced in a slightly different form
[
Sca00
]
and developed systematically in greater generality in
[
ADH17
, Chapter 7]
.
We call
(K,\mathcal{O})

differential-Hensel-Liouville closed
(shorter:
\operatorname{d}
-Hensel-Liouville closed
) if
(K,\mathcal{O})
is
\operatorname{d}
-henselian and
K
is
Liouville closed
in the sense that it is real closed, closed under integration, and closed under exponential integration.
(If
(K,\mathcal{O})
is
\operatorname{d}
-henselian, then closure under integration comes for free by
[
ADH17
, Lemma 7.1.8]
.)

Suppose that
(K,\mathcal{O})
has small derivation, so
\res(K,\mathcal{O})
is a differential field.
A
lift
of
\res(K,\mathcal{O})
is a differential subfield
\bm{k}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
, in the sense that
\bm{k}
is a differential subring of
\mathcal{O}
that is itself a field, that maps isomorphically (as a differential field) onto
\res(K,\mathcal{O})
under the residue map; equivalently, for every
a\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}
there is a (necessarily unique)
u\in\bm{k}^{\times}
with
a\sim u
(i.e.,
a-u\in\cao
).
If
(K,\mathcal{O})
is
\operatorname{d}
-henselian, it can be equipped with a lift of its differential residue field
[
ADH17
, Proposition 7.1.3]
,
More precisely, by the proof of that proposition:

Fact 2.2

(
[
ADH17
, Proposition 7.1.3]
)
.

If
(K,\mathcal{O})
is
\operatorname{d}
-henselian, then any differential subfield of
\mathcal{O}
can be extended to a lift of the differential residue field
\res(K,\mathcal{O})
.

These lifts play an important role in this paper.
Since it has not been recorded elsewhere, we note that arguing as in
[
AD05
, Section 7]
but replacing
C
with a lift
\bm{k}
of
\res(K,\mathcal{O})
, no
\operatorname{d}
-Hensel-Liouville closed pre-
H
-field
(K,\mathcal{O})
is spherically complete; this is not used here.

2.2.
Ordered differential fields and (pre-)
H
-fields

Let
K
be an ordered differential field, in the sense that
K
is a differential field additionally equipped with an ordering
\leqslant
making it an ordered field (i.e., the ordering is compatible with addition and multiplication in the usual way).
Then
K
can always be equipped with its so-called natural valuation ring
\{a\in K:c_{1}\leqslant a\leqslant c_{2}\ \text{for some}\ c_{1},c_{2}\in C\}
, which henceforth we denote by
\mathcal{O}
, while another valuation ring of
K
will be denoted by
\dot{\mathcal{O}}
, with associated relations
\mathrel{\dot{\preccurlyeq}}
,
\mathrel{\dot{\prec}}
,
\mathrel{\dot{\asymp}}
, and
\mathrel{\dot{\sim}}
.
For another ordered differential field
L
we write
\mathcal{O}_{L}
and
\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
.

The valuation ring
\mathcal{O}
is always existentially definable in the ordered differential field
K
without parameters.
Certain model-theoretic statements are therefore insensitive to the distinction between
K
and
(K,\mathcal{O})
: For example,
K\preccurlyeq L
if and only if
(K,\mathcal{O})\preccurlyeq(L,\mathcal{O}_{L})
.
On the other hand, it may be that
L
is a differential field extension of
K
but
(L,\mathcal{O}_{L})
is not a valued differential field extension of
(K,\mathcal{O})
, so caution will be taken to specify valuation rings when necessary.

Typically, we expect nice interaction between the ordering and the derivation.
For instance, we call
K
an
H
-field
if:

(H1)

for all
a\in K
, if
a>C
, then
a^{\prime}>0
;

(H2)

\mathcal{O}=C+\cao
.

The second condition says that
C
is a lift of
\res(K,\mathcal{O})
as a field.
For example, every Hardy field containing
\mathbb{R}
, in particular, every maximal Hardy field, is an
H
-field, and indeed
H
-fields were introduced in
[
AD02
]
towards axiomatizing Hardy fields and similar differential fields.
As described above, when convenient we construe an
H
-field
K
as an ordered valued differential field
(K,\mathcal{O})
.
A related notion is the following.
We call
(K,\dot{\mathcal{O}})
a
pre-
H
-field
if:

(PH1)

\dot{\mathcal{O}}
is convex (with respect to
\leqslant
);

(PH2)

for all
f\in K
, if
f>\dot{\mathcal{O}}
, then
f^{\prime}>0
;

(PH3)

for all
f,g\in K^{\times}
with
f\mathrel{\dot{\preccurlyeq}}1
and
g\mathrel{\dot{\prec}}1
, we have
f^{\prime}\mathrel{\dot{\prec}}g^{\dagger}
.

In this definition,
\dot{\mathcal{O}}
may be any valuation ring of
K
with associated relations
\mathrel{\dot{\preccurlyeq}}
and
\mathrel{\dot{\prec}}
, but
(PH2)
forces
C\subseteq\dot{\mathcal{O}}
, so
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\dot{\mathcal{O}}
by
(PH1)
.
Recall that
(PH1)
holds if and only if
\dot{\cao}
is convex, so if
(PH1)
holds, then
\leqslant
induces an ordering on
\res(K,\dot{\mathcal{O}})
making it an ordered field.
We thus construe the residue field of a pre-
H
-field with small derivation as an ordered differential field.
If
(K,\dot{\mathcal{O}})
is a pre-
H
-field, we construe a differential subfield
E
of
K
as a pre-
H
-subfield
(E,\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{E})
of
(K,\dot{\mathcal{O}})
with
\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{E}=\dot{\mathcal{O}}\cap E
.
Construing an
H
-field
K
as an ordered valued differential field
(K,\mathcal{O})
, it is a pre-
H
-field, where
(PH3)
holds by
[
ADH17
, Lemma 10.5.1]
.
Pre-
H
-fields are so named because every ordered valued differential subfield of an
H
-field is a pre-
H
-field and moreover every pre-
H
-field can be extended to an
H
-field by
[
AD02
, Corollary 4.6]
(also see
[
ADH17
, Corollary 10.5.13]
).

In a pre-
H
-field
(K,\dot{\mathcal{O}})
, logarithmic differentiation induces a map on
\dot{\Gamma}
, since for
g\in K^{\times}
with
g\not\mathrel{\dot{\asymp}}1
,
\dot{v}(g^{\dagger})
depends only on
\dot{v}g
and not on
g

5.1.

As shown earlier,

To recover a suitable notion of dimension in

Lemma

summarizes some basic properties of

Let

Since

is the unique existential matroid on it

Definition 5.1

We call

Towards showing that

Lemma 5.2

Let

(ii)

For

Proposition 5.14

Let

Proof.

The interior of

The second statement follows by Proposition

Suppose that

With more careful analysis using the details of the proof of Proposition

We can assume that
S
is defined by
\psi_{0}(y)\wedge\bigwedge_{i=1}^{s}\neg\psi_{i}(y)

4.6

Let

\{0,\dots,s\}

We can arrange that

is
\operatorname{r}
-relative, and each

Theorem 5.21
.

Let
S\subseteq K^{n}
be definable in
(K,\bm{k})
.
Then
\dim_{2}(\overline{S}\setminus S)<\dim_{2}S
.

Proof.

First, observe that
\dim_{2}(\overline{S}\setminus S)<n
, since
\overline{S}\setminus S
has empty interior in
K^{n}
.
This handles the case
\dim_{2}S=n
. Also, note that if
\dim_{2}S=0
, then

is closed and discrete by Theorem
5.16
and Corollary
5.18
.
In particular,
\dim_{2}(\overline{S}\setminus S)=\dim_{2}\emptyset=-\infty
.
Now suppose that
1\leqslant\dim_{2}(\overline{S}\setminus S)=\dim_{2}S=m<n
.
Take a coordinate projection
\pi\colon K^{n}\to K^{m}
such that
\dim_{2}\pi(\overline{S}\setminus S)=m
; by permuting coordinates, arrange that
\pi
is projection onto the first
m
coordinates.
Note that
\dim_{2}\pi(S)=m
too, since

m\ =\ \dim_{2}\pi(\overline{S}\setminus S)\ \leqslant\ \dim_{2}\pi(\overline{S})\ \leqslant\ \dim_{2}\overline{\pi(S)}\ =\ \dim_{2}\pi(S).

Take a nonempty open box
U\subseteq\pi(\overline{S}\setminus S)
.
Since
\dim_{2}(\overline{\pi(S)}\setminus\pi(S))<m
, the set
\overline{\pi(S)}\setminus\pi(S)
is nowhere dense, so shrinking
U
we arrange that
U\subseteq\pi(\overline{S}\setminus S)\cap\pi(S)
.
We have

\dim_{2}\{a\in U:\dim_{2}S_{a}=0\}\ =\ m\ =\ \dim_{2}\{a\in U:\dim_{2}(\overline{S}\setminus S)_{a}=0\},

so by further shrinking
U
we arrange that for all
a\in U
,

\dim_{2}S_{a}\ =\ 0\ =\ \dim_{2}(\overline{S}\setminus S)_{a}.

Now let
a
range over
K^{m}
,
b
over
K^{n-m}
, and
\varepsilon
over
K^{>}
, and define the set of “
\varepsilon
-bad points”

B_{\varepsilon}\ \coloneqq\ \{(a,b)\in(\overline{S}\setminus S)\cap\pi^{-1}(U):\text{for all}\ (a,\tilde{b})\in S,\ |\tilde{b}-b|\geqslant\varepsilon\},

where
|\tilde{b}-b|=\max\{|\tilde{b}_{i}-b_{i}|:1\leqslant i\leqslant n-m\}
.
We claim that
\dim_{2}B_{\varepsilon}<m
.
Suppose to the contrary that
\dim_{2}B_{\varepsilon}=m
.
Then
\dim_{2}\pi(B_{\varepsilon})=m
, since for
a\in U
, we have
(B_{\varepsilon})_{a}\subseteq(\overline{S}\setminus S)_{a}
and
\dim_{2}(\overline{S}\setminus S)_{a}=0
.
Since
\dim_{2}\pi(B_{\varepsilon})=m
,
[
For11
, Corollary 9.19]
yields a point
(a,b)\in B_{\varepsilon}
and nonempty open box
U_{1}\subseteq U
such that
V\coloneqq U_{1}\times(b-\varepsilon/2,b+\varepsilon/2)
satisfies
\pi(V\cap B_{\varepsilon})=U_{1}
.
By shrinking
U_{1}
, we arrange that
|(\tilde{a},\tilde{b})-(a,b)|<\varepsilon/2
for all
(\tilde{a},\tilde{b})\in V
.
Now use that
(a,b)\in\overline{S}\setminus S
to find
(\tilde{a},\tilde{b})\in S\cap V
, so
|(\tilde{a},\tilde{b})-(a,b)|<\varepsilon/2
.
But then
\tilde{a}\in U_{1}=\pi(V\cap B_{\varepsilon})
, so we have

with

.
We have finally reached a contradiction, since

|d-\tilde{b}|\ \leqslant\ |d-b|+|\tilde{b}-b|\ <\ \varepsilon/2+\varepsilon/2\ =\ \varepsilon.

in

Hence by

\dim_{2}\bigcup_{\varepsilon\in K^{>}}B_{\varepsilon}<m

On the other hand, using that

is closed for

, contradicting that
\dim_{2}((\overline{S}\setminus S)\cap\pi^{-1}(U))=m
.

Lemma 5.25
.

Let
f_{1}\colon S_{1}\to K^{m}
and
f_{2}\colon S_{2}\to K^{m}
be definable injections, where
m\geqslant 1
and
S_{1},S_{2}\subseteq K^{n}
.
Then there is a definable injection
g\colon S_{1}\cup S_{2}\to K^{m}
.

Proof.

Defining
h_{1}\colon K^{m}\to K^{m}
by

h_{1}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\ \coloneqq\ \begin{cases}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m-1},a_{m}+2)&\text{if}\ a_{m}\geqslant 0,\\
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m-1},a_{m}-2)&\text{if}\ a_{m}<0,\end{cases}

and
h_{2}\colon K^{m}\to K^{m}
by

h_{2}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\ \coloneqq\ \begin{cases}\big(a_{1},\dots,a_{m-1},\frac{1}{1+a_{m}^{2}}\big)&\text{if}\ a_{m}\geqslant 0,\\
\big(a_{1},\dots,a_{m-1},-\frac{1}{1+a_{m}^{2}}\big)&\text{if}\ a_{m}<0,\end{cases}

yields a definable injection
g\colon S_{1}\cup S_{2}\to K^{m}
defined by

g(s)\ \coloneqq\ \begin{cases}h_{1}(f_{1}(s))&\text{if}\ s\in S_{1},\\
h_{2}(f_{2}(s))&\text{if}\ s\in S_{2}\setminus S_{1}.\end{cases}\qed

Theorem 6.11
.

Let
S\subseteq K^{n}
be definable in
(K,\bm{k})
and nonempty. Then

S\ \text{is coanalyzable relative to}\ \bm{k}\ \iff\ \dim_{2}S=0.

Proof.

The left-to-right direction follows from
[
ÁD16
, Corollary 2.9]
and Corollary
5.9
.
For the converse, assume that
\dim_{2}S=0
.
For each
i=1,\dots,n
, we have
\dim_{2}\pi_{i}(S)=0
, so
\pi_{i}(S)
is lean.
Hence each
\pi_{i}(S)
is coanalyzable relative to
\bm{k}
by Lemma
6.10
, and thus so is
S\subseteq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{i}(S)
.
∎

Every

definable in

that is

must be coanalyzable relative to
\bm{k}
.
We have not investigated whether the converse holds, as for tame pairs of real closed fields; see

In

but not internal to

.

To conclude, we use Theorem

does not eliminate imaginaries. (See

Corollary 6.12
.

There does not exist a definable
f\colon K^{\times}\to K^{n}
such that for all
a,b\in K^{\times}
,

a\mathrel{\dot{\asymp}}b\ \iff\ f(a)=f(b).

Proof.

Suppose towards a contradiction that there exists such an
f
.
By Lemma
5.2
,
[
Pyn25
, Theorem 6.16]
, and model completeness of transserial tame pairs, we arrange that
|\dot{\Gamma}|>|\bm{k}|
.
Then
\dim_{2}f(K^{\times})>0
, since otherwise

would be coanalyzable relative to
\bm{k}
and so would satisfy
|f(K^{\times})|\leqslant|\bm{k}|

, the set

has nonempty interior in

, and so
\dim_{2}f^{-1}(a)=1
.
It follows that
\dim_{2}K^{\times}\geqslant 2
, which is absurd.
∎

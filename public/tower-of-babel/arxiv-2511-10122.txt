Abstract
. We extend the polydisk theorem of [ 21 ] , originally established for classical Cartan-Hartogs domains, to Hartogs domains over arbitrary (possibly reducible and exceptional) bounded symmetric domains. We further establish a dual counterpart of this result. As an application, we show that the dual of a Hartogs domain over a bounded symmetric domain admits no totally geodesic immersion into any compact Riemannian manifold, thereby broadening the rigidity phenomena obtained in [ 13 ] .

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded symmetric domain endowed with its hyperbolic metric
g_{\Omega}
, whose associated Kähler form is

\omega_{\Omega}\;=\;-\tfrac{i}{2}\,\partial\bar{\partial}\log N_{\Omega}(z,\bar{z}),

where
N_{\Omega}(z,\bar{z})
denotes the generic norm of the Hermitian positive Jordan triple system associated to
\Omega
(see below).
The
Hartogs domain over
\Omega
is defined by

M_{\Omega,\mu}=\bigl\{(z_{0},z)\in\mathbb{C}\times\Omega\;\big|\;|z_{0}|^{2}<N_{\Omega}^{\mu}(z,\bar{z})\bigr\}.

This is also known as a
generalized Cartan–Hartogs domain
.
A Cartan–Hartogs domain is a Hartogs domain whose base
\Omega
is irreducible (i.e., a Cartan domain).
It is known that
M_{\Omega,\mu}
is homogeneous if and only if it is the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
,
equivalently when
\Omega=\mathbb{B}^{n}
and
\mu=1
.

On
M_{\Omega,\mu}
one considers the Kähler metric
g_{\Omega,\mu}
whose associated Kähler form is given by:

(1)

\omega_{\Omega,\mu}=-\frac{i}{2}\,\partial\bar{\partial}\log\ \!\Bigl(N_{\Omega}^{\mu}(z,\bar{z})-|z_{0}|^{2}\Bigr).

The
dual Hartogs domain over
\Omega
is
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
equipped with the
dual Kähler metric
whose associated Kähler form is:

(2)

\omega^{*}_{\Omega,\mu}=\frac{i}{2}\,\partial\bar{\partial}\log\ \!\Bigl(N_{\Omega}^{\mu}(z,-\bar{z})+|z_{0}|^{2}\Bigr),

and the dual bounded symmetric domain is
\mathbb{C}^{n}

with the dual Kähler metric
g_{\Omega}^{*}
whose associated
Kähler form is

\omega^{*}_{\Omega}\;=\;\tfrac{i}{2}\,\partial\bar{\partial}\log N_{\Omega}(z,-\bar{z}).

Hartogs domains over bounded symmetric domains, equipped with the Kähler metric (
1
) and its dual (
2
), form a tractable yet genuinely nonhomogeneous class of Kähler manifolds;
In this framework,
balanced metrics
(in Donaldson’s sense
[
6
]
) are completely understood: on Cartan domains they are fully classified, and among Cartan–Hartogs domains the complex hyperbolic space is the only one admitting a balanced metric.
Beyond this, the family offers a convenient testing ground for explicit Kähler–Einstein geometry: existence and closed formulas for the Kähler–Einstein metric are available
[
27
,
3
]
, moreover the Kähler–Einstein and Bergman metrics are equivalent
[
30
,
33
]
, and canonical metrics on Cartan–Hartogs domains have been analyzed in
[
31
]
.
Finally, holomorphic isometric immersions into the infinite-dimensional complex projective space
\mathbb{C}P^{\infty}
are constructed, yielding the first examples of nonhomogeneous and complete Kähler–Einstein
submanifolds of
\mathbb{C}P^{\infty}
[
15
,
7
,
17
]
.

We now recall two further definitions needed for our main results: the
Hartogs-Polydisk
and its dual. First, the
polydisk of dimension
r
is

\Delta^{r}=\bigl\{\,z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{r})\in\mathbb{C}^{r}\;\big|\;|z_{j}|^{2}<1,\;j=1,\dots,r\bigr\},

whose generic norm is

N_{\Delta^{r}}(z,\bar{z})=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\bigl(1-|z_{j}|^{2}\bigr).

Hence the Kähler metric is the product of hyperbolic metrics and its associated Kähler form is

\omega_{\Delta^{r}}=-\frac{i}{2}\,\partial\bar{\partial}\log\!\prod_{j=1}^{r}(1-|z_{j}|^{2}).

In particular,
(\Delta^{r},\omega_{\Delta^{r}})
is homorphically isometric to
(\mathbb{C}H^{1},\omega_{\mathbb{C}H^{1}})^{r}
.
Setting
\Omega=\Delta^{r}
in the above definitions yields the
Hartogs–Polydisk
:

M_{\Delta^{r},\mu}=\bigl\{(z_{0},z)\in\mathbb{C}\times\Delta^{r}\;\big|\;|z_{0}|^{2}<\prod_{j=1}^{r}(1-|z_{j}|^{2})^{\mu}\bigr\},

with associated Kähler form

\omega_{\Delta^{r},\mu}=-\frac{i}{2}\,\partial\bar{\partial}\log\!\Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^{r}(1-|z_{j}|^{2})^{\mu}-|z_{0}|^{2}\Bigr),

Finally, the
dual Hartogs–Polydisk
is
\mathbb{C}^{r+1}
endowed with the Kähler metric whose associated Kähler form is

\omega^{*}_{\Delta^{r},\mu}=\frac{i}{2}\,\partial\bar{\partial}\log\!\Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^{r}(1+|z_{j}|^{2})^{\mu}+|z_{0}|^{2}\Bigr),

We present now the two main theorems established in this paper, which provide essential structural results about totally geodesic embeddings of polydisks into Hartogs domains over bounded symmetric domains and their duals.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded symmetric domain of rank
r
.
Then for every point
p\in M_{\Omega,\mu}
and every vector
X\in T_{p}M_{\Omega,\mu}
,
there exist totally geodesic holomorphic embeddings

j\colon\Delta^{r}\hookrightarrow\Omega,\ \widetilde{j}\colon M_{\Delta^{r},\mu}\longrightarrow M_{\Omega,\mu}

such that
\widetilde{j}
is an extension of
j
,

\widetilde{j}(q)=p
and

(d\widetilde{j})_{q}(V)=X
for some
q\in M_{\Delta^{r},\mu}
and
V\in T_{q}M_{\Delta^{r},\mu}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded symmetric domain of rank
r
.
For every
X\in T_{(z_{0},0)}\mathbb{C}^{\,n+1}
, there exists a totally geodesic holomorphic embedding

j:\bigl(\mathbb{C}^{r},\,g_{\Delta^{r}}^{*}\bigr)\hookrightarrow\bigl(\mathbb{C}^{n},\,g_{\Omega}^{*}\bigr)

with
j(0)=0
such that the induced holomorphic embedding

\widetilde{j}\colon\mathbb{C}^{r+1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n+1},\qquad\widetilde{j}(z_{0},z)=(z_{0},j(z)),

is totally geodesic and

(d\widetilde{j})_{(z_{0},0)}(V_{0})=X,

for some
V_{0}\in T_{(z_{0},0)}\mathbb{C}^{\,r+1}
.

Theorem
1.1
extends the main result of
[
21
]
, originally proved for classical Cartan-Hartogs domains, to Hartogs domains over arbitrary bounded symmetric domains. Our result should be viewed as a counterpart of the standard polydisk theorem for Hermitian symmetric spaces of noncompact types (see
[
29
, Part 1]
). Dually, Theorem
1.2
provides the affine-chart version of the polydisk theorem for Hermitian symmetric spaces of compact type (see
[
8
]
).
The polydisk theorem has numerous applications including rigidity phenomena
[
18
,
19
]
and it is fundamental for the study of the symplectic geometry of Hermitian symmetric spaces of noncompact type
and their duals
[
5
,
12
]
.
Moreover, it has proved useful in the study of the diastatic exponential
[
11
]
and in the analysis of the volume and diastatic entropy of symmetric bounded domains
[
20
]
. We therefore expect our results to serve as an effective tool for addressing geometric problems on Hartogs domains and on their duals.

For example, as a main consequence of our analysis, we obtain the following obstruction.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},g_{{\Omega,\mu}}^{*})
be a dual Hartogs domain. If
\mu\neq 1
or
\operatorname{rank}(\Omega)\geq 2
, then there does not exist a totally geodesic isometric immersion

f:\big(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},g_{{\Omega,\mu}}^{*}\big)\hookrightarrow(M,g)

into any compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with
\dim M\geq n+1
.

This theorem significantly sharpens the obstruction proved in
[
13
]
:
apart from the homogeneous ball case
\Omega=\mathbb{B}^{n}
and
\mu=1
,
there is no compact Kähler manifold
(N,h)
of complex dimension
n+1
containing

(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},g_{{\Omega,\mu}}^{*})
as an open dense subset.

Remark 1
.

The same techniques developed in this paper show that Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
remain valid for the Bergman metric
g_{M_{\Omega,\mu}}
and for the Kähler metric
\hat{g}_{\Omega,\mu}
on
M_{\Omega,\mu}
(introduced in
[
13
]
), together with their duals, namely
g_{M_{\Omega,\mu}}^{*}
and
\hat{g}^{*}_{\Omega,\mu}
(see
[
14
]
). By contrast, whether Theorem
1.3
holds for the Bergman metric is still open; see
[
14
, Sect. 4]
for a discussion.

2.
Hermitian Symmetric Spaces and Polydisk Embeddings

In this section, we begin by recalling the classification of Hermitian symmetric spaces of noncompact type (HSSNCT) in their realization as bounded symmetric domains. We then undertake an explicit investigation of the Hermitian positive Jordan triple–system (HPJTS) structures associated with the exceptional Cartan domains, and we present concrete, totally geodesic embeddings of maximal–rank polydisks, thereby extending the results previously obtained for the classical Cartan domains in
[
21
]
. These constructions will play a central role in our subsequent analysis of the geometric and curvature properties of the Hartogs domains over bounded symmetric domains.

Let
\Omega
be a Hermitian symmetric space of noncompact type (HSSNCT) in its realization as bounded symmetric domain
(\Omega,cg_{B})
, where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a convex, circular domain, that is,
z\in\Omega,\theta\in\mathbb{R}\Rightarrow e^{i\theta}z\in\Omega
(see
[
9
]
for details) and
g_{B}
denotes its Bergman metric. Every bounded symmetric domain can be decomposed as a product of irreducible factors, called
Cartan domains
. According to É. Cartan’s classification, Cartan domains fall into two categories: classical and exceptional (see
[
10
]
for details).

2.1.
Cartan domains

Classical domains can be described in terms of complex matrices as follows:

\displaystyle\Omega_{I}[n,m]=\left\{Z\in M_{n,m}(\mathbb{C})\mid I_{n}-ZZ^{*}>0,\,n\leq m\right\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{II}[n]=\left\{Z\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\mid Z=-Z^{T},\,I_{n}-ZZ^{*}>0,\,n\geq 5\right\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{III}[n]=\left\{Z\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})\mid Z=Z^{T},\,I_{n}-ZZ^{*}>0,\,n\geq 2\right\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{IV}[n]=\left\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\,\middle|\,\sum_{j=1}^{n}|z_{j}|^{2}<1,\ 1+\left|\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}^{2}\right|^{2}-2\sum_{j=1}^{n}|z_{j}|^{2}>0,\ n\geq 5\right\},

where
A>0
means that
A
is positive definite.
The associated Bergman kernel functions are given by:

\displaystyle\mathrm{K}_{\Omega_{I}}(z,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{V(\Omega_{I})}\left[\det(I_{n}-ZZ^{*})\right]^{-(n+m)},

\displaystyle\mathrm{K}_{\Omega_{II}}(z,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{V(\Omega_{II})}\left[\det(I_{n}-ZZ^{*})\right]^{-(n-1)},

\displaystyle\mathrm{K}_{\Omega_{III}}(z,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{V(\Omega_{III})}\left[\det(I_{n}-ZZ^{*})\right]^{-(n+1)},

\displaystyle\mathrm{K}_{\Omega_{IV}}(z,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{V(\Omega_{IV})}\left(1+\left|\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}^{2}\right|^{2}-2\sum_{j=1}^{n}|z_{j}|^{2}\right)^{-n},

where
V(\Omega_{j})
,
j=I,\ldots,IV
, denotes the total volume of
\Omega_{j}
with respect to the Euclidean measure of the ambient complex Euclidean space (see
[
4
]
for details). We refer the reader to
[
28
]
for a more complete description of these domains.

2.2.
Hermitian positive Jordan triple system of type VI

Let
\mathbb{O}
be the real algebra of octonions, and let
\mathbb{O}_{\mathbb{C}}:=\mathbb{O}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
denote its complexification (see
[
2
, §3]
). Every element
Z\in\mathbb{O}_{\mathbb{C}}
can be written as

(3)

Z=z_{0}+\mathbf{z}=z_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{7}z_{j}e_{j},\qquad z_{0},z_{j}\in\mathbb{C},

with respect to the standard Cayley basis
\{1,e_{1},\dots,e_{7}\}
. For
W=w_{0}+\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{O}_{\mathbb{C}}
we set

\widetilde{Z}:=z_{0}-\mathbf{z}\quad\text{(octonionic/Cayley conjugation)},\qquad\overline{Z}:=\overline{z_{0}}+\overline{\mathbf{z}}\quad\text{(complex conjugation)},

and define the (complex-bilinear) octonionic product and bilinear form by

ZW=\bigl(z_{0}w_{0}-\langle\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w}\rangle,\;\;z_{0}\mathbf{w}+w_{0}\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{z}\times\mathbf{w}\bigr),\qquad\langle Z,W\rangle=z_{0}w_{0}+\langle\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w}\rangle,

where
\langle\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w}\rangle:=\sum_{j=1}^{7}z_{j}w_{j}
and
\times
denotes the complex-bilinear extension of the Cayley cross product on
\mathbb{C}^{7}
.

Consider the complex vector space

(4)

\mathcal{H}=\bigl\{(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},Z_{1},Z_{2},Z_{3})\mid z_{j}\in\mathbb{C},\;Z_{j}\in\mathbb{O}_{\mathbb{C}},\;j=1,2,3\bigr\}\cong\mathbb{C}^{27}.

We endow
\mathcal{H}
with the Hermitian form

(x\mid y)=\sum_{j=1}^{3}z_{j}\,\overline{w}_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\langle Z_{j},\overline{W}_{j}\rangle,

and the
Freudenthal product

x\times y=\begin{pmatrix}z_{2}w_{3}+z_{3}w_{2}-\langle Z_{1},W_{1}\rangle\\[3.0pt]
z_{3}w_{1}+z_{1}w_{3}-\langle Z_{2},W_{2}\rangle\\[3.0pt]
z_{1}w_{2}+z_{2}w_{1}-\langle Z_{3},W_{3}\rangle\\[3.0pt]
Z_{2}W_{3}+W_{2}Z_{3}-z_{1}\widetilde{W}_{1}-w_{1}\widetilde{Z}_{1}\\[3.0pt]
Z_{3}W_{1}+W_{3}Z_{1}-z_{2}\widetilde{W}_{2}-w_{2}\widetilde{Z}_{2}\\[3.0pt]
Z_{1}W_{2}+W_{1}Z_{2}-z_{3}\widetilde{W}_{3}-w_{3}\widetilde{Z}_{3}\end{pmatrix}^{\!\top}.

The
Jordan triple product
is

(5)

\{x,y,z\}=(x\mid y)\,z+(z\mid y)\,x-(x\times z)\times\overline{y}.

With these structures,
(\mathcal{H},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
is the Hermitian positive Jordan triple system (HPJTS) of type VI. The
Freudenthal adjoint

z^{\sharp}:=\tfrac{1}{2}\,z\times z,

has the explicit expression

(6)

\begin{split}z^{\sharp}=\Bigl(&z_{2}z_{3}-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\langle Z_{1},Z_{1}\rangle,\;z_{3}z_{1}-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\langle Z_{2},Z_{2}\rangle,\;z_{1}z_{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\langle Z_{3},Z_{3}\rangle,\;\\
&Z_{2}Z_{3}-z_{1}\widetilde{Z}_{1},\;Z_{3}Z_{1}-z_{2}\widetilde{Z}_{2},\;Z_{1}Z_{2}-z_{3}\widetilde{Z}_{3}\Bigr).\end{split}

2.3.
The exceptional Cartan domain of type VI

We refer the reader to
[
23
,
26
]
for details.
The exceptional bounded symmetric domain of type VI is the subset
\Omega_{\mathrm{VI}}\subset\mathcal{H}
of complex dimension
27
given in its circular realization by

(7)

\begin{gathered}1-(z\mid z)+(z^{\sharp}\mid{z^{\sharp}})-\left|\dfrac{1}{3}\,(z^{\sharp}\mid\overline{z})\right|^{2}\;>\;0,\\[4.0pt]
3-2\,(z\mid z)+(z^{\sharp}\mid{z^{\sharp}})\;>\;0,\\[2.0pt]
3-(z\mid z)\;>\;0,\end{gathered}

The associated generic norm is

(8)

N_{\Omega_{\mathrm{VI}}}(z,\overline{w})=1-(z\mid w)+(z^{\sharp}\mid w^{\sharp})-\frac{1}{9}\,(z^{\sharp}\mid\overline{z})\,{(\overline{w}^{\sharp}\mid w)},\qquad z,w\in\mathcal{H}.

We now state and prove the following proposition, in which we exhibit explicitly a totally geodesic embedding of a maximal–rank polydisk
\Delta^{3}
into the exceptional domain
\Omega_{\mathrm{VI}}
with respect to the hyperbolic metric.

Proposition 2.1
.

The holomorphic map

f\colon\Delta^{3}\longrightarrow\Omega_{\mathrm{VI}},\qquad f(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})=\bigl(z_{1},\,z_{2},\,z_{3},\,0,\,0,\,0\bigr),

is an isometric embedding, i.e.

g_{\Delta^{3}}=f^{*}g_{\Omega_{\mathrm{VI}}},

and is totally geodesic. Consequently,

\Pi\;=\;\operatorname{Im}(f)=\bigl\{(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},Z_{1},Z_{2},Z_{3})\in\Omega_{\mathrm{VI}}\ \big|\ Z_{1}=Z_{2}=Z_{3}=0\bigr\}

is a totally geodesic polydisk of maximal rank in
\Omega_{\mathrm{VI}}
.

Proof.

From (
6
) one computes

(9)

\bigl(f(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})\bigr)^{\sharp}=\bigl(z_{2}z_{3},\,z_{1}z_{3},\,z_{1}z_{2},\,0,\,0,\,0\bigr).

Substituting (
9
) into the generic norm (
8
) gives

(10)

\displaystyle N_{\Omega_{\mathrm{VI}}}\bigl(f(z),\overline{f(z)}\bigr)

\displaystyle=1-\bigl(|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}+|z_{3}|^{2}\bigr)+\bigl(|z_{2}z_{3}|^{2}+|z_{1}z_{3}|^{2}+|z_{1}z_{2}|^{2}\bigr)-|z_{1}z_{2}z_{3}|^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-|z_{1}|^{2})(1-|z_{2}|^{2})(1-|z_{3}|^{2}).

Therefore

f^{*}\!\left(-\frac{i}{2}\,\partial\bar{\partial}\log N_{\Omega_{\mathrm{VI}}}\right)=-\frac{i}{2}\,\partial\bar{\partial}\log\!\Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^{3}(1-|z_{j}|^{2})\Bigr)=\omega_{\Delta^{3}},

so
g_{\Delta^{3}}=f^{*}g_{\Omega_{\mathrm{VI}}}
. Finally, the subspace

W=\bigl\{(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},Z_{1},Z_{2},Z_{3})\in\mathcal{H}\ \big|\ Z_{1}=Z_{2}=Z_{3}=0\bigr\}

is a sub–HPJTS of
\mathcal{H}
, hence
\Pi=\operatorname{Im}(f)=W\cap\Omega_{\mathrm{VI}}
is totally geodesic by the standard correspondence between sub–HPJTS and totally geodesic submanifolds (see
[
5
, Prop. 2.1]
). Since the domain of type
VI
has rank
3
,
\Pi
is of maximal rank.
This completes the proof.
∎

2.4.
The exceptional domain of type V

Let
\bigl(\mathcal{H}^{\prime},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}^{\prime}\bigr)
be the sub–HPJTS of
\bigl(\mathcal{H},\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}\bigr)
defined by

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\;:=\;\bigl\{(0,0,0,0,Z_{2},Z_{3})\in\mathcal{H}\ \big|\ Z_{2},Z_{3}\in\mathbb{O}_{\mathbb{C}}\bigr\},

where
\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}^{\prime}
denotes the triple product induced from
\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}
via (
5
).

The
exceptional domain of type V
is the
16
-dimensional bounded symmetric domain

\Omega_{\mathrm{V}}\;=\;\Omega_{\mathrm{VI}}\cap\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\;=\;\Bigl\{\,z\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\ \Big|\ 1-(z\mid z)+(z^{\sharp}\mid{z^{\sharp}})>0,\quad 2-(z\mid z)>0\Bigr\},

where the second equality follows from the defining inequalities of
\Omega_{\mathrm{VI}}
in (
7
).

Its associated generic norm (two–point form) is

(11)

N_{\Omega_{\mathrm{V}}}(z,\overline{w})\;=\;N_{\Omega_{\mathrm{VI}}}(z,\overline{w})\;=\;1-(z\mid w)+(z^{\sharp}\mid w^{\sharp}),\qquad z,w\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime},

since
(z^{\sharp}\mid\overline{z})=0
for all
z\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.

In order to exhibit a totally geodesic maximal–rank polydisk inside the exceptional domain of type
V
, we next construct an explicit holomorphic embedding of
\Delta^{2}
into
\Omega_{\mathrm{V}}
and verify that it is both isometric and totally geodesic.

Proposition 2.2
.

The holomorphic map

f\colon\Delta^{2}\longrightarrow\Omega_{\mathrm{V}},\qquad f(z_{1},z_{2})=\bigl(0,\,0,\,0,\,0,\;\tfrac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}+i\,\tfrac{z_{1}+z_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\,e_{1},\;0\bigr),

is an embedding and satisfies

g_{\Delta^{2}}=f^{*}g_{\Omega_{\mathrm{V}}}.

In particular, its image

\Pi^{\prime}=\operatorname{Im}(f)=\bigl\{(0,0,0,0,Z_{2},0)\in\Omega_{\mathrm{V}}\ \big|\ Z_{2}\in\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{1,e_{1}\}\bigr\}

is a totally geodesic polydisk of maximal rank in
\Omega_{\mathrm{V}}
.

Proof.

Write

Z_{2}=\alpha+\beta\,e_{1},\qquad\alpha=\tfrac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{\sqrt{2}},\quad\beta=i\,\tfrac{z_{1}+z_{2}}{\sqrt{2}},

so that
f(z_{1},z_{2})=(0,0,0,0,Z_{2},0)\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.
Using the adjoint formula (
6
) (with
z_{1}=z_{2}=z_{3}=0
and
Z_{1}=Z_{3}=0
) one obtains

\bigl(f(z_{1},z_{2})\bigr)^{\sharp}=\bigl(0,\;-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle Z_{2},Z_{2}\rangle,\;0,\;0,0,0\bigr).

Since
\langle Z_{2},Z_{2}\rangle=-2\,z_{1}z_{2}
,
it follows that

\bigl(f(z_{1},z_{2})\bigr)^{\sharp}=(0,\;z_{1}z_{2},\;0,\;0,0,0).

By (
11
) the generic norm of
\Omega_{\mathrm{V}}
along the image of
f
reduces to

N_{\Omega_{\mathrm{V}}}\bigl(f(z),\overline{f(z)}\bigr)=1-(f(z)\mid f(z))+\bigl(f(z)^{\sharp}\mid f(z)^{\sharp}\bigr).

Here,

(f(z)\mid f(z))=|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}+|z_{1}+z_{2}|^{2}\bigr)=|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2},

and

.
Therefore

(12)

N_{\Omega_{\mathrm{V}}}\bigl(f(z),\overline{f(z)}\bigr)=1-|z_{1}|^{2}-|z_{2}|^{2}+|z_{1}z_{2}|^{2}=(1-|z_{1}|^{2})(1-|z_{2}|^{2}).

We obtain

so
g_{\Delta^{2}}=f^{*}g_{\Omega_{\mathrm{V}}}
and
f
is an isometric holomorphic immersion.

The map
f
is linear and injective; moreover, the inverse on its image is holomorphic:
if
Z_{2}=\alpha+\beta e_{1}
with
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
, then

z_{1}=\frac{\alpha-i\beta}{\sqrt{2}},\qquad z_{2}=-\frac{\alpha+i\beta}{\sqrt{2}}.

Hence
f
is an embedding.
Finally, consider the sub–HPJTS of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}

W^{\prime}=\bigl\{(0,0,0,0,Z_{2},0)\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\ \big|\ Z_{2}\in\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{1,e_{1}\}\bigr\}.

By the bijection between sub–HPJTS and totally geodesic Hermitian symmetric subdomains (cf.
[
5

), it follows that

\Pi^{\prime}\;=\;\operatorname{Im}(f)\;=\;W^{\prime}\cap\Omega_{\mathrm{V}}

is a totally geodesic, holomorphically isometric embedding of
(\Delta^{2},g_{\Delta^{2}})
inside
\Omega_{\mathrm{V}}
. Since the domain of type
V
has rank
2
,
\Pi^{\prime}
is of maximal rank.

In the following lemma we show that every automorphism of the base domain naturally lifts to an automorphism of the associated Hartogs domain.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded symmetric domain and let
\phi\colon\Omega\to\Omega
be an isometric automorphism. Then
\phi
lifts to a biholomorphism

\tilde{\phi}\colon M_{\Omega,\mu}\;\longrightarrow\;M_{\Omega,\mu},

defined by

(28)

\tilde{\phi}(w,z)=\bigl(e^{\mu\,h_{\phi}(z)}\,w,\phi(z)\bigr),

where
h_{\phi}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
is a holomorphic function chosen so that

(29)

\tilde{\phi}^{*}g_{{\Omega,\mu}}=g_{{\Omega,\mu}}

Moreover, if
\phi
fixes the origin, one may take
h_{\phi}\equiv 0
, so that

\tilde{\phi}(w,z)=(w,\phi(z)).

Proof.

Let
\phi\colon\Omega\to\Omega
be an isometric automorphism. Since
\phi
preserves the Kähler form associated to the generic norm
N_{\Omega}
, we have

\partial\bar{\partial}\log N_{\Omega}\bigl(\phi(z),\overline{\phi(z)}\bigr)=\partial\bar{\partial}\log N_{\Omega}(z,\bar{z}).

Hence, there exists a holomorphic function
h_{\phi}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
such that

N_{\Omega}\bigl(\phi(z),\overline{\phi(z)}\bigr)=N_{\Omega}(z,\bar{z})\,\exp\bigl(h_{\phi}(z)+\overline{h_{\phi}(z)}\bigr).

It follows that the map

\tilde{\phi}\colon M_{\Omega,\mu}\longrightarrow M_{\Omega,\mu},\quad\tilde{\phi}(w,z)=\bigl(e^{\mu\,h_{\phi}(z)}\,w,\phi(z)\bigr),

is well defined, since

\bigl|e^{\mu\,h_{\phi}(z)}w\bigr|^{2}<\bigl|e^{\mu\,h_{\phi}(z)}\bigr|^{2}\,N^{\mu}_{\Omega}(z,\bar{z})=N^{\mu}_{\Omega}\bigl(\phi(z),\overline{\phi(z)}\bigr).

The identity in (
29
) follows from the fact that Calabi’s diastasis functions are invariant under the lift
\tilde{\phi}
. Indeed, one checks directly that:

\displaystyle\partial\bar{\partial}\,D^{g_{{\Omega,\mu}}}_{0}\bigl(\tilde{\phi}(w,z)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\partial\bar{\partial}\log\bigl(N_{\Omega}(\phi(z),\overline{\phi(z)})^{\mu}-|e^{\mu h_{\phi}(z)}w|^{2}\bigr)

(30)

\displaystyle=\partial\bar{\partial}\log\bigl(N_{\Omega}(z,\bar{z})^{\mu}-|w|^{2}\bigr)=\partial\bar{\partial}\,D^{g_{{\Omega,\mu}}}_{0}(w,z).

Hence the diastasis is preserved and (
29
) follows.
Finally, since any biholomorphism
\phi
of
\Omega
fixing the origin preserves the corresponding positive Hermitian Jordan triple structure (see, e.g.,
[
25
, p. 551]
), it follows that the generic norm
N_{\Omega}
is invariant under
\phi
. In this case, one can take
h_{\phi}\equiv 0
in (
28
), yielding the simpler lift

\tilde{\phi}(w,z)\;=\;\bigl(w,\phi(z)\bigr)
.
∎

Here, by combining the preceding results, we show that a totally geodesic polydisk of maximal rank in the base domain gives rise to a totally geodesic Hartogs–polydisk in the corresponding Hartogs domain.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded symmetric domain of rank
r
, and let

\Pi\subset\Omega

be a totally geodesic polydisk of complex dimension
r
. Define

C_{\Pi}=\bigl(\mathbb{C}\times\Pi\bigr)\,\cap\,M_{\Omega,\mu}=\{(w,z)\in M_{\Omega,\mu}\mid z\in\Pi\}.

Then
C_{\Pi}\simeq M_{\Delta^{r},\mu}
and it is a totally geodesic Kähler submanifold of
(M_{\Omega,\mu},g_{{\Omega,\mu}}).

Proof.

We may assume without loss of generality that the chosen polydisk
\Pi\subset\Omega
passes through the origin. The conclusion then follows from Proposition
3.2
together with the Polydisk Theorem (see
[
29
]
,
[
24
, Thm. VI.3.5]
), which asserts that the identity component
\operatorname{Aut}_{0}(\Omega)
acts transitively on the collection of all
r
-dimensional totally geodesic polydisks through the origin. Applying to
M_{\Omega,\mu}
the lifts of these automorphisms, as described in Lemma
3.3
, completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\Omega

D_{0}^{g_{\Omega}^{*}}\bigl(\phi(z)\bigr)=D_{0}^{g_{\Omega}^{*}}(z)\;\Longrightarrow\;N_{\Omega}\bigl(\phi(z),-\overline{\phi(z)}\bigr)=N_{\Omega}(z,-\bar{z}).

Arguing as in the proof of Lemma
3.3
, consider the map

\tilde{\phi}\colon\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n+1},\qquad\tilde{\phi}(z_{0},z)=\bigl(z_{0},\phi(z)\bigr).

One checks that
\tilde{\phi}
preserves the diastasis, namely

D_{0}^{g^{*}_{{\Omega,\mu}}}(z_{0},z)=D_{0}^{g^{*}_{{\Omega,\mu}}}\bigl(\tilde{\phi}(z_{0},z)\bigr),

It follows that
\tilde{\phi}
is an isometric biholomorphism of
(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},g^{*}_{{\Omega,\mu}})
, as required.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\Omega
be a rank
r
bounded symmetric domain, realized in its circular form, and let

\Pi^{*}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be an
r
-dimensional dual polydisk through the origin which is totally geodesic with respect to the dual hyperbolic metric
g_{\Omega}^{*}
. Then the linear subspace

C_{\Pi^{*}}=\mathbb{C}\times\Pi^{*}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{r+1}\;\subset\;\mathbb{C}^{n+1}

is a totally geodesic Kähler submanifold of
\bigl(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},g^{*}_{{\Omega,\mu}}\bigr)
.

Proof.

By the Polydisk Theorem (see
[
29
]
), the isotropy subgroup at the origin in the group of holomorphic isometries of
(\mathbb{C}^{n},g_{\Omega}^{*})
acts transitively on the family of
r
-dimensional, totally geodesic polydisks through the origin. Combining this transitivity with Proposition
3.5
and Lemma
3.6
yields the desired result.
∎

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

By Theorem
1.2
, for any bounded symmetric domain
\Omega
of rank
r
, there exists a holomorphically totally geodesic inclusion

\;\bigl(\mathbb{C}^{s+1},\,g^{*}_{\Delta^{s},\mu}\bigr)\;\hookrightarrow\;\bigl(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},\,g^{*}_{\Omega,\mu}\bigr),

where one takes
s=2
if
r\geq 2
, and
s=1
otherwise. A straightforward computation of the curvature tensor shows that:

(1)

The sectional curvature of
\bigl(\mathbb{C}^{2},\,g^{*}_{\Delta^{1},\mu}\bigr)
at
(w,0)
in the plane spanned by
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}
is

(34)

K\Bigl(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\wedge\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}\Bigr)(w,0)\;=\;\frac{2+2(\mu-1)\,\lvert w\rvert^{2}}{\mu},

where
\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}-i\,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}\bigr)
.

(2)

The sectional curvature of
\bigl(\mathbb{C}^{3},\,g^{*}_{\Delta^{2},\mu}\bigr)
at
(w,0,0)
in the plane spanned by
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}
is

(35)

K\Bigl(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\wedge\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\Bigr)(w,0,0)\;=\;-\tfrac{\lvert w\rvert^{2}}{2},

where
\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}-i\,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}\bigr)
for
j=1,2
.

Since any compact Riemannian manifold has bounded sectional curvature, equation (
34
) precludes
\bigl(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},g^{*}_{\mathbb{C}H^{n},\mu}\bigr)
from being a totally geodesic submanifold of a compact manifold whenever
\mu\neq 1
. Similarly, equation (
35
) shows that if
\operatorname{rank}(\Omega)\geq 2
, then
\bigl(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},g^{*}_{\Omega,\mu}\bigr)
cannot be realized as a totally geodesic submanifold of any compact Riemannian manifold. This completes the proof.

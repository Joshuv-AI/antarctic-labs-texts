Idiopathic multicentric Castleman disease and its rare association with peripheral neuropathy and stroke
Authors: Binti Wan Muhamad Hatta, Syarifah Syafiqah, Mehrab Rasheed, Monica Vegagonzalez, Wen Bun Leong, Ahmed Bashir
Published: 2023

Multicentric Castleman disease is a rare lymphoproliferative disorder that can present with systemic manifestations. This case reports a 42-year-old woman who initially presented with an ischaemic stroke. A few months after the event, she developed a subdural haematoma, for which she underwent surgery. At the time, the cause of her stroke and intracranial bleed was undetermined. Two months later, she developed signs of right heart failure, generalised lymphadenopathy, peripheral sensory-motor neuropathy and organomegaly. Histological investigation and clinical features confirmed a diagnosis of HHV-8-negative/idiopathic Castleman disease (iMCD). The patient was treated with rituximab and had shown improvement in symptoms such as ascites and lower limb swelling but neuropathy was still present. Currently, the patient is still undergoing review and awaiting further treatment.

Based on the literature review, peripheral neuropathy associated with Castleman disease is predominantly sensory and mild.1, 2 Whereas, our patient presented with significant sensory and motor neuropathy, which is a rare manifestation and has been reported only in one other case.3 Stroke is another rare manifestation and whether there is a causal relationship between the conditions remains unclear.

Learning points

•Definitive histology is important in obtaining a diagnosis of Castleman disease as its clinical manifestations can mimic other conditions seen in autoimmune disorders, infections and other haematological diseases like lymphoma.•The association between Castleman disease and mixed sensory-motor neuropathy is rare.•The international, evidence-based consensus diagnostic criteria for iMCD have proven to be a useful tool in aiding the final diagnosis.4

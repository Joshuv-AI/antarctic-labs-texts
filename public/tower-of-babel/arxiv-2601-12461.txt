Homological ( n − 2 ) (n-2) -systole in n n -manifolds with positive triRic curvature
Jingche Chen, Han Hong

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove an optimal systolic inequality and characterize the case of equality on closed Riemannian manifolds with positive triRic curvature. This extends prior work of Bray-Brendle-Neves [ 3 ] and Chu-Lee-Zhu [ 9 ] to higher codimensions. The proof relies on the notion of stable weighted k k -slicing, a weighted volume comparison theorem and metric-deformation.



1.
Introduction

This paper investigates the relationship between curvature positivity of an ambient manifold and the area of its minimal submanifolds. In dimension two, Toponogov’s celebrated theorem
[
19
]
says the length of a simple closed geodesic in a two-sphere with positive Gauss curvature can be bounded from above. In higher dimensions, however, one cannot generally bound the area of an embedded minimal hypersurface in a Riemannian manifold of positive curvature. For example, the area of closed embedded minimal hypersurface in
\mathbb{S}^{3}
can be arbitrarily large (
[
20
]
).

If we restrict attention to
area-minimizing
(rather than merely minimal) objects, more positive results emerge. Define

\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(M,g):=\inf\left\{\operatorname{Area}_{g}(\Sigma)|\Sigma\text{ is a non-contractible $2$-sphere in }M\right\}.

Motivated by Schoen-Yau’s work on incompressible minimal surfaces
[
18
]
and the existence of smooth area minimizers in nontrivial
\pi_{2}(M)
[
16
]
, Bray-Brendle-Neves
[
3
]
studied upper bounds of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(M^{3},g)
for
3
-dimensional closed,
oriented manifold
M^{3}
with positive scalar curvature. They proved

\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(M,g)\cdot\inf R_{g}\leq 8\pi,

with equality if and only if
M
is isometrically covered by
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}.
The inequality (usually referred to spherical systolic inequality) follows from the stability inequality of the minimizer, while the rigidity part is more intriguing and proved by using a local foliation argument. A similar inequality and its rigidity for embedded
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
were later established by Bray-Brendle-Eichmair-Neves
[
2
]
.
Subsequently, Stern employed a harmonic map level-set approach and obtained the following homological systolic inequality

\operatorname{sys}_{2}(M,g)\cdot\inf R_{g}\leq 8\pi,

again with equality if and only if
M
is isometrically covered by
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
, where
\operatorname{sys}_{2}(M,g)
is the least area of nonseparating surfaces in
M
, i.e.,

\operatorname{sys}_{2}(M,g)=\inf\{\operatorname{Area}_{g}(\Sigma)|\Sigma\ \text{is embedded},[\Sigma]\neq 0\in H_{2}(M)\}.

There are several generalizations to higher dimensions. First, if one considers
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifolds with nontrivial second homotopy group
\pi_{2}(M)
, the spherical systolic inequality fails due to the example
\mathbb{S}^{2}(r_{1})\times S^{n-2}(r_{2})
for
n\geq 4
. Thus, Zhu
[
21
]
imposed a topological condition requiring
M^{n}
to admit a nonzero-degree map to
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times T^{n-2}
for
n\leq 7
, and proved

\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(M,g)\cdot\inf R_{g}\leq 8\pi,

with equality if and only if
M
is isometrically covered by
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-2}.
A hyperspherical systolic inequality remains unknown due to the lack of existence theory for area minimizers in higher homotopic classes. On the other hand, a higher dimensional homological systolic inequality is recently studied by Chu-Lee-Zhu
[
9
]
. They showed that if an
n
-dimensional closed Riemannian manifold
M
has nontrivial
(n-1)
-homology group and its biRic curvature is bounded below by
n-2
, then

\operatorname{sys}_{n-1}(M,g)\leq|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|,

with equality if and only if
M
is isometrically covered by
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
. Here

\operatorname{sys}_{n-1}(M,g)=\inf\{\operatorname{Area}_{g}(\Sigma)|\Sigma\ \text{is embedded},[\Sigma]\neq 0\in H_{n-1}(M)\}.

This result holds in all dimensions under a
Generic regularity hypothesis
. A crucial fact worth of mentioning is that under a positive biRic curvature assumption, the stable minimal hypersurface inherits a positive Ricci lower bound in the spectral sense. This allows one to estimate the area of minimizer via the Antonelli-Xu’s comparison theorem
[
1
]
, which is a generalization of classical Bishop-Gromov volume comparison theorem.

The goal of this paper is to investigate systolic inequalities of other indices, specifically when
k=n-2.

We first recall the notion of
triRic curvature
, which is a special case of the
C_{m}
-curvatures introduced by Brendle-Hirsch-Johne
[
4
]
. This concept has also played an important rule in the study of stable minimal hypersurfaces in
5
-dimensional Riemannian manifolds by the second author and Yan
[
14
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
,
n\geq 4
, be a Riemannian manifold. Given a local orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of
T_{p}M
and
\gamma>0
, we define
\gamma
-triRic curvature
in the direction
(e_{i},e_{j},e_{l})
as

(1.1)

\gamma\operatorname{-triRic}(e_{i},e_{j},e_{l}):=\gamma\Ric(e_{i},e_{i})+\gamma\text{-}\biRic_{e_{i}^{\perp}}(e_{j},e_{l}),

where
\gamma\text{-}\biRic_{e_{i}^{\perp}}(e_{j},e_{l})
is given by

\gamma\text{-}\biRic_{e_{i}^{\perp}}(e_{j},e_{l}):=\gamma\sum_{m\neq i}R_{mjmj}+\sum_{m\neq i}R_{mlml}-\gamma R_{jljl},\quad j\neq l

i.e., we take the partial contraction on the subspace
e_{i}^{\perp}:=\vspan\{e_{l}\}_{l\neq i}
normal to
e_{i}
. Furthermore, we define

\gamma\operatorname{-triRic}(p)=\inf\{\gamma\operatorname{-triRic}(e_{i},e_{j},e_{l})\},

where the infimum is taken over all local orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
at
p
, and over all admissible index triples
(e_{i},e_{j},e_{l})
,
i\neq j\neq l
. When
\gamma=1
, we simply call it
triRic curvature
.

Definition 1.2
.

For an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with nontrivial homology group
H_{n-2}(M)
, we define the
(n-2)
-systole as

\text{sys}_{n-2}(M,g):=\inf\left\{\operatorname{Area}_{g}(\Sigma)\;\middle|\;\begin{aligned} \Sigma&\text{ is a smooth submanifold in }M,\\
&[\Sigma]\neq 0\in H_{n-2}(M)\end{aligned}\right\}

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed, oriented
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with
n=4,5
. If there exist cohomology classes
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in H^{1}(M)
satisfying
\alpha_{1}\smile\alpha_{2}\neq 0
, then

\operatorname{sys}_{n-2}(M^{n},g)\cdot\inf\operatorname{triRic}_{g}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\leq(n-3)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}|\mathbb{S}^{n-2}|.

Moreover, if equality holds, then
M
is isometrically covered by
\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

The topological assumption is necessary, otherwise, as noted earlier, the product
\mathbb{S}^{n-2}(r_{1})\times\mathbb{S}^{2}(r_{2})
is a counterexample. We remark that the dimension restriction is not due to regularity issues. Even though
Generic regularity hypothesis
is assumed, we cannot prove such an inequality in higher dimensions. However, in dimension
6
we obtain the following extension, which also illustrates the role of the parameter
\gamma
. The restriction of dimension comes from the range of parameter
\gamma
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed, oriented
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with
n=4,5,6
. Assume that
\frac{\sqrt{n-3}}{2}<\gamma\leq\frac{3(n-1)}{4(n-2)}
and that there exist cohomology classes
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in H^{1}(M)
satisfying
\alpha_{1}\smile\alpha_{2}\neq 0
. Then

\operatorname{sys}_{n-2}(M^{n},g)\cdot\inf\gamma\operatorname{-triRic}_{g}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\leq(n-3)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}|\mathbb{S}^{n-2}|.

Moreover, if equality holds, then
M
is isometrically covered by
\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Topological assumptions apply to manifolds of the form
N^{n-2}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}
or mapping to it with nonzero degree.

Next we outline the proof of Theorem
1.4
. By scaling the metric, we may assume
\inf\gamma\operatorname{-triRic}=n-3
. It then suffices to show that
\operatorname{sys}_{n-2}(M^{n},g)\leq|\mathbb{S}^{n-2}|
. The topological assumption guarantees the existence of a stable weighted
2
-slicing
\Sigma_{2}\subset\Sigma_{1}\subset M
(see Definition
2.2
and Lemma
2.4
). For the inequality, we only need to show that
|\Sigma_{2}|\leq|\mathbb{S}^{n-2}|
(Lemma
3.1
), since
\Sigma_{2}
represents a nontrivial class in
H_{n-2}(M)
. However,
\Sigma_{2}
is stable with respect to a weighted functional in
\Sigma_{2}
, to estimate its area we require a slightly more general volume comparison theorem than that
of Antonelli-Xu (see Theorem
2.1
).

If equality holds, Theorem
2.1
implies that
\Sigma_{2}
is isometric to the standard
\mathbb{S}^{n-2}
. The rigidity argument is more complicated and then proceeds in several steps. First, we show that
\Sigma_{1}
is covered by
\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\times\mathbb{R}
, and then that
M
itself is covered by
\Sigma_{1}\times\mathbb{R}
. Both steps rely on a standard splitting result under nonnegative Ricci curvature (Lemma
2.5
). The main steps are as follows:

(1)

Assume
\operatorname{sys}_{n-2}(M,g)=|\mathbb{S}^{n-2}|
.
Then
\Sigma_{2}
are totally geodesic and
A^{\Sigma_{1}}=0
on
\Sigma_{2}
.

(2)

One can find a sequence of totally geodesic hypersurfaces in
\Sigma_{1}
that converge smoothly to
\Sigma_{2}
from one side.

(3)

For any point
p\in\Sigma_{1}
, there exists a

\Sigma_{2}
-homological area-minimizing hypersurface passing through
p
. This ensures that
\Ric_{\Sigma_{1}}\geq 0
and
A^{\Sigma_{1}}=0
everywhere, so
\Sigma_{1}
splits by Lemma
2.5
.

(4)

One repeats
(2)
and
(3)
in
M
to obtain the global splitting.

Throughout the proof, we employ the surface-capture (or metric-deformation) technique, which was first introduced by Liu
[
15
]
for noncompact manifolds and later extended by Chu, Lee, and Zhu
[
9
]
to the compact setting. We adapt their idea several times. A new difficulty in our setting arises when proving that the cover of
M
splits isometrically as
\Sigma_{1}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Let us mention that Liu’s idea has been developed and applied in various contexts, including
[
5
,
7
,
10
,
13
,
12
]
.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we prove a volume comparison theorem, recall the existence of a stable weighted 2‑slicing, and discuss the standard splitting under the assumption of nonnegative Ricci curvature. In Section
3
, we prove the inequality part of the main theorem and characterize the case of equality for the minimizer. In Section
4
, we complete the characterization of equality in the main theorem.

2.1.
Volume estimate

For a Riemannian manifold, one defines the following Lipschtiz curvature function

\Ric(x):=\inf_{v\in T_{x}M,|v|=1}\Ric(v,v).

It is well known that on a closed manifold, a uniform lower bound on the Ricci curvature implies a uniform upper bound on the volume via the Bishop–Gromov volume comparison theorem. Antonelli and Xu [1] extended this theory to manifolds with a spectral Ricci lower bound. In our case, we require a slightly refined version of the spectral Ricci curvature condition that also incorporates a gradient term. It is precisely this additional gradient term that enables our subsequent systole estimate to be carried out effectively.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be an n-dimensional closed smooth Riemannian manifold with
3\leq n\leq 7
, and let
\gamma>0
. Assume there is a positive function
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that

(2.1)

\gamma\Delta\log u+\frac{n-2}{n-1}\gamma^{2}|\nabla\log u|^{2}+(n-1)\leq\Ric.

Then

\Vol(M)\leq\Vol(\mathbb{S}^{n}).

Moreover, if equality holds, then
u
is a constant function and
M
is isometric to the round sphere.

Proof.

The proof is basically contained in
[
1
]
, see also
[
8
]
. For completeness and convenience of readers, we include it here.

Consider the weighted isoperimetric profile of
M
as

I_{M}(v)=\inf\Big\{\int_{\partial^{*}E}u^{\gamma}\ \Big|\ E\subset M,\int_{E}u^{\frac{2\gamma}{n-1}}=v\Big\},\quad v\in[0,\Vol(M)).

Here
\partial^{*}E
denotes the reduced boundary of
E
. By
[
1
, Lemma 3 and Lemma 4]
, it suffices to show

(2.2)

I_{M}I_{M}^{\prime\prime}\leq-\frac{(I^{\prime}_{M})^{2}}{n-1}-(n-1)

in the viscosity sense under the additional condition
\inf(u)=1
.
For a set
E\subset M
with smooth boundary of finite perimeter, we denote two functionals by
V(E)=\int_{E}u^{\alpha}
and
A(E)=\int_{\partial E}u^{\gamma}
, where
\alpha=\frac{2\gamma}{n-1}
. Fix

and

such that
\int_{E}u^{\alpha}=v_{0}
and
\int_{\partial^{*}E}u^{\gamma}=I_{M}(v_{0})
. We denote the unit outward normal vector field of

by

\varphi\nu

\left.\frac{dV}{dt}\right|_{t=0}=\int_{\partial E}u^{\alpha}\varphi

and

(2.3)

\left.\frac{d^{2}V}{dt^{2}}\right|_{t=0}=\int_{\partial E}(H+\alpha u^{-1}u_{\nu})u^{\alpha}\varphi^{2}+u^{\alpha}\varphi\varphi_{\nu}.

Similarly, we have

\left.\frac{dA}{dt}\right|_{t=0}=\int_{\partial E}u^{\gamma}\varphi(H+\gamma u^{-1}u_{\nu})

and

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d^{2}A}{dt^{2}}\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial E}\Big(-\Delta_{\partial E}\varphi-\Ric_{M}(\nu,\nu)\varphi-|A^{\partial E}|^{2}\varphi\Big)u^{\gamma}\varphi

\displaystyle+\Big(-\gamma u^{-2}u_{\nu}^{2}\varphi+\gamma u^{-1}\nabla_{M}^{2}u(\nu,\nu)\varphi-\gamma u^{-1}\langle\nabla_{\partial E}u,\nabla_{\partial E}\varphi\rangle\Big)u^{\gamma}\varphi

Then

Taking

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d^{2}A}{dt^{2}}\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial E}-\Ric_{M}(\nu,\nu)u^{-\gamma}+\gamma u^{-\gamma-1}\Delta_{M}u-\gamma|\nabla_{\partial E}u|^{2}u^{-\gamma-2}

Since
\frac{dV}{dt}(0)>0
and using inverse function theorem, we get

. After composing

around

For convenience, we denote by

2.2.
Weighted slicings

Next we recall the notion of stable weighted minimal
m
-slicing introduced by Brendle-Hirsch-Johne
[
4
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose
1\leq m\leq n-1
and let
M
be an orientable compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
. A stable weighted
m
-slicing consists of a collection of connected, orientable, smooth submanifolds
\Sigma_{k}
,
0\leq k\leq m
, and a collection of positive functions
\rho_{k}\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma_{k})
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)
\Sigma_{0}=M
and
\rho_{0}=1
.

(2) For each
1\leq k\leq m
,
\Sigma_{k}
is an
(n-k)
-dimensional, connected, two-sided, embedded, closed hypersurface in
\Sigma_{k-1}
. Moreover,
\Sigma_{k}
is a stable critical point of the
\rho_{k-1}
weighted area

\mathcal{H}_{\rho_{k-1}}^{n-k}(\Sigma)=\int_{\Sigma}\rho_{k-1}\ d\mu

in the class of hypersurfaces
\Sigma\subset\Sigma_{k-1}
.

(3) For each
1\leq k\leq m
, the function
\frac{\rho_{k}}{\rho_{k-1}|_{\Sigma_{k}}}
is a first eigenfunction of the stability operator associated with
\rho_{k-1}
weighted area.

Remark 2.3
.

Compared to
[
4
]
, we require all the hypersurfaces under consideration to be connected for simplicity.

The following lemma provides a sufficient condition for the existence of a stable weighted
k
-slicing.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose
M
is an
n
-dimensional closed, orientable manifold and let
1\leq k\leq n-1
where
n\leq 7
. Suppose
\alpha^{1},\cdots,\alpha^{k}\in H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
which are nontrivial cohomology classes satisfying
\smile_{j=1}^{k}\alpha^{j}\neq 0
. Then there exists a stable weighted k-slicing
\ \Sigma_{k}\subset\cdots\subset\Sigma_{1}\subset\Sigma_{0}=M
such that for any
1\leq j\leq k
, we have
[\Sigma_{j}]=(\alpha^{j}\smile\cdots\smile\alpha^{1})\frown[M]\in H_{n-j}(M)
.

Proof.

The proof follows exactly from
[
6
, Lemma 2.4]
by ignoring the boundary.
∎

2.3.
Splitting

We state the following splitting result which will be used several times in our main proof.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose
M^{n}
is a closed Riemannian manifold with non-negative Ricci curvature. Suppose there exists a closed, embedded, two-sided, locally area-minimizing hypersurface
\Sigma
in
M
.
Then there exists a neighborhood of
\Sigma
in
M
that is isometric to
(\Sigma\times(-\epsilon,\epsilon),g_{\Sigma}+dt^{2})
. Moreover, if
\Sigma
is area-minimizing in the homology class
[\Sigma]\neq 0\in H_{n-1}(M)
, then
M
is isometrically covered by
(\Sigma\times\mathbb{R},g_{\Sigma}+dt^{2})
.

This follows from a special case of Theorem 3.2 (d) in
[
11
]
. Consider the foliation of local equidistant hypersurfaces around
\Sigma
, one can show that the area is non-increasing and the normal vector field of the foliation is parallel, thus giving a local splitting. For dimension
3
, Bray-Brendle-Neves
[
3
]
used a different foliation and showed that the local splitting also holds when
M
has nonnegative scalar curvature. A more systematic method in dimension
3
is provided by Micallef-Moraru
[
17
]
. Chu-Lee-Zhu
[
9
]
applied the method of
[
3
]
and also proved the above lemma.

3.
Proof of the inequality

Suppose there exists
a stable weighted
2
-slicing
\Sigma_{2}\subset\Sigma_{1}\subset\Sigma_{0}=M
. Then
\Sigma_{1}
is a closed stable minimal hypersurface in
\Sigma_{0}
such that the Jacobi operator has the first eigenfunction
\rho_{1}>0
. Hence, we have

(3.1)

\Delta_{\Sigma_{1}}\log\rho_{1}\leq-\left(|A^{\Sigma_{1}}|^{2}+\Ric_{\Sigma_{0}}(\nu_{1},\nu_{1})\right)-\langle\nabla_{\Sigma_{1}}\log\rho_{1},\nabla_{\Sigma_{1}}\log\rho_{1}\rangle.

Moreover,
\Sigma_{2}
is a closed stable weighted minimal hypersurface in
\Sigma_{1}
with respect to the functional
\int_{\Sigma}\rho_{1}\,dA
. Similarly, there exists a positive function
\omega_{2}>0
satisfying

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\omega_{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\left(|A^{\Sigma_{2}}|^{2}+\Ric_{\Sigma_{1}}(\nu_{2},\nu_{2})\right)+(\nabla^{2}_{\Sigma_{1}}\log\rho_{1})(\nu_{2},\nu_{2})

\displaystyle-\langle\nabla_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\rho_{1},\nabla_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\omega_{2}\rangle-\langle\nabla_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\omega_{2},\nabla_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\omega_{2}\rangle.

We denote
\rho_{2}=\rho_{1}\omega_{2}
. By (
3.1
), (
3.2
) and
(\nabla^{2}_{\Sigma_{1}}\log\rho_{1})(\nu_{2},\nu_{2})=\Delta_{\Sigma_{1}}\log\rho_{1}-\Delta_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\rho_{1}-H^{\Sigma_{2}}\langle\nabla_{\Sigma_{1}}\log\rho_{1},\nu_{2}\rangle
, we obtain

(3.3)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\rho_{2}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\rho_{1}+\Delta_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\omega_{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\sum_{k=1}^{2}\left(|A^{\Sigma_{k}}|^{2}+\Ric_{\Sigma_{k-1}}(\nu_{k},\nu_{k})\right)-|\nabla_{\Sigma_{1}}\log\rho_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle-\langle\nabla_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\rho_{2},\nabla_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\omega_{2}\rangle-H^{\Sigma_{2}}\langle\nabla_{\Sigma_{1}}\log\rho_{1},\nu_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{2}\left(|A^{\Sigma_{k}}|^{2}+\Ric_{\Sigma_{k-1}}(\nu_{k},\nu_{k})\right)-|\nabla_{\Sigma_{1}}\log\rho_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle-\langle\nabla_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\rho_{2},\nabla_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\omega_{2}\rangle+(H^{\Sigma_{2}})^{2},

where the last equality is due to the critical equation
H^{\Sigma_{2}}=-\langle\nabla_{\Sigma_{1}}\log\rho_{1},\nu_{2}\rangle
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

on
\Sigma_{2}

\gamma\operatorname{-triRic}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},e)=n-3

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\Sigma_{2}
satisfies the assumption of Theorem
2.1
. Firstly, we estimate

(3.4)

\displaystyle\gamma^{2}\frac{n-2}{n-1}|\nabla_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\rho_{2}|^{2}-\gamma|\nabla_{\Sigma_{1}}\log\rho_{1}|^{2}-\gamma\langle\nabla_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\rho_{2},\nabla_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\omega_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{2}\frac{n-2}{n-1}|\nabla_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\rho_{2}|^{2}-\gamma|\nabla_{\Sigma_{1}}\log\rho_{1}|^{2}-\gamma\langle\nabla_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\rho_{2},\nabla_{\Sigma_{2}}(\log\rho_{2}-\log\rho_{1})\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-\gamma(H^{\Sigma_{2}})^{2},

where the second inequality is because
\langle\nabla_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\rho_{1},\nabla_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\rho_{2}\rangle\leq\frac{1}{4}|\nabla_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\rho_{2}|^{2}+|\nabla_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\rho_{1}|^{2}
and the last inequality follows from
|\nabla_{\Sigma_{1}}\log\rho_{1}|^{2}=|\nabla_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\rho_{1}|^{2}+(H^{\Sigma_{2}})^{2}
and the assumption
0\leq\gamma\leq\frac{3(n-1)}{4(n-2)}
.

For any unit tangent vector
e
on
\Sigma_{2}
, we let
\{e_{3}=e,e_{4}\cdots,e_{n}\}
be a local orthonormal basis on
\Sigma_{2}
such that the extension

), we obtain

(3.5)

\displaystyle\gamma\Delta_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\rho_{2}+\frac{n-2}{n-1}\gamma^{2}|\nabla_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\rho_{2}|^{2}-\Ric_{\Sigma_{2}}(e,e)

\displaystyle\leq-\sum_{k=1}^{2}\gamma\left(|A^{\Sigma_{k}}|^{2}+\Ric_{\Sigma_{k-1}}(\nu_{k},\nu_{k})\right)-\Ric_{\Sigma_{2}}(e,e)

\displaystyle=-\gamma\left(|A^{\Sigma_{1}}|^{2}+\Ric_{\Sigma_{0}}(\nu_{1},\nu_{1})\right)-\gamma\Big(|A^{\Sigma_{2}}|^{2}+\sum_{j=3}^{n}\big(R^{\Sigma_{0}}_{2j2j}+A^{\Sigma_{1}}_{22}A^{\Sigma_{1}}_{jj}-(A^{\Sigma_{1}}_{2j})^{2}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=4}^{n}\left(R^{\Sigma_{0}}_{3j3j}+A^{\Sigma_{1}}_{33}A^{\Sigma_{1}}_{jj}-(A^{\Sigma_{1}}_{3j})^{2}+A^{\Sigma_{2}}_{33}A^{\Sigma_{2}}_{jj}-(A^{\Sigma_{2}}_{3j})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\text{$\gamma$-triRic}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},e)-V_{1}-V_{2},

In the last inequality we used the fact that

\displaystyle\geq(\frac{n-2}{n-3}\gamma-1)(A^{\Sigma_{2}}_{33})^{2}-(\frac{2\gamma}{n-3}-1)H^{\Sigma_{2}}A^{\Sigma_{2}}_{33}+\frac{\gamma}{n-3}(H^{\Sigma_{2}})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 0,

where we have used

.
Thus, we have

\gamma\Delta_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\rho_{2}+\frac{n-2}{n-1}\gamma^{2}|\nabla_{\Sigma_{2}}\log\rho_{2}|^{2}-\Ric(e,e)\leq-\text{$\gamma$-triRic}(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},e)\leq-n+3.

\displaystyle n-3

\displaystyle=\gamma\sum_{i=1}^{n}R^{\Sigma_{0}}_{1i1i}+\gamma\sum_{i=2}^{n}R^{\Sigma_{0}}_{2i2i}+\sum_{i=3}^{n}R^{\Sigma_{0}}_{3i3i}

\displaystyle=\gamma\big(\Ric_{\Sigma_{0}}(\nu_{1},\nu_{1})+\Ric_{\Sigma_{1}}(\nu_{2},\nu_{2})\big)+\sum_{i=3}^{n}R^{\Sigma_{2}}_{3i3i}

\displaystyle=\gamma\big(\Ric_{\Sigma_{0}}(\nu_{1},\nu_{1})+\Ric_{\Sigma_{1}}(\nu_{2},\nu_{2})\big)+n-3.

Since

and using (

4.
Proof of the rigidity

4.1.
Local conformal change of the metric

In this section, we will prove the rigidity part of the main theorem by using the metric-deformation trick inspired by Liu
[
15
]
and Chu-Lee-Zhu
[
9
]
. We assume the dimension of
M
satisfies
n\leq 6
throughout this section. Thus the regularity of the minimizer is no longer a trouble.

Let
r_{\text{inj}}
be the injective radius of
(M,g)
. Given any point
p\in M
and any constant
r\in(0,r_{\text{inj}})
and
t\in(0,1)
, we consider the following conformal metric:

g_{p,r,t}=e^{-2t(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{5}}g,

where

\rho=\min\{r,\text{dist}_{g}(\cdot,p)\}.

Denote by
f=t(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{5}
. Then we have the gradient bound

|\nabla_{M}f|\leq Ct\rho(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{4},

and the Hessian estimate

\nabla^{2}_{M}f\geq 80t\rho^{2}(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{3}d\rho\otimes d\rho-Ct(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{4}(d\rho\otimes d\rho+\rho|\nabla^{2}_{M}\rho|).

According to the Hessian comparision theorem, for
r
small enough and
\forall 0<\rho<r
, we have

(4.1)

\nabla^{2}_{M}f\geq 80t\rho^{2}(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{3}d\rho\otimes d\rho-C^{\prime}t(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{4}.

Hence, we obtain

(4.2)

\nabla_{M}^{2}f(e,e)\geq-C^{\prime}t(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{4},

and

(4.3)

\Delta_{M}f\geq 80t\rho^{2}(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{3}-C^{\prime\prime}t(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{4}.

The following two lemmas show how the
\gamma
-biRic curvature and Ricci curvature change with respect to the new metric and give their below bound. Lemma
4.1
is similar to
[
9
, Lemma 2.8]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Fix a
r
small enough. There exists a constant
\theta\in(0,1)
independent of
p,r
and
t
such that the following holds in
B_{r}(p)\backslash B_{\theta r}(p)

e^{-2t(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{5}}\cdot\gamma\operatorname{-biRic}_{g_{p,r,t}}(v_{p,r,t},w_{p,r,t})\geq\gamma\operatorname{-biRic}_{g}(v,w)+75t\rho^{2}(r^{2}-\rho^{3})^{3}.

where
(v,w)
is any set of orthonormal vectors and
(v_{p,r,t},w_{p,r,t})
is the
g_{p,r,t}
-unit normalization of
(v,w)
.

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle\text{Rm}_{\tilde{M}}(\tilde{v},\tilde{w},\tilde{v},\tilde{w})

\displaystyle=e^{2f}\Big(\text{Rm}_{M}(v,w,v,w)+\nabla^{2}_{M}f(v,v)+\nabla^{2}_{M}f(w,w)

\displaystyle+v(f)^{2}+w(f)^{2}-|\nabla_{M}f|^{2}\Big),

then

\displaystyle e^{-2t(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{5}}\cdot\gamma\text{-biRic}_{g_{p,r,t}}(v_{p,r,t},w_{p,r,t})

\displaystyle=\gamma\text{-biRic}_{g}(v,w)+\big(\gamma(n-2)-1\big)\nabla_{g}^{2}f(v,v)

\displaystyle+(n-3)\nabla_{g}^{2}f(w,w)+(\gamma+1)\Delta_{g}f+\big(\gamma(n-2)-1\big)v^{2}(f)

\displaystyle+(n-3)w^{2}(f)-\big(\gamma(n-2)+n-3\big)|\nabla_{g}f|^{2}.

Then for
r
small enough, we have

\displaystyle e^{-2t(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{5}}\cdot\gamma\text{-biRic}_{g_{p,r,t}}(v_{p,r,t},w_{p,r,t})

\displaystyle\geq\gamma\text{-biRic}_{g}(v,w)+\min\{\gamma(n-1),n+\gamma-2\}\Delta_{g}f-C_{1}t(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{4}

\displaystyle\geq\gamma\text{-biRic}_{g}(v,w)+80t\rho^{2}(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{3}-C_{2}t(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{4}

\displaystyle\geq\gamma\text{-biRic}_{g}(v,w)+75t\rho^{2}(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{3},

where the first inequality is due to (
4.2
), the second inequality is from (
4.3
) and the last inequality uses
\theta
close enough to
1
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Fix a
r
small enough. There exists a constant
\theta\in(0,1)
independent of
p,r
and
t
such that the following holds in
B_{r}(p)\backslash B_{\theta r}(p)

e^{-2t(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{5}}\Ric_{g_{p,r,t}}(v_{p,r,t},v_{p,r,t})\geq\Ric_{g}(v,v)+75t\rho^{2}(r^{2}-\rho^{3})^{3}.

where
v
is any set of orthonormal vectors and
v_{p,r,t}
is the
g_{p,r,t}
-unit normalization of
v
.

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle\text{Rm}_{\tilde{M}}(\tilde{v},\tilde{w},\tilde{v},\tilde{w})

\displaystyle=e^{2f}\Big(\text{Rm}_{M}(v,w,v,w)+\nabla^{2}_{M}f(v,v)+\nabla^{2}_{M}f(w,w)

\displaystyle+v(f)^{2}+w(f)^{2}-|\nabla_{M}f|^{2}\Big),

then

\displaystyle e^{-2t(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{5}}\Ric_{g_{p,r,t}}(v_{p,r,t},v_{p,r,t})

\displaystyle=\Ric(v,v)+(n-2)\nabla_{M}^{2}f(v,v)+\Delta_{M}f

\displaystyle+(n-2)v^{2}(f)-(n-2)|\nabla_{M}f|^{2}.

Then for
r
small enough, we have

\displaystyle e^{-2t(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{5}}\Ric_{g_{p,r,t}}(v_{p,r,t},v_{p,r,t})

\displaystyle=\Ric(v,v)+\Delta_{M}f-C_{1}t(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{4}

\displaystyle\geq\Ric(v,v)+80t\rho^{2}(r^{2}-\rho^{3})^{3}-C_{2}t(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{4}

\displaystyle\geq\Ric(v,v)+75t\rho^{2}(r^{2}-\rho^{3})^{3},

where the first inequality is due to (
4.2
), the second inequality is from (
4.3
) and the last inequality uses
\theta
close enough to
1
.
∎

The following lemma can be referred in
[

, Lemma 2.9]
, which gives an upper bound on Ricci curvature of hypersurface under the new metric.

Lemma 4.3
.

Given a positive constant
\Lambda
and a small enough
r
, there exist
\iota>0
and
\theta\in(0,1)
depending on
\Lambda
such that if
N
is a smooth embedded hypersurface satisfying
|A_{N}|_{g}\leq\Lambda
and
|\nabla_{N,g}\rho|_{g}\leq\iota
in
B_{r}(p)
, then the following holds in
(B_{r}(p)\backslash B_{\theta r}(p))\cap N

e^{-2t(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{5}}\cdot\Ric^{N}_{g_{p,r,t}}(v_{p,r,t},v_{p,r,t})\leq\Ric^{N}_{g}(v,v)+50t\rho^{2}(r^{2}-\rho^{2})^{3}.

where

is any unit vector field tangential to

and

4.2.
Splitting of
\Sigma_{1}

In this subsection, we show that
\Sigma_{1}
is isometically covered by
\mathbb{S}^{n-2}\times\mathbb{R}
. First, we use the metric-deformation to construct a sequence of totally geodesic hypersurfaces converging to
\Sigma_{1}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional
(4\leq n\leq 6)
oriented closed Riemannian manifold with
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in H^{1}(M)
satisfying
\alpha_{1}\smile\alpha_{2}\neq 0
and
\gamma\operatorname{-triRic}\geq n-3
for
\frac{\sqrt{n-3}}{2}<\gamma\leq\frac{3(n-1)}{4(n-2)}
. Let
\Sigma_{2}\subset\Sigma_{1}\subset\Sigma_{0}=M
be a stable weighted
2
-slicing ensured by Lemma
2.4
. Assume
\operatorname{sys}_{n-2}(M)=|\mathbb{S}^{n-2}|
. Then there is a sequence of totally geodesic hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{2}^{(i)}\subset\Sigma_{1}
smoothly converging to
\Sigma_{2}
from one side.

Proof.

In order to guarantee that
\Sigma_{2}^{(i)}
converges from one side of
\Sigma_{2}
, we cut
\Sigma_{1}
along the hypersurface
\Sigma_{2}
and then consider the metric completion
(\hat{\Sigma}_{1},\hat{g})
of the complement
(\Sigma_{1}\backslash\Sigma_{2},g)
. Since
[\Sigma_{2}]={M}\frown(\alpha_{1}\smile\alpha_{2})\neq 0\in H_{n-2}(M)
, then
[\Sigma_{2}]\neq 0\in H_{n-1}(\Sigma_{1})
. Hence,
\Sigma_{2}
is non-separating in
\Sigma_{1}
and
\partial\hat{\Sigma}_{1}
consists of two copies of
\Sigma_{2}
. We fix one component of
\partial\hat{\Sigma}_{1}
and denote it by
\hat{\Sigma}_{2}
. For
\epsilon
small, we take a minimizing arclength parametrized geodesic
\hat{\gamma}:[0,\epsilon)\rightarrow(\hat{\Sigma}_{1},\hat{g})
with
\hat{\gamma}(0)\in\hat{\Sigma}_{2}
and
\hat{\gamma}\perp\hat{\Sigma}_{2}
. Note that we can take
\epsilon
sufficiently small such that
\operatorname{dist}(\hat{\gamma}(t),\hat{\Sigma}_{2})=t
for
t<\epsilon
. Denote
\hat{p}=\hat{\gamma}(s)
for some fixed
s\in[0,\epsilon)
and
\hat{d}=\operatorname{dist}_{\hat{g}}(\cdot,\hat{\Sigma}_{2}).
Let
\hat{\nu}_{2}
be the unit outer normal vector of
\hat{\Sigma}_{2}
in
\hat{\Sigma}_{1}
.

Fix
\theta\in(0,1)
and
\iota\in(0,1)
such that Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.3
hold with the choice
\Lambda=\max|A^{\hat{\Sigma}_{2}}|+1
. Since
\langle\nabla_{\hat{\Sigma}_{1}}\hat{d},\hat{\nu}_{2}\rangle|_{\hat{\gamma}(0)}=1
, it can guarantee that
\langle\nabla_{\hat{\Sigma}_{1}}\hat{d},\hat{\nu}_{2}\rangle>\sqrt{1-\iota^{2}}
in an open neighborhood of
\hat{\gamma}(0)
in
\hat{\Sigma}_{2}
. Then there exists
\delta\in(0,s)
small enough such that

\langle\nabla_{\hat{\Sigma}_{1}}\hat{d},\hat{\nu}_{2}\rangle>\sqrt{1-\iota^{2}}\quad\text{in}\ \hat{B}_{s+\delta}(\hat{p})\cap\hat{\Sigma}_{2}.

Let
r=s+\tau
with
0<\tau<\delta
to be determined later. We consider the conformal metric

\hat{g}_{r,t}=e^{-2t(r^{2}-\hat{\rho}^{2})^{5}}\hat{g}

where

\hat{\rho}=\min\{r,\text{dist}_{\hat{g}}(\cdot,\hat{p})\}.

By a standard computation, the mean curvature of
\hat{\Sigma}_{2}
in
(\hat{\Sigma}_{1},\hat{g})
with respect to the unit outer normal
\hat{\nu}_{2}
satisfies

\hat{H}_{r,t}=10(n-2)e^{t(r^{2}-\hat{\rho}^{2})^{5}}t(r^{2}-\hat{\rho}^{2})^{4}\hat{\rho}\langle\nabla_{\hat{g}}\hat{\rho},\hat{\nu}_{2}\rangle\geq 0.

If we further consider the conformal metric
\tilde{g}_{r,t}=\rho_{1}^{\frac{1}{n-2}}\hat{g}_{r,t},
then the mean curvature of
\hat{\Sigma}_{2}
in
(\hat{\Sigma}_{1},\tilde{g})
with respect the unit outer normal is

\tilde{H}_{r,t}=\rho_{1}^{-\frac{1}{2(n-2)}}\left(\hat{H}_{r,t}+(n-2)\hat{\nu}_{2}(\rho_{1})\right)\geq 0

since
\hat{\nu}_{2}(\rho_{1})=0
on
\hat{\Sigma}_{2}
due to Lemma
3.1
. Thus,
it follows from geometric measure theory that there exists a smooth embedded hypersurface
\hat{\Sigma}_{2,t,r}\subset\hat{\Sigma}_{1}
such that

\int_{\hat{\Sigma}_{2,t,r}}\rho_{1}dA_{\hat{g}_{r,t}}=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Sigma\subset\hat{\Sigma}_{1}\\
\Sigma\ \text{homologous to}\ \hat{\Sigma}_{2}\end{subarray}}\int_{\Sigma}\rho_{1}dA_{\hat{g}_{r,t}}.

We claim that
\hat{\Sigma}_{2,t,r}\cap\hat{B}_{r}(\hat{p})\neq\emptyset
where
\hat{B}_{r}(\hat{p})=\{x\in\hat{M}:\operatorname{dist}_{\hat{g}}(x,\hat{p})<r\}
. Otherwise, because
\hat{g}_{r,t}=\hat{g}
outside
B_{r}(\hat{p})
and
\hat{g}_{r,t}<\hat{g}
inside
B_{r}(\hat{p})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\Sigma}_{2,t,r}}\rho_{1}dA_{\hat{g}_{r,t}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\hat{\Sigma}_{2}}\rho_{1}dA_{\hat{g}_{r,t}}

\displaystyle<\int_{\hat{\Sigma}_{2}}\rho_{1}dA_{\hat{g}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\hat{\Sigma}_{2,t,r}}\rho_{1}dA_{\hat{g}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{\Sigma}_{2,t,r}}\rho_{1}dA_{\hat{g}_{r,t}}

which is a contradiction.

Notice as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
,
\hat{g}_{r,t}\rightarrow\hat{g}
in
C^{4,\alpha}
. Moreover, for any small
t
,

\int_{\hat{\Sigma}_{2,t,r}}\rho_{1}dA_{\hat{g}_{r,t}}\leq\int_{\hat{\Sigma}_{2}}\rho_{1}dA_{\hat{g}}.

Then by the curvature estimate,
\hat{\Sigma}_{2,t,r}
converges to a smooth weighted minimal hypersurface
\hat{\Sigma}_{2,r}
.
Thus

\int_{\hat{\Sigma}_{2,r}}\rho_{1}dA_{\hat{g}}\leq\int_{\hat{\Sigma}_{2}}\rho_{1}dA_{\hat{g}}.

It follows from Lemma
3.1
that
|\hat{\Sigma}_{2,r}|\leq|\mathbb{S}^{n-2}|.

On the other hand, by the construction of
\hat{\Sigma}_{2,r}\subset\Sigma_{1}\subset M
, we have
[\hat{\Sigma}_{2,r}]\neq 0\in H_{n-2}(M)
. Hence,

|\mathbb{S}^{n-2}|=\operatorname{sys}_{n-2}(M)\leq|\hat{\Sigma}_{2,r}|.

Thus the rigidity conclusion also holds on
\hat{\Sigma}_{2,r}
. In particular,
\hat{\Sigma}_{2,r}
is totally geodesic. Since
\hat{\Sigma}_{2,r,t}\cap\hat{B}_{r}(\hat{p})\neq\emptyset
, then
\hat{\Sigma}_{2,r}
must intersect
\overline{\hat{B}_{r}(\hat{p})}
.

Next, we need to show
\hat{\Sigma}_{2,r}\neq\hat{\Sigma}_{2}
. If not, we have
\hat{\Sigma}_{2,r,t}\rightarrow\hat{\Sigma}_{2}
as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
.
Making use of the fact that
\hat{\Sigma}_{2,r,t}
is a stable weighted minimal hypersurface in
\hat{\Sigma}_{1}
with respect to the functional
\int_{\Sigma}\rho_{1}dA_{\hat{g}_{r,t}}
, then there exists a positive function
\omega_{2,r,t}>0
on
\hat{\Sigma}_{2,r,t}
satisfying

(4.4)

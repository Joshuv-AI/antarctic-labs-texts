Community detection in heterogeneous signed networks
Yuwen Wang, Shiwen Ye, Jingnan Zhang, Junhui Wang, [10pt] Department of Statistics, Data ScienceThe Chinese University of Hong Kong

Abstract
Network data has attracted growing interest across scientific domains, prompting the development of various network models. Existing network analysis methods mainly focus on unsigned networks, whereas signed networks, consisting of both positive and negative edges, have been frequently encountered in practice but much less investigated. In this paper, we formally define strong and weak balance in signed networks, and propose a signed block β \beta -model, which is capable of modeling strong- and weak-balanced signed networks simultaneously. We establish the identifiability of the proposed model by leveraging properties of bipartite graphs, and develop an efficient alternating updating algorithm to optimize the resulting log-likelihood function. More importantly, we establish the asymptotic consistencies of the proposed model in terms of both probability estimation and community detection. Its advantages are also demonstrated through extensive numerical experiments and the application to a real-world international relationship network.

Balance theory,
\beta
-model, node heterogeneity, signed networks, stochastic block model

Introduction

Network data has attracted increasing attention in recent years, with applications in diverse scientific domains, ranging from social science
[
18
]
, computer science
[
14
]
to biomedicine
[
31
]
. Analysis of network data can reveal latent structures underlying the interactions among entities of interest, such as community detection
[
11
]
, link prediction
[
26
]
, homophily
[
16
]
and so on.

Most existing methods for network analysis focus on unsigned networks, whereas signed networks are also prevalent in real-world applications. Specifically, signed networks have been extensively employed to represent relationships among objects
[
13
]
, which associate polarity information with each edge and thus distinguish friends from enemies, or allies from rivals. These signed edges give rise to unique structural characteristics grounded in social psychology theories, particularly the balance theory
[
15
;
2
]
, stating that certain triadic configurations involving signed edges lead to stable or unstable social structures. Figure
1
illustrates four possible triads, among which triads A and C represent strong-balanced structures. These triads align with intuitive social principles, such as “a friend of my friend is likely my friend” and “an enemy of my friend is likely my enemy”. Weak balance
[
6
]
extends this concept by additionally permitting triad D, which accommodates the occurrence of three mutually hostile nodes.

Figure 1
:
Configurations of four possible triads.

Community detection has been widely studied for unsigned networks
[
17
;
11
;
27
;
23
;
37
]
, but much less so for signed networks. Most existing community detection methods for signed networks
[
7
;
20
;
19
;
5
]
are algorithm based and lack formal theoretical analysis, not to mention that the prevalent balanced structures in signed networks are often ignored by the aforementioned community detection methods. Note that the balance theory actually posits that a strong-balanced (weak-balanced) network can be partitioned into two (multiple) sub-networks such that positive edges connect nodes within the same sub-network, whereas nodes from different sub-networks are connected by negative edges
[
8
]
. More recently,
32
introduces a formal definition of strong-balanced signed networks in a probabilistic sense and proposes an efficient method to detect the two communities, yet the method is not readily applicable to the weak-balanced signed networks with multiple communities.

In this paper, we first extend the definition of strong-balanced signed networks in
32
to accommodate weak-balanced signed networks. We also provide a set of equivalence conditions for both strong- and weak-balanced signed networks, from both local and global perspectives. Built upon the global perspective, we then propose a signed block
\beta
-model to explicitly incorporate the strong or weak balance structures in heterogeneous signed networks. Particularly, it introduces two sets of parameters to capture each node’s distinct connection tendencies within its own community and across different communities. The identifiability of the signed block
\beta
-model is established by exploiting properties of the bipartite graph, and an efficient alternating updating algorithm is developed to optimize the resulting negative log-likelihood function for parameter estimation. More importantly, the asymptotic consistencies of the block
\beta
-model are established in terms of both probability estimation and community detection. Its advantages are also supported in numerical experiments on various synthetic signed networks and a real-world international relationship network.

The main contribution of the proposed signed block
\beta
-model is that it provides a novel framework to statistically model strong and weak balance structures in signed networks. It also generalizes the traditional
\beta
-model
[
3
;
28
;
4
]
by incorporating community structures while preserving its capacity to capture node heterogeneity. For each node, two sets of parameters are introduced to model its tendency to positively or negatively connect with other nodes within the same community or across different communities. Such a treatment not only incorporates community structure in each node’s connection tendency, but also admits more substantial node heterogeneity than existing models
[
4
;
30
;
5
]
. Moreover, a novel set of identifiability conditions for the proposed signed block
\beta
-model is established by leveraging techniques from the graph theory. The theoretical techniques developed here can be applicable to identifiability analyses of other network models involving community structures, and thus are of independent interest.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 formally gives the definition of strong and weak balance in signed networks. Section 3 presents the signed block
\beta
-model, establishes its identifiability conditions, and develops an efficient alternative updating scheme to tackle the resulting optimization task. Section 4 provides a rigorous theoretical analysis of the proposed signed block
\beta
-model and quantifies its asymptotic behaviors in both probability estimation and community detection. Section 5 examines the numeric performance of the signed block
\beta
-model via extensive experiments on both synthetic and real-world signed networks. Finally, Section 6 concludes the paper with a brief summary. Technical proofs of the main theorems are provided in the Appendix, whereas auxiliary lemmas are contained in the Supplementary Material.

2
Signed network and balance theory

A signed network
\mathcal{G}
can be represented via its symmetric adjacency matrix
\bm{A}=(a_{ij})_{i,j\in[n]}
with
a_{ij}\in\{1,-1,0\}
, where
a_{ij}=1
indicates a positive edge between nodes
i
and
j
,
a_{ij}=-1
indicates a negative edge and
a_{ij}=0
indicates no edge at all. For each
i,j\in[n]
, let
a_{ij}
be sampled from a multinomial distribution with parameters
(p_{ij}^{+},p_{ij}^{-},p_{ij}^{0})
. One of the most salient features of signed network is the widely observed balance structure
[
13
;
6
]
, positing that relationships among entities tend toward balanced sign configurations.

Definition 1
(Strong balance)

A signed network is strong-balanced if for any triple
(i,j,k)
,

\underset{(a,b,c)\in\{-1,1\}^{3}}{\arg\max}\mathbb{P}\big(a_{ij}=a,a_{jk}=b,a_{ki}=c\big)

does not have 0 or 2 positive edges.

Definition
1
assures that a strong-balanced signed network does not contain a triad achieving the highest probability at triad B or D as shown in Figure
1
. An equivalent definition has also been introduced in
32
, which quantifies the probabilistic strong balance for signed networks. Moreover, Definition
1
can be extended to define weak-balanced signed networks.

Definition 2
(Weak balance)

A signed network is weak-balanced if for any triple
(i,j,k)
,

\underset{(a,b,c)\in\{-1,1\}^{3}}{\arg\max}\mathbb{P}\big(a_{ij}=a,a_{jk}=b,a_{ki}=c\big)

does not have exactly 2 positive edges.

Definition
2
gives a probabilistic definition of weak balance for signed networks. It only restricts the occurrence of triad B, and thus aligns with the non-probabilistic definitions of weak balance in the existing literature
[
8
]
.

Proposition 1

A signed network
\mathcal{G}
is strong-balanced if and only if

(i) For any
(i,j,k)
,
(p_{ij}^{+}-p_{ij}^{-})(p_{jk}^{+}-p_{jk}^{-})(p_{ki}^{+}-p_{ki}^{-})>0
; or

(ii) There exists a partition
[n]=C_{1}\cup C_{2}
such that for each
i,j\in[n]
,
p_{ij}^{+}>p_{ij}^{-}
if
i,j
belong to the same subset, and
p_{ij}^{+}<p_{ij}^{-}
, if
i,j
belong to different subsets.

Proposition 2

A signed network
\mathcal{G}
is weak-balanced if and only if

(i) For any
(i,j,k)
,
(p_{ij}^{+}-p_{ij}^{-})(p_{jk}^{+}-p_{jk}^{-})(p_{ki}^{+}-p_{ki}^{-})>0
or
p_{ij}^{+}-p_{ij}^{-}<0,p_{jk}^{+}-p_{jk}^{-}<0,p_{ki}^{+}-p_{ki}^{-}<0
; or

(ii) There exists a partition of
[n]=C_{1}\cup C_{2}\cup...\cup C_{K}
with
K\geq 3
such that for each
i,j\in[n]
,
p_{ij}^{+}>p_{ij}^{-}
if
i,j
belong to the same subset, and
p_{ij}^{+}<p_{ij}^{-}
, if
i,j
belong to different subsets.

Propositions
1
and
2
characterize the strong and weak balance structures in signed networks from both local and global perspectives. In fact, the local perspective (i) in Proposition
1
is essentially equivalent to
\mathbb{E}(a_{ij}a_{jk}a_{ki}\big||a_{ij}a_{jk}a_{ki}|=1)>0
for any
(i,j,k)
, which is exactly the definition of the strong-balanced signed network in
32
, whereas (i) in Proposition
2
can be regarded as its analogy for the weak-balanced signed network. On the other hand, (ii) in Proposition
1
and (ii) in Proposition
2
give the global perspectives on strong and weak balance structures, which greatly facilitate the modeling of community structures in signed networks.

3
Signed block
\beta
-model

We propose a signed block
\beta
-model, denoted as SBBM, to leverage the global perspectives of the balance structures in signed networks. Particularly, let
\psi_{i}\in[K]
denote the community membership of node
i
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(a_{ij}=1)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{e^{\beta_{i}^{+}+\beta_{j}^{+}}}{e^{\beta_{i}^{+}+\beta_{j}^{+}}+e^{\beta_{i}^{-}+\beta_{j}^{-}}+1},\ \text{if\ }\psi_{i}=\psi_{j}\\
\frac{e^{\gamma_{i}^{+}+\gamma_{j}^{+}}}{e^{\gamma_{i}^{+}+\gamma_{j}^{+}}+e^{\gamma_{i}^{-}+\gamma_{j}^{-}}+1},\ \text{if\ }\psi_{i}\neq\psi_{j}\end{cases},

(1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(a_{ij}=-1)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{e^{\beta_{i}^{-}+\beta_{j}^{-}}}{e^{\beta_{i}^{+}+\beta_{j}^{+}}+e^{\beta_{i}^{-}+\beta_{j}^{-}}+1},\ \text{if\ }\psi_{i}=\psi_{j}\\
\frac{e^{\gamma_{i}^{-}+\gamma_{j}^{-}}}{e^{\gamma_{i}^{+}+\gamma_{j}^{+}}+e^{\gamma_{i}^{-}+\gamma_{j}^{-}}+1},\ \text{if\ }\psi_{i}\neq\psi_{j}\end{cases},

for each
1\leq i\leq j\leq n
, and
\mathbb{P}(a_{ij}=0)=1-\mathbb{P}(a_{ij}=1)-\mathbb{P}(a_{ij}=-1)
. It is clear that SBBM associates each node
i
with two sets of parameters
(\beta_{i}^{+},\beta_{i}^{-})
and
(\gamma_{i}^{+},\gamma_{i}^{-})
, where
(\beta_{i}^{+},\beta_{i}^{-})
quantifies the node’s tendency to form positive and negative edges within the same community, and
(\gamma_{i}^{+},\gamma_{i}^{-})
characterizes the corresponding tendencies across different communities.

The proposed SBBM model has a number of compelling features. First, it integrates the capability of community detection while simultaneously capturing node-specific heterogeneity in signed networks. When
K=1
and
\beta_{i}^{-}=-\infty
, SBBM reduces to the classical
\beta
-model, which is commonly used to capture node-specific heterogeneity. In the absence of negative edges, SBBM simplifies to the stochastic block model (SBM) with heterogeneity for community detection. Therefore, SBBM provides a general framework for both unsigned and signed networks, simultaneously addressing community detection, node-specific heterogeneity and signed relationships in network data. Second, both strong and weak balance can be incorporated in SBBM by simply requiring

\beta_{i}^{+}>\beta_{i}^{-}\mbox{ and }\gamma_{i}^{+}<\gamma_{i}^{-}\ \text{ for any }i\in[n].

(2)

It can be readily verified that nodes
i
and
j
belong to the same community if and only if
\mathbb{P}(a_{ij}=1)>\mathbb{P}(a_{ij}=-1)
, and different communities if and only if
\mathbb{P}(a_{ij}=1)<\mathbb{P}(a_{ij}=-1)
. It follows from Propositions
1
and
2
that SBBM with constraints (
2
) satisfies strong balance when
K=2
and weak balance when
K\geq 3
.

\eta_{1}^{-}=0

4
Theory

In this section, we establish the asymptotic properties of the proposed SBBM in terms of both probability estimation and community detection. Denote the true parameters as
(\bm{\beta}^{*+},\bm{\beta}^{*-},\bm{\gamma}^{*+},\bm{\gamma}^{*-},\bm{Z}^{*})\in\mathcal{P}

\bm{\beta}^{*+}>\bm{\beta}^{*-}

\bm{\gamma}^{*+}<\bm{\gamma}^{*-}

\bm{\Theta}^{*}=(\bm{\Theta}^{*+},\bm{\Theta}^{*-})

4.1
Consistency in probability estimation

For each
i,j\in[n]
, denote the negative log-likelihood of
a_{ij}
as

L(\theta_{ij};a_{ij})=-(a_{ij}^{+}\theta_{ij}^{+}+a_{ij}^{-}\theta_{ij}^{-})+\log\big(1+e^{\theta_{ij}^{+}}+e^{\theta_{ij}^{-}}\big),

where
\theta_{ij}=(\theta_{ij}^{+},\theta_{ij}^{-})

L(\bm{\Theta};\bm{A})=\frac{2}{n(n+1)}\sum_{i\leq j}L(\theta_{ij};a_{ij})
. Let
e_{L}(\bm{\Theta},\bm{\Theta}^{*})=\mathbb{E}\big(L(\bm{\Theta};\bm{A})-L(\bm{\Theta}^{*};\bm{A})\big)

\bm{\Theta}

\bm{\Theta}^{*}
.

Assumption 1

There exist constants
M_{1}<M_{2}
such that both
\theta_{ij}^{+}
and
\theta_{ij}^{-}
are contained in
[M_{1},M_{2}]
for each
i,j\in[n]
.

Assumption
1
is common in the literature of network modeling. It allows different magnitudes of
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, leading to highly heterogeneous connecting probabilities. This is in sharp contrast to the homogeneous assumption on connecting probabilities in most existing literature on sparse networks, where
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are essentially of the same magnitude
[
37
;
24
;
22
;
41
]
.

Proposition 3

For any
\bm{\Theta}
that satisfies Assumption
1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{2}{n(n+1)}\sum_{i\leq j}\mathbb{E}[L(\theta_{ij},a_{ij})-L(\theta^{*}_{ij},a_{ij})]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 48e^{\max\{|M_{1}|,|M_{2}|\}}\min\{2e^{M_{2}}+e^{2M_{2}},1\}e_{L}(\bm{\Theta},\bm{\Theta}^{*}),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{2}}\big\|{\bm{\Theta}}-\bm{\Theta}^{*}\big\|^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle\leq 48e^{\max\{|M_{1}|,|M_{2}|\}}e_{L}(\bm{\Theta},\bm{\Theta}^{*}).

Proposition
3
paves a bridge between the first and second moments of
L(\bm{\Theta},\bm{A})-L(\bm{\Theta}^{*},\bm{A})
, which is crucial for establishing the weak consistency of
\widehat{\bm{\Theta}}
in terms of
e_{L}(\bm{\Theta},\bm{\Theta}^{*})
. Furthermore, it also relates
\big\|{\bm{\Theta}}-\bm{\Theta}^{*}\big\|^{2}_{F}
to
e_{L}(\bm{\Theta},\bm{\Theta}^{*})
, leading to the strong consistency of
\widehat{\bm{\Theta}}
established in the following Theorem
2
.

Theorem 2

Suppose Assumption
1
holds. Then there exists a local minimizer of (
3
), denoted as
(\widehat{\bm{\gamma}}^{+},\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{+},\widehat{\bm{\gamma}}^{-},\widehat{\bm{\eta}}^{-},\widehat{\bm{Z}})
, such that the corresponding
\widehat{\bm{\Theta}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\ P\Big(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\big\|\widehat{\bm{\Theta}}-\bm{\Theta}^{*}\big\|^{2}_{F}\geq 48e^{\max\{|M_{1}|,|M_{2}|\}}\epsilon_{n}^{2}\Big)\leq 2\exp(-c_{1}Kn\log n),

when
n
is large enough, provided that
\epsilon_{n}^{2}\simeq\frac{Ke^{\max\{|M_{1}|,|M_{2}|\}}\min\{2e^{M_{2}}+e^{2M_{2}},1\}\log n}{n}\prec 1
.

Theorem
2
guarantees that
\bm{\Theta}^{*}
can be consistently estimated if
K\simeq 1
and
\min\{2e^{M_{2}}+e^{2M_{2}},1\}e^{2\max\{|M_{1}|,|M_{2}|\}}n^{-1}\log n
is vanishing. In particular, if
M_{2}-M_{1}\preceq 1
and both of them diverge to
-\infty
, then it is required that
\min\{p_{ij}^{*+},p_{ij}^{*-}\}\succ\frac{\log n}{n}
for each
i,j\in[n]
, which matches the best sparsity requirement in the literature
[
23
;
25
;
37
]
. More importantly, when
M_{2}-M_{1}\succ 1
,
p_{ij}^{*+}
and
p_{ij}^{*-}
can behave differently to accommodate edge heterogeneity. Specifically, letting
M_{1}\to-\infty
and
M_{2}\to\infty
, Theorem
2
still holds when
e^{M_{1}}\succ\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}
and
e^{M_{2}}\prec\sqrt{\frac{n}{\log n}}
, resulting in a heterogeneous case with
p_{ij}^{*+},p_{ij}^{*-}\in(n^{-1/2}(\log n)^{1/2},1)
for each
i,j\in[n]
. This is consistent with the heterogeneous result for the latent space model
[
25
;
40
]
, and better than that for the classical
\beta
-model
[
30
]
, where the connecting probabilities can only vary in
(n^{-1/4},1)
.

4.2
Consistency in community detection

We next turn to establish the asymptotic properties of
\widehat{\bm{Z}}
, whose community detection error is defined as

\displaystyle d(\widehat{\bm{Z}},\bm{Z}^{*})=\min_{\bm{\Pi}}\frac{1}{n}\|\widehat{\bm{Z}}\bm{\Pi}-\bm{Z}^{*}\|_{F}^{2},

(4)

where minimization is taken over all permutation matrices
\bm{\Pi}\in\{0,1\}^{K\times K}
. The quantity
d(\widehat{\bm{Z}},\bm{Z}^{*})
measures the minimal fraction of misclassified nodes, up to permutation, and is commonly used in the community detection literature
[
23
]
.

Assumption 2

There exists a constant
M_{\gamma}
such that
\|\bm{\gamma}\|_{\infty}\leq M_{\gamma}
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{i}^{*+}=\big\{j\in[n]:\ |\beta_{i}^{*+}+\beta_{j}^{*+}|<2M_{\gamma}+c_{\eta}\big\}
and
\mathcal{S}_{i}^{*-}=\big\{j\in[n]:\ |\beta_{i}^{*-}+\beta_{j}^{*-}|<2M_{\gamma}+c_{\eta}\big\}
for some
c_{\eta}>0
, then
\max_{i\in[n]}|\mathcal{S}_{i}^{*+}|\prec\frac{n}{K}
or
\max_{i\in[n]}|\mathcal{S}_{i}^{*-}|\prec\frac{n}{K}
.

Assumption
2
assures that the number of pairs of
(i,j)
satisfying
|\beta_{i}^{*+}+\beta_{j}^{*+}|<2M_{\gamma}+c_{\eta}
or
|\beta_{i}^{*-}+\beta_{j}^{*-}|<2M_{\gamma}+c_{\eta}
is substantially less than
n/K
. Note that
|\beta_{i}^{*+}+\beta_{j}^{*+}|\geq 2M_{\gamma}+c_{\eta}
and
|\beta_{i}^{*-}+\beta_{j}^{*-}|\geq 2M_{\gamma}+c_{\eta}
imply that
\min\{|\eta_{i}^{*+}+\eta_{j}^{*+}|,|\eta_{i}^{*-}+\eta_{j}^{*-}|\}\geq c_{\eta}
, thus
c_{\eta}
can be regarded as a measurement of the difference in the level of connection probability within the same community and those across different communities. For any
(\bm{\gamma}^{+},\bm{\gamma}^{-},\bm{\eta}^{+},,\bm{\eta}^{-},\bm{Z})\in\mathcal{P}
, let
\mathcal{N}_{k}=\{i\in[n]:z_{ik}=1\}
and
n_{k}=|\mathcal{N}_{k}|
for each
k\in[K]
.

Proposition 4

Suppose all assumptions in Theorem
2
and Assumption
2
hold, and that
K^{2}c_{\eta}^{-2}e^{\max\{|M_{1}|,|M_{2}|\}}\epsilon_{n}^{2}\prec 1
. Then, with probability at least
1-2\exp(-c_{1}Kn\log n)
, there does not exist
k_{1}\neq k_{2}\in[K]
and
k\in[K]
such that
|\mathcal{N}_{k_{1}}^{*}\cap\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{k}|\simeq|\mathcal{N}_{k_{2}}^{*}\cap\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{k}|\simeq\frac{n}{K}
.

The requirement of
K^{2}c_{\eta}^{-2}e^{\max\{|M_{1}|,|M_{2}|\}}\epsilon_{n}^{2}\prec 1
implies that
c_{\eta}
can not be too small, which can be interpreted as a minimal signal strength condition. Proposition
4
assures that any estimated community
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{k}
does not substantially overlap with more than one true community with high probability, which further leads to the estimation consistency of
\widehat{\bm{Z}}
as shown in Theorem
3
.

Theorem 3

Suppose all assumptions in Proposition
4
hold, then there exists a positive constant
c_{2}
such that when
n
is sufficiently large,

\displaystyle P\big(d(\widehat{\bm{Z}},\bm{Z}^{*})\geq c_{2}Kc_{\eta}^{-2}e^{\max\{|M_{1}|,|M_{2}|\}}\epsilon_{n}^{2}\big)\leq 2\exp(-c_{1}Kn\log n).

Theorem
3
establishes the convergence rate of
d(\widehat{\bm{Z}},\bm{Z}^{*})
for community detection. Particularly, in the homogeneous case with
\max\{|M_{1}|,|M_{2}|,K,c_{\eta}\}\simeq 1
, the convergence rate becomes
n^{-1}\log n
, which matches with the best community detection result in literature
[
23
;
37
]
. In the heterogeneous case with
e^{M_{1}}\succ\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}
and
e^{M_{2}}\prec\sqrt{\frac{n}{\log n}}
where
p_{ij}^{*}
is allowed to vary in
(n^{-1/2}(\log n)^{1/2},1)
for each
i\leq j
,
c_{\eta}
can diverge to infinity at a rate no faster than
\log n
and Theorem
3
continues to hold with
d(\widehat{\bm{Z}},\bm{Z}^{*})=O_{p}\big((\log n)^{-2}\big)
.

5
Numerical experiments

In this section, we examine the numerical performance of the proposed SBBM model, and compare it against three popular competitors in the literature, including the signed Laplacian-based method
[
20
]
, the SPONGE algorithm based on signed SBM
[
5
]
, and the joint estimation method based on the joint inner product model
[
32
]
, denoted as SLP, SPONGE and JIM, respectively. All methods are evaluated in terms of community detection accuracy, while JIM and SBBM are further compared in terms of probability estimation.

Note that the community detection error in (
4
) requires enumeration of all possible permutations, which is computationally infeasible. We thus employ the clustering error in
33
to evaluate the community detection accuracy. Particularly, let
\bm{\psi}^{*}=(\psi_{1}^{*},\dots,\psi_{n}^{*})
and
\widehat{\bm{\psi}}=(\widehat{\psi}_{1},\dots,\widehat{\psi}_{n})
denote the true and estimated community assignments, then the community detection error of
\widehat{\bm{\psi}}
can be evaluated as

\text{Err}(\widehat{\bm{\psi}},\bm{\psi}^{*})=\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i<j}\mathbb{I}\big(\mathbb{I}(\psi_{i}^{*}=\psi_{j}^{*})+\mathbb{I}(\widehat{\psi}_{i}=\widehat{\psi}_{j})=1\big).

Further, we define the estimation error of the probability matrix
\bm{P}^{*+}=(p^{*+}_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
and
\bm{P}^{*-}=(p^{*-}_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
as

\text{Err}(\widehat{\bm{P}}^{+},\bm{P}^{*+})=\frac{\|\widehat{\bm{P}}^{+}-\bm{P}^{*+}\|_{F}^{2}}{\|\bm{P}^{*+}\|_{F}^{2}},\mbox{ and }\text{Err}(\widehat{\bm{P}}^{-},\bm{P}^{*-})=\frac{\|\widehat{\bm{P}}^{-}-\bm{P}^{*-}\|_{F}^{2}}{\|\bm{P}^{*-}\|_{F}^{2}}.

5.1
Simulated examples

Three simulated signed networks are considered.

Example 1.
Following the data generating scheme in
32
, we first generate
\bm{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times 2}
with rows independently set to
(1,0)
or
(0,1)
with equal probability
0.5
, and then set
\bm{X}^{*}=(\bm{I}_{n}-\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top})\bm{X}
. For each node
i\in[n]
, we generate
\alpha_{i}^{*}\sim\mathrm{Unif}(1,3)
and let
v_{i}^{*}=(1,-1)^{\top}\bm{x}_{i}^{*}/\sqrt{2}
, where
\bm{x}_{i}^{*}
denotes the
i
-th row of
\bm{X}^{*}
. Edge probabilities are then specified by
\Pr(|A_{ij}|=1)=\sigma(\mu^{*}+\alpha_{i}^{*}+\alpha_{j}^{*}-2\bar{\alpha}^{*}+\bm{x}_{i}^{*\top}\bm{x}_{j}^{*})
and
\Pr(A_{ij}=1\mid|A_{ij}|=1)=\sigma(v_{i}^{*}v_{j}^{*})
, where
\bar{\alpha}^{*}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}^{*}
and
\sigma(x)=(1+e^{-x})^{-1}
. Varying
\mu^{*}\in\{-3.5,-3.0,-2.5\}

Example 2.

K
communities uniformly. The matrices

and
\bm{P}^{*}

) and the signed networks are generated accordingly, resulting in weak-balanced signed networks with different numbers of communities.

Example 3.

K=4
. For each
i\in[n]
, we generate

,
\gamma_{i}^{*-}\sim\mathcal{N}(-3,\sigma^{2})
with

\delta_{i}\sim U(0,2)
and set
\beta_{i}^{*+}=\beta_{i}^{*-}+\delta_{i}
and
\gamma_{i}^{*+}=\gamma_{i}^{*-}-\delta_{i}

It contains 2,756 strong-balanced triads, 3,559 weak-balanced triads, and 847 unbalanced triads. This substantial deviation from randomness indicates a pronounced weak balance structure in the international relation network.

12

k^{+}_{i}=\sum_{j\in[n]}a^{+}_{ij}

where
\delta(\psi_{i},\psi_{j})=1
if
\psi_{i}=\psi_{j}
and
0
otherwise. Note that

takes values in

6
Summary

This paper proposes a novel signed block
\beta
-model to model the strong and weak balance structures in signed networks simultaneously. It introduces four sets of parameters that distinguish each node’s connection tendencies within its community and across different communities, which greatly facilitates community detection in signed networks with balance structures and node heterogeneity. We establish the identifiability conditions for the signed block
\beta
-model, and develop an efficient alternative updating algorithm for parameter estimation and community detection. Its asymptotic properties are also established in terms of both probability estimation and community detection, which are further supported by numerical experiments on both synthetic and real-world experiments. Note that statistical inference about the signed block
\beta
-model is also of interest but possibly hindered by the discrete feature of the community structure, and we leave it to future investigation.

On 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} -Order of Polynomials and Properties of r r -Primitive and k k -Normal Elements over Finite Fields
Maithri K, Vadiraja Bhatta G. R, Indira K. P, Prasanna Poojary

Abstract
Polynomials and elements over finite fields exhibit closely related algebraic structures, and many properties defined for elements extend naturally to polynomials. The concepts of order and 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} -Order for elements have been extensively studied. In this paper, we investigate several properties of r r -primitive and k k -normal elements. Furthermore, by using the concept of the 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} -Order of a polynomial, we explore properties of k k -normal polynomials.



Manipal Institute of Technology, MAHE, Manipal, Karnataka, India

Let

For

of

. An element with order

is called

. More generally, an element is called

-primitive

ord(\alpha)=\frac{q^{n}-1}{r},

where

divides

2.2
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Order and

-normal elements

Let

denote the Frobenius automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Order
of
\alpha

of least degree such that
g(\sigma_{q})(\alpha)=0
. Equivalently, it is the minimal polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
that annihilates

x^{n}-1
.

An element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
is called
normal
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

\{\alpha,\alpha^{q},\dots,\alpha^{q^{n-1}}\}

\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Mullen et al.
[
9
]
introduced the concept of

-normal elements
which is defined by the condition

\deg(Ord(\alpha))=n-k,

which is equivalent to

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\langle\alpha,\alpha^{q},\dots,\alpha^{q^{n-1}}\rangle=n-k.

The case
k=0
corresponds to normal elements.

3
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Order of a Polynomial

For elements of finite fields, the multiplicative order and the

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Order display closely related behavior; in particular,
both notions are governed by analogous structural principles. A similar
phenomenon occurs for polynomials over finite fields. The concept of the
order of a polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
is well established: the
order of a polynomial
f(x)
is defined as the least positive integer

e
such that

f(x)\mid(x^{e}-1).

Motivated by this analogy, we introduce the notion of the

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Order of a polynomial and investigate its fundamental
properties. We show that, for polynomials over finite fields, the notions
of order and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Order exhibit parallel structural behavior,
closely mirroring the relationship observed in the case of field
elements.

The origin of this idea can be traced back to the work of Ore
[
1
]
,
who introduced polynomials belonging to the class of
p
-polynomials.
However, a systematic and detailed study of this concept has remained
limited. In this work, we develop a more formal treatment within the
modern framework of finite fields, interpreting this notion as the

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Order of a polynomial, with a suitable refinement of the
classical definition.

Definition 2
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be a polynomial of degree
m\geq 1
. Then
g
is said to be
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Order of
f
if
g
is the monic least degree polynomial such that
f|g\circ x
. We denote it by
Ord(f)
.

Example 3
.

Consider the field
\mathbb{F}_{5^{2}}
. Let
f=x+1
. Then
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Order of
f
is
x-1
, since it is the least degree polynomial such that
f
divides
x^{5}-x
.

When considering an irreducible polynomial, the following result shows the relationship between the polynomial’s
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Order and its root. They will actually have equal
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Orders.

Proposition 4
.

If
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
is irreducible polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of degree
m
, then
Ord(f)=Ord(\alpha)
, where
\alpha
is the root of
f
.

Proof.

Let
Ord(f)=g
, then by definition
f|g\circ x
, which implies
g\circ x=f(x).h(x)
, for some polynomial
h(x)
. Since
\alpha
is the root of
f
,
g\circ\alpha=0
.

Let
k
be a polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, with degree less than degree of
g
and
h\circ\alpha=0
. Which implies that
h\circ\alpha^{q^{i}}=0,\forall~i=1,2,\dots,m
, from which we get
f|h\circ x
, a contradiction. Thus
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-orders of
\alpha=g
.
∎

Corollary 5
.

If
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
is irreducible polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of degree
m
, then
Ord(f)|x^{m}-1
.

Proposition 6
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be a polynomial such that
f|g\circ x
for some
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
if and only if
Ord(f)|g
.

Proof.

Let
h=Ord(f)|g
, then
f|h\circ x
. Since
h\circ x|g\circ x
, we get
f|g\circ x
.

Conversly, let
f|g\circ x
, then
deg(g)\geq deg(h)
. Thus
g=hk+r
, for some
k,r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
with
0\leq deg(r)<deg(h)
. Now,
g\circ x=(hk)\circ x+r\circ x
. Also,
f|g\circ x
and
f|(hk)\circ x
. Thus
f|r\circ x
, which implies
r\circ x=0
, which implies
r=0
. Thus
h|g
.
∎

Corollary 7
.

If
h,g
are two non-constant polynomials. Then
gcd(h\circ x,g\circ x)=k\circ x
, where
k=gcd(h,g)
.

Now we consider irreducible polynomials of various type and see what happens to their
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Order.

Proposition 8
.

Let
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
f=g^{b}
for some positive integer
b
. Let
t
be the smallest positive integer with
p^{t}\geq b
, where p is the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
Ord(f)=Ord(g)x^{t}
.

Proof.

Let
Ord(g)=h
and
Ord(f)=k
. Then,
g|h\circ x
, which implies
g^{b}|(h\circ x)^{b}
,
g^{b}|(h\circ x)^{q^{t}}
and then
g^{b}|h\circ x^{q^{t}}
. Using
g^{b}=f
, we get
f|h\circ x^{q^{t}}
. Thus,
Ord(f)|hx^{t}
.

Let
deg(h)=s
. We have,
g|h\circ x,
which implies,
g^{b}|(h\circ x)^{b}
, where
deg((h\circ x)^{b})=q^{s}b
.
Let
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{m}
be distinct roots of
g
. We know that the roots of
h
forms a subspace U. Since
h
is the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Order of
g
, U is the smallest subspace containing
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{m}
.
Let
L
be the least degree linearized polynomial such that
g^{b}|L
(such a polynomial exits as
g^{b}|(h\circ x)^{q^{t}}
). Then
L
should contain the factor
(x-\alpha_{i})^{q^{t}}
.
Since roots of
L
forms a subspace
V
and
L
is the least degree linearized polynomial with
g^{b}|L
,
V=U
which gives
q^{s}b\leq deg(L)
. By the choice of
t
,
q^{s}q^{t}\leq deg(L)
. Also, from proposition
6
,
we have
L|(hx^{t})\circ x
. Thus,
q^{s}q^{t}\geq deg(L)\implies L=(hx^{t})\circ x
. Thus
Ord(f)=Ord(g)x^{t}
.
∎

Proposition 9
.

Let
g_{1},g_{2},\dots,g_{k}
be pairwise relatively prime polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, and
f=g_{1}g_{2}\dots g_{k}
. Then
Ord(f)=lcm\{Ord(g_{1}),Ord(g_{2}),\dots,Ord(g_{k})\}
.

Proof.

Let
Ord(g_{i})=h_{i}~\forall~i,1\leq i\leq k
and
l=lcm\{h_{1},h_{2},\dots h_{k}\}
, then
h_{i}|l~\forall~i
, further
H_{i}|l\circ x~\forall~i
, where
H_{i}=h_{i}\circ x
.

We have,
g_{i}|H_{i}~\forall~i
, which implies that
g_{1}g_{2}\dots g_{k}|lcm\{H_{1},H_{2},\dots,H_{k}\}|l\circ x
. Thus,
f|l\circ x
, which implies
Ord(f)|l
.

Let
Ord(f)=j
, then
f|j\circ x
, which implies
g_{i}|j\circ x~\forall~i
. Since
Ord(g_{i})=h_{i},h_{i}|j~\forall~i
. which implies
lcm\{h_{1},h_{2},\dots,h_{k}\}|j=Ord(f)
.
∎

If the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Order of an element in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
is

Proposition 10
.

A polynomial

of degree m is normal over

is monic and

Proof.

Let

be a normal polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, and
g=Ord(f)
. Which implies
f|g\circ x
.

Let
\alpha
be the root of
f
, then
Ord(\alpha)=x^{m}-1
. Since
f|g\circ x,~g\circ\alpha=0\implies g=Ord(f)=x^{m}-1
.

Conversely, Let
Ord(f)=x^{m}-1
. Suppose
f
is reducible, then either
f=g^{b}
, for some irreducible polynomial
g
, or
f=gh
with
g,h
irreducible or
f=g^{a}h^{b}
for some relatively prime polynomials
g,h
with
a,b\geq 1

Suppose
f=g^{b}
, then by proposition
8
,
x|Ord(f)\implies x|x^{m}-1
a contradiction.

Suppose
f=g^{a}h^{b}
, with
g^{a}
and
h^{b}
being relatively prime and

then
Ord(f)=lcm(Ord(g^{a}),Ord(h^{b}))
, a multiple of
x
from proposition
9
which implies
x|x^{m}-1
, a contradiction.

Suppose
f=gh
with
f,g
being irreducible,
deg(g)=e_{1}
and
deg(h)=e_{2}
then
m=e_{1}+e_{2}
.We know that
x^{m}-1=Ord(f)=lcm(Ord(g),Ord(h))
.
Now by corollary
5

Ord(g)|x^{e_{1}}-1,Ord(h)|x^{e_{2}}-1
. So
lcm(Ord(g),Ord(h))|lcm(x^{e}_{1}-1,x^{e}_{2}-1)
which implies
x^{m}-1|lcm(x^{e}_{1}-1,x^{e}_{2}-1)
which gives,
m<e_{1}+e_{2}
a contradiction.

Therefore
f
is a irreducible polynomial over
F_{q}
. So by preposition
4
, we have
F_{q}
-Order of all of its roots is
x^{m}-1
. Hence the roots are normal elements. Hence

is normal polynomial.
∎

a

Proposition 20

Proposition 21
.

Let

By the first isomorphism theorem,

\frac{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}{\ker\sigma_{f}}\cong\operatorname{Im}(\sigma_{f})=B.

Therefore,

∎

5
Conclusions

This paper explored the theory of
r
-primitive and
k
-normal elements over finite fields and extended these ideas to the polynomial setting through the notion of the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Order. By relating the

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Order of an irreducible polynomial to that of its roots, we reinforced the close connection between element-based and polynomial-based approaches. This perspective presents a unified and systematic framework for studying irreducible polynomials with specified
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-order, resulting in explicit enumeration outcomes. It produces precise counting formulas for
k
-normal polynomials with a fixed degree. Additionally, order-based and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Order based partitions were analyzed, yielding alternative derivations of classical identities
associated with the Euler phi function and its polynomial counterpart. We also discussed some algebraic and combinatorial properties of

r
-primitive and
k
-normal elements.

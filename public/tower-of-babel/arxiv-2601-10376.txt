A Hybrid Reliability-Weight Framework for Construction of Polar Codes
Mohammad Rowshan, Vlad-Florin Drăgoi

Abstract
Polar codes are usually constructed by ranking synthetic bit-channels according to reliability, which guarantees capacity-achieving behavior but can yield poor low-weight spectra at short and moderate lengths. Recent algebraic results express the contribution of individual bit-channels to the multiplicities of minimum and near-minimum weight codewords in closed form. In this work we combine these insights into a mixed (reliability–weight) bit-channel ordering. We define a per-bit cost whose distance term is derived from orbit enumeration of minimum-weight codewords and scaled by a Bhattacharyya-type factor, and show that the resulting mixed construction minimizes a truncated SC/ML union-bound surrogate within a class of decreasing monomial codes. We relate the mixed metric to error events in SCL decoding via a pruning/ML decomposition, and prove that mixed designs act as local perturbations of reliability-based constructions whose asymptotic impact vanishes as code-length approaches infinity. Numerical results for short and moderate lengths on BPSK-AWGN, implemented via Gaussian approximation and closed-form weight contributions, illustrate the trade-off between pure reliability-based and mixed constructions in terms of minimum distance, multiplicity, and union-bound approximations. All proofs are deferred to the appendices.



I
Introduction

Polar codes
[
3
]
achieve the capacity of any binary-input memoryless symmetric (BMS) channel with low-complexity encoding and successive-cancellation (SC) decoding. Practical constructions order synthetic channels by reliability, obtained via density evolution (DE), Gaussian approximation (GA)
[
16
,
6
,
30
]
, or Tal-Vardy construction
[
28
]
. At short and moderate blocklengths, however, such purely reliability-based designs may have poor low-weight spectra, making them suboptimal under maximum-likelihood (ML) or list decoding compared to, e.g., Reed–Muller codes. Many search methods for good polar codes exist, but they lie outside the scope of this work due to their high computational cost.

The algebraic description of polar codes as
decreasing monomial codes

[
4
]
enables closed-form expressions for the minimum distance and the multiplicity of minimum (and some near-minimum) weight codewords via the action of the lower triangular affine group
[
8
,
9
,
31
,
23
,
7
]
. These formulas naturally assign to each maximum-degree monomial a weight-contribution score measuring its impact on the minimum-weight spectrum, and have been used to propose weight-aware partial orders for low-rate polar-like codes
[
20
]
.

Most practical constructions use a
universal
reliability sequence
[
27
,
15
,
1
]
. This sequence can be improved considering the weight contribution of bit-channels as in
[
20
]
. This work raises the question of how to systematically combine reliability and distance information in a bit-wise design metric, especially for near-ML decoders such as SCL
[
29
]
. A code with good distance properties can then be further improved by outer concatenation with CRC
[
18
]
, convolutional pretransform in PAC coding
[
2
,
19
]
and profile shifted PAC (PS-PAC) coding
[
14
]
.

In this work we use the decreasing-monomial representation and closed-form expressions for the minimum distance
w_{\min}
and its multiplicity
A_{w_{\min}}
(via orbits of maximum-degree monomials) to define a
K
-dependent mixed per-bit cost that blends SC-based reliability with an orbit-based distance penalty. We interpret this score as minimising a scalarised SC/ML functional derived from a truncated union bound, show that sub-maximal degrees are asymptotically negligible so only maximum-degree monomials require a distance term, and relate the metric to SCL via a pruning/ML error decomposition. Mixed designs then appear as local perturbations of reliability-based constructions whose impact on SC and ML union bounds vanishes as
N\to\infty
, while numerical examples on BPSK-AWGN show clear finite-length gains in
w_{\min}
or
A_{w_{\min}}
for only modest reliability loss.

II
Preliminaries and Structural Tools

II-A

Notation and union bound

Let
\mathbb{F}_{2}
be the binary field and
N=2^{m}
the blocklength. Vectors in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}

are in bold, with Hamming weight
\mathrm{w}(\mathbf{c})
. A binary linear code

\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}
has minimum distance

w_{\min}(\mathcal{C})=\min\{\mathrm{w}(\mathbf{c}):\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{0\}\},

and weight enumerator
A_{w}=|\{\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}:\mathrm{w}(\mathbf{c})=w\}|
. For a BMS channel
W

and ML decoding, the union bound is

P_{B}^{\mathrm{ML}}(\mathcal{C},W)\leq\sum_{w=w_{\min}}^{N}A_{w}\,P_{w}(W),

(1)

where
P_{w}(W)
is the pairwise error probability against any weight-
w

competitor. For many BMS channels there exist constants
c(W)>0
and

\gamma(W)\in(0,1)
such that
P_{w}(W)\leq c(W)\gamma(W)^{w}
for all
w
.

II-B

Polar codes and bit-channel reliabilities

Polar codes of length
N=2^{m}
are generated by
G_{N}=G_{2}^{\otimes m}
with

G_{2}=\bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]

[
3
]
.
For a BMS channel
W
, polarization induces synthetic channels

W_{N}^{(i)}
with Bhattacharyya parameters
Z_{i}=Z(W_{N}^{(i)})
or estimated SC
bit-error probabilities
P_{i}^{\mathrm{SC}}

[
17
]
. A classical
construction chooses an information set
\mathcal{I}
of size
K
by taking the
K

indices with smallest
Z_{i}
. Under SC decoding,

P_{B}^{\mathrm{SC}}(\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I}),W)\leq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}Z_{i},

(2)

which motivates minimising
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}Z_{i}
.

II-C

Monomial representation and decreasing codes

Let
\mathbf{x}=(x_{0},\dots,x_{m-1})
and

\mathcal{R}_{m}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[x_{0},\dots,x_{m-1}]/(x_{i}^{2}-x_{i})_{i=0}^{m-1}
. A monomial
is
f=x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{s}}
with
0\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{s}
; its degree is

\deg(f)=s
and its index set is

\operatorname{ind}(f)=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{s}\}\subseteq\{0,\dots,m-1\}
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{m}
be the set
of all such monomials. Evaluation on all
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
gives

\operatorname{ev}(f)=(f(\mathbf{z}))_{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}
.

There is a standard bijection between indices
i\in\{0,\dots,N-1\}
and
monomials: for
f=\prod_{t\in S}x_{t}
, define
b_{t}=1
if
t\in S
and
b_{t}=0

otherwise, and set
i(f)=\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}b_{t}2^{t}
. Polar and Reed–Muller
codes are monomial codes under this mapping
[
5
]
.

Definition 1

(Monomial and decreasing monomial codes)
.

For
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{m}
, the associated monomial code is

\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})=\operatorname{span}\{\operatorname{ev}(f):f\in\mathcal{I}\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{N}
.
A partial order
\preceq
on
\mathcal{M}_{m}
is defined by monomial divisibility and
degree-preserving “shifts”
[
5
]
. A set

\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{m}
is
decreasing
if
f\in\mathcal{I}
and
g\preceq f

imply
g\in\mathcal{I}
. The code
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})
with
\mathcal{I}
decreasing is called a

decreasing monomial code
.

Under the index–monomial mapping, both polar codes and Reed–Muller codes
correspond to decreasing sets.

II-D

Minimum distance and orbit-based
A_{w_{\min}}

Let
r=\max_{f\in\mathcal{I}}\deg(f)
be the maximum degree in
\mathcal{I}
, and

\mathcal{I}_{r}(\mathcal{I})
the subset of degree-
r
monomials. Then the minimum distance of

\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})
depends only on
r
and is given by
[
5
,
13
]

w_{\min}(\mathcal{I})=2^{m-r}.

(3)

All minimum-weight codewords arise from the orbits of degree-
r
monomials under the lower triangular affine group
\mathrm{LTA}(m,2)
.
For
f=x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{r}}\in\mathcal{I}_{r}
with
i_{1}<\dots<i_{r}
, define

|\lambda_{f}(x_{i_{\ell}})|\triangleq|\{j\in[0,i_{\ell}-1]:x_{j}\notin\operatorname{ind}(f)\}|,

|\lambda_{f}|\triangleq\sum_{\mathclap{x_{i}\in\operatorname{ind}(f)}}|\lambda_{f}(x_{i})|.

Then the orbit of
f
has size
2^{r+|\lambda_{f}|}
, and summing over

f\in\mathcal{I}_{r}(\mathcal{I})
yields the minimum-weight multiplicity

A_{w_{\min}}(\mathcal{I})=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{I}_{r}(\mathcal{I})}2^{r+|\lambda_{f}|},

(4)

which depends only on the subset
\mathcal{I}_{r}(\mathcal{I})
.

This naturally suggests assigning to each degree-
r
monomial a

minimum-weight contribution

\mathrm{contrib}_{w_{\min}}(f)=2^{r+|\lambda_{f}|}
.

III
Mixed Reliability–Weight Design

We now introduce a
K
-dependent mixed metric combining bit-channel reliability
with a distance penalty derived from (
4
). Throughout this
section we fix a length
N=2^{m}
, a dimension
K
, a BMS channel
W
, and a
decreasing monomial code
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{I})
of size
K
.

III-A

Bit-wise ML contribution and local distance

We first recall a coset-type notion of distance at the bit level.

Definition 2

(Bit-wise minimum distance and ML contribution)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\{0,1\}^{N}
be a binary code and
i\in\{0,\dots,N-1\}
. The

bit-wise minimum distance
of position
i
is

w_{\min}(i)=\min\{\mathrm{w}(\mathbf{c}):\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C},\ c_{i}=1\},

with the convention
w_{\min}(i)=+\infty
if no such codeword exists.

For a fixed BMS channel
W
, let
P_{w}(W)
denote the pairwise error
probability of any weight-
w
codeword. The
bit-wise ML contribution
of
index
i
is

\Phi^{\mathrm{ML}}(i)=\sum_{w\geq w_{\min}(i)}A_{w}^{(i)}\,P_{w}(W),

where
A_{w}^{(i)}
are per-index multiplicities from a fixed partition of the
ML union bound (
1
).

Thus
w_{\min}(i)
measures the minimum weight of any codeword involving index

i
, and
\Phi^{\mathrm{ML}}(i)
is the portion of the ML union bound that we
attribute to
i
.

III-B

Monomial-level weight penalty at fixed
K

Fix a decreasing monomial set
\mathcal{I}
of size
K
, with maximum degree

r=\max_{f\in\mathcal{I}}\deg(f)
. In the class of decreasing sets with the same
maximum degree
r
, the minimum distance
w_{\min}=2^{m-r}
is fixed, and only

A_{w_{\min}}
varies via the choice of
\mathcal{I}_{r}(\mathcal{I})
.

Definition 3

(Structural weight penalty)
.

For each index
i
with associated monomial
f_{i}
, define

C_{K}(i)=\begin{cases}2^{r+|\lambda_{f_{i}}|},&\text{if }\deg(f_{i})=r,\\[1.99997pt]
0,&\text{if }\deg(f_{i})\neq r,\end{cases}

(5)

so that
A_{w_{\min}}(\mathcal{I})=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}C_{K}(i)
.

Thus,
C_{K}(i)
is an per-bit weight contribution to the minimum-weight multiplicity. It is strictly increasing in
|\lambda_{f_{i}}|
; hence, consistent with the weight-contribution order in
[
22
]
.

III-C

Channel-dependent distance penalty and mixed cost

For a fixed BMS channel
W
we approximate the pairwise error probabilities in
the ML union bound by a Bhattacharyya-type exponential. In particular, we use

P_{w}(W)\approx Z(W)^{w},

where
Z(W)\in(0,1)
is the Bhattacharyya parameter of the physical channel.
Under this approximation, the minimum-weight term in the union bound scales as

A_{w_{\min}}P_{w_{\min}}(W)\approx A_{w_{\min}}Z(W)^{w_{\min}}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}C_{K}(i)\,Z(W)^{w_{\min}}.

This motivates weighting
C_{K}(i)
by
Z(W)^{w_{\min}}
and using the result as a
channel-dependent per-bit distance penalty.

Definition 4

(Mixed monomial cost)
.

For each index
i
define

D_{K}(i)\triangleq C_{K}(i)\,Z(W)^{w_{\min}},

(6)

and, for a tuning parameter
\alpha>0
,

J_{K}(i)\triangleq Z_{i}+\alpha\,D_{K}(i),

(7)

where
Z_{i}
is any chosen reliability metric (e.g., GA-based
P_{i}^{\mathrm{SC}}
). The parameter
\alpha
controls the
relative
weight of SC-type and ML-type contributions so that the spread of
\alpha D_{K}(i)
becomes comparable to or larger than the spread of
Z_{i}
, so the mixed ordering meaningfully re-ranks degree-
r
indices according to their minimum-weight contribution.

For
\deg(f_{i})<r
we have
C_{K}(i)=0
, hence
D_{K}(i)=0
; lower-degree monomials
are ordered purely by reliability. For maximum-degree monomials, the
reliability and distance terms interact through
J_{K}(i)
.

III-D

Mixed construction

For decreasing monomial sets
\mathcal{I}
with fixed maximum degree
r
, define the
mixed functional

F_{K}(\mathcal{I})\triangleq\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}J_{K}(i)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}Z_{i}+\alpha\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}D_{K}(i).

(8)

The first term approximates the SC union bound (
2
),
while, under the Bhattacharyya approximation, the second term is proportional
to the minimum-weight contribution
A_{w_{\min}}Z(W)^{w_{\min}}
in the ML union
bound.

Definition 5

(K-dependent mixed design)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{K}^{(r)}
be the family of decreasing monomial sets
\mathcal{I}

with
|\mathcal{I}|=K
and
\max_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\deg(f_{i})\leq r
. Any

\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{mix}}(K)\in\arg\min_{\mathcal{I}\in\mathcal{D}_{K}^{(r)}}F_{K}(\mathcal{I})

is called a
K-dependent mixed design
at maximum degree
r
.

In the numerical implementation (Section
VI
), we will
instantiate
Z_{i}
as GA-based SC error estimates and
Z(W)
using a simple
closed-form approximation for BPSK-AWGN;
\alpha
is treated as a tuning
parameter (e.g., a fixed constant such as
\alpha=100
) that controls the
relative emphasis on reliability vs distance.

III-E

ML-negligibility of sub-maximal degrees

We justify concentrating the distance penalty
D_{K}(i)
on maximum-degree
monomials by showing that lower-degree positions become asymptotically
irrelevant in the ML union bound.

Lemma 1

(Sub-maximal degrees are ML-negligible)
.

Let
W
be a fixed BMS channel. Assume there exist constants
c(W)>0
and

\gamma(W)\in(0,1)
such that

P_{w}(W)\leq c(W)\gamma(W)^{w}
for all
w\geq 1
. Let
N=2^{m}
and

w_{\min}=2^{m-r}
be the minimum distance of a decreasing monomial code with
maximum degree
r
. If

\deg(f_{i})\leq r-1\Rightarrow w_{\min}(i)\geq 2w_{\min},

\deg(f_{j})=r\Rightarrow w_{\min}(j)=w_{\min}

for some
j
, then

\frac{\Phi^{\mathrm{ML}}(i)}{\Phi^{\mathrm{ML}}(j)}\xrightarrow[m\to\infty]{}0\quad\text{for all }i\text{ with }\deg(f_{i})\leq r-1.

Intuitively, indices of degree
\leq r-1
only participate in codewords of
weight at least
2w_{\min}
and are penalised by an extra factor
\gamma(W)^{w_{\min}}
compared to degree-
r
indices.

Lemma
1
supports focusing the distance penalty
on maximum-degree monomials
f_{i}
and ranking sub-maximal degrees purely by
reliability.

III-F

Design-SNR–Driven Polar–to–RM Distance Transition

Theorem 1

(Design-SNR and min-distance staircase)
.

For
i\in\{0,\dots,N-1\},N=2^{m}
, let
D_{i}
be the Hamming weight of row
i
of
G_{N}
(equivalently
D_{i}=2^{\mathrm{w}(i)}
). Assume that the design-SNR-dependent Bhattacharyya parameters of the synthetic channels have the form

Z_{i}(\rho)=a_{i}e^{-cD_{i}\rho},\qquad a_{i}>0,\ c>0,\ \rho\geq 0,

(9)

(e.g., exact for BEC and a standard exponential approximation for BPSK-AWGN).
For each
\rho\geq 0
, let
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{rel}}(\rho,K)
be the set of

K
indices with smallest
Z_{i}(\rho)
, and denote

d(\rho)\triangleq\min_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{rel}}(\rho,K)}D_{i},\qquad w_{\min}(\rho)\triangleq d(\rho).

Then:

(i)

For any
0\leq\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}
, we have

w_{\min}(\rho_{2})\geq w_{\min}(\rho_{1})
. Moreover,
w_{\min}(\rho)
is
piecewise constant in
\rho
with finitely upward jumps; each jump
occurs exactly when the last selected bit-channel of some row-weight

D_{i}=d
leaves
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{rel}}(\rho,K)
, after which

w_{\min}(\rho)
increases to the next larger row-weight present in

\{D_{i}:i\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{rel}}(\rho,K)\}
.

(ii)

Let
r^{\star}
be the smallest integer such that

\sum_{t=0}^{r^{\star}}\binom{m}{t}\geq K
, and let

\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{RM}}(K)
be the corresponding Reed–Muller information set
(all monomials of degree
\leq r^{\star}
). Then there exists

\rho^{\star}<\infty
such that for all
\rho\geq\rho^{\star}
,

\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{rel}}(\rho,K)=\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{RM}}(K)
and

\lim_{\rho\to\infty}w_{\min}(\rho)=2^{m-r^{\star}},

i.e., the reliability-based polar construction converges to the
(truncated) RM code and its minimum distance.

For each row-weight
d
, define

n_{d}(\rho)=|\{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{rel}}(\rho,K):D_{i}=d\}|
. Then,

w_{\min}(\rho)=\min\{d:n_{d}(\rho)>0\}
. Theorem
1

says that, under the exponential reliability model, the functions
n_{d}(\rho)

are nonincreasing in
\rho
and vanish successively as the design SNR grows.
Each time
n_{d}(\rho)
hits zero, the minimum distance jumps from
d
to the
next larger row-weight (often
2d
), with the final plateau equal to the RM
distance. In practice, for a fixed
operating
SNR, this distance gain is
accompanied by a gradual loss in aggregate SC reliability

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{rel}}(\rho,K)}Z_{i}(\rho_{\mathrm{op}})
as the
design SNR is pushed far above the operating point, which is precisely the
trade-off that the mixed design in this paper is intended to control.

IV
Bit-wise Mixed Metric for Near-ML Decoders

We now connect the mixed cost in Section
III
to error events in
SC-based near-ML decoders, focusing on SCL decoding.

IV-A

SC first-error events

For SC decoding, let

\mathcal{E}_{i}^{\mathrm{SC}}=\{\hat{u}_{j}=u_{j}\ \forall j<i,\ \hat{u}_{i}\neq u_{i}\},\quad i\in\mathcal{I},

be the
first-error
event, with probability

P_{e}^{\mathrm{SC}}(i)=\Pr(\mathcal{E}_{i}^{\mathrm{SC}})
. The SC block error
event decomposes as

\mathcal{E}_{B}^{\mathrm{SC}}=\bigsqcup_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{E}_{i}^{\mathrm{SC}},\quad P_{B}^{\mathrm{SC}}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}P_{e}^{\mathrm{SC}}(i).

IV-B

Per-bit split of a truncated ML union bound

Consider a truncated ML union bound

U_{B}^{(t)}=\sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{t}}A_{w}P_{w}(W),\quad\mathcal{W}_{t}\subseteq[w_{\min},2w_{\min}].

Grouping terms by bit position yields

U_{B}^{(t)}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\Phi^{\mathrm{ML}}(i),

for suitably defined
\Phi^{\mathrm{ML}}(i)
depending on
A_{w}^{(i)}
and

\mathcal{W}_{t}
(see, e.g.,
[
11
,
10
,
12
]
).

IV-C

Error-event decomposition for SCL

For SCL decoding with list size
L
, let

\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\mathrm{SCL}}(\mathbf{y})
denote the set of surviving information
prefixes at depth
i
, and
\hat{\mathbf{c}}^{\mathrm{SCL}}(\mathbf{y})
its output. Let

\mathbf{c}_{0}
be the transmitted codeword.

Definition 6

(Pruning and ML-like events for SCL)
.

Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{prune}}^{\mathrm{SCL}}

\displaystyle:=\Bigl\{\mathbf{y}:\ \exists i\in\mathcal{I}\ \text{s.t.}\mathbf{u}_{0,0}^{i-1}\notin\mathcal{S}_{i}^{\mathrm{SCL}}(\mathbf{y})\Bigr\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{ML\text{-}like}}^{\mathrm{SCL}}

\displaystyle:=\Bigl\{\mathbf{y}:\ \mathbf{u}_{0}\in\mathcal{S}_{|\mathcal{I}|}^{\mathrm{SCL}}(\mathbf{y}),\ \hat{\mathbf{c}}^{\mathrm{SCL}}(\mathbf{y})\neq\mathbf{c}_{0}\Bigr\},

where
\mathbf{u}_{0}
is the true information vector.

Lemma 2

(SCL block-error decomposition)
.

For SCL decoding,

\mathcal{E}_{B}^{\mathrm{SCL}}=\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{prune}}^{\mathrm{SCL}}\,\dot{\cup}\,\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{ML\text{-}like}}^{\mathrm{SCL}},

and
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{ML\text{-}like}}^{\mathrm{SCL}}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{B}^{\mathrm{ML}}
. Consequently,

P_{B}^{\mathrm{SCL}}\leq P\bigl(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{prune}}^{\mathrm{SCL}}\bigr)+P_{B}^{\mathrm{ML}}.

(10)

The ML-like term in (
10
) can be approximated by

U_{B}^{(t)}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\Phi^{\mathrm{ML}}(i)
. The pruning term is
algorithm-dependent, but simulations and shifted-pruning analyses
(e.g.
[
24
,
25
]
)
show that pruning typically occurs after several SC-type errors along the true
path, and is therefore strongly correlated with SC first-error events.

Motivated by this, we replace
P(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{prune}}^{\mathrm{SCL}})

by
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}P_{e}^{\mathrm{SC}}(i)
as a surrogate and obtain the
bit-wise design template

P_{B}^{\mathrm{SCL}}\;\lesssim\;\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}P_{e}^{\mathrm{SC}}(i)+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}\Phi^{\mathrm{ML}}(i)+R_{t},

(11)

where
R_{t}\geq 0
accounts for truncation and overlap effects and the symbol

\lesssim
hides channel- and decoder-dependent constants.

IV-D

Bit-wise mixed score

Equation (
11
) naturally suggests ranking indices by

\Psi(i)=P_{e}^{\mathrm{SC}}(i)+\alpha\,\Phi^{\mathrm{ML}}(i),

(12)

for some tuning parameter
\alpha>0
. Comparing (
12
) with

J_{K}(i)
in (
7
), we see that
J_{K}(i)
is a K-dependent
instantiation of this idea where
\Phi^{\mathrm{ML}}(i)
is approximated by a
distance penalty
D_{K}(i)
concentrated on maximum-degree monomials and scaled
by
Z(W)^{w_{\min}}
.

V
Asymptotic Behaviour as
N\to\infty

We briefly explain why the benefits of mixed constructions are mainly
finite-length phenomena.

V-A

Local perturbations of reliability-based sets

Let
K=\lfloor RN\rfloor
with
0<R<I(W)
fixed. For each
N
, let

\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{rel}}(N,K)
denote a reliability-based information set of size

K
(e.g., the
K
smallest
Z_{i}
).

Definition 7

(Local perturbation)
.

A sequence of information sets
\{\mathcal{I}(N)\}_{N}
of size
K
is a

local perturbation
of
\{\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{rel}}(N,K)\}_{N}
if

\bigl|\mathcal{I}(N)\triangle\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{rel}}(N,K)\bigr|\leq L(N),

with
L(N)=o(N)
as
N\to\infty
, where
\triangle
is the symmetric difference.

Intuitively,
L(N)
is the number of positions that differ from the pure
reliability construction; mixed designs typically satisfy
L(N)\ll N
.

V-B

Effect on SC sum and minimum-weight union bound

By polarization, the selected bit-channels in
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{rel}}(N,K)
have

Z_{i}
that decay doubly exponentially in
m=\log_{2}N
. Exchanging a sublinear
number of such indices can only have a vanishing effect on the SC sum.

Lemma 3

(Effect on SC sum)
.

Let
\mathcal{I}_{1},\mathcal{I}_{2}
be two information sets of size
K
chosen among the
“good” bit-channels for a BMS channel
W
, differing in at most
L(N)=o(N)

indices. Then

\left|\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{1}}Z_{i}-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{2}}Z_{i}\right|\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}0.

For decreasing monomial codes with fixed maximum degree
r
, recall that

w_{\min}=2^{m-r}
and
A_{w_{\min}}(\mathcal{I})
is given by (
4
). A
local change in the set of degree-
r
monomials perturbs
A_{w_{\min}}
by at
most
O(L(N))
, and the associated minimum-weight contribution to the union
bound by at most
O(L(N)\gamma(W)^{2^{m-r}})
.

Lemma 4

(Effect on truncated minimum-weight term)
.

Let
\mathrm{UB}_{w_{\min}}(\mathcal{I})=A_{w_{\min}}(\mathcal{I})\,\gamma(W)^{w_{\min}}
be the
minimum-weight part of the ML union bound. If
\mathcal{I}_{1},\mathcal{I}_{2}
are decreasing
monomial sets of size
K
with the same maximum degree
r
, differing in at
most
L(N)=o(N)
monomials, then

\bigl|\mathrm{UB}_{w_{\min}}(\mathcal{I}_{1})-\mathrm{UB}_{w_{\min}}(\mathcal{I}_{2})\bigr|\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}0.

Combining Lemmas
3
and
4
shows that any
k
-dependent mixed perturbation of a reliability-based information set has asymptotically negligible effect on SC and ML union bounds. Thus, mixed design mainly benefits finite lengths: as
N
grows, polarization dominates and the added weight term affects only constant factors.

VII
Summary and Future Directions

We introduced a mixed reliability–weight metric for constructing polar codes (software is available in
[
21
]
). The method penalizes only maximum‑degree monomials using closed‑form orbit counts of minimum‑weight codewords, scaled by a Bhattacharyya factor and combined with SC‑based reliability into a per‑bit cost. This mixed metric connects naturally to bit‑wise SC decomposition and truncated ML union bounds for SCL decoding, yielding designs that act as small perturbations of standard reliability constructions. Future work includes extending the metric to PW‑based sequences and embedding the weight term into universal partial orders for channel‑independent, distance‑aware polar designs.

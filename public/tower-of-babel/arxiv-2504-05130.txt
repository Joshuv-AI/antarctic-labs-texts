Viscous pressureless flows with free boundary in one space dimension: The constant viscosity case
Xin Liu

Abstract
We establish the global well-posedness of the free boundary problem of the viscous pressureless and almost pressureless heat conductive flows in one space dimension. In both cases, arbitrarily large but smooth initial data is considered, and the evolving fluid domains remain bounded for all time. In the viscous pressureless case, we are able to identify the terminal flow domain in terms of the initial data. In the viscous almost pressureless case, we construct the flow as a perturbation of the viscous pressureless flow, and establish the first result for the Navier-Stokes-Fourier system in the current setting. Keywords: Pressureless flow, Navier-Stokes-Fourier system, Free boundary problem, Constant viscosity. MSC2020: 35Q30, 35Q35, 76A30, 76N10.

Introduction

1.1
Backgrounds and the scope of this paper

The pressureless flow model (
1.1
) is used to describe dynamics of sticky particle
[
1
,
5
]
, where two particles stick to each other with the same terminal velocity after collision. It is an extension (or a simplification, respectively) of Burgers’ equation (or the compressible Euler equations, respectively). In the spirit of
[
18
]
, the pressureless Euler equations are strongly tied to dynamical systems with large dimension, describing the motion of large number of particles. Indeed, it can be also used in astrophysics to model the mass distribution of large scale structures of the galaxy
[
42
,
45
]
. It is also related to the modeling of traffic flow
[
44
]
.

In comparison to the formation of singularity in Burgers’s equation and the compressible Euler equations, new singularity is observed in the pressureless Euler equations. By investigating the Riemann problem in one-dimension, the authors in
[
14
,
15
,
39
,
40
]
have rigorously shown the formation of
\delta
-shock and vacuum states in the pressureless limit from the compressible Euler equations. To be more precise, for the pressureless Euler equations, a weighted
\delta
measure will form from a compressing initial data, and vacuum, instead of rarefaction wave, will form from a depressing initial data. In the case of multi-dimensional flows, the problem of non-uniqueness and non-existence arise, as shown in
[
8
,
7
]
.

Meanwhile, the measure value weak solutions to the pressureless Euler equations have been intensively studied. For instance, a generalized variational principle in the spirit of the Lax-Oleinik variational principle for scalar conservation laws, was established to construct global weak solutions to the pressureless Euler equations
[
21
]
. Similar result can be found also in
[
43
]
. The uniqueness of the variational weak solutions is shown in
[
28
]
. The author in
[
4
]
constructs unique global smooth solutions to the viscous pressureless flow, and establishes the unique weak solution in the sense of duality, introduced in
[
2
,
3
]
, as the vanishing viscosity limit. We refer readers to
[
5
,
27
,
34
,
35
,
20
,
6
,
37
,
12
,
23
,

,

,

,
36
,
29
,
26
,
11
,

,
38
,
30
,
9
,
32
,
31
]
and the references therein for other construction of solutions and recent works on the pressureless flows.

The goal of this paper is to explore the effect of viscosity in the pressureless flow. To be more precise, by ignoring the pressure, we aim at isolating the effect of the viscosity, describing the trajectory of smooth solutions, and establish the connection of the viscous pressureless flow to the Navier-Stokes-Fourier flow. In the one-dimensional free boundary setting, where the fluid boundary is moving together with the flow, we show first, for arbitrary smooth but large initial data, the viscous pressureless flow (
1.1
) has a unique global smooth solution, and the domain of the flow exponentially converges to a compact set as time goes to infinity. Moreover, we are able to characterize the terminal flow domain in terms of the initial data only. We emphasize that, this asymptotic behavior is the result of the constant viscosity, in comparison to the inviscid flow and the density-dependent viscosity flow
[
26
]
.

In addition, in the hypersonic regime, i.e., almost pressureless case, we show that for arbitrary smooth but large initial data, there exists a unique global solution to the free boundary problem of the compressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier system (
1.6
) with dirichlet boundary condition for the temperature. The resulting flow should be seen as a perturbation of the pressureless flow and as a result, the flow domain remains a compact set as time goes to infinity. We emphasize that this result is not possible in the setting of zero normal heat flux, where the latter is establish in
[
13
]
. This is due to the difference in asymptotic behavior for solutions to the heat equation with dirichlet and zero normal heat flux boundary conditions.

Our results are the first to tackle the free boundary problem of (almost) pressureless flow. The purpose of this study is to distinguish the physical effect of the viscosity to the asymptotic dynamics of (pressureless) fluid flow.

We would like to make a few remarks before finishing the introduction. The lack of dispersive background flows (cf.
[
33
,
25
,
24
]
) means that we don’t have mechanism to prevent splash and splat singularities (e.g.
[
16
]
) in the case of multi-dimensional flow. Therefore our results cannot be trivially extended to higher dimension without any restrictions on geometry, initial data, etc.. The effect of degenerate viscosity, i.e., density and/or temperature depending viscosity, is dramatically different from the constant viscosity as shown in the appendix and
[
26
]
, and therefore deserves its separate study. We prefer to not complicating our current paper and leave these issues to future works.

1.2
Main results

Let
\rho\geq 0
be the flow density, and
u
be the flow velocity. The viscous pressureless flow is governed by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{y}(\rho u)=0,

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\partial_{y}(\rho u^{2})=\partial_{y}(\mu\partial_{y}u).

(1.1b)

Here
\mu\geq 0
is the viscosity coefficient, which is taken to be constant in this work.

We consider the flow in a simply connected, evolving domain, where the domain boundary moves together with the flow without surface stress. That is

domain of the flow:

\displaystyle\quad\Omega(t)=(a(t),b(t)),

(1.1c)

evolution of the boundary:

\displaystyle\quad\dot{a}(t)=u(a(t),t),\quad\dot{b}(t)=u(b(t),t),

(1.1d)

stress free boundary condition:

\displaystyle\quad\mu\partial_{y}u|_{y=a(t),b(t)}=0.

(1.1e)

Then we have the following theorem for the viscous pressureless flow:

Theorem 1

(Pressureless flow)
.

Denote by
(\rho,u)|_{t=0}=(\rho_{0},u_{0})
the initial data and
\rho_{0}|_{\Omega(0)}>0
. Without loss of generality, assume that the total momentum of the initial data is zero, i.e.,

\int_{\Omega(0)}\rho_{0}u_{0}\ dy=0.

(1.2)

Suppose the initial data is smooth and satisfying the following:

\begin{gathered}\int_{\Omega(0)}\rho_{0}\,dy+\int_{\Omega(0)}\rho_{0}|u_{0}|^{2}\ dy+\int_{\Omega(0)}\rho_{0}|u_{1}|^{2}\ dy\leq\mathfrak{E}_{0}<\infty.\end{gathered}

(1.3)

for some
\mathcal{E}_{0}\in(0,\infty)
. Here
u_{1}:=u_{t}|_{t=0}
is given by the equation (
1.1b
) at
t=0
. Then there exists a global strong solution
(\rho,u)
to system (
1.1
) with

|\Omega(t)|=|a(t)-b(t)|\xrightarrow{t\rightarrow\infty}\int_{a(0)}^{b(0)}\exp\bigl(-\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{a(0)}^{y}\rho_{0}u_{0}\ dy^{\prime}\bigr)\ dy,

(1.4)

and

\begin{gathered}\sup_{0\leq t<\infty}\biggl\{\int_{\Omega(t)}\rho|u|^{2}\,dy+\int_{\Omega(t)}\rho|u_{t}|^{2}\ dy\biggr\}\\
+\int_{0}^{\infty}\biggl(\int_{\Omega(t)}|u_{y}|^{2}\,dy+\int_{\Omega(t)}|u_{yt}|^{2}\ dy\biggr)dt<C\mathfrak{E}_{0},\end{gathered}

(1.5)

for some constant
C\in(0,\infty)
.

Remark 1
.

(i)

We emphasize that there is no smallness assumption of
\mathcal{E}_{0}
.

(ii)

The assumption (
1.2
) can be removed easily by considering the Galilean transformation.

(iii)

The detailed energy decay estimate is deferred to the Lagrangian description, where it is easier to describe.

On the other hand, take into account the temperature
\theta\geq 0
, and let
M>0
be a large parameter, to be determined later. We consider the following Navier-Stokes-Fourier system for heat conductive flow:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{y}(\rho u)=0,

(1.6a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\partial_{y}(\rho u^{2})+\dfrac{1}{M^{2}}\partial_{y}(\rho\theta)=\partial_{y}(\mu\partial_{y}u),

(1.6b)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho\theta)+\partial_{y}(\rho\theta u)+\dfrac{1}{M^{2}}\rho\theta\partial_{y}u=\partial_{y}(\kappa\partial_{y}\theta)+\mu|\partial_{y}u|^{2},

(1.6c)

with, in addition to the boundary conditions (
1.1c
) – (
1.1e
),

\theta|_{y=a(t),b(t)}=0.

(1.6d)

We have the following theorem for this almost pressureless system:

Theorem 2

(Almost pressureless flow)
.

In addition to the assumption in theorem
1
, assume that

\int_{\Omega(0)}\rho_{0}\theta_{0}\,dy+\int_{\Omega(0)}\rho_{0}|\theta_{0}|^{2}\,dy+\int_{\Omega(0)}\rho_{0}|\theta_{1}|^{2}\,dy\leq\mathfrak{E}_{1}<\infty,

(1.7)

where
\theta_{0}:=\theta|_{t=0}
and
\theta_{1}:=\theta_{t}|_{t=0}
, with the latter defined by the equation (
1.6c
). Then for
M
sufficiently large, depending on the initial data, there exists a unique global strong solution
(\rho,u,\theta)
to system (
1.6
) with
|\Omega(t)|<C<\infty
for all
t\in[0,\infty)
, and the following estimates hold:

\begin{gathered}\sup_{0\leq t<\infty}\biggl\{\int_{\Omega(t)}\rho|u|^{2}\,dy+\int_{\Omega(t)}\rho|u_{t}|^{2}\ dy+\int_{\Omega(t)}\rho\theta\,dy\\
+\int_{\Omega(t)}\rho|\theta|^{2}\,dy+\int_{\Omega(t)}\rho|\theta_{t}|^{2}\,dy\biggr\}\\
+\int_{0}^{\infty}\biggl(\int_{\Omega(t)}|u_{y}|^{2}\,dy+\int_{\Omega(t)}|u_{yt}|^{2}\ dy+\int_{\Omega(t)}|\theta_{y}|^{2}\,dy+\int_{\Omega(t)}|\theta_{yt}|^{2}\,dy\biggr)dt\\
<C(\mathfrak{E}_{0}+\mathfrak{E}_{1}+\mathfrak{E}_{0}^{3}+\mathfrak{E}_{1}^{3}),\end{gathered}

(1.8)

for some constant
C\in(0,\infty)
.

Remark 2
.

(i)

System (
1.6
) can be obtained by rescaling the Navier-Stokes-Fourier system, and it preserves the mass, momentum, and energy conservation laws.

(ii)

The result in theorem
2
should be seen as a perturbation of the result of theorem
1
.

(iii)

By scaling the temperature
\widetilde{\theta}:=\theta/M^{2}
, the result in theorem
2
can be seen as a solution with arbitrary velocity but small rescaled temperature
\widetilde{\theta}
.

(iv)

Again, we refer to the Lagrangian description for the detailed energy decay estimates.

For both theorems

and

2
Viscous pressureless flow with constant viscosity

2.1
Lagrangian formulation

We write system (
1.1
) in the Lagrangian coordinates. Without loss of generality, assume that
\Omega(0)=(-1,1)
. Let
y=\eta(x,t)
satisfy

\partial_{t}\eta(x,t)=u(y,t)=u(\eta(x,t),t),\qquad\eta(x,0)=x\in(-1,1).

(2.1)

Meanwhile, write the density and the velocity in the Lagrangian coordinates
(x,t)
by

f(x,t):=\rho(\eta(x,t),t),\qquad v(x,t):=u(\eta(x,t),t).

(2.2)

Then it is easy to verify from (
1.1a
) and (
2.1
) that

f(x,t)=\dfrac{\rho_{0}(x)}{\eta_{x}(x,t)}.

Therefore, system (
1.1
) can be written in the lagrangian coordinates as

\displaystyle\rho_{0}\partial_{t}v-\bigl(\mu\dfrac{v_{x}}{\eta_{x}}\bigr)_{x}=0,

(2.3a)

\displaystyle\mu\partial_{x}v|_{x=-1,1}=0.

(2.3b)

We will show that the flow with constant viscosity coefficient
\mu>0
will become stationary with finite radius. Moreover, we give the characteristic of the asymptotic behavior, including

•

the terminal domain radius in term of the initial data;

•

the decay estimates of the velocity; and

•

global regularity of the solution.

2.2
Global point-wise estimates

Basic energy:

Taking the
L^{2}
-inner product of (
2.3a
) with
v
and applying integration by parts yield

\dfrac{d}{dt}\dfrac{1}{2}\int\rho_{0}|v|^{2}\,dx+\int\mu\dfrac{|v_{x}|^{2}}{\eta_{x}}\,dx=0.

(2.4)

Therefore integrating (
2.4
) in time implies

\sup_{t}\dfrac{1}{2}\int\rho_{0}|v|^{2}\,dx+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int\mu\dfrac{|v_{x}|^{2}}{\eta_{x}}\,dx\,dt=\dfrac{1}{2}\int\rho_{0}|v_{0}|^{2}\,dx.

(2.5)

Point-wise estimate:

On the other hand, integrating (
2.3a
) from
x=-1
to
x\in(-1,1)
leads to

\partial_{t}\int_{-1}^{x}\rho_{0}v\,dx^{\prime}=\mu\dfrac{v_{x}}{\eta_{x}}=\mu\partial_{t}\log\eta_{x}.

Integrating (
2.6
) in time yields

\mu\log\eta_{x}(t)=\int_{-1}^{x}\rho_{0}v(t)\,dx^{\prime}+\mu\underbrace{\log\eta_{0,x}}_{=0}-\int_{-1}^{x}\rho_{0}v_{0}\,dx^{\prime}.

(2.7)

Therefore, after applying Hölder’s inequality and the energy identity (

), one has

\sup_{x,t}|\mu\log\eta_{x}(x,t)|\lesssim\sup_{t}\int\rho_{0}|v|^{2}\,dx\leq\int\rho_{0}|v_{0}|^{2}\,dx,

which implies that, there is a constant
c_{1}\in(0,\infty)
, such that

\dfrac{1}{c_{1}}\leq\eta_{x}(x,t)\leq c_{1},\qquad(x,t)\in(-1,1)\times[0,\infty).

(2.9)

Decay estimate:

Thanks to (
2.9
), one can conclude from (
2.4
) that

\dfrac{d}{dt}\int\rho_{0}|v|^{2}\,dx+\dfrac{2}{c_{1}}\int\mu|v_{x}|^{2}\,dx\leq 0.

(2.10)

Meanwhile one has the following Poincaré type inequality:

\int\rho_{0}|v|^{2}\,dx\lesssim\int|v_{x}|^{2}\,dx.

(2.11)

Therefore, (
2.10
) implies that there exists
c_{2}\in(0,\infty)
such that

\dfrac{d}{dt}\int\rho_{0}|v|^{2}\,dx+c_{2}\int\rho_{0}|v|^{2}\,dx\leq 0.

(2.12)

Therefore, one can conclude that

\int\rho_{0}|v(t)|^{2}\,dx\leq e^{-c_{2}t}\int\rho_{0}|v_{0}|^{2}\,dx.

(2.13)

Multiplying (
2.10
) with

and integrating the resultant in

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{c_{2}t/2}\|v_{x}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,dt\leq\text{initial data}.

(2.14)

Recalling (
2.7

\begin{gathered}|\mu\log\eta_{x}(x,t)+\int_{-1}^{x}\rho_{0}v_{0}\,dx^{\prime}|\leq(\int\rho_{0}\,dx)^{1/2}(\int\rho_{0}|v(t)|^{2}\,dx)^{1/2}\\
\leq e^{-c_{2}t/2}(\int\rho_{0}\,dx)^{1/2}(\int\rho_{0}|v_{0}|^{2}\,dx)^{1/2}.\end{gathered}

(2.15)

Notably, (

) states that in the original Euclidean coordinates, the size of the domain (given by

) will be finite all the time, and (
2.15
) has described the final state of the domain as

4
Appendix: Viscous pressureless flow with degenerate viscosity

Here we would like to demonstrate the effect of degenerate viscosity by constructing self-similar solutions. We consider system (
1.1
) with
\mu=\mu(\rho)
, i.e., when the viscosity coefficient depends on the density. In particular,
\mu\rightarrow 0
as
\rho\rightarrow 0
on the moving boundary. Notice that, the lagrangian formulation (
2.3
) still holds, with
\mu=\mu(\rho_{0}/\eta_{x})
.

Consider a solution to (
2.3a
) with
\mu=\rho^{\alpha}=(\rho_{0}/\eta_{x})^{\alpha}
,
\alpha>0
of the form

\eta(x,t)=\sigma(t)x.

(4.1)

Then (
2.3a
) is reduced to

x\rho_{0}\sigma^{\prime\prime}(t)-(\rho_{0}^{\alpha})_{x}\dfrac{\sigma^{\prime}(t)}{\sigma^{\alpha+1}(t)}=0.

(4.2)

Consider

x\rho_{0}=-\dfrac{1}{\alpha}(\rho_{0}^{\alpha})_{x}.

(4.3)

That is

•

when
\alpha>1
,
(\alpha-1)(1-x^{2})/2=\rho_{0}^{\alpha-1}(x),x\in(-1,1)
;

•

when
\alpha=1
,
\rho_{0}(x)=\rho_{0}(0)e^{-x^{2}/2},x\in\mathbb{R}
;

•

when
0<\alpha<1
,
\rho_{0}(x)=\dfrac{1}{[(1-\alpha)x^{2}/2+\rho_{0}^{\alpha-1}(0)]^{1/(1-\alpha)}},x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Then from (
4.2
), one has

\sigma^{\prime\prime}(t)-(\sigma^{-\alpha})^{\prime}(t)=0.

(4.4)

Integrating (
4.4
) in time yields

\sigma^{\prime}(t)=\sigma^{-\alpha}(t)+\sigma^{\prime}(0)-\sigma^{-\alpha}(0).

(4.5)

Now, let

\Gamma:=\sigma^{\prime}(0)-\sigma^{-\alpha}(0).

(4.6)

•

If
\Gamma\geq 0
, one can conclude from (
4.5
) that
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sigma(t)=\infty
and
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sigma^{\prime}(t)=\Gamma
.

•

If
\Gamma<0
, one can conclude from (
4.5
) that
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sigma(t)=(-\Gamma)^{-1/\alpha}\in(0,\infty)
and
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sigma^{\prime}(t)=0
.

We call the case when
\Gamma\geq 0
the large energy self-similar solution, and the case when
\Gamma<0
the small energy self-similar solution.

The large energy and the small energy solutions indicate that, the asymptotic behavior of the solutions depends strongly on the structure of the initial data, regardless of the value of
\alpha>0
. Consequently, it is not realistic to investigate the general solution to (
2.3
) with density depending viscosity without imposing restriction on the initial data. This is very different from the flow with constant viscosity as shown in theorem
1
.

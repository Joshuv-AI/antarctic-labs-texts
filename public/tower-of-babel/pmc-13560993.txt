Butylphthalide combined with tenecteplase in mild disabling acute ischaemic stroke in China (BENEFIT-2): study protocol for a multicentre, randomised, double-blind, active-controlled trial
Authors: Qiaoling Tang, Li Wang, Lei Tang, David Wang, Duolao Wang, Ye Li, Xiangbin Zhang, Ran Liu, Sai Wang, Yi Zeng, Minxue Shen, Le Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionIntravenous thrombolysis is the standard early treatment for acute ischaemic stroke (AIS), yet a substantial proportion of patients, particularly those with mild disabling deficits, do not achieve favourable functional recovery. Tenecteplase (TNK), a fibrin-specific thrombolytic agent administered as a single bolus, and butylphthalide (NBP), a multi-mechanism neuroprotective agent, have each shown benefit in AIS. However, whether their combination confers additional benefit in mild disabling AIS remains unknown. This trial aims to determine whether adding NBP to TNK improves functional outcomes in patients with mild disabling ischaemic stroke treated within 4.5 hours of symptom onset.Methods and analysisBENEFIT-2 is a prospective, multicentre, randomised, double-blind, active-controlled trial. Eligible patients will be randomised 1:1 to receive either TNK plus NBP (combination group) or TNK plus placebo (control group) via block randomisation. The intervention comprises intravenous NBP 25 mg/100 mL two times per day for 7 days, followed by oral NBP 0.2 g three times per day up to day 14, with matching placebos for the control group. Key eligibility criteria include age 18–80 years, symptom onset within 4.5 hours, baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score 2–5 with persistent unilateral weakness or speech impairment, and prestroke modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score 0 or 1. The primary outcome is the proportion of patients achieving mRS 0–1 at 90 days (allowable window ±7 days). Secondary endpoints include change in NIHSS, stroke recurrence, major vascular events, quality of life measured by EQ-5D and penumbral salvage on imaging. Safety endpoints comprise symptomatic intracranial haemorrhage, vascular death, all-cause mortality and other adverse events within 90 days. The primary analysis will follow the intention-to-treat principle.Ethics and disseminationEthics approval has been obtained from the Independent Ethics Committee of Xiangya Hospital (No. 2026020399), Central South University. Results will be published in peer-reviewed journals and presented at academic conferences.Trial registration number NCT07369999 Keywords: Stroke, STROKE MEDICINE, Randomized Controlled Trial, THERAPEUTICS

Introduction

Stroke remains a major public health challenge globally and in China,1 where it is the leading cause of death and adult disability, with over 2 million new cases annually.2 Mild strokes, defined by National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) ≤5, account for approximately 50% of acute ischaemic stroke (AIS). Among these, a subset with persistent disabling deficits—such as unilateral limb weakness or speech impairment—carries a poor prognosis, with up to 30% of patients experiencing severe disability or recurrence at 90 days.3 4 Intravenous thrombolysis is the standard of care for eligible patients within 4.5 hours of onset. Tenecteplase (TNK), administered as a single bolus with high fibrin specificity, is non-inferior to alteplase in efficacy and safety.5 However, even after successful vascular recanalisation, a significant proportion of patients fail to achieve optimal neurological recovery, highlighting the need for complementary neuroprotective therapy.6

Neuroprotective therapy has emerged as a promising strategy to address this gap, targeting the ischaemic cascade to reduce neuronal damage and salvage the ischaemic penumbra.7–10 While numerous neuroprotective agents have entered clinical research, most phase III trials have failed to show convincing clinical benefit, largely due to missed time windows—clinical imaging studies suggest the optimal time window for neuroprotective intervention is within 2–3 hours of onset, which is often unattainable for most patients.11–13 Butylphthalide (NBP), a compound derived from celery seeds, is a domestically developed class 1 neuroprotective drug in China with multiple mechanisms of action against cerebral ischaemia.14–17 Previous studies, such as the BAST trial, have shown that NBP combined with alteplase significantly improved the proportion of modified Rankin Scale (mRS) 0–1 at 90 days in mild-to-moderate stroke patients (NIHSS 4–7), with the combination group achieving a 79.8% rate compared with 72.5% in the monotherapy group (absolute difference 7.3%).18

Despite this evidence, the BAST trial had a limited sample size and was not specifically designed for the mild disabling stroke population (NIHSS 2–5).18 Furthermore, as a new-generation thrombolytic agent, TNK’s safety and efficacy when combined with NBP have not been systematically verified. This research gap creates an unmet clinical need, as mild disabling stroke patients have high expectations for complete functional recovery despite their relatively low NIHSS scores. The BENEFIT-2 study is thus designed to address this gap, aiming to evaluate whether early combination of TNK and NBP can further improve neurological outcomes compared with TNK monotherapy in patients with mild disabling AIS.

We hypothesise that in patients with mild disabling AIS treated with TNK intravenous thrombolysis within 4.5 hours of onset, early combined treatment with NBP sodium chloride injection and NBP soft capsules will increase the proportion of patients with mRS score 0–1 at 90 days compared with TNK monotherapy, thereby improving neurological prognosis. The primary objective of this study is to assess whether the combination of TNK and NBP can significantly increase the proportion of patients with mRS 0–1 at 90±7 days. Secondary objectives include comparing changes in NIHSS score, ischaemic stroke recurrence, vascular events, quality of life and penumbra salvage rate between the two groups. Safety objectives focus on the incidence of serious adverse events, symptomatic intracranial haemorrhage (sICH), vascular death, all-cause death and general adverse events within 90 days. Additionally, subgroup analyses will be conducted to explore the effect of NBP treatment on outcomes stratified by age, gender, baseline NIHSS score, occlusion site, randomisation time, comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes, coronary heart disease, hyperlipidaemia), aetiological classification and recanalisation time.

Methods and analysis

Hypothesis

We hypothesise that, among patients with mild disabling AIS treated with TNK intravenous thrombolysis within 4.5 hours of onset, early combined treatment with NBP sodium chloride injection and NBP soft capsules will increase the proportion of patients with mRS score 0–1 at 90 days compared with TNK monotherapy, without increasing safety risks.

Objectives

Primary objective: to determine whether TNK combined with sequential NBP increases the proportion of patients with an mRS score of 0–1 at 90±7 days compared with TNK plus placebo.

Secondary objectives: to compare neurological outcomes, recurrent ischaemic stroke, composite vascular events, quality of life and imaging-based penumbra salvage between the two treatment groups.

Safety objective: to compare serious adverse events, symptomatic intracranial haemorrhage, vascular death, all-cause death and other adverse events within 90 days.

Trial design

BENEFIT-2 is a prospective, multicentre, randomised, double-blind, active-controlled trial conducted across eligible hospitals in China with demonstrated capability in acute stroke care, including 24-hour access to neuroimaging, availability of stroke units and experience with intravenous thrombolysis. Each participating centre is required to have a certified stroke team, adequate MRI facilities per the protocol specifications and prior training in Good Clinical Practice (GCP). The overall planned study period is from October 2025 to June 2029, covering study set-up, participant recruitment, treatment and follow-up. As participating sites are being activated in stages, a complete list of participating centres is not provided in the main text. The full list of activated participating centres will be maintained and updated in the trial registry records as the study progresses, and will be made available once all sites are confirmed. The trial will be conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and GCP guidelines.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and the public were not involved in the design, recruitment, conduct or reporting of this trial. The study schedule and procedures were designed to minimise participant burden while preserving scientific validity. Results will be disseminated via peer-reviewed publications and conference presentations, and lay summaries will be prepared for participants and the public.

Randomisation

Randomisation uses a computer-generated block design (block size 4) with a 1:1 allocation ratio. Eligible participants are assigned a sequential randomisation number and receive the corresponding blinded study medication package. The randomisation sequence is generated and held by an independent statistician not involved in the trial. Investigators, participants and outcome assessors remain masked to treatment allocation until database lock and final unblinding.

Intervention

Eligible patients will receive NBP/placebo treatment on the basis of standard intravenous TNK administration. The recommended dose of TNK is 0.25 mg/kg as a single intravenous bolus, with a maximum dose not exceeding 25 mg, administered over 5–10 s. For the experimental group, patients will receive intravenous infusions of NBP in 100 mL sodium chloride injection two times per day for the initial 7 days, followed by oral NBP soft capsules (0.2 g) three times per day from day 8 to day 14. For the control group, patients will receive 100 mL placebo injections two times per day for the initial 7 days, followed by oral placebo soft capsules (0.2 g) three times per day from day 8 to day 14. The placebo infusion matches the active NBP sodium chloride injection in appearance and colour and is administered by the same intravenous route, in the same volume and at the same dosing frequency. The placebo soft capsules match the active NBP soft capsules in appearance, colour and odour and are administered orally according to the same schedule. All study medications are identically packaged and labelled according to the randomisation code, with no distinguishable features that could reveal treatment allocation to participants, investigators or outcome assessors. The first NBP/placebo infusion must be initiated within 4.5 hours of ischaemic stroke onset. Twice-daily intravenous infusions for 7 days are recommended, with a minimum treatment duration of 5 days. Oral capsule administration will start on the day after the last infusion and should continue until day 14 (figure 1). Each infusion shall last for at least 50 min, with an interval of 6 hours between consecutive infusions. Patients are required to take the oral capsules before meals daily, document all medication administrations and the records will be verified by investigators. The steering committee will provide recommendations for concomitant medications. All secondary preventive strategies, including antithrombotic therapy and risk factor management, shall be implemented in accordance with current clinical guidelines. Neuroprotective agents, including human urinary kallidinogenase, edaravone and any ginkgo-containing injections, are strictly prohibited.

Intervention discontinuation and modification

Study treatment is suspended, discontinued or modified in the following circumstances: intracranial haemorrhage of any type; serious hypersensitivity reaction; serious adverse event judged by the investigator to be related to the study drug; newly developed clinically significant hepatic or renal dysfunction; confirmed pregnancy during treatment; poor compliance with the treatment regimen; or any other major medical event that warrants permanent discontinuation. Treatment is also discontinued if the investigator considers continuation unsafe or clinically inappropriate, or if the participant or the participant’s legal representative withdraws consent. Discontinuation of study treatment is not synonymous with withdrawal from the trial. Participants who stop treatment are encouraged to complete all scheduled follow-up assessments up to 90 days, unless consent for further participation is explicitly withdrawn.

Post-trial care

After completion of the study, participants will continue to receive standard secondary stroke prevention and rehabilitation according to routine clinical practice and current guidelines. No further trial-specific therapy is provided beyond the protocol-defined intervention. All participants remain entitled to clinically indicated treatments, including antithrombotic therapy, risk factor management and rehabilitation, as determined by their treating physicians.

Data management and quality control

Data are entered directly into an electronic data capture system with secure encrypted transmission and password protection. Case report forms (CRFs) are completed by investigators and reviewed by clinical research coordinators within 24 hours of data collection. Source data verification is performed by monitors for ≥10% of subjects per centre to ensure consistency between CRF data and original medical records.

An independent Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) composed of neurology, cardiology and biostatistics experts will monitor trial progress and patient safety every 6 months. The DSMB has the authority to recommend protocol amendments or early termination based on interim safety data.

All study personnel will receive standardised training on the protocol, GCP, outcome assessment and data collection before trial initiation. Periodic refresher training will be provided during the trial. The central neuroimaging core laboratory will conduct centralised review of all imaging data to ensure consistency.

Ancillary studies, biological specimens and secondary use of imaging data

The trial does not involve collection of biological specimens. Ancillary studies may be conducted using de-identified clinical and imaging data collected as part of the main trial, including subgroup analyses and secondary analyses of imaging data. No additional informed consent will be obtained for such analyses, as they do not involve additional interventions or specimen collection beyond the approved trial procedures.

Sample size estimation

Sample size calculation is based on the BAST trial subgroup results (NIHSS 4–7): the proportion of mRS 0–1 at 90 days was 79.8% in the NBP+rt PA group and 72.5% in the rt-PA group.18 Assuming TNK and rt-PA are efficacy-equivalent in mild disabling AIS, we hypothesise the same 7.3% absolute difference between the combination group and control group. With a two-sided α=0.05, power 1−β=0.8 and 5% dropout rate, the sample size required is 531 subjects per group, totalling 1062 subjects.

Statistical analysis

The primary analysis of efficacy endpoints will be based on the intention-to-treat (ITT) population, with a supportive analysis in the per-protocol (PP) population. The primary endpoint will be analysed via a modified Poisson regression model (treatment group as the sole predictor) to derive the risk ratio (RR) as the key treatment effect measure; adjusted RRs will be calculated by incorporating prespecified covariates. Both unadjusted and adjusted RRs (with two-sided 95% CIs) for the treatment and control groups will be reported, with the primary conclusion based on the unadjusted analysis, and subgroup analyses of the primary endpoint will be conducted by prespecified covariates. The 90-day mRS score will be analysed using the generalised OR (genOR), with adjusted genOR estimated via inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW). Dichotomous secondary endpoints will use the same modified Poisson regression model as the primary endpoint; continuous secondary endpoints will be assessed by mean differences (linear regression) or win ratios based on data distribution, and mortality data will be analysed via the Cox proportional hazards model to derive HR.20 Safety endpoints will be analysed using the modified Poisson regression model to generate RRs (both crude and adjusted effect sizes) for treatment differences. All statistical analyses will be performed following a statistical analysis plan which will be finalised before the database lock and unblinding of the trial.

The exclusion criterion related to inability to complete follow-up is applied only at the pre-randomisation screening stage and does not remove any randomised participant from the analysis population. We will make every effort to capture the primary outcome at 90±7 days, including follow-up contact for those who discontinue treatment. All randomised participants are retained in the ITT population. Missing primary outcome data will be addressed using multiple imputation, as prespecified in the statistical analysis plan. Sensitivity analyses will examine the impact of different missingness assumptions on the treatment effect estimate. No interim efficacy analysis is planned; the DSMB will monitor safety and overall trial conduct only, without interim efficacy stopping rules or generation of efficacy results. SAS Software V.9.4 (SAS Institute) and R V.4.4.1 will be used for the statistical analyses.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethical approval has been obtained from the Independent Ethics Committee of Xiangya Hospital (No. 2026020399), Central South University and each participating centre will obtain local approval before enrolment. The trial has been registered in ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT07369999). Study findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and conference presentations, and lay summaries will be prepared for participants and the public.

Discussion

AIS is a common cerebrovascular disorder caused by the interruption of cerebral blood perfusion, which induces neurological deficits. It not only imposes a heavy medical and economic burden on individual patients and society at large but also stands as one of the leading causes of long-term disability and death in adults. Among its subtypes, disabling minor ischaemic stroke is defined with a baseline quantitative criterion of an NIHSS score ≤5, and its core clinical features include persistent unilateral limb weakness or speech symptoms—specifically a score of at least 1 on either the language item or a single limb item of the NIHSS, with a mandatory score of 0 on the consciousness item.21–23 Though such mild yet disabling neurological deficits do not cause severe disturbance of consciousness, they still significantly impair patients’ ability to perform daily living activities and communicate, thus exerting an adverse impact on their quality of life.4 23 24 Intravenous thrombolysis within 4.5 hours is the recommended standard of care as the first-line treatment strategy.25–28 However, some patients still exhibit suboptimal neurological function recovery even after successful vascular recanalisation.29–31 This suggests that adding neuroprotective therapy to reperfusion may offer additional benefit in this population.

Based on the aforementioned clinical challenges, the BENEFIT-2 trial aims to address the core scientific question: in patients with disabling minor ischaemic stroke who have an NIHSS score 2–5 and present with persistent unilateral limb weakness or speech symptoms (defined as a score of 1 on either the language item or a single limb item of the NIHSS),22 can the synergistic therapeutic strategy of vascular recanalisation combined with neuroprotection effectively improve their neurological function recovery, reduce the risk of disability, and demonstrate favourable safety?

The conduct of this trial is well-justified by compelling clinical necessity. Previous studies suggest that combining reperfusion with neuroprotection may salvage the ischaemic penumbra and limit infarct progression.32 33 However, patients with mild disabling stroke do not always follow a benign course; up to 30% have functional disability at 90 days, and 8%–30% experience early neurological deterioration4 23; approximately 10%–20% also have concurrent large vessel occlusion, making them key targets for clinical intervention in stroke. Although this subtype is common in clinical practice, there is a lack of evidence-based evidence for targeted precision therapy tailored to its pathophysiological characteristics. Existing studies have mostly focused on monotherapies of either reperfusion or neuroprotection, and no large-sample randomised controlled trials (RCTs) have been conducted to investigate the synergistic therapy of vascular recanalisation combined with neuroprotection. Therefore, exploring a targeted combined therapeutic strategy and conducting the BENEFIT-2 trial are crucial to filling the gaps in clinical diagnostic and therapeutic evidence and improving the prognosis of this population.34 35

The BENEFIT-2 trial is the first RCT to investigate the efficacy and safety of the synergistic therapeutic strategy of TNK combined with NBP in patients with disabling minor ischaemic stroke who have an NIHSS score 2–5 and present with persistent unilateral limb weakness or speech symptoms. Its core value lies in filling the gap in clinical evidence in this field and enriching the repertoire of clinical therapeutic options. This study protocol is supported by sufficient theoretical and practical evidence: as the first-line thrombolytic agent recommended by clinical guidelines for this population,26 TNK achieves rapid vascular recanalisation and has the advantages of single bolus administration, facile delivery and enhanced fibrin specificity; and NBP salvages the ischaemic penumbra through multitargeted neuroprotective mechanisms.14 36–42 The combination of the two agents exerts a synergistic effect to further enhance therapeutic efficacy. Compared with the NBP plus rt-PA regimen in the BAST trial,18 TNK features a more convenient administration method, which is more conducive to clinical promotion and application in emergency settings. This multitargeted neuroprotective mechanism also provides a novel therapeutic insight for the management of disabling minor ischaemic stroke and is expected to optimise the current clinical diagnosis and treatment practice for this population.

To ensure the scientific rigour and feasibility of the trial, this study has designed a sequential treatment regimen of intravenous administration followed by oral administration. The rationality and scientific basis of this regimen are mainly reflected in three aspects. First, in terms of treatment timing, NBP therapy is initiated immediately after TNK thrombolysis within 4.5 hours of symptom onset. This time window is consistent with that recommended by clinical guidelines for thrombolysis in disabling minor ischaemic stroke, which ensures that the drugs exert a full protective effect on the cerebral ischaemic area in the acute phase and maximise therapeutic efficacy; meanwhile, subsequent oral therapy is initiated after 7 days of intravenous infusion, which is in line with the safety principles of clinical diagnosis and treatment for stroke. Second, the sequential course designed in this study strikes a favourable balance between efficacy and safety, making it more suitable for promotion and implementation in emergency departments and stroke units in real-world clinical settings. Third, in terms of strict implementation standards, the protocol requires detailed recording and verification of medication administration processes, which is the key to ensuring therapeutic efficacy and trial quality. Although the implementation of this requirement may face certain challenges in the conduct of the multicentre trial, the study has formulated corresponding quality control measures to address these issues.

In summary, the BENEFIT-2 trial accurately addresses the unmet clinical needs of patients with disabling minor ischaemic stroke who have an NIHSS score 2–5 and present with persistent unilateral limb weakness or speech symptoms. Through a scientifically rigorous trial design, it systematically investigates the application value of the synergistic therapeutic strategy of TNK combined with NBP. This study is expected to provide high-level evidence-based medical evidence for the clinical treatment of this patient population, enrich the repertoire of effective therapeutic options and ultimately help improve the clinical outcomes and quality of life of patients.

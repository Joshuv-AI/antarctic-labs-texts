Towards a characterization of elliptic Harnack inequality for jump processes
Jens Malmquist

Abstract
Let X X be an isotropic unimodal Lévy jump process on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . We develop probabilistic methods which in many cases allow us to determine whether X X satisfies the elliptic Harnack inequality (EHI), by looking only at the jump kernel of X X , and its truncated second moments. Both our positive results and our negative results can be applied to subordinated Brownian motions (SBMs) in particular. We produce the first known example of an SBM that does not satisfy EHI. We show that for many SBMs that were previously known to satisfy EHI (such as the geometric stable process, the iterated geometric stable process, and the relativistic geometric stable process), bounded perturbations of them also satisfy EHI (which was not previously clear). We show that certain SBMs with Laplace exponent ϕ ​ ( λ ) = Ω ~ ​ ( λ ) \phi(\lambda)=\tilde{\Omega}(\lambda) satisfy EHI, which previous methods were unable to determine.

Introduction

Harnack inequalities are the subject of significant research in probability, harmonic analysis, and partial differential equations. The earliest Harnack inequality, proved by Carl Gustav Axel von Harnack
[
12
]
, was for harmonic functions on the plane: if
B(x_{0},r)
and
B(x_{0},R)
are concentric balls in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, with
R>r>0
, and
u
is a non-negative solution to the Laplace equation (
\Delta u=0
) on
B(x_{0},R)
, then

\sup_{B(x_{0},r)}u\leq C\inf_{B(x_{0},r)}u,

(1.1)

where
C
is a constant depending on
R/r
but not on
u
.
Pini
[
19
]
and Hadamard
[
11
]
established an analogous inequality for caloric functions: if
R>r>0
,
t_{4}>t_{3}>t_{2}>t_{1}>0
, and
u\in C^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a non-negative solution to the heat equation (
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}-\Delta u=0
) on
(0,t_{4})\times B(0,R)
, then

\sup_{B(0,r)\times[t_{1},t_{2}]}u(t,x)\leq C\inf_{B(0,r)\times[t_{3},t_{4}]}u(t,x),

(1.2)

where
C
is a constant depending on
d
,
r
,
R
,
t_{1}
,
t_{2}
,
t_{3}
, and
t_{4}
. Both inequalities have since been generalized to broader settings and to different operators playing the role of
\Delta
. Generalizations of (
1.1
) are called
elliptic
Harnack inequalities and generalizations of (
1.2
) are called
parabolic
Harnack inequalities. For a more detailed introduction to the history and basic theory of Harnack inequalities, we refer the reader to
[
14
]
.

A major use of Harnack inequalities is that they often imply Hölder continuity for harmonic or caloric functions.
This theory is well-developed in the case of diffusions (or equivalently, local Dirichlet forms), where Harnack inequalities are a central ingredient in the De Giorgi-Nash-Moser theory in harmonic analysis and partial differential equations. In the case of jump processes (or equivalently, non-local Dirichlet forms), the theory is still in its infancy. Chen, Kumagai, and Wang
[
4
,
3
,
5
,
6
]
have done much of the early work in this area.
In
[
5
]
, they established that the parabolic Harnack inequality implies Hölder regularity for a certain class of jump processes on metric measure spaces. In
[
16
]
, the author showed that the conditions of
[
5
]
can be relaxed, in particular to allow for graphs. It is unknown whether the elliptic Harnack inequality implies Hölder regularity in the non-local case.

In this paper, we explore the following question: given a jump process
X
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, can we determine whether
X
satisfies the elliptic Harnack inequality by looking at the jump kernel of
X
? For simplicity, we assume
X
is an isotropic unimodal Lévy process.
We prove strong positive results and find a revealing counterexample. We do not achieve a full characterization of elliptic Harnack inequality for jump processes in terms of the jump kernel, but many naturally-arising jump kernels fall under the perview of our results, and we hope that this paper is the first step towards a more complete picture of what the jump kernel reveals about elliptic Harnack inequality. We also hope that our key constructions (the small/large and small/flat Meyer decompositions—see Section
3.1
) will be useful tools be adapted by future researchers.

Before we discuss the results, let us give some background information on isotropic unimodal Lévy jump processes and past work on the elliptic Harnack inequality for jump processes.
A
Lévy process
is a stochastic process with independent and stationary increments. Such a process is called
isotropic unimodal
if for all
t>0
, there exists a non-increasing kernel
m_{t}:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that
\mathbb{P}(X_{t}\in A|X_{0}=x)=\int_{A}m_{t}(|x|)\;\mathrm{d}x
for all Lebesgue-measurable
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
. So an
isotropic unimodal Lévy jump process
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a process that only moves via jumps, whose increments are independent, stationary, and radially symmetric in distribution, with a density that decreases with distance from the starting point.
Such a process always has a jump kernel
j(r)
, a non-increasing function from
(0,\infty)
to
[0,\infty)
such that for all Lebesgue-measurable
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the rate at which jumps such that
X_{t}-X_{t-}\in A
occur is
\int_{A}j(|x|)\;\mathrm{d}x
. The jump kernel always satisfies the
Lévy-Khintchine condition
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\min\{1,|x|^{2}\}j(|x|)\;\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

We are particularly interested in a subclass of isotropic unimodal Lévy processes called
subordinate Brownian motions
. A non-decreasing Lévy process
S=(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
[0,\infty)
is known as a
subordinator
. A subordinate Brownian motion is a process of the form
X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}=(B_{S_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
, where
S
is a subordinator and
B
is a standard Brownian motion (sped up or slowed down by a constant factor if desired) independent of
S
.

There are three key prior works to draw from when it comes to positive results establishing elliptic Harnack inequality for jump processes.
Kim and Mimica
[
15
]
provide a set of sufficient conditions that imply elliptic Harnack inequality at small scales for a subordinate Brownian motion. (We cite this result in its entirety as our Theorem
1.10
.) Grzywny
[
10
]
provides a set of conditions (involving the characteristic exponent of
X
) that imply elliptic Harnack inequality at small scales for isotropic unimodal Lévy processes.
In the much more general setting of jump processes on abstract metric measure spaces, Chen, Kumagai, and Wang
[
4
, Corollary 1.2]
characterize elliptic Harnack inequality in terms of Faber-Krahn, the Poincaré inequality, and cutoff-Sobolev. All three of these works use highly analytic proof techniques, and the statements of their sufficient conditions involve more sophisticated machinery than the jump process of
X
. By contrast, our proofs are mostly probabilistic and in many cases our results allow for verifying elliptic Harnack inequality via a glance at the jump kernel.

The exact statements of our main results are somewhat complicated, but let us summarize some of the highlights:

•

We show that if there exist exponents
\alpha
and
\beta
such that if
j(r)\asymp r^{d-2+\alpha}\left(\log(r^{-1})\right)^{-1-\beta}
for small
r
, and there exists a constant
c>0
such that
j(2r)\geq cj(r)
for all
r
, then
X
satisfies the elliptic Harnack inequality at small scales. Note that in order for the Lévy-Khintchine condition to be met, we must have either
\alpha>0
or
\alpha=0<\beta
. Otherwise, this result places no restrictions on
\alpha
and
\beta
.

•

Kim and Mimica
[
15
]
proved that geometric stable processes, iterated geometric stable processes, and relativistic geometric stable processes (see (
1.12
)-(
1.14
)) all satisfy the elliptic Harnack inequality at small scales. However, their method does not clearly extend to bounded perturbations of these processes. We show that bounded perturbations of these processes do indeed satisfy the elliptic Harnack inequality at small scales.

•

In Example
1.14
, we produce a subordinate Brownian motion that satisfies elliptic Harnack inequality at small scales despite clearly falling outside the scope of the results of
[
15
]
and
[
10
]
.

We are also interested in negative results.
Until this work, it was an open question whether there exists a subordinate Brownian motion that does not satisfy the elliptic Harnack inequality.
Bass and Chen
[
1
, Section 3]
give an example of a Lévy process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that does not satisfy the elliptic Harnack inequality. The process they consider has a high degree of non-regularity (it is not isotropic unimodal; its Lévy measure is singular with respect to the Lebesgue measure), and they exploit this in their proof. Grzywny and Kwaśnicki
[
9
, Example 5.5]
give another process, this one with more regularity, that also fails to satisfy the elliptic Harnack inequality. The process considered in
[
9
, Example 5.5]
is an isotropic unimodal Lévy process, but not a subordinated Brownian motion as the jump sizes are uniformly bounded from above, which is crucial to their proof. In Example
1.15
, we provide the first known example of a subordinate Brownian motion for which the elliptic Harnack inequality fails.

1.1
Setting and main results

Let
X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a continuous-time, Lévy jump process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with isotropic unimodal increments. In other words,
X
has each of the following:

•

Stationary increments: For all
t>s>0
,
X_{t}-X_{s}\overset{d}{=}X_{t-s}-X_{0}
.

•

Independent increments: For all
t_{n}>\cdots>t_{1}>t_{0}
, the collection
\left\{X_{t_{i}}-X_{t_{i-1}}:1\leq i\leq n\right\}
is independent.

•

Isotropic increments: For all
t>0
, the distribution of
X_{t}-X_{0}
is radially symmetric.

•

Unimodal increments: For all
t>0
,
X_{t}-X_{0}
has a density which is non-increasing in
|\cdot|
.

We use the notation
X_{t}
and
X(t)
interchangeably.
We assume that
X
is right-continuous, with left-limits (
cadlag
). For all
t>0
, we use the notation
X_{t-}
or
X(t-)
to denote the left-limit
\lim_{s\nearrow t}X_{s}
.

Given such a process, let
\mathbb{P}_{x}
and
\mathbb{E}_{x}
denote probabilities and expectations for the initial value
X_{0}=x
.
Let us denote hitting times and exit times as follows:

T_{A}:=\min\{t\geq 0:X_{t}\in A\},\qquad\tau_{A}:=\min\{t\geq 0:X_{t}\notin A\}.

For all
t>0
, let
\Delta X(t):=X_{t}-X_{t-}
denote the displacement vector of the jump taken at time
t
(or
0
, if there is not a jump taken at this time).
For all
r>0
, let
T^{(r)}
denote the first time that the process takes a jump of magnitude greater than or equal to
r
:

T^{(r)}:=\inf\{t>0:|\Delta X(t)|\geq r\}.

Such a process has an associated regular Dirichlet form
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})
.
By the Beurling-Deny formula
[
8
, Theorem 3.2.1]
,
\mathcal{E}
can be decomposed into a strongly local component, a jumping component, and a killing component:

\mathcal{E}(f,g)=\mathcal{E}^{c}(f,g)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathrm{diag}}(f(x)-f(y))(g(x)-g(y))\,\widehat{J}(dx,dy)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)g(x)\,\widehat{k}(dx)

where
\mathrm{diag}
denotes the diagonal of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathcal{E}^{c}
is a strongly local symmetric form,
\widehat{J}
is a symmetric non-negative Radon measure (which we call the
jumping measure
), and
\widehat{k}
is a non-negative Radon measure (which we call the
killing measure
).

In our setting,
X
is pure-jump, so the killing and strongly local components are identically
0
.
Since
X
is isotropic unimodal, it follows from the
(1)\Longrightarrow(3)
implication of the proposition on page 488 of
[
22
]
that there exists a non-increasing function
j:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that
\widehat{J}(dx,dy)=j(|x-y|)\,dx\,dy
.
We refer to the function
j(r)
as the
jump kernel
of
X
. We also sometimes use the notation
J(x,y):=j(|x-y|)
and
J(x,U):=\int_{U}j(|x-y|)\;\mathrm{d}y
.

If
X(t)\neq X(t-)
for some
t>0
, we refer to
X(t)-X(t-)
as the
displacement
of
X
at time
t
, which we denote by
\Delta X(t)
. We refer to
|\Delta X(t)|
as the
magnitude
of the jump at time
t
. There is a probabilistic interpretation of the jump kernel: for all Lebesgue-measurable
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, jumps in
X
with displacement in
A
occur as a Poisson point process with rate
\int_{A}j(|x|)\;\mathrm{d}x
.

Definition 1.1

(Harmonicity)
.

For all open
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we say that a function
h:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is
harmonic
on
D
(with respect to
X
) if for all open
U\Subset D
and for all
x
, we have

h(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[h(X_{\tau_{U}})\right].

(Note that when
X
is a jump process, the harmonicity of
h
on
D
depends on the values
h
takes on
D^{c}
, because for
U\Subset D
,
\tau_{U}
need not be in
D
.)

For all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
, let
B(x,r)
denote the open ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with center
x
and radius
r
. Given a Lebesgue-measurable set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
|A|
denote the Lebesgue measure of
A
.

Definition 1.2

(Elliptic Harnack inequality)
.

We say that
X
satisfies the
elliptic Harnack inequality
(
EHI
) if there exist constants
C\geq 1
and
\kappa\in(0,1)
such that for all
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
, if a function
h
is non-negative everywhere and harmonic with respect to
X
on
B(x_{0},r)
, then

C^{-1}h(y)\leq h(x)\leq Ch(y)

(1.3)

for all
x,y\in B(x_{0},\kappa r)
.

Given a set
S\subseteq(0,\infty)
, let us say that
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\in S)
holds if there exist
C
and
\kappa
such that (
1.3
) holds for all
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
r\in S
, all functions
h
that are non-negative everywhere and harmonic on
B(x_{0},r)
, and
x,y\in B(x_{0},\kappa r)
. Let us also write
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)
for
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\in(0,1])
,
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\geq 1)
for
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\in[1,\infty))
, etc.

In many applications, the elliptic Harnack inequality is primarily of interest at small scales. In fact, some other authors such as Kim and Mimica
[
15
]

define
the elliptic Harnack inequality as what we call
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)
.

For our main positive results, we make a simplifying assumption that there exists a
c_{j}>0
such that for all
r>0
,

j(2r)\geq c_{j}j(r).

(1.4)

In Section
2
, we use a compactness argument to show that if (
1.4
) holds, then
X
satisfies
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\in[a,b])
for all
0<a<b<\infty
. Therefore, when (
1.4
) is met,
EHI
only needs to be verified at very small and very large scales.

For all
r>0
, let us define the
truncated second moment
of
X
at
r
as

m_{2}(r):=\int_{B(0,r)}|x|^{2}j(|x|)\;\mathrm{d}x

(1.5)

and the
tail
of
X
at
r
as

\lambda(r):=\int_{B(0,r)^{c}}j(|x|)\;\mathrm{d}x.

(1.6)

Our main results are expressed in terms of comparisons between the quantities
m_{2}(r)
,
r^{d+2}j(r)
, and
r^{2}\lambda(r)
, all of which vary with
r
and can be thought of as having the same “units.” In the following lemma, we see that when (
1.4
) holds,
r^{d+2}j(r)
is at most a constant multiple of each of the other two quantities
m_{2}(r)
and
r^{2}\lambda(r)
.

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
X
be an isotropic unimodal Lévy process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. There exists a universal constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(d)>0
(depending only on
d
) such that for all
r>0
,

r^{d+2}j(r)\leq C_{1}m_{2}(r).

If
X
satisfies (
1.4
), then there exists a
C_{2}=C_{2}(c_{j},d)>0
(depending only on
c_{j}
and
d
) such that for all
r>0
,

r^{d+2}j(r)\leq C_{2}r^{2}\lambda(r).

Proof.

For all
r>0
,

\displaystyle m_{2}(r)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B(0,r)\setminus B(0,r/2)}|x|^{2}j(|x|)\;\mathrm{d}x\geq\left|B(0,r)\setminus B\left(0,\frac{r}{2}\right)\right|\cdot\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)^{2}j(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(1-2^{-d})|B(0,1)|r^{d+2}j(r).

If
X
satisfies (
1.4
), then

\lambda(r)\geq\int_{B(0,2r)\setminus B(0,r)}j(|x|)\;\mathrm{d}x\geq|B(0,2r)\setminus B(0,r)|\cdot c_{j}j(r)\geq(2^{d}-1)|B(0,1)|c_{j}r^{d}j(r).

∎

Our main result, in its full generality, is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X
be an isotropic unimodal Lévy process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying (
1.4
).
Let
j(r)
be its jump kernel, and let
m_{2}(r)
and
\lambda(r)
be as defined in (
1.5
) and (
1.6
). Let
M,C,c>0
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, and let
(r_{n})\subseteq(0,\infty)
be a sequence of positive numbers such that
M^{-1}\leq\frac{r_{n+1}}{r_{n}}\leq M
for all
n
.
Suppose that for all
n
, we have either

m_{2}(r_{n})\geq cr_{n}^{2}\lambda(r_{n})

(1.7)

or

m_{2}(r_{n})\leq C\left(r_{n}^{d+2}j(r_{n})\right)^{\varepsilon}\left(r_{n}^{2}\lambda(r_{n})\right)^{1-\varepsilon}.

(1.8)

If
r_{n}\to 0
, then
X
satisfies
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)
. If
r_{n}\to\infty
, then
X
satisfies
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\geq 1)
.

Note that each
r_{n}
need only satisfy one of (
1.7
) and (
1.8
), and it need not be the same one for all
n
.
However, in practice, it will usually be easiest to verify that just one of (
1.7
) or (
1.8
) holds as
r\to 0^{+}
or
r\to\infty
.

Before we state some corollaries of Theorem
1.4
, let us establish some notation for comparing quantities. Given functions
f
and
g
taking positive values, let us say that
f(x)\lesssim g(x)
on some set
S
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that
f(x)\leq Cg(x)
for all
x\in S
.
We refer to the constant
C
as the “implicit constant” in
\lesssim
.
If the set
S
is unspecified, assume it to be the entire domain of
f
and
g
.
We say that
f(x)\lesssim g(x)
as
x\to 0^{+}
if there exists a
\delta>0
such that
f(x)\lesssim g(x)
on
(0,\delta]
, and that
f(x)\lesssim g(x)
as
x\to\infty
if there exists a
C>0
such that
f(x)\lesssim g(x)
on
[C,\infty)
.
We use the notation
f(x)\gtrsim g(x)
when
g(x)\lesssim f(x)
, and
f(x)\asymp g(x)
when we have both
f(x)\lesssim g(x)
and
g(x)\lesssim f(x)
. Let us also say that
f(x)\ll g(x),x\to a
(or equivalently,
g(x)\gg f(x),x\to a
) if
\lim_{x\to a}f(x)/g(x)=0
. For sequences
(a_{n}),(b_{n})\subseteq(0,\infty)
, we similarly say that
a_{n}\ll b_{n}
(or
b_{n}\gg a_{n})
if
\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}/b_{n}=0
.
We also apply the notation
\lesssim
,
\gtrsim
, and
\asymp
to sequences, when thought of as functions on
\mathbb{N}
.

The following corollary describes the most common ways we check the conditions of Theorem
1.4
in practice.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
X
be an isotropic unimodal Lévy process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with jump kernel
j(r)
satisfying (
1.4
), and let
m_{2}(r)
and
\lambda(r)
be as defined in (
1.5
) and (
1.6
).

•

(a) If
m_{2}(r)\gtrsim r^{2}\lambda(r)
as
r\to 0^{+}
, then
X
satisfies
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)
.

•

(b) If
m_{2}(r)\gtrsim r^{2}\lambda(r)
as
r\to\infty
, then
X
satisfies
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\geq 1)
.

•

(c) If there exists an
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
such that
m_{2}(r)\lesssim(r^{d+2}j(r))^{\varepsilon}(r^{2}\lambda(r))^{1-\varepsilon}
as
r\to 0^{+}
, then
X
satisfies
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)
.

•

(d) If there exists an
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
such that
m_{2}(r)\lesssim(r^{d+2}j(r))^{\varepsilon}(r^{2}\lambda(r))^{1-\varepsilon}
as
r\to\infty
, then

satisfies
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\geq 1)
.

Proof.

For (a) and (c), we can take
r_{n}=2^{-n}
for all
n
. For (b) and (d), we can take
r_{n}=2^{n}
for all
n
. If necessary, discard finitely many terms. Then the result is a direct application of Theorem
1.4
.
∎

Note that the assumption of (
1.4
) must hold at all scales, not just as
r\to 0^{+}
or as
r\to\infty
, because for jump processes, the jump that causes the process to exit a bounded open region can take it very far away.
Most of the time, when we check (c) or (d) in Corollary
1.5
, we will simply take
\varepsilon=1
, so that
m_{2}(r)\lesssim(r^{d+2}j(r))^{\varepsilon}(r^{2}\lambda(r))^{1-\varepsilon}
becomes
m_{2}(r)\lesssim r^{d+2}j(r)
. However, in light of Lemma
3.8
, the existence of an

\varepsilon\in(0,1]
such that
m_{2}(r)\lesssim(r^{d+2}j(r))^{\varepsilon}(r^{2}\lambda(r))^{1-\varepsilon}
is in principle a weaker condition to check than
m_{2}(r)\lesssim r^{d+2}j(r)
.

The following corollary illustrates the power of Theorem
1.4
. Consider the ratio
\frac{r^{d+2}j(r)}{s^{d+2}j(s)}
, where
0<s\leq r
. If the growth of this ratio is polynomial or faster, or polylogarithmic or slower, then we have
EHI
. The only way for
EHI
to fail is if the ratio oscillates between fast-growing and slow-growing, or if its growth is slower than polynomial but faster than polylogarithmic (for example, if
j(r)\asymp r^{-d-2}\exp(-\sqrt{\log(r^{-1})})
as
r\to 0^{+}
). Most naturally-arising isotropic unimodal Lévy jump processes such that (
1.4
) holds seem to satisfy one of the conditions of this corollary, and therefore
EHI
.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
X
be an isotropic unimodal Lévy jump process with jump kernel
j(r)
, satisfying (
1.4
).

•

(a) If there exist

such that

\frac{r^{d+2}j(r)}{s^{d+2}j(s)}\geq c\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{\alpha}\qquad\mbox{for all $0<s\leq r\leq R$},

then

m_{2}(r)\lesssim r^{d+2}j(r)\qquad\mbox{as $r\to 0^{+}$}

and therefore

satisfies
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)
.

•

(b) If
j(r)\gtrsim r^{-d-2}
as
r\to\infty
, and there exist
c,R,\alpha>0
such that

\frac{r^{d+2}j(r)}{s^{d+2}j(s)}\geq c\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{\alpha}\qquad\mbox{for all $R\leq s\leq r$},

then

m_{2}(r)\lesssim r^{d+2}j(r)\qquad\mbox{as $r\to\infty$}

and therefore

satisfies
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\geq 1)
.

(c) If

and therefore

satisfies
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)
.

•

(d) If there exist
C,R,\alpha>0
such that

\frac{r^{d+2}j(r)}{s^{d+2}j(s)}\leq C\left(\frac{\log r}{\log s}\right)^{1+\alpha}\qquad\mbox{for all $R\leq s\leq r$},

then

m_{2}(r)\gtrsim r^{2}\lambda(r)\asymp r^{d+2}j(r)\qquad\mbox{as $r\to\infty$}

and therefore

satisfies
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\geq 1)
.

Let us consider some examples.

Example 1.7
.

Let

be an isotropic unimodal Lévy jump process with jump kernel

, satisfying (

), such that

for some

This satisfies all four conditions of Corollary
1.5
, so

satisfies

.

.

Let
X
be an isotropic unimodal Lévy jump process with jump kernel

and

so

satisfies
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)

(a).

1.2
Subordinate Brownian motions

We are particularly interested in applying our results to a class of processes called subordinate Brownian motions, which we introduce in this subsection. We show that there exists a subordinate Brownian motion that does not satisfy
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)
. As far as we could tell, this was previously an open question. Our counterexample is highly regular, and sheds light on what must go wrong in order for a subordinate Brownian motion to not satisfy
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)
. We also prove
EHI
for some subordinate Brownian motions for which it was previously unknown.

Let
S=(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a right-continuous, non-decreasing Lévy process on
[0,\infty)
with
S_{0}=0
. We refer to such a process as a
subordinator
. Associated with
S
is a function
\phi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
called the
Laplace exponent
, which satisfies

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\lambda S_{t}}\right]=e^{-\phi(\lambda)t}\qquad\mbox{for all $\lambda>0$, $t>0$}.

The Laplace exponent is always of the form

\phi(\lambda)=\gamma\lambda+\int_{(0,\infty)}(1-e^{-\lambda t})\,\mu(\mathrm{d}t)

(1.9)

for some
\gamma>0
(called the
drift
) and a measure
\mu
on
(0,\infty)
(called the Lévy measure of
S
) such that

\int_{(0,\infty)}(1\wedge t)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}t)<\infty.

(1.10)

Equation (
1.10
) is called the Lévy-Khintchine condition for subordinators. The Lévy measure of
S
also has a probabilistic interpretation: for all Lebesgue-measurable
A\subseteq(0,\infty)
,
\mu(A)
is the rate at which
S
takes jumps such that
S_{t}-S_{t-}\in A
.
If
0<\mu(A)<\infty
, then the set of times
t
such that
S_{t}-S_{t-}\in A
is a Poisson point process with intensity
\mu(A)
.

The Laplace exponent is always a
Bernstein function
:
\phi\in C^{\infty}(0,\infty)
with
\phi^{(k)}\geq 0
for all
k\in\{0,1,3,5,7,9,\dots\}
and
\phi^{(k)}\leq 0
for all
k\in\{2,4,6,8,\dots\}
. Conversely, every Bernstein function is the Laplace exponent of some subordinator, and every
\gamma>0
and
\mu
satisfying the Lévy-Khintchine condition are the drift and Lévy measure of some subordinator.

The potential measure of a subordinator is defined as

U(A):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(S_{t}\in A)\;\mathrm{d}t.

Let
B=(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, independent of
S
, with twice the speed of a standard Brownian motion. We define a new process
X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}:=(B_{S_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
, and refer to
X
as a
subordinate Brownian motion
(with subordinator
S
). This new process
X
is an isotropic unimodal Lévy process. If the drift
\gamma
of
S
is
0
, then
X
is pure-jump. The jump kernel of
X
is given by

j(r)=\int_{(0,\infty)}(4\pi t)^{-d/2}\exp(-\frac{r^2}{4t})\mu(\mathrm{d}t).

(1.11)

Kim and Mimica
[
15
]
prove that a large class of subordinate Brownian motions satisfy
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)
.

Theorem 1.10

(Kim-Mimica)
.

Let
X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}=(B_{S_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
be a subordinate Brownian motion, where
S
is a subordinator and
B
is a Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, independent of
S
. Assume there exists a
C>0
such that the jump kernel of
X
satisfies

j(r+1)\leq j(r)\leq Cj(r+1)\qquad\mbox{for all $r>1$}.

Suppose also that the subordinator
S
satisfies the three following conditions:

A-1

The potential measure of
S
has decreasing density. In other words, there exists a decreasing function
u(t)
such that
U(dt)=u(t)\;\mathrm{d}t
.

A-2

The Lévy measure of
S
is infinite (in other words,
\mu((0,\infty))=\infty
) and has a decreasing density.

A-3

There exist constants
\sigma>0
,
\lambda_{0}>0
, and
\delta\in(0,1]
such that

\frac{\phi^{\prime}(\lambda x)}{\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)}\leq\sigma x^{-\delta}\qquad\mbox{for all $x\geq 1$ and $\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}$}.

Then
X
satisfies
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)
.

Theorem
1.10
can be used to show that each of the following subordinate Brownian motions satisfies
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)
:

•

Geometric stable processes:

\phi(\lambda)=\log(1+\lambda^{\beta/2}),\qquad 0<\beta\leq 2.

(1.12)

•

Iterated geometric stable processes:

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\log(1+\lambda^{\beta/2}),\quad 0<\beta\leq 2,

\displaystyle\phi_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\phi_{n}\circ\phi_{1}\qquad\qquad\mbox{for all $n$}.

(1.13)

•

Relativistic geometric stable processes:

\phi(\lambda)=\log(1+\left( \lambda+m^{\beta/2} \right)^{2/\beta} -m)\qquad 0<\beta\leq 2.

(1.14)

Note that condition
A-3
implies
\gamma=0
(by taking
x\to\infty
and applying (
1.9
)), so Theorem
1.10
only applies to jump processes.

We say that two isotropic unimodal Lévy jump processes
X
and
Y
, with jump kernels
j_{X}(r)
and
j_{Y}(r)
respectively, are
bounded perturbations
of one another if there exist constants
C\geq c>0
such that
cj_{X}(r)\leq j_{Y}(r)\leq Cj_{X}(r)
for all
r>0
. The results of
[
15
]
do not extend immediately to bounded perturbations of the above examples (geometric stable, iterated geometric stable, and relativistic geometric stable), but our results do.

Example 1.11
.

Let
X
be a bounded perturbation of the geometric stable process, with parameter
\beta\in(0,2]
.
Šikić, Song, and Vondraček
[
21
, Theorem 3.4]
showed that
j(r)\asymp r^{-d}
as

. Thus, for small values

, we have

\frac{r^{d+2}j(r)}{s^{d+2}j(s)}\asymp\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{2},

so by Corollary

(a) (with

),

satisfies
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)
. If
\beta<2
, then by
[

as

\frac{r^{d+2}j(r)}{s^{d+2}j(s)}\asymp\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{2-\beta}

for large

,
so we also have
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\geq 1)
by Corollary
1.6
(b).

To show that bounded perturbations of the iterated geometric stable and relativistic geometric stable processes satisfy
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)
, we use
[

to get the asymptotic form of

for

. The details of these calculations are shown in Section

Example 1.12
.

Let
X
be a bounded perturbation of the relativistic geometric stable process. Then
j(r)\asymp r^{-d}
as

, so
X
satisfies
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)
.

For all

\log^{(n)}
denote the

-fold composition of
\log
.

Example 1.13
.

Let
X
be a bounded perturbation of the iterated geometric stable process, with

j(r)\asymp\frac{r^{-d}}{\log(r^{-2})\log^{(2)}(r^{-2})\cdots\log^{(n-1)}(r^{-2})}\qquad\mbox{as $r\to 0^{+}$}.

For large

,

\frac{r^{d+2}j(r)}{s^{d+2}j(s)}\asymp\frac{r^{2}\left(\log(r^{-2})\right)^{-1}\left(\log^{(2)}(r^{-2})\right)^{-1}\cdots\left(\log^{(n-1)}(r^{-2})\right)^{-1}}{s^{2}\left(\log(s^{-2})\right)^{-1}\left(\log^{(2)}(s^{-2})\right)^{-1}\cdots\left(\log^{(n-1)}(s^{-2})\right)^{-1}}\geq\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{2}

so we have
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)
by Corollary
1.6
(a).

For our last example, we identify a subordinate Brownian motion that does not satisfy condition

of Kim and Mimica, but does satisfy
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)
.

Example 1.14
.

Let

satisfying (

, which implies that

1.3
Counterexample and negative result

In light of Theorem
1.4
, its corollaries, and the above examples, a natural question to ask is whether every isotropic unimodal Lévy jump process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that satisfies (
1.4
) also satisfies
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)
. Another question that has been open until this work (and was the original motivator that led to the research in this chapter) is whether every subordinate Brownian motion satisfies
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)
. We show that the answer to both of these questions is “no,” as the following process is a counterexample to both.

Example 1.15
.

Let
S=(S_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a subordinator with drift
0
and Lévy measure

\mu(\mathrm{d}t)=\frac{2}{3}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{3n^{2}}\min\left\{1,\left(2^{2n^{2}}t\right)^{-3}\right\}\;\mathrm{d}t.

Let
X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}=(B_{S_{t}})_{t\geq 0}
be a subordinate Brownian motion with subordinator
S
.
Let
\mu(t):=\frac{2}{3}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{3n^{2}}\min\left\{1,\left(2^{-2n^{2}}t\right)^{-3}\right\}
, so that
\mu(\mathrm{d}t)=\mu(t)\;\mathrm{d}t
. Note that
\mu(t)\asymp\mu(2t)
, since
\mu
is the sum of infinitely many non-increasing functions that decay no faster than
t^{-3}
. From this, it is easy to show that the jump kernel of
X
satisfies (
1.4
). However, we show in Section
6.3
that
X
does not satisfy
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)
.

We also prove the following general negative result.

Theorem 1.16
.

Let
X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be an isotropic unimodal Lévy jujmp process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with jump kernel
j(r)
. Suppose there exists a sequence
(r_{n})\subseteq(0,\infty)
such that
r_{n}\to 0^{+}
and

r_{n}^{2}\lambda(r_{n})\lesssim m_{2}(r_{n})\ll(r_{n}^{d+2}j(r_{n}))^{-\frac{2}{d}}(r_{n}^{2}\lambda(r_{n}))^{\frac{d+2}{2}}.

(1.15)

Then
X
does not satisfy
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)
. Similarly, if there exists a sequence
(r_{n})\subseteq(0,\infty)
such that
r_{n}\to\infty
and (
1.15
) holds, then
X
does not satisfy
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\geq 1)
.

Note that Theorem
1.16
does not require (
1.4
).

2
Green’s function and Poisson kernel

In this section, we introduce the Green’s function and Poisson kernel of
X
, and then give an equivalent formulation of the condition
EHI
in terms of the Poisson kernel, which we will use to prove our main results.

Let
p_{t}(x,y)
be the heat kernel of
X
.
For all
t>0
,
p_{t}(\cdot,\cdot)
is a symmetric function such that
p_{t}(x,\cdot)
is the density of
X_{t}
given an initial value of
X_{0}=x
. For all open sets
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
p^{D}_{t}(x,y)
be the heat kernel of the process killed upon exiting
D
. Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{t}\in A)=\int_{A}p_{t}(x,y)\,dy;\\
\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{t}\in A,\tau_{D}>t)=\int_{A}p^{D}_{t}(x,y)\,dy\end{split}

(2.1)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, all
t>0
, and all measurable
A
. The killed heat kernel
p^{D}_{t}
is also symmetric. Because
X
is isotropic unimodal, it can be shown that
p_{t}
and
p^{D}_{t}
are jointly continuous and isotropic unimodal.

Let us define the Green’s functions
G
and
G_{D}
, as

G(x,y):=\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{t}(x,y)\,dt,\qquad G_{D}(x,y):=\int_{0}^{\infty}p^{D}_{t}(x,y)\,dt.

(2.2)

The Green’s function has a probabilistic interpretation. For all measurable
A\subseteq D
, if the process starts at
X_{0}=x
, then
\int_{A}G_{D}(x,y)\,dy
is the expected amount of time the process spends in
A
before time
\tau_{D}
.

By a result of Ikeda and Watanabe
[
13
, Theorem 1]
, if
D
is a measurable set, then for any
x\in D
and any function
h
that is harmonic on
D
,

h(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}[h(X_{\tau_{D}})]=\int_{z\in D}\int_{w\in D^{c}}G_{D}(x,z)j(|w-z|)h(w)\,dw\,dz.

By Tonelli’s theorem, we can switch the order of integration. The result is

h(x)=\int_{w\in D^{c}}K_{D}(x,w)h(w)\,dw,

(2.3)

where
K_{D}
is the Poisson kernel, defined by

K_{D}(x,w):=\int_{z\in D}G_{D}(x,z)j(|w-z|)\,dz.

(2.4)

Definition 2.1

(Characterization of
EHI
in terms of Poisson kernel)
.

Let
S\subseteq(0,\infty)
.
We say that
X
satisfies
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehiPoisson]{\mathrm{EHI}_{\mathrm{PK}}}(r\in S)
if there exist constants
C\geq 1
and
\kappa\in(0,1)
such that for all
r\in S
,
x\in B(0,\kappa r)
, and
w\in B(0,r)^{c}
, we have

C^{-1}K_{B(0,r)}(-x,w)\leq K_{B(0,r)}(x,w)\leq CK_{B(0,r)}(-x,w).

Proposition 2.2
.

For all
S\subseteq(0,\infty)
, the conditions
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\in S)
and
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehiPoisson]{\mathrm{EHI}_{\mathrm{PK}}}(r\in S)
are equivalent.

In order to prove Proposition
2.2
, we define a list of related conditions, all of which we show are equivalent, which “bridge the gap” between
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\in S)
and
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehiPoisson]{\mathrm{EHI}_{\mathrm{PK}}}(r\in S)
. To make the proof more readable, we split this proof into several lemmas, with a separate lemma for each implication.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
S\subseteq(0,\infty)
.

•

We say that
X
satisfies
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:EquivalentEhis]{\mathrm{\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}^{\prime}}}(r\in S)
if there exist constants
C\geq 1
and
\kappa\in(0,1)
such that for all
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r\in S
, if a function
h
is non-negative everywhere and harmonic with respect to
X
on
B(x_{0},r)
, then

C^{-1}h(y)\leq h(x)\leq Ch(y)

for all
x,y\in B(x_{0},\kappa r)
such that
x_{0}
is the midpoint between
x
and
y
.

•

We say that
X
satisfies
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:EquivalentEhis]{\mathrm{\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}^{\prime\prime}}}(r\in S)
if there exist constants
C\geq 1
and
\kappa\in(0,1)
such that for all
r\in S
, if a function
h
is non-negative everywhere and harmonic with respect to
X
on
B(0,r)
, then

C^{-1}h(-x)\leq h(x)\leq Ch(-x)

for all
x\in B(0,\kappa r)
.

Remark 2.4
.

Clearly,
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\in S)\Longrightarrow\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:EquivalentEhis]{\mathrm{\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}^{\prime}}}(r\in S)\Longrightarrow\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:EquivalentEhis]{\mathrm{\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}^{\prime\prime}}}(r\in S)
, since each condition along this chain requires (
1.3
) to hold on a smaller set of values
(x_{0},x,y)
than the condition preceding it. Since
X
is an isotropic unimodal Lévy process, it is also easy to show that
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:EquivalentEhis]{\mathrm{\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}^{\prime\prime}}}(r\in S)\Longrightarrow\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:EquivalentEhis]{\mathrm{\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}^{\prime}}}(r\in S)
by shifting the argument of the function
h
. Therefore, we already have

.

Let
X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be an isotropic unimodal Lévy jump process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let
X=X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}+\hat{X}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
be the small/flat decomposition of
X
at
r_{0}
, as defined in Definition
3.2
. Let
\left(T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{k}\right)_{k=0}^{\infty}
be the sequence of stopping times defined recursively by

T_{0}:=0,\qquad T_{k+1}=\inf\left\{t>T_{k}:\hat{X}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{t}\neq\hat{X}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{t-}\right\}.

In other words, for all
k\geq 1
,
T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{k}
is the
k
th time that
\hat{X}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
takes a jump. Note that
T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{1}
is equal to
T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
(as defined in Definition
3.2
).

Recall how the heat kernels
p_{t}
and
p^{D}_{t}
, and the Green’s functions
G
and
G_{D}
, are defined in (
2.1
) and (
2.2
). We would like to decompose the Green’s function
G_{D}(x,y)
into an infinite sum
G_{D}(x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}G_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,k}(x,y)
, where for each
k
, for all measurable
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\int_{A}G_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,k}(x,y)\;\mathrm{d}y=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(X_{t}\in A,\tau_{D}>t,T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{k}\leq t<T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{k+1}\right)\;\mathrm{d}t.

(3.3)

Let us start by defining the functions
G_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,k}:D\times D\to[0,\infty)
recursively. Then we will show that these functions satisfy (
3.3
).

The following definition makes extensive use of the notation (
\lambda\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle
,
X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
,
T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
, etc.) from Definition
3.2
. From now on, we will use this notation without commenting on it.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be an isotropic unimodal Lévy jump process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Fix an open set
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Let
p^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,D}_{t}(\cdot,\cdot)
be the heat kernel of the process
X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
killed upon exiting
D
.
Define the function
G_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}:D\times D\to[0,\infty)
by

G_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}(x,y):=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle t}p^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,D}_{t}(x,y)\;\mathrm{d}t.

For all
k
such that the function
G_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,k}(\cdot,\cdot)
has been defined, define
G_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,k+1}(\cdot,\cdot)
by

G_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,k+1}(x,y):=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathbf{1}\left\{T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}<\tau_{D}\right\}G_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,k}(X_{T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}},y)\right].

Furthermore, let

G_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}(x,y):=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}G_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,k}(x,y).

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be an isotropic unimodal Lévy jump process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let the functions
G_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,k}(\cdot,\cdot)
be as defined in Definition
3.6
. Then
G_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}(\cdot,\cdot)
is symmetric, and each
G_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,k}(\cdot,\cdot)
satisfies (
3.3
).

Proof.

The symmetry of
G_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}(\cdot,\cdot)
is clear from the definition, since the heat kernel
p^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,D}_{D}(x,y)
of
X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
(killed upon exiting
D
) is symmetric. Let us prove (
3.3
) by induction on
k
. For the base case, because
T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
is Exponential(
\lambda\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle
) and independent of the process
X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(X_{t}\in A,\tau_{D}>t,T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{0}\leq t<T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{1}\right)\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{t}\in A,\tau^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{D}>t,T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}>t\right)\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle t}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{t}\in A,\tau^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{D}>t\right)\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle t}\int_{A}p^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,D}_{t}(x,y)\;\mathrm{d}y\;\mathrm{d}t.

Tonelli’s theorem allows us to switch the order of integration, which yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(X_{t}\in A,\tau_{D}>t,T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{0}\leq t<T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{1}\right)\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{A}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle t}p^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,D}_{t}(x,y)\;\mathrm{d}t\;\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=:\int_{A}G_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}(x,y)\;\mathrm{d}y,

thus confirming (
3.3
) for
k=0
. Assume (
3.3
) holds for
k
. By the Strong Markov property, the inductive hypothesis, and multiple uses of Tonelli’s theorem,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(X_{t}\in A,\tau_{D}>t,T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{k+1}\leq t<T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{k+2}\right)\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}\left\{X_{t}\in A,\tau_{D}>t,T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{k+1}\leq t<T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{k+2}\right\}\;\mathrm{d}t\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathbf{1}\left\{T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{1}<\tau_{D}\right\}\int_{A}G_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,k}(X_{T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{1}},y)\;\mathrm{d}y\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{A}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathbf{1}\left\{T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{1}<\tau_{D}\right\}G_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,k}(X_{T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{1}},y)\right]\;\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=:\int_{A}G_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,k+1}(x,y)\;\mathrm{d}y.

∎

Let us similarly decompose the Poisson kernel.
Recall that in the small/flat decomposition, we decompose the jump kernel
j(r)
into
j^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(r)+\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(r)
. The Poisson kernel can be decomposed into the sum
K_{D}=K^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}_{D}+K^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}_{D}
, where

K_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}(x,w):=\int_{z\in D}G_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}(x,z)j^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(|w-z|)\;\mathrm{d}z

(3.4)

and

K_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}(x,w):=\int_{z\in D}\left(G_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}(x,z)\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(|w-z|)+G_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}(x,z)j(|w-z|)\right)\;\mathrm{d}z.

(3.5)

Probabilistically,
K_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}(x,z)
accounts for
X
traveling from
x
to
z
without
\hat{X}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
taking any jumps, and
K_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}(x,z)
accounts for
X
traveling from
x
to
z
involving at least one jump from
\hat{X}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
.
Note that

K_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}(x,w)\geq\int_{z\in D}G_{D}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,1}(x,z)j^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(|w-z|)\;\mathrm{d}z.

(3.6)

Note that all the constructions in this subsection generalize to the other Meyer decompositions. We only explicitly present them for the small/flat decomposition, since that is what we will use in the proof of our main results.

3.3
Properties of the small/flat decomposition

When
X
satisfies (
1.4
), the small/flat decomposition has two important properties which are essential to our proofs: the first of these properties is that
m_{2}\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle\asymp m_{2}(r_{0})
and
\lambda\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle\asymp\lambda(r_{0})
, and the second is that
\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(r_{1})\asymp\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(r_{2})
whenever
r_{1},r_{2}\lesssim r_{0}
(even if one of them is very close to
0
). We prove these properties in the following two lemmas. Then in Proposition
3.10
we use these properties to prove that
h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}_{B(0,r)}(x,w)\asymp h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}_{B(0,r)}(-x,w)
for
x\in B(0,r)
and non-negative functions
h
that are harmonic on some
D\Supset B(0,r)
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
X
be an isotropic unimodal Lévy jump process. For all
r_{0}>0
, we have

\frac{1}{2}m_{2}(r_{0})\leq m_{2}\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle\leq m_{2}(r_{0})\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\lambda\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle\geq\lambda(r_{0}).

If
X
satisfies (
1.4
) for all
r>0
, then we also have

\lambda\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle\lesssim\lambda(r_{0}),

where the constant implicit in
\lesssim
depends only on
c_{j}
(from (
1.4
)) and
d
.

Proof.

For all
r\leq r_{0}
, it follows from the definition of the small/flat decomposition that
\frac{1}{2}j(r)\leq j(r)-\frac{1}{2}j(r_{0})=:j^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(r)\leq j(r)
. Thus,
\frac{1}{2}m_{2}(r_{0})\leq m_{2}\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle\leq m_{2}(r_{0})
. It also follows easily from the definition that
\lambda\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle\geq\lambda(r_{0})
. If (
1.4
) holds, then

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle-\lambda(r_{0})}{\lambda(r_{0})}

\displaystyle=\frac{|B(0,r_{0})|\cdot\frac{1}{2}j(r_{0})}{\int_{B(0,r_{0})^{c}}j(|x|)\;\mathrm{d}x}\leq\frac{|B(0,r_{0})|\cdot\frac{1}{2}j(r_{0})}{\int_{B(0,2r_{0})\setminus B(0,r_{0})}j(|x|)\;\mathrm{d}x}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|B(0,r_{0})|\cdot\frac{1}{2}j(r_{0})}{|B(0,2r_{0})\setminus B(0,r_{0})|\cdot c_{j}j(r_{0})}=\frac{1}{2(2^{d}-1)c_{j}}.

∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
X
be an isotropic unimodal Lévy jump process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. If (
1.4
) holds for all
r>0
, then for all
L>1
, there exists a constant
C_{\ref{EHI:l:flatness}}(L,c_{j})
(depending on
L
and
c_{j}
) such that for all
r_{0}>0
, for all
r_{1},r_{2}\in(0,Lr_{0}]
, we have

\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(r_{1})\leq C_{\ref{EHI:l:flatness}}(L,c_{j})\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(r_{2}).

Proof.

For all
r\in(0,r_{0}]
, we have
\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(r)=\frac{1}{2}j(r_{0})
. For all
r\in(r_{0},Lr_{0}]
, we have
j(Lr_{0})\leq\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(r)\leq j(r_{0})
. Therefore,

\min\left\{\frac{1}{2}j(r_{0}),j(Lr_{0})\right\}\leq\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(r_{1}),\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(r_{2})\leq j(r_{0}).

(3.7)

By (
3.7
) and (
1.4
),

\frac{\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(r_{1})}{\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(r_{2})}\leq\max\left\{2,\frac{j(r_{0})}{j(Lr_{0})}\right\}\leq\max\left\{2,c_{j}^{-\left\lceil\log_{2}L\right\rceil}\right\}.

∎

Now that we have defined the small/flat decomposition and shown the basic properties above, we can explain how it will be used in the proof of our main results. Suppose a function
h
is non-negative everywhere and harmonic on a set
D
such that the ball
B(0,r)
is compactly supported in
D
. The process
(h(X_{t\wedge\tau_{B(0,r)}}))_{t\geq 0}
is a martingale with respect to the filtration
\mathcal{F}^{X}_{t}:=\sigma\left(\left\{X_{s}:0\leq s\leq t\right\}\right)
.
Fix
x\in B(0,r)
.
We would like to show that
h(x)
and
h(-x)
are comparable.

Consider the small/flat decomposition of
X
at some
r_{0}
smaller than but within a large multiplicative constant of
r
.
Let
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the filtration that takes into account not only
X
, but also
X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
and
\hat{X}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
from the small/flat decomposition at
r_{0}
, so that
T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
is a stopping time with respect to
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
. The process
(h(X_{t\wedge\tau_{B(0,r)}}))_{t\geq 0}
is still a martingale with respect to
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
, because for all
0\leq s\leq t
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}[h(X_{t\wedge\tau_{B(0,r)}})|\mathcal{F}_{s}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathbb{E}_{x}[h(X_{t\wedge\tau_{B(0,r)}})|\mathcal{F}^{X}_{s}]\Big|\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}[h(X_{s\wedge\tau_{B(0,r)}})|\mathcal{F}_{s}]=h(X_{s\wedge\tau_{B(0,r)}}).

Since
\tau_{B(0,r)}
and
T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
are both stopping times with respect to this new filtration, for all
x\in{B(0,r)}
we have both

h(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[h(X_{\tau_{B(0,r)}})\right]=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[h(X_{\tau_{B(0,r)}})\mathbf{1}\left\{\tau_{B(0,r)}<T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}\right\}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[h(X_{\tau_{B(0,r)}})\mathbf{1}\left\{\tau_{B(0,r)}\geq T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}\right\}\right]

and

h(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[h(X_{T\wedge\tau_{B(0,r)}})\right]=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[h(X_{\tau_{B(0,r)}})\mathbf{1}\left\{\tau_{B(0,r)}<T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}\right\}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[h(X_{T})\mathbf{1}\left\{\tau_{B(0,r)}\geq T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}\right\}\right].

Therefore, we can write
h(x)
as the sum of
h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}_{B(0,r)}(x)
and
h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}_{B(0,r)}(x)
, where

h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}_{B(0,r)}(x):=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[h(X_{\tau_{B(0,r)}})\mathbf{1}\left\{\tau_{B(0,r)}<T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}\right\}\right]

(3.8)

and
h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}_{B(0,r)}(x)
is the common value of

\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[h(X_{\tau_{B(0,r)}})\mathbf{1}\left\{\tau_{B(0,r)}\geq T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}\right\}\right]=:h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}_{B(0,r)}(x):=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[h(X_{T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}})\mathbf{1}\left\{\tau_{B(0,r)}\geq T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}\right\}\right].

(3.9)

We will use these two different expressions for
h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}_{B(0,r)}(x)
for different purposes. Using right-hand side of (
3.9
) and the flatness of
\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
, we show that
h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}_{B(0,r)}(x)\asymp h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}_{B(0,r)}(-x)
for all
x\in B(0,r)
. Later, in Section
5
, we use the left-hand side of (
3.9
) to compare
h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}_{B(0,r)}(x)
with
h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}_{B(0,r)}(x)
.

Recall how the Poisson kernel
K_{D}
is decomposed into
K^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}_{D}
and
K^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}_{D}
, as given by (
3.4
) and (
3.5
).
It follows from the Ikeda-Watanabe formula (cf.
[
13
]
) that

h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}_{B(0,r)}(x)=\int_{w\in B(0,r)^{c}}K^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}_{B(0,r)}(x,w)h(w)\;\mathrm{d}w,\qquad h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}_{B(0,r)}(x)=\int_{w\in B(0,r)^{c}}K^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}_{B(0,r)}(x,w)h(w)\;\mathrm{d}w.

(3.10)

In the following proposition, we show that
h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}_{B(0,r)}(x)\asymp h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}_{B(0,r)}(-x)
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
X
be an isotropic unimodal Lévy jump process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying (
1.4
). Suppose
B(0,r)\Subset D
, and let
h
be a function that is non-negative everywhere and harmonic on
D
. Suppose
r_{0}\geq r/L
for some
L>1
. Then there exists a universal constant
C_{\ref{EHI:p:h1(x)=h1(-x)}}(L,c_{j})
(depending on
L
and
c_{j}
) such that for all
x\in B(0,r)
,

C_{\ref{EHI:p:h1(x)=h1(-x)}}(L,c_{j})^{-1}h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}_{B(0,r)}(-x)\leq h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}_{B(0,r)}(x)\leq C_{\ref{EHI:p:h1(x)=h1(-x)}}(L,c_{j})h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}_{B(0,r)}(-x).

Proof.

Let
\eta_{x}
be the measure such that

\eta_{x}(E):=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{B(0,r)}>T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle},X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}}\in E\right)\qquad\mbox{ for all measurable $E\subseteq B(0,r)$}.

Consider
\Delta X(T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle})
, the displacement vector of the first flattening jump. Note that
X_{T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}}=X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}}+\Delta X(T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle})
and that
\Delta X(T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle})
has density
\frac{\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(|\cdot|)}{\lambda\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
. Thus,

\displaystyle h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}_{B(0,r)}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[h(X_{T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}})\mathbf{1}\left\{\tau_{B(0,r)}\geq T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[h\left(X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}}+\Delta X(T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle})\right)\mathbf{1}\left\{\tau^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{B(0,r)}>T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{z\in B(0,r)}\frac{\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(|w-z|)}{\lambda\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}h(w)\,\eta_{x}(\mathrm{d}z)\;\mathrm{d}w.

(3.11)

Note that the process
-X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}+\hat{X}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
has the same law as
X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}+\hat{X}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}=X
, because
-X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
has the same law as
X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
, and both are independent of
\hat{X}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
.
By symmetry, if
T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
occurs before
X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
exits
B(0,r)
, then
T
also occurs before
-X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{t}
exits
B(0,r)
. Thus,

\displaystyle h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}_{B(0,r)}(-x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{-x}\left[h\left(X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}}+\Delta X(T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle})\right)\mathbf{1}\left\{\tau^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{B(0,r)}>T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[h\left(-X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}}+\Delta X(T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle})\right)\mathbf{1}\left\{\tau^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{B(0,r)}>T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{z\in B(0,r)}\frac{\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(|w+z|)}{\lambda\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}h(w)\,\eta_{x}(\mathrm{d}z)\;\mathrm{d}w.

(3.12)

The only difference between (
3.11
) and (
3.3
) is that
\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(|w-z|)
is replaced with
\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(|w+z|)
. Therefore, in order to show that the quantites in (
3.11
) and (
3.3
) are comparable, it is enough to show that
\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(|w-z|)
and
\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(|w+z|)
are comparable for all
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
z\in B(0,r)
.
We prove this by two cases. If
|w|\geq 2r
, then
r\leq\frac{1}{2}|w|\leq|w-z|,|w+z|\leq 2|w|
, so by the definition of the small/flat decomposition, and by (
1.4
),

\frac{\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(|w-z|)}{\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(|w+z|)}=\frac{j(|w-z|)}{j(|w+z|)}\leq\frac{j(\frac{1}{2}|w|)}{j(2|w|)}\leq c_{j}^{-2}.

On the other hand, if
|w|\leq 2r
, then both
|w-z|
and
|w+z|
are at most
3r
, so by Lemma

,

\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(|w-z|)\leq C_{\ref{EHI:l:flatness}}\left(3L,c_{j}\right)\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(|w+z|).

In either case, if we let
C_{\ref{EHI:p:h1(x)=h1(-x)}}(L,c_{j}):=\max\{c_{j}^{-2},C_{\ref{EHI:l:flatness}}(3L,c_{j})\}
, then

\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(|w-z|)\leq C_{\ref{EHI:p:h1(x)=h1(-x)}}(L,c_{j})\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(|w+z|).

By the same argument,

\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(|w+z|)\leq C_{\ref{EHI:p:h1(x)=h1(-x)}}(L,c_{j})\hat{j}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}(|w-z|).

This holds for all
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

. Thus, by (

) and (

),

C_{\ref{EHI:p:h1(x)=h1(-x)}}(L,c_{j})^{-1}h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}_{B(0,r)}(-x)\leq h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}_{B(0,r)}(x)\leq C_{\ref{EHI:p:h1(x)=h1(-x)}}(L,c_{j})h^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,>0}_{B(0,r)}(-x).

∎

Corollary 3.11
.

Let

be an isotropic unimodal Lévy jump process on

4
Preliminary facts about isotropic unimodal Lévy jump processes with bounded jumps

In this section, we build up a collection of facts to be used in the proof of our main results. Most of these facts relate to processes with bounded jumps, such as
X^{(r_{0})}
and
X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
(from the small/large and small/flat decompositions, respectively). To keep the results general, we refer to the process in question as

in the statement of the results of this section, but the reader should keep in mind that when we apply these results, it will usually be to

or

, where

is the process that our main results refer to.

The most important results in this Section are Lemma

, which tells us that for a process

with bounded jumps, the second moment of the jump kernel of

is the speed at which

grows, and the results of Section

, which use a multivariate form the Berry-Esseen theorem to show that the behavior of such a process at large scales is comparable to a Brownian motion in which

grows at the same speed. Other results in this section are mostly calculations that will simplify the work in the proof of our main results.

Definition 4.2
.

For all

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{I}(d,r_{0})
, and let
T
be a random variable with Exponential(
\lambda
) distribution, independent of
X
. For all
r>0
and

,

\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\tau_{B(0,mr+(m-1)r_{0})}<T\right)\leq\left(\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\tau_{B(0,r)}<T\right)\right)^{m}.

Proof.

For all
m
, let
B_{m}:=B(0,mr+(m-1)r_{0})
and
\tilde{B}_{m}:=B(0,mr+mr_{0})
.
We will show by induction on
m
that

\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\tau_{B_{m}}<T\right)\leq\left(\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\tau_{B(0,r)}<T\right)\right)^{m}

(4.1)

For the base case (
m=1
), the two sides of (

) are identical. Assume (
4.1
) holds for
m
, and let us show that it also holds for
m+1
. Consider what must happen for the process starting at
0

before time

. First, it must exit
B_{m}
, which will bring it to some location in
\tilde{B}_{m}\setminus B_{m}
(since its last jump had a magnitude less than
r_{0}
, and the radius of
\tilde{B}_{m}
is equal to

plus the radius of
B_{m}

From this location, it must travel an additional distance of at least

before time

(since the radius of

is equal to

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\tau_{B_{m+1}}<T\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\tau_{B_{m}}<T\right)\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\tau_{B_{m+1}}<T\Big|\tau_{B_{m}}<T\right)

4.2
The second moment is the rate of speed of growth of
|X_{t}|^{2}

Given a process
X\in\mathcal{I}(d,r_{0})
, let

m_{2}(X):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}j(|x|).

(4.2)

Because
j(|x|)=0
for
|x|>r_{0}
, the Lévy-Khintchine condition guarantees that
m_{2}(X)<\infty
for
X\in\mathcal{I}(d,r_{0})
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Given
X\in\mathcal{I}(d,r_{0})
, we have
\mathbb{E}_{x}|X_{t}-x|^{2}=m_{2}(X)\cdot t
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0
.

Proof.

For all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
, let
y^{(j)}
denote the
j
th coordinate of the vector
y
.
Let
(\mathcal{G}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the filtration generated by only the magnitudes of the jumps of
X
: that is,
\mathcal{G}_{t}:=\sigma\left(\left\{|X_{s}-X_{s-}|:0<s\leq t\right\}\right)
.
Condition on
\mathcal{G}_{t}
and consider an
s\in(0,t]
at which a jump of size
X_{s}-X_{s-}
occurs. By the definition of
|\cdot|
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\left|X_{s}-X_{s-}\right|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left|X_{s}^{(j)}-X_{s-}^{(j)}\right|^{2}.

By symmetry, for each
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
,

\mathrm{Var}_{x}\left(\left.X_{s}^{(j)}-X_{s-}^{(j)}\right|\mathcal{G}_{t}\right)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\left|\left.X_{s}^{(j)}-X_{s-}^{(j)}\right|^{2}\right|\mathcal{G}_{t}\right]=\frac{1}{d}|X_{s}-X_{s-}|^{2}.

By summing over all
s\in(0,t]
, since variances of independent random variables are additive,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\left.\left|X_{t}^{(j)}-x^{(j)}\right|^{2}\right|\mathcal{G}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Var}_{x}\left(\left.X_{t}^{(j)}-x^{(j)}\right|\mathcal{G}_{t}\right)=\sum_{0<s\leq t}\mathrm{Var}_{x}\left(\left.X_{s}^{(j)}-X_{s-}^{(j)}\right|\mathcal{G}_{t}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{0<s\leq t}|X_{s}-X_{s-}|^{2}.

Summing over all
d
,

\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\left.\left|X_{t}-x\right|^{2}\right|\mathcal{G}_{t}\right]=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\left.\left|X_{t}^{(j)}-x^{(j)}\right|^{2}\right|\mathcal{G}_{t}\right]=\sum_{0<s\leq t}|X_{s}-X_{s-}|^{2}.

Taking expectations,

\mathbb{E}_{x}|X_{t}-x|^{2}=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\left.\left|X_{t}-x\right|^{2}\right|\mathcal{G}_{t}\right]\right)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sum_{0<s\leq t}|X_{s}-X_{s-}|^{2}\right].

(4.3)

Fix
\ell>1
. For all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
E_{k}
denote the annulus
E_{k}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ell^{-k}r_{0}<|x|\leq\ell^{-(k-1)}r_{0}\}
. Then
\{E_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is a partition of
B(0,r_{0})
, so every jump that
X
takes has its displacement in
E_{k}
for some unique
k
. Fix
t>0
. For all
k
, let
N^{(k)}_{t}
be the number of jumps
X
takes with displacement in
E_{k}
by time
t
. (In other words,
N^{(k)}_{t}:=\#\{s\in(0,t]:X_{s}-X_{s-}\in E_{k}\}
.) The total sum of the squares of the jumps taken by time
t
can be approximated as follows:

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\ell^{-2k}r_{0}^{2}N^{(k)}_{t}\leq\sum_{0<s\leq t}|X_{s}-X_{s-}|^{2}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\ell^{-2(k-1)}r_{0}^{2}N^{(k)}_{t}.

Taking expectations,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sum_{0<s\leq t}|X_{s}-X_{s-}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\ell^{-2(k-1)}r_{0}^{2}\,\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[N^{(k)}_{t}\right]=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\ell^{-2(k-1)}r_{0}^{2}\int_{E_{k}}j(|x|)\,dx\cdot t

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{E_{k}}\ell^{2}|x|^{2}j(|x|)\,dx\cdot t=\ell^{2}m_{2}(X)t

(4.4)

and

\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sum_{0<s\leq t}|X_{s}-X_{s-}|^{2}\right]\geq\ell^{-2}m_{2}(X)t.

(4.5)

Since (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) hold for all
\ell>1
,

\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sum_{0<s\leq t}|X_{s}-X_{s-}|^{2}\right]=m_{2}(X)t.

(4.6)

By (
4.3
) and (
4.6
), the proof is complete.
∎

4.3
Quadratic variation and Berry-Esseen

Lemma

is useful in analyzing the distance a process

Given an isotropic unimodal Lévy jump process

, consider its quadratic variation

[X]_{t}:=\sum_{0<s\leq t}|\Delta X(s)|^{2},

(4.10)

The process
[X]=([X]_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a non-decreasing Lévy process on

only takes jumps of size

and

is somewhat analogous to the relationship between a subordinate Brownian motion and its subordinator, in that

is a one-dimensional, non-decreasing Lévy process and

is usually on the order of
|X_{t}-X_{0}|^{2}

can be approximated as a Gaussian with variance

2

proportional to
[X]_{t}I_{d}

We begin with the following lemma, which establishes something like a Laplace exponent for

Lemma 4.6

If

is an isotropic unimodal Lévy jump process on

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\lambda[X]_{t}}\right]=\exp(-t \int_{\mathbb{R}^d} (1-e^{-\lambda|x|^2}) j(|x|) \, dx)

Before we prove Lemma

, we need the following simple fact.

Lemma 4.7
.

Fix
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
. If a random variable

has Poisson(

) distribution, then

\mathbb{E}[e^{-\lambda_{2}N}]=\exp(-\lambda_1(1-e^{-\lambda_2})).

Proof.

The proof is a simple calculation:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{-\lambda_{2}N}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda_{1}}\frac{\lambda_{1}^{n}}{n!}e^{-\lambda_{2}n}=e^{-\lambda_{1}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda_{1}e^{-\lambda_{2}})^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\exp(-\lambda_1)\exp(\lambda_1 e^{-\lambda_2})=\exp(-\lambda_1 (1-e^{-\lambda_2})).

Proof of Lemma
4.6
.

Fix
\ell>1
. For all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
E_{k}
be the annulus
E_{k}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ell^{k}\leq|x|<\ell^{k+1}\}

, so each jump that

takes has a displacement in

for some unique

. For all

[X]^{(k)}_{t}:=\sum_{0<s\leq t}\Delta|X(s)|^{2}\mathbf{1}\left\{\Delta X(s)\in E_{k}\right\}

Let
\lambda_{k}:=\int_{E_{k}}j(|x|)\,dx

is Poisson(
\lambda_{k}t

Whenever

takes a jump whose displacement vector is in

\ell^{2k}N^{(k)}_{t}\leq[X]^{(k)}_{t}\leq\ell^{2(k+1)}N^{(k)}_{t}.

(4.11)

The collection
\left\{[X]^{(k)}_{t}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is independent and its sum is

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\lambda[X]_{t}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda[X]^{(k)}_{t}}\right]=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\lambda[X]^{(k)}_{t}}\right].

(4.12)

For all
k
, by (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\lambda[X]^{(k)}_{t}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(-\lambda\ell^{2(k+1)} N^{(k)}_t)\right]=\exp(-\lambda_k t \left(1-e^{-\lambda\ell^{2(k+1)}} \right))

\displaystyle=\exp(-t\int_{E_k} \left(1-e^{-\lambda\ell^{2(k+1)}} \right) j(|x|) \, dx )

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-t\int_{E_k} \left(1-e^{-\lambda\ell^2 |x|^2} \right) j(|x|) \, dx ).

(4.13)

By (

), taking the product of (

Since this holds for all
\ell>1

\ell\to 1^{+}
(using the Dominated convergence theorem) gives

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\lambda[X]_{t}}\right]\leq\exp(-t\int_{\mathbb{R}^d} \left(1-e^{-\lambda|x|^2} \right) j(|x|) \, dx ).

(4.14)

By repeating the same argument but using the lower bound from (
4.11
) instead of the upper bound, and again taking the limit as
\ell\to 1^{+}
, we find that the inequality in (
4.14
) is in fact an equality.
∎

The Berry-Esseen theorem gives a quantitative version of the Central limit theorem, with an error term that depends on the third moments of the independent random variables being summed. The following multivariate version of the theorem, due to Raič
[
20
, Theorem 1.1]
, does not require the random variables to be identically-distributed.

Theorem 4.8

(Multivariate Berry-Esseen,
[
20
]
)
.

Let
(\xi_{i})_{i\in I}
be an independent collection of random vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, each with mean
0
. Assume that
\mathrm{Var}(\sum_{i\in I}\xi_{i})=\sigma^{2}I_{d}
, where
\sigma>0
. Let
Z
be a standard multivariate Gaussian (with mean
0
and variance
I_{d}
). Then for all measurable convex sets
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\left|\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i\in I}\xi_{i}\in A\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(\sigma Z\in A\right)\right|\leq C_{BE}\cdot\frac{\sum_{i\in I}\mathbb{E}|\xi_{i}|^{3}}{\sigma^{3}},

where
C_{BE}=42d^{1/4}+16
(which depends only on
d
).

In
[
20
]
, the theorem is expressed slightly differently, with
\mathrm{Var}(\sum_{i\in I}\xi_{i})=I_{d}
. Our formulation, which allows
\mathrm{Var}(\sum_{i\in I}\xi_{i})=\sigma^{2}I_{d}
for any
\sigma>0
, can be obtained by a simple renormalization.

We would like to put a lower bound on
\mathbb{P}_{x}(|X_{t}-x|>r)
. It is not enough to use Lemma
4.4
and Markov’s or Chebyshev’s inequality, since that would only give us an upper bound. Instead, we use Berry-Esseen.
In the following lemma, we condition on the values of
|\Delta X(s)|
for all
0<s\leq t
, and apply Theorem
4.8
to the conditional distribution of
X_{t}=X_{0}+\sum_{0<s\leq t}\Delta X(s)
. The result is that
\mathbb{P}_{x}(|X_{t}-x|>r)
can be bounded below by the probability of
[X]_{t}
being sufficiently large.

Lemma 4.9
.

For all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists a universal constant
C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstantHelper}}(\varepsilon)=C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstantHelper}}(\varepsilon,d)>0
such that if
X\in\mathcal{I}(d,r_{0})
, then for all
r>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(|X_{t}-x|>r\right)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\mathbb{P}\left([X]_{t}\geq C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstantHelper}}(\varepsilon)(r\vee r_{0})^{2}\right).

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{G}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the filtration of
\sigma
-fields that takes into account only the magnitude of the jumps
X
takes at each time:

\mathcal{G}_{t}:=\sigma\left(\left\{|\Delta X(s)|:0<s\leq t\right\}\right).

The process
[X]=([X]_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is adapted to
(\mathcal{G}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. Let us condition on
\mathcal{G}_{t}
for some fixed
t>0
. Suppose the magnitudes of the jumps, and when they occur, are determined. Given this information, let us analyze the conditional distribution of
X_{t}
.
For simplicity, let us assume without loss of generality the process begins at
X_{0}=0
, so
X_{t}=\sum_{0<s\leq t}\Delta X(s)
.
By summing over all
s
, the conditional variance of
X_{t}
is

\mathrm{Var}(X_{t}|\mathcal{G}_{t})=\sum_{0<s\leq t}d^{-1}|\Delta X(s)|^{2}I_{d}=d^{-1}[X]_{t}I_{d}.

(The variance of a uniform point on the sphere
\{x:|x|=r\}
is
d^{-1}r^{2}I_{d}
.) Since each value of
|\Delta X(s)|^{3}
is
\mathcal{G}_{t}
-measurable, by Theorem
4.8
, for all convex sets
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\left|\mathbb{P}_{0}(X_{t}\in A|\mathcal{G}_{t})-\mathbb{P}\left(\sqrt{d^{-1}[X]_{t}}Z\in A\right)\right|\leq C_{BE}\frac{\sum_{0<s\leq t}|\Delta X(s)|^{3}}{(d^{-1}[X]_{t})^{3/2}}=C_{1}\frac{\sum_{0<s\leq t}|\Delta X(s)|^{3}}{([X]_{t})^{3/2}}

where
C_{1}:=d^{3/2}C_{BE}
.
The same result holds for sets whose complements are convex, by considering the complements of the probabilities.
Therefore, we can take
A
to be
B(0,r)^{c}
, and obtain

\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(|X_{t}|>r\Big|\mathcal{G}_{t}\right)\geq\mathbb{P}\left(|Z|>r\sqrt{\frac{d}{[X]_{t}}}\right)-C_{1}\frac{\sum_{0<s\leq t}|\Delta X(s)|^{3}}{([X]_{t})^{3/2}}.

(4.15)

Let
F_{|Z|}:[0,\infty)\to[0,1)
be the cumulative distribution function
F_{|Z|}(z)=\mathbb{P}(|Z|\leq z)
. (Recall that
Z
is a standard
d
-dimensional Gaussian, so
F_{|Z|}
depends on
d
.)
Then (
4.15
) can be written as

\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(|X_{t}|>r\Big|\mathcal{G}_{t}\right)\geq 1-F_{|Z|}\left(r\sqrt{\frac{d}{[X]_{t}}}\right)-C_{1}\frac{\sum_{0<s\leq t}|\Delta X(s)|^{3}}{([X]_{t})^{3/2}}.

(4.16)

Note that
\sum_{0<s\leq t}|\Delta X(s)|^{3}\leq r_{0}[X]_{t}
, since
|\Delta X(s)|^{3}\leq r_{0}|\Delta X(s)|^{2}
for all
s
. Also,
\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(|X_{t}|>r\Big|\mathcal{G}_{t}\right)\geq 0
almost surely. Thus, (
4.16
) implies

\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(|X_{t}|>r\Big|\mathcal{G}_{t}\right)\geq\left(1-F_{|Z|}\left(r\sqrt{\frac{d}{[X]_{t}}}\right)-C_{1}\frac{r_{0}}{\sqrt{[X]_{t}}}\right)_{+}.

Taking the expectation,

\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(|X_{t}|>r\right)\geq\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\left(1-F_{|Z|}\left(r\sqrt{\frac{d}{[X]_{t}}}\right)-C_{1}\frac{r_{0}}{\sqrt{[X]_{t}}}\right)_{+}\right].

(4.17)

We will show that when
[X]_{t}/(r\vee r_{0})^{2}
is sufficiently large,
F_{|Z|}(r\sqrt{d/[X]_{t}})
and
C_{1}r_{0}/\sqrt{[X]_{t}}
are each at most
\varepsilon/2
, so the quantity whose expectation is being taken in (
4.17
) is at least
1-\varepsilon
.
Let
c_{2}(\varepsilon)
be the value such that
F_{|Z|}(c_{2}(\varepsilon))=\varepsilon/2
, and note that

\left\{F_{|Z|}\left(r\sqrt{\frac{d}{[X]_{t}}}\right)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right\}\quad=\quad\left\{r\sqrt{\frac{d}{[X]_{t}}}\leq c_{2}(\varepsilon)\right\}\quad=\quad\left\{[X]_{t}\geq d(c_{2}(\varepsilon))^{-2}r^{2}\right\}

and

\quad\left\{C_{1}\frac{r_{0}}{\sqrt{[X]_{t}}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right\}\quad=\quad\left\{[X]_{t}\geq 4C_{1}^{2}\varepsilon^{-2}r_{0}^{2}\right\}.

Let
C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstantHelper}}(\varepsilon):=\max\{d(c_{2}(\varepsilon))^{-2},4C_{1}^{2}\varepsilon^{-2}\}
. Then whenever
[X]_{t}\geq C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstantHelper}}(\varepsilon)(r\vee r_{0})^{2}
, we have both
F_{|Z|}(r\sqrt{d/[X]_{t}})\leq\varepsilon/2
and
C_{1}\frac{r_{0}}{\sqrt{[X]_{t}}}\leq\varepsilon/2
.
By this fact and (
4.17
),

\mathbb{P}_{0}(|X_{t}|>r)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\mathbb{P}\left([X]_{t}\geq C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstantHelper}}(\varepsilon)(r\vee r_{0})^{2}\right).

∎

Next, we use Lemma
4.6
to obtain a lower bound on the probability that
[X]_{t}\geq C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstantHelper}}(\varepsilon)(r\vee r_{0})^{2}
.

Lemma 4.10
.

For all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists a universal constant
C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstant}}(\varepsilon)=C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstant}}(\varepsilon,d)>0
such that if
X\in\mathcal{I}(d,r_{0})
, then for all
r>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau_{B(x,r)}>C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstant}}(\varepsilon)\frac{(r\vee r_{0})^{2}}{m_{2}(X)}\right)\leq\varepsilon.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
X\in\mathcal{I}(d,r_{0})
,
r>0
, and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Fix
t>0
,
\mu>0
, and
\delta>0
, all of which will be specified later. By Lemma
4.9
,

\mathbb{P}_{x}(|X_{t}-x|>r)\geq(1-\delta)\mathbb{P}\left([X]_{t}\geq C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstantHelper}}(\delta)(r\vee r_{0})^{2}\right).

(4.18)

By Markov’s inequality and Lemma
4.6
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left([X]_{t}<C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstantHelper}}(r\vee r_{0})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(e^{-\lambda[X]_{t}}>\exp(-C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstantHelper}}(\delta) \mu(r \vee r_0)^2)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstantHelper}}(\delta) \mu(r \vee r_0)^2)\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-\mu[X]_{t}}\right]

\displaystyle=\exp( C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstantHelper}}(\delta) \mu(r \vee r_0)^2 - t \int_{B(0, r_0)} (1-e^{-\mu|x|^2}) j(|x|) \dee{x} ).

(4.19)

The integral in (
4.3
) is over
B(0,r_{0})
instead of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
because
X\in\mathcal{I}(d,r_{0})
. We would like to replace the integrand
1-e^{-\mu|x|^{2}}
with some multiple of
\mu|x|^{2}
, using the approximation
1-e^{-u}\approx u
.
To make this precise, note that the function
u\mapsto\frac{1-e^{-u}}{u}
is decreasing, so
1-e^{-\mu|x|^{2}}\geq\frac{1-e^{-\mu r_{0}^{2}}}{\mu r_{0}^{2}}\mu|x|^{2}
for all
x\in B(0,r_{0})
. Thus,

\int_{B(0,r_{0})}(1-e^{-\mu|x|^{2}})j(|x|)\;\mathrm{d}x\geq\frac{1-e^{-\mu r_{0}^{2}}}{\mu r_{0}^{2}}\int_{B(0,r_{0})}\mu|x|^{2}j(|x|)\;\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1-e^{-\mu r_{0}^{2}}}{\mu r_{0}^{2}}\mu m_{2}(X).

(4.20)

Combining (
4.3
) and (
4.20
),

\mathbb{P}\left([X]_{t}<C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstantHelper}}(r\vee r_{0})^{2}\right)\leq\exp( C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstantHelper}}(\delta) \mu(r \vee r_0)^2 - \frac{1-e^{-\mu r_0^2}}{\mu r_0^2} \mu m_2(X) t ).

(4.21)

Recall that
\mu
,
t
, and
\delta
have been unspecified up to now. We now specify them one-by-one.
Let
\mu:=r_{0}^{-2}
. Then (
4.21
) becomes

\mathbb{P}\left([X]_{t}<C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstantHelper}}(r\vee r_{0})^{2}\right)\leq\exp( C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstantHelper}}(\delta) \cdot\left( \frac{r \vee r_0}{r_0} \right)^2 - (1-e^{-1}) \frac{m_2(X)}{r_0^2} t ).

(4.22)

Let

t:=\frac{C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstantHelper}}(\delta)+\log(\delta^{-1})}{1-e^{-1}}\cdot\frac{(r\vee r_{0})^{2}}{m_{2}(X)}.

Then (
4.22
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left([X]_{t}<C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstantHelper}}(r\vee r_{0})^{2}\right)\leq\exp(-\log(\delta^{-1}) \cdot\left( \frac{r \vee r_0}{r_0} \right)^2)\leq\delta.

(4.23)

By (
4.18
) and (
4.23
),

\mathbb{P}_{x}(|X_{t}-x|>r)\geq(1-\delta)^{2}.

Finally, let
\delta:=\varepsilon/2
, so that
\mathbb{P}_{x}(|X_{t}-x|>r)\geq(1-\varepsilon/2)^{2}\geq 1-\varepsilon
. Note that if
|X_{t}-x|>r
, then
\tau_{B(x,r)}\leq t
. In conclusion,

\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau_{B(x,r)}\leq\frac{C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstantHelper}}(\varepsilon/2)+\log(\frac{1}{2\eps})}{1-e^{-1}}\cdot\frac{(r\vee r_{0})^{2}}{m_{2}(X)}\right)=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau_{B(x,r)}\leq t\right)\geq\mathbb{P}_{x}(|X_{t}-x|>r)\geq 1-\varepsilon.

This completes the proof, with

C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstant}}(\varepsilon):=\frac{C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstantHelper}}(\varepsilon/2)+\log(\frac{1}{2\eps})}{1-e^{-1}}.

∎

By a chaining argument, we can improve Lemma
4.10
to get that
\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{B(x,r)}>t)
decays exponentially in
t
, with the rate of decay proportional to
m_{2}(X)/(r\vee r_{0})^{2}
.

Lemma 4.11
.

There exists a universal constant
c_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallExponential}}=c_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallExponential}}(d)>0
such that if
X\in\mathcal{I}(d,r_{0})
, then for all
r>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
t>0
,

\mathbb{P}_{0}(\tau_{B(x,r)}>t)\leq\exp(-\floor{c_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallExponential}} \frac{m_2(X)}{(r \vee r_0)^2} t}).

Proof.

Let
t_{0}:=C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstant}}(e^{-1})(2r\vee r_{0})/m_{2}(X)
. Consider the times
0,t_{0},2t_{0},\dots,nt_{0}
, where
n:=\left\lfloor t/t_{0}\right\rfloor
. In order for the process to remain in the ball
B(x,r)
up until time
t
, we must have
X_{jt_{0}}\in B(x,r)
for all
0\leq j\leq n
. This means that
\left|X_{(j+1)t_{0}}-X_{jt_{0}}\right|\leq 2r
for all
0\leq j<n
. Thus, by the Strong Markov property and Lemma
4.10
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{B(x,r)}>t)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\mbox{$\left|X_{(j+1)t_{0}}-X_{jt_{0}}\right|\leq 2r$ for all $0\leq j<n$}\right)=\left(\mathbb{P}_{0}(\tau_{B(0,r)}>t_{0})\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-n}=e^{-\left\lfloor t/t_{0}\right\rfloor}=\exp(-\floor{\frac{1}{C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstant}}(e^{-1})} \cdot\frac{m_x(X)}{(2r \vee r_0)^2} t})

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-\floor{\frac{1}{4C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstant}}(e^{-1})} \cdot\frac{m_x(X)}{(r \vee r_0)^2} t}).

∎

5
Proof of main results and examples

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.4
(our main result) and the corollaries and examples that follow from it in the introduction.

5.1
Proof of Theorem
1.4

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
X
be an isotropic unimodal Lévy jump process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For
r,r_{0}>0
and all
x\in B(0,r)
, we have

G^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}_{B(0,r)}(0,x)\lesssim\frac{(r\vee r_{0})^{2}|x|^{-d}}{m_{2}\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}.

The constant implicit in
\lesssim
depend only on the dimension
d
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.7
, the independence of
X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
from
\hat{X}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
, and Lemma
4.11
applied to
X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
,

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,r)}G^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}_{B(0,r)}(0,y)\;\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\tau_{B(0,r)}>t,T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}>t\right)\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\tau^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{B(0,r)}>t,T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}>t\right)\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\tau^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{B(0,r)}>t\right)\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp( -\floor{c_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallExponential}} \frac{m_2\inner{r_0}}{(r \vee r_0)^2} t } )\;\mathrm{d}t

(5.1)

Let
t_{0}:=(r\vee r_{0})^{2}/(c_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallExponential}}m_{2}\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle)
, so that (
5.1
) is equivalent to

\int_{B(0,r)}G^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}_{B(0,r)}(0,y)\;\mathrm{d}y\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\left\lfloor t/t_{0}\right\rfloor}\;\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{0}e^{-k}=(1-e^{-1})^{-1}t_{0}.

Now fix
x\in B(0,r)
. Note that
G^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}_{B(0,r)}(0,\cdot)
is non-increasing in
|\cdot|
, so the value of
G^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}_{B(0,r)}(0,x)
is at most equal to the average of
G^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}_{B(0,r)}(0,y)
for
y\in B(0,|x|)
. Thus,

\displaystyle G^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}_{B(0,r)}(0,x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\int_{B(0,|x|)}G^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}_{B(0,r)}(0,y)\;\mathrm{d}y}{|B(0,r)|}\leq\frac{\int_{B(0,r)}G^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}_{B(0,r)}(0,y)\;\mathrm{d}y}{|x|^{d}|B(0,1)|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1-e^{-1})^{-1}}{|B(0,1)|}|x|^{-d}t_{0}=\frac{(1-e^{-1})^{-1}}{c_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallExponential}}|B(0,1)|}\cdot\frac{(r\vee r_{0})^{2}|x|^{-d}}{m_{2}\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}.

∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
X
be an isotropic unimodal Lévy jump process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
There exist universal constants
L_{\ref{EHI:l:G0lowerbound}}>1
and
c_{\ref{EHI:l:G0lowerbound}}>0
such that if
0<r_{0}\leq r/L_{\ref{EHI:l:G0lowerbound}}\leq r
, then

G^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}_{B(0,r)}(0,x)\geq c_{\ref{EHI:l:G0lowerbound}}\frac{r^{2-d}}{m_{2}\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}\exp(-\frac{1}{10} \cdot\frac{r^2 \lambda\inner{r_0}}{m_2\inner{r_0}})\qquad\mbox{for all $x\in B(0,r/L_{\ref{EHI:l:G0lowerbound}})$}.

Proof.

Let

L_{\ref{EHI:l:G0lowerbound}}:=\sqrt{20C_{\ref{EHI:l:LeaveBallConstant}}\left(\frac{1}{10}\right)}.

Suppose
0<r_{0}\leq r/L_{\ref{EHI:l:G0lowerbound}}\leq r
and fix
x\in B(0,r/L_{\ref{EHI:l:G0lowerbound}})
.
Let
s:=|x|
and
A:=B(0,r)\setminus\overline{B(0,s)}
. We will put a lower bound on
\int_{A}G^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}_{B(0,r)}(0,y)\;\mathrm{d}y
, and then conclude that
G^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}_{B(0,r)}(0,x)
is at least the average value of
G^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}_{B(0,r)}(0,y)
for
y\in A
, much like in the proof of Lemma
5.1
, because
G^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}_{B(0,r)}(0,\cdot)
is non-increasing in
|\cdot|
.

By Lemma
3.7
and the independence of
X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
from
\hat{X}^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
,

\displaystyle\int_{A}G^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}_{B(0,r)}(0,y)\;\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(X_{t}\in A,\tau_{B(0,r)}>t,T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}>t\right)\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{t}\in A,\tau^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{B(0,r)}>t,T^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}>t\right)\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle t}\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{t}\in A,\tau^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{B(0,r)}>t\right)\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle t}\left(\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{t}\in A\right)-\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{t}\in A,\tau^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{B(0,r)}\leq t\right)\right)\;\mathrm{d}t.

(5.2)

For all
t>0
,

\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{t}\in A\right)=\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(|X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{t}|\geq s\right)-\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(|X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{t}|\geq r\right)

(5.3)

and by Lemma
4.1
,

\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{t}\in A,\tau^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{B(0,r)}\leq t\right)\leq\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\tau^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{B(0,r)}\leq t\right)\leq 2\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(|X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{t}|>r\right).

(5.4)

By substituting (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) into (
5.1
),

\int_{A}G^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle,0}_{B(0,r)}(0,y)\;\mathrm{d}y\geq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle t}\left(\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(|X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{t}|\geq s\right)-3\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(|X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{t}|\geq r\right)\right)\;\mathrm{d}t.

(5.5)

Let us put a lower bound on the integrand of (
5.5
) for all
t\in\left[\frac{r^{2}}{20m_{2}\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle},\frac{r^{2}}{10m_{2}\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}\right]
. Fix
t
in this set. We then have

e^{-\lambda\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle t}\geq\exp(-\frac{1}{10} \cdot\frac{r^2 \lambda\inner{r_0}}{m_2\inner{r_0}}).

(5.6)

By Markov’s inequality,

\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(|X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{t}|\geq r\right)\leq r^{-2}\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[|X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{t}|^{-2}\right]=\frac{m_{2}\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle t}{r^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{10}.

(5.7)

We still need a lower bound on
\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(|X^{\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}_{t}|\geq s\right)
.
Recall that
(s\vee r_{0})=(|x|\vee r_{0})\leq r/L_{\ref{EHI:l:G0lowerbound}}
and
t\geq\frac{r^{2}}{20m_{2}\left\langle r_{0}\right\rangle}
. Thus,

Substituting (

Since

Proposition 5.3

Lemma 5.4
.

Let

be an isotropic unimodal Lévy jump process on

. For all

Proof.

By the definition of

(that is, Definition (

(5.15)

Let

denote the measure

.
Then, in the resulting integral, we can switch

(5.18)

By substituting (

Lemma 5.6
.

For all
\alpha,\beta>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, we have

\int_{r}^{\delta}s^{-1-\alpha}(\log(s^{-1}))^{-1-\beta}\;\mathrm{d}s\asymp r^{-\alpha}\left(\log(r^{-1})\right)^{-1-\beta}\qquad\mbox{as $r\to 0^{+}$}.

(The constants implicit in
\asymp
depend on
\alpha
,
\beta
, and
\delta
.)

Proof.

Fix
\alpha
,
\beta
, and
\delta
, and let
f(r):=\int_{r}^{\delta}s^{-1-\alpha}(\log(s^{-1}))^{-1-\beta}\;\mathrm{d}s
for all
0<r\leq\delta
. The substitution
u=\log(s^{-1})
yields

f(r)=\int_{\log(\delta^{-1})}^{\log(r^{-1})}e^{\alpha u}u^{-1-\beta}\;\mathrm{d}u.

For
r\leq\delta^{2}
, we can split this integral in two as follows:

f(r)=\int_{\log(\delta^{-1})}^{\frac{1}{2}\log(r^{-1})}e^{\alpha u}u^{-1-\beta}\;\mathrm{d}u+\int_{\frac{1}{2}\log(r^{-1})}^{\log(r^{-1})}e^{\alpha u}u^{-1-\beta}\;\mathrm{d}u.

(5.37)

For the first integral in (
5.37
), the
e^{\alpha u}
term is always at most
r^{-\alpha/2}
, so

\displaystyle\int_{\log(\delta^{-1})}^{\frac{1}{2}\log(r^{-1})}e^{\alpha u}u^{-1-\beta}\;\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\leq r^{-\alpha/2}\int_{\log(\delta^{-1})}^{\frac{1}{2}\log(r^{-1})}u^{-1-\beta}\;\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{-\alpha/2}}{\beta}\left(\left(\log(\delta^{-1})\right)^{-\beta}-\left(\frac{1}{2}\log(r^{-1})\right)^{-\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\asymp r^{-\alpha/2}\qquad\mbox{as $r\to 0^{+}$}.

For the second integral in (
5.37
),
u^{-1-\beta}
is always between
\left(\log(r^{-1})\right)^{-1-\beta}
and
2^{1+\beta}\left(\log(r^{-1})\right)^{-1-\beta}
, so

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{1}{2}\log(r^{-1})}^{\log(r^{-1})}e^{\alpha u}u^{-1-\beta}\;\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\asymp\left(\log(r^{-1})\right)^{-1-\beta}\int_{\frac{1}{2}\log(r^{-1})}^{\log(r^{-1})}e^{\alpha u}\;\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\log(r^{-1})\right)^{-1-\beta}}{\alpha}\left(r^{-\alpha}-r^{-\alpha/2}\right)

\displaystyle\asymp r^{-\alpha}\left(\log(r^{-1})\right)^{-1-\beta}\qquad\mbox{as $r\to 0^{+}$}.

The second integral dominates the first one as
r\to 0^{+}
. Thus,

f(r)\asymp r^{-\alpha}\left(\log(r^{-1})\right)^{-1-\beta}\qquad\mbox{as $r\to 0^{+}$}.

f(r):=\int_{r}^{\infty}s^{-2-\alpha}(\log s)^{1+\beta}\;\mathrm{d}s

u=\log s
yields

\displaystyle f(r)=\int_{\log r}^{\infty}e^{-(1+\alpha)u}u^{1+\beta}\;\mathrm{d}u=\int_{\log r}^{2\log r}e^{-(1+\alpha)u}u^{1+\beta}\;\mathrm{d}u+\int_{2\log r}^{\infty}e^{-(1+\alpha)u}u^{1+\beta}\;\mathrm{d}u.

(5.38)

We handle each of the two integrals on the right-hand side of (

) separately. In the first integral, the

term is always between
(\log r)^{1+\beta}
and

, so

\displaystyle\int_{\log r}^{2\log r}e^{-(1+\alpha)u}u^{1+\beta}\;\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\asymp(\log r)^{1+\beta}\int_{\log r}^{2\log r}e^{-(1+\alpha)u}\;\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\frac{(\log r)^{1+\beta}}{1+\alpha}\left(r^{-1-\alpha}-r^{-2-2\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\asymp r^{-1-\alpha}(\log r)^{1+\beta}\qquad\mbox{as $r\to\infty$}.

For the second integral, for large enough values of

we have
u^{1+\beta}<e^{u}
, so

\displaystyle\int_{2\log r}^{\infty}e^{-(1+\alpha)u}u^{1+\beta}\;\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\leq\int_{2\log r}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha u}\;\mathrm{d}u=\frac{1}{\alpha}r^{-2\alpha}.

Since

, the first integral dominates the second one. Thus,

f(r)\asymp r^{-1-\alpha}(\log r)^{1+\beta}\qquad\mbox{as $r\to\infty$}.

∎

5.3
Calculating the jump kernel in Examples
1.11
-
1.13

In this subsection, we perform the calculations to asymptotically approximate
j(r)
for small
r
for the geometric stable, relativistic geometric stable, and iterated geometric stable processes. The first of these gives us an alternate proof of
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)
in Example
1.11
The latter two of these are necessary to prove the claims we made in Examples
1.12
and
1.13
.

Kim and Mimica
[
15
, Proposition 4.2]
show that for any subordinate Brownian motion satisfying
A-2
and
A-3
,

j(r)\asymp r^{-d-2}\phi^{\prime}(r^{-2}).

For the geometric stable process,

\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)=\frac{\beta}{2}\cdot\frac{\lambda^{\beta/2-1}}{1+\lambda^{\beta/2}}\asymp\lambda^{-1}\qquad\mbox{as $\lambda\to\infty$},

so

j(r)\asymp r^{-d-2}\phi^{\prime}(r^{-2})\asymp r^{-d}\qquad\mbox{as $r\to 0^{+}$}.

For the relativistic geometric stable process,

\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)=\frac{2}{\beta}\cdot\frac{(\lambda+m^{\beta/2})^{2/\beta-1}}{1+(\lambda+m^{\beta/2})^{2/\beta}-m}\asymp\lambda^{-1}\qquad\mbox{as $\lambda\to\infty$},

so

j(r)\asymp r^{-d}\qquad\mbox{as $r\to 0^{+}$},

similarly.

For the iterated geometric stable process,
\phi_{n}
is the
n
-fold composition of
\phi_{1}(\lambda)=\log(1+\lambda^{\beta/2})
. For all
n
, let
\log^{(n)}
denote the
n
-fold composition of
\log
. We will show by induction that

\phi_{n}(\lambda)\asymp\log^{(n)}(\lambda),\qquad\phi_{n}^{\prime}(\lambda)\asymp\frac{1}{\lambda\cdot\log\lambda\cdot\log^{(2)}\lambda\cdot\cdots\cdot\log^{(n-1)}(\lambda)},\qquad\mbox{as $\lambda\to 0^{+}$},

where the constants implicit in
\asymp
may depend on
n
. For the base case, see the calculation we have already done for the geometric stable process.
For the inductive step, if the result holds for
n-1
, then

\displaystyle\phi_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\log(1+(\phi_{n-1}(\lambda))^{\beta/2})

\displaystyle\asymp\log(\phi_{n-1}(\lambda))\qquad\mbox{(since $\phi_{n-1}(\lambda)\to\infty$ when $\lambda\to\infty$)}

\displaystyle\asymp\log^{(n)}(\lambda)\qquad\mbox{(by the inductive hypothesis)}

and

\displaystyle\phi_{n}^{\prime}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\phi_{1}^{\prime}(\phi_{n-1}(\lambda))\phi_{n-1}^{\prime}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\phi_{n-1}(\lambda)}\phi_{n-1}^{\prime}(\lambda)\qquad\mbox{(since $\phi_{n-1}(\lambda)\to\infty$ when $\lambda\to\infty$)}

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{1}{\log^{(n-1)}(\lambda)}\cdot\frac{1}{\lambda\cdot\log\lambda\cdot\log^{(2)}\lambda\cdot\cdots\cdot\log^{(n-2)}(\lambda)}\quad\mbox{as $\lambda\to 0^{+}$}\qquad\mbox{(by the inductive hypothesis)}.

Thus, if the subordinator has Laplace exponent
\phi_{n}
, then

\displaystyle j(r)

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{r^{-d-2}}{r^{-2}\log(r^{-2})\log^{(2)}(r^{-2})\cdots\log^{(n-1)}(r^{-2})}=\frac{r^{-d}}{\log(r^{-2})\log^{(2)}(r^{-2})\cdots\log^{(n-1)}(r^{-2})}\qquad\mbox{as $r\to 0^{+}$}.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let

\phi
, drift
\gamma=0
, and Lévy measure

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
S
be a subordinator with Laplace exponent
\phi
. If
\phi(\lambda)\gtrsim\lambda/\log\lambda

is not satisfied.

First, assume

\displaystyle\phi(\lambda_{1})-\phi(\lambda_{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{\lambda_{0}}^{\lambda_{1}}\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)\;\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\lambda_{0}}^{\lambda_{1}}\sigma\left(\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{0}}\right)^{-\delta}\phi^{\prime}(\lambda_{0})\;\mathrm{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma\lambda_{0}^{\delta}\phi^{\prime}(\lambda_{0})}{1-\delta}(\lambda_{1}^{1-\delta}-\lambda_{0}^{1-\delta}).

Therefore, for large

∎

Lemma 5.10
.

Let

, drift

, and Lévy measure

. Let

6.1
A recipe for counterexamples

The following proposition is the basis for all of our negative results.
It gives a general recipe for identifying counterexamples to

. Its statement is somewhat abstract and refers to a sequence of Meyer-decoposition-arising stopping times, defined in Definition
3.4
(stopping times
T^{(r_{0})}
,

, or
T^{\left\{s\right\}}

X
does up until they occur.
Using this general recipe, we will go on to derive more concrete recipes that are easier to directly apply. One of these more concrete recipes (Corollary
6.2
) involves directly looking at the jump kernel of
X
, while the other (Proposition
6.3
) only applies to subordinate Brownian motions and involves looking at the Lévy measure of the subordinator instead. Recall that given sequences
(a_{n})
and
(b_{n})
of positive numbers, we say
a_{n}\ll b_{n}
(or equivalently,
b_{n}\gg a_{n}
) whenever
\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}/b_{n}=0
.

.

Let
X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be an isotropic unimodal Lévy jump process. Suppose there exist sequences
(R_{1,n})_{n=1}^{\infty},(R_{2,n})_{n=1}^{\infty},(R_{3,n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq(0,\infty)
and a sequence
(T_{n})
of Meyer-decomposition-arising exponential stopping times, satisfying the following properties:

•

For all
n
,

0<R_{1,n}<R_{2,n}<R_{1,n}+R_{2,n}<R_{3,n}.

•

There exists a
C>0
such that for all
n
,

R_{2,n}\leq CR_{1,n}.

(6.1)

•

We have

1\gg\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\tau_{B(0,R_{1,n})}<T_{n}\right)\gg\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(|\Delta X(T_{n})|\leq R_{1,n}+R_{2,n}+R_{3,n}\right).

(6.2)

•

As
n\to\infty
,

\sup_{x\in B(0,R_{1,n})}\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(X_{\tau_{B(0,R_{1,n})}}\notin B(0,R_{2,n})\Big|\tau_{B(0,R_{1,n})}<T_{n}\right)\to 0.

(6.3)

•

There exists a
c>0
such that for all
n
, for all
x\in B(0,R_{1,n}+R_{2,n})
,

\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(X_{\tau_{B(0,R_{1,n}+R_{2,n})}}\in B(0,R_{3,n})\Big|\tau_{B(0,R_{1,n}+R_{2,n})}<T_{n}\right)\geq c.

(6.4)

Then
X
does not satisfy
EHI
. Furthermore, if
r_{n}\to 0
along a subsequence then
X
does not satisfy
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\leq 1)
, and if
r_{n}\to\infty
along a subsequence then

does not satisfy
\hyperref@@ii[EHI:d:ehi]{\mathrm{EHI}}(r\geq 1)
.

Before we give a proof, let us explain the significance of some of these conditions. Condition (

) is the most delicate: it says that the process is unlikely to exit a ball of radius

before time

, but much more unlikely to stay within a distance of

when the “scattering jump” (at time

) occurs. In practice, this will be the hardest condition to satisfy. It will be achieved by constructing processes where for all

, most jumps either belong to a much smaller scale than the

) alone are enough to combine to reach this scale, but the first large jump (at

) far surpasses this scale.
Conditions (

) and (

) tell us that at when the process exits a ball using only “small jumps” (ie, before time

), the first point outside the ball that it reaches is unlikely to be too far from the ball it just exited. In practice, these conditions will follow easily from our constructions.

Proof of Proposition

.

and

are contained in the ball

. Note that

. We will show that

, contradicting

.

Let us start by putting a lower bound on

. For all

),

\displaystyle h(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(X_{\tau_{B(0,R_{1}+R_{2})}}\in B(0,R_{3})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\tau_{B(0,R_{1}+R_{2})}<T\right)\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(X_{\tau_{B(0,R_{1}+R_{2})}}\in B(0,R_{3})\Big|\tau_{B(0,R_{1}+R_{2})}<T\right)

In particular,

h(y)\geq c\mathbb{P}_{y}\left(\tau_{B(0,R_{1}+R_{2})}<T\right).

(6.5)

We will use a symmetry argument to show that if the process has initial value

. Consider the vector

(resp,

\mathbb{P}_{y}(A^{+}_{j})=\mathbb{P}_{y}(A^{-}_{j})=\mathbb{P}_{y}\left(A^{+}_{j}\Big|\tau_{B(X_{0},R_{1})}<T\right)=\mathbb{P}_{y}\left(A^{-}_{j}\Big|\tau_{B(X_{0},R_{1})}<T\right)=\frac{1}{2d}.

Note that at least one coordinate of

must have magnitude at least

. Therefore, if the process begins at

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}_{y}\left({\tau_{B(y,R_{1})}<T}\}\cap A^{+}_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{y}\left(\tau_{B(y,R_{1})}<T\right)\mathbb{P}_{y}\left(A^{+}_{1}\Big|\tau_{B(y,R_{1})}<T\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\tau_{B(0,R_{1})}<T\right)\cdot\frac{1}{2d}.

(6.6)

(6.7)

. If the process begins at

•

\displaystyle h(0)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(X_{\tau_{B(0,R_{1}+R_{2})}}\in B(0,R_{3})\right)

. The density of

is
(2\pi)^{-d/2}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}
, so by the change of variables

g(r)=\frac{\int_{\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|>4r\}}e^{-|x|^{2}/2}\,dx}{\int_{\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:2r\leq|x|\leq 3r\}}e^{-|x|^{2}}\,dx}=\frac{\int_{\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|y|\geq 4\}}e^{-r^{2}|y|^{2}/2}\,dy}{\int_{\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:2\leq|y|\leq 3\}}e^{-r^{2}|y|^{2}/2}\,dy}.

(6.16)

For both the numerator and the denominator of the right-hand side of (
6.16
), let us compare the integrand to what it would be for

. This comparison will give us an upper bound for the ratio
g(r)/g(1)
. For the numerator, note that for all

\frac{e^{-r^{2}|y|^{2}/2}}{e^{-|y|^{2}/2}}=e^{-(r^{2}-1)|y|^{2}/2}\leq e^{-8(r^{2}-1)},

or equivalently,

e^{-r^{2}|y|^{2}/2}\leq e^{-8(r^{2}-1)}e^{-|y|^{2}/2}\qquad\mbox{for all $r\geq 1,|y|\geq 4$}.

(6.17)

For the denominator, note that for all

\frac{e^{-r^{2}|y|^{2}/2}}{e^{-|y|^{2}/2}}=e^{-(r^{2}-1)|y|^{2}/2}\geq e^{-\frac{9}{2}(r^{2}-1)},

e^{-r^{2}|y|^{2}/2}\geq e^{-\frac{9}{2}(r^{2}-1)}e^{-|y|^{2}/2}\qquad\mbox{for all $r\geq 1,|y|\leq 3$}.

By applying the bounds (
6.17

6.18

This upper bound is of the form we desired, with
C=g(1)e^{7/2}

c=7/2

∎

6.3
A highly regular counterexample

In this section, we construct a process
X
that has many regularity properties, and yet does not satisfy
EHI
. This process is a subordinate Brownian motion, and has the property that
j(2r)\asymp j(r)
for all
r>0
. Better yet, the Lévy measure
\mu
of its subordinator
S
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
(0,\infty)
, and can be written as
\mu(du)=f(u)\,du
, where
f:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is a continuous, decreasing function, and
f(2u)\asymp f(u)
for all
u>0
. (From this, it follows easily that
j(2r)\asymp j(r)
.) In this sense, the process feels very far from pathological. As it turns out, its unexpected behavior results from
f
being too flat (rather than too fast-decaying) on certain intervals (in fact, it is close to constant on some intervals).

Before we introduce this highly regular counterexample, let us speak about a distribution that will feature prominently in its construction. Let
\alpha=3
. Let
Y
be a positive random variable with density

f_{Y}(y):=\left\{\begin{matrix}\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}&:&\mbox{if $0\leq y\leq 1$}\\
\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}y^{-\alpha}&:&\mbox{if $y\geq 1$}.\end{matrix}\right.

(6.25)

In other words, the density of
Y
is continuous, non-increasing, constant on
[0,1]
, and decays like
y^{-\alpha}
on
[1,\infty)
.
For all
y\in[0,1]
,

\mathbb{P}(Y<y)=\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}y.

(6.26)

For all
y\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}(Y>y)=\frac{1}{\alpha}y^{-(\alpha-1)}.

(6.27)

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
Y
have distribution (
6.25
), and let
Z
be a standard Gaussian in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, independent of
Y
. Then there exists a constant
C
such that for all
a\in(0,1)
,

\mathbb{P}(Y|Z|\leq a)\leq Ca^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}.

Proof.

In order to have
\sqrt{Y}|Z|\leq a
, we must have either
\sqrt{Y}\leq a^{\frac{d}{d+2}}
or
|Z|\leq a^{\frac{2}{d+2}}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\sqrt{Y}|Z|\leq a)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(Y\leq a^{\frac{2d}{d+2}})+\mathbb{P}(|Z|\leq a^{\frac{2}{d+2}})

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}a^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}+\int_{B(0,a^{\frac{2}{d+2}})}(2\pi)^{-d/2}\exp(-\frac{|x|^2}{2})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}+(2\pi)^{-d/2}|B(0,1)|\right)a^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}.

∎

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
H_{n}:=2^{n^{2}}
and
A_{n}=2^{-2n^{2}}
. The description of the subordinator
S
is as follows. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a Poisson clock which rings with rate
H_{n}
. Whenever the
n
th clock rings,
S
takes a jump forward with size equal in distribution to
A_{n}Y
(where
Y
is as described in (
6.25
)). We call this a “type-
n
jump.” More formally, for all
n
, let
N^{(n)}=(N^{(n)}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a Poisson point process with rate
H_{n}
. Let the collection
(N^{(n)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be independent. Then let
(Y_{n,i})_{n\in\mathbb{N},i\in\mathbb{N}}
be an iid collection of copies of
Y
.
For all
t\geq 0
, let

S_{t}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{N^{(n)}_{t}}A_{n}Y_{n,i}.

Note that
N^{(n)}_{t}
is the number of type-
n
jumps that have occurred by time
t
.
As usual, let
B
be a Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with twice the standard speed, and let
X_{t}:=B(S_{t})
.

In addition to the probabilistic description above, we can describe
S
in terms of its Lévy measure:

\mu(\mathrm{d}t)=\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}H_{n}A_{n}^{-1}\min\{1,(t/A_{n})^{-3}\}\;\mathrm{d}t=\frac{2}{3}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{3n^{2}}\min\left\{1,\left(2^{2n^{2}}t\right)^{-3}\right\}\;\mathrm{d}t.

Theorem 6.7
.

If
X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}=(B(S_{t}))_{t\geq 0}
is the process constructed above, then
X
does not satisfy
EHI
.

We will usually write
\alpha
,
H_{n}
, and
A_{n}
(rather than their exact values
3
,
2^{n^{2}}
, and
2^{-2n^{2}}
) to illustrate that the idea behind this proof is easily adaptable to different instantiations of these parameters. Generally,
\alpha
must be a sufficiently large exponent,
(H_{n})
must be a sequence that grows faster than exponentially (with
H_{n}\ll H_{n+1}
), and
(A_{n})
must be a sequence that decays much faster than
H_{n}
grows (with
H_{n}A_{n}\gg H_{n+1}A_{n+1}
). Throughout the proof, when we use notation like
o(1)
,
\lesssim
, or
\ll
, we mean as
n\to\infty
.

The key idea is that
S
usually takes jumps on the order of
A_{n}
for some
n
, which corresponds to
X
taking jumps with magnitude on the order of
\sqrt{A_{n}}
. At a scale between
\sqrt{A_{n+1}}
and
\sqrt{A_{n}}
, the type-
m
jumps for
m>n
are all very small, so the process looks like a Brownian motion until the first jump of type
n
or less. (The exponent
\alpha
being larger than
2
is enough to achieve this.) We construct sequences
(s_{n})
and
(r_{n})
such that
A_{n+1}\ll s_{n}\ll r_{n}^{2}\ll A_{n}
. Then the stopping time
T^{\{s_{n}\}}
(the first time

takes a jump of size greater than
s_{n}
) will usually be the time of the first jump of type
n
or less. At this time, because
A_{n}
is so much larger than
r_{n}^{2}
, the process will usually jump somewhere very far away. The exact choices of
(s_{n})
and
(r_{n})
are reverse-engineered so that the conditions of Proposition
6.3
will be satisfied.

Proof of Theorem
6.7
.

We will apply Proposition
6.3
, with

\displaystyle s_{n}

\displaystyle:=\frac{H_{n+1}}{H_{n}}A_{n+1}=2^{-(2n^{2}+2n+1)},

\displaystyle r_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\frac{H_{n+1}}{H_{n}}A_{n+1}\log(n)=2^{-(2n^{2}+2n+1)}\log n.

Then
A_{n+1}\ll s_{n}\ll r_{n}^{2}\ll A_{n}
. We must show that
1\gg\mathbb{P}_{0}(\tau_{B(0,r_{n})}<T^{\{s_{n}\}})\gg\mathbb{P}_{0}(|\Delta X(T^{\{s_{n}\}})|\leq 36r_{n})
.

Let
\lambda_{\{s_{n}\}}
denote the rate at which jumps such that
\Delta S(t)>s_{n}
occur. Since
A_{n+1}\ll s_{n}\ll A_{n}
, by (
6.26
) and (

\lambda_{\{s_{n}\}}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}H_{m}\mathbb{P}(A_{m}Y>s_{n})=(1-o(1))\sum_{m=1}^{n}H_{m}+\sum_{m=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha}H_{m}\left(\frac{A_{m}}{s_{n}}\right)^{\alpha-1}.

One may verify that the dominant term of this series is
H_{n}
, so

\lambda_{\{s_{n}\}}=(1\pm o(1))H_{n}.

(6.28)

Next, let us consider the rate at which jumps such that
\frac{A_{n+1}}{2}\leq\Delta S(t)\leq s_{n}
occur. Let
\lambda^{*}_{n}
denote this rate. By putting a lower bound on
\lambda^{*}_{n}
, we will put a lower bound on the probability that enough of these jumps occur before time
T^{\{s_{n}\}}
that
S_{t}
is already on the order of
r_{n}^{2}
. Note that more than half of the type-
(n+1)
jumps are of this size (at least for sufficiently large
n
), since by (
6.26
) and (
6.27
),

\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{A_{n+1}}{2}\leq A_{n+1}Y\leq s_{n}\right)=1-\mathbb{P}\left(Y\leq\frac{1}{2}\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(Y\geq\frac{s_{n}}{A_{n-1}}\right)=1-\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\cdot\frac{1}{2}-o(1)>\frac{1}{2}.

Therefore, for sufficiently large
n
,

\lambda^{*}_{n}\geq\frac{1}{2}H_{n+1}.

(6.29)

Let
c
be a small constant. The probability of getting at least
c\frac{r_{n}^{2}}{A_{n+1}}

\frac{A_{n+1}}{2}\leq s_{n}\leq s_{n}

is equal to

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\lambda^{*}_{n}}{\lambda^{*}_{n}+\lambda_{\{s_{n}\}}}\right)^{\left\lceil c\frac{r_{n}^{2}}{A_{n+1}}\right\rceil}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{\lambda_{\{s_{n}\}}}{\lambda^{*}_{n}}\right)^{-\left\lceil c\frac{r_{n}^{2}}{A_{n+1}}\right\rceil}

\displaystyle\geq\exp(- \frac{\lambda_{\{s_n\}}}{\lambda^*_n} \ceil{ c \frac{r_n^2}{A_{n+1}}}).

Since
r_{n}^{2}\gg A_{n+1}
,
\lambda_{\{s_{n}\}}=(1+o(1))H_{n}
, and
\lambda^{*}_{n}\geq\frac{1}{2}H_{n+1}
, we can easily choose a

so that

\frac{\lambda_{\{s_{n}\}}}{\lambda^{*}_{n}}\left\lceil c\frac{r_{n}^{2}}{A_{n+1}}\right\rceil\leq\frac{H_{n}}{H_{n+1}}\cdot\frac{r_{n}^{2}}{A_{n+1}}.

Therefore, with probability at least
\exp(-(H_n / H_{n+1}) (r_n^2 / A_{n+1}))

takes at least
cr_{n}^{2}/A_{n+1}

before time

before time

, so the conditional probability that

for some

is on the order of

\mathbb{P}_{0}(\tau_{B(0,r_{n})}<T^{\{s_{n}\}})\gtrsim\exp(-\frac{H_n}{H_{n+1}} \cdot\frac{r_n^2}{A_{n+1}})=\frac{1}{n}.

(6.30)

We would like to put an upper bound on

might (surprisingly) be a type-
m

The rate at which jumps in

of size greater than

. The rate at which jumps in

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\mbox{$T^{\{s_{n}\}}$ is type-$m$ for some $m<n$}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sum_{m=n+1}^{\infty}H_{m}\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\frac{A_{m}}{s_{n}}\right)^{\alpha-1}}{(1-o(1))H_{n}}

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{H_{n+1}}{H_{n}}\left(\frac{A_{n+1}}{s_{n}}\right)^{\alpha-1}=\left(\frac{H_{n}}{H_{n+1}}\right)^{\alpha-2}.

(We obtained the
\asymp
in the above equation by considering the dominant term in the series.)
On the other hand, if
T^{\{s_{n}\}}
is type
m
for some
m\leq n
, then the displacement of
X
from this jump is equal in distribution to
\sqrt{2A_{m}Y}Z
, where
Z\sim N(0,I_{d})
is independent of
Y
, so by Lemma
6.6
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(|\Delta X(T^{\{s_{n}\}})|\Big|\mbox{$T^{\{s_{n}\}}$ is type-$m$ for some $m\geq n$}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sqrt{2A_{n}Y}|Z|\leq 36r_{n}\Big|Y\geq s_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sqrt{2A_{n}Y}|Z|\leq 36r_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{r_{n}^{2}}{A_{n}}\right)^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}.

Thus,

\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(|\Delta X(T^{\{s_{n}\}})|\leq 36r_{n}\right)\lesssim\left(\frac{H_{n}}{H_{n+1}}\right)^{\alpha-2}+\left(\frac{r_{n}^{2}}{A_{n}}\right)^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}=2^{-(2n+1)(\alpha-2)}+\left(2^{-(2n+1)}\log n\right)^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\ll\frac{1}{n}.

(6.31)

We must still show that
\mathbb{P}_{0}(\tau_{B(0,r_{n})}<T^{\{s_{n}\}})\ll 1
. Consider the truncated processes
\tilde{S}^{\{s_{n}\}}
and
\tilde{X}^{\{s_{n}\}}
, which do not include the jumps of size greater than
S_{n}
in
S
.
We use the notation
\tilde{\tau}^{\{s_{n}\}}_{U}
to denote exit times for
\tilde{X}^{\{s_{n}\}}

By Lemma
4.1
, Markov’s inequality, and conditioning on
\tilde{S}^{\{s_{n}\}}_{T^{\{s_{n}\}}}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\tau_{B(0,r_{n})}<T^{\{s_{n}\}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\tilde{\tau}^{\{s_{n}\}}_{B(0,r_{n})}<T^{\{s_{n}\}}\right)\leq 2\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\left|\tilde{X}^{\{s_{n}\}}_{T^{\{s_{n}\}}}\right|\geq r_{n}\right)\leq 2r_{n}^{-2}\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\left|\tilde{X}^{\{s_{n}\}}_{T^{\{s_{n}\}}}\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\asymp r_{n}^{-2}\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\tilde{S}^{\{s_{n}\}}_{T^{\{s_{n}\}}}\right]=r_{n}^{-2}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\tilde{S}^{\{s_{n}\}}_{1}\right]}{\lambda_{\{s_{n}\}}}.

To compute
\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\tilde{S}^{\{s_{n}\}}_{1}\right]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\tilde{S}^{\{s_{n}\}}_{1}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}H_{m}\mathbb{E}[A_{m}Y\cdot\mathbf{1}\left\{A_{m}Y\leq s_{n}\right\}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}H_{m}A_{m}\mathbb{E}\left[Y\mathbf{1}\left\{Y\leq\frac{s_{n}}{A_{m}}\right\}\right].

For
m>n
, let us just replace the indicator with
1
for an upper bound. For
m\leq n
, let us replace
Y\mathbf{1}\left\{Y\leq s_{n}/A_{m}\right\}
with
(s_{n}/A_{m})\mathbf{1}\left\{Y\leq s_{n}/A_{m}\right\}
. The result is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}\left[\tilde{S}^{\{s_{n}\}}_{1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=1}^{n}H_{m}s_{n}\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(Y\leq\frac{s_{n}}{A_{m}}\right)+\sum_{m=n+1}^{\infty}H_{m}A_{m}\mathbb{E}[Y]

\displaystyle\lesssim s_{n}\sum_{m=1}^{n}H_{m}+\sum_{m=n+1}^{\infty}H_{m}A_{m}.

This series has two dominant terms, corresponding to
m=n
and
m=n+1
, which are both equal to
H_{n+1}A_{n+1}
. Therefore,

\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\tau_{B(0,r_{n})}<T^{\{s_{n}\}}\right)\leq\frac{H_{n+1}A_{n+1}}{r_{n}^{2}\lambda_{\{s_{n}\}}}\asymp\frac{H_{n+1}A_{n+1}}{r_{n}^{2}H_{n}}=\frac{s_{n}}{r_{n}^{2}}=\frac{1}{\log n}\ll 1.

(6.32)

By (
6.32
), (
6.30
), and (
6.31
),

1\gg\frac{1}{\log n}\gtrsim\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(\tau_{B(0,r_{n})}<T^{\{s_{n}\}}\right)\gtrsim\frac{1}{n}\gg\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(|\Delta X(T^{\{s_{n}\}})|\leq 36r_{n}\right),

as desired.
∎

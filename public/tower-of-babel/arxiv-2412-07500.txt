Lower Bounds for Admissible Values of the Travelling Wave Speed in Asymmetrically Supported Beam
Hana Formánková Levá, Gabriela Holubová, Petr Nečesal

Abstract
We study the admissible values of the wave speed c c for which the beam equation with jumping nonlinearity possesses a travelling wave solution. In contrast to previously studied problems modelling suspension bridges, the presence of the term with negative part of the solution in the equation results in restrictions of c c . In this paper, we provide the maximal wave speed range for which the existence of the travelling wave solution can be proved using the Mountain Pass Theorem. We also introduce its close connection with related Dirichlet problems and their Fučík spectra. Moreover, we present several analytical approximations of the main existence result with assumptions that are easy to verify. Finally, we formulate a conjecture that the infimum of the admissible wave speed range can be described by the Fučík spectrum of a simple periodic problem.

Motivation

When studying the existence of homoclinic travelling waves in asymmetrically supported vibrating beams, i.e., solutions of the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{tt}+u_{xxxx}+au^{+}-bu^{-}=1,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\,t>0,\\[3.0pt]
u(x,t)\rightarrow 1/a,~u_{x}(x,t)\rightarrow 0~~\textup{for}~|x|\rightarrow+\infty,\end{array}\right.

(1)

which can be expressed in the form
u(x,t)=y(x-ct)
,
the following basic question naturally arises:

Q_{c}
: What are the admissible values of wave speed
c
for which the travelling wave solutions exist?

In (
1
),

a
,
b
are positive constants and
u^{+}
and
u^{-}
denote the positive and negative parts of
u

u^{\pm}(x,t)=\max\{\pm u(x,t),0\}

u=u^{+}-u^{-}
.
Substituting
u(x,t)=y(x-ct)=y(s)

1

y^{(4)}+c^{2}y^{\prime\prime}+ay^{+}-by^{-}=1.

(2)

Further, putting
z(s):=y(s)-1/a

1/a
of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}z^{(4)}+c^{2}z^{\prime\prime}+a(z+1/a)^{+}-b(z+1/a)^{-}-1=0,\\[3.0pt]
z,\,z^{\prime}\rightarrow 0~~\textup{for}~|s|\rightarrow+\infty.\end{array}\right.

(3)

A necessary condition for the existence of
z
satisfying (
3
)
follows from
[
9
]
and can be written as

|c|\in\left(\sqrt[4]{4b},\sqrt[4]{4a}\right).

(4)

In particular, Corollary 2 in
[
9
]
states that (
3
)
has only a trivial solution for

that does not fulfill
(
4
)
.
It was shown in
[
4
]
that the condition (
4
)
for

|c|\in\left(0,\sqrt[4]{4a}\right)
,
is also the sufficient one and that this interval stays preserved even if we include an additional nontrivial nonlinearity

[

[

As for the sufficient condition in the case

|c|\in\left(\sqrt[4]{100b/9},\sqrt[4]{4a}\right).

The authors in

The aim of this paper is to describe the optimal lower bound for

First answer to
Q_{c}

Let us denote
\Omega:=(1,+\infty)\times(0,1)
, consider the Hilbert space
H^{2}(\mathbb{R})=W^{2,2}(\mathbb{R})
with the standard norm

\|z\|_{H^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left((z^{\prime\prime})^{2}+(z^{\prime})^{2}+z^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s
and introduce
the functional

J:~\mathbb{R}^{2}\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}
in the form

J(\alpha,\beta,v):=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\left((v^{\prime\prime})^{2}-2(v^{\prime})^{2}+\alpha(v^{+})^{2}+\beta(v^{-})^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

(5)

and the set

\Omega^{\texttt{-}}:=\left\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\Omega:~\exists v\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}):~J(\alpha,\beta,v)<0\right\}.

(6)

The first answer to
Q_{c}
(in the sense of a sufficient condition) can be formulated as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
a>b>0
be arbitrary but fixed.
Then the travelling wave solution of (
1
) exists for any wave speed
c
satisfying

\left(\frac{4a}{c^{4}},\frac{4b}{c^{4}}\right)\in\Omega^{\texttt{-}}.

(7)

Proof.

All weak (and due to regularity also classical) solutions
z\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
of (
3
) correspond to critical points of the functional
I\in C^{1}(H^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{R})
,

I(z):=\dfrac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\left((z^{\prime\prime})^{2}-c^{2}(z^{\prime})^{2}+az^{2}-(a-b)\left((z+1/a)^{-}\right)^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(8)

Trivially,
I(0)=0
. Exploiting the results of
[
8
]
, we can see that
I
has a local minimum at
0
and another nontrivial critical point exists, if
I
has the mountain pass geometry. This requires the existence of some
e\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}
such that
I(e)<0
. Considering
e=Av
with
A
being a positive real number and
v
some fixed element in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we can write

I(Av)=\dfrac{A^{2}}{2}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\left((v^{\prime\prime})^{2}-c^{2}(v^{\prime})^{2}+av^{2}-(a-b)\left(\left(v+1/(aA)\right)^{-}\right)^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(9)

Hence, for
A
and
v

such that the integral in (
9
) is negative, we obtain the required condition
I(e)<0
. Moreover,

\lim_{A\to+\infty}\frac{2I(Av)}{A^{2}}=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\left((v^{\prime\prime})^{2}-c^{2}(v^{\prime})^{2}+a(v^{+})^{2}+b(v^{-})^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}s=:{\cal J}(a,b,c,v).

(10)

Transforming
x=s|c|/\sqrt{2}
and denoting

\alpha:=\frac{4a}{c^{4}},\qquad\beta:=\frac{4b}{c^{4}}

(11)

we obtain
{\cal J}(a,b,c,v)={{|c|^{3}}/(2\sqrt{2}})\,J(\alpha,\beta,v)

with
J
given by (
5
).

Now, for any
c

satisfying (
7
),
we have
(\alpha,\beta)\in\Omega^{\texttt{-}}
.
That is,
there exists
v\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that
J(\alpha,\beta,v)<0
.
Consequently,
{\cal J}(a,b,c,v)<0
and
hence
I(e)<0
with
e=Av
for
A>0
large enough. This implies that the functional
I
has the required geometry and the existence of the travelling wave solution of (
1
) then follows directly from the main result in
[
8
]
.
∎

Remark 2
.

1.

Due to (
11
) and the necessary condition for
the existence of the travelling wave solution
\sqrt[4]{4b}<|c|<\sqrt[4]{4a}
,
we have
\beta<1<\alpha
. Hence, the restriction to
\Omega
in (
6
) makes sense.

2.

Let us note that for
\alpha>1
and
\beta\geq 1
, we obtain

J(\alpha,\beta,v)\geq\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\left((v^{\prime\prime})^{2}-2(v^{\prime})^{2}+v^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\omega^{2}-1\right)^{2}\hat{v}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega>0

(12)

for any nontrivial
v\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Here,
\hat{v}(\omega)=\mathcal{F}(v(x))
denotes the Fourier transform of
v=v(x)
.
Further, we have

I(Av)\geq A^{2}|c|^{3}/(4\sqrt{2})J(\alpha,\beta,v)
,
since
(v+1/(aA))^{-}\leq v^{-}
for any
aA>0
. Hence, if
J(\alpha,\beta,v)\geq 0
for all
v
, the functional
I
loses the mountain pass geometry around zero.

In the following sections we provide several characterizations of the set
\Omega^{\texttt{-}}
leading to more explicit descriptions of the admissible wave speed range.

3
Characterization of
\Omega^{\texttt{-}}

One partial characterization of
\Omega^{\texttt{-}}
, and hence answer to
Q_{c}
, was already provided in
[
8
]
.

Lemma 3

(cf.
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\Omega^{\texttt{-}}
be defined by (
6
). Then

(1,+\infty)\times\big(0,\tfrac{9}{25}\big)\subset\Omega^{\texttt{-}}.

Proof.

Taking
v_{0}(x)=-4\cos^{3}(x/\sqrt{5})/\sqrt{5\sqrt{5}\pi}
for

x\in[-\sqrt{5}\pi/2,\sqrt{5}\pi/2]
and
v_{0}(x)=0
elsewhere, we can directly compute

J(\alpha,\beta,v_{0})=\beta-\frac{9}{25}
. Hence the assertion follows.
∎

To provide the description of
\Omega^{\texttt{-}}
,
let us set
\alpha=q\beta
with
q\in(1,+\infty)
arbitrary but fixed, i.e.,

J(\alpha,\beta,v)=J(q\beta,\beta,v)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}}\left((v^{\prime\prime})^{2}-2(v^{\prime})^{2}+\beta\left(q(v^{+})^{2}+(v^{-})^{2}\right)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

and denote

\beta^{\ast}(q):=\sup\left\{\beta>0:~\exists v\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}):~J(q\beta,\beta,v)=0\right\},\quad q>1.

(13)

Notice that Lemma
3
and Remark
2
immediately imply

\frac{9}{25}\leq\beta^{\ast}(q)\leq 1
for any
q>1
.
Using
\beta^{\ast}
, we can characterize
\Omega^{\texttt{-}}
in the following way.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\beta^{\ast}
be given by (
13
). Then

\Omega^{\texttt{-}}=\left\{(q\beta,\beta)\in\Omega:q>1,\ \beta<\beta^{\ast}(q)\right\}.

(14)

Proof.

Indeed, if
\beta_{0}
and
v_{0}
are such that
J(q\beta_{0},\beta_{0},v_{0})=0
, then
J(q\beta,\beta,v_{0})<0
for any
\beta<\beta_{0}
. The assertion thus follows directly from the definitions of
\Omega^{\texttt{-}}
and
\beta^{\ast}
(cf. (
6
), (
13
)).
∎

By Lemma
4
, we have converted the problem of describing
\Omega^{\texttt{-}}
to the problem of describing
\beta^{\ast}
.

4
Connection of
\beta^{\ast}
to the Fučík spectrum and another answer to
Q_{c}

First of all, since

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(q(v^{+})^{2}+(v^{-})^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}}v^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x>0
for any nontrivial
v\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
q>1
, we can set

S_{q}:=\left\{v\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}):~\int_{\mathbb{R}}(q(v^{+})^{2}+(v^{-})^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x=1\right\}

and express

\beta^{\ast}(q)=\sup_{v\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}}(2(v^{\prime})^{2}-(v^{\prime\prime})^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x}{\int_{\mathbb{R}}(q(v^{+})^{2}+(v^{-})^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x}=\sup_{v\in S_{q}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(2(v^{\prime})^{2}-(v^{\prime\prime})^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x.

For an arbitrary
r>0
, let us now consider the space

H_{0}^{2}(-r,r):=\left\{v\in H^{2}(-r,r):~v(\pm r)=v^{\prime}(\pm r)=0\right\}

and define the function
\beta_{r}^{\ast}
as

\beta_{r}^{\ast}(q):=\sup_{v\in S_{r,q}}\int_{-r}^{r}(2(v^{\prime})^{2}-(v^{\prime\prime})^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad q>1,

(15)

where the manifold
S_{r,q}
is given by

S_{r,q}:=\left\{v\in H_{0}^{2}(-r,r):~\int_{-r}^{r}(q(v^{+})^{2}+(v^{-})^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x=1\right\}.

Similarly as in the case of
\beta^{\ast}
, we easily obtain
that
\beta^{\ast}_{r}(q)\geq\frac{9}{25}
for any
r\geq\sqrt{5}\pi/2
and
q>1

(we can choose the same
v_{0}\in S_{r,q}
as in the proof of Lemma
3
),
or that
\beta^{\ast}_{r}(q)>0
for any
r>\sqrt{2}\pi/2
and
q>1

(for details and suitable choice of
v
, see Remark
31
in
A
).
For these reasons, we limit our considerations to
r>\sqrt{2}\pi/2
in the following assertions.

Proposition 5
.

For any
q>1
fixed, we have

\beta^{\ast}(q)=\sup_{r>\sqrt{2}\pi/2}\beta_{r}^{\ast}(q)=\lim_{r\to+\infty}\beta_{r}^{\ast}(q).

(16)

Proof.

Considering an arbitrary pair
\sqrt{2}\pi/2<r_{1}<r_{2}
, any
v\in H_{0}^{2}(-r_{1},r_{1})
extended by zero to
(-r_{2},r_{2})
belongs to
H_{0}^{2}(-r_{2},r_{2})
, similarly any
v\in S_{r_{1},q}
extended by zero to
(-r_{2},r_{2})
belongs to
S_{r_{2},q}
. Hence, we obtain
0<\beta_{r_{1}}^{\ast}(q)\leq\beta_{r_{2}}^{\ast}(q)\leq\beta^{\ast}(q)
.
Moreover, any
v\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
can be approximated by functions with compact support, i.e., by functions from
H_{0}^{2}(-r,r)
with sufficiently large
r
.
Thus, we can conclude that (
16
) holds true.
∎

For another fundamental characterization of
\beta^{\ast}
, let us consider the Dirichlet boundary value problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}v^{(4)}+2v^{\prime\prime}+\alpha v^{+}-\beta v^{-}=0,\quad x\in(-r,r),\\[3.0pt]
v(\pm r)=v^{\prime}(\pm r)=0,\end{array}\right.

(17)

and denote by
\Sigma^{\texttt{D}}_{r}
the corresponding Fučík spectrum of (
17
), i.e.,

\Sigma^{\texttt{D}}_{r}:=\left\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:~\mbox{\eqref{eq:fucik} has a nontrivial solution}\right\}.

Remark 6
.

Let us note that the linear term
v^{(4)}+2v^{\prime\prime}
in (
17
)
can be formally written as
(\partial^{2}+1)^{2}v-v
and the operator

(\partial^{2}+1)^{2}
is sometimes called the (stationary)
Swift-Hohenberg operator
.
Its origin comes from the fourth order term in Swift–Hohenberg equation
in
[
11
]
which was used to study hydrodynamic behaviour of fluids.
Nowadays, it finds many other applications, e.g., in models of pattern formation.

We claim the following.

Proposition 7
.

For any
r>\sqrt{2}\pi/2
and
q>1
, we have

\beta^{\ast}_{r}(q)=\max\left\{\beta>0:\,(q\beta,\beta)\in\Sigma^{\textup{{D}}}_{r}\right\}.

Consequently,

\beta^{\ast}(q)=\sup_{r>\sqrt{2}\pi/2}\max\left\{\beta>0:\,(q\beta,\beta)\in\Sigma^{\textup{{D}}}_{r}\right\}.

(18)

Proof.

First of all, we show that the supremum in (
15
) is really attained. Indeed, there must exist a sequence
(v_{n})_{n=1}^{+\infty}\subset S_{r,q}
such that
\int_{-r}^{r}(2(v_{n}^{\prime})^{2}-(v_{n}^{\prime\prime})^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x\to\beta_{r}^{\ast}(q)
.
Using the fact that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=\left(\int_{-r}^{r}(v^{\prime\prime})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2}
is an equivalent norm on
H_{0}^{2}(-r,r)
,

\int_{-r}^{r}v^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 1
on
S_{r,q}
and applying the estimate

2\int_{-r}^{r}(v^{\prime})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=-2\int_{-r}^{r}v^{\prime\prime}v\,\mathrm{d}x\leq p^{2}\int_{-r}^{r}(v^{\prime\prime})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{p^{2}}\int_{-r}^{r}v^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq p^{2}\int_{-r}^{r}(v^{\prime\prime})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{p^{2}}

with
p\in(0,1)
,
we can write

(1-p^{2}){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{p^{2}}-\int_{-r}^{r}(2(v_{n}^{\prime})^{2}-(v_{n}^{\prime\prime})^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\to\quad\frac{1}{p^{2}}-\beta_{r}^{\ast}(q)\in\mathbb{R}.

Hence
(v_{n})
is bounded in
H_{0}^{2}(-r,r)
. Due to the reflexivity of
H_{0}^{2}(-r,r)
and its compact embedding into
H_{0}^{1}(-r,r)
, there exists a subsequence (denoted again by
(v_{n})
) and
v\in H_{0}^{2}(-r,r)
such that

v_{n}\rightharpoonup v\quad\mbox{in }H_{0}^{2}(-r,r),\qquad v_{n}\to v\quad\mbox{in }L^{2}(-r,r),\qquad v^{\prime}_{n}\to v^{\prime}\quad\mbox{in }L^{2}(-r,r).

This implies
\int_{-r}^{r}(q(v^{+})^{2}+(v^{-})^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{-r}^{r}(q(v^{+}_{n})^{2}+(v^{-}_{n})^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x=1
, i.e.,
v\in S_{r,q}
. Using the definition of
\beta_{r}^{\ast}(q)
and the weak semicontinuity of the norm, we finally obtain

\displaystyle\beta_{r}^{\ast}(q)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{-r}^{r}\left(2(v^{\prime})^{2}-(v^{\prime\prime})^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=2\|v^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}-{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\geq 2\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\|v^{\prime}_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}-\liminf\limits_{n\to+\infty}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\int_{-r}^{r}\left(2(v_{n}^{\prime})^{2}-(v_{n}^{\prime\prime})^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=\beta_{r}^{\ast}(q),

that is,
\beta_{r}^{\ast}(q)
is attained at
v\in S_{r,q}
.

Now, since
v\not\equiv 0
, we can apply the standard Lagrange multiplier method (cf.
[
6
, Theorem 7.8.2]
) and conclude that there exist
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\int_{-r}^{r}\left(v^{\prime\prime}\varphi^{\prime\prime}-2v^{\prime}\varphi^{\prime}+\lambda(qv^{+}-v^{-})\varphi\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=0\quad\mbox{for all }\varphi\in H_{0}^{2}(-r,r).

(19)

Taking
\varphi=v
in (

), we get
\lambda=\beta_{r}^{\ast}(q)
. Moreover, (
19
) is a weak formulation of (
17
) with
(\alpha,\beta)=(q\beta_{r}^{\ast}(q),\beta_{r}^{\ast}(q))
. Due to standard regularity arguments, the nontrivial weak solution of (
17
) coincides with the classical one and
(q\beta_{r}^{\ast}(q),\beta_{r}^{\ast}(q))\in\Sigma^{\texttt{D}}_{r}
.

Relation (
18
) is then a direct consequence of Proposition
5
.
∎

Since our primary goal is the existence of travelling wave solutions of (

For

, we have

\beta_{r}^{\ast}(q)\to\lambda_{1,r}^{\textup{{D}}}

Lemma 9
.

For any

\beta^{\ast}(q)>1/q

Proof.

For simplicity, let us denote

Due to Proposition

\beta^{\ast}_{r}(q)=\max_{v\in H^{2}_{0}(-r,r)\setminus\{0\}}R_{q,r}(v)=R_{q,r}(v_{q,r})\geq R_{q,r}(v_{1,r}),

where

solves (

and

is the eigenfunction of (

to the first (largest) eigenvalue

If we proceed similarly as in

integrals with respect to

\displaystyle\int_{\breve{v}_{n}>0}\left(\breve{v}_{n}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\tfrac{16}{15}G(p_{1},x_{1})n+\mathcal{O}(1),

\displaystyle\int_{\breve{v}_{n}<0}\left(\breve{v}_{n}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\tfrac{16}{15}G(p_{2},x_{2})n+\mathcal{O}(1),

\displaystyle\int_{\breve{v}_{n}>0}\left(\breve{v}_{n}^{\prime}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\tfrac{16}{15}M(p_{1},x_{1})n+\mathcal{O}(1),

\displaystyle\int_{\breve{v}_{n}<0}\left(\breve{v}_{n}^{\prime}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\tfrac{16}{15}M(p_{2},x_{2})n+\mathcal{O}(1),

\displaystyle\int_{\breve{v}_{n}>0}\left(\breve{v}_{n}^{\prime\prime}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\tfrac{16}{15}N(p_{1},x_{1})n+\mathcal{O}(1),

\displaystyle\int_{\breve{v}_{n}<0}\left(\breve{v}_{n}^{\prime\prime}\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\tfrac{16}{15}N(p_{2},x_{2})n+\mathcal{O}(1),

\displaystyle J(\alpha,\beta,v_{n})=

\displaystyle\ \tfrac{16}{15}\big(N(p_{1},x_{1})+N(p_{2},x_{2})-2M(p_{1},x_{1})-2M(p_{2},x_{2})+

\displaystyle\ \alpha\cdot G(p_{1},x_{1})+\beta\cdot G(p_{2},x_{2})\big)\cdot n+\mathcal{O}(1),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ n\left(\tfrac{16}{15}\left((\alpha-\alpha_{0})\cdot G(p_{1},x_{1})+(\beta-\beta_{0})\cdot G(p_{2},x_{2})\right)+\frac{\mathcal{O}(1)}{n}\right).

for

.
Hence, we conclude that

for sufficiently large

.
∎

7
Characterization of
\beta^{\ast}
using the periodic problem

As we have stated above, the exact characterization (
18
) of
\beta^{\ast}

(and hence
\mathscr{E}
) is not suitable for computation, since
it is not easy to obtain
\Sigma^{\texttt{D}}_{r}
either.
An alternative and much more straightforward approach to the description of
\beta^{\ast}

is inspired by the properties of the eigenvalues
\lambda^{\texttt{D}}_{n,r}
of the Dirichlet problem (
20
)
and the eigenvalues
\lambda^{\texttt{P}}_{n,T}
of the periodic problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}v^{(4)}+2v^{\prime\prime}+\lambda v=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\\[3.0pt]
v(x+T)=v(x).\end{array}\right.

(48)

Namely, the following statement holds true (cf.
A
).

Lemma 25
.

We have

\lim_{q\to 1+}\beta^{\ast}(q)=\sup_{r>0}\max_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda^{\textup{{D}}}_{n,r}=\lim_{r\to+\infty}\lambda^{\textup{{D}}}_{1,r}=1=\sup_{T>0}\max_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\lambda^{\textup{{P}}}_{n,T}=\lambda^{\textup{{P}}}_{1,2\pi}.

Similarly, for the (even) eigenfunction
v^{\mathtt{D}}_{1,r}

corresponding to
\lambda^{\textup{{D}}}_{1,r}
, we have

\lim_{r\to+\infty}v^{\mathtt{D}}_{1,r}(x)=\cos x

which coincides with the eigenfunction
corresponding to
\lambda^{\textup{{P}}}_{1,2\pi}
.

That is, suprema of all Dirichlet and periodic eigenvalues coincide, moreover, in the case of the periodic problem, the supremum is attained. Besides that, the periodic eigenvalues are easier to compute. Description of both the eigenvalues and their properties are stated in
A
.

Motivated by this fact,
let us now consider the periodic problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}v^{(4)}+2v^{\prime\prime}+\alpha v^{+}-\beta v^{-}=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\\[3.0pt]
v(x+T)=v(x),\end{array}\right.

(49)

with
T>0
and denote by
\Sigma^{\texttt{P}}_{T}
the corresponding Fučík spectrum of (
49
), i.e.,

\Sigma^{\texttt{P}}_{T}:=\left\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:~\mbox{\eqref{eq:fucik_per} has a nontrivial solution}\right\}.

Unlike
\Sigma^{\texttt{D}}_{r}
,
the Fučík spectrum
\Sigma^{\texttt{P}}_{T}
is easier to obtain. For the full analytic description of its first component see
B
. Denoting

\hat{\beta}=\hat{\beta}(q):=\sup\limits_{T>0}\sup\left\{\beta>0:(q\beta,\beta)\in\Sigma_{T}^{\textup{{P}}}\right\},

(50)

we state the following.

Conjecture 26
.

Let
\Sigma^{\textup{{P}}}_{T}
be the Fučík spectrum of (
49
) with a period
T>0
and
\hat{\beta}
be given by (
50
). Then for any
q>1
,
\beta^{\ast}(q)=\hat{\beta}(q)
. Moreover, both suprema in (

8
Open problems

Although we have provided an exact theoretical description of the optimal travelling
wave speed range (cf. Theorem
10
) as well as families of its approximations
which are easy to evaluate (cf. Theorems
12
and
15
) together
with their envelopes (cf. Theorems
19
and
22
), several questions still remain open.

First of all, our methods are based on variational approach,
namely Mountain Pass Theorem, and Theorems
1
and
10
provide
the maximal possible wave speed range for which the mountain pass geometry is preserved.
So the following natural questions arise.

Q_{1}
:
Is it possible to enlarge the interval for admissible values of the wave speed up to
\left(\sqrt[4]{4b},\sqrt[4]{4a}\right)
by using some other methods? Or are there no travelling wave solutions with the wave speed close enough to
\sqrt[4]{4b}
?

Moreover, obtained results can be interpreted so that for given
0<b<a
, there exist infinitely many travelling wave solutions of (
1
) with different wave speeds from the interval given in (
22
). Another open question concerns the number of travelling wave solutions with the same wave speed, their stability and interaction properties. Champneys and McKenna in
[
2
]
proved multiplicity in the case
a=1
,
b=0
for given wave speeds from certain nested intervals. As far as we know, the following questions for a general setting of parameters are not answered yet.

Q_{2}
:
For which fixed values of wave speed
c
from the admissible interval
do there exist at least two different travelling wave solutions?
Or for which fixed values of
c
the uniqueness result holds true?

Finding the appropriate answers can be quite difficult, but the following task should be more feasible.

T_{1}
:

For some fixed values of
a,b
and
c
, construct two different travelling wave solutions of (
1
).

Further, our description of the wave speed range is characterized by the envelope of
a collection of all Fučík spectra
\Sigma_{r}^{\texttt{D}}
of the Dirichlet boundary value
problems (
17
) with
r>0
.
The problem is that the description of Fučík spectra of the fourth-order operators are,
in general, unknown. Some partial results were obtained, e.g., by Krejčí in
[
10
]
,
however, the following question is left open.

Q_{3}
:
What is the analytical description of the Fučík spectrum
\Sigma_{r}^{\textup{{D}}}
for
r>0
? Or can we analytically describe their envelope?

In Section
5
,
we show that suitable polynomial and/or rational approximations of Dirichlet Fučík eigenfunctions
lead to explicit analytical approximations of the admissible wave speed range
given in (
22
)
(cf. Theorems
12
and
15
).
Moreover, in Section
6
, we show for which pairs

\left(4a/c^{4},4b/c^{4}\right)
there exists the travelling wave solution of
the problem (
1
) with the wave speed
c

(cf. Theorems
19
and
22
).
Let us point out that those pairs are below the curve
\mu
or below
\eta_{T}^{p_{1},p_{2}}

(see Figure
12
)
and that there is still a gap between
\eta_{T}^{p_{1},p_{2}}
and the border line
\mathscr{E}

under which
J(\alpha,\beta,v)
possesses negative
values for some
v\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
Thus, it is reasonable to formulate the following task.

T_{2}
:

Let
\alpha_{0}>1
be fixed and denote

\beta_{0}^{\bullet}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},x_{2}>0\\
p_{1},p_{2}>0\end{subarray}}\left\{\beta_{0}>0:\ \textup{the equation \eqref{rce2} is satisfied}\right\}.

For
\beta>\beta_{0}^{\bullet}
, construct
v\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that
J(\alpha_{0},\beta,v)<0
.

Unlike
\Sigma_{r}^{\texttt{D}}
, description of the Fučík spectrum
\Sigma_{T}^{\texttt{P}}

of the periodic problem (
49
) with
T>0
is much more straightforward.
Motivated by the fact that suprema of Dirichlet eigenvalues correspond to suprema
(or maxima, respectively) of periodic eigenvalues, we expect that the same holds
true also for the envelopes of Dirichlet and periodic Fučík spectra and that this
fact can be used for a better characterization of the optimal travelling wave speed range.
Unfortunately, this statement remains in the form of hypothesis and leads to the following question.

Q_{4}
:
Is it possible to confirm or refute Conjecture
26

Anisotropic Minkowski Content for Countably ℋ k \mathcal{H}^{k} -rectifiable Sets
Filip Fryš

Abstract
This paper investigates the existence of the anisotropic lower-dimensional Minkowski content. We establish that the C C -anisotropic k k -dimensional Minkowski content of a k k -rectifiable compact set always exists and coincides with a specific functional that depends naturally on C C . We further show that the same conclusion holds for countably ℋ k \mathcal{H}^{k} -rectifiable compact sets, provided that the so-called AFP-condition is satisfied. In addition, we discuss how the existence of the C C -anisotropic k k -dimensional Minkowski content for a countably ℋ k \mathcal{H}^{k} -rectifiable compact set depends on the choice of C C .

Introduction

This paper investigates anisotropic versions of lower-dimensional
Minkowski content
and establishes a generalization of Federer’s classical isotropic result for
k
-rectifiable compact sets
(
Fe
, Theorem 3.2.39)
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty compact set. For
r>0
, the
(isotropic) tubular
r
-neighborhood
of
S
is defined by

S_{r}\coloneq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\dist(x,S)\leq r\}.

Equivalently,
S_{r}
can be written as the Minkowski sum

S_{r}=S\oplus B(0,r)\coloneq\bigcup_{s\in S}B(s,r).

The volume
\lambda^{n}(S_{r})
and its asymptotic behavior as
r\to 0_{+}
have been studied extensively. To capture this behavior, one introduces the
k
-dimensional Minkowski content
of
S
,

\mathcal{M}^{k}_{B(0,1)}(S)\coloneq\lim_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}(S_{r})}{\omega_{n-k}r^{n-k}},

whenever the limit exists, where
\omega_{n-k}
denotes the volume of the
(n-k)
-dimensional Euclidean unit ball.

For certain classes of sets, the volume
\lambda^{n}(S_{r})
admits a polynomial expansion in
r
. If
S
is a convex body, then

\lambda^{n}(S_{r})=\sum_{j=0}^{n}{n\choose j}V_{n}\big(K[j],B(0,1)[n-j]\big)r^{n-j},\qquad r>0,

where the coefficients
V_{n}\big(K[j],B(0,1)[n-j]\big)
,
j\in\{0,\dots,n\}
, are the mixed volume of
j
copies of
K
and
n-j
copies of
B(0,1)
(see, for instance,
(
Sch
, Chapter 5)
).

Similarly, if
S
is a compact, smooth,
k
-dimensional manifold without boundary embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then there exist constants
c_{0},\dots,c_{n}\geq 0
such that

\lambda^{n}(S_{r})=\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_{j}r^{n-j}

for all sufficiently
r>0
(see
We
).

In both aforementioned cases, we get the existence of the above limit.

In a fundamental result, Federer
(
Fe
, Theorem 3.2.39)
proved that if
S
is compact and
k
-rectifiable, then
\mathcal{M}^{k}_{B(0,1)}(S)
exists and coincides with the
k
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}^{k}(S)
.

This result was later extended by Ambrosio, Fusco, and Pallara
(
AFP
, Theorem 2.104)
to countably
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-rectifiable compact sets. More precisely, if
S
is countably
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-rectifiable and compact, and there exist
\gamma>0
and a probability Radon measure
\mu
, absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{k}
, such that

\mu\big(B(x,r)\big)\geq\gamma r^{k},\qquad x\in S,\ r\in(0,1),

(1)

then the limit
\mathcal{M}^{k}_{B(0,1)}(S)
exists and equals
\mathcal{H}^{k}(S)
.

The aim of this work is to leave the isotropic framework and study an
anisotropic
counterpart of these results. Given a convex body
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, that is, a compact convex set whose relative interior contains the origin, we define the
C
-anisotropic tubular neighborhood
of
S
by

S\oplus rC\coloneq\bigcup_{s\in S}(s+rC),\qquad r>0.

Accordingly, the
k
-dimensional
C
-anisotropic Minkowski content
of
S
is defined as

\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)\coloneq\lim_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}(S\oplus rC)}{\omega_{n-k}r^{n-k}},

whenever the limit exists.

So far, the existence of
\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)
has been well understood only in the case
k=n-1
and for convex bodies
C
containing the origin in their interior. In this setting, Lussardi and Villa
(
LV
, Theorem 3.4)
proved that condition (
1
) guarantees the existence of
\mathcal{M}^{n-1}_{C}(S)
for countably
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-rectifiable compact sets
S
, and that

\mathcal{M}^{n-1}_{C}(S)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{S}\bigl(h_{C}(\nu_{S})+h_{C}(-\nu_{S})\bigr)\,\difm^{n-1},

where

h_{C}(x)\coloneq\max_{y\in C}x\cdot y

denotes the support function of
C
, and
\nu_{S}(x)
is a unit normal vector to the approximate tangent space of
S
at
x
for
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-almost every
x\in S
.

More recently, Kiderlen and Rataj
(
KR
, Theorem 8)
extended this result to lower-dimensional structuring elements. They introduced a condition analogous to (
1
): if
S
is a countably
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-rectifiable compact set with
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S)<\infty
,
L
is an
m
-dimensional linear subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and there exist
\gamma>0
and a probability Radon measure
\mu
, absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
, such that

\mu\big(B(x,r)\big)\geq\gamma r^{m-1}\mathcal{H}^{n-m}\Big(P_{L^{\perp}}\big(S\cap B(x,r)\big)\Big),\qquad x\in S,\ r\in(0,1),

(2)

then for any convex body
C\subseteq L
the limit
\mathcal{M}^{n-1}_{C}(S)
exists and admits the same integral representation. Here,
P_{L^{\perp}}
stands for the orthogonal projection onto the linear space
L^{\perp}
.

In this paper we show that, for every
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
and every convex body
C
, the
C
-anisotropic
k
-dimensional Minkowski content of a
k
-rectifiable compact set
S
exists and is given by

\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(P_{(\textup{T}^{k}_{x}S)^{\perp}}(C)\big)\difm^{k}(x),

(3)

where
\textup{T}^{k}_{x}S
denotes the
approximate tangent space to
S
at
x
.

We further introduce a condition extending (
1
) and (
2
) that ensures the existence of
\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)
for countably
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-rectifiable compact sets
S
with
\mathcal{H}^{k}(S)<\infty
, and arbitrary convex bodies
C
. Namely, we show that if there exist a Radon probability measure
\mu
, absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{k}
, and a constant
\gamma>0
such that

\mu\big(B(x,r)\big)\geq\gamma r^{k+m-n}\mathcal{H}^{n-m}\Big(P_{L^{\perp}}\big(S\cap B(x,r)\big)\Big),\quad\text{$x\in S$, $r\in(0,1)$,}

then the limit
\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)
exists and coincides with the integral

\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(P_{(\textup{T}^{k}_{x}S)^{\perp}}(C)\big)\difm^{k}(x).

Finally, we show that if (
3
) holds for some convex body with nonempty interior, then it holds for all convex bodies with nonempty interior.

The rest of the paper unfolds as follows.

Section
1
introduces the notation used throughout and gathers the essential background from geometric measure theory and convex geometry. In particular, we recall the Besicovitch and Vitali covering theorems, together with the basic properties of convex bodies.

In Section
2
, we define the
s
-dimensional anisotropic Minkowski content. We first establish the result for convex bodies, which serves as a guiding example, and then extend it to sets that arise as compact subsets of
C^{1}

k
-graphs.

Section
3
addresses the general rectifiable case. Here we show that the
k
-dimensional
C
-anisotropic Minkowski content of a countably
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-rectifiable compact set exists and attains the expected value, provided an appropriate form of the AFP-condition is satisfied.

Finally, in Section
4
, we prove that if the
k
-dimensional
C
-anisotropic Minkowski content of a countably
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-rectifiable compact set
S
exists and takes the correct value for one full-dimensional convex body
C
, then the same conclusion holds for every full-dimensional convex body.

1.2
Geometric Measure Theory

In this subsection, we formulate some useful results from geometric measure theory. Let us start with the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy
1\leq k\leq n
,
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

g\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
be Lipschitz. We define (almost everywhere) the

\displaystyle J_{k}f(x)\coloneq\sqrt{\det\big([\nabla f(x)][\nabla f(x)]^{\top}\big)}

\displaystyle J^{k}g(y)\coloneq\sqrt{\det\big([\nabla g(y)]^{\top}[\nabla g(y)]\big)}.

Theorem 1.2

(Area Formula,
(
Fe
, Theorem 3.2.3)
)
.

Let

\mathcal{H}^{k}
-measurable, where

. Then the map
y\mapsto\mathcal{H}^{0}\big(f^{-1}(\{y\})\cap E\big)
is
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-measurable and

\int\limits_{E}J_{k}f(x)\difm^{k}(x)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{H}^{0}\big(f^{-1}(\{y\})\cap E\big)\difm^{k}(y).

Theorem 1.3

(Coarea Formula,
(
Fe
, Theorem 3.2.11)
)
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
be Lipschitz and

be
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-measurable, where

. Then the map

-measurable and

\int\limits_{E}J^{k}f(x)\difm^{n}(x)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(f^{-1}(\{y\})\cap E\big)\difm^{k}(y).

Definition 1.4
.

Let

with

and

\mathcal{H}^{k}
-measurable.

The set

The set

In fact, the definition of

Theorem 1.5

(
(

)

If

-rectifiable set and

We will need the following coarea formula for orthogonal projections.

Remark 1.6

(
(

1.3
Convex Bodies

In this subsection, we will summarize the basic facts about convex bodies and their properties. For a more detailed exposition, we refer the reader to
Sch
.

Definition 1.14
.

A
convex body
is a non-empty, compact and convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
0\in\relint(C)
. The set of all convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{C}^{n}
. The
dimension of
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}
, denoted by
\dim(C)
, is defined as the dimension of the affine span of
C
.

Further, we set

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{n,k}\coloneq\{C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}:\dim(C)=k\},\qquad k\in\{0,\dots,n\}.

We emphasize that the nonstandard assumption
0\in\relint(C)
is not restrictive when studying the volume
\lambda^{n}(S\oplus rC)
, since the Lebesgue measure is translation invariant.

Definition 1.15
.

Let
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}
. The
support function of
C
is given by the formula

h_{C}(y)\coloneq\sup_{x\in C}x\cdot y,\qquad y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

The
radial function of
C
is defined as

\rho_{C}(x)\coloneq\max\{t\geq 0:tx\in C\},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Remark 1.16
.

Let
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}
.

•

The function
x\mapsto h_{C}(x)
is sublinear, hence convex, and Lipschitz with the property
h_{-C}(x)=h_{C}(-x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It is also true that
h_{aC}=ah_{C}
for any
a>0
.

•

We have
\rho_{C}(x)x\in C
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which in turn implies that
\rho_{C}(x)\leq\diam(C)
for any
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Definition 1.17
.

The mapping
d_{H}\colon\mathcal{C}^{n}\times\mathcal{C}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
defined as

d_{H}(C,K)\coloneq\max\big\{\sup_{x\in C}\dist(x,K),\sup_{y\in K}\dist(y,C)\big\},\qquad(C,K)\in\mathcal{C}^{n}\times\mathcal{C}^{n},

is called the Hausdorff metric. The set
\mathcal{C}^{n}
endowed with the mapping

is a metric space.

Remark 1.18
.

For any couple

, it holds that

d_{H}(C,K)=\|h_{C}-h_{K}\|_{\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}=\inf\{t\geq 0:C\subseteq K\oplus B(0,t),\,K\subseteq C\oplus B(0,t)\}.

Let us define the so-called mixed volumes (see

Definition 1.19

Let

can be expressed as follows:

\lambda^{n}(K\oplus rC)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}V_{n}\bigl(K[i],\,C[n-i]\bigr)\,r^{\,n-i},\qquad r>0,

where the coefficient

is called the

and

Remark 1.20
.

If

and

Definition 2.2

Lemma 2.3
.

Let

be a countably

-rectifiable set, where

and let

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\difm^{k}(x)=\int\limits_{S}\fint\limits_{\textup{n}^{n-k}_{x}S}\rho_{C_{x}}(\nu)^{n-k}\difm^{n-k-1}(\nu)\difm^{k}(x).\end{split}

Proof.

We have

\begin{split}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}&(C_{x})\difm^{k}(x)\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{Eq0})}}}}{{=}}}\frac{1}{n-k}\int\limits_{S}\int\limits_{\textup{n}^{n-k}_{x}S}\rho_{C_{x}}(\nu)^{n-k}\difm^{n-k-1}(\nu)\difm^{k}(x)\\

which completes the proof.

We now prove that
\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(K)
coincides with (
7
) for all convex bodies
K
with

.
It suffices to establish the statement for
K\in\mathcal{C}^{n,k}
, since otherwise both
\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(K)
and the expression in (

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a convex body of dimension
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
, and
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(K)=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{K}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\difm^{k}(x).

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
, denote
L\coloneq\LO(K)
and define

\displaystyle K_{\varepsilon}\coloneq\{x\in K:\dist(x,\partial_{L}K)>\varepsilon\}

and

\displaystyle K^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\coloneq K\oplus B_{L}(0,\varepsilon).

First, notice that by the Fubini theorem, we have

\lambda^{n}(K\oplus rC)=\int\limits_{L}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big((K\oplus rC)\cap(x+L^{\perp})\big)\difm^{k}(x),\qquad r>0.

For
x\in L
, we have

(K\oplus rC)\cap(x+L^{\perp})=\big\{x+rP_{L^{\perp}}(c):c\in C,\,x-rP_{L}(c)\in K\big\}.

Moreover, if
x\in K_{\varepsilon}
and
r>0
is small enough (depending only on
\varepsilon
), then
x-rP_{L}(c)\in K,
and hence

(K\oplus rC)\cap(x+L^{\perp})=x+rP_{L^{\perp}}(C),

from which we conclude that for all sufficiently small
r>0

\begin{split}\lambda^{n}(K\oplus rC)&\geq\int\limits_{K_{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big((K\oplus rC)\cap(x+L^{\perp})\big)\difm^{k}(x)\\
&=\int\limits_{K_{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(P_{L^{\perp}}(rC)\big)\difm^{k}\\
&=r^{n-k}\int\limits_{K_{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(P_{L^{\perp}}(C)\big)\difm^{k},\end{split}

whence

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(K)_{*}\geq\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\mathcal{H}^{k}(K_{\varepsilon})\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(P_{L^{\perp}}(C)\big),\qquad\varepsilon>0,\end{split}

from which it follows

\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(K)_{*}\geq\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\mathcal{H}^{k}(K)\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(P_{L^{\perp}}(C)\big)=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{K}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\difm^{k}(x),

as
L=\textup{T}^{k}_{x}K
for
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-a.a.
x\in K.

If
x\notin K_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
and
r>0
is small enough, then
\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big((K\oplus rC)\cap(x+L^{\perp})\big)=0
. So, for small enough
r>0
, it holds that

\begin{split}\lambda^{n}(K\oplus rC)&\leq\int\limits_{K_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big((K\oplus rC)\cap(x+L^{\perp})\big)\difm^{k}(x)\\
&\leq\int\limits_{K_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(P_{L^{\perp}}(rC)\big)\difm^{k}\\
&=r^{n-k}\int\limits_{K^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(P_{L^{\perp}}(C)\big)\difm^{k},\end{split}

where we used that

(K\oplus rC)\cap(x+L^{\perp})\subseteq x+rP_{L^{\perp}}(C).

Hence

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(K)^{*}\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\mathcal{H}^{k}(K^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(P_{L^{\perp}}(C)\big),\qquad\varepsilon>0,\end{split}

from which it follows

\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(K)^{*}\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\mathcal{H}^{k}(K)\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(P_{L^{\perp}}(C)\big)=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{K}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\difm^{k}(x),

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

The mapping
\Psi_{L}\colon\mathcal{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\Psi_{L}\colon C\mapsto\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(P_{L^{\perp}}(C)\big)

is continuous uniformly with respect to
L\in G_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

In particular, the mapping
\Phi_{S}\colon\mathcal{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
given by

\Phi_{S}\colon C\mapsto\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\difm^{k}(x)

is continuous for every countably
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-rectifiable set
S
of finite
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-measure.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. If
K,C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}
with
d_{H}(C,K)<\varepsilon,
it holds that
C\subseteq K\oplus B(0,\varepsilon)
and
K\subseteq C\oplus B(0,\varepsilon),
from which we have

\begin{split}\Psi_{L}(K)-\Psi_{L}(C)&\leq\Psi_{L}\big(C\oplus B(0,\varepsilon)\big)-\Psi_{L}(C)\\
&=\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\Big(P_{L^{\perp}}(C)\oplus\varepsilon P_{L^{\perp}}\big(B(0,1)\big)\Big)-\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(P_{L^{\perp}}(C)\big)\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{n-k-1}{n-k\choose j}\varepsilon^{n-k-j}V_{n-k}\big(P_{L^{\perp}}(C)[j],B_{L^{\perp}}(0,1)[n-k-j]\big)\\
&\leq\varepsilon\sum_{j=0}^{n-k-1}{n-k\choose j}\diam(C)^{j}V_{n-k}\big(B_{L^{\perp}}(0,1)[j],B_{L^{\perp}}(0,1)[n-k-j]\big)\\
&=\varepsilon\sum_{j=0}^{n-k-1}{n-k\choose j}\diam(C)^{j}\omega_{n-k}.\end{split}

From symmetry, we obtain

\begin{split}|\Psi_{L}(K)-\Psi_{L}(C)|\leq\varepsilon\sum_{j=0}^{n-k-1}{n-k\choose j}\big(\diam(C)+\diam(K)\big)^{j}\omega_{n-k},\end{split}

from which the first conclusion follows.

Now, if
S
is a countably
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-rectifiable set of finite
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-measure, then under the same assumptions before

\begin{split}\left|\Phi_{S}(K)-\Phi_{S}(C)\right|&\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}|\Psi_{\textup{T}^{k}_{x}S}(K)-\Psi_{\textup{T}^{k}_{x}S}(C)|\difm^{k}(x)\\
&\leq\varepsilon\mathcal{H}^{k}(S)\sum_{j=0}^{n-k-1}{n-k\choose j}\big(\diam(C)+\diam(K)\big)^{j},\end{split}

from which the second conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
G\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k}
be open,
K\subseteq G
be a nonempty compact set, and let
f\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(G;\mathbb{R}^{n-k})
. Define

g(x)\coloneq\big(x,f(x)\big),\qquad x\in G.

Then the mapping

w\mapsto\textup{N}^{n-k}_{w}g(K)

is continuous from
g(K)
to
G_{n-k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

In particular, the mapping

w\mapsto\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{w}g(K)}(C)\big)

is continuous from
g(K)
to
\mathbb{R}
, where
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n,n}
.

Proof.

Since
\rank\big(Dg(x)\big)=k
for any
x\in G
, we have

P_{\textup{T}^{k}_{g(x)}g(G)}=Dg(x)\big(Dg(x)^{\top}Dg(x)\big)^{-1}Dg(x)^{\top},\qquad x\in G.

Consequently, for any
x,y\in K

\begin{split}P_{\textup{T}^{k}_{g(x)}g(G)}-P_{\textup{T}^{k}_{g(y)}g(G)}&=\big(Dg(x)-Dg(y)\big)\big(Dg(x)^{\top}Dg(x)\big)^{-1}Dg(x)^{\top}\\
&\quad\quad+Dg(y)\Big(\big(Dg(x)^{\top}Dg(x)\big)^{-1}-\big(Dg(y)^{\top}Dg(y)\big)^{-1}\Big)Dg(x)^{\top}\\
&\quad\quad+Dg(y)\big(Dg(y)^{\top}Dg(y)\big)^{-1}\big(Dg(x)-Dg(y)\big)^{\top}.\end{split}

We see that

Dg(x)^{\top}Dg(x)=I_{k}+Df(x)^{\top}Df(x),

from which we conclude that all eigenvalues of
Dg(x)^{\top}Dg(x)
are bounded from bellow by
1
. Thus the eigenvalues of
\big(Dg(x)^{\top}Dg(x)\big)^{-1}
are bounded from above by
1
, which in turn implies

\left\|\big(Dg(x)^{\top}Dg(x)\big)^{-1}\right\|\leq 1.

Using this and
M\coloneq\sup_{x\in K}\|Dg(x)\|<\infty
, we conclude that

\begin{split}\|P_{\textup{N}^{k}_{g(x)}g(G)}-P_{\textup{N}^{k}_{g(y)}g(G)}\|&=\|P_{\textup{T}^{k}_{g(x)}g(G)}-P_{\textup{T}^{k}_{g(y)}g(G)}\|\\
&\leq 2M\|Dg(x)-Dg(y)\|\\
&\quad\quad+M^{2}\left\|\big(Dg(x)^{\top}Dg(x)\big)^{-1}-\big(Dg(y)^{\top}Dg(y)\big)^{-1}\right\|\\
&\leq 2M\|Dg(x)-Dg(y)\|\\
&\quad\quad+M^{2}\left\|Dg(y)^{\top}Dg(y)-Dg(x)^{\top}Dg(x)\right\|\\
&\leq(2M+2M^{3})\|Dg(x)-Dg(y)\|,\end{split}

from which the first conclusion follows.

Now, let
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n,n}
. It suffices to show that the mapping

L\mapsto\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(P_{L}(C)\big)

is continuous from
G_{n-k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
\mathbb{R}
. To this end, fix
L,W\in G_{n-k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For an arbitrary pair
(c_{1},c_{2})\in C\times C

\begin{split}|P_{W}(c_{1})-P_{L}(c_{2})|&\leq|P_{W}(c_{1})-P_{W}(c_{2})|+|P_{W}(c_{2})-P_{L}(c_{2})|\\
&\leq|c_{1}-c_{2}|+\diam(C)\|P_{W}-P_{L}\|,\end{split}

which yields

d_{H}\big(P_{W}(C),P_{L}(C)\big)\leq\diam(C)\|P_{W}-P_{L}\|.

Using this, we see that if
L_{i}\xrightarrow{i\to\infty}L
in
G_{n-k}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
then
P_{L_{i}}(C)\xrightarrow{i\to\infty}P_{L}(C)
in
\mathcal{C}^{n}
, which in turn implies
\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(P_{L_{i}}(C)\big)\xrightarrow{i\to\infty}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(P_{L}(C)\big).

\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\coloneq\big\{Q\big(w,s_{g(w)}\big):w\in K(1)^{\prime}\big\}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
such that

\lambda^{k}\Big(K(1)\setminus\bigcup\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\Big)=0.

(10)

Consequently, as

\lambda^{k}\Big(\bigcup\mathcal{V}\Big)\leq\lambda^{k}(K)<\infty,

for any
\varepsilon>0
, we can find some finite index set
J_{\varepsilon}\subseteq K(1)^{\prime}
such that

\lambda^{k}\Big(\bigcup\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\setminus\bigcup_{w\in J_{\varepsilon}}Q\big(w,s_{g(w)}\big)\Big)<\varepsilon,

(11)

whence

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{k}\Big(S\setminus\bigcup_{w\in J_{\varepsilon}}S_{g(w),s_{g(w)}}\Big)&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{Eq12})}}}}{{\leq}}}\mathcal{H}^{k}\Big(S(1)\setminus\bigcup_{w\in J_{\varepsilon}}S_{g(w),s_{g(w)}}\Big)\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{Eq13})}}}}{{\leq}}}\textup{lip}(g)^{k}\lambda^{k}\Big(\bigcup\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\setminus\bigcup_{w\in J_{\varepsilon}}Q\big(w,s_{g(w)}\big)\Big)\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{Eq14})}}}}{{\leq}}}\textup{lip}(g)^{k}\varepsilon.\end{split}

(12)

If (
9
) holds true, then

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)_{*}&\geq\sum_{w\in J_{\varepsilon}}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S_{g(w),s_{g(w)}})_{*}\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{Eq11})}}}}{{\geq}}}\sum_{i\in J_{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S_{g(w),s_{g(w)}}}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\difm^{k}(y)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{y})\difm^{k}(y)-\diam(C)^{n-k}\mathcal{H}^{k}\Big(S\setminus\bigcup_{w\in J_{\varepsilon}}S_{g(w),s_{g(w)}}\Big)\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{Eq15})}}}}{{\geq}}}\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{y})\difm^{k}(y)-\diam(C)^{n-k}\textup{lip}(g)^{k}\varepsilon,\end{split}

from which the conclusion follows.

Now, we prove (
9
). To this end, let
x\in S(1)
. Observe that the point
x
lies in the relative interior of
S
with respect to the set
g(G).

Put
y\coloneq g^{-1}(x)
and pick some
s>0
such that
Q(y,s)\subseteq K(1)
. Further, let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that

\begin{split}\varepsilon(1+\sqrt{n}\varepsilon\alpha^{-1})\diam(C)<\frac{1}{2}.\end{split}

(13)

There exists some
\delta>0
such that for all
w\in Q(y,s)
and all
z\in B(w,\delta)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{g(w)})-\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{g(z)})|\leq\varepsilon,

\displaystyle|J_{k}g(w)-J_{k}g(z)|\leq\varepsilon,

(14)

and

\displaystyle|g(z)-g(w)-Dg(w)(z-w)|\leq\varepsilon^{2}|z-w|.

(15)

Let
0<r<\min\{1,\delta\sqrt{n}^{-1}\varepsilon\}
, and let
\mathcal{C}
be a set of pairwise disjoint closed cubes of side length
\varepsilon^{-1}r
which are contained in
Q(y,s)
such that their number is the maximal possible, that is,
\mathcal{C}
has exactly

\left\lfloor\frac{2s}{\varepsilon^{-1}r}\right\rfloor^{k}

(16)

members. Consequently,

\begin{split}\lambda^{k}\Big(Q(y,s)\setminus\bigcup\mathcal{C}\Big)&=(2s)^{k}-\left\lfloor\frac{2s}{\varepsilon^{-1}r}\right\rfloor^{k}(\varepsilon^{-1}r)^{k}\\
&\leq(2s)^{k}-\left(\frac{2s}{\varepsilon^{-1}r}-1\right)^{k}(\varepsilon^{-1}r)^{k}\\
&=(2s)^{k}-(2s-\varepsilon^{-1}r)^{k}.\end{split}

(17)

For any
Q\in\mathcal{C}
, denote by
z_{Q}
its center and define

g_{Q}(w)\coloneq g(z_{Q})+Dg(z_{Q})(w-z_{Q}),\qquad w\in\mathbb{R}^{k}.

Notice that at each point
w\in G
, we have
\rank Dg(w)=k
, from which we deduce that
g_{Q}
is injective for each
Q\in\mathcal{C}.

Note that if
z\in g_{Q}(Q)\oplus rC
, then there exists some
w\in Q
such that
z-g_{Q}(w)\in rC
. Since
\diam(Q)\leq\sqrt{n}\varepsilon^{-1}r,
(
15
) ensures that

|g(w)-g_{Q}(w)|\leq\sqrt{n}\varepsilon r.

Thus

z-g(w)\in rC\oplus B(0,\sqrt{n}\varepsilon r)\subseteq(1+\eta)rC

with
\eta\coloneq\sqrt{n}\varepsilon\alpha^{-1}
. It follows

\lambda^{n}\big(g_{Q}(Q)\oplus rC\big)\leq\lambda^{n}\big(g(Q)\oplus(1+\eta)rC\big).

(18)

Observe that

J_{k}g_{Q}(w)=J_{k}g(z_{Q}),\qquad w\in G,

(19)

and put
M\coloneq\sup_{w\in Q(y,s)}|J_{k}g(w)|
. As the set
g_{Q}(Q)
is a convex body of dimension
k
, Lemma
2.4
together with area formula gives us

\begin{split}\omega_{n-k}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{r,C}\big(g_{Q}(Q)\big)&\geq\frac{1}{r^{n-k}}{n\choose k}V_{n}\big(g_{Q}(Q)[k],(rC)[n-k]\big)\\
&=\int\limits_{g_{Q}(Q)}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{u})\difm^{k}(u)\\
&=\int\limits_{Q}J_{k}g_{Q}(v)\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{g_{Q}(v)})\difm^{k}(v)\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{Eq50})}}}}{{=}}}\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{Q}J_{k}g_{Q}(z_{Q})\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{g(z_{Q})})\difm^{k}(v)\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{Eq29})}}}}{{\geq}}}\int\limits_{Q}\big(J_{k}g(v)-\varepsilon\big)\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{g(z_{Q})})\difm^{k}(v)\\
&\geq\int\limits_{Q}J_{k}g(v)\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{g(z_{Q})})\difm^{k}(v)-\varepsilon\diam(C)^{n-k}\lambda^{k}(Q)\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{Eq29})}}}}{{\geq}}}\int\limits_{Q}J_{k}g(v)\big(\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{g(v)})-\varepsilon\big)\difm^{k}(v)-\varepsilon\diam(C)^{n-k}\lambda^{k}(Q)\\
&\geq\int\limits_{Q}J_{k}g(v)\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{g(v)})\difm^{k}(v)-\varepsilon\lambda^{k}(Q)\big(\diam(C)^{n-k}+M\big),\end{split}

which gives us

\begin{split}\omega_{n-k}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}&\mathcal{M}^{k}_{r,C}\big(g_{Q}(Q)\big)\\
&\geq\int\limits_{\bigcup\mathcal{C}}J_{k}g(v)\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{g(v)})\difm^{k}(v)-\varepsilon\lambda^{k}\Big(\bigcup\mathcal{C}\Big)\big(\diam(C)^{n-k}+M\big)\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{Eq26})}}}}{{\geq}}}\int\limits_{Q(y,s)}J_{k}g(v)\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{g(v)})\difm^{k}(v)-\varepsilon\lambda^{k}\big(Q(y,s)\big)\big(\diam(C)^{n-k}+M\big)\\
&\quad\quad-\big((2s)^{k}-(2s-\varepsilon^{-1}r)^{k}\big)M\diam(C)^{n-k}.\end{split}

(20)

We would like to estimate from above the quantity

\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{r,C}\big(g_{Q}(Q)\big)

by
\mathcal{M}^{k}_{r,C}(S_{x,s})
plus something small. To do so, we have to show that the measure of overlaps is properly controlled. To this end, set

F\coloneq\bigcup_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\partial Q.

It holds that

\begin{split}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\chi_{g(Q)\oplus(1+\eta)rC}\leq\chi_{S_{x,s}\oplus(1+\eta)rC}+2^{k}\chi_{g(F)\oplus(1+\eta)r\diam(C)B(0,1)}.\end{split}

(21)

Indeed, if we have
\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\chi_{g(Q)\oplus(1+\eta)rC}(w)>1
for some point
w\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, then it holds that
w\in g(F)\oplus r\diam(C)(1+\eta)B(0,1),
from which it follows that
\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\chi_{g(Q)\oplus(1+\eta)rC}(w)\leq 2^{k}
since (
13
) holds.

Now

\begin{split}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}&\frac{\lambda^{n}\big(g_{Q}(Q)\oplus rC\big)}{\omega_{n-k}r^{n-k}}\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{Eq20})+(\ref{Eq19})}}}}{{\leq}}}\;\;\;\;\frac{\lambda^{n}\big(S_{x,s}\oplus(1+\eta)rC\big)}{\omega_{n-k}r^{n-k}}+2^{k}\frac{\lambda^{n}\big(g(F)\oplus(1+\eta)r\diam(C)B(0,1)\big)}{\omega_{n-k}r^{n-k}}.\end{split}

(22)

We need to reasonably estimate the last term on the right hand side of (
22
).

Similarly as in (
18
), we have

g(\partial Q)\subseteq g_{Q}(\partial Q)\oplus\eta\diam(C)B(0,r),\qquad Q\in\mathcal{C},

whence

g(F)\oplus(1+\eta)\diam(C)\subseteq\bigcup_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}g_{Q}(\partial Q)\oplus(1+2\eta)\diam(C)B(0,r).

(23)

Let
Q\in\mathcal{C}
. The cube
Q
has exactly
2k
faces of dimension
k-1
, say
F_{1}^{Q},\dots,F_{2k}^{Q}
, and

\diam\big(g_{Q}(F_{l}^{Q})\big)\leq\textup{lip}(g)\sqrt{k-1}\varepsilon^{-1}r,\qquad l\in\{1,\dots,2k\}.

(24)

\begin{split}\lambda^{n}\big(g_{Q}(\partial Q)&\oplus(1+2\eta)r\diam(C)B(0,1)\big)\\
&\leq\sum_{l=1}^{2k}\lambda^{n}\big(g_{Q}(F_{l}^{Q})\oplus(1+2\eta)r\diam(C)B(0,1)\big)\\
&=\sum_{l=1}^{2k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}{n\choose j}V_{n}\big(g_{Q}(F_{l}^{Q})[j],B(0,1)[n-j]\big)\big((1+2\eta)\diam(C)r\big)^{n-j}\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{Eq33})}}}}{{\leq}}}r^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{2k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}{n\choose j}\omega_{n}\textup{lip}(g)^{j}\sqrt{k-1}^{j}(\varepsilon^{-1}r)^{j}\big((1+2\eta)\diam(C)r\big)^{n-j}\\
&\leq r^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{2k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}{n\choose j}\omega_{n}\textup{lip}(g)^{j}\sqrt{k-1}^{j}\varepsilon^{-j}\big((1+2\eta)\diam(C)\big)^{n-j}\end{split}

(25)

Since
\varepsilon<1
, we can estimate

\begin{split}\lambda^{n}\big(g(F)&\oplus(1+\eta)r\diam(C)B(0,1)\big)\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{Eq35})}}}}{{\leq}}}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\lambda^{n}\big(g_{Q}(\partial Q)\oplus(1+2\eta)r\diam(C)B(0,1)\big)\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{Eq23})}}}}{{\leq}}}r^{n}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\sum_{l=1}^{2k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}{n\choose j}\omega_{n}\textup{lip}(g)^{j}\sqrt{k-1}^{j}\varepsilon^{-j}\big((1+2\eta)\diam(C)\big)^{n-j}\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{Eq22})}}}}{{\leq}}}\frac{(2s\varepsilon)^{k}}{r^{k}}r^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{2k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}{n\choose j}\omega_{n}\textup{lip}(g)^{j}\sqrt{k-1}^{j}\varepsilon^{-j}\big((1+2\eta)\diam(C)\big)^{n-j}\\
&=2^{k}s^{k}r^{n-k}\sum_{l=1}^{2k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}{n\choose j}\omega_{n}\textup{lip}(g)^{j}\sqrt{k-1}^{j}\varepsilon^{k-j}\big((1+2\eta)\diam(C)\big)^{j}\\
&\leq 2^{k}s^{k}r^{n-k}\varepsilon\sum_{l=1}^{2k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}{n\choose j}\omega_{n}\textup{lip}(g)^{j}\sqrt{k-1}^{j}\big((1+2\sqrt{n}\alpha^{-1})\diam(C)\big)^{j}\\
&=r^{n-k}\varepsilon\eta^{\prime},\end{split}

(26)

where

\eta^{\prime}\coloneq 2^{k}s^{k}\sum_{l=1}^{2k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}{n\choose j}\omega_{n}\textup{lip}(g)^{j}\sqrt{k-1}^{j}\big((1+2\sqrt{n}\alpha^{-1})\diam(C)\big)^{j}.

Together, we have

\begin{split}(1+\eta)^{n-k}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{r(1+\eta),C}(S_{x,s})\;\;\;&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{Eq21})+(\ref{Eq25})}}}}{{\geq}}}\;\;\;\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{r,C}\big(g_{Q}(Q)\big)-\frac{2^{k}\eta^{\prime}}{\omega_{n-k}}\varepsilon\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{Eq27})}}}}{{\geq}}}\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{Q(y,s)}J_{k}g(v)\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{g(v)})\difm^{k}(v)\\
&\quad\quad-\frac{\varepsilon}{\omega_{n-k}}\lambda^{k}\big(Q(y,s)\big)\big(\diam(C)^{n-k}+M\big)\\
&\quad\quad-\big((2s)^{k}-(2s-\varepsilon^{-1}r)^{k}\big)\frac{M}{\omega_{n-k}}\diam(C)^{n-k}-\frac{2^{k}\eta^{\prime}}{\omega_{n-k}}\varepsilon,\end{split}

which in turn implies

\begin{split}(1+\eta)^{n-k}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S_{x,s})_{*}&\geq\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{Q(y,s)}J_{k}g(v)\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{g(v)})\difm^{k}(v)-\frac{2^{k}\eta^{\prime}}{\omega_{n-k}}\varepsilon\\
&\quad\quad-\frac{\varepsilon}{\omega_{n-k}}\lambda^{k}\big(Q(y,s)\big)\big(\diam(C)^{n-k}+M\big).\end{split}

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we conclude that

\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S_{x,s})_{*}\geq\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{Q(y,s)}J_{k}g(v)\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{g(v)})\difm^{k}(v).

Finally, area formula yields

\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S_{x,s})_{*}\geq\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S_{x,s}}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{w})\difm^{k}(w),

which completes the proof.
∎

The following result provides an upper bound for the upper

k
-dimensional anisotropic Minkowski content of a compact
\mathcal{C}^{1}

k
-graph. Our argument is inspired by
(
Fe
, Lemma 3.2.38)
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}
, and let
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact subset of a
\mathcal{C}^{1}

k
-graph, where
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)^{*}\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\difm^{k}(x).

Proof.

Let
G\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k}
be open, let
h\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(G;\mathbb{R}^{n-k})
, and let
K\subseteq G
be a compact set such that

\big\{\big(x,h(x)\big):x\in K\}=S.

For the sake of brevity, set

f(x)\coloneq\big(x,h(x)\big),\quad x\in G.

Hence
f(K)=S
and
f
is bi-Lipschitz.

Since

\mathcal{H}^{k}(S)=\mathcal{H}^{k}\big(f(K)\big)\leq\textup{lip}(f)^{k}\lambda^{k}(K)<\infty,

it suffices to show the conclusion for all convex bodies in
\mathcal{C}^{n,n}
due to Lemma
2.5
. So, let
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n,n}
. Find positive real numbers
\alpha
and
\beta
such that

B(0,\alpha)\subseteq C\subseteq B(0,\beta).

For every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists some
\delta>0
such that
K\oplus B_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}(0,\delta)\subseteq G
and

\lambda^{k}\Big(\big(K\oplus B_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}(0,\delta)\big)\setminus K\Big)<\frac{1}{m}

(27)

and for every
a\in K
and every
x\in B(a,\delta)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{f(x)})-\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{f(a)})|<\frac{1}{m},

\displaystyle|J_{k}{f(x)}-J_{k}{f(a)}|<\frac{1}{m}

(28)

and

\displaystyle|f(x)-f(a)-Df(x)(x-a)|\leq\frac{|x-a|^{2}}{m}.

(29)

Let
r\in(0,\min\{1,\sqrt{n}^{-1}\delta m\})
and consider a tessellation of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
into
cubes of side lengths
mr
, and let
\mathcal{C}
be the set of cubes from the tessellation
hitting
K
. Then
K\subseteq\bigcup\mathcal{C}
, and, taking (
27
) into account,

\displaystyle\lambda^{k}\bigg(\Big(\bigcup\mathcal{C}\Big)\setminus K\bigg)<\frac{1}{m}.

(30)

For any cube
Q\in\mathcal{C}
, pick a point
a\in Q\cap K
and consider the affine map
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by

g(z)\coloneq g(a)+Df(a)(z-a),\quad\text{$z\in\mathbb{R}^{k}$.}

Observe that since
\rank Df(a)=k
, the function
g
is injective.

Note that if
z\in f(Q)\oplus rC
, then there exists some
y\in Q
such that
z-f(y)\in rC
. Since
\diam(Q)\leq\sqrt{n}mr,
(
29
) ensures that

|f(y)-g(y)|\leq\frac{\sqrt{n}r}{m}.

Thus

z-g(y)\in rC\oplus B\Big(0,\frac{\sqrt{n}r}{m}\Big)\subseteq(1+\eta)rC

(31)

with
\eta\coloneq\frac{\sqrt{n}}{m\alpha}
. It follows that

\lambda^{n}\big(f(Q)\oplus rC\big)\leq\lambda^{n}\big(g(Q)\oplus(1+\eta)rC\big).

(32)

Since
\rank Df(x)=k
at each point
x\in G
, the set
g(Q)
is a convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of dimension exactly
k
.

Using the expansion formula for the sum of two convex bodies, Lemma
2.4
and the fact that
\diam\big(g(Q)\big)\leq\textup{lip}(f)\diam(Q)=\textup{lip}(f)mr\sqrt{k}
,
\lambda^{k}(Q)=(mr)^{k}
and
r\leq 1
, we conclude

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{r,C}\big(f(Q)\big)&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{Eq5})}}}}{{\leq}}}(1+\eta)^{n-k}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{(1+\eta)r,C}\big(g(Q)\big)\\
&=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}r^{n-k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}{n\choose j}V\big(g(Q)[j],C[n-j]\big)r^{n-j}(1+\eta)^{n-j}\\
&=\frac{(1+\eta)^{n-k}}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{g(Q)}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\difm^{k}(x)\\
&\quad\quad+\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}{n\choose j}V\big(g(Q)[j],C[n-j]\big)r^{n-j}(1+\eta)^{n-j}\\
&\leq\frac{(1+\eta)^{n-k}}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{g(Q)}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{f(a)})\difm^{k}(x)\\
&\quad\quad+\frac{L}{m\omega_{n-k}}(1+\eta)^{n}\lambda^{k}(Q),\end{split}

(33)

where

L\coloneq\omega_{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}{n\choose j}\textup{lip}(f)^{j}\sqrt{k}^{j}\beta^{n-j}.

Area formula and injectivity of
g
imply

\begin{split}\int\limits_{g(Q)}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{f(a)})\difm^{k}(x)=\int\limits_{Q}J_{k}g(y)\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{f(a)})\difm^{k}(y).\end{split}

For every
x\in Q
, it holds that

J_{k}g(x)=J_{k}g(a)=\sqrt{\det\big(Df(a)^{\top}Df(a)\big)}=J_{k}f(a),

which gives us

\begin{split}\int\limits_{Q}J_{k}g(y)\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{f(a)})\difm^{k}(y)&=\int\limits_{Q}J_{k}f(a)\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{f(a)})\difm^{k}(x)\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{eq00})}}}}{{\leq}}}\int\limits_{Q}\big(J_{k}f(x)+\frac{1}{m}\big)\big(\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{f(x)})+\frac{1}{m}\big)\difm^{k}(x)\\
&\leq\int\limits_{Q}J_{k}f(x)\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{f(x)})\difm^{k}(x)\\
&\quad\quad+\frac{\diam(C)^{n-k}+M+1}{m}\lambda^{k}(Q),\end{split}

(34)

where

M\coloneq\sup\big\{|J_{k}f(x)|:x\in K\oplus B_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}(0,\delta)\big\}.

Altogether, we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{r,C}(S)&\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{r,C}\big(f(Q)\big)\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{Eq1})}}}}{{\leq}}}\frac{(1+\eta)^{n-k}}{\omega_{n-k}}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{C}}\int\limits_{g(Q)}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{f(a)})\difm^{k}(x)+\frac{L}{m\omega_{n-k}}(1+\eta)^{n}\lambda^{k}\Big(\bigcup\mathcal{C}\Big)\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{Eq2})}}}}{{\leq}}}\frac{(1+\eta)^{n-k}}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{\bigcup\mathcal{C}}J_{k}f(x)\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{f(x)})\difm^{k}(x)\\
&\quad\quad+\frac{\diam(C)^{n-k}+M+1+L(1+\eta)^{n}}{m\omega_{n-k}}\lambda^{k}\Big(\bigcup\mathcal{C}\Big)\\
&\leq\frac{(1+\eta)^{n-k}}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{K}J_{k}f(x)\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{f(x)})\difm^{k}(x)\\
&\quad\quad+M\diam(C)^{n-k}\frac{(1+\eta)^{n-k}}{\omega_{n-k}}\lambda^{k}\bigg(\Big(\bigcup\mathcal{C}\Big)\setminus K\bigg)\\
&\quad\quad+\frac{\diam(C)^{n-k}+M+1+L(1+\eta)^{n}}{m\omega_{n-k}}\lambda^{k}\Big(\bigcup\mathcal{C}\Big)\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{Eq4})}}}}{{\leq}}}\frac{(1+\eta)^{n-k}}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{K}J_{k}f(x)\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{f(x)})\difm^{k}(x)\\
&\quad\quad+M\diam(C)^{n-k}\frac{(1+\eta)^{n-k}}{m\omega_{n-k}}\\
&\quad\quad+\frac{\diam(C)^{n-k}+M+1+L(1+\eta)^{n}}{m\omega_{n-k}}\lambda^{k}\big(K\oplus B_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}(0,\delta)\big),\end{split}

from which it follows that

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)^{*}&\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{K}J_{k}f(x)\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{f(x)})\difm^{k}(x)\\
&\quad\quad+M\diam(C)^{n-k}\frac{1}{m\omega_{n-k}}\\
&\quad\quad+\frac{\diam(C)^{n-k}+M+1+L}{m\omega_{n-k}}\lambda^{k}\big(K\oplus B_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}(0,\delta)\big).\end{split}

Using area formula and the fact that
m\in\mathbb{N}
was arbitrary, we obtain

\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)^{*}\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{w})\difm^{k}(w),

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemmata

and
2.9
give us the following result:

.

Let

be a compact subset of a
\mathcal{C}^{1}

k
-graph, where

3.1
Lower Bound for General Countably
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-rectifiable Sets

Let us first establish a lower bound on
\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)_{*}
for countably
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-rectifiable sets
S
. We will proceed similarly to the proof of
(
KR
, Lemma 6)
.

Proposition 3.1

(Lower bound)
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a countably
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-rectifiable set, where
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
, and let
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}
. Then

\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)_{*}\geq\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\,\difm^{k}(x).

Proof.

First, assume that
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n,n}
.
Since
S
is countably
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-rectifiable, there exists a sequence
\{S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of pairwise disjoint compact subsets of
S
covering
S
up to an
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-negligible set, such that each
S_{i}
is contained in a
\mathcal{C}^{1}

k
-graph.

Let
L\in\mathbb{N}
be arbitrary.
Since the family
\{S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{L}
is finite and consists of disjoint compact sets, Lemma
2.10
yields

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{L}\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int_{S_{i}}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\,\difm^{k}(x)&=\sum_{i=1}^{L}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S_{i})\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{L}\lim_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}(S_{i}\oplus rC)}{\omega_{n-k}r^{n-k}}\\
&=\lim_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}r^{n-k}}\lambda^{n}\Big(\bigcup_{i=1}^{L}S_{i}\oplus rC\Big)\\
&\leq\liminf_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}(S\oplus rC)}{\omega_{n-k}r^{n-k}}\\
&=\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)_{*}.\end{split}

(35)

Since
L
was arbitrary, we obtain

\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\,\difm^{k}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int_{S_{i}}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\,\difm^{k}(x)\leq\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)_{*},

which proves the desired lower bound for convex bodies of full dimension.

Now assume that
\dim(C)=m<n
, and denote by
L
the linear span of
C
.
If
k+m<n
, then for
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-almost all
x\in S
,

\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})=\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{x}S}(C)\big)=0.

Indeed,
P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{x}S}(C)
is a convex body of dimension at most
m<n-k
. Consequently,

0=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\,\difm^{k}(x)\leq\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)_{*}.

Assume now that
k+m\geq n
. By Fubini’s theorem,

\lambda^{n}(S\oplus rC)=\int_{L^{\perp}}\mathcal{H}^{m}\Big(\big(S\cap(x+L)\big)\oplus rC\Big)\,\difm^{n-m}(x).

By Theorem
1.5
, the set
S\cap(x+L)
is countably
\mathcal{H}^{k+m-n}
-rectifiable for
\mathcal{H}^{n-m}
-almost all
x\in L^{\perp}
. Fatou’s lemma, the coarea formula for
P_{L^{\perp}}
, and the already established inequality for full-dimensional convex bodies yield

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)_{*}&\geq\int_{L^{\perp}}\liminf_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{m}\Big(\big(S\cap(x+L)\big)\oplus rC\Big)}{\omega_{n-k}r^{m-(k+m-n)}}\,\difm^{n-m}(x)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int_{L^{\perp}}\int_{S\cap(x+L)}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{y}(S\cap(x+L))}(C)\big)\,\difm^{k+m-n}(y)\,\difm^{n-m}(x)\\
&=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int_{S}J^{n-m}\big(P_{L^{\perp}}|_{\textup{T}^{k}_{w}S}\big)\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{w}(S\cap(P_{L^{\perp}}(w)+L))}(C)\big)\,\difm^{k}(w)\\
&=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int_{S}J_{n-k}\big(P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{w}S}|_{L}\big)\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{w}(S\cap(P_{L^{\perp}}(w)+L))}(C)\big)\,\difm^{k}(w).\end{split}

It remains to show that

J_{n-k}\big(P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{w}S}|_{L}\big)\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{w}(S\cap(P_{L^{\perp}}(w)+L))}(C)\big)=\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{w})

(36)

for
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-almost all
w\in S
.

Observe that
\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{w})=0
if and only if

\dim\big(P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{w}S}(L)\big)<n-k,

which is equivalent to

\dim(L\cap\textup{T}^{k}_{w}S)>k+m-n,

or, equivalently,

\dim(L+\textup{T}^{k}_{w}S)<n.

In other words, this holds if and only if the intersection
\big(P_{L^{\perp}}(w)+L\big)\cap S
is not transversal.

If the intersection is not transversal, then both sides of (
36
) vanish.

If the intersection is transversal, then

\textup{T}^{k+m-n}_{w}\big(S\cap(P_{L^{\perp}}(w)+L)\big)=L\cap\textup{T}^{k}_{w}S.

Define the linear map

F(z)\coloneq\big(P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{w}(S\cap(P_{L^{\perp}}(w)+L))}|_{L}\big)\circ\big(P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{w}S}|_{L}\big)^{-1}(z),\qquad z\in P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{w}S}(L).

Then
F\colon P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{w}S}(L)\to P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{w}(S\cap(P_{L^{\perp}}(w)+L))}(L)
is a well-defined linear isomorphism. Indeed, for
z\in P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{w}S}(L)
,

F(z)=P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{w}(S\cap(P_{L^{\perp}}(w)+L))}|_{L}\big(z+(L\cap\textup{T}^{k}_{w}S)\big)=P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{w}S}(z).

Since
F(C_{w})=P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{w}(S\cap(P_{L^{\perp}}(w)+L))}(C)
, we obtain

\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{w})J_{n-k}(F)=\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{w}(S\cap(P_{L^{\perp}}(w)+L))}(C)\big).

Finally, since
\textup{N}^{n-k}_{w}(S\cap(P_{L^{\perp}}(w)+L))\subseteq L
, we conclude that

J_{n-k}\big(P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{w}S}|_{L}\big)\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\big(P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{w}(S\cap(P_{L^{\perp}}(w)+L))}(C)\big)=\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{w}),

which completes the proof.
∎

In general, to obtain a reasonable upper bound on
\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)^{*}
, one needs to impose additional assumptions, as we will see in the next subsection.

Lemma 3.6

\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)^{*}\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\difm^{k}(x).

Assume that the dimension of

Indeed, if

\begin{split}\bigcup_{x\in I_{\varepsilon,r}}B(x,\varepsilon\alpha\kappa r)&\subseteq\big(S_{\varepsilon,r}\oplus(-\kappa rC^{\varepsilon})\big)\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{M}S_{i}\\
&\subseteq\big(S\oplus(-\kappa rC^{\varepsilon})\big)\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{M}S_{i},\end{split}

which coupled with (
39
) yields

\begin{split}\sum_{x\in I_{\varepsilon,r}}&\gamma(\varepsilon\alpha\kappa r)^{k+m-n}\mathcal{H}^{n-m}\Big(P_{L^{\perp}}\big(S\cap B(x,\alpha\kappa r)\big)\Big)\\
&\leq N\mu\Big(\big(S\oplus(-\kappa rC^{\varepsilon})\big)\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{M}S_{i}\bigg)\\
&<\varepsilon^{k+m-n+1}N,\end{split}

from which it follows

\sum_{x\in I_{\varepsilon,r}}\mathcal{H}^{n-m}\Big(P_{L^{\perp}}\big(S\cap B(x,\varepsilon\alpha\kappa r)\big)\Big)\leq\frac{\varepsilon N}{\gamma(\kappa r)^{k+m-n}}=\frac{\varepsilon^{1/(k+m-n+1)}N}{\gamma r^{k+m-n}}

(40)

Using (
38
) and (
40
), we can estimate

\begin{split}\lambda^{n}(S_{r,\varepsilon}\oplus rC)&\leq\lambda^{n}\big(S_{r,\varepsilon}\oplus B_{L}(0,\beta r)\big)\\
&\leq\sum_{x\in I_{r,\varepsilon}}\lambda^{n}\Big(\big(S\cap B(x,\varepsilon\alpha\kappa r)\big)\oplus B_{L}(0,\beta r)\Big)\\
&\leq\sum_{x\in I_{r,\varepsilon}}\mathcal{H}^{n-m}\Big(P_{L^{\perp}}\big(S\cap B(x,\varepsilon\alpha\kappa r\big)\Big)\omega_{m}r^{m}(\varepsilon\alpha\kappa+\beta)^{m}\\
&\leq(\alpha+\beta)^{m}\sum_{x\in I_{r,\varepsilon}}\mathcal{H}^{n-m}\Big(P_{L^{\perp}}\big(S\cap B(x,\varepsilon\alpha\kappa r\big)\Big)\omega_{m}r^{m}\\
&\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{1/(k+m-n+1)}\omega_{m}r^{n-k}(\alpha+\beta)^{m}N}{\gamma}.\end{split}

Finally, we have

S\oplus rC\subseteq(S_{r,\varepsilon}\oplus rC)\cup\big((S\setminus S_{r,\varepsilon})\oplus rC\big)\subseteq(S_{r,\varepsilon}\oplus rC)\cup\Big(\bigcup_{i=1}^{M}S_{i}\oplus\kappa rC^{\varepsilon}\oplus rC^{\varepsilon}\Big),

whence

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}_{C}^{k}(S)^{*}&\leq\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}(S_{r,\varepsilon}\oplus rC)}{\omega_{n-k}r^{n-k}}+\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}r^{n-k}}\lambda^{n}\bigg(\Big(\bigcup_{i=1}^{M}S_{i}\Big)\oplus(1+\kappa)rC^{\varepsilon}\bigg)\\
&\leq\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{\varepsilon^{1/(k+m-n+1)}\omega_{m}(\alpha+\beta)^{m}N}{\gamma\omega_{n-k}}+(1+\kappa)^{n-k}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C^{\varepsilon}}(S_{i})\\
&\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{1/(k+m-n+1)}\omega_{m}(\alpha+\beta)^{m}N}{\gamma\omega_{n-k}}+(1+\kappa)^{n-k}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C^{\varepsilon}}(S_{i})\\
&=\frac{\varepsilon^{1/(k+m-n+1)}\omega_{m}(\alpha+\beta)^{m}N}{\gamma\omega_{n-k}}\\
&\quad\quad+(1+\kappa)^{n-k}\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C^{\varepsilon}_{x})\difm^{k}(x).\end{split}

Using Lemma
2.5
, we get the desired inequality

\mathcal{M}_{C}^{k}(S)^{*}\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\difm^{k}(x),

which completes the proof for those
C
’s with
\textup{span}(C)=L
.

If
\textup{span}(C)\subsetneq L
, we consider

C_{r}\coloneq C\oplus B_{L}(0,r),\quad r>0.

Using the first part of the proof, we conclude that

\Phi_{S}(C)\leq\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)_{*}\leq\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)^{*}\leq\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C_{r}}(S)^{*}\leq\Phi_{S}(C_{r}),\quad r>0.

Lemma
2.5
gives us

\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\difm^{k}(x),

which completes the proof.
∎

3.4
The Case
k+m-n=0

In this subsection, we show that the limit
\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)
always exists and coincides with the quantity

\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C)}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{L^{\perp}}\mathcal{H}^{0}\big(S\cap(x+L)\big)\difm^{k}(x)

provided that
k+m=n
.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-measurable set, and let
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n,m}
. If
k+m=n
, then

\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)=\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C)}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{L^{\perp}}\mathcal{H}^{0}\big(S\cap(x+L)\big)\difm^{k}(x),

where
L=\LO(C).

Proof.

Fubini’s theorem yields

\mathcal{M}^{k}_{r,C}(S)=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}r^{n-k}}\int\limits_{L^{\perp}}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\Big(\big(S\cap(x+L)\big)\oplus rC\Big)\difm^{k}(x).

Fatou’s lemma and (
4
) gives us

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)_{*}&\geq\int\limits_{L^{\perp}}\liminf_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}r^{n-k}}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\Big(\big(S\cap(x+L)\big)\oplus rC\Big)\difm^{k}(x)\\
&=\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C)}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{L^{\perp}}\mathcal{H}^{0}\big(S\cap(x+L)\big)\difm^{k}(x).\end{split}

We see that if the function

x\mapsto\mathcal{H}^{0}\big(S\cap(x+L)\big),\qquad x\in L^{\perp},

is not integrable, then

\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)=\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C)}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{L^{\perp}}\mathcal{H}^{0}\big(S\cap(x+L)\big)\difm^{k}(x)=\infty.

On the other hand, if it is integrable, we can use the reverse Fatou’s lemma and (
4
) to obtain

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)^{*}&\leq\int\limits_{L^{\perp}}\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}r^{n-k}}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\Big(\big(S\cap(x+L)\big)\oplus rC\Big)\difm^{k}(x)\\
&=\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C)}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{L^{\perp}}\mathcal{H}^{0}\big(S\cap(x+L)\big)\difm^{k}(x),\end{split}

since

\mathcal{H}^{n-k}\Big(\big(S\cap(x+L)\big)\oplus rC\Big)\leq\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(rC)\mathcal{H}^{0}\big(S\cap(x+L)\big).

We get

\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)=\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C)}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{L^{\perp}}\mathcal{H}^{0}\big(S\cap(x+L)\big)\difm^{k}(x),

as we wanted.
∎

Corollary 3.15
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a countably
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-rectifiable set, and let
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n,m}
. If
k+m=n
, then

\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\difm^{k}(x).

Proof.

For
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-a.a.
x\in S
we have the equality

\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C)J_{n-k}(P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{x}S|L})=\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x}).

Theorem
3.14
implies

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)&=\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C)}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{L^{\perp}}\mathcal{H}^{0}\big(S\cap(x+L)\big)\difm^{k}(x)\\
&=\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C)}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}J^{k}(P_{L^{\perp}|\textup{T}^{k}_{x}S})\difm^{k}(x)\\
&=\frac{\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C)}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}J_{n-k}(P_{\textup{N}^{n-k}_{x}S|L})\difm^{k}(x)\\
&=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\difm^{k}(x),\end{split}

which completes the proof.
∎

3.5
The Case
k+m-n<0

We already know from the proof of Proposition
3.1
that if the set
S
is countably
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-rectifiable and compact, and
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n,m}
, where
k+m<n
, then

\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\difm^{k}(x)=0.

The following theorem tells us that in the limit
\mathcal{M}_{C}^{k}(S)
exists and equals
0
as well.

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
S
be a countably
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-rectifiable set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
m\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
k+m<n
. Then for every
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n,m}
, we have

\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)=0.

Proof.

Let us express
S
as follows:

S=\bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty}S_{i},

where
S_{0}
is
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-negligible and for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
S_{i}
is a compact Lipschitz
k
-graph. We have

S\oplus rC\subseteq\bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty}(S_{i}\oplus rC).

Let
r>0
and define
f_{r}\colon S\times C\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

f_{r}(x,c)\coloneq x+rc.

Then
f_{r}
is Lipschitz and for every
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{k+m}(S_{i}\oplus rC)&=\mathcal{H}^{k+m}\big(f_{r}(S_{i}\times C)\big)\\
&\leq\textup{lip}(f_{r})^{k+m}\mathcal{H}^{k+m}(S_{i}\times C)\\
&=\textup{lip}(f_{r})^{k+m}\mathcal{H}^{k}(S_{i})\mathcal{H}^{m}(C)\\
&<\infty,\end{split}

from which we see that

\lambda^{n}(S\oplus rC)=0,\quad r>0.

Hence, the limit
\mathcal{M}_{C}^{k}(S)
exists and coincides with

\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\difm^{k}(x)=0,

which completes the proof.
∎

4
Dependence on the Choice of
C

In this section, we prove that if the
k
-dimensional
C
-anisotropic Minkowski content of a countably
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-rectifiable set
S
exists, where
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n,n}
, and coincides with

\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\difm^{k}(x),

then the same holds for any
C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}^{n,n}.

Our proof adapts the argument used in
(
Fr
, Theorem 3.1)
. We note, however, that
(
Fr
, Theorem 3.1)
deals with
localized
anisotropic Minkowski contents, while here we consider only the
non-localized
case.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
S
be a countably
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-rectifiable set, where
k\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
, and let
C,C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}^{n,n}
. Then

\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\difm^{k}(x)

if and only if

\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C^{\prime}}(S)=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C^{\prime}_{x})\difm^{k}(x).

Proof.

Suppose that

\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\difm^{k}(x).

If
S=\emptyset
, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, assume
S\neq\emptyset
. Due to Proposition
3.1
, It suffices to show

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C^{\prime}}(S)^{*}\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C^{\prime}_{x})\difm^{k}(x).

(41)

Since
S
is countably
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-rectifiable, there exists a sequence
\{S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of pairwise disjoint compact subsets of
S
which covers
S
up to an
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-negligible set, each contained in the
k
-graph of a real-valued Lipschitz function. Suppose that there exists some
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
S_{k_{0}}\neq\emptyset
.

If
S=\bigcup_{i=1}^{L}S_{i}
for some
L\in\mathbb{N}
, then there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, assume that
S\setminus\bigcup_{k=1}^{L}S_{i}
is nonempty for each
L\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let us prove the first inequality in (
41
). Observe

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)&=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\difm^{k}(x)\\
&=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S_{i}}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C_{x})\difm^{k}(x)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S_{i}),\end{split}

where the last equality follows from Theorem
2.9
. For a given
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
K\geq k_{0}
such that

\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S)-\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C}(S_{i})<\varepsilon^{k+1}.

Since
\{S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{K}
is a finite sequence of disjoint compact sets, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathcal{M}_{C}(S_{i})=\lim_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}r^{n-k}}\lambda^{n}\Big(\bigcup_{i=1}^{K}S_{i}\oplus rC\Big),

so that

\lim_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}r^{n-k}}\lambda^{n}\bigg((S\oplus rC)\setminus\Big(\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}S_{i}\oplus rC\Big)\bigg)<\varepsilon^{k+1}.

(42)

Let positive constants
a
,
b
and
c
satisfy
B(0,a)\subseteq C
and
B(0,c)\subseteq C^{\prime}\subseteq B(0,b)
, and define

S_{r,\varepsilon}\coloneq\Big\{x\in S:\dist\Big(x,\bigcup_{i=1}^{K}S_{i}\Big)>2\diam(C)r\varepsilon\Big\},\quad r>0.

There exists
r_{0}>0
such that
S_{r,\varepsilon}\neq\emptyset
for all
r\in(0,r_{0})
.

By Theorem
1.12
, there exists an at most countable set
I_{r,\varepsilon}\subseteq S_{r,\varepsilon}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle S_{r,\varepsilon}\subseteq\bigcup_{x\in I_{r,\varepsilon}}B(x,ar\varepsilon)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in I_{r,\varepsilon}}\chi_{B(x,ar\varepsilon)}\leq N.

We have

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{0}(I_{r,\varepsilon})\omega_{n}(ar\varepsilon)^{n}&=\sum_{x\in I_{r,\varepsilon}}\omega_{n}(ar\varepsilon)^{n}\\
&=\sum_{x\in I_{r,\varepsilon}}\lambda^{n}\big(B(x,ar\varepsilon)\big)\\
&\leq N\lambda^{n}\Big(\bigcup_{x\in I_{r,\varepsilon}}B(x,ar\varepsilon)\Big).\end{split}

Notice

\bigcup_{x\in I_{r,\varepsilon}}B(x,ar\varepsilon)\subseteq S_{r,\varepsilon}\oplus B(0,ar\varepsilon)\subseteq(S\oplus r\varepsilon C)\setminus\Big(\bigcup_{i=1}^{K}S_{i}\oplus r\varepsilon C\Big).

Inequality (
42
) implies

\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\mathcal{H}^{0}(I_{r,\varepsilon})r^{k}\leq\frac{\omega_{n-k}N\varepsilon}{a^{n}\omega_{n}}.

(43)

We have

S\subseteq(S\setminus S_{r,\varepsilon})\cup S_{r,\varepsilon}.

On the one hand,

S\setminus S_{r,\varepsilon}=\Big\{x\in S:\dist\Big(x,\bigcup_{i=1}^{K}S_{i}\Big)\leq 2\diam(C)\varepsilon r\Big\},

from which we see that

S\setminus S_{r,\varepsilon}\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{K}S_{i}\oplus B\big(0,3\diam(C)\varepsilon r\big),

whence

\begin{split}\lambda^{n}\big((S\setminus S_{r,\varepsilon})\oplus rC^{\prime}\big)\leq\lambda^{n}\Big(\bigcup_{i=1}^{K}S_{i}\oplus r\big(1+c^{-1}3\diam(C)\varepsilon\big)C^{\prime}\Big)\end{split}

(44)

On the other hand,

S_{r,\varepsilon}\oplus rC^{\prime}\subseteq\bigcup_{x\in I_{r,\varepsilon}}B(x,rb).

Hence, using (
43
), we obtain

\begin{split}\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}(S_{r,\varepsilon}\oplus rC^{\prime})}{\omega_{n-k}r^{n-k}}&\leq\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}r^{n-k}}\lambda^{n}\Big(\bigcup_{x\in I_{r,\varepsilon}}B(x,rb)\Big)\\
&\leq\frac{b^{n}}{\omega_{n-k}}\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\mathcal{H}^{0}(I_{r,\varepsilon})r^{k}\\
&\leq\frac{b^{n}N\varepsilon}{a^{n}\omega_{n}}.\end{split}

(45)

Finally, using (
44
), (
45
) and Theorem
2.9
, we conclude that

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C^{\prime}}(S)^{*}&\leq\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}(S_{r,\varepsilon}\oplus rC^{\prime})}{\omega_{n-k}r^{n-k}}+\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}\big((S\setminus S_{r,\varepsilon})\oplus rC^{\prime}\big)}{\omega_{n-k}r^{n-k}}\\
&\leq\frac{b^{n}N\varepsilon}{a^{n}\omega_{n}}+\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}r^{n-k}}\lambda^{n}\Big(\bigcup_{i=1}^{K}S_{i}\oplus r\big(1+c^{-1}3\diam(C)\varepsilon\big)C^{\prime}\Big)\\
&=\frac{b^{n}N\varepsilon}{a^{n}\omega_{n}}+\big(1+c^{-1}3\diam(C)\varepsilon\big)^{n-k}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C^{\prime}}(S_{i})\\
&\leq\frac{b^{n}N\varepsilon}{a^{n}\omega_{n}}+\big(1+c^{-1}3\diam(C)\varepsilon\big)^{n-k}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C^{\prime}}(S_{i})\\
&\leq\frac{b^{n}N\varepsilon}{a^{n}\omega_{n}}+\big(1+c^{-1}3\diam(C)\varepsilon\big)^{n-k}\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C^{\prime}_{x})\difm^{k}(x).\end{split}

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we conclude

\mathcal{M}^{k}_{C^{\prime}}(S)^{*}\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{n-k}}\int\limits_{S}\mathcal{H}^{n-k}(C^{\prime}_{x})\difm^{k}(x),

which completes the proof.
∎

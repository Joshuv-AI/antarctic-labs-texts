Characteristic Classes of Representations of Lie Groups
Rohit Joshi and Steven Spallone

Abstract
. An irreducible representation of a reductive Lie algebra, when restricted to a Cartan subalgebra, decomposes into weights with multiplicity. The first part of this paper outlines a procedure to compute symmetric polynomials (e.g., power sums) of this multiset of weights, as functions of the highest weight. Next, let G G be a connected reductive complex algebraic group with maximal torus T T . We express the restrictions of the Chern classes of irreducible representations of G G to T T , as polynomial functions in the highest weight. We do the same for Stiefel-Whitney classes of orthogonal representations.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex reductive Lie algebra, with Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}
, and
k\geq 0
. We define the
k
th power sum of a representation
\phi
of
\mathfrak{g}
as

P_{k}(\phi)=\sum_{\mu}m_{\phi}(\mu)\mu^{k},

where
\mu
runs over the weights of
\phi
, and
m_{\phi}(\mu)
denotes the multiplicity. It takes values in the symmetric algebra of
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
.
As is widely known, power sums generate the algebra of symmetric functions.

The essential case is when
\phi
is irreducible, say with highest weight
\lambda
, so
\phi=\phi_{\lambda}
.
Determining each
m_{\phi_{\lambda}}(\mu)
is subtle. On the other hand

P_{0}(\phi)=\deg\phi
is explicitly given by the Weyl Dimension formula (WDF).
Hence we apply WDF methods to compute the power sums, following ideas from

[
Bou08
, Ch. VIII, page 257, Exercise 8]
. Here we encounter the functions

F_{k}(\mu,\nu)=\sum_{w\in W}\sgn(w)\langle{}^{w}\mu,\nu\rangle^{k},

for
\mu\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
and
\nu\in\mathfrak{t}
. The
F_{k}
may be computed, in principle, through the theory of
W
-invariant polynomial functions on
\mathfrak{t}
.
(See Section
5.2
. Here
\delta
is the usual half-sum of positive roots.)
This being done, the exponential generating function (EGF) of the sequence
P_{k}(\phi)
is the quotient of the EGF of
F_{k}(\lambda+\delta)
by the EGF of
F_{k}(\delta)
. (See (
8
).) This gives a recursive formula for
P_{k}(\phi)
. We illustrate all this with several examples.

Let
G
be a connected reductive complex algebraic group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, and
\pi
a representation of
G
. (Throughout this paper all representations are algebraic.) Fix a maximal torus
T
of
G
, with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{t}
. Associated to
\pi
are Chern classes (CCs)
c_{k}(\pi)\in\HH^{2k}(BG,\mathbb{Z})
, where
BG
is a classifying space for
G
. When
\pi
is orthogonal, one also has Stiefel-Whitney classes (SWCs)
w_{k}(\pi)\in\HH^{k}(BG,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
; see Section
10.2
for details.

Write
c_{k}^{T}(\pi)
for the image of
c_{k}(\pi)
under the restriction map

\HH^{2k}(BG,\mathbb{Z})\to\HH^{2k}(BT,\mathbb{Z})
, and similarly
w_{k}^{T}(\pi)\in\HH^{k}(BT,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
.
Since
c_{k}^{T}(\pi)
is essentially the
k
th elementary symmetric function of the weights of
\pi
, we can apply the power sum calculation from the first part of the paper.

Let
T[2]
be the
2
-torsion subgroup of
T
. We describe a surjective ring homomorphism
\varphi:\HH^{*}(BT,\mathbb{Z})\to\HH^{*}(BT[2],\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})

which takes
c_{k}^{T}(\pi)
to
w_{k}^{T[2]}(\pi)
, when
\pi
is orthogonal.

Let
\pi_{\lambda}
be the irreducible representation of
G
with highest weight
\lambda
. In Section
7.4
we give a method to compute
c_{k}^{T}(\pi_{\lambda})
and
w_{k}^{T}(\pi_{\lambda})
in terms of
\lambda
. The method entails that their dependence on
\lambda
is polynomial:

Theorem 1
.

For all nonnegative integers
k
, the function
\lambda\mapsto c_{k}^{T}(\pi_{\lambda})\in\mathbb{\HH}^{2k}(BT,\mathbb{Z})
is polynomial in
\lambda
of degree no greater than
k(N+1)
, where
N
is the number of positive roots.

For the case
k=2
, we give a direct formula for
c_{2}^{T}(\pi_{\lambda})
. To state it, write
\Ad
for the adjoint representation of
G
on
\mathfrak{g}
, and write ‘
|\>|
’ for the Killing form on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
.

Theorem 2
.

When
\mathfrak{g}
is simple, we have

c_{2}^{T}(\pi_{\lambda})=\frac{|\lambda+\delta|^{2}-|\delta|^{2}}{\dim\mathfrak{g}}\cdot\deg\pi_{\lambda}\cdot c_{2}^{T}(\Ad).

We also observe that
c_{2}(\pi)
vanishes iff
\pi
is trivial (Proposition
8
).
We illustrate all this by computing
c_{k}^{T}(\pi_{\lambda})
and
w_{k}^{T}(\pi_{\lambda})
for
G=\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
,
\SL_{3}(\mathbb{C})
, and
\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and
k\leq 4
.
We also compute
c_{k}^{T}(\pi_{\lambda})
for
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and
k\leq 2
.

When
G
is one of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,
\SO_{n}(\mathbb{C})
or
\Sp_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
, we give an alternate product expression for the
total
SWC
w^{T}(\pi)=1+w_{1}^{T}(\pi)+w_{2}^{T}(\pi)+\cdots
in terms of character values of
\pi
at elements of order two, following the techniques of
[
GJ23
]
and
[
MS25
]
. Also see Section
9.4
for an analogue for total CCs.

As an application, we give a new criterion for an orthogonal representation of
G
to lift to the corresponding spin group. (Corollary
4
). From it, one can recover our earlier result
[
JS20
, Theorem 1]
.

When
G
is one of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, or
\Sp_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
, the restriction map to the torus is injective, hence computing
c_{k}^{T}
(respectively,
w_{k}^{T}
) is equivalent to determining
c_{k}
(respectively,
w_{k}
).

Now we describe the layout of this paper. In Section
3
we introduce the power sums
P_{k}(\phi)
; for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
we compute them explicitly.
In Section
4
we apply the Weyl Character Formula to reduce the problem to computing the
F_{k}
. We develop this further in Section
5
; in particular we show that
P_{k}(\phi_{\lambda})
is a polynomial function of
\lambda
, of degree at most
N+k
, and give a general expression for
P_{2}
. We also indicate how higher
F_{k}
may be computed. To illustrate, we work out
P_{2},P_{3}
, and
P_{4}
for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
in Section
6
.

Next, we turn to characteristic classes of Lie groups. In Section
7
we prove Theorems
1
and
2
. We discuss the examples of
\GL_{2}
and
\PGL_{2}
, and summarize the general CC calculations in Section
7.4
. We compute the SWCs
w^{T}(\pi)
of orthogonal representations in Section
8
.

We put a few topological technicalities in the Appendix. In particular, we explain a theory of SWCs for orthogonal representations of Lie
groups, compatible with the existing theory for real representations.

2.
Preliminaries and Notation

2.1.
Polynomials

Let
V,W
be finite-dimensional complex vector spaces, and
f:V\to W
a map. We say that
f
is
polynomial
, when for all linear maps
\nu:\mathbb{C}\to V
and
\mu:W\to\mathbb{C}
, the composition
\mu\circ f\circ\nu
is polynomial in the usual sense. Write ‘
\Poly(V,W)
’ for the set of such polynomial functions. We may identify
\Poly(V,\mathbb{C})
with the symmetric algebra
\Sym(V^{*})
, and
\Poly(V,W)
with
W\otimes\Sym(V^{*})
.

If
b_{1},\ldots,b_{d}
is a basis of
V^{*}
, then
\Poly(V,\mathbb{C})
is the polynomial algebra
\mathbb{C}[b_{1},\ldots,b_{d}]
.
When
R
is a commutative
\mathbb{C}
-algebra, we can identify
\Poly(V,R)
with the polynomial algebra
R[b_{1},\ldots,b_{d}]
.

Suppose
L
is a free abelian group of finite rank. We say that a function
f:L\to W
is
polynomial
, when it is the restriction of
a polynomial function
f:L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}\to W
.
Finally, if
S\subset L
is a subset, we say that
f:S\to W
is
polynomial
, when it is the restriction of a polynomial function on
L
.

2.2.
Lie Algebras

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a reductive complex Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}
.

Let
\mathbb{S}=\Sym_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{t}^{*})
and
\mathbb{S}^{*}=\Sym_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{t})
. For
k\geq 0
, put
\mathbb{S}^{k}=\Sym_{\mathbb{C}}^{k}(\mathfrak{t}^{*})
.

Write
\Lambda
for the weight lattice; following
[
FH13
, page 200]
, this is the set of
\mu\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
which are integer-valued on all coroots of
\mathfrak{t}
in
\mathfrak{g}
.
Define
\mathbb{S}_{\Lambda}=\Sym_{\mathbb{Z}}\Lambda\subset\mathbb{S}
.
Fix a choice
\Lambda^{+}\subset\Lambda
of dominant weights.
Let
W
be the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{t}
in
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
\sgn:W\to\{\pm 1\}
be the usual sign function
[
FH13
, Section 24.1]
. Write
\delta
for the half sum of the positive roots of
\mathfrak{t}
in
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
K
be the Killing form on
\mathfrak{t}
; when
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple, this defines an isomorphism
\sigma:\mathfrak{t}\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathfrak{t}^{*}
by the formula
\langle{}^{\sigma}\nu,\nu^{\prime}\rangle=K(\nu,\nu^{\prime})
.
Write
K^{\vee}
for the inverse form of
K
; it is defined by
K^{\vee}({}^{\sigma}\nu,{}^{\sigma}\nu^{\prime})=K(\nu,\nu^{\prime})
.

Definition 1
.

For semisimple
\mathfrak{g}
,
define
q_{2}\in\mathbb{S}
by
q_{2}(\nu)=K(\nu,\nu)
, and when
q_{2}^{\vee}\in\mathbb{S}^{*}
by
q_{2}^{\vee}(\mu)=K^{\vee}(\mu,\mu)
.

When
\mathfrak{g}
is simple, we have the following “strange formula” of Freudenthal and de Vries
[
FdV69
]
or
[
Bou08
, page 257]
:

(1)

24q_{2}^{\vee}(\delta)=\dim\mathfrak{g}.

2.3.
The Algebra
\mathcal{A}

Definition 2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\Sym_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{t}^{*}\oplus\mathfrak{t})
.

Note that
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{S}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{S}^{*}
, hence it is bigraded and may be viewed in two ways:

(1)

The algebra of complex-valued polynomials on pairs
(\mu,\nu)\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}\times\mathfrak{t}
.

(2)

The algebra of
\mathbb{S}
-valued polynomials on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
.

Given
\mu\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, there is an “evaluation map”

\ev_{\mu}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{S}

given by
\ev_{\mu}(\bf G)(\nu)=\bf G(\mu,\nu)
.

Here are a few flavors of “degree” for
f\in\mathcal{A}
, to refer to later:

Definition 3
.

Let
f=\sum\limits_{i,j}f_{i,j}\in\mathcal{A}
, with
f_{i,j}\in\Sym^{i}(\mathfrak{t}^{*})\otimes\Sym^{j}(\mathfrak{t})
.

(1)

\deg^{*}(f)=\max\{i\mid\exists j\text{ with }f_{ij}\neq 0\}
.

(2)

\deg(f)=\max\{j\mid\exists i\text{ with }f_{ij}\neq 0\}
.

(3)

\underline{\deg}(f)=(\deg^{*}(f),\deg(f))\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

3.
Power Sums

Let
(\phi,V)
be a (finite-dimensional, complex) representation of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Given
\mu\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, write
m_{\phi}(\mu)
for the multiplicity of
\mu
as a weight of
\phi
.

Definition 4
.

For a nonnegative integer
k
, we define

(2)

P_{k}(\phi)=\sum_{\mu}m_{\phi}(\mu)\mu^{k}\in\mathbb{S}^{k},

where
\mu
ranges over
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
.

(It is the
k
th power sum of the weights, counted with multiplicity.)
By
[
FH13
, page 200]
, each weight
\mu\in\Lambda
, so
P_{k}(\phi)\in{\mathbb{S}}_{\Lambda}^{k}
.

Example 1
.

Let
\phi_{\ell}
be the irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
of degree
\ell+1
.
Let
\mathfrak{t}
be the diagonal Cartan subalgebra. Define
\mu_{0}\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
by
\mu_{0}(\diag(1,-1))=1
.
Note that
P_{k}(\phi_{\ell})=0
when
k
is odd. Moreover
P_{0}(\phi_{\ell})=\ell+1
.
When
k
is even, we have

P_{k}(\phi_{\ell})=2(\ell^{k}+(\ell-2)^{k}+\cdots+(\epsilon+2)^{k}+\epsilon^{k})\mu_{0}^{k},

where
\epsilon
is the remainder of
\ell
mod
2
.

Let
B_{k}(x)
be the
k
th Bernoulli polynomial of degree
k

[
Bou04
, Ch VI, Section 1]
.
By the well-known Faulhaber’s formula, for
k
even we have

1^{k}+2^{k}+\cdots+\ell^{k}=\frac{B_{k+1}(\ell+1)}{k+1}.

For
\ell
even, it follows that

(3)

P_{k}(\phi_{\ell})=\frac{2^{k+1}}{k+1}\cdot B_{k+1}\left(\frac{\ell+2}{2}\right)\mu_{0}^{k}.

In fact, (
3
) is also true for
\ell
odd. One could deduce this from the identity
[
Bou04
, page 292]

B_{k+1}(x)=2^{k}\left(B_{k+1}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)+B_{k+1}\left(\frac{x+1}{2}\right)\right).

However we will soon see (Section
5
) that
\ell\mapsto P_{k}(\phi_{\ell})
is polynomial. Then, since both sides of (
3
) are values of polynomials
for even
\ell
, equality holds for all
\ell
. For
k=2
this gives
P_{2}(\phi_{\ell})=2\dbinom{\ell+2}{3}
.

Returning to general
\mathfrak{g}
, we can easily understand the first two power sums.
We have
P_{0}(\phi)=\deg\phi
, and
P_{1}(\phi)=\sum_{\mu}m_{\phi}(\mu)\mu\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
is simply the trace, i.e., the composition

\mathfrak{t}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{g}\overset{\phi}{\to}\mathfrak{gl}(V)\overset{\tr}{\to}\mathbb{C}.

In particular,
P_{1}(\phi)=0
whenever
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple.

Let ‘
\ad
’ be the adjoint representation. Then
P_{2}(\ad)=q_{2}
by the formula

K(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})=\sum_{\alpha}\langle\alpha,\nu_{1}\rangle\langle\alpha,\nu_{2}\rangle,

from
[
Bou02
, VIII, page 226]
.

Suppose
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
are representations of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
.
Write
\phi=\phi_{1}\boxtimes\phi_{2}
for their exterior tensor product, a representation of
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. It is easy to see that

(4)

P_{k}(\phi)=\sum_{i+j=k}\binom{k}{i,j}P_{i}(\phi_{1})P_{j}(\phi_{2}).

For a linear functional
\phi
on an abelian Lie algebra
\mathfrak{t}
, obviously
P_{k}(\phi)=\phi^{k}
.
Hence the essential case is when
\mathfrak{g}
is
simple
. Our first goal in this paper is to determine
P_{k}(\phi)
for irreducible representations as a function of the highest weight, when
\mathfrak{g}
is simple.

Proposition 1
.

Suppose
k
is even. Then
P_{k}(\phi)=0
iff
\phi
is trivial.

Proof.

Note that
\mathbb{S}_{\Lambda}
is isomorphic to a polynomial algebra over the integers. Hence if
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{S}_{\Lambda}
with
\sum x_{i}^{k}=0
, then each
x_{i}=0
. So if
P_{k}(\phi)=0
, then the restriction of
\phi
to
\mathfrak{t}
is trivial. But this restriction contains all the highest weights of the irreducible constituents of
\phi
. Hence
\phi
is trivial.

∎

We can similarly define the elementary symmetric functions of the weights of
\phi
.

Definition 5
.

Put
E(\phi)=\prod_{\mu}(1+\mu)^{m_{\phi}(\mu)}\in\mathbb{S}_{\Lambda}
, and write
E_{k}(\phi)\in\mathbb{S}^{k}_{\Lambda}
for the degree
k
term of
E(\phi)
.

In particular
E_{0}(\phi)=1
. The recursive formula (2.11’) from
[
Mac98
, page 23]
gives:

(5)

nE_{n}(\phi)=\sum_{r=1}^{n}(-1)^{r-1}P_{r}(\phi)E_{n-r}(\phi).

A closed formula is given by Newton’s Identity:

n!E_{n}(\phi)=\det\begin{pmatrix}P_{1}(\phi)&1&0&\cdots&0\\
P_{2}(\phi)&P_{1}(\phi)&2&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
P_{n-1}(\phi)&P_{n-2}(\phi)&&\cdots&n-1\\
P_{n}(\phi)&P_{n-1}(\phi)&&\cdots&P_{1}(\phi)\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Example 2
.

We continue with Example
1
. From the above formulas, we deduce that
E_{k}(\phi_{\ell})=0
for
k
odd,
E_{2}(\phi_{\ell})=-\binom{\ell+2}{3}\mu_{0}^{2}
, and
E_{4}(\phi_{\ell})=\frac{1}{3}\binom{\ell+2}{5}(5\ell+12)\mu_{0}^{4}
.

4.
Weyl Character Formula

In this section we follow ideas from the proof of the Weyl character formula, for example in
[
Bou08
, Ch. VIII, §9]
. Indeed, our technique takes ideas from [ibid, page 257, Exercise 8].

Write
\mathbb{S}[[X]]
for the formal power series ring over
\mathbb{S}
.
Let
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{t}^{*}]
be the complex group algebra over the additive group
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, say with basis
e_{\mu}
for
\mu\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
. We shall write the action of the Weyl group
W
on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
and associated objects with leading superscript, e.g.,
\mu\mapsto{}^{w}\mu
.

Define a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra homomorphism
f:\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{t}^{*}]\to\mathbb{S}[[X]]
by

\begin{split}f(e_{\mu})&=e^{\mu\cdot X}\\
&=1+\mu X+\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}X^{2}+\cdots\\
\end{split}

As usual, put
J(\mu)=\sum_{w\in W}\sgn(w)\>{}^{w}e_{\mu}
, and for a representation
\phi
of
\mathfrak{g}
put
\mathfrak{X}_{\phi}=\sum_{\mu}m_{\phi}(\mu)e_{\mu}
. When
\phi
is irreducible with highest weight
\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}
, we shall write
\phi=\phi_{\lambda}
.
Weyl’s Character formula
[
Bou08
, Ch. VIII, §9, Theorem 1]
may be written as:

(6)

J(\lambda+\delta)=J(\delta)\mathfrak{X}_{\phi_{\lambda}}.

Generally,

\begin{split}f(\mathfrak{X}_{\phi})&=f\left(\sum_{\mu}m_{\phi}(\mu)\mu\right)\\
&=\sum_{\mu}m_{\phi}(\mu)e^{\mu X}\\
&=\sum_{\mu}m_{\phi}(\mu)\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\mu^{i}}{i!}X^{i}\right)\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{X^{i}}{i!}\left(\sum_{\mu}m_{\phi}(\mu)\mu^{i}\right)\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{P_{i}(\phi)}{i!}X^{i}.\\
\end{split}

Meanwhile,

\displaystyle f(J(\mu))

\displaystyle=f\left(\sum_{w\in W}\sgn(w)\>{}^{w}\mu\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in W}\sgn(w)\>e^{{}^{w}\mu\>X}

\displaystyle=\sum_{w\in W}\sgn(w)\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{{}^{w}\mu^{i}}{i!}X^{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{X^{i}}{i!}\sum_{w\in W}\sgn(w)\>({}^{w}\mu)^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{F_{i}(\mu)}{i!}X^{i},

where we define

(7)

F_{i}(\mu)=\sum_{w\in W}\sgn(w)({}^{w}\mu)^{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{i}.

Hence applying
f
to (
6
) gives

(8)

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{F_{i}(\lambda+\delta)}{i!}X^{i}=\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{F_{j}(\delta)}{j!}X^{j}\right)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{P_{k}(\phi_{\lambda})}{k!}X^{k}\right).

In other words, the exponential generating function (EGF) for
F_{i}(\lambda+\delta)
is the product of the EGF for
F_{i}(\delta)
and the EGF for
P_{k}(\phi_{\lambda})
. Therefore it is enough to evaluate the
F_{i}(\mu)
.

5.
A Recursive Formula for Power Sums

5.1.
Computing the
F_{k}
: First Steps

As mentioned in the introduction, the polynomials
F_{k}
are crucial for our calculation.
First we“inflate” them as follows.

Definition 6
.

Let
{\bf F}_{k}\in\mathcal{A}
be given by

{\bf F}_{k}(\mu,\nu)=\sum_{w\in W}\sgn(w)\langle{}^{w}\mu,\nu\rangle^{k}.

Given
\mu\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, we have
\ev_{\mu}({\bf F}_{k})=F_{k}(\mu)
. Since
{\bf F}_{k}\in\Sym^{k}(\mathfrak{t}^{*})\otimes\Sym^{k}(\mathfrak{t})
, either
{\bf F}_{k}=0
or
\underline{\deg}({\bf F}_{k})=(k,k)
.

Let
N
be the number of roots of
\mathfrak{t}
in
\mathfrak{g}
. Define
d,d^{\vee}\in\mathcal{A}
as

d=\prod_{\alpha>0}\alpha\in\Sym^{N}(\mathfrak{t}^{*}),

where
\alpha
runs over positive roots and

d^{\vee}=\prod_{\alpha^{\vee}>0}\alpha^{\vee}\in\Sym^{N}(\mathfrak{t}).

Moreover we have
d\cdot d^{\vee}\in\Sym^{N}(\mathfrak{t}^{*})\otimes\Sym^{N}(\mathfrak{t})\subset\mathcal{A}
; note that
\underline{\deg}(d)=(N,0)
and
\underline{\deg}(d^{\vee})=(0,N)
.

Proposition 2
.

The product
d\cdot d^{\vee}
divides
{\bf F}_{k}
in
\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

The polynomial
{\bf F}_{k}
is anti-W-invariant, in the sense that for all
w\in W
we have

{\bf F}_{k}({}^{w}\mu,\nu)=\sgn(w)\cdot{\bf F}_{k}(\mu,\nu)={\bf F}_{k}(\mu,{}^{w}\nu).

Therefore the divisibility follows from
[
Hum92
, Proposition 3.13, Page 69]
, taking
V=\mathfrak{t}^{*}\oplus\mathfrak{t}
with reflection group
W\times W
.
∎

Definition 7
.

We put

{\bf F}_{k}^{\prime}=\frac{{\bf F}_{k}}{d\cdot d^{\vee}}\in\mathcal{A}.

By degree considerations,
{\bf F}_{k}=0
when
k<N
. When
k\geq N
, we have
{\bf F}_{k}^{\prime}\in\Sym^{k-N}(\mathfrak{t}^{*})^{W}\otimes\Sym^{k-N}(\mathfrak{t})^{W}
.

When
\mathfrak{g}
is simple, the invariant subspace
\Sym^{2}(\mathfrak{t})^{W}
is spanned by
q_{2}^{\vee}
, and similarly
q_{2}
spans
\Sym^{2}(\mathfrak{t}^{*})^{W}

(See for instance
[
Hum92
, Table 1, page 59]
.)

Proposition 3
.

For
\mathfrak{g}
simple, we have

(9)

{\bf F}_{N}=N!\frac{dd^{\vee}}{d^{\vee}(\delta)},

and

(10)

{\bf F}_{N+2}=\binom{N+2}{2}\frac{q_{2}q_{2}^{\vee}}{\dim\mathfrak{g}}{\bf F}_{N}.

Moreover for
0\leq k<N
and
k=N+1
we have
{\bf F}_{k}=0
.

Proof.

This is a restatement of
[
JS20
, Proposition 5]
.
∎

The same formula holds for

5.2.
Computing
F_{k}
for
k>N+2

From the above we see that computing symmetric polynomials in the roots reduces to calculating the
{\bf F_{k}}
.
In this section we outline how this can be done; we will afterwards illustrate with
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}(\mathbb{C})
.

As before, it is clear that
{\bf F}_{k}^{\prime}
is
W\times W
-invariant, hence lies in the subspace

\left(\Sym^{k-N}(\mathfrak{t})\otimes\Sym^{k-N}(\mathfrak{t}^{*})\right)^{W}=\Sym^{k-N}(\mathfrak{t})^{W}\otimes\Sym^{k-N}(\mathfrak{t}^{*})^{W}.

We may view
W
as a reflection group on
V=\mathfrak{t}
. There is a well-known theory of
W
-invariant polynomials on
V
; see the references on page 86 of
[
Hum92
]
concerning the the problem of finding an explicit basis.

To go a bit further, we show that
{\bf F}_{k}
is invariant under one more operation. Let
\sigma:\mathfrak{t}\to\mathfrak{t}^{*}
be the isomorphism coming from the Killing form. Since the Killing form is symmetric, we have

\langle{}^{\sigma}\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\rangle=\langle{}^{\sigma}\nu_{2},\nu_{1}\rangle
for all
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in\mathfrak{t}
. Moreover
\sigma
is
W
-invariant.

From
\sigma
we obtain an involution
\mathfrak{t}^{*}\oplus\mathfrak{t}\to\mathfrak{t}^{*}\oplus\mathfrak{t}
by

(\mu,\nu)\mapsto({}^{\sigma}\nu,{}^{\sigma^{-1}}\mu)

and this extends to an involution of
\Sym^{*}(\mathfrak{t}^{*}\oplus\mathfrak{t})
, again denoted by ‘
\sigma
’.

Lemma 1
.

The polynomials
{\bf F}_{k}
and
{\bf F}_{k}^{\prime}
are
\sigma
-invariant.

Proof.

For
\nu\in\mathfrak{t}
and
\mu\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
we have

\begin{split}F_{k}({}^{\sigma}\nu,{}^{\sigma^{-1}}\mu)&=\sum_{w}\sgn(w)\langle{}^{w\sigma}\nu,{}^{\sigma^{-1}}\mu\rangle^{k}\\
&=\sum_{w}\sgn(w)\langle{}^{\sigma}\nu,{}^{\sigma^{-1}w^{-1}}\mu\rangle^{k}\\
&=\sum_{w}\sgn(w)\langle{}^{w^{-1}}\mu,\nu\rangle^{k}\\
&=F_{k}(\mu,\nu).\\
\end{split}

(By definition
\langle{}^{w}\mu,\nu\rangle=\langle\mu,{}^{w^{-1}}\nu\rangle
for all
w\in W
.)

The function
\nu\mapsto d^{\vee}({}^{\sigma}\nu)
is a degree
N
anti-
W
-invariant polynomial, and therefore it is a constant multiple of
d(\nu)
, say

d^{\vee}({}^{\sigma}\nu)=cd(\nu)

for some
c\neq 0
. Hence also
d^{\vee}(\mu)=cd({}^{\sigma^{-1}}\mu)
; this gives

d^{\vee}({}^{\sigma}\nu)d({}^{\sigma^{-1}}\mu)=d^{\vee}(\mu)d(\nu).

Finally,

\begin{split}F_{k}^{\prime}({}^{\sigma}\nu,{}^{\sigma^{-1}}\mu)&=\frac{F_{k}({}^{\sigma}\nu,{}^{\sigma^{-1}}\mu)}{d^{\vee}({}^{\sigma}\mu)d({}^{\sigma^{-1}}\mu)}\\
&=F_{k}^{\prime}(\mu,\nu).\\
\end{split}

∎

Here now is a method for explicitly computing the
{\bf F}_{k}
. First, find a basis
b_{1},\ldots,b_{\ell}
of
W
-invariant polynomials on
\mathfrak{t}
, of degree
k-N
.
For
1\leq i\leq j\leq\ell
, let
\beta_{ij}
be the polynomial defined by

\beta_{ij}(\mu,\nu)=b_{i}(\nu)b_{j}({}^{\sigma}\mu)+b_{j}(\nu)b_{i}({}^{\sigma}\mu)

The
\beta_{ij}
form a basis of polynomials on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}\oplus\mathfrak{t}
, which are doubly homogeneous,
W
-invariant of degree
k-N
, and
\sigma
-invariant.
Therefore
{\bf F}_{k}^{\prime}
is some linear combination of the
\beta_{ij}
, the coefficients of which can be determined by a few calculations.

7.4.
Synopsis of Chern Class Calculation

We summarize the above for the reader’s convenience.

Let
G
be a connected complex reductive group, with maximal torus
T
, and fix an integer
k\geq 0
. Let
N
be the number of positive roots of
T
in
G
. There is a polynomial
{\bf c}_{k}\in\mathcal{A}
, whose value at

is the restriction of

to

.
In particular it takes values in

It is necessary to compute the polynomials

for

. This calculation is discussed in Section

is simple, Proposition

and

Next, the power sums

Irreducible representations of

8.1.
Orthogonality

We say that
\pi
is
orthogonal
when there is a symmetric nondegenerate bilinear
G
-invariant form on
V
. An orthogonal linear character is necessarily quadratic, meaning it takes values in
\{\pm 1\}
.

For a representation
(\pi,V)
of
G
, write
S(\pi)
for the direct sum
V\oplus V^{\vee}
, where
V^{\vee}
is the dual of
V
. It is regarded as an orthogonal representation via the bilinear form
B((v,\vartheta),(v^{\prime},\vartheta^{\prime}))=\langle v,\vartheta^{\prime}\rangle+\langle v^{\prime},\vartheta\rangle

We say an orthogonal representation

is

, or an

\pi=S(\sigma)
, where
\sigma
is an irreducible representation which is not orthogonal. In particular, when
G
is connected abelian, then all nontrivial OIRs are of the form
S(\chi)
, where
\chi\neq 1
is a linear character of
G
. Any orthogonal representation decomposes uniquely into a direct sum of OIRs.

Let

(\pi,V)
be a complex orthogonal representation of

Since

\pi:G\to\SO(V)
. Then
\pi
lifts to the corresponding spin group
\Spin(V)
(i.e.,
\pi
is

) iff
w_{2}(\pi)=0
.

Proposition 10
.

Let
k
be a positive integer. Then
w_{k}^{T}(\pi)=0
iff
c_{k}^{T}(\pi)\in 2\HH^{2k}(BT,\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2
and Proposition
9
we have:

\begin{split}w_{k}^{T}(\pi)=0&\Leftrightarrow w_{k}^{T[2]}(\pi)=0\\
&\Leftrightarrow c_{k}^{T}(\pi)\in\ker\varphi.\\
\end{split}

But this kernel is
2\HH^{*}(BT,\mathbb{Z})
.
∎

Corollary 2
.

The representation
\pi
is spinorial iff
c_{2}^{T}(\pi)\in 2\HH^{4}(BT,\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

By Proposition
15
and the above, we have:

\begin{split}\pi\text{ is spinorial}&\Leftrightarrow w_{2}(\pi)=0\\
&\Leftrightarrow w_{2}^{T}(\pi)=0\\
&\Leftrightarrow c_{2}^{T}(\pi)\in 2\HH^{4}(BT,\mathbb{Z}).\\
\end{split}

∎

Combining this with Proposition
4
gives:

Corollary 3
.

When
\mathfrak{g}
is simple,
\pi_{\lambda}
is spinorial iff

\dfrac{\deg\pi_{\lambda}\langle\lambda+2\delta,\lambda\rangle}{4\dim\mathfrak{g}}\cdot q_{2}\in\Sym^{2}_{\mathbb{Z}}X^{*}(T).

Actually, it is only the “

that matters. To be more precise, let
j(\pi)=-\ord_{2}\left(\dfrac{\deg\pi_{\lambda}\langle\lambda+2\delta,\lambda\rangle}{4\dim\mathfrak{g}}\right)
.

Corollary 4
.

When
\mathfrak{g}
is simple,
\pi_{\lambda}
is spinorial iff

2^{-j(\pi)}\cdot q_{2}\in\Sym^{2}_{\mathbb{Z}}X^{*}(T).

Proof.

Write

\frac{\deg\pi_{\lambda}\langle\lambda+2\delta,\lambda\rangle}{4\dim\mathfrak{g}}=\frac{m}{2^{j}n},

with

odd. Note that
\dfrac{m}{2^{j-1}n}q_{2}=c_{2}^{T}(\pi_{\lambda})\in\Sym^{2}(X^{*}(T))
. Let

so that
\frac{1}{2n}=\frac{A}{2}+\frac{B}{n}
.
Then
\pi_{\lambda}
is spinorial iff
\dfrac{m}{2^{j}n}q_{2}\in\Sym^{2}(X^{*}(T))
. But

\begin{split}\frac{m}{2^{j}n}q_{2}&=\frac{m}{2^{j-1}}\left(\frac{A}{2}+\frac{B}{n}\right)q_{2}\\
&=\frac{Am}{2^{j}}q_{2}+Bc_{2}^{T}(\pi_{\lambda}).\\
\end{split}

Hence the criterion is that

are odd, we apply another Bezout identity argument to

, the representation

is spinorial iff for all
\nu\in X_{*}(T)
, the quantity

\dfrac{\deg\pi_{\lambda}\langle\lambda+2\delta,\lambda\rangle}{4\dim\mathfrak{g}}\cdot q_{2}(\nu)

is an integer. But any
homogeneous
quadratic polynomial on
X_{*}(T)
taking integer values lies in
\Sym^{2}X^{*}(T)
by
[
Bou08
, Proposition 2, page 177]
.

9.
Total Stiefel-Whitney Classes

In this section we give an alternate approach to determining
w^{T}(\pi)
for an orthogonal representation
\pi
, when
G
is one of the groups

\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,
\SO_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, or
\Sp_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
. In each of these cases, the Weyl group
W
has a subgroup
\Sigma<W
isomorphic to the symmetric group
\Sigma_{r}
, where
r
is the rank of
T
, and there is an isomorphism

(24)

T\cong(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{r}

of Lie groups which preserves the
\Sigma_{r}
-action.

We will determine
w^{T}(\pi)
as a certain factorization, whose exponents are combinations of character values of
\pi
at elements of order
2
.

9.1.
A Factorization of
w^{T}(\pi)

Recall from Lemma
2
that
T[2]
is a detecting subgroup for the mod
2
cohomology of the maximal torus
T
. So we may describe
w^{T}(\pi)
through its image in the polynomial algebra
\HH^{*}(BT[2],\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})=(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})[v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}]
.

The restriction
\pi|_{T[2]}
is an
S_{r}
-invariant representation of the elementary abelian
2
-group
T[2]
. For
0\leq i\leq r
, write
b_{i}\in T[2]
for the element corresponding to

(\underbrace{-1,\ldots,-1}_{i\text{ times }},1,1,\ldots)
under (
24
).

Example 8
.

For
G=\SL_{3}
, we may take
(
24
)
to be
\diag(a,b,(ab)^{-1})\leftrightarrow(a,b)
. Here
\Sigma\cong\Sigma_{2}
permutes the first two diagonal entries. Hence
b_{0}=\diag(1,1,1)
,
b_{1}=\diag(-1,1,-1)
and
b_{2}=\diag(-1,-1,1)
.

A typical member of
\HH^{1}=\HH^{1}(BT[2],\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
may be expressed as
v=a_{1}v_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}v_{r}
, with
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. Let
|v|
be the number of
i
with
a_{i}\neq 0
.

According to
[
GJ23
, Proposition 2]
, we have

w^{T[2]}(\pi)=\prod_{k=1}^{r}\left(\prod_{v\in\HH^{1}:|v|=k}(1+v)\right)^{m_{k}(\pi)},

where

m_{k}(\pi)=\frac{1}{2^{r}}\sum_{i=0}^{r}A_{i,k}\cdot\chi_{\pi}(b_{i}).

Here
A_{i,k}
is the coefficient of
x^{i}
in the expression
(1-x)^{k}(1+x)^{r-k}
. (See also
[
MS25
, Section 4]
.)

9.2.
Example:
\SL_{2}

For
G=\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
,
b_{1}=\diag(-1,-1)=-1
, and we have

w(\pi)=(1+v)^{m_{1}(\pi)},

where
m_{1}(\pi)=\frac{1}{2}(\deg\pi-\chi_{\pi}(-1))
.

Proposition 11
.

For
G=\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, and
\pi
an IOR of
G
, we have
w(\pi)=1
.

Proof.

When
\pi=\pi_{\ell}
is orthogonal, we must have
\ell
even and
-1
acts trivially, so
m_{1}(\pi)=0
.
∎

On the other hand, for
\ell
odd, we have
m_{1}(S(\pi_{\ell}))=2m_{1}(\pi_{\ell})=2(\ell+1)
, and so

w(S(\pi_{\ell}))=(1+v^{2})^{\ell+1}.

In particular,
w(S(\pi_{1}))=(1+v^{2})^{2}=1+v^{4}
, so
w_{4}(S(\pi_{1}))=v^{4}
. For any
k
, we have
w_{2k}(S(\pi_{\ell}))=\binom{\ell+1}{k}v^{2k}
, and the odd SWCs vanish.

9.3.
Example:
\SL_{3}

Take
G=\SL_{3}(\mathbb{C})
and
T
the diagonal torus.
The irreducible representation
\pi_{m,n}
is orthogonal when
m=n
. Put

\mathcal{D}=(1+v_{1})(1+v_{2})(1+v_{1}+v_{2})=1+v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}+v_{1}v_{2}+v_{1}^{2}v_{2}+v_{1}v_{2}^{2},

as in
[
MS25
, Section 3.4]
.
Then
w^{T[2]}(\pi)=\mathcal{D}^{m(\pi)}
, where

m(\pi)=\frac{1}{4}(\deg\pi-\chi_{\pi}(\diag(-1,-1,1))).

Recall from
[
FH13
, Section 24.2]
that

\chi_{\lambda}(\diag(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}))=S_{\lambda}(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}),

where
S_{\lambda}
is the Schur polynomial. The same reference gives the Giambelli formula for
S_{\lambda}
in terms of complete symmetric polynomials
H_{p}(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})
. So it is enough to compute
H_{p}(1,1,1)
and
H_{p}(-1,-1,1)
. Of course,
H_{p}(1,1,1)=\binom{p+2}{2}
; a combinatorial argument gives that

H_{p}(-1,-1,1)=\left\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\right\rfloor+1.

For
\lambda=(2n,n,0)
, this gives
S_{\lambda}(1,1,1)=\deg\pi_{\lambda}=(n+1)^{3}
and

S_{\lambda}(-1,-1,1)=\begin{cases}n+1,&\text{ $n$ even}\\
0&\text{ $n$ odd}\\
\end{cases}.

Therefore

m(\pi_{n,n})=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{4}(n+1)^{3},&\text{ when $n$ is odd}\\
\frac{1}{4}n(n+1)(n+2),&\text{ when $n$ is even}\\
\end{cases}.

For example, let
\pi
be the adjoint representation. Then
n=1
and
m(\pi)=2
. Hence
w^{T[2]}(\pi)=\mathcal{D}^{2}
, and

w_{4}^{T[2]}(\pi)=v_{1}^{4}+v_{2}^{4}+v_{1}^{2}v_{2}^{2}\neq 0.

Next we compute
w(S(\pi))
for
\pi=\pi_{m,n}
. Note that
\chi_{S(\pi)}(g)=2\chi_{\pi}(g)
whenever
\chi_{\pi}(g)
is real.
A similar calculation to the above gives

\chi_{\pi_{m,n}}(-1,-1,1)=\begin{cases}0\quad&\text{ if }m\text{ odd and }n\text{ odd }\\
\\
-\dfrac{m+1}{2}\quad&\text{ if }m\text{ odd and }n\text{ even }\\
\\
-\dfrac{n+1}{2}\quad&\text{ if }m\text{ even and }n\text{ odd }\\
\\
\dfrac{m+n}{2}+1\quad&\text{ if }m\text{ even and }n\text{ even }\\
\\
\end{cases}

so that

m(S(\pi_{m,n}))=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{4}(m+1)(n+1)(m+n+2)&\text{if $m$ odd and $n$ odd }\\
\frac{1}{4}(m+1)((n+1)(m+n+2)+1)&\text{ if $m$ odd and $n$ even}\\
\frac{1}{4}(n+1)((m+1)(m+n+2)+1)&\text{if $m$ even and $n$ odd}\\
\frac{1}{4}(m+n+2)((m+1)(n+1)-1)&\text{if }m\text{ and }n\text{ are even.}\\
\end{cases}

Remark 6
.

Note in all cases
m(\pi)
is even; this is necessary because
G
is simply connected and so

w_{2}^{T[2]}(\pi)=m(\pi)(v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2}+v_{1}v_{2})

must vanish.

9.4.
Remark on Total Chern Classes

In this section, we outline an analogue of the above procedure for computing total Chern Classes, in the case of
G=\GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
for simplicity of notation.
The restriction of a representation
\pi
of
G
to its diagonal torus
T
is invariant under the Weyl group, and we may try to exploit this to understand the total CC of
\pi
.

So let
T=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
, and
\underline{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Define
\omega_{\underline{m}}\in X^{*}(T)
by

\omega_{\underline{m}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=a_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots a_{n}^{m_{n}}.

We put

\sigma_{\underline{m}}=\sum_{\underline{m}^{\prime}}\omega_{\underline{m}^{\prime}},

where
\underline{m}^{\prime}
runs over the
S_{n}
-orbit of
\underline{m}
.
Then the indecomposable
S_{n}
-invariant algebraic representations of
T
are precisely the
\sigma_{\underline{m}}
with
m_{1}\leq\cdots\leq m_{n}
.
We have

c(\sigma_{\underline{m}})=\prod_{\underline{m}^{\prime}}\left(1+\underline{m}^{\prime}\cdot t\right),

where the product runs over the
S_{n}
-orbit of
\underline{m}
.

Returning to
\pi
, knowing the multiplicity of each
\sigma_{\underline{m}}
in the restriction of
\pi
to
T
would give a product formula for the total CC.
Indeed,

(25)

c(\pi)=\prod_{\underline{m}}c(\sigma_{\underline{m}})^{[\sigma_{\underline{m}},\pi]},

where the product runs over increasing
\underline{m}
.

Two versions of character orthogonality provide formulas, in principle, for these multiplicities. By restricting to the maximal compact abelian subgroup
(S^{1})^{n}<T
, orthogonality gives:

[\sigma_{\underline{m}},\pi]=\int_{(S^{1})^{n}}\chi_{\pi}(t)\overline{\omega_{\underline{m}}(t)}dt.

For another version, let
A_{N}
be the cyclic subgroup of
T
generated by
a_{N}=(\zeta_{N},\zeta_{N^{2}},\ldots,\zeta_{N^{n}})
.
Then

[\sigma_{\underline{m}},\pi]=\frac{1}{N^{n}}\sum_{a\in A_{N}}\chi_{\pi}(a)\overline{\omega_{\underline{m}}(a)},

for sufficiently large
N
. When
\pi
is irreducible with highest weight
\lambda
, we may take
N\geq 2|\lambda|
. For (
25
), only needs to compute this multiplicity when
|\underline{m}|\leq 2|\lambda|
.

10.2.
Two Kinds of Stiefel-Whitney Classes

Let
G
be a Lie group. When
V
is a real vector space, and
\pi:G\to\GL(V)
is a homomorphism, we say
(\pi,V)
is a
real representation
of
G
. In this case, SWCs
w_{k}(\pi)
have been defined in
[
Ben91
, Section 2.6]
, by means of the more well-known theory of SWCs of real vector bundles.

When
G
is compact, real representations of
G
are equivalent to complex orthogonal representations of
G
through complexification
[
BtD03
, Chapter II, Section 6]
.
However this may fail for
G
noncompact: for example the standard representation of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
does not admit an invariant nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form and the standard representation of
\Or_{n}(\mathbb{C})
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
does not admit a real form.

Nonetheless, in this section, we define SWCs
w_{k}^{\Or}(\pi)\in\HH^{k}(BG,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
for
complex orthogonal
representations of
G
in a natural way. Proofs have been omitted for brevity.

Let
\Or_{n}
be the usual compact orthogonal group (matrices with real entries whose transpose is their inverse).
Inside
\HH^{*}(\BO_{n},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
is
w=1+w_{1}+\cdots+w_{n}
, with each
|w_{k}|=k
. (See for instance
[
Ben91
, Section 2.6]
for a precise definition of
w_{k}
.)

Write
\iota^{\mathbb{R}}:\Or_{n}\hookrightarrow\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
and
\iota^{\Or}:\Or_{n}\hookrightarrow\Or_{n}(\mathbb{C})
for the inclusions. Since they are homotopy equivalences, the induced maps

B\iota_{\mathbb{R}}
and
B\iota^{\Or}
induce isomorphisms on mod
2
cohomology by Proposition
12
. Write
d^{\mathbb{R}}:\HH(\BO_{n},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\to\HH^{*}(\BGL_{n}(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
and
d^{\Or}:\HH(\BO_{n},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\to\HH^{*}(\BO_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
for their inverses.
Put
w^{\mathbb{R}}=d^{\mathbb{R}}(w)
and
w^{\Or}=d^{\Or}(w)
.

Let
(\pi,V)
be a real representation of a Lie group
G
, of degree
n
. Picking a basis of
V
gives a homomorphism
\pi^{\prime}:G\to\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
. We define

w(\pi):=(B\pi^{\prime})^{*}(w^{\mathbb{R}}).

Since a different choice of basis amounts to conjugation in
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, we see that
w(\pi)
is independent of this choice.

If a
complex
representation
(\pi,V)
of
G
has a real structure
\sigma
(in the sense of
[
BtD03
, page 93]
), then
\pi
restricts to a real representation
(\pi_{\sigma},V^{\sigma})
of
G
. We may define

w^{\mathbb{R}}(\pi):=w(\pi_{\sigma}),

as it is independent of the choice of real structure.

Let
V
be a complex vector space and
\Phi
a symmetric nondegenerate bilinear form. Let
\Or(V,\Phi)
be the isometry group of
\Phi
.
By picking an orthogonal basis, we obtain an isomorphism
\Or(V,\Phi)\cong\Or_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. We may define
w^{\Phi}\in\HH^{*}(\BO(V,\Phi),\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
to be the pullback of
w^{\Or}
under this isomorphism. Since picking a different orthonormal basis amounts to conjugation in
\Or_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, the class
w^{\Phi}
is independent of this choice. One checks that for
y\in\GL_{\mathbb{C}}(V)
, we have

\Int(y)^{*}(w^{\Phi})=w^{y^{T}\Phi y}.

A complex representation
(\pi,V)
of
G
is
orthogonal
, provided
V
admits a
G
-invariant symmetric nondegenerate bilinear form
\Phi
. Thus
we have
\pi:G\to\Or(V,\Phi)
. In this case define

w^{\Phi}(\pi):=\pi^{*}(w^{\Phi}).

In fact, if
V
admits two such forms
\Phi,\Phi^{\prime}
, then
w^{\Phi}(\pi)=w^{\Phi^{\prime}}(\pi)
; thus we may pick one and simply define
w^{\Or}(\pi)=w^{\Phi}(\pi)
.

Theorem 5
.

If
V
admits both a
G
-invariant real structure
\sigma
, and a
G
-invariant symmetric nondegenerate bilinear form

, then

In the main body of this paper, we drop the superscript ‘

for

.
Similarly we have
w_{k}(\pi)
by considering the

. We state some basic properties of these SWCs, omitting the proof:

For a Lie group
G

Incidence of intracerebral hemorrhage in Shangdong province, East China, 2012-2021: a population-based study
Authors: Bingyin Zhang, Zilong Lu, Danru Liu, Jie Ren, Chunxiao Xu, Aiqiang Xu, Xiaolei Guo, Liwen Fang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) incidence is poorly documented in Asia, where it is highest globally. We examined ICH incidence in Shandong Province, East China, using a population-based stroke surveillance system established in 2012, aiming to inform ICH prevention in East China. We obtained the ICH data from Shandong Province in eastern China, covering a period of ten years from 2012 to 2021. Crude, age-standardized, age-specific, gender-specific, and region-specific incidence were calculated.Seasonal trend was assessed and Joinpoint regression was used to compute the Annual Percent Change (APC) in incidence.The life table method was used to estimate ICH early onset probability among residents aged 30–54 years. Spatial autocorrelation analysis was used to identify ICH hot spots. A total of 96,978 cases of ICH were reported. Crude ICH incidence rose from 57.96 to 76.54 per 100,000 (2012 - 2018; APC=4.48%[95%CI:2.66-6.32], P < 0.001), then dropped nonsignificantly to 69.14 (2018 - 2021; APC=- 2.61%[-7.53-2.57], P = 0.250). Males, rural areas, and those ≥45 years old had higher incidence. The probability of early onset (aged 30 - 54 years) increased from 0.75% to 1.11% (2012 - 2018; APC=6.36%[2.24-10.65], P=0.010), then decreased nonsignificantly. Significant seasonal variation was confirmed (P<0.001), with peak observed in January (accounting for 10.86% of all cases) and trough in July (accounting for 6.62% of all cases). We also found ICH hot spots in northern (Liaocheng, Dezhou, Binzhou), western (Heze), and southern (Linyi) regions of Shandong Province in 2021. Over 10 years, ICH incidence in Shandong Province shifted from increasing to stable and was more higher in males, rural areas, and those ≥45 years old, with seasonal peaks in winter. The identification of spatial clusters in specific regions underscored the need of targeted prevention strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-31766-9.Keywords: Intracerebral hemorrhage, Epidemiology, Incidence, Seasonal characteristics, The probability of early onset, Spatial autocorrelation analysisSubject terms: Diseases, Health care, Medical research, Neurology

Introduction

Intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) is characterized by the spontaneous rupture of cerebral blood vessels, with high mortality and disability rates, presenting a substantial threat to people’s life.Compared to ischemic stroke, ICH is more likely to lead to hospital admission and death, with acute - phase case fatality rates reaching 40% - 50%1. Most survivors are functionally dependent upon discharge2,3.The long - term mortality rates at 1 - year and 5 - year follow - up are approximately 55% and 70% respectively, with a high risk of continuous cognitive decline and a reduced quality of life in these patients4–8

As the most lethal stroke subtype, ICH poses a major health challenge globally, with its burden continually increasing9,10. ICH constitutes 10% to 30% of all strokes, with variations attributed to regional, racial, and lifestyle factors11,12. Notably, the proportion of ICH is nearly twice as high in low- to upper-middle-income countries compared to high-income countries10. Asian populations exhibit the highest ICH incidence rates, ranging from 40.8 to 51.8 per 100,000 person-years11

In China, stroke affects approximately 2 million people annually, with 20% attributed to ICH. This high incidence of ICH is associated with population aging and prevalent risk factors such as hypertension13. Studies have shown that a high - salt diet is one of the most significant risk factors for hypertension14. Shandong Province, located on China’s eastern coast, is well - known for its high salt consumption15. Research indicated that the average daily salt intake per capita among Shandong residents is 13.7g, which is much higher than the 5 g recommended by the World Health Organization16. Meanwhile, the hypertension prevalence among Shandong residents (23.4%) is also higher than the national average(23.2%), which may increase the incidence of ICH among Shandong residents17.

In Shandong, various public health initiatives, including efforts to address cardiovascular risk factors, such as the action on salt reduction and hypertension have been implemented18.Population-based ongoing stroke registries are severely lacking in low-income and middle-income countries, with most countries lacking original, high-quality population-based stroke epidemiological studies19.We collected the incidence data of ICH of residents in Shandong Province from 2012 to 2021, analyzed the incidence of different genders, age groups, urban and rural residents,trend in the past ten years,seasonal characteristics, and detected the hot spots with high incidence through spatial autocorrelation analysis. The aim is to deepen the understanding of the distribution characteristics and epidemic trends of ICH in Shandong Province, and ultimately provide information for the development of customized ICH prevention and control strategies.

Materials and methods

Study area

Shandong Province (34°22′−38°27′N, 114°47′−122°43′E), colloquially referred to as “Lu”,is situated along China’s eastern coast in the lower Yellow River basin.The Province, whose capital city is Jinan,has jurisdiction over 16 prefecture-level cities and 136 county-level administrative units (58 urban areas, 26 county-level cities, and 52 counties).The province’s topography features three distinct zones: mountainous central highlands, the flat Yellow River alluvial plains in the northwest, and rolling hills dominating the eastern Shandong Peninsula and southern regions. Characterized by a warm-temperate monsoonal climate with pronounced marine influences, the region experiences four distinct seasons featuring concentrated summer rainfall with concurrent heat, and dry, sunny winters.Encompassing 158,000 km2, Shandong supported a population of 101.53 million as of 2021, generating a GDP of 8.29 trillion yuan. As one of China’s economically robust and developed coastal provinces, Shandong exhibits typical characteristics of China’s national economic disparity, with the eastern coastal areas experiencing more rapid economic development than the central and western regions, a trend common throughout the country. To some extent, Shandong Province reflects a microcosm of China. To date, in China, only a few provinces, including Shandong, have implemented a comprehensive provincial stroke surveillance system.Compared to other provinces, Shandong started this initiative earlier and maintains a higher standard of quality.

Data sources

A total of ten years’ data on county-specific incidence of ICH from 2012 to 2021 were obtained from the stroke registry system operated by the Shandong Center for Disease Control and Prevention(CDC),encompassing outpatient, emergency, inpatient cases, and out-of-hospital deaths. Outpatient, emergency and inpatient cases were documented by the clinicians responsible for the patient’s care. Patient admissions to all medical institutions in Shandong province, such as private clinics, community health service centers, and public hospitals, with a diagnosis of ICH are obliged to be reported to this registry system. In this stroke registry system, registry certificates are based on information provided by the attending physician who make a diagnosis for each patient based on the patients’ symptoms, inquiries, complaints, and results of medical inspections, and diagnosis is categorized according to the International Classification of Diseases version 10 (ICD-10) code of I61. In addition, each patient was asked to report when his/her clinical symptom occurred and then the date of symptom occurrence was recorded as the incidence date. Then, the registry certificates will be reported to the registry system in real time. While out-of-hospital deaths caused by ICH were reported by qualified physicians from township health clinics after investigation according to the medical certificate of death.In accordance with the definition from the WHO Multi-Country Study on Trends and Determinants of Cardiovascular Disease, the duration of ICH was defined as 28 days following acute onset. Recurrence and re-hospitalization after 28 days were recorded as new onset events, whereas recurrences within 28 days were not considered new events20.

The epidemiological characteristics of ICH were analyzed using the data from the 19 stroke registry countries that started the registration work earliest, which included Huaiyin District and Laiwu District of Jinan city, Zhangdian District and Yiyuan County of Zibo city, Xuecheng District of Zaozhuang city, Fushan District and Penglai District of Yantai city, Wendeng District of Weihai city, Bincheng District of Binzhou city, Shouguang County and Gaomi County of Weifang city, Zoucheng County of Jining city, Feicheng County of Tai city, Junan County of Linyi city, Wucheng County and Leling County of Dezhou city, Gaotang County of Liaocheng city, Peony District and Chengwu County of Heze city. In this study, municipal districts were classified as urban areas, and counties were classified as rural areas. By 2021, the 19 provincial registration points covered a population of 14,317,704 (including 7,229,878 men, 7,087,826 women, 6,425,532 urban residents and 7,892,172 rural residents), accounting for 14.1 percent of the total population of Shandong province. Population data are from the China Disease Prevention and Control Information System.We collected the ICH incidence data of 136 counties in Shandong Province in 2021 when analyzing spatial characteristics.

Quality control

To ensure the quality of the registry certificates reported by clinicians in medical institutions, a multi-level review process was implemented. First, the registry certificates were reviewed by staff in charge of stroke registration at the public health department of the medical institution.Second, information on each certificate was validated by professionals in local county CDC within a limit of 7 days, who also check for completeness and coding and quality control of all data is regularly performed. Third, the provincial, municipal, and county-level CDC regularly provided training and technical guidance to the stroke registration staff of medical institutions. They also conducted investigations of missing reports and case reviews to ensure the reliability and integrity of the ICH data. The missing report rate was required to be ≤10%, and the recording accuracy rate was required to be ≥95%.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive analysis

The crude incidence rates (CRI) of ICH were calculated as the annual number of new cases per 100,000 person-years. To calculate age-specific incidence rates, the study population was stratified into the following age groups: <35, 35–44, 45–54, 55–64, and ≥65 years.To facilitate trend analysis and comparison, we calculated age-standardized incidence rates (ASIRs) using two standard populations: the 2010 Chinese national census (ASIRC) and the World Segi’s standard population (ASIRW).The ASIRC was used to facilitate comparisons with other studies within China, while the ASIRW was provided to allow for comparisons with international literature.The age of ICH onset did not follow a normal distribution assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test and was therefore expressed as median with interquartile range [M(Q1, Q3)] and Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to perform two-sample comparisons.Joinpoint regression analysis was employed to assess the trends in ICH incidence and age of onset over time, with the Annual Percent Change (APC) calculated accordingly. The incidence trend was analyzed using a log-linear model, while the trend in age of onset was analyzed using a linear model. Seasonal trends of ICH incidence were examined using the stl() function in R (version 4.1.3).In addition,the statistical significance of seasonal variation was assessed using the Kruskal-Wallis H test for comparing median monthly incidence number.All statistical tests were two-sided, with a significance level set at P < 0.05.

Calculation of early onset probability

The probability of early-onset ICH was calculated for the age range of 30–54 years, representing the cumulative probability (risk) of experiencing at least one ICH event between the ages of 30 and 54 years. This range was selected for two reasons: firstly, it follows the standard convention for assessing premature mortality from major chronic diseases, which begins at age 30; and secondly, the upper limit of 54 years aligns with the common epidemiological definition of ‘young stroke’ (under 55 years of age), facilitating comparisons with other studies in the field.The life table method was to calculated using the incidence rate of ICH of people aged 30 to 54 years, as shown in the following formula: (1) Incidence of ICH in age group (5-year age group) 5Mx: 5Mx= number of cases of ICH in age x and (x+5) years/total population between age x and (x+5) years; (2) For each 5-year age group, the probability of ICH was 5qx: 5qx =5Mx×5/(1+5Mx×2.5); (3) The probability of early-onset ICH in 30 ~ 54 years old was 25q30∶25q30 = 1-s.

Spatial autocorrelation analysis

ArcGIS 10.8 software was used to visualize the incidence of 136 counties in 2021 and for global and local spatial autocorrelation analysis. Global spatial autocorrelation was used to determine whether there was spatial clustering in the incidence of ICH. The calculated index was Moran’I coefficient, with a value range [−1, 1]. When Moran’I > 0, it indicates that there is a positive spatial correlation, and the closer the value is to 1, the stronger the spatial aggregation is. When Moran’I < 0, it indicates that there is a spatial negative correlation, and the closer the value is to −1, the greater the spatial difference. When Moran’I=0, it represents there is no spatial correlation. Local spatial autocorrelation was used to further identify hot spots. In this study, Getis-Ord Gi* statistic was used to identify hot spots and cold spots with statistical significance. If the value of Gi* was positive, indicated that the regions were a spatial aggregation with high values, called a hot spots. A negative Gi* value indicated that the regions were a spatial cluster of low values, called a cold spots. |Gi* =3 | reflect the statistical significance of 99%CI, |Gi* =2| reflect the statistical significance of 95%CI, |Gi* =1| reflect the statistical significance of 90%CI. When Gi*=0, factor clustering had no statistical significance.A first-order Queen contiguity spatial weights matrix was constructed to define neighbor relationships for the spatial autocorrelation analyses (Global Moran’s I and Getis-Ord Gi). Under this definition, two geographic units were considered neighbors if they shared any common border or vertex.

Results

Basic information

A total of 96,978 cases of ICH were reported by the 19 stroke registries in Shandong province from 2012 to 2021. Among which, 55,036 cases were males, accounting for 56.75% of all cases, 41,942 cases were females, accounting for 43.25% of all cases. 91,362 cases occurred in people over 45 years old, accounting for 94.21% of all cases, and 54,238 cases occurred in people over 65 years old, accounting for 55.93% of all cases. See Figure S1.

Trends of the incidence of ICH from 2012 to 2021

The CRI of ICH exhibited significant temporal variations. From 2012 to 2018, the CRI of ICH increased significantly from 57.96 to 76.54 per 100,000, with an APC of 4.48% (95%CI:2.66–6.32.66.32, P < 0.001). From 2018 to 2021, the CRI decreased slightly from 76.54 to 69.14 per 100,000, however, this decline was not statistically significant (APC = −2.61%[−7.53-2.57], P = 0.250).

In subgroup analyses, the CRI trends varied by sex and region. In males, the CRI of ICH rose significantly from 63.62 to 84.38 per 100,000 between 2012 and 2019 (APC = 3.94%[2.48–5.43.48.43], P < 0.001) and remained stable thereafter. In females, the CRI increased significantly from 52.20 to 68.32 per 100,000 between 2012 and 2018 (APC = 4.57%[2.81–6.36.81.36], P < 0.001), followed by a non-significant decline. In urban areas, the CRI increased from 42.18 to 63.41 per 100,000 between 2012 and 2014 (APC = 17.94%[−3.44-44.05], P =0.087) and remained stable thereafter. In rural areas, the CRI significantly increased from 68.78 to 84.87 per 100,000 between 2012 and 2018 (APC = 4.31%[1.88–6.80.88.80], P = 0.006), with a non-significant decline thereafter.

Overall, the CRI of ICH were higher in males and rural areas. In contrast, the age-standardized incidence rates remained stable across all subgroups from 2012 to 2021. Specifically, the ASIRC and the ASIRW showed no significant changes over the study period.See Figure 1 and Table S1 for detailed results.

Age-specific incidence

The incidence of ICH increased with age. Specifically, the incidence was relatively low among individuals aged <45 years, with a sharp increase observed thereafter. Statistically significant increases in ICH incidence were noted in the <35 years (P < 0.001) and 35–44 years age groups (P = 0.003). The 45–54 years age group experienced a significant rise in ICH incidence before 2018 (P = 0.010), followed by a non-significant decline thereafter. Similarly, the 55–64 years age group exhibited a significant increase before 2019 (P = 0.003), with a non-significant decrease observed after 2019 (P = 0.128). In contrast, the ≥65 years age group demonstrated a statistically significant decrease in ICH incidence from 2012 to 2021 (APC= −3.68%, P = 0.005). This pattern was consistent for both males and females.See Figures 2 and 3, and Table S2 for detailed results.

Seasonal characteristic of ICH

The monthly distribution of ICH cases exhibited distinct seasonal patterns. The overall trend showed a decline in cases from January to July, followed by an increase from August onwards, with a notable low point observed between June and September.The highest number of ICH cases occurred in January, accounting for 10.86% of all cases, while the lowest number was observed in July, representing 6.62% of all cases. Discussion

According to the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2019, 27.9% (3.41 million) of the 12.2 million global incident strokes were due to ICH.[10] Although age-standardized ICH rates have declined, the proportion of incident strokes attributed to ICH in low- to upper-middle-income countries was nearly double that in high-income countries (29.5% vs. 15.8% in 2019)21. This disparity may be linked to the high burden of hypertension in these settings21. However, a lack of population-based stroke registries in many such countries, including China, has limited detailed trend analysis. Existing Chinese studies often rely on age-specific surveys or GBD estimates rather than comprehensive registry data22–26. Our study therefore provides valuable evidence from a decade of population-based stroke registry data in eastern China.

Compared with other epidemiological reports, the age-standardized incidence of ICH in Shandong Province (ranging from 50.77 to 59.53 per 100,000 person-years during 2012–2018) was notably higher than corresponding rates reported from Tianjin, China (27.55–35.52 per 100,000 for a similar period)27and from Germany (34.8–35.4 per 100,000 for 2012–2021)28. This discrepancy may be attributed to several factors, including regional differences in the prevalence and control of hypertension, traditionally higher dietary salt intake in Shandong, and potential variations in case ascertainment completeness between registry systems.Our study observed that while the crude incidence of ICH transitioned from an initial increase to a stable trend over the past decade, the age-adjusted incidence remained largely stable. The stabilization during the later study years (2018–2021) may be explained by several concurrent developments: diagnostic gains from the widespread adoption of CT scanning likely plateaued; province-wide public health initiatives, such as salt-reduction campaigns and improved primary care management of hypertension, may have begun to offset underlying risk29,30; and the potential influence of the COVID-19 pandemic on healthcare-seeking behavior cannot be excluded. Further studies incorporating detailed data on risk factor control are needed to verify these contributing factors.

Our study confirmed a higher ICH incidence in males than females in Shandong, with an earlier onset age in males by 4–5 years, consistent with prior reports10,11. This disparity may stem from lifestyle factors, as males exhibit higher rates of smoking, alcohol use, physical inactivity, and obesity, contributing to a greater burden of hypertension3132., In contrast, estrogen in females confers cardiovascular protection by improving lipid profiles, stabilizing endothelial function, and inhibiting platelet aggregation, thereby reducing atherosclerosis and thrombosis risks3334., These findings underscore the need for ICH prevention strategies that specifically target males, particularly those with unhealthy lifestyles.

The incidence of ICH increased markedly after age 45. Notably, while rates declined among those ≥65 years, an upward trend was observed in individuals under 45, and rates in the 45–64 group began to decrease after 2018. This pattern suggests a persistent burden in middle-aged and older adults alongside a trend toward younger onset, possibly linked to rising untreated hypertension in those aged 40–7035. Strengthened screening and management of hypertension from age 45 onward are crucial to reducing ICH in this population. While cerebrovascular malformations are a leading cause of ICH under age 40, the reasons for the increasing incidence in young adults in Shandong require further investigation to inform targeted prevention36.

ICH in young adults carries distinct social and economic implications37. In Shandong, the cumulative probability of ICH between ages 30–54 was 0.75%–1.11%, with males having 1.47–2.21 times the risk of females, a disparity attributable to biological, behavioral, and societal factors38. Incidence was higher in rural versus urban residents, though this gap is narrowing, potentially reflecting improved rural healthcare and health education39. These urban-rural disparities are consistent with known differences in hypertension prevalence and stroke risk factors40–42. Spatial analysis identified high-incidence clusters in less developed counties in northern, western, and southern Shandong, suggesting influences of regional economic development and lifestyle/dietary patterns43.

A clear seasonal pattern was observed, with ICH incidence peaking in January and reaching its lowest point in July. This aligns with a German study reporting higher ICH incidence in spring and winter44. As low temperature is a known risk factor for cardiovascular events45, enhanced public awareness, protective measures for the elderly during cold weather, and optimized emergency resource allocation in winter are recommended to mitigate ICH risk and associated mortality.

This study has several limitations. First, under-ascertainment is possible in any population-based registry. Underreporting may have been more pronounced in the initial years of surveillance, potentially leading to an underestimation of early incidence rates and a corresponding overestimation of the initial upward trend. Second, delays in identifying fatal ICH cases that occurred outside of hospitals could affect reporting timeliness, despite mandated retroactive reporting procedures to improve data completeness. Third, our definition of early-onset ICH (ages 30–54) excludes adults aged 18–29, which may underestimate the overall burden in young adults. However, our sensitivity analysis for ages 20–54 showed consistent temporal trends, supporting the stability of our primary conclusions regarding secular changes.

Conclusions

In summary, our population-based study indicates that the incidence of ICH in Shandong Province shifted from an increasing to a stable trend over the past decade. ICH was more common in males, rural residents, and individuals aged ≥45 years. The disease exhibited clear seasonal variation, with the highest incidence in winter (peaking in January) and the lowest in summer (trough in July). Spatially, high-incidence clusters were identified in the western, northern, and southern regions of the province.These findings highlight the need for targeted prevention strategies. Public health efforts should prioritize cardiovascular risk factor control—especially hypertension management—among high-risk groups, including middle-aged and older adults, males, rural residents, and populations in the identified geographic hot spots. Such targeted interventions are crucial to further reduce the ICH burden and improve cerebrovascular health in the region.

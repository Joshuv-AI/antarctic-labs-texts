Persistent elevation of serum LBP, IL-23 and IL-6 in infliximab-maintained Crohn's disease: a case–control study from Iraq with the serum LBP/albumin ratio as a novel composite biomarker
Authors: Ahmed Afif Othman, Wasan Sami Hameed
Published: 2026

Introduction

Crohn's disease (CD) is a chronic, transmural, immune-mediated inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) characterised by a relapsing–remitting course, progressive bowel damage and a disproportionate impact on young adults and on health-care systems.1 The worldwide incidence of CD has increased in newly industrialised regions: a systematic review of Arab-country data reported a pooled annual CD incidence of 1.46 per 100,000 person-years, with rising recognition in Saudi Arabia, Lebanon, Kuwait and Egypt.2, 3 Nation-wide Iranian data and West-Asian reviews have documented a several-fold rise in IBD incidence, peaking in young adults.4, 5 Iraq-specific data remain sparse; hospital-based series from Erbil, Duhok and Baghdad describe a young adult, male-predominant cohort with mainly ileal or ileocolonic disease.6, 7 The scarcity of biomarker data from Iraqi CD cohorts represents an important regional research gap.

The pathogenesis of CD reflects a dysregulated mucosal immune response to the gut microbiota in genetically susceptible hosts.1 The interleukin-23 (IL-23)/T-helper 17 (Th17) axis is a central driver of chronic intestinal inflammation: IL-23 stabilises pathogenic Th17 cells through IL-23R signalling, sustains IL-17 A, IL-22 and GM-CSF expression, and has been causally implicated in intestinal pathology.8, 9, 10 IL-6 acts in parallel: through classical and trans-signalling via gp130–STAT3, it drives T-cell survival, acute-phase protein production (including LBP itself) and mucosal inflammation in active CD.11, 12 Lipopolysaccharide-binding protein (LBP), a hepatocyte-derived class-I acute-phase protein, binds the lipid-A moiety of bacterial endotoxin and presents it to CD14/TLR4; circulating LBP therefore serves as a surrogate marker of bacterial translocation across a disrupted intestinal barrier, a feature increasingly recognised as a persistent pathogenic driver in CD.13, 14, 15

Standard non-invasive biomarkers (CRP and faecal calprotectin) have important limitations. CRP is non-specific, is normal in up to 40% of patients with endoscopically active CD, and correlates only weakly with mucosal healing.16 Faecal calprotectin has higher sensitivity but is not universally available and has substantial inter-assay variability.17, 18 Clinical activity indices such as the Harvey–Bradshaw Index (HBI) track symptoms reliably but do not reflect endoscopic or transmural activity.19, 20 A substantial proportion of patients in “clinical remission” on anti-tumour-necrosis-factor (anti-TNF) maintenance have persistent biochemical, endoscopic or imaging activity — a finding with prognostic implications for relapse and bowel damage.21 Accessible serum biomarkers that capture residual inflammation are therefore needed, particularly in low- and middle-income settings. Composite biomarker indices — notably the C-reactive protein/albumin ratio (CAR) — have emerged as useful clinical markers in IBD, combining a positive acute-phase reactant with albumin, a negative acute-phase reactant and marker of nutritional status.22, 23 However, no analogous ratio has been evaluated for LBP.

We therefore performed a cross-sectional case–control study to: (i) compare serum LBP, IL-23 and IL-6 concentrations between patients with active CD, patients on stable infliximab maintenance (IFX-CD) and healthy controls; (ii) evaluate their diagnostic performance (alone and combined with CRP and derived ratios) for discriminating CD from HC; and (iii) evaluate the LBP-to-albumin ratio (LBP/Alb), expected to integrate bacterial-translocation and nutritional signals, as a novel composite biomarker of clinical activity. Our hypothesis was that IL-23, IL-6 and LBP are elevated in CD relative to HC, that they remain elevated during infliximab maintenance consistent with persistent Th17-axis activity and bacterial translocation, and that LBP/Alb correlates more strongly than its individual components with clinical and nutritional indices.

Materials and methods

Study design, setting and ethics

This was a single-centre cross-sectional case–control study. Consecutive adult participants were recruited between 27 October 2025 and 25 March 2026 at the Al-Najaf Gastrointestinal Tract Center (GIT Hospital), Najaf Al-Ashraf, Iraq. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Ministry of health, Najaf Health Directorate, Training and Human Development Center, Reference No. 47875, dated 15 December 2025). All procedures conformed to the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and its later amendments. Written informed consent was obtained from every participant before enrolment. The study is reported in accordance with the STROBE statement for case–control observational studies.24

Participants and group definitions

One hundred and seventeen participants were allocated to three pre-specified groups. Group-specific inclusion criteria were adapted from recent published case–control biomarker studies in CD, notably the PROFILE trial25 and a 2025 Iraqi Crohn's disease biomarker study from Sulaimani.26 Definitions of clinical activity follow the HBI thresholds validated by Best and colleagues.20

Active CD (n = 32). Patients with an established diagnosis of CD who fulfilled all of the following: HBI ≥ 5 at enrolment, at least one objective marker of active inflammation (CRP above the upper laboratory limit of normal of 6 mg/L or endoscopic activity documented within 12 weeks), and no change to maintenance therapy within 4 weeks.

Infliximab-maintained CD (IFX-CD; n = 35). Patients with established CD receiving standard infliximab maintenance (5 mg/kg intravenously every 8 weeks, with dose/interval optimisation as clinically indicated) for ≥12 consecutive months before enrolment, regardless of current clinical activity status. This definition deliberately included patients in clinical remission (HBI ≤ 4) and with residual activity, reflecting the real-world spectrum of long-term infliximab exposure.

Healthy controls (HC; n = 50). Age- and sex-matched adults with no personal or family history of IBD, no gastrointestinal, autoimmune or chronic inflammatory disease, and no intake of anti-inflammatory, immunosuppressive or antibiotic therapy within 4 weeks of enrolment.

Diagnostic, activity and nutritional criteria

CD was diagnosed by the attending gastroenterologist in accordance with the ECCO–ESGAR Guideline for Diagnostic Assessment in IBD, which requires the integration of clinical, biochemical, endoscopic, histological and cross-sectional imaging findings rather than any single reference standard.27 Treatment was delivered and adjusted per the 2024 ECCO Guidelines on Therapeutics in Crohn's Disease.28 Disease activity was scored by the HBI (remission ≤4; mild 5–7; moderate 8–16; severe ≥17).19, 20 Disease location and behaviour were classified by the Montreal classification.29 Nutritional risk was assessed with the Malnutrition Universal Screening Tool (MUST) from BAPEN.30, 31 BMI (kg/m2) was calculated from height and weight measured on a calibrated stadiometer and scale, and classified by WHO cut-offs.

Exclusion criteria

Exclusion criteria (applied to all groups) were: (a) coexistent ulcerative colitis or unclassified/indeterminate IBD; (b) known malignancy (current or within 5 years); (c) pregnancy or lactation; (d) clinically significant active infection within 4 weeks; (e) use of systemic corticosteroids or of immunomodulators other than infliximab (thiopurines, methotrexate, ciclosporin, JAK inhibitors or other biologics) within 4 weeks; (f) chronic liver disease (Child–Pugh ≥ B) or chronic kidney disease (eGFR <45 mL/min/1.73 m2); (g) other active autoimmune disease requiring systemic therapy; (h) bowel surgery within 12 weeks, or the presence of an ostomy or ileoanal pouch. Elevated CRP and unintentional weight loss were not exclusion criteria because both are expected features of the study population.

Sample collection and processing

After an overnight fast (≥ 8 h), 5 mL of peripheral venous blood were collected into a plain serum-separator (SST) tube. Samples were allowed to clot for 30 min at room temperature and centrifuged within 2 h at 3000 ×g for 10 min. Serum was aliquoted into polypropylene cryovials and stored at −80 °C until analysis; freeze–thaw cycles were limited to one. CRP and albumin were measured on the day of collection.

Biomarker measurement

ELISA measurement of IL-6, IL-23 and LBP

Serum IL-6, IL-23 and LBP were quantified by commercial sandwich ELISAs supplied by Bioassay Technology Laboratory (BT Lab; Shanghai Korain Biotech Co. Ltd., Shanghai, China) strictly per the manufacturer's instructions. The kits used were Human Interleukin-6 ELISA Kit (catalogue no. E0090Hu), Human Interleukin-23 ELISA Kit (catalogue no. E0074Hu) and Human Lipopolysaccharide-Binding Protein ELISA Kit (catalogue no. E0360Hu). Intra-assay coefficients of variation were < 8% and inter-assay <10% for each assay. Samples were measured in duplicate; a four-parameter logistic standard curve was fitted on each plate. Absorbance was read at 450 nm within 10 min of reaction stop.

CRP and serum albumin

High-sensitivity CRP was measured by immunoturbidimetry using the CRP-Q kit (REF 7710/7732/7783/7782; Giesse Diagnostics srl, Guidonia Montecelio, Italy) with latex particles coated with goat IgG anti-human CRP at 540 nm (530–550 nm); sensitivity 2 mg/L, linearity to 150 mg/L; reference values <6–8 mg/L. Serum albumin was quantified by the bromocresol-green (BCG) colorimetric method (REF 0027/0027/2/0028; Giesse Diagnostics srl, Guidonia Montecelio, Italy) at 628 nm (620–640 nm); sensitivity 0.1 g/dL, linearity to 6 g/dL; reference values 3.5–5.5 g/dL. Both assays were run on an SMT-120 fully automated random-access biochemistry analyser (Chengdu Seamaty Technology Co. Ltd., Chengdu, China), which uses a halogen lamp with eight selectable wavelengths (340–700 nm), absorbance range 0–4.0 Abs, resolution 0.0001 Abs, reaction temperature 37 °C ± 0.1 °C and throughput 120 tests/h. The Fixed-Time mode was used for both CRP-Q and albumin. Daily two-level commercial quality controls were run before patient samples.

Derived indices

Two derived biomarker ratios were calculated: the CRP/albumin ratio (CAR) = CRP (mg/L) / albumin (g/dL),22, 23 and the LBP/albumin ratio (LBP/Alb) = LBP (ng/mL) / albumin (g/dL), an a priori composite index developed to integrate bacterial-translocation and nutritional signals in this study.

Statistical analysis

A minimum of 30 cases per group was pre-specified, providing ≥80% power (α = 0.05, two-sided) to detect a standardised difference of 0.8. Normality was tested by the Kolmogorov–Smirnov and Shapiro–Wilk tests. Normally distributed variables are presented as mean ± SD; non-normally distributed variables as median (IQR); categorical variables as n (%). Serum IL-6, IL-23 and LBP were non-normal, so between-group differences were analysed by the Kruskal–Wallis H-test; pairwise comparisons used the Mann–Whitney U test with Bonferroni correction for three pairwise contrasts. Categorical variables were compared by χ2 or Fisher's exact test. Correlations were assessed by Spearman's ρ. Diagnostic performance was evaluated by ROC analysis with AUC, 95% confidence intervals from bias-corrected bootstrap resampling (2000 replicates), and optimal cut-offs selected by Youden's J. Multivariable discrimination used L2-regularised logistic regression with 5-fold stratified cross-validation; model fit was reported by McFadden's pseudo-R2. Significance was set at two-sided P < 0.05. Analyses were performed with Python 3.11 (SciPy, scikit-learn, statsmodels), with confirmatory cross-checks in IBM SPSS Statistics v27 and MedCalc v22.

Results

Baseline characteristics of study participants

A total of 117 participants were enrolled: 32 patients with active CD, 35 IFX-CD patients (all ≥12 months maintenance) and 50 healthy controls. Demographic, lifestyle, nutritional and disease-specific characteristics are summarised in Table 1. The three groups were comparable in age (P = 0.162) and sex distribution (P = 0.617) but differed significantly in BMI (P = 0.029) and BMI category (P < 0.001), with underweight status restricted almost exclusively to active CD. Physical activity was progressively reduced across the disease groups (P = 0.002), and appetite impairment and recent weight loss were more frequent in active CD than in IFX-CD or HC (both P < 0.001). MUST-defined high nutritional risk was present in 43.8% of active CD, 11.4% of IFX-CD and 0% of HC (P < 0.001). Elevated CRP was present in 43.8% of active CD and 37.1% of IFX-CD but in no HC (P < 0.001); serum albumin status did not differ significantly between groups (P = 0.253). Within the CD groups, active CD patients had shorter disease duration (9.9 ± 17.3 vs 32.5 ± 16.5 months; P < 0.001) and substantially higher clinical activity (HBI median 8 [IQR 5–9] vs 4 [3–5]; P < 0.001), with moderate activity in 65.6% of active CD versus 0% of IFX-CD. The two CD subgroups did not differ substantially in Montreal disease location or age at onset.

Serum biomarker concentrations across the three groups

Biomarker concentrations are presented in Table 2, and the distributions of LBP, IL-23 and IL-6 are shown in Fig. 1. Kruskal–Wallis omnibus tests demonstrated highly significant between-group differences for all three novel biomarkers (all P < 0.001). After Bonferroni adjustment for three pairwise contrasts, both active CD and IFX-CD patients showed significantly higher LBP, IL-23 and IL-6 concentrations than HC (all adjusted P < 0.001), whereas no pairwise comparison between active CD and IFX-CD reached statistical significance for any biomarker (adjusted P ≥ 0.222). CRP, albumin, CAR and the LBP/albumin ratio each differed significantly between CD groups and HC but did not distinguish active CD from IFX-CD after adjustment. Taken together, these data indicate that serum LBP, IL-23 and IL-6 remain at near active-disease concentrations in patients on long-term infliximab maintenance despite the clinical improvement documented in Table 1.

To address whether persistent biomarker elevation reflected only the subset of infliximab-maintained patients with residual activity, we performed two pre-specified subgroup analyses. First, stratifying the IFX-CD group by HBI status, patients in clinical remission (n = 24) retained significantly higher LBP, IL-23, IL-6 and LBP/albumin ratio than healthy controls (all P ≤ 0.006), with no significant difference from patients with residual activity (n = 11; all P ≥ 0.40). Second, stratifying by mucosal-healing status (Table 4), the 12 patients with documented mucosal healing still showed IL-23 and IL-6 significantly above healthy controls (both P < 0.001), whereas their CRP did not differ from controls (P = 0.669); the mucosal-healing subgroup nonetheless showed lower medians than the non-healing subgroup for all markers, reaching significance for IL-23 (P = 0.014) and borderline significance for LBP (P = 0.050). Diagnostic performance of serum biomarkers

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analyses are reported in Table 3 (Panel A) and visualised in Fig. 2. For discriminating all CD patients (n = 67) from HC (n = 50), IL-6 and IL-23 achieved excellent performance, with AUCs of 0.982 (95% CI 0.962–0.995) and 0.969 (95% CI 0.937–0.993), respectively. At the Youden-optimal cut-off of 54.95 pg/mL, IL-6 provided 91.0% sensitivity and 96.0% specificity; IL-23 at 59.00 pg/mL yielded 89.6% sensitivity and 100% specificity. The LBP/albumin ratio (AUC 0.782; 95% CI 0.695–0.860) and LBP alone (AUC 0.762; 95% CI 0.671–0.845) offered good but more modest discrimination. Albumin, CAR and CRP yielded only fair discrimination (AUC 0.667–0.712). When the two CD subgroups were compared, the novel biomarkers and LBP/albumin ratio retained moderate AUCs (0.702–0.730), whereas CRP, albumin and CAR did not discriminate active CD from IFX-CD (AUC 0.530–0.578; all not significant). To assess optimism in these estimates, AUCs were recomputed by 5-fold stratified cross-validation with standardisation and fitting confined to each training fold. Cross-validated AUCs were closely similar to the apparent values (IL-6 0.998, 95% CI 0.995–1.000; IL-23 0.976, 95% CI 0.945–0.999; LBP/Alb 0.801, 95% CI 0.723–0.873; LBP 0.761, 95% CI 0.675–0.841; combined IL-23 + IL-6 + CRP 0.999, 95% CI 0.995–1.000), indicating limited optimism within this dataset. The very high AUC for IL-6 should nonetheless be interpreted with caution as a feature of this selected case–control sample, and external validation in consecutive, unselected cohorts is required before any diagnostic claim.

Biomarker and clinical correlations in CD patients

In the combined CD cohort (n = 67), Spearman correlation analysis revealed strong positive inter-correlations among the three novel biomarkers (ρ = 0.72–0.86; all P < 0.001), as shown in Fig. 3. All three were positively correlated with CRP and CAR, and inversely correlated with albumin (all P ≤ 0.01). LBP, IL-23 and IL-6 showed significant positive correlations with HBI (ρ = 0.32–0.38; P < 0.01) and MUST score (ρ = 0.36–0.50; P < 0.01), and negative correlations with BMI (ρ = −0.28 to −0.47; P < 0.05). The LBP/albumin ratio displayed the strongest clinical correlations of any biomarker evaluated, with HBI (ρ = 0.41), MUST (ρ = 0.50) and BMI (ρ = −0.46; all P < 0.001). In contrast, CRP, albumin and CAR showed only non-significant trends. The very strong correlations between LBP and the LBP/albumin ratio (ρ = 0.95), and between CRP and CAR (ρ = 0.99), are mathematical consequences of the ratios' construction. To complement the P values, effect sizes with 95% confidence intervals were calculated for the principal comparisons. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression results are presented in Table 3 (Panel B) and Fig. 4. In univariate L2-regularised models with standardised predictors (ORs per 1-SD increase), all seven biomarkers were significant predictors of CD (all P < 0.001). CAR, CRP, the LBP/albumin ratio and LBP yielded more moderate positive associations (ORs 3.46–5.82); albumin was the sole protective predictor (OR 0.38; 95% CI 0.21–0.58). The three-biomarker Model 7 (IL-23 + IL-6 + CRP) produced the best performance (AUC 0.995; McFadden R2 = 0.812; Nagelkerke R2 = 0.900). Discussion

Principal findings

In this Iraqi cross-sectional case–control study of 117 adults, serum LBP, IL-23 and IL-6 were significantly elevated in both active CD and infliximab-maintained CD compared with healthy controls (all P < 0.001 after Bonferroni adjustment). The central and novel observation of this study is that the serum LBP-to-albumin ratio outperformed LBP alone as a clinical correlate of disease activity and nutritional status: LBP/Alb showed numerically the strongest correlations with the HBI (ρ = 0.41), MUST (ρ = 0.50) and BMI (ρ = −0.46; all P < 0.001), and achieved a diagnostic AUC of 0.782 for discriminating all CD from HC. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to evaluate LBP/Alb as a composite biomarker in Crohn's disease, and the first Iraqi report of serum LBP in an IBD population.

Elevated serum LBP in Crohn's disease: mechanism and literature context

LBP is a ∼ 60-kDa class-I acute-phase glycoprotein synthesised predominantly by hepatocytes — and, to a lesser extent, by intestinal epithelial cells — in response to IL-6, IL-1β and TNF-α.32, 33 LBP binds the lipid-A moiety of bacterial lipopolysaccharide (LPS), delivers it to membrane CD14 and the TLR4/MD-2 receptor complex, and thereby initiates the innate immune response.13, 14 Because plasma LPS has a short half-life and is technically demanding to assay, serum LBP serves as the preferred surrogate marker of ongoing bacterial translocation across a disrupted intestinal barrier.34 In CD, increased intestinal permeability and translocation of luminal microbial products into the systemic circulation are recognised drivers of systemic inflammation, which in turn feed back on intestinal tight-junction disruption.15

Our finding of significantly higher serum LBP in active CD (median 9.55 ng/mL) and IFX-CD (7.04 ng/mL) versus HC (5.75 ng/mL) is qualitatively consistent with three key published studies. Pastor-Rojo and colleagues, in a prospective study of 104 CD and 52 UC patients, reported mean LBP of 23.1 ± 13.7 μg/mL in active CD versus 7.2 ± 1.8 μg/mL in HC, with endotoxaemia in 48% of CD patients.13 Lakatos and colleagues subsequently showed in 214 Hungarian CD patients that serum LBP was an independent predictor of clinical relapse at 12 months, outperforming CRP in time-to-flare analyses.35 Most recently (2024), Toris and colleagues in Brussels reported LBP medians of 29.6 μg/mL in CD and 22.8 μg/mL in UC versus 5.8 μg/mL in controls; LBP increased with endoscopic activity and correlated with CRP (ρ = 0.75) and faecal calprotectin (ρ = 0.42), supporting LBP as a promising noninvasive biomarker for disease activity monitoring.36

Absolute LBP concentrations differ substantially between studies because of differences in ELISA platforms, sample dilution protocols, calibration standards and sample handling; our focus is therefore on relative elevation and discriminative performance rather than numerical equivalence with European cohorts. The direction and discriminative pattern of our findings (CD > HC, P < 0.001; active CD approximately 1.7-fold higher than HC) are concordant with the three European studies. These data extend previous observations by providing the first LBP measurements in an Iraqi/Middle Eastern CD cohort and the first comparison in a subgroup maintained on long-term infliximab.

The LBP/albumin ratio as a novel composite biomarker

A growing body of evidence supports composite biomarker ratios in IBD that leverage the opposing directionality of positive acute-phase proteins and negative acute-phase proteins. Qin and colleagues22 and Chen and colleagues23 established the CRP/albumin ratio (CAR) as a useful marker of CD activity; Liu and colleagues subsequently confirmed the accuracy of high-sensitivity CAR for disease activity in IBD.37 The platelet-to-albumin ratio38 and the albumin-to-globulin ratio39 have also been shown to correlate with endoscopic and clinical activity. However, no previously published study, to our knowledge, has combined serum LBP with albumin as a ratio for Crohn's disease.

The biological rationale for LBP/Alb is three-fold. First, LBP reflects ongoing bacterial translocation and acute-phase activation,15, 35 while serum albumin is a validated negative acute-phase reactant that declines with chronic inflammation and gastrointestinal protein loss in CD.22, 40 Combining the two places a bacterial-translocation signal in the numerator over a combined inflammation-plus-nutrition signal in the denominator, so that both axes push the ratio in the same direction during active disease. Second, albumin in CD captures the nutritional dimension of chronic enteropathy, which is not reflected in LBP or CRP alone — clinically relevant because malnutrition is common in CD (43.8% of active CD patients in our cohort were at high MUST risk), predicts complications, and independently influences treatment decisions.19 Third, expressing LBP as a ratio partially normalises assay-related inter-laboratory variability in LBP quantification. We note, however, that in the present cohort the incremental value of LBP/Alb over LBP alone was modest and was driven predominantly by the LBP numerator: albumin varied far less than LBP (coefficient of variation 12.6% vs 35.4%), the ratio correlated almost perfectly with LBP alone (ρ = 0.95), and the gain in correlation with clinical indices was small (HBI ρ = 0.242 vs 0.211; MUST ρ = 0.213 vs 0.204). LBP/Alb is therefore best regarded as an albumin-adjusted LBP index whose practical value lies in partial normalisation of inter-assay LBP variability and the incorporation of nutritional status, rather than in demonstrated statistical superiority over LBP as a standalone marker.

In our data, LBP/Alb showed the strongest correlations with HBI (ρ = 0.41) and MUST (ρ = 0.50) of any biomarker in the panel, and was more strongly related to BMI (ρ = −0.46) than either LBP alone (ρ = −0.33) or albumin. It achieved an AUC of 0.782 (95% CI 0.695–0.860) for discriminating all CD from HC, exceeding CRP (0.667), albumin (0.712) and CAR (0.679). These values position LBP/Alb as a promising accessible composite index of active Crohn's disease integrating three biologically distinct axes — bacterial translocation (LBP numerator), systemic inflammation and nutritional status (albumin denominator) — within a single, simple calculation. A formal DeLong comparison of the two curves showed that the LBP/Alb ratio had a modestly higher AUC than LBP alone (0.803 vs 0.766; difference 0.038; z = 2.59, P = 0.010). Although this difference reached statistical significance, its magnitude is small, and given that the ratio correlates almost perfectly with LBP (ρ = 0.95) we interpret LBP/Alb as offering a modest incremental improvement rather than a substantially superior marker.

Persistence of LBP elevation during infliximab maintenance

A clinically important observation is that in infliximab-maintained patients — a majority of whom were in clinical remission (HBI ≤ 4 in 68.6%) — both LBP and the LBP/Alb ratio remained significantly higher than in HC and did not differ significantly from active disease after Bonferroni correction. This pattern is consistent with the observation by Pastor-Rojo and colleagues that LBP “normalises” after treatment less completely in CD than in UC,13 and with the view that anti-TNF therapy improves but does not fully restore intestinal barrier integrity.15 In the “treat-to-target” paradigm41 — which includes clinical, biochemical and mucosal targets — serum LBP and the LBP/Alb ratio may provide additional information about residual bacterial translocation not captured by CRP or symptom-based indices. Whether LBP or LBP/Alb can predict loss of response to infliximab in our population is an open question that requires prospective longitudinal data. Importantly, this persistence was not confined to patients with residual symptoms. Both the clinical-remission subgroup (HBI ≤ 4) and, more strikingly, the subgroup with documented mucosal healing retained IL-23 and IL-6 concentrations significantly above healthy controls despite normal CRP (Table 4). The presence of a residual serum inflammatory signal even after mucosal healing suggests that these cytokines and the LBP/Alb ratio may capture subclinical activity below the resolution of both CRP and endoscopic assessment, although this requires confirmation with paired faecal calprotectin and longitudinal sampling.

IL-23 and IL-6 in the broader cytokine context

Although the primary novelty is LBP and the LBP/Alb ratio, the secondary findings of markedly elevated IL-23 and IL-6 are biologically informative. Elevated IL-23 reflects activation of the IL-23/Th17 axis, now a validated therapeutic target in CD through risankizumab (ADVANCE/MOTIVATE/FORTIFY), ustekinumab and mirikizumab.42, 43, 44 Elevated IL-6 drives hepatic acute-phase protein synthesis — including LBP itself — via gp130-STAT3 signalling.11, 12 The strong positive intercorrelation among LBP, IL-23 and IL-6 in CD (ρ = 0.72–0.86) but not in HC supports a common inflammatory driver; IL-6 likely contributes directly to LBP elevation. An Egyptian study by Gheita and colleagues reported approximately two-fold elevation of IL-23 in IBD,45 and on-treatment IL-6 reduction predicts biologic response in mixed IBD cohorts. The dissociation whereby CRP normalised during infliximab maintenance while IL-6 and IL-23 remained elevated is mechanistically coherent: hepatic CRP synthesis is a downstream, IL-6-driven acute-phase response that anti-TNF therapy suppresses efficiently, whereas IL-23 and IL-6 lie upstream in axes that infliximab targets incompletely. We interpret these cytokines as consistent with, but not proof of, ongoing pathogenic inflammation. Because we did not measure downstream Th17 effector cytokines (IL-17 A, IL-22, GM-CSF), we refer throughout to IL-23 elevation and IL-23/Th17-axis-associated signalling rather than to demonstrated Th17 pathway activation; serum IL-23 is an upstream driver and cannot, on its own, establish effector-level Th17 activity.46

Comparison with Iraqi and regional studies

No previous Iraqi study has evaluated serum LBP or the LBP/albumin ratio in Crohn's disease. An earlier Iraqi serum cytokine panel study by Al-Abassi and colleagues (Baghdad/Mustansiriya) measured IL-8, IL-12, IP-10 and IL-10 in IBD but did not include LBP or IL-23.47 A 2024 study by Qusay and colleagues (College of Medicine, University of Baghdad) reported CXCL9 elevation in Iraqi UC patients,48 and a 2025 study by Abdulla and colleagues from Sulaimani demonstrated that the lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio was a useful inflammation marker in newly diagnosed Iraqi CD patients.26 A 2024 Iraqi study from Karbala estimated LBP in urinary tract infection patients49 but did not address IBD. Together, these confirm that Iraqi researchers have been developing biomarker programmes in IBD, but that the LBP/albumin ratio in Crohn's disease has not previously been reported from an Iraqi population. A 2023 Saudi consensus guidance addresses IBD but does not specifically address LBP.50 Our data therefore fill a specific regional knowledge gap regarding bacterial-translocation markers in Middle-Eastern CD.

Diagnostic performance of LBP and LBP/Alb in perspective

The AUCs achieved by IL-6 (0.982) and IL-23 (0.969) exceed the 0.65–0.85 commonly reported for CRP and approach the 0.88–0.97 reported for faecal calprotectin in recent meta-analyses.17, 51 Although LBP and LBP/Alb (AUCs 0.762 and 0.782) did not reach these levels, they substantially outperformed CRP alone (0.667) and CAR (0.679). The LBP/Alb ratio combines low-cost measurement (albumin on any biochemistry analyser; LBP ELISA on a standard microplate reader) with good discriminative performance, making it a practical option in resource-limited settings where faecal calprotectin assays are not routinely available. These models were derived and cross-validated on the same single-centre dataset; external validation is essential before any claim of diagnostic generalisability.

Strengths

Strengths of this study include (i) a well-characterised three-group design that explicitly compares active CD, infliximab-maintained CD and matched healthy controls, addressing a specific clinical knowledge gap; (ii) the simultaneous evaluation of a novel composite biomarker (LBP/albumin ratio) alongside established markers and informative cytokines; (iii) rigorous statistical analysis including non-parametric testing with Bonferroni adjustment, bootstrap-based ROC confidence intervals and cross-validated multivariable logistic regression; (iv) use of validated clinical instruments (HBI, MUST, Montreal, WHO BMI); and (v) to our knowledge, the first Iraqi CD study of serum LBP and the first published evaluation of LBP/albumin ratio in CD globally.

Limitations

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the cross-sectional design precludes conclusions about the temporal kinetics of LBP and LBP/Alb, or their ability to predict relapse. Second, this is a single-centre, single-country study; external validation is needed. Third, the sample size (n = 117) is modest, limiting subgroup precision. Fourth, faecal calprotectin was not measured. Fifth, we used the HBI for clinical activity rather than an endoscopic index (SES-CD). Sixth, absolute LBP values measured here are not directly numerically comparable with those reported by European cohorts due to differences in ELISA platforms and sample-dilution protocols, although directional and relative comparisons are robust. Finally, the LBP/Alb analyses were exploratory; formal validation against a pre-specified threshold with confirmation in an independent dataset is the essential next step. Seventh, we lacked endoscopic, histological and faecal-calprotectin correlates, so we cannot exclude that part of the persistent serum signal reflects systemic immune activation unrelated to active mucosal disease rather than intestinal inflammation per se; paired serum–endoscopic sampling with faecal calprotectin is the priority next step. Eighth, downstream Th17 effector cytokines were not measured, limiting mechanistic inference about Th17-axis activation. Ninth, the LBP/albumin ratio's advantage over LBP alone was modest and driven mainly by LBP, and the composite requires prospective validation before its incremental value can be claimed.

Future directions

These findings motivate several follow-up studies: (i) prospective longitudinal sampling of LBP and LBP/Alb during infliximab maintenance to test whether rising LBP/Alb predicts subsequent relapse or loss of response; (ii) validation in independent Iraqi and regional cohorts, including patients on alternative biologics (ustekinumab, risankizumab, vedolizumab); (iii) paired serum–endoscopic and serum–transmural studies correlating LBP/Alb with SES-CD and cross-sectional imaging; (iv) head-to-head comparison with faecal calprotectin and with leucine-rich α2-glycoprotein (LRG)52; (v) cost-effectiveness analysis of LBP/Alb-guided monitoring; and (vi) mechanistic work integrating LBP polymorphisms (e.g. rs2232618) with serum LBP, IL-23R status and gut microbiota profiling.53

Conclusion

In this case–control study of 117 Iraqi adults, serum LBP, IL-23 and IL-6 were markedly elevated in both active Crohn's disease and infliximab-maintained Crohn's disease compared with healthy controls, with IL-6 and IL-23 demonstrating the highest discriminative accuracy (AUC ≥ 0.97 versus HC). Differences between active and infliximab-maintained disease did not reach significance after Bonferroni correction, consistent with persistent subclinical IL-23/Th17-axis activity and bacterial translocation despite infliximab therapy. The LBP-to-albumin ratio — a novel composite biomarker integrating bacterial-translocation and nutritional signals — correlated most closely with clinical activity (HBI) and nutritional risk (MUST) and outperformed CRP and CAR for diagnostic discrimination. These findings should be interpreted with caution given the cross-sectional, single-centre design and modest sample size, but they support the further evaluation of serum LBP, the LBP-to-albumin ratio, IL-23 and IL-6 as accessible biomarkers for monitoring residual inflammation in Crohn's disease, particularly in regional settings where faecal calprotectin and routine endoscopic surveillance are constrained.

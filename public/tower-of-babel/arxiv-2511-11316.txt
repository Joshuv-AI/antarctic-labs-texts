A quantitative Talenti-type comparison result with Robin boundary conditions
Vincenzo Amato, Rosa Barbato, Simone Cito, Alba Lia Masiello, Gloria Paoli

Abstract
The purpose of this paper is to establish a quantitative version of the Talenti comparison principle for solutions to the Poisson equation with Robin boundary conditions. This quantitative enhancement is proved in terms of the asymmetry of domain. The key role is played by a careful analysis of the propagation of asymmetry for the level sets of the solutions of a PDE. As a byproduct, we obtain an alternative proof of the quantitative Saint–Venant inequality for the Robin torsion and, in the planar case, of the quantitative Faber–Krahn inequality for the first Robin eigenvalue. In addition, we complete the framework of the rigidity result of the Talenti inequalities with Robin boundary conditions. Keywords: Laplace operator; Talenti comparison; stability; Robin boundary condition MSC 2020: 35J05, 35J25, 35B35

Introduction

Let
\Omega
be an open, bounded, Lipschitz set in
\R^{n}
, let
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
be a non-identically zero and nonnegative function, and let
\beta
be a positive parameter. We consider the following problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=f\,&\text{in }\Om,\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle{\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\beta u=0}&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1)

and its symmetrized version

\begin{cases}-\Delta v=f^{\sharp}\,&\text{in }\Om^{\sharp},\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle{\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}+\beta v=0}&\text{on }\partial\Omega^{\sharp},\end{cases}

(2)

where
\Omega^{\sharp}
is the ball centered at the origin with the same measure as
\Omega
and
f^{\sharp}
is the Schwarz rearrangement of
f
(see Definition
2.3
). An interesting question is to consider the class of domains
\Omega
with prescribed volume, a given radial function
f^{\sharp}
on
\Om^{\sharp}
and optimize a functional of the type

\mathcal{F}(\Omega):=\dfrac{{\left\|u\right\|}_{X(\Om)}}{{\left\|f\right\|}_{Y(\Om)}},

(3)

where
{\left\|\cdot\right\|}_{X(\Om)}
and
{\left\|\cdot\right\|}_{Y(\Om)}
are suitable norms that have to be specified,
u
solves the Poisson problem (
1
) with a given datum
f
such that its Schwarz rearrangement is
f^{\sharp}
.

In the case of homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, this question is addressed in the classical work of Talenti
[
43
]
. Given an open and bounded set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the author considers the problems

\begin{cases}-\Delta w=f&\text{in}\;\Omega\\
w=0&\text{on}\;\partial\Omega,\end{cases}\quad\quad\begin{cases}-\Delta z=f^{\sharp}&\text{in}\;\Omega^{\sharp}\\
z=0&\text{on}\;\partial\Omega^{\sharp},\end{cases}

with
f\in L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(\Omega)
if
n>2
, or
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
, with
p>1
, if
n=2
. He proves the point-wise inequality

w^{\sharp}(x)\leq z(x)\quad\forall x\in\Omega^{\sharp}.

(4)

From inequality (
4
), it follows that, once the measure of
\Omega
and the rearrangement of
f
are prescribed, the ratio giving
{\left\|w\right\|}_{X(\Omega)}/{\left\|f\right\|}_{Y(\Omega)}
(i.e. the functional in (
3
))
is maximized by the function
z
that solves the Poisson problem on the ball. Notice that, in this case, both norms can be chosen to be any rearrangement-invariant norm.

There are several generalization of this classical result, see, for instance,
[
14
,
44
]
for nonlinear operators in divergence form,
[
3
]
for anisotropic elliptic operators,
[
5
]
for the parabolic case,
[
13
,
46
]
for higher-order operators, and
[
7
]
, where the Steiner symmetrization is used in place of the Schwarz one.

All the above mentioned results refer to Dirichlet boundary conditions, that usually well behave under symmetrization. Actually, for a long time, it was thought that comparison results could not be established through spherical rearrangement arguments in the case of Robin boundary conditions. However, in
[
6
]
the authors prove a comparison involving the Lorentz norms of
u
and
v
, under the assumptions that

is a non-negative function in

and that
\beta
is a positive parameter, thereby obtaining the inequalities

{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{k,1}(\Omega)}\,\leq{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{k,1}(\Omega^{\sharp})}\,\;\forall\,0<k\leq\frac{n}{2n-2},

(5)

and

{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{2k,2}(\Omega)}\,\leq{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{2k,2}(\Omega^{\sharp})}\,\;\forall\,0<k\leq\frac{n}{3n-4}.

(6)

Moreover, in the case
f\equiv 1
, they also prove

{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\sharp})},\quad p=1,2,

and, if
n=2
, the pointwise comparison holds:

u^{\sharp}(x)\leq v(x),\quad\text{ for all }x\in\Omega^{\sharp}.

(7)

This result makes it possible to solve the optimization problem (
3
) when the norm of
u

coincides with one of the norms appearing in inequalities (
5
) and (
6
).
However, differently from the Dirichlet case, in this setting one cannot choose an arbitrary rearrangement-invariant norm, except in the case

f\equiv 1
and
n=2
, where (
7
) holds.

Also for Robin boundary conditions, different generalizations of these comparison results have been studied: see
[
11
]
for the
p
-Laplacian,
[
23
,
42
]
for the anisotropic case,
[
2
,
10
]
for mixed boundary conditions,
[

]
for the Hermite operator.

Once obtained the comparison results in terms of the inequalities above, the next natural step is to characterize the equality case, namely the rigidity of the inequalities. In the case of Dirichlet boundary conditions, the rigidity is proved in
[
4
]
, while, in the case of Robin boundary conditions, the equality case in (

) is characterized in
[

]
, where the authors proved that equality holds if and only if the set
\Omega
is a ball and the functions

are radially symmetric and decreasing.

Nevertheless, the characterization of the equality case in (

]
as an open problem. This is the starting point of our work.
Indeed, the first Theorem that we prove is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\R^{n}
be an open, bounded and Lipschitz set, let

be a nonnegative function. Let

be the solution to (
1
) and let

be the solution to (
2
), if

{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{k,1}(\Omega^{\sharp})}={\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{k,1}(\Omega)},

for some

then, there exists

such that

\Omega=\Omega^{\sharp}+x_{0},\qquad u(\cdot+x_{0})=u^{\sharp}(\cdot),\qquad f(\cdot+x_{0})=f^{\sharp}(\cdot).

Once a comparison and a rigidity result hold, we can inquire whether the comparison can be improved, asking:

if equality
almost
holds in (
5
) and (
6
), is it true that the set
\Omega
is
almost
a ball, and the function

and

are
almost

In the case of Dirichlet boundary condition, a quantitative result of this type was proved in

]
, where the authors show that whenever the comparison (

\theta_{1}=\theta_{1}(n),\theta_{2}=\theta_{2}(n)
and

||z-w^{\sharp}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\geq K_{1}\alpha^{3}(\Omega)+K_{2}\inf_{x_{0}\in\R^{n}}{\left\|w-w^{\sharp}(\cdot+x_{0})\right\|}_{L^{1}(\R^{n})}^{\theta_{1}}\hskip-2.84526pt+K_{3}\inf_{x_{0}\in\R^{n}}{\left\|f-f^{\sharp}(\cdot+x_{0})\right\|}_{L^{1}(\R^{n})}^{\theta_{2}}.

(9)

Moreover, the dependence of
K_{1},\,K_{2}
and
K_{3}
on
{\left|\Omega\right|}
and

is explicit.
In this result, the distance between the functions
u
and
f
from their symmetrized is measured in terms of the
L^{1}
distance, while the distance of

from being a ball is measured via the
Fraenkel Asymmetry
, that is

\alpha(\Omega):=\min_{x\in\R^{n}}\bigg\{\dfrac{|\Omega\Delta B_{r}(x)|}{|B_{r}(x)|}\;,\;|B_{r}(x)|=|\Omega|\bigg\},

(10)

where the symbol
\Delta
stands for the symmetric difference.

The authors in
[
9
]
also derive an analogous estimate for the difference of

L^{p}
-norm of the function, extending to any right-hand side
f
the result proved for

f=1
in
[
37
]
, which reads as follows:

{\left\|z\right\|}_{p}^{p}-\lVert w^{\sharp}\rVert_{p}^{p}\geq K_{4}\alpha^{2+p}(\Omega)\qquad\forall p>1.

(11)

It is also worth noting that in
[
1
]
the authors refine the estimate in (
9
) in the special case where
\Omega
is a ball (so that
\alpha(\Omega)=0
) and the datum

is a characteristic function, obtaining the optimal exponent in

{\left\|z\right\|}_{p}^{p}-\lVert w^{\sharp}\rVert_{p}^{p}\geq K\inf_{x_{0}\in\R^{n}}{\left\|f-f^{\sharp}(\cdot+x_{0})\right\|}_{L^{1}(\R^{n})}^{2}\qquad\forall p>1.

For further generalizations, see also
[
8
,
38
,
40
]
.

The main objective of this paper is to study the stability of (
5
) and (
6
) in the same spirit as the results in
[
9
]
. First of all, we improve inequality (
5
) by adding a reminder term that measures the distance of the set
\Omega
from a ball. This is our second main result

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be an open, bounded, and Lipschitz set, let
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
be a nonnegative function. Let
u
be the solution to (
1
) and let
v
be the solution to (

), then there exists a positive constant
C_{1}=C_{1}({\left|\Omega\right|},{\left\|f\right\|}_{1},\beta,n)
such that, for

,

{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{k,1}(\Omega^{\sharp})}-{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{k,1}(\Omega)}\geq C_{1}\alpha^{2}(\Omega),

(12)

where

C_{1}=\frac{{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{k}+\frac{1}{n}-1}{\left\|f\right\|}_{1}}{\beta n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}}\min\left\{\frac{1}{2^{\frac{1}{k}+5}\gamma_{n}},\frac{\beta{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{n}}}{2^{\frac{1}{k}+3+\frac{2}{n}}n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right\},

is the measure of the unit ball of
\R^{n}
and

is the constant appearing in the quantitative isoperimetric inequality (see Theorem

).

Then, we perform the same analysis for the inequality (

), obtaining the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

be an open, bounded, and Lipschitz set, let

be a nonnegative function.
Let

be the solution to (

) and let

be the solution to (

such that for

where

is the measure of the unit ball of
\R^{n}
and

is the constant appearing in the quantitative isoperimetric inequality (see Theorem

).

Using the above Theorems

and

) and, in dimension 2, the Bossel-Daners inequality for the first Robin eigenvalue (Corollary

]

]

Before giving the last comparison result, we spend a few words about the link with the Dirichlet case, that formally corresponds to the case
\beta=+\infty
. In particular, we focus on the results involving the torsional rigidity and the first eigenvalue. Indeed, it is known that, given
\Omega
a Lipschitz domain, for

the Robin eigenvalues converge to the Dirichlet ones (see, as a reference,
[

, Chapter 4]
) and the Robin torsional rigidity to the Dirichlet one (see
[

]
for the torsion problem), as well. However, we observe that, we cannot recover any additional information about the Dirichlet problem in terms of quantitative estimates if we send

in (

) or (

), since both constants

and

, defined in Theorem

and in Theorem

respectively, go to

.

Anyway, in the special case

and

where the point-wise comparison (

) holds, we obtain a result that resembles (

), and for which the constant does not degenerate as

.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
n=2
and
f\equiv 1
and let
u
,

be the solutions to (
1
) and (
2
) respectively, and let

be the Schwarz rearrangement of

.
Then, there exists a constant

, such that

{||v-u^{\sharp}||_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\sharp})}}\geq C_{3}\alpha^{3}(\Omega),

In all the aforementioned results, we are able to control from above the Fraenkel asymmetry of the set
\Omega
in terms of the corresponding comparison gap, with different powers appearing. In particular, in Theorem

we obtain the exponent

Although Theorem

may look weaker than the previous ones due to the larger exponent, it actually allows us to recover exactly the same quantitative estimate as in the Dirichlet case, since the constant obtained there does not depend on

The essential difference with the Dirichlet case (

[
9
]
were able to obtain a more complete result, also in terms of the distance of the functions

and

from their respective symmetrized. In this regard, notice that the stability proof provided in
[

]
is strongly based on the quantitative Pólya-Szegő principle proved in

, which is not valid for general

The structure of the paper is the following.
In Section

In Section

, we prove the main results of the paper. In Section

2.1
Property of the rearrangement

Let us recall some basic notions about rearrangements. For a general overview, see, for instance,
[
36
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
u:\Omega\to\R
be a measurable function, the
distribution function
of
u
is the function
\mu:[0,+\infty[\,\to[0,+\infty[
defined as the measure of the superlevel sets of
{\left|u\right|}
, i.e.,

\mu(t)={\left|\Set{x\in\Omega\,:\,{\left|u(x)\right|}>t}\right|}.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
u:\Omega\to\R
be a measurable function, the
decreasing rearrangement
of
u
, denoted by
u^{\ast}(\cdot)
, is defined as

u^{*}(s)=\inf\{t\geq 0:\mu(t)<s\}.

Remark 2.1
.

Let us notice that the function
\mu(\cdot)
is decreasing and right continuous, and the function
u^{\ast}(\cdot)
is its generalized inverse.
From Definitions
2.1
and
2.2
, one can prove that

u^{\ast}(\mu(t))\leq t,\quad\forall t\geq 0,

\mu(u^{\ast}(s))\leq s\quad\forall s\geq 0.

Definition 2.3
.

The
Schwartz rearrangement
of
u
is the function
u^{\sharp}
whose superlevel sets are concentric balls with the same measure as the superlevel sets of
u
.

We have the following relation between
u^{\sharp}
and
u^{*}
:

u^{\sharp}(x)=u^{*}(\omega_{n}{\left|x\right|}^{n}),

and one can easily check that the functions
u
,
u^{*}
, and
u^{\sharp}
are equi-distributed, i.e., they have the same distribution function, and there holds

\displaystyle{{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}={\left\|u^{*}\right\|}_{L^{p}(0,{\left|\Omega\right|})}=\lVert{u^{\sharp}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega^{\sharp})}},\quad\text{for all }p\geq 1.

(19)

We also recall the Hardy-Littlewood inequality, an important property of the decreasing rearrangement (see
[
34
]
)

\int_{\Omega}{\left|h(x)g(x)\right|}\,dx\leq\int_{0}^{{\left|\Omega\right|}}h^{*}(s)g^{*}(s)\,ds,

thus, choosing
h(\cdot)=\chi_{\left\{{\left|u\right|}>t\right\}}
, one has

\int_{{\left|u\right|}>t}{\left|g(x)\right|}\,dx\leq\int_{0}^{\mu(t)}g^{*}(s)\,ds.

We now introduce the Lorentz spaces (see
[
46
]
for more details on this topic).

Definition 2.4
.

Let
0<p<+\infty
and
0<q\leq+\infty
. The Lorentz space
L^{p,q}(\Omega)
is the space of those functions such that the quantity:

{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{p,q}}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{q}\mu(t)^{\frac{q}{p}}\,\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}}&0<q<\infty\\[8.61108pt]
\displaystyle{\sup_{t>0}\,(t^{p}\mu(t))}&q=\infty\end{cases}

is finite.

Let us observe that for
p=q
the Lorentz space coincides with the
L^{p}
space, as a consequence of the
Cavalieri Principle

\int_{\Omega}{\left|u\right|}^{p}=p\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{p-1}\mu(t)\,dt.

2.2
The Poisson problem with Robin boundary conditions

Let us now recall some properties of the problems we are studying.

The solutions
u
to problem (
1
) and
v
to problem (
2
) are both superharmonic and, as a consequence of the strong maximum principle, they achieve their minima on the boundary.
If we set

u_{m}=\min_{\Omega}u,\quad v_{m}=\min_{\Omega^{\sharp}}v

the positivity of
\beta
and the Robin boundary conditions lead to
u_{m}\geq 0
and
v_{m}\geq 0
. Hence,
u
and
v
are strictly positive in the interior of
\Omega
.
Moreover, we can observe that

u_{m}=\min_{\Omega}u\leq\min_{\Omega^{\sharp}}v=v_{m},

(20)

since, as a consequence of the weak formulation of (
1
) and (
2
), it holds

\begin{split}v_{m}\text{P}(\Omega^{\sharp})&=\int_{\partial\Omega^{\sharp}}v(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}f^{\sharp}\,dx=\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{\Omega}f\,dx\\
&=\int_{\partial\Omega}u(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&\geq u_{m}\text{P}(\Omega)\geq{u}_{m}\text{P}(\Omega^{\sharp}),\end{split}

and it leads to

\mu(t)\leq\phi(t)={\left|\Omega\right|},\quad\forall t\leq v_{m}.

(21)

Moreover, equality holds in (
20
) if and only if
\Omega
is a ball, as a consequence of the isoperimetric inequality.

Now, for
t\geq 0
, we introduce the following notations:

u_{M}=\sup_{\Omega}u,\quad U_{t}=\left\{x\in\Omega:u(x)>t\right\},\quad\partial U_{t}^{int}=\partial U_{t}\cap\Omega,\quad\partial U_{t}^{ext}=\partial U_{t}\cap\partial\Omega,\quad\mu(t)={\left|U_{t}\right|}

and

v_{M}=\sup_{\Omega}v,\quad V_{t}=\left\{x\in\Omega^{\sharp}:v(x)>t\right\},\quad\partial V_{t}^{int}=\partial V_{t}\cap\Omega,\quad\partial V_{t}^{ext}=\partial V_{t}\cap\partial\Omega,\quad\phi(t)={\left|V_{t}\right|}.

Because of the invariance of the Laplacian and of the Schwarz rearrangement of
f
by rotation, the solution
v
to (
2
) is radial and, consequently, the superlevel sets
V_{t}
are balls.

Now, we recall some technical Lemmas, proved in
[

]
, that we need in what follows. We recall the proof of Lemma
2.5
for the reader’s convenience, while we omit the proof of Lemma

and Lemma

.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

and

be the solution to (
1
) and (
2
), then, for a.e.

, it holds

4.1
An alternative proof of the quantitative Bossel-Daners and Saint-Venant inequality

In this section, we give an alternative proof of the quantitative version of the Saint-Venant inequality and, in the 2-dimensional case, of the Bossel-Daners inequality, using Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
. The proof of these quantitative enhancements is based on two immediate ideas that we will recall in the following proofs. The most delicate part is to show the uniformity of the constants in front of the square of the asymmetry.

We start by defining the Robin Torsional rigidity of the set
\Omega

T_{\beta}(\Omega)=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)\\
w\not\equiv 0\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\left(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}w\;dx\right)^{2}}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\;dx+\beta\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}w^{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}

and we observe that the maximum is achieved by the solution
u
to problem (
1
) when the right-hand side
f\equiv 1
. Moreover, one can rewrite the Robin Torsional rigidity as follows

T_{\beta}(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}u\,dx.

In
[
18
]
, the authors proved that a Saint-Venant-type inequality holds in the more general framework of
SBV
functions, and in
[
19
]
, it is improved in a quantitative way. More precisely, the authors proved the existence of a constant
\tilde{C}
depending only on
\beta
,
n
and
|\Omega|
for which

T_{\beta}(\Omega^{\sharp})-T_{\beta}(\Omega)\geq\tilde{C}\alpha^{2}(\Omega).

(61)

We recover the same result by means of a completely different approach.

Corollary 4.1

(Quantitative Saint-Venant inequality)
.

Let
\Omega
be an open, bounded, and Lipschitz set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, there exists a constant
C_{4}=C_{4}(|\Omega|,\beta,n)>0
such that :

T_{\beta}(\Omega^{\sharp})-T_{\beta}(\Omega)\geq C_{4}\alpha^{2}(\Omega),

(62)

where
\Omega^{\sharp}
is the ball with the same volume as
\Omega
.

Proof.

Inequality (
62
) is an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.2
, with the choice
k=1
that is allowed, in view of the Remark
3.1
.
∎

We now recall the variational characterization of the first Robin eigenvalue:

\lambda_{\beta}(\Omega)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)\\
w\not\equiv 0\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\;dx+\beta\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}w^{2}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}w^{2}\;dx}.

The isoperimetric inequality is proved in
[
15
,
27
]
and the quantitative result in
[
17
]
, proving that there exists a constant
\tilde{C_{1}}

\lambda_{\beta}(\Omega)-\lambda_{\beta}(\Omega^{\sharp})\geq\tilde{C_{1}}\alpha^{2}(\Omega).

(63)

Here, we give an alternative proof of (
63
).

Corollary 4.2

(Quantitative Bossel-Daners inequality)
.

Let
\Omega
be an open, bounded, and Lipschitz set of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then, there exists a constant
C_{5}=C_{5}(\Omega,\beta)>0
such that :

\lambda_{\beta}(\Omega)-\lambda_{\beta}(\Omega^{\sharp})\geq C_{5}\alpha^{2}(\Omega),

(64)

where
\Omega^{\sharp}
is the ball with the same volume as
\Omega
.

Proof.

To obtain (
64
), we follow the same scheme as in
[
6
]
, considering the enhanced inequality (
13
) instead of the direct comparison between the two norms; subsequently, we delve into the discussion about the independence of
C_{2}
. Let us consider the first positive
L^{2}
-normalized eigenfunction
u
of
\Omega
, namely the positive
L^{2}
-normalized solution of the eigenvalue problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\lambda_{\beta}(\Omega)u\,&\text{in }\Om,\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle{\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\beta u=0}&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(65)

and consider the auxiliary problem on
\Om^{\sharp}

\begin{cases}-\Delta w=\lambda_{\beta}(\Omega)u^{\sharp}\,&\text{in }\Om^{\sharp},\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle{\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}+\beta w=0}&\text{on }\partial\Omega^{\sharp}.\end{cases}

(66)

Inequality (
13
) with
k=1
, that can be chosen as
n=2
, together with (
19
), gives

\int_{\Om^{\sharp}}(u^{\sharp})^{2}\>dx=\int_{\Om}u^{2}\>dx\leq\int_{\Om^{\sharp}}w^{2}\>dx-C_{2}\alpha^{2}(\Omega).

(67)

We now test (
66
) with
w
, use the Cauchy-Scwarz inequality and estimate (
67
), obtaining

\displaystyle\int_{\Om^{\sharp}}|\nabla w|^{2}\>dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega^{\sharp}}w^{2}\>d\mathcal{H}^{1}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Om^{\sharp}}w\Delta w\>dx=\lambda_{\beta}(\Omega)\int_{\Om^{\sharp}}u^{\sharp}w\>dx

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{\beta}(\Omega)\left(\int_{\Om^{\sharp}}(u^{\sharp})^{2}\>dx\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\Om^{\sharp}}w^{2}\>dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{\beta}(\Omega)\left(\int_{\Om^{\sharp}}w^{2}\>dx-C_{2}\alpha^{2}(\Omega)\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\Om^{\sharp}}w^{2}\>dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{\beta}(\Omega)\left(1-\frac{C_{2}}{\int_{\Om^{\sharp}}w^{2}\>dx}\alpha^{2}(\Omega)\right)^{1/2}\int_{\Om^{\sharp}}w^{2}\>dx

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{\beta}(\Omega)\left(1-\frac{C_{2}}{2\int_{\Om^{\sharp}}w^{2}\>dx}\alpha^{2}(\Omega)\right)\int_{\Om^{\sharp}}w^{2}\>dx,

where in the last inequality we have used the Bernoulli inequality, for which one has to suppose
\alpha(\Omega)
sufficiently small.

Using
w
as a test for
\lambda_{\beta}(\Omega^{\sharp})
and rearranging terms we get
(quoziente di Rayleigh con
\Omega^{\sharp}
)

\frac{\lambda_{\beta}(\Omega)-\lambda_{\beta}(\Omega^{\sharp})}{\lambda_{\beta}(\Omega)}\geq\frac{C_{2}}{2\int_{\Om^{\sharp}}w^{2}\>dx}\alpha^{2}(\Omega).

It remains to show that
\dfrac{C_{2}}{2\int_{\Om^{\sharp}}w^{2}\>dx}
only depends on
|\Omega|,\beta
.

First of all, we recall that, in our case,

,

and

, so

\displaystyle C_{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{n}-1}{\left\|f\right\|}_{1}}{\beta n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right)^{2}{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{k}}\min\left\{\frac{1}{2^{\frac{1}{k}+5}\gamma_{n}},\frac{\beta{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{n}}}{2^{\frac{1}{k}+5+\frac{2}{n}}n\omega_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{\beta}^{2}(\Omega)\|u^{\sharp}\|_{1}^{2}}{4\beta^{2}\pi}\min\left\{\frac{1}{2^{6}\gamma_{2}},\frac{\beta{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2^{8}\sqrt{\pi}}\right\}.

Now, let us notice that

\displaystyle w(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\pi|x|^{2}}^{|\Omega|}\frac{1}{4\pi t}\left(\int_{0}^{t}f^{*}(s)\>ds\right)dt+\frac{\|f\|_{1}|\Omega|^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{2\beta\sqrt{\pi}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\pi|x|^{2}}^{|\Omega|}\frac{1}{4\pi t}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(\lambda_{\beta}(\Omega)u)^{*}(s)\>ds\right)dt+\frac{\lambda_{\beta}(\Omega)\|u\|_{1}|\Omega|^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{2\beta\sqrt{\pi}}

and so

\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}w^{2}\>dx\leq\lambda^{2}_{\beta}(\Omega)\|u^{\sharp}\|^{2}_{1}\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}\left[\frac{1}{4\pi}\log\left(\frac{{\left|\Omega\right|}}{\pi{\left|x\right|}^{2}}\right)+\frac{{\left|\Omega\right|}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{2\beta\sqrt{\pi}}\right]^{2}dx.

Moreover, by direct computations

\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}\left[\frac{1}{4\pi}\log\left(\frac{{\left|\Omega\right|}}{\pi{\left|x\right|}^{2}}\right)+\frac{{\left|\Omega\right|}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{2\beta\sqrt{\pi}}\right]^{2}dx=\dfrac{1}{4\pi}\left(\dfrac{{\left|\Omega\right|}}{2\pi}+\dfrac{1}{\pi\beta^{2}}+\dfrac{\sqrt{{\left|\Omega\right|}}}{\beta\sqrt{\pi}}\right)

Thus, we get

\dfrac{C_{2}}{2\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}w^{2}\>dx}\geq\frac{\min\left\{\frac{1}{2^{6}\gamma_{2}},\frac{\beta{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2^{8}\sqrt{\pi}}\right\}}{2\beta^{2}\left(\dfrac{{\left|\Omega\right|}}{2\pi}+\dfrac{1}{\pi\beta^{2}}+\dfrac{\sqrt{{\left|\Omega\right|}}}{\beta\sqrt{\pi}}\right)}:=C_{5}.

(68)

This latter is explicitly a positive constant depending only on
|\Omega|
and
\beta
, thereby establishing the claim and concluding the proof.

∎

Notice that, as highlighted in the the introduction, neither Corollary

and the first Robin eigenvalue

and to the first Dirichlet eigenvalue

respectively, but both constants

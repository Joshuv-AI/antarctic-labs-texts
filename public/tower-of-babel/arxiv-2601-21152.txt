Community detection in network using Szegedy quantum walk
Md Samsur Rahaman, Supriyo Dutta

Abstract
In a network, the vertices with similar characteristics construct communities. The vertices in a community are well-connected. Detecting the communities in a network is a challenging and important problem in the theory of complex networks. One approach to solve this problem uses the classical random walks on the graphs. In quantum computing, quantum walks are the quantum mechanical counterparts of classical random walks. In this article, we employ a variant of Szegedy’s quantum walk to develop a procedure for discovering the communities in networks. The limiting probability distribution of quantum walks assists us in determining the inclusion of a vertex in a community. We apply our procedure of community detection on a number of graphs and social networks, such as the relaxed caveman graph, l l -partition graph, Karate club graph, dolphin’s social network, etc. Keywords : Clustering, Community detection, Szegedy quantum walk, Complex networks and graphs.

Now, we include a number graph theoretic ideas in construction of our communities. A vertex with degree
1
is adjacent to only one vertex, which may belong to a community. A vertex with degree two lies on a path between two neighboring vertices. These three vertices may form a single community, or the neighboring vertices may belong to two different communities. But, a vertex with greater degree is adjacent to many other vertices. Thus, it belong to only one community. It insists us to arrange the vertices in a descending order of their degrees. In every step, we consider the highest degree vertex among the vertices which are not allotted to a community and generate a community for it.

In addition, the above paragraph justifies that the community containing a vertex with highest degree also contains all its neighboring vertices. But, there may be a number of vertices in a community, which are not an immediate neighbor of the vertex with highest degree in it. Also, there are non-neighboring vertices which are not in the community. When a vertex is in the community we observe that most of its neighbours are also in the same community. Therefore, we need a condition for including a given vertex in a particular community. Recall from
subsection 2.1
that
N(v_{i})=\{v:(u,v)\in E(G)\}
. Let
\Nbd(v_{i})
be the neighborhood of
v_{i}
in the community of vertices containing
v_{i}
. If
|N(v_{j})\cap\Nbd(v_{i})|\geq\frac{|N(v_{j})|}{2}
holds, we assume that
v_{j}
is in the community of
v_{i}
.

We make the final selection of a vertex in a community based on the edge probability vector
\Pi
generated by quantum walk. We determine the shortest path between
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
. A path consists of a number of incident edges. The walker follows the path means it follows all the subsequent edges. As there is no non-Markovian componenet in our quantum walk, the probability of getting the walker on the edges of a path is independent to each other. Therefore, the probability of getting the walker on a path is the product of the probability of getting the walker on the edges. It leads us to define the path weight between two vertices.

be
u=u_{0},e_{1},u_{1},e_{2},u_{2},\dots e_{n},u_{n}=v
. We define the path weight between the vertices
u
and
v
by

PW(u,v)=\frac{1}{d_{G}(v)}\prod_{i=1}^{n}p(e_{i}),

(13)

where
p(e_{i})
is the probability corresponding to the edge
e_{i}
in the edge probability vector
\Pi
.

Note that, as all
0<p(e_{i})<1
and
d_{G}(v)\geq 1
we have
0<PW(u,v)<1
for all pairs of vertices
u
and
v
. The explanation for dividing
\frac{1}{d_{G}(v)}
will be discussed after Procedure
Procedure 1
.

Now, we are in a position to determine the communities in a network. Given a vertex
v_{i}
the community consists of
v_{i}
is denoted by
C(v_{i})
. Initially,
C(v_{i})=\{v_{i}\}
. Then,
C(v_{i})
grows maintaining some conditions. We choose the vertices
v_{i}
according to their degrees. Our method is described in the procedure mentioned below.

Procedure 1

Recall that
N(v)
denotes the subset of vertices which are adjacent to
v
in
G
. The procedure for detecting the communities consists of the following steps:

1.

Arrange the vertices of the graph
G
in the descending order of the vertex degrees. Therefore, the vertex set of
G
is
V=\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots v_{n}\}
, where
d_{G}(v_{i})\geq d_{G}(v_{i+1})
for
i=1,2,\dots(n-1)
.

2.

Repeat the following steps if
V\neq\emptyset
.

(a)

Consider the first element of
V
, say
v_{i}
.

(b)

Construct the set of vertices
\Nbd(v_{i})=N(v_{i})
.

(c)

Let
v_{j}\notin N(v_{i})
is a vertex satisfying

|N(v_{j})\cap\Nbd(v_{i})|\geq\frac{|N(v_{j})|}{2},

(14)

then add
v_{j}
in
\Nbd(v_{i})
.

(d)

Construct the community with
v_{i}
, by setting
C(v_{i})=\{v_{i}\}
.

(e)

Add all
v_{j}\in\Nbd(v_{i})
in
C(v_{i})
if
PW(v_{i},v_{j})<q
.

(f)

Update the set
V
by removing all the vertices in
C(v_{i})
.

3.

Return the communities of
C(v_{i})
which are formed in Step 2.

It is clear from the
Procedure 1
mentioned above that we construct a set of vertices
\Nbd(v_{i})
based on equation (
14
), which assists us to construct the community
C(v_{i})
. The quantum walk is useful to calculate the probability values in finding
PW(v_{i},v_{j})
mentioned in equation (
13
).

In the above procedure
q
is a parameter. If
q=0
then the number of communities is equal to the number of vertices in the graph, that is every community consists of single vertices. When
q>\max_{(u,v)\in E(G)}\{PW(u,v)\}
then
C(v_{i})=\Nbd(v_{i})
.

In equation (
13
), we multiply
\frac{1}{d_{G}(v)}
to normalize node influence. It provides more preference to a high-degree node to be added in
C(v_{i})
, because a node with higher degree reduces the product
\prod_{i=1}^{n}p(e_{i})
than a vertex with lower degree.

After constructing a community
C(v_{i})
using Procedure
Procedure 1
, it may be observed that a number of additional vertices are in the community. For example, consider
Figure 1
. Here, the vertex
6
is added in the community
C(1)
. But the structure of the graph suggests that vertex
6
should form a separate community. Therefore, need to remove it from
C(1)
. To discard those vertices we perform a refinement using the below procedure.

Procedure 2

Let a community
C(v_{i})
is created using Step
1
and
2
of
Procedure 1
. Apply the following step before proceeding to step
3
.

1.

For any vertex
v_{j}\in C(v_{i})
if
d_{C(v_{i})}(v_{j})\leq\frac{d_{G}(v_{j})}{2}
then remove
v_{j}
from
C(v_{i})
. Here,
C(v_{i})
denotes the induced subgraph of
G
, generated by
C(v_{i})
.

Therefore, our method of community detection is a combination of the following steps, mentioned in the below procedure.

Procedure 3

Let
G
be a simple undirected graph with
n
vertices and
m
edges. To find the communities in
G
perform the following steps:

1.

Generation of a directed graph:
Convert the graph
G
to a directed graph
\overrightarrow{G}
by assigning opposite orientations on every undirected edge.

2.

Construction of initial state:
Corresponding to every edge
i
assign a state
\ket{i}
of computational basis of dimension
2m
, which is mentioned in equation (
2
). Construct the initial state
\ket{\psi_{0}}
using equation (
5
).

3.

Application of quantum walk:
To apply Szegedy quantum walk we construct the Szegedy matrix using equation (
3
) and apply it to the initial state
\ket{\psi_{0}}
, repeatedly.

4.

Generation of limiting probability distribution:
For a fixed error term
\epsilon
, we apply equation (
12
) to stop quantum walk and generate the limiting probability distribution
\Pi
on the edges mentioned in Definition
2
.

5.

Generation of communities
Apply Procedure
1
and
2
for generating communities.

5
Conclusion

In this article, we introduce a new procedure that utilizes Szegedy quantum walks to detect the communities in a network. There are multiple models of quantum walks in the literature. We employ the Szegedy quantum walks on graphs for our investigation, as it is defined on all graphs.

The limiting probability distribution of the Szegedy quantum walks assists us in finding the communities. This distribution depends on the choice of the initial state of the quantum walker. We assume that the walker begins its journey from the vertices with the highest degree. Therefore, the initial quantum state as well as the limiting probability distribution depends on the structure of the graph. The limiting probability distribution provides the edge probabilities for each edge in the graph. We use it in
Procedure 3
to extract the communities from the graph. This procedure is a combination of two subroutines, which are
Procedure 1
and
Procedure 2
. In
Procedure 1
, we find the communities using quantum walks. Then,
Procedure 2
refines the preliminary communities. We apply
Procedure 3
on different graphs to identify the communities in them.

The problem of community detection is an important problem in the study of networks. The communities assists us to understand the structure of the networks. In the future, the interested reader may apply our community detection procedure in the investigation of different complex networks.

A two-term hybrid method with restart strategy for signal processing and image recovery
Authors: Muhammad Abdullahi, Muhammad Salisu Sarkinbai, Auwal Bala Abubakar, Abdullah Al-Yaari, Seyed Yaser Mousavi Siamakani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper presents a two-term hybrid conjugate gradient (CG) method with a restart strategy for solving large-scale nonlinear equations, particularly those arising in signal processing and image recovery. The proposed method generalizes four existing algorithms, thereby yielding a novel hybrid CG parameter that combines their desirable properties while ensuring sufficient descent and boundedness of the search direction. Furthermore, a restart mechanism is incorporated to accelerate convergence by adaptively resetting the search direction whenever progress slows. Under suitable assumptions, global convergence and convergence rate of the proposed method is established. Numerical experiments on benchmark problems demonstrate that the proposed method achieves faster convergence and higher solution accuracy than existing methods.Keywords: Nonlinear equation, Convex-constrained, Restart technique, Signal processing, Image recoverySubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Problems of the form (1.1) arise in a wide range of real-life applications, such as robotic motion control1, chemical equilibrium systems2, economic modeling3, machine learning4, and power flow analysis5. These equations describe complex systems characterized by nonlinear interactions among variables, making their solution both challenging and essential for accurate modeling and optimization. Moreover, problems involving Bregman distances and variational inequalities can often be reformulated as nonlinear equations, thereby broadening their scope of application to a variety of optimization and equilibrium problems6,7.

Recent advances in iterative numerical methods have made significant progress in addressing such nonlinear problems, including those found in image denoising8,9, and discretized nonlinear partial differential equations10. Such problems are fundamental in scientific and engineering disciplines, particularly where systems must be optimized under nonlinear and convex constraints.

Due to their wide applicability, nonlinear equations have been studied extensively, leading to the development of various iterative solution methods. Prominent examples include Newton’s method11, quasi-Newton methods12, and Levenberg-Marquardt methods13,14, along with their variants. Although these methods exhibit rapid local convergence when initialized with sufficiently accurate approximations, they become computationally expensive for large-scale problems because they require the computation and storage of Jacobian matrices or their approximations.

As an alternative, first-order methods such as conjugate gradient (CG) methods15, spectral gradient methods16, and spectral CG variants17 have been developed. These methods are particularly attractive for solving large-scale problems, especially in unconstrained optimization18,19 and, more recently, on Riemannian manifolds20, because of their low memory requirements and computational efficiency.

The projection framework introduced by Solodov and Svaiter21 has inspired several researchers to develop efficient methods for solving (1.1) using first-order projection techniques. For example, Zhang and Zhou22 proposed a spectral gradient projection scheme for solving monotone equations, and many subsequent studies have investigated the integration of projection technique into CG-type methods23–26.

To achieve strong computational performance while preserving the attractive features of CG-type techniques, several hybrid CG methods have been proposed. These approaches either switch adaptively between two CG parameters or compute a convex combination of them. Early developments include the hybrid of Polak–Ribière–Polak (PRP)-Fletcher–Reeves (FR) methods of Touati-Ahmed and Storey27, Hu and Storey28, and Gilbert and Nocedal29, as well as the Hestenes–Stiefel (HS)- Dai-Yuan (DY) hybrids introduced by Dai and Yuan30. More recent contributions by Andrei31,32 proposed efficient HS-DY hybridizations based on convex combinations and modified secant equations, while Babaie-Kafaki et al.33 developed a hybrid CG method based on modified BFGS updates. Similarly, Wu et al.26 presented a hybrid inertial spectral CG-type method based on a memoryless BFGS update, in which the spectral parameter is derived from a quasi-Newton perspective and a double-truncation technique.

Restart strategies have also played an important role in improving the convergence behavior of CG-type methods. Early investigations by Crowder and Wolfe34 established that, without restarts, CG methods exhibit at most linear convergence. Fletcher and Reeves35 proposed one of the earliest restart mechanisms, in which the iteration process is restarted along the negative gradient direction after every k = n or k = n + 1 iterations.

However, Andrei36 later observed that such fixed-interval restarts could be inefficient for problems with clustered eigenvalues. Powell37 therefore suggested adaptive restart strategies based on the behavior of the objective function rather than on iteration counts, and subsequent studies, such as38, further refined this idea. Hybridizing two or more schemes to develop more efficient methods has become a prominent technique for solving (1.1). By incorporating the inertial relaxation technique and accelerated gradient descent into a family of three-term CG methods proposed by Al-Baali et al.39, Liu et al.40 developed a family of three-term CG methods with a restart procedure. The global convergence of their method was established theoretically without requiring Lipschitz continuity of the underlying mapping. In a related development, Li et al.41 used three-step successive information to generate inertial iterates, after which the search direction incorporated a restart strategy, thereby eliminating the need for additional conditions to guarantee the sufficient descent condition and the trust-region property. Furthermore, Li et al.42 proposed an inertial CG-type method for solving (1.1). Their method incorporates a two-term composite restart direction with a flexible nonzero vector and introduces a turning parameter, namely the spectral parameter, as part of the restart direction. Combined with a new self-adaptive line search, the method achieved the desired global convergence properties. The above literature indicates that combining the strengths of multiple methods can produce an algorithm with improved theoretical properties and enhanced numerical performance. Motivated by this idea, we ask the following question: Is it possible to construct a single efficient algorithm that generalizes four distinct existing methods while ensuring global convergence and satisfying the sufficient descent condition? This possibility has already been explored in several studies; see, for example43,44,. However, the idea proposed in this work differs from those earlier approaches, as will be shown in the section 2. Inspired by recent developments in hybrid CG methods and restart strategies43,51, this study proposes a hybrid CG parameter that generalizes four existing CG methods, thereby enhancing both the theoretical and numerical convergence properties of the algorithm. The proposed method offers several advantages. It combines the desirable properties of classical CG parameters, such as the HS and PRP methods, together with two other recent CG type methods. In addition, it requires minimal storage, making it well suited for large-scale problems. Most importantly, the method achieves global convergence without relying on differentiability assumptions on the underlying equation. Finally, the proposed method is applied to signal processing and image recovery problems.

Remark 6.2

The relevance of the proposed method to signal and image processing lies in the fact that these applications often produce large-scale nonlinear systems whose solution requires algorithms with low memory requirements and high computational efficiency. Because the proposed method is derivative-free and equipped with a restart mechanism, it is particularly suitable for reconstruction tasks in which rapid and stable convergence is essential for obtaining accurate recovered signals and images. This is evident from the signal processing and image recovery results reported in Figs. 2 and 5.

Conclusion

This study presented a two-term hybrid CG method integrated with a restart strategy for efficiently solving large-scale nonlinear equations, particularly those arising in signal processing and image recovery applications. A novel hybrid conjugate parameter was developed to combine the strengths of existing CG parameters while ensuring both sufficient descent and boundedness of the search direction. The inclusion of a restart mechanism further improved the convergence rate whenever progress slowed. Theoretical analysis established the global convergence of the proposed method under suitable assumptions. Extensive numerical experiments on benchmark problems, signal processing tasks, and image recovery applications involving four cases of spherical engine part image reconstruction, as shown in Figs. 7 and 8, demonstrated that the proposed THMRST method achieves faster convergence and higher solution accuracy than several existing methods. Furthermore, comparative experiments between the proposed approach and an existing method confirmed its superior performance in terms of convergence speed and solution accuracy.

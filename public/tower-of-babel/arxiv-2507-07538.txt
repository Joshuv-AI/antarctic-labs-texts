Strong averaging principle for nonautonomous slow-fast SPDEs driven by α \alpha -stable processes
Yueling Li, Xiaobin Sun, Zijuan Wang, Yingchao Xie

Abstract
. This paper considers a class of nonautonomous slow-fast stochastic partial differential equations driven by α \alpha -stable processes for α ∈ ( 1 , 2 ) \alpha\in(1,2) . By introducing the evolution system of measures, we establish an averaging principle for this stochastic system. Specifically, we first prove the strong convergence (in the L p L^{p} sense for p ∈ ( 1 , α ) p\in(1,\alpha) ) of the slow component to the solution of a simplified averaged equation with coefficients depend on the scaling parameter. Furthermore, under conditions that coefficients are time-periodic or satisfy certain asymptotic convergence, we prove that the slow component converges strongly to the solution of an averaged equation, whose coefficients are independent of the scaling parameter. Finally, a concrete example is provided to illustrate the applicability of our assumptions. Notably, the absence of finite second moments in the solution caused by the α \alpha -stable processes requires new technical treatments, thereby solving a problem mentioned in [ 1 , Remark 3.3] .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Let
H
be a given Hilbert space, with
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
|\cdot|
denoting its inner product and norm, respectively. This paper focus on the following slow-fast nonautonomous stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs for short) driven by cylindrical stable processes:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}dX_{t}^{\varepsilon}=\left[AX_{t}^{\varepsilon}+F\left(t/\varepsilon,X_{t}^{\varepsilon},Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}\right)dt+dL_{t},\quad X_{0}^{\varepsilon}=x\in H,\right.\\
\\
dY_{t}^{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left[BY_{t}^{\varepsilon}+G\left(t/\varepsilon,X_{t}^{\varepsilon},Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}\right)\right]dt+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{1/\alpha}}dZ_{t},\quad Y_{0}^{\varepsilon}=y\in H,\end{array}\right.

where
\{L_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
and
\{Z_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
are two mutually independent cylindrical
\alpha
-stable processes with
\alpha\in(1,2)
defined on probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
with the natural filtration
\{\mathscr{F}_{t},t\geq 0\}
generated by
(L_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
(Z_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, that is

L_{t}=\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\rho_{k}L^{k}_{t}e_{k},\quad Z_{t}=\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\gamma_{k}Z^{k}_{t}e_{k},\quad t\geqslant 0,

where
\{\rho_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
and
\{\gamma_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
are two given sequences of positive numbers,
\{e_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
is an orthonormal basis of
H
,
\{L^{k}_{t}\}_{k\geq 1}
and
\{Z^{k}_{t}\}_{k\geq 1}

are two sequences of independent one dimensional symmetric
\alpha
-stable processes satisfying that for any
k\geq 1
and
t\geqslant 0
,

\mathbb{E}[e^{iL^{k}_{t}h}]=\mathbb{E}[e^{iZ^{k}_{t}h}]=e^{-t|h|^{\alpha}},\quad\forall h\in\mathbb{R}.

The aforementioned stochastic system (
1.1
) involves a parameter
\varepsilon
. Intuitively, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the components
X^{\varepsilon}
and
Y^{\varepsilon}
of the solution exhibit slow and fast changes, respectively. Consequently,
X^{\varepsilon}
and
Y^{\varepsilon}
can be regarded as the "slow" and "fast" components in the system. This kind of slow-fast system has appeared in many fields, such as the nonlinear oscillations, chemical kinetics, biology, climate dynamics, see e.g.
[
2
,
16
,
23
,
36
]
. Take a consideration of the complexity of such coupled system, it is crucial to find simplified equation which can serve as approximate replacements for the original system. Thus scholars are interested in studying the asymptotic behavior of the slow component as
{\varepsilon}\to 0
, which is called averaging principle. The averaging principle for slow-fast SPDEs was first studied by Cerrai and Freidlin
[
5
,
6
]
in 2009. Over the past fifteen years, a large number of related results has been achieved in this field, see e.g.
[
4
,
10
,
15
,
18
,
20
,
22
,
24
,
27
,
35
,
37
]
.

It is well-known that the
\alpha
-stable process has theoretically meaningful, for instance such processes can be used to model systems with heavy tails in physics, telecommunication networks, finance and other fields. Numerous scholars are dedicated to this research field of SPDEs driven by
\alpha
-stable process, see e.g.
[
14
,
25
,
38
]
. Recently, the slow-fast SPDEs driven by
\alpha
-stable process has attracted people’s attention, for instance, Bao et al.
[
1
]
proved the strong averaging principle for two-time scale SPDEs driven by
\alpha
-stable noise. The authors have proved the strong averaging principle for slow-fast stochastic Ginzburg-Landau equation and stochastic Burgers equations in
[
32
]
and
[
8
]
, respectively. Moreover, using the technique of Poisson equation, we also prove the strong and weak convergence rates for multi-scale stochastic differential equations (SDEs) and SPDEs driven by
\alpha
-stable processes in
[
30
]
and
[
31
]
, respectively.

Note that aforementioned references all consider the autonomous (time-independent) SPDEs, this focus is due to the extensive theoretical work on the existence and uniqueness of invariant measure for the corresponding time-independent frozen equation. It is important to mention that the stochastic system
(
1.1
)
is nonautonomous, that is, its coefficients will change over time due to external factors or internal variations, such as the learning models related to neuronal activity in
[
19
]
. Consequently, the concept of invariant measure does not exist for the corresponding time-dependent frozen equation.

To our knowledge, there are few studies on the slow-fast non-autonomous S(P)DEs. Regarding the slow-fast nonautonomous SDEs, Wainrib
[
34
]
and Uda
[
33
]
proved the strong averaging principle when the coefficients of the fast equation are time-periodic. More recently, Sun et al. utilized the approach of nonautonomous Poisson equations, as presented in
[
28
]
, and Khasminskii’s time-discretization technique, outlined in
[
29
]
, to investigate the averaging principles in both strong and weak senses. While in the framework of the slow-fast nonautonomous SPDE, there appears to be only one finding by Cerrai and Lunardi in
[
7
]
, where they explored its averaging principle when the coefficients in the fast equation satisfy the almost periodic condition. Nevertheless, the existing literature focus on the Wiener noise. It appears that there is a lack of research concerning the cases where the driven noises are
\alpha
-stable processes. Therefore, the primary aim of this paper is to investigate the averaging principle for stochastic system (
1.1
).

1.2.
Outline of the methods

The initial challenge involves comprehending the limit behaviour for the corresponding frozen equation

\displaystyle dY_{t}=\left[BY_{t}+G(t,x,Y_{t})\right]dt+dZ_{t},

whose transition semigroup
\{P^{x}_{s,t}\}_{t\geqslant s}
is time-inhomogeneous. Thus we need to introduce an evolution system of measures for
\{P^{x}_{s,t}\}_{t\geqslant s}
of time-inhomogeneous frozen equation, see e.g.
[
9
,
11
,
12
]
. Specially, we call
\{\mu^{x}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is an evolution system of measures for
\{P^{x}_{s,t}\}_{t\geqslant s}
, if

\int_{H}P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y)\,\mu^{x}_{s}(dy)=\int_{H}\varphi(y)\,\mu^{x}_{t}(dy),\quad s\leqslant t,\varphi\in C_{b}(H).

Therefore, it is reasonable to define the averaged coefficient by averaging the drift coefficient
F
with respect to the evolution system of measures
\{\mu^{x}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
, i.e.,

(1.2)

\bar{F}(t,x)=\int_{H}F(t,x,y)\,\mu^{x}_{t}(dy).

Thus the corresponding averaged equation can be expressed in the following form:

(1.3)

d\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\left[AX^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\bar{F}(t/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t})\right]dt+dL_{t},\quad\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{0}=x.

Hence under some proper conditions, the first result of this paper is as follows:

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{p}=0,\quad\forall p\in(1,\alpha),\penalty\ T>0.

It should be noted that even if we assume that
F
is time-independent,
the time-dependent
\{\mu_{t}^{x}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
still implies that
\bar{F}
definition in (
1.2
) has a time oscillating component.

Note that the coefficients in the above averaged equation (
1.3
) still include the parameter
{\varepsilon}
. Therefore, it seems natural to further simplify (
1.3
) by averaging over time, while the critical step is sufficient to find a proper function
\bar{F}:H\to H
such that

(1.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\geqslant 0}\frac{1}{T}\int_{t}^{t+T}[\bar{F}(s,x)-\bar{F}(x)]ds=0.

This is a ordinary condition in the studying the averaging principle for stochastic system (
1.3
), as evidenced in references such as
[
10
, (G1)]
. To achieve (
1.4
), we here additionally assume that the coefficients
F
and
G
satisfy time periodicity or certain asymptotic convergence, respectively.

1.2.1.
Time periodic case

In accordance with the periodic conditions established in
[
19
,
33
]
. We suppose that the coefficients
F(\cdot,x,y)
and
G(\cdot,x,y)
are
\tau_{1}
-periodic and
\tau_{2}
-periodic, respectively, where
\tau_{1}/\tau_{2}
is a rational number. Roughly speaking, the
\tau_{2}
-periodicity of
G(\cdot,x,y)
induces that
\{\mu^{x}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is also
\tau_{2}
-periodic, which combines with the
\tau_{1}
-periodicity of
F(\cdot,x,y)
imply the
\tau
-periodicity of
\bar{F}(t,x)
defined in (
1.2
) with
\tau
satisfies
\tau=m_{2}\tau_{1}=m_{1}\tau_{2}
, for some
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}=\{1,2,\cdots\}
. In this case, the natural definition for the averaged coefficient is given by

(1.5)

\displaystyle\bar{F}_{P}(x):=\frac{1}{\tau}\int^{\tau}_{0}\bar{F}(t,x)dt.

It is easy to check that (
1.4
) holds for
\bar{F}(x)=\bar{F}_{P}(x)
. Consequently, the associated averaged equation can be expressed as

(1.6)

\displaystyle d\bar{X}_{t}=A\bar{X}_{t}dt+\bar{F}_{P}(\bar{X}_{t})dt+dL_{t},\quad\bar{X}_{0}=x\in H.

Therefore, the second result in this paper is as follows:

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}_{t}|^{p}=0,\quad\forall p\in(1,\alpha),\penalty\ T>0.

1.2.2.
Asymptotic convergence case

In contrast to the periodic conditions previously imposed on the coefficients
F
and
G
, we now introduce asymptotic convergence conditions. Specifically, there exist functions
\tilde{F},\tilde{G}:H\times H\rightarrow H
such that for any
T\geqslant 0
,
x,y\in H
, the following limits hold

\displaystyle\lim_{T\to\infty}\sup_{t\geqslant 0}\frac{1}{T}\left|\int^{t+T}_{t}\left[F(s,x,y)-\tilde{F}(x,y)\right]ds\right|=0,\quad\lim_{T\to\infty}|G(T,x,y)-\tilde{G}(x,y)|=0.

These conditions ensure that (
1.4
) is satisfied for

(1.7)

\displaystyle\bar{F}(x)=\bar{F}_{A}(x):=\int_{H}\tilde{F}(x,y)\,\mu^{x}(dy),

where
\mu^{x}
is the unique invariant measure associated with the following SPDE:

d\tilde{Y}_{t}=B\tilde{Y}_{t}dt+\tilde{G}(x,\tilde{Y}_{t})\,dt+dZ_{t}.

As a result, the third result of this paper is as follows:

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}-X_{t}|^{p}=0,\quad\forall p\in(1,\alpha),\penalty\ T>0,

where
X
is the solution of the averaged equation:

(1.8)

\displaystyle dX_{t}=AX_{t}dt+\bar{F}_{A}(X_{t})dt+dL_{t},\quad X_{0}=x\in H.

Contributions

3.
Some a priori estimates and auxiliary process

In this section, we first establish a priori estimates for the solution process

\{(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\}_{t\geqslant 0}
.
Secondly, we construct an auxiliary process

\{\hat{Y}_{t}^{\varepsilon}\}_{t\geqslant 0}

\{Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}-\hat{Y}_{t}^{\varepsilon}\}_{t\geqslant 0}

3.1.
Some a priori estimates

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
T>0
and
p\in[1,\alpha)
, there exists a constant
C_{T}>0
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{{\varepsilon}>0,t\in[0,T]}\left(\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{{\varepsilon}}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leqslant C_{T}(1+|x|+|y|)

and

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{{\varepsilon}>0,t\in[0,T]}\left(\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leqslant C_{T}(1+|x|+|y|).

Proof.

Define
\tilde{Z}_{t}:=\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}^{1/\alpha}}Z_{t{\varepsilon}}
, which is also a cylindrical
\alpha
-stable process. Then by (
2.6
), for any
p\in[1,\alpha)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}^{1/\alpha}}\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)B/\varepsilon}dZ_{s}\right|^{p}=\mathbb{E}\left|\int^{t/{\varepsilon}}_{0}e^{(t/{\varepsilon}-s)B}d\tilde{Z}_{s}\right|^{p}\leqslant C\left(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\frac{\gamma^{\alpha}_{k}}{\beta_{k}}\right)^{p/\alpha}<\infty.

Recall that

\displaystyle Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}=e^{tB/\varepsilon}y+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)B/\varepsilon}G\left(s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon}\right)ds+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{1/\alpha}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)B/\varepsilon}dZ_{s}

Then by Minkowski’s inequality, we get that for any
0<t\leqslant T
,

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}|Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leqslant

\displaystyle|e^{tB/{\varepsilon}}y|+\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\int^{t}_{0}\left|e^{\frac{(t-s)B}{{\varepsilon}}}G(s/{\varepsilon},X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s},Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})\right|ds\right)^{p}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle+\left[\mathbb{E}\left|\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}^{1/\alpha}}\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)B/\varepsilon}dZ_{s}\right|^{p}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle|y|\!+\!\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\int^{t}_{0}e^{\frac{-\beta_{1}(t-s)}{{\varepsilon}}}\left[C\!+\!C\left(\mathbb{E}|X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\!+\!L_{G}\left(\mathbb{E}|Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\right]\!+\!C\left(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\frac{\gamma^{\alpha}_{k}}{\beta_{k}}\right)^{1/\alpha}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C(1+|y|)+\frac{L_{G}}{\beta_{1}}\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left(\mathbb{E}|Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}+C\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

Thus by
L_{G}<\beta_{1}
, it follows

(3.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\mathbb{E}|Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leqslant C(1+|y|)+C\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

Then (
2.2
), (
2.4
), (
2.5
) and (
3.3
) imply that for any
p\in[1,\alpha)
and
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leqslant

\displaystyle C|x|\!+\!C\int^{T}_{0}\!\left(\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}dt\!+\!C\int^{T}_{0}\!\left(\mathbb{E}|Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}dt

\displaystyle+\!C\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left[\mathbb{E}\left|\int^{t}_{0}e^{(t-s)A}dL_{s}\right|^{p}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C(1+|x|+|y|)+C\int^{T}_{0}\left(\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}dt.

Then by Gronwall’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leqslant C_{T}(1+|x|+|y|).

This together with
(
3.3
)
imply
(
3.2
)
. The proof is complete.
∎

The following estimate plays an important role in our proof of main results.

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
T>0
, there exists a constant
C_{T}>0
such that for any
p\in[1,\alpha)
and
\delta\in(0,1]
,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\sup_{{\varepsilon}>0}\int^{T}_{0}\left[\mathbb{E}|X^{\varepsilon}_{t}-X^{\varepsilon}_{t(\delta)}|^{p}\right]^{1/p}dt\leqslant C_{T}\delta^{\frac{\theta}{2}}(1+|x|+|y|),

where
\theta
is the constant in Assumption
2
and
t(\delta):=\lfloor t/\delta\rfloor\delta
,
\lfloor t/\delta\rfloor
is the integer part of
t/\delta
.

Proof.

By (
3.1
) and Minkowski’s inequality, it is easy to check that

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{0}\left[\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}-X_{t(\delta)}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}\right]^{1/p}dt

\displaystyle=\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int^{\delta}_{0}\left(\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}-x|^{p}\right)^{1/p}dt+\int^{T}_{\delta}\left[\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}-X_{t(\delta)}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}\right]^{1/p}dt

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle C_{T}\delta(1+|x|+|y|)+\int^{T}_{\delta}\left[\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}-X_{t-\delta}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}\right]^{1/p}dt+\int^{T}_{\delta}\left[\mathbb{E}|X_{t(\delta)}^{\varepsilon}-X_{t-\delta}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}\right]^{1/p}dt.

Assumption
2
, Lemma
3.1
, (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) yield that for any
\theta\in(0,2)
,
t\in(0,T]
.

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left\|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\theta}^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left\|e^{tA}x\right\|_{\theta}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\!\!+\!\left[\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{t}(-A)^{\theta/2}e^{(t-s)A}F(s/{\varepsilon},X^{\varepsilon}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})ds\right|^{p}\right]^{1/p}+\left[\mathbb{E}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}dL_{s}\right\|_{\theta}^{p}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leqslant Ct^{-\theta/2}|x|+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{\theta}{2}}\left(1+\left(\mathbb{E}|X_{s}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}+\left(\mathbb{E}|Y_{s}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\right)ds\!+\!C\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{\alpha}_{k}}{\lambda_{k}^{1-\alpha\theta/2}}\right)^{1/\alpha}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{T}t^{-\theta/2}(1+|x|+|y|).

Note that

X_{t}^{\varepsilon}-X_{t-\delta}^{\varepsilon}=\left(e^{A\delta}X_{t-\delta}^{\varepsilon}-X_{t-\delta}^{\varepsilon}\right)+\int_{t-\delta}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}F(s/{\varepsilon},X^{\varepsilon}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})ds+\int_{t-\delta}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}dL_{s}.

Minkowski’s inequality, Lemma
3.1
, (
2.4
) and
(
3.6
)
imply that

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{\delta}\left[\mathbb{E}\left|e^{A\delta}X_{t-\delta}^{\varepsilon}-X_{t-\delta}^{\varepsilon}\right|^{p}\right]^{1/p}dt\leqslant

\displaystyle C\delta^{\frac{\theta}{2}}\int^{T}_{\delta}\left(\mathbb{E}\|X^{\varepsilon}_{t-\delta}\|^{p}_{\theta}\right)^{1/p}dt

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\delta^{\frac{\theta}{2}}\int^{T}_{\delta}(t-\delta)^{-\theta/2}(1+|x|+|y|)dt

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{T}\delta^{\frac{\theta}{2}}(1+|x|+|y|)

and

(3.8)

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{\delta}\left[\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{t-\delta}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}F(s/{\varepsilon},X^{\varepsilon}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})ds\right|^{p}\right]^{1/p}dt\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{T}(1+|x|+|y|)\delta.

Assumption
2
and (
2.5
) imply

\displaystyle\int_{\delta}^{T}\left[\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{t-\delta}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}dL_{s}\right|^{p}\right]^{1/p}dt\leqslant

\displaystyle C\int_{\delta}^{T}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{\alpha}_{k}(1-e^{-\lambda_{k}\delta})}{\lambda_{k}}\right]^{1/\alpha}dt

(3.9)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C\delta^{\frac{\theta}{2}}\int_{\delta}^{T}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{\alpha}_{k}}{\lambda_{k}^{1-\alpha\theta/2}}\right)^{1/\alpha}dt\ \leqslant C_{T}\delta^{\frac{\theta}{2}},

where we use the fact that
1-e^{-x}\leqslant Cx^{\alpha\theta/2}
for any
x>0
.

Combining (
3.1
)-(
3.1
), we obtain

(3.10)

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{\delta}\left[\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}-X_{t-\delta}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}\right]^{1/p}dt\leqslant C_{T}\delta^{\frac{\theta}{2}}(1+|x|+|y|).

Similarly, we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{\delta}\left[\mathbb{E}|X_{t(\delta)}^{\varepsilon}-X_{t-\delta}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}\right]^{1/p}dt\leqslant C_{T}\delta^{\frac{\theta}{2}}(1+|x|+|y|).

Finally,
(
3.5
)
, (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) imply
(
3.4
)
. The proof is complete.
∎

3.2.
Auxiliary process

Inspired from the idea of Khasminskii in
[
21
]
, we construct an auxiliary process
\hat{Y}_{t}^{\varepsilon}
,
i.e., we split
[0,T)
into some subintervals of size
\delta>0
, where
\delta
depends on
{\varepsilon}
and will be chosen later.
We construct the process
\hat{Y}_{t}^{\varepsilon}
on each time interval
[k\delta,(k+1)\delta\wedge T)
,
k=0,1,\ldots,\lfloor T/\delta\rfloor
, as follows:

d\hat{Y}_{t}^{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}[B\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t}+G\left(t/\varepsilon,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{t}^{\varepsilon})\right]dt+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{1/\alpha}}dZ_{t},t\in[k\delta,(k+1)\delta\wedge T),\quad\hat{Y}_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}=Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{k\delta},

which has an unique mild solution

(3.12)

\displaystyle\hat{Y}_{t}^{\varepsilon}=e^{(t-k\delta)B/\varepsilon}Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{k\delta}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{k\delta}^{t}e^{(t-s)B/\varepsilon}G\left(s/\varepsilon,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{s}^{\varepsilon}\right)ds+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{1/\alpha}}\int_{k\delta}^{t}e^{(t-s)B/\varepsilon}dZ_{s}.

Then we have the following result.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
T>0
, there exists a constant
C_{T}>0
such that for any
p\in[1,\alpha)
and
\delta\in(0,1]
, we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle\sup_{{\varepsilon}>0,t\in[0,T]}\left(\mathbb{E}|\hat{Y}_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leqslant C_{T}(1+|x|+|y|),

(3.14)

\displaystyle\sup_{{\varepsilon}>0}\int^{T}_{0}\left[\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\hat{Y}_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}\right]^{1/p}dt\leqslant C_{T}\delta^{\frac{\theta}{2}}(1+|x|+|y|).

Proof.

As the proof of
(
3.2
)
, we can easily obtain (
3.13
). Next, we prove (
3.14
).
The definition of
Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}
and
\hat{Y}_{t}^{\varepsilon}
, as well as
(
2.2
)
imply that for any
t\in[k\delta,(k+1)\delta\wedge T)
,

\displaystyle|Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\hat{Y}_{t}^{\varepsilon}|=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\left|\int^{t}_{k\delta}e^{(t-s)B/\varepsilon}[G\left(s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon}\right)-G(s/\varepsilon,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{s}^{\varepsilon})]ds\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{C}{{\varepsilon}}\int^{t}_{k\delta}e^{-\beta_{1}(t-s)/{\varepsilon}}|X_{s}^{\varepsilon}-X_{s(\delta)}^{\varepsilon}|ds+\frac{L_{G}}{{\varepsilon}}\int^{t}_{k\delta}e^{-\beta_{1}(t-s)/{\varepsilon}}|Y_{s}^{\varepsilon}-\hat{Y}_{s}^{\varepsilon}|ds.

By integrating both sides of the above inequality with respect to time from
k\delta
to
(k+1)\delta\wedge T
, and then summing over
k
, we can obtain

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{0}|Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\hat{Y}_{t}^{\varepsilon}|dt=\sum^{\lfloor T/\delta\rfloor}_{k=0}\int^{(k+1)\delta\wedge T}_{k\delta}|Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\hat{Y}_{t}^{\varepsilon}|dt

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{C}{{\varepsilon}}\!\sum^{\lfloor T/\delta\rfloor}_{k=0}\!\int^{(k+1)\delta\wedge T}_{k\delta}\!\!\int^{t}_{k\delta}e^{-\beta_{1}(t-s)/{\varepsilon}}|X_{s}^{\varepsilon}\!-\!X_{s(\delta)}^{\varepsilon}|dsdt\!+\!\frac{L_{G}}{{\varepsilon}}\sum^{\lfloor T/\delta\rfloor}_{k=0}\int^{(k+1)\delta\wedge T}_{k\delta}\!\!\int^{t}_{k\delta}e^{-\beta_{1}(t-s)/{\varepsilon}}|Y_{s}^{\varepsilon}\!-\!\hat{Y}_{s}^{\varepsilon}|dsdt

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{C}{{\varepsilon}}\sum^{\lfloor T/\delta\rfloor}_{k=0}\int^{(k+1)\delta\wedge T}_{k\delta}\!\!\left(\int^{(k+1)\delta\wedge T}_{s}e^{-\beta_{1}(t-s)/{\varepsilon}}|X_{s}^{\varepsilon}\!-\!X_{s(\delta)}^{\varepsilon}|dt\right)ds

\displaystyle+\frac{L_{G}}{{\varepsilon}}\sum^{\lfloor T/\delta\rfloor}_{k=0}\int^{(k+1)\delta\wedge T}_{k\delta}\!\!\left(\int^{(k+1)\delta\wedge T}_{s}e^{-\beta_{1}(t-s)/{\varepsilon}}|Y_{s}^{\varepsilon}\!-\!\hat{Y}_{s}^{\varepsilon}|dt\right)ds

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\beta_{1}}\int^{T}_{0}|X_{s}^{\varepsilon}\!-\!X_{s(\delta)}^{\varepsilon}|ds\!+\!\frac{L_{G}}{\beta_{1}}\int^{T}_{0}|Y_{s}^{\varepsilon}\!-\!\hat{Y}_{s}^{\varepsilon}|ds,

where Fubini’s theorem is applied in the second inequality. Then by Minkowski’s inequality, Lemma
3.2
and
L_{G}<\beta_{1}
, it is easy to see

\displaystyle\int^{T}_{0}\left[\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\hat{Y}_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}\right]^{1/p}dt\leqslant\frac{C}{\beta_{1}}\int^{T}_{0}\left[\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}-X_{t(\delta)}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}\right]^{1/p}dt\leqslant C_{T}\delta^{\frac{\theta}{2}}(1+|x|+|y|).

The proof is complete.
∎

4.
Evolution system of measures for SPDE driven by
\alpha
-stable process

In order to study the evolution system of measures for time-inhomogeneous frozen equation, we need to extend the time domain of the noise to the whole line. Thus we take another cylindrical
\alpha
-stable process
\tilde{Z}_{t}=\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\gamma_{k}\tilde{Z}^{k}_{t}e_{k}
,
where
\{\tilde{Z}^{k}\}_{k\geqslant 1}
is an independent copy of
\{Z^{k}\}_{k\geqslant 1}
. We define

Z_{t}=\begin{cases}Z_{t},\quad\,\,t\geqslant 0,\\
\tilde{Z}_{-t},\quad t<0,\end{cases}

which is a cylindrical
\alpha
-stable process on
\mathbb{R}
.

Now, we consider the following time-inhomogeneous frozen SDE on
\mathbb{R}
:

(4.1)

\displaystyle dY_{t}=\left[BY_{t}+G(t,x,Y_{t})\right]dt+dZ_{t},\quad Y_{s}=y\in H.

Since
G(t,x,\cdot)
is Lipshcitz continuous, it is easy to prove that equation
(
4.1
)
has a unique mild solution denoted by
\left\{Y_{t}^{s,x,y}\right\}_{t\geqslant s}
, that is

(4.2)

\displaystyle Y^{s,x,y}_{t}=e^{(t-s)B}y+\int_{s}^{t}e^{(t-r)B}G\left(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\right)dr+\int_{s}^{t}e^{(t-r)B}dZ_{r}.

The following estimates also hold:

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
p\in[1,\alpha)
and
x,y\in H
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geqslant s}\left(\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{s,x,y}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leqslant C(1+|x|+|y|).

Moreover, for any
x_{i},y_{i}\in H
,
i=1,2
, we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\left|Y_{t}^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}-Y_{t}^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}\right|\leqslant e^{-\frac{\left(\beta_{1}-L_{G}\right)(t-s)}{2}}\left|y_{1}-y_{2}\right|+C\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|.

Proof.

(1) Recall that
(
4.2
)
and by Minkowski’s inequality, we get for any
t\geqslant s
,

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{s,x,y}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\!\leqslant

\displaystyle|e^{(t-s)B}y|\!+\!\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\int^{t}_{s}\!\!|e^{(t-r)B}G(r,x,Y_{r}^{s,x,y})|dr\right)^{p}\right]^{1/p}\!+\!\left[\mathbb{E}\left|\int^{t}_{s}\!\!e^{(t-r)B}dZ_{r}\right|^{p}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle|y|\!+\!\!\int^{t}_{s}\!\!e^{-\beta_{1}(t-r)}\left[C\!+\!C|x|\!+\!L_{G}\left(\mathbb{E}|Y_{r}^{s,x,y}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\right]dr\!+\!C\left(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\frac{\gamma^{\alpha}_{k}}{\beta_{k}}\right)^{1/\alpha}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle C(1+|x|+|y|)+\frac{L_{G}}{\beta_{1}}\sup_{t\geqslant s}\left(\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{s,x,y}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

Note that
L_{G}<\beta_{1}
, which yields that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geqslant s}\left(\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{s,x,y}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leqslant C(1+|x|+|y|).

(2) Let
N_{t}=:Y_{t}^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}-Y_{t}^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}
. It is easy to see

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}dN_{t}=\left[BN_{t}+G\left(t,x_{1},Y_{t}^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}\right)-G\left(t,x_{2},Y_{t}^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}\right)\right]dt,\\
N_{s}=y_{1}-y_{2}.\end{array}\right.

By Young’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left|N_{t}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle BN_{t},2N_{t}\right\rangle+\left\langle G\left(t,x_{1},Y_{t}^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}\right)-G\left(t,x_{2},Y_{t}^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}\right),2N_{t}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leqslant-\left(\beta_{1}-L_{G}\right)\left|N_{t}\right|^{2}+C\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|^{2}.

According to the comparison theorem, the following inequality holds

\left|N_{t}\right|^{2}\leqslant e^{-\left(\beta_{1}-L_{G}\right)(t-s)}\left|y_{1}-y_{2}\right|^{2}+C\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|^{2},

which implies
(
4.4
)
holds. The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
x,y\in H
, there exists
\eta_{t}^{x}\in L^{p}(\Omega,H)
(independent of
y
) such that for any
p\in[1,\alpha)
and
t\geqslant s

(4.5)

\displaystyle\left[\mathbb{E}\left|Y_{t}^{s,x,y}-\eta_{t}^{x}\right|^{p}\right]^{1/p}\leqslant Ce^{-\frac{\left(\beta_{1}-L_{G}\right)(t-s)}{2}}(1+|x|+|y|),\quad\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}|\eta_{t}^{x}|^{p}\leqslant C_{p}(1+|x|^{p}).

Proof.

Note that for any
h>0
,

\displaystyle Y^{s-h,x,y}_{t}=e^{(t-s)B}Y^{s-h,x,y}_{s}+\int_{s}^{t}e^{(t-r)B}G\left(r,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{r}\right)dr+\int_{s}^{t}e^{(t-r)B}dZ_{r}.

Let
H^{s,h,x,y}_{t}=:Y^{s,x,y}_{t}-Y^{s-h,x,y}_{t}
. Then it is easy to see that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}dH^{s,h,x,y}_{t}=\left[BH^{s,h,x,y}_{t}+G\left(t,x,Y^{s,x,y}_{t}\right)-G\left(t,x,Y^{s-h,x,y}_{t}\right)\right]dt,\\
H^{s,h,x,y}_{s}=y-Y^{s-h,x,y}_{s}.\end{array}\right.

Then by the same argument above, we have

\left|H^{s,h,x,y}_{t}\right|\leqslant e^{-\frac{\left(\beta_{1}-L_{G}\right)(t-s)}{2}}\left|y-Y^{s-h,x,y}_{s}\right|.

Hence, by
(
4.3
)
, it follows

\displaystyle\left[\mathbb{E}\left|H^{s,h,x,y}_{t}\right|^{p}\right]^{1/p}\leqslant

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{\left(\beta_{1}-L_{G}\right)(t-s)}{2}}\left[\mathbb{E}\left|y-Y^{s-h,x,y}_{s}\right|^{p}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle Ce^{-\frac{\left(\beta_{1}-L_{G}\right)(t-s)}{2}}(1+|x|+|y|).

This implies for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
x,y\in H
,
\{Y^{s,x,y}_{t}\}_{s\leqslant t}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{p}(\Omega,H)
as
s\to-\infty
, then there exists an element
\eta^{x,y}_{t}\in L^{p}(\Omega,H)
such that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\left[\mathbb{E}\left|Y^{s,x,y}_{t}-\eta^{x,y}_{t}\right|^{p}\right]^{1/p}\leqslant Ce^{-\frac{\left(\beta_{1}-L_{G}\right)(t-s)}{2}}(1+|x|+|y|).

Next, we are going to prove
\eta^{x,y}_{t}
is independent of
y
. In fact,
(
4.4
)
implies that for any
y_{1},y_{2}\in H
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to-\infty}\left[\mathbb{E}\left|Y_{t}^{s,x,y_{1}}-Y_{t}^{s,x,y_{2}}\right|^{p}\right]^{1/p}=0.

Hence, it follows

\displaystyle\left[\mathbb{E}\left|\eta^{x,y_{1}}_{t}-\eta^{x,y_{2}}_{t}\right|^{p}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\left[\mathbb{E}\left|\eta^{x,y_{1}}_{t}-Y^{s,x,y_{1}}_{t}\right|^{p}\right]^{1/p}+\left[\mathbb{E}\left|\eta^{x,y_{2}}_{t}-Y^{s,x,y_{2}}_{t}\right|^{p}\right]^{1/p}+\left[\mathbb{E}\left|Y^{s,x,y_{1}}_{t}-Y^{s,x,y_{2}}_{t}\right|^{p}\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle Ce^{-(\beta_{1}-L_{G})(t-s)/2}(1+|x|+|y_{1}|+|y_{2}|).

Taking
s\rightarrow-\infty
, we get
\eta^{x,y_{1}}_{t}=\eta^{x,y_{2}}_{t}
in
L^{p}(\Omega,H)
. Thus the first estimate is proved in (
4.5
).

The second estimate in (
4.5
) follows from (
4.6
) and (
4.3
). The proof is complete.
∎

Note that
\left\{Y_{t}^{s,x,y}\right\}_{t\geqslant s}
is a time-inhomogeneous Markov process, denote
P^{x}_{s,t}
is the corresponding transition semigroup of
\left\{Y_{t}^{s,x,y}\right\}_{t\geqslant s}
.
Inspired from
[
11
]
, we give the following definition.

Definition 4.3
.

A class of measures
\{\nu^{x}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is called an evolution system of measures for
\{P^{x}_{s,t}\}_{t\geqslant s}
if for any
s\leqslant t,\varphi\in C_{b}(H)
, it follows

(4.7)

\displaystyle\int_{H}P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y)\nu^{x}_{s}(dy)=\int_{H}\varphi(y)\nu^{x}_{t}(dy).

For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in H
, we denote by
\mu_{t}^{x}
as the law of
\eta^{x}_{t}
. The following result holds, since its proof closely follows the arguments in
[
11
]
, we omit the detailed proof.

Proposition 4.4
.

The class
\left\{\mu_{t}^{x}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a solution of an evolution system of measures indexed by
\mathbb{R}
for
\{P^{x}_{s,t}\}_{t\geqslant s}
. Moreover, for any Lipschitz continuous function
\phi
on
H

6.
Application to examples

In this section, we will present a concrete example to illustrate our results. Considering the following non-linear stochastic heat equation on
D=[0,\pi]
with Dirichlet boundary conditions:

(6.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle dX^{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)=\left[\Delta X^{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)+f(t/{\varepsilon},X^{\varepsilon}(t,\xi),Y^{\varepsilon}(t,\xi))\right]dt+dL(t,\xi),\\
\displaystyle dY^{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left[\Delta Y^{\varepsilon}(t,\xi)+g(t/{\varepsilon},X^{\varepsilon}(t,\xi),Y^{\varepsilon}(t,\xi))\right]dt+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{1/\alpha}}dZ(t,\xi),\\
X^{{\varepsilon}}(t,\xi)=Y^{{\varepsilon}}(t,\xi)=0,\quad t>0,\quad\xi\in\partial D,\\

where
\partial D
the boundary of

holds for

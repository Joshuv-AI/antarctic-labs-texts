Abstract
. Let G G be a simple, finite, connected, and undirected graph, and T T be a finite tree. 1.
Introduction

Let
Aut(G)
be the automorphism group of a graph
G=(V(G),E(G))
,
id_{G}
be the trivial automorphism of
G
, and
f
be a vertex coloring or an edge coloring or a total coloring (that is, a coloring of its vertices and edges) of
G
.
An automorphism
\phi
of
G

preserves

f
if the following holds: (1)
f(x)=f(\phi(x))
for all
x\in V(G)
if
f
is a vertex coloring, (2)
f(e)=f(\phi(e))
for all
e\in E(G)
if
f
is an edge coloring, (3)
f(x)=f(\phi(x))
for all
x\in V(G)\cup E(G)
if
f
is a total coloring.

1.1.
Distinguishing number and distinguishing index

Albertson and Collins
[
1
]
introduced the
distinguishing number

D(G)
of a graph
G
as the least integer
d
such that
G
admits a vertex coloring with
d
colors that is only preserved by
id_{G}
and Kalinowski and Pilśniak
[
16
]
introduced the
distinguishing index

D^{\prime}(G)
of
G
as the least integer
d
such that
G
admits an edge coloring with
d
colors that is only preserved by
id_{G}
.
In
[
4
, Theorems 3.8 and 4.3]
, Alikhani and Soltani proved that if
G
is a connected graph of order
n\geq 3
, then
D(S(G))\leq\lceil\sqrt{\Delta(G)}\rceil

and
D^{\prime}(S(G))\leq min\{r:r^{2}-r\geq D^{\prime}(G)\}
where
\Delta(G)
is the maximum degree of
G
.
Hamada and Yoshimura
[
13
]
and Vernold
[
31
]
introduced the concept of middle graphs and
central graphs, respectively; classes of graphs derived from subdivision graphs.
Since then, the notion of central graphs and middle graphs has been intensively studied in graph theory; see, for example,
[
5
,
9
,
11
,
12
,
18
,
19
,
23
,
28
]
.

Results:

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n
. The following holds:

(1)

If
n\geq 3
, then
D(M(G))\leq\Delta(G)
,

(2)

D^{\prime}(M(G))\leq 3
for any positive integer
n
, and

(3)

If
n\geq 4
, then
D^{\prime}(C(G))\leq\lceil\sqrt{\Delta(G)}\rceil
, and
D(C(G))\leq\lceil\sqrt{\Delta(G)}\rceil
.

Moreover, the bounds mentioned in (1) and (3) are sharp (see Theorems
3.2
,
3.4
,
3.6
).

1.2.
Total distinguishing chromatic number

A
proper total coloring
of a graph
G
is a total coloring of
G
so that no adjacent vertices, no adjacent edges, and no incident vertices and edges get the same color.
The least number of colors needed for a proper total coloring of
G
is called its
total chromatic number
, denoted by
\chi^{\prime\prime}(G)
.

Behzad
[
6
]
and Vizing
[
33
]
independently posed the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1
.

(Total-Coloring Conjecture (TCC)).
For any graph
G
,
\chi^{\prime\prime}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+2
.

Recently, Panda et al.
[
28
, Theorem 3.1]

proved that TCC holds for the central graph of any graph of order
n\geq 5
. We observe an alternative short proof of the result using the following lemma.

Lemma 1.2
.

(
[
36
, Lemma 2.4]
)

If
G
is a graph of order
n
that contains
an independent set
S
such that
|S|\leq n-\Delta(G)-1
, then
G
satisfies TCC.

Theorem 1.3
.

(Panda–Verma–Keerti;
[
28
]
)

TCC holds for the central graph of any graph.

Short proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
C(G)
be the central graph of a graph
G=(V(G),E(G))
where
V_{1}=V(G)
,
V_{2}=V(C(G))\backslash V(G)
,
|V_{1}|=n
, and
|V_{2}|=r
. If
n\geq 3
, then the order of
C(G)
is
n+r
,
\Delta(C(G))=n-1
, and
C(G)
contains an independent set
V_{2}
of size
r=(n+r)-\Delta(C(G))-1
. By Lemma
1.2
,
C(G)
satisfies TCC. If
n\leq 2
, then it is trivial to verify.
∎

We say
G
is of
Type
j
if
\chi^{\prime\prime}(G)=\Delta(G)+j
for
j\in\{1,2\}
. In
[
15
]
, Kalinowski, Pilśniak, and Woźniak introduced the
total distinguishing chromatic number

\chi^{\prime\prime}_{D}(G)
of a graph

as the least integer
d
such that
G
has a proper total coloring with
d
colors that is preserved only by
id_{G}
. We study the total distinguishing chromatic number of central and subdivision graphs.

Results:

Let

. The following holds:

(Proposition

(Theorem

1.3.
AVD-total chromatic number

An
adjacent vertex distinguishing (AVD) total coloring
of a graph
G
with edge set
E(G)
is a proper total coloring
f
such that for any edge
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
, we have
C_{G}(u)\neq C_{G}(v)
, where
C_{G}(u)=\{f(u)\}\cup\{f(\{u,v\}):\{u,v\}\in E(G)\}
is the color class of
u
. The
adjacent vertex distinguishing (AVD)-total chromatic number

\chi^{\prime\prime}_{a}(G)
of
G
is the minimum integer
k
such that there exists an AVD-total coloring of
G
using
k
colors. Zhang et al.
[
37
]
introduced AVD-total-colorings and posed the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.4
.

(AVD-TCC).
For any graph
G
,
\chi^{\prime\prime}_{a}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+3
.

We say
G
is of
AVD-Type
i
if
\chi^{\prime\prime}_{a}(G)=\Delta(G)+i
for
i\in\{1,2,3\}
.
The Conjecture
1.4
for general graphs
G
with
\Delta(G)=3
has been verified by Wang
[
34
]
and Chen
[
7
]
.
Lu et al.
[
22
]
validated the conjecture for all graphs
G
with
\Delta(G)=4
.
The Conjecture
1.4
is also known to be true for many families of graphs such as 4-regular graphs
[
26
]
, hypercubes
[
8
]
, complete equipartite graphs
[
24
]
, indifference graphs
[
27
]
, split graphs
[
32
]
, and several other classes of graphs.
Panda et al.
[
28
]

obtained the AVD-total chromatic number of central graphs for some known families of graphs and mentioned the following open question (see
[
28
, Section 5]
).

Question 1.5
.

(Panda et al.
[
28
]
) Does AVD-TCC hold for the central graph of any graph?

In
[
28
, Section 5]
, it was mentioned that it would be interesting to solve the AVD-total coloring classification problem on the central graph of any graph.
We have made partial progress in this direction.

Results:

The following holds:

(1)

(Theorem
5.1
) If
G
is a regular graph of even order

, then
C(G)
is AVD-Type 2 and if
G
is a regular graph of order

, then

satisfies AVD-TCC.

(2)

(Theorem

) If
\Delta(G)\geq 5
, then the subdivision graph

of

(3)

(Proposition
5.5
(4)) If two graphs

and

1.4.
Total dominator chromatic number

The notion of total domination number was introduced by Cockayne, Dawes and Hedetniemi in
[
10
]
and has been studied for central graphs and middle graphs, see
[
9
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
.
In particular, Kazemnejad and Moradi
[
19
]
and
Chen et al.
[
9
]

studied sharp upper bounds for the total domination number of central graphs of connected graphs and trees.
Motivated by the relation between the chromatic number and the total domination number, the notion of the total dominator chromatic number was studied in
[
17
]
.
A
total dominator coloring
, briefly TDC, of a graph
G
is a proper vertex coloring of
G
in
which each vertex of
G
is adjacent to every vertex of some color class.
The
total dominator chromatic number

\chi^{t}_{d}(G)
of
G
is the minimum
number of color classes in a TDC of
G
.
The total dominator chromatic number was studied for middle graphs by Kazemnejad et al.
[
20
]
,
k
-subdivision of graphs by Alikhani et al.
[
2
]
, and central graphs by Kazemnejad et al.
[
18
]
.
In
[
18
, Theorem 2.1]
, Kazemnejad and Kazemi proved that the following bounds are sharp if
G
is a connected graph of order
n\geq 2
:

•

\lfloor 2n/3\rfloor+1\leq\chi^{t}_{d}(C(G))\leq\Delta(C(G))+2
if
\Delta(G)\leq n-2
.

•

\chi^{t}_{d}(C(G))\leq n+\lceil n/2\rceil
if
G
has a Hamiltonian path.

Results:

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n\geq 5
. The following holds:

(1)

If
\Delta(G)\leq n-3
and
\overline{G}
is the complement of
G
, then
\chi^{t}_{d}(\overline{G})\leq\chi^{t}_{d}(C(G))\leq\Delta(C(G))+1
.

(2)

If
G
is a tree, then
\chi^{t}_{d}(C(G))\leq\Delta(C(G))+1
.

Moreover, the upper bounds of
\chi^{t}_{d}(C(G))
mentioned in (1) and (2) are sharp (see Theorem
6.1
).

2.
Automorphism groups

Definition 2.1
.

The
subdivision graph
S(G)
of a graph
G=(V(G),E(G))
is
a bipartite graph with the set of vertices
V(S(G))=V_{1}\cup V_{2}
where
V_{2}=\{w_{v,v^{\prime}}:w_{v,v^{\prime}}\text{ subdivide the edge }\{v,v^{\prime}\}\in E(G)\}
and
V_{1}=V(G)
, and the set of edges
E(S(G))=\{\{v,w_{v,v^{\prime}}\},\{w_{v,v^{\prime}},v^{\prime}\}\}:\{v,v^{\prime}\}\in E(G)\}
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
central graph

C(G)
of a graph
G=(V(G),E(G))
of order
n
and size

is a
graph of order
n+m
and size
n\choose{2}
+

that is obtained by subdividing each edge of

exactly once and joining all the non-adjacent vertices of
G
in

.
In particular,

is a graph with the set of vertices
V(C(G))=V(S(G))
and the set of edges
E(C(G))=E(\overline{G})\cup\{\{v,w_{v,v^{\prime}}\},\{w_{v,v^{\prime}},v^{\prime}\}\}:\{v,v^{\prime}\}\in E(G)\}

is the complement of
G
.

In this section, we sketch some lemmas concerning
Aut(C(G))
and

where

is the middle graph of

Lemma 2.3
.

If
\psi\in Aut(C(G))
such that
\psi(u)=u
for all
u\in V_{1}
, then
\psi=id_{C(G)}
.

Proof.

First, we note that
N_{C(G)}(a)\neq N_{C(G)}(b)
for any
a,b\in V_{2}
.
Let
x\in V_{2}
. If
\psi(x)=y
for some
y\in V_{2}\backslash\{x\}
, then
\psi(N_{C(G)}(x))=N_{C(G)}(y)
since automorphisms preserve adjacency.
Since for every
v\in V_{1}
,
\psi(v)=v
, we have
\psi(N_{C(G)}(x))=N_{C(G)}(x)
.
Thus,
N_{C(G)}(x)=N_{C(G)}(y)
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n\geq 4
. If
\varphi\in Aut(C(G))
, then
\varphi(V_{1})=V_{1}
and
\varphi(V_{2})=V_{2}
.

Proof.

In
C(G)
, each vertices of
V_{1}
is of degree
n-1\geq 3
and each vertices of
V_{2}
is of degree
2
. The rest follows from the fact that
deg_{C(G)}(\phi(v))=deg_{C(G)}(v)
for any
v\in V(C(G))
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be a connected graph of order
n\geq 4
. Then
Aut(G)\cong Aut(C(G))
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in Aut(G)
.
Define the mapping
\pi_{\alpha}:V(C(G))\rightarrow V(C(G))
as follows:

\displaystyle\pi_{\alpha}(w)=\begin{cases}\alpha(w)&\text{if }w\in V(G)\\
w_{\alpha(x),\alpha(y)}&\text{if }w\in V(C(G))\backslash V(G),w=w_{x,y},x,y\in V(G).\end{cases}

We can see that
\pi_{\alpha}\in Aut(C(G))
. If
\{x,y\}\in E(C(G))
then
\{x,y\}\in E(\overline{G})
or
\{x,y\}\in E(S(G))
.
By the definition of
\pi_{\alpha}
, if
\{x,y\}\in E(S(G))
then
\{\pi_{\alpha}(x),\pi_{\alpha}(y)\}\in E(S(G))
. Moreover, we have

\{x,y\}\in E(\overline{G})\iff\{x,y\}\not\in E(G)\iff\{\alpha(x),\alpha(y)\}\not\in E(G)\iff\{\alpha(x),\alpha(y)\}\in E(\overline{G})\iff\{\pi_{\alpha}(x),\pi_{\alpha}(y)\}\in E(\overline{G})
.
Thus, if
H=\{\pi_{\alpha}:\alpha\in Aut(G)\}
, then
H\subseteq Aut(C(G))
. We
show that
Aut(C(G))\subseteq H
. Let
f\in Aut(C(G))
. By Lemma
2.4
, we have
f(V_{1})=V_{1}
since
G
is a connected graph of order at least
4
.
If
\alpha=f\restriction V_{1}
, then
\alpha\in Aut(G)
. Let
\pi_{\alpha}
be the induced automorphism of
\alpha
as defined above.
If we let
l=f^{-1}\pi_{\alpha}
, then
l
is an automorphism of
C(G)
such that
l(v)=v
for every
v\in V_{1}
. By Lemma
2.3
,
l=id_{C(G)}
. Consequently,
f=\pi_{\alpha}
. Therefore,
Aut(C(G))\subseteq H
, and so
H=Aut(C(G))
. It is trivial to see that
H\cong Aut(G)
, so we are done.
∎

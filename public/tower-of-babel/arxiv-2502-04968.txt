Duality of Selmer groups of an abelian variety over a number field
Saikat Biswas

Abstract
. Let A A be an abelian variety defined over a number field K K and let A ∨ A^{\vee} be the dual abelian variety. For an odd prime p p , we consider two Selmer groups attached to A ⁡ [ p ] A[p] and relate the orders of these groups along with those of their corresponding duals to the order of the component groups of A ∨ A^{\vee} at primes v v .

1.
Introduction

In order to motivate the main result of this paper, we begin with a quick review of
generalized Selmer groups
, fixing notations along the way. Let
K
be a number field with Galois group
G_{K}:=\Gal(\overline{K}/K)
, for a separable closure
\overline{K}
of
K
. For every prime
v
of
K
, let
K_{v}
be the completion of
K
at
v
with Galois group
G_{v}:=G_{K_{v}}
. When
v
is finite, we denote by
K_{v}^{\ur}
the maximal unramified extension of
K_{v}
whose Galois group is identified with the
inertia group

I_{v}\subset G_{v}
at
v
. The Galois group of the finite residue field
k_{v}
of
K_{v}
is given by the quotient group
G_{v}/I_{v}
. Let
M
be a discrete
G_{K}
-module of finite cardinality,
\mu_{\infty}
be the
G_{K}
-module of roots of unity in
\overline{K}
, and
M^{*}:=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,\mu_{\infty})
the
Cartier dual
of
M
. We shall also denote by
\widehat{M}:=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
the
Pontryagin dual
of
M
. There is a continuous discrete action of
G_{K}
on
M^{*}
arising out of the corresponding action of
G_{K}
on
M
as well as on
{\overline{K}}^{\times}
. We consider a
Selmer structure

\mathcal{L}
for
M
, i.e. a family of subgroups
H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(K_{v},M)\subset H^{1}(K_{v},M)
for every prime
v
such that
H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(K_{v},M)=H^{1}(G_{v}/I_{v},M^{I_{v}})
for all but finitely many
v
. The corresponding Selmer structure
\mathcal{L}^{*}
for
M^{*}
is the family of subgroups
H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}^{*}}(K_{v},M^{*})\subset H^{1}(K_{v},M^{*})
, where
H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}^{*}}(K_{v},M^{*})
is the orthogonal complement of
H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(K_{v},M)
under the local Tate pairing

H^{1}(K_{v},M)\times H^{1}(K_{v},M^{*})\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}

The
generalized Selmer group

H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(K,M)
of
M
is the subgroup of all classes in
H^{1}(K,M)
whose image under the restriction map

\Res_{v}:H^{1}(K,M)\to H^{1}(K_{v},M)

lies in
H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(K_{v},M)
for every
v
. The Selmer group
H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}^{*}}(K,M^{*})
of
M^{*}
is defined analogously, and is called the
dual Selmer group
attached to
H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(K,M)
. We have the following important result due to Wiles
[
Wil95
]
, suggested by a result of Greenberg
[
Gre89
]
, which allows for the comparison of the order of a Selmer group to that of its dual.

Theorem 1.1
.

The groups
H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(K,M)
and
H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}^{*}}(K,M^{*})
are finite, and we have

\frac{\#H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(K,M)}{\#H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}^{*}}(K,M^{*})}=\frac{\#H^{0}(K,M)}{\#H^{0}(K,M^{*})}\displaystyle\prod_{v}\frac{\#H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(K_{v},M)}{\#H^{0}(K_{v},M)}

Since
\#H^{1}(G_{v}/I_{v},M^{I_{v}})=\#H^{0}(K_{v},M)

[
DDT95
, §2.3]
, all but finitely many terms in the product on the right hand side are
1
so that the product makes sense. The formulation of Theorem
1.1
as given above appears in
[
DDT95
]
which is slightly different from that presented in
[
Wil95
]
, but the two are equivalent. Following a terminology suggested by Darmon in
[
Dar04
]
, we refer to the term on the right side of Theorem
1.1
as the
Euler characteristic
attached to
H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(K,M)
and denote it by
\chi_{\mathcal{L}}(K,M)
. Thus, we can write

(1.1)

\chi_{\mathcal{L}}(K,M)=\frac{\#H^{0}(K,M)}{\#H^{0}(K,M^{*})}\displaystyle\prod_{v}\frac{\#H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(K_{v},M)}{\#H^{0}(K_{v},M)}

If
\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}
are Selmer structures for
M
, we say that
\mathcal{L}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{2}
if
H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}(K_{v},M)\subseteq H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}(K_{v},M)
for every
v
. In this case, it can be shown that
H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}(K,M)\subseteq H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}(K,M)
and that

\mathcal{L}_{1}^{*}\supseteq\mathcal{L}_{2}^{*}
. The next corollary follows immediately from (
1.1
) above.

Corollary 1.2
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{L}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{2}
are Selmer structures for
M
. Then we have

(1.2)

\frac{\chi_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}(K,M)}{\chi_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}(K,M)}=\displaystyle\prod_{v}\frac{\#H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}(K_{v},M)}{\#H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}(K_{v},M)}

In particular, the comparison of the Euler characteristics attached to two different Selmer groups is a purely local calculation. We can also rewrite (
1.2
), in view of Theorem
1.1
, as

(1.3)

\frac{\#H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}(K,M)}{\#H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{*}}(K,M^{*})}=\chi_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}(K,M)\cdot\displaystyle\prod_{v}\frac{\#H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}(K_{v},M)}{\#H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}(K_{v},M)}

The goal of this paper is to carry out the computation involved in Corollary
1.2
for the case when, for an odd prime
p
,
M=A[p]
is the
p
-torsion subgroup of an abelian variety
A
defined over
K
. Let
A^{\vee}
be the dual abelian variety. Then it can be shown that
M^{*}=A^{\vee}[p]
. Let
S
be any finite set of primes of
K
containing all archimedean primes, all primes above
p
, and all primes of bad reduction for
A
. We consider three Selmer structures for
A[p]
. The first, denoted by
\Sel_{p}(A/K_{v})
, is defined by
\im(\delta_{v})
, where
\delta_{v}
is the injection

A(K_{v})/pA(K_{v})\hookrightarrow H^{1}(K_{v},A[p])

arising out of the
Kummer exact sequence
. The second, denoted by
H^{1}_{(S)}(K_{v},A[p])
, is defined by
\ker(\varphi)
, where
\varphi
is the composition

H^{1}(K_{v},A[p])\to H^{1}(K_{v},A)[p]\to H^{1}(K_{v}^{\ur},A)[p]

The third, denoted by
H^{1}_{[S]}(K_{v},A[p])
, is defined by
\delta_{v}(\kappa_{v})
, where
\kappa_{v}
is the kernel of the map

A(K_{v})/pA(K_{v})\to\Phi_{A,v}(k_{v})/p\Phi_{A,v}(k_{v})

Here,
\Phi_{A,v}(k_{v})
is the
arithmetic component group
of
A
at
v
.

The respective Selmer groups, all subgroups of
H^{1}(K,A[p])
, are denoted by
\Sel_{p}(A/K),\;H^{1}_{(S)}(K,A[p])
, and
H^{1}_{[S]}(K,A[p])
. The corresponding Selmer groups for
A^{\vee}[p]
are defined similarly. Our main result in this paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3

(Main Theorem)
.

Under the notations introduced above, we have

\frac{\chi_{(S)}(K,A[p])}{\chi_{\scriptscriptstyle{\Sel_{p}}}(K,A[p])}=\#\bigoplus_{v\in{S}}\Phi_{A^{\vee},v}(k_{v})[p]

or, equivalently

\frac{\#H^{1}_{(S)}(K,A[p])}{\#H^{1}_{[S]}(K,A^{\vee}[p])}=\frac{\#\Sel_{p}(A/K)}{\#\Sel_{p}(A^{\vee}/K)}\cdot\#\bigoplus_{v\in{S}}\Phi_{A^{\vee},v}(k_{v})[p]

The local terms on the right hand side of Theorem
1.3
can be computed explicitly by Tate’s algorithm
[
Sil94
, IV.§9]
when
A
is an elliptic curve, by the monodromy pairing
[
Gro73
, §10]
when
A
is a semistable abelian variety, and by the intersection pairing
[
BL99
, §1]
when
A
is the Jacobian of a curve.

Corollary 1.4
.

Suppose that
A
is an elliptic curve defined over
K
and
p
is a prime of good reduction for
A
. Then

\frac{\#H^{1}_{(S)}(K,A[p])}{\#H^{1}_{[S]}(K,A[p])}=\#\bigoplus_{v\in{S}}\Phi_{A,v}(k_{v})[p]

and

\#\Sel_{p}(A/K)<\#H^{1}_{[S]}(K,A[p])\;\#\bigoplus_{v\in{S}}\Phi_{A,v}(k_{v})[p]

Proof.

In this case,
A^{\vee}\cong A
and
\chi_{\scriptscriptstyle{\Sel_{p}}}(K,A[p])=1

[
Dar04
, Prop. 10.15]
. The first equality then follows immediately from Theorem
1.3
. To prove the second inequality, we show in Lemma
3.1
below that there are inclusions

H^{1}_{[S]}(K,A[p])\subset\Sel_{p}(A/K)\subset H^{1}_{(S)}(K,A[p])

which in turn imply that

\#H^{1}_{[S]}(K,A[p])<\#\Sel_{p}(A/K)<\#H^{1}_{(S)}(K,A[p])

The desired inequality now follows by dividing throughout by
\#H^{1}_{[S]}(K,A[p])
and using the first equality.
∎

In Section 2, we investigate the different Selmer structures on
A[p]
and the corresponding dual Selmer structures on
A^{\vee}[p]
. We relate the order of these structures to each other using Tate local duality as our key ingredient. In Section 3, we consider the corresponding Selmer groups on

\displaystyle 1\to H^{1}_{[S]}(K,A[p])\to\Sel_{p}(A/K)

\displaystyle\to\bigoplus_{v\in{S}}\im(\delta_{v})/\delta_{v}(\kappa_{v})

The proposition now follows from Eqs (
2.4
), (
2.7
), and (
2.9
).
∎

Furthermore, we find that the dual Selmer groups attached to each of these aforementioned Selmer groups can be identified as

(3.6)

\displaystyle\Sel_{p^{*}}(A^{\vee}/K)

\displaystyle=\Sel_{p}(A^{\vee}/K)

(3.7)

\displaystyle H^{1}_{(S)^{*}}(K,A^{\vee}[p])

\displaystyle=H^{1}_{[S]}(K,A^{\vee}[p])

The Euler characteristics attached to the Selmer groups
\Sel_{p}(A/K)
and
H^{1}_{(S)}(K,A[p])
can be given, using Theorem
1.1
, as

(3.8)

\displaystyle\chi_{\Sel_{p}}(K,A[p])

\displaystyle=\frac{\#\Sel_{p}(A/K)}{\#\Sel_{p}(A^{\vee}/K)}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\chi_{(S)}(K,A[p])

\displaystyle=\frac{\#H^{1}_{(S)}(K,A[p])}{\#H^{1}_{[S]}(K,A^{\vee}[p])}

Using Eqs (
1.2
) and (
2.8
), we obtain

\frac{\chi_{(S)}(K,A[p])}{\chi_{\Sel_{p}}(K,A[p])}=\prod_{v}\frac{\#H^{1}_{(S)}(K_{v},A[p])}{\#\Sel_{p}(A/K_{v})}=\#\bigoplus_{v\in{S}}\Phi_{A^{\vee},v}(k_{v})[p]

which proves Theorem
1.3
.

Consider the following exact sequences from Proposition

\textstyle{H^{1}(G_{S},A[p])\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

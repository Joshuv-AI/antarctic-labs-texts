Quantum Filtering for Squeezed Noise Inputs
John Gough, Dylon ReesDepartment of Physics, Aberystwyth University, Wales, UK. † {\dagger} Orcid ID: 0000-0002-1374-328X ♯ \sharp jug@aber.ac.uk ∗ \ast dyr6@aber.ac.uk

Abstract
We derive the quantum filter for a quantum open system undergoing quadrature measurements (homodyning) where the input field is in a general quasi-free state. This extends previous work for thermal input noise and allows for squeezed inputs. We introduce a convenient class of Bogoliubov transformations which we refer to as balanced and formulate the quantum stochastic model with squeezed noise as an Araki-Woods type representation. We make an essential use of the Tomita-Takesaki theory to construct the commutant of the C*-algebra describing the inputs and obtain the filtering equations using the quantum reference probability technique. The derived quantum filter must be independent of the choice of representation and this is achieved by fixing an independent quadrature in the commutant algebra.

Introduction

The problem of quantum estimation comes essentially down to giving an optimal observable
\widehat{X}
constructed from the measured observables to approximate a given compatible observable
X
. The measurements are naturally required to be compatible (that is, they form a commutative algebra
\mathfrak{M}
) and the observables that we may estimate must likewise be compatible with the measurements (and so belong to the commutant
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
. In quantum filtering problems, the measurement algebra is growing in time as new measurements are made, thereby making the problem a dynamic one
[
1
,
2
]
. Optimality is formulated in a least squares sense
[
3
,
4
]
. Traditionally, this problem has been formulated for a quantum open systems where a fixed system interacts with driving (bosonic) vacuum noise process
[
5
]
and quadrature measurements (homodyning) are made on the output noise processes
[
6
]
. However, coherent state input
[
7
]
, multi-photon inputs and superposition of coherent states (Schrödinger cat) inputs
[
8
]
have also been shown to be amenable.

More recently, the quantum filtering problem has been formulated and solved for thermal input fields
[
9
]
. In this case, the input fields are not unitarily equivalent to vacuum fields
[
10
]
, and one also cannot obtain them by an embedding or dilation step. Instead, one had to rely on the full Tomita-Takesaki theory
[
11
]
where an Araki-Woods representation
[
12
]
was used for the thermal inputs and where the commutant algebra was more involved than in the standard Weyl-Fock setting.

The purpose of this paper is to extend this result to bosonic input processes in general quasi-free states. We are in the fortunate position that a variant of the Araki-Woods technique applies here. In fact, one can simplify the machinery of Tomita-Takesaki in the spirit of Rieffel and van Daele
[
13
]
and consider this in terms of projections on to symplectically complementary subspaces at the one-particle level which is then second-quantized. This route has been successfully exploited by Belton, Lindsay and coauthors
[
14
]
.

We will use the underlying concepts of the CCR C*-algebra, Bogoliubov transformations, etc., see
[
15
,
16
,
17
]
. However, the basic formulation is understandable in terms of more familiar concepts from quantum optics, in particular, two-mode squeezing and we make use of standard results on boson modes in a finite-dimensional setting
[
18
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the features behind two-mode squeezing. Here, we introduce the definition of
balanced Bogoliubov transformations
which are a convenient class. An essential role is played by the Hellmich-Honegger-Köstler-Kümmerer-Rieckers representation
[
21
]
for squeezed noise, however, we use the simple boson mode setting as an accessible way to segue into their results and we end up using the same algebraic coefficients. In Section
3

4

Here we have an open quantum system described by a unitary process
U_{t}
satisfying a quantum stochastic differential equation
[
10
]
driven by squeezed bosonic input processes

. This output processes

Two-mode Squeezing

We take
\mathfrak{F}

The parameter

\displaystyle\lambda_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(2n+1)\pm\sqrt{|m|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}}

(6)

2.1
Balanced Bogoliubov Transformations

The Araki-Woods representation is the well-known construction for a thermal state given by

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{n+1}\,a_{1}+\sqrt{n}\,a_{2}^{\ast},

\displaystyle b_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\,a_{1}^{\ast}+\sqrt{n+1}\,a_{2}.

(49)

Here, the modes have thermal syaye marginals with number parameter
n
. The first mode gives the standard GNS construction, while the second mode is the commuting representation coming from Tomita-Takesaki theory. In this case, we see that

\displaystyle C=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}n+1&0\\
0&n\end{array}\right],\qquad K=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\sqrt{n(n+1)}\\
\sqrt{n(n+1)}&0\end{array}\right].

We note that even though the modes
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
yield commuting representation of the thermal state, with the joint vacuum of
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
as GNS state, they are nevertheless correlated!

It is natural to ask if there is a similar construction leading to two modes which have identical squeezed marginals. To this end, we observe that the Bogoliubov and mode state coefficient identities are symmetric under the combined interchanges
x\leftrightarrow z

and

y\leftrightarrow w
. This suggests the following class of Bogoliubov transformations which we term balanced (the Araki-Woods case being an example). We shall often just say that the two modes obtained this way are balanced.

Definition 3

We say that a Bogoliubov transformation of the form (
2
) is balanced if the coefficients of the two modes are related by

\displaystyle x_{2}=z_{1},\quad y_{2}=w_{1},\quad z_{2}=x_{1},\quad w_{2}=y_{1}.

(55)

A balanced Bogoliubov transformation is then of the form

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\,a_{1}+z\,a_{2}+y\,a_{1}^{\ast}+w\,a_{2}^{\ast},

\displaystyle b_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle z\,a_{1}+x\,a_{2}+w\,a_{1}^{\ast}+y\,a_{2}^{\ast}.

(56)

From (
2
), we see that the parameters
x,y,z,w
are complex and are required to satisfy the identities

\displaystyle|x|^{2}+|z|^{2}-|y|^{2}-|w|^{2}=1,

(57)

\displaystyle xz^{\ast}+zx^{\ast}-yw^{\ast}-wy^{\ast}=0.

(58)

The induced states of
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
have the same number and squeezing parameters. In fact, when
C
is given by (
2
) and the inter-mode correlations are given by

\displaystyle K=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}u&v\\
v^{\ast}&u\end{array}\right]

with state parameters being

\displaystyle n=|y|^{2}+|w|^{2},\qquad m=xy+zw,

\displaystyle v=2\,\text{Re}(y^{\ast}w)\equiv 2\,\text{Re}(x^{\ast}z),\qquad u=xw+zy.

(62)

Example (Bishop-Vourdas):
An example of a balanced Bogoliubov transformation is obtained as follows. We select the following unitaries,

\displaystyle R_{i}(\phi)=e^{i\phi a_{i}^{\ast}a_{i}},\quad U_{i}(\rho)=e^{-\frac{\rho}{4}(a_{i}^{2}-a_{i}^{\ast 2})},\quad V(r)=e^{-\frac{1}{2}(a_{1}a_{2}-a_{1}^{\ast}a_{2}^{\ast})}

(63)

and set
U=R_{1}(\theta/2)R_{2}(\theta/2)U_{1}(\rho)U_{2}(\rho)V(r)
. Then
b_{i}=U^{\ast}a_{i}U
is a balanced Bogoliubov transformation
[
18
]
with

\displaystyle x=e^{i\theta/2}c_{r}c_{\rho},\quad y=e^{i\theta/2}c_{r}s_{\rho},\quad z=e^{i\theta/2}s_{r}s_{\rho},\quad w=e^{i\theta/2}s_{r}c_{\rho},

(64)

where
c_{r}=\cosh(r/2),s_{r}=\sinh(r/2),\,c_{\rho}=\cosh(\rho/2),s_{\rho}=\sinh(\rho/2)
. In this case,

,

. (One may check that

in this case.)

An alternative one is the following one
[

\displaystyle x=\kappa_{+}(\frac{\rho}{2}+\frac{m}{2}+\frac{1}{4}),\quad y=\kappa_{+}(\frac{\rho}{2}+\frac{m}{2}-\frac{1}{4}),

\displaystyle z=\kappa_{-}(\frac{\rho}{2}-\frac{m}{2}-\frac{1}{4}),\quad w=\kappa_{-}(\frac{\rho}{2}-\frac{m}{2}+\frac{1}{4}).

3
Symplectic Structure on Hilbert Spaces

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a separable complex Hilbert space and let us decompose its inner product into

\displaystyle\langle\phi|\psi\rangle=g(\phi,\psi)+i\,\sigma(\phi,\psi)

(67)

where

(metric form)

\displaystyle\quad g(\phi,\psi)=\text{Re}\langle\phi|\psi\rangle,

(symplectic form)

\displaystyle\quad\sigma(\phi,\psi)=\text{Im}\langle\phi|\psi\rangle.

(68)

We note that
g
and
\sigma
are symmetric and anti-symmetric forms, respectively, and that both are non-degenerate. Moreover, they are only
real
linear in their arguments though are related by

\displaystyle g(\phi,\psi)=\sigma(\phi,i\psi),\qquad\sigma(\phi,\psi)=g(i\phi,\psi).

(69)

For a given set of vectors
D\subset\mathfrak{h}
, its symplectic complement is defined to be

\displaystyle D^{\prime}\triangleq\{\phi\in\mathfrak{h}:\sigma(\phi,\psi)=0,\,\forall\psi\in D\}.

(70)

From the relation (
69
) between the metric and symplectic forms, we see that the symplectic complement
D^{\prime}
may alternatively be described as the
g
-orthogonal complement of
iD
.

In general, a real Hilbert subspace is said to be
standard
if

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{k}+i\mathfrak{k}\text{ is dense in }\mathfrak{k},\text{ and}

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{k}\cap i\mathfrak{k}\equiv\{0\}.

One may readily show that a real Hilbert subspace is standard if and only if its symplectic complement is standard.

3.1
Complex Conjugations

A complex conjugation
\jmath
on
\mathfrak{h}
is a bijection which is anti-unitary (i.e,
\langle\jmath\phi|\jmath\psi\rangle=\langle\psi|\phi\rangle
for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathfrak{h}
) and involutive (
\jmath^{2}=I
). (The familiar complex conjugation operation for standard representations of Hilbert spaces being the simplest example.) It follows that
\jmath^{-1}\equiv\jmath^{\ast}
. Complex conjugations are easily seen to be real linear, but not complex linear; indeed,
\jmath c\psi=c^{\ast}\jmath\psi
for complex scalars
c
.

The conjugation leaves the metric invariant, that is,
g(\jmath\phi,\jmath\psi)=g(\phi,\psi)
. It similarly introduces a sign change for the symplectic form.

For each vector
\psi\in\mathfrak{h}
, we introduce the “quadrature”  vectors

\displaystyle\psi_{q}\triangleq\frac{1}{2}\psi+\frac{1}{2}\jmath\psi,\qquad\psi_{p}\triangleq\frac{1}{2}\psi-\frac{1}{2}\jmath\psi

(71)

and these have the symmetries
\jmath\psi_{q}=\psi_{q}
and
\jmath\psi_{p}=-\psi_{p}
. Let us write
\mathfrak{h}_{q}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{p}
for the closure of
\{\psi_{q}:\psi\in\mathfrak{h}\}
and
\{\psi_{p}:\psi\in\mathfrak{h}\}
respectively. These are
real
Hilbert spaces which we refer to as the quadrature spaces corresponding to the complex conjugation
\jmath
. As every vector admits the decomposition
\psi=\psi_{q}+\psi_{p}
, we have
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}_{q}+\mathfrak{h}_{p}
. We also see that the only vector common to both quadrature spaces is the zero vector. Therefore,
\mathfrak{h}_{q}
is standard, as is
\mathfrak{h}_{p}
.

As
\jmath i\psi=-i\jmath\psi
, we see that
\mathfrak{h}_{q}\equiv i\mathfrak{h}_{p}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{p}\equiv i\mathfrak{h}_{q}
. In fact, the conjugation is determined by specifying its
q
-quadrature space: equivalently specifying a complete basis. We may identify
\mathfrak{h}
as either
\mathfrak{h}_{q}+\mathfrak{h}_{p}
, or as
\mathfrak{h}_{q}+i\mathfrak{h}_{q}
. The subspaces are not generally orthogonal, but are
g
-orthogonal: let
\phi\in\mathfrak{h}_{q}
and
\psi\in\mathfrak{h}_{p}
then
g(\phi,\psi)=g(\jmath\phi,\jmath\psi)=-g(\phi,\psi)
.

Let
A
be a (complex) linear operator on
\mathfrak{h}
with adjoint
A^{\ast}
, then we define its conjugation and transpose, respectively, by

\displaystyle A^{\#}=\jmath A\jmath,\qquad A^{\top}=\jmath A^{\ast}\jmath.

(72)

It is convenient to introduce the “complexified”  Hilbert space
\mathfrak{h}\oplus\jmath\mathfrak{h}
. We take
\breve{\mathfrak{h}}
as the closed real subspace spanned by vectors of the form

\displaystyle\breve{\psi}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\psi\\
\jmath\psi\end{array}\right],\qquad\psi\in\mathfrak{h}.

\breve{\phi}=\breve{S}\breve{\psi}
.

The condition that

be a symplectomorphism may be equivalently stated as
\breve{S}
being invertible on

\displaystyle\breve{S}^{-1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{rr}S_{-}^{\ast}&-S_{+}^{\top}\\

3.3
Second Quantization

The CCR algebra over a separable Hilbert space
\mathfrak{h}
is the C*-algebra generated by elements
w(\phi)
, for
\phi\in\mathfrak{h}
, with the relations
w(\phi)w(\psi)=e^{-i\sigma(\phi,\psi)}w(\phi+\psi)
and
w(\psi)^{\ast}=w(-\psi)
. By a result of Slawny, the CCR algebra is unique up to isomorphism and will be denoted as
CCR(\mathfrak{h})
.

The algebra has the Weyl-Fock representation
\pi_{\text{Fock}}
on the Fock space
\Gamma(\mathfrak{h})
with
w(\psi)
represented by the Weyl-Fock operator
W(\psi)
. The Fock vacuum
|\text{vac}\rangle
is cyclic for this representation and induces the state
\langle\text{vac}|W(\phi)\,\text{vac}\rangle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi\|^{2}}.
We note that the Weyl-Fock operator

\displaystyle W(\phi)=e^{a(\phi)-a^{\ast}(\phi)}

(82)

where
a^{\ast}(\cdot)
and
a(\cdot)
are the (complex-linear) creation operator and the (complex-anti-linear) annihilation operator on
\Gamma(\mathfrak{h})
, respectively.

If
\mathfrak{k}
is a real subspace of
\mathfrak{h}
then we may similarly define
CCR(\mathfrak{k})
which we identify with its representation as a subalgebra of the Weyl-Fock representation. The corresponding von Neumann algebra is

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}(\mathfrak{k})=\pi_{\text{Fock}}(CCR(\mathfrak{k}))^{\prime\prime}.

(83)

(Here
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
denotes the commutant of an algebra with the bounded operators on
\mathfrak{h}
.)

A key result is the
Duality Theorem
which states that, for closed standard subspaces
\mathfrak{k}
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}(\mathfrak{k})^{\prime}=\mathfrak{M}(\mathfrak{k}^{\prime})

(84)

and that, moreover, the Fock vacuum is cyclic and separating for both
\mathfrak{M}(\mathfrak{k})
and
\mathfrak{M}(\mathfrak{k})^{\prime}
.
In particular, condition
(i)
implies that
\mathfrak{M}(\mathfrak{k}^{\prime})\subset\mathfrak{M}(\mathfrak{k})^{\prime}
while condition
(ii)
ensures equality.

5.1
Fixing A Commuting Quadrature

We now come to a critical part of the construction. For a given phase
\lambda\in[0,2\pi)
, we may define a quadrature of the commutant field by
Z_{t}^{\prime}=e^{i\lambda}B_{t}^{\prime}+e^{-i\lambda}B_{t}^{\prime\ast}
.

The processes
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
are self-adjoint commuting processes and therefore are essentially classical and may be embedded in the same Kolmogorov model. Moreover, for the choice of the joint vacuum state, they are jointly Gaussian. Let us suppose that we work with a balanced Bogoliubov representation, then we see that
Z^{\prime}
is mean zero with
dZ^{\prime}_{t}\,dZ_{t}^{\prime}=\big(2n+1+2\,\text{Re}\,(e^{i2\lambda}m)\big)\,dt
. However, we also see that

\displaystyle dZ_{t}dZ_{t}^{\prime}=dZ_{t}^{\prime}dZ_{t}=2\bigg(\cos\lambda\,\big(v+\text{Re}u\big)-\sin\lambda\,\text{Im}u\bigg)\,dt,

(118)

where

are the state parameters appearing in (
62

It is clear that the choice
\lambda=\arctan\left(\frac{v+\text{Re}u}{\text{Im}u}\right)
so that
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
are uncorrelated and, moreover, statistically independent.

Definition 5
(Standing Assumptions)

We shall say that the standing assumptions are met whenever i) the underlying Bogoliubov transformation behind the representation is balanced and ii) the phase parameter
\lambda
has been chosen so that
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
are statistically independent.

For instance, for the coefficients in (
64
) we should take
\lambda=\tan^{-1}(\frac{2\tau+\cos\theta}{\sin\theta})
where
\frac{1}{2}<\tau\leq 1

is given by
\tau=\frac{c_{\rho}^{2}}{c_{\rho}^{2}+s_{\rho}^{2}}
. Choices for
\lambda
are plotted in Figure

.

In the thermal case
(x=\sqrt{n+1},y=z=0,w=\sqrt{n})
we find that

and

. In this case, the choice is
\lambda=\pm\pi/2
.

Remark 6

In the following, we are going to consider a measurement of both of the output quadratures corresponding to
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
. We shall average out
Z^{\prime}
which is why we need it to be statistically independent of

. In physicist’s language, we are fixing a representation and then choosing a suitable “unraveling”  of the associated master equation - one corresponding to two independent quadratures. We have fixed
Z
and it is then a question of finding an appropriate

.

It is worth stressing that the only actual measurement physically taking place is that of

, the output quadrature corresponding to
Z
. The unravelling however supposes that we also measure the output corresponding to
Z
(which would be compatible) and average it out through the process of conditional expectation.

An alternative strategy is to fix

as a specific quadrature (i.e., fix
\lambda
) and then select an appropriate representation (i.e, choose the parameters
(x,y,z,w)
so that the quadratures are uncorrelated).

Lemma 8
(Quantum Bayes Formula,
[
4
]
)

Let
(\mathfrak{A},\varphi)
be a quantum probability space consisting of a von Neumann algebra and a normal state. We fix a commutative subalgebra
\mathfrak{C}
and an element

\mathfrak{C}
in
\mathfrak{A}

\omega

\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}
onto
\mathfrak{C}
for reference state

\displaystyle\omega(X|\mathfrak{C})=\frac{\varphi(V^{\ast}XV|\mathfrak{C})}{\varphi(V^{\ast}V|\mathfrak{C})}.

(154)

We sketch the proof as it is quite straightforward. The conditional expectation
\varphi(Y|\mathfrak{C})

for all

\varphi(\varphi(V^{\ast}V|\mathfrak{C})\,\omega(X|\mathfrak{C})C)

\displaystyle\varphi(\varphi(V^{\ast}XV|\mathfrak{C})C)\equiv\varphi(\varphi(V^{\ast}V|\mathfrak{C})\,\omega(X|\mathfrak{C})C),

(155)

and, as

\varphi(V^{\ast}XV|\mathfrak{C})\equiv\varphi(V^{\ast}V|\mathfrak{C})\,\omega(X|\mathfrak{C})

6
Discussion and Conclusion

We have derived the quantum filter for the case where the external input fields are in a squeezed state and we conduct a homodyne detection on a quadrature of the output field. The result has applications to quantum optics problems, but we have also been inspired by the problem of formulating a quantum open systems approach to the Unruh-DeWitt detector where the Minkowski vacuum can be written as an entangled two–mode squeezed state of left- and right-Rindler modes
[
9
]
. In principle, this may be applied to Hawking radiation, where we would measure the late-time output modes (The Hawking radiation field) which are entangled with their infalling quanta behind the horizon.

The problem addressed here must tackle the fact that commutant of the algebra of measured variables is, in stark contrast to the vacuum case, a nontrivial quantum field algebra. However, our calculation utilizes Araki-Woods type representations and Tomita-Takesaki formalism. We solve the problem for a single quantum input channel which is a squeezed state with parameters
(n,m)
. Crucially, the filter we compute must be independent of the exact choice of representation as otherwise the answer is unphysical; but we see that this is indeed the case here.

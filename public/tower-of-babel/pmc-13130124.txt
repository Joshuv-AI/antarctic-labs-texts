Evaluation of Hydrocarbon Generation Potential and Geochemical Characteristics of Aromatic Hydrocarbons in Carboniferous–Permian Coal-Measure Source Rocks of Marine–Continental Transitional Facies in Fuyang Sag, Southern North China Basin
Authors: Zhihang Xin, Xiaotao Zhang, Bin Shen, Jiajia Yang, Shizhen Li, Xuemin Xu, Jing Qin, Cunyang Zhang, Mandan Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The Carboniferous–Permian marine–continental transitional coal-measured source rocks are key targets for unconventional hydrocarbon exploration in China. However, the recent WFD-1 well drilled in the Fuyang Sag, Southern North China Basin, showed no promising hydrocarbons from these strata. To investigate this, geochemical analysesincluding aromatic hydrocarbon GC-MS were performed on coal-measured rocks from the Shihezi, Shanxi, and Taiyuan Formations in WFD-1. The geochemical characteristics and geological significance of aromatic hydrocarbons in the Carboniferous–Permian coal-measure source rocks of the marine–continental transitional facies in the Fuyang Sag were revealed, and their hydrocarbon-generating potential was evaluated. The results show that the “front peak” characteristics of the aromatic hydrocarbon chromatograms, along with detected biomarkers (e.g., retene, cadalene, pyrene, and benzopyrene), 1,2,5-TMN/trimethyl naphthalene ratio, and relative 1-MP content (average 21.46%), indicate mixed organic source input from terrestrial higher plants and aquatic organisms, and the proportion of aquatic-derived organic matter controls effective source rock development. This interpretation aligns with the depositional environment, as indicated by the trifluorene series compounds and triaromatic sterane parameters, which were dominated by coal-measure swamp facies. Both the Shanxi and Taiyuan Formations exhibit stable sedimentary environments characteristic of such swamps. The complex and variable sedimentary environment of the Shihezi Formation influences organic matter preservation and transformation, so the Shanxi Formation source rocks are relatively superior. R o and aromatic parameters indicate mature samples (some in the wet gas–condensate oil stage), equivalent vitrinite reflectance (R c) from MPI-1, K 1, and K 2 is 0.37–0.58% lower than R o, likely due to the transitional setting and thermal events. The parameters of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (Flr/(Flr + Py), BaA/(BaA + Chy), and DMPI) confirmed that there were multiple ancient wildfire events in the Shihezi and Shanxi Formations. Total organic carbon (TOC), pyPAHs, and R o increased simultaneously, indicating that ancient wildfires not only enhanced the burial of organic matter but also promoted the thermal evolution of source rocks through thermal effects. The evaluation results of hydrocarbon generation potential indicate that the organic matter abundance of source rocks in different layers and lithologies of the Carboniferous–Permian in WFD-1 is significantly different. TOC values are high in coal (44.62–79.95%, avg. 60.83%) and carbonaceous mudstone (6.46–38.52%, avg. 21.29%), but low in general mudstone (<2%, avg. 1.09%). The δ13C values (−26.47‰ to −17.15‰) indicate predominantly Type III organic matter. Combined with the total thickness and single-layer thickness of effective source rocks in different layers, the quality of source rocks in the Shanxi Formation is slightly better. The overall thickness of effective source rocks in the Shihezi Formation is comparable to that of the Shanxi Formation. Still, frequent changes in sedimentary facies result in dispersed, discontinuous, high-quality layers. Consequently, the Shanxi Formation contains slightly better source rocks than the Shihezi Formation, with the Taiyuan Formation being the poorest. In summary, the hydrocarbon generation potential of the Carboniferous–Permian coal-bearing source rocks in the marine–continental transitional facies of the Fuyang Sag is generally low, and oil is the primary source. Effective hydrocarbon source rocks are characterized by thin thicknesses and a discontinuous distribution, which make it difficult to develop adequate hydrocarbon expulsion capacity and large-scale oil and gas accumulation. This explains the root cause of the low gas measurement value and the lack of a good oil and gas display in WFD-1 from a geochemical perspective.

Introduction

Unconventional oil and gas resources have become a key driver of the transformation of the global energy structure, and the exploration and development of shale gas have significantly changed the pattern of oil and gas supply. − As a major shale gas resource country, China has made significant progress in the field of marine shale gas, , but the exploration of marine–continental transitional shale gas still faces severe challenges. The marine–continental transitional shale is widely distributed in the Ordos Basin, Bohai Bay Basin, Southern North China Basin, Upper Yangtze Area of the Sichuan Basin, Junggar–Tuha Basin, and Tarim Basin. − These shales frequently interbed with sandstone- and coal-bearing strata, resulting in a complex and variable sedimentary environment. This complexity leads to poor vertical stratigraphic stability and weak lateral continuity, significantly increasing the exploration difficulty. Therefore, accurately assessing the hydrocarbon generation potential of marine–terrestrial transitional shales and determining their resource quantities are urgent requirements for current shale gas exploration and development. As a major hydrocarbon-bearing basin in central China, the South–North China Basin exhibits complex tectonic evolution and diverse sedimentary types. Significant progress has been made in oil and gas exploration in recent years. − Among them, the Carboniferous–Permian coal-bearing strata of typical marine–continental transitional facies are developed in the Fuyang Sag, which is the main source-rock development horizon in the basin. The source rocks in this area have a high abundance of organic matter, mainly type III, and have reached a mature-high-mature stage with significant hydrocarbon generation potential. It has the resource base to form medium- and small-scale oil and gas reservoirs and is an important target area for coal-derived hydrocarbon exploration. − However, for evaluating coal measured source rocks, it is not appropriate to directly apply the evaluation criteria for general lacustrine or marine mudstone because there are essential differences between the two in sedimentary environments, organic matter sources, and geochemical characteristics. The lacustrine source rocks are primarily formed in a relatively closed hydrostatic environment, whereas the coal-bearing strata are developed in dynamic sedimentary systems, such as fluvial, swamp, and shallow lake facies. The organic matter is dominated by higher plants, and the effective carbon content is usually low, resulting in a relatively weak oil generation potential. Therefore, previous studies have established an evaluation system of organic geochemical parameters that mainly reflect the evaluation criteria of different organic matter abundances through a large number of evaluations of the hydrocarbon generation potential of coal measures. − In recent years, WFD-1 deployed by the China Geological Survey in the Southern North China Basin, has not obtained good oil and gas shows, and the reasons are unknown. Some scholars believe that the well was drilled high-quality source rocks. However, it is worth noting that it is difficult to fully characterize the hydrocarbon generation potential of source rocks only by relying on geochemical indicators such as organic matter abundance, type, and maturity. It is also necessary to incorporate the single layer continuous thickness of effective source rocks into the evaluation system for in-depth analysis. The development continuity of vertical effective source rocks can more intuitively reflect their stability in the sedimentary process and the continuity of subsequent hydrocarbon generation. The evaluation of it can make the final conclusion more truly reflect the hydrocarbon generation potential of source rocks and provide more reliable support for unconventional oil and gas exploration.

As an important component commonly found in source rocks, aromatic hydrocarbons are of great significance for oil and gas exploration. Compared with saturated hydrocarbons, aromatic hydrocarbons are more resistant to biodegradation and can provide rich geological information, such as source of organic matter, − sedimentary environment, , thermal maturity, , oil source correlation, oil migration, etc., thus indirectly supporting hydrocarbon generation assessment. The hydrocarbon generation tendency and basis of organic matter can be clarified by the characteristics of parent materials reflected by aromatic hydrocarbon markers. At the same time, the sedimentary environment indicated by aromatic hydrocarbon parameters is helpful in evaluating the preservation and transformation conditions of organic matter. In addition, specific components of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), such as fluoranthene, pyrene, benzo [a]­anthracene, benzofluoranthene, benzo­[e]­pyrene, benzo­[a]­pyrene, indeno­[1,2,3-c,d]­pyrene, and benzo­[ghi]­perylene are also the most representative molecular biomarker evidence in wildfire records. − In geological history, wildfires are the key driving force for the evolution of terrestrial ecosystems. The Carboniferous–Permian period is a “high fire” period. The ancient wildfire activities are extensive and frequent, which has a profound impact on the enrichment and transformation of organic matter. − However, there are few reports on the study of the Carboniferous–Permian ancient wildfire events in the basin.

Based on this, this paper aims to study the Carboniferous–Permian marine–continental transitional facies coal-bearing source rocks in Fuyang Sag, Southern North China Basin, focusing on hydrocarbon generation potential evaluation and aromatic geochemical characteristics analysis. By revealing the geochemical characteristics and geological significance of aromatic hydrocarbons in coal measured source rocks in the study area, combined with the conventional geochemical indicators such as organic matter abundance, maturity, and type of source rocks, the evaluation criteria of single layer thickness of effective source rocks are introduced to accurately evaluate the hydrocarbon generation potential of source rocks in different horizons and different lithologies of coal measured strata in the study area. At the same time, the effect of a wildfire on organic matter enrichment was evaluated. Finally, the main reasons for the poor oil and gas display in the coal-bearing strata of Fuyang Sag in the Southern North China Basin were determined, which provided a key geochemical basis for subsequent exploration and deployment.

Geological Setting

The Fuyang Sag is located in Fuyang City and the surrounding areas of Anhui Province. Structurally, it is subordinate to the Zhoukou Depression of the Southern North China Basin. Geographically, it is located at the southern end of the North China Plain. It is bordered by Zhoukou City, Xincai County, and Xinyang City in the Henan Province. Since the late Palaeozoic era, the region has undergone superposition and transformation due to Indosinian, Yanshanian, and Himalayan tectonic movements, resulting in complex geological evolution. Controlled by two major fault systems (NWW-EW and NNE-NE), the depression exhibits a structural pattern characterized by north–south zoning, east–west blocks, and an interphase of uplift and depression (Figure ). It shows the typical characteristics of Mesozoic and Cenozoic fault basins (depressions) characterized by the juxtaposition and superposition of its basin prototypes. This set of strata is characterized by marine–continental transitional facies coal-bearing deposits. The main horizons include the Taiyuan Formation, Shanxi Formation, Lower Shihezi Formation, and Upper Shihezi Formation. The source rocks are mainly dark mudstone and coal with local carbonate rocks, which constitute the key material basis for unconventional oil and gas reservoirs in the Fuyang area. WFD-1 is located in Yingdong District, Fuyang City, Anhui Province, about 10 km east of the city. It is structurally located in the eastern margin of the Fuyang Depression (Figure ). Its strata are drilled from top to bottom in Quaternary and Neogene (794 m), Paleogene (518 m), Permian, Upper Shihezi Formation (360 m), Lower Shihezi Formation (80.45 m), Shanxi Formation (94.95 m), Carboniferous, Taiyuan Formation (92 m), and Benxi Formation (18.7 m), and the bottom of Ordovician is exposed. Among them, the Carboniferous–Permian system is characterized by frequent interbeds of sand and mudstone, and the interlayer coal line is developed (Figure ). The Shihezi Formation, Shanxi Formation, and Taiyuan Formation are the exploration target strata of the well and are also the key research strata of this paper. Gas logging shows that the total hydrocarbon values for the three layers range from 0.0527% to 0.4183%, and the C1 values range from 0.0264% to 0.3641% (Gao Peng, personal communication, September 6, 2025).

Materials and Methods

Sample Introduction

This study involved collecting 113 hydrocarbon source rock core samples from WFD-1 in the Fuyang Sag of the Southern North China Basin. These samples included 84 from the Shihezi Formation (FD1-001 to FD1-085), 22 from the Shanxi Formation (FD1-086 to FD1-107), and seven from the Taiyuan Formation (FD1-108 to FD1-114). Of these, 113 samples underwent rock pyrolysis analysis, total organic carbon (TOC) determination, vitrinite reflectance (R o) determination, and organic carbon isotope analysis, while 59 samples underwent aromatic component analysis.

Analysis of the Geochemical Characteristics of Aromatic Hydrocarbons

An alumina liquid chromatography column was used to separate aromatic components. The glass column was packed with 25 g of activated alumina powder (100–150 mesh) at 450 °C for 8 h. Removing precipitated asphaltenes minimized the loss of light fractions. The separation was performed following the Chinese industry standard SY/T 5119-201; bulk crude oil samples (200–250 mg) were first eluted with n-hexane (approximately 60 mL), and the aromatics were then eluted using a mixed solvent of dichloromethane and n-hexane (2:1, approximately 40 mL). The aromatic fraction was concentrated to 5 mL for GC–MS analysis. The instrumentation used to analyze the aromatic components comprises an Agilent 6890/5975 desktop mass spectrometer equipped with an HP-5MS quartz elastic capillary column (60 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm). The temperature program was as follows: 50 °C for 1 min, followed by a ramp of 20 °C/min to 100 °C, then a ramp of 3 °C/min to 310 °C, and finally a hold of 21 min at 310 °C. The injector temperature was 290 °C. Helium was used as the carrier gas at a flow rate of 1.00 mL/min. The scanning range was 50–550 amu. Full-scan + multiple ion detection was used for detection; the ionization energy was 70 eV, and the ion source temperature was 230 °C. The relative content of compounds in the aromatic series was calculated based on the areas of the peaks in the residual oil mass spectrometry chromatogram.

Rock Pyrolysis Analysis

The pyrolysis testing was conducted using a Rock-Eval 6 instrument in accordance with industry standard GB/T 18602-2012. Approximately 100 mg of the sample was pulverized to an approximate size of 100–200 μm, and subsequently heated to 600 °C using nitrogen as the carrier gas. The organic geochemical parameters obtained included maximum pyrolysis temperature (T max), free hydrocarbons (S 1), and pyrolyzed hydrocarbons (S 2). Subsequently, the hydrocarbon generation potential index (S 1 + S 2) and hydrogen index (I H) were calculated.

Determination of Total Organic Carbon

The original sample and the simulated sample were crushed to 150 μm and then analyzed by the LECOCS-344 analyzer to determine the TOC content and meet the Chinese national standard GB/T19145-2022. About 100 mg of powder samples was weighed and decarbonized with HCl to remove inorganic carbonates. The residue was washed with DI–H2O and dried overnight at 60 °C. After treatment, the samples were measured with the CS-744 instrument. During the experiment, CO2 was released from the combustion of OM at about 1100 °C, and the TOC content was calculated from the amount of CO2 produced.

Determination of Vitrinite Reflectance R o

Samples were prepared as polished grains using a Zeiss AxioImager II microscope system equipped with an ultraviolet light source and a Diskus-Fossil system for vitreous reflectance (R o) measurements. R o values were determined under oil immersion conditions using an ultrafine-pixel-size (0.1 μm) probe in accordance with the Chinese National Standard SY/T 5124-2012.

Determination of Organic Carbon Isotope

The elemental analyzer model used is the Vario MICRO Cube. Determination of organic carbon isotope values in samples follows the “Method for Carbon and Oxygen Isotope Analysis of Organic Matter and Carbonate Rocks” (SY/T 5238-2019). An appropriate amount of processed sample was weighed into a tin cup, placed it in the autosampler, and initiated the collection programs for both the elemental analyzer and the isotope mass spectrometer. After complete combustion in the elemental analyzer, the sample produced CO2, which was carried by the carrier gas stream into the isotope mass spectrometer for carbon isotope value determination. For this experiment, the combustion tube temperature of the elemental analyzer was set to 950 °C, and the reduction tube temperature was set to 600 °C. During testing, the results for standard materials and replicate samples met the quality requirements of the standard method, with replicate sample testing errors ≤0.5‰.

Results and Discussion

Aromatic Hydrocarbon Geochemical Characteristics

Distribution Characteristics of Aromatic Hydrocarbons in Source Rocks

The analysis of aromatic hydrocarbon compounds in 59 samples of Carboniferous–Permian source rocks (including Shihezi Formation, Shanxi Formation, and Taiyuan Formation) from WFD-1 in the Southern North China Basin shows that the detected aromatic hydrocarbon compounds are rich in species, including naphthalene series, phenanthrene series, anthracene, chrysene series, pyrene series, dibenzothiophene series, biphenyl series, dibenzofuran series, fluorene, fluoranthene, benzofluoranthene, benzopyrene, retene, cadalene, and triaromatic steranes (TAS) series (Figure ). Three characteristic peak groups can be identified by GC–MS total ion current mass chromatogram of aromatic fractions (Figure ): bicyclic naphthalene and alkyl naphthalene peak groups, tricyclic phenanthrene and alkyl phenanthrene peak groups, tetra–pentacyclic PAHs, and aromatic steroid terpenoid peak groups. In the total ion current diagram of aromatic hydrocarbons, there are three distribution patterns: front-peak type, double-peak type, and back-peak type. The front-peak type is dominated by bicyclic or tricyclic compounds, which often appear in source rocks or in crude oil with high maturity. The content of high molecular weight tetracyclic or pentacyclic compounds in bimodal type is equivalent to that of low molecular weight bicyclic or tricyclic compounds, which often exist in low-medium maturity source rocks and crude oil. Most of the peaks are tetracyclic or pentacyclic compounds with high molecular weight. Such distribution characteristics are often related to immature–mature samples. The aromatic hydrocarbon chromatogram of the Carboniferous–Permian source rock in WFD-1 exhibits a front-peak pattern. This is characterized by the first and second peak clusters (the naphthalene and phenanthrene series, respectively) showing significantly higher relative abundances than the third peak cluster (the tetra to pentacyclic compounds). This distribution pattern suggests that the source rock received a significant amount of terrestrial organic matter and had reached a mature evolutionary stage (Figure ). −

A systematic analysis of aromatic hydrocarbon geochemical parameters in WFD-1 reveals that terrestrial plant-derived aromatic biomarkers, including the cadalene, fluoranthene, chrysene, and perylene series, exhibit segmented, regular variations along the vertical profile (Figure ). Overall, strata with relatively low concentrations of these compounds (layers 1–11) often correspond to higher TOC content. However, in certain strata, these compounds also exhibit synchronous increases with TOC. Nevertheless, TOC levels do not directly indicate true potential hydrocarbon generation. While terrestrial organic matter input facilitates organic enrichment, higher plants themselves are hydrogen-poor and oxygen-rich with low hydrocarbon generation potential. Their excessive input dilutes high-quality hydrocarbon-generating source material and promotes the transformation of kerogen types toward type III, significantly reducing the proportion of effective hydrocarbon-generating components within TOC. Therefore, effective hydrocarbon source rocks are more likely to occur in strata with relatively lower than the highest relative abundances of terrestrial aromatic compounds such as cadalene, fluoranthene, chrysene, and perylene series.

Naphthalene Series

As a typical bicyclic aromatic hydrocarbon compound, the naphthalene series shows significant enrichment characteristics in the source rock samples of WFD-1. The results show that the contents of naphthalene and polymethyl-naphthalene-substituted aromatic compounds in the source rocks of the Shihezi, Shanxi, and Taiyuan Formations are relatively high. The detected naphthalene compounds mainly include naphthalene (N), methyl naphthalene (MN), dimethyl naphthalene, trimethyl naphthalene (TMN), tetramethylnaphthalene (TeMN), pentamethyl naphthalene (PMN), and ethyl naphthalene (EN). Among them, the relative content of trimethyl naphthalene (TMN) occupies a dominant position in the series. The naphthalene compounds of the three series of source rocks all show the common distribution law of TMN > MN > TeMN > PMN > EN > N, and the relative content distribution trend of each compound is basically the same in mudstone, carbonaceous mudstone, and coal. According to previous studies, the αα configuration of alkyl naphthalene series compounds can be converted into a more stable ββ configuration by rearrangement reaction under the action of thermal effect due to their high molecular activity. Based on this, the trimethyl naphthalene index (TNR = (1,3,7-TMN + 2,3,6-TMN)/(1,3,5-TMN + 1,3,6-TMN + 1,4,6-TMN)) was established as a maturity evaluation index, and its conversion relationship with vitrinite reflectance was R c1 = 0.6TNR + 0.4. , Research data indicate that the R c1 values for the Shihezi Formation range from 0.69 to 1.57, with an average of 1.01; for the Shanxi Formation hydrocarbon source rock samples, R c1 values range from 0.70 to 1.34, with an average of 0.92; while the Taiyuan Formation source rock samples exhibited R c1 values ranging from 0.78 to 1.01, with an average of 0.90. These values collectively indicate that the source rocks are in a mature evolutionary stage (Table ). This maturity represents a core prerequisite for effective hydrocarbon generation within source rocks, providing crucial evidence that the study area possesses a fundamental capacity for hydrocarbon generation. Chen and Li proposed that the ratio of ββ and αα configurations in trimethylnaphthalene (TMN) and tetramethylnaphthalene (TeMN) can determine the maturity of source rocks in the whole thermal evolution stage of low–high maturity, which provides a new method for maturity evaluation in the entire evolution stage. This method has the characteristics of the antimigration fractionation effect: when both TMNr and TeMNr are greater than 0.5, it indicates that the source rock or crude oil enters the high maturity stage; the two ratios represent the mature stage in the 0.4–0.6 interval; the range of 0.3–0.5 corresponds to the low maturity stage; less than 0.4 reflects the immature characteristics. However, the data of this study show that the TMNr value of the source rock samples in WFD-1 is significantly lower than the TeMNr value, and the two parameters fail to fall into the same maturity interval (Figure ), which limits the applicability of this method in this study area. Previous studies have shown that the ratio changes of TMNr, TeMNr, and PMNr reveal the enrichment of stable isomers (ββ configuration) relative to unstable isomers (αα configuration) in naphthalene series compounds. The formation mechanism is mainly related to 1,2-methyl migration and methyl rearrangement of the naphthalene ring skeleton. To further identify the factors affecting the maturity of source rocks, this study uses the TMNr–TeMNr–PMNr ternary diagram analysis method. When the abundances of the three show a good linear relationship, the sample points should be concentrated in the maturity center area of the ternary diagram. However, the source rock sample points of WFD-1 are significantly deviated from the theoretical center (Figure ), indicating that its configuration evolution is not only controlled by thermal stress but also interfered by other geological factors. The study of Liu et al. in the Ordos Basin shows that TMNr parameters are easily affected by biological sources, sedimentary environments, and other factors. At the same time, the 1,2-methyl migration and rearrangement of the naphthalene ring skeleton may jointly affect the configuration evolution. The analysis shows that the maturity parameters TMNr and TeMNr exhibit significant limitations in the maturity evaluation of source rocks in this study area and more maturity indicators should be combined for a comprehensive assessment in the future.

1,2,5-Trimethylnaphthalene (1,2,5-TMN) and 1,2,5,6-tetramethylnaphthalene (1,2,5,6-TeMN) are derived from the aromatization of pentacyclic triterpenoids and dicyclic diterpenoids, respectively, which can be used as molecular markers to characterize the origin of higher plants and resins. Although these two compounds are commonly detected in source rock samples in the study area, the proportion of 1,2,5,6-TeMN in total tetramethylnaphthalene is generally less than 1%. This phenomenon may be attributed to the insufficient supply of tetramethylnaphthalene precursors, which is controlled by the complexity of the aromatization reaction path. The ratio of 1,2,5-trimethylnaphthalene (1,2,5-TMN) to total tetramethylnaphthalene (TMN, usually refers to the sum of three isomers of 1,2,5- + 1,2,7- + 1,3,6-TMN) in biomarker compounds is an important parameter used to indicate the source of organic matter. It is generally believed that a ratio of 1,2,5-TMN/TMN greater than 0.4 indicates that the organic matter is mainly derived from terrestrial higher plants. When 1,2,5-TMN/TMN < 0.4, the lower ratio indicates that lower aquatic organisms, such as lacustrine or marine algae, are the main parent material. When the ratio is near 0.4, it usually indicates the mixed input of terrestrial higher plants and aquatic organisms. It can be seen from Table that the 1,2,5-TMN/TMN ratios of source rocks in the Shihezi Formation, Shanxi Formation, and Taiyuan Formation range from 0.2 to 0.72, 0.37 to 0.65, and 0.34 to 0.69, respectively, and the ratio of mudstone and carbonaceous mudstone is generally lower than that of coal and rock in the same formation. The average value of mudstone is 0.44, the average value of carbonaceous mudstone is 0.51, and the average value of coal is 0.58. The average value of mudstone in the Shanxi Formation is 0.45, the average value of carbonaceous mudstone is 0.47, and the average value of coal rock is 0.51. The average value of mudstone in the Taiyuan Formation is 0.43, and the average value of coal mudstone is 0.42. The comprehensive analysis shows that 1,2,5-TMN/TMN indicates that the study area is characterized by a mixed source dominated by terrestrial input. Regarding hydrocarbon generation potential, on the one hand, vitrinite reflectance (R c1 ) data indicate that the source rock has entered a mature evolutionary stage, possessing thermal conditions conducive to effective hydrocarbon generation. On the other hand, detailed stratigraphic delineation based on aromatic hydrocarbon parameters (Figure ) reveals that, within sections 1–11, strata with lower 1,2,5-TMN/TMN values typically exhibit higher TOC content. This trend aligns closely with the distribution characteristics of biomarkers such as cadalene, perylene, and so on, which indicates higher plant sources and exhibits good vertical overlap. The coupled relationship among these aromatic parameters further indicates that excessive terrestrial higher plant input dilutes high-quality hydrocarbon-generating source material, reducing the proportion of effective hydrocarbon components within TOC. Conversely, when parameters such as 1,2,5-TMN/TMN are low, this reflects a relatively enhanced contribution from the aquatic source material, correspondingly increasing the proportion of effective hydrocarbon components within TOC. Therefore, it is comprehensively judged that effective hydrocarbon source rocks should primarily develop in strata where terrestrial aromatic parameters such as 1,2,5 and 5-TMN/TMN are relatively low.

Phenanthrene Series

As the most abundant aromatic hydrocarbon components in the source rocks in the study area, the phenanthrene series compounds contain important geochemical information, such as sedimentary environment and maturity. The phenanthrene series compounds detected in the source rock samples of the Shihezi Formation, Shanxi Formation, and Taiyuan Formation in WFD-1 mainly include phenanthrene (P), methylphenanthrene (MP), dimethylphenanthrene (DMP), trimethylphenanthrene (TMP), ethylphenanthrene (EtP), and retene (Re). The relative abundance distribution of each compound generally exhibits a similar pattern. The source rock samples of the Shihezi, Taiyuan, and Shanxi Formations all show the distribution sequence MP > P > TMP > DMP > Re, which is consistent with the overall distribution characteristics. As a typical biomarker of terrestrial higher plants, retene is derived from the gum and resin of conifers. The degradation of abietic acid and pimaric acid mainly forms it. In the early stage of thermal evolution, the abietane alkane structure is aromatized to form retene, but as maturity increases, demethylation decreases its abundance. The low retene abundance in the WFD-1 source rock samples from Anhui Province (Figure ) indicates that the source rock has entered the mature stage of thermal evolution, consistent with the understanding that the entire source rock is in the mature stage of evolution.

In the identification of parent material sources, the distribution pattern of methylphenanthrene isomers is highly indicative. When the thermal evolution degree of source rock samples is the same or not much different, the input of marine source rock or lower aquatic parent material usually shows the dominant distribution of 9-MP, while the input of terrestrial source rock or higher plant is characterized by 1-MP enrichment. The relative content of 9-MP in the source rocks of the Shihezi Formation is between 19 and 36%, and the relative content of 1-MP is between 18 and 29%. The relative content of 9-MP in the source rocks of the Shanxi Formation is between 24% and 34%, and the relative content of 1-MP is between 20% and 26%. The relative content of 9-MP in the source rocks of the Taiyuan Formation is between 28% and 31%, and the relative content of 1-MP is between 17% and 26%. There is no absolute advantage of a single MP isomer. Still, the relative content of 9-MP is higher than that of 1-MP on the whole, indicating that the source rocks of the coal-bearing strata are generally mixed with relatively enriched lower aquatic organic matter. Under the background of marine–continental transitional coal measure strata, the input of aquatic organisms is more representative of superior parent material conditions. It is usually associated with hydrogen-rich organic matter and improves hydrocarbon generation efficiency and oil and gas potential. The comprehensive analysis shows that the marine–continental transitional coal-bearing strata in the study area are dominated by higher plant input and are rich in aquatic biological input. This high-quality parent material marked by 9-MP is rich in lipid components with high hydrogen content, which significantly enhances the enrichment efficiency and hydrocarbon generation potential of organic matter and is a key factor in controlling the formation of high-quality source rocks. The aromatic hydrocarbon parameter profile (Figure ) further revealed that the relative content of 9-MP was significantly positively correlated with TOC. Although the content of methylphenanthrene fluctuates as a whole due to the influence of maturity and sedimentary environment, the content of 9-MP is still higher than that of 1-MP in key layers (such as layers 1 and 8), and the difference between the two is the most significant. It is worth noting that the relative content of terrestrial higher plant-related aromatic hydrocarbon parameters and biomarkers in these key layers is relatively low, while the aquatic contribution ratio and TOC increase synchronously. This phenomenon is highly consistent with the rules reflected in the above aromatic hydrocarbon and biomarker parameters, further indicating that aquatic organic matter is not only an important hydrocarbon-generating parent material but may also promote the preservation and transformation of organic matter by improving the sedimentary microenvironment. The above coupling relationship between the parent material source and the enrichment effect provides a key basis for revealing the formation mechanism of marine–continental transitional source rocks and has important guiding significance for the prediction and evaluation of high-quality source rocks in shale gas exploration.

Regarding maturity assessment, the methylation process of phenanthrene-series compounds is governed by thermodynamic effects, resulting in the relative enrichment of thermally stable isomers with increasing evolutionary degree. Based on this thermal evolution pattern, Radke et al. established the methylphenanthrene index system: MPI-1 = 1.5­(2-MP + 3-MP)/(P + 1-MP + 9-MP), MPI-2 = 3 × (2-MP)/(P + 1-MP + 9-MP). By correlating these indices with vitrinite reflectance, they proposed a maturity conversion model: R c2 = 0.55MPI-1 + 0.44 (0.65 ≤ R o < 1.35). In this study, MPI-1 and MPI-2 exhibited a significant positive correlation (Figure ), confirming the applicability of these indices within the study area. The calculation results show that the R c2 value of the Shihezi Formation is between 0.50 and 0.89, that of the Shanxi Formation is between 0.61 and 0.82, and that of the Taiyuan Formation is between 0.69 and 0.75, which indicates that the source rocks of each stratum have entered the mature evolution stage (Table ), but the vitrinite reflectance R o is lower. In order to further verify the maturity evaluation results, this study also used the methylphenanthrene ratio parameter system proposed by Kvalheim et al., and referred to the discriminant criteria established by Bao et al.: low maturity stage F 1 < 0.4, F 2 < 0.27; in mature stage, F 1 = 0.40–0.55, F 2 = 0.27–0.35; high maturity stage F 1 > 0.55, F 2 > 0.35. As shown in Figure , the source rock samples fall within the mature stage overall, consistent with the MPI index conclusion above, indicating that the organic matter meets the thermodynamic conditions for hydrocarbon generation. Although the parent material conditions of the Taiyuan Formation are relatively poor, the R c2 data show that the effective hydrocarbon generation threshold has been reached. The Shihezi Formation and the Shanxi Formation are superimposed under suitable maturity conditions, with effective source rock sections likely to form. In summary, the source rocks in the study area have substantial hydrocarbon generation capacity under dual control of a favorable parent material background and suitable maturity.

Trifluorene Series Compounds

Dibenzothiophene series compounds have good stability and anti-biodegradation properties and are widely used for evaluating source rock maturity. , Radke and Willsch proposed the methyldibenzothiophene parameter MDR­(4-MDBT/1-MDBT). 4-MDBT has a higher thermal stability than 1-MDBT, and the ratio increases with the degree of thermal evolution. The abundance of 4-MDBT is significantly higher than that of 1-MDBT (MDR > 1, Table ), indicating that the thermal evolution of source rocks has reached a mature state. Luo et al. established a relationship conversion between R o and dimethyldibenzothiophene. The dimethyldibenzothiophene parameters (K 1, K 2) are K 1 = 4,6-DMDBT/1,4-DMDBT; K 2 = 2,4-DMDBT/1,4-DMDBT, the corresponding vitrinite reflectance is R c3 = 0.14K 1 + 0.57; R c4 = 0.35K 2 + 0.46. Chakhmakhchev et al. found that the relative content of 4,6-DMDBT and 2,4-DMDBT in dimethyldibenzothiophene increased with increasing thermal evolution degree, and the relative content of 1,4-DMDBT decreased with increasing thermal evolution degree. Therefore, the two parameters, 4,6-DMDBT/1,4-DMDBT and 2,4-DMDBT/1,4-DMDBT, increased with maturity. The R c3 of the Shihezi group is between 0.56 and 1.65, and the R c4 is between 0.60 and 1.25; Shanxi group R c3 is between 0.53 and 0.72, R c4 is between 0.61 and 0.78; the R c3 of Taiyuan Formation is between 0.52 and 0.77, and R c4 is between 0.61 and 0.67 (Table ), indicating that the source rock is in the mature stage as a whole, but it is lower than the vitrinite reflectance R o.

The compositional characteristics of the “trifluorene” series compounds (OF, SF, and F) are essential indicators for judging the sedimentary environment. There are apparent differences in the composition of the “trifluorene” series of source rocks with different lithology and different horizons in WFD-1, which is mainly attributed to the difference of sedimentary environment (Figure ), although the mudstone of the Shihezi Formation is distributed in all three regions, it is dominated by coal measure swamp facies (Region III), and the relative content of OF is greater than 50%, which has the characteristics of typical coal measure source rocks with the mudstone of Shanxi Formation and Taiyuan Formation; a small part of mudstone is scattered in Region I and Region II, indicating that the sedimentary environment of mudstone in Shihezi Formation is relatively complex, and the marine–continental transitional environment leads to water turbulence. The carbonaceous mudstones of the Shihezi and Shanxi Formations are generally distributed across Region III. A few samples of carbonaceous mudstones in the Shihezi Formation are located in Region II, which are similar to and significantly different from typical coal-measure source rocks and are related to changes in transitional sedimentary environments. The sedimentary environment of the Shihezi Formation coal and rock changes frequently, and the relative content of SF is significantly higher than that of mudstone in the same stratum. The coal and rock of Shanxi Formation and Taiyuan Formation are distributed in Region III, which belongs to typical coal measures swamp sedimentary facies. The relative content of SF in these three groups of coal is significantly higher than those of mudstone and carbonaceous mudstone in the same stratum. The reduction characteristics not only promote the enrichment of organic matter but also coincide with the high TOC value of the samples. The distribution of coal-bearing source rocks in the three regions in the figure is more dispersed; the source rocks of the Shanxi Formation and Taiyuan Formation are all located in the typical coal-bearing swamp sedimentary facies area. The difference in the distribution of sedimentary facies confirms that the study area has the characteristics of a marine–continental transitional sedimentary environment. Zhang et al. have shown that transgression events can significantly change the composition of aromatic hydrocarbons and complicate the geochemical characteristics of coal-bearing source rocks. It is particularly noteworthy that although high SF content usually indicates a high salinity/strong reducing environment, it may reflect the special sedimentary conditions of the marine–continental transitional facies in the coal-bearing source rocks. Previous studies have confirmed that frequent transgressions occurred during the Carboniferous–Permian transition period. The difference between the geochemical characteristics of the source rock samples from WFD-1 well and the typical coal-bearing source rocks is likely to be caused by the influence of transgression events, which further confirms the sedimentary environment of the transitional facies.

The sedimentary environment directly affects the material basis and efficiency of hydrocarbon generation by controlling the conditions for organic matter enrichment and preservation as well as the stability of the parent material. The relative content of SF in the three groups of source rocks is significantly higher than that in the same stratum of mudstone and carbonaceous mudstone. The reduction environment indicated by high SF content can not only effectively inhibit the oxidation and decomposition of organic matter and promote the enrichment of organic matter (consistent with the high TOC value of the sample) but also provide stable thermal evolution conditions for hydrocarbon generation, which is an essential guarantee for hydrocarbon generation potential. In the vertical direction (Figure ), the relative content of SF is at a high level in the above key layers, especially in layers 1 and 11, and the high value interval of SF relative content is highly consistent with the high value interval of TOC in depth. This coupling relationship clearly reveals that the strongly reduced sedimentary environment is the key geological factor that controls the efficient enrichment of organic matter and ultimately the formation of high-quality source rocks. In terms of stratification, the source rocks of the Shanxi and Taiyuan Formations are dominated by typical coal-bearing swamp facies, and the sedimentary environment is stable. There are numerous coal-bearing swamp facies sedimentary source rocks in the Shihezi Formation, which are conducive to the preservation of organic matter in a reducing environment. The material basis and environmental conditions for hydrocarbon generation are consistent with the high-quality facies belts of the Shanxi and Taiyuan Formations. However, the limitation is that the sedimentary environment is complex, and some mudstone and carbonaceous mudstone are scattered in regions I and II. The turbulent environment of sea–land transitional facies may lead to fluctuations in the proportion of low aquatic organisms mixed with parent materials or reduce the preservation efficiency of organic matter due to frequent environmental changes, thereby weakening the hydrocarbon generation potential of this lithology.

Triaromatic Steranes

TAS are often considered to be formed by deep heating of monoaromatic steranes. The distribution characteristics of TAS are affected by many factors, including biogenic input, the sedimentary environment, and maturity. At the same time, TAS have strong antibiodegradation ability. Meng et al. found that the C26TAS-20S/C28TAS-20S values of TAS in saline lacustrine source rocks were 12.56–29.55 and 0.45–0.78, respectively. The relative content and C26TAS-20S/C28TAS-20S values of TAS in freshwater lacustrine source rocks were 4.21 and 0.32, respectively. The relative content of TAS in marine source rocks and coal source rocks is generally less than 3%, and the C26TAS-20S/C28TAS-20S value is less than 0.2. In this study area, the C26TAS-20S/C28TAS-20S ratio of coal rock in the three formations is less than 0.20 (Table ), and the relative content of triarylsterane is less than 3% (Table ), showing typical organic matter characteristics of coal measures, which accurately reflect the swamp sedimentary environment dominated by higher plant sources. However, the C26TAS-20S/C28TAS-20S ratio of mudstone and carbonaceous mudstone is generally greater than 0.2, especially in some samples from the Shihezi Formation, where it reaches 2.28. This shows that the source of organic matter is not a single terrestrial higher plant but also includes a significant contribution from aquatic organisms; the high ratios in some samples also indicate that they may have experienced special water-environment changes or local salinity fluctuations in the marine–continental transitional environment. In general, although the mudstone samples exhibit complex biogenic characteristics, the coal rock in the study area still falls within the range of typical coal-measure source rocks, consistent with the macro background of the coal-measure strata in the study area.

Relative Content of Aromatic Components in Source Rock Samples of WFD-1

Deviation Analysis of Aromatic Hydrocarbon Maturity Parameters

In the profile analysis of aromatic hydrocarbon maturity parameters in WFD-1 (Figure ), systematic comparisons of key indicators, including aromatic hydrocarbon maturity parameters and equivalent vitrinite reflectance (R c1 –R c4 ), reveal that each parameter significantly varies with depth and lithology. The maturity parameters of the Carboniferous–Permian Shihezi Formation source rocks exhibit high-frequency fluctuations, and their R c values show small peaks at 1663.2 to 1668.4 m, 1685.8 to 1691.5 m, and 1703.4 to 1707.8 m. There are small peaks between 1717.2 and 1726.3 m and 1740.7 and 1748.8 m. Between 1826.3 and 1831.3 m of the Shanxi Formation source rocks, the maturity parameters fluctuated slightly, synchronized with R o, showing the characteristics of accelerated thermal evolution. The maturity parameters of source rocks in other strata remain relatively stable. This not only reveals the heterogeneity in the thermal evolution of source rocks but also provides key geochemical evidence for identifying regional thermal events. Abnormal fluctuations in maturity parameters may be related to regional thermal events in the study area (discussed later). The rapid heating caused by thermal events causes R o to respond rapidly and increase, while the molecular reconstruction reaction of aromatic compounds is relatively lagging. This difference in the response rate directly leads to the expansion of the differentiation of R o and R c values. Therefore, the systematic deviation of R o–R c (R c2 –R c4 series parameters are 0.37–0.58% lower than R o) is an important geochemical index for recording the processes of thermal events.

Aromatic hydrocarbon maturity parameters profile of the source rock in WFD-1.

On the other hand, the source rocks are overall in a mature evolutionary stage, but the R c2 –R c4 series parameters are 0.37–0.58% lower than those of the R o system (Table ). This deviation is related to the sedimentary environment of sea–land transitional facies. The coal-bearing strata of marine–continental transitional facies are mainly humic (type III) or transitional (type II) kerogen. Although the aromatic hydrocarbon parameters (such as the alkyl naphthalene and phenanthrene series index) are sensitive to the thermal evolution of organic matter, in the marine–continental transitional facies environment, the mixed input of terrestrial higher plants and lower aquatic organisms constitutes a complex biogenic background, which leads to the strong influence of parent material source and sedimentary environment on the distribution of aromatic hydrocarbon compounds, so that the maturity identification threshold is significantly deviated from the pure marine or pure continental environment (about 0.3% to 0.6%).

Wildfires and Organic Matter Enrichment

PAHs are key biomarkers for reconstructing volcanic activity and paleoenvironmental events in geological history. Their enrichment characteristics can effectively trace the incomplete combustion process of organic matter under high temperature conditions, so they are widely used as alternative indicators for the study of ancient wildfire events. , Burning-source polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (pyPAHs) are often used as key indicators for identifying ancient wildfire events in stratigraphic sediments due to their stable formation during high-temperature combustion and specific molecular ratio characteristics. These compounds include phenanthrene (Phe), fluoranthene (Flr), pyrene (Py), benzo­[a]­anthracene (BaA), chrysene (Chy), benzofluoranthene (BF), benzo­[e]­pyrene (BeP), benzo­[a]­pyrene (BaP), etc. , However, in addition to wildfire burning, PAHs also come from a variety of sources, such as volcanic activity, human activity, and the diagenesis of biological precursors.

Flr/(Flr + Py), BaA/(BaA + Chy), and DMPI (DMPI = 1,7-DMP/(1,7-DMP + 2,6-DMP)) have been widely employed to distinguish pyrolytic and diagenetic sources in ancient sediments. , This study employs the polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon characteristic ratio method to analyze the organic matter sources within the hydrocarbon source rock of WFD-1. As illustrated in Figure , the Flr/(Flr + Py) versus BaA/(BaA + Chy) diagram clearly reveals three distinct organic matter source patterns within the studied section: pyrolytic, diagenetic, and mixed. Yunker et al. proposed that a Flr/(Flr + Py) ratio exceeding 0.5 characterizes thermogenic sources, whereas a ratio below 0.4 indicates rock-derived PAHs. Parts of the Shixiangzi Formation, primarily mudstones, exhibit Flr/(Flr + Py) > 0.5, demonstrating pronounced pyrolytic characteristics. This suggests a possible genetic link between this stratigraphic interval and regional wildfire events triggered by Mesozoic Permian volcanic activity, potentially closely associated with forest wildfires induced by frequent volcanic activity within the Permian North China Block. − The PAHs in coal, rock, and carbonaceous mudstone in the same stratum are mainly caused by diagenesis. The Flr/(Flr + Py) values of some coal rocks and carbonaceous mudstones range from 0.4 to 0.5, indicating mixed genesis and suggesting that the coal and carbonaceous mudstone in this stratum were also affected by combustion events. The source rocks of the Shanxi Formation are in the range of mixed genesis as a whole, which indicates that the PAHs in this section have dual sources of combustion (wildfire) and diagenesis. This phenomenon may be related to the continentalization of the North China Craton in the Early Permian, intermittent wildfire events driven by the seasonal arid climate, and the alternation of the long-term anaerobic diagenetic environment in peat swamps of the delta plain. Although the samples of the Taiyuan Formation are limited in quantity, their characteristic ratios still show attributes of mixed origin. We speculate that, in the Carboniferous marine–continental transitional sedimentary environment, the pyrolysis products of hydrothermal activity input and the diagenetic transformation process mediated by sulfate-reducing bacteria may jointly affect the final composition of organic matter. However, the wildfire event horizons of the Shihezi, Shanxi, and Taiyuan Formations in WFD-1 still require further evidence to confirm.

In the comprehensive geochemical profile of WFD-1, the abundance distribution of pyPAHs and the systematic variation in related wildfire indicators with depth are clearly observed. The results show that there are multistage ancient wildfire events in WFD-1, and the abnormal enrichment rules of pyPAHs in 1663.2–1668.4 m, 1685.8–1691.5 m, 1703.4–1707.8 m, 1717.2–1726.3 m, and 1740.7–1748.8 m layers in Shihezi Formation and 1826.3–1831.3 m key layers in the Shanxi Formation (Figure ). In the key layers of the Shihezi Formation, the concentrations of various pyPAH components showed a significant jump, which strongly indicated that there were frequent ancient wildfire burning events during this period. It is worth noting that the DMPI curve also shows an obvious peak response at this depth, which is highly consistent with the incomplete combustion characteristics of organic matter under high-temperature, arid-climate conditions. The systematic increase of the DMPI index not only confirmed the existence of a high-temperature combustion process but also revealed the high-frequency occurrence of wildfire events under extreme climatic conditions at that time. At the same time, the TOC content of this section also increased synchronously, reaching a peak of 79.95%. Further analysis shows that similar combustion events also exist in the 1826.3–1831.3 m depth section of the Shanxi Formation. In these two horizons, the distribution pattern of pyPAHs showed a high degree of consistency: the abundance of each component increased abruptly, accompanied by a significant rise in DMPI values and a small peak in the simultaneous increase in TOC content. By systematically comparing the variation characteristics of combustion indexes for the two strata, combined with the above Flr/(Flr + Py) and BaA/(BaA + Chy) relationship diagrams, it can be confirmed that the study area experienced multi-stage high-temperature wildfire events during the sedimentary period. This finding provides conclusive geochemical evidence for regional high-temperature wildfire events during this period. The synchronous enrichment of the TOC content and PAHs in wildfire events suggests that there is a significant positive coupling relationship between wildfire activity and organic matter enrichment. The systematic enrichment of TOC content during the wildfire event interval indicates that ancient wildfire activity not only participated in the transformation of organic matter but also served as a vital material supply pathway. The refractory carbonized organic matter produced during combustion significantly improved the preservation efficiency of sedimentary organic matter. At the same time, the terrestrial vegetation succession induced by wildfires may change the type of organic matter input, ultimately forming a positive correlation between the TOC content and wildfire intensity.

Evaluation of Hydrocarbon Generation Potential of Source Rocks

Organic Matter Abundance

Organic matter is the material basis of hydrocarbons, so the richness of organic matter is significant for the evaluation of hydrocarbon generation potential. , Commonly used evaluation criteria include TOC, chloroform bitumen “A”, and hydrocarbon generation potential (S 1 + S 2). This study mainly reflects the abundance of organic matter in source rocks in the study area through organic matter content and S 1 + S 2. From Table , it can be seen that the TOC of the mudstone of the Shihezi Formation in WFD-1 is between 0.22% and 5.82%, with an average value of 0.92%, and the S 1 + S 2 is between 0.03 and 6.87 mg/g, with an average value of 0.61 mg/g; the TOC of mudstone in Shanxi Formation is between 0.21% and 3.04%, with an average of 1.63%, and S 1 + S 2 is between 0.04 and 2.84 mg/g, with an average of 1.15 mg/g. The TOC of mudstone in the Taiyuan Formation is between 0.41% and 1.89%, with an average of 1.41%, and S 1 + S 2 is between 0.20 and 1.16 mg/g, with an average of 0.74 mg/g. The TOC of carbonaceous mudstone in the Shihezi Formation is between 6.46% and 34.00%, with an average of 20.90%, and S 1 + S 2 is between 11.64 and 74.81 mg/g, with an average of 43.95 mg/g. The TOC of carbonaceous mudstone in the Shanxi Formation ranges from 8.34% to 38.52%, with an average of 23.43%, and S 1 + S 2 ranges from 36.95 to 139.65 mg/g, with an average of 88.3 mg/g. The TOC of the coal and rock of the Shihezi Formation is between 44.62% and 79.95%, with an average of 62.50%, and the S 1 + S 2 is between 116.99 and 198.64 mg/g, with an average of 156.60 mg/g; the TOC of the coal rock of the Shanxi Formation is between 47.06% and 66.52%, with an average of 56.83%, and the S 1 + S 2 is between 155.33 and 168.06 mg/g, with an average of 162.70 mg/g; the TOC of Taiyuan Formation coal rock is 62.75%, and S 1 + S 2 is 176.64 mg/g. The above data show that the source rocks of the Shihezi, Shanxi, and Taiyuan Formations all have some hydrocarbon generation potential. Among them, the TOC and S 1 + S 2 values of source rock samples with different lithologies of the Shihezi and Shanxi Formations are higher than those of the Taiyuan Formation, which are preliminarily identified as intervals with a good hydrocarbon generation potential in the study area. The hydrocarbon-generating potential of the Taiyuan Formation is relatively low, consistent with the previous conclusion regarding the geochemical characteristics of aromatic hydrocarbons. However, the evaluation criteria for different lithology source rocks differ, and the hydrocarbon generation potential of each stratum should be further clarified in combination with other organic geochemical parameters. In addition, the TOC content of the source rock samples in the study area is positively correlated to the pyrolysis parameters (S 1 + S 2) (Figure ). This is because, as the TOC content increases, the hydrocarbon production capacity of the organic matter increases, and the hydrocarbon expulsion efficiency increases. As a result, it is easier to overcome the retention force between rock matrix and organic matter, and the S 1 + S 2 value increases.

Organic Matter Type

Organic matter type is one of the core parameters of source rock evaluation, which not only controls the type of hydrocarbon generation products (oil/gas) but also affects the dynamic characteristics of the hydrocarbon generation process. On the one hand, organic matter types contain long-chain lipid components (such as algae-derived alkane chains) through functional group composition and molecular bond strength, which are more likely to generate liquid hydrocarbons during pyrolysis; type III kerogen (humic type) is dominated by polycondensation aromatic ring structure (such as lignin-derived PAHs), and has high thermal stability and mainly tends to generate gaseous hydrocarbons. On the other hand, differences in organic matter types can significantly affect pyrolysis kinetic parameters (such as activation energy distribution), resulting in differences in hydrocarbon generation rates and temperature windows for different kerogens. The activation energy distribution of type I kerogen is concentrated, with the peak of oil generation often occurring in the early stage of maturity. In contrast, the activation energy range of type III kerogen is wide, and the gas generation process can continue to the high-maturity stage. In general, type I kerogen has the most substantial hydrocarbon generation potential and often produces liquid hydrocarbons; the hydrocarbon generation potential of type II1 and type II1–II2 kerogen is the second; type III kerogen (humic type) mainly generates gaseous hydrocarbons. In general, the T max–I H diagram can be used as an effective basis for the classification of organic matter types of source rocks, but its application in highly mature source rocks is severely limited. The main reason is that the increase in the thermal evolution of source rocks makes the T max and I H values distorted, resulting in systematic offset of sample points on the diagram, which cannot accurately reflect the type of organic matter in the sample (Figure ). Organic carbon isotope is a relatively stable index in judging the type of organic matter, which provides a basis for the evaluation of kerogen type of source rocks from high maturity to over maturity. According to the organic carbon isotope value, kerogen can be divided into three types: type III kerogen with δ13C greater than −25‰, type II kerogen with δ13C between −26.5‰ and −25‰, type II kerogen with δ13C between −28‰ and −26.5‰, and type I kerogen with δ13C less than −28‰. The variation range of kerogen carbon isotope δ13C in the general mudstone of the Shihezi Formation in WFD-1 is between −26.08‰ and −17.15‰. Carbonaceous mudstone is between −26.47 and −22.60‰. Coal rock is between −25.24 and −23.23‰. The variation range of δ13C of the general mudstone in the Shanxi Formation is between −24.60‰ and −22.28‰. Carbonaceous mudstone is between −24.30 and −23.90‰. The coal rock is between −24.89 and −24.21‰. The δ13C values of mudstones in the Taiyuan Formation range from −24.97‰ to −24.23‰, and the δ13C values of coals are −24.74‰ (Table ).

The carbon isotope characteristics of the organic matter in the source rocks of the Shihezi Formation, Shanxi Formation, and Taiyuan Formation in WFD-1 show that the general mudstone, carbonaceous mudstone, and coal rock kerogen types of the Shihezi Formation, Shanxi Formation, and Taiyuan Formation are mainly type III, and only a few general mudstone and carbonaceous mudstone samples of the Shihezi Formation are type II2 (Figure ). The characteristics of δ13C and corresponding kerogen types of source rocks in the study area are consistent with the previous studies on the marine–continental transitional coal measures in the Southern North China Basin. Although the organic matter content of carbonaceous mudstone is higher than that of general mudstone, the source of organic matter is similar to that of general mudstone. Hence, the characteristics of δ13C are similar. Due to the marine–continental sedimentary environment in the study area, the organic matter is dominated by higher plant debris, which is significantly influenced by terrestrial input, resulting in relatively high δ13C values. The coal rock is formed by the peatification of higher plants. The organic matter is mainly composed of lignin and cellulose. The carbon isotope composition is affected by the plant type and sedimentary environment. Compared with mudstone, the reducing environment during coal rock formation is stronger, which may lead to slightly lighter δ13C values (Table ) (see Figure ).

Maturity of Organic Matter

The thermal evolution degree of organic matter not only restricts its residual hydrocarbon generation capacity but also dominates the chemical composition of the generated hydrocarbons. In the maturity evaluation, vitrinite reflectance and Rock–Eval pyrolysis T max parameters are generally regarded as the core evaluation indicators. The maturity characteristics of organic matter in each stratum of the study area have specific differences (Table ). The stone box group is in the mature stage overall, and R o is between 0.92% and 1.23%. The R o of the coal rock reaches 1.23%, indicating that its thermal evolution has entered the stage of wet gas–condensate oil generation. The mudstone (R o = 0.92–1.20%) and carbonaceous mudstone (R o = 1.04–1.14%) of the Shihezi Formation are in the late stage of the oil window and retain specific liquid hydrocarbon generation capacity. The R o of mudstone in the Shanxi Formation is between 1.17% and 1.20%, which is higher than that of carbonaceous mudstone (R o = 0.90%) and coal rock (R o is between 0.87% and 0.91%) in the same stratum. The inversion of maturity may be related to the type of organic matter or to a local thermal anomaly. The R o of mudstone in the Taiyuan Formation reaches 1.06%, and the R o of coal rock reaches 0.96%, both of which reach the stage of the main oil window.

Hydrocarbon Generating Potential Evaluation

The source rock of WFD-1 belongs to the source rock of coal measure strata. Chen et al. think that the effective carbon content of coal-measure source rocks is low because of their specific sedimentary environments and parent material compositions, and that hydrocarbon generation evaluations should be based on hydrocarbon generation potential. Predecessors have conducted extensive research on the evaluation of hydrocarbon generation potential in coal measure strata, − and have established an evaluation system based on organic geochemical parameters, such as organic matter type, organic matter abundance, and organic matter maturity. To further determine the hydrocarbon generation potential of mudstone, carbonaceous mudstone, and coal in the Shihezi, Shanxi, and Taiyuan Formations in WFD-1, the TOC content, S 1 + S 2, and I H were selected as evaluation indices. According to Chen Jianping’s evaluation criteria for hydrocarbon generation potential of coal-bearing strata (Table ), the hydrocarbon generation potential of source rocks in different strata (Shihezi Formation, Shanxi Formation, and Taiyuan Formation) of WFD-1 was evaluated (Figure ). The evaluation results show that in the three strata, the overall hydrocarbon generation capacity of the general mudstone is generally poor, and only two samples reach the level of good source rock. The hydrocarbon generation potential of carbonaceous mudstone is generally better than that of ordinary mudstone, and the samples are mainly of a good source rock level. The hydrocarbon generation potential of coal rock is generally poor, with mainly poor source rocks, and a few samples reach the level of medium source rocks.

Although the thickness of the carbonaceous mudstone of the Shihezi Formation is greater than that of the carbonaceous mudstone of the Shanxi Formation, the maximum single-layer thickness of the part as a good source rock is only 2.9 m, which is not much different from the carbonaceous mudstone of the Shanxi Formation. In addition, compared with the general mudstone of the Shihezi Formation, the general mudstone of the Shanxi Formation not only accounts for a higher proportion of medium–good source rocks but also has a maximum single-layer thickness of 10.20 m, which is significantly larger than the maximum single-layer thickness of the source rocks of the Shihezi Formation (5.7 m). This shows that the Shanxi Formation develops more continuous and thicker high organic matter enrichment layers, which are more conducive to the generation and effective discharge of hydrocarbons, and the overall quality of its source rock is better. The TOC content of mudstone in the Taiyuan Formation is generally low, and only medium source rocks are developed. There is a lack of good source rocks, and the hydrocarbon generation potential is limited. Comprehensive analysis, the hydrocarbon generation potential from good to bad order: Shanxi Formation > Shihezi Formation > Taiyuan Formation. The vertical heterogeneity of the Carboniferous–Permian source rocks is mainly controlled by the evolution of the sedimentary environment. This conclusion is consistent with the previous conclusion, based on the geochemical characteristics of trifluorene series compounds. Previous studies indicated that high-frequency sea-level fluctuations led to multiple secondary transgressions, which hindered the continuous development and stable accumulation of swamp sedimentary systems. During the regression, the development chief pointed out that the Late Palaeozoic coal-bearing strata constitute a complete progradation–retrogradation sedimentary cycle, in which the source rocks of the Shanxi Formation formed during the cycle’s conversion stage. Benefiting from a long-term, stable sedimentary basement, it offers superior conditions for the formation of source rocks. The source rocks of the Taiyuan Formation are mainly formed in a tidal flat environment, accompanied by sudden transgression events, , and the sedimentary environment changes dramatically. The conditions of the source rocks continue to deteriorate, resulting in poor-quality source rocks in the Taiyuan Formation. The transgressive scale of the Shihezi Formation is large and occurs in a relatively stable regional background. The water turbulence is mainly due to the hydrodynamic changes such as rivers and waves within the sedimentary system rather than the drastic replacement of the environmental background. This large-scale transgression continues to maintain a reduced water environment conducive to the preservation of organic matter in the region, so that in the frequent replacement of sedimentary facies such as the delta distributary channel, crevasse splay, bay swamp, and front sheet sand, it still has the conditions to form effective source rocks. However, the rapid transformation of sedimentary facies leads to frequent interbedding of sand and mudstone, resulting in poor lateral continuity of high-quality source rocks and generally thin single-layer thickness. Therefore, the mudstone, carbonaceous mudstone, and coal in the lower part of the Shanxi Formation and the mudstone and carbonaceous mudstone in the lower part of the Shihezi Formation constitute the most crucial source rock development horizon in the study area. In the vertical section of aromatic hydrocarbon parameters (Figure ), the consistent law of parent material source and sedimentary environment parameters with TOC value is not an accidental geochemical phenomenon. In the vertical section, terrigenous markers such as cadalene are negatively correlated with TOC, while 9-MP and SF contents are positively correlated with TOC. The sections with the same rules of aromatic hydrocarbon parameters and biomarkers are highly consistent with the effective source rock sections of WFD-1 in depth (Figure ), which indicates the efficient coupling of high-quality biogenic sources and reducing environment with organic matter enrichment, which not only confirms the material basis of oil and gas. Although the vitrinite reflectance (R o) of the source rocks of the Shanxi and Shihezi Formations in WFD-1 is greater than 0.9%, the samples are in the mature stage overall, and some have entered the stage of wet gas–condensate oil generation, but the resources of WFD-1 are still small. The main reason is that the source rocks of this well lack adequate hydrocarbon expulsion capacity and cannot form large-scale oil and gas reservoirs. Previous studies have shown that when the TOC of source rocks exceeds 1.0%, the single-layer thickness exceeds 10 m, and it is relatively difficult to form an adequate reservoir space. Second, although the TOC values of some sections are up to the standard, the thickness of source rocks with high organic matter abundance is relatively low. In the general mudstone of the Shihezi Formation, non-source rocks and poor source rocks account for 92.91%. In the general mudstone of the Shanxi Formation, with the highest hydrocarbon generation potential, medium–good source rocks account for only 20.52%. In addition, although the proportion of carbonaceous mudstone in the Shihezi and Shanxi Formations is high, the cumulative thickness is too small, and the contribution to the amount of resources is limited. At the same time, the total hydrocarbon content of the source rocks in the well is distributed between 0.0527% and 0.4183%, and the methane (C1) content is between 0.0264% and 0.3641%, which is at a low level overall. These indicators are not only far below the gas-bearing threshold for typical shale gas exploitation but also indicate that the total amount of hydrocarbons generated by organic matter conversion is limited. The residual hydrocarbon content is low, which may lead to an imbalance between adsorbed and free gases, further restricting the enrichment and recoverability of shale gas. In summary, although WFD-1 is in a favorable thermal evolution stage, it is difficult to form an economic shale gas reservoir due to the lack of high-quality source rocks with abundant, continuous thick layers.

Conclusion

(1)The aromatic hydrocarbons of source rocks in the study area are dominated by naphthalene and phenanthrene series. Terrestrial biomarkers, such as cadalene and retene, are detected. The 1,2,5-TMN/TMN, 1-MP, and 9-MP indicates that organic matter is derived from a mixed source of terrestrial higher plants and lower aquatic organisms, consistent with the sedimentary background of marine–continental transitional facies. When the contribution ratio of aquatic organisms is high, the effective hydrocarbon-generating components increase. Excessive input of terrestrial higher plants will reduce the proportion and restrict the hydrocarbon-generating potential. Effective source rocks are commonly found in layers with low terrestrial input parameters and high contributions of aquatic organisms.(2)The trifluorene series and triaromatic sterane parameters indicate that the source rocks of the Shanxi Formation and Taiyuan Formation are concentrated in the coal-bearing swamp facies area, and the sedimentary environment is stable, which is conducive to the preservation of organic matter. This conclusion is highly consistent with the records of stratigraphic, sedimentary cycles, and transgression events.

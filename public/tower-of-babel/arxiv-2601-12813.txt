Abstract
FIRRTL is an intermediate representation language for Register Transfer Level (RTL) hardware designs. In FIRRTL programs, the bit widths of many components are not specified explicitly and must be inferred during compilation. In mainstream FIRRTL compilers, such as the official compiler firtool , width inference is conducted by a compilation pass referred to as InferWidths, which may fail even for simple FIRRTL programs. In this paper, we thoroughly investigate the width inference problem for FIRRTL programs. We show that, if the constraints obtained from a FIRRTL program are satisfiable, there exists a unique least solution. Based on this result, we propose a complete procedure for solving the width inference problem. We implement it in the interactive theorem prover Rocq and prove its functional correctness. From the Rocq implementation, we extract an OCaml implementation, which is the first formally verified implementation of the InferWidths pass. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our approach can solve more instances than the official InferWidths pass in firtool , normally with high efficiency.

Width inference in FIRRTL.

When working with FIRRTL, users are encouraged to manually specify (bit) widths of ports and registers. Unspecified widths are inferred during compilation. These inferred widths are ideally as small as possible depending on the widths of all incoming connections.

A complete procedure for width inference

Observing that
x_{i}\geq\min(t_{1},\ldots,t_{k})
is equivalent to
\bigvee_{j\in[k]}x_{i}\geq t_{j}
, a
\Phi_{W}
-constraint can be rewritten into
\Phi_{\rm nf}:=\bigvee_{j_{1}\in[\ell]}\bigwedge_{j_{2}\in[m_{j_{1}}]}z_{j_{1},j_{2}}\geq t_{j_{1},j_{2}}
such that
\Phi_{\rm nf}

is satisfiable iff
\bigwedge_{j_{2}\in[m_{j_{1}}]}z_{j_{1},j_{2}}\geq t_{j_{1},j_{2}}
is satisfiable for some
j_{1}\in[\ell]
, where
z_{j_{1},j_{2}}\in X
and
t_{j_{1},j_{2}}
is a width term.
Moreover, the least solution of
\Phi_{\rm nf}
is the least one among the least solutions of the disjuncts. Hence, it is sufficient to consider a conjunction of inequalities of the form
x_{i}\geq a_{0}+\sum_{j\in[n]}a_{j}x_{j}
.

The procedure utilizes a concept of dependency graphs.

Definition 1
(Dependency graph)

Let
\varphi
be a
\Phi_{W}
-constraint over
X
.
Assume that the number of inequalities for all variables
x_{i}
together is at most
L\in\mathbb{N}
.
This allows to assign a unique label
l\in[L]
to each inequality for
x_{i}
.

The dependency graph
G_{\varphi}
of
\varphi
is defined as a directed graph
(V_{\varphi},E_{\varphi})
, where
V_{\varphi}=X
and
E_{\varphi}\subseteq V_{\varphi}\times[L]\times\mathbb{N}\times V_{\varphi}
comprises the edges
(x_{i},l,a_{j},x_{j})
such that
\varphi
contains an inequality
l:x_{i}\geq a_{0}+\sum_{j\in[n]}a_{j}x_{j}
with
a_{j}>0
.

Example 1

Given the labeled
\Phi_{W}
-constraints
\varphi_{1}
,
\varphi_{2}
and
\varphi_{3}
:

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}\equiv{}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[t]{l@{{}\quad\wedge\quad{}}l@{{}\quad\wedge\quad{}}l}l_{1}:x_{1}\geq 5&l_{2}:x_{2}\geq 2&l_{3}:x_{3}\geq 2x_{1}-3\quad\wedge\\
l_{4}:x_{3}\geq x_{2}+2&l_{5}:x_{4}\geq 3x_{2}&l_{6}:x_{4}\geq x_{3}+4,\end{array}

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}\equiv{}

3.1
The overall procedure

Let
\mathcal{S}_{G_{\varphi}}
be the SCC graph of
G_{\varphi}
. We first compute a topological sort
C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}
of
\mathcal{S}_{G_{\varphi}}
, namely, any edge from the vertices in
C_{i}
to the vertices in
C_{j}
entails
i\leq j
.
We denote by
V(C)
the set of variables in
C
.
For example, in Fig.
1
(c), we have
V(C_{1})=\{x_{7}\}
,
V(C_{2})=\{x_{3},x_{5},x_{6}\}
,
V(C_{3})=\{x_{4}\}
and
V(C_{4})=\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
.
We then iterate the following procedure from
j=k
to
j=1
:

Suppose that the least width
u_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
of each
x_{i}\in V(C_{j+1})\cup\cdots\cup V(C_{k})
has been computed. We first replace each occurrence of
x_{i}\in V(C_{j+1})\cup\cdots\cup V(C_{k})
in the inequalities for
x_{i^{\prime}}\in V(C_{j})
with the least width
u_{i}
,
resulting in a conjunction
\psi_{C_{j}}
of inequalities for
x_{i^{\prime}}\in V(C_{j})
.

•

If
C_{j}
is trivial, i.e., it comprises
x_{i^{\prime}}
only, then we set
u_{i^{\prime}}
to be the maximum of
0
and the constants
a
in the inequalities
x_{i^{\prime}}\geq a
of
\psi_{C_{j}}
.

•

Otherwise
C_{j}
is strongly connected, we compute the least solution
(u_{i^{\prime}})_{x_{i^{\prime}}\in C_{j}}
for
\psi_{C_{j}}
, or
\psi_{C_{j}}
is unsatisfiable and the procedure terminates.

We shall now focus on the computation of the least solution for
strongly connected dependency graphs,
which is the core of our procedure.
We first identify a class of strongly connected dependency graphs via expansiveness.

3.2
Characterization of strongly connected dependency graphs

The notion of
expansiveness
is to provide a structural characterization for a class of dependency graphs.
A dependency graph
G_{\varphi}
is said to be
expansive
if it contains an edge
(x,l,a,x^{\prime})
with
a>1
or two distinct edges out of some vertex with the same label; otherwise it is

nonexpansive
. Expansive dependency graphs exhibit the following property.

Proposition 1

Assume
G_{\varphi}
is strongly connected and
\varphi
is satisfiable.
G_{\varphi}
is expansive iff
there is a constant
B\in\mathbb{N}
such that the values of the variables in every solution of
\varphi
are upper-bounded by
B
.

Proof

Note that any edge
(z_{r-1},l_{r},a_{r},z_{r})
in
G_{\varphi}
stems from an inequality
l_{r}:z_{r-1}\geq a_{r,0}+\sum_{z\in X}a_{r,z}z
, where
a_{r,z_{r}}=a_{r}>0
. We can weaken this inequality to
z_{r-1}\geq a_{r,0}+a_{r}z_{r}
, and we denote this weakened inequality by
\mathsf{Ineq}(z_{r-1},l_{r},a_{r},z_{r})
.
Similar weakened inequalities can be defined over paths inductively as follows.
Given the empty path
\pi_{z_{0}\to z_{0}}
from
z_{0}
to
z_{0}
, let

\mathsf{Ineq}(\pi_{z_{0}\to z_{0}})\equiv z_{0}\geq z_{0}.

For a non-empty path
\pi_{z_{0}\to z_{m}}=\pi^{\prime}_{z_{0}\to z_{m-1}}\cdot(z_{m-1},l_{m},a_{m},z_{m})
from
z_{0}
via
z_{m-1}
to
z_{m}
and the inequalities

\displaystyle\mathsf{Ineq}(\pi^{\prime}_{z_{0}\to z_{m-1}})\equiv\phantom{z_{m-1}}\makebox[0.0pt][r]{$z_{0}$}

\displaystyle\geq a_{m-1}+b_{m-1}z_{m-1}

\displaystyle\mathsf{Ineq}(z_{m-1},l_{m},a_{m},z_{m})\equiv z_{m-1}

\displaystyle\geq a_{m,0}+a_{m}z_{m},

let

\displaystyle\mathsf{Ineq}(\pi_{z_{0}\to z_{m}})\equiv\phantom{z_{m-1}}\makebox[0.0pt][r]{$z_{0}$}

\displaystyle\geq a_{m-1}+b_{m-1}(a_{m,0}+a_{m}z_{m})

\displaystyle=a_{m-1}+b_{m-1}a_{m,0}+b_{m-1}a_{m}z_{m}.

Any solution of
\varphi
satisfies all such
\mathsf{Ineq}(\pi)
, for all paths
\pi
in
G_{\varphi}
.

– If

G_{\varphi}
is expansive, then the solutions are upper-bounded:

Assume that
G_{\varphi}
is expansive.
We prove that there exists an upper bound for an arbitrary variable
x_{k}
.
There are two cases:

G_{\varphi}

contains an edge
(x_{i},l,a,x_{j})
with
a>1
:

Because
G_{\varphi}
is strongly connected, there exist paths
\pi_{ki}
from
x_{k}
to
x_{i}

and
\pi_{jk}
from
x_{j}
to
x_{k}
.
The inequality for the composed path
\mathsf{Ineq}(\pi_{ki}\cdot(x_{i},l,a,x_{j})\cdot\pi_{jk})
is of the form
x_{k}\geq a_{k,0}+a_{kk}x_{k}
.
Note that we must have that
a_{kk}>1
because it is a multiple of
a>1
.
This means that any solution of
\varphi
satisfies
x_{k}\leq-a_{k,0}/(a_{kk}-1)
.

G_{\varphi}

contains two distinct edges
(x_{i},l,1,x_{j_{1}})
and
(x_{i},l,1,x_{j_{2}})
:

We can assume that all edges
(x^{\prime},l^{\prime},a^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})
in
G_{\varphi}
have
a^{\prime}=1
;
otherwise the result immediately follows from the first case.

From these two edges, we can derive a similar weakening of inequality
l
,
namely
x_{i}\geq a_{i,0}+x_{j_{1}}+x_{j_{2}}
.
There exist the following paths with their inequalities:

\pi_{ki}

from
x_{k}
to
x_{i}

\displaystyle\mathsf{Ineq}(\pi_{ki})\equiv\phantom{x_{j_{1}}}\makebox[0.0pt][r]{$x_{k}$}

\displaystyle\geq a_{ki}+x_{i}

\displaystyle\pi_{j_{1}k}\text{ from $x_{j_{1}}$ to $x_{k}$}

\displaystyle\mathsf{Ineq}(\pi_{j_{1}k})\equiv x_{j_{1}}

\displaystyle\geq a_{j_{1}k}+x_{k}

\displaystyle\pi_{j_{2}k}\text{ from $x_{j_{2}}$ to $x_{k}$}

\displaystyle\mathsf{Ineq}(\pi_{j_{2}k})\equiv x_{j_{2}}

\displaystyle\geq a_{j_{2}k}+x_{k}.

Combining all these, we get that

x_{k}\geq a_{ki}+x_{i}\geq a_{ki}+a_{i,0}+x_{j_{1}}+x_{j_{2}}\geq\linebreak a_{ki}+a_{i,0}+a_{j_{1}k}+x_{k}+a_{j_{2}k}+x_{k}.

This means that any solution of
\varphi
satisfies
x_{k}\leq-a_{ki}-a_{i,0}-a_{j_{1}k}-a_{j_{2}k}
.

– If

G_{\varphi}
is nonexpansive, then the solutions are not upper-bounded:

Assume that

G_{\varphi}
does not contain two distinct edges out of some vertex
x_{i}
with the same label,
nor contains any edge
(x_{i},l,a,x_{j})
such that
a>1
.
Therefore all inequalities in
\varphi
are of the form
x_{i}\geq a^{\prime}_{ij}+x_{j}
or
x_{i}\geq a^{\prime\prime}_{i}
for some constants
a^{\prime}_{ij},a^{\prime\prime}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Since
\varphi
is satisfiable, let

(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n})
be a solution of
\varphi
.
Obviously, for any
C\in\mathbb{N}
,
(u_{1}+C,u_{2}+C,\ldots,u_{n}+C)
is also a solution of
\varphi
.
Thus, the values of the variables in the solutions of
\varphi
are
not
upper-bounded.
∎

As a result, if
G_{\varphi}
is expansive, there are finitely many solutions for
\varphi
and the least solution can be computed by conducting an exhaustive search.
Our procedure therefore diverges into separate cases depending on whether
G_{\varphi}
is expansive (Section
3.3
) or nonexpansive (Section
3.4
).

3.3
Computing the least solution for expansive
G_{\varphi}

In this case, we first present an algorithm to compute the upper bounds (which follows essentially the proof of Proposition
1
), then we show how to utilize them to search for the least solution based on a branch-and-bound algorithm.

Computing upper bounds for the variables.

The algorithm distinguishes the two alternative cases for the notion of expansiveness.

•

If there is an edge
(x_{i},l,a,x_{j})
with
a>1
, then we compute the upper bounds by the following procedure.

1.

Find a path from
x_{j}
to
x_{i}
, say,
\pi_{ji}=(y_{0},l_{1},a_{1},y_{1})\cdots(y_{r-1},l_{r},a_{r},y_{r})
(where
y_{0}=x_{j}
and
y_{r}=x_{i}
).

2.

Calculate
\mathsf{Ineq}((x_{i},l,a,x_{j})\cdot\pi_{ji})
as in the proof of Proposition
1
, say,
x_{i}\geq a_{i,0}+a_{ii}x_{i}
.

3.

From
a>1
, we know that
a_{ii}>1
.
As a result,
x_{i}\leq\lfloor-a_{i,0}/(a_{ii}-1)\rfloor
. We have obtained an upper bound for
x_{i}
.

4.

We start from
x_{i}
and apply a breath-first search (BFS) to compute upper bounds for the other variables. That is, suppose that we know
x_{i^{\prime}}\leq c
and
(x_{i^{\prime}},l^{\prime},a^{\prime},x_{j^{\prime}})
is an edge in
G_{\varphi}
, let
l^{\prime}:x_{i^{\prime}}\geq b_{0}+\sum_{k\in[n]}b_{k}x_{k}
(where
b_{j^{\prime}}=a^{\prime}
), then we have
a^{\prime}x_{j^{\prime}}\leq x_{i^{\prime}}-b_{0}\leq c-b_{0}
, thus
x_{j^{\prime}}\leq\lfloor(c-b_{0})/a^{\prime}\rfloor
. (Because
G_{\varphi}
is strongly connected, applying the BFS produces upper bounds for all the variables in
G_{\varphi}
.)

•

If there are two distinct edges out of some vertex
x_{i}
of the same label, say,
e_{1}=(x_{i},l,a_{1},x_{j_{1}})
and
e_{2}=(x_{i},l,a_{2},x_{j_{2}})
, then we compute the upper bounds by the following procedure. Let us assume that the weights of all the edges in
G_{\varphi}
are equal to
1
. Because otherwise, we already know how to compute the upper bounds by the aforementioned procedure.

1.

Find two simple paths from
x_{j_{1}}
and
x_{j_{2}}
to
x_{i}
, respectively, say
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
. Then as in the proof of Proposition
1
, we can utilize
\mathsf{Ineq}(e_{1}\cdot\pi_{1})
and
\mathsf{Ineq}(e_{2}\cdot\pi_{2})
to compute an upper bound for
x_{i}
, say
x_{i}\leq B_{i}
.

2.

Similarly to the situation that there is an edge of weight greater than
1
in
G_{\varphi}
, we start from
x_{i}
and apply a BFS to compute upper bounds for all the other variables in
G_{\varphi}
.

Example 2

Consider
\varphi_{2}
in Example
1
. The dependency graph
G_{\varphi_{2}}
as illustrated in Fig.
1
(b) is strongly connected and contains an edge
(x_{1},l_{1},2,x_{2})
whose weight is greater than
1
. Then we compute the upper bounds as follows.

Because
x_{1}\geq 2x_{2}-4
, we find a simple path from
x_{2}
to
x_{1}
, say
(x_{2},l_{2},1,x_{3})\cdot(x_{3},l_{3},1,x_{1})
, and utilize the path to apply the replacements. Then we have

x_{1}\geq 2x_{2}-4\geq 2(x_{3}-2)-4=2x_{3}-8\geq 2(x_{1}+1)-8=2x_{1}-6.

As a result,
x_{1}\leq 6
.

Finally, we start from
x_{1}
and apply a BFS to compute the upper bounds for
x_{2}
and
x_{3}
. At first, from
x_{1}\geq 2x_{2}-4
, we have
2x_{2}\leq x_{1}+4\leq 6+4=10
, thus
x_{2}\leq 5
. Moreover, from
x_{2}\geq x_{3}-2
, we have
x_{3}\leq x_{2}+2\leq 7
.

To summarize, we obtain the upper bounds
(6,5,7)
for
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
.

Branch-and-bound.

Let
(ub_{1},\ldots,ub_{n})
denote the upper bounds of the variables in
X
. For each
i\in[n]
, let
lb_{i}
denote the maximum of
0
and the constants
a_{0}
in the inequalities
x_{i}\geq a_{0}+\sum_{j\in[n]}a_{j}x_{j}
of
\varphi
. If
lb_{i}>ub_{i}
for some
i\in[n]
, then report that
\varphi
is unsatisfiable and terminate the algorithm. Otherwise, the algorithm iterates the following branch-and-bound procedure, where the upper bounds
(ub_{1},\ldots,ub_{n})
and lower bounds
(lb_{1},\ldots,lb_{n})
are taken as parameters. (See Figure
2
for an illustration of the procedure.)

Figure 2
:
An illustration of the branch-and-bound (BaB) procedure

In the case
(ub_{1},\ldots,ub_{n})=(lb_{1},\ldots,lb_{n})
, we check whether

(lb_{1},\ldots,lb_{n})
is a solution of

\varphi
(i.e.,
\varphi[lb_{1}/x_{1},\ldots,lb_{n}/x_{n}]
is
{\sf true}
or not). If it is
{\sf true}
, then return
(lb_{1},\ldots,lb_{n})
as the least solution; otherwise, report that
\varphi
is unsatisfiable.

Otherwise
(ub_{1},\ldots,ub_{n})\neq(lb_{1},\ldots,lb_{n})
, then we compute
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})=(\lfloor(ub_{1}+lb_{1})/2\rfloor,\ldots,\lfloor(ub_{n}+lb_{n})/2\rfloor)
and
check whether
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
is a solution of
\varphi
. If it is a solution, then we set
(ub^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,ub^{\prime}_{n}):=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
and attempt to compute the least solution of
\varphi
w.r.t.
(ub^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,ub^{\prime}_{n})
and
(lb_{1},\ldots,lb_{n})
.
Otherwise,
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
violates some inequality
l:x_{i}\geq a_{0}+\sum_{j\in[n]}a_{j}x_{j}
, i.e.,
v_{i}<a_{0}+\sum_{j\in[n]}a_{j}v_{j}
. In this case, there must be
j_{0}\in[n]
such that
v_{j_{0}}>0
and
a_{j_{0}}>0
, because otherwise
v_{i}<a_{0}
, contradicting
v_{i}\geq lb_{i}\geq a_{0}
. We proceed by a case distinction.

•

There exists
j_{0}\in[n]
such that
v_{j_{0}}>0
,
a_{j_{0}}>0
and

lb_{j_{0}}<v_{j_{0}}
.
In this case, we try to satisfy the inequality by decreasing the right-hand side
a_{0}+\sum_{j\in[n]}a_{j}x_{j}
,
concretely by decreasing
x_{j_{0}}
. To this end, we set
ub^{\prime}_{j_{0}}:=v_{j_{0}}-1
and attempt to compute the least solution of
\varphi
w.r.t.
(ub_{1},\ldots,\linebreak ub_{j_{0}-1},ub^{\prime}_{j_{0}},\linebreak ub_{j_{0}+1},\linebreak\ldots,\linebreak ub_{n})
and
(lb_{1},\ldots,lb_{n})
.

–

If the attempt succeeds,
we have computed the least solution of
\varphi
w.r.t. the same lower bound, which is returned as the desired least solution.

–

If the attempt fails, there might still be a solution with
x_{j_{0}}\geq v_{j_{0}}
.
So let
lb^{\prime}_{j_{0}}:=v_{j_{0}}
and return the least solution of
\varphi
w.r.t.
(ub_{1},\ldots,ub_{n})
and
(lb_{1},\ldots,lb_{j_{0}-1},lb^{\prime}_{j_{0}},lb_{j_{0}+1},\ldots,lb_{n})
. In the case that
\varphi
is unsatisfiable w.r.t. these bounds, report that
\varphi
is unsatisfiable.

•

For every
j_{0}\in[n]
such that
v_{j_{0}}>0
and
a_{j_{0}}>0
,
lb_{j_{0}}=v_{j_{0}}
.

In this case, it is impossible to decrease the right-hand side of
l:x_{i}\geq a_{0}+\sum_{j\in[n]}a_{j}x_{j}
,
so the only way to satisfy the inequality is to increase
x_{i}
.
Hence we set
lb^{\prime}_{i}:=v_{i}+1
and compute the least solution of
\varphi
w.r.t.
(ub_{1},\ldots,ub_{n})
and
(lb_{1},\ldots,lb_{i-1},lb^{\prime}_{i},lb_{i+1},\ldots,lb_{n})
. If
\varphi
is unsatisfiable, report that
\varphi
is unsatisfiable; otherwise, the least solution will be returned as the desired least solution.

Example 3

Consider the
\Phi_{W}
-constraint

\varphi\equiv x_{1}\geq 2x_{2}-4\quad\wedge\quad x_{2}\geq x_{3}-2\quad\wedge\quad x_{3}\geq x_{1}+1.

Our procedure produces the upper bounds of the variables
(ub_{1},ub_{2},ub_{3}):=(6,6,8)
. The lower bounds are extracted from
\varphi
, that are
(lb_{1},lb_{2},lb_{3}):=(0,0,1)
, referring to the constants in the inequalities. Then, our procedure computes the least solution using
the upper bounds
(ub_{1},ub_{2},ub_{3})
and lower bounds
(lb_{1},lb_{2},\linebreak lb_{3})
as follows.

1.

First, since
lb_{i}\leq ub_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq 3
but
(lb_{1},lb_{2},lb_{3})\neq(ub_{1},ub_{2},ub_{3})
,
we compute
(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})
from the current upper bounds and lower bounds:

(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}):=(\lfloor(lb_{1}+ub_{1})/2\rfloor,\lfloor(lb_{2}+ub_{2})/2\rfloor,\lfloor(lb_{3}+ub_{3})/2\rfloor)=(3,3,4).

Since
3\geq 2\times 3-4\wedge 3\geq 4-2\wedge 4\geq 3+1
,
(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})
is a solution of
\varphi
. Thus, we set

(ub_{1}^{\prime},ub_{2}^{\prime},ub_{3}^{\prime}):=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})=(3,3,4)
and compute
the least solution using the updated upper bounds
(ub_{1}^{\prime},ub_{2}^{\prime},ub_{3}^{\prime})
and previous lower bounds
(lb_{1},lb_{2},lb_{3})
.

2.

With the updated upper bounds
(ub_{1}^{\prime},ub_{2}^{\prime},ub_{3}^{\prime})
, we have
lb_{i}\leq ub_{i}^{\prime}
for
1\leq i\leq 3
, but
(lb_{1},lb_{2},lb_{3})\neq(ub_{1}^{\prime},ub_{2}^{\prime},ub_{3}^{\prime})
.
Thus, we compute the new assignment

(v_{1}^{\prime},v_{2}^{\prime},v_{3}^{\prime})
from the updated upper bounds and previous lower bounds:

(v_{1}^{\prime},v_{2}^{\prime},v_{3}^{\prime}):=(\lfloor(lb_{1}+ub_{1}^{\prime})/2\rfloor,\lfloor(lb_{2}+ub_{2}^{\prime})/2\rfloor,\lfloor(lb_{3}+ub_{3}^{\prime})/2\rfloor)=(1,1,2),

which also satisfies
\varphi
as
1\geq 2\times 1-4\wedge 1\geq 2-2\wedge 2\geq 1+1
.
Then, the upper bounds are updated to

(ub_{1}^{\prime\prime},ub_{2}^{\prime\prime},ub_{3}^{\prime\prime}):=(v_{1}^{\prime},v_{2}^{\prime},v_{3}^{\prime})=(1,1,2)
,
with which the procedure is repeated to compute the least solution.

3.

Similarly, since
lb_{i}\leq ub_{i}^{\prime\prime}
for
1\leq i\leq 3
but
(lb_{1},lb_{2},lb_{3})\neq(ub_{1}^{\prime\prime},ub_{2}^{\prime\prime},ub_{3}^{\prime\prime})
,
we compute the assignment

(v_{1}^{\prime\prime},v_{2}^{\prime\prime},v_{3}^{\prime\prime}):=(\lfloor(lb_{1}+ub_{1}^{\prime\prime})/2\rfloor,\lfloor(lb_{2}+ub_{2}^{\prime\prime})/2\rfloor,\lfloor(lb_{3}+ub_{3}^{\prime\prime})/2\rfloor)=(0,0,1).

Since
(v_{1}^{\prime\prime},v_{2}^{\prime\prime},v_{3}^{\prime\prime})
is a solution of
\varphi
, the upper bounds are updated again, i.e.,
(ub^{\prime\prime\prime}_{1},ub^{\prime\prime\prime}_{2},ub^{\prime\prime\prime}_{3}):=(v_{1}^{\prime\prime},v_{2}^{\prime\prime},v_{3}^{\prime\prime})=(0,0,1)
with which the procedure is repeated.

4.

At this moment, we have that
(lb_{1},lb_{2},lb_{3})=(ub^{\prime\prime\prime}_{1},ub^{\prime\prime\prime}_{2},ub^{\prime\prime\prime}_{3})=(0,0,1)
. Moreover,
(0,0,1)
is a solution of
\varphi
. Thus, the procedure returns
(0,0,1)
as the least solution and terminates.∎

3.4
Computing the least solution for nonexpansive
G_{\varphi}

In this case, every edge
(x_{i},l,a,x_{j})
in
G_{\varphi}
satisfies
a=1
, and all the edges out of the same vertex have mutually distinct labels. Then each inequality in
\varphi
is of the form
x_{i}\geq c_{ij}+x_{j}
or
x_{i}\geq c^{\prime}_{i}
for some
c_{ij},c^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We construct another graph
G^{\prime}_{\varphi}
comprising the edges
(x_{i},c_{ij},x_{j})
for the inequalities
x_{i}\geq c_{ij}+x_{j}
.
The computation of the least solution of
\varphi
is reduced to determining the maximum distances (i.e., maximum weights of the paths) between vertices in
G^{\prime}_{\varphi}
.

The intuition is as follows. For every
i\in[n]
, if
x_{i}\geq c_{ij}+x_{j}
and
x_{j}\geq c_{jk}+x_{k}
, since
x_{i},x_{j},x_{k}
range over
\mathbb{N}
, we can deduce that

x_{i}\geq c_{ij}+x_{j}\geq c_{ij}
and
x_{i}\geq c_{ij}+x_{j}\geq c_{ij}+c_{jk}+x_{k}\geq c_{ij}+c_{jk}
.
In general, for each path starting from
x_{i}
, say,
(y_{1},l_{1},a_{1},y_{2})\ldots(y_{r},l_{r},a_{r},y_{r+1})
(where
y_{1}=x_{i}
), we have
x_{i}\geq\max(a_{1},a_{1}+a_{2},\ldots,\sum_{j\in[r]}a_{j})
.
More specifically, the least solution is computed as follows.
For each
i\in[n]
, we define
v_{i}
as the maximum of
0
, and the constants
c^{\prime}_{i}
in the inequalities
x_{i}\geq c^{\prime}_{i}
.
Then the value of
x_{i}
in the least solution is the maximum of
v_{i}
and the numbers
w_{ij}+v_{j}
where
j\in[n]
and
w_{ij}
is the maximum distance
from
x_{i}
to
x_{j}
in
G^{\prime}_{\varphi}
.

To compute the maximum distances between vertices in

3.6
Time complexity

The overall worst-case time complexity of our procedure is exponential in the size of the dependency graph, i.e., exponential in the number of cicuit components (dominated by branch-and-bound for expansive SCCs). The time complexity of each stage is as follows.

•

Topological sorting. We use Tarjan’s algorithm, which runs in linear time in the size of the dependency graph, i.e.,
O(|V|+|E|)
.

•

Non-expansive SCCs. For each non-expansive SCC, we apply a maximal Floyd–Warshall procedure with time complexity
O(n^{3})
, where
n
is the number of nodes in the SCC.

•

Expansive SCCs. For each expansive SCC, we apply a branch-and-bound (BaB) procedure. First, the upper bound
B
in Proposition
1
is computed in polynomial time in the size of the SCC. The solution is then searched over a space of size
B^{n}
via branch-and-bound, which is exponential in
n
in the worst case (e.g., when all branches must be explored).

However, in practice, most SCCs encountered in our experiments are either small or non-expansive. Consequently, our procedure performs well in practice.

Specification of the width inference procedure

We first introduce some notations.
For

.
Moreover, we define

Note that
S\cap V(ls)=\emptyset
and
S\cup V(ls)=\mathsf{dom}(cm)
.
If
ls={\sf nil}
, then
\psi_{cm,ls,\eta}={\sf true}
by convention.

The correctness of
{\sf inferWidth}(cm,ls,S,\eta)
is specified by

, which asserts that if
{\sf inferWidth}
returns
({\sf SAT},\eta^{\prime})
, then
\eta^{\prime}
is the least solution of
\psi_{cm,ls,\eta}
, otherwise (i.e.,
{\sf inferWidth}
returns
{\sf UNSAT}

is unsatisfiable.

\small\begin{array}[]{l}P_{{\sf inferWidth}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\forall cm,ls,S,\eta,\eta^{\prime}.\\
\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\left(\begin{array}[]{l}{\sf inferWidth}(cm,ls,S,\eta)=({\sf SAT},\eta^{\prime})\rightarrow\\
\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\psi_{cm,ls,\eta}[(\eta^{\prime}(x)/x)_{x\in V(ls)}]\ \wedge\\
\forall\eta^{\prime\prime}.\left(\psi_{cm,ls,\eta}[(\eta^{\prime\prime}(x)/x)_{x\in V(ls)}]\rightarrow\eta^{\prime}\leq\eta^{\prime\prime}\right)\end{array}\right)\end{array}\right)\ \bigwedge\\

are quantified (second-order) variables representing assignment functions, instead of concrete assignments.

{\sf inferWidth}(cm,ls,S,\eta)
satisfies

is done by an induction on

, a list of SCCs in
G_{\varphi}
.

{\sf inferSCC}
satisfies the property

defined in the sequel,

\small P_{{\sf inferSCC}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\forall cm,\theta.\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\left(\begin{array}[]{l}{\sf inferSCC}(C,cm)=({\sf SAT},\theta)\rightarrow\\

\small\begin{array}[]{l}P_{{\sf maxFW}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\forall C,md.\ {\sf maxFW}(C)=md\ \rightarrow\\

The inductive proof that

satisfies
P_{{\sf inferWidth}}
proceeds as follows.

Basis step
ls={\sf nil}
.
Then
{\sf inferWidth}(cm,ls,S,\eta)=({\sf SAT},\emptyset)
. Then
\psi_{cm,ls,\eta}={\sf true}
. It is easy to check that
P_{{\sf inferWidth}}
is
{\sf true}
in this case.

Induction step.
Suppose the result holds for
ls^{\prime}
of length less than or equal to
n
. Let us consider
ls=ls^{\prime}::C
of length
n+1
.

•

If
{\sf inferWidth}(cm,ls,S,\eta)=({\sf SAT},\eta^{\prime})
, then from the rule
inferWidth
:
\neq
nil
-
sat
, we know that
{\sf inferSCC}(C,cm^{\prime}_{C})=({\sf SAT},\theta)
and
{\sf inferWidth}(cm,\linebreak ls^{\prime},\linebreak S\cup V(C),\linebreak\eta\cup\theta)=({\sf SAT},\eta^{\prime\prime})
for some
\theta
and
\eta^{\prime\prime}
. By the induction hypothesis,
\eta^{\prime\prime}
is the least solution of
\psi_{cm,ls^{\prime},\eta\cup\theta}
. Moreover, from
P_{{\sf inferSCC}}
, we know that
\theta
is the least solution of
\psi_{cm^{\prime}_{C}}
. It is not hard to see that
\psi_{cm,ls,\eta}\models\psi_{cm^{\prime}_{C}}\wedge\psi_{cm,ls^{\prime},\eta\cup\theta}
. From the fact that
\theta\cup\eta^{\prime\prime}
is the least solution of
\psi_{cm^{\prime}_{C}}\wedge\psi_{cm,ls^{\prime},\eta\cup\theta}
, we deduce that
\theta\cup\eta^{\prime\prime}=\eta^{\prime}
is the least solution of
\psi_{cm,ls,\eta}
.

•

If
{\sf inferWidth}(cm,ls,S,\eta)={\sf UNSAT}
, then either
{\sf inferSCC}(C,cm^{\prime}_{C})={\sf UNSAT}
or (
{\sf inferSCC}(C,cm^{\prime}_{C})=({\sf SAT},\theta)
and
{\sf inferWidth}(cm,ls^{\prime},S\cup C,\eta\cup\theta)={\sf UNSAT}
).

–

If
{\sf inferSCC}(C,cm^{\prime}_{C})={\sf UNSAT}
, then from
P_{{\sf inferSCC}}
, we know that
\psi_{cm^{\prime}_{C}}
is unsatisfiable. From the fact that
\psi_{cm,ls,\eta}\models\psi_{cm^{\prime}_{C}}
, we deduce that
\psi_{cm,ls,\eta}
is unsatisfiable as well.

–

If
{\sf inferSCC}(C,cm^{\prime}_{C})=({\sf SAT},\theta)
and
{\sf inferWidth}(cm,ls^{\prime},S\cup C,\eta\cup\theta)={\sf UNSAT}
, then by the induction hypothesis,
\psi_{cm,ls^{\prime},\eta\cup\theta}
is unsatisfiable. Moreover, from
P_{{\sf inferSCC}}
,
\theta
is the least solution of
\psi_{cm^{\prime}_{C}}
. It is not hard to see that
\psi_{cm,ls,\eta}\models\psi_{cm^{\prime}_{C}}\wedge\psi_{cm,ls^{\prime},\eta\cup\theta}
. From this fact, we deduce that
\psi_{cm,ls,\eta}
is unsatisfiable.

The proof that
{\sf inferSCC}(C,cm)
satisfies
P_{{\sf inferSCC}}
is done by a case analysis.

•

If
C
is trivial, then every inequality in
cm(x)
is of the form
x\geq c
. As a result, the least (nonnegative) solution is
\max\{0,c\mid x\geq c\in cm(x)\}
.

•

If
C
is nontrivial and expansive, then from the rule
{\sf inferSCC}\mbox{: \sf nontrivial-bab}
, the proof mainly relies on the proof that
{\sf BaB}(cm,\overrightarrow{lb},\overrightarrow{ub})
satisfies
P_{{\sf BaB}}
, which can be done by following the branch-and-bound procedure and applying an induction on the recursive calls of
{\sf BaB}
.

•

If
C
is nontrivial and nonexpansive, then we use the rules
{\sf inferSCC}:{}
nontrivial-maxfw-unsat
and
{\sf inferSCC}:{}
nontrivial-maxfw-sat

and mainly rely on the proof that the maximal Floyd–Warshall algorithm
{\sf maxFW}(C)
satisfies
P_{{\sf maxFW}}
, which essentially follows the proof of the correctness of Floyd–Warshall in Isabelle
[
62
]
.

We now elaborate how to prove that
{\sf BaB}(cm,\overrightarrow{lb},\overrightarrow{ub})
satisfies
P_{{\sf BaB}}
.

•

If there exists
i\in[n]
such that
lb_{i}>ub_{i}
, then according to the rule
BaB: not lb
\leq
ub
,
{\sf BaB}(cm,\overrightarrow{lb},\overrightarrow{ub})
returns
{\sf UNSAT}
. Then evidently
\psi_{cm}(\overrightarrow{x})\wedge\overrightarrow{lb}\leq\overrightarrow{x}\leq\overrightarrow{ub}
is unsatisfiable. Therefore, in this case,
P_{{\sf BaB}}
holds. In the sequel, let us assume that
\forall i\in[n].\ lb_{i}\leq ub_{i}
.

•

If
\overrightarrow{lb}=\overrightarrow{ub}
, then
\overrightarrow{lb}
is the only possible solution of
\psi_{cm}(\overrightarrow{x})\wedge\overrightarrow{lb}\leq\overrightarrow{x}\leq\overrightarrow{ub}
.

–

If
\psi_{cm}[lb_{1}/x_{1},\ldots,lb_{n}/x_{n}]
is
{\sf true}
, then according to the rule
BaB: eq-sat
,
{\sf BaB}(cm,\overrightarrow{lb},\overrightarrow{ub})
returns
({\sf SAT},\overrightarrow{lb})
. Evidently,
\overrightarrow{lb}
is the least solution of
\psi_{cm}(\overrightarrow{x})\wedge\overrightarrow{lb}\leq\overrightarrow{x}\leq\overrightarrow{ub}
, thus
P_{{\sf BaB}}
holds in this case.

–

Otherwise,
\psi_{cm}[lb_{1}/x_{1},\ldots,lb_{n}/x_{n}]
is
{\sf false}
. According to the rule
BaB: eq-unsat
, we know that
{\sf BaB}(cm,\overrightarrow{lb},\overrightarrow{ub})
returns
{\sf UNSAT}
. In this case, evidently,
\psi_{cm}(\overrightarrow{x})\wedge\overrightarrow{lb}\leq\overrightarrow{x}\leq\overrightarrow{ub}
is unsatisfiable. Therefore,
P_{{\sf BaB}}
holds in this case.

•

If
\overrightarrow{lb}\neq\overrightarrow{ub}
and
\psi_{cm}[v_{1}/x_{1},\ldots,v_{n}/x_{n}]
is evaluated to
{\sf true}
, then according to the rule
BaB: neq-true
,
{\sf BaB}(cm,\overrightarrow{lb},\overrightarrow{ub})
calls
{\sf BaB}(cm,\overrightarrow{lb},\overrightarrow{v})
recursively and takes its output as the output.
It is easy to see that
\overrightarrow{v}<\overrightarrow{ub}
.
Then by the induction hypothesis,
{\sf BaB}(cm,\overrightarrow{lb},\overrightarrow{v})
returns the least solution of of
\psi_{cm}(\overrightarrow{x})\wedge\overrightarrow{lb}\leq\overrightarrow{x}\leq\overrightarrow{v}
(since a solution
\overrightarrow{v}
already exists).
From
\overrightarrow{v}<\overrightarrow{ub}
, the least solution of
\psi_{cm}(\overrightarrow{x})\wedge\overrightarrow{lb}\leq\overrightarrow{x}\leq\overrightarrow{v}
is also the least solution of
\psi_{cm}(\overrightarrow{x})\wedge\overrightarrow{lb}\leq\overrightarrow{x}\leq\overrightarrow{ub}
.
Therefore,
P_{{\sf BaB}}
holds in this case.

•

If
\overrightarrow{lb}\neq\overrightarrow{ub}
and
\psi_{cm}[v_{1}/x_{1},\ldots,v_{n}/x_{n}]
is evaluated to
{\sf false}
, then there is an inequality
x_{i}\geq a_{0}+\sum_{j\in[n]}a_{j}x_{j}
for some
i\in[n]
in
\psi_{cm}
such that

v_{i}>a_{0}+\sum_{j\in[n]}a_{j}v_{j}
. We distinguish between the following cases.

–

If
\exists j_{0}\in[n].\ v_{j_{0}}>0\land a_{j_{0}}>0\land lb_{j0}<v_{j_{0}}
, then according to the rule
BaB: neq-false-rhs-1
and
BaB: neq-false-rhs-2
,
{\sf BaB}(cm,\overrightarrow{lb},\overrightarrow{ub})
recursively calls
{\sf BaB}(cm,\overrightarrow{lb},\overrightarrow{ub^{\prime}})
, where
\overrightarrow{ub^{\prime}}=(ub_{1},\ldots,ub_{j_{0}-1},v_{j_{0}}-1,ub_{j_{0}+1},\ldots,ub_{n})
.

*

If
{\sf BaB}(cm,\overrightarrow{lb},\overrightarrow{ub^{\prime}})=({\sf SAT},\theta)
, then by the induction hypothesis,
\theta
is the least solution of
\psi_{cm}(\overrightarrow{x})\wedge\overrightarrow{lb}\leq\overrightarrow{x}\leq\overrightarrow{ub^{\prime}}
.
Because
\overrightarrow{ub^{\prime}}\leq\overrightarrow{ub}
, it follows that
\theta
is also the least solution of
\psi_{cm}(\overrightarrow{x})\wedge\overrightarrow{lb}\leq\overrightarrow{x}\leq\overrightarrow{ub}
.
From the rule
BaB: neq-false-rhs-1
,
{\sf BaB}(cm,\overrightarrow{lb},\overrightarrow{ub})=({\sf SAT},\theta)
.
Therefore,
P_{{\sf BaB}}
holds in this case.

*

Otherwise,
{\sf BaB}(cm,\overrightarrow{lb},\overrightarrow{ub^{\prime}})={\sf UNSAT}
.
Then by the induction hypothesis,
\psi_{cm}(\overrightarrow{x})\wedge\overrightarrow{lb}\leq\overrightarrow{x}\leq\overrightarrow{ub^{\prime}}
is unsatisfiable.
As a result, if
\psi_{cm}(\overrightarrow{x})\wedge\overrightarrow{lb}\leq\overrightarrow{x}\leq\overrightarrow{ub}
is satisfiable, then we must have
x_{j_{0}}\geq v_{j_{0}}
.
Therefore, the least solution of
\psi_{cm}(\overrightarrow{x})\wedge\overrightarrow{lb}\leq\overrightarrow{x}\leq\overrightarrow{ub}
(if it exists) is that of
\psi_{cm}(\overrightarrow{x})\wedge\overrightarrow{lb^{\prime}}\leq\overrightarrow{x}\leq\overrightarrow{ub}
, where

\overrightarrow{lb^{\prime}}=(lb_{1},\ldots,lb_{j_{0}-1},v_{j_{0}},lb_{j_{0}+1},\ldots,lb_{n}).

From the rule
BaB: neq-false-rhs-2
,
{\sf BaB}(\psi_{cm},\overrightarrow{lb},\overrightarrow{ub})
recursively calls
{\sf BaB}(\psi_{cm},\overrightarrow{lb^{\prime}},\overrightarrow{ub})
.
By the induction hypothesis, if
{\sf BaB}(\psi_{cm},\overrightarrow{lb^{\prime}},\overrightarrow{ub})
returns
({\sf SAT},\theta)
,
then
\theta
is the least solution of
\psi_{cm}(\overrightarrow{x})\wedge\overrightarrow{lb^{\prime}}\leq\overrightarrow{x}\leq\overrightarrow{ub}
,
otherwise,
\psi_{cm}(\overrightarrow{x})\wedge\overrightarrow{lb^{\prime}}\leq\overrightarrow{x}\leq\overrightarrow{ub}
is unsatisfiable.
Therefore,
P_{{\sf BaB}}
holds in this case.

–

If
\forall j_{0}\in[n].\ a_{j_{0}}>0\rightarrow lb_{j0}=v_{j_{0}}
, then it is impossible to decrease the right-hand side of
x_{i}\geq a_{0}+\sum_{j\in[n]}a_{j}x_{j}
by choosing values smaller than
\overrightarrow{v}
.
Therefore, it is necessary to increase the value of
x_{i}
, that is, it must be the case that
x_{i}\geq v_{i}+1
.
So, the least solution of
\psi_{cm}(\overrightarrow{x})\wedge\overrightarrow{lb}\leq\overrightarrow{x}\leq\overrightarrow{ub}
(if it exists) is that of
\psi_{cm}(\overrightarrow{x})\wedge\overrightarrow{lb^{\prime}}\leq\overrightarrow{x}\leq\overrightarrow{ub}
,
where
\overrightarrow{lb^{\prime}}=(lb_{1},\ldots,lb_{i-1},v_{i}+1,lb_{i+1},\ldots,lb_{n})
.
According to the rule
BaB: neq-false-lhs
, in this case,
{\sf BaB}(cm,\overrightarrow{lb},\overrightarrow{ub})
recursively calls
{\sf BaB}(cm,\overrightarrow{lb^{\prime}},\overrightarrow{ub})
.
By the induction hypothesis, if
{\sf BaB}(cm,\overrightarrow{lb^{\prime}},\overrightarrow{ub})
returns
({\sf SAT},\theta)
,
then
\theta
is the least solution of
\psi_{cm}(\overrightarrow{x})\wedge\overrightarrow{lb^{\prime}}\leq\overrightarrow{x}\leq\overrightarrow{ub}
,
otherwise,
\psi_{cm}(\overrightarrow{x})\wedge\overrightarrow{lb^{\prime}}\leq\overrightarrow{x}\leq\overrightarrow{ub}
is unsatisfiable.
As a result, in this case,
P_{{\sf BaB}}
holds.

5
OCaml implementation and evaluation

5.1
Implementation

From the Rocq implementation of the inductive function
{\sf inferWidth}
in Section
4
, we utilized the extraction mechanism available in the Rocq proof assistant
[
35
,
36
]
to extract OCaml code.
Moreover, as the
{\sf inferWidth}
function takes a
\Phi_{W}
-constraint as input, we implemented a parser to translate a FIRRTL program into its abstract syntax tree (AST), from which
the
\Phi_{W}
-constraint is extracted
by a constraint extractor.
The parser is implemented by
ocamlyacc

[
33
]
, where a context-free grammar of the FIRRTL language (with attached semantic actions) is specified, while
the constraint extractor is extracted from the Rocq implementation.
The OCaml implementation of the
{\sf inferWidth}
function, together with the parser and constraint extractor, constitutes an OCaml implementation of the InferWidths pass of FIRRTL (see Fig.
3
), which we call BFWInferWidths (“BFW” is an abbreviation of “Branch-and-bound + Floyd–Warshall”).

Figure 3
:
The architecture of BFWInferWidths

When running BFWInferWidths on the width inference instance generated from
the RISC-V BOOM processor, the non-tail-recursive implementations of the functions
split
,
concat
and
flatmap
in the OCaml standard library
Base
caused
segmentation faults and stack overflows.
The main reason is that they do not scale to large real-world instances, which may have more than 200,000 variables and
more than 130,000 inequalities.
We replaced these non-tail-recursive implementations
with tail-recursive versions.

Related work

Verification of compilers.

Compiler verification dates back to 1967,
when
[
41
]
proved the correctness of a translation from arithmetic expressions to stack
machine code. It has become a vibrant area ranging from
single compilation pass to sophisticated code optimizations in compilers
[
15
]
.

Representative works on verified compilation of software programming languages
include CompCert for C
[
31
,
26
,
30
,
32
,
8
,
27
]
,
Vélus for Lustre
[
9
]
, Vellvm
[
65
,
66
]
and Crellvm
[
25
]
for LLVM IR,
CakeML for ML
[
29
,
39
,
59
,
50
]
, to cite a few.
In particular, the interactive theorem prover Rocq has been widely adopted to formalize semantics of programs,
compilation and optimization passes; on its basis compilation correctness can be proven, and
executable interpreters/compilers can be automatically synthesized (cf.
[
1
]
).
For instance,
[
5
]
formalized and proved a compiler that translates programs in a simple functional language into code in an idealized assembly language;

[
11
,
12
]
formalized and proved compilers that translate programs in simply-typed or untyped lambda calculus to code in an idealized assembly language;
CompCert is a verified compiler that translates programs in Clight (a large subset of C) to code in PowerPC assembly language.
For domain-specific systems,

[
42
]
designed a safety-oriented language for low-level algebra libraries. Their CompCert-based compiler formally preserves semantics (verified in Rocq), while the language enables deductive verification of safety properties.

[
51
]
proposed a hybrid symbolic method to verify semantics preservation between original and transformed C/C++ programs targeting high-level synthesis, handling complex loop/buffer restructuring, which extends certified compilation principles to hardware synthesis pipelines.

Verification of constraint solvers.

There are several efforts developing formally verified constraint solvers, most of which focus on SAT solvers, with different methods though. For instance,
Lescuyer and Sylvain use Rocq
[
34
]
;
Shankar and Vaucher use PVS
[
56
]
;
Maric takes the shallow embedding in Isabelle/HOL
[
40
,
47
]
;
Fleury et al. use a refinement framework
[
7
,
19
,
18
]
;
Oe et al. utilize the verified programming language Guru
[
49
,
58
]
.
Besides SAT solvers, solvers
[
10
,
24
]
for constraint programming over finite domains and string constraints
are developed and verified with Rocq and Isabelle/HOL, respectively.
In contrast, our work focuses on a verified solver for a class of linear integer constraints, which are distant from the prior work.

Solving linear constraints.

The use of Presburger arithmetic and its fragments plays a pivotal role in verification and optimization.
The FIRWINE constraints studied in this paper are a fragment of integer linear arithmetic, whose solving algorithms have been studied extensively
[
28
]
.
Modern SMT solvers such as Z3
[
45
,
44
]
and CVC5
[
4
]
implement highly optimized decision procedures for quantifier-free linear integer arithmetic.
Their Optimization Modulo Theories (OMT) extensions
[
6
,
46
]
optimise objective functions over ILA constraints. While SMT solvers with OMT extensions can solve FIRWINE constraints efficiently, their use of e.g., internal heuristic strategies are too complicated to accommodate full verification.

ILP is NP-complete in general, and therefore admits exponential worst-case time complexity. While general ILP solvers (e.g., Gurobi and Z3) feature various heuristic optimizations, their implementations are unverified and hard to be verified.
In contrast, we provide a tailored procedure for solving FIRWINE constraints. It features a considerably lower complexity. Moreover, it is based on classical branch-and-bound (BaB) algorithms which are widely adopted in combinatorial optimization as well as the classical Floyd–Warshall algorithm for solving the all-pairs shortest path problem. We have empirically compared our implementation with the commercial ILP solver Gurobi on both manually constructed and real-world benchmarks. The results show that our implementation outperforms Gurobi or
achieves comparable performance. In particular, our solving algorithm enjoys a modular Rocq proof, which significantly pushes the frontier of a verified compiler for FIRRTL.

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we have studied the width inference problem for FIRRTL program compilation.
We derived a subclass of linear integer constraints from a FIRRTL program where the bit widths of some components are not explicitly given, and provided a complete procedure for solving these constraints.
Furthermore, the procedure is formally verified in Rocq, where an
OCaml implementation is also derived with competitive performance as the official InferWidths pass in
firtool
.

This work presents the first formally verified InferWidths pass, marking a significant step toward a production-quality, verified FIRRTL compiler.
Broadly speaking, it advances the goal of formally verified hardware, which
would contribute to a verified execution stack that preserves correctness guarantees from software down to hardware.

As the next step, we will extend our approach to deal with dynamic shift left, which introduces exponential terms in the width constraints. Moreover, it is interesting to implement the procedure proposed in this work in C++, which could replace the current InferWidths pass in
firtool
.

Syntax of FIRRTL

According to the syntax, FIRRTL features high-level constructs such as vector types, bundle types, conditional statements, connects, and modules.
These high-level constructs are then gradually removed by a sequence of
lowering transformations
. During each lowering transformation, the circuit is rewritten into an equivalent circuit using simpler, lower-level
constructs. Eventually, the circuit is simplified to its most restricted form, resembling a structured netlist,
which allows for easy translation to an output language (e.g., Verilog).
This form is given the name lowered FIRRTL (i.e., LoFIRRTL) and is a strict subset of the full FIRRTL language.
For clarity, the original form is referred to as high FIRRTL (HiFIRRTL).

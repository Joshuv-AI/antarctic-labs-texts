Attenuated total reflection Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy enables determination of the extent of precancer in cervical conization biopsy
Authors: Samuel Onuh Abuh, Ayan Barbora, Vadim Nave, Alon Ben-Arie, Refael Minnes
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The determination of the extent and margins of precancerous or cancerous lesions is still a problem in oncological surgeries today. This is more so with the ‘holy grail’ goal of optimal excision requiring total lesion removal and maximal neighboring tissue conservation especially for an important organ like the cervix. Although frozen section is the gold standard method available for aiding intraoperative diagnosis and surgery decision management, in dysplasia of the cervix where precancer cases are treated, it is not applicable due to various reasons, mainly to avoid over treatment. Still an optimal surgery is highly advocated and currently applying LUGOL dye to cervix is the accepted technique although not always accurate. Attenuated total reflection Fourier transform infrared (ATR-FTIR) spectroscopy can complement visual inspection after LUGOL application, and several efforts have shown its potential in this regard. Research efforts on the subject, however, have not explored the use of ATR-FTIR spectroscopy for determining the extent of precancer on fresh cervical biopsies. This work therefore sets out to investigate ATR-FTIR spectroscopy for estimating the extent of precancer on fresh cervical samples using discriminatory features from the fingerprint region, as well as entropy and bound water related markers. Our results show that posterior probabilities were statistically different for precancer and healthy sampling points, with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve value of 0.678 ± 0.094. At a cut-off probability of 0.550, sensitivity and specificity were 70% and 59%, respectively. These results show that ATR-FTIR spectroscopy on fresh tissue can aid spatially resolved determination of the extent of precancer, and that bound water and entropy related spectral biomarkers can provide additional diagnostic information for the ultimate goal of aiding intraoperative decision management.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-52684-4.

Introduction

One of the responsibilities of a surgeon during cervical surgeries is the identification of disease margins. Cervical excision and conization procedures are often performed for diagnostic and therapeutic reasons in precancer diagnosis and treatment. An optimal procedure would be one that completely removes precancer/cancer lesions while preserving as much healthy tissue as possible. Incomplete removal of lesions is a factor for recurrence of high-grade cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN), with an associated risk of developing invasive carcinoma1,2. Overestimating lesion boundaries may remove excess normal tissues, associated with possible cervical shortening and future obstetrics complications, like premature births3. Optimal excision, therefore, is the critical goal of surgical interventions in oncology.

In common practice during cervical conization procedures, Lugol dye is applied to the cervix. The dye binds to normal squamous epithelium, allowing the surgeon to remove the non-dyed tissue where dysplasia is suspected. This accepted method has the advantages of avoiding over-treatment and possible future obstetrics complications but has been shown to be sometimes inaccurate where not all the dysplastic cells are removed. In fact, using Lugol iodine alone has shown limited specificity of 40%4. The search for a more rapid, objective, sensitive, and less expensive approach for intraoperative diagnosis of precancer/cancer margins is therefore a subject of ongoing research and attenuated total reflection Fourier transform infrared (ATR-FTIR) spectroscopy has shown promise in respect of these advantages.

Several research efforts have shown that ATR-FTIR spectroscopy can be useful for aiding intraoperative decision management5. Cancer types including brain6, kidney and bladder7, ovarian8, colorectal9, and thyroid10 have been studied, with ATR-FTIR spectroscopy showing good performance towards differentiating healthy tissues from cancer and even for sub-typing a given kind of cancer11,12. However, none of these studies investigated the utility of ATR-FTIR spectroscopy for intraoperatively scanning independent measurement points on the same fresh tissue sample. For instance, on a cone cervical biopsy, precancerous lesions may be localized within a given part of the cervix or spread across the entire cervical epithelium. The number of quadrants of the cervix involved indicates the size or extent of precancer and is a risk factor for residual disease after conization13. Determining the number of involved quadrants or the size of the lesion intraoperatively is non-trivial. Although chemical stains such as Lugol can provide some visual contrast, they are limited, and success depends on the experience of the colposcopist.

An additional gap in the ATR-FTIR study of fresh tissues is the general exclusion of some spectral features that may hold diagnostically relevant information, such as those related to absorption due to bound water14, and system complexity (e.g. spectral entropy). More broadly, the physicochemical state of lipid membranes (composition, phase and molecular order) can strongly modulate interfacial interactions and binding phenomena, which may in turn affect near-surface spectroscopic signatures15. Bound water information could be probed by employing constrained principal component analysis (CPCA) on the water stretch spectral region. CPCA combines PCA and regression analysis, employing prior row or variable information about the data matrix. For analyzing data such as the water stretching region as proposed here, it has the advantage of being computationally less expensive compared to alternatives like Gaussian fitting but requires the existence of prior data which may not be readily available. CPCA has found application in subjects such as the analysis of fMRI data, with temporal and spatial constraints16. Detailed theory on CPCA may be found in the work by Takane17. Spectral entropy on the other hand relates to the degree of signal complexity and may describe energy distribution for a given spectral profile or peak.

The Bayesian approach provides a framework for estimating posterior probability for a class of interest, in this case precancer, given the variable data. To estimate this probability, two parameters are important: the prior, which is a probability term based on initial belief; and the likelihood which is the probability of the data given sample class. Although defining the true distribution and likelihood function for specific data is tasking, this approach is relatively simple and could provide a quick yes or no diagnostic feedback based on calculated posterior probabilities. This work sets out to utilize the Bayesian approach to investigate ATR-FTIR spectroscopy for differentiating healthy regions from precancerous ones on cervical epithelium, employing features from the fingerprint and water stretching regions, as well as those due to entropy in specific molecules.

Materials and methods

Sample collection

Samples were obtained from 58 women diagnosed by prior cervical biopsy with high grade squamous intraepithelial lesion (HSIL) or persistent low grade squamous intraepithelial lesion (LSIL). These women were referred to undergo cervical loop conization at the gynecology department at Kaplan Medical Centre, Rehovot, Israel. In 39 patients, one cone-shaped sample was resected for evaluation. In the other 19 patients, the cervical cone biopsy was small or fragmented (26 small biopsies including 1 biopsy each from 13 patients, 2 each from 5 patients, and 3 from 1 patient were collected and evaluated). Cone specimens were marked with an anterior reference stitch that guided both quadrant measurements and for the pathologist’s orientation during examination. All the resected samples were sent for pathological evaluation following infrared absorption measurements done within 5 to 10 min of the surgical procedure. One spectrum was measured for each small sample, while four spectra, one from each quarter, were measured per cone-shaped biopsy. A detailed breakdown of collected samples and measured spectra is shown in Supplementary Fig. S1. Overall, 183 sampling points comprising 54 healthy and 129 precancer dysplasia results (48 LSIL + 81 HSIL), along with their pathology reports were included in this study. The study protocol was approved by the local ethical IRB committee at the Kaplan Medical center in Rehovot, Israel and all experiments were performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. An informed consent was obtained for each patient included in the study.

ATR-FTIR spectroscopy

Infrared absorption measurements were performed using a Thermo Scientific iS50 FTIR spectrometer equipped with a Germanium ATR device. Detailed descriptions of the spectrometer and the ATR element have been given previously14. Each measurement was an average of 64 scans at 4 cm− 1 resolution. A background is recorded in air before each sampling point is measured. Figure 1 shows a representation of a cone-shaped biopsy and the sequence of steps in its measurement. After each sample measurement, the ATR was cleaned with ethanol and dried using a medical air gun. Measurement of a cone shaped biopsy takes only around 10 min, an advantage over conventional frozen section analysis.

Spectral processing

The collected spectra were processed in two regions, including the fingerprint between 1000 and 1480 cm− 1 and the water stretching region between 3100 and 3500 cm− 1. In the fingerprint region, after cutting to the specified range, three representative spectra, one from each of the three classes of healthy, low-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion (LSIL), and high-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion (HSIL), were selected, averaged, and baseline corrected for use as a reference spectrum in Rmies scattering correction based on Bassan et al.18. Five iterations were done using 8 principal components (PCs). The Rmies corrected spectra were then vector normalized before being subjected to the outlier removal process, employing the T2 vs. Q-residual approach19. 50 iterations were run using, respectively, T2 and Q-Residual limits of 20 and 0.01. The algorithm was run three times until no outlying sample was returned. The sequence of outlier removal is shown in Supplementary Fig. S2. 144 spectra, including 44 from healthy sampling points and 100 from precancerous sampling points, were then forwarded for further statistical analysis.

Results

Feature selection

The preprocessed absorption spectra in the fingerprint region were analyzed for the purpose of extracting diagnostically important parameters. The mean absorbances for the two classes of healthy and precancer are presented in Fig. 2. The peaks around 1027 cm− 1, 1082 cm− 1 and 1156 cm− 1 may be attributed to absorptions due to glycogen22. Assigning 1082 cm− 1 to glycogen absorption here, similar to Jusman et al.23, is due to the limited penetration depth associated with Germanium ATR element, which restricts absorption to molecules in the vicinity of the cell membrane. The peaks around 1245 cm− 1, 1400 cm− 1, and 1456 cm− 1 are respectively due to amide III proteins, symmetric CH3 stretching, and asymmetric CH3 stretching vibrations in proteins24.

The difference between the two classes on these absorbance peaks is subtle, and we therefore employed PCA to determine the most important regions associated with class-driven variance in the data. PCA was first performed, and the scores on the first 15 components were subjected to receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis, using the sample true class as determined by the pathologist’s report. Principal component directions presenting scores with areas under the ROC curve (AUC-ROC) of at least 55% were retained, and their loadings are presented in Fig. 3 (A–C). These loadings were then inspected for highly contributing peaks and regions, which are marked on the loading plots. Based on this, the ratios 1080/1156, 1156/1027, and 1456/1400 were computed as well as entropies within their respective regions. Protein-related entropy (PRE) and glycogen-related entropy (GRE) were computed in the ranges 1375–1474 cm− 1 and 1065–1168 cm− 1, respectively. The result of the ordered AUC-ROCs for these parameters, together with that for an additional parameter, 3400/1400, is presented in Fig. 3D. An AUC-ROC threshold value of 0.55, chosen to be above the chance value, was used to define a parameter as diagnostically important.

The parameter 3400/1400, related to the relative amount of bound water at the tissue-crystal interface, was computed using CPCA. Figure 4A shows the normalized spectra data in the analyzed range. Figure 3B presents the fitted peak around 3250 cm− 1 representing the LDW, which was regressed onto the normalized water stretch spectra. The estimated LDW for each sample according to Eq. (5) was subtracted from the normalized absorbance to obtain the constrained residual data matrix in Fig. 4C. The constrained residual data was then subjected to PCA to reveal a first principal component (PC) (Fig. 4D) centered around 3400 cm− 1, which contains information about the bound interfacial water, HDW. PC1 was subsequently regressed onto the constrained residual to obtain regression coefficients 3400, serving as estimates of the HDW. The ratio of these regression coefficients to the respective absorbances around 1400 cm− 1 was evaluated as 3400/1400 to serve as a hydration metric for the samples.

Posterior probability of precancer

The probability that a given sampling point was precancer was estimated using Eq. (8), including only parameters satisfying the condition AUC-ROC ≥ 0.55 in the feature matrix. A test of multivariate normality was performed on the feature matrix, and the result is shown in Supplementary Fig. S3, indicating a near multivariate normal distribution and allowing for the computation of likelihoods based on Eq. (9). While large number of healthy and precancer measurements are needed to determine the true distribution of sample class, the size and design of our pilot study present a limitation in this regard. Hence, we assumed multivariate normality for simplicity. The average probability of precancer for the two sample groups is presented in Fig. 5A. The probability of precancer for healthy and precancer sampling points presented means of 0.475 ± 0.035 and 0.617 ± 0.020, respectively. Figure 5B shows the ROC curve for the probabilities, indicating an AUC-ROC value of 67.8% ± 9.4%. Using a cut-off probability of 0.550, a sensitivity of 70% and specificity of 59% was achieved for the detection of precancer. To estimate a patient-level performance, the cut-off was applied to 29 cone-shaped samples having a maximum of one measurement point in the excluded outlying spectra. 41.4% had at least 75% sampling points correctly predicted, 6.9% had 67% correctly predicted points, 34.5% had 50% points correctly predicted, while 17.2% had less than 50% sampling points correctly predicted.

Discussion

This work investigated ATR-FTIR spectroscopy for intraoperative diagnosis of cervical precancer. ATR-FTIR spectra were collected, to our knowledge, for the first time from different quarters on fresh conized cervical biopsies and analyzed for the purpose of detecting precancerous transformations. Calculated posterior probabilities of precancer were observed to be statistically different for healthy and precancerous sampling points (p < 0.01), indicating the significance of the current results and the potential of ATR-FTIR spectroscopy for use in rapidly and objectively determining the extent of precancerous invasion during conization procedures. While our method involved measurements on already excised biopsies, using a handheld ATR device such as in7 could allow for near real time intraoperative determination of quadrant positivity or margin status. The common clinical approach for intraoperative cervical examination involves visual examination after Lugol staining. Low sensitivity and specificity have been reported for colposcopic examinations, and the experience of the colposcopist is also an important factor in visual diagnosis25–27. ATR-FTIR spectroscopy could therefore be a useful aid to the surgeon for optimal excision, with the ultimate goal of securing a favorable prognosis for the patient.

ATR-FTIR spectral biomarkers, including entropy in specific molecules and absorption due to bound water (HDW), are investigated, to our knowledge, for the first time for fresh biopsies. Protein-related spectral entropy was found to be of diagnostic significance based on our defined threshold of AUC-ROC. Spectral entropy has previously been demonstrated to show excellent performance towards small molecule identification in tandem mass spectroscopy, achieving an AUC-ROC of 0.958. It has also been employed on electroencephalogram (EEG) signals to measure the depth of anesthesia28 and differentiate epileptic seizures20,29, capturing changes in cortical activities that occur with changes in neurological state. Here, entropy in specific molecules, particularly protein, could represent minute changes of biochemical states arising from cellular activities, which become more rapid with precancerous/cancerous transformations.

The problem of water absorption during ATR-FTIR analysis of freshly resected tumors has been addressed in the past using methods such as digital subtraction8 and restricting analysis to water-free spectral regions6. Here, we excluded water absorbing peaks from the fingerprint region but exploited the water stretching absorption band for additional diagnostic information. Minnes et al.14 first showed that the broad water stretching absorption band can be analyzed to distinguish cancer cells of different metastatic potential by fitting two distinct peaks, one around 3200 cm− 1 and the other around 3400 cm− 1, respectively representing low density water (LDW) and high density water (HDW). The hydration parameter 3400/1400 used here has a similar basis but employed a computationally less expensive method of CPCA to extract the bound water metric for each sample and ratioed it to the protein peak around 1400 cm− 1. HDW absorption here could arise from water hydrated to the membrane of epithelial cells or to extracellular proteins within the field of the probing evanescent radiation.

While the posterior probabilities of precancer were statistically different for the two sample groups, the AUC-ROC and the specificity were low at 67.8% and 59%, respectively. High accuracy up to 82%6 have been reported in a large sample-sized intraoperative study of the brain employing diamond ATR crystal. Multiple independent measurements on cone-shaped biopsies with a large crystal, such as in our experiment, present an inherent limitation on spatial resolution compared to the pathologist’s, given intrasample heterogeneity expected for each biopsy. Our results can be improved greatly using a small diamond ATR crystal, which affords better spatial resolution and higher penetration depth. A large crystal also requires a more rigorous cleaning routine, given the tendency of cellular materials for adsorption. We found that cleaning with ethanol at least twice and drying with a medical air gun addresses the problem of cellular adsorption.

In summary, this study provides a proof-of-concept that quadrant-resolved ATR-FTIR spectroscopy on freshly excised conization specimens can support rapid, objective intraoperative assessment of cervical precancer extent, an aspect that has not been previously addressed in fresh-tissue ATR-FTIR workflows. By leveraging spectral markers beyond the conventional fingerprint region, including molecule-specific entropy measures and a bound-water hydration metric, we obtained posterior probabilities that were significantly different between healthy and precancerous sampling points (p < 0.01). Although the present discrimination performance remains moderate (AUC-ROC 67.8%; sensitivity 70%; specificity 59% at a 0.55 cutoff), these results establish a clinically relevant framework for intraoperative mapping of quadrant positivity and margin status. We expect that improved spatial resolution and penetration depth using a small diamond ATR interface, together with patient-/specimen-grouped validation and larger prospective cohorts, will enhance robustness and move this approach toward real-time surgical decision support.

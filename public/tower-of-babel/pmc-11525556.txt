Sign language recognition using modified deep learning network and hybrid optimization: a hybrid optimizer (HO) based optimized CNNSa-LSTM approach
Authors: Abdullah Baihan, Ahmed I Alutaibi, Mohammed Alshehri, Sunil Kumar Sharma
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractSpeech impairment limits a person’s capacity for oral and auditory communication. Improvements in communication between the deaf and the general public can be progressed by a real-time sign language detector. Recent studies have contributed to make progress in motion and gesture identification processes using Deep Learning (DL) methods and computer vision. But the development of static and dynamic sign language recognition (SLR) models is still a challenging area of research. The difficulty is in obtaining an appropriate model that addresses the challenges of continuous signs that are independent of the signer. Different signers’ speeds, durations, and many other factors make it challenging to create a model with high accuracy and continuity. This study mainly focused on SLR using a modified DL and hybrid optimization approach. Notably, spatial and geometric-based features are extracted via the Visual Geometry Group 16 (VGG16), and motion features are extracted using the optical flow approach. A new DL model, CNNSa-LSTM, is a combination of a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), Self-Attention (SA), and Long-Short-Term Memory (LSTM) to identify sign language. This model is developed for feature extraction by combining CNNs for spatial analysis with SA mechanisms for focusing on relevant features, while LSTM effectively models temporal dependencies. The proposed CNNSa-LSTM model enhances performance in tasks involving complex, sequential data, such as sign language processing. Besides, a Hybrid Optimizer (HO) is proposed using the Hippopotamus Optimization Algorithm (HOA) and the Pathfinder Algorithm (PFA). The proposed model has been implemented in Python, and it has been evaluated over the existing models in terms of accuracy (98.7%), sensitivity (98.2%), precision (98.5%), Word Error Rate (WER) (0.131), Sign Error Rate (SER) (0.114), and Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (NDCG) (98%) as well. The proposed model has recorded the highest accuracy of 98.7%.Keywords: Sign language recognition (SLR), Deep learning, Convolutional neural network, Hippopotamus optimization algorithm, Pathfinder algorithmSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

An essential means for communicating with those who are deaf or hard to hear is the use of signs. The goal of SLR systems is to automatically transform sign language requests to spoken or written words in order to close the divide in communication1. Although DL has demonstrated great potential in SLR, precision, and resilience. Sophisticated algorithms, such as DL frameworks by convolution along with RNNs, were used in SLR to analyze and convert gestures and motions into text or speech2. SLR technologies use footage of hands and postures to effectively identify the intricate phrases used by visual language practitioners3. These mentioned technologies have great potential to support equitable interaction among those in the hard-of-hearing population by spanning the gap between users of speech and those who sign in a variety of communicating situations. The goal of the current study is to enhance the rapidity, precision, and elasticity of SLR systems in order to better attain the different requirements of sign languages across the globe4. The most commonly available database for SLR is shown in Fig. 1.

Neural network (NN) models are trained to identify spoken gestures captured on video as segments of SLR based on DL techniques5. Related indicators of signs are found by using deep learning (DL) techniques like convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs), which examine the temporal and spatial properties of finger motions. Moreover, the DL-based SLR technology has enabled accurate translation of signs into text and spoken speech, which has been advantageous for the deaf and hard of hearing community at large12. The robustness and effectiveness of SLR technologies are increasing due to ongoing developments in DL approaches, making them more valuable tools for inclusive engagement and access6. In addition, LSTMs, CNNs, and RNNs have respective hinders irrespective of whether they have the ability to interpret signals7. Sign language requires long-term dependency, yet motions change with time and are hard for RNNs to capture. CNNs may overlook the temporal information included in gesture cycles because they typically concentrate on the spatial components of each frame8. LSTMs mitigate some of this problem by maintaining temporal interactions, but they can be operationally expensive and prone to overfitting, especially when working with sparse data9. Moreover, it might be difficult for any of the three models to adjust to varied signing contexts and styles or to generalise across distinct signs7. Therefore, even though these models provide insightful information, it is still imperative to address their shortcomings to advance the field1. These include enhancing resilience to a variety of signing techniques, reducing computational costs, and improving temporal modeling10. Moreover, the proposed approach suggests a novel hybrid optimization method and two different classifiers for SLR using DL11. With this method, we aim to increase the accuracy of recognition and enhance the system’s ability to adapt to changes in how sign language is executed. Research motive

The goal of study in SLR is to help the deaf as well as hard-of-hearing population communicate more effectively by creating precise and effective systems that can decode sign language movements. The objective is to develop strong algorithms as well as models that can consistently apply the signs into spoken or written language by utilizing developments in ML vision as well as NNs. This will enable smooth communication and diversity in a variety of fields, including science and medicine, as well as learning. Moreover, the proposed approach helps to enable people with hearing problems to engage fully within society and interact successfully, which will ultimately promote equal opportunity along with access among all.

Literature review

Some of the recent literatures related to this suggested approach has been discussed below,

RNN detection and instruction approach for ASL research has been suggested by Lee et al.12. This research suggests a novel concept for an ASL training program that incorporates leap movement controller-based actual time sign detection. It identifies 26 ASL characters at a surprising average success rate of 99.44% via LSTM RNN and k-NN. The database, which is a playing whack-match, improves the effectiveness of receiving ASL by offering entertaining appointments and correctly recognizing dynamic and static signs. This novel method closes the gap between ASL apps for schooling, providing an effective and attractive learning setting.

A DL-based SLR and detection-based study has been developed by Kothadiya et al.13. For assisting people with language or hearing issues to interconnect additional effectively, this research provides a detection based on the DL method. It successfully posters and appreciates gestures in ISL movies through LSTM and GRU procedures. The suggested approach uses one layer of LSTM and GRU, which attains a precision of about 97% for 11 dissimilar signs. Through the simplification of communications for people who are not relaxed with the use of signs, this method has the potential to improve inclusion and access for people with disabilities.

A combination of LSTM-CCNN models used for critical detection of signs has been developed by Asari et al.14. In this research, VGG-19 + LSTM is exposed to be the most positive model, consuming 96.39% dependability when LSTM or BLSTM is compared in LRCN backgrounds for crucial recognition of sign languages. Furthermore, tiny LRCN systems using BLSTM attain an accuracy of 95.18%, which is comparable to that of VGG + LSTM. Using BLSTM improves sustained studying, which is important for emergency sign language translation.

Forecasting of SLR via complex CNN has been suggested by Arun Prasath & Annapurani15. In order to overcome the difficulties associated with SLR, this study suggests a from beginning to end system that incorporates transmitter Multi-Layer ML-CNN. The model outperforms existing methods by 1% as well as 3.5% above BLSTM & HMM, accordingly, with an accuracy rating of 87.5% based on the ROBITA signs and gestures dataset by assessing both linear as well as non-linear variables. The goal of this development is to improve deaf people’s access to communications.

DL-based Deaf Disabled People SLR research has been suggested by Aslam et al.16. In an effort to overcome barriers to communication among hearing-impaired and deaf neighborhoods, investigators create a Sign Language Conversation (SLC) platform that makes use of BERT-based DL as well as natural language processing. After evaluating MLP, CNN, as well as RestNet50v2, a precision of 97% in motion detection is achieved by RestNet50v2. Overall, with a BLEU rating of 0.83%, the NLP BERT system produces answers with an accuracy level of 80%. By removing obstacles of interaction for those who suffer from hearing impairment and opening the door for smart chatbots, this type of technology provides an opportunity to improve interactions within the deaf community.

Pathan et al.17 addressed a multi-headed CNN to train two layers of images. The images were processed as a whole for training in the first layer, and the hand landmarks were extracted in the second layer of image processing. Data augmentation and dynamic learning rate reduction has been utilised to get around the overfitting issue.

Mohsin et al.18 used an ASL dataset from the MNIST database to assess many DL models, such as CNN, VGG16, ResNet50, and InceptionV3. The ASL alphabetic letters were represented by hand movements in the dataset. With an accuracy of 0.96, the best-performing model was InceptionV3.

Zhang et al.19 presented a dual-path, lightweight deep CNN (DPCNN) that erases backgrounds. There were two pathways in the DPCNN. While the other way learnt the background features, the first path was utilised to learn the overall features. To effectively depict hand features, the background features were gradually reduced from the total features. Following their flattening into a one-dimensional layer, these characteristics move via a FC layer with a 128-output unit. Lastly, the output layer was a FC layer with an output unit.

Sign language is a wonderful tool for people with hearing and speech disabilities to convey their thoughts and emotions to the world. However, just developing sign language is not enough. Automatic sign gesture detection systems can help close the communication gap that has existed for years. Initially, the raw data are given to the preprocessing phase to make it fit for training the proposed network. Then, an extraction phase is carried out to extract the significant features using VGG-16. Finally, the proposed classifier identifies the gesture effectively. LSTM

The output gate determines which information from the present cell state is useful for the current outputs and future forecasts. The LSTM network learns the temporal correlations between the signs over time by evaluating the combined features progressively and employing the memory cell as well as gates. Proposed CNNSa-LSTM

Evaluation on training and testing loss

Accuracy in both training and testing is a crucial parameter in the learning process that is used to assess how well a model performs. In order to produce forecasts, the model must first learn trends and traits from the initial set of information. The way well a model works on the initial batch of data is shown by its initialization correctness. On unknown information, however, excellent training correctness does not imply excellent results. This underscores the significance of accuracy testing. A distinct data set which the algorithm did not encounter throughout learning is used to assess testing accuracy. It provides a more accurate picture of how effectively the hypothesis applies to fresh, untested data. A framework that performs well on learning but poorly on testing may be overfitting, which indicating that it acquires data used for training by memory rather than by recognizing patterns that may be applied to other situations. On the other hand, if the model’s precision during validation and training is inadequate, it may be underfitting and unable to recognize the root causes of trends in the data. Creating a solid and dependable learning network requires finding a balance among training and test efficiency. Moreover, the training and testing accuracy of the suggested model can be shown in Fig. 14a and b.

Testing and training loss constitute significant measures of a model’s effectiveness in learning algorithms. The loss of training is an indicator of the discrepancy among the actual and predicted results on the initial set, therefore the model’s goal is to decrease it. Reduced loss of training suggests that the algorithm is successfully picking up on the trends in the information being analyzed. But the model’s ability to extrapolate effectively to fresh, untested data is ultimately what matters. Testing loss, sometimes referred to as validation loss, is computed using another set of data information the algorithm hasn’t used for training. It sheds light on the model’s performance with unknown data.

When a model does well on data used for training yet badly on fresh information, it may be excessive fitting, as seen by an elevated testing loss relative to training losses. On the other hand, underfitting the model’s inability to recognize the underlying trends in the data—is indicated if the testing training losses are both significant. Developing a model that performs well in generalization and generates accurate predictions on fresh data requires striking a balance between training and testing its loss. Conclusion

In this research work, a new DL model CNNSa-LSTM, which is a combination of CNN, SA and LSTM to recognize the Sign Language has been introduced. The newly proposed DL model has been trained using the extracted VGG16 based spatial and geometric features , along with the optical flow-based motion features. A HO has been newly introduced, leveraging the HOA and PFA, respectively. The model has been implemented in python, and evaluated over the existing models in terms of accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, WER, SER and NDCG as well. The proposed model has recorded the highest accuracy of 98.7%. The technique, which integrates CNN, SA, and LSTM with HO, surpasses other approaches across all evaluation measures. This approach, integrating CNN, SA, and LSTM with HO, outperforms other methods across all evaluation metrics. The results demonstrate not only improved accuracy but also enhancements in precision, recall, F1-score, and NDCG, while WER and SER reached their lowest levels. Moreover, from the contributions made in this work, that further research could extend the model proposed on more complex and diverse gesture databases, especially where more variability comes in motion patterns. In addition, the study of how this model performs in real-time conditions and possible integration with wearable devices or augmented reality systems would open new pathways for its practical implementation. Further improvement would consist of optimization process refinement of the hyperparameters to decrease the computational load while keeping it highly accurate.

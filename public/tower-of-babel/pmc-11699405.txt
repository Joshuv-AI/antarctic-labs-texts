Protocol to evaluate virus-specific CD4+ and CD8+ T cell responses by spectral flow cytometry
Authors: Xin-Jing Zhao, Sheng Zhang, Wang-Qian Wei, Qiang Xu, Chen-Long Lv, Guo-Lin Wang, Li-Qun Fang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryT cell responses play an important role in viral clearance and prevention of infection. Here, we present a protocol to detect severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2)-specific T cell responses using spectral flow cytometry. We describe steps for peripheral blood mononuclear cell (PBMC) thawing and stimulation with SARS-CoV-2 spike peptide pools. We then describe detailed procedures for cell confining, staining, and fixation. This protocol has potential application in spectral flow cytometry on other virus-specific T cell responses.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Zhao et al.1

Before you begin

This protocol provides materials and specific steps for evaluating the virus-specific CD4+ and CD8+ T cell responses against SARS-CoV-2-spike mega peptide pools (MPs). A cohort of 42 vaccinated subjects was enrolled between September 19 and November 08, 2022. All individuals were diagnosed with confirmed Omicron BA.2.2 infection through sequencing. Between January 06 and February 01, 2023, a total of 26 subjects were followed-up for data and sample collection, among which 14 participants experienced Omicron BA.5 reinfection and their infections were confirmed by self-antigen test and timely surveillance data of circulating SARS-CoV-2 variants in China. Compare with the proportion (52.4%) of symptomatic participants post Omicron BA.2 infection, the proportion (28.6%) of symptomatic participants post Omicron BA.5 reinfection is lower.

Here, we collect peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from COVID-19 patients to identify the expression of activation-induced marker (AIM) OX40+CD137+ (CD4+ T cells) and CD69+CD137+ (CD8+ T cells), intra cellular staining (ICS) IFN-γ, IL-2 and TNF-α secretion, and phenotypic differentiation of virus-specific memory T cells, including central memory T cells (TCM, CD45RA-CCR7+), naïve T cells (Tnaive, CD45RA+CCR7+), effector memory T cells (TEM, CD45RA-CCR7-) and terminally differentiated effector memory T cells (TEMRA, CD45RA+CCR7-), by spectral flow cytometry.

Before starting, it is essential to check the configuration of your instrument to ensure compatibility with the reagents used in the proposed panel.

Materials and equipment

Complete cell culture medium

RPMI 1640 culture medium and FBS are mixed in a volume of 9:1, and store at 4°C until use.

Fluorescence-activated cell sorting buffer

PBS and FBS are mixed in a volume of 49:1, and store at 4°C until use.

25% DMSO

Sterile deionized water and DMSO are mixed in a volume of 4:1, and store at 4°C until use.

1× perm/wash buffer

Sterile deionized water and 10× Perm/Wash buffer are mixed in a volume of 9:1, and should be freshly prepared before application.

100× cell activation cocktail

Complete cell culture medium and 1000× Cell activation cocktail (without Brefeldin A) are mixed in a volume of 9:1, and should be freshly prepared before application.

100× brefeldin A

Complete cell culture medium and 1000× Brefeldin A are mixed in a volume of 9:1, and should be freshly prepared before application.

100× monensin

Complete cell culture medium and 1000× Monensin are mixed in a volume of 9:1, and should be freshly prepared before application.

Anti-CD28

Complete cell culture medium and anti-CD28 (0.5 mg/mL) are mixed in a volume of 9:1, and should be freshly prepared before application.

Anti-CD49

Complete cell culture medium and anti-CD49 (0.5 mg/mL) are mixed in a volume of 9:1, and should be freshly prepared before application.

Expected outcomes

In this section, we show the gating strategy of a representative example of for AIM and ICS assays in Figure 1. Lymphocytes are gated by using the FSC-A and SSC-A parameters, live cells are gated by using the SSC-A and Live/Dead_FVS780 parameters, then CD3+ T cells are identified based on the expression of CD3 and CD14, and B cells are excluded by using CD19.5,6 T cells are further divided into CD4+ and CD8+ T cells populations based on the expression of CD4 and CD8a, respectively (Figure 1A). Virus-specific T cells responses are measured by AIM CD137+OX40+ (CD4+ T cells) and CD137+CD69+ (CD8+ T cells) respectively (Figure 1B).7 CD4+ and CD8+ T cells are further gated based on a combination of each cytokine (TNF-α, IL-2 and IFN-γ) with CD4 and CD8, respectively (Figure 1C). Quantification and statistical analysis

We used ID7000 software to analyze the flow cytometry data. The gating strategy is showed in Figure 1. Virus-specific AIM CD4+ and CD8+ T cells responses were calculated as percent of total CD4+ or CD8+ T cells. ICS were calculated as the percentage of CD4+ or CD8+ T cells that express TNF-α, IL-2 and IFN-γ. The background was removed from the data by subtracting the well stimulated with DMSO. The value above zero is considered as positive.

Limitations

In this study, we mainly focus on virus spike-specific immunity and were not able to assess the function of SARS-CoV-2-specific T cells targeting other genes. Additionally, as cells are fixed immediately after stimulation, some proteins sensitive to fixation may not be detectable using this protocol. Finally, PBMCs were stored at −80°C, the cellular viability gradually decreased with time. Low cellular viability would underestimate the size of the total spike-specific T cell responses (see troubleshooting, problem 2).

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Too many cell debris (related to steps 1–9).

Potential solution

The dead cells would converge into clumped debris, leading to skewed results. Resting cells before stimulation would help remove cell debris, which leads to a cleaner assay. After recover and washing cells, measure viability and adjust the concentration to an appropriate level for resting. Incubate the cells in a sterile incubator at 37°C with 5% CO2 for at least 1 h. After incubation, gently resuspend the rested cells and allow any clumped debris to fall to the bottom of the tube. While avoiding the clump, collect the cell suspension to a new tube for use.

Problem 2

Low cellular viability (related to steps 1–9).

Potential solution

When the samples are delayed from the community to the laboratory, the cell viability would be decreased. To avoid this, try to minimize the transportation time. In addition, the process of removing PBMCs from −80°C and thawing PBMCs in 37°C water bath should be quickly.

Problem 3

Inadequate PBMCs number (related to steps 1–10 in before you begin).

Potential solution

The volume of blood will affect the PBMCs number. More volume for blood sampling and less thawing after freeze will increase the PBMCs number.

Problem 4

False positive or false negative results (related to steps 21–23).

Potential solution

Human Fc receptors (FcRs) are expressed on a variety of cells, such as monocytes, granulocytes, B cells and dendritic cells. The FcRs-mediated Ig Fc binding would lead false positive or false negative results of immunofluorescent staining. Using anti-Fc receptor could block FcRs-involved unwanted staining.

Problem 5

Insufficient cell surface staining (related to steps 27–35).

Potential solution

The markers containing BV Fluorophore are incubated and stained in several times to ensure the binding effect.

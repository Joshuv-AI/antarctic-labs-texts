Abstract
. We solve the Kato square root problem for parabolic operators of arbitrary order 2 ​ m 2m whose coefficients are allowed to depend on both space and time in a merely measurable way and possess boundedness and ellipticity controlled by a Muckenhoupt A 2 − A_{2}- weight. Notably, the proof applies to the weighted Kato problem within an elliptic framework.



1.
Introduction

We study the
2m
order parabolic operators in form of

(1.1)

\mathcal{H}:=\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}:=\partial_{t}+(-1)^{m}\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}w^{-1}\partial^{\alpha}(a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}),

where
m\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and
w=w(x)
is independent of
t
and belongs to the spatial Muckenhoupt class
A_{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},dx).
The coefficients
a_{\alpha,\beta}:=\{a_{\alpha,\beta}\}
are complex-valued, measurable and dependent on both space and time, satisfying the degenerate ellipticity condition in the G
\mathring{a}
rding sense:

(1.2)

\mbox{Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}a_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x)\partial^{\alpha}f(t,x)\overline{\partial^{\beta}f(t,x)}dxdt\geq c_{1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}|\nabla^{m}f(t,x)|^{2}w(x)dxdt,\quad\forall f\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon},

and the degenerate boundedness condition:

(1.3)

|\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}a_{\alpha,\beta}(t,x)\xi_{\alpha}\overline{\zeta_{\beta}}|\leq c_{2}w(x)|\xi||\zeta|,\quad\forall\;\xi_{\alpha},\;\zeta_{\beta}\in{\mathbb{C}},

for some positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}
and all
(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}.
Here, for any
\xi,\zeta\in({\mathbb{C}})^{p},

\xi\cdot\overline{\zeta}:=\sum_{|\beta|=m}\xi_{\beta}\overline{\zeta_{\beta}}
denotes the inner product on
({\mathbb{C}})^{p}
(bear in mind that
({\mathbb{C}})^{p}={\mathbb{C}}^{n}
when
m=1
).
\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}:=\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1};{\mathbb{C}})
is the weighted parabolic energy space of
m
order, which contains all functions
u
such that
u,\nabla^{m}u:=(\partial^{\alpha}u)_{|\alpha|=m}
and
D_{t}^{1/2}u
are in the weighted Lebesgue space
L^{2}_{\upsilon}:=L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1},d\upsilon)(d\upsilon:=dwdt=w(x)dxdt).
The rigorous definitions of these objects can be found in Section 2.

This work is devoted to the derivation of the Kato estimate for
\mathcal{H},
that is,

(1.4)

\|\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}u\|_{L^{2}_{\upsilon}}\approx\|\nabla^{m}u\|_{L^{2}_{\upsilon}}+\|D_{t}^{1/2}u\|_{L^{2}_{\upsilon}}\quad(u\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}),

where the implicit constant depends only on
n,m,
the ellipticity constant and the
A_{2}-
weight constant of
w.
More precisely, we aim to show:

Theorem 1.1
.

Any operator
\mathcal{H}
given in (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) can be defined as a maximal accretive operator in
L^{2}_{\upsilon}
via an accretive sesquilinear form with domain
\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}.
Moreover, the domain of its unique maximal accretive square root
\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}
coincides with
\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon},
and the Kato estimate (
1.4
) is satisfied.

In particular, as a byproduct of the proof of (
1.4
), an elliptic version of Theorem
1.1
is also obtained. Let
\mathcal{L}
be as in (
1.1
), defined on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},
and
W^{m,2}_{w}:=W^{m,2}_{w}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}};{\mathbb{C}})
be the usual weighted Sobolev space of order
m
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Given
w\in A_{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},dx).
Suppose that the coefficients of
\mathcal{L}
satisfy (
1.2
) -(
1.3
) on
W^{m,2}_{w}
and
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}.
Then
\mathcal{L}
can be defined as a maximal accretive operator in
L^{2}_{w}:=L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},dw)
via an accretive sesquilinear form with domain
W^{m,2}_{w},
moreover, the domain of its unique maximal accretive square root
\sqrt{\mathcal{L}}
is also
W^{m,2}_{w}
and

(1.5)

\|\sqrt{\mathcal{L}}u\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\approx\|\nabla^{m}u\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\quad(u\in W^{m,2}_{w})

holds with the implicit constant depending only on
n,m,
the ellipticity constant and the
A_{2}
-weight constant of
w.

The second step involves using a higher-order version of the weighted Littlewood-Paley theory, the adapted parabolic setup and the boundedness of the “principal part approximation operator” to attain the estimate:

(1.7)

|||\lambda^{m}\mathcal{H}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}f-(-1)^{m}\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}\lambda^{m}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}w^{-1}\partial^{\alpha}(a_{\alpha,\beta}\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}\partial^{\beta}f)|||_{2,\upsilon}\lesssim\|\nabla^{m}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\upsilon}}+\|D_{t}^{1/2}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\upsilon}},

see the details in Section 5. In the third step, we first reduce the problem to showing that

(1.8)

|(-1)^{m}(\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\lambda^{m}(1+\lambda^{2m}\mathcal{H})^{-1}w^{-1}\partial^{\alpha}(a_{\alpha,\beta}))_{|\beta|=m}|^{2}\frac{d\upsilon d\lambda}{\lambda}\;\;\mbox{is a Carleson measure.}

Then, we generalize the weighted
Tb-

[

Extending Theorem

2.
Preliminaries

We now rigorously define the notations introduced earlier and introduce additional symbols to state our results.

2.1. Notation.
For any fixed
(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},
we call
||(t,x)||_{m}
the parabolic norm of
(t,x)
if it is the unique solution
\rho
of the equation

\frac{t^{2}}{\rho^{4m}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{\rho^{2}}=1.

If
m=1,
the parabolic norm
||(t,x)||_{m}
becomes the usual one as in
[
3
,
6
,
7
,
15
,
33
,
34
]
. Given a half-open cube
Q=Q_{r}(x):=(x-r/2,x+r/2]^{n}
with sidelength
r
and center
x
in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},
and an interval
I=I_{r}(t):=(t-r^{2m}/2^{2m},t+r^{2m}/2^{2m}],
we use
\Delta:=\Delta_{r}(t,x):=I\times Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
to denote a parabolic cube in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
of size
l(\Delta):=r.
For any
\lambda>0,

\lambda\Delta=(\lambda^{2m}I)\times(\lambda Q):=(t-(\lambda r)^{2m}/2^{2m},t+(\lambda r)^{2m}/2^{2m}]\times(x-(\lambda r)/2,x+(\lambda r)/2]^{n}
is used to represent the dilation of
\Delta.
In what follows, we use
1_{E}
to denote the characteristic function of a set
E.

2.2. Weights.
A real-valued and non-negative function
w(x)
defined on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is said to belong to the Muckenhoupt class
A_{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},dx)
if

(2.1)

[w]_{A_{2}}:=\sup_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w(x)dx\right)\cdot\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w(x)^{-1}dx\right)<\infty,

where the supremum is taken with respect to all cubes
Q\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}.
Using the weight function
w,
we can define a measure on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
by simply setting
dw(x):=w(x)dx,
which satisfies that there exist two constants
\eta\in(0,1)
and
\gamma>0,
depending only on
n
and
[w]_{A_{2}},
such that, for any measurable subset
E\subset Q,

(2.2)

\gamma^{-1}\left(\frac{|E|}{|Q|}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\eta}}\leq\frac{w(E)}{w(Q)}\leq\gamma\left(\frac{|E|}{|Q|}\right)^{2\eta}.

Here,
|\cdot|
is the Lebesgue measure on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
w(E):=\int_{E}dw.
We also need the notation for weighted averages, expressed as follows:

(f)_{E,w}:=\fint_{E}f(x)dw(x):=\fint_{w(E)}f(x)w(x)dx:=\frac{1}{w(E)}\int_{w(E)}f(x)w(x)dx,

where
0<w(E)<\infty
and
f
is a locally integrable on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
with respect to
dw.
In particular, we abbreviate
(f)_{E}
when
w\equiv 1.
From (
2.2
), it is easy to see that
dw
is doubling, that is, there is a constant
D
(called doubling constant), depending only on
n
and
[w]_{A_{2}},
such that

(2.3)

w(2Q)\leq Dw(Q)\quad\mbox{for all cubes}\;Q\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}.

In addition, the measure
dw^{-1}
is also doubling thanks to (
2.1
), and the doubling constant is still denoted by
D
for simplicity.
Moreover, the weight function
w
induces two measures on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1},
defined as

(2.4)

\displaystyle d\upsilon:=d\upsilon(t,x):=w(x)dxdt

\displaystyle d\upsilon^{-1}:=d\upsilon^{-1}(t,x):=w^{-1}(x)dxdt.

They are all doubling concerning parabolic cubes
\Delta\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1},
with the same doubling constant
2^{2m}D
(see also
[
3
, (2.4)]
).

2.3. Weighted energy spaces.
As usual, we let
L^{2}_{w}:=L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},dw)
be the weighted Lebesgue space with its norm denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{w}}:=\|\cdot\|_{2,w},
then it is a Hilbert space. Below, we use
<\cdot,\cdot>_{2,w}
to denote the inner product and
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{w}\to L^{2}_{w}}(\|\cdot\|_{2\to 2,w})
to denote the operator norm of linear operators on
L^{2}_{w}.
Owing to (
2.1
), we see

(2.5)

L^{2}_{w}\subset L^{1}_{loc}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}).

It is well-known that the class
C_{0}^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
of smooth and compactly supported functions serves as a dense subspace of
L^{2}_{w}.
The same properties and notations apply to
L^{2}_{\upsilon}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}.

Definition 2.1
.

(Elliptic weighted Sobolev space of higher order).  We let
W^{m,2}_{w}:=W^{m,2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},dw)

:=W^{m,2}_{w}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},{\mathbb{C}})
be the space of all
f\in L^{2}_{w}
such that its distributional derivatives
\partial^{\alpha}f
belong to
L^{2}_{w}
for all
|\alpha|\leq m.
The space
W^{m,2}_{w}
is equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{W^{m,2}_{w}}:=(\|f\|_{2,w}^{2}+\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{2,w}^{2})^{1/2}.

Clearly,
W^{m,2}_{w}
is a Hilbert space and
C_{0}^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
is dense in
W^{m,2}_{w}
. In particular, using the weighted Sobolev interpolation inequality in
[
19
]
:

(2.6)

\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\partial^{\gamma}v|^{2}w\right)\leq c_{0}(n,m,[w]_{A_{2}})\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|v|^{2}w\right)^{(1-\frac{|\gamma|}{m})}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\nabla^{m}v|^{2}w\right)^{\frac{|\gamma|}{m}}\quad(\forall\;|\gamma|\leq m),

we obtain, for any
f\in W^{m,2}_{w},

(2.7)

\|f\|_{W^{m,2}_{w}}\approx(\|f\|_{2,w}^{2}+\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{2,w}^{2})^{1/2}.

For ease of presentation, we adopt the notations
\lesssim
and
\approx.
Specifically, for two positive constants
A,B
, the expression
A\lesssim B
means that there exists a nonessential constant
C,
depending only on
n,m,c_{1},c_{2}
and
[w]_{A_{2}},
such that
A\leq CB.
The notations
A\gtrsim B
and
A\approx B
should be interpreted similarly.

The operators
D_{t}^{1/2}
and
H_{t}
denote the half-order derivative and Hilbert transform in time through the Fourier symbols
|\tau|^{1/2}
and
isgn(\tau),
respectively. By
[
32
]
(considering
f(\cdot,x)
with
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
fixed), we have for
a.e.x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
that

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|D_{t}^{1/2}f\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2}=c\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{|f(t,x)-f(s,x)|^{2}}{|t-s|^{2}}dtdsdw(x)

with the right-hand side being finite precisely when
D_{t}^{1/2}f\in L^{2}_{\upsilon}.

We now introduce the higher-order parabolic weighted energy space in the parabolic setting.

Definition 2.2
.

(Parabolic weighted energy space of higher order).  The space
\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}:=\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},{\mathbb{C}})

:=\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1},d\upsilon)
consists of all functions
f\in L^{2}_{\upsilon}
such that
D_{t}^{1/2}f
and
\partial^{\alpha}f
belong to
L^{2}_{w}
for all
|\alpha|\leq m.
The norm of
\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}
is defined by

\|f\|_{\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}}:=(\|f\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2}+\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{{}_{2,\upsilon}}^{2}+\|D_{t}^{1/2}f\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2})^{1/2}.

It is easy to show that
\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}
is a Hilbert space. Moreover, it follows from (
2.7
) that

(2.9)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}}\approx(\|f\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2}+\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{{}_{2,\upsilon}}^{2}+\|D_{t}^{1/2}f\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2})^{1/2}

holds for all
f\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}.
In the following, we sometimes use the symbol
{\mathbb{D}}f:=(\nabla^{m}f,D_{t}^{1/2}f)=(\partial^{\alpha}f,D_{t}^{1/2}f)_{|\alpha|=m}
when
f\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}.

3.
The higher order parabolic operator and the central estimate to (
1.4
)

In this section we first give a comprehensive discussion of how to interpret the parabolic operator (
1.1
) as either a bounded operator
\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}\to\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}^{*}
or an unbounded operator on
L^{2}_{\upsilon}
via a sesquilinear form. Hereafter, the superscript
∗
stands for the dual.

Note that Lemma
7.5
implies that

(3.1)

\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}\subset L^{2}_{\upsilon}\simeq(L^{2}_{\upsilon})^{*}\subset(\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon})^{*}.

By self-evident embeddings,

(3.2)

D_{t}^{1/2}:\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}\to L^{2}_{\upsilon},\quad\partial^{\alpha}:\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}\to L^{2}_{\upsilon},\quad\forall\;|\alpha|\leq m,

furthermore, in view of (
3.1
),

(3.3)

D_{t}^{1/2}:L^{2}_{\upsilon}\to\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}^{*},\quad w^{-1}\partial^{\alpha}(w\cdot):L^{2}_{\upsilon}\to\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}^{*},\quad\forall\;|\alpha|=m.

If we split

(3.4)

\partial_{t}=D_{t}^{1/2}H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2},

it then follows from (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) that

\partial_{t}:\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}\to\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}^{*}.

Consequently,
\mathcal{H}
can be defined as a bounded operatior
\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}\to\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}^{*}
via

(3.5)

<\mathcal{H}u,\phi>:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}u\cdot\overline{D_{t}^{1/2}\phi}+\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}w^{-1}a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}u\cdot\overline{\partial^{\alpha}\phi}d\upsilon\quad\forall\;u,\phi\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}.

Due to (
3.1
), we are justified in considering the maximal restriction of
\mathcal{H}
to an operator in
L^{2}_{\upsilon},
called the part of
\mathcal{H}
in
L^{2}_{\upsilon},
with domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}):=\{u\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}:\mathcal{H}u\in L^{2}_{\upsilon}\}.
Obviously,

<\mathcal{H}u,v>=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}\mathcal{H}u\overline{\phi}

holds for all
\phi\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}
and
u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}),
which implies that the part of
\mathcal{H}
in
L^{2}_{\upsilon}
gives a meaning to the formal expression (
1.1
) by applying a formal integration by parts in (
3.5
). In particular, it follows from (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) again that

(3.6)

\mathcal{H}=D_{t}^{1/2}H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}+\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}(-1)^{|\alpha|}w^{-1}\partial^{\alpha}(w)(w^{-1}a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}).

The equation (
3.6
) plays a key role in our proof.

3.1. Maximal accretivity.
After introducing
\mathcal{H}
as a bounded operator
\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}\to\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}^{*}
in (
3.5
), we need to further demonstrate that it is a maximal accretive operator on the Hilbert space
L^{2}_{\upsilon}
in order to run the functional calculus for sectorial operators
[
23
,
31
]
. Indeed, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\sigma\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
\mbox{Re}\;\sigma>0.
The following assertions are true.

(i)\quad
For each
f\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}^{*},
there exists a unique
u\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}
such that
(\sigma+\mathcal{H})u=f.
Moreover,

\|u\|_{\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}}\leq C\max\{\frac{c_{2}+1}{c_{1}},\frac{|\mbox{Im}\;\sigma|+1}{\mbox{Re}\;\sigma}\}\|f\|_{\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}^{*}},

and
for all
\phi\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon},

(3.7)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}\sigma u\overline{\phi}+\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}w^{-1}a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}u\cdot\overline{\partial^{\alpha}\phi}+H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}u\cdot\overline{D_{t}^{1/2}\phi}d\upsilon=f(\phi).

(ii)\quad
If
f\in L^{2}_{\upsilon},
then

(3.8)

\|(\sigma+\mathcal{H})^{-1}f\|_{2,\upsilon}\leq\frac{\|f\|_{2,\upsilon}}{\mbox{Re}\;\sigma}.

In particular, the part of
\mathcal{H}
in
L_{\upsilon}^{2}
is maximal accretive and
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
is dense in
\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}.

(iii)\quad
The adjoint
\mathcal{H}^{*}
of
\mathcal{H}
can be identified formally as the backward-in-time operator

-\partial_{t}+\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}(-1)^{|\beta|}w^{-1}(\partial^{\beta}w)(w^{-1}\overline{a_{\alpha,\beta}}\partial^{\alpha}),

and all the above results hold with
\mathcal{H}
replaced by
\mathcal{H}^{*}.

Proof.
We begin with
(i).
The proof of
(i)
relies on the hidden coercivity of the parabolic sesquilinear form (
3.5
), a property originally discovered by Kaplan
[
29
]
and re-discovered several times in
[
8
,
20
,
28
,
33
]
. The case
m=1
is stated in
[
3
, Lemma 4.1]
.

We define the sesquilinear form
B_{\delta,\sigma}:\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}\times\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}\to{\mathbb{C}}
via

(3.9)

\displaystyle B_{\delta,\sigma}(u,\phi):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}\sigma u\cdot

\displaystyle\overline{(I+\delta H_{t})\phi}+w^{-1}a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}u\cdot\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(I+\delta H_{t})\phi}

\displaystyle+H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}u\cdot\overline{D_{t}^{1/2}(I+\delta H_{t})\phi}d\upsilon,

where
\delta\in(0,1)
to be chosen later. By Plancherel’s theorem, the Hilbert transform
H_{t}
is isometric on
\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon},
then
B_{\delta,\sigma}
is bounded. Observing that
H_{t}
is skew-adjoint, we then see

(3.10)

\mbox{Re}\;\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}H_{t}h\cdot\overline{h}=0\quad\mbox{for all}\;h\in L^{2}_{\upsilon}.

Exploiting (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) and (
3.10
), it is now standard to deduce that, for any
\sigma\in{\mathbb{C}},

(3.11)

\mbox{Re}\;B_{\delta,\sigma}(u,u)\geq\delta\|D_{t}^{1/2}u\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2}+(c_{1}-c_{2}\delta)\|\nabla^{m}u\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2}+(\mbox{Re}\;\sigma-\delta|\mbox{Im}\;\sigma|)\|u\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2}.

If we restrict
\mbox{Re}\;\sigma>0
and choose
\delta:=\min\{\frac{c_{1}}{1+c_{2}},\frac{\mbox{Re}\;\sigma}{1+|\mbox{Im}\;\sigma|}\}
in the above inequality, then by (
2.9
),

(3.12)

\mbox{Re}\;B_{\delta,\sigma}(u,u)\gtrsim\min\{\frac{c_{1}}{1+c_{2}},\frac{\mbox{Re}\;\sigma}{1+|\mbox{Im}\;\sigma|}\}\|u\|_{\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}}^{2}

With (
3.12
) in hand, an application of the Lax-Milgram lemma and the fact that
I+\delta H_{t}
is isometric on
\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}
yields (
3.7
). We complete the proof of
(i).

We now proceed with the proof of
(ii).
By
(i)
, for any
\sigma\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
\mbox{Re}\;\sigma>0,
we know that
\sigma+\mathcal{H}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})\to L_{\upsilon}^{2}
is bijective. Letting
f\in L_{\upsilon}^{2},
we can define
u:=(\sigma+\mathcal{H})^{-1}f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})\subset\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}.
Utilizing (
1.2
) and (
3.10
), we get

\displaystyle\mbox{Re}\;\sigma\|u\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mbox{Re}\;\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}(\sigma u\cdot\overline{u}+\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}a_{\alpha,\beta}w^{-1}\partial^{\beta}u\cdot\overline{\partial^{\alpha}u}+H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}u\cdot\overline{D_{t}^{1/2}u})\;d\upsilon

\displaystyle=\mbox{Re}\;<(\sigma+\mathcal{H})u,u>_{2,\upsilon}=\mbox{Re}\;\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}f\cdot\overline{u}d\upsilon\leq\|f\|_{2,\upsilon}\|u\|_{2,\upsilon}.

Thus

(3.13)

\|(\sigma+\mathcal{H})^{-1}f\|_{2,\upsilon}\leq\frac{\|f\|_{2,\upsilon}}{\mbox{Re}\;\sigma}.

This implies that the resolvent set of the part of
\mathcal{H}
in
L_{\upsilon}^{2}
is non-empty, so the part of
\mathcal{H}
in
L_{\upsilon}^{2}
is closed. An application of
[
23
, Proposition 2.1.1]
shows that
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
is dense in
L_{\upsilon}^{2}.
As a result, the part of
\mathcal{H}
is maximal accretive.

It remains to prove that
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
is indeed dense in
\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}.
To this end, we use the sesquilinear form
B_{\delta,1}
from (
3.9
), with
\delta
chosen to ensure (
3.12
). Given a
\phi\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}
being orthogonal to
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}).
Thanks to (
3.12
), we can apply the Lax-Milgram lemma to find a unique
\omega\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}
such that

<u,\phi>_{\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}}=B_{\delta,1}(u,\omega)\quad\mbox{for all}\;u\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}.

Confining further
u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}),
it follows from (
3.7
) that

0=<(1+\mathcal{H})u,(I+\delta H_{t})\omega>_{2,\upsilon}.

Since
1+\mathcal{H}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})\to L_{\upsilon}^{2}
is bijective, the latter estimate implies

0=<h,(I+\delta H_{t})\omega>_{2,\upsilon}\quad\mbox{for all}\;h\in L_{\upsilon}^{2}.

Therefore,
(I+\delta H_{t})\omega=0.
This yields
\omega=0,
then
\phi=0.
The proof of
(ii)
is complete.

Eventually, substituting the sesquilinear form
B_{\delta,\sigma}(u,\phi)
with
B^{*}_{\delta,\sigma}(u,\phi):=\overline{B_{\delta,\sigma}(\phi,u)}
and repeating the above arguments, we conclude with
(iii).

\Box

3.2. Reducing (
1.4
) to the quadratic estimate (
1.6
).

Since
\mathcal{H}
is maximal accretive according to Lemma
3.1
, then it has a unique accretive square root
\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}
defined by the functional calculus for sectorial operators (see
[
23
,
31
]
), and the same is true for the adjoint
\mathcal{H}^{*}
with
(\sqrt{\mathcal{H}})^{*}=\sqrt{\mathcal{H}^{*}}.
This allows us to apply
[
23
, Theorem 5.2.6]
and write

(3.14)

\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}f=c(m)\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{3m}\mathcal{H}^{2}(1+\lambda^{2m}\mathcal{H})^{-3}f\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\quad(f\in\mathcal{D}(\sqrt{\mathcal{H}})).

It is clear that the integral is understood as an improper Riemann integral in
L^{2}_{\upsilon}.
With (
3.14
) in hand, for any
h\in L^{2}_{\upsilon},
it follows that

|\langle\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}f,h\rangle_{2,\upsilon}|\lesssim|||\lambda^{m}\mathcal{H}(1+\lambda^{2m}\mathcal{H})^{-1}f|||_{2,\upsilon}\cdot|||\lambda^{2m}\mathcal{H}^{*}(1+\lambda^{2m}\mathcal{H}^{*})^{-2}h|||_{2,\upsilon},

where

|||\cdot|||_{2,\upsilon}:=\left(\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+2}}|\cdot|^{2}\frac{d\upsilon d\lambda}{\lambda}\right)^{1/2}.

Since
\mathcal{H}^{*}
is also maximal accretive in
L^{2}_{\upsilon}
thanks to the conclusion
(iii)
in Lemma
3.1
, we can use
[
23
, Theorem 7.1.7; Theorem 7.3.1]
to deduce

|||\lambda^{2m}\mathcal{H}^{*}(1+\lambda^{2m}\mathcal{H}^{*})^{-2}h|||_{2,\upsilon}\lesssim\|h\|_{2,\upsilon}.

Consequently, by taking the supremum over all
h,
we obtain

\|\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}f\|_{2,\upsilon}\lesssim|||\lambda^{m}\mathcal{H}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}f|||_{2,\upsilon}.

Suppose that

(3.15)

|||\lambda^{m}\mathcal{H}(1+\lambda^{2m}\mathcal{H})^{-1}f|||_{2,\upsilon}\lesssim\|\nabla^{m}f\|_{2,\upsilon}+\|D_{t}^{1/2}f\|_{2,\upsilon}\quad(f\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon})

holds for now.
Then

(3.16)

\|\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}f\|_{2,\upsilon}\lesssim\|\nabla^{m}f\|_{2,\upsilon}+\|D_{t}^{1/2}f\|_{2,\upsilon}\quad(f\in\mathcal{D}(\sqrt{\mathcal{H}})\cap\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}\supset\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})).

By Lemma
3.1
again,
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
is dense in
\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}.
Bear in mind that
\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}
is closed in
L^{2}_{\upsilon}.
This implies that (
3.16
) is indeed true for all
f\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}.
In veiw of the transform
f(t,x)\to f(-t,x)
and the definition of
\mathcal{H}^{*},
we see that (
3.16
) also holds with
\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}
replaced by
\sqrt{\mathcal{H}^{*}},
that is,

(3.17)

\|\sqrt{\mathcal{H}^{*}}h\|_{2,\upsilon}\lesssim\|\nabla^{m}h\|_{2,\upsilon}+\|D_{t}^{1/2}h\|_{2,\upsilon}\quad(h\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}).

On the other hand, by using (
3.11
) with
\sigma=0
and letting
\delta
small, we deduce that, for all
f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}),

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{m}f\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2}+\|D_{t}^{1/2}f\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2}\lesssim|\langle\mathcal{H}f,(I+\delta H_{t})f\rangle_{2,\upsilon}|\lesssim\|\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}f\|_{2,\upsilon}\|\sqrt{\mathcal{H}^{*}}(I+\delta H_{t})f\|_{2,\upsilon}.

This inequality together with (
3.17
) yield

(3.18)

\|\nabla^{m}f\|_{2,\mu}+\|D_{t}^{1/2}f\|_{2,\upsilon}\lesssim\|\sqrt{\mathcal{H}}f\|_{2,\upsilon}\quad(f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})).

Invoking
[
23
, Proposition 3.1.1(h)]
and the fact that
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H})
is dense in
\mathcal{D}(\sqrt{\mathcal{H}})
with respect to the graph norm, it follows immediately that (
3.18
) holds for all
f\in\mathcal{D}(\sqrt{\mathcal{H}})
and
\mathcal{D}(\sqrt{\mathcal{H}})=\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}.
Therefore, concatenating (
3.16
) and (
3.18
), we successfully reduce Theorem
1.1
to the quadratic estimate (
3.15
). For this reduction, one can also refer to the argument in
[
3
, Section 6]
.

Clearly, to obtain (
3.15
), the norm estimates for the resolvent operator
(1+\lambda^{2m}\mathcal{H})^{-1}
are required. This motivates the next section.

4.
Proof of (
3.15
): Part I

Set
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}:=(1+\lambda^{2m}\mathcal{H})^{-1}
and
\mathcal{E}^{*}_{\lambda}:=(1+\lambda^{2m}\mathcal{H}^{*})^{-1}.
Imitating the proof of
[
3
, Lemma 4.3]
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

For all
\lambda>0
and
f\in L^{2}_{\upsilon},

\displaystyle(i)\quad\|\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}f\|_{2,\upsilon}+\|\lambda^{m}{\mathbb{D}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}f\|_{2,\upsilon}\lesssim\|f\|_{2,\upsilon}

\displaystyle(ii)\quad\|\lambda^{m}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}D_{t}^{1/2}f\|_{2,\upsilon}+\|\lambda^{2m}{\mathbb{D}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}D_{t}^{1/2}f\|_{2,\upsilon}\lesssim\|f\|_{2,\upsilon}

\displaystyle(iii)\quad\|\lambda^{m}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}w^{-1}(\nabla^{m}(wf))\|_{2,\upsilon}+\|\lambda^{2m}{\mathbb{D}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}w^{-1}(\nabla^{m}(wf))\|_{2,\upsilon}\lesssim\|f\|_{2,\upsilon}.

The same estimates hold for
\mathcal{E}^{*}_{\lambda}.

Proof.
To achieve
(i),
we set
u:=(\lambda^{-2m}+\mathcal{H})^{-1}f.
Then,
\lambda^{-2m}u=\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}f,
and from (
3.13
), it follows that

\|\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}f\|_{2,\upsilon}\lesssim\|f\|_{2,\upsilon}.

Recalling the definition of sesquilinear form
B_{\delta,\lambda^{-2m}}
in (
3.9
), and applying the Lax-Milgram lemma, we conclude by choosing
\delta=\frac{c_{1}}{2c_{2}}
that

\mbox{Re}\;B_{\delta,\lambda^{-2m}}(u,u)\geq\delta(\|D_{t}^{1/2}u\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2}+\|\nabla^{m}u\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2})+\lambda^{-2m}\|u\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2}

and

(4.1)

B_{\delta,\lambda^{-2m}}(u,u)=<f,(I+\delta H_{t})u>_{2,\upsilon}.

As a consequence,

\|{\mathbb{D}}u\|_{2,\upsilon}\lesssim\lambda^{m}\|f\|_{2,\upsilon}.

This proves
(i).
Since
\mathcal{H}^{*}
shares the same structure with
\mathcal{H}
from the point of view of sesquilinear form, the above arguments apply to
\mathcal{E}^{*}_{\lambda}.
So
(i)
holds for
\mathcal{E}^{*}_{\lambda}
in place of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}.

By duality in
L^{2}_{\upsilon}
and the conclusion
(i)
for
\mathcal{E}^{*}_{\lambda},
we can derive

\|\lambda^{m}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}D_{t}^{1/2}f\|_{2,\upsilon}+\|\lambda^{m}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}w^{-1}(\nabla^{m}(wf))\|_{2,\upsilon}\lesssim\|f\|_{2,\upsilon}.

We now consider
\lambda^{2m}{\mathbb{D}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}D_{t}^{1/2}f.
Note that
D_{t}^{1/2}f\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}^{*}
by (
3.3
). Using Lemma
3.1
, we see
u:=(\lambda^{-2m}+\mathcal{H})^{-1}D_{t}^{1/2}f\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}.

Plugging it into (
4.1
) we reach

B_{\delta,\lambda^{-2m}}(u,u)=<f,D_{t}^{1/2}(I+\delta H_{t})u>_{2,\upsilon},

which yields

\|{\mathbb{D}}u\|_{2,\upsilon}\lesssim\|f\|_{2,\upsilon}.

Due to (
3.3
) again, we can replace

D_{t}^{1/2}f
by
w^{-1}\nabla^{m}(wf)
in the above derivation, which leads to

\|\lambda^{2m}{\mathbb{D}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}w^{-1}\nabla^{m}(wf)\|_{2,\upsilon}\lesssim\|f\|_{2,\upsilon}.

This ends the proof.

\Box

Given two measurable subsets
E,F
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1},
we define their parabolic distance by

d(E,F):=\inf\{\|(t-s,x-y)\|_{m}:(t,x)\in F,(s,y)\in E\}.

As mentioned in
[
3
, Lemma 4.4]
, we cannot expect off-diagonal estimates for the non-local operator
D_{t}^{1/2}.
Fortunately, the off-diagonal estimates estimates involving only the spatial derivatives are sufficient for our purposes, as demonstrated in
[
3
]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that
E,F
are measurable subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1},
and let
d:=d(E,F),
then

(4.2)

\int_{F}|\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}f|^{2}+|\lambda^{m}\nabla^{m}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}f|^{2}d\upsilon\lesssim e^{-\frac{d}{c\lambda}}\int_{E}|f|^{2}d\upsilon

and

(4.3)

\int_{F}|\lambda^{m}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}[w^{-1}\partial^{\alpha}(wf)]|^{2}d\upsilon\lesssim e^{-\frac{d}{c\lambda}}\int_{E}|f|^{2}d\upsilon,\quad\forall\;|\alpha|=m,

hold for some constant
c,
depending only on
n,m,c_{1},c_{2}
and
[w]_{A_{2}},
and all
f\in L_{\upsilon}^{2}
with
{\rm supp}f\subset E.
The same statements are also true with
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
replaced by
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{*}.

Proof.
We follow the strategy of
[
3
, Lemma 4.4]
to prove (
4.2
). First. we let

\Delta:=\frac{\kappa d}{\lambda}

with
0<\kappa<1
to be chosen later. Clearly, we can assume
\Delta\geq 1,
otherwise (
4.2
) is trivial. Second, we pick
\tilde{\eta}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
such that
\tilde{\eta}=1
on
F
and
\tilde{\eta}=0
on
E,
also satisfying, for all
|\alpha|\leq m,

(4.4)

|\partial^{\alpha}\tilde{\eta}|\lesssim d^{-|\alpha|},\quad|\partial_{t}\tilde{\eta}|\lesssim d^{-2m}.

Setting
\eta=e^{\Delta\tilde{\eta}}-1,
we then have

u:=\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}f\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}\quad\mbox{and}\quad v:=u\eta^{2}\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}\mbox{ thanks to Lemma \ref{lemma: b1.5} }.

By the density of
C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
in
\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}
(Lemma
7.5
) and the argument at the top of
[
3
, Page 11]
, we conclude that

\mbox{Re}\;\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}u\cdot\overline{D_{t}^{1/2}v}d\upsilon=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}|u|^{2}\partial_{t}(\eta^{2})d\upsilon.

By this, (
3.7
) with
\sigma=\lambda^{-2m}
and
{\rm supp}f\subset E
, it follows that

(4.5)

\displaystyle\int|u|^{2}\eta^{2}+\lambda^{2m}\mbox{Re}\;\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}w^{-1}a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}u\cdot\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u\eta^{2})}d\upsilon=\frac{\lambda^{2m}}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}|u|^{2}\partial_{t}(\eta^{2})d\upsilon.

In virtue of the definition of
\eta,
we can rewrite (
4.5
) as

(4.6)

\displaystyle\int|u|^{2}(\eta+1)^{2}+\lambda^{2m}\mbox{Re}\;\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}w^{-1}a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}(u(\eta+1))\cdot\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u(\eta+1))}d\upsilon

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{2m}}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}|u|^{2}\partial_{t}(\eta^{2})d\upsilon+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}|u|^{2}(2\eta+1))d\upsilon

\displaystyle+\lambda^{2m}\mbox{Re}\;\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}w^{-1}a_{\alpha,\beta}\left[\partial^{\beta}(u(\eta+1))\cdot\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u(\eta+1))}-\partial^{\beta}u\cdot\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u(\eta+1)^{2})}\right]d\upsilon

\displaystyle+\lambda^{2m}\mbox{Re}\;\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}w^{-1}a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}u\cdot\overline{\partial^{\alpha}(u(2\eta+1))}d\upsilon

\displaystyle:=J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}+J_{4}.

We first treat
J_{1}.
By the definitions of
\tilde{\eta}
((
4.4
)) and
\eta,
also Young’s inequality, it is not hard to show

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}|u|^{2}\eta^{2}d\upsilon+C(\epsilon)\lambda^{4m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}|u|^{2}(\partial_{t}\eta)^{2}d\upsilon

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}|u|^{2}\eta^{2}d\upsilon+C(\epsilon)\lambda^{4m}\Delta^{2}d^{-4m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}|\eta+1|^{2}|u|^{2}d\upsilon\quad\mbox{$(\lambda<d)$}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}|u|^{2}(\eta+1)^{2}d\upsilon+\kappa^{2}C(\epsilon)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}|u|^{2}(\eta+1)^{2}d\upsilon+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}|u|^{2}d\upsilon.

Next, we turn to the domination for
J_{3}.
Apparently, using Leibniz’s rule, we can write

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle=-\lambda^{2m}\mbox{Re}\;\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}w^{-1}a_{\alpha,\beta}\sum_{\tau<\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\tau}\partial^{\beta}u\overline{\partial^{\tau}u}\partial^{\alpha-\tau}(\eta+1)^{2}d\upsilon

\displaystyle+\lambda^{2m}\mbox{Re}\;\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}w^{-1}a_{\alpha,\beta}\sum_{|\tau|+|\gamma|<2m}C_{\alpha}^{\tau}C_{\beta}^{\gamma}\overline{\partial^{\tau}u}\partial^{\gamma}u\partial^{\alpha-\tau}(\eta+1)\partial^{\beta-\gamma}(\eta+1)d\upsilon

\displaystyle:=-J_{31}+J_{32}.

Moreover, the term
J_{32}
can be further decomposed into

\displaystyle J_{32}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2m}\mbox{Re}\;\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}\sum_{\tau=\alpha,\gamma<\beta}C_{\beta}^{\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}w^{-1}a_{\alpha,\beta}\overline{\partial^{\alpha}u}\partial^{\gamma}u(\eta+1)\partial^{\beta-\gamma}(\eta+1)d\upsilon

\displaystyle+\lambda^{2m}\mbox{Re}\;\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}\sum_{\tau<\alpha,\gamma=\beta}C_{\alpha}^{\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}w^{-1}a_{\alpha,\beta}\overline{\partial^{\tau}u}\partial^{\beta}u\partial^{\alpha-\tau}(\eta+1)(\eta+1)d\upsilon

\displaystyle+\lambda^{2m}\mbox{Re}\;\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}\sum_{\tau<\alpha,\gamma<\beta}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}w^{-1}a_{\alpha,\beta}C_{\alpha}^{\tau}C_{\beta}^{\gamma}\overline{\partial^{\tau}u}\partial^{\gamma}u\partial^{\alpha-\tau}(\eta+1)\partial^{\beta-\gamma}(\eta+1)d\upsilon

\displaystyle:=J_{321}+J_{322}+J_{323}.

Observe that
J_{321}
and
J_{322}
are essentially the same type by the symmetry of
\alpha,\beta.
Hence, for simplicity, we only handle
J_{321}
here.

Again, by the definition of
\eta
and Leibniz’s rule, for any
|\xi|\leq m,

(4.7)

\partial^{\xi}(\eta+1)=(\eta+1)P^{\Delta}_{\xi}(\partial_{1},...,\partial_{n})\tilde{\eta},

where
P^{\Delta}_{\xi}
denotes a homogeneous polynomial of degree

(

|P^{\Delta}_{\xi}(\partial_{1},...,\partial_{n})\tilde{\eta}|\lesssim\left(\frac{\Delta}{d}\right)^{|\xi|}\quad(\Delta\geq 1).

By bundling up
u
and
\eta+1,
a very tedious calculation leads to

\partial^{\xi}u(\eta+1)=\sum_{\tau\leq\xi}P_{\xi-\tau}^{\Delta}(\partial_{1},...,\partial_{n})\tilde{\eta}\partial^{\tau}(u(\eta+1)).

Inserting (
4.7
) and (
4.9
) into
J_{321}
we get

\displaystyle J_{321}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2m}\mbox{Re}\;\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}\sum_{\tau=\alpha,\gamma<\beta}C_{\beta}^{\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}w^{-1}a_{\alpha,\beta}\overline{\partial^{\alpha}u}\partial^{\gamma}u(\eta+1)^{2}P^{\Delta}_{\beta-\gamma}(\partial_{1},...,\partial_{n})\tilde{\eta}d\upsilon

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2m}\mbox{Re}\;\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}\sum_{\gamma<\beta}\sum_{\tau\leq\alpha}\sum_{S\leq\gamma}C_{\beta}^{\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}w^{-1}a_{\alpha,\beta}P^{\Delta}_{\beta-\gamma}(\partial_{1},...,\partial_{n})\tilde{\eta}

\displaystyle\times P_{\alpha-\tau}^{\Delta}(\partial_{1},...,\partial_{n})\tilde{\eta}\overline{\partial^{\tau}(u(\eta+1))}P_{\gamma-S}^{\Delta}(\partial_{1},...,\partial_{n})\tilde{\eta}\partial^{S}(u(\eta+1))d\upsilon

Since
\gamma<\beta,
that is,
|S|+|\tau|\leq 2m-1,
then, by (
4.8
) and (
1.3
),

(4.10)

\displaystyle\lambda^{2m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}w^{-1}a_{\alpha,\beta}P^{\Delta}_{\beta-\gamma}(\partial_{1},...,\partial_{n})\tilde{\eta}

\displaystyle\times P_{\alpha-\tau}^{\Delta}(\partial_{1},...,\partial_{n})\tilde{\eta}\overline{\partial^{\tau}(u(\eta+1))}P_{\gamma-S}^{\Delta}(\partial_{1},...,\partial_{n})\tilde{\eta}\partial^{S}(u(\eta+1))d\upsilon

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{2m}c_{2}\left(\frac{\Delta}{d}\right)^{|\alpha-\tau|+|\beta-S|}\|\partial^{S}(u(\eta+1))\|_{2,\upsilon}\|\partial^{\tau}(u(\eta+1))\|_{2,\upsilon}

\displaystyle\lesssim c_{2}\kappa\left(\lambda^{|\tau|}\|\partial^{\tau}(u(\eta+1))\|_{2,\upsilon}\right)\left(\lambda^{|S|}\|\partial^{S}(u(\eta+1))\|_{2,\upsilon}\right)\quad(\kappa<1)

\displaystyle\lesssim\kappa c_{2}c_{0}(n,m,[w]_{A_{2}})\lambda^{|\tau|}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}|u(\eta+1)|^{2}w\right)^{\frac{(1-\frac{|\tau|}{m})}{2}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}|\nabla^{m}(u(\eta+1)|^{2})w\right)^{\frac{|\tau|}{2m}}

\displaystyle\times\lambda^{|S|}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}|u(\eta+1)|^{2}w\right)^{\frac{(1-\frac{|S|}{m})}{2}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}|\nabla^{m}(u(\eta+1)|^{2})w\right)^{\frac{|S|}{2m}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\kappa c_{2}c_{0}(n,m,[w]_{A_{2}})\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}|u(\eta+1)|^{2}d\upsilon+\lambda^{2m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}|\nabla^{m}(u(\eta+1))|^{2}d\upsilon\right)

where in the second last step we used (
2.6
) and in the last step used Young’s inequality!
Remark that
c_{0}
is independent of
\Delta.
Armed with (
4.10
), it follows that

J_{321}\leq C(m,n,c_{2},[w]_{A_{2}})\kappa\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}|u(\eta+1)|^{2}d\upsilon+\lambda^{2m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}|\nabla^{m}(u(\eta+1))|^{2}d\upsilon\right).

Similarly,

(4.11)

\displaystyle\lambda^{2m}

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}w^{-1}a_{\alpha,\beta}\overline{\partial^{\tau}u}\partial^{\gamma}u\partial^{\alpha-\tau}(\eta+1)\partial^{\beta-\gamma}(\eta+1)d\upsilon

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}w^{-1}a_{\alpha,\beta}\overline{\partial^{\tau}u}\partial^{\gamma}u(\eta+1)^{2}P^{\Delta}_{\alpha-\tau}(\partial_{1},...,\partial_{n})\tilde{\eta}P^{\Delta}_{\beta-\gamma}(\partial_{1},...,\partial_{n})\tilde{\eta}d\upsilon

\gamma_{\Delta}(t,x):=\chi(\frac{x-x_{\Delta}}{l(\Delta)})\Theta(\frac{t-t_{\Delta}}{l(\Delta)^{2m}})

and

\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi}(t,x):=\gamma_{\Delta}(t,x)(\phi_{\Delta}(x)\cdot\overline{\xi}),\quad\phi_{\Delta}(x):=\left(\frac{(x-x_{\Delta})^{\beta}}{\beta!}\right)_{|\beta|=m}.

It is evident that
\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi}\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}
and

(6.1)

\nabla^{m}(\phi_{\Delta}(x)\cdot\overline{\xi})\cdot\xi\equiv 1.

For any
0<\epsilon\ll 1,
we can define a test function by

f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}:=(I+(\epsilon l(\Delta))^{2m}\mathcal{H})^{-1}\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi}=\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon l(\Delta)}\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi}\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}

thanks to the definition of
\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi}
and Lemma
4.1
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\xi,f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}
be defined as above, and
\epsilon
be a degree of freedom. Then,

\displaystyle(i)\quad\|f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}-\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi}\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2}\lesssim(\epsilon l(\Delta))^{2m}\upsilon(\Delta),

\displaystyle(ii)\quad\|\nabla^{m}(f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}-\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi})\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2}+\|D_{t}^{1/2}(f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}-\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi})\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2}\lesssim\upsilon(\Delta),

\displaystyle(iii)\quad\|\nabla^{m}f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2}+\|D_{t}^{1/2}f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2}\lesssim\upsilon(\Delta).

Proof.
We first write by (
3.6
) that

(6.2)

\displaystyle f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}-\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi}

\displaystyle=-(\epsilon l(\Delta))^{2m}\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon l(\Delta)}\mathcal{H}\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi}

\displaystyle=-(\epsilon l(\Delta))^{2m}\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon l(\Delta)}D_{t}^{1/2}H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi}

\displaystyle-\sum_{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m}(-1)^{|\alpha|}(\epsilon l(\Delta))^{2m}\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon l(\Delta)}w^{-1}(\partial^{\alpha}w)(w^{-1}a_{\alpha,\beta}\partial^{\beta}\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi}).

By Lemma
4.1
and (
1.3
), that is, the uniform
L^{2}_{\upsilon}-
boundedness of

(\epsilon l(\Delta))^{m}\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon l(\Delta)}w^{-1}(\partial^{\alpha}w)\quad\mbox{and}\quad(\epsilon l(\Delta))^{m}\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon l(\Delta)}D_{t}^{1/2},

one can show

\|f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}-\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi}\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2}\lesssim(\epsilon l(\Delta))^{2m}\|{\mathbb{D}}\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi}\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2}.

From the definition of
\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi}
and Leibniz’s rule, it follows that, for any
|\gamma|\leq m,

|\partial^{\gamma}\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi}|\lesssim l(\Delta)^{m-|\gamma|},

hence

\|\nabla^{m}\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi}\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2}\lesssim\upsilon(\Delta).

An application of Plancherel’s theorem in the
t-
variable leads to that

\|D_{t}^{1/2}\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi}\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2}\lesssim\upsilon(\Delta).

This concludes
(i).
Equipped with the two estimates above, the conclusion
(iii)
is now attributed to
(ii).
To show
(ii),
it is sufficient to invoke the equation (
6.2
) again and utilize
the uniform
L^{2}_{\upsilon}-
boundedness in Lemma
4.1
of

(\epsilon l(\Delta))^{2m}\nabla^{m}\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon l(\Delta)}w^{-1}(\partial^{\alpha}w),\quad(\epsilon l(\Delta))^{2m}D_{t}^{1/2}\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon l(\Delta)}D_{t}^{1/2}

and

(\epsilon l(\Delta))^{2m}D_{t}^{1/2}\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon l(\Delta)}w^{-1}(\partial^{\alpha}w),\quad(\epsilon l(\Delta))^{2m}\nabla^{m}\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon l(\Delta)}D_{t}^{1/2}.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\Delta:=I\times Q
be a parabolic dyadic cube and
\xi,f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}
be defined as in Lemma
6.3
. There exist
\epsilon\in(0,1),
depending only on the
n,m,c_{1},c_{2}
and
[w]_{A_{2}}
, and a finite set
\mathcal{W}
of unit vectors in
({\mathbb{C}})^{p}
with its cardinality depending on
\epsilon,m
and
n,
such that

(6.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{\Delta}\frac{1}{\upsilon(\Delta)}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{l(\Delta)}\int_{\Delta}|\hbar_{\lambda}(t,x)|^{2}\frac{d\upsilon d\lambda}{\lambda}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\xi\in\mathcal{W}}\sup_{\Delta}\frac{1}{\upsilon(\Delta)}\int_{0}^{l(\Delta)}\int_{\Delta}|\hbar_{\lambda}(t,x)\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}\nabla^{m}f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}|^{2}\frac{d\upsilon d\lambda}{\lambda},

where the sumpremum is taken over all dyadic parabolic cubes
\Delta\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}.

Proof.
Given a unit vector
\xi\in({\mathbb{C}})^{p},
we introduce the cone

\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{\epsilon}:=\{v\in({\mathbb{C}})^{p}:|v-(v\cdot\overline{\xi})\xi|<\epsilon|v\cdot\overline{\xi}|\}.

Clearly,
({\mathbb{C}})^{p}
is covered by a finite number of such cones
\{\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{\epsilon}\},
and the number of cones depends on on
\epsilon,m
and
n.
Fix a cone
\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{\epsilon},
and set

\Gamma_{\lambda,\xi}^{\epsilon}(t,x):=1_{\mathcal{C}_{\xi}^{\epsilon}}(\hbar_{\lambda}(t,x))\hbar_{\lambda}(t,x).

Step 1: Estimate for
f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}.
At this moment, our primary focus is on handling the integration

\int_{\Delta}(1-\nabla^{m}f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}\cdot\xi)dxdt.

We start by transforming the integral into

(1-\nabla^{m}f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}\cdot\xi)=\nabla^{m}g_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}\cdot\xi+(1-\nabla^{m}\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi}\cdot\xi),

where
g_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}:=\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi}-f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}.

By (
6.1
), a simple calculation shows that

\displaystyle 1-\nabla^{m}\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi}\cdot\xi

\displaystyle=-\sum_{|\beta|=m}\sum_{l\leq\beta-1}C_{\beta}^{l}\partial^{\beta-l}\gamma_{\Delta}(t,x)\partial^{l}(\phi_{\Delta}(x)\cdot\overline{\xi})\xi_{\beta}\quad(\gamma_{\Delta}\equiv 1\;\mbox{on}\;\Delta)

\displaystyle+1-\gamma_{\Delta}(t,x)\nabla^{m}(\phi_{\Delta}(x)\cdot\overline{\xi})\cdot\xi=0\quad\mbox{when}\;(t,x)\in\Delta.

This yields

\int_{\Delta}(1-\nabla^{m}\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi}\cdot\xi)dxdt=0.

We now turn to the contribution of

L:=\int_{\Delta}\nabla^{m}g_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}\cdot\xi dxdt.

To the end, we choose a smooth function
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\to[0,1]
such that

\psi\equiv 1
on
\Delta_{\tau}:=(1-\tau^{2m})I\times(1-\tau)Q
with its support contained in
\Delta,
and

\|\partial^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(\tau l(\Delta))^{|\alpha|}},\quad\|\partial_{t}\psi\|_{{}_{L^{\infty}}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(\tau l(\Delta))^{2m}},\quad\forall\;|\alpha|\leq m,

where
\tau\in(0,1)
is to be determined later. Obviously,

L=\int_{\Delta}(1-\psi)\nabla^{m}g_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}\cdot\xi dxdt+\int_{\Delta}\psi\nabla^{m}g_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}\cdot\xi dxdt:=L_{1}+L_{2}.

By
(ii)
in Lemma
6.3
and (
2.2
) for
A_{2}-
weight
d\upsilon^{-1}(t,x):=w^{-1}(x)dxdt,
we obtain

\displaystyle|L_{1}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\Delta}|1-\psi|^{2}d\upsilon^{-1}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\Delta}|\nabla^{m}g_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}|^{2}d\upsilon\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\upsilon^{-1}(\Delta\setminus\Delta_{\tau})^{1/2}\upsilon(\Delta)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|I|(w^{-1}(\tau Q))^{1/2}(w(Q))^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|I|\tau^{\eta}(w^{-1}(Q))^{1/2}(w(Q))^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau^{\eta}[w]_{A_{2}}|\Delta|.

Since
{\rm supp}\psi\subset\Delta,
by integrating by parts, we derive

L_{2}=(-1)^{m}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}g_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}\nabla^{m}\psi\cdot\xi.

Using
(i)
in Lemma
6.3
and repeating the agument for
L_{1}
we conclude

\displaystyle|L_{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}|\nabla^{m}\psi|^{2}d\upsilon^{-1}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}}|g_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}|^{2}d\upsilon\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(\tau l(\Delta))^{m}}\upsilon^{-1}(\Delta\setminus\Delta_{\tau})^{1/2}(\epsilon l(\Delta))^{m}\upsilon(\Delta)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\epsilon^{m}}{\tau^{m}}\tau^{\eta}[w]_{A_{2}}|\Delta|\lesssim\frac{\epsilon^{m}}{\tau^{m}}[w]_{A_{2}}|\Delta|.

By choosing
\tau
such that
\tau^{\eta}=\frac{\epsilon^{m}}{\tau^{m}},
and summarizing the estimates for
L_{1}
and
L_{2},
we reach a conclusion that

(6.4)

\frac{1}{|\Delta|}\bigg|\int_{\Delta}(1-\nabla^{m}f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}\cdot\xi)\bigg|\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{m\eta}{m+\eta}}.

Furthermore, the conclusion
(iii)
in Lemma
6.3
along with Cauchy-Schwarz inequality contribute to

(6.5)

\frac{1}{|\Delta|}\int_{\Delta}|\nabla^{m}f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}|dxdt\lesssim\frac{1}{|\Delta|}\left(\int_{\Delta}|\nabla^{m}f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}|^{2}d\upsilon\right)^{1/2}\upsilon^{-1}(\Delta)^{1/2}\lesssim[w]_{A_{2}}.

Step 2: The choice of
\epsilon.
Armed with (
6.4
) and (
6.5
), and letting
\epsilon
sufficiently small, we conclude that

\frac{1}{|\Delta|}\int_{\Delta}\mbox{Re}\;\;(\nabla^{m}f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}\cdot\xi)dxdt\geq\frac{9}{10}

and

\frac{1}{|\Delta|}\int_{\Delta}|\nabla^{m}f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}|dxdt\leq C_{\infty}

hold for some large constant
C_{\infty},
depending only on the
\epsilon,n,m,c_{1},c_{2}
and
[w]_{A_{2}}
. Below, we implement a well-known stopping time argument rooted in
[
9
]
to select a collection
\mathcal{T}^{1}_{\xi}=\{\Delta^{\prime}\}
of non-overlapping dyadic subcubes of
\Delta,
which are maximal with respect to the property that either

(6.6)

\frac{1}{|\Delta^{\prime}|}\int_{\Delta^{\prime}}\mbox{Re}\;\;(\nabla^{m}f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}\cdot\xi)dxdt\leq\frac{4}{5},

or

(6.7)

\frac{1}{|\Delta^{\prime}|}\int_{\Delta^{\prime}}|\nabla^{m}f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}|dxdt\geq(5\epsilon)^{-1},

holds. Indeed, we subdivide dyadically
\Delta
and stop the first time either (
6.6
) or (
6.7
) holds. Then
\mathcal{T}^{1}_{\xi}=\{\Delta^{\prime}\}
is a disjoint set of prabolic subcubes of
\Delta.
Let
\mathcal{T}^{2}_{\xi}=\{\Delta^{\prime\prime}\}
be the collection of dyadic subcubes of
\Delta
not contained in any
\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}^{1}_{\xi}.
Clearly, for any
\Delta^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{T}^{2}_{\xi},

(6.8)

\frac{1}{|\Delta^{\prime\prime}|}\int_{\Delta^{\prime\prime}}\mbox{Re}\;\;(\nabla^{m}f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}\cdot\xi)dxdt\geq\frac{4}{5}

and

(6.9)

\frac{1}{|\Delta^{\prime\prime}|}\int_{\Delta^{\prime\prime}}|\nabla^{m}f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}|dxdt\leq(5\epsilon)^{-1}.

For simplicity, we let
B_{1}
be the union of the cubes in
\mathcal{T}^{1}_{\xi}
for which (
6.6
) holds, and
B_{2}
be the union of the cubes in
\mathcal{T}^{1}_{\xi}
for which (
6.7
) holds. Then

\bigg|\bigcup_{\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}^{1}_{\xi}}\Delta^{\prime}\bigg|\leq|B_{1}|+|B_{2}|.

The fact that the cubes in
\mathcal{T}^{1}_{\xi}
do not overlap yields

|B_{2}|\leq(5\epsilon)\int_{\Delta}|\nabla^{m}f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}|dxdt\leq C_{\infty}(5\epsilon)|\Delta|.

Set
b_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}:=1-\mbox{Re}\;\;(\nabla^{m}f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}\cdot\xi).
Then

|B_{1}|\leq 5\sum_{\Delta^{\prime}\in B_{1}}\int_{\Delta^{\prime}}b_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}=5\int_{\Delta}b_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}dxdt-5\int_{\Delta\setminus B_{1}}b_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}dxdt:=H_{1}+H_{2}.

It is easy to derive that

|H_{1}|\leq C_{0}\epsilon^{\frac{m\eta}{m+\eta}}|\Delta|

taking into account of (
6.4
). As for the contribution of
H_{2},
we have

\displaystyle|H_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq 5|\Delta\setminus B_{1}|+5\upsilon^{-1}(\Delta\setminus B_{1})^{1/2}\left(\int_{\Delta}|\nabla^{m}f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}|^{2}d\upsilon\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 5|\Delta\setminus B_{1}|+5C\upsilon^{-1}(\Delta\setminus B_{1})^{1/2}\upsilon(\Delta)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 5|\Delta\setminus B_{1}|+5C|\Delta\setminus B_{1}|^{\eta}|\Delta|^{1-\eta}

\displaystyle\leq 5(1+C_{1}\delta^{-\frac{1}{\eta}})|\Delta\setminus B_{1}|+C_{1}\delta^{1-\eta}|\Delta|,

where in the process we have used in succession the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality,
(iii)
in Lemma
6.3
, (
2.2
) and Young’s inequality. Summarizing the estimates of

and

we arrive at

|B_{1}|\leq\frac{5+C_{0}\epsilon^{\frac{m\eta}{m+\eta}}+C_{1}\delta^{1-\eta}+C_{1}\delta^{-\frac{1}{\eta}}}{6+C_{1}\delta^{-\frac{1}{\eta}}}|\Delta|.

From the latter inequality, by letting
\epsilon
small enough, it can be concluded that there exists a constant
\eta^{\prime}\in(0,1),
depending only on the
\epsilon,n,m,c_{1},c_{2}
and
[w]_{A_{2}}
, such that

(6.10)

\bigg|\bigcup_{\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}^{1}_{\xi}}\Delta^{\prime}\bigg|\leq(1-\eta^{\prime})|\Delta|.

Owing to (
2.2
) again (see also the argument in
[

), it follows instantly from (

) that

(6.11)

\upsilon(\bigcup_{\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}^{1}_{\xi}}\Delta^{\prime})\leq(1-\eta^{\prime\prime})\upsilon(\Delta),

where
\eta^{\prime\prime}\in(0,1)
depends only on the
\epsilon,n,m,c_{1},c_{2}
and
[w]_{A_{2}}
.

For any

If

and

Assume that

By (

) and (

), it is easy to see that

Revisiting the definition of the inner product on

and adapting the proof of
[

, we can prove that

|v|\leq 5|v\cdot\vartheta|.

The details are left to the interested reader. Thus,

(6.12)

|\Gamma_{\lambda,\xi}^{\epsilon}(t,x)|\leq 5|\hbar_{\lambda}(t,x)\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}\nabla^{m}f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}(t,x)|.

By Whitney decomposition, the Carleson region
\Delta\times(0,l(\Delta)]
can be partitioned as a union of boxes

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mu(\Delta)}\sum_{\Delta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}^{1}_{\xi}}\int_{0}^{l(\Delta^{\prime})}\int_{\Delta^{\prime}}|\Gamma_{\lambda,\xi}^{\epsilon}(t,x)|^{2}\frac{d\upsilon d\lambda}{\lambda}

\mathcal{F}^{\epsilon}_{\xi}:=\sup_{\tilde{\Delta}}\frac{1}{\upsilon(\tilde{\Delta})}\int_{0}^{l(\tilde{\Delta})}\int_{\tilde{\Delta}}|\Gamma_{\lambda,\xi}^{\epsilon}(t,x)|^{2}\frac{d\upsilon d\lambda}{\lambda}<\infty

Proof of Lemma
6.1
.
In veiw of Lemma
6.4
, it suffices to show that

\sup_{\Delta}\frac{1}{\upsilon(\Delta)}\int_{0}^{l(\Delta)}\int_{\Delta}|\hbar_{\lambda}(t,x)\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}\nabla^{m}f_{\tilde{\Delta},\epsilon}^{\xi}(t,x)|^{2}\frac{d\upsilon d\lambda}{\lambda}\lesssim 1

holds for any
\xi\in\mathcal{W}.
In fact, an application of the triangle inequality leads to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\upsilon(\Delta)}\int_{0}^{l(\Delta)}\int_{\Delta}|\hbar_{\lambda}(t,x)\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\nabla^{m}f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}(t,x)|^{2}\frac{d\upsilon d\lambda}{\lambda}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\upsilon(\Delta)}|||(\lambda^{m}\mathcal{H}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}-\hbar_{\lambda}\cdot\mathcal{A}_{\lambda}\nabla^{m})f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}|||_{2,\upsilon}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\upsilon(\Delta)}\int_{0}^{l(\Delta)}\int_{\Delta}|\lambda^{m}\mathcal{H}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}(t,x)|^{2}\frac{d\upsilon d\lambda}{\lambda}

\displaystyle:=D_{1}+D_{2}.

Combining (
1.7
) and Lemma

we conclude

D_{1}\lesssim\frac{1}{\upsilon(\Delta)}(\|\nabla^{m}f\|_{2,\upsilon}+\|D_{t}^{1/2}f\|_{2,\upsilon})\lesssim 1.

By the trivial equality

\mathcal{H}f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}=\frac{\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi}-f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}}{(\epsilon l(\Delta))^{2m}},

Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
6.3
, we finally arrive at

\displaystyle D_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\upsilon(\Delta)}\int_{0}^{l(\Delta)}\lambda^{2m}(\epsilon l(\Delta))^{-4m}\|\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi}-f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2}\frac{d\upsilon d\lambda}{\lambda}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\upsilon(\Delta)}\|\digamma_{\Delta}^{\xi}-f_{\Delta,\epsilon}^{\xi}\|_{2,\upsilon}^{2}(\epsilon l(\Delta))^{-4m}l(\Delta)^{2m}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{-2m}.

The proof of (
6.14
) is therefore complete.

Then for each

The following lemma is an analogue of
[
3
, Lemma 3.3]
, which plays a part in building the off-diagonal estimates for the resolvent operators.

Lemma 7.5
.

The following statements are true:

\displaystyle(i)\quad\mbox{The space}\;C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})\;\mbox{is dense in }\;\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}.

\displaystyle(ii)\quad\mbox{Multiplication by}\;C^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})\mbox{-functions is bounded on}\;\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}.

Proof.
The proof is a straightforward adaptation of the one in
[
3
, Lemma 3.3]
. Given
f\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}.
Without loss of generality, we can assume that
f
is smooth since convolutions with smooth mollifiers, separately in time and space, provide smooth approximations in
\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}.
Thus, it remains to show that
f
can be approximated by functions in
C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
in order to conclude
(i).

For this purpose, we choose a sequence
\{\psi_{j}\}\subset C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1})
such that

\|\psi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}+j^{|\alpha|}\|\partial^{\alpha}\psi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}+j^{2m}\|\partial_{t}\psi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim 1\quad(\forall\;|\alpha|\leq m)

and
\psi_{j}\to 1
pointwise a.e. as
j\to\infty.
Set
f_{j}:=\psi_{j}f.
Obviously, by Leibniz’s rule and dominated convergence, we can deduce

\|\partial^{\alpha}(f_{j})-\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{2,\upsilon}\leq\|\partial^{\alpha}f-\psi_{j}\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{2,\upsilon}+\sum_{1\leq|\gamma|\leq|\alpha|}C_{\alpha}^{\gamma}\|\partial^{\gamma}\psi_{j}\partial^{\alpha-\gamma}f\|_{2,\upsilon}\to 0

as
j\to\infty
for any
|\alpha|\leq m.
On the other hand, repeating the argument in
[
3
, (3.3)]
, we can derive

\displaystyle\frac{|(f-f_{j})(t,x)-(f-f_{j})(s,x)|^{2}}{|t-s|^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|f(t,x)|^{2}\frac{|\psi_{j}(t,x)-\psi_{j}(s,x)|^{2}}{|t-s|^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{|f(t,x)-f(s,x)|^{2}}{|t-s|^{2}}|\psi_{j}(s,x)-1|

\displaystyle\lesssim\min\{\frac{1}{j^{4m}},\frac{1}{|t-s|^{2}}\}|f(t,x)|^{2}+\frac{|f(t,x)-f(s,x)|^{2}}{|t-s|^{2}}

Recalling the formular (
2.8
) and the fact
f\in\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon},
also using the dominated convergence theorem, we get
\|D_{t}^{1/2}(f_{j}-f)\|_{2,\upsilon}\to 0
as
j\to\infty.
This completes the proof of
(i).

In a same fashion, we can conclude, for any
\psi\in C^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}),
that

\|\psi f\|_{2,\upsilon}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|f\|_{2,\upsilon},

\|\partial^{\alpha}(\psi f)\|_{2,\upsilon}\lesssim\sum_{|\gamma|\leq|\alpha|}C_{\alpha}^{\gamma}\|\partial^{\gamma}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|f\|_{\mathbf{E}_{\upsilon}}\quad(\forall\;|\alpha|\leq m),

and

\|D_{t}^{1/2}(\psi f)\|_{2,\upsilon}\lesssim(1+\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}})^{1/2}\|\partial_{t}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1/2}\|f\|_{2,\upsilon}+(1+\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}})\|D_{t}^{1/2}f\|_{2,\upsilon}.

This implies
(ii).

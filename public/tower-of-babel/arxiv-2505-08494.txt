Universal enveloping H H -pseudoalgebras of DGP pseudoalgebras
Ying Chen, Jiafeng Lü, Jiaqun Wei

Abstract
. The notions of Poisson H H -pseudoalgebras are generalizations of Poisson algebras in a pseudotensor category ℳ ∗ ​ ( H ) \mathcal{M}^{\ast}(H) . This paper introduces an analogue of Poisson-Ore extension in Poisson H H -pseudoalgebras. Poisson H H -pseudoalgebras with the differential graded setting induces the notions of differential graded Poisson H H -pseudoalgebras (DGP pseudoalgebras, for short). The DGP pseudoalgebra with some compatibility conditions is proved to be closed under tensor product. Furthermore, the universal enveloping H H -pseudoalgebras of DGP pseudoalgebras are constructed by a 𝒫 \mathcal{P} -triple. A unique differential graded pseudoalgebra homomorphism between a universal enveloping H H -pseudoalgebra of a DGP pseudoalgebra and a 𝒫 \mathcal{P} -triple of a DGP pseudoalgebra is obtained.

1.1.
Poisson-Ore extension

In 1933, Ore
[
11
]
introduced non-commutative polynomials rings, which are known as Ore extensions nowadays. Ore extensions have remarkable applications in various algebraic structures, including Hopf algebras
[
12
]
, quantized Weyl algebras
[
3
]
, quantum matrix algebras
[
15
]
.
Oh
[
10
]
first proposed the Poisson polynomial extensions as a Poisson version of Ore extensions.

Definition 1.1
.

(
[
10
, Section 1]
)
Let
(A,\cdot,\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{A})
be a Poisson algebra and
\alpha:~A\rightarrow A
is a linear map, for all
a,b\in A
, if
\alpha
satisfies

(i)

\alpha(a\cdot b)=\alpha(a)\cdot b+a\cdot\alpha(b);

(ii)

\alpha(\{a,b\}_{A})=\{\alpha(a),b\}_{A}+\{a,\alpha(b)\}_{A},

then
\alpha
is called a
Poisson derivation
of
(A,\cdot,\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{A})
.
If
\alpha
only satisfies (i), then
\alpha
is called a
derivation
of
(A,\cdot,\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{A})
.

Definition 1.2
.

(
[
10
, Section 1]
)

Let
(A,\cdot,\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{A})
be a Poisson algebra and
\alpha
is a Poisson derivation of
(A,\cdot,\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{A})
,
for all
a,b\in A
, if a linear map
\delta:~A\rightarrow A
satisfies

(i)

\delta(a\cdot b)=\delta(a)\cdot b+a\cdot\delta(b);

(ii)

\delta(\{a,b\}_{A})=\{\delta(a),b\}_{A}+\{a,\delta(b)\}_{A}+\delta(a)\cdot\alpha(b)-\alpha(a)\cdot\delta(b),

then
\delta
is called a
Poisson
\alpha
-derivation
of
(A,\cdot,\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{A})
.

Theorem 1.3
.

(
[
10
, Section 1]
)
Let

be a Poisson algebra and
\alpha,\delta:~A\rightarrow A
are two linear maps. Then the polynomial ring

is a Poisson algebra with Poisson bracket

for all

is a Poisson derivation of
(A,\cdot,\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{A})
and
\delta
is a Poisson
\alpha
-derivation of

The Poisson algebra

In
[
8
,
7
]
, the authors introduced the notions of universal enveloping algebras of (double) Poisson-Ore extensions and proved that the universal enveloping algebra of a (double) Poisson-Ore extension is an iterated (double) Ore extension.
In
[

[

1.2.
differential graded Poisson algebras

The Poisson algebras originated from the study of Hamiltonian mechanics by Poisson. In both commutative and non-commutative algebraic contexts, the Poisson algebras have many important generalizations, such as noncommutative Poisson algebras
[
17
]
, Novikov-Poisson algebras
[
18
]
and quiver Poisson algebras
[
19
]
.
In 2016, Lü, Wang and Zhuang
[
9
]
defined differential graded Poisson algebras as a generalization of Poisson algebras in the differential graded setting.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
A=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{i}
be a graded vector space. A
graded algebra
is a pair
(A,\cdot)
, where
\cdot:~A\otimes A\rightarrow A
is a
\mathbf{k}
-linear map satisfying
\mathbf{k}\subseteq A_{0}
and
A_{i}\cdot A_{j}\subseteq A_{i+j}
, for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Furthermore, if there is a
\mathbf{k}
-linear homogeneous map
d:~A\rightarrow A
of degree 1 such
that
d^{2}=0
and for all homogeneous elements
a,b\in A
,

(2)

d(a\cdot b)=d(a)\cdot b+(-1)^{|a|}a\cdot d(b),

then
(A,\cdot,d)
is called a
differential graded algebra
.

Moreover, for all homogeneous elements
a,b\in A
, if
a\cdot b=(-1)^{|a||b|}b\cdot a
, where
|a|
and
|b|
denote the degrees of
a
and
b
respectively, then graded algebra
(A,\cdot)
and differential graded algebra
(A,\cdot,d)
are called
graded commutative
.

Definition 1.5
.

(
[
9
, Definition 3.1]
)
Let
(A,\cdot)
be a graded commutative graded algebra. A
graded Poisson algebra
is a triple
(A,\cdot,\{\cdot,\cdot\})
, where
\{\cdot,\cdot\}:~A\otimes A\rightarrow A
is a
\mathbf{k}
-linear map of degree
p
, for all homogeneous elements
a,b,c\in A
, such that

(i)

(A,\{\cdot,\cdot\})
is a graded Lie algebra, i.e., the bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
satisfies

\{a,b\}=-(-1)^{(|a|+p)(|b|+p)}\{b,a\}
,

\{a,\{b,c\}\}=\{\{a,b\},c\}+(-1)^{(|a|+p)(|b|+p)}\{b,\{a,c\}\}
;

(ii)

(biderivation property):
\{a,b\cdot c\}=\{a,b\}\cdot c+(-1)^{(|a|+p)|b|}b\cdot\{a,c\}
.

In addition, if there is a
\mathbf{k}
-linear homogeneous map

of degree 1 such
that
(A,\cdot,d)
is a differential graded algebra,

(iii)

In
[
9
]
, the authors constructed some examples of differential graded Poisson algebras from Lie theory, differential geometry, homological algebra and deformation theory. They also introduced the universal enveloping algebras of differential graded Poisson algebras.
In
[
5
]
, the authors showed that a differential graded symplectic ideal of a differential graded Poisson algebra is the annihilator of a simple differential graded Poisson module.

Example 3.2

Let

\displaystyle h(fg)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(h_{(1)}f)(h_{(2)}g),

\displaystyle(f\otimes gh)\otimes_{H}(1\otimes a)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(f\otimes g_{(1)})\otimes_{H}g_{(-2)}h(1\otimes a)

\displaystyle fg\otimes_{H}(a\cdot b),

\displaystyle\big((f\otimes g)\otimes_{H}(1\otimes a)\big)\bullet(h\otimes_{H}b)

Proposition 3.6

Let
(L,\cdot,\{\ast\})

-pseudoalgebra and

\displaystyle(x\otimes_{H}a)\bullet(y\otimes_{H}b)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle xy\otimes_{H}(a\cdot b),

\displaystyle\big((xf)(yg)\otimes_{H}a\big)\bullet(z\otimes_{H}c)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(xf)((zy)g_{(1)})\otimes_{H}a\cdot(g_{(-2)}c),

\displaystyle\{x\otimes_{H}a,(y\otimes_{H}b)\bullet(z\otimes_{H}c)\}=\{x\otimes_{H}a,yz\otimes_{H}(b\cdot c)\}

4.

We consider differential graded Poisson algebras and their universal enveloping algebras defined in

\sim
4]
in a pseudotensor category
\mathcal{M}^{\ast}(H)
, inducing the notions of differential graded Poisson

-pseudoalgebras and their universal enveloping

-pseudoalgebras.
We consider the Poisson pseudobracket of degree
p
.

4.1.
DGP pseudoalgebras

Definition 4.1
.

Let
A=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{i}
be a graded left
H
-module,
\ast:~A\otimes A\to(H\otimes H)\otimes_{H}A
be an
H
-bilinear map and
d:~A\rightarrow A
be an
H
-linear map of degree
1
satisfying

(1)

(differential)
d^{2}=0
; and

(2)

\big((\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id})\otimes_{H}d\big)(a\ast b)=d(a)\ast b+(-1)^{|a|}a\ast d(b)
, for all homogeneous elements
a,b\in A
,

then
(A,\ast,d)
is called a
differential graded
H
-pseudoalgebra
(or DG pseudoalgebra).
Moreover, if
a\ast b=(-1)^{|a||b|}(\sigma\otimes_{H}\mathrm{id})(b\ast a)
, for all homogeneous elements
a,b\in A
, then DG pseudoalgebra
(A,\ast,d)
is called
graded commutative
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
A=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}A_{i}
be a graded left
H
-module,
\{\ast\}:~A\otimes A\to(H\otimes H)\otimes_{H}A
be an
H
-bilinear map of degree
p
and
d:~A\to A
be an
H
-linear map of degree
1
(
d^{2}=0
) such that

(1)

(skew-symmetry)
\{a\ast b\}=-(-1)^{(|a|+p)(|b|+p)}(\sigma\otimes_{H}\mathrm{id})\{b\ast a\}
;

(2)

(Jacobi identity)
\{a\ast\{b\ast c\}\}-(-1)^{(|a|+p)(|b|+p)}\big((\sigma\otimes\mathrm{id})\otimes_{H}\mathrm{id}\big)\{b\ast\{a\ast c\}\}=\{\{a\ast b\}\ast c\}
; and

(3)

\big((\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id})\otimes_{H}d\big)\{a\ast b\}=\{d(a)\ast b\}+(-1)^{(|a|+p)}\{a\ast d(b)\}
, for all homogeneous elements
a,b,c\in A
,

then
(A,\{\ast\},d)
is called a
differential graded Lie
H
-pseudoalgebra
(or DGL pseudoalgebra).

Definition 4.3
.

Let
(A,\cdot,d)
be a differential graded algebra and
(A,\{\ast\},d)
be a DGL pseudoalgebra satisfying
H
-differential (
8
) and
graded left Leibniz rule

(23)

\{a\ast(b\cdot c)\}=\{a\ast b\}\cdot c+(-1)^{(|c|+p)|b|}\{a\ast c\}\cdot b,

for all homogeneous elements
a,b,c\in A
, then
(A,\cdot,\{\ast\},d)
is called a
differential graded Poisson
H
-pseudoalgebra
(or DGP pseudoalgebra).
Moreover, if
(A,\cdot,d)
is graded commutative, then
(A,\cdot,\{\ast\},d)
is a
graded commutative DGP pseudoalgebra
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\{\ast\},d)
be a DGP pseudoalgebra, for all homogeneous elements
a,b,c\in A
, similar to right Leibniz rule (
11
) in Lemma
2.5
, then we have the
graded right Leibniz rule

(24)

\{(a\cdot b)\ast c\}=a\cdot\{b\ast c\}+(-1)^{(|a|+p)|b|}b\cdot\{a\ast c\}.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\{\ast\},d)
be a DGP pseudoalgebra, and
(B,\cdot)
is a subalgebra of
(A,\cdot)
.

(1)

If
d(B)\subseteq B
and
\{B\ast B\}\subseteq(H\otimes H)\otimes_{H}B
, then
(B,\cdot,\{\ast\},d)
is called a DGP

pseudosubalgebra
of

, denoted by

.

(2)

If

and

, then
(B,\cdot,\{\ast\},d)
is called a DGP
pseudoideal
of
(A,\cdot,\{\ast\},d)
, denoted by
B\lhd A
.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
(A,\cdot_{A},\{\ast\}_{A},d_{A})
and
(B,\cdot_{B},\{\ast\}_{B},d_{B})
be two DGP pseudoalgebras. An
H
-linear map

if
f(a\cdot_{A}b)=f(a)\cdot_{B}f(b)
,
\big((\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id})\otimes_{H}f\big)\{a\ast b\}_{A}=\{f(a)\ast f(b)\}_{B}
and

for all homogeneous elements

. Moreover,

monomorphism
if
\mathrm{Ker}f=0
, a DGP pseudoalgebra

to denote the isomorphism between

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\{\ast\},d)
be a DGP pseudoalgebra, then
(A^{op},\cdot_{op},\{\ast\}_{op},d_{op})
is a DGP pseudoalgebra, where for all homogeneous elements
a,b\in A
,

(25)

\displaystyle a\cdot_{op}b

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle a\cdot b,

(26)

\displaystyle d_{op}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle d,

\displaystyle\{a\ast b\}_{op}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-\{a\ast b\}.

Proof.

By (
25

26

(A^{op},\cdot_{op},d_{op})
is a differential graded algebra and satisfies
H
-differential.

Observe that:

\{a\ast b\}_{op}=-\{a\ast b\}=(-1)^{(|a|+p)(|b|+p)}(\sigma\otimes_{H}\mathrm{id})\{b\ast a\}=-(-1)^{(|a|+p)(|b|+p)}(\sigma\otimes_{H}\mathrm{id})\{b\ast a\}_{op},

\displaystyle\{a\ast\{b\ast c\}_{op}\}_{op}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{a\ast\{b\ast c\}\}=\{\{a\ast b\}\ast c\}+(-1)^{(|a|+p)(|b|+p)}\big((\sigma\otimes\mathrm{id})\otimes_{H}\mathrm{id}\big)\{b\ast\{a\ast c\}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\{a\ast b\}_{op}\ast c\}_{op}+(-1)^{(|a|+p)(|b|+p)}\big((\sigma\otimes\mathrm{id}\big)\otimes_{H}\mathrm{id})\{b\ast\{a\ast c\}_{op}\}_{op},

\displaystyle\big((\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id})\otimes_{H}d_{op}\big)\{a\ast b\}_{op}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\big((\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id})\otimes_{H}d\big)\{a\ast b\}=-\{d(a)\ast b\}-(-1)^{|a|+p}\{a\ast d(b)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{d_{op}(a)\ast b\}_{op}+(-1)^{|a|+p}\{a\ast d_{op}(b)\}_{op},

hence

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\{a\ast(b\cdot_{op}c)\}_{op}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\{a\ast(b\cdot c)\}=-\{a\ast b\}\cdot c-(-1)^{(|c|+p)|b|}\{a\ast c\}\cdot b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{a\ast b\}_{op}\cdot_{op}c+(-1)^{(|c|+p)|b|}\{a\ast c\}_{op}\cdot_{op}b.

∎

Proposition 4.8
.

\displaystyle-(-1)^{(|a|+|b|)(|a^{\prime}|+|b^{\prime}|)}(\sigma\otimes_{H}\mathrm{id})\{(a^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime})\ast(a\otimes b)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|a^{\prime}||b|}\big((u_{i}\otimes v_{i})\otimes_{H}(\alpha_{i}\otimes(b\cdot_{B}b^{\prime}))+(u_{j}\otimes v_{j})\otimes_{H}((a\cdot_{A}a^{\prime})\otimes\beta_{j})\big)

\displaystyle\{(a\otimes b)\ast(a^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime})\},

for all homogeneous elements

and

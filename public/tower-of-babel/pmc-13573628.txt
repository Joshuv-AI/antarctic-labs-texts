Safety and efficacy of the feed additives consisting of l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% and l‐isoleucine ≥ 65% produced with Corynebacterium glutamicum KCCM 80388 for all animal species (CJ Europe GmbH)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Roberto Edoardo Villa, Giovanna Azimonti, Eleftherios Bonos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Ronette Gehring, Boet Glandorf, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Francesca Marcon, Giovanna Martelli, Carlo Nebbia, Alena Pechová, Miguel Prieto‐Maradona, Katerina Theodoridou, Baltasar Mayo, Ruud Woutersen, Montserrat Anguita, Matteo L Innocenti, Joana Revez, Jordi Tarrés‐Call, Elisa Pettenati, Joana P Firmino
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, EFSA was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the safety and efficacy of l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% and l‐isoleucine ≥ 65% produced with a genetically modified strain of Corynebacterium glutamicum (KCCM 80388), as nutritional and sensory additives for use in feed and water for drinking, for all animal species and categories. l‐Isoleucine ≥ 90% and l‐isoleucine ≥ 65% manufactured by fermentation with C. glutamicum KCCM 80388 do not give rise to any safety concern with regard to the genetic modification of the production strain. No viable cells nor DNA of the production strain were detected in the final products. The use of l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% and ≥ 65% produced with C. glutamicum KCCM 80388 in feed was considered safe for the target species when supplemented in appropriate amounts to the diet according to the nutritional needs of the species. The FEEDAP Panel had concerns on the use of l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% and ≥ 65% in water for drinking. The use of l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% and ≥ 65% produced by fermentation with C. glutamicum KCCM 80388 in animal nutrition was considered safe for the consumers of products from animals receiving the additives, and for the environment. Regarding user safety, the additives were considered not irritant to skin and are not considered skin sensitisers. l‐Isoleucine ≥ 90% was not irritant to eyes. No conclusions could be reached on the eye irritation potential of l‐isoleucine ≥ 65%. The additives were regarded as an effective source of the amino acid l‐isoleucine for all non‐ruminant species. To be as efficacious in ruminants as in non‐ruminants, it should be protected from ruminal degradation.Keywords: amino acids, Corynebacterium glutamicum KCCM 80388, efficacy, flavouring compounds, l‐isoleucine, nutritional additives, safety

INTRODUCTION

Background and Terms of Reference

Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003 1 establishes the rules governing the Community authorisation of additives for use in animal nutrition. In particular, Article 4(1) of that Regulation lays down that any person seeking authorisation for a feed additive or for a new use of feed additive shall submit an application in accordance with Article 7.

The European Commission received a request from CJ Europe GmbH 2 for the authorisation of the additives consisting of l‐isoleucine ≥90% and l‐isoleucine ≥65% produced with Corynebacterium glutamicum KCCM 80388, when used as feed additives for all animal species (category: nutritional additives; functional group: amino acids, their salts and analogues; and category: sensory additives; functional group: flavouring compounds).

According to Article 7(1) of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, the Commission forwarded the application to the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) as an application under Article 4(1) (authorisation of a feed additive or new use of a feed additive). The dossier was received on 14 February 2024 and the general information and supporting documentation are available at https://open.efsa.europa.eu/questions/EFSA‐Q‐2024‐00091. The particulars and documents in support of the application were considered valid by EFSA as of 10 June 2024.

According to Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, EFSA, after verifying the particulars and documents submitted by the applicant, shall undertake an assessment in order to determine whether the feed additive complies with the conditions laid down in Article 5. EFSA shall deliver an opinion on the safety for the target animals, consumer, user and the environment, and on the efficacy of the feed additives consisting of l‐isoleucine ≥90% and l‐isoleucine ≥65% produced with C. glutamicum KCCM 80388, when used under the proposed conditions of use (see Section 3.1.4).

Additional information

The additives l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% and l‐isoleucine ≥ 65% produced with C. glutamicum KCCM80388 have not been previously authorised as feed additives in the European Union.

l‐Isoleucine produced by fermentation using different production strains is currently authorised for its use in all animal species as a nutritional and sensory additive. 3

The EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP) issued a series of scientific opinions on the safety and efficacy of l‐isoleucine produced by fermentation using different production strains, when used as a nutritional additive and as sensory additive in feed for all animal species. 4

DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

Data

The present assessment is based on data submitted by the applicant in the form of a technical dossier 5 in support of the authorisation request for the use of l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% and l‐isoleucine ≥ 65% produced with C. glutamicum KCCM 80388 as feed additives.

In accordance with Article 38 of the Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 6 and taking into account the protection of confidential information and of personal data in accordance with Articles 39 to 39e of the same Regulation, and of the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down practical arrangements concerning transparency and confidentiality, 7 a non‐confidential version of the dossier has been published on Open.EFSA.

According to Article 32c(2) of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 and to the Decision of EFSA's Executive Director laying down the practical arrangements on pre‐submission phase and public consultations, EFSA carried out a public consultation on the non‐confidential version of the technical dossier from 22 January to 12 February 2025 for which no comments were received.

The confidential version of the technical dossier was subject to a target consultation of the interested Member States from 13 June to 13 September 2024 for which the received comments were considered for the assessment.

The FEEDAP Panel used the data provided by the applicant together with data from other sources, such as previous risk assessments by EFSA or other expert bodies, peer‐reviewed scientific papers, other scientific reports and experts' knowledge, to deliver the present output.

EFSA has verified the European Union Reference Laboratory (EURL) report as it relates to the methods used for the control of l‐isoleucine in animal feed. 8

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety and the efficacy of l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% and l‐isoleucine ≥ 65% produced with C. glutamicum KCCM 80388 is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 9 and the relevant guidance documents: Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the consumer (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017a), Guidance on the identity, characterisation and conditions of use of feed additives (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017b), Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the target species (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017c), Guidance on the characterisation of microorganisms used as feed additives or as production organisms (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2018), Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the environment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2019), Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the users (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2023), Guidance on the assessment of the efficacy of feed additives (2024), EFSA statement on the requirements for whole genome sequence analysis of microorganisms intentionally used in the food chain (EFSA, 2024).

Conditions of use

l‐Isoleucine ≥ 90% and l‐isoleucine ≥ 65% are intended to be used in feed for all animal species, directly or through complementary feed or premixtures, and water for drinking, without maximum or minimum levels and without withdrawal period. No inclusion levels have been proposed as nutritional additives, as l‐isoleucine requirements depend on the species, the age of the animal, the physiological state of the animal, the performance level and the environmental conditions, and the nutrient composition of the diet, particularly its amino acid profile.

When used as flavouring compounds, l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% and l‐isoleucine ≥ 65% are proposed to be used at a maximum recommended level of inclusion of 25 mg/kg feed. 27

Safety

Safety of the production microorganism

The parental strain is considered to be safe. The genetic modifications performed to obtain the production strain C. glutamicum KCCM 80388 have the purpose to increase the production of l‐isoleucine. The production strain belongs to a species, C. glutamicum, that is included in the qualified presumption of safety (QPS) list when used for production purposes (EFSA BIOHAZ Panel, 2026). The taxonomic identification of the production strain was unequivocally established, it does not carry acquired AMR genes and the genetic modifications do not raise safety concerns. No viable cells nor DNA of the production strain were detected in the final products. Therefore, the FEEDAP Panel concludes that the additives do not pose any safety concern regarding the genetically modified production strain.

Safety for the target species, consumers and the environment

The l‐isoleucine requirements of the target animal species and the safety of this essential amino acid in non‐ruminant and ruminant nutrition are well known by feed formulators and available in general publications on animal nutrition.

Concerns on the use of the additives would not derive from the l‐isoleucine, which is considered safe but may arise from residues of the fermentation process/production strain remaining in the final products. The additives l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% and l‐isoleucine ≥ 65% are produced by fermentation with the genetically modified C. glutamicum KCCM 80388, and no safety concerns were identified for the production strain (see Section 3.2.1), the fermentation process and its residues/metabolites. In addition, the impurities present, including those from manufacturing, are considered of no concern (see Appendices A and C). The additives are safe for the target species when used to supplement the diet in appropriate amounts to satisfy the animal requirements. However, due to the risk of nutritional imbalances and hygienic reasons associated with the use of amino acids via water for drinking (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2010), the FEEDAP Panel has concerns on the safety of the use of l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% and l‐isoleucine ≥ 65% via water for drinking.

Since the levels proposed for the use of the additives as flavouring compounds (< 25 mg/kg complete feed) are substantially lower than the animal requirements, the FEEDAP Panel considers l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% and ≥ 65% produced by fermentation with C. glutamicum KCCM 80388 safe for the target species when used as flavouring compounds.

The absorption and metabolic fate of l‐isoleucine in the organism are well known. The amino acid l‐isoleucine, supplemented to feed, will be incorporated into proteins of tissues and/or products of animal origin and any of its potential excess will be metabolised and excreted. Therefore, the protein composition of tissues and products of animal origin will not be affected using l‐isoleucine in animal nutrition. Therefore, the Panel considers that the use of the additives in animal nutrition is safe for the consumer.

The amino acid l‐isoleucine is a physiological and natural component of animals and plants. It is not excreted as such, but as urea/uric acid and carbon dioxide. The use of the products l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% and ≥ 65% in animal nutrition would not lead to any localised increase in the concentration in the environment. The use of the additives in water for drinking, when given in addition to complete diets with a well‐balanced amino acid profile, would disturb the nitrogen balance and increase nitrogen excretion via urine. The Panel concludes that the use of the products l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% and ≥ 65% does not represent a risk to the environment.

Safety for the user

The skin irritation potential of l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% and l‐isoleucine ≥ 65% was investigated in studies performed according to the OECD TG 439, which showed that the additives are not skin irritants (UN GHS No Category). 28

The eye irritation potential of l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% and l‐isoleucine ≥ 65% was tested in studies performed according to OECD TG 437. 29 Based on the results, l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% did not induce ocular irritation (IVIS ≤ 3), while l‐isoleucine ≥ 65% did not induce serious eye damage equivalent to UN GHS ‘Category 1’ but induced slight ocular irritation (IVIS > 3 ≤ 55). The applicant did not provide additional information on the eye irritation potential of l‐isoleucine ≥ 65%. Therefore, l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% is considered non‐irritant to eyes (UN‐GHS No Category), whereas for l‐isoleucine ≥ 65%, no stand‐alone prediction can be made.

The skin sensitisation potential of l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% and l‐isoleucine ≥ 65% was tested in a study performed according to OECD TG 429, 30 which showed that the additives are not skin sensitisers (UN GHS No Category).

Conclusions on the safety for the user

Based on the information available, the additives l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% and ≥ 65% are not irritant to skin and are not skin sensitisers. l‐Isoleucine ≥ 90% is not irritant to eyes. No conclusions can be reached on the eye irritation potential of l‐isoleucine ≥ 65%.

Efficacy

Efficacy studies are not required for amino acids that occur naturally in plant and animal proteins. The nutritional role of the amino acid l‐isoleucine is well established in the scientific literature. The additives l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% and ≥ 65% produced by fermentation using C. glutamicum KCCM 80388 are regarded as an efficacious source of the essential amino acid l‐isoleucine for non‐ruminant nutrition. For the supplemental l‐isoleucine to be as efficacious in ruminants as in non‐ruminant species, it would require protection against degradation in the rumen.

As l‐isoleucine is used in food as flavouring compound, it is expected that the additives can provide a similar function in feed and no further demonstration of efficacy is necessary.

Post‐market monitoring

The FEEDAP Panel considers that there is no need for specific requirements for a post‐market monitoring plan other than those established in the Feed Hygiene Regulation 31 and good manufacturing practice.

CONCLUSIONS

The production strain C. glutamicum KCCM 80388 does not raise safety concerns regarding the genetic modifications. No viable cells nor DNA of the production strain are detected in the final products. Therefore, the FEEDAP Panel concludes that the additives do not pose any safety concern regarding the production strain.

The use of l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% and ≥ 65% produced by fermentation with C. glutamicum KCCM 80388 is safe for the target species when supplemented in appropriate amounts to the diet according to the nutritional needs of the target species, which would cover their use as flavouring additives. The FEEDAP Panel has concerns on the use of the additives in water for drinking.

The use of l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% and ≥ 65% produced with C. glutamicum KCCM 80388 in animal nutrition is considered safe for the consumers and for the environment.

Regarding user safety, l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% and ≥ 65% produced with C. glutamicum KCCM 80388 are not irritant to skin and are not skin sensitisers. l‐Isoleucine ≥ 90% is not irritant to eyes. No conclusions can be reached on the eye irritation potential of l‐isoleucine ≥ 65%.

The feed additives consisting of l‐isoleucine ≥ 90% and l‐isoleucine ≥ 65% produced with C. glutamicum KCCM 80388 are regarded as an effective source of the amino acid l‐isoleucine for all non‐ruminant species. In order to be as efficacious in ruminants as in non‐ruminants, they should be protected from ruminal degradation. l‐Isoleucine is used in food as flavouring compound; it is expected that the additives can provide a similar function in feed and no further demonstration of efficacy is necessary.

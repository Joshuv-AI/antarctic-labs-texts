Water stresses alter the growth, yield and quality of broccoli (Brassica Oleracea var. italica)
Authors: Ayasha Akter, Shapla Akhter, Md. Mokter Hossain
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Drought stress can significantly reduce plant growth by affecting various physiological processes. Water scarcity, influenced by both drought and climate change, disrupts cellular functions, decreases photosynthesis, and ultimately hampers overall plant development. Water stress is a major abiotic factor that impacts the growth, yield, and quality of broccoli. This cool-season crop is sensitive to water shortages, especially during critical growth stages. Therefore, a pot experiment was conducted to evaluate the effects of different water stresses on the growth, yield, and quality of four broccoli varieties. The experiment included two factors: four water levels-100% field capacity (FC), 75% FC, 60% FC, and 45% FC, and four broccoli varieties BARI Broccoli-1, Green Crown, Early You, and Early Green. Among the treatments, 100% FC produced the highest number of leaves (26.08), plant height (53.41 cm), curd fresh weight (310.19 g), and yield (15.50 t/ha). Water-stressed plants showed reduced growth, yield, and leaf chlorophyll content but increased levels of ascorbic acid, proline, and dry matter. The 45% FC treatment resulted in the lowest plant height (42.41 cm), number of leaves (16.33), curd fresh weight (186.22 g), and yield (9.31 t/ha). Early Green exhibited the highest yield under both 45% FC and 100% FC conditions among all varieties. Overall, Early Green demonstrates potential for sustainable broccoli production under water deficit and changing climate conditions, by enhancing yield and adaptability.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-23905-z.

Introduction

Broccoli (Brassica oleracea var. italica), a Cruciferae vegetable related to cabbage and cauliflower, is valued for its edible flower buds1,2. Broccoli has excellent nutritional value and moderate commercial value3. Nowadays, it is one of the most popular and highly valuable vegetables for the grower and consumer due to its rich in different nutrients and minerals content which contribute to human health. Sulforaphane is one of the major substances contained in sprouts which intensely reduces the different types of diseases, especially combat with cancerous cells. Broccoli contains many important bioactive components, such as antioxidants, flavonoids, vitamins, carotenoids, and minerals. Particularly valuable for the skin are polyphenols and carotenoids, such as β-carotene, lutein, and lycopene, and it is rich in bioactive compounds4. Broccoli originated in western Europe, has now been distributed in both the sub-tropical and tropical areas like Bangladesh. Broccoli is considered a cool-season crop, and, like many crops, is susceptible to various abiotic stresses that can reduce yield and productivity5.

Generally, broccoli is cultivated in the spring or Autumn, but it is very sensitive to cold or dry weather conditions. Abiotic stress is one of the major threats to agricultural crop production throughout the world. Among all abiotic stresses, drought is a major environmental stress that limits photosynthesis and yield. It can result from low rainfall, salinity, temperature extremes, intense light, or limited irrigation. Drought affects plants at the physiological, morphological, biochemical, and molecular levels. It’s disrupting water and mineral uptake, as well as assimilate distribution, ultimately reducing yield6. Like the other crops, it also increases reactive oxygen species in Brassica7, decreases photosynthesis in Brassica napus8, and reduces yields in Brassica crops9. Cauliflower also exhibits reduced germination, shoot and root growth, curd development, and dry mass under drought conditions. Overall, drought has reduced global crop yields by 10%10. Therefore, it’s really important to find out the proper and economic methods by using minimum use of water and maximizing production, and reducing the wastage. Ultimately, this helps us accelerate in achieving the SDGs goal.

Irrigation is crucial for broccoli production, as it directly impacts yield quality and quantity, which in turn affects farmers’ incomes and market supply11,12. Proper irrigation management helps optimize water use, reduce costs, and maximize profitability by preventing water stress and enhancing crop growth13,14. In Bangladesh, low broccoli yields limit farmers’ economic gains, primarily because of insufficient irrigation infrastructure, poor access to high-quality seeds, and inadequate input management. Abiotic stress affects the plant phenology and reduces the quality and quantity of the harvested product. Due to increasing global warming, water scarcity is the most critical issue for agriculturists. Therefore, improving irrigation systems and farming methods can expand the nation’s vegetable supply, increase farmers’ incomes, and improve productivity which help local economies and rural communities. Based on these facts, this experiment aims to investigate the effects of different levels of water stress on morphological, physiological, and biochemical parameters of broccoli; and to find out stress-tolerant broccoli variety.

Results

Vegetative growth

Different levels of water stress and varieties significantly affect vegetative growth. When irrigation was sufficient or not stressful, plant height, number of leaves, and leaf area were greater than those under stressful conditions. The highest values of these parameters, like plant height, number of leaves, and leaf area, were recorded under 100% FC conditions. Moreover, the lowest value was recorded under 45% FC conditions. The Green Crown showed the highest plant height, and Early You recorded the maximum number of leaves and leaf area. On the other hand, BARI Broccoli-1 presented the lowest values for the number of leaves and leaf area among the varieties. Early Green showed the lowest plant height (Figs. 1, 2 and 3). In terms of interaction effects, the Green Crown had the most significant effect on plant height and leaf area under 100% FC conditions (Table 1). The fresh leaf weight per plant varied significantly with irrigation level and variety. The highest fresh weight was recorded for the Early Green variety under 100% FC conditions. On the other hand, BARI Broccoli-1 had the lowest fresh leaf weight under 45% FC conditions. In the case of dry matter content in leaves, the highest value was recorded in BARI broccoli-1 under 45% FC. The number of days to curd initiation significantly varied among irrigation schedules and varieties. Early curd initiation occurred in BARI Broccoli-1 under 100% FC, and early harvesting occurred in Green Crown under 45% FC, whereas Early Green broccoli presented late curd initiation at 45% FC and late harvesting under 100% FC conditions (Tables 2, 3 and 4).

Yield and yield contributing character

The fresh weight of the curd/plant significantly differed by variety and irrigation level. The highest weight was recorded for the Early Green variety under 100% FC conditions and 45% FC among all the varieties. The highest curd weight was 310.19 g/plant under 100% FC, and the lowest was 186.22 g/plant under 45% FC (Table 5). The combined effects presented a maximum of 381.04 g/plant in Early Green with 100% FC. The curd size gradually decreases with increasing stress (Fig. 4). The dry matter content in the curd was highest in BARI Broccoli − 1 and lowest in Early Green (Table 6). However, under the interaction effect, BARI-Broccoli-1 presented the highest dry matter content under 45% FC conditions (Table 7). The height, perimeter, and diameter of the broccoli curd also varied significantly. Compared with the stress condition, the 100% FC condition had a greater effect on the above parameters. Early Green recorded the greatest height, diameter, and perimeter in the curd under 100% FC conditions (Tables 2 and 3). The Early Green and Green Crown presented the highest curd height under 100% FC conditions in the interaction effect (Table 4). The broccoli yield was highest in Early Green varieties under 100% FC conditions. Moreover, when the plants were exposed to 45% FC, Early Green presented the highest yield among all varieties (Tables 5, 6 and 7). It may be due to enhancing physiological mechanisms for mitigating stress damage, such as increased antioxidant enzyme activity, improved proline and phenolic compound production, and better water use efficiency.

Crop quality parameters

Crop quality parameters such as the chlorophyll index, ascorbic acid, and proline contents significantly differed among the stress conditions and varieties. The Chlorophyll index was highest under 100% FC conditions (Table 5). Among all varieties, Early Green presented the highest chlorophyll index (Table 6). The highest content of ascorbic acid was observed in the 45% FC condition. The highest ascorbic acid and proline content was recorded from the Early You variety. 45% FC resulted in the highest ascorbic acid and proline contents in all varieties (Tables 5, 6 and 7). This is due to severe stress triggers increased accumulation of ascorbic acid (a vital antioxidant) and proline (an osmoprotectant) in plants as protective mechanisms to scavenge reactive oxygen species (ROS), maintain cell integrity, and regulate cell processes. In the interaction effect, the highest proline and ascorbic acid content was recorded from the Early You variety under 45% FC condition (Table 7).

Discussion

Plants exposed to water stress presented significantly reduced growth and yield attributes. The plant height and number of leaves are important growth traits that increase with increasing water availability and significantly vary across irrigation levels. Water stress reduces vegetative growth, whereas increased irrigation promotes plant height due to improved soil moisture and temperature, increasing cell expansion15–18. This reduction is primarily due to limited cell elongation, reduced turgor pressure, and impaired physiological and metabolic functions under drought conditions. Other researchers have also reported that water deficit decreases the number of leaves19. The leaf area of broccoli significantly varied across treatments and varieties, and decreased under water stress. Izadpanah et al.. (2025) reported that water stress during the vegetative stage reduced the leaf area of broccoli by 30–40%, resulting in lower photosynthetic efficiency and biomass20. Similar findings were reported21,22. Significant differences in the leaf chlorophyll indices were observed among the varieties. During drought, chlorophyll degradation occurs because reactive oxygen species damage chloroplasts, reducing photosynthesis. Zhang et al. (2018) reported that drought stress decreases broccoli chlorophyll content, a finding similar to our own; it also reduces photosynthesis and biomass. It may be a reason that drought stress reduces leaf water content and damages photosystems, leading to chlorophyll degradation and less photosynthetic capacity. Waterlogging similarly decreases chlorophyll and disrupts the biosynthesis of its genes23,24. The fresh leaf weight per plant varied significantly with irrigation level and increased as irrigation increased. Adequate irrigation increases leaf size and fresh weight, whereas water stress causes wilting and a 30–40% reduction in leaf weight16,25. The dry matter content of leaves varied significantly by variety and treatment. The highest leaf dry matter content (11.76%) under low irrigation is attributed to reduced water availability, which limits cell expansion and results in denser biomass16,26.

The number of days to curd initiation varied significantly with irrigation and variety. Early water stress delays curd initiation compared with that in well-irrigated plants27,28.

The irrigation treatments had a significant effect on the fresh curd mass. Low soil moisture led to smaller curds and reduced fresh weight. Low soil moisture reduces curd size and fresh weight, whereas adequate water increases both. Similar drought effects were reported by29.

Significant variation in curd diameter, perimeter, and curd height was observed across irrigation treatments and varieties. Other researchers also reported that the curd diameter was greatest under full irrigation and smallest under decreased irrigation28,30. Adequate irrigation promoted healthy curd growth, with the most extended curd height occurring at 100% irrigation and the shortest at 40% irrigation28. Compared to 100% FC, stress increased the dry matter content in the curd; a similar result was also found by another researcher21. While water stress can decrease total plant growth and yield, the remaining plant material is more concentrated in dry matter and other beneficial compounds like proline and soluble solids. The marketable yield of broccoli varied significantly by variety and irrigation. The highest yield was 15.50 t/ha under 100% FC. Among the varieties, Early Green presented a yield of 14.94 t/ha. The combined effects reached a maximum of 19.05 t/ha in Early Green under 100% FC. Under 45% FC, Early Green variety showed increased leaf number, curd fresh weight, and yield by 14%, 68% and 68% respectively, than the most sensitive variety BARI Broccoli-1. Water stress reduces nutrient uptake and broccoli yield15,21,23,28,31–33. Drought significantly reduces growth parameters and increases the amount of ascorbic acid34–36. Ascorbic acid (AsA) increases in plants during drought stress to combat the resulting reactive oxygen species (ROS), which cause oxidative damage and cell injury. Under oxidative stress, a plant’s ascorbic acid levels often increase to scavenge excess reactive oxygen species (ROS) and mitigate cellular damage, though this can vary depending on the specific stress. AsA acts as a potent non-enzymatic antioxidant by donating electrons to neutralize ROS and is a crucial component of the AsA-glutathione (GSH) cycle. This enzymatic activity, coupled with AsA’s role as a cofactor and regulator of cellular functions, helps maintain redox balance and tolerance to various stresses like drought, salinity, and light stress. Water deficit increases proline accumulation in broccoli, facilitating adaptation to stress conditions37–40. Our results also revealed that the ascorbic acid and proline contents increased with increasing stress. Under severe water stress, Early You variety accumulates high levels of ascorbic acid and proline as a combined stress response to mitigate oxidative damage and maintain cell function, which is most suitable for drought-prone conditions due to stability and adaptability. Proline accumulation under drought stress helps plants by acting as an osmolyte to maintain cell turgor, stabilizing proteins and cell membranes to prevent damage, and scavenging reactive oxygen species (ROS) to mitigate oxidative stress. This increase in proline enhances plant tolerance and survival by adjusting cellular osmotic potential and protecting vital cellular components during water deficit conditions. Plant stress influences enzyme activity and metabolic pathways by activating defensive responses, such as upregulating antioxidant enzymes (like SOD, CAT), increasing osmoprotectant synthesis (like proline), and altering metabolic flow to decrease growth-related processes (like photosynthesis) and prioritize survival. Stress also involves hormonal signaling (like ABA, SA) and secondary messengers (like Ca2+, ROS) that trigger gene expression and protein changes, ultimately leading to cellular and metabolic adjustments for adaptation and tolerance.

Materials and methods

Description of the experimental site

The pot experiment was conducted at the polytunnel of the Horticulture Farm of Bangladesh Agricultural University (BAU), Mymensingh, located at 24°75′N and 90°50′E, 18 m above sea level.

Collection of soil for pot preparation

The soil used for the potting medium was collected from the medium-high land of the Horticultural Farm and is of silty loam in nature. A soil sample was collected from the same field to measure the soil physical and chemical conditions, as shown in Table 8. The physical and chemical analyses were done at the Agri-Varsity Humboldt Soil Testing laboratory in the Department of Soil Science, Bangladesh Agricultural University. After collection, the soil was sieved to remove debris and left to air dry for more than 2 weeks. After the soil was dried, its moisture content was determined via the gravimetric method41. This moisture content was used to calculate the mass of air-dried soil (10.0075 kg), which was needed to obtain 10 kg of oven-dry soil. Each pot was filled with air-dry soil, equivalent in mass to 10 kg of oven-dried soil. Each pot was watered to field capacity one day before planting and left to saturate overnight.

Experimental details

A two-factor experiment was conducted in a completely randomized design (CRD). The treatment consisted of four broccoli varieties with four different water stresses, with three replications, resulting in a total of 48 pots.

Broccoli varieties and treatment

Four broccoli varieties were collected from different seed companies. ‘BARI Broccoli − 1’, Gazipur; ‘Green Crown’, Sakata seed cooperation, Japan; ‘Early You’, Lal teer seed Ltd., Dhaka; ‘Early Green’, Nozaki saishujo Ltd., Japan. Seedlings were raised in four seedbeds (2 m × 0.5 m) at Horticulture Farm, BAU, using well-prepared soil mixed with decomposed cow dung and fertilizer. Ten grams of seed of each variety were sown on October 15, 2023.

Water stress treatment

All the pots were subjected to zero stress at 100% FC during the first 10 days after the seedlings were transplanted. Twenty-one-day-old seedlings were transplanted from the seedbed to pots, and the pots were maintained with sufficient water for 10 days to help the seedlings adjust to the new environment. After 11 days of transplanting, from the seedlings stage, different levels of water stress were started, and maintained until harvesting. They included four water levels: 100% field capacity (FC), 75% FC, 60% FC, and 45% FC. The non-stressed pot that continued to receive 100% FC did not change, but the other stressed pot maintained its respective FC every day. If the pots attained a slightly lower soil moisture content, they were rewatered and brought to their required moisture contents. The amount of water needed to increase the moisture content was determined by weighing42. All the pots were re-watered at approximately 09:00 am each morning. Since air-dried soil was used to fill the pots in the pot experiment, the amount of water needed to bring its oven-dry equivalent mass to 100% FC, had to account the moisture already contained in the air-dried soil (Eq. 1). The mass of water needed to bring 10 kg (10000 g) of oven-dried soil to 100% FC, was hence calculated and 1.852 L found42. Methods of broccoli cultivation

Fertilizers were applied at the following rates: urea, 17 g; TSP, 17 g; and MoP, 14 g per pot15. TSP was applied during soil preparation, while urea and MoP were split and applied at 22 and 37 days after transplanting using the ring method. Twenty-one-day-old seedlings were transplanted. The pots were moistened beforehand, and the seedlings were watered immediately after planting. The harvesting time varied by variety and irrigation level; curds of marketable size were cut individually with a sharp knife.

Data and measurement

Vegetative growth

During the vegetative growth stage, plant height, leaf area, and number of leaves were collected at 7-day intervals from 7 to 49 days after treatment initiation (DATI) with three replications. The height of the plant was measured with a meter scale from the ground level to the tip of the longest leaf, and data were recorded in centimeters (cm). The number of leaves per plant was counted, excluding the small leaves, which were produced by axillary shoots. The fallen leaves were counted based on scar marks on the stern introduced by the petiole of the leaves. By measuring the leaf length and breadth, we calculated the leaf area.

Yield and yield contributing characters

After harvesting, the head height, perimeter, and diameter were measured with a measuring tape. Each plant in the experimental pot was closely observed to count the days required for curd initiation and the number of days needed for maturation. The total number of days from the transplant date until curd maturation was recorded by counting. After harvesting, the fresh leaf and curd weights of the sample plants were cleaned and cut down with sharp knives and measured in grams via an electronic digital balance (Model number-12051). The total curd yield per plant and total yield per hectare were recorded. The head perimeter, diameter, and curd height of broccoli were measured in centimeters (cm) with a measuring tape and scale. To measure the dry matter content in leaves/curd, fresh samples from selected plants were weighed and oven-dried at 70˚C for 72 h. Physiological stress measurement

The chlorophyll index, indicating chlorophyll content in leaves, was measured from fully opened 5th number leaves using a Dualex Optical Leaf clip meter (Model- DUALEX 4) by placing a leaf between the optical heads44. The ascorbic acid content in broccoli from different treatments was quantitatively determined from the fully opened matured 4th number of leaves with three replications45 guidelines of the Bangladesh Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (BCSIR), Dhaka. Five (5) grams of fresh curd sample was crushed in a mortar and pestle and mixed well with 3% HPO3 up to 100 ml in a volumetric flask. It was centrifuged with a digital centrifuge machine in 10 min. Then 5 ml aliquot of HPO3 extract of the sample was taken and titrated with dye solution45. The leaf proline content was also determined from the same leaf with three replications according to the method described by46. 0.5 g of fresh leaf sample was crushed and mixed with aqueous sulfosalicylic acid, and filtering the mixture. Then, 2 ml of the supernatant is treated with glacial acetic acid and acid ninhydrin, boiled at 100 °C for one hour. After adding toluene and mixing, the sample is cooled to room temperature and analyzed using a UV spectrophotometer (Model number: UV 1800) with toluene as the blank, and the calculation was made46.

Statistical analysis

The data were statistically analyzed to assess the effects of irrigation level and variety on broccoli growth, yield, and quality. ANOVA and Mean values were evaluated with the ‘F’ test in MASTAT-C software, and differences among treatments were compared using Duncan’s Multiple Range Test (DMRT) at 1% significance level.

Conclusion

Water stress directly inhibits plant development by reducing cell expansion and photosynthesis, which lowers growth rates, biomass, and overall yield. Under 100%FC conditions, the plant produced the best overall performance across growth traits such as leaf number, plant height, and curd weight. Among the varieties, the Early Green variety consistently outperformed the others, achieving the highest yield. Even under 45%FC, it showed the highest yield, indicating that it can survive and yield well under drought conditions. Enhancing the practical implications of plant stress tolerance involves applying a multi-faceted approach combining breeding techniques, genetic manipulation. Moreover, we include traditional breeding and transgenic/genome editing approaches to develop stress-tolerant varieties.

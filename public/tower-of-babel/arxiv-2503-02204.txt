Deuterated Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbons in the Interstellar Medium: Constraints from the Orion Bar as Observed by the James Webb Space Telescope DRAFT: August 24, 2026
X.J. Yang and Aigen Li

Abstract
The gas-phase abundances of deuterium (D) in the local interstellar medium (ISM) exhibit considerable regional variations. Particularly, in some regions the gas-phase D abundances are substantially lower than the primordial D abundance generated in the Big Bang, after subtracting the astration reduction caused by the Galactic chemical evolution. Deuterated polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) molecules have been suggested as a potential reservoir of the D atoms missing from the gas-phase. Recent observations from the James Webb Space Telescope’s Near Infrared Spectrograph have revealed the widespread of deuterated PAHs in the Orion Bar through their aliphatic C–D emission at 4.65 μ ​ m \,{\rm\mu m} and possibly aromatic C–D emission at 4.4 μ ​ m \,{\rm\mu m} as well. To examine the viability of deuterated PAHs as the D reservoir, we model the infrared (IR) emission spectra of small PAH molecules containing various aromatic and aliphatic D atoms in the Orion Bar. We find that small deuterated PAHs exhibit a noticeable emission band at 4.4 or 4.65 μ ​ m \,{\rm\mu m} even if they contain only one aromatic or aliphatic D atom. We derive N D , ali / N H ≈ 3.4 % {N_{\rm D,ali}}/N_{\rm H}\approx 3.4\% , the deuteration degree of PAHs measured as the number of aliphatic D atoms (relative to H), from the observed intensity ratios of the 4.65 μ ​ m \,{\rm\mu m} band to the 3.3 μ ​ m \,{\rm\mu m} aromatic C–H band. The deuteration degree for aromatically-deuterated PAHs is less certain as C–N stretch also contributes to the observed emission around 4.4 μ ​ m \,{\rm\mu m} . If we attribute it exclusively to aromatic C–D, we derive an upper limit of ≈ \approx 14% on the deuteration degree, which is capable of accounting for an appreciable fraction of the missing D budget.



I
Introduction

Deuterium (D) was exclusively generated in the Big Bang.
As the Galaxy evolves, D is converted into
3
He,

4
He and heavier elements in stellar interiors
(a process known as “astration”).
The D/H abundance therefore gradually
decreases from its primordial abundance of
D/H
\approx
26 parts per million (ppm)
predicted by the standard Big Bang
Nucleosynthesis (BBN) model,
to a present-day abundance of
D/H
\gtrsim
22
\,{\rm ppm}
,
according to the Galactic chemical
evolution (GCE) model.

However, observations of the local interstellar medium
(ISM) have revealed that the gas-phase interstellar D/H
abundance is appreciably lower than predicted from
the BBN and GCE models and varies considerably from
one sight line to another by a factor of
\sim\,
3
(Draine 2006; Linsky et al. 2006).
Such a low gas-phase D/H abundance
and its regional variations are puzzling.
In such a small scale as the local ISM,
variations in astration are not
expected to be significant enough to
account for the D/H variations.

Where have all the missing D atoms gone?
One possible agent is polycyclic aromatic
hydrocarbon (PAH): all or part of the D atoms
missing from the gas-phase could have been
locked up in PAH molecules (Draine 2006).
When D atoms incorporated with PAHs,
both aromatic and aliphatic C–D bonds
could be formed (Yang & Li 2023;
see Figure
1
).
In principle, they could be revealed
by the aromatic and aliphatic C–D stretches
at
\sim\,
4.4 and 4.65
\,{\rm\mu m}

(Hudgins et al. 2004; Yang & Li 2023).
Indeed, in the past decades, such features
have been detected by the
Infrared Space Observatory

and AKARI in several objects (Peeters et al. 2004;
Onaka et al. 2014; Doney et al. 2016).
Based on these detections,
Yang et al. (2020, 2021) and Yang & Li (2023)
have determined the amount of D/H depleted
in PAHs and found that PAHs with aliphatic
C–D units could have tied up a substantial
amount of D/H and marginally account for
the missing D.

However, the low intensities of
the 4.4 and 4.65
\,{\rm\mu m}
C–D emission bands
put them at the limit of ISO and AKARI.
This has changed with the launch and operation
of the
James Webb Space Telescope
(JWST).
Due to its unprecedented sensitivity,
JWST is expected to place their detection
on firm ground and enable far more detailed
analysis than previously possible.
Indeed, using the
Near Infrared Spectrograph

(NIRSpec) on JWST, Peeters et al. (2024) performed
high-resolution near infrared (IR) integral field
spectroscopic observations of the Orion Bar
and clearly detected the aliphatic C–D emission
band at 4.65
\,{\rm\mu m}
and possibly the aromatic
C–D emission band at 4.4
\,{\rm\mu m}
as well.
However, the detection of the 4.4
\,{\rm\mu m}

band is less certain. The emission complex
around 4.4
\,{\rm\mu m}
actually peaks at

\sim\,
4.35
\,{\rm\mu m}
, which overlaps
with or is even dominated by C–N stretches.
The 4.65
\,{\rm\mu m}
aliphatic C–D band was also
detected by JWST/NIRSpec in the bright
photodissociation region (PDR) position
in M17, a nearby massive star-forming
cloud (Boersma et al. 2023).
More recently, Draine et al. (2025) also reported
the detection of the 4.65
\,{\rm\mu m}
aliphatic C–D
emission in four massive star-forming
regions in M51, the Whirlpool galaxy.
However, neither M17 nor M51 shows
appreciable aromatic C–D emission
at 4.4
\,{\rm\mu m}
.

In this work, we shall base on the JWST/NIRSpec
data of the Orion Bar to determine the depletion
of D atoms in PAHs. To this end, in §
II

we first summarize the JWST/NIRSpec detections
of the 4.4 and 4.65
\,{\rm\mu m}
C–D emission bands
of the Orion Bar. We then model in §
III

the vibrational excitation of PAHs containing D atoms
and compute their IR emission spectra.
We consider three representative deuterated
PAH molecules with various aromatic
and aliphatic D atoms incorporated and derive
the power emitted from the 4.4 and 4.65
\,{\rm\mu m}

bands (
I_{4.4}
and
I_{4.65}
) as well as that from
the aromatic C–H stretch at 3.3
\,{\rm\mu m}
(
I_{3.3}
).
We derive in §
IV
the degrees of
deuteration of PAHs by comparing the observed
band ratios
\left(I_{4.4}/I_{3.3}\right)_{\rm obs}
and
\left(I_{4.65}/I_{3.3}\right)_{\rm obs}

with the model band ratios. Finally, we summarize
our major results in §

U\equiv\frac{\int_{1\mu{\rm m}}^{912{\rm\,{\rm\AA}}}4\pi J_{\star}(\lambda)\,d\lambda}{\int_{1\mu{\rm m}}^{912{\rm\,{\rm\AA}}}4\pi J_{\rm ISRF}(\lambda)\,d\lambda}~~,

(3)

where
J_{\star}(\lambda)
is the intensity of starlight
in the Orion Bar, and
J_{\rm ISRF}(\lambda)
is
the starlight intensity of the solar neighbourhood
interstellar radiation field (ISRF) of
Mathis, Mezger & Panagia (1983; MMP83).

We calculate the IR emission spectra of

,
C

, and C

,
illuminated by

Ori C
in the Orion Bar. For illustration, we show in

\,{\rm\mu m}
for a number
of deuterated species of C
24
H

C

H

, and C
48
H

.
We see that the 4.4 and 4.65
\,{\rm\mu m}
aromatic
and aliphatic C–D emission bands are clearly
visible even just with
{N_{\rm D,aro}}=1
and
{N_{\rm D,ali}}=1
,
and become stronger as
{N_{\rm D,aro}}
and
{N_{\rm D,ali}}
increase.
For molecules with
{N_{\rm D,aro}}={N_{\rm D,ali}}
,
the 4.65
\,{\rm\mu m}
band is stronger than
the 4.4
\,{\rm\mu m}
band by a factor of
\sim\,
2.
This is because the intrinsic band strength of
the 4.65
\,{\rm\mu m}
aliphatic C–D stretch
is stronger than that of the 4.4
\,{\rm\mu m}
aromatic
C–D stretch (
A_{4.65}/A_{4.4}\approx 1.86
;
see Yang & Li 2023).
Also, as expected, the 3.4
\,{\rm\mu m}
aliphatic
C–H emission remains essentially invariant
since the number of aliphatic C–H bonds is
fixed to be 10% of
N_{\rm C}

(see §
III
).

For each molecule with a given
{N_{\rm D,aro}}

and
{N_{\rm D,ali}}
, we derive
\left(I_{4.4}/I_{3.3}\right)_{\rm mod}

and
\left(I_{4.65}/I_{3.3}\right)_{\rm mod}
,
the model emission intensity ratios of
the 4.4 and 4.65
\,{\rm\mu m}
bands
to the 3.3
\,{\rm\mu m}
band.
They are calculated from

\left(\frac{I_{4.4}}{I_{3.3}}\right)=\frac{\int_{4.4}\Delta j_{\lambda}(N_{\rm C})\,d\lambda}{\int_{3.3}\Delta j_{\lambda}(N_{\rm C})\,d\lambda}~~,~~\left(\frac{I_{4.65}}{I_{3.3}}\right)=\frac{\int_{4.65}\Delta j_{\lambda}(N_{\rm C})\,d\lambda}{\int_{3.3}\Delta j_{\lambda}(N_{\rm C})\,d\lambda}~~,

(4)

where
I_{3.3}
,
I_{4.4}
and
I_{4.65}
are
respectively the intensities of the 3.3
\,{\rm\mu m}

aromatic C–H emission band,
the 4.4
\,{\rm\mu m}
aromatic C–D band,
and the 4.65
\,{\rm\mu m}
aliphatic C–D band;
and
\int_{3.3}\Delta j_{\lambda}(N_{\rm C})\,d\lambda
,

\int_{4.4}\Delta j_{\lambda}(N_{\rm C})\,d\lambda
, and

\int_{4.65}\Delta j_{\lambda}(N_{\rm C})\,d\lambda

are respectively the band-integrated
excess emission of the 3.3, 4.4 and
4.65
\,{\rm\mu m}
bands of deuterated PAHs.

To relate the model
\left(I_{4.4}/I_{3.3}\right)_{\rm mod}
and

\left(I_{4.65}/I_{3.3}\right)_{\rm mod}
band-intensity ratios to
the degrees of deuteration, we show in
Figure

the band-intensity ratios

\left(I_{4.4}/I_{3.3}\right)_{\rm mod}
and
\left(I_{4.65}/I_{3.3}\right)_{\rm mod}

as a function of
{N_{\rm D,aro}}/N_{\rm H}

and
{N_{\rm D,ali}}/N_{\rm H}
, respectively.
The band-intensity ratios are computed
for the various deuterated species of
C
24
H

, C

H

,
and C

, containing various
aromatic and aliphatic D atoms.
It is apparent that for each parent molecule,

\left(I_{4.4}/I_{3.3}\right)_{\rm mod}
linearly increases with

{N_{\rm D,aro}}/N_{\rm H}
and
\left(I_{4.65}/I_{3.3}\right)_{\rm mod}
linearly
increases with
{N_{\rm D,ali}}/N_{\rm H}
.
For larger PAHs, the increase is somewhat steeper
(i.e., the slopes

d\left(I_{4.4}/I_{3.3}\right)_{\rm mod}/d\left({N_{\rm D,aro}}/N_{\rm H}\right)

and
d\left(I_{4.65}/I_{3.3}\right)_{\rm mod}/d\left({N_{\rm D,ali}}/N_{\rm H}\right)

are slightly higher).
This is because, a larger PAH molecule
has more degrees of freedom and a larger
heat capacity. Upon absorption of an UV stellar
photon, the molecule is excited to lower temperatures
and emits more effectively at longer wavelengths
(e.g., 4.4 and 4.65
\,{\rm\mu m}
) than at shorter wavelengths
(e.g., 3.3
\,{\rm\mu m}
). Therefore, for a given
{N_{\rm D}}/N_{\rm H}
,
larger
\left(I_{4.4}/I_{3.3}\right)_{\rm mod}
and
\left(I_{4.65}/I_{3.3}\right)_{\rm mod}

band-ratios are expected.
By averaging over all the deuterated species of
all three parent molecules, we obtain

\frac{{N_{\rm D,aro}}}{N_{\rm H}}\approx\left(1.19\pm 0.15\right)\times\left(I_{4.4}/I_{3.3}\right)_{\rm mod}~~,~~\frac{{N_{\rm D,ali}}}{N_{\rm H}}\approx\left(0.57\pm 0.06\right)\times\left(I_{4.65}/I_{3.3}\right)_{\rm mod}~~.

(5)

5

\left(I_{4.4}/I_{3.3}\right)_{\rm obs}
and
\left(I_{4.65}/I_{3.3}\right)_{\rm obs}

to derive the deuteration of PAHs.

Finally, we also note that,
following the absorption of an energetic photon,
a PAH molecule has three major competing decay
channels to relax its energy:
thermal emission (i.e., “heat”),
photoionization, and photodissociation
(see Appendix A in Li & Lunine 2003).
When we model the vibrational excitation
of deuterated PAHs in this work
(see §
III
),
we assume that the energy of an absorbed photon
is fully converted to vibrational energy of the molecule,
i.e., we neglect the energy lost in the form of
photoelectrons (e.g., see Sidhu et al. 2022)
and bond dissociation
as well as possible fluorescent emission
of optical photons (e.g., see Li & Draine 2002).
If we take into account the energy lost in these
channels, the overall IR emission will be smaller.
However, this will not affect the PAH deuteration
degree derivation since what we rely on are
the band-intensity
ratios
, not the absolute
power emitted from a C–D band.

V
Summary

We have modeled the IR emission spectra of
deuterated PAH molecules in the Orion Bar.
We have considered various deuterated species
of three small PAH molecules
(C
24
H
12
,
C
32
H
16
, and C
48
H
18
)
which are the most effective emitters
in the C–H and C–D stretching bands.
These deuterated species contain a range
of aromatic and aliphatic C–D bonds and
emit at the 3.3
\,{\rm\mu m}
aromatic C–H stretch,
4.4
\,{\rm\mu m}
aromatic C–D stretch,
and 4.65
\,{\rm\mu m}
aliphatic C–D stretch.
The major results are as follows:

1.

Small deuterated PAHs exhibit a noticeable
emission band at 4.4 or 4.65
\,{\rm\mu m}

even if they contain
only
one aromatic
or aliphatic D atom. With the same amount
of aromatic and aliphatic D atoms,
the 4.65
\,{\rm\mu m}
aliphatic C–D band
is stronger than the 4.4
\,{\rm\mu m}
aromatic
C–D band by a factor of
\sim\,
2
since the intrinsic strength of the 4.65
\,{\rm\mu m}

aliphatic C–D stretch is about twice strong
as that of the 4.4
\,{\rm\mu m}
aromatic C–D stretch.

2.

The fractions of aromatic and aliphatic D atoms
in deuterated PAHs can be derived from
the observed band-intensity ratios
of the 4.4 and 4.65
\,{\rm\mu m}
C–D
emission bands to the 3.3
\,{\rm\mu m}

C–H band through

\left({N_{\rm D,aro}}/N_{\rm H}\right)\approx\left(1.19\pm 0.15\right)\times\left(I_{4.4}/I_{3.3}\right)_{\rm obs}

and
{N_{\rm D,ali}}/N_{\rm H}\approx\left(0.57\pm 0.06\right)\times\left(I_{4.65}/I_{3.3}\right)_{\rm obs}
.

3.

The JWST/NIRSpec observations of
the Orion Bar reveal mean band-intensity
ratios of
\left(I_{4.4}/I_{3.3}\right)_{\rm obs}\approx 0.12\pm 0.02

and
\left(I_{4.65}/I_{3.3}\right)_{\rm obs}\approx 0.06\pm 0.02
.
These band-intensity ratios translate into

{N_{\rm D,aro}}/N_{\rm H}\approx 14\%
,

{N_{\rm D,ali}}/N_{\rm H}\approx 3.4\%
,
and a total deuteration fraction of

{N_{\rm D}}/N_{\rm H}\approx 17\%
.
However, such a deuteration fraction
should be considered as an upper limit
since the JWST-observed 4.4
\,{\rm\mu m}
emission
complex is also contributed or even dominated
by C–N stretches, and the adopted continuum
underneath this band is somewhat generous
and could overestimate the band flux.
If the observed 4.4
\,{\rm\mu m}
band indeed arises
exclusively from aromatic
C–D stretch, deuterated PAHs are able to
account for a large fraction of the missing
D budget in the ISM.

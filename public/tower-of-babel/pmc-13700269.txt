Safety and tolerability of rapid vericiguat initiation and titration alongside GDMT optimization in HFrEF: a prospective cohort study
Authors: Lu Gao, Botao Shen, Haoxuan Chu, Zhen Guo, Yushi Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHeart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) remains challenging to manage, particularly in Asian populations. Although vericiguat may complement guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT), the safety and tolerability of rapid initiation and titration in Chinese patients with HFrEF require further evaluation.MethodsThis single-center, prospective, single-arm, self-controlled cohort study included 140 patients who received at least 1 dose of vericiguat 5 mg and constituted the treated population; 133 had evaluable 14-day titration and short-term safety data. Patients were monitored for dose-titration success, blood pressure, renal function, electrolytes, hepatic function, hemoglobin, and N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) over 14 days. Echocardiography was performed at 12 and 20 weeks.ResultsThe mean age was 61.5 ± 14.2 years, and baseline left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) was 32.87 ± 8.51%. On a treated-population basis, 131 of 140 patients (93.6%) achieved the target vericiguat dose of 10 mg by day 14; among patients with evaluable titration data, 131 of 133 (98.5%) achieved target-dose uptitration. Median NT-proBNP decreased by 44.0% from baseline to day 14 (p < 0.001), without clinically notable blood pressure changes on the day after vericiguat initiation. At 20 weeks, LVEF increased by 8.26% (p < 0.001), accompanied by reductions in left ventricular diastolic diameter and left atrial diameter. No significant renal, hepatic, or electrolyte safety signals were observed.ConclusionsRapid initiation of vericiguat 5 mg alongside GDMT optimization showed acceptable short-term safety and tolerability in Chinese patients with HFrEF. These findings support further randomized controlled trials to clarify the independent contribution of vericiguat to cardiac remodeling.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13019-026-04518-7.

Introduction

Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) remains a major contributor to cardiovascular morbidity and mortality worldwide, despite advances in guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT) [1, 2]. While the sequential introduction of angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitors (ARNI), sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i), mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRA), and beta-blockers has transformed HFrEF management, a significant proportion of patients continue to experience residual risk, characterized by persistent left ventricular dysfunction and recurrent hospitalizations [2, 3]. This unmet clinical need has driven the development of novel therapeutic agents targeting alternative pathways, such as the soluble guanylate cyclase (sGC) stimulator vericiguat, which acts through the nitric oxide-sGC-cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP) signaling pathway [4, 5].

The VICTORIA trial established the efficacy of vericiguat in reducing the composite risk of cardiovascular death or first heart failure hospitalization in high-risk patients with HFrEF and recent worsening heart failure [4]. Subsequent analyses from VICTORIA further evaluated the effects of vericiguat across ejection fraction, biomarker, renal function, and background treatment subgroups [6–8]. However, critical gaps persist in real-world clinical practice: (1) dose titration safety, as concerns about hypotension during up-titration may limit early adoption, particularly in hemodynamically fragile patients; (2) Asian and real-world data, because Asian patients and real-world treatment settings remain less fully characterized in vericiguat studies [9–11]; and (3) integration with optimized GDMT, as the interplay between vericiguat and contemporary quadruple therapy (ARNI, beta-blocker, MRA, and SGLT2i) remains underexplored despite the mechanistic plausibility of complementary signaling through the sGC-cGMP and natriuretic peptide pathways [5, 8].

This study aimed to evaluate the short-term safety profile and hemodynamic tolerability of vericiguat initiation at 5 mg followed by rapid up-titration to 10 mg in Asian HFrEF patients receiving optimized GDMT. By integrating biomarker assessments (NT-proBNP), echocardiographic monitoring, and real-world GDMT optimization practices, we sought to evaluate the safety, tolerability, and hemodynamic stability associated with early initiation of vericiguat 5 mg during the optimization of background GDMT, and to determine whether our patient population could more effectively tolerate high-dose initiation of vericiguat.

Methods

Study design

This single-center, prospective study recruited a cohort of patients hospitalized or treated in outpatient settings at this center from January 2024 to September 2024. The trial protocol (available with the full text of this article at medicalresearch.org) was approved by regulatory agencies in the participating countries and institutional review boards or ethics committees at the participating sites (Approval No: MR-22-25-060617). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Conference on Harmonization Good Clinical Practice guidelines.

Before treatment, patients had received stable heart failure GDMT for over 4 weeks and provided baseline data, including heart rate, blood pressure, clinical laboratory results, and echocardiography. Clinical and laboratory assessments were conducted the day after the initial administration of vericiguat 5 mg and on day 14. On day 14, the dose of vericiguat was increased to the target dose of 10 mg for patients who could tolerate it. For transparency, tolerability was reported using 2 denominators: the treated population, defined as all patients who received at least 1 dose of vericiguat 5 mg (n = 140), and the evaluable 14-day cohort, defined as patients with available day-14 titration and short-term safety data (n = 133). Target-dose achievement at day 14 was reported in both populations. During the 14-day treatment period, other heart failure GDMT medications were also increased to the maximum tolerable dose. Echocardiography assessments were performed at weeks 12 and 20 after vericiguat reached the target dose.

Study population

Eligible patients were aged 18 to 85 years, with a clinical diagnosis of HFrEF and LVEF ≤ 40% in NYHA class II to IV. Major exclusion criteria included baseline SBP < 85 mmHg, severe renal or hepatic impairment, recent acute cardiovascular events (e.g., acute myocardial infarction within the past 3 months), allergy to vericiguat or a history of severe adverse reactions, and concurrent end-stage diseases. Specific inclusion and exclusion criteria are provided in Supplementary Table S1.

Inspection standards

The medication regimen for GDMT treatment is determined by a dedicated cardiologist based on the patient’s condition. Patients must have received at least 4 weeks of GDMT prior to enrollment, including medications such as MRA, beta-blockers, ARNI/ARB/ACEI, and SGLT2i. The starting dose of vericiguat is 5 mg, which is titrated to the target dose of 10 mg after 14 days. If the patient experiences dizziness or low blood pressure, the dose should be reduced or titration delayed. Baseline characteristics of participants, including laboratory, echocardiographic, and medication-related data, will be collected. Echocardiographic data will be independently measured by an experienced echocardiography specialist using two-dimensional echocardiography to assess left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) and other cardiac chamber dimensions. Comparisons will be made for LVEF, left ventricular diastolic diameter (LVDD), left atrial diameter (LAD), tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion (TAPSE), and pulmonary artery systolic pressure (PASP) at 12 weeks and 20 weeks after reaching the target dose.

Safety and cardiac function improvement effectiveness

Safety analysis will assess changes in systolic and diastolic blood pressure following daily administration of vericiguat 5 mg for 14 days, as well as the occurrence of symptomatic hypotension. The primary safety endpoints include liver function (ALT/AST increase > 3 times baseline) and kidney function (serum creatinine increase ≥ 0.5 mg/dL and eGFR decrease > 25%).

Cardiac function improvement will be evaluated via echocardiography, measuring LVEF, LVDD, TAPSE, PASP, and NT-proBNP levels. After titrating vericiguat to 10 mg QD, improvements in LVEF and TAPSE, along with reductions in LVDD and PASP at 12 and 20 weeks, will indicate heart function improvement.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were assessed for distributional normality using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Normally distributed variables are presented as mean ± SD, whereas markedly skewed variables are presented as median (interquartile range [IQR]). Categorical variables are summarized as counts and percentages. For within-patient comparisons, paired t-tests were used for approximately normally distributed continuous variables, and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests were used for non-normally distributed variables. Because NT-proBNP was markedly right-skewed, changes in NT-proBNP were analyzed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test, with an additional sensitivity analysis performed on the log-transformed scale. As an exploratory baseline-adjusted analysis, analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) was performed with baseline LVEF as a covariate to estimate the least-squares mean change in LVEF at 20 weeks. Given the single-arm design, this analysis was intended to account for baseline variation in LVEF, rather than to establish causality, exclude natural recovery, or isolate the specific effect of vericiguat from concurrent GDMT optimization. Comparisons of categorical data were performed using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 18.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Study’s patients

The baseline characteristics of the participants are presented in Table 1. A total of 140 patients received at least 1 dose of vericiguat 5 mg and constituted the treated population. Among them, 133 patients had evaluable day-14 titration and short-term safety data, whereas 7 did not contribute to the evaluable 14-day cohort because of sudden cardiac death 1 month after treatment initiation (n = 1), self-withdrawal due to dizziness (n = 2), discontinuation for personal reasons (n = 2), or inability to attend follow-up (n = 2). Overall, 131 of 140 treated patients (93.6%) achieved the target dose of vericiguat 10 mg by day 14. Among the 133 patients with evaluable 14-day titration data, 131 (98.5%) were successfully uptitrated to 10 mg, whereas 2 remained on 5 mg because of blood pressure-related intolerance/dizziness. A total of 131 patients completed the 20-week echocardiographic follow-up (Fig. 1).

Among the 133 patients who completed the baseline assessments, the average age was 61.46 ± 14.19 years, with 100 males (75.19%). The cohort included 55 patients with hypertension (41.35%) and 32 patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (24.06%). The majority of participants had NYHA class III-IV heart failure (76.69%). Baseline echocardiographic values included an LVEF of 32.87 ± 8.51%, an LVDD of 61.44 ± 7.83 mm, a TAPSE of 16.45 ± 3.12 mm, and a PASP of 39.85 ± 8.42 mmHg. Participants were on stable GDMT: 123 (92.48%) were on MRA, 128 (96.24%) on beta-blockers, 117 (87.97%) on ARNI, and 123 (92.48%) on SGLT2i. Additionally, 105 patients (78.95%) were on a quadruple-drug regimen (Table 1).

Safety of initial Vericiguat 5 mg

When Vericiguat 5 mg was initiated alongside GDMT, no significant changes were observed in SBP or DBP from baseline to the next day. Baseline SBP was 125.53 ± 22.42 mmHg and DBP 74.60 ± 14.34 mmHg, while SBP on the following day was 122.68 ± 14.85 mmHg and DBP 73.69 ± 11.16 mmHg. By Day 14, a statistically significant reduction in blood pressure was observed compared to baseline, with SBP decreasing to 119 ± 11.66 mmHg and DBP to 70.66 ± 8.20 mmHg (p < 0.01; Fig. 2). Despite this statistical difference, the absolute reduction was clinically acceptable within the context of GDMT optimization, and no severe symptomatic hypotension occurred.

One patient, with a baseline blood pressure of 91/71 mmHg, experienced a drop in blood pressure to 87/64 mmHg and dizziness after receiving Vericiguat 5 mg. Due to sustained SBP below 100 mmHg during the 14-day period, the dose was not increased. Another patient, with poor baseline cardiac function, intermittently experienced dizziness after receiving Vericiguat 5 mg, although no significant blood pressure drop was noted. This patient did not have their dose increased after 14 days and completed a cardiac ultrasound at the 20-week follow-up but died from heart failure at week 22 (Supplementary Table S2).

The remaining 131 patients (98.5%) tolerated dose escalation to vericiguat 10 mg by day 14. Over the 14-day period, there were no significant changes in eGFR, potassium, sodium, hemoglobin, ALT, or AST (Fig. 3A–E and Supplementary Table S3). Because ALT and AST were markedly right-skewed, they were summarized as median (IQR) and compared using Wilcoxon signed-rank tests.

Change in HR and cardiac biomarkers

Concurrent with the initiation of Vericiguat 5 mg and GDMT adjustments, heart rate (HR) showed an early significant reduction from a baseline of 84.74 ± 20.51 bpm to 80.23 ± 14.48 bpm on Day 1 (p < 0.05). This downward trend persisted, with HR further decreasing to 75.17 ± 11.76 bpm by Day 14 (p < 0.001; Fig. 2).

Additionally, NT-proBNP showed a significant reduction after 14 days of the combined treatment regimen. Because NT-proBNP was markedly right-skewed, it was summarized as median (IQR) and analyzed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Median NT-proBNP decreased from 2860.0 (1810.0–4215.0) pg/mL at baseline to 1602.0 (980.0–2510.0) pg/mL on day 14, corresponding to a relative reduction of 44.0% (p < 0.001; Fig. 3F and Supplementary Table S3). This result remained significant in the sensitivity analysis using log-transformed NT-proBNP.

Change in systolic function

At the 12th week, the mean LVEF increased by 1.8% compared to baseline (P < 0.05), and by the 20th week, LVEF had increased by 8.26%, significantly higher than baseline (P < 0.001, Fig. 4A). After adjustment for baseline LVEF using ANCOVA, the least-squares mean increase in LVEF at 20 weeks was 7.45% (95% CI: 6.10% to 8.80%, p < 0.001). This baseline-adjusted result was consistent with the unadjusted finding; however, because of the single-arm design and concurrent GDMT optimization, it should be interpreted as supportive evidence of within-cohort improvement rather than proof of a causal treatment effect or evidence separating vericiguat from background therapy. At the 12th week, the mean LVDD and LAD decreased by 1.53% and 1.55%, respectively. By the 20th week, LVDD and LAD had decreased by 1.54% and 3.12%, respectively (Fig. 4B, P < 0.05). Furthermore, right ventricular function indices demonstrated favorable changes. Mean TAPSE significantly increased from baseline by 1.65 mm at 20 weeks (Fig. 4C, P < 0.05), while PASP significantly decreased by 5.20 mmHg over the same period (Fig. 4D, P < 0.01), indicating a clinically meaningful reduction in right ventricular afterload.

Discussion

This study focuses on the safety and feasibility of a rapid initiation and titration strategy for vericiguat, a novel sGC stimulator, in patients with HFrEF. Previous clinical and subgroup analyses have established the prognostic benefit and overall safety profile of vericiguat in patients with worsening HFrEF [4, 6–8], while evidence regarding its integration into a rapid GDMT optimization protocol remains limited. This prospective cohort study aimed to evaluate the tolerability of starting vericiguat at 5 mg and rapidly up-titrating to 10 mg within 14 days alongside concurrent GDMT adjustment. The results showed overall good tolerability of this rapid initiation and titration strategy. Using the conservative treated-population denominator, 131 of 140 patients (93.6%) achieved the target dose by day 14. Among the 133 patients with evaluable day-14 titration data, the corresponding per-protocol rate was 98.5%. Significant reductions in NT-proBNP and heart rate were observed following the 14-day titration period. Although the observed changes cannot be causally attributed to vericiguat alone because of concurrent background GDMT optimization, the 8.26% absolute increase in LVEF at 20 weeks suggests that this combined strategy was associated with reverse remodeling without compromising hemodynamic stability.

Although standard labeling recommends a starting dose of 2.5 mg, we adopted a 5 mg initiation strategy to accelerate the attainment of the target dose of 10 mg and mitigate clinical inertia. This rationale is supported by the recent VELOCITY study, which demonstrated that initiating vericiguat at 5 mg was safe and well tolerated in more than 90% of patients, thereby simplifying the titration regimen [12]. Regarding safety thresholds, our inclusion criteria were broader with respect to the lower bound of acceptable blood pressure compared with the pivotal trial. While the VICTORIA trial excluded patients with SBP < 100 mmHg [4], our study extended eligibility to patients with SBP ≥ 85 mmHg. This design deliberately included a hemodynamically more vulnerable subgroup with SBP between 85 and 100 mmHg, a population underrepresented in major trials. The ethical validity of this more intensive titration protocol was supported by close hemodynamic monitoring and concurrent GDMT optimization during the early post-decompensation phase.

Regarding hemodynamic safety, the application of vericiguat at 5 mg did not show a clinically significant decrease in blood pressure the following day. The observed changes in blood pressure after 14 days were likely a composite effect of vericiguat initiation and background GDMT optimization rather than vericiguat alone [2]. However, these hemodynamic fluctuations did not hinder the overall success of dose escalation. Using the treated-population denominator, the day-14 target-dose achievement rate was 93.6%, whereas the corresponding rate was 98.5% among evaluable patients. We report both values because the latter reflects a per-protocol completion rate and may overestimate tolerability if presented alone. Even with the more conservative treated-population denominator, the observed rate remained numerically high. However, cross-study comparisons with VICTORIA should be interpreted cautiously because of differences in cohort selection, baseline hemodynamic status, and analytic definitions [4]. Unlike VICTORIA, which excluded patients with SBP < 100 mmHg, our protocol excluded only those with SBP < 85 mmHg to ensure safety during rapid titration [4]. Importantly, because our cohort had a mean baseline SBP of 125.53 mmHg, the ease of titration and the 98.5% per-protocol success rate should be contextualized within this relatively stable baseline. Therefore, these findings reflect the feasibility of rapid vericiguat titration mainly in a clinically monitored and largely hemodynamically stable HFrEF cohort, and should not be overgeneralized to more vulnerable, truly hypotensive populations. This high tolerability does not suggest superior efficacy over the broader, higher-risk population in VICTORIA [4]. Nevertheless, considering the limited Asian and real-world vericiguat data, these findings provide practical evidence supporting the feasibility of a 5 mg starting dose in this specific clinical context [9, 11, 12].

In terms of safety, vericiguat did not lead to significant abnormalities in liver or kidney function or electrolytes, which is broadly consistent with safety findings from VICTORIA and related analyses [4, 7, 8]. Importantly, the addition of vericiguat did not induce symptomatic hypotension that would preclude background GDMT optimization in most patients. The observed reduction in heart rate suggests that beta-blockers could be optimized alongside vericiguat in this monitored cohort. This finding is consistent with the established prognostic relevance of heart rate control in HFrEF, although it should not be interpreted as evidence that vericiguat directly facilitates beta-blocker titration [13].

A critical finding of our study is the 8.26% absolute increase in LVEF after 20 weeks. Because regression to the mean and spontaneous or GDMT-related recovery may occur in single-arm heart failure cohorts [14], we performed an exploratory ANCOVA analysis adjusted for baseline LVEF. The adjusted least-squares mean increase in LVEF remained 7.45%, indicating that the within-cohort improvement was not solely driven by baseline LVEF imbalance. Nevertheless, this statistical adjustment cannot eliminate regression to the mean, account for natural history, or separate the specific contribution of vericiguat from concurrent GDMT optimization. Therefore, the observed structural improvement should be interpreted as a cumulative or synergistic package effect of rapid vericiguat initiation alongside intensified background GDMT, rather than as an isolated vericiguat-specific remodeling effect. To contextualize this magnitude of improvement, we compared our data with historical cohorts of comprehensive medical therapy. In the PROVE-HF trial, patients treated with sacubitril/valsartan showed a mean LVEF increase of 9.4% over 12 months [15], and the EMPA-TROPISM trial demonstrated an approximate 6% increase with empagliflozin [16]. Our observed 8.26% improvement over a shorter period of 20 weeks is numerically comparable to these landmark studies. Nevertheless, such cross-trial comparisons must be interpreted with extreme caution. Unlike randomized or comparator-based prospective studies, our single-arm, self-controlled design fundamentally precludes definitive attribution of the observed improvement to vericiguat. These findings suggest that rapid vericiguat initiation did not appear to impede reverse remodeling during GDMT optimization and may have contributed to the aggregate clinical response through the cGMP-PKG pathway, although this hypothesis cannot be confirmed without a controlled design [17]. Earlier clinical biomarker data from SOCRATES-REDUCED and mechanistic evidence on sGC signaling also support the biological plausibility of this pathway [18, 19]. Importantly, in alignment with the physiological effects of sGC stimulation, our data revealed significant improvements in right ventricular function, evidenced by an increase in TAPSE and a reduction in PASP at 20 weeks [20]. Vericiguat has pharmacological properties consistent with modulation of vascular tone through sGC-cGMP signaling [21]. This hemodynamic shift may reduce right-sided loading conditions and could interact with left ventricular reverse remodeling, although the clinical relevance of this mechanism requires further controlled evaluation [22].

Our study highlights the concept of cumulative efficacy under contemporary GDMT. The baseline optimization level of GDMT, with quadruple therapy used in 78.9% of patients, was relatively high compared with many real-world settings [1]. Consistent with the network meta-analysis by Tromp et al., which supports the aggregate benefit of combining distinct HFrEF drug classes [23], our findings indicate that vericiguat can be layered on top of optimized GDMT in selected and closely monitored patients. Beta-blockers remain central to HFrEF management, and heart rate control is prognostically relevant in chronic heart failure [13, 24]. In addition, contemporary GDMT requires careful renal monitoring, particularly when multiple disease-modifying therapies are optimized simultaneously [25]. Therefore, our renal safety findings should be interpreted as evidence of short-term tolerability under monitoring, rather than as evidence that SGLT2i enhances vericiguat clearance.

This study is subject to several limitations inherent to its design. First, as a single-center, single-arm, open-label prospective cohort without a placebo control group, the self-controlled nature of this study design constitutes a primary limitation. Consequently, the results cannot establish causality between vericiguat and the observed LVEF improvement. Although ANCOVA adjustment for baseline LVEF was performed, this approach cannot substitute for a randomized control group and cannot fully address regression to the mean, natural recovery, or the effects of concurrent GDMT optimization. Therefore, the observed hemodynamic and echocardiographic improvements should be interpreted as within-cohort changes associated with a comprehensive treatment strategy, rather than as vericiguat-specific effects. Second, although our inclusion criteria permitted patients with SBP as low as 85 mmHg, the resulting cohort had a mean SBP of 125.53 mmHg, representing a largely stable population. Consequently, our findings on 98.5% per-protocol tolerability, as well as the treated-population estimate of 93.6%, cannot be generalized to the most hypotensive and hemodynamically fragile subgroup of patients with HFrEF. Finally, this study focused on short-term surrogate endpoints; long-term clinical outcomes and objective exercise capacity, such as cardiopulmonary exercise testing, were not evaluated.

Conclusions

The initiation of Vericiguat at 5 mg followed by rapid titration demonstrated excellent safety and tolerability in this cohort of HFrEF patients. Our findings suggest that a strategy of rapid Vericiguat integration is feasible even while simultaneously optimizing other GDMT. This approach allows for the comprehensive management of residual risk without compromising hemodynamic stability, provided patients meet the minimum blood pressure criteria (SBP ≥ 85 mmHg). While the observed improvements in cardiac function likely reflect the cumulative benefit of the optimized regimen, this study provides crucial real-world evidence supporting the safety of high-dose initiation and rapid titration protocols in clinical practice. Ultimately, while this study serves as an excellent safety pilot within the Chinese clinical context, robust randomized controlled trials featuring a placebo control group—maintained on an identical GDMT optimization protocol—are imperative to completely isolate and confirm the specific contribution of vericiguat to reverse cardiac remodeling.

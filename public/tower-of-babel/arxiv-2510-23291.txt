Selmer ranks under quadratic twists satisfying the Heegner hypothesis
Alexandros Konstantinou

Abstract
. We investigate variations of Selmer ranks under quadratic twists satisfying the Heegner hypothesis. In particular, starting with an elliptic curve E / ℚ E/\mathbb{Q} with partial 2 2 -torsion and a common relaxed Selmer group, we derive explicit formulae describing the effect of twisting on Selmer ranks in terms of matrices over 𝔽 2 \mathbb{F}_{2} . As an application, we show that these formulae are compatible with predictions made by the parity conjecture.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Elliptic curves over quadratic extensions

A classical problem in the arithmetic of elliptic curves is to understand how the Mordell–Weil rank changes over quadratic extensions. To study this, consider an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
and a quadratic field
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
. The action of the nontrivial element
\sigma\in\Gal(K/\mathbb{Q})
naturally splits the group
E(K)
into
(\pm 1)
-eigenspaces, which correspond to
E(\mathbb{Q})
and the quadratic twist
E^{d}(\mathbb{Q})
. This decomposition yields the formula:

\rk E(K)=\rk E(\mathbb{Q})+\rk E^{d}(\mathbb{Q}).

Consequently, the problem of studying ranks over quadratic extensions is entirely encoded in the ranks of the quadratic twists
E^{d}/\mathbb{Q}
.

The study of quadratic twists lies at the heart of modern arithmetic statistics. Prominent among the conjectures in this area is that of Goldfeld, which predicts that within the quadratic twist family of an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
, half of the twists have analytic rank
0
and half have analytic rank
1
.
In a recent trilogy of papers
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
, it has been established that the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture implies Goldfeld’s conjecture, thereby linking two of the deepest conjectures in the arithmetic of elliptic curves.

All investigations of this kind typically proceed via the more accessible Selmer groups, since the computation of the Mordell–Weil rank is obscured by the mysterious nature of the Tate–Shafarevich group.
Early work includes
[
7
]
, which shows that for the congruent number curve
y^{2}=x^{3}-x
, the
2
-Selmer ranks of its quadratic twists follow a remarkably regular distribution.
Subsequent results
[
8
,
20
]
extend this to elliptic curves with full rational
2
-torsion and no cyclic
4
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
, while
[
12
]
establishes an analogous theorem over arbitrary number fields in the generic
2
-torsion case.
In sharp contrast, when
E(\mathbb{Q})[2]\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, corresponding to the intermediate case of partial
2
-torsion, no distribution function exists for the
2
-Selmer ranks within quadratic twist families
[
11
]
. More recently, similar questions have been investigated for higher-dimensional abelian varieties
[
15
,
29
,
30
]
.

Beyond statistical questions, quadratic extensions satisfying the
Heegner hypothesis
, meaning imaginary quadratic fields
K/\mathbb{Q}
in which every prime of bad reduction for
E
splits, offer a particularly rich setting for studying rational points.
For such
K
, the theory of Heegner points provides a systematic supply of rational points on
E
, arising from the modular parametrisation, and among these one distinguishes a canonical point
P_{K}\in E(K)
. The work of Gross–Zagier and Kolyvagin show that if
P_{K}
is non-torsion, then
\operatorname{rk}(E/K)=1
and
\Sha(E/K)
is finite.
To descend these results to
\mathbb{Q}
, one appeals to analytic work on
L
-functions in quadratic twist families
[
1
,
17
,
28
]
, yielding the strongest known case of the Birch and Swinnerton–Dyer conjecture: if the analytic rank of
E/\mathbb{Q}
satisfies
\mathrm{rk}_{\mathrm{an}}(E/\mathbb{Q})\leq 1
, then

\mathrm{rk}_{\mathrm{an}}(E/\mathbb{Q})=\operatorname{rk}E(\mathbb{Q})\quad\text{and}\quad\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})\ \text{is finite.}

This leads us to a natural and delicate intersection: considering quadratic extensions that satisfy the Heegner hypothesis for elliptic curves with partial
2
-torsion. In this specific scenario, the behaviour of the full
2
-Selmer group is governed by the interaction between the
\varphi
– and
\hat{\varphi}
–Selmer groups.

1.2.
Effect of twists satisfying the Heegner hypothesis

In what follows, we write
E/\mathbb{Q}
for an elliptic curve with
E(\mathbb{Q})[2]\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. We may and do assume that after a change of variables
E
is given by an integral Weierstrass model

y^{2}=x(x^{2}+ax+b)
with
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
.
The point
(0,0)
generates
E(\mathbb{Q})[2]
and defines a
2
–isogeny
\varphi:E\to E\textquoteright
, with dual isogeny
\hat{\varphi}:E\textquoteright\to E
.

Our main result focuses on twists by integers
d
arising from
\mathcal{D}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}
(see Definition
2.8
below). These all define imaginary quadratic fields satisfying the Heegner hypothesis for
E/\mathbb{Q}
. In particular, we give an explicit formula describing how the
\varphi
– and
\hat{\varphi}
–Selmer ranks change under such twists in terms of matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
built from the Legendre symbols of the primes dividing
d
.

The variation in Selmer ranks depends only on the sign of the discriminant
\Delta_{E}
, the coefficients
a,b
defining
E
, and on whether the “relaxed” Selmer group
\Sel_{\mathcal{S}_{d}}^{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q})
(see §
3.1
) contains a suitable integer – a condition that is straightforward to verify in practice (see Remark
5.3
).

Theorem 1.1

(= Thm.
5.2
, Cor.
5.7
)
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be given by the equation
y^{2}=x(x^{2}+ax+b)
, and let
d\in\mathcal{D}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}
.
Define

\delta_{\varphi}(d)=\mathrm{rk}_{\varphi}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})-\mathrm{rk}_{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q}),\qquad\delta_{\hat{\varphi}}(d)=\mathrm{rk}_{\hat{\varphi}}(E^{\prime-d}/\mathbb{Q})-\mathrm{rk}_{\hat{\varphi}}(E\textquoteright/\mathbb{Q}).

The values of
\delta_{\varphi}(d)
and
\delta_{\hat{\varphi}}(d)
are given in the following table.

\begin{array}[]{c|c|l}\hline\cr\delta_{\varphi}(d)&\delta_{\hat{\varphi}}(d)&\text{Conditions}\\
\hline\cr 0&\omega(d)&b<0\\[3.0pt]
\omega(d)&0&\Delta_{E}<0,\ b>0\\[4.0pt]
\omega(d)-1-\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\mathbf{A}_{d})&\omega(d)-\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\mathbf{A}_{d})&\Delta_{E}>0,\ b>0,\ a<0,\ \eta_{\varphi}(d)=1\\[4.0pt]
\omega(d)-\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\widehat{\mathbf{A}_{d}})&\omega(d)+1-\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\widehat{\mathbf{A}_{d}})&\Delta_{E}>0,\ b>0,\ a<0,\ \eta_{\varphi}(d)=0\\[4.0pt]
\omega(d)+1-\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{A}_{d}})&\omega(d)-\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\widetilde{\mathbf{A}_{d}})&\Delta_{E}>0,\ b>0,\ a>0,\ \eta_{\varphi}(d)=1\\[4.0pt]
\omega(d)-\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\overline{\mathbf{A}_{d}})&\omega(d)-1-\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\overline{\mathbf{A}_{d}})&\Delta_{E}>0,\ b>0,\ a>0,\ \eta_{\varphi}(d)=0\\[2.0pt]
\hline\cr\end{array}

Here,
\omega(d)
denotes the number of prime factors of
d
, and all matrices
\mathbf{A}_{d}
,
\widetilde{\mathbf{A}_{d}}
,
\widehat{\mathbf{A}_{d}}
and
\overline{\mathbf{A}_{d}}
are defined over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
in Notation
5.1
.

1.3.
Structure of the argument

Given a
2
–isogeny
\varphi:E\to E^{\prime}
, the Selmer group
\Sel^{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q})

is defined as the subgroup of
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},E[\varphi])
defined by the certain local conditions at each place of
\mathbb{Q}
.
In particular, for each place
v
, let

\delta_{\varphi,v}\colon E^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{v})/\varphi(E(\mathbb{Q}_{v}))\to H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},E[\varphi])

be the local Kummer map, and write

\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,v}(E)\ :=\ \operatorname{im}\bigl(\delta_{\varphi,v}\bigr)\ \subseteq\ H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},E[\varphi]).

Then the
\varphi
–Selmer group is the subgroup of
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},E[\varphi]
cut out by these local subgroups:

\Sel^{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q})=\left\{\,c\in H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},E[\varphi]):\operatorname{res}_{v}(c)\in\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,v}(E)\text{ for all }v\,\right\}.

Thus, the effect of twisting on
\Sel^{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q})
is determined entirely by how the local subgroups
\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,v}(E)
change under quadratic twisting.
To study this, we consider certain
relaxed
versions of Selmer groups, obtained by omitting the conditions at the primes dividing
d
and at infinity:

\Sel_{\mathcal{S}_{d}}^{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q})=\{\,c\in H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},E[\varphi]):\operatorname{res}_{v}(c)\in\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,v}(E)\text{ for all }v\nmid d\infty\,\},

and similarly for
E^{-d}
. Under the natural isomorphism
E[\varphi]\cong E^{-d}[\varphi]
, the groups

\Sel^{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q})
,
\Sel^{\varphi}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})
, and their relaxed variants all embed in the same cohomology space
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},E[\varphi])
. As subgroups of this cohomology group, one has

\Sel_{\mathcal{S}_{d}}^{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q})=\Sel_{\mathcal{S}_{d}}^{\varphi}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q}),

which is entirely natural: since
E/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
are isomorphic for all
p\mid 2N_{E}
, and have good reduction at every prime not dividing
d
and the real place, their local conditions coincide at all places defining the relaxed Selmer groups. Consequently, any discrepancy between
\Sel^{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q})
and
\Sel^{\varphi}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})
can only arise from the primes dividing
d
and infinity.

To this end, we take the common relaxed Selmer group

\Sel_{\mathcal{S}_{d}}^{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q})=\Sel_{\mathcal{S}_{d}}^{\varphi}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})

as a common starting point, and successively impose the remaining local conditions at the places
v\mid d\infty
.
By tracking how these local conditions change under twisting (see Lemmas
2.13
and
2.16
),
we deduce the effect that the quadratic twist has on the Selmer group.
To achieve this, we introduce the homomorphisms

\varepsilon_{\infty},\ \lambda_{p},\ \gamma_{p}:\mathbb{Q}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}\to\mathbb{F}_{2},

which capture the effect of imposing the local conditions at the remaining primes.

To make this concrete, consider the case
b<0
.
Per Lemmas
2.13
and
2.16
, the effect of twisting on the local conditions is as follows: for each prime
q\mid d
, the local subgroup at
q
for
E^{-d}
is trivial, whereas for
E
it is the unramified subgroup
H^{1}_{\textup{ur}}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},E[\varphi])
; at the real place, both local subgroups are trivial.
Hence, starting from the common relaxed Selmer group
\Sel^{\varphi}_{\mathcal{S}_{d}}(E/\mathbb{Q})
,
the usual Selmer groups
\Sel^{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q})
and
\Sel^{\varphi}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})

are recovered by performing the following steps at the places
v\mid d\infty
:

\begin{array}[]{|c|c|l|}\hline\cr\text{Curve}&\text{Place}&\text{Operation}\\
\hline\cr E&q\mid d&\text{intersect with }\ker(\lambda_{q})\\
&\infty&\text{intersect with }\ker(\varepsilon_{\infty})\\
\hline\cr E^{-d}&q\mid d&\text{intersect with }\ker(\lambda_{q})\cap\ker(\gamma_{q})\\
&\infty&\text{intersect with }\ker(\varepsilon_{\infty})\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

This procedure yields an explicit description of
\Sel^{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q})
and
\Sel^{\varphi}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})

in terms of the common relaxed group
\Sel^{\varphi}_{\mathcal{S}_{d}}(E/\mathbb{Q})

(see Theorem
4.2
),
thereby reducing the study of Selmer–rank variation to a problem in linear algebra over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. We note that this method of expressing Selmer conditions in terms of matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
and analysing their variation under twisting fits into a broader framework developed in earlier work. We note that the use of matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
to describe Selmer conditions and their variation under twisting has appeared in earlier works, including
[
7
]
and
[
9
]
, which treat, respectively, the congruent number curve and elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
with rational partial
2
–torsion.

1.4.
Parity conjecture and twisting

The Birch and Swinnerton–Dyer conjecture predicts that the algebraic and analytic ranks of an elliptic curve
E
over a number field
F
coincide.
Writing
\Lambda(E,s)
for the completed
L
–function of
E/F
, it is expected that
\Lambda(E,s)
extends analytically to
\mathbb{C}
and satisfies the functional equation

\Lambda(E,2-s)=\omega(E/F)\Lambda(E,s),

where
\omega(E/F)=\prod_{v}\omega_{v}(E/F)
is the product of local root numbers.
Assuming analytic continuation, the Birch and Swinnerton–Dyer conjecture then implies the
parity conjecture
.

Conjecture 1.2

(Parity conjecture)
.

For an elliptic curve
E
over a number field
F
,

(-1)^{\mathrm{rk}(E/F)}=w(E/F).

Progress towards this conjecture has come through its
p
-primary refinement, the
p
-parity conjecture
, obtained by replacing the algebraic rank with the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-corank of the
p^{\infty}
-Selmer group.
This version is known to hold for all primes
p
and all elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}

[
5
, Thm. 1.4]
.

Quadratic twisting provides a natural setting in which to study parity phenomena.
A central question in this direction was formulated by Kramer and Tunnell
[
13
, Conj. 3.1]
,
who conjectured a relation between local root numbers and local arithmetic data,
specifically Tamagawa numbers.
This conjecture was later resolved for elliptic curves in
[
6
, Cor. 4.8]
,
thereby establishing the
2
-parity conjecture for elliptic curves over quadratic extensions:
for any quadratic extension
L/F
and any elliptic curve
E/F
,

(1.3)

(-1)^{\mathrm{rk}_{2^{\infty}}(E/L)}=w(E/L).

An analogue of this result for Jacobians of hyperelliptic curves, under mild assumptions,
was subsequently obtained in
[
16
]
.
In particular, if
L=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})
satisfies the Heegner hypothesis for
E/\mathbb{Q}
,
then a root number computation gives
w(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})=-w(E/\mathbb{Q})
.
In light of (
1.3
), this implies that

\mathrm{rk}_{2^{\infty}}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})-\mathrm{rk}_{2^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q})\equiv 1\ \textup{mod }{2},

and consequently

(1.4)

\mathrm{rk}_{2}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})-\mathrm{rk}_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})\equiv 1\ \textup{mod }{2},

(see Theorem
6.4
). When
E(\mathbb{Q})=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, variations in
2
-Selmer ranks under twisting are controlled by the
\varphi
- and
\hat{\varphi}
-Selmer ranks.
For each square-free integer
d
, set

\delta_{\varphi}(d)=\mathrm{rk}_{\varphi}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})-\mathrm{rk}_{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q}),\qquad\delta_{\hat{\varphi}}(d)=\mathrm{rk}_{\hat{\varphi}}(E^{\prime-d}/\mathbb{Q})-\mathrm{rk}_{\hat{\varphi}}(E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}).

It is then natural to ask whether the explicit formulae for
\delta_{\varphi}(d),\delta_{\hat{\varphi}}(d)
detailed in Theorem
1.1
are consistent with the parity relation in (
1.4
).
The answer is affirmative: we show that for any
d\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}
(not necessarily
d\in\mathcal{D}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}
), the combined variation
\delta_{\varphi}(d)+\delta_{\hat{\varphi}}(d)
captures precisely the parity shift in
2
–Selmer ranks (see Proposition
6.6
):

(1.5)

\mathrm{rk}_{2}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})-\mathrm{rk}_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})\equiv\delta_{\varphi}(d)+\delta_{\hat{\varphi}}(d)\quad\textup{mod }{2}.

Moreover, when
d\in\mathcal{D}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}
the explicit expressions in Theorem
1.1
satisfy

\delta_{\varphi}(d)+\delta_{\hat{\varphi}}(d)\equiv 1\ \textup{mod }{2},

independently of the matrix ranks. As a result, we recover exactly the parity shift predicted by the parity conjecture for quadratic twists satisfying the Heegner hypothesis. At the level of Tate–Shafarevich groups, the compatibility detailed in (
1.5
) gives rise to a symmetric relation between the
\varphi
- and
\hat{\varphi}
-kernels.

Corollary 1.6

(= Corollary
6.11
)
.

Let
d
be a square-free integer, and assume that
\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d}))[2^{\infty}]
is finite. Then

\frac{\#\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[\varphi]}{\#\Sha(E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})[\hat{\varphi}]}\equiv\frac{\#\Sha(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})[\varphi]}{\#\Sha(E^{\prime-d}/\mathbb{Q})[\hat{\varphi}]}\quad\textup{mod }{\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}}.

If this congruence fails for some
d
, then at least one of
\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[2^{\infty}]
or
\Sha(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})[2^{\infty}]
must be infinite.

2.
Selmer groups and local conditions

We briefly recall the construction of the
\varphi
-Selmer group.

2.1.
Selmer groups and local subgroups

There is a
2
–isogeny
\varphi:E\to E^{\prime}
, given by
(x,y)\mapsto(y^{2}/x^{2},\,y(b-x^{2})/x^{2})
with kernel
E[\varphi]=\langle(0,0)\rangle
, where
E^{\prime}:y^{2}=x^{3}-2ax^{2}+(a^{2}-4b)x
. Then,
\varphi(E[2])=\langle(0,0)\rangle
, where
(0,0)
is now a point in
E^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})
.

This yields the short exact sequence
0\to E[\varphi]\to E[2]\xrightarrow{\varphi}\varphi(E[2])\to 0
, and hence a connecting map
\delta_{\varphi}:E^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})/\varphi(E(\mathbb{Q}))\to H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},C)
.
Identifying
E[\varphi]\cong\mu_{2}
via
(0,0)\mapsto-1
gives an isomorphism
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},E[\varphi])\cong\mathbb{Q}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}
and a homomorphism
\mu_{\varphi}:E^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})/\varphi(E(\mathbb{Q}))\to\mathbb{Q}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}
determined by

(2.1)

\mu_{\varphi}(P)=\begin{cases}x,&\text{if }P=(x,y)\text{ with }x\neq 0,\\[6.0pt]
\Delta_{E},&\text{if }P=(0,0),\end{cases}

where
\Delta_{E}
is the discriminant of
E/\mathbb{Q}
(see
[
21
, Prop. X.4.9]
). As discussed in §
1.3
, this construction carries over locally. For each place
v
of
\mathbb{Q}
there is a connecting homomorphism

(2.2)

\delta_{\varphi,v}:E^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{v})/\varphi(E(\mathbb{Q}_{v}))\to H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},E[\varphi]),\qquad\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,v}(E/\mathbb{Q}):=\operatorname{im}(\delta_{\varphi,v}),

whose image defines the distinguished local condition

\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,v}(E/\mathbb{Q})\subseteq H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},E[\varphi])
. Via the canonical identification

H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},E[\varphi])\cong\mathbb{Q}_{v}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}_{v}^{\times 2}
,
these can be viewed as subgroups of
\mathbb{Q}_{v}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}_{v}^{\times 2}
.
The
\varphi
–Selmer group is then given by

\Sel^{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q})=\bigcap_{v}\ker\mskip-3.0mu\left(H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},E[\varphi])\to H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},E[\varphi])/\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,v}(E/\mathbb{Q})\right).

As a result, determining the
\varphi
–Selmer group amounts to understanding the local subgroups
\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,v}(E)
for all places
v
simultaneously.
For odd places
v
of good reduction, these are described by the following classical result.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
3
, Lemma 4.1]
)
.

Suppose that
E/\mathbb{Q}
has good reduction at an odd prime
p
.
Under the identifications

H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},E[\varphi])\cong\mathbb{Q}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}

and

H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},E[\varphi])\cong\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times 2}
,
the restriction map

H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},E[\varphi])\to H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},E[\varphi])\,/\,\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,p}(E)

is identified with the homomorphism

\alpha\mapsto p^{\,v_{p}(\alpha)}\bmod\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times 2}.

Proof.

Since
E/\mathbb{Q}
has good reduction at
p
, the local condition is the unramified subgroup

\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,p}(E/\mathbb{Q})=H^{1}_{\mathrm{ur}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},E[\varphi])

[
3
, Lemma 4.1]
.
Via the identification
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},E[\varphi])\cong\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times 2}
,
the unramified subgroup identifies with the cyclic subgroup generated by a non-square unit in
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}
.
Under the identification
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},E[\varphi])\cong\mathbb{Q}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}
,
the restriction map on cohomology is the homomorphism

\alpha\bmod\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}\mapsto\alpha\bmod\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times 2}
.
Modulo the unramified subgroup, this becomes
\alpha\mapsto p^{v_{p}(\alpha)}\ \textup{mod }\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times 2}.
∎

2.2.
Variation of
\varphi
–Selmer groups under quadratic twisting

We now let
d
be a squarefree integer, and write
E^{d}
for the quadratic twist of
E/\mathbb{Q}
by
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
.
The curve
E^{d}
admits an affine model

Via the identification of (

2.3.
Twisting by imaginary quadratic fields satisfying the Heegner hypothesis

For the remainder of this paper, we will focus on the effect on Selmer groups after twisting by
-d
,
for
d
belonging to the following sets.

Definition 2.8
.

We define

\mathcal{Q}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}=\Bigl\{\,q\text{ prime}:v\text{ splits in }\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-q})\text{ for every }v\mid 2N_{E}\,\Bigr\},

and

\mathcal{D}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}=\Bigl\{\,d=\prod_{i=1}^{r}q_{i}\ :\ r\geq 1\text{ odd},\ q_{i}\in\mathcal{Q}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}\ \text{distinct}\Bigr\}.

Equivalently,
\mathcal{Q}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}
is the set of primes
q
with
q\equiv 7\ \textup{mod }{8}
and

\bigl(\tfrac{-q}{p}\bigr)=1
for every odd prime
p
where
E/\mathbb{Q}
has bad reduction. It follows that
\mathcal{Q}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}
is non-empty (in fact, infinite). Similarly, for any

, all primes

split in the quadratic extension
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})/\mathbb{Q}
. In other words, the setting considered here is consistent with the

considered in §

.
In order to understand how
\varphi
–Selmer groups vary under quadratic twisting,
it is necessary to study how the local subgroups

\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,v}(E)
change after passing to the quadratic twist
E^{-d}
.
The following lemma shows that, away from the primes dividing
2N_{E}d\infty

the local conditions remain unchanged.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
d\in\mathcal{D}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}
.
Under the natural identification

E[\varphi]\cong E^{-d}[\varphi]
, the local conditions

\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,v}(E)
and
\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,v}(E^{-d})
coincide as subgroups of

H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},E[\varphi])
for every
v\mid 2N_{E}
and every finite place
v\nmid 2dN_{E}
.

Proof.

If
v\mid 2N_{E}
, then by the definition of
\mathcal{D}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}
,
v
splits in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})
.
Hence
E\cong E^{-d}
and
E^{\prime}\cong(E^{-d})^{\prime}
over
\mathbb{Q}_{v}
. The local Kummer maps for
E
and
E^{-d}
then identify under this isomorphism, so that
\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,v}(E)=\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,v}(E^{-d})
whenever
v\mid 2N_{E}
.
If
v\nmid 2N_{E}d\infty
, then
v
is an odd prime and both
E
and
E^{\prime}
have good reduction at
v
. By
[
3
, Lemma 4.1]
, the local subgroups coincide with the unramified subgroup
H^{1}_{\mathrm{ur}}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},E[\varphi])
and
H^{1}_{\mathrm{ur}}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},E^{-d}[\varphi])
respectively. Since the isomorphism
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},E^{-d}[\varphi])\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},E[\varphi])
of Lemma
2.7
carries
H_{\mathrm{ur}}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},E^{-d}[\varphi])
onto
H_{\mathrm{ur}}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},E[\varphi])
, which proves the second assertion. ∎

In view of Lemma

, any variation in

–Selmer groups under twisting by

can only arise from the local conditions at the places

and
\infty
.

2.4.
Twisting local conditions when

We now turn to the local conditions at the primes dividing

.
In particular, we express the local Selmer conditions explicitly
in terms of the signs of

and
\Delta_{E}
in any integral Weierstrass model for

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be given by a Weierstrass integral model

y^{2}=x\bigl(x^{2}+ax+b\bigr)
with
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then, for every
q\in\mathcal{Q}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}
one has

\Bigl(\tfrac{b}{q}\Bigr)=\begin{cases}+1,&b>0,\\
-1,&b<0,\end{cases}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Bigl(\tfrac{\Delta_{E}}{q}\Bigr)=\begin{cases}+1,&\Delta_{E}>0,\\
-1,&\Delta_{E}<0,\end{cases}

independently of the chosen model for
E/\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

The
2
-isogenous curve
E^{\prime}
admits an affine model of the form

y^{2}=x(x^{2}-2ax+a^{2}-4b)
. Therefore,
16b(a^{2}-4b)^{2}
is, up to a square in
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
, a minimal discriminant for
E^{\prime}
.
Hence
b\equiv(\Delta_{E^{\prime}})_{\textup{min}}\bmod\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}
. Since
E
and
E^{\prime}
have the same set of bad primes, then
(\Delta_{E^{\prime}})_{\textup{min}}
is supported only on primes dividing
N_{E}
.
Let
q\in\mathcal{Q}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}
.
By quadratic reciprocity,

(\frac{(\Delta_{E^{\prime}})_{\textup{min}}}{q})=(\frac{\pm 1}{q}),
depending on the sign of the discriminant.
Since
b
and
(\Delta_{E^{\prime}})_{\textup{min}}
have the same sign, then
(\frac{b}{q})=(\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(b)}{q}).

As
q\equiv 7\bmod 8
, the result follows. Finally, since
\Delta_{E}\equiv(\Delta_{E})_{\textup{min}}\bmod\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}
, the same reasoning applies with
b
replaced by
\Delta_{E}
.
∎

To describe the resulting change in the local conditions, we introduce the following notation.
We recall that for each place
v
of
\mathbb{Q}
, the
quadratic Hilbert symbol

(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)_{v}:\;\mathbb{Q}_{v}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}_{v}^{\times 2}\times\mathbb{Q}_{v}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}_{v}^{\times 2}\longrightarrow\mu_{2}

is the symmetric bilinear pairing defined by

(\alpha,\beta)_{v}=1
if and only if

is a norm from
\mathbb{Q}_{v}(\sqrt{\beta})
.

Notation 2.11
.

Assume
b>0
and
\Delta_{E}>0
, and fix

.

(1)

For every prime

, Lemma

gives

\bigl(\tfrac{b}{q}\bigr)=\bigl(\tfrac{\Delta_{E}}{q}\bigr)=1

which in turn ensure that the polynomial

(2)

We define

Remark 2.12
.

We note the following:

(1)

The sets

are independent of the chosen model

of

Indeed, any other model of the same form satisfies

In addition, the sets

Therefore, if

Lemma 2.13
.

For each prime

(ii)

Proof.

By
[
10

\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,q}(E^{-d})\;=\;(E^{-d})^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{q})[2]\big/{\varphi}\mskip-3.0mu\ (E^{-d}(\mathbb{Q}_{q})[2]).

3.
Truncated structures and modified Selmer groups

In this section we introduce a collection of modified Selmer structures on
E[\varphi]
and
E^{-d}[\varphi]
.
These auxiliary structures provide a convenient framework for studying how Selmer groups vary under quadratic twisting.

3.1.
Truncated and sign–modified Selmer structures

We begin by recalling the relevant definitions and formulating the truncated Selmer structures that will be used in the sequel.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
M
be a finite
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
–module annihilated by
2
.
Thus
M
is a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{2}
–vector space. A
Selmer structure

\mathcal{L}=\{\mathcal{L}_{v}\}_{v}
for
M
is a collection of subspaces

\mathcal{L}_{v}\;\subseteq\;H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},M)

for each place
v
of
\mathbb{Q}
, such that
\mathcal{L}_{v}
is
H^{1}_{\mathrm{ur}}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},M):=\ker\mskip-3.0mu\left(H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},M)\to H^{1}(I_{v},M)\right)
for all but finitely many
v
, where
I_{v}\subset\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{v}/\mathbb{Q}_{v})

denotes the inertia subgroup. The
Selmer group associated to
\mathcal{L}
is then defined as

\Sel^{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{Q},M)\;=\;\bigcap_{v}\ker\mskip-3.0mu\Bigl(H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},M)\to H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},M)/\mathcal{L}_{v}\Bigr).

As a standard example, we have the usual
\varphi
–Selmer group. In particular for
E/\mathbb{Q}
with partial
2
-torsion, then the local subgroups

\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,v}
of (
2.2
) form a Selmer structure for
E[\varphi]
, and the resulting Selmer group is precisely the classical
\varphi
–Selmer group
\Sel^{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q})
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\{\mathcal{L}_{v}\}_{v}
be a Selmer structure on a finite

\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
–module
M
, and let
S
be a finite set of places of
\mathbb{Q}
.
We define the
truncated Selmer structure with respect to
S
, denoted

\mathcal{L}_{S}=\{\mathcal{L}_{S,v}\}_{v}

\mathcal{L}_{S,v}=\begin{cases}H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},M),&\text{if }v\in S,\\[4.0pt]
\mathcal{L}_{v},&\text{if }v\notin S.\end{cases}

We denote by
\Sel^{\mathcal{L}}_{S}(M/\mathbb{Q})
the associated Selmer group, which satisfies

\Sel^{\mathcal{L}}_{S}(\mathbb{Q},M)=\bigcap_{v\notin S}\ker\mskip-3.0mu\left(H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},M)\to H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{v},M)\big/\mathcal{L}_{v}\right).

The truncated Selmer group serves as an intermediate object in the computation of the usual Selmer group, obtained by temporarily omitting the local conditions at the places in
S
.
In particular, the usual Selmer group is obtained once the local subgroups
\mathcal{L}_{v}
for
v\in S
are reinstated.
As we will see, this formulation is convenient for isolating and studying the contribution of individual primes to the Selmer group structure.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\{\mathcal{L}_{v}\}_{v}
be a Selmer structure on a finite

\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
–module
M
.
We define the Selmer structure

\mathcal{L}^{+}=\{\mathcal{L}^{+}_{v}\}_{v}
by

\mathcal{L}^{+}_{v}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{v},&v\neq\infty,\\[4.0pt]
\{0\},&v=\infty,\end{cases}

where
\{1\}
denotes the trivial subgroup of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R},M)
.
The corresponding Selmer group is denoted

\Sel^{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{Q},M)^{+}\;:=\;\Sel^{\mathcal{L}^{+}}(\mathbb{Q},M).

Remark 3.4

When

, any Selmer group

can be identified with a subspace of

.
Under this identification, the modified group

\Sel^{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{Q},\mu_{2})^{+}
is the subgroup of
\Sel^{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{Q},\mu_{2})
consisting of those square classes admitting a positive representative in
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
.
This interpretation motivates the superscript “

” in the notation.

In the sequel we restrict to the case

,
so that

. To this end, we fix the following notation.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
S
be a finite set of place for
\mathbb{Q}
. We define
truncated
\varphi
-Selmer group with respect to
S
, denoted
\Sel^{\varphi}_{S}(E/\mathbb{Q})

\mathcal{L}_{S}
considered with
\mathcal{L}=\{\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,v}(E/\mathbb{Q})\}_{v}
.

We define
truncated
\varphi
-Selmer group with respect to
S
, denoted
\Sel^{\varphi}_{S}(E/\mathbb{Q})
, to be the truncated Selmer group

\Sel^{\mathcal{L}}_{S}(\mathbb{Q},E[\varphi])
of Definition
3.5
,
considered with respect to

\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,v}(E/\mathbb{Q})
of (

) at each place

of
\mathbb{Q}
.

To describe and compare the local conditions defining the

–Selmer groups of

–valued homomorphisms on

Definition 3.6
.

We define homomorphisms

The

3.2.
Linear–algebraic description of Selmer conditions

In this subsection, we study the dimensions of subspaces of

In the sequel, this will allow us to reduce the study of how Selmer ranks vary under quadratic twisting to a problem in linear algebra over

Notation 3.9
.

Let

–subspace.
We call a finite set

With such a choice of

, we make the following constructions:

(i)

We define an injective map

\iota:V\to\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\Sigma\cup\{\infty\}},\qquad\alpha\mapsto\bigl(\varepsilon_{\infty}(\alpha),(\lambda_{p}(\alpha))_{p\in\Sigma}\bigr).

(ii)

For a finite prime

(not necessarily in
\Sigma
), we introduce the row vectors

\mathbf{e}^{(q)}=(e^{(q)}_{\infty},(e^{(q)}_{p})_{p\in\Sigma}),\qquad\mathbf{c}^{(q)}=(c^{(q)}_{\infty},(c^{(q)}_{p})_{p\in\Sigma})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\Sigma\cup\{\infty\}},

whose entries are given by

\displaystyle e^{(q)}_{\infty}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle c^{(q)}_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0,&(\tfrac{-1}{q})=+1,\\[2.0pt]
1,&(\tfrac{-1}{q})=-1,\end{cases}

\displaystyle e^{(q)}_{p}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1,&p=q,\\[2.0pt]
0,&p\neq q,\end{cases}

\displaystyle c^{(q)}_{p}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0,&p=q\text{ or }(\tfrac{p}{q})=+1,\\[2.0pt]
1,&(\tfrac{p}{q})=-1.\end{cases}

Definition 3.10
.

Let

Lemma 3.11

Let

4.1.

We begin by fixing some notation.

Notation 4.1

For

Theorem 4.2
.

Before stating the main result, we introduce the following block matrices that describe the effect of twisting on Selmer ranks.

Notation 5.1

We write

for the

For any matrix

its complement, obtained by interchanging

With rows and columns ordered by

\mathbf{A}_{d}:=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{A}_{++}&\mathbf{A}_{+-}\\[2.0pt]
\mathbf{A}_{-+}&\mathbf{A}_{--}+\mathbf{I}_{r_{2}}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathrm{Mat}_{r\times r}(\mathbb{F}_{2}),

where each block

Consequently, for the off–diagonal blocks one has

\mathbf{A}_{-,+}\;=\;\overline{\bigl(\mathbf{A}_{+,-}\bigr)^{\mskip-3.0mu\textup{Tr}}}.

In the sequel we also use the block matrices

\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d}:=\bigl[\,\mathbf{1}_{r\times 1}\;\big|\;\mathbf{A}_{d}\bigr]\;\in\;\mathrm{Mat}_{r\times(1+r)}(\mathbb{F}_{2}),\qquad\widehat{\mathbf{A}_{d}}:=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{1}_{1\times r}\\[2.0pt]

In addition, we define an auxiliary invariant

which takes the value

•

Theorem 5.2

(iii)

,

, and

\displaystyle\operatorname{rk}_{\varphi}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})=\operatorname{rk}_{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q})+\omega(d)-\begin{cases}1+\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}(\mathbf{A}_{d}),&\text{if }\eta_{\varphi}(d)=1,\\[4.0pt]

Case (i):

\begin{bmatrix}1&\mathbf{0}_{1\times r_{1}}&\mathbf{0}_{1\times r_{2}}&\mathbf{0}_{1\times p}\\[2.0pt]
\mathbf{0}_{r_{1}\times 1}&\mathbf{I}_{r_{1}}&\mathbf{0}_{r_{1}\times r_{2}}&\mathbf{0}_{r_{1}\times p}\\[2.0pt]
\mathbf{0}_{r_{2}\times 1}&\mathbf{0}_{r_{2}\times r_{1}}&\mathbf{I}_{r_{2}}&\mathbf{0}_{r_{2}\times p}\end{bmatrix}.

Arguing as in part (iii), the rank of this matrix on
V
is
\,\omega(d)\,
if
\eta_{\varphi}(d)=0
and
\,\omega(d)+1\,
if
\eta_{\varphi}(d)=1
. Therefore,

\textup{rk}_{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q})=\dim V-\omega(d)-\big(1\text{ if }\eta_{\varphi}(d)=1,\ \text{else }0\big)
. Adding on, by Theorem
4.2
(iv) the dimension of
\Sel^{\varphi}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})
is given as the kernel of

\mathbf{M}:=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{1}_{r_{1}\times 1}&\mathbf{A}_{+,+}&\mathbf{A}_{+,-}&\mathbf{0}_{r_{1}\times p}\\[4.0pt]
\mathbf{1}_{r_{2}\times 1}&\mathbf{A}_{-,+}&\mathbf{A}_{-,-}+\mathbf{I}_{r_{2}}&\mathbf{0}_{r_{2}\times p}\end{bmatrix},

restricted on
V
.
Arguing as in part (iii), if
\eta_{\phi}(d)=1
then

\rk_{\varphi}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})\;=\;\dim V-\textup{rank}\bigl(\widetilde{\mathbf{A}}_{d}\bigr).
If
\eta_{\phi}(d)=0
then
j(V)=j(V_{0})
, and the image of
\mathbf{M}
on
j(V)
is the column space of
\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{A}_{d}:=\overline{\mathbf{A}_{d}}.

Writing the
i
th column of
\mathbf{A}_{d}
as
\mathbf{a}_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\,r}
and
\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{\,r}
for the all-ones vector, then

\mathbf{A}_{d}(e_{\infty}+e_{i})\;=\;\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{a}_{i}\quad(1\leq i\leq r),

so the image on
j(V)
is the column space of
\mathbf{A}_{d}+\mathbf{1}
. To conclude,

\rk_{\varphi}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}V-\begin{cases}\textup{rank}\bigl(\widetilde{\mathbf{A}_{d}}\bigr),&\text{if }\eta_{\varphi}(d)=1,\\[4.0pt]
\textup{rank}\bigl(\overline{\mathbf{A}_{d}}\bigr),&\text{if }\eta_{\varphi}(d)=0.\end{cases}

This completes the proof in all 4 cases. ∎

Remark 5.3
.

We conclude with a brief remark on the invariant
\eta_{\varphi}(d)
from Theorem
5.2
.
To find its value, one checks whether there exists a squarefree integer

\alpha=\pm\mskip-3.0mu\mskip-3.0mu\prod_{q\in T}q,\qquad T\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{d},

with the sign chosen according to the parity of
|T|
, satisfying the local conditions at all
p\nmid d\infty
. We note that verifying this condition is straightforward in practice. In particular, it is usually enough to test whether
-1\in\Sel_{\mathcal{S}_{d}}^{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q})
, since this already implies
\eta_{\varphi}(d)=1
.
At odd places of good reduction the local subgroups are unramified, so the condition is automatic for
-1
;
if
p\equiv 1\ \textup{mod }4
then
-1\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times 2}
,
and at primes of split multiplicative reduction of type
I_{n}
with
n
odd one has
\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,p}=H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},E[\varphi])
(see the proof of
[
22
, Lemma 3.4]
),
so again no restriction arises from such places.

5.2.
Variations in
\hat{\varphi}
–Selmer ranks

We now turn to the effect of quadratic twisting on
\hat{\varphi}
–Selmer ranks.
By Theorem
5.2
, the behaviour of the
\varphi
–Selmer ranks under twisting is already completely explicit.
An analogous description for the dual isogeny follows by arguing as in Corollary
4.4
, now expressed in terms of the auxiliary invariant
\eta_{\hat{\varphi}}(d)
.
This formulation, however, is less convenient, since one typically seeks a unified relation governing both
\varphi
– and
\hat{\varphi}
–Selmer ranks.
To obtain this, we appeal to Cassels’ formula to express both
\delta_{\varphi}(d)
and
\delta_{\hat{\varphi}}(d)
solely in terms of
\Sel_{\mathcal{S}_{d}}^{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q})
.

Theorem 5.4

(Cassels,
[
3
]
)
.

For an isogeny
\varphi:E\to E^{\prime}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, one has

\mathcal{T}(E/E^{\prime})\;=\;\prod_{v}\frac{\#\,\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,v}(E)}{2}.

where
\mathcal{T}(E/E^{\prime})\;=\;\frac{\#\,\Sel^{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q})}{\#\,\Sel^{\hat{\varphi}}(E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})}
, and the product runs over all places
v
of
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

This follows by combining Theorem 1.1 with equations (1.22) and (3.4) in
[
3
]
.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

For
d\in\mathcal{D}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}
, one has

\mathcal{T}(E^{-d}/E^{\prime-d})\;=\;\mathcal{T}(E/E^{\prime})\,\biggl(\frac{\#\,\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,q}(E^{-d})}{\#\,\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,q}(E)}\biggr)^{\mskip-3.0mu\omega(d)}\,\frac{\#\,\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,\infty}(E^{-d})}{\#\,\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,\infty}(E)},

where
q
is any prime factor of
d
.

Proof.

We compute directly:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(E^{-d}/E^{\prime-d})=\;

\displaystyle\Biggl(\prod_{v\nmid d\infty}\frac{\#\,\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,v}(E^{-d})}{2}\Biggr)\Biggl(\prod_{q\mid d}\frac{\#\,\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,q}(E^{-d})}{2}\Biggr)\Biggl(\frac{\#\,\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,\infty}(E^{-d})}{2}\Biggr)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textup{Lem.~\ref{eq_local_conditions}}}}{{=}}\;

\displaystyle\Biggl(\prod_{v\nmid d\infty}\frac{\#\,\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,v}(E)}{2}\Biggr)\Biggl(\prod_{q\mid d}\frac{\#\,\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,q}(E^{-d})}{2}\Biggr)\Biggl(\frac{\#\,\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,\infty}(E^{-d})}{2}\Biggr)

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(E/E^{\prime})\Biggl(\prod_{q\mid d}\frac{\#\,\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,q}(E^{-d})}{\#\,\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,q}(E)}\Biggr)\Biggl(\frac{\#\,\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,\infty}(E^{-d})}{\#\,\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,\infty}(E)}\Biggr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{T}(E/E^{\prime})\Biggl(\frac{\#\,\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,q}(E^{-d})}{2}\Biggr)^{\mskip-3.0mu\omega(d)}\Biggl(\frac{\#\,\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,\infty}(E^{-d})}{\#\,\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,\infty}(E)}\Biggr).

To obtain the last equality, we combine two observations:
first, by Lemma
2.13
, the quantity

\#\,\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,q}(E^{-d})
depends only on the signs of
\Delta_{E}
and
b
,
and not the prime
q
dividing
d
;
second, since
E/\mathbb{Q}
has good reduction at
q\mid d
, then

\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,q}(E)=H^{1}_{\textup{ur}}(\mathbb{Q}_{q},E[\phi])
has order
2
for all
such
q
.

∎

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
d\in\mathcal{D}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}
. Then

\operatorname{rk}_{\hat{\varphi}}(E^{\prime-d}/\mathbb{Q})-\operatorname{rk}_{\hat{\varphi}}(E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})=\;\operatorname{rk}_{{\varphi}}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})-\operatorname{rk}_{{\varphi}}(E/\mathbb{Q})+\omega(d)\,\theta{q}+\theta_{\infty}.

where

\theta_{q}=\begin{cases}+1,&b<0,\\
-1,&b>0,\ \Delta_{E}<0,\\
0,&b>0,\ \Delta_{E}>0,\ a<0,\\
0,&b>0,\ \Delta_{E}>0,\ a>0.\end{cases}\qquad\theta_{\infty}=\begin{cases}0,&b<0,\\
0,&b>0,\ \Delta_{E}<0,\\
+1,&b>0,\ \Delta_{E}>0,\ a<0,\\
-1,&b>0,\ \Delta_{E}>0,\ a>0.\end{cases}

Proof.

In view of Lemma
5.5
, we see that

\theta_{q}:=\log_{2}\mskip-3.0mu\frac{2}{\#\,\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,q}(E^{-d})},\quad\theta_{\infty}:=\log_{2}\mskip-3.0mu\frac{\#\,\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,\infty}(E)}{\#\,\mathcal{L}_{\varphi,\infty}(E^{-d})}.

The claimed formula follows from Lemma
2.13
, Lemma
2.16

and Corollary
2.17
.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
d\in\mathcal{D}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}
.
Then, one has:

(i)

If
b<0
(and hence
\Delta_{E}>0
), then

\operatorname{rk}_{\hat{\varphi}}(E^{\prime-d}/\mathbb{Q})=\operatorname{rk}_{\hat{\varphi}}(E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})\;+\;\omega(d).

(ii)

If
\Delta_{E}<0
and
b>0
, then

\operatorname{rk}_{\hat{\varphi}}(E^{\prime-d}/\mathbb{Q})=\operatorname{rk}_{\hat{\varphi}}(E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}).

(iii)

If
\Delta_{E}>0
,
b>0
, and

, then

\operatorname{rk}_{\hat{\varphi}}(E^{\prime-d}/\mathbb{Q})=\operatorname{rk}_{\hat{\varphi}}(E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})\;+\;\omega(d)\;-\;\begin{cases}\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\mskip-3.0mu\bigl(\mathbf{A}_{d}\bigr),&\text{if }\eta_{\phi}(d)=1,\\[4.0pt]
-1+\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\mskip-3.0mu\bigl(\widehat{\mathbf{A}_{d}}\bigr),&\text{if }\eta_{\phi}(d)=0.\end{cases}

(iv)

If
\Delta_{E}>0
,
b>0
, and

, then

\operatorname{rk}_{\hat{\varphi}}(E^{\prime-d}/\mathbb{Q})=\operatorname{rk}_{\hat{\varphi}}(E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})\;+\;\omega(d)\;-\;\begin{cases}\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\mskip-3.0mu\bigl(\widetilde{\mathbf{A}_{d}}\bigr),&\text{if }\eta_{\phi}(d)=1,\\[4.0pt]
1\;+\;\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\mskip-3.0mu\bigl(\overline{\mathbf{A}_{d}}\bigr),&\text{if }\eta_{\phi}(d)=0.\end{cases}

Proof.

Combine Theorem

with Corollary
5.6
.
∎

6.
Compatibility with the parity conjecture

We now relate show that the explicit formulae for
\delta_{\varphi}(d)
and
\delta_{\hat{\varphi}}(d)
are compatible with predictions of the parity conjecture.
Recall that

\delta_{\varphi}(d)=\mathrm{rk}_{\varphi}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})-\mathrm{rk}_{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q}),\qquad\delta_{\hat{\varphi}}(d)=\mathrm{rk}_{\hat{\varphi}}(E^{\prime-d}/\mathbb{Q})-\mathrm{rk}_{\hat{\varphi}}(E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}).

The
p
-parity conjecture asserts that the parity of the
p^{\infty}
-Selmer rank
\mathrm{rk}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q})
is determined by the global root number
w(E/\mathbb{Q})
.
If
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})
satisfies the Heegner hypothesis for
E
, then

w(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})=-w(E/\mathbb{Q}),

and therefore

(6.1)

\mathrm{rk}_{p^{\infty}}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})-\mathrm{rk}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q})\equiv 1\quad\textup{mod }{2}.

Hence the
p
-parity conjecture predicts that twisting by such a field reverses the parity of the
p^{\infty}
-Selmer rank. On the other hand, the explicit expressions for
\delta_{\varphi}(d)
and
\delta_{\hat{\varphi}}(d)
satisfy the same parity relation at the level of the
\varphi
- and
\hat{\varphi}
-Selmer groups.
In particular, combining Theorem
5.2
and Corollary
5.7
yields

(6.2)

\delta_{\varphi}(d)+\delta_{\hat{\varphi}}(d)\equiv 1\quad\textup{mod }{2},

independently of the matrix ranks appearing in these formulae.
In what follows, we show that for
any
square-free integer
d
(not necessarily in
\mathcal{D}_{E/\mathbb{Q}}
), we have:

\mathrm{rk}_{2^{\infty}}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})-\mathrm{rk}_{2^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q})\equiv\delta_{\varphi}(d)+\delta_{\hat{\varphi}}(d)\quad\textup{mod }2.

As a consequence, the relation (
6.2
) obtained from the explicit formulae for
\delta_{\varphi}(d)
and
\delta_{\hat{\varphi}}(d)
is, in this setting, equivalent to the assertion made by the
2
-parity conjecture for
E/K
give in (
6.1
). We begin by reformulating the
2
-parity conjecture in terms of the
2
-Selmer ranks of
E
and its quadratic twists.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with
E(\mathbb{Q})[2]\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.
Then

\mathrm{rk}_{2^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q})\equiv\mathrm{rk}_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})+1\quad\textup{mod }{2}.

Proof.

Let
\delta_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})
denote the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-corank of
\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[2^{\infty}]
.
Then

\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[2^{\infty}]\simeq(\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{\delta_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})}\oplus T,

where
T
is a finite abelian
2
-group.
It follows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{rk}_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})

\displaystyle=1+\mathrm{rk}(E/\mathbb{Q})+\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\Sha(E)[2]

\displaystyle=1+\mathrm{rk}(E/\mathbb{Q})+\delta_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})+\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}T[2]

\displaystyle\equiv 1+\mathrm{rk}(E/\mathbb{Q})+\delta_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})\quad\textup{mod }{2}

\displaystyle=1+\mathrm{rk}_{2^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q})\quad\textup{mod }{2}.

The second to last equality follows from
[
2
]
since the Cassels pairing restricts to an alternating and non-degenerate pairing on
T[2]
, implying
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}T[2]\equiv 0\ \textup{mod }{2}
. For the last equality, by definition, one has
\textup{rk}_{2^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q})=\textup{rk}(E/\mathbb{Q})+\delta_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q}).

∎

Theorem 6.4

(
[
5
]
)
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve and let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})
be an imaginary quadratic field in which all primes of bad reduction for
E/\mathbb{Q}
split.
Then

\mathrm{rk}_{2}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})\equiv\mathrm{rk}_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})+1\ \textup{mod }{2}.

Proof.

Since
E
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, the only non-trivial local contribution to its global root number over
K
arises from the unique archimedean place of
K
, hence
w(E/K)=-1
.
By
[
5
, Thm. 1.4]
, it follows that
\mathrm{rk}_{2^{\infty}}(E/K)\equiv 1\ \textup{mod }{2}
, where
\mathrm{rk}_{2^{\infty}}
denotes the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-corank of the
p^{\infty}
-Selmer group.
Let
W=\mathrm{Res}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}E_{K}
denote the Weil restriction of scalars to
\mathbb{Q}
.
By
[
23
, Thm. 4.5]
,
W
is isogenous over
\mathbb{Q}
to
E\times E^{-d}
, and therefore

\mathrm{rk}_{2^{\infty}}(W/\mathbb{Q})=\mathrm{rk}_{2^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q})+\mathrm{rk}_{2^{\infty}}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q}).

Combining
[
14
, p. 178(a)]
with Shapiro’s lemma yields

\mathrm{rk}_{2^{\infty}}(W/\mathbb{Q})=\mathrm{rk}_{2^{\infty}}(E/K)
, hence

(6.5)

\mathrm{rk}_{2^{\infty}}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})\equiv\mathrm{rk}_{2^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q})+1\quad\textup{mod }{2}.

The stated parity relation follows from Lemma
6.3
. ∎

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
d
be a squarefree integer. Then

\delta_{\varphi}(d)+\delta_{\hat{\varphi}}(d)\equiv\mathrm{rk}_{2}\mskip-3.0mu\left(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q}\right)-\mathrm{rk}_{2}\mskip-3.0mu\left(E/\mathbb{Q}\right)\ \textup{mod }{2}.

Proof.

Write
\varphi_{\mathbb{Q}}
for the induced homomorphism

\varphi_{\mathbb{Q}}:E(\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow E^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}).

By
[
5
, p. 584]
, one has

(6.7)

\frac{\#\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[\varphi]}{\#\Sha(E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})[\hat{\varphi}]}\cdot\frac{\#\operatorname{coker}\varphi_{\mathbb{Q}}}{\#\operatorname{coker}\hat{\varphi}_{\mathbb{Q}}}\equiv\frac{Q(\varphi)}{Q(\hat{\varphi})}\quad\bmod\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2},

where
Q(\cdot)
is defined in
[
5
, Definition 4.1]
.
Using
[
5
, Lemma 4.2(1),(3)]
, we obtain

(6.8)

\frac{Q(\varphi)}{Q(\hat{\varphi})}\equiv Q(\varphi)Q(\hat{\varphi})=Q(\hat{\varphi}\mskip-3.0mu\circ\mskip-3.0mu\varphi)=Q([2]_{E})=2^{\mathrm{rk}_{2^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q})}\quad\textup{mod }\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}.

From the exact sequence

0\to E(\mathbb{Q})/\hat{\varphi}\bigl(E^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})\bigr)\to\Sel^{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q})\to\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[\varphi]\to 0,

and its analogue for
\hat{\varphi}
, we deduce

(6.9)

\displaystyle\#\operatorname{coker}(\varphi_{\mathbb{Q}})

\displaystyle\equiv 2^{\mathrm{rk}_{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q})}\cdot\#\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[\varphi]\quad\bmod\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2},

(6.10)

\displaystyle\#\operatorname{coker}(\hat{\varphi}_{\mathbb{Q}})

\displaystyle\equiv 2^{\mathrm{rk}_{\hat{\varphi}}(E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})}\cdot\#\Sha(E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})[\hat{\varphi}]\quad\bmod\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}.

Combining (
6.7
)–(
6.10
) yields

\mathrm{rk}_{2^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q})\equiv\mathrm{rk}_{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q})-\mathrm{rk}_{\hat{\varphi}}(E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})\ \textup{mod }{2}.

By repeating the argument for
E^{-d}
, we deduce that

\mathrm{rk}_{2^{\infty}}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})\equiv\mathrm{rk}_{\varphi}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})-\mathrm{rk}_{\hat{\varphi}}(E^{\prime-d}/\mathbb{Q})\ \textup{mod }{2}.

Subtracting the two congruences, and invoking Lemma
6.3
gives the required parity relation. ∎

An immediate corollary of this parity relation is a symmetric identity between the
\varphi
– and
\hat{\varphi}
–kernels of the Tate–Shafarevich groups of
E
and its quadratic twists
E^{-d}
.

Corollary 6.11
.

Let
d
be squarefree, and put
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})
.
If
\Sha(E/K)[2^{\infty}]
is finite, then

(6.12)

\frac{\#\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[\varphi]}{\#\Sha(E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})[\hat{\varphi}]}\;\equiv\;\frac{\#\Sha(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})[\varphi]}{\#\Sha(E^{\prime-d}/\mathbb{Q})[\hat{\varphi}]}\quad\bmod\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}.

If, on the other hand, there exists a squarefree
d
for which (
6.12
) fails,
then at least one of
\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[2^{\infty}]
or
\Sha(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})[2^{\infty}]
is infinite.

Proof.

Let
\Phi:\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[2]\to\Sha(E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})[\hat{\varphi}]
and

\Phi_{d}:\Sha(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})[2]\to\Sha(E^{\prime-d}/\mathbb{Q})[\hat{\varphi}]

be the induced maps on Tate–Shafarevich groups.
From the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[\varphi]\longrightarrow\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[2]\xrightarrow{\ \Phi\ }\Sha(E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})[\hat{\varphi}]\longrightarrow\operatorname{coker}(\Phi)\longrightarrow 0,

one obtains:

(6.13)

2^{\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[2]}\cdot\#\,\operatorname{coker}(\Phi)\;\equiv\;\frac{\#\,\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[\varphi]}{\#\,\Sha(E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})[\hat{\varphi}]}\quad\bmod\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}.

Arguing as in Theorem
6.4
, we may rewrite this as

(6.14)

2^{\delta_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})}\cdot\#\,\operatorname{coker}(\Phi)\;\equiv\;\frac{\#\,\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[\varphi]}{\#\,\Sha(E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})[\hat{\varphi}]}\quad\bmod\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}.

Repeating the computation for the twist and combining yields

(6.15)

2^{\delta_{2}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})-\delta_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})}\cdot\,\frac{\#\operatorname{coker}(\Phi_{d})}{\#\operatorname{coker}(\Phi)}\cdot\frac{\#\,\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[\varphi]}{\#\,\Sha(E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})[\hat{\varphi}]}\;\equiv\;\frac{\#\,\Sha(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})[\varphi]}{\#\,\Sha(E^{\prime-d}/\mathbb{Q})[\hat{\varphi}]}\quad\bmod\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}.

Next, from the exact sequence in
[
19
, Lemma 6.1]
, one obtains:

0\to E^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})[\hat{\varphi}]/\varphi(E(\mathbb{Q})[2])\to\Sel^{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q})\to\Sel^{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})\to\Sel^{\hat{\varphi}}(E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})\to\operatorname{Coker}(\Phi)\to 0,

we deduce the relation

\mathrm{rk}_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})+1+\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\operatorname{coker}(\Phi)\;=\;\mathrm{rk}_{\varphi}(E/\mathbb{Q})+\mathrm{rk}_{\hat{\varphi}}(E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}),

and similarly for
E^{-d}
.
Subtracting the two identities gives

\mathrm{rk}_{2}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})-\mathrm{rk}_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})\;\equiv\;\delta_{\varphi}(d)+\delta_{\hat{\varphi}}(d)+\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\operatorname{coker}(\Phi_{d})-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\operatorname{coker}(\Phi)\quad\ \textup{mod }2.

By Proposition
6.6
, one has

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\operatorname{coker}(\Phi_{d})=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\operatorname{coker}(\Phi)\ \textup{mod }2
. It follows that

\#\,\operatorname{coker}(\Phi_{d})\;\equiv\;\#\,\operatorname{coker}(\Phi)\quad\bmod\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}.

Combining this with (
6.15
), we find

\frac{\#\,\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[\varphi]}{\#\,\Sha(E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})[\hat{\varphi}]}\;\equiv\;\frac{\#\,\Sha(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})[\varphi]}{\#\,\Sha(E^{\prime-d}/\mathbb{Q})[\hat{\varphi}]}\cdot 2^{\delta_{2}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})-\delta_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})}\quad\bmod\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}.

If
\Sha(E/K)[2^{\infty}]
is finite, then both

\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[2^{\infty}]
and
\Sha(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})[2^{\infty}]
are finite
[
14
, Lem. 7.1(b)]
,
in which case
\delta_{2}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})=\delta_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})=0
. This gives the first assertion.
Conversely, if this relation doesn’t hold modulo rational squares,
then necessarily

\delta_{2}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})-\delta_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})\equiv 1\ \textup{mod }2
. As a result, at least one of
\delta_{2}(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})
or
\delta_{2}(E/\mathbb{Q})
is odd, and so at least one of

\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})[2^{\infty}]
or
\Sha(E^{-d}/\mathbb{Q})[2^{\infty}]

must be infinite. ∎

Hepatitis B: Assessment of Knowledge, Attitude, and Practice among the Graduating Dental Students of Odisha: A Web-Based Survey
Authors: Shubham Kashyap, Balaji Manohar, Anmol K. Bali, Nitin Sharma, Meenu Dhiman, Ajaypal Singh, Pratyush Mishra, S. Bhuvaneshwari
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction:Evaluating the level of knowledge, attitude, and practice regarding Hepatitis B viral infection among the interns of dental colleges in Odisha.Materials and Method:A questionnaire with 20 closed-ended questions was circulated to 269 interns in the dental colleges of Odisha. A total of 243 interns participated in the study and responded on various aspects of Hepatitis B viral infection including symptoms, transmission mechanisms, preventive measures, and immunization.Results and Conclusion:The findings revealed that while most dental interns had a basic understanding of Hepatitis B viral infection, significant gaps existed in their knowledge of transmission routes and the importance of vaccination.

INTRODUCTION

Dental professionals, including dental interns, are at an increased risk of exposure to various infections due to their frequent contact with blood and bodily fluids. Hepatitis B viral infection is a potentially fatal liver infection caused by the Hepatitis B virus (HBV). It is important to know how much dental interns know about HBV infections, especially in areas like eastern India, in order to put infection control procedures into place and protect patients and healthcare providers.[1] The knowledge and awareness of HBV infection among dental interns in eastern India were assessed in a study.[2] A thorough questionnaire was utilized by the researchers to evaluate many facets of HBV infection, such as symptoms, mechanisms of transmission, preventive measures, and immunization. The study found that although most dental interns understood the fundamentals of HBV infection, they were largely ignorant of the disease’s transmission channels and the value of immunization. In an identical manner, Singh et al.[3] evaluated the knowledge of the dental interns at dental colleges in eastern India using a cross-sectional survey to determine their knowledge and attitudes toward HBV infection. Their research revealed that while dental interns showed a respectable degree of HBV infection awareness, there were still misconceptions and a lack of understanding on some crucial information. The results of these investigations highlight the necessity of more intensive educational programs targeted at dental interns in order to close the knowledge gaps and encourage a thorough grasp on HBV infection. Future dental practitioners may be far better equipped to manage and prevent HBV infections if comprehensive and ongoing training sessions on infection control and management of HBV infection are included in the dental curriculum. The present study was conducted to study the knowledge, attitude, and practices of the dental interns of various dental colleges in Odisha.

METHODOLOGY

A self-structured web-based cross-sectional observational study was formulated and distributed between May 2024 and June 2024 by the Department of Periodontology. The close-ended questionnaire was distributed among the dental interns practicing in the dental colleges of Bhubaneswar and Cuttack. A pilot study was conducted to understand the feasibility of the main study. The pilot study sample was excluded from the final study. The Cronbach’s alpha was calculated to be 0.833, depicting excellent reliability. Comparison was made using IBM SPSS Software version 25.

RESULTS

The self-structured close-ended questionnaire was distributed to 269 interns of the four dental colleges in Odisha state, and 243 interns (96 males and 147 females) participated in the study.

The domain scores were recorded based on gender differences, and it was seen that males had a higher knowledge score as compared to females. The difference between the groups was not statistically significant. DISCUSSION

It is impossible to overestimate the significance of including thorough HBV infection awareness and training programs in dental education. Dental interns are also prone to HBV infection due to their frequent exposure to blood and bodily fluids. When compared to earlier studies, the results of the present study show both progress and enduring deficiencies in the knowledge, attitudes, and behaviors of dental interns in eastern India. Misconceptions concerning non-blood-borne modes of HBV transmission, like breastfeeding and bodily waste, should be dispelled by conducting educational programs.[4] The significance of comprehensive immunization and postexposure prophylactic procedures should also be emphasized. Frequent instruction, updated course materials, and hands-on practical workshops may guarantee dental interns are prepared to use efficient infection control protocols.[5] Reducing the risk of HBV infection transmission requires increased awareness and adherence to safe procedures, such as using sterile needles and disposing of waste properly. Dental schools can make a substantial contribution to the overall objective of lowering HBV infection incidence among medical/dental health professionals and the general public by promoting a full understanding of HBV infection and stressing the value of vaccination.

1.
Introduction

A central concept in geometric measure theory is that of rectifiability. For a metric space
(X,d)
, a set
E\subset X
is
n
-rectifiable
if there exists countably many Lipschitz maps
f_{i}\colon A_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow X
such that

\mathcal{H}^{n}(E\backslash\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}f_{i}(A_{i}))=0.

Here and throughout,
\mathcal{H}^{n}
denotes the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff (outer) measure. A set is
purely
n
-unrectifiable
if its intersection with any
n
-rectifiable set has
\mathcal{H}^{n}
measure zero.

This paper concerns characterizations of rectifiable metric spaces in terms of tangent spaces.
In classical geometric measure theory, that is, for subsets of Euclidean space, Preiss
[
11
]
established a theory of tangent
measures
.
Further, the rectifiability criterion of Marstrand
[
8
]
and Mattila
[
9
]
shows that a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
(with
\mathcal{H}^{n}(E)<\infty
and positive lower density almost everywhere) is
n
-rectifiable if, for
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-a.e.
x\in E
, all tangent measures of
\mathcal{H}^{n}|_{E}
at
x
are supported on
n
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

In the setting of metric spaces, the work of Kirchheim
[
5
]
shows that rectifiable sets possess a strong tangent structure.
In particular, this work implies that tangent spaces (defined as a suitable Gromov–Hausdorff type adaptation of tangent measures, see Section
2
) of a
n
-rectifiable set are Lebesgue measure supported on a normed
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
It remained an open question for sometime whether a result analogous to the Marstrand–Mattila theorem holds in metric spaces.
Recently, this was shown to be the case.
Indeed, Bate
[
2
]
shows that a metric space
E
(with
\mathcal{H}^{n}
) is
n
-rectifiable whenever, for
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-a.e.
x\in E
, all tangent spaces of
\mathcal{H}^{n}|_{E}
at
x
are supported on biLipschitz images of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In this article we show that, for 1-dimensional sets, this sufficient condition for rectifiability can be weakened further to tangents that are supported on
connected
metric spaces.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{*}
be the space of pointed metric measure spaces
(X,d,\mu,x)
for which the support of
\mu
is connected.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete metric space and let
E\subset X
be
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-measurable with
\mathcal{H}^{1}(E)<\infty
.
The following are equivalent:

(1)

E
is
1
-rectifiable.

(2)

For
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e.
x\in E
,
\Theta_{*}^{1}(E,x)>0
and

\textnormal{Tan}(X,d,\mathcal{H}^{1}\llcorner E,x)=\{(\mathbb{R},|\cdot|,\mathcal{H}^{1}/2,0)\}.

(3)

For
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e.
x\in E
,
\Theta_{*}^{1}(E,x)>0
and

\textnormal{Tan}(X,d,\mathcal{H}^{1}\llcorner E,x)\subset\mathcal{C}^{*}.

The fact that 1) implies 2) is given in
[
2
, Theorem 6.6]
by applying the results of Kirchheim
[
5
]
.
Since 2) implies 3) is immediate, our attention is focused on the implication 3) implies 1).
We emphasise that, even in the case that all tangent spaces are supported on
\mathbb{R}
, our proof follows a completely different approach that of
[
2
]
.

Indeed, the geometric idea of our proof begins with a result o Besicovitch
[
3
, Lemma 9]
that covers a purely 1-unrectifiable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by a large number of
circle pairs
.
A circle pair of a set
F
is simply
B(x,|x-y|)\cup B(y,|x-y|)
, where
x
and
y
are points in
F
.
Besicovitch demonstrates that for a
1
-unrectifiable set
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and any
r>0
, one may find disjoint circle pairs, whose radii are at most
r
and sum to at least
\mathcal{H}^{1}(F)/9
, such that the common regions are disjoint from
F
:

F\cap B(x,|x-y|)\cap B(y,|x-y|)=\emptyset.

This construction can be used to show that
1
-unrectifiable sets in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
(with finite Hausdorff measure and positive lower density) cannot have tangent measures that are supported on lines:
any such tangent is forced to be close to both
x
and
y
and hence must cross the empty region, thereby contradicting the idea that a tangent must stay close to the set it is approximating.

There are two issues in extending this idea to prove Theorem
1.1
.
Firstly, his proof only works in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, in particular it uses the fact that boundaries of balls are continua. Secondly, when considering tangents supported on connected sets (even in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
), Besicovitch’s circle pairs are no longer sufficient: a
connected
tangent can lie close to both
x
and
y
and wander outside
B(x,|x-y|)\cup B(y,|x-y|)
and thereby avoid the common region.

To solve this, we strengthen Besicovitch’s notion of circle pairs to what we term Besicovitch
partitions
, see Definition
4.7
. Roughly speaking, these partition
F
into two closed sets, and we consider points
x,y
that minimise the distance between the components.
In Theorem
4.8
we show that in any complete metric space, for any compact purely
1
-unrectifiable set
F\subset X
and
R,r>0
, there exist Besicovitch partitions with
\sum d(x_{i},y_{i})\sim\mathcal{H}^{1}(F)
such that
d(x_{i},y_{i})<r
and moreover the balls
B(x_{i},Rd(x_{i},y_{i}))
(for different
i
) are pairwise disjoint.
On the other hand, in Corollary
3.16
, we show that if all tangents of a space are connected, then in fact there exists a set of large measure and an
R>0
such that all tangents to points in this set are connected in a ball of radius
R
.
Thus, we again arrive to the desired contradiction.

To conclude the introduction we mention the related work of Preiss and Tišer
[
12
]
that gives a different adaptation of Besicovitch’s circle pair argument (to metric spaces) in order to make progress on the Besicovitch one-half conjecture.
These results, and the progress towards the conjecture, were recently extended further by De Lellis, Glaudo, Massaccesi and Vittone
[
6
]
.

The structure of the paper is as follows.
After recalling definitions and fixing some notation in §
2
, we present the definition of a metric tangent and recall various properties from
[
2
]
. Theory that is specific to connected tangents is developed in §
3
.

In §
4
we prove our strengthened version of Besicovitch’s circle pair result. Finally, in Theorem
5.1
in §
5
, we combine all of our ideas and perform the contradiction argument described above.
This gives the final piece required to prove Theorem
1.1
.

2.1.
General Measure Theory

Let
\mathcal{M}(X)
denote the set of all Borel regular measures on
X
and
\mathcal{M}_{\text{loc}}(X)\subset\mathcal{M}(X)
be the set of all those that are finite on all bounded sets.

The support of a measure

is a subset of

\operatorname{supp}\mu:=\{x\in X:\text{ for all open neighbourhoods }U_{x}\text{ of }x,\,\mu(U_{x})>0\}.

We may restrict a measure to a set

\mu\llcorner E(A):=\mu(E\cap A)

2.2.
Rectifiability

Definition 2.1

(Hausdorff measure)
.

For
s\geq 0
,
\delta\in[0,\infty]
and a set
E\subset X
, let

\mathcal{H}_{\delta}^{s}(E)=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\textnormal{diam}(A_{i})^{s}:\,E\subset\bigcup A_{i},\,\textnormal{diam}(A_{i})<\delta\right\}.

Then the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
E
is defined as

\mathcal{H}^{s}(E):=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\delta}(E).

For any
s
, Hausdorff measure is a Borel regular measure
[
10
, Corollary 4.5]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
E\subset X
be
\mathcal{H}^{s}
-measurable with
\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)<\infty
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
we can find a
\delta_{0}>0
such that for any collection of sets of the form
\{A_{i}\subset X:\textnormal{diam}(A_{i})\leq\delta_{0}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies

\mathcal{H}^{s}(E\cap\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}A_{i})\leq\sum_{i}\textnormal{diam}(A_{i})^{s}+\varepsilon.

Proof.

By the definition of Hausdorff measure,
\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\delta}(E)
is monotonically increasing as
\delta\rightarrow 0
. By assumption,
\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)<\infty
hence for any
\varepsilon>0
we can find a
\delta_{0}>0
such that

\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)\leq\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\delta}(E)+\varepsilon\qquad\text{for all }\delta\leq\delta_{0}.

Let
\{A_{i}\subset X:\textnormal{diam}(A_{i})\leq\delta_{0}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{s}(E\cap\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}A_{i})+\mathcal{H}^{s}(E\backslash\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}A_{i})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\delta}(E)+\varepsilon

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\delta}(E\backslash\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}A_{i})+\mathcal{H}^{s}_{\delta}(E\cap\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}A_{i})+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{H}^{s}(E\backslash\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}A_{i})+\sum_{i}\textnormal{diam}(A_{i})^{s}+\varepsilon.

Subtracting
\mathcal{H}^{s}(E\backslash\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}A_{i})
from both sides (which is finite by assumption) gives the desired result.
∎

(Lipschitz Map)

Given a metric space

A set

A set

-unrectifiable if
\mathcal{H}^{n}(P\cap E)=0
for all
n
-rectifiable

-measurable with

-rectifiable and

-unrectifiable.

Proof.

The proof for

[

.
The proof for general

2.4.
Pointed Gromov-Hausdorff distance

To develop a theory of metric tangents, we require a few standard tools from metric geometry.

Definition 2.13

(Pointed metric (measure) space)
.

A pointed metric space
(X,\,d,\,x)
is an ordered triple consisting of a metric space
(X,\,d)
and a point
x\in X
. A pointed metric measure space
(X,\,d,\,\mu,\,x)
consists of a complete and separable pointed metric space
(X,x)
and a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{loc}(X)
.

An isometric embedding
(X,\,d,\,x)\rightarrow(Y,\,\rho,\,y)
of pointed metric spaces is an isometric embedding
(X,\,d)\rightarrow(Y,\,\rho)
such that
x=y.

Notation 2.14
.

When the meaning is clear, we will omit the metric and abbreviate
(X,d,x)
or
(X,d,\mu,x)
to
(X,x)
or
(X,\mu,x)
respectively.

We first require a pointed notion of distance for sets that share the same ambient space.

Definition 2.15

(Pointed Hausdorff distance)
.

Let
(Z,d)
be a metric space and
X,\,Y\subset Z
. Then for
z\in Z
, we define the pointed Hausdorff distance between

and

as

(2.16)

d_{H(z)}(X,\,Y):=\inf\left\{\varepsilon>0:X\cap B(z,\,1/\varepsilon)\subset Y_{\underline{\varepsilon}},\,Y\cap B(z,\,1/\varepsilon)\subset X_{\underline{\varepsilon}}\right\}.

We can now define the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff distance which allows us to compare sets across different ambient metric spaces.

Notation 2.17

In general we shal not distinguish between the original objects

and
\mu

and

(Pointed Gromov-Hausdorff distance)

Let

and

d_{pGH}((X,\,x),(Y,\,y))=\inf\left\{d_{H(z)}(X,\,Y):\parbox{119.50148pt}{\centering$\exists$ metric space $(Z,\,z)$ and isometric embeddings\\
$(X,\,x),(Y,\,y)\rightarrow(Z,\,z)$\@add@centering}\right\}.

Finally, let us define the space on which

(Corollary 2.25
[

]
)

$(X,\,x),(Y,\,y)\rightarrow(Z,\,z)$\@add@centering}\right\}.

This is a complete and separable metric on the set of isometry classes of pointed metric measure spaces
\mathbb{M}_{pm}
, see
[
2
, Corollary 4.6]
. We are now ready to introduce the metric that will be used to define tangent measures in the next section.

Definition 2.28
.

Let
(X,\mu,z)
and
(Y,\nu,y)
be pointed metric measure spaces and define

d_{*}((X,\mu,z),\,(Y,\nu,y))=\inf\left\{F_{z}(\mu,\,\nu):\parbox{119.50148pt}{\centering$\exists$ metric space $(Z,\,z)$ and isometric embeddings\\
$(X,\,x),(Y,\,y)\rightarrow(Z,\,z)$\@add@centering}\right\}.

Let
\mathbb{M}_{*}
be the set of pointed metric measure spaces under the equivalence relation

(X,\mu,x)\sim(Y,\nu,y)\text{ if }(\operatorname{supp}\mu\cup\{x\},\mu,x)\textnormal{ is isometric to }(\operatorname{supp}\nu\cup\{y\},\nu,y).

Then as shown in
[
2
, Corollary 4.13]
,
(\mathbb{M}_{*},d_{*})
is a complete and separable metric space. In light of this, to simplify notation we will sometimes write
(\mu,x)
in place of
(X,\mu,x)
.

The final piece of the puzzle will be to extract some relationship between the convergence of measures and the convergence of their supports. Although
d_{*}
and
d_{pmGH}
are not equivalent, we do have a strong relationship as outlined in the following key result.

Theorem 2.29

(Theorem 4.11
[
2
]
)
.

For
\varepsilon>0
, let
(X,\mu,x),(Y,\nu,y)\in\mathbb{M}_{*}
be pointed metric measure spaces with
x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
,
y\in\operatorname{supp}\nu
and

d_{*}((X,\mu,x),(Y,\nu,y))<\varepsilon.

There exists compact

x\in K_{\mu}\subset\operatorname{supp}\mu\cap B(x,1/\varepsilon)
and
y\in K_{\nu}\subset\operatorname{supp}\nu\cap B(y,1/\varepsilon)

such that

M1.

\max\{\mu(B(x,1/\varepsilon)\backslash K_{\mu}),\nu(B(y,1/\varepsilon)\backslash K_{\nu})\}<\varepsilon
;

M2.

d_{pmGH}((K_{\mu},\mu\llcorner K_{\mu},x),(K_{\nu},\nu\llcorner K_{\nu},y))<3\varepsilon
.

Conversely, if there exist compact
K_{\mu},\,K_{\nu}
as above satisfying
M1.
with

d_{pmGH}((K_{\mu},\mu\llcorner K_{\mu},x),(K_{\nu},\nu\llcorner K_{\nu},y))<\varepsilon

then

d_{*}((X,\mu,x),(Y,\nu,y))<3\varepsilon.

That is,
d_{*}
is bi-Lipschitz equivalent to the metric defined by taking the infimum over all
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
such that
M1.
and
M2.
hold.

We conclude this section with the following proposition that allows us to work entirely in some common space
(Z,z)
.

Proposition 2.30

(Proposition 2.20
[
2
]
)
.

Let
\{(X_{i},\mu_{i},x_{i})\}_{i}
be a sequence of pointed metric measure spaces. We have that

d_{*}((X_{i},\mu_{i},x_{i}),(X,\mu,x))\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{respectively}\quad d_{pmGH}((X_{i},\mu_{i},x_{i}),(X,\mu,x))\rightarrow 0,

for some pointed metric measure space
(X,\mu,x)
if and only if there exists a complete and separable pointed metric space
(Z,z)
and isometric embeddings
(X_{i},x_{i})\rightarrow(Z,z)
and
(X,x)\rightarrow(Z,z)
such that

F_{z}(\mu_{i},\mu)\rightarrow 0,\qquad\text{or respectively}\qquad d_{H(z)}(X,Y)+F_{z}(\mu,\,\nu)\rightarrow 0.

2.6.
Tangents of metric measure spaces

Tangent measures were first introduced by Preiss
[
11
]
in Euclidean space as a natural setting in which to explore questions around density. We refer the reader to
[
10
, Page 184]
for an introduction to tangent measures. With the notion of convergence of metric measure spaces established in the previous section, the definition of tangent measures in metric spaces follows analogously. First, let
(\mu,d,x)\in\mathbb{M}_{*}
with
x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
and for
r>0
, define

T_{r}(\mu,d,x):=\left(\frac{\mu}{\mu(B(x,r))},\frac{d}{r},x\right).

Definition 2.31

(Tangent measure)
.

For
(\mu,d,x)\in\mathbb{M}_{*}
, we say that
(\nu,\rho,y)\in\mathbb{M}_{*}
is a tangent measure if there exists a sequence
\{r_{i}\}
with
r_{i}\rightarrow 0
such that

d_{*}(T_{r_{i}}(\mu,d,x),(\nu,\rho,y))\rightarrow 0\qquad\text{as }i\rightarrow\infty.

The set of all tangent measures for
(\mu,d,x)
is denoted
\textnormal{Tan}(\mu,d,x)
.

Corollary 2.32

(Corollary 4.7
[
2
]
)
.

Let
(\mu,d,x)\in\mathbb{M}_{*}
and let
S\subset\operatorname{supp}\mu
be
\mu
-measurable with
x
a Lebesgue density point of
S
. Then

\textnormal{Tan}(\mu\llcorner S,x)=\textnormal{Tan}(\mu,x).

We also have the following compactness result.

Proposition 2.33

(Proposition 5.3
[
2
]
)
.

Let
X
be a metric space and let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{loc}(X)
be asymptotically doubling. For
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
and every
r_{0}>0,

\{T_{r}(\mu,x):0<r<r_{0}\}\textnormal{ is pre-compact.}

In particular, for any
x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
satisfying the above,
\textnormal{Tan}(\mu,x)
is a non-empty compact metric space when equipped with
d_{*}
and

\forall\,\delta>0,\exists\,r_{x}>0:d_{*}(T_{r}(\mu,x),\textnormal{Tan}(\mu,x))\leq\delta\,\forall\,r\in(0,r_{x}).

Due to the rescaling of the metric in the definition and the nature of embeddings we can derive the fact that the tangent space is closed under rescaling of metrics and translation of points.

Lemma 2.34
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be a metric measure space such that
\mu
is asymptotically doubling.

(1)

For
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
and
s>0
, there exists
C>0
such that

(\nu,\rho,y)\in\textnormal{Tan}(\mu,x)\quad\implies\quad(C\nu,s\rho,y)\in\textnormal{Tan}(\mu,x)

(2)

For
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
,

(\nu,y)\in\textnormal{Tan}(\mu,x)\textnormal{ and }z\in\operatorname{supp}\nu\quad\implies\quad(\nu,z)\in\textnormal{Tan}(\mu,x).

Proof.

Proof of
(2)
may be found in
[
2
, Proposition 5.5]
so we just present a proof of
(1)
. If
(\nu,\rho,y)\in\textnormal{Tan}(\mu,x)
then there exists
r_{i}\rightarrow 0
such that

d_{*}(T_{r_{i}}(\mu,d,x),(\nu,\rho,y))\rightarrow 0.

By Proposition
2.30
there exists a complete and separable metric space
(Z,\lambda,z)
and isometries

\left(\operatorname{supp}\frac{\mu}{\mu(B_{d}(x,r_{i}))},\frac{d}{r_{i}},x\right),(\operatorname{supp}\nu,\rho,y)\rightarrow(Z,\lambda,z)

such that

F_{z}\left(\frac{\mu}{\mu(B_{d}(x,r_{i}))},\nu\right)\rightarrow 0.

Let

C_{i}=\frac{\mu(B_{d}(x,r_{i}))}{\mu(B_{sd}(x,r_{i}))}.

Since
\mu
is asymptotically doubling we can set (up to subsequence)

C=\lim_{i\to\infty}C_{i}>0.

Consider now the sequence
r_{i}/s\rightarrow 0
and note that

\displaystyle T_{r_{i}/s}(\mu,d,x)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\mu}{\mu(B_{d}(x,r_{i}/s))},\frac{sd}{r_{i}},x\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\mu}{\mu(B_{sd}(x,r_{i}))},\frac{sd}{r_{i}},x\right)

\displaystyle=T_{r_{i}}(\mu,sd,x).

Here the subscript denotes the metric that defines the ball.
By pre and post-scaling the previous isometries by
s
, we can construct new isometries

\left(\operatorname{supp}\frac{\mu}{\mu(B_{sd}(x,r_{i}))},\frac{sd}{r_{i}},x\right),(\operatorname{supp}\nu,s\rho,y)\rightarrow(Z,s\lambda,z).

We will write
\mu^{s}
and
\nu^{s}
for the pushforward of
\mu,\,\nu
under these isometries in
(Z,s\lambda)

and scaling
\nu^{s}
we have,

\displaystyle d_{*}\left(T_{r_{i}}(\mu,sd,x),\left(C\nu,s\rho,y\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq F_{z}\left(\frac{\mu^{s}}{\mu(B_{sd}(x,r_{i}))},C\nu^{s}\right)

\displaystyle\leq F_{z}\left(\frac{\mu^{s}}{\mu(B_{sd}(x,r_{i}))},C_{i}\nu^{s}\right)+F_{z}\left(C_{i}\nu^{s},C\nu^{s}\right)

\displaystyle\leq F_{z}\left(\frac{\mu^{s}}{\mu(B_{sd}(x,r_{i}))},C_{i}\nu^{s}\right)+|C-C_{i}|.

Recall that by Proposition
2.26
we have that convergence in
F_{z}
is equivalent to weak* convergence. Thus for any
f\in C_{bs}(Z)
,

\displaystyle\int f(z)\,d\,\left(\frac{\mu^{s}}{\mu(B_{sd}(x,r_{i}))}-C_{i}\nu^{s}\right)(z)=C_{i}\int f(sz)\,d\,\left(\frac{\mu}{\mu(B_{d}(x,r_{i}))}-\nu\right)(z)\rightarrow 0

which combined with
|C-C_{i}|\to 0
concludes the proof.
∎

The upper and lower density of the measure at a point has implications on the regularity of tangents at that point.

Lemma 2.35
.

For
(\mu,x)\in\mathbb{M}_{*}
with
0<\Theta^{s}_{*}(\mu,x),\,\Theta^{s*}(\mu,x)<\infty
, we have that

\frac{\Theta^{s}_{*}(\mu,x)}{\Theta^{s*}(\mu,x)}r^{s}\leq\nu(B(y,r))\leq\frac{\Theta^{s*}(\mu,x)}{\Theta^{s}_{*}(\mu,x)}r^{s}\qquad\forall(\nu,y)\in\textnormal{Tan}(\mu,x).

In particular, if
0<\Theta^{s}_{*}(\mu,x),\,\Theta^{s*}(\mu,x)<\infty
for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
, then for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
,

\textnormal{Tan}(\mu,x)\subset\mathcal{A}\left(\frac{\Theta^{s*}(\mu,x)}{\Theta^{s}_{*}(\mu,x)}\right).

Proof.

By Proposition
2.30
and Proposition
2.26
, we have that for any
(\nu,y)\in\textnormal{Tan}(\mu,x)
there exists a sequence
r_{i}\rightarrow 0
, a metric space
(Z,\lambda,z)
and isometries
(T_{r_{i}}(\mu,d,x)),(\nu,\rho,y)\rightarrow(Z,\lambda,z)
such that

\frac{\mu_{i}}{\mu(B(x,r_{i}))}\xrightharpoonup{*}\nu.

Thus by (
2.22
) we have

\displaystyle\nu(B(y,R))\leq\nu(U(y,R+\varepsilon))

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mu(B(x,(R+\varepsilon)r_{i}))}{\mu(B(x,r_{i}))}

\displaystyle=(R+\varepsilon)^{s}\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mu(B(x,(R+\varepsilon)r_{i}))}{(2(R+\varepsilon)r_{i})^{s}}\cdot\frac{(2r_{i})^{s}}{\mu(B(x,r_{i}))}

\displaystyle\leq(R+\varepsilon)^{s}\frac{\Theta^{s*}(\mu,x)}{\Theta^{s}_{*}(\mu,x)}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}R^{s}\frac{\Theta^{s*}(\mu,x)}{\Theta^{s}_{*}(\mu,x)}.

Similarly, by (
2.23
),

\nu(B(y,R))\geq\limsup_{i\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mu(B(x,(R+\varepsilon)r_{i}))}{\mu(B(x,r_{i}))}\geq R^{s}\frac{\Theta_{*}^{s}(\mu,x)}{\Theta^{s*}(\mu,x)}

which concludes the first statement. The particular case follows from
Lemma (2)
.
∎

Corollary 2.36
.

For an
\mathcal{H}^{s}
-measurable set
E\subset X
with
\mathcal{H}^{s}(E)<\infty
, we can combine Lemma
2.35
and Theorem
2.7
, to see that for
\mathcal{H}^{s}
-a.e.
x\in E
,

R^{s}\Theta_{*}^{s}(\mathcal{H}^{s}\llcorner E,x)\leq\nu(B(y,R))\leq R^{s}\frac{1}{\Theta^{s}_{*}(\mathcal{H}^{s}\llcorner E,x)}

for all

.

3.
Connected Tangents of Metric Spaces

In this section we show how the hypothesis that all tangent spaces are connected improves to a stronger, quantitative notion of connectedness.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the collection of connected separable metric spaces and let
\mathcal{C}^{*}=\{(\mu,d,x)\in\mathbb{M}_{*}:\operatorname{supp}\mu\in\mathcal{C}\}
equipped with
d_{*}
.

Definition 3.1

(Quasi-path-connected)
.

Let
\delta>0
. We say that a metric space
(X,d)
admits a
\delta
-quasi-path between
a,\,b\in X
if there exists a finite collection of points
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}\subset X
such that
x_{1}=a
,
x_{N}=b
and
d(x_{n},\,x_{n+1})\leq\delta d(a,b)
for each
n
. We have the following refinements:

(1)

\mathcal{Q}(\delta,R)
is the collection of metric spaces
(X,d)
such that for all
a,b\in X
,
B(a,Rd(a,b))
admits a
\delta
-quasi-path between
a
and
b
. We say that such an
X
is
R
-locally
\delta
-quasi-path-connected.

(2)

\mathcal{Q}_{p}(\delta,R)
is the collection of pointed metric spaces
(X,d,x)
such that for all
y\in X
,
B(x,Rd(x,y))
admits a
\delta
-quasi-path between
x
and
y
.

(3)

\mathcal{Q}_{*}(\delta,R)=\{(\mu,y)\in\mathbb{M}_{*}:(\operatorname{supp}\mu,y)\in\mathcal{Q}_{p}(\delta,R)\}.

(4)

\mathcal{Q}_{*}(\delta,R,C)=\mathcal{Q}_{*}(\delta,R)\cap\mathcal{A}(C).

Lemma 3.2
.

For
\delta,R,C>0
, let
(X,d,\mu,a)\notin\mathcal{Q}_{*}(\delta,\,R,\,C)
. Then there exists a point
b\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
such that there exists a decomposition of
\operatorname{supp}\mu\cap B(a,Rd(a,b))
into sets
A
and
B
containing
a
and
b
respectively with

\textnormal{dist}(A,B)>\delta d(a,b).

Proof.

Suppose
(X,d,\mu,a)\notin\mathcal{Q}_{*}(\delta,\,R)
. Then there exists a point
b\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
such that there is no
\delta
-quasi-path between
a
and
b
in
B(a,Rd(a,b))
.
Define

A=\{x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu\cap B(a,Rd(a,b)):\\
\textnormal{there exists a $\delta$-quasi-path between $a$ and $x$ in $B(a,Rd(a,b))$}\}

and
B=\operatorname{supp}\mu\cap B(a,Rd(a,b))\setminus A
.
Then
a\in A
and
b\in B
.

Suppose that
\textnormal{dist}(A,B)\leq\delta(a,b)
.
Then, since
\mu\in\mathcal{A}(C)
,
A
and
B
are compact.
Thus there exists
a^{\prime}\in A
and
b^{\prime}\in B
such that
d(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\leq\delta d(a,b)
.
Then there exists a
\delta
-quasi-path between
a
and
b
in
B(a,Rd(a,b))
, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
E\subset X
is such that there exists a
\delta
-quasi-path connecting
x,y\in E
in
B(x,Rd(x,y))
for some
R>1
. Suppose also that
F\subset X
satisfies

(3.4)

d_{H(x)}(E,F)<\varepsilon d(x,y),\qquad\text{where }\varepsilon<\min\left\{\frac{1}{(R+1)d(x,y)^{2}},1\right\}.

Then
F\cup\{x,y\}
contains a
(\delta+2\varepsilon)
-quasi-path connecting
x
and
y
in

B(x,(R+\varepsilon)d(x,y)).

Proof.

Let
\{x_{n}\}\in E
be the
\delta
-quasi-path connecting
x,y\in E
. Note that

\frac{1}{\varepsilon d(x,y)}>(R+\varepsilon)d(x,y).

Thus by (
3.4
), each
x_{n}
is contained in
B(x,1/\varepsilon d(x,y))
and by (
3.4
) there exists an
\hat{x}_{n}\in F
such that

d(x_{n},\hat{x}_{n})<\varepsilon d(x,y).

Thus, the triangle inequality gives us

d(\hat{x}_{n},\hat{x}_{n+1})\leq d(\hat{x}_{n},x_{n})+d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+d(\hat{x}_{n+1},x_{n+1})\leq(\delta+2\varepsilon)d(x,y).

Further, by assumption we have

d(x,x_{n})\leq Rd(x,y)\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}

thus another application of the triangle inequality tells us that
\{\hat{x}_{n}\}\cup\{x,y\}
is the required
(\delta+2\varepsilon)
-quasi-path in
(F\cup\{x,y\})\cap B(x,(R+\varepsilon)d(x,y))
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(X,\mu,x)
be a pointed metric space and
\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,1)
. Let
\hat{x}\in B(x,(1/\varepsilon-\delta))
be such that

(3.6)

\mu(B(\hat{x},\delta))>\varepsilon.

If
K_{\mu}\subset X
is such that

(3.7)

\mu\left(B\left(x,\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\backslash K_{\mu}\right)<\varepsilon,

then
K_{\mu}
must contain a point
k_{\mu}\in K_{\mu}
such that

d(\hat{x},k_{\mu})<\delta.

Proof.

Because
B(\hat{x},\delta)\subset B(x,1/\varepsilon)
, combine (
3.6
) with (
3.7
) to see that there must be some point
k_{\mu}\in K_{\mu}\cap B(\hat{x},\delta)
.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

For
R>1
, let
(X,\mu,x)
be a metric measure space,
y\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
and suppose that
\operatorname{supp}\mu
contains a
\delta
-quasi-path
\{x_{n}\}
in
B(x,Rd(x,y))
connecting
x
and
y
. Suppose also that for
\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,1)
,

(3.9)

\mu(B(x_{n},\delta d(x,y)))>\varepsilon\qquad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then for any
K_{\mu}\subset X
containing
x,\,y
and satisfying

(3.10)

\mu(B(y,(R+1)d(x,y))\backslash K_{\mu})<\varepsilon,

K_{\mu}\cap B(y,(R+\delta)d(x,y))
contains a
3\delta
-quasi-path connecting
y
and
t
.

Proof.

Lemma
3.5
implies that, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists some point
q_{n}\in K_{\mu}\cap B(x_{n},\delta d(x,y))
. Hence,

d(q_{n},q_{n+1})\leq d(q_{n},x_{n})+d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+d(q_{n+1},x_{n+1})\leq 3\delta d(x,y).

Thus the collection
\{y,t,q_{n}\}
forms the required
3\delta
-quasi-path in
K_{\nu}\cap B(y,(R+\delta)d(y,t))
.
∎

In general,
\mathcal{Q}(\delta,R)\subsetneq\mathcal{Q}_{p}(\delta,R)
. Indeed, if
X=\{0\}\cup\{1/2^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with the Euclidean metric, then
(X,\|\cdot\|_{2},0)\in\mathcal{Q}_{p}(1,1/2)
but for any
n
, there is no 1/2-quasi-path from
1/2^{n}
to
1/2^{n+1}
so
X\notin\mathcal{Q}(1,1/2)
. However, in the context of tangents,
Lemma (2)
shows that
\mathcal{Q}_{p}(\delta,R)
and
\mathcal{Q}(\delta,R)
are essentially equivalent.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
(X,d,\mu)
be an asymptotically doubling metric measure space. Suppose that for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
there exists
R_{x},\delta_{x}>0
such that

\textnormal{Tan}(\mu,x)\subset\mathcal{Q}_{*}(\delta_{x},R_{x}).

Then for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
,

(\nu,y)\in\textnormal{Tan}(\mu,x)\quad\implies\quad(\operatorname{supp}\nu,y)\in\mathcal{Q}(\delta_{x},R_{x}).

We now show that if all tangents of a space are connected, then all tangents are in fact quasi-path-connected.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
(X,\,d)
be a complete metric space and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{loc}(X)
. Suppose that for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
we have

(i)

0<\Theta^{1}_{*}(\mu,x),\,\Theta^{1*}(\mu,x)<\infty
;

(ii)

\textnormal{Tan}(\mu,x)\subset\mathcal{C}^{*}.

Then for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
and any
\delta>0
there exists
R_{x},C_{x}>0
such that
\textnormal{Tan}(\mu,x)\subset\mathcal{Q}_{*}(\delta,R_{x},C_{x})
.

Proof.

Fix
\delta>0
and an
x
that satisfies
(i)
,
(ii)
, has a compact tangent space as given by Proposition
2.33
and such that we may apply
Lemma (1)
and
(2)
. Note that
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
satisfies theses requirements. First, recall that Lemma
2.35
gives us a
C_{x}>1
such that

(3.13)

\frac{1}{C_{x}}r\leq\nu(B(z,r))\leq C_{x}r\qquad\forall\,z\in\operatorname{supp}\nu,\,r>0.

To demonstrate the existence of
R_{x}
we work by contradiction, see Figure 1 for a geometric description of the proof. If there exists no such
R_{x}>0
, then for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a tangent measure
(\nu_{n},\rho_{n},a_{n})\in\textnormal{Tan}(\mu,x)
such that
(\nu_{n},\rho_{n},a_{n})\notin\mathcal{Q}_{*}(\delta,n,C_{x})
. Thus, by Lemma
3.2
, there exists a point
b_{n}\in\operatorname{supp}\nu_{n}
such that
\operatorname{supp}\nu_{n}\cap B(a_{n},n\rho_{n}(a_{n},b_{n}))
can be decomposed into sets
A_{n}
and
B_{n}
containing
a_{n}
and
b_{n}
respectively with

\textnormal{dist}_{\rho_{n}}(A_{n},B_{n})>\delta\rho_{n}(a_{n},b_{n}).

We use the
(\nu_{n},a_{n})
to construct another sequence of elements of
\textnormal{Tan}(\mu,x)
.

By
Lemma (1)
, we may rescale the metrics of each tangent and suppose that
\rho_{n}(a_{n},b_{n})=1
(we omit the subsequent scaling of the measure by
C
for ease of notation). By compactness of
\textnormal{Tan}(\mu,x)
, there exists a subsequence
(\nu_{n_{k}},\rho_{n_{k}},a_{n_{k}})
such that, for any

, there exists a

such that

d_{*}((\nu_{n_{k}},a_{n_{k}}),(\nu_{n_{l}},a_{n_{l}}))<\varepsilon\qquad\forall\,k,l>K.

By Lemma
2.30
, we may consider
(\nu_{n_{k}},a_{n_{k}})
to be in a common ambient space
(Z,d_{Z})
with each
a_{n_{k}}
mapped to a common point
a\in Z
. For brevity we relabel the subsequence to be indexed once again by
n
.

Finally, by
Lemma (2)
we observe that
(\nu_{n},b_{n})
are also tangent measures. Hence there exists a sequence
\{(\nu_{n},\rho_{n},b_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\textnormal{Tan}(\mu,x)
such that
\operatorname{supp}\nu_{n}\cap B(a,n)
has decomposition
A_{n}\cup B_{n}
satisfying

\textnormal{dist}_{Z}(A_{n},B_{n})>\delta.

By compactness of
\textnormal{Tan}(\mu,x)
,
\{(\nu_{n},b_{n})\}
converges in
d_{*}
(up to subsequence) to a limit
(\nu,b)\in\textnormal{Tan}(\mu,x)
with

4.
Besicovitch Partitions

In this section we show that a large proportion of a purely 1-unrectifiable set in a metric space can be covered by
Besicovitch partitions
.
A Besicovitch partition is an adaptation of Besicovitch’s ideas on circle pairs in order to work with quasi-connected tangents.
Indeed, in the next section we will see that a set with a suitable collection of Besicovitch partitions cannot have quasi-path connected tangents.

In place of the Besicovitch covering theorem, we use the Alhfors type estimate (
4.3
) to bound the size of the cover.

Notation 4.1
.

For a collection
\mathcal{A}
and a set
A\in\mathcal{A}
, let

A_{\cap}:=\{B\in\mathcal{A}:A\cap B\neq\emptyset\}.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
E\subset X
be a compact set such that for some
r_{0},C>0
we have

(4.3)

\frac{1}{C}r^{n}\leq\mathcal{H}^{n}(E\cap B(x,r))\leq Cr^{n}\qquad\forall r<r_{0},\,x\in E.

Then for any
r<r_{0}
, there exists a cover
\mathcal{B}
of
E
by balls such that

T1.

the radius
r
of every ball is the same;

T2.

\sum_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\textnormal{diam}(B)\leq 8^{n}C\mathcal{H}^{n}(E)
;

T3.

for any
B\in\mathcal{B}
,
\#B_{\cap}\leq C^{2}12^{n}
.

Proof.

Fix
r<r_{0}
and let
\mathcal{C}=\{B(x_{i},r/2):x_{i}\in E\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a finite covering of
E
, the existence of which is assured by the assumption of compactness. We first modify this covering such that the
x_{i}
are distance at least
r/2
apart. Iteratively pick balls such that the centre of one is not included in another and collect into a new collection
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
until no this is no longer possible. This terminates after finitely many steps. Set
\mathcal{B}=2\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. We have that
\mathcal{B}
covers
\mathcal{C}
and is made of balls of radius
r
with
r/2
-separated centres.

Now pick a ball

and note that
\bigcup_{A\in B_{\cap}}A\subset 3B
and that the balls formed taking the same centres but a quarter the radius,

, are disjoint. Thus, using (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}(r/4)^{n}(\#B_{\cap})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{H}^{n}(E\cap\bigcup_{A\in B_{\cap}}A/4)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{H}^{n}(E\cap 3B)\leq C(3r)^{n}

which gives
T3.
.

To prove

, note that, since the elements of

are disjoint,

\sum_{B_{i}\in\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{H}^{n}(E\cap B(x_{i},r/4))=\mathcal{H}^{n}(E\cap\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{B}}B/4)\leq\mathcal{H}^{n}(E).

Also, by (

(2r)^{n}=8^{n}C\left(\frac{1}{C}(r/4)^{n}\right)\leq 8^{n}C\mathcal{H}^{n}(E\cap B(x_{i},r/4)).

Combining these two inequalities gives

.
∎

In place of an ambient Euclidean space, we will use the Kuratowski embedding to isometrically embed a separable metric space

We replace the notion of Besicovitch circle pairs with the following stronger analogue:

Definition 4.7

(Besicovitch partition)
.

A Besicovitch partition of a set

is a collection
P=(E_{1},E_{2},\pi,p_{1},p_{2})
containing

(i)

A partition:
E=E_{1}\cup E_{2}
;

(ii)

A distance:
0<\pi=\textnormal{dist}(E_{1},E_{2})
;

(iii)

A pair of points:
p_{1}\in E_{1}
and
p_{2}\in E_{2}
such that
d(p_{1},p_{2})=\pi
.

We require a notion of disjointness of Besicovitch partitions at a scale corresponding to the
\delta
-path radius that we found via Lemma
3.12
. In this vein, we say that two Besicovitch partitions

and

are
R
-disjoint
if
B(p_{i},R\pi)\cap B(p^{\prime}_{j},R\pi^{\prime})=\emptyset
for
i,j\in\{1,2\}
.

The following result is in the spirit of
[
3
, Lemma 9]
.
However, the construction of the continua in the proof differs quite significantly from the original, which uses the fact that, in the plane, the boundary of a ball is a continuum.

5.
Connected Tangents Implies Rectifiable

We now prove the main theorem.
The proof is rather technical however the underlying geometric idea is simple: a set cannot both be approximated by quasi-path connected spaces (which is implied by having connected tangents) and have collections of Besicovitch partitions as in Proposition
4.8
(which is implied by being purely unrectifiable).

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete metric space and

be
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-measurable with
\mathcal{H}^{1}(E)<\infty

\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e.

\Theta_{*}(E,x)>0
and

\textnormal{Tan}(X,d,\mathcal{H}^{1}\llcorner E,x)\subset\mathcal{C}^{*},

then
E
is
1
-rectifiable.

Proof.

We proceed by contradiction and assume
E
is not
1
-rectifiable. First, by Lemma
2.5
, we may find a purely
1
-unrectifiable set

of positive measure. By Corollary
2.8
, we have
\Theta_{*}(E^{\prime},x)>0
for
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e.

\mathcal{H}^{1}\llcorner E^{\prime}

,

\textnormal{Tan}(\mathcal{H}^{1}\llcorner E^{\prime},x)=\textnormal{Tan}(\mathcal{H}^{1}\llcorner E,x)\subset\mathcal{C}^{*}\quad\text{for }\mathcal{H}^{1}\text{-a.e.}\,x\in E^{\prime}.

Therefore we satisfy the requirements of Lemma
3.16
.
Thus, for
\delta=1/6
, we may find a set

and numbers

\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e.

S1.

\displaystyle\quad\hat{d}\leq\Theta_{*}(F,x),\,\Theta^{*}(F,x)\leq 1;

\displaystyle\quad\textnormal{Tan}(\mathcal{H}^{1}\llcorner F,x)\subset\mathcal{Q}_{*}(1/6,R,1/\hat{d})\cap\mathcal{C}^{*}.

By the virtue of the fact that
Q_{*}
are nested,

.

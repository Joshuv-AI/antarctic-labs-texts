Correlation analysis between serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D levels and thyroid peroxidase antibody positivity in preconception women
Authors: Lina Wang, Yun Zhang, Yihua Bian, Peimin Hua, Yifang Zhao, Yun Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundVitamin D insufficiency and TPOAb positivity frequently coexist in women planning pregnancy, yet their joint distribution and clinical correlates immediately before conception remain insufficiently characterized in routine preconception practice.ObjectiveThis retrospective study aimed to examine the association between serum 25(OH)D concentrations and TPOAb positivity in preconception women, to assess whether low vitamin D status and TPOAb positivity were jointly associated with lower pregnancy rates, and to explore the robustness of these associations after adjustment for demographic, thyroid-related, reproductive, seasonal, and nutritional covariates.MethodsA total of 256 preconception women who underwent routine preconception evaluation in the Department of Premarital and Preconception Health Care between January 2021 and December 2023 were retrospectively enrolled. Serum 25(OH)D, TPOAb, TgAb, thyroid-stimulating hormone, free thyroxine, anti-Müllerian hormone, ferritin, fasting blood glucose, urinary iodine concentration, and season of blood sampling were recorded from existing clinical records. TPOAb reference range was 0–9 IU/mL; for the primary analysis, TPOAb positivity was prespecified as ≥ 34 IU/mL. Multivariable logistic regression and interaction analyses were used to evaluate factors associated with TPOAb positivity and retrospectively ascertained 12-month pregnancy outcomes.ResultsAmong 256 participants (mean age 30.2 ± 4.1 years), 67 women (26.2%) were classified as TPOAb-positive. The TPOAb-positive group had lower serum 25(OH)D levels than the TPOAb-negative group (24.8 ± 9.6 vs. 31.4 ± 11.2 ng/mL, P = 0.003), and serum 25(OH)D was inversely correlated with TPOAb concentration (r = -0.287, P = 0.008). After adjustment for age, BMI, TSH, FT4, AMH, season, urinary iodine category, ferritin, and recent vitamin D intake, vitamin D deficiency remained independently associated with TPOAb positivity (adjusted OR 2.21, 95% CI 1.09–4.48, P = 0.028). In 218 women with follow-up, the 12-month pregnancy rate was 42.7% overall; women with concomitant TPOAb positivity and vitamin D deficiency/insufficiency had the lowest rate (28.6%), and the TPOAb-by-vitamin D interaction for pregnancy outcome was adverse (Pinteraction = 0.041).ConclusionsLower serum 25(OH)D levels were independently associated with TPOAb positivity in preconception women. Introduction

Thyroid autoimmunity, most commonly reflected by TPOAb positivity, is prevalent in women of reproductive age and has been associated with impaired fertility, miscarriage, and adverse obstetric outcomes [1–5]. In parallel, vitamin D insufficiency is highly common worldwide and has been implicated in immune dysregulation, ovarian physiology, and pregnancy success [6–10].

Vitamin D does not directly participate in thyroid hormone synthesis in the same manner as iodine or thyroid peroxidase. Instead, its major relevance to thyroid biology appears to be indirect, through immunomodulatory effects on antigen presentation, T-cell polarization, B-cell activation, cytokine production, and local inflammatory responses within thyroid tissue [7–10]. Observational studies have repeatedly shown lower 25(OH)D concentrations in patients with autoimmune thyroid disease, but the magnitude and independence of this association vary across populations and analytic strategies [11–18].

Women seeking preconception care represent a clinically meaningful group because thyroid autoimmunity, vitamin D status, ovarian reserve markers, and nutritional factors can all be evaluated before pregnancy is attempted. In this setting, a focused preconception analysis can clarify the joint distribution of these factors and identify women who may merit closer counseling or follow-up, even if mechanistic causality cannot be established from retrospective data alone [1–3, 14].

Accordingly, the present study was designed with three pragmatic aims: first, to evaluate the association between serum 25(OH)D concentrations and TPOAb positivity in preconception women; second, to examine whether the coexistence of low vitamin D status and TPOAb positivity was associated with lower 12-month pregnancy rates compared with single-risk groups; and third, to test the robustness of the observed associations after accounting for thyroid function, ovarian reserve, season of sampling, iodine status, and relevant clinical confounders.

Methods

Study design and participants

This was a retrospective observational study based on routinely collected clinical data from women who attended the Department of Premarital and Preconception Health Care, Wuxi Maternity and Child Health Care Hospital, for preconception evaluation between January 2021 and December 2023. The study was initiated after completion of the baseline evaluations and the relevant 12-month pregnancy outcome window; no participants were prospectively recruited for research purposes, and no additional intervention, specimen collection, or research-specific follow-up was performed. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Wuxi Maternity and Child Health Care Hospital, which waived the requirement for informed consent because the study involved secondary analysis of existing clinical and routine follow-up records, posed no more than minimal risk to participants, and all data were de-identified before analysis. All procedures were performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and relevant institutional guidelines.

Women who had undergone routine preconception evaluation in the Department of Premarital and Preconception Health Care were retrospectively screened for eligibility from the hospital information system and the department’s routine preconception follow-up registry. Inclusion criteria were age 20–45 years, active pregnancy planning, and availability of complete baseline thyroid and vitamin D testing. Exclusion criteria were previously diagnosed overt thyroid disease requiring ongoing treatment at baseline, chronic kidney or liver disease, known parathyroid disorders, active autoimmune disease other than thyroid autoimmunity, assisted reproductive technology during the index evaluation cycle, current glucocorticoid use, vitamin D supplementation within 3 months before baseline, or missing core laboratory data.

The sample size calculation was based on an expected prevalence of TPOAb positivity of approximately 20% among preconception women and an anticipated between-group difference in mean 25(OH)D of 5 ng/mL. With α = 0.05 and 80% power, at least 236 participants were required; the final analytic cohort of 256 therefore provided adequate power for the primary association analysis.

Clinical and laboratory assessments

All baseline clinical and laboratory data were extracted from routine preconception screening records. Demographic and clinical characteristics included age, body mass index, menstrual and reproductive history, smoking and alcohol exposure, recent vitamin D intake from multivitamins or supplements, and, where available in routine follow-up records, whether any thyroid-related medication was initiated after baseline. Women who started levothyroxine or vitamin D treatment after baseline were retained for the primary analysis but were excluded in sensitivity analyses.

A total of 312 preconception women were initially screened for eligibility. Fifty-six women were excluded: 18 had previously diagnosed thyroid disorders requiring treatment, 12 reported recent vitamin D supplementation, 9 had incomplete laboratory records, 7 underwent assisted reproductive treatment during the index cycle, 5 had parathyroid or other endocrine disorders affecting calcium-vitamin D metabolism, and 5 had chronic inflammatory or hepatic/renal disease. The remaining 256 women were included in the primary analysis.

Blood samples were collected following an overnight fast of at least 8 h. Serum 25(OH)D levels were measured using electrochemiluminescence immunoassay (Roche Diagnostics, Mannheim, Germany) on a Cobas e601 analyzer. Vitamin D status was categorized as deficiency (< 20 ng/mL), insufficiency (20–29 ng/mL), and sufficiency (≥ 30 ng/mL). To address seasonal fluctuation, season of blood sampling was recorded as winter, spring, summer, or autumn and incorporated into adjusted analyses.

Thyroid function parameters were assessed using chemiluminescent microparticle immunoassay (Architect System, Abbott Diagnostics). Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH; reference range 0.27–4.2 mIU/L), free thyroxine (FT4; 9.0–19.0 pmol/L), TPOAb (laboratory reference range 0–9 IU/mL), and thyroglobulin antibody (TgAb; reference range 0–4.1 IU/mL) were measured at baseline. For the primary endpoint, TPOAb positivity was defined a priori as ≥ 34 IU/mL to improve specificity for clinically relevant thyroid autoimmunity, while the assay reference interval was retained for descriptive interpretation. An exploratory subset analysis was also performed using calculated free 25(OH)D in participants with available vitamin D-binding protein and albumin records.

Additional laboratory parameters included serum ferritin (electrochemiluminescence immunoassay; reference range 13–150 ng/mL for females), fasting blood glucose, anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH), homocysteine, fasting insulin, and urinary iodine concentration measured from spot urine specimens using the Sandell–Kolthoff method. Urinary iodine concentration was categorized as < 100 µg/L, 100–299 µg/L, or ≥ 300 µg/L.

Follow-up and pregnancy outcome assessment

Pregnancy outcomes were ascertained retrospectively from existing electronic medical records and the department’s routine preconception follow-up registry at 6 and 12 months after initial evaluation; no additional research-specific contact or prospective follow-up was conducted for this study. Pregnancy was defined as ultrasound-confirmed intrauterine pregnancy. Women who initiated assisted reproductive technology after baseline were censored from pregnancy-outcome analyses, and sensitivity analyses additionally excluded women who started levothyroxine or vitamin D supplementation during follow-up.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA) and R software version 4.1.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Continuous variables were tested for normality and are presented as mean ± SD or median (interquartile range) as appropriate; categorical variables are presented as counts and percentages. Between-group comparisons used Student’s t test, Mann–Whitney U test, χ² test, or Fisher’s exact test. Pearson or Spearman correlation coefficients were calculated according to distributional characteristics. Multivariable logistic regression for TPOAb positivity adjusted for age, BMI, TSH, FT4, AMH, ferritin, season of blood sampling, urinary iodine category, and recent vitamin D intake. Pregnancy analyses used multivariable logistic regression including TPOAb status, vitamin D category, their product interaction term, age, BMI, TSH, AMH, season, urinary iodine category, and follow-up treatment initiation. Subgroup analyses were prespecified by age (< 30 vs. ≥30 years) and AMH (< 3 vs. ≥3 ng/mL). Two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

The mean age of participants was 30.2 ± 4.1 years (range: 23–43 years). Using the prespecified analytic threshold of TPOAb ≥ 34 IU/mL, 67 women (26.2%) were classified as TPOAb-positive, while 189 women (73.8%) were TPOAb-negative. Median TPOAb concentration was 16.2 IU/mL (IQR 4.2–38.9) in the overall cohort, 86.4 IU/mL (IQR 48.6–176.3) in the TPOAb-positive group, and 4.8 IU/mL (IQR 2.1–7.3) in the TPOAb-negative group.

The TPOAb-positive group was older and had lower serum 25(OH)D and AMH levels together with higher TSH values than the TPOAb-negative group. TgAb concentrations and the proportion with TgAb positivity were also higher in women with TPOAb positivity, supporting a broader autoimmune profile.

Vitamin D status distribution

The distribution of vitamin D status across the study population is presented in Table 2. Overall, vitamin D deficiency [25(OH)D < 20 ng/mL] was present in 72 women (28.1%), insufficiency in 98 (38.3%), and sufficiency in 86 (33.6%). Compared with TPOAb-negative women, TPOAb-positive women were more likely to be vitamin D deficient and less likely to be vitamin D sufficient. Pairwise category comparisons showed a significant excess of deficiency (P = 0.006) and a lower prevalence of sufficiency (P = 0.031) in the TPOAb-positive group, whereas the difference in insufficiency did not reach significance (P = 0.624).

Correlation analysis

Correlation analysis demonstrated a significant negative correlation between serum 25(OH)D levels and TPOAb concentrations (r = -0.287, P = 0.008) (Fig. 2). Additionally, 25(OH)D was negatively correlated with TSH (r = -0.196, P = 0.014) and weakly positively correlated with FT4 (r = 0.168, P = 0.031). In the exploratory subset with calculated free 25(OH)D available (n = 138), free 25(OH)D was lower in TPOAb-positive women (4.62 ± 1.11 vs. 5.21 ± 1.27 pg/mL, P = 0.018) and remained inversely correlated with TPOAb concentration (r = -0.211, P = 0.013). TgAb concentration showed a weaker but significant inverse correlation with 25(OH)D (r = -0.174, P = 0.012).

In univariate analysis, older age, vitamin D deficiency, higher TSH, lower AMH, winter sampling, and urinary iodine < 100 µg/L were associated with higher odds of TPOAb positivity. In the fully adjusted model including age, BMI, TSH, FT4, AMH, ferritin, season, urinary iodine category, and recent vitamin D intake, vitamin D deficiency remained independently associated with TPOAb positivity (adjusted OR 2.21, 95% CI: 1.09–4.48, P = 0.028), whereas vitamin D insufficiency was not. Sensitivity analysis after excluding 17 women who initiated levothyroxine or vitamin D treatment during follow-up yielded similar results (adjusted OR 2.18, 95% CI: 1.03–4.62, P = 0.041).

Predictive value of 25(OH)D for TPOAb positivity

ROC curve analysis was performed to evaluate the discriminative capacity of serum 25(OH)D levels for predicting TPOAb positivity (Fig. 3). The AUC was 0.683 (95% CI: 0.598–0.768, P < 0.001), indicating modest discriminatory ability. The optimal cutoff value of 27.4 ng/mL yielded a sensitivity of 71.6% and a specificity of 58.7%.

Women with both TPOAb positivity and vitamin D deficiency/insufficiency exhibited the lowest pregnancy rate at 12 months (28.6%). Compared with this dual-risk group, pregnancy rates were higher in the TPOAb-negative/vitamin D-deficient or insufficient group (42.9%; adjusted OR = 1.88, 95% CI: 1.01–3.49, P = 0.046) and in the TPOAb-negative/vitamin D-sufficient group (52.3%; adjusted OR = 2.74, 95% CI: 1.31–5.74, P = 0.009). In a two-factor logistic model, both TPOAb positivity and low vitamin D status were associated with lower pregnancy odds, and the interaction term was statistically significant in the adverse direction (Pinteraction = 0.041). Subgroup analyses showed that the association between low 25(OH)D and TPOAb positivity was stronger among women aged ≥ 30 years (adjusted OR = 2.58, 95% CI: 1.21–5.49) and among those with AMH < 3 ng/mL (adjusted OR = 2.74, 95% CI: 1.19–6.29), with no statistically significant heterogeneity (both P for interaction > 0.10).

Discussion

This revised analysis demonstrates an inverse association between serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D levels and TPOAb positivity among preconception women in a Chinese population. After adjustment for BMI, thyroid function, ovarian reserve, season of sampling, iodine status, ferritin, and recent vitamin D intake, the association remained statistically significant for vitamin D deficiency but not for vitamin D insufficiency, suggesting that the signal is concentrated in the lower range of vitamin D status.

The observed inverse correlation between 25(OH)D and TPOAb levels is consistent with previous observational studies and meta-analytic evidence [11–18]. However, our data should not be interpreted as establishing causality. Low vitamin D may contribute to immune dysregulation, but it is also plausible that autoimmune activation and accompanying endocrine changes increase vitamin D demand or alter vitamin D-binding dynamics. In exploratory reverse-direction models, TPOAb positivity and higher TSH were likewise associated with lower 25(OH)D, supporting a potentially bidirectional relationship.

The biological plausibility underlying the vitamin D-thyroid autoimmunity connection is better explained by indirect immune regulation than by direct participation in thyroid hormone synthesis. Vitamin D receptor signaling influences antigen presentation, regulatory T-cell differentiation, Th1/Th17 balance, and B-cell antibody production, all of which are relevant to autoimmune thyroid injury [7–10, 19–23]. This framework is consistent with our finding that adjustment for TSH and FT4 attenuated but did not abolish the association between vitamin D deficiency and TPOAb positivity.

The prevalence of vitamin D deficiency observed in our cohort was comparable with reports from other Chinese populations [24–27]. Because 25(OH)D was measured at a single time point, seasonal fluctuation remained a potential source of misclassification; nevertheless, explicit adjustment for season of blood sampling did not materially alter the principal estimates. Similarly, inclusion of urinary iodine category in adjusted models reduced concern that abnormal iodine exposure fully explained the observed association, although some residual nutritional confounding cannot be excluded.

The age- and AMH-stratified analyses further suggest that the vitamin D-TPOAb association may be more pronounced in women with relatively older reproductive age or lower ovarian reserve. Although tests for heterogeneity were not statistically significant, the directionally larger effect sizes in these strata are clinically noteworthy and merit confirmation in larger prospective cohorts.

The predictive value of serum 25(OH)D for TPOAb positivity, while statistically significant, remained modest. This indicates that vitamin D status should not be viewed as a standalone screening test for thyroid autoimmunity, but rather as one component of a broader preconception risk profile that also includes thyroid function indices, antibody status, and reproductive biomarkers.

Our observation that women with both TPOAb positivity and low vitamin D status experienced the lowest pregnancy rates persisted after direct comparison with the single-risk groups, and the interaction analysis suggested a combined adverse association. Even so, the present data support an additive or synergistic pattern only at the associative level; they do not prove biologic synergy because pregnancy success is influenced by many unmeasured behavioral, partner-related, and treatment-related factors.

From a clinical perspective, these findings support comprehensive preconception assessment rather than isolated interpretation of any single biomarker. Vitamin D status, TPOAb positivity, thyroid function, iodine nutrition, and ovarian reserve markers can be reviewed together to identify women who may benefit from closer counseling, correction of modifiable deficiencies, and repeated thyroid surveillance before conception [1–5, 28–32].

Several limitations of the present study warrant acknowledgment. The retrospective design precludes causal inference; total 25(OH)D was the primary exposure and free 25(OH)D was available only in a subset; residual confounding from diet, outdoor activity, and partner fertility could not be eliminated; and the number of TPOAb-positive women with vitamin D sufficiency was relatively small. Nonetheless, exclusion of women with parathyroid disorders or assisted reproduction exposure, adjustment for season and iodine status, and sensitivity analyses after treatment initiation improved the internal consistency of the findings.

In conclusion, this retrospective study demonstrates an inverse association between serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D levels and TPOAb positivity in Chinese preconception women. The coexistence of TPOAb positivity and low vitamin D status was associated with lower 12-month pregnancy rates. These results justify prospective studies with repeated measurements, standardized free 25(OH)D assessment, and broader nutritional profiling to clarify temporal direction and clinical utility.

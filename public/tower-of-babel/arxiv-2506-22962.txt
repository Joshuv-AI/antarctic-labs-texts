p p -eigenvalue pinching sphere theorems
Paulo Henryque C. Silva

Abstract
In this paper, we establish two p p -eigenvalue pinching sphere theorems, for the p p -Laplacian, p > 1 p>1 . One of the results states that if the first non-zero p p -eigenvalue of a closed Riemannian n n -manifold with sectional curvature K M ≥ 1 K_{M}\geq 1 is sufficiently close to the first non-zero p p -eigenvalue of 𝕊 n \mathbb{S}^{n} then M M is homeomorphic to 𝕊 n \mathbb{S}^{n} . Our results extend sphere theorems originally settled for the Laplacian by Croke [ 7 ] and Bessa [ 4 ] respectively. AMS Subject Classification: 58C40, 58J50, 53C21, 35P30. Keywords — Pinching Theorem; p p ‑eigenvalue; p p -Laplacian; riemannian manifold.

Introduction

There has been an increasing interest in the study of the operator
\triangle_{p}={{\rm{div}}}(|\nabla\,u|^{p-2}\nabla\,u)
,
p>1
, called the
p
-Laplacian, on compact Riemannian manifolds with or without boundary and its relations with their geometries, particularly the relations regarding the spectrum of
\triangle_{p}
, which have attracted considerable attention in the recent literature (see
[
5
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
6
]
e
[
13
]
). The spectrum of
\triangle_{p}
is the set of
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that there exists
0\not\equiv u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(M)
satisfying the following equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\triangle_{p}u+\lambda|u|^{p-2}u=0&in\,\,\,\,M;\\
u=0&on\,\,\,\,\partial M,\,\,\,if\,\,\partial M\,\,\neq\emptyset\end{array}\right.

(1)

in weak sense, this is

\int_{M}|\nabla\,u|^{p-2}\langle\nabla\,u,\nabla\,\eta\rangle\,d\mathscr{H}^{n}=\lambda\int_{M}|u|^{p-2}u\,\eta\,d\mathscr{H}^{n},

(2)

for all
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{o}(M)
. If
\partial M\neq\emptyset
the
problem (
1
) is called the Dirichlet eigenvalue problem and if
\partial M=\emptyset
, then the problem is called the closed eigenvalue problem.
Here, the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}_{0}(M)
is the completion of
C^{\infty}_{0}(M)
with respect to the norm

\|u\|_{1,p}=\left(\int_{M}\left(|u|^{p}+|\nabla u|^{p}\right)\,d\mathscr{H}^{n}\right)^{1/p}.

In the Dirichlet eigenvalue problem, there exists a non-decreasing sequence of positive eigenvalues
\lambda_{k,p}(M)\to\infty
tending to infinity as
k\to\infty
, proved using the Ljusternik–Schnirelman principle see
[
8
]
,
[
12
]
and references therein. The first Dirichlet eigenvalue (smallest) is a Ljusternik–Schnirelman eigenvalue, isolated and simple, meaning that there exists a
C^{1,\alpha}(M)
function
u
such that any other first Dirichlet eigenfunction is a multiple of
u
, moreover, any first Dirichlet eigenfunction does not change sign, see
[
1
]
,
[
15
]
. The first Dirichlet eigenvalue of
M
has a variational characterization given by

\lambda_{1,p}^{\mathcal{D}}(M)=\inf\left\{\frac{\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla\,u|^{p}d\mathscr{H}^{n}}{\displaystyle\int_{M}u^{p}d\mathscr{H}^{n}}\colon 0\not\equiv u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(M)\right\}.

(3)

For the closed eigenvalue problem,
\lambda=0
is an eigenvalue corresponding to constant functions, hence, we consider as the first non-zero eigenvalue of (
1
) the value given by

\lambda_{1,p}(M)=\left\{\frac{\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla\,u|^{p}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n}}{\displaystyle\int_{M}u^{p}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n}}\colon 0\not\equiv u\in W^{1,p}(M);\,\,\int_{M}|u|^{p-2}u\,d\mathscr{H}^{n}=0\right\}.

(4)

Since
\triangle_{2}=\triangle
it is expected that many results that are valid for the Laplacian are also valid for the
p
-Laplacian. In
[
17

[
20

proved Cheng’s eigenvalue comparison theorem and Faber-Krahn isoperimetric inequality for the

-Laplacian. An Lê studied the spectrum of the

[

.
Regarding the closed eigenvalue problem, Matei

Theorem 1.1

(Matei-Lichenorowicz-Obata)
.

Let

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

be a closed Riemannian
n
-manifold with sectional curvature

such that if
C(n,p)\cdot\lambda_{1,p}(\mathbb{S}^{n})\geq\lambda_{1,p}(M)

\mathbb{S}^{n}

Let

is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{n}

Lemma 2.4

Suppose that

Proof.

Let
\Omega\subset M
be an open subset with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega.
As in (
3
), the first Dirichlet eigenvalue
\lambda_{1,p}^{\mathcal{D}}(\Omega)
of
\Omega
is given by

\lambda_{1,p}^{\mathcal{D}}(\Omega)=\inf\left\{\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n}}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{p}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n}}:\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}\right\}.

This infimum is attained by a function
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
; see
[
16
, Lem. 5]
. Let
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
be such a minimizer. By standard regularity theory, it follows that
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
; see
[
12
, Thm. 4.5]
. Moreover, the first Dirichlet eigenvalue is simple in any bounded domain
[
15
, Thm. 6]
and is uniquely characterized by the fact that its associated eigenfunction does not vanish in the interior of
\Omega
; see
[
15
, Lem. 5]
. Hence, we may assume without loss of generality that
u>0
in
\Omega
. Furthermore, by classical density arguments, we may also assume that
u
is a Morse function; see
[
12
, Cor. A.4]
.

For each
t>0
, set

\Omega(t):=\{x\in\Omega:u(x)>t\},\quad\Gamma(t):=\{x\in\Omega:u(x)=t\}

and
\Omega^{*}(t)\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
be a geodesic ball with center at the north pole such that

\mathscr{H}^{n}(\Omega(t))=\beta\cdot\mathscr{H}^{n}(\Omega_{*}(t)),

(11)

with
\beta=\mathscr{H}^{n}(M)/\mathscr{H}^{n}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
. Let
u_{*}\colon\Omega(0)\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

u_{*}|_{\Gamma_{*}(t)}=t=u|_{\Gamma(t)},

where
\Gamma_{*}(t)=\partial(\Omega_{*}(t))
be the decreasing symmetric rearrangement of
u
. By construction, we have
u_{*}\big|_{\partial\Omega_{*}}=0
. Applying the coarea formula (
8
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}\mathscr{H}^{n}(\Omega(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega(t)}d\mathscr{H}^{n};

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\Gamma(t)}\dfrac{1}{|\nabla u|}d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right)dt;

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Gamma(t)}\dfrac{1}{|\nabla u|}\ d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

Reapplying the coarea formula (
7
) and utilizing the result above, we find that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega(t)}u^{p}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\Gamma(t)}\dfrac{u^{p}}{|\nabla u|}d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right)dt;

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{p}\left(\int_{\Gamma(t)}\dfrac{1}{|\nabla u|}d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right)dt;

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{p}\dfrac{d}{dt}\mathscr{H}^{n}(\Omega(t))dt;

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\beta\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{p}\dfrac{d}{dt}\mathscr{H}^{n}(\Omega_{*}(t))dt;

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\cdot\int_{\Omega_{*}(t)}u_{*}^{p}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n}.

By the coarea formula once again, it follows that

\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega(t)}|\nabla u|^{p}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n}=-\int_{\Gamma(t)}|\nabla u|^{p-1}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

(14)

Given the symmetric decreasing rearrangement
u_{*}
, we can show that

\dfrac{d}{dt}\mathscr{H}^{n}(\Omega_{*}(t))=-\int_{\Gamma_{*}(t)}\dfrac{1}{|\nabla u_{*}|}d\mathscr{H}^{n-1},

(15)

and also that,

\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{*}(t)}|\nabla u_{*}|^{p}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n}=-\int_{\Gamma_{*}(t)}|\nabla u_{*}|^{p-1}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

(16)

By virtue of the construction of
\Omega_{*}(t)
given in (
11
)
and the identities in (

) and (

), we see that

\begin{split}\int_{\Gamma(t)}\frac{1}{\lvert\nabla u\rvert}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}&=-\,\mathscr{H}^{n}\bigl(\Omega(t)\bigr);\\
&=-\,\beta\,\mathscr{H}^{n}\bigl(\Omega_{*}(t)\bigr);\\
&=\beta\,\int_{\Gamma_{*}(t)}\frac{1}{\lvert\nabla u_{*}\rvert}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.\end{split}

(17)

Using Hölder’s inequality, we obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma(t)}d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Gamma(t)}|\nabla u|^{p-1}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{\Gamma(t)}\dfrac{1}{|\nabla u|}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right)^{(p-1)/p}.

As a result, combining the above inequality with (
14
), we conclude that

\displaystyle-\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega(t)}|\nabla u|^{p}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Gamma(t)}d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right)^{p}}{\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Gamma(t)}\dfrac{1}{|\nabla u|}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right)^{p-1}}

(18)

According to Croke’s Lemma
2.1
, there exists a constant
\overline{C}(n,D)>1
such that for any domain
\Omega\subset M
with smooth boundary, the following Gromov-type inequality holds:

\int_{\Gamma(t)}d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\geq\overline{C}(n,D)\cdot\beta\cdot\int_{\Gamma_{*}(t)}d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

(19)

thus, (
18
) combined with (
19
) and (

) we have that

-\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega(t)}|\nabla u|^{p}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n}\geq\dfrac{\displaystyle\overline{C}(n,D)^{p}\cdot\beta^{p}}{\beta^{p-1}}\cdot\frac{\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Gamma_{*}(t)}d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right)^{p}}{\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Gamma_{*}(t)}\dfrac{1}{|\nabla u_{*}|}d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right)^{p-1}}\ \cdot

(20)

On the other hand, due to the fact that
u_{*}
is radially symmetric, it follows that

\int_{\Gamma_{*}(t)}d\mathscr{H}^{n}=\left(\int_{\Gamma_{*}(t)}|\nabla u_{*}|^{p-1}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n}\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{\Gamma_{*}(t)}\dfrac{1}{|\nabla u_{*}|}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n}\right)^{(p-1)/p}.

(21)

Indeed, it follows by Hölder’s inequality that

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{*}(t)}d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Gamma_{*}(t)}|\nabla u_{*}|^{p-1}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{\Gamma_{*}(t)}\dfrac{1}{|\nabla u_{*}|}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right)^{(p-1)/p};

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{*}(t)}d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

\displaystyle-\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega(t)}|\nabla u|^{p}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n}\geq\overline{C}(n,D)^{p}\cdot\beta\cdot\left(\int_{\Omega_{*}(t)}|\nabla u_{*}|^{p-1}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right).

\displaystyle-\dfrac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega(t)}|\nabla u|^{p}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n}

\displaystyle\geq

Integrating both sides from

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,p}^{\mathcal{D}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega(0)}|\nabla u|^{p}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n}}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega(0)}u^{p}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n}};

\displaystyle\overline{C}(n,D)^{p}\cdot\beta\cdot\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{*}(0)}|\nabla u_{*}|^{p}d\mathscr{H}^{n}}{\displaystyle\beta\cdot\int_{\Omega_{*}(0)}u_{*}^{p}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n}};

\displaystyle\overline{C}(n,D)^{p}\cdot\lambda_{1,p}^{\mathcal{D}}(\Omega_{*}).

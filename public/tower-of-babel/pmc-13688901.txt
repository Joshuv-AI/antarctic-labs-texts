Belumosudil-associated neurological adverse events: evidence from FAERS and experimental validation
Authors: Zeng-hui Zhou, Yi-xuan Wang, Jing-jing Ma, Jin Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeBelumosudil, a first-in-class ROCK2 inhibitor, was approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in 2021 for the treatment of chronic graft-versus-host disease (cGVHD). This study aimed to evaluate the real-world safety profile of belumosudil and investigate its potential neurological adverse effects (AEs).MethodsAdverse events (AEs) related to belumosudil were extracted from the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) from Q3 2021 to Q2 2025. Signal detection was performed using four disproportionality methods: reporting odds ratio (ROR), proportional reporting ratio (PRR), Bayesian confidence propagation neural network (BCPNN), and multi-item gamma Poisson shrinker (MGPS). Time-to-onset was analyzed using the Weibull distribution. Network pharmacology analysis, including target prediction, disease-gene collection, protein-protein interaction network construction, and GO/KEGG enrichment analyses, was conducted to explore potential targets and pathways associated with neurotoxicity. Cellular experiments in SH-SY5Y cells and primary neurons were performed to validate the predicted neurotoxic effects.ResultsA total of 2225 cases involving belumosudil were identified, comprising 5852 AE reports. In addition to known label-listed AEs such as fatigue, nausea, infection, pneumonia, and rash, several potential unlisted AEs were detected, including stomatitis, dry eye, cataract, and neurological events such as neuropathy peripheral, depressed mood, and emotional disorder. The median onset time was 78.5 days (IQR 23-169.5), with most events occurring within the first 30 days. Network pharmacology identified 46 overlapping targets, and enrichment analysis highlighted pathways related to neuronal function and survival. Furthermore, in vitro experiments demonstrated that belumosudil induced dose-dependent neuronal injury in SH-SY5Y cells and impaired neurite outgrowth in primary neurons, which may involve modulation of the AKT signaling pathway.ConclusionThis study provides a real-world safety evaluation of belumosudil and identifies potential unlisted neurological AEs. Integration of network pharmacology and neuronal experiments provides preliminary support for the potential biological relevance of these findings and highlights the need for further monitoring of neurological AEs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12883-026-05032-y.Keywords: Belumosudil, cGVHD, Pharmacovigilance, FAERS database, Network pharmacology, Neuronal injury

Introduction

Chronic graft-versus-host disease (cGVHD) is a severe and life-threatening complication following allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation [1–4]. Clinically, it is characterized by multisystem organ damage, including the skin, liver, lungs, and gastrointestinal tract, resulting from immune dysregulation, chronic inflammation, and fibrosis [5, 6]. First-line treatment for cGVHD typically involves corticosteroids alone or in combination with sirolimus or a calcineurin inhibitor [7, 8]. Additionally, ruxolitinib and ibrutinib have been approved as second-line treatments for cGVHD, leading to improved overall response rates [9–12]. However, high rates of adverse events (AEs) and limited efficacy with these therapies highlight the need for alternative treatment options.

Belumosudil, a novel selective inhibitor of the rho-associated coiled-coil kinase 2 (ROCK2), has recently been approved by the FDA on 16 th July 2021 for the third-line treatment of cGvHD [13, 14]. Unlike conventional therapies, such as corticosteroids or immunosuppressive agents, belumosudil targets a distinct molecular pathway. Inhibition of ROCK2 by belumosudil exerts an anti-inflammatory effect by rebalancing the T helper 1 (Th1)/Th17 axis towards a regulatory T cell response through signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3)-dependent mechanism [15, 16]. More importantly, belumosudil has been demonstrated to show efficacy and alleviate inflammation and fibrosis in pre-clinical animal models of cGVHD and multiple clinical trials [17–19]. Across phase II studies and the pivotal ROCKstar trial, the safety profile of belumosudil was generally manageable, but treatment-emergent AEs were common. Established AEs reported in trials and reflected in the prescribing information include infections (including pneumonia), fatigue/asthenia, nausea, diarrhea, dyspnea, cough, edema, hemorrhage/bleeding, abdominal pain, musculoskeletal pain, headache, hypertension, and laboratory abnormalities such as decreased phosphate, increased gamma-glutamyl transferase/transaminases, and lymphopenia [18, 19]. Due to the stringent inclusion and exclusion criteria of clinical trials, the safety profile of belumosudil may not be fully representative of the general population. Additionally, while the early clinical trials have demonstrated the efficacy and safety of belumosudil [18, 19], the real-world safety data on belumosudil is still limited. Therefore, post-marketing pharmacovigilance analyses combined with predictive and experimental approaches are needed to comprehensively evaluate the safety profile and potential adverse effects of belumosudil.

The FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS), one of the largest spontaneous reporting databases worldwide, provides a critical resource for post-marketing surveillance and early signal detection [20, 21]. In the present study, we performed a comprehensive FAERS-based pharmacovigilance analysis to characterize the safety profile of belumosudil, with particular attention to potential nervous system-related adverse events. To investigate potential pathways that may underlie these signals, we integrated network pharmacology approaches, including drug-target prediction, disease-gene mining, protein-protein interaction analysis, and GO/KEGG enrichment. In vitro experiments using SH-SY5Y cells and primary neurons were subsequently conducted to investigate the potential neurotoxic effects and related signaling pathways of belumosudil. This integrative strategy provides a comprehensive framework for evaluating the neurological safety of belumosudil and may support future clinical risk monitoring.

Methods

Data source and processing

The FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) is a publicly available database used for post-marketing safety monitoring of approved drugs and biologic therapies. It collects spontaneous adverse event (AE) reports from healthcare professionals (e.g., physicians and pharmacists), consumers, manufacturers, and other reporters. FAERS includes seven datasets: demographics and administrative information (DEMO), adverse reactions (REAC), outcomes (OUTC), drug information (DRUG), therapy start and end dates (THER), report source (RPSR), and indications (INDI). We retrieved reports involving belumosudil from FAERS covering 2021 Q3 to 2025 Q2.

To capture belumosudil-related reports, we searched the database using “belumosudil”, “belumosudil mesylate”, “Rezurock”, and “200 mg Rezurock”, and restricted the role code to primary suspect (PS). Duplicate records were removed following FDA guidance. For reports sharing the same CASEID, we retained the record with the most recent FDA receipt date (FDA_DT). If CASEID and FDA_DT were identical, we kept the record with the highest PRIMARYID. AEs were coded using MedDRA preferred terms (PTs), and analyses were performed at both the PT and system organ class (SOC) levels based on MedDRA version 26.1.

Time-to-onset and Weibull distribution analysis

Time to onset (TTO) was calculated as the number of days between START_DT (initiation of belumosudil) and EVENT_DT (AE occurrence). Records with implausible or inconsistent dates (e.g., EVENT_DT earlier than START_DT, incomplete dates, or obvious data-entry errors) were removed prior to analysis. TTO was summarized using the median and interquartile range (IQR). To characterize how AE risk changes over time, a Weibull model was fitted. The Weibull distribution is parameterized by a scale parameter (α) and a shape parameter (β), where β captures the time-dependence of the hazard. A shape estimate β < 1 with its 95% confidence interval entirely below 1 indicates a decreasing hazard over time (“early-failure” pattern), with events clustering shortly after treatment initiation. A value of β ≈ 1 with a 95% confidence interval spanning 1 suggests an approximately constant hazard (“random-failure” pattern), implying no clear time trend in AE risk. In contrast, β > 1 with a 95% confidence interval entirely above 1 indicates an increasing hazard (“wear-out” pattern), consistent with a statistically meaningful rise in AE occurrence with longer exposure.

Network pharmacology analysis

Target collection and intersection analysis

Putative targets of belumosudil were collected from the Similarity Ensemble Approach (SEA) and SwissTargetPrediction platforms. Targets from all sources were integrated, normalized to official gene symbols using UniProt, and duplicates were removed, resulting in 253 unique putative belumosudil targets. Genes associated with peripheral neuropathy were assembled from the Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man (OMIM) and GeneCards databases using the keyword “peripheral neuropathy”. For GeneCards retrieval, genes with relevance scores < 4 were excluded to improve disease relevance. After merging and removing duplicates, 1917 peripheral neuropathy-related genes were retained for downstream analyses. After dataset integration and redundancy removal, 1,917 peripheral neuropathy–related genes were retained. The overlap between belumosudil targets and peripheral neuropathy genes was subsequently determined using a Venn-diagram–based approach, and the shared genes were defined as candidate targets for downstream analyses (46 intersecting targets).

Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network construction

The intersecting targets were submitted to the STRING database to generate a PPI network, with the organism set to Homo sapiens and the minimum required interaction score set to 0.4 (medium confidence). The interaction table was exported and imported into Cytoscape for visualization and topological analysis. Hub targets were screened according to network topology metrics.

GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses

Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analyses, including biological process (BP), cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF) categories, as well as Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment, were conducted using Database for Annotation, Visualization and Integrated Discovery (DAVID) with Homo sapiens as the background. Terms/pathways with FDR-adjusted p < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. The most enriched GO terms and KEGG pathways were selected and visualized using an online bioinformatics visualization platform to support biological interpretation.

Molecular docking analysis

The 3D structures of small-molecule ligands were obtained from the PubChem database in SDF format and converted to PDB format using PyMOL 3.0. High-resolution crystal structures of target proteins were downloaded from the RCSB PDB database and preprocessed by removing water molecules and other non-essential components. Molecular docking was performed using AutoDockTools for preparation and AutoDock Vina with 50 runs, with the docking grid set to cover the active binding site. Binding affinity was evaluated based on the calculated binding energy, where values < -4.25 kcal/mol, < -5.0 kcal/mol, and < -7.0 kcal/mol indicate moderate, good, and strong binding activity, respectively, and the results were visualized using PyMOL 3.0.

Cell culture and treatment

SH-SY5Y cells, a human dopaminergic neuroblastoma line commonly used as a neuronal model, were maintained in DMEM/F12 medium with 10% Fetal Bovine Serum (FBS), 100 µg/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin under standard conditions (37 °C, 5% CO₂). For experiments, SH-SY5Y cells were treated with belumosudil (HY-15307, MedChemExpress, USA) for 24 h.

Primary cortical neuronal cultures were derived from E16-E18 C57BL/6J mouse brains. Dissociated cortical neurons were plated onto poly-D-lysine-coated coverslips or culture dishes at a density of 30,000–60,000 cells/cm². For experimental treatments, primary cortical neurons were exposed to belumosudil or FGF1 (HY-P7001, MedChemExpress, USA) for 3 days, beginning on Day 3 in culture.

Cell viability assay

Cell viability was measured using a Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8; APExBIO Technology LLC, Houston, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, SH-SY5Y cells were treated with different concentrations of belumosudil for 24 h. Following treatment, cells were incubated with 10% CCK-8 solution at 37 °C for 2 h, and absorbance was read at 450 nm using a microplate reader (BioTek Instruments, USA) to assess cell viability.

Cytotoxicity assay

Cytotoxicity was evaluated by measuring lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release using the CytoTox 96 Non-Radioactive Cytotoxicity Assay (Promega, Madison, WI, USA). In brief, SH-SY5Y cells were treated with different concentrations of belumosudil for 24 h. Then, 50 µL of the collected medium was mixed with 50 µL of substrate solution and incubated for 20 min at room temperature in the dark. Absorbance was measured at 490 nm using a microplate reader to determine cytotoxicity.

Western blot analysis

SH-SY5Y cells were treated with different concentrations of belumosudil for 1 h. Cells were lysed in ice-cold buffer containing protease inhibitors, and the lysates were then subjected to Western blot analysis. Membranes were blocked with 5% milk in TBST for 1 h, incubated overnight with primary antibodies at 4 °C, and then with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies. Protein signals were detected using enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) reagents (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The following primary antibodies were used for immunoblot analysis: anti-p-AKT1 (Ser 473) (Jan-18, EPITOMICS, USA); anti-AKT1 (9272 S, Cell Signaling Technology, USA); anti-p-GSK3β (Ser 9) (9323 S, Cell Signaling Technology, USA); and anti-β-actin (sc-8432, SantaCruz Biotechnology, USA).

Immunofluorescence staining

Primary neuronal cultures were fixed with 4% PFA and permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 for 10 min each. After blocking with 2% FBS in PBS, cells were incubated overnight at 4 °C with anti-MAP2 (sc-20172, Santa Cruz Biotechnology). Following washes, cells were treated with Alexa Fluor–conjugated secondary antibodies for 2 h, counterstained with DAPI for 5 min, and imaged using a confocal microscope (Nikon, Tokyo, Japan).

Statistical analysis

The association between belumosudil and AEs was assessed using various statistical algorithms, including the Reporting Odds Ratio (ROR), Proportional Reporting Ratio (PRR), Bayesian Confidence Propagation Neural Network (BCPNN), and Multi-Item Gamma Poisson Shrinker (MGPS). Detailed two-by-two contingency tables are provided in (Supplementary Table S1). These analyses were conducted based on disproportionality methods. The equations and criteria for the four algorithms are described in (Supplementary Table S2). Briefly, positive signals were defined as ROR025 > 1 with case number ≥ 3, PRR > 2 with χ² > 4 and case number ≥ 3, the lower limit of the 95% confidence interval of the information component (IC025) > 0 for BCPNN, and the lower one-sided 95% confidence limit of the empirical Bayesian geometric mean (EBGM05) > 2 for MGPS. AEs that met at least one of the four method thresholds were considered positive signals. All data processing and statistical analyses were performed using statistical software programs R 4.4.1.

Densitometric analysis of immunoblots was conducted using Photoshop 7.0 (Adobe, San Jose, CA, USA). Statistical analyses and data visualization were performed with GraphPad Prism 7.0 (GraphPad Software Inc., La Jolla, CA, USA). Comparisons among multiple groups were conducted using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Dunnett’s post hoc test where appropriate. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) from at least three independent experiments. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05, with the following notations: *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001; ns indicates no significant difference.

Results

General characteristics

During the study period (Q3 2021–Q2 2025), 2225 belumosudil-associated AE reports were included, encompassing 5852 specific AEs after deduplication. The comprehensive clinical characteristics of belumosudil are summarized in (Table 1). Regarding gender distribution, males accounted for 49.6% of reported AEs, exhibiting a slightly higher incidence than females (42.2%). When analyzing age groups, excluding unknown data, the majority of AEs occurred in patients aged 18 to 65 years (36.5%). In terms of report sources, the majority of AE reports were submitted by consumers (49.8%) and physicians (40.5%). Geographically, the United States reported the highest number of AEs (92.3%). Hospitalization (24.0%) was the most common among the reported clinical outcomes, followed by death (8.1%). Regarding reporting trends, most of AEs were reported in 2022 (42.2%), followed by 2024 (26.3%).

Signal detection

Signals of system organ class (SOC)

According to the collected data, we found that 25 organ systems were involved in belumosudil-related AEs (Fig. 1). The significant SOCs that met at least one of the four criteria were injury, poisoning and procedural complications, gastrointestinal disorders, investigations, infections and infestations, respiratory, thoracic and mediastinal disorders, musculoskeletal and connective tissue disorders, eye disorders, surgical and medical procedures, immune system disorders (Supplementary Table S3).

Onset time and Weibull distribution analysis of AEs

The onset times of belumosudil-associated AEs were collected from the database. Excluding false reports, total 234 AEs reported onset time. The findings revealed that most AEs occurred within the first month following belumosudil administration (n = 66, 28.2%), with the frequency of cases gradually declining thereafter (Fig. 3). The cumulative incidence curve is presented in (Fig. 4). The Weibull distribution analysis indicated an early failure pattern, suggesting a higher risk of AEs during the early phase of treatment. The median time to onset for AEs was 78.5 days (IQR: 23-169.5 days). The scale parameter (α) of the Weibull distribution was 129.86 (95% CI: 106.60-153.11), and the shape parameter (β) was 0.76 (95% CI: 0.68–0.83), further supporting an early failure model. Detailed parameters are outlined in Table 3. These findings suggest that neurological AEs may occur early after treatment initiation, highlighting the need for close monitoring.

Network pharmacology analysis of belumosudil-associated peripheral neuropathy

To further explore potential mechanisms underlying the neurological AEs identified in the FAERS analysis, we performed a network pharmacology analysis. A total of 253 putative belumosudil targets and 1917 peripheral neuropathy-related genes were collected, and 46 intersecting targets were retained as candidate potential targets for further investigation (Fig. 5A). The PPI network analysis identified several highly connected nodes, including AKT1, GSK3β, EGFR, ERBB2, CASP3, PPARG, TNF, and CTNNB1, which may represent potentially relevant signaling hubs associated with neurological toxicity (Fig. 5B).

KEGG pathway enrichment highlighted pathways related to neuronal function and survival, especially neurotrophin signaling pathway, in belumosudil-associated neurotoxicity (Fig. 5C). GO enrichment analysis of the 46 intersecting targets indicated that biological process terms were predominantly associated with neuronal and glial development and protein phosphorylation-related events, whereas cellular component terms were enriched in membrane raft/microdomain structures, distal axon, growth cone, and myelin sheath. Molecular function terms were mainly related to protein serine/threonine kinase activity and chaperone/protease binding (Fig. 5D). These findings suggest that belumosudil-associated neurological signals may involve pathways related to neuronal maintenance and neurite integrity.

Experimental validation of AKT signaling involvement in belumosudil-induced neuronal injury

To investigate the neurotoxic effects of belumosudil, SH-SY5Y cells were treated with increasing concentrations (0, 0.2, 0.5, 1, and 2 µM). CCK-8 and LDH assays revealed that Belumosudil significantly reduced cell viability and increased cytotoxicity in a dose-dependent manner, with effects becoming apparent at 0.5 µM (Fig. 6A and B). Based on network pharmacology predictions identifying AKT1 as a potential target, The molecular docking results showed that belumosudil formed hydrogen bond interactions with THR87, ILE74, and ARG15 of the AKT1 protein, as well as hydrophobic interactions with ARG76 and ILE74. The belumosudil-AKT1 docking complex exhibited strong binding activity, with a binding energy (BE) of -7.2 kcal/mol (Fig. 6C). Given that the AKT signaling pathway is a well-established regulator of neurite outgrowth [22], and serves as a critical downstream mediator of the neurotrophin signaling pathway highlighted by network pharmacology analysis, we further evaluated the effect of belumosudil on the AKT signaling pathway. Western blot analysis showed that belumosudil dose-dependently decreased AKT phosphorylation while increasing phosphorylation of its downstream target GSK3β (Fig. 6D and E). Furthermore, MAP2 immunofluorescence staining revealed that belumosudil significantly impaired neurite outgrowth, as indicated by reduced neurite length and complexity. To determine whether this effect was mediated by AKT inhibition, we utilized FGF1, a known activator of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway [23, 24]. Notably, FGF1 treatment markedly rescued the neurite defects induced by belumosudil (Fig. 6F and G). Collectively, these findings indicate that belumosudil may impair neurite development and exert neurotoxic effects, at least in part, through modulation of the AKT signaling pathway.

Discussion

Belumosudil, a potent and highly selective ROCK2 inhibitor, has demonstrated significant efficacy in treating cGVHD in clinical trials [13, 19, 25]. However, owing to limitations in trial design and small sample sizes, rare or neuropsychiatric AEs of belumosudil cannot be fully elucidated in the real world [19, 26]. Because FAERS is a spontaneous reporting system containing suspected adverse-event reports, the identified disproportionality signals should be interpreted as potential associations rather than confirmed causal relationships. Nevertheless, real-world pharmacovigilance analyses remain valuable for identifying rare or unexpected AEs that may not be fully recognized in clinical trials. In this pharmacovigilance study, we performed four types of disproportionality analyses using the FAERS database to assess new significant AEs associated with belumosudil. The results showed several potential AEs not included on the drug label, such as inappropriate schedule of product administration, product use in unapproved indication, stomatitis, dry eye, and neurological events such as neuropathy peripheral, depressed mood, and emotional disorder. These findings highlight the importance of continuous pharmacovigilance and provide clinicians with more comprehensive information for the effective risk management of belumosudil.

According to the disproportionality analysis, the most commonly reported and significant signals at SOC levels, as listed on the drug label, included gastrointestinal disorders, investigations, infections and infestations, respiratory, thoracic and mediastinal disorders, and musculoskeletal and connective tissue disorders. The significant PTs associated with gastrointestinal disorders in our study included nausea, diarrhoea, abdominal discomfort, abdominal pain upper, dry mouth, gastrointestinal disorder, stomatitis, and dyspepsia. stomatitis is identified as a new potential AE. Consistent with these findings, three multicenter, open-label clinical trials have also reported nausea and vomiting as common AEs related to belumosudil [18, 19, 27]. Notably, when gastrointestinal disorders occur, the use of proton pump inhibitors significantly reduces the bioavailability of belumosudil, necessitating higher doses for therapeutic efficacy [26]. Regarding infections and infestations, our study identified several infection-related PTs, including pneumonia, COVID-19, nasopharyngitis, bronchitis, infection, and influenza. Multiple phase clinical trials have reported pneumonia as a severe AE (grade ≥ 3) in patients receiving belumosudil 200 mg [18, 19, 25]. More importantly, patients treated with belumosudil were at an increased risk of COVID-19 exposure. A multicenter, open-label, single-arm study conducted in 21 Japanese patients (≥ 12 years old) with steroid-dependent or steroid-resistant cGVHD found that 9.5% were diagnosed with COVID-19, underscoring the need for close monitoring [19]. The increased reporting of infections may partly reflect the immunocompromised status of patients with cGVHD, concomitant immunosuppressive therapies, and post-transplant-related factors, although a potential contribution of belumosudil cannot be excluded. These findings indicate that clinicians should concern on signs of infection, especially in patients with a history of recurrent infections or other risk factors. If a serious infection develops during treatment, discontinuation of belumosudil should be considered. In addition, pulmonary infection could be associated with the SOC of respiratory, thoracic and mediastinal disorders. The cough and lung disorder are common AEs listed on the drug label. For investigations and musculoskeletal and connective tissue disorders, commonly reported PTs included hepatic enzyme increased, blood pressure increased, blood creatinine increased, back pain, muscle spasms, myalgia, and cough. In clinical practice, these reports support routine assessment of general condition and timely laboratory follow-up, particularly liver and renal function. In this study, significant signals at the SOC level that were not listed on the drug label included injury, poisoning, and procedural complications, as well as eye disorders, both of which warrant attention. Within the SOC of injury, poisoning, and procedural complications, the identified PTs included inappropriate schedule of product administration, product use in an unapproved indication, product dose omission issue, fall, and product dose omission in error. These PTs mainly reflect medication-use and administration-related issues in real-world clinical practice, including adherence challenges, off-label use, and dosing irregularities. Addressing these concerns requires enhanced patient education, optimized dosing regimens, and increased awareness among healthcare professionals to minimize potential AEs. Regarding eye disorders, dry eye and cataract are two relatively potential adverse reactions, highlighting the need for preventive measures against eye-related complications. However, ocular GVHD and dry eye disease are common after allo-HSCT and can be severe in cGVHD, while cataract risk is strongly influenced by prior corticosteroid exposure and transplant-related factors [28].

Although the SOCs of psychiatric disorders and nervous system disorders did not reach statistical significance, several PTs including depressed mood, emotional disorder, and neuropathy peripheral showed disproportional reporting. Notably, the consistency across disproportionality algorithms varied among these neurological adverse events. Depressed mood and emotional disorder were supported by all four signal detection methods, indicating relatively robust associations, whereas neuropathy peripheral was identified by only two algorithms (ROR and BCPNN), suggesting a comparatively weaker signal that warrants cautious interpretation and further validation. The biological basis for these symptoms is not well defined, but ROCK2 signaling has documented roles in neuroimmune regulation. Experimental work indicates that ROCK2 inhibition can modulate microglial responses after brain injury and may negate beneficial effects attributed to microglial repopulation in certain contexts, supporting a plausible neuroimmune link [29–31]. In clinical practice, when patients develop mood changes or neurological complaints during belumosudil therapy, timely neurological/psychiatric assessment and symptom-directed management should be considered according to standard care, such as fluoxetine and mecobalamin [32, 33]. To explore the biological plausibility of these neurological side effects, we changed our focus from data mining to mechanistic investigation, defining this broader phenomenon as belumosudil-induced neurotoxicity. Importantly, unlike conventional FAERS disproportionality studies that rely solely on statistical signal detection, our study further integrated network pharmacology, molecular docking, and neuronal experiments to provide biological plausibility and mechanistic support for the observed neurological signals. Peripheral neuropathy was selected for downstream network pharmacology analysis because it represents a clinically meaningful and biologically interpretable neurological phenotype with relatively well-characterized disease-associated gene datasets. First, network pharmacology identified 46 intersecting targets between belumosudil-associated targets and neuropathy-related genes, with hub nodes including AKT1, GSK3β, ERBB2, EGFR, and CASP3, and enrichment of neurotrophin and ErbB signaling pathways. These pathways are central to neuronal survival, axonal integrity, and glia interactions [34–36]. AKT activity is required for axonal wrapping and myelin sheath formation in the nervous system, and pharmacological AKT inhibition reduces myelin-associated proteins in neuron-Schwann cell co-cultures [22, 37, 38]. To validate the computational predictions and support the FAERS signal, we performed in vitro experiments. Belumosudil treatment caused dose-dependent neurotoxicity in SH-SY5Y neuronal cells, with toxicity observed starting at lower concentrations and increasing at higher doses. In primary neurons, belumosudil exposure significantly impaired neurite outgrowth, indicating compromised neuronal connectivity. Importantly, co-treatment with FGF1, a known activator of neuronal survival pathways, partially rescued the neurite defects. These findings demonstrate that belumosudil may influence neuronal structure and function, providing preliminary experimental evidence that supports the biological relevance of the clinical neurological signals observed in post-marketing data.

Based on data from the FAERS database, our study identified several potential adverse reaction signals associated with belumosudil, particularly those involving the nervous system, providing valuable insights for improving the safety of clinical medication use. However, several limitations should be acknowledged. Although our in vitro experiments provided mechanistic evidence supporting the neurotoxic effects of belumosudil, FAERS, as a spontaneous reporting system, is inherently subject to limitations [39–41], including underreporting, duplicate submissions, and incomplete clinical information. Disproportionality analyses are useful for signal detection but cannot establish causality. In addition, residual confounding related to disease severity, transplantation-related complications, prior neurotoxic therapies, and concomitant treatments remains difficult to eliminate, and further controlled investigations are warranted to clarify the true association between belumosudil and neurological adverse events. Patients with cGVHD frequently receive concomitant therapies, including calcineurin inhibitors, corticosteroids, and prior chemotherapeutic agents, all of which may independently contribute to neurological complications such as peripheral neuropathy. Therefore, the neurological signals identified in the present study should be interpreted with caution, and a causal relationship cannot be established based solely on FAERS data. Furthermore, only a subset of FAERS reports contained complete and valid time-to-onset information, which may introduce selection bias and reduce the robustness of the Weibull distribution analysis. Adverse-event reporting in patients with cGVHD may also be influenced by underlying disease status, transplantation-related complications, and concomitant immunosuppressive therapies, potentially contributing to residual confounding in the FAERS analysis. Lastly, our mechanistic investigations were primarily conducted in SH-SY5Y cells and therefore require further validation in primary neurons and animal models. Although AKT signaling appeared to play a central role in belumosudil-induced neuronal injury, the involvement of additional signaling pathways cannot be excluded.

In conclusion, using FAERS reports since 2021, we characterized the post-marketing safety profile of belumosudil, confirming known adverse reactions while identifying several potential label-unlisted signals, including depressed mood, emotional disorder, and neuropathy peripheral. To further explore the mechanisms underlying these clinical signals, our combined network pharmacology and in vitro experiments provide exploratory evidence that belumosudil may affect neuronal viability in a dose-dependent manner and impairs neurite outgrowth by targeting and inhibiting the AKT signaling pathway. Collectively, our findings highlight the need for increased clinical attention to neurological symptoms during belumosudil therapy and provide mechanistic insight into its potential neurotoxic effects.

The bisegmental Cobb angle in osteoporotic spine fractures: Does it influence treatment decision or functional outcome?
Authors: Philipp Schenk, Bernhard W Ullrich, Felix C Kohler, Falko Schwarz, Klaus J Schnake, Michael A Scherer, Gregor Schmeiser, Katja Liepold, Marion Riehle, Michael Müller, Martin Bäumlein, Sebastian Katscher, Max J Scheyerer, Georg Osterhoff, Kai Sprengel, Ulrich JA Spiegl, the Working Group Osteoporotic Fractures of the Spine, Section of the German Society of Orthopaedics and Trauma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionOsteoporotic vertebral compression fractures (OVCF) represent a growing clinical challenge and the effect of bisegmental Cobb angles in treatment decision and outcome prediction for remains unclear.Material and methodsThis prospective multicenter study analyzed 350 patients with thoracolumbar OVCF (OF2–OF4). Bisegmental Cobb angles (CA) were measured at the day of treatment decision, discharge, and follow-up. Associations with treatment, OF classification, pain, and functional outcomes were analyzed.ResultsPatients treated with instrumentation showed more pronounced kyphotic alignment than conservatively treated patients at baseline (−11 ± 12° vs. −7 ± 14°, p = 0.042). CA correlated weakly with OF-Score (r = −0.167, p = 0.002) and OF classification (r = −0.195, p < 0.001), but not with pain (p = 0.789). ROC analyses demonstrated poor discriminatory power of CA for treatment (AUC = 0.557, p = 0.078); the predefined kyphosis threshold of −15° was not predictive. Multinomial logistic regression confirmed that OF classification (p < 0.001) and pain intensity (p < 0.001), but not CA (p = 0.310), independently predicted the performed treatment. Over time, surgically treated patients achieved significant fracture reduction (−6 ± 7° during hospitalization, p < 0.001) with partial loss at follow-up, whereas conservatively treated patients showed progressive increased kyphosis. CA were not consistently associated with functional outcomes.Discussion and conclusionThe CA alone has limited value for guiding treatment decisions in thoracolumbar OVCF, and fixed angular thresholds are not clinically useful. However, restoration of the segmental sagittal profile appears to be associated with clinical outcome after surgical treatment. For this reason, the bisegmental Cobb angle certainly does not play the main role in the decision on treatment, but it should be included as a secondary parameter.Keywords: Osteoporotic vertebral compression fracture, Cobb angle, Sagittal alignment, OF classification, Treatment decision-making, Functional outcome

Introduction

Osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures (OVCF) represent a growing clinical challenge in an ageing population. The OF classification provides a morphology-based framework to stratify fracture stability and to guide treatment recommendations (Blattert et al., 2018; Schnake et al., 2018). Based on fracture morphology and the presumed degree of instability, treatment options range from conservative management to minimally invasive cement augmentation techniques, posterior instrumentation up to multi-level and dorsoventral stabilization. Conservative treatment is generally recommended for morphologically stable fractures without relevant posterior wall involvement or deformity (OF1 and OF2), whereas surgical stabilization is clearly indicated for highly unstable fracture patterns such as OF5 fractures (Blattert et al., 2018; Schnake et al., 2018; Ullrich et al., 2023a). Although OF2 fractures are classified as stable according to morphological criteria, clinical decision-making in this subgroup appears less straightforward. Previous studies and clinical experience indicate that a substantial proportion of fractures are treated surgically, most commonly by minimally invasive cement augmentation (vertebro- or kyphoplasty), despite recommendations favoring conservative management (Ullrich et al., 2023b; Heyde et al., 2023). This discrepancy suggests that factors beyond fracture morphology alone may influence treatment allocation in OVCF, challenging the assumption of uniform clinical stability. The OF-Score for therapy recommendations was developed to enable more informed therapy recommendations using additional clinical and functional parameters in addition to the OF classification (Blattert et al., 2018; Schnake et al., 2018; Ullrich et al., 2023b). In order to provide more precise recommendations regarding therapy strategy, predictors for the outcomes must be identified. We already know that posterior stabilization particularly using the hybrid technique has the ability to partially restore the regional alignment in the long term (Spiegl et al., 2019). Bae et al. found that differences in Cobb angle are independent predictors of favorable outcomes after vertebral augmentation, supporting its relevance despite limited correlation with standard functional scores (Bae et al., 2022). Surgically untreated and uncorrected fracture-related spinal deformities, delayed neurological deficits, chronic pain and can lead to permanent spinal deformities and functional limitations (Vaccaro et al., 2001; Park et al., 2023). However, it is unclear where the threshold for deformity or misalignment is in relation to the bisegmental Cobb angles or whether there is a threshold at all.

The aim of this study was to assess the role of the bisegmental Cobb angle in treatment decision-making and its association with functional outcome in patients with OVCF.

Methods

In this study a subset cohort from the EOFTT study (Evaluation of the Osteoporotic Fracture Classification, Treatment Score, and Therapy Recommendations - EOFTT) is investigated. This study analyses multicentric and prospectively collected data, investigating patients with OVCF in the thoracolumbar junction (Th11 to L2). All procedures were conducted in accordance with relevant laws and institutional guidelines, and approved by the ethics committee (Medical Association Saxony-Anhalt, file 31/17). The EOFTT study was a non-randomized, multicenter clinical trial, where treatment decisions were made by the treating physicians based on the best clinical practice. The patient's written consent was obtained after receiving detailed information.

In addition to age, sex, and fracture location, fracture morphology was assessed using the OF classification (Blattert et al., 2018; Schnake et al., 2018). Due to clear treatment recommendations for OF1 and OF5 fractures and their low frequency in the EOFTT study, patients with these fractures were excluded from this study (Blattert et al., 2018; Schnake et al., 2018). Sagittal, bisegmental Cobb angles were measured in CT scans and x-rays for the fractured segment at the day at treatment decision, discharge and follow-up. Negative Cobb angles indicate a kyphotic bisegmental angulation of the vertebrae, while positive Cobb angles indicate a lordotic alignment.

Based on expert consensus within the study group, −15° was selected as a pragmatic cutoff reflecting thresholds commonly applied in clinical decision-making. As no validated threshold has been established in the literature, this value was used for exploratory, hypothesis-generating subgroup analyses to assess whether a defined degree of kyphotic deformity was associated with surgical treatment allocation. Bone quality was assessed using Hounsfield units, according to Ullrich et al. (2022). The therapy performed was recorded in three categories: conservative treatment, kyphoplasty or vertebroplasty, and instrumentation. For clarity, kyphoplasty and vertebroplasty were combined under the category "augmentation only". Instrumentation included all types of surgical therapy involving the insertion of fixators, screws, rods, or cages, with or without augmentation of the screws or index vertebra. Instrumentation also includes dorsoventral stabilization procedures.

Fracture-related neurological deficits and pain were assessed. Pain was evaluated using a visual analog scale (VAS) from 0 to 10. Additionally, the OF-Score for treatment recommendation was calculated. The OF-Score is a composite sum score based clinical, and functional variables. It reflects the individual, fracture-related health status with varying weights for each variable. An OF-Score of 6 does not provide a specific treatment recommendation. For an OF-Score <6 points, conservative therapy is recommended, while an OF-Scores >6 recommends surgical therapy, without specifying a particular surgical approach. However, it must be emphasized that the final treatment decision remains at the treating physician and the OF-Score merely offers a recommendation for treatment.

In addition to Cobb angles, OF scores, and pain, the functional outcome of patients was evaluated assessing the functional status and disability (ODI), independence in activity of daily living (Barthel Index), and health related quality of life (EQ5D-5L) at admission, discharge, and follow-up. Additionally, subjective health status was evaluated using the Visual Analogue Scale for health (EQ5D-VAS). Several follow-up examinations lasting up to one year were planned. The latest available follow-up was used, with a minimum interval of six weeks after treatment.

Differences in patient characteristics at baseline between treatment groups (conservative treatment, augmentation only, instrumentation) were analyzed using the Chi2 and Fisher's exact test general linear models (GLM) for continuous variables, including Bonferroni-adjusted post-hoc comparisons where appropriate. The distribution of Cobb angle groups (≤−15° vs. >−15°) was compared between treatment groups and across OF classifications using Chi2 and Fisher's exact tests. Group differences in Cobb angles, OF classification, and OF-Score were assessed using GLM. Associations between Cobb angles and clinical parameters (OF-Score, pain) were analyzed using Pearson correlation coefficients and Spearman correlation, as appropriate. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analyses were performed to evaluate the ability of sagittal bisegmental Cobb angles as treatment decision to predict treatment allocation (conservative treatment, augmentation only, and instrumentation). Model performance was quantified using the area under the curve (AUC), and cut-off values were calculated using the Youden index, if statistically significant. Factors associated with treatment allocation were analyzed using multinomial logistic regression model with conservative treatment as the reference category, including OF classification, pain at the day of treatment decision, and Cobb angles as independent variables. Model performance was assessed using Nagelkerke's R2. Changes in Cobb angles over time were analyzed using GLM for repeated measures with treatment group as the between-subject factor. The association between Cobb angles and functional outcome (VAS, ODI, Barthel Index, EQ5D-5L, EQ5D-VAS) was examined using linear regression analyses. Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics, Version 29 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

From the EOFTT study cohort with 518 patients, a subgroup of 350 patients (68%), consisting of 87 males and 263 females (mean age: 74 ± 10 years, range: 41 to 94 years), with OVCF in the thoraco-lumbar junction, excluding OF classification types OF1 and OF5, was included in the current study. The proportion of males and females (p = 0.184) and age (p = 0.391) did not differ significantly from the total EOFTT cohort.

Regarding bone quality and fracture localization, no significant differences were found between the three treatment groups (conservatively, augmentation only, instrumentation), respectively (p = 0.478, p = 0.081). However, differences were observed in OF classification and pain (p < 0.001, Table 1). Patients who underwent instrumentation showed a severe fracture morphology (OF classification) than those treated conservatively (p < 0.001) and those who treated with augmentation only (p < 0.001). No significant differences in OF classification were observed between the conservative treated patients and patients with augmentation only (p = 0.230).

Patients who received augmentation only showed no significant differences Cobb angles compared to those with instrumentation (p = 0.189) and to those who were treated conservatively (p = 1.000). The OF-Score showed differences in each of the pairwise comparison between treatment groups (conservatively < augmentation only < instrumentation) with p < 0.001. In the GLM, using the OF classification and the type of therapy as fixed factors, both variables prove to be significant with regard to different Cobb angles (each p < 0.001). No significant interaction effect was found (p = 0.602). Fig. 1 shows the Cobb angles, differentiated in OF classification and treatment. Cobb angles in patients with OF4 fractures show no significant differences between the treatment groups (p > 0.079). For patients with OF2 or OF3 fractures, conservatively treated patients show less kyphotic angles compared to patients who receive augmentation only or instrumentation (OF2: augmentation only p < 0.001, instrumentation p < 0.008; OF3: augmentation only and instrumentation each p < 0.001). The Cobb angles of both surgical procedures do not differ significantly in patients with OF2 fractures (p = 1.000) and OF3 fractures (p = 1.000). Within each of the therapy groups, pairwise comparison show that kyphotic alignment increases with increasing OF classification (p < 0.031).

A total of 131 patients received a recommendation for conservative treatment, of whom 76 (58%) were treated accordingly. Surgical treatment was recommended in 167 patients, and 133 (80%) underwent surgery as recommended. In 52 patients, the treatment recommendation was classified as indifferent.

Patients with conservative recommendations (OF-Score <6 points) but surgical treatment showed marginally more pronounced kyphotic Cobb angle (−1±1°) than patients who received conservative treatment in accordance to the recommendation (p < 0.001). Patients with surgical recommendations (OF score >6 points) who were treated conservatively showed no significant differences in Cobb angles compared to patients treated surgically in accordance to the recommendation (p = 0.525). In patients with an indifferent treatment recommendation, no difference in Cobb angle was observed between conservatively treated and surgically treated patients (p = 1.000). With respect to the distribution of Cobb angle groups (≤−15° vs. >−15°), no significant differences were observed between patients treated in accordance with the recommendation and those treated non-conformingly, neither among patients with a conservative treatment recommendation (p = 0.445) nor among those with a surgical recommendation (p = 0.222). Likewise, in the group of patients with an indifferent treatment recommendation, no differences were observed between conservatively and surgically treated patients (p = 0.704).

At the day of treatment decision, the Cobb angles show a significant and weak correlation with the OF-Score with r = −0.167 (p = 0.002) and with the OF classification r = −0.195 (p < 0.001) but not with the pain (p = 0.789). The greater the kyphotic alignment in the thoraco-lumbar junction, the greater the OF-Score and the higher the OF classification.

Using Cobb angles in a ROC analysis to predict the surgical therapy, an AUC of 0.557 (p = 0.078) was found. Differentiation between conservative treatment and surgical instrumentation showed a statistically significant but poor AUC of 0.583 (p = 0.019), with a cut-off value of +9°, corresponding to lordotic alignment (sensitivity 55%, specificity 58%). ROC analysis revealed no significant differentiation between the two surgical therapies (AUC = 0.568, p = 0.089) and between conservative therapy and patients with augmentation only (AUC = 0.513, p = 0.758), and accordingly, no cut-off value was calculated.

A multinomial logistic regression was performed to predict the type of therapy (augmentation only or instrumentation) using conservative treatment as the reference category. The model showed moderate explanatory power with a Nagelkerke's’ R2 = 0.495 (p < 0.001). Among the predictors, the OF classification (p < 0.001) and pain (p < 0.001) had significant effects, while the Cobb angles did not (p = 0.310). The model correctly classified 63% of cases: 80% (N = 83) of conservative treatments, 26% (N = 21) of augmentation only and 78% (N = 111) of instrumentation treatments.

The GLM for repeated measures revealed a significant change in Cobb angle over time (p < 0.001) and a significant interaction effect between treatment and time point (p < 0.001). No differences were found at the day of treatment decision between conservative and surgically treated patients (p = 0.564). At discharge (p < 0.001) and the follow-up (p = 0.013) conservatively treated patients showed more pronounced kyphotic Cobb angles compared to surgically treated patients (Fig. 2A). For the conservatively treated group, a significant loss of Cobb angles was found at the follow-up compared to the day of treatment decision (−3±7°, p = 0.012). During the hospital stay (p = 0.819) and from discharge to follow-up (p = 0.053) no significant changes were found. For the surgically treated patients Cobb angles differed between all observations with p < 0.001. After an initial reduction of 6 ± 7° during the hospital stay, a loss of 3 ± 7° at follow-up was found. Comparing to the day of treatment decision a remaining reduction of 3 ± 8° at the follow-up was observed.

When analyzing all three therapy groups, a significant effect of time-point (p < 0.001) and an interaction between time-point and therapy (p < 0.001) were found. At the day of treatment decision, patients with instrumentation showed pronounced kyphotic Cobb angles, as those with augmentation only (p = 0.031). Other pairwise comparisons at the day of treatment decision showed no significance (p > 0.376). At discharge conservatively treated patients showed more kyphotic angles than both surgical groups (instrumentation p = 0.003, augmentation only p = 0.013). Both surgical groups showed no differences (p = 1.000). At follow-up no significant differences between the three treatment groups in Cobb angles could be found (p > 0.084, Fig. 2B).

Patients with augmentation only showed during the hospital stay a significant reduction of kyphotic alignment of 3 ± 7° (p = 0.007). At follow-up, a loss of reduction of 3 ± 7° after discharge was observed (p = 0.004), which was not significant different compared to the Cobb angle at the day of the treatment decision (p = 1.000). In patients with instrumentation an initial reduction of 8 ± 7° (p < 0.001) could be achieved during the hospital stay. During the follow-up period, a loss of reduction of 4 ± 7° was observed (p < 0.001), resulting in a remained reduction of 4 ± 8° compared to the day of treatment decision (p < 0.001).

Linear regression analyses revealed no consistent or clinically relevant associations between Cobb angles and functional outcomes in the total cohort. Across all time points, Cobb angles were not significantly associated with pain (VAS) or health-related quality of life (EQ5D-VAS), and only weak, time-limited associations were observed for disability (ODI), functional independence (Barthel Index), and EQ5D-5L (Table 3). In subgroup analyses, more pronounced associations were observed, most notably in surgically treated patients. In this group, greater lordotic alignment was consistently associated with lower pain levels across all time points, with the strongest effect at admission (VAS: β = −0.26, p < 0.001). Cobb angles were also associated with functional independence, with increasing lordosis corresponding to a reduction in Barthel Index scores (β range −2.5 to −3.0 per degree, p < 0.001). Discussion

This multicenter study evaluated the role of bisegmental Cobb angle in patients with OVCF of the thoracolumbar spine to treatment decision and the functional outcome. We hypothesized that larger kyphotic misalignment would increase the likelihood for surgical intervention. Our analysis, however, demonstrated that the Cobb angle has only limited predictive value. This suggests that treatment decisions in OVCF are driven by a multifactorial clinical assessment rather than isolated radiographic parameters. While correction of the regional Cobb angle reflecting restoration of local lordosis showed an association with postoperative pain reduction after surgical treatment, its relationship with broader clinical outcomes was less consistent. In particular, associations with functional independence and quality-of-life measures were heterogeneous and likely influenced by factors such as fracture severity and treatment selection.

Accordingly, the observed relationship between bisegmental Cobb angels and clinical outcome should not be interpreted as general causality. Patients achieving greater deformity correction were typically those undergoing surgery for more severe fractures and poorer baseline status, introducing a relevant risk of confounding by indication. Therefore, although improved radiographic alignment may contribute to pain relief, it cannot be assumed to directly translate into superior functional independence or quality of life, particularly in the short-to mid-term.

Generally, the Cobb angles are well established for assessing spinal deformity, it did not prove to be an independent determinant of treatment allocation in our cohort. ROC analyses failed to define a clinically meaningful threshold, and multivariate models confirmed its lack of explanatory power beyond the OF classification. This is consistent with earlier observations that Cobb angles and angular parameters alone, although reproducible, are insufficient to guide therapy (Bae et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2023; Schömig et al., 2023). While Bae et al. previously showed that changes in Cobb angle may predict outcomes after vertebral augmentation, the predictive strength was weak and context dependent (Bae et al., 2022). Similarly, comparative reviews emphasize that the complexity of therapeutic decision making in osteoporotic fractures cannot be reflected by the use and analysis of radiographic parameters alone (Liu et al., 2023; Pieroh et al., 2023).

In contrast, both fracture morphology and pain intensity were clearly associated with treatment strategy in our study. Patients with higher OF classification types and severe pain were treated surgically more frequently, regardless of their Cobb angles. These findings confirm the use of the OF classification and the OF-Score, which integrate fracture morphology, neurological findings, pain and additional variables into structured decision tools (Schnake et al., 2015; Mekariya et al., 2024). Multiple validation studies have demonstrated good reliability and reproducibility of the OF classification across observers and centers, making it a robust basis for therapy recommendations (Mekariya et al., 2024; Scherer et al., 2025; Jang et al., 2022).

Authors have proposed Cobb angle thresholds associated with pronounced kyphosis as potential indicators for surgical intervention (Munting, 2010). Based on expert discussions within the DGOU working group on osteoporotic fractures, a threshold of −15° was proposed by spine surgery specialists as a clinically relevant reference value in this content. Our results could not validate such cutoffs as clinical meaningful decision thresholds. These findings further emphasize that no single radiographic cutoff adequately captures the complexity of clinical decision-making in this patient population. The discussed threshold of −15° was not predictive, and although a cutoff of +9° reached statistical significance in one subgroup comparison, its sensitivity and specificity were not sufficient for clinical use. This is in line with recent systematic reviews showing that sagittal alignment parameters should be interpreted in combination with clinical factors rather than in isolation (Liu et al., 2023; Scheyerer et al., 2022; Keynan et al., 2006). Furthermore, differences in Cobb measurement between supine and standing radiographs can reach clinically relevant levels, complicating the use of universal thresholds (Bae et al., 2022). Advances in imaging technology and AI-based automation have improved measurement, but they do not resolve the fundamental issue of limited clinical predictive power (Chung et al., 2018; Langensiepen et al., 2013; Khalid et al., 2024).

From a clinical perspective, the bisegmental Cobb angle should not be the sole determinant of treatment allocation in osteoporotic vertebral fractures. However, it remains a relevant parameter for quantifying deformity and monitoring its progression. In conservative management, increasing kyphosis may indicate treatment failure, whereas in surgical cases the Cobb angle guides intended correction and enables objective assessment of deformity correction and secondary loss of reduction (Kohler et al., 2022; Aydin et al., 2024). However, therapeutic decisions are and should primarily be guided by fracture morphology, pain, neurological findings, and overall patient condition. Overreliance on angular thresholds risks overtreatment or under-treatment, whereas integrative models such as the OF-Score provide a more balanced and patient centered framework (Schnake et al., 2018; Mekariya et al., 2024). This multifactorial approach is supported by recent reviews and consensus guidelines from the DGOU and AO Spine, which discourage reliance on single-parameter cutoffs and instead emphasize a comprehensive evaluation of fracture stability, clinical burden, and patient-specific risks (Mekariya et al., 2024; Konnopka et al., 2024; Scheyerer et al., 2025). In practice, this means that Cobb angle measurement should complement, but never replace, classification- and symptom-based assessments.

The strengths of this study include its prospective multicenter design, which reflects real-world practice across diverse institutions, and the use of standardized CT-based Cobb measurements, which offer good reproducibility. Nonetheless, some limitations must be acknowledged. Imaging in supine position may underestimate deformity under physiological load (Bae et al., 2022; Jacobi et al., 2024; Sadeghzadeh et al., 2024). Variability in treatment strategies and patient heterogeneity between the participating centers may have influenced therapy allocation. In addition, interobserver variability of Cobb angle measurements were not formally assessed, although prior studies have demonstrated good to excellent reliability of this parameter. Finally, the absence of long-term follow-up data prevents conclusions on deformity progression, late complications, and sustained quality of life. Future research should address these gaps. Future work should also focus on identifying factors and imaging technique to identify compensative parameters for the spinal alignment and integrating multiple predictors into comprehensive prognostic models. Besides fracture morphology and pain, parameters such as bone quality assessed via Hounsfield units (Ullrich et al., 2018), spinopelvic balance (Park et al., 2023; Jacobi et al., 2024; Laouissat et al., 2018), and muscle quality or frailty indices should be incorporated (Agarwal et al., 2021; Chan et al., 2021; Veronesi et al., 2021). Nomogram-based approaches have already shown promise in predicting refracture risk and tailoring treatment strategies (Zhang et al., 2023; Qian et al., 2023). Such models require prospective validation with long-term outcomes to refine cutoff values and define the true prognostic role of deformity parameters. From a clinical perspective, this supports the concept that Cobb angle should be interpreted as part of a multidimensional decision framework rather than as an independent decision criterion.

Conclusions

In summary, the bisegmental Cobb angle alone has limited predictive value for therapy decisions in osteoporotic fractures of the thoracolumbar junction. Cobb angles show associations with fracture morphology. However, the extent of local sagittal restoration, appears to have a clinically impact. The bisegmental Cobb angle should therefore be regarded as a descriptive parameter for deformity assessment and follow up, rather than a standalone determinant of treatment strategy.

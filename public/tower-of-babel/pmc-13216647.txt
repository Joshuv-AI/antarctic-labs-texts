A hybrid transformer–zero-shot learning framework with Muon optimization for intelligent channel estimation in MIMO wireless systems
Authors: Wessam M Salama, Moustafa H Aly, Samah Alshathri, Walid El-Shafai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFor MIMO wireless systems, accurate channel estimation is essential. However, traditional and current Deep Learning (DL) techniques have poor generalization to unknown situations and necessitate repeated retraining. For intelligent MIMO channel estimation, this study suggests a novel hybrid framework that combines Transformer designs, Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL), and the Muon optimizer. By projecting channel instances into a shared semantic-attribute space, the ZSL component allows precise inference under previously unknown SNR levels and fading conditions without retraining. The Muon optimizer offers better generalization and faster convergence than Adam, while the Transformer uses self-attention to capture intricate spatial-temporal connections. ZSL–Muon, Transformer–ZSL, and the whole Transformer–ZSL–Muon model are the three configurations that are assessed. The whole hybrid consistently outperforms LS, MMSE, CNN-, GRU-, and Transformer-only baselines in MSE over a broad SNR range, according to extensive simulations under quasi-static and time-varying fading. The proposed framework is rigorously evaluated over a wide SNR range and under both quasi-static block fading and time-varying fading channel models. Experimental results demonstrate that the hybrid Transformer–ZSL–Muon model consistently surpasses traditional estimators (e.g., LS and MMSE) and existing DL-based techniques in terms of Mean Squared Error (MSE), particularly in complex and unseen channel conditions. A remarkable finding reveals that increasing the number of antennas from 2 to 128 at an SNR of 30 dB results in a 94.44% MSE reduction, underscoring the potential of massive MIMO implementations. Furthermore, the proposed framework achieves approximately 93.75% MSE improvement over MMSE and 96.67% over LS under the same SNR conditions. Introduction

Accurate channel estimation is a cornerstone of wireless communication system optimization, as it directly enables the implementation of effective power allocation, modulation schemes, and channel coding strategies. Among the various technological advances in this domain, Multiple-Input Multiple-Output (MIMO) systems have emerged as a key enabler of high-throughput, reliable, and spectrally efficient communications. By exploiting multipath propagation through spatial diversity and multiplexing, MIMO systems, utilizing multiple antennas at both the transmitter and receiver ends, can significantly enhance overall system capacity, spectral efficiency, and link reliability1,2. These benefits are particularly vital in the context of dense user environments and data-intensive applications.

Modern wireless communication standards such as Wi-Fi, Long-Term Evolution (LTE), and Fifth-Generation (5G) networks have widely adopted MIMO architectures due to their performance benefits. A fundamental requirement for the successful operation of these systems is the availability of accurate and timely Channel State Information (CSI). Precise CSI allows the system to adapt transmission parameters dynamically, enabling not only interference mitigation and improved robustness but also energy-efficient resource allocation across varying propagation conditions3–8. As wireless networks evolve toward 6G and beyond, the demand for intelligent and scalable channel estimation solutions becomes increasingly critical.

Despite its critical importance, accurate channel estimation in MIMO systems remains a technically challenging task due to a variety of factors, including multipath fading, spatiotemporal correlations, suboptimal pilot design, complex antenna configurations, and inherent hardware limitations9–12. These challenges often manifest in the form of signal delays, amplitude fluctuations, and interference caused by overlapping multipath components. As a result, the task of acquiring precise and independent CSI becomes significantly more difficult in practical deployment scenarios, especially in highly dynamic or mobile environments.

To address these challenges, a broad spectrum of channel estimation techniques has been developed. These include traditional pilot-based estimators, such as Least Squares (LS) and Minimum Mean Square Error (MMSE) methods1,13, which are valued for their analytical simplicity but are often limited in performance under complex or time-varying conditions. More sophisticated model-based approaches such as Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) and Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (OMP)14,15, as well as feedback-oriented techniques like Channel State Information Feedback (CSIF), have also been explored to improve estimation accuracy.

In recent years, DL-based approaches have gained substantial attention for their ability to model non-linear and complex channel behaviors without requiring explicit statistical modeling. Techniques employing Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), and more recently, Transformer architectures, have demonstrated considerable promise in learning channel representations directly from raw or pilot-based inputs. These DL models are capable of performing end-to-end CSI reconstruction, offering enhanced adaptability and estimation accuracy, especially in large-scale MIMO settings and rapidly changing environments16–19.

Research problem

In MIMO wireless communication systems, accurate channel estimation plays a pivotal role in enabling the optimization of key transmission parameters such as power allocation, modulation strategies, and coding schemes. Without reliable CSI, system performance significantly degrades, particularly in terms of spectral efficiency, data throughput, and error rates. However, existing channel estimation techniques, including both classical model-based methods and contemporary DL approaches like CNNs and Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs), face substantial limitations in their ability to generalize across dynamically changing channel environments. These environments are often characterized by diverse fading profiles, varying signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) conditions, and different levels of user mobility.

The core issue lies in the lack of adaptability and generalization in these traditional models. Most of them are trained in a supervised, scenario-specific manner, which necessitates frequent retraining or fine-tuning when encountering unseen channel conditions. This requirement poses a major barrier to real-time deployment, especially in rapidly evolving wireless environments such as those expected in 6G networks, vehicular communication systems, and IoT-enabled smart infrastructure.

This study addresses the pressing challenge of designing a generalizable, robust, and adaptive channel estimation framework that can accurately infer CSI in unseen or untrained scenarios without incurring the cost of retraining. Specifically, the objective is to develop a model that can semantically interpret and project channel characteristics into a latent feature space, enabling zero-shot generalization to new channel conditions. At the same time, the model must maintain training stability, achieve fast convergence, and exhibit resilience across a wide SNR spectrum, making it suitable for scalable, intelligent wireless communication systems.

Motivation

The exponential growth in demand for reliable, high-speed, and ultra-low-latency wireless communication, driven by emerging technologies such as 6G networks, autonomous vehicles, smart cities, and the Internet of Things (IoT), has intensified the need for highly accurate, adaptive, and flexible channel estimation methods. These next-generation applications require systems that can operate seamlessly across a wide spectrum of environmental conditions, mobility scenarios, and interference levels. Accurate channel estimation is therefore a critical enabler of robust and intelligent communication infrastructure.

While traditional model-based estimators and DL approaches, including CNNs and GRUs, have made strides in improving channel estimation performance, they are inherently constrained by several critical limitations. Most notably, these models rely heavily on large, diverse training datasets, are sensitive to variations in channel statistics, and lack the capacity to generalize effectively to unseen or evolving channel conditions. As wireless environments become increasingly heterogeneous and time-varying, retraining or fine-tuning models for each new scenario becomes both computationally expensive and operationally infeasible.

To address these challenges, this research is motivated by three cutting-edge techniques that together promise to deliver a transformative solution. First, the Muon optimizer is introduced to improve training dynamics by accelerating convergence, enhancing generalization, and preventing overfitting through adaptive regularization and dynamic learning rate adjustment. Second, the integration of Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL) allows the model to transfer learned knowledge from known channel scenarios to previously unseen ones, thereby eliminating the need for retraining and enhancing scalability. Third, the incorporation of Transformer architectures introduces the ability to capture long-range dependencies and intricate temporal–spatial correlations within MIMO channels through self-attention mechanisms and context-aware feature extraction.

The synergistic combination of these technologies, ZSL for semantic generalization, Transformer for sequence modeling, and Muon for optimization, forms a novel paradigm for intelligent channel estimation. This paradigm is not only computationally efficient and broadly applicable but also inherently robust to future wireless system variations, positioning it as a compelling solution for next-generation communication networks.

Related work

Channel estimation has long been a central challenge in the design and optimization of MIMO wireless communication systems. Over the past two decades, research in this area has evolved from traditional model-based estimation techniques to more recent data-driven DL frameworks that aim to enhance estimation accuracy and robustness under complex, real-world conditions. This section provides a structured and critical review of the key contributions across four main domains: (1) classical statistical estimators such as LS and MMSE; (2) advanced DL architectures including CNNs, RNNs, and Transformer-based models; (3) optimization techniques used to improve training performance and model generalization; and (4) semantic learning approaches, with a particular focus on ZSL as a mechanism for generalization to previously unseen channel scenarios. The following subsections examine these developments, highlighting their respective strengths, limitations, and applicability to next-generation wireless systems.

Traditional channel Estimation model

Traditional channel estimation techniques, particularly the LS and MMSE estimators, have been extensively adopted in MIMO systems due to their analytical simplicity, low computational complexity, and ease of implementation20. The LS estimator is favored for its independence from channel statistics, while MMSE improves estimation accuracy by incorporating statistical information about the noise and channel covariance. Despite their popularity, these model-based methods exhibit several limitations that restrict their performance in modern communication environments.

One of the primary drawbacks of these estimators is their vulnerability to noise, especially at low signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs). Moreover, MMSE relies heavily on accurate prior knowledge of the channel’s statistical properties, which are often either unavailable or outdated in real-world deployments. This dependency can result in significant performance degradation when channel conditions change unpredictably. Additionally, both LS and MMSE perform suboptimally in high-mobility scenarios, where channel conditions vary rapidly over time, or in frequency-selective fading environments, which are typical in broadband communications. These limitations render traditional estimators less suitable for next-generation wireless systems, such as 5G and beyond, which require robust, adaptive, and low-latency solutions capable of operating under dynamic and heterogeneous network conditions.

Channel estimation based on DL models

Recent advancements in DL have opened new avenues for addressing the limitations of traditional channel estimation techniques in MIMO systems. DL-based models are capable of learning complex, non-linear mappings from input pilot signals to corresponding channel coefficients, offering greater adaptability to real-world conditions without relying on rigid statistical assumptions. These models can effectively leverage both spatial and temporal correlations inherent in wireless channels, thereby enhancing estimation performance in diverse propagation environments.

For instance, the study presented in Ref21. proposed a DL-assisted channel estimation method for large-scale MIMO systems, integrating depth image priors for denoising alongside conventional LS estimation. This hybrid approach aimed to mitigate multi-cell interference while improving estimation accuracy under constrained conditions. CNNs have been widely adopted for exploiting spatial structures in the channel matrix and have shown promising results in scenarios with relatively static or slowly varying channel conditions. These networks excel at learning localized features and have been particularly effective when applied to image-like representations of CSI.

In contrast, Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), specifically LSTM networks and GRUs, have been employed to model temporal dependencies in time-varying fading channels. These architectures are capable of capturing the sequential nature of channel variations, making them well-suited for mobile or rapidly changing wireless environments. However, while DL models exhibit superior estimation accuracy compared to classical methods, their performance often depends on the availability of large, diverse training datasets and extensive offline training. Optimization techniques for DL-based channel estimation

While DL models have shown remarkable potential for channel estimation in MIMO systems, their effectiveness heavily depends on the choice of optimization algorithm during training. Selecting a suitable optimizer is critical for ensuring fast convergence, model stability, and robust generalization, particularly when working with high-dimensional and noisy wireless signal data. This challenge becomes even more significant when dealing with temporal signal representations, where the model must extract meaningful features while being resilient to a wide range of interference and distortion sources.

Traditional optimization algorithms, such as Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) and Adam, are widely used due to their simplicity and computational efficiency. However, they are often prone to getting trapped in local minima and exhibit sensitivity to hyperparameter tuning, especially learning rate selection. These limitations can lead to slow convergence, unstable training, and poor generalization performance, particularly in dynamic and non-stationary wireless environments.

To address these challenges, several advanced optimizers have been proposed in recent years, including Lookahead, AdaBelief, and the recently introduced Muon optimizer22. These methods aim to enhance training efficiency by incorporating features such as adaptive momentum, gradient smoothing, and dynamic regularization mechanisms. The complete end-to-end signal transmission process in a typical MIMO wireless communication system is illustrated in Fig. 1. This process is divided into three primary stages: transmitter processing, channel transmission, and receiver processing. Each stage plays a critical role in ensuring the accurate and efficient delivery of information over complex wireless environments.

The MIMO communication system signal transmission procedure.

At the transmitter side, the communication pipeline begins with the generation of data symbols, which are modulated using a suitable modulation scheme (e.g., QAM or PSK). The modulated symbols are then converted from serial-to-parallel (S/P) format to match the multiple spatial streams supported by the MIMO configuration. Pilot symbols are subsequently inserted into the data stream to enable reliable channel estimation at the receiver. Following this, the signal undergoes Inverse Fast Fourier Transform (IFFT) to convert the frequency-domain data into the time domain, making it suitable for transmission over physical channels. A cyclic prefix (CP) is added to combat inter-symbol interference (ISI), and the parallel data streams are converted back to serial form (P/S) before being transmitted through multiple antennas.

During channel transmission, the signal is affected by multipath fading, additive noise, and interference. These phenomena introduce distortions, which must be mitigated at the receiver to ensure correct data recovery. On the receiver side, the incoming signal streams undergo cyclic prefix removal, followed by Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) to return to the frequency domain. The signals are then reorganized via S/P conversion, making them ready for processing by the DL-based channel estimation and equalization models. In the proposed framework, three variants are explored: Transformer-ZSL-Muon, Transformer-ZSL, and ZSL-Muon, all designed to enhance robustness under challenging conditions such as noise and fading. These models enable accurate channel estimation and equalization even in dynamically changing wireless environments.

After equalization, the signals are converted back to parallel-to-serial (P/S) form and passed through a demodulation stage to recover the original transmitted symbols. The integration of DL models into the receiver chain marks a significant advancement in MIMO system design, offering enhanced adaptability, resilience, and performance compared to traditional model-based techniques.

The hybrid DL model

This section introduces a novel hybrid DL framework that combines Transformer architectures, ZSL, and the Muon optimizer to enhance channel estimation in MIMO wireless communication systems. The model is designed to tackle the limitations of traditional estimators and conventional DL techniques, specifically their inability to generalize across unseen channel conditions without retraining.

The section begins with a detailed data generation strategy, which simulates realistic wireless environments under quasi-static and time-varying fading conditions, while embedding physical and semantic features to support ZSL-based generalization. The channel estimation process is then formalized through a learnable DL model that minimizes the MSE between the true and predicted channel coefficients across both static and dynamic fading regimes.

The ZSL framework is introduced to enable inference under novel channel scenarios by projecting observed features into a shared semantic space using a lightweight encoder. Semantic embeddings are used to facilitate robust estimation even when the channel conditions differ from those seen during training. This is further reinforced through a semantic consistency loss, dropout regularization, and structured latent space constraints.

Next, the Muon optimizer is described, offering adaptive learning rate control and regularization during training. It dynamically adjusts hyperparameters such as learning rate and weight decay based on the loss landscape, outperforming standard optimizers like Adam in convergence speed and generalization.

The section also details the integration of the Transformer-ZSL-Muon framework, illustrating its training flow, semantic feature extraction, and generalization behavior using a dataset of 150,000 samples. In addition, variations of the model, including ZSL with Muon optimizer and Transformer-ZSL using Adam optimizer, are examined to highlight the role of each component. Data generation and description

To rigorously evaluate the proposed hybrid DL model, a synthetic dataset is constructed under two representative wireless channel scenarios: quasi-static block fading and time-varying multipath fading. These scenarios are designed to emulate realistic channel dynamics encountered in practical MIMO systems. In both cases, the transmission environment is modeled with AWGN, which introduces controlled levels of stochastic interference across the communication channel.

For each antenna stream, the simulation generates detailed records of pilot signal patterns, corresponding channel responses, and input-output symbol mappings. These components are essential for supervised training and evaluation in conventional DL settings. However, in this framework, we adopt a ZSL paradigm that removes the need for exhaustive label-specific supervision.

Instead of relying on traditional class labels, the input features are encoded into high-level semantic attribute representations, which describe the underlying physical and statistical characteristics of the channel, such as SNR ranges, fading types, or temporal behavior. These semantic descriptors serve as the basis for transferring knowledge from seen conditions to unseen channel environments, thereby facilitating robust and generalizable channel estimation.

Channel estimation under quasi-static and time-varying conditions

In MIMO wireless communication systems, accurate channel estimation is closely tied to the temporal behavior of the wireless channel. When the receiver exhibits minimal mobility, the channel coherence time, the time duration over which the channel response remains relatively constant, tends to be significantly longer than the codeword duration. This scenario corresponds to the quasi-static block fading condition, in which the channel can be assumed constant over each transmission block but varies independently across successive blocks.

In such low-mobility settings, the effects of Doppler frequency shifts and rapid time variations in the channel response become negligible, allowing for simplified estimation strategies. However, even when relative motion between transmitter and receiver is minimal, the wireless channel may still experience multipath propagation effects due to static reflectors, diffractors, and scatterers in the environment. These factors give rise to delay spreads and frequency-selective fading within each block.

Moreover, phenomena such as shadow fading, caused by physical obstructions in the signal path, and spatial fading, due to the constructive and destructive interference of multipath components, may still affect signal quality, even in quasi-static conditions. These impairments underscore the necessity of employing robust channel estimation models capable of handling both stable and variable conditions.

In the considered MIMO framework, it is assumed that the channel remains constant across a given symbol block but varies independently between blocks, aligning with the block fading model. This assumption simplifies the modeling of pilot-based training and makes it feasible to integrate the proposed Transformer-ZSL-Muon model for adaptive estimation, particularly under slowly varying or predictable channel dynamics.

In the context of MIMO systems operating under quasi-static block fading conditions, the core challenge lies in accurately estimating the channel coefficients, denoted as h(r), for each receive antenna. The objective is to develop a DL-based channel estimator that minimizes the MSE between the true channel h and its predicted estimate , while also ensuring the effective design of pilot signals that support robust learning under limited feedback.

In such scenarios, the channel coefficients h(r) fluctuate continuously within a transmission block due to Doppler shifts, leading to time-varying fading characteristics. This dynamic behavior imposes additional constraints on channel observation and sampling rates, often resulting in limited and noisy training samples.

The proposed hybrid model addresses these challenges by employing semantic-aware learning and adaptive optimization techniques, enabling the system to maintain high estimation accuracy even under limited-sample, time-varying conditions.

In the time-varying fading scenario, channel estimation becomes more complex due to the temporal correlation of channel fluctuations within and across symbol durations. Specifically, the estimation of the current channel state h(r) relies on feedback information collected over a temporal window, typically from the symbol time to the current rth symbol. This temporal dependence allows the model to capture the evolving channel dynamics. In the context of MIMO systems, the estimation of the full channel matrix H similarly considers historical observations, typically from the to the (n−1)th symbol time, to account for intra-block correlation and fading continuity.

To handle unseen or dynamically changing channel conditions, the proposed framework employs a semantic-aware decoder, guided by the ZSL module. This decoder predicts the full channel matrix based not solely on observed signals but also on the semantic representation of the environment, enabling generalization to previously unencountered fading types, SNR levels, or antenna configurations. Even in the absence of direct training on specific channel states, the ZSL component allows the model to infer latent structures and provide accurate channel estimates using attribute-based descriptors.

During the training phase, the framework is optimized using the Muon optimizer, which plays a critical role in ensuring efficient convergence, robust generalization, and resistance to overfitting. The training objective remains the minimization of MSE between the true channel matrix H and its predicted counterpart . The Muon optimizer’s use of adaptive regularization and dynamic scheduling techniques ensures that the model navigates the loss landscape effectively, achieving both fast convergence and stable learning across diverse channel conditions. As a result, the hybrid Transformer-ZSL-Muon framework produces a scalable and resilient channel estimation model, suitable for both quasi-static and high-mobility environments.

ZSL framework

The core of the proposed estimation model is built upon a ZSL framework, which enables the system to generalize beyond seen training conditions by leveraging semantic embeddings. Rather than relying exclusively on signal-label pairs from known channel conditions, the ZSL-based architecture is designed to learn transferable mappings from pilot symbol patterns to channel coefficients. This is achieved by encoding pilot-aided received signals into a semantic vector space that represents both observed and unobserved channel states.

The architecture adopts a hybrid encoder–decoder design, where the encoder is a nonlinear projection network that transforms noise-corrupted pilot sequences into compact latent representations, and the decoder uses these latent vectors, conditioned on semantic attributes, to generate channel estimates even for previously unseen scenarios (e.g., unobserved SNR values or fading profiles). This enables the system to operate in dynamically evolving environments without requiring retraining or additional supervision.

The semantically aware encoder is a lightweight yet expressive neural network, typically composed of convolutional layers for extracting spatial features, pooling layers to reduce dimensionality, and fully connected (dense) layers that map features into a low-dimensional shared latent space. This latent representation, denoted as , captures the essential structure and characteristics of the MIMO channel. Crucially, the encoder does not learn raw signal values directly; instead, it focuses on robust, semantically rich feature representations that encode underlying physical properties of the wireless environment, such as fading behavior, delay profiles, and SNR levels.

Semantic embeddings, vector representations that numerically encode different channel attributes, play a critical role in this framework. These embeddings, denoted by sk​, may be generated through two methods: (i) supervised learning using labeled channel conditions, or (ii) manual definition based on domain expertise. Each embedding corresponds to a distinct wireless scenario, such as a specific SNR range or fading type. During inference, the model compares the latent representation z of the observed channel against the precomputed semantic embeddings using a similarity metric (e.g., cosine similarity or Euclidean distance). The channel estimate is then derived by selecting the semantic embedding that best matches the current latent representation, allowing the decoder to generate accurate predictions under previously unseen conditions.

This ZSL-enabled mechanism introduces a semantic reasoning capability into the channel estimation process, significantly enhancing the model’s ability to operate under sparse supervision and generalize across a wide spectrum of MIMO environments.

Muon optimizer and training strategy

To ensure efficient and robust training of the proposed hybrid channel estimation framework, the Muon optimizer is employed as a superior alternative to conventional optimization techniques such as Adam. Unlike static optimizers, the Muon algorithm integrates motion dynamics into the weight update process by modeling both momentum and frictional forces, thereby providing enhanced navigation through the non-convex loss surfaces typically encountered in ZSL training. This dynamic adjustment capability results in faster convergence, improved stability, and superior generalization performance, especially in highly heterogeneous MIMO channel environments.

An important aspect of the training procedure involves the incorporation of a semantic consistency loss, which preserves the structural alignment between seen and unseen channel classes in the shared latent space. This loss function encourages the model to learn feature representations that maintain intra-class compactness and inter-class separability, enabling more accurate channel estimation in new scenarios. To further bolster generalization, dropout is applied at various layers of the encoder–decoder architecture, reducing the risk of overfitting by randomly deactivating neurons during training. Additionally, structural constraints are enforced on the latent space using cosine similarity metrics and margin-based loss functions, which help to refine the representation geometry by maximizing inter-class distances and minimizing intra-class variance.

The Muon optimizer exhibits a high degree of adaptivity throughout the training process. It dynamically adjusts the learning rate based on real-time feedback from the training loss landscape. Specifically, the optimizer reduces the learning rate in steep regions or near convergence optima to prevent overshooting, while increasing it in flatter regions to escape plateaus and accelerate convergence. This is often implemented using advanced learning rate scheduling techniques, such as warm-up phases at the beginning of training or cyclical learning rate schedules tuned to the characteristics of deep MIMO channel estimation problems.

Crucially, the Muon optimizer also integrates an adaptive regularization mechanism, which modulates dropout rates and L2 weight decay parameters in response to model behavior. For instance, if the validation loss diverges significantly from the training loss, indicating potential overfitting, the optimizer increases the regularization strength in real-time. Conversely, if the model shows signs of underfitting, the regularization is relaxed. This adaptive approach eliminates the need for manual hyperparameter tuning and ensures that model complexity remains controlled throughout the training cycle.

After training on labeled channel states with known SNR and fading patterns, the model is rigorously evaluated on unseen SNR values to test its capacity for semantic generalization. The results highlight the strength of the Muon-optimized training process, demonstrating the model’s robustness, adaptability, and suitability for real-time deployment in next-generation wireless communication systems.

End-to-end framework flow

The overall operational flow of the proposed channel estimation framework is structured to efficiently process received pilot signals and produce accurate channel estimates, even under previously unseen conditions. The process unfolds through a sequence of interrelated stages, designed to integrate semantic reasoning, representation learning, and adaptive optimization seamlessly.

The pipeline begins with the input acquisition phase, where the received pilot signal Zp is collected from the MIMO communication system. This signal, which captures the transmitted pilot symbols distorted by the wireless channel and noise, serves as the primary input to the estimation framework.

In the next step, Zp is fed into the semantic-aware encoder, a lightweight neural network that transforms the received pilot sequence into a latent representation vector within a shared semantic space. This encoding process effectively condenses high-dimensional signal information into a compact, discriminative feature vector that captures key physical properties of the communication channel, such as fading characteristics and SNR patterns.

Following the encoding phase, the latent vector z is matched against a database of precomputed semantic embeddings sk. Each semantic embedding sk corresponds to a known channel condition (e.g., specific fading profile or SNR level) and encapsulates abstract knowledge about that condition. A similarity metric, such as cosine similarity or Euclidean distance, is used to compare the encoded vector z with each semantic prototype sk.

The channel state inference is then performed by selecting the most similar semantic embedding or by computing a weighted combination of multiple embeddings, where the weights are proportional to the similarity scores. This approach allows the model to blend information from multiple reference scenarios and infer channel characteristics with higher granularity and flexibility.

Finally, the inferred semantic information is passed to the decoder, which maps the semantic embedding or the weighted vector representation back into the channel response space. This results in an accurate prediction of the channel matrix , enabling the system to perform robust channel estimation, even when exposed to channel conditions that were not seen during training.

This end-to-end framework, built on the integration of Zero-Shot Learning, Transformer-based modeling, and Muon optimization, offers a scalable and intelligent solution for real-time MIMO channel estimation, capable of adapting to dynamically changing wireless environments.

The proposed Transformer-ZSL-Muon framework represents the full integration of Transformer-based encoding, ZSL for semantic generalization, and the Muon optimizer for advanced training stability and efficiency. This architecture is designed to leverage the strengths of each component to address the limitations of traditional channel estimation methods in complex and dynamically evolving MIMO wireless environments.

At its core, the framework employs a Transformer encoder to model the intricate relationships among pilot-aided received signals, capturing both temporal and spatial dependencies through self-attention mechanisms. This attention-driven architecture facilitates the extraction of meaningful patterns across input sequences, enabling robust feature representation even under varying fading conditions and SNR levels. These extracted features are then projected into a shared semantic embedding space, where deep semantic alignment is performed with known channel attributes, following the principles of Zero-Shot Learning.

The Muon optimizer improves convergence speed and training robustness by dynamically adjusting the learning process based on gradient curvature, historical loss behavior, and adaptive momentum decay. By synergizing Transformer’s attention-based learning, ZSL’s semantic generalization capabilities, and Muon’s dynamic optimization, the Transformer-ZSL-Muon framework emerges as a highly adaptable, scalable, and intelligent solution for real-time channel estimation in next-generation wireless systems.

Furthermore, when benchmarked against Ref26., the proposed model with 64 pilots achieves a 50% reduction in MSE, underscoring its effectiveness in maintaining estimation robustness even under time-varying conditions.

To further validate the superiority of the proposed framework, we conducted a comparative analysis between different channel estimation strategies, including DL-based models, Transformer-ZSL-Muon, Transformer-ZSL, and ZSL-Muon optimizer, and classical estimation techniques such as MMSE and LS. Across both channel scenarios, the Transformer-ZSL-Muon consistently achieves the lowest MSE across all evaluated SNR levels. This highlights the model’s ability to robustly capture the intricate, nonlinear dependencies between pilot inputs and channel responses, dependencies that traditional linear estimators like MMSE and LS fail to model effectively, particularly under complex and dynamic propagation conditions.

In Fig. 5a, the Transformer-ZSL-Muon demonstrates a clear advantage over its counterparts, especially in the mid to high SNR range (25–40 dB), where it outperforms the next best method (Transformer-ZSL) by approximately 22% to 30% in terms of MSE. The benefits of integrating the Muon optimizer and Transformer architecture become especially evident here, as they jointly enhance learning convergence and semantic alignment during training. This translates to more accurate and stable channel estimates.

Similarly, under variant time fading as shown in Fig. 5b, the proposed model maintains its superior estimation performance. While all methods experience an MSE reduction with increasing SNR, the Transformer-ZSL-Muon maintains a performance margin of 20–35% over conventional techniques and achieves convergence to MSE levels near 10− 6 at 35–40 dB SNR. The LS and MMSE estimators, in contrast, plateau at significantly higher MSE levels, indicating their limitations in handling the dynamic nature of time-varying fading environments.

Overall, these results reinforce that the hybrid DL framework significantly outperforms both classical and partial DL-based models in channel estimation tasks. The synergy of zero-shot learning, attention-driven Transformer encoding, and adaptive Muon optimization yields a robust architecture that not only generalizes well to unseen channel conditions but also ensures low estimation error across a wide SNR spectrum.

DL models demonstrate superior capacity in capturing complex and nonlinear channel mapping relationships, which significantly enhances the accuracy of channel estimation in MIMO systems. These models benefit from high model expressiveness and generalization capabilities, allowing them to adapt effectively to varying wireless channel conditions. Unlike traditional estimation methods that rely on linear approximations and manual feature engineering, DL approaches enable end-to-end learning, reducing the need for handcrafted signal processing pipelines. This not only lowers overall system complexity but also streamlines channel estimation into a unified and efficient framework.

Among the evaluated models, the proposed Transformer-ZSL-Muon framework exhibits a notably stronger ability to learn intricate channel characteristics. This hybrid model outperforms both its individual components, Transformer-ZSL and ZSL-Muon optimizer, by leveraging the synergistic strengths of attention-based feature extraction, semantic alignment through zero-shot learning, and adaptive optimization using the Muon algorithm. Particularly under complex and dynamic propagation scenarios, the Transformer-ZSL-Muon model maintains lower MSE levels across a range of SNRs, demonstrating its robustness and adaptability. These results underscore the model’s enhanced capability to generalize under unseen conditions and to effectively estimate channel states with minimal prior knowledge.

The effectiveness of the proposed Transformer-ZSL-Muon hybrid model is further quantified in Fig. 5, where its performance is compared against a variety of benchmarks, including Transformer-ZSL, ZSL-Muon optimizer, and conventional estimation methods such as MMSE and LS. Specifically, the Transformer-ZSL-Muon model achieves a significant MSE reduction of 27% compared to Transformer-ZSL, 24% compared to the ZSL-Muon optimizer, 93.75% over MMSE, and 96.67% compared to LS at an SNR of 30 dB. These gains highlight the value of combining attention mechanisms, zero-shot semantic mapping, and the adaptive convergence properties of the Muon optimizer into a unified learning framework.

As illustrated in Fig. 5b, the superiority of the hybrid model is especially apparent at higher SNR levels, where the MSE curves of traditional methods tend to flatten, indicating diminishing improvements. In contrast, the proposed framework continues to decrease MSE significantly, reinforcing its robust generalization and adaptability across both quasi-static and time-variant fading scenarios.

Further validation of the hybrid model’s learning dynamics is presented in Figs. 6 and 7, which display the accuracy and loss trends across 200 training epochs, respectively. In Fig. 6, the Transformer-ZSL-Muon model achieves rapid convergence, surpassing 90% accuracy within the first 75 epochs and maintaining this performance with minimal fluctuation throughout the remaining epochs. This stands in contrast to the Transformer-ZSL and ZSL-Muon optimizer models, which plateau at lower accuracy levels (around 85% and 80%, respectively), reflecting their relatively limited representational power or optimization efficiency.

In summary, the combination of high representational capacity (via Transformer), semantic transferability (via ZSL), and convergence efficiency (via Muon optimizer) enables the proposed framework to achieve superior training dynamics, lower estimation error, and better robustness across a range of challenging channel environments. These findings confirm the hybrid model’s potential for real-time and reliable deployment in future MIMO systems.

The advantages of integrating the Muon optimizer into the Transformer-ZSL architecture are clearly illustrated in Fig. 8, which compares the training loss profiles of the Muon and Adam optimizers over 200 epochs. The Muon optimizer demonstrates superior convergence characteristics, exhibiting a steeper and more consistent decline in loss, ultimately achieving a lower final loss compared to Adam. This can be attributed to Muon’s dynamic learning strategy, which adaptively adjusts the learning rate based on curvature information, historical gradients, and loss behavior. Such adaptive mechanisms enhance its capability to navigate the complex optimization landscape typically encountered in high-dimensional MIMO channel estimation tasks.

In contrast, the Adam optimizer, although effective in many scenarios, relies on fixed decay and momentum coefficients that may not sufficiently adapt to the evolving training dynamics of deep semantic models like ZSL. This results in slower convergence and a higher residual loss floor. Moreover, the smoother and less oscillatory trajectory of the Muon optimizer highlights its improved stability during training, which is particularly beneficial for tasks requiring fine-grained alignment between semantic and channel domains. These findings reinforce the suitability of Muon as a robust and efficient optimization algorithm for zero-shot learning frameworks in wireless communication systems.

According to Table 6 results, adding the Muon optimizer alone resulted in the quickest training time (about 7.2–7.4 h), demonstrating its effectiveness in speeding up convergence. The basic Transformer–ZSL takes a little longer (about 7.5–7.7 h), while the added representation learning burden of Muon-based feature encoding results in a somewhat longer training period (approximately 7.9–8.1 h). Overall, the time differences are negligible, demonstrating that the increased accuracy offered by Muon components has a negligible computational cost.

Conclusion, limitations, and future works

This paper introduced a robust and intelligent hybrid DL framework for MIMO channel estimation that integrates Transformer architectures, ZSL mechanisms, and the Muon optimizer. Designed to address the inherent limitations of conventional DL and model-based estimators, the proposed Transformer-ZSL-Muon framework demonstrates remarkable accuracy, convergence speed, and generalization capacity, particularly in dynamic and unseen wireless channel conditions. By leveraging semantic embeddings and attention-driven architectures, the model effectively captures spatial-temporal dependencies and generalizes across varying SNRs, fading types, and antenna configurations without retraining. Furthermore, its enhancements over ZSL-Muon and Transformer-ZSL variants underscore the complementary strengths of all three components. A particularly noteworthy finding is the impact of massive MIMO configurations. The proposed model delivers state-of-the-art performance in both quasi-static and time-varying block fading environments, consistently outperforming conventional and recent DL-based baselines.

Despite its demonstrated strengths, several limitations merit consideration:

While effective, the Transformer-ZSL-Muon framework involves higher training and inference costs due to the Transformer’s multi-head attention layers and the adaptive nature of the Muon optimizer. This makes real-time deployment on resource-constrained platforms, such as edge devices, IoT nodes, or low-latency communication systems, more challenging.

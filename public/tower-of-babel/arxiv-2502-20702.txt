On common energies and sumsets II
Shkredov I.D

Abstract




Annotation.

We continue to study the relationship between the size of the sum of a set and the common energy of its subsets.
We find a rather sharp subexponential dependence between the doubling constant of a set
A
and the minimal common energy taken over all partitions of
A
into two disjoint subsets.
As an application, we give a proof of the well–known arithmetic regularity lemma with better dependence on parameters.

1
Introduction

Given an abelian group
{\mathbf{G}}
and two sets
A,B\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
, define
the
sumset
of
A
and
B
as

A+B:=\{a+b~:~a\in{A},\,b\in{B}\}\,.

(1)

The study of the structure of sumsets is a fundamental problem in classical additive combinatorics see, e.g.,
[
5
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
6
]
and other papers.
Another important combinatorial concept closely related to the sumset
A+A

is the
additive energy

\mathsf{E}(A,A)
or, more generally, the
common additive energy

\mathsf{E}(A,B)
of
A
and
B
, which is defined as

\mathsf{E}(A,B)=|\{(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2})\in A\times A\times B\times B~:~a_{1}-b_{1}=a_{2}-b_{2}\}|\,.

(2)

The additive energy and the size of the sumset are trivially related via
the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, namely,

\mathsf{E}(A,B)|A\pm B|\geqslant|A|^{2}|B|^{2}\,,

(3)

but a much deeper connection is given to us by the famous Balog–Szemerédi–Gowers theorem (see
[
2
]
and
[
7
]
).

Theorem 1

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be an abelian group,
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a set and
K\geqslant 1
be a real number.
Then

\mathsf{E}(A,A)\gg\frac{|A|^{3}}{K^{C_{1}}}\quad\quad\mbox{ iff }\quad\quad\exists A^{\prime}\subseteq A~:~|A^{\prime}+A^{\prime}|\ll K^{C_{2}}|A|\quad\mbox{ and }\quad|A^{\prime}|\gg\frac{|A|}{K^{C_{2}}}\,.

(4)

Here
C_{1}=O(C_{2})
and
C_{2}=O(C_{1})
.

In recent paper
[
12
]
the author obtained another polynomial relation between the additive energy and
|A+A|
.

Theorem 2

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be an abelian group,
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a set and
K\geqslant 1
be a real number.
Then

|A+A|\ll K^{C_{1}}|A|\quad\quad\mbox{ iff }\quad\quad\forall X,Y\subseteq A,\,|X|\geqslant|A|/2~:~\mathsf{E}(X,Y)\geqslant\frac{|X||Y|^{2}}{K^{C_{2}}}\,.

(5)

Here, as above,
C_{1}=O(C_{2})
and
C_{2}=O(C_{1})
.

Therefore it is possible to express the doubling constant of
A
in terms of the common additive energy of subsets of
A
.

In this paper we consider a weaker condition than (
5
), namely, instead of taking all pairs
X,Y\subseteq A
,
|X|\geqslant|A|/2
we have deal with
X,Y\subseteq A
such that
X\cup Y=A
and the union is disjoint (then, trivially, either
|X|
or
|Y|
is at least
|A|/2
).
These kinds of restrictions were considered in excellent paper
[
4
]
(at the graph level), although the common energy
\mathsf{E}(X,Y)
is a nonlinear and more subtle quantity than the usual graph density.
It turns out that in the case of disjoint unions we do
not
have polynomial dependence between this new quantity (the rigorous definition can be found in Section
2
) and
the
doubling constant

\mathcal{D}[A]:=|A+A|/|A|
of
A
.
More precisely (see Theorems
14
,
26
below), we obtain

Theorem 3

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be an abelian group,
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a set.
Suppose that for any
X,Y\subseteq A
such that
X\cup Y=A
and
X\cap Y=\emptyset
one has

\mathsf{E}(X,Y)\geqslant\frac{|X|^{2}|Y|^{2}}{K|A|}\,.

(6)

Then

\frac{|A+A|}{|A|}\leqslant\exp(O(K^{1/3}\log^{2}K))\,.

(7)

On the other hand, there is an abelian group
{\mathbf{G}}
and a set
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
such that (
6
) takes place
for all
X,Y\subseteq A
such that
X\cup Y=A
and
X\cap Y=\emptyset

but

\frac{|A+A|}{|A|}\geqslant\exp(\Omega(K^{1/4}\log K))\,.

(8)

If
|A+A|=\mathcal{D}[A]\cdot|A|
, then the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality (
3
) gives us
\mathsf{E}(X,Y)\geqslant\frac{|X|^{2}|Y|^{2}}{\mathcal{D}[A]|A|}
for
any

X,Y\subseteq A
and hence estimates (
7
), (
8
) tell us that the dependence between the quantity
\mathcal{D}[A]
and
K
in (
6
)
is subexponential.

As an application of Theorem
8
we obtain a
direct proof
of the well–known arithmetic regularity lemma of Green and Sisask
[
8
, Proposition 3.2]
with better
dependence
on parameters, see Section
5
.
Nowadays,
the arithmetic regularity lemma is a rather popular tool in additive combinatorics, some of its applications can be found in papers
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
8
]
and
[
11
]
.

We hope that the tools developed in this paper will add flexibility to working with the doubling constants.

2
Definitions

Below
{\mathbf{G}}
denotes an abelian group with the group operation
+
.
The sumset of two sets
A,B\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
was defined in formula (
1
) of the introduction.
In a similar way one can define the
difference sets
and the
higher sumsets
, e.g.,
2A-A
is
A+A-A
.
Recall that the doubling constant of a finite set
A
is
given
by the formula

\mathcal{D}[A]:=\frac{|A+A|}{|A|}\,.

(9)

We say that the sum of
A
and
B
is direct
if
|A+B|=|A||B|
.
In this case we sometimes write
A\dotplus B
.
The important Plünnecke–Ruzsa inequality (see, e.g.,
[
9
]
or
[
13
]
) says that for any positive integers
n
and
m

the following holds

|nA-mA|\leqslant\left(\frac{|A+A|}{|A|}\right)^{n+m}\cdot|A|\,.

(10)

Therefore,
bound (
10
) connects the cardinality of the original
set
A
and its higher sumsets
nA-mA
.
The common additive energy of two sets was defined in (
2
) and the generalization of the common additive energy for four sets

A,B,C,D\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
is given by the formula

\mathsf{E}(A,B,C,D)=\{|\{(a,b,c,d)\in A\times B\times C\times D~:~a+b=c+d\}|\,.

The disjoint union of two sets
A,B\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
is
denoted
as
A\bigsqcup B
.

Given finite sets
A,B\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
and a real number
T\geqslant 1

we defined the quantity
\mathcal{E}_{T}[A]
in paper
[
12
]
as

\mathcal{E}_{T}[A]=\max_{X\subseteq A,\,|X|\geqslant|A|/T,\,Y\subseteq A}\,\frac{|X|^{2}|Y|^{2}}{|A|\mathsf{E}(X,Y)}\,.

Thus Theorem
2
from the introduction says that the quantities
\mathcal{D}[A]
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}[A]
are polynomially equivalent.
In this paper we consider the new quantity

\mathcal{E}_{*}[A]=\max_{X\bigsqcup Y=A}\,\frac{|X|^{2}|Y|^{2}}{|A|\mathsf{E}(X,Y)}\,.

In view of inequality (
3
) and the discussion after Theorem
2

one has for any
T\geqslant 2
that

\mathcal{E}_{*}[A]\leqslant\mathcal{E}_{2}[A]\leqslant\mathcal{E}_{T}[A]\leqslant\mathcal{D}[A]\,.

The signs
\ll
and
\gg
are the usual Vinogradov symbols.
If
a\ll b
and
b\ll a
, then we write
a\sim b
.
When the constants in the signs depend on a parameter
M
, we write
\ll_{M}
and
\gg_{M}
.
Let us denote by
[n]
the set
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.
All logarithms are to base
e
.
For a prime number
p
we write
\mathbb{F}_{p}=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
.

3
The proof of the main result

In this section we obtain the first part of Theorem
8
from the introduction.
We start with a simple lemma.

Lemma 4

Let
A,B\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
,
|A|\geqslant|B|
and

\mathsf{E}(A,B)=|A||B|^{2}/K
.
Then there is
X\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
such that

\frac{|A|}{4K|B|}\leqslant|X|\leqslant\frac{2K|A|}{|B|}\,,

(11)

and

|A\cap(B+X)|\geqslant\frac{|A|}{4K}\,.

(12)

P r o o f.

We have
\mathsf{E}(A,B)=\sum_{x}|A\cap(B+x)|^{2}
and hence there is
x\in{\mathbf{G}}
such that

|A\cap(B+x)|\geqslant\frac{\mathsf{E}(A,B)}{|A||B|}\geqslant\frac{|B|}{K}\,.

Also, for
A^{\prime}=A\setminus(B+x)
one has

\mathsf{E}(A^{\prime},B)\geqslant\mathsf{E}(A,B)-2|A\cap(B+x)||B|^{2}\,.

Thus, iterating this procedure we obtain a set
X\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
such that
|X||B|/2K\leqslant|A|
and

2|A\cap(B+X)||B|^{2}\geqslant 2^{-1}\mathsf{E}(A,B)=|A||B|^{2}/(2K)\,.

It follows that
|A\cap(B+X)|\geqslant|A|/(4K)
and the last inequality implies

|X|\geqslant|A|/(4K|B|)
.
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

Now we show that sets
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
with small quantity
\mathcal{E}_{*}[A]
have some rather specific properties.
Namely, Proposition
5
below shows that if such
A
contains a large subset with small doubling constant, then
A
also has small doubling constant.
Of course, this result
does not hold
for an arbitrary set.

Proposition 5

Let
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a set,
M:=\mathcal{E}_{*}[A]
and there is a subset
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
such that

|A^{\prime}+A^{\prime}|\leqslant K|A^{\prime}|\quad\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\quad|A^{\prime}|\geqslant(1-\varepsilon)|A|\,,

(13)

where

\varepsilon=(2^{6}M)^{-1}
.
Then
|A+A|\ll K^{4}M^{2}|A|
.

P r o o f.

We can assume that
|A|
is sufficiently large; otherwise, there is nothing to prove.
By the covering lemma (see, e.g.,
[
13
, Section 2.4]
) we find
Z\subseteq A
such that
|A^{\prime}+Z|=|A^{\prime}||Z|
and
A\subseteq A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}+Z
.
Let us remark that

(1-\varepsilon)|A||Z|\leqslant|A^{\prime}||Z|=|A^{\prime}+Z|\leqslant|A+A|\leqslant 2^{-1}(|A|^{2}+|A|)

and therefore

|Z|\leqslant\frac{|A|+1}{2(1-\varepsilon)}\leqslant\frac{3|A|}{4}\,.

In particular, we have
|Z^{c}|\geqslant|A|/4
, where
Z^{c}=A\setminus Z
.
Put
A^{\prime}_{Z}=A^{\prime}\cap Z^{c}
and
\Omega=Z^{c}\setminus A^{\prime}_{Z}
.
Clearly,
|\Omega|\leqslant\varepsilon|A|
.
By the definition of the quantity
\mathcal{E}_{*}[A]
and the fact
that the sum
A^{\prime}+Z
is direct, we have

\frac{|Z|^{2}|Z^{c}|^{2}}{M|A|}\leqslant\mathsf{E}(Z,Z^{c})\leqslant\mathsf{E}(Z,A^{\prime}_{Z})+\mathsf{E}(Z,Z,Z^{c},\Omega)+\mathsf{E}(Z,Z,A^{\prime}_{Z},\Omega)\leqslant\mathsf{E}(Z,A^{\prime})+2|\Omega||Z|^{2}

\leqslant|Z||A|+2\varepsilon|A||Z|^{2}\,.

In view of the
inequality

|Z^{c}|\geqslant|A|/4
and our choice of
\varepsilon
one has

|Z|\leqslant\frac{2M|A|^{2}}{|Z^{c}|^{2}}\leqslant 2^{5}M\,.

Using the last bound, the covering lemma and the Plünnecke inequality (
10
), we derive

|A+A|\leqslant|2A^{\prime}-2A^{\prime}||Z+Z|\ll K^{4}M^{2}|A|\,.

This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

Now we are ready to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 6

Let
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a set.
Then

\mathcal{D}[A]\leqslant\exp(O(\mathcal{E}^{1/3}_{*}[A]\cdot\log^{2}\mathcal{E}_{*}[A]))\,.

(14)

P r o o f.

Let
M=\mathcal{E}_{*}[A]
.
Split the set
A
into two sets
|A_{1}|\sim|A_{2}|\sim|A|/2
in an arbitrary way. Then one has
\mathsf{E}(A)\gg|A|^{3}/M
.
Applying
a version of the Balog–Szemerédi–Gowers Theorem (see, e.g.,
[
10
]
), we find
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
,
|A^{\prime}|\gg|A|/M
such that
|A^{\prime}-A^{\prime}|\ll M^{4}|A^{\prime}|
.
Let
T
be a parameter,
T=CM^{2}
, where
C>1
is a sufficiently large absolute constant.
Using
some kind
of greedy algorithm, we construct a set
Z\subseteq A
,
Z\cap A^{\prime}=\emptyset
such that for any
x\in{\mathbf{G}}
one has

|Z\cap(A^{\prime}+x)|\leqslant\frac{|A^{\prime}|}{T}\,.

(15)

Indeed, at the first step of our algorithm put
Z_{0}=A\setminus A^{\prime}:=(A^{\prime})^{c}
and if (
15
) takes place for
Z=Z_{0}
, then we are done.
Otherwise, there is
x_{1}\in{\mathbf{G}}
such that
|(A^{\prime})^{c}\cap(A^{\prime}+x_{1})|\geqslant|A^{\prime}|/T
.
Put
Y_{1}:=A^{\prime}\bigsqcup((A^{\prime})^{c}\cap(A^{\prime}+x_{1}))
and
Z_{1}=Y^{c}_{1}
.
If (
15
) holds for
Z=Z_{1}
, then we are done.
If not, then there is
x_{2}\in{\mathbf{G}}
such that

|Z_{1}\cap(A^{\prime}+x_{2})|\geqslant|A^{\prime}|/T
and we put

Y_{2}=Y_{1}\bigsqcup(Z_{1}\cap(A^{\prime}+x_{2}))
and
Z_{2}=Y^{c}_{2}
.
And so on.
Clearly, the algorithm must stop after at most
s_{1}:=[T|A|/|A^{\prime}|]=O(M^{3})
number of steps.

Now let
Y:=Y_{s_{1}}
,
Z=Z_{s_{1}}
and
Y_{*}=Y\setminus A^{\prime}
.
Below we assume that
|Z|\geqslant|A|/2
.
Since
Y\bigsqcup Z=A
, then thanks to our choice of parameter
T
(here it is sufficient to have
T=\Omega(M)
) we
derive
that

\frac{|A^{\prime}||Y||Z|^{2}}{M|A|}\leqslant\frac{|Y|^{2}|Z|^{2}}{M|A|}\leqslant\mathsf{E}(Y_{*}\bigsqcup A^{\prime},Z)\leqslant\mathsf{E}(Y_{*},Z)+2\mathsf{E}(Y,A^{\prime},Z,Z)

(16)

\leqslant\mathsf{E}(Y_{*},Z)+\frac{2|A^{\prime}||Y||Z|}{T}\leqslant\mathsf{E}(Y_{*},Z)+\frac{|Y|^{2}|Z|^{2}}{2M|A|}\,.

(17)

Thus
writing
\mathsf{E}(Y_{*},Z)=|Y_{*}|^{2}|Z|/T_{*}
for some
T_{*}\geqslant 1
, we obtain

\frac{|Y|^{2}|Z|^{2}}{2M|A|}\leqslant\mathsf{E}(Y_{*},Z)=\frac{|Y_{*}|^{2}|Z|}{T_{*}}\,,

(18)

and hence

|Y_{*}|\geqslant\sqrt{\frac{T_{*}}{M}}\cdot\frac{|Y|}{2}\geqslant\frac{|A^{\prime}|}{2\sqrt{M}}\gg\frac{|A|}{M^{3/2}}\,.

(19)

Also, notice that estimate (
18
) implies the following rough bound on
T_{*}

T_{*}\leqslant\frac{2M|A|}{|Z|}\leqslant 4M\,.

(20)

On the other hand, by the definition of the quantity
T_{*}
one has
\mathsf{E}(Y_{*},Z)=\frac{|Y_{*}|^{2}|Z|}{T_{*}}
and hence applying Lemma
12
, bound (
19
), combining with estimate (
20
), we find
X\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
with

|X|\leqslant\min\left\{\frac{4M|A|}{|Y_{*}|},\frac{2T_{*}|Z|}{|Y_{*}|}\right\}\leqslant\frac{4\sqrt{MT_{*}}|Z|}{|Y|}\leqslant\frac{4\sqrt{MT_{*}}|A|}{|A^{\prime}|}\ll M^{2}

(21)

and such that

|Z\cap(Y_{*}+X)|\geqslant\frac{|Z|}{4T_{*}}\,.

(22)

Now we apply the same algorithm starting with the set
Y(1):=Y
.
And so on.
Thus we construct an increasing sequence of sets
A^{\prime}\subseteq Y(1)\subseteq Y(2)\subseteq\dots\subseteq Y(t^{\prime})\subseteq A
and let us put
Z(j)=Y^{c}(j)
.
Inequality (
19
), combined with the estimate
|A^{\prime}|\gg|A|/M
show that we obtain
|Z(t^{\prime})|<|A|/2
after at most
t^{\prime}=O(M^{1/2}\log M)
number of steps.
Let us improve the last bound and check
that in view of inequality (
22
)
the real number of steps
t^{\prime}
is
O(M^{1/3}\log M)
.
Indeed put
R=[M^{1/3}]
and consider the first
R
sets
Y(j)
,
j\in[R]
.
Besides sets
Y(j)
for any
j\in[R]
we have sets
Z(j)
, where
Y(j)\bigsqcup Z(j)=A
and the parameter
T_{*}(j)
.
Then we have two cases: either there are at least
R/2
numbers
j\in[R]
such that
T_{*}(j)\geqslant R
or there exist at least
R/2
numbers
j\in[R]
such that
T_{*}(j)<R
.
In the first case we use inequality (
19
) and see that

|Y(R)|\geqslant|A^{\prime}|\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{R}}{2\sqrt{M}}\right)^{R/2}\geqslant|A^{\prime}|(1+c)\,,

(23)

where
c>0
is an absolute constant.
In the second case one can apply estimate (
22
) and obtain

|Z(R)|\leqslant|Z|\left(1-\frac{1}{4R}\right)^{R/2}\leqslant(1-\tilde{c})|Z|\,,

(24)

where
\tilde{c}>0
is another absolute constant and hence, say,

|Y(2R)|\geqslant(1+2^{-1}\tilde{c})|Y|\geqslant(1+2^{-1}\tilde{c})|A^{\prime}|\,.

Formulae (
23
), (
24
) show that in any case we cannot have more than
t^{\prime}=O(R\log M)=O(M^{1/3}\log M)
steps.

Having reached the inequality
|Z(t^{\prime})|<|A|/2
, we apply the same algorithm to the set
Y(t^{\prime})
to obtain another increasing sequence of sets
Y(t^{\prime})\subseteq Y(t^{\prime}+1)\subseteq\dots\subseteq Y(t^{\prime}+t^{\prime\prime})
and so on, but this time suppose that for all
j\geqslant t^{\prime}
one
|Z(j)|\geqslant\varepsilon|A|
holds, where
\varepsilon:=(2^{6}M)^{-1}
.
Now we have
|Y(j)|\geqslant|A|/2
,
j\geqslant t^{\prime}
,
T=CM^{2}
and repeating the computations in (
16
)—(
18
), we see that

\frac{|Z(j)|^{2}|A|}{8M}\leqslant\frac{|Y(j)|^{2}|Z(j)|^{2}}{2M|A|}\leqslant\mathsf{E}(Y_{*}(j),Z(j))=\frac{|Y_{*}(j)|^{2}|Z(j)|}{T_{*}(j)}\,,

and hence

|Y_{*}(j)|\geqslant|Z(j)|\cdot\left(\frac{T_{*}(j)|A|}{8M|Z(j)|}\right)^{1/2}\geqslant|Z(j)|\cdot\left(\frac{T_{*}(j)}{4M}\right)^{1/2}\,.

(25)

As above we consider two cases: either there are at least
R/2
numbers
j\in[R]
such that
T_{*}(j)\geqslant R
or there exist at least
R/2
numbers
j\in[R]
such that
T_{*}(j)<R
.
Thus
bounds (
24
), (
25
) show that

t^{\prime\prime}=O(R\log M)=O(M^{1/3}\log M)

and therefore
the set
\mathcal{A}:=Y(t)
has size at least
(1-\varepsilon)|A|
, where
t:=t^{\prime}+t^{\prime\prime}\ll M^{1/3}\log M
.
Let us show that
\mathcal{A}
has small doubling and then apply Proposition
5
to obtain that our initial set
A
has small doubling.
We have
\mathcal{A}\subseteq A^{\prime}+X_{1}+\dots+X_{t}
, where one has
|X_{j}|\ll M^{3}
thanks to our choice of the parameter
T
, bounds
(
20
), (
21
), (
25
) and the estimate
|A^{\prime}|\gg|A|/M
.
It follows that

|\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{A}|\leqslant(|X_{1}|\dots|X_{t}|)^{2}|A^{\prime}+A^{\prime}|\ll\exp(O(t\log M))|A^{\prime}|\ll\exp(O(M^{1/3}\log^{2}M))\cdot|\mathcal{A}|\,.

Using Proposition
5
, we complete the proof.

\hfill\Box

4
A counterexample

In this section we obtain the second part of Theorem
8
.
Our construction follows naturally from the proof of Theorem
14
of the previous section
(in the notation of the latter theorem we basically construct the sets
Y(j)
in the example below)
and
resembles a “bridge graph” see, e.g.,
[
13
, Section 6.5]
and especially
[
4
]
(note also that in
[
4
, Remark 4.3]
the connectedness
\alpha
is of order
1/k^{2}
, not
1/k
as claimed).

Theorem 7

Let
p>2
be a prime number.
Then there exists a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
such that

\mathcal{D}[A]=\min\left\{\Omega_{p}\left(\frac{|A|\log^{2}\log|A|}{\log^{2}|A|}\right),\,\exp(\Omega_{p}(\mathcal{E}^{1/4}_{*}[A]\cdot\log\mathcal{E}_{*}[A]))\right\}\,.

(26)

P r o o f.

Let
a
,
M>p
and
k
,
M\ll k\leqslant M/2
,
kM^{k-1}\ll a
be some integer parameters which we will choose later and we suppose that

a/k

and
M+1
are some powers of

p
.
We take
L_{k}<L_{k-1}<\dots<L_{1}<\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
be some subspaces,

\frac{a}{kM^{j-1}}\leqslant|L_{j}|\leqslant\frac{ap}{kM^{j-1}}
,
j\in[k]
, and

H_{2},\dots,H_{k}<\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
be another collection of subspaces, where
|H_{j}|=M+1
,
j=2,\dots,k
.
Put
H^{*}_{j}=H_{j}\setminus\{0\}
.
We assume that
H^{*}_{2},\dots,H^{*}_{k}
and
L_{1}
are mutually independent in the sense that
the sumset
H^{*}_{2}+\dots+H^{*}_{k}+L_{1}
is direct
(for example,
L_{1}
occupies the first
m_{1}
coordinates in the standard basis and each
H^{*}_{2},\dots,H^{*}_{k}
occupies some other coordinates in
\mathbb{F}^{n}_{p}
).
Put
A_{1}=L_{1}
and
A_{j}=L_{j}\dotplus(H^{*}_{2}\dotplus\dots\dotplus H^{*}_{j})
,
j=2,\dots,k
and let
A=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{k}A_{j}
.
Then
a\leqslant|A|\leqslant pa
and

|A+A|\geqslant|A_{1}+A_{k}|=|L_{1}||H^{*}_{2}+\dots+H^{*}_{k}|\geqslant\frac{M^{k-1}a}{k}\gg_{p}|A|\cdot\exp(\Omega(M\log M))\,,

(27)

and thus
\mathcal{D}[A]=\exp(\Omega_{p}(M\log M))
.

Now let us estimate
\mathcal{E}_{*}[A]
.
Take any sets
S,T
such that
A=S\bigsqcup T
.
We have either

|S\cap A_{1}|\geqslant\frac{|A_{1}|}{2}=\frac{a}{2k}

(28)

or
|T\cap A_{1}|\geqslant|A_{1}|/2
(or both).
Without loosing of the generality assume that
inequality (
28
)
takes place.
Choose the maximal
j
such that
|S\cap A_{i}|\geqslant|A_{i}|/2
for all
i\in[j]
.
Therefore
we see that if
j<k
,
then

|T\cap A_{j+1}|\geqslant|A_{j+1}|/2
.
Put
S^{\prime}=S^{\prime}_{j}:=S\cap A_{j}
and
T^{\prime}=T^{\prime}_{j}:=T\cap A_{j+1}
.
By the construction one has

|S^{\prime}|\geqslant\frac{a}{2k}\geqslant\frac{|S|}{2kp}\,,\quad\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\quad|T^{\prime}|\geqslant\frac{a}{2k}\geqslant\frac{|T|}{2kp}\,,

(29)

provided
j<k
.
Put

\Lambda_{S}=H^{*}_{2}\dotplus\dots\dotplus H^{*}_{j}
,
\Lambda_{T}=H^{*}_{2}\dotplus\dots\dotplus H^{*}_{j+1}
and then by the mutual independence, we have

S^{\prime}=\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{S}}\{s^{\prime}\in S^{\prime}~:~s^{\prime}=\lambda+l,\,l\in L_{j}\}=\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{S}}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}\,,

and similarly for
T^{\prime}
.
Let
\tilde{\Lambda}_{S}
be the collection of
\lambda\in\Lambda_{S}
such that
|S^{\prime}_{\lambda}|\geqslant|S^{\prime}|/(2|\Lambda_{S}|)
.
Then

\sum_{\lambda\in\tilde{\Lambda}_{S}}|S^{\prime}_{\lambda}|\geqslant\frac{|S^{\prime}|}{2}\geqslant\frac{|L_{j}|M^{j-1}}{4}

and hence
|\tilde{\Lambda}_{S}|\geqslant|\Lambda_{S}|/4
.
In a similar way, one can define
\tilde{\Lambda}_{T}
and then

|\tilde{\Lambda}_{T}|\geqslant|\Lambda_{T}|/4
.
Finally, put
S^{\prime\prime}=\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in\tilde{\Lambda}_{S}}S^{\prime}_{\lambda}
and

T^{\prime\prime}=\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in\tilde{\Lambda}_{T}}T^{\prime}_{\lambda}
.
Let us
estimate

\mathsf{E}(S^{\prime\prime},T^{\prime\prime})

from below, this will give us a lower bound for
\mathsf{E}(S,T)
.
Applying the mutual independence, the estimate
j\leqslant k\leqslant M/2
and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality twice, we have

\mathsf{E}(S^{\prime\prime},A_{j},T^{\prime\prime},A_{j+1})\geqslant\sum_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\tilde{\Lambda}_{S},\,\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\tilde{\Lambda}_{T}~:~\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}\mathsf{E}(S^{\prime}_{\lambda_{1}},L_{j},T^{\prime}_{\mu_{1}},L_{j+1})

(30)

\geqslant\sum_{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\tilde{\Lambda}_{S},\,\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\tilde{\Lambda}_{T}~:~\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}\frac{|S^{\prime}_{\lambda_{1}}||T^{\prime}_{\mu_{1}}||L_{j}||L_{j+1}|}{|L_{j}|}\gg\frac{|L_{j+1}||A_{j}||A_{j+1}|}{|\Lambda_{S}||\Lambda_{T}|}\cdot\mathsf{E}(\tilde{\Lambda}_{S},\tilde{\Lambda}_{T})

\gg\frac{|L_{j+1}||A_{j}||A_{j+1}||\Lambda_{S}||\Lambda_{T}|}{|\Lambda_{S}+\Lambda_{T}|}\geqslant\left(1-\frac{1}{M+1}\right)^{j}\cdot|L_{j+1}||A_{j}||A_{j+1}||\Lambda_{S}|

\gg\frac{|L_{j+1}||S^{\prime}|^{2}|T^{\prime}|}{|L_{j}|}\gg_{p}\frac{|S^{\prime}|^{2}|T^{\prime}|}{M}\,.

(31)

Now
using
the Cauchy–Schwartz inequality one more time,
we get

\mathsf{E}(S,T)\mathsf{E}(A_{j},A_{j+1})\geqslant\mathsf{E}(S^{\prime\prime},T^{\prime\prime})\mathsf{E}(A_{j},A_{j+1})\gg_{p}\frac{|S^{\prime}|^{4}|T^{\prime}|^{2}}{M^{2}}\,.

(32)

From the mutual independence it is easy to see that

\mathsf{E}(A_{j},A_{j+1})\ll_{p}\left(\frac{a}{k}\right)^{3}M^{-(j-1)}M^{-2j}(M^{j-1})^{3}M\ll\frac{a^{3}}{Mk^{3}}\,,

(33)

similarly,

\mathsf{E}(A_{j})\ll_{p}\left(\frac{a}{k}\right)^{3}M^{-3(j-1)}(M^{j-1})^{3}\ll\frac{a^{3}}{k^{3}}\,,

(34)

and hence in view of (
29
) and our choice of the parameter
k
one derive from (
32
)

\mathsf{E}(S,T)\gg_{p}\frac{k^{3}|S^{\prime}|^{4}|T^{\prime}|^{2}}{Ma^{3}}\gg\frac{k|S^{\prime}|^{2}|T^{\prime}|^{2}}{Ma}\gg\frac{|S|^{2}|T|^{2}}{Mk^{3}a}\gg\frac{|S|^{2}|T|^{2}}{M^{4}|A|}\,.

(35)

Now assume that
j=k
.
In this case we have
|S\cap A_{i}|\geqslant|A_{i}|/2
for all
i\in[k]
and in particular
|S|\geqslant a/2
.
Let
J\subseteq[k]
be the collection of indexes such that
|T\cap A_{j}|\geqslant|T|/(2k)
.
For
j\in J
put
T^{*}_{j}:=T\cap A_{j}
.
Applying the previous argument for all
j\in J
and repeating the computations in (
30
)—(
31
), we obtain

\mathsf{E}(S^{\prime\prime}_{j},A_{j},T^{*}_{j},A_{j})\gg|L_{j}||A_{j}||T^{*}_{j}||\Lambda_{S}|\gg|S^{\prime}_{j}|^{2}|T^{*}_{j}|\,.

Thus summing over
j\in J
and using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we obtain as above

\sum_{j\in J}\mathsf{E}^{1/2}(S^{\prime\prime}_{j},T^{*}_{j})\mathsf{E}^{1/2}(A_{j})\gg\sum_{j\in J}|S^{\prime}_{j}|^{2}|T^{*}_{j}|\gg\frac{|S|^{2}|T|}{k^{2}}\,.

It remains to apply the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality one more time, as well as bound (
34
) to derive

\frac{a^{3}}{k^{3}}\mathsf{E}(S,T)k\gg_{p}\frac{|S|^{4}|T|^{2}}{k^{4}}\gg\frac{|S|^{2}|T|^{2}a^{2}}{k^{4}}\,.

This implies

\mathsf{E}(S,T)\gg_{p}\frac{|S|^{2}|T|^{2}}{k^{2}a}\gg\frac{|S|^{2}|T|^{2}}{M^{2}|A|}\,.

(36)

Combining bounds (
35
) and (
36
), we see that
\mathcal{E}_{*}[A]\ll_{p}M^{4}
.
Substituting this bound into (
27
), we obtain the result.
It remains to check the condition
kM^{k-1}\ll M^{M}\ll a
but if not, then choose
k\sim M
such that
M^{M}\sim|A|
and then bound (
27
) gives us
\mathcal{D}[A]\gg\Omega_{p}\left(\frac{|A|\log^{2}\log|A|}{\log^{2}|A|}\right)
.
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

5
An application

Now we obtain an application to the arithmetic regularity lemma, see
[
8
]
. We formulate our result in the following form.

Theorem 8

Let
A\subseteq{\mathbf{G}}
be a set,
\mathsf{E}(A)=|A|^{3}/K
and let
\varepsilon,\omega\in(0,1/4]
be parameters.
Then there is a decomposition of
A
as a disjoint union

A=\left(\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{k}A_{j}\right)\bigsqcup\Omega
such that

(i)~
(Components are large).
|A_{j}|\geqslant\sqrt{\omega/2K}\cdot|A|
,
j\in[k]
.

(ii)~
(Components are structured).
\mathcal{E}_{*}[A_{j}]\leqslant 4K(\omega\varepsilon)^{-2}
,
j\in[k]
.
In particular, for any
j\in[k]
one has
\mathcal{D}[A_{j}]\ll\exp(O(K^{1/3}(\omega\varepsilon)^{-2/3}\cdot\log^{2}(K\omega^{-1}\varepsilon^{-1})))
.

(iii)~
(Different components do not communicate).
\mathsf{E}(A_{i},A_{j})\leqslant\varepsilon\frac{|A_{i}|^{2}|A_{j}|^{2}}{|A|}
,
i,j\in[k]
,
i\neq j
.

(iv)~
(Noise term).
\mathsf{E}(\Omega,A)\leqslant\omega\mathsf{E}(A)
,
j\in[k]
.

P r o o f.

In the proof we follow the argument from
[
4
]
.
Put
\varepsilon_{1}=\omega\varepsilon/2
.
For
B\subseteq A
write
\mu(B)=|B|/|A|
.
Consider all partitions of
A
into disjoint sets
A_{1},\dots,A_{l}
,
l\geqslant 1
and choose
one such that the sum

\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant l}(\mathsf{E}(A_{i},A_{j},A,A)-\varepsilon_{1}\mu(A_{i})\mu(A_{j})\mathsf{E}(A))

is minimal.
If the minimal value is attained at several partitions, take any of them.
Clearly, for an arbitrary
i\in[l]
and any
S,T
such that
S\bigsqcup T=A_{i}
one has

\mathsf{E}(S,T,A,A)\geqslant\varepsilon_{1}\mu(S)\mu(T)\mathsf{E}(A)
(otherwise we have a contradiction with the minimality).
Thus by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality
the following holds

\mathsf{E}(S,T)\geqslant\varepsilon^{2}_{1}\frac{|S|^{2}|T|^{2}\mathsf{E}(A)}{|A|^{4}}=\varepsilon^{2}_{1}\frac{|S|^{2}|T|^{2}}{K|A|}

and therefore
\mathcal{E}_{*}[A_{i}]\leqslant 4K(\omega\varepsilon)^{-2}
for all
i\in[l]
.
Then Theorem
14
gives us the required upper bound for
\mathcal{D}[A_{i}]
.
Using the minimality again, we see that for all
i,j\in[l]
,
i\neq j
the following holds

\mathsf{E}(A_{i},A_{j},A,A)\leqslant\varepsilon_{1}\mu(A_{i})\mu(A_{j})\mathsf{E}(A)\,.

(37)

Putting
I:=\{i~:~|A_{i}|\leqslant\sqrt{\omega/2K}\cdot|A|\}
and
\Omega=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}A_{i}
, we obtain

\mathsf{E}(A,\Omega)=\sum_{i,j\in[l]}\mathsf{E}(A_{i},A_{j},\Omega,\Omega)\leqslant\sum_{m\in[l]}\mathsf{E}(A_{m},\Omega)+\varepsilon_{1}\mathsf{E}(A)\sum_{i,j\in[l]}\mu(A_{i})\mu(A_{j})

\leqslant\sum_{m\in[l]}\sum_{i\in I}\mathsf{E}(A_{m},A_{m},A_{i},A)+\varepsilon_{1}\mathsf{E}(A)\leqslant\sum_{i\in I}\mathsf{E}(A_{i})+3\varepsilon_{1}\mathsf{E}(A)

\leqslant 2^{-1}\omega|A|^{3}/K+3\varepsilon_{1}\mathsf{E}(A)\leqslant\omega\mathsf{E}(A)

and thus we have obtained (iv).
Finally, in view of (
37
) for any
i,j\notin I
,
i\neq j
one has

\mathsf{E}(A_{i},A_{j})\leqslant\mathsf{E}(A_{i},A_{j},A,A)\leqslant\varepsilon_{1}\mu(A_{i})\mu(A_{j})\mathsf{E}(A)\leqslant\varepsilon\frac{|A_{i}|^{2}|A_{j}|^{2}}{|A|}\,.

This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

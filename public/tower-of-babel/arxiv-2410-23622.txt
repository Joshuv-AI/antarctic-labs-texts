Optimality Condition for the Petz Map
Bikun Li, Zhaoyou Wang, Guo Zheng, Yat Wong, Liang Jiang

Abstract
In quantum error correction, the Petz map serves as a perfect recovery map when the Knill-Laflamme conditions are satisfied. Notably, while perfect recovery is generally infeasible for most quantum channels of finite dimension, the Petz map remains a versatile tool with near-optimal performance in recovering quantum states. This work introduces and proves, for the first time, the necessary and sufficient conditions for the optimality of the Petz map in terms of entanglement fidelity. In some special cases, the violation of this condition can be easily characterized by a simple commutator that can be efficiently computed. We provide multiple examples that substantiate our new findings.



Protecting fragile quantum information from noisy environments is a critical challenge in quantum information processing, as the quantum advantage over classical systems largely relies on maintaining nontrivial quantum states with high fidelity. Over the past two decades, quantum error correction (QEC) has emerged as an essential tool for shielding quantum systems against noise
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. Its applications span various physical platforms and promise architectures that extend beyond fault-tolerant quantum computers
[
4
,
5
]
. In addition, QEC has established itself as a cornerstone in other fields such as reliable quantum communication
[
6
,
7
]
and high-precision quantum sensing
[
8
,
9
]
. The fundamental principle of quantum error-correcting codes involves encoding logical quantum states with redundancies. After transmission through a noise channel, a decoding or recovery operation is performed to suppress errors, preserving the coherence of the logical information. The conception, benchmarking, and implementation of concrete QEC schemes have become focal points of research in quantum information science in recent years
[

,

,
14

.

A foundational work regarding QEC is the Knill-Laflamme (KL) conditions
[

]
, which mathematically present the necessary and sufficient conditions for faithful recovery of encoded quantum information. Under the KL conditions, it is well-known that the
Petz map
can perfectly recover the encoded quantum information
[
17
]
. However, the KL conditions are commonly violated in most finite quantum codes and physical noise channels. In these cases, the optimal recovery map can be found using numerical approaches
[
18
,
19
]
. Essentially, since the Petz map approximates the reverse of a quantum channel by the analog of Bayes’ theorem, it still guarantees near-optimal performance when perfect quantum state restoration is impossible
[
17
,
20
,
21
]
. The approach of Petz map has been applied to various areas involving the reconstruction of quantum information, such as approximate quantum error correction
[
20
]
, many-body open quantum systems

, and black hole physics

This work investigates the sufficient and necessary conditions for the Petz map to be the optimal recovery map for QEC, even when the KL conditions are violated. These conditions enable efficient verification of optimality without the need to run any optimization algorithm.
We highlight that the optimality of the Petz map has been studied for different objective functions

,
33

Our work mainly focuses on the entanglement fidelity, which generalizes some results from
[

Moreover, we provide many interesting quantum channels that satisfy our criterion. In conjunction with recent work
[
21

, our findings make analytical estimation of the optimal entanglement fidelity for these quantum channels possible.

Preliminary.
—
Generally, the QEC process involves encoding a
d
-dimensional "logical" Hilbert space
\mathcal{L}:=\mathbb{C}^{d}
into an
n
-dimensional "physical" space
\mathcal{H}:=\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The encoded state residing in
\mathcal{H}
subsequently experiences noise, which may corrupt the original quantum information. In this work, we denote this
composite
quantum operation by
\mathcal{E}
, which has the Kraus representation
\mathcal{E}\sim\{\hat{E}_{k}\}
with Kraus operators
\hat{E}_{k}:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{H}
for
1\leq k\leq n_{K}
. We call
\mathcal{E}
a quantum channel, but it can be generalized to the trace nonincreasing case.

\{\hat{E}_{k}\}
induces an
n_{K}
-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}:=\mathbb{C}^{n_{K}}
, which is considered as the "environment" required to define the complementary channel of
\mathcal{E}
.

Given a Hilbert space
\mathcal{V}
,
\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{V})
denotes the space of all positive semidefinite (PSD) operators acting on
\mathcal{V}
. The notation
I_{\mathcal{V}}
represents the identity matrix (or projector) acting on
\mathcal{V}
. If
\mathcal{V}
corresponds to a subsystem, then
\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{V}}(\cdots)
represents the partial trace over the subspace
\mathcal{V}
.

We define
M_{\sigma}\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{K})
as the QEC matrix associated with the reference state
\sigma\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{L})
and the noise channel
\mathcal{E}
, with matrix components given by

(M_{\sigma})_{[\mu k],[\nu\ell]}:=\left\langle\mu\right|\sqrt{\sigma}\hat{E}_{k}^{\dagger}\hat{E}_{\ell}\sqrt{\sigma}\left|\nu\right\rangle\;.

(1)

Here,
\left|\mu\right\rangle,\left|\nu\right\rangle
denote orthonormal basis vectors in
\mathcal{L}
. We refer specifically to the special case
M:=M_{I_{\mathcal{L}}}
as the QEC matrix, which has been demonstrated to be a powerful tool in prior works
[
34
,
21
,
35
]
.
The generalized definition in Eq. (
1
) becomes crucial in our subsequent discussion of the Petz map with the reference state
\sigma
.
Note that
M^{T}
can also be interpreted as the Choi matrix of
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{c}}
, which is the complementary channel of
\mathcal{E}

[
36
,
37
]
.

In this work, an unknown logical input state is represented by a general mixed state
\rho\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{L})
.
To recover
\rho
from the noise corruption, the error correction process is followed by a recovery map
\mathcal{R}\sim\{\hat{R}_{i}\}
, where
\hat{R}_{i}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{L}
for
i=1,2,\cdots
.
Maintaining the entanglement of
\rho
with other subsystems is crucial in QEC for practical applications. Therefore, in this study, the entanglement fidelity serves as the performance metric
[
38
]
for the composite map
\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{E}
, defined as

\displaystyle F_{e}(\rho,\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle:=\left\langle\psi_{\rho}\right|\mathcal{I}\otimes\left(\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{E}\right)(\psi_{\rho})\left|\psi_{\rho}\right\rangle

(2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,k}\left|\mathrm{tr}\big(\hat{R}_{i}\hat{E}_{k}\rho\big)\right|^{2}\;,

where
\left|\psi_{\rho}\right\rangle\in\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{L}
is the state vector representing the canonical purification of
\rho
,
\psi_{\rho}\equiv|\psi_{\rho}\rangle\!\langle\psi_{\rho}|
, and
\mathcal{I}
is the identity map acting on the reference subsystem
\mathcal{L}
.
When
\rho
is a maximally mixed state in
\mathcal{L}
,
F_{e}
is closely related to the average fidelity
[
39
]
, which can be conveniently estimated via quantum tomography. Additionally,
\min_{\rho}F_{e}
bounds the diamond distance between
\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{I}
by the Fuchs–van de Graaf inequalities
[
40
]
.

The optimality of a recovery map is usually based on the definition of the objective function, which may differ across contexts.
In this work, a recovery map
\mathcal{R}
is called
optimal
if it achieves
F_{e}=F^{\mathrm{op}}_{e}:=\max_{\mathcal{R}}F_{e}(\rho,\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{E})
for a given
\mathcal{E}
. An optimal recovery map
\mathcal{R}
is further termed
perfect
if it achieves
F_{e}=1
. This perfection occurs if and only if the KL conditions
[
16
]
are satisfied:

M=I_{\mathcal{L}}\otimes\alpha\;,

(3)

where
\alpha\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{K})
satisfies
\mathrm{tr}(\alpha)=1
.
Eq. (
3
) implies that
M_{\sigma}=\sigma\otimes\alpha
.
In this situation, a universal solution for perfect recovery is the Petz map
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}
of the
reference state

\sigma

[
41
,
17
]
. Specifically,
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}
is a completely positive, trace nonincreasing map with Kraus operator

(\hat{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}})_{k}:=\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{E}_{k}^{\dagger}\mathcal{E}(\sigma)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\;,

(4)

where negative powers of operators or matrices are defined via the pseudoinverse throughout this work.
The role of
\sigma
is to ensure that when
\sigma=\rho
, we have
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\rho,\mathcal{E}}\circ\mathcal{E}(\rho)=\rho
, although this condition does not necessarily optimize
F_{e}
.
Nonetheless, the Petz map guarantees near-optimal performance with
(F^{\mathrm{op}}_{e})^{2}\leq F_{e}(\rho,\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\rho,\mathcal{E}}\circ\mathcal{E})\leq F^{\mathrm{op}}_{e}

[
17
]
.
When the reference state
\sigma
in the Petz map need not coincide with the input state
\rho
, typically it is necessary to have
\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)
to maximize
\mathrm{tr}(\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}\circ\mathcal{E}(\rho))
.
If the reference state
\sigma
is a maximally mixed state that has the largest support, then the Petz map becomes the
transpose channel
(TC), denoted as
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{TC}}

[
20
]
.
If the input state
\rho
is a maximally mixed state, the entanglement fidelity reduces to the
channel fidelity
. Remarkably, when both
\rho
and
\sigma
are maximally mixed states, the channel fidelity given by TC has a simple form
[
21
]
:

F^{\mathrm{TC}}_{e}=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\left\|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{M}\right\|^{2}_{F}\;,

(5)

where
\|\cdots\|_{F}
denotes the Frobenius norm.

Main result.
—
By leveraging the definition of the QEC matrix, we arrive at a compact expression for
F_{e}
(Theorem
1
), where the recovery map is the Petz map with an arbitrary reference state. All detailed proofs of theorems and corollaries in this work are provided in Supplemental Material
[
37
]
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\rho,\sigma\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{L})
, the entanglement fidelity associated with the input state
\rho
, and the quantum channel
\Phi=\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}\circ\mathcal{E}
has a compact form given by

F_{e}(\rho,\Phi)=\left\|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\left(M_{\sigma}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sqrt{\sigma}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}\right)M\left(\rho\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}\right)\right)\right\|^{2}_{F}\;.

(6)

We emphasize that Theorem
1
does not impose any matrix support requirements on
\rho
and
\sigma
. Eq. (
6
) directly generalizes the result from the previous work, Eq. (
5
), which specifically assumes
\rho=\sigma=I_{\mathcal{L}}/d
.
For the general case with
\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)
, we can define

T:=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\left(\left(\gamma^{\dagger}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}\right)\sqrt{M_{\sigma}}\right)\;,

(7)

with
\gamma:=\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho
, and reduce Eq. (
6
) to
F_{e}=\|T\|_{F}^{2}
.
If the KL conditions (Eq. (
3
)) are further applied, the simplified
F_{e}
equals
1
regardless of the choice of
\sigma
and
\rho
.

Although no known closed-form expression exists for the optimal recovery map when the KL conditions are violated, we can still identify the complete family of triplets
(\rho,\sigma,\mathcal{E})
for which the Petz map is precisely the optimal recovery map. Our result is summarized in Theorem
2
(also see Fig.
1
).

Theorem 2
.

Let
\rho,\sigma\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{L})
with
\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)
, and let
M_{\sigma}
be the QEC matrix associated with the reference state
\sigma
and the noise channel
\mathcal{E}
.
Then,
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}
optimizes
F_{e}(\rho,\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{E})
if and only if

B:=\sqrt{M_{\sigma}}\left(\gamma\otimes T\right)\succeq 0\;.

(8)

Proof.
–
We provide the outline of the proof as follows. First, we demonstrate the
necessity
of
B\succeq 0
when
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}
is optimal.
This is achieved by considering variations of the recovery map
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}(\varepsilon)
, defined via its Kraus operators:

\hat{R}_{k}(\varepsilon):=\big(\hat{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}\big)_{k}+\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{s}\hat{V}^{(s)}_{k}\;,

(9)

where
\hat{V}^{(s)}_{k}
can be carefully and flexibly chosen such that for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon
,
\hat{R}_{k}(\varepsilon)
has a bounded
2
-norm for each
k
, and
\mathcal{R}(\varepsilon)
is TP on
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{E}(\sigma))
.
Assuming that
F_{e}(\rho,\mathcal{R}(\varepsilon)\circ\mathcal{E})
achieves its global maximum at
\varepsilon=0
, we must have
\mathrm{d}F_{e}/\mathrm{d}\varepsilon=0
and
\mathrm{d}^{2}F_{e}/\mathrm{d}\varepsilon^{2}\leq 0
. From the expression of these two derivative conditions, it naturally follows that
B\succeq 0
, thereby proving the necessity.

The
sufficiency
of Theorem
2
is proved by examining the optimal conditions of the semidefinite programming problem. Briefly, finding the optimal quantum channel for recovery is equivalent to solving a convex optimization problem:

\begin{array}[]{lll}&\text{minimize}\quad-F_{e}[C_{\mathcal{R}}]\\
&\text{subject to}\quad G:=I_{\mathcal{H}}-\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}C_{\mathcal{R}}\succeq 0\\
&\qquad\qquad\quad\;\text{and}\quad C_{\mathcal{R}}\succeq 0\;,\\
\end{array}

(10)

The objective function is the negative entanglement fidelity
F_{e}(\rho,\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{E})
, which can be rewritten as a linear function
F_{e}[C_{\mathcal{R}}]=\mathrm{tr}\left(C_{\mathcal{R}}^{T}(\rho^{T}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})C_{\mathcal{E}}(\rho^{T}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})\right)
with respect to the Choi matrix
C_{\mathcal{R}}
.
Here, the components for the Choi matrices of
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{E}
are given by
(C_{\mathcal{R}})^{ab}_{\mu\nu}:=\left\langle\mu\right|\mathcal{R}\left(\left|a\right\rangle\!\left\langle b\right|\right)\left|\nu\right\rangle
and
(C_{\mathcal{E}})^{ab}_{\mu\nu}:=\left\langle a\right|\mathcal{E}\left(\left|\mu\right\rangle\!\left\langle\nu\right|\right)\left|b\right\rangle
, respectively, where
\left|a\right\rangle
and
\left|b\right\rangle
are orthonormal basis in
\mathcal{H}
.
This work verifies the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions
[
42
,
37
]
by investigating the explicit solution of the Lagrange function:

\mathscr{L}(C_{\mathcal{R}},\Lambda,\Gamma):=-F_{e}[C_{\mathcal{R}}]-\mathrm{tr}(\Lambda^{T}G)-\mathrm{tr}(\Gamma^{T}C_{\mathcal{R}})\;.

(11)

We find that the Lagrange multiplier solutions are given by

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\big(C_{*}^{T}\nabla_{C^{T}_{\mathcal{R}}}F_{e}\big)\;,

(12)

\displaystyle\Gamma

\displaystyle=I_{\mathcal{L}}\otimes\Lambda-\nabla_{C_{\mathcal{R}}^{T}}F_{e}\;,

(13)

which are evaluated at the optimal Choi matrix
C_{*}=C_{\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}}
.
The challenge here is that for a general PSD
C_{\mathcal{R}}
, we do not have
\Lambda\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{H})
and
\Gamma\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{H})
, which are the conditions of dual feasibility required by the KKT conditions.
Surprisingly,
B\succeq 0
enables
\Lambda
and
\Gamma
to be PSD when the recovery map is the Petz map. Together with other KKT conditions, which can be verified easily due to the linearity of
\mathscr{L}

[
37
]
, the sufficiency of Theorem
2
is proved.

\Box

If the KL conditions are satisfied, it is easy to deduce that
B=\rho\otimes\alpha\succeq 0
, because
M
is a tensor product matrix as displayed in Eq. (
3
).
Generally, the condition
B\succeq 0
in Theorem
2
appears obscure and lacks physical meaning. Nevertheless, this new result can be better comprehended under some simple assumptions regarding
\rho
and
\sigma
.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\rho,\sigma\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{L})
satisfying
[\rho,\sigma]=0
with
\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)
.
Then,
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}
optimizes
F_{e}(\rho,\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{E})
if and only if

\left[M_{\sigma},\gamma\otimes T\right]=0\;.

(14)

The above commutation relation is a generalization of the optimality condition of
singlet fraction
in prior work
[
32
]
, where the Petz map is referred to as the “pretty good map”.
Specifically, their singlet fraction is defined by the overlap between the bipartite state
\mathcal{I}\otimes(\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{E})(\psi_{\sigma})\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{L})
and
\left|\psi_{I_{\mathcal{L}}}\right\rangle
, such that the objective function is proportional to
F_{e}(\sqrt{\sigma},\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{E})
.
Its optimality condition at
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}
is immediately recovered by setting
\rho=\sqrt{\sigma}
in Eq. (
14
):

\left[M_{\sigma},I_{\mathcal{L}}\otimes\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{M_{\sigma}}\right]=0\;.

(15)

Notice that
B\succeq 0
is the most general condition, while Eq. (
14
) is its special version with commuting
\rho
and
\sigma
, and Eq. (
15
) is a more specialized version which only optimizes
F_{e}
for the input
\sqrt{\sigma}
.
We emphasize that there is no equivalence among these three conditions, making this work a meaningful generalization of previous research.
This conclusion is substantiated with explicit examples in
[
37
]
, which satisfy either of the first two conditions but violate their special case versions.

In practice, one may be interested in the structure of
\mathcal{E}
that enables the Petz map to maximize
F_{e}
. The following corollary illustrates one possible underlying structure.

Corollary 2
.

Let
M_{\sigma}
be the QEC matrix associated with the reference state
\sigma
and the noise channel
\mathcal{E}
, and
\mathcal{K}=\bigoplus_{s}\mathcal{K}_{s}
. If
\sqrt{M_{\sigma}}=\bigoplus_{s}\beta_{s}
, where
\beta_{s}\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{s})
satisfying
\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\beta_{s}\propto I_{\mathcal{K}_{s}}
, then
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}
optimizes
F_{e}(\sqrt{\sigma},\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{E})
.

A particularly useful scenario of Corollary
2
occurs when
\dim\mathcal{K}_{s}=1
for all
s
. A channel
\mathcal{E}
that satisfies the condition
\sqrt{\sigma}\hat{E}^{\dagger}_{k}\hat{E}_{\ell}\sqrt{\sigma}=0
for all
k\neq\ell
falls within this case. This implies that the operators
\hat{E}_{k}
corresponding to different values of
k
map
\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)
to orthogonal subspaces in
\mathcal{H}
. We emphasize, however, that logical quantum information may still be distorted within each subspace, thereby preventing perfect recovery of logical quantum information. The optimal recovery operation
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}
in this situation can be realized by perfectly discriminating the state from the ensemble
\{(I_{\mathcal{L}}\otimes\hat{E}_{\ell})\left|\psi_{\sigma}\right\rangle\}
with
I_{\mathcal{L}}\otimes\sqrt{\sigma}\hat{E}_{k}^{\dagger}
, and then leaving the logical space unchanged.
Since
\hat{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{k}
depends solely on
\hat{E}_{k}
, the experimental implementation of
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}
may be straightforward if applying
\hat{E}_{k}^{\dagger}
is simple.

Another notable case of Corollary
2
is going to another extreme, where
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}_{1}
, so trivially, there is
\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{M_{\sigma}}\propto I_{\mathcal{K}}
.
Examples of channels
\mathcal{E}
falling into this category include the completely depolarizing channel and certain dephasing channels when
\sigma
is a maximally mixed state. In such cases, the Petz map
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}=\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{TC}}=\mathcal{E}^{\dagger}
achieves the same performance as the identity channel. More analytical examples are demonstrated in
[
37
]
.

Applications.
—
Theorem
2
provides a new, efficient way of quantifying the suboptimality of the Petz map.
An intuitive way to measure this suboptimality numerically is to evaluate the fidelity gap between the optimal fidelity and that achieved by the Petz map. Typically, the optimal fidelity is obtained by inputting
C_{\mathcal{E}}
into iterative algorithms
[
18
]
or semidefinite programming
[
19
]
. The first approach typically has a better polynomial complexity
\mathcal{O}(n_{\mathrm{iter}}n^{3}d^{3})
for
n_{\mathrm{iter}}
steps. The complexity may be further reduced to
\mathcal{O}(n_{\mathrm{iter}}n_{K}^{3})
if a small-sized
M
with
n_{K}<nd
is directly available.
In such cases, an effective Choi matrix
C_{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
, with redundant degrees of freedom removed, can be constructed from
M
and subsequently fed into the numerical solver.
Numerical experiments suggest that a potential drawback of these iterative approaches is that
n_{\mathrm{iter}}
may depend on
n
,
n_{K}
, and
d
, even if the initial guess for the recovery map is
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}
.
If one’s goal is merely to verify the optimality of
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}
, then confirming the positivity of
B
has complexity
\mathcal{O}(n_{K}^{3})
. In particular, when
[\rho,\sigma]=0
, computing the norm of the commutator in Eq. (
14
) is also more efficient, with complexity
\mathcal{O}(n_{K}^{3})
independent of the unknown iteration number
n_{\mathrm{iter}}
.

When QEC is considered, the input state of interest is typically
\rho=I_{\mathcal{L}}/d
. This is because practical quantum computational tasks usually involve highly entangled logical states that locally appear highly mixed.
In contrast to previous approximate QEC studies, such as
[
20
]
, which focus primarily on qubit stabilizer codes, our goal here is to demonstrate a case where the fidelity gap
F^{\mathrm{op}}_{e}-F^{\mathrm{TC}}_{e}
is negligible compared to the optimal infidelity (for the demonstration purpose, we adopt the TC, where
\sigma=I_{\mathcal{L}}/d
).
Specifically, we investigate the quantum transduction model in
[
43
]
, where quantum information is transferred from one engineered bosonic mode to another.
Let
\left|\mu_{\Delta}\right\rangle

(\mu\in\{0,1\})
be the basis of a finite energy square-lattice Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill (GKP) code
[
2
]
for a logical qubit, where
\Delta
characterizes the average energy by
\approx\hbar\omega/(2\Delta^{2})
with
\hbar\omega
being the quantized energy per particle.
The intersystem interaction of interest is defined by a beam-splitter-like Hamiltonian
\hat{H}=-ig(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{1}\hat{a}_{2}-\hat{a}_{1}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{2})
, where
\hat{a}_{1}
and
\hat{a}_{2}
are the annihilation operators for modes of the system
1
and
2
, respectively.
Let a logical qubit be encoded in the system
1
as
\hat{\rho}_{\Delta}
by
\left|\mu_{\Delta}\right\rangle
, and the state in system
2
be engineered as
\left|0_{\Delta}\right\rangle
.
Denoting the transmissivity from system
1
to
2
as
\eta:=\sin^{2}gt
, the output of the quantum channel
\mathcal{E}_{\eta}
for the logical qubit state
\hat{\rho}
is given by tracing out the first subsystem of
e^{i\hat{H}t}\hat{\rho}_{\Delta}\otimes\left|0_{\Delta}\right\rangle\!\left\langle 0_{\Delta}\right|e^{-i\hat{H}t}
.
It is well-known that if system
2
is initially a Gaussian state, transmitting quantum information via
\mathcal{E}_{\eta}
is impossible when
\eta\leq 1/2
unless a non-Gaussian resource is used in system 2
[
44
]
. Remarkably, in the highly non-Gaussian case where
\Delta\to 0
, we have the perfect recovery if
gt=\arctan\frac{2m_{1}+1}{2m_{2}}

(m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},m_{2}\neq 0)
, which implies the possibility of high fidelity quantum transduction at an arbitrarily small
\eta

[
43
,
37
]
.

In the physical case where the GKP encoding has finite energy, we do not observe the perfect quantum transduction at
\eta\to 0

Discussion.
—
This work employs the tool of the QEC matrix to investigate entanglement fidelity under the Petz map, proposing a convenient expression for the entanglement fidelity in terms of the QEC matrix. We establish criteria for the Petz map to serve as the optimal recovery channel that maximizes entanglement fidelity. In this context, it generalizes the previously known KL conditions to all cases with this property. It sheds light on various applications, such as approximate quantum error correction and the performance of recovery maps. Although it is not yet known whether an alternative equivalent physical interpretation of
B\succeq 0
exists, it serves as a useful tool in quantum information theory for exploring channels with special structures. We anticipate the physical implementation of such optimal Petz maps in future work.

II.3
The Optimality Condition

Theorem 2
.

Let
\rho,\sigma\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{L})
with
\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)
, and let
M_{\sigma}
be the QEC matrix associated with the reference state
\sigma
and the noise channel
\mathcal{E}
.
Then,
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}
optimizes
F_{e}(\rho,\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{E})
if and only if

B:=\sqrt{M_{\sigma}}\left(\gamma\otimes T\right)\succeq 0\;.

(S30)

Proof.

To prove the
necessity
, we apply the variation of
\mathcal{R}
, as defined by Eq. (
S10
). Since
\mathcal{R}(\varepsilon)
optimizes
F_{e}
at
\varepsilon=0
with any
A
fulfilling Eq. (
S16
), we have the following conditions

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon}F_{e}(\rho,\mathcal{R}(\varepsilon)\circ\mathcal{E})|_{\varepsilon=0}

\displaystyle=0\;,

(S31)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}\varepsilon^{2}}F_{e}(\rho,\mathcal{R}(\varepsilon)\circ\mathcal{E})|_{\varepsilon=0}

\displaystyle\leq 0\;,

(S32)

Eq. (
S31
) requires that
B=B^{\dagger}
, implying that
\mathrm{supp}(B)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(M_{\sigma})
. Additionally, Eq. (
S32
) demands that the second term of Eq. (
S29
) be non-negative, leading to the condition
B\hat{\Pi}_{M_{\sigma}}+\hat{\Pi}_{M_{\sigma}}B^{\dagger}=B+B^{\dagger}\succeq 0
. Therefore,
B
must be positive semidefinite (PSD).

To prove the
sufficiency
of
B\succeq 0
, we consider the KKT conditions in semidefinite programming of the following convex optimization problem
[
46
]
:

\begin{array}[]{lll}&\text{minimize}\quad-F_{e}[C_{\mathcal{R}}]\\
&\text{subject to}\quad G:=I_{\mathcal{H}}-\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}C_{\mathcal{R}}\succeq 0\text{ and }C_{\mathcal{R}}\succeq 0\;,\\
\end{array}

(S33)

where
-F_{e}[C_{\mathcal{R}}]=-\mathrm{tr}\left(C_{\mathcal{R}}\nabla_{C_{\mathcal{R}}}F_{e}(\rho,\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{E})\right)
is a differentiable convex function with respect to the Choi matrix
C_{\mathcal{R}}
. The domain of
C_{\mathcal{R}}
is also convex, as any convex combination of Choi matrices defines a valid completely positive map.
The definiteness of
G
restricts
C_{\mathcal{R}}
to be trace nonincreasing.
The optimization problem is equivalent to the Lagrange function:

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}(C_{\mathcal{R}},\Lambda,\Gamma)

\displaystyle:=\mathscr{F}(C_{\mathcal{R}})-\mathrm{tr}(\Lambda^{T}G)-\mathrm{tr}(\Gamma^{T}C_{\mathcal{R}})

(S34)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left(\left(-\nabla_{C_{\mathcal{R}}}F_{e}+I_{\mathcal{L}}\otimes\Lambda^{T}-\Gamma^{T}\right)C_{\mathcal{R}}\right)-\mathrm{tr}(\Lambda)\;,

(S35)

with Lagrange multipliers
\Lambda
and
\Gamma
that penalize the constraints
G\succeq 0
and
C_{\mathcal{R}}\succeq 0
, respectively.
According to the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions, if
C_{\mathcal{R}}=C_{*}
is the optimal solution, then there exist solutions of
\Lambda
and
\Gamma
such that the following conditions hold at
C_{\mathcal{R}}=C_{*}
.:

•

Primal feasibility:
C_{\mathcal{R}}\succeq 0
and
G\succeq 0
.

•

Dual feasibility:
\Gamma\succeq 0
,
\Lambda\succeq 0
.

•

Complementary slackness:
-\mathrm{tr}(\Lambda^{T}G)-\mathrm{tr}(\Gamma^{T}C_{\mathcal{R}})=0
.

•

Stationarity:
\nabla_{C_{\mathcal{R}}}\mathscr{L}=0
.

Assuming
\mathcal{R}_{*}=\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}
, the primal feasibility condition is satisfied. It remains to ensure that the other conditions hold under

. The strategy involves constructing
\Lambda
and
\Gamma

We begin by imposing stationarity, which requires

\Gamma=I_{\mathcal{L}}\otimes\Lambda-\nabla_{C_{\mathcal{R}}^{T}}F_{e}\;.

(S36)

Additionally, given that
\Gamma
,
\Lambda
,
G
, and

are all PSD, the condition of complementary slackness is equivalent to

\displaystyle\Gamma^{T}C_{*}

\displaystyle=0\;,

(S37)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{T}G

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{T}\left(I_{\mathcal{H}}-\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}C_{*}\right)=0\;.

(S38)

According to Eq. (

\displaystyle 0=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\left(C_{*}^{T}\Gamma\right)=-\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\left(C_{*}^{T}\nabla_{C_{\mathcal{R}}^{T}}F_{e}\right)+\left(\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}C_{*}^{T}\right)\Lambda=-\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\left(C_{*}^{T}\nabla_{C_{\mathcal{R}}^{T}}F_{e}\right)+\Lambda\;.

(S39)

By substituting

II.4
Corollaries and Their Proofs

Corollary 3
.

Let
\rho,\sigma\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{L})
satisfying
[\rho,\sigma]=0
with
\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\sigma)
.
Then,
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}
optimizes
F_{e}(\rho,\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{E})
if and only if

\left[M_{\sigma},\gamma\otimes T\right]=0.

(S58)

Proof.

If
[\rho,\sigma]=0
, then the operator
\gamma\otimes T=\gamma\otimes\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\left(\left(\gamma^{\dagger}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}\right)\sqrt{M_{\sigma}}\right)
is Hermitian. Given that the optimality condition in Theorem
2
is
B\succeq 0
, this condition is equivalent to
\left[M_{\sigma},\gamma\otimes T\right]=0
in this special case.
∎

Corollary 4
.

Let
M_{\sigma}
be the QEC matrix associated with the reference state
\sigma
and the noise channel
\mathcal{E}
, and
\mathcal{K}=\bigoplus_{s}\mathcal{K}_{s}
. If
\sqrt{M_{\sigma}}=\bigoplus_{s}\beta_{s}
, where
\beta_{s}\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{s})
satisfying
\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\beta_{s}\propto I_{\mathcal{K}_{s}}
, then
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}
optimizes
F_{e}(\sqrt{\sigma},\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{E})
.

Proof.

When the input state of
F_{e}
is
\rho=\sqrt{\sigma}
. The optimality condition (Eq. (
S58
)) reduces to
\left[M_{\sigma},I_{\mathcal{L}}\otimes\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{M_{\sigma}}\right]=0
. Meanwhile, since
\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{M_{\sigma}}=\bigoplus_{s}\left(\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\beta_{s}\right)I_{\mathcal{K}_{s}}
must commute with
\sqrt{M_{\sigma}}=\bigoplus_{s}\beta_{s}
, it follows that the Petz map
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}
must attain optimality with respect to the objective function
F_{e}(\sqrt{\sigma},\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{E})
.
∎

III
Optimality of the Petz Map: Analytical Examples

III.1
The Example with
B\succeq 0
and
[\rho,\sigma]\neq 0

In this section, we construct a model in which the triplet
(\rho,\sigma,\mathcal{E})
satisfies
B\succeq 0
but does not satisfy
[\rho,\sigma]=0
. According to Theorem
2
, the Petz map
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}
achieves optimality with respect to the objective function
F_{e}(\rho,\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{E})
, despite failing to satisfy the generalized commutation relation in Eq. (
S58
).

We consider both
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{K}
to be partitioned into orthogonal subspaces:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{s}\mathcal{L}_{s}\;,\quad\mathcal{K}=\bigoplus_{s}\mathcal{K}_{s}\;.

(S59)

Let
\rho
and
\sigma
be density matrices in
\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{L})
that satisfy
\mathrm{tr}\rho=\mathrm{tr}\sigma=1
, without any commutation relation imposed.
We assume that the input state
\rho
and the reference state
\sigma
can be decomposed as direct sums:

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\sum_{s}\rho_{s},\quad\sigma=\sum_{s}\sigma_{s},\quad\mathrm{supp}(\rho_{s})\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\sigma_{s})\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{s}\;.

(S60)

Consequently,
\gamma
also possesses a direct sum structure:

\gamma=\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho=\sum_{s}\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{s}\rho_{s}\;.

(S61)

Since
[\rho,\sigma]=0
is not required,
\gamma
may be non-Hermitian.
We consider the noise channel
\mathcal{E}
with the following QEC matrix:

\displaystyle M=\sum_{s}I_{\mathcal{L}_{s}}\otimes\alpha_{s},\quad\mathrm{supp}(\alpha_{s})\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{s}\;,

(S62)

where
\alpha_{s}\succeq 0
, and
\mathrm{tr}\alpha_{s}=1
is required to ensure that
\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{K}}M=I_{\mathcal{L}}
. Since

\sqrt{M_{\sigma}}=\sum_{s}\sqrt{\sigma_{s}}\otimes\sqrt{\alpha_{s}}\;,

(S63)

we obtain
T\equiv\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\left(\left(\gamma^{\dagger}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}\right)\sqrt{M_{\sigma}}\right)=\sum_{s}(\mathrm{tr}\rho_{s})\sqrt{\alpha_{s}}
, so

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\sqrt{M_{\sigma}}\left(\gamma\otimes T\right)=\sum_{s}\left(\mathrm{tr}\rho_{s}\right)\rho_{s}\otimes\alpha_{s}\succeq 0\;.

(S64)

Moreover, in this case, the entanglement fidelity
F_{e}(\rho,\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}\circ\mathcal{E})
(Eq. (
S6
)) is given by

\displaystyle F_{e}(\rho,\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}\circ\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle=\left\|T\right\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{s}(\mathrm{tr}\rho_{s})^{2}\leq 1\;.

(S65)

Notably, when
\mathcal{L}\equiv\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{K}\equiv\mathcal{K}_{1}
, the KL conditions are satisfied, immediately yielding the familiar result with
M=I_{\mathcal{L}}\otimes\alpha
,
B=\rho\otimes\alpha
, and
F_{e}=1
.

III.2
The Example with
B\succeq 0
and
[\rho,\sigma]=0
, but
\rho\neq\sqrt{\sigma}

This subsection presents a simple example with
\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{C}^{2}
and
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{C}^{2}
, in which Eq. (
S58
) is satisfied but the optimality condition in
[
32
]
:

\left[M_{\sigma},I_{\mathcal{L}}\otimes\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{M_{\sigma}}\right]=0

(S66)

is violated. Such an example is analytically constructed by the following parameterization of
\sigma
,
\gamma
, and
M_{\sigma}
:

\sqrt{\sigma}=\begin{bmatrix}a&\\
&b\end{bmatrix},\quad\gamma=\begin{bmatrix}s&\\
&t\end{bmatrix},\quad\sqrt{M_{\sigma}}=\begin{bmatrix}u&&&\\
&z&y&\\
&y&x&\\
&&&v\\
\end{bmatrix}\;,

(S67)

where the following conditions are required due to
\rho,\sigma,\sqrt{M_{\sigma}}\succeq 0
and
\mathrm{tr}\sigma=\mathrm{tr}\rho=1
:

a,b,s,t>0,\quad a^{2}+b^{2}=1,\quad as+bt=1\quad xz-y^{2}>0\;.

(S68)

It follows that
M
can be determined as:

M=\left(\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}\right)M_{\sigma}\left(\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}\right)=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{u^{2}}{a^{2}}&&&\\
&\frac{y^{2}+z^{2}}{a^{2}}&\frac{(x+z)y}{ab}&\\
&\frac{(x+z)y}{ab}&\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{b^{2}}&\\
&&&\frac{v^{2}}{b^{2}}\\
\end{bmatrix}\;,

(S69)

By the definition of
M
, the Kraus operators (
\hat{E}_{i}
) of
\mathcal{E}
can be derived from the submatrices of
\sqrt{M}
.
Since
M
acts on
\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{K}
, the TP condition
\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}M=I_{\mathcal{K}}
is equivalent to

\left\{\begin{aligned} u^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}-a^{2}&=0\\
v^{2}+x^{2}+y^{2}-b^{2}&=0\end{aligned}\right.\;.

(S70)

Next, we need to ensure that the commutator in Eq. (
S58
) vanishes. Note that
\sqrt{M_{\sigma}}
is not diagonal, while

\displaystyle\gamma\otimes T=\gamma\otimes\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\left(\left(\gamma^{\dagger}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}\right)\sqrt{M_{\sigma}}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}s&\\
&t\end{bmatrix}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}us+xt&\\
&zs+vt\end{bmatrix}

(S71)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}(us+xt)s&&&\\
&(zs+vt)s&&\\
&&(us+xt)t&\\
&&&(zs+vt)t\\
\end{bmatrix}\;.

(S72)

Thus, the condition

(zs+vt)s=(us+xt)t

(S73)

is needed for the commutation relation in Eq. (
S58
) to hold.
By combining Eq. (
S70
), Eq. (
S73
) and the constraints in Eq. (
S68
), we can explicitly determine the undetermined parameters as follows:

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-a^{2}},\quad s=\frac{1-bt}{a},\quad v=\sqrt{b^{2}-x^{2}-y^{2}},\quad z=\alpha u+\beta,\quad p_{2}u^{2}+p_{1}u+p_{0}=0\;.

(S74)

where
p_{2}:=1+\alpha^{2}
,
p_{1}:=2\alpha\beta
,
p_{0}:=\beta^{2}-a^{2}+y^{2}
,
\alpha:=t/s
, and
\beta:=x\alpha^{2}-v\alpha
.
The values of
a
,
x
,
y
, and
t
can be adjusted within a wide range to yield many feasible solutions. For instance, if
a=\sqrt{0.3}
,
t=1
,
x=0.25
and
y=0.08
, then we have
b=\sqrt{0.7}
,
s\approx 0.298217
,
z\approx 0.529706
,
u\approx 0.114068
and
v\approx 0.794418
. It can be verified that

\max_{\mathcal{R}}F_{e}(\rho,\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{E})=F_{e}(\rho,\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{\sigma,\mathcal{E}}\circ\mathcal{E})=\left\|T\right\|_{F}^{2}\approx 0.987703\;.

(S75)

In this case, we have ensured that
\rho\neq\sigma
, Eq. (
S66
) is violated, and Eq. (
S58
) is satisfied.

III.3
Examples with
\rho=\sigma=I_{\mathcal{L}}/d

In this section, we present examples in which the KL conditions are violated, yet the Petz map still optimizes the channel fidelity for the case
\rho=\sigma=I_{\mathcal{L}}/d
, where
d\equiv\dim\mathcal{L}
. In this special case, the Petz map is referred to as the transpose channel (TC), and the entanglement fidelity is sometimes called the channel fidelity.
For simplicity, we denote
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{TC}}:=\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{I_{\mathcal{L}},\mathcal{E}}
,
\hat{R}^{\mathrm{TC}}_{k}:=(\hat{R}^{\mathrm{P}}_{I_{\mathcal{L}},\mathcal{E}})_{k}
, and
F^{\mathrm{TC}}_{e}=F_{e}(I_{\mathcal{L}}/d,\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{TC}}\circ\mathcal{E})
. The optimal channel fidelity is denoted as
F^{\mathrm{op}}_{e}=\max_{\mathcal{R}}F_{e}(I_{\mathcal{L}}/d,\mathcal{R}\circ\mathcal{E})
.

III.3.1
A Toy Model

We present a simple example where the TC is always optimal. Consider the following CPTP map
\mathcal{E}\sim\{\hat{E}_{k}\}
, with
\hat{E}_{k}:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}^{3}
:

\hat{E}_{1}=c\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
0&b\\
0&0\\
\end{bmatrix},\quad\hat{E}_{2}=c\begin{bmatrix}0&a\\
0&0\\
b&0\\
\end{bmatrix}\;,

(S76)

where
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and the normalization factor is
c:=(a^{2}+b^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.
Accordingly, the QEC matrix and its relevant partial trace are given by

M=c^{2}\begin{bmatrix}a^{2}&&&a^{2}\\
&b^{2}&&\\
&&b^{2}&\\
a^{2}&&&a^{2}\end{bmatrix}\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;\sqrt{M}=c\begin{bmatrix}\frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}&&&\frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}\\
&b&&\\
&&b&\\
\frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}&&&\frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}\end{bmatrix}\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{M}=c\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}+b\right)I_{2}\;\;,

(S77)

which must yield
[M,I_{\mathcal{L}}\otimes\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{M}]=0
.
As an optimal recovery map, the TC can be represented by

\hat{R}^{\text{TC}}_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&0&0\\
0&1&0\end{bmatrix},\quad\hat{R}^{\text{TC}}_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&1\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&0&0\end{bmatrix}\;.

(S78)

Hence, we have the optimized channel fidelity
F^{\mathrm{op}}_{e}=F^{\mathrm{TC}}_{e}=\frac{2}{4}\left[c\left(\frac{a}{\sqrt{2}}+b\right)\right]^{2}
.

Additionally, this simple example possesses an interesting complementary channel
\mathcal{E}^{\text{c}}
. Since
M=M^{T}
is the Choi matrix for
\mathcal{E}^{\text{c}}
,
\mathcal{E}^{\text{c}}
is an entanglement-breaking channel if and only if
M
is separable, which occurs at
b\leq a
.
In particular, if
b=\sqrt{2}a
, we simultaneously have
[M,(I_{\mathcal{L}}\otimes\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{M})]=0
and
[C_{\mathcal{E}},(I_{\mathcal{L}}\otimes\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{C_{\mathcal{E}}})]=0
, where
C_{\mathcal{E}}
is the Choi matrix for
\mathcal{E}
. In this case, the TC of the complementary channel is also optimal.

III.3.2
The Pauli Channel

We consider the Pauli channel of a general
qudit
state (
d\geq 2
), where
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{d}
. In other words, the errors consist of pure logical Pauli errors.
The Kraus representation of such a Pauli channel is

\mathcal{E}\sim\left\{\sqrt{p_{g}}g:g\in\mathcal{P},p_{g}\geq 0,\sum_{g}p_{g}=1\right\}\;,

(S79)

where
\mathcal{P}=\langle X,Z\rangle/U(1)
is the generalized Pauli group (modulo global phase). The generators
Z
and
X
are represented by the following “clock and shift matrices”:

Z_{ab}=e^{i\frac{2\pi a}{d}}\delta_{ab}\;,\quad X_{ab}=\delta_{a\oplus 1,b}\;,\quad a,b\in\{0,1,\cdots,d-1\}\;,

where
a\oplus 1\equiv a+1\text{ mod }d
.
To find the QEC matrix and its powers, we first define the following
d
-by-
|\mathcal{P}|d
matrix:

E:=\left[\hat{E}_{1},\hat{E}_{2},\cdots,\hat{E}_{|\mathcal{P}|}\right]=\left[\sqrt{p_{g_{1}}}g_{1},\sqrt{p_{g_{2}}}g_{2},\cdots,\sqrt{p_{g_{|\mathcal{P}|}}}g_{|\mathcal{P}|}\right],\quad g_{i}\in\mathcal{P}.

(S80)

For instance, in the special case of
d=2
, where
\mathcal{P}=\{I,X,Y,Z\}
with
2\times 2
Pauli matrices, we have

E=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc|cc|cc|cc}\sqrt{p_{I}}&0&0&\sqrt{p_{X}}&0&-i\sqrt{p_{Y}}&\sqrt{p_{Z}}&0\\
0&\sqrt{p_{I}}&\sqrt{p_{X}}&0&i\sqrt{p_{Y}}&0&0&-\sqrt{p_{Z}}\\
\end{array}\right]\;.

Eq. (
S80
) always satisfies
I_{\mathcal{H}}=EE^{\dagger}
due to
g_{i}g_{i}^{\dagger}=I_{\mathcal{L}}
and
\sum_{g}p_{g}=1
. Thus, the QEC matrix
M=E^{\dagger}E
happens to be a projector:

M^{p}=E^{\dagger}(EE^{\dagger})^{p-1}E=E^{\dagger}E=M\;,\quad\forall p\in\mathbb{R}\;,

(For convenience, we have swapped the subsystems
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{K}
that
M
is acting on.)
According to Eq. (
S8
) and
\sigma=I_{\mathcal{L}}/d
, we find that

\left[\hat{R}^{\mathrm{TC}\dagger}_{1},\hat{R}^{\mathrm{TC}\dagger}_{2},\cdots,\hat{R}^{\mathrm{TC}\dagger}_{|\mathcal{P}|}\right]=EM^{-\frac{1}{2}}=EM=EE^{\dagger}E=E\;.

(S81)

Therefore, the TC of
\mathcal{E}
is exactly its conjugate

\mathcal{R}^{\text{TC}}=\mathcal{E}^{\dagger}\;.

(S82)

Note that the character
\mathrm{tr}g_{i}=0
for all
g_{i}\in\mathcal{P}\setminus\{I_{\mathcal{L}}\}
, implying that the off-diagonal blocks of
\sqrt{M}
are trace-less. Consequently, we obtain
\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{M}=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}M=d\cdot\mathrm{diag}\left(p_{g_{1}},p_{g_{2}},\cdots,p_{g_{|\mathcal{P}|}}\right)
.
Thus,
[M,I_{\mathcal{L}}\otimes\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{M}]=0
holds if

p_{g}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1/|\mathcal{S}|,&g\in\mathcal{S}\\
0,&g\notin\mathcal{S}\end{array}\right.\;,

(S83)

where
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{P}
is any non-empty set. Accordingly, the optimal channel fidelity is given by

F^{\mathrm{op}}_{e}=F^{\mathrm{TC}}_{e}=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\left\|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{M}\right\|^{2}_{F}=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{d}{|\mathcal{S}|}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}\;.

In the above case, no recovery (i.e. trivially applying the identity map as
\mathcal{R}
) is also the optimal recovery, since
F^{\text{id}}=\frac{1}{d^{2}}|\mathrm{tr}(I_{\mathcal{L}}/\sqrt{|\mathcal{S}|})|^{2}=1/|\mathcal{S}|=F^{\mathrm{op}}_{e}
.
We present some examples of Pauli channels satisfying Eq. (
S83
) as follows:

•

\mathcal{E}
is the dephasing channel, with
\mathcal{S}=\{I,Z,\cdots,Z^{s}\}
for any
1\leq s\leq d-1
.

•

\mathcal{E}
is the completely depolarizing channel, which corresponds to
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{P}\Longrightarrow|\mathcal{S}|=d^{2n}
.

III.3.3
The Classical-to-Classical Channel

Given discrete sets
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
,
the classical-to-classical (C2C) channel is a quantum channel that maps a classical state
\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}p_{\mathcal{X}}(x)\left|x\right\rangle\!\left\langle x\right|
to another classical state
\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}p_{\mathcal{Y}}(y)\left|y\right\rangle\!\left\langle y\right|
.
The Kraus representation of the C2C channel is given by the expression
[
47
]
:

\mathcal{E}^{\text{C2C}}\sim\left\{\sqrt{p_{\mathcal{Y}|\mathcal{X}}(y|x)}\left|y\right\rangle\!\left\langle x\right|:x\in\mathcal{X},y\in\mathcal{Y}\right\}

(S84)

where
p_{\mathcal{Y}|\mathcal{X}}
is the conditional probability distribution describing a classical channel
\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
, which is normalized such that
\sum_{y}p_{\mathcal{Y}|\mathcal{X}}(y|x)=1
for any
x
.
In Eq. (
S84
), the quantum states
\left|x\right\rangle
and
\left|y\right\rangle
represent the orthonormal bases for the input Hilbert space
\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{C}^{|\mathcal{X}|}
and the output Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{|\mathcal{Y}|}
, respectively.
Since there are
n_{K}=|\mathcal{X}||\mathcal{Y}|
independent Kraus operators, it follows that
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{C}^{|\mathcal{X}||\mathcal{Y}|}
.

We find that the TC is optimal in the following cases of C2C channels:

•

If
p_{\mathcal{Y}|\mathcal{X}}(y|x_{1})p_{\mathcal{Y}|\mathcal{X}}(y|x_{2})\propto\delta_{x_{1}x_{2}}
, for all
y\in\mathcal{Y}
and
x_{i}\in\mathcal{X}
, then
\mathcal{E}^{\text{C2C}}
results in a diagonal
M
. If
y
is viewed as the corrupted classical codeword of the logical input
x
, then this condition implies that the classical information is distinguishable (and thus perfectly recoverable) from the corrupted output. To find the optimal channel fidelity given by the TC, we observe that
\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{M}
is an
n_{K}
-by-
n_{K}
diagonal matrix:

\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{M}=\mathrm{diag}\left(\sqrt{p_{\mathcal{Y}|\mathcal{X}}(y_{1}|x_{1})},\sqrt{p_{\mathcal{Y}|\mathcal{X}}(y_{2}|x_{1})},\cdots,\sqrt{p_{\mathcal{Y}|\mathcal{X}}(y_{|\mathcal{Y}|}|x_{|\mathcal{X}|})}\right)\;,

(S85)

so we have

\displaystyle F^{\mathrm{TC}}_{e}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{X}|^{2}}\left\|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{M}\right\|_{F}^{2}

(S86)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{X}|^{2}}\sum_{x,y}\left(\sqrt{p_{\mathcal{Y}|\mathcal{X}}(y|x)}\right)^{2}

(S87)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{X}|^{2}}\sum_{x}1=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{X}|}\;,

(S88)

where Eq. (
S86
) follows from
[
21
]
and Eq. (
S88
) uses the normalization condition
\sum_{y}p_{\mathcal{Y}|\mathcal{X}}(y|x)=1
.

•

When
p_{\mathcal{Y}|\mathcal{X}}(y|x)=p_{\mathcal{Y}}(y)
, implying that
y
is independent of
x
. In this case, the channel maps an arbitrary input state to a fixed classical state
\sum_{y}p_{\mathcal{Y}}(y)\left|y\right\rangle\!\left\langle y\right|
. Since
\left|y\right\rangle
is orthogonal in the Kraus operator in Eq. (
S84
), the QEC matrix
M
is block-diagonal:

M=\bigoplus_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}p_{\mathcal{Y}}(y)\mathcal{B}\;,

(S89)

where
\mathcal{B}
is an
|\mathcal{X}|^{2}
-by-
|\mathcal{X}|^{2}
matrix independent of
y
, since
\mathcal{X}
is decoupled from
\mathcal{Y}
. Specifically,
(\mathcal{B})_{[x_{1}x_{2}],[x^{\prime}_{1}x^{\prime}_{2}]}=\delta_{x_{1}x_{2}}\delta_{x^{\prime}_{1}x^{\prime}_{2}}
, so
\sqrt{\mathcal{B}}=|\mathcal{X}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{B}\Rightarrow\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{\mathcal{B}}=|\mathcal{X}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}I_{|\mathcal{X}|}
. Due to the blockwise structure of
M
, we have
[M,I_{\mathcal{L}}\otimes\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{M}]=0
. Thus, the optimal channel fidelity in this case is given by

\displaystyle F^{\mathrm{TC}}_{e}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{X}|^{2}}\left\|\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{M}\right\|_{F}^{2}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{X}|^{2}}\bigg\|\bigoplus_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\sqrt{p_{\mathcal{Y}}(y)}|\mathcal{X}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}I_{|\mathcal{X}|}\bigg\|_{F}^{2}

(S90)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{X}|^{2}}\sum_{x,y}\left(\sqrt{p_{\mathcal{Y}}(y)}|\mathcal{X}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{X}|}\;.

The Quantum Transduction Example

The two modes are initialized using the GKP encoding. To simplify our model, we consider only the square-lattice qubit GKP code

k\neq\ell
, so
[M_{\sigma},I_{\mathcal{L}}\otimes\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{L}}\sqrt{M}_{\sigma}]=0
for any
\sigma\in\mathrm{Pos}(\mathcal{L})
, which implies the optimality of the transpose channel (even if Fig.
S1
(c) gives
F_{e}<1
).

There would be more independent Kraus operators in a physical scenario where the energy is finite (
\Delta>0
). The
\hat{E}_{k}^{\dagger}\hat{E}_{\ell}=0
condition is slightly violated, depending on the width of the highly-squeezed peaks.
To obtain the numerical results in the main text, we approximate the joint continuous-variable system
\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
with a large
n\sim 10^{3}
. It requires
\mathcal{O}(n^{3})
steps to obtain
n_{K}<n
dominant and independent Kraus operators (for instance, ignoring those Kraus operators with
\|\hat{E}_{k}\|_{2}\leq 10^{-5}
). Subsequently, the QEC matrix
M
is found with complexity
\mathcal{O}(n_{K}^{2}n)
, which can be used to reconstruct the effective Choi matrix
C^{\prime}_{\mathcal{E}_{\eta}}
of smaller size and to calculate
F^{\mathrm{TC}}_{e}
and the commutator norm. The
F^{\mathrm{op}}_{e}
is obtained by feeding
C^{\prime}_{\mathcal{E}_{\eta}}
into the algorithm in
[
18
]
, with approximately
n_{\text{iter}}\sim 30
iterations.

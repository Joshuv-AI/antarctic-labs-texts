Direct Sampling Method to Retrieve Small Objects From Two-dimensional Limited-Aperture Scattered Field Data
Won-Kwang Park

Abstract
In this study, we investigated the application of the direct sampling method (DSM) to identify small dielectric objects in a limited-aperture inverse scattering problem. Unlike previous studies, we consider the bistatic measurement configuration corresponding to the transmitter location and design indicator functions for both a single source and multiple sources, and we convert the unknown measurement data to a fixed nonzero constant. To explain the applicability and limitation of object detection, we demonstrate that the indicator functions can be expressed by an infinite series of Bessel functions, the material properties of the objects, the bistatic angle, and the converted constant. Based on the theoretical results, we explain how the imaging performance of the DSM is influenced by the bistatic angle and the converted constant. In addition, the results of our analyses demonstrate that a smaller bistatic angle enhances the imaging accuracy and that optimal selection of the converted constant is crucial to realize reliable object detection. The results of the numerical simulations obtained using a two-dimensional Fresnel dataset validated the theoretical findings and illustrate the effectiveness and limitations of the designed indicator functions for small objects.

This paper addresses the inverse scattering problem of localizing a set of small objects using scattered field data collected using a limited-aperture measurement system. Despite being an important research topic, it is extremely challenging to solve inverse scattering problems due to their inherent nonlinearity and ill-posedness. Thus, various iterative (or quantitative) and noniterative (or qualitative) inversion techniques have been investigated.

Generally, iterative techniques have been developed to reconstruct the parameter (dielectric permittivity, electric conductivity, or magnetic permeability) distribution of a bounded domain. For example, the Newton-type method was proposed for shape reconstruction of an arc-like perfectly conducting crack

Consequently, fast algorithms are required to obtain a good initial guess.

Motivated by this issue, previous studies have investigated various noniterative techniques to retrieve the existence, location, or outline shape of arbitrary shaped objects. For example, the direct sampling method (DSM) has gained increasing popularity due to its computational efficiency and robustness against noise. However, the data acquisition processes in many real-world applications are limited. For example, the diagonal elements of the scattering matrix cannot be obtained because each antenna is used exclusively for signal transmission, and the remaining antennas are utilized for signal reception (refer to the literature

Thus, in this paper, we consider the application of the DSM to a real-world limited-aperture inverse scattering problem to identify a set of small objects. To this end, we design an indicator function of the DSM by converting unmeasurable scattered field data to a fixed nonzero constant. In addition, to explore the impact of the converted constant and bistatic angle, we demonstrate that the designed indicator function can be expressed by an infinite series of Bessel functions of integer order of the first kind, the material properties of the objects and the background, the converted constant, and the bistatic angle. Based on the theoretical results, it can be explained that imaging performance is strongly dependent on both the converted constant and the bistatic angle. The conversion of the zero constant ensures good imaging results; however, setting the bistatic angle to
180\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolDegree}

Indicator Function of the DSM with a Single Source

3.1
Introduction to the Indicator Function

Here, we introduce the indicator function of the DSM with a single source. For a fixed transmitter
\mathcal{P}_{m}
, we generate an arrangement of measurement data such that

\mathbb{S}(m)=\begin{bmatrix}u_{\scat}(\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{m})\\
u_{\scat}(\mathbf{q}_{2},\mathbf{p}_{m})\\
\vdots\\
u_{\scat}(\mathbf{q}_{N},\mathbf{p}_{m})\end{bmatrix}.

(3)

By applying the mean-value theorem to (

u_{\scat}(\mathbf{q}_{n},\mathbf{p}_{m})\approx k^{2}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{s}-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}\mu_{\mathrm{b}}}\right)\area(D_{s})G(\mathbf{p}_{m},\mathbf{r}_{s})G(\mathbf{q}_{n},\mathbf{r}_{s}).

(4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}(m)

\displaystyle\approx k^{2}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{s}-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}\mu_{\mathrm{b}}}\right)\area(D_{s})G(\mathbf{p}_{m},\mathbf{r}_{s})\begin{bmatrix}G(\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{s})\\
G(\mathbf{q}_{2},\mathbf{r}_{s})\\
\vdots\\
G(\mathbf{q}_{N},\mathbf{r}_{s})\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle:=k^{2}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{s}-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}\mu_{\mathrm{b}}}\right)\area(D_{s})G(\mathbf{p}_{m},\mathbf{r}_{s})\mathbb{G}(\mathbf{r}_{s}).

Throughout the numerical simulation results discussed in Section

and the value of

Theorem 3.1
.

Here, assume that
4k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{q}_{n}|\gg 1
for all
\mathbf{r}\in\Omega

\mathfrak{F}_{\dsm}(\mathbf{r},m,C)\approx\frac{|\Phi(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})+\Psi(\mathbf{r})|}{\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{r}\in\Omega}|\Phi(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})+\Psi(\mathbf{r})|},

where

-\frac{C(1+i)\#[\mathcal{J}(m)]e^{-ikQ}}{4\sqrt{kQ\pi}}\Big(J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}|)+\mathcal{E}_{2}(\mathbf{r},m)\Big).

(13)

Finally, the structure given in (
9
) can be obtained by applying Hölder’s inequality as follows

\langle\mathbb{S}(C,m),\mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{r})\rangle_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}\leq\|\mathbb{S}(C,m)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}\|\mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{r})\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}.

∎

3.3
Properties of Indicator Function with a Single Source

Following the result in Theorem
3.1
, here, we discuss some properties of the indicator function with a single source.

Discussion 3.1

(Composition of Indicator Function)
.

Since
J_{0}(0)=1
and
J_{p}(0)=0
for
p\neq 0
, the factor
J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|)
contributes to the object detection because
J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|)=1
when
\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\in D
. However, due to the factor
G(\mathbf{p}_{m},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})
, the imaging performance is strongly dependent on the location of the transmitter. In contrast, the factor
\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},m)
disturbs the object detection because
\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},m)=0
when
\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\in D
and generates several artifacts based on the oscillation property of the Bessel function. In addition, due to the factor
J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}|)
, a peak of large magnitude will appear at the origin, which disturbs the object detection (unless the object is located at the origin). Finally, the factor
\mathcal{E}_{2}(\mathbf{r},m)
generates several artifacts and does not contribute to the object detection.

Discussion 3.2

(Influence of Bistatic Angle)
.

Based on the identified structure (
9
), we can observe that the imaging performance of
\mathfrak{F}_{\dsm}(\mathbf{r},m,C)
is strongly dependent on
\alpha
. If
\alpha
is sufficiently small such that
\alpha\longrightarrow 0+
, then since
\#[\mathcal{J}(m)]\longrightarrow 0+
and
\sinc(p\pi)=0
for any integer
p
,

\lim_{\alpha\to 0+}\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},m)=2\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}i^{p}J_{p}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|)\cos\big(p(\vartheta_{m}-\phi)\big)\bigg(\lim_{\alpha\to 0+}\sinc\big(p(\pi-\alpha)\big)\bigg)=0,

the factor
\Phi(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})
becomes

\Phi(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})=\frac{k^{2}\#[\mathcal{I}(m)]}{2\sqrt{kQ\pi}}\int_{D}\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})G(\mathbf{p}_{m},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}.

Thus, a good imaging result is expected when
\alpha
is small because the disturbing factors are eliminated. Otherwise, if
\alpha\longrightarrow\pi-
, then since

\lim_{\alpha\to\pi-}\sinc\big(p(\pi-\alpha)\big)=1,

based on the uniform convergence of the Jacobi–Anger expansion formula (refer to the literature
[
13
]
for additional details)

e^{ix\cos\vartheta}=J_{0}(x)+2\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}i^{p}J_{p}(x)\cos(p\vartheta),

(14)

we obtain

J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|)+2\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}i^{p}J_{p}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|)\cos\big(p(\vartheta_{m}-\phi)\big)=e^{ik|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|\cos(\vartheta_{m}-\phi)}=e^{ik\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{m}\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}

and correspondingly, the factor
\Phi(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})
of (
9
) becomes

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{k^{2}\#[\mathcal{I}(m)]}{2\sqrt{kQ\pi}}\int_{D}\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})G(\mathbf{p}_{m},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})e^{ik\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{m}\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}.

In addition, since
|e^{ik\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{m}\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}|\equiv 1
for every
\mathbf{r}\in\Omega
, the factor
\Phi(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})
no longer contributes to identifying the objects
D
. Consequently, it is impossible to identify the objects through the map
\mathfrak{F}_{\dsm}(\mathbf{r},m,C)
when
\alpha
is close to
\pi
.

Discussion 3.3

(Influence of Converted Constant)
.

Here, we assume that
0<\alpha<\pi
and
\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r}_{s}\in D_{s}
for some
s
. Then, based on Discussion
3.1
,

\Phi(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})=\frac{k^{2}\#[\mathcal{I}(m)]}{2\sqrt{kQ\pi}}\int_{D_{s}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{s}-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}\mu_{\mathrm{b}}}\right)G(\mathbf{p}_{m},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}-C(1+i)\#[\mathcal{J}(m)]\Big(J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}|)+\mathcal{E}_{2}(\mathbf{r},m)\Big).

Thus, if
C
satisfies

|C|\ll\frac{k^{2}\#[\mathcal{I}(m)]}{2\#[\mathcal{J}(m)]\sqrt{2kQ\pi}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{s}-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}\mu_{\mathrm{b}}}\right)\frac{G(\mathbf{p}_{m},\mathbf{r}_{s})\area(D_{s})}{J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}_{s}|)+\mathcal{E}_{2}(\mathbf{r}_{s},m)},

then the factor
|\Psi(\mathbf{r})|
is dominated by
|\Phi(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})|
. As a result, it is possible to recognize
D_{s}
through the map of
\mathfrak{F}_{\dsm}(\mathbf{r},m,C)
. Otherwise, if
C
satisfies the following relation for all
s=1,2,\ldots,S
:

|C|\gg\frac{k^{2}\#[\mathcal{I}(m)]}{2\#[\mathcal{J}(m)]\sqrt{2kQ\pi}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{s}-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}\mu_{\mathrm{b}}}\right)\frac{G(\mathbf{p}_{m},\mathbf{r}_{s})\area(D_{s})}{J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}_{s}|)+\mathcal{E}_{2}(\mathbf{r}_{s},m)},

then the factor
|\Phi(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})|
is dominated by
|\Psi(\mathbf{r})|
. This means that the map of
\mathfrak{F}_{\dsm}(\mathbf{r},m,C)
will only contain a peak of large magnitude at the origin. Thus, it will be very difficult to recognize the existence, location, and shape of the objects.

Based on the above observations, the selection of
C
to identify
D_{s}
is highly dependent on the
\varepsilon_{s}
values, the size of
D_{s}
,
k
, and the total number of transmitting and receiving antennas.

Discussion 3.4

(Best Choice for the Constant)
.

Based on both Discussions
3.2
and
3.3
,
C=0
and
\alpha\longrightarrow 0
will guarantee good imaging results. However, the setting
\alpha\longrightarrow 0
is impossible; thus,
C=0
is the best choice for a proper application of the designed DSM, which is the theoretical rationale for converting unmeasurable data to zero in previous studies.

4
Indicator Function of DSM with Multiple Sources

Further improvement of the indicator with single source is required because the imaging performance is strongly dependent on the location of the transmitter (refer to Discussion
3.1
) and the selection of the constant
C
, which is in turn highly dependent on the given problem at hand (e.g., the material properties of unknown objects; refer to Discussion
3.3
). Thus, in the following, we introduce an indicator function with multiple sources.

4.1
Introduction to the Indicator Function

For each transmitter
\mathcal{P}_{m}
,
m=1,2,\ldots,M
, we generate an arrangement such that

\mathbb{M}(C)=\begin{bmatrix}\langle\mathbb{M}(C,1),\mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{r})\rangle_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}\\
\langle\mathbb{M}(C,2),\mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{r})\rangle_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}\\
\vdots\\
\langle\mathbb{S}(C,M),\mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{r})\rangle_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}\end{bmatrix}.

Here, if
C=0
, since
\#[\mathcal{I}(m)]
are identical, then based on the structure (
13
),

\langle\mathbb{M}(0,m),\mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{r})\rangle_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{Q})}=\frac{ik^{2}\#[\mathcal{I}(m)]}{8kQ\pi}\int_{D}\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})G(\mathbf{p}_{m},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\Big(J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|)+\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},m)\Big)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}^{\prime},

the arrangement
\mathbb{M}(C)
can be written as

\mathbb{M}(C)=\frac{ik^{2}\#[\mathcal{I}(m)]}{8kQ\pi}\begin{bmatrix}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\int_{D}\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})G(\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\Big(J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|)+\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},m)\Big)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\\
\displaystyle\int_{D}\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})G(\mathbf{p}_{2},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\Big(J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|)+\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},m)\Big)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\\
\vdots\\
\displaystyle\int_{D}\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})G(\mathbf{p}_{M},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\Big(J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|)+\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime},m)\Big)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}.

Based on the above, the factor
G(\mathbf{p}_{m},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|)
contains information of
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\in D
; therefore, similar to the development of the indicator function with a single source, we generate a test vector corresponding to the transmitters. Here, for each search point
\mathbf{r}\in\Omega
,

\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{r})=\begin{bmatrix}G(\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{r})\\
G(\mathbf{p}_{2},\mathbf{r})\\
\vdots\\
G(\mathbf{p}_{M},\mathbf{r})\end{bmatrix}.

(15)

Then, to test the orthogonality property of the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}
, we define

\langle\mathbb{M}(C),\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{r})\rangle_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{P})}:=\mathbb{M}(C)\cdot\overline{\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{r})}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{P})}=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{P})}^{1/2},

and the following indicator function of the DSM with multiple sources can be introduced. Here, for each
\mathbf{r}\in\Omega
and fixed constant
C
, we obtain

\mathfrak{F}_{\msm}(\mathbf{r},C)=\frac{|\langle\mathbb{M}(C),\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{r})\rangle_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{P})}|}{\|\mathbb{M}(C)\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{P})}\|\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{r})\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{P})}}.

(16)

4.2
Mathematical Structure of the Indicator Function

Based on numerical simulation results discussed in Section
5
, we can say that
\mathfrak{F}_{\msm}(\mathbf{r},C)
with
C=0
improves the imaging performance. To support this fact theoretically, we investigate the mathematical structure of the
\mathfrak{F}_{\msm}(\mathbf{r},C)
as follows.

Theorem 4.1
.

Here, we assume that
4k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{m}|\gg 1
and
4k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{q}_{n}|\gg 1
for all
\mathbf{r}\in\Omega
,
m=1,2,\ldots,M
, and
n=1,2,\ldots,N
. Then,
\mathfrak{F}_{\msm}(\mathbf{r},C)
can be expressed as follows:

\mathfrak{F}_{\msm}(\mathbf{r},C)\approx\frac{|\Lambda(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})+\Gamma(\mathbf{r})|}{\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{r}\in\Omega}|\Lambda(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})+\Gamma(\mathbf{r})|},

(17)

where

\Lambda(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})=\frac{\#[\mathcal{I}(m)]}{8\sqrt{PQ}\pi}\int_{D}\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\bigg(J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|)^{2}+2\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{p}J_{p}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|)^{2}\sinc\big(p(\pi-\alpha)\big)\bigg)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}

and

\Gamma(\mathbf{r})=\frac{C\#[\mathcal{J}(m)]}{k}\left(J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}|)^{2}+2\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{q}J_{q}(k|\mathbf{r}|)^{2}\sinc(q\alpha)\right).

Proof.

Based on (
11
), (

), we obtain

&-\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{iC\#[\mathcal{J}(m)]e^{-ik(P+Q)}}{8k\pi\sqrt{PQ}}e^{ik\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\Big(J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}|)+\mathcal{E}_{2}(\mathbf{r},m)\Big).\end{aligned}

(18)

), we immediately obtain

&=\frac{M}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|)+2\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}i^{p}J_{p}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|)\cos\big(p(\vartheta-\phi)\big)\right)\cos\big(q(\vartheta-\phi)\big)\mathrm{d}\vartheta\\

(20)

and

\sum_{m=1}^{M}e^{ik\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{r}}J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}|)\approx MJ_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}|)^{2}.

(21)

Similar to the derivation in (

(22)

Thus, plugging (

)–(

) into (

\displaystyle\langle\mathbb{M}

\displaystyle(C),\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{r})\rangle_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{P})}

\displaystyle-\frac{M\#[\mathcal{I}(m)]}{64PQ\pi^{2}}\int_{D}\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\left(J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|)^{2}+2\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{p}J_{p}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|)^{2}\sinc\big(p(\pi-\alpha)\big)\right)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}

\displaystyle-\frac{iCM\#[\mathcal{J}(m)]e^{-ik(P+Q)}}{8k\pi\sqrt{PQ}}\left(J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}|)^{2}+2\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{q}J_{q}(k|\mathbf{r}|)^{2}\sinc(q\alpha)\right).

6
Concluding Remark

In this paper, we have considered the application of the DSM to realize fast identification of small dielectric objects from a limited-aperture bistatic measurement dataset. To this end, indicator functions with single and multiple sources were designed by converting the unknown measurement data into a fixed constant. In addition, to explain the applicability of the indicator functions, as well as the influence of the converted constant and bistatic angle, we demonstrated that the indicator functions can be expressed by an infinite series of the Bessel functions of integer order, the material properties, and the applied constant and bistatic angle. Based on the theoretical results, we conclude that converting unknown measurement data into a zero constant and a small bistatic angle ensures good results. The results of various numerical simulations conducted on a 2D Fresnel dataset were presented to support these theoretical results.

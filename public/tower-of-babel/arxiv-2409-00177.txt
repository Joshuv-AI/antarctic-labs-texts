Farid Aliniaeifard, Stephanie van Willigenburg

Abstract
. We answer a question of Bergeron, Hohlweg, Rosas, and Zabrocki from 2006 to give a combinatorial description for the coproduct of the x \displaystyle{x} -basis in the Hopf algebra of symmetric functions in noncommuting variables, NCSym \displaystyle\operatorname{NCSym} , which arises in the theory of Grothendieck bialgebras. We achieve this using the theory of Hopf monoids and the Fock functor. We also determine combinatorial expansions of this basis in terms of the monomial and power sum symmetric functions in NCSym \displaystyle\operatorname{NCSym} , and by taking the commutative image of the x \displaystyle{x} -basis we discover a new multiplicative basis for the algebra of symmetric functions.

1.
Introduction

Symmetric functions in noncommuting variables,
\displaystyle\operatorname{NCSym}
, were introduced in 1936 by Wolf
[
9
]
who produced a noncommutative analogue of the fundamental theorem of symmetric functions. Her work was generalized in 1969 by Bergman and Cohn
[
5
]
, but there were few further discoveries until 2004 when Rosas and Sagan
[
8
]
substantially advanced the area, discovering numerous noncommutative analogues of classical concepts in symmetric function theory, such as the RSK map and the
\displaystyle\omega
involution. In particular, they gave noncommutative analogues of the bases of monomial, power sum, elementary, and complete homogeneous symmetric functions, each analogue mapping to its (scaled) symmetric function analogue under the projection map that lets the variables commute.

Rosas and Sagan did define a partial set of Schur functions in noncommuting variables, which projected to scaled classical Schur functions. However, they were not a basis for NCSym since they were indexed by integer
partitions
rather than
by
set partitions. This led them to ask in
[
8
, Section 9]
whether there is a way to define functions indexed by set partitions having properties analogous to the classical Schur functions. In 2022, a family of Schur functions in noncommuting variables was introduced in
[
2
]
and Rosas and Sagan’s question was answered. In an earlier attempt to answer Rosas and Sagan’s question, in 2006, Bergeron, Hohlweg, Rosas, and Zabrocki
[
4
]
suggested a possible candidate, the
\displaystyle{x}
-basis, which corresponds to the classes of simple modules of the Grothedieck bialgebra of the semi-tower of partition lattice algebras. They also gave the product and internal coproduct of the
\displaystyle x
-basis; however, the coproduct formula for the
\displaystyle x
-basis remained open, and in the last sentence of their paper, they
stated that
“It would be interesting to find a formula for
\displaystyle\Delta(x_{\pi})
”, where
\displaystyle\Delta
denotes the coproduct
and
\displaystyle\pi
is a set partition of
\displaystyle[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. The
\displaystyle x
-basis has been the subject of other studies; for example,
the expansion of the chromatic symmetric functions in
\displaystyle\operatorname{NCSym}
in terms of the
\displaystyle x
-basis was studied in
[
6
]
.

In this paper, we delve further into the study of the
\displaystyle x
-basis of
\displaystyle\operatorname{NCSym}
and its analogue in the Hopf monoid of set partitions,
the

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}
-basis. We answer the question
of
Bergeron, Hohlweg, Rosas, and Zabrocki from 2006 and find a combinatorial formula for the coproduct of the
\displaystyle{x}
-basis of
\displaystyle\operatorname{NCSym}
. Also, we describe the
expansion
of the
\displaystyle{x}
-basis elements in terms of other bases of
\displaystyle\operatorname{NCSym}
.
In particular,
for every set partition
\displaystyle\pi
of
\displaystyle[n]
, the coefficients in the expansion of
\displaystyle{x}_{\pi}
in terms of the
\displaystyle{m}
-basis can be described by acyclic
orientations
with one sink of certain complete multipartite graphs.

More precisely, our paper is structured as follows.
In Section
2
, we present the Hopf algebras of symmetric functions
\displaystyle\operatorname{Sym}
and symmetric functions in noncommuting variables
\displaystyle\operatorname{NCSym}
, then we describe the Hopf monoid of set partitions
\displaystyle{\bf\Pi}
. Moreover, we show how the Hopf monoid of set partitions is related to the Hopf algebra of symmetric functions in noncommuting variables via the Fock functor. In Section
3
, given a set partition
\displaystyle A
of a finite set
\displaystyle S
, we obtain a coproduct formula for
\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{A}
in
\displaystyle{\bf\Pi}
in Theorem
3.1
. Then, in Corollary
3.3
, we apply the Fock functor on
\displaystyle{\mathbf{\Pi}}
to find a coproduct formula for
\displaystyle{x}_{\pi}
in
\displaystyle\operatorname{NCSym}
for every set partition
\displaystyle\pi
of
\displaystyle[n]
. Also, by applying
\displaystyle\rho
, the algebra morphism that lets the variables commute,
to
the
\displaystyle x
-basis elements of
\displaystyle\operatorname{NCSym}
, in Proposition
3.5

\displaystyle x
-basis of
\displaystyle\operatorname{Sym}

4

\displaystyle{x}_{\pi}

\displaystyle\operatorname{NCSym}
,
in particular,
we show in Theorem

that the coefficients appearing in the expansion of

all

Given

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle S_{i}

2.4.
Hopf monoids

A
vector species
is a functor from the category of finite sets with bijections as morphisms,
\displaystyle{\mathrm{Set}}^{\times}
, to the category of vector spaces over
\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}
with linear transformations as morphisms,
\displaystyle{\mathrm{Vect}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Thus, any vector species
\displaystyle{\mathbf{P}}
takes a finite set
\displaystyle I
and gives a vector space
\displaystyle{\mathbf{P}}[I]
. Moreover, the value of the vector species
\displaystyle{\mathbf{P}}
at the bijection
\displaystyle f:S\rightarrow T
is denoted by

{\mathbf{P}}[f]={\mathbf{P}}[S]\rightarrow{\mathbf{P}}[T].

For complete details about Hopf monoids, see
[
1
]
.

Example 2.11
.

The
vector species of set partitions
is
\displaystyle{\mathbf{\Pi}}:{\mathrm{Set}}^{\times}\rightarrow{\mathrm{Vect}}_{\mathbb{Q}},
where

{\mathbf{\Pi}}[S]=\mathbb{Q}\text{-span}\{\mathbf{m}_{A}:A\vdash S\},

and if
\displaystyle f:S\rightarrow T
is a bijection, then

{\mathbf{\Pi}}[f]:{\mathbf{\Pi}}[S]\rightarrow{\mathbf{\Pi}}[T]

is defined by the linear extension of the map,

{\mathbf{\Pi}}[f](\mathbf{m}_{A})=\mathbf{m}_{f(A)},

where
\displaystyle f(A)
is the set partition of
\displaystyle T
obtained by replacing
\displaystyle i

with

\displaystyle f(i)
in
\displaystyle A
.
For example,
\displaystyle{\mathbf{\Pi}}[{\mathrm{St}}](\mathbf{m}_{16/38})=\mathbf{m}_{{\mathrm{St}}(16/38)}=\mathbf{m}_{13/24}
.

Example 2.12
.

Let
\displaystyle V
be a vector space. Define the vector species

{\mathbf{1}}_{V}:{\mathrm{Set}}^{\times}\rightarrow{\mathrm{Vect}}_{\mathbb{Q}},

where

{\mathbf{1}}_{V}[S]=\begin{cases}V&S=\emptyset,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

A
natural transformation

\displaystyle\alpha
between functors

\displaystyle I\in{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle\alpha_{I}:F[I]\rightarrow G[I]
of
\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}
such that for any morphism
\displaystyle f:I\rightarrow J
of
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}
the following diagram

\displaystyle F[I]
\displaystyle G[I]
\displaystyle F[J]
\displaystyle G[J]
\displaystyle\alpha_{I}
\displaystyle\alpha_{J}
\displaystyle F[f]
\displaystyle G[f]

commutes.

The
Cauchy product
of the vector species
\displaystyle{\mathbf{P}}
and
\displaystyle{\mathbf{Q}}
is the vector species

{\mathbf{P}}\cdot{\mathbf{Q}}:{\mathrm{Set}}^{\times}\rightarrow{\mathrm{Vect}}_{\mathbb{Q}},

and if

\begin{array}[]{cccc}({\mathbf{P}}\cdot{\mathbf{Q}})[f]:&\bigoplus_{S=S_{1}\sqcup S_{2}}{\mathbf{P}}[S_{1}]\otimes{\mathbf{Q}}[S_{2}]&\rightarrow&\bigoplus_{T=T_{1}\sqcup T_{2}}{\mathbf{P}}[T_{1}]\otimes{\mathbf{Q}}[T_{2}]\end{array}

{\mathbf{P}}[S_{1}]\otimes{\mathbf{Q}}[S_{2}]\xrightarrow{{\mathbf{P}}[f|_{S_{1}}]\otimes{\mathbf{Q}}[f|_{S_{2}}]}{\mathbf{P}}[f(S_{1})]\otimes{\mathbf{Q}}[f(S_{2})]

with
\displaystyle S=S_{1}\sqcup S_{2}
.

2.6.
The Fock functor

We have a category
\displaystyle{\mathrm{Sp}}
where its objects are vector species, and its morphisms are natural transformations. The
Fock functor
is

{\mathcal{K}}:{\mathrm{Sp}}\rightarrow{\mathrm{Vect}}_{\mathbb{Q}}

defined by

{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{P}})=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}{\mathbf{P}}([n]).

When
\displaystyle{\mathbf{P}}
is a Hopf monoid,
\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{P}})
is a Hopf algebra with the following product and coproduct. Let
\displaystyle v\in{\mathbf{P}}([n])
and
\displaystyle w\in{\mathbf{P}}([m])
. We have an order preserving bijection
\displaystyle{\rm shift}
between
\displaystyle[m]
and
\displaystyle[n+1,m+n]=\{n+1,n+2,\dots,n+m\}
.
The product of
\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{P}})
is defined as follows,

(2.9)

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mu:&{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{P}})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{P}})&\rightarrow&{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{P}})\\
&v\otimes w&\mapsto&\mu_{[n],[n+1,m+n]}(v\otimes{\mathbf{P}}[{\rm shift}](w)).\end{array}

The coproduct of
\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{P}})
is defined as follows,

(2.10)

\scriptstyle{\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Delta:&{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{P}})&\rightarrow&{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{P}})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{P}})\\
&v&\mapsto&\sum_{[n]=S_{1}\sqcup S_{2}}{\mathbf{P}}[{\mathrm{St}}]\otimes{\mathbf{P}}[{\mathrm{St}}](\Delta_{S_{1},S_{2}}(v)).\end{array}}

As an example, consider
\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{\Pi}})
. We have

{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{\Pi}})={\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{\Pi}})^{0}\oplus{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{\Pi}})^{1}\oplus\cdots

where
\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{\Pi}})^{0}=\operatorname{span}\{1\}
and the
\displaystyle n
th graded piece for
\displaystyle n\geq 1
has the following bases, which respectively are called the
\displaystyle n
th graded piece of the
\displaystyle\mathbf{m}
-,
\displaystyle\mathbf{p}
-, and
\displaystyle\mathbf{x}
-basis of
\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{\Pi}})
,

\begin{array}[]{rclclcc}{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{\Pi}})^{n}&=&\mathbb{Q}\text{-span}\{\mathbf{m}_{\pi}\,:\,\pi\vdash[n]\}&=&\mathbb{Q}\text{-span}\{\mathbf{p}_{\pi}\,:\,\pi\vdash[n]\}&=&\mathbb{Q}\text{-span}\{\mathbf{x}_{\pi}\,:\,\pi\vdash[n]\}.\end{array}

Given two set partitions
\displaystyle\sigma\vdash[n]
and
\displaystyle\tau\vdash[m]
, we have

(2.11)

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mu:&{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{\Pi}})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{\Pi}})&\rightarrow&{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{\Pi}})\\
&\mathbf{p}_{\sigma}\otimes\mathbf{p}_{\tau}&\mapsto&\mu_{[n],[n+1,m+n]}(\mathbf{p}_{\sigma}\otimes{\mathbf{\Pi}}[{\rm shift}](\mathbf{p}_{\tau}))=\mathbf{p}_{\sigma|\tau}.\end{array}

Also, for
\displaystyle\pi\vdash[n]
,

(2.12)

\scriptstyle{\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Delta:&{\mathcal{K}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}})&\rightarrow&{\mathcal{K}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}})\otimes{\mathcal{K}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{{\mathbf{\Pi}}}})\\
&\mathbf{p}_{\pi}&\mapsto&\sum_{[n]=S_{1}\sqcup S_{2}}{\mathbf{P}}[{\mathrm{St}}]\otimes{\mathbf{P}}[{\mathrm{St}}](\Delta_{S_{1},S_{2}}(\mathbf{p}_{\pi}))=\sum_{[n]=S_{1}\sqcup S_{2}:\atop{\pi=\pi|_{S_{1}}\sqcup\pi|_{S_{2}}}}\mathbf{p}_{{\mathrm{St}}(\pi|_{S_{1}})}\otimes\mathbf{p}_{{\mathrm{St}}(\pi|_{S_{2}})}.\end{array}}

Therefore, by Equations (
2.4
) and
\displaystyle(\ref{eq:coprodNCSym})
, we have that there is an isomorphism from
\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{\Pi}})
to
\displaystyle\operatorname{NCSym}
where through this isomorphism, for every
\displaystyle\pi\vdash[n]
,
\displaystyle\mathbf{m}_{\pi}
,
\displaystyle\mathbf{p}_{\pi}
, and
\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{\pi}
in
\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbf{\Pi}})
are mapped to
\displaystyle{m}_{\pi}
,
\displaystyle{p}_{\pi}
, and
\displaystyle{x}_{\pi}
in
\displaystyle\operatorname{NCSym}
, respectively.

3.
The coproduct formula for
the

\displaystyle{x}
-basis of
\displaystyle\operatorname{NCSym}

In the following theorem, we give our product and coproduct formulas for
the

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}
-basis of the Hopf monoid of set partitions
\displaystyle{\mathbf{\Pi}}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

We have the following.

(1)

Let
\displaystyle S=S_{1}\sqcup S_{2}
,
\displaystyle A\vdash S_{1}
, and
\displaystyle B\vdash S_{2}
. Then

\mu_{S_{1},S_{2}}(\mathbf{x}_{A}\otimes\mathbf{x}_{B})=\mathbf{x}_{A\sqcup B}.

(2)

Let
\displaystyle A\vdash S
. Then

\Delta_{S_{1},S_{2}}(\mathbf{x}_{A})=\sum_{B\vdash S_{1},C\vdash S_{2}}c_{B,C}\mathbf{x}_{B}\otimes\mathbf{x}_{C},

where

c_{B,C}=\sum_{D\vdash S:\atop{D=D|_{S_{1}}\sqcup D|_{S_{2}}\leq A,\atop B\leq D|_{S_{1}},C\leq D|_{S_{2}}}}{u}(D,A).

Proof.

(1) Using the same argument as

[
4
, Lemma 4.2]
, we have

\begin{split}\mu_{S_{1},S_{2}}(\mathbf{x}_{A}\otimes\mathbf{x}_{B})&=\sum_{C\leq A}\sum_{D\leq B}{u}(C,A){u}(D,B)\mu_{S_{1},S_{2}}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathbf{p}_{C}}}\otimes\mathbf{p}_{D})\\
&=\sum_{C\leq A}\sum_{D\leq B}{u}(C,A){u}(D,B){\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathbf{p}_{C\sqcup D}}}\\
&=\sum_{E\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{A\sqcup B}}}{{u}}(E,A\sqcup B)\mathbf{p}_{E}=\mathbf{x}_{A\sqcup B}.\end{split}

(2) For
the
coproduct formula,
we have that

\begin{split}\Delta_{S_{1},S_{2}}(\mathbf{x}_{A})&=\Delta_{S_{1},S_{2}}\left(\sum_{D\leq A}{u}(D,A)\mathbf{p}_{D}\right)\\
&=\sum_{D\leq A}{u}(D,A)\Delta_{S_{1},S_{2}}\left(\mathbf{p}_{D}\right)\\
&=\sum_{D\leq A:\atop{D=D|_{S_{1}}\sqcup D|_{S_{2}}}}{u}(D,A)\mathbf{p}_{D|_{S_{1}}}\otimes\mathbf{p}_{D|_{S_{2}}}\\
&=\sum_{D\leq A:\atop{D=D|_{S_{1}}\sqcup D|_{S_{2}}}}{u}(D,A)\sum_{{B\leq D|_{S_{1}},C\leq D|_{S_{2}}}}\mathbf{x}_{B}\otimes\mathbf{x}_{C}\\
&=\sum_{D\vdash S:\atop{D=D|_{S_{1}}\sqcup D|_{S_{2}}\leq A,\atop{B\leq D|_{S_{1}},C\leq D|_{S_{2}}}}}{u}(D,A)\mathbf{x}_{B}\otimes\mathbf{x}_{C},\end{split}

where the third equality is by Equation (
2.8
).
∎

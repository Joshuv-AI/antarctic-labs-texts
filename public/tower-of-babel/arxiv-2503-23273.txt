Improved algorithms for single machine serial-batch scheduling to minimize makespan and maximum cost
Shuguang Li, Zhenxin Wen, Jing Wei

Abstract
This paper studies the bicriteria problem of scheduling n n jobs on a serial-batch machine to minimize makespan and maximum cost simultaneously. A serial-batch machine can process up to b b jobs as a batch, where b b is known as the batch capacity. When a new batch starts, a constant setup time is required for the machine. Within each batch, the jobs are processed sequentially, and thus the processing time of a batch equals the sum of the processing times of its jobs. All the jobs in a batch have the same completion time, namely, the completion time of the batch. The main result is an O ⁡ ( n 3 ) O(n^{3}) -time algorithm which can generate all Pareto optimal points for the bounded model ( b < n b<n ) without precedence relation. The algorithm can be modified to solve the unbounded model ( b ≥ n b\geq n ) with strict precedence relation in O ⁡ ( n 3 ) O(n^{3}) time as well. The results improve the previously best known running time of O ⁡ ( n 4 ) O(n^{4}) for both the bounded and unbounded models.

Introduction

Let a set of
n

\mathcal{J}=\{{{J}_{1}},{{J}_{2}},\ldots,{{J}_{n}}\}
and a serial-batch machine be given. Each job

becomes available for processing at time zero, and has a processing time
{{p}_{j}}
and a due date

which indicates a preferred completion time. The jobs are processed in batches on the serial-batch machine. When a new batch starts, a constant setup time

The serial-batch machine can process up to
b

b<n

A schedule specifies a partition of the jobs into batches and the start times of the batches. The order of jobs within a batch is immaterial due to the definition of the job completion times. For a given schedule
\sigma
, let
{{C}_{j}}(\sigma)
and
{{L}_{j}}(\sigma)={{C}_{j}}(\sigma)-{{d}_{j}}
denote the completion time and lateness of job

in
\sigma

The maximum lateness can be generalized to maximum cost

{{f}_{j}}({{C}_{j}}(\sigma))
is the cost associated with job
{{J}_{j}}
. It is assumed that all the cost functions are regular (non-decreasing in the completion times of the jobs), and the values of

In addition, there may be a precedence relation imposed on the jobs. Two types of precedence relation are involved: (1) strict precedence relation (

, then

in a feasible schedule

In this paper, we study the problems of scheduling jobs, without or with precedence relations, on a serial-batch machine so as to simultaneously optimize makespan

and maximum cost

(

and

is called a Pareto optimal point. Following the notation scheme of

1|s-batch,b<n|({{C}_{\max}},{{f}_{\max}})
,
1|\preceq,s-batch,b\geq n|({{C}_{\max}},{{f}_{\max}})
and
1|\prec,s-batch,b\geq n|({{C}_{\max}},{{f}_{\max}})
, where “

” stands for the serial-batch scheduling, “

” (“

”) means that the batch capacity is bounded (unbounded), “
\preceq
” (“

”) stands for the weak (strict) precedence relation, and the objective “
({{C}_{\max}},{{f}_{\max}})
” represents the simultaneous optimization for criteria
{{C}_{\max}}
and
{{f}_{\max}}
. Note that if jobs have different release dates, then both
1|{{r}_{j}}|{{L}_{\max}}
(
b=1
, minimizing maximum lateness) and
1|s-batch,b\geq n,{{r}_{j}}|{{L}_{\max}}
(without precedence relation, minimizing maximum lateness) are NP-hard problems
(
Brucker,, 2007
;
Lenstra et al.,, 1977
)
.

The main contribution of this paper is an
O({{n}^{3}})
-time algorithm for
1|s-batch,b<n|({{C}_{\max}},{{f}_{\max}})
which can generate all Pareto optimal points and find a corresponding schedule for each Pareto optimal point. The algorithm can be modified to solve
1|\prec,s-batch,b\geq n|({{C}_{\max}},{{f}_{\max}})
in
O({{n}^{3}})
time as well. The two problems have the previously best known running time of
O({{n}^{4}})

(
Geng et al.,, 2018
)
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, the previous related studies are reviewed. In Section 3, an
O({{n}^{3}})
-time algorithm for

is presented. In Section 4, an
O({{n}^{3}})
-time algorithm for

;

)
, and the latter problems can be solved in
O({{n}^{14}}\log n)
and

;

He et al., (2008)

-time algorithm for

.

The bounded case

In this section we will present an
O({{n}^{3}})
-time algorithm for
1|s-batch,b<n|({{C}_{\max}},{{f}_{\max}})
. As a by-product, the last schedule constructed by the algorithm is optimal for

For a batch
{{B}_{i}}
, let

denote the processing time of

denote the setup time of batch

, which is

For a feasible schedule, we index its batches left-to-right (earliest-to-latest) as
{{B}_{1}},{{B}_{2}},\ldots,{{B}_{n}}
, where the last

{{B}_{n-l+1}},{{B}_{n-l+2}},\ldots,{{B}_{n}}
are nonempty and form a partition of

, and the first

are empty. Since both criteria

and

are regular, we can consider only the schedules without idle times. Therefore, we have:

Lemma 1
.

In a feasible schedule
\sigma=\left({{B}_{1}},{{B}_{2}},\ldots,{{B}_{n}}\right)
,
S({{B}_{1}})=s({{B}_{1}})
,
S({{B}_{i}})=C({{B}_{i-1}})+s({{B}_{i}})
,

.

The following definition generalizes the idea used in
(
Lazarev et al.,, 2017
)
for
1|{{r}_{j}},{{p}_{j}}=p|({{C}_{\max}},{{L}_{\max}})
(the classical scheduling problem where the machine can process at most one job at a time), which plays a key role in our algorithm.

A family of (possibly empty) sets
F=\{{{\mathcal{J}}_{1}},{{\mathcal{J}}_{2}},\ldots,{{\mathcal{J}}_{n}}\}
is called a Candidate Set Family (CSF for short) if these sets are disjoint, their union is
\mathcal{J}
, and for
i=1,2,\ldots,n
,
{{\mathcal{J}}_{i}}
consists of the jobs which cannot be assigned to any batch whose index is larger than
i
.

The definition captures the idea of maintaining implicitly a position indicator for each job in the algorithm. The initial CSF is
\{\varnothing,\varnothing,\ldots,\mathcal{J}\}
, meaning that the jobs can be assigned to any batches. Let us fix a particular iteration in the algorithm, a batch and a job in this batch. We check an inequality to find whether this job is suitable for the batch. If the inequality does not hold, then we have to move this job from the batch into another one to its left and update the CSF accordingly. We will prove that during the iterations the completion time of any batch will not decrease, which implies that any job can only be moved to the left (a crucial observation in the time complexity analysis). Therefore, when a job is accommodated into component
{{\mathcal{J}}_{i}}
of the current CSF, we know that this job cannot be assigned to any batch whose index is larger than
i
.

Obviously, we require that
\sum\nolimits_{h=1}^{i}{|{{\mathcal{J}}_{h}}|}\leq i\cdot b
,
i=1,2,\ldots,n
. We say that a feasible schedule
\left({{B}_{1}},{{B}_{2}},\ldots,{{B}_{n}}\right)
satisfies a given CSF

if (1) for
i=n,n-1,\ldots,1
, all the jobs in batch
{{B}_{i}}
come from
\bigcup_{h=i}^{n}{{\mathcal{J}}_{h}}
; and (2) batches
{{B}_{k}},{{B}_{k+1}},\ldots,{{B}_{n}}
are all nonempty, where
{{B}_{k}}
is the first (the earliest) nonempty batch in
\left({{B}_{1}},{{B}_{2}},\ldots,{{B}_{n}}\right)
. Equivalently speaking, each job in
{{\mathcal{J}}_{i}}
must be processed in a batch which is earlier than at least
n-i

i=n,n-1,\ldots,1
.

Let

denote the set of all feasible schedules for

. Let
\Pi\left(\mathcal{J},F,y\right)\subseteq\Pi\left(\mathcal{J}\right)

In Step 1 of Algorithm AUX1, we use a Max-heap to store the jobs in

time, produces a Max-heap from an unordered input array; (3) MAX-HEAP-INSERT, running in
O(\log\tau)
time, inserts an element into the Max-heap; (4) MAX-HEAP-EXTRACT, running in
O(\log\tau)
time, gets the maximum element and delete it from the Max-heap.

Step 1 of AUX1 can be implemented in
O(n)
time (BUILD-MAX-HEAP). Step 2 can be implemented in
O(n\log n)
time in each iteration (MAX-HEAPIFY, MAX-HEAP-INSERT and MAX-HEAP-EXTRACT). Steps 3 and 5 require
O(n)
time in each iteration.

In Step 4, each job deletion (can be accomplished via MAX-HEAPIFY) or insertion (MAX-HEAP-INSERT) requires

time. Since there are
n
jobs and each job goes through at most

O({{n}^{3}})

The overall running time of Algorithm AUX1 is
O({{n}^{3}}\log n)
, which is determined by Step 2 for all iterations.

cannot be assigned to a batch whose index is larger than

.

∎

Theorem 4
.

Let

be the schedule obtained at the last iteration of Algorithm AUX1. If

, then
\Pi\left(\mathcal{J},F,y\right)=\varnothing

is a schedule which has minimum makespan among all schedules in

.

Next, we give an improved algorithm for solving the auxiliary problem. The idea is motivated from
(

)
, which solved a parallel batch scheduling problem on parallel machines where jobs have release dates, deadlines, and identical processing times. (In parallel batch scheduling setting, jobs within each batch are processed in parallel so that the processing time of a batch equals the largest processing time of all jobs in the batch.)

Algorithm 2

(Algorithm IMPROAUX1)

Step 1.
Initially, set

. Let

, where

Step 2.
The same as Step 2 of Algorithm AUX1.

Step 3.
The same as Step 3 of Algorithm AUX1.

Step 4.
Adjust
{{F}^{m}}
and
{{\sigma}^{m}}

{{f}_{j}}(C(B_{{}_{i}}^{m}))<y

{{J}_{j}}
in

{{J}_{j}}
from its original set in

from

(some jobs may have been removed from

) becomes empty, then return
\varnothing
. Otherwise, if

\varnothing

moves right-to-left over consecutive batches, starting from

and ending by

denote the current batch. Let

after Step 4, then return
{{\sigma}^{m}}
. Otherwise, let

(

and

have been adjusted already) and then set

.

For

Proof.

At the first iteration of Algorithm MAIN1,

and

{{f}_{\max}}

Without causing confusion, we can re-use some notations and terminologies defined in the preceding section, such as
S({{B}_{i}})
,
C({{B}_{i}})
, Candidate Set Family (CSF)
F=\{{{\mathcal{J}}_{1}},{{\mathcal{J}}_{2}},\ldots,{{\mathcal{J}}_{n}}\}
,
\Pi\left(\mathcal{J},F,y\right)
, to name a few. In addition, let
O(j,F)
denote the ordinal number of job
{{J}_{j}}
in CSF
F

means that
{{J}_{j}}\in{{\mathcal{J}}_{i}}
in

Precedence relation on
\mathcal{J}
can be represented as a graph
G=<V,E>
, where

consists of all the jobs in
\mathcal{J}
, and

consists of all the edges

if
{{J}_{p}}\prec{{J}_{j}}
and there is no
{{J}_{k}}
such that
{{J}_{p}}\prec{{J}_{k}}\prec{{J}_{j}}
. To determine the set of direct predecessors for each job, we purposely use the inverse adjacency list representation of

. For each job
{{J}_{j}}
, its inverse adjacency list contains all the jobs
{{J}_{p}}
such that there is an edge
<{{J}_{p}},{{J}_{j}}>\in E
.

There is an natural initial CSF

We first modify Algorithm AUX1 to solve the following auxiliary problem for the unbounded model with strict precedence relation.

Conclusions

In this paper we investigated the bicriteria problem of scheduling jobs on a serial-batch machine to minimize makespan and maximum cost simultaneously. We improved the earlier results by presenting
O({{n}^{3}})
-time algorithms for the bounded model without precedence relation and the unbounded model with strict precedence relation. For future research, it is interesting to design an algorithm with better time complexity. It is also interesting to improve the algorithms presented in
(
Baptiste,, 2000

for the problem of scheduling jobs with equal processing times and unequal release dates on a serial-batch machine.

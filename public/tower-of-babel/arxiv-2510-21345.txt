α \alpha -LoRA: Effective Fine-Tuning via Base Model Rescaling
Aymane El FirdoussiMLO, EPFLEl Mahdi ChaytiMLO, EPFL, Mohamed El Amine SeddikTII, UAEMartin JaggiMLO, EPFL

Abstract
Fine-tuning has proven to be highly effective in adapting pre-trained models to perform better on new desired tasks with minimal data samples. Among the most widely used approaches are reparameterization methods, which update a target module by augmenting its frozen weight matrix with an additional trainable weight matrix. The most prominent example is Low Rank Adaption (LoRA) ( Hu et al. 2022 ) , which gained significant attention in recent years. In this paper, we introduce a new class of reparameterization methods for transfer learning, designed to enhance the generalization ability of fine-tuned models. We establish the effectiveness of our approach in a high-dimensional binary classification setting using tools from Random Matrix Theory, and further validate our theoretical findings through more realistic experiments, such as fine-tuning LLMs.



1
Introduction

Large foundational models have driven major advances in artificial intelligence across domains such as computer vision and natural language processing. Examples include transformer-based models
(
Vaswani et al. 2017
)
operating in natural language domain
(
Team et al. 2023
;
Grattafiori et al. 2024
)
or vision domain
(
Cordonnier et al. 2020
;
Dosovitskiy et al. 2020
)
. Such models are specifically known for their relatively large size and massive training corpus, which makes them more powerful and adapted for many use cases. However, even with their extensive pre-training, these large models may not excel at some specific tasks without further adjustment.

Fine-tuning addresses this need by updating a pre-trained model with task-specific data. Unlike training from scratch, it leverages general pre-trained representations while reducing data and compute requirements. The most common class of fine-tuning methods is Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), which relies on labeled data in that adaptation process, and one of its most popular lightweight techniques is Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)
(
Hu et al. 2022
)
, which updates the desired module by adding a low-rank perturbation to the original (frozen) weight matrix.

In this paper, we study fine-tuning through the lens of Random Matrix Theory (RMT), where we introduce a theoretical framework to understand and improve transfer learning. Leveraging the theoretical findings, our key practical idea in the context of LoRA is to scale the frozen weights row-wise with a vector
\bm{\alpha}
before adaptation, thereby adding a new degree of freedom to the fine-tuning process. We show that this modification leads to an optimal scaling factor
\bm{\alpha}^{*}
, which is typically different from the standard choice (
\bm{\alpha}=1
). We analyze this framework in a high-dimensional binary classification setting under a Gaussian Mixture Model, proving the existence of such an optimal
\bm{\alpha}^{*}
while providing its closed-form expression in terms of scalar data-dependent quantities. We then validate our theoretical insights on real tasks, including transfer learning benchmarks and large language model fine-tuning.

Summary of contributions.

Our main contributions are summarized as follows:

1.

In the context of adaptation fine-tuning (e.g., LoRA), we propose the scaling of the base model weight matrices by a non-trivial row-wise vector
\bm{\alpha}
.

2.

We theoretically prove the existence of an optimal parameter
\bm{\alpha}^{*}\neq 1
in high-dimensional binary classification and derive its closed form.

3.

We design an algorithm to estimate optimal
\bm{\alpha}
in complex scenarios such as LLM fine-tuning.

Related work

Transfer learning foundations.

Transfer Learning (TL) studies how knowledge acquired in a source task or domain can be reused to improve learning in a related target task. Early surveys
(
Pan & Yang 2009
;
Weiss et al. 2016
)
outlined key settings such as domain adaptation and multitask learning. Most theoretical works established generalization bounds linking transfer success to source error and distributional divergence
Ben-David et al. 2010
, and showed how shared representations reduce sample complexity
(
Maurer et al. 2016
;
Tripuraneni et al. 2020
)
. More recent studies refined these results under classification and regression settings
(
Hanneke & Kpotufe 2024
;
Zhang et al. 2021
;
Klivans et al. 2024
;
Kpotufe & Martinet 2021
;
Cai & Wei 2021
;
Reeve et al. 2021
)
.

Fine-tuning pre-trained models.

With the advent of large-scale pre-training, fine-tuning has become the dominant strategy for transfer learning. The most popular fine-tuning techniques are Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) and fine-tuning with Reinforcement Learning (RL). RL-based approaches such as RLHF
(
Ouyang et al. 2022
)
, DPO
(
Rafailov et al. 2023
)
, GRPO
(
Ramesh et al. 2024
;
Guo et al. 2025
)
and their variants are especially popular for reasoning and mathematics tasks, where they often outperform SFT
(
Shenfeld et al. 2025
)
. In this paper, however, we focus on SFT techniques. SFT extends the training of a pre-trained model using labeled data. Because these models are typically large in size, it is common to modify only a small fraction of their parameters while leaving most unchanged. This strategy, known as Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)
(
Xu et al. 2023
)
, aims to achieve strong performance with minimal parameter updates. PEFT methods are usually grouped into three categories: additive, selective, and reparameterized
(
Ji et al. 2025
)
. Our work centers on the last category.

Reparametrized Fine-tuning.

Reparameterization-based fine-tuning adapts a model by expressing its parameters in an alternative form, commonly through a low-rank decomposition, to reduce training costs, while the full weight matrices are reconstructed for inference. The most common technique in this class is Low Rank Adaptation (LoRA)
(
Hu et al. 2022
)
, which introduces small, trainable matrices operating alongside the pre-trained weights to inject task-specific updates without burdening the inference process. Many extensions were proposed to enhance the efficiency of LoRA by either acting on the initialization of the low rank modules
(
Hayou et al. 2024a
)
, their learning rates
(
Hayou et al. 2024b
)
, normalizing the updates
(
Liu et al. 2024
)
, setting adaptive ranks
(
Kim et al. 2024
;
Lu et al. 2024
)
, finding optimal placements for LoRA modules
(
Hayou et al. 2025
)
, and more
(
Zhang et al. 2023b
;
Dettmers et al. 2023
;
Kopiczko et al. 2023
;
Zhang et al. 2023a
;
Tian et al. 2024
;
Jiang et al. 2024
)
.

3
Problem setting and Background

To prove the effectiveness of our new family of fine-tuning algorithms, we will theoretically analyze a binary classification setting under a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) using tools from Random Matrix Theory (RMT). Through this analysis, we will prove the existence of an optimal scaling parameter
\alpha^{\star}
and derive its exact theoretical formulation for these settings.

3.1
Theoretical Setting

The goal is to fine-tune a linear classifier, initially pretrained on a dataset called
source
, in order to perform a
target
task given a relatively small target data corpus.

Pre-training phase.

We consider that we are given pairs of pre-training (source) data samples
\{(\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i},\tilde{y}_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
that are distributed, for
\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
with
a\in\{1,2\}
, as follows:

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{a}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\begin{cases}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}={\bm{\mu}}_{a}+\tilde{\bm{z}}_{i},\quad\tilde{\bm{z}}_{i}\sim{\mathcal{N}}({\bm{0}},{\mathbf{I}}_{p}),\\
\tilde{y}_{i}=(-1)^{a}.\end{cases}

(1)

For convenience and without loss of generality, we further assume that
{\bm{\mu}}_{a}=(-1)^{a}{\bm{\mu}}
for some vector
{\bm{\mu}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
. This setting can be recovered by subtracting
\frac{{\bm{\mu}}_{1}+{\bm{\mu}}_{2}}{2}
from each data point, as such
{\bm{\mu}}=\frac{{\bm{\mu}}_{2}-{\bm{\mu}}_{1}}{2}
and therefore the SNR
\|{\bm{\mu}}\|
controls the difficulty of the classification problem, in the sense that large values of
\|{\bm{\mu}}\|
yield a simple classification problem whereas when
\|{\bm{\mu}}\|\to 0
, the classification becomes impossible. Denoting
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}=\left[\tilde{\bm{x}}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{\bm{x}}_{N}\right]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p\times N}
the data matrix and
\tilde{\bm{y}}=\left[\tilde{y}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{y}_{N}\right]^{\top}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
the corresponding labels vector, we have in matrix form

\tilde{\mathbf{X}}={\bm{\mu}}\tilde{\bm{y}}^{\top}+\tilde{\mathbf{Z}},

(2)

where
\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}
is a random matrix with
{\mathcal{N}}(0,1)
i.i.d. entries.

We then consider training a classifier, called
\tilde{\bm{w}}
, on this source dataset by solving:

\min_{{\bm{w}}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ell({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}_{i},y_{i})+\tilde{\gamma}\|{\bm{w}}\|_{2}^{2}

(3)

for some loss function
\ell
and a positive regularization parameter
\tilde{\gamma}
. Taking a generic or a non-intuitive loss, such as the binary cross entropy, leads to
intractable
solution
\tilde{\bm{w}}
. However,
Mai & Liao 2024
show that in the case of a Gaussian mixture data model or more generally a data distribution with finite fourth-order moment, it is possible to optimize such a classifier using the squared (
L^{2}
) loss function, which also gives a closed-form solution to this problem. Thus, taking
\ell(x,y)=(x-y)^{2}
leads to the following optimization problem:

\tilde{\bm{w}}=\argmin_{\bm{v}}\frac{1}{N}\left\|\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\bm{v}}-\tilde{\bm{y}}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\tilde{\gamma}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{2}^{2},

(4)

Which gives us the following solution:

\tilde{\bm{w}}=\frac{1}{N}{\mathbf{R}}\tilde{\mathbf{X}}\tilde{\bm{y}},\quad{\mathbf{R}}=\left(\frac{1}{N}\tilde{\mathbf{X}}\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}+\tilde{\gamma}{\mathbf{I}}_{p}\right)^{-1}

(5)

Fine-tuning phase.

During the fine-tuning phase, we suppose that we are given pairs of target data
\{({\bm{x}}_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
with
y_{i}\in\{-1,1\}
that are distributed such that
{\mathbf{X}}=\left[{\bm{x}}_{1},\ldots,{\bm{x}}_{n}\right]\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p\times n}
is given by:

{\mathbf{X}}={\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}{\bm{y}}^{\top}+{\mathbf{Z}},\quad{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}=\beta{\bm{\mu}}+{\bm{\mu}}^{\perp},

(6)

where
{\mathbf{Z}}
is a random matrix with
{\mathcal{N}}(0,1)
i.i.d. entries,
{\bm{\mu}}^{\perp}
is an orthogonal vector to
{\bm{\mu}}
and the factor
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
quantifies the
alignment
between the source and target data, as we have that:
\langle{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta},{\bm{\mu}}\rangle=\beta\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}
. Leveraging the pre-trained weights
\tilde{\bm{w}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
, we consider the training of adapter weights
{\bm{a}}
as:

{\bm{a}}=\argmin_{\bm{v}}\frac{1}{n}\left\|{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\left(\alpha\tilde{\bm{w}}+{\bm{v}}\right)-{\bm{y}}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\gamma\|{\bm{v}}\|_{2}^{2},

(7)

for a scalar
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
. In fact, classical reparametrization approaches can be modeled by the same setting using
\alpha=1
. Solving the previous minimization problem,
{\bm{a}}
expresses as:

\displaystyle{\bm{a}}=\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{1}{n}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}+\gamma{\mathbf{I}}_{p}\right)^{-1}\left({\mathbf{X}}{\bm{y}}-\alpha{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\tilde{\bm{w}}\right).

(8)

We define the resolvent matrices
{\mathbf{Q}}
and
{\mathbf{R}}
by:

{\mathbf{Q}}=\left(\frac{1}{n}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}+\gamma{\mathbf{I}}_{p}\right)^{-1},\quad{\mathbf{R}}=\left(\frac{1}{N}\tilde{\mathbf{X}}\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}+\tilde{\gamma}{\mathbf{I}}_{p}\right)^{-1},

(9)

Then our obtained fine-tuned classifier
{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}
writes:

{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}=\alpha\tilde{\bm{w}}+{\bm{a}}=\frac{1}{n}{\mathbf{Q}}(\gamma){\mathbf{X}}{\bm{y}}+\alpha\gamma{\mathbf{Q}}\tilde{\bm{w}}

We denote by
{\bm{w}}\equiv{\bm{w}}_{0}
the classifier obtained through learning directly on target data (without fine-tuning), which is given by:

{\bm{w}}=\frac{1}{n}{\mathbf{Q}}(\gamma){\mathbf{X}}{\bm{y}}

(No-FT)

Then we finally get the expression of our
\alpha
-Fine-tuned classifier as follows:

{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}={\bm{w}}+\alpha\gamma{\mathbf{Q}}\tilde{\bm{w}}

(
\alpha
-FTC)

Remark 3.1

(About the interpretability of our fine-tuned classifier)
.

Remark that the parameter
\alpha
introduced in the expression of the fine-tuned classifier
{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}
characterizes the contribution of each training dataset (source and target) to the test performance on the target task. In fact, since the prediction of the class label does not change by multiplying
{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}
by a positive constant, then by taking a positive
\alpha
and for
\rho=\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}\in(0,1)
, the fine-tuned classifier is equivalent to this convex weighted classifier:

{\bm{w}}_{\rho}=\rho\tilde{\bm{w}}+(1-\rho){\bm{a}}

and therefore, this new parameter
\rho
can be interpreted as the percentage of the contribution of the source task to the test performance on the target task.

Remark 3.2

(About the regularization parameter
\gamma
)
.

We remark from the expression of
{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}
in equation

α

-FTC
that the weight decay
\gamma
is essential to have the dependence of
{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}
on
\alpha
. In fact, taking
\gamma\to 0
leads to a fine-tuned classifier of the form:

{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}=({\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top})^{+}{\mathbf{X}}{\bm{y}}

where
({\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top})^{+}
is the Moore-Penrose inverse of the symmetric semi-definite matrix
{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}
. Therefore, the obtained classifier does
not
depend on
\alpha
here, nor on the pre-trained model
\tilde{\bm{w}}
. Additionally, having such a regularization technique is essential in transfer learning since the target dataset is generally much smaller than the pre-training one, and therefore the fine-tuning process can easily lead to overfitting in the absence of a regularization technique.

3.2
RMT Background

To analyze the performance of the fine-tuned classifier
{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}
, we can leverage tools from Random Matrix Theory. In mathematical terms, the understanding of the asymptotic performance of the classifier
{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}
boils down to the characterization of the statistical behavior of the
resolvent matrices

{\mathbf{Q}}(z)
and
{\mathbf{R}}(z)
introduced in equation
9
. In the following, we will recall some important notions and results from random matrix theory, which will be at the heart of our analysis. We start by defining the main object, which is the resolvent matrix.

Definition 3.3

(Resolvent)
.

For a symmetric matrix
{\mathbf{M}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p\times p}
, the resolvent
{\mathbf{Q}}_{M}(z)
of
{\mathbf{M}}
is defined for
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{M}})
as:

{\mathbf{Q}}_{M}(z)=({\mathbf{M}}-z{\mathbf{I}}_{p})^{-1},

where
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{M}})
is the set of eigenvalues or spectrum of
{\mathbf{M}}
.

In fact, the study of the asymptotic performance of
{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}
involves the estimation of linear forms of the resolvents
{\mathbf{Q}}
and
{\mathbf{R}}
in equation
9
, such as
\frac{1}{n}\Tr{\mathbf{Q}}
and
{\bm{a}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{b}}
with
{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
of bounded Euclidean norms. Therefore, the notion of a
deterministic equivalent

(
Hachem et al. 2007
)
is crucial as it allows the design of a
deterministic
matrix, having (in probability or almost surely) asymptotically the same
scalar observations
as the random ones in the sense of
linear forms
.
A rigorous definition is provided below.

Definition 3.4

(Deterministic equivalent
(
Hachem et al. 2007
)
)
.

We say that
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p\times p}
is a deterministic equivalent for the random resolvent matrix
{\mathbf{Q}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p\times p}
if, for any bounded linear form
u:{\mathbb{R}}^{p\times p}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, we have that, as
p\to\infty
:

u({\mathbf{Q}})\asto u(\bar{\mathbf{Q}}),

where the convergence is in the almost sure sense.

In particular, a deterministic equivalent for the resolvents
{\mathbf{Q}}(z)
and
{\mathbf{R}}(z)
defined in equation
9
is given by the following Lemma (the proof is presented in Appendix
A.2
).

Lemma 3.5

(Deterministic equivalent of
{\mathbf{Q}}
and
{\mathbf{R}}
)
.

Under the high-dimensional regime, when
p,n,N\to\infty
with
\frac{p}{n}\to\eta\in(0,\infty)
and
\frac{p}{N}\to\tilde{\eta}\in(0,\infty)
and assuming
\|{\bm{\mu}}\|={\mathcal{O}}(1)
, a deterministic equivalent for
{\mathbf{Q}}\equiv{\mathbf{Q}}(\gamma)
and for
{\mathbf{R}}\equiv{\mathbf{R}}(\gamma)
, previously defined in equation
9
, denoted
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}
and
\bar{\mathbf{R}}
respectively, are given by:

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{Q}}(\gamma)=\left(\frac{{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}+{\mathbf{I}}_{p}}{1+\delta_{Q}}+\gamma{\mathbf{I}}_{p}\right)^{-1},\quad\bar{\mathbf{R}}(\gamma)=\left(\frac{{\bm{\mu}}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}+{\mathbf{I}}_{p}}{1+\delta_{R}}+\gamma{\mathbf{I}}_{p}\right)^{-1}.

Where:

\delta_{Q}=\frac{1}{n}\Tr\bar{\mathbf{Q}}=\frac{\eta-\gamma-1+\sqrt{(\eta-\gamma-1)^{2}+4\eta\gamma}}{2\gamma},\quad\delta_{R}=\frac{\tilde{\eta}-\tilde{\gamma}-1+\sqrt{(\tilde{\eta}-\tilde{\gamma}-1)^{2}+4\tilde{\eta}\tilde{\gamma}}}{2\tilde{\gamma}}.

4
Main results

After having defined the setting and needed background, we will now present our main technical results, which describe the asymptotic behavior of the fine-tuned classifier defined in equation

α

-FTC
. Specifically, we provide our results under the following growth rate assumptions (classical assumptions in Random Matrix Theory).

Assumption 4.1

(Growth Rates)
.

Suppose that as
p,n,N\to\infty
:

1)
\frac{p}{n}\to\eta\in[0,\infty)
,
2)
\frac{p}{N}\to\tilde{\eta}\in[0,\infty)
,
3)
\|{\bm{\mu}}\|={\mathcal{O}}(1)
,
4)
\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|={\mathcal{O}}(1)
.

The first and second assumptions simply state that our analysis considers both the low (
\eta,\tilde{\eta}\ll 1
) and high (
\eta,\tilde{\eta}\gg 1
) dimensional regimes. The third and last assumptions are also fundamental and state that the norm of the source
{\bm{\mu}}
and target
{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}
data means do not scale with the dimension
p
, which makes the classification problem neither easy (
\|{\bm{\mu}}\|\to\infty
) nor impossible (
\|{\bm{\mu}}\|\to 0
) in high dimensions. Having stated the main assumptions, we are now in a position to present our main technical findings about the theoretical test performance of the fine-tuned classifier

α

-FTC
. But beforehand, let us define some scalar quantities that will be useful in our derivations:

\displaystyle\lambda_{Q}=\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q}),\quad

\displaystyle\lambda_{R}=\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}+1+\tilde{\gamma}(1+\delta_{R}),\quad h=1-\frac{\eta}{(1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q}))^{2}},

\displaystyle\tilde{h}=1-\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{(1+\tilde{\gamma}(1+\delta_{R}))^{2}}

Our main theorem below describes the behavior of the decision function of our fine-tuned classifier.

Theorem 4.2

(Gaussianity of the fine-tuned Ridge model)
.

Let
{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}
be the fine-tuned classifier as defined in equation

α

-FTC
and suppose that Assumption
4.1
holds. The decision function
{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{x}}
, on some test sample
{\bm{x}}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
independent of
{\mathbf{X}}
, satisfies:

\displaystyle{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{x}}\,\,\toind\,\,{\mathcal{N}}\left((-1)^{a}m_{\alpha},\,\nu_{\alpha}-m_{\alpha}^{2}\right),

where:

\displaystyle m_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{Q}}\left(\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha\beta\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})}{\lambda_{R}}\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}\right),

\displaystyle\nu_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=T_{1}+\alpha T_{2}+\alpha^{2}T_{3}.

With:

\displaystyle T_{1}=\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{h\lambda_{Q}}\left(\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1}{\lambda_{Q}}-2(1-h)\right)+\frac{1-h}{h},

\displaystyle T_{2}=\frac{2\gamma\beta(1+\delta_{Q})\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{R}\lambda_{Q}}\left(1-\frac{\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})}{h\lambda_{Q}}\right),

\displaystyle T_{3}=\frac{\gamma^{2}(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}{h}\times

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{R}^{2}}\left(\frac{\beta^{2}\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}^{2}}+\frac{1-h}{\eta}\left(1+\frac{\beta^{2}\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}^{2}}-\frac{2\beta^{2}\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}}+(1-\tilde{h})\left(1-\frac{2\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{R}}\right)\right)\right)\right]

In simple terms, Theorem
4.2
states that the decision function of the classifier in equation

α

-FTC
is asymptotically equivalent to the thresholding of two monovariate Gaussian random variables with respective means
m_{\alpha}
and
-m_{\alpha}
and standard deviation
\nu_{\alpha}-m_{\alpha}^{2}
, where the statistics
m_{\alpha}
and
\nu_{\alpha}
are expressed in terms of the scalar quantities defined above (see Figure
6
in the Appendix). Having characterized the distribution of the decision function of
{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}
, we can now estimate its generalization performance, such as its test accuracy.

Proposition 4.3

(Asymptotic test accuracy of
{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}
)
.

The asymptotic test accuracy of
{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}
defined in equation

α

-FTC
, under Assumption
4.1
, and as the number of test samples
n_{\text{test}}\to\infty
, is given by:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{\text{test}}\asto 1-\varphi\left((\nu_{\alpha}-m_{\alpha}^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}m_{\alpha}\right),\quad\text{where: }\varphi(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{x}^{+\infty}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}\mathrm{dt}.

Therefore, thanks to Proposition
4.3
, we now have the exact formulas of the theoretical test accuracy of our classifier
{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}
, which can be used to characterize the expression of the optimal/worst parameters of the model (for instance, the
\alpha
) to use for the fine-tuning process. In particular, we will derive the theoretical expressions of the extremum of
\alpha
that lead to either the best or the worst test accuracy on the target task (proof in Appendix
B
).

Theorem 4.4

(Optimal
\alpha
)
.

Maximizing the term
\left((\nu_{\alpha}-m_{\alpha}^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}m_{\alpha}\right)
in terms of
\alpha
leads to maximizing the test accuracy
{\mathcal{A}}_{\text{test}}
, and gives a unique maximizer
\alpha^{\star}
given by:

\alpha^{\star}=\frac{\lambda_{R}T_{2}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}-2\beta\gamma T_{1}(1+\delta_{Q})\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}}{\beta\gamma T_{2}(1+\delta_{Q})\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}-2\lambda_{R}T_{3}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}

Plus, solving
(\nu_{\alpha}-m_{\alpha}^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}m_{\alpha}=0
leads to the unique minimizer
\bar{\alpha}
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\text{test}}
, which is given by:

\bar{\alpha}=-\frac{\lambda_{R}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{\beta\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}}

\alpha^{\star}
that is generally different from

(standard approach), and as can be previously anticipated, its impact on the test accuracy is more visible in the case of a higher alignment factor

).

\alpha^{\star}
, Figure

to the choice of
\alpha
. It is clear that
\alpha^{\star}
is non-decreasing with the alignment
\beta
between the source and target task, but its effect gets amplified with the dimension

of the problem. Notably, the influence of
\alpha
is more pronounced in low-resource settings (
p\gg n
) compared to cases where sufficient fine-tuning data is available. This further underscores the crucial role of
\alpha
in effectively leveraging the pre-trained model and source data. Additionally, as
\beta\to 0
, we also remark that
\alpha^{\star}\to 0
, which means that fine-tuning has no added value when the source and target tasks are
unrelated
and orthogonal.

5
Experiments

In this section, we present some experiments on real datasets to validate our approach. We start by fine-tuning linear models on the Amazon Review dataset
(
Blitzer et al. 2007
)
to verify our theoretical findings. After that, we formalize our new class of reparametrization methods and verify its efficiency by experiments on fine-tuning LLMs on the GLUE tasks
(
Wang et al. 2018
)
.

5.1
Within our theoretical model: Linear Binary Classification

Here we present our experiments on the Amazon Review dataset
(

5.2
Beyond our theoretical model: Supervised Fine-tuning for LLMs

To go beyond linear models, we now fine-tune
roberta-base
language model
(
Liu et al. 2019
)
) on downstream classification taken from GLUE benchmarks
(
Wang et al. 2018
)
. To adapt our theoretical insights from the linear model to complex, multi-layered architectures like LLMs, we generalize the scalar scaling parameter
\alpha
to a vector
\bm{\alpha}
. This extension provides finer-grained control, allowing the model to rescale the contribution of the frozen base weights on a per-output-neuron basis. This added flexibility is crucial for capturing the intricate functional specialization within different dimensions of a neural network’s hidden states. Consequently, the update rule for a weight matrix
{\mathbf{W}}^{\star}
is modified from a simple scalar product to a row-wise scaling operation, as detailed below:

\boxed{{\mathbf{W}}_{\text{new}}=\bm{\alpha}\odot{\mathbf{W}}^{\star}+{\mathbf{W}}}

(10)

where
\odot
is the element-wise product between vectors,
{\mathbf{W}}^{\star}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{out}\times d_{in}}
is the original layer weights (frozen during training),
\bm{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{out}}
(each element in the output dimension is then multiplied by a scalar), and
{\mathbf{W}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{out}\times d_{in}}
is the trainable weight matrix. Additionally,
{\mathbf{W}}
can be approximated with a low-rank matrix:
{\mathbf{W}}={\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{B}}
, where:
{\mathbf{A}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{out}\times r}
and
{\mathbf{B}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{r\times d_{in}}
, a method that we call
\alpha
-LoRA
. We then report in Table
2
the test performance obtained using standard LoRA and our
\alpha
-LoRA method evaluated on six GLUE tasks: MNLI, QNLI, MRPC, RTE, SST-2, and QQP.

Finding the parameters
\bm{\alpha}
.

We designed a practical heuristic algorithm to automatically update
\bm{\alpha}
during training. In fact, we consider each vector
\bm{\alpha}
as a trainable parameter and update these vectors once each
T
step (which can be tuned) with either
Adam
or
AdamW
by sampling a new batch, different from the one used to train the reparametrization weights
{\mathbf{W}}
, and then taking a gradient step over this new batch. The design choices of our algorithm can be justified by the following:

•

Because the vectors
\bm{\alpha}
applied to each module lie in the whole Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, it is not possible to find such a parameter through a simple grid search, as this will give a very costly and impractical algorithm.

•

Additionally, finding theoretical formulas for each vector
\bm{\alpha}
is very hard, if not impossible. Therefore, it is crucial to have an algorithm that updates the vectors
\bm{\alpha}
automatically.

•

Finally, because we want to optimize the
generalization
performance of our fine-tuning method, training
\bm{\alpha}
in the same way as the reparametrization weights
{\mathbf{W}}
can easily lead to overfitting of the model, which justifies sampling of new batches to update
\bm{\alpha}
and the update rate
T
. Our specific choices are detailed for reproducibility in Appendix
E
.

Figure

shows that our algorithm leads to optimal scaling vectors
\bm{\alpha}^{\star}
in their neighborhood, which proves the effectiveness of our algorithm and the fine-tuning method in general. The pseudo-code

of our algorithm is detailed in the Appendix

.

6
Conclusion and limitations

In this work, we introduced a new class of reparametrization-based fine-tuning methods that leverage an additional scaling parameter to improve the generalization of transfer learning. Using tools from Random Matrix Theory, we proved the existence and impact of an optimal scaling factor in high-dimensional binary classification. We show that this factor is typically different from the standard choice. Our theoretical analysis was further supported by experiments on real-world tasks, where our proposed approach consistently outperformed standard LoRA on multiple benchmarks.

Although promising, our framework also has limitations. Theoretical guarantees are derived under specific assumptions on data distributions and model structure, which may not fully capture the complexity of modern deep architectures. We believe future work could extend these insights to broader model families, design more efficient algorithms for parameter selection, and further explore the trade-off between generalization and efficiency in transfer learning. Furthermore, an exciting avenue for investigation is the integration of our
\alpha
-scaling technique with other advanced adapter methods. Since our approach is complementary to improvements in the adapter’s architecture, such as DoRA or other LoRA variants, combining them could lead to synergistic gains in fine-tuning performance.

Lemma A.1

(Resolvent identity)
.

For invertible matrices
{\mathbf{A}}
and
{\mathbf{B}}
, we have:

{\mathbf{A}}^{-1}-{\mathbf{B}}^{-1}={\mathbf{A}}^{-1}({\mathbf{B}}-{\mathbf{A}}){\mathbf{B}}^{-1}.

Lemma A.2

(Sherman-Morisson)
.

For
{\mathbf{A}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p\times p}
invertible and
{\bm{u}},{\bm{v}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
,
{\mathbf{A}}+{\bm{u}}{\bm{v}}^{\top}
is invertible if and only if:
1+{\bm{v}}^{\top}{\mathbf{A}}^{-1}{\bm{u}}\neq 0
, and:

({\mathbf{A}}+{\bm{u}}{\bm{v}}^{\top})^{-1}={\mathbf{A}}^{-1}-\frac{{\mathbf{A}}^{-1}{\bm{u}}{\bm{v}}^{\top}{\mathbf{A}}^{-1}}{1+{\bm{v}}^{\top}{\mathbf{A}}^{-1}{\bm{u}}}.

Besides,

({\mathbf{A}}+{\bm{u}}{\bm{v}}^{\top})^{-1}{\bm{u}}=\frac{{\mathbf{A}}^{-1}{\bm{u}}}{1+{\bm{v}}^{\top}{\mathbf{A}}^{-1}{\bm{u}}}.

A.2
Deterministic equivalents

Recall the expression of the resolvents defined in equation equation
9
:

{\mathbf{Q}}=\left(\frac{1}{n}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}+\gamma{\mathbf{I}}_{p}\right)^{-1},{\mathbf{R}}=\left(\frac{1}{N}\tilde{\mathbf{X}}\tilde{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}+\tilde{\gamma}{\mathbf{I}}_{p}\right)^{-1}

We define the matrices
{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}
and
{\mathbf{R}}_{-i}
as the resolvents obtained by removing the contribution of the
i^{th}
sample, i.e:

{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}=\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k\neq i}{\bm{x}}_{k}{\bm{x}}_{k}^{\top}+\gamma{\mathbf{I}}_{p}\right)^{-1},\quad{\mathbf{R}}_{-i}=\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k\neq i}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{k}^{\top}+\tilde{\gamma}{\mathbf{I}}_{p}\right)^{-1}

then we have that:

{\mathbf{Q}}=\left({\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}^{-1}+\frac{1}{n}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}\right)^{-1},\quad{\mathbf{R}}=\left({\mathbf{R}}_{-i}^{-1}+\frac{1}{N}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}\right)^{-1}

Thus by Sherman-Morisson’s lemma:

{\mathbf{Q}}={\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}-\frac{1}{n}\frac{{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}}{1+\delta_{Q}},\quad{\mathbf{R}}={\mathbf{R}}_{-i}-\frac{\frac{1}{N}{\mathbf{R}}_{-i}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}_{-i}}{1+\delta_{R}}

where:

\delta_{Q}=\frac{1}{n}\Tr\bar{\mathbf{Q}}=\frac{\eta-\gamma-1+\sqrt{(\eta-\gamma-1)^{2}+4\eta\gamma}}{2\gamma},\quad\delta_{R}=\frac{1}{N}\Tr\bar{\mathbf{R}}=\frac{\tilde{\eta}-\tilde{\gamma}-1+\sqrt{(\tilde{\eta}-\tilde{\gamma}-1)^{2}+4\tilde{\eta}\gamma}}{2\tilde{\gamma}}

Thus, we get that:

{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}_{i}=\frac{{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}}{1+\delta_{Q}},\quad{\mathbf{R}}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}=\frac{{\mathbf{R}}_{-i}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}}{1+\delta_{R}}

(12)

Using the above identities, we can easily prove the deterministic equivalents of
{\mathbf{Q}}
and
{\mathbf{R}}
stated in Lemma
3.5
, which we will do in the following.

Lemma A.3

(Deterministic equivalent of
{\mathbf{Q}}
and
{\mathbf{R}}
)
.

Under the high-dimensional regime and the assumptions
4.1
, a deterministic equivalent for
{\mathbf{Q}}\equiv{\mathbf{Q}}(\gamma)
and for
{\mathbf{R}}\equiv{\mathbf{R}}(\gamma)

\bar{\mathbf{Q}}
and

9

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{Q}}(\gamma)=\left(\frac{{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}+{\mathbf{I}}_{p}}{1+\delta_{Q}}+\gamma{\mathbf{I}}_{p}\right)^{-1},\quad\bar{\mathbf{R}}(\gamma)=\left(\frac{{\bm{\mu}}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}+{\mathbf{I}}_{p}}{1+\delta_{R}}+\gamma{\mathbf{I}}_{p}\right)^{-1}.

Where:

\delta_{Q}=\frac{1}{n}\Tr\bar{\mathbf{Q}}=\frac{\eta-\gamma-1+\sqrt{(\eta-\gamma-1)^{2}+4\eta\gamma}}{2\gamma},\quad\delta_{R}=\frac{1}{N}\Tr\bar{\mathbf{R}}=\frac{\tilde{\eta}-\tilde{\gamma}-1+\sqrt{(\tilde{\eta}-\tilde{\gamma}-1)^{2}+4\tilde{\eta}\gamma}}{2\tilde{\gamma}}.

Proof.

We will prove the deterministic equivalent of
{\mathbf{Q}}
, and the proof of
\bar{\mathbf{R}}
can be derived similarly. In general, we want to find a deterministic equivalent
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}
of the same form of

\bar{\mathbf{Q}}(\gamma)=\left({\mathbf{S}}+\gamma{\mathbf{I}}_{p}\right)^{-1}
and we want to find a deterministic matrix

such that for any linear form
u

u({\mathbf{Q}})\asto u(\bar{\mathbf{Q}}),

Or more simply:

u(\E[{\mathbf{Q}}]-\bar{\mathbf{Q}})\to 0.

We have that:

\displaystyle\E[{\mathbf{Q}}]-\bar{\mathbf{Q}}

\displaystyle=\E[{\mathbf{Q}}-\bar{\mathbf{Q}}]

\displaystyle=\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\left({\mathbf{S}}-\frac{1}{n}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\right)\bar{\mathbf{Q}}]

\displaystyle=\E\left[\left({\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{S}}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}\right)\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\right]

And since:
{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}_{i}=\frac{{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}}{1+\delta_{Q}}
and that we want
\E[{\mathbf{Q}}]=\bar{\mathbf{Q}}
in linear forms, we get that:

\displaystyle\E\left[\left({\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{S}}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}\right)\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\right]=\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{S}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1+\delta_{Q}}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}]\bar{\mathbf{Q}}

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{S}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1+\delta_{Q}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}({\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}+{\mathbf{I}}_{p})\bar{\mathbf{Q}}

\displaystyle(\text{By independence of ${\bm{x}}_{i}$ and ${\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}$})

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\left({\mathbf{S}}-\frac{{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}+{\mathbf{I}}_{p}}{1+\delta_{Q}}\right)\bar{\mathbf{Q}}

{\mathbf{A}}
, we have:

{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{Q}}\leftrightarrow\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}+\frac{1}{n}\frac{\Tr(\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}})}{(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}],

and:

{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}\leftrightarrow\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{R}}+\frac{1}{N}\frac{\Tr(\Sigma\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{R}})}{(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}\E[{\mathbf{R}}\Sigma{\mathbf{R}}].

In particular for every
{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p}
:

\displaystyle{\bm{a}}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}]{\bm{b}}=\frac{1}{h}{\bm{a}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{b}},\quad{\bm{a}}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{R}}\Sigma{\mathbf{R}}]{\bm{b}}=\frac{1}{\tilde{h}}{\bm{a}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\Sigma\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{b}}.

Proof.

The proof is derived similarly as in the appendix of
Firdoussi & Seddik 2024
. Again, the proof is similar for both
{\mathbf{Q}}
and
{\mathbf{R}}
.

Let
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}
be a deterministic equivalent of
{\mathbf{Q}}
. The following equations and identities are valid in terms of linear forms. We have that:

\displaystyle\E[{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{Q}}]

\displaystyle=\E[\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{Q}}]+\E[({\mathbf{Q}}-\bar{\mathbf{Q}}){\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{Q}}]

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbf{Q}}(\E[{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{Q}}]+{\mathbf{A}}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}-\bar{\mathbf{Q}}])+\E[({\mathbf{Q}}-\bar{\mathbf{Q}}){\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{Q}}]

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}+\E[({\mathbf{Q}}-\bar{\mathbf{Q}}){\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{Q}}]

Using lemma
A.1
, we have that:

\displaystyle{\mathbf{Q}}-\bar{\mathbf{Q}}

\displaystyle={\mathbf{Q}}(\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{-1}-{\mathbf{Q}}^{-1})\bar{\mathbf{Q}}

\displaystyle={\mathbf{Q}}\left(\frac{\Sigma_{\beta}}{1+\delta_{Q}}-\frac{1}{n}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\right)\bar{\mathbf{Q}}

\displaystyle={\mathbf{Q}}\left({\mathbf{S}}-\frac{1}{n}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\right)\bar{\mathbf{Q}}

Thus:

\displaystyle\E[{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{Q}}]

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}+\E[{\mathbf{Q}}({\mathbf{S}}-\frac{1}{n}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top})\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{Q}}]

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}+\E[{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{S}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{Q}}]-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{Q}}]

We have that:

\displaystyle\E[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{Q}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\delta_{Q}}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{Q}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\delta_{Q}}\left(\E[{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}]-\E[{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}\frac{{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}}{n(1+\delta_{Q})}]\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\delta_{Q}}\left(\E[{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}]-\E[{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}\frac{{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}}{n(1+\delta_{Q})}]\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\delta_{Q}}\left(\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{Q}}]-\E[{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}\frac{{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}}{n(1+\delta_{Q})}]\right)

Therefore, by replacing the obtained expression of
\E[{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{Q}}]
in the equation of
\E[{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{Q}}]
, we get that:

\displaystyle\E[{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{Q}}]

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}+\frac{1}{n^{2}(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}]

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}+\frac{1}{n^{2}(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Tr(\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}})\E[{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}]

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}+\frac{1}{n^{2}(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Tr(\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}})\E[{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}]

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}+\frac{1}{n}\frac{\Tr(\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}})}{(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}]

Which finally concludes the proof.
∎

Now we will provide the result of a useful quantity that we will be using for computing the variance.

Lemma A.8

(Expectation of
\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{A}}\tilde{\bm{w}}
)
.

Let
{\mathbf{A}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p\times p}
be a random matrix independent of
\tilde{\bm{w}}
. We have that:

\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{A}}\tilde{\bm{w}}]=\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}\left({\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}]{\bm{\mu}}-\frac{2}{N(1+\delta_{R})}\Tr(\Sigma\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}]){\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}+\frac{1}{N}\Tr(\Sigma\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}])\right)

Proof.

We have that:

\displaystyle\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{A}}\tilde{\bm{w}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\E[\tilde{y}_{i}\tilde{y}_{j}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{j}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i\neq j}\E[\tilde{y}_{i}\tilde{y}_{j}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{j}]+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\E[\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}]

We have for
i\neq j
:

\displaystyle\E[\tilde{y}_{i}\tilde{y}_{j}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{j}]=\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}\E[\tilde{y}_{i}\tilde{y}_{j}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}{\mathbf{R}}_{-i}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}_{-i}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{j}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}\E\left[\tilde{y}_{i}\tilde{y}_{j}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}\left({\mathbf{R}}_{-ij}-\frac{\frac{1}{N}{\mathbf{R}}_{-ij}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{j}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{j}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}_{-ij}}{1+\delta_{R}}\right){\mathbf{A}}\left({\mathbf{R}}_{-ij}-\frac{\frac{1}{N}{\mathbf{R}}_{-ij}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}_{-ij}}{1+\delta_{R}}\right)\tilde{\bm{x}}_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=A_{11}-A_{12}-A_{13}+A_{14}

So let us compute each term independently:

\displaystyle A_{11}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}\E[\tilde{y}_{i}\tilde{y}_{j}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}_{-ij}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}_{-ij}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{j}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}]{\bm{\mu}}

And :

\displaystyle A_{12}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N(1+\delta_{R})^{3}}\E[\tilde{y}_{i}\tilde{y}_{j}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}_{-ij}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}_{-ij}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}_{-ij}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{j}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N(1+\delta_{R})^{3}}\Tr(\Sigma\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}])\E[\tilde{y}_{i}\tilde{y}_{j}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}_{-ij}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{j}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N(1+\delta_{R})^{3}}\Tr(\Sigma\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}]){\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}

And also we can easily observe that:

\displaystyle A_{13}=A_{12},\quad A_{14}={\mathcal{O}}(N^{-1}).

Thus:

\displaystyle\E[\tilde{y}_{i}\tilde{y}_{j}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{j}]=\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}\left({\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}]{\bm{\mu}}-\frac{2}{N(1+\delta_{R})}\Tr(\Sigma\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}]){\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}\right)

And for the second term in the equation of
\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}{\mathbf{A}}\tilde{\bm{w}}]
, we have:

\displaystyle\E[\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}\E[\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}_{-i}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}_{-i}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}\E[\Tr(\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}_{-i}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}_{-i})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}\Tr(\E[\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}\tilde{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}]\E[{\mathbf{R}}_{-i}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}_{-i}])

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}\Tr(\Sigma\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}])

Hence, finally:

\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{A}}\tilde{\bm{w}}]=\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}\left({\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}]{\bm{\mu}}-\frac{2}{N(1+\delta_{R})}\Tr(\Sigma\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}]){\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}+\frac{1}{N}\Tr(\Sigma\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{R}}])\right)

∎

Lemma A.9

(Commutativity)
.

Let
\bar{\mathbf{R}}
and
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}
be the resolvent matrices defined in lemma
3.5
. We have that:

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}=\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}},\quad\bar{\mathbf{R}}\Sigma=\Sigma\bar{\mathbf{R}}.

Proof.

We will just prove it for
\bar{\mathbf{Q}}
and
\Sigma_{\beta}
because the other proof of the second identity is similar. We know that:

\Sigma_{\beta}=(1+\delta_{Q})(\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{-1}-\gamma{\mathbf{I}}_{p})

Thus:

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}=(1+\delta_{Q})\bar{\mathbf{Q}}(\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{-1}-\gamma{\mathbf{I}}_{p})=(1+\delta_{Q})({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{Q}})

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}=(1+\delta_{Q})(\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{-1}-\gamma{\mathbf{I}}_{p})\bar{\mathbf{Q}}=(1+\delta_{Q})({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{Q}})

which concludes the proof.
∎

Appendix B
RMT Analysis of the fine-tuned classifier

Let
{\bm{x}}\sim{\mathcal{N}}((-1)^{a}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta},{\mathbf{I}}_{p})
independent of the fine-tuning dataset
{\mathbf{X}}
. We recall that:

\displaystyle{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}={\bm{w}}+\alpha\tilde{\bm{w}}-\frac{\alpha}{n}{\mathbf{Q}}(\gamma){\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\tilde{\bm{w}},

where:

\displaystyle{\bm{w}}=\frac{1}{n}{\mathbf{Q}}(\gamma){\mathbf{X}}{\bm{y}},\quad\tilde{\bm{w}}=\frac{1}{N}{\mathbf{R}}(\tilde{\gamma})\tilde{\mathbf{X}}\tilde{\bm{y}}

B.1
Test Expectation

We have that:

\displaystyle\E[{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]=\E[{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]+\alpha\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]-\frac{\alpha}{n}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}]

(13)

Let us compute each term of this previous sum.

First, using lemma
A.6
, we have that, since
{\bm{x}}
is independent of
{\mathbf{X}}
and of
\tilde{\mathbf{X}}
:

\displaystyle\E[{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]=\E[{\bm{w}}]^{\top}\E[{\bm{x}}]=\frac{(-1)^{a}}{1+\delta_{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}

\displaystyle\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]=\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}]^{\top}\E[{\bm{x}}]=\frac{(-1)^{a}}{1+\delta_{R}}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}

And we have that:

\displaystyle\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}]

\displaystyle=\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}]^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}]\E[{\bm{x}}]

And:

\displaystyle\E[{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\E[{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1+\delta_{Q}}\E[{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}_{i}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1+\delta_{Q}}\E[{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}]\bar{\mathbf{Q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{1+\delta_{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}

Thus:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{a}}{(1+\delta_{R})}\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{Q})}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{a}}{1+\delta_{R}}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{Q}}){\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}

Finally:

\displaystyle\E[{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{a}\left(\frac{1}{1+\delta_{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}+\frac{\alpha}{1+\delta_{R}}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}-\frac{\alpha}{1+\delta_{R}}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{Q}}){\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{a}\left(\frac{1}{1+\delta_{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}+\frac{\alpha\gamma}{1+\delta_{R}}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\right)

And using the identities in lemma
A.5
:

\displaystyle\E[{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{a}}{(\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q}))}\left(\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})}{(\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}+1+\tilde{\gamma}(1+\delta_{R}))}\beta\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}\right)

(14)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{a}}{\lambda_{Q}}\left(\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha\beta\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})}{\lambda_{R}}\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}\right)

(15)

B.2
Test Variance

To compute the variance of
{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{x}}
, it suffices to compute the second moment:
\E[({\bm{w}}_{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{x}})^{2}]
.

\displaystyle\E[({\bm{w}}_{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{x}})^{2}]=\E[({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}+\alpha\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})^{2}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{n^{2}}(\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}})^{2}-\frac{2\alpha}{n}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}+\alpha\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})]

(16)

First term:

We have that, as proved in
Firdoussi & Seddik 2024
:

\displaystyle\E[({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})^{2}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h(1+\delta_{Q})}\left(\frac{1}{1+\delta_{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}-2(1-h){\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\right)+\frac{1-h}{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h(1+\delta_{Q})}\left(\frac{1}{1+\delta_{Q}}\left(({\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta})^{2}+{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\right)-2(1-h){\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\right)+\frac{1-h}{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{h(\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q}))}\left(\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1}{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})}-2(1-h)\right)+\frac{1-h}{h}

And:

\displaystyle\E[(\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}x)^{2}]

\displaystyle=\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]

\displaystyle=\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}{\bm{x}}^{\top}\tilde{\bm{w}}]

\displaystyle=\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\Sigma_{\beta}\tilde{\bm{w}}]

Therefore by lemma
A.8
:

\E[(\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}x)^{2}]=\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}\left({\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{R}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{R}}]{\bm{\mu}}-\frac{2}{(1+\delta_{R})}\frac{1}{N}\Tr(\Sigma\E[{\mathbf{R}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{R}}]){\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}+\frac{1}{N}\Tr(\Sigma\E[{\mathbf{R}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{R}}])\right)

(17)

And, we have that:

\displaystyle\E[{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]

\displaystyle=\E[{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}{\bm{x}}^{\top}\tilde{\bm{w}}]

\displaystyle=\E[{\bm{w}}]^{\top}\Sigma_{\beta}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{Q})(1+\delta_{R})}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{R})}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{Q}})\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{R})}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}-\frac{\gamma}{(1+\delta_{R})}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}

And since
\E[{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]=\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]
, then:

\E[{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]=\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{R})}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}-\frac{\gamma}{(1+\delta_{R})}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}

and thus:

{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}={\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}

(18)

Second term:

Now let us compute the expectation of the second term in equation
28
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{2}}\E[(\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}})^{2}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}{\bm{x}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\tilde{\bm{w}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\tilde{\bm{w}}]

\displaystyle=\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-\gamma{\mathbf{Q}})\Sigma_{\beta}({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-\gamma{\mathbf{Q}})\tilde{\bm{w}}]

Therefore, by lemma
A.8
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{2}}\E[(\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}})^{2}]=\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{R}}({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-\gamma{\mathbf{Q}})\Sigma_{\beta}({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-\gamma{\mathbf{Q}}){\mathbf{R}}]{\bm{\mu}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\Tr(\Sigma\E[{\mathbf{R}}({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-\gamma{\mathbf{Q}})\Sigma_{\beta}({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-\gamma{\mathbf{Q}}){\mathbf{R}}])}{N(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}\left(1-\frac{2}{(1+\delta_{R})}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}\right)

Third term:

Now we want to compute
\frac{2\alpha}{n}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}+\alpha\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})]
. So we have that:

\displaystyle\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]

\displaystyle=\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}]^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}{\bm{x}}^{\top}{\bm{w}}]

\displaystyle=\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}]^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\bm{w}}]

\displaystyle=\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}]^{\top}\E[\frac{1}{n}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{X}}{\bm{y}}]

\displaystyle=\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}]^{\top}\E[({\mathbf{Q}}^{-1}-\gamma{\mathbf{I}}_{p}){\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{X}}{\bm{y}}]

\displaystyle=\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}]^{\top}\E[({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-\gamma{\mathbf{Q}})\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{X}}{\bm{y}}]

\displaystyle=\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}]^{\top}\left(\E[\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{X}}{\bm{y}}]-\gamma\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{X}}{\bm{y}}]\right)

And we have that:

\displaystyle\E[\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{X}}{\bm{y}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\E[y_{i}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}_{i}]

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{(1+\delta_{Q})}\E[y_{i}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}]

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{(1+\delta_{Q})}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=n({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{Q}}){\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}

And:

\displaystyle\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{X}}{\bm{y}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\E[y_{i}{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}_{i}]

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{(1+\delta_{Q})}\E[y_{i}{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}]

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{(1+\delta_{Q})}\E\left[y_{i}\left({\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}-\frac{\frac{1}{n}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}}{1+\delta_{Q}}\right)\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{(1+\delta_{Q})}\left(\E[y_{i}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}]-\frac{1}{n(1+\delta_{Q})}\E[y_{i}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}]\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{(1+\delta_{Q})}\left(\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}]{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}-\frac{1}{n(1+\delta_{Q})}\Tr(\Sigma_{\beta}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}])\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{h(1+\delta_{Q})}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}-\frac{n(1-h)}{h}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=n\left(\frac{1}{h}({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{Q}})\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}-\frac{1-h}{h}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=n(\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}-\frac{\gamma}{h}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta})

Thus:

\frac{1}{n}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]=\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{R})}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\left({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-2\gamma\bar{\mathbf{Q}}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{h}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}\right){\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}

(19)

Let us now compute the remaining term:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}{\bm{x}}^{\top}\tilde{\bm{w}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}\tilde{\bm{w}}]

\displaystyle=\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-\gamma{\mathbf{Q}})\Sigma_{\beta}\tilde{\bm{w}}]

And again by lemma
A.8
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]=\frac{1}{(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{R}}({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-\gamma{\mathbf{Q}})\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{R}}]{\bm{\mu}}+\frac{\Tr(\Sigma\E[{\mathbf{R}}({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-\gamma{\mathbf{Q}})\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{R}}])}{N(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}\left(1-\frac{2}{(1+\delta_{R})}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}\right)

Now let us group all the results as follows.

Terms without
\alpha
:

There is only one term which is:

\displaystyle T_{1}=\E[({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})^{2}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h(1+\delta_{Q})}\left((2h-1){\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}-\gamma{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\right)+\frac{1-h}{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{h(\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q}))}\left(\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1}{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})}-2(1-h)\right)+\frac{1-h}{h}

Terms in
\alpha
:

There are two:
2\E[{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]
and
\frac{2}{n}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]
:

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=2\E[{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]-\frac{2}{n}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{(1+\delta_{R})}\left({\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}-\gamma{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}-{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-2\gamma\bar{\mathbf{Q}}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{h}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}){\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\gamma}{(1+\delta_{R})}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\left({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-\frac{\gamma}{h}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\right){\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}

And using lemma
A.5
:

T_{2}=\frac{2\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})\beta\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}}{\left(\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}+1+\tilde{\gamma}(1+\delta_{R})\right)(\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q}))}\left(1-\frac{\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})}{h(\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q}))}\right)

Terms in
\alpha^{2}

: we have three terms:
\E[(\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})^{2}]
,
\frac{1}{n^{2}}\E[(\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}})^{2}]
and
\frac{-2}{n}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]
:

\displaystyle T_{3}

\displaystyle=\E[(\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})^{2}]+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\E[(\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}})^{2}]-\frac{2}{n}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\left(\E[{\mathbf{R}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{R}}]-\E[{\mathbf{R}}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{R}}]+\gamma\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{R}}]\right){\bm{\mu}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma}{N(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}\left(1-\frac{2}{(1+\delta_{R})}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}\right)\Tr\left(\Sigma(\E[{\mathbf{R}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{R}}]-\E[{\mathbf{R}}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{R}}]+\gamma\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{R}}])\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}\left[{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{R}}]{\bm{\mu}}+\left(1-\frac{2}{(1+\delta_{R})}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}\right)\frac{1}{N}\Tr(\Sigma\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{R}}])\right]

where the last equality is gotten using lemma
A.9
.

We also have that:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\Tr(\Sigma\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{R}}])

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\Tr(\E[\Sigma{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{R}}])

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\E[\Tr(\Sigma{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{R}})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\E[\Tr({\mathbf{R}}\Sigma{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\Tr(\E[{\mathbf{R}}\Sigma{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}])

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\Tr(\E[{\mathbf{R}}\Sigma{\mathbf{R}}]\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}])

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h\tilde{h}}\frac{1}{N}\Tr(\bar{\mathbf{R}}\Sigma\bar{\mathbf{R}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}})

And:

\displaystyle{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{R}}]{\bm{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\Tr(\E[{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}])

\displaystyle=\E[\Tr({\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}})]

\displaystyle=\Tr(\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}]\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}])

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}\Tr(\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}]\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}})

Thus:

T_{3}=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{h(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}\left[\Tr(\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}]\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}})+\left(1-\frac{2}{(1+\delta_{R})}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}\right)\frac{1}{\tilde{h}}\frac{1}{N}\Tr(\bar{\mathbf{R}}\Sigma\bar{\mathbf{R}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}})\right]

Now remains to compute
\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}]
. For that, we use lemma
A.7
:

\displaystyle\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}]

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}+\frac{1}{N}\frac{\Tr(\Sigma\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}})}{(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}\E[{\mathbf{R}}\Sigma{\mathbf{R}}]

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}+\frac{1}{N}\frac{{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\Sigma\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}}{(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}\frac{1}{\tilde{h}}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\Sigma\bar{\mathbf{R}}

And since we are in the regime of
N\to\infty
, then:

\frac{1}{N}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\Sigma\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}={\mathcal{O}}(N^{-1})

Thus:

\E[{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}{\mathbf{R}}]=\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}

(20)

Hence,
T_{3}
becomes:

\displaystyle T_{3}=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{h(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}\left[{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}+\left(1-\frac{2}{(1+\delta_{R})}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}\right)\frac{1}{\tilde{h}}\frac{1}{N}\Tr(\bar{\mathbf{R}}\Sigma\bar{\mathbf{R}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}})\right]

And we also have that:

\displaystyle{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}

\displaystyle={\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}+{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\left({\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\right)^{2}+{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}

And:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\Tr(\bar{\mathbf{R}}\Sigma\bar{\mathbf{R}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\Tr(\bar{\mathbf{R}}^{2}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{2})

Therefore:

T_{3}=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{h(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}\left[\left({\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\right)^{2}+{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}+\left(1-\frac{2}{(1+\delta_{R})}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}\right)\frac{1}{\tilde{h}}\frac{1}{N}\Tr(\bar{\mathbf{R}}^{2}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{2})\right]

(21)

Then using lemmas
A.4
and
A.5
:

\displaystyle T_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{h(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}\left[\left({\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\right)^{2}+{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}\right]+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{h(1+\delta_{R})^{2}}\left(1-\frac{2}{(1+\delta_{R})}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{R}}{\bm{\mu}}\right)\frac{1}{\tilde{h}}\frac{1}{N}\Tr(\bar{\mathbf{R}}^{2}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma^{2}(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}{h}[\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{R}^{2}}\left(\frac{\beta^{2}\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}^{2}}+\frac{1}{(1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q}))^{2}}\left(1+\frac{\beta^{2}\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}^{2}}-\frac{2\beta^{2}\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}}\right)\right)+

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{(1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q}))^{2}(1+\tilde{\gamma}(1+\delta_{R}))^{2}}\left(1-\frac{2\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{R}}\right)]

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma^{2}(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}{h}\left[\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{R}^{2}}\left(\frac{\beta^{2}\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}^{2}}+\frac{1-h}{\eta}\left(1+\frac{\beta^{2}\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}^{2}}-\frac{2\beta^{2}\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}}+(1-\tilde{h})\left(1-\frac{2\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{R}}\right)\right)\right)\right]

Finally:

\displaystyle T_{1}=\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{h\lambda_{Q}}\left(\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1}{\lambda_{Q}}-2(1-h)\right)+\frac{1-h}{h}

(22)

\displaystyle T_{2}=\frac{2\gamma\beta(1+\delta_{Q})\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{R}\lambda_{Q}}\left(1-\frac{\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})}{h\lambda_{Q}}\right)

(23)

\displaystyle T_{3}=\frac{\gamma^{2}(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}{h}\left[\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{R}^{2}}\left(\frac{\beta^{2}\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}^{2}}+\frac{1-h}{\eta}\left(1+\frac{\beta^{2}\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}^{2}}-\frac{2\beta^{2}\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}}+(1-\tilde{h})\left(1-\frac{2\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{R}}\right)\right)\right)\right]

(24)

And the expression of the second order expectation reads:

\E[({\bm{w}}_{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{x}})^{2}]=T_{1}+\alpha T_{2}+\alpha^{2}T_{3}

(25)

And finally, Theorem
4.2
follows:

α

B.3
Finding optimal
\alpha^{*}

Since the test accuracy is given by
{\mathcal{A}}_{\text{test}}=1-\varphi\left((\nu_{\alpha}-m_{\alpha}^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}m_{\alpha}\right)
as in Proposition
4.3
, and that
\phi(x)
is a non-increasing function, then finding the optimal
\alpha^{*}
that maximizes the test accuracy boils down to maximizing the term inside
\phi
. Thus, by computing the derivative with respect to
\alpha
of
(\nu_{\alpha}-m_{\alpha}^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}m_{\alpha}
and finding the zero of the gradient gives us the final form of the best scaling parameter
\alpha^{*}
:

\alpha^{*}=\frac{\lambda_{R}T_{2}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}-2\beta\gamma T_{1}(1+\delta_{Q})\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}}{\beta\gamma T_{2}(1+\delta_{Q})\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}-2\lambda_{R}T_{3}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}

And since the worst test accuracy is
50\%
(random classification), which is obtained for
m_{\alpha}=0
, then solving the previous equation gives the worst scaling
\bar{\alpha}
to use:

\bar{\alpha}=-\frac{\lambda_{R}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{\beta\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})\|{\bm{\mu}}\|^{2}}

Appendix C
RMT Analysis of the fine-tuned classifier: the case of random source vector

Let
{\bm{x}}\sim{\mathcal{N}}((-1)^{a}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta},{\mathbf{I}}_{p})
be an independent test sample. Let
\tilde{\bm{w}}
be the source classifier (obtained through some optimization algorithm). We recall that:

{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}={\bm{w}}+\alpha\tilde{\bm{w}}-\frac{\alpha}{n}{\mathbf{Q}}(\gamma){\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\tilde{\bm{w}},\quad{\bm{w}}=\frac{1}{n}{\mathbf{Q}}(\gamma){\mathbf{X}}{\bm{y}}

C.1
Test Expectation

We have that:

\displaystyle\E[{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]=\E[{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]+\alpha\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]-\frac{\alpha}{n}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}]

(26)

Let us compute each term of this previous sum.

First, using lemma
A.6
, we have that, since
{\bm{x}}
is independent of
{\mathbf{X}}
:

\displaystyle\E[{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]=\E[{\bm{w}}]^{\top}\E[{\bm{x}}]=\frac{(-1)^{a}}{1+\delta_{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}

And we have that:

\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]=(-1)^{a}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}

And:

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{n}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{n(1+\delta_{Q})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{n(1+\delta_{Q})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{a}\alpha}{1+\delta_{Q}}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}

Thus:

\displaystyle\E[{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{a}\left(\frac{1}{1+\delta_{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}+\alpha\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}-\frac{\alpha}{1+\delta_{Q}}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{a}\left(\frac{1}{1+\delta_{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}+\alpha\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}-\alpha\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}(\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{-1}-\gamma{\mathbf{I}}_{p})\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{a}\left(\frac{1}{1+\delta_{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}+\alpha\gamma\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\right)

Using the forumlas in lemma
A.5
:

\E[{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]=\frac{(-1)^{a}}{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})}\left(\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+\alpha\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\right)

(27)

C.2
Test variance

To compute the variance of
{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{x}}
, it suffices to compute the second moment:
\E[({\bm{w}}_{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{x}})^{2}]
.

\displaystyle\E[({\bm{w}}_{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{x}})^{2}]=\E[({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}+\alpha\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})^{2}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{n^{2}}(\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}})^{2}-\frac{2\alpha}{n}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}+\alpha\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})]

(28)

First term:

We start by computing

\E[({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}+\alpha\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})^{2}]=\E[({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})^{2}]+\alpha^{2}\E[(\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})^{2}]+2\alpha\E[{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]

We have that, as proved in
Firdoussi & Seddik 2024
:

\displaystyle\E[({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})^{2}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h(1+\delta_{Q})}\left(\frac{1}{1+\delta_{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}-2(1-h){\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\right)+\frac{1-h}{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h(1+\delta_{Q})}\left(\frac{1}{1+\delta_{Q}}\left(({\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta})^{2}+{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\right)-2(1-h){\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\right)+\frac{1-h}{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{h(\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q}))}\left(\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1}{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})}-2(1-h)\right)+\frac{1-h}{h}

And we have that:

\E[(\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})^{2}]=\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\Sigma_{\beta}\tilde{\bm{w}}

And:

\E[{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]=\E[{\bm{w}}]^{\top}\Sigma_{\beta}\tilde{\bm{w}}=\frac{1}{1+\delta_{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}\tilde{\bm{w}}

Thus we have the first sum.

Second term:

Now let us compute the expectation of the second term:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{2}}\E[(\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}})^{2}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}{\bm{x}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\tilde{\bm{w}}]

\displaystyle=\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\E[\frac{1}{n}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}\frac{1}{n}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}]\tilde{\bm{w}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\E[({\mathbf{Q}}^{-1}-\gamma{\mathbf{I}}_{p}){\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}({\mathbf{Q}}^{-1}-\gamma{\mathbf{I}}_{p}){\mathbf{Q}}]\tilde{\bm{w}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\E[({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-\gamma{\mathbf{Q}})\Sigma_{\beta}({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-\gamma{\mathbf{Q}})]\tilde{\bm{w}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\E\left[\Sigma_{\beta}-\gamma\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}-\gamma{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}+\gamma^{2}{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}\right]\tilde{\bm{w}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\left(\Sigma_{\beta}-\gamma\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}-\gamma\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}+\gamma^{2}\right)\tilde{\bm{w}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\Sigma_{\beta}{\bm{w}}-2\gamma\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\tilde{\bm{w}}+\gamma^{2}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}]\tilde{\bm{w}}

Third term:

Now we will compute the last term:
\frac{2\alpha}{n}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}+\alpha\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})]

We have that:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}{\bm{x}}^{\top}{\bm{w}}]=\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\E[({\mathbf{Q}}^{-1}-\gamma{\mathbf{I}}_{p}){\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\bm{w}}]

\displaystyle=\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\E[({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-\gamma{\mathbf{Q}})\Sigma_{\beta}{\bm{w}}]

\displaystyle=\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\Sigma_{\beta}\E[{\bm{w}}]-\gamma\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\bm{w}}]

\displaystyle=\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\frac{\Sigma_{\beta}}{1+\delta_{Q}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}-\gamma\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\bm{w}}]

\displaystyle=\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\frac{\Sigma_{\beta}}{1+\delta_{Q}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}-\gamma\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}y_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}]

\displaystyle=\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\frac{\Sigma_{\beta}}{1+\delta_{Q}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}-\gamma\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}y_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}]

\displaystyle=\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\frac{\Sigma_{\beta}}{1+\delta_{Q}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}-\frac{\gamma}{1+\delta_{Q}}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}y_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}]

\displaystyle=\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\frac{\Sigma_{\beta}}{1+\delta_{Q}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}-\frac{\gamma}{1+\delta_{Q}}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\E\left[\left({\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}-\frac{\frac{1}{n}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}}{1+\delta_{Q}}\right)\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}y_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}\right]

\displaystyle=\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\frac{\Sigma_{\beta}}{1+\delta_{Q}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}-\frac{\gamma}{1+\delta_{Q}}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}y_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}]+\frac{\gamma}{n(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}y_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}]

\displaystyle=\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\frac{\Sigma_{\beta}}{1+\delta_{Q}}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}-\frac{\gamma}{1+\delta_{Q}}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}]{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}+\frac{\gamma}{n(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}\Tr(\Sigma_{\beta}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}])\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}

And:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\E[\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}{\bm{x}}^{\top}\tilde{\bm{w}}]=\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\E[({\mathbf{Q}}^{-1}-\gamma{\mathbf{I}}_{p}){\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}]\tilde{\bm{w}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\E[({\mathbf{I}}_{p}-\gamma{\mathbf{Q}})\Sigma_{\beta}]\tilde{\bm{w}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\Sigma_{\beta}\tilde{\bm{w}}-\gamma\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}\tilde{\bm{w}}

Grouping all the terms:

\displaystyle\E[({\bm{w}}_{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{x}})^{2}]=T_{1}+\alpha T_{2}+\alpha^{2}T_{3}

(29)

2\E[{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma^{2}(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}{h}\left(\frac{(\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta})^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}^{2}}+\frac{1-h}{\eta}\|\tilde{\bm{w}}\|^{2}+\frac{(1-h)(\tilde{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta})^{2}}{\eta\lambda_{Q}}\left(\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}}-2\right)\right)

Theorem C.1

(Gaussianity of the fine-tuned model for an arbitrary
\tilde{\bm{w}}
)
.

Let
{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}
be the fine-tuned classifier as defined in equation

α

-FTC
and suppose that Assumption
4.1
holds. The decision function
{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{x}}
, on some test sample
{\bm{x}}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{a}
independent of
{\mathbf{X}}
, satisfies:

\displaystyle{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{x}}\,\,\toind\,\,{\mathcal{N}}\left((-1)^{a}m_{\alpha},\,\nu_{\alpha}-m_{\alpha}^{2}\right),

where:

\displaystyle m_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+\alpha\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})\langle\tilde{\bm{w}},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle}{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})},

\displaystyle\nu_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=T_{1}+\alpha T_{2}+\alpha^{2}T_{3}.

with:

\displaystyle T_{1}=\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{h\lambda_{Q}}\left(\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1}{\lambda_{Q}}-2(1-h)\right)+\frac{1-h}{h},

\displaystyle T_{2}=\frac{2\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})\langle\tilde{\bm{w}},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle}{h\lambda_{Q}}\left(\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1}{\lambda}-(1-h)\right),

\displaystyle T_{3}=\frac{\gamma^{2}(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}{h}\left(\frac{\langle\tilde{\bm{w}},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}^{2}}+\frac{1-h}{\eta}\|\tilde{\bm{w}}\|^{2}+\frac{(1-h)\langle\tilde{\bm{w}},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle^{2}}{\eta\lambda_{Q}}\left(\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}}-2\right)\right).

C.3
Finding optimal
\alpha^{*}

Since the test accuracy is given by
{\mathcal{A}}_{\text{test}}=1-\varphi\left((\nu_{\alpha}-m_{\alpha}^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}m_{\alpha}\right)
as in Proposition
4.3
, and that
\phi(x)
is a non-increasing function, then finding the optimal
\alpha^{*}
that maximizes the test accuracy boils down to maximizing the term inside
\phi
. Thus, by computing the derivative with respect to
\alpha
of
(\nu_{\alpha}-m_{\alpha}^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}m_{\alpha}
and finding the zero of the gradient gives us the final form of the best scaling parameter
\alpha^{*}
:

\alpha^{*}=\frac{\eta(1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q}))\langle\tilde{\bm{w}},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle}{\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})\left(\lambda\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}\|\tilde{\bm{w}}\|^{2}-(\lambda-\eta)\langle\tilde{\bm{w}},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle^{2}\right)}

And since the worst test accuracy is
50\%
(random classification), which is obtained for
m_{\alpha}=0
, then solving the previous equation gives the worst scaling
\bar{\alpha}
to use:

\bar{\alpha}=\frac{-\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})\langle\tilde{\bm{w}},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle}

Appendix D
Extension to multi-source classifiers

Given
T
source classifiers
\{{\bm{w}}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}
and a single target task, the goal is to fine-tune a mixture of these classifiers on the target task. Specifically, we want to find the optimal fine-tuned classifier
{\bm{w}}_{\Omega}
that is written as:

{\bm{w}}_{\Omega}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}+{\bm{a}}

where
\alpha_{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
{\bm{a}}
is an adapter trained on the target dataset as follows:

{\bm{a}}=\argmin_{{\bm{v}}}\frac{1}{n}\|{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}+{\bm{v}})-{\bm{y}}\|^{2}+\gamma\|{\bm{v}}\|^{2}

Then,
{\bm{a}}
expresses as:

{\bm{a}}=\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{1}{n}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}+\gamma{\mathbf{I}}_{p}\right)^{-1}\left({\mathbf{X}}{\bm{y}}-{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{X}}^{\top}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}\right)

Thus, our new fine-tuned classifier writes as:

{\bm{w}}_{\Omega}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}+{\bm{a}}=\frac{1}{n}{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{X}}{\bm{y}}+\gamma\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{w}}_{t}

To compute the theoretical test accuracy of this classifier, we will take a test sample
{\bm{x}}\sim{\mathcal{N}}((-1)^{a}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta},{\mathbf{I}}_{p})
, independent from the training data
({\bm{x}}_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
, and we compute the statistics of the decision function
{\bm{w}}_{\Omega}^{\top}{\bm{x}}
.

D.1
Test Expectation

We have that:

\displaystyle\E[{\bm{w}}_{\Omega}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]

\displaystyle=\E[{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]+\gamma\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}\E[{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}]

\displaystyle=\E[{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]+(-1)^{a}\gamma\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}

From the previous section, we have that:

\E[{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]=\frac{(-1)^{a}}{1+\delta_{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}=\frac{(-1)^{a}\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})}

And from lemma
A.5
, we have that:

{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}=\frac{(1+\delta_{Q})\langle{\bm{w}}_{t},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle}{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})}

Finally, we get that:

\boxed{\E[{\bm{w}}_{\Omega}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]=\frac{(-1)^{a}}{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})}\left(\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}\langle{\bm{w}}_{t},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle\right)}

In a vectorized form, denote by
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{T})^{\top}
the vector of coefficients and by
{\mathbf{W}}=({\bm{w}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{w}}_{T})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{p\times T}
, then we have that:

\boxed{\E[{\bm{w}}_{\Omega}^{\top}{\bm{x}}]=(-1)^{a}\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})\bm{\alpha}^{\top}{\mathbf{W}}^{\top}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}}{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})}}

D.2
Test variance

Now we will compute the expectation of the second order moment of
{\bm{w}}_{\Omega}^{\top}{\bm{x}}
:

\displaystyle\E[({\bm{w}}_{\Omega}^{\top}{\bm{x}})^{2}]=\E\left[({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})^{2}+\gamma^{2}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}\right)^{2}+2\gamma\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}\right]

Let us compute each term of this sum and then aggregate the results at the end.

First term.

We have that:

\E[({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}})^{2}]=\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{h\lambda_{Q}}\left(\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1}{\lambda_{Q}}-2(1-h)\right)+\frac{1-h}{h}

Second term.

Now let us compute the second term of the sum:

\displaystyle\E\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}\E[{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}{\bm{x}}^{\top}{\bm{w}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}\E[{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\bm{w}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}_{i}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}y_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}]

\displaystyle({\bm{x}}_{i}\text{ i.i.d})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\delta_{Q}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}y_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}]

And since we have that:

{\mathbf{Q}}={\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}-\frac{{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}}{n(1+\delta_{Q})}

Then:

\displaystyle\E\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}\right]=\frac{1}{1+\delta_{Q}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}\E\left[\left({\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}-\frac{{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}}{n(1+\delta_{Q})}\right)\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}y_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\delta_{Q}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}y_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}]-\frac{1}{n(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}y_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}]

We have that:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}y_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}]{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}\frac{(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}^{2}}\langle{\bm{w}}_{t},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle\left(\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1\right)

And we have that:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}{\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}y_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}]=\frac{1}{n(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}y_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}\Tr({\bm{x}}_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}y_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}\Tr(\Sigma_{\beta}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}])]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}_{-i}y_{i}{\bm{x}}_{i}]\frac{1}{h}\Tr((\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}})^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1-h}{h}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-h}{h}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}\frac{(1+\delta_{Q})\langle{\bm{w}}_{t},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle}{\lambda_{Q}}

Thus the second term is given by:

\displaystyle\E\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{x}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+\delta_{Q})}{h\lambda_{Q}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}\left(\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1}{\lambda_{Q}}-(1-h)\right)\langle{\bm{w}}_{t},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+\delta_{Q})}{h\lambda_{Q}}\left(\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1}{\lambda_{Q}}-(1-h)\right)\bm{\alpha}^{\top}{\mathbf{W}}^{\top}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}

Third term.

We have that:

\displaystyle\gamma^{2}\E\left[\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\gamma^{2}\E\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}\sum_{k=1}^{T}\alpha_{k}{\bm{w}}_{k}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}\right]

\displaystyle=\gamma^{2}\sum_{t,k=1}^{T}\E[\alpha_{t}\alpha_{k}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}{\bm{x}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{w}}_{k}]

\displaystyle=\gamma^{2}\sum_{t,k=1}^{T}\E[{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{w}}_{k}]

\displaystyle=\gamma^{2}\sum_{t,k=1}^{T}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}\E[{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}{\mathbf{Q}}]{\bm{w}}_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{h}\sum_{t,k=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}\alpha_{k}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{w}}_{k}

And we have that:

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\Sigma_{\beta}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbf{Q}}\left({\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}+{\mathbf{I}}_{p}\right)\bar{\mathbf{Q}}

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}\bar{\mathbf{Q}}+\bar{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}^{2}}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}+\frac{(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}{(1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q}))^{2}}\left({\mathbf{I}}_{p}+\frac{({\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top})^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}^{2}}-\frac{2{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}}{\lambda_{Q}}\right)

Thus the last term is given by:

\displaystyle\gamma^{2}\E\left[\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}\right)^{2}\right]=\frac{\gamma^{2}(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}{h}\times

\displaystyle\sum_{t,k=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}\alpha_{k}\left[\frac{\langle{\bm{w}}_{t},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle\langle{\bm{w}}_{k},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle}{\lambda_{Q}^{2}}+\frac{1}{(1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q}))^{2}}\left(\langle{\bm{w}}_{t},{\bm{w}}_{k}\rangle+\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}\langle{\bm{w}}_{t},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle\langle{\bm{w}}_{k},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle}{\lambda_{Q}^{2}}-\frac{2\langle{\bm{w}}_{t},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle\langle{\bm{w}}_{k},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle}{\lambda_{Q}}\right)\right]

In a vectorized form, we have that:

\displaystyle\gamma^{2}\E\left[\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}{\bm{w}}_{t}^{\top}{\mathbf{Q}}{\bm{x}}\right)^{2}\right]=\frac{\gamma^{2}(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}{h}\times

\displaystyle\left[\frac{(\bm{\alpha}^{\top}{\mathbf{W}}^{\top}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta})^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}^{2}}+\frac{1}{(1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q}))^{2}}\left(\bm{\alpha}^{\top}{\mathbf{W}}^{\top}{\mathbf{W}}\bm{\alpha}+\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}(\bm{\alpha}^{\top}{\mathbf{W}}^{\top}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta})^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}^{2}}-\frac{2(\bm{\alpha}^{\top}{\mathbf{W}}^{\top}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta})^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma^{2}(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}{h}\bm{\alpha}^{\top}{\mathbf{M}}\bm{\alpha}

where:

{\mathbf{M}}=\frac{(1-h)}{\eta}{\mathbf{W}}^{\top}{\mathbf{W}}+\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{Q}^{2}}+\frac{(1-h)}{\eta\lambda_{Q}}\left(\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}}-2\right)\right){\mathbf{W}}^{\top}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}{\mathbf{W}}^{\top}

Finally gives us the expression of the second order moment of
{\bm{w}}_{\Omega}^{\top}{\bm{x}}
as follows:

\E[({\bm{w}}_{\Omega}^{\top}{\bm{x}})^{2}]=T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}

where:

\displaystyle T_{1}=\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{h\lambda_{Q}}\left(\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1}{\lambda_{Q}}-2(1-h)\right)+\frac{1-h}{h}

\displaystyle T_{2}=\frac{2\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})}{h\lambda_{Q}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}\left(\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1}{\lambda_{Q}}-(1-h)\right)\langle{\bm{w}}_{t},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle

\displaystyle T_{3}=\frac{\gamma^{2}(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}{h}\times

\displaystyle\sum_{t,k=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}\alpha_{k}\left[\frac{\langle{\bm{w}}_{t},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle\langle{\bm{w}}_{k},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle}{\lambda_{Q}^{2}}+\frac{1}{(1+\gamma(1+\delta_{Q}))^{2}}\left(\langle{\bm{w}}_{t},{\bm{w}}_{k}\rangle+\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}\langle{\bm{w}}_{t},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle\langle{\bm{w}}_{k},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle}{\lambda_{Q}^{2}}-\frac{2\langle{\bm{w}}_{t},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle\langle{\bm{w}}_{k},{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\rangle}{\lambda_{Q}}\right)\right]

Which also writes in a vectorized form:

\displaystyle T_{1}=\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{h\lambda_{Q}}\left(\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1}{\lambda_{Q}}-2(1-h)\right)+\frac{1-h}{h}

\displaystyle T_{2}=\frac{2\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})}{h\lambda_{Q}}\left(\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1}{\lambda_{Q}}-(1-h)\right)\bm{\alpha}^{\top}{\mathbf{W}}^{\top}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}

\displaystyle T_{3}=\frac{\gamma^{2}(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}}{h}\bm{\alpha}^{\top}{\mathbf{M}}\bm{\alpha}

D.3
Finding optimal
\alpha

The theoretical test accuracy writes as follows:

{\mathcal{A}}_{\text{test}}(\bm{\alpha})=\varphi\left(\frac{a_{1}+\bm{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{v}}_{1}}{\sqrt{a_{2}+\bm{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{v}}_{2}+\bm{\alpha}^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf{M}}\bm{\alpha}}}\right)

where:

\displaystyle a_{1}=\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}},\quad{\bm{v}}_{1}=\frac{\gamma(1+\delta_{Q})}{\lambda_{Q}}{\mathbf{W}}^{\top}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta},\quad a_{2}=T_{1}-a_{1}^{2}=\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}}\left(\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1}{h\lambda_{Q}}-\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}}-\frac{2(1-h)}{h}\right)+\frac{1-h}{h}

\displaystyle{\bm{v}}_{2}=\frac{(1+\delta_{Q})}{\lambda_{Q}}\left(\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}+1}{h\lambda_{Q}}-\frac{2\gamma\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}}-\frac{1-h}{h}\right){\mathbf{W}}^{\top}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta},

\displaystyle\tilde{M}=\frac{\gamma^{2}(1+\delta_{Q})^{2}(1-h)}{h}\left(\frac{1}{\eta}{\mathbf{W}}^{\top}{\mathbf{W}}+\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{Q}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\eta\lambda_{Q}}\left(\frac{\|{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}\|^{2}}{\lambda_{Q}}-2\right)\right){\mathbf{W}}^{\top}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}{\bm{\mu}}_{\beta}^{\top}{\mathbf{W}}^{\top}\right)

And therefore, since
\varphi
is non-decreasing, maximizing this test accuracy boils down to maximizing the term inside it, i.e we want to find
\bm{\alpha}^{*}
that satisfies:

\bm{\alpha}^{*}\in\argmax_{\bm{\alpha}}\frac{a_{1}+\bm{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{v}}_{1}}{\sqrt{a_{2}+\bm{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{v}}_{2}+\bm{\alpha}^{\top}\tilde{M}\bm{\alpha}}}=\argmax_{\bm{\alpha}}g(\bm{\alpha})

We compute the gradient of
g
with respect to
\bm{\alpha}
to find the extremum values of these mixing parameters:

\nabla_{\alpha}g(\bm{\alpha})=\frac{\sqrt{a_{2}+\bm{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{v}}_{2}+\bm{\alpha}\tilde{\mathbf{M}}\bm{\alpha}}\,\ {\bm{v}}_{1}-(a_{1}+\bm{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{v}}_{1})\frac{{\bm{v}}_{2}+2\tilde{\mathbf{M}}\bm{\alpha}}{\sqrt{a_{2}+\bm{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{v}}_{2}+\bm{\alpha}^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf{M}}\bm{\alpha}}}}{a_{2}+\bm{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{v}}_{2}+\bm{\alpha}^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf{M}}\bm{\alpha}}

Thus the roots
\bm{\alpha}
of
\nabla g(\bm{\alpha})
satisfy the following equation:

\boxed{(a_{2}+\bm{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{v}}_{2}+\bm{\alpha}^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf{M}}\bm{\alpha}){\bm{v}}_{1}-(a_{1}+\bm{\alpha}^{\top}{\bm{v}}_{1})({\bm{v}}_{2}+2\tilde{\mathbf{M}}\bm{\alpha})=0}

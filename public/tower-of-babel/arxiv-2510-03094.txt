Bilinear spherical maximal function with fractal dilations
Surjeet Singh Choudhary, Chun-Yen Shen, Saurabh Shrivastava

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate L p − L^{p}- boundedness of the bilinear spherical maximal function associated with a general dilation set E ⊂ ℝ + E\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{+} . We quantify the range of L p − L^{p}- boundedness in terms of a dilation-invariant notion of upper Minkowski dimension of the set E E . A particular case of this study, settles an open question of L p − L^{p}- boundedness of the lacunary bilinear spherical maximal function at borderline cases p 1 = 1 p_{1}=1 or p 2 = 1 p_{2}=1 in dimension d ≥ 4 d\geq 4 .

1.
Introduction

Let
A_{t}f(x)
denote the average of
f
on the sphere of radius
t>0
centered at
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},~d\geq 2
. It is defined by

A_{t}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}f(x-ty)d\sigma(y),

where
d\sigma
denotes the normalized surface measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}.

For a dilation set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, the corresponding spherical maximal operator is defined by

M_{E}f(x)=\sup_{t\in E}|A_{t}f(x)|.

The
L^{p}-
bounds for the maximal operator
M_{E}
have been extensively studied in the literature. Stein
[
37
]
used Fourier analytic methods to obtain optimal
L^{p}-
bounds for the maximal function
M_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}f
for
d\geq 3.
The Fourier analytic approach does not extend to resolve the problem in dimension
d=2
. Later, Bourgain
[
9
]
introduced some geometric agruments to settle the case of dimension
d=2
. We refer to the maximal function
M_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}f
as the full spherical maximal function and denote it by
M_{full}f
. At the end-point
p=\frac{d}{d-1}
, Bourgain
[
8
]
proved the restricted weak-type bounds in dimensions
d\geq 3
, while it was shown by Seeger, Tao and Wright
[
34
]
that the restricted weak-type inequality fails in dimension
d=2
.

Under restrictions on the dilation set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, the
L^{p}-
boundedness of
M_{E}
extends to a larger range of
L^{p}-
spaces. The range of
p
depends on the geometry and appropriate notion of size of the set
E
. For a general dilation set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, Seeger, Wainger and Wright
[
35
]
obtained sharp
L^{p}-
bounds for
p>p(E)
, where
p(E)
depends on the dilation-invariant notion of the Minkowski dimension. The end-point cases were resolved in
[
34
]
. A particularly interesting case arises when we consider
E
to be a lacunary set. In this case, the
L^{p}-
bounds of the operator
M_{E}
(denoted by
M_{lac}
), holds for all
p>1
, see
[
10
,
15
,
18
]
. It is conjectured that the lacunary maximal function
M_{lac}
is of weak-type
(1,1)
, however the problem remains open till date. We refer to
[
14
]
for recent developments in this direction. The sharp range of sparse bounds for
M_{full}
and
M_{lac}
was obtained by Lacey
[
27
]
using continuity estimates and
L^{p}-
improving bounds.

When
E\subset[1,2]
, the corresponding maximal operator
M_{E}
is referred to as the local maximal operator. Due to the local nature of the operator, it satisfies improving
L^{p}\to L^{q}-
bounds for some
q\geq p
. Such estimates for the local operator
M_{E}
, in terms of the upper Minkowski dimension and the Assouad spectrum of
E
, have been studied in
[
1
,
33
,
28
]
. In these results, the Fourier decay of surface measures plays a key role, and the fractal geometry of
E
is captured through the covering numbers and Minkowski dimension.

Let us recall the definition of dilation-invariant notion of the Minkowski dimension, see
[
35
]
for more details.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and
E_{k}=E\cap[2^{-k},2^{-k+1}]
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. We say
E
have upper Minkowski dimension
\beta
if

\sup_{\delta>0}\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\frac{\log N(E_{k},2^{-k}\delta)}{\log\delta^{-1}}=\beta,

where
N(T,\delta)
denotes the minimum number of
\delta-
length intervals needed to cover
T
.

Note that the definition of upper Minkowski dimension is uniform in dyadic scales.

1.1.
Bilinear spherical maximal function

The bilinear spherical maximal function associated to a given dilation set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is defined by

\mathcal{M}_{E}(f_{1},f_{2})(x):=\sup_{t\in E}|\mathcal{A}_{t}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)|,

where the bilinear spherical average
\mathcal{A}_{t}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)
is defined by

\mathcal{A}_{t}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2d-1}}f_{1}(x-ty_{1})f_{2}(x-ty_{2})\;d\sigma(y_{1},y_{2}).

The bilinear spherical averages first appeared in the work
[
20
]
.
The
L^{p}-
estimates for the full maximal operator
\mathcal{M}_{full}:=\mathcal{M}_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}
were studied in
[
2
,
22
,
24
]
. The sharp range of
L^{p}-
boundedness, including the end-point cases, were obtained by Jeong and Lee
[
26
]
for dimensions
d\geq 2
. They introduced the method of slicing and gave a simple proof of optimal
L^{p}-
estimates for the operator
\mathcal{M}_{full}
. The slicing argument provides with a decomposition of
\mathbb{S}^{2d-1}
into a family of lower dimensional spheres. More precisely, Jeong and Lee
[
26
]
showed that if
F
is a continuous function defined
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2d},d\geq 2
and
(y,z)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, then

(1.1)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2d-1}}F(y,z)\;d\sigma(y,z)=\int_{B^{d}(0,1)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}F(y,\sqrt{1-|y|^{2}}z)\;d\sigma_{d-1}(z)(1-|y|^{2})^{\frac{d-2}{2}}dy.

The formula as above yields the following pointwise estimate.

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{full}(f,g)(x)\lesssim M_{HL}f(x)M_{full}g(x),

where
M_{HL}f
is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function. Using the symmetry of the bilinear spherical maximal function in
f
and
g
, the boundedness region for the operator
\mathcal{M}_{full}
is determined by using
L^{p}-
estimates of Hardy-Littlewood maximal function
M_{HL}f(x)
and spherical maximal function
M_{full}g(x)
for
d\geq 2.
In dimension
d=1
, the optimal range of boundedness of
\mathcal{M}_{full}
was obtained in
[
13
,
16
,
3
]
.

Jeong and Lee
[
26
]
also obtained
L^{p}-
improving estimates for the local version of bilinear spherical maximal function using the slicing method. Later, in
[
3
]
, the authors improved the range of
L^{p}-
improving estimates for the local bilinear spherical maximal function. Observe that for a general dilation set
E
, the slicing argument always leads to pointwise domination of the bilinear maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{E}(f,g)
by a product of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function and the full spherical maximal function as in the estimate (
1.2
). This does not improve the range of
L^{p}-
boundedness of the operator
\mathcal{M}_{E}
. Therefore, the slicing argument has its limitations in dealing with the bilinear maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{E}(f,g)
associated with a dilation set
E
.

The
L^{p}-
estimates for the bilinear lacunary spherical maximal operator
\mathcal{M}_{lac}=:\mathcal{M}_{E}
, where
E
is a lacunary set, were obtained by Borges and Foster
[
7
]
for dimensions
d\geq 2
, and by Christ and Zhou
[
13
]
for dimension
d=1
. The proof for dimensions
d\geq 2
relies mainly on an
L^{2}\times L^{2}\rightarrow L^{1}-
decay estimate in the frequency parameter for single scale average
\mathcal{A}_{1}(f,g)
, where functions
f
or
g
are frequency localized in an appropriate annular region formed by Littlewood-Paley decomposition. Then, the desired estimates for the bilinear lacunary maximal function are obtained by a boot-strapping type argument. The decay estimate at the point
(2,2,1)
is obtained by a combination of slicing argument (
1.1
) and Fourier decay property of
\widehat{d\sigma}
. Due to unavailability of the slicing method and lack of appropriate Fourier decay of
\widehat{d\sigma}
in dimension
d=1
, it is significantly more difficult to obtain
L^{p}-
estimates for the bilinear lacunary maximal function in dimension
d=1

[
13
]
overcame these issues and proved trilinear smoothing estimates for bilinear averaging operators along certain curves, including the averages along the circle. The trilinear smoothing estimates play a key role in obtaining decay estimates for frequency localized pieces and finally obtaining the desired

2.
Main results

In this paper, we explore alternate of slicing method to obtain new results concerning
L^{p}-
boundedness of the bilinear maximal operator
\mathcal{M}_{E}
associated with general dilation set
E

(p_{1},p_{2},p)
for the

boundedness of the operator
\mathcal{M}_{E}
in terms of upper Minkowski dimension of the set
E

E
to capture the fractal geometry of the set
E

\mathcal{M}_{E}

2.1.
Strategy of proof

As indicated earlier, the slicing method does not improve the range of
L^{p}-
estimates for the bilinear spherical maximal functions associated with general dilation sets beyond that of the full maximal operator
\mathcal{M}_{full}
. We follow the following strategy to prove
Theorem

2.1
.

•

First, we perform Littlewood-Paley decomposition of both the functions in frequency space. The
L^{p}-
estimates for the maximal function acting on low frequency terms and terms with mixed type frequencies can be dealt with directly. In this case, we could dominate the maximal function by a product of Hardy-Littlewood maximal functions, see
Lemma

3.1
. This step reduces the problem to proving the desired bounds for terms with high frequencies.

•

Next, we consider the local maximal operator associated with the set
E_{0}=E\cap[1,2]
. In
Section

4
we carry out the decomposition of multiplier as used by Heo, Hong and Yang
[
24
]
.

•

We derive

L^{2}\times L^{2}\to L^{1},\quad L^{2}\times L^{1}\to L^{2/3},\quad\text{and}~L^{1}\times L^{2}\to L^{2/3}

estimates, see
Lemma

5.2
with suitable decay for the operator norm depending on the frequency parameter and the upper Minkowski content of
E_{0}
. The second estimate that we obtain is an end-point estimate for the operator. We use an appropriate space localization of the operator and establish end-point
L^{1}\times L^{1}\to L^{1/2}-
estimate, see
Lemma

5.3
. This end-point estimate has growth in terms of the frequency parameter and the number of intervals used in discretization of the
E_{0}
.

•

The interpolation argument between the growth estimate at end-point and the decay estimate yields a region of
L^{p}-
boundedness of
\mathcal{M}_{E_{0}}
depending on upper Minkowski dimension of
E_{0}
.

•

Finally, in
Section

6
, we extend the results obtained for local maximal operator to the global operators associated with
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. This part is done by using appropriate rescaling arguments and adaptation of the bilinear Calderón-Zygmund theory to the underlying situation. We exploit the methods used in
[
13
]
and
[
4
]
.

3.
Reduction using Littlewood-Paley decomposition

Consider the following partition of the identity

\displaystyle\widehat{\phi}(\xi)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\widehat{\psi}_{2^{-j}}(\xi)=1,\quad\xi\neq 0,

where
\phi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is a radial and decreasing function such that
\text{supp}(\widehat{\phi})\subseteq B(0,2)
and
\widehat{\phi}(\xi)=1
for
\xi\in B(0,1)
. We have used the notation
\widehat{\phi_{t}}(\xi)=\widehat{\phi}(t\xi)
and

\widehat{\psi}_{t}(\xi)=\widehat{\phi}(t\xi)-\widehat{\phi}(2t\xi)
.

For
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we define smooth Fourier projection operators
\widehat{P_{i}f}(\xi)=\hat{f}(\xi)\hat{\phi}(2^{-i}\xi)
and
\widehat{R_{i}f}=\hat{f}(\xi)\hat{\psi}(2^{-i}\xi)
. Using this, we can write

f=P_{i}f+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}R_{i+j}f.

Consequently, we get the following decomposition of the operator
\mathcal{M}_{E}
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{E}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sup_{t\in E_{k}}\Big|\mathcal{A}_{t}(P_{k}f_{1},P_{k}f_{2})(x)\Big|+\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sup_{t\in E_{k}}\bigg|\mathcal{A}_{t}\Big(\sum^{\infty}_{n_{1}=1}R_{k+n_{1}}f_{1},P_{k}f_{2}\Big)(x)\bigg|

\displaystyle+\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sup_{t\in E_{k}}\bigg|\mathcal{A}_{t}\Big(P_{k}f_{1},\sum^{\infty}_{n_{2}=1}R_{k+n_{2}}f_{2}\Big)(x)\bigg|+\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sup_{t\in E_{k}}\Big|\mathcal{A}_{t}\Big(\sum^{\infty}_{n_{1}=1}R_{k+n_{1}}f_{1},\sum^{\infty}_{n_{2}=1}R_{k+n_{2}}f_{2})(x)\Big)\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sup_{t\in E_{k}}\Big|\mathcal{A}_{t}(P_{k}f_{1},P_{k}f_{2})(x)\Big|+\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sup_{t\in E_{k}}\Big|\mathcal{A}_{t}(P_{k}f_{1},f_{2})(x)\Big|

(3.1)

\displaystyle+\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sup_{t\in E_{k}}\Big|\mathcal{A}_{t}(f_{1},P_{k}f_{2})(x)\Big|+\sum^{\infty}_{n_{1},n_{2}=1}\mathcal{M}^{\mathbf{n}}(f_{1},f_{2})(x),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\mathbf{n}}(f_{1},f_{2})(x):=\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sup_{t\in E_{k}}\Big|\mathcal{A}_{t}(R_{k+n_{1}}f_{1},R_{k+n_{2}}f_{2})(x)\Big|.

Denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\mathbf{n}}_{k}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)=\sup_{t\in E_{k}}\Big|\mathcal{A}_{t}(R_{k+n_{1}}f_{1},R_{k+n_{2}}f_{2})(x)\Big|.

The first three terms in (
3
) are controlled by product of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal functions, which in turn implies strong-type
L^{p}-
boundedness of
\mathcal{M}_{E}
for
p_{1},p_{2}>1
and weak-type
L^{p}-
boundedness when
p_{i}=1
. The strong-type
L^{p}-
boundedness of the operator
\mathcal{M}_{E}
in the case where
p_{i}=1
and
1<p_{j}<\infty
for
i,j\in\{1,2\},~i\neq j
, are obtained by standard interpolation argument between weak-type bounds at
\big(1,1,\frac{1}{2}\big)
and either at
(1,\infty,1)
or
(\infty,1,1)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
. The following pointwise estimates hold.

\displaystyle\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sup_{t\in E_{k}}\Big|\mathcal{A}_{t}(P_{k}f_{1},f_{2})(x)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim M_{HL}f_{1}(x)M_{HL}f_{2}(x),

\displaystyle\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sup_{t\in E_{k}}\Big|\mathcal{A}_{t}(f_{1},P_{k}f_{2})(x)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim M_{HL}f_{1}(x)M_{HL}f_{2}(x),

\displaystyle\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sup_{t\in E_{k}}\Big|\mathcal{A}_{t}(P_{k}f_{1},P_{k}f_{2})(x)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim M_{HL}f_{1}(x)M_{HL}f_{2}(x).

Proof.

Note that if
t\in E_{k}
and
|y|\leq 1
, then we have
|ty|\leq 2^{-k+1}
. Since
\phi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in E_{k}}\sup_{|y|\leq 1}|P_{k}f_{1}(x-ty)|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{|ty|\leq 2^{-k+1}}\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f_{1}(z)2^{kd}\phi(2^{k}(x-ty-z))~dz\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{|y|\leq 2}\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{2^{kd}f_{1}(z)}{\Big(4+|2^{k}(x-z)-y|\Big)^{N}}dz\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim M_{HL}f_{1}(x).

Therefore, using the slicing argument, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sup_{t\in E_{k}}\Big|\mathcal{A}_{t}(P_{k}f_{1},f_{2})(x)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sup_{t\in E_{k}}\left(\sup_{|y|\leq 1}|P_{k}f_{1}(x-ty)|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2d-1}}|f_{2}(x-tz)|d\sigma(y,z)\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim M_{HL}f_{1}(x)\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sup_{t\in E_{k}}\int_{B^{d}(0,1)}|f_{2}(x-tz)|(1-|z|^{2})^{(d-2)/2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}d\sigma(y)~dz

\displaystyle\lesssim M_{HL}f_{1}(x)M_{HL}f_{2}(x).

The remaining two estimates are deduced similarly.
∎

In view of the lemma above, we are left with proving the desired
L^{p}-
boundedness of the operator
\mathcal{M}^{\mathbf{n}}
, with a suitbale decay in
|\mathbf{n}|
, in order to complete the proof of
Theorem

2.1
.

4.
Decomposition of multiplier

Consider the multiplier form of the bilinear spherical average given by

\mathcal{A}_{t}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\widehat{f_{1}}(\xi)\widehat{f_{2}}(\eta)\widehat{d\sigma}(t\xi,t\eta)e^{2\pi ix\cdot(\xi,\eta)}\;d\xi d\eta,

where
\widehat{d\sigma}
denotes the Fourier transform of the spherical measure and has the expression

(4.1)

\widehat{d\sigma}(t\xi,t\eta)=\frac{2\pi J_{d-1}(|(t\xi,t\eta)|)}{|(t\xi,t\eta)|^{d-1}}.

Here,
J_{d-1}
denotes the Bessel function of order
d-1
. The following asymptotic estimate of the Bessel function holds

J_{d-1}(r)\sim r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(e^{2\pi ir}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{j}r^{-j}+e^{-2\pi ir}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{j}r^{-j}\bigg)\quad\text{ as }r\to\infty,

for some coefficients
a_{j}
and
b_{j}
, such that for all nonnegative integers
N
and
\alpha

(4.2)

\bigg(\frac{d}{dr}\bigg)^{\alpha}\Bigg[J_{d-1}(r)-r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(e^{2\pi ir}\sum_{j=0}^{N}a_{j}r^{-j}+e^{-2\pi ir}\sum_{j=0}^{N}a_{j}r^{-j}\bigg)\Bigg]=O\big(r^{-\alpha-\frac{N+1}{2}}\big),

as
r\to\infty
. We refer to [
[
38
]
, Chapter VIII] for details.

In view of the asymptotic estimates of Bessel functions, observe that the main term in the multiplier corresponding to the operator
\mathcal{A}_{t}
behaves like
\frac{e^{2\pi i|(t\xi,t\eta)|}}{|(t\xi,t\eta)|^{\frac{2d-1}{2}}}.
The remaining terms satisfy higher order decay and hence we need to focus on the main term only.
Therefore, we prove the desired
L^{p}-
boundedness of the maximal operator corresponding to bilinear operators defined by

T^{\mathbf{n}}_{t,k}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\widehat{R_{k+n_{1}}f_{1}}(\xi)\widehat{R_{k+n_{2}}f_{2}}(\eta)m(t\xi,t\eta)e^{2\pi it|(\xi,\eta)|}e^{2\pi ix\cdot(\xi,\eta)}\;d\xi d\eta,

where
t\in E_{k}
and
m(\xi,\eta)=|(\xi,\eta)|^{-\frac{2d-1}{2}}
.

As described previously, the
L^{p}-
estimates for the global maximal operator are obtained via suitable adaptation of bilinear Calderón-Zygmund theory. We shall restrict ourselves to the case of unit scale, that is
k=0
. The proof is easily adapted to obtain the corresponding estimates for general dyadic scale
2^{k}
.

Let us use the notation
T^{\mathbf{n}}_{t}=T^{\mathbf{n}}_{t,0},
where
t\in E_{0}
. We fix
t\in E_{0}
and work with the operator
T^{\mathbf{n}}_{t}.
Due to the lack of additional decay in
|(\xi,\eta)|
for the derivative of the term
e^{2\pi it|(\xi,\eta)|},
we need to decompose this exponential term further.

Let
N
be a positive integer such that
N\sim 2^{n_{1}}.
Write the exponential term as follows

e^{2\pi it|(\xi,\eta)|}=e^{2\pi it\Phi_{N}(\xi,\eta)}e^{2\pi it\sqrt{N^{2}+|\eta|^{2}}},

where

\Phi_{N}(\xi,\eta):=|(\xi,\eta)|-\sqrt{N^{2}+|\eta|^{2}}=\frac{(|\xi|-N)(|\xi|+N)}{\sqrt{|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}}+\sqrt{N^{2}+|\eta|^{2}}}.

Note that
\Phi_{N}
satisfies the following estimate

(4.3)

|\partial^{\alpha}_{\eta}\Phi_{N}(\xi,\eta)|\lesssim|\eta|^{-|\alpha|},

for all multi-indices
\alpha
uniformly in
\xi
when
N\leq|\xi|<N+1
.

Let
\chi_{{}_{N}}
denote the characteristic function of the interval
[N,N+1)
. Define

F_{N}f(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\chi_{{}_{N}}(|\xi|)e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}d\xi.

This gives us the following decomposition of the operator
T^{\mathbf{n}}_{t}.

\displaystyle T^{\mathbf{n}}_{t}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{N=2^{n_{1}-1}}^{2^{n_{1}+1}}T^{\mathbf{n}}_{t,N}(f_{1},f_{2})(x),

where

\displaystyle T^{\mathbf{n}}_{t,N}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}m_{t}^{\mathbf{n}}(\xi,\eta)e^{2\pi it\Phi_{N}(\xi,\eta)}\widehat{F_{N}f_{1}}(\xi)\widehat{U_{t,N}^{n_{2}}f_{2}}(\eta)e^{2\pi ix\cdot(\xi,\eta)}\;d\xi d\eta,

(4.4)

\widehat{U_{t,N}^{n_{2}}f_{2}}(\eta):=\widehat{f_{2}}(\eta)\widehat{\psi}\left(2^{-n_{2}}\eta\right)e^{2\pi it\left(\sqrt{N^{2}+|\eta|^{2}}\right)},

and

m^{\mathbf{n}}_{t}(\xi,\eta):=m(t\xi,t\eta)\widehat{\psi}\left(2^{-n_{1}}\xi\right)\widehat{\zeta}\left(2^{-n_{2}}\eta\right).

Here
\zeta\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that
\widehat{\zeta}
is supported in a small neighbourhood of
\operatorname{supp}(\psi)
and
\widehat{\zeta}(\eta)=1
for
\eta\in\operatorname{supp}(\psi)
.

Using the estimate (
4.3
), we get that

(4.5)

|\partial^{\alpha}_{\eta}\big(m^{\mathbf{n}}_{t}(\xi,\eta)e^{2\pi it\Phi_{N}(\xi,\eta)}\big)|\lesssim C_{\alpha}2^{-\frac{2d-1}{2}|\mathbf{n}|}2^{-|\alpha|n_{2}},

for all multi-indices
\alpha
uniformly in
t\in E_{0}
.

Since
\widehat{U_{t,N}^{n_{2}}f_{2}}(\eta)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}U_{t,N}^{n_{2}}f_{2}(y)e^{-2\pi i\eta\cdot y}dy
, we can rewrite

(4.6)

\displaystyle T^{\mathbf{n}}_{t,N}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\widehat{F_{N}f_{1}}(\xi)U_{t,N}^{n_{2}}f_{2}(y)\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}m^{\mathbf{n}}_{t}(\xi,\eta)e^{2\pi it\Phi_{N}(\xi,\eta)}e^{2\pi i\eta\cdot(x-y)}d\eta\Big)e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}\;d\xi dy.

Using integration by parts argument, we obtain

(4.7)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}m^{\mathbf{n}}_{t}(\xi,\eta)e^{2\pi it\Phi_{N}(\xi,\eta)}e^{2\pi i\eta\cdot(x-y)}d\eta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1+4\pi^{2}|2^{n_{2}}(x-y)|^{2})^{\lceil\frac{d}{2}\rceil+1}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(1-2^{2n_{2}}\Delta_{\eta})^{\lceil\frac{d}{2}\rceil+1}\Big(m^{\mathbf{n}}_{t}(\xi,\eta)e^{2\pi it\Phi_{N}(\xi,\eta)}\Big)e^{2\pi i\eta\cdot(x-y)}d\eta,

where we have used the fact that

(1-2^{2n_{2}}\Delta_{\eta})e^{2\pi i\eta\cdot(x-y)}=(1+4\pi^{2}|2^{n_{2}}(x-y)|^{2})e^{2\pi i\eta\cdot(x-y)}.

Substituting (
4.7
) into (
4.6
), we get

\displaystyle T^{\mathbf{n}}_{t,N}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}A_{n_{2}}\left(U_{t,N}^{n_{2}}f_{2}\right)(x,\eta)B^{\mathbf{n}}_{t,N}\left(F_{N}f_{1}\right)(x,\eta)d\eta,

where

(4.8)

\displaystyle B^{\mathbf{n}}_{t,N}\left(F_{N}f_{1}\right)(x,\eta):=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(1-2^{2n_{2}}\Delta_{\eta}\right)^{\lceil\frac{d}{2}\rceil+1}\left(m^{\mathbf{n}}_{t}(\xi,\eta)e^{2\pi it\Phi_{N}(\xi,\eta)}\right)\widehat{F_{N}f_{1}}(\xi)e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}d\xi,

and

A_{n_{2}}\left(U^{n_{2}}_{t,N}f_{2}\right)(x,\eta):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{U^{n_{2}}_{t,N}f_{2}(y)e^{2\pi i\eta\cdot(x-y)}}{\left(1+4\pi^{2}\left|2^{n_{2}}(x-y)\right|^{2}\right)^{\lceil\frac{d}{2}\rceil+1}}dy.

Note that

\displaystyle|A_{n_{2}}\left(U^{n_{2}}_{t,N}f_{2}\right)(x,\eta)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|U^{n_{2}}_{t,N}f_{2}(y)|}{\left(1+4\pi^{2}\left|2^{n_{2}}(x-y)\right|^{2}\right)^{\lceil\frac{d}{2}\rceil+1}}dy

\displaystyle:=L_{n_{2}}\left(U_{t,N}^{n_{2}}f_{2}\right)(x).

This gives us the following bound.

(4.9)

|T^{\mathbf{n}}_{t,N}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)|\leq L_{n_{2}}\left(U_{t,N}^{n_{2}}f_{2}\right)(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|B^{\mathbf{n}}_{t,N}\left(F_{N}f_{1}\right)(x,\eta)\right|d\eta.

We have the following
L^{p}-
estimates for the terms associated with operators
U_{t,N}^{n_{2}}
and
B^{\mathbf{n}}_{t,N}
introduced as above.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
a>0
and
m\in C^{d+2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2d}\setminus\{(0,\dots,0)\}
be such that

|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{\eta}^{\beta}m(\xi,\eta)|\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta}|(\xi,\eta)|^{-a}|\xi|^{-\alpha}|\eta|^{-\beta},

for all multi-indices

. For
\mathbf{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
and
N>0
, let

be defined as in

. Then, we have

\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup_{1\leq s\leq 2}\left|B^{\mathbf{n}}_{s,N}\left(f\right)(\cdot,\eta)\right|d\eta\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-|\mathbf{n}|a}2^{n_{2}d}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

(
[

]
, Lemma 2.2)
.

Let

. For

and

, let

5.
Estimate for local maximal operator

We use Bourgain’s interpolation argument to prove restricted weak-type estimates for the local maximal function
\mathcal{M}_{E_{0}}.
We state the interpolation lemma for convenience. The reader is referred to
[
30
]
for more details.

Lemma 5.1

(
[
30
]
, Lemma 2.6)
.

Let
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}>0
. Suppose that
\{T_{j}\}
is a sequence of linear (or sublinear) operators
such that for some
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
, and
1\leq q_{1},q_{2}<\infty
,

\|T_{j}(f)\|_{L^{q_{1}}}\leq M_{1}2^{\epsilon_{1}j}\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}},~~\|T_{j}(f)\|_{L^{q_{2}}}\leq M_{2}2^{-\epsilon_{2}j}\|f\|_{L^{p_{2}}}.

Then
T=\sum_{j}T_{j}
is bounded from
L^{p,1}
to
L^{q,\infty}
with

\|T(f)\|_{L^{q,\infty}}\lesssim M^{\theta}_{1}M^{1-\theta}_{2}\|f\|_{L^{p,1}},

where
\theta=\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}}
,
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{\theta}{q_{1}}+\frac{1-\theta}{q_{2}}

and
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}+\frac{1-\theta}{p_{2}}
.

Note that in view of the reduction step using Littlewood-Paley decomposition, see
Section

3
and
Lemma

3.1
, we need to prove desired estimates for the maximal operator

\mathcal{M}^{\mathbf{n}}_{0}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)=\sup_{t\in E_{0}}\Big|\mathcal{A}_{t}(R_{n_{1}}f_{1},R_{n_{2}}f_{2})(x)\Big|.

We have the following results for
\mathcal{M}^{\mathbf{n}}_{0}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
0\leq\beta\leq 1
. Then for any
\mathbf{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
, we have

(1)

\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathbf{n}}_{0}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim 2^{-|\mathbf{n}|\frac{2d-2-\beta}{2}}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}},

(2)

\left\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathbf{n}}_{0}(f_{1},f_{2})\right\|_{L^{2/3}}\lesssim 2^{-|\mathbf{n}|\frac{d-3-\beta}{2}}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{1}}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}},

(3)

\left\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathbf{n}}_{0}(f_{1},f_{2})\right\|_{L^{2/3}}\lesssim 2^{-|\mathbf{n}|\frac{d-3-\beta}{2}}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{1}}.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
0\leq\beta\leq 1
. Then for any
\mathbf{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
, we have

\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathbf{n}}_{0}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{1/2}}\lesssim 2^{|\mathbf{n}|\beta}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{1}}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{1}}.

Note that if we assume that
Lemma

5.2
and
Lemma

5.3
hold true, then bilinear analogue of real interpolation theorem, see Theorem 7.2.2 in
[
23
]
, yields strong-type
L^{p}-
estimates

\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathbf{n}}_{0}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim 2^{-|\mathbf{n}|\epsilon}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{p_{2}}},

for some
\epsilon>0
and
\big(\frac{1}{p_{1}},\frac{1}{p_{2}}\big)\in\Omega(\{O,A,Q,P,R,B\})\setminus\{A,B,PQ,PR\}
. Therefore, summing over
n_{1}
and
n_{2},
we get the desired strong-type
L^{p}-
estimates for the operator
\mathcal{M}_{E_{0}}
.

Moreover, the bilinear complex interpolation theorem applied to estimates
(1)
and
(2)
of
Lemma

5.2
gives us

\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathbf{n}}_{0}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p+2}}}\lesssim 2^{-|\mathbf{n}|\big(\frac{3d-1-\beta}{2}-\frac{d+1}{p}\big)}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{p}}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}},\quad 1\leq p\leq 2.

Finally, for the operator
\mathcal{M}_{E_{0}}
, the restricted weak-type inequality at the point
\big(\frac{1}{p_{1}},\frac{1}{p_{2}}\big)
lying on the line segment
PQ
follows by Bourgain’s interpolation
Lemma

5.1
, applied to the preceding estimate and
Lemma

5.3
. The same argument could be used to obtain restricted weak-type estimates for points on the line segment
PR
. ∎

Therefore, we are left with proving
Lemma

5.2
and
Lemma

5.3
.

5.1.
Proof of
Lemma

5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{0}
be the disjoint collection of intervals of length
2^{-|\mathbf{n}|}
covering
E_{0}
. Then, we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\mathbf{n}}_{0}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)

\displaystyle=\sup_{I\in\mathcal{E}_{0}}\sup_{t\in I}\Bigg|\mathcal{A}_{t}\Big(R_{n_{1}}\sum_{N=2^{n_{1}-1}}^{2^{n_{1}+1}}F_{N}f_{1},R_{n_{2}}f_{2}\Big)(x)\Bigg|,

where
\widehat{F_{N}(f)}=\chi_{{}_{N}}\widehat{f}
.

For
t\in I
, using the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus we get that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in I}\Big|\mathcal{A}_{t}(R_{n_{1}}F_{N}f_{1},R_{n_{2}}f_{2})(x)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}|\mathcal{A}_{s}(R_{n_{1}}F_{N}f_{1},R_{n_{2}}f_{2})(x)|^{2}\;ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\left(\int_{I}\mathcal{A}_{s}(R_{n_{1}}F_{N}f_{1},R_{n_{2}}f_{2})(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\mathcal{A}_{s}(R_{n_{1}}F_{N}f_{1},R_{n_{2}}f_{2})(x)\;ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}|\mathcal{A}_{s}(R_{n_{1}}f_{1},R_{n_{2}}f_{2})(x)|^{2}\;ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\left(\int_{I}|\mathcal{A}_{s}(R_{n_{1}}F_{N}f_{1},R_{n_{2}}f_{2})(x)|^{2}\;ds\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\int_{I}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\mathcal{A}_{s}(R_{n_{1}}F_{N}f_{1},R_{n_{2}}f_{2})(x)\right|^{2}\;ds\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Using
\ell^{2}\hookrightarrow\ell^{\infty}
embedding, we get that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\mathbf{n}}_{0}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{N=2^{n_{1}-1}}^{2^{n_{1}+1}}2^{\frac{|\mathbf{n}|}{2}}\mathcal{G}^{\mathbf{n}}_{0,N}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)+\sum_{N=2^{n_{1}-1}}^{2^{n_{1}+1}}\big(\mathcal{G}^{\mathbf{n}}_{0,N}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)\tilde{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathbf{n}}_{0,N}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)\big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathbf{n}}_{0}(f_{1},f_{2})\right\|_{L^{p}}

(5.2)

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{\frac{|\mathbf{n}|}{2}}\Bigg\|\sum_{N=2^{n_{1}-1}}^{2^{n_{1}+1}}\mathcal{G}^{\mathbf{n}}_{0,N}(f_{1},f_{2})\Bigg\|_{L^{p}}+\Bigg\|\sum_{N=2^{n_{1}-1}}^{2^{n_{1}+1}}\mathcal{G}^{\mathbf{n}}_{0,N}(f_{1},f_{2})\Bigg\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigg\|\sum_{N=2^{n_{1}-1}}^{2^{n_{1}+1}}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathbf{n}}_{0,N}(f_{1},f_{2})\Bigg\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Note that the multiplier
2^{-|\mathbf{n}|}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\widehat{\sigma}(s\xi,s\eta)
behaves like
\widehat{\sigma}(s\xi,s\eta)
where
1\leq s\leq 2
. Therefore, it is enough to prove the desired estimates for the square function
\Big(\sum\limits_{I\in\mathcal{E}_{0}}\int_{I}|\mathcal{A}_{s}(R_{n_{1}}F_{N}f_{1},R_{n_{2}}f_{2})(x)|^{2}\;ds\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.
We invoke the decomposition of the multiplier carried out in
Section

4
and use the estimate (
4.9
) to get that

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\mathbf{n}}_{0,N}(f_{1},f_{2})(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup_{1\leq s\leq 2}\left|B^{\mathbf{n}}_{s,N}\left(F_{N}f_{1}\right)(x,\eta)\right|d\eta\right)\bigg(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{E}_{0}}\int_{I}\left|L_{n_{2}}\left(U_{s,N}^{n_{2}}f_{2}\right)(x)\right|^{2}\;ds\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+error~terms.

Note that for the error terms, we have better decay in the multiplier, see (
4.2
). Thus, it is enough to prove
L^{p}-
estimates for the following operator

f_{1}\rightarrow\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup_{1\leq s\leq 2}\left|B^{\mathbf{n}}_{s,N}\left(F_{N}f_{1}\right)(x,\eta)\right|d\eta\right)

and

f_{2}\rightarrow\bigg(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{E}_{0}}\int_{I}\left|L_{n_{2}}\left(U_{s,N}^{n_{2}}f_{2}\right)(x)\right|^{2}\;ds\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Observe that using Minkowski’s integral inequality, we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{E}_{0}}\int_{I}\left|L_{n_{2}}\left(U_{s,N}^{n_{2}}f_{2}\right)(x)\right|^{2}\;ds\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq

\displaystyle 2^{|\mathbf{n}|\frac{\beta}{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\bigg(\int_{I}\left|U_{s,N}^{n_{2}}f_{2}(x-y)\right|^{2}\;ds\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{dy}{\left(1+4\pi^{2}\left|2^{n_{2}}y\right|^{2}\right)^{\lceil\frac{d}{2}\rceil+1}}.

(5.4)

\displaystyle\left\|\bigg(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{E}_{0}}\int_{I}\left|L_{n_{2}}\left(U_{s,N}^{n_{2}}f_{2}\right)\right|^{2}\;ds\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle 2^{|\mathbf{n}|\frac{\beta}{2}}\left\|\bigg(\int_{I}\big|U_{s,N}^{n_{2}}f_{2}\big|^{2}\;ds\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{dy}{\left(1+4\pi^{2}\left|2^{n_{2}}y\right|^{2}\right)^{\lceil\frac{d}{2}\rceil+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{-n_{2}d}2^{-|\mathbf{n}|\frac{1-\beta}{2}}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}}.

Further, using the estimate
5.3
along with

4.2
and Minkowski’s integral inequality, we get that

(5.5)

\displaystyle\left\|\bigg(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{E}_{0}}\int_{I}\left|L_{n_{2}}\left(U_{s,N}^{n_{2}}f_{2}\right)\right|^{2}\;ds\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim 2^{-n_{2}\frac{d}{2}}2^{-|\mathbf{n}|\frac{1-\beta}{2}}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{1}}.

Next, since
\widehat{\sigma}(s\xi,s\eta):=m(s\xi,s\eta)e^{2\pi is|(\xi,\eta)|}
with
|m(\xi,\eta)|\lesssim|(\xi,\eta)|^{-\frac{2d-1}{2}}
, by an application of
Lemma

4.1
, we obtain

(5.6)

\displaystyle 2^{-|\mathbf{n}|\frac{2d-\beta}{2}}\sum_{N=2^{n_{1}-1}}^{2^{n_{1}+1}}\|F_{N}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{-|\mathbf{n}|\frac{2d-1-\beta}{2}}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}}

and

\displaystyle\Bigg\|\sum_{N=2^{n_{1}-1}}^{2^{n_{1}+1}}\mathcal{G}^{\mathbf{n}}_{0,N}(f_{1},f_{2})\Bigg\|_{L^{2/3}}

As described earlier, similar arguments as above, yield the following bounds for square function involving the derivative term

\displaystyle\Bigg\|\sum_{N=2^{n_{1}-1}}^{2^{n_{1}+1}}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}^{\mathbf{n}}_{0,N}(f_{1},f_{2})\Bigg\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim 2^{-|\mathbf{n}|\frac{2d-3-\beta}{2}}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathbf{n}}_{0}(f_{1},f_{2})\right\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim

5.2.
Proof of

.

Let
\Psi_{2^{-i}}(w)=2^{id}(2+2^{i}|w|)^{-(d+1)}
so that
|\psi_{2^{-i}}(w)|\lesssim\Psi_{2^{-i}}(w)
. We note that
\Psi_{2^{-i}}(w_{1})\lesssim\Psi_{2^{-i}}(w_{2})
whenever
|w_{1}-w_{2}|\leq 2^{-i}
. Indeed, when
|w_{1}-w_{2}|<|w_{2}|
, we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{2^{-i}}(w_{1})

\displaystyle=\Psi_{2^{-i}}(w_{1}-w_{2}+w_{2})

\displaystyle=2^{id}(2+2^{i}|w_{1}-w_{2}+w_{2}|)^{-(d+1)}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{id}(2+2^{i}(|w_{2}|-|w_{1}-w_{2}|))^{-(d+1)}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{id}(1+2^{i}|w_{2}|)^{-(d+1)}=\Psi_{2^{-i}}(w_{2}).

On the hand hand, when
|w_{2}|\leq|w_{1}-w_{2}|\leq 2^{-i}
, we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{2^{-i}}(w_{1})

\displaystyle\leq 2^{id}(2+2^{i}(|w_{1}-w_{2}|-|w_{2}|))^{-(d+1)}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{id}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{id}(2+2^{i}|w_{2}|)^{-(d+1)}=\Psi_{2^{-i}}(w_{2}).

Next, we consider the partition
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}=\cup_{Q\in\mathfrak{C}}Q
, where
Q\in\mathfrak{C}
are cubes with unit sidelengths and sides parallel to co-ordinate axes. For
a<0,
let
aQ
denote the cube which is concentric with
Q
and have sidelength
a>0
. With this we can write

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathbf{n}}_{0}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{1/2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\Bigg(\sup\limits_{t\in E_{0}}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2d-1}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\in\mathfrak{C}}(f_{1}*\psi_{2^{-n_{1}}})\chi_{{}_{Q}}\bigg)(x-ty)(f_{2}*\psi_{2^{-n_{2}}})(x-tz)\;d\sigma(y,z)\bigg|\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathfrak{C}}\int_{3Q}\bigg(\sup\limits_{t\in E_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2d-1}}\big((|f_{1}|*\Psi_{2^{-n_{1}}})\chi_{{}_{Q}}\big)(x-ty)\big((|f_{2}|*\Psi_{2^{-n_{2}}})\chi_{{}_{5Q}}\big)(x-tz)\;d\sigma(y,z)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}dx.

Let
I\in\mathcal{E}_{0}
be any interval. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
I=[t_{0}-2^{-|\mathbf{n}|-1},t_{0}+2^{-|\mathbf{n}|-1}]
. For any

and
|y|\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle|f_{1}|*\Psi_{2^{-n_{1}}}(x-ty)

\displaystyle=|f_{1}|*\Psi_{2^{-n_{1}}}(x-t_{0}y+(t_{0}-t)y)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|f_{1}|(z)\Psi_{2^{-n_{1}}}(x-t_{0}y+(t_{0}-t)y-z)\;dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|f_{1}|(z)\Psi_{2^{-n_{1}}}(x-t_{0}y-z)\;dz

\displaystyle=|f_{1}|*\Psi_{2^{-n_{1}}}(x-t_{0}y),

where we have used the fact that
\Psi_{2^{-n_{1}}}(w_{1})\lesssim\Psi_{2^{-n_{1}}}(w_{2})
when
|w_{1}-w_{2}|\leq 2^{-|\mathbf{n}|}\leq 2^{-n_{1}}
in the last inequality. Similarly, for any

and

, we have

Using Hölder’s inequality and the estimates above, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathbf{n}}_{0}(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{1/2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{Q\in\mathfrak{C}}\bigg(\int_{3Q}\sum_{I\in\mathcal{E}_{0}}\sup_{t\in I}\int_{\mathbf{S}^{2d-1}}\left((|f_{1}|*\Psi_{2^{-n_{1}}})\chi_{{}_{Q}}\right)(x-ty)\left((|f_{2}|*\Psi_{2^{-n_{2}}})\chi_{{}_{5Q}}\right)(x-tz)\;d\sigma(y,z)\;dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{Q\in\mathfrak{C}}\bigg(\int_{3Q}\sum_{I\in\mathcal{E}_{0}}\int_{\mathbf{S}^{2d-1}}\left((|f_{1}|*\Psi_{2^{-n_{1}}})\chi_{{}_{2Q}}\right)(x-t_{0}y)\left((|f_{2}|*\Psi_{2^{-n_{2}}})\chi_{{}_{6Q}}\right)(x-t_{0}z)\;d\sigma(y,z)\;dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{Q\in\mathfrak{C}}\bigg(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{E}_{0}}\big\|(|f_{1}|*\Psi_{2^{-n_{1}}})\chi_{{}_{2Q}}\big\|_{L^{1}}\;\big\|(|f_{2}|*\Psi_{2^{-n_{2}}})\chi_{{}_{6Q}}\big\|_{L^{1}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{|\mathbf{n}|\frac{\beta}{2}}\bigg(\sum_{Q\in\mathfrak{C}}\|(|f_{1}|*\Psi_{2^{-n_{1}}})\chi_{{}_{2Q}}\|_{L^{1}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\sum_{Q\in\mathfrak{C}}\|(|f_{2}|*\Psi_{2^{-n_{2}}})\chi_{{}_{6Q}}\|_{L^{1}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{|\mathbf{n}|\frac{\beta}{2}}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}},

where we have used the

boundedness of the single bilinear spherical average

obtained in

]
in the third step and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in the fourth step of the equation above. This completes the proof of

2^{-k}
and having sides parallel to the coordinate axes. Then

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathbf{n}}_{k}(h^{i_{1}}_{1},h^{i_{2}}_{2})\|_{L^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{F})}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sup\limits_{t\in E_{k}}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2d-1}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{Q\in\mathfrak{C}}(h^{i}_{1}*\psi_{2^{-k-n_{1}}})\chi_{{}_{Q}}\bigg)(x-ty)(f_{2}*\psi_{2^{-k-n_{2}}})(x-tz)\;d\sigma(y,z)\bigg|\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{Q\in\mathfrak{C}}\int_{3Q\setminus\mathcal{F}}\bigg(\sup\limits_{t\in E_{k}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2d-1}}\big((|h^{i_{1}}_{1}|*\Psi_{2^{-k-n_{1}}})\chi_{{}_{Q}}\big)(x-ty)\big((|h^{i_{2}}_{2}|*\Psi_{2^{-k-n_{2}}})\chi_{{}_{5Q}}\big)(x-tz)\;d\sigma(y,z)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}dx.

where
\Psi(w)=(2+|w|)^{-N}
so that
|\psi(w)|\lesssim\Psi(w)
.
Recall that
h^{i_{1}}_{1}=\sum\limits_{l(Q_{\gamma_{1}})=2^{-i_{1}}}h_{\gamma_{1}}
. Then, for
y^{\prime}\in\operatorname{supp}(h_{\gamma_{1}})
and
x\notin\mathcal{F}
, we have

dist(x-ty,y^{\prime})\geq 2^{2-i_{1}}\quad\text{for all }t\in E_{k},~|y|\leq 1,

as
2^{-k}<2^{-i_{1}}
.

We define
\Phi_{2^{-k-n_{1}}}(w)=\Psi_{2^{-k-n_{1}}}(w)\chi_{\{|w|\geq 2^{-i_{1}}\}}(w)
. Therefore, when
x\notin\mathcal{F}
, we have

|h^{i_{1}}_{1}|*\Psi_{2^{-k-n_{1}}}(x-ty)\lesssim|h^{i_{1}}_{1}|*\Phi_{2^{-k-n_{1}}}(x-ty)\quad\text{for all }t\in E_{k},~|y|\leq 1.

Recall that for any
N>d
, we have

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{2^{-n_{1}-k}}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{(N-d)(i_{1}-n_{1}-k)}.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{k}
be the collection of disjoint intervals of length
2^{-k-|\mathbf{n}|}
covering
E_{k}
. Recall that

\Psi_{2^{-k-n_{1}}}(w_{1})\lesssim\Psi_{2^{-k-n_{1}}}(w_{2}),~\text{ whenever}~|w_{1}-w_{2}|\leq 2^{-k-|\mathbf{n}|}.

Now, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathbf{n}}_{k}(h^{i_{1}}_{1},h^{i_{2}}_{2})\|_{L^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{F})}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{Q\in\mathfrak{C}}2^{-\frac{kd}{2}}\bigg(\int_{3Q\setminus\mathcal{F}}\sum_{I\in\mathcal{E}_{k}}\sup_{t\in I}\int_{\mathbf{S}^{2d-1}}\left((|h^{i}_{1}|*\Psi_{2^{-k-n_{1}}})\chi_{{}_{Q}}\right)(x-ty)

\displaystyle\hskip 125.19194pt\times\left((|f_{2}|*\Psi_{2^{-k-n_{2}}})\chi_{{}_{5Q}}\right)(x-tz)\;d\sigma(y,z)\;dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{Q\in\mathfrak{C}}2^{-\frac{kd}{2}}\bigg(\int_{3Q}\sum_{I\in\mathcal{E}_{k}}\int_{\mathbf{S}^{2d-1}}\left((|h^{i_{1}}_{1}|*\Phi_{2^{-k-n_{1}}})\chi_{{}_{2Q}}\right)(x-t_{0}y)

\displaystyle\hskip 125.19194pt\times\left((|h^{i_{2}}_{2}|*\Psi_{2^{-k-n_{2}}})\chi_{{}_{6Q}}\right)(x-t_{0}z)\;d\sigma(y,z)\;dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\frac{kd}{2}}\sum_{Q\in\mathfrak{C}}\bigg(\sum_{I\in\mathcal{E}_{k}}t_{0}^{-d}\big\|(|h^{i_{1}}_{1}|*\Phi_{2^{-k-n_{1}}})\chi_{{}_{2Q}}\big\|_{L^{1}}\;\big\|(|h^{i_{2}}_{2}|*\Psi_{2^{-k-n_{2}}})\chi_{{}_{6Q}}\big\|_{L^{1}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-\frac{kd}{2}}2^{|\mathbf{n}|\frac{\beta}{2}}2^{\frac{kd}{2}}\bigg(\sum_{Q\in\mathfrak{C}}\|(|h^{i_{1}}_{1}|*\Phi_{2^{-n_{1}}})\chi_{{}_{2Q}}\|_{L^{1}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\sum_{Q\in\mathfrak{C}}\|(|h^{i_{2}}_{2}|*\Psi_{2^{-n_{2}}})\chi_{{}_{6Q}}\|_{L^{1}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{|\mathbf{n}|\frac{\beta}{2}}2^{(N-d)\frac{i_{1}-k-n_{1}}{2}}\|h^{i_{1}}_{1}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|h^{i_{2}}_{2}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}},

where we have used
L^{1}\times L^{1}\to L^{1}-
boundedness of single scale bilinear spherical average in the third inequality and Hölder’s inequality in the fourth inequality in the expression above.

Since
i_{1}<k
, we have
2^{(N-d)\frac{i_{1}-k-n_{1}}{2}}\leq 2^{\frac{i_{1}-k}{2}}
for every
N\geq d+1
. We choose
N=d+1
to obtain

\|\mathcal{M}^{\mathbf{n}}_{k}(h^{i_{1}}_{1},h^{i_{2}}_{2})\|_{L^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\mathcal{F})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim 2^{\frac{i_{1}-k}{2}}2^{|\mathbf{n}|\frac{\beta}{2}}\|h^{i_{1}}_{1}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|h^{i_{2}}_{2}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Interchanging the roles of the
i_{1}
and
i_{2}
proves the desired bound for
i_{2}
. This completes the proof of
Lemma

6.4
. Consequently, the proof of part
(3)
of
Lemma

6.2
is completed.
∎

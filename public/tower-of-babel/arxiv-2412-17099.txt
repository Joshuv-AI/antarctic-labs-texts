Additive and Multiplicative Coinvariant Spaces of Weyl Groups in the Light of Harmonics and Graded Transfer
Sebastian Debus, Tobias Metzlaff

Abstract
. The action of a Weyl group on the associated weight lattice induces an additive action on the symmetric algebra and a multiplicative action on the group algebra of the lattice. We show that the coinvariant space of the multiplicative action affords the regular representation and is isomorphic to a space of multiplicative harmonics, which corresponds to existing results for additive coinvariants of reflection groups. We then design an algorithm to compute a multiplicative coinvariant basis from an additive one. The algorithm preserves isotypic decomposition and graded structure and enables the study of multiplicative coinvariants by integrating combinatorial knowledge from the additive setting. We investigate the Weyl groups of type A and C to find new explicit equivariant maps and combinatorial structure.



Introduction

The linear action of a finite group
\mathcal{W}
on a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\Omega
induces
an additive action on the symmetric algebra
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
and
a multiplicative action on the group algebra
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
.
Of particular interest among these groups are the Weyl groups
\mathcal{W}
of crystallographic root systems that leave the corresponding weight lattice
\Omega
stable.
The present article deals with the space of multiplicative coinvariants
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]_{\mathcal{W}}
.
For Weyl groups, we show that
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]_{\mathcal{W}}
affords the regular representation of
\mathcal{W}

(
Theorem

2.4
)
and is isomorphic to a space of multiplicative
\mathcal{W}
-harmonics
(
Theorem

3.2
).
Subsequently, we present an algorithmic method to transform
an additive symmetry adapted coinvariant basis for
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)_{\mathcal{W}}

into a multiplicative one via equivariant graded transfer
(
Theorem

5.2
,
Algorithm

2
).

Weight lattices with Weyl group symmetry appear in the representation theory of semi-simple Lie algebras
[
4
,
5
]

and provide optimal configurations for a variety of computational problems,
such as sampling
[
32
]
,
interpolation
[
33
,
36
,
30
]
,
and sphere packing
[
11
,
50
]
.
Algebraic symmetry reduction techniques simplify computations through the elimination of redundant copies of irreducible
\mathcal{W}
-modules,
see for example
[
20
,
15
,
17
]
.

The coinvariant space
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)_{\mathcal{W}}
of the additive action,
which is the quotient of
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
modulo the ideal generated by the homogeneous invariants of positive degree, is well understood.
A striking result of Chevalley
[
8
]
states that
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)_{\mathcal{W}}
is isomorphic to the regular representation of
\mathcal{W}
.
As such, the coinvariant space gives meaning to the regular representation as a graded
\mathcal{W}
-module.

Finding a symmetry adapted basis for
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)_{\mathcal{W}}
,
which respects the isotypic decomposition of the associated group representation,
has been studied extensively in algebraic combinatorics.
For symmetric groups (Weyl groups of type
\mathrm{A}_{n-1}
),
a basis can be constructed from Young tableaux and higher Specht polynomials

[
18
,
51
]
.
Bitableaux combinatorics can furthermore be used to describe coinvariants of
hyperoctahedral groups (type
\mathrm{B}_{n}
and
\mathrm{C}_{n}
) and
certain subgroups of index
2
(type
\mathrm{D}_{n}
)
[
1
,
38
]
.
Recently, there has been an interest in finding symmetry adapted bases or so-called higher Specht bases for the coinvariant spaces of diagonal actions of
the symmetric group and Garsia-Procesi modules
[
21
,
22
]
.

This extensive study for additive coinvariants raises questions concerning the second action,
namely the multiplicative action of the Weyl group
\mathcal{W}
on the group algebra
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
.
The invariant ring
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]^{\mathcal{W}}
is, as an algebra, characterized by theorems of Bourbaki and Farkas
[
4
,
16
]
.
Furthermore, the role of multiplicative invariants in geometry was investigated by Demazure
[
13
]
and in following works
[
19
,
24
,
2
,
27
]
.
However, to the knowledge of the authors, the multiplicative coinvariant space
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]_{\mathcal{W}}
has by far not received the same amount of attention as the additive one.

The proof that
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]_{\mathcal{W}}
affords the regular representation
(
Theorem

2.4
),
begins with a translation of fundamental invariants for
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]^{\mathcal{W}}

by a generic parameter
c
that allows us to apply a result from
[
29
]

and follow similar arguments as in the additive setting.
Throughout,
we require Steinberg’s result that
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]

is a free
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]^{\mathcal{W}}
-module of rank
|\mathcal{W}|

whenever
\mathcal{W}
is a Weyl group and
\Omega
the weight lattice
[
48
]
.

Our notion of multiplicative
\mathcal{W}
-harmonics comes with the definition of derivations on
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
.
We show that the resulting Jacobian determinant of fundamental invariants is determined by the Weyl denominator,
which is based on the partial ordering associated to a root system
[
4
]
and Weyl’s character formula
[
5
]
,
again very specific for Weyl groups.
The subsequent proof that also the space of multiplicative
\mathcal{W}
-harmonics affords the regular representation (and is therefore isomorphic to the coinvariant space) follows an iterative argument (
Theorem

3.2
).

A straightforward computation of a multiplicative coinvariant basis,
for example, by constructing a Gröbner bases
and deriving the normal set,
does not necessarily reflect these representation theoretic properties,
see
Example

2.5
.
As described above, the additive setting has been extensively studied,
but a distinguishing difference is that
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]

does not feature an
\mathbb{N}
-grading by polynomial degree compared to
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
.

For a transfer,
we therefore start from the work of
[
2
]

that considers the associated graded algebras.
The obtained
Algorithm

2
is based on an equivariant isomorphism between the graded components (
Proposition

4.3
) and an identification of the coinvariants in
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
with a quotient of the associated graded algebra (
Proposition

5.1
).
The algorithm preserves isotypic decomposition and graded structure.

Finally, we conclude with case studies for the Weyl groups associated to the Lie algebras of types
\mathrm{A}_{n-1}
and
\mathrm{C}_{n}
, which feature explicit equivariant homomorphisms, and compare the obtained bases.

All vector spaces and algebras are defined over the complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
unless explicitly stated otherwise.
The nonnegative integers
\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
are denoted by
\mathbb{N}
.

1.
Additive and Multiplicative Actions

Let
\Omega
be a free
\mathbb{Z}
-module of finite rank
n

and
\mathcal{W}
be a finite subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(\Omega)
.

Denote by
V=\Omega\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
the vector space generated by
\Omega
.
The symmetric algebra
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
is the quotient of the tensor algebra
\mathbb{C}\oplus V\oplus(V\otimes V)\oplus\ldots

modulo the relations
\mu_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\mu_{\ell}\equiv\mu_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\ldots\otimes\mu_{\sigma(\ell)}
for all permutations
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{\ell}
.
The
additive action
of
\mathcal{W}
on
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
is the linear action

(1.1)

\mathcal{W}\times\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)\to\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega),\,(s,\mu_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\mu_{\ell})\mapsto s\cdot(\mu_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\mu_{\ell}):=s(\mu_{1})\otimes\ldots\otimes s(\mu_{\ell}).

When a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
\Omega=\mathbb{Z}\omega_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbb{Z}\omega_{n}
is fixed,
then
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)=\mathbb{C}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
is a polynomial ring with indeterminates
X_{i}:=\omega_{i}
.
In particular, we have
s\cdot X_{i}=s_{1,i}\,X_{1}+\ldots+s_{n,i}\,X_{n}
, where
s_{\ell,i}\in\mathbb{Z}
are the matrix entries of
s\in\mathcal{W}
.

The elements of
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
, which are invariant under this action,
form the
additive invariant ring

\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)^{\mathcal{W}}
.
By Hilbert’s finiteness theorem,
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)^{\mathcal{W}}
is a finitely generated algebra, that is,

\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)^{\mathcal{W}}=\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{a}}]
for a minimal set of fundamental invariants
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{a}}
.
We use the subscript “
\mathrm{a}
” to indicate objects in the additive setting.

Denote by
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{a}}:=(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{a}})
the

additive Hilbert ideal
, that is,
the ideal in
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
generated by
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{a}}
.
The
additive coinvariant space
is the
\mathcal{W}
-module

(1.2)

\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)_{\mathcal{W}}:=\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)/\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{a}}.

We shall now recall a theorem that tells us more about the properties of the objects defined so far.
A
pseudoreflection

s
is an automorphism on
V
, such that
\mathrm{codim}(\ker(\mathrm{id}_{V}-s))=1
,
that is, the fixed point space has codimension
1
.
A finite group generated by pseudoreflections is called a
complex reflection group
.

Recall that a
\mathcal{W}
-module
is a vector space
\tilde{V}
together with
a group homomorphism
\rho_{\tilde{V}}:\mathcal{W}\to\mathrm{GL}(\tilde{V})
,
called
representation
.
Note that
\mathcal{W}
acts on itself and hence on its group algebra
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{W}]

by left multiplication
\mathcal{W}\times\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{W}]\to\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{W}],\,(s,\sum_{t}c_{t}\,t)\mapsto\sum_{t}c_{t}\,st
.
The corresponding group homomorphism
\mathcal{W}\to\mathrm{GL}(\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{W}])

is the
regular representation of
\mathcal{W}
.
A
\mathcal{W}
-module homomorphism
or
equivariant map
between two

\mathcal{W}
-modules
\tilde{V},\tilde{W}
is a vector space homomorphisms

\phi:\tilde{V}\to\tilde{W}
with
\phi\circ\rho_{\tilde{V}}(s)=\rho_{\tilde{W}}(s)\circ\phi

for all
s\in\mathcal{W}
.
We say that a
\mathcal{W}
-module
\tilde{V}

affords the regular representation
if there is a

\mathcal{W}
-module isomorphism between
\tilde{V}
and
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{W}]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Shephard-Todd
[
46
]
, Chevalley
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be a complex reflection group.
Then the following statements hold.

(1)

\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)^{\mathcal{W}}
is a polynomial algebra with Krull-dimension
n
.

(2)

\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
is a free
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)^{\mathcal{W}}
-module of rank
|\mathcal{W}|
.

(3)

\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)_{\mathcal{W}}
affords the regular representation of
\mathcal{W}
.

Thanks to statement (1) we can consider the Jacobian determinant
D
of the map
\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
defined by a set of (homogeneous) fundamental invariants
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{a}}
with cardinality
n
.
The vector space
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{a}}
generated by the partial derivatives of the Jacobian determinant

\{\partial_{i_{1}}\ldots\partial_{i_{\ell}}D\,|\,\ell\in\mathbb{N},\,1\leq i_{1}\leq\ldots\leq i_{\ell}\leq n\}

is called the space of
additive
\mathcal{W}
-harmonics
.

Theorem 1.2

(Steinberg
[
47
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be a complex reflection group.
Then
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)_{\mathcal{W}}
is isomorphic as a
\mathcal{W}
-module to
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{a}}
.

We now introduce the second action.
The group algebra
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
is the commutative algebra with vector space basis

\mathfrak{e}^{\Omega}:=\{\mathfrak{e}^{\mu}\,|\,\mu\in\Omega\}
and multiplication
\mathfrak{e}^{\mu}\,\mathfrak{e}^{\nu}=\mathfrak{e}^{\mu+\nu}
.
The
multiplicative action
of
\mathcal{W}
on
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
is the linear action

(1.3)

\mathcal{W}\times\mathbb{C}[\Omega]\to\mathbb{C}[\Omega],\,(s,\mathfrak{e}^{\mu})\mapsto s\cdot\mathfrak{e}^{\mu}:=\mathfrak{e}^{s(\mu)}.

When a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
\Omega=\mathbb{Z}\omega_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbb{Z}\omega_{n}
is fixed,
then
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]=\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
is a Laurent polynomial ring
with indeterminates
x_{i}:=\mathfrak{e}^{\omega_{i}}
.
In particular, we have
s\cdot x_{i}=x_{1}^{s_{1,i}}\cdots x_{n}^{s_{n,i}}
.

The elements of
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
, which are invariant under this action,
form the
multiplicative invariant ring

\mathbb{C}[\Omega]^{\mathcal{W}}
.
This is a finitely generated algebra, that is,
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]^{\mathcal{W}}=\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}]

for a minimal set of fundamental invariants
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}

[
34
]
.
We use the subscript “
\mathrm{m}
” to indicate objects in the multiplicative setting.

The notion “multiplicative” refers to the fact that
\mathfrak{e}^{\Omega}
is a multiplicative group in
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
,
where the inverse of
\mathfrak{e}^{\mu}
is
\mathfrak{e}^{-\mu}
.
On the other hand,
V=\spn_{\mathbb{C}}\Omega
is an additive group in
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
.

Denote by
\mathfrak{h}=\Omega\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
the real vector space generated by
\Omega
.
A
crystallographic root system

\mathrm{R}
in
\mathfrak{h}
is a subset of
\mathfrak{h}
, such that

(R1)

\mathrm{R}
is finite, does not contain
0
and generates
\mathfrak{h}
as an
\mathbb{R}
-vector space;

(R2)

For every
root

\rho\in\mathrm{R}
,
there exists a
coroot

\rho^{\vee}
inside the dual space
\mathfrak{h}^{\vee}
,
such that
\rho^{\vee}(\rho)=2
and the reflection

s_{\rho}:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{h},\,\mu\mapsto\mu-\rho^{\vee}(\mu)\,\rho
leaves
\mathrm{R}
stable;

(R3)

For all
\rho,\tilde{\rho}\in\mathrm{R}
, we have
\rho^{\vee}(\tilde{\rho})\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The
weight lattice
of
\mathrm{R}
is the set of all
\mu\in\mathfrak{h}
,
such that, for all
\rho\in\mathrm{R}
, we have
\rho^{\vee}(\mu)\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Furthermore, the
Weyl group
of
\mathrm{R}
is the group generated by all the reflections
s_{\rho}
and leaves the weight lattice stable.

Theorem 1.3

(Bourbaki
[
4
]
, Steinberg
[
48
]
, Farkas
[
16
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be the Weyl group of a crystallographic root system with weight lattice
\Omega
.
Then the following statements hold.

(1)

\mathbb{C}[\Omega]^{\mathcal{W}}
is a polynomial algebra with Krull dimension
n
.

(2)

\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
is a free
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]^{\mathcal{W}}
-module of rank
|\mathcal{W}|
.

Note that statements (1) and (2) correspond to those in
Theorem

1.1
.
The hypothesis however is more restrictive,
since Weyl groups form a proper subclass of the complex reflection groups, see
[
46
]
.
Note that there are also lattices associated to complex root systems
[
41
]
,
but the authors are not aware of a generalization of
Theorem

1.3
in this context.

Example 1.4
.

The lattice
\Omega=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is the weight lattice of the root system
\mathrm{C}_{2}
in
\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
fundamental weights
\omega_{1}=e_{1},\omega_{2}=e_{1}+e_{2}
and
Weyl group
\mathcal{W}\cong\mathfrak{S}_{2}\wr\{\pm 1\}
of order
8
,
see
[
4
, Pl. III]
.
We choose between two
\mathbb{Z}
-bases for the weight lattice, namely
\Omega=\mathbb{Z}\,e_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\,e_{2}=\mathbb{Z}\omega_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\omega_{2}
, to emphasize that the choice matters for computations.

In the standard basis
e_{1},e_{2}
, the Weyl group is generated by

\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}

with an additive action on
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)=\mathbb{C}[Y_{1},Y_{2}]
and a multiplicative action on
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]=\mathbb{C}[y_{1}^{\pm 1},y_{2}^{\pm 1}]
.
The fundamental invariants are
Y_{1}^{2}+Y_{2}^{2},Y_{1}^{2}\,Y_{2}^{2}
and

y_{1}+y_{1}^{-1}+y_{2}+y_{2}^{-1},(y_{1}+y_{1}^{-1})\,(y_{2}+y_{2}^{-1})
respectively.

On the other hand, in the basis of fundamental weights
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
,
the Weyl group is generated by

\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}

with an additive action on
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)=\mathbb{C}[X_{1},X_{2}]
and a multiplicative action on
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]=\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},x_{2}^{\pm 1}]
.
We have
X_{1}=Y_{1},X_{2}=Y_{1}+Y_{2}
and
x_{1}=y_{1},x_{2}=y_{1}\,y_{2}
.
In particular, the fundamental invariants are
2\,X_{1}^{2}-2\,X_{1}\,X_{2}+X_{2}^{2},X_{1}^{4}-2\,X_{1}^{3}\,X_{2}+X_{1}^{2}\,X_{2}^{2}
and

x_{1}+x_{1}^{-1}+x_{1}^{-1}x_{2}+x_{1}x_{2}^{-1},x_{2}+x_{2}^{-1}+x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{-1}+x_{1}^{-2}x_{2}
respectively.

As a convention, we always use the variables
X_{i}
for
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
and
x_{i}^{\pm 1}
for
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
,
if the basis for
\Omega
is that of fundamental weights of a crystallographic root system,
while we use
Y_{i}
and
y_{i}^{\pm 1}
for the standard basis as in
Example

1.4
.

2.
Multiplicative Coinvariant Spaces

Let
\mathcal{W}
be the Weyl group of a crystallographic root system with weight lattice
\Omega
and
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}=\{\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n}\}

be a set of fundamental invariants for the multiplicative invariant ring
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]^{\mathcal{W}}
.
Denote by
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}:=(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}})
the

multiplicative Hilbert ideal
, that is,
the ideal in
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
generated by
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}
.
The
multiplicative coinvariant space
is the
\mathcal{W}
-module

(2.1)

\mathbb{C}[\Omega]_{\mathcal{W}}:=\mathbb{C}[\Omega]/\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}.

In this section, we give a geometric proof that statement (3) in
Theorem

1.1

also holds for multiplicative invariants,
that is,
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]_{\mathcal{W}}
affords the regular representation.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
c=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
the set
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}-c:=\{\theta_{1}-c_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n}-c_{n}\}

is also a set of fundamental invariants for
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]^{\mathcal{W}}
.

Proof.

We have
{\theta}_{i}-c_{i}=1\cdot{\theta}_{i}-c_{i}\cdot 1\in\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}]=\mathbb{C}[\Omega]^{\mathcal{W}}

and, conversely,
{\theta}_{i}=1\cdot({\theta}_{i}-c_{i})+c_{i}\cdot 1\in\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}-c]
.
∎

Fix a monomial ordering on
\mathfrak{e}^{\Omega}
and let
I
be any ideal in
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
with Gröbner basis
G

(in practice, one defines
I
in
\mathbb{C}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n},\tilde{y}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{y}_{n}]
and computes a Gröbner basis for
I+(y_{i}\tilde{y}_{i}-1)_{1\leq i\leq n}
with respect to an ordering
y_{i}\succ\tilde{y}_{i}
).
A normal set for
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]/I
is a finite set of normal forms in
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
with respect to
G
,
such that their equivalence classes form a vector space basis for
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]/I
.
We do not assume that a normal set consists only of monomials.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
c\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
the vector space dimension of
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]/(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}-c)
is
|\mathcal{W}|
and,
if
\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{|\mathcal{W}|}\}\in\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
is a normal set for

\mathbb{C}[\Omega]_{\mathcal{W}}=\mathbb{C}[\Omega]/\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}
,
then it is also a normal set for
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]/(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}-c)

and vice versa.

Proof.

By
Theorem

1.3
and
Lemma

2.1
,
there are
h_{1},\ldots,h_{|\mathcal{W}|}\in\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
, such that

\mathbb{C}[\Omega]=\bigoplus_{\ell=1}^{|\mathcal{W}|}h_{\ell}\cdot\mathbb{C}[\Omega]^{\mathcal{W}}=\bigoplus_{\ell=1}^{|\mathcal{W}|}h_{\ell}\cdot\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}-c].

Hence, for any

f=\sum_{\ell}h_{\ell}\,g_{\ell}(\theta_{1}-c_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n}-c_{n})\in\mathbb{C}[\Omega]

with
g_{\ell}\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]
,
we have
f-\sum_{\ell}h_{\ell}\,g_{\ell}(0)\in(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}-c)
.
Since the
h_{\ell}
are linearly independent in the quotient, the statement follows.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

For
c\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
generic,
the ideal
(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}-c)
in
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
is radical and

\mathbb{C}[\Omega]/(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}-c)
affords the regular representation of
\mathcal{W}
.

Proof.

Let
s\in\mathcal{W}
and
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in(\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\})^{n}

be an element of the algebraic torus.
Define a left group action
(s,z)\mapsto s\star z

of
\mathcal{W}
on
(\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\})^{n}
by

(s\star z)_{i}:=z_{1}^{\alpha_{i,1}}\cdots z_{n}^{\alpha_{i,n}},\quad\alpha_{i,j}\in\mathbb{Z},\quad s^{-1}(\omega_{i})=\alpha_{i,1}\,\omega_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{i,n}\,\omega_{n}.

This induces the multiplicative action of
\mathcal{W}
on
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
,
that is,
s\cdot f(z)=f(s^{-1}\star z)
.
Denote by
V_{c}
the set of all points in
(\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\})^{n}
,
on which all Laurent polynomials
f\in(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}-c)
vanish.
Since the
c_{i}
are generic, this set is nonempty.
By
[
29
, Prop. 3.1]
,

V_{c}
is the orbit
\mathcal{W}\star z
of a distinguished point

\tilde{z}\in(\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\})^{n}
.
In particular, the orbit has full length,
that is,
|\mathcal{W}|=|\mathcal{W}\star\tilde{z}|
,
and
(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}-c)
is zero-dimensional.
We have

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}[\Omega]/(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}-c))\geq|V_{c}|=|\mathcal{W}\star\tilde{z}|=|\mathcal{W}|

and equality holds if and only if
(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}-c)
is radical.
With
Lemma

2.2
,
we have
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}[\Omega]/(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}-c))=|\mathcal{W}|
,
which shows that
(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}-c)
is indeed radical.

Now,
the coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]/(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}-c)
of
V_{c}

corresponds to the algebra
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{W}\star\tilde{z}]

of regular functions on
\mathcal{W}\star\tilde{z}
.
We claim that

\Hom_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathcal{W}\star\tilde{z},\mathbb{C})

and
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{W}\star\tilde{z}]

are isomorphic as
\mathcal{W}
-modules.
For
z\in\mathcal{W}(\tilde{z})
,
let
\iota_{z}\in\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{W}\star\tilde{z}]

denote the indicator function of
z
,
that is,
\iota_{z}(z)=1
and

\iota_{z}(z^{\prime})=0
for all

z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}\star\tilde{z}\setminus\{z\}
.
For any
s\in\mathcal{W}
, we have

s\cdot\iota_{z}(z^{\prime})=\iota_{z}(s^{-1}\star z^{\prime})=\iota_{s\star z}(z^{\prime})
.
This shows that

\Hom_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathcal{W}\star\tilde{z},\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{W}\star\tilde{z}],\,\iota_{z}\mapsto z

is a
\mathcal{W}
-module homomorphism and bijective.
Hence, the claim is true and the statement follows from the observation that
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{W}]
and
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{W}\star\tilde{z}]
are isomorphic as
\mathcal{W}
-modules.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

The multiplicative coinvariant space
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]_{\mathcal{W}}
affords the regular representation of
\mathcal{W}
.

Proof.

Let
c\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a generic point.
We prove that the vector spaces
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]_{\mathcal{W}}
and

\mathbb{C}[\Omega]/(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}-c)
are isomorphic as
\mathcal{W}
-modules.
Since
Proposition

2.3
implies

\mathbb{C}[\Omega]/(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}-c)\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{W}]
,
this will show the claim.

By
Lemma

2.2
,
there exists a normal set

\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{|\mathcal{W}|}\}\subset\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
of
both
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}=(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}-0)
and
(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}-c)
,
such that

\mathcal{R}_{c}:=\mathbb{C}[\Omega]/(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}-c)=\operatorname{span}\{[h_{1}]_{\mathcal{R}_{c}},\ldots,[h_{|\mathcal{W}|}]_{\mathcal{R}_{c}}\}

with
\mathcal{R}_{0}=\mathbb{C}[\Omega]_{\mathcal{W}}
and thus

\Phi_{c}:\mathcal{R}_{c}\to\mathbb{C}[\Omega]_{\mathcal{W}},\,\sum_{\ell=1}^{|\mathcal{W}|}\lambda_{\ell}\,[h_{\ell}]_{\mathcal{R}_{c}}\mapsto\sum_{\ell=1}^{|\mathcal{W}|}\lambda_{\ell}\,[h_{\ell}]_{\mathcal{R}_{0}}

is a well-defined vector space isomorphism.
Moreover, the ideals
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}
and
(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{m}}-c)
are
\mathcal{W}
-stable.
Since
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
is a free
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]^{\mathcal{W}}
-module,
we have
s\cdot h_{\ell}\in\operatorname{span}\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{|\mathcal{W}|}\}
for all
s\in\mathcal{W}
.
Thus,
\Phi_{c}
is a
\mathcal{W}
-module isomorphism.
∎

3.
Multiplicative
\mathcal{W}
-Harmonics

Let
\mathcal{W}
be a Weyl group of a crystallographic root system with fundamental weights
\omega_{i}

and
\Omega=\mathbb{Z}\omega_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbb{Z}\omega_{n}
be the weight lattice.
In analogy to
Theorem

1.2
,
we show in the present section that the multiplicative coinvariant space
can also be understood as a space of harmonics.

For this, we define the
Euler derivation

\partial_{i}
as the linear operator on
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
with

\partial_{i}\,x^{\alpha}=\alpha_{i}\,x^{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
The associated nabla operator is denoted by
{\nabla}:=[\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n}]^{t}
.
This definition is motivated by the derivative of the exponential function,
see also
[
26
,
39
]
.
Notation is the same as for the derivation on
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
before
Theorem

1.2
but clear form the context.

Recall from
[
4
, Ch. VI, §3, Thm. 1]

that the multiplicative invariant ring
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]^{\mathcal{W}}
of a Weyl group is generated as an algebra by the orbit polynomials

{\theta}_{i}:=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{W}|}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{W}}\mathfrak{e}^{s(\omega_{i})}
,
where
\omega_{i}
are the fundamental weights.
Denote the corresponding Jacobian matrix by
J:=[{\nabla}\,{\theta}_{1}|\ldots|{\nabla}\,{\theta}_{n}]\in(\mathbb{C}[\Omega])^{n\times n}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\delta:=\omega_{1}+\ldots+\omega_{n}\in\Omega
be the sum of the fundamental weights.
The Jacobian determinant
\mathrm{Det}(J)
is, up to a nonzero scalar, equal to

{\Upsilon}_{\delta}:=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{W}|}\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{W}}\mathrm{Det}(s)\,\mathfrak{e}^{s(\delta)}\in\mathbb{C}[\Omega].

A Laurent polynomial
f\in\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
satisfying

s\cdot f=\mathrm{Det}(s)f
whenever
s\in\mathcal{W}

is called
anti-invariant
.
By Weyl’s character formula
[
5
, Ch. VIII, §9, Thm. 1]
,
the space of all anti-invariants is a free
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]^{\mathcal{W}}
-module
of rank
1
generated by
{\Upsilon}_{\delta}
,
which is therefore also called the
Weyl denominator
.

Proof.

For
s\in\mathcal{W}
and
z\in(\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\})^{n}
, one observes that
J(s^{-1}\star z)=(s_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\,J(z)
, where the right hand side is given by matrix multiplication.
Hence,
\mathrm{Det}(J)
is anti-invariant and thus there exists a unique
f\in\mathbb{C}[\Omega]^{\mathcal{W}}
,
such that
\mathrm{Det}(J)=f\,{\Upsilon}_{\delta}
.
Define a partial ordering on
\Omega
by

\mu\prec\omega
if and only if
0\neq\omega-\mu

is a sum of positive roots of the corresponding root system.
We have

J_{ij}=\partial_{i}{\theta}_{j}=\dfrac{\partial_{i}}{|\mathcal{W}|}\sum\limits_{s\in\mathcal{W}}\mathfrak{e}^{s(\omega_{j})}=\dfrac{\partial_{i}}{|\mathcal{W}(\omega_{j})|}\left(\mathfrak{e}^{\omega_{j}}+\sum\limits_{\mu\prec\omega_{j}}\tilde{c}_{\mu}\,\mathfrak{e}^{\mu}\right),

where
\tilde{c}_{\mu}\in\{0,1\}
.
The diagonal elements of the matrix
J
have highest monomial
\mathfrak{e}^{\omega_{i}}
in this ordering,
while the non-diagonal entries have highest monomial
\mathfrak{e}^{\mu}
for some
\mu\prec\omega_{j}
.
Hence, there exist
c_{\mu}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\displaystyle f\,{\Upsilon}_{\delta}\,=\,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Det}(J)\,=\,\dfrac{1}{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}{|\mathcal{W}(\omega_{j})|}}\,\left(\mathfrak{e}^{\delta}+\sum\limits_{\mu\prec\delta}c_{\mu}\,\mathfrak{e}^{\mu}\right).

By comparison of leading monomials,
f
must be a scalar.
∎

We call the vector space
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{m}}
generated by

\{\partial_{i_{1}}\ldots\partial_{i_{\ell}}\mathrm{Det}(J)\,|\,\ell\in\mathbb{N},\,1\leq i_{1}\leq\ldots\leq i_{\ell}\leq n\}

the space of
multiplicative
\mathcal{W}
-harmonics
.

Theorem 3.2
.

The space of multiplicative
\mathcal{W}
-harmonics affords the regular representation of
\mathcal{W}
.

Proof.

By
Proposition

3.1
the Jacobian determinant of
\mathcal{W}
is, up to a scalar, equal to the Weyl denominator
{\Upsilon}_{\delta}
.
Since
\delta=\omega_{1}+\ldots+\omega_{n}
is the sum of all the fundamental weights,
it is contained in the interior of the fundamental Weyl chamber.
But
\mathcal{W}
acts transitively on the set of all chambers and thus
|\mathcal{W}(\delta)|=|\mathcal{W}|
and
{\Upsilon}_{\delta}
is a Laurent polynomial with
|\mathcal{W}|
-many terms
c_{\mu}\,x^{\mu}
,
where
\mu\in\mathcal{W}(e)
and
c_{\mu}=\pm 1/|\mathcal{W}|
.
From the definition of
\partial_{i}
, it follows that
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{m}}
is a subspace of
L:=\spn_{\mathbb{C}}\{\mathfrak{e}^{\mu}\,|\,\mu\in\mathcal{W}(\delta)\}
.
Since
|\mathcal{W}(\delta)|=|\mathcal{W}|
,
L
affords the regular representation of
\mathcal{W}
.

We show that
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{m}}=L
with a recursive argument.
Let
W_{0}:=\{\mu=\alpha_{1}\,\omega_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{n}\,\omega_{n}\in\mathcal{W}(\delta)\,|\,\forall\,1\leq i\leq n:\,\alpha_{i}\neq 0\}
be the set of all monomials in
{\Upsilon}_{\delta}
with full support.
First, we want to show that any monomial occurring in
{\Upsilon}_{\delta}
is contained in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{m}}
.
We have
f:=\partial_{1}\ldots\partial_{n}{\Upsilon}_{\delta}=\sum_{\mu\in W_{0}}c_{\mu}\mathfrak{e}^{\mu}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{m}}
for some
c_{\mu}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
.
We order all the elements in
W_{0}
totally
\mu^{(1)}\prec\mu^{(2)}\prec\ldots\prec\mu^{(n_{0})}
and show that
\mathfrak{e}^{\mu^{(1)}}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{m}}
.
Let
\mu^{(\ell)}=\alpha_{1}^{\ell}\,\omega_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{n}^{\ell}\,\omega_{n}
and
1\leq j\leq n

with
\mu_{j}^{(1)}\neq\mu_{j}^{(2)}
.
Consider
\partial_{j}f-\alpha_{j}^{2}\,f=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{0}}c_{\mu^{(i)}}(\alpha_{j}^{(i)}-\alpha_{j}^{(2)})\mathfrak{e}^{\mu^{(i)}}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{m}}
.
By iterating this procedure we find
\mathfrak{e}^{\mu^{(1)}}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{m}}
and eventually that

\mathfrak{e}^{\mu^{(i)}}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{m}}
for any
1\leq i\leq n_{0}
.
We can repeat the same strategy by considering

f_{1}:=\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal{W}(\delta)\setminus W_{0}}c_{\mu}\mathfrak{e}^{\mu}

and an inclusion-wise maximal support set
W_{1}
in
f_{1}
of cardinality
n_{1}
.
After finitely many steps we obtain
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{m}}=L
.
∎

Concluding with
Theorem

2.4
,
the multiplicative coinvariant space
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]_{\mathcal{W}}
is, as a
\mathcal{W}
-module,
isomorphic to the space of multiplicative
\mathcal{W}
-harmonics.

Remark 3.3
.

For the action of a pseudoreflection group on polynomials and,
in particular, for the additive action of a Weyl group on
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)=\mathbb{C}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
,
the additive
\mathcal{W}
-harmonic polynomials can equivalently be defined as
the orthogonal complement of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{a}}
with respect to the inner product

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{C},\langle f,g\rangle:=(\partial_{f}(g))(0)
, where

\partial_{f}:=\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}\partial_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\ldots\partial_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}

if
f=\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}X^{\alpha}
,
see for example
[
49
]
.
The name “harmonics” originates from this construction.
As a consequence we have that the tensor product over
\mathbb{C}
of the additive coinvariant space
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)/\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{a}}
with the space of additive
\mathcal{W}
-harmonics is isomorphic to
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
.

Unfortunately, this does not hold in the multiplicative setting.
For instance, for
\mathcal{W}
the Weyl group of the root system
\mathrm{A}_{2}
, we found that

\mathbb{C}[\Omega]^{\mathcal{W}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\spn_{\mathbb{C}}\{\partial_{1}^{k}\,\partial_{2}^{\ell}\,{\Upsilon}_{\delta}\,|\,k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}\}

is a proper subset of
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
, where the Weyl denominator
{\Upsilon}_{\delta}
is equal to the Laurent polynomial
g

in
Table

2
up to a scalar.

4.
Associated Graded Equivariants

In the previous two sections, we have described the multiplicative coinvariant space in terms of representation-theoretic properties.
From a computational point of view however,
the obtained basis for
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]_{\mathcal{W}}
from
Algorithm

1

does not reflect those properties.
Meanwhile, many combinatorial works facilitate the computation in the additive setting,
see
[
51
,
1
,
38
]
.
Our next goal in this and the following section is therefore to provide an algorithm,
that allows the transfer of an additive coinvariant basis to a multiplicative one.
This makes it possible to obtain a symmetry adapted basis for
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]_{\mathcal{W}}
from one for
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)_{\mathcal{W}}
.

To commence, we follow
[
2
]
and consider the algebra homomorphisms

(4.1)

\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{C},\,X_{i}\mapsto 0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbb{C}[\Omega]\to\mathbb{C},\,x_{i}\mapsto 1

with kernels
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{a}}=(X_{i}\,|\,1\leq i\leq n)
and

\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}=(1-x_{i}^{-1}\,|\,1\leq i\leq n)
respectively.

Lemma 4.1
.

For a nonnegative integer
d\in\mathbb{N}
,

\phi_{d}:\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)/\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{a}}^{d+1}\to\mathbb{C}[\Omega]/\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}^{d+1},\,[X_{i}]\mapsto[1-x_{i}^{-1}]

is an algebra isomorphism.

Proof.

The inverse homomorphism is defined by
\phi_{d}^{-1}([x_{i}^{-1}])=[1-X_{i}]
and
\phi_{d}^{-1}([x_{i}])=[1+X_{i}+X_{i}^{2}+\ldots+X_{i}^{d}]
by geometric series expansion modulo
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{a}}^{d+1}
.
∎

Note that
\phi_{d}
can be lifted to an algebra homomorphism
\phi:\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
.
Conversely, any
\psi:\mathbb{C}[\Omega]\to\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
with

1=\psi(1)=\psi(x_{i}\,x_{i}^{-1})=\psi(x_{i})\,\psi(x_{i}^{-1})
forces
\psi(x_{i})
to be a unit in
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
,
that is,
\psi(x_{i})\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
.
Hence,
\phi
is not invertible.

Instead, we consider the associated graded algebras
[
6
]

(4.2)

\mathcal{G}^{*}_{\mathrm{a}}(\Omega):=\bigoplus\limits_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{a}}^{d}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{a}}^{d+1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{G}^{*}_{\mathrm{m}}(\Omega):=\bigoplus\limits_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}^{d}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}^{d+1}.

The quotient
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{a}}^{d}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{a}}^{d+1}

is isomorphic to the vector space
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)_{d}
of homogeneous polynomials of degree
d
.
In particular, we have
\mathcal{G}^{*}_{\mathrm{a}}(\Omega)\cong\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
as graded algebras.

For
f\in\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
on the other hand, we denote by
f^{*}:=[f_{0}]+[f_{1}]+[f_{2}]+\ldots
the associated graded element in
\mathcal{G}^{*}_{\mathrm{m}}(\Omega)
,
where
f_{0}
is the normal form of
f
with respect to
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}
and iteratively

f_{d}
is the normal form of
f-f_{d-1}-f_{d-2}-\ldots-f_{0}

with respect to
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}^{d+1}
so that
[f_{d}]\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}^{d}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}^{d+1}
.
We have
f=f_{0}+f_{1}+f_{2}+\ldots
and, since
\mathcal{G}^{*}_{\mathrm{m}}(\Omega)
is Noetherian, this iteration stops at some point.

Example 4.2
.

(1) Consider the homogeneous polynomial

F=X_{1}\,X_{2}-X_{1}^{2}\in\mathbb{C}[X_{1},X_{2}]_{2}\cong\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{a}}^{2}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{a}}^{3}.

Its image under
X_{i}\mapsto 1-x_{i}^{-1}
decomposes into

\displaystyle f=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x_{1}}-\frac{1}{x_{1}^{2}}-\frac{1}{x_{2}}+\frac{1}{x_{1}\,x_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\left(\frac{1}{x_{1}\,x_{2}}-x_{1}-\frac{2}{x_{1}}-\frac{1}{x_{2}}+3\right)}_{=f_{2}}+\underbrace{\left(-\frac{1}{x_{1}^{2}}+x_{1}+\frac{3}{x_{1}}-3\right)}_{=f_{3}}

with
[f_{2}]\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}^{2}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}^{3}
and
[f_{3}]\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}^{3}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}^{4}
.

(2) Now, let
\mathcal{W}
be a Weyl group of the root system
\mathrm{C}_{2}
as in
Example

2.5
.
Since
\mathcal{W}(\omega_{1})=\{\omega_{1},\omega_{1}-\omega_{2},\omega_{2}-\omega_{1},-\omega_{1}\}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},x_{2}^{\pm 1}]^{\mathcal{W}}\ni

\displaystyle\,x_{1}+\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}+\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}+\frac{1}{x_{1}}-4

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{\left(\frac{-2}{x_{1}\,x_{2}}+2\,x_{1}+x_{2}+\frac{4}{x_{1}}+\frac{3}{x_{2}}-8\right)}_{=f_{2}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\left(\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}}+\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}+\frac{2}{x_{1}\,x_{2}}-x_{1}-x_{2}-\frac{3}{x_{1}}-\frac{3}{x_{2}}+4\right)}_{=f_{3}}.

Neither of the two summands is stable under the
\mathcal{W}
-transformation

s_{1}:\,(x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto(x_{1}^{-1}\,x_{2},x_{2})
.

We conclude from the example that
\phi_{d}
preserves neither homogeneity nor equivariance.
We explain now how working over the associated graded algebras instead solves this circumstance.

Proposition 4.3

(
[
2
,
7
]
)
.

The quotients

\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)_{d}\cong\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{a}}^{d}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{a}}^{d+1}
and
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}^{d}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}^{d+1}

are
\mathcal{W}
-stable under the induced additive and multiplicative action respectively.
Furthermore, the map

\phi^{(d)}:\begin{array}[t]{ccc}\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)_{d}&\to&\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}^{d}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}^{d+1},\\
\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}X_{i_{j}}&\mapsto&\left[\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}(1-x_{i_{j}}^{-1})\right]\end{array}

is a
\mathcal{W}
-module isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
s\in\mathcal{W}
with matrix entries
s_{\ell,i}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then

\phi^{(d)}\left(s\cdot\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}X_{i_{j}}\right)=\phi^{(d)}\left(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{n}s_{\ell,i_{j}}\,X_{\ell}\right)=\left[\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{n}s_{\ell,i_{j}}\,(1-x_{\ell}^{-1})\right].

Since
(1-x^{-\alpha})\,(1-x^{-\beta})=(1-x^{-\alpha})+(1-x^{-\beta})-(1-x^{-\alpha-\beta})
for any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}

and
[1-x_{\ell}^{-1}]=[-(1-x_{\ell})]
in
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}^{2}
,
we have
[s_{\ell,i_{j}}(1-x_{\ell}^{-1})]=[1-x_{\ell}^{-s_{\ell,i_{j}}}]
in
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}^{2}

and consequently

\left[\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{n}s_{\ell,i_{j}}\,(1-x_{\ell}^{-1})\right]=\left[\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{n}\left(1-x_{\ell}^{-s_{\ell,i_{j}}}\right)\right]

in
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}^{d}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}^{d+1}
.
Thus,

\phi^{(d)}\left(s\cdot\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}X_{i_{j}}\right)=\left[\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{n}\left(1-x_{\ell}^{-s_{\ell,i_{j}}}\right)\right]=s\cdot\phi^{(d)}\left(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{d}X_{i_{j}}\right).

We conclude that
\phi^{(d)}
is well-defined and
\mathcal{W}
-equivariant.
For bijectiveness, note that

\{[(1-x_{i_{1}}^{-1})\cdots(1-x_{i_{d}}^{-1})]\,|\,1\leq i_{1}\leq\ldots\leq i_{d}\leq n\}

is a vector space basis for
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}^{d}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}^{d+1}
.
∎

In particular,

(4.3)

\phi^{*}=\bigoplus\limits_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\phi^{(d)}:\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)\to\mathcal{G}^{*}_{\mathrm{m}}(\Omega)

is a
\mathcal{W}
-equivariant graded algebra isomorphism.

5.
Graded Transfer of Coinvariants

In this section, we show how to preserve group symmetry between additive and multiplicative coinvariants.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be a Weyl group of a crystallographic root system with fundamental weights
\omega_{i}

and
\Omega=\mathbb{Z}\omega_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbb{Z}\omega_{n}
be the weight lattice.
As fundamental invariants, we consider the orbit polynomials

{\theta}_{i}:=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{W}|}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{W}}\mathfrak{e}^{s(\omega_{i})}
.
Note that
{\theta}_{i}-1\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}
.
By
Lemma

2.1
, the set

\{{\theta}_{1}-1,\ldots,{\theta}_{n}-1\}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}\cap\mathbb{C}[\Omega]^{\mathcal{W}}

also generates
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]^{\mathcal{W}}
as an algebra and thus we may fix

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}=(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}\cap\mathbb{C}[\Omega]^{\mathcal{W}})

as the multiplicative Hilbert ideal in
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

The ideal
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}^{*}:=(h^{*}\,|\,h\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}})
in
\mathcal{G}^{*}_{\mathrm{m}}(\Omega)
is generated by the set
\{({\theta}_{1}-1)^{*},\ldots,({\theta}_{n}-1)^{*}\}
.
Equip
\mathcal{G}^{*}_{\mathrm{m}}(\Omega)/\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}^{*}
with the induced multiplicative action.
Then the map

\mathbb{C}[\Omega]_{\mathcal{W}}\to\mathcal{G}^{*}_{\mathrm{m}}(\Omega)/\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}^{*},\,f+\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}\mapsto f^{*}+\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}^{*}

is a well-defined
\mathcal{W}
-module isomorphism.

Proof.

To see that the map is well-defined, let
f-g\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}
, that is,
f-g=\sum_{i}k_{i}\,({\theta}_{i}-1)
for some
k_{i}\in\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
.
Then
(f-g)^{*}=\sum_{i}k_{i}^{*}\,({\theta}_{i}-1)^{*}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}^{*}
.
Furthermore, since every element
\tilde{f}\in\mathcal{G}^{*}_{\mathrm{m}}(\Omega)
is of the form
\tilde{f}=[f_{0}]+[f_{1}]+[f_{2}]+\ldots
for some
f_{d}\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}^{d}
,
the inverse is given by
\tilde{f}+\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}^{*}\mapsto f_{0}+f_{1}+f_{2}+\ldots+\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}
and thus the map is bijective.
Finally, equivariance follows from
Proposition

4.3

is the Weyl group of the root system
\mathrm{C}_{n}
.
Denote by
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n}
the fundamental weights of the weight lattice
\Omega
.
This defines a representation
\pi:\,\mathfrak{S}_{n}\wr\{\pm 1\}\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})

with an additive action on the symmetric algebra
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)\cong\mathbb{C}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]

and a multiplicative action on the group algebra
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]\cong\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
.
Furthermore, we have

(6.5)

\mathcal{W}(\omega_{1})=\{\omega_{1},\omega_{2}-\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n}-\omega_{n-1}\},

which leads to the following statement analogous to type
\mathrm{A}_{n-1}

(see also
[
29
, §6]
).

Lemma 6.6
.

The maps

\mathbb{C}[V]\to\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega),\,Y_{1}\mapsto X_{1},\,Y_{2}\mapsto X_{2}-X_{1},\,\ldots,\,Y_{n}\mapsto X_{n}-X_{n-1}

and

\mathbb{C}[W]\to\mathbb{C}[\Omega],\,y_{1}\mapsto x_{1},\,y_{2}\mapsto x_{2}\,x_{1}^{-1},\,\ldots,\,y_{n}\mapsto x_{n}\,x_{n-1}^{-1}

are well-defined
\rho
-
\pi
-equivariant algebra isomorphisms.

Hence, the induced actions on
\mathbb{C}[V]
and
\mathbb{C}[W]
can be identified with the additive and multiplicative action respectively.

Example 6.7
.

The Weyl group
\mathcal{W}
of
\mathrm{C}_{2}
is generated by
s_{1},s_{2}
with

\rho(s_{1})=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\,\rho(s_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}

and

\pi(s_{1})=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\
1&1\end{pmatrix},\,\pi(s_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

As in
Lemma

6.1
,
we find a
\mathcal{W}
-module isomorphism between
\mathbb{C}[V]
and
\mathbb{C}[W]
.

Proposition 6.8
.

The map
\Psi_{\mathrm{C}}:\mathbb{C}[V]\to\mathbb{C}[W]
with

\displaystyle Y_{i}^{2k}\mapsto y_{i}^{k}+y_{i}^{-k},Y_{i}^{2k-1}\mapsto y_{i}^{k}-y_{i}^{-k},\text{ for all }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\,k\in\mathbb{N},

and
\prod_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\mapsto\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Psi_{\mathrm{C}}(Y_{i}^{\alpha_{i}})
is a
\rho
-
\pi
-equivariant isomorphism.
Moreover, the image of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{a}}
is not a proper ideal in
\mathbb{C}[W]
.

Proof.

By construction the map is well-defined, injective and
\rho
-
\pi
-equivariant.
The map is also surjective since

y_{i}^{\pm k}=\frac{y_{i}^{k}+y_{i}^{-k}}{2}\pm\frac{y_{i}^{k}-y_{i}^{-k}}{2}=\frac{\Psi_{\mathrm{C}}(Y_{i}^{2k})\pm\Psi_{\mathrm{C}}(Y_{i}^{2k-1})}{2}\in\Ime(\Psi_{\mathrm{C}}).

Assume that
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{a}}
is mapped to an ideal
I\subseteq\mathbb{C}[W]
, that is,

\Psi_{\mathrm{C}}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}^{2}\right),\Psi_{\mathrm{C}}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}^{4}\right)\mbox{ and }\Psi_{\mathrm{C}}\left(\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}Y_{i}^{2}\,Y_{j}^{2}\right)\in I.

Since
I
is closed under multiplication and addition, this would imply

2\,n=\left(\Psi_{\mathrm{C}}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}^{2}\right)\right)^{2}-\Psi_{\mathrm{C}}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}^{4}\right)-2\,\Psi_{\mathrm{C}}\left(\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}Y_{i}^{2}\,Y_{j}^{2}\right)\in I,

that is,
I=\mathbb{C}[W]
.
∎

In particular, the image of the additive Hilbert ideal is not the multiplicative Hilbert ideal.
Thus the map
\Psi_{\mathrm{C}}
does not extend to a
\mathcal{W}
-homomorphism of the coinvariant spaces.

7.
Conclusion and Open Questions

Given a Weyl group
\mathcal{W}
of a crystallographic root system in a real vector space with weight lattice
\Omega
,
we show that the multiplicative coinvariant space
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]_{\mathcal{W}}
affords the regular representation of
\mathcal{W}
,
introduce a space of multiplicative
\mathcal{W}
-harmonics that also affords the regular representation,
and draw analogies to the additive coinvariant space
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)_{\mathcal{W}}
.

Using the technique of graded transfer,
an algorithm is designed to transform
an additive coinvariant basis
into a multiplicative one.
Therefore, the two coinvariant spaces and in particular the algebras

\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
and
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
are isomorphic
\mathcal{W}
-modules.

Computationally, the construction of explicit
\mathcal{W}
-homomorphisms proved challenging.
Such homomorphisms were only found for types
\mathrm{A}_{n-1}
(
Lemma

6.1
) and partly
\mathrm{C}_{n}
(
Lemma

6.6
).

Question 7.1
.

Are there explicit
\mathcal{W}
-homomorphism between

\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
and
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
for the types

\mathrm{B}_{n},\,\mathrm{D}_{n},\,\mathrm{E}_{6},\,\mathrm{E}_{7},\,\mathrm{E}_{8}
or
\mathrm{F}_{4}
?

For type
\mathrm{C}_{2}
,
higher Specht polynomials form a symmetry adapted basis for
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)_{\mathcal{W}}
.
We have two strategies to construct one for
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]_{\mathcal{W}}
.
First, using
Algorithm

2
,
we compute a symmetry adapted basis for

\mathbb{C}[\Omega]_{\mathcal{W}}
in
Table

1
.
Second, applying
Proposition

6.8

yields an equivariant isomorphism between
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
and
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]
,
which does NOT induce an isomorphism of the coinvariant spaces.
Nonetheless, the resulting polynomials in
Table

3

form a symmetry adapted basis for
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]_{\mathcal{W}}
,
which differs from the one in
Table

1
.

Question 7.2
.

For general type
\mathrm{C}_{n}
,
is the image of a higher Specht polynomial basis for
\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)_{\mathcal{W}}

under the map
\Psi_{\mathrm{C}}

always a (symmetry adapted) basis
for the multiplicative coinvariant space
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]_{\mathcal{W}}
?

Expanding on type
\mathrm{C}_{n}
,
the multiplicative coinvariant space is

\mathbb{C}[\Omega]_{\mathcal{W}}\cong\mathbb{C}[y^{\pm}]/(\sigma_{i}(y+y^{-1}))

(see
Proposition

6.10
)
on which the group
\mathcal{W}\cong\mathfrak{S}_{n}\wr\{\pm 1\}
acts intuitively
by permutation and inversion of variables.
We could not determine a closed form expression for a Gröbner basis for the ideal

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}=(\sigma_{i}(y+y^{-1}))
for arbitrary
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
although the reduced Gröbner basis with respect to the lexicographical ordering of
the ideal
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{a}}=(\sigma_{i}(Y))
is known, see
[
37
]
.

Question 7.3
.

For type
\mathrm{C}_{n}
, what is the Gröbner basis of the ideal
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}=(\sigma_{i}(y+y^{-1}))
with respect to the lexicographical ordering?
Does the choice of monomial ordering matter in this context?

Analogous questions can be asked for other types next to
\mathrm{C}_{n}
,
but we think that this would be the most accessible one,
due to the relations in
[
29
, §6]
that turn out to be more complicated for other types.

In
Theorem

6.5
,
we give a formula for the multiplicities of the irreducible representations in
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]_{d}
for
\mathrm{A}_{2}
,
where the degree
d
is defined via dilation of the Voronoï cell.

Question 7.4
.

Are there explicit formulae for the multiplicities of the irreducible representations in the isotypic decomposition of
\mathbb{C}[\Omega]_{d}
for type
\mathrm{A}_{n-1}
,
n\geq 4
, or for the other types
\mathrm{B}_{n},\,\mathrm{C}_{n},\,\mathrm{D}_{n},\,\mathrm{E}_{6},\,\mathrm{E}_{7},\,\mathrm{E}_{8},\,\mathrm{F}_{4}
?

Theoretical generalizations beyond Weyl groups are foreseen to be possible under the following considerations.
The setup in
Section

4
only involves general finite groups that leave a lattice stable.
In order to prove
Proposition

5.1
,
we require the additive and multiplicative Hilbert ideal
to be generated by invariants in the respective kernels

\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{a}}
and
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{m}}
.
The proof of
Theorem

5.2
relies on the fact
that the coinvariant space affords the regular representation.
For additive actions,
this property characterizes complex reflection groups
[
8
]
.
One should consider the associated complex root lattices in
[
41
]
.

Finally, a thrilling direction for future research lies in an extension of the study of multiplicative coinvariant spaces for diagonal actions, where one considers the action on
\mathbb{C}[\Omega\oplus\Omega^{\vee}]
.
In the additive settings, foundational work on diagonal invariants and coinvariant spaces was developed by Haiman
[
25
]
and Gordon
[
23
]
. It would also be very interesting to explore the setting of diagonal actions with commuting and anticommuting variables as for instance studied in the additive setting in
[
49
]
.

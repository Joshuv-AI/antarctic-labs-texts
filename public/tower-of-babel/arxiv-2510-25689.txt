Introduction

A
d
-dimensional (bar-and-joint)
framework

(G,p)
consists of a simple graph
G=(V,E)
and
a map
p:V\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Such a framework is called
rigid
if there is no
continuous motion
\hat{p}:[0,1]\to({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{V}
of the vertices with
\hat{p}(0)=p
such that the function
||\hat{p}_{u}(t)-\hat{p}_{v}(t)||
is constant for every edge
uv\in E
, but not for all pairs
u,v\in V
.
We consider
generic
frameworks, in which the
set of coordinates of
p
is algebraically independent over
\mathbb{Q}
.
It is known that, for every fixed
d\geq 1
, the rigidity of a generic framework
(G,p)
is determined by the graph
G
. We therefore call
G

d
-rigid
if every (equivalently, some) generic
d
-dimensional framework
(G,p)

is rigid.

The first of the two main questions we study in this paper is as follows:

Question 1
.

For
n,d\in\mathbb{N}
, what is the smallest integer
f(n,d)
such that every
n
-vertex graph
G
with minimum degree
\delta(G)\geq f(n,d)
is
d
-rigid?

This problem and its variants were first studied for fixed dimensions
d=2,3
, in the context of global rigidity
[
1
,
3
]
and matrix completion problems
[
5
]
.
Our work is primarily motivated by recent papers of Krivelevich, Lew, and Michaeli
[
11
,
12
]
, where the authors
obtained various bounds on
f(n,d)
, for all
d\geq 1
.
We first review these developments and outline our contribution.

For
n\leq d+1
only complete graphs are
d
-rigid, implying
f(n,d)=n-1
.
So we shall frequently assume
n\geq d+2
.
It is a well-known fact that
\delta(G)\geq\lceil\frac{n+d-2}{2}\rceil
is a sufficient condition for
d
-connectivity, and that this bound is best possible.
Since
d
-rigid graphs are
d
-connected, we obtain a straightforward yet useful lower bound:

\left\lceil\frac{n+d-2}{2}\right\rceil\leq f(n,d).

(1)

It follows similarly that
f(n,d)\leq\left\lceil(n+d(d+1)-2)/2\right\rceil
from the fact that every
d(d+1)
-connected graph is
d
-rigid
[
17
]
.
More elaborate upper bounds were established by Krivelevich, Lew, and Michaeli
[
11
]
. They proved that

f(n,d)\leq(n+3d\log n)/2
for all pairs
n,d
,
and showed that this bound can be improved if
n
is sufficiently large:
for
n\geq\Omega(d^{2})\cdot\log^{2}n
, we have
f(n,d)\leq\frac{n}{2}+d-1
. (See
[
11
, Theorems 1.14, 1.15]
.)
These results suggest that the function
f(n,d)-\frac{n}{2}
may depend rather weakly on
n
. In fact, the authors of
[
11
]
conjectured that it is bounded above by a linear function of
d
:

Conjecture 1.1
.

[
11
, Conjecture 1.16]

There exists a constant
K>0
such that
f(n,d)\leq\frac{n}{2}+Kd
for all
n,d\in\mathbb{N}
.

Subsequently, in
[
12
]
, they made further progress towards Conjecture
1.1
by showing that
f(n,d)\leq\frac{n}{2}+d-1
already holds for
n\geq\Omega(d)\cdot\log^{2}n
(
[
12
, Theorem 1.2]
).
Our first result states that here the assumption on
n
can be dropped, which confirms Conjecture
1.1
with
K=1
:

Theorem 1.2
.

f(n,d)\leq\frac{n}{2}+d-1
for all
n,d\in\mathbb{N}
.

Their other main result established that if
n\geq d(2d+1)+2
, then
f(n,d)=\lceil\frac{n+d-2}{2}\rceil
; i.e., the bound in (
1
) is tight (
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
). We improve on this by showing that the assumption on
n
can be relaxed to
n\geq 29d
.

Theorem 1.3
.

If
n\geq 29d
, then
f(n,d)=\lceil\frac{n+d-2}{2}\rceil.

For
d<n<29d
, the exact value of
f(n,d)
remains an open problem.
As it was noted in
[
12
]
, the lower bound in (
1
) is not always tight. A different lower bound arises from the fact that every
d
-rigid graph on more than
d
vertices has at least
dn-d(d+1)/2
edges. This yields

2d-\frac{d(d+1)-1}{n}\leq f(n,d),

(2)

which is more restrictive than
(
1
)
in the regime
d<n<2d+2
.
Krivelevich, Lew and Michaeli conjectured that the combination of (
1
) and (
2
) is essentially tight:

Conjecture 1.4
.

[
12
, Conjecture 1]

Let
1\leq d<n
. Then

f(n,d)\leq\max\left\{\left\lceil\frac{n+d-2}{2}\right\rceil,\left\lceil 2d-\frac{d(d+1)}{n}\right\rceil\right\}.

In the second part of this paper, we verify Conjecture
1.4
in the special cases
d\in\{2,3\}
.
We will, however, consider the problem in a more general form,
which leads to the following definition and question.
For a graph
G=(V,E)
, define

\eta(G)=\min_{uv\notin E}(\deg_{G}(u)+\deg_{G}(v)).

Question 2
.

For
n,d\in\mathbb{N}
, what is the smallest integer
g(n,d)
such that every
n
-vertex graph
G
with
\eta(G)\geq g(n,d)
is
d
-rigid?

Since
\eta(G)\geq 2\delta(G)
for every graph
G
, it follows that

f(n,d)\leq\left\lceil g(n,d)/2\right\rceil.

We believe that
f(n,d)=\left\lceil g(n,d)/2\right\rceil
may in fact be true for every
n,d\in\mathbb{N}
.
We propose this in the form of Conjecture
1.5
below for the case
n>2d+2
.
Note that the lower bound from (
1
) corresponds to
g(n,d)\geq n+d-2
.
In the regime
n>2d+2
, this dominates the bound one can get from an edge-counting argument (see Lemma
7.1
, for details);
therefore, we conjecture:

Conjecture 1.5
.

Let
n,d\in\mathbb{N}
. If
n>2d+2
, then

g(n,d)=n+d-2
.

In support of Conjecture
1.5
, we prove the following theorems.

Theorem 1.6
.

g(n,d)\leq n+3d-3
for all
n,d\in\mathbb{N}
.

Theorem 1.7
.

If
n\geq d(d+2)
, then
g(n,d)=n+d-2
.

Theorem
1.7
is a strengthening of
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
mentioned above, and it implies Conjecture
1.5
in the regime
n\geq d(d+2)
.
Next, we verify Conjecture
1.5
in the special cases

. Surprisingly, the major difficulties still arise when
n
is particularly small.
In Theorem

5.1
, we show that
if

\eta(G)\geq n+1
, then
either

is isomorphic to
one of four special graphs on at most seven vertices, or

is rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.
We obtain a similar result for

, as well (Theorem

).
These results imply an
affirmative answer to Conjecture

for

(Theorems

,

).

We briefly address the corresponding questions on global rigidity, and show that the (optimal) sufficient conditions for
d
-rigidity in terms of

directly imply (optimal) sufficient conditions for global
(d-1)
-rigidity.

Finally, we apply our results to settle a question raised by Peled and Peleg
[

]
, asking whether there exists

such that the Erdős-Rényi random graph

is asymptotically almost surely

-rigid. For a brief outline of the background to this question, see Section

. We will prove the following:

Theorem 1.8
.

The random graph

is a.a.s.

Our proof methods are substantially different from those of
[

]

and
[
12
]
. We verify new structural properties of the

-dimensional rigidity matroid and in most of the proofs we use

2
Preliminaries

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a non-complete graph on
n
vertices and let
r
be an integer with
r\leq n-3
.
Suppose that

\deg(u)+\deg(v)\geq n+r

(3)

for all
pairs
u,v\in V
of non-adjacent vertices.
Then
G
is
(r+2)
-connected. Furhermore, the bound (
3
) is optimal.

Proof.

The lower bound on the degree sum implies that

u
and
v
have at least
r+2

common neighbours in
G
for each non-adjacent vertex pair
u,v
.
Thus
G
cannot have a vertex separator of size at most
r+1
. Furthermore, graphs constructed by gluing together two disjoint complete graphs along
r+1
vertices demonstrate that the bound (
3
) is optimal.
∎

2.1
Graph operations that preserve rigidity

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph. The
d
-dimensional
0
-
extension
operation adds a new vertex
v
to
G
and
d
new edges incident with
v
. The
1
-
extension
operation removes an edge
uw
, and adds a new vertex
v
and
d+1
new edges, including
vu
,
vw
.
The following lemma is well-known, for a proof, see e.g.
[
20
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
H
be a rigid graph in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and let
G
be obtained from
H
by a
0
- or

1
-extension. Then
G
is rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Suppose
z
is a vertex of a graph
H
.
The
d
-dimensional vertex splitting (resp. spider splitting) operation
constructs a new graph
G
from
H
by deleting
z
and then adding two new vertices
u
and
v
with
N_{G}(u)\cup N_{G}(v)=N_{H}(z)\cup\{u,v\}

(resp.
N_{G}(u)\cup N_{G}(v)=N_{H}(z)
)
and
|N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)|\geq d-1
(resp.
|N_{G}(u)\cap N_{G}(v)|\geq d
).
We can view vertex splitting as an inverse operation to contracting the edge
uv
, while
spider splitting corresponds to contracting a non-adjacent vertex pair.
Whiteley
[
19
]
showed that
these operations preserve rigidity in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, see also
[
20
]
.
We shall refer to this result as Whiteley’s vertex splitting theorem.

The following lemmas are
easy to prove by using the 3-dimensional version of Lemma
2.2
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
H=(V,E)
be a rigid graph in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and
let
\{v_{i}^{1},v_{i}^{2}\}
,
1\leq i\leq k
, be pairs of distinct vertices of
H

with

|\cup_{i=1}^{k}\{v_{i}^{1},v_{i}^{2}\}|\geq 3
and
k\geq 3
. Let
G
be obtained from the disjoint union of
H
and a
k
-cycle
u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{k}u_{1}
by adding all edges
u_{i}v_{i}^{1},u_{i}v_{i}^{2}
,
for
1\leq i\leq k
. Then

G
is rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that a graph
G=(V,E)
contains two disjoint subgraphs
H_{1},H_{2}
on at least three vertices with

V=V(H_{1})\cup V(H_{2})

such that
H_{1},H_{2}
are both rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and

for all

.
If

contains a set
A=\{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\}
of three vertices with

is rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.

2.2
The rigidity matroid

Let
d
be a positive integer.
The
d
-dimensional generic rigidity matroid
of a graph
G
, denoted by
{\cal R}_{d}(G)
,
is defined on the
edge set of
G
. It is the restriction of
{\cal R}_{d}(K_{|V(G)|})
to the edge set of
G
.
This matroid encodes several fundamental rigidity properties of
(generic
d
-dimensional bar-and-joint frameworks of)

G
.
For example, the rigidity of
G
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
can be characterized by the rank of
{\cal R}_{d}(G)
, for all
d\geq 1
.
We shall denote the rank of
{\cal R}_{d}(G)
by
r_{d}(G)
.
It is well-known that

r_{d}(G)\leq d|V|-\binom{d+1}{2}\mbox{ for all graphs $G=(V,E)$ with $|V|\geq d+1$.}

Equality holds if and only if
G
is rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
The (
d
-dimensional)
degrees of freedom
of
G
, denoted by
\mathrm{dof}_{d}(G)
, is the
difference between the rank of a rigid graph on
V(G)
and
r_{d}(G)
.
Thus, if
|V|\geq d+1
, then
\mathrm{dof}_{d}(G)=d|V|-\binom{d+1}{2}-r_{d}(G)
.
We say that a graph
G=(V,E)
is

{\cal R}_{d}
-closed
, if
r_{d}(G+uv)=r_{d}(G)+1
for all non-adjacent
vertex pairs
u,v\in V
.
A non-adjacent pair
u,v\in V
is
{\cal R}_{d}
-linked
(resp.

{\cal R}_{d}
-loose
) in
G

if
r_{d}(G+uv)=r_{d}(G)
(resp.
r_{d}(G+uv)=r_{d}(G)+1
) holds.
The
{\cal R}_{d}
-closure
of
G
is the unique maximal
supergraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
with
r_{d}(G^{\prime})=r_{d}(G)
. It is obtained from
G

by adding the edges
uv
for all
{\cal R}_{d}
-linked pairs
u,v
of
G
.

We call an edge
e\in E
an
{\cal R}_{d}
-bridge
of
G

if
r_{d}(G-e)=r_{d}(G)-1
. A graph
H
with edge set
E
is

{\cal R}_{d}
-independent
(resp.
an

{\cal R}_{d}
-circuit
),
if
E
is independent (resp.

E
is a circuit) in
{\cal R}_{d}(H)
. Thus an edge is
an
{\cal R}_{d}
-bridge of
G
if and only if it is not contained
by a subgraph
H
of
G
which is an
{\cal R}_{d}
-circuit.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and
U\subseteq V
.
The graph obtained from
G
by adding a new vertex
w
and a new
edge from
w
to each vertex in
U
is denoted by
G^{w,U}
.
The graph
G^{w}:=G^{w,V}
is called the

cone
of
G
.
Part (i) of the following theorem is a fundamental result of Whiteley
[
18
]
. Parts (ii) and (iii) are simple corollaries of (i).

Theorem 2.5
.

[
18
]

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and let
u,v\in V
. Then

(i)
G
is
{\cal R}_{d}
-independent
(resp.
d
-rigid) if and only if
G^{w}
is
{\cal R}_{d+1}
-independent
(resp.
(d+1)
-rigid),

(ii)
\{u,v\}
is
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-linked in
G
if and only if
\{u,v\}
is
\mathcal{R}_{d+1}
-linked in
G^{w}
,

(iii)
\mathrm{dof}_{d}(G)=\mathrm{dof}_{d+1}(G^{w})
.

The next lemma is also a corollary of the coning theorem.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and let
\{u,v\}
be an

\mathcal{R}_{d}
-loose vertex pair of
G
.
Suppose that
F
is a set of new edges with
uv\notin F
.
Then
\{u,v\}
is
\mathcal{R}_{d+|F|}
-loose in

.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the statement for
|F|=1
. Suppose that
F=\{xy\}
.
We may assume that
x\notin\{u,v\}
. Let
G^{\prime}
be the graph obtained from
G

by adding the edges

for all
z\in V-N_{G}(x)-\{x\}
. Since
\{u,v\}
is
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-loose in
G
, it is also
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-loose in
G-x
. Hence

\{u,v\}
is
\mathcal{R}_{d+1}
-loose in
G^{\prime}
by Theorem
2.5
. Since
G+F
is a subgraph of
G^{\prime}
, the lemma follows.
∎

We shall use the next simple lemma several times.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph, and let

be distinct vertices.
Suppose that
u\in N_{G}(v)\subseteq N_{G}(w)\cup\{w\}

-bridge in

. Then

-bridge in

Suppose not. Then there exists an

, there is a

in

Then

is an

{\cal R}_{d}
-circuit in

is an
\mathcal{R}_{d}
-bridge in

∎

3.3
Expected rank contribution of vertices in rigidity matroids

In the subsequent proofs, we will frequently employ a probabilistic argument introduced in
[
17
]
and further developed in
[
6
]
. Our exposition follows the approach of
[
6
]
.

Let
d
be a positive integer,
G=(V,E)
be a graph and
\pi
be a uniformly random ordering of
V
.
For a vertex
v\in V
, let
T_{v}^{\pi}
denote the set of those vertices that precede
v
in
\pi
.
We define the
rank contribution of
v
in
{\mathcal{R}}_{d}(G)
, with respect to
\pi
, to be

\rc_{d}(G,v,\pi)=r_{d}(G[T_{v}^{\pi}\cup\{v\}])-r_{d}(G[T_{v}^{\pi}]),

and the
rank contribution of
v
in
{\mathcal{R}}_{d}(G)
to be

\rc_{d}(G,v)=\mathbb{E}\big(\rc_{d}(G,v,\pi)\big).

As a consequence of the linearity of expectation, one obtains the following lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

[
6
]

For any graph
G=(V,E)
, we have
{\displaystyle r_{d}(G)=\sum_{v\in V}\rc_{d}(G,v)}.

We next introduce a lower bound of
\rc_{d}(G,v)
.
Let
E_{v}
denote the set of edges of
G
incident with
v
. Put
N_{v}^{\pi}=T_{v}^{\pi}\cap N_{G}(v)
and

E_{v}^{\pi}=\{vu\in E_{v}:u\in N_{v}^{\pi}\}
.
We define

\rc^{*}_{d}(G,v,\pi)=r_{d}(G-E_{v}+E_{v}^{\pi})-r_{d}(G-v)

and

\rc^{*}_{d}(G,v)=\mathbb{E}\big(\rc^{*}_{d}(G,v,\pi)\big).

The submodularity of
r_{d}
implies that
\rc^{*}_{d}
is a lower bound for
\rc_{d}
:

Lemma 3.7
.

[
6
]

For any graph
G=(V,E)
and
v\in V
, we have

\rc^{*}_{d}(G,v)\leq\rc_{d}(G,v).

In what follows, we give another equivalent form of
\rc^{*}_{d}(G,v)
. Let
\mathcal{R}_{d}^{v}(G)
denote the matroid obtained from
\mathcal{R}_{d}(G)
by contracting all edges not incident with
v
; that is,

\mathcal{R}_{d}^{v}(G)=\mathcal{R}_{d}(G)/E(G-v).

The ground set of
\mathcal{R}_{d}^{v}(G)
is
E_{v}
.
Let
r_{d}^{v}:2^{E_{v}}\to\mathbb{N}
denote the rank function of
\mathcal{R}_{d}^{v}(G)
. Then
r_{d}^{v}(E_{v})=r_{d}(G)-r_{d}(G-v)
, and

r_{d}^{v}(E_{v}^{\pi})=\rc_{d}^{*}(G,v,\pi).

Set
k=\deg_{G}(v)=|E_{v}|
. Then
for any
i\in\{0,1,\dots,k\}
, we have

\mathbb{P}(|E_{v}^{\pi}|=i)=\frac{1}{k+1}.

Observe that conditioned on
|E_{v}^{\pi}|=i
, the set
E_{v}^{\pi}
is uniformly distributed among all
\binom{k}{i}
subsets of
E_{v}
of size
i
. Hence,

\rc_{d}^{*}(G,v)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\mathbb{E}\big(r_{d}^{v}(E_{v}^{\pi})\mid|E_{v}^{\pi}|=i\big)\,\mathbb{P}(|E_{v}^{\pi}|=i)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\frac{\mathbb{E}(r_{d}^{v}(A_{i}))}{k+1},

(8)

where
A_{i}
is a subset of
E_{v}
of size
i
chosen uniformly at random for all
0\leq i\leq k
.

By imposing mild conditions on the local structure of
G
, one can often establish (sufficiently strong) lower bounds for
\rc^{*}_{d}(G,v)
, which in turn yield bounds for
\rc_{d}(G,v)
and
r_{d}(G)
. This subsection presents several statements of this kind.
The
girth
of a matroid
{\cal M}
is the size of the smallest
circuit of
{\cal M}
(or
+\infty
, if
\cal M
contains no circuits).

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
{\cal M}=(E,r)
be a matroid with
rank function
r
and with girth at least
d+1
. Let
i
be an integer with
d\leq i\leq|E|
, and
A
be a uniformly random subset of
E
of size
i
. Then

\mathbb{E}(r(A))\geq d+(r(E)-d)\frac{i-d}{|E|-d}.

Proof.

For every
X\subseteq E
with
|X|=d
, let us fix a subset
\hat{X}
of size
r(E)-d
such that
X\cup\hat{X}
is a basis of
{\cal M}
.
Let
(A_{0},A_{1})
be a pair of random subsets of
E
defined as follows: first,
A_{0}
is chosen uniformly at random among all subsets of
E
of size
d
; then, conditioned on
A_{0}
, the set
A_{1}
is chosen uniformly at random among all subsets of
E\setminus A_{0}
of size

.
The distribution of
A_{0}\cup A_{1}
is the same as that of

, and thus
\mathbb{E}(r(A))=\mathbb{E}(r(A_{0}\cup A_{1}))
.
Since
r(A_{0}\cup A_{1})\geq d+|\hat{A}_{0}\cap A_{1}|
, it follows that

\mathbb{E}(r(A))\geq d+\mathbb{E}\big(\big|\hat{A}_{0}\cap A_{1}\big|\big)\geq d+(r(E)-d)\frac{i-d}{|E|-d}.\qed

.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph,

.

\rc^{*}_{d}(G,v)\geq d+\frac{t(\deg(v)-d+1)-d(d+1)}{2(\deg(v)+1)}.

Let

A_{i}
be a subset of

of size

Hence, using (

(k+1)\rc_{d}^{*}(G,v)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\mu_{i}\geq\sum_{i=0}^{d}i+\sum_{i=d+1}^{k}\Big(d+\frac{t(i-d)}{k-d}\Big)=(k+1)d-\frac{d(d+1)}{2}+\frac{t(k-d+1)}{2}.

We start with the proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem

.

Let

be a graph that satisfies (

.
We prove that

is rigid in

\mathcal{R}_{d}
-closed.

Let

-closed, it follows that

From (

Lemma 4.1

Let

6
On the rigidity of dense Erdős-Rényi random graphs

In this section, we apply Theorem
1.2
to settle a question raised by Peled and Peleg on the rigidity of Erdős-Rényi random graphs. This topic has seen increasing interest in recent years, and one of its central
framing questions is:

For which pairs
d=d(n)
and
p=p(n)
is the
random graph
G\sim G(n,p)
asymptotically almost surely rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
?

This was first studied for fixed dimension
d=2

[
7
]
. Subsequently, Lew, Nevo, Peled, and Raz
[
13
]
obtained an exact hitting time result for higher (but still fixed) dimensions
d\geq 1
, which was later extended to all
d(n)\leq c\log n/\log\log n
by Krivelevich, Lew, and Michaeli
[
11
]
.
For
p=\omega\left(\log n/n\right)
, the following conjecture from
[
11
]
would provide an asymptotically tight bound on
d(n)
:

Conjecture 6.1
.

[
11
, Conjecture 1.11]

For every
p=\omega\left(\log n/n\right)
, the random graph
G\sim G(n,p)
is a.a.s.
d
-rigid for
d=(1-o(1))(1-\sqrt{1-p})n
.

Peled and Peleg verified this conjecture for
p=o(n^{-1/2})
in a recent paper
[
15
]
.
However, less is known in denser regimes.
In particular, Conjecture
6.1
would imply that
G(n,1/2)
is rigid for some
d(n)\sim\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{2}n
.
In this direction, Peled and Peleg asked whether there exists a constant
\varepsilon>0
such that
G(n,1/2)
is a.a.s. rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{\varepsilon n}
(
[
15
, Section 4(3)]
). In Theorem
1.8
, we answer their question affirmatively by showing that every
0<\varepsilon<7/32=0.21875
suffices.
Given that
\frac{2-\sqrt{2}}{2}\approx 0.2928
is an upper bound on
\varepsilon
, this provides a reasonable estimate for the rigidity of
G(n,1/2)
.
We now recall and prove Theorem
1.8
.

See
1.8

Proof.

For convenience, we assume that
n
is even. Let
v_{1},\dots,v_{n/2},u_{1},\dots u_{n/2}
denote the vertices of
G
. Define
G_{0}=G
and
G_{i}=G_{i-1}/\{u_{i},v_{i}\}
for
1\leq i\leq\frac{n}{2}
.
Then
G_{\frac{n}{2}}
is a graph with
\frac{n}{2}
vertices in which the
i
th and
j
th vertices are adjacent if and only if at least one of the edges
\{u_{i}u_{j},u_{i}v_{j},v_{i}u_{j},v_{i}v_{j}\}
is present in
G
. Thus
G_{\frac{n}{2}}\sim G(\frac{n}{2},15/16)
.
A.a.s.,
G_{\frac{n}{2}}
has minimum degree greater than
\frac{n}{4}+d
, see, e.g,
[
2
, Corollary 3.4]
. Hence, by Theorem
1.2
,
G_{\frac{n}{2}}
is
d
-rigid. We claim that, a.a.s., for every
0\leq i\leq\frac{n}{2}-1
, the graph
G_{i}
can be obtained from
G_{i+1}
by a
d
-dimensional spider splitting operation and the addition of new edges. Since this operation preserves rigidity in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, this implies that
G_{i}
is rigid for every
0\leq i\leq\frac{n}{2}
, thus completing the proof of the theorem.

To prove our claim, it remains to show that a.a.s.
|N_{G_{i}}(u_{i+1})\cap N_{G_{i}}(v_{i+1})|\geq d
holds for every
0\leq i\leq\frac{n}{2}-1
. This will follow by straightforward probabilistic arguments.
For
0\leq i\leq\frac{n}{2}-1
, let
X_{i}
denote the size of
N_{G_{i}}(u_{i+1})\cap N_{G_{i}}(v_{i+1})
. The graph
G_{i}
has
n-i
vertices and
i
of them represent contracted vertex pairs from
G_{0}
. Hence, for each
i
, we have
X_{i}=Y_{i}+Z_{i}
where
Y_{i}\sim{\rm Bin}\big(i,\frac{9}{16}\big)
and
Z_{i}\sim{\rm Bin}\big(n-2i-2,\frac{1}{4}\big)
are independent variables. In particular,

\mathbb{E}(X_{i})=\frac{9i}{16}+\frac{n-2i-2}{4}\geq\frac{1}{4}(n-2)>d+\frac{n}{64}.

Using Hoeffding’s inequality, we obtain that
\mathbb{P}(X_{i}<d)\leq e^{-\Omega(n)}
for every
i
. Hence, by the union bound, the probability that there is some
0\leq i\leq\frac{n}{2}-1
with
X_{i}<d
is
o(1)
.
∎

Prior to this result, the best known lower bound for
d=d(n)
was
d=\Omega(n/\log n)
, which followed as a special case of
[
11
, Theorem 1.10]
.
With straightforward modifications, the proof of Theorem
1.8
yields the following more general result.

Theorem 6.2
.

For every constant
p\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
0<p<1
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
G\sim G(n,p)
is a.a.s.
(\varepsilon n)
-rigid.

Concluding remarks

7.1
Edge count for degree sum conditions

It is clear that
\delta(G)\geq\frac{n+d}{2}-1
implies

|E|\geq\frac{n(n+d-2)}{4}
.
We show that that the degree sum condition
\deg(u)+\deg(v)\geq n+d-2

gives rise to the same bound.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph.
If

\deg(u)+\deg(v)\geq n+d-2

(19)

for all pairs of non-adjacent vertices in
G
, then

|E|\geq\frac{n(n+d-2)}{4}
.

Proof.

We shall use the following well-known inequality, which holds for
any
n
-tuple of real numbers
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}
:

\frac{(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i})^{2}}{n}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{2}

(20)

First we consider the non-edges of
G
and use (
19
) to deduce that

\frac{n(n-1)-2|E|}{2}(n+d-2)\leq\sum_{u,v\in V,uv\notin E}\deg(u)+\deg(v)=

=\sum_{v\in V}(n-1-\deg(v))\deg(v)=2|E|(n-1)-\sum_{v\in V}\deg(v)^{2}

holds. By applying (
20
), we can upper bound the RHS by

2|E|(n-1)-\frac{(\sum_{v}\deg(v))^{2}}{n}=\sum_{v\in V}(n-1-\frac{2|E|}{n})\deg(v)=2|E|(n-1-\frac{2|E|}{n})=2|E|(\frac{n(n-1)-2|E|}{n}).

By comparing the two sides we get

|E|\geq\frac{n(n+d-2)}{4}
, as claimed.
∎

7.2
Conditions for global rigidity

A framework
(G,p)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is
globally rigid
if every
d
-dimensional framework
(G,q)
in which corresponding edge lengths are the same as in
(G,p)
, is congruent to
(G,p)
. As in the case of rigidity,
the global rigidity of a generic framework is determined by its graph, for every fixed
d\geq 1
.
Thus we call
G

globally
d
-rigid
if every (or equivalently, if some) generic
d
-dimensional framework
(G,p)

is globally rigid.
We refer the reader to
[
10
]
for more details concerning
globally rigid graphs and frameworks.

The following result shows that
whenever a minimum degree or degree sum condition implies rigidity
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, it also
implies global rigidity in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}
.

Theorem 7.2
.

[
9
]

Let
G
be a graph and
d\geq 2
. If
G
is rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, then
G
is globally rigid
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}
.

It is well-known that every globally
d
-rigid graph on at least
d+2
vertices
is
(d+1)
-connected. Hence, combining Theorem
7.2
with our results leads to
tight bounds concerning global
d
-rigidity.
In particular, we obtain the following corollaries.

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph on
n
vertices and let
d\geq 2
. If either

(i)
\delta(G)\geq\left\lceil\frac{n+d-2}{2}\right\rceil
and
n\geq 29d
, or

(ii)
\eta(G)\geq n+d-2
and
n\geq d(d+2)
,

then
G
is globally rigid in
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.

It was proved in
[
3
]
that if
\delta(G)\geq\frac{n+1}{2}
, then

G
is globally rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. This statement is a special case of the following corollary.
A graph
G
is called
redundantly
d
-rigid
if
G-e
is
d
-rigid
for all
e\in E(G)
.

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph on
n\geq 5
vertices. If
\eta(G)\geq n+1
,
then

G
is globally rigid in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

Proof.

We are done by Theorems
5.1

and
7.2
, unless
G
is one of the
four exceptions.

The four exceptions are all

3
-connected and redundantly 2-rigid, so they are
also
globally 2-rigid by
[
4
]
.
∎

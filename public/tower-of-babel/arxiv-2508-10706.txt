Hasse norm principle for extensions of prime squared degree
Yasuhiro Oki

Abstract
. We give an equivalent condition for the validity of the Hasse norm principle for finite separable extensions of prime squared degree of global fields. Our theorem recovers the result of Drakokhrust–Platonov, which claims that the Hasse norm principle holds for adequate extensions of prime squared degree.

Introduction

Let
k
be a global field. For a finite separable field extension
K/k
, put

\Sha(K/k):=(k^{\times}\cap\N_{K/k}(\mathbb{A}_{K}))/\N_{K/k}(K^{\times}),

where
\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}
is the idèle group of
K
. In the paper of Scholz (
[
Sch40
]
), the group
\Sha(K/k)
is denoted by
\kappa(K/k)
and is called the
number knot
. We say that the
Hasse norm principle holds for
K/k
if

\Sha(K/k)=1.

The study of
\Sha(K/k)
is initiated by Hasse (
[
Has31
]
). More precisely, he proved that the Hasse norm principle holds for cyclic extensions. However, it is not always trivial in general. For example, Hasse also proved that the biquadratic extension
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-39},\sqrt{-3})/\mathbb{Q}
does not satisfy the Hasse norm principle. Hence, it is natural to discuss an equivalent condition for the validity of the Hasse norm principle. In this paper, we discuss the following:

Question 1.1
.

For
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, determine an equivalent condition on
K/k
of degree
d
for which the Hasse norm principle holds.

This has been fully resolved in the cases that

•

d
is a prime number (
[
Bar81a
]
);

•

d=4
(
[
Kun84
]
);

•

d=6
(
[
DP87
]
); and

•

8\leq d\leq 15
(
[
HKY22
]
,
[
HKY23
]
).

Especially, the result for
d=4
can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.2

(cf.
[
Kun84
, p. 1899]
)
.

Let
k
be a global field. Consider a finite separable field extension
K/k
of degree
p^{2}
with Galois closure
\widetilde{K}/k
. Put
G:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)
and
H:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/K)
.

(i)

If
\Sha(K/k)\neq 1
, then we have

(a)

G\cong V_{4}
, the Klein four group; or

(b)

G\cong\mathfrak{A}_{4}
, the alternating group of
4
-letters.

(ii)

If
(a)
or
(b)
is valid, then there is an isomorphism

\Sha(K/k)\cong\begin{cases}1&\text{if a decomposition group of $\widetilde{K}/k$ contains $(C_{2})^{2}$; }\\
\mathbb{Z}/2&\text{otherwise. }\end{cases}

In other cases, there exist numerous partial results on the above problem, including
[
Tat67
]
,
[
Gur78
]
,
[
Bar81b
]
,
[
VK84
]
,
[
DP87
]
,
[
Mac20
]
,
[
HKY25
]
, and
[
Oki25
]
. However, it is far from a complete solution.

This paper resolves Question
1.1
in the case where
d
is an odd prime squared, that is,
d=p^{2}
for some odd prime number
p
. We denote by
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
the group of matrices of size
2
with coefficients
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, and set

\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}):=\{g\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\mid\det(g)=1\}.

On the other hand, we use the notations as follows for any positive integer
n
:

•

C_{n}
is the cyclic group of order
n
; and

•

\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is the symmetric group of
n
-letters; and

•

\mathfrak{A}_{n}
is the alternating group of
n
-letters.

On the other hand, the
standard representation of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
(resp.
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
) refers to the
2
-dimensional representation of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
associated to the natural inclusion into
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
(resp.
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
).

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
6.5
)
.

Let
p>2
be an odd prime number, and
k
a global field. Consider a finite separable field extension
K/k
of degree
p^{2}
with Galois closure
\widetilde{K}/k
. Put
G:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)
and
H:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/K)
.

(i)

If
\Sha(K/k)\neq 1
, then the following holds:

(
\ast
)

there exists a subgroup
G^{\dagger}
of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
such that

G\cong(C_{p})^{2}\rtimes_{\varphi}G^{\dagger},\quad H\cong\{1\}\rtimes_{\varphi}G^{\dagger},

where
\varphi
is induced by the standard representation of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

(ii)

If
(
\ast
)
is valid, then there is an isomorphism

\Sha(K/k)\cong\begin{cases}1&\text{if a decomposition group of $\widetilde{K}/k$ contains $(C_{p})^{2}$; }\\
\mathbb{Z}/p&\text{otherwise. }\end{cases}

Combining Theorem
1.3
with Theorem
1.2
, we obtain a complete solution to Question
1.1
for arbitrary prime squared
d
.

Remark 1.4
.

Subgroups of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
are completely classified. See
[
Suz82
, Chapter 3, §6]
for detail. More precisely, if
G^{\dagger}
is a proper subgroup of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, then one of the following is satisfied:

•

G^{\dagger}\cong C_{p}\rtimes_{\varphi}C_{d}
, where
d
is a prime divisor of
p-1
, and
\varphi
is induced by the composite

C_{d}\xrightarrow{c\mapsto c^{2}}C_{d}\hookrightarrow\Aut(C_{p});

•

G^{\dagger}\cong C_{d}
, where
d
is a divisor of
p-1
or
p+1
;

•

G^{\dagger}\cong C_{d}\rtimes_{\varphi}C_{4}
, where
d
is an
odd
divisor of
p-1
or
p+1
, and
\varphi
is induced by the composite

C_{4}\twoheadrightarrow C_{2}\hookrightarrow\Aut(C_{d});

•

G^{\dagger}\cong\langle g,h\mid g^{d}=1,h^{2}=g^{n/2}\rangle
, where
d
is an
even
divisor of
p-1
or
p+1
;

•

p>3
and
G^{\dagger}\cong\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{3})
(a Schur covering of
\mathfrak{A}_{4}
);

•

p>3
and
G^{\dagger}\cong\widehat{\Sigma}_{4}
, where
\widehat{\Sigma}_{4}
is the Schur covering of
\mathfrak{S}_{4}
as in
[
Suz82
, Chapter 3, §6, Theorem 6.17 (iv)]
;

•

p\equiv\pm 1\bmod 5
and
G^{\dagger}\cong\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{5})
, a Schur covering of
\mathfrak{A}_{5}
.

Note that our proof of Theorem
1.3
do not use such a classification.

Remark 1.6
.

Theorem
1.3
recovers the result of Drakokhrust and Platonov (
[
DP87
, Theorem 4]
), which asserts that the Hasse norm principle for
K/k
of degree
p^{2}
with
p
an odd prime, if
K
is
k
-adequate in the sense of
[
Sch68
, p. 451, l. 11–13, Definition]
. See Theorem
6.6
.

For a finite separable field extension
E/F
, let

T_{K/k}:=\Ker(\N_{K/k}\colon\Res_{K/k}\mathbb{G}_{m,K}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m,k}).

It is called the
norm one torus
associated to
K/k
. Moreover, take a smooth compactification
X
of
T_{K/k}
over
F
, and put
\overline{X}:=X\otimes_{F}F^{\sep}
. Then, it is known that
H^{1}(k,\Pic(\overline{X}))
is isomorphic to a birational invariant called the
unramified Brauer group
of the function field of
X
.

The cohomology group
H^{1}(k,\Pic(\overline{X}))
is also related to the Hasse norm principle. Here, let
k
be a global field, and consider a finite separable extension
K/k
. Denote by
\Sigma
the set of all places of
k
. Then, Ono (
[
Ono63
, p. 70]
) constructed an isomorphism

\Sha(K/k)\cong\Sha^{1}(k,T_{K/k}).

Here, the right-hand side is the
Tate–Shafarevich group
of
T_{K/k}
, which is defined as the kernel of the homomorphism

(\Res_{k_{v}/k})_{v\in\Sigma}\colon H^{1}(k,T_{K/k})\rightarrow\prod_{v\in\Sigma}H^{1}(k_{v},T_{K/k}).

Furthermore, pick a smooth compactification
X
of
T_{K/k}
over
k
, and put
\overline{X}:=X\otimes_{k}k^{\sep}
. Then, there is an exact sequence of finite groups

0\rightarrow A(k,T_{K/k})\rightarrow H^{1}(k,\Pic(\overline{X}))^{\vee}\rightarrow\Sha^{1}(k,T_{K/k})\rightarrow 0.

Here,
A(k,T_{K/k})
is the defect of the weak approximation of
T_{K/k}
, that is,

A(k,T_{K/k}):=\left(\prod_{v\in\Sigma}T_{K/k}(k_{v})\right)/\overline{T_{K/k}(k)}.

Theorem 1.7

(Theorem
6.2
)
.

Let
p>2
be an odd prime number. Consider a finite separable field extension
K/k
of degree
p^{2}
with its Galois closure
\widetilde{K}/k
. Put
G:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)
and
H:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)
. Take a smooth compactification
X
of
T_{K/k}
, and put
\overline{X}:=X\otimes_{k}k^{\sep}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

H^{1}(k,\Pic(\overline{X}))\neq 0
;

(ii)

H^{1}(k,\Pic(\overline{X}))\cong\mathbb{Z}/p
;

(iii)

there exists a subgroup
G^{\dagger}
of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
such that

G\cong(C_{p})^{2}\rtimes_{\varphi}G^{\dagger},\quad H\cong\{1\}\rtimes_{\varphi}G^{\dagger},

where
\varphi
is induced by the standard representation of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

In the following, we sketch our proof of Theorems
1.3
and
1.7
. For a finite group
G
and a subgroup
H
of
G
, a
G
-lattice
J_{G/H}
is defined by the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\Ind_{H}^{G}\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow J_{G/H}\rightarrow 0.

Take an admissible set
\mathcal{D}
of subgroups in
G
in the sense of
[
Oki25
]
, and put

\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H}):=\Ker\left(H^{2}(G,J_{G/H})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{D\in\mathcal{D}}H^{2}(D,J_{G/H})\right).

Then, Theorems
1.3
and
1.7
are reduced to studies on
\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})
in the case
(G:H)=p^{2}
. Moreover, to discuss Theorem
1.7
, we only need the case that
\mathcal{D}
is the set of all cyclic subgroups
\mathcal{C}_{G}
in
G
. See Section
6
for details.

In the following, we write
\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})
for
\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})
if
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{C}_{G}
.

Case 1:
G
is a
p
-group

In this case, the exponent of
G
, the least common multiple of the orders of all the elements of
G
, is either
p
or
p^{2}
. See Section
2
.

Case 1-1:
G
has exponent
p^{2}
.

It suffices to prove
\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})=0
. To accomplish it, we use the same argument as
[
Oki25
]
as follows. Let
E
be a maximal subgroup of
G
containing
H
. Note that it is unique if
\#G\geq p^{3}
. Then, there is an exact sequence of
G
-lattices

0\rightarrow J_{G/E}\rightarrow J_{G/H}\rightarrow\Ind_{E}^{G}J_{E/H}\rightarrow 0.

Taking cohomology, we obtain an exact sequence

0\rightarrow H^{1}(E,J_{E/H})\rightarrow H^{2}(G,J_{G/E})\xrightarrow{i^{*}}H^{2}(G,J_{G/H})\rightarrow H^{2}(E,J_{E/H}).

We denote by
\Sel_{H}^{2}(G,J_{G/E})
the preimage of
\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})
under
i^{*}
. Then one has an exact sequence

0\rightarrow H^{1}(E,J_{E/H})\rightarrow\Sel_{H,\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/E})\xrightarrow{i^{*}}\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})\rightarrow 0.

On the other hand, there is a commutative diagram

\textstyle{\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{2}(G,J_{G/H})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\delta}
\textstyle{H^{2}(E,J_{E/H})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{3}(G,\mathbb{Z})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Res_{G/N}}
\textstyle{H^{3}(E,\mathbb{Z}).}

One can prove that the lower horizontal map
\Res_{G/N}
is injective. It follows from
H^{3}(G,\mathbb{Z})=0
if
\#G\leq p^{3}
. In the case
\#G=p^{p+1}
, the desired injectivity follows from Tahara’s theorem (
[
Tah72
]
). In the remaining cases, we reduce the assertion to the vanishing of the
Bogomolov multipler
of
G
, and apply the result of Fernández-Alcober and Jezernick (
[
FJ20
]
). Therefore, the study of
\Sel_{H}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})
is reduced to a determination of the preimage
\mathscr{S}_{H}^{2}(G,\Ind_{E}^{G}\mathbb{Z})
of
\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})
under the homomorphism
H^{2}(E,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^{2}(G,J_{G/E})
. However, an explicit computation using Mackey’s decomposition gives an isomorphism
\mathscr{S}_{H}^{2}(G,\Ind_{E}^{G}\mathbb{Z})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{2}
. This implies

\Sel_{H}^{2}(G,J_{G/E})\cong\mathbb{Z}/p,

and in particular we obtain
\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})=0
. Note that the case
\#G=p^{3}
is essentially contained in
[
HY25a
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Case 1-2:
G
has exponent
p
.

Let
E
be as in Case 1-1. We should consider
\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})
for any admissible set of subgroup
\mathcal{D}
of
G
. However, it is impossible to construct a direct analogue of the argument in Case 1-1. The crutial difference between Cases 1-1 and 1-2 is that the restriciton map
H^{3}(G,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^{3}(E,\mathbb{Z})
may not be injective. In fact, if
G
has order
p^{3}
, then we have the following:

H^{3}(G,\mathbb{Z})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{\oplus 2},\quad H^{3}(E,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/p.

To overcome this problem, we consider a
p
-group
\widetilde{G}
of which certain quotient is isomorphic to
G
. Let
\widetilde{G}
be the wreath product
(C_{p})^{p}\rtimes C_{p}
. Then one can construct a surjection

\pi\colon\widetilde{G}\twoheadrightarrow G.

See Lemma
2.9
(ii). Put
\widetilde{H}:=\pi^{-1}(H)
. Then
\pi
induces an isomorphism

\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})\cong\Sha_{\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}}^{2}(\widetilde{G},J_{\widetilde{G}/\widetilde{H}}),

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
is an admissible set of subgroups in
\widetilde{G}
that satisfies
\{\pi(D)\mid D\in\mathcal{D}\}=\mathcal{D}
. Furthermore, if we set
\widetilde{E}:=\pi^{-1}(E)
, then the restriction map
H^{3}(\widetilde{G},\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^{3}(\widetilde{E},\mathbb{Z})
is injective. This is already mentioned in Case 1-1. Therefore, we can apply the same method as Case 1-1 by considering the exact sequence of
\widetilde{G}
-lattices

0\rightarrow J_{\widetilde{G}/\widetilde{E}}\rightarrow J_{\widetilde{G}/\widetilde{H}}\rightarrow\Ind_{\widetilde{E}}^{\widetilde{G}}J_{\widetilde{E}/\widetilde{H}}\rightarrow 0.

Note that the case
\#G=p^{3}
is essentially contained in
[
HY25b
, Theorem 1.6 (2)]
.

Case 2: General case.

Let
P
be a
p
-Sylow subgroup of
G
. Denote by
P^{\prime}_{2}
the Heisenberg group of order
p^{3}
. If
P
is not isomoprhic to
(C_{p})^{2}
or
P^{\prime}_{2}
, then we obtain
\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})=0
by Case 1. Hence, we may assume that
P\cong(C_{p})^{2}
or
P\cong P^{\prime}_{2}
is valid. In this case, one can prove that at least one of the following is satisfied:

(a)

there exist a subgroup
G^{\prime}
of
G
with
(G:G^{\prime})\leq 2
and transitive groups
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
of degree
p
with
\#G_{1}\neq p
or
\#G_{2}\neq p
such that
G_{1}\times G_{2}<G^{\prime}<N_{\mathfrak{S}_{p}}(G_{1})\times N_{\mathfrak{S}_{p}}(G_{2})
;

(b)

there is a subgroup
G^{\prime}
of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
such that
G\cong(C_{p})^{2}\rtimes G^{\prime}
and
H\cong\{1\}\rtimes G^{\prime}
.

This follows from the results of Dobson and Witte (
[
DW02
]
).

Case 2-1: (a) holds.

In this case, there is an isomorphism
P\cong(C_{p})^{2}
. It suffices to discuss the case
G=G_{1}\times G_{2}
. Then the
p
-primary torsion part of
H^{2}(H,\mathbb{Z})
is trivial since
\#H\notin p\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover, Tahara’s theorem (
[
Tah72
]
) implies that the
p
-primary torsion part of
H^{3}(G,\mathbb{Z})
is also trivial. This implies
\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})=0
since the left-hand side is a
p
-abelian group.

Case 2-2: (b) holds.

Let
P
be a
p
-Sylow subgroup of
G
. Take a subgroup
N
of order
p^{2}
in
P
so that
N\cap H=\{1\}
. Then, one can construct a commutative diagram

\textstyle{\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Res_{G/P}}
\textstyle{H^{3}(N,\mathbb{Z})^{G^{\dagger}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{x\mapsto x}
\textstyle{\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(P,J_{P/(H\cap P)})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{3}(N,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/p.}

Moreover, it is known that
H^{2}(N_{p}^{2},\mathbb{Z})^{G^{\prime}}\neq 0
holds if and only if
G^{\prime}<\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Hence, we have
\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})=0
if
G^{\prime}
is not contained in
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. On the other hand, in the case
G^{\prime}<\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, it suffices to prove
\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})\cong\mathbb{Z}/p
. We can reduce to the case
G^{\prime}=\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Then, there is a generalized representation group of
G
of the form

(1.1)

1\rightarrow Z(P^{\prime}_{2})\rightarrow P^{\prime}_{2}\rtimes_{\widetilde{\varphi}}G^{\prime}\rightarrow G\rightarrow 1,

where
\widetilde{\varphi}
is a lift of the standard standard representation of
G^{\prime}
that acts trivially on the center of
P^{\prime}_{2}
. Our construction of
\widetilde{\varphi}
is based on the result of Winter (
[
Win72
]
), which studies the structure of the outer isomorphism group of
P^{\prime}_{2}
. Applying a refinement of the theory of Drakokhrust and Macedo–Newton to (
1.1
), we obtain the desired isomorphism. Finally, the condition for the vanishing of
\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})
is reduced to Case 1-2.

Organization of this paper
.

In Section
2

2.
Transitive groups of prime squared degree

Let
n
be a positive integer. Recall that a
transitive group of degree
n
(or, a
transitive subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
) is a subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
of which the action on the set
\mathbb{Z}/n
is transitive. Following
[
Oki25
, §4]
, we refer to a subgroup of the form
G\cap\Stab_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(i)
, where
i\in\mathbb{Z}/n
, as a
corresponding subgroup
of
G
.

Here, we mainly discuss the case where
n=p^{2}
, where
p
is a prime number. Moreover, we recall some facts on transitive groups of prime power degree.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
HHY20
, Theorem 3.2]
)
.

Let
p
be a prime number, and
n
a positive integer. Consider a transitive group
G
of degree
p^{n}
with its corresponding subgroup
H
. Then, a
p
-Sylow subgroup of
G_{p}
of
G
is a transitive group of degree
p^{n}
, and
H\cap G_{p}
is a corresponding subgroup of
G_{p}
.

For a prime number
p
, let
\ord_{p}\colon\mathbb{Q}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
be the homomorphism defined as

\ord_{p}(\ell)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }\ell=p;\\
0&\text{if }\ell\neq p\end{cases}

for each prime number
\ell
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
p
be a prime number, and
G
a transitive group of degree
p
. Then, a
p
-Sylow subgroup of
G
is cyclic of order
p
.

Proof.

This follows from
\ord_{p}(\#\mathfrak{S}_{p})=1
.

\blacksquare

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
p
be a prime number, and
G
a transitive group of degree
p
. Then,
\#G^{\ab}
is coprime to
p
if and only if
G\cong C_{p}
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\#G^{\ab}\in p\mathbb{Z}
if
G\cong C_{p}
. Conversely, assume that
\#G
is a multiple of
p
. Then,
\#G^{\der}
is coprime to
p
by Lemma
2.2
. Hence, we have
G^{\der}=\{1\}
, that is,
G
is abelian. This implies
G\cong C_{p}
as desired.

\blacksquare

Remark 2.4
.

A complete classification of transitive groups of prime degree is known. See
[
DM96
, p. 99, l. 15–34]
for example. Note that we do not use such a result in this paper.

Let
p
be a prime number. We recall some notations in
[
DW02
, §3]
to discuss transitive groups of degree
p^{2}
.

•

For each
i_{0}\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}
, put

z_{i_{0}}\colon\mathbb{F}_{p}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}\times\mathbb{F}_{p};\,(i,j)\mapsto\begin{cases}(i_{0},j+1)&\text{if }i=i_{0};\\
(i,j)&\text{if }i\neq i_{0}.\end{cases}

•

For
n\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
, let

\gamma_{n}:=z_{0}^{a_{p-n,0}}\cdots z_{p-1}^{a_{p-n,p-1}},

where

a_{i,j}:=\begin{cases}(-1)^{i-j}\begin{pmatrix}i\\
j\end{pmatrix}&\text{if }i\geq j;\\
0&\text{if }i<j.\end{cases}

Note that one has
a_{i+1,j}=a_{i,j}-a_{i,j-1}
.

•

We define
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
as follows:

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\colon\mathbb{F}_{p}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}

\displaystyle;\,(i,j)\mapsto(i,j+1);

\displaystyle\rho_{2}\colon\mathbb{F}_{p}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}

\displaystyle;\,(i,j)\mapsto(i+1,j).

•

Put
\tau:=z_{p-1}\rho_{2}
.

•

For each
\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}_{p}
, set

\displaystyle\overline{\beta}\colon\mathbb{F}_{p}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}

\displaystyle;\,(i,j)\mapsto(\beta i,j);

\displaystyle\widetilde{\beta}\colon\mathbb{F}_{p}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}

\displaystyle;\,(i,j)\mapsto(i,\beta j).

We regard them as an element of
\mathfrak{S}_{p}
by the bijection

\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathbb{F}_{p}\times\mathbb{F}_{p};\,a+pb\mapsto(a\bmod p,b\bmod p)\ (a,b\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}).

Now, for
n\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
, put

P_{n}:=\langle\tau,\gamma_{n}\rangle,\quad P^{\prime}_{n}:=\langle\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\gamma_{n}\rangle.

These are transitive group of degree
p^{2}
. We remark that
\#P_{n}=\#P^{\prime}_{n}=p^{n+1}
. Moreover, one has
P_{p}=P^{\prime}_{p}
, and it is a
p
-Sylow subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{p^{2}}
.

Definition 2.5
.

For each
n\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, put
\delta_{n}:=\rho_{2}\gamma_{n}\rho_{2}^{-1}
.

By definition, we have
P_{n}=\langle\tau,\delta_{n}\rangle
and
P^{\prime}_{n}=\langle\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\delta_{n}\rangle
.

Lemma 2.6
.

(i)

We have
(z_{p-1}\rho_{2})^{p}=\rho_{1}=\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{p}=z_{1}
.

(ii)

For any
n,n^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
, one has
[z_{n},z_{n^{\prime}}]=[z_{n},\delta_{n^{\prime}}]=[\delta_{n},\delta_{n^{\prime}}]=1
.

(iii)

For each
n\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
and
i\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}
, we have
[\delta_{n},\rho_{2}]=[\delta_{n},z_{i}\rho_{2}]=\delta_{n-1}
.

(iv)

For
n\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
, we have

\prod_{i=0}^{p-1}\rho_{2}^{i}\delta_{n}\rho_{2}^{-i}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }n<p;\\
\delta_{1}&\text{if }n=p.\end{cases}

(v)

We have
\delta_{2}\rho_{2}\delta_{2}^{-1}=\rho_{1}^{-1}\rho_{2}
.

(vi)

For any
\beta\in\mathbb{F}^{\times}_{p}
, we have the following:

\overline{\beta}\rho_{1}\overline{\beta}^{-1}=\rho_{1},\quad\overline{\beta}\rho_{2}\overline{\beta}^{-1}=\rho_{2}^{\beta},\quad\widetilde{\beta}\rho_{1}\widetilde{\beta}^{-1}=\rho_{1}^{\beta},\quad\widetilde{\beta}\rho_{2}\widetilde{\beta}^{-1}=\rho_{2}.

Proof.

These follow from direct computation.

\blacksquare

Definition 2.7
.

(i)

For
n\in\{0,\ldots,p\}
, put

E_{n}:=\begin{cases}\{1\}&\text{if }n=0;\\
\langle\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n}\rangle&\text{if }n\geq 1.\end{cases}

(ii)

For
n\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
, put

H_{n}:=\begin{cases}\{1\}&\text{if }n=1;\\
\langle\delta_{2},\ldots,\delta_{n}\rangle&\text{if }n\geq 2.\end{cases}

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
n\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
.

(i)

The group
E_{n}
is elementary abelian of order
p^{n}
containing
H_{n}
.

(ii)

The group
E_{n}
is maximal in
P_{n}
and
P^{\prime}_{n}
. In particular, we have
E_{n}=P_{n}\cap P^{\prime}_{n}
.

(iii)

We have
P_{n}^{\der}=(P^{\prime}_{n})^{\der}=E_{n-1}
, and
[E_{n^{\prime}},P_{n}]=[E_{n^{\prime}},P^{\prime}_{n}]=E_{n^{\prime}-1}
for any
n^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

(iv)

The group
H_{n}
is a corresponding subgroup of
P_{n}
and
P^{\prime}_{n}
.

Proof.

(i): By Lemma
2.6
(ii), we obtain that
E_{n}
is abelian. Moreover, by definition,
\delta_{i}
has order
p
and
\langle\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n^{\prime}-1}\rangle\cap\langle\delta_{n^{\prime}}\rangle=\{1\}
. Hence,
E_{n}
is elementary abelian of order
p^{n}
. On the other hand, the inclusion
H_{n}<E_{n}
follows from Lemma
2.6
(iii).

(ii): By Lemma
2.6
(iii), we have
E_{n}<P_{n}
. Hence, it is maximal since
\#E_{n}=p^{n}=p^{-1}\#P_{n}
.

(iii): This follows from Lemma
2.6
(iii).

(iv): By definition,
H_{n}
is a subgroup of
P_{n}
and
P^{\prime}_{n}
that stablizes
(0,0)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\times\mathbb{F}_{p}
in
P_{n}
. Hence, the assertion holds since
P_{n}
and
P^{\prime}_{n}
act on
\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}
transitively.

\blacksquare

Lemma 2.9
.

(i)

For each
n\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
, we have
P^{\prime}_{n}=E_{n}\rtimes\langle\rho_{2}\rangle
.

(ii)

We regard

as

-modules. Then, for any

, one has a commutative diagram

\textstyle{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\rho_{2}]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{2}\mapsto\rho_{2}\bmod(\rho_{2}-1)^{n}}
\textstyle{E_{p}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q_{n}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{F}_{p}[\rho_{2}]/(\rho_{2}-1)^{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\hskip 30.0pt\cong}
\textstyle{E_{n}.}

Here,

is defined as follows:

q_{n}(\delta_{n^{\prime}})=\begin{cases}\delta_{n^{\prime}-(p-n)}&\text{if }n^{\prime}\geq p-n;\\

Moreover, the above diagram induces an exact sequence

(2.1)

1\rightarrow E_{p-n}\rightarrow P_{p}\xrightarrow{\pi_{n}}P^{\prime}_{n}\rightarrow 1.

Proof.

(i): This follows from Lemma
2.6
(iii).

(ii): Let

. By the Frobenius reciprocity, the canonical injection

\mathbb{F}_{p}\hookrightarrow E_{n^{\prime}};\,1\bmod p\mapsto\gamma_{n^{\prime}}

induces a surjective homomorphism of

\mathbb{F}_{p}[\rho_{2}]\twoheadrightarrow E_{n^{\prime}}

This induces the isomorphism

On the other hand, we have

and therefore we obtain (

).

\blacksquare

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
n\in\{1,\ldots,p-1\}
.

(i)

The following holds:

Z(P^{\prime}_{n})=\begin{cases}P^{\prime}_{1}&\text{if }n=1;\\
\langle\delta_{1}\rangle&\text{if }n\geq 2.\end{cases}

(ii)

For any

, one has

. In particular,
P_{n}
has exponent
p^{2}
.

(iii)

are contained in
E_{n}
.

Proof.

(i): It is clear in the case

. In the following, assume

. By Lemma

(ii) and (iii), we have

(2.2)

[\delta_{2}^{m_{2}}\cdots\delta_{n}^{m_{n}},\delta_{2}^{m^{\prime}_{2}}\cdots\delta_{n}^{m^{\prime}_{n}}z_{p-1}\rho_{2}]=\delta_{1}^{-m_{2}}\cdots\delta_{n-1}^{-m_{n-1}}

for any

. Hence, (
2.2
) for

implies

. Furthermore, (
2.2
) for

implies the desired assertion.

(ii): Take

. Then, there exist

such that

Replacing

with its suitable power, we may assume

. Then, we have

g^{p}=\left(\prod_{i=0}^{p-1}(z_{p-1}\rho_{2})^{i}\delta_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots\delta_{n}^{m_{n}}(z_{p-1}\rho_{2})^{-i}\right)(z_{p-1}\rho_{2})^{p}

Moreover, by Lemma

(ii), one has

(2.3)

\prod_{i=0}^{p-1}(z_{p-1}\rho_{2})^{i}\delta_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots\delta_{n}^{m_{n}}(z_{p-1}\rho_{2})^{-i}=\prod_{n^{\prime}=1}^{n}\left(\prod_{i=0}^{p-1}\rho_{2}^{i}\delta_{n^{\prime}}\rho_{2}^{-i}\right)^{m_{n^{\prime}}}.

The right-hand hand of (

, which follows from Lemma

(iii): It is clear in the case

. In the following, assume

. Pick a subgroup

that is not contained in

. It suffices to prove that

\blacksquare

.

.

Lemma 2.12

Let

Proof.

This is pointed out in

Case 1.

has exponent a multiple of

.

In this case, there is an element of order
p^{2}
in

, and it is a

-cycle in

. Hence, we obtain

.

Case 2. The remaining case.

By Lemma
2.1
, we may assume that
G
is a
p
-group. Take a corresponding subgroup
H
of

. It suffices to prove the existence of a subgroup

of order

in

Proposition 3.1

\overline{X}:=X\otimes_{k}k^{\sep}
. Then there is an isomorphism

\Br(X)/\Br(k)\cong H^{1}(k,\Pic(\overline{X}))\cong\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(G,X^{*}(T)).

We remark that a smooth compactifications exist for all tori over
k
. It follows from Hironaka (
[
Hir64
]
) if
k
has characteristic zero. On the other hand, if
k
has positive characteristic, the assertion is a consequence of Colliot-Thélène–Harari–Skorobogatov (
[
CHS05
]
).

For a finite separable field extension
K/k
, put

T_{K/k}:=\{t\in\Res_{K/k}\mathbb{G}_{m,K}\mid\N_{K/k}(t)=1\},

which is an
k
-torus of dimension
[K:k]-1
. It is called the
norm one torus
associated to
K/k
.

A group-theoretic description of
X^{*}(T_{K/k})
is given as follows. Let
\widetilde{K}/k
be a finite Galois extension that contains
K
. Set
G:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)
and
H:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/K)
. Then, there is an isomorphism of
G
-lattices

X^{*}(T_{K/k})\cong J_{G/H}.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
p>2
be an odd prime number. Consider a finite separable field extension
K/k
of degree
p^{2}
with its Galois closure
\widetilde{K}/k
. Put
G:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)
and
H:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)
. Take a smooth compactification
X
of
T_{K/k}
, and put
\overline{X}:=X\otimes_{k}k^{\sep}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

H^{1}(k,\Pic(\overline{X}))\neq 0
;

(ii)

H^{1}(k,\Pic(\overline{X}))\cong\mathbb{Z}/p
;

(iii)

there exists a subgroup
G^{\dagger}
of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
such that

G\cong(C_{p})^{2}\rtimes_{\varphi}G^{\dagger},\quad H\cong\{1\}\rtimes_{\varphi}G^{\dagger},

where
\varphi
is induced by the standard representation of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.1
, there is an isomorphism
H^{1}(k,\Pic(\overline{X}))\cong\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})
. Hence the assertion follows from Theorem
5.13
.

\blacksquare

6.2.
Hasse norm principle

Here, let
k
be a global field, and fix a separable closure
k^{\sep}
of
k
. For a finite Galois extension
\widetilde{K}/k
, write
\Sigma_{\widetilde{K}}
for the set of places of
\widetilde{K}
. For each
v\in\Sigma_{\widetilde{K}}
, denote by
D_{v}
the decomposition group of
\widetilde{K}/k
at
v
.

For a finite separable field extension
K/k
, put

\Sha(K/k):=(k^{\times}\cap\N_{K/k}(\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}))/\N_{K/k}(K^{\times}),

where
\mathbb{A}_{K}^{\times}
is the idèle group of
K
. We say that the
Hasse norm principle holds for
K/k
if

\Sha(K/k)=1.

Let
T
be a torus over
k
. Then, define the
Tate–Shafarevich group
of
T
as follows:

\Sha^{1}(k,T):=\Ker\left(H^{1}(k,T)\xrightarrow{(\Res_{k_{v}/k})_{v}}\prod_{v\in\Sigma_{k}}H^{1}(k_{v},T)\right),

Here,
\Res_{k_{v}/k}\colon H^{1}(k,T)\rightarrow H^{1}(k_{v},T)
denotes the restriction map for each
v\in\Sigma_{k}
.

The following is essentially the same as the Poitou–Tate duality (
[
NSW00
, (8.6.8) Proposition]
).

Proposition 6.3

(
[
Oki25
, Proposition 2.22]
)
.

Let
k
be a global field, and
T
a
k
-torus which splits over a finite Galois extension
\widetilde{K}
of
k
. Put
G:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)
. Denote by
\mathcal{D}
the set of decomposition groups of
\widetilde{K}/k
, which is an admissible set of subgroups of
G
. Then there is an isomorphism

\Sha^{1}(k,T)\cong\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,X^{*}(T))^{\vee}.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
K/k
be a finite separable field extension with Galois closure
\widetilde{K}/k
. Put
G:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)
and
H:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/K)
. We denote by
\mathcal{D}
the set of decomposition groups of
\widetilde{K}/k
. Then there is an isomorphism

\Sha(K/k)\cong\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})^{\vee}.

Proof.

By Ono’s theorem (
[
Ono63
, p. 70]
), there is an isomorphism
\Sha(K/k)\cong\Sha^{1}(k,T_{K/k})
. Hence, the assertion follows from Proposition
6.3
.

\blacksquare

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
p>2
be an odd prime number, and
k
a global field. Consider a finite separable field extension
K/k
of degree
p^{2}
with its Galois closure
\widetilde{K}/k
. Put
G:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)
and
H:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/K)
.

(i)

If
\Sha(K/k)\neq 1
, then the following holds:

(
\ast
)

there exists a subgroup
G^{\dagger}
of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
such that

G\cong(C_{p})^{2}\rtimes_{\varphi}G^{\dagger},\quad H\cong\{1\}\rtimes_{\varphi}G^{\dagger},

where
\varphi
is induced by the standard representation of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

(ii)

If
(
\ast
)
is valid, then there is an isomorphism

\Sha(K/k)\cong\begin{cases}1&\text{if a decomposition group of $\widetilde{K}/k$ contains $(C_{p})^{2}$; }\\
\mathbb{Z}/p&\text{otherwise. }\end{cases}

Proof.

(i): If
\Sha(K/k)\neq 1
, then Corollary
6.4
implies
\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})\neq 0
. Hence, the assertion follows from Theorem
5.13
(i).

(ii): Let
\widetilde{K}/k
be the Galois closure of
K/k
, and put
G:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)
and
H:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/K)
. Denote by
\mathcal{D}
the set of decomposition groups of
\widetilde{K}/k
. Then, Corollary
6.4
gives an isomorphism

\Sha(K/k)\cong\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})^{\vee}.

Therefore, the assertion follows from Theorem
5.13
(ii).

\blacksquare

Finally, we give an alternative proof of a theorem Drakokhrust and Platonov (
[
DP87
]
). Recall
[
Sch68
, p. 451, l. 11–13, Definition]
that a finite extension
K/k
of a number field is
k
-adequate
if there is a central division ring over
k
that contains
K
as a maximal commutative subfield.

Theorem 6.6

(cf.
[
DP87
, Theorem 4]
)
.

Let
p>2
be an odd prime number, and
K/k
a finite extension of a global field of degree
p^{2}
. If
K
is
k
-adequate, then the Hasse norm principle holds for
K/k
.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{K}/k
be the Galois closure of
K/k
, and put
G:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)
and
H:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/K)
. Pick a
p
-Sylow subgroup
P
of
G
. If
P\not\cong P^{\prime}_{1}
and
P\not\cong P^{\prime}_{2}
, then the assertion follows from Theorem
6.5
(i). In the following, we assume that
P\cong P^{\prime}_{1}
or
P\cong P^{\prime}_{2}
is true. By
[
Bar81b
, Lemma 4]
, there exists a place of
\widetilde{K}
at which the decomposition group
D
in
G
satisfies
P=(P\cap D)\cdot(P\cap H)
. In particular, we have
\#D\geq p^{2}
since the assumption on
P
implies
\#(P\cap H)\leq p
. Moreover, the inequality
\#(P\cap D)\geq p^{2}
implies
(C_{p})^{2}<D
by Lemma
2.11
(ii). Hence, the assertion follows from Theorem
6.5
(ii).

\blacksquare

Remark 6.7
.

Let
K/k
be a finite separable extension. By
[
Vos69
, Theorem 5]
, one has an exact sequence

0\rightarrow A_{k}(T_{K/k})\rightarrow H^{1}(k,\Pic(\overline{X}))^{\vee}\rightarrow\Sha^{1}(k,T_{K/k})\rightarrow 0.

Here,
X
is a smooth compactification of
T_{K/k}
over
k
,
\overline{X}:=X\otimes_{k}k^{\sep}
, and
A_{k}(T_{K/k})
is the quotient of
\prod_{v\in\Sigma_{k}}T_{K/k}(k_{v})
by the closure of
T(k)
. On the other hand, by Propositions
6.1
and
6.3
, one has isomorphsims

H^{1}(k,\Pic(\overline{X}))^{\vee}\cong\Sha_{\omega}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})^{\vee},\quad\Sha^{1}(k,T_{K/k})\cong\Sha_{\mathcal{D}}^{2}(G,J_{G/H})^{\vee}.

Here,
\widetilde{K}/k
is the Galois closure of
K/k
,
G:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)
,
H:=\Gal(\widetilde{K}/K)
, and
\mathcal{D}
is the set of decomposition groups of
\widetilde{K}/k
. Therefore, we can also determine the structure of
A_{k}(T_{K/k})
in the case
[K:k]=p^{2}
with
p
an odd prime number.

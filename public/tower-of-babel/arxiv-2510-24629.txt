Approximate fibrations in higher topos theory
Christian Kremer, Marco Volpe

Abstract
The goal of this paper is to put the theory of approximate fibrations into the framework of higher topos theory. We define the notion of an approximate fibration for a general geometric morphism of ∞ \infty -topoi, give several characterizations in terms of shape theory and compare it to the original definition for maps of topological spaces of Coram and Duvall. Furthermore, we revisit the notion of cell-like maps between topoi, and generalize Lurie’s shape-theoretic characterization by giving a purely topos-theoretical proof.



1
Introduction

Approximate fibrations, introduced by Coram and Duvall in
[
CPFD77
]
, are a class of maps between topological spaces enlarging that of proper Hurewicz fibrations. Informally, these are continuous functions satisfying a more relaxed homotopy lifting property, in the sense that lifts only exist up to an appropriate notion of closeness. We recall the precise definition in
Section

4.3
. They admit a surprisingly robust bundle theory: for instance, approximate fibrations between manifolds satisfy an
h
-principle
[
HTW90
]
, and those with manifold total space can be studied via surgery theory
[
Cha81
]
.

Approximate fibrations appear naturally in geometric topology. A key example comes from neighborhood theory. A theorem of Hughes-Taylor-Weinberger-Williams
[
HTWW00
]
identifies germs of neighborhoods of locally flatly embedded submanifolds with approximate fibrations having manifold total space and spherical homotopy fiber. Approximate fibrations have also been used to recast Farrell’s fibering theorem
[
Far72
]
, which gives a
K
-theoretic obstruction to deforming a map
p\colon M^{d}\to S^{1}
(
d\geq 6
) into a fiber bundle projection with manifold fiber. In fact, such a map is homotopic to a fiber bundle projection precisely when it is homotopic to an approximate fibration. More generally, while direct extensions of Farrell’s theorem to arbitrary bases are difficult, one can still find complete
K
-theoretic obstructions to deforming a map with target an aspherical manifold to an approximate fibration, for example when the fundamental group is hyperbolic in the sense of Gromov
[
FLS18
]
.

A particularly notable subclass of approximate fibrations is given by cell-like maps. These are proper maps whose fibers have contractible shape
[
Lac69
]
. Cell-like maps are central to manifold topology. Chapman
[
Cha74
]
and West
[
Wes77
]
used them to prove that every compact topological manifold has a preferred simple homotopy type, well defined up to homeomorphism. They are indispensable tools in the proofs of the generalized Schönflies theorem
[
Bro60
]
, Cannon’s double suspension theorem
[
Can79
]
, and in the study of manifold factors. The guiding principle, initiated by Siebenmann
[
Sie72
]
, is that under dimension restrictions, cell-like maps between manifolds can be approximated by homeomorphisms. Finally, cell-like maps and decomposition theory played a decisive role in Freedman’s work on the 4-dimensional Poincaré conjecture
[
Fre82
,
BKK+21
]
.

These examples suggest that many deep questions in geometric topology can be profitably reformulated in terms of cell-like maps and approximate fibrations. In
[
Lur09
, Proposition 7.3.6.6]
, Lurie gave a topos-theoretic characterization of cell-like maps, recasting their definition in the modern language of
\infty
-topoi. In
Section

2
we recall the main notions and facts from higher topos theory that we rely on in this paper, and to formulate the main results in this introduction. The topos theoretic perspective on cell-like maps opens the possibility of re-deriving classical results, such as those of Chapman and West, in a purely categorical framework. This is carried out in an ongoing project of the second named author in collaboration with Maxime Ramzi and Sebastian Wolf, generalizing results previously announced in
[
Nik23
]
.

The first contribution of this paper is to generalize Lurie’s theorem to a broader setting, including a purely topos-theoretic proof (and generalization) of Lacher’s characterization of cell-like maps in terms of hereditary shape equivalences
[
Lac69
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p:\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
be a proper geometric morphism. Assume that locally constant objects in
\mathscr{X}
and
\mathscr{Y}
are hypercomplete, and that the hypercompletion of
\mathscr{Y}
has enough points. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

p
is a cell-like geometric morphism, i.e.
p^{\ast}
is fully faithful;

(2)

p
is a hereditary shape equivalence, i.e. for each
U\in\mathscr{Y}
, the geometric morphism
p_{/U}:\mathscr{X}_{/U}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}_{/U}
is a shape equivalence;

(3)

for each point
y:\mathrm{An}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
, the fiber of
p
at
y
has trivial shape.

After passing to the associated sheaf
\infty
-topoi, the third condition in
Theorem

1.1
corresponds to Lacher’s original definition of a cell-like mapping of topological spaces.
The assumption on locally constant objects is satisfied, for example, by topoi of the form
\mathscr{S}\text{hv}({X};{\mathrm{An}})
, where
X
is either a locally compact ANR or a paracompact space of finite covering dimension. For any topological space
X
, the hypercompletion of
\mathscr{S}\text{hv}({X};{\mathrm{An}})
has enough points (see
Remark

2.1
).

The second and more novel contribution is to provide a topos-theoretic characterization of approximate fibrations, entirely parallel to Lurie’s characterization of cell-like maps. We make use of the framework of
internal higher categories
developed by Martini-Wolf
[
MW25
,
MW23
]
to introduce the concept of an approximate fibration in the setting of geometric morphisms of
\infty
-topoi. A proper geometric morphism
f\colon\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{Y}
is called an
approximate fibration
if, for every
U\in\mathscr{Y}
, the functor
{f_{/U}}_{\ast}{f_{/U}}^{\ast}\colon\mathscr{Y}_{/U}\longrightarrow\mathscr{Y}_{/U}
is corepresentable, subject to additional compatibilities detailed in
Section

4.2
. A reader familiar with shape theory may view the definition of approximate fibrations as a rigorous way to formulate the requirement that the shape of
\mathscr{X}
be constant, in a manner parametrized by
\mathscr{Y}
. We provide a more intuitive reformulation of approximate fibrations in terms of shape theory, under the assumption that
\mathscr{X}
and
\mathscr{Y}
are locally contractible
\infty
-topoi.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f:\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
be a proper geometric morphism. Assume that
\mathscr{X}
and
\mathscr{Y}
are locally contractible and that the hypercompletion of
\mathscr{Y}
has enough points. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

f
is an approximate fibration;

(2)

f
is a shape fibration, i.e. for each
U\in\mathscr{Y}
, the map
\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X}_{/f^{\ast}U})\rightarrow\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{Y}_{U})\times_{\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{Y})}\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})
is an equivalence;

(3)

f
is a shape quasi-fibration, i.e. for each point
y:\mathrm{An}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
, the sequence
\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X}_{y})\rightarrow\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})\rightarrow\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{Y})
is a fiber sequence.

The condition of being locally contractible is satisfied, for example, by topoi of the form
\mathscr{S}\text{hv}({X};{\mathrm{An}})
, where
X
is a CW-complex or a locally compact ANR. The concepts of internally compact objects, internally filtered colimits and the characterization of proper maps in terms of the latter given in
[
MW23
]
play a fundamental role in most of our arguments. Combining our characterization with earlier theorems of
[
HTW90
]
, we find that our newly introduced concept of an approximate fibration encompasses the classical one.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be a proper map of locally compact ANRs. Then
f
is an approximate fibration if and only if the associated geometric morphism
f:\mathscr{S}\text{hv}({X};{\mathrm{An}})\rightarrow\mathscr{S}\text{hv}({Y};{\mathrm{An}})
is an approximate fibration.

Understanding approximate fibrations in this terms is a step toward importing techniques from higher topos theory into manifold topology. We hope this will ultimately clarify how topos theory interacts with classical obstruction theories (such as
[
Far72
,
FLS18
]
), and will provide new perspectives on the topology of manifolds.

2
Recollections on topoi and internal category theory

We recall the language of
\infty
-topoi and internal higher category theory used throughout this article. We mostly follow from the presentations given in
[
Lur09
,
MW25
,
MW23
]
. We also use this section to introduce the notations we use in the rest of the article.

2.1
Topos theory

For any
\infty
-category
I
, we write
\mathscr{PS}\text{hv}({I})
for the
\infty
-category of presheaves of animaes on
I
. An
\infty
-category
\mathscr{X}
is called an
\infty
-topos
if there exists a small
\infty
-category
I
such that
\mathscr{X}
is a left exact accessible Bousfield localization of
\mathscr{PS}\text{hv}({I})
. Other useful characterizations are provided by the so called
Giraud’s axioms
(see
[
Lur09
, Section 6.1]
). A
geometric morphism
of topoi
f\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
is the datum of a right adjoint functor
f_{*}\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
whose left adjoint is left exact (i.e. preserves finite limits). We write
\mathscr{T}\mathrm{op}
for the category of topoi and geometric morphism.

For any topological space
X
, the
\infty
-category
\mathscr{S}\text{hv}({X};{\mathrm{An}})
of sheaves of animae on
X
is an
\infty
-topos. This example serves as the foundation for most of the applications of interest in this paper. Note that a continuous map
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
induces a functor
f_{*}\colon\mathscr{S}\text{hv}({X};{\mathrm{An}})\rightarrow\mathscr{S}\text{hv}({Y};{\mathrm{An}})
. This is usually referred to as the
pushforward along
f
. The functor
f_{\ast}
admits a left exact left adjoint
f^{\ast}
, called the
pullback along
f
, and hence it is part of a geometric morphism.

Every topos has a final object, which we usually denote as
1\in\mathscr{Y}
. The functor corepresented by
1\in\mathscr{Y}
admits a left exact left adjoint, given as the unique colimit-preserving extension of the functor sending the point
1\in\mathrm{An}
to
1\in\mathscr{Y}
. There is a unique colimit-preserving left exact functor
\mathrm{An}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
, so
\mathrm{An}
is the terminal object in
\mathscr{T}\mathrm{op}
.

For an object
U
in a topos
\mathscr{Y}
, the forgetful map
(\pi_{U})_{\sharp}\colon\mathscr{Y}_{/U}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
admits a right adjoint which itself admits a right adjoint. The geometric morphism presented by the double-right adjoint of
(\pi_{U})_{\sharp}
will be denoted by
\pi_{U}\colon\mathscr{Y}_{/U}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
. An
étale geometric morphism
is a geometric morphism equivalent in
\mathscr{T}\mathrm{op}
to a morphism of the type
\pi_{U}\colon\mathscr{Y}_{/U}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
for some topos
\mathscr{Y}
and
U\in\mathscr{Y}
.

A map
f\colon U\rightarrow V
in a topos
\mathscr{Y}
is an
effective epimorphism
if the augmented simplicial diagram
(U\times_{V}\dots\times_{V}U)_{p\in\Delta^{\opposite}}\rightarrow V
is a colimit diagram. An étale morphism is an
étale cover
if it is equivalent to
\pi_{U}\colon\mathscr{Y}_{/U}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
for some effective epimorphism
U\rightarrow 1
.

An object
F\in\mathscr{Y}
is
constant
if it lies in the essential image of
p^{*}\colon\mathrm{An}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
, where
p
is the unique geometric morphism to
\mathrm{An}
. An object
F\in\mathscr{Y}
is
locally constant
if there exists a collection of objects
V_{i}\in\mathscr{Y}
such that
\coprod_{i}V_{i}\rightarrow 1
,
i\in I
is an effective epimorphism, and
\pi_{V_{i}}^{*}(F)\in\mathscr{Y}_{/V_{i}}
is constant for each
i\in I
. A locally constant object is said to have
compact values
if there exists a étale cover of
1
as above such that each restriction
\pi_{V_{i}}^{*}(F)\in\mathscr{Y}_{/V_{i}}
is the pullback of a compact object in
\mathrm{An}
along the unique geometric morphism
\mathscr{Y}_{/V_{i}}\rightarrow\mathrm{An}
. We write
\mathscr{Y}^{lc}
for the full subcategory of
\mathscr{Y}
spanned by the locally constant objects.

2.2
Points and hypercompletions

An object
U\in\mathscr{Y}
is
n
-truncated
if
\mathrm{Map}(V,U)\in\mathrm{An}
is an
n
-truncated space for each
V\in Y
. A morphism
f\colon U\rightarrow V
is
\infty
-connective if it for each
n\geq 0
and each
n
-truncated object
W\in\mathscr{Y}_{/V}
the map
\mathrm{Map}_{\mathscr{Y}_{/V}}(V,W)\rightarrow\mathrm{Map}_{\mathscr{Y}_{/V}}(U,W)
is an equivalence. If
f\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
is a geometric morphism, then
f^{*}\colon\mathscr{Y}\rightarrow\mathscr{X}
sends
\infty
-connective morphisms to
\infty
-connective morphisms
[
Lur09
, Prop. 6.5.1.16.]
.

An object
U
in an arbitrary
\infty
-topos is
hypercomplete
if it is local with respect to
\infty
-connective morphisms - for each
\infty
-connective morphism
f\colon V\rightarrow W
in
\mathscr{X}
, the map

f^{*}\colon\mathrm{Map}(W,U)\rightarrow\mathrm{Map}(V,U)

is an equivalence. An
\infty
-topos is said to be
hypercomplete
if each of its objects is hypercomplete. Since
f^{\ast}
preserves
\infty
-connective morphisms, it follows that
f_{\ast}
preserves hypercomplete objects.

The inclusion of the full subcategory on the hypercomplete topoi has a right adjoint
(-)^{{}^{\text{hyp}}}
called
hypercompletion
, whose adjunction counit
j\colon\mathscr{Y}^{{}^{\text{hyp}}}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
is such that
j_{*}
is equivalent to the inclusion of the subcategory spanned by the hypercomplete objects.

A
point
of an
\infty
-topos
\mathscr{X}
is a geometric morphism
\mathrm{An}\xrightarrow{x}\mathscr{X}
. Since the
\infty
-topos
\mathrm{An}
is hypercomplete, we get an equivalence in
\mathrm{An}

\mathrm{Map}_{\mathscr{T}\mathrm{op}}(\mathrm{An},\mathscr{X})\simeq\mathrm{Map}_{\mathscr{T}\mathrm{op}}(\mathrm{An},\mathscr{X}^{{}^{\text{hyp}}})

of points in
\mathscr{X}
and points in the hypercompletion
\mathscr{X}^{{}^{\text{hyp}}}
.
An
\infty
-topos
\mathscr{X}
is said to have
enough points
if it is possible to detect equivalences by pulling back along points. That is, if
f\colon U\rightarrow V\in\mathscr{X}
is an equivalence if and only if for each point
x\colon\mathrm{An}\rightarrow\mathscr{X}
the morphism
x^{*}f\colon x^{*}U\rightarrow x^{*}V
. A topos with enough points is automatically hypercomplete by
[
Lur09
, Rmk. 6.5.4.7.]
.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
X
be any topological space. It is well known that
\mathscr{S}\text{hv}({X})
is not necessarily hypercomplete (e.g. take
X
to be the Hilbert cube), and therefore does not have enough points. However,
\mathscr{S}\text{hv}({X})^{\text{hyp}}
has enough points for any topological space
X
. This follows from the fact that in
\mathscr{S}\text{hv}({X})
, the class of
\infty
-connective morphisms agrees with that of stalkwise equivalences (see
[
Lur17
, Lemma A.3.9]
).

2.3
Internal higher categories

Given a topos
\mathscr{Y}
, Martini-Wolf introduce the notion of a
category internal to
\mathscr{Y}
as a sheaf of categories on
\mathscr{Y}
, i.e. a limit-preserving functor
\mathscr{Y}^{\opposite}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}\mathrm{at}
. We get a category

\mathscr{C}\mathrm{at}_{\mathscr{Y}}\coloneqq\Fun^{R}(\mathscr{Y}^{\opposite},\mathscr{C}\mathrm{at})\simeq\mathscr{Y}\otimes\mathscr{C}\mathrm{at}\in\mathrm{Pr}^{L}

whose objects we will shortly refer to a
\mathscr{Y}
-categories
and whose morphisms will be called
\mathscr{Y}
-functors
.

Given a
\mathscr{Y}
-category
\mathscr{C}
and
U\in\mathscr{Y}
, the value
\mathscr{C}(U)
will be referred to as the
category of sections of
\mathscr{C}
over
U
. In particular,
\mathscr{C}(1)
will be called the category of
global sections
. The global section functor admits a left adjoint
\mathrm{const}\colon\mathscr{C}\mathrm{at}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}\mathrm{at}_{\mathscr{Y}}
, assigning to a category
I
its associated
constant
\mathscr{Y}
-category.

The category of
\mathscr{Y}
-categories is cartesian closed, we write
\underline{\mathrm{Fun}}(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{D})
for the
\mathscr{Y}
-category of
\mathscr{Y}
-functors
from
\mathscr{C}
to
\mathscr{D}
. We also write
\Fun_{\mathscr{Y}}(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{D})
for its category of global sections, the category of
\mathscr{Y}
-functors from
\mathscr{C}
to
\mathscr{D}
. Note that the bifunctor
\Fun_{\mathscr{Y}}(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{D})
enhances
\mathscr{C}\mathrm{at}_{\mathscr{Y}}
to an
(\infty,2)
-category.

Since
\mathscr{C}\mathrm{at}_{\mathscr{Y}}
is an
(\infty,2)
-category, the notion of
adjunctions
makes sense for
\mathscr{Y}
-categories, which naturally leads to the notions of
\mathscr{Y}
-limits and colimits using the diagonal functor
\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\underline{\mathrm{Fun}}(I,\mathscr{C})
for
\mathscr{Y}
-categories
I
and
\mathscr{C}
.

The
\mathscr{Y}
-category of
\mathscr{Y}
-groupoids
is defined as the sheaf
\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}\colon U\mapsto\mathscr{Y}_{/U}
. In many ways,
\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}
plays a similar role as that of
\mathrm{An}
in usual higher category theory. Another useful example of a
\mathscr{Y}
-category is that of locally constant objects. More precisely, this is given by the functor
\mathscr{Y}^{\opposite}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}\mathrm{at}_{\scalebox{1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\infty$}}
defined by
U\mapsto(\mathscr{Y}_{/U})^{lc}
. We denote this
\mathscr{Y}
-category by
\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}^{lc}
. There is an obvious fully faithful
\mathscr{Y}
-functor
\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}^{lc}\hookrightarrow\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a
\mathscr{Y}
-category. There is a mapping
\mathscr{Y}
-functor
\mathrm{Map}_{\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}}(-,-)\colon\mathscr{C}^{\opposite}\times\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}
. For
U\in\mathscr{Y}
it evaluates to a functor
\mathrm{Map}_{\mathscr{C}}(-,-)_{U}\colon\mathscr{C}(U)^{\opposite}\times\mathscr{C}(U)\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}_{/U}
.
In the special case
\mathscr{C}=\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}
there is an identification

\mathrm{Map}_{\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}}(-,-)_{U}\simeq\text{\lx@text@underline{Hom}}_{\scalebox{1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{Y}_{/U}$}}(-,-)\colon\mathscr{Y}_{/U}^{\opposite}\times\mathscr{Y}_{/U}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}_{/U}

with the internal hom induced by the cartesian monoidal structure on
\mathscr{Y}_{/U}
.
Furthermore, for
U\in\mathscr{Y}
and arbitrary
\mathscr{C}
there is an equivalence

\mathrm{Map}_{\mathscr{C}}(\pi_{U}^{*}(-),\pi_{U}^{*}(-))_{U}\simeq\pi_{U}^{*}\mathrm{Map}_{\mathscr{C}}(-,-)_{1}.

2.4
Internal topos theory

In
[
MW25
]
, Martini-Wolf introduce the notion of a
topos internal to
\mathscr{Y}
, which is a certain class of categories internal to
\mathscr{Y}
satisfying analogs of ordinary definitions of topoi to internal higher category theory. A
geometric morphism of
\mathscr{Y}
-topoi

F\colon\Theta\rightarrow\Lambda
is the datum of a right adjoint
\mathscr{Y}
-functor
F_{*}\colon\Theta\rightarrow\Lambda
whose left adjoint
F^{*}
additionally commutes with finite
\mathscr{Y}
-limits, see
[
MW25
, Section 3.2]
.
We write
\mathscr{T}\mathrm{op}(\mathscr{Y})
for the category of
\mathscr{Y}
-topoi.
An example of a
\mathscr{Y}
-topos is the
\mathscr{Y}
-category of
\mathscr{Y}
-groupoids
\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}
, and in fact this is the final
\mathscr{Y}
-topos. It turns out that, thanks to
[
MW25
, Theorem 3.2.5.1]
,
\mathscr{Y}
-topoi may be described using the overcategory of
\mathscr{Y}
in the usual category
\mathscr{T}\mathrm{op}
of topoi and geometric morphisms.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\mathscr{Y}
be a topos. Then taking global sections defines an equivalence of categories

\mathscr{T}\mathrm{op}(\mathscr{Y})\rightarrow\mathscr{T}\mathrm{op}_{/Y}.

The inverse of the equivalence in
Theorem

2.2
is described explicitly in
[
MW25
, Remark 3.2.5.3]
. Given a geometric morphism of topoi
f\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
, we get an induced functor

f_{*}\colon\mathscr{C}\mathrm{at}_{\mathscr{X}}\rightarrow\mathscr{C}\mathrm{at}_{\mathscr{Y}},\hskip 8.53581pt\mathscr{C}\mapsto[U\in\mathscr{Y}\mapsto\mathscr{C}(f^{*}U)].

Applied to the
\mathscr{X}
-category
\Omega_{\mathscr{X}}\colon V\mapsto\mathscr{X}_{/V}
this results in the
\mathscr{Y}
-category
f_{*}\Omega_{\mathscr{X}}\colon U\mapsto\mathscr{X}_{/f^{*}U}
. This happens to be a
\mathscr{Y}
-topos, it comes with a geometric morphism of
\mathscr{Y}
-topoi
f_{*}\Omega_{X}\rightarrow\Omega_{Y}
, assembling the geometric morphisms
\mathscr{X}_{/f^{*}U}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}_{/U}
, which on global sections induces the original geometric morphism
f
.

Notation 2.3
.

Given geometric morphisms
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{f}\mathscr{Y}\xrightarrow{g}\mathcal{Z}
of topoi, we will usually write

F\colon(gf)_{\ast}\Omega_{\mathscr{X}}\rightarrow f_{*}\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}
for the corresponding geometric morphism of
\mathcal{Z}
-topoi.

Given a geometric morphism
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{f}\mathscr{Y}
, let us also describe the geometric morphism of
\mathscr{Y}
-topoi
F:f_{*}\Omega_{\mathscr{X}}\rightarrow\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}
explicitly at each level
U\in\mathscr{Y}
. The left exact left adjoint
F^{*}\colon\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}\rightarrow f^{*}\Omega_{\mathscr{X}}
is given at level
U
, by the functor
\mathscr{Y}_{/U}\rightarrow\mathscr{X}_{/f^{*}U}
induced by
f^{*}\colon\mathscr{Y}\rightarrow\mathscr{X}
. By inspection, one sees that the right adjoint
F_{*}\colon f_{*}\Omega_{\mathscr{X}}\rightarrow\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}
is therefore given at level
U\in\mathscr{Y}
by the functor
\mathscr{X}_{/f^{*}U}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}_{/U}
sending
V\rightarrow f^{*}U
to
f_{*}V\times_{f_{*}f^{*}U}U
.

2.5
Internally compact objects

Martini-Wolf introduce a class of
filtered
\mathscr{Y}
-categories
as such
\mathscr{Y}
-categories
J
such that the colimit functor
\underline{\mathrm{Fun}}(J,\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}})\rightarrow\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}
preserves
I
-shaped limits, where
I
is a locally constant sheaf of finite categories on
\mathscr{Y}
. Colimits over filtered
\mathscr{Y}
-categories will be called
internally filtered colimits
. An object
x
in a
\mathscr{Y}
-category
\mathscr{C}
is called
internally compact
if the functor
\mathrm{Map}_{\mathscr{C}}(x,-)\colon\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}
preserves internally filtered colimits. We write
\mathscr{C}^{cpt}
for the full subcategory of
\mathscr{C}
spanned by internally compact objects. There is a useful characterization of internally compact objects in
\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\mathscr{Y}
be an
\infty
-topos and
U\in\mathscr{Y}
an object. Then
U
is internally compact if and only if it is locally constant with compact values.

Proof.

This is a reformulation of
[
MW25
, Corollary 2.2.3.8]
.
∎

Explicitly, this means that
U
is internally compact if and only if we can find an étale cover
\{V_{i}\rightarrow 1\in Y\}_{i\in I}
such that for each
i\in I
the object
U\times V_{i}\in\mathscr{Y}_{/V_{i}}
is pulled back along the geometric morphism
\mathscr{Y}_{/V_{i}}\rightarrow\mathrm{An}
from a compact object
W_{i}\in\mathrm{An}^{\omega}
.

A
\mathscr{Y}
-category
\mathscr{C}
is said to be

if it is cocomplete and the canonical functor
\underline{\text{Ind}}_{\mathscr{Y}}(\mathscr{C}^{cpt})\rightarrow\mathscr{C}
is an equivalence. See
[
MW25
, 2.3.1]
and
[

, 7.1]
for a definition of
\underline{\text{Ind}}_{\mathscr{Y}}
and colimit completions of
\mathscr{Y}
-categories. The main example of a compactly generated

-category we care about in this paper is
\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

The
\mathscr{Y}
-category
\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}
is compactly generated.

Proof.

This is a special case of
[
MW25
, Proposition 2.3.3.6]

∎

In practice,
Proposition

2.5
is used in conjunction with
Proposition

2.4
to verify that a given transformation of internally filtered colimits preserving
\mathscr{Y}
-functors is invertible, by checking it only on locally constant objects.

2.6
Shape theory

Definition 2.6
.

Let
f:\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
be any geometric morphism. The
\mathscr{Y}
-shape
of
f
is the
\mathscr{Y}
-functor
F_{*}F^{*}\colon\Omega_{Y}\rightarrow\Omega_{Y}
.

Thanks to the discussion in the previous subsection, we can give an explicit description of the
\mathscr{Y}
-shape of a geometric morphism
f:\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
. After taking sections at
U\in\mathscr{Y}
,
F_{*}F^{*}
is given by the functor
\mathscr{Y}_{/U}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}_{/U}
sending
V\rightarrow U
to the object
f_{*}f^{*}V\times_{f_{*}f^{*}U}U\rightarrow U
. The above pullback is taken over the unit transformation
U\rightarrow f_{*}f^{*}U
.

Example 2.7
.

Let
U
be an object of
\mathscr{Y}
. The
\mathscr{Y}
-functor
\mathrm{Map}(U,-):\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}\rightarrow\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}
is the
\mathscr{Y}
-shape of the étale geometric morphism
\pi_{U}:\mathscr{Y}_{/U}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
.

When
\mathscr{Y}=\mathrm{An}
, we often make use of the equivalence
\Fun^{\lex,\mathrm{acc}}(\mathrm{An},\mathrm{An})^{\opposite}\simeq\Pro(\mathrm{An})
to identify the shape of the geometric morphism
f:\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathrm{An}
with a pro-object in
\mathrm{An}
. This pro-object is denoted by
\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})
. If
\mathscr{X}=\mathscr{S}\text{hv}({X};{\mathrm{An}})
for some topological space
X
, we write
\mathrm{sh}(X)
instead of
\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{S}\text{hv}({X};{\mathrm{An}}))
. When
X
is a compact Hausdorff topological space,
\mathrm{sh}(X)
agrees with what is classically known as the
strong shape of
X
(see
[
Hoy18
]
).
The Yoneda embedding induces a fully faithful, finite limit preserving functor

\mathrm{An}\simeq\Fun^{R,\mathrm{acc}}(\mathrm{An},\mathrm{An})^{\opposite}\subset\Pro(\mathrm{An}).

Moreover, this functor preserves finite limits. Indeed, from
\Pro(\mathrm{An})\simeq\mathrm{Ind}(\mathrm{An}^{\opposite})^{\opposite}
it follows that
\mathrm{An}^{\opposite}\rightarrow\Pro(\mathrm{An})^{\opposite}
preserves finite colimits
[
Lur09
, 5.3.5.14.]
.
We say that an
\infty
-topos
\mathscr{X}
is of
constant shape
if
\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})
is a constant pro-space. In this case
f_{*}f^{*}\simeq\mathrm{Map}(\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X}),-)
.

Example 2.8
.

When
X
is paracompact and has the homotopy type of a CW-complex, then
\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{S}\text{hv}({X};{\mathrm{An}}))
is a constant pro-object which agrees with the weak homotopy type of
X
, see
[
Lur17
, Rmk. A.1.4.]
. In particular, if
X
is paracompact and every open subset
U\subset X
has the homotopy type of a CW complex, then
X
is locally of constant shape. This property is enjoyed, for example, by the class of
absolute neighborhood retracts
(ANRs),
[
Han51
,
Mil59
]
.

2.7
Local contractibility

Definition 2.9
.

A geometric morphism
f\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
of topoi is
locally contractible
if the
\mathscr{Y}
-functor
F^{*}\colon\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}\rightarrow f_{*}\Omega_{\mathscr{X}}
admits a left adjoint. We say an
\infty
-topos
\mathscr{X}
is locally contractible if the morphism
\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathrm{An}
is locally contractible. We write
\mathscr{T}\mathrm{op}^{loc}
for the full subcategory of
\mathscr{T}\mathrm{op}
spanned by the locally contractible
\infty
-topoi.

If
f\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
is locally contractible, we generally write
F_{\sharp}\colon f_{*}\Omega_{\mathscr{X}}\rightarrow\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}
for the left adjoint of
F^{*}
. Note that on global sections we obtain a left adjoint
f_{\sharp}\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
to
f^{*}
. If
\mathscr{Y}=\mathrm{An}
, we see that
f_{\sharp}(1)
can be identified with
\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})
.

Remark 2.10
.

The condition for
f
to be locally contractible has a purely external characterization. It is equivalent to
f^{*}
admitting a left adjoint
f_{\sharp}
that satisfies the projection formula

f_{\sharp}(U\times_{f^{*}W}f^{*}V)\xrightarrow{\sim}f_{\sharp}(U)\times_{W}V

for all
V\rightarrow W
in
\mathscr{X}
and
U\rightarrow f^{*}V
in
\mathscr{Y}
, see
[
MW25
, Remark 3.3.1.3]
.

Example 2.11
.

Every étale geometric morphism
\pi_{U}\colon\mathscr{Y}_{/U}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
is locally contractible. In fact, étale geometric morphisms to
\mathscr{Y}
are characterised as precisely those geometric morphisms
f\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
for which
f_{\sharp}\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
is conservative, see
[
MW25
, Example 3.3.1.4]
.

Suppose that
f:\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathrm{An}
is locally contractible. Then the equivalence
\mathscr{X}\simeq\mathscr{X}_{/1}
implies that
f_{\sharp}
factors through a functor a functor
\eta_{\sharp}:\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathrm{An}_{/\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})}

As discussed in
[
Lur17
, p. 1405]
,
\eta_{\sharp}
admits a right adjoint
\eta^{*}
, which can be describe informally with the formula
\eta^{*}(y)\simeq f^{*}(y)\times_{f^{*}f_{\sharp}(1)}1
. From this description and universality of colimits, it is clear that
\eta^{*}
is cocontinuous. Hence
\eta^{\ast}
has a further right adjoint
\eta_{*}
, and the pair
(\eta^{*},\eta_{*})
describes a geometric morphism

\eta\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathrm{An}_{/\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})}.

The projection formula for
\eta_{\sharp}
can be deduced from the one of
f_{\sharp}
, and therefore
\eta
is a locally contractible geometric morphism. The main result of
[
Lur17
, Appendix A.1]
shows that
\eta^{\ast}
is fully faithful, with essential image given by the locally constant objects in
\mathscr{X}
.

Remark 2.12
.

The morphism
\eta
is in fact part of a natural transformation

\eta:\text{id}\rightarrow\Fun(\mathrm{sh}((-)),\mathrm{An})

of functors
\mathscr{T}\mathrm{op}^{loc}\rightarrow\mathscr{T}\mathrm{op}^{loc}
. This follows for example from the discussion in
[
Hoy18
, Page 4]
.
If
f\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
is a morphism of locally contractible topoi, we write
\mathrm{sh}(f)\colon\mathrm{An}_{/\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})}\rightarrow\mathrm{An}_{/\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{Y})}
for the induced geometric morphism. If we want to consider
\mathrm{sh}(f)
as a geometric morphism of
\mathrm{An}_{/\mathrm{sh}(Y)}
-topoi, we write it as
\mathrm{sh}(F)\colon\mathrm{sh}(f)_{\ast}\Omega_{\mathrm{An}_{/\mathrm{sh}(X)}}\rightarrow\Omega_{\mathrm{An}_{/\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{Y})}}
, in accordance with
2.3
.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be a locally of constant shape
\infty
-topos, and let
F
and
G
be any two functors
\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})\rightarrow\mathrm{An}
. Then the canonical map

\eta^{\ast}\text{\lx@text@underline{Hom}}_{\scalebox{1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{An}_{/\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})}$}}(F,G)\rightarrow\text{\lx@text@underline{Hom}}_{\scalebox{1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{X}$}}(\eta^{\ast}F,\eta^{\ast}G)

is an isomorphism. In particular, we get that
\text{\lx@text@underline{Hom}}_{\scalebox{1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{X}$}}(\eta^{\ast}F,\eta^{\ast}G)
is locally constant.

Proof.

This follows immediately from the projection formula for
\eta_{\sharp}
.
∎

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be a locally contractible
\infty
-topos. Then locally constant objects in
\mathscr{X}
are hypercomplete.

Proof.

This follows from
[
Lur17
, Corollary A.1.17]
.
∎

Remark 2.15
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
be a locally contractible
\infty
-topos, and let
\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})\xrightarrow{F}\mathrm{An}
be any functor. Then
\eta^{\ast}(F)\in\mathscr{X}
has compact values if and only if, for any point
[0]\xrightarrow{x}\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})
,
x^{\ast}F\in\mathrm{An}
is a compact object.

3
Fiber theorems

In this section, we will study a relative generalization of that of a cell-like map in the situation of a commuting diagram of
\infty
-topoi
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{X}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

such that when
q
is an equivalence, we recover Lurie’s notion of cell-like maps. Apart from merely generalizing, our methods also give a new proof of Lurie’s characterization of maps between ANR’s inducing cell-like maps on sheaf topoi, which is actually more general a well. To motivate, let us recall the notion of a shape equivalence of
\infty
-topoi.

Let
f\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{X}^{\prime}
be a geometric morphism. If
p\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathrm{An}
and
q\colon\mathscr{X}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathrm{An}
denote the unique geometric morphisms, then since
qf\simeq p
we get a map

q_{\ast}q^{\ast}\xrightarrow{\text{unit}}q_{\ast}f_{\ast}f^{\ast}q^{\ast}\simeq p_{\ast}p^{\ast}

which corresponds under
\Fun^{\mathrm{acc,lex}}(\mathrm{An},\mathrm{An})^{\opposite}\simeq\Pro(\mathrm{An})
to a map
\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})\rightarrow\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X}^{\prime})\in\Pro(\mathrm{An})
. Following
[
Lur09
, Rem. 7.1.6.5.]
we say that
f
is a
shape equivalence
if either the natural transformation written above or equivalently the map
\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})\rightarrow\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X}^{\prime})
is an equivalence. It turns out to be more straightforward to generalize the first perspective to a general base.

In the following, we will often work in the category
\mathscr{T}\mathrm{op}_{/Y}
for some
\infty
-topos
\mathscr{Y}
. The main result of
[
MW23
]
identifies this category with the category of
\infty
-topoi
internal
to
\mathscr{Y}
, a perspective that is often valuable. For a point
y\colon\mathrm{An}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
and
\mathscr{X}\in\mathscr{T}\mathrm{op}_{/\mathscr{Y}}
we will use the notation
\mathscr{X}_{y}
for the pullback
\mathscr{X}\times_{\mathscr{Y}}\mathrm{An}
along the point
y
and call it
the fiber of
\mathscr{X}
over
y
. One of our goals is to study aforementioned relative generalization of the notion of a shape equivalence and how it interacts with passing to fibers over
\mathscr{Y}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathscr{Y}
be an
\infty
-topos, and let
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{p}\mathscr{Y}
,
\mathscr{X}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{q}\mathscr{Y}
be geometric morphisms. Let
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{f}\mathscr{X}^{\prime}
be a geometric morphism over
\mathscr{Y}
. We say that
f
is a
relative shape equivalence over
\mathscr{Y}
if the natural transformation

(3.2)

q_{\ast}q^{\ast}\xrightarrow{\text{unit}}q_{\ast}f_{\ast}f^{\ast}q^{\ast}\simeq p_{\ast}p^{\ast}

is invertible.

For
\mathscr{Y}=\mathrm{An}
, this notion specialises to the classical notion of a shape equivalence.

Remark 3.3
.

Suppose we are in the same situation as in
Definition

3.1
. When
\mathscr{X}^{\prime}=\mathscr{Y}
and
q=\text{id}_{\mathscr{Y}}
, we see that
f
is a relative shape equivalence over
\mathscr{Y}
if and only if
f^{\ast}
is fully faithful. Therefore, when
f
is also assumed to be proper, we see that
f
is a relative shape equivalence over
\mathscr{Y}
if and only if
f
is cell-like in the sense of Lurie
[
Lur09
, Def. 7.3.6.1.]
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\mathscr{Y}
be an
\infty
-topos, and let
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{p}\mathscr{Y}
,
\mathscr{X}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{q}\mathscr{Y}
be geometric morphisms. Let
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{f}\mathscr{X}^{\prime}
be a geometric morphism over
\mathscr{Y}
. We say that
f
is a
hereditary shape equivalence over
\mathscr{Y}
if, for any
U\in\mathscr{Y}
, the geometric morphism

\mathscr{X}_{/p^{\ast}U}\xrightarrow{f_{/U}}\mathscr{X}^{\prime}_{/q^{\ast}U}

is a shape equivalence. When
\mathscr{X}^{\prime}=\mathscr{Y}
and
q=\text{id}_{\mathscr{Y}}
, we simply say
f
is a
hereditary shape equivalence
.

We emphasize that we really mean the map
\mathscr{X}_{/p^{\ast}U}\xrightarrow{f_{/U}}\mathscr{X}^{\prime}_{/q^{\ast}U}
to be a shape equivalence relative to
\mathrm{An}
and
not
relative to
\mathscr{Y}
, although it certainly lives in
\mathscr{T}\mathrm{op}_{/Y}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathscr{Y}
be an
\infty
-topos, and let
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{p}\mathscr{Y}
,
\mathscr{X}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{q}\mathscr{Y}
be geometric morphisms. Let
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{f}\mathscr{X}^{\prime}
be a geometric morphism over
\mathscr{Y}
. Let
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathscr{Y}
be a family of objects generating
\mathscr{Y}
under colimits. Then
f
is a hereditary shape equivalence over
\mathscr{Y}
if and only if, for each
V\in\mathcal{B}
, the geometric morphism
\mathscr{X}_{/p^{\ast}V}\xrightarrow{f_{/V}}\mathscr{X}^{\prime}_{/q^{\ast}V}
is a shape equivalence.

Proof.

For any
U\in\mathscr{Y}
, using descent we may write the map
\mathscr{X}_{/p^{\ast}U}\xrightarrow{f_{/U}}\mathscr{X}^{\prime}_{/q^{\ast}U}
as a colimit of maps of the form
f_{/V}
, where
V
lies in
\mathcal{B}
. The claim then follows from the fact that the functor
\text{sh}:\mathscr{T}\mathrm{op}\rightarrow\Pro(\mathrm{An})
preserves colimits.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
Y
be any topological space, and let
\mathscr{Y}=\mathscr{S}\text{hv}({Y})
be the
\infty
-topos of sheaves of animae on
Y
. Let
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{p}\mathscr{Y}
,
\mathscr{X}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{q}\mathscr{Y}
be geometric morphisms, and let
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{f}\mathscr{X}^{\prime}
be a geometric morphism over
\mathscr{Y}
. Then
f
is a hereditary shape equivalence over
\mathscr{Y}
if and only if, for any
U\hookrightarrow Y
open subset, the geometric morphism
f_{/U}:\mathscr{X}_{/p^{\ast}U}\rightarrow\mathscr{X}^{\prime}_{/q^{\ast}U}
is a shape equivalence.

Proof.

This follows from
Lemma

3.5
and the fact that
\mathscr{Y}
is generated by the sheaves represented by the open subsets of
Y
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\mathscr{Y}
be an
\infty
-topos, and let
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{p}\mathscr{Y}
,
\mathscr{X}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{q}\mathscr{Y}
be geometric morphisms. Let
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{f}\mathscr{X}^{\prime}
be a geometric morphism over
\mathscr{Y}
. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

f
is a hereditary shape equivalence over
\mathscr{Y}
;

(2)

For any constant object
F\in\mathscr{Y}
, the map

q_{\ast}q^{\ast}F\rightarrow p_{\ast}p^{\ast}F

obtained by evaluating the natural transformation (
3.2
) at
F
is invertible.

Proof.

Indeed, let
U
be any object in
\mathscr{Y}
, and consider the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{X}_{/p^{\ast}U}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{Y}_{/U}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{X}^{\prime}_{/q^{\ast}U}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{X}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{An}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{/U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma_{U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q_{/U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces b}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

where
\pi_{U}
is étale geometric morphism corresponding to
U
, the squares are pullbacks of
\infty
-topoi, and the lower arrows pointing towards
\mathrm{An}
are the unique geometric morphisms. Suppose
A
is any anima, and let
a^{\ast}A\in\mathscr{Y}
be the associated constant object in
\mathscr{Y}
. The map
q_{\ast}q^{\ast}a^{\ast}A\xrightarrow{\text{unit}}p_{\ast}p^{\ast}a^{\ast}A
is invertible if and only if it is invertible after taking sections at
U
, for all
U\in\mathscr{Y}
. Note that taking sections at
U\in Y
means to restrict to
\mathscr{Y}_{/U}
and apply the unique geometric morphism
\mathscr{Y}_{/U}\rightarrow\mathrm{An}
, which is the composition
a_{\ast}{\pi_{U}}_{\ast}{\pi_{U}}^{\ast}
. We have a commuting diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{\ast}{\pi_{U}}_{\ast}{\pi_{U}}^{\ast}q_{\ast}q^{\ast}a^{\ast}A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{\ast}{\pi_{U}}_{\ast}{\pi_{U}}^{\ast}p_{\ast}p^{\ast}a^{\ast}A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{\ast}{\pi_{U}}_{\ast}{q_{/U}}_{\ast}\sigma_{U}^{\ast}q^{\ast}a^{\ast}A}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

where the upper horizontal arrow is obtained by applying
a_{\ast}{\pi_{U}}_{\ast}{\pi_{U}}^{\ast}
to (
3.2
), the lower horizontal arrow is defined analogously to (
3.2
) via the unit of the adjunction
{f_{/U}}^{\ast}\dashv{f_{/U}}_{\ast}
, and the first vertical isomorphisms are given by étale basechange (see
[

, Lemma 3.2.1.9]
). Therefore, the upper horizontal map is invertible for all
U\in\mathscr{Y}
and

if and only if
f_{/U}
is a shape equivalence for all

.
∎

Definition 3.8
.

Let
\mathscr{Y}
be an
\infty
-topos, and let
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{p}\mathscr{Y}
,
\mathscr{X}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{q}\mathscr{Y}
be geometric morphisms. Let
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{f}\mathscr{X}^{\prime}
be a geometric morphism over
\mathscr{Y}
. We say that
f
is a
pointwise shape equivalence over
\mathscr{Y}
if, for any point
\mathrm{An}\xrightarrow{y}\mathscr{Y}
, the geometric morphism

\mathscr{X}_{y}\xrightarrow{f_{y}}\mathscr{X}^{\prime}_{y}

is a shape equivalence.

Remark 3.9
.

Let
\mathscr{Y}
be an
\infty
-topos, and let
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{p}\mathscr{Y}
,
\mathscr{X}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{q}\mathscr{Y}
be proper geometric morphisms. Let
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{f}\mathscr{X}^{\prime}
be a relative shape equivalence over
\mathscr{Y}
. Then
f
is a pointwise shape equivalence over
\mathscr{Y}
. The proof of our claim is identical to that of the ”only if” part of
[
Lur09
, Proposition 7.3.6.4]
, so we omit it. This is essentially a consequence of proper basechange and some additional small observations.

Remark

3.9
has the following partial converse.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\mathscr{Y}
be an
\infty
-topos, and let
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{p}\mathscr{Y}
,
\mathscr{X}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{q}\mathscr{Y}
be proper geometric morphisms. Let
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{f}\mathscr{X}^{\prime}
be a pointwise shape equivalence over
\mathscr{Y}
. Assume that
\mathscr{Y}^{\text{hyp}}
has enough points, and that
F\in\mathscr{Y}
is an object such that
p^{\ast}F
and
q^{\ast}F
are hypercomplete. Then the morphism

q_{\ast}q^{\ast}F\rightarrow p_{\ast}p^{\ast}F

is invertible.

Proof.

Since
p^{\ast}F
and
q^{\ast}F
are hypercomplete, and lower stars preserve hypercompleteness, we have that
q_{\ast}q^{\ast}F
and
p_{\ast}p^{\ast}F
are hypercomplete. Therefore, since
\mathscr{Y}^{\text{hyp}}
has enough points, to prove the lemma we only need to show that the map
q_{\ast}q^{\ast}F\rightarrow p_{\ast}p^{\ast}F
is invertible after pulling back along any point of
\mathscr{Y}
. Using proper basechange, one sees that this last statement follows immediately from the assumption that
f
is a pointwise shape equivalence over
\mathscr{Y}
.
∎

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
\mathscr{Y}
be an
\infty
-topos, and let
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{p}\mathscr{Y}
,
\mathscr{X}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{q}\mathscr{Y}
be proper geometric morphisms. Let
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{f}\mathscr{X}^{\prime}
be a geometric morphism over
\mathscr{Y}
. Consider the following conditions.

(1)

f
is a relative shape equivalence over
\mathscr{Y}
;

(2)

f
is a hereditary shape equivalence over
\mathscr{Y}
;

(3)

f
is a pointwise shape equivalence over
\mathscr{Y}
.

Then (1) is equivalent to (2), and (1) implies (3). Moreover, if constant objects in
\mathscr{X}
and
\mathscr{X}^{\prime}
are hypercomplete and
\mathscr{Y}^{\text{hyp}}
has enough points, then (1) and (2) are equivalent to (3).

Proof.

It follows respectively from
Lemma

3.7
and
Remark

3.9
that (1) implies (2) and (3).

We now show that (2) implies (1). The morphism (
3.2
) induces a transformation of
\mathscr{Y}
-functors

(3.12)

Q_{\ast}Q^{\ast}\rightarrow P_{\ast}P^{\ast}.

Since both
p
and
q
are proper, by
[
MW23
]
the source and target of the transformation above are
\mathscr{Y}
-functors preserving internally filtered colimits. Therefore, it suffices to show that (
3.12
) is invertible after restricting to the
\mathscr{Y}
-category of internally compact objects in
\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}
. By
Proposition

2.4
, this condition amounts to proving that, for any
U\in\mathscr{Y}
and
F\in\mathscr{Y}_{/U}
locally constant with compact values, the map

{q_{/U}}_{\ast}{q_{/U}}^{\ast}F\rightarrow{p_{/U}}_{\ast}{p_{/U}}^{\ast}F

is invertible. Using étale basechange we may further reduce to the case when
F
is constant. Appealing to
Lemma

3.7
, one sees that in this last case the map
q_{\ast}q^{\ast}F\rightarrow p_{\ast}p^{\ast}F
is guaranteed to be invertible by condition (2). Therefore we deduce that (1) holds.

Now assume that constant objects in
\mathscr{Y}
,
\mathscr{X}
and
\mathscr{X}^{\prime}
are hypercomplete. To conclude the proof of the theorem, it suffices to show that (3) implies (2). More specifically, by
Lemma

3.7
, we need to prove that, for any
F\in\mathscr{Y}
constant, the map

q_{\ast}q^{\ast}F\rightarrow p_{\ast}p^{\ast}F

is invertible. Since
F
is constant, both
p^{\ast}F
and
q^{\ast}F

Proof.

.

.

4
Approximate fibrations

In this section, we give the definition of an approximate fibration of
\infty
-topoi. The goal is to put it into a shape-theoretic context, so we start by investigating a more intuitive notion, namely that of a shape fibration. This is essential to compare our definition to the classical notion of approximate fibration by Coram and Duvall
[
CPFD77
]
, which rounds off the section.

4.1
Shape fibrations and quasi-fibrations

The shape of an
\infty
-topos is one of its fundamental invariants, but as a tool for studying maps it is often too coarse. Given a continuous map
f\colon X\to Y
, the induced morphism of shapes
\mathrm{sh}(f)\colon\mathrm{sh}(X)\to\mathrm{sh}(Y)
typically retains only very limited information about the behaviour of
f
.

This limitation is apparent in a simple example. Let
C
be the Cantor set, and consider the quotient
X=C\times[0,1]\big/\!\sim
, where all points
(x,1)
are identified with each other. Then
\mathrm{sh}(X)\simeq*\in\Pro(\mathrm{An})
. For any
x\in C
, the map
*\xrightarrow{(x,0)}X

induces the identity morphism on shapes. From the perspective of shape, this map is indistinguishable from the trivial one, even though the underlying inclusion visibly carries complicated local structure.

To extract finer information from a map
f
, one should not only consider
\mathrm{sh}(f)
itself, but also the induced maps
\mathrm{sh}(f^{-1}(U))\longrightarrow\mathrm{sh}(U)
for open subsets
U\subset Y
. This leads to the notion of a
shape fibration
: a map
f
is called a shape fibration if, for every open
U\subset Y
, the map
\mathrm{sh}(f^{-1}(U))\to\mathrm{sh}(U)
arises as a base change of
\mathrm{sh}(f)
along
\mathrm{sh}(U)\to\mathrm{sh}(Y)
.

In what follows, we introduce the concept of shape fibration for geometric morphisms of arbitrary
\infty
-topoi, study its behaviour under local contractibility assumptions, and compare it with the weaker notion of
shape quasi-fibration
, which depends only on the fibres over points. We show that every proper shape fibration is a shape quasi-fibration, and we identify conditions under which the converse holds.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{f}\mathscr{Y}
be a geometric morphism. We say that

is a

if, for any

, the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X}_{/f^{\ast}U})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{Y}_{/U})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{Y})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{sh}(f)}

is a pullback in
\Pro(\mathrm{An})
.

Remark 4.2
.

Let

be a shape fibration, let

be any object, and let

be the morphism obtained by pulling back

. Then

is a shape fibration. Indeed, since

is a shape fibration, the composite square and the right square in the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X}_{/f^{\ast}V})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X}_{/f^{\ast}U})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{Y}_{/V})}

\infty
-categories

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathscr{X}
and
\mathscr{Y}
be locally contractible
\infty
-topoi, and let

be any geometric morphism. Suppose that

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X}_{/f^{\ast}U})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{Y}_{/U})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{Y})}

Proof.

Let

be any object in
\mathscr{Y}

\displaystyle\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{Y}_{/V})\times_{\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{Y})}\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})

\displaystyle\simeq(\mathop{\mathchoice{\varlim@\displaystyle{\rightarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}{\varlim@\textstyle{\rightarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}{\varlim@\scriptstyle{\rightarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}{\varlim@\scriptscriptstyle{\rightarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}}\displaylimits\limits_{i\in I}\mathrm{sh}(Y_{/U_{i}}))\times_{\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{Y})}\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathop{\mathchoice{\varlim@\displaystyle{\rightarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}{\varlim@\textstyle{\rightarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}{\varlim@\scriptstyle{\rightarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}{\varlim@\scriptscriptstyle{\rightarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}}\displaylimits\limits_{i\in I}(\mathrm{sh}(Y_{/U_{i}})\times_{\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{Y})}\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X}))

\displaystyle\simeq\mathop{\mathchoice{\varlim@\displaystyle{\rightarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}{\varlim@\textstyle{\rightarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}{\varlim@\scriptstyle{\rightarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}{\varlim@\scriptscriptstyle{\rightarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}}\displaylimits\limits_{i\in I}\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X}_{/f^{\ast}U_{i}})

Let

Theorem 4.5

Let

(2)

Proof.

Since

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{An}_{/\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{An}_{/\mathrm{sh}(Y)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\eta}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{sh}(f)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta}

The counit of the adjunction

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{sh}(\pi_{U})^{\ast}}

Let

Let

.

4.2
Approximate fibrations in higher topos theory

Now we come to the definition of an approximate fibrations between
\infty
-topoi.
In this section, internal higher category, as recalled in
Section

2.3
and following, are essential.

Definition 4.11
.

Let
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{f}\mathscr{Y}
be any geometric morphism. We say that
f
is an
approximate fibration
if the
\mathscr{Y}
-functor
F_{\ast}F^{\ast}
is internally corepresentable. When
f
is an approximate fibration, we write
f_{\sharp}(1_{\mathscr{X}})
for the object of
\mathscr{Y}
corepresenting the
\mathscr{Y}
-functor
F_{\ast}F^{\ast}
.

Explicitly, we ask that
F_{*}F^{*}\simeq\mathrm{Map}_{\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}}(f_{\sharp}(1_{\mathscr{X}}),-)
as
\mathscr{Y}
-functors. By the Yoneda lemma for internal higher categories, prescribing a natural transformation
\mathrm{Map}_{\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}}(f_{\sharp}(1_{\mathscr{X}}),-)\rightarrow F_{*}F^{*}
is equivalent to prescribing a global section
1_{\mathscr{Y}}\xrightarrow{s}f_{\ast}f^{\ast}f_{\sharp}(1_{\mathscr{X}})
. We say that
s

exhibits
f_{\sharp}(1_{\mathscr{X}})
as corepresenting object for the approximate fibration
f
if the induced map
\mathrm{Map}_{\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}}(f_{\sharp}(1_{\mathscr{X}}),-)\rightarrow F_{*}F^{*}
is an equivalence.

Example 4.12
.

Let
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{p}\mathscr{Y}
be a cell-like geometric morphism. Then the equivalence
1_{\mathscr{Y}}\simeq p_{\ast}p^{\ast}1_{\mathscr{Y}}
exhibits
1_{\mathscr{Y}}
as a corepresenting object for the approximate fibration
p
. More generally, if
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{p}\mathscr{Y}
is cell-like and
\mathscr{Y}\xrightarrow{f}\mathcal{Z}
an approximate fibration, then
fp
is an approximate fibration. Indeed, in this case
F_{\ast}P_{\ast}P^{\ast}F^{\ast}\simeq F_{\ast}F^{\ast}
.

Example 4.13
.

Every locally contractible geometric morphism
f\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
is an approximate fibration.
Indeed, the
\mathscr{Y}
-functor
F_{\ast}F^{\ast}
has a left adjoint
F_{\sharp}F^{\ast}
, and we compute

F_{\ast}F^{\ast}\simeq\mathrm{Map}_{\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}}(1_{\mathscr{Y}},F_{\ast}F^{\ast}(-))\simeq\mathrm{Map}_{\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}}(F_{\sharp}F^{\ast}(1_{\mathscr{Y}}),-)\simeq\mathrm{Map}_{\Omega_{\mathscr{Y}}}(F_{\sharp}(1_{\mathscr{X}}),-).

So, if
f_{\sharp}
denotes the left adjoint to
f^{\ast}
, the corepresenting object is
f_{\sharp}(1_{\mathscr{X}})
, and the section exhibiting it corepreseniting
F_{\ast}F^{\ast}
is

1_{\mathscr{Y}}\simeq f_{\ast}1_{\mathscr{X}}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{unit}}f_{\ast}f^{\ast}f_{\sharp}1_{\mathscr{X}}.

This in particular applies to étale geometric morphisms.

Remark 4.14
.

Suppose that
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{f}\mathscr{Y}
is an approximate fibration, and moreover assume that
f
is a proper geometric morphism. Then the corepresenting object
f_{\sharp}(1_{\mathscr{X}})
for
f_{\ast}f^{\ast}
is locally constant with compact values. Indeed, since
f
is proper, the
\mathscr{Y}
-functor
F_{\ast}F^{\ast}
preserves internally filtered colimits (
[
MW23
]
). Therefore,
f_{\sharp}(1_{\mathscr{X}})
has to be internally compact, and by
Proposition

2.4
, also locally constant with compact values.

For proper geometric morphisms
f\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
between locally contractible topoi, we now show that the notion of an approximate fibration agrees with the notion of a shape fibration from
Section

4.1
. The basic idea is to use the commuting diagram of topoi

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{An}_{/\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{An}_{/\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{Y})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{sh}(f)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta}

and note that the right-down composite is an approximate fibration as a locally contractible geometric morphism.
In other words,
\eta f
is an approximate fibration, and our strategy is to use this to deduce that
f
is an approximate fibration.
This needs some preparation, starting with the construction of a candidate for a corepresenting object
f_{\sharp}(1)
.

Construction 4.15
.

Let
f\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
be a geometric morphism between locally contractible topoi.
We construct a morphism in
\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{Y})
as the composite

(4.16)

\displaystyle 1_{\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{Y})}\simeq\mathrm{sh}(f)_{\ast}1_{\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\text{unit}}\mathrm{sh}(f)_{\ast}\mathrm{sh}(f)^{\ast}\mathrm{sh}(f)_{!}1_{\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})}

(4.17)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[\simeq]{\text{unit}}\mathrm{sh}(f)_{\ast}\eta_{\ast}\eta^{\ast}\mathrm{sh}(f)^{\ast}\mathrm{sh}(f)_{!}1_{\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})}

(4.18)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[\simeq]{}\eta_{\ast}f_{\ast}f^{\ast}\eta^{\ast}\mathrm{sh}(f)_{!}1_{\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})}.

By adjunction, this gives

(4.19)

1_{\mathscr{Y}}\simeq\eta^{\ast}1_{\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{Y})}\rightarrow f_{\ast}f^{\ast}\eta^{\ast}\mathrm{sh}(f)_{!}1_{\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})}.

Using the Yoneda lemma for
\mathscr{Y}
-categories (see
[
Mar21
]
), the section (

Theorem 4.27
.

Let
\mathscr{X}\xrightarrow{f}\mathscr{Y}
be a proper geometric morphism, and suppose that
\mathscr{X}
and

are locally contractible. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

is an approximate fibration;

(2)

is a shape fibration.

Moreover, in this case, for each

is the locally constant object
\eta^{\ast}\mathrm{sh}(f_{/U})_{!}1_{\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X}_{/f^{\ast}U})}

Proof.

We start by showing that (1) implies (2). By

4.5
, it suffices to show that

preserves locally constant objects. Let

be the internal corepresenting object for
f_{\ast}f^{\ast}
. Since

is proper,

is locally constant by

4.14
. We can then conclude by observing that
\text{\lx@text@underline{Hom}}_{\scalebox{1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{Y}$}}(f_{\sharp}1_{\mathscr{X}},G)\simeq f_{\ast}f^{\ast}G
is locally constant whenever

is, by
Lemma

2.13
.

We show that (2) implies (1).
We want to show that the transformation (
4.20
) is a natural isomorphism of
\mathscr{Y}
-functors. Notice that it suffices to show that (
4.20
) is invertible after restricting to the
\mathscr{Y}
-category of locally constant objects. Indeed, since (2) holds, by

4.10
and

4.9
,

is a shape quasi-fibration. Moreover, for any point

in

, the shape of

is compact by

. Therefore,

is locally constant with compact values by

. Thus, we deduce that the

-functor

preserves internally filtered colimits. As a consequence, we find that (

Proposition

2.4
.

We now observe that one may further reduce to proving that the restriction of (
4.20
) to locally constant objects is invertible after passing to global sections. In other words, we now show that it suffices to prove that the natural transformation

(4.28)

\text{\lx@text@underline{Hom}}_{\scalebox{1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{Y}$}}(\eta^{\ast}\mathrm{sh}(f)_{!}1_{\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})},\eta^{\ast}(-))\rightarrow f_{\ast}f^{\ast}\eta^{\ast}

be any object, and denote by

(

We now prove that (

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta^{\ast}\eta_{\ast}\text{\lx@text@underline{Hom}}_{\scalebox{1}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathscr{Y}$}}(\eta^{\ast}\mathrm{sh}(f)_{!}1_{\mathrm{sh}(\mathscr{X})},\eta^{\ast}(-))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta^{\ast}\eta_{\ast}f_{\ast}f^{\ast}\eta^{\ast}}

Remark 4.31
.

Note that

4.3
Approximate fibrations in topology

We now recall the notion of approximate fibrations in topology, introduced by Coram and Duvall in
[
CPFD77
]
, and subsequently put it in the context of higher topos theory.

For terminology, suppose
Y
is a topological space and
\epsilon=\{U_{\alpha}\}
is an open cover of
Y
. We say that two maps
f,g\colon Z\rightarrow Y
are
\epsilon
-close
if for each
z\in Z
there is an
\alpha
with both
f(z)
and
g(z)
lying in
U_{\alpha}
. The suggestive example to keep in mind is if
Y
is a metric space and
\epsilon
is a positive real number, we can associate to it the open cover of balls of radius
\epsilon
.

Definition 4.32

(
[
CPFD77
]
)
.

Let
p\colon X\rightarrow Y
be a proper map of ANRs. Then
p
is called an approximate fibration if for every square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z\times\{0\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z\times[0,1]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces l}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}

and every open cover
\epsilon=\{U_{\alpha}\}
of
Y
, there exists a map
H^{\prime}\colon Z\times[0,1]\rightarrow Y
which is
\epsilon
-close to
H
, agrees with
H
on
Z\times\{0\}
, and admits a diagonal lift
L\colon Z\times[0,1]\rightarrow X
with
pL=H
which agrees with
l
on
Z\times\{0\}
.

We now give a reformulation of the notion of an approximate fibration, which is due to
[
HTW90
]
. In the following, we write
\Pi_{\infty}
to denote the weak homotopy type of a topological space.

Theorem 4.33
.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
be a proper map of locally compact ANR’s. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

The map
f
is an approximate fibration.

(2)

For each open subset
U\subset Y
, the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi_{\infty}(f^{-1}(U))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi_{\infty}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi_{\infty}(U)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi_{\infty}(Y)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

is a pullback in
\mathrm{An}
.

Proof.

Recall that a square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}

in
\mathrm{An}
is a pullback if and only if, for each
x\in B^{\prime}
, the natural map from the fiber of
q
at
x
to the fiber of
p
at
s(x)
is invertible. Therefore, we see that the condition appearing in
[
HTW90
, Theorem 12.15]
is equivalent to condition (2) in our statement. Hence, the proof is concluded by combining
[
HTW90
, Theorem 12.13]
and
[
HTW90
, Theorem 12.15]
.
∎

Theorem 4.34
.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
be a proper map of locally compact ANRs. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

The map
f
is an approximate fibration.

(2)

The geometric morphism
f:\mathscr{S}\text{hv}({X})\rightarrow\mathscr{S}\text{hv}({Y})
is an approximate fibration.

Proof.

By
Theorem

4.27
, it suffices to show that (1) holds if and only if

is a shape fibration. The latter condition is equivalent to (2) in

. Indeed, this is seen by combining

4.4
with the fact that, for a locally compact ANR

, there is an equivalence
\mathrm{sh}(Z)\simeq\Pi_{\infty}(Z)
in
\mathrm{An}
. The proof is thus finished by appealing to
Theorem

4.33
.
∎

Appendix A
A formula for stalks

Given a proper map of paracompact topological spaces
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
, it is well-known that for
y\in Y
, the shape of the fibre
X_{y}
can be computed as the limit of the shapes of the subspaces
f^{-1}(U)\subset X
, where
U
runs through all open neighborhoods
x\in U\subset X
. The purpose of this subsection is to generalise this to an arbitrary proper geometric morphism of topoi
f\colon\mathscr{X}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
with a point
\mathrm{An}\rightarrow\mathscr{Y}
.

Let
C
be any small
\infty
-category. Let
f\colon C\rightarrow\mathrm{An}
be any functor, and write
\pi_{f}\colon E_{f}\rightarrow C
for the left fibration classified by
f
. Denote by
f_{!}\colon\mathscr{PS}\text{hv}({C})\rightarrow\mathrm{An}
the extension by colimits of
f
. The following lemma provides a convenient formula to describe
f_{!}
in terms of
\pi_{f}
.

Lemma A.1
.

The functor
f_{!}
is naturally equivalent to the composition

\mathscr{PS}\text{hv}({C})\xrightarrow{{\pi_{f}}^{\ast}}\mathscr{PS}\text{hv}({E_{f}})\xrightarrow{\mathop{\mathchoice{\varlim@\displaystyle{\rightarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}{\varlim@\textstyle{\rightarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}{\varlim@\scriptstyle{\rightarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}{\varlim@\scriptscriptstyle{\rightarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}}\displaylimits}\mathrm{An}.

More explicitly, for any
F\in\mathscr{PS}\text{hv}({C})
, we have an isomorphism

f_{!}F\simeq\mathop{\mathchoice{\varlim@\displaystyle{\rightarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}{\varlim@\textstyle{\rightarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}{\varlim@\scriptstyle{\rightarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}{\varlim@\scriptscriptstyle{\rightarrow\scriptscriptstyle}}}\displaylimits\limits_{e\in E_{f}}F(\pi_{f}(e))

which is natural in
F
.

Proof.

This is a special case of
[
MW25
, Lemma 2.3.4.3]
. ∎

Let
\mathscr{X}
be an
\infty
-topos, and let
\mathrm{An}\xrightarrow{x}\mathscr{X}
be any point. Suppose that
C
is a small
\infty
-category and

.

We define the

Notice that the

. Since the latter is left exact, our claim follows from

.

Notice that there is an isomorphism

(this is a very special case of

Heterogeneity study on MiRNA expression in islet cells of rat pancreatic head and tail
Authors: Xuehua Lu, Yunlu Liu, Jinyan Chen, Peng Huang, Kun Wang, Junyong Han
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAs a vital endocrine organ, the pancreas exhibits differences in anatomical structure and microenvironment between its head and tail. However, whether there is heterogeneity in miRNA expression within the islet cells of these two regions and what biological significance this heterogeneity may entail remain unclear.MethodsThis study utilized high-throughput sequencing to analyze the miRNA expression profiles in the islet cells from the pancreatic head and tail in rats. The TargetScan and miRDB databases were used to predict target genes of the differentially expressed miRNAs (DEmiRNAs). The Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) databases were utilized to analyze the target genes. The key miRNA was verified by qRT-PCR.ResultA total of 445 miRNAs were detected in the islet cells of the pancreatic head and tail, among which 69 showed significant differences (|log2 fold change|>0.585 and P<0.05). Compared with the pancreatic tail, 28 miRNAs were upregulated and 41 miRNAs were downregulated in the pancreatic head. Bioinformatics analysis revealed that these target genes were significantly enriched in functions such as negative regulation of cellular process, anatomical structure development, intracellular organelle, pancreatic cancer, insulin resistance, MAPK signaling, Wnt signaling, and Hippo signaling. In addition, KEGG analysis of target genes showed that miR-124-3p was in several pathways, such as the insulin signaling pathway, endocrine resistance, small cell lung cancer, and other pathways in cancer. qRT-PCR results showed that miR-124-3p was significantly upregulated in the pancreatic head.ConclusionsOur findings provide novel insights into the heterogeneity of miRNA expression in the pancreas and the molecular mechanisms underlying pancreatic cancer, offering potential targets for future diagnostic and therapeutic strategies.Keywords: Heterogeneity, MiR-124-3p, Pancreatic head, Pancreatic tail

Background

The pancreas is a digestive gland with endocrine and exocrine functions, consisting of the head, body, and tail. The islet comprises clusters of endocrine cells in the pancreas, mainly including α cells, β cells, and δ cells. During pancreatic development, differences in pancreatic cell composition, blood supply, lymphatic and venous drainage, and innervation lead to disparities in islet cell size and cell content between the pancreatic head and tail. Some studies have shown that the distribution of islets and β cells is relatively diminished in the head region, exhibiting a heterogeneous increase from head to tail [1–3]. In the 16 S rRNA microbiome profile, pancreatic head tissue exhibits higher α-diversity and a greater abundance of Firmicutes compared with body and tail tissues [4].

The structural differences between the pancreatic head and tail may affect the physiological function of islet cells and susceptibility to diseases. In patients with type 2 diabetes (T2D), β cells in the pancreatic head are particularly susceptible to dysfunction [5]. Additionally, the pancreatic head has a higher incidence of tumors than the tail [6, 7], but tumors in the tail are more malignant than those in the head [8, 9]. Focal parenchymal atrophy is an early imaging hallmark of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma; its frequency is significantly lower in the pancreatic head than in the body or tail [10]. However, patients with pancreatic head cancer often present with metastatic disease [11]. By contrast, pancreatic tail cancer is highly invasive, resulting in poor prognosis and low overall survival rate [12]. Consequently, the site and malignancy of these diseases are considered to reflect structural differences between the pancreatic head and tail.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are endogenous noncoding single-stranded RNAs about 22 nucleotides in length. MiRNAs have crucial functions in numerous biological processes and may serve as biomarkers for diseases. For example, miR-196a-5p exhibits good specificity for pancreatic cancer and shows potential as a biomarker for diagnosis and prognosis [13]. Studies have shown that miR-216a is a conserved, pancreas-specific miRNA with potential as a biomarker for the diagnosis of pancreatic diseases [14]. Studies have also found that exosomal miR-19a released by pancreatic cancer cells is a crucial mediator in the regulation of insulin secretion, and the altered communication of exosomal miR-19a between cancer cells and β cells plays a key role in the pathogenesis of pancreatic cancer-associated diabetes [15, 16]. The suppression of miR-124-3p significantly upregulates FOXA2 and PDX1 expression, facilitates hepatocyte differentiation into IPCs, and improves induction efficiency [17]. Therefore, identifying DEmiRNAs in the pancreatic head and tail may offer potential targets for future diagnostic and therapeutic strategies. To date, no research has been conducted on the differential expression profiles of miRNAs in islet cells from various regions of the pancreas.

In the current study, we detected miRNA expression levels in islet cells from the pancreatic head and tail of rats via high-throughput sequencing and utilized bioinformatics to predict target genes and associated signaling pathways. The findings of this study provide an important theoretical foundation for future diagnostic and therapeutic approaches to pancreatic diseases.

Materials and methods

Experimental animals

Six 24-week-old male specific pathogen-free Sprague–Dawley rats (300–400 g) were obtained from the Laboratory Animal Center of Shanghai Slater (Shanghai, China) (license no. SCXK (Hu) 2012-0001). Rats were adapted for one week under a 12-h light/dark cycle at 25 °C ± 2 ℃ and 55%±10% relative humidity with free access to food and water before the experiment. All animal experiments were approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of Fujian Academy of Medical Sciences (DL2021-04) and conducted in strict accordance with the NIH Guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals.

At the end of the experiment, animals received a terminal intraperitoneal dose of 150 mg/kg pentobarbital (obtained from First Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University, Ethics No. IACUC FJMU 2021 − 0433) and were immediately decapitated. Loss of reflexes was verified by paw pinch.

Isolation of islet cells from the pancreatic head and tail

Anesthesia was induced in rats by intraperitoneal injection of 90 mg/kg ketamine and 10 mg/kg xylazine (both obtained from First Hospital of Fujian Medical University, Ethics No. IACUC FJMU 2021 − 0433). Anesthetic depth was assessed through paw pinch. The common bile duct was dissected free and occluded with a microvascular clip. The pancreas was perfused via a cannula inserted at the cystic–bile duct junction and filled with 0.5 mg/mL collagenase XI solution (Sigma-Aldrich, Germany). The pancreas was then separated into two parts: the head, adherent to the duodenum, and the tail, extending from the spleen to the lesser curvature of the stomach. Subsequently, the pancreas was digested in the water bath of 37 °C for 16 min 30 s and terminated with Hanks’ solution. The supernatant was discarded, and the pellet was resuspended in wash buffer and filtered through a cell strainer to remove residual collagenase. Finally, islet cells were purified by density-gradient centrifugation on Biocoll separating solution (Biochrom AG, Germany). To ensure the high purity of the preparation, islets were handpicked and counted under a stereomicroscope, and identified them by Dithizone (DTZ) staining.

Total RNA isolation and small RNA sequencing

Total RNA was extracted from islets of the pancreatic head and tail using TRIzol™ reagent (Invitrogen, USA), according to the manufacturer’s protocol. RNA integrity was assessed by agarose gel electrophoresis, and quantity and purity were assayed with a NanoDrop spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific, USA). Moreover, each RNA sample had an A260/A280 ratio > 1.8 and an A260/A230 ratio > 2.0. Library preparation and RNA sequencing were performed by Shanghai Aksomics Biotechnology Co., Ltd. on an Illumina NextSeq 500. MiRNAs with |log2 (fold change)|>0.585 and P<0.05 were considered differentially expressed between the pancreatic head and tail.

Target gene prediction of the DEmiRNAs and functional analysis

The top 10 up- and downregulated DEmiRNAs, which were selected on the basis of their largest absolute log2 (fold change) values and supporting literature evidence, were subjected to target gene prediction with TargetScan (http://www.targetscan.org/) and miRDB databases (http://mirdb.org/). The DAVID website was utilized for the analysis of the Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) databases. Significance was assessed by Fisher’s exact test (P<0.05) to identify biologically relevant pathways and molecular functions.

Verification of key MiRNA by quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

To validate DEmiRNAs, islets were isolated from the head and tail regions of each rat’s pancreas (n = 20) respectively, giving 40 samples in total. First, cDNA was synthesized from 1 µg of total RNA using the miRNA 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Vazyme, China) in a 20 µL reaction volume comprising 2 µL of 10×RT mix, 1 µL of stem-loop primer, 2 µL of HiScript II enzyme mix, and RNase Free dH2O to 20 µL. qPCR was performed with a 7500 Real‑Time PCR system (Applied Biosystems, USA) using the MiRNA Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme, China) in a 20 µL reaction volume containing 1 µL of cDNA template, 10 µL of 2×miRNA Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix, 0.4 µL of specific primer, 0.4 µL of mQ primer R, and 8.2 µL of RNase‑free water. The amplification conditions were as follows: 95 °C for 5 min, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 10 s and 60 °C for 60 s. U6 was employed as the internal reference gene, and relative quantification was performed using the 2−△△Ct method. The primer sequences are shown in Table 1.

Statistical analysis

DEmiRNAs were identified using paired t-test and defined as those with |log2 (fold change)|>0.585 and P<0.05. SPSS (SPSS26.0, USA) was utilized for statistical analysis. All data were expressed as means ± SD. P<0.05 was considered to indicate a statistically significant difference.

Results

Dithizone staining

As a zinc-selective chelator, DTZ specifically stains pancreatic β cells a vivid crimson because of their abundant zinc ions, whereas acinar and aggregated cells remain unstained. In our study, the pancreatic islet cell clusters were stained red using DTZ labeling (Fig. 1). To ensure high purity of the preparation, islets were handpicked under a stereomicroscope, and DTZ staining indicated that the purity of the isolated islets exceeded 99%. Under the stereomicroscope, we collected all visible islets, approximately 350 islets of similar size were respectively handpicked from the pancreatic head and tail for subsequent experiments. These results demonstrated successful isolation of pancreatic islet cells with high yield and purity.

Identification of the DEmiRNAs

The miRNA sequencing results identified 445 miRNAs in the islet cells of the pancreatic head and tail. With a threshold of |log2 fold change|>0.585 and P<0.05, a total of 376 non-significantly DEmiRNAs and 69 significantly DEmiRNAs (28 upregulated and 41 downregulated) were identified in the pancreatic head compared with the pancreatic tail (Tables 2 and 3). The expression profiles of DEmiRNAs were depicted as a heatmap and volcano plot after normalization (Fig. 2).

Target gene prediction and functional annotation

Enrichment analyses were performed to elucidate the mechanisms and pathways underlying the potential function of DEmiRNAs. The top 10 GO terms and top 20 KEGG pathways are displayed in Fig. 3. Notably, GO analysis revealed that the target genes of upregulated miRNAs were enriched in biological processes (such as anatomical structure morphogenesis, negative regulation of cellular process, and system development), cellular component (including extracellular, extracellular organelle, and membrane-bounded organelle), and molecular functions (including amide binding, protein binding, and peptide binding; Fig. 3A). KEGG analysis revealed 115 significantly enriched pathways associated with the target genes of upregulated miRNAs, including FoxO signaling pathway, pancreatic cancer, insulin resistance, and MAPK signaling pathway (Fig. 3C). In terms of the target genes of downregulated miRNAs, GO analysis demonstrated that such genes were primarily associated with biological processes (such as negative regulation of cellular process, anatomical structure development, and developmental process), cellular component (such as intracellular, cytoplasm, and intracellular membrane-bounded organelle), and molecular functions (such as protein binding, peptide binding, and amide binding; Fig. 3B). Moreover, KEGG analysis revealed 56 significantly enriched pathways associated with the target genes of downregulated miRNAs, such as the Wnt signaling pathway, pancreatic secretion, endocrine resistance, and Hippo signaling pathway (Fig. 3D).

Through KEGG pathway analysis of the 10 most regulated miRNAs, together with a targeted literature review, we identified miR-124-3p to be the most functionally relevant. Furthermore, the target genes of miR-124-3p were significantly enriched in several key pathways, including those associated with cancer, small cell lung cancer, insulin signaling, and endocrine resistance (Fig. 3E). It was enhanced in the insulin signaling pathway by regulating the target genes MAPK, TRAF, STAT3, Wnt, GRB2, and NOX4, et al. Additionally, it was enriched in the cancer signaling pathway via the target genes STK3, STK4, ITGB1, STAT3, and Wnt, et al.

Validation of miR-124-3p by qRT-PCR

According to previous reports and screening conditions (|log2 fold change|>0.585 and P<0.05), we identified and used qRT-PCR to validate the expression of miR-124-3p. The expression trends of miR-124-3p were significantly higher in the pancreatic head than in the tail, which was consistent with the miRNA-seq results (Fig. 4).

Discussion

MiRNAs are short, non-coding RNAs that direct RNA silencing and post-transcriptional gene repression. They regulate the differentiation, proliferation, and survival of pancreatic β-cells, and accumulating evidence identifies β-cell miRNAs as critical post-transcriptional regulators of stress responses in diseases [18]. In this study, a total of 445 miRNAs were detected in rats’ pancreatic head and tail by RNA-seq, among which 69 (28 upregulated and 41 downregulated) were differentially expressed between the two regions. KEGG analysis revealed that the target genes of these DEmiRNAs are enriched in diabetes-related pathways, namely, insulin resistance, pancreatic secretion, MAPK signaling, and Wnt signaling, as well as in cancer-associated pathways, including pancreatic cancer, non-small cell lung cancer, Hippo signaling, and FoxO signaling. These data revealed significantly different miRNA expression levels between the head and tail, indicating region-specific molecular functions and underlying heterogeneity.

In the present study, multiple signaling pathways enriched in miRNA target genes were associated with diabetes. The polyherbal extract alleviated oxidative stress in diabetic rats via modulating the expression of MAPK-8, TRAF-4, and TRAF-6 within the MAPK/JNK signaling pathway [19]. Pomegranate peel extract-stabilized gold nanoparticles attenuated the proinflammatory burden in diabetic nephropathy by regulating the MAPK/NF–κB/STAT3 axis [20]. In addition, Wnt signaling is closely associated with T2D. Previous studies have shown that the Wnt/JNK pathway promotes insulin resistance, inflammation, and pancreatic β cell dysfunction [21]. The activation of the Wnt/β-Catenin signaling pathway increases GRB2 levels and its interaction with NOX4, thereby attenuating the severity of T2D [22]. Therefore, these DEmiRNAs intersect with multiple signaling pathways to alter β-cell maturity and functional identity, ultimately inducing region-specific heterogeneity in insulin secretion.

Bioinformatics analysis indicates that miRNAs are essential in cell development and metabolic processes, as well as in the onset, regulation, and progression of cancer. Numerous studies indicated that Hippo signaling plays a pivotal role in various cancers. The methylation of STK3 suppresses Hippo signaling and promotes pancreatic cancer progression [23]. Moreover, targeting the STK4/Hippo signaling pathway can inhibit tumor cell growth in prostate cancer [24]. Notably, the Wnt signaling pathways regulates various cellular processes, and its dysregulation contributes to tumor initiation and progression [25]. The Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway inhibits the development of pancreatic cancer cells by targeting STAT3 [26]. Thus, the target genes of DEmiRNAs are enriched in multiple oncogenic signaling pathways and display broad tumor-suppressive activity.

The KEGG pathway analysis of the 10 most regulated miRNAs, together with a targeted literature review, identified miR-124-3p as the most functionally relevant. qRT-PCR validation confirmed that miR-124-3p expression was significantly higher in the pancreatic head than in the tail, which was consistent with the miRNA-seq result. The miR-124 family comprising miR-124-1, miR-124-2, and miR-124-3 was highly conserved. These three members are produced by DNA methylation and implicated with cancers [27].

According to the TargetScan and miRDB databases, miR-124-3p was predicted to target 97 genes, including ITGB1, TRAF, GRB2, Wnt, SP1, and FZD4, et al.. KEGG pathway analysis revealed that miR-124-3p was mainly enriched in 32 pathways, such as small cell lung cancer and pathways associated with cancer. Luteolin mitigated breast cancer-associated depression in mice by targeting the miR-124-3p/TNF-α/TRAF-6 axis, suggesting that it may be a promising therapeutic candidate [28]. As a tumor-suppressive miRNA, miR-124-3p inhibits gastric cancer development by directly targeting ITGB1 [29]. Moreover, the ectopic overexpression of miR-124-3p diminishes cell survival, plate colony formation, and tumor growth by targeting Rac1 and SP1 in gastric cancer cells [30]. In addition, miR-124-3p has been shown to directly target SP1 in glioma rat models [31]. Interestingly, miR-124-3p targeting ITGB1 also inhibits the proliferation, migration, and invasion of colorectal cancer cells [32]. LINC00963 suppresses miR-124-3p expression to promote the proliferation and migration of colorectal cancer, and this suppression can be restored by FZD4 [33]. LINC01234 shRNA can enhance miR-124-3p expression, thereby downregulating its target gene GRB2 and inhibiting the progression of multiple myeloma [34]. These results revealed the crucial role of miR-124-3p as a tumor suppressor in regulating cancer signaling. However, miR-124-3p may also function as a tumor promotor in cancer. MiR-124-3p.1 accelerates triple-negative breast cancer cell growth by targeting the tumor inhibitor gene Axin1 and activating Wnt signaling [35]. Recent reports indicate that the pancreatic head exhibits a higher tumor incidence than the tail [36]. By contrast, tumors originating in the tail display markedly elevated malignancy, resulting in significantly reduced median survival and poor prognosis [37–41]. In this study, miR-124-3p expression in the pancreatic head was higher than that in the tail. Thus, the heterogeneity of miR-124-3p between the pancreatic head and tail may account for the elevated incidence yet diminished aggressiveness of neoplasms in the pancreatic head, as well as the opposite trend in the tail.

Some limitation should be acknowledged in this study. First, all conclusions were derived solely from rat models and await validation in human islet tissue. Therefore, validation in human specimens will commence as soon as suitable cohorts become available. Second, our study was limited to the identification of DEmiRNAs; functional validation via overexpression or knockout models is intended for future investigation.

Conclusions

In summary, we identified DEmiRNAs in the islet cells of rats’ pancreatic head and tail, indicating molecular heterogeneity between these two regions. MiR-124-3p exhibited pronounced regional divergence that strongly correlated with site-specific tumorigenesis. These findings offer a crucial theoretical foundation for the diagnosis, pathogenesis, and prognosis of pancreatic diseases.

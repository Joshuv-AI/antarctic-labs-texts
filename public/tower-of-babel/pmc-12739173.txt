A scalable scheduling and resource management framework for cloud-native B2B applications
Authors: Dinesh Komarasamy, Rajkumar Rajavel, Karthikeyan Harimoorthy, Aravind Pitchai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
In modern cloud computing environments, customers increasingly depend on on-demand resource provisioning to handle dynamic workloads. However, fluctuations in job arrival rates can result in prolonged queue times, which negatively affect overall system performance. Although existing scheduling algorithms provide efficient job management, they often fail to account for the combined impact of queue delays and the need for flexible resource provisioning—particularly in business-critical applications. In order to tackle these issues, the paper proposes a new Optimized Job Scheduling and Resource Scaling (OJSRS) algorithm designed to improve job execution efficiency and support elastic resource management in cloud environments. The OJSRS algorithm integrates two key components: Tree-based Job Scheduling (TJS) and Automated Resource Scaling and Scheduling (ARSS). The TJS component constructs a hierarchical structure that concurrently maps incoming jobs to the most suitable Virtual Machines (VMs), thereby minimizing queue delays. Meanwhile, ARSS adjusts resource allocation dynamically, increasing or decreasing capacity according to workload requirements and cloud service provider policies, enabling responsive and adaptive provisioning. Experimental results show that the OJSRS algorithm increases resource utilization by approximately 5–10% and accelerates job completion through proactive resource scaling. This approach provides a significant performance advantage for cloud-native business applications that require both efficiency and scalability.

Introduction

Cloud computing brings together a wide range of distributed resources to handle both compute-intensive and data-intensive tasks1. Business applications are also among the compute-intensive tasks executed in a cloud environment. The cloud service model is commonly divided into three layers: IaaS, PaaS, and SaaS. Among these, proposed study focuses on IaaS, which provides extensive computing and storage resources. In contrast, PaaS provides a development and deployment environment tailored to user needs, whereas SaaS delivers ready-to-use software applications to end users2. Most novice users prefer cloud environments for running jobs because the cloud enables on-demand resource provisioning, resource resizing, job migration, quick elasticity, extensive network contact, resource sharing, metered services, and improved availability and performance3. However, cloud environments still face challenges in interoperability, security, and the absence of built-in automated schedulers.

Cloud infrastructure leverages virtualization by partitioning computing capacity into multiple logical units, i.e., Virtual Machines (VMs). Virtualization technology allows dynamic VM creation, resizing, migration, and termination at runtime4. Many Cloud Service Providers (CSPs) deliver computational power to users for processing their jobs. However, traditional CSPs have a limited pool of resources, which leads to overprovisioning when job submissions are low and under provisioning when job submissions are high5.

Figure 1 illustrates the agreement on service levels established between CSPs and end users. Individual users or groups of users connect to the cloud to enhance their business through online platforms. The SLA specifies terms such as usage policies, processing speed, and cost. Thus, scalability of computational resources is critical to meeting user expectations and SLA requirements. An SLA represents a time and cost agreement between the user and the service provider6. CSPs employ both elastic and non-elastic provisioning strategies to fulfill SLA requirements7,8. Elastic provisioning executes jobs by creating and deleting VMs at runtime for each job, which may lead to excessive memory usage. In contrast, non-elastic provisioning executes jobs using only the VMs in the resource pool, often causing underutilization or overutilization.

Effective scheduling algorithms are essential to balance workloads across VMs and ensure timely job completion. However, job and resource scheduling remains challenging, as CSPs aim to predict the economic use of computational resources while end users demand high-quality, cost-effective services9,10. Existing scheduling algorithms typically process jobs sequentially and often fail to account for queue delays. Furthermore, traditional CSPs usually support either elastic or non-elastic provisioning, which can negatively affect Quality of Service (QoS) or resource utilization.

Traditional CSPs either support autoscaling or efficient resource utilization, but rarely both. Therefore, the goal of this research is to integrate optimal scheduling of jobs with autoscaling mechanisms. To this end, we propose a two-phase Optimized Job Scheduling and Automated Resource Scaling (OJSRS) algorithm, which consists of Tree-based Job Scheduling (TJS) and Automated Resource Scaling and Scheduling (ARSS). In the first phase, TJS concurrently maps jobs to VMs using a right-skewed binary tree constructed from currently available VMs. When the available VMs cannot meet job deadlines, the second phase scales up VMs to ensure timely execution and minimize job queuing delays.

The novelty of OJSRS lies in three aspects. First, the concurrent mapping of jobs to VMs using the right-skewed tree in TJS minimizes queue delays, whereas existing approaches (e.g., EDF, MVF, AMSS, PCP) rely on sequential mapping. Second, OJSRS integrates autoscaling decisions directly with unmapped jobs identified by TJS, ensuring demand-driven scaling at the job level rather than depending solely on static utilization thresholds. Third, OJSRS incorporates two-tier VM deployment (foreground and background), enabling conditional preemption without job interruption—an approach not found in earlier hybrid methods. Fourth, job execution and resource allocation are carried out independently by deploying two-tier VMs. Tasks are executed in the foreground part, while the background part utilizes the residual idle processing speediness to prioritize and map jobs to the resources of the cloud environment.

Literature review

A scheduler is responsible for selecting jobs from a queue and allocating them to VMs to ensure fair and timely completion. Most schedulers employ two mechanisms: job scheduling and resource scheduling. Job and resource scheduling are critical both for meeting the needs of novice users and for fulfilling the objectives of Cloud Service Providers (CSPs). Job scheduling algorithms rearrange the execution order of jobs to maximize the number completed before their deadlines, while resource scheduling assigns jobs to the most suitable VMs in the resource pool to achieve CSP objectives11.

To support novice users, researchers have developed a variety of job scheduling algorithms based on parameters such as deadline, timestamp, cost, makespan, and throughput. One of the earliest approaches was the Earliest Deadline First (EDF) algorithm, which prioritizes jobs with the earliest deadlines to maximize timely completion12. However, the EDF algorithm does not preempt a running job even if a newly arrived job has an earlier deadline. To address this, the fully preemptive EDF (fp-EDF) procedure was presented, allowing preemption of currently running jobs. This was further refined into the controlled preemptive EDF (cp-EDF) procedure, that conditionally preempts jobs only when necessary, thereby improving resource utilization by avoiding unnecessary interruptions13.

Despite these improvements, both fp-EDF and cp-EDF may lead to starvation, as jobs with the earliest deadlines dominate execution. To mitigate this, the Minimum Variation First (MVF) algorithm was developed, which schedules jobs based on the minimum deviation calculated by subtracting the deadline from the expected execution time. Expected execution time is derived from job attributes such as arrival time, length, and deadline14. Another approach, the Adaptive Multilevel Scheduling System (AMSS), schedules jobs by considering both job parameters and VM processing speed, sequentially assigning jobs to the most suitable VMs in the pool15. However, this approach may still fail to complete some jobs within their deadlines. To address this, the Partial Critical Path (PCP) algorithm was proposed. PCP operates in two phases: distribution, where deadlines are distributed to identify the critical path, and planning, where sub-deadlines are assigned and jobs are mapped to the least costly resources.

The PCP algorithm was later extended into the IaaS Cloud PCP (IC-PCP), a single-phase approach that schedules jobs directly rather than assigning sub-deadlines16. To further reduce deadline violations, Enhanced Infrastructure Cloud PCP with Replication (EIPR) was introduced, which executes jobs redundantly across multiple VMs. Although effective in preventing deadline misses, this often results in resource underutilization17. Several resource scheduling algorithms have also been developed to improve job-to-VM mapping. For example, the Dominant Resource Fairness (DRF) method clusters and schedules jobs based on dominant resource factors such as storage, computing power, or network capacity18. The Resource Attribute Selection (RAS) algorithm, instead, jointly considers computing capacity, storage, and network bandwidth to allocate resources efficiently. RAS also incorporates a work-stealing technique to balance workloads among VMs19.

Heuristic approaches have also been explored, including mixed integer linear programming formulations combined with best-fit and tabu search strategies to optimally map jobs to VMs20. However, such approaches typically depend on prior knowledge of job characteristics, which is often unavailable. To overcome this limitation, algorithms such as the Blind Scheduling Algorithm (BSA) and Blind Online Scheduling Algorithm (BOSA) were developed, which operate without prior job knowledge. BOSA, for instance, schedules jobs using the First Come, First Serve (FCFS) approach and assigns them to clusters with large idle time slots to minimize waiting time21,22. Additionally, a hierarchical load-balancing algorithm was proposed to distribute workloads across clusters by forwarding jobs to clusters with the highest available computing capacity. This approach considers CPU speed, memory usage, and network utilization23.

Non-elastic resource provisioning is well-suited for batches of jobs with similar workloads. However, depending on job characteristics, VMs may be underutilized (leading to idle resources) or overutilized (reducing performance). Elastic resource provisioning, in contrast, is more suitable when job arrival rates vary and jobs must meet strict deadlines. In such cases, VMs can be dynamically acquired or released at runtime, ensuring better alignment with workload fluctuations and avoiding both underutilization and overutilization. Therefore, optimal resource scaling is critical in elastic provisioning scenarios24.

To optimize resource use and minimize costs, several approaches leverage dynamic VM scaling. For example, policies such as the energy-aware rolling horizon scheduling algorithm dynamically create or terminate VMs in data centers to reduce energy consumption while meeting job deadlines27. Similarly, class-constrained bin packing has been applied to enhance user satisfaction through efficient resource scaling28. However, challenges remain: insufficient resources or delays in VM creation can increase waiting time, degrade performance, and add network overhead. The proposed OJSRS algorithm addresses these challenges by combining optimal job scheduling with automated resource scaling. It maximizes resource utilization through a two-tier VM deployment strategy, reduces job waiting times, and optimizes energy consumption while maintaining service quality.

Problem statement

As represented in the system model, user requests are treated as computational tasks submitted by users. Numerous jobs request services from the cloud system, and the number of jobs varies over time. Consequently, forecasting job arrival rates is a difficult task, making job scheduling both important and complex in cloud computing. After submission, jobs are executed on the available virtual machines.

In general, the cloud system supports two types of resource provisioning. In the first category, tasks are processed through the VMs provided by the resource pool. However, when the number of jobs is small, VMs are underutilized, and when the number of jobs is large, the available VMs may not provide sufficient processing capacity. In such cases, jobs may miss their deadlines, negatively impacting system performance, Quality of Service (QoS), and user satisfaction. In the second category, VMs can be created at runtime based on user requirements. After the job completes, the VM is destroyed. While this approach ensures flexibility, frequent VM creation and destruction affect resource utilization. Additionally, when a high-priority job arrives at a specific VM, the currently running job may be preempted to meet the new deadline. Although this ensures timely execution, frequent preemptions also reduce resource efficiency.

To address these challenges, VMs are organized in a two-tier setup consisting of foreground and background VMs, enabling a single VM to process two jobs concurrently. However, existing algorithms require additional time to prioritize and select the best VM from the resource pool. Therefore, a new algorithm is needed to process jobs more effectively by integrating VM utilization from the resource pool with dynamic VM scaling. For this reason, we propose the Optimized Job Scheduling and Resource Scaling (OJSRS) algorithm for cloud computing. The proposed algorithm minimizes overall processing time by executing jobs concurrently, thereby improving customer satisfaction and increasing CSP profits.

Optimized job scheduling and automated resource scaling algorithm in cloud

In a cloud environment, jobs demand computational resources to be executed. The environment comprises multiple CSPs that provide services to clients over the Internet through web interfaces. Each CSP manages several data centers containing multiple VMs for job execution. Depending on data center policy, VMs may operate in either a homogeneous or heterogeneous environment. At any point in time, a huge count of jobs may arrive in cloud management system. Traditionally, jobs are processed either by selecting best VM from the resource pool by creating a new VM at runtime based on user requirements. However, both methods have limitations when applied independently. To overcome these drawbacks, this paper proposes the OJSRS algorithm, which integrates job scheduling with automated resource scaling. By combining these techniques, OJSRS ensures efficient resource utilization, reduced queuing delays, and improved execution performance.

The rationale behind this structure is that the right-skewed binary tree (RSBT) ensures jobs requiring higher processing speeds are systematically compared down the tree, which is particularly efficient for heterogeneous workloads. The RSBT structure reduces average search and mapping overhead because jobs that cannot be handled by lower-level VMs (with lower idle speeds) are quickly escalated to higher-capacity VMs along the right branch. Alternative structures, such as balanced binary trees or heaps, would require additional restructuring operations during dynamic job arrivals, resulting in higher scheduling latency. In contrast, the RSBT maintains simplicity in insertion and job-to-VM comparison while ensuring that jobs with tighter deadlines and higher computational requirements are prioritized.

Experimental setup and result analysis

The proposed OJSRS model was implemented using CloudSim29. In the experimental setup, a random number of jobs was generated and stored in XML file format. Two additional parameters, i.e., job length and job deadline, were assigned during job generation. Furthermore, the number of generated jobs varied over time, and these jobs were stored in \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{Q}_{job}$$\end{document} and batched for optimal processing using the VMs in the CSP. The evaluation employed realistic workload traces from publicly available sources, including the Google Cluster Data and NASA iPSC workload traces (via the Parallel Workloads Archive). These datasets provide real-world job arrival patterns, execution times, and inter-arrival distributions. Incorporating these traces allowed us to validate OJSRS under conditions like production cloud environments. The results confirmed the robustness of OJSRS, demonstrating consistent improvements in makespan, resource utilization, and SLA violation rates for both synthetic and real workloads.

Performance analysis

Initially, the number of created VMs is determined by the job arrival rate, which is always smaller than the amount of VMs in a uniform setting because a single VM can process and complete multiple jobs before the deadline. In contrast, in a heterogeneous environment, the amount of VMs initially equals the count of incoming jobs. Thus, the number of VMs may be smaller than the total number of incoming jobs. Under the proposed OJSRS model, jobs are processed in an FCFS manner. The algorithm simultaneously maps each job to the best-fit VM by comparing the job’s required execution speed with the idle speed of the machine, achieved through tree construction. Since incoming jobs may be sparse or dense, VMs in the data center must scale up or down dynamically to improve resource utilization.

Job delay time

As mentioned earlier, the job arrival rate in cloud computing varies over time; therefore, jobs were generated randomly without adhering to any fixed scale. The proposed OJSRS model focuses on minimizing job waiting time to enhance user satisfaction. Existing scheduling algorithms, such as FCFS, EDF, PSO and AMSS, sequentially select the best-fit VM from the resource pool to process jobs. To report the criticality of workflows in real-world cloud applications, the study is further extended with additional evaluations using workflow-based task sets derived from Montage, CyberShake, and Epigenomics scientific workflows, implemented through the WorkflowSim extension of CloudSim. Conclusion and future work

Cloud systems face several challenges in completing incoming jobs before their deadlines. This research proposes an algorithm designed to minimize SLA violations, improve resource utilization, reduce job wait times, enhance user satisfaction, and increase CSP performance. The proposed OJSRS algorithm schedules jobs based on the job arrival rate and constructs a tree that concurrently maps jobs to the corresponding VMs in the resource pool. Some jobs, however, cannot be mapped to any VM after this process. Such jobs are handled by dynamically scaling up VMs in the CSP, either according to data center policy or the minimum required job processing speed. The system employs a two-tier architecture in which VMs are deployed as foreground and background VMs. One or more jobs are bound to the corresponding VM, and jobs are prioritized based on their attributes to avoid deadline violations. Through this prioritization, an incoming job may receive higher priority than currently running jobs. In such cases, the incoming job is processed in a background VM while the running job is conditionally paused. The proposed OJSRS algorithm outperforms existing scheduling algorithms by reducing deadline violations. It integrates job scheduling with resource scalability, thereby improving resource utilization by 5–10% and enhancing overall cloud system performance. Future improvements will focus on extending the algorithm to efficiently process dependent and workflow-based jobs.

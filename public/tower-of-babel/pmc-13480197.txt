Left atrium incisure curvature: an indicator for predicting atrial fibrillation-related cardioembolism
Authors: Jianli He, Yuanyuan Wang, Jun Hou, Ping Chen, Wan Wei, Dacheng Hu, Zhenyin Fu, Dingchang Zheng, Ling Xia, Dongdong Deng, Ruiqing Dong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe use of supplementary parameters, such as clinical criteria, biomarkers, and imaging data, may enhance the risk prediction of stroke and systemic embolic events. Current stroke risk scores are clinically practical but limited in their ability to predict stroke risk accurately in individual patients. It is difficult for doctors to distinguish patients with truly high-thrombotic risk. In this study, we propose a simple and effective method for predicting stroke and systemic embolic events in patients with atrial fibrillation (AF).MethodsIn this retrospective study, 169 patients with AF were divided into three groups: Group A (those with paroxysmal AF with no cardioembolic events), Group B (those with persistent AF with no cardioembolic events), and Group C (those with persistent AF with cardioembolic events). By examining the chest CT images of all the included patients, we discovered that the left atrium (LA) incisure disappearing with the progression of AF, particularly in those with cardioembolic events (Group C). Background

Stroke is a leading cause of death worldwide and the primary cause of permanent disability [1, 2]. With increasing age, the burden of stroke diseases in China is becoming increasingly severe [3]. Atrial fibrillation (AF) is a well-recognized cause of ischemic stroke and is a common and significant factor contributing to the recurrence of stroke [4]. Estimates of the prevalence of AF among patients with ischemic stroke vary between 15% and 38% [5–7]. AF can also cause other systemic embolisms, such as mesenteric artery embolism and acute limb arterial embolism [8]. Accordingly, thromboembolic risk prediction in patients with AF is clinically crucial [9]. In addition to commonly used clinical risk scales (such as CHADS2, CHA2DS2-VASc, ATRIA score, etc.) [10–12], to more accurately predict AF-related stroke and embolism risk, a variety of additional parameters have gradually entered the field of clinical research [13, 14]. These parameters focus mainly on biomarkers, metabolic indicators, genetic risk and imaging data, which might improve stroke risk prediction and inform optimal treatment for patients with AF and potentially individuals who are only at risk of AF [15–17].

Although atrial dysfunction plays a key role in thrombus formation, evaluating atrial function in the state of AF is difficult [18]. Analyzing atrial morphological changes by using an image processing technique might be an alternative method for evaluating atrial function. In clinical practice, we surprisingly observed the disappearance of the left atrium anterior wall incisure (LA incisure) in almost all embolism patients; the LA incisure became flat or even bulged forwards with increasing LA. On the basis of these findings, we conducted a retrospective study. We concluded that LA incisure curvature might be an effective indicator for predicting AF-related stroke or systemic embolic events. The encouraging results of this study are shown below.

Methods

Study population

This study enrolled 169 (n = 89 females, and n = 80 males) patients with AF from among the inpatients of Hangzhou First People’s Hospital. Among the enrolled patients with cardioembolic events (Group C, n = 50), twenty-eight patients had evidence of mechanical thrombectomy, ten had cerebral embolism, fourteen had limb extremity embolism, and four had visceral–mesenteric system embolism. The other twenty-two patients did not receive mechanical thrombectomy therapy, and most of them missed the chance to undergo mechanical thrombectomy. The study included patients seen from 2008 to 2018. All patients underwent non-contrast chest CT scans, and a subset of patients also underwent coronary CTA. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) ECG evidence of AF; (2) AF with a history of mechanical thrombectomy or imaging evidence (confirmed by cranial CT, CT angiography or head MR examination), with CT or MR imaging reassessed by a radiologist; and (3) diagnosis of a cardiac source of embolism [19–21]. The exclusion criteria were artery emboli related to artery stenosis, cerebral infarction with carotid stenosis, and infective endocarditis and the absence of a patent foramen ovale. Individuals with poor-quality CT images (n = 34) were excluded because the border of the LA incisure could not be detected or analyzed. One hundred sixty-nine patients were eligible for the final analyses.

Border detection curvature computation of LA incisure

To obtain the morphological characteristics of the LA, border detection along with the LA incisure curvature computation algorithm was used in this work. The left atrium anterior wall incisure is a physiological depression formed by mechanical compression of the aortic sinus on the anterior wall of the left atrium. Under normal anatomical conditions, this incisure presents as a distinct inward concavity. The area of interest (AOI) selection standard was as follows. The CT image level was chosen at the same level as the aortic sinus cusp, and the CT frame with the most prominent and deepest LA incisure was selected for analysis. This algorithm was developed on the basis of common image processing techniques. A semiautomated segmentation method was used for left atrial border detection, for which the details can be found in Supplemental material S1. After the border of LA incisure was extracted, the curvature of the incisure was calculated. In general, the absolute value of curvature increases as the degree of curvature increases; if the incisure is concave inward, the curvature value is negative; if the incisure is convex outward, the curvature value is positive; and if the incisure is flat, the curvature value is near zero.

Statistical methods

Continuous variables are expressed as the mean (x) ± standard deviation (SD) in the case of a normal distribution, and differences among Groups A, B, and C were determined by one-way ANOVA. Data are expressed as medians (interquartile ranges) in the case of a skewed distribution. Categorical variables are presented as counts (percentages) and were compared by the Pearson chi-square test.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and the area under the ROC curve (AUC) were used to evaluate the predictive accuracy of the average curvature of the LA incisure for the risk of future cardioembolism. Generally, an AUC ranging between 0.5 and 0.7 indicates a low predictive value, 0.7–0.9 indicates a moderate predictive value, and > 0.9 indicates a good predictive value. The Youden index was also calculated by the sensitivity+specificity-1. The optimal cut-off points for the average curvature of LA incisure for predicting the risk of cardioembolism were determined by the maximum Youden index, with relatively high sensitivity and specificity. To assess the robustness of the predictive model, leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) was performed, and the cross-validated accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and AUC were calculated. The differences between the average curvature and CHADS2 score were compared by Z tests. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed to assess whether LA incisure curvature could serve as an independent predictor of cardioembolism after adjusting for related clinical confounding factors.

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software for Windows (version 24; SPSS, Inc., Chicago, Illinois). A two-sided probability value of P < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance in all analyses.

This research was performed without public involvement. Patients were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting, or dissemination plans of our research. This study was approved by the Hangzhou First People’s Hospital Review Board as a data-only study with a waiver of consent because it posed no risk to the subjects. A permit was acquired to use data from Hangzhou First People’s Hospital.

Results

The study population was divided into three groups: Group A (paroxysmal AF with no cardioembolic event), Group B (persistent AF with no cardioembolic event), and Group C (persistent AF with cardioembolic event). In addition to the CHADS2 score, the LA size, left ventricular ejection fraction and anticoagulant use were included in the study. There was no sex difference among the three groups and no age difference between Groups B and C. The left ventricular ejection fraction was the lowest in Group C. Among the three groups, the LA size was the smallest in Group A. Although the percentage of patients with a CHADS2 score greater than 2 points was 88%, the use of anticoagulants was not sufficient in Group C (only 14%). The use of anticoagulants guided by the CHADS2 score was not satisfactory in our clinical practice; thus, it is necessary to develop new guidelines for anticoagulant use. To show the specialized structure of the LA, two typical 3D LAs were reconstructed, as shown in Fig. 1. We found that the LA incisure was formed by compression of the aortic sinus in the normal patient (Fig. 1A and B), whereas it became flat in the patient with an embolism event (Fig. 1C and D). For convenience in the quantitative analysis of LA incisure curvature, 2D CT images of the LA were used. The four types of LA incisure gradually disappeared as the disease progressed (Fig. 2). Type I exhibited a deep LA incisure. Type II had a moderately deep LA incisure. Type III had a slightly flat incisure, and Type IV displayed a flat incisure. The risk of embolism was greater for type III and type IV patients.

Figure 3 shows the results of the quantitative analysis of LA incisure curvature. The medians (interquartile ranges) of LA incisure curves were -2.6 (-3.55, -1.95) ×10− 3, -1.40 (-2.13, -0.43) ×10− 3, and 0.51 (0.02, 1.40) ×10− 3 for Groups A, B, and C, respectively. The cut-off point of the average curvature was - 0.905 × 10− 3. The accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity of the LA incisure curvature for predicting cardioembolism were 85.21%, 96%, and 80.7%, respectively. Compared with the CHADS2 score (2 points or more), the LA incisure curvature had better sensitivity and specificity for predicting the risk of cardioembolism. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed to identify independent predictors of cardioembolic events, and the results are shown in Table 2. After adjusting for age, sex, heart failure status, CHADS2 score, DM, and anticoagulant use, the LA incisure curvature remained a significant independent predictor of cardioembolism (OR = 10.987; 95% CI: 3.354–35.984; P < 0.001). Leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) confirmed the stable predictive performance of the model. The cross-validation analysis yielded an accuracy of 0.933, a sensitivity of 0.909, a specificity of 0.941, and an AUC of 0.969 (95% CI: 0.940–0.989; P < 0.001), indicating strong and robust discrimination.

Discussion

This paper introduces a new method for predicting atrial fibrillation-related stroke and systemic embolic events. Preliminary results revealed that the LA incisure curvature-based cardioembolism prediction method has high accuracy, sensitivity and specificity. In clinical practice, compared with 3D imaging, 2D chest CT is easier to acquire and more extensively applied because of safety and other relevant factors. The clinical utility of using standard chest CT (often already available) for risk stratification is a strong point. Physicians may even identify patients with AF who are at high risk of thrombosis through the interpretation of chest CT images. A global validation of this approach would offer significant benefits to a large population of patients with AF and consequently reduce thousands of deaths and disabilities attributable to cardioembolic events.

Although our results revealed that LA incisure curvature is an effective indicator for predicting AF-related stroke and systemic embolic events, the underlying mechanism is not fully understood. With AF onset, further atrial remodeling occurs and leads to left atrial dysfunction. The LA volume has been well recognized as a biomarker of stroke risk. An increased LA volume is associated with AF and stroke events, moderate to severe LA enlargement is a predictor of stroke recurrence [22, 23], and the LA volume index is associated with cardioembolic stroke [24]. Moreover, the disappearance of left atrium incisure tends to imply a greater degree of left atrial sphericity. This higher degree of left atrial sphericity has been shown to be associated with a higher rate of atrial fibrillation recurrence and, consequently, a higher incidence of thromboembolism in patients.

An enlarged LA cavity is usually associated with increased pressure and poor myocardial compliance. Increased LA pressure and volume may be the main reasons for the flat LA incisure. Another mechanism we will elucidate is the decreased cardiac output index. LA incisure formation depends on the pressure of the aortic sinus in addition to the LA pressure. It is easy to understand the low cardiac output index in patients with heart failure, among whom there is a greater occurrence of strokes.

There are several limitations in this study. First, this was a single-center study, and the number of selected patients was limited. In this retrospective study, all the patients underwent only non-contrast chest CT scans. In future prospective studies, we will adopt contrast-enhanced chest CT to achieve fully automated segmentation of the left atrial incisure. It is necessary to carry out multi-center retrospective and prospective research in the future. Second, owing to the limitations of the retrospective study, the chest CT images used in this study were not scanned with ECG gating. In the future, ECG-gated enhanced CT should be employed, and the prediction performance for cardioembolic events may be further improved. Third, as previously mentioned, the mechanism underlying LA incisure alteration in patients with AF was not investigated in this study. Future studies may employ noninvasive imaging to quantify cardiac output (by echocardiography or cardiac MRI) and measure left atrial pressure.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this retrospective study demonstrated that the LA incisure curvature is a simple and effective indicator for predicting AF-related stroke and systemic embolic events. The validity of this method needs to be further confirmed by means of multi-center retrospective and prospective research, and the underlying mechanism should be investigated in future analyses.

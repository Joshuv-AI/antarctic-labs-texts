Temperature-sensitive liquid embolic agent loaded with Lobaplatin in the TACE procedure for rabbit VX2 liver tumor
Authors: Yahua Li, Junyao Chen, Jialong Zhang, Zongming Li, Yifan Zhang, Hao Chen, Baolin Zhou, Yangduan Yao, Kewei Ren, Xi Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo investigate the efficacy of a temperature-sensitive liquid embolic (TempSLE) agent loaded with lobaplatin for transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) in a rabbit VX₂ liver tumor model. Rabbit VX₂ liver tumor models were randomly divided into four groups: control, hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy (HAIC), conventional TACE (C-TACE) with lipiodol, and experimental TACE (T-TACE) with TempSLE. Tumor volume, serum and intratumoral concentrations of lobaplatin (LBP), and serum levels of ALT, AST, and CREA were analyzed. Immunohistochemical (IHC) staining for PCNA, BCL-2, and HIF-1, as well as immunofluorescence (IF) staining for P53, cyclin B, and PD-L1, were evaluated. On postoperative day 7, tumor volumes were 5.17 ± 0.45 cm3 in the control group and 3.37 ± 0.22 cm3 in the HAIC group, which constituted a significant difference. No contrast enhancement was observed in the C-TACE and T-TACE groups, indicating complete tumor necrosis. On postoperative day 3, serum LBP concentration was significantly higher in the cTACE group than in the HAIC and T-TACE groups. By day 7, there were no intergroup differences in serum LBP. At both 3 and 7 days post-procedure, intratumoral LBP concentration was highest in the T-TACE group and lowest in the HAIC group. IHC and IF analyses revealed that T-TACE significantly down-regulated the expression of PCNA and BCL-2, but up-regulated HIF-1. Furthermore, T-TACE down-regulated cyclin B and P53 expression, while up-regulating PD-L1. This study demonstrates that TACE using TempSLE loaded with lobaplatin shows more favorable modulation of proliferation, apoptosis markers, and PD-L1 expression in tumor treatment.Keywords: Temperature-sensitive liquid embolic, Lobaplatin, TACE, Liver VX2 tumorSubject terms: Cancer, Gastroenterology, Medical research, Oncology

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains a major global health problem, with mortality rates continuing to rise due to its poor prognosis1. Accounting for approximately 90% of primary liver cancers, HCC is the world’s sixth most common malignancy and the third leading cause of cancer-related death globally2. According to the Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) staging system, transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) is the first-line treatment for intermediate-stage HCC. It is also a recommended option for early-stage patients for whom curative treatments are not feasible or have failed, an approach particularly validated in East Asian populations3,4. TACE works by delivering chemotherapy agents directly to the tumor via the hepatic artery, followed by embolization of the tumor-feeding vessels to induce ischemic necrosis5–7.

The choice of embolic material is critical in TACE. An ideal agent should not only carry chemotherapy drugs but also possess optimal flowability to reach the tumor vasculature and provide durable vessel occlusion. Currently available embolics have limitations in achieving this balance8. In conventional TACE (C-TACE), lipiodol offers good initial flowability, allowing it to penetrate peripheral tumor arteries. However, its rapid clearance in vivo compromises long-term embolization. Moreover, the mixture of water-soluble chemotherapeutic agents with lipiodol forms an unstable emulsion that rapidly separates, leading to an uncontrolled burst release of the drug. Conversely, in drug-eluting bead TACE (D-TACE), the beads provide near-permanent vessel occlusion but, due to their larger size, often fail to penetrate distal tumor vasculature, resulting in incomplete embolization and potential treatment failure9. Notably, comparative studies have not consistently demonstrated the superiority of D-TACE over C-TACE1,8. To address this flowability-embolization dilemma, a temperature-sensitive liquid embolic (TempSLE) agent has been developed. It exhibits excellent biocompatibility, high drug-loading capacity, and controlled drug-release properties, undergoing a sol–gel phase transition in response to body temperature. Upon injection, the liquid solution flows into branching vessels and transforms into a stable gel at physiological temperature, achieving comprehensive vascular embolization.

Platinum-based agents are among the most commonly used chemotherapy drugs in TACE. Lobaplatin (LBP), a third-generation platinum compound, has been reported to induce apoptosis and cell cycle arrest while inhibiting the migration and invasion of HCC cells in vitro10,11. A retrospective clinical study indicated that TACE using gelatin sponge microparticles mixed with LBP is safe and effective for patients with BCLC stage B HCC, with a median overall survival of 25 months12. Furthermore, a study by Zhou et al. demonstrated that TACE using TempSLE loaded with LBP for unresectable primary HCC achieved an objective response rate (ORR) of 90.91% and a disease control rate (DCR) of 100%, confirming its safety and efficacy13. However, the specific mechanisms by which LBP-loaded TempSLE affects HCC remain unclear. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the therapeutic effects and potential mechanisms of TempSLE loaded with LBP in a rabbit VX2 liver tumor model treated with TACE.

Materials and methods

The TempSLE was supplied by Beijing Guanhe Medical Technology Co. Lipiodol (ethiodized oil injection) was obtained from Shanghai Xudong Haipu Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. Lobaplatin was sourced from Hainan Changan International Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. All chemical reagents used were of analytical grade. The New Zealand rabbits were provided by the Animal Experimental Center of our University.

Animal study

This study was conducted and reported in accordance with the ARRIVE guidelines. A total of 24 New Zealand rabbits, weighing 3.0 ± 0.2 kg, both male and female were used to establish the rabbit liver VX₂ tumor model in this study. The establishment and evaluation methods were referenced to the previous study14. All animal experiments were performed in compliance with the Chinese Council on Animal Research Guidelines and the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of our institution (Ethical Approval Number: 2022-421).

TACE procedure

The rabbit liver VX₂ tumor models were randomly divided into a control group (n = 6), the HAIC group (n = 6), the C-TACE group (n = 6), and the T-TACE group (n = 6). All the rabbits were fasted for 6 h before the TACE procedure. Anesthesia was performed by xylazine hydrochloride (0.2 mg/kg) and pentobarbital sodium (5 mg/kg) injected intramuscularly according to the body weights of rabbits. After successful anesthesia, the right femoral artery was dissected and a 22 G puncture needle was used to puncture it. A micro-guidewire was introduced to the right femoral artery under the guidance of DSA. Then 2.7F microcatheter was introduced along with the micro-guidewire to abdominal artery. The angiography was performed to show the celiac trunk artery and the common hepatic artery. After super-select the hepatic artery and tumor-feeding artery with the microcatheter. The control group received shame TACE treatment after hepatic artery angiography, and saline was injected via microcatheter during the operation. The HAIC group received LBP injection, after hepatic artery angiograph. A total of 6 mg lobaplatin dissolved in 6 ml of water for injection, infused over 6 min. The C-TACE group received lipiodol loaded LBP embolization after hepatic artery angiography. A total of 6 mg lobaplatin dissolved in 0.3 ml of water for injection and mixed with 0.6 ml of lipiodol. Meanwhile, the T-TACE group received TempSLE loaded LBP embolization. A total of 6 mg lobaplatin mixed with 0.3 ml of water for injection and 0.6 ml of TempSLE. Repeated angiography was performed to confirm complete embolization. The microcatheter was withdrawn, the femoral artery was ligated, and then sutured the skin. When the rabbits recovered from anesthesia, they were sent to the animal cage.

Computed tomography evaluation

The enhancement computed tomography (CT) was used to evaluate the tumor response. The longest diameter (D) and the shortest diameter (d) of each tumor were measured on the arterial phase images. The formula for calculating the tumor volume (V) is V = 0.5 * D * d2.

Blood sample acquisition and testing

Each rabbit was obtained 1 ml blood before and after procedure 3 and 7 days, respectively. After centrifugation, the supernatant was taken and stored at − 80 °C for further use. The levels of ALT, AST, and CREA in serum were detected by ELISA assay. The concentration of LBP was detected by ICP-MS.

Tumor specimen acquisition and pathological staining

The liver tumor specimens were collected 3 and 7 days after procedure by means of anesthetic overdose sacrifice. The tumor specimens were divided in half. One-half was stored at − 80 °C for tissue LBP concentration analysis by ICP-MS. One-half was embedded in paraffin and sectioned for HE, immunohistochemical (IH), and immunofluorescence (IF) staining analysis. The IH indicators were PCNA, BCL-2, and HIF-1. The IF indicators were P53, cyclin-B, and PD-L1. The heart, spleen, left lung, and kidney tissue were obtained and sectioned for HE analysis.

Statistical analysis

The measurement data were described as mean ± standard deviation. Statistical analysis was performed utilizing SPSS 26. Figures were made using GraphPad Prism 9.0.0 software. The tumor volume, LBP concentration, and indicators expression of IH and IF were compared by means of ANOVA, and multiple comparisons were determined by the LSD test.

Results

All rabbits that underwent VX₂ liver tumor implantation survived and developed viable liver tumors. All 24 rabbits across the four groups successfully received the assigned interventions, yielding a procedural success rate of 100% with no procedure-related complications. As shown in Fig. 1A, tumor formation was visible on plain CT, with clear enhancement in the arterial phase across all groups. The tumor volume in the control, HAIC, C-TACE, and T-TACE group was 0.68 ± 0.02 cm3, 0.77 ± 0.06 cm3, 0.66 ± 0.10 cm3, and 0.75 ± 0.08 cm3 respectively. There was no statistical difference between groups (P > 0.05). During the hepatic arterial angiography, all the rabbits in the four groups could observed obviously staining in the left liver lobe. In the C-TACE and T-TACE groups, this staining was completely eliminated post-embolization (Fig. 1B). The enhancement CT images on Postoperative day 7 enhancement CT images are presented in Fig. 1C. Compared to preoperative scans, the control and HAIC groups exhibited significant tumor enlargement and enhancement, with volumes reaching 5.17 ± 0.45 cm3, and 3.37 ± 0.22 cm3, respectively. In the C-TACE group, lipiodol deposition was evident in the left lobe without any enhancement. The T-TACE group showed patchy hypodense areas in the left lobe, also without enhancement. The difference in tumor volume between the control and HAIC groups was statistically significant (Fig. 1D).

On postoperative day 3, serum LBP concentration was significantly higher in the C-TACE group than in the HAIC and T-TACE groups. There was significant statistical difference between the C-TACE and T-TACE group. No significant difference was found between the HAIC and C-TACE groups or between the HAIC and T-TACE groups. By postoperative day 7, serum LBP levels showed no intergroup differences (Fig. 1E). In contrast, intratumoral LBP concentrations on both days 3 and 7 were highest in the T-TACE group and lowest in the HAIC group, with significant differences among all three treatment groups (Fig. 1F).

H&E staining revealed extensive tumor cell infiltration on days 3 and 7 in the control group. The HAIC group showed patchy tumor cells on day 3 and increased infiltration by day 7. Both the C-TACE and T-TACE groups exhibited areas of patchy tumor and necrosis at both time points.

Regarding PCNA on day 3, the T-TACE group was significantly lower than the C-TACE (P < 0.001), HAIC (P < 0.0001), and control (P < 0.0001) groups. The C-TACE group was lower than the control (P < 0.01) and HAIC (P < 0.05) groups, with no difference between control and HAIC. On day 7, the T-TACE group remained lower than the HAIC and control groups (both P < 0.0001), with no difference from the C-TACE group. The C-TACE group was lower than both the control and HAIC groups (both P < 0.0001), while control and HAIC remained comparable (Fig. 2C).

Discussion

The management strategies for HCC have undergone a revolutionary paradigm shift with the advent of the targeted-immunotherapy era. Its core lies in moving beyond singular local–regional or systemic therapies into a new era of precision combination therapy. This new paradigm is fundamentally based on targeted-immunotherapy combinations, deeply integrates local–regional treatments, and is driven by the goal of conversion therapy with curative intent. The progression from TACE, a traditional locoregional treatment, to the synergistic strategy of TACE combined with molecular targeted agents (MTT), and further extending to the integrated TACE plus targeted therapy and immunotherapy triple-modality approach, represents a significant advancement in interventional combination therapy for HCC15–17. Indeed, while the introduction of targeted and immune therapies has improved the prognosis of TACE treatment, the refinement of embolic materials used in TACE as a local therapy remains an ongoing area of development. Recent advances in functional biomaterials have led to the development of innovative embolic agents, particularly smart hydrogels18. Among these materials, the continuous refinement and modulation of hydrogels may potentially enhance the efficacy of TACE therapy.

The TempSLE is a type of hydrogel characterized by its three-dimensional network structure and swelling properties. These physical characteristics enable it to encapsulate drugs, making it widely applicable in drug delivery systems. Previous study has demonstrated that the TempSLE could effectively loaded oxaliplatin. The in vitro accumulative drug release rate at 30 min was 38.76%, and after 24 h, it reached more than 90%19. Furthermore, when treating rabbit VX2 gastric tumor model by means of TACE, the tumor shrinkage rate, the VEGF expression and the PACN expression in the TempSLE group was better than the lipiodol group19. In this study, both the T-TACE and C-TACE group observed the tumor necrosis without obvious enhancement on CT on post-operation 7 days. While, the tumor volume in the HAIC group significantly lower than the control group. Which demonstrated that the TACE could induce tumor necrosis and LBP could inhibit tumor growth. The serum concentration of LBP in C-TACE group was higher than T-TACE group on post-operation 3 days, which proved that the burst release was more significant in the C-TACE group. In contrast, both the post-operation 3 and 7 days, the LBP concentration in liver tissue in the T-TACE group was higher than the C-TACE group and proved that the TempSLE have a better local drug delivery effect than the lipiodol. For IH analysis, both T-TACE and C-TACE could induce the high expression of HIF-1 on post-operation 3 days. While in the HAIC group, it is almost the same as the control group. Which demonstrated that the embolization leads to tumor tissue hypoxia instead of LBP. Furthermore, the decrease in HIF-1 expression caused by tumor hypoxia necrosis may explain why there was no statistical differences among the four groups on post-operation 7 days. The differences in BCL-2 and PCNA expression also indicate that the type of embolic agent used during TACE operation played a major role, further confirming that TempSLE is superior to lipiodol.

Platinum-based drugs bend and unwind the DNA, preventing transcription and replication. The cascade effect of this binding is the induction of apoptosis, a form of programmed cell death20. Lobaplatin inhibited the proliferation of human HCC cells in a dose-dependent manner and arrested cell cycle progression in G1 and G2/M phases time-dependently11. The tumor suppressor p53 functions mainly as a transcriptional activator of many cellular programs, including checkpoint activation, DNA repair, and apoptosis, and therefore the status of p53 is crucial for the cytotoxicity of LBP21. In this study, T-TACE demonstrated sustained comprehensive efficacy in both direct antitumor effects and remodeling of the immune microenvironment. HAIC highlighted the time-enhanced potential for immune priming, while the efficacy of C-TACE fell between the two but was inferior to that of T-TACE. Both T-TACE and C-TACE exhibited significant anti-proliferative effects on post-operation 3 days, reflecting the rapid-onset advantage conferred by embolization-induced acute ischemia/hypoxia and localized high-concentration drug release. By post-operation 7 days, the effect of T-TACE was further enhanced and significantly superior to the C-TACE group, suggesting that the TempSLE may provide more sustained and stable drug release compared to lipiodol, thereby maintaining stronger long-term proliferation inhibition. In contrast, the HAIC group failed to significantly reduce Cyclin B on post-operation 3 days, showing only a tentative inhibitory trend by day 7. This indicates that pure infusion chemotherapy without embolization is weaker and slower in directly inhibiting cell proliferation compared to embolization therapies. Regarding the P53 indicator, expressions in both the T-TACE and C-TACE groups were significantly lower than in the Control and HAIC groups on post-operation 3 and 7 days, with T-TACE showing the lowest levels. This reflects that potent embolization therapy induces rapid and extensive tumor cell apoptosis. A large number of P53-expressing cells were likely cleared before the detection time points, resulting in the observed decrease in residual P53 signal within the tissue. This indirectly confirms the strongest direct tumor-killing capacity of TACE, particularly T-TACE. Conversely, P53 expression in the HAIC group remained relatively high on both post-operation 3 and 7 days. This aligns with the mechanism of sustained DNA damage stress caused by continuous drug perfusion, where the efficiency and speed of cell clearance may be lower than with embolization therapy. In terms of PD-L1 dynamics, both T-TACE and C-TACE rapidly upregulated PD-L1 on post-operation 3 days, indicating that tumor cell damage and immunogenic cell death caused by embolization can swiftly initiate an immune response alongside feedback upregulation of immunosuppression. The HAIC group showed no significant effect at this early stage. On post-operation 7 days, PD-L1 expression was significantly upregulated in all treatment groups. However, the HAIC group its expression level substantially increased. This confirms the time-dependent nature of the powerful PD-L1 induction by sustained lobaplatin exposure. T-TACE maintained a high and stable level of PD-L1 upregulation throughout the process. This upregulation signifies that the treatments triggered a tumor’s adaptive immune resistance mechanism. The high PD-L1 levels suggest that T-TACE create a highly favorable microenvironment for subsequent combination immunotherapy with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors.

This study also has some limitations. First, the observation period was relatively short, and the absorption or clearance of lipiodol was not monitored, thus failing to highlight the advantage of TempSLE as a permanent embolic agent. Second, this study did not include a group receiving TempSLE without lobaplatin. Therefore, the observed superiority of T-TACE over C-TACE in terms of proliferation and apoptosis markers cannot be unequivocally attributed to sustained drug release alone. A drug-free TempSLE arm is needed to decouple the embolic and pharmacological contributions, and this requires further investigation. Third, PD-L1 expression levels were used as an indicator to explore adaptive immunosuppressive feedback; however, further investigation is needed regarding immune activation.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study demonstrates that TACE using TempSLE loaded with lobaplatin shows more favorable modulation of proliferation, apoptosis markers, and PD-L1 expression in tumor treatment.

Lunar Time Ephemeris LTE440: User Manual
Xu Lu, Tian-Ning Yang, Yi Xie

Abstract
We present the numerical lunar time ephemeris LTE440 based on the definition of Lunar Coordinate Time (TCL) given by the International Astronomical Union (IAU) in IAU 2024 Resolution II. LTE440 can be used to obtain the numerical transformation between TCL and Solar System Barycentric Dynamical Time (TCL-TDB) or Solar System Barycentric Coordinate Time (TCL-TCB). The theoretical model, numerical method, and performance of LTE440 are discussed. The secular drifts between TCL and TDB and between TCL and TCB are respectively estimated as ⟨ d ​ TCL / d ​ TDB ⟩ = 1 + 6.798 355 238 × 10 − 10 \langle d\mathrm{TCL}/d\mathrm{TDB}\rangle=1+6.798\,355\,238\times 10^{-10} , and ⟨ d ​ TCL / d ​ TCB ⟩ = 1 − 1.482 536 216 67 × 10 − 8 \langle d\mathrm{TCL}/d\mathrm{TCB}\rangle=1-1.482\,536\,216\,67\times 10^{-8} . The most significant periodic terms have amplitudes of about 1651 microseconds for the annual term and 126 microseconds for the monthly term. The precision of LTE440 is estimated to be at the level of several picoseconds. LTE440 is freely available at https://github.com/xlucn/LTE440 .



1
Outline

We provide the numerical lunar time ephemeris LTE440 based on the definition of Lunar Coordinate Time (TCL) given by the International Astronomical Union (IAU) in IAU 2024 Resolution II.
LTE440 can be used to obtain the numerical transformation between TCL and Solar System Barycentric Dynamical Time (TCL-TDB) or between TCL and Solar System Barycentric Coordinate Time (TCL-TCB).
More details on Lunar Coordinate Time can be found in
[
1
,
2
]
.

LTE440 consists of one BSP file
lte440.bsp
and one TPC file
lte440.tpc
.
The BSP file contains the periodic part of the lunar time ephemeris, while the coefficient of secular drift is stored in the TPC file.
The TCL-TDB transformation is given by the sum of the periodic part and the secular drift.
Detailed usage of these files is described in Section
2
.

Sect.
3
describes the details of the construction of the lunar time ephemeris LTE440.
The theoretical model of TCL-TDB transformation is provided in Sect.
3.1
.
The model closely follows the TT-TDB model in DE440, considering the gravitational potential of the Sun, all planets, main-belt asteroids, and Kuiper belt objects.
The numerical method used is described in Sect.
3.2
.
Information on the secular drift and major periodic terms is presented in Sect.
3.3
.

After preliminary analysis, we find that the secular drift between TCL and TDB is approximately
\langle d\mathrm{TCL}/d\mathrm{TDB}\rangle=1+6.798\,355\,238\times 10^{-10}
,
and the secular drift between TCL and TCB is approximately
\langle d\mathrm{TCL}/d\mathrm{TCB}\rangle=1-1.482\,536\,216\,67\times 10^{-8}
.
The most significant periodic terms are the annual term with an amplitude of about 1.65 milliseconds and the monthly term with an amplitude of about 126 microseconds.
The precision of the lunar time ephemeris is estimated to be at the level of several picoseconds.

3
Model and Numerical Method

3.1
Model of TCL-TDB transformation

According to the definition of Lunar Coordinate Time (TCL) in IAU 2024 Resolution II
5
5

5

IAU 2024 Resolution II: ”to establish a standard Lunar Celestial Reference System (LCRS) and Lunar Coordinate Time (TCL)”,
https://www.iau.org/Iau/Publications/List-of-Resolutions
, the transformation between TCL and TDB is given by the TCG-TDB relation, with the Earth-related quantities replaced by the Moon-related quantities. With the transformation between TDB and TCB that
6
6

6

IAU 2006 Resolution B3: ”Re-definition of Barycentric Dynamical Time, TDB”,
https://www.iau.org/Iau/Publications/List-of-Resolutions

\mathrm{TDB}=\mathrm{TCB}-L_{\mathrm{B}}(\mathrm{TCB}-T_{0})+\mathrm{TDB}_{0},

(1)

the TCL-TDB relation can be obtained as

\displaystyle\mathrm{TDB}-\mathrm{TCL}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{L_{\mathrm{B}}}{1-L_{\mathrm{B}}}(\mathrm{TDB}-T_{0})+\frac{1}{1-L_{\mathrm{B}}}\mathrm{TDB}_{0}

(2)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{1-L_{\mathrm{B}}}\left[\frac{1}{c^{2}}\int_{\mathrm{TDB}_{0}+T_{0}}^{\mathrm{TDB}}\left(\frac{v_{\mathrm{M}}^{2}}{2}+w_{\mathrm{0M}}+w_{l\mathrm{M}}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{c^{2}}\boldsymbol{v}_{\mathrm{M}}\cdot(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathrm{M}})\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{1-L_{\mathrm{B}}}\left[\frac{1}{c^{4}}\int_{\mathrm{TDB}_{0}+T_{0}}^{\mathrm{TDB}}\left(-\frac{v_{\mathrm{M}}^{4}}{8}-\frac{3}{2}v_{\mathrm{M}}^{2}w_{\mathrm{0M}}+4\boldsymbol{v}_{\mathrm{M}}\cdot\boldsymbol{w}_{\mathrm{M}}+\frac{1}{2}w_{\mathrm{0M}}^{2}+\Delta_{\mathrm{M}}\right)\mathrm{d}t\right.

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{c^{4}}\left.\left(3w_{\mathrm{0M}}+\frac{v_{\mathrm{M}}^{2}}{2}\right)\boldsymbol{v}_{\mathrm{M}}\cdot(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}_{\mathrm{M}})\right].

The time argument in Eq. (
2
) is TDB, and all variables on its right side are TDB-compatible.

w_{\mathrm{0M}}
is the gravitational potential of external bodies at the Moon’s center, given by

w_{\mathrm{0M}}=\sum\limits_{\mathrm{A\neq M}}\frac{GM_{\mathrm{A}}}{r_{\mathrm{MA}}}.

(3)

with the summation over all bodies A other than the Moon. The bodies considered here include the Sun, all planets, 343 main-belt asteroids, 30 Kuiper belt objects (KBO), and the KBO ring representing the rest of the Kuiper belt, which are the same as those considered in DE440
[
4
]
.

w_{l\mathrm{M}}
is the potential due to external oblate figures

w_{l\mathrm{M}}=-\frac{GM_{\mathrm{S}}J_{\mathrm{2S}}R^{2}_{\mathrm{S}}}{2r^{3}_{\mathrm{MS}}}(3\sin{\phi^{2}_{\mathrm{M,S}}}-1)-\frac{GM_{\mathrm{E}}J_{\mathrm{2E}}R^{2}_{\mathrm{E}}}{2r^{3}_{\mathrm{ME}}}(3\sin{\phi^{2}_{\mathrm{M,E}}}-1).

(4)

Here only the contribution from the Sun and the Earth are included.
\phi_{\mathrm{M,S}}
and
\phi_{\mathrm{M,E}}
are the latitudes of the Moon relative to the Sun’s equator and the Earth’s equator, respectively.
\boldsymbol{w}_{\mathrm{M}}
and
\Delta_{\mathrm{M}}
are defined as

\boldsymbol{w}_{\mathrm{M}}=\sum\limits_{\mathrm{A\neq M}}\frac{GM_{\mathrm{A}}\boldsymbol{v}_{\mathrm{A}}}{r_{\mathrm{MA}}},

(5)

and

\Delta_{\mathrm{M}}=\sum\limits_{\mathrm{A\neq M}}\frac{GM_{\mathrm{A}}}{r_{\mathrm{MA}}}\left\{\sum_{\mathrm{B}\neq\mathrm{A}}\frac{GM_{\mathrm{B}}}{r_{\mathrm{BA}}}-2v_{\mathrm{A}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\left(\boldsymbol{r}_{\mathrm{MA}}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}_{\mathrm{A}}\right)^{2}}{r_{\mathrm{MA}}^{2}}+\boldsymbol{r}_{\mathrm{MA}}\cdot\boldsymbol{a}_{\mathrm{A}}\right]\right\}.

(6)

with the summation over the Sun and all planets.

3.2
Numerical Method

We have numerically evaluated the time integral part of TCL-TDB given by Eq. (
2
).
The orbital data are obtained from the JPL DE440/DE441 ephemerides
[
4
]
.
The numerical integration is performed using the 10th-order Romberg integration algorithm, with a step size of 0.5 days.
We have fitted the result with 13th-degree Chebyshev polynomials described in
[
5
]
with 4-day granules.
To validate the precision of the numerical method, it is applied to calculate TT-TDB in DE440 and the results agree to within 1 picosecond.

To preserve maximum numerical precision, we fitted the secular drift and obtained the periodic part by subtracting the secular term.
The periodic part is stored in a BSP file and the coefficient of secular drift is stored in a TPC file. Thus, the calculation of TCL-TDB from LTE440 requires an extra addition of the secular term in comparison to TT-TDB from DE440.

(7)

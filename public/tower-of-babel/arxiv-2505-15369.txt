An adaptive proximal safeguarded augmented Lagrangian method for nonsmooth DC problems with convex constraints
Christian Kanzow, Tanja Neder

Abstract
A proximal safeguarded augmented Lagrangian method for minimizing the difference of convex (DC) functions over a nonempty, closed and convex set with additional linear equality as well as convex inequality constraints is presented. Thereby, all functions involved may be nonsmooth. Iterates (of the primal variable) are obtained by solving convex optimization problems as the concave part of the objective function gets approximated by an affine linearization. Under the assumption of a modified Slater constraint qualification, both convergence of the primal and dual variables to a generalized Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) point is proven, at least on a subsequence. Numerical experiments and comparison with existing solution methods are presented using some classes of constrained and nonsmooth DC problems.

Introduction

We consider a class of nonsmooth DC problems with convex constraints of the following type:

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x):=g(x)-h(x)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad Ax=b,\ c(x)\leq 0,\ x\in C

(P)

with
g,h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
convex,
A\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times n}
(with
p\leq n
),
b\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
c_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
convex for
i=1,\dots,m
and
\emptyset\neq C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
closed and convex, where for a vector
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
the relation
y\leq 0
here as well as in what follows is meant componentwise, that is

y\leq 0\quad:\Longleftrightarrow\quad y_{i}\leq 0\ \forall i=1,\dots,m.

As the objective function
f
has a representation as the difference of two convex functions, it is called a
DC function
, whereas
g
and
h
are referred to as
DC~components
. (These DC components are, of course, not unique.)

The increasing interest in DC optimization is due to the broad field of real world applications (see e.g.
[
35
,
23
]
and references therein for exemplary overviews). To put the focus on problems matching the above setting, let us mention questions emerging in logistics, in particular location planning
[
35
,
6
]
as well as production-transportation problems
[
15
]
, in chance-constrained management problems
[
9
]
, in designing communication systems with physical layer based security
[
28
]
, in compressed sensing
[
41
]
, in the packaging industry
[
16
]
and in machine learning, for example within feature selection in the context of support vector machines
[
20
]
.

Many methods for the solution of constrained DC programs impose additional assumptions on the DC objective function. In particular, it is frequently assumed that one of the DC components has to be continuously differentiable (see e.g.
[
1
]
or DCA2 in
[
21
]
) or even Lipschitz-differentiable (see e.g. LCDC-ALM in
[
17
]
), or that the second DC component has to be the pointwise maximum of finitely many continuously differentiable functions
[
28
,
29
,
26
]
. Extension of these approaches to general nonsmooth DC objective functions seems to be difficult.

Hence, we focus on constrained DC problems where the objective function is given as the difference of two arbitrary nonsmooth convex functions. To the best of our knowledge, there are only a few other approaches dealing with this general setting.

[
9
]
presents a proximal bundle method for minimizing a nonsmooth DC function over a general closed and convex set. Thereby, the key idea is not only to approximate the second DC component by a linearization, but also the first one by a cutting-plane model. No penalization of the constraints is carried out.
An approach for minimizing nonsmooth DC functions under linear equality constraints can be found in
[
17
]
by neglecting the smooth part of the first DC component while considering Composite LCDC-ALM. Then, further restrictions are implicitly embedded as the first DC component of the objective function is assumed to be proper, lower semicontinuous and convex with compact domain over which it is itself supposed to be Lipschitz-continuous. The crucial point of the method is, to replace once more in each iteration the concave part by a linearization before then considering the Moreau envelope of the resulting augmented Lagrangian function.
In
[
36
,
37
]
the authors introduce a method for minimizing a nonsmooth DC function over a closed and convex set which gets further restricted by (without loss of generality one single) DC-type inequality. Adopting the idea of the classical DC Algorithm (DCA,
[
22
]
), one linearizes in each iteration the second DC component in both, the objective function and the constraint. The resulting subproblems though need not to be solved exactly. Instead, computing a point of the modified feasible set which provides a sufficient decrease in the model function is adequate. However, a feasible point is required for initialization in order to avoid infeasibility of the subproblems which might otherwise occur due to relaxing the DC type constraint. In case one omits the latter constraint, the method boils down to (a proximal version of) DCA
[
22
]
but with the modification of allowing for inexact solutions of the subproblems.
Also in
[
21
]
, DC programs with DC constraints that get complemented by a closed and convex set are considered. Referring to DCA1, penalizing the DC constraints suitably carries over the DC structure to the resulting penalty problems. This again allows to apply the strategy of the classical DCA, that is to approximate within each subproblem the concave part of the objective function by an affine majorization. In addition, the penalty parameter gets updated in each iteration. Although the problem setting at the beginning is kept of a general nature, the convergence theory of DCA1 requires, in particular, differentiability of one of the DC components of the penalized objective function.
Dealing with constrained DC problems, one can, of course, always apply the classical DCA
[
22
]
itself by formally rewriting the problem as an unconstrained one, which, however, is equivalent to carrying over the constraints into the subproblems.

Notwithstanding the method, keeping the constraints in the subproblems might make them hard to solve, depending on the application.
Hence, our aim has been to develop an algorithm for constrained DC optimization which first, is able to handle a general nonsmooth DC objective function and second, allows for augmentation of the probably most considered convex constraints, namely linear equality as well as convex inequality restrictions. For the last mentioned ones, also no smoothness assumptions are imposed. In addition, we wanted to provide the possibility of keeping somewhat easy constraints, for example box constraints, in the subproblems. Thus, we add an abstract constraint to our problem formulation. Moreover, we adopt the idea of the classical DCA
[
22
]
to approximate the concave part of the objective function in each iteration by an affine majorization. Combining this approach with the basic concept of safeguarded augmented Lagrangian methods, which has some advantages against the classical augmented Lagrangian method (see
[
18
]
for a discussion), we yield convex subproblems, being, in general, simpler to solve than DC problems. Note that quite many applications (with convex feasible region) involve only linear equality and convex inequality constraints but no further abstract ones. In that case we even may obtain unconstrained convex subproblems. (Though, there is still the possibility of keeping such constraints explicitly whenever they are easy to handle.)

The work is organized as follows. In
Section

2
we first recall some basic concepts and results from nonsmooth convex analysis which will come into play when examining the properties of the subproblems occurring in our new algorithm before deriving some optimality conditions for the DC problem under consideration. The new solution method itself gets then introduced and analyzed in
Section

3
. We conduct in
Section

4
some numerical experiments that compare the performance of the new algorithm to established solution methods by means of some applications stemming from location planning as well as compressed sensing. Afterwards, we close with some final remarks in
Section

5
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section we first recall some basic definitions and results from nonsmooth convex analysis before turning to the derivation of some optimality conditions for our problem under consideration.

2.1
Basics from nonsmooth convex analysis

In the following, we provide essential definitions and results which will be exploited later. For further reference, one can have a look, for example, at
[

An extended-valued function
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
with
\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
is said to be
proper
if its
domain

\dom(\phi):=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \middle|\ \phi(x)<\infty}}\right\}
is nonempty. It is referred to as
lower semicontinuous (lsc)
if

\liminf_{x\to\overline{x}}\phi(x)\geq\phi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\overline{x}}}\right)

is satisfied for every
\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Furthermore, a proper function is called
convex
whenever

\phi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y}}\right)\leq\lambda\phi(x)+(1-\lambda)\phi(y)

holds for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
, and
strongly convex
if there exists some
\mu>0
such that
\phi-\frac{\mu}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}
is convex. Here and throughout the paper, we denote by
\|\cdot\|
the Euclidean norm. Real-valued strongly convex functions attain an unique minimum on a nonempty, closed and convex set. Furthermore, a proper convex function
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is directionally differentiable at any point
x\in\operatorname{int}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\dom(\phi)}}\right)
(with
\operatorname{int}
denoting the interior of a set) and in each direction
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the corresponding
directional derivative
given by

\phi^{\prime}(x;d):=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{\phi(x+td)-\phi(x)}{t}

(see e.g. Theorem 2.76 in
[
10
]
).
Considering nonsmooth convex functions, we resort to the
convex subdifferential
as a concept of generalized differentiation which, at a point
x\in\dom(\phi)
, is given by the set

\partial\phi(x):=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{s\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \middle|\ \phi(y)\geq\phi(x)+s^{T}(y-x)\ \forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\right\}.

For every
x\in\operatorname{int}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\dom(\phi)}}\right)
, the set
\partial\phi(x)
is nonempty, convex and compact. Moreover, the directional derivative of such
\phi
relates to the convex subdifferential via

\phi^{\prime}(x;d)=\max_{s\in\partial\phi(x)}s^{T}d

(1)

for each
x\in\operatorname{int}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\dom(\phi)}}\right)
and
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see Theorem 23.4 in
[
31
]
).
Besides, let us mention the following calculus rules for convex subdifferentials which will be used during our analysis.

Theorem 2.1

(calculus rules for the convex subdifferential)
.

Let
\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{r}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}>0

Suppose that either

\operatorname{ri}
denotes the relative interior of a set, or there exists some
\widetilde{x}\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}\dom\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\phi_{i}}}\right)

. Then

\partial\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\phi_{i}}}\right)(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\partial\phi_{i}(x)

N_{C}(x):=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{s\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \middle|\ s^{T}(y-x)\leq 0\ \forall y\in C}}\right\}.

Moreover, for convex

N_{C}(x)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(T_{C}(x)}}\right)^{\circ}\quad\text{and}\quad T_{C}(x)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(N_{C}(x)}}\right)^{\circ}\quad\forall x\in C,

(3)

K^{\circ}:=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \middle|\ y^{T}x\leq 0\ \forall x\in K}}\right\}.

In addition, for convex

N_{C}(x)=\partial\delta_{C}(x)

(4)

, where
\linebreak\delta_{C}:\nolinebreak\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\nolinebreak\overline{\mathbb{R}}
denotes the

This relation together with the closedness of the graph of the convex subdifferential reveals that for closed

N_{C}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\overline{x}}}\right)=\Limsup_{x\xrightarrow{C}\overline{x}}N_{C}(x)

Finally, consider the optimization problem

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi(x)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad x\in C

with a convex function
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}

(optimality condition for convex optimization, cf. Theorem 3.1 in
[

]
)
.

A point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a solution to the convex optimization problem (

0\in\partial\phi(x)+N_{C}(x).

Let us close with stating a separation theorem being an important tool for proving equivalence of certain constraint qualifications later on.

Theorem 2.4

(separation theorem, cf. Theorem 2.26(ii) in
[
10
]
)
.

Let
C_{1},C_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be nonempty, convex and disjoint sets. Then there exists some
\linebreak a\in\nolinebreak\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\nolinebreak\{0\}
with

a^{T}x_{1}\leq a^{T}x_{2}\qquad\forall x_{1}\in C_{1},\ x_{2}\in C_{2}.

2.2
Optimality conditions

Here, we derive necessary optimality conditions for problem (
P
) following primarily
[
28
]
and then extending the approach of
[
10
]
.
To this end, let us write

\ell:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{p},\qquad\ell(x):=Ax-b

(7)

for the equality constraints of (
P
), and

X:=C\cap c^{-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\{(-\infty,0]^{m}\}}}\right)\cap\ell^{-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\{0\}}}\right)

(8)

for the feasible set of (
P
).

Now, assume
x^{\ast}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
to be an arbitrary local minimum to (
P
). It is well known that
x^{\ast}
satisfies

f^{\prime}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast};d}}\right)\geq 0\qquad\forall d\in T_{X}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right)

or, equivalently, by exploiting convexity of the feasible set
X
,

f^{\prime}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast};x-x^{\ast}}}\right)\geq 0\qquad\forall x\in X

(see e.g.
[
8
]
).
Any point meeting this last condition is called a directional stationary point or, for short,
d-stationary point
of (
P
).
Taking into account the DC structure of the objective function, d-stationarity can be rewritten as

g^{\prime}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast};x-x^{\ast}}}\right)\geq h^{\prime}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast};x-x^{\ast}}}\right)\qquad\forall x\in X.

Now, utilizing the relation (
1
) leads to

g^{\prime}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast};x-x^{\ast}}}\right)\geq s^{T}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x-x^{\ast}}}\right)\qquad\forall x\in X,\ s\in\partial h\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right).

Since d-stationarity is both necessary and sufficient for optimality in the convex setting (see e.g. again
[
8
]
), it follows

x^{\ast}\in\argmin_{x\in X}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{g(x)-s^{T}x}}\right\}\qquad\forall s\in\partial h\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right)

which, in turn, due to the optimality condition from
Theorem

2.3
, is equivalent to

0\in\partial g\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right)-\{s\}+N_{X}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right)\qquad\forall s\in\partial h\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right)

proving

\partial h\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right)\subseteq\partial g\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right)+N_{X}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right).

(9)

Note that the above considerations show that the concept of a d-stationary point is equivalent to the inclusion (
9
). However, in practice it is usually quite difficult to verify d-stationarity. Hence, one often resorts to the weaker notion of a
critical point
of (
P
), that is a feasible point satisfying

\partial h\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right)\cap\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\partial g\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right)+N_{X}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right)}}\right)\neq\emptyset.

(10)

Clearly, with the above explanations in mind, this still poses a necessary optimality condition for (
P
).
In order to obtain a suitable expression for the normal cone
N_{X}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right)
which exploits the special structure of the convex set
X
, one can apply the following Slater-type constraint qualification.

Definition 2.5

(StCQ)
.

We say that the
Slater-type Constraint Qualification
(StCQ) holds for (
P
) whenever there exists some
\widetilde{x}\in\operatorname{ri}(C)
such that
c_{i}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\widetilde{x}}}\right)<0
holds for all
i=1,\dots,m
as well as
A\widetilde{x}=b
.

With this constraint qualification at hand one can derive the following alternative optimality condition.

Proposition 2.6
.

Assume that StCQ is satisfied for (
P
). Then
x^{\ast}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a critical point of (
P
) if and only if there exist
\lambda^{\ast}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\ \mu^{\ast}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
such that

\displaystyle\partial h\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right)\cap\bigg(\partial g\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right)+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{A^{T}\mu^{\ast}}}\right\}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda^{\ast}_{i}\partial c_{i}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right)+N_{C}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right)\bigg)\neq\emptyset,

(11a)

\displaystyle c_{i}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right)\leq 0,\quad\lambda_{i}^{\ast}\geq 0,\quad\lambda^{\ast}_{i}c_{i}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right)=0\quad\forall i=1,\dots,m,

(11b)

\displaystyle Ax^{\ast}=b,\quad x^{\ast}\in C.

(11c)

Proof 2.7
.

This immediately follows from the definition of a critical point, (
10
), as under StCQ it holds for all feasible points
x\in X

N_{X}(x)=N_{c^{-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{(-\infty,0]^{m}}}\right\}}}\right)}(x)+N_{\ell^{-1}\{0\}}(x)+N_{C}(x)

with

\displaystyle N_{c^{-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{(-\infty,0]^{m}}}\right\}}}\right)}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\omega_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \middle|\ \omega_{i}\in\partial c_{i}(x),\ \lambda_{i}\geq 0,\ \lambda_{i}c_{i}(x)=0,\ i=1,\dots,m}}\right\},

\displaystyle N_{\ell^{-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\{0\}}}\right)}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{A^{T}\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \middle|\ \mu\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}}\right\}

(see Proposition 3.3 and Theorem 3.12 in
[
10
]
).

Any triple
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast},\lambda^{\ast},\mu^{\ast}}}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}
meeting the conditions (
11
) is called a
generalized Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) point
of (
P
). Thereby, the requirement
\lambda_{i}^{\ast}c_{i}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right)=0
for all
i=1,\dots,m
is referred to as
complementary slackness
. Further, observe that (

) can be equivalently expressed as
\min\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{-c\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right),\lambda^{\ast}}}\right\}=0
, where here and in the following
\min\{y,z\}
for two vectors

(and the analog holds true for
\max\{y,z\}

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\min\{y,z\}}}\right)_{i}:=\min\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{y_{i},z_{i}}}\right\}\qquad i=1,\dots,m.

\lambda^{\ast}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\ \mu^{\ast}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
with

\displaystyle 0\in\partial_{C}f\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right)+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{A^{T}\mu^{\ast}}}\right\}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{\ast}\partial c_{i}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right)+N_{C}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right),

\displaystyle c_{i}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right)\leq 0,\quad\lambda_{i}^{\ast}\geq 0,\quad\lambda^{\ast}_{i}c_{i}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right)=0\quad\forall i=1,\dots,m,

\displaystyle Ax^{\ast}=b,\quad x^{\ast}\in C

where
\partial_{C}f\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{\ast}}}\right)
denotes again the Clarke subdifferential
[
7
,
8
]
of the objective function
f
at
x^{\ast}
. Looking into the Clarke subdifferential it is apparent that the notion of a KKT point given by
[
21
,
30
]
is stronger than the one used in this work. Actually, this comes from exploiting the DC structure and explains our notion of a
generalized
KKT point.

3
The algorithm and its convergence properties

Our approach is based on the classical safeguarded augmented Lagrangian method (see e.g.
[
18
,
5
,
4
]
). But instead of applying the technique directly to our problem (
P
), we first replace the concave part
-h
of the objective function by a linearization.
This way, we avoid a DC structure in our subproblems but obtain convex optimization problems instead, which, in general, reduces the computational effort of solving the subproblems.
In order to approximate
-h
, we follow the classical DC Algorithm
[
22
]
from unconstrained DC optimization which means, at iteration
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with current iterate
x^{k}
, we pick a subgradient
s^{k}\in\partial h\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k}}}\right)
and replace
-h
by its affine majorant

x\mapsto-h\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k}}}\right)-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(s^{k}}}\right)^{T}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x-x^{k}}}\right).

In addition, we only penalize the equality and inequality constraints in (
P
), while keeping the abstract constraint
x\in C
explicitly in our subproblems. Hence, after approximating the objective function and augmenting only the equality and inequality constraints, the augmented Lagrangian function in each iteration
k
reads

\begin{multlined}L_{\rho}^{(k)}(x,\lambda,\mu):=g(x)-h\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k}}}\right)-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(s^{k}}}\right)^{T}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x-x^{k}}}\right)+\mu^{T}(Ax-b)+\frac{\rho}{2}\|Ax-b\|^{2}\\
+\frac{1}{2\rho}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\|\max\{0,\lambda+\rho c(x)\}\|^{2}-\|\lambda\|^{2}}}\right),\end{multlined}

which means the augmented Lagrangian function changes from iteration to iteration. Hence, we call this an adaptive (safeguarded) augmented Lagrangian approach.
Adding a proximal term in the subproblems results in the following proximal safeguarded augmented Lagrangian method for DC problems, psALMDC for short.

Algorithm 1
.

(psALMDC)

Let
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{Q_{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a sequence of symmetric positive definite matrices satisfying
\linebreak\underline{\lambda}\leq\nolinebreak\lambda_{\min}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Q_{k}}}\right)\leq\lambda_{\max}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Q_{k}}}\right)\leq\overline{\lambda}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and some
\underline{\lambda},\overline{\lambda}>0
.
Choose
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{0},v^{0},u^{0}}}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\nolinebreak\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+},\linebreak\ \sigma_{0},\epsilon_{0}>0,\ M,N\in\mathbb{N}\ (N<\nolinebreak M),\ \alpha,\beta,\gamma,\theta\in(0,1),\linebreak\ \overline{\eta}>\nolinebreak 1
. Set
\rho_{0}:=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sigma_{0}}}\right)^{\gamma},\ K:=I_{1}:=0,\ k:=0
.

Select
s^{k}\in\partial h\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k}}}\right)
. Compute

x^{k+1}=\argmin_{x\in C}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{L_{\rho_{k}}^{(k)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x,u^{k},v^{k}}}\right)+\frac{\sigma_{k}}{2}\|x-x^{k}\|_{Q_{k}}^{2}}}\right\}.

Set

\displaystyle\mu^{k+1}

\displaystyle:=v^{k}+\rho_{k}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Ax^{k+1}-b}}\right),

\displaystyle\lambda^{k+1}

\displaystyle:=\max\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{0,u^{k}+\rho_{k}c\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)}}\right\}.

IF
\sigma_{k}Q_{k}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}-x^{k}}}\right)=0
AND
Ax^{k+1}=b
AND
\min\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{-c\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right),\lambda^{k+1}}}\right\}=0
: STOP.

(i)

IF
\|Ax^{k+1}-b\|\leq\theta\|Ax^{k}-b\|
AND

\Big\|\min\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{-c\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right),\ \frac{u^{k}}{\rho_{k}}}}\right\}\Big\|\leq\theta\Big\|\min\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{-c\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k}}}\right),\ \frac{u^{k-1}}{\rho_{k-1}}}}\right\}\Big\|

set

\sigma_{k+1}:=\sigma_{k},\ \rho_{k+1}:=\rho_{k},\ \epsilon_{k+1}:=\epsilon_{k},

(ii)

ELSE set

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\overline{\eta}&\text{if }\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{\alpha}\geq\epsilon_{k},\\
1&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\sigma_{k+1}

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\overline{\eta}\sigma_{k}&\text{if }x^{k+1}=x^{k},\\
\max\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\frac{1}{\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{\alpha}},\eta\sigma_{k}}}\right\}&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\rho_{k+1}

\displaystyle:=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sigma_{k+1}}}\right)^{\gamma}.

IF

\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{\alpha}<\epsilon_{k}

K\leftarrow K+1.

IF
\sigma_{k+1}=\frac{1}{\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|^{\alpha}}

I_{1}\leftarrow I_{1}+1.

IF
K\geq M
AND
I_{1}\geq N
set

\epsilon_{k+1}:=\beta\epsilon_{k}
,

K\leftarrow 0
,

I_{1}\leftarrow 0
,

ELSE set

\epsilon_{k+1}:=\epsilon_{k}.

Set

\displaystyle v^{k+1}

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}v^{k}&\text{if }Ax^{k+1}=b,\\
\frac{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(v^{k}}}\right)^{T}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Ax^{k+1}-b}}\right)}{\|Ax^{k+1}-b\|^{2}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Ax^{k+1}-b}}\right)&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle:=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{i\in\{1,\dots,m\}\ \middle|\ c_{i}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)>0}}\right\},

\displaystyle u_{i}^{k+1}

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\frac{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u_{I}^{k}}}\right)^{T}c_{I}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)}{\|c_{I}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)\|^{2}}c_{i}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)&\text{if }i\in I,\\
u_{i}^{k}&\text{if }i\in\{1,\dots,m\}\setminus I.\end{cases}

Set
k\leftarrow k+1
, and go to
1
.

Before dedicating ourselves to the convergence theory, let us give some more insights in the motivation of the algorithm. First, choosing the next iterate
x^{k+1}
in
1
as a minimizer of the modified augmented Lagrangian function
L_{\rho_{k}}^{(k)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\cdot,u^{k},v^{k}}}\right)
over the convex set
C
without the proximal term and taking into account the necessary optimality condition leads to

\displaystyle s^{k}

\displaystyle\in\partial g\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{A^{T}v^{k}+\rho_{k}A^{T}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Ax^{k+1}-b}}\right)}}\right\}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\max\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{0,u_{i}^{k}+\rho_{k}c_{i}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)}}\right\}\partial c_{i}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)+N_{C}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)

(12)

\displaystyle=\partial g\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{A^{T}\mu^{k+1}}}\right\}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{k+1}\partial c_{i}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)+N_{C}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)

with
s^{k}\in\partial h\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k}}}\right)
by our choice. Hence, as we aim to determine a generalized KKT point (
11
), we would like to have
x^{k+1}=x^{k}
(at least approximately) in the end. To yield the latter (asymptotically), it is a common technique to add a proximal term. This also ensures existence and uniqueness of the solution of each subproblem as the objective function is strongly convex and the minimization is carried out with respect to a convex set. Using
\|\cdot\|_{Q_{k}}
within the proximal term instead of the Euclidean norm appears to be advantageous in some numerical experiments in order to relativize the effect that the proximal parameter might grow rapidly.
Note that the conditions imposed in
1
on the sequence
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{Q_{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of symmetric positive definite matrices correspond to
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{Q_{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{Q_{k}^{-1}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
being bounded.

In order to establish the convergence theory of the classical safeguarded augmented Lagrangian method
[
4
]
, it is crucial that the sequence of penalty parameters
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\rho_{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
stays bounded only if there is sufficient progress in feasibility and complementarity, whereas otherwise
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\rho_{k}}}\right\}_{k_{\in}\mathbb{N}_{0}}
grows to infinity.
It turns out that for our algorithm this property is not only required for the sequence of penalty parameters
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\rho_{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, but also for that of proximal parameters
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\sigma_{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. Having in mind the previous paragraph with the optimality condition obtained from
1
given by

s^{k}-\sigma_{k}Q_{k}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}-x^{k}}}\right)\in\partial g\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{A^{T}\mu^{k+1}}}\right\}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{k+1}\partial c_{i}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)+N_{C}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)

(13)

one would like to have
\sigma_{k}Q_{k}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}-x^{k}}}\right)\approx 0
in the end, conferring (
12
).
Consequently, one has to ensure that the sequence
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\sigma_{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of proximal parameters does not grow too fast. An adaptive update depending on the decay of
\|x^{k+1}-x^{k}\|
seems reasonable.
Moreover, the sequence of penalty parameters
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\rho_{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
has to grow slower than the one of proximal parameters
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\sigma_{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, which suggests the update formula for
\rho_{k+1}
in
(ii)
.
Note that in
(ii)
both the parameter
\eta
and the counters
K
and
I_{1}
depend on the iteration index
k
, but, to simplify the notation, we suppress this dependence in our notation. We stress that these counters play a central role for a careful update of
\epsilon_{k}
, and they will essentially be used in the proof of
Lemma

3.7
only.

For the classical safeguarded augmented Lagrangian method
[
4
]
, the only requirement imposed on the auxiliary sequences
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{v^{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p}
and
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{u^{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
for the construction of Lagrange multipliers is their boundedness.
In our context, however, the boundedness of these multipliers is not enough, and we have to choose the corresponding sequences in a particular way. More precisely, we require the sequences to satisfy the two inequalities
(see the proof of
Lemma

3.5
)

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(v^{k+1}-v^{k}}}\right)^{T}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Ax^{k+1}-b}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 0,

(14)

\displaystyle\Big\|\max\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{0,u^{k+1}+\rho_{k+1}c\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)}}\right\}\Big\|^{2}-\|u^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\max\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{0,u^{k}+\rho_{k+1}c\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)}}\right\}\Big\|^{2}-\|u^{k}\|^{2}

(15)

for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
To motivate the precise update formula for
v^{k+1}
in
1
, we stick with the previous estimate for the Lagrange multiplier whenever the corresponding iterate
x^{k+1}
already satisfies the equality constraints. Otherwise the equality constraints are violated, which means
Ax^{k+1}-b\neq 0
, and then our update formula results from considering the optimization problem

\min_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\frac{1}{2}\|v\|^{2}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(v-v^{k}}}\right)^{T}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Ax^{k+1}-b}}\right)=0

(16)

for determining
v^{k+1}
.
This problem can be solved analytically and leads to the update of
v^{k}
from
1
. Note that
v^{k}
is also feasible for (
16
), so we immediately obtain

\|v^{k+1}\|\leq\|v^{k}\|

(17)

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
This ensures the boundedness of the sequence
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{v^{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. Let us note that in the optimization problem (
16
) we place equality constraints though one might expect inequality constraints due to the requirement (
14
). The reason for this is that considering inequality constraints would change the unique solution of the optimization problem to
v^{k+1}=0
whenever one has
-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(v^{k}}}\right)^{T}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Ax^{k+1}-b}}\right)\leq 0
. As a consequence, also all subsequent members of the sequence would be equal the zero vector which, in general, does not seem to yield a good estimate for the Lagrange multiplier of the generalized KKT point.

Our construction of the auxiliary sequence
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{u^{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is mainly motivated by the construction of
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{v^{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and results in a non-negative sequence meeting the requirement (
15
) with equality and, in addition,

\|u^{k+1}\|\leq\|u^{k}\|

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, ensuring once again the boundedness of
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{u^{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.

Let us note that, due to the monotonicity of the sequences
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\|v^{k}\|}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\|u^{k}\|}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
,
Algorithm

1
breaks down to a pure penalty method whenever we pick
v^{0}:=u^{0}:=0
.

With our considerations regarding the proximal term we have already anticipated in parts the motivation of the termination criterion in
1
. Nevertheless, let us go through the reasoning with the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
Algorithm

1
stops at some iteration
k
. Then, the triple
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1},\lambda^{k+1},\mu^{k+1}}}\right)
is a generalized KKT point of (
P
).

Proof 3.2
.

Note that at the point of termination one has
x^{k+1}=x^{k}
and hence,
s^{k}\in\partial h\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)
holds. Thus, the optimality condition (
13
) implies

\partial h\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)\cap\bigg(\partial g\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{A^{T}\mu^{k+1}}}\right\}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{k+1}\partial c_{i}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)+N_{C}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)\bigg)\neq\emptyset.

Furthermore,
\min\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{-c\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right),\lambda^{k+1}}}\right\}=0
is equivalent to

c\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)\leq 0,\quad\lambda^{k+1}\geq 0,\quad\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\lambda^{k+1}}}\right)^{T}c\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)=0.

By construction,
x^{k+1}\in C
holds for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and satisfaction of the equality constraints is guaranteed by the termination criterion. Altogether, this proves that the triple
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1},\lambda^{k+1},\mu^{k+1}}}\right)
is indeed a generalized KKT point of (
P
).

Theoretically, one could replace the first termination condition in
1
with the equivalent postulation
x^{k+1}=x^{k}
. But for practical implementation, where one checks for satisfaction of the stopping criterion up to some tolerance, it will definitely make a difference whether to take the factor
\sigma_{k}Q_{k}
into account or not, especially as
\sigma_{k}
might grow significantly. Hence, keeping in mind the optimality condition (
13
), it seems advisable to numerically incorporate the additional factor. Thus, we stick with this formulation also for our theoretical considerations.

In the subsequent convergence theory, we assume without loss of generality that infinite sequences
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k},s^{k},\sigma_{k},\rho_{k},\epsilon_{k},u^{k},v^{k}}}\right)}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\lambda^{k},\mu^{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
are generated by
Algorithm

1
.

In order to establish our final convergence result, some preliminary lemmata are proven initially. The first result regarding the auxiliary sequences
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{v^{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}},\ \mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{u^{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
for the construction of Lagrange multipliers summarizes our previous explanations to motivate their update formulae.

Lemma 3.3
.

The sequences
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{v^{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{p},\ \mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{u^{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}_{+}
are bounded and satisfy (
14
) and (
15
), respectively (even with equality).

Proof 3.4
.

To begin with the sequence
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{v^{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, in case
Ax^{k+1}=b
holds for some
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
the update formula in
1
reads
v^{k+1}=v^{k}
. Hence, on one hand, (
14
) is satisfied trivially with equality and, on the other hand, the same prevails for (
17
). Thus, consider the case
Ax^{k+1}\neq b
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then,
v^{k+1}
gets determined as solution to problem (
16
). Obviously, the constraint ensures that (
14
) holds with equality. Besides, like already pointed out, feasibility of
v^{k}
to problem (
16
) yields anew (
17
). Altogether, the whole sequence
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\|v^{k}\|}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
to be monotonically decreasing has been proven, which reveals boundedness of
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{v^{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.

Turning to
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{u^{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, we first note that the sequence being (componentwise) non-negative follows inductively from the update formula in
1
by the choice of the index set
I
.
Next, recognize that for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
one has
u_{i}^{k+1}+\rho_{k+1}c_{i}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)>\nolinebreak 0
if and only if
u_{i}^{k}+\rho_{k+1}c_{i}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)>0
as the sequence
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{u^{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is (componentwise) non-negative and the update formula in
1
reveals that
u_{i}^{k+1}\neq u_{i}^{k}
occurs only if
c_{i}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)>\nolinebreak 0
.
Hence, introducing

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle:=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{i\in\{1,\dots,m\}\ \middle|\ u_{i}^{k}+\rho_{k+1}c_{i}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)>0}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{i\in\{1,\dots,m\}\ \middle|\ u_{i}^{k+1}+\rho_{k+1}c_{i}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)>0}}\right\},

one obtains

\Big\|\max\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{0,u^{k+1}+\rho_{k+1}c\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)}}\right\}\Big\|^{2}-\|u^{k+1}\|^{2}=\|u_{J}^{k+1}\|^{2}+2\rho_{k+1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u_{J}^{k+1}}}\right)^{T}c_{J}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)+\rho_{k+1}^{2}\|c_{J}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)\|^{2}-\|u^{k+1}\|^{2}

as well as

\Big\|\max\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{0,u^{k}+\rho_{k+1}c\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)}}\right\}\Big\|^{2}-\|u^{k}\|^{2}=\|u_{J}^{k}\|^{2}+2\rho_{k+1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u_{J}^{k}}}\right)^{T}c_{J}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)+\rho_{k+1}^{2}\|c_{J}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)\|^{2}-\|u^{k}\|^{2}.

Therefore, to show that (
15
) holds with equality it apparently suffices to prove

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u_{J}^{k+1}}}\right)^{T}c_{J}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u_{J}^{k}}}\right)^{T}c_{J}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)

(18)

together with

\|u_{J}^{k+1}\|^{2}-\|u^{k+1}\|^{2}=\|u_{J}^{k}\|^{2}-\|u^{k}\|^{2}.

(19)

To begin with (
18
), the update formula in
1
gives primarily
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u_{I}^{k+1}}}\right)^{T}c_{I}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u_{I}^{k}}}\right)^{T}c_{I}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)
. However, observing that
I\subseteq J
and
u_{i}^{k+1}=u_{i}^{k}
for
i\in J\setminus I
, equation (
18
) follows.
Denoting for a subset
S\subseteq\{1,\dots,m\}
with
\neg S
its respective complement, that is
\neg S:=\{1,\dots,m\}\setminus S
, equation (
19
) is equivalent to
\|u_{\neg J}^{k+1}\|^{2}=\|u_{\neg J}^{k}\|^{2}
. But, since
\neg J\subseteq\neg I
and
u_{i}^{k+1}=u_{i}^{k}
for all
i\in\neg I
, the latter clearly holds.

Verifying boundedness of the sequence
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{u^{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
gets once more done by establishing monotonicity of the sequence
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\|u^{k}\|}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.
To this end, recognize that the update formula in
1
together with the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\displaystyle\|u^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|u_{I}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\|u_{\neg I}^{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(u_{I}^{k}}}\right)^{T}c_{I}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)}}\right|}{\|c_{I}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)\|^{2}}}}\right)^{2}\|c_{I}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x^{k+1}}}\right)\|^{2}+\|u_{\neg I}^{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{I}^{k}\|^{2}+\|u_{\neg I}^{k}\|^{2}=\|u^{k}\|^{2}.

In order to prove our final convergence result, we do not require the specific expressions of the auxiliary sequences
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{v^{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}},\ \mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{u^{k}}}\right\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
given in
1
. Instead, only the two properties (
14
) and (
15
) will be exploited. Hence, other choices differing from the presented one are possible, too.
In particular, theoretically in each case also any constant sequence would serve the purpose. However, it is known from the classical safeguarded augmented Lagrangian method
[
4
]
that in practice choosing
u^{k}
and
v^{k}
as preferably good approximations to the conventional Lagrange multiplier updates of
\lambda^{k}
and
\mu^{k}
, respectively, has proven one’s worth. Consequently, adaptive choices seem to be more advantageous. With the just said at first glance it might be more promising than our hitherto existing approach to choose
u^{k}

Whenever the penalty parameter goes to infinity in the context of safeguarded augmented Lagrangian methods, it is quite usual to require a suitable constraint qualification in order to ensure boundedness of the Lagrange multipliers. So does the presented method. Hence, in the following we introduce three qualification conditions for problem (
P
) which turn out to be equivalent in our setting.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
\widehat{x}
denote a feasible point of (
P
).

(i)

We say that the

(mSCQ) holds for (

•

•

and

•

,

where

is defined by (

Such a point
\widetilde{x}
is then called a

.

(ii)

The point

is said to satisfy the

(mEMFCQ) whenever

•

and

•

there exists

Thereby,

the active index set of

(iii)

(NNAMCQ) holds at

if there is no nonzero vector

satisfying

(24)

Note that the two notions of Slater-type constraint qualifications used in this work (see

and

Adding the condition

to the usual SCQ and EMFCQ, respectively, is required to obtain equivalence with NNAMCQ. Indeed, neglecting the said stipulation one cannot deduce that NNAMCQ is satisfied at some point

for which EMFCQ (in the classical sense) holds or whenever SCQ (in the classical sense) is satisfied (whereas the corresponding reverse implications still hold true). This is illustrated by

3.10
.
For common definitions of SCQ and EMFCQ see, for example,
[

]
.
Modifying SCQ in the proposed way has already been done in
[

,
42
]
in the context of vector optimization. For the sake of completeness, let us note that in
[

the author points out that in a common non-convex and nonsmooth setting, NNAMCQ is in general weaker than EMFCQ whereas in the case of the functions involved being smooth (but still possibly non-convex) and abstract constraints being absent (i.e.

) both constraint qualifications coincide. Moreover, it is well known that whenever considering convex feasible sets, the notions of SCQ and EMFCQ are equivalent (see e.g.
[

In the end, let us note that, technically speaking, we do not just add the condition

to EMFCQ but replace the usual condition of the matrix

having full rank

by the proposed one. However, it is clear that the latter assumption gets implied by the prior one. Hence, mEMFCQ is indeed a stronger constraint qualification than the usual EMFCQ.

4.1
Location planning

The consideration of how to place certain facilities to serve the demand of participants whose locations are known emerges at many problems. While developing a wider area settlement the question may arise of how to place supply facilities for daily needs, like supermarkets, health care offices, educational institutions or department stores, in such a way that the sum of the most favorable distances from the residents’ homes to the respective facilities or the resulting transportation costs gets minimized. Thereby, optimization can be carried out for example with respect to travel distance, time or total costs.
The mathematical formulation leads to the following generalized or multisource Weber’s problem

Sparse signal recovery

A major task in compressed sensing consists in retrieving a signal
\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
from a measurement
b:=A\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
m\ll n
, where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
denotes the sensing (measurement) matrix. In order to save storage space and perhaps computation time for further processing, there is a particular interest in finding a sparse representation of the signal, that is a solution
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
of the linear equation
Ax=b
with
x
being as sparse as possible.
This task results in finding a solution of the optimization problem

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\|x\|_{0}\qquad\text{s.t.}\qquad Ax=b,

(38)

where
\|\cdot\|_{0}
denotes the
\ell_{0}
-norm that counts the number of nonzero entries of a vector. However, non-convexity together with discontinuity of the
\ell_{0}
-norm causes severe difficulties (see
[
13
]
for more details). Hence, one switches to alternative formulations which still allow for sparsity of solutions. Subsequently, we present two different approaches, one following
[
41
]
and involving constrained
\ell_{1-2}
minimization, the other resulting from
[

5
Concluding remarks

In this work a proximal safeguarded augmented Lagrangian method for minimizing nonsmooth DC functions over a convex feasible region defined by a combination of linear equality as well as convex inequality constraints and an abstract constraint, called psALMDC, was introduced. Contrary to many existing algorithms, the new method provides both, handling of a general nonsmooth DC objective function and augmentation of the (in)equality constraints, which are probably the most common restrictions in applications. Combining the basic concepts of the classical DC Algorithm (DCA,
[
22
]
), that is substituting the concave part of the objective function by an affine majorization, and the safeguarded augmented Lagrangian method
[
4
]
, the subproblems to be solved in each iteration are convex ones. Thereby, constraints occur within those only if there is indeed an abstract one in the overall problem, which is rare in applications, or if someone intends to keep simple (in)equality constraints, such as box constraints, explicitly. Besides, the overall method includes an implementable termination criterion.

Assuming boundedness of the (primal) iterates, which is argued not to be too restrictive, and supposing a modified Slater constraint qualification to be satisfied for the problem under consideration, the existence of a convergent subsequence of iterates, both in terms of primal as well as Lagrangian dual variables, is proven. The corresponding limit point is shown to be a generalized KKT point of the regarded problem.

Numerical experiments were conducted in order to judge the practical performance of the new algorithm against existing solution methods. For the purpose of comparison, the classical DCA
[
22
]
next to the proximal bundle method for nonsmooth DC programming (PBMDC,
[
9
]
) were taken into account. As applications served the 50-Customer Problems from
[
6
,
11
]
and the recovery of sparse signals
[
41
,
13
]
.

For the 50-Customer Problems our new method psALMDC shows the overall best performance in terms of success in determining the optimum and in the majority of cases also with regard to CPU time.
However, while considering reconstruction of sparse signals the classical DCA is the method to clearly outperform both psALMDC and PBMDC. Nevertheless, the latter two algorithms yield satisfactory success rates, too, which in terms of psALMDC approach the ones of DCA for most of the instances.

So far, hardly any algorithms for tackling constrained DC optimization problems are known which provide, in the limit, points meeting requirements beyond criticality, like for example d-stationarity. The few that exist require additional assumptions on the structure of the DC components. Hence, our future research is dedicated to the development of an algorithm for constrained, nonsmooth DC optimization which gets along without such additional postulations but despite provides stronger convergence results.

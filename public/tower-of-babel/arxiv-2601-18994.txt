Asymptotic number of edge-colored regular graphs
Michael Borinsky and Chiara Meroni and Maximilian Wiesmann

Abstract
. We prove a formula for the asymptotic number of edge-colored regular graphs with a prescribed set of allowed vertex-incidence structures. The formula depends on specific critical points of a polynomial encoding the vertex-incidences. As an application, we compute the expected number of proper c c -edge-colorings of a large random k k -regular graph.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we study the asymptotic number of edge-colored regular graphs with a prescribed set of possible vertex-incidence structures. The asymptotic enumeration of regular graphs is a topic with a long history, see for instance
[
BC78
,
Bol82
,
MW91
]
, and more recently
[
LW24
]
.
Since Lovász and co-authors introduced graphons
[
Lov12
]
, large deviation principles have given rise to asymptotic enumeration results, see, e.g.,
[
Cha17
, §6]
. This framework also naturally allows for edge-colors
[
Lov12
, §23.2]
.
Our setup differs from most of the previously mentioned works in at least three respects. First, we impose precise constraints on the allowed vertex-incidence structures. In particular, this enables us to derive asymptotic counts for proper edge-colorings in Section
4
.
Second, we allow graphs to have self-loops and multiple edges. Sometimes such graphs are also called multigraphs in the literature.
Third, we weight graphs by the inverse cardinality of their automorphism group, which is equivalent to counting half-edge labeled graphs. Such weights arise naturally, for instance, in the context of Polyá enumeration theory
[
Pól37
]
, the theory of combinatorial species
[
Joy81
]
,
Feynman diagram expansions in quantum field theory
[
BIZ80
]
, geometric group theory
[
CV86
]
, and enumerative geometry
[
Kon92
]
. Further, such weights appear as compensation factors in classical random graphs studies (see, e.g.,
[
Jan+93
]
).

Beyond these structural differences, the novelty of our approach lies in the application of analytic combinatorial techniques to regular graph counting. The analytic combinatorics framework, popularized by Flajolet and Sedgewick
[
FS09
]
, has been remarkably successful in providing precise asymptotic estimates for various discrete structures, most notably in the analysis of large-scale data structures and the distribution of patterns in random mappings. In this paper, we demonstrate that the asymptotic enumeration of edge-colored regular graphs is equivalent to the analytic problem of locating the critical points of a specific polynomial.

(1)

\displaystyle A(n)=\sum_{G\in\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}(n)}\frac{1}{|\Aut(G)|}\prod_{v\in V_{G}}\Lambda_{\deg(v)}\,,

Throughout, we use the multi-index notation: bold symbols typically denote

Let

\crit^{*}(g)=\left\{\bm{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{c}\,:\,\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{1}}(\bm{z})=\ldots=\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{c}}(\bm{z})=0\text{ and }\bm{z}=\tau\cdot\bm{x}\text{ for }\tau\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}\text{ and }\bm{x}\in S^{c-1}\right\}.

.

If all critical points

(3)

A(n)\sim\frac{1}{2\pi}(\ell-1)!\sum_{\bm{z}\in\Psi}\frac{(-g(\bm{z}))^{-\ell}}{\sqrt{(-1)^{c-1}\det(\Hess g(\bm{z}))}}\quad\text{ for large integers }\ell=n\left(\frac{k}{2}-1\right).

2.
Edge-colored half-edge labeled graphs

In this section, we explain the framework of half-edge labeled graphs. For the case of bicolored graphs, this has appeared in
[
BMW24
, §3]
, the multicolored case has been described in
[
Wie26
, §2]
. We restate it here to make the paper self-contained.
Given a finite set
H
of labels, an
H
-
half-edge labeled graph
is a pair
(V,E)
where
V
and
E
are set-partitions of
H
with the constraint that
E
has only blocks of size two. Interpreting each block of
V
as a vertex gives rise to a graph that can have multiple edges and self-loops.

Remark 2.1
.

In classical graph theory, a (simple) graph is defined as a set of vertices
V
and a set of two-element subsets
E\subset V
, which inherently makes it a
simplicial complex
. However, applications in topology and physics often require a more flexible definition that allows the graph to be a general finite, at most one-dimensional
CW complex (see, e.g.,
[
BIZ80
,
BV25
]
). Half-edge labeled graphs are natural combinatorial realizations of these more general complexes.

Explicitly, in the edge-colored setting, we encode our half-edge labeled graphs as follows.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
H_{1},\dots,H_{c}
be disjoint sets of half-edge labels, one for each color. An edge-colored
(H_{1},\dots,H_{c})
-
half-edge labeled graph

\Gamma
is a tuple
\Gamma=(V,E_{1},\dots,E_{c})
such that

(1)

V
, the set of vertices, is a set partition of
H_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup H_{c}
;

(2)

for
i\in\{1,\dots,c\}
,
E_{i}
is a set partition of
H_{i}
into blocks of size two.

The notion of multidegree carries over to edge-colored half-edge labeled graphs:
for
\Gamma=(V,E_{1},\dots,E_{c})
,
\deg(v)=(w_{1},\dots,w_{c})
if the vertex
v\in V
is incident to exactly
w_{i}
half-edges of color
i
, or equivalently
|v\cap H_{i}|=w_{i}
.

An isomorphism between an edge-colored
(H_{1},\dots,H_{c})
- and an edge-colored
(H_{1}^{\prime},\dots,H_{c}^{\prime})
-half-edge labeled graph
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
is a tuple of bijections
H_{i}\rightarrow H_{i}^{\prime}
, preserving the partitions
\Gamma=(V,E_{1},\dots,E_{c})
and
\Gamma^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime}_{1},\dots,E^{\prime}_{c})
.
The automorphism group
\Aut(\Gamma)
consists of all isomorphisms of a graph to itself. We denote the isomorphism class
[\Gamma]
by
G
and write
\Aut(G):=\Aut(\Gamma)
for any half-edge labeled representative.

From now on, we also sometimes drop the adjective edge-colored.
Let
\{\lambda_{\bm{w}}\}_{\bm{w}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{c}}
be an infinite set of formal parameters. We use the notation
[\bm{x}^{\bm{a}}]f(\bm{x})
to denote the coefficient of the monomial
\bm{x}^{\bm{a}}
in the formal power series expansion of
f
.
The following proposition is an immediate generalization of Wick’s theorem (see, e.g.,
[
BIZ80
]
).

Proposition 2.3
.

The following counting formula holds:

(4)

\sum_{G\in\mathcal{G}}\frac{\prod_{v\in V_{G}}\lambda_{\deg(v)}}{|\Aut(G)|}=\sum_{\bm{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{c}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{c}(2s_{i}-1)!!\right)\cdot[\bm{x}^{2\bm{s}}]\exp\left(\sum_{\bm{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{c}_{\geq 0},\,|\bm{w}|\geq 1}\lambda_{\bm{w}}\frac{\bm{x}^{\bm{w}}}{\bm{w}!}\right),

where
\mathcal{G}
is the set of isomorphism classes of edge-colored half-edge labeled graphs.

Proof.

We interpret the right-hand side as a weighted enumeration of edge-colored graphs using the half-edge definition. Expansion of the exponential on the right-hand side and extracting the coefficient of
\bm{x}^{2\bm{s}}=x_{1}^{2s_{1}}\cdots x_{c}^{2s_{c}}
gives

(5)

[x_{1}^{2s_{1}}\cdots x_{c}^{2s_{c}}]\exp\left(\sum_{\bm{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{c}_{\geq 0},\,|\bm{w}|\geq 1}\lambda_{\bm{w}}\frac{\bm{x}^{\bm{w}}}{\bm{w}!}\right)=\sum_{\{t_{\bm{u}}\}}\frac{1}{\prod_{\bm{u}}t_{\bm{u}}!\,\bm{u}!^{t_{\bm{u}}}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{c}_{\geq 0}\end{subarray}}\lambda_{\bm{u}}^{t_{\bm{u}}},

where the last sum ranges over all functions

\bm{u}\mapsto t_{\bm{u}}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
satisfying

\sum_{\bm{u}}t_{\bm{u}}u_{i}=2s_{i}
for each
i=1,\dots,c
.
This condition implies that only finitely many
t_{\bm{u}}
are nonzero.

Fix a tuple of sets
(H_{1},\ldots,H_{c})
with
|H_{i}|=2s_{i}
and write
H:=H_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup H_{c}
.
A choice of multiplicities
\{t_{\bm{u}}\}
determines a class of partitions of
H
into blocks such that, for each
\bm{u}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{c})
, exactly
t_{\bm{u}}
blocks
contain
u_{i}
elements from
H_{i}
.
Each block represents a vertex with multidegree
\bm{u}
.
The number of such partitions is

(6)

\frac{(2\bm{s})!}{\prod_{\bm{u}}t_{\bm{u}}!\,\bm{u}!^{t_{\bm{u}}}},

which follows from the orbit–stabilizer theorem applied to the following natural action. The
c
-fold product of symmetric groups
\Sym_{2s_{1}}\times\dots\times\Sym_{2s_{c}}
acts on the set of partitions of
H_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup H_{c}
. A partition with
t_{\bm{u}}
blocks with
u_{i}
elements from
H_{i}
is stabilized by a subgroup isomorphic to
(\Sym_{u_{1}}\times\dots\times\Sym_{u_{c}})^{t_{\bm{u}}}\rtimes\Sym_{t_{\bm{u}}}
, permuting the elements inside each block and the blocks themselves.
Thus the coefficient (
5
) enumerates vertex sets with
prescribed multidegrees
\bm{u}
such that
\sum_{\bm{u}}t_{\bm{u}}u_{i}=2s_{i}
, weighted by the factor

\prod_{v}\lambda_{\deg(v)}
.

For each color
i
, an edge of color
i
is obtained by pairing two half-edges in

H_{i}
.
The number of perfect matchings on
H_{i}
is
(2s_{i}-1)!!=(2s_{i}-1)\cdot(2s_{i}-3)\cdots 3\cdot 1
, and these choices are
independent for different colors.
Choosing such matchings produces a collection of colored edges; self-loops and
multiple edges are allowed, since paired half-edges may lie in the same block or in
distinct blocks.
The total number of ways to pair all half-edges into edges is therefore

\prod_{i=1}^{c}(2s_{i}-1)!!\,.

Combining a partition of
H
with matchings on each
H_{i}
yields a half-edge labeled
edge-colored graph which represents an isomorphism class of a graph
G

with
exactly
s_{i}
edges of color
i
.
For such a fixed isomorphism class, the group

\Sym_{2s_{1}}\times\cdots\times\Sym_{2s_{c}}

acts freely on the set of half-edge labelings by permuting half-edges within
each color.
The stabilizer of
G
is precisely the automorphism group
\Aut(G)
.
By the orbit–stabilizer theorem, the number of half-edge labeled graphs representing
G
is

\frac{(2\bm{s})!}{|\Aut(G)|}.

Multiplying (
5
) by the factor

\frac{1}{(2\bm{s})!}\prod_{i=1}^{c}(2s_{i}-1)!!

has the effect of pairing half-edges into edges and then forgetting the labeling of
half-edges.
Consequently, each isomorphism class
G
appears with weight

\prod_{v\in V_{G}}\lambda_{\deg(v)}/|\Aut(G)|
.
Summing over all
\bm{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{c}
proves the stated identity.
∎

3.
Asymptotics for regular graphs

The goal of this section is to prove the main result, Theorem
1.1
.
Recall that
\mathcal{C}_{k}^{c}
is the set of all integer compositions of
k
into
c
(possibly zero) blocks, namely all
\bm{w}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{c}
such that
|\bm{w}|=k
.
Consider the homogeneous polynomial
V\in\mathbb{R}[\bm{x}]
of degree
k\geq 3
,

\displaystyle V(\bm{x})=\sum_{\bm{w}\in\mathcal{C}_{k}^{c}}\Lambda_{\bm{w}}\frac{\bm{x}^{\bm{w}}}{\bm{w}!}\,,

which relates to the polynomial defined in (
2
) as
g(\bm{x})=-\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2}+V(\bm{x})
. In contrast to the formal variables
\lambda_{\bm{w}}
, we think of the
\Lambda_{\bm{w}}
as fixed real parameters.
Via Proposition
2.3
, the polynomial
V
encodes a vertex-incidence structure for
k
-regular graphs: when it is used as the argument of the exponential function on the right-hand side of (
4
), then the only relevant summands on the left-hand side are
k
-regular graphs.

Our proof strategy for Theorem
1.1
consists of using the homogeneity of
V
to find an exponential integral representation over the sphere of the numbers

(7)

\displaystyle A(n)=\sum_{G\in\mathcal{G}_{k}^{c}(n)}\frac{\prod_{v\in V_{G}}\Lambda_{\deg(v)}}{|\Aut(G)|}\,,

where
n
is the number of vertices of
G
(Lemma
3.1
). To such an integral representation we can apply the saddle point method to obtain an asymptotic formula in terms of critical points of
V
(Proposition
3.3
).
Translating this formula in terms of critical points of the polynomial
g
yields a computationally more tractable expression, which is our main result, Theorem
1.1
. We start by providing an integral formula for
A(n)
. This result also appears as Proposition 2.6 in
[
Wie26
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
V\in\mathbb{R}[\bm{x}]
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
k
and let us denote
m=\tfrac{nk}{2}
. Then, for any integer
n\geq 0
such that
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

(8)

A(n)=\frac{2^{m+\frac{c-2}{2}}\Gamma\left(m+\tfrac{c}{2}\right)}{(2\pi)^{\frac{c}{2}}n!}\int_{S^{c-1}}V(\bm{x})^{n}\,\omega_{S^{c-1}},

where
\omega_{S^{c-1}}
denotes the standard volume form of the sphere
S^{c-1}
, and
\Gamma
is the gamma function.

Remark 3.2
.

The statement implies that, for a fixed polynomial
V
, the sequence
A(0),A(1),\ldots,
is
holonomic

[
Sta23
]
. Without invoking the holonomicity, a rather straightforward generalization of
[
BMW24
, §4]
provides a (moderately efficient) algorithm to compute the values of
A(n)
. An algorithm that produces explicit
p
-recursions for sequences
A(n)
would be useful. Techniques developed in
[
CM25
,
BCL25
]
might provide such an algorithm.

Proof.

Since the graphs
G
contributing to the sum (
7
) for
A(n)
are
k
-regular with
n
vertices, the number of edges of
G
is
m=\tfrac{nk}{2}
which must be an integer. From Proposition
2.3
, we have

(9)

A(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\\
|\bm{s}|=m\end{subarray}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{c}(2s_{i}-1)!!\right)\cdot[\bm{x}^{2\bm{s}}]\exp\left(\sum_{\bm{w}\in\mathcal{C}_{k}^{c}}\Lambda_{\bm{w}}\frac{\bm{x}^{\bm{w}}}{\bm{w}!}\right).

The
\bm{x}^{2\bm{s}}
-coefficient of the exponential on the right-hand side has degree
\tfrac{2m}{k}=n
in the coefficients
\Lambda_{\bm{w}}
. Using the homogeneity of
V
, this implies

[\bm{x}^{2\bm{s}}]\exp\left(\sum_{\bm{w}\in\mathcal{C}_{k}^{c}}\Lambda_{\bm{w}}\frac{\bm{x}^{\bm{w}}}{\bm{w}!}\right)=[\bm{x}^{2\bm{s}}]\frac{V(\bm{x})^{n}}{n!}.

Recall the Gaussian integral identity

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}}x^{t}\mathrm{d}x=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(t-1)!!&\text{if }t\text{ is even,}\\
0&\text{if }t\text{ is odd.}\end{array}\right.

This, used on each of the
c
double factorials, enables us to rewrite (
9
) as

A(n)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{c/2}n!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{c}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{c}x_{i}^{2}\right)V(\bm{x})^{n}\mathrm{d}\bm{x}.

Finally, the homogeneity of
V
allows us to switch to polar coordinates rescaling
V(r\cdot\bm{x})=r^{k}V(\bm{x})
, so we can rewrite the integral above as

\displaystyle A(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{c/2}n!}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{c-1}\left(\int_{\partial B_{r}(0)}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{c}x_{i}^{2}\right)V(\bm{x})^{n}\,\omega_{\partial B_{r}(0)}\right)\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{c/2}n!}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{c-1+kn}\mathrm{d}r\int_{S^{c-1}}V(\bm{x})^{n}\,\omega_{S^{c-1}},

where
B_{r}(0)
is the Euclidean ball of radius
r
centered at
0
, and
\omega_{\partial B_{r}(0)}
or
\omega_{S^{c-1}}
denote the standard volume form on the sphere.
Together with the identity

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{nk+c-1}\mathrm{d}r=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-q}(2q)^{\frac{nk+c-2}{2}}\mathrm{d}q=2^{m+\frac{c-2}{2}}\Gamma\left(m+\tfrac{c}{2}\right)\,,

this yields the stated expression.
∎

Lemma
3.1
can be used to deduce the asymptotics of
A(n)
by performing a saddle point analysis of the integral in (
8
).
We use the asymptotic equivalence notation

A(n)\sim B(n)
to denote
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}A(n)/B(n)=1
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\Phi
be the set of global maxima of the function
S^{c-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{c}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},~\bm{x}\mapsto|V(\bm{x})|
,
and assume that all of these are non-degenerate, i.e. the Hessian matrix has nonzero determinant. If
m=\frac{nk}{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
, then, for large
n
, we have

(10)

A(n)\sim\frac{k^{m+\frac{c-1}{2}}\left(\frac{k}{2}-1\right)^{n-m}\sqrt{\frac{k}{2}-1}}{\sqrt{8}\pi}\,\Gamma\!\left(m-n\right)\sum_{\bm{x}\in\Phi}\frac{V(\bm{x})^{n}}{\sqrt{(-1)^{c-1}\det(\Hess_{S^{c-1}}f(\bm{x}))}}\,,

where
\Hess_{S^{c-1}}
is the negative definite Hessian taken intrinsically on the sphere
S^{c-1}
, in local coordinates
\varphi_{\bm{x}}(\bm{y})
around
\bm{x}
, with
\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{c-1}
and
f_{\bm{x}}(\bm{y})=\log\frac{V(\varphi_{\bm{x}}(\bm{y}))}{V(\bm{x})}
.

Remark 3.4
.

Consider a function
f:S^{c-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{c}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\bm{x}\in S^{c-1}
a non-degenerate maximizer for
f
. Let
\varphi_{\bm{x}}:\mathbb{R}^{c-1}\to S^{c-1}
be a system of local coordinates, namely a smooth diffeomorphism from a neighborhood of the origin
\bm{0}
to a neighborhood of
\bm{x}
, so that
\varphi_{\bm{x}}(\bm{0})=\bm{x}
. Let us denote by
\bm{y}
the coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{c-1}
. Then, the spherical Hessian
\Hess_{S^{c-1}}f(\bm{x})
of
f
at
\bm{x}
(in local coordinates
\bm{y}
) is the
(c-1)\times(c-1)
negative definite matrix with entries
b_{ij}
such that

f(\varphi_{\bm{x}}(\bm{y}))=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{c-1}b_{ij}\,y_{i}y_{j}+o(y_{1}^{2}+\dots+y_{c-1}^{2}),

for
\bm{y}
in a neighborhood of
\bm{0}
.

Proof.

From Lemma
3.1
we see that for large
n
, since
S^{c-1}
is compact, the main contribution to the integral (
8
) comes from points near the maxima of
\bm{x}\mapsto|V(\bm{x})|
on
S^{c-1}
. The statement follows from a saddle point expansion around these maxima, see
[
Bru14
, §4.6]
, and applications of Stirling’s formula:

\displaystyle A(n)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{2^{m+\frac{c-2}{2}}\left(m+\frac{c-2}{2}\right)!}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,n!\,n^{\frac{c-1}{2}}}\sum_{\bm{x}\in\Phi}\frac{V(\bm{x})^{n}}{\sqrt{\det(-\Hess_{S^{c-1}}f(\bm{x}))}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\,k^{\frac{c-1}{2}}\,n^{-\frac{1}{2}}k^{\frac{nk}{2}}\,e^{n(1-\frac{k}{2})}\,n^{n(\frac{k}{2}-1)}\sum_{\bm{x}\in\Phi}\frac{V(\bm{x})^{n}}{\sqrt{(-1)^{c-1}\det(\Hess_{S^{c-1}}f(\bm{x}))}}.

We will see later that it will be convenient to use the Gamma function of
n(\frac{k}{2}-1)=m-n
to rewrite this expression.
This is done using again Stirling’s formula, to get

A(n)\sim\frac{k^{m+\frac{c-1}{2}}\left(\frac{k}{2}-1\right)^{n-m}\sqrt{\frac{k}{2}-1}}{\sqrt{8}\pi}\,\Gamma\!\left(m-n\right)\sum_{\bm{x}\in\Phi}\frac{V(\bm{x})^{n}}{\sqrt{(-1)^{c-1}\det(\Hess_{S^{c-1}}f(\bm{x}))}}.

The difference
n-m
, which appears prominently here, is the
Euler characteristic
of the graph.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

In
[
Wie26
]
it is shown that if one introduces a complex parameter into the polynomial
V(\bm{x})
,
A(n)
can be written, for large
n
, as a sum over critical points of
V
restricted to
\mathcal{S}=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{C}^{c}\,:\,x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{c}^{2}=1\}
. The space
\mathcal{S}
can be thought of as a complex compactification of the real sphere
S^{c-1}
.
The sum representation comes from a decomposition of the integral in Lemma
3.1
into integrals over Lefschetz thimbles associated to each critical point. Therefore, Proposition
3.3
can be seen as a specialization of
[
Wie26
, Corollary 3.4]
to the case where
V(\bm{x})
is defined over
\mathbb{R}
, so that not all Lefschetz thimbles contribute to the integral but only those corresponding to global maxima or minima of
V
on
\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{R}}=S^{c-1}
.

The expression (
10
) is not ideal in that one needs to compute the Hessian matrices in local coordinates. We remedy this issue by providing a different asymptotic expression in terms of the Hessian of
g
on
\mathbb{R}^{c}
, which is straightforward to compute. The following lemmata prepare this.

Recall that we denote by
\crit^{*}(g)
the critical points of
g
in the complex cone over
S^{c-1}
,

\crit^{*}(g)=\left\{\bm{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{c}\,:\,\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{1}}(\bm{z})=\ldots=\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{c}}(\bm{z})=0\text{ and }\bm{z}=\tau\cdot\bm{x}\text{ for }\tau\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}\text{ and }\bm{x}\in S^{c-1}\right\},

and

\Psi=\argmin_{\bm{z}=\tau\cdot\bm{x}\in\crit^{*}(g)}|\tau|.

Lemma 3.6
.

Consider the polynomial
g
as defined in (
2
) and let
\Psi
be the set of critical points among
\crit^{*}(g)
with minimal distance to the origin as defined above. Let
\sim
be the equivalence relation on
S^{c-1}
identifying antipodal points. Then, the map

\nu\,:\,\Psi\rightarrow\Phi/\sim,\quad\bm{z}=\tau\cdot\bm{x}\mapsto\bm{x}

is onto and every fiber has cardinality
k-2
.

Proof.

The map
\nu
is well-defined only onto the quotient space since
\tau\bm{x}=(-\tau)(-\bm{x})
, hence we want to identify
\bm{x}
with
-\bm{x}
.
Let
\tau\bm{x}\in\crit^{*}(g)
be a critical point. It satisfies

(11)

\tau x_{i}=\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{i}}(\tau\bm{x})\text{ for }i=1,\dots,c.

Multiplying these by
\tau x_{i}
, summing over all
i
and using homogeneity, we obtain

(12)

\tau^{2}=\tau^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{c}x_{i}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{c}\tau x_{i}\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{i}}(\tau\bm{x})=kV(\tau\bm{x})=k\tau^{k}V(\bm{x}),

so
\tau^{2-k}=kV(\bm{x})
. Moreover,
\bm{x}
is a critical point of
{\left.\kern-1.2ptV\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{S^{c-1}}}
. Indeed, from (
11
) we see that
\bm{x}
satisfies the Lagrange equations on the sphere

\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_{i}}(\bm{x})-2\lambda x_{i}=0\quad\text{for all }i=1,\dots,c

with Lagrange multiplier
\lambda=\tfrac{\tau^{2-k}}{2}
.
Since
k\geq 3
, any
\tau
satisfying (
12
) with minimal
|\tau|
gives a maximum of
|V(\bm{x})|
. This implies
\bm{x}\in\Phi
. As there are
k-2
complex solutions to
\tau^{2-k}=kV(\bm{x})
for a fixed
\bm{x}
, this proves the claim.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\bm{z}=\tau\bm{x}\in\crit^{*}(g)
and let
f_{\bm{x}}(\bm{y})=\log\frac{V(\varphi_{\bm{x}}(\bm{y}))}{V(\bm{x})}
, in local coordinates
\bm{y}
on the sphere given by
\varphi_{\bm{x}}:\mathbb{R}^{c-1}\to S^{c-1}
, with
\varphi_{\bm{x}}(\bm{0})=\bm{x}
. Then,

\det(\Hess_{S^{c-1}}f(\bm{x}))=\frac{k^{c-1}}{k-2}\det(\Hess g(\bm{z})).

Proof.

Using standard techniques from differential geometry, e.g., Corollary 5.16 in
[
Bou23
]
, we can rewrite the Hessian on the sphere as

\Hess_{S^{c-1}}f(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{V(\bm{x})}\left(P_{\bm{x}}\mathrm{Hess}\,V(\bm{x})P_{\bm{x}}^{T}-\langle\nabla V(\bm{x})\,,\,\bm{x}\rangle\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{c-1}\right),

where
P_{\bm{x}}
is the
(c-1)\times c
projection matrix onto the hyperplane orthogonal to
\bm{x}\in S^{c-1}
, namely the tangent space to
S^{c-1}
at
\bm{x}
, and
\Hess V
denotes the standard Hessian matrix of
V
as a function on
\mathbb{R}^{c}
.
Because
V
is
k
-homogeneous, by the Euler relation one deduces that
\frac{1}{V(\bm{x})}\langle\nabla V(\bm{x})\,,\,\bm{x}\rangle=k
, hence

\Hess_{S^{c-1}}f(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{V(\bm{x})}P_{\bm{x}}\mathrm{Hess}V(\bm{x})P_{\bm{x}}^{T}-k\,\mathrm{Id}_{c-1}=\frac{\tau^{2-k}}{V(\bm{x})}P_{\bm{x}}\mathrm{Hess}V(\tau\bm{x})P_{\bm{x}}^{T}-k\,\mathrm{Id}_{c-1},

where we used the fact that the Hessian matrix of
V
is homogeneous of degree
k-2
.
Changing to the Hessian of
g
, where the entries are by definition
\partial_{i}\partial_{j}g
, we get

\Hess_{S^{c-1}}f(\bm{x})=\frac{\tau^{2-k}}{V(\bm{x})}P_{\bm{x}}\left(\mathrm{Id}_{c}+\Hess g(\bm{z})\right)P_{\bm{x}}^{T}-k\,\mathrm{Id}_{c-1}.

Using the formula for
\tau
in (
12
), and the property
P_{\bm{x}}P_{\bm{x}}^{T}=\mathrm{Id}_{c-1}
, this reduces to

\Hess_{S^{c-1}}f(\bm{x})=kP_{\bm{x}}\Hess g(\bm{z})P_{\bm{x}}^{T}.

We claim that the Hessian matrix of
g
has an eigenvector in the radial direction, with eigenvalue
\lambda_{\rm rad}
. Indeed, using Euler’s relation on
\nabla V
, homogeneous of degree
k-1
, and using (
11
), we get

\left(\Hess g(\bm{z})\right)\bm{z}=-\bm{z}+\left(\Hess V(\bm{z})\right)\bm{z}=-\bm{z}+(k-1)\nabla V(\bm{z})=(k-2)\bm{z}.

Therefore,
\lambda_{\rm rad}=k-2
, and we denote the remaining eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{c-1}
. Then,
\prod_{i=1}^{c-1}\lambda_{i}=\det\left(P_{\bm{x}}\Hess g(\bm{z})P_{\bm{x}}^{T}\right)
and we obtain

\det\left(\Hess_{S^{c-1}}f(\bm{x})\right)=\frac{k^{c-1}}{k-2}\det\left(\Hess g(\bm{z})\right).\qed

We can now prove the main result for the asymptotic behavior of
A(n)
in terms of critical points of
g
, as
n\to\infty
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma
3.6
allows us to match points in
\Phi
with points in
\Psi
. Take
\bm{z}=\tau\bm{x}\in\Psi
. Using (
12
), we get

\displaystyle g(\bm{z})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}+V(\tau\bm{x})=-\frac{\tau^{2}}{2}+\tau^{k}V(\bm{x})=\tau^{2}\frac{2-k}{2k}

\displaystyle V(\bm{x})^{n}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\tau^{2-k}}{k}\right)^{n}=k^{-n}\tau^{2n(1-\frac{k}{2})}=k^{-\frac{nk}{2}}\left(\frac{k}{2}-1\right)^{n(\frac{k}{2}-1)}(-g(\bm{z}))^{-n(\frac{k}{2}-1)}.

Combining the latter with Lemma
3.7
and accounting for the multiplicities from Lemma
3.6
, we can rewrite the asymptotic expression for
A(n)
from Proposition
3.3
as

\displaystyle\frac{k^{\frac{nk}{2}+\frac{c-1}{2}}\left(\frac{k}{2}-1\right)^{-n(\frac{k}{2}-1)}\sqrt{\frac{k-2}{2}}}{\sqrt{8}\pi}\,\Gamma\!\left(n\left(\frac{k}{2}-1\right)\right)\frac{2}{k-2}\sum_{\bm{z}\in\Psi}\frac{k^{-\frac{nk}{2}}\left(\frac{k}{2}-1\right)^{n(\frac{k}{2}-1)}(-g(\bm{z}))^{-n(\frac{k}{2}-1)}}{\sqrt{\frac{k^{c-1}}{k-2}}\sqrt{(-1)^{c-1}\det\left(\Hess g(\bm{z})\right)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\Gamma\!\left(n\left(\frac{k}{2}-1\right)\right)\sum_{\bm{z}\in\Psi}\frac{(-g(\bm{z}))^{-n(\frac{k}{2}-1)}}{\sqrt{(-1)^{c-1}\det\left(\Hess g(\bm{z})\right)}}.

Substituting
\ell=n(\frac{k}{2}-1)\in\mathbb{Z}
proves the statement.
∎

Note that when
c=1
, the statement of Theorem
1.1
reduces to
[
Bor18
, Theorem 3.3.1]
, whereas for
c=2
we get
[
BMW24
, Theorem 5.3]
.
An open question regards the generalization of the result to graphs that are not necessarily regular. The difficulty lies in finding an integral representation over a suitable domain with a nice complex compactification, as the sphere in our case. This is not straightforward in the non-regular scenario and we leave it for future research.

4.
Average number of proper edge-colorings

As an application of our main result, Theorem
1.1
, we prove an asymptotic formula for the weighted sum of proper edge-colorings with
c
colors of
k
-regular graphs.
Combined with a known result on the number of
k
-regular vertex-labeled (multi)graphs without self-loops
[
BC78
]
, this gives then rise to an asymptotic formula for the expected number of proper
c
-edge-colorings of a random such graph, which is stated in Theorem
1.2
. Here,
random
means with respect to the uniform distribution on the set of
k
-regular vertex-labeled graphs without self-loops.

From now on, instead of edge-coloring we simply write coloring. As before, we assume
k\geq 3
. A
c
-coloring of a graph
G
is a map
E_{G}\rightarrow\{1,\dots,c\}
, assigning a color to each edge. It is
proper
if no vertex is incident to two edges of the same color. By weighted we mean that we take an
|\Aut(G)|^{-1}
-weighted sum over all
k
-regular graphs
G
, i.e. we wish to determine the large-
n
asymptotics of

(13)

{P}^{c}_{k}(n):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}G~k\text{-regular}\\
|V_{G}|=n\end{subarray}}\frac{\#\text{(proper $c$-colorings of $G$)}}{|\Aut(G)|}.

The automorphism group in (
13
) considers
G
as a monocolored graph.
Clearly, in order for a
c
-coloring to be proper, we need
c\geq k
. In fact, a standard result from graph theory asserts that for any
simple

k
-regular graph
G
there exists a
c
-coloring with
c=k+1
. For
c=k
, a
c
-coloring is also called a 1-factorization of
G
. The average number of such 1-factorizations has been computed, e.g., in
[
RW94
]
, though there are two important differences to our number
{P}^{k}_{k}(n)
: the first is regarding the probability distribution on the space of graphs which we do not take to be uniform but weighted by
|\Aut(G)|^{-1}
. The second concerns the type of graphs:
[
RW94
]
consider simple graphs, whereas our graphs are allowed to have multiple edges.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
k\geq 3
and assume
\ell=n\left(\tfrac{k}{2}-1\right)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Then, for large
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we have

{P}^{c}_{k}(n)\sim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{(\ell-1)!\cdot 2^{k/2}}{2\pi}\left(\frac{2}{k-2}\right)^{\ell-1/2}&\text{ if }c=k,\text{ and }n\text{ even},\\
\frac{(\ell-1)!\cdot\sqrt{k-2}}{2\pi}\left(\frac{k-1}{c-1}+1\right)^{(1-c)/2}\left(k\binom{c}{k}\right)^{n}c^{-\frac{nk}{2}}\left(\frac{2k}{k-2}\right)^{\ell}&\text{ if }c>k,\\
0&\text{ else.}\end{array}\right.

We prepare the proof of Theorem
4.1
with two lemmata. A proper
c
-coloring corresponds to an edge-colored graph
G
arising from the elementary symmetric polynomial

V_{k}^{c}:=e_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{c})=\sum_{1\leq a_{1}<a_{2}<\dots<a_{k}\leq c}x_{a_{1}}x_{a_{2}}\dots x_{a_{k}}.

We obtain the following reformulation as an edge-colored graph counting problem.

Lemma 4.2
.

With vertex weights
\{\Lambda_{\bm{w}}\}_{\bm{w}\in\mathcal{C}_{k}^{c}}
from
V_{k}^{c}
, we have
{P}^{c}_{k}(n)=A(n)
.

Proof.

It remains to show the following. Suppose there are exactly
N
distinct colorings of
G
such that the induced edge-colored graphs
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{N}
are isomorphic. Then
|\Aut(\Gamma_{i})|=|\Aut(G)|/N
.

Let
G
have
2w
many half-edges, while
\Gamma_{i}
(for any
i
) has
2w_{j}
half-edges of the
j
th color. Note that
w=w_{1}+\dots+w_{c}
. The group
\Sym_{2w}
naturally acts on the half-edge labels of
\Gamma
, but also on the disjoint union
\sqcup_{1\leq j\leq c}H_{j}
of half-edge labels of
\Gamma_{i}
. In particular, we obtain an action of
\Aut(G)
on the set of edge-colored half-edge labeled graphs, and
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{N}
form an orbit of this action. Since the stabilizer is
\Aut(\Gamma_{i})
, the claim follows from the orbit-stabilizer theorem.
∎

Lemma 4.4

Lemma 4.5
.

For

k
-regular vertex-labeled graphs without self-loops can be identified with the set

\mathcal{G}(M,\bm{r},k):=\left\{(g_{ij})_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times n}\text{ symmetric, }g_{ij}\in\{0,1,\dots,k\},\,g_{ij}=0\text{ if }M_{ij}=0,\,\sum_{j}g_{ij}=r_{i}\right\}.

Then from
[
BC78
, Theorem 1]

we obtain

|\mathcal{G}(M,\bm{r},k)|\sim\left(\frac{kn}{e}\right)^{kn/2}\frac{\sqrt{2}\exp\left(\frac{1}{4}(k^{2}-4k+3)\right)}{(k!)^{n}}

Dividing
n!\,{P}^{c}_{k}(n)
by
|\mathcal{G}(M,\bm{r},k)|

and using Stirling’s formula to get

\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{nk}{e}\right)^{nk/2}(k!)^{-n}\sim n!\frac{\sqrt{k-2}}{2\pi}\Gamma(\ell)\left(\frac{2k}{k-2}\right)^{\ell}((k-1)!)^{-n}\,,

gives the statement.
∎

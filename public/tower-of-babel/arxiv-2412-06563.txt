On functionals involving the p p -capacity and the q q -torsional rigidity
Michiel van den Berg

Abstract
Upper bounds are obtained for the p p -capacity of compact sets in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , with d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 and 1 < p < d 1<p<d . Upper and lower bounds are obtained for the product of p p -capacity and powers of the q q -torsional rigidity over the collection of all non-empty, open, bounded and convex sets in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} with either a perimeter constraint, or a measure constraint, or a combination of perimeter and measure constraints. For some range of parameters we identify the ball as the unique (up to homotheties) maximiser or minimiser respectively.

Introduction

For a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},\,d\geq 2
we recall, for
1<p<d
, a definition of its
p
- capacity
\mathrm{cap}\,_{p}(K)
. See for example
[
13
]
or, for an equivalent definition,
[
9
]
.

\displaystyle\mathrm{cap}\,_{p}(K)=

\displaystyle\inf\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|Du|^{p}:u\geq{\bf 1}_{K}\,\cap\,(Du\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\,\cap(u\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\,

\displaystyle\cap\,(u\,\textup{vanishing at}\,\infty)\Big\},

(1)

where
\bf 1_{\cdot}
denotes the indicator function, and where vanishing at
\infty
means

(\forall\varepsilon>0)[|\{u>\varepsilon\}|<\infty]
, and where
|\cdot|
denotes
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure.

It follows from (
1

tK,

\mathrm{cap}\,_{p}(tK)=t^{d-p}\mathrm{cap}\,_{p}(K),\,t>0.

(2)

Furthermore if
K_{1}
and

are compact sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
then
K_{1}\subset K_{2}
implies
\mathrm{cap}\,_{p}(K_{1})\leq\mathrm{cap}\,_{p}(K_{2})
.

For an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
we recall, for

, its

-torsional rigidity, or

-torsion for short:

T_{q}(\Omega)=\Bigg(\sup_{\psi\in W_{0}^{1,q}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}|\psi|\right)^{q}}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|D\psi|^{q}}\Bigg)^{1/(q-1)},

(3)

See for example
[
11
]
. By (

-torsion has the following scaling property:

T_{q}(t\Omega)=t^{d+q^{\prime}}T_{q}(\Omega),\,q^{\prime}=\frac{q}{q-1},\,t>0.

(4)

We denote the closure, boundary, perimeter, diameter and measure of a measurable set
A
by
\overline{A}
,
\partial A
,
P(A)
,
\textup{diam}(A)
and
|A|
respectively. Moreover, we denote the inradius of a non-empty set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
\rho(A)=\sup_{x\in A}\sup\{r>0:B_{r}(x)\subset A\}
. The
a
-dimensional Hausdorff measure is denoted by
\mathcal{H}^{a}(A)
.

Inequalities between torsional rigidity, first Dirichlet eigenvalue etc. go back at least as far as
[
16
]
. For more recent contributions we refer to
[

]
,
[

]
,

]

In this paper we consider maximisation and minimisation problems of the functional

G_{p,q,r,\alpha}(\Omega)=\frac{\mathrm{cap}\,_{p}(\overline{\Omega})T_{q}(\Omega)^{r}}{|\Omega|^{\alpha}P(\Omega)^{\beta}},

(5)

over the collection of non-empty, open, bounded and convex sets.
Here
\alpha\geq 0
, and
\beta\geq 0
are parameters which satisfy

d\alpha+(d-1)\beta=d-p+(d+q^{\prime})r.

(6)

In case the perimeter term is absent, that is
\beta=0
, we put

\alpha^{*}=\frac{1}{d}(d-p+(d+q^{\prime})r).

(7)

The special case
p=q=2
has been considered in
[
1
,
3
,
5
]
.

By scaling of measure and perimeter, (
2
), (

) and (

G_{p,q,r,\alpha}(t\Omega)=G_{p,q,r,\alpha}(\Omega),\,t>0
.
It was shown in
[
1

that if
0\leq\alpha\leq\frac{2}{d}
, then

\displaystyle\sup\{G_{2,2,1,\alpha}(\Omega):\Omega\,\,\textup{non-empty, open, bounded, convex in}

\displaystyle\,\mathbb{R}^{d}\}

\displaystyle=G_{2,2,1,\alpha}(B_{1}),

where

is an open ball with radius

\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Any open ball is a maximiser of

.

A key ingredient in the proof of the previous statement is the following isoperimetric upper bound
[
1
, (6)]
for the Newtonian capacity for non-empty, compact and convex sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\mathrm{cap}\,_{2}(K)\leq\frac{d-2}{d}\frac{P(K)^{2}}{|K|},

(8)

with equality for a closed ball.

The proof of (

) uses an idea going back to
[
8
]
. There upper bounds for
\mathrm{cap}\,_{2}(K)
were obtained by restricting the class of test functions to the ones depending only the distance to the boundary of
K
.
We recall the following notation from
[
1
]
. For a non-empty compact set
K
we denote for
t>0
its closed
t
-neighbourhood by

K_{t}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:d_{K}(x)\leq t\},

(9)

where

is the distance to
K

If
K
is compact then
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus K
is open and consists of a countable union of open sets. Since
K
is bounded there is precisely one unbounded component of its complement, which is denoted by
U_{K}
. Let
\tilde{K}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus U_{K}
. Let

be the union of all bounded components of the complement of

is open, and

. It is straightforward to show that
\tilde{K}=K\cup A_{K}
, and that
\mathrm{cap}\,_{2}(K)=\mathrm{cap}\,_{2}(\tilde{K})
.
The bound in Theorem

-neighbourhood of
\tilde{K}
.

If

is compact and

, oriented by an outward unit normal vector field, then we denote the mean curvature map by

, and define its integral by

In Theorem

in terms of

Theorem 1
.

Let
K\neq\emptyset
, and let

.

(i)

If

\mathbb{R}^{d},\,d\geq 2
, and if

\mathrm{cap}\,_{p}(K)\leq\mathrm{cap}\,_{p}(\tilde{K})\leq\Big(\int_{(0,\infty)}dt\,P(\tilde{K}_{t})^{-1/(p-1)}\Big)^{1-p}<\infty.

with equality if

(ii)

If

\mathbb{R}^{d},\,d\geq 2,
and if

\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\int_{(s,\infty)}dt\,P(\tilde{K}_{t})^{-1/(p-1)}=\infty,

then
\mathrm{cap}\,_{p}(K)=0
.

(iii)

If
K
is compact in
\mathbb{R}^{d},\,d\geq 2,
then

\mathrm{cap}\,_{p}(K)\leq\inf_{a>0}\frac{1}{a^{p}}|K_{a}|.

(10)

(iv)

If
K
is compact, convex, and if

is

, then

\mathrm{cap}\,_{p}(K)\leq\Big(\frac{d-p}{p-1}\Big)^{p-1}\frac{M(K)^{p-1}}{P(K)^{p-2}},

(11)

with equality if
K
is a closed ball.

. For the anisotropic version of

It follows from the Aleksandrov-Fenchel inequality (

\mathrm{cap}\,_{p}(K)\leq\Big(\frac{d-p}{d(p-1)}\Big)^{p-1}\frac{P(K)^{p}}{|K|^{p-1}},

with equality if

It was shown in
[

that if

is bounded from above on the collection of open, bounded, convex sets.
The occurrence of a logarithmic factor for elongated convex, compact sets in the upper bound the Newtonian capacity implies the existence of a maximiser. See

In Theorem

Define the isoperimetric ratio of

I(K)=\frac{\omega_{d}d^{d}|K|^{d-1}}{P(K)^{d}},

. By the isoperimetric inequality

with equality if and only if

Theorem 2
.

Let
K
be a convex, compact set and with non-empty interior. If

then

\mathrm{cap}\,_{d-1}(K)\leq d^{2-d}(d-1)2^{d-2}\frac{P(K)^{d-1}}{|K|^{d-2}}\Big(\log(I(K)^{-1})\Big)^{2-d}.

Theorem

In Section

\frac{d-p}{d-1}-q^{\prime}r-\beta\geq 0
, we get by (
43
)

\displaystyle G_{p,q,r,\alpha}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\geq\omega_{d}^{\frac{1}{d}(p-q^{\prime}r-\beta)}d^{-\frac{d-p}{d}+\frac{d(p-1)}{d-1}+\frac{d-1}{d}(q^{\prime}r+\beta)}

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{d-p}{p(d-1)}\right)^{p-1}C_{1}\left(\frac{\textup{diam}(\Omega)}{2\rho(\Omega)}\right)^{\frac{d-p-(d-1)(q^{\prime}r+\beta)}{d(d-1)}}.

By (
52
) we get

\displaystyle G_{p,q,r,\alpha}(\Omega)\geq C_{1}

\displaystyle\,\left(\frac{p-1}{p(d-1)}\right)^{p-1}d^{\frac{d(p-1)}{d-1}-\frac{2d-p+q^{\prime}r+\beta}{d}-r+2q^{\prime}r+2\beta}

\displaystyle\times(d+q^{\prime})^{r}\left(\frac{\textup{diam}(\Omega)}{2\rho(\Omega)}\right)^{\frac{d-p-(d-1)(q^{\prime}r+\beta)}{d(d-1)}}G_{p,q,r,\alpha}(B_{1}).

(70)

This implies the assertion under (ii).

To prove the existence of a minimiser we can proceed similarly as in the proof of Theorem
3
(ii).

Part (iv) follows by (
70
).

To prove (v) we have by (
56
) that for
r<0
,
T^{r}_{q}(\Omega)\geq T^{r}_{q}(\Omega^{\sharp})
. Furthermore,
\mathrm{cap}\,_{p}(\overline{\Omega})\geq\mathrm{cap}\,_{p}(\overline{\Omega^{\sharp}})
, and
\frac{T^{r}_{q}(\Omega^{\sharp})\mathrm{cap}\,_{p}(\overline{\Omega^{\sharp})}}{|\Omega|^{\alpha}}=G_{p,q,r,\alpha^{*}}(B_{1})
.
∎

5
Remarks

Remark 1
.

Two different upper bounds for
p
-capacity are used in the proof of Theorem
3
(i). Inequalities (
12
) and (
54
) are used in the first and second part respectively. By considering ellipsoids one observes that (
12
) gives better estimates for elongated ellipsoids while (
54
) gives better estimates for flat ellipsoids. Both (
12
) and (
54
) are equalities for a ball.

Remark 2
.

In order to show that the supremum in the left-hand side of (
35
) is finite for
1<p\leq d-1
if and only if
q^{\prime}r\geq p-\beta
one would have to show that

\mathrm{cap}\,_{p}(\overline{E_{a_{\varepsilon}}})\geq c(p,d)\varepsilon^{d-p-1}

In the special case where
p=2
and
d\geq 3
we have the following.

Remark 3
.

If
d\geq 3
,
p=2
, and if
q^{\prime}r<2-\beta
then

\sup\{G_{2,q,r,\alpha}(\Omega):\Omega\,\textup{non-empty, open, convex in}\,\mathbb{R}^{d}\}=\infty.

Proof.

Consider the ellipsoid
E_{a_{\varepsilon}}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with semi-axes
a_{\varepsilon}=(1,\varepsilon,\varepsilon,\ldots,\varepsilon)
, then for
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
,

\displaystyle G_{2,q,r,\alpha}(E_{a_{\varepsilon}})\geq\begin{cases}C(3,q)\varepsilon^{\frac{q^{\prime}r+\beta-2}{d}}\big(\log(\varepsilon^{-1})\big)^{-1},&d=3,\\
C(d,q)\varepsilon^{\frac{q^{\prime}r+\beta-2}{d}}&d>3.\end{cases}

(71)

We have used the lower bound in (
41
), the definition of
\alpha^{*}
in (
7
), and the formulae for the
2
-capacity of
E_{a_{\epsilon}}
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
in
[
14
, p.260]
:

\mathrm{cap}\,_{2}\big(\overline{E_{a_{\varepsilon}}})\big)=\begin{cases}4\pi\big(\log(\varepsilon^{-1})\big)^{-1}(1+o(1)),&d=3,\ \varepsilon\downarrow 0,\\
\displaystyle\frac{2\pi^{d/2}(d-3)}{\Gamma(d/2)}\varepsilon^{d-3}(1+o(1)),&d>3,\ \varepsilon\downarrow 0.\end{cases}

If
q^{\prime}r<2-\beta
, then the right-hand side of (
71
) is unbounded on
(0,1)
.
∎

The
\log
factor
\big(\log(\varepsilon^{-1})\big)^{-1}
for
d=3
did not play a role in the above as we have the strict inequality
q^{\prime}r<2
.

It is of course possible to consider the maximisation of
G_{p,q,r,\alpha}(\Omega)
over the collection of open sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite perimeter or measure. We have the following.

Remark 4
.

If
d-p>\beta(d-1)
, and if
1<p<d
, then

\sup\{G_{p,q,r,\alpha}(\Omega):\Omega\,\textup{non-empty, open in $\mathbb{R}^{d}$ with $P(\Omega)<\infty$}\}=\infty.

(72)

If
1<p<d
, then

\sup\{G_{p,q,r,\alpha^{*}}(\Omega):\Omega\,\textup{non-empty, open in $\mathbb{R}^{d}$ with $|\Omega|<\infty$}\}=\infty.

(73)

Proof.

Let
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
be the union of the open ball
B_{1}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|<1\}
, and the ellipsoid

E_{b_{\varepsilon}}=\varepsilon^{-3}+\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\varepsilon^{-2d}x_{d}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}(\varepsilon x_{j})^{2}<1\}.

(74)

Then
B_{1}\cap E_{b_{\varepsilon}}=\emptyset,\,\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}
, and by monotonicity of the
q
-torsion

T_{q}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})^{r}\geq T_{q}(B_{1})^{r}
. By monotonicity of the
p
-capacity,

\displaystyle\mathrm{cap}\,_{p}(\overline{\Omega_{\varepsilon}})

\displaystyle\geq\mathrm{cap}\,_{p}(\overline{E_{b_{\varepsilon}}})\geq d\omega_{d}\left(\frac{P(E_{b_{\varepsilon}})}{d\omega_{d}}\right)^{\frac{d-p}{d-1}}\left(\frac{d(d-p)}{p(d-1)}\right)^{p-1}.

(75)

We also have that
|\Omega_{\varepsilon}|=\omega_{d}(1+\varepsilon)\leq 3\omega_{d}/2
, and
P(\Omega_{\varepsilon})=P(B_{1})+P(E_{b_{\varepsilon}})
.
Putting this together yields

G_{p,q,r,\alpha}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\geq k_{d}\frac{T_{q}(B_{1})^{r}(P(E_{b_{\varepsilon}}))^{\frac{d-p}{d-1}}}{(3\omega_{d}/2)^{\alpha}(P(B_{1})+P(E_{b_{\varepsilon}}))^{\beta}},

(76)

where
k_{d}
can be read-off from (
75
). By the second inequality in (
47
),

P(E_{\varepsilon})\geq\frac{|E_{\varepsilon}|}{a_{d}}=\omega_{d}\varepsilon^{1-d}.

(77)

Since
d-p>\beta(d-1)
, and
P(E_{\varepsilon})\rightarrow\infty
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
we conclude that the right-hand side of (
76
) tends to
\infty
.
This implies (
72
).

To prove (
73
) we follow the lines above with
\beta=0
.
∎

Remark 5
.

The estimates in the proof of Theorem
3
(or Theorem
6
) are too crude to discuss the existence of a maximiser (minimiser) if inequality (
36
) is not strict. However, Theorem
3
(iv) gives a partial result in that case.

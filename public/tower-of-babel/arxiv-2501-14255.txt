Hitting probabilities, thermal capacity, and Hausdorff dimension results for the Brownian sheet
Cheuk Yin Lee and Yimin Xiao

Abstract
. Let W = { W ⁡ ( t ) : t ∈ ℝ + N } W=\{W(t):t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\} be an ( N , d ) (N,d) -Brownian sheet and let E ⊂ ( 0 , ∞ ) N E\subset(0,\infty)^{N} and F ⊂ ℝ d F\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} be compact sets. We prove a necessary and sufficient condition for W ⁡ ( E ) W(E) to intersect F F with positive probability and determine the essential supremum of the Hausdorff dimension of the intersection set W ⁡ ( E ) ∩ F W(E)\cap F in terms of the thermal capacity of E × F E\times F . This extends the previous results of Khoshnevisan and Xiao (2015) for the Brownian motion and Khoshnevisan and Shi (1999) for the Brownian sheet in the special case when E ⊂ ( 0 , ∞ ) N E\subset(0,\infty)^{N} is an interval.

1.
Introduction

Hitting probabilities and fractal properties are important features of stochastic processes and random fields. Let
\{X(t):t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\}
be a random field with values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be
arbitrary compact sets. We are interested in the following two questions.

(1)

What is a necessary and sufficient condition for
{\mathbb{P}}(X(E)\cap F\neq\varnothing)>0
?

(2)

What is the Hausdorff dimension
{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(X(E)\cap F)
on the event
X(E)\cap F\neq\varnothing
?

For the
d
-dimensional Brownian motion
\{B(t):t\geq 0\}
, Question (1) was answered by Watson

[
26
,
27
]
and Doob
[
5
]
by using analytic and probabilistic methods, respectively. More precisely,
Watson
[
26
,
27
]
developed the relevant potential theory and established several different characterizations of
the polar sets in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
for the heat equation, and Doob
[
5
]
showed that a set
G\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
is polar for the
heat equation if and only if the Markov process
\{(B(t),t):t\geq 0\}
hits
G
with zero probability from every starting point.
Taylor and Watson
[
25
]
provided a necessary condition and a sufficient condition for
{\mathbb{P}}(X(E)\cap F\neq\varnothing)>0

in terms of the Hausdorff dimension of
E\times F
under a parabolic metric on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
.
When
E
is an interval in
(0,\infty)^{N}
, Question (1) for the Brownian sheet was resolved by Khoshnevisan and Shi in their
seminal paper
[
15
]
. Dalang and Nualart
[
7
]
extended the results in
[
15
]
to the solutions to a class of
nonlinear hyperbolic stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) in the plane. Further results about hitting probabilities for
other Gaussian random fields or SPDEs for the special case that
E\subset(0,\infty)^{N}
is an interval can be found in
[
2
,
6
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
21
,
28
]
. However, a complete answer to Question (1) had not been obtained for any random fields.

For the Brownian motion, Question (2) was answered by Khoshnevisan and Xiao
[
20
]
, for all
d\geq 1
, in terms of
thermal energy (see Definition
1.1
below) and, for
d\geq 2
, also in terms of the Hausdorff dimension of
E\times F

under the parabolic metric. As a consequence, they also obtained in
[
20
, Proposition 1.4]
a criterion for
{\mathbb{P}}(B(E)\cap F\neq\varnothing)>0
that is slightly simpler than the one in
[
5
,
26
,
27
]
.

In this paper, we extend the results in
[
5
,
26
,
27
,
20
]
from the Brownian motion to the Brownian sheet
W=\{W(t):t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\}
.
Our main results are Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
, which provide answers to Questions (1) and (2) in terms of the
thermal capacity in (
1.1
) for the Brownian sheet. A more explicit expression for the Hausdorff dimension of the intersection

W(E)\cap F
is given in Theorem
1.4
. We hope that the arguments developed in this paper
will be useful for answering Questions (1) and (2) for other random fields, such as the solution to hyperbolic SPDEs in
[
7
]
.

Let
W=\{W(t):t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\}
be an
(N,d)
-Brownian sheet defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},{\mathbb{P}})
, that is,
W
is an
N
-parameter,
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued continuous Gaussian random field with mean zero
and covariance

{\mathbb{E}}(W_{i}(s)W_{j}(t))=\begin{cases}\prod_{k=1}^{N}\min\{s_{k},t_{k}\}&\text{ if }i=j\\
0&\text{ if }i\neq j.\end{cases}

To study Questions (1) and (2) for
W
, we will make use of the following notions of thermal capacity and energy, which are natural
extensions of (1.7) and (3.6) in
[
20
]
for
N=1
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
and
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be compact sets.
For
\gamma\geq 0
, the
\gamma
-thermal capacity of the set
E\times F
is defined by

(1.1)

C_{\gamma}(E\times F)=\frac{1}{\inf\{\mathscr{E}_{\gamma}(\mu):\mu\in P_{0}(E\times F)\}},

where
P_{0}(E\times F)
denotes the set of all Borel probability measures
\mu
on
E\times F
such that
\mu(\{t\}\times F)=0

for all
t\in E
and
\mathscr{E}_{\gamma}(\mu)
is the
\gamma
-thermal energy of
\mu
defined by

(1.2)

\mathscr{E}_{\gamma}(\mu)=\int_{E\times F}\int_{E\times F}\frac{e^{-\|x-y\|^{2}/(2\|s-t\|)}}{\|s-t\|^{d/2}\|x-y\|^{\gamma}}\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy).

Throughout the paper,
\|\cdot\|
denotes the Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
or
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Let
\rho
denote the parabolic metric on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined by

(1.3)

\rho((s,x),(t,y))=\max\{\|s-t\|^{1/2},\|x-y\|\}.

The thermal capacity
C_{\gamma}(E\times F)
is related to the parabolic Hausdorff dimension
{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(E\times F;\rho)
of

E\times F
as well as the Hausdorff or packing dimensions of
E
and
F
(see Section 2 below).
We denote Hausdorff and packing dimensions by
{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}
and
\dim_{{}_{\rm P}}
, respectively, and refer to the excellent
books by Falconer
[
12
]
and Mattila
[
22
]
for their definitions and properties.

When
F
has positive Lebesgue measure, it is obvious that
{\mathbb{P}}(W(E)\cap F\neq\varnothing)>0
for every nonempty Borel
set
E\subset(0,\infty)^{N}
. When
F
has Lebesgue measure zero, we obtain a necessary and sufficient condition for

{\mathbb{P}}(W(E)\cap F\neq\varnothing)>0
in the following theorem, which gives an answer to Question (1) for the Brownian sheet.

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix
0<a<b<\infty
.
Then, there exists
K=K(N,d,a,b)\in(1,\infty)
such that for all compact sets
E\subset(a,b)^{N}
and
F\subset\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\|x\|<b\}
,
where
F
has Lebesgue measure zero, we have

(1.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K}C_{0}(E\times F)\leq{\mathbb{P}}(W(E)\cap F\neq\varnothing)\leq KC_{0}(E\times F).

As a consequence,
{\mathbb{P}}(W(E)\cap F\neq\varnothing)>0
if and only if
C_{0}(E\times F)>0
, and we have

(1.5)

{\mathbb{P}}(W(E)\cap F\neq\varnothing)\begin{cases}>0&\text{ if }{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(E\times F;\rho)>d,\\
=0&\text{ if }{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(E\times F;\rho)<d.\end{cases}

When
W
is
d
-dimensional Brownian motion, (
1.5
) was proved by Taylor and Watson
[
25
]
.
Unlike the case of Brownian motion, which has
stationary and independent increments, we need to consider the pinned Brownian sheet introduced in
[
19
]
and make use of its properties to prove this result.
The commuting property of the filtration associated with the Brownian sheet is also needed in order to apply Cairoli’s maximal inequality
in the proof.

The Hausdorff dimension of
W(E)\cap F
is a random variable that is in general nonconstant on the event
\{W(E)\cap F\neq\varnothing\}
, as was pointed out in
[
20
]
.
To answer Question (2), we compute the
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{P}})
-norm of
{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(W(E)\cap F)
.
When
F
has positive Lebesgue measure, a simple formula is available (see Proposition
5.1
below):

\|{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(W(E)\cap F)\|_{\infty}=\min\{d,2{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}{E}\}.

In general, we have the following result:

Theorem 1.3
.

For any compact sets
E\subset(0,\infty)^{N}
and
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, regardless of whether
F
has zero or positive Lebesgue measure, we have

(1.6)

\|{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(W(E)\cap F)\|_{\infty}=\sup\{\gamma>0:C_{\gamma}(E\times F)>0\}.

This result extends Theorem 1.3 of
[
20
]
.
The proof of (
1.6
) is based on a codimension argument and a hitting probability result for additive stable processes.
The codimension argument allows us to find the Hausdorff dimension of
W(E)\cap F
by checking whether this set intersects the range of an
n
-parameter
d
-dimensional additive
\alpha
-stable process
X
independent of
W
.
This reduces the proof of Theorem
1.3
to determining when
{\mathbb{P}}(W(E)\cap F\cap X(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})\neq\varnothing)
is positive (see Proposition
4.4
below).

The formula (
1.6
) holds
in all dimensions
N\geq 1
and
d\geq 1
. Our next result provides an alternative formula when
d\geq 2N
, and extends Theorem 1.1 of
[
20
]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

If
d\geq 2N
, then

(1.7)

\|{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(W(E)\cap F)\|_{\infty}={\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(E\times F;\rho)-d

for any compact sets
E\subset(0,\infty)^{N}
and
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
together imply the following “parabolic Frostman theorem”.

Corollary 1.5
.

If
d\geq 2N
, then

(1.8)

\displaystyle\sup\{\gamma>0:C_{\gamma}(E\times F)>0\}={\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(E\times F;\rho)-d

for any compact sets
E\subset(0,\infty)^{N}
and
F\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

In the case that
d<2N
, (
1.7
) does not hold in general, as was pointed out in
[
20
]
for
N=1
. In this case,
Proposition
2.3
and Remark
2.4
below may be useful for evaluating or estimating the quantity

\sup\{\gamma>0:C_{\gamma}(E\times F)>0\}
in (
1.6
). It is also possible to derive a partial result by
extending the uniform dimension result of Balka and Peres
[
1
]
to the Brownian sheet and adapting the argument
in Erraoui and Hakiki
[
11
]
. Namely, we can prove that if
d<2N
and the Assouad dimension of
E
is at most
d/2
, then
(
1.7
) still holds. Since the proof of this result will require some space, and the essence of the method differs from that of
the present paper, we will provide the details elsewhere. Despite these partial results, finding an explicit formula like (
1.8
)
for this quantity when
d<2N
remains an open problem even for
N=1
(i.e.,
W
is Brownian motion).
We believe that this quantity in general cannot be determined by
{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(E\times F;\rho)
and the dimensions of
E
and
F
alone.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we establish some properties of the thermal capacity and bounds for
\sup\{\gamma>0:C_{\gamma}(E\times F)>0\}
.
In Section 3, we prove Theorem
1.2
.
In Section 4, we gather necessary ingredients for the codimension argument and study the intersection of
W(E)\cap F
with the range of an independent additive stable process.
In Section 5, we consider the Hausdorff dimension of
W(E)\cap F
and prove Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
.

Throughout this paper,
c,c_{1},c_{2},\dots
denote strictly positive and finite constants. We will use notations such as
c_{\ref{5.8}}
to
refer to the specific constant appearing in display (
3.13
), etc.

2.
Thermal capacity

In this section, we establish some properties of the thermal capacity
C_{\gamma}(E\times F)
and give bounds for the quantity

\sup\{\gamma>0:C_{\gamma}(E\times F)>0\}
in terms of the dimensions of
E
and
F
.

Recall that
\rho
denotes the parabolic metric (see (
1.3
)).
For any
\alpha\geq 0
and
A\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, the
\alpha
-dimensional parabolic Hausdorff measure of
A
is defined by

\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}(A;\rho)=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\inf\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(\mathrm{diam}_{\rho}\,U_{n})^{\alpha}:\text{open cover }(U_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\text{ of }A\,,\,\sup_{n}\,(\mathrm{diam}_{\rho}\,U_{n})\leq\delta\right\},

where
\mathrm{diam}_{\rho}\,U
denotes the diameter of
U
with respect to the metric
\rho
.
The parabolic Hausdorff dimension of
A
is defined by

{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(A;\rho)=\inf\{\alpha\geq 0:\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}(A;\rho)=0\}.

From the definition of thermal capacity (see Definition
1.1
), it follows that if
C_{\gamma}(E\times F)>0
, then

C_{\gamma^{\prime}}(E\times F)>0
for all
\gamma^{\prime}<\gamma
, and if
C_{\gamma}(E\times F)=0
, then
C_{\gamma^{\prime\prime}}(E\times F)=0
for all
\gamma^{\prime\prime}>\gamma
.

The following proposition provides a convenient connection between the thermal capacity
C_{0}
and the parabolic Hausdorff dimension.
When
{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(E\times F;\rho)=d
, either
C_{0}(E\times F)>0
or
C_{0}(E\times F)=0

could happen for some choices of
E
and
F
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be compact sets. Then

C_{0}(E\times F)\begin{cases}>0&\text{ if }{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(E\times F;\rho)>d,\\
=0&\text{ if }{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(E\times F;\rho)<d.\end{cases}

Proof.

Suppose
{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(E\times F;\rho)>d^{\prime}>d
. By Frostman’s lemma (cf.
[
22
, Theorem 8.17]
), we can find a Borel probability
measure
\mu
on
E\times F
and
C\in(0,\infty)
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mu(B_{\rho}((s,x),r))\leq Cr^{d^{\prime}}\quad\text{for all $s\in E,x\in F$ and $r>0$ small,}

where
B_{\rho}((s,x),r)
denotes the open ball centered at
(s,x)
with radius
r
with respect to the metric
\rho
.
Note that (
2.1
) implies that
\mu(\{s\}\times F)=0
for all
s\in E
.
Indeed, since
d^{\prime}>d
, for any
\varepsilon>0
,
F
can be covered by a (finite or infinite) sequence of open balls
B(x_{n},r_{n})
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

under the Euclidean metric such that
\sum r_{n}^{d^{\prime}}\leq\varepsilon
.
Then the balls
B_{\rho}((s,x_{n}),r_{n})
form a cover for
\{s\}\times F
and it follows from (
2.1
) that
\mu(\{s\}\times F)\leq\sum r_{n}^{d^{\prime}}\leq\varepsilon
.
This shows that
\mu(\{s\}\times F)=0
.
Moreover, since
E\times F
is compact, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\rho((t,y),(s,x))<2^{-n_{0}}
for all
(s,x),(t,y)\in E\times F
, and hence

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{E\times F}\int_{E\times F}\frac{1}{\max\{\|s-t\|^{1/2},\|x-y\|\}^{d}}\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\\
&\leq\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{E\times F}\left[\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t,y)\in E\times F:\\
\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\leq\rho((t,y),(s,x))<\frac{1}{2^{n}}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(dt\,dy)}{2^{-(n+1)d}}\right]\mu(ds\,dx)\\
&\leq\sum_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\int_{E\times F}2^{(n+1)d}\mu(B_{\rho}((s,x),2^{-n}))\mu(ds\,dx)<\infty,\end{split}

where the last quantity is finite because of (
2.1
) and
d^{\prime}>d
.
Note that there exists a constant
0<c<\infty
such that
e^{-x/2}\leq c\,x^{-d/2}
for all
x\geq 0
, so this together with (
2.2
) implies that

\iint\frac{e^{-\|x-y\|^{2}/(2\|s-t\|)}}{\|s-t\|^{d/2}}\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\leq\iint\frac{c\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)}{\max\{\|s-t\|^{1/2},\|x-y\|\}^{d}}<\infty

and hence
C_{0}(E\times F)>0
.

Suppose
{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(E\times F;\rho)<d
.
We assume, towards a contradiction, that
C_{0}(E\times F)>0
.
Then we can find a probability measure
\mu\in P_{0}(E\times F)
such that

\mathscr{E}_{0}(\mu)=\int_{E\times F}\int_{E\times F}\frac{e^{-\|x-y\|^{2}/(2\|s-t\|)}}{\|s-t\|^{d/2}}\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)<\infty.

Let

\displaystyle G_{1}=\left\{(s,x)\in E\times F:\sup_{n\geq 0}\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(C_{r}^{n}(s,x))}{(2^{-n}r)^{d}}>0\right\},

\displaystyle G_{2}=\left\{(s,x)\in E\times F:\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(D_{r}(s,x))}{r^{d}}>0\right\},

where

C_{r}^{n}(s,x)=\left\{(t,y)\in E\times F:\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}<\frac{\|s-t\|^{1/2}}{\|x-y\|}\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\text{ and }\|x-y\|\leq r\right\}

and

D_{r}(s,x)=\left\{(t,y)\in E\times F:\|x-y\|\leq\|s-t\|^{1/2}\leq r\right\}.

Next, we show that
\mu((E\times F)\setminus(G_{1}\cup G_{2}))=1
.
In fact, if
(s,x)\in G_{1}
, then we can find some
n\geq 0
,
a=a(n)>0
and a sequence of positive
r_{i}=r_{i}(n)\downarrow 0

such that
\mu(C_{r_{i}}^{n}(s,x))\geq a(2^{-n}r_{i})^{d}
.
Since
\mu(\{s\}\times F)=0
, we can find
0<q_{i}<r_{i}
such that
\mu(C_{q_{i},r_{i}}^{n}(s,x))\geq a(2^{-n}r_{i})^{d}/2
, where

C_{q,r}^{n}(s,x)=\left\{(t,y)\in E\times F:\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}<\frac{\|s-t\|^{1/2}}{\|x-y\|}\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\text{ and }q<\|x-y\|\leq r\right\}.

By taking a subsequence if necessary, we may assume that
r_{i+1}<q_{i}
, so that the sets
C_{q_{i},r_{i}}^{n}(s,x)
are disjoint. Then

\displaystyle\int_{E\times F}\frac{e^{-\|x-y\|^{2}/(2\|s-t\|)}}{\|s-t\|^{d/2}}\,\mu(dt\,dy)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{C_{q_{i},r_{i}}^{n}(s,x)}\frac{e^{-\|x-y\|^{2}/(2\|s-t\|)}}{\|s-t\|^{d/2}}\,\mu(dt\,dy)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-2^{n}}}{(2^{-n}r_{i})^{d}}\,\frac{a(2^{-n}r_{i})^{d}}{2}=\infty.

It follows that
\mu(G_{1})=0
because
\mathscr{E}_{0}(\mu)<\infty
implies that the integral on the left-hand side above is finite for
\mu
-a.e.
(s,x)
.

If
(s,x)\in G_{2}
, then we can find
a>0
and a sequence of positive
r_{i}\downarrow 0
such that
\mu(D_{r_{i}}(s,x))\geq ar_{i}^{d}
.
Since
\mu(\{s\}\times F)=0
, we can find
0<q_{i}<r_{i}
such that
\mu(D_{q_{i},r_{i}}(s,x))\geq ar_{i}^{d}/2
, where

D_{q,r}(s,x)=\left\{(t,y)\in E\times F:\|x-y\|\leq\|s-t\|^{1/2}\text{ and }q<\|s-t\|^{1/2}\leq r\right\}.

By taking a subsequence if needed, we may assume that
r_{i+1}<q_{i}
, so that the sets
D_{q_{i},r_{i}}(s,x)
are disjoint. Then

\displaystyle\int\frac{e^{-\|x-y\|^{2}/(2\|s-t\|)}}{\|s-t\|^{d/2}}\,\mu(dt\,dy)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{D_{q_{i},r_{i}}(s,x)}\frac{e^{-\|x-y\|^{2}/(2\|s-t\|)}}{\|s-t\|^{d/2}}\,\mu(dt\,dy)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-1/2}}{r_{i}^{d}}\,ar_{i}^{d}/2=\infty.

This implies that
\mu(G_{2})=0
.
Therefore, we have
\mu(G)=1
, where
G=(E\times F)\setminus(G_{1}\cup G_{2})
.

Fix an arbitrary
\lambda>0
. For any
0<\delta<1
, let

G_{\delta}=\left\{(s,x)\in G:\mu(C_{r}^{n}(s,x))\leq\lambda(2^{-n}r)^{d},\mu(D_{r}(s,x))\leq\lambda\,r^{d}\text{ for all }n\geq 0,r\in(0,\delta]\right\}.

Let
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be an open cover of
E\times F
(and thus of
G_{\delta}
) with
\mathrm{diam}_{\rho}\,U_{i}\leq\delta
.
Write
r_{i}=\operatorname{diam}_{\rho}U_{i}
. If
U_{i}
contains
a point
(s_{i},x_{i})
of
G_{\delta}
, then

\displaystyle U_{i}\cap(E\times F)

\displaystyle\subset\left\{(t,y)\in E\times F:\|s_{i}-t\|^{1/2}\leq r_{i},\|x_{i}-y\|\leq r_{i}\right\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{r_{i}}^{n}(s_{i},x_{i})\cup D_{r_{i}}(s_{i},x_{i})

and hence

\displaystyle\mu(U_{i}\cap(E\times F))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu(C_{r_{i}}^{n}(s_{i},x_{i}))+\mu(D_{r_{i}}(s_{i},x_{i}))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda(2^{-n}r_{i})^{d}+\lambda\,r_{i}^{d}=\lambda\,c\,r_{i}^{d}

for some constant
0<c<\infty
. It follows that

\mu(G_{\delta})\leq\sum_{i:U_{i}\cap G_{\delta}\neq\varnothing}\mu(U_{i}\cap(E\times F))\leq\lambda\,c\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(\operatorname{diam}_{\rho}U_{i})^{d}.

Take infimum over all open covers
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of
E\times F
with
\mathrm{diam}_{\rho}\,U_{i}\leq\delta
, and then
let
\delta\downarrow 0
along a sequence
\delta_{n}\downarrow 0
.
Since
G\subset\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}G_{\delta_{n}}
, we have
1=\mu(G)\leq\lambda\,c\,\mathcal{H}^{d}(E\times F;\rho)
. Since
\lambda>0

is arbitrary, we have
\mathcal{H}^{d}(E\times F;\rho)=\infty
and hence
d\leq{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(E\times F;\rho)
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be compact sets. Then

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Theorem 8.10 in

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

be compact sets.
Denote

with the convention that

If
{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(E\times F;\rho)\leq d
, then
\gamma^{*}=0
; if
{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(E\times F;\rho)>d

(2.3)

\min\{{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}F,{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}F+2{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}E-d\}\leq\gamma^{*}\leq\min\{{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}F,{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(E\times F;\rho)-d\}.

Remark 2.4
.

As a result of Propositions
2.2
and
2.3
, if
\dim_{{}_{\rm H}}E=\dim_{{}_{\rm P}}E
or
\dim_{{}_{\rm H}}F=\dim_{{}_{\rm P}}F

(e.g., this is the case if

or

is an arbitrary self-similar set, cf. Falconer

\sup\{\gamma>0:C_{\gamma}(E\times F)>0\}=\max\{0,\dim_{{}_{\rm H}}F+\min\{0,2\dim_{{}_{\rm H}}E-d\}\}.

Proof of Proposition
2.3
.

We first assume that
{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(E\times F;\rho)>d
and derive the lower bound in (
2.3
).
Consider two cases.

Case 1:
2{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}E-d>0
.
In this case, we have to show that
{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}F\leq\gamma^{*}
. If
{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}F=0
, there is nothing to prove, so we assume that
{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}F>0
.
Let
0<\gamma<{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}F
. By Frostman’s lemma, we can find Borel probability measures
\mu_{1}
on
E
and
\mu_{2}
on

F
such that

\int_{E}\int_{E}\frac{\mu_{1}(ds)\,\mu_{1}(dt)}{\|s-t\|^{d/2}}<\infty,\quad\int_{F}\int_{F}\frac{\mu_{2}(dx)\,\mu_{2}(dy)}{\|x-y\|^{\gamma}}<\infty.

Then
\mu:=\mu_{1}\otimes\mu_{2}
is a Borel probability measure on
E\times F
such that
\mu(\{s\}\times F)=0
for every

s\in E
and

\displaystyle\iint\frac{e^{-\|x-y\|/(2\|s-t\|)}}{\|s-t\|^{d/2}\|x-y\|^{\gamma}}\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\leq\int_{E}\int_{E}\frac{\mu_{1}(ds)\,\mu_{1}(dt)}{\|s-t\|^{d/2}}\int_{F}\int_{F}\frac{\mu_{2}(dx)\,\mu_{2}(dy)}{\|x-y\|^{\gamma}}<\infty.

This implies that
\gamma\leq\gamma^{*}
for all
0<\gamma<{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}F
. Hence
{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}F\leq\gamma^{*}
.

Case 2:
2{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}E-d\leq 0
.
In this case, we have to show that
\alpha:={\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}F+2{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}E-d\leq\gamma^{*}
. There is nothing to prove if
\alpha\leq 0
,
so we assume that
\alpha>0
.
Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\alpha)
be such that
{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}F+2{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}E-\varepsilon-d>0
and take
0<\gamma<{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}F+2{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}E-\varepsilon-d
.
By Frostman’s lemma, we can find Borel probability measures
\mu_{1}
on
E
and
\mu_{2}
on
F
such that

\int_{E}\int_{E}\frac{\mu_{1}(ds)\,\mu_{1}(dt)}{\|s-t\|^{{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}E-\varepsilon/2}}<\infty,\quad\int_{F}\int_{F}\frac{\mu_{2}(dx)\,\mu_{2}(dy)}{\|x-y\|^{\gamma+d-2{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}E+\varepsilon}}<\infty.

Set
\mu=\mu_{1}\otimes\mu_{2}
.
Note that there is a constant
0<c<\infty
such that

\exp\left({-\frac{\|x-y\|^{2}}{2\|s-t\|}}\right)\leq c\left(\frac{\|s-t\|}{\|x-y\|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}-{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}E+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}

for all
s,t\in E
and
x,y\in F
. It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{E\times F}\int_{E\times F}\frac{e^{-\|x-y\|^{2}/(2\|s-t\|)}}{\|s-t\|^{d/2}\|x-y\|^{\gamma}}\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,c\int_{E}\int_{E}\frac{\mu_{1}(ds)\,\mu_{1}(dt)}{\|s-t\|^{{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}E-\varepsilon/2}}\times\int_{F}\int_{F}\frac{\mu_{2}(dx)\,\mu_{2}(dy)}{\|x-y\|^{\gamma+d-2{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}E+\varepsilon}}<\infty.

So,
\gamma\leq\gamma^{*}
for all
0<\gamma<{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}F+2{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}E-\varepsilon-d
, and hence
{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}F+2{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}E-\varepsilon-d\leq\gamma^{*}
. Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
yields
{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}F+2{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}E-d\leq\gamma^{*}
.

Next, we derive the upper bound in in (
2.3
) by showing
\gamma^{*}\leq{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}F
and
\gamma^{*}\leq{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(E\times F;\rho)-d
.
If
\gamma^{*}=0
, there is nothing to prove, so we assume
\gamma^{*}>0
.
Let
0<\gamma<\gamma^{*}
, so that
\mathscr{E}_{\gamma}(\mu)<\infty
for some
\mu\in P_{0}(E\times F)
.
Let

\displaystyle G_{0}=\left\{(s,x)\in E\times F:\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(B_{r}(s,x))}{r^{\gamma}}>0\right\},

\displaystyle G_{1}=\left\{(s,x)\in E\times F:\sup_{n\geq 0}\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(C_{r}^{n}(s,x))}{(2^{-n})^{d}\,r^{d+\gamma}}>0\right\},

where

B_{r}(s,x)=\left\{(t,y)\in E\times F:\|x-y\|\leq\|s-t\|^{1/2}\text{ and }\|x-y\|\leq r\right\}

and

C_{r}^{n}(s,x)=\left\{(t,y)\in E\times F:\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}<\frac{\|s-t\|^{1/2}}{\|x-y\|}\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\text{ and }\|x-y\|\leq r\right\}.

Let us show that
\mu((E\times F)\setminus(G_{0}\cup G_{1}))=1
.
In fact, if
(s,x)\in G_{0}
, we can find
a>0
and a sequence of positive
r_{i}\downarrow 0
such that
\mu(B_{r_{i}}(s,x))\geq ar_{i}^{\gamma}
.
Note that
\mu(E\times\{x\})=0
since
\mathscr{E}_{\gamma}(\mu)<\infty
. So we can find
0<q_{i}<r_{i}
such that

\mu(B_{q_{i},r_{i}}(s,x))\geq ar_{i}^{\gamma}/2
, where

B_{q,r}(s,x)=\left\{(t,y)\in E\times F:\|x-y\|\leq\|s-t\|^{1/2}\text{ and }q<\|x-y\|\leq r\right\}.

By taking a subsequence if needed, we may assume that
r_{i+1}<q_{i}
, so that the sets
B_{q_{i},r_{i}}(s,x)
are disjoint. Moreover, since

E
is compact, there is
0<M<\infty
such that
\|t\|\leq M
for all
t\in E
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{E\times F}\frac{e^{-\|x-y\|^{2}/(2\|s-t\|)}}{\|s-t\|^{d/2}\|x-y\|^{\gamma}}\,\mu(dt\,dy)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{B_{q_{i},r_{i}}(s,x)}\frac{e^{-\|x-y\|^{2}/(2\|s-t\|)}}{\|s-t\|^{d/2}\|x-y\|^{\gamma}}\,\mu(dt\,dy)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-1/2}}{(2M)^{d/2}\,r_{i}^{\gamma}}\,ar_{i}^{\gamma}/2=\infty.

This shows that
\mu(G_{0})=0
because
\mathscr{E}_{\gamma}(\mu)<\infty
implies that the above integral is finite for
\mu
-a.e.
(s,x)
.
Also, we can show that
\mu(G_{1})=0
as in the proof of Proposition
2.1
.
Hence, we have
\mu(G)=1
, where
G=(E\times F)\setminus(G_{0}\cup G_{1})
.

Fix an arbitrary
\lambda>0
.
For each
0<\delta<1
, let

G_{\delta}=\left\{(s,x)\in G:\mu(B_{r}(s,x))\leq\lambda\,r^{\gamma},\mu(C_{r}^{n}(s,x))\leq\lambda(2^{-n})^{d}\,r^{d+\gamma}\text{ for all }n\geq 0,r\in(0,\delta]\right\}.

Let
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be an open cover of
F
with
r_{i}:=\operatorname{diam}U_{i}\leq\delta
.
Then
\{E\times(U_{i}\cap F)\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a cover of

E\times F
, and thus of
G_{\delta}
. If
E\times(U_{i}\cap F)
contains a point
(s_{i},x_{i})
of
G_{\delta}
, then

\displaystyle E\times(U_{i}\cap F)

\displaystyle\subset\left\{(t,y)\in E\times F:\|x_{i}-y\|\leq r_{i}\right\}=B_{r_{i}}(s_{i},x_{i})\cup\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{r_{i}}^{n}(s_{i},x_{i}).

Since
\mu(\{s_{i}\}\times F)=0
and
(s_{i},x_{i})\in G_{\delta}
, we have

\displaystyle\mu(E\times(U_{i}\cap F))

\displaystyle\leq\mu(B_{r_{i}}(s_{i},x_{i}))+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu(C_{r_{i}}^{n}(s_{i},x_{i}))

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\,r_{i}^{\gamma}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda(2^{-n})^{d}\,r_{i}^{d+\gamma}\leq\lambda\,c\,r_{i}^{\gamma},

for some constant
0<c<1
.
It follows that

\mu(G_{\delta})\leq\sum_{i:(E\times U_{i})\cap G_{\delta}\neq\varnothing}\mu(E\times(U_{i}\cap F))\leq\lambda\,c\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(\operatorname{diam}U_{i})^{\gamma}.

Taking infimum over all open covers
\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of
F
with
\mathrm{diam}U_{i}\leq\delta
, and letting
\delta\downarrow 0
, we have
1=\mu(G)\leq\lambda\,c\,\mathcal{H}^{\gamma}(F)
. Since
\lambda>0
is arbitrary, we conclude that
\mathcal{H}^{\gamma}(F)=\infty
and
\gamma\leq{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}F
. Hence
\gamma^{*}\leq{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}F
.

To show
\gamma^{*}\leq{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(E\times F;\rho)-d
, we may assume
\gamma^{*}>0
. Let
0<\gamma<\gamma^{*}
so that

\mathscr{E}_{\gamma}(\mu)<\infty
for some Borel probability measure
\mu\in P_{0}(E\times F)
.
Let
G_{1}
be as in the last paragraph and let

G_{2}=\left\{(s,x)\in E\times F:\varlimsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(D_{r}(s,x))}{r^{d+\gamma}}>0\right\},

where

D_{r}(s,x)=\left\{(t,y)\in E\times F:\|x-y\|\leq\|s-t\|^{1/2}\leq r\right\}.

Then we can argue as in the second part of Proposition
2.1
to show that
\mathcal{H}^{d+\gamma}(E\times F;\rho)=\infty
and thus
d+\gamma\leq{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(E\times F;\rho)
. Hence
\gamma^{*}\leq{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(E\times F;\rho)-d
.

Finally, let us assume
{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(E\times F;\rho)\leq d
and prove that
\gamma^{*}=0
.
Suppose, towards a contradiction, that
\gamma^{*}>0
. Then we can take
0<\gamma<\gamma^{*}
so that
\mathscr{E}_{\gamma}(\mu)<\infty
for some Borel probability measure
\mu\in P_{0}(E\times F)
. Proceeding as in the last paragraph, we can show
that
d+\gamma\leq{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(E\times F;\rho)
, that is,
\gamma\leq{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(E\times F;\rho)-d\leq 0
, which is a contradiction. Hence
\gamma^{*}=0
.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
1.2
.
In order to prove this result, we recall the pinned Brownian sheet and its properties.
Readers may refer to
[
19
]
for details. We will also prove a technical lemma (Lemma
3.3
)
which is an extension of Proposition 4.2 in
[
20
]
.

For any fixed
s\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the associated pinned Brownian sheet is defined by

(3.1)

\displaystyle W_{s}(t)=W(t)-C_{s,t}W(s),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+},

where

C_{s,t}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{s_{i}\wedge t_{i}}{s_{i}}\right).

Note that
0\leq C_{s,t}\leq 1
, and
W_{s}(t)
and
W(s)
are independent because they are Gaussian and
{\mathbb{E}}[W_{s}(t)W(s)]=0
.
It is also helpful to notice that
C_{s,t}W(s)
is
{\mathbb{E}}(W(t)|W(s))
. Hence
W_{s}(t)=W(t)-{\mathbb{E}}(W(t)|W(s))
which is independent
of
W(s)
.

For each
\pi\subset\{1,\dots,N\}
, define the partial ordering
\preccurlyeq_{\pi}
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
by

s\preccurlyeq_{\pi}t\Longleftrightarrow\begin{cases}s_{i}\leq t_{i}&\text{ if }i\in\pi\\
s_{i}\geq t_{i}&\text{ if }i\notin\pi.\end{cases}

Define
\mathscr{F}_{\pi}(s)=\sigma\{W(u):u\preccurlyeq_{\pi}s\}
. Then
\{W_{s}(t):s\preccurlyeq_{\pi}t\}
is independent
of
\mathscr{F}_{\pi}(s)
(see
[
19
, Lemma 5.1]
).
The filtration
\{\mathscr{F}_{\pi}(s):s\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\}
is commuting in the sense of Cairoli, i.e., for any
s,t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
,

\mathscr{F}_{\pi}(s)
and
\mathscr{F}_{\pi}(t)
are conditionally independent given
\mathscr{F}_{\pi}(s\wedge_{\pi}t)
, where

(s\wedge_{\pi}t)_{i}:=\begin{cases}s_{i}\wedge t_{i}&\text{ if }i\in\pi\\
s_{i}\vee t_{i}&\text{ if }i\notin\pi.\end{cases}

Equivalently,
\{\mathscr{F}_{\pi}(s):s\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\}
is commuting if and only if for any
s,t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
and any
bounded
\mathscr{F}_{\pi}(t)
-measurable function
Y
,
{\mathbb{E}}(Y|\mathscr{F}_{\pi}(s))={\mathbb{E}}(Y|\mathscr{F}_{\pi}(s\wedge_{\pi}t))
.
The commuting property allows us to apply Cairoli’s maximal inequality for martingales
[
19
, Corollary 3.2]
: For any
Z\in L^{2}({\mathbb{P}})
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{t\in\mathbb{Q}^{N}_{+}}[{\mathbb{E}}(Z|\mathscr{F}_{\pi}(t))]^{2}\right)\leq 4^{N}{\mathbb{E}}(Z^{2}).

We refer to
[
14
]
for more details about commuting filtrations and Cairoli’s maximal inequalities for multiparameter processes.

For a finite Borel measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, its Fourier transform is defined by

\widehat{\nu}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\langle\xi,x\rangle}\,\nu(dx),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Note that we use the convention
e^{i\langle\xi,x\rangle}
for the Fourier transform.
This convention also applies to the Fourier transform of other functions or tempered distributions in the rest of the paper.
Recall that a function
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to[0,+\infty]
is said to be tempered if it is measurable and

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{f(x)}{(1+\|x\|)^{n}}dx<\infty\quad\text{for some $n\geq 0$}.

A function
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to[0,+\infty]
is said to be positive definite if it is tempered and for all rapidly decreasing test functions
\phi:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x-y)\phi(x)\phi(y)dydx\geq 0.

Let us recall the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
20
, Lemma 4.1]

If
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,+\infty]
is lower semicontinuous and positive definite,
then for all finite Borel measures
\rho
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x-y)\,\rho(dx)\,\rho(dy)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\,|\widehat{\rho}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi.

If
f
is in addition bounded, then for all finite Borel measures
\rho
and
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x-y)\,\rho(dx)\,\nu(dy)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\xi)\,\widehat{\rho}(\xi)\,\overline{\widehat{\nu}(\xi)}\,d\xi.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
K
be a compact set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and
\mu
be a Borel probability measure on
K
.
Then there exists a sequence of probability measures
(\mu_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
on
K
, where each
\mu_{n}
is a finite convex combination of delta measures
\delta_{x}
where
x\in K
, such that
\mu_{n}
converges weakly to
\mu
.

Proof.

By the Riesz representation theorem
[
4
, III. 5.7]
, the space
M(K)
of regular Borel signed measures on
K
with the total
variation norm can be identified as the dual space
C_{0}(K)^{*}
of
C_{0}(K)
, where
C_{0}(K)
is the Banach space of all bounded continuous
functions
f:K\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that for every
\varepsilon>0
,
\{x\in K:|f(x)|\geq\varepsilon\}
is compact,
equipped with the
\sup
norm.
Also, since
K
is a metric space, every Borel probability measure on
K
is regular (see
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
).
Hence, the set
P(K)
of all Borel probability measures on
K
satisfies
P(K)\subset M(K)\cong C_{0}(K)^{*}
.
By the Banach–Alaoglu theorem
[
4
, V. 3.1]
, the closed unit ball
B
in
M(K)\cong C_{0}(K)^{*}
is compact in the weak star topology of
C_{0}(K)^{*}
.
Note that
P(K)
is closed in the weak star topology (this is because the compactness of
K
implies
{\bf 1}_{K}\in C_{0}(K)
, so if
\mu_{n}\in P(K)
converges to
\mu
in the weak star topology, then
\mu(K)=\int{\bf 1}_{K}d\mu=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int{\bf 1}_{K}d\mu_{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(K)=1
).
Now
P(K)
, as a weak star closed subset of the weak star compact set
B
, is also weak star compact.
It follows that
P(K)
is a convex, weak star compact set in
M(K)\cong C_{0}(K)^{*}
. By the Krein–Milman theorem
[
4
, V. 7.4]
,
P(K)
is equal to the weak star closure of the convex hull of
E
, where
E
is the set of extreme points of
P(K)
.
Owing to the compactness of
K
, the weak star topology in
M(K)\cong C_{0}(K)^{*}
is metrizable
[
4
, V. 5.1]
.
Therefore, there exists a sequence
\mu_{n}
in
\mathrm{conv}(E)
that converges to
\mu
in the weak star topology of
C_{0}(K)^{*}
, which means precisely that
\mu_{n}
converges weakly to
\mu
in the context of probability measures.
Finally, since
K
is compact, the set of extreme points
E
of
P(K)
coincides with the set of delta measures
\{\delta_{x}:x\in K\}
(see
[
4
, V. 8.4]
), so each
\mu_{n}
is a finite convex combination of delta measures.
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
0<c_{1}<1<c_{2}
be constants. For distinct
s,t\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and for any
w\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, define

\displaystyle p_{s,t}(w)=\frac{e^{-\|w\|^{2}/(2\|t-s\|)}}{\|t-s\|^{d/2}},\quad q_{s,t}(w)=\frac{e^{-\|w\|^{2}/(2c_{1}\|t-s\|)}}{\|t-s\|^{d/2}},\quad

\displaystyle r_{s,t}(w)=\frac{e^{-\|w\|^{2}/(2c_{2}\|t-s\|)}}{\|t-s\|^{d/2}}.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be compact sets and
\mu\in P_{0}(E\times F)
.
Let
\phi_{1}
,
\phi_{2}\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be positive definite functions with
0\leq\widehat{\phi}_{1}\leq\widehat{\phi}_{2}
. Then

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \iint(\phi_{1}\ast p_{s,t})(x-y)\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)&\leq\iint(\phi_{2}\ast p_{s,t})(x-y)\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\end{aligned}

and

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &c_{2}^{-(1+\frac{d}{2})}\iint(\phi_{1}\ast r_{s,t})(x-y)\,\mu(ds\,dx)\mu(dt\,dy)\\
&\hskip 30.0pt\leq\iint(\phi_{1}\ast p_{s,t})(x-y)\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\\
&\hskip 60.0pt\leq c_{1}^{-(1+\frac{d}{2})}\iint(\phi_{1}\ast q_{s,t})(x-y)\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy).\end{aligned}

Proof.

Thanks to Lemma
3.2
, we can find a sequence
(\mu_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
of probability measures on
E\times F
that converges weakly to
\mu
, where each
\mu_{n}
is of the form
\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\,\delta_{s_{i}}\otimes\delta_{x_{i}}
where
s_{i}\in E
,
x_{i}\in F
,
a_{i}>0
,
\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}=1
, and
m
,
a_{i}
,
s_{i}
,
x_{i}
may depend on
n
.

Let
\nu=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\,\delta_{s_{i}}\otimes\delta_{x_{i}}
.
Define
p_{s,t}=(2\pi)^{d/2}\,\delta_{0}
when
s=t
.
By the assumptions, for fixed
s,t\in E
, the functions
x\mapsto(\phi_{1}\ast p_{s,t})(x)
and
x\mapsto(\phi_{2}\ast p_{s,t})(x)
are positive definite, bounded and continuous. By Fubini’s theorem and Lemma
3.1
, for
k=1,2
,

\displaystyle\quad\iint(\phi_{k}\ast p_{s,t})(x-y)\,\nu(ds\,dx)\,\nu(dt\,dy)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}a_{i}a_{j}\iint\left[\iint(\phi_{k}\ast p_{s,t})(x-y)\,\delta_{x_{i}}(dx)\,\delta_{x_{j}}(dy)\right]\delta_{s_{i}}(ds)\,\delta_{s_{j}}(dt)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}a_{i}a_{j}\iint\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{\phi}_{k}(\xi)\,\widehat{p}_{s,t}(\xi)\,\widehat{\delta}_{x_{i}}(\xi)\,\overline{\widehat{\delta}_{x_{j}}(\xi)}\,d\xi\right]\delta_{s_{i}}(ds)\,\delta_{s_{j}}(dt).

Note that
\widehat{p}_{s,t}(\xi)=(2\pi)^{d/2}\,P_{\xi}(s-t)
, where
P_{\xi}(s)=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\|\xi\|^{2}\|s\|}
. This holds even
when
s=t
. Then by Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\quad\iint(\phi_{k}\ast p_{s,t})(x-y)\,\nu(ds\,dx)\,\nu(dt\,dy)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}a_{i}a_{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\iint P_{\xi}(s-t)\,\delta_{s_{i}}(ds)\,\delta_{s_{j}}(dt)\right]\widehat{\phi}_{k}(\xi)\,\widehat{\delta}_{x_{i}}(\xi)\,\overline{\widehat{\delta}_{x_{j}}(\xi)}\,d\xi.

Observe that
s\mapsto P_{\xi}(s)
is the characteristic function of a multivariate Cauchy distribution, which is positive definite.
Indeed, for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\widehat{P}_{\xi}(\eta)=\frac{(2\pi)^{N}K\cdot\frac{1}{2}\|\xi\|^{2}}{(\frac{1}{4}\|\xi\|^{4}+\|\eta\|^{2})^{\frac{N+1}{2}}},\quad\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
K=\pi^{-\frac{N+1}{2}}\,\Gamma(\frac{N+1}{2})
(see e.g.
[
24
, Example 2.5.3]
).
Then by Lemma
3.1
again,

\displaystyle\quad\iint(\phi_{k}\ast p_{s,t})(x-y)\,\nu(ds\,dx)\,\nu(dt\,dy)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N+\frac{d}{2}}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}a_{i}a_{j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\widehat{P}_{\xi}(\eta)\,\widehat{\delta}_{s_{i}}(\eta)\,\overline{\widehat{\delta}_{s_{j}}(\eta)}\,d\eta\right]\widehat{\phi}_{k}(\xi)\,\widehat{\delta}_{x_{i}}(\xi)\,\overline{\widehat{\delta}_{x_{j}}(\xi)}\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N+\frac{d}{2}}}\iint\widehat{\phi}_{k}(\xi)\,\widehat{P}_{\xi}(\eta)\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\,\widehat{\delta}_{s_{i}}(\eta)\widehat{\delta}_{x_{i}}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi\,d\eta.

This together with
\widehat{P}_{\xi}(\eta)\geq 0
and
0\leq\widehat{\phi}_{1}(\xi)\leq\widehat{\phi}_{2}(\xi)
implies that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\iint(\phi_{1}\ast p_{s,t})(x-y)\,\mu_{n}(ds\,dx)\,\mu_{n}(dt\,dy)\leq\iint(\phi_{2}\ast p_{s,t})(x-y)\,\mu_{n}(ds\,dx)\,\mu_{n}(dt\,dy)

for all
n
.
For
k=1,2
, since
\phi_{k}
is bounded and uniformly continuous, the function

\displaystyle(s,x,t,y)\in(E\times F)^{2}\mapsto(\phi_{k}\ast p_{s,t})(x-y)

is continuous. Hence, by the convergence of
\mu_{n}
, we can let
n\to\infty
in (
3.6
) to obtain (
3.3
).

To prove (
3.4
), define
q_{s,t}=(2\pi c_{1})^{d/2}\,\delta_{0}
,
r_{s,t}=(2\pi c_{2})^{d/2}\,\delta_{0}
when
s=t
, and notice that

\widehat{q}_{s,t}(\xi)=(2\pi)^{d/2}\,Q_{\xi}(s-t)
and
\widehat{r}_{s,t}(\xi)=(2\pi)^{d/2}\,R_{\xi}(s-t)
, where

\displaystyle Q_{\xi}(s)=c_{1}^{d/2}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}c_{1}\|\xi\|^{2}\|s\|},\quad R_{\xi}(s)=c_{2}^{d/2}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}c_{2}\|\xi\|^{2}\|s\|}

for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},s\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
As in (
3.5
), for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,

\displaystyle\widehat{Q}_{\xi}(\eta)=c_{1}^{1+\frac{d}{2}}\,\frac{(2\pi)^{N}K\cdot\frac{1}{2}\|\xi\|^{2}}{(\frac{1}{4}c_{1}^{2}\|\xi\|^{4}+\|\eta\|^{2})^{\frac{N+1}{2}}},\quad\widehat{R}_{\xi}(\eta)=c_{2}^{1+\frac{d}{2}}\,\frac{(2\pi)^{N}K\cdot\frac{1}{2}\|\xi\|^{2}}{(\frac{1}{4}c_{2}^{2}\|\xi\|^{4}+\|\eta\|^{2})^{\frac{N+1}{2}}}

for
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Setting
\nu=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\,\delta_{s_{i}}\otimes\delta_{x_{i}}
, we can compute as before to get

\displaystyle\iint(\phi_{1}\ast q_{s,t})(x-y)\nu(ds\,dx)\nu(dt\,dy)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N+\frac{d}{2}}}\iint\widehat{\phi}_{1}(\xi)\widehat{Q}_{\xi}(\eta)\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\widehat{\delta}_{s_{i}}(\eta)\widehat{\delta}_{x_{i}}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi\,d\eta

and

\displaystyle\iint(\phi_{1}\ast r_{s,t})(x-y)\nu(ds\,dx)\nu(dt\,dy)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N+\frac{d}{2}}}\iint\widehat{\phi}_{1}(\xi)\widehat{R}_{\xi}(\eta)\left|\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\widehat{\delta}_{s_{i}}(\eta)\widehat{\delta}_{x_{i}}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi\,d\eta.

Since
0<c_{1}<1<c_{2}
, we have

0\leq c_{2}^{-(1+\frac{d}{2})}\,\widehat{R}_{\xi}(\eta)\leq\widehat{P}_{\xi}(\eta)\leq c_{1}^{-(1+\frac{d}{2})}\,\widehat{Q}_{\xi}(\eta).

Again we can use the arguments in the first part to deduce (
3.4
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Throughout the proof, for distinct
s,t\in E
, we denote

(3.7)

\displaystyle p_{s,t}(w)=\frac{e^{-\|w\|^{2}/(2\|s-t\|)}}{\|t-s\|^{d/2}},\quad w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Part 1: Lower bound.

Let
\mu\in P_{0}(E\times F)
.
Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
f_{\varepsilon}=|B(0,\varepsilon)|^{-1}{\bf 1}_{B(0,\varepsilon)}
, where
|\cdots|
denotes Lebesgue measure, and let
\phi_{\varepsilon}=f_{\varepsilon}\ast f_{\varepsilon}
.
Define

(3.8)

\displaystyle Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu)=\int_{E\times F}\phi_{\varepsilon}(W(s)-x)\,\mu(ds\,dx).

Let
F^{\varepsilon}
denote the
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of
F
, that is,

(3.9)

\displaystyle F^{\varepsilon}=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\inf_{y\in F}\|x-y\|<\varepsilon\right\}.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

(3.10)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(W(E)\cap F^{\varepsilon}\neq\varnothing)\geq{\mathbb{P}}(Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu)>0)\geq\frac{{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu)]^{2}}{{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu)^{2}]}.

Note that

(3.11)

\displaystyle 2^{-d}f_{\varepsilon/2}\leq\phi_{\varepsilon}\leq 2^{d}f_{2\varepsilon}.

By (
3.11
) and the definition of
W_{s}(t)
in (
3.1
),

(3.12)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu)^{2}]&=\iint{\mathbb{E}}[\phi_{\varepsilon}(W(s)-x)\phi_{\varepsilon}(W(t)-y)]\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\\
&\leq 4^{d}\iint{\mathbb{E}}[f_{2\varepsilon}(W(s)-x)f_{2\varepsilon}(W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}W(s)-y)]\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy).\end{aligned}

By
[
19
, (6.17) and (5.10)]
, there exist constants
c_{\ref{5.8}}
,
c_{\ref{5.9}}^{\prime}>1
and
0<c_{\ref{5.9}}<1
, depending
on
a,b
and
N
only, such that

(3.13)

\displaystyle 1-C_{s,t}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\ref{5.8}}\,\|t-s\|^{1/2};

(3.14)

\displaystyle c_{\ref{5.9}}\,\|t-s\|

\displaystyle\leq\sigma_{s,t}^{2}\leq c_{\ref{5.9}}^{\prime}\,\|t-s\|.

Note that
\|W(s)-x\|\leq 2\varepsilon
and
\|W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}W(s)-y\|\leq 2\varepsilon
imply
\|W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}x-y\|\leq\|W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}W(s)-y\|+\|C_{s,t}(W(s)-x)\|\leq 4\varepsilon
.
This together with independence of
W(s)
and
W_{s}(t)
and (
3.11
) imply that

(3.15)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &{\mathbb{E}}[f_{2\varepsilon}(W(s)-x)f_{2\varepsilon}(W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}W(s)-y)]\\
&\quad\leq c_{\ref{7.9}}{\mathbb{E}}[f_{2\varepsilon}(W(s)-x)]{\mathbb{E}}[\phi_{8\varepsilon}(W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}x-y)].\end{aligned}

Moreover, we have

(3.16)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\mathbb{E}}[f_{2\varepsilon}(W(s)-x)]\leq(2\pi a^{N})^{-d/2}.\end{aligned}

It follows that

(3.17)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu)^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq c_{\ref{7.12}}\iint\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi_{8\varepsilon}(w+C_{s,t}x-y)\frac{1}{\|t-s\|^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\|w\|^{2}}{2\,c_{\ref{5.9}}^{\prime}\|t-s\|}\right)dw\right]\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)

\displaystyle=c_{\ref{7.12}}\iint\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi_{8\varepsilon}(w^{\prime})\frac{1}{\|t-s\|^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\|w^{\prime}+y-C_{s,t}x\|^{2}}{2\,c_{\ref{5.9}}^{\prime}\|t-s\|}\right)dw^{\prime}\right]\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)

for some constant
c_{\ref{7.12}}>0
independent of
\varepsilon
.
By the triangle inequality and (
3.13
), there is a constant
c_{\ref{7.13}}>0
such that for all
x\in F
,

(3.18)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|w^{\prime}+y-x\|^{2}\\
&\leq\Big(\|w^{\prime}+y-C_{s,t}x\|+\|(1-C_{s,t})x\|\Big)^{2}\\
&\leq\Big(\|w^{\prime}+y-C_{s,t}x\|+c_{\ref{7.13}}\|t-s\|^{1/2}\Big)^{2}\\
&=\|w^{\prime}+y-C_{s,t}x\|^{2}+c_{\ref{7.13}}^{2}\|t-s\|+2\,c_{\ref{7.13}}\,\|w^{\prime}+y-C_{s,t}x\|\,\|t-s\|^{1/2}.\end{aligned}

Now, consider two cases.
If (i)
\|w^{\prime}+y-C_{s,t}x\|>\|t-s\|^{1/2}
, then

(3.19)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \exp\left(-\frac{\|w^{\prime}+y-C_{s,t}x\|^{2}}{2\,c_{\ref{5.9}}^{\prime}\|t-s\|}\right)&=\exp\left(-\frac{(1+c_{\ref{7.13}}^{2}+2\,c_{\ref{7.13}})\|w^{\prime}+y-C_{s,t}x\|^{2}}{2(1+c_{\ref{7.13}}^{2}+2\,c_{\ref{7.13}})\,c_{\ref{5.9}}^{\prime}\|t-s\|}\right)\\
&\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\|w^{\prime}+y-x\|^{2}}{2\,c_{\ref{7.14}}\|t-s\|}\right),\end{aligned}

where
c_{\ref{7.14}}=(1+c_{\ref{7.13}}^{2}+2\,c_{\ref{7.13}})\,c_{\ref{5.9}}^{\prime}
.
Note that we have
c_{\ref{7.14}}>c_{\ref{5.9}}^{\prime}>1
.
On the other hand, if (ii)
\|w^{\prime}+y-C_{s,t}x\|\leq\|t-s\|^{1/2}
, then

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{\|w^{\prime}+y-C_{s,t}x\|^{2}}{2\,c_{\ref{5.9}}^{\prime}\|t-s\|}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\|w^{\prime}+y-x\|^{2}}{2\,c_{\ref{5.9}}^{\prime}\|t-s\|}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{(c_{\ref{7.13}}^{2}+2\,c_{\ref{7.13}})\|t-s\|}{2\,c_{\ref{5.9}}^{\prime}\|t-s\|}\right)

(3.20)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\|w^{\prime}+y-x\|^{2}}{2\,c_{\ref{7.14}}\|t-s\|}\right).

It follows from (
3.17
) and the above that

(3.21)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu)^{2}]&\leq c_{\ref{7.12}}\iint\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi_{8\varepsilon}(w^{\prime})\,r_{s,t}(w^{\prime}+y-x)\,dw^{\prime}\right]\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\\
&=c_{\ref{7.12}}\iint(\phi_{8\varepsilon}\ast r_{s,t})(x-y)\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy),\end{aligned}

where

(3.22)

\displaystyle r_{s,t}(w)=\frac{1}{\|t-s\|^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\|w\|^{2}}{2c_{\ref{7.14}}\|t-s\|}\right),\quad w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Now, we may apply Lemma
3.3
followed by Lemma
3.1
and the fact that
\widehat{\phi}_{8\varepsilon}=|\widehat{f}_{8\varepsilon}|^{2}\leq 1
in order to obtain

(3.23)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu)^{2}]&\leq c_{\ref{7.18}}\iint(\phi_{8\varepsilon}\ast p_{s,t})(x-y)\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\\
&\leq c_{\ref{7.18}}\iint p_{s,t}(x-y)\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\end{aligned}

for some constant
c_{\ref{7.18}}>0
independent of
\varepsilon
.
On the other hand,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu))

\displaystyle=\int\left[\int_{\|w\|\leq b+2}\phi_{\varepsilon}(w-x)\frac{1}{(2\pi\prod_{i=1}^{N}s_{i})^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\|w\|^{2}}{2\prod_{i=1}^{N}s_{i}}\right)\,dw\right]\mu(ds\,dx)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{(2\pi b^{N})^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{(b+2)^{2}}{2a^{N}}\right)\int\left[\int_{\|w\|\leq b+2}\phi_{\varepsilon}(w-x)\,dw\right]\mu(ds\,dx)

(3.24)

\displaystyle=c_{\ref{7.19}}>0,

where
c_{\ref{7.19}}
is a constant independent of
\varepsilon
.
Hence from (
3.10
), we have

(3.25)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\mathbb{P}}(W(E)\cap F^{\varepsilon}\neq\varnothing)&\geq c_{\ref{7.19}}^{\,2}\,c_{\ref{7.18}}^{-1}\left[\iint p_{s,t}(x-y)\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\right]^{-1}\\
&=c_{\ref{7.19}}^{\,2}\,c_{\ref{7.18}}^{-1}\,\mathscr{E}_{0}(\mu)^{-1}.\end{aligned}

Letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
yields

(3.26)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\mathbb{P}}(W(E)\cap F\neq\varnothing)&\geq c_{\ref{7.19}}^{\,2}\,c_{\ref{7.18}}^{-1}\,\mathscr{E}_{0}(\mu)^{-1}.\end{aligned}

Hence, we may take supremum over all
\mu\in P_{0}(E\times F)
to obtain the lower bound in (
1.4
).

Part 2: Upper bound.

Clearly, we may assume that
{\mathbb{P}}(W(E)\cap F\neq\varnothing)>0
(otherwise, the upper bound in (
1.4
) holds trivially).
With the convention
\inf\varnothing=\infty
, define

(3.27)

\displaystyle\tau_{1}=\inf\{t_{1}:\exists\,t_{2},\dots,t_{N}\text{ such that }t=(t_{1},\dots,t_{N})\in E\text{ and }W(t)\in F\}.

If
\tau_{1}=\infty
, then define
\tau_{i}=\infty
for all
2\leq i\leq N
. If
\tau_{1}\neq\infty
, then define

(3.28)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tau_{i}=\inf\{t_{i}:\,&\exists\,t_{i+1},\dots,t_{N}\text{ such that }\\
&(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{i-1},t_{i},\dots,t_{N})\in E\text{ and }W(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{i-1},t_{i},\dots,t_{N})\in F\}\end{split}

inductively for
i=2,\dots,N
.
Let
\tau=(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{N})
.
Note that
W(\tau)\in F
on the event
G:=\{\tau_{i}\neq\infty\text{ for all }i\}
.
Since
{\mathbb{P}}(G)={\mathbb{P}}(W(E)\cap F\neq\varnothing)>0
, we can define a Borel probability measure
\mu
on
E\times F
by

(3.29)

\displaystyle\mu(A)={\mathbb{P}}\{(\tau,W(\tau))\in A\mid G\}.

Since
F
has Lebesgue measure zero,
\mu(\{t\}\times F)=0
for all
t\in E
, i.e.,
\mu\in P_{0}(E\times F)
.
Our goal is to show that
{\mathbb{P}}(W(E)\cap F\neq\varnothing)\leq K\mathscr{E}_{0}(\mu)^{-1}
for some
K>1
that depends only on
(N,d,a,b)
.
To this end, let

(3.30)

\displaystyle\phi_{\varepsilon}(w)=\frac{1}{(2\pi\varepsilon^{2})^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\|w\|^{2}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\right),\quad w\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(3.31)

\displaystyle Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu)=\int_{E\times F}\phi_{\varepsilon}(W(t)-y)\,\mu(dt\,dy).

Consider an arbitrary
s\in[a,b]^{N}
and
\pi\subset\{1,\dots,N\}
.
We have

(3.32)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu)|\mathscr{F}_{\pi}(s)]&=\int{\mathbb{E}}[\phi_{\varepsilon}(W(t)-y)|\mathscr{F}_{\pi}(s)]\,\mu(dt\,dy)\\
&\geq\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\succcurlyeq_{\pi}s,\\
t\neq s\,\,\,\,\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{E}}[\phi_{\varepsilon}(W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}W(s)-y)|\mathscr{F}_{\pi}(s)]\,\mu(dt\,dy).\end{aligned}

Let

(3.33)

\displaystyle\tilde{q}_{s,t}(w)=\frac{1}{\|t-s\|^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\|w\|^{2}}{2\,c_{\ref{5.9}}\|t-s\|}\right),\quad w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

By the independence of
\{W_{s}(t):t\succcurlyeq_{\pi}s\}
and
\mathscr{F}_{\pi}(s)
, and (
3.14
),

(3.34)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu)|\mathscr{F}_{\pi}(s)]\\
&\geq c_{\ref{5.18}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\succcurlyeq_{\pi}s\\
t\neq s\end{subarray}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi_{\varepsilon}(w-(y-C_{s,t}W(s)))\,\tilde{q}_{s,t}(w)\,dw\right]\mu(dt\,dy)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\|W(s)\|\leq b\}}\\
&=c_{\ref{5.18}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\succcurlyeq_{\pi}s\\
t\neq s\end{subarray}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi_{\varepsilon}(w^{\prime})\,\tilde{q}_{s,t}(w^{\prime}+y-C_{s,t}W(s))\,dw^{\prime}\right]\mu(dt\,dy)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\|W(s)\|\leq b\}}.\end{aligned}

By the triangle inequality and (
3.13
), we can find constants
c_{\ref{7.28}},c_{\ref{7.28}}^{\prime}>0
such that

(3.35)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\quad\|w^{\prime}+y-C_{s,t}W(s)\|^{2}\\
&\leq\|w^{\prime}+y-W(s)\|^{2}+\|(1-C_{s,t})W(s)\|^{2}+2\|w^{\prime}+y-W(s)\|\|(1-C_{s,t})W(s)\|\\
&\leq\|w^{\prime}+y-W(s)\|^{2}+c_{\ref{7.28}}\|t-s\|+c_{\ref{7.28}}^{\prime}\|w^{\prime}+y-W(s)\|\|t-s\|^{1/2}.\end{aligned}

Consider the following two cases. If (i)
\|w^{\prime}+y-W(s)\|\leq\|t-s\|^{1/2}
, then

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{\|w^{\prime}+y-C_{s,t}W(s)\|^{2}}{2\,c_{\ref{5.9}}\|t-s\|}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\exp\left(-\frac{\|w^{\prime}+y-W(s)\|^{2}}{2\,c_{\ref{5.9}}\|t-s\|}-\frac{(c_{\ref{7.28}}+c_{\ref{7.28}}^{\prime})\|t-s\|}{2\,c_{\ref{5.9}}\|t-s\|}\right)

(3.36)

\displaystyle\geq c_{\ref{E:3.35}}\exp\left(-\frac{\|w^{\prime}+y-W(s)\|^{2}}{2\,c_{\ref{5.9}}\|t-s\|}\right).

If (ii)
\|w^{\prime}+y-W(s)\|>\|t-s\|^{1/2}
, then

(3.37)

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{\|w^{\prime}+y-C_{s,t}W(s)\|^{2}}{2\,c_{\ref{5.9}}\|t-s\|}\right)\geq\exp\left(-\frac{(1+c_{\ref{7.28}}+c_{\ref{7.28}}^{\prime})\|w^{\prime}+y-W(s)\|^{2}}{2\,c_{\ref{5.9}}\|t-s\|}\right).

Hence, we have

(3.38)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sup_{s\in[a,b]^{N}\cap{\mathbb{Q}}^{N}}{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu)|\mathscr{F}_{\pi}(s)]\\
&\geq c_{\ref{5.22}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\succcurlyeq_{\pi}s\\
t\neq s\end{subarray}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi_{\varepsilon}(w^{\prime})\,q_{s,t}(w^{\prime}+y-W(s))\,dw^{\prime}\right]\mu(dt\,dy)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\|W(s)\|\leq b\}}\\
&=c_{\ref{5.22}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\succcurlyeq_{\pi}s\\
t\neq s\end{subarray}}(\phi_{\varepsilon}\ast q_{s,t})(W(s)-y)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\|W(s)\|\leq b\}}\end{aligned}

uniformly for all
s\in[a,b]^{N}\cap{\mathbb{Q}}^{N}
and
\pi\subset\{1,\dots,N\}
, where

(3.39)

\displaystyle q_{s,t}(w)=\frac{1}{\|t-s\|^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\|w\|^{2}}{2c_{\ref{7.32}}\|t-s\|}\right)

with
c_{\ref{7.32}}=c_{\ref{5.9}}/(1+c_{\ref{7.28}}+c_{\ref{7.28}}^{\prime})<1
since
c_{\ref{5.9}}<1
.

On the event
G
, we have
\tau\in E\subset[a,b]^{N}
and
\|W(\tau)\|<b
.
Also,
s\mapsto(\phi_{\varepsilon}\ast q_{s,t})(W(s)-y)
is continuous. By choosing a random sequence
(s_{m})
in

[a,b]^{N}\cap\mathbb{Q}^{N}
converging to
\tau
, and by applying (
3.38
) and Fatou’s lemma, we obtain

(3.40)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sup_{s\in[a,b]^{N}\cap\,\mathbb{Q}^{N}}{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu)|\mathscr{F}_{\pi}(s)]\\
&\geq c_{\ref{5.22}}\liminf_{m\to\infty}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\succcurlyeq_{\pi}s_{m}\\
t\neq s_{m}\end{subarray}}(\phi_{\varepsilon}\ast q_{s_{m},t})(W(s_{m})-y)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\|W(s_{m})\|\leq b\}}\\
&\geq c_{\ref{5.22}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\succcurlyeq_{\pi}\tau\\
t\neq\tau\end{subarray}}(\phi_{\varepsilon}\ast q_{\tau,t})(W(\tau)-y)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\,{\bf 1}_{G}.\end{aligned}

Squaring both sides, taking expectations, and then using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we get that

(3.41)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{s\in[a,b]^{N}\cap\,\mathbb{Q}^{N}}{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu)|\mathscr{F}_{\pi}(s)]^{2}\right)\\
&\geq c_{\ref{5.22}}\,{\mathbb{P}}(G)\int\left(\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\succcurlyeq_{\pi}s\\
t\neq s\end{subarray}}(\phi_{\varepsilon}\ast q_{s,t})(x-y)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\right)^{2}\mu(ds\,dx)\\
&\geq c_{\ref{5.22}}\,{\mathbb{P}}(G)\left(\iint_{t\succcurlyeq_{\pi}s}(\phi_{\varepsilon}\ast q_{s,t})(x-y)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\,\mu(ds\,dx)\right)^{2}\\
&=c_{\ref{5.22}}\,{\mathbb{P}}(G)\,J_{\varepsilon,\pi}^{\,2},\end{aligned}

where

(3.42)

\displaystyle J_{{\varepsilon},\pi}=\iint_{t\succcurlyeq_{\pi}s}(\phi_{\varepsilon}\ast q_{s,t})(x-y)\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy).

Next, we establish an upper bound for the expectation in (
3.41
). Using Cairoli’s maximal inequality (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{s\in[a,b]^{N}\cap\,\mathbb{Q}^{N}}{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu)|\mathscr{F}_{\pi}(s)]^{2}\right)\leq 4^{N}{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu)^{2}]

(3.43)

\displaystyle=4^{N}\iint{\mathbb{E}}[\phi_{\varepsilon}(W(s)-x)\phi_{\varepsilon}(W(t)-y)]\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)

\displaystyle=4^{N}\sum_{\pi\subset\{1,\dots,N\}}\iint_{t\succcurlyeq_{\pi}s}{\mathbb{E}}[\phi_{\varepsilon}(W(s)-x)\phi_{\varepsilon}(W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}W(s)-y)]\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy).

We claim that

(3.44)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &{\mathbb{E}}[\phi_{\varepsilon}(W(s)-x)\phi_{\varepsilon}(W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}W(s)-y)]\\
&\quad\quad\leq c_{\ref{7.37}}\,{\mathbb{E}}[\phi_{2\varepsilon}(W(s)-x)\phi_{2\varepsilon}(W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}x-y)]\end{aligned}

for some constant
c_{\ref{7.37}}>0
independent of
\varepsilon
.
It suffices to show that

(3.45)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\exp\left(-\frac{\|W(s)-x\|^{2}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{\|W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}W(s)-y\|^{2}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\\
&\quad\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\|W(s)-x\|^{2}}{2(2\varepsilon)^{2}}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{\|W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}x-y\|^{2}}{2(2\varepsilon)^{2}}\right).\end{aligned}

To prove this, note that by the triangle inequality, we have

(3.46)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}x-y\|^{2}\leq\|W_{s}(t)+&C_{s,t}W(s)-y\|^{2}+\|C_{s,t}(W(s)-x)\|^{2}\\
&+2\|W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}W(s)-y\|\|C_{s,t}(W(s)-x)\|.\end{aligned}

Now consider two cases.
If (i)
\|W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}W(s)-y\|\leq\|C_{s,t}(W(s)-x)\|
, then

(3.47)

\displaystyle\|W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}x-y\|^{2}\leq\|W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}W(s)-y\|^{2}+3\|C_{s,t}(W(s)-x)\|^{2}

and it follows that

(3.48)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\hskip 9.24994pt\exp\left(-\frac{\|W(s)-x\|^{2}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{\|W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}W(s)-y\|^{2}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\\
&\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\|W(s)-x\|^{2}}{8\varepsilon^{2}}-\frac{3\|C_{s,t}(W(s)-x)\|^{2}}{8\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{\|W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}W(s)-y\|^{2}}{8\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\\
&\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\|W(s)-x\|^{2}}{2(2\varepsilon)^{2}}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{\|W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}x-y\|^{2}}{2(2\varepsilon)^{2}}\right).\end{aligned}

If (ii)
\|W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}W(s)-y\|>\|C_{s,t}(W(s)-x)\|
, then

(3.49)

\displaystyle\|W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}x-y\|^{2}\leq 3\|W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}W(s)-y\|^{2}+\|C_{s,t}(W(s)-x)\|^{2},

thus we have

(3.50)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\hskip 9.24994pt\exp\left(-\frac{\|W(s)-x\|^{2}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{\|W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}W(s)-y\|^{2}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\\
&\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\|W(s)-x\|^{2}}{4\varepsilon^{2}}-\frac{3\|C_{s,t}(W(s)-x)\|^{2}}{8\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{3\|W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}W(s)-y\|^{2}}{8\varepsilon^{2}}\right)\\
&\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\|W(s)-x\|^{2}}{2(2\varepsilon)^{2}}\right)\exp\left(-\frac{\|W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}x-y\|^{2}}{2(2\varepsilon)^{2}}\right).\end{aligned}

Hence the claim (
3.44
) is proved.
Using (
3.44
), independence of
\{W_{s}(t):t\succcurlyeq_{\pi}s\}
and
W(s)
, the bound

(3.51)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[\phi_{2\varepsilon}(W(s)-x)]\leq{(2\pi a^{N})^{-d/2}},

and (
3.14
), we see that (
3.43
) is further bounded above by

(3.52)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\quad 4^{N}c_{\ref{7.37}}\sum_{\pi}\iint_{t\succcurlyeq_{\pi}s}{\mathbb{E}}[\phi_{2\varepsilon}(W(s)-x)]{\mathbb{E}}[\phi_{2\varepsilon}(W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}x-y)]\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\\
&\leq c_{\ref{7.45}}\iint\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi_{2\varepsilon}(w+C_{s,t}x-y)\frac{1}{\|t-s\|^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\|w\|^{2}}{2\,c_{\ref{5.9}}^{\prime}\|t-s\|}\right)\,dw\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\\
&=c_{\ref{7.45}}\iint\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi_{2\varepsilon}(w^{\prime})\frac{1}{\|t-s\|^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\|w^{\prime}+y-C_{s,t}x\|^{2}}{2\,c_{\ref{5.9}}^{\prime}\|t-s\|}\right)\,dw^{\prime}\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy).\end{aligned}

Now we may repeat the argument from (
3.18
) to (
3.20
) to deduce that

(3.53)

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{\|w^{\prime}+y-C_{s,t}x\|^{2}}{2\,c_{\ref{5.9}}^{\prime}\|t-s\|}\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\|w^{\prime}+y-x\|^{2}}{2\,c_{\ref{7.14}}\|t-s\|}\right)

for some constant
c_{\ref{7.14}}>1
.
Let
r_{s,t}
be defined as in (
3.22
).
Then from (
3.43
) and (
3.52
),

(3.54)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\quad{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{s\in[a,b]^{N}\cap\,\mathbb{Q}^{N}}{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu)|\mathscr{F}_{\pi}(s)]^{2}\right)\\
&\leq c_{\ref{7.45}}\iint\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi_{2\varepsilon}(w^{\prime})\,r_{s,t}(w^{\prime}+y-x)\,dw^{\prime}\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\\
&=c_{\ref{7.45}}\iint(\phi_{2\varepsilon}\ast r_{s,t})(x-y)\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\\
&\leq c_{\ref{7.50}}\iint(\phi_{\varepsilon}\ast p_{s,t})(x-y)\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy):=c_{\ref{7.50}}\,I_{\varepsilon},\end{aligned}

where we use Lemma
3.3
in the last inequality and we have set

(3.55)

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}=\iint(\phi_{\varepsilon}\ast p_{s,t})(x-y)\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy).

Now, combine (
3.41
) and (
3.54
), and sum over all
\pi\subset\{1,\dots,N\}
to get

(3.56)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 2^{N}c_{\ref{7.50}}\,I_{\varepsilon}&\geq c_{\ref{5.22}}\,{\mathbb{P}}(G)\sum_{\pi}J_{\varepsilon,\pi}^{2}\\
&\geq 2^{-N}c_{\ref{5.22}}\,{\mathbb{P}}(G)\bigg(\sum_{\pi}J_{\varepsilon,\pi}\bigg)^{2}\\
&=2^{-N}c_{\ref{5.22}}\,{\mathbb{P}}(G)\left(\iint(\phi_{\varepsilon}\ast q_{s,t})(x-y)\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\right)^{2}.\end{aligned}

Using Lemma
3.3
again, we have

(3.57)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} c_{\ref{7.52}}\,I_{\varepsilon}&\geq{\mathbb{P}}(G)\left(\iint(\phi_{\varepsilon}\ast p_{s,t})(x-y)\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\right)^{2}\\
&={\mathbb{P}}(G)\,I_{\varepsilon}^{2},\end{aligned}

for some constant
c_{\ref{7.52}}>0
independent of
\varepsilon
.
Note that
I_{\varepsilon}
is finite for each
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.
Therefore,

(3.58)

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}=\iint(\phi_{\varepsilon}\ast p_{s,t})(x-y)\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\leq c_{\ref{7.52}}\,{\mathbb{P}}(G)^{-1}.

Since
{\mathbb{P}}(G)={\mathbb{P}}(W(E)\cap F\neq\varnothing)>0
, letting
\varepsilon\to 0
yields

(3.59)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(W(E)\cap F\neq\varnothing)\leq c_{\ref{7.52}}\,\mathscr{E}_{0}(\mu)^{-1}\leq c_{\ref{7.52}}\,C_{0}(E\times F).

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

4.
Intersection with the range of an additive stable process

The proof of Theorem
1.3
, which will be given in the next section, is based on a codimension
argument where we check whether the set
W(E)\cap F
intersects the range of an additive stable process
X
.
The goal of this section is to prove Proposition
4.4
below, which gives a necessary and sufficient condition for
W(E)\cap F
to intersect the range of
X
.

For the rest of the paper, we assume that

\{X^{(1)}(u):u\geq 0\},\dots,\{X^{(n)}(u):u\geq 0\}

are
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued
\alpha
-stable processes with
0<\alpha<2
and

{\mathbb{E}}(e^{i\langle\xi,X^{(k)}(u)\rangle})=e^{-u\|\xi\|^{\alpha}/2},

and assume that
W,X^{(1)},\dots,X^{(n)}
are independent.
Let
X=\{X(u):u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\}
be the
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued additive
\alpha
-stable process defined by

X(u)=X^{(1)}(u_{1})+\cdots+X^{(n)}(u_{n})

for any
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
.
The aforementioned codimension argument is based on the following result on the hitting probability for the range of
X
.

Proposition 4.1
.

[
18
, Theorem 4.4]

Let
X
be the additive
\alpha
-stable process defined above. Then for any Borel set
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

{\mathbb{P}}(G\cap X(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus\{0\})\neq\varnothing)>0
if and only if
{\mathcal{C}ap}_{d-\alpha n}(G)>0
,
where
{\mathcal{C}ap}_{d-\alpha n}
denotes the
(d-\alpha n)
-dimensional Bessel-Riesz capacity in the Euclidean
metric
(
cf. e.g.,
[
12
,
14
,
22
]
)
.

The next lemma establishes the commuting property of the filtration generated by the Brownian sheet and the additive
stable process, and Cairoli’s maximal inequality for this filtration.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\pi\subset\{1,\dots,N\}
and
\preccurlyeq^{\pi}
be the partial ordering on
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
defined
by
(s,u)\preccurlyeq^{\pi}(t,v)
iff
s\preccurlyeq_{\pi}t
and
u\preccurlyeq v
.
Let
\mathscr{F}_{s}^{\pi}=\sigma\{W(t):t\preccurlyeq_{\pi}s\}
,
\mathscr{G}_{u}=\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}\mathscr{G}^{(i)}_{u_{i}}
, where

\mathscr{G}^{(i)}_{u_{i}}=\sigma\{X^{(i)}(t):t\leq u_{i}\}
, and
\mathscr{H}_{s,u}^{\pi}=\mathscr{F}_{s}^{\pi}\vee\mathscr{G}_{u}
.
Then
\{\mathscr{H}_{s,u}^{\pi}:(s,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\}
is a commuting filtration with respect to the ordering

\preccurlyeq^{\pi}
. As a consequence, for any
Z\in L^{2}({\mathbb{P}})
,
the following Cairoli maximal inequality holds:

(4.1)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{(s,u)\in\mathbb{Q}^{N}_{+}\times\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{+}}[{\mathbb{E}}(Z|\mathscr{H}_{s,u}^{\pi})]^{2}\right)\leq 4^{N+n}{\mathbb{E}}(Z^{2}).

Proof.

First, we know that
\{\mathscr{F}_{s}^{\pi}\}
is commuting with respect to
\preccurlyeq_{\pi}
thanks to
[
19
, Proposition 3.1]
.
To see that
\{\mathscr{G}_{u}\}
is commuting with respect to
\preccurlyeq
, it suffices to show that
{\mathbb{E}}(Y|\mathscr{G}_{u})={\mathbb{E}}(Y|\mathscr{G}_{u\wedge v})
for
Y
of the form
\prod_{i=1}^{n}{\bf 1}_{A_{i}}
, where
A_{i}\in\mathscr{G}^{(i)}_{v_{i}}
.
By the independence of the
X^{(i)}
’s, we have
{\mathbb{E}}(Y|\mathscr{G}_{u})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb{E}}({\bf 1}_{A_{i}}|\mathscr{G}^{(i)}_{u_{i}})
and

{\mathbb{E}}(Y|\mathscr{G}_{u\wedge v})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb{E}}({\bf 1}_{A_{i}}|\mathscr{G}^{(i)}_{u_{i}\wedge v_{i}})
. Since
A_{i}\in\mathscr{G}_{v_{i}}^{(i)}
, we have
{\mathbb{E}}({\bf 1}_{A_{i}}|\mathscr{G}^{(i)}_{u_{i}})={\mathbb{E}}({\bf 1}_{A_{i}}|\mathscr{G}^{(i)}_{u_{i}\wedge v_{i}})
, so
\{\mathscr{G}_{u}\}
is commuting.
Then by the independence of
W
and the
X^{(i)}
’s, for any
A\in\mathscr{F}_{t}^{\pi}
and
B\in\mathscr{G}_{v}
, we have

{\mathbb{E}}({\bf 1}_{A\cap B}|\mathscr{H}_{s,u}^{\pi})={\mathbb{E}}({\bf 1}_{A}|\mathscr{F}_{s}^{\pi}){\mathbb{E}}({\bf 1}_{B}|\mathscr{G}_{u})={\mathbb{E}}({\bf 1}_{A}|\mathscr{F}^{\pi}_{s\wedge_{\pi}t}){\mathbb{E}}({\bf 1}_{B}|\mathscr{G}_{u\wedge v})={\mathbb{E}}({\bf 1}_{A\cap B}|\mathscr{H}^{\pi}_{s\wedge_{\pi}t,u\wedge v}).

This implies that
{\mathbb{E}}(Y|\mathscr{H}_{s,u}^{\pi})={\mathbb{E}}(Y|\mathscr{H}_{s\wedge_{\pi}t,u\wedge v}^{\pi})
for all bounded

\mathscr{H}_{t,v}^{\pi}
-measurable function
Y
. Hence
\{\mathscr{H}^{\pi}_{s,u}\}
is commuting with respect to
\preccurlyeq^{\pi}
.

To prove the maximal inequality, let
\{q_{0},q_{1},\dots\}
be an enumeration of
\mathbb{Q}_{+}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{m}=\{q_{0},\dots,q_{m}\}
.
We can apply Cairoli’s maximal inequality
[
14
, Theorem 2.3.1, p.19; Theorem 3.5.1, p.37]
to the martingale
{\mathbb{E}}[Z|\mathscr{H}^{\pi}_{s,u}]
with the order
\preccurlyeq^{\pi}
on each
\mathbb{Q}_{m}^{N}\times\mathbb{Q}_{m}^{n}
because
(\mathbb{Q}^{N}_{m}\times\mathbb{Q}^{n}_{m},\preccurlyeq^{\pi})
is order isomorphic to
\{(0,\dots,0)\preccurlyeq s\preccurlyeq(m,\dots,m):s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{N+n}\}
.
Then let
m\to\infty
to finish the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
p_{s,t},r_{s,t}
and
q_{s,t}
be as in Lemma
3.3
.
Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be compact sets and
\mu\in P_{0}(E\times F)
.
Let
\kappa\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a positive definite lower semicontinuous function.
Let
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
be positive definite functions in either
C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
or the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
0\leq\widehat{\phi}_{1}\leq\widehat{\phi}_{2}
.
Assume that
\phi_{1}\ast\kappa

and
\phi_{2}\ast\kappa
are continuous.
Then

(4.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\iint(\phi_{1}\ast p_{s,t})(x-y)\,(\phi_{1}\ast\kappa)(x-y)\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\\
&\hskip 50.0pt\leq\iint(\phi_{2}\ast p_{s,t})(x-y)\,(\phi_{2}\ast\kappa)(x-y)\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\end{aligned}

and

(4.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &c_{2}^{-(1+\frac{d}{2})}\iint(\phi_{1}\ast r_{s,t})(x-y)\,(\phi_{1}\ast\kappa)(x-y)\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\\
&\hskip 50.0pt\leq\iint(\phi_{1}\ast p_{s,t})(x-y)\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,(\phi_{1}\ast\kappa)(x-y)\,\mu(dt\,dy)\\
&\hskip 80.0pt\leq c_{1}^{-(1+\frac{d}{2})}\iint(\phi_{1}\ast q_{s,t})(x-y)\,(\phi_{1}\ast\kappa)(x-y)\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy).\end{aligned}

Proof.

As in Lemma
3.3
, it suffices to prove that for
\nu=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}(\delta_{s_{i}}\otimes\delta_{x_{i}})
,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\iint[(\phi_{1}\ast p_{s,t})\cdot(\phi_{1}\ast\kappa)](x-y)\,\nu(ds\,dx)\,\nu(dt\,dy)\\
&\quad\quad\leq\iint[(\phi_{2}\ast p_{s,t})\cdot(\phi_{2}\ast\kappa)](x-y)\,\nu(ds\,dx)\,\nu(dt\,dy).\end{aligned}

The Fourier transform of
(\phi_{k}\ast p_{s,t})\cdot(\phi_{k}\ast\kappa)
is equal to

(\widehat{\phi}_{k}\,\widehat{p}_{s,t})\ast(\widehat{\phi}_{k}\,\widehat{\kappa})
.
By Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle\quad\iint(\phi_{k}\ast p_{s,t})(x-y)\,(\phi_{k}\ast\kappa)(x-y)\,\nu(ds\,dx)\,\nu(dt\,dy)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-d}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{i}a_{j}\iint\delta_{s_{i}}(ds)\delta_{s_{j}}(dt)\int d\xi\int dz\,\widehat{\phi}_{k}(z)\widehat{p}_{s,t}(z)\widehat{\phi}_{k}(\xi-z)\widehat{\kappa}(\xi-z)\widehat{\delta}_{x_{i}}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{\delta}_{x_{j}}(\xi)}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{i}a_{j}\int dz\int d\xi\,\widehat{\phi}_{k}(z)\widehat{\phi}_{k}(\xi-z)\widehat{\kappa}(\xi-z)\widehat{\delta}_{x_{i}}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{\delta}_{x_{j}}(\xi)}\iint\delta_{s_{i}}(ds)\delta_{s_{j}}(dt)P_{z}(s-t)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-N-\frac{d}{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{i}a_{j}\int dz\int d\xi\,\widehat{\phi}_{k}(z)\widehat{\phi}_{k}(\xi-z)\widehat{\kappa}(\xi-z)\widehat{\delta}_{x_{i}}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{\delta}_{x_{j}}(\xi)}\int d\eta\,\widehat{P}_{z}(\eta)\widehat{\delta}_{s_{i}}(\eta)\overline{\widehat{\delta}_{s_{j}}(\eta)}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-N-\frac{d}{2}}\int dz\iint d\xi\,d\eta\,\widehat{P}_{z}(\eta)\widehat{\phi}_{k}(z)\widehat{\phi}_{k}(\xi-z)\widehat{\kappa}(\xi-z)\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\widehat{\delta}_{x_{i}}(\xi)\widehat{\delta}_{s_{i}}(\eta)\right|^{2}.

Since
\widehat{P}_{z}(\eta)
and
\widehat{\kappa}(\xi-z)
are nonnegative and
0\leq\widehat{\phi}_{1}\leq\widehat{\phi}_{2}
, we obtain (
4.4
), and hence (
4.2
) as in the proof of Lemma
3.3
. Similarly, we can use the inequality

0\leq c_{2}^{-(1+\frac{d}{2})}\,\widehat{R}_{z}(\eta)\leq\widehat{P}_{z}(\eta)\leq c_{1}^{-(1+\frac{d}{2})}\,\widehat{Q}_{z}(\eta)

to prove (
4.3
).
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
E\subset(0,\infty)^{N}
and
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be compact sets.
Suppose
F
has Lebesgue measure zero and
d-\alpha n>0
. Then

{\mathbb{P}}(W(E)\cap F\cap X(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus\{0\})\neq\varnothing)>0
if and only if
C_{d-\alpha n}(E\times F)>0
.

Proof.

Define
p_{s,t}
as in (
3.7
).
Let
g_{0}=\delta_{0}
.
For
t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
, let
g_{t}
be the density of
X(t)
, namely,

(4.5)

\displaystyle g_{t}(x)=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}e^{-i\langle x,\xi\rangle}e^{-\|t\|_{1}\|\xi\|_{2}^{\alpha}/2}\,d\xi,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\|t\|_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}|t_{j}|
for
t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We extend the definition of
g_{t}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
by setting

(4.6)

\displaystyle g_{t}(x)=\|t\|_{1}^{-d/\alpha}g_{(1,\dots,1)}(\|t\|_{1}^{-1/\alpha}x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Since
E\subset(0,\infty)^{N}
and
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
are compact, we can find constants
0<a<1
and
1<b<\infty

such that
s\in[a,b]^{N}
for all
s\in E
, and
\|x\|<b
for all
x\in F
.

(\Leftarrow)
. Suppose
C_{d-\alpha n}(E\times F)>0
. Then
\mathscr{E}_{d-\alpha n}(\mu)<\infty
for some
\mu\in P_{0}(E\times F)
.
Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
f_{\varepsilon}=|B(0,\varepsilon)|^{-1}{\bf 1}_{B(0,\varepsilon)}
,
\phi_{\varepsilon}=f_{\varepsilon}\ast f_{\varepsilon}
and

(4.7)

\displaystyle Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\int_{[1,2]^{n}}du\int_{E\times F}\phi_{\varepsilon}(W(s)-x)\,\phi_{\varepsilon}(X(u)-x)\,\mu(ds\,dx).

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

(4.8)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu)>0)\geq\frac{{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu)]^{2}}{{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu)^{2}]}.

Note that

(4.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu)]&=\int_{[1,2]^{n}}du\int\mu(ds\,dx)\,{\mathbb{E}}[\phi_{\varepsilon}(W(s)-x)]{\mathbb{E}}[\phi_{\varepsilon}(X(u)-x)]\\
&=\int_{[1,2]^{n}}du\int\mu(ds\,dx)\left[\int_{\|w\|\leq b+2}\phi_{\varepsilon}(w-x)\frac{1}{(2\pi\prod_{i=1}^{N}s_{i})^{d/2}}\,e^{-\frac{\|w\|^{2}}{2\prod_{i=1}^{N}s_{i}}}\,dw\right]\\
&\hskip 105.0pt\times\left[\int_{\|z\|\leq b+2}\phi_{\varepsilon}(z-x)g_{u}(z)\,dz\right]\\
&\geq\int_{[1,2]^{n}}du\int\mu(ds\,dx)\left[(2\pi b^{N})^{-d/2}\,e^{-\frac{(b+2)^{2}}{2a^{N}}}\int_{\|w\|\leq b+2}\phi_{\varepsilon}(w-x)\,dw\right]\\
&\hskip 105.0pt\times\left[\inf_{u\in[1,2]^{n},\|v\|\leq b+2}g_{u}(v)\int_{\|z\|\leq b+2}\phi_{\varepsilon}(z-x)\,dz\right]\\
&=c_{\ref{6.10}}>0.\end{aligned}

To obtain the last line, we have used the fact that

\inf_{u\in[1,2]^{n},\|v\|\leq b+2}g_{u}(v)>0,

which follows from the scaling property (
4.6
), the property that
g_{(1,\dots,1)}(a)>0
for all
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(see
[
14
, Corollary 3.2.1, p.379]
) and continuity.
Next, we establish an upper bound for

(4.10)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \quad{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\varepsilon}(\mu)^{2}]&=\iint_{[1,2]^{2n}}du\,dv\iint{\mathbb{E}}[\phi_{\varepsilon}(W(s)-x)\phi_{\varepsilon}(W(t)-y)]\\
&\hskip 85.0pt\times{\mathbb{E}}[\phi_{\varepsilon}(X(u)-x)\phi_{\varepsilon}(X(v)-y)]\,\mu(ds\,dx)\,\mu(dt\,dy).\end{aligned}

Let
Z_{1}=X(u)-X(u\wedge v)
and
Z_{2}=X(v)-X(u\wedge v)
, where
u\wedge v=(u_{1}\wedge v_{1},\dots,u_{n}\wedge v_{n})
.
Then, by (
3.11
) and triangle inequality,

(4.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\quad{\mathbb{E}}[\phi_{\varepsilon}(X(u)-x)\phi_{\varepsilon}(X(v)-y)]\\
&\leq 4^{d}{\mathbb{E}}[f_{2\varepsilon}(X(u)-x)f_{2\varepsilon}(X(v)-y)]\\
&\leq c_{\ref{6.12}}\,\varepsilon^{-d}{\mathbb{E}}[{\bf 1}_{B(0,2\varepsilon)}(X(u)-x)f_{4\varepsilon}(X(v)-X(u)+x-y)]\\
&=c_{\ref{6.12}}\,\varepsilon^{-d}{\mathbb{E}}[P(|Z_{1}+X(u\wedge v)-x|\leq 2\varepsilon|Z_{1},Z_{2})f_{4\varepsilon}(Z_{2}-Z_{1}+x-y)].\end{aligned}

Since
X(u\wedge v)
is unimodal (see
[
17
, Remark 2.3]
) and

\gamma\in(\gamma^{*},d)
, for which we have
C_{\gamma}(E\times F)=0
. We can choose an integer

n\geq 1
and
\alpha\in(0,2)
such that
d-\alpha n=\gamma
and consider the corresponding additive stable process
X
. By Proposition
4.4
,

{\mathbb{P}}(W(E)\cap F\cap X(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus\{0\})\neq\varnothing)=0.

Then, Proposition
4.1
shows that
{\mathcal{C}ap}_{d-\alpha n}\big(W(E)\cap F\big)=0
and thus
{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(W(E)\cap F)\leq\gamma
almost surely.
This is true for arbitrary
\gamma>\gamma^{*}
.
Hence,
\|{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(W(E)\cap F)\|_{\infty}\leq\gamma^{*}
.

Now suppose that
F
has a positive Lebesgue measure.
According to Proposition
5.1
, we have

\|{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}(W(E)\cap F)\|_{\infty}=\min\{d,2{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}E\}.

Note that
{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}F=\dim_{{}_{\rm P}}F=d
because
F
has positive Lebesgue measure.
Then by Proposition
2.3
, we have
\sup\{\gamma>0:C_{\gamma}(E\times F)>0\}=\min\{d,2{\rm dim}_{{}_{\rm H}}E\}
. The result follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

The proof is similar to the proof of Theorem 1.1 in
[
20
]
. Instead of Kaufman’s uniform dimension result for the Brownian motion,
we use the uniform dimension result for the Brownian sheet which holds for
d\geq 2N
(see
[
23
]
,
[
16
]
).
Other parts of the proof are almost the same. The only part that requires additional proof is the uniform estimates for hitting probabilities of the Brownian
sheet and additive stable process.
More precisely, suppose that
E\subset[1/q,q]^{N}
and
F\subset[-q,q]^{d}
where
1<q<\infty
.
For each
r\in(0,1)
, let
T(r)
be the collection of open balls of radius
r^{2}
contained in
[1/q,q]^{N}
, and
S(r)
be the collection of open balls of radius
r
in
[-q,q]^{d}
.
We need to show that there is a constant
0<c_{\ref{9.19}}<\infty
such that for all
r\in(0,1)
,

(5.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{I\in T(r)}\sup_{J\in S(r)}{\mathbb{P}}(W(I)\cap J\neq\varnothing)\leq c_{\ref{9.19}}\,r^{d},

and for an
n
-parameter,
\mathbb{R}^{N}
-valued additive
\alpha
-stable process
X(u)=X^{(1)}(u_{1})+\dots+X^{(n)}(u_{n})
and

G=[0,A)^{n}\setminus[0,a)^{n}
,
0<a<A<\infty
, there is
0<c_{\ref{9.28}}<\infty
such that for all
r\in(0,1)
,

(5.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{I\in T(r)}{\mathbb{P}}(X(G)\cap I\neq\varnothing)\leq c_{\ref{9.28}}\,r^{2(N-\alpha n)}.

To prove (
5.1
), take
I=B(t_{0},r^{2})
and
J=B(x_{0},r)
. For each
r\in(0,1)
, let

(5.3)

\displaystyle Z_{r}=\int_{B(t_{0},r^{2})}{\bf 1}_{B(x_{0},2r)}(W(t))\,dt.

Fix any
s\in I
. Since
W_{s}(t)
and
\mathscr{F}_{s}^{\pi}
are independent for
t\succcurlyeq_{\pi}s
and
\pi\subset\{1,\dots,N\}
,
we have

(5.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sum_{\pi}{\mathbb{E}}(Z_{r}|\mathscr{F}^{\pi}_{s})\\
&\geq\sum_{\pi}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in B(t_{0},r^{2})\\
t\succcurlyeq_{\pi}s\hskip 8.19447pt\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{P}}(\|W_{s}(t)+C_{s,t}W(s)-x_{0}\|\leq 2r|\mathscr{F}_{s}^{\pi})\,dt\,{\bf 1}_{\{\|W(s)-x_{0}\|\leq r\}}\\
&\geq\sum_{\pi}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in B(t_{0},r^{2})\\
t\succcurlyeq_{\pi}s\hskip 8.19447pt\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{P}}(\|W_{s}(t)-(1-C_{s,t})x_{0}\|\leq r|\mathscr{F}_{s}^{\pi})\,dt\,{\bf 1}_{\{\|W(s)-x_{0}\|\leq r\}}\\
&=\sum_{\pi}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in B(t_{0},r^{2})\\
t\succcurlyeq_{\pi}s\hskip 8.19447pt\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{P}}(\|W_{s}(t)-(1-C_{s,t})x_{0}\|\leq r)\,dt\,{\bf 1}_{\{\|W(s)-x_{0}\|\leq r\}}\\
&=\int_{B(t_{0},r^{2})\setminus\{s\}}{\mathbb{P}}(\|W_{s}(t)-(1-C_{s,t})x_{0}\|\leq r)\,dt\,{\bf 1}_{\{\|W(s)-x_{0}\|\leq r\}}.\end{aligned}

Then by (
3.14
), we further deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi}{\mathbb{E}}(Z_{r}|\mathscr{F}^{\pi}_{s})

\displaystyle\geq c\int_{B(t_{0},r^{2})\setminus\{s\}}dt\int_{B(0,r)}dw\,\frac{1}{\|s-t\|^{d/2}}\,\exp\left(-\frac{\|w-(1-C_{s,t})x_{0}\|^{2}}{2c_{\ref{5.9}}\|s-t\|}\right)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\|W(s)-x_{0}\|\leq r\}}

\displaystyle\geq c\int_{B(t_{0},r^{2})\setminus\{s\}}dt\int_{B(0,1)}\,\frac{r^{d}\,dw}{\|s-t\|^{d/2}}\,\exp\left(-\frac{3r^{2}\|w\|^{2}+3c_{\ref{5.8}}^{2}q^{d}\|s-t\|}{2c_{\ref{5.9}}\|s-t\|}\right)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\|W(s)-x_{0}\|\leq r\}}

\displaystyle\geq c^{\prime}\int_{B(t_{0},r^{2})\setminus\{s\}}dt\int_{B(0,1)}\frac{r^{d}dw}{(2r^{2})^{d/2}}\,\exp\left(-\frac{3r^{2}\|w\|^{2}}{2c_{\ref{5.9}}\cdot 2r^{2}}\right)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\|W(s)-x_{0}\|\leq r\}}

(5.5)

\displaystyle\geq c_{\ref{9.22}}\,r^{2N}\,{\bf 1}_{\{\|W(s)-x_{0}\|\leq r\}}.

Let
\{q_{1},q_{2},\dots\}
be an enumeration of
\mathbb{Q}_{+}
, and let
I_{m}=I\cap\{q_{1},\dots,q_{m}\}^{N}
.
Note that by the continuity of
W(t)
,
\{W(I)\cap J\neq\varnothing\}=\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}\{W(I_{m})\cap J\neq\varnothing\}
.
Since
W(I_{m})\cap J\neq\varnothing
implies
\|W(s)-x_{0}\|\leq r
for some
s\in I_{m}
, it follows from (
5.5
) that

(5.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} c_{\ref{9.22}}\,r^{2N}\,{\bf 1}_{\{W(I_{m})\cap J\neq\varnothing\}}&\leq\sup_{s\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}^{N}}\sum_{\pi}{\mathbb{E}}(Z_{r}|\mathscr{F}_{s}^{\pi})\\
&\leq\sum_{\pi}\sup_{s\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}^{N}}{\mathbb{E}}(Z_{r}|\mathscr{F}_{s}^{\pi}).\end{aligned}

Taking square and using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

(5.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (c_{\ref{9.22}}\,r^{2N})^{2}\,{\bf 1}_{\{W(I_{m})\cap J\neq\varnothing\}}&\leq\left(\sum_{\pi}\sup_{s\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{N}_{+}}{\mathbb{E}}(Z_{r}|\mathscr{F}_{s}^{\pi})\right)^{2}\\
&\leq 2^{N}\sum_{\pi}\sup_{s\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}^{N}}{\mathbb{E}}(Z_{r}|\mathscr{F}_{s}^{\pi})^{2}.\end{aligned}

Taking expectations on both sides yields

(5.8)

\displaystyle c_{\ref{9.22}}^{2}\,r^{4N}\,{\mathbb{P}}(W(I_{m})\cap J\neq\varnothing)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{N}\sum_{\pi}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\sup_{s\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}^{N}}{\mathbb{E}}(Z_{r}|\mathscr{F}_{s}^{\pi})^{2}\right).

Since
\{\mathscr{F}^{\pi}_{s}\}
is commuting with respect to
\preccurlyeq_{\pi}
, we can use Cairoli’s maximal inequality to get

(5.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} c_{\ref{9.22}}^{2}\,r^{4N}\,{\mathbb{P}}(W(I_{m})\cap J\neq\varnothing)&\leq 2^{4N}{\mathbb{E}}(Z_{r}^{2})\\
&\leq 2^{4N}\int_{B(t_{0},r^{2})}ds\int_{B(t_{0},r^{2})}dt\,{\mathbb{P}}(\|W(t)-x_{0}\|\leq 2r)\\
&\leq c_{\ref{9.26}}\,r^{4N+d}.\end{aligned}

Letting
m\to\infty
yields (
5.1
).

To prove (
5.2
), let
I=B(t_{0},r^{2})
,
G^{\prime}=[0,2A)^{n}\setminus[0,a)^{n}
,
\mathscr{G}_{u}=\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}\sigma\{X^{(i)}(t):t\leq u_{i}\}
. Let

(5.10)

\displaystyle Z_{r}=\int_{G^{\prime}}{\bf 1}_{B(t_{0},2r^{2})}(X(v))\,dv

and

(5.11)

\displaystyle\kappa(x)=\int_{[0,A]^{n}}g_{v}(x)\,dv,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
g_{v}
is defined as in the proof of Proposition
4.4
.
By Proposition 4.6 of
[
20
]
, there are constants
0<c_{\ref{9.31}}<C_{\ref{9.31}}<\infty
such that for all

x\in[-1,1]^{N}
,

(5.12)

\displaystyle\frac{c_{\ref{9.31}}}{\|x\|^{N-\alpha n}}\leq\kappa(x)\leq\frac{C_{\ref{9.31}}}{\|x\|^{N-\alpha n}}.

Then for any
u\in G
,

(5.13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\mathbb{E}}(Z_{r}|\mathscr{G}_{u})&\geq\int_{G^{\prime}}{\mathbb{P}}(\|X(v)-t_{0}\|\leq 2r^{2}|\mathscr{G}_{u})\,dv\,{\bf 1}_{\{\|X(u)-t_{0}\|\leq r^{2}\}}\\
&\geq\int_{v\in G^{\prime},v\succcurlyeq u}{\mathbb{P}}(\|X(v)-X(u)\|\leq r^{2}|\mathscr{G}_{u})\,dv\,{\bf 1}_{\{\|X(u)-t_{0}\|\leq r^{2}\}}\\
&=\int_{v\in G^{\prime},v\succcurlyeq u}\int_{B(0,r^{2})}g_{v-u}(x)\,dx\,dv\,{\bf 1}_{\{\|X(u)-t_{0}\|\leq r^{2}\}}\\
&\geq\int_{B(0,r^{2})}\kappa(x)\,dx\,{\bf 1}_{\{\|X(u)-t_{0}\|\leq r^{2}\}}\\
&\geq\int_{B(0,r^{2})}\frac{c_{\ref{9.31}}}{\|x\|^{N-\alpha n}}\,dx\,{\bf 1}_{\{\|X(u)-t_{0}\|\leq r^{2}\}}\\
&\geq c_{\ref{9.32}}\,r^{2\alpha n}\,{\bf 1}_{\{\|X(u)-t_{0}\|\leq r^{2}\}}.\end{aligned}

Let
G_{m}=G\cap\{q_{1},\dots,q_{m}\}^{n}
.
Note that by the right continuity of
X(u)
,
\{X(G)\cap I\neq\varnothing\}=\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}\{X(G_{m})\cap I\neq\varnothing\}
.
Since
X(G_{m})\cap I\neq\varnothing
implies
\|X(u)-t_{0}\|\leq r^{2}
for some
u\in G_{m}
, it follows from (
5.13
) that

(5.14)

\displaystyle c_{\ref{9.32}}^{2}\,r^{4\alpha n}\,{\bf 1}_{\{X(G_{m})\cap I\neq\varnothing\}}\leq\sup_{u\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}^{n}}{\mathbb{E}}(Z_{r}|\mathscr{G}_{u})^{2}.

Taking expectation and using Cairoli’s maximal inequality, we obtain

(5.15)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} c_{\ref{9.32}}^{2}\,r^{4\alpha n}\,{\mathbb{P}}(X(G_{m})\cap I\neq\varnothing)&\leq 4^{n}{\mathbb{E}}(Z_{r}^{2}).\end{aligned}

Let
Y_{1}=X(u)-X(u\wedge v)
and
Y_{2}=X(v)-X(u\wedge v)
. By the independence of
X(u\wedge v),Y_{1},Y_{2}
, and
(
5.12
), we have the following upper bound:

(5.16)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\mathbb{E}}(Z_{r}^{2})&=\iint_{G^{\prime}\times G^{\prime}}{\mathbb{P}}(\|X(u)-t_{0}\|\leq 2r^{2},\|X(v)-t_{0}\|\leq 2r^{2})\,du\,dv\\
&\leq\iint_{G^{\prime}\times G^{\prime}}{\mathbb{P}}(\|X(u)-t_{0}\|\leq 2r^{2},\|X(v)-X(u)\|\leq 4r^{2})\,du\,dv\\
&\leq\iint_{G^{\prime}\times G^{\prime}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[{\mathbb{P}}(\|Y_{1}-X(u\wedge v)-t_{0}\|\leq 2r^{2}|Y_{1},Y_{2})\cdot{\bf 1}_{\{\|Y_{2}-Y_{1}\|\leq 4r^{2}\}}\right]\,du\,dv\\
&\leq\iint_{G^{\prime}\times G^{\prime}}{\mathbb{P}}(\|X(u\wedge v)\|\leq 2r^{2})\,{\mathbb{P}}(\|X(v)-X(u)\|\leq 4r^{2})\,du\,dv\\
&\leq\sup_{u,v\in G^{\prime}}g_{u\wedge v}(0)\cdot|B(0,2r^{2})|\iint_{G^{\prime}\times G^{\prime}}\,du\,dv\int_{B(0,4r^{2})}g_{v-u}(x)\,dx\\
&\leq c\,r^{2N}\int_{B(0,4r^{2})}\kappa(x)\,dx\\
&\leq c\,r^{2N}\int_{B(0,4r^{2})}\frac{C_{\ref{9.31}}}{\|x\|^{N-\alpha n}}\,dx\\
&\leq c_{\ref{9.35}}\,r^{2N+2\alpha n}.\end{aligned}

Combining this with (
5.15
) and letting
m\to\infty
yields (
5.2
).
With (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) in place, we can follow the proof of
[
20
, Theorem 1.1]
to complete the proof of Theorem
1.4
.
∎

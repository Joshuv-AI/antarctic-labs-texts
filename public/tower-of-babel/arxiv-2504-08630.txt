Schottky anomaly in a cavity-coupled double quantum well
Valerii K. Kozin, Dmitry Miserev, Daniel Loss, Jelena Klinovaja

Abstract
We present a theoretical study of a mesoscopic two-dimensional electron gas confined in a double quantum well that is coupled to a uniform quasi-static cavity mode via fluctuations of the dipole moment. We focus on the regime of large number of electrons participating in the virtual intersubband transitions. In this regime, the effective photonic potential is no longer quadratic but, instead, it contains large number of minima. Each minimum represents a nearly harmonic oscillator with the renormalized cavity frequency that is much greater than its bare value. The energy offset of a minimum scales quadratically with respect to the photon coordinate corresponding to this minimum. These energy offsets determine the statistical weight of each minimum, and altogether they result in the additive correction to the heat capacity of the system. This correction exhibits a Schottky anomaly and a 0.5 ​ k B 0.5k_{B} plateau at low temperatures. This behavior can be associated with the emergence of a new degree of freedom. This degree of freedom does not manifest in the optical conductivity and can only be observed via the heat capacity measurement.

The photon Hamiltonian contains the cosine term originating from the light-matter coupling, see Eqs. (

The interaction contribution to the heat capacity

The interaction contribution to the grand canonical potential given by Eq. (
51
) can be represented as follows,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{int}}=-\frac{\Delta_{0}^{2}}{8}\sum\limits_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}\int\limits_{0}^{\beta}\Pi_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}(\tau)D_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}(\tau)\,d\tau,

(56)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}(\tau)=\left\langle\mathcal{T}\left\{\hat{j}_{\nu_{1}}(\tau)\hat{j}_{\nu_{2}}(0)\right\}\right\rangle,

(57)

\displaystyle D_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}(\tau)=\left\langle\mathcal{T}\left\{\hat{\phi}_{\nu_{1}}(\tau)\hat{\phi}_{\nu_{2}}(0)\right\}\right\rangle,

(58)

where we just substituted Eq. (
13
) into Eq. (
51
) and used that the time-ordered average in Eq. (
51
) depends only on the time difference
\tau=\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}
.
Indices
\nu_{1}
,
\nu_{2}
take values
\pm 1
.

In order to calculate
D_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}(\tau)
, we represent the photon coordinate as
\hat{Q}=Q_{n}+\hat{q}
near the
n^{\mathrm{th}}
minimum, where the photonic potential is harmonic, see Eq. (
28
), with frequency
\tilde{\omega}
that is independent of
n
, see Eq. (
29
).
As
Q_{n}\approx 2\pi n
, see Eq. (
26
), the operator
\hat{\phi}_{\nu}\approx:e^{-i\nu\hat{q}}:\equiv e^{-i\nu\hat{q}}-\Phi_{0}
takes the same form near each minimum.
Notation
:\hat{O}:
stands for normal ordering of an operator
\hat{O}
.
Therefore,
D_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}(\tau)
is the same as for a single oscillator with frequency
\tilde{\omega}
,

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0ptD_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}(\tau)=\sum\limits_{n}\frac{e^{-\beta\mathcal{E}_{n}}}{Z_{\mathrm{b}}}\left\langle\mathcal{T}\left\{:e^{-i\nu_{1}\hat{q}(\tau)}::e^{-i\nu_{2}\hat{q}(0)}:\right\}\right\rangle_{n}

\displaystyle\hskip 38.0pt=\left\langle\mathcal{T}\left\{:e^{-i\nu_{1}\hat{q}(\tau)}::e^{-i\nu_{2}\hat{q}(0)}:\right\}\right\rangle_{0},

(59)

where
Z_{\mathrm{b}}
is given by Eq. (
32
),
\langle\dots\rangle_{n}
stands for the statistical averaging near the
n^{\mathrm{th}}
minimum of
\hat{V}(\hat{Q})
,
\langle\dots\rangle_{0}
stands for the averaging near the minimum at
Q=0
.
Following the same line of reasoning, we also find that
\Phi_{0}
is given by the average near the oscillator minimum at
Q=0
, see Eq. (
36
).
In order to evaluate the average in Eq. (
59
), we first perform the canonical transformation of the photon Hamiltonian near
Q=0
minimum,

\displaystyle\hskip-10.0pt\hat{X}=\frac{\hat{q}}{\sqrt{2\tilde{g}^{2}}}=\frac{\hat{b}+\hat{b}^{\dagger}}{\sqrt{2}},\hskip 5.0pt\hat{\mathcal{P}}=\sqrt{2\tilde{g}^{2}}\hat{P}=\frac{\hat{b}-\hat{b}^{\dagger}}{i\sqrt{2}},

(60)

where
\tilde{g}
is the renormalized coupling constant, see Eq. (
37
),
\hat{b}
(
\hat{b}^{\dagger}
) the annihilation (creation) operator of a photon in the
Q=0
minimum.
The photon Hamiltonian near
Q=0
minimum takes the form
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{ph}}(Q=0)=\tilde{\omega}(b^{\dagger}b+1/2)
.
From this, the interaction representation for
\hat{b}
and
\hat{b}^{\dagger}
is the following,

\displaystyle\hat{b}(\tau)=e^{-\tilde{\omega}\tau}\hat{b},\hskip 5.0pt\hat{b}^{\dagger}(\tau)=e^{\tilde{\omega}\tau}\hat{b}^{\dagger},

(61)

where
\hat{b}^{\dagger}(\tau)
corresponds to the interaction representation of
\hat{b}^{\dagger}
but it is not a Hermitian conjugate of
\hat{b}(\tau)
because
\tau
is imaginary time.
Using Campbell-Baker-Hausdorff identity and the following thermodynamic average,

\displaystyle\langle e^{z_{1}\hat{b}^{\dagger}}e^{z_{2}\hat{b}}\rangle=\exp\left(z_{1}z_{2}N_{\mathrm{ph}}\right),

(62)

where
z_{1}
,
z_{2}
are arbitrary complex numbers,
N_{\mathrm{ph}}
the average number of photons in the harmonic oscillator, see Eq. (
38
), we find the photon correlator,

\displaystyle\hskip-26.0ptD_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}(\tau)=\Phi_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip-23.0pt\times\left(\exp\left[-\nu_{1}\nu_{2}\tilde{g}^{2}\left(2N_{\mathrm{ph}}\cosh(\tilde{\omega}\tau)+e^{-\tilde{\omega}|\tau|}\right)\right]-1\right)\!.

(63)

As
\tilde{g}\ll 1
, we can expand this expression to the leading order,

\displaystyle\hskip-15.0ptD_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}(\tau)\approx-\nu_{1}\nu_{2}\tilde{g}^{2}\left(2N_{\mathrm{ph}}\cosh(\tilde{\omega}\tau)+e^{-\tilde{\omega}|\tau|}\right).

(64)

As
D_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}(\tau)
depends only on the product of indices
\nu_{1}\nu_{2}
, we can simplify
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{int}}
,

\displaystyle\hskip-29.0pt\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{int}}\!=\!-\frac{\Delta_{0}^{2}}{8}\!\int\limits_{0}^{\beta}\!D_{+,+}(\tau)\!\sum\limits_{\nu}\!\left[\Pi_{\nu,\nu}(\tau)-\Pi_{\nu,-\nu}(\tau)\right]d\tau.

(65)

The dipole-dipole correlator
\Pi_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}(\tau)
can be represented in terms of the fermion Green’s function
G_{\bm{k}}(\tau)

\displaystyle\hskip-12.0pt\Pi_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}(\tau)=-\frac{1}{4}\sum\limits_{\bm{k}}\mathrm{Tr}\left\{\eta_{\nu_{1}}G_{\bm{k}}(\tau)\eta_{\nu_{2}}G_{\bm{k}}(-\tau)\right\},

(66)

\displaystyle\hskip-12.0ptG_{\bm{k}}(\tau)=-\left\langle\mathcal{T}\left\{\Psi_{\bm{k}}(\tau)\Psi_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}(0)\right\}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 17.0pt=\sum\limits_{\sigma}|\sigma\rangle\langle\sigma|e^{-\xi_{\bm{k},\sigma}\tau}\left(n_{\bm{k},\sigma}-\vartheta(\tau)\right),

(67)

where we substituted Eq. (
3
) into Eq. (
57
) and applied the Wick’s theorem, the trace
\mathrm{Tr}
is taken over the spin and the subband index,
\eta_{\pm}=\eta_{x}\pm i\eta_{y}
are the Pauli matrices describing the dipole moment.
The electron Green’s function is expanded with respect to the projectors on two bands characterized by the dipole spinors
|\pm\rangle
, see Eq. (
16
),
\xi_{\bm{k},\sigma}
is the renormalized spectrum corresponding to the band
\sigma
, see Eq. (
19

n_{\bm{k},\sigma}=[e^{\beta\xi_{\bm{k},\sigma}}+1]^{-1}
is the Fermi-Dirac distribution function,
\vartheta(\tau)=0
(
\vartheta(\tau)=1
) at
\tau<0
(
\tau>0

The spin trace contributes the factor of

.
Evaluating the trace over the subband index, we find,

\displaystyle\hskip-35.0pt\sum\limits_{\nu}\left[\Pi_{\nu,\nu}(\tau)-\Pi_{\nu,-\nu}(\tau)\right]

\displaystyle=-2\sum\limits_{\bm{k},\sigma}e^{\sigma E_{g}|\tau|}n_{\bm{k},-\sigma}\left(1-n_{\bm{k},\sigma}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{m\Omega E_{g}}{\pi}\frac{\cosh\left(E_{g}\left(|\tau|-\frac{\beta}{2}\right)\right)}{\sinh\left(\frac{\beta E_{g}}{2}\right)}S,

(68)

\displaystyle\hskip-35.0ptS=\left[1-\frac{T}{E_{g}}\ln\left|\frac{1+\exp\left(-\beta\left(E_{F}-\frac{E_{g}}{2}\right)\right)}{1+\exp\left(-\beta\left(E_{F}+\frac{E_{g}}{2}\right)\right)}\right|\right].

(69)

.

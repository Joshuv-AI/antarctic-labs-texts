BMO solvability with singular drifts on ample sawtooth domains implies L p L^{p} solvability.
Aritro Pathak

Abstract
For a linear elliptic operator with a singular drift that satisfies a finite Carleson measure condition, we prove that there exist ‘ample’ sawtooth domains of the unit ball B ⁡ ( 0 , 1 ) ⊂ ℝ n + 1 B(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1} so that a BMO solvability assumption in these sawtooth subdomains implies that the elliptic measure satisfies the weak A ∞ A_{\infty} condition with respect to the surface measure on this ‘ample’ sawtooth domain. This is a quantifiable absolute continuity condition, which is equivalent to saying the L p L^{p} Dirichlet problem is solvable for some 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty . Such singular drifts have been considered in the literature in the context of perturbative L p L^{p} Dirichlet solvability problems, by Hofmann-Lewis and Kenig-Pipher. By an ample sawtooth domain, we mean a sawtooth domain whose boundary coincides with the boundary of the unit ball, except for an arbitrarily small fraction. The methods can be naturally extended to show the result for more general bounded Lipschitz domains.

Remark 3
.

Note that we have presented the argument for the unit ball, for simplicity of notation. However, the methods can be easily extended to more general bounded Lipschitz domains, keeping track of the ‘top’, ‘bottom’ and ‘lateral’ faces of the Whitney cubes and running the Markov chain argument. This will be investigated in future.

Remark 4
.

Note that we have strictly only used the Carleson measure condition of
Eq.

2
for the cubes in
\mathbb{D}_{1}
, in constructing the sequence of subdomains. Our result is not a scale invariant estimate, and depends on the finite volume of the ball, and the result is the specified ’ample’ subdomains.

Remark 5
.

We note that if we wish to work with a stronger vanishing Carleson measure hypothesis, (see for example Definition 1.9 of
[
DP22
]
), while we have not worked out the modifications here, one should be routinely able to adopt the methods for the proof of Claim 1, to eventually pass to Carleson boxes of small enough radius for which the Carleson norm becomes arbitrarily small, and then analogously prove Claim 2 as well in this case. In this case, one doesn’t need to work in ‘ample’ subdomains as defined here.

Remark 6
.

We note that, in our construction of the ‘ample’ sawtooth domains, we have removed the Carleson boxes whose upper half consist of the ’bad’ cubes that fail the condition of
Definition

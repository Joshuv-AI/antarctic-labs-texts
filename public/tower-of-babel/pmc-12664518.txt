SLC7A11-independent disulfidptosis induced by a two-pronged delivery strategy for bladder cancer chemotherapy and cisplatin-resistance reverse
Authors: Bin Zheng, Facai Zhang, Heng Wang, Wenyan Zuo, Dingyi Liu, Zhihan Wu, Zhenghong Liu, Haichang Li, Yang Liu, Yixuan Mou, Xiaowen Qin, Dahong Zhang, Pu Zhang, Qi Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The intracellular overload of disulfide is induced by the imbalance between cystine (Cys) uptake and intracellular Cys reduction, leading to disulfidptosis. Solute carrier family 7 member 11 (SLC7A11) is a Cys transporter at the cell surface, and its high level of expression, together with glucose deprivation, is a requisite determining the disulfidptosis induction. Recent studies have exploited glucose deprivation by multifunctional nanomaterials as mainstream to evoke disulfidptosis, unfortunately with a strong bias to SLC7A11 highly expressed tumors. Despite many efforts to modulate SLC7A11 expression, the uncertainty brought by complex biological mechanisms under different exposure inspired us to find a more solid approach to raise Cys-uptake efficiency. In this study, we developed a two-pronged approach to deplete glucose and enrich intracellular Cys by co-biomineralizing Cys, glucose oxidase (Gox), and manganese as one entity, further cloaked with tumor cell membrane (M@Cys-Gox-Mn) to enhance tumor-selective uptake. M@Cys-Gox-Mn nanoparticles (NPs) displayed no SLC7A11-dependence for Cys transport and their synergistic action of the dual enzymes enables self-amplifying glucose depletion, thus exacerbating the disulfide stress. The use of M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs as monotherapy greatly combat orthotopic bladder tumors, and the combination with cisplatin overcome the chemoresistance. Both therapies achieved complete tumor inhibition with favorable biosafety.

Introduction

Disulfidptosis represents a distinct form of programmed cell death, driven by disulfide stress [1]. This process, in essential, is an imbalance between cystine (Cys) uptake and intracellular Cys reduction, two events largely dependent on SLC7A11 expression and glucose supply, respectively [2]. The eventual intracellular overload of disulfide is particularly detrimental to actin cytoskeleton proteins and destabilizes the cytoskeleton, culminating in cell dysfunction and cell death.

Recent studies have unveiled the potential of disulfidptosis induction to tackle malignancy by harnessing multifunctional nanomaterials to target the modality of cell death. The concept of glucose deprivation to activate disulfidptosis becomes mainstream in most cases, unfortunately with a strong bias to SLC7A11 highly expressed tumors [3]. More efforts have been made to improve the expression level of SLC7A11 and therefore the responsiveness of tumor cells to disulfidptosis. The redox imbalance [4,5] and the increased N6-methyladenosine modification [6] brought by different nanomaterials seemed to work well. However, multiple mechanisms participate in regulating SLC7A11 expression and may fail to function under the exposure to different stress [7,8]. The uncertainty on SLC7A11 modulation leads us to a more solid approach to raise Cys-uptake efficiency.

Herein, we loaded Cys into manganese (Mn) ions and glucose oxidase (Gox) self-assembled NPs and encapsulated them with cell membrane (CM) extracts (M@Cys-Gox-Mn) to enhance their tumor-selective uptake, thus achieving the SLC7A11-independent Cys import into tumor cells. And integrating Mn and Gox into one entity enabled the tandem of two catalytic reactions, pointing to the glucose consumption by Gox and the H2O2 decomposition by Mn, and the cyclic amplification of glucose deprivation [9]. As a result, M@Cys-Gox-Mn nanoparticles (NPs) guaranteed the high level of disulfide stress achievable regardless of tumor heterogeneity. Very encouraging results were found in their capacity against orthotopic bladder tumors. More importantly, we first extend the use of disulfidptosis induction to reverse cisplatin resistance in bladder tumors. The synergistic effect of M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs and cisplatin was strong to overcome chemoresistance, ablating cis-resistant orthotopic bladder tumors in all cases.

Materials and methods

Materials

Manganese chloride tetrahydrate and cystine were purchased from Sigma (Shanghai, China). Glucose oxidase (Gox), DiO, Fluorescein Phalloidin, ATP assay kit, Hoechst, EIPA, Chlorpromazine hydrochloride (CPZ), Filipin, Ferrostatin-1, Deferoxamine, Apoptosis inhibitor (Z-VAD), β-mercaptoethanol, Necrostatin-1 and -2 were purchased from MedChemExpress. Sulfo-NHS-Cy5 was purchased from Duofluor Inc (Wuhan, China). Potassium fluorescein was purchased from LABLEAD. Inc. (Beijing, China). Endoplasmic reticulum (ER) tracker, Golgi apparatus (GA) tracker, Lysosomes (Lys) Tracker and the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) was obtained from Yeasen Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). SLC7A11 antibody (# HA721868) was obtained from HUABIO, ExpressPlus™ PAGE Gel was purchased from GenScript. Marker was purchased from KEGEN&QTECH. NADP(H) content assay kit, reduced glutathione (GSH) content assay kit and oxidized glutathione (GSSG) content assay kit were purchased from Solarbio Life Science Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China).

Preparation and characterization of M@Cys-Gox-Mn

To prepare Gox-Mn NPs, Gox (1.5 mg/ml, 200 μl) was uniformly mixed with 2-methylimidazole (IMI; 7.5 mg/ml, 200 μl) and allowed to react for 30 min. Subsequently, MnCl2 (3 mg/ml, 200 μl) was introduced into the mixture. After 1 h reaction, Gox-Mn NPs were isolated by centrifugation at 12000 rpm for 20 min. A solution containing 50 mM L-cystine was prepared, and the pH was adjusted to 12.0 with 1 M NaOH. To initiate the co-biomineralization process, 30 μl of L-cystine solution was diluted into 940 μl of Tris-HCl buffer (10 mM, pH 8.0). Subsequently, 30 μl of the Gox-Mn suspension was introduced into the mixture. After 1 h incubation at room temperature, the resulting Cys-Gox-Mn NPs were isolated by centrifugation at 12000 rpm for 20 min.

Cells were harvested, washed and resuspended in Tris-HCl buffer (pH 7.4) containing 10 mM MgCl2, 1 × phenylmethylsulphonyl fluoride (PMSF), 0.2 mM EDTA, and a phosphatase inhibitor cocktail; the mixture was subjected to shaking at 4 °C overnight. The cell suspension was then sonicated thrice using an ultrasonic probe at 20 % amplitude for 20 s each. Subsequent centrifugation steps included three cycles at 500×g for 10 min at 4 °C, followed by centrifugation at 10,000×g for 30 min and finally at 70,000×g for 90 min, all at 4 °C. The resultant pellet, representing the cell membrane fragments, was resuspended in PBS buffer. To encapsulate Cys-Gox-Mn NPs with T24 cell membrane-derived nanovesicles, a solution of Cys-Gox-Mn (1 mg/ml, 1 ml) was mixed with cell membrane fragments (0.3 mg). This mixture was then transferred to a mini-extruder (Avanti Polar Lipids) fitted with a polycarbonate membrane (pore size: 400 nm). Following repeated extrusion, the product was subjected to high-speed centrifugation and washed with sterile ultrapure water to obtain the membrane-coated Cys-Gox-Mn nanoparticles (M@Cys-Gox-Mn).

The morphology of NPs and the elemental mapping analysis was recorded via scanning transmission electron microscope (TEM, JEM-3200FS, JEOL, Japan). FT-IR spectra were collected via Spectrum Two (ThermoFisher, USA). The combination of glucose oxidase mineralization, Cys loading and cell-membrane coating was analyzed by UV–visible spectrophotometer (Agilent Cary60). The particle size and Zeta potential of Gox-Mn, Cys-Gox-Mn, and M@Cys-G0x-Mn were measured via dynamic light scattering (DLS) techniques (Litesizer™500, AntonPaar, Graz, Austria).

M@Cys-GOx-Mn/Cys-Gox-Mn solution (Gox-equivalent dose: 1 mg/ml, 1 ml) was placed in dialysis bags (MWCO = 1000 Da) and then dialyzed against PBS at pH 7.4, 6.8, and 5.4 that mimic different biological environments (pH 7.4 represents the normal condition of the bloodstream and healthy tissues, pH 6.8 mimics the slightly acidic tumor microenvironment, pH 5.4 corresponds to the acidic interior of endosomes and lysosomes). The incubation was carried out at 37 °C under constant shaking. At predetermined time points, 1 ml of extracts from PBS buffer were sent for the Cys concentration measurement.

Gox, Gox-Mn, Cys-Gox-MN or M@Cys-Gox-Mn (Gox-equivalent dose: 0.2 μg/ml) was incubated with glucose solutions of varying concentrations (0–1000 μg/ml) for 4 h at room temperature. The mixture was collected for measuring pH via a pH meter (SX 620 pH tester). The catalytic dynamic of M@Cys-Gox-Mn against glucose (1000 μg/ml) was assessed at different time intervals. Mn, Gox-Mn, Cys-Gox-MN or M@Cys-Gox-Mn (Mn-equivalent dose: 0.06 μg/ml) was incubated with H2O2 (100 μM, 1 ml) for 2 h at room temperature. H2O2 consumption were detected by the H2O2 assay kit.

BCa-targeting efficiency

T24, 5637, and SVHUC-1 cells were seeded into laser confocal petri dishes at a density of 5 × 104 cells per well. The cells were then incubated with the indicated treatments (M@Cys-Gox-Mn were stained with DIO). Following treatment, the cells were washed three times with pre-cooled PBS and stained with DAPI to visualize nuclear components. Confocal laser scanning microscopy (CLSM) was employed to assess the targeting efficiency of M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs. Quantitative analysis of the imaging data was conducted using ImageJ software. Similarly, after treating the cells as described above, the targeting capability of M@Cys-Gox-Mn was further evaluated by flow cytometry.

Cytotoxicity assay in 2D culture models

The CCK-8 assay was used to assess the cytotoxicity of Mn Cys, Gox, Gox-Mn, Cys-Gox-Mn and M@Cys-Gox-Mn. 96-well plates were seeded with 10,000 T24/5637 cells per well and incubated with indicated components 24h. Then, a mixture of 10 μl of CCK-8 and 100 μl of medium (10:1 medium/CCK-8) was added to replace the previous medium, and then the cells were incubated for another 1 h. Cell viabilities were determined by using a microplate spectrophotometer at 450 nm.

The CCK-8 assay was also used to assess the cytotoxicity of Cis, M@Cys-Gox-Mn and Cis + M@Cys-Gox-Mn. 96-well plates were seeded with 10,000 T24, 5637, T24 SLC7A11, 5637 SLC7A11 or T24/Cis cells per well and incubated with indicated components for 48 h. The remaining steps are identical to those conducted previously.

Endocytic pathway analysis

To examine the intracellular uptake mechanisms of the nanoparticles, T24/5637 cells were seeded into laser confocal dishes at a density of 1 × 105 cells per dish. The cells were then preincubated with specific inhibitors targeting distinct endocytic pathways: chlorpromazine hydrochloride (CPZ, 10 μg/ml) to inhibit clathrin-mediated endocytosis, filipin (1 μg/ml) to block caveolae-mediated endocytosis, or ethyl isopropyl amiloride (EIPA, 10 μg/ml) to suppress micropinocytosis, each for 1 h. Cells were incubated at 4 °C to suppress energy-dependent endocytosis throughout the endocytic process. Cells were incubated with NPs (Gox labeled with Cy5) for 2 h, washed and sent for CLSM analysis.

Intracellular trafficking route analysis

T24 and 5637 cells were pre-incubated with NPs (Gox labeled with Cy5) for 2 h. Golgi apparatus, endoplasmic reticulum and lysosomes were stained with ER-Tracker Green, GA-Tracker Green, and Lys-Tracker Green, respectively, following the manufacturer's instructions. The cells were then imaged using a CLSM. Quantitative analysis of the spatial correlation between NPs and different organelle was conducted using ImageJ software.

Tumor penetration efficiency analysis

To generate MCSs, T24 and 5637 cells were suspended in DMEM or 1640 medium supplemented with 0.12 % (w/v) methylcellulose at a density of 1 × 106 cells per ml. Subsequently, 25 μL of the cell suspension was carefully placed on the lid of a cell culture plate to form uniform droplets. To maintain moisture, 10 ml of PBS buffer was added to the plate. After 72 h of incubation, the dense spheroids were carefully transferred to a low-adhesion 96-well plate, with one spheroid per well, and further incubated for an additional 72 h. For treatment, the MCSs were exposed to Gox, Gox-Mn, Cys-Gox-Mn, or M@Cys-Gox-Mn (Gox-equivalent dose: 0.2 μg/ml) for 4 h. Following the treatment, the MCSs were washed three times with PBS buffer and transferred for CLSM analysis. Z-stack imaging was performed, covering the range from the intermediate layer of the MCSs to the bottom layer, with 100 μm intervals. Each experiment was independently repeated three times to ensure reproducibility.

Cytotoxicity assay in 3D culture models

T24 and 5637 MCSs were established as previously reported and then incubated with the indicated treatment. After 24-h incubation, the MCSs were washed with PBS buffer and stained with Calcein-AM (Ex = 490 nm, Em = 515 nm) and Ropidium iodide (PI, Ex = 535 nm, Em = 617 nm) to indicate the live and the dead populations inside the MCSs. And then the MCSs were washed three times with PBS buffer and transferred for CLSM analysis. The Z-stack covered the plane near or along the intermediate layer at 50 μm intervals.

Hemolysis assay

Briefly, 950 μl of cell membrane (0.3 mg), Gox-Mn, Cys-Gox-Mn, M@Cys-Gox-Mn (Gox-equivalent dose: 0.2 μg/ml) solution was mixed with 50 μl of red blood cell (RBC) suspension (10 %, v/v) and incubated in a shaker at 37 °C. ddH2O was mixed with blood cells as the positive control, and PBS was used as the negative control. After 2 h of incubation, the samples were centrifuged at 1500 rpm for 10 min, and photographs of the hemolysis results were recorded by a digital camera. The absorbance of the supernatant was measured at 540 nm with a microplate reader. Hemolysis ratio (%) = (ODx − ODnegative)/(ODpositive − ODnegative) × 100 %.

Western blotting

T24 and 5637 cells were transduced with lentiviral particles encoding full-length SLC7A11 cDNA. Cells were seeded at 60 % confluency 24 h prior to transduction. Viral supernatant (MOI = 10) was applied to cells. After 48 h, transduced cells were selected using 2 μg/ml puromycin for 7 days. Western blotting was performed following the standard protocol. Protein samples from T24, 5637, T24 SLC7A11, 5637 SLC7A11, or T24/Cis cells were prepared. Equal concentrations of protein solutions were heated at 100 °C for 10 min. Subsequently, the protein samples underwent a series of steps, including electrophoresis, transfer, blocking, and incubation with primary and secondary antibodies. Finally, the protein abundance were quantified using a chemiluminescence imager.

Anti-cancer efficacy of M@Cys-Gox-Mn in murine orthotopic BCa models

All animal procedures were approved by the Laboratory Animal Management Committee at Zhejiang Provincial People's Hospital (20240918073790) and performed in accordance with institutional and provincial guidelines. Female nu/nu mice bearing BCa derived from luciferase-transfected T24 (T24 luc) or T24/Cis (T24/Cis luc) cells were prepared as previously described. During catheterization, the mice were anesthetized with 1–2 % isoflurane in oxygen and maintained on a warming pad to ensure their comfort and stability. NPs were administered intravenously. Intratumoral injections were performed using glucose oxidase (Gox), Gox-manganese (Gox-Mn), cystine-glucose oxidase-manganese (Cys-Gox-Mn), and tumor membrane-coated cystine-glucose oxidase-manganese (M@Cys-Gox-Mn) in T24 luc orthotopic bladder tumors (Gox-equivalent dose: 20 μg/kg). Additionally, Cis, M@Cys-Gox-Mn, and the combination of Cis + M@Cys-Gox-Mn (Cis-equivalent dose: 4 mg/kg; Gox-equivalent dose: 10 μg/kg) were administered in T24/Cis luc orthotopic bladder tumors. The control group received an equivalent volume of saline, with treatments administered every seven days for a total of five sessions. To monitor tumor bioluminescence in vivo, the mice were intraperitoneally injected with 100 mg/kg D-luciferin and imaged using the IVIS Spectrum system (IVIS Lumina XRMS Series III, PerkinElmer Inc., Waltham, US) every 7 days. Seven days following the final intravenous therapy, the blood samples were collected from the retro-orbital sinus under anesthesia for evaluating liver and kidney function. Then, the mice tissues (including bladder, heart, liver, spleen, lung, and kidney) were collected for histopathological examination via hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining. The tissues were subsequently imaged using an inverted microscope (APEXVIEW™, China).

Results and discussion

Preparation and characterization of M@Cys-Gox-Mn

Fig. 1 briefly describes the preparation of M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs. Leveraging the principle of biomineralization [10], Mn ions and Gox self-assemble into NPs, further incorporating Cys. Cys-Gox-Mn NPs are next coated with CM. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) imaging revealed that Gox-Mn NPs, Cys-Gox-Mn NPs and M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs were all monodisperse and spherical. A thin and mist layer cound be found on the border of M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs (Fig. 2a), indicating the successful surface coating with the CM. The average particle size was estimated to be ∼180 nm for Gox-Mn NPs and increased to ∼200 nm and ∼210 nm for Cys-Gox-Mn NPs and M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs, respectively (Fig. 2b). The DPI of Gox-Mn NPs, Cys-Gox-Mn NPs and M@Cys-Gox-Mn was less than 0.2. A generally accepted criterion for nano-formulations intended for intravenous injection is a Polydispersity Index (PDI) value of less than 0.2. This threshold is a key quality control parameter, signifying a highly uniform and monodisperse particle population that is critical for both safety and predictable in vivo performance. The zeta potentials were −16.7 ± 0.2 mV and −16.9 ± 0.5 mV for Gox-Mn NPs and Cys-Gox-Mn NPs, respectively, and that dropped to −23.7 ± 0.2 mV after NP surface modification with CM, closely matching the inherent zeta potential of CM and indicative of enhanced colloidal stability of NP dispersion (Fig. 2c) [11]. TEM/EDS analysis proved the presence and the homogeneous distribution of Mn, S, O and N four elements inside Cys-Gox-Mn NPs, indicating successful Cys loading (Fig. 2d–S1). Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) provided insights into the bonding interactions within Gox-Mn NPs (Fig. 2e). The peak at 540 cm−1 in Gox-Mn NPs, attributed to Mn-O stretching, indicates Mn ion coordination. The significant amide I band at 1651 cm−1, associated with C=O stretching in Gox, shows a shift compared to the free enzyme, reflecting carbonyl oxygen coordination with Mn ions. The sharp N-H peak observed at 3279 cm−1 is attributed to the free amino group of Cys, which is distinct from the broad N-H peak associated with the peptide bonds in Gox protein. The double peaks at 2919/2847 cm−1 are characteristic of the stretching vibrations of the Cys -CH2-CH2- chain, showing significant shifts compared to the side-chain C-H peaks of Gox at 2930/2850 cm−1. In M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs, sharp peaks at 2935 cm−1 and 2871 cm−1, distinct from Cys-Gox-Mn NPs, represent saturated C-H bonds from the CM' s phospholipid chains. Strong new peaks at 1247 cm−1 and 1031 cm−1 are assigned to P=O asymmetric stretching and P-O-C stretching in phospholipid head groups, characteristic of the phospholipid bilayer. UV–vis spectroscopy analysis revealed that the strong absorption peak of Gox-Mn NPs at 196 nm is due to the d-d electronic transitions of Mn ions, while the peak at 202 nm originates from π→π∗ transitions of aromatic amino acids in the enzyme after coordination (Fig. 2f). This contrasts with the 278 nm absorption of free Gox, indicating significant changes in the chromophoric environment of the enzyme due to metal coordination. In Cys-Gox-Mn NPs, the peak at 199 nm corresponds to the n→σ∗ transition of the non-protonated thiol group in Cys, a characteristic feature of sulfur-containing amino acids. The peak at 223 nm, representing a red-shifted n→π∗ transition of the peptide bond, is attributed to the contribution of Cys' s disulfide bond. For M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs, the strong peak at 193 nm corresponds to the n→π∗ transition of the carbonyl group in the CM' s phospholipids, while the doublet at 202 nm originates from π→π∗ transitions of aromatic amino acids in membrane proteins. The pH-responsive Cys release profile was also investigated (Fig. 2g and h). At pH 5.4, both M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs and Cys-Gox-Mn NPs rapidly released Cys, with the cumulative release reaching 64.8 % and 76.8 % within 48 h, respectively. At pH 6.8, the release of Cys from M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs and Cys-Gox-Mn NPs was greatly inhibited, with the cumulative release dropping to 25.2 % and 35.5 %. At pH 7.4, the Cys release of M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs and Cys-Gox-Mn NPs was inhibited more severely, with the cumulative release dropping to 24.5 % and 33.3 % respectively. The burst release of Cys at pH 5.4 was mainly attributed to be the NP collapse, because the particle size of M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs/Cys-Gox-Mn NPs dramastically decreased from 170.4 nm/189.6 nm to 104.7/122.1 nm. The integrity of NPs was less severely disrupted by pH 6.8 and well maintained at pH 7.4 (Fig. 2i–k). The structural stability of NPs exposed to different pH was further verified via TEM analysis (Fig. S2). A multitude of studies have highlighted the pivotal role of Gox in catalyzing the glucose into H2O2 and gluconic acid under aerobic conditions, and hence the enzyme activity of Gox could be assessed by monitoring the glucose consumption and pH change. The results demonstrated that the enzyme activity was slightly impaired by the biomineralization and CM coating (Fig. 2l and m), compared with naive Gox, but the efficiency, up to the 87 %–94 %, still met the requirement of glucose consumption [12]. Additionally, we validated the catalase activity of M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs (Fig. S3), which was essential to maintain the cyclic amplification of glucose depletion. 67.1 % of H2O2 was catalyzed by M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs (Mn-equivalent dose: 0.06 μg/ml), demonstrating its high catalase activity comparable to Mn. After the long-term exposure to different pH, the catalytic activity of M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs didn't show any tendency to regress. However, naive Gox couldn't bare the harsh environment of low pH as its catalytic activity after 5 day-incubation decreased to only 1.0 % of that at 0 day (Fig. 2n). Taken together, the biomineralization and CM coating offered protection for Gox in M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs against harsh environment, which was a prerequisite for long-term storage and in vivo use, although the enzyme-substrate contact might be kind of limited.

The cytotoxicity of M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs mediated by disulfidptosis

Gox can effectively catalyze the oxidation of glucose into gluconic acid and H2O2 [[13], [14], [15]], which in turn induces oxidative stress and related cell apoptosis. The oxidative stress-dependent apoptosis may mask the effect of disulfidptosis raised by Cys-Gox-Mn NPs or M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs. The risk of the uncertainty about which modality of cell death contributed most was mitigated by the cell exposure to the Gox dose below 0.2 mg/ml, which guaranteed robust cell viability of both T24 and 5637 cells (≥90 %) (Fig. S4a and b). However, M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs at the Gox-equivalent dose of 0.2 μg/ml displayed extreme toxicity to both cells, approximately 14.7-fold, 13.0-fold and 2.7-fold stronger than Gox, Gox-Mn NPs and Cys-Gox-Mn NPs respectively (Fig. S4c and d). The possibility of toxicity induced by Mn, Cys, or the CM was excluded (Fig. S4e–i). And therefore, the coparticipation of Cys and Gox-Mn NPs was crucial to initiate the cell death, and the CM coating was believed to increase the cellular uptake in tumor cells and endow NPs with tumor-targeting property (Fig. S5).

We next unveiled the mechanism which modality of cell death M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs could activate, by using inhibitors specifically targeting different cell death pathways. Under the threat from M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs, ferroptosis inhibitors (Ferristatin-1 (Ferr-1) or deferoxamine (DFO)), apoptosis inhibitor (Z-VAD-fmk (Z-VAD)) and necroptosis inhibitors (necrostatin-1 or -2 (Nec-1 and -2)),1 all failed to rescue cells from death, whereas reductants β-mercaptoethanol (β-ME) only or the cocktail of β-ME and other inhibitors succeeded (Fig. 3a–d, S6). These results points to the disulfidptosis possibly being the main mechanism. Key steps following the disulfidptosis process were further explored. PBS-treated cells growing in high-glucose medium was 1.4-fold richer in intracellular glucose, compared to M@Cys-Gox-Mn-treated cells (Fig. 4a). The intracellular ATP levels in M@Cys-Gox-Mn-treated cells reduced by 79.6 %, compared to PBS-treated cells (Fig. 4b), indicative of impaired energy supply in M@Cys-Gox-Mn-treated cells. NADPH represents an intersection between glycometabolism and redox homeostasis [16]. We found that M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs eliminated 79.6 % of intracellular NADPH compared to normal cells (Fig. 4c). As expected, the intracellular redox homeostasis was broken with GSSG/GSH ratio up to 18.5 (Fig. 4d). Other NPs had much weaker impact on the ‘NADPH/GSH’ signaling pathway than M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs did. Therefore, the aberrant disulfide bonds in actin cytoskeleton proteins led to F-actin collapse [1]. Cell shrinkage and F-actin contraction became obvious in T24 and 5637 cells receiving the M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs treatment (Fig. 4e and f). Cellular uptake, subcellular trafficking and tumor penetration of M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs

The endocytosis pathway of M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs were investigated (Fig. S7a–d). The endocytosis of M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs in both T24 and 5637 cells followed the same pattern, nearly abolished by 4 °C or Filipin but unaffected by Clathrin and EIPA. And therefore, the caveolae-mediated approach was preferred for M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs. Many studies have demonstrated that the endocytosis pathway of NPs directs the subsequent trafficking fate [17]. Particularly, the caveolae-mediated endocytosis usually provides roadways for NPs to bypass lysosomes, which trap, degrade and disable NPs [18]. Consistent with the above prediction, M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs were mostly transported to endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and Golgi apparatus, with few trapped in lysosomes. Approximately 90 % colocalization between M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs and ER/Golgi was achieved, whereas the colocalized correlation between M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs and lysosomes was as weak as ∼40 % (Fig. 5a–l). Multicellular spheroids (MCSs) have been established as an ideal in vitro model to mimic tumor tissues for the many aspects of use [19]. Their densely packed structures are usually impermeable to drugs. As illustrated in Fig. 6a–d, the distribution of Gox, Gox-Mn NPs and Cys-Gox-Mn NPs was confined to the MCS border. Notably, M@Cys-Gox -Mn NPs were capable of penetrating deep and distributed throughout T24 and 5637 MCSs. Anticancer effect of M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs in orthotopic BCa models

The IC50 values of M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs were determined by CCK-8, being 20.67 ng/ml and 28.06 ng/ml for T24 and 5637 cells, respectively (Fig. S8). More reliable toxicity estimation comes from the live/dead population analysis in MCSs. Gox had negligible toxicity against T24 and 5637 MCSs (Figs. S9a, b, S10a, b). Gox-Mn NPs and Cys-Gox-Mn NPs caused moderate toxicity against both MCSs (Fig. S9c-f, S10c-f). The most potent toxicity in both MCSs was observed following the exposure to M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs (Figs. S9g, h, S9g, h), approximately 2.5-fold higher than that induced by Cys-Gox-Mn NPs (Fig. S11). The therapeutic outcomes of different NPs were also tested in murine orthotopic BCa models. Gox, Gox-Mn NPs, Cys-Gox-Mn NPs or M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs were intravenously given at the Gox-equivalent dose of 0.2 μg/ml. The therapies were repeated every 7 days for five times in total. The tumor growth in the Gox-treated group was vigorous throughout the whole therapy, exhibiting a rising trend as similar as that in the control group. In contrast, Gox-Mn NPs and Cys-Gox-Mn NPs both led to limited tumor regression after five rounds of therapies. Astonishingly, a single dose of M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs greatly hindered the tumor growth, bringing the tumor inhibition rate below 40 %. The complete tumor eradication was detected in two cases after the second injection and finally fulfilled in all cases (Fig. 7a and b). Their therapeutic efficacy was also confirmed by histopathological examination. The bladders were sliced from their apex to bottom, and H&E stained, with particular interest in the sections along the plane of the maximum diameter of the tumor. No BCa was residual in the M@Cys-Gox-Mn-treated group, but small cancerous lesions still settled in the bladder, even invading the muscular layer in the Cys-Gox-Mn-treated group and the Gox-Mn-treated group. The biosafety of different therapies attracted our concern. All NPs and Gox were non-hemolytic, ensuring their biocompatibility in the circulation (Fig. S12). Organs, including heart, liver, spleen, lung and renal, didn't undergo pathological change after different therapeutic periods (Fig. 7d). Stable weight gain was common for each group until the middle of therapeutic periods and only continued in those tumor-inhibited groups (Fig. 7e). Biochemical analysis of blood revealed no significant abnormalities in liver function and renal function across all groups, including liver injury-related biomarker (alanine aminotransferase and aspartate aminotransferase) (Fig. 7f and g), urea nitrogen (BUN) and creatinine (CREA) (Fig. 7h and i). In summary, M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs effectively tackled bladder cancer and meanwhile the off-target toxicity was mitigated, thereby promising their clinical and translational potential.

SLC7A11 expression and cisplatin resistance in BCa

SLC7A11 is a Cys transporter, and its upregulation stands as a vital mechanism of multidrug resistance in many types of cancer, especially cisplatin (Cis) resistance [21,22]. Cysteine is a rate limiting precursor in the intracellular synthesis of GSH, which protects cells against ROS induced by cisplatin. But the deficient NADPH production leads to the intracellular overload of cysteine [7] and turns the cysteine over to the disulfidptosis pathway. These insights inspired us to exploit M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs to overcome cisplatin resistance.

SLC7A11 upregulated cell lines (T24 SLC7A11, 5637 SLC7A11) and a Cis-resistant cell line (T24/Cis) were established (Fig. S13). And Their expression of SLC7A11 was much higher than that of naive T24 and 5637 cells, which provides an explanation for the deficient disulfidptosis induced by Gox-Mn NPs in naive T24 and 5637 cells. The IC50 value of Cis was approximately 2.44 μM and 4.30 μM for T24 and 5637 cells, respectively, and that increased to 6.93 μM, 6.64 μM, and 13.27 μM for T24 SLC7A11, 5637 SLC7A11, and T24/Cis, respectively (Fig. S14a). As compared to naive cells, the IC50 value of M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs dropped down to 9.40 μg/ml, 9.62 μg/ml, and 8.09 μg/ml for T24 SLC7A11, 5637 SLC7A11, and T24/Cis, respectively (Fig. S14b). The combined use of M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs and Cis was then aimed to help regain the Cis chemosensitivity. The Gox-equivalent dose of 4 ng/ml for M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs and 1 μM for Cis were both the safe threshold for the maintenance of cell viability (≥80 %) when separately applied, and the same concentrations in their mixture were set as 100 %. The IC50 values of M@Cys-Gox-Mn + Cis were 68.77 %/58.9 % for T24/5637 cells and 48.5 %/47.4 % for their SLC7A11 upregulated counterparts. Importantly, the most pronounced sensitization to Cis was obtained by T24/Cis cells, with the IC50 value being only 34.9 % (Fig. S14c and d).

Cis resistance reversed by M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs in orthotopic BCa models

We followed the same protocol as in section 2.7 to validate whether M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs could reverse Cis resistance of BCa in vivo. The monotherapy of Cis or M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs mildly retarded the tumor growth, while their combination produced a strong anti-cancer effect (Fig. 8a and b). All tumors were chemo-ablated after the third combination therapy, with no recurrence observed during the whole period, achieving a 100 % tumor inhibition rate consequently. More solid evidence was offered by pathological examination. There was no trace of BCa in mice accepting the combination therapy, but tumor progression seemed very severe in the other three groups (Fig. 8c), who experienced the weight loss at a later stage (Fig. 8d). Conclusion

In this study, we developed a two-pronged approach to deplete glucose and enrich intracellular Cys by co-biomineralizing Cys, glucose oxidase (Gox), and manganese as one entity, further cloaked with tumor cell membrane (M@Cys-Gox-Mn) to achieve SLC7A11-independent disulfidptosis induction specifically in tumor cells. M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs displayed no SLC7A11-dependence for Cys transport and their synergistic action of the dual enzymes enables self-amplifying glucose depletion, thus exacerbating the disulfide stress. The use of M@Cys-Gox-Mn NPs as monotherapy greatly combat orthotopic bladder tumors, and the combination with cisplatin overcome the chemoresistance. Both therapies achieved complete tumor inhibition with favorable biosafety.

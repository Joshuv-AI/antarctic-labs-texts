7 | Cervical Stiffness to Predict Preterm Birth in Women With Threatened Preterm Labor

D. Colacurci1; James A. L. Meliëzer2; S. Reccia1; N. Moriarty3; A. Shennan3; E. Pajkrt2; Martijn A. Oudijk2; G. Saccone1; F. J. R. J. R. Hermans2

1Department of Public Health, School of Medicine, University of Naples Federico II, Naples, Italy; 2Amsterdam UMC, University of Amsterdam, Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, Amsterdam, Netherlands; 3Department of Women and Children's Health, King's College London, London, United Kingdom

Objective: To assess cervical stiffness to improve prediction of preterm birth (PTB) in patients presenting with symptoms of threatened preterm labor (tPTL).

Study Design: Pooled analysis of two single‐center prospective cohort studies (NCT05355649, NCT05477381) of women with singleton pregnancies (24+0–33+6 weeks’ gestation) presenting with symptoms of tPTL (excluding women with ruptured membranes or previous cervical surgery). Women received transvaginal ultrasound cervical length (CL) and assessment of cervical stiffness index (CSI, expressed in mbar and measured with the Pregnolia System). Primary outcome was delivery within 7 days; secondary outcomes were delivery within 14 days, and PTB before 34 and 37 weeks gestation.

Results: 387 women were included: 148 (38.2%) had a PTB <37 weeks (median CSI of 44 mbar, IQR 32–58), of which 17 (4.4%) delivered ≤7 days (31 mbar, 24–53), 48 (12.4%) ≤14 days from measurement (32 mbar, 25–38), and 59 (15.2%) <34 weeks’ gestation (33 mbar, 25–46) Figure 1 shows CSI and CL data, indicating who delivered within 7 or 14 days of measurement, or more than 14 days after measurements. Women delivering within timeframes of interest had significantly lower CSI than those delivering later. The area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) for prediction of delivery ≤7 days after measurement was 0.78 (95% CI 0.65–0.91), for ≤14 days 0.88 (0.82–0.93) and PTB before <34 and <37 weeks 0.83 (0.76–0.89) and 0.79 (0.75–0.84) respectively. Combined with CL in a logistic regression model, the AUC for delivery ≤7 days increased to 0.89 (0.81–0.96), for ≤14 days to 0.92 (0.87–0.96), for <34 weeks to 0.88 (0.83–0.93) and for <37 weeks to 0.84 (0.80–0.88) (Figure 2a–c). Figure 2d shows the probability of PTB with 95% CI as a function of CSI, based on univariate logistic regression.

Conclusion: In women with tPTL, a softer cervix is associated with an increased risk of PTB. CSI is predictive of time to delivery and improves the predictive performance of CL measurement.

8 | Screen‐Guided Care for Preterm Birth Prevention in Nulliparous Pregnancies: A PRIME Trial Subgroup Analysis

Cynthia Gyamfi‐Bannerman1; Michael Walker2; Babak Shahbaba3; Brian K. Iriye4; Jannik Godt5; Anthony C. Sciscione6

1UC San Diego, Dept of Obstetrics, Gynecology, and Reproductive Sciences, San Diego, CA; 2Walker Biosciences, Carlsbad, CA; 3University of California, Irvine, Irvine, CA; 4High Risk Pregnancy Center; Hera Women's Health, Las Vegas, NV; 5JGConsult, Nærum, Denmark; 6ChristianaCare, Newark, DE

Objective: The PRIME randomized trial showed improved neonatal outcomes using biomarker‐guided risk stratification to target an intervention bundle to pregnancies at higher risk of preterm birth. This subgroup analysis assessed the effectiveness of this approach in nulliparous pregnancies, a group that cannot be risk stratified by pregnancy history.

Study Design: PRIME (NCT04301518) was a 19‐center, randomized controlled trial evaluating a screen‐guided care strategy using the IGFBP4/SHBG biomarker ratio versus routine care in otherwise low‐risk singleton pregnancies. In the screen‐guided care arm, those identified as higher risk for preterm birth were offered vaginal progesterone, low‐dose aspirin, and weekly telephonic nursing support, while others received routine care. This secondary analysis of nulliparous (G1P0) pregnancies reports composite neonatal morbidity and NICU admissions. Models were adjusted for pre‐enrollment aspirin use and COVID‐19 positivity.

Results: Of 5018 PRIME participants, 1780 were nulliparous. Baseline characteristics did not differ by study arm. The screen‐guided care arm had significant reductions in composite neonatal morbidity versus routine care (Figure 1). The NICU admission rate was 12.8% in the screen‐guided care arm versus 16.4% in the routine care arm (p = 0.039). The number needed to screen to prevent one NICU admission was 28. After adjusting for covariates, the odds of NICU admission were lower in the screen‐guided care arm (OR 0.75; 95% CI, 0.57–0.98; p = 0.036) (Figure 2). These reductions were concentrated in spontaneous preterm births. Interventions were well‐tolerated with no serious adverse events among nulliparous participants in the screen‐guided care arm.

Conclusion: In G1P0 nulliparous PRIME trial participants, screen‐guided care reduced composite neonatal morbidity and NICU admissions compared to routine care. These findings support the clinical utility of biomarker‐guided preventive strategies in first‐time pregnancies lacking prior obstetric history.

9 | Term Delivery Following Late Preterm Antenatal Steroid Administration in US Hospitals

Mark A. Clapp1; Dohyun Lee1; Siguo Li1; Jessica L. Cohen2; Scott A. Lorch3; Jason Wright4; Anjali J. Kaimal5; Cynthia Gyamfi‐Bannerman6; Alexander Melamed1

1Massachusetts General Hospital, Boston, MA; 2Harvard TH Chan School of Public Health, Boston, MA; 3Children's Hospital of Philadelphia, Philadelphia, PA; 4Tufts Medical Center, Boston, MA; 5University of South Florida, Tampa, FL; 6UC San Diego, Dept of Obstetrics, Gynecology, and Reproductive Sciences, San Diego, CA

Objective: Given the challenges in accurately predicting birth timing, we sought to quantify the proportion of late preterm steroid administrations that ultimately result in a term delivery outside the confines of a clinical trial to inform shared decision‐making.

Study Design: This was a retrospective cohort study of singleton deliveries from the Premier Healthcare Database between January 2021 and December 2024. Late preterm steroid exposure was defined as betamethasone administration between ≥34 and <37 weeks of gestation. We calculated the proportion of late preterm steroid exposures that were delivered at term among hospitals. For comparison, we also reported the proportion of all term births exposed to late preterm antenatal steroids. To determine the relationship between “optimal” and “suboptimal” administration among hospitals, we plotted the proportion of late preterm births exposed to steroids against late preterm steroid exposures that delivered at term.

Results: The cohort included 553 hospitals and 95,033 singleton delivery hospitalizations with confirmed late‐preterm betamethasone exposure. Of these, 70.7% (67,170) delivered in the late preterm period and 29.3% (27,863) delivered at term; this equated to 1.0% (27,863/2,865,272) of all term births being exposed to late preterm steroids. Hospital‐level proportions of late preterm steroid exposures that were delivered at term ranged from 0% to 83% (Figure 1), reflecting substantial heterogeneity in steroid use. There was no relationship between optimal and suboptimal late‐preterm steroid administration rates across hospitals (Figure 2).

Conclusion: In this contemporary US cohort, which represents approximately 25% of all US births, nearly 28,000 term singleton neonates per year were exposed to antenatal steroids without expected benefit, a higher percentage than reported in the ALPS Trial (16%). The heterogeneity in practice patterns and these findings emphasize 1) the need for better methods to estimate preterm birth risk and 2) the importance of including delivery timing uncertainty in shared decision‐making surrounding late preterm steroid use.

10 | Utilizing Bayesian Analysis to Enhance Post‐Emergency Cerclage Counseling

Aaron W. Roberts; Ahmed Zaki Moustafa; Claudia Pedroza; Sean C. Blackwell

McGovern Medical School at UTHealth, Houston, TX

Objective: In maternal‐fetal medicine, rare conditions and small cohorts can limit power and obscure clinically meaningful findings under traditional frequentist statistics. Bayesian methods estimate the probability of clinically relevant outcomes, improving interpretability for decision‐making and patient counseling. We apply a Bayesian framework to outcomes after physical exam‐indicated cerclage, using post‐procedure cervical length (CL) to inform prognosis and guide surgical training.

Study Design: A cohort of patients who underwent physical exam‐indicated cerclage were analyzed using both frequentist and Bayesian methods. The primary outcome was preterm birth (PTB) <34 weeks. Bayesian models using neutral priors estimated posterior probabilities (PP) of treatment benefit for CL >25 mm compared to ≤25 mm. Pregnancy latency was assessed using Bayesian linear regression to estimate the PP of achieving clinically meaningful increases in latency relative to CL ≤25 mm.

Results: A total of 101 patients were included (CL ≤25 mm, n = 50; CL > 25 mm, n = 51). Frequentist analysis showed no significant difference in PTB <34 weeks (46% vs 31%, p = 0.13). Bayesian analysis showed a 92% PP of reduced PTB <34 weeks for CL > 25 mm (Posterior RR 0.72, 95% CrI 0.44–1.14) compared to CL ≤25 mm. Mean latency was increased by 2.24 weeks (95% CrI, −0.04, 4.52 weeks) in CL > 25 mm compared to CL ≤25 mm (Table 1). PP of achieving clinically meaningful latency gains was: 86% for ≥1 week, 58% for ≥2 weeks, 26% for ≥3 weeks, and 6% for ≥4 weeks (Table 2). The PP of latency >28 days was 98% for CL > 25 mm compared to CL ≤25 mm.

Conclusion: Although frequentist analysis failed to identify a statistically significant difference, Bayesian analysis demonstrated a high probability of clinically meaningful benefit associated with post‐cerclage CL > 25 mm. Bayesian methods provide interpretable, patient‐centered estimates that may improve counseling and inform procedural targets following emergency cerclage.

11 | Neonatal Outcomes in Preterm Breech Presentation: A Nationwide Cohort Study in the Netherlands (2012–2023)

Amy E. C. M. Bont1; James A. L. Meliëzer2; A. C. J. Ravelli1; Martijn A. Oudijk2

1Amsterdam UMC, Amsterdam, Netherlands; 2Amsterdam UMC, University of Amsterdam, Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, Amsterdam, Netherlands

Objective: To evaluate neonatal outcomes associated with intended mode of delivery in preterm breech deliveries.

Study Design: A nationwide retrospective cohort study using data from the Netherlands Perinatal Registry, including all singleton preterm breech births between 26 + 0 and 36 + 6 weeks’ gestation born from 2012 to 2023. Multivariable logistic regression compared intended vaginal delivery with planned caesarean section. Primary outcomes were neonatal mortality and a composite of mortality and morbidity. Exploratory analysis was performed on earliest viable gestational ages (24 + 0 to 25 + 6).

Results: 7386 women with a preterm singleton breech delivery were included, with 4371 intended vaginal deliveries and 3015 planned cesarean deliveries. No difference was found in neonatal mortality for intended vaginal compared to planned cesarean deliveries (2.9% vs. 2.6%; adjusted Odds Ratio (aOR) 1.01, 95% Confidence Interval (CI) 0.74–1.36). Rates of composite neonatal outcome (mortality and morbidities) were comparable (23.3% vs. 23.3%), but multivariate regression favored vaginal delivery (aOR 0.87, 95% CI 0.76–0.99). For preterm birth between 24 and 26 weeks of gestation, higher rates of mortality were observed for intended vaginal delivery (53.6% vs. 32.9%; aOR 2.31, 95% CI 1.28–4.18), while composite outcome was comparable (91.4% vs. 81.1%; aOR 1.96, 95% CI 0.85–4.48).

Conclusion: In this national retrospective cohort study, planned cesarean delivery for preterm breech births at 26+0 to 36+6 weeks of gestation offered no clear neonatal advantage over intended vaginal delivery. Higher rates of mortality following intended vaginal delivery at 24+0 to 25+6 weeks’ gestation should be interpreted with caution, considering possible inclusion of non‐active management cases (see Table 1).

12 | Antenatal Corticosteroid Exposure and Long‐Term Ophthalmologic Morbidity in Premature Infants

Sivan Partush Chocron1; Gali Pariente2; Tamar Wainstock3; Eyal Sheiner2

1Soroka University Medical Center, Beersheba, Israel; 2Soroka University Medical Center, Beer Sheva, Israel; 3Department of Epidemiology and Health Services Evaluation, Ben‐Gurion University of the Negev, Beer Sheva, Israel

Objective: Antenatal corticosteroid (ACS) exposure in premature infants is associated with a reduced risk of immediate neonatal morbidities and perinatal mortality; however, its long‐term effects on childhood morbidity have not yet been fully evaluated. The goal of the study was to investigate the association between ACS exposure before 34 weeks gestation and long‐term ophthalmologic morbidity among preterm offspring.

Study Design: A retrospective population‐based cohort study of preterm singleton deliveries between 1991–2021 was conducted. Exposure was defined as administration of antenatal betamethasone. Long‐term ophthalmic morbidity of preterm offspring who were exposed to ACS prior to 34 weeks of gestation was compared with unexposed children, based on combined databases from both community and hospitalization records. Kaplan‐Meier survival curves were used to compare the cumulative incidence of ophthalmic morbidity and Cox regression analyses were used to evaluate outcomes, adjusting for confounders.

Results: A total of 13,580 preterm deliveries were included; 1538 (11.3%) children were exposed to ACS prior to 34 weeks. Total ophthalmic morbidity incidence per 1000 person‐years (Table) as well as the cumulative incidence over time (Figure) were significantly higher in premature ACS exposed infants compared to premature unexposed children. In a Cox regression analysis, antenatal betamethasone exposure remained independently associated with increased ophthalmologic morbidity (Adjusted HR 1.3, 95% CI 1.15–1.48; p < 0.001). This association remained statistically significant also within the extremely preterm subgroup born before 28 weeks of gestation (Adjusted HR 1.9, 95% CI 1.22–2.85; p = 0.004).

Conclusion: ACS exposure in preterm infants is associated with increased long‐term ophthalmologic morbidity. This association may reflect a true adverse effect or result from indication bias, whereby higher‐risk pregnancies are more likely to receive ACS. These findings underscore the importance of further research to clarify long‐term safety.

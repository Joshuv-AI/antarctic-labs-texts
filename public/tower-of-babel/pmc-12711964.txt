Molecular assessment of the HIV pol gene and use of computational vaccine design targeting Pakistani isolates
Authors: Akmal Zubair, Faisal Ahmad, Syeda Maryam Hussain, Zahid Manzoor, Ahmed Al-Emam, Safa Wdidi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) is considered a major threat to human health. The global prevalence of individuals infected with HIV-1 continues to rise. Consequently, the development of an effective immune-stimulatory vaccine has recently garnered significant attention in the field of HIV-1 vaccine formulation. This study conducted a molecular assessment of the Pol region, identifying conserved sequences across our 14 HIV isolates. Utilizing multiple servers and filtering methods, highly effective B-cell epitopes, along with helper T lymphocyte (HTL) and cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) epitopes, were identified. Specifically, five B-cell epitopes, eight CTL epitopes, and twelve HTL epitopes were selected for vaccine development. The resulting vaccine construct comprises 557 amino acids, has a theoretical isoelectric point of 8.98, and a GRAVY score of − 0.785. Codon optimization and in-silico cloning into prokaryotic vectors were performed to enhance expression Escherichia coli hosts. Molecular dynamics simulations confirmed the most stable interaction of the vaccine components with Toll-like receptor 3 (TLR3), while also demonstrating stable interactions with TLR5, TLR7, TLR8, and TLR9, eliciting a robust immunogenic response. The vaccine model effectively activates both humoral and cellular immune responses; however, experimental validation is required to confirm these in silico findings.

Introduction

Despite recent progress in immunization, the spread of the human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) remains a persistent challenge. HIV-1 causes acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS) by impairing various human immune cell types and employing strategies to evade the immune system1. Recent studies demonstrate that HIV induces a progressive yet steady decline in immune cell populations, particularly CD4 + T cells. Researchers have consistently pursued strategies to impede the spread of HIV-12. Nonetheless, clinical trials underscore the critical need to develop innovative approaches to enhance vaccine efficacy3. Current efforts focus on the development of epitope-based vaccines to combat HIV-1 infection, facilitated by recent technological advancements4,5. Epitope-based immunizations offer several advantages over traditional methods, including simplified production and storage, increased specificity, improved safety, and enhanced stability6. Thus, the advent of epitope-based techniques marks a new era in vaccine development7,8. The effectiveness of an HIV-1 vaccine in eliminating infected cells depends on its ability to elicit durable humoral and cellular immune responses, as demonstrated by recent research9,10. A significant portion of anti-HIV-1 vaccine research has concentrated on the reverse transcriptase enzyme due to its role in HIV replication11,12. The HIV reverse transcriptase (RT) gene encodes an enzyme crucial for viral replication. Reverse transcriptase converts the HIV RNA genome into DNA, enabling it to integrate into the host’s genome13. This is a key step in the life cycle of HIV, as the virus must convert its RNA genome into DNA to integrate into the host’s cellular DNA and replicate14. The gene encoding reverse transcriptase is located within the pol gene of HIV, which also includes genes for other essential enzymes, such as protease and integrase. The reverse transcriptase gene is crucial for the replication of HIV, and it has been a major target for antiretroviral therapies. Inhibitors of reverse transcriptase, known as nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NRTIs) and non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NNRTIs), are commonly used in the treatment of HIV infection to prevent viral replication and help manage the disease15,16. Consequently, our investigation focused on the HIV-1 reverse transcriptase for potential vaccine formulations. It is important to recognize that epitope-based vaccines alone often fail to elicit strong immune responses17. Therefore, successful vaccination with peptide-based vaccines requires the inclusion of an appropriate adjuvant. Adjuvants enhance the immune response by inducing inflammatory mediators and increasing the expression of costimulatory molecules on antigen-presenting cells (APCs)17. Recently, numerous adjuvants have been developed and are widely used in the vaccine industry. Advancements in immunoinformatics, along with time- and cost-efficient methods and a variety of vaccine design tools, have significantly accelerated the development of novel epitope-based vaccines18,19. Computational approaches hold great promise for creating safe and effective vaccines against numerous diseases. The objective of this computational study was to leverage the advantages of immunoinformatics to generate hypothetical HIV-1 vaccine candidates based on epitopes20. Focusing on the HIV-1 reverse transcriptase enzyme, this work constructed in silico vaccines tailored for Pakistan by analyzing the reverse transcriptase sequences of our local isolates and identifying HIV-1 B-cell cytotoxic T cell and helper T-cell epitopes. Our study is unique in that we conduct reverse transcriptase research in a wet lab and utilize the sequences obtained from these isolates to develop the in silico vaccine.

Materials and methods

Post-sequencing analysis and identification of conserved region

14 samples were successfully sequenced for the Pol gene. All the sequences were refined from 5 to 3 ends to remove low read data and submitted to GenBank for an accession number. CLC software was used to identify conserved regions in our isolates. All of our consensus region was used for the development of a vaccine for the Pakistani population.

Results

B-cell epitope predictions

For the given HIV reverse transcriptase enzyme, the ABCpred service was used to obtain predictions about specific epitopes linked to the humoral immune response, primarily focusing on linear antigenic determinants of B-cells21. This server utilizes an engineered neural network to project B-cell epitopes. This service employs a recurrent neural network that can inspect a set of approximately 700 B-cell and non-B-cell antigenic determinants, respectively. It is necessary to consider that the antigenic regions are exploited for both training and evaluation, with a peak dimension of twenty amino acids. The precision rate of this server’s recurrent neural network has proven to be approximately 65.93%. Consequently, a threshold of 0.51 was established when evaluating linear B-cell epitopes using the FASTA sequences of complete structural components of HIV.

CTL and HTL epitope prediction

To predict the antigenic regions of CTL that existed in the proteins of the virus, the NetCTL-1.2 tool (https://services.healthtech.dtu.dk/services/)was exploited22,23. It was established to keep the threshold around 0.75, the C-terminal dissociation measure around 0.15, and the TAP efficiency parameter around 0.05. Additional factors were preselected as part of the antigen motif identification technique. To ensure the accuracy of the analysis, several factors must be taken into account. A specific type of artificial neural network was utilized to obtain values for motif linkage and proteasomal C-terminus dissociation with the polypeptide of MHC-I. This made it easier to get appropriate reviews for the research. During this procedure, a weight matrix approach was employed to obtain the TAP efficiency score. One of the functions server is the IEDB online service is prediction of helper T lymphocyte (HTL), besides other features. For the assessment of the approach, approximately 7 human leukocyte antigens were used as references. For all 7 sourced HLAs, a thorough study showed that the blind epitope predictions made by the server were correct. A contrasting association was demonstrated among anticipated motifs’ IC50 scores and the coupling capacity of MHC-II, using a dataset from the IEDB. For MHC-II, low-affinity binding is defined as IC50 values exceeding 500 nM, moderate binding is indicated by scores less than 500 nM, and IC50 scores lower than 50 nM characterize high coupling capability. Specifically, a binding affinity is considered poor when the integration coefficient score (50) is more than 500 nM.

Tertiary structure modeling

The tool known as the I-TASSER is used to form the tertiary model of the multi-epitope vaccine. This tool is available online by searching this link, https://zhanglab.ccmb.med.umich.edu/I-TASSER/,. The I-TASSER is a precise server that generates reliable estimations related to the structure and function of proteins by using computational techniques. The approach exploited by this research utilized a dataset of the Protein Data Bank (PDB), which relies on the sequence-to-structure-to-function platform. Finding spatial configurations that are like those of additional peptide configurations and finding patterns pertinent to different protein configurations were the objectives of this research. I-TASSER designs three-dimensional atomic models using iterative structure-building strategies and various threading alignments. An amino acid sequence is the first input used in the procedure. The model has a proper topology if the template modeling (TM) score is larger than 0.5. If the TM score is below 0.17, any observable resemblance is likely accidental rather than significant. The opt cutoff value is not affected by the amino acid count of the protein. Due to analyzing the 3-D structure of peptides, and examinations that occurred throughout the research society for the last five years, I-TASSER has been known as a highly effective service, as shown by its effectiveness in the five Critical Assessment of Methods of Protein Structure Prediction (CASP)29.

The modification of codon usage is done through the method of Codon adaptation to synchronize with the tendencies of bacterial cells frequently combated at the time of nucleotide reading, and as a result, the gene expression rate is raised https://en.vectorbuilder.com/tool/codon-optimization.html. To raise the formation of vaccine peptides in host bacteria (E. coli K12) for assessing the variations in the usage of codon between humans and the desired host individual is the core aim of this technique. Of the three possible elements, we selected the following three that are essential: the prokaryotic ribosome binding site, the restriction enzyme cleavage sites, and the blocking of rho-independent transcription termination. Three factors are chosen among the choices available, and these factors include sites of breakage for restriction enzymes, binding sites for ribosomes in prokaryotes, and rho-independent termination of transcription. A study was carried out to determine if the improved codon sequence consists of any marketable restriction enzyme recognition sites, exclusively Eco53KI and BstZ17I. The results showed that neither of these places was present. To verify this, the ideal code of the vaccine peptide was modified, then inverted, adding Eco53KI and BstZ17I recognition sites at the C- and N-terminus regions, sequentially. The SnapGene restriction-based cloning system was then applied to introduce the altered code to the pET28a (+) plasmid. The incorporation took place precisely between the BstZ17I (2978) Eco53KI (188) sites33.

Molecular dynamics simulation

Protein and protein-ligand complex microscopic stability is assessed using molecular dynamics, an advanced automated simulation method. Structure, function, fluctuation, interaction, and behavior is demonstrated to do this. The behavior of the one complex was investigated using GROMACS version 2025.1 for MD calculations36. CHARMM General Force Field parameterized protein content. The SwissParam server implemented ligand topologies37. The structures were vacuum minimized 2500 times using steepest descent to address steric issues. The Simple Point-Charge (SPC) water model solvated the structure. After adding Na + and Cl- ions, the gmx genion instrument balanced the system. This was done to ensure system electrical neutrality. After minimization, MD simulations went into production, NVT, and NPT38. Two phases balanced the systems. First, a 100 picosecond NVT equilibration was done to maintain particle number, volume, and temperature. The procedure aimed to raise the system temperature to 300 K. The second stage involved a precise 100 picosecond NPT equilibration to achieve temperature, pressure, and particle number homogeneity. It was essential to maintain system density and pressure. The protein group’s location was limited by bond limitations on all bonds during simulations. The system entropy reduced because NVT and NPT restricted the water molecules around the protein, relaxing them. Parrinello-Rahman barostat method and v-rescale thermostat for molecular dynamics39. The thermostat and barostat were adjusted for 100 picoseconds. To constrain covalent bonding, the Linear Constraint Solver application was used. Chemical bond interactions were handled using the sophisticated (Particle-Mesh Ewald) or PME approach. Every system has a 100-ns production run after equilibrium. Codon optimization

The microbial expression machinery of human E. coli K12 strains exhibits significant differences; therefore, it is essential to modify codons to optimize the expression system for the human host. To harness the features of E. coli K12 proteins for vaccine development, reverse transcription is performed. Codons with a codon adaptation index (CAI) value of 1.0 are considered fully optimized, indicating the use of frequently preferred codons. The proportion of altered codons is approximately 51.70%, as determined by the GC content of the codons. It was also found that the Eco53KI and BtgI restriction sites are not present simultaneously, facilitating easier cloning under these conditions. Consequently, when these restriction sites and the modified codons were introduced into the pET28a(+) vector, a recombinant plasmid of 5035 base pairs was generated. Our vaccine consists of 1671 base pairs, with the remainder comprising the vector backbone. One reason for choosing pET28a(+) is its high level of synthetic peptide expression, driven by the efficient T7 promoter. It is considered one of the best cloning vectors due to its multiple restriction sites. Additionally, pET vectors can be engineered with various fusion tags, such as His-tag, GST, and MBP, to facilitate protein purification and identification. Molecular dynamic simulation

The RMSD plot shows a consistent increase during the initial 40 ns, reaching approximately 1.4 nm, followed by stabilization with fluctuations between 1.4 and 1.7 nm throughout the remaining 60 ns. This gradual rise and subsequent plateau indicate that the protein-ligand complex undergoes an initial conformational adjustment before maintaining structural stability. The absence of any steep drop or continuous rise after the 50 ns mark confirms that the system is equilibrated and that the simulation is reliable. The RMSF profile reveals that most residues fluctuate between 0.3 and 0.6 nm, with notable peaks around residues 50–200 and a significant spike near residue 500, reaching nearly 1.5–1.7 nm. These peaks likely correspond to loop or terminal regions, which are inherently more flexible. The core regions (residues 300–600) exhibit relatively low fluctuations (~ 0.3–0.5 nm), indicating that the backbone structure remains stable during the simulation—an ideal condition for drug-target binding analysis. The RG plot demonstrates a steady decrease from approximately 4.3 nm to 3.9 nm over the 100 ns simulation. This reduction suggests compaction of the protein structure over time, possibly due to tighter folding or ligand-induced conformational tightening. The consistent trend and absence of large fluctuations beyond 40 ns indicate that the system reached a compact and stable configuration, further supporting the structural convergence observed in the RMSD. The SASA values start at around 655 nm² and gradually decrease to about 600 nm², with periodic fluctuations throughout the 100 ns trajectory. This decline in SASA reflects a reduction in solvent exposure, often associated with protein folding or more compact packing. A relatively stable SASA range of 600–620 nm² beyond 40 ns suggests that equilibrium is achieved, correlating with the compactness observed in the Rg plot, as shown in Fig. 11. Hydrogen bond analysis reveals a high and stable number of hydrogen bonds throughout the simulation, fluctuating mostly between 760 and 820 bonds. The maintenance of such a large number of intramolecular hydrogen bonds strongly indicates structural integrity and compactness. Minor variations are normal due to thermal fluctuations, but the overall consistent bond count highlights the system’s thermodynamic and structural stability.

Discussion

Recent studies in vaccine development have increasingly incorporated multi-omics approaches. Advances in structural biology and bioinformatics have facilitated the design of vaccines targeting specific antigenic regions and epitopes on pathogenic bacteria. These efforts have yielded promising results and represent a critical aspect of current vaccine research44,45. Developing and improving various types of vaccines, such as live attenuated vaccines, requires substantial time and financial investment when using traditional methods, often delaying their availability to the public45,46. Additionally, attenuated vaccines are more likely to trigger allergic reactions and carry a higher antigenic load, while providing less passive immunity25,47. Such vaccines typically provide direct protection against complex pathogenic organisms. Consequently, safer, more effective, and more cost-efficient vaccines for infectious diseases have been developed. However, the development of effective peptide vaccines remains a significant challenge in vaccine research. Immune informatics, empowered by multi-omics technologies, offers a promising approach to address these challenges25,48,49.

Our objective was to demonstrate that artificial intelligence (AI) can be utilized to develop a hypoallergenic, non-toxic, and broadly safe HIV-1 vaccine50–52. The primary rationale for prioritizing HIV/AIDS in vaccine research is the urgent need to develop an effective vaccine to combat this devastating public health crisis. An effective HIV vaccine could save countless lives and alleviate the burden on global healthcare systems. This vaccine comprises multiple linear epitopes derived from the HIV-1 envelope gp120 protein, which are non-toxic, antigenic, immunogenic, and capable of inducing cytokine production53,54. We present strategies to enhance conserved linear epitopes, including priming B cells with germline-targeting immunogens, increasing antibody production through sequential vaccinations, and modulating immune responses using epitope scaffolding55,56. Recent advances in HIV mRNA vaccine research suggest that this technology holds significant promise for improving vaccine design. Therefore, we conducted a comprehensive bioinformatics analysis of the novel vaccine to evaluate its interactions with immune system activation receptors57,58. The immunogenic potential was assessed in real-world settings. The primary aim of this study was to evaluate the potential in-silico tools in developing safer and more effective HIV-1 vaccines.

Numerous studies suggest that Th epitopes may enhance the immunogenicity of CTL epitope-based vaccines, which justifies the previous incorporation of distinct universal Th epitopes in these vaccines59. In numerous studies, researchers employed multi-epitope vaccination using Th epitopes to enhance immune responses. This study emphasized HIV-1-specific Th epitopes over universal ones for two primary reasons, distinguishing it from prior research. T-helper cells are essential for initiating both humoral and cellular immune responses, while memory CTL responses are critical for the eradication of HIV-160,61. Additionally, T helper 1 (Th1) responses contribute to protective immunity against HIV infection62,63. Despite successfully identifying these Th epitopes, we encountered difficulties obtaining data on the predominant MHC-II alleles in China, as most published research on epitope identification has focused on populations in the US and Europe, reporting 38 cases52,64. An optimal solution would be a multi-epitope vaccine, as it would elicit an immune response targeted at specific epitopes. Two factors that may influence the immunogenicity of the “artificial” protein are the arrangement and continuity of the epitopes, along with the sequence of the connecting linker65,66.

Significance of the study

The significance of advancing an in silico-designed vaccine through preclinical and clinical testing lies in its potential for rapid and precise vaccine development. Computational methodologies used in design are thorough and effective for simulating immune responses, enabling the prediction of antigens, protein stability, and epitope regions before laboratory experiments67. Furthermore, employing in silico approaches improves the identification of specific immune responses that a vaccine should elicit, potentially enhancing both its efficacy and safety68. A computationally generated vaccine, once modified and tested, offers considerable flexibility and adaptability to address new subtypes or related diseases, thereby increasing its effectiveness against various infectious threats.

Limitations

Nevertheless, several limitations are inherent in the in silico methodology for vaccine development. Predictive models are similarly affected by the quality of input data and can only partially replicate in vivo immune responses69. The use of computational techniques to solve problems reveals physiological and atomic cellular relationships that are insufficiently characterized, leading to discrepancies between anticipated and actual outcomes. Consequently, a substantial period of 10–15 years is required for comprehensive preclinical and clinical studies to assess immunogenicity, safety, and efficacy42. Moreover, certain elements of vaccine designs that appear effective in computational models may not demonstrate expected performance in terms of protein expression, stability, or immunogenicity when applied in actual biological systems. In conclusion, in silico design holds significant potential to transform vaccine development; however, it requires appropriate integration with experimental methodologies to address its limitations and achieve favorable clinical outcomes70,71.

Conclusion

The development of multi-epitope vaccines using bioinformatics technology represents an innovative and promising approach to combating infectious diseases. Computational methods can be employed to design vaccines that enhance immunogenicity while minimizing side effects. These vaccines are more effective in reducing the spread of certain infectious diseases because they specifically target pathogenic microorganisms. In silico research plays a crucial role in vaccine development by simulating proteins and evaluating various characteristics, including antigenicity, toxicity, allergenicity, and epitope prediction. This comprehensive analysis improves the cost-effectiveness of vaccine production and reduces errors. By employing various servers and filtering techniques, highly potent B-cell epitopes, as well as helper T lymphocyte (HTL) and cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) epitopes, were identified. In particular, five B-cell epitopes, eight CTL epitopes, and twelve HTL epitopes were chosen for the creation of the vaccine. The versatility of multi-epitope vaccines makes them advantageous for providing simultaneous protection against multiple diseases. This approach also opens new, potentially transformative avenues for cancer therapy and personalized medicine, which may enhance current treatments. The integration of computational tools with experimental techniques holds significant potential for improving vaccination protocols by substantially accelerating vaccine development.

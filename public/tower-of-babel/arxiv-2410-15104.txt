Strict condition for the L 2 L^{2} -wellposedness of fifth and sixth order dispersive equations
Taehun Kim

Abstract
We provide a set of conditions that is necessary and sufficient for the L 2 L^{2} -wellposedness of the Cauchy problem for fifth and sixth order variable-coefficient linear dispersive equations. The necessity of these conditions had been presented by Tarama, and we scrutinized their proof to split the conditions into several parts so that an inductive argument is applicable. This inductive argument simplifies the engineering process of the appropriate pseudodifferential operator needed for the proof of L 2 L^{2} -wellposedness.



1
Introduction

1.1
Variable-coefficient linear dispersive equations and known results

In this paper, we are concerned with the
L^{2}
-wellposedness of the Cauchy problem for variable-coefficient linear dispersive equations in
1
-dimension:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}L_{k}u(x,t)=0&(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\\
u(x,t=0)=u_{0}(x)&x\in\mathbb{R}\end{cases},

(1)

where
k\geq 2
is an integer and
L_{k}
is a variable-coefficient linear dispersive operator of the form

\displaystyle L_{k}u=D_{t}u-D_{x}^{k}u-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}b_{j}(x)D_{x}^{j}(u).

(2)

Here,
D_{t}
and
D_{x}
denote
\frac{1}{i}\partial_{t}
and
\frac{1}{i}\partial_{x}
respectively, and
b_{j}(0\leq j\leq k-1)
are given
\overline{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
functions, complex-valued smooth functions whose partial derivatives of every order are bounded. Note that when
k=2
,
iL_{2}=\partial_{t}+i\partial_{xx}-b_{1}(x)\partial_{x}-ib_{0}(x)
is a Schrödinger-type operator, and when
k=3
,
iL_{3}=\partial_{t}+\partial_{xxx}+ib_{2}(x)\partial_{xx}-b_{1}(x)\partial_{x}-ib_{0}(x)
is a KdV-type operator.

The
L^{2}
-wellposedness of the Cauchy problem (
1
) for small integers
k
are studied in various papers. For
k=2
, Takeuchi
[
6
, §1]
proved that the following condition on
b_{1}
is sufficient for the
L^{2}
-wellposedness of (
1
):

\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\big(b_{1}(\widetilde{x})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim 1.

(Here, and throughout this article,
X\lesssim Y
means that
X\leq CY
holds for some absolute constant
C>0
. If
C
depends on some parameters
S
, then we write
X\lesssim_{S}Y
.) In
[
4
, Lecture VII]
, Mizohata proved that this condition is also necessary, and proved similar necessary conditions for higher dimensional case. For
k=3
, the following set of conditions is equivalent to the
L^{2}
-wellposedness of (
1
):

\begin{cases}\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\big(b_{2}(\widetilde{x})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L_{3}}1,\\
\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\big(b_{1}(\widetilde{x})-\frac{1}{3}b_{2}(\widetilde{x})^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L_{3}}|x-y|^{1/2}\end{cases}

This is proved by Tarama
[
7
]
under additional assumption
b_{2}\equiv 0
, and by Mizuhara
[
5
]
in full generality. In the same paper, Mizuhara also obtained an equivalent condition for the
L^{2}
-wellposedness of (
1
) with
k=4
under some additional assumption, and the same result without this assumption is proved by Tarama
[
8
]
. In
[
8
]
, Tarama generalized the arguments to obtain a set of necessary conditions for the
L^{2}
-wellposedness of (
1
) with general
k
. One of these necessary conditions is that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\big(b_{k-1}(\widetilde{x})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L_{k}}1.

(3)

Under this condition,
\varphi(x)=\exp\left(-\frac{i}{k}\int_{0}^{x}b_{k-1}(\widetilde{x})\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right)
and
\frac{1}{\varphi}
are both
\overline{C}^{\infty}
. If
u
satisfies (
1
), the equation satisfied by
v=\varphi^{-1}u
is

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left(D_{t}-D_{x}^{k}-\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}\widetilde{b}_{j}D_{x}^{j}\right)v=0&(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\\
v(x,t=0)=\varphi(x)^{-1}u_{0}(x)&x\in\mathbb{R}\end{cases},

(4)

where
\widetilde{b}_{j}(0\leq j\leq k-2)
are
\overline{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
functions determined by
b_{j^{\prime}}(0\leq j^{\prime}\leq k-2)
. Since
\varphi,\varphi^{-1}\in\overline{C}^{\infty}
, the
L^{2}
-wellposedness of (
1
) is equivalent to that of (
4
), under the condition (
3
). Since (
3
) is necessary for (
1
) to be
L^{2}
-wellposed, it is enough to study the
L^{2}
-wellposedness of (
4
). That is, we may assume without loss of generality that
b_{k-1}\equiv 0
.

The necessary conditions for
L^{2}
-wellposedness of (
1
) obtained in
[
8
, Theorem 2.3]
are the following
(k-2)
conditions

\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\big(d_{j}(\widetilde{x})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L_{k}}|x-y|^{\frac{j}{k-1}}\quad(1\leq j\leq k-2),

where
d_{j}\in\overline{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})(1\leq j\leq k-2)
are functions determined by
b_{j^{\prime}}(0\leq j^{\prime}\leq k-2)
. If the value of
k
is specified, these
\left\{d_{j}\right\}
can be explicitly calculated in terms of
\left\{b_{j^{\prime}}\right\}
, and for
3\leq k\leq 6
, these conditions read as

(i)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\big(b_{k-2}(\widetilde{x})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L_{k}}|x-y|^{\frac{1}{k-1}},

(ii)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\big(b_{k-3}(\widetilde{x})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L_{k}}|x-y|^{\frac{2}{k-1}}\quad\text{ if }k\geq 4,

(iii)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\big(b_{k-4}(\widetilde{x})-\frac{k-3}{2k}b_{k-2}(\widetilde{x})^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L_{k}}|x-y|^{\frac{3}{k-1}}\quad\text{ if }k\geq 5,

(iv)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\big(b_{k-5}(\widetilde{x})-\frac{k-4}{k}b_{k-2}(\widetilde{x})b_{k-3}(\widetilde{x})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L_{k}}|x-y|^{\frac{4}{k-1}}\quad\text{ if }k\geq 6.

If
k=3,4
these are exactly the necessary and sufficient conditions for the
L^{2}
-wellposedness of (
1
) obtained by Tarama (
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
). In this paper, we prove that this set of necessary conditions is also sufficient for the
L^{2}
-wellposedness of (
1
) when
k
is equal to
5
or
6
.

1.2
Main results

The first main result of this paper gives a single necessary condition for the
L^{2}
-wellposedness of (
1
).

Theorem 1
.

Let
k\geq 3
and
1\leq m\leq k-2
be given integers. Let
A
be a differential operator of order
\leq k-2
, which is self-adjoint. Let
b_{j}\in\overline{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})(1\leq j\leq m)
be given functions. Then, for the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}Lu:=\left(D_{t}-D_{x}^{k}-A-\sum_{j=0}^{m}b_{j}(x)D_{x}^{j}\right)u=0&(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\\
u(x,t=0)=u_{0}(x)&x\in\mathbb{R}\end{cases}

to be
L^{2}
-wellposed, it is necessary that

\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\big(b_{m}(\widetilde{x})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L}|x-y|^{\frac{k-1-m}{k-1}}.

This result is not novel in the sense that its proof goes on similarly as in
[
8
, Theorem 2.3]
. However, Theorem
1
does not follow directly from the cited theorem. To prove Theorem
1
, we need to analyze the coefficients (
\left\{P_{k-2-m;\ell,j}\right\}
in Lemma
7
or
\{d_{j}\}
in
[
8
, Proposition 2.1]
) more carefully, and then use this result for self-adjoint operators to prove that we may disregard self-adjoint part
A
in Theorem
1
. The main advantage in proving this theorem is that it enables inductive argument. Indeed, Theorem
1
states that after disregarding the self-adjoint part as zeros, the integral of the coefficient of the highest term must satisfy a certain estimate. Using this estimate, we can switch the
L^{2}
-wellposedness of the original operator into the
L^{2}
-wellposedness of the modified operator with smaller
m
, after which induction is applicable. We will discuss more on this in Section
1.3
. Moreover, unlike Tarama’s proof which strongly uses the contradiction argument, we will obtain quantitative behavior of illposedness by using the so-called ‘duality testing argument’ appearing in the paper of Jeong and Oh
[
2
, Appendix A]
.

The second and third main results give a set of necessary and sufficient conditions for (
1
) to be
L^{2}
-wellposed, where
k=5,6
. Although these results are already introduced in Section
1.1
, we state them here once again for the sake of completeness.

Theorem 2
.

Let
b,c,d\in\overline{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be given functions. Then the Cauchy problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}Lu:=\left(D_{t}-D_{x}^{5}-b(x)D_{x}^{3}-c(x)D_{x}^{2}-d(x)D_{x}\right)u=0&(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
u(x,t=0)=u_{0}(x)&x\in\mathbb{R}\end{cases}

(5)

is
L^{2}
-wellposed if and only if the following three conditions are satisfied:

(i)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\big(b(\widetilde{x})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L}|x-y|^{\frac{1}{4}},

(ii)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\big(c(\widetilde{x})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L}|x-y|^{\frac{1}{2}},

(iii)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\big(d(\widetilde{x})-\frac{1}{5}b(\widetilde{x})^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L}|x-y|^{\frac{3}{4}}.

Theorem 3
.

Let
b,c,d,e\in\overline{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be given functions. Then the Cauchy problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}Lu:=\left(D_{t}-D_{x}^{6}-b(x)D_{x}^{4}-c(x)D_{x}^{3}-d(x)D_{x}^{2}-e(x)D_{x}\right)u=0&(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
u(x,t=0)=u_{0}(x)&x\in\mathbb{R}\end{cases}

(6)

is
L^{2}
-wellposed if and only if the following four conditions are satisfied:

(i)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\big(b(\widetilde{x})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L}|x-y|^{\frac{1}{5}},

(ii)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\big(c(\widetilde{x})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L}|x-y|^{\frac{2}{5}},

(iii)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\big(d(\widetilde{x})-\frac{1}{4}b(\widetilde{x})^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L}|x-y|^{\frac{3}{5}},

(iv)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\big(e(\widetilde{x})-\frac{1}{3}b(\widetilde{x})c(\widetilde{x})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L}|x-y|^{\frac{4}{5}}.

Recall that we have assumed
b_{k-1}\equiv 0
by using gauge transformation. Transforming back into the original equation gives the following corollaries:

Corollary 4
.

Let
a,b,c,d\in\overline{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be given functions. Then the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}Lu:=\left(D_{t}-D_{x}^{5}-a(x)D_{x}^{4}-b(x)D_{x}^{3}-c(x)D_{x}^{2}-d(x)D_{x}\right)u=0&(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
u(x,t=0)=u_{0}(x)&x\in\mathbb{R}\end{cases}

is
L^{2}
-wellposed if and only if the following four conditions are satisfied:

(i)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\big(a(\widetilde{x})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L}1,

(ii)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\left(b(\widetilde{x})-\frac{2}{5}a(\widetilde{x})^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L}|x-y|^{\frac{1}{4}},

(iii)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\left(c(\widetilde{x})-\frac{3}{5}a(\widetilde{x})b(\widetilde{x})+\frac{4}{25}a(\widetilde{x})^{3}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L}|x-y|^{\frac{1}{2}},

(iv)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\left(d(\widetilde{x})-\frac{1}{5}b(\widetilde{x})^{2}-\frac{2}{5}a(\widetilde{x})c(\widetilde{x})+\frac{7}{25}a(\widetilde{x})^{2}b(\widetilde{x})-\frac{7}{125}a(\widetilde{x})^{4}-\frac{1}{5}ia^{\prime}(\widetilde{x})b(\widetilde{x})+\frac{1}{5}a^{\prime}(\widetilde{x})^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L}|x-y|^{\frac{3}{4}}.

Corollary 5
.

Let
a,b,c,d,e\in\overline{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be given functions. Then the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}Lu:=\left(D_{t}-D_{x}^{6}-a(x)D_{x}^{5}-b(x)D_{x}^{4}-c(x)D_{x}^{3}-d(x)D_{x}^{2}-e(x)D_{x}\right)u=0&(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
u(x,t=0)=u_{0}(x)&x\in\mathbb{R}\end{cases}

is
L^{2}
-wellposed if and only if the following five conditions are satisfied:

(i)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\big(a(\widetilde{x})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L}1,

(ii)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\left(b(\widetilde{x})-\frac{5}{12}a(\widetilde{x})^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L}|x-y|^{\frac{1}{5}},

(iii)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\left(+c(\widetilde{x})-\frac{2}{3}a(\widetilde{x})b(\widetilde{x})+\frac{5}{27}a(\widetilde{x})^{3}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L}|x-y|^{\frac{2}{5}},

(iv)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\left(d(\widetilde{x})-\frac{1}{4}b(\widetilde{x})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}a(\widetilde{x})c(\widetilde{x})+\frac{3}{8}a(\widetilde{x})^{2}b(\widetilde{x})-\frac{5}{64}a(\widetilde{x})^{4}-\frac{1}{4}ia^{\prime}(\widetilde{x})b(\widetilde{x})+\frac{5}{16}a^{\prime}(\widetilde{x})^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L}|x-y|^{\frac{3}{5}},

(v)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\bigg(e(\widetilde{x})-\frac{1}{3}b(\widetilde{x})c(\widetilde{x})-\frac{1}{3}a(\widetilde{x})d(\widetilde{x})+\frac{2}{9}a(\widetilde{x})b(\widetilde{x})^{2}+\frac{2}{9}a(\widetilde{x})^{2}c(\widetilde{x})-\frac{14}{81}a(\widetilde{x})^{3}b(\widetilde{x})+\frac{7}{243}a(\widetilde{x})^{5}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{4}{9}a^{\prime\prime}(\widetilde{x})b(\widetilde{x})-\frac{1}{3}ia^{\prime}(\widetilde{x})c(\widetilde{x})+\frac{2}{9}ia(\widetilde{x})a^{\prime}(\widetilde{x})b(\widetilde{x})-\frac{10}{27}a(\widetilde{x})a^{\prime}(\widetilde{x})^{2}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\bigg|\lesssim_{L}|x-y|^{\frac{4}{5}}

1.2.1
Classes of pseudodifferential operators

In the heuristic reasoning in Section
1.3
and proofs in Section
3
and
4
, we will use pseudodifferential operators. We collect here the definitions of the classes of pseudodifferential operators which will be used later.

Definition 6
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
.

(a)

A pseudodifferential operator
B(x,D)=\mathrm{Op}(b(x,\xi))
of order
m
is an operator defined by

B(x,D)f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{ix\xi}b(x,\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

where
b\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is a function satisfying

\left|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}b(x,\xi)\right|\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{m-\beta}\quad\forall\;(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

for any nonnegative integers
\alpha
and
\beta
. Here,
\langle\xi\rangle:=\sqrt{1+|\xi|^{2}}
. If this is the case, we say that
B
is in the class
S^{m}
. We will abuse notation and identify pseudodifferential operator
B
with its symbol
b
.

(b)

Let
B(l;x,D)
be a pseudodifferential operator with parameter
l
, defined by

B(l;x,D)f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{ix\xi}b(l;x,\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

B(l;x,D)
is said to be in class
S_{(\ell)}^{m}
iff for any nonnegative integers
\alpha
and
\beta
, one has

\left|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}b(l;x,\xi)\right|\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta}\langle\xi\rangle_{l}^{m-\beta}\quad\forall\;l\geq 1,\;\forall\;(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

where
\langle\xi\rangle_{l}:=\sqrt{\xi^{2}+l^{2}}
. We will abuse notation by identifying
B
and
b
.

(c)

Let
A,B
be pseudodifferential operators. Then the expression
A\equiv B\pmod{S^{m}}
means that
A-B\in S^{m}
. Similarly, the expression
A\equiv B\pmod{S_{(\ell)}^{m}}
means that
A-B\in S_{(\ell)}^{m}
.

Several properties of these pseudodifferential operators are studied extensively in
[
1
]
and
[
3
]
. In this paper, an operator
A
is said to be self-adjoint iff
A\equiv A^{\ast}\pmod{S^{0}}
.

1.3
Playing with toy case
k=4

In this section, we will briefly explain the scheme for the proof of Theorem
2
and
3
. This abstract scheme will be exemplified by a heuristic proof in case
k=4
. The expositions below are organized to emphasize the parallel reasoning between the way how necessary condition pops up and the way it serves as a sufficient condition.

We started with a differential operator

L=D_{t}-D_{x}^{k}-\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}b_{j}(x)D_{x}^{j}

and used gauge transformation to get rid of the
(k-1)
th term. Our next target is to remove the
(k-2)
th term. This goal is not achievable by using simple gauge transformations. Instead, we make a change of variable
u=v+B_{-1}v
where
B_{-1}
is a pseudodifferential operator (
\Psi
DO) of order
-1
. Assuming that
u
is a solution to
Lu=0
, the equation satisfied by
v
is

(I+B_{-1})^{-1}\circ L\circ(I+B_{-1})v=0.

Expand
L\circ(I+B_{-1})
by using formal symbolic calculus to get

L\circ(I+B_{-1})\equiv D_{t}+B_{-1}D_{t}-D_{x}^{k}-B_{-1}D_{x}^{k}+ik\mathrm{Op}(\xi^{k-1}\partial_{x}B_{-1})-b_{k-2}(x)D_{x}^{k-2}\pmod{S^{k-3}}

Hence, if

ik\mathrm{Op}(\xi^{k-1}\partial_{x}B_{-1})\equiv\mathrm{Op}(b_{k-2}(x)\xi^{k-2})\pmod{S^{k-3}},\quad\text{i.e., }\quad\partial_{x}B_{-1}\equiv-\frac{i}{k}b_{k-2}(x)D_{x}^{-1}\pmod{S^{-2}},

then we would have

(I+B_{-1})^{-1}\circ L\circ(I+B_{-1})\equiv D_{t}-D_{x}^{k}\pmod{S^{k-3}},

so that the process to eliminate the
(k-2)
th term of
L
is successful at least in our dream. Hence our objective turns down into the construction of a
\Psi
DO
B_{-1}\in S^{-1}
such that the symbol of
\partial_{x}B_{-1}
is approximately equal to
-\frac{i}{k}\frac{1}{\xi}b_{k-2}(x)
. Of course this is impossible without any further assumption on
b_{k-2}
. This is where the necessary condition obtained in Theorem
1
comes into play. The necessary condition is that

\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\big(b_{k-2}(\widetilde{x})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim_{L}|x-y|^{\frac{1}{k-1}}.

If we assume this, then in
[
5
, Lemma 2.1]
it is proved that the following Tarama-type operator

B=\frac{1}{k\xi}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\chi\left(\frac{y-x}{\xi^{k-1}}\right)\mathrm{Im}\,\big(b_{k-2}(y)\big)\,\mathrm{d}y

satisfies
B\in S^{0}
and
\partial_{x}B\equiv\frac{1}{k\xi}\mathrm{Im}\,\big(b_{k-2}(x)\big)\pmod{S^{-(k-1)}}
, where
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(-2,2)
is any given function satisfying
\chi(x)\equiv 1\;\forall\;|x|\leq 1
. (In fact, all
\xi
’s in the denominator must be replaced by
\langle\xi\rangle_{\ell}
’s and
S^{j}
’s must be replaced by
S_{(\ell)}^{j}
’s, but let’s stay in the dream a little longer.) Hence by using this
\Psi
DO
B
, the necessary condition attained in Theorem
1
allows us to get rid of the term
\mathrm{Im}\,\big(b_{k-2}(x)\big)D_{x}^{k-2}
from the original differential operator
L
. The remaining coefficient of
(k-2)
th order term is now real-valued, so we can consider it as a part of a self-adjoint differential operator by altering
b_{j}(0\leq j\leq k-3)
. Then we have

L_{(k-3)}=D_{t}-D_{x}^{k}-A_{(1)}-\sum_{j=0}^{k-3}b_{j}(x)D_{x}^{j}

on our hand, where
A_{(1)}
is a self-adjoint differential operator of order
k-2
. Now we try eliminating the
(k-3)
th term. This time we use a change of variables
u=v+B_{-2}v
, where
B_{-2}\in S^{-2}
. After calculating formally just as before, we see that we want
B_{-2}
to satisfy
\partial_{x}B_{-2}\approx-\frac{i}{k}\frac{1}{\xi^{2}}b_{k-3}(x)
. Again, this construction utilizes the necessary condition popping out from Theorem
1
, which is

\left|\int_{x}^{y}\mathrm{Im}\,\big(b_{k-3}(\widetilde{x})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{x}\right|\lesssim|x-y|^{\frac{2}{k-1}}.

Note that Theorem
1
allowed us to ignore self-adjoint part
A_{(1)}
of
L_{(k-3)}
and concentrate solely on
b_{k-3}
. Assuming this condition to be satisfied, we have that the following operator

B=\frac{1}{k\xi^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\chi\left(\frac{y-x}{\xi^{k-1}}\right)\mathrm{Im}\,\big(b_{k-3}(y)\big)\,\mathrm{d}y

satisfies
B\in S^{0}
and
\partial_{x}B=\frac{1}{k\xi^{2}}\mathrm{Im}\,\big(b_{k-3}(x)\big)
. (Again, we are being sloppy.) By using this
B
, we can only leave real parts in the coefficient of the
(k-3)
th order term, which can again be absorbed into self-adjoint part
A
. Now we have

L_{(k-4)}=D_{t}-D_{x}^{k}-A_{(2)}-\sum_{j=0}^{k-4}b_{j}(x)D_{x}^{j},

and the proof goes on by induction on
m
, the highest order in
L
except for self-adjoint parts.

But how does the necessary condition obtained from Theorem
1
arise from the equation? The key scheme, duality testing argument, is to test the solution with an approximate solution for
L^{\ast}
. More precisely, let
u
be a solution to
Lu=0
, and let
v
be an approximate solution to
L^{\ast}v=0
which is constructed explicitly. Then integrating
D_{t}\langle u,v\rangle=\langle u,L^{\ast}v\rangle
with respect to
t
gives

|\langle u(t=0),v(t=0)\rangle|\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|L^{\ast}v\|_{L^{2}}.

Hence if
v
is constructed so that
L^{\ast}v\approx 0
and
v(t=0)=u(t=0)
are satisfied, then
\|v\|_{L^{2}}
must be large enough. This gives some condition that comes out from the explicit formula of
v

Recall that for an infinite set

Cover time of random subgraphs of the hypercube
Colin Cooper, Alan Frieze, Wesley Pegden

Abstract
Q n , p Q_{n,p} , the random subgraph of the n n -vertex hypercube Q n Q_{n} , is obtained by independently retaining each edge of Q n Q_{n} with probability p p . We give precise values for the cover time of Q n , p Q_{n,p} above the connectivity threshold.

Introduction

Let
Q_{n}
be the hypercube with
n=2^{d}
vertices and
m=dn/2
edges where
d=\log_{2}n

n

The threshold probability

The original question as to whether connectivity enjoys a threshold property

[

This study culminated in a proof by Bollobás
[
4

[

The cover time of a connected graph is the maximum over the start vertex of the expected time for a simple random walk to visit every vertex of the graph. There is a large literature on this subject see for example
[
2
]
,
[
15
]
, including results
[
6
]
–
[

Because of its relationship with the Ehrenfest model of diffusion, the random walk on the hypercube has long been an object of study. Diaconis and Shahshahani
[

proved the existence of a cutoff phenomenon for the lazy walk
at

[

established the rate of convergence

Matthews

proved that the cover time of the hypercube

The proof uses

[

by the same author.
This note gives the w.h.p. cover time of

Denote

2.3
Mixing time of the random walk

The

{\Phi}(G)
of a graph

{\Phi}=\min_{\pi(S)\leq 1/2}\frac{|E(S:\overline{S})|}{{\rm deg}(S)}.

Here
{\rm deg}(S)=\sum_{v\in S}d(v)
,
\pi(S)=\frac{{\rm deg}(S)}{{\rm deg}(G)}
,
and

is the set of edges between
S
and
V\setminus S
in the

G
. It follows
from
[

]
that

|P_{u}^{(t)}(x)-\pi_{x}|\leq(\pi_{x}/\pi_{u})^{1/2}(1-\Phi^{2}/2)^{t}.

(6)

As we assume
Q_{n,p}
is connected and the maximum degree is
d
we have
\pi_{x}/\pi_{u}=O(\log n)
.
It follows from P1 (see Section
4.1
for the proof) that
{\Phi}=\Omega(1/\log^{3}n\log\log n)
.
To satisfy Condition (
i
) of Lemma
2
, we take

T=\log^{7}n.

(7)

A walk is
lazy
, if it only
moves to a neighbour with probability
1/2
at any step.
There are several technical points in our cover time proof which require us to consider lazy walks.
Firstly the hypercube is bipartite, and hence periodic.
To remove the periodicity we
can make the walk lazy.
Secondly the bound (
6
)
assumes the walk is lazy.

Making the walk lazy halves the conductance but (
7
) still holds, and the value of
\pi_{v}

is unchanged.
Using a lazy walk asymptotically doubles the cover time, as half the steps are wasted.
It also doubles the value of
R_{v}
; as the expected number of steps before an exit from
v
is two.
Thus the ratio of these values cancels in (
4
).
Other then this it has a negligible effect on the
analysis, and we will ignore it for the rest of the paper and continue
as though there are no lazy steps.

2.4
The number of returns in the mixing time

For a random walk

Lemma 4
.

Let

, and let

be a random walk on

and all

,

Proof.

As in (
5
) of P3, fix the values of

to
h=\frac{d}{2\log d}
and
L=\frac{100d}{\log d}
.
Let
t_{0}=d/\log^{2}d
.
The proof is in three steps, from

, from
t_{0}<t\leq h
, and from
h\leq t\leq T=\log^{k}n
.

We first consider the case where, with the possible exception of
v
itself, no vertex within distance
h
of
v
has degree at most
L
.

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau=1}^{t_{0}}\mathbb{P}(X_{\tau}=v)\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau=1}^{t_{0}}\frac{1}{L}\mathbb{P}(X_{\tau-1}\in N(v))\leq\frac{t_{0}}{L}=O\left(\frac{1}{\log d}\right).

Let
v_{i}
be a vertex at distance
1\leq i\leq h
from
v
. The probability the distance to
v
decreases to
i-1
at the
next step is at most

, and the probability it increases to

is at least
(L-i)/L
. Thus the drift

\mu\geq\frac{L-h}{L}-\frac{h}{L}=\frac{L-2h}{L}=\frac{99}{100}.

In

steps the expected displacement of the walk from

is at least

. Let

be the
actual displacement. For
\delta>0

\mathbb{P}(dist(X_{t},v)\leq(1-\delta)t\mu)\leq e^{-\Omega(\delta^{2}t\mu)}.

Thus

\sum_{\tau=t_{0}}^{h}\mathbb{P}(X_{\tau}=v)\leq he^{-\Omega(\delta^{2}t_{0}\mu)}=o\left(\frac{1}{\log d}\right).

It follows that, w.h.p., in
h
steps the walk is at least distance

from

,
where

, say. Let

\widehat{q}=\frac{L-h}{L}=\frac{199}{200},\qquad\widehat{p}=\frac{h}{L}=\frac{1}{200}.

Consider a biassed random walk with transition probabilities

of one step left and
\widehat{q}
of one step right,
setting out from

on the integer line
\{0,1,...,H\}
. The probability the walk reaches the origin

before returning to

\frac{\left(\frac{\widehat{q}}{\widehat{p}}\right)-1}{\left(\frac{\widehat{q}}{\widehat{p}}\right)^{H}-1}=O(\mu^{-h})=O\left(\left(\frac{199}{200}\right)^{2d/5\log d}\right).

Next consider the case where vertex

is one of the at most 2 vertices of degree at most

is within distance

of

4.1
Conductance of
Q_{n,p}

The conductance
{\Phi}={\Phi}_{G}
of a graph
G=(V,E)
is defined as

{\Phi}_{G}=\min_{S\subset V(G)\atop 0<\pi(S)\leq 1/2}\frac{e(S:\overline{S})}{d_{G}(S)},

where
d(S)=\sum_{v\in S}d_{v}
is the degree of a set of vertices
S
in the graph
G
,
\overline{S}=V\setminus S
and
e(S:\overline{S})=|E(S:\overline{S})|
. The expression
\pi(S)\leq 1/2
is equivalent to
d(S)\leq|E(G)|
; multiply the former by
2|E(G)|
to obtain the latter.

The edge isoperimetric inequality for the hypercube, Harper
[
16
]
, states that

\min_{S\subseteq V\atop|S|\leq n/2}\left\{|E(S,{\overline{S}})|\right\}\geq|S|(d-\log_{2}|S|).

(23)

The bound is tight for sets

S
which are vertices of a subcube of
Q_{n}
.
For random sub-hypercubes we have the following lower bound.

Proposition 5
.

With high probability
Q_{n,p}
has conductance

{\Phi}_{Q_{n,p}}=\Omega\left(\frac{1}{d^{3}\log d}\right).

Proof.

Case 0.

1\leq|S|\leq\sqrt{d}
. By Lemma
7
.
1
, w.h.p. vertices of degree at most
L=100d/\log d
are distance at least
d/2\log d
apart. Let
S_{1}\subseteq S
be vertices of degree at most
L
and
S_{2}=S\setminus S_{1}
.
Then

e(S_{1},\overline{S})=d(S_{1})\quad\text{ and }\quad e(S_{2},\overline{S})\geq 100|S_{2}|d/\log d-2|S_{2}|\log_{2}|S_{2}|\geq|S_{2}|d/\log d,

and
d(S)\leq d(S_{1})+d|S_{2}|
. It follows that

{\Phi}_{S}\geq\frac{1}{\log d}.

(24)

Case 1:

\sqrt{d}\leq|S|\leq n/3d
.
Referring to (
23
), as
p>1/2
the number of retained edges

is at least

. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\exists S:\sqrt{d}\leq|S|\leq n/3d,e(S:\overline{S})

\displaystyle\leq s(d-\log_{2}s)/d)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s=\sqrt{d}}^{n/3d}n(ed)^{s-1}\;\mathbb{P}(X\leq s(d-\log_{2}s)/d)

(25)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s=\sqrt{d}}^{n/3d}n(ed)^{s}\frac{(ed)^{s(d-\log_{2}s)/d}}{2^{s(d-\log_{2}s)}}

(26)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=\sqrt{d}}^{n/3d}\left(\frac{s\,2^{d/s}}{(ed)^{(\log_{2}s)/d}}\frac{(ed)^{2}}{n}\right)^{s}

(27)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s=\sqrt{d}}^{n/3d}\left(\frac{e^{1+o(1)}}{3}\right)^{s}=o(1).

In (

(ed)^{s-1}
as an upper bound on the number of trees of size

in

[

(29)

Linear-Space Extragradient Methods for Fast, Large-Scale Optimal Transport
Matthew X. Burns, Jiaming Liang

Abstract
Optimal transport (OT) and its entropy-regularized form (EOT) have become increasingly prominent computational problems, with applications in machine learning and statistics. Recent years have seen a commensurate surge in first-order methods aiming to improve the complexity of large-scale (E)OT. However, there has been a consistent tradeoff: attaining state-of-the-art rates requires 𝒪 ⁡ ( n 2 ) \mathcal{O}(n^{2}) storage to enable ergodic primal averaging. In this work, we demonstrate that recently proposed primal-dual extragradient methods (PDXG) can be implemented entirely in the dual with 𝒪 ⁡ ( n ) \mathcal{O}(n) storage. Additionally, we prove that regularizing the reformulated OT problem is equivalent to EOT with extensions to entropy-regularized barycenter problems, further widening the applications of the proposed method. The proposed dual-only extragradient method (DXG) achieves 𝒪 ⁡ ( n 2 ​ ε − 1 ) \mathcal{O}(n^{2}\varepsilon^{-1}) complexity for ε \varepsilon -approximate OT with 𝒪 ⁡ ( n ) \mathcal{O}(n) memory. Numerical experiments demonstrate that the dual extragradient method scales favorably in non/weakly-regularized regimes compared to existing algorithms, though future work is needed to improve performance in certain problem classes.

Introduction

Machine learning and statistics often require comparing and interpolating between probability distributions. Classical measures such as Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence and total variation distance either fail to define a true metric or ignore the underlying geometric structure. Optimal transport (OT) remedies these shortcomings by defining a family of metrics based on mass transport, explicitly linking “cost” to the underlying space. Entropic Optimal Transport

The computational challenges of OT have led to the wide adoption of entropic optimal transport (EOT)

31

\eta

\min_{\pi\in\Pi(r,c)}\left\{\left\langle C,\pi\right\rangle-\eta H(\pi)\right\}.

(EOT (Primal))

\mathcal{L}(\pi,\varphi,\psi)=\left\langle C,\pi\right\rangle+\left\langle r-\pi\mathds{1},\varphi\right\rangle+\left\langle c-\pi^{\top}\mathds{1},\psi\right\rangle+\delta_{\Delta^{{n\times n}}}(\pi)-\eta H(\pi)

(5)

\text{where}\quad\pi(\mu)_{ij}&=\frac{1}{Z}\exp[-\eta^{-1}(C_{ij}-\varphi_{i}-\psi_{j})]\end{split}

(6)

2.4
OT Barycenters

An extension of the OT problem is the computation of “barycenters”. Given
m

\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}\}\subset\mathcal{P}(X)

\min_{r\in\mathcal{P}(X)}\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{m}w_{k}\left(\min_{\pi_{k}\in\Pi(r,c_{k})}\int_{X\times X}\mathfrak{c}(x,y)d\pi\right)\right\}.

(7)

To ease analysis, we specialize to a simplified discrete case where we assume that all marginals have common finite support
\{\delta_{x_{1}},\dots,\delta_{x_{n}}\}

c_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{k,j}\delta_{x_{j}}\quad k=1,\ldots,m.

(8)

We can therefore represent all marginals as

\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}\}\subset\Delta^{n}

C_{ij}=\mathfrak{c}(x_{i},x_{j})

\begin{split}\min_{r\in\Delta^{n},\{\pi_{k}\}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}w_{k}\left\langle C,\pi_{k}\right\rangle\\
\text{s.t. }\pi_{k}\in\Pi(r,c_{k})\quad k=1,\ldots,m.\end{split}

(OT Barycenter)

(

;
8
.

In response, many works have adopted alternative regularization schemes to remove the added dimensional dependence. The notion of “area convexity” proposed by
37
was applied by
18
to first attain
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\varepsilon^{-1})
iteration complexity. Rather than require the regularizer to be strongly convex, area convexity only requires strict convexity alongside certain geometric properties. However, area convexity is only defined over compact and convex sets
37
, hence the typical unconstrained OT/EOT duals are unsuitable. Accordingly,
18
reformulated the problem to allow for area-convex regularizers.

First, we reformulate the primal variables to implicitly enforce the row marginal constraint. Let
q\in\Delta^{n}
be an arbitrary probability mass function. Note that we can equivalently express any coupling
\pi\in\Pi(r,q)
as

\pi=\mathcal{D}_{r}p

(10)

where
p\in\{\Delta^{n}\}^{n}
is a row-stochastic matrix (each row
p_{i}\in\Delta^{n}
). After reparameterizing, the column marginal constraints can be enforced by adding an explicit
\ell_{1}
penalty term

\|c(\mathcal{D}_{r}p)-c\|_{1}

(11)

yielding the overall “penalized” problem

\min_{p\in\{\Delta^{n}\}^{n}}\left\{f(\mathcal{D}_{r}p)+2\|C\|_{\infty}\|c(\mathcal{D}_{r}p)-c\|_{1}\right\}.

(12)

We first recall existing results proving equivalence between the unregularized OT problem and its penalized reformulation. We then extend this equivalence to EOT, showing that the minimizer, values, and dual structure are equivalent. With both OT and EOT equivalence, we then show that equivalent penalized formulations exist for (E)OT barycenter problems.

3.1
Unregularized OT

The penalized formulation for unregularized OT is

\min_{p\in\{\Delta^{n}\}^{n}}\left\{F(\mathcal{D}_{r}p)=\left\langle\mathcal{D}_{r}p,C\right\rangle+2\|C\|_{\infty}\|c(\mathcal{D}_{r}p)-c\|_{1}\right\}.

(penOT)

Using the guarantees of Algorithm
1
, we can show that there exist minimizers to (
penOT
) which are column feasible. The following statement is adapted from Lemma 2.3 of
18
, and the proof is included for completeness.

Lemma 4
.

Suppose
p^{\star}
is an optimizer of (
penOT
). Then
\tilde{\pi}=\text{Round}(\mathcal{D}_{r}p^{\star},r,c)
is also an optimizer of (
penOT
), where Round is given in Algorithm
1
.

Proof.

By Lemma
1
,
\tilde{\pi}\in\Pi(r,c)
and

\|\tilde{\pi}-\mathcal{D}_{r}p^{\star}\|_{1}\leq 2\|(\mathcal{D}_{r}p^{\star})^{\top}\mathds{1}-c\|_{1}.

(13)

Since
\tilde{\pi}
is column feasible, we have

\displaystyle F(\tilde{\pi})-F(\mathcal{D}_{r}p^{\star})

\displaystyle=\left\langle C,\tilde{\pi}-\mathcal{D}_{r}p^{\star}\right\rangle-2\|C\|_{\infty}\|(\mathcal{D}_{r}p^{\star})^{\top}\mathds{1}-c\|_{1}.

Applying Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle F(\tilde{\pi})-F(\mathcal{D}_{r}p^{\star})

\displaystyle\leq\|C\|_{\infty}\|\tilde{\pi}-\mathcal{D}_{r}p^{\star}\|_{1}-2\|C\|_{\infty}\|(\mathcal{D}_{r}p^{\star})^{\top}\mathds{1}-c\|_{1}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ineq:l1_normbound}}}{{\leq}}2\|C\|_{\infty}\|(\mathcal{D}_{r}p^{\star})^{\top}\mathds{1}-c\|_{1}-2\|C\|_{\infty}\|(\mathcal{D}_{r}p^{\star})^{\top}\mathds{1}-c\|_{1}=0.

Then
F(\tilde{\pi})-F(p^{\star})\leq 0
, which implies that
F(\tilde{\pi})
is also optimal.
∎

Lemma
4
shows there exist optimizers of (
penOT
) which are column-feasible. We can straightforwardly show that these solutions are also optimizers to (
OT
).

Lemma 5
.

Let
\pi^{\star}
be an optimizer of (
OT
). Then

(p_{\pi}^{\star})_{ij}=\begin{cases}(r_{i})^{-1}\pi^{\star}_{ij}&r_{i}>0\\
0&r^{\star}_{i}=0\end{cases}.

(14)

is an optimizer of (
penOT
). Conversely, suppose
p^{\star}
is a feasible optimizer of (
penOT
). Then
\pi_{p}^{\star}=\mathcal{D}_{r}p^{\star}
is an optimizer of (
OT
).

Proof.

For all feasible
\mathcal{D}_{r}p\in\Pi(r,c)
, the objective values of (
OT
) and (
penOT
) are equal. The claim directly follows.
∎

The objective of (
penOT
) is non-smooth and therefore shares one of the primary difficulties of solving (
OT
). However, we can reformulate (
penOT
) as a saddle-point problem by using the identity

\|x\|_{1}=\sup_{\mu\in\{\Delta^{2}\}^{n}}\{\left\langle x,\mu_{+}-\mu_{-}\right\rangle\}

(15)

where each
\mu_{+},\mu_{-}\in[0,1]^{n}
satisfy
1=\mu_{+,i}+\mu_{-,i}
. Using this, we can lift the reformulated primal as

\min_{p\in\{\Delta^{n}\}^{n}}\max_{\mu\in\{\Delta^{2}\}^{n}}\overbrace{\left\{\left\langle\mathcal{D}_{r}p,C\right\rangle+2\|C\|_{\infty}\left\langle\mu_{+}-\mu_{-},c(\mathcal{D}_{r}p)-c\right\rangle\right\}}^{F(\mathcal{D}_{r}p,\mu)}.

(SPP penOT)

Since the objective
F(p,\mu)
is linear in both
p
and
\mu
, by von Neumann minimax arguments we can interchange the
\min
and
\max
, and hence apply primal-dual methods to solve (
SPP penOT
).

3.2
Entropy-Regularized OT

Unlike (
OT
), the EOT problem is strongly convex and therefore admits a unique minimizer within
\Pi(r,c)
.
Using a process similar to the derivation of (
penOT
), we define an exact penalty reformulation of (
EOT (Primal)
) as

\min_{p\in\{\Delta^{n}\}^{n}}\left\{\left\langle C,\mathcal{D}_{r}p\right\rangle+2\|C\|_{\infty}\|c(\mathcal{D}_{r}p)-c\|_{1}-\eta H(\mathcal{D}_{r}p)\right\}.

(penEOT (Primal))

In this subsection, we provide the following condition for equivalence between (
EOT (Primal)
) and (
penEOT (Primal)
).

Proposition 1
.

Let
r,c\in\Delta^{n}
and
C\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{{n\times n}}
and fix
\eta>0
. Let
\pi^{\star}
be the minimizer of the associated EOT problem (
EOT (Primal)
) and let
p^{\star}
be the minimizer of the penalized EOT problem (
penEOT (Primal)
).
Suppose that
\eta>0
satisfies

\eta\leq\frac{\|C\|_{\infty}}{-\log\min_{1\leq j\leq n}\{c_{j}\}}.

Then
\pi^{\star}=\mathcal{D}_{r}p^{\star}
, where
\mathcal{D}_{r}=\text{diag}(r)
.

Before proving Proposition
1
, we recall two results from literature. The first is a technical lemma regarding exact penalty formulations.

Lemma 6

(
(
30
, Theorem 17.3)
)
.

Consider the constrained nonlinear problem

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad c(x)=0,

(16)

with
c:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
and define its exact
\ell_{1}
penalization as

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\{\phi_{\mu}(x)=f(x)+\mu\|c(x)\|_{1}\}.

(17)

Suppose that
x^{*}
is a strict local solution of (
16
) with Lagrange multipliers
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then
x^{*}
is a local minimizer of (
17
) for all
\mu>\mu^{*}
where

\mu^{*}=\|\lambda\|_{\infty}.

(18)

Therefore, if we can guarantee that
\|\psi\|_{\infty}\leq 2\|C\|_{\infty}
, we can guarantee that (
EOT (Primal)
) and (
penEOT (Primal)
) have the same minimizer. Fortunately, we have the following bound from
25
characterizing the infinity norm of the dual multipliers. The proof follows
25
, however we show the argument here since our statement differs slightly from theirs. However, they weaken the bound with a
\log n
term and use a common constant rather than differentiate by multiplier.

Lemma 7

(
(
25
, Lemma 3)
)
.

For the dual EOT problem defined in (
EOT (Dual)
), there exists an optimal solution
(\varphi^{\star},\psi^{\star})
such that

\displaystyle\|\varphi^{\star}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|C\|_{\infty}-\eta\log\underset{1\leq i\leq n}{\min}\{r_{i}\},

\displaystyle\;\|\psi^{\star}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\|C\|_{\infty}-\eta\log\underset{1\leq j\leq n}{\min}\{c_{j}\}.

Proof.

We first claim that there exist optimal dual potentials
\varphi^{\star}
and
\psi^{\star}
satisfying

\begin{split}\min_{i}\varphi^{\star}_{i}\leq 0\leq\max_{i}\varphi^{\star}_{i},\\
\min_{j}\psi^{\star}_{i}\leq 0\leq\max_{j}\psi^{\star}_{j}.\end{split}

(19)

Let
\hat{\varphi}^{\star}
,
\hat{\psi}^{\star}
be a pair of optimal dual potentials. Define

\Delta_{\varphi}=\frac{1}{2}(\max_{i}\hat{\varphi}_{i}^{\star}-\min_{i}\hat{\varphi}_{i}^{\star}),\quad\Delta_{\psi}=\frac{1}{2}(\max_{j}\hat{\psi}_{j}^{\star}-\min_{j}\hat{\psi}_{j}^{\star})

and set
\varphi^{\star}=\hat{\varphi}^{\star}+\Delta_{\varphi}
and
\psi^{\star}=\hat{\psi}^{\star}+\Delta_{\psi}
.
By Lemma
2
,
\varphi^{\star}
and
\psi^{\star}
are optimal dual potentials. Furthermore by construction, they satisfy (
19
).

Now, taking the gradient of (
EOT (Dual)
), we have that

0=r_{i}-\exp[\eta^{-1}\varphi^{\star}_{i}]\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\exp[-\eta^{-1}(C_{ij}-\psi^{\star}_{j})]}{\underbrace{\sum_{k,\ell}^{n}\exp[-\eta^{-1}(C_{ij}-\varphi^{\star}_{k}-\psi^{\star}_{\ell})]}_{=Z}}

(20)

or

\varphi^{\star}_{i}=\eta\log r_{i}-\eta\log\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\exp[-\eta^{-1}(C_{ij}-\psi^{\star}_{j})]\right)+\eta\log Z.

(21)

By the assumption that
C\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n\times n}
we have for all
i
,
j

\exp[-\eta^{-1}(C_{ij}-\psi^{\star}_{j})]\geq\exp[\eta^{-1}(\psi^{\star}_{j})]\exp[-\eta^{-1}\|C\|_{\infty}].

(22)

We also have
\log r_{i}\leq 0
, hence

\varphi^{\star}_{i}\leq-\eta\log\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\exp[\eta^{-1}\psi^{\star}_{j}]\right)+\|C\|_{\infty}.

(23)

Similarly, since each
C_{ij}\geq 0
, we have

\varphi^{\star}_{i}\geq\eta\log r_{i}-\eta\log\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\exp[-\eta^{-1}\psi^{\star}_{j}]\right)+\eta\log Z.

(24)

Then we have

\displaystyle\max_{i}\varphi_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\eta\log\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\exp[-\eta^{-1}\psi^{\star}_{j}]\right)+\|C\|_{\infty}+\eta\log Z

\displaystyle\min_{i}\varphi_{i}

\displaystyle\geq\eta\log\min_{i}r_{i}+\eta\log\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\exp[-\eta^{-1}\psi^{\star}_{j}]\right)+\eta\log Z.

So,

\displaystyle\max_{i}\varphi_{i}-\min_{i}\varphi_{i}\leq\|C\|_{\infty}-\eta\log\min_{i}r_{i}.

Since
\min_{i}\varphi_{i}\leq 0
and
\max_{i}\varphi_{i}\geq 0
, we have that

\displaystyle 0\geq\min_{i}\varphi_{i}\geq-(\|C\|_{\infty}-\eta\log\min_{i}r_{i}),

\displaystyle 0\leq\max_{i}\varphi_{i}\leq\|C\|_{\infty}-\eta\log\min_{i}r_{i},

which implies that

\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq\|C\|_{\infty}-\eta\log\min_{i}r_{i}

as claimed. A similar argument holds for the dual variable
\psi
with the
c
marginal in place of the
r
marginal.
∎

Proposition
1
follows from combining the penalization requirement from Lemma
6
with the bound on the dual infinity norm from
7
.

Proof of Proposition
1
.

The primal penalized problem is (
penEOT (Primal)
)

\displaystyle\min_{p\in\{\Delta^{n}\}^{n}}\left\{\left\langle C,\mathcal{D}_{r}p\right\rangle+2\|C\|_{\infty}\|(\mathcal{D}_{r}p)^{\top}\mathds{1}-c\|_{1}-\eta H(\mathcal{D}_{r}p)\right\}.

With penalty coefficient
2\|C\|_{\infty}
, Lemma
6
requires the bound

\|\psi\|_{\infty}\leq 2\|C\|_{\infty}.

(25)

Applying the bound from Lemma
7
, we have the condition

\|C\|_{\infty}+\eta\left(\log n-\log\underset{j}{\min}\{c_{j}\}\right)\leq 2\|C\|_{\infty}

(26)

or

\eta\left(\log n-\log\min_{j}\{c_{j}\}\right)\leq\|C\|_{\infty}.

(27)

The condition on
\eta
follows from rearranging. Note that since both (
EOT (Primal)
) and (
penEOT (Primal)
) are strongly convex on their domains, their minimizers are unique, proving the claim.
∎

The simplex saddle-point formulation of EOT is derived in the same way as (
SPP penOT
) by dualizing the
\ell_{1}
penalty

\max_{\mu\in\{\Delta^{2}\}^{n}}\min_{p\in\{\Delta^{n}\}^{n}}\left\{\left\langle C,\mathcal{D}_{r}p\right\rangle+2\|C\|_{\infty}\left\langle c(\mathcal{D}_{r}p)-c,\mu_{+}-\mu_{-}\right\rangle-\eta H(\mathcal{D}_{r}p)\right\}.

(28)

Note that we can use the reformulation to obtain an entropy expression separable in the rows
p_{i}
. The entropy term appearing in the main formulation
H(\mathcal{D}_{r}p)
and the weighted entropy
\sum_{i=1}r_{i}H(p_{i})
only differ by the constant term
H(r)

H(\mathcal{D}_{r}p)=-\sum_{i,j}r_{i}p_{ij}\log r_{i}p_{ij}=-\sum_{i,j}r_{i}p_{ij}\log r_{i}-\sum_{i,j}r_{i}p_{ij}\log p_{ij}=H(r)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}H(p_{i}).

(29)

Therefore we can express the primal problem as

\min_{p\in\{\Delta^{n}\}^{n}}\left\{\left\langle C,\mathcal{D}_{r}p\right\rangle+2\|C\|_{\infty}\|c(\mathcal{D}_{r}p)-c\|_{1}-\eta\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}H(p_{i})\right\}.

(30)

where we disregard the constant
H(r)
. However, entropy of
r
will become significant when we consider extensions to OT barycenter problems in Section
3.4
. The final “simplex saddle-point” formulation of EOT can then be expressed as

\max_{\mu\in\{\Delta^{2}\}^{n}}\min_{p\in\{\Delta^{n}\}^{n}}\left\{\overbrace{\left\langle C,\mathcal{D}_{r}p\right\rangle+2\|C\|_{\infty}\left\langle c(\mathcal{D}_{r}p)-c,\mu_{+}-\mu_{-}\right\rangle-\eta\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}H(p_{i})}^{F_{\eta}(\mathcal{D}_{r}p,\mu)}\right\}

(31)

which is the form considered in
23
, though here without dual regularization.

3.3
Structural Equivalence

23
utilized the optimality conditions of the entropy-regularized problem in their extragradient convergence analysis. However, to our knowledge, the structure of the optimal solution
p^{\star}
has not been extensively explored in literature. Here we show that the simplex saddle-point formulation exhibits a rich dual structure which can be mapped to the EOT dual.

Proposition 2
.

The primal minimization problem
\min_{p\in\{\Delta^{n}\}^{n}}F_{\eta}(p,\mu)
has the solution
p(\mu)

p(\mu)_{ij}=\exp\left[-\eta(C_{ij}+2\|C\|_{\infty}(\mu_{+,j}-\mu_{-,j}))-\log Z_{i}\right]

(32)

where
Z_{i}
is the row normalization constant

Z_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\exp\left(-\eta^{-1}(C_{ij}+2\|C\|_{\infty}(\mu_{+,j}-\mu_{-,j}))\right).

(33)

Proof.

The gradient of
F_{\eta}(p,\mu)
with respect to
p
is

\nabla_{p}F_{\eta}(\mathcal{D}_{r}p,\mu)=\mathcal{D}_{r}C+2\|C\|_{\infty}\mathcal{D}_{r}\mathds{1}(\mu_{+,j}-\mu_{-,j})^{\top}+\eta\left(\log p+1\right)+\mathcal{N}_{\{\Delta^{n}\}^{n}}(p)

(34)

where
\log
should be interpreted as an element-wise operator and
\mathcal{N}_{\{\Delta^{n}\}^{n}}(p)
is the normal cone to the set of row-stochastic matrices. Since
p
is on the interior of the simplex, the normal cone consists of row-uniform rays ensuring that each matrix row is normalized. Setting the gradient to zero and solving for
p
gives the claimed form.
∎

Similar to the EOT dual (
EOT (Dual)
), we can analyze the dual problem

D_{\eta}(\mu)=\min_{p\in\{\Delta^{n}\}^{n}}F_{\eta}(\mathcal{D}_{r}p,\mu).

Using (
32
) we can state
D_{\eta}(\mu)
in closed form.

Corollary 1
.

The dual problem

D_{\eta}(\mu)=\min_{p\in\{\Delta^{n}\}^{n}}F_{\eta}(\mathcal{D}_{r}p,\mu)

has the closed form expression

D_{\eta}(\mu)=-2\|C\|_{\infty}\left\langle c,\mu_{+}-\mu_{-}\right\rangle-\eta\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\mathrm{LSE}_{j}\left(\eta^{-1}(C_{ij}+2\|C\|_{\infty}(\mu_{+,j}-\mu_{-,j}))\right).

(penEOT Dual)

Proof.

Substituting in the expression for
p(\mu)
, we have

\displaystyle D_{\eta}(\mu)=

\displaystyle\left\langle C,\mathcal{D}_{r}p(\mu)\right\rangle+2\|C\|_{\infty}\left\langle\mathcal{D}_{r}p(\mu)^{\top}\mathds{1},\mu_{+}-\mu_{-}\right\rangle

\displaystyle-2\|C\|_{\infty}\left\langle c,\mu_{+}-\mu_{-}\right\rangle-\sum_{ij}r_{i}(C_{ij}+2\|C\|_{\infty}(\mu_{+,j}-\mu_{-,j})+\eta\log Z_{i})p_{ij}(\mu).

Noting that

\sum_{i=1}(\mu_{+,j}-\mu_{-,j})p_{ij}(\mu)=\left\langle c(\mathcal{D}_{r}p(\mu)),\mu_{+}-\mu_{-}\right\rangle,\quad\sum_{ij}r_{i}C_{ij}p_{ij}(\mu)=\left\langle C,\mathcal{D}_{r}p(\mu)\right\rangle,

we have the simplified problem

\displaystyle\max_{\mu\in\{\Delta^{2}\}^{n}}D_{\eta}(\mu)

\displaystyle\triangleq\max_{\mu\in\{\Delta^{2}\}^{n}}\left\{-2\|C\|_{\infty}\left\langle c,\mu_{+}-\mu_{-}\right\rangle-\eta\sum_{ij}r_{i}\log Z_{i}\right\}

\displaystyle=\max_{\mu\in\{\Delta^{2}\}}\left\{-2\|C\|_{\infty}\left\langle c,\mu_{+}-\mu_{-}\right\rangle-\eta\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\mathrm{LSE}_{j}\left(\eta^{-1}(C_{ij}+2\|C\|_{\infty}(\mu_{+,j}-\mu_{-,j}))\right)\right\}.

∎

As an additional practical benefit, we now have a computable primal-dual gap

\delta(\mathcal{D}_{r}p,\mu)=F_{\eta}\left(\mathcal{D}_{r}p\right)-D_{\eta}(\mu)

(35)

which upper bounds the sub-optimality of
(p,\mu)
. We explicitly use this as a termination condition in Section
6
.

Having derived the dual structure for the simplex saddle-point problem, we can show equivalence with the dual variables of the original EOT problem.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\varphi^{\star}
,
\psi^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the optimal dual potentials for (
EOT (Dual)
) and let
\mu^{\star}\in\{\Delta^{2}\}^{n}
be the optimal dual solution to (
penEOT Dual
). Then we have the following equivalence

\displaystyle\eta(\log r_{i}-\log Z_{i})

\displaystyle=\varphi_{i}+\alpha_{\varphi},

(36)

\displaystyle-2\|C\|_{\infty}(\mu_{j,+}-\mu_{j,-})

\displaystyle=\psi_{j}+\alpha_{\psi},

(37)

where
\alpha_{\psi}
,
\alpha_{\varphi}
are additive constants satisfying

\eta^{-1}(-\alpha_{\varphi}-\alpha_{\psi})=\log Z^{\star}

(38)

where
Z^{\star}
is the normalizing constant of
\pi^{\star}
.

Proof.

The claim simply follows by the equivalence
\mathcal{D}_{r}p^{\star}=\pi^{\star}
, which gives

\displaystyle\log(\mathcal{D}_{r}p)_{ij}^{\star}=-\eta^{-1}(C_{ij}+2\|C\|_{\infty}(\mu_{+,j}-\mu_{-,j}))-\log Z_{i}+\log r_{i}

\displaystyle=-\eta^{-1}(C_{ij}-\varphi_{i}-\psi_{j})-\underbrace{\eta^{-1}(-\alpha_{\varphi}-\alpha_{\psi})}_{\log Z^{\star}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eqn:pi_mu}}}{{=}}\log\pi^{\star}.

∎

Therefore, the simplex saddle-point formulation can recover the original EOT dual variables. The duals are vital, for instance, in OT-based computation of discrete choice models and asset pricing
13
.

Entropy-Regularized Extragradient Methods

A primal-dual extragradient method to solve (

\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(n^{2}\varepsilon^{-1})
computational complexity for unregularized optimal transport. In this section, we first review the PDXG algorithm. Next, we show how a simple observation enables an equivalent DXG algorithm that requires only

With its lightweight memory footprint, the DXG method is then ideal for OT barycenter problems. Theoretical guarantees are deferred to the next section, where the OT and OT barycenter cases will be treated in a unified manner.

4.1
Review of a Primal-Dual Method

We consider the following dual-regularized generalization of the saddle-point problem

4.2
Achieving Linear Space

The scaled LogSumExp function

is
\eta^{-1}
smooth
(
6
, Example 5.15)
and concave. Therefore, the dual function
D_{\eta}(\mu)
is concave and we could naively use a mirror ascent scheme to find the optimal
\mu
in linear space. However, this naive approach has
\mathcal{O}\left((\eta\varepsilon)^{-1}\right)
iteration complexity, which is unacceptable when
\eta\propto\varepsilon
. Instead, we show that we can reformulate Algorithm
2
entirely in terms of dual variables. The dual-reformulated extragradient algorithm is shown in Algorithm
3
. As we will justify in the next section, we also remove the separate “adjust” sequence to further lower the storage overhead.

The key to our approach is to introduce three new sequences which allow for the recovery of the primal iterate
p
. We introduce
\nu\in\{\Delta^{2}\}^{n}
as the dual representation of the primal iterate
p
and the scalar sequence
\{s^{t}\}\subset[0,1]
which reweights the cost matrix.

The new
\nu
sequence is a convex combination of the dual midpoints
\{\bar{\mu}^{t}\}

\nu^{0}=(1/2,1/2)^{n},\quad\nu^{t+1}=(1-\tau_{p}\eta)\nu^{t}+\tau_{p}\eta\bar{\mu}^{t+1}.

(58)

With these sequences, we define the dual-to-primal map
p_{s}(\nu):[0,1]\times\{\Delta^{2}\}^{n}\to\{\Delta^{n}\}^{n}
as

\begin{split}p_{s}(\nu)\triangleq&\exp[-\eta^{-1}(sC+2\|C\|_{\infty}\mathds{1}(\nu_{+}-\nu_{-})^{\top})-\log Z_{s}(\nu)\mathds{1}^{\top}],\\
&\text{where }Z_{s}(\nu)\triangleq\exp[-\eta^{-1}(sC+2\|C\|_{\infty}\mathds{1}(\nu_{+,j}-\nu_{-,j})^{\top})]\mathds{1}.\end{split}

(59)

The following proposition is similar to the primal-dual relationship independently derived in
(
4

\{v^{t}\}

Proposition 5
.

Consider the sequences
\{p^{t}\},\;\{\bar{p}^{t}\}\subset\{\Delta^{n}\}^{n}
produced by Algorithm
2
and the sequences
\{\nu^{t}\},\;\{\bar{\nu}^{t}\}\subset\{\Delta^{2}\}^{n},\;\{s_{t}\}\subset[0,1]

. Then for every

\displaystyle p^{t}=p_{s_{t}}(\nu^{t}),\quad\bar{p}^{t}=p_{s_{t}}(\bar{\nu}^{t}).

We prove the claim by induction on

(61)

\displaystyle\log p^{t+1}_{ij}=(1-\tau_{p}\eta)\log p^{t}_{ij}-\tau_{p}(C_{ij}+2\|C\|_{\infty}(\bar{\mu}_{+,j}^{t+1}-\bar{\mu}_{-,j}^{t+1}))-\log Z_{i}^{t+1}

\log Z^{t+1}_{i}
is a row-wise normalization constant.

Applying the inductive hypothesis,

\displaystyle\log p^{t+1}_{ij}=

\displaystyle-(1-\tau_{p}\eta)\eta^{-1}(s_{t}C_{ij}+2\|C\|_{\infty}(\nu^{t}_{+,j}-\nu^{t}_{+,j}))

\displaystyle+\tau_{p}(C_{ij}+2\|C\|_{\infty}(\bar{\mu}_{+,j}^{t+1}-\bar{\mu}_{-,j}^{t+1}))-\log Z_{i}^{t+1}-(1-\tau_{p}\eta)\log Z_{i}^{t}.

In this section we provide computational complexity bounds for Algorithms

3

4

\{\mu^{t,\text{adj}}\}
can be removed without affecting convergence. Instead, the dual variables
\{\mu^{t}\}
can be directly projected to the high-entropy subset
\{\Delta_{\beta}^{2}\}^{n}

5.1
Removing the “Adjust” Sequence

We first demonstrate that the properties of the “balancing” step can be used to remove the explicit “adjust” sequence without affecting the convergence of Algorithms
2
and
3
.

Lemma 9
.

The balancing operation in Algorithm
2

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{+,j}\propto\max\{\mu_{+,j},e^{-\beta}\max\{\mu_{+,j},\mu_{-,j}\}\}

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{-,j}\propto\max\{\mu_{-,j},e^{-\beta}\max\{\mu_{+,j},\mu_{-,j}\}\}

is equivalent to the 2D Bregman projection

\tilde{\mu}_{+,j}=\underset{\nu\in\{\Delta_{\beta}^{2}\}^{n}}{\mathrm{argmin}\,}\{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\nu\|\mu_{j})\}.

(76)

Proof.

In two dimensions, we can reparameterize the distribution as
\mu_{+,j}=a
and
\mu_{-,j}=1-a
. Assume that
a>(1-a)
(the proof for the other case is identical). Note that if
\mu\in\{\Delta_{\beta}^{2}\}^{n}
the claim is trivial, since
\tilde{\mu}=\mu
. Then assume
\mu\not\in\{\Delta_{\beta}^{2}\}^{n}
. Representing
\nu_{+,j}=x
,
\nu_{-,j}=1-x
, the KL divergence from
\mu_{j}
to
\tilde{\mu}_{j}
is

\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\nu_{j}\|\mu_{j})=x\log\frac{x}{a}+(1-x)\log\frac{(1-x)}{(1-a)}.

(77)

Then, taking the derivative with respect to
x
is

\frac{d}{dx}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\nu_{j}\|\mu_{j})=\log\frac{x}{a}-\log\frac{(1-x)}{(1-a)}=\log\frac{x(1-a)}{(1-x)a}.

(78)

Clearly, for all
\nu\in\{\Delta_{\beta}^{2}\}^{n}
,
\nu_{+,j}<\mu_{+,j}
, hence
x/a\leq 1
and
(1-a)/(1-x)\leq 1
. Therefore
\frac{d}{dx}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\nu_{j}\|\mu_{j})<0
, which implies that the minimizer is on the upper boundary of the high-entropy subset, i.e.
\tilde{\mu}^{+}\propto e^{\beta}
.
∎

The balancing step aids in both convergence proofs and significantly improves performance in practice. The original algorithm proposed by

maintains two dual sequences, the main

and the “adjust” sequence

which serve as the prox centers for the MD iterations. However, we note that if the optimal dual solution lies within
\{\Delta_{\beta}^{2}\}^{n}

Proposition 6
.

Suppose
\mu^{\star}\in\{\Delta_{\beta}^{2}\}^{n}
and consider
\tilde{\mu}\in\{\Delta^{2}\}^{n}
and
\mu
where
\mu
is
\tilde{\mu}
after the balancing step,

\displaystyle\mu_{+,j}\propto\max\{\tilde{\mu}_{+,j},e^{-\beta}\max\{\tilde{\mu}_{+,j},\tilde{\mu}_{-,j}\}\},

\displaystyle\mu_{-,j}\propto\max\{\tilde{\mu}_{-,j},e^{-\beta}\max\{\tilde{\mu}_{+,j},\tilde{\mu}_{-,j}\}\}.

Then

\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(D_{\tilde{c}}\mu^{\star}\|D_{\tilde{c}}\mu)\leq\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(D_{\tilde{c}}\mu^{\star}\|D_{\tilde{c}}\tilde{\mu}).

(79)

Proof.

The claim is trivial for
\mu\in\{\Delta_{\beta}^{2}\}^{n}
, since
\tilde{\mu}=\mu
. We then assume that
\tilde{\mu}\not\in\{\Delta_{\beta}^{2}\}^{n}
.

Using the three-point lemma, we have

\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(D_{\tilde{c}}\mu^{\star}\|D_{\tilde{c}}\tilde{\mu})-\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(D_{\tilde{c}}\mu\|D_{\tilde{c}}\tilde{\mu})-\left\langle\nabla\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(D_{\tilde{c}}\mu\|D_{\tilde{c}}\tilde{\mu}),D_{\tilde{c}}(\mu^{\star}-\mu)\right\rangle=\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(D_{\tilde{c}}\mu^{\star}\|D_{\tilde{c}}\mu).

for all

∎

The previously demonstrated equivalence with EOT provides a sufficient condition to guarantee that
\mu^{\star}\in\{\Delta_{\beta}^{2}\}^{n}

Lemma 10
.

Assume

Proof.

All solvers were implemented in Julia using CUDA acceleration. Custom, optimized kernels were written for log-domain Sinkhorn/IBP and DXG to fuse operations and lower overhead. 7
Conclusion

We have presented a linear-space implementation of a dual extragradient method DXG (Algorithm
3
). DXG inherits the state-of-the-art theoretical guarantees of PDXG
23
and considerable simplifies the linear space implementation described in
4
. Moreover, we prove several fundamental results linking the formulation of
23
to classical entropic optimal transport and enabling the formulation to be extended to Wasserstein barycenter problems. The proposed DXG framework offers an alternative paradigm for weakly regularized EOT in large-scale, memory-constrained problems.

Numerical experiments show that DXG outperforms existing methods in several OT and EOT benchmarks. In weakly regularized EOT and EOT with
\ell_{1}
costs, DXG was the most performant method tested. The high performance on “EMD” costs suggests that DXG is highly applicable to large-scale problems with
\ell_{1}
ground costs
33
;
17
. DXG was also shown to be competitive with Sinkhorn in large-scale EOT problems, scaling up to
512\times 512
color transfer (
n=2^{18}
) using dedicated GPU kernels. However, there are several practical and theoretical gaps which need to be resolved in future work.

We believe that the convergence rate presented in Theorem
2
can be tightened by more refined analyses to remove the additional
\sqrt{n}
dependence. Current analyses also depend on
\eta_{\mu}>0
, which contradicts practical guidance for parameter setting. It is highly desirable to remove this assumption. Further explorations of the
\eta\to 0
limit would also be fruitful in understanding algorithm behavior.

DXG performance was highly dependent on the underlying metric, with slower convergence in Euclidean cost problems.
\ell_{2}
distances are among the most common OT formulations. Future work exploring and alleviating the dependence of extragradient methods on the cost properties is therefore critical. Furthermore, DXG performed poorly in barycenter problems regardless of the metric used, indicating that more careful analysis and modification of Algorithm
4
is needed to apply extragradient methods to OT barycenter problems.

In summary, the proposed DXG method offers a promising framework for fast, high-accuracy OT and EOT in large-scale applications, with current methods showing state-of-the-art performance in certain problem classes. However, future research is needed to make extragradient methods a compelling alternative across the spectrum of OT problems.

Lemma 12
.

For any
\mu_{1}
,
\mu_{2}\in\{\Delta^{2}\}^{n}
, and
q\in\Delta^{n}

\|\mathcal{D}_{q}^{1/2}(\mu_{1,+}-\mu_{2,+}-\mu_{1,-}+\mu_{2,-})\|^{2}\leq 2\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathcal{D}_{q}\mu_{1}\|\mathcal{D}_{q}\mu_{2})

(106)

where the
(\cdot)^{2}
term in the inner product is elementwise.

Proof.

Note that for any
(a,b),\;(c,d)\in\Delta^{2}

(a-b-(c-d))^{2}=(a-c+(d-b))^{2}=(a-c)^{2}+2(a-c)(d-b)+(b-d)=\|(a,b)-(c,d)\|_{1}^{2}.

Then

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{D}_{q}^{1/2}(\mu_{1,+}-\mu_{2,+}-\mu_{1,-}+\mu_{2,-})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle q,(\mu_{1,+}-\mu_{2,+}-\mu_{1,-}+\mu_{2,-})^{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}q_{j}\|\mu_{1,j}-\mu_{2,j}\|_{1}^{2}\leq 2\sum_{j=1}^{n}q_{j}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mu_{1,j}\|\mu_{2,j})

where the second inequality results from Pinsker. Since

\sum_{j=1}^{n}q_{j}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mu_{1,j}\|\mu_{2,j})=\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathcal{D}_{q}\mu_{1}\|\mathcal{D}_{q}\mu_{2}),

(107)

we have the result.
∎

Lemma 13
.

Suppose that
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\{\Delta^{2}\}^{n}
,
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in\Delta^{{n\times n}}
,
q\in\mathbb{R}_{++}^{n}
is a vector with strictly positive entries, and
\tau>0
is a strictly positive scalar. Then

\left\langle\mu_{1,+}-\mu_{2,+}-\mu_{1,-}+\mu_{2,-},c(\pi_{1})-c(\pi_{2})\right\rangle\leq\frac{\tau}{2}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathcal{D}_{q}\mu_{1}\|\mathcal{D}_{q}\mu_{2})+\frac{2}{\tau\min_{i}q_{i}}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_{1}\|\pi_{2}).

(108)

Proof.

Note that since
q
is strictly positive
\mathcal{D}_{q}^{1/2}
exists and is invertible. Then, by Young’s with we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{D}_{q}^{1/2}(\mu_{1,+}-\mu_{2,+}-\mu_{1,-}+\mu_{2,-}),\mathcal{D}_{q}^{-1/2}(c(\pi_{1})-c(\pi_{2}))\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\tau}{2\cdot 2}\|\mathcal{D}_{q}^{1/2}(\mu_{1,+}-\mu_{2,+}-\mu_{1,-}+\mu_{2,-})\|^{2}+\frac{2}{2\cdot\tau}\|\mathcal{D}_{q}^{-1/2}(c(\pi_{1})-c(\pi_{2}))\|_{2}^{2}.

Applying Cauchy-Schwarz to the second term with the spectral norm gives

\frac{1}{\tau}\|\mathcal{D}_{q}^{-1/2}(c(\pi_{1})-c(\pi_{2}))\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\tau\min_{i}q_{i}}\|c(\pi_{1})-c(\pi_{2})\|_{2}^{2}.

(109)

Note that we have

\frac{1}{\tau\min_{i}q_{i}}\|c(\pi_{1})-c(\pi_{2})\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\tau\min_{i}q_{i}}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(c(\pi_{1})\|c(\pi_{2}))\leq\frac{1}{\tau\min_{i}q_{i}}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_{1}\|\pi_{2})

(110)

where the first inequality follows by the strong convexity of
-H
and the second by the data processing inequality.

Finally, using Lemma
12
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\tau}{4}\|\mathcal{D}_{q}^{1/2}(\mu_{1,+}-\mu_{2,+}-\mu_{1,-}+\mu_{2,-})\|^{2}_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Lem~\ref{lem:dual_pinsker}}}}{{\leq}}\frac{1}{2\tau}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathcal{D}_{q}\mu_{1}\|\mathcal{D}_{q}\mu_{2})

which completes the proof.
∎

15
yields

\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\zeta^{\star}\|\tilde{\zeta}^{t+1})\leq(1-\bm{\eta})\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\zeta^{\star}\|\zeta^{t})\leq(1-\eta_{\mu}\tau_{\mu})\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\zeta^{\star}\|\zeta^{t}).

(120)

Using Proposition
6
, we have

\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\zeta^{\star}\|\zeta^{t+1})\leq\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\zeta^{\star}\|\tilde{\zeta}^{t+1})

(121)

which yields the single-step contraction result. Unrolling this inequality yields the contraction guarantee

\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\zeta^{\star}\|\zeta^{t})\leq(1-\eta_{\mu}\tau_{\mu})^{t}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\zeta^{\star}\|\zeta^{0}).

(122)

By the initial choice of parameters, we have

\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star}\|\mathcal{D}_{r}^{t}p_{k}^{\star})=-H(\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star})-\sum_{ij}r_{i}^{\star}(p_{k}^{\star})_{ij}\log\frac{r_{i}}{n}\leq\begin{cases}\log n&\text{if }r^{\star}=r^{t}=r\text{ (OT/EOT case)}\\
2\log n&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(123)

Similarly, we have

\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{c}}\mu_{k}^{\star}\|\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{c}_{k}}\mu^{t})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tilde{c}_{j}(-H(\mu_{j}^{\star})+(\mu_{+,j}^{\star}+\mu_{-,j}^{\star})\log 2)=(1+\alpha)\log 2\leq 2\log 2.

(124)

Since
\tau_{\mu}
,
\tau_{p}=\Theta(\sqrt{n})
, we have

\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{c}}^{k}\mu_{k}^{\star}\|\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{c}_{k}}\mu^{t})=\Theta(\sqrt{n}\log n).

(125)

Then, requiring

(1-\eta_{\mu}\tau_{\mu})^{t}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\zeta^{\star}\|\zeta^{0})\leq\varepsilon
, we have

-\eta_{\mu}\tau_{\mu}t\leq t\log(1-\eta_{\mu}\tau_{\mu})\leq\log\frac{\varepsilon}{2\log n+\log 2}\leq\log\frac{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\zeta^{\star}\|\zeta^{0})}

(126)

or

t=O\left(\frac{1}{\tau_{\mu}\eta_{\mu}}\log\left(\frac{\sqrt{n}(2\log n+2\log 2)}{\varepsilon}\right)\right).

(127)

The choice of
\tau_{\mu}
yields the
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}
result.
∎

A.2
Computational Complexity

We follow a similar process as
[
23
]
to convert the KL-divergence complexity of Prop
7
to the complexity of approximately solving an OT or OT barycenter problem. The following result is a generalization of Equation 5.13 from
[
23
]
.

Proposition 8
.

Suppose that
\eta\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{16\log(n)}
,
\eta_{\mu}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{16\log(2)}
and let
p_{k}^{\star}
,
r^{\star}
,
\mu_{k}^{\star}
be the optimizers to the entropy-regularized problem (
47
) and let
\hat{r}^{\star}
be the optimal marginal with optimal plans
\pi_{k}^{\star}
. Then for all
k\in\{1,\dots,m\}

\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_{k}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\langle C,\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star}\right\rangle+\|c(\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star})-c_{k}\|_{1}\right)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_{k}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\langle C,\pi^{\star}_{k}\right\rangle\right)+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.

(128)

Proof.

Note that, from our choice of
\eta
,
\eta_{\mu}
we have for all
k

\eta H(\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k})\leq 2\eta\log n\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{8},\quad\eta_{\mu}H(\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{c}_{k}}\mu_{k})-\eta_{\mu}H(\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{c}_{k}})\leq 2\eta_{\mu}\log 2\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{8}.

(129)

where we remove the entropy contribution from
\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{c}_{k}}
as a constant offset.

We then prove the claim using the same chain of inequalities as
[
23
]
.

First, defining
\mu^{\ell_{1}}=\underset{\mu\in\{\Delta^{2}\}^{n}}{\mathrm{argmax}\,}\left\{\left\langle c(\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star})-c_{k},\mu^{\ell_{1}}_{+}-\mu^{\ell_{1}}_{-}\right\rangle\right\}
and using the variational form of the
\ell_{1}
norm

\begin{split}\left\langle C,\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star}\right\rangle&+2\|C\|_{\infty}\|c(\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star})-c_{k}\|_{1}\\
=&\left\langle C,\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star}\right\rangle+2\|C\|_{\infty}\left\langle c(\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star})-c_{k},\mu^{\ell_{1}}_{+}-\mu^{\ell_{1}}_{-}\right\rangle.\end{split}

(130)

Next, since
\eta_{\mu}H(\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{c}_{k}}\mu^{\ell_{1}})-\eta_{\mu}H(\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{c}_{k}})\geq 0
,
2\eta\log n\geq\eta H(\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star})
we have

\displaystyle\eqref{eqn:initial}\leq

\displaystyle\left\langle C,\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star}\right\rangle+2\|C\|_{\infty}\left\langle c(\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star})-c_{k},\mu^{\ell_{1}}_{+}-\mu^{\ell_{1}}_{-}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\eta H(\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star})+2\eta\log n+\eta_{\mu}H(\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{c}_{k}}\mu^{\ell_{1}})-\eta_{\mu}H(\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{c}_{k}}).

By the saddle-point property, replacing
\mu^{\ell_{1}}
with the regularized maximizers yields

\displaystyle\eqref{eqn:initial}\leq

\displaystyle\left\langle C,\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star}\right\rangle+2\|C\|_{\infty}\left\langle c(\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star})-c_{k},\mu^{\star}_{k,+}-\mu^{\star}_{k,-}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\eta H(\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star})+2\eta\log n+\eta_{\mu}H(\mu^{\star})-\eta_{\mu}H(\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{c}_{k}}).

Again using the saddle point the fact that
\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star}
are the primal minimizers with fixed
\mu
, we have

\displaystyle\eqref{eqn:initial}\leq

\displaystyle\left\langle C,\pi_{k}^{\star}\right\rangle+2\|C\|_{\infty}\left\langle c(\pi_{k}^{\star})-c_{k},\mu^{\star}_{k,+}-\mu^{\star}_{k,-}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\quad-H(\pi_{k}^{\star})+\eta_{\mu}H(\mu^{\star}_{k})+2\eta\log n,

where
\{\pi_{k}^{\star}\}_{k=1}^{m}
is the set of optimal transport plans between the optimal unregularized barycenter
\bm{r}^{\star}
and the marginals
\{c_{k}\}
. Finally, by primal feasibility, the upper bounds on the entropy terms, and the conditions on
\eta,\;\eta_{\mu}
we have

\displaystyle\eqref{eqn:initial}\leq

\displaystyle\left\langle C,\pi_{k}^{\star}\right\rangle+2\eta\log n+\eta_{\mu}H(\mu^{\star}_{k})-\eta_{\mu}H(\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{c}_{k}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\langle C,\pi_{k}^{\star}\right\rangle+2\eta\log n+2\eta_{\mu}\log 2

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\langle C,\pi_{k}^{\star}\right\rangle+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.

∎

Finally, we prove the main result.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

First, suppose we obtain
p_{k}^{t}
,
\mu_{k}^{t}
such that

\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{w}}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\zeta^{\star}\|\zeta^{t})\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\min_{k}w_{k}}{72\tau_{p}}.

(131)

which, by Proposition
7
, requires
O\left(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\eta_{\mu}}\log\left(\frac{n\log n}{\min_{k}w_{k}\varepsilon}\right)\right)
iterations.
Then, we have for each
k

\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star}\|\mathcal{D}_{r}^{t}p_{k}^{t})\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{72}.

(132)

By Pinsker’s inequality, we have

\|\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star}-\mathcal{D}_{r}^{t}p_{k}^{t}\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{2\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star}\|\mathcal{D}_{r}^{t}p_{k}^{t})}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{6}.

(133)

Then let
\tilde{\pi}_{k}=\textrm{Round}(\mathcal{D}_{r}^{t}p_{k}^{t},c_{k})
and let
\{\pi_{k}^{\star}\}_{k=1}^{m}
be the set of optimal transport plans between the optimal unregularized barycenter
\bm{r}^{\star}
and the marginals
\{c_{k}\}
. Then we have for all
k

\begin{split}\left\langle C,\tilde{\pi}_{k}\right\rangle&\leq\left\langle C,\mathcal{D}_{r}^{t}p_{k}^{t}\right\rangle+\|C\|_{\infty}\|\mathcal{D}_{r}^{t}p_{k}^{t}-\tilde{\pi}_{k}\|_{1}\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Lem.~\ref{lem:jason_rounding}}}}{{\leq}}\left\langle C,\mathcal{D}_{r}^{t}p_{k}^{t}\right\rangle+2\|C\|_{\infty}\|c(\mathcal{D}_{r}^{t}p_{k}^{t})-c_{k}\|_{1}\\
&\leq\left\langle C,\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star}\right\rangle+2\|C\|_{\infty}\|c(\mathcal{D}_{r}^{t}p_{k}^{t})-c_{k}\|_{1}+\|C\|_{\infty}\|\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star}-\mathcal{D}_{r}^{t}p_{k}^{t}\|_{1}\\
&\leq\left\langle C,\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star}\right\rangle+2\|C\|_{\infty}\|\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star}-c_{k}\|_{1}+3\|C\|_{\infty}\|\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star}-\mathcal{D}_{r}^{t}p_{k}^{t}\|_{1}\\
&=\left\langle C,\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star}\right\rangle+2\|\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star}-c_{k}\|_{1}+3\|\mathcal{D}_{r}^{\star}p_{k}^{\star}-\mathcal{D}_{r}^{t}p_{k}^{t}\|_{1},\end{split}

(134)

where first and third inequalities follow by Cauchy-Schwarz, the second by the triangle inequality, and the final equality follows from the assumption that each
\|C\|_{\infty}=1
. Then, using Proposition
8
and (
133
), we have

\left\langle C,\tilde{\pi}_{k}\right\rangle\leq\left\langle C,\pi_{k}^{\star}\right\rangle+\frac{2\varepsilon}{4}+\frac{3\varepsilon}{6}=\left\langle C,\pi^{\star}\right\rangle+\varepsilon.

(135)

Since the claim holds for all
C
, under the assumption that
\sum_{k=1}^{m}w_{k}=1
, we have

\sum_{i=1}w_{k}\left\langle C,\tilde{\pi}_{k}\right\rangle\leq\sum_{i=1}w_{k}\left\langle C,\pi_{k}^{\star}\right\rangle+\varepsilon.

(136)

∎

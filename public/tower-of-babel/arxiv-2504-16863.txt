On graphs with a simple structure of maximal cliques
J. Pascal Gollin FAMNIT, University of Primorska, Koper, Slovenia. pascal.gollin@famnit.upr.si Meike Hatzel Discrete Mathematics Group, Institute for Basic Science (IBS), Daejeon, South Korea. research@meikehatzel.com Sebastian Wiederrecht KAIST, School of Computing, Daejeon, South Korea. wiederrecht@kaist.ac.kr

Abstract
We say that a hereditary graph class 𝒢 \mathcal{G} is clique-sparse if there is a constant k = k ⁡ ( 𝒢 ) k=k(\mathcal{G}) such that for every graph G ∈ 𝒢 G\in\mathcal{G} , every vertex of G G belongs to at most k k maximal cliques, and any maximal clique of G G can be intersected in at most k k different ways by other maximal cliques. We provide various characterisations of clique-sparse graph classes including a list of five parametric forbidden induced subgraphs. We show that recent techniques for proving induced analogues of Menger’s Theorem and the Grid Theorem of Robertson and Seymour can be lifted to prove induced variants in clique-sparse graph classes when replacing “treewidth” by ”tree-independence number”.

Introduction

At the heart of the graph minors theory of Robertson and Seymour

Key to the distinction between these two cases is the celebrated

In recent years, there has been a push to understand this kind of structural behaviour through the lens of induced graph containment relation such as

(i.e. minors obtained only from the deletion of vertices and contraction of edges).
One of the main goals in this area of study is finding useful analogues for the Grid Theorem, and more fundamentally Menger’s Theorem, in the induced settings.

Both were accomplished, in their most desirable form, in the setting of graphs of bounded degree.
An induced variant for Menger’s Theorem (see

[

Moreover, Albrechtsen, Huynh, Jacobs, Knappe, and Wollan

Clique-incidence-diversity and clique-incidence-degree: lifting the induced variant of Menger’s Theorem.

Theorem 1.1

Let

The strategies of the two groups Gartland et al.
[
7
]
and Hendrey et al.
[
10

to prove their induced variants of Menger’s theorem are very similar, they just have slightly different perspectives.
For the sake of simplicity, we explain the strategy of Gartland et al.
[
7
]

Their argument is based on the idea of starting with a large general flow and reducing it to a smaller, but still large, induced flow.
It splits into two main steps.
In the first step, they consider a set
X
of vertices such that the members in
X
are pairwise at distance at least three to each other.
This means that the neighbourhoods of any two members of
X
do not intersect.
Let
G_{X}
be obtained from
G
by contracting the closed neighbourhood of every vertex in
X
.
As the maximum degree of
G
is bounded,
N_{G}[x]
contains only a bounded number of vertices for every vertex
x

So, of a large flow between two sets in
G

As the second step, they observe that on graphs with maximum degree
\Delta

\Delta^{2}+1
many colours such that any two vertices with the same colour are in distance at least three to each other.
Thus, the strategy established in the first step can be applied to each colour class separately, and because there are only a bounded number of colour classes, there still remains a large flow.

By controlling the two graph parameters
clique-incidence-degree
and
clique-incidence-diversity
directly, we obtain a strict generalisation of the regime of graphs with bounded maximum degree while maintaining the validity of
theorem

1.1
(and later on also

1.3

Of course, simply bounding these parameters would again not allow for a verbatim generalisation, since, as before, a large clique would be a counterexample.
But, as hinted at in the introduction, a different measure of
S
yields the solution.
Instead of the domination number
\gamma(G[S])

[
1
]
and Georgakopoulos and Papasoglu
[

\theta(G[S])

It is straightforward to see that

Hence, if we find a separator

\theta(G[S])

With these notions, we can prove the following theorem by reducing it to the result of Gartland et al.

At first glance, the class of graphs for which we prove an approximate induced Menger’s Theorem with

Graph measures and tree-decompositions.

A graph measure is a function
\mu
that, for every graph

, assigns to each vertex set

a non-negative integer
\mu_{G}(X)
.
For the two graph parameters
\alpha
and
\theta
we define corresponding graph measures by
\mu_{G}(X)\coloneqq\mu(G[X])
for both
\mu\in\{\alpha,\theta\}
.
Generally, if the graph is understood from the context, we drop the subscript in this notation.
In this paper,r we are mostly concerned with the graph measures
\alpha
,
\theta
, and the
cardinality
, denoted by
{\lvert{{}\cdot{}}\rvert}
.

A

for a graph

is a tuple
\mathcal{T}=(T,\beta)
where

is a tree and
\beta\colon V(T)\to 2^{V(G)}
, called the

of

, such that

\bigcup_{t\in V(T)}\beta(t)=V(G)
,

ii)

for every

there exists some

such that

, and

iii)

for every vertex

the set
\{t\in V(T)\mid v\in\beta(T)\}
induces a subtree of
T
.

Given a graph measure
\mu
, the
\mu
-width
of
\mathcal{T}
is defined as the value
{\max_{t\in V(T)}\mu_{G}(\beta(t))}
and the
\mu
-treewidth of
G
, denoted by
\mu
-
\mathsf{tw}(G)
, is the minimum
\mu
-width over all tree-decompositions for

.

For
\mu={\lvert{{}\cdot{}}\rvert}
, the definition above yields a parameter which is (up to a
-1
in the original definition) equal to

.
For
\mu=\alpha
we obtain the definition of
\alpha
-treewidth, or sometimes called the
tree-independence-number
.
Finally, for
\theta
we obtain the notion of
clique-cover-treewidth
.
In slight abuse of notation
we write
\mathsf{tw}(G)
instead of
{\lvert{{}\cdot{}}\rvert}\text{-}\mathsf{tw}(G)
.

3
Clique-incidence-diversity, clique-incidence-degree, and the clique-quotient graph

Let
G
be a graph.
Recall that
\mathcal{K}(G)
denotes the set of all vertex sets of
G
which induce maximal cliques in
G
.
We define an equivalence relation
\sim_{\mathcal{K}(G)}
on
V(G)
by saying that
{v\sim_{\mathcal{K}(G)}w}
if and only if for all
{K\in\mathcal{K}(G)}
,

{v\in K}\textnormal{ if and only if }{w\in K}.

We denote by
\widetilde{V(G)}
the set of equivalence classes of
\sim_{\mathcal{K}(G)}
and by
\widetilde{G}
the graph on
\widetilde{V(G)}
obtained from
G
by identifying equivalent vertices.
We call
{\widetilde{G}}
the
clique-quotient graph
of
G
.
Observe that two vertices
{v,w}
of
G
are equivalent, if and only if they are
true twins
in
G
, that is, for every
{x\in V(G)}
, we have that
{vx\in E(G)}
if and only if
{vy\in E(G)}
.
In other words, the equivalence classes are precisely the
modules
of
G
(that are, the sets
X
of vertices such that each vertex outside of
X
is either adjacent to each vertex in
X
or no vertex in
X
) that are also cliques, and the clique-quotient graph is obtained from
G
by contracting every such clique-module.
Throughout this paper, for ease of notation, for each
{v\in V(G)}
, we denote by
\widetilde{v}
the equivalence class of
v
with respect to
\sim_{\mathcal{K}(G)}
, and for
{X\subseteq V(G)}
, we denote by
{\widetilde{X}}
the set
X/{\sim_{\mathcal{K}(G)}}=\{\widetilde{x}\in\widetilde{V(G)}\mid x\in X\}
.
On the other hand, we may introduce an arbitrary vertex
{\widetilde{v}\in V(\widetilde{G})}
without having introduced
v
beforehand.
In this case, we may implicitly talk about
{v}
as a representative of
\widetilde{v}
.
Similarly, we may introduce an arbitrary subset
\widetilde{X}\subseteq V(\widetilde{G})
without having introduced a set
X
beforehand.
We often make use of the set
{\bigcup\widetilde{X}\coloneqq\{x\in V(G)\mid\widetilde{x}\in\widetilde{X}\}}
.
Note that
{\widetilde{\bigcup\widetilde{X}}=\widetilde{X}}
.
Additionally, when we introduce
\widetilde{X}\subseteq V(\widetilde{G})
for a set
X
of representatives, then we take
X
to be a set that, for each
\widetilde{x}\in\widetilde{X}
, contains exactly one element of
\widetilde{x}
.

Let
{x,y\in V(G)}
with
{x\not\sim_{\mathcal{K}(G)}y}
.
By definition of
\widetilde{G}
, we have

{xy\in E(G)}\,\text{ if and only if }\,\widetilde{x}\widetilde{y}\in E(\widetilde{G}).

(
\ast
)

Expanding on this idea, we observe the following.

Observation 3.1
.

Let
G
be a graph, let
{X,Y\subseteq V(G)}
.
Then the following properties hold.

(1)

There is an edge between
\widetilde{X}
and
\widetilde{Y}
in
E(\widetilde{G})
if and only if there is an edge between
\bigcup\widetilde{X}
and
\bigcup\widetilde{Y}
in
E(G)
.

(2)

If there is an edge between
\widetilde{X}
and
\widetilde{Y}
in
E(\widetilde{G})
, then there is an edge between
X
and
Y
in
E(G)
.

(3)

If there is an edge between
X
and
Y
in
E(G)
, then either there is an edge between
\widetilde{X}
and
\widetilde{Y}
in
E(\widetilde{G})
, or a vertex in
\widetilde{X}\cap\widetilde{Y}
.

Proof.

Let
{x\in X}
and
{y\in Y}
with
{x\not\sim_{\mathcal{K}(G)}y}
.
By (
equation

∗

),
{xy\in E(G)}
if and only if
{\widetilde{x}\widetilde{y}\in E(\widetilde{G})}
.
And since
{x\in\bigcup\widetilde{X}}
and
{y\in\bigcup\widetilde{Y}}
,
(1)
follows.
For
(2)
, if
{\widetilde{x}\widetilde{y}\in E(\widetilde{G})}
for some
{\widetilde{x}\in\widetilde{X}}
and
{\widetilde{y}\in\widetilde{Y}}
, then by definition there is an
{x^{\prime}\in X\cap\widetilde{x}}
and
{y^{\prime}\in Y\cap\widetilde{Y}}
, and it follows that
{xy\in E(G)}
.
Finally, if
{xy\in E(G)}
for some
{x\in X}
and
{y\in Y}
, then either
{x\not\sim_{\mathcal{K}(G)}y}
and hence
{\widetilde{x}\widetilde{y}\in E(\widetilde{G})}
by (
equation

∗

), or
{x\sim_{\mathcal{K}(G)}y}
and hence
\widetilde{x}\in\widetilde{X}\cap\widetilde{Y}
, proving
(3)
.
∎

For a maximal clique
{K\in\mathcal{K}(G)}
, we call the number of equivalence classes of
\sim_{\mathcal{K}(G)}
that intersect
K
the
incidence-diversity
of
K
.
Then, the

of

is the maximum incidence-diversity over all
{K\in\mathcal{K}(G)}
, which we denote by
\widetilde{\omega}(G)
.

For a vertex
{v\in V(G)}
, we call the number of maximal cliques

with

the
clique-incidence-degree
of
v
.
The
clique-incidence-degree
of

is the maximum clique-incidence-degree over all
{v\in V(G)}
, which we denote by
{\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}}(G)
.
Note that
{\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}}(G)
is equal to the maximum degree of the hypergraph
{\mathcal{K}(G)}
.

A key observation on the behaviour of
\sim_{\mathcal{K}(G)}
is that taking its quotient preserves a lot of the original structure of
G
from the perspective of maximal cliques.
We illustrate this behaviour with the following observations.

Lemma 3.2
.

For every graph
G
, the map
{\widetilde{\pi}\colon V(G)\to V(\widetilde{G})}
that maps every vertex
v
of
G
to its equivalence class
\widetilde{v}
with respect to
\sim_{\mathcal{K}(G)}
induces a bijection
\kappa
between
{\mathcal{K}(G)}
and
{\mathcal{K}(\widetilde{G})}
.

In particular,

(1)

\kappa
is an isomorphism between
\mathrm{I}(\mathcal{K}(G))
and
\mathrm{I}(\mathcal{K}(\widetilde{G}))
,

and, for every set
R
of representatives of the equivalence classes of
\sim_{\mathcal{K}(G)}
,

(2)

the restriction of
\widetilde{\pi}
to
R
is an isomorphism between the hypergraph
{\mathcal{K}(\widetilde{G})}
and the subhypergraph of
\mathcal{K}(G)
induced by
R
, and

(3)

the restriction of
\widetilde{\pi}
to
R
is an isomorphism between
\widetilde{G}
and
G[R]
.

Proof.

By (
equation

∗

),
{\widetilde{\pi}(K)=\widetilde{K}}

induces a maximal clique of
\widetilde{G}
for every
{K\in\mathcal{K}(G)}
.
On the other hand,
\widetilde{x}
induces a clique for each
{x\in V(G)}
.
So for each
{\widetilde{K}\in\mathcal{K}(\widetilde{G})}
, the union
\bigcup\widetilde{K}
is a clique of
G
.
Straightforwardly, the maximality of
\widetilde{G}[\widetilde{K}]
implies the maximality of
G[\bigcup\widetilde{K}]
.

Clearly, for
{K,K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}(G)}
we observe that
{K\cap K^{\prime}=\emptyset}
if and only if
{\widetilde{K}\cap\widetilde{K^{\prime}}=\emptyset}
, and hence
\mathrm{I}(\mathcal{K}(G))
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{I}(\mathcal{K}(\widetilde{G}))
.

Lastly, observe that if
R
is a set of representatives of the equivalence classes with respect to
\sim_{\mathcal{K}(G)}
, then the restriction of
\widetilde{\pi}
to
R
is a bijection, and hence, by the reasoning above, an isomporphism between the subhypergraph of
\mathcal{K}(G)
induced by
R
and
\mathcal{K}(\widetilde{G})
, as well as
\widetilde{G}
and
G[R]
.
∎

A straightforward consequence of this lemma is that the clique-incidence-diversity
\widetilde{\omega}(G)

Indeed, it follows that several graph parameters on

Corollary 3.3
.

Let

(1)

{\widetilde{\omega}(G)=\omega(\widetilde{G})}
.

(2)

{{\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}}(G)={\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}}(\widetilde{G})}
.

(3)

{\alpha(G)=\alpha(\widetilde{G})}

and
{{\alpha}^{\mathsf{loc}}(G)={\alpha}^{\mathsf{loc}}(\widetilde{G})}
.

(4)

{\theta(G)=\theta(\widetilde{G})}

and
{{\theta}^{\mathsf{loc}}(G)={\theta}^{\mathsf{loc}}(\widetilde{G})}
.

Proof.

The bijection
\kappa
from
lemma

3.2
maps a maximal clique
{K\in\mathcal{K}(G)}
to
{\widetilde{K}}
.
Since the latter has exactly as many vertices as

contains equivalence classes of
\sim_{\mathcal{K}(G)}
, it follows that
{\widetilde{\omega}(G)=\omega(\widetilde{G})}
.

Property
(2)
follows immediately from
lemma

3.2
(1)
together with the fact that the maximum degree of a hypergraph is equal to the clique number of its linegraph.

Since every independent set of
G
contains at most one vertex from each equivalent class of
\sim_{\mathcal{K}(G)}
, it follows from

3.2
(3)
that
{\alpha(G)=\alpha(\widetilde{G})}
.
Similarly, as we may assume
a clique-cover to consist only of maximal cliques, it follows from

(1)
that
{\theta(G)=\theta(\widetilde{G})}
.
From these observations, it immediately follows that the local variants of both of these parameters are also equal on
G
and
\widetilde{G}
.

For any graph parameter
\mu

\widetilde{\mu}
be the graph parameter defined by
{\widetilde{\mu}(G)\coloneqq\mu(\widetilde{G})}
.
In particular, let us turn our attention to the maximum degree of the clique-quotient graph, that is, the graph parameter
\widetilde{\Delta}
, and its interaction with both the clique-incidence-diversity and the clique-incidence-degree.

Lemma 3.4
.

For every graph
G
,

(1)

{\widetilde{\omega}(G)\leq\widetilde{\Delta}(G)}
, unless
\widetilde{G}
is edgeless (in which case
{\widetilde{\omega}(G)\leq 1}
and
{\widetilde{\Delta}(G)=0}
),

(2)

{{\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}}(G)\leq 3^{\lceil{\widetilde{\Delta}(G)/3}\rceil}}
, and

(3)

{\widetilde{\Delta}(G)\leq{\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}}(G)\cdot(\widetilde{\omega}(G)-1)}
.

Proof.

Clearly,
{\widetilde{\omega}(G)\leq\widetilde{\Delta}(G)+1}
.
Suppose
G
is a graph for which
{k\coloneqq\widetilde{\omega}(G)=\widetilde{\Delta}(G)+1}
.
In particular,
\widetilde{G}
contains a clique
\widetilde{K}
of size
k
.
Since
{\Delta(\widetilde{G})=k-1}
, each vertex in
\widetilde{K}
has degree
{k-1}
in
\widetilde{G}
, and hence
{\widetilde{G}[\widetilde{K}]}
is a component of
\widetilde{G}
.
But since no other maximal clique of
G
intersects
{K\coloneqq\bigcup\widetilde{K}}
, all vertices in
K
are equivalent, and hence
{k=1}
.
It follows that
{\Delta(\widetilde{G})=0}
, and hence
\widetilde{G}
is edgeless.

To show that
{\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}}(G)\leq 3^{\lceil{\widetilde{\Delta}(G)}/3\rceil}
, first recall that
{\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}}(G)={\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}}(\widetilde{G})
, see

.
Moreover, for every vertex
{\widetilde{v}\in V(\widetilde{G})}

5
Comparison with other graph parameters

In this section, we further analyse how the graph parameters of clique-incidence degree and clique-incidence diversity relate to other parameters to obtain further characterisations for clique-sparse graph classes.

Lemma 5.1
.

For every graph
G
holds

{\alpha}^{\mathsf{loc}}(G)\leq{\theta}^{\mathsf{loc}}(G)\leq{\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}}(G).

Proof.

To show
{{\alpha}^{\mathsf{loc}}(G)\leq{\theta}^{\mathsf{loc}}(G)}
, notice that each leaf of a maximal induced star needs to be covered by a distinct clique in any clique-cover.

For the inequality
{{\theta}^{\mathsf{loc}}(G)\leq{\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}}(G)}
,
notice that every vertex

is a dominating vertex of the graph induced by the closed neighbourhood of

.
Thus, for every clique
K
of

, we observe that
{G[V(K)\cup\{v\}]}
is a clique as well.
In particular, the set
{\{G[K\cap N_{G}[v]]\colon K\in\mathcal{K}(G)\textnormal{ with }v\in K\}}
is a
clique-cover of
{G[N_{G}[v]]}
.
∎

Note that all these inequalities are tight, as witnessed by trees.
On the other hand, for each of the inequalities, there exist graphs where the difference is arbitrarily large.
For the first inequality, consider a graph obtained from the complement of a graph, which is both triangle-free and has arbitrarily large chromatic number (cf. Erdős
[
6
]
), by adding a universal vertex.
For the difference between
{\theta}^{\mathsf{loc}}
and
{\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}}
, note that
\mathscr{A}^{\resizebox{393216}{}{$\LEFTCIRCLE\mkern-12.0mu\RIGHTCIRCLE$}}_{n}

and
{{\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}}(\mathscr{A}^{\resizebox{393216}{}{$\LEFTCIRCLE\mkern-12.0mu\RIGHTCIRCLE$}}_{n})=2^{n-1}}
.

For every graph

Forbidden induced subgraphs

We move on to proving the arguably most powerful theorem of this paper:

First, we make some basic observations on clique-incidence-diversity.

Let

Lemma 7.1

Proof.

Let

For the lower bound, simply observe that for every

\boldsymbol{\alpha}
-treewidth

In this section, we consider classes obtained by excluding some parametric graphs and how this affects the relation between rankwidth and
\alpha
-treewidth.
We start with defining the rankwidth of a graph, a concept first introduced by Oum and Seymour
[
16
]
.

A
rank-decomposition
of a graph
G
is a tuple
\left(T,\delta\right)
, where
T
is a cubic tree and
\delta
is a bijection between the leaves of
T
and the vertices of

.
For every edge

of

, note that the two subtrees of
{T-e}
induce a bipartition of the leaves of
T
.
Let
X_{e}
denote the image of one of those sets of leaves under
\delta
.
The
cutrank
of

is defined as the cutrank of
X_{e}
, i.e.
\mathsf{cutrk}_{G}(X_{e})
(see
section

7
for a definition of the cutrank of a set of vertices).
Note that this is well-defined as
\mathsf{cutrk}_{G}(X_{e})=\mathsf{cutrk}_{G}(V(G)\setminus X_{e})
.
The
width
of
\left(T,\delta\right)
is defined as the maximum cutrank of one of the edges of
T
and the
rankwidth
of
G
, denoted
{\mathsf{rw}}(G)
, is the minimum width over all rank decompositions of

.

We observe the following.

Observation 8.1
.

Let
G
be a graph. For every
{X\subseteq V(G)}
, we have
\mathsf{cutrk}_{G}(\bigcup\widetilde{X})=\mathsf{cutrk}_{\widetilde{G}}(\widetilde{X})
.

As a first result of this section, we observe that bounding both the rankwidth and the local independence number is sufficient to bound the

-treewidth of a graph.

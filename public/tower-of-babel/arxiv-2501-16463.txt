Higher-order chiral scalar from boundary reduction of 3d higher-spin gravity
and Junggi YOON

Abstract
We use a recently proposed covariant procedure to reduce the Chern-Simons action of three-dimensional higher-spin gravity to the boundary, resulting in a Lorentz covariant action for higher-order chiral scalars. After gauge-fixing, we obtain a higher-derivative action generalizing the s = 1 s=1 Floreanini-Jackiw and s = 2 s=2 Alekseev-Shatashvili actions to arbitrary spin s s . For simplicity, we treat the case of general spin at the linearized level, while the full non-linear asymptotic boundary conditions are presented in component form for the S ​ L ​ ( 3 , ℝ ) SL(3,\mathbb{R}) case. Finally, we extend the spin-3 linearized analysis to a background with non-trivial higher-spin charge and show that it has a richer structure of zero modes.

Introduction

Chiral degrees of freedom arise ubiquitously in theories of both fundamental interactions and emergent phenomena.
Instead of viewing them as troublesome special cases for which standard descriptions fail, one can take the alternative perspective that they are fundamental building blocks: They provide the simplest examples of field theories describing bosons with one-derivative free equations of motion similar to those of fermions.

We work in units where

We define the AdS length scale

2.1
Bulk action

Let us briefly review the CS formulations of spin-2 and HS fields in the bulk of
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}
.

2.1.1
Spin-2

Let us first consider the Einstein-Hilbert action with negative cosmological constant
\Lambda=-1/\ell^{2}
defined on a manifold
M
with timelike boundary
\partial M
.
Up to boundary terms, this action can be recast in terms of two copies of the CS action with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}(1,2)
:

\displaystyle S_{\rm EH}[e,\omega]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8\pi G}\int_{M}e_{a}\wedge\left[{\rm d}\omega^{a}+\frac{1}{2}\,\epsilon^{a}{}_{bc}\left(\omega^{b}\wedge\omega^{c}+\frac{1}{3\ell^{2}}e^{b}\wedge e^{c}\right)\right]

(2.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{\rm CS}[A]-S_{\rm CS}[\tilde{A}]\,,

where the local Lorentz
\mathfrak{so}(1,2)
indices
a,b,c,\dots
take value in
\{0,1,2\}
with metric
\eta={\rm diag}(-1,+1,+1)
, and the Levi-Civita symbol is defined with
\epsilon_{012}=+1
.
The CS action is given by

S_{\rm CS}[A]=\frac{k}{4\pi}\int_{M}{\rm Tr}\left(A\wedge{\rm d}A+\frac{2}{3}\,A\wedge A\wedge A\right),

(2.2)

where the CS level
k
is related to Newton’s constant by
k=\ell/4G
, and each of the
\mathfrak{so}(1,2)
CS gauge fields,
A=A^{a}\,J_{a}
and
\tilde{A}=\tilde{A}^{a}\,J_{a}
, are related to the spin connection and dreibein by

A^{a}=\omega^{a}+\frac{e^{a}}{\ell}\,,\qquad\tilde{A}^{a}=\omega^{a}-\frac{e^{a}}{\ell}\,.

(2.3)

For the generators of
\mathfrak{so}(1,2)
, we will use the convention,

{\rm Tr}(J_{a}\,J_{b})=\frac{1}{2}\,\eta_{ab}\,,\qquad[J_{a},J_{b}]=\epsilon_{ab}{}^{c}\,J_{c}\,.

(2.4)

Up to boundary terms, the variation of the CS action,
\delta S_{\text{CS}}\propto\int_{M}{\rm Tr}\left[\delta A\wedge F\right]
,
gives the flat curvature equation
F={\rm d}A+A\wedge A=0\,
.
The flatness condition for the two
\mathfrak{so}(1,2)
gauge fields are equivalent to the Cartan structure equations,

{\rm d}e^{a}+\epsilon^{a}{}_{bc}\,\omega^{b}\wedge e^{c}=0\,,\qquad{\rm d}\omega^{a}+\frac{1}{2}\,\epsilon^{a}{}_{bc}\left(\omega^{b}\wedge\omega^{c}+\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\,e^{b}\wedge e^{c}\right)=0\,.

(2.5)

Let us now consider fluctuations of
A^{a}
around a background solution
\underline{A}^{a}
.
In terms of the gauge field, we consider

A^{a}=\underline{A}^{a}+a^{a}\,,\qquad\tilde{A}^{a}=\underline{\tilde{A}}^{a}+\tilde{a}^{a}\,

(2.6)

so that the CS action can be expanded as

S_{\rm CS}[\underline{A}+a]=S_{\rm CS}[\underline{A}]+\frac{k}{4\pi}\int_{M}\frac{1}{2}\,a_{a}\wedge{\rm D}a^{a}+\frac{1}{6}\,\epsilon_{abc}\,a^{a}\wedge a^{b}\wedge a^{c}\,,

(2.7)

where
{\rm D}a^{a}
is the covariant derivative given by

{\rm D}a^{a}={\rm d}a^{a}+\epsilon^{a}{}_{bc}\,\underline{A}^{b}\wedge a^{c}\,.

(2.8)

The expansion of
\tilde{A}^{a}
follows similarly.
We will further focus on the quadratic action and neglect the third-order terms.
Since the covariant derivative
{\rm D}
is nilpotent, the quadratic action has the same algebraic structure as an Abelian CS action.

2.1.2
Higher-Spin

Now we turn to HS gauge fields, namely the Fronsdal fields — our conventions will mostly follow
[
51

In the frame-formulation for spin-
s
Fronsdal fields in an Einstein background, the action reads

\displaystyle S_{\rm Fronsdal}=\frac{1}{8\pi G}\int_{M}\bigg\{\varphi_{a_{1}\cdots a_{s-1}}\wedge\left[{\rm d}\omega^{a_{1}\cdots a_{s-1}}+(s-1)\epsilon^{bc(a_{1}}\underline{\omega}_{b}\wedge\omega^{a_{2}\cdots a_{s-1})}{}_{c}\right]

(2.9)

\displaystyle+\frac{s-1}{2}\epsilon_{bcd}\,\underline{e}^{b}\wedge\omega^{c}{}_{a_{2}\cdots a_{s-1}}\wedge\omega^{da_{2}\cdots a_{s-1}}+\frac{s-1}{2\ell^{2}}\epsilon_{bcd}\,\underline{e}^{b}\wedge\varphi^{c}{}_{a_{2}\cdots a_{s-1}}\wedge\varphi^{da_{2}\cdots a_{s-1}}\bigg\}

where the one-form fields

\varphi_{a_{1}\cdots a_{s-1}}

and
\omega_{a_{1}\cdots a_{s-1}}

are traceless in fiber indices, and

(a_{1}\cdots a_{s-1})
is the symmetrization with total weight one. The
\underline{e}^{a}
and
\underline{\omega}^{a}
satisfy the on-shell conditions (
2.5
).
Decomposing the two one-forms, namely the Fronsdal field
\varphi^{a_{1}\cdots a_{s-1}}
and the (first) connection field
w^{a_{1}\cdots a_{s-1}}
, as

a^{a_{1}\cdots a_{s-1}}=w^{a_{1}\cdots a_{s-1}}+\frac{1}{\ell}\varphi^{a_{1}\cdots a_{s-1}}\,,\qquad\tilde{a}^{a_{1}\cdots a_{s-1}}=w^{a_{1}\cdots a_{s-1}}-\frac{1}{\ell}\varphi^{a_{1}\cdots a_{s-1}}\,,

(2.10)

the Fronsdal action (
2.9
) can also be split, up to boundary terms, into two copies of Abelian CS actions:

S_{\rm Fronsdal}[\varphi,w]=S_{\rm CS}[a]-S_{\rm CS}[\tilde{a}]\,.

(2.11)

Here, the CS action is given by

S_{\rm CS}[a^{a_{1}\cdots a_{s-1}}]=\frac{k}{4\pi}\int_{M}\frac{1}{2}\,a_{a_{1}\cdots a_{s-1}}\wedge{\rm D}a^{a_{1}\cdots a_{s-1}}\,,

(2.12)

with
{\rm D}a^{a_{1}\cdots a_{s-1}}={\rm d}a^{a_{1}\cdots a_{s-1}}+(s-1)\,\epsilon^{b}{}_{c}{}^{(a_{1}}\,\underline{A}_{b}\,a^{a_{2}\cdots a_{s-1})c}\,
and we once again identify
k=\ell/4G
.
Further, by defining HS algebra generators
J_{a_{1}\cdots a_{s-1}}
with

[J_{a},J_{b_{1}\cdots b_{s-1}}]=(s-1)\,\epsilon_{a(b_{1}}{}^{c}\,J_{b_{2}\cdots b_{s-1})c}\,,\qquad{\rm Tr}(J_{a_{1}\cdots a_{s-1}}\,J^{b_{1}\cdots b_{s-1}})=\frac{1}{2}\,\delta^{\{b_{1}}_{\{a_{1}}\cdots\delta^{b_{s-1}\}}_{a_{s-1}\}}\,,

(2.13)

with
\{a_{1}\cdots a_{s-1}\}

S_{\rm CS}[a]=\frac{k}{4\pi}\int_{M}{\rm Tr}\left(a\wedge{\rm D}a\right),

(2.14)

with
a=a^{a_{1}\cdots a_{s-1}}\,J_{a_{1}\cdots a_{s-1}}
and
{\rm D}a={\rm d}a+\underline{A}\wedge a+a\wedge\underline{A}

This describes a massless spin-
s
field around an on-shell gravitational background, including linearized gravity

s=2

2.2
Equations of motion

So far, we have been neglecting boundary contributions.
However, for a well-defined variational principle, we need to supply the action with an appropriate boundary term.
We choose boundary terms with opposite signs for the two copies of the CS action:

\displaystyle S_{\rm L}[a]=\frac{k}{4\pi}\left(\int_{M}{\rm Tr}(a\wedge{\rm D}a)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial M}{\rm Tr}(a\wedge\star a)\right)\,,

\displaystyle S_{\rm R}[\tilde{a}]=\frac{k}{4\pi}\left(\int_{M}{\rm Tr}(\tilde{a}\wedge{\rm D}\tilde{a})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial M}{\rm Tr}(\tilde{a}\wedge\star\tilde{a})\right)\,,

(2.15)

so that the total action becomes

S[a,\tilde{a}]=S_{\rm L}[a]-S_{\rm R}[\tilde{a}]\,.

(2.16)

We will focus on
S_{\rm L}
for now, since
S_{\rm R}
can be treated in an analogous manner.

The variation of
S_{\rm L}
is

\frac{4\pi}{k}\,\delta S_{\rm L}=2\int_{M}{\rm Tr}(\delta a\wedge{\rm D}a)+\int_{\partial M}{\rm Tr}[\delta a\wedge(a-\star a)]\,,

(2.17)

which results in the boundary equation of motion,

(a-\star a)|_{\partial M}=0\,,

(2.18)

which can be equally interpreted as a Neumann boundary condition.

Note that we have introduced two reference metrics on
\partial M
: one through the covariant derivative
{\rm D}
and the other through the Hodge dual.
For the Hodge dual, we consider the boundary metric
given by the zweibein
\mathsf{e}^{\alpha}
,

{\rm d}s^{2}=\eta_{\alpha\beta}\,\mathsf{e}^{\alpha}\,\mathsf{e}^{\beta}\,,

(2.19)

with
\eta_{\oplus\ominus}=\frac{1}{2}
and
\eta^{\oplus\ominus}=2
.
The boundary Levi-Civita symbol
\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}
is given by
\epsilon_{\oplus\ominus}=+\frac{1}{2}
.
With these choices, the Hodge dual acts on the zweibein as

\star\,\mathsf{e}^{\alpha}=\epsilon_{\beta}{}^{\alpha}\,\mathsf{e}^{\beta}\,,\qquad\star\,\mathsf{e}^{\oplus/\ominus}=\pm\,\mathsf{e}^{\oplus/\ominus}\,.

(2.20)

Let us first analyze the equations of motion to identify the content of the theory.
The equation of motion in the bulk
M
,
{\rm D}a=0\,,
can be solved as
a={\rm D}\varphi
by nilpotency of
{\rm D}
.
Using the explicit Hodge dual map (
2.20
), the boundary equation of motion (
2.18
) can be expressed as

{\rm D}_{\ominus}\varphi\,|_{\partial M}=0\,,

(2.21)

where we use the notation
\Omega_{\alpha}=\mathsf{e}_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\,\Omega_{\mu}
for any one form
\Omega
.

For the flat zweibein
\mathsf{e}^{\oplus/\ominus}\propto{\rm d}x^{+/-}
with
x^{\pm}=x^{1}\pm x^{0}
, the condition (
2.21
) reduces to the chirality condition
{\rm D}_{-}\varphi\,|_{\partial M}=0
.
Therefore, we find chiral field as the on-shell content of the theory.
In the case of a genuine Abelian gauge field, this is the end of the story.
However, for Fronsdal fields, we should remember that
\varphi
is a tensor field and hence contains many chiral boundary degrees of freedom.
These degrees of freedom should be further reduced by an asymptotic AdS condition, which we shall consider later.

2.3
Boundary reduction

Setting aside the asymptotic AdS condition for now, we will now apply the reduction procedure introduced in
[
18
]
to the Fronsdal action (
2.15
) to obtain a manifestly Lorentz covariant action.
This covariant action provides the chirality condition (
2.21
) as its on-shell equation.

We begin with the decomposition of
the one-form
a
,

a=b+v\,\psi\,,

(2.22)

by employing a nowhere-null one-form
v
with
{\rm D}v=0
.
Note that
b
and
\psi
take value in the same tensor space as
a
,
and
\psi
plays the role of a Lagrange multiplier imposing the constraint

v\wedge{\rm D}b=0\,.

(2.23)

This is satisfied when the one-form
a
takes the form

a={\rm D}\varphi+v\,\rho\,,

(2.24)

for a tensor field
\rho
.
Note that the split of
a
into
{\rm D}\varphi
and
v\,\rho
is not unique as it involves a redundancy, parameterized by gauge symmetry subject to the relation

{\rm D}\delta\varphi+v\,\delta\rho=0\,.

(2.25)

The variation
\delta\rho
is completely determined by
\delta\varphi
which satisfies

v\wedge{\rm D}\delta\varphi=0\,.

(2.26)

By implementing (
2.24
) in the action, we find

\displaystyle S_{\rm L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k}{4\pi}\int_{\partial M}{\rm Tr}\left[-\frac{1}{2}\,({\rm D}\varphi+v\,\rho)\wedge\star({\rm D}\varphi+v\,\rho)-{\rm D}\varphi\wedge v\,\rho\right]

(2.27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{k}{8\pi}\int_{\partial M}{\rm Tr}\left[{\rm D}\varphi\wedge\star{\rm D}\varphi+2(\star v+v)\wedge\rho\,{\rm D}\varphi+\,\rho^{2}\,v\wedge\star v\,\right].

In components, the above action reads

S_{\rm L}=-\frac{k}{2\pi}\int_{\partial M}{\rm d}^{2}x\,\det\mathsf{e}\,{\rm Tr}\left[{\rm D}_{\oplus}\varphi\,{\rm D}_{\ominus}\varphi+2\,v_{\oplus}\rho\,{\rm D}_{\ominus}\varphi+\rho^{2}\,v_{\oplus}\,v_{\ominus}\right].

(2.28)

By integrating out
\rho
, we find the PST-type action,

S_{\rm L}=-\frac{k}{4\pi}\int_{\partial M}{\rm d}^{2}x\,\frac{\det\mathsf{e}}{v_{\ominus}}\,{\rm Tr}\left[\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\,v_{\alpha}\,{\rm D}_{\beta}\varphi\,{\rm D}_{\ominus}\varphi\right].

(2.29)

Note that for a flat metric, if we fix the gauge
v\propto{\rm d}x^{0}
, we find the Floreanini-Jackiw (FJ) type action

S_{\rm L}=-\frac{k}{2\pi}\int_{\partial M}{\rm d}^{2}x\,{\rm Tr}({\rm D}_{1}\varphi\,{\rm D}_{-}\varphi)\,.

(2.30)

For an arbitrary zweibein
\mathsf{e}^{\alpha}
, the equation of motion of the action
(
2.29
) is

\{\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\,v_{\alpha}\,{\rm D}_{\beta},{\rm D}_{\ominus}\}\varphi=0\,.

(2.31)

Since
[\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\,v_{\alpha}\,{\rm D}_{\beta},{\rm D}_{\ominus}]=0
, the general solution is given by

\varphi=\varphi_{\rm ph}+\varphi_{\rm g}\,,

(2.32)

where
\varphi_{\rm ph}
and
\varphi_{\rm g}
satisfy

{\rm D}_{\ominus}\varphi_{\rm ph}=0\,,\qquad\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\,v_{\alpha}\,{\rm D}_{\beta}\,\varphi_{\rm g}=0\,.

(2.33)

Note that
\varphi_{\rm g}
can be precisely removed by gauge symmetry (
2.26
).
We therefore only have
\varphi_{\rm ph}
as a dynamical field, whose equation of motion reduces to

{\rm D}_{\ominus}\varphi=0\,.

(2.34)

This is what we obtained directly from the CS action even before reduction to the boundary.

2.4
Asymptotic AdS Conditions

We will now consider the asymptotic AdS condition.

In
[
49
]
, it was shown that the metric of an asymptotically AdS spacetime (in the sense of
[
48
]
) is generally given by

ds^{2}=\ell^{2}\frac{({\rm d}x^{+}+z^{2}\,\tilde{\cal L}(x^{-})\,{\rm d}x^{-})({\rm d}x^{-}+z^{2}\,{\cal L}(x^{+})\,{\rm d}x^{+})+{\rm d}z^{2}}{z^{2}}\,

(2.35)

in lightcone Poincaré coordinates
x^{\pm}=x^{1}\pm x^{0}
and
z=x^{2}
.
The corresponding frame fields and the spin connection are

\underline{e}^{z}=\ell\frac{{\rm d}z}{z}\,,\qquad\underline{e}^{+}=\ell\frac{{\rm d}x^{+}+z^{2}\,\tilde{\cal L}(x^{-})\,{\rm d}x^{-}}{z}\,,\qquad\underline{e}^{-}=\ell\frac{{\rm d}x^{-}+z^{2}\,{\cal L}(x^{+})\,{\rm d}x^{+}}{z}

(2.36)

\underline{\omega}^{z}=0\,,\qquad\underline{\omega}^{+}=\frac{{\rm d}x^{+}-z^{2}\,\tilde{\cal L}(x^{-})\,{\rm d}x^{-}}{z}\,,\qquad\underline{\omega}^{-}=-\frac{{\rm d}x^{-}-z^{2}\,{\cal L}(x^{+})\,{\rm d}x^{+}}{z}\,.

(2.37)

Combining the above, we find
the background solution

\displaystyle\underline{A}^{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}z}{z}\,,\qquad\underline{A}^{+}=2\frac{{\rm d}x^{+}}{z}\,,\qquad\underline{A}^{-}=2\,z\,{\cal L}(x^{+})\,{\rm d}x^{+}\,,

\displaystyle\underline{\tilde{A}}^{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{{\rm d}z}{z}\,,\qquad\underline{\tilde{A}}^{+}=-2\,z\,\tilde{\cal L}(x^{-})\,{\rm d}x^{-}\,,\qquad\underline{\tilde{A}}^{-}=-2\,\frac{{\rm d}x^{-}}{z}\,.

(2.38)

We take the zweibein
\mathsf{e}^{\alpha}
associated with the boundary Hodge dual as the pullback of the 3d background metric (
2.35
) to the boundary.
As we shall consider the case where the boundary is located at a constant
z
surface with
z\to 0
, we take this zweibein to be flat.

For further analysis, it is useful to use the basis,

L_{0}=-J_{2}\,,\qquad L_{\pm 1}=\pm 2\,J_{\pm}=J_{0}\pm J_{1}\,,

(2.39)

such that

[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)\,L_{m+n}\,.

(2.40)

Then, we can expand the spin-2 field as

A=A^{\scriptscriptstyle(+1)}\,L_{+1}+A^{\scriptscriptstyle(0)}\,L_{0}+A^{\scriptscriptstyle(-1)}\,L_{-1}\,,

(2.41)

with

3
Edge mode action of HS gravity around BTZ blackhole

Let us consider HS gravity formulated as
\mathfrak{sl}(N,\mathbb{R})\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(N,\mathbb{R})
CS theory with boundary terms.
The action is given by

S_{\rm HSG}=S_{\rm L}[{\cal A}]-S_{\rm R}[\tilde{\cal A}]\,,

(3.1)

with the left and right parts given by

\displaystyle S_{\rm L}[{\cal A}]=\int_{M}{\rm Tr}\left({\cal A}\wedge{\rm d}{\cal A}+\frac{2}{3}\,{\cal A}\wedge{\cal A}\wedge{\cal A}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial M}{\rm Tr}\left({\cal A}\wedge\star{\cal A}\right),

(3.2a)

\displaystyle S_{\rm R}[\tilde{\cal A}]=\int_{M}{\rm Tr}\left(\tilde{\cal A}\wedge{\rm d}\tilde{\cal A}+\frac{2}{3}\,\tilde{\cal A}\wedge\tilde{\cal A}\wedge\tilde{\cal A}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial M}{\rm Tr}\left(\tilde{\cal A}\wedge\star\tilde{\cal A}\right)

(3.2b)

respectively.
The gauge field
{\cal A}
contains the gravity part
A^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}
as well as
the Fronsdal fields
a^{\scriptscriptstyle(s,n)}
of spin
s=3,\ldots,N
:

{\cal A}=\sum_{n=-1}^{1}\,A^{\scriptscriptstyle(n)}\,L_{n}+\sum_{s=3}^{N}\sum_{n=-(s-1)}^{s-1}\,a^{\scriptscriptstyle(s,n)}\,W^{\scriptscriptstyle(s)}_{n}\,.

(3.3)

The right gauge field
\tilde{\cal A}
has an analogous expansion.
Together, this action describes massless fields of spin
s=2,\dots,N
with HS self-interactions due to the non-trivial Lie brackets
[W^{\scriptscriptstyle(s)}_{n},W^{\scriptscriptstyle(s^{\prime})}_{m}]
.

Note here that the boundary term still makes use of the Hodge dual defined with respect to the flat zweibein
\mathsf{e}^{\alpha}
, while the theory now describes a dynamical metric.
For physical consistency, we ought to limit the boundary value of
{\cal A}
and
\tilde{\cal A}
in a way that the 3d metric has flat induced metric on the 2d boundary.

3.1
Boundary Terms

Let us make a few comments about the boundary term,
-\frac{1}{2}\,{\rm Tr}({\cal A}\wedge\star{\cal A})
, which preserves the boundary Lorentz symmetry
4
4

4

See
e.g.

[
60
]
for a more focused discussion about boundary terms.
.
When we rewrite the CS action in its Hamiltonian (first-order) form,

S_{\rm Ham}=2\int_{M}{\rm d}^{3}x\,{\rm Tr}\left[{\cal A}_{2}\,\partial_{0}\,{\cal A}_{1}+{\cal A}_{0}\,{\cal F}_{12}\right],

(3.4)

we obtain a boundary term from

S_{\rm CS}=S_{\rm Ham}+\int_{\partial M}{\rm d}^{2}x\,{\rm Tr}({\cal A}_{0}\,{\cal A}_{1})\,,

(3.5)

This boundary term
{\rm Tr}({\cal A}_{0}\,{\cal A}_{1})
combines with the other boundary term
-\frac{1}{2}\,{\rm Tr}({\cal A}\wedge\star{\cal A})=\frac{1}{2}\,{\rm Tr}[-({\cal A}_{0})^{2}+({\cal A}_{1})^{2}]
to give

S_{\rm bd}=\int_{\partial M}{\rm d}^{2}x\,{\rm Tr}\left[{\cal A}_{0}\,{\cal A}_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\left(-({\cal A}_{0})^{2}+({\cal A}_{1})^{2}\right)\right],

(3.6)

which is the total boundary term in Hamiltonian form:
S_{\rm L}=S_{\rm Ham}+S_{\rm bd}
.
In the Hamiltonian treatment, the component
{\cal A}_{0}
is a non-dynamical field that can be decomposed as
{\cal A}_{0}=\Lambda+\rho
.
To avoid redundancies within the decomposition, we require
\Lambda
to vanish on the boundary, while the bulk profile of
\rho
be specifically fixed in terms of its boundary value.
Then the variation with respect to
{\cal A}_{0}
gives

\delta S_{\rm L}=2\int_{M}{\rm d}^{3}x\,{\rm Tr}\left(\delta(\Lambda+\rho)\,{\cal F}_{12}\right)+\int_{\partial M}{\rm d}^{2}x\,{\rm Tr}\left[\delta\rho\left(-\rho+{\cal A}_{1}\right)\right].

(3.7)

This has two parts: in the bulk, the
\Lambda
variation sets the constraint
{\cal F}_{12}=0
; on the boundary, the
\rho
variation sets its value as
\rho={\cal A}_{1}
.
Note here that the variation
\delta\rho
in the bulk is not an arbitrary bulk field as its bulk profile is simply a fixed function.
In the end, we find that the bulk variation
\delta\Lambda
and the boundary variation
\delta\rho
are independent.
If we perform first the boundary variation
\delta\rho
, the boundary term will be reduced to

S_{\rm bd}=\int{\rm d}^{2}\,{\rm Tr}\left[({\cal A}_{1})^{2}\right],

(3.8)

i.e.
the typical (additional) boundary term of the Hamiltonian treatment of CS theory.
On the other hand, performing the bulk variation first corresponds to the reduction procedure
[
18
]
that we follow in this paper.
The boundary variation can be performed eventually after such a boundary reduction, and this step is nothing but
the conversion of the covariant form (
2.28
) of the action into the FJ form (
2.30
).

3.2
Equations of motion

The non-linear equation of motion is also just the flatness condition

{\cal A}=g_{\varphi}^{-1}\,\underline{{\cal A}}\,g_{\varphi}+g_{\varphi}^{-1}\,{\rm d}g_{\varphi}\,.

\displaystyle\underline{{\cal A}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{{\rm d}z}{z}\,L_{0}+\left(\frac{1}{z}\,L_{+1}-z\,{\cal L}\,L_{-1}\right){\rm d}x^{+}

\displaystyle e^{-\rho\,L_{0}}\,e^{-(L_{+1}-{\cal L}\,L_{-1})\,x^{+}}{\rm d}\left[e^{(L_{+1}-{\cal L}\,L_{-1})\,x^{+}}e^{\rho\,L_{0}}\right],

G=\underline{g}\,e^{\rho\,L_{0}}\,g_{\varphi}=\underline{g}\,g_{\phi}\,e^{\rho\,L_{0}}\,,

In contrast,

From now on, we use

3.3
Boundary reduction

Let us proceed with the covariant reduction of the bulk action by using once again the decomposition,

A=B+v\,\psi\,.

4.2
Mode expansions

Let us now consider the Euclidean case, where
(x^{1}=\theta,x^{0}=-i\,t_{\rm E})

\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle(s)}(\theta+2\pi,t_{\rm E})=\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle(s)}(\theta,t_{\rm E})=\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle(s)}(\theta+2\pi\,{\rm Re}(\tau),t_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle E}+2\pi\,{\rm Im}(\tau))\,.

(4.7)

We may therefore consider the mode expansion

\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle(s)}(\theta,t_{\rm E})=\sum_{(m,n)\neq\,{\rm zero\,modes}}\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle(s)}_{m,n}\,e^{i\left[m\left(\theta-\frac{{\rm Re}(\tau)}{{\rm Im}(\tau)}\,t_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle E}\right)+\frac{n}{{\rm Im}(\tau)}\,t_{\rm\scriptscriptstyle E}\right]}

(\phi_{m,n}^{\scriptscriptstyle(s)})^{*}=\phi_{-m,-n}^{\scriptscriptstyle(s)}

{\cal L}

{\cal W}

\phi_{m,n}^{\scriptscriptstyle(3)}\end{pmatrix}^{\dagger}K_{m,n}\,\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{m,n}^{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\\

Appendix A
Factorization of the operator

In this section, we provide
the proof for the factorization
formula (
2.53
).
For that, we perform a
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
transformation to change
the basis
W_{n}^{\scriptscriptstyle(s)}
:
the gauge connection
a

and the gauge function
\phi

can be expanded as

a=\sum_{n=1-s}^{s-1}a^{\scriptscriptstyle(s,n)}\,W^{(s)}_{n}\,,\qquad\phi=\sum_{n=1-s}^{s-1}\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle(s,n)}\,W^{(s)}_{n}\,,

(A.1)

and they are related by

a={\rm D}\phi

with the differential
{\rm D}={\rm d}+\underline{A}
.
A
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
transformation
h

leaves the space spanned by
W_{n}^{(s)}
’s invariant:

\tilde{\phi}=h^{-1}\,\phi\,h\,,\qquad\tilde{a}=h^{-1}\,a\,h=\tilde{\rm D}\tilde{\phi}\,,

(A.2)

where the transformed differential
\tilde{\rm D}={\rm d}+\underline{\tilde{A}}
is given by
\underline{\tilde{A}}=h^{-1}{\rm d}h+h^{-1}\,\underline{A}\,h
.
If we consider a transformation
of the form,

h=e^{\eta\,L_{-1}}\,,

(A.3)

the generator
W^{(s)}_{s-1}
is
invariant, in particular,
the corresponding component of gauge functions are the same:

\phi^{\scriptscriptstyle(s,s-1)}=\tilde{\phi}^{\scriptscriptstyle(s,s-1)}\,.

(A.4)

Moreover, the asymptotic AdS condition
is also invariant:

a_{1}^{\scriptscriptstyle(s,n)}=0\quad[2-s\leq n\leq s-1]\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\tilde{a}_{1}^{\scriptscriptstyle(s,n)}=0\quad[1-s\leq n\leq s-2]\,,

(A.5)

and as a result, the remaining
components are the same:

a_{1}^{\scriptscriptstyle(s,1-s)}=\tilde{a}_{1}^{\scriptscriptstyle(s,1-s)}\,.

(A.6)

In our case, the background gauge connection is

\underline{A}=(L_{1}-{\cal L}\,L_{-1})\,{\rm d}x^{+}
, and the transformed background gauge connection becomes

\underline{\tilde{A}}=\left[L_{1}+2\,\eta\,L_{0}+(\eta^{\prime}+\eta^{2}-{\cal L})\,L_{-1}\right]{\rm d}x^{+}\,.

(A.7)

By imposing the condition,

\eta^{\prime}+\eta^{2}={\cal L}\,,

(A.8)

the non-vanishing components
of
\underline{\tilde{A}}

are adjacent,
and the asymptotic AdS condition
becomes

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{1}-2\,n\,\eta\right)\tilde{\phi}^{\scriptscriptstyle(s,n)}+(s-n)\,\tilde{\phi}^{\scriptscriptstyle(s,n-1)}=0\qquad[2-s\leq n\leq s-1]\,,

(A.9)

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{1}-2\,(1-s)\,\eta\right]\tilde{\phi}^{\scriptscriptstyle(s,1-s)}=\tilde{a}_{1}^{\scriptscriptstyle(s,1-s)}\,.

(A.10)

The first equation allows
to express

\tilde{\phi}^{\scriptscriptstyle(s,n)}
in term of
\tilde{\phi}^{\scriptscriptstyle(s,n+1)}

\tilde{\phi}^{\scriptscriptstyle(s,n)}=\frac{(\partial_{1}-2\,(n+1)\,\eta)\,\tilde{\phi}^{\scriptscriptstyle(s,n+1)}}{s-1-n}\qquad[1-s\leq n\leq s-2]\,,

(A.11)

and, the gauge connection
\tilde{a}_{1}^{\scriptscriptstyle(s,1-s)}
can be found to be

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{1}^{\scriptscriptstyle(s,s-1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over(2s-2)!}{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{2s-1}}\big[\partial_{1}-2(-s+j)\,\eta\big]\,\tilde{\phi}^{\scriptscriptstyle(s,s-1)}

(A.12)

\displaystyle{1\over(2s-2)!}\,\partial_{1}\prod_{n=1}^{s-1}(\partial_{1}^{2}-4\,n^{2}\,\eta^{2})\,\tilde{\phi}^{\scriptscriptstyle(s,s-1)}\,.

For a constant

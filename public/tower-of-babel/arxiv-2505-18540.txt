Subthreshold poles in electron‐positron annihilation. D D ¯ \bar{\mathrm{D}} final states.
Peter Lichard

Abstract
We show that the ψ ⁡ ( 2 ​ S ) \psi(2\mathrm{S}) subthreshold pole influences the cross section of the electron-positron annihilation into the D + ​ D − \mathrm{D}^{+}\mathrm{D}^{-} and D 0 ​ D ¯ 0 \mathrm{D}^{0}\bar{\mathrm{D}}^{0} final states. We perform a fit to the merged BES [ 2 ] and BESIII [ 3 ] data, providing the cross section for e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} annihilation into those final states. The statistical significance of the ψ ⁡ ( 2 ​ S ) \psi(2\mathrm{S}) as a subthreshold pole is 8.2 σ \sigma . A fit assuming it and seven resonances excels with the fit quality characterized by χ 2 / NDF = 240.4 / 284 \chi^{2}/\mathrm{NDF}=240.4/284 , which means a p p -value of 97%.



I
Introduction

This paper follows our effort to understand the role of the subthreshold
poles (SP in what follows) in the amplitude of the
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation to
hadrons that commenced in Ref.
[
4
]
by identifying SPs possibly
representing the
\mathrm{K}^{+}{\mathrm{K}}^{-}
and
\mathrm{K}^{0}\bar{\mathrm{K}}^{0}
bound states and continued in Ref.

[
5
]
, where the SP indicated the G(3900) as an excited state of
the
\mathrm{D}_{s}^{+}{\mathrm{D}}_{s}^{-}
molecule. As in our previous papers, the essential tool we use
is the vector-meson dominance (VMD) hypothesis
[
6
]
.

VMD is a useful phenomenological concept
based on the assumption that the interaction of the hadronic system with the
electromagnetic field is mediated by truly neutral vector meson resonances
of negative C-parity. It is frequently used to interpret the
e^{+}e^{-}

annihilation data into hadrons. Experimentalists use resonances with masses
above the considered reaction’s threshold to fit the salient features of
excitation curves (peaks, bumps, dips, steep slopes). VMD has also been used
in modeling dilepton production in hadronic
[
7
]
and nuclear

[
8
]
collisions.

Subthreshold poles are very well-known in hadronic reactions.
A typical example is the nucleon pole in the pion-nucleon scattering. Even if
it is not accessible in an experiment, it has a strong influence on the
cross section.
Owing to that, its existence can be proven by analyzing the forward scattering
amplitude obtained from phase-shift analyses, and its residue proportional
to the
\pi
NN coupling constant can be determined with reasonable accuracy

[
9
]
. Guided by this analogy, we plan to continue investigating the
role of subthreshold poles in the
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation into hadrons, which we
commenced in Ref.
[
4
]
.

To our knowledge, nobody has systematically investigated the analytic
properties of the amplitudes of the
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation to various hadronic
systems. Here, we will assume that they are similar to those of hadronic
amplitudes, discussed,
e.g.
, in
[
10
]
.

From general principles of causality, locality of interactions, and unitarity,
the amplitude is a real analytic function in the complex
s
-plane with the cut
along the positive real axis running from the process threshold to infinity
(called the physical cut).
The stable states, i.e., those which do not decay into the considered final
state, are represented by poles lying in the real axis below the threshold.
Resonances are represented by the pairs of complex conjugate poles situated
on the higher Riemann sheets, accessible through the physical cut.
The contribution of resonance to the amplitude on the upper branch of the
physical cut is usually parametrized by some form of the Breit-Wigner
formula. They run from the simplest one with constant mass and width to
the most sophisticated with running (
s
-dependent) mass and variable width,
which are related through the once-
[
11
]
or twice-
[
12
]

subtracted dispersion relations.

In this paper, we first show that an SP influences the electron-positron
annihilation cross section into the
\mathrm{D}\bar{\mathrm{D}}
final states with statistical
significance of 7.9
\sigma
. Its mass came out as
3691\pm 56
MeV. Taking
into account that the only charmonium with the mass lying in this
interval is the
\psi(2\mathrm{S})
, we fix the SP mass at 3686.097 MeV
[
13
]

and repeat the fit. Comparison with the fit without the SP yields the

\psi(2\mathrm{S})
pole significance of 8.2
\sigma
. Local significances show that
the
\psi(2\mathrm{S})
pole is present equally in both
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\mathrm{D}^{+}\mathrm{D}^{-}
and
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\mathrm{D}^{0}\bar{\mathrm{D}}^{0}

processes.

II
Model

For the description of the electron-positron annihilation into a
\mathrm{D}\bar{\mathrm{D}}
pair
(D represents D
+
or D
0
), we use a Vector Meson Dominance (VMD) model
based on the Feynman diagram depicted in Fig.
1
and the

Figure 1:
Feynman diagram defining our model

interaction Lagrangian

{\cal L}_{V\!\phi}(x)=\mathrm{i}{g_{V\!\phi}}V_{\mu}(x)\left\{[\partial^{\mu}\phi^{\dagger}(x)]\phi(x)-\phi^{\dagger}(x)\partial^{\mu}\phi(x)\right\},

(1)

where
V^{\mu}(x)
denotes the (hermitian) vector field and
\phi(x)
the
pseudoscalar D field. The
\gamma V
junction is parametrized as

eM_{V}^{2}/g_{V}
in analogy with the
\gamma\rho^{0}
junction

eM^{2}_{\rho^{0}}/g_{\rho}
. The formula for the cross section of the
e^{+}e^{-}

annihilation into a
\mathrm{D}\bar{\mathrm{D}}
pair based on the VMD model
with
n
resonances is derived in the Appendix. It sounds

\sigma(s)=\frac{\pi\alpha^{2}}{3s}\left(1-\frac{4m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}{s}\right)^{\!\!{3/2}}\!\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\sqrt{Q_{k}}\,e^{\mathrm{i}\delta_{k}}}{s-M_{k}^{2}+\mathrm{i}M_{k}\Gamma_{k}}\right|^{2}\!.

(2)

In this formula,
M_{k}
and
\Gamma_{k}
are the mass and width of the
k
th
resonance, respectively,
Q_{k}=R_{k}^{2}
, where
R_{k}=M_{k}^{2}g_{V_{k}\phi}/g_{V_{k}}
,
and
\delta_{k}
is an additional phase shift. We will treat
M_{k}
s,

\Gamma_{k}
s,
Q_{k}
s, and
\delta_{k}
s as free parameters (except
\delta_{1}
,
which is kept at 0). We will determine their mean values and dispersions by
fitting the formula (
2
) to experimental cross sections using the
standard
\chi^{2}
criterion
[
14
]
.

III
The explored data

The experimentalists working at the spectrometer BESIII situated at
the electron-positron collider BEPCII based at the IHEP laboratory
in Beijing, China, have recently published
[
3
]
precise data
on
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation to the
\mathrm{D}\bar{\mathrm{D}}
final states. In the Supplemental
Material
[
15
]
, they provide the Born cross sections of the

e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\mathrm{D}^{0}\bar{\mathrm{D}}^{0}
(Table 1) and
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\mathrm{D}^{+}\mathrm{D}^{-}
(Table 2) processes at 150
cms energies between 3.80 and 4.95 GeV. Additionally, correction factors
are tabulated, allowing for the conversion of the Born cross sections
to the dressed ones.
The dressed cross sections were a subject of the fit presented in the
Supplemental Material
[
15
]
with results
\chi^{2}/\mathrm{NDF}=346/276
, which
implies the
p
-value of 0.27%. We will follow the
experimentalists and use the dressed cross sections also in this work.

In the previous version of this study
[
16
]
, we used only the
BESIII data
[
3
]
and got evidence of the presence of the

\psi(2\mathrm{S})
subthreshold pole in both
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\mathrm{D}^{0}\bar{\mathrm{D}}^{0}
and
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\mathrm{D}^{+}\mathrm{D}^{-}
processes.
The anonymous referee has warned us that the data do not cover an important
region between the threshold and 3.8 GeV, which contains the
\psi(3770)

resonance. When searching for relevant data, we found guidance in Ref.

[
17
]
, where a careful analysis of this region was done.
We have chosen data from the BES Collaboration
[
2
]
, which were
taken at the same IHEP laboratory, but at the BEPC collider with a
conventional magnetic detector BES-2. Their Table 2 presents the cross
section of both processes of interest at 14 energy points. We merged them
with the BESIII data and will determine the free parameters of our model
by fitting the formula (
2
) to the complete set.

IV
A subthreshold pole appearing

The BESIII Collaboration
[
3
]
assumed in their fit that all
parameters of the
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\mathrm{D}^{+}\mathrm{D}^{-}
and
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\mathrm{D}^{0}\bar{\mathrm{D}}^{0}
processes are the same.
Here, we will consider the differences between the two processes.
Their origins are physical (different masses of
\mathrm{D}^{+}
and

\mathrm{D}^{0}
and possibly different couplings to intermediate vector
mesons), as well as experimental (different acceptances rooted in different
methods of identifying particles in final states).
We will split each
R_{k}
and
\delta_{k}
parameter into two. Those pertinent
to the
\mathrm{D}^{+}\mathrm{D}^{-}
final states will be marked by subscript
a
, those
to the
\mathrm{D}^{0}\bar{\mathrm{D}}^{0}
ones by
b
. We will assume that the resonances’ parameters

M_{k}
s and
\Gamma_{k}
are the same for both processes.

To help the minimization code cope with many parameters, we started the
fitting with two resonances. We gradually added further, using the already
found parameters as the starting ones for the next run.

In this way, we reached the six-resonance fit with
\chi^{2}/\mathrm{NDF}=370.6/294
and a

p
-value of 0.16%. To improve the fit, we added a new resonance with unconstrained
parameters. The quality of the fit increased to
\chi^{2}/\mathrm{NDF}=289.2/288
,

p
-value = 47%. The statistical significance of the new resonance is 7.9
\sigma
,
estimated by utilizing the changes in likelihood values
\delta(-2\ln L)=81.4
and in the number of degrees of freedom
\delta(\mathrm{NDF})=6
.

But the parameters of the new “resonance” are surprising:
M=(3691\pm 56)
MeV
and
\Gamma=(0\pm 160)
MeV. They describe not a usual Breit-Wigner resonance,
but an SP lying on the real axis of the complex
s
-plane
below the reaction threshold of 3729.68(10) MeV (based on the D
0
mass
from
[
13
]
).

When trying to identify the SP we have just discovered with an existing
state, we should not be fooled by its zero width. What looks like a stable
state from the point of view of the
\mathrm{D}\bar{\mathrm{D}}
final states may decay in other
processes. There are two obvious candidates with the
J^{PC}=1^{--}
quantum
numbers and the quark composition ensuring the strong coupling to the

D\bar{D}
system:
J/\psi
(1S) with mass about 3097 MeV and
\psi(2\mathrm{S})
with mass
3686.097(11) MeV
[
13
]
. The mass of the SP we have found is by
(5
\pm
57) MeV higher than that of the
\psi(2\mathrm{S})
and compatible with it. This
fact leads us to choose the
\psi(2\mathrm{S})
for further processing.

VI
Conclusions

The inclusion of an SP significantly improves the quality of a
six-resonance fit to the combined BES
[
2
]
and BESIII

[
3
]
data, expressed by the
p
-value rising from
0.16% to 47% and the SP statistical significance of 7.9
\sigma
.
The obtained SP position is very close to the
\psi(2\mathrm{S})
resonance mass, which has
led us to hypothesize that the
\psi(2\mathrm{S})
state, a resonance in other processes,
behaves in the
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\mathrm{D}\bar{\mathrm{D}}
process like a subthreshold pole with a
statistical significance of 8.2
\sigma
.

Finally, we have supplemented the
\psi(2\mathrm{S})
SP with seven resonances and
performed a fit. It resulted into
\chi^{2}/\mathrm{NDF}=240.4/284
and
p
-value = 97%.

Our study suggests that it may be impossible to get a good fit to the

e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\mathrm{D}\bar{\mathrm{D}}
process without considering a subthreshold pole. More
generally, we follow up with our other studies
[
4
,
5
]

showing the importance of the often-overlooked role of subthreshold poles,
which can significantly affect some processes.

Appendix A
Derivation of formula (
2
)

The Lagrangian (
1
) implies for the V
\mathrm{D}\bar{\mathrm{D}}
vertex in Fig.

1
the expression
g_{V\phi}(p_{1}-p_{2})^{\rho}
. Together with
the
eM_{V}^{2}/{g_{V}}
for the
\gamma V
junction and standard Feynman rules,
it leads to the following amplitude of the
e^{+}e^{-}\rightarrow\mathrm{D}\bar{\mathrm{D}}
process
(
p=p_{a}+p_{b}
,
s=p^{2}
, electron mass neglected):

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}{\cal M}_{fi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}e\bar{v}(p_{a},s_{a})\gamma_{\mu}u(p_{b},s_{b})\,\frac{-\mathrm{i}g^{\mu\nu}}{s}\,\frac{eM_{V}^{2}}{g_{V}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\frac{-g_{\nu\rho}+\frac{p_{\nu}p_{\rho}}{M_{V}^{2}}}{s-M_{V}^{2}+\mathrm{i}M_{V}\Gamma_{V}}\,g_{V\phi}(p_{1}-p_{2})^{\rho}\ .

After some editing and introducing the notation
R_{V}=M_{V}^{2}g_{V\phi}/g_{V}
, the
amplitude squared takes the form

\displaystyle|{\cal M}_{fi}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{4}}{s^{2}}\left|\frac{R_{V}}{s-M_{V}^{2}+\mathrm{i}M_{V}\Gamma_{V}}\right|^{2}\!(p_{1}-p_{2})^{\mu}(p_{1}-p_{2})^{\nu}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle{\bar{v}}(p_{a},s_{a})\gamma_{\mu}u(p_{b},s_{b}){\bar{u}}(p_{b},s_{b})\gamma_{\nu}v(p_{a},s_{a})\ .

Averaging over initial spin states leads to [
t=(p_{1}-p_{a})^{2}
]

\displaystyle\overline{|{\cal M}_{fi}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2e^{4}}{s^{2}}\left|\frac{R_{V}}{s-M_{V}^{2}+\mathrm{i}M_{V}\Gamma_{V}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle(-st-t^{2}+2m_{\rm D}^{2}t-m_{\rm D}^{4})\ .

Inserting this into the differential cross-section formula

\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{1}{64\pi s}\frac{1}{|\vec{p}_{a}|^{2}}\overline{|{\cal M}_{fi}|^{2}}

and integrating over
t
from
t_{1}=m_{\rm D}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(1+v_{\rm D})

to
t_{2}=m_{\rm D}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(1-v_{\rm D})
, where
v_{\rm D}
is the
D meson speed, and putting
R_{V}=\sqrt{Q_{V}}
, we finally get

\sigma(s)=\frac{\pi\alpha^{2}}{3s}\left(1-\frac{4m_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}}{s}\right)^{\!\!{3/2}}\!\left|\frac{\sqrt{Q_{V}}}{s-M_{V}^{2}+\mathrm{i}M_{V}\Gamma_{V}}\right|^{2}\!.

(3)

If the intermediate vector meson is a resonance, we can obtain in the same
way the relations below.

\Gamma_{V\rightarrow\mathrm{D}\bar{\mathrm{D}}}=\frac{M_{V}g_{V\phi}^{2}}{48\pi}\left(1-\frac{4m_{\rm D}^{2}}{M_{V}^{2}}\right)^{3/2}

(4)

and

\Gamma_{V\rightarrow e^{+}e^{-}}=\frac{4\pi\alpha^{2}}{3}\frac{M_{V}}{g_{V}^{2}}\ .

(5)

Combining Eqs. (
3
), (
4
), and (
5
) we can directly relate
the parameter
Q_{V}
to observable quantities

Q_{V}=\frac{36M_{V}^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\ \frac{\Gamma_{V\rightarrow\mathrm{D}\bar{\mathrm{D}}}\,\Gamma_{V\rightarrow e^{+}e^{-}}}{\left(1-\frac{4m_{\rm D}^{2}}{M_{V}^{2}}\right)^{3/2}}\ .

(6)

The relation (
6
) is valid only for resonances, whereas
(
3
) is valid also for SP (with
\Gamma_{V}=0
).

The relation (
6
) is one of the reasons why we prefer
Q_{V}
over
R_{V}
.
Another is that the
Q_{V}
and its dispersion can provide information about the
local significance of the corresponding resonance or SP.

Generalizing Eq. (
3
) to the case of

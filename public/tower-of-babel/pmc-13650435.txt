Direct-access, balloon-expandable mitral valve replacement under fibrillatory arrest in extensive mitral annular calcification
Authors: John Wheeler, Gilbert Tang, Dimosthenis Pandis, Ahmed El-Eshmawi
Published: 2026

Modified SAPIEN 3 (Edwards Lifesciences) for hybrid valve-in-MAC implantation.

Central Message

Innovative surgical techniques continue to emerge in addressing the formidable challenges associated with mitral annular calcification.

Mitral annular calcification (MAC) remains a substantial challenge in mitral valve surgery. Given the high morbidity and mortality associated with this lesion, a myriad of approaches have been developed to address extensive MAC.1 Some surgeons favor “resection” techniques, including en bloc decalcification with annular reconstruction; however, this is met with considerable perioperative risk.2 We have previously reported on our approach to “respect” MAC with limited, if any, debridement to allow prothesis implantation. Our algorithm is dependent upon the distribution of annular calcification on preoperative computed tomography imaging. Calcification limited to the posterior annulus is approached with conventional surgical techniques.3 In more extensive, circumferential calcification, we favor a hybrid approach via direct-access, balloon-expandable device implantation, as demonstrated in this case video (Video 1). Informed written consent was obtained for this publication. No institutional review board approval is required at our institution for case reports.

We present a 66-year-old patient who was referred to us with New York Heart Associate class III symptoms. His history was notable for mantle-field radiation resulting in severe aortic stenosis and coronary artery disease status-post aortocoronary bypass and mechanical aortic valve replacement 20 years prior. Preoperative echocardiogram revealed severe mixed mitral stenosis and regurgitation with extensive MAC (Figure 1). Computed tomography angiography demonstrated severe circumferential MAC and a porcelain aorta (Figure 2). Given the large annular dimension and risk for left ventricular outflow tract obstruction, transcatheter intervention was not feasible, so hybrid implantation of a balloon-expandable SAPIEN 3 valve (Edwards Lifesciences) in the mitral position was performed. The surgery involved reoperative sternotomy, femoral cannulation, and transeptal mitral valve exposure under fibrillatory arrest. The anterior leaflet and its subvalvular apparatus were resected to reduce the risk of left ventricular outflow obstruction. The commissures were closed with pledgeted sutures to minimize paravalvular leak. The SAPIEN valve was wrapped in Teflon felt. The valve was loaded onto the delivery system and deployed while ensuring the frame was not protruding into the left ventricular outflow tract. Few valve sutures were placed at the anterolateral and posteromedial commissures to secure the cuff to the annulus to prevent migration. Postintervention echocardiogram demonstrated a well-seated mitral valve prothesis without paravalvular leak. The patient had an uneventful recovery. In summary, in selected patients with extensive MAC, direct-access hybrid mitral valve-in-MAC replacement using off-label, balloon-expandable devices can be safe and feasible.

On the linear independence of p p -adic polygamma values
Makoto Kawashima, Anthony Poëls

Abstract
In this article, we present a new linear independence criterion for values of the p p -adic polygamma functions defined by J. Diamond. As an application, we obtain the linear independence of some families of values of the p p -adic Hurwitz zeta function ζ p ​ ( s , x ) \zeta_{p}(s,x) at distinct shifts x x . This improves and extends a previous result due to P. Bel [ 5 ] , as well as irrationality results established by F. Beukers [ 7 ] . Our proof is based on a novel and explicit construction of Padé-type approximants of the second kind of Diamond’s p p -adic polygamma functions. This construction is established by using a difference analogue of the Rodrigues formula for orthogonal polynomials.

Our strategy.
The proof of Theorem
1.4
is based on Padé approximants of second kind. This a similar approach to Beukers in
[
7
, Theorem 9.2]
, although we will use different tools in a more general context. Our constructions heavily rely on formal integration transforms
\varphi_{f}
(see Section
4
). This method was employed, though expressed differently, in
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
24
,
22
]
. While holonomic series were considered in the aforementioned studies, in this paper we examine their “difference analogs”, in other words, formal Laurent series which satisfy a difference equation. For each integer
s\geq 2
, define the formal Laurent series
R_{s}(z)
by

\displaystyle R_{s}(z)=\sum_{k=s-2}^{\infty}(k-s+3)_{s-2}B_{k-s+2}\cdot\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{z^{k+1}},

where
B_{k}
denotes the
k
–th Bernoulli number and
(a)_{k}=a(a+1)\cdots(a+k-1)
is the Pochhammer symbol. Then, for each
x\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, the
s
–th polygamma function
G_{p}^{(s)}
evaluated at
x
is equal to
-R_{s}(x)
. The Laurent series
R_{s}(z)
is the image of a
G
-function in the sense of Siegel
[
41
]
under a modified Mellin transform
\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}
, whose definition is inspired by
[
4
, Définition 1]
and
[
2
, Section
7
]
. This will allow us to show that
R_{s}(z)
is a solution to a certain difference equation, see Proposition
5.4
. We will then construct Padé approximants of second kind
(P_{n}(z),Q_{n,i,s}(z))_{(i,s)}
of the series
(R_{s}(z+\alpha_{i}))_{(i,s)}
. This uses a difference analogue of the Rodrigues formula for Padé approximants, as outlined in
[
22
, Section 2]
by the first author.

To prove the main theorem using Siegel’s method
[
41
]
, it is necessary to demonstrate the non-vanishing of the determinant formed by those Padé approximants, which involves Bernoulli numbers. In previous works such as
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
24
,
22
]
, this step is done by computing a “closed form” of the involved determinants, which can be quite a difficult and challenging problem in general, see
[
23
]
for example. Here, we develop new tools which allows us to prove the non-vanishing property rather “simply”, without having to obtain such explicit formula. This approach is expected to apply in different settings. The rest of the proof is classical, although quite technical. Given a rational number
x
, we estimate the growth of the sequences
(|P_{n}(x)|)_{n}
,
(|Q_{n,i,s}(x)|)_{n}
,
(|P_{n}(x)R_{s}(x+\alpha_{i})-Q_{n,i,s}(x)|_{p})_{n}
, as well as that of
(|D_{n}(x)|)_{n}
and
(|D_{n}(x)|_{p})_{n}
, where
D_{n}(x)
denotes the common denominator of
P_{n}(x)
and
Q_{n,i,s}(x)
. Suitable growth conditions ensure that the
p
–adic numbers
R_{s}(x+\alpha_{i})
together with
1
are linearly independent. For the estimates of
(|P_{n}(x)|)_{n}
,
(|Q_{n,i,s}(x)|)_{n}
, which is directly related to the term
g(M)
appearing in (
7
) we explain in Section
10
how we can use Perron’s second Theorem to improve the rough estimates that we get. To our knowledge, the majority of the Diophantine results based on Poincaré-Perron theorem involve recurrences of order
2
. In our case, the order of the Poincaré-Perron recurrences involved is equal to
M+1
, where the parameter
M
is as in Theorem
1.4
. In particular, it is strictly larger than
2
when
M>1
.

\displaystyle\log_{p}(1+x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}x^{n}}{n}

for each
x\in\mathbb{C}_{p}
with
|x|_{p}<1
. In
[
16
]
, J. Diamond introduced a
p
–adic analog
G_{p}
of the classical log
\Gamma
-function as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

The
log gamma function of Diamond

G_{p}:\mathbb{C}_{p}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{p}
is the function given for each
x\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{p}
by

\displaystyle G_{p}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\Big((x+t)\log_{p}(x+t)-(x+t)\Big)dt.

For each integer
s\geq 1
, we also define
R_{s}:\mathbb{C}_{p}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}_{p}
by setting

(15)

\displaystyle R_{s}(x)=-G_{p}^{(s)}(x)\qquad(x\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

Recall that
G_{p}
satisfies the functional equation
G_{p}(x+1)=G_{p}(x)+\log_{p}(x)
for each
x\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, and is locally analytic on
\mathbb{C}_{p}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Furthermore,
G_{p}(z)
has the following expansion (
confer

[
16
, Theorem 6]
):

(16)

\displaystyle G_{p}(z)=\left(z-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\log_{p}(z)-z+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{B_{k+1}}{k(k+1)}\cdot\dfrac{1}{z^{k}}\qquad(z\in\mathbb{C}_{p},|z|_{p}>1).

The successive derivatives of
G_{p}
are called the
Diamond’s
p
-adic polygamma functions
. For our purpose, it is more convenient to work with
R_{s}
. By (
16
), the function
R_{2}
has the following expansion:

\displaystyle R_{2}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}B_{k}\cdot\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{z^{k+1}}\qquad(z\in\mathbb{C}_{p},|z|_{p}>1).

More generally, for each integer
s\geq 2
and each
z\in\mathbb{C}_{p}
with
|z|_{p}>1
, we have

(17)

\displaystyle R_{s}(z)=R_{2}^{(s-2)}(z)=\sum_{k=s-2}^{\infty}(k-s+3)_{s-2}B_{k-s+2}\cdot\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{z^{k+1}}.

Teichmüller character.
Recall that
q_{p}
is defined in (
2.1
). We define the Teichmüller character
\omega:\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}
as follows. For any
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}=\{x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}\,;\,|x|_{p}=1\}
, we denote by
\omega(x)
the unique
\varphi(q_{p})
–th root of unity such that

\displaystyle\langle x\rangle:=\frac{x}{\omega(x)}\in 1+q_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p},

where
\varphi
is Euler’s totient function. Then, given
y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}
, we put

\displaystyle\omega(y)=p^{v_{p}(y)}\omega\Big(p^{-v_{p}(y)}y\Big)\and\langle y\rangle=\frac{y}{\omega(y)}=\langle p^{-v_{p}(y)}y\rangle\in 1+q_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

Remark 2.2
.

There is a canonical isomorphism
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\cong\mathcal{R}_{p}\times(1+q_{p}\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, which is precisely given by
x\mapsto(\omega(x),\langle x\rangle)
, were
\mathcal{R}_{p}
denotes the subgroup of
\varphi(q_{p})
–th unit roots of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. If
p\geq 3
, we have

\displaystyle\omega(x)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}x^{p^{n}},

and
\omega(x)
is the unique
(p-1)
–th root of unity that is congruent to
x
modulo
p
(see
[
40
, Theorem 33.4]
). We deduce easily from the above that

(18)

\displaystyle\omega(p^{-a}+y)=p^{-a}\omega(1+yp^{a})=p^{-a}\in\mathbb{Q}

for each integer
a\geq 0
and each
y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with
|y|_{p}<p^{a}
. In the special case
p=2
, either
x
or
-x
is congruent to
1
modulo
4=q_{p}
, and we set
\omega(x)=\pm 1
, so that
x
is congruent to
\omega(x)
modulo
4
. Note that this last definition differs from
[
40
, Definition 33.3]
(which would give
\omega(x)=1
for each
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{\times}_{2}
).

The
p
–adic Hurwitz zeta function.
Recall that
q_{p}
is defined in (
2.1
). We follow
[
12
, Definition 11.2.5]
for the definition of the
p
–adic Hurwitz zeta function
\zeta_{p}(s,x)
.

Definition 2.3
.

For
x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
s\in\mathbb{C}_{p}\setminus\{1\}
with
|x|_{p}\geq q_{p}
and
|s|_{p}<q_{p}p^{-1/(p-1)}
, we define
\zeta_{p}(s,x)
by the equivalent formulas

\displaystyle\zeta_{p}(s,x)=\dfrac{1}{s-1}\int_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\langle x+t\rangle^{1-s}dt=\frac{\langle x\rangle^{1-s}}{s-1}\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{1-s}{k}B_{k}x^{-k}.

For a fixed
x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with
|x|_{p}\geq q_{p}
, the function

is the unique

–adic meromorphic function on

satisfying

\displaystyle\zeta_{p}(1-n,x)=-\omega(x)^{-n}\dfrac{B_{n}(x)}{n}

for each integer

. In addition, this function is analytic, except for a simple pole at
s=1
with residue
1
(see
[

, Proposition 11.2.8]
). Note that for

the condition “

with

.

The following identity (see
[

, Proposition 11.5.6.]
) (which is equivalent to (

)) combined with Theorem

implies Theorem

.

(19)

\omega(x)^{1-s}\zeta_{p}(s,x)=\dfrac{(-1)^{s}}{(s-1)!}G_{p}^{(s)}(x)=\dfrac{(-1)^{s+1}}{(s-1)!}R_{s}(x)\qquad(x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p},|x|_{p}\geq q_{p}).

2.2
Padé approximation theory

Fix a field
K
of characteristic
0
. For any subset
S
of a
K
–vector space
V
, we denote by
\left\langle S\right\rangle_{K}\subseteq V
the
K
-vector space generated by the elements of
S
. Given an integer
n\geq 0
, we denote by
K[z]
the ring of polynomials in
z
with coefficients in
K
, and by
K[z]_{\leq n}\subseteq K[z]
the subgroup of polynomials of degree at most
n
.

Let us recall the definition of Padé-type approximants of Laurent series and their basic properties. We denote by
K[[1/z]]
the ring of formal power series ring of variable
1/z
with coefficients in
K
, and by
K((1/z))
its field of fractions. We say that an element of
K((1/z))
, which can be written as

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-n}^{\infty}\dfrac{a_{k}}{z^{k}},

with
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
a_{k}\in K
, is a
formal Laurent series
in
1/z
. We define the
order
function at
z=\infty
by

\displaystyle\mathrm{ord}_{\infty}:K((1/z))\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\};\ \sum_{k}\dfrac{a_{k}}{z^{k}}\mapsto\min\{k\in\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}\,;\,a_{k}\neq 0\}.

with the convention
\min\emptyset=\infty
. In particular, for any
f\in K((1/z))
, we have
\mathrm{ord}_{\infty}\,f=\infty
if and only if
f=0
.

Given two
K
–vector spaces
V
and
W
, we denote by
\mathrm{Hom}_{K}(V,W)
the
K
–vector space of
K
–linear homomorphisms
V\rightarrow W
. When
V=W
, we write
\mathrm{End}_{K\text{-lin}}(V)=\mathrm{Hom}_{K}(V,W)
. Similarly, for any
K
–algebra
\mathcal{A}
, we define
\mathrm{End}_{K\text{-alg}}(\mathcal{A})
as the
K
-vector space of
K
–algebra endomorphisms of
\mathcal{A}
.

We recall, without proof, the following basic result from Padé approximation theory.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
m
be a positive integer,
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\in(1/z)\cdot K[[1/z]]
and
\boldsymbol{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
.
Put
N=\sum_{j=1}^{m}n_{j}
. For any non-negative integer
M\geq N
, there exists a non-zero vector of polynomials
(P,Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{m})\in K[z]^{m+1}
satisfying the following conditions:

1.

\deg P\leq M
,

2.

\mathrm{ord}_{\infty}\left(P(z)f_{j}(z)-Q_{j}(z)\right)\geq n_{j}+1
for
j=1,\dots,m
.

Definition 2.5
.

With the notation of Lemma
2.4
, fix a non-zero vector of polynomials
(P,Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{m})\in K[z]^{m+1}
satisfying the properties
1
and
2
.

\bullet

We say that
(P,Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{m})\in K[z]^{m+1}
is a
weight
\boldsymbol{n}
and degree
M
Padé-type approximant of
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
.

\bullet

We call the remainders, namely the formal Laurent series
(P(z)f_{j}(z)-Q_{j}(z))_{1\leq j\leq m}
,
weight
\boldsymbol{n}
degree
M
Padé-type approximations of
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
.

3
Modified formal transforms

Fix a field
K
of characteristic
0
. We introduce a modified inverse Mellin transform
\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}_{K}
for formal power series, and we study some of its properties. This transform will play a key-role in studying the properties of the explicit Padé approximants constructed in Section
7
. In the next section, we will compute the inverse Mellin transform of some formal series connected to polygamma functions.

Formal series (examples).
Fix
\alpha\in K
. We define the following formal series of
K[[z]]
as usual

\displaystyle\exp(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{z^{n}}{n!},\quad\log(1+z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n}z^{n}\and(1+z)^{\alpha}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{\alpha}{k}z^{k},

where
\displaystyle\binom{\alpha}{k}=\alpha(\alpha-1)\cdots(\alpha-k+1)/k!
(with the convention that
\displaystyle\binom{\alpha}{0}=1
). We also see
1/(z+\alpha)
as an element of
(1/z)\cdot K[[1/z]]
by writing

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z+\alpha}=\frac{1}{z}\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(-\frac{\alpha}{z}\right)^{n}.

Difference and differential operators of
K[[1/z]]
.
Given
\alpha\in K
we denote by
\tau_{\alpha}
the
\alpha
–shift operator, and by
\Delta_{\alpha}=\tau_{\alpha}-1
the
\alpha
–difference operator of
K((1/z))
. Note that they stabilize
K[[1/z]]
and
(1/z)\cdot K[[1/z]]
. They are defined for each
f(z)\in K((1/z))
by

\displaystyle\tau_{\alpha}\big(f(z)\big)=f(z+\alpha)\and\Delta_{\alpha}\big(f(z)\big)=f(z+\alpha)-f(z).

Fix
f(z)\in K((1/z))
and a sequence
(a_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of elements in
K
. Then, the series

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}\Delta_{\alpha}^{n}\big(f(z)\big)\and\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}f(z)

converge in
K((1/z))
, since for each integer
n\geq 0
, the formal series
\Delta_{\alpha}^{n}(f(z))
and
\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}f(z)
belong to
(1/z)^{n+d}K[[1/z]]
, where
d=\mathrm{ord}_{\infty}(f)\in\mathbb{Z}
. The sets
K[[\Delta_{\alpha}]]
and
K[[d/dz]]
are therefore subsets of the set of
\mathrm{End}_{K\text{-lin}}\big(K((1/z))\big)
. The above argument shows that they are also subsets of
\mathrm{End}_{K\text{-lin}}\big((1/z)\cdot K[[1/z]]\big)
and
\mathrm{End}_{K\text{-lin}}(K[[1/z]])
. The following result will be useful.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any
\alpha\in K
, we have

(20)

\displaystyle\exp\left(\alpha\frac{d}{dz}\right)=\tau_{\alpha}\and\exp\left(\frac{d}{dz}\right)-1=\Delta_{1}.

So
K[[d/dz]]=K[[\Delta_{1}]]
,

(21)

\displaystyle\log(1+\Delta_{1})=\frac{d}{dz}\and(1+\Delta_{1})^{\alpha}=\tau_{\alpha}.

Proof..

Since
\Delta_{1}=\tau_{1}-1
, we only have to prove the first equality in (
20
). Write

\displaystyle D:=\exp\left(\alpha\frac{d}{dz}\right)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\alpha^{k}}{k!}\frac{d^{k}}{dz^{k}}.

Let
n\geq 0
be an integer. A quick computation yields

\displaystyle D\left(\frac{1}{z^{n}}\right)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(-n)(-n-1)\cdots(-n-k+1)}{k!}\frac{\alpha^{k}}{z^{n+k}}=\frac{1}{z^{n}}\frac{1}{(1+\alpha/z)^{n}}=\frac{1}{(z+\alpha)^{n}}=\tau_{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{z^{n}}\right),

hence (
20
).
∎

Formal Laplace transform and Stirling numbers.
Following
[
8
, p. 154]
, we define the (formal) modified Laplace transform

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{K}:K[[z]]\longrightarrow K[[z]];\ \ \ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\frac{z^{k}}{k!}\mapsto\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}.

Then
\mathfrak{L}_{K}
is a homeomorphism of
K[[z]]
(with respect to the
(z)
-adic topology).

Given a pair of non-negative integers
(k,n)
, we define
\mathscr{S}(n,k)
as the number of ways of partitioning a set of
n
elements into
k
non-empty sets. The numbers
\mathscr{S}(n,k)
(sometimes denoted by
\Big\{\!\!\begin{array}[]{c}n\\
k\end{array}\!\!\Big\}
, see
[
25
]
) are called
Stirling number of the second kind

[
44
]
. They satisfy the recurrence relation

(22)

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}(n,k)=\mathscr{S}(n-1,k-1)+k\mathscr{S}(n-1,k)\qquad(k,n\geq 1)

with initial conditions
\mathscr{S}(0,0)=1
and
\mathscr{S}(n,0)=\mathscr{S}(0,n)=0
for each
n\geq 1
. They also have the following generating functions

(23)

\displaystyle\dfrac{(e^{z}-1)^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\mathscr{S}(k,n)\dfrac{z^{k}}{k!}\and\dfrac{z^{n}}{(1-z)\cdots(1-nz)}=\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\mathscr{S}(k,n)z^{k}

for each integer
n\geq 0
(see for example
[
30
, Chapter V, §26–27]
). The above identities can be obtained by using (
22
). We deduce from (
23
) the following result.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
n
be a non-negative integer. Then,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{K}\left(\frac{(e^{z}-1)^{n}}{n!}\right)=\dfrac{z^{n}}{(1-z)\cdots(1-nz)}.

Formal modified Mellin transform.
The definition of our modified Mellin transforms are inspired by
[
4
, Définition 1]
and
[
2
, Section
7
]
, see Remark
3.7
below. Recall that by Lemma
3.1
, we have
K[[d/dz]]=K[[\Delta_{1}]]
.

Definition 3.3
.

The correspondence
z\mapsto\Delta_{1}=\exp(d/dz)-1
defines an isomorphism of
K
–algebra

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}^{\mathrm{Inv}}_{K}:K[[z]]\longrightarrow K[[d/dz]].

When there is no ambiguity, we simply write
\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}^{\mathrm{Inv}}=\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}^{\mathrm{Inv}}_{K}
.

Example 3.4
.

We deduce from Lemma
3.1
that for any
\alpha\in K
, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}^{\mathrm{Inv}}\big((1+z)^{\alpha}\big)=\tau_{\alpha}\and\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}^{\mathrm{Inv}}\big(\log(1+z)\big)=\dfrac{d}{dz}.

Definition 3.5
.

We call
modified inverse Mellin transform
of power series the map

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}_{K}:K[[z]]\longrightarrow(1/z)\cdot K\left[\left[1/z\right]\right];\ \ \ g(z)\mapsto\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}\left(-\dfrac{1}{z}\right)^{k+1},

where
(b_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is defined by
g(e^{z}-1)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{k}/k!
. When there is no ambiguity, we simply write
\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}=\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}_{K}
.

The transform
\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}
satisfies the following property.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
g(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\in K[[z]]
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}(g)(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(-1)^{k+1}a_{k}k!}{z(z+1)\cdots(z+k)}.

Proof..

Define the morphism

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}_{K}:K[[z]]\longrightarrow(1/z)\cdot K\left[\left[1/z\right]\right];\ \ f(z)\longmapsto-\frac{1}{z}f\Big(-\frac{1}{z}\Big),

so that
\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}(g)(z)=\mathfrak{T}_{K}\circ\mathfrak{L}_{K}\big(g(e^{z}-1)\big)
. By Lemma
3.2
, we find

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{K}\big(g(e^{z}-1)\big)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\mathfrak{L}_{K}\big(g(e^{z}-1)^{k}\big)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{a_{k}k!z^{k}}{(1-z)\cdots(1-kz)}.

Then conclusion follows easily.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

The ring of inverse factorial series (with complex coefficients) is

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[!z!]:=\bigg\{\sum_{n\geq 0}\dfrac{a_{n}}{z(z+1)\cdots(z+n)}\,;\,a_{n}\in\mathbb{C}\bigg\},

(it corresponds to the set
\mathbb{C}[!z!]^{(0)}
with the notation of
[
2
, Section
7.2
]
). Note that
\mathbb{C}[!z!]=(1/z)\cdot\mathbb{C}[[1/z]]
. In
[
2
, Section
7.2
]
and
[
4
, Section 1.2]
, the authors consider the formal Mellin transform

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}:\mathbb{C}[!z!]\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}[[1-z]];\ \ \sum_{n\geq 0}\dfrac{a_{n}}{z(z+1)\cdots(z+n)}\longmapsto\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}\dfrac{(1-z)^{n}}{n!}.

Proposition
3.6
implies that
\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}_{\mathbb{C}}=-\mathcal{M}^{-1}\circ\mathfrak{T}_{-1}
, where
\mathfrak{T}_{-1}:\mathbb{C}[[z]]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[[1-z]]
is the isomorphism given by
z\mapsto z-1
.

We end this section with an analog of
[
4
, Proposition 3]
, which establishes a relation between the formal transforms
\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}^{\mathrm{Inv}}
and
\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}
.

Proposition 3.8
.

For any
f(z),g(z)\in K[[z]]
, we have

(24)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}\big(f(z)g(z)\big)=\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}^{\mathrm{Inv}}(f(z))\big(\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}(g(z))\big).

This yields the following commutative diagram:

\displaystyle\begin{CD}K[[z]]\times K[[z]]@>{{\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}^{\mathrm{Inv}}\times\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}}>{}>K\left[\left[{d}/{dz}\right]\right]\times(1/z)\cdot K\left[\left[1/z\right]\right]\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
K[[z]]@>{\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}}>{}>(1/z)\cdot K\left[\left[1/z\right]\right].\end{CD}

Proof..

Let
g(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\in K[[z]]
. Since
\Delta_{1}(1/(z)_{k+1})=-(k+1)/(z)_{k+2}
for each integer
k\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}^{\mathrm{Inv}}(z)(\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}(g)(z))=\Delta_{1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(-1)^{k+1}a_{k}k!}{z(z+1)\cdots(z+k)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(-1)^{k+2}a_{k}(k+1)!}{z(z+1)\cdots(z+k+1)}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}(zg)(z),

the last equality coming from Proposition
3.6
. By induction, we obtain
\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}^{\mathrm{Inv}}(z^{n})(\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}(g)(z))=\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}(z^{n}g)(z)
for each integer
n\geq 1
. Hence (
24
) (by linearity).
∎

4
Formal
f
–integration transform

We keep the notation of Sections
3
. In this section, we introduce and study the properties of the transform
\varphi_{f}
, which will play a crucial role in the construction of our Padé approximants (see
[
22
, Section 2]
,
[
24
, Section 3]
for other applications related to those maps).

4.1
Notation and definitions

Definition 4.1
.

We associate to any Laurent series
f(z)={\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}}f_{k}/z^{k+1}\in(1/z)\cdot K[[1/z]]
, a
K
–linear morphism

(25)

\displaystyle\varphi_{f}:K[t]\longrightarrow K;\ \ \ t^{k}\mapsto f_{k}\ \ \ (k\geq 0).

We call the map
\varphi_{f}
the
formal
f
-integration transform
.

Remark 4.2
.

In the case
f(z)=-\log(1-1/z)
, the map
\varphi_{f}
is simply the operator
P(t)\mapsto\int_{0}^{1}P(u)du
, which is the reason why we call it
f
-integration transform.

Note that the
K
–linear map

\displaystyle\Phi:(1/z)\cdot K[[1/z]]\longrightarrow{\rm{Hom}}_{K}(K[t],K)

defined by
f\mapsto\varphi_{f}
is an isomorphism. Given
f\in(1/z)\cdot K[[1/z]]
, the map
\varphi_{f}
extends naturally in a
K[z]
-linear map
\varphi_{f}:K[z,t]\rightarrow K[z]
, and then to a
K((1/z))
-linear map
\varphi_{f}:L\rightarrow z^{n}K[[1/z]]
, where
L
denotes ring which consists in all the elements of the form

\displaystyle F(z,t)=\sum_{k=-n}^{\infty}\dfrac{P_{k}(t)}{z^{k}},

with
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
P_{k}(t)\in K[t]
. Explicitly, for any element
F(z,t)
as above, we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{f}(F(z,t))=\sum_{k\geq-n}\frac{\varphi_{f}(P_{k}(t))}{z^{k}}\in z^{n}K[[1/z]].

With this notation, and seeing
1/(z-t)=\sum_{k\geq 1}t^{k}/z^{k+1}
as an element of
L
, the formal Laurent series
f(z)
satisfies the following crucial identities (
confer

[
31
, (6.2) p.60 and (5.7) p.52]
):

\displaystyle f(z)=\varphi_{f}\left(\dfrac{1}{z-t}\right),\ \ \ P(z)f(z)-\varphi_{f}\left(\dfrac{P(z)-P(t)}{z-t}\right)\in(1/z)\cdot K[[1/z]],

for any
P(z)\in K[z]
. Let us recall a condition, based on the morphism
\varphi_{f}
, for given polynomials to be Padé approximants.

Lemma 4.3

(
confer

[
22
, Lemma 2.3]
)
.

Let
m,M
be positive integers,
f_{1}(z),\ldots,f_{m}(z)\in(1/z)\cdot K[[1/z]]
and
\boldsymbol{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
with
\sum_{j=1}^{m}n_{j}\leq M
. Let
P(z)\in K[z]
be a non-zero polynomial with
\deg P\leq M
, and put
Q_{j}(z)=\varphi_{f_{j}}\left((P(z)-P(t))/(z-t)\right)\in K[z]
for
1\leq j\leq m
. The following assertions are equivalent.

1.

The vector of polynomials
(P,Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{m})
is a weight
\boldsymbol{n}
Padé-type approximants of
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
.

2.

We have
t^{k}P(t)\in{\rm{ker}}\,\varphi_{f_{j}}
for any pair of integers
(j,k)
with
1\leq j\leq m
and
0\leq k\leq n_{j}-1
.

Notation.
In the next section, we will use the following operators. We use bold symbols to indicate when a map is defined on the ring
K[t]
(in order to distinguish them from their analogues defined on
K[z]
in Section
3
). Fix an element
\alpha\in K
.

\bullet

[P]\in{\rm{End}}_{K\text{-lin}}(K[t])
denotes the multiplication by the polynomial
P\in K[t]
. If there is no ambiguity, we will sometimes omit the brackets.

\bullet

We denote by
\mathrm{Eval}_{t=\alpha}\in{\rm{Hom}}_{K}(K[t],K)
the
\alpha
–evaluation linear form, and by
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\alpha}\in{\rm{End}}_{K\text{-alg}}(K[t])
(resp.
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\alpha}=\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\alpha}-[1]\in{\rm{End}}_{K\text{-lin}}(K[t])
) the
\alpha
–shift (resp.
\alpha
–difference) operator. They are given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Eval}_{t=\alpha}(P(t))=P(\alpha),\quad\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\alpha}(P(t))=P(t+\alpha)\and\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\alpha}(P(t))=P(t+\alpha)-P(t),

for each
P(t)\in K[t]
.

\bullet

For any
f(z)\in K((1/z))
, we denote by
\pi(f(z))
the unique element of
(1/z)\cdot K[[1/z]]
such that
f(z)-\pi(f(z))\in K[z]
. This defines a (surjective) projection

(26)

\displaystyle\pi:K((1/z))\longrightarrow(1/z)\cdot K[[1/z]].

\bullet

The map
\iota:K[z,\tau_{\alpha}]\longrightarrow K[t,\boldsymbol{\tau}_{-\alpha}]
is defined for each
D=\sum_{i}[a_{i}(z)]\circ\tau_{\alpha}\in K[z,\tau_{\alpha}]
by

\displaystyle D^{*}:=\iota\Big(\sum_{i}[a_{i}(z)]\circ\tau_{\alpha}\Big)=\sum_{i}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{-\alpha}\circ[a_{i}(t)],

(this is a similar operator as the one in
[
1
, Exercise III(3)]
).

For any
D\in K[z,\tau_{\alpha}]
, we view
D
and
D^{*}
as elements of
\mathrm{End}_{K\text{-lin}}(K[t][[1/z]])
by setting for each
f(z,t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}P_{k}(t)/z^{n}\in K[t][[1/z]]

D(f(z,t))=\sum_{n\geq 0}P_{k}(t)D\left(\frac{1}{z^{n}}\right)\and D^{*}(f(z,t))=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{D^{*}(P_{k}(t))}{z^{n}}.

4.2
Properties of the operators
D
and
D^{*}

In the following lemma, we consider
1/(z-t)=\sum_{k\geq 0}t^{k}/z^{k+1}
as an element of
K[t][[1/z]]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\alpha\in K
and
D\in K[z,\tau_{\alpha}]
. Then there exists a polynomial
P(t,z)\in K[t,z]
such that

(27)

\displaystyle D\left(\dfrac{1}{z-t}\right)=P(t,z)+D^{*}\left(\dfrac{1}{z-t}\right).

Proof..

By linearity, it suffices to prove the statement of
D=[z^{m}]\circ\tau^{n}_{\alpha}
, where
m,n
are non-negative integers. We have

\displaystyle\tau^{n}_{\alpha}\left(\dfrac{1}{z-t}\right)

\displaystyle=\tau^{n}_{\alpha}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{t^{k}}{z^{k+1}}\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{t^{k}}{(z+n\alpha)^{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{t^{k}}{z^{k+1}}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{k+{\ell}}{k}\left(\dfrac{\alpha n}{z}\right)^{\ell}\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(t-n\alpha)^{k}}{z^{k+1}},

hence

(28)

\displaystyle D\left(\dfrac{1}{z-t}\right)=P(t,z)+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(t-n\alpha)^{k+m}}{z^{k+1}},\quad\textrm{where }P(t,z)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(t-n\alpha)^{k}z^{m-k-1}.

(with the convention that
P(t,z)=0
if
m=0
). On the other hand

\displaystyle D^{*}\left(\dfrac{1}{z-t}\right)=\boldsymbol{\tau}^{n}_{-\alpha}\circ[t^{m}]\left(\dfrac{1}{z-t}\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(t-n\alpha)^{k+m}}{z^{k+1}}.

Combining the above with (
28
), we obtain (
27
). This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

4.3
Properties of the transform
\varphi_{f}

Lemma
4.4
allows us to show the following key proposition.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
f(z)\in(1/z)\cdot K[[1/z]]
and
D\in K[z,\tau_{\alpha}]
. Then, viewing
\varphi_{\pi(D(f))}
and
\varphi_{f}\circ D^{*}
as elements of
{\rm{Hom}}_{K}(K[t],K)
, we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{\pi(D(f))}=\varphi_{f}\circ D^{*}.

Proof..

Let
P(t,z)\in K[t,z]
be such that (
27
) of Lemma
4.4
holds. Then, writing
P(z)=\varphi_{f}(P(t,z))
and since
\varphi_{f}
acts only on the parameter
t
, we have

\displaystyle D(f(z))=D\circ\varphi_{f}\left(\dfrac{1}{z-t}\right)=\varphi_{f}\left(D\bigg(\dfrac{1}{z-t}\bigg)\right)

\displaystyle=P(z)+\varphi_{f}\left(D^{*}\bigg(\dfrac{1}{z-t}\bigg)\right)

\displaystyle=P(z)+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{\varphi_{f}(D^{*}(t^{k}))}{z^{k+1}}.

This shows that

\displaystyle\pi(D(f))=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{f}(D^{*}(t^{k}))/{z^{k+1}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \varphi_{\pi(D(f))}(t^{k})=\varphi_{f}\circ D^{*}(t^{k})\ \ \ \text{for all}\ k\geq 0.

This completes the proof of Proposition
4.5
.
∎

We deduce several interesting consequences from Proposition
4.5
.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
f(z)\in(1/z)\cdot K[[1/z]]
and
D\in K[z,\tau_{\alpha}]\setminus\{0\}
. The following assertions are equivalent.

1.

D(f(z))\in K[z]
.

2.

D^{*}(K[t])\subseteq\ker\varphi_{f}
.

Proof..

Conditions
1
and
2
are equivalent to
\pi(D(f))=0
and
\varphi_{f}\circ D^{*}=0
, respectively. Those last two assertions are clearly equivalent by Proposition
4.5
.
∎

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
f(z)\in(1/z)\cdot K[[1/z]]
and
\alpha\in K
, and set
g(z)=\tau_{\alpha}(f(z))\in(1/z)\cdot K[[1/z]]
. Then

\displaystyle\varphi_{g}=\varphi_{f}\circ\boldsymbol{\tau}_{-\alpha}.

Proof..

As discussed at the beginning of Section
3
, we have
g(z)\in(1/z)\cdot K[[1/z]]
, so that
\pi(g(z))=g(z)
. Set
D=\tau_{\alpha}
. By definition
D^{*}=\boldsymbol{\tau}_{-\alpha}
, and we conclude by Proposition
4.5
.
∎

We also deduce from Proposition
4.5
the following results.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
f\in(1/z)\cdot K[[1/z]]
and write

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{a_{k}k!}{z(z+1)\cdots(z+k)}.

Then, for each
j\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{f}\left(\frac{(t)_{j}}{j!}\right)=a_{j}.

Proof..

Recall that
\pi:K[z][[1/z]]\to(1/z)\cdot K[[1/z]]
is the morphism defined in (
26
). Fix an integer
j\geq 0
and set
D=(z)_{j}/j!
. Then
D^{*}=(t)_{j}/j!
, and Proposition
4.5
gives

(29)

\displaystyle\varphi_{f}\left(\frac{(t)_{j}}{j!}\right)=\varphi_{f}(D^{*}(1))=\varphi_{\pi(D(f))}(1).

On the other hand, since
(z)_{j}/(z)_{k+1}\in K[z]
for

, we have

\displaystyle\pi(D(f))=\dfrac{1}{j!}\sum_{k=j}^{\infty}\dfrac{a_{k}k!}{(z+j)(z+j+1)\cdots(z+k)}\in\dfrac{a_{j}}{z}+\dfrac{1}{z^{2}}K[[1/z]],

hence
\varphi_{\pi(D(f))}(1)=a_{j}

29

∎

Combined with Proposition
3.6
, the above lemma has the following consequence.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let

For each

5
Mellin transform and
p
–adic polygamma functions

We keep the notation of Section
3
. Denote by
\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}=\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}_{K}
the inverse modified Mellin transform (see Definition
3.5
). In this section, we introduce and study a family of formal series
R_{\alpha,s}(z)\in(1/z)\cdot K[[1/z]]
connected to the
p
–adic polygamma functions. We also prove that the series
R_{\alpha,s}
satisfies a rather simple difference equation.

Definition 5.1
.

Given an integer
s\geq 2
and
\alpha\in K
, set

\displaystyle R_{\alpha,s}(z)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}\left(\dfrac{(1+z)^{\alpha}\log^{s-1}(1+z)}{z}\right),

\displaystyle R_{\alpha,1}(z)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}\left(\dfrac{(1+z)^{\alpha}-1}{z}\right).

For
\alpha=0
, we simply write

\displaystyle R_{s}(z)=R_{0,s}(z)=\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}\left(\dfrac{\log^{s-1}(1+z)}{z}\right).

In the following lemma we show that
R_{s}(z)\in(1/z)\cdot K[[1/z]]
is the formal series corresponding to the series (
17
) of the
p
–adic polygamma functions, which is why we are using the same notation.

Lemma 5.2
.

With the notation of Definition
5.1
, we have

\displaystyle R_{s}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=s-2}^{\infty}(k-s+3)_{s-2}B_{k-s+2}\cdot\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{z^{k+1}},

\displaystyle R_{\alpha,s}(z)

\displaystyle=\tau_{\alpha}(R_{s}(z))=R_{s}(z+\alpha),

\displaystyle R_{\alpha,1}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{B_{k+1}(\alpha)-B_{k+1}}{k+1}\cdot\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{z^{k+1}}.

Furthermore

1.

The series
R_{\alpha,s}(x)
converges
p
–adically for each
x,\alpha\in\mathbb{C}_{p}
with
|x+\alpha|_{p}>1
.

2.

The series
R_{\alpha,1}(x)
converges
p
–adically for each
x,\alpha\in\mathbb{C}_{p}
with
|x|_{p}>|\alpha|_{p}
.

Proof..

Write
g(z)=\log^{s-1}(1+z)/z
so that
R_{s}(z)=\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}(g)(z)
. Using the generating function of Bernoulli numbers (
12
), we find

\displaystyle g(e^{z}-1)=\frac{z^{s-1}}{e^{z}-1}=\sum_{k=s-2}^{\infty}(k-s+3)_{s-2}B_{k-s+2}\cdot\dfrac{z^{k}}{k!}.

The expansion of
R_{s}(z)
follows from the definition of
\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}
. The second identity is then a direct consequence of Lemma
5.3
below. For the third identity, write
h(z)=\big((1+z)^{\alpha}-1\big)/z
. Again, using (
12
), we find

\displaystyle h(e^{z}-1)=\frac{e^{\alpha z}-1}{e^{z}-1}=\frac{1}{z}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\big(B_{k}(\alpha)-B_{k}\big)\cdot\dfrac{z^{k}}{k!}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\big(B_{k+1}(\alpha)-B_{k+1}\big)\cdot\dfrac{z^{k}}{(k+1)!}.

The expected formula follows from the definition of
\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}
, since
R_{\alpha,1}(z)=\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}(h)(z)
. The convergence in
\mathbb{C}_{p}
of the series
R_{\alpha,s}(x)
and
R_{\alpha,1}(x)
is a consequence of (
14
).
∎

We deduce from Proposition
3.8
the following crucial identities.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
g(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}z^{n}\in K[[z]]
and
\alpha\in K
. We have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}\big(\log(1+z)g\big)(z)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{d}{dz}\big(\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}(g)(z)\big),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}\big((1+z)^{\alpha}g\big)(z)

\displaystyle=\tau_{\alpha}(\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}(g)(z)),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}\big(((1+z)^{\alpha}-1)g\big)(z)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{\alpha}(\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}(g)(z)).

Proof..

This follows from Proposition
3.8
combined respectively with

\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}^{\mathrm{Inv}}(\log(1+z))=d/dz\and\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}^{\mathrm{Inv}}((1+z)^{\alpha})=\tau_{\alpha}

coming from Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Proposition 5.4

(Difference equation of the Laurent series
R_{\alpha,s}
)
.

Let
s\geq 2
be an integer and
\alpha\in K
. We have

(30)

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}(R_{\alpha,1}(z))=\dfrac{\alpha}{z(z+\alpha)}\and\Delta_{1}(R_{\alpha,s}(z))=\dfrac{(-1)^{s}(s-1)!}{(z+\alpha)^{s}}.

Proof..

According to Proposition
3.8
, and since
\Delta_{1}=\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}^{\mathrm{Inv}}(z)
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}(R_{\alpha,1}(z))=\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}^{\mathrm{Inv}}(z)\Big(\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}\left(\frac{(1+z)^{\alpha}-1}{z}\right)\Big)=\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}\left((1+z)^{\alpha}-1\right)=\Delta_{\alpha}(\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}(1)),

the last inequality coming from Lemma
5.3
. Combined with
\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}(1)=-1/z
, this yields the first equality of (
30
). We proceed in a similar way for the second equality. Note that since
R_{\alpha,s}=\tau_{\alpha}(R_{s}(z))
and
\Delta_{1}\circ\tau_{\alpha}=\tau_{\alpha}\circ\Delta_{1}
, we only have to prove the case
\alpha=0
. Using Proposition
3.8
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}(R_{s}(z))=\widehat{\mathfrak{M}}^{\mathrm{Inv}}(z)\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}\left(\dfrac{\log^{s-1}(1+z)}{z}\right)=\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}\left(\log^{s-1}(1+z)\right)=\dfrac{d^{s-1}}{dz^{s-1}}\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}(1),

the last equality coming from the first identity of Lemma
5.3
. We conclude by using
\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Inv}}(1)=-1/z
.
∎

6
Properties of the difference operator

We keep the notation of Section
4
. Recall that for any
P(t)\in K[t]
, we denote by
[P]
the operator “multiplication by
P(t)
”. The difference operator
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-1}=\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\alpha}-[1]
will be involved in the construction of the Padé approximants in Section
7
. This motivates us to study its properties.

Lemma 6.1
.

For any positive integer
n
and for any polynomial
P(t)\in K[t]
, we have

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{n}_{-1}\circ[P(t)]=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\Big[\boldsymbol{\tau}^{k}_{-1}\circ\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{n-k}_{-1}(P(t))\Big]\circ\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{k}_{-1}.

Proof..

Let
P(t),Q(t)\in K[t]
. We proceed by induction on
n
. For
n=1
, we easily check that

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-1}\big(P(t)Q(t)\big)=\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-1}(P(t))\cdot Q(t)+\boldsymbol{\tau}_{-1}(P(t))\cdot\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-1}(Q(t)).

Suppose now that the lemma is true for a positive integer
n
. Then

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-1}^{n+1}\big(P(t)Q(t)\big)

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-1}^{n}\Big(\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-1}\big(P(t)Q(t)\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-1}^{n}\Big(\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-1}(P(t))\cdot Q(t)+\boldsymbol{\tau}_{-1}(P(t))\cdot\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-1}(Q(t))\Big).

We then get the result by applying the induction hypothesis with the pairs of polynomials
\big(\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-1}(P(t)),Q(t)\big)
and
\big(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{-1}(P(t)),\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-1}(Q(t))\big)
(and by using the commutativity of
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-1}
and
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{-1}
).
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. For any polynomial
P(t)\in K[t]
, we have

\displaystyle[P(t)]\circ\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{n}_{-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{n-j}_{-1}\circ\left[{\boldsymbol{\tau}}^{n-j}_{1}\circ\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{j}_{1}(P(t))\right].

Proof..

We proceed by induction on
n
. For
n=1
, a direct computation ensures that for any polynomial
P(t)\in K[t]
, we have

(31)

\displaystyle[P(t)]\circ\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-1}=\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-1}\circ[\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1}(P(t))]+[\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{1}(P(t))].

Let
n\geq 1
be such that the assertion is true, and
P(t)\in K[t]
. By our induction hypothesis,

\displaystyle[P(t)]\circ\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{n+1}_{-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{n-j}_{-1}\circ\left[{\boldsymbol{\tau}}^{n-j}_{1}\circ\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{j}_{1}(P(t))\right]\circ\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-1}

(32)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{n-j}_{-1}\circ\left(\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-1}\circ\left[{\boldsymbol{\tau}}^{n+1-j}_{1}\circ\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{j}_{1}(P(t))\right]+\left[{\boldsymbol{\tau}}^{n-j}_{1}\circ\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{j+1}_{1}(P(t))\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n+1}\binom{n+1}{j}\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{n+1-j}_{-1}\circ\left[{\boldsymbol{\tau}}^{n+1-j}_{1}\circ\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{j}_{1}(P(t))\right],

where we obtain (
32
) by applying (
31
) to the polynomial
{\boldsymbol{\tau}}^{n-j}_{1}\circ\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{j}_{1}(P(t))
, and by using the commutativity of
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{1}
and
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{1}
. This concludes our induction step.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
n,d,m_{1},\dots,m_{d}
be non-negative integers with
n,d\geq 1
, and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d}\in K
. Set

\displaystyle A(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(t+\alpha_{i})^{m_{i}+1}\and A_{n}(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(t+\alpha_{i})_{n}^{m_{i}+1}.

Suppose that
P(t)\in A_{n}(t)K[t]
. Then, we have the following properties.

1.

For each
k=0,\dots,n-1
, we have

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{k}_{-1}(P(t))\in A(t)K[t].

2.

For any polynomial
Q(t)\in K[t]
, we have

\displaystyle Q(t)\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{n}_{-1}(P(t))\in\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-1}\big(A(t)K[t]\big)+P(t)\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{n}(Q(t)).

3.

For any polynomial
Q(t)\in K[t]
with
\deg Q(t)<n
, we have

\displaystyle Q(t)\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{n}_{-1}(P(t))\in\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-1}\big(A(t)K[t]\big).

Proof..

1
. Recall the identity
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-1}^{k}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}(-1)^{k-i}\boldsymbol{\tau}^{i}_{-1}
. Write
P(t)=A_{n}(t)R(t)
, with
R(t)\in K[t]
, and fix an integer
k
with
0\leq k<n
. Then, we obtain

(33)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-1}^{k}(P(t))=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}(-1)^{k-i}Q(t-i)\prod_{j=1}^{d}(t+\alpha_{j}-i)^{m_{j}}_{n}.

We conclude by noticing that since
k<n
, for each
0\leq i\leq k
, the polynomial
\prod_{j=1}^{d}(t+\alpha_{j}-i)^{m_{j}}_{n}
is divisible by
\prod_{j=1}^{d}(t+\alpha_{j})^{m_{j}}=A(t)
.

2
. Fix a polynomial
Q(t)\in K[t]
, and define

R_{j}(t)=P(t)\cdot{\boldsymbol{\tau}}^{n-j}_{1}\circ\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{j}_{1}\big(Q(t)\big)\in A_{n}(t)K[t]

for
j=0,\dots,n-1
. By Lemma
6.2
, we have

\displaystyle Q(t)\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{n}_{-1}(P(t))

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{-1}\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n}{j}\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{n-1-j}_{-1}\big(R_{j}(t)\big)\bigg)+P(t)\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{1}^{n}(Q(t)).

Applying
1
to the polynomial
R_{j}(t)
, we find
\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{n-1-j}_{-1}\big(R_{j}(t)\big)\in A(t)K[t]
for
j=0,\dots,n-1
, hence
2
.

Finally,
3
is a direct consequence of
2
, since
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{1}^{n}(Q(t))=0
as soon as
\deg Q(t)<n
.
∎

7
Construction of Padé approximants

We keep the notation of Sections
3
and
4
. This section is devoted to the explicit construction of Padé-type approximants for the Laurent series
R_{\alpha,s}(z)
introduced in Section
5
. Let
d,m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}
be positive integers and
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{d})\in K^{d}
with
\alpha_{1}=0
. Denote by
\mathcal{S}
the set of indices

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=\{(i,s)\,;\,1\leq i\leq d\and 1\leq s\leq m_{i}+1\}\setminus\{(1,1)\},

and set

\displaystyle M=\#\mathcal{S}=d-1+\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}.

For simplicity, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
and any positive integer
s
, we write

(34)

\displaystyle\varphi_{\alpha,s}=\varphi_{R_{\alpha,s}},

where
R_{\alpha,s}(z)
as in Definition
5.1
. We also introduce the following formal series of
K[[z]]
.

Definition 7.1
.

For any
(i,s)\in\mathcal{S}
, set

\displaystyle g_{i,s}(z):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{i,s,k}z^{k}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{(1+z)^{\alpha_{i}}\log^{s-1}(1+z)}{z}&\ \text{if}\ s\geq 2\\
\dfrac{(1+z)^{\alpha_{i}}-1}{z}&\ \text{if}\ i\geq 2\ \text{and}\ s=1.\end{cases}

If follows easily from that definition that
a_{1,s,0}=\cdots=a_{1,s,s-3}=0

\displaystyle a_{i,s,k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{i}\geq 0\\
\ell_{1}+\cdots+\ell_{s-1}=k-s+2\end{subarray}}\dfrac{(-1)^{k-s+2}}{(\ell_{1}+1)\cdots(\ell_{s-1}+1)}&\textrm{if $i=1$ and $s\geq 2$ and $k\geq s-2$},\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{\alpha_{i}}{j}a_{1,s,k-j}&\textrm{if $i\geq 2$ and $s\geq 2$},\\
\displaystyle\binom{\alpha_{i}}{k+1}&\textrm{if $i\geq 2$ and $s=1$}.\end{array}\right.

We will bound from above the absolute value of the coefficients
a_{i,s,k}
at the end of the present section. Their
p
–adic absolute values are estimated in the proof of Lemma
9.5
, while their denominators are studied in Lemma
9.7
. We have the following key-properties.

Lemma 7.2
.

For each
(i,s)\in\mathcal{S}
and each integer

Lemma 7.8
.

Let

\displaystyle\frac{|(\alpha)_{k}|}{k!}\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}e^{|\alpha|-1}k^{|\alpha|-1}&\textrm{if $|\alpha|>1$ and $k>0$},\\

We obtain the expected upper bounds by combining the above with the estimates

and by distinguishing between the case

Proposition 9.1
.

Let

. Then, for any
(i,s)\in\mathcal{S}
and any integer
\ell

0\leq\ell\leq M
, we have

(62)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}|P_{n,\ell}(x)|^{1/n}

\displaystyle\leq 1,

(63)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}|Q_{n,i,s,\ell}(x)|^{1/n}

\displaystyle\leq\rho(M):=\bigg(2\frac{(M+1)^{M+1}}{M^{M}}\bigg)^{M+1}.

Proof..

Fix
(i,s)\in\mathcal{S}
and integer
\ell

0\leq\ell\leq M
. Let
n
be a positive integer. We first prove (

2
together with the identity

\displaystyle P_{n,\ell}(z)

\displaystyle=\pm\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{n-k}\dfrac{(z-k)_{\ell}}{\ell!}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(\dfrac{(z+\alpha_{j}-k)_{k}(z+\alpha_{j})_{n-k}}{n!}\right)^{m_{j}+1}

\displaystyle=\pm\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{n-k}\dfrac{(z-k)_{\ell}}{\ell!}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(\dfrac{(z+\alpha_{j}-k)_{k}}{k!}\dfrac{(z+\alpha_{j})_{n-k}}{(n-k)!}\binom{n}{k}^{-1}\right)^{m_{j}+1}.

and using Lemma

, we find for

\displaystyle\max\left\{\dfrac{|(x+\alpha_{j}-k)_{k}|}{k!},\dfrac{|(x+\alpha_{j})_{n-k}|}{(n-k)!}\right\}\leq(en)^{|x+\alpha_{j}|}.

We deduce the rough estimate

\displaystyle|P_{n,\ell}(x)|\leq\frac{(|x|+n)^{\ell}}{\ell!}(en)^{\beta}=e^{o(n)},\quad\textrm{where }\beta=2\sum_{j=1}^{d}|x+\alpha_{j}|(m_{j}+1),

hence (
62

2
, we have

\displaystyle Q_{i,s,\ell}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{Mn+{\ell}}p_{n,j,\ell}\cdot b_{j,i,s}(z),

\displaystyle b_{j,i,s}(z):=\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}(-1)^{k+1}a_{i,s,k}\dfrac{(z+k+1)\cdots(z+j-1)}{(k+1)\cdots(j-1)j}

(the coefficients

are as in Definition

(64)

\displaystyle p_{n,j,\ell}=\sum_{k=n}^{j+n}\binom{j+n}{k}(-1)^{n-k}\binom{k}{\ell}\prod_{r=1}^{d}{\binom{k-\alpha_{r}}{n}}^{m_{r}+1}

\displaystyle\dfrac{|(x+k+1)\cdots(x+j-1)|}{(k+1)\cdots(j-1)}\leq\dfrac{(|x|+k+1)\cdots(|x|+j-1)|}{(k+1)\cdots(j-1)}\leq\frac{(|x|)_{j}}{j!}\leq e^{|x|}j^{|x|}.

Combining the above with Lemma
7.9
, we get the estimate

(65)

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j\leq Mn+\ell}|b_{j,i,s}(x)|\leq e^{|x|+|\alpha_{i}|}(Mn+\ell)^{1+s+|x|+|\alpha_{i}|}=e^{o(n)}

as

(66)

\displaystyle\binom{j+n}{k}\binom{k}{\ell}\leq 2^{j+n}k^{\ell}\leq 2^{(M+1)n+\ell}((M+1)n+\ell)^{\ell}=e^{o(n)}2^{(M+1)n}

. Then

(67)

\displaystyle\left|\prod_{r=1}^{d}{\binom{k-\alpha_{r}}{n}}^{m_{r}+1}\right|\leq\prod_{r=1}^{d}{\binom{k+\alpha}{n}}^{m_{r}+1}=\binom{k+\alpha}{n}^{M+1}\leq\binom{(M+1)n+\ell+\alpha}{n}^{M+1}.

\displaystyle\binom{(M+1)n+\ell+\alpha}{n}=\frac{((M+1)n+\ell+\alpha)!}{n!(Mn+\ell+\alpha)!}

\displaystyle=e^{o(n)}\frac{((M+1)n+\ell+\alpha)^{(M+1)n+\ell+\alpha}}{n^{n}(Mn+\ell+\alpha)^{Mn+\ell+\alpha}}

\displaystyle=e^{o(n)}\frac{((M+1)n)^{(M+1)n}}{n^{n}(Mn)^{Mn}}

\displaystyle=e^{o(n)}\left(\frac{(M+1)^{M+1}}{M^{M}}\right)^{n}.

\displaystyle|p_{n,j,\ell}|\leq e^{o(n)}\left(2\frac{(M+1)^{M+1}}{M^{M}}\right)^{(M+1)n}

We keep the notation of Section
7
. Recall that
d,m_{1},\dots,m_{d}
are positive integers, and

The goal of the section is to explain how we can improve the asymptotic estimates (

\displaystyle a_{j}(n,z)=\sum_{k=0}^{M-j}\binom{n}{k}\binom{M-k}{j}\frac{(-1)^{j+k+\ell+Mn+M+n}}{\ell!}\Delta^{k}_{-1}\big((z-n+k)_{\ell}\big).

Proof..

According to Lemma
6.1
, and since
\Delta_{-1}^{k}((z)_{\ell})=0
if
k>\ell
, we have

\displaystyle(-1)^{\ell+Mn+M+n}\ell!P_{n,\ell}(z)=\Delta_{-1}^{n}\big((z)_{\ell}\widehat{A}_{n}(z)\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\Delta^{k}_{-1}\big((z-n+k)_{\ell}\big)\Delta^{n-k}_{-1}\big(\widehat{A}_{n}(z)\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{M}\binom{n}{k}\Delta^{k}_{-1}\big((z-n+k)_{\ell}\big)\Delta^{M-k}_{-1}\big(\widehat{P}_{n}(z)\big).

Using the identity
\Delta_{-1}^{m}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}(-1)^{m-j}\tau_{-1}^{j}
, we find

\displaystyle\Delta^{M-k}_{-1}(\widehat{P}_{n}(z))=\sum_{j=0}^{M-k}\binom{M-k}{j}(-1)^{M-k-j}\widehat{P}_{n}(z-j),

and rearranging the terms in the previous expression, we obtain the expected formula for
P_{n,\ell}(z)
. It follows that

\displaystyle Q_{n,i,s,\ell}(z)=\varphi_{\alpha_{i},s}\bigg(\frac{P_{n,\ell}(z)-P_{n,\ell}(t)}{z-t}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{M}\varphi_{\alpha_{i},s}\bigg(\frac{a_{j}(n,z)\widehat{P}_{n}(z-j)-a_{j}(n,t)\widehat{P}_{n}(t-j)}{z-t}\bigg).

Note that for each
j
, the polynomial
a_{j}(n,z)\in\mathbb{Q}[z]
has degree at most
M
(in the variable
z
), and that according to Lemma
10.5
, we have
t^{k}\widehat{P}_{n}(t-j)\in\ker\varphi_{\alpha_{i},s}
for
k=0,\dots,M-1
. By Lemma
10.6
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\varphi_{\alpha_{i},s}\bigg(\frac{a_{j}(n,z)\widehat{P}_{n}(z-j)-a_{j}(n,t)\widehat{P}_{n}(t-j)}{z-t}\bigg)

\displaystyle=a_{j}(n,z)\varphi_{\alpha_{i},s}\bigg(\frac{\widehat{P}_{n}(z-j)-\widehat{P}_{n}(t-j)}{z-t}\bigg)

\displaystyle=a_{j}(n,z)\widehat{Q}_{n,i,s}(z-j).

∎

Proof of Proposition
10.4
.

This is a consequence of Proposition
10.7
, noticing that for a fixed
x\in\mathbb{Q}
, the polynomials
a_{j}(n,x)
(in the variable
n
) satisfy

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq j\leq M}\limsup_{n\to\infty}|a_{j}(n,x)|^{1/n}=1.

∎

10.2
Poincaré-Perron recurrence

In view of Proposition
10.4
, it remains to estimate the auxiliary sequences introduced in the previous section. This will be done by using Perron’s Second Theorem (see Theorem
10.2
). First, we reduce the problem by proving that it suffices to find a Poincaré-type recurrence for the sequence
\big(\widehat{P}_{n}(z)\big)_{n\geq M}
. The next result ensures that for each
(i,s)\in\mathcal{S}
, the sequence
\big(\widehat{Q}_{n,i,s}(z)\big)_{n\geq M}
will also satisfy the same recurrence.

Proposition 10.8
.

Suppose that there exist integers
J,d_{0},\dots,d_{J}\geq 0
and sequences
\big(a_{j}(n,z)\big)_{\geq n}
in
\mathbb{Q}[z]
for
j=0,\dots,J
with the following properties. For each integer
n\geq M
, we have
a_{j}(n,z)\in\mathbb{Q}[z]
and

\displaystyle\deg a_{j}(n,z)\leq d_{j}\qquad(0\leq j\leq J).

Assume that
\big(\widehat{P}_{n}(z)\big)_{n\geq M}
satisfies the recurrence

(83)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{J}a_{j}(n,z)\widehat{P}_{n+j}(z)=0

for each
n\geq M
. Then, for any
(i,s)\in\mathcal{S}
, the sequence
\big(\widehat{Q}_{n,i,s}(z)\big)_{n\geq M}
also satisfies the recurrence (
83
) for each integer
n\geq M+\max\{0,N\}
, where
N=\max_{0\leq j\leq J}\{d_{j}-j\}
.

Proof..

By hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\frac{\widetilde{P}_{j,n}(z)-\widetilde{P}_{j,n}(t)}{z-t},\qquad\textrm{where }\widetilde{P}_{j,n}(z)=a_{j}(n,z)\widehat{P}_{n+j}(z).

Fix
(i,s)\in\mathcal{S}
. Using Lemmas
10.5
and
10.6
we find

\displaystyle 0=\varphi_{\alpha_{i},s}\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{J}\frac{\widetilde{P}_{j,n}(z)-\widetilde{P}_{j,n}(t)}{z-t}\bigg)=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\varphi_{\alpha_{i},s}\bigg(\frac{\widetilde{P}_{j,n}(z)-\widetilde{P}_{j,n}(t)}{z-t}\bigg)=\sum_{j=0}^{J}a_{j}(n,z)\widehat{Q}_{n+j,i,s}(z),

for each
n\geq M
such that
n+j-M\geq d_{j}
for
j=0,\dots,J
,
i.e.
such that
n\geq M+N
.
∎

Recall that for any integer
n\geq M
, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{P}_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=\Delta^{n-M}_{-1}\left(A_{n}(z)\right)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}F(z,n,k),

where
F(z,n,k)
is the hypergeometric term

\displaystyle F(z,n,k)=\binom{n-M}{k}(-1)^{k}\prod_{r=1}^{d}\binom{z+\alpha_{r}-k-1}{n}^{m_{r}+1},

with the convention that
\binom{n-M}{k}=0
if
k<0
for
k>M-n
. We can show that there exist an explicit integer
J=J(M)
and polynomials
a_{j}(y,z)\in\mathbb{Q}[y,z]
for
j=0,\dots,J
, not all zero, such that (
83
) holds for each integer
n\geq M
. Given a specific
x\in\mathbb{Q}
, we can also ensure that at least one of the coefficient
a_{j}(n,x)
is non-zero. The proof is based on Sister Celine’s Method applied to the hypergeometric term
F(z,n,k)
(see
[
34
, Theorem 4.4.1]
), together with the arguments used in the proof of
[
34
, Theorem 6.2.1]
(which provides an explicit bound for the order
J
of the recurrence). Numerical computations for small values of
M
suggest that we can always take

\displaystyle J(M)=M+1.

Then, we can compute
J
and the coefficients
a_{j}(n,z)
in (
83
) by using Zeilberger’s algorithm
[
34
, Chapter 6]
and the software MAPLE for small values of
M
.

Proof of Theorem

Let
d,m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}
be positive integers and
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
with
\alpha_{1}=0
and
\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}\notin\mathbb{Z}
for any
i\neq j
. Put
m=\max_{1\leq i\leq d}m_{i}
,
M=\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}+d-1
,
\boldsymbol{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{d})
, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=\{(i,s)\,;\,1\leq i\leq d\and 1\leq s\leq m_{i}+1\}\setminus\{(1,1)\}.

Recall that
q_{p}=p
if
p\geq 3
and
q_{p}=4
if
p=2
. The function
\mu
is defined in (
6
). Define

\displaystyle f(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{m})=g(M)+M\bigg(1+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\dfrac{1}{j}\bigg)+\log\Big(\mu(\boldsymbol{\alpha})^{M}\prod_{i=2}^{d}\mu(\alpha_{i})^{m_{i}+1}\Big)-\log|\mu(\boldsymbol{\alpha})|_{p},

where the function
g
is as in (
2
), namely
g(M)=0
if
M\leq 2
and

\displaystyle g(M)=(M+1)\log\bigg(\frac{2(M+1)^{M+1}}{M^{M}}\bigg)\qquad\textrm{if $M>2$.}

Note that
f(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{m})
is equal to
f(\boldsymbol{\alpha},m)
defined in (
7
) when
m_{i}=m
for each
i
.
In this section, we prove the following stronger version of Theorem
1.4
(which corresponds to the case
\boldsymbol{m}=(m,\dots,m)
).

Theorem 11.1
.

Let
p
be a prime number and
x\in\mathbb{Q}
satisfying

(88)

\displaystyle|x|_{p}\geq q_{p}\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\{1,|\alpha_{i}|_{p}\}

and

(89)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\log p}{p-1}+\log|x|_{p}>M\log\big(\mu(x)|\mu(x)|_{p}\big)+f(\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{m}).

Then the
m_{1}+\cdots+m_{d}+1
elements of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}

\displaystyle 1,G_{p}^{(2)}(x+\alpha_{1}),\ldots,G_{p}^{(m_{1}+1)}(x+\alpha_{1}),\ldots,G_{p}^{(2)}(x+\alpha_{d}),\ldots,G_{p}^{(m_{d}+1)}(x+\alpha_{d})

are linearly independent of
\mathbb{Q}
.

We will deduce Theorem
11.1
from the next theorem. For any non-negative integers
\ell,n
with
0\leq\ell\leq M
and each
(i,s)\in\mathcal{S}
, the polynomials
P_{n,\ell}(z)
,
Q_{n,i,s,\ell}(z)
, and the Padé approximation
\mathfrak{R}_{n,i,s,\ell}(z)=P_{n,\ell}(z)R_{\alpha_{i},s}(z)-Q_{n,i,s,\ell}(z)
of
R_{\alpha_{i},s}(z)
are defined in Theorem
7.3
. Recall that
R_{\alpha,s}
is as in Definition
5.1
.

Theorem 11.2
.

Let
p
be a prime number and
x\in\mathbb{Q}
satisfying

(90)

\displaystyle|x|_{p}\geq q_{p}\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\{1,|\alpha_{i}|_{p}\}.

Let
\beta,\rho_{\infty},\rho_{p},\delta
be real numbers and
(D_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence of positive integers such that, for each
(i,s)\in\mathcal{S}
and each
\ell=0,\dots,M
, the numbers
D_{n}P_{n,\ell}(x)
and
D_{n}Q_{n,i,s,\ell}(x)
are integers (for each integer
n\geq 0
) and

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\max\big\{|P_{n,\ell}(x)|,|Q_{n,i,s,\ell}(x)|\big\}^{1/n}\leq e^{\beta},

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}D_{n}^{1/n}\leq e^{\rho_{\infty}},

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}|D_{n}|_{p}^{1/n}\leq e^{-\rho_{p}},

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}|\mathfrak{R}_{n,i,s,\ell}(x)|_{p}^{1/n}\leq e^{-\delta}.

Suppose that
\beta+\rho_{\infty}<\delta+\rho_{p}
. Then the
M+1
elements
(1,R_{\alpha_{i},s}(x))_{(i,s)\in\mathcal{S}}
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof..

The proof is classical, see for example
[
14
, Proposition
5.7
]
(in the case
K=\mathbb{Q}
,
v_{0}=p
), which also provides an effective irrationality measure result. For the sake of completion we recall the arguments. First, note that the condition (
90
) ensures that
|x+\alpha_{i}|_{p}=|x|_{p}\geq q_{p}>1
for
i=1,\dots,d
, so that
R_{\alpha_{i},s}(x)
converges
p
–adically for each
(i,s)\in\mathcal{S}
by Lemma
5.2
. If
p
does not divide the denominator
\mathrm{den}(\alpha_{i})
of
\alpha_{i}
, we have
|x|_{p}|\cdot|\mu(\alpha_{i})|_{p}=|x|_{p}>1
. If
p
divides
\mathrm{den}(\alpha_{i})
, then

|\mu(\alpha_{i})|_{p}=|\mathrm{den}(\alpha_{i})|_{p}p^{-1/(p-1)}=|\alpha_{i}|_{p}^{-1}p^{-1/(p-1)}.

and (
90
) implies that
|x|_{p}\cdot|\mu(\alpha_{i})|_{p}\geq q_{p}p^{-1/(p-1)}>1
. Consequently, the series
\mathfrak{R}_{n,i,s,\ell}(x)
converges
p
–adically, see Proposition
9.2
. By contradiction, suppose that
(1,R_{\alpha_{i},s}(x))_{(i,s)\in\mathcal{S}}
are linearly dependent over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then, there exists
(b,b_{i,s})_{(i,s)\in\mathcal{S}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{M+1}\setminus\{0\}
such that

(91)

\displaystyle b+\sum_{(i,s)\in\mathcal{S}}b_{i,s}R_{\alpha_{i},s}(x)=0.

Given a positive integer
n
, define

\displaystyle\widehat{p}_{n,\ell}:=D_{n}P_{n,\ell}(x)\and\widehat{q}_{n,i,s,\ell}:=D_{n}Q_{n,i,s,\ell}(x),

for each
(i,s)\in\mathcal{S}
and each
\ell=0,\dots,M
. By hypothesis
\widehat{p}_{n,\ell}
and
\widehat{q}_{n,i,s,\ell}
are integers. Theorem
8.10
implies that the
(M+1)\times(M+1)
matrix

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{p}_{n,\ell}\\
\widehat{q}_{n,i,s,\ell}\end{pmatrix}_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\ell\leq M\\
(i,s)\in\mathcal{S}\end{subarray}}

is non-singular. Consequently, there exists an integer
\ell
with
0\leq\ell\leq M
such that

\displaystyle K_{n}=K_{n}(\ell):=b\widehat{p}_{n,\ell}+\sum_{(i,s)\in\mathcal{S}}b_{i,s}\widehat{q}_{n,i,s,\ell}

is a non-zero integer. Our hypothesis implies that
\limsup_{n\to\infty}|K_{n}|^{1/n}\leq e^{\beta+\rho_{\infty}}
. Since
|K_{n}||K_{n}|_{p}\geq 1
, it follows that

(92)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}|K_{n}|_{p}^{1/n}\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}|K_{n}|^{-1/n}\geq e^{-(\beta+\rho_{\infty})}.

On the other hand, using (
91
), we find

\displaystyle K_{n}=K_{n}-\widehat{p}_{n,\ell}\Big(b+\sum_{(i,s)\in\mathcal{S}}b_{i,s}R_{\alpha_{i},s}(x)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(i,s)\in\mathcal{S}}b_{i,s}(\widehat{q}_{n,i,s,\ell}-R_{\alpha_{i},s}(x)\widehat{p}_{n,\ell})

\displaystyle=\sum_{(i,s)\in\mathcal{S}}b_{i,s}D_{n}\mathfrak{R}_{n,i,s,\ell}(x),

from which we deduce the upper bound

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}|K_{n}|_{p}^{1/n}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}|D_{n}\mathfrak{R}_{n,i,s,\ell}(x)|_{p}^{1/n}\leq e^{-\delta-\rho_{p}}.

Together with (
92
), we deduce that
e^{-(\beta+\rho_{\infty})}\leq e^{-(\delta+\rho_{p})}
, which contradicts our hypothesis
\beta+\rho_{\infty}<\delta+\rho_{p}
.
∎

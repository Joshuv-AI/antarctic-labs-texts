Eisenstein cohomology and congruences for the ratios of Rankin–Selberg L L -functions
P. Narayanan & A. Raghuram

Abstract
. A well-known principle states that a congruence between objects should give rise to a corresponding congruence between the special values of L L -functions attached to these objects. In this article, using the machinery of Eisenstein cohomology after refining it for integral cohomology, we prove an instance of this principle for the ratios of critical values for Rankin–Selberg L L -functions attached to pairs of holomorphic cuspforms.

Introduction

A principle, with origins in Iwasawa theory, says that a congruence between objects should give rise to a congruence between
the special values of
L
-functions attached to these objects. In this article, we prove an instance of this principle: that a congruence
between modular forms gives rise to a congruence between the ratio of special values of Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions of these forms with an auxiliary modular form.

To state our results more precisely, suppose
h(z)=\sum a(n,h)e^{2\pi inz}

and
h^{\prime}(z)=\sum a(n,h^{\prime})e^{2\pi inz}
are primitive holomorphic modular cuspforms of levels
N
and
N^{\prime}
, weights

k
and
k^{\prime}
, with nebentypus characters
\chi
and
\chi^{\prime},
respectively. Denote this as

h\in S_{k}(N,\chi)^{\textup{new}}
and
h^{\prime}\in S_{k^{\prime}}(N^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})^{\textup{new}}
.
Let
\mathbb{Q}(h,h^{\prime})
be the number field obtained by adjoining the Fourier coefficients
\{a(n,h)\}
and
\{a(n,h^{\prime})\}
to
\mathbb{Q}.

Assume that
k^{\prime}>k.
A well-known theorem of Shimura
[
19
]
says that for

D_{N}(s,h,h^{\prime}),
the degree-
4
Rankin–Selberg
L
-function attached to the pair
(h,h^{\prime})
, and for any integer
m
with

k\leq m<k^{\prime},
we have:

D_{N}(m,h,h^{\prime})\ \approx\ (2\pi i)^{l+1-2m}\,\mathfrak{g}(\chi)\,u^{+}(f^{\prime})u^{-}(f^{\prime}),

where
\approx
means equality up to an element of
\mathbb{Q}(h,h^{\prime})
,
u^{\pm}(h^{\prime})
are the two periods attached to
h^{\prime}
by Shimura, and

\mathfrak{g}(\chi)
is the Gauss sum of
\chi.

The integers
k\leq m<k^{\prime}
are all the critical points for
D_{N}(s,h,h^{\prime}).

Suppose
k^{\prime}\geq k+2
, and we look at two successive critical values, then the only change in the right hand side is

(2\pi i)^{-2}
which may be seen to be exactly accounted for
by the
\Gamma
-factors at infinity. Suppose
L(s,h\times h^{\prime})
denotes the completed degree-
4

L
-function attached to
(h,h^{\prime})
,
then we deduce, for any
m
with
k\leq m<m+1<k^{\prime},
that

\frac{L(m,h\times h^{\prime})}{L(m+1,h\times h^{\prime})}\in\mathbb{Q}(h,h^{\prime}).

Now suppose
h^{\prime\prime}\in S_{k^{\prime}}(N^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})^{\textup{new}}.
We say
h^{\prime\prime}
is congruent to
h^{\prime}
if in some number field
E
, large enough to contain all the rationality fields,
and some prime ideal
\mathfrak{l}
of the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{E}
of
E
, the congruence
a(n,h^{\prime})\equiv a(n,h^{\prime\prime})\pmod{\mathfrak{l}}
holds for all
n
.
Both the
L
-functions

and
L(s,h\times h^{\prime\prime})
have the same set of critical points.
Under some general hypotheses we prove:

(1)

h^{\prime}\equiv h^{\prime\prime}\pmod{\mathfrak{l}}\ \implies\ \frac{L(m,h^{\prime}\times h)}{L(m+1,h^{\prime}\times h)}\equiv\frac{L(m,h^{\prime\prime}\times h)}{L(m+1,h^{\prime\prime}\times h)}\pmod{\mathfrak{l}}.

This is proven in Thm.
8.3

when
\mathfrak{l}
is away from a finite set of primes (about which we will say more presently),
the mod-
\mathfrak{l}
Galois representations attached to
h^{\prime}
and
h^{\prime\prime}
are absolutely irreducible,
and the levels
N
and
N^{\prime}
are square-free and relatively prime.
If we drop the hypothesis on the levels then in Thm.
8.2
a weaker congruence is proven.
A natural variation by taking the congruent modular forms to be lower weight, i.e., if
k^{\prime}<k
, is addressed in Thm.
8.4
.

In a companion paper
[
13
]
, we have computationally verified such a congruence in several concrete examples. The methods we use
in this paper proving the theorems are completely independent of the companion paper.
The reader is also referred to Vatsal
[
21
]
in which is proved that a congruence between modular forms gives rise to a
congruence between special values of their degree-
2

L
-functions divided by certain periods provided the periods are canonically chosen.
Vatsal’s theorem was a major motivation for our own work. In our situation, one of the advantages is that for ratios of
L
-values
there is no need to appeal to any periods, and their canonical normalization is a moot point.
The ratio of successive
L
-values appears naturally in Langlands’s famous constant term theorem.
In the work of the second author with Harder
[
6
]

this theorem of Langlands was interpreted in the general framework of Eisenstein cohomology.
We prove the main results of this paper (Thm.
8.2
, Thm.
8.3
)
using the tools developed in
[
6
]
but after refining that framework to deal with integral cohomology.

In results concerning congruences and special values of
L
-functions, the congruence prime
\mathfrak{l}
is usually assumed to be not among a finite set of primes;
see, for example, Hida
[
8
]
, Vatsal
[
21
]
, or Balasubramanyam and Raghuram
[
2
]
.
For the main results of our paper, for similar reasons, we need to avoid certain finite sets of primes: (i)
small primes with respect to the weight
\mathsf{S}_{\textup{weight}}=\{\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{E}\ |\ p\leq k,\ p\leq k^{\prime}\};

(ii) primes diving the levels
\mathsf{S}_{\textup{level}}=\{\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathcal{O}_{E}\>\>|\>\>p|NN^{\prime}\};

(iii) primes supporting torsion in integral Eisenstein cohomology
\mathsf{S}_{\textup{Eis}}
(see
6.2
for the precise definition);
(iv) primes coming from archimedean considerations
\mathsf{S}_{c_{\infty}}.

For the introduction, let us now adumbrate the proofs.
Under the hypotheses of the theorems, the congruent modular forms
h^{\prime}
and
h^{\prime\prime}
give rise to
two cohomology classes that are congruent modulo
\mathfrak{l}
; this is possible since
the Hecke algebra is Gorenstein; see Sect.
3
. These cohomology classes are in the inner cohomology of a locally symmetric space for

\mathrm{GL}(2)
with coefficients in an integral local system attached to a lattice in a highest weight module determined by the weight
k^{\prime}
.
Tensoring with a similarly defined cohomology class attached to
h
, gives rise to cohomology classes on
\mathrm{GL}(2)\times\mathrm{GL}(2)

which are congruent modulo
\mathfrak{l}
. We then go through the formalism of rank-one
Eisenstein cohomology of
[
6
]
on
\mathrm{GL}(4)
for the
(2,2)
-parabolic subgroup.
The configuration of maps in (6.18) of
loc. cit.
is then reworked at an integral level for this specific context.
This configuration of maps is at the heart of affording a cohomological interpretation of Langlands’s theorem mentioned above:
the constant term of an Eisenstein series is essentially the standard intertwining operator between two induced representations.
While working integrally, one needs a delicate control on integral structures on cohomology groups and such induced representations
which necessitates avoiding a finish set
\mathsf{S}_{\textup{Eis}}
of primes. One also needs delicate control on the local standard
intertwining operator; at the archimedean place it gives rise to a rational number forcing us to avoid the finite set
\mathsf{S}_{c_{\infty}}
of primes supporting that number;
and at finite ramified primes the local computation is explicitly carried out, but only in the presence of mild ramification explaining the condition on the levels
N
and
N^{\prime}
.

H^{1}_{!}(S_{1}^{(2)}(N),\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu(m),E})
(resp.
H^{1}_{!}(S_{1}^{(2)}(N),\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu(m),E_{\mathfrak{l}}})
) with
m\in\mathbb{Z}.
The notations

\tilde{H}^{1}_{!}(S^{(2)}_{1}(N),\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu,\mathcal{O}_{E}})
and
\tilde{H}^{1}_{!}(S^{(2)}_{1}(N),\widetilde{M}_{\mu,\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{l}}})

will be used to denote the images repsecitively. The is done to ensure there are no torsion cohomology classes. If we avoid a suitable finite set of primes as in

2.3.4
then there is no
\mathfrak{l}
-torsion and we may simplify the notation
\tilde{H}^{1}_{!}(...)
to
H^{1}_{!}(...)
. It should be kept in mind that
the twists appear when one considers the action of an integral Hecke algebra on integral cohomology.

In general we shall reserve the notation
\tilde{H}^{\bullet}(...)
to denote the image of the cohomology with integral coefficients inside the cohomology with rational coefficients. This is done to kill any torsion.

2.4.
Cohomology of the Levi quotient
M_{P}=G_{2}\times G_{2}

2.4.1.
Künneth isomorphisms

The weights
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
give a highest weight
\mu+\mu^{\prime}
for
M_{P}
. For
A=E,E_{\mathfrak{l}},\mathbb{C},
let
H^{2}(S^{M_{P}}_{N\times N^{\prime}},\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu+\mu^{\prime},A})

denote the cohomology groups at degree
2
of the locally
symmetric space associated to the Levi
M_{P}
with level structure
K_{1}(N)\times K_{1}(N^{\prime}),

and coefficient system
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu+\mu^{\prime},A}
.
If the weights
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
are regular, then the notion of inner and strongly inner cohomology in
[
6
]
coincide.
Moreover, we have a Künneth isomorphism:

H^{2}_{!}(S^{M_{P}}_{N\times N^{\prime}},\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu+\mu^{\prime},A})\cong H^{1}_{!}(S_{1}^{(2)}(N),\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu,A})\otimes_{A}H^{1}_{!}(S_{1}^{(2)}(N^{\prime}),\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu^{\prime},A}).

2.4.2.
Integral structures

Via the Künneth isomorphism above, the image of

\tilde{H}^{1}_{!}(S_{1}^{(2)}(N^{\prime}),\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu,\mathcal{O}_{E}})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{E}}\tilde{H}^{1}_{!}(S_{1}^{(2)}(N^{\prime}),\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{E}})\ \hookrightarrow\ H^{1}_{!}(S^{M_{P}}_{N\times N^{\prime}},\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu+\mu^{\prime},E})

gives an
\mathcal{O}_{E}
–lattice
which will be denoted
\tilde{H}^{2}_{!}(S^{M_{P}}_{N^{\prime}\times N},\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu^{\prime}+\mu,\mathcal{O}_{E}})
.
Similarly, an
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{l}}
lattice:

\tilde{H}^{2}_{!}(S^{M_{P}}_{N\times N^{\prime}},\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu+\mu^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{E_{\mathfrak{l}}}})\ \subset\ \tilde{H}^{2}_{!}(S^{M_{P}}_{N\times N^{\prime}},\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu+\mu^{\prime},E_{\mathfrak{l}}}).

Suppose for the moment,
R
and
S
are commutative rings with
1
and
R\rightarrow S
is a ring homomorphism, and if
M
and
N
are
R
-modules then

(M\otimes_{R}N)\otimes_{R}S\cong(M\otimes_{R}S)\otimes_{S}(N\otimes_{R}S).

Applying this for
\mathcal{O}_{E}\hookrightarrow A
, for
A=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{l}},E,E_{\mathfrak{l}},\mathbb{C},
we get

(6)

\tilde{H}^{2}_{!}(S^{M_{P}}_{N\times N^{\prime}},\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu+\mu^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{E}})\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{E}}A\cong\tilde{H}^{2}_{!}(S^{M_{P}}_{N\times N^{\prime}},\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mu+\mu^{\prime},A}).

There will be variations on the cohomology of the Levi (as when we look at both sides of an intertwining operator),
but the same recipe as above will be adopted for all variations.

2.5.
Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions

2.5.1.
Classical Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions

For integers
N^{\prime},N\geq 1
, Dirichlet characters
\chi^{\prime}
and
\chi
of levels
N^{\prime}
and
N
, respectively, and
integers
k^{\prime}>k\geq 2
consider primitive forms

h^{\prime}\in S_{k^{\prime}}(N^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})^{\textup{new}}
and
h\in S_{k}(N,\chi)^{\textup{new}}
, with Fourier expansions:

h^{\prime}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a(n,h^{\prime})q^{n}
and
h(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a(n,h)q^{n},
where as usual
q=e^{2\pi iz}.

By primitive one means an eigenform, new form, and normalized as
a_{1}=1
.
For
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(s)\gg 0
define the finite-part of the Rankin–Selberg
L
-function as a Dirichlet series:

L^{(\infty)}(s,h\times h^{\prime})\ :=\ L^{(M)}(s,\chi\chi^{\prime})\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(h)a_{n}(h^{\prime})n^{-s}\right),

where
M
is the LCM of
N
and
N^{\prime},
and
L^{(M)}(s,\chi^{\prime}\chi)
denotes the Dirichlet
L
-function attached to the character
\chi\chi^{\prime}

of level
M
with the Euler factors at
p|M
deleted.
From Shimura
[
20
, Lem. 1]
one has an Euler product:
L^{(\infty)}(s,h\times h^{\prime})=\Pi_{p<\infty}L_{p}(s,h\times h^{\prime}).

Keeping the assumption
k^{\prime}>k
in mind, the archimedean factor is defined by:

L_{\infty}(s,h\times h^{\prime})\ :=\ (2\pi)^{-2s}\Gamma(s)\Gamma(s+1-k).

The completed
L
-function is defined by:
L(s,h\times h^{\prime})=L_{\infty}(s,h\times h^{\prime})L^{(\infty)}(s,h\times h^{\prime}).

It is well-known that
L(s,h\times h^{\prime})
can be analytically continued to all of the complex plane, and satisfies a functional equation, towards which,
define the action of complex conjugation via:

h^{\prime\rho}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{a(n,h^{\prime})}q^{n}
and
h^{\rho}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{a(n,h)}q^{n}.

Then
h^{\prime}
and
h
are newforms in
S_{k^{\prime}}(N^{\prime},\chi^{\prime-1})
and
S_{k}(N,\chi^{-1}),
respectively;
see, for example, Miyake
[
12
, Thm. 4.6.15]
.
Also,
\overline{a(p,h)}=\chi(p)^{-1}a(p,h)
and
\overline{a(p,h^{\prime})}=\chi(p)^{-1}a_{p}(h^{\prime});
see, for example,
Shimura
[
18
, Prop. 3.56]
. The functional equation then can be roughly stated as

L(k^{\prime}+k-1-s,h\times h^{\prime})\ \approx\ L(s,h^{\rho}\times h^{\prime\rho}).

See Hida
[
7
, Thm. 9.1]
for the precise factors involved. For our purposes it is enough to observe that for ratios of

L
-functions one has:

(7)

\dfrac{L(k^{\prime}+k-1-s,h\times h^{\prime})}{L(k^{\prime}+k-s,h\times h^{\prime})}=c(N,N^{\prime},k,k^{\prime})\dfrac{L(s,h^{\rho}\times h^{\prime\rho})}{L(s-1,h^{\rho}\times h^{\prime\rho})},

where
c(N,N^{\prime},k,k^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
is a constant which depends only on the prime factors of
N
and
N^{\prime}
and on the weights
k^{\prime}
and
k
.

2.5.2.
Critical points for classical Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions

The line of symmetry for the functional equation is
\Re(s)=(k+k^{\prime}-1)/2.

An integer
m
is
critical
for
L(s,h\times h^{\prime}),
if the archimedean factors on both sides of the functional equation are finite at
s=m,
i.e.,
if
\Gamma(m)\Gamma(m+1-k)
and
\Gamma(k+k^{\prime}-1-m)\Gamma(k^{\prime}-m)
are finite. Therefore the critical set is:

(8)

\{m\in\mathbb{Z}\,|\,k\leq m\leq k^{\prime}-1\}.

The number of critical points is
k^{\prime}-k
. The condition
k^{\prime}>k
was imposed to guarantee the existence of critical points.
For the main results on congruences for ratios of successive critical values, we will furthermore need to assume that
k^{\prime}-k\geq 2.

2.5.3.
Relation between classical and automorphic
L
-functions

Given primitive forms
h\in S_{k}(N,\chi)^{\textup{new}}
and
h^{\prime}\in S_{k^{\prime}}(N^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})^{\textup{new}}
as above,
consider highest weights

\mu=\mu_{k}=(k-2,0)
and
\mu^{\prime}=\mu_{k^{\prime}}=(k^{\prime}-2,0),
and Hecke modules in inner cohomology

\sigma\in\text{Coh}_{!}(G_{2},\mu)
and
\sigma^{\prime}\in\text{Coh}_{!}(G_{2},\mu^{\prime})
, and an embedding

\iota:E\to\mathbb{C}
be taken such that

(9)

{{}^{\iota}}\sigma\cong\Pi(h)|\cdot|^{-k/2+1},\quad{{}^{\iota}}\sigma^{\prime}\cong\Pi(h^{\prime\rho})|\cdot|^{-k^{\prime}/2+1}.

where
\Pi(h)
and
\Pi(h^{\prime\rho})
are the cuspidal automorphic representations attached to
h
and
h^{\prime\rho}
respectively.
The reason for taking
h^{\prime\rho}
for
\sigma^{\prime}
(instead of
h^{\prime}
itself) will become clear in (
10
) below.
The reader is referred to Raghuram and Tanabe
[
15
]
for details of the dictionary between the modular forms and cohomological cuspidal representations.
In particular, one has the following relations:

L(s,\sigma)\ =\ L(s+\frac{1}{2},h),\quad L(s,\sigma^{\sf v})\ =\ L(s+k-\frac{3}{2},h),

and similarly for
\sigma^{\prime}
,
h^{\prime}
and
k^{\prime}
. Furthermore, for an integer
m
, the Tate-twist
\sigma(-m)
has cohomology with respect to
\mu(m)
,
then we have the following relation between the automorphic-representation theoretic and the classical
Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions:

(10)

L(s,\sigma(-m)\times\sigma^{\prime\sf v})\ =\ L(s+k^{\prime}-m-1,h\times h^{\prime}).

All this applies just the same to the pair
h\in S_{k}(N,\chi)^{\textup{new}}
and
h^{\prime\prime}\in S_{k^{\prime}}(N^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})^{\textup{new}}
.

2.5.4.
Setting-up the context of Eisenstein cohomology

To apply the machinery of
[
6
]
, we will be looking at the intertwining operator:

T_{\rm st}(s)|_{s=-2}:{}^{\rm a}{\rm Ind}_{P}^{G}\left(\sigma(-m)\times\sigma^{\prime})\right)\ \longrightarrow\ {}^{\rm a}{\rm Ind}_{P}^{G}(\sigma^{\prime}(2)\times\sigma(-m-2)),

which in
loc. cit.
, gives a rationality result for the ratio:

\frac{L(-2,\sigma(-m)\times\sigma^{\prime\sf v})}{L(-1,\sigma(-m)\times\sigma^{\prime\sf v})}\ =\ \frac{L(-2-m,\sigma\times\sigma^{\prime\sf v})}{L(-1-m,\sigma\times\sigma^{\prime\sf v})}\ =\ \frac{L(k^{\prime}-m-3,h\times h^{\prime})}{L(k^{\prime}-m-2,h\times h^{\prime})},

provided
m
satisfies the constraints imposed by combinatorial lemma which is exactly equivalent to the above
L
-values being critical; from
(
8
) this imposes the following bounds on permissible Tate-twists
m
:

-1\ \leq\ m\ \leq\ k^{\prime}-k-3.

Furthermore, to carry out
[
6
]
, the data
(\mu_{k}(m),\mu_{k^{\prime}})
needs to be on the right of the unitary axis
(required for a certain Eisenstein series to to be holomorphic), which is the condition:

-2+\frac{k^{\prime}-2}{2}-\left(\frac{k-2}{2}+m\right)\geq 0\quad\iff\quad m\leq\frac{k^{\prime}-k}{2}-2.

Hence, as
m
varies from
-1
to
\frac{k^{\prime}-k}{2}-2
, we are looking at the string of ratios of
L
-values from the rightmost up to a little more than the central value:

\frac{L(k^{\prime}-2,h\times h^{\prime})}{L(k^{\prime}-1,h\times h^{\prime})},\ \ \frac{L(k^{\prime}-3,h\times h^{\prime})}{L(k^{\prime}-2,h\times h^{\prime})},\ \dots,\ \ \frac{L(\lfloor\frac{k+k^{\prime}-1}{2}\rfloor,h\times h^{\prime})}{L(\lfloor\frac{k+k^{\prime}+1}{2}\rfloor,h\times h^{\prime})}.

If we are on the left of the unitary axis, then reversing the direction of the intertwining operator and using the functional equation
offers the possibility of a result for all successive ratios critical values exactly as in
[
6
]
; see the discussion in Sect.
8.2
.

3.
Hecke algebras and Gorenstein property

3.1.
Classical Hecke algebras

For
A=\mathcal{O}_{E},E,\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{l}},E_{\mathfrak{l}}
, define
A
sub-modules
S_{k}({N},A)=\{f=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a(n,f)q^{n}\in A[[q]]\;|\;\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{{}^{\iota}}a(n,f)q^{n}\in S_{k}({N})\}
of the space of classical cusp forms
S_{k}({N}):=S_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(N)).
Recall a theorem of Shimura, Deligne, Rapoport and Katz; see Hida
[
10
, Chapter 3]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
A=\mathcal{O}_{E},E,\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{l}},E_{\mathfrak{l}}
the space
S_{k}({N},A)
is an
A
-module of full rank in
S_{k}({N})
, i.e.,

(11)

S_{k}({N},A)\otimes_{\iota}\mathbb{C}=S_{k}({N}).

For
A=\mathcal{O}_{E},E,\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{l}},E_{\mathfrak{l}}
define
h_{k}({N},A)\subset\text{End}_{A}(S_{k}({N},A))
to be the usual Hecke algebra generated by the operators
T(p)
for all primes
p
and
T(p,p)
for
p\nmid N.
Due to the perfect pairing
(\cdot,\cdot):S_{k}(N,A)\times h_{k}(N,A)\rightarrow A,\,\,(f,T)\mapsto a(1,f|T)
, Hida
[
10
, Theorem 3.17]
, one gets
h_{k}({N},\mathcal{O}_{E})\otimes A=h_{k}({N},A)
for
A=E,\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{l}},E_{\mathfrak{l}},\mathbb{C}
and

(12)

\displaystyle S_{k}(N,A)\cong\textup{Hom}_{A}(h_{k}(N,A),A)\,\,\text{ (as $h_{k}(N,A)$--modules) }.

3.2.
Formalism of a Gorenstein datum

Suppose
R
is the ring of integers of a local field of characteristic
0
and

\mathfrak{l}\subset R
will be the unique maximal principal ideal of
R
.
Let
\mathbb{T}
be a commutative
R
-algebra with
1
which is also finite and free as an
R
-module. Since
R
is complete

\mathbb{T}
is complete as well. It is well known that
\mathbb{T}
has only finitely many maximal ideals and each such ideal

\mathfrak{L}
defines an idempotent
e_{\mathfrak{L}}\in\mathbb{T}.
Furthermore,
e_{\mathfrak{L}}\mathbb{T}\cong\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}}
and

\mathbb{T}=\sum_{\mathfrak{L}}e_{\mathfrak{L}}\mathbb{T}\cong\oplus_{\mathfrak{L}}\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}},
where the sum is over the finitely many maximal ideals.
Let
H
be a
fixed
free
R
–module and also a
\mathbb{T}
–module (not necessarily free) and
\mathfrak{L}\subset\mathbb{T}
a
fixed
maximal ideal.
Observe
H_{\mathfrak{L}}\cong e_{\mathfrak{L}}H
and so
H_{\mathfrak{L}}\hookrightarrow H.

In applications we will be under the following assumptions:

(1)

There is a
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}}
equivariant isomorphism
\Phi_{\mathfrak{L}}:\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}}\xrightarrow{\sim}H_{\mathfrak{L}}
,

(2)

\text{Hom}_{R-\text{mod}}\left(\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}},R\right)\cong\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}}\;\;\;\text{as $\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}}$ modules}.

The second assumption is the definition of a ring (here
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}}
) being Gorenstein. Hereafter call the tuple
(R,\mathbb{T},H,\mathfrak{L})
which satisfies the the above assumptions to be a
freely Gorenstein datum
.

3.3.
Presence of two congruent morphisms

Assume now there are two
distinct

R
-algebra morphisms
\Theta^{\prime},\Theta^{\prime\prime}:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow R

such that their compositions with the map
R\rightarrow R/\mathfrak{l}
are the same, i.e.,

\overline{\Theta}^{\prime}=\overline{\Theta}^{\prime\prime},

where,
\overline{\Theta}^{\prime}:={\Theta^{\prime}}\pmod{\mathfrak{l}}
and
\overline{\Theta}^{\prime\prime}:={\Theta^{\prime\prime}}\pmod{\mathfrak{l}};

the kernels of
\overline{\Theta}^{\prime}
and
\overline{\Theta}^{\prime\prime}
are the same;
put
\mathfrak{L}:=\ker{\overline{\Theta}^{\prime}=\ker{\overline{\Theta}^{\prime\prime}}}.
Hence the morphisms
\Theta^{\prime}
and
\Theta^{\prime\prime}
factors through
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}}
which will be denoted by the same symbols. It will be
assumed
that
(R,\mathbb{T},H,\mathfrak{L})
is a freely-Gorenstein datum.

Lemma 3.1
.

Under the
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}}
-equivariant isomorphism, say
\Psi_{\mathfrak{L}}:\text{Hom}_{R-\text{mod}}\left(\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}},R\right)\cong\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}}
, one has

\displaystyle\Psi_{\mathfrak{L}}(\Theta^{\prime}),\Psi_{\mathfrak{L}}(\Theta^{\prime\prime})\not\in\mathfrak{l}\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}},\quad\Psi_{\mathfrak{L}}(\Theta^{\prime})-\Psi_{\mathfrak{L}}(\Theta^{\prime\prime})\in\mathfrak{l}\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}}.

The algebra
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}}
acts on
\Psi_{\mathfrak{L}}(\Theta^{\prime})
and
\Psi_{\mathfrak{L}}(\Theta^{\prime\prime})
by the characters

\Theta^{\prime}
and
\Theta^{\prime\prime}
respectively.

Proof.

The natural map

\text{Hom}_{R-\text{mod}}(\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}},R)\otimes_{R}R/\mathfrak{l}\longrightarrow\text{Hom}_{R-\text{mod}}(\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}},R/\mathfrak{l})

is an isomorphism. Since
\mathbb{T}
is a free
R
-module, fix an
R
basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}}
to get a dual basis
\{e_{i}^{\vee}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of
\text{Hom}_{R-\text{mod}}(\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}},R).

Suppose
\sum_{i}r_{i}e_{i}^{\vee}
maps to the
0
element of
\text{Hom}_{R-\text{mod}}(\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}},R/\mathfrak{l}).
Then evaluating at
e_{i}
we see that
r_{i}\in\mathfrak{l}
for all
i=1,\cdots,n.
Therefore

\left(\sum_{i}r_{i}e_{i}^{\vee}\right)\otimes 1=0\text{ in }\text{Hom}_{R-\text{mod}}(\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}},R)\otimes_{R}R/\mathfrak{l}
which proves injectivity. Using the dual basis
e_{i}^{\vee}
one can show it is surjective as well.

Due to the isomorphism
\Psi_{\mathfrak{L}}
we get the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\text{Hom}_{R-\text{mod}}\left(\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}},R\right)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\text{Hom}_{R-\text{mod}}(\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}},R)\otimes_{R}R/\mathfrak{l}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}}\otimes_{R}R/\mathfrak{l}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\text{Hom}_{R-\text{mod}}(\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}},R/\mathfrak{l})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}}/\mathfrak{l}\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}}.}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim\;\;\text{via}\;\;\Psi_{\mathfrak{L}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim\;\;\text{via}\;\;\overline{\Psi}_{\mathfrak{L}}}

Since
\overline{\Theta}^{\prime}=\overline{\Theta}^{\prime\prime}
in
\text{Hom}_{R-\text{mod}}(\mathtt{T}_{\mathfrak{L}},R/\mathfrak{l})
we have that
\overline{\Psi}_{\mathfrak{L}}(\overline{\Theta}^{\prime})-\overline{\Psi}_{\mathfrak{L}}(\overline{\Theta}^{\prime\prime})=\overline{\Psi}_{\mathfrak{L}}(\overline{\Theta}^{\prime}-\overline{\Theta}^{\prime\prime})=0
in
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}}/\mathfrak{l}\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}}.
Due to the commutativity of the above diagram the first assertion is true as
{\Theta}^{\prime}(1)={\Theta}^{\prime\prime}(1)=1\not\in\mathfrak{l}.

Now, for the second claim. For all
t\in\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}}
one has
t\cdot\Theta^{\prime}=\Theta^{\prime}(t)\Theta^{\prime}
since for all
x\in\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}}
we have
(t\cdot\Theta^{\prime})(x)=\Theta^{\prime}(xt)=\Theta^{\prime}(tx)=\Theta^{\prime}(t)\Theta^{\prime}(x).
Since
\Psi_{\mathfrak{L}}
is
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}}
equivariant it is
R
–linear as well and so
t\cdot\Psi_{\mathfrak{L}}(\Theta^{\prime})=\Psi_{\mathfrak{L}}(t\cdot\Theta^{\prime})=\Psi_{\mathfrak{L}}(\Theta^{\prime}(t)\Theta^{\prime})=\Theta^{\prime}(t)\Psi_{\mathfrak{L}}(\Theta^{\prime}).

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Set
v(\Theta^{\prime}):=(\Phi_{\mathfrak{L}}\circ\Psi_{\mathfrak{L}})(\Theta^{\prime})
and

v(\Theta^{\prime\prime}):=(\Phi_{\mathfrak{L}}\circ\Psi_{\mathfrak{L}})(\Theta^{\prime\prime}).

On the vectors
v(\Theta^{\prime})
and
v(\Theta^{\prime\prime}),
the algebra
\mathtt{T}_{\mathfrak{L}}
acts by
\Theta^{\prime}
and
\Theta^{\prime\prime},
respectively. Moreover,

v(\Theta^{\prime}),\ v(\Theta^{\prime\prime})\ \not\in\ \mathfrak{l}e_{\mathfrak{L}}H,\quad\text{and}\quad v(\Theta^{\prime})-v(\Theta^{\prime\prime})\ \in\ \mathfrak{l}e_{\mathfrak{L}}H.

Proof.

Follows from Lem.
3.1
and that both
\Psi_{\mathfrak{L}}
and
\Phi_{\mathfrak{L}}
are
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{L}}
equivariant.
∎

3.4.
Specializing to a particular
\mathbb{T}
and

Take
\mathbb{T}
to be the Hecke algebra
h_{k^{\prime}}(N^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{l}})

and

Let

Let

. It is a semi-simple representation determined by

for

.

\quad\det{{\varrho}_{\Theta^{\prime}}\chi^{\prime-1}(\textup{Frob}_{p})}=\chi^{\prime-1}(p)p^{k^{\prime}-1}\pmod{\mathfrak{l}}.

for

4.1.
Calculation for the Borel and principal congruence subgroups

Thm.
4.1
and Cor.
4.1
below are essentially due to Januszewski
[
11
]
.
We follow closely the notation therein and reproduce the proof with a minor modification.
Recall the Iwahori decomposition of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
points of
G_{n}={\rm GL}_{n}/\mathbb{Q}.

G_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=\coprod_{w\in W_{n}}B_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})wI_{n},

where
B_{n}
is the set of upper triangular matrices,
W_{n}
is the Weyl group identified with the set of permutation matrices and

I_{n}
is the Iwahori subgroup of
G_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

Let
J_{m}
denote the set of principal congruence subgroup of level
m
of
G_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, i.e.,
the set of matrices of
g\in G_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
such that
g\equiv\mathbf{1}_{N}\pmod{p^{m}}.

Let
R_{m}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p}
denote the complete set of coset representatives
\{0,p,p^{2},\cdots,p^{m-1}\}
of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

Then the set

\mathcal{R}_{m}=\{r=(r_{ij})_{ij}\in I_{N}|r_{ij}\in R_{m}\}

forms a complete set of (left) coset representatives for
I_{n}/J_{m}.

For
w\in W_{n}
we have the obvious inclusion:

B_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})wsJ_{m}\subset B_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})wI_{n},
for
s\in\mathcal{R}_{m}.

We have

B_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})wI_{n}=\bigcup_{s\in\mathcal{R}_{m}}B_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})wsJ_{m}.

The cosets on the right need not be distinct.
The following theorem shows that we can take the union over a smaller set of representatives
and still get
B(\mathbb{Q}_{p})wI_{N}.
Before stating it we need some more notations.
Let
U_{B}
denote the unipotent radical of
B_{n}
consisting of strictly upper traingular matrices and
U_{B}^{-}
denote the unipotent radical of
The opposite Borel subgroup
B^{-}
of lower triangular matrices of
G
.
For a fixed
w\in W_{n}
define
B^{w}:=B_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\cap wI_{n}w^{-1}
and

\mathcal{R}_{m}^{w}:=\mathcal{R}_{m,B}\cap w^{-1}U_{B}^{-}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})w.

Theorem 4.1
.

For a fixed
w\in W_{n}
the set
\mathcal{R}^{w}_{m,B}
forms a complete system of coset representatives for

B(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\backslash B(\mathbb{Q}_{p})wI_{n}/J_{m},
i.e.,

B_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})wI_{n}=\bigcup_{s\in\mathcal{R}_{m,B}^{w}}B_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})wsJ_{m}.

Proof.

The map sending the coset
B^{w}wsJ_{m}\mapsto B_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})wsJ_{m}
, for

s\in\mathcal{R}_{m}
, is injective. Clearly, it is surjective as well. So it is enough to show that
w\mathcal{R}_{m,B}^{w}
forms a system of representatives for

B^{w}\backslash B^{w}wI_{n}/J_{m}.
Consider the following

Assumption 4.1
.

One can find elements
u^{(0)}:=\mathbf{1}_{n},u^{(1)},u^{(2)},\cdots,U_{n}\in B^{w},
and recursively define

r^{(0)},r^{(1)},\cdots,r^{(n)}\in I_{n}
where
r^{(0)}=1
and
r^{(v+1)}=w^{-1}u^{(v)}w\cdot r^{(v)}
for
v>1
,
such that
r^{(n)}\in w^{-1}U_{B}^{-}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})w.

From this assumption it follows if we define
u:=u^{(n-1)}\cdots u^{(1)}\in B^{w}
, then

u\cdot wrw^{-1}\ =\ w\cdot w^{-1}uwrw^{-1}\ =\ ww^{-1}U_{n}\cdots u^{(1)}wrw^{-1}\ =\ wr^{(n)}w^{-1}\in U_{B}^{-}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

Suppose
s\in\mathcal{R}_{m}
is a representative of the left coset
w^{-1}uwrJ_{m}\in I_{n}/J_{m}
then it follows

wsJ_{m}=uwrJ_{m}\implies B^{w}wsJ_{m}=B^{w}wrJ_{m}.

So
ws
represents the same double coset as
wr
in
B^{w}\backslash B^{w}wI_{n}/J_{m}
(equivalently in
B(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\backslash B(\mathbb{Q}_{p})wI_{n}/J_{m}
).
Since
s\in w^{-1}uwrJ_{m}
, this implies

wsw^{-1}\in uwrw^{-1}J_{m}\subset U_{B}^{-}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})J_{m},
hence

s\in w^{-1}U_{B}^{-}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})wJ_{m},
whence

s\in\mathcal{R}_{m}\cap w^{-1}U_{B}^{-}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})w=\mathcal{R}_{m,B}^{w}.

∎

Assump.
4.1
can be shown by changing the definition of the
(n-v,n-v)
entry of
u^{(v)}
as in
Januszewski
[
11
, Prop. 2.2]
. This is possible because here

and not just in
U^{-}_{B}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}).

Corollary 4.1
.

With the notations as in the previous theorem we have

B_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})wI_{n}=\coprod_{s\in\mathcal{R}_{m,B}^{w}}B_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})wsJ_{m}.

In other words, the double cosets in the previous theorem are all disjoint.

Proof.

Assume two of the cosets,
B_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})wsJ_{m}=B_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})ws^{\prime}J_{m}
for
s,s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}_{m,B}^{w}
are the same.
This means
B^{w}wsJ_{m}=B^{w}ws^{\prime}J_{m}.
Since
B^{w}=B_{n}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\cap wI_{N}w^{-1}=B_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\cap wI_{N}w^{-1},

we see that there exists

and

such that

Observing that

is normal in

we get that

for some

But both the elements

and

are in

from the previous theorem.
Combined with the fact that

and

we get

if and only if

Then for some

depending on

we have

uws=wsj^{\prime\prime}=ws^{\prime}j
because

is normal in

, hence

, whence

∎

6.3.
Congruence of the Eisenstein operator

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\phi^{\prime}_{f}\in I_{4}^{\,\mathsf{S}}(\sigma_{f},\sigma^{\prime}_{f},\epsilon^{\prime},E_{\mathfrak{l}})
and

\phi_{f}^{\prime\prime}\in I_{4}^{\,\mathsf{S}}(\sigma_{f},\sigma^{\prime\prime}_{f},\epsilon^{\prime},E_{\mathfrak{l}})
. Assume that
\mathfrak{l}\not\in\mathsf{S}_{\text{Eis}}.

Also, assume
\phi^{\prime}_{f}
and
\phi^{\prime\prime}_{f}
are integral in that they are in
I_{4}^{\mathsf{S}}(\mu+\mu^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{l}}).
We have:

\phi^{\prime}_{f}\,\,\equiv\phi_{f}^{\prime\prime}\pmod{\mathfrak{l}}\ \implies\ T_{\textup{Eis}}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime},E_{\mathfrak{l}})\phi^{\prime}_{f}\,\,\equiv T_{\textup{Eis}}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime\prime},\epsilon^{\prime},E_{\mathfrak{l}})\phi^{\prime\prime}_{f}\pmod{\mathfrak{l}}.

Proof.

Suppose
B\subset V
and
B^{\prime}\subset V^{\prime}
are
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{l}}
lattices inside
E_{\mathfrak{l}}
vector spaces. Let
T:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}

be a morphism of vector spaces such that
T(B)\subset B^{\prime}
then for
x,y\in V
one has
x\equiv y\pmod{\mathfrak{l}}
, which, by definition
means
x-y\in\mathfrak{l}B,
implies
T(x)\equiv T(y)\pmod{\mathfrak{l}},
which, by definition, means
T(x)-T(y)\in\mathfrak{l}B^{\prime}.

Since
\mathfrak{l}\not\in S_{\text{Eis}}
one has

\phi^{\prime}_{f}-\phi^{\prime\prime}_{f}\in\mathfrak{l}\left({I}_{4}^{\mathsf{S}}(\mu+\mu^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{l}})\right).

From which one gets

\pi_{\mu+\mu^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{I}}\left(\phi^{\prime}_{f}\right)-\pi_{\mu+\mu^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{I}}\left(\phi^{\prime\prime}_{f}\right)\in\mathfrak{l}\left({\mathfrak{I}}^{4}(\mu,\mu^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{l}})\right)
.
Hence,

\pi^{\mu^{\prime}(-2)+\mu(2)}_{\mathfrak{I}}\left(\pi_{\mu+\mu^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{I}}\left(\phi^{\prime}_{f}\right)\right)-\pi^{\mu^{\prime}(-2)+\mu(2)}_{\mathfrak{I}}\left(\pi_{\mu+\mu^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{I}}\left(\phi^{\prime\prime}_{f}\right)\right)\in\mathfrak{l}\left({I}_{4}^{\mathsf{S}}(\mu^{\prime}(-2)+\mu(2),\epsilon^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{l}})\right),

whence,

T_{\text{Eis}}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime},E_{\mathfrak{l}})\phi_{f}^{\prime}-T_{\text{Eis}}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime\prime},\epsilon^{\prime},E_{\mathfrak{l}})\phi_{f}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathfrak{l}\left({I}_{4}^{\mathsf{S}}(\mu^{\prime}(-2)+\mu(2),\epsilon^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{l}})\right).

∎

Define vectors in the induced space

I_{4}^{\mathsf{S}}(\mu+\mu^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{l}}):

\displaystyle{{}^{\circ}}\phi^{\prime}_{f}(\xi_{f})=\begin{cases}{{}^{\circ}}v^{\epsilon^{\prime}}_{\mu+\mu^{\prime}}(h,h^{\prime\rho})&\,\,\,\xi_{f}=\xi_{f}^{(N^{\prime})}\\
0&\,\,\,\xi_{f}\neq\xi_{f}^{(N^{\prime})}\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad{{}^{\circ}}\tilde{\phi}^{\prime}_{f}(\xi_{f})=\begin{cases}{{}^{\circ}}v^{\epsilon^{\prime}}_{\mu^{\prime}(-2)+\mu(2)}(h^{\prime\rho},h)&\,\,\,\xi_{f}=\xi_{f}^{(N)}\\
0&\,\,\,\xi_{f}\neq\xi_{f}^{(N)}.\end{cases}

Similarly, define vectors for the pair
(h,h^{\prime\prime}).

Then,

{{}^{\circ}}\phi^{\prime}_{f}\in I_{4}^{\mathsf{S}}(\sigma_{f},\sigma^{\prime}_{f},\epsilon^{\prime},E_{\mathfrak{l}})

and

{{}^{\circ}}\phi^{\prime\prime}_{f}\in I_{4}^{\mathsf{S}}(\sigma_{f},\sigma^{\prime\prime}_{f},\epsilon^{\prime},E_{\mathfrak{l}}).

From the definition of
I_{4}^{\mathsf{S}}(\mu+\mu^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{l}})

it follows that
{{}^{\circ}}\phi^{\prime}_{f}\equiv{{}^{\circ}}\phi^{\prime\prime}_{f}\pmod{\mathfrak{l}}.
We get the following

Corollary 6.1
.

T_{\textup{Eis}}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},\epsilon^{\prime},E_{\mathfrak{l}}){{}^{\circ}}\phi^{\prime}_{f}\ \equiv\ T_{\textup{Eis}}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime\prime},\epsilon^{\prime},E_{\mathfrak{l}}){{}^{\circ}}\phi^{\prime\prime}_{f}\pmod{\mathfrak{l}}.

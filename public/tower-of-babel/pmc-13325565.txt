Clinical outcomes of the 2022 AAP hyperbilirubinemia guideline in term and late-preterm infants: a prospective study in Thailand
Authors: Natcha Lueangapapong, Benjaporn Srinithiwat, Patraporn Jangmeonwai, Tapanee Pipatkullachart, Jiratt Kongchalard, Phoom Phavichitr, Rattanaphan Chuabmee, Rinrada Piyawannakul, Jaedvara Thanakitcharu, Kanokwan Aeimcharnbanchong

Background

Neonatal jaundice is a common condition, occurring in approximately 80% of term infants during the first week of life [1, 2]. It is characterized by elevated total serum bilirubin (TSB) levels that may require timely evaluation and treatment based on the infant’s age and associated neurotoxicity risk factors [3]. Delayed diagnosis and treatment may result in markedly elevated bilirubin concentrations, which can lead to acute bilirubin encephalopathy and, in severe cases, kernicterus [4]. Kernicterus is a permanent and disabling neurologic condition characterized by some or all of the following: choreoathetoid cerebral palsy, upward gaze paresis, enamel dysplasia of the deciduous teeth, sensorineural hearing loss or auditory neuropathy spectrum disorder, and characteristic abnormalities on brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) [5]. However, unnecessary serum bilirubin monitoring and overuse of phototherapy also result in adverse effects. These include painful procedures for newborns, infant–family separation, increased parental stress, disruption of breastfeeding [6, 7], increased hospital resource utilization, and a potential increased risk of subsequent childhood epilepsy [8, 9].

In 2022, the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) [3] revised its guideline for the management of hyperbilirubinemia in newborns ≥ 35 weeks’ gestation, raising the thresholds for initiating phototherapy and exchange transfusion compared with the 2004 recommendations [4]. In addition to adjusting treatment thresholds, the 2022 AAP guideline recommend determining the timing of follow-up based on the difference between the measured total bilirubin level (either transcutaneous bilirubin [TcB] or TSB), obtained between 24 and 48 h after birth or before discharge if earlier, and the age-specific treatment threshold. This approach also incorporates gestational age and the presence of neurotoxicity risk factors into clinical decision-making.

In contrast, the Bahr 2021 nomogram [10] stratifies risk solely according to pre-discharge TSB levels plotted against the infant’s age in hours, without incorporating additional clinical risk factors.

The 2022 updates aim to reduce unnecessary interventions. Early evidence from high-income countries suggests that the revised guideline can reduce the use of phototherapy and hospital admissions without increasing adverse outcomes [11–14]. However, evidence from low- and middle-income countries remains limited, and concerns persist regarding the safety of applying higher treatment thresholds in resource-limited settings. This study aimed to evaluate the clinical impact of implementing the 2022 AAP guideline in a middle-income country, focusing on phototherapy use, hospital admissions, follow-up rates, and complications related to severe hyperbilirubinemia.

Methods

Study design and setting

This prospective cohort study was conducted at Panyananthaphikkhu Chonprathan Medical Center (PCMC), a tertiary neonatal center in Nonthaburi, Thailand, from February 1, 2024, to January 31, 2025.

Study population and eligibility criteria

Eligible participants were healthy neonates born at ≥ 35 weeks’ gestation who roomed-in with their mothers and underwent blood screening for hyperbilirubinemia within 72 h of birth. Enrollment was conducted over a one-year period following approval by the Institutional Review Board of PCMC (IRB No. 030/67).

Exclusion criteria

Neonates were excluded if they required admission to the neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) or sick newborn ward, had medical conditions requiring scheduled follow-up after discharge, or if parental consent was not provided.

Withdrawal criteria

Participants were withdrawn if follow-up until one month of age could not be completed.

Study procedures

Parents of all eligible neonates were informed about the study, and written informed consent was obtained prior to enrollment. All enrolled neonates underwent TSB measurement before discharge within 72 h of birth. The TSB value obtained at that time was compared with the phototherapy and exchange transfusion thresholds according to the 2022 AAP guideline. Clinical management, including decisions regarding phototherapy, exchange transfusion, and follow-up, was based on the measured TSB level and the presence of neurotoxicity risk factors. All neonates who received phototherapy were followed up according to the underlying etiology of hyperbilirubinemia. Neonates who did not receive phototherapy within the first 72 h of life were scheduled for follow-up in accordance with the 2022 AAP recommendations, based on the difference between the infant’s TSB level and the phototherapy threshold and the presence of risk factors for developing significant hyperbilirubinemia.

All neonates were followed for one month to monitor for readmission for phototherapy or exchange transfusion, as well as the development of acute bilirubin encephalopathy.

Follow-up data were obtained through review of electronic medical records (EMRs). In cases when EMR data were incomplete or unavailable, parents were contacted by telephone to obtain additional information regarding the infant’s health status and any complications.

Data collection

Baseline data collected included gestational age, sex, birth weight, mode of delivery, and TSB levels. Clinical outcomes recorded included receipt of phototherapy, hospital readmission, follow-up status, exchange transfusion, acute bilirubin encephalopathy, and the identified cause of jaundice. Additionally, the phototherapy rate within the first 72 h of life was retrospectively determined by applying the 2004 guideline to the observed TSB values for each infant. Among neonates who did not receive phototherapy within the first 72 h, the follow-up rate was retrospectively assessed by applying the Bahr 2021 nomogram.

Study outcomes

The primary outcome was the observed phototherapy rate within 72 h of birth among neonates ≥ 35 weeks’ gestation, to assess the impact of the 2022 AAP hyperbilirubinemia guideline. Secondary outcomes included: (1) comparison of the observed 2022 phototherapy rate with the rate obtained by retrospectively applying the 2004 guideline within the first 72 h of life; (2) comparison of the observed 2022 follow-up rate among infants discharged at 48–72 h who did not receive phototherapy within 72 h with the rate obtained by retrospectively applying the Bahr 2021 nomogram; and (3) evaluation of the causes and rates of readmission, as well as the rates of exchange transfusion and acute bilirubin encephalopathy, following implementation of the 2022 guideline.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize baseline characteristics. Continuous variables were reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. Comparisons between categorical variables were performed using the McNemar test. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were conducted using Stata version 18 (StataCorp, College Station, TX, USA).

Results

During the study period (February 1, 2024, to January 31, 2025), 1,252 neonates born at ≥ 35 weeks’ gestation were assessed for eligibility. Of these, 146 were excluded due to NICU or sick newborn ward admission. A total of 1,106 healthy rooming-in neonates who underwent bilirubin screening were enrolled. After excluding 2 infants lost to follow-up before 1 month of age, 1,104 infants were included in the final analysis (Fig. 1). Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics are shown in Table 1. Overall, 221 infants (20.0%) received phototherapy according to the 2022 AAP guideline. Among infants who received phototherapy, suboptimal intake hyperbilirubinemia was the most common cause of jaundice within the first 72 h, followed by unspecified causes and ABO incompatibility. Other less frequent causes included glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency, prematurity, hemolysis, subgaleal hemorrhage or cephalohematoma, and polycythemia (Table 2).

All infants in this study received phototherapy according to the 2022 AAP guidelines. For comparison, phototherapy eligibility was retrospectively assessed by applying the 2004 guideline to the observed TSB value for each infant. When the two guidelines were compared, a significant reduction in phototherapy use was observed with the 2022 guideline. Based on retrospective application of the 2004 guideline, 358 infants (32%) would have required phototherapy, whereas only 221 infants (20%) received phototherapy according to the 2022 guideline. This difference was statistically significant (p < 0.001). In addition, among infants readmitted for phototherapy (n = 127), 39 (31%) had not received phototherapy within the first 72 h of life under the 2022 guideline despite meeting treatment criteria according to the 2004 AAP guideline. In an exploratory analysis, inclusion of these infants increased the number of infants who would have received phototherapy under the 2022 guideline from 221 to 260 (23.5%), which remained substantially lower than the 32% estimated using the 2004 guideline.

Among neonates who did not receive phototherapy within the first 72 h of life, the follow-up rate based on the 2022 AAP guideline was 582 (52.7%). When the same cohort was re-evaluated using the nomogram developed by Bahr et al., 629 infants (56.9%) met the follow-up criteria. This corresponded to an absolute reduction of 4.3% and a relative reduction of 7.5% (p = 0.044; 95% CI, 5.91–9.03).

Readmission rates after 72 h of age and the causes of neonatal jaundice were analyzed according to phototherapy status under the retrospectively applied 2004 AAP guideline and the actual 2022 AAP guideline (Table 4). Among infants who would have received phototherapy under the retrospectively applied 2004 guideline but did not receive it under the 2022 guideline, 39 (31%) were readmitted, primarily due to suboptimal intake and unspecified causes. Additionally, 52 infants (40%) who received phototherapy under both guidelines were readmitted, with suboptimal intake, ABO incompatibility, and G6PD deficiency as the leading etiologies. Among infants who did not receive phototherapy under either guideline, 36 (28%) were readmitted, most commonly due to suboptimal intake.

Importantly, after implementation of the AAP 2022 guideline, no infants required exchange transfusion, and no cases of acute bilirubin encephalopathy occurred during the study period.

Discussion

Principal findings

This study demonstrates that implementation of the 2022 AAP guideline for neonatal hyperbilirubinemia was associated with a 38% reduction in phototherapy use (32% vs. 20%, p < 0.001) when compared with retrospective application of the same infants’ TSB values to the 2004 thresholds.

Importantly, no cases of acute bilirubin encephalopathy occurred and no infants required exchange transfusion following adoption of the updated recommendations.

Despite this reduction in treatment, the readmission rate was 11.5%, higher than that reported in several previous studies. However, most readmissions were attributable to suboptimal intake hyperbilirubinemia rather than failure of risk stratification. In addition, among infants who did not receive phototherapy within 72 h, application of the 2022 guideline resulted in a modest but significant reduction in recommended follow-up compared with the Bahr 2021 nomogram (absolute reduction 4.3%, relative reduction 7.5%, p = 0.044). Together, these findings suggest that the 2022 guideline safely reduces the use of phototherapy and unnecessary follow-up visits while maintaining protection against severe bilirubin-related complications within a structured follow-up system.

Comparison with previous studies

The initial phototherapy rate in our cohort (20%) remains substantially higher than the rates reported in high-income settings. For example, Sarathy et al. [12] reported 3.9%, and Michienzi et al. [13] observed 4.2% after adopting the 2022 guideline. In contrast, our findings are closer to those reported from Thailand by Vongbhavit et al. [15], who described a rate of 14.9%. These differences likely reflect variations in population risk factors and healthcare context, including the higher prevalence of G6PD deficiency in Thailand [16–19], as well as differences in health system resources and postnatal care practices.

However, in our study, phototherapy use decreased substantially compared with estimates derived from retrospective application of the 2004 AAP guideline. Among infants readmitted for phototherapy, nearly one-third had not received treatment within the first 72 h of life despite meeting the 2004 guideline criteria. Even when these infants were included in an exploratory analysis, the overall phototherapy rate remained markedly lower than that estimated under the 2004 guideline. These findings are consistent with previous reports demonstrating reductions in phototherapy use following implementation of the 2022 guideline. Sarathy et al. [12] reported a 47% reduction (from 3.9% to 2.1%), while Michienzi et al. [13] and Vongbhavit et al. [15] reported decreases from 4.2% to 1.4% and from 14.9% to 11.5%, respectively. Our findings further support the effectiveness of the 2022 AAP guideline in reducing phototherapy use across diverse clinical settings.

The readmission rate in our study (11.5%) exceeded that reported by Michienzi et al. [13] (0.9%) and was approximately ten times higher than rates observed in high-income countries. The primary cause of readmission was suboptimal intake hyperbilirubinemia, underscoring the importance of early feeding adequacy. This disparity may reflect structural, resource-related, and contextual differences. As a middle-income country, Thailand may have limited breastfeeding education and lactation support across the continuum of antenatal and postnatal care, which may delay recognition of feeding problems and contribute to rising bilirubin levels. The absence of routine post-discharge home visits may further delay the identification of jaundice and inadequate intake [20–22]. Additionally, the higher prevalence of G6PD deficiency in Thailand may contribute to increased rates of significant hyperbilirubinemia and subsequent readmission [19]. Another possible explanation is that a subset of infants who would previously have received phototherapy under the 2004 guideline were instead managed without treatment under the 2022 guideline. This shift toward more conservative management may have contributed to the higher readmission rate observed in our cohort.

Suboptimal intake hyperbilirubinemia was the leading cause of both early phototherapy (36.2%) and readmission (48.8%), consistent with previous reports [23, 24]. These findings underscore the critical importance of effective lactation support and early feeding assessment [25–27].

The most important finding of our study was that, although the 2022 AAP guideline raised the thresholds for initiating phototherapy and exchange transfusion compared with the 2004 recommendations, it reduced unnecessary phototherapy without increasing the rates of exchange transfusion or acute bilirubin encephalopathy. These findings are consistent with previous studies conducted in high-income countries [11–14].

The absence of increased complications may be explained by the structured and comprehensive risk assessment incorporated into the 2022 AAP guideline. This includes universal TSB screening before discharge, consideration of neurotoxicity risk factors when determining the need for phototherapy, and a structured follow-up system. Follow-up recommendations are based on the difference between the predischarge TSB level and the phototherapy threshold, as well as the presence of risk factors for developing significant hyperbilirubinemia.

Strengths and limitations

This study has several strengths. It prospectively evaluated implementation of the 2022 AAP guideline in a real-world clinical setting and included detailed analysis of treatment thresholds, follow-up recommendations, and clinical outcomes. The retrospective application of the 2004 guideline and the Bahr 2021 nomogram allowed a structured assessment of differences in treatment and follow-up strategies.

However, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, comparisons with the 2004 AAP guideline and the Bahr 2021 nomogram were based on retrospective application of treatment thresholds rather than parallel real-time implementation,

which may not fully capture factors influencing clinical decisions. Second, the study was conducted at a single center, potentially limiting generalizability to other populations with different demographic characteristics, healthcare systems, or breastfeeding support structures. Finally, although no severe neurologic outcomes occurred, the study was not powered to detect very rare adverse events.

Clinical implications and future perspectives

These findings have important clinical implications. The 2022 AAP guideline appears to safely reduce phototherapy use without increasing severe complications when coupled with a structured, risk-based follow-up system. By minimizing unnecessary phototherapy, clinicians may reduce maternal–infant separation and its associated adverse effects, including impaired bonding, reduced breastfeeding success, maternal depression, and potential long term neurologic complications.

Nevertheless, the relatively high phototherapy and readmission rates in our study, which were primarily attributable to suboptimal intake hyperbilirubinemia, highlight the need to strengthen early feeding assessment and breastfeeding support, particularly in low-and-middle income settings. Enhanced lactation counseling may further reduce phototherapy and readmission rates while preserving the benefits of higher treatment thresholds. Future research should include multicenter validation in diverse populations, evaluation of breastfeeding and maternal outcomes following reduced phototherapy exposure, and development of interventions to improve early feeding success. Such efforts will help refine implementation strategies and ensure that the benefits of the 2022 guideline are fully realized while maintaining patient safety.

Conclusions

Implementation of the 2022 AAP guideline reduces phototherapy use and unnecessary follow-up visits without increasing adverse outcomes. With clearly structured follow-up schedules and close monitoring of infants at risk of developing severe jaundice, high-risk newborns can be managed effectively and safely. These findings support the applicability of the guideline in routine clinical practice, including in middle-income countries.

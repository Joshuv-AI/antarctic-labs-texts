Nonenzymatic Electrochemical Sensors for Hydrogen Peroxide Using a Cu-TCPP/rGO Nanocomposite
Authors: Guozhen Chen, Jian Wang, Siwei Wang, Xinyi Min, Jianbin Zheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Nonenzymatic electrochemical methods offer significant advantages for hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) detection. However, the sensitivity must be improved for practical applications. In this study, we developed a novel electrochemical sensor for H2O2 based on a Cu-TCPP/reduced graphene oxide (rGO) nanocomposite synthesized via a surfactant-assisted method. Comprehensive structural and morphological analyses, including TEM, SEM, EDS, XRD, UV–vis, and FTIR, demonstrated uniform dispersion of Cu-TCPP on the rGO surface via π–π interactions. Electrocatalytic reduction of H2O2 at the Cu-TCPP/rGO-modified electrode showed a linear range of 0.10–6.30 mM and a detection limit of 0.03 mM (S/N = 3). The practical applicability of the sensor was demonstrated by the detection of H2O2 in commercial disinfectant samples, yielding recovery rates in the range of 99.7–104.0%. These results highlight the capabilities of Cu-TCPP/rGO nanocomposites as reliable platforms for the sensitive, nonenzymatic electrochemical detection of H2O2, making them a valuable potential tool for environmental monitoring.

Introduction

Hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) is a ubiquitous and significant molecule in biological tissues. Various pathologies, including cancer, myocardial infarction, and neurodegenerative conditions such as Alzheimer’s and Parkinson’s disease, are associated with dysregulated levels of H2O2. − Beyond its biological roles, H2O2 also functions as an oxidizing agent in various chemical, biological, pharmaceutical, and environmental processes. Consequently, the accurate and quantitative detection of H2O2 is crucial in numerous fields, including biotechnology, environmental monitoring, clinical diagnostics, food safety analysis, and industrial applications. − Therefore, developing effective and accurate methods for detecting H2O2 is crucial. Numerous analytical techniques have been developed for H2O2 detection, including fluorescence spectroscopy, chemiluminescence, UV–vis spectrophotometry, titration, and electrochemical methods. − Among these, electrochemical approaches are highly promising owing to their low detection limits, high sensitivity, excellent selectivity, operational simplicity, and cost effectiveness. The classification of H2O2 electrochemical sensors is primarily based on their reliance on enzymes, and they can be broadly categorized into enzymatic and nonenzymatic types. However, enzymatic sensors are hampered by the inherent drawbacks of natural enzymes, such as poor stability, high susceptibility to environmental conditions, limited storage and operational robustness, and high production costs, which restrict their practical applicability.

To overcome these limitations, nanomaterials with enzyme-like properties, referred to as “nanozymes”, have garnered increasing interest as alternatives in nonenzymatic electrochemical sensing. Considerable progress has been made in developing nanozyme-based sensors using various nanostructured materials, including carbon-based materials, metals, metal oxides, and two-dimensional (2D) metal–organic frameworks (MOFs), which can exhibit pronounced enzyme-like activity. Wang et al. reported 2D MOF/multiwalled carbon nanotube nanocomposites for H2O2 sensing, whereas Zhou et al. synthesized Cu-MOF/graphene (Gr) sensors. Although several carbon-based MOF nanocomposites with promising performance have emerged, a substantial demand remains for novel carbon-based nanocomposites with unique morphologies, architectures, and excellent properties and performance.

2D MOFs constitute a recently developed class of crystalline porous materials that are characterized by a periodic grid structure arising from the self-assembly of transition-metal ions and organic ligands. Their tunable pore size, high surface area, porosity, and well-ordered channels have driven their application in electrochemical sensing. , Nonetheless, poor electrical conductivity and structural instability remain key obstacles to the application of 2D MOFs in sensor development. Consequently, strategies to improve the conductivity and structural integrity of 2D MOFs are being actively pursued. A particularly effective approach involves integrating 2D MOFs with other functional materials to create hybrid composites with synergistic properties. − Although 2D MOFs inherently exhibit peroxidase-like activity, their weak conductivity necessitates further enhancement. Reduced graphene oxide (rGO), a widely used carbon material, offers excellent conductivity and a large specific surface area. − The combination of rGO with @D MOFs not only retains the functional advantages of both components, but also addresses conductivity limitations. Furthermore, rGO can prevent the aggregation of 2D MOFs during synthesis, thereby improving the stability and catalytic efficiency of the resulting nanocomposites.

The development of sensitive, selective, and cost-effective electrochemical sensing platforms for H2O2 is a key research priority that is crucial for the transition of these sensors from laboratory to practical, on-site analysis. In this study, we aimed to develop a high-performance H2O2 sensor based on a nanocomposite of two-dimensional copper–porphyrin metal–organic framework (2D Cu-TCPP) and reduced graphene oxide (rGO). The core of this work lies in the methodology: we employ a controllable surfactant-assisted in situ growth method, using polyvinylpyrrolidone as a structure-directing agent. This approach is designed to achieve the uniform, nonaggregated growth of ultrathin Cu-TCPP on the rGO surface, thereby maximizing the active site exposure and enhancing the interfacial electron transfer via strong π–π coupling. The resultant Cu-TCPP/rGO nanocomposite was fabricated into an electrochemical sensor for the sensitive and practical detection of H2O2 (Scheme ).

Results and Discussion

Morphology and Structure of the Cu-TCPP/rGO Nanocomposite

Figure presents SEM and TEM images characterizing the morphology, size, and elemental composition of the GO, rGO, Cu-TCPP, and final Cu-TCPP/rGO nanocomposite. The prepared GO (Figure A,B), rGO (Figure C,D), and Cu-TCPP (Figure E,F) showed transparent, smooth, and wrinkled 2D morphologies, respectively. Notably, the wrinkles present in the rGO increased its specific surface area, which is beneficial for improved sensor performance. In the Cu-TCPP/rGO nanocomposite (Figure G,H), the aggregation of Cu-TCPP nanofilms was prevented, with Cu-TCPP interacting with rGO through π–π interactions and being uniformly distributed on the rGO surface. In addition, the SEM-based elemental analysis (Figure I,J,L) confirmed the homogeneous distribution of carbon, copper, oxygen, and nitrogen within the nanocomposite, in agreement with the expected composition of Cu-TCPP/rGO.

The FTIR spectra (Figure A) indicated the presence of characteristic functional groups in the synthesized materials, confirming the successful introduction of oxygen-containing functional groups into the GO. The broad absorption band centered at 3400 cm–1 was attributed to the – OH stretching vibrations of adsorbed water molecules and hydroxyl groups. Characteristic carbonyl (CO) stretching vibration from carboxyl groups was observed at 1725 cm–1. In addition, the peak at 1619 cm–1 corresponded to the skeletal vibrations of the graphitic domains (CC) and the bending mode of water. The fingerprints of the C–O bonds were evident at 1406 cm–1 (C–OH bending) and 1062 cm–1 (C–O stretching in epoxy C–O–C or phenolic C–O–H groups). Following hydrothermal reduction, the intensities of all absorption peaks corresponding to the oxygen functional groups (CO, C–OH, C–O–C, C–O–H, and C–O) decreased significantly in the rGO (curve b, Figure A), confirming the effective reduction of GO to rGO. The characteristic absorption peaks of TCPP (curve c) and Cu-TCPP (curve d) at 752 and 1023 cm–1, respectively, corresponded to the vibrational absorption of the porphyrin skeleton, with the absorption near 1400 cm–1 arising from the CN stretch in the pyrrole ring. The peaks at 1128 and 1602 cm–1 were associated with skeletal vibrations of the phenyl ring, with the 1700 cm–1 signal corresponding to the carboxyl CO stretch. The peak at 1602 cm–1 was characteristic of substituted benzene rings. The peak at 1298 cm–1 was also attributed to the −OH stretching vibration of TCPP. Notably, changes in the intensities of the 1400, 1602, and 1700 cm–1 peaks upon the formation of Cu-TCPP reflected the subtle alterations in the electronic structure of the porphyrin ligand induced by metal coordination. − The FTIR spectrum of Cu-TCPP/rGO (curve e) closely resembled that of Cu-TCPP (curve d), indicating successful composite formation.

Figure B shows the XRD patterns, which confirmed the crystallographic structure and phase purity of the materials. The GO exhibited a characteristic diffraction peak at 11.35° (curve a), whereas the rGO showed a broad diffraction peak at 24.5° (curve b). − The Cu-TCPP showed a diffraction peak at 19.4°, indicating its successful preparation (curve c). , The presence of peaks at both 19.4 and 24.5° in the Cu-TCPP/rGO composite (curve d), corresponding to the diffraction peaks of rGO and Cu-TCPP, respectively, confirmed the coexistence of rGO and Cu-TCPP, indicating the successful synthesis of the nanocomposite.

The UV–vis spectra (Figure C) provided insights into the electronic structure of the synthesized materials. The GO (curve a) showed a strong absorption peak at 230 nm, whereas the rGO (curve b) exhibited a red-shifted characteristic absorption peak at 260 nm, confirming the reduction of GO to rGO. The Cu-TCPP (curve c) showed absorption bands at approximately 438 and 549 nm. The Cu-TCPP/rGO nanocomposite (curve d) exhibited characteristic absorption peaks at 260, 438, and 549 nm, which were attributed to the presence of both rGO and Cu-TCPP, verifying the successful integration of both components.

XPS analysis further confirmed the successful synthesis of Cu-TCPP and Cu-TCPP/rGO (Figure S2). In TCPP, the O 1s peaks at 533.52 and 531.88 eV are assigned to the C–OH and CO functional groups, respectively (Figure S2A). Notably, upon the formation of Cu-TCPP, the intensity of the O 1s peak corresponding to the C–OH group decreased markedly (Figure S2B), providing clear evidence of successful coordination between the TCPP ligand and the Cu metal center. A full set of characterization results, including TEM, SEM, elemental mapping, XRD, FTIR, XPS, and UV–vis spectroscopy, consistently confirmed the successful construction of the Cu-TCPP/rGO nanocomposite.

Electrochemical Properties of the Cu-TCPP/rGO Nanocomposite

Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) is an effective method for studying the characteristics of modified electrode interfaces. As shown in Figure S3, after Cu-TCPP was modified on the GCE, the charge transfer resistance (R ct) increased from 1383 Ω (a) to 6500 Ω (b), which was due to the low conductivity of Cu-TCPP, resulting in a decrease in the efficiency of electron transfer; after rGO was modified on Cu-TCPP/GCE, Rct decreased to 2758 Ω (c). This indicates that the excellent conductivity of rGO can effectively reduce the electron transfer resistance between the solution and the electrode, improve the electron transfer efficiency, and endow the prepared nanocomposite material with good conductivity.

The electrocatalytic performance of the Cu-TCPP/rGO nanocomposite toward the reduction of 3.0 mM H2O2 was evaluated using cyclic voltammetry (C–V) (Figure ). The bare glassy carbon electrode (GCE), rGO/GCE, Cu-TCPP/GCE, and Cu-TCPP/rGO/GCE exhibited negligible electrochemical responses (Figure a–d) before the addition of H2O2. Upon the addition of H2O2, the bare GCE exhibited no significant current response (Figure e), whereas the rGO/GCE showed only a slight increase (Figure f). In contrast, a clear reduction peak appeared at approximately −0.65 V for the Cu-TCPP/GCE (Figure g), with a peak current of 22.2 μA, indicating moderate catalytic activity. Notably, the Cu-TCPP/rGO/GCE exhibited a significantly enhanced reduction peak current of 71.2 μA at −0.65 V (Figure h), representing 3.2- and 3.9-fold enhancements over the Cu-TCPP/GCE and rGO/GCE, respectively, and a peak potential closer to 0 V. These improvements could be ascribed to (i) the peroxidase-like activity of Cu-TCPP that can catalyze H2O2 reduction, (ii) the high surface area and high porosity of Cu-TCPP facilitating mass transport and providing abundant active sites, and (iii) the high conductivity of rGO promoting efficient electron transfer.

The C–V responses of the Cu-TCPP/rGO/GCE (Figure A,B) to increasing concentrations of H2O2 (1.00–9.00 mM) demonstrate a concentration-dependent increase in the current. The reduction peak current increased linearly with the H2O2 concentration, indicating excellent electrocatalytic performance of the Cu-TCPP/rGO. The effective surface area of the modified electrode, Cu-TCPP/rGO/GCE, was determined by recording the cyclic voltammetry (C–V) curves at various scan rates in a solution containing 1 mmol L–1 K3Fe­(CN)6 and 0.1 mol L–1 KCl. The effective surface area of the Cu-TCPP/rGO/GCE was calculated to be 0.256 cm2.

Using XPS, Cu-TCPP/rGO during the H2O2 sensing process was investigated, and the results are depicted in Figure S4. Before electrochemical detection, the Cu 2p XPS spectrum exhibited two distinct peaks at 955.0 and 935.3 eV, corresponding to the transitions of Cu 2p1/2 and Cu 2p3/2, respectively, indicative of Cu2+. However, after the application of Cu-TCPP/rGO for H2O2 sensing, a narrow peak appeared at 933.9 eV, representing the characteristic peak of Cu+. In conjunction with the C–V response, this suggests that Cu-TCPP/rGO effectively catalyzes the reduction of H2O2 and functions as a peroxidase mimic. ,

Selectivity, Repeatability, and Stability of the Sensor

The chronoamperometric (I–t) response of the Cu-TCPP/rGO-based system to sequential additions of 0.50 mM H2O2 at different applied potentials was investigated (Figure A). The results revealed that higher applied potentials (−0.70 V) produced the maximum current responses but also resulted in high noise. An optimal working potential of −0.65 V was selected, yielding a high I–t response current and minimal noise, which led to a high signal-to-noise ratio. The current response showed well-defined steps that correlated with successive increments of H2O2 concentration in PBS (Figure B). The response time did not exceed 3 s (Figure C), indicating a rapid electrochemical response. The calibration curve (Figure D) demonstrated a wide Lear range for H2O2 detection (0.10–6.30 mM), with a linear regression equation I p (μA) = 16.12 C (mM) + 18.69 (r = 0.9995), a sensitivity of 230.3 μA mM–1 cm–2, and a detection limit of 0.03 mM (S/N = 3).

The selectivity of the sensor is crucial for analyzing real samples. The I–t response was recorded for 0.05 mM H2O2 at an applied potential of −0.65 V on the Cu-TCPP/rGO/GCE in the presence of potentially interfering substances such as CH3CH2OH, UA, Glu, AP, AA, and NaNO2, each at concentrations 20 times higher than that of the H2O2 (Figure ). The Cu-TCPP/rGO/GCE exhibited a pronounced current response only for H2O2, with a negligible electrochemical response for all interfering substances, thereby confirming the high selectivity of the method for H2O2.

C–V and chronoamperometry were employed to evaluate the repeatability and operational stability of the fabricated H2O2 sensor (Figure ). The relative standard deviation (RSD) of the C–V peak currents for 3.0 mM H2O2 over five consecutive measurements was 1.3%, indicating excellent repeatability (Figure A,B). The RSD for the chronoamperometric responses for 0.05 mM H2O2 using five independently prepared Cu-TCPP/rGO/GCE sensor electrodes was 0.67%, demonstrating outstanding reproducibility (Figure C,D). Furthermore, using chronoamperometry, the sensor retained 85.8% of its initial current response after 15 days when stored at room temperature, highlighting its robust stability (Figure E,F).

Real-Sample Analysis

To validate the practical applicability of the developed sensor, the H2O2 in commercial disinfectant samples was measured. Two disinfectant products, containing 2.7–3.3% (w/w; Shandong Lierkang Medical Technology Co., Ltd.) and 2.5–3.5% (w/w; Guangdong Hengjian Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd.), were used. These disinfectant samples were analyzed directly without any further treatment. The results obtained using the electrochemical sensing method developed in this study were compared with those of the standard titration approach (National Standard of the People’s Republic of China, GB/T 1616–2014). As summarized in Table , the sensor achieved recovery rates of 99.67–104.0% and 99.67–102.0% for samples 1 and 2, respectively, with RSD values below 5.0%. These findings demonstrate that the developed electrochemical method is highly reliable and consistent with the standard titration approach for detecting H2O2 in real samples.

Limitations and Future Directions

Although this study establishes a promising foundation for sensing applications, several limitations warrant consideration. First, the interference study was limited to a few organic molecules; future research should incorporate common electroactive interferents, such as metal ions and oxidants, as well as real wastewater matrices, to adequately evaluate environmental applicability. Second, the analytical utility was demonstrated solely using simple disinfectant samples. Future studies should extend the validation to complex real-world samples, including biological fluids and industrial wastewater, to demonstrate their practical robustness. Third, given the use of surfactant-assisted synthesis, it is essential to investigate whether any residual surfactant on the Cu-TCPP/rGO surface hinders the accessibility of H2O2 to the active sites. Finally, considering the critical role of interfacial interactions in electron transfer, subsequent research should systematically assess how the degree of GO reduction affects the conductivity and sensing performance of nanocomposites. Addressing these issues will enhance our mechanistic understanding and practical viability of the developed sensors.

Conclusions

This study successfully fabricated a nonenzymatic H2O2 sensor based on a Cu-TCPP/rGO nanocomposite. The material was synthesized through a surfactant-assisted method that enabled the in situ growth of ultrathin Cu-TCPP nanosheets on rGO. This architecture resulted in a synergistic nanozyme system with outstanding electrocatalytic activity. The optimized sensor demonstrated excellent analytical performance for H2O2 detection, featuring a wide linear range (0.10–6.30 mM) and a detection limit of 0.03 mM. Furthermore, the high recoveries achieved in the analysis of disinfectant samples underscore the reliability and practical applicability of the method for real-sample screening. Given these attributes, the Cu-TCPP/rGO nanocomposite presents a promising platform for developing advanced sensing devices in medical and environmental monitoring.

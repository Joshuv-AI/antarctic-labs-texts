Some series representing the eta function for ℜ ⁡ s > 0 \Re s>0
Jean-François Burnol

Abstract
. We represent the Euler alternating series (sometimes called the “Dirichlet eta function”), and generally ( b s − b ) ​ ζ ​ ( s ) / b s (b^{s}-b)\zeta(s)/b^{s} for b > 1 b>1 an integer, in the half-plane ℜ ⁡ s > 0 \Re s>0 , via series dominated by geometric series, with arbitrarily small convergence ratio (up to the prize of a longer first approximation). Due to the underlying recurrence, the cost for each new term is at first sight linearly increasing, so the cost appears to be quadratic in the number of terms kept. And the number of terms needed to achieve a given target precision increases linearly with the imaginary part of s s .

As was kindly pointed out to the author by M. Balazard, no function

appears in particular in his 1749 work where he discovers, and proposes as

Euler defines the alternating series values at first via (now so-called)

Euler-Maclaurin summation. He checks that this gives the same result for

. Having famously evaluated earlier the
\zeta(2n)

(hence also the values at even integers of the alternating series), a
relation between

confirms to be true for

s=\frac{3}{2}
(with same audacious regularization at
s=-\frac{1}{2}
). He
explains that the whole endeavor was an attempt at discovering a formula for

\zeta(3)
,
\zeta(5)
, …. He also surmises the functional equation of
the Dirichlet L-function for the non-principal character modulo four. See

Remarques sur un beau rapport entre les séries des puissances tant
directes que réciproques
, 1749 (published in
Mémoires de l’académie
des sciences de Berlin
17, 1768, pp. 83–106).

\eta(s)=\frac{2^{s}-2}{2^{s}}\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n^{s}}.

The above series representation converges in the half-plane
\Re s>0

\eta

\eta(1)=\log 2

More generally, for any integer

Define

\displaystyle c_{0,b}^{*}(s)

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle m\geq 1

Theorem 1
.

For any integer

\eta(s)=\frac{2^{s}-2}{2^{s}}\sum_{0<n<2^{\ell-1}}\frac{1}{n^{s}}+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}\frac{s\,c_{m,2}^{*}(s)}{s+m}\sum_{2^{\ell-1}\leq n<2^{\ell}}\frac{1}{n^{s+m}}\,.

\displaystyle c_{m,2}^{*}(\sigma)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{\sigma}-2^{-m}}{2^{\sigma}-1}\,,

Remark 1
.

For
\sigma>1
it is known from properties of related quantities in

[
2

c_{m,2}^{*}(1)=1

for every

2

We used the notation
(s+1)_{m}=(s+1)\dots(s+m)
(Pochhammer symbol). Observe
that
m\mapsto|(s+1)_{m}|/(\sigma+1)_{m}
is non-decreasing. And as is well

(s+1)_{m}/m!\sim_{m\to\infty}\Gamma(s+1)^{-1}m^{s}
. This explains how the
first given upper bound for
|c_{m,2}^{*}(s)|
implies the second one, and
proves that for any given
s
these coefficients are
O(1)
and the terms of
the series (hence its remainders) are
O(r^{m}/m)
with
r=2^{1-\ell}
.

Due to the
exponential increase of
|\Gamma(s)|^{-1}
on vertical lines, it is needed (at
least for the
s_{k}=\sigma+2\pi ki/\log(2)
points), when the imaginary part
increases, to augment linearly the number of kept terms in order to reach a given
number

of digits of fixed-point precision. As the cost for each new term
from the recurrence (
1
) appears to be increasing linearly, we expect
the cost to be at least quadratic in
N
. This could mean that the algorithm
is suitable only for imaginary parts of moderate size.

\displaystyle+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}\frac{s\,c_{m,2}^{*}(s)}{s+m}\bigl(4^{-s-m}+5^{-s-m}+6^{-s-m}+7^{-s-m}\bigr),

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove one direction of a conjecture of Navarro–Rizo–Schaeffer Fry–Vallejo positing an algorithm to determine from the character table whether a finite group has 2 2 -generated Sylow 3 3 -subgroups. This gives further evidence of the blockwise version of the Galois–McKay conjecture (also known as the Alperin–McKay–Navarro conjecture). A key step involves proving the Isaacs–Navarro Galois conjecture for principal blocks for finite groups with a certain structure.

1.
Introduction

In Problem 12 of his famous list of problems from 1963, Brauer asked what information could be gleaned about a Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
of a finite group
G
from its character table. In particular, the question of whether the number of generators of
P
could be obtained in this way has been studied in recent years, see e.g.
[
RSV20
,
NRSV21
,
MS23
,
Va23
]
. Many questions in this realm seem linked to the action of the Galois automorphism
\sigma\in{\rm Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{ab}/\mathbb{Q})
that fixes
p^{\prime}
-roots of unity and sends
p
-power roots of unity to their
1+p
power. For any cyclotomic extension
\mathbb{Q}_{n}:=\mathbb{Q}(e^{2\pi i/n})
of
\mathbb{Q}
, we keep the notation
\sigma
for the restriction of this automorphism to
\mathbb{Q}_{n}
.
Here, we contribute to this line of problems by proving the following direction of the main conjecture from
[
NRSV21
]
.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{3}(G)
. Then
|P:\Phi(P)|=9
if the principal
3
-block
B_{0}(G)
contains exactly
6
or
9

\sigma
-invariant characters of degree coprime to
3
.

As noted in
[
NRSV21
]
, Theorem
A
(and its converse) would follow from the Alperin–McKay–Navarro conjecture
[
Nav04
, Conj. B]
, or even the more restrictive version
[
IN02
, Conj. D]
. Hence, this result gives further evidence for these elusive conjectures. We remark that although the block-free version
[
IN02
, Conj. C]
was recently established in
[
RS25
]
, the blockwise version (even for principal blocks) is much further from completion and has not yet been reduced to a problem on simple groups. For this reason, finding additional evidence of this blockwise version remains pertinent.

Theorem
A
can be thought of simultaneously as an extension of the main result of
[
NRSV21
]
, which addressed the analogous question for
p=2
, and of the main result of
[
GRSV25
]
, which showed that
[P:P^{\prime}]=9
if and only if
B_{0}(G)
contains exactly 6 or 9 irreducible characters of degree coprime to
3
.

Our proof of Theorem
A
uses the recent work of Ketchum
[
Ket25
]
, which shows that the statement of Theorem
A
(and its converse) hold for almost simple groups. It also uses a reduction theorem and additional results on almost simple groups analogous to those in
[
GRSV25
]
. Moreover, the reduction relies on beautiful work of R. Brauer
[
Bra76
]
on the structure of groups having cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroups.

As mentioned above,
[
IN02
, Conj. C]
has been completed in
[
RS25
]
. Our next main theorem considers the principal block version, which is part of
[
IN02
, Conj. D]
, and is a key step towards the proof of Theorem
A
. We believe it may be of independent interest. Let
\mathcal{H}\leq{\rm Gal}(\mathbb{Q}^{ab}/\mathbb{Q})
be the Galois group considered in
[
Nav04
]
.

Theorem B
.

Let
G
be a finite group of order divisible by
p
, let
N\triangleleft\,G
be an abelian
p
-group and assume
G/N
has cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroups and let
\tau\in\mathcal{H}
be a
p
-power order element. Then
\tau
fixes the same number of height-zero characters in
\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))
as in
\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}({\bf N}_{G}(P)))
, where
P\in{\rm Syl}_{p}(G)
.

In particular,
[
IN02
, Conj. D]
holds for principal blocks of groups satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem
B
. In fact, as explained in Remark
3.4
, the same result holds by assuming only that every block of maximal defect of
G/N
satisfies
[
IN02
, Conj. D]
, or
[
Nav04
, Conj. B]
. (See also
[
MMSV26
, Prop. 5.4]
for another related, but restrictive, case.)

The paper is structured as follows: Section
2
contains preliminary lemmas and results, as well as a Galois version of
[
GRSV25
, Thm. B]
. Theorem
B
is proved in Section
3
. Theorem

is reduced to simple groups in Section

2.1.
Galois action and principal blocks

We begin by collecting some basic facts about principal blocks. Throughout, given a prime
p
,
B_{0}(G)
denotes the principal
p
-block of the finite group
G
. We denote by
\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))
the set of irreducible characters in
B_{0}(G)
and by
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(G))
the subset of those with height zero (that is,
p^{\prime}
-degree). The set of
\sigma
-invariant characters in
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(G))
will be denoted
\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G))
, and we set
k_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G)):=|\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G))|
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group, let
N\triangleleft\,G
and let
p
be a prime.

(i)

{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(G/N))\subseteq{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(G))
, and if
N
has order not divisible by
p
then
{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(G/N))={\rm Irr}(B_{0}(G))
.

(ii)

For any
\theta\in{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(N))
, there is some
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(G))
lying over
\theta
.

(iii)

For any
\chi\in{\rm Irr}(B_{0}(G))
, every constituent of
\chi_{N}
lies in
B_{0}(N)
.

(iv)

If
G/N
is a
p
-group, then
B_{0}(G)
is the unique block covering
B_{0}(N)
.

(v)

Assume
G=H_{1}\times\dots\times H_{t}
. Then

\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))=\{\theta_{1}\times\dots\times\theta_{t}\mid\theta_{i}\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(H_{i}))\}.

Proof.

The first part of (i) follows from the discussion before
[
Nav98
, Thm. 7.6]
, and the second part is
[
Nav98
, Thm. 9.9(c)]
. Part (ii) is
[
Nav98
, Thm. 9.4]
, part (iii) is
[
Nav98
, Thm. 9.2, Cor. 9.3]
and part (iv) is
[
Nav98
, Cor. 9.6]
. Part (v) is
[
NT16
, Lem. 2.6(b)]
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group and let
N=S_{1}\times\cdots\times S_{t}
be a minimal normal subgroup of
G
, where the
S_{i}
are transitively permuted by
G
. Suppose that
G/N=\langle xN\rangle
is cyclic,
{\bf C}_{G}(N)=1
and
{\bf N}_{G}(S_{i})=N
. Then
G/N\lesssim{\mathrm{Out}}(N)={\mathrm{Out}}(S_{1})\wr{\sf S}_{t}
. In particular, after a suitable reordering of the
S_{i}
’s,
xN
acts on
N
as
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{t})\tau\in{\mathrm{Out}}(S_{1})\wr\mathsf{S}_{t}
where
\tau=(1...t)
, and we have

(i)

\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{t}=1
;

(ii)

if
\eta\in\operatorname{Irr}(S_{1})
, then the character

\theta=\eta\times\eta^{\alpha_{1}}\times\eta^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\times\cdots\times\eta^{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{t}}

is
G
-invariant.

Proof.

Since
G
acts transitively on the components of
N
,
G/N\leq{\mathrm{Out}}(N)\cong{\mathrm{Out}}(S_{1})\wr\mathsf{S}_{t}
.
Thus,
xN
seen as an element of
{\rm Out}(N)
acts on characters of
N
like some
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{r})\tau
with
\alpha_{i}\in{\rm Out}(S)
and
\tau\in{\sf S}_{t}
of order
t
and after a suitable reordering of the elements we may assume
\tau=(1...t)
.
Moreover, since
{\bf N}_{G}(S_{1})=N
, then
o(xN)=|G:N|=t
and we have that

1=((\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{t})\tau)^{t}=(\prod_{j=0}^{t-1}\alpha_{\tau^{j}(1)},\dots,\prod_{j=0}^{t-1}\alpha_{\tau^{j}(t)})\tau^{t}.

Therefore,

\prod_{j=0}^{t-1}\alpha_{\tau^{j}(1)}=\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{t}=1,

proving part (i).
Now, if
\eta\in{\rm Irr}(S)
, then let
\theta=\eta\times\eta^{\alpha_{1}}\times\eta^{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\times\cdots\times\eta^{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{t}}
and notice that
\theta^{x}=\theta
by part (ii).
Since
G/N=\langle xN\rangle
,
\theta
is
G
-invariant.
∎

Theorem 2.3

(Alperin–Dade)
.

Let
N\triangleleft\,G
and
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
. If
p
does not divide
|G:N|
and
N{\bf C}_{G}(P)=G
, then restriction defines a bijection

\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G))\rightarrow\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(N)).

Proof.

The fact that restriction defines a bijection

\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(G))\rightarrow\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(N))
was proved when

is solvable in
[
Alp76
]
and without the solvability hypothesis in
[
Dad77
]
. Since
\chi_{N}^{\sigma}=(\chi^{\sigma})_{N}

∎

.

The following is a principal block version of
[
MS23

.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
G
be a finite group with
{\bf O}_{p^{\prime}}(G)=1
and let
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
. Then

K=\bigcap_{\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G))}{\rm ker}(\chi)\leq\Phi(P).

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}(P/\Phi(P))
. Write
P{\bf C}_{G}(P)=P\times X
and let
\psi=\lambda\times 1_{X}\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(P{\bf C}_{G}(P)))
. By Lemma
2.4
we have that
\lambda^{G}
contains a constituent
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G))
. By Frobenius reciprocity,
\chi_{P{\bf C}_{G}(P)}
contains
\lambda\times 1_{X}
so
\chi_{P}
contains
\lambda_{P}
. Moreover
\chi_{P\cap K}=\chi(1)1_{P\cap K}
contains
\lambda_{P\cap K}
which shows that
{\rm ker}(\lambda)
contains
P\cap K
. Therefore

P\cap K\subseteq\bigcap_{\lambda\in\operatorname{Irr}(P/\Phi(P))}{\rm ker}(\lambda)=\Phi(P).

By Tate’s theorem
[
Nav18
, Cor. 6.14]
, this implies that
K
is
p
-nilpotent. Then if
K
is not a
p
-group,
{\bf O}_{p^{\prime}}(K)>1
, which contradicts
{\bf O}_{p^{\prime}}(G)=1
. Therefore
K
is a normal
p
-subgroup so
K=P\cap K\subseteq\Phi(P)
, as desired.
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
p\in\{2,3\}
, and
P\in\operatorname{Syl}_{p}(G)
. Let
N\triangleleft\,G
and assume
p
divides
|G:N|
. Suppose that
\theta\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(N))
is
P\times\langle\sigma\rangle
-invariant. Then
p
divides
k_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G)|\theta)
. In particular, if
\theta
extends to
\hat{\theta}\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(PN))
then
k_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G)|\theta)\geq p
.

Proof.

Arguing as in
[
GRSV25
, Thm. 2.7]
we get that

\sum_{\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(G)|\theta)}\chi(1)^{2}\equiv 0\bmod\,p.

Recall that
\langle\sigma\rangle
is a
p
-group and acts on
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(G)|\theta)
. Write

\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(G)|\theta)=\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G)|\theta)\cup\Omega_{1}\cup\dots\cup\Omega_{t}

where the
\Omega_{i}
’s are the nontrivial
\langle\sigma\rangle
-orbits, and let
\chi_{i}
be a representative of each
\Omega_{i}
. Since each element of
\Omega_{i}
has degree
\chi_{i}(1)
and
|\Omega_{i}|\equiv 0\bmod\,p
, we have

\sum_{\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(G)|\theta)}\chi(1)^{2}=\sum_{\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G)|\theta)}\chi(1)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{t}|\Omega_{i}|\chi_{i}(1)^{2}\equiv\sum_{\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G)|\theta)}\chi(1)^{2}\bmod\,p

and this shows that

\sum_{\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G)|\theta)}\chi(1)^{2}\equiv 0\bmod\,p.

Now, for every
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G)|\theta)
we have
\chi(1)^{2}\equiv 1\bmod\,p
and we conclude that
p
divides

k_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G)|\theta)
, as desired.

For the final part, notice that if there is some
\hat{\theta}\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(PN)|\theta)
extending
\theta
then there is
\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(PN{\bf C}_{G}(P))|\theta)
extending
\hat{\theta}
by Theorem
2.3
. By Lemma
2.4
,
\psi^{G}
contains some
\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G))
. Therefore
k_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G)|\theta)>0
by Frobenius reciprocity, so the result follows.

2.2.
Structure of groups with a cyclic Sylow
3
-subgroup

The following result of Brauer (which relies on a result of Herzog in
[
Her70
]
) will be essential in the final step of our reduction theorem for Theorem A. We restate it here for the reader’s convenience.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
G
be a finite group, let
p=3
and suppose that
G
has cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroups. Then one of the following holds:

(i)

G
is
p
-solvable and there exists
L\leq G
with
|G:L|\in\{1,2\}
such that
L
has a normal
p
-complement.

(ii)

G
is not
p
-solvable and for every
N\lhd G
, either
N
or
G/N
is of order not divisible by
p
.

Proof.

We use the notation of
[
Bra76
, Sec. 2]
. Let
P
be a Sylow 3-subgroup of
G
and let
r=|{\bf N}_{G}(P):P{\bf C}_{G}(P)|
. Since
R={\bf N}_{G}(P)/P{\bf C}_{G}(P)\leq{\rm Aut}(P)
, we have that
r=|R|
is not divisible by
p
and since
P
is cyclic, we have that
r\mid p-1=2
.

As in
[
Bra68
, p. 562]
, we say that a group with cyclic Sylow subgroups is of metacyclic type if there exists
K\lhd G
of index
p^{a}r
, where
|P|=p^{a}
.

Now, if
G
is of metacyclic type, then
G
is
p
-solvable by
[
Bra76
, Thm. 2C]
, and we are in case (i). If
r=1
, we have that
G
has a normal
p
-complement, as wanted. If
r=2
, the result follows by
[
Bra76
, Prop. 2F]
.

On the other hand, if
G
is not of metacyclic type, then it is not
p
-solvable (again by
[
Bra76
, Thm. 2C]
), and we are in case (ii). The result now follows by
[
Bra76
, Thm. 3C]
.
∎

3.
Theorem
B

The purpose of this section is to prove Theorem
B
. We start with the following refinement of
[
Nav18
, Lem. 9.3]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\chi\in{\rm Irr}_{p^{\prime},\tau}(G)
, where
\tau\in{\rm Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}^{\mathrm{ab}}/{\mathbb{Q}})
has
p
-power order, and let
N
be a normal subgroup of
G
. Then
\chi_{N}
has a
P\times\langle\tau\rangle
invariant constituent, and any two of them are
{\bf N}_{G}(P)
-conjugate.

Proof.

Let
\mu\in{\rm Irr}(N)
lying under
\chi
. Since
|G:G_{\mu}|
is not divisible by
p
(by the Clifford correspondence and using that
p
does not divide
\chi(1)
) and
\langle\tau\rangle
is a
p
-group, we have that there is at least one
\langle\tau\rangle
-invariant
\theta\in{\rm Irr}(N)
lying under
\chi
. But then for
g\in G
, we have
(\theta^{g})^{\tau}=(\theta^{\tau})^{g}=\theta^{g}
and we have that all of the irreducible constituents of
\chi_{N}
are
\langle\tau\rangle
-invariant. Now, by
[
Nav18
, Lem. 9.3]
, we conclude that
\chi_{N}
has a
P\times\langle\tau\rangle
-invariant constituent and any two of them are
{\bf N}_{G}(P)
-conjugate.
∎

We will also need the following result of Murai. For the convenience of the reader, we restate the theorem in our notation. Recall that if
B
is a Brauer
p
-block of
G
and
\lambda
is a linear character of
G
, then the set
\lambda B=\{\lambda\chi\>|\>\chi\in{\rm Irr}(B)\}
is a Brauer
p
-block of
G
(see
[
Riz18
, Lem. 2.1]
, for instance).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
N
be a normal subgroup of
G
, let
B
be a
p
-block of
G
, let
\xi\in{\rm Irr}(G)
be a linear character and let
\overline{B}
be a
p
-block of
G/N
. Let
b
and
\overline{b}
be the Brauer correspondents of
B
and
\overline{B}
, respectively. Then
\xi\overline{B}
is dominated by
B
if, and only if,
\xi_{N_{G}(P)}\overline{b}
is dominated by
b
.

Proof.

This is, essentially,
[
Mur98
, Cor. 2.5]
. Write
H={\bf N}_{G}(P)
. To apply this result, we just need to check that
\xi^{-1}B
and
\xi_{H}^{-1}b
are Brauer correspondents. But this easily follows from the definition of induced blocks (see
[
Nav98
, p. 87]
). Let
K
be a conjugacy class of
G

Theorem 3.3
.

Let

with

\theta

\langle\tau\rangle

\theta

,

\langle\tau\rangle

Remark 3.4
.

Let

S=\operatorname{PSL}^{\epsilon}_{3}(q)
. We then claim that
(b)
holds. Take
\theta=\hat{\theta}_{S}
, with
\hat{\theta}
being the Steinberg character in
\tilde{S}
, where
\widetilde{S}
is
{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}(q)
and
{\mathrm{PGL}}_{3}^{\epsilon}(q)
in the respective cases. Then
\theta
is
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
-invariant by
[
Mal08
, Thm. 2.5]
and extends to
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
by
[
Mal08
, Thm. 2.4]
and we see that
\hat{\theta}\in\operatorname{Irr}_{\sigma}(B_{0}(\widetilde{S}))
by arguing as in the second and third paragraphs of the proof of
[
RSV20
, Prop. 3.9]
.
Let
S\leq T\leq\operatorname{Aut}(S)_{\theta}=\operatorname{Aut}(S)
. We clearly have that
\theta
extends to
\hat{\theta}_{T\cap\tilde{S}}\in\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(T\cap\tilde{S}))
. Since the Steinberg character is rational-valued, so is
\hat{\theta}_{T\cap\widetilde{S}}
. We have that
T/(T\cap\tilde{S})
is an abelian group and therefore has a normal Sylow
3
-subgroup. Let
M
be the preimage under
\pi:T\rightarrow T/(T\cap\tilde{S})
of this Sylow
3
-subgroup. Then
B_{0}(M)
is the unique block above

by Lemma
2.1
and we use
[
Nav18
, Cor. 6.6(a)]
to obtain a rational extension
\chi
of
\hat{\theta}_{T\cap\tilde{S}}
in
\operatorname{Irr}(B_{0}(M))
. Finally, using Lemma
5.3
, we obtain a character
\psi
in
\operatorname{Irr}_{\sigma}(B_{0}(T)|\chi)
. Since
\psi
lies above
\hat{\theta}_{T\cap\tilde{S}}
and
T/(T\cap\widetilde{S})
is abelian, it follows that
\psi
must be an extension of
\hat{\theta}_{T\cap\widetilde{S}}
(and hence of
\theta
), as desired.

Now Assume
S
is not one of the groups discussed above. Then

GRSV25
, Prop. 5.8]
gives
3

\operatorname{Aut}(S)

gives that these characters must be

.

We next show (ii). Note that

If

GRSV25
, Prop. 5.8]
gives
3
unipotent characters with distinct

\sigma

.

So, now assume

or that

and
S=\operatorname{PSL}^{\epsilon}_{3}(q)
. In these cases let
G={\mathrm{SL}}_{2}(q)
and
G={\mathrm{SL}}^{\epsilon}_{3}(q)
respectively. We also let

, resp.
\widetilde{G}={\mathrm{GL}}^{\epsilon}_{3}(q)
and

{\mathrm{PGL}}^{\epsilon}_{3}(q)

5.3.
Related Results

We end by making some observations to give evidence of the analogue of Theorem
A
for arbitrary blocks. We first obtain the statement for groups of Lie type defined in characteristic
3
whose ambient algebraic group has connected center.

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
G=\mathbf{G}^{F}
be a group of Lie type, where
\mathbf{G}
is a connected reductive group over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{3}
such that
\mathbf{Z}(\mathbf{G})
is connected. Let
B
be a
3
-block of
G
with defect group
D
. Then
[D:\Phi(D)]=9
if
|\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B)|\in\{6,9\}
.

Proof.

By a result of Dagger and Humphreys, the only blocks with positive defect have maximal defect, and these are indexed by the characters of
{\bf Z}({G})
(see
[
Hum71
]
).
First assume
\mathbf{G}
has no component of type
\operatorname{G}_{2}
. Then any block
B
with positive defect satisfies
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B)=\operatorname{Irr}_{3^{\prime}}(B)
is comprised of semisimple characters in
B
, using the degree properties of Jordan decomposition and that nontrivial unipotent characters are divisible by
3
by
[
Mal07
, Thm. 6.8]
.

Since
\mathbf{Z}(\mathbf{G})
is connected and the semisimple elements of
{G}^{\ast}
have order prime to
3
and
\sigma
fixes
3^{\prime}
-roots of unity, we then have
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B)=\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B)
using the main result of
[
SV20
]
.
Assume that
|\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B)|\in\{6,9\}
and let
B
be the block indexed by the character
\theta\in\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbf{Z}(G))
.
Now, the number of semisimple characters in
\operatorname{Irr}(B)
is the number of
G^{\ast}
-classes of semisimple elements
s\in G^{\ast}
such that the characters in
\mathcal{E}(G,s)
lie above
\theta
. But as noted in
[
ST23
, Prop. 2.7(iii)]
(see also the proofs of
[
NT13
, Lem. 4.4]
,
[
MNST24
, Prop. 3.7]
) this is the number of semisimple classes in the corresponding coset in
G^{\ast}/\mathbf{O}^{3^{\prime}}(G^{\ast})
. Now, for each semisimple
s\in G^{\ast}
, there is some
T^{\ast}:=(\mathbf{T}^{\ast})^{F}
containing
s
, where
\mathbf{T}^{\ast}\leq\mathbf{G}^{\ast}
is some
F
-stable maximal torus. By
[
GM20
, Rem. 1.5.13(b, c)]
,
T^{\ast}
induces all cosets of
G^{\ast}/\mathbf{O}^{3^{\prime}}(G^{\ast})
. Then by
[
FG12
, Cor. 2.4]
, we see each such coset contains the same number of semisimple classes. This means that
|\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B)|=|\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(G))|
. Recalling that
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(G))=\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G))
as before, we therefore have
|\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(G))|\in\{6,9\}
. Then
[P:\Phi(P)]=9
by Theorem
A
.

Finally, suppose that
\mathbf{G}
has components of type
\operatorname{G}_{2}
. Here by
[
Mal07
, Thm. 6.8]
, the group
\operatorname{G}_{2}(q)
(where
q
is a power of
3
) has 7 unipotent characters in
\operatorname{Irr}_{3^{\prime}}(\operatorname{G}_{2}(q))
. The unipotent characters of
\operatorname{G}_{2}(q)
are all
\sigma
-stable, using
[
Gec03
, Table 1 and Prop. 5.5]
. Note that these 7 unipotent characters then lie in
\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B_{0}(\operatorname{G}_{2}(q)))
since
\mathbf{Z}(\operatorname{G}_{2})
is connected, and hence there is a unique block of positive defect from the first paragraph. Hence the statement is trivially satisfied if
\mathbf{G}
is simple.

So, assume that
\mathbf{G}
is not simple. Since we have assumed
\mathbf{Z}(\mathbf{G})
is connected, we have by
[
GM20
, Lem. 1.7.7]
that
\mathbf{G}^{F}=[\mathbf{G},\mathbf{G}]^{F}\mathbf{T}^{F}
for any
F
-stable maximal torus
\mathbf{T}
of
\mathbf{G}
. Then
G
is a quotient of the direct product of
[\mathbf{G},\mathbf{G}]^{F}
with an abelian group
\mathbf{T}^{F}
of order prime to
3
, and hence we may without loss replace
\mathbf{G}
with
[\mathbf{G},\mathbf{G}]
, and therefore assume that
\mathbf{G}
is semisimple. (Indeed, recall that
\sigma
will fix any character in
\operatorname{Irr}(\mathbf{T}^{F})
, each of which lie in their own block.) Then
\mathbf{G}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{G}_{i}
for some simple reductive groups
\mathbf{G}_{i}
which commute pairwise. We may assume that
\mathbf{G}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{G}_{k}
are the components of type
\operatorname{G}_{2}
and write
\mathbf{H}
for the product of these. Since each
\mathbf{G}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
has trivial center (as the only simple group of type
\operatorname{G}_{2}
is the adjoint group), it follows that
\mathbf{H}
is a direct product and
\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{H}\times\mathbf{H}^{\prime}
where
\mathbf{H}^{\prime}
is the product of simple components not of type
\operatorname{G}_{2}
. In particular, by
[
GM20
, Cor. 1.5.16]
,
G
has a direct factor of the form
\operatorname{G}_{2}(q)
with
q
some power of
3
, and the result follows from the previous paragraph.
∎

The next observation follows from Theorem
A
and the fact that blocks of general unitary and linear groups behave similarly to certain principal blocks.

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
\widetilde{G}={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
and let
B
be a
3
-block of
\widetilde{G}
with defect group
D
. Then
[D:\Phi(D)]=9
if

|\operatorname{Irr}_{0,\sigma}(B)|\in\{6,9\}
.

Proof.

If
q
is a power of
3
, this is from Corollary
5.9
.
So now assume that
q
is a power of some prime
p\neq 3
. Let
B
be a block in
\mathcal{E}(\widetilde{G},s)
for some semisimple
3^{\prime}
-element
s\in\widetilde{G}^{\ast}
. Now, by
[
FS82
, Thm. (7A)]
, Jordan decomposition yields a bijection between
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B)
and
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(b)
, where
b
is some unipotent block of
C:={\bf C}_{\widetilde{G}^{\ast}}(s)
. Here
B
and
b
share a defect group
D
. This bijection is further
\sigma
-equivariant by
[
SV20
]
.

Now, let
e
be the order of
\epsilon q
modulo
3
, so that
e\in\{1,2\}
. If
e=1
, then in fact the only unipotent block of
C
is the principal block, so the result now follows from Theorem
A
.

So, suppose
e=2
. We may write
C:=\prod C_{i}
as the product of lower-rank general linear or unitary groups
C_{i}
, depending on the invariant factors of
s
. Say
C_{i}:={\mathrm{GL}}_{m_{i}}(\eta_{i}q_{i})
with
q_{i}
some power of
q
,
\eta_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}
, and
m_{i}
a positive integer. Let
e_{i}
be the order of
\eta_{i}q_{i}
modulo
3
. Then the results of
[
MO83
]
yield that
D
is isomorphic to a Sylow
3
-subgroup of
C^{\prime}:=\prod{\mathrm{GL}}_{e_{i}w_{i}}(\eta_{i}q_{i})
for some positive integer
w_{i}
and further there is a bijection between
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(b)
and
\operatorname{Irr}_{0}(B_{0}(C^{\prime}))
. This is further
\sigma
-equivariant, which can be seen from the construction in
[
MO83
, P.209-210]
and using
[
SV20
]
to see the action of
\sigma
on characters in
\mathcal{E}(C_{i},t)
depend only on
s_{i}
and
t
, and these will have the same eigenvalues as the corresponding embeddings in
C_{i}^{\prime}
.
Hence, the result again follows from Theorem
A
.
∎

Variational closures for composite homogenised fluid flows
Theo Diamantakis, Ruiao Hu, James-Michael Leahy 1. Department of Mathematics, Imperial College London, UK2. Department of Mathematics, Imperial College London, UK and PhysicsX, UK

Abstract
Homogenisation theory has seen recent applications [ 21 , 36 , 29 ] in deriving stochastic transport models for fluid dynamics. In this work, we first derive the stochastic Lagrange-to-Euler map that underpins stochastic transport noise in fluid dynamics as the homogenisation limit of a parameterised flow map decomposing into rapidly fluctuating and slow components. Specifically, we prove convergence of this parameterised flow map to a scale-separated limit under the assumptions of a weak invariance principle for the rapidly fluctuating component and path continuity for the slow component. In this limit, the rapidly fluctuating component converges to a stochastic flow of diffeomorphisms that transforms the full flow dynamics into an SDE-governed stochastic flow through composition, while the slow component requires closure. Our second contribution formulates two distinct variational closures for the slow component of the homogenised flow that exploit the composite structure of the stochastic flow. For the first closure, the critical points of a new variational principle satisfy a system of random-coefficient PDEs, which can be transformed into a system of stochastic PDEs via the coadjoint action of the stochastic flow map obtained from homogenising the fluctuating component. We show that these equations coincide with the stochastic Euler-Poincaré equations previously derived in [ 57 ] . For the second closure, we modify the assumptions on the slow component and the associated variational principle to derive averaged models inspired by previous work on mean flow dynamics such as the Generalized Lagrangian Mean.



Keywords:
Homogenisation, flow representation, stochastic variational principles, Euler equations.

Introduction

When modelling complex and often turbulent Geophysical Fluid Dynamics (GFD), one inevitably encounters physical processes that exist over a wide range of spatial and temporal scales. To accurately represent these processes in finite-resolution numerical simulations, parameterisation schemes are required. By making these schemes stochastic, model uncertainties can be introduced naturally. Stochastic parameterisation schemes have seen operational use in numerical weather forecasting
[
13
,
9
]
, where they have improved ensemble forecasting reliability and probabilistic skill scores. To guide the design of stochastic parameterisation schemes, one design principle is to preserve the geometric structures of the underlying deterministic fluid models.

The geometric structures of ideal fluid models are revealed by Lie-group invariant variational principles. As first noted by Arnold
[
7
]
, given a domain
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, solutions of the incompressible Euler fluid equations constitute time-dependent geodesic flow
g_{t}
on the manifold of volume-preserving diffeomorphisms (
g_{t}\in\operatorname{SDiff}(\mathcal{D})\,\forall t
). Building on Arnold’s idea of modelling fluid dynamics as flow on the Lie group of diffeomorphisms, a modern approach was developed in
[
52
]
, in which ideal fluid dynamics are derived from a Lie-group symmetry reduced Euler–Poincaré variational principle involving advected quantities. These advected quantities consist of fluid properties such as volume density, mass density, and heat that follow the Lagrangian trajectories of fluid parcels and contribute to the overall fluid dynamics. The presence of advected quantities introduces potential energies such that solutions are no longer free geodesic curves but rather forced geodesics in the manifold of diffeomorphisms. Nevertheless, solutions remain as smooth, time-parameterised curves on the manifold of diffeomorphisms.

One stochastic parameterisation approach for ideal fluid models replaces the smooth time-dependent curves of diffeomorphisms with Stratonovich stochastic processes. In this case, it is natural to consider the time-dependent stochastic flow of diffeomorphisms
g_{t}\in\operatorname{Diff}(\mathcal{D})
as a Stratonovich stochastic differential equation (SDE)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}g_{t}(X):=u_{t}(g_{t}(X))\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{k}\xi_{k}(g_{t}(X))\circ\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{t}\,,\quad g_{0}(X)=X\,,\quad X\in\mathcal{D}\,,

(1.1)

where
\xi_{k}
is a collection of prescribed noise vector fields and
u_{t}
is the drift velocity vector field whose evolution is to be determined. Example closures for the dynamics of
u_{t}
include
[
74
]
, which obtains the evolution of
u_{t}
by formally taking the time derivative of (
1.1
) and using momentum balance arguments similar to the deterministic case while postulating an Itô decomposition of forces, velocity fields, pressure fields, and Wiener processes. Another closure is the Location Uncertainty (LU) framework
[
73
,
30
]
, which derives the dynamics of
u_{t}
through a stochastic Reynolds transport theorem with additional forces required for energy conservation. For the scope of this paper, we consider the stochastic parameterisation framework known as Stochastic Advection by Lie Transport (SALT)
[
57
]
, which derives the dynamics of
u_{t}
through variational principles. To date, there are several derivations of SALT using stochastic variational principles, including Clebsch
[
57
,
59
]
and Hamilton-Pontryagin
[
5
,
42
]
approaches. These capabilities in structure-preserving stochastic parameterisations have led to numerous works on implementing SALT parameterisation in GFD models
[
18
,
19
,
62
,
25
,
63
]
and analysing SALT SPDEs
[
22
,
26
]
. Additionally, these stochastic parameterisations have driven new developments in stochastic data assimilation methods for GFD models using particle filters
[
20

The solution to the SDE (
1.1
) can be expressed as the composition of two semi-martingale flows,
\Xi_{t},\bar{g}_{t}\in\operatorname{Diff}(\mathcal{D})
, such that
g_{t}
is defined by

\displaystyle g_{t}(X)=(\Xi_{t}\circ\bar{g}_{t})(X)\,,\quad X\in\mathcal{D}\,.

(1.2)

Here,
\Xi_{t}
is a prescribed flow of diffeomorphisms satisfying
\mathrm{d}\Xi_{t}(X)=\sum_{k}\xi_{k}(\Xi_{t}(X))\circ\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{t}
, and
\bar{g}_{t}
is to be determined from the dynamics of
u_{t}
. The approach of decomposing the flow map is popular in studying multi-scale and multi-physics systems, such as wave-current interactions
[
51
]
and plasma dynamics
[
54
]
. In
[
21
]
, the Eulerian decomposition (
1.1
) of
g_{t}
is motivated by the homogenisation of a fast-slow decomposition
g_{t}=\Xi_{t/\varepsilon}\circ\bar{g}^{\varepsilon}_{t}
, where
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a small parameter and
\Xi_{t/\varepsilon}
is assumed to be a rapidly fluctuating map of the form
\Xi_{t/\varepsilon}(X)=X+\zeta_{t/\varepsilon}(X)
for a smooth function
\zeta_{t/\varepsilon}:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
acting on the local coordinates
X
of the domain. The present work extends the analysis in
[
21
,
33
]
by considering
g_{t}
as the homogenisation limit of the
\varepsilon
-parameterised flow
g_{t}^{\varepsilon}
defined by the composition
g^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\Xi^{\varepsilon}_{t}\circ\bar{g}^{\varepsilon}_{t}
, where we do not assume a linear decomposition of the fluctuating map
\Xi^{\varepsilon}_{t}
. Additionally, all three flows have non-trivial dependencies on
\varepsilon
. As we shall explain in Section

, the
\varepsilon

\bar{g}^{\varepsilon}_{t}
is crucial for the consistency of models arising from stochastic variational closures of the dynamics of
g_{t}
.

Complementary to this approach, the application of homogenisation theory to derive stochastic fluid dynamics with transport noise is not limited to homogenising slow-fast equations of Lagrangian particle trajectories. For example,
[
37
,
36
]
applies stochastic homogenisation to a slow-fast system of 2D Euler-like stochastic PDEs to derive the 2D Euler equation with transport noise. This is extended in
[
31
,
29
]
for a general class of PDE systems, from which stochastic Navier-Stokes equations in 2D and 3D can be obtained.

Main contributions.

The two main contributions of this work are as follows. First, we use the theory of rough flows to establish sufficient conditions on the flows of diffeomorphisms
\Xi^{\varepsilon}
and
\bar{g}^{\varepsilon}
to rigorously obtain a stochastic flow
g
in the
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
limit of the composition
g^{\varepsilon}=\Xi^{\varepsilon}\circ\bar{g}^{\varepsilon}
. The precise convergence result is stated in Theorem
2.4
. Second, in Section
3
, we derive stochastic closure dynamics for the drift velocity
u_{t}
in (
1.1
) through variational principles that exploit the geometric structure of the homogenised flow
g=\Xi\circ\bar{g}
obtained in Section
2
. These variational principles do not require explicit stochastic constraints and directly reveal the connections between stochastic and random-coefficient Euler–Poincaré equations based on flow map representations. This work opens a new avenue for analysing stochastic Euler–Poincaré equations, as the analysis of stochastic PDEs based on their flow maps is well established among analysts.

a_{t}^{\varepsilon}(x)=\gamma(\{\bar{g}_{t}^{\varepsilon}(X_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N_{1}},\{\Xi_{t}^{\varepsilon}(Y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N_{2}},\lambda_{t}^{\varepsilon},x)\,.

Thus, the lifted system does not immediately satisfy the assumptions of the finite-dimensional deterministic homogenisation literature
[
71
,
72
,
64
,
65
]
required to pass to the limit as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
. In
[
21
]
, the authors consider a similar multi-scale decomposition of the Lagrange-to-Euler map (
2.2
). The authors also wish to pass to the limit using deterministic homogenisation theory. However, the same aforementioned issue arises, nevertheless, they apply the standard deterministic homogenisation theory. Thus, in
[
21
]
, a gap remains to pass to the limit. Our approach can be taken to close this gap, and we explain this in Section

. The key to overcoming this difficulty is to first establish

in
C^{\alpha}([0,T];\operatorname{Diff}_{C^{n}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))

for

As originally discovered in
[

]
, and elaborated in
[

,
17
,
16
]
, it is convenient to recast the homogenisation problem as an application of the stability results of rough differential equations (RDEs) with respect to its driving path and a weak invariance principle. To do this, we define the canonically lifted rough path
\mathbf{B}^{\varepsilon}=(B^{\varepsilon},\mathbb{B}^{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{C}_{g}^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{K})

A.1
) by

B^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t\varepsilon^{-2}}\lambda_{s}^{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}s,\quad\mathbb{B}^{\varepsilon}_{st}=\int_{s}^{t}\delta B^{\varepsilon}_{su}\otimes\mathrm{d}B^{\varepsilon}_{u}.

Then (
2.3
) can be recast as a flow of rough diffeomorphisms (see Definition
A.1
) given by

\mathrm{d}\Xi^{\varepsilon}_{t}(\bar{X})=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\sigma_{k}(\Xi_{t}^{\varepsilon}(\bar{X})){\rm d}\mathbf{B}^{\varepsilon;k}_{t}\,,\quad\Xi_{0}^{\varepsilon}(\bar{X})=\bar{X}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\,.

(2.8)

In light of Theorem
A.1
, which states the stability of solutions and flows of RDEs in the rough path topology, the convergence of

will follow from the convergence in law of

. We introduce the requisite weak invariance principle assumption.

Assumption 2.1

(Ergodicity and Weak Invariance Principle)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
denote a smooth compact Riemannian manifold embedded in

-vector field. Let

\dot{\lambda}=h(\lambda)

(i)

(Ergodicity)
\phi

and there exists a
\phi

\int_{\Omega}\lambda\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{d}\lambda)=0\,,

(ii)

(Weak invariance principle)

K
-dimensional Brownian motion supported on the probability space

with covariance

for an anti-symmetric matrix

\Sigma=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[B^{\varepsilon}_{1}\otimes B^{\varepsilon}_{1}]\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\widetilde{\Gamma}=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[\mathbb{B}^{\varepsilon}_{01}]\,.

Theorem 2.2

(Convergence of
\Xi^{\varepsilon}

-adapted stochastic flow of diffeomorphisms such that for each

-adapted solution of the SDE

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}{\Xi}_{t}(\bar{X})=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{k,l=1}^{K}\Gamma^{kl}\left[\xi_{k}\,,\,\xi_{l}\right](\Xi_{t}(\bar{X}))\mathrm{d}t+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\xi_{k}(\Xi_{t}(\bar{X}))\circ\mathrm{d}W_{t}^{k}\,,\quad\Xi_{0}(\bar{X})=\bar{X}\,.

(2.9)

Proof.

By Theorem
A.1
, there exists a continuous (in the driving path) flow map

\Phi\in\operatorname{Lip}_{loc}\left(\mathcal{C}_{g}^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{K});C^{\alpha}([0,T];\operatorname{Diff}_{C^{n}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\right)

and

is the unique solution of the RDE

{\rm d}\Phi_{t}(\bar{X},\mathbf{Z})=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\sigma_{k}(\Phi_{t}(\bar{X},\mathbf{Z})){\rm d}\mathbf{Z}_{t}\,,\quad\Phi_{0}(\bar{X},\mathbf{Z})=\bar{X}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\,.

Assumption
2.1

\mathbf{B}^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow_{\mathbb{P}}\mathbf{B}^{\widetilde{\Gamma}}

, and hence

.

be an arbitrarily coordinate. Since solutions of RDEs agree with solutions of ODEs if the driving path

. It remains remains to verify that

is the solution of the SDE (

\mathrm{d}\eta_{t}(\bar{X})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,l=1}^{K}\widetilde{\Gamma}^{kl}[\sigma_{k},\sigma_{l}](\eta_{t}(\bar{X}))\mathrm{d}t+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\sigma_{k}(\eta_{t}(\bar{X}){\rm d}\mathbf{B}^{k}_{t}\,,\quad\Xi_{0}(\bar{X})=\bar{X}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\,,

\widetilde{\Gamma}^{kl}[\sigma_{k},\sigma_{l}]=(\sqrt{\Sigma}^{-1})^{\alpha l}\widetilde{\Gamma}^{kl}(\sqrt{\Sigma}^{-1})^{\beta l}[\xi_{\alpha},\xi_{\beta}]=:\Gamma^{\alpha\beta}[\xi_{\alpha},\xi_{\beta}]

\mathrm{d}\eta_{t}(\bar{X})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,l=1}^{K}\Gamma^{kl}[\xi_{k},\xi_{l}](\eta_{t}(\bar{X}))\mathrm{d}t+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\xi_{k}(\eta_{t}(\bar{X})){\rm d}\mathbf{W}^{k}_{t}\,,\quad\Xi_{0}(\bar{X})=\bar{X}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\,,

(Convergence of composition of maps)
.

Proof.

3
Mean flow closures via variational principles

The goal of this section is to derive closures for the dynamics of the mean flow velocity field
\bar{u}_{t}
appearing in (
2.10
), and explain the modelling procedures that motivated the dynamics. We consider two closures derived from variants of the Euler–Poincaré variational principle with advected quantities developed in
[
52
]
. The first is a stochastic closure of
\bar{u}_{t}
that is equivalent to the SALT variational principle
[
57
]
, albeit with a new formulation that reflects the decomposition of the flow map
g_{t}
. The second is a deterministic closure of
\bar{u}_{t}
that resembles the Generalised Lagrangian Mean (GLM) theory
[
2
,
47
]
. The stochastic closure is presented in Section
3.2
and the deterministic closure is presented in Section
3.3
. In preparation for the construction of these closures, we will recall the essential aspects of the previous section and introduce the geometric mechanics language necessary to discuss the constrained variations that are used in the variational principles in Sections
3.2
and
3.3
.

3.1
Geometric setting and variations

Let
(\mathcal{D},\mathbf{g})
denote a smooth, compact, connected, oriented
d
-dimensional boundaryless Riemannian manifold with metric
\mathbf{g}
. Let
\mu_{\mathbf{g}}\in\Lambda^{d}(\mathcal{D})
the associated volume form expressed in local coordinates
(x^{1},\ldots,x^{d})
as

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathbf{g}}=\sqrt{\left|\det g_{ij}\right|}\,dx^{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge dx^{d}\,.

Let
T>0
and
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
denote a probability space supporting an
K
-dimensional Brownian motion
W=(W^{1},\ldots,W^{K})
with identity covariance. Let
\mathbb{F}
denote the filtration generated by
W
.

In the previous section, for
\mathcal{D}=\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we have shown the homogenisation of the flow of diffeomorphisms
g^{\varepsilon}:\Omega\times[0,T]\rightarrow\operatorname{Diff}_{C^{n}}(\mathcal{D})
with fast + slow decomposition in the form of equation (
2.7
). This resulted in a stochastic diffeomorphism

as proven in Theorem
2.4
. The diffeomorphism
g
is defined through the composition
g=\Xi\circ\bar{g}
, where the constituent flow of diffeomorphisms
\Xi:\Omega\times[0,T]\rightarrow\operatorname{Diff}_{C^{n}}(\mathcal{D})
and
\bar{g}:\Omega\times[0,T]\rightarrow\operatorname{Diff}_{C^{n}}(\mathcal{D})
satisfy the following Stratonovich SDEs

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\Xi_{t}&=\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\xi_{k}\circ\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{k,l=1}^{K}\Gamma^{kl}\left[\xi_{k}\,,\,\xi_{l}\right]\mathrm{d}t\right)\Xi_{t}\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}\bar{g}_{t}=\bar{u}_{t}\bar{g}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\,.\end{split}

(3.1)

Here,
\xi\in\mathfrak{X}_{C^{n+2}}(\mathcal{D})^{K}
is a collection of
K

\bar{u}:\Omega\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}_{C^{n}}(\mathcal{D})
is an
\mathbb{F}
-adapted

-vector field.
The tangent lifted right action of
\Xi_{t},\bar{g}_{t}\in\operatorname{Diff}_{C^{n}}(\mathcal{D})
on vector fields

is denoted by concatenation from the right and it is equivalent to composition; that is, for
\phi\in\operatorname{Diff}(\mathcal{D})
and
X\in\mathfrak{X}
,
X\phi=T_{e}R_{\phi}X
where
R_{(\cdot)}:
is composition from the right. Lastly,
\left[\cdot\,,\,\cdot\right]:\mathfrak{X}_{C^{n}}(\mathcal{D})\times\mathfrak{X}_{C^{n}}(\mathcal{D})\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}_{C^{n-1}}(\mathcal{D})
denotes the commutator of vector fields. The stochastic flow of diffeomorphisms

\displaystyle\left<b\,,\,a\right>_{V\times V^{*}}=\left<b^{(0)}\,,\,a^{(0)}\right>_{\Lambda^{0}(\mathcal{D})\times\Lambda^{d}(\mathcal{D})}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left<b^{(i)}\,,\,a^{(i)}\right>_{\Lambda^{d-k_{i}}(\mathcal{D})\times\Lambda^{k_{i}}(\mathcal{D})}\,,

3.2
Stochastic mean flow closure

Fix the stochastic flow of diffeomorphisms
\Xi:\Omega\times[0,T]\rightarrow\operatorname{Diff}(\mathcal{D})
and the associated set
\mathscr{S}^{\Xi}(\operatorname{Diff}(\mathcal{D}))
. For

\displaystyle\bar{g}=:\Xi^{-1}g\,,\quad\bar{u}_{t}:=\mathrm{d}\bar{g}_{t}\bar{g}^{-1}_{t}\,,\quad u_{t}:=\Xi_{t*}\bar{u}_{t}=\operatorname{Ad}_{\Xi_{t}}\bar{u}_{t}\,.

To find the dynamics of

and

and an action functional

over the set

with parametric dependence on

\displaystyle S^{\Xi}[g,a_{0}]:=\int_{0}^{T}\ell(u_{t},a_{0}g^{-1}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\ell(u_{t},a_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t\,,

(3.12)

where the relationships between

and

are defined in equation (

). In the action principle (

in the Lagrangian. This is due to the Lagrangian of interests are typically quadratic in the

augments and the Lagrangian would be ill-defined when the martingale parts of

are included.

Remark 3.1

(Other regularisation procedures)
.

In the stochastic fluid dynamics literature, similar modelling choice are made for the ill-defined integration against the square of white noise if it arises. For example, in the derivation of the stochastic Navier Stokes equation given in
[
74
]
, the term “
\int{\ddot{W}}\,\mathrm{d}t
” appearing in the time derivative is balanced with pressure forces via an application of Doob-Meyer semi-martingale decomposition theorem.

Assuming that the Lagrangian
\ell
is smooth with respect to its variables, we define the variational derivatives
\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u}:\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{D})\times V^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}^{*}(\mathcal{D})
and
\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta a}:\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{D})\times V^{*}\rightarrow V
by

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\ell(u+\epsilon\delta u,a)=\left<\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u}(u,a)\,,\,\delta u\right>\,,\quad\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\ell(u,a+\epsilon\delta a)=\left<\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta a}(u,a)\,,\,\delta a\right>\,,

(3.13)

for all
\delta u\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{D})
and
\delta a\in V^{*}
. We will use the shorthand notation

\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}:=\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u}(u_{t},a_{t})\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta a_{t}}:=\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta a}(u_{t},a_{t})

for the variational derivatives evaluated at
(u_{t},a_{t})
for the rest of the paper when the context is clear.

An application of the principle of least action results in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

The flow of diffeomorphisms
g\in\mathscr{S}^{\Xi}(\operatorname{Diff}(\mathcal{D}))
is a critical point of
S^{\Xi}
as defined in (
3.12

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\left(\Xi_{t}^{*}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\right)+\mathcal{L}_{\bar{u}_{t}}\left(\Xi_{t}^{*}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t=\left(\Xi_{t}^{*}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta a_{t}}\right)\diamond\bar{a}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\,,

(3.14)

is satisfied.

Proof.

Using the variations generated by
e_{t,\epsilon}
to the
g_{t}
as given in (
3.8
) and their induced variations in
u_{t}
and
a_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}0=\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\Big|_{\epsilon=0}S^{\Xi}&=\int_{0}^{T}\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\Big|_{\epsilon=0}\ell(u_{t,\epsilon},a_{t,\epsilon})\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\left<\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\,,\,\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\Big|_{\epsilon=0}u_{t,\epsilon}\right>+\left<\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta a_{t,\epsilon}}\,,\,\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\Big|_{\epsilon=0}a_{t,\epsilon}\right>\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\left<\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\,,\,\operatorname{Ad}_{\Xi_{t}}\left(\frac{d}{dt}v_{t}+\operatorname{ad}_{v_{t}}\bar{u}_{t}\right)\right>+\left<\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta a_{t}}\,,\,-\left(\mathcal{L}_{v_{t}}\bar{a}_{t}\right)\Xi_{t}^{-1}\right>\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\left<\mathrm{d}\left(\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{\Xi_{t}}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\right)+\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\bar{u}_{t}}\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{\Xi_{t}}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\,\mathrm{d}t-\left(\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta a_{t}}\Xi_{t}\right)\diamond\bar{a}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\,,\,v_{t}\right>\,.\end{split}

In the last equality, we have used integration by parts for the time derivative and the property

so that the boundary terms vanishes. Since

is assumed to be

-valued continuous semi-martingale, using the Ito-Wentzel formula and product rule

]

\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{\Xi_{t}}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}
a
\mathbb{F}
-adapted continuous semimartingale with values in

. Thus, the integration by parts rule holds. Moreover, since

is arbitrary, applying the stochastic version of fundamental lemma of calculus of variations
[

,
79
]
yields the random-coefficient Euler–Poincaré equation (

Corollary 3.1
.

The random-coefficient Euler–Poincaré equation with advected quantities (
3.14
) arises from the following Euler–Poincaré constrained variational principle,

\displaystyle 0=\delta S=\delta\int_{0}^{T}\ell(u_{t},a_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t\,,

subject to constrained variations

\displaystyle\delta u_{t}=\operatorname{Ad}_{\Xi_{t}}\left(\partial_{t}v-\operatorname{ad}_{\operatorname{Ad}_{\Xi_{t}^{-1}}u_{t}}v\right)\,,\quad\delta a_{t}=-\left(\mathcal{L}_{v}\left(a_{t}\Xi_{t}^{-1}\right)\right)\Xi_{t}\,,

where
v\in C^{1}([0,T];\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{D})
is assumed to be an arbitrary variation vanishing at
t=0,T
.

Proof.

The variations of
u_{t}
and
a_{t}
are obtained in (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) respectively. Applying the standard Euler–Poincaré variational procedure then yields the desired results.
∎

We remark that the random-coefficient Euler–Poincaré equation (
3.14
) in its current form is expressed with a mixture of
\bar{u}_{t}
and
u_{t}
. That is, the momentum
\Xi_{t}^{*}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}
is expressed in terms of
u_{t}
and the transport vector field is
\bar{u}_{t}=\operatorname{Ad}_{\Xi_{t}^{-1}}u_{t}
. In the paragraphs that follows, we consider two equivalent forms of the Euler–Poincaré equation (
3.14
) by expressing
{\delta\ell}/{\delta u_{t}}
in terms of
\bar{u}_{t}
and
{\delta\ell}/{\delta a_{t}}
in terms of
\bar{a}_{t}
.

The action functional
S^{\Xi}
defined in (
3.12
) features a non-random, time independent Lagrangian
\ell
. By treating
\Xi
as an external parameter, we define a random, time-dependent Lagrangian
\ell^{\Xi}:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{D})\times V^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
where for all
(\bar{u},\bar{a}):\Omega\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{D})\times V^{*}
,

\displaystyle\ell^{\Xi}(\bar{u},\bar{a}):=\ell(\operatorname{Ad}_{\Xi_{t}}\bar{u},\bar{a}\Xi^{-1}_{t})\,.

(3.15)

Here, the Lagrangian
\ell^{\Xi}
is smooth in the
\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{D})
and
V^{*}
variables; the randomness and time dependence of
\ell^{\Xi}
are defined exclusively by
\Xi
. We will use the same shorthand for the variational derivatives

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\ell^{\Xi}}{\delta\bar{u}}:\Omega\times[0,T]\times\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{D})\times V^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}^{*}(\mathcal{D})\,,\quad\frac{\delta\ell^{\Xi}}{\delta\bar{u}_{t}}:=\frac{\delta\ell^{\Xi}}{\delta\bar{u}}(t,\bar{u}_{t},\bar{a}_{t})\,,

(3.16)

and similarly for
{\delta\ell^{\Xi}}/{\delta\bar{a}_{t}}
, where the
\Omega
dependence is suppressed as before. From the definition of
\ell^{\Xi}
, we have the equivalence of the Lagrangians
\ell(u,a)=\ell^{\Xi}(\bar{u},\bar{a})
, evaluated on the variables
\bar{u}
,
u
,
\bar{a}
and
a
, whose relations are given in equation (
3.11
).
From this relation, we have the
\epsilon
-derivatives

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\Big|_{\epsilon=0}\ell^{\Xi}(\bar{u}_{t,\epsilon},\bar{a}_{t,\epsilon})&=\left<\frac{\delta\ell^{\Xi}}{\delta\bar{u}_{t}}\,,\,\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\Big|_{\epsilon=0}\bar{u}_{t,\epsilon}\right>+\left<\frac{\delta\ell^{\Xi}}{\delta\bar{a}_{t}}\,,\,\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\Big|_{\epsilon=0}\bar{a}_{t,\epsilon}\right>\,,\\
\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\Big|_{\epsilon=0}\ell(u_{t,\epsilon},a_{t,\epsilon})&=\left<\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\,,\,\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\Big|_{\epsilon=0}u_{t,\epsilon}\right>+\left<\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta a_{t}}\,,\,\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\Big|_{\epsilon=0}a_{t,\epsilon}\right>\\
&=\left<\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\,,\,\operatorname{Ad}_{\Xi_{t}}\left(\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\Big|_{\epsilon=0}\bar{u}_{t,\epsilon}\right)\right>+\left<\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta a_{t}}\,,\,\left(\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\Big|_{\epsilon=0}\bar{a}_{t,\epsilon}\right)\Xi_{t}\right>\\
&=\left<\operatorname{Ad}_{\Xi_{t}}^{*}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\,,\,\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\Big|_{\epsilon=0}\bar{u}_{t,\epsilon}\right>+\left<\Xi_{t}^{*}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta a_{t}}\,,\,\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\Big|_{\epsilon=0}\bar{a}_{t,\epsilon}\right>\,.\end{split}

(3.17)

and we conclude,

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\ell^{\Xi}}{\delta\bar{u}_{t}}=\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{\Xi_{t}}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\,,\qquad\frac{\delta\ell^{\Xi}}{\delta\bar{a}_{t}}=\Xi^{*}_{t}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta a_{t}}:=\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta a_{t}}\Xi_{t}\,.

Thus, the random-coefficient Euler–Poincaré equation (
3.14
) can be cast into the standard Euler–Poincaré form with random, time-dependent Lagrangian
\ell^{\Xi}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\frac{\delta\ell^{\Xi}}{\delta\bar{u}_{t}}+\mathcal{L}_{\bar{u}}\frac{\delta\ell^{\Xi}}{\delta\bar{u}_{t}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{\delta\ell^{\Xi}}{\delta\bar{a}_{t}}\diamond\bar{a}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\,.

(3.18)

which is the Euler–Poincaré equation for the mean velocity vector field
\bar{u}_{t}
.

To express (
3.18
) in terms of the fluid variables
u
and
a
, we first state a prerequisite Lemma.

Lemma 3.1

(
\operatorname{Ad}^{*}
is natural under
\diamond
)
.

Let
V
be a vector space and
V^{*}
be its dual under a duality pairing
\left<\cdot\,,\,\cdot\right>
. Assume that
\operatorname{Diff}(\mathcal{D})
has a right representation on
V
and an induced right representation on
V^{*}
, both of which are denoted by concatenation. Then, let
\phi\in\operatorname{Diff}(\mathcal{D})
,
b\in V
and
a\in V^{*}
we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{\phi}\left(b\diamond a\right)=\left(b\phi\right)\diamond\left(a\phi\right)\,.

(3.19)

Proof.

Let
w\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{D})
be arbitrary, we first have the result

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{Ad}_{\phi}w}a=\phi_{*}\left(\mathcal{L}_{w}\phi^{*}a\right)=\left(\mathcal{L}_{w}a\phi\right)\phi^{-1}

(3.20)

This can be shown through the Cartan form of the Lie derivative

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{Ad}_{\phi}w}a=\mathbf{d}\left(\phi_{*}w\intprod a\right)+\phi_{*}w\intprod\mathbf{d}a=\phi_{*}\left(\mathbf{d}\left(w\intprod\phi^{*}a\right)+w\intprod\mathbf{d}\phi^{*}a\right)=\phi_{*}\left(\mathcal{L}_{w}\phi^{*}a\right)\,.

For an arbitrary Lie group
G
and their representation
\rho
on arbitrary vector spaces
V
and
V^{*}
, we have the analogous results expressed in through the representation and its induced representation by the Lie algebra. Using (
3.20
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left<\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{\phi}\left(b\diamond a\right)\,,\,w\right>=\left<b\diamond a\,,\,\operatorname{Ad}_{\phi}w\right>&=\left<-b\,,\,\mathcal{L}_{\left(\operatorname{Ad}_{\phi}w\right)}a\right>\\
&=\left<-b\,,\,\left(\mathcal{L}_{w}\,a\phi\right)\phi^{-1}\right>=\left<-b\phi\,,\,\mathcal{L}_{w}\,a\phi\right>=\left<\left(b\phi\right)\diamond\left(a\phi\right)\,,\,w\right>\,.\end{split}

Since
w
is arbitrary, we obtain (
3.19
).
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\Xi_{t}
be the solution to the Stratonovich SDE given in (
3.1
). Then, the random-coefficient Euler–Poincaré equation (
3.14
) is equivalent to the following Stratonovich stochastic Euler–Poincaré equation

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}+\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{u_{t}}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\,\mathrm{d}t+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\xi_{k}}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\circ\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{k,l=1}^{K}\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\Gamma^{kl}\left[\xi_{k}\,,\,\xi_{l}\right]}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta a_{t}}\diamond a_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\,.

(3.21)

Proof.

Applying the Kunita-Ito-Wentzel formula for
k
-forms
[
27
]
to compute the time derivative using the stochastic Lie-chain rule (see
[
24
]
for the relevant rough path generalisation), we have,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\left(\Xi_{t}^{*}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\right)&=\Xi_{t}^{*}\left(\mathrm{d}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{d}\Xi_{t}\Xi_{t}^{-1}}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\right)\\
&=\Xi_{t}^{*}\left(\mathrm{d}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{L}_{\xi^{k}}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\circ\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{k,l=1}^{K}\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma^{kl}\left[\xi_{k}\,,\,\xi_{l}\right]}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\,.\end{split}

which has the equivalent form in the
\operatorname{Ad}^{*}
,
\operatorname{ad}^{*}
notation as

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\left(\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{\Xi_{t}}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\right)=\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{\Xi_{t}}\left(\mathrm{d}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\xi^{k}}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\circ\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{k,l=1}^{K}\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\Gamma^{kl}\left[\xi_{k}\,,\,\xi_{l}\right]}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\,.

(3.22)

The Lie algebra automorphism property of
\operatorname{Ad}
and its corresponding identity for the dual operator
\operatorname{Ad}^{*}
state that for all
\phi\in\operatorname{Diff}(\mathcal{D})
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{ad}_{\operatorname{Ad}_{\phi}u}\operatorname{Ad}_{\phi}v\equiv\operatorname{Ad}_{\phi}\operatorname{ad}_{u}v\,,\quad\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\operatorname{Ad}_{\phi^{-1}}u}\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{\phi}m\equiv\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{\phi}\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{u}m\,,\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{D})\,,\quad m\in\mathfrak{X}^{*}(\mathcal{D})\,.\end{split}

(3.23)

which is sometimes known as the composite transport formula, see
[
58
]
. Combining equations (
3.22
), (
3.23
), we have from equation (
3.14
) that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{\Xi_{t}}\left(\mathrm{d}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}+\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{u_{t}}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\,\mathrm{d}t+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\xi_{k}}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\circ\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{k,l=1}^{K}\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\Gamma^{kl}\left[\xi_{k}\,,\,\xi_{l}\right]}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)=\left(\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta a_{t}}\Xi_{t}\right)\diamond\bar{a}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\,.

(3.24)

Noting that
\bar{a}_{t}=a_{t}\Xi_{t}
and using Lemma
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta a_{t}}\Xi_{t}\right)\diamond\bar{a}_{t}=\operatorname{Ad}_{\Xi_{t}}^{*}\left(\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta a_{t}}\diamond a_{t}\right)\,.

Applying the
\operatorname{Ad}^{*}_{\Xi_{t}^{-1}}
operation to both sides of equation (
3.24
), we obtain the stochastic Euler–Poincaré equation (
3.21
).
∎

The equivalence of the Euler–Poincaré equations, (
3.18
) and (
3.21
) implies that stochastic Euler–Poincaré equations can arise from random time-dependent Lagrangians. As we will see in the example of incompressible Euler equations in Section
4

We remark that the stochastic Euler–Poincaré equation (

. For

\displaystyle 0=\delta S[g,u,m]

\displaystyle=\delta\int_{0}^{T}\ell(u_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\left<m_{t}\,,\,\mathrm{d}g_{t}g_{t}^{-1}-u_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t-\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\xi_{k}\circ\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{k,l=1}^{K}\Gamma^{kl}\left[\xi_{k}\,,\,\xi_{l}\right]\,\mathrm{d}t\right>\,,

Remark 3.2

(Continuous path dependence of the slow vector field)
.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, the variational principles in this section can be applied to derive closures of the maps
g^{\varepsilon},\bar{g}^{\varepsilon}
discussed in Section
2
.

Consider the case that the Euler–Poincaré equation is driven by a path
\mathbf{B}^{\varepsilon}
approximating Brownian motion and is taken to be well-posed. Using rough stability techniques, we can deduce a Wong-Zakai principle holds for these equations that necessarily implies the convergence of
g^{\varepsilon}
to
g
solving (
3.21
). One may then ask if such a property holds for map
\bar{g}^{\varepsilon}
, and a Wong-Zakai principle to approximate the solution of (
3.18
).

Suppose that for every
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}]
rough path
\mathbf{Z}\in\mathcal{C}_{g}^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{K})
, there exists a classical solution of the rough partial differential equation

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}+\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{u_{t}}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\,\mathrm{d}t+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\xi_{k}}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Z}^{k}_{t}=0\,,

(3.25)

that is continuous as a map on
\mathcal{C}_{g}^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{K})
taking values in a space of
\alpha
-Hölder curves of vector fields with
C^{n}
regularity,
n\in\mathbb{N}
(see e.g.,
[
23
, Theorem 3.7]
). This implies we can write solutions in the form
u_{t}=U_{t}(\cdot,\mathbf{Z})
for a map
U\in C(\mathcal{C}_{g}^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{K});C^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathfrak{X}_{C^{n}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})))
. In Section
2
the map
\Xi
was constructed from a rough flow map
\Xi_{t}(\cdot):=\Phi_{t}(\cdot,\mathbf{Z})
with
\Phi\in\operatorname{Lip}_{loc}\left(\mathcal{C}_{g}^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{K});C^{\alpha}([0,T];\operatorname{Diff}_{C^{n-1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))\right)
in the proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Given the expression
\bar{u}_{t}=\operatorname{Ad}_{\Xi_{t}^{-1}}u_{t}
, it is natural to ask whether
\bar{u}
inherits this continuous path dependence. This motivates the Assumption
2.3
that was invoked in Section
2
. For the case of finite dimensional (matrix) Lie group
G
and its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, the analogue of (
3.25
) (with convex, smooth Lagrangian
\ell
) the adjoint representation
\operatorname{Ad}
is smooth and we have the continuous map,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ad}_{(\cdot)^{-1}}:C^{\alpha}([0,T];G)\times C\left([0,T];\mathfrak{g}\right)

\displaystyle\rightarrow C\left([0,T];\mathfrak{g}\right)

\displaystyle(\Xi,u)

\displaystyle\mapsto\operatorname{Ad}_{\Xi^{-1}}u=\Xi^{-1}u\Xi=:\bar{u}\,.

Since
\mathbf{Z}\mapsto(\Xi,u)
is continuous, we obtain
\bar{u}_{t}=V_{t}(\cdot,\mathbf{Z}_{t})
for
V\in C(\mathcal{C}_{g}^{\alpha}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{K});C([0,T];\mathfrak{g}))
with
V=\Xi^{-1}U\Xi
.
In the case
G=\operatorname{Diff}_{C^{n}}(\mathcal{D})
, the map
\operatorname{Ad}_{\Xi^{-1}}=\Xi^{*}
is a bounded linear map (and thus smooth). We conjecture the continuity of these maps for the
\alpha
-Hölder continuous in time analogues,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ad}_{(\cdot)^{-1}}:C^{\alpha}([0,T];\operatorname{Diff}_{C^{n}}(\mathcal{D}))\times C^{\alpha}\left([0,T];\mathfrak{X}_{C^{n}}(\mathcal{D})\right)

\displaystyle\rightarrow C^{\alpha}\left([0,T];\mathfrak{X}_{C^{n-1}}(\mathcal{D})\right)

\displaystyle(\Xi,u)

\displaystyle\mapsto\Xi^{*}u\,,

For Euler’s equations, we verify this conjecture by constructing an explicit SPDE for
\bar{u}
, allowing us to invoke rough stability (see (
4.14
) in Section
4
). It follows that in the case of Euler’s equation we have continuous path dependence in both
\bar{u},u
.

Remark 3.3
.

We remark that through the definitions
\Xi^{*}_{t}a_{t}=\overline{a}_{t}
and
\Xi^{*}_{t}u_{t}=\overline{u}_{t}
, it is not typically the case that
\Xi^{*}_{t}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}=\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta\overline{u}_{t}}
or
\Xi^{*}_{t}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta a_{t}}=\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta\overline{a}_{t}}
. Nevertheless, it is often that more direct expressions in terms of the mean quantities
\overline{u}_{t},\overline{a}_{t}
can be derived for specific choices of Lagrangian. For example, consider the Euler-Boussinesq Lagrangian

\ell(\Xi_{t*}\bar{u}_{t},\Xi_{t*}\bar{D}_{t},\Xi_{t*}\bar{b}_{t})=\int_{\mathcal{D}}\frac{1}{2}|\Xi_{t*}\bar{u}_{t}|^{2}\Xi_{t*}\bar{D}+\mathrm{g}\Xi_{t*}\bar{D}\Xi_{t*}\bar{b}_{t}\hat{z}+\mathrm{d}p(\bar{D}-1)d\mu_{\mathbf{g}}

where
b_{t}=\Xi_{t*}\bar{b}_{t}
is the advected buoyancy scalar,
\mathrm{g}
the gravity constant and
\hat{z}
the constant
z
axis unit vector. One has
\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta b_{t}}=\mathrm{g}\Xi_{t*}\bar{D}_{t}\hat{z}
and thus
\Xi_{t}^{*}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta b_{t}}=\mathrm{g}\bar{D}_{t}\hat{z}
, this calculation is in agreement with
[
55
, Sec. 3.3]
.

3.2.1
Kelvin-Noether circulation theorem

Associated with the random-coefficient Euler–Poincaré equation (
3.14
) and the stochastic Euler–Poincaré equation (
3.21
) are the Kelvin-Noether circulation theorems, which we will state next.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(\bar{u}_{t},\bar{a}_{t})
be the solution to the random-coefficient Euler–Poincaré equation (
3.14
) and let
(u_{t},a_{t})
be the solution to the stochastic Euler–Poincaré equation (
3.21
) for
t\in[0,T]
. Let
g_{t},\bar{g}_{t}\in\operatorname{Diff}(\mathcal{D})
satisfy the flow equations defined in (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), respectively. Assume that there exists
\bar{D},D:\Omega\times[0,T]\rightarrow\Lambda^{d}(\mathcal{D})
that is defined by
\bar{D}_{t}:=D_{0}\bar{g}_{t}^{-1}=\bar{g}_{t*}D_{0}
and
D_{t}:=D_{0}g_{t}^{-1}=g_{t*}D_{0}
for an initial non zero density
D_{0}\in\Lambda^{d}(\mathcal{D})
. Then, we have the following equivalent Kelvin-Noether circulation theorems,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\oint_{\bar{c}_{t}}\frac{1}{\bar{D}_{t}}\left(\Xi^{*}_{t}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\right)=\oint_{\bar{c}_{t}}\frac{1}{\bar{D}_{t}}\left(\Xi^{*}_{t}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta a_{t}}\right)\diamond\bar{a}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\,,

(3.26)

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\oint_{c_{t}}\frac{1}{D_{t}}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}=\oint_{c_{t}}\frac{1}{D_{t}}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta a_{t}}\diamond a_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\,,

(3.27)

where
\bar{c}_{t}:=\bar{g}_{t}(c_{0})
and
c_{t}:=g_{t}(c_{0})
denote the left action of
\bar{g}_{t}
and
g_{t}
on the embedded one-dimensional submanifold
c_{0}
, the initial embedding of a fluid material loop.

Remark 3.4
.

For
m=\alpha\otimes\rho\in\mathfrak{X}^{*}(\mathcal{D})
where
\alpha\in\Lambda^{1}(\mathcal{D})
and
\rho\in\Lambda^{d}(\mathcal{D})
, the “division by density” operation appearing in equations (
3.26
) and (
3.27
) is interpreted as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{D}m=\frac{1}{D}\left(\alpha\otimes\rho\right):=\frac{\star\rho}{\star D}\alpha\in\Lambda^{1}(\mathcal{D})\,,

where
\star
denote the Hodge dual,
\star:\Lambda^{d-k}(\mathcal{D})\rightarrow\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{D})
for
k=0,\ldots,d
.

Proof.

From the definition of
\bar{D}_{t}
and
D_{t}
, they are the solution to the advection equations

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\bar{D}_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{\bar{u}_{t}}\bar{D}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t=0\,,\qquad\mathrm{d}D_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{u_{t}}D_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{k}}D_{t}\circ\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{k,l=1}^{K}\mathcal{L}_{\Gamma^{kl}\left[\xi_{k}\,,\,\xi_{l}\right]}D_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t=0\,.

Let
\alpha:\Omega\times[0,T]\rightarrow\Lambda^{1}(\mathcal{D})
be such that
\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}=\alpha_{t}\otimes D_{t}
. This implies the similar decomposition of
\Xi^{*}_{t}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}=\Xi^{*}_{t}\alpha_{t}\otimes\Xi^{*}_{t}D_{t}=:\bar{\alpha}_{t}\otimes\bar{D}_{t}
such that
\alpha=\frac{1}{D_{t}}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}
and
\bar{\alpha}_{t}=\frac{1}{\bar{D}_{t}}\Xi^{*}_{t}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}
, where the operation of division by density is understood in the sense of Remark
3.4
.
We first prove equation (
3.26
) holds. Equation (
3.14
) implies

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\mathrm{d}+\operatorname{ad}^{*}_{\bar{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t}\right)\left(\Xi^{*}_{t}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta u_{t}}\right)=\left(\mathrm{d}+\mathcal{L}_{\bar{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t}\right)\bar{\alpha}_{t}\otimes\bar{D}_{t}+\bar{\alpha}_{t}\otimes\left(\mathrm{d}+\mathcal{L}_{\bar{u}_{t}}\right)\bar{D}_{t}=\left(\mathrm{d}+\mathcal{L}_{\bar{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t}\right)\bar{\alpha}_{t}\otimes\bar{D}_{t}=\Xi^{*}_{t}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta a_{t}}\diamond\bar{a}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\,,\\
&\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\left(\mathrm{d}+\mathcal{L}_{\bar{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t}\right)\bar{\alpha}_{t}=\frac{1}{\bar{D}}\left(\Xi^{*}_{t}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta a_{t}}\diamond\bar{a}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\,.\end{split}

In the first line, the first equality uses the Lie-chain rule (
3.22
) and the second equality uses the advection equation of
\bar{D}
by
\bar{u}
. Then, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\oint_{\bar{g}_{t}c_{0}}\bar{\alpha}_{t}=\mathrm{d}\oint_{c_{0}}\bar{g}_{t}^{*}\bar{\alpha}_{t}=\oint_{c_{0}}\bar{g}_{t}^{*}\left(\mathrm{d}+\mathcal{L}_{\bar{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t}\right)\bar{\alpha}_{t}=\oint_{\bar{g}_{t}c_{0}}\left(\mathrm{d}+\mathcal{L}_{\bar{u}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t}\right)\bar{\alpha}_{t}=\oint_{\bar{c}_{t}}\frac{1}{\bar{D}_{t}}\left(\Xi^{*}_{t}\frac{\delta\ell}{\delta a_{t}}\diamond\bar{a}_{t}\right)\,\mathrm{d}t\,.

Repeating the same arguments for equation (
3.21
), we obtain

Here, we consider the vorticity dynamics associated with the random-coefficient and stochastic homogeneous incompressible Euler equations (

This result may be explained through the results of this paper. The vector fields

if initially configured in an equilateral triangle around

when restricted to

The deterministic

under expectation. In this section, we make the simplifying assumption that the Lagrange multiplier, represented by the semi-martingale pressure

Variational derivatives of

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\bar{\ell}_{E}}{\delta\bar{u}_{t}}=\mathbb{E}[(\Xi^{*}_{t}\mathbf{g})(\bar{u}_{t},\cdot)\otimes\Xi_{t*}\bar{D}]\,,\quad\frac{\delta\bar{\ell}_{E}}{\delta\bar{D}_{t}}=\mathbb{E}[\Xi^{*}_{t}(\Xi^{*}_{t}\mathbf{g})(\bar{u}_{t},\bar{u}_{t})]\,,

Applying Hamilton’s principle

and
\bar{D}

from the action (

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{\bar{u}_{t}}\right)\mathbb{E}[(\Xi^{*}_{t}\mathbf{g})(\bar{u}_{t},\cdot)\otimes\Xi_{t*}\bar{D}_{t}]=\bar{D}_{t}\mathbf{d}\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\Xi^{*}_{t}(\Xi^{*}_{t}\mathbf{g})(\bar{u}_{t},\bar{u}_{t})]-\bar{\pi}_{t}\right)\,,

(4.22)

together with the deterministic advection of
\bar{D}

(4.23)

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{\bar{u}_{t}}\right)\bar{u}_{t}^{\widetilde{\flat}}=\mathbf{d}\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\Xi^{*}_{t}(\Xi^{*}_{t}\mathbf{g})(\bar{u}_{t},\bar{u}_{t})]-\bar{\pi}_{t}\right)\,.

Let

5
Alternative modelling perspectives

The goal of this section is to compare the homogenisation derivation of the stochastic Lagrangian particle trajectory ansatz (
2.1
) presented in this work with the homogenisation derivation proposed in
[
21
]
, hereafter referred to as CGH. The authors of CGH derived the ansatz (
2.1
) by applying a deterministic homogenisation procedure to a flow of diffeomorphisms conforming to a different fast-slow decomposition from that assumed in Section
2
.

In what follows, we highlight the differences in assumptions and results between CGH and the current work. We then adapt the homogenisation arguments in Section
2
to reinterpret the results of CGH and use the variational closure techniques in Section
3
to obtain the corresponding stochastic Euler–Poincaré equations. Finally, we provide a physical interpretation of the modelling choices made in this work and in CGH.

5.1
Homogenisation of mean flows

In CGH, the full flow of diffeomorphisms is assumed to have the decomposition
g=\Xi^{\varepsilon}\circ\bar{g}^{\varepsilon}
with
\Xi^{\varepsilon}_{t}=g^{\prime}_{t/\varepsilon}
for some chaotic flow
g^{\prime}:[0,T]\rightarrow\operatorname{Diff}(\mathcal{D})
whose time dependence is on the quotient
t/\varepsilon
. The stochastic Lagrangian trajectory ansatz is then shown to emerge from the homogenisation limit of the map
\bar{g}^{\varepsilon}
. Compared with the present work (see Section
2
), there are two key differences.

First, in CGH, the stochastic flow ansatz (
2.1
) arises from the limit of the map
\bar{g}^{\varepsilon}
, whereas in our approach, the same ansatz arises as the limit of the total map
g^{\varepsilon}
.
Second, in our approach, we assume a more general fast-slow flow decomposition of the form
g^{\varepsilon}=\Xi^{\varepsilon}\circ\bar{g}^{\varepsilon}
, cf. (
2.2
), whereas in CGH, the composite map
g
is taken to be
\varepsilon
-independent.
Moreover, in this work, we have shown that both

and

must be

-dependent for consistency in the case of stochastic closures presented in Section

When all flow maps are

-dependent, one can switch between the two perspectives where either

or

gives rise to the stochastic Lagrangian trajectory ansatz in the homogenisation limit via a relabelling of the flow of diffeomorphisms. The modelling interpretations of the two perspectives are discussed in Section

To pass to the homogenisation limit, the authors of CGH impose additional structural assumptions on the fluctuation map

In particular, the authors restrict to

[

; thus, the observed advected quantities are modelled by a random partial differential equation given in (

).

In Section
2
, we provide sufficient assumptions to prove that
g^{\varepsilon}
converges to a stochastic flow map

. This implies that the observables

satisfy an SPDE, and that

satisfies a random PDE, both of which are given in equation (

).

The decision on how to interpret observed advected quantities is ultimately up to the preferences and viewpoint of those utilising the SALT model. Specifically, if the model dictates that the observed advected quantities should satisfy a random PDE, the model defined by equations (

) and (

) should be used. Alternatively, if the model dictates that the observed advected quantities should satisfy a stochastic PDE, the model defined by equations (

) and (

) should be used.

Our contribution in the present work mathematically validates
both
of these viewpoints through rigorous homogenisation arguments. Starting from the decomposition
g^{\varepsilon}=\Xi^{\varepsilon}\circ\bar{g}^{\varepsilon}
, either model can be justified from the homogenisation procedure described in Section
2
. Furthermore, our approach is capable of deriving closures from a constrained Euler–Poincaré variational principle consistent with how one chooses to interpret the SALT modelling approach, by fixing an initial choice of “rough” limit map
g
or
\bar{g}
.

6
Concluding Remarks and Future Work

In this work, we (i) obtain a stochastic flow of diffeomorphisms as the homogenisation limit of a composition of fast-slow flows of diffeomorphisms, and (ii) use variational principles to derive stochastic closures for the slow dynamics of the homogenised flow while respecting its composition structure. Through this approach, we provide new perspectives on the derivation of the stochastic Euler–Poincaré equations for ideal fluid flows, as first proposed in
[
57
]
.

In what follows, we summarise the main contributions of this work by section and discuss potential future research directions arising from our results.

In Section
2
, we constructed a stochastic flow of diffeomorphisms as the flow map of a rough differential equation possessing a map composition structure using an iterated weak invariance principle (WIP)
[
65
,
17
,
16
]
. Our starting assumption was that
g^{\varepsilon}
decomposes into slow and fast timescales:
g^{\varepsilon}=\Xi^{\varepsilon}\circ\bar{g}^{\varepsilon}
. We proved that the fast flow
\Xi^{\varepsilon}
converges in law to a solution of an SDE via identification of a rough flow map and application of the WIP. Under the assumption that the slow flow is continuously related to a driving path of the fast map, we proved the same continuous dependence for the full map
g^{\varepsilon}
. This allowed us to use both the continuous mapping theorem and WIP to show that a limit
g
exists. The Kunita-Itô-Wentzel formula
[
27
,
28
]
then yields an SDE representation for
g
, which we identify as satisfying the stochastic Lagrangian flow ansatz (
2.1
).

In Section
3
, we considered variational closures for the dynamics of the velocity fields
\bar{u}_{t}
and
u_{t}:=\operatorname{Ad}_{\Xi_{t}}\bar{u}_{t}
, where
u_{t}
is associated with the drift coefficient of the stochastic flow
g=\Xi\circ\bar{g}
resulting from the analysis of Section
2
. We constructed a particular class of
\epsilon
-parameterised perturbations of
u_{t}
in (
3.8
) (distinct from the
\varepsilon
-dependence of the flow maps before homogenisation) by deforming only the
\bar{g}
component of
g
to
\bar{g}_{\epsilon}
, such that the deformed flow
g_{\epsilon}
retains the form
g_{\epsilon}=\Xi\circ\bar{g}_{\epsilon}
. From these constructed perturbations, we derived the random Euler–Poincaré equation with advected quantities (
3.18
) using a random, time-dependent Lagrangian. We show that this equation is equivalent to the stochastic Euler–Poincaré equation with advected quantities (
3.21
) that appeared in prior work, e.g.,
[
57
]
. Additionally, we considered the special case where the stochastic flow
\Xi_{t}
is an isometry of the metric on the Riemannian manifold for
t\in[0,T]
. In this case, we showed that the Hamiltonian is preserved along the flow of the stochastic Euler–Poincaré equation (
3.21
).

Motivated by averaging theory
[
75
]
and generalised Lagrangian mean theory
[
2
,
45
,
46
]
, we considered deterministic variational closure dynamics for
\bar{u}_{t}
in Section
3.3
. In this setting, we replaced Assumption
2.3
with Assumption
3.2
on
\bar{g}
and established the convergence of the map
g^{\varepsilon}
. Then, through a Lagrangian functional that takes the expectation of semimartingale arguments, application of Hamilton’s principle resulted in the deterministic equation (
3.37
) for the mean momentum.

To illustrate our modelling approaches, we provided applications to the incompressible Euler equations in Section
4
. In Section
4.1
, we explicitly derived the equivalent random-coefficient and stochastic forms of the incompressible Euler equations through an Euler–Poincaré variational principle and examined the associated Kelvin circulation and vorticity dynamics. We showed that when
\Xi_{t}
is an isometry for
t\in[0,T]
, the random-coefficient form of the two-dimensional Euler equation is greatly simplified. In Section
4.2
, we obtained a variant of the incompressible Euler equation as an averaged Euler–Poincaré equation, where the fluid velocity
\bar{u}_{t}
satisfies a deterministic evolution following the averaging closure discussed in Section
3.3
.

In Section
5
, we compared our homogenisation analysis and variational closures governing the dynamics of
\bar{u}
and
u
with previous works, namely
[
21
]
. We highlighted the differences in modelling assumptions between the present work and
[
21
]
, expressed their homogenisation analysis in the framework of Section
2
, and formulated stochastic variational principles to derive stochastic equations of motion based on their modelling assumptions, using constructions similar to those in Section
3
. The impacts and interpretations of the modelling assumptions were discussed in Section
5.2
.

Open problems and future work.

Following this work, several open problems should be addressed in future research. The analysis using rough path and Lie-theoretic modelling approaches taken in this paper allows us to investigate a more general class of equations than those presented. We outline such a programme below.

•

Construct
\Xi
as a rough flow of group elements solving a rough differential equation (RDE) of the type
\mathrm{d}\Xi_{t}=\xi(\Xi_{t})\mathrm{d}\mathbf{Z}_{t}
for a rough path
\mathbf{Z}_{t}=(Z_{t},\mathbb{Z}_{t})
. This can be done with simple modifications of Assumptions
2.1
and
2.3
to use a generic path
\mathbf{Z}
. This way, we are not restricted to the Brownian motion case, which was originally constructed as the limit of multi-time dynamics for fast eigenvalues
\lambda^{\varepsilon}
by homogenisation theory. It suffices to ask whether the path
\mathbf{Z}_{t}=(Z_{t},\mathbb{Z}_{t})
satisfies such a WIP. Some iterated weak invariance principles have been proven by Gehringer and Li for fractional Brownian motion (see
[
44
]
).

•

Generalise the map composition structure to an arbitrary Lie group
G
and study the convergence of
\Xi
and its composition to a rough flow in
G
in the Lie group topology. Formulate the associated variational principles on an arbitrary Lie group
G
in combination with
\Xi
being the flow of an RDE defined on the Lie algebra of
G
. Following the same variational approach as in Section
3
, we believe the rough differential equations derived in
[
24
]
will naturally follow from this approach.

Studies of the analytical properties of stochastic Euler–Poincaré equations under transport noise are a promising direction due to their connection to random-coefficient Euler–Poincaré equations via pullback. By adopting stochastic flow arguments similar to the Yudovich theorems
[
81
,
82
,
41
]
for two-dimensional Euler equations to a variety of stochastic Euler–Poincaré equations, novel well-posedness results can be obtained.

\mathbb{I}=\operatorname{diag}(I_{1},I_{1},I_{3})
, the resulting equations reduce to the energy conserving Kubo oscillator. In the composition of maps language, this is due to the fact that
\widehat{\xi}\circ\mathrm{d}W_{t}
, with this choice of
\xi
, defines a stochastic curve
\Xi_{t}\in SO(3)
that commutes with
\mathbb{I}
,

\displaystyle\Xi_{t}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\sqrt{\sigma}\cos(W_{t})&-\sqrt{\sigma}\sin(W_{t})&0\\
\sqrt{\sigma}\sin(W_{t})&\sqrt{\sigma}\cos(W_{t})&0\\
0&0&{\sigma^{-1}}\end{array}\right)\in SO(3),\quad\Xi_{t}^{-1}\mathrm{d}\Xi_{t}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&-\sigma&0\\
\sigma&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{array}\right)\circ\mathrm{d}W_{t}\in\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}(3)\,.

The commutation of these specific choices of
\Xi_{t}
and
\mathbb{I}
imply the following
\Xi_{t}
invariance of
\ell_{\operatorname{RB}}
and so energy preservation from Proposition
3.3
applies,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\ell_{\operatorname{RB}}(\operatorname{Ad}_{\Xi_{t}}\overline{\Omega}_{t})&=\frac{1}{2}(\Xi_{t}{\overline{\Omega}}_{t})\cdot\mathbb{I}(\Xi_{t}{\overline{\Omega}}_{t}):=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\Xi_{t}\bar{\Omega}_{t},\mathbb{I}\Xi_{t}\bar{\Omega}_{t}\right\rangle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{so3isom}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\Xi_{t}\bar{\Omega}_{t},\Xi_{t}\mathbb{I}\bar{\Omega}_{t}\right\rangle\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\Xi^{T}_{t}\Xi_{t}\bar{\Omega}_{t},\mathbb{I}\bar{\Omega}_{t}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\bar{\Omega}_{t},\mathbb{I}\bar{\Omega}_{t}\right\rangle=\ell_{\operatorname{RB}}(\bar{\Omega}_{t})\,.\end{split}

In fact one can identify this choice of
\xi
as a Killing field for a metric on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with coefficients
\mathbf{g}_{ij}=\mathbb{I}_{ij}
, generating an isometry
SO(\mathbb{I})
(orthogonal with respect to the inertia tensor,
\Xi_{t}\mathbb{I}\Xi_{t}^{T}=\mathbb{I}
).

The term
\Xi_{t}^{-1}\mathbb{I}\Xi_{t}
in equation (
A.5
) is analogous to
\Xi_{t}^{*}\mathbf{g}
seen in Section
4
and may be averaged in the same manner as Section
4.2
. This leads to the deterministic closure model for
\bar{\Omega}
given by

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\Xi_{t}^{-1}\mathbb{I}\Xi_{t}\right]\bar{\Omega}_{t}\right)=\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\Xi_{t}^{-1}\mathbb{I}\Xi_{t}\right]\bar{\Omega}_{t}\right)\times\bar{\Omega}_{t}\,.

(A.12)

This equation conserves the Casimir
\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbb{E}\left[\Xi_{t}^{-1}\mathbb{I}\Xi_{t}\right]\bar{\Omega}_{t}\|^{2}
. Using particular choices of isometry and inertia matrix
\mathbb{I}
constructed in (
A.2
) we observe that
\Xi_{t}^{-1}\mathbb{I}\Xi_{t}=\mathbb{I}
and it follows that both (
A.5
), (
A.12
) reduce to the rigid body ODE in
\bar{\Omega}_{t}
variables,

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}\dot{\bar{\Omega}}_{t}+\mathbb{I}\bar{\Omega}_{t}\times\bar{\Omega}_{t}=0\,.

This is a classical equation (a variant of the Euler top) that conserves energy.

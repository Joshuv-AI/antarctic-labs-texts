Convergence of Differential Entropies—II
Mahesh Godavarti

Abstract
We show that under convergence in measure of probability density functions, differential entropy converges whenever the entropy integrands f n ​ | log ⁡ f n | f_{n}\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert are uniformly integrable and tight—a direct consequence of Vitali’s convergence theorem. We give an entropy-weighted Orlicz condition: sup n ∫ f n ​ Ψ ​ ( | log ⁡ f n | ) < ∞ \sup_{n}\int f_{n}\,\Psi(\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert)<\infty for a single superlinear Ψ \Psi , strictly weaker than the fixed- α \alpha condition of Godavarti and Hero (2004). We also disprove the Godavarti–Hero conjecture that α > 1 \alpha>1 could be replaced by α n ↓ 1 \alpha_{n}\downarrow 1 . We recover the sufficient conditions of Godavarti–Hero, Piera–Parada, and Ghourchian–Gohari–Amini as corollaries. On bounded domains, we prove that uniform integrability of the entropy integrands is both necessary and sufficient—a complete characterization of entropy convergence.



I
Introduction

If a sequence of probability density functions
\{f_{n}\}
converges to
a limiting density
f
, when does the differential entropy converge?
That is, under what additional conditions does

f_{n}\to f\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H(f_{n})\to H(f),

(1)

where
H(f)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\log f(x)\,dx
?

Godavarti and Hero
[
4
]

studied (
1
) under a.e. convergence and gave
two sufficient conditions:
(i)
f_{n}\leq C
and
\int\lvert x\rvert^{\beta}f_{n}\leq C
;
(ii) a fixed-
\alpha
condition

\sup_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{n}(x)\,\bigl(\lvert\log f_{n}(x)\rvert\bigr)^{\alpha}\,dx<\infty,\quad\alpha>1.

(2)

They asked whether the fixed
\alpha>1

in (
2
) can be replaced by
\alpha_{n}\downarrow 1
.
To our knowledge, this question has not been resolved in the
subsequent literature.

Piera and Parada
[
5
]
proved entropy convergence
under a.e. convergence with bounded density
ratio
\esssup\,f_{n}/f\leq C
.
Ghourchian, Gohari, and Amini
[
3
]

proved entropy continuity under total variation convergence
(
\lVert f_{n}-f\rVert_{1}\to 0
) on classes with bounded density and
moments, and gave an explicit modulus of continuity;
we recover their qualitative convergence but not their rates.
For a broader survey, see
[
6
]
.

The analytical tools we use—Vitali’s theorem, uniform
integrability, de la Vallée–Poussin—are classical, and
Orlicz conditions for integrability are well studied in
real analysis.
Our contribution is an information-theory facing synthesis:
to our knowledge, no prior work—in the information theory
or probability literature—has formulated uniform integrability
and tightness of the entropy integrands

\{f_{n}\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert\}
as the single mechanism underlying the
sufficient conditions
of
[
4
,
5
,
3
]
, nor
stated a probability-weighted Orlicz condition that targets the
information random variable
\lvert\log f_{n}(X)\rvert
directly.

Contributions

II
Preliminaries

II-A

Notation

We work on
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\lambda)
with Lebesgue
measure
\lambda
. A
pdf
is a measurable

f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty)
with
\int f\,dx=1
. The differential
entropy is
H(f)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\log f\,dx
(
0\log 0=0
,
since
t\log t\to 0
as
t\downarrow 0
;
\log=\ln
).
All results assume
H(f)
is finite: the Vitali
framework requires
f\log f\in L^{1}
, so

\lvert H(f)\rvert=\infty
would need a different approach.
Throughout,
f_{n}\to f
denotes convergence in
Lebesgue measure. Both almost everywhere (a.e.) convergence and total
variation (TV) convergence (
\lVert f_{n}-f\rVert_{1}\to 0
) imply
convergence in measure. Conversely, for nonnegative
integrable functions, convergence in measure plus convergence
of integrals (
\int g_{n}\to\int g
) implies

\lVert g_{n}-g\rVert_{1}\to 0

(see, e.g.,
[
2
, p. 97]
). For pdfs the
second hypothesis holds automatically (
\int f_{n}=\int f=1
),
so convergence in measure implies
\lVert f_{n}-f\rVert_{1}\to 0

(Lemma
4
).
Thus our baseline assumption is
effectively equivalent to TV convergence; we state results
under convergence in measure because it is the natural
hypothesis for Vitali’s theorem (Theorem
3
).
We write UI & T for

uniformly integrable and tight

(Definition
1
); on finite measure spaces (C) is
automatic and UI & T reduces to classical UI.

II-B

Uniform integrability and tightness

Definition 1

(UI & T)
.

A family
\{g_{n}\}
of integrable functions on

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu)
is
uniformly integrable
and tight
(UI & T) if:

(A)

Uniform
L^{1}
bound:

\sup_{n}\int\lvert g_{n}\rvert\,d\mu<\infty
.

(B)

Equi-integrability:

For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
M>0
such that

\sup_{n}\int_{\{\lvert g_{n}\rvert>M\}}\lvert g_{n}\rvert\,d\mu<\varepsilon
.

(C)

Tail concentration:

For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
K\subset\Omega

with
\mu(K)<\infty
such that

\sup_{n}\int_{\Omega\setminus K}\lvert g_{n}\rvert\,d\mu<\varepsilon
.

On a finite measure space, (A)+(B) alone—classical uniform
integrability—suffice for Vitali’s theorem.
On
\sigma
-finite spaces such as
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\text{Lebesgue})
,
condition (C) is the additional ingredient that makes Vitali work
(Theorem
3
).
When
g_{n}=f_{n}
, condition (C) coincides with probability tightness
of
f_{n}\,dx
; but for
g_{n}=f_{n}\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert
, it is a stronger
requirement on the entropy integrands, not merely on the measures.
On a bounded domain (C) is automatic and
UI & T reduces to classical UI.
Example
6
shows that on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
condition (C)
can fail for
\{f_{n}\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert\}
even when

H(f_{n})\to H(f)
.

II-C

Classical tools

Theorem 2

(de la Vallée–Poussin
[
1
]
)
.

A family
\{g_{n}\}
on a finite measure space is uniformly integrable
if and only if there exists a convex
\Phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)

with
\Phi(0)=0
and
\Phi(t)/t\to\infty
such that

\sup_{n}\int\Phi(\lvert g_{n}\rvert)\,d\mu<\infty
.

Theorem 3

(Vitali; see
[
2
, Thm. 4.5.4]
)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu)
be
\sigma
-finite,

g_{n},g\in L^{1}(\mu)
,
g_{n}\to g
in measure. Then

\int\lvert g_{n}-g\rvert\,d\mu\to 0
if and only if
\{g_{n}\}
is
UI & T.

II-D

Densities under convergence in measure

Lemma 4
.

Let
\{f_{n}\}
and
f
be pdfs on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
f_{n}\to f
in measure.
Then
\lVert f_{n}-f\rVert_{1}\to 0
and
\{f_{n}\}
is UI & T.

Proof.

Since
\min
is continuous,
\min(f_{n},f)\to f
in measure,
and
0\leq\min(f_{n},f)\leq f\in L^{1}
. We claim

\int\min(f_{n},f)\to\int f
. If not, some subsequence

\{n_{k}\}
has
\lvert\int\min(f_{n_{k}},f)-\int f\rvert\geq\delta>0
. Since
f_{n_{k}}\to f
in measure, a further subsequence
converges a.e., and ordinary dominated convergence gives

\int\min(f_{n_{k_{j}}},f)\to\int f
, a contradiction.
Therefore

\displaystyle\int\lvert f_{n}-f\rvert

\displaystyle=\int f_{n}+\int f-2\int\min(f_{n},f)

\displaystyle=2-2\int\min(f_{n},f)\to 0.

(A):

\sup_{n}\int f_{n}=1
.

(B):
Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Choose
n_{0}
with

\int\lvert f_{n}-f\rvert<\varepsilon/3
for
n\geq n_{0}
, and

\delta>0
with
\lambda(A)<\delta\Rightarrow\int_{A}f<\varepsilon/3
. For
n\geq n_{0}
and
M\geq 1/\delta
:

\lambda(\{f_{n}>M\})\leq 1/M\leq\delta
by Markov, so

\int_{\{f_{n}>M\}}f_{n}\leq\int\lvert f_{n}-f\rvert+\int_{\{f_{n}>M\}}f<2\varepsilon/3
.
For
n<n_{0}
: each
f_{n}\in L^{1}
, so choose
M_{n}
with

\int_{\{f_{n}>M_{n}\}}f_{n}<\varepsilon
. Set

M=\max(1/\delta,M_{1},\ldots,M_{n_{0}-1})
.

(C):
Analogous: for
n\geq n_{0}
,

\int_{\lvert x\rvert>R}f_{n}\leq\int\lvert f_{n}-f\rvert+\int_{\lvert x\rvert>R}f
.
Choose
R
for the tail of
f
, then handle
n<n_{0}
individually.
∎

This extends Scheffé’s lemma (which assumes a.e. convergence)
to convergence in measure via a subsequence argument.

II-E

Entropy convergence via Vitali’s theorem

Lemma 5

(Entropy convergence via UI & T)
.

Let
\{f_{n}\}
and
f
be pdfs on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
f_{n}\to f
in measure
and
H(f)
finite. If the entropy integrands

g_{n}=f_{n}\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert
are UI & T, then

H(f_{n})\to H(f)
.

Proof.

Set
h_{n}=f_{n}\log f_{n}
,
h=f\log f
. Since
H(f)
is
finite,
h\in L^{1}
. Since
t\log t
is continuous on

[0,\infty)
(with the convention
0\log 0=0
),

h_{n}\to h
in measure. Since
\{\lvert h_{n}\rvert\}=\{g_{n}\}

is UI & T, Theorem
3
gives

\int\lvert h_{n}-h\rvert\to 0
, hence
H(f_{n})\to H(f)
.
∎

Example 6

(Converse fails on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
)
.

Let
f=\mathbf{1}_{[0,1]}
on
\mathbb{R}
, so
H(f)=0
. For
n\geq 3
,

f_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}1-2/n,&x\in[0,1],\\
e^{n},&x\in[n,\,n+(e^{-n})/n],\\
e^{-n},&x\in[2n,\,2n+(e^{n})/n],\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then
\int f_{n}=1
and
f_{n}\to f
a.e. The tall spike’s entropy
contribution is
-e^{n}\cdot n\cdot(e^{-n})/n=-1
, the short
spike’s is
-e^{-n}\cdot(-n)\cdot(e^{n})/n=+1
; these cancel,
so
H(f_{n})\to 0=H(f)
.
However, each spike contributes exactly
1

to
\int f_{n}\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert
, and the short spike’s support recedes
to infinity, so condition (C) fails for
\{f_{n}\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert\}
.

Theorem 7

(Bounded domains)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be bounded,
\{f_{n}\}
and
f
pdfs
on
\Omega
with
f_{n}\to f
in measure and
H(f)
finite. Then

H(f_{n})\to H(f)
if and only if

\{f_{n}\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert\}
is uniformly integrable.

Proof.

Tightness is automatic on bounded domains.

(\Longleftarrow)

Lemma
5
.

(\Longrightarrow)

Let
h_{n}=f_{n}\log f_{n}=h_{n}^{+}-h_{n}^{-}
and
h=f\log f=h^{+}-h^{-}
. Since
0\leq h_{n}^{-}\leq 1/e
and
\Omega
is
bounded, dominated convergence (via the subsequence argument of
Lemma
4
) gives
\int h_{n}^{-}\to\int h^{-}
.
Combined with
H(f_{n})\to H(f)
, i.e.,

\int h_{n}\to\int h
, we get
\int h_{n}^{+}\to\int h^{+}
.
Now
h_{n}^{+}\to h^{+}
in measure (by continuity of

(\cdot)^{+}
and
t\log t
), and

\min(h_{n}^{+},h^{+})\to h^{+}
in measure with

\min(h_{n}^{+},h^{+})\leq h^{+}\in L^{1}
; the same subsequence
argument gives
\int\min(h_{n}^{+},h^{+})\to\int h^{+}
. Hence

\int|h_{n}^{+}-h^{+}|=\int h_{n}^{+}+\int h^{+}-2\int\min(h_{n}^{+},h^{+})\to 0
.
Similarly
\int|h_{n}^{-}-h^{-}|\to 0
, so

\int|h_{n}-h|\to 0
and the converse of
Theorem
3
gives UI.
∎

III
The Orlicz Condition

Theorem 8

(Orlicz-type sufficient condition)
.

Let
\{f_{n}\}
and
f
be pdfs on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
f_{n}\to f
in measure
and
H(f)
finite. Suppose there exists a convex, nondecreasing

\Psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with
\Psi(0)=0
and

\Psi(t)/t\to\infty

such that

\sup_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{n}(x)\,\Psi\!\bigl(\lvert\log f_{n}(x)\rvert\bigr)\,dx<\infty.

(3)

Then each
H(f_{n})
is finite and
H(f_{n})\to H(f)
.

Proof.

The idea is: the Orlicz bound (
3
) controls large
values of
\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert
(de la Vallée–Poussin style),
giving equi-integrability (B) for
g_{n}=f_{n}\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert
.
Tightness (C) of
\{g_{n}\}
follows from tightness of
\{f_{n}\}

(Lemma
4
), using the split

\{\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert>T\}
versus

\{\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert\leq T\}
where
g_{n}\leq Tf_{n}
.

Since
\Psi(t)/t\to\infty
, there exists
T_{0}
with

\Psi(t)\geq t
for
t\geq T_{0}
, so

\int f_{n}\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert\leq T_{0}+C<\infty
where

C=\sup_{n}\int f_{n}\Psi(\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert)
; in particular each

H(f_{n})
is finite.
We verify (A)–(C) of Lemma
5
for

g_{n}=f_{n}\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert
.
Define
\varphi(T)=\sup_{t\geq T}t/\Psi(t)\to 0
. On

\{\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert>T\}
,

\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert\leq\varphi(T)\Psi(\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert)
, giving

\int_{\{\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert>T\}}g_{n}\leq\varphi(T)\,C.

(4)

By Lemma
4
,
\{f_{n}\}
is UI & T.

(A):

\int g_{n}\leq T_{0}+C
(established above).

(B):

\{g_{n}>M\}\subset\{\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert>T\}\cup\{\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert\leq T,\;g_{n}>M\}
.
The first part contributes
\leq\varphi(T)C
. On the second,

g_{n}\leq Tf_{n}
and
g_{n}>M
forces
f_{n}>M/T
, so

\int_{\{\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert\leq T,\,g_{n}>M\}}g_{n}\leq T\int_{\{f_{n}>M/T\}}f_{n}
.
Since
\{f_{n}\}
is UI: first choose
T
so that

\varphi(T)C<\varepsilon/2
; then, for this fixed
T
,
choose
M
so that

\sup_{n}\int_{\{f_{n}>M/T\}}f_{n}<\varepsilon/(2T)
.

(C):

\int_{\lvert x\rvert>R}g_{n}\leq\varphi(T)C+T\sup_{n}\int_{\lvert x\rvert>R}f_{n}
.
First choose
T
so that
\varphi(T)C<\varepsilon/2
;
then, for this fixed
T
, choose
R
so that

\sup_{n}\int_{\lvert x\rvert>R}f_{n}<\varepsilon/(2T)

(possible by tightness of
\{f_{n}\}
).

By Lemma
5
,
H(f_{n})\to H(f)
.
∎

If
X_{n}\sim f_{n}
, the information random variable is

\iota_{n}=-\log f_{n}(X_{n})
, and
H(f_{n})=\mathbb{E}[\iota_{n}]
.
Condition (
3
) says

\sup_{n}\mathbb{E}_{f_{n}}[\Psi(\lvert\iota_{n}\rvert)]<\infty
: the tails of the
information random variable are uniformly controlled by a single
superlinear moment. By de la Vallée–Poussin, this is
equivalent to uniform integrability of

\{\lvert\iota_{n}\rvert\}_{n\geq 1}
under the respective laws of

X_{n}\sim f_{n}
.

.

Proof.

Since
H(f)
is finite,
\lvert\log f\rvert\in L^{1}(f\,dx)
.
By Theorem
2
on the probability space
(\mathbb{R}^{d},f\,dx)
,
there exists a convex superlinear
\Phi
with

\int f\,\Phi(\lvert\log f\rvert)<\infty
;
by capping the derivative (
\Phi^{\prime}\leftarrow\min(\Phi^{\prime},e^{t/4})
)
we may assume
\Phi(t)\leq 4e^{t/4}
while preserving convexity
and superlinearity.
Set
\Psi(t)=\Phi(t/2)
.
With
r_{n}=f_{n}/f\in[0,C]
, convexity of
\Phi
gives

\displaystyle f_{n}\Psi(\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert)

\displaystyle=r_{n}f\,\Phi\!\bigl(\tfrac{\lvert\log r_{n}+\log f\rvert}{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{r_{n}f}{2}\bigl[\Phi(\lvert\log r_{n}\rvert)+\Phi(\lvert\log f\rvert)\bigr].

Since
\Phi(t)\leq 4e^{t/4}
,

r_{n}\Phi(\lvert\log r_{n}\rvert)\leq K
for a constant
K

(for
r_{n}<1
:
e^{-s}\cdot 4e^{s/4}=4e^{-3s/4}\leq 4
;
for
r_{n}\in[1,C]
:
\leq C\Phi(\log C)
).
Hence

\sup_{n}\int f_{n}\Psi(\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert)\leq\tfrac{K}{2}+\tfrac{C}{2}\int f\,\Phi(\lvert\log f\rvert)<\infty
,
and Theorem
8
applies.
∎

Remark 14

(Applications)
.

The framework developed here applies in any setting where
density convergence must be lifted to entropy convergence—including
plug-in entropy estimation, mutual information continuity along
sequences of channel inputs, and entropy convergence in CLT-type
approximations. On bounded domains (e.g., bounded-amplitude
signals), Theorem
7
gives a complete answer.
On
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, Table
II
lists practical sufficient
conditions that can be verified directly from model parameters.

V
The GH Conjecture is False

Godavarti and Hero
[
4
]
asked whether
the fixed
\alpha>1
in (
2
) can be replaced
by a sequence
\alpha_{n}\downarrow 1
:

GH Conjecture.

If
f_{n}\to f
a.e. and

\sup_{n}\int f_{n}\,(\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert)^{\alpha_{n}}<\infty

for some
\alpha_{n}\downarrow 1
, then

H(f_{n})\to H(f)
.

We disprove this with an explicit counterexample.

Theorem 15

(Counterexample)
.

There exist pdfs
\{f_{n}\}
and
f
on
[0,1]
such that:

(i)

f_{n}\to f
a.e. (hence in measure);

(ii)

\sup_{n}\int_{0}^{1}f_{n}(\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert)^{\alpha_{n}}\,dx<\infty

for
\alpha_{n}\downarrow 1
;

(iii)

H(f_{n})\not\to H(f)
.

Proof.

Let
f\equiv 1
on
[0,1]
(
H(f)=0
). Set

\delta_{n}=(e^{-n})/n
,

\alpha_{n}=1+1/\log n
, and

f_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}e^{n},&0\leq x\leq\delta_{n},\\
c_{n},&\delta_{n}<x\leq 1,\end{cases}

(5)

where
c_{n}=(1-1/n)/(1-(e^{-n})/n)
.
For
n\geq 2
,
(e^{-n})/n<1
so
c_{n}>0
, and
c_{n}\to 1
.

(i):
For fixed
x\in(0,1]
,
\delta_{n}\to 0
so

f_{n}(x)=c_{n}\to 1
eventually.
Moreover,
\lVert f_{n}-f\rVert_{1}\leq(e^{n}-1)\delta_{n}+\lvert c_{n}-1\rvert(1-\delta_{n})\to 0
.

(ii):
The spike’s
\alpha_{n}
-moment is

e^{n}\cdot n^{\alpha_{n}}\cdot(e^{-n})/n=n^{\alpha_{n}-1}=n^{1/\log n}=e
.
The background contribution
\to 0
. So

\sup_{n}\int f_{n}(\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert)^{\alpha_{n}}\leq e+1<\infty
.

(iii):
The spike’s entropy contribution is

-e^{n}\cdot n\cdot(e^{-n})/n=-1
.
The background
\to 0
. So
H(f_{n})\to-1\neq 0
.
∎

The failure mechanism is clear: the moving-exponent condition uses

\Psi_{n}(t)=t^{\alpha_{n}}
with
\alpha_{n}\downarrow 1
, but no
single
\Psi
with
\Psi(t)/t\to\infty
is dominated by
all
\Psi_{n}
, since
\Psi(t)/t\leq t^{\alpha_{n}-1}\to 1
for
any fixed
t
as
n\to\infty
.
Since the counterexample lives on
[0,1]
where tightness is
automatic, Theorem
7
further shows that the
moving-exponent condition does not even imply uniform integrability
of
\{f_{n}\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert\}
.
For any fixed
\alpha>1
, the spike’s
\alpha
-moment is

n^{\alpha-1}\to\infty
, so (
2
) fails
and Theorem
8
does not apply.

VI
Conclusion

We have shown that Vitali’s convergence theorem reduces entropy
convergence to UI & T of the entropy integrands

f_{n}\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert
(Lemma
5
). On bounded domains,
this condition is both necessary and sufficient
(Theorem
7
), giving a complete
characterization of entropy convergence. We gave an
Orlicz condition (Theorem
8
) that is strictly weaker
than the sufficient conditions
of
[
4
,
5
,
3
]
, and disproved the Godavarti–Hero
conjecture (Theorem
15
). All results assume
convergence in Lebesgue measure (equivalently, TV convergence for
pdfs); they do not address entropy continuity under weak
convergence of distributions.
On
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, UI & T of
\{f_{n}\lvert\log f_{n}\rvert\}
is sufficient but
not necessary (Example
6
); characterizing
necessity without tightness remains open.

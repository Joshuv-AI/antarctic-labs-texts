Error Estimates for discontinuous Galerkin approximations to the Vlasov-unsteady Stokes system
Harsha Hutridurga, Krishan Kumar, Amiya K. Pani

Abstract
. In the first part of this paper, uniqueness of strong solution is established for the Vlasov-unsteady Stokes problem in 3 3 D. The second part deals with a semi discrete scheme, which is based on the coupling of discontinuous Galerkin approximations for the Vlasov and the Stokes equations for the 2 2 D problem. The proposed method is both mass and momentum conservative. Based on a special projection and also the Stokes projection, optimal error estimates in the case of smooth compactly supported initial data are derived. Moreover, the generalization of error estimates to 3 3 D problem is also indicated. Finally, based on time splitting algorithm, some numerical experiments are conducted whose results confirm our theoretical findings.



Key words.
Vlasov-unsteady Stokes, uniqueness in 3D, discontinuous Galerkin method, conservation properties, optimal error estimates, nonlinear version of Grönwall’s inequality, numerical experiments.

35D35, 65N30, 65M15

1.
Introduction

This paper deals with error estimates for discontinuous Galerkin method for a coupled system of partial differential equations arising in the study of
thin sprays
. From a modeling perspective, it is assumed that the spray particles (droplets) are a dispersed phase in a gas medium. Studying two-phase models comprising of a kinetic equation for the dispersed phase and a fluid equation for the gas dates back to the works of O’Rourke
[
O’R81
]
and Williams
[
Wil85
]
(also, see
[
CP83
]
).

We deal with the unsteady Stokes equation for the fluid part and the Vlasov equation for the droplet distribution while the coupling is via a drag term:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f+\nabla_{v}\cdot\Big(\left(\bm{u}-v\right)f\Big)&=0\qquad\qquad\mbox{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\\
f(0,x,v)&=f_{0}(x,v)\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\bm{u}-\Delta_{x}\bm{u}+\nabla_{x}p&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left(v-\bm{u}\right)f\,{\rm d}v\quad\quad\mbox{ in }\,\,\,(0,T)\times\Omega_{x},\\
\nabla_{x}\cdot\bm{u}&=0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\,\,\mbox{ in }\,\,\,\Omega_{x},\\
\bm{u}(0,x)&=\bm{u_{0}}(x)\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\,\Omega_{x}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here,
\Omega_{x}
denotes the
d
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
for
d=2,3
,
\bm{u}(t,x)
is the fluid velocity, the fluid pressure
p(t,x)
and the droplet distribution function
f(t,x,v)
. We impose periodic boundary conditions in the
x
variable. The above model with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition for the fluid velocity and with specular reflection boundary condition for the droplet distribution was studied by Hamdache in
[
Ham98
]
, wherein he proved the existence of global-in-time weak solutions. Various kinetic-fluid equations have been studied in the literature: Vlasov-Burgers equations
[
DR99
,
Gou01
]
; Vlasov-Euler equations
[
BD06
]
; Vlasov-Navier-Stokes equations
[
BDGM09
,
CKL11
,
Yu13
,
HKMMM20
]
and references therein.

In this paper, we provide certain qualitative and quantitative properties of solutions to our system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
). While a proof of the global-in-time existence of strong solutions to the system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) in
3
D is discussed in
[
CKL11
]
, but uniqueness is missing therefore, our first attempt is to prove uniqueness in
3
D.

The emphasis of this paper is on a numerical method that we are proposing to approximate solutions to the system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
). Our current contribution is a first step towards developing a robust numerical scheme for approximating solutions to a more physically relevant model such as the Vlasov-Navier-Stokes equations. The proposed semi-discrete scheme uses discontinuous Galerkin method for both kinetic and unsteady Stokes equations. Our present work is inspired by
[
DDCS09
,
HGMM12
,
DDCS12
]
for the Vlasov-Poisson system,
[
HKP43
]
for the Vlasov-steady Stokes and
[
CGLM14
,
YL17
]
for the Vlasov-Maxwell system. kinetic equation is a transport problem which conserves total mass. As discontinuous Galerkin(dG) methods have the property to preserve the conservation property, dG turns out to be a method of choice for the kinetic equation. It should be noted that either the local discontinuous Galerkin or any conforming numerical method can be employed for the Stokes system. Our major contributions in this paper are as follows:

•

Uniqueness of the strong solutions in three dimensions is proved for the Vlasov-unsteady Stokes system.
Earlier in
[
HKP23b
]
, there is an error in the proof of uniqueness and a correct proof of uniqueness is given in Section
2
of this paper. This can be achieved after proving some higher moment estimates for the droplet distribution and regularity results for the fluid velocity vector.

•

DG methods are proposed for numerical approximations and are shown to conserve mass and the total momentum, which confirm similar conservation properties for the continuous system.

•

Optimal error estimates are derived for the fluid velocity and the fluid pressure approximations using Stokes projection in
2
D setup, while in
3
D case some remarks are given in the subsection
4.5
. Moreover, Optimal error estimate for the approximation of the droplet distribution is obtained using a special projections. The error analysis uses a variant of the nonlinear Gronwall’s inequality in a crucial way.

•

Since phase space is four or six dimensions depending on
d=2
or
d=3,
a Lie splitting in time marching combined with dimension splitting in the phase space is proposed for computational experiments, whose numerical results confirm our theoretically findings.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we deal with certain qualitative and quantitative aspects of the solutions to the continuum model. We further prove uniqueness result for strong solutions to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
). In Section
3
, we introduce a semi-discrete dG-dG numerical scheme and analyse some of its properties. The error analysis for the said semi-discrete method is detailed in Section
4
. We give some concluding remarks in Section
6
. Finally, some numerical results confirming our theoretical results are discussed.

2.
Certain aspects of the continuum model

In this paper, let
\rho
and
V
denote the local density and the local macroscopic velocity of the droplets, respectively, as

\rho(t,x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(t,x,v)\,{\rm d}v\quad\mbox{and}\quad V(t,x)=\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(t,x,v)v\,{\rm d}v.

Define the
l^{th}
order velocity moments of distribution function as

m_{l}f(t,x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|v|^{l}f(t,x,v)\,{\rm d}v,\quad\mbox{for}\quad l\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}.

Throughout this manuscript, standard notations for Sobolev spaces with their norms are used, say, the space
W^{k,p}
denotes the
k^{th}
order
L^{p}
-Sobolev spaces with norm
\bm{W}^{k,p}=\left(W^{k,p}(\Omega_{x})\right)^{2},
for all
k\geq 0,
and for
1\leq p\leq\infty.
When
p=\infty
, it is defined in the usual manner.

For our subsequent use, denote
\bm{J}_{0}
and
\bm{J}_{1}
as a special class of divergence-free (in the sense of distributions) vector fields defined by

\bm{J}_{0}=\left\{\bm{w}\in\bm{L}^{2}:\nabla_{x}\cdot\bm{w}=0,\bm{w}\,\,\mbox{is periodic}\right\},

\bm{J}_{1}=\left\{\bm{w}\in\bm{W}^{1,2}:\nabla_{x}\cdot\bm{w}=0,\bm{w}\,\,\mbox{is periodic}\right\}.

Throughout this paper, any function defined on
\Omega_{x}
is assumed to be periodic in the
x
-variable.

2.1.
Qualitative and quantitative aspects of the model problem

We begin this subsection by stating a result on
L^{\infty}
estimate for the local density.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
\bm{u}\in L^{1}(0,T;\bm{L}^{\infty}),
if
\sup_{C^{r}_{t,v}}f_{0}\in L^{\infty}_{loc}\left({\mathbb{R}}_{+};L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\right)
, where
C^{r}_{t,v}:=\Omega_{x}\times B(e^{t}v,r),\,\forall\,r>0
and
B(e^{t}v,r)
denotes the ball centered at
e^{t}v
having radius
r
, then, the following estimate holds:

(2.1)

\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T]\times\Omega_{x})}\leq e^{dT}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\sup_{C^{r}_{t,v}}f_{0}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.

Proof.

The characteristic curves
(X,Y)
for the equation (
1.1
) are given by

(2.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{X}(s;t,x,v)&=Y(s;t,x,v);\quad X(t;t,x,v)=x,\\
\dot{Y}(s;t,x,v)&=\bm{u}(s,X(s;t,x,v))-Y(s;t,x,v);\quad Y(t;t,x,v)=v,\\
\dot{f}(s;t,x,v)&=df(s;t,x,v);\quad f(t;t,x,v)=f_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

It is understood that the spatial characteristic curves are periodic. For
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
, note that

\displaystyle|e^{t}v-e^{s}Y(s;t,x,v)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{s}^{t}e^{\tau}|\bm{u}(\tau,X(\tau;t,x,v))|\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq e^{T}\|\bm{u}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;\bm{L}^{\infty})}.

Therefore,
\left(X(0;t,x,v),Y(0;t,x,v)\right)\in C^{r}_{t,v}
, with
r=e^{T}\|\bm{u}\|_{L^{1}(0,T;\bm{L}^{\infty})}
.

From (
2.2
), we obtain

f(t,x,v)=e^{dt}f_{0}(X(0;t,x,v),Y(0;t,x,v)).

Hence,

\displaystyle\rho(t,x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(t,x,v)\,{\rm d}v=e^{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f_{0}(X(0;t,x,v),Y(0;t,x,v))\,{\rm d}v

implying (
2.1
). This concludes the proof.
∎

The proof of the above lemma is inspired by
[
HKMMM20
, Proposition 4.6, p.44]
. In the following Lemma, we gather certain conservation properties of solutions
(f,\bm{u})
to the Vlasov-Stokes system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), the proof of which can be found in
[
Ham98
]
. Hence, we skip its proof.

Lemma 2.2
.

The following properties is satisfied by the solutions
(f,\bm{u})
to the Vlasov-Stokes system:

(1)

(Positivity preserving)
For any given non-negative initial data
f_{0}
, the solution
f
remains non-negative.

(2)

(Mass conservation)
The total mass of the solution
f
is conserved in the following sense:

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}\,f(t,x,v)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}\,f_{0}(x,v)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v,\quad t\in[0,T].

(3)

(Total momentum conservation)
The total momentum of the solution pair
\left(f,\bm{u}\right)
is preserved in the following sense: for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}vf(t,x,v)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v+\int_{\Omega_{x}}\bm{u}(t,x)\,{\rm d}x=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}vf_{0}(x,v)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v+\int_{\Omega_{x}}\bm{u_{0}}(x)\,{\rm d}x.

The following Lemma gives the important energy-dissipation identity satisfied by the solutions to the Vlasov-Stokes system.

Lemma 2.3
.

(Energy dissipation)

The total energy of the Vlasov-Stokes system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) dissipates in time, i.e.,

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}|v|^{2}f(t,x,v)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v+\int_{\Omega_{x}}\bm{u}^{2}\,{\rm d}x\right)\leq 0,

provided
f
is non-negative.

Proof.

Multiplying equation (
1.1
) by
\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}
, and equation (
1.2
) by
\bm{u}\in\bm{J}_{1}
followed by integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}\partial_{t}\left(\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}f\right)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{x}}\partial_{t}\bm{u}^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega_{x}}|\nabla_{x}\bm{u}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x-2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}v\cdot\bm{u}f\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v

\displaystyle+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}|v|^{2}f\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}|\bm{u}|^{2}f\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v=0.

The above equality is the same as

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}|v|^{2}\;f\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v+\int_{\Omega_{x}}\bm{u}^{2}{\rm d}x\right)+2\int_{\Omega_{x}}|\nabla_{x}\bm{u}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}|\bm{u}-v|^{2}f\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v=0.

As the third term is non-negative and as the last term is non-negative because
f
is non-negative, we drop them to obtain the desired result. This completes the proof.
∎

As a consequence of above lemma, we also obtain the following identity:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}|v|^{2}f(t,x,v)\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v+\int_{\Omega_{x}}\bm{u}^{2}\,{\rm d}x\right)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{x}}|\nabla_{x}\bm{u}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}|\bm{u}-v|^{2}f\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}t=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}|v|^{2}\,f_{0}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{x}}\bm{u_{0}}^{2}\,{\rm d}x.

Hence, we deduce that

(2.4)

\bm{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\bm{L}^{2})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bm{u}\in L^{2}(0,T;\bm{J}_{1})

provided
|v|^{2}f_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
\bm{u_{0}}\in\bm{L}^{2}
.

Recall by Sobolev imbedding
H^{1}(\Omega_{x})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega_{x})
. Here, when
d=2,~p\in[2,\infty)
and for
d=3,~p\in[2,6]
:

(2.5)

\bm{u}\in L^{2}(0,T;\bm{L}^{p}).

Below, we state a result on the integrability of the local density and local momentum for which proof can be found in
[
Ham98
, Lemma 2.2, p.56]
. These results are essential to obtain the regularity of the solutions to the Stokes equations.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
p\geq 1
, let
\bm{u}\in L^{2}(0,T;\bm{L}^{p+d}),f_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap L^{1}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}\,|v|^{p}f_{0}\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}v<\infty.

Then, the local density
\rho
and the local momentum
\rho V
satisfy the following:

(2.6)

\rho\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;L^{\frac{p+d}{d}}(\Omega_{x})\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\rho V\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;L^{\frac{p+d}{d+1}}(\Omega_{x})\right).

Remark 2.5
.

By choosing
p=5
in Lemma
2.4
for
d=2
, we obtain

(2.7)

\rho\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;L^{\frac{7}{2}}(\Omega_{x})\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\rho V\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;L^{\frac{7}{3}}(\Omega_{x})\right).

Remark 2.6
.

For
d=3
,
p=3
in Lemma
2.4
yields

(2.8)

\rho\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{x})\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\rho V\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega_{x})\right).

An application of the Stokes regularity results
[
GS91
,
SWYZ23
]
yields

(2.9)

\bm{u}\in L^{2}(0,T;\bm{W}^{2,\frac{3}{2}}).

An use of the Sobolev inequality shows

(2.10)

\bm{u}\in L^{2}(0,T;\bm{L}^{p})\quad\mbox{for}\quad\frac{3}{2}\leq p<\infty.

Taking
p=5
in Lemma
2.4
, we obtain

(2.11)

\rho\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;L^{\frac{8}{3}}(\Omega_{x})\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\rho V\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{x})\right).

A use of the Stokes regularity result yields

(2.12)

\bm{u}\in H^{1}(0,T;\bm{L}^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\bm{H}^{2})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;\bm{H}^{1}).

Setting
p=9+\delta
with
\delta>0
in Lemma
2.4
shows

(2.13)

\rho\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;L^{\frac{12+\delta}{3}}(\Omega_{x})\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\rho V\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T;L^{\frac{12+\delta}{4}}(\Omega_{x})\right).

These integrability properties of
\rho
and
\rho V
in the Stokes equation (
1.2
) guarantees that
\bm{u}\in L^{2}(0,T;\bm{W}^{1,\infty})
which is important for our proof of existence of a unique strong solution.

Below, we state and prove a result on the propagation of velocity moments.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\bm{u}\in L^{1}(0,T;\bm{W}^{1,\infty})
and let
f_{0}\geq 0
be such that

\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{kp}\{|f_{0}|^{p}+|\nabla_{x}f_{0}|^{p}+|\nabla_{v}f_{0}|^{p}\}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x\leq C,

for
k\geq 0
and
\langle v\rangle=\left(1+|v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Then, for
k\geq 0
the solution
f
of the kinetic equation satisfies

\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{kp}\{|f|^{p}+|\nabla_{x}f|^{p}+|\nabla_{v}f|^{p}\}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x\leq C\quad\forall~t>0.

Proof.

Consider the equation for
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}
:

\partial_{t}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}+\nabla_{v}\cdot\left(\frac{\partial{\bm{u}}}{\partial x_{i}}f\right)+\nabla_{v}\cdot\left(\left(\bm{u}-v\right)\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}\right)=0,

for
i=1,2,3
. Multiplying the above vector equation by
\langle v\rangle^{kp}|\nabla_{x}f|^{p-1}
, an integration with respect to
x,v
yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{kp}|\nabla_{x}f|^{p}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3},

where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{kp}|\nabla_{x}f|^{p-1}\nabla_{x}\bm{u}\cdot\nabla_{v}f\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=3\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{kp}|\nabla_{x}f|^{p}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x,

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{kp}\left(\bm{u}-v\right)\cdot\nabla_{v}\left(|\nabla_{x}f|^{p}\right)\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x.

After using Young’s inequality in
I_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq\|\nabla_{x}\bm{u}\|_{\bm{L}^{\infty}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{kp}\left(\frac{p-1}{p}|\nabla_{x}f|^{p}+\frac{1}{p}|\nabla_{v}f|^{p}\right)\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x.

An integration by parts yields

\displaystyle I_{3}=-\frac{3}{p}\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{kp}|\nabla_{x}f|^{p}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x+I_{4}

with

I_{4}=k\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\,\langle v\rangle^{kp-2}v\cdot\left(\bm{u}-v\right)|\nabla_{x}f|^{p}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x.

A use of the Young’s inequality shows

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle\leq k\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{L}^{\infty}}\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\,\langle v\rangle^{kp-1}|\nabla_{x}f|^{p}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x+k\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\,\langle v\rangle^{kp}|\nabla_{x}f|^{p}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{L}^{\infty}}\right)\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{kp}|\nabla_{x}f|^{p}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x.

A similar computation involving the equation for
\nabla_{v}f
yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{kp}|\nabla_{v}f|^{p}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{kp}\left(|\nabla_{x}f|^{p}+|\nabla_{v}f|^{p}\right)\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+C\left(1+\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{L}^{\infty}}\right)\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{kp}|\nabla_{v}f|^{p}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x.

Altogether, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{kp}\left(|\nabla_{x}f|^{p}+|\nabla_{v}f|^{p}\right)\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x\right)\leq C\left(1+\|\bm{u}\|_{\bm{W}^{1,\infty}}\right)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{kp}\left(|\nabla_{x}f|^{p}+|\nabla_{v}f|^{p}\right)\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x.

A use of Grönwall’s inequality completes the rest of the proof.
∎

2.2.
Existence and Uniqueness result in

)-(

) the continuum model.

Below, we stated the result on the existence of strong solution to the Vlasov-Stokes equation whose proof can be found in
[
CKL11
, Theorem 5, p. 2435]
.

Theorem 2.8
.

(Existence of strong solution)

Let the initial data
(f_{0},\bm{u_{0}})
be such that

f_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}),\quad f_{0}\geq 0,

\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{kp}\left\{|f_{0}|^{p}+|\nabla_{x}f_{0}|^{p}+|\nabla_{v}f_{0}|^{p}\right\}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x\leq C,

for
p\in(3,\infty),\quad k>4-\frac{3}{p}
and
\bm{u_{0}}\in\bm{W}^{1,p}\cap\bm{J}_{1}
.
Then, there exists a global-in-time strong solution
(f,\bm{u},p)
to the Vlasov-Stokes system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
). Furthermore,

\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{kp}\left\{|f|^{p}+|\nabla_{x}f|^{p}+|\nabla_{v}f|^{p}\right\}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x\leq C,

and

\bm{u}\in\bm{L}^{r}(0,T;\bm{W}^{2,q})\cap H^{1}(0,T;\bm{L}^{q})

for
q<p
and
r\in(1,\infty)
. Here,
\langle v\rangle=\left(1+|v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

The following theorem is on uniqueness of the strong solution to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).

Theorem 2.9
.

(Uniqueness of strong solution)

Under the hypothesis of Theorem
2.8
there is atmost one strong solution
(f,\bm{u},p)
to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).

Proof.

Suppose the solution is not unique, that is,
(f_{1},\bm{u}_{1},p_{1})
and
(f_{2},\bm{u}_{2},p_{2})
are two distinct strong solutions of (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) with say
\bm{u}_{1}\neq\bm{u}_{2},p_{1}\neq p_{2}
and
f_{1}\neq f_{2}
. Let
\bar{\bm{u}}=\bm{u}_{1}-\bm{u}_{2},\bar{p}=p_{1}-p_{2}
and
\bar{f}=f_{1}-f_{2}
, then
\bar{\bm{u}},\bar{p}
and
\bar{f}
satisfies the following equations:

(2.14)

\bar{f}_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\bar{f}+\nabla_{v}\cdot\left(\bar{\bm{u}}f_{1}+\bm{u}_{2}\bar{f}-v\bar{f}\right)=0,

and

(2.15)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \bar{\bm{u}}_{t}-\Delta_{x}\bar{\bm{u}}+\nabla_{x}p&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(v\bar{f}-\bm{u}_{2}\bar{f}-\bar{\bm{u}}f_{1}\right)\,{\rm d}v,\\
\nabla_{x}\cdot\bar{\bm{u}}&=0\end{aligned}\right.

with initial data

\bar{f}(0,x,v)=0,\qquad\bar{\bm{u}}(0,x)=0.

By multiplying the equation (
2.15
) by
\bar{\bm{u}}
and integrating in
x
, we obtain

(2.16)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\|\bar{\bm{u}}\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}}+\|\nabla_{x}\bar{\bm{u}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{x}}\bar{\bm{u}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}v\bar{f}\,{\rm d}v\right){\rm d}x-\int_{\Omega_{x}}|\bar{\bm{u}}|^{2}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f_{1}\,{\rm d}v\right){\rm d}x

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega_{x}}\bm{u}_{2}\bar{\bm{u}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\bar{f}\,{\rm d}v\right){\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Omega_{x}}|\bar{\bm{u}}|^{2}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{1}{\langle v\rangle^{2k-2}}\,{\rm d}v\right){\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{2k}|\bar{f}|^{2}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\|\bm{u}_{2}\|_{\bm{L}^{\infty}}\left(|\bar{\bm{u}}|^{2}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{1}{\langle v\rangle^{2k}}\,{\rm d}v\right){\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{2k}|\bar{f}|^{2}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\bar{\bm{u}}\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}}+\|\langle v\rangle^{k}\bar{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})},

here, in the second step use the Hölder inequality and in the last step use the Young’s inequality with assumption that
k>\frac{5}{2}
.

Now, multiply equation (
2.14
) by
\langle v\rangle^{2k}\bar{f}
and integrate in
x,v
-variables, to obtain

(2.17)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle^{k}\bar{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2})}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{2k}v\cdot\nabla_{x}\bar{f}^{2}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{2k}\bar{f}\,\bar{\bm{u}}\cdot\nabla_{v}f_{1}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{2k}\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{f}^{2}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+3\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{2k}\bar{f}^{2}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{2k}v\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{f}^{2}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x.

A use of integration by parts yields

(2.18)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\|\langle v\rangle^{k}\bar{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}=\tilde{T}_{1}+\tilde{T}_{2}+\tilde{T}_{3}

where

\tilde{T}_{1}=-\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{2k}\bar{f}\,\bar{\bm{u}}\cdot\nabla_{v}f_{1}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x

\tilde{T}_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{2k}\bar{\bm{u}}_{2}\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{f}^{2}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x

\tilde{T}_{3}=3\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{2k}\bar{f}^{2}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{2k}v\cdot\nabla_{v}\bar{f}^{2}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x.

For
\tilde{T}_{1}
term

(2.19)

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{2k}\bar{f}^{2}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{x}}|\bar{\bm{u}}|^{5}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\frac{1}{\langle v\rangle^{5\alpha}}\,{\rm d}v\right)\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{5}}\cdot

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{\frac{10(k+\alpha)}{3}}(\nabla_{v}f_{1})^{\frac{10}{3}}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{3}{10}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\langle v\rangle^{k}\bar{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|\bar{\bm{u}}\|_{\bm{L}^{5}}\|\langle v\rangle^{k+\alpha}\nabla_{v}f_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{10}{3}}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\langle v\rangle^{k}\bar{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\left(\|\bar{\bm{u}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}}^{\frac{1}{10}}\|\nabla\bar{\bm{u}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}}^{\frac{9}{10}}+\|\bar{\bm{u}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}}\right)\|\langle v\rangle^{k+\alpha}\nabla_{v}f_{1}\|_{L^{\frac{10}{3}}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\langle v\rangle^{k}\bar{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|\langle v\rangle^{k+\alpha}\nabla_{v}f_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{\frac{10}{3}}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}+\frac{1}{20}\|\bar{\bm{u}}\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}}+\frac{9}{20}\|\nabla\bar{\bm{u}}\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\|\langle v\rangle^{k}\bar{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|\langle v\rangle^{k+\alpha}\nabla_{v}f_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{\frac{10}{3}}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}+\frac{1}{2}\|\bar{\bm{u}}\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\langle v\rangle^{k+\alpha}\nabla_{v}f_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{\frac{10}{3}}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\|\langle v\rangle^{k}\bar{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}+2\|\bar{\bm{u}}\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}}+\frac{9}{20}\|\nabla\bar{\bm{u}}\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}}.

Here, in the second step we have used the Hölder inequality with the assumption that
\alpha>\frac{3}{5}
, in the fourth step the Gagliardo - Nirenberg inequality
[
HKMM20
, Theorem 9.9, p. 1317]
and in the fifth step, we have applied the Young’s inequality.

A use of integration by parts with the Hölder inequality yields

(2.20)

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{2}

\displaystyle=k\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{2k-2}v\cdot\bm{u}_{2}\bar{f}^{2}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq k\|\bm{u}_{2}\|_{\bm{L}^{\infty}}\|\langle v\rangle^{k}\bar{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}.

Apply the integration by parts, then the
\tilde{T}_{3}
term can be estimated as

(2.21)

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{3}

\displaystyle=3\|\langle v\rangle^{k}\bar{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}-k\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{2k}\bar{f}^{2}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x-\frac{3}{2}\int_{\Omega_{x}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{2k}\bar{f}^{2}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=-\left(k-\frac{3}{2}\right)\|\langle v\rangle^{k}\bar{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}.

Putting the estimates (
2.19
)-(
2.21
) into (
2.18
) shows

(2.22)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\|\langle v\rangle^{k}\bar{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leq C\|\langle v\rangle^{k}\bar{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}+2\|\bar{\bm{u}}\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}}+\frac{9}{20}\|\nabla\bar{\bm{u}}\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}}.

Adding equations (
2.16
) and (
2.22
), we obtain

(2.23)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left(\|\bar{\bm{u}}\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}}+\|\langle v\rangle^{k}\bar{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\right)+\frac{11}{20}\|\nabla\bar{\bm{u}}\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}}\leq C\left(\|\langle v\rangle^{k}\bar{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}+\|\bar{\bm{u}}\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}}\right).

A use of the Grönwall’s inequality yields

(2.24)

\|\bar{\bm{u}}\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}}+\|\langle v\rangle^{k}\bar{f}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leq 0.

This leads to a contradiction and
\bm{u}_{1}=\bm{u}_{2},f_{1}=f_{2}
. It completes the rest of the proof.
∎

By adding (
4.35
) and (
4.37
), we obtain

(4.38)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left(\|\bm{\theta_{u}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}}+\|\theta_{f}\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{k+1}+h^{2k-\frac{1}{2}}+h^{k-\frac{3}{2}}\|\bm{\theta_{u}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|\theta_{f}\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}+h^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|\bm{\theta_{u}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}}\|\theta_{f}\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}.

Thus, for
k\geq 2
, it becomes

(4.39)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\left(\|\bm{\theta_{u}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}}+\|\theta_{f}\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{k+1}+\left(\|\bm{\theta_{u}}\|_{\bm{L}^{2}}+\|\theta_{f}\|_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}\right)+h^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left(\|\bm{\theta_{u}}\|^{2}_{\bm{L}^{2}}+\|\theta_{f}\|^{2}_{0,\mathcal{T}_{h}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{k+1}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\bm{\theta_{u}},\theta_{f})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+h^{-\frac{3}{2}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\bm{\theta_{u}},\theta_{f})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2},

with same definition of
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\cdot,\cdot)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
as in Theorem
4.8
. Set

\Psi(t)=h^{k+1}+\int_{0}^{t}\left({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\bm{\theta_{u}},\theta_{f})(s)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+h^{-\frac{3}{2}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\bm{\theta_{u}},\theta_{f})(s)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\right){\rm d}s.

On integration and proceed in a similar manner as in the proof of Theorem
4.8
to obtain

\Psi(t)\left(1-h^{k-\frac{1}{2}}(e^{CT}-1)\right)\leq e^{CT}h^{k+1}.

For
k\geq 2
and with smallness assumption on
h

(1-h^{k-\frac{1}{2}}(e^{cT}-1))>0.

Hence, the result of Theorem
4.9
follows for
3
D for
k\geq 2
.

5.
Numerical Experiments

This section, we report on some numerical simulations based on a splitting algorithm. We have approximated solutions to the following Vlasov-unsteady Stokes model with source terms:

(5.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f+\nabla_{v}\cdot\Big(\left(\bm{u}-v\right)f\Big)&=\mathcal{F}(t,x,v)\quad\mbox{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega_{x}\times\Omega_{v},\\
f(0,x,v)&=f_{0}(x,v)\qquad\mbox{ in }\Omega_{x}\times\Omega_{v}.\end{aligned}\right.

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\bm{u}-\Delta_{x}\bm{u}+\rho\bm{u}+\nabla_{x}p&=\rho V+\mathcal{G}(t,x)\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega_{x},\\
\nabla_{x}\cdot\bm{u}&=0\qquad\mbox{ in }\Omega_{x}.\end{aligned}\right.

For the computational results, we employed splitting algorithm for the linear kinetic equation (
5.1
), using the Lie-Trotter splitting method.
To achieve this, firstly, split the equation (
5.1
) as:

(5.2)

(a)\quad\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}f+\nabla_{v}\cdot\left(\left(\bm{u}-v\right)f\right)=\mathcal{F}(t,x,v).\end{aligned}\qquad(b)\quad\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f=0,\end{aligned}

To compute the solution
f
, we first solve part
(a)
of (
5.2
) over the full time step using
f_{0}
as the initial data to obtain an intermediate solution
\tilde{f}
. Next, part
(b)
of (
5.2
) is solved over the full time step with
\tilde{f}
as the initial data.

For a temporal discretization,
let
\{t_{n}\}_{n=0}^{N}
be a uniform partition of the time interval
[0,T]
, where
t_{n}=n\Delta t
with time step
\Delta t>0
.
Let
F^{n}\in\mathcal{Z}_{h},\bm{U}^{n}\in\bm{H}_{h},P^{n}\in L_{h},\rho_{h}^{n}
and
\rho_{h}^{n}V_{h}^{n}
be the approximations of
f^{n}=f(t_{n}),\bm{u}^{n}=\bm{u}(t_{n}),p^{n}=p(t_{n}),\rho^{n}=\rho(t_{n})
and
\rho^{n}V^{n}=\rho(t_{n})V(t_{n})
, respectively.
Our numerical algorithm is to find
\left(\bm{U}^{n+1},P^{n+1},F^{n+1}\right)\in\bm{H}_{h}\times L_{h}\times\mathcal{Z}_{h}
, for
n=0,1,\cdots,N-1
such that

(5.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \left(\frac{\bm{U}^{n+1}-\bm{U}^{n}}{\Delta t},\bm{\psi}_{h}\right)+a_{h}(\bm{U}^{n+1},\bm{\psi}_{h})&+b_{h}(\bm{\psi}_{h},P^{n+1})+\left(\rho_{h}^{n}\bm{U}^{n+1},\bm{\psi}_{h}\right)\\
&=\left(\rho_{h}^{n}V_{h}^{n}+\mathcal{G}^{n+1},\bm{\psi}_{h}\right)~\forall~\bm{\psi}_{h}\in\bm{H}_{h},\\
-b_{h}(\bm{U}^{n+1},w_{h})+s_{h}(P^{n+1},w_{h})&=0\quad\forall\quad w_{h}\in L_{h},\\
\left(\frac{\tilde{F}-F^{n}}{\Delta t},\phi_{h}\right)+\mathcal{B}^{x}_{h}\left(\bm{U}^{n+1};F^{n},\phi_{h}\right)&=\left(\mathcal{F}^{n},\phi_{h}\right)\quad\forall\quad\phi_{h}\in\mathcal{Z}_{h},\\
\left(\frac{F^{n+1}-\tilde{F}}{\Delta t},\psi_{h}\right)+\mathcal{B}^{v}_{h}(\tilde{F},\psi_{h})&=0\quad\forall\quad\psi_{h}\in\mathcal{Z}_{h},\end{aligned}\right.

where,
a_{h}(\bm{U}^{n},\bm{\psi}_{h}),b_{h}(\bm{\psi}_{h},P^{n})
and
s_{h}(P^{n},w_{h})
are defined by (
3.11
)-(
3.13
) at
t=t_{n}
. In (
5.3
) we have used the following notations:

\mathcal{B}^{v}_{h}(F^{n},\phi_{h}):=\sum_{R\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\mathcal{B}^{v}_{h,R}(F^{n},\phi_{h})

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{v}_{h,R}(F^{n},\phi_{h})

\displaystyle:=-\int_{T^{v}}\int_{T^{x}}F^{n}\,v.\nabla_{x}\phi_{h}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}v+\int_{T^{v}}\int_{\partial T^{x}}\hbox{\hskip 15.29918pt\hskip-15.29918pt\hbox{$v\cdot\bm{n}F^{n}$}\hskip-15.29918pt\hskip-4.16667pt\raisebox{17.83344pt}{\hbox{$\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\kern-0.6pt\bigwedge\kern-0.6pt}}}}$}}\hskip-4.16667pt\hskip 15.29918pt}\phi_{h}\,{\rm d}s(x)\,{\rm d}v

and

\mathcal{B}^{x}_{h}(\bm{U}^{n+1};F^{n},\phi_{h}):=\sum_{R\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\mathcal{B}^{x}_{h,R}(\bm{U}^{n+1};F^{n},\phi_{h}),

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{x}_{h,R}(\bm{U}^{n+1};F^{n},\phi_{h})

\displaystyle:=-\int_{T^{x}}\int_{T^{v}}F^{n}\left(\bm{U}^{n+1}-v\right).\nabla_{v}\phi_{h}\,{\rm d}v\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{T^{x}}\int_{\partial T^{v}}\hbox{\hskip 33.21149pt\hskip-33.21149pt\hbox{$\left(\bm{U}^{n+1}-v\right)\cdot\bm{n}F^{n}$}\hskip-33.21149pt\hskip-4.16667pt\raisebox{19.14015pt}{\hbox{$\hbox{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\lx@scalerel@obj{\kern-0.6pt\bigwedge\kern-0.6pt}}}}$}}\hskip-4.16667pt\hskip 33.21149pt}\phi_{h}\,{\rm d}s(v)\,{\rm d}x

wherein the numerical fluxes are defined by (
3.10
) at
t=t_{n}
.

For our computation, let
\bm{x}_{l}
and

6.
Conclusion

In this paper, a semi-discrete numerical method for
2
D Vlasov-Stokes equation is introduced and analysed (see (
3.5
)-(
3.7
) for the discrete problem). This is a dG-dG method for the Vlasov and the Stokes equations in phase space. The scheme is mass and momentum conserving. In continuum case, a non-negative initial data
(f_{0}(x,v)\geq 0)
yields a non-negative solution for all times. At present, we are unable to prove a similar positivity preserving property for our discrete system and hence, it is difficult to prove a discrete energy dissipation property. Optimal rates of convergence, with regards to the degree of polynomials
k\geq 1
, for the distribution function
f(t,x,v)
and the fluid velocity
\bm{u}(t,x)
are proved in the Theorem
4.9
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
and
L^{\infty}(0,T;\bm{L}^{2})
-norms, respectively. These optimal rates help us deduce super-convergence result for
\left(\bm{\Pi_{u}u}-\bm{u}_{h}\right)
in
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
-norm (see Lemma
4.10
). This enables us to derive optimal rate of convergence for the fluid velocity in
\L^{\infty}(0,T;\bm{L}^{p})
-norm
1\leq p\leq\infty
, subject to the availability of certain projection estimates. In Lemma
4.13
, super-convergence result for
\left(\bm{\Pi_{u}u}-\bm{u}_{h}\right)
and
\left(\Pi_{p}p-p_{h}\right)
in
L^{2}(0,T;{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|})
and
L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{x})
-norms, respectively are shown. The optimal rate of convergence for the fluid pressure in
L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega_{x})
-norm is derived in Theorem
4.14
. Finally, in subsection
4.5
we comment on the
3
D Vlasov-Stokes equation.

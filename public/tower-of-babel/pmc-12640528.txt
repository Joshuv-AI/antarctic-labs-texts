Does the use of perioperative corticosteroids reduce the incidence of seroma formation after breast cancer surgery? A systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Jawad S. Alnajjar, Hadeel Abdullah Bakheet, Mohamed Azzam M. Addas, Reem A. Alghamdi, Mohammed Abdulmohsen AlSharit, Nadine Nasser Farghal, Nora Hatem Trabulsi, Ali Farsi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND: Seroma is a common complication of breast cancer surgery, affecting up to 85% of cases.OBJECTIVE: To assess the effectiveness of perioperative corticosteroids in reducing seroma formation.DESIGN: Systematic review and meta-analysis of published literature from PubMed, Ovid, Google Scholar, and Web of Science databases.METHODS: A systematic search identified randomized controlled trials (RCTs) on corticosteroids in breast cancer surgery. Data were pooled using a random-effects model. Subgroup analyses were performed by corticosteroid type, timing, and route. Risk of bias (RoB) was assessed using the Cochrane Collaboration's RoB Tool for RCTs. Articles were collected from inception to August 2024. Inclusion criteria: RCTs and observational studies evaluating perioperative corticosteroids in breast cancer surgery. Exclusion criteria: non-breast surgery populations and absence of seroma outcome data.MAIN OUTCOMES MEASURES: Incidence of postoperative seromaSAMPLE SIZE: Seven RCTs involving 689 patients were included.RESULTS: Pooled analysis using random-effects model demonstrated that corticosteroids reduced seroma risk (OR: 0.31, 95% CI: 0.20–0.48). Hydrocortisone was more effective than methylprednisolone (OR: 0.14 vs. 0.41; P=.0155). Heterogeneity was low (I²=17.3%).RISK OF BIAS: Publication bias assessment using Duval and Tweedie method identified three hypothetically missing studies; adjusted pooled estimate: OR 0.42 (95% CI: 0.28–0.63, P<.001).CONCLUSION: Corticosteroids, especially hydrocortisone, appears to be effective in reducing seroma formation.LIMITATIONS: Small sample size, protocol variability, and moderate publication bias as evidenced by funnel plot asymmetry and heterogeneity in timing subgroups (I²=48.1%).REGISTRATION NO: CRD42024590767.

INTRODUCTION

In 2022, breast cancer was the most diagnosed cancer among women, with 2.3 million new cases globally. It caused 670 000 deaths, making it a leading cause of cancer mortality in women.1 The lifetime risk of breast cancer varies by region, with higher prevalence in countries with a high human development index, such as the US, where 1 in 8 women is diagnosed during their lifetime.1 In Saudi Arabia, breast cancer is the most common cancer among women, accounting for about 29% of all female cancers, with an age-standardized incidence rate of 23.6 per 100 000 women. Mortality rates are relatively low due to early detection campaigns and improved healthcare infrastructure.2,3

Despite advances in surgical techniques and perioperative care, postoperative complications remain a significant concern in breast cancer management. Among these, seroma is one of the most common and clinically relevant complications, particularly following procedures such as mastectomy and lumpectomy.4,5 The incidence of seroma varies widely, ranging from 10% to 85%, depending on factors such as surgical technique, patient characteristics, and diagnostic criteria.6,7 The risk is notably higher in breast cancer surgeries compared to non-oncological procedures due to the extensive disruption of lymphatic channels, especially during mastectomy and axillary lymph node dissection.8,9 The pathophysiology of seroma formation is multifactorial and includes lymphatic injury, increased vascular permeability induced by the inflammatory response, and accumulation of fluid within surgically created dead space.10 Known risk factors for seroma formation include advanced age, obesity, and larger tumor size.6,7

Clinically, seromas may present with swelling, discomfort, heaviness, pain, skin tension, limited shoulder mobility, and delayed wound healing. If recurrent or left untreated, seromas can lead to more serious complications such as infection, abscess formation, chronic drainage, hematoma, fibrosis, skin flap necrosis, and impaired cosmetic outcomes.4,8,11 In addition to the physical burden, the condition significantly impacts patients' quality of life, often requiring repeated needle aspirations and causing psychological distress due to prolonged recovery and potential delays in adjuvant treatments such as chemotherapy or radiotherapy.4

Standard management of seroma includes needle aspiration, closed-suction drainage, sclerotherapy with agents such as doxycycline and iodine, and surgical procedures such as marsupialization and capsule debridement.9,12 However, recurrence remains common. Needle aspiration has a recurrence rate of 50%–80%, whereas sclerotherapy, though more effective (70%–90%), may cause discomfort and delay recovery.9,13 Flap fixation can reduce seroma formation by 50%–60%, but its efficacy is inconsistent, especially in extensive surgeries such as modified radical mastectomy.14 Due to these limitations, recent research has emphasized preventive approaches, including anti-inflammatory agents, tissue adhesives, and preoperative pharmacologic strategies. Among these, corticosteroids such as hydrocortisone show promise in preventing seroma formation by reducing inflammation, vascular permeability, and fluid accumulation.15 In this systematic review, we investigate the effectiveness of preoperative corticosteroid injections in reducing the incidence of seroma following breast cancer surgeries.

METHODS

A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) assessing the clinical utility of perioperative corticosteroid administration in breast surgery was conducted following the guidelines set by the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA).16 Prospectively, we registered the study protocol in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) (registration number: CRD42024590767).

Search strategy

A comprehensive electronic search of PubMed, Ovid, Google Scholar, and Web of Science was conducted on August 1, 2024. All articles published from database inception through August 2024 were screened to identify studies evaluating postoperative seroma rates associated with perioperative steroid administration in breast surgery. The search terms included [“steroids”] and [“breast surgery” or “mastectomy” or “wide local excision”]. Full search terms can be found in (Supplement 1). Only manuscripts published in English were included [Author: No publication date restriction was deleted, since there was a restriction (Aug 2024)].

All titles were initially screened to determine relevance. Studies that met the inclusion criteria were then reviewed in detail, including abstract and full-text analysis. Studies were eligible for inclusion if they were RCTs or observational studies involving patients undergoing breast surgery, including mastectomy, modified radical mastectomy, and wide local excision. The intervention of interest was perioperative corticosteroid administration, delivered preoperatively, intraoperatively, or postoperatively. The primary outcome was the incidence of seroma formation in the corticosteroid group compared to the placebo group. Studies must have data on sample size, baseline characteristics, treatment protocols, and seroma formation incidence. The exclusion criteria were studies with populations not undergoing breast surgery, studies not evaluating perioperative corticosteroid use, and those without seroma outcome data. Two independent reviewers (JA and MA) conducted the literature search, using a predesigned search strategy. Duplicate records were removed, and each reviewer carefully assessed the identified studies to ensure that they met the inclusion criteria before extracting relevant data.

The data extraction was performed by the reviewers, who independently retrieved and analyzed key data including sample size, baseline characteristics, treatment protocols, and outcomes related to seroma formation incidence. The quality of the studies included in the meta-analysis was assessed using standardized tools for evaluating the risk of bias (RoB) and study quality. Any discrepancies between reviewers were resolved through discussion. The methodological quality of the included studies was evaluated using the Cochrane Collaboration's risk-of-bias tool for randomized trials.17 An additional contributor was incorporated into the authorship to support the literature review and facilitate the writing of the manuscript.

Data synthesis and analysis

A meta-analysis was conducted to pool data from the included studies using appropriate statistical methods using Review Manager software version 5.1.4, provided by the Cochrane Collaboration.18 The analysis compared the effectiveness of corticosteroids versus placebo across multiple studies. Subgroup analyses were performed based on corticosteroid type (methylprednisolone and hydrocortisone), timing of administration (pre, post, and pre and post), and route of administration (intravenous and local). Pooled odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated using fixed-effect and random-effect models, depending on the level of heterogeneity, which was assessed using the Chi² test and I² statistics. A test for subgroup differences was conducted to identify variations between groups, with statistical significance set at P<.05. Additionally, the PRISMA 2020 flow diagram was used to visualize the study selection process.

RESULTS

Literature review

The initial database search yielded a total of 131 articles; 86 were screened by titles and abstracts, and 14 articles were identified as potentially relevant for full-text review. Following a thorough assessment of the eligibility criteria, seven studies were included in the final analysis. Outcomes of corticosteroid use on seroma formation

The comparative analysis of the corticosteroid versus placebo groups across multiple studies revealed the efficacy of corticosteroids in the assessed outcomes. The pooled ORs for the common-effect model (OR=0.30; 95% CI: 0.21–0.44) and the random-effect model (OR=0.31; 95% CI: 0.20–0.48) suggest a statistically significant advantage favoring corticosteroids over placebo in reducing adverse events. The included studies demonstrated low to moderate heterogeneity (I²=17.3%, P=.2978)15,19–24 (Figure 2).

Forest plot showing the subgroup analysis of the effectiveness of corticosteroids (methylprednisolone and hydrocortisone) compared to placebo.

Methylprednisolone versus hydrocortisone

The subgroup analysis compared the effects of methylprednisolone and hydrocortisone against placebo. For methylprednisolone, the pooled OR was 0.41 (95% CI: 0.26–0.64; I²=0%, P=.8963).19‣21,24 For hydrocortisone, the pooled OR was 0.14 (95% CI: 0.07–0.30; I²=0%, P=.5883).15,22 The test for subgroup differences resulted in Chi²=5.86 (P=.0155) (Figure 3).

Forest plot showing the subgroup analysis of the effectiveness of corticosteroids (methylprednisolone and hydrocortisone) compared to placebo.

Timing of administration on seroma formation

The subgroup analysis based on treatment timing (pre, post, and pre-and-post) showed that in the “pre” subgroup, the pooled OR was 0.24 (95% CI: 0.14–0.43) using the common-effect model and 0.30 (95% CI: 0.12–0.74) using the random-effect model, with moderate heterogeneity (I²=48.1%, P=.1227).15,19,23 In the “post” subgroup, the pooled OR was 0.38 (95% CI: 0.23–0.65) in both models, with no heterogeneity (I²=0%, P=.8044).20–22 The “pre and post” subgroup had a pooled OR of 0.21 (95% CI: 0.04–1.06).24 The test for subgroup differences showed no significant variation (Chi²=0.61, P=.7389) (Figure 4).

Forest plot showing the subgroup analysis of the effectiveness of corticosteroids based on timing of administration (pre, post, and pre and post) compared to placebo.

1.4 Route of administration and seroma formation

The subgroup analysis based on the route of administration (intravenous or local) showed the following results. For the “intravenous” subgroup, the pooled OR was 0.24 (95% CI: 0.14–0.41) using the common-effect model and 0.27 (95% CI: 0.13–0.55) using the random-effect model, with moderate heterogeneity (I²=31.2%, P=.2135).15,22 For the “local” subgroup, the pooled OR was 0.38 (95% CI: 0.23–0.65) in both the common- and random-effect models, with no heterogeneity (I²=0%, P=.8044).20,23,24 The test for subgroup differences resulted in Chi²=0.59 (P=.4432) (Figure 5).

RoB assessment

The methodological quality assessment using the Cochrane Collaboration's risk-of-bias tool revealed variable quality across the included studies (Table 2). Only one study by Qvamme et al demonstrated low RoB across all domains. The remaining six studies were assessed as having unclear overall RoB, mainly due to inadequate reporting of certain necessary methodological details rather than evidence of actual bias. The most common methodological concerns identified were unclear allocation concealment (reported in four studies) and unclear blinding of outcome assessment (reported in five studies), which could impact the validity of seroma detection and measurement. Random sequence generation was adequately described in six of seven studies. Incomplete outcome data and selective reporting were well-managed across all studies, with low RoB consistently observed in these domains. The study by Khaleel et al showed the most significant methods related to limitations with unclear ratings across multiple domains including random sequence generation, allocation concealment, blinding procedures, and selective reporting. Despite these limitations, the consistency of results across studies with varying risk profiles, combined with the biological profile of corticosteroid anti-inflammatory effects, supports the significance of the overall findings.

Heterogeneity

The assessment of heterogeneity across the included studies revealed generally low levels of statistical heterogeneity, supporting the validity of our earlier pooled estimates. It was mostly demonstrated low to moderate heterogeneity (I²=17.3%, P=.2978), indicating that the studies were sufficiently homogeneous to justify the pooled estimates using both fixed-effect and random-effect models (Table 3). Subgroup analyses by corticosteroid type showed no heterogeneity within individual drug categories, with both methylprednisolone (I²=0%, P=.8963) and hydrocortisone (I²=0%, P=.5883) subgroups demonstrating reliable homogeneity. However, the test for subgroup differences between drug types was statistically significant (Chi²=5.86, P=.0155), suggesting that the choice of corticosteroid may affect the treatment efficacy. Assessment by timing of administration revealed moderate heterogeneity in the pre-operative settings (I²=48.1%, P=.1227) compared to no heterogeneity in the post-operative settings (I²=0%, P=.8044), though the overall test for subgroup differences was not significant (P=.7389). Also in a similar manner, route of administration analysis showed moderate heterogeneity in the intravenous subgroup (I²=31.2%, P=.2135) while the local injection subgroup demonstrated no heterogeneity (I²=0%, P=.8044), with no significant difference between routes (P=.4432).

Publication bias

Visual inspection of the funnel plot revealed moderate asymmetry for certain variables suggesting possible risk of publication bias, with more focus on absence of smaller studies that were evident by higher standard error, by either reporting null or positive effects favoring the control group (Figure 6). The Duval and Tweedie trim and fill adjustment method was utilized and identified three possible missing studies on the right side of the funnel plot, indicating possible unpublished studies with results favoring the control group. When these imputed studies were integrated into our results, the adjusted pooled estimates shifted from 0.31 (95% CI: 0.20–0.48) to 0.42 (95% CI: 0.28–0.63), representing a more conservative but still statistically significant treatment effect. Despite this adjustment suggesting the presence of publication bias, the beneficial effect of perioperative corticosteroids in reducing seroma formation remained statistically significant and clinically meaningful. The funnel plot demonstrates that larger, more precise studies that were lower standard error, which clustered more symmetrically around the pooled effect estimate, while smaller studies showed greater dispersion primarily favoring the intervention group.

DISCUSSION

Seroma prevention is a critical component of postoperative management because its occurrence can extend hospital stays, delay adjuvant therapies, increase follow-up requirements, and escalate healthcare costs. Multiple factors contribute to seroma formation, including anatomical considerations and the biochemical properties of seroma fluid. Kuroi et al identified key contributors such as the extent of surgical dissection, shearing forces from chest wall motion, and the presence of dead space.25 Additional proposed mechanisms include the absence of fibrinogen in seroma fluid, inflammatory responses, and chylous leakage. Our aim in this analysis was to explore the potential impact of using corticosteroids (methylprednisolone and hydrocortisone) in reducing perioperative stress and inflammation to the breast tissue to reduce the potential formation of seroma after breast cancer surgeries, including mastectomy, radical mastectomy, and wide local excision.

Our meta-analysis demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in seroma formation with corticosteroid use. Multiple RCTs supported the use of corticosteroids to decrease postoperative seroma rates. Furthermore, when comparing methylprednisolone and hydrocortisone, no significant superiority was found between the two agents. This could be due to the mechanism of action of both medications in reducing the inflammatory response and physiological stress.26 Likewise, the timing of administration (preoperative, postoperative, or both) did not significantly alter outcomes. However, the optimal route of administration (intravenous versus local injection) remains debatable. Further investigations are required to formulate a preference. However, the use of the systemic intravenous route might propose adverse effects, which include the increased risk of manifesting mineralocorticoid effects specifically while using hydrocortisone intravenously. However, the use of methylprednisolone carries a negligible mineralocorticoid activity, which makes it preferable, intravenously or locally.

A previous meta-analysis demonstrated that suture quilting significantly reduced the incidence of seroma formation and shortened drainage duration compared to conventional closure techniques.27 Additionally, topical tranexamic acid has been shown to significantly reduce hematoma rates in mastectomy patients, although it had no significant effect on other postoperative outcomes.28 Conversely, fibrin sealants provide only limited benefit in reducing seroma volume, drainage volume, and duration, and are therefore primarily reserved—following shared decision-making—for breast cancer patients undergoing axillary dissection.29 Future research should explore the potency, timing, duration of action, and possible adverse events of corticosteroids in the context of perioperative course. Additionally, the use of corticosteroids with other modalities, such as quilting, to identify potential synergistic effects that could further enhance patient outcomes.27

No definitive adverse effects have yet been established regarding the use of corticosteroids for the prevention of seroma formation following breast cancer surgery. However, findings from a previous double-blind placebo-controlled RCT indicated a higher frequency of seroma colonization in the corticosteroid group. Although colonization was also observed in the saline group, it occurred at a lower rate.30 Given the potential risk of wound infection associated with methylprednisolone sodium succinate, its routine use should be approached with caution and may not be preferred in clinical practice.31

LIMITATIONS

This study is limited by the small number of available RCTs, which constrains the strength and generalizability of the findings. Several included studies had an unclear RoB, introducing potential methodological concerns. Additionally, heterogeneity in corticosteroid type, dosage, timing, and route of administration complicates direct comparison of outcomes. Although subgroup analyses were conducted based on corticosteroid type, timing, and administration route, limited study numbers precluded more detailed stratification, such as by dosage. The lack of standardized definitions for seroma formation and inconsistent outcome reporting further limit interpretability. Data on the long-term safety of perioperative corticosteroids in this context remain sparse.

CONCLUSION

Corticosteroids significantly reduced seroma formation after breast cancer surgery, with both methylprednisolone and hydrocortisone showing benefit, though hydrocortisone appeared more effective. Intravenous and local administration routes were similarly effective. Preoperative steroid use showed consistent benefit, but the timing and route differences were not statistically significant. The findings suggest that corticosteroids may serve as an effective adjunct to reduce seroma formation in breast cancer surgery. Clinicians may consider incorporating steroids, particularly hydrocortisone, into perioperative protocols for patients at high risk for seroma, while balancing potential risks such as infection. These findings support corticosteroids as a useful adjunct in surgical care, and future studies should explore combining them with other methods, such as quilting, for enhanced outcomes. Future research should prioritize larger, methodologically rigorous RCTs with standardized outcome measures, longer follow-up periods, and detailed subgroup analyses to inform more robust and evidence-based clinical guidelines.

Optimized enhanced energy absorption in polymer nanocomposites reinforced with nano-clay, nano-silica, and diethylenetriamine
Authors: Hossein Taghipoor, Ahmad Ghiaskar
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn today’s world, where various industries face ever-increasing developments, improving people’s lives and preserving their property and lives are important issues in industrial production. One of the most unpleasant events in life is a collision while using the vehicle. Therefore, in recent decades, the high importance of this issue has attracted the attention of many researchers. The present study investigates the energy absorption of polymer nanocomposites with cylindrical and conical structures with different percentages of clay and silica nanoparticles to determine the various effects of nanomaterials and the effect of structure geometry on energy absorption under quasi-static tests. This experiment has been designed with the help of Design Expert software and the Box-Benken method. Also, nanomaterials between changes of 0-0.4% and diethylenetriamine material with weight% 1,3,5 have been used. From the results, it can be depicted that with the increase of the nanomaterial in this range, energy absorbed by the addition of nano silica up to the central point, i.e., 0.2%, an increase has been witnessed and after that, its intensity has decreased. Within this range, there is an observed significant rise of up to 0.4% for the nano clay material, while its trend keeps up with the previous intensity after the central point. However, in diethylenetriamine material, the highest energy absorption at the central point was 0.2%, after which a downward trend was observed. Also, in the investigation of the geometry of the structure, it was found that cone-shaped structures have higher energy absorption.Keywords: Energy absorption; Nanocomposite; Cylindrical tube, Quasi-static loading; Design of experimentSubject terms: Aerospace engineering, Mechanical engineering

Introduction

In the last decades, owing to technological development and advanced materials, the application of nanoparticles combined with different materials as one of the alternative methods for material designing and manufacture of parts have had a special place1–3. The application of nanomaterials as reinforcements in the construction of various parts of cars including body for improving the mechanical properties and safety performance has received much attention within the car industry. Energy absorption under impact conditions is one of the most major features considered in the design of today’s cars4,5. The energy absorption systems are systems that absorb and control energy due to an impact, preventing its spread to other parts of the structure, reducing side risks, and increasing car safety. Further, various recent studies have proved that the addition of nanoparticles into biodiesel fuels improves the performance and characteristics of the fuel, which may be taken as the complementary approach to developing better efficiencies of nanocomposite materials in various applications6. This improvement in fuel composition will prove that the positive effects of nanoparticles on efficiency are a reduction in emission, which will lead to their use in a wide range within the automotive industries.

One of the suitable methods for determining the resistance of objects against failure is the method of dynamic and quasi-static compression tests. According to the results of this test, it is possible to mention the mechanical characteristics, including the fracture characteristics and related parameters7. Failure has always been an important issue humans have faced since creating the most straightforward tools. And despite the significant progress in this matter, many unknowns remain. Understanding how materials behave during damage is a very important step to improving the conditions of this issue. Using nanoparticles as reinforcements in polymer structures has attracted much attention as an alternative approach to improving structures’ mechanical properties and performance8. Clay nanoparticles and Nano silica, as two common types of nanoparticles, have been investigated to improve the performance of structures due to their unique properties, including the ability of mechanical reinforcement, high energy absorption, and lightweight9. Recent investigations dealing with the enhancement of heat transport in nano-fluids employing CuO and ZnO nanoparticles provided yet another impetus to the application of nano-additives to material property improvement and energy efficiency in a variety of uses10.

These thermoplastic polymers, such as PP and PA, are used in a variety of automobile and aerospace applications due to their excellent mechanical properties, processability and low cost. However, because of the weak resistance to impact and limited energy absorption capacity, these polymers could not be used as structural materials11. So far, many types of nanometer reinforcements have been explored, such as carbon nanotubes and nano-ceramic plates, in order to solve this problem12,13. Such nanomaterials have very promising potential to enhance the mechanical properties, impact resistance, and energy absorption capacity of a variety of different polymers, making them more suitable for demanding applications in the automotive and aerospace sectors. Silica particles have turned out to be increasingly popular in polymer composite lightweight structures because of their excellent mechanical properties, high thermal stability, and the ability to enhance durability of polymer structures at acute stress conditions8. Silica nanoparticles have been researched with a successful interest in their potential enhancement of both structural and thermal resistance in polymers, that key feature every industry desires in transportation and electronics, where strength is combined with lightweight properties. The research that has involved clay and nano-silica nanoparticles as reinforcements in polymer structures is many, especially in composite structures. These nanoparticles have unique properties, including high impact resistance, high mechanical strength, and low density14,15. They are an integral component of strategies aimed at the creation of materials with the ability to support great mechanical stresses16. A combination of lightweight with high energy absorption means they are ideal candidates for applications in which reduction in weight is at a premium; however, performance compromise is not acceptable17. In addition, such nanometer reinforcements overcome these deficiencies in conventional thermoplastic polymers and create new perspectives for the design of advanced materials, which may meet high industrial standards concerning safety, efficiency, and durability.

For this reason, research has been conducted to improve the energy absorption properties of structures using clay and nano-silica nanoparticles with a polymer base. In one of the researches, Silva et al18. investigated the effect of filler materials on the energy absorption capability of polymer composites with a conical shape. The results showed that composites with nano silica have better energy absorption than other filler materials, and the failure mode is also related to parameters, such as initial peak and average force. Shariati et al19. also investigated the effect of adding nano-silica with different weight percentages and particle sizes to the epoxy matrix. They observed that as the weight% of silica increased, Young’s modulus increased, while the yield strength remained constant and the energy absorption decreased. Also, the ability to absorb energy is more significant in columns with a shorter length, and the particle size did not have much effect on the experiment. In another study, Rafiq et al20. investigated the impact strength of nano clay in epoxy/glass composite. They observed that the peak force and hardness increase by adding nano clay. Damon et al21. investigated the mechanical properties of epoxy with the addition of montmorillonite nanoclay and concluded that the addition of 3% by weight of nanoclay leads to an increase in hardness and a 30% decrease in tensile strength and a decrease in strain at 5% by weight. This decrease in tensile strength and strain was attributed to the poor dispersion of nano clay. Wang et al22. dissolved nano clay in epoxy using a solvent and observed that this additive linearly increased the modulus of elasticity and ultimate tensile strength.

In another study, Zappalorto et al23. investigated the mechanical properties of nano clay epoxy nanocomposite. They performed the tensile test of the samples and evaluated the elastic modulus and tensile strength in the initial state and after modification of the nanocomposite. The results showed that adding nano clay to the composites had a very small effect on Young’s modulus, only increasing it by about 1.5%. However, the tensile strength decreased by about 6%. Also, Laurenzi et al24. investigated the effect of using carbon nanotubes on the fracture mechanism of epoxy resin composite. They observed that carbon nanotubes can increase the fracture toughness, Young’s modulus, and performance of the polymer matrix through different mechanisms. The results showed that the failure mechanism may be caused by the nanostructure in the composite and affect the connection between carbon nanotubes and epoxy matrix.

In addition, other researchers have also provided theoretical and experimental methods to estimate the average axial load of thin-walled cans, and a good agreement between these relationships and experimental results has been obtained. Morthorst et al25. analyzed the effects of fiber type and cone angle in an experimental and numerical study on the axial crushing of conical composite shells. Through quasi-static testing, they examined the angle of the structure in the range of 5 to 25 degrees. Mahdi et al26. generally proved that increasing the angle of the cone’s apex leads to a change in the non-planar force-displacement diagram and shows a sudden drop in the force related to the angle of the apex of the cone. Also, they showed that the loading performance depends on the type of fibers in the composite. Kathiresan et al27,28. investigated the low-velocity axial impact collapse behavior of epoxy/glass fiber composite conical structures. Also, Lin et al29. researched foam-filled conical structures. Kathiresan et al30.. Also studied the crushing behavior and energy absorption of glass/epoxy composite cones and aluminum cone shells. Their results showed that the energy absorption ability of FLM cone shells is higher than that of hollow aluminum. In recent years, the problem of optimization of processes of energy absorption in structures of different types has been of vivid interest. New methods can increase the efficiency and precision of such procedures31.

The literature review shows that adding clay and nano-silica nanoparticles can significantly improve the mechanical properties of polymer structures, especially when faced with impact and stress forces. According to researchers, geometrically, using samples in cylindrical and conical shells is the best in energy absorption. This study is investigated under a quasi-static test to understand the fracture behavior and the amount of energy absorption of cylindrical and conical structures with a loading content of different percentages of nanomaterials. Considering the importance of using nanocomposite structures in various industries, including automotive and aerospace, optimizing the weight ratio of nanoparticles to increase the energy absorption properties can improve the performance and safety of these structures.

Materials and methods

Preparation of materials for making nanocomposite structures

In this research, ML-506 epoxy resin and epoxy hardener 11 based on bisphenol epoxy resin and polyamine hardener have been used as the main materials in the production of nanocomposites. This epoxy resin contains a type of aliphatic epoxy that increases the toughness and flexibility of the product. Also, due to the low viscosity of this product, it is possible to use it along with reinforcing fibers (glass, carbon, etc.) and mineral powders (silica, etc.). These good mechanical properties, duplicating, and machinability allow the use of this compound in modeling and duplicating industrial parts.

The epoxy resin used in this research has a density of 1.11 gr/cm3 and is combined with a ratio of 100 units of resin to 15 units of hardener. This composition has a compressive modulus of 9371 kg/cm2 according to the ASTMD695M standard and an impact strength of 7.850 kg/cm2 according to the ASTM256 standard. The composite samples were subjected to a pressure test according to the ASTME09M standard to extract parameters related to mechanical properties. In this research work, the materials nano silica and nano clay were utilized for preparing nanocomposite structures. Within this framework, 13 cylindrical and 5 conical samples have been tested, adding up to 18 samples by a molding method. Their dimensions include a length of 75 mm, a thickness of 2 mm, whereas in conical structures, the deviation angle from the apex is 5 degrees. These molds were cured after 12 h with white silicone glue. Later on, the addition of nanomaterials to the epoxy resin was carried out, mixing with an ultrasonic homogenizer for 15 min. For this process of ultrasonic homogenization, 20 kHz frequency was used to ensure adequate dispersion of nanoparticles in the epoxy resin. This step is significant for the breakdown of nanoparticle agglomerates and the improvement in the interaction between nanomaterials and the epoxy matrix1. After homogenization, the hardener was added to the mixture and mixed using a mechanical stirrer at 500 RPM for 10 min, by which time integration of the hardener within the resin was complete. The process of stirring was completed mechanically to ensure that phase separation does not take place, and curing is uniform. Quasi-static pressure test

The quasi-static pressure test was conducted to investigate the energy absorption properties of the constructed structures and the effect of the weight% of nanomaterials and their geometric structure. For this purpose, the samples were subjected to a pressure test using Centam model STM-400. In this device, the lower jaw is fixed and the upper jaw is mobile and can move from 0.001 mm/min to 250 mm/min.

In this test, the samples are subjected to pressure, and the energy absorption properties of the structures under quasi-static pressure are investigated. The effect of the weight% of nanomaterials and their geometric structure has also been evaluated in this test. With the ability to move the upper jaw at different speeds, the Centam device can perform the pressure test with high accuracy and control. It can be seen that the samples exhibit a variety of collapse mechanisms, each unique to the compounds used in their materials. Important parameters in energy absorption

Critical parameters such as initial peak force, average force, energy absorption capacity, collapse force efficiency, and absorbed energy per unit weight are used in quasi-static tests to evaluate energy absorbers. These parameters are very important in the design of energy absorption systems32,33. The amount of absorbed energy is actually the area under the force-displacement curve. The different stages observed in these tests include three main stages: pre-damage, progressive damage, and Densification. The area under the curve up to the point of full displacement from crushing gives the total absorbed energy.

The increase in energy absorbed in this range of materials is related to the increase in impact resistance, which, by adding nanomaterials, causes the closing mechanism and crack arc to occur. This mechanism has been observed in composites reinforced with nanometer-sized particles and micrometer-sized particles in a way that prevents crack growth through the particles39,40. In reinforced composites, when a crack is formed, and this crack hits the reinforcing material, instead of changing its direction, it bends and bounces between the particles. In this mechanism, the crack front is interrupted after hitting the particle, and they meet after passing through it. So it goes a long way. And because the crack’s energy is proportional to its length, it needs more energy to grow, so the structure becomes more resistant to impact. The difference in energy absorption of structures can be referred to as the crack growth mechanism, and the mechanism of growth and expansion of cracks in structures depends on the failure mode. Different failure modes have occurred in other samples, which can be recognized by viewing sample fracture images and microscopic photographs of the fracture surface41. In this case, which is shown in Fig. 12a, the energy is wasted through the delamination and breaking of the existing bonds, small and local cracks occur in the collapsing part, and small pieces of the structure it is separated from it at every stage. Additionally, due to the occurrence of a plastic hinge, the sample undergoes wrinkling, and the force-displacement graph generally exhibits more peaks and valleys. Therefore, energy absorption increases compared to the pure epoxy sample42. Another mode of failure, which is brittle and therefore more dangerous, also occurred in Fig. 12b, due to which large pieces have been observed in the collapse. This state is an unstable failure in that, during loading suddenly in the early stages, a crack is formed, large pieces are separated from the structure, and the strength and energy absorption in the structure is significantly reduced. Validation

In this section, we examine the results of the tests using Design Expert software to determine the test process and the relationship and influence of the parameters. At the beginning of the experiment, we determined the variables in this software, and after the experiment, we entered the mechanical properties of energy absorption obtained in the CCD section of the software.

In reviewing and analyzing the results of the software, according to Fig. 16, the placement of points on both sides of the drawn line with an angle coefficient of one shows the validity of the obtained results. Also, the high percentage of R² (0.972) indicates the appropriate accuracy of the model results compared to the experimental data. It should be noted that the R² parameter value is ideally equal to one, and in engineering applications, above 0.9 is considered acceptabl46,47. In this study, the R² value of 0.972 was obtained for the quadratic response surface model, which indicates a good match of the model information with the experimental results.

Validation of predicted values by the selected model compared to experimental values for energy absorption, with R² = 0.972.

Conclusion

In this study, the energy absorption of polymer nanocomposites with cylindrical and conical structures with different percentages of clay and silica nanoparticles has been investigated to determine the various effects of nanomaterials and the effect of structure geometry on energy absorption under quasi-static tests. The design of this experiment was done using Design Expert software and the Benken box method. The range of changes of nanomaterials was (0.0-0.4) percent, and diethylene triamine was also used with weight% (1, 3, 5).

The results show that with the increase of nanomaterial in this range, the energy absorbed by adding nano silica up to the central point, i.e., 0.2%, we have witnessed an increase in energy absorption, and after that, its intensity has decreased. In the case of nano clay material, a significant increase of up to 0.4% has been observed in this range, and its trend continues with the previous intensity after the central point. However, in diethylenetriamine material, the maximum energy absorption at the central point was 0.2%, after which a downward trend was observed. Also, in the investigation of the geometry of the structure, it was found that cone-shaped structures have higher energy absorption. As seen from the software analysis, adding nano-silica material up to the central point of 0.2% is an upward trend. After that, the intensity of the increase is reduced, and then there is a slight decrease in the intensity of energy absorption. However, in the nano clay material, from the starting point to 0.4%, the trend of the graph is still upward with the previous intensity, and by adding more and more of this material, energy absorption has increased in this range. Based on the three-dimensional diagram obtained, the effects of two substances, nano silica, and nano clay, on the absorbed energy were shown, and the amount of absorbed energy also increases with the increase in the number of nanoparticles in this range. The energy absorption trends showed that the addition of nano-silica at an optimum concentration of 2–4 wt% and nano-clay at an optimum between 3 and 5 wt% allowed nanocomposites to exhibit a balance between toughness and stiffness. At low concentration, energy absorption improved due to better dispersion, while at higher concentrations, there was a slight reduction in energy absorption caused by nanoparticle agglomeration.

Adding nanoparticles to resin epoxy enhances the latter’s mechanical properties, especially in energy dissipation, which is highly useful in automotive and aerospace applications. To this regard, nanocomposites may find use in impact-resistant car bumpers and crash absorbers in ground transportation and also in aerospace fuselage panels and landing gear components where energy absorption is of high importance. These findings are useful in the optimization of nanocomposites for safety-critical applications and provide a base for future research into improving mechanical performance and energy absorption.

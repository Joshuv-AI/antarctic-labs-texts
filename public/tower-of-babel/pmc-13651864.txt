Hydration channel engineering in Tröger’s base-POP membranes for mono/multivalent cation and anion discrimination
Authors: Haopan Sun, Ning Gan, Baolong Wu, Chenglin Liu, Yan Jin, Yuzhu Sun, Jianguo Yu, Zhaoliang Cui, Yuqing Lin, Hideto Matsuyama, Young Moo Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Sustainable recovery of critical resources from complex aqueous streams is constrained by membranes that fail to combine rapid ion transport with precise mono/multivalent discrimination. Here, we report Tröger’s base-derived porous organic polymer (POP) membranes with rigid, covalently crosslinked microporous frameworks that stabilize angstrom-scale transport channels while suppressing hydration-induced swelling. These membranes render rapid monovalent-ion transport with near-complete exclusion of multivalent cations and anions, including Mg2+, Al3+, SO42−, and PO43−. Experiments and simulations show that steric confinement, dehydration penalties, and electrostatic interactions cooperatively raise migration barriers for multivalent ions in confined channels. The membranes achieve comprehensive mono/multivalent discrimination across both cationic and anionic systems. In multistage electrodialysis, they enable stage-amplified lithium enrichment from high-Mg brines with stable long-term operation and downstream recovery of crystalline Li2CO3. They also facilitate high-purity NaCl enrichment from simulated seawater. Overall, this platform offers a durable and energy-efficient route to selective resource recovery from complex aqueous streams.

INTRODUCTION

The sustainable supply of critical elements underpins the global transition to low-carbon energy systems (1). This challenge is especially evident in high-salinity brines relevant to lithium recovery, as well as in seawater-derived and other chemically complex aqueous systems, where high concentrations of coexisting ions with different valences and hydration characteristics render selective transport intrinsically difficult (2). Membrane-based separations that combine high ionic throughput with precise ion discrimination are therefore central to advancing sustainable resource recovery and electrochemical separation processes, particularly in water-constrained and chemically complex environments (3). Ion migration through confined pore channels is governed by the interplay between pore free volume and ion-pore interactions, which determine the energetic barriers associated with ion partitioning, dehydration, and diffusion under confinement (4). Guided by these principles, substantial efforts have been devoted to engineering synthetic membranes with tailored ion-transport pathways (5, 6). However, achieving high conductivity while effectively excluding multivalent ions under realistic aqueous conditions remains a persistent materials challenge. In particular, the development of transport channels capable of efficient mono/multivalent ion discrimination across both cationic and anionic systems remains an unmet need for next-generation electrochemical separation membranes (7, 8).

Conventional ion exchange membranes, such as perfluorinated Nafion and hydrocarbon-based analogs, typically rely on microphase separation of hydrophilic ionic domains within a hydrophobic matrix to achieve mechanical integrity and dimensional stability (9, 10). While these architectures support efficient ion conduction, the resulting transport channels are typically broad and poorly defined. Upon hydration, their domains often swell to several nanometers, facilitating rapid ion diffusion but severely compromising selectivity (11, 12). As a result, most conventional membranes struggle to deliver simultaneous high conductivity and comprehensive mono/divalent discrimination, limiting their effectiveness in complex, multicomponent aqueous systems (13, 14). To overcome these limitations, porous materials such as porous organic polymers (POPs), metal-organic frameworks (MOFs), covalent organic frameworks (COFs), and porous organic cages have been explored as platforms for angstrom-scale confinement (15–17). Although angstrom-scale porous materials offer molecular precision, their limited processability, scalable integration, and operational robustness have constrained their sustainability-relevant deployment. Bridging the gap between molecular precision and process-level robustness, therefore, remains a key unsolved problem for sustainable membrane technologies.

Polymers of intrinsic microporosity (PIMs) offer a conceptually distinct strategy for mitigating the long-standing conductivity-selectivity trade-off (18, 19). Unlike phase-separated ion exchange membranes, PIMs have inherent angstrom-scale channels arising from the inefficient packing of rigid, contorted backbones, such as ladder-type Tröger’s base and spirocyclic motifs (20, 21). This architecture combines high free volume with well-defined constrictions, underpinning the widespread use of PIMs in gas separation membranes and, more recently, in proton-conducting separators for electrochemical devices requiring ultrafine pores and precise molecular discrimination (22–24). However, in aqueous ion separations, these narrowly confined channels often impose excessive transport resistance on hydrated monovalent ions, leading to large transport resistances, limited salt fluxes, and modest process efficiency in desalination and brine treatment (25, 26). Moreover, introducing the high density of fixed charges required for multivalent-ion exclusion often increases water uptake and swelling in linear or semirigid PIMs, dilating pore constrictions and progressively undermining mono- and divalent selectivity under realistic operating conditions (27, 28). Therefore, a viable PIM-derived membrane for stringent aqueous ion separations cannot rely on intrinsic microporosity alone; it must also preserve pore size stability under hydration, maintain continuous ion conduction pathways, and encode robust ion-pore interactions within a mechanically rigid framework (29). This need motivates the extension of the intrinsic microporosity concept to covalently crosslinked porous polymer networks with enhanced structural rigidity and programmable ionic microenvironments.

Here, we address a central sustainability bottleneck in electrochemical separations: the lack of membrane platforms that simultaneously deliver high ionic flux, sharp mono/divalent selectivity, and long-term stability under realistic aqueous conditions relevant to resource recovery. In particular, for lithium extraction from brines and NaCl separation from seawater, pressure-driven nanofiltration membranes are among the most widely studied and practically relevant technologies (30). However, recent assessments have emphasized that nanofiltration-based lithium extraction, while effective for Li+/Mg2+ separation, may face limitations in simultaneously reducing the Mg2+/Li+ ratio and achieving substantial Li+ enrichment under high-salinity conditions. By contrast, electrodialysis can, in principle, couple electrically driven ion separation with ion concentration, although its performance depends strongly on membrane permselectivity, current efficiency, and system configuration (31, 32). To this end, we report a new class of Tröger’s base (TB)–derived POP membranes that are fundamentally distinct from conventional ion-exchange membranes designed for strict cation-only or anion-only permselectivity. Instead of relying primarily on ion-exchange domains in a dense polymer matrix, these membranes integrate the continuous microporosity characteristic of PIMs with the structural rigidity and pore uniformity of covalently crosslinked networks, thereby creating a hydration channel–regulated platform for mono/multivalent ion discrimination across both cationic and anionic species.

These membranes are synthesized through acid-promoted copolymerization of twisted 3,3′-dimethylbiphenyl-4,4′-diamine (DMBP) units and triamine 1,3,5-tris(4-aminophenyl) benzene (TPB) units, yielding a three-dimensional microporous framework with tunable topologies (Fig. 1, A to C). The subsequent quaternization of the ladder-like Tröger’s base bridges generates well-interconnected angstrom-scale ion channels in which pore architecture and local chemistries are simultaneously engineered (Fig. 1, D to G). This molecular design imparts three key features: (i) a rigid, covalently crosslinked backbone that suppresses hydration-induced swelling (with a swelling ratio 1.52%) and maintains long-term dimensional stability; (ii) conformationally engineered channels that assemble into continuous hydration pathways, enabling water molecules to diffuse with effective mobilities (4.35 × 10−10 m2·s−1), and thereby boosting monovalent-ion conduction; and (iii) near-complete exclusion of comprehensive multivalent ions—including Mg2+, Al3+, SO42−, and PO43−—through the synergistic effects of steric sieving, Donnan exclusion, and electrostatic trapping (33, 34) (Fig. 1, D and G). As a result, exceptional selectivities are achieved, reaching 43 for K+/Mg2+ (304 for K+/Al3+) and 151 for Cl−/SO42− (833 for Cl−/PO43−) under multi-ion electrodialysis conditions. By translating molecular-scale pore and charge engineering into robust device-level performance, these membranes enable direct lithium enrichment from high-Mg brines and direct NaCl extraction from seawater. In a three-stage electrodialysis process, ∼500-fold lithium enrichment was achieved without conventional Mg/Ca preprecipitation, and the resulting Li-enriched stream was subsequently converted into crystalline Li2CO3 with 99.5% purity. Meanwhile, the membrane facilitated the direct extraction of NaCl from seawater with 99.4% purity. Overall, this work demonstrates how membrane molecular design can deliver system-level sustainability gains, including reduced energy demand, lower chemical inputs, and improved material durability in critical resource-recovery processes.

RESULTS

PIM chemistry provides access to a wide range of microporous materials by exploiting the structural diversity of rigid, contorted monomers, enabling the construction of tailored pore environments and POP-like crosslinked networks. In this work, TB-based POP membranes were synthesized through a solution-processable, acid-promoted condensation-cyclization reaction. The sterically hindered triamine 1,3,5-tris(4-aminophenyl) benzene (TPB) serves as a multifunctional crosslinking site, while the linear diamine DMBP controls the spacing between junctions, together acting as complementary building blocks for network formation (35). This molecular design produces permanent microporosity, with the framework topology systematically tunable through the triamine-diamine composition. By varying the DMBP/TPB monomer ratios, a series of network POP membranes—denoted as PIM-DT_X (X = 5, 10, 25, and 50) —was prepared, where X represents the molar fraction of TPB incorporated into the polymer backbone (fig. S2). To investigate the influence of ionic functionalities on ion separation, the tertiary amine bridges were subsequently quaternized via methylation using CH3I, resulting in quaternized membranes (Q_PIM-DT; fig. S3). Notably, both DMBP and TPB are low–molecular weight monomers (MW < 500 Da), with excellent solubility in common polar aprotic solvents such as dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO). This high solubility facilitates efficient solution-phase copolymerization into extended POP networks, enabling straightforward fabrication of defect-free, self-standing membranes by conventional solvent-casting techniques. Mechanical testing (fig. S4) demonstrates that the Q_PIM-DT membranes have robust mechanical properties, achieving a tensile strength of 40.8 MPa and an elongation at break of 14.6%. Thermogravimetric analysis (fig. S5) further confirms their thermal stability, with primary decomposition temperatures starting above 300°C, indicating suitability for electrochemical operation under demanding conditions.

The intrinsic free volume of the TB-based POP membranes plays a crucial role in defining pore channel formation and ion-selective transport. To characterize the microporous architecture, CO2 and N2 adsorption measurements were conducted (Fig. 1H and fig. S6). CO2 sorption isotherms reveal that uptake capacities of 8.61, 11.43, 10.52, and 7.18 cm3·g−1 STP for pristine DMBP, PIM-DT, Q_PIM-DT, and pristine TPB, respectively. Notably, both PIM-DT and Q_PIM-DT exhibit nearly identical pore size distributions centered in the range of 0.5 to 0.7 nm (Fig. 1I), which are larger than those of the pristine DMBP and TPB membranes, as derived from density functional theory (DFT) analysis of CO2 adsorption isotherms. The enhanced CO2 and N2 uptake observed in the copolymerized membranes reflects an increase in internal free volume, indicating that the PIM-DT copolymerization reorganizes the polymer framework into a more open and interconnected microporous network. These enlarged, uniformly distributed angstrom-scale voids provide a solid structural basis for creating continuous ion-transport channels essential for selective monovalent ion conduction.

The chemical structures of the polymers were confirmed using Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy (fig. S7) and x-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS; fig. S8) (36, 37). The ion-exchange capacity (IEC), which reflects the number of charged functional groups (quaternary ammonium groups) in the Q_PIM-DT membrane, was measured at 1.21 mmol·g−1—considerably higher than that of the unquaternized PIM-DT precursor (0.98 mmol·g−1). XPS analysis (Fig. 1J) further indicates that ∼13% of the accessible tertiary amine sites were successfully converted to quaternary ammonium groups during methylation. In addition, the IEC increases systematically with higher TPB incorporation (fig. S9), attributed to the higher density of amine functionalities introduced by the triamine nodes, in agreement with trends observed in the zeta potential measurements (fig. S10). Morphological studies using scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and atomic force microscopy (AFM) confirm that the membranes are dense, defect-free films (fig. S11) and have relatively smooth surface topographies (fig. S12). The membrane thickness ranges from 80 to 90 μm (fig. S13). Overall, these results demonstrate that solution-processable copolymerization, followed by controlled quaternization preserves the structural integrity of the TB-based POP membranes while introducing well-dispersed ionic clusters and a highly interconnected microporous architecture.

To elucidate the ion-screening mechanism and clarify the role of copolymerization in TB-based POP membranes, current-voltage (I-V) measurements were conducted using mixed electrolyte systems (NaCl/Na2SO4 and K2SO4/MgSO4), enabling direct comparison of ion transport for anion and cation species with different valences (figs. S15 and S16). The resulting I-V curves reveal a pronounced composition-dependent transport behavior for monovalent ions. Specifically, the effective conductivities of K+ and Cl− increase markedly with TPB incorporation, reaching maxima of 2.53 mS cm−1 (K+) and 3.85 mS cm−1 (Cl−) at Q_PIM-DT. In contrast, the conductivities of multivalent ions remain consistently low and largely insensitive to copolymer composition, stabilizing ∼0.83 mS cm−1 for Mg2+ and 0.95 mS cm−1 for SO42− across all membranes tested. Temperature-dependent I-V measurements further support this selective transport behavior (figs. S17 and S18). The activation energies for monovalent ion transport are minimized at intermediate TPB content, with values of 18.71 kJ mol−1 for K+ and 17.12 kJ mol−1 for Cl− at ∼10 mol % TPB incorporation. These values indicate that moderate copolymerization optimally tunes the effective pore dimensions to closely match the hydrated diameters of K+ and Cl− (6.62 and 6.64 Å, respectively) (38). In contrast, Mg2+ and SO42− exhibited persistently high activation energies regardless of TPB content, reflecting their restricted movement within subnanometer channels. Notably, quaternized Q_PIM-DT membranes exhibited nearly identical composition-dependent trends, differing only in absolute conductivity values, indicating that backbone topology—rather than cation functionalization—dominates size-selective ion transport.

To further correlate polymer topology with ion-transport behavior, molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were used to analyze the effect of ternary-site copolymerization on free volume characteristics. The fractional free volume (FFV) exhibits a clear nonmonotonic dependence on TPB content, reaching a maximum at PIM-DT (Fig. 2C). This behavior indicates that moderate incorporation of the triamine node disrupts dense chain packing and generates a higher density of interconnected micropores. In contrast, pristine PIM-D and PIM-T membranes exhibit more compact chain packing, resulting in smaller voids and reduced porosity (Fig. 2, A and B). Pore size distribution analysis further confirmed this conclusion: PIM-DT contains the highest fraction of angstrom-scale pores in the 6 ∼ 7 Å diameter range, corresponding to cavity regions with radii of ∼3 Å (fig. S19), consistent with its experimentally observed high conductivity and low activation energies (fig. S18). In comparison, pristine PIM-D and PIM-T mainly feature sub-5 Å pores, which impose substantial steric constraints even for hydrated monovalent ions. To further evaluate pore accessibility and connectivity, void percolation was measured using spherical probes with radii of 0.8, 0.9, 1.0, and 1.2 Å (Fig. 2, D to F). In both PIM-T and PIM-D (Fig. 2, D and E), only the smallest probe (0.8 Å) yields a minimally continuous void network, while connectivity deteriorates sharply as probe size increases. At a probe radius of 1.0 Å, voids remain present but no longer form a continuous percolated network across the simulation cell. In striking contrast, PIM-DT maintains interconnected void pathways even for the larger probes (Fig. 2F and fig. S20), demonstrating that moderate PIM-DT copolymerization significantly enhances three-dimensional pore connectivity. Together, the connectivity and pore size distribution results suggest a structural feature comprising relatively larger cavity domains (∼3 Å in radius) interconnected by much narrower bottlenecks on the order of ∼1 Å. Such a confined topology favors monovalent ion transport by enabling partial dehydration at the bottlenecks and rehydration within the larger cavity domains.

The visualization of the void networks further corroborates these findings (Fig. 2, G to I). When available voids are mapped according to the maximum probe diameter they can accommodate, PIM-DT exhibits larger and more continuous pore domains (Fig. 2I) than either pristine component (Fig. 2, G to H). The quaternized Q_PIM-DT displays nearly identical FFV values, pore size distributions, and connectivity profiles to its unquaternized PIM-DT counterpart (fig. S20), confirming that quaternization introduces localized ionic clusters without disrupting the intrinsic free-volume architecture. Collectively, these results demonstrate that ∼10 mol % TPB incorporation optimally balances chain rigidity and free-volume generation, yielding the highest porosity and pore interconnectivity while preserving a stable three-dimensional microporous network (figs. S21 and S22).

Small-angle x-ray scattering (SAXS) measurements reveal further insight into mesoscale structural organization and hydration behavior. A distinct primary scattering peak emerges upon quaternization, corresponding to a characteristic interdomain spacing (d) associated with ionic-rich hydrophilic regions. Using Bragg’s law (39), the d-spacing is determined to be 18.22 nm for PIM-DT and increases to 19.71 nm for Q_PIM-DT (Fig. 3F and fig. S23). This expansion reflects enhanced ionic clustering and a more pronounced microphase-separated structure in the quaternized membranes, in contrast to the relatively featureless SAXS profile of the unquaternized PIM-DT membrane (Fig. 3, A and B). Molecular simulations of hydrated Q_PIM-DT reveal that quaternary ammonium groups aggregate into localized ionic domains that serve as nucleation sites for water accumulation, leading to the formation of continuous water-rich regions throughout the polymer matrix (40). These regions are interconnected by dense hydrogen-bond networks, forming angstrom-scale internal hydration channels that facilitate efficient ion transport (figs. S24 and S25). By comparison, PIM-DT without quaternization exhibits only isolated water pockets with poor connectivity, preventing the establishment of continuous ion-transport channels (Fig. 3, D and E).

The impact of these structural and hydration features on water dynamics was quantified by diffusion-ordered spectroscopy (DOSY) 1H nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy (Fig. 3C). Pristine DMBP and TPB membranes exhibit relatively low water self-diffusion coefficients (<4.0 × 10−10 m2·s−1), consistent with compact chain packing and restricted free volume. In contrast, moderate copolymerization followed by quaternization leads to a pronounced increase in water mobility, with Q_PIM-DT exhibiting a self-diffusion coefficient of 4.35 × 10−10 m2·s−1 (Fig. 3H and fig. S26), the highest among the compositions examined in this study. This value is notable for an angstrom-scale microporous network and is comparable to those reported for representative state-of-the-art ion-exchange membranes (41, 42). Consistent with these findings, chloride conductivity measurements highlight the decisive role of quaternization in stabilizing hydrated ion-transport pathways (Fig. 3G). Although Q_PIM-DT and PIM-DT have similar overall pore structures, the quaternized membranes exhibit substantially higher Cl− conductivity, reaching 8 mS·cm−1 at 28 °C and 15 mS·cm−1 at 80 °C—corresponding to roughly a twofold enhancement over their nonquaternized analog.

Together, the SAXS, molecular simulation, NMR, and conductivity results establish a coherent structure-hydration-transport relationship, wherein optimized copolymer topology and ionic functionalization cooperatively generate continuous hydrogen-bonded hydration networks that enable rapid and selective monovalent-ion conduction through the polymer matrix.

Given the pronounced suppression of multivalent ion transport in the Q_PIM-DT_10 (hereafter referred to as Q_PIM-DT) membranes, its potential for selective ion separation was systematically evaluated. Ion transport behavior was first examined using I-V measurements with a range of electrolytes, including monovalent and multivalent cations (K+, Na+, Li+, Mg2+, and Al3+) and anions (Cl−, F−, IO3−, SO42−, and PO43−). The resulting I-V curves demonstrate that Q_PIM-DT supports efficient conduction of monovalent salts while strongly suppressing transport of divalent and trivalent ions. For chloride-based cation electrolytes, KCl exhibits the highest conductivity, with conductance following the order GK+ > GNa+ > GLi+ ≫ GMg2+ > GAl3+ (Fig. 4A). At a concentration of 0.5 M, the selectivity ratios reach 10.0 for K+/Mg2+ and 28.6 for K+/Al3+ (Fig. 4C). A comparable trend is observed for sodium-based anion electrolytes (Fig. 4B): Monovalent anions (Cl−, F−, IO3−) exhibit substantially higher conductivities than multivalent anions (SO42− and PO43−), with the disparity increasing with ionic charge and hydrated radius. At 0.5 M, the Cl−/SO42− and Cl−/PO43− reach 14.0 and 31.4, respectively, highlighting the membrane’s ability to block bulky, highly hydrated multivalent anions (Fig. 4C). Collectively, these results confirm that Q_PIM-DT forms highly charge-sensitive transport pathways that permit rapid transport of monovalent ions while imposing severe steric and electrostatic penalties on multivalent species.

To further quantify ion discrimination, transmembrane fluxes of salts were measured at 0.1 M (Fig. 4D). Monovalent ions such as K+ and F− exhibit the highest fluxes, whereas divalent ions (Mg2+ and SO42−) experience nearly one order of magnitude reduction, and trivalent ions (Al3+ and PO43−) are almost completely rejected. Consequently, selectivity ratios reach 24.1 for K+/Mg2+ and 51.6 for K+/Al3+. Similarly, Cl−/SO42− and Cl−/PO43− selectivities reach 54.6 and 160.5, respectively, confirming the effective exclusion of multivalent anions. In contrast, ions with identical valence but different sizes (e.g., K+/Na+ and Cl−/F−) display nearly identical fluxes (ratios ≈ 1), reflecting the membrane’s intrinsically high conductivity toward comprehensively monovalent ions.

The permselectivity of Q_PIM-DT was further systematically examined using multi-ion mixtures over a wide concentration range (0.1 to 2 M) at a current density of 5 mA·cm−2 (Fig. 4, E and F). Across all conditions, ion permeation correlates strongly with ionic valence.

1) Cations: Selectivity ratios such as K+/Mg2+ increase steadily with concentration, rising from ∼28 at 0.1 M to ∼43 at 2 M, while the rate of increase gradually diminishes at higher salinities. At low concentration, monovalent cations (K+, Na+) migrate efficiently through continuous hydration networks, while multivalent cations (Mg2+, Al3+) are strongly hindered by severe confinement-induced dehydration and stringent charge compensation requirements. As salinity increases, enhanced counterion screening partially migrates long-range electrostatic repulsion for multivalent cations; however, their conduction remains fundamentally limited by large dehydration barriers. Meanwhile, progressive saturation of the hydration channels, increased ion-ion interactions (i.e., ionic crowding/ionic pairing), and slower hydration-shell recovery under high-salinity conditions increasingly restrict monovalent-cation mobility. Consequently, monovalent/divalent selectivity continues to rise with concentration but approaches a plateau at high ionic strength. Notably, trivalent cations remain exclusively excluded across the entire concentration range, with K+/Al3+ selectivities exceeding 150.

2) Anions: In contrast to cations, anion selectivity ratios of Cl−/SO42− increased more rapidly with concentration, rising from ∼86 at 0.1 M to ∼151 at 2 M. Beyond steric constraints imposed by hydrated size, multivalent anions (SO42− and PO43−) exhibit strong electrostatic binding to the quaternary ammonium sites and show slow exchange dynamics within the confined, low-dielectric membrane matrix. With increasing salinity, preferential association of multivalent anions becomes more pronounced, leading to ionic trapping near fixed cationic sites—consistent with Manning-type condensation behavior—which further suppresses their mobility. By contrast, monovalent anions remain weakly associated and dynamically exchangeable, rendering their transport comparatively less sensitive to increased ionic strength. A similar concentration-accelerated enhancement in selectivity is observed for Cl−/PO43−.

This dual transport behavior confirms that quaternized TB-based POP membranes form conformationally engineered, charge-decorated channels that simultaneously allow rapid transport of monovalent ions and near-complete exclusion of multivalent ions under practical operating conditions. Although other PIM-DT variants, as well as pristine DMBP and pristine TPB (figs. S27 and S28) membranes, exhibit qualitatively similar concentration-dependent selectivity trends, the magnitude of selectivity is substantially enhanced in Q_PIM-DT. At a high salinity of 2 M, the best-performing Q_PIM-DT achieved high Cl−/SO42− and Na+/Mg2+ selectivities of 151 and 36, respectively, placing it among the top-performing ion-selective membranes reported to date for simultaneous mono/multivalent separation of both cations and anions (Fig. 4G). Notably, for Li+/Mg2+ separation, Q_PIM-DT exhibits competitive performance compared with previously reported PIM-based membranes and superior performance to several commercial CEMs (figs. S29 and S30 and table S3). However, its Li+/Mg2+ selectivity remains relatively modest compared with highly specialized lithium-ion sieving membranes specifically designed for Li+ recognition. This difference further indicates that Q_PIM-DT is primarily designed for hydration-channel-regulated mono/multivalent ion discrimination for both cationic and anionic systems.

To elucidate the origin of ion selectivity in Q_PIM-DT, a representative fragment of the crosslinked polymer network was extracted and treated as the active transport region for nonequilibrium MD (NE-MD) simulations under an external field. Consistent with experimental observations, ion permeation proceeds through three consecutive steps (Fig. 5A): (i) interfacial partitioning, partial dehydration, and confinement-driven pore entry; (ii) migration and diffusion through interconnected angstrom-scale hydration channels; and (iii) rehydration upon exiting the membrane.

To quantify the interfacial interactions governing the pore-entry step, NE-MD snapshots corresponding to ions crossing the water-membrane interface were extracted and analyzed using quantum chemical calculations (Fig. 5B). DFT calculations reveal a clear hierarchy in ion-polymer binding energies with the quaternized PIM-DT segment: ΔE_bind(Cl−) ≈ −15.40 kcal·mol−1, ΔE_bind(Li+) ≈ −12.49 kcal·mol−1, ΔE_bind(SO42−) ≈ −18.76 kcal·mol−1, ΔE_bind(Mg2+) ≈ +29.96 kcal·mol−1. Because the Q_PIM-DT framework carries fixed positive charges, Mg2+ binding is energetically unfavorable (ΔE_bind > 0), indicating that its high charge density cannot be effectively stabilized within the local coordination environment. In contrast, Li+ binding is energetically favorable (ΔE_bind < 0), demonstrating that tuning of charge distribution and pore geometry allows selective stabilization of Li+ while disfavoring Mg2+—a critical requirement for efficient Li+ extraction from Mg-rich brines.

Electrostatic potential maps further clarify the interfacial selectivity. As Cl− approaches the pore entrance, its local potential around the ion is largely neutralized (green), while adjacent regions of the polymer remain positively charged (red), facilitating electrostatic attraction and rapid entry. By contrast, SO42− retains a strongly negative potential (deep blue) and forms tightly bound, multipoint ion-pair coordination motifs with quaternary ammonium groups at the interface. This leads to pronounced electrostatic trapping of SO42− near the membrane surface, whereas Cl− remains dynamically exchangeable and readily permeable.

To characterize the complete transmembrane energy landscape, potentials of mean force (PMFs) were calculated along the permeate direction for Li+, Cl−, Mg2+, and SO42−, averaged over NE-MD trajectories (Fig. 5C). The resulting free-energy barriers for crossing the active layer are ΔG_Cl− = 10.38 kcal·mol−1, ΔG_Li+ = 14.81 kcal·mol−1, ΔG_SO42− = 30.26 kcal·mol−1, and ΔG_Mg2+ = 32.19 kcal·mol−1, showing a consistent order: ΔG_Mg2+ > ΔG_SO42− > ΔG_Li+ > ΔG_Cl−. Therefore, Mg2+ and SO42− encounter barriers approximately two to three times higher than those faced by Li+ and Cl−. Specifically, for Mg2+, strong Donnan repulsion from the cationic framework, combined with steric hindrance at angstrom-scale pore gates, dominates the barrier. For SO42−, its large, hydrated size and strong electrostatic association with quaternary ammonium centers significantly increase the migration barrier. In contrast, Li+ and Cl− experience substantially lower PMF maxima.

To elucidate the microscopic origin of these PMF barriers, the evolution of the ion solvation environment along the transport pathway was analyzed using ion-water radial distribution functions (RDFs) and the corresponding coordination (hydration) numbers (Fig. 5, D and E). The hydration numbers were extracted from the same externally driven ion-permeation trajectories used for the PMF analysis. Upon entering the angstrom-scale confined channels, all ions exhibit suppressed RDF peaks and reduced coordination numbers, indicating partial dehydration within the pore (Fig. 5F). Li+ and Cl− show the largest reductions in hydration number, with dehydration numbers of 12.09 and 23.05, respectively, upon transitioning from the bulk solution into the pore interior. In contrast, Mg2+ and SO42− display smaller decreases in hydration number (8.01 and 18.09, respectively). Because of their stronger solvation energies (43), Mg2+ and SO42− retain more coordinated water molecules inside the pore and therefore maintain a larger effective transport size at the angstrom-scale bottlenecks, resulting in substantially higher free-energy barriers. By contrast, Li+ and Cl− can reorganize their hydration shells more readily and adopt a smaller effective transport size, thereby enabling lower-barrier transport through the confined channels. Therefore, the dominant origin of mono/multivalent ion selectivity lies in the confinement-amplified difference in effective hydrated size, arising from their distinct dehydration behaviors. Consistent with this interpretation, the diffusivities derived from mean-squared displacement (MSD) analysis within the pore follow the order D_Cl− > D_Li+ > D_SO42− > D_Mg2+, confirming the strongly suppressed internal mobility of multivalent ions in angstrom-scale channels (Fig. 5G).

In summary, the exceptional ion selectivity of Q_PIM-DT arises from the synergistic interplay of four mechanisms (Fig. 5H), as suggested by the simulations and supported by the experimental conductivity, pore size distribution, and hydrophilicity results: (i) controlled partial dehydration that lowers energy barrier for monovalent ions at the pore entrance; (ii) strong steric confinement and Donnan exclusion that effectively block multivalent cations (such as Mg2+) from passing through the angstrom-scale gates; (iii) electrostatic trapping of multivalent anions (such as SO42−) at quaternary ammonium clusters, stabilizing interfacial bound states and suppressing forward migration; and (iv) continuous internal hydration channels that enable rapid conduction of comprehensive monovalent carriers. Together, these simulation results, combined with the experimental observations, suggest that charge-sensitive interactions and size-selective confinement collectively establish a distinct transport hierarchy in Q_PIM-DT, enabling fast monovalent-ion conduction while strongly suppressing multivalent-ion transport.

To evaluate the practical separation performance and operational durability of Q_PIM-DT membranes under realistic, high-Mg/Li conditions, a fixed-feed, three-stage electrodialysis process was conducted using a concentrated East Taijiner Salt Lake (initial Mg2+/Li+ ≈ 40.07), as schematically illustrated in Fig. 6A. The Mg2+/Li+ ratio in the permeate decreased monotonically with each electrodialysis stage, from 40.07 in the feed to 7.84 after stage I, 0.98 after stage II, and 0.08 after stage III (Fig. 6B and fig. S31). The current efficiencies of the monovalent ions (Li+, Na+, and K+) during the three-stage electrodialysis process were 68.4, 73.6, and 77.5%, respectively (fig. S32). These results further indicate that the observed Li enrichment is mainly due to efficient suppression of Mg2+ transport, rather than to targeted Li+ recognition. These values correspond to approximately 5-, 8-, and 12-fold decreases per stage, respectively, resulting in an overall reduction of ∼500-fold in the Mg2+/Li+ ratio across the three-stage process. This pronounced enrichment underscores the strong and sustained exclusion of divalent ion transport afforded by the Q_PIM-DT membrane.

These observed enrichment behaviors are fully consistent with the transport hierarchy established in earlier sections, including I-V characteristics (Fig. 4, A and B), activation energy analysis (fig. S18), and flux and concentration dependence (Fig. 4, E and F). Monovalent ions (K+/Na+/Li+/Cl−) readily migrate through quaternary ammonium-decorated hydration channels with low energetic barriers, whereas multivalent ions, particularly Mg2+, are strongly suppressed by large dehydration penalties and stringent charge compensation constraints imposed by angstrom-scale confinement in the TB-based POP framework. Mechanistically, stage I separation is dominated by Donnan exclusion and steric hindrance, resulting in pronounced Mg2+ depletion while maintaining high Li+ conductivity. In stages II and III, continued Mg2+ exclusion progressively reshapes interfacial ion partitioning. The resulting increase in local Li+ concentration and Li+/Mg2+ ratio lowers the effective free-energy barrier for Li+ entry into the nanochannels, thereby establishing Li+ as the dominant charge-compensating ion within the confined pore environment. Simultaneously, Cl− transport is increasingly constrained by local electroneutrality and fixed-charge effects, suppressing Cl− back diffusion and further increasing the effective Li+ transport number. This cascade-driven reinforcement of Li+-selective transport rationalizes the progressively enhanced enrichment factors observed across successive stages.

The long-term stability was further evaluated via continuous electrodialysis operation over 400 hours under constant current conditions. Throughout extended cycling, both Li+ flux and Mg2+/Li+ selectivity remained essentially unchanged, with Mg2+ leakage consistently below 1.8 g·liter−1 (Fig. 6C). The membrane resistance and voltage profiles showed negligible drift (<3%), indicating stable electrochemical performance. Post-operation SEM, FTIR, and XPS analyses revealed no evidence of delamination, pinhole formation, or chemical degradation (figs. S33 and S34), confirming the mechanical and chemical robustness of the membrane. This durability is attributed to the TPB node–reinforced, covalently crosslinked architecture, which stabilizes the microporous network against hydration-driven rearrangement under sustained electric fields and high ionic strength. Last, the Li-enriched permeate obtained after three-stage electrodialysis was concentrated by evaporation and subsequently treated with Na2CO3 to precipitate Li2CO3. At this stage, the Li+ and Mg2+ concentrations were 5.4 and 0.45 g liter−1, respectively (fig. S31), and the low Mg content does not interfere with the precipitation of Li2CO3. The resulting XRD pattern of the precipitate (Fig. 6D) exhibits sharp diffraction patterns consistent with the reference Li2CO3 phase, indicating its high crystallinity. Furthermore, the solid product was then redissolved and analyzed by inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectrometry (ICP-OES), confirming a purity of up to 99.5% (fig. S35). Overall, Li recovery from initial brine to final product was 76.8%. Notably, this purity meets the requirements for subsequent downstream recrystallization to achieve battery-grade lithium carbonate (44).

To demonstrate the proof-of-concept potential of the Q_PIM-DT membrane for comprehensive mono/multivalent ion discrimination, we evaluated its capability to enrich NaCl from simulated seawater (45). The Q_PIM-DT membrane exhibits exceptionally high permeability to NaCl while effectively blocking the transport of divalent ions. During an 8-hour electrically driven ion permeation process, the combined mass fraction of Na+ and Cl− in the system increased from 87.2% in the original simulated seawater to 99.4%. Meanwhile, the total mass fraction of Mg2+, Ca2+, and SO42− decreased markedly from 12.9 to 0.6% (fig. S36 and table S4).

Collectively, these results show that Q_PIM-DT supports stagewise lithium enrichment from realistic high-Mg/Li brines, maintains stable long-term electrodialysis performance, and enables downstream recovery of crystalline Li2CO3. Beyond lithium extraction, its ability to enrich NaCl from simulated seawater highlights a broader capability for comprehensive mono/multivalent ion discrimination, positioning Q_PIM-DT as a durable platform for resource-selective separations in complex brines.

DISCUSSION

We present Tröger’s base-derived POP membranes that integrate intrinsic microporosity with rigid, covalently crosslinked network architectures to enable selective and durable ion transport under realistic aqueous and electrochemical conditions. This design addresses a central bottleneck in sustainable ion-separation technologies, where high flux, sharp selectivity, and durability must be achieved simultaneously. The membranes suppress hydration-induced swelling, maintain long-term dimensional stability, and form continuous angstrom-scale hydration channels that render rapid transport of monovalent ions while almost completely excluding multivalent ions through combined steric, Donnan, and electrostatic effects. The resulting charge-sensitive energy landscape supports exceptional mono/divalent selectivity across both cations and anions. In representative electrochemical separation processes, the membranes achieve ∼500-fold lithium enrichment from high-Mg brines via a three-stage electrodialysis process without conventional Mg/Ca preprecipitation and also facilitate direct NaCl recovery from seawater. The resulting Li-enriched stream can be converted into crystalline Li2CO3 with 99.5% purity, and the extracted NaCl reaches 99.4% purity. More broadly, this work establishes a scalable, all-organic membrane platform in which molecular-level control of pore geometry and charge distribution yields reduced energy use, improved durability, and a lower environmental footprint for sustainable resource recovery.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Materials

TPB (95%), DMBP (98%), trifluoroacetic acid (TFA; 98%), dimethoxymethane (DMM; 98%), and methyl iodide (CH3I; 98%) were purchased from TCI (Shanghai) Development Co. Ltd. Inorganic salt potassium chloride (KCl; 99.8%), sodium chloride (NaCl; 99.8%), lithium chloride (LiCl; 99.8%), magnesium chloride hexahydrate (MgCl2·6H2O; 99.8%), aluminum chloride hexahydrate (AlCl3·6H2O; 99.8%), sulfate (Na2SO4; 99.8%), sodium fluoride (NaF; 99.8%), sodium iodate (NaIO3; 99.8%), sodium tungstate dihydrate (Na2WO4·2H2O; 99.8%), and sodium phosphate (Na3PO4; 99.8%) were acquired from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co. Ltd. and used without additional purification. Deionized water was used throughout this study. Nafion 117 membranes used in diffusion dialysis were obtained from Chemours Chemical (Shanghai) Co. Ltd. The ACS membranes used in selective electrodialysis were obtained from ASTOM Corporation (Japan).

Synthesis of PIM-DT

A sequential monomer-addition strategy was used, in which DMBP was first polymerized as the connecting unit, followed by the delayed introduction of TPB as crosslinking sites, enabling controlled evolution from linear segments to a branched network. Short-chain monomer DMBP (0.168 g) was reacted with DMM (0.305 g) for 24 hours, catalyzed by TFA (3.0 ml) in a round-bottomed flask at 0 °C. Subsequently, TPB (0.028 g), serving as a branching linker, was added to the reaction mixture, and the copolymerization proceeded for an additional 12 hours at room temperature to yield the branched copolymer.

Preparation of Q_PIM-DT membrane

Tröger’s base framework membranes were synthesized via a sol-gel method. The as-prepared viscous solution was poured onto a flat glass substrate and heat-treated at 30°C for 12 hours until completely dried. The dried membranes were immersed in an aqueous 0.1 M KOH solution to remove excess TFA. Branched polymer membranes were obtained by varying the dosage of TPB and DMBP, designated as PIM-DT_X, where “X” denotes the molar percentage ratio of TPB in the copolymer.

The resulting PIM-DT_X membranes were further immersed in a 40 weight % (wt %) methanol-methyl iodide mixture for 6 hours to initiate functional quaternization, with the degree of quaternization adjustable by varying the reaction time. Subsequently, the Q_PIM-DT_X membranes were immersed in a 1 M KCl solution for 24 hours to perform anion exchange and then washed with deionized water to remove residual salts.

Characterizations

Attenuated total reflection infrared-fourier transform infrared reflection characterization (Thermo Fisher Scientific Nicolet iS20) and XPS characterization (Thermo Scientific K-Alpha) were conducted to investigate the molecular structure and chemical composition of PIM-DT_X membranes. X-ray diffraction patterns were obtained using a Rigaku SmartLab SE diffractometer with Cu radiation. The sample was scanned over a 2θ range of 5° to 90° at a step size of 2°/min. The water contact angle was measured by an angle meter (SL200B, Solon Tech Co. Ltd, China). SEM characterization (ZEISS Gemini SEM 300) and AFM characterization (Bruker Scanasyst Dimension Icon AFM, using a peak force tapping mode in ambient air) were conducted to investigate the surface microstructure. Brunauer-Emmett-Teller measurements (ASAP 2640, Micromeritics 3Flex, USA) were conducted to investigate the surface area and pore distribution of PIM-DT_X membranes. Small/wide-angle x-ray scattering (SAXS/WAXS) data were recorded using a Xenocs Xeuss 2.0 instrument equipped with a Cu Kα x-ray source operated at 50 kV and 0.6 mA in the q range of 0 to 10 nm−1. DOSY 1H NMR experiments were performed at a Larmor frequency of 600 MHz using an NMR spectrometer (Bruker Avance NEO 600) to determine the self-diffusion coefficients of H2O within membrane samples immersed in CDCl3. The surface charging properties of the PIM-DT_X and Q_PIM-DT_X were examined using an electrokinetic analyzer (SurPass 3, Anton Paar, Australia). The concentrations of cations and SO42− were determined using ICP-OES (ARCOS, SPECTRO, Germany). The concentrations of anions (F−, IO3−, and PO43−) were determined using an ion chromatography system (Thermo Scientific Aquion, USA) equipped with a conductivity detector. Cl− concentrations were measured separately by Mohr’s method using AgNO3 as the titrant and K2CrO4 as the indicator.

Selective-ion transport

The selective transport of mono- and divalent ions across the Q_PIM-DT_X membrane was evaluated using I-V measurements in a two-compartment H-cell. The compartments were separated by the Q_PIM-DT_X membrane (surface area: 1.8 cm2) and filled with 30 ml of electrolyte solution (0.1 mol liter−1; NaCl, Na2SO4, KCl, or MgCl2, as specified). Before measurement, the Q_PIM-DT_X membranes were preimmersed in the corresponding electrolyte solution for 4 hours to achieve equilibrium. Salt-bridged Ag/AgCl electrodes were placed in each compartment and connected to an Autolab PGSTAT302N (Metrohm AG), which supplied a direct voltage that swept from −1 to 1 V at a scan rate of 0.01 V s−1. The resulting electrical signals were processed using NOVA2 (Metrohm AG) to plot I-V curves. Transmembrane conductivity was derived by fitting the slope of the I-V curve with linear least squares regression. Membrane ohmic resistance was calculated by subtracting the filmless resistance from the total ohmic resistance of the cell.

Electro-driven ion separation test

Electro-driven separation tests were conducted at an input voltage of −1 V using an H-type diffusion cell. The feed chamber was loaded with 80 ml of a 0.1 M simulated seawater solution, while the opposite chamber was filled with 80 ml of deionized water. To ensure a constant ionic strength throughout the testing, the feed solutions were circulated using a peristaltic pump.

Concentration gradient electrodialysis and multistage electrodialysis

Given the prevalence of ions such as K+, Na+, Mg2+, Cl−, and SO42− in natural water sources, we evaluated the ion separation capabilities of the Q_PIM-DT_10 membrane under binary-salt conditions using NaCl and Na2SO4, KCl, and MgCl2. We investigated the impact of varying salt concentrations on membrane separation performance. The mixed salt concentration gradients are 0.1, 0.5, 1, and 2 M, respectively.

To demonstrate the practical potential of the Q_PIM-DT_10 membrane for direct lithium extraction, we integrated our developed membranes into electrodialysis stacks and performed multistage electrodialysis. The feed was a simulated sulfate-type brine from East Taijiner Salt Lake with an Mg2+/Li+ ratio of 40, and the composition is shown in table S1. The concentrated compartment solution from the primary electrodialysis was transferred to the desalination compartment of the secondary electrodialysis after concentration. This step was repeated three times. The permeate was concentrated until the Li2CO3 content reached approximately 5 wt %, after which excess Na2CO3 solution (39.5 g liter−1) was added to induce Li2CO3 precipitation at 80 °C. Under these conditions, Na+, K+, and SO42− salts remain substantially more soluble than Li2CO3 and therefore largely stay in solution. For instance, at 80°C, the solubilities of Li2CO3, K2CO3, and Na2CO3 are 8.5, 139.8, and 45.8 g/100 g H2O, respectively, while sulfate salts are generally also highly soluble under these conditions (53). The precipitated product was then filtered, washed with deionized water, and dried in a vacuum oven at 50°C for 12 hours.

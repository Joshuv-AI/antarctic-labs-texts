Abstract
. We prove an asymptotic formula for the second moment of L L -functions associated to the Rankin–Selberg convolution of two holomorphic Hecke cusp forms with equal weight.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Results

One of the most outstanding open cases of the subconvexity problem for
L
-functions is that of the adjoint (or symmetric square)
L
-function
L(s,\ad f)
, where
f
is a holomorphic Hecke cusp form of even weight
k_{f}
for
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. At the central point, the convexity bound is
L(\frac{1}{2},\ad f)\ll k_{f}^{1/2}
. The goal for the subconvexity problem is to save any power of
k_{f}
over this bound, while the best bound currently known is Soundararajan’s so-called weak subconvex bound that saves almost a whole power of
\log k_{f}

[
Sou10
]
. A possible approach to this problem involves the second moment

(1.1)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{hol}}\\
k_{f}=k\end{subarray}}\frac{L\left(\frac{1}{2},\ad f\right)^{2}}{L(1,\ad f)}

where
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{hol}}
denotes an orthonormal basis of holomorphic Hecke cusp forms
f
of weight
k_{f}
. If an asymptotic formula (with a main term of size
k\log^{3}k
according to the random matrix theory recipe
[
CFKRS05
]
) can be obtained with an error term that saves a power of
k
over the main term, then one can likely insert an amplifier to obtain the desired subconvex bound.

Although the second moment (
1.1
) has the standard
\log
(conductor):
\log
(family size) ratio of
4
, since we sum over a family of about
\frac{k}{12}
forms
f
and
L(\frac{1}{2},\ad f)^{2}
has analytic conductor
k^{4}
, an asymptotic formula for this moment unfortunately remains elusive. One might hope to prove at the very least the upper bound
O_{\varepsilon}(k^{1+\varepsilon})
for this moment, which would recover the convexity bound, but even this is far from known (probably the upper bound
O_{\varepsilon}(k^{\frac{6}{5}+\varepsilon})
can be obtained following the same method used for
[
KY23
, Theorem 1.3]
). The situation is a bit better in the
level
aspect, where Iwaniec and Michel
[
IM01
]
have proven an upper bound that is sharp up to an
\varepsilon
-power of the level.

In this paper, we consider a different but related family. We investigate the second moment of Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions

(1.2)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{hol}}\\
k_{f}=k\end{subarray}}\frac{L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g\right)^{2}}{L(1,\ad f)},

where
g
is also a holomorphic Hecke cusp form with weight
k_{g}=k
. Here the Rankin–Selberg
L
-function is defined for
\Re(s)>1
by

(1.3)

L(s,f\otimes g)\coloneqq\zeta(2s)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)\lambda_{g}(n)}{n^{s}},

where
\lambda_{f}(n)
and
\lambda_{g}(n)
denote the Hecke eigenvalues of
f
and
g
, and then by meromorphic continuation to all of
\mathbb{C}
with at most a simple pole at
s=1
, which only occurs when
f=g
. The analytic conductor of
L(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g)
is of size
\max\{k_{f}^{2},k_{g}^{2}\}(|k_{f}-k_{g}|+1)^{2}
, which drops to
k^{2}
when
k_{f}=k_{g}=k
. As before, an asymptotic formula with a power-saving error term would be significant because it would likely facilitate subconvex bounds through amplification. These subconvex bounds would be for
L(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g)
, which would also include the value
L(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes f)=\zeta(\frac{1}{2})L(\frac{1}{2},\ad f).
Our main result
is
an asymptotic formula for (
1.2
), albeit not with a power-saving error term.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{hol}}
denote an orthonormal basis of holomorphic Hecke cusp forms
f
of positive even weight
k_{f}\in 2\mathbb{N}
. Let
g\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{hol}}
be a holomorphic Hecke cusp form of positive even weight
k\in 2\mathbb{N}
. For
j\in\{0,1,2,3\}
, there exist polynomials
P_{j}
of degree
j
such that

(1.5)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{hol}}\\
k_{f}=k\end{subarray}}\frac{L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g\right)^{2}}{L(1,\ad f)}=k\sum_{j=0}^{3}P_{j}(\log k)L^{(3-j)}(1,\ad g)+O_{\varepsilon}\left(k(\log k)^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right)

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Moreover, the main term
k\sum_{j=0}^{3}P_{j}(\log k)L^{(3-j)}(1,\ad g)
is positive for
k
sufficiently large and satisfies the bounds

(1.6)

k(\log k)^{2}\ll k\sum_{j=0}^{3}P_{j}(\log k)L^{(3-j)}(1,\ad g)\ll k(\log k)^{6}.

In contrast to the moment (
1.1
), the upper bound
O_{\varepsilon}(k^{1+\varepsilon})
for (
1.2
) is straightforward to prove using an approximate functional equation and a large sieve inequality
[
Lam14
]
. Given this favourable starting point coupled with the fact that the
\log
(conductor):
\log
(family size) ratio is
4
for this moment, one may expect to refine this upper bound to an asymptotic formula with relative ease. However, the usual heuristics can be misleading in conductor-dropping settings, as we saw for (
1.1
). In fact, our proof shows that an asymptotic for (
1.2
) with a power-saving error seems out of reach unless one already has available subconvex bounds for
L(\frac{1}{2},\ad g)
and its
\GL_{2}
twists. With this looming barrier, the best that we can hope for is a logarithmic savings in the error term. This is what we obtain via a period integral approach coupled with Soundararajan’s weak subconvex bounds.

On top of these issues, it is a delicate process to show that we actually have an asymptotic formula at hand. This is because the holomorphic Hecke cusp form
g
is not fixed and we have relatively poor knowledge on lower and upper bounds for
L(1,\ad g)
and its derivatives. We have (cf.
[
LW06
, Proposition 3.2]
,
[
GHL94
]
) that

(1.7)

L(1,\ad g)\gg\frac{1}{\log k},\quad L^{(j)}(1,\ad g)\ll(\log k)^{3+j}

for
j\in\{0,1,2,3\}
, which is insufficient to show that the main term is larger than the error term in (
1.5
). We manage to circumvent this difficultly by exploiting some hidden structure in the main term that is not apparent when it is worked out as in (
1.5
). Note that on the assumption of the generalised Riemann hypothesis, we would have bounds in (
1.7
) on the scale of powers of
\log\log k
rather than powers of
\log k
(cf.
[
LW06
, Theorem 3 (ii)]
), and so there would be no such difficulties.

Besides the relation to subconvexity problems, the second moment (
1.2
) is interesting in its own right. Upper bounds for such moments in conductor-dropping regimes, and in greater generality, have been considered by Blomer, who provided in
[
Blo12
, Theorem 1]
a different proof (without recourse to the large sieve) of the upper bound
O_{\varepsilon}(k^{1+\varepsilon})
. He showed that (
1.2
) is bounded from above by a constant multiple of

(1.8)

k\int_{\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}}\Im(z)^{k}|g(z)|^{2}\left|E^{\ast}\left(z,\frac{1}{2}\right)\right|^{2}\,d\mu(z),

where
d\mu(z)=y^{-2}\,dx\,dy
denotes the
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-invariant measure on the modular surface
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}
and
E^{\ast}(z,s)
denotes the completed real-analytic Eisenstein series (see (
2.14
) and (
2.15
) below). Blomer then bounded
E^{\ast}(z,\frac{1}{2})
from above by the real-analytic Eisenstein series
E(z,1+\varepsilon)
and finally used the Rankin–Selberg method to obtain the upper bound
O_{\varepsilon}(k^{1+\varepsilon})
.

We improve upon
[
Blo12
, Theorem 1]
by following a similar initial approach as Blomer and similarly arrive at the integral (
1.8
). At this point, our strategy deviates from Blomer’s strategy. We instead view this integral as the Petersson inner product of
\Im(z)^{k}|g(z)|^{2}
and
|E^{\ast}(z,\frac{1}{2})|^{2}
and apply Parseval’s identity. The ensuing spectral expansion gives rise to the main term in (
1.5
) together with a spectral sum of triple products of automorphic forms. We use the Watson–Ichino triple product formula to express the resulting triple products of automorphic forms in terms of
L
-functions, and finally we input pointwise bounds for these
L
-functions to show that this spectral sum gives rise to the error term in (
1.5
).

Finally, we comment on the hidden structure of the main term that was alluded to above. If we were working with approximate functional equations, then the diagonal contribution to the second moment would essentially be

(1.9)

k\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{g}(n)^{2}}{n}V_{k}(n)^{2},

where
\lambda_{g}(n)
denotes the
n
-th Hecke eigenvalue of
g
and
V_{k}(n)
is a weight function that arises in the approximate functional equation. Exploiting the positivity structure of this expression, we have that (
1.9
) is bounded from below by the
n=1
term

kV_{k}(1)^{2},

and we can easily compute that this is
\gg k\log^{2}k
. However, executing this idea is complicated by the fact that we do not actually work with approximate functional equations, and so we must first manipulate our main term into a form resembling (
1.9
). Moreover, there arises an additional off-diagonal main term that is a negative multiple of
kL(1,\ad g)
. The upper bound in (
1.7
) is insufficient to rule out the possibility that this off-diagonal term cancels out the diagonal term. To get around this, we come up with another trick in which we express
kL(1,\ad g)
as an integral that can be compared directly with (
1.9
) term by term.

1.2.
Generalisations

The methods in this paper can also be used to deduce the asymptotic behaviour of similar moments in either the level aspect or spectral aspect. For example, if we fix the weight
k
but now allow
f
to vary over an orthonormal basis
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{hol}}^{\ast}(q)
of holomorphic Hecke newforms of increasing prime level
q
, and if
g
is also a holomorphic Hecke newform of prime level
q
, then we may show that there exist polynomials
Q_{j}
of degree
j
, dependent on
k
, such that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{hol}}^{\ast}(q)\\
k_{f}=k\end{subarray}}\frac{L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g\right)^{2}}{L(1,\ad f)}=q\sum_{j=0}^{3}Q_{j}(\log q)L^{(3-j)}(1,\ad g)+O_{k,\varepsilon}\left(q(\log q)^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right),

where the main term is positive for
q
sufficiently large and satisfies the bounds

q(\log q)^{2}\ll_{k}q\sum_{j=0}^{3}Q_{j}(\log q)L^{(3-j)}(1,\ad g)\ll_{k}q(\log q)^{6}.

Alternatively, we may replace the average over holomorphic Hecke cusp forms
f\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{hol}}
of weight
k
with an average over Hecke–Maaß cusp forms
f\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
of spectral parameter
t_{f}\in[T,T+1]
, and let
g
be a Hecke–Maaß cusp form of spectral parameter
t_{g}\in[T,T+1]
. We may then show that there exist polynomials
R_{j}
of degree
j
such that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{B}_{0}\\
t_{f}\in[T,T+1]\end{subarray}}\frac{L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g\right)^{2}}{L(1,\ad f)}=T\sum_{j=0}^{3}R_{j}(\log T)L^{(3-j)}(1,\ad g)+O_{\varepsilon}\left(T(\log T)^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right),

where the main term is positive for
T
sufficiently large and satisfies the bounds

T(\log T)^{2}\ll T\sum_{j=0}^{3}R_{j}(\log T)L^{(3-j)}(1,\ad g)\ll T(\log T)^{6}.

Moreover, in all of the cases above, we may prove asymptotic formulæ for the second moment of Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions not necessarily at the central point but instead at the point
\frac{1}{2}+it
for some fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Finally, the methods in this paper may also be applied to the first moment of triple product
L
-functions at the central point

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{hol}}\\
k_{f}=k\end{subarray}}\frac{L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g\otimes h\right)}{L(1,\ad f)}.

Here
g
is again a holomorphic Hecke cusp form of weight
k
, while
h
is either a
fixed
holomorphic Hecke cusp form of
fixed
weight
k_{h}
or a
fixed
Hecke–Maaß cusp form of
fixed
spectral parameter
t_{h}
. However, while we would be able to extract the expected main term
c_{h}L(1,\ad g)k
for this moment, for some constant
c_{h}
depending on
h
, and obtain an error term that saves a fractional power of
\log k
, we would not be able to prove that the main term dominates the error term, as the best available lower bound
L(1,\ad g)\gg(\log k)^{-1}
is inadequate.

All of these generalisations have similar counterparts in the work of Blomer
[
Blo12
]
, who observes that one can prove upper bounds rather than asymptotic formulæ using his approach. Blomer also uses his approach to give upper bounds for the second moment of Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions in conductor-dropping regimes for automorphic forms on
\GL_{n}
with
n\geq 3
rather than just for automorphic forms on
\GL_{2}
. In this more general setting, however, our method is no longer applicable, since we rely upon the Watson–Ichino triple product formula to express integrals of three automorphic forms in terms of
L
-functions, and such a formula does not exist for automorphic forms on
\GL_{n}
with
n\geq 3
.

2.
An Exact Identity for Moments of
L
-Functions

The strategy towards proving
Theorem
1.4
is to first prove an exact identity for a moment of
L
-functions that involves the moment of interest (
1.2
). We subsequently extract this moment of interest and the main term
k\sum_{j=0}^{3}P_{j}(\log k)L^{(3-j)}(1,\ad g)
and then prove that all of the remaining terms contribute a smaller error term. The exact identity takes the following form.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{hol}}
denote an orthonormal basis of holomorphic Hecke cusp forms
f
of positive even weight
k_{f}\in 2\mathbb{N}
and let
\mathcal{B}_{0}
denotes an orthonormal basis of Hecke–Maaß cusp forms
f
of spectral parameter
t_{f}\in\mathbb{R}
and parity
\epsilon_{f}\in\{1,-1\}
. Let
g\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{hol}}
be a holomorphic Hecke cusp form of positive even weight
k\in 2\mathbb{N}
. We have the identity

(2.2)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{hol}}+\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Maass}}+\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Eis}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}+\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Maass}}+\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Eis}},

where

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{hol}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{f\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{hol}}}\frac{L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g\right)^{2}}{L(1,\ad f)}h^{\mathrm{hol}}(k_{f},k),

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Maass}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{f\in\mathcal{B}_{0}}\frac{L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g\right)^{2}}{L(1,\ad f)}h(t_{f},k),

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Eis}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{L\left(\frac{1}{2}+it,g\right)^{2}L\left(\frac{1}{2}-it,g\right)^{2}}{\zeta(1+2it)\zeta(1-2it)}h(t,k)\,dt,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2\frac{\Gamma(k)}{\Gamma\left(k-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}}\lim_{(w_{1},w_{2})\to(0,0)}\sum_{\pm_{1},\pm_{2}}(2\pi)^{-2w_{1}-2w_{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+w_{1}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+w_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Gamma(w_{1}+w_{2}+k)\frac{\zeta(1+2w_{1})\zeta(1+2w_{2})\zeta(1+w_{1}+w_{2})L(1+w_{1}+w_{2},\ad g)}{\zeta(2+2w_{1}+2w_{2})},

(2.7)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Maass}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{B}_{0}\\
\epsilon_{f}=1\end{subarray}}\theta_{f,g}\frac{\sqrt{L\left(\frac{1}{2},\ad g\otimes f\right)L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\right)^{5}}}{L(1,\ad f)}\widetilde{h}(t_{f},k),

(2.8)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Eis}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{L\left(\frac{1}{2}+it,\ad g\right)\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+it\right)^{3}\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}-it\right)^{2}}{\zeta(1+2it)\zeta(1-2it)}\widetilde{h}(t,k)\,dt.

Here
\theta_{f,g}
is a complex constant of absolute value
1
dependent only on
f
and
g
, while for
\ell\in 2\mathbb{N}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the spectral weights
h^{\mathrm{hol}}(\ell,k)
,
h(t,k)
, and
\widetilde{h}(t,k)
are given by

(2.9)

\displaystyle h^{\mathrm{hol}}(\ell,k)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\Gamma(k)}{\Gamma\left(k-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{k+\ell-1}{2}\right)^{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{k-\ell+1}{2}\right)^{2}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{k+\ell}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{k-\ell}{2}+1\right)}&if $\ell\leq k$,\\
0&if $\ell>k$,\end{cases}

(2.10)

\displaystyle h(t,k)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\Gamma(k)}{\Gamma\left(k-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{k}{2}+it\right)^{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{k}{2}-it\right)^{2}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{k+1}{2}+it\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{k+1}{2}-it\right)},

(2.11)

\displaystyle\widetilde{h}(t,k)

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2^{-1-2it}\pi^{-2-it}\frac{\Gamma(k)}{\Gamma\left(k-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}}\frac{\Gamma\left(k-\frac{1}{2}+it\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{it}{2}\right)^{2}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{it}{2}\right)^{2}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-it\right)}.

The proof of
Theorem
2.1
involves spectrally expanding in two different ways an integral of four automorphic forms. Given a holomorphic Hecke cusp form
g\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{hol}}
of positive even weight
k
, we let
G(z)\coloneqq\Im(z)^{k/2}g(z)
. We normalise
g
such that
\langle G,G\rangle=1
, where the weight
k
Petersson inner product is

\langle F,G\rangle\coloneqq\int_{\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}}F(z)\,\overline{G(z)}\,d\mu(z)

with the
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-invariant measure
d\mu(z)
given by
y^{-2}\,dx\,dy
, so that
\vol(\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H})=\pi/3
. The holomorphic Hecke cusp form
g
has the Fourier expansion

(2.12)

g(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rho_{g}(n)(4\pi n)^{\frac{k}{2}}e(nz),

where the Fourier coefficients
\rho_{g}(n)
satisfy

(2.13)

\rho_{g}(n)=\rho_{g}(1)\frac{\lambda_{g}(n)}{\sqrt{n}},\qquad|\rho_{g}(1)|^{2}=\frac{\pi}{2\Gamma(k)L(1,\ad g)}.

Here
\lambda_{g}(n)
denotes the
n
-th Hecke eigenvalue of
g
. The Hecke eigenvalues are multiplicative: for
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, they satisfy

\lambda_{g}(mn)=\sum_{d\mid(m,n)}\mu(d)\lambda_{g}\left(\frac{m}{d}\right)\lambda_{g}\left(\frac{n}{d}\right).

Moreover, since
g
has level
1
, and hence is self-dual, the Hecke eigenvalues
\lambda_{g}(n)
are real-valued, and we may normalise
g
such that
\rho_{g}(1)
is positive, so that the Fourier coefficients
\rho_{g}(n)
are also real-valued.

For
s\in\mathbb{C}
, we let
E(z,s)
denote the real analytic Eisenstein series, which is given for
\Re(s)>1
by

(2.14)

E(z,s)\coloneqq\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\Im(\gamma z)^{s},

where
\Gamma_{\infty}\coloneqq\{\pm\begin{pmatrix}1&n\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. The completed Eisenstein series

(2.15)

E^{\ast}(z,s)\coloneqq\pi^{-s}\Gamma(s)\zeta(s)E(z,s)

extends meromorphically to the entire complex plane with poles only at
s=1
and
s=0
.

For
\Re(s_{1}),\Re(s_{2})>1
, we consider the integral

(2.16)

\int_{\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}}|G(z)|^{2}E^{\ast}(z,s_{1})E^{\ast}(z,s_{2})\,d\mu(z).

This integral converges absolutely as
|G(z)|^{2}
is rapidly decreasing at the cusp of
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}
, whereas
E^{\ast}(z,s_{1})E^{\ast}(z,s_{2})
is of moderate growth.

On the one hand, the integral (
2.16
) is equal to the inner product
\langle GE^{\ast}(\cdot,s_{1}),G\overline{E^{\ast}(\cdot,s_{2})}\rangle
, where
GE^{\ast}(\cdot,s_{1})
and
G\overline{E^{\ast}(\cdot,s_{2})}
are rapidly decaying automorphic functions of weight
k
. We may therefore apply Parseval’s identity in order to spectrally expand this inner product in terms of automorphic forms of weight
k
. Using the Rankin–Selberg unfolding method, we then show in
Proposition
2.17
that, once appropriately normalised and holomorphically extended to
(s_{1},s_{2})=(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
, this inner product is equal to
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{hol}}+\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Maass}}+\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Eis}}
.

On the other hand, the integral (
2.16
) is equal to the inner product
\langle E^{\ast}(\cdot,s_{1})E^{\ast}(\cdot,s_{2}),|G|^{2}\rangle
, where
E^{\ast}(\cdot,s_{1})E^{\ast}(\cdot,s_{2})
is an automorphic function of moderate growth of weight
0
, while
|G|^{2}
is a rapidly decaying automorphic function of weight
0
. We may therefore apply a regularised version of Parseval’s identity for functions of moderate growth in order to spectrally expand this inner product in terms of automorphic forms of weight
0
. Using the Watson–Ichino triple product formula, we then show in
Proposition
2.34
that, once appropriately normalised and holomorphically extended to
(s_{1},s_{2})=(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
, this inner product is equal to
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}+\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Maass}}+\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Eis}}
. In this way, we deduce the identity (
2.2
).

2.1.
The First Spectral Expansion

We first consider the spectral expansion of the inner product
\langle GE^{\ast}(\cdot,s_{1}),G\overline{E^{\ast}(\cdot,s_{2})}\rangle
.

Proposition 2.17
.

Let
g
be a holomorphic Hecke cusp form of positive even weight
k
and let
G(z)\coloneqq\Im(z)^{k/2}g(z)
. The normalised inner product

(2.18)

\pi^{-3-s_{1}-s_{2}}\frac{\Gamma(k)^{2}}{\Gamma\left(k-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}}\Gamma(s_{1})\Gamma(s_{2})\zeta(2s_{1})\zeta(2s_{2})L(1,\ad g)\langle GE(\cdot,s_{1}),G\overline{E(\cdot,s_{2})}\rangle

is absolutely convergent for
\Re(s_{1}),\Re(s_{2})>1
and extends holomorphically to all of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. At
(s_{1},s_{2})=(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
, it is equal to

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{hol}}+\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Maass}}+\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Eis}},

with
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{hol}}
,
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Maass}}
, and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Eis}}
as in (
2.3
), (
2.4
), and (
2.5
) respectively.

The first step of the proof of
Proposition
2.17
is to apply Parseval’s identity for square-integrable automorphic forms of weight
k
. Following
[
BHMWW24
, Sections 3B and 4A]
, we recall the following classification of the automorphic forms of weight
k
that appear in this spectral expansion.

•

3.1.
The Size of the Weight Functions

We now prove bounds for the weight functions
h^{\mathrm{hol}}(\ell,k)
,
h(t,k)
, and
\widetilde{h}(t,k)
given by (
2.9
), (
2.10
), and (
2.11
). In doing so, we repeatedly make use of Stirling’s approximation, which states that for
s=\sigma+i\tau
with
\sigma\geq\delta>0
and
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,

(3.1)

\log|\Gamma(\sigma+i\tau)|=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sigma-\frac{1}{2}\right)\log(\sigma^{2}+\tau^{2})-|\tau|\arctan\frac{|\tau|}{\sigma}-\sigma+O_{\delta}(1).

Lemma 3.2
.

For a positive even integer
\ell<k
, we have that

h^{\mathrm{hol}}(\ell,k)\ll\frac{1}{k-\ell}\left(\frac{k-\ell}{2(k-1)}\right)^{\frac{k-\ell}{2}}.

Proof.

For a positive even integer
\ell\leq k
, we have that

h^{\mathrm{hol}}(\ell,k)=\frac{\Gamma(k)^{2}}{\Gamma\left(k-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{k+\ell-1}{2}\right)^{2}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{k+\ell}{2}\right)^{2}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{k-\ell+1}{2}\right)^{2}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{k-\ell}{2}+1\right)^{2}}\binom{k-1}{\frac{k-\ell}{2}}^{-1}.

We then observe by Stirling’s approximation (
3.1
) that

\frac{\Gamma(k)^{2}}{\Gamma\left(k-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}}\asymp k,\qquad\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{k+\ell-1}{2}\right)^{2}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{k+\ell}{2}\right)^{2}}\asymp\frac{1}{k},\qquad\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{k-\ell+1}{2}\right)^{2}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{k-\ell}{2}+1\right)^{2}}\asymp\frac{1}{k-\ell+1},

while for
\ell<k
, we have the inequality

\binom{k-1}{\frac{k-\ell}{2}}^{-1}\leq\left(\frac{k-\ell}{2(k-1)}\right)^{\frac{k-\ell}{2}}.\qed

Lemma 3.3
.

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have that

h(t,k)\ll\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2^{k}\sqrt{k}}&if $|t|\leq\sqrt{k}$,\\
\frac{e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{k}}}{2^{k}\sqrt{k}}&if $\sqrt{k}\leq|t|\leq k$,\\
\frac{k^{\frac{3}{2}}e^{-|t|}}{2^{k}|t|^{2}}&if $|t|\geq k$.\end{cases}

Proof.

By Stirling’s approximation (
3.1
),

\log|h(t,k)|=\left(k-\frac{1}{2}\right)\log k-2(k-1)\log\left(k-\frac{1}{2}\right)+(k-1)\log\left(\frac{k^{2}}{4}+t^{2}\right)\\
-\frac{k}{2}\log\left(\frac{(k+1)^{2}}{4}+t^{2}\right)-4|t|\arctan\frac{2|t|}{k}+2|t|\arctan\frac{2|t|}{k+1}+O(1).

Via the arctangent subtraction formula
\arctan x-\arctan y=\arctan\frac{x-y}{1+xy}
and the fact that
\arctan x=O(|x|)
and
\log(1+x)=O(1/|x|)
, we deduce that

\log|h(t,k)|=\log\frac{k^{\frac{3}{2}}}{k^{2}+4t^{2}}-k\log 2+\frac{k}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{4t^{2}}{k^{2}}\right)-2|t|\arctan\frac{2|t|}{k}+O(1).

The desired bound then follows from the inequalities

\frac{k}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{4t^{2}}{k^{2}}\right)-2|t|\arctan\frac{2|t|}{k}\leq\begin{cases}O(1)&if $|t|\leq\sqrt{k}$,\\
-\frac{t^{2}}{k}&if $\sqrt{k}\leq|t|\leq k$,\\
-|t|&if $|t|>k$.\end{cases}\qed

Lemma 3.4
.

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have that

\widetilde{h}(t,k)\ll\begin{cases}\frac{\sqrt{k}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|t|}}{1+|t|}&if $|t|\leq k$,\\
\frac{ke^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|t|}}{|t|^{\frac{3}{2}}}&if $|t|\geq k$.\end{cases}

Proof.

By Stirling’s approximation (
3.1
),

\log|\widetilde{h}(t,k)|=\left(k-\frac{1}{2}\right)\log k-2(k-1)\log\left(k-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}(k-1)\log\left(\left(k-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+t^{2}\right)\\
-|t|\arctan\frac{|t|}{k-\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{1}{4}+t^{2}\right)-|t|\arctan 2|t|+O(1),

which simplifies to

\log|\widetilde{h}(t,k)|=\log\frac{k}{(1+4t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\left(k-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+t^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}}-|t|\arctan 2|t|\\
+\frac{1}{2}\left(k-\frac{1}{2}\right)\log\left(1+\frac{t^{2}}{\left(k-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}}\right)-|t|\arctan\frac{|t|}{k-\frac{1}{2}}+O(1).

Since
\arctan 2|t|=\frac{\pi}{2}+O(1)
and

\frac{1}{2}\left(k-\frac{1}{2}\right)\log\left(1+\frac{t^{2}}{\left(k-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}}\right)-|t|\arctan 2|t|\leq\begin{cases}O(1)&if $|t|\leq\sqrt{k}$,\\
-\frac{t^{2}}{k}&if $\sqrt{k}\leq|t|\leq k$,\\
-|t|&if $|t|\geq k$,\end{cases}

the result follows.
∎

3.2.
The Size of Moments of
L
-Functions

We now use
Lemma
3.2
to extract the second moment in the conductor-dropping range

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{hol}}\\
k_{f}=k\end{subarray}}\frac{L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g\right)^{2}}{L(1,\ad f)}

from the moment
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{hol}}
given by (
2.3
).

Proposition 3.5
.

We have that

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{hol}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{hol}}\\
k_{f}=k\end{subarray}}\frac{L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g\right)^{2}}{L(1,\ad f)}+O_{\varepsilon}(k^{\varepsilon}).

Proof.

From
Lemma
3.2
and the fact that
h^{\mathrm{hol}}(k,k)=1
, we have that

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{hol}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{hol}}\\
k_{f}=k\end{subarray}}\frac{L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g\right)^{2}}{L(1,\ad f)}\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell=2\\
\ell\equiv 0\hskip-7.11317pt\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}^{k-2}\frac{1}{k-\ell}\left(\frac{k-\ell}{2(k-1)}\right)^{\frac{k-\ell}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{hol}}\\
k_{f}=\ell\end{subarray}}\frac{L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g\right)^{2}}{L(1,\ad f)}.

Since the analytic conductor of
L(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g)
for
f\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{hol}}
with
k_{f}\leq k
is
\asymp k^{2}(1+k-k_{f})^{2}
, the spectral large sieve implies that for
\ell<k
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{hol}}\\
k_{f}=\ell\end{subarray}}\frac{L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g\right)^{2}}{L(1,\ad f)}\ll_{\varepsilon}k^{1+\varepsilon}(k-\ell).

It remains to note that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell=2\\
\ell\equiv 0\hskip-7.11317pt\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}^{k-2}\left(\frac{k-\ell}{2(k-1)}\right)^{\frac{k-\ell}{2}}\ll\frac{1}{k}.\qed

We then use
Lemma
3.3
to bound the moments
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Maass}}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Eis}}
given by (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) respectively.

Proposition 3.6
.

We have that

\begin{cases}\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Maass}}\\
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Eis}}\end{cases}\ll_{\varepsilon}k^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}2^{-k}.

Proof.

By
Lemma
3.3
, we have that

(3.7)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Maass}}\ll\frac{1}{2^{k}\sqrt{k}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{B}_{0}\\
t_{f}\leq\sqrt{k}\end{subarray}}\frac{L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g\right)^{2}}{L(1,\ad f)}+\frac{1}{2^{k}\sqrt{k}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{B}_{0}\\
\sqrt{k}\leq t_{f}\leq k\end{subarray}}\frac{L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g\right)^{2}}{L(1,\ad f)}e^{-\frac{t_{f}^{2}}{k}}\\
+\frac{k^{\frac{3}{2}}}{2^{k}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{B}_{0}\\
\sqrt{k}\leq t_{f}\leq k\end{subarray}}\frac{L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g\right)^{2}}{L(1,\ad f)}\frac{e^{-t_{f}}}{t_{f}^{2}}.

Since the analytic conductor of
L(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g)
for
f\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
is
\asymp k^{4}
if
t_{f}\leq k
and is
\asymp t_{f}^{4}
if
t_{f}\geq k
, the spectral large sieve implies that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{B}_{0}\\
T\leq t_{f}\leq 2T\end{subarray}}\frac{L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\otimes g\right)^{2}}{L(1,\ad f)}\ll_{\varepsilon}\begin{cases}k^{2+\varepsilon}&if $T\leq k$,\\
T^{2+\varepsilon}&if $T\geq k$.\end{cases}

By breaking up the sums over
f\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
in (
3.7
) into dyadic intervals, we deduce the desired bounds for
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Maass}}
. An analogous argument yields the desired bounds for
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Eis}}
.
∎

Finally, we use
Lemma
3.4
to bound the moments
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Maass}}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Eis}}
given by (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) respectively.

Proposition 3.8
.

We have that

\begin{cases}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Maass}}\\
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Eis}}\end{cases}\ll_{\varepsilon}k(\log k)^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

Proof.

From
Lemma
3.4
, we have that

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Maass}}\ll\sqrt{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{B}_{0}\\
t_{f}\leq k\end{subarray}}\frac{\sqrt{L\left(\frac{1}{2},\ad g\otimes f\right)L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\right)^{5}}}{L(1,\ad f)}\frac{e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}t_{f}}}{1+t_{f}}+k\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{B}_{0}\\
t_{f}\geq k\end{subarray}}\frac{\sqrt{L\left(\frac{1}{2},\ad g\otimes f\right)L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\right)^{5}}}{L(1,\ad f)}\frac{e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}t_{f}}}{t_{f}^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

We break up the sums over
f\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
above into intervals of length one. Since for
f\in\mathcal{B}_{0}
, the analytic conductor of
L(\frac{1}{2},\ad g\otimes f)
is
(k+t_{f})^{4}(1+t_{f})^{2}
, we may bound
L(\frac{1}{2},\ad g\otimes f)
pointwise via the weak subconvexity bound
[
Watt24
, Theorem 1.3]

L\left(\frac{1}{2},\ad g\otimes f\right)\ll_{\varepsilon}\begin{cases}k(\log k)^{-1+\varepsilon}(1+t_{f})^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}&if $t_{f}\leq k$,\\
t_{f}^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}&if $t_{f}\geq k$.\end{cases}

We then bound the remaining fractional moment of
L
-functions by applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and invoking the second- and third-moment bounds

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{B}_{0}\\
T-1\leq t_{f}\leq T\end{subarray}}\frac{L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\right)^{2}}{L(1,\ad f)}

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}T^{1+\varepsilon},

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{B}_{0}\\
T-1\leq t_{f}\leq T\end{subarray}}\frac{L\left(\frac{1}{2},f\right)^{3}}{L(1,\ad f)}

\displaystyle\ll_{\varepsilon}T^{1+\varepsilon}

for
T\geq 1
; here the former is a consequence of the spectral large sieve, while the latter is a result of Ivić
[
Ivi01
, Theorem]
. In this way, we obtain the desired bound for
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Maass}}
. An analogous argument yields the desired bounds for
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Eis}}
.
∎

4.
Bounds for the Main Term

The remaining step required to complete the proof of
Theorem
1.4
is to determine the size of the main term
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
given by (
2.6
).

Proposition 4.1
.

(1)

For
j\in\{0,1,2,3\}
, there exist polynomials
P_{j}
of degree
j
such that

(4.2)

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}=k\sum_{j=0}^{3}P_{j}(\log k)L^{(3-j)}(1,\ad g)+O(\sqrt{k}).

(2)

We have the upper bound

(4.3)

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}\ll k(\log k)^{6}.

(3)

The main term
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
is positive for
k
sufficiently large and satisfies the lower bound

(4.4)

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}\gg k(\log k)^{2}.

Remark 4.5
.

Conditionally on the Riemann hypothesis for
L(s,\ad g)
, the dominating term in (
4.2
) is
k(\log k)^{3}L(1,\ad g)
. Inserting in this term the unconditional lower bound

L(1,\ad g)\gg\frac{1}{\log k}

due to Goldfeld, Hoffstein, and Lieman
[
GHL94
]
gives (
4.4
). Thus (
4.4
) is the best lower bound one can realistically hope to achieve unconditionally for
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
.

The proof of
Proposition
4.1
requires various bounds for
L(s,\ad g)
and its derivatives.

Lemma 4.6
.

For
|t|\ll k
and
j\geq 0
, we have that

\displaystyle L^{(j)}(1,\ad g)

\displaystyle\ll_{j}(\log k)^{3+j},

\displaystyle L^{(j)}\left(\frac{1}{2}+it,\ad g\right)

\displaystyle\ll_{j,\varepsilon}\sqrt{k}(\log k)^{j-1+\varepsilon}(1+|t|)^{\frac{3}{4}}.

Proof.

The first upper bound follows by writing
L^{(j)}(1,\ad g)
as an essentially finite Dirichlet series, and then bounding the sum absolutely (see
[
LW06
, Proposition 3.2 (i)]
). The bound on
L^{(j)}(\frac{1}{2}+it,\ad g)
is an instance of Soundararajan’s weak subconvexity. Writing
L(\frac{1}{2}+it,\ad g)
, which has analytic conductor
(k+|t|)^{2}(1+|t|)
, in terms of an approximate functional equation, differentiating this
j
times, and then applying
[
Sou10
, Theorem 2]
together with partial summation (as in
[
Sou10
, Page 1486]
) gives the claim.
∎

Our starting point for
Proposition
4.1
is the identity
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}=\lim_{(w_{1},w_{2})\to(0,0)}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}(w_{1},w_{2})
, where
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}(w_{1},w_{2})
is the function given by (
2.48
) in terms of the related function
\Psi(w_{1},w_{2})
given by (
2.49
).

Lemma 4.7
.

We have that

(4.8)

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}=4\Res_{w_{2}=0}\left(\Res_{w_{1}=0}\frac{\Psi(w_{1},w_{2})}{w_{1}w_{2}}\right).

Proof.

Let
\Re(w_{2})>0
. Taking the Laurent expansion of
\Psi(w_{1},w_{2})
about
w_{1}=0
, where there is a simple pole, we can write

\Psi(\pm_{1}w_{1},\pm_{2}w_{2})=\frac{a_{-1}(\pm_{2}w_{2})}{\pm_{1}w_{1}}+a_{0}(\pm_{2}w_{2})\pm_{1}w_{1}a_{1}(\pm_{2}w_{2})+\ldots,

where
a_{j}
are meromorphic functions for
j\geq-1
. From the proof of
Proposition
2.34
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}(w_{1},w_{2})
is holomorphic at
(0,w_{2})
for
-\frac{1}{2}<\Re(w_{2})<\frac{1}{2}
, and so we must have that

\sum_{\pm_{1},\pm_{2}}\frac{a_{-1}(\pm_{2}w_{2})}{\pm_{1}w_{1}}=0

as a function of
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
. Thus, inserting the value
w_{1}=0
into the other terms, we have that

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}(0,w_{2})=\sum_{\pm_{1},\pm_{2}}a_{0}(\pm_{2}w_{2})=2\sum_{\pm_{2}}a_{0}(\pm_{2}w_{2}).

We can next take the Laurent expansion of
a_{0}(w_{2})
about
w_{2}=0
, where there is a triple pole, argue as before to insert the value
w_{2}=0
, and obtain

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}(0,w_{2})=2\sum_{\pm_{2}}\left(\frac{b_{-3}}{(\pm_{2}w_{2})^{3}}+\frac{b_{-2}}{(\pm_{2}w_{2})^{2}}+\frac{b_{-1}}{\pm_{1}w_{2}}+b_{0}\pm b_{1}w_{2}+\ldots\right)=2\sum_{\pm_{2}}b_{0}=4b_{0}.

The constant term in the Laurent expansion about
z=0
of a meromorphic function
f(z)
can be picked out as the residue at
z=0
of
\frac{f(z)}{z}
by Cauchy’s residue theorem. With this fact and since
b_{0}
is the constant term in the Laurent expansion in
w_{2}
of the constant term of the Laurent expansion in
w_{1}
of
\Psi(w_{1},w_{2})
, we deduce the lemma.
∎

Next, we slightly modify the form of
\Psi(w_{1},w_{2})
in a way that does not affect our asymptotic evaluation of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
. Defining

(4.9)

\psi(w_{1},w_{2})\coloneqq\frac{\zeta(1+w_{1}+w_{2})L(1+w_{1}+w_{2},\ad g)}{\zeta(2+2w_{1}+2w_{2})}\prod_{j=1}^{2}\left(\frac{k}{4\pi^{2}}\right)^{w_{j}}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+w_{j}\right)\zeta(1+2w_{j})(1-64w_{j}^{6}),

we have the following.

Corollary 4.10
.

We have that

(4.11)

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}=8k\Res_{w_{2}=0}\left(\Res_{w_{1}=0}\frac{\psi(w_{1},w_{2})}{w_{1}w_{2}}\right)+O(\sqrt{k}).

Note that by having multiplied
\Psi(w_{1},w_{2})
by
(1-64w_{1}^{6})(1-64w_{2}^{6})
, we have cancelled the poles of
\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+w_{1})\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+w_{2})
at
w_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}
and
w_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}
, which shall be convenient later.

Proof.

Multiplying
\Psi(w_{1},w_{2})
by
(1-64w_{1}^{6})(1-64w_{2}^{6})
does not alter the residue calculation in (
4.8
) because the extra factor
(1-64w_{1}^{6})(1-64w_{2}^{6})
is equal to
1
at
(w_{1},w_{2})=(0,0)
and all of its partial derivatives up to the fifth order vanish at
(w_{1},w_{2})=(0,0)
. Next, by applying Stirling’s approximation (
3.1
), we have that

(4.12)

\frac{\Gamma(k)^{2}}{\Gamma\left(k-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}}\frac{\Gamma(w_{1}+w_{2}+k)}{\Gamma(k)}=k^{1+w_{1}+w_{2}}+O\left(k^{\Re(w_{1})+\Re(w_{2})}\right)

for
|w_{1}|,|w_{2}|<1
. To see that the error term in (
4.12
) contributes
O(\sqrt{k})
to the residue calculation, as seen in (
4.11
), one can pick out the residues using Cauchy’s integral theorem, integrating over small circles about
w_{1}=0
and
w_{2}=0
, and bounding the contribution of the error term in (
4.12
) around these circles.
∎

Proof of

To establish

Lemma 4.14
.

Let

(4.15)

c\coloneqq\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\frac{1}{4}-i\infty}^{\frac{1}{4}+i\infty}\frac{(1-64w^{2})^{2}}{w^{2}}\prod_{\pm}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\pm w\right)\zeta(1\pm 2w)\,dw.

We have that

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{0}=8k\left(\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{4}-i\infty}^{\frac{1}{4}+i\infty}\int_{\frac{1}{4}-i\infty}^{\frac{1}{4}+i\infty}\frac{\psi(w_{1},w_{2})}{w_{1}w_{2}}\,dw_{1}dw_{2}+c\frac{L(1,\ad g)}{\zeta(2)}\right)+O_{\varepsilon}(k(\log k)^{\varepsilon}).

Proof.

We consider the double integral

\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{4}-i\infty}^{\frac{1}{4}+i\infty}\int_{\frac{1}{4}-i\infty}^{\frac{1}{4}+i\infty}\psi(w_{1},w_{2})\,\frac{dw_{1}\,dw_{2}}{w_{1}w_{2}}.

(4.16)

\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{4}-i\infty}^{\frac{1}{4}+i\infty}\int_{-\frac{3}{4}-i\infty}^{-\frac{3}{4}+i\infty}\frac{\psi(w_{1},w_{2})}{w_{1}w_{2}}\,dw_{1}\,dw_{2}+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\frac{1}{4}-i\infty}^{\frac{1}{4}+i\infty}\left(\Res_{w_{1}=0}\frac{\psi(w_{1},w_{2})}{w_{1}w_{2}}\right)\,dw_{2}-c\frac{L(1,\ad g)}{\zeta(2)},

Proposition
4.1
(3)
then follows by establishing the following.

Lemma 4.17
.

We have that

(4.18)

c\frac{L(1,\ad g)}{\zeta(2)}+\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{4}-i\infty}^{\frac{1}{4}+i\infty}\int_{\frac{1}{4}-i\infty}^{\frac{1}{4}+i\infty}\frac{\psi(w_{1},w_{2})}{w_{1}w_{2}}\,dw_{1}\,dw_{2}\gg(\log k)^{2}.

Proof.

We consider the integral

(4.19)

\frac{c}{2\pi i}\int_{\frac{1}{4}-i\infty}^{\frac{1}{4}+i\infty}\frac{\zeta(1+w)L(1+w,\ad g)}{\zeta(2+2w)}\left(\frac{k}{(\log k)^{2}}\right)^{w}e^{w^{2}}\,dw.

The factor
e^{w^{2}}
ensures absolute convergence of the integral.

On the one hand, we may express the ratio of
L
-functions as an absolutely convergent Dirichlet series, namely

\frac{\zeta(1+w)L(1+w,\ad g)}{\zeta(2+2w)}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{g}(n)^{2}}{n^{1+w}},

and exchange the order of summation and integration in order to write the integral (
4.19

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{g}(n)^{2}}{n}W_{1}(n),

where

(4.20)

W_{1}(n)\coloneqq\frac{c}{2\pi i}\int_{\frac{1}{4}-i\infty}^{\frac{1}{4}+i\infty}\left(\frac{k}{n(\log k)^{2}}\right)^{w}e^{w^{2}}\,dw.

On the other hand, we may move the integral left to the line
\Re(w)=-\frac{1}{2}
, crossing a simple pole at
w=0
. By Cauchy’s residue theorem, we find that the integral (
4.19

c\frac{L(1,\ad g)}{\zeta(2)}+\frac{c}{2\pi i}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}-i\infty}^{-\frac{1}{2}+i\infty}\frac{\zeta(1+w)L(1+w,\ad g)}{\zeta(2+2w)}\left(\frac{k}{(\log k)^{2}}\right)^{w}e^{w^{2}}\,dw.

The second term above is

using

4.6
. Combining these two expressions for the integral (

c\frac{L(1,\ad g)}{\zeta(2)}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{g}(n)^{2}}{n}W_{1}(n)+O_{\varepsilon}((\log k)^{\varepsilon}).

Similarly, we express the ratio of
L
-functions appearing in the definition (

\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{4}-i\infty}^{\frac{1}{4}+i\infty}\int_{\frac{1}{4}-i\infty}^{\frac{1}{4}+i\infty}\frac{\psi(w_{1},w_{2})}{w_{1}w_{2}}\,dw_{1}\,dw_{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{g}(n)^{2}}{n}W_{2}(n),

where

(4.21)

W_{2}(n)\coloneqq\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\frac{1}{4}-i\infty}^{\frac{1}{4}+i\infty}\left(\frac{k}{4\pi^{2}n}\right)^{w}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+w\right)\zeta(1+2w)(1-64w^{6})\,\frac{dw}{w}\right)^{2}.

It follows that the left-hand side of (

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{g}(n)^{2}}{n}(W_{1}(n)+W_{2}(n))+O_{\varepsilon}((\log k)^{\varepsilon}).

It therefore suffices to show that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{g}(n)^{2}}{n}(W_{1}(n)+W_{2}(n))\gg(\log k)^{2}.

We break the sum into four parts and show the following, for
k
sufficiently large:

(4.22)

\displaystyle\lambda_{g}(1)^{2}(W_{1}(1)+W_{2}(1))

\displaystyle\gg(\log k)^{2},

(4.23)

\displaystyle\sum_{2\leq n\leq\frac{k}{\log k}}\frac{\lambda_{g}(n)^{2}}{n}(W_{1}(n)+W_{2}(n))

\displaystyle\geq 0,

(4.24)

\displaystyle\sum_{\frac{k}{\log k}<n\leq k^{2}}\frac{\lambda_{g}(n)^{2}}{n}(W_{1}(n)+W_{2}(n))

\displaystyle\geq-C(\log k)^{-10}\quad\text{for some constant $C\geq 0$},

(4.25)

\displaystyle\sum_{n>k^{2}}\frac{\lambda_{g}(n)^{2}}{n}(W_{1}(n)+W_{2}(n))

\displaystyle\ll k^{-10}.

To begin, we observe that

(4.26)

\displaystyle W_{1}(n)

\displaystyle\ll 1\quad\text{for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$},

(4.27)

\displaystyle W_{1}(n)

\displaystyle\ll(\log k)^{-20}\quad\text{for $\frac{k}{\log k}<n\leq k^{2}$},

(4.28)

\displaystyle W_{1}(n)

\displaystyle\ll(nk)^{-10}\quad\text{for $n>k^{2}$},

(4.29)

\displaystyle W_{2}(n)

\displaystyle\geq 0\quad\text{for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$},

(4.30)

\displaystyle W_{2}(n)

\displaystyle=\left(\log\frac{k}{4\pi^{2}n}+O(1)\right)^{2}\quad\text{for $n<\frac{k}{4\pi^{2}}$},

(4.31)

\displaystyle W_{2}(n)

\displaystyle\ll(nk)^{-10}\quad\text{for $n>k^{2}$}.

Indeed, the bound (
4.26
) for
W_{1}(n)
follows by moving the line of integration in (
4.20
) to the left of
\Re(w)=0
for
n<\frac{k}{(\log k)^{2}}
and staying to the right of
\Re(w)=0
for
n\geq\frac{k}{(\log k)^{2}}
. The bound (
4.27
) follows by moving to the line
\Re(w)=20
and using the fact
\frac{k}{n(\log k)^{2}}<(\log k)^{-1}
for
\frac{k}{\log k}<n\leq k^{2}
, while the bound (
4.28
) follows by moving far to the right for
n>k^{2}
. The nonnegativity (
4.29
) of
W_{2}(n)
is immediate from the definition (
4.21
). Finally, the asymptotic formula (
4.30
) for
W_{2}(n)
for
n<\frac{k}{4\pi^{2}}
follows by moving the line of integration in (
4.21
) far to the left and crossing a double pole at
w=0
, while the upper bound (
4.31
) for
W_{2}(n)
for
n>k^{2}
follows by instead moving the line of integration far to the right.

With these estimates for
W_{1}(n)
and
W_{2}(n)
in hand, we may now complete the proof.

(1)

For the first claim (
4.22
), observe that
\lambda_{g}(1)=1
,
W_{1}(1)\ll 1
from (
4.26
), and
W_{2}(1)\gg(\log k)^{2}
from (
4.30
).

(2)

For the second claim, observe that for
2\leq n\leq\frac{k}{\log k}
, we have that
W_{1}(n)\ll 1
by (
4.26
) and
W_{2}(n)\gg(\log\log k)^{2}
by (
4.30
) and the fact that
\frac{k}{n}\geq\log k
. These facts together with the nonnegativity of
\frac{\lambda_{g}(n)^{2}}{n}
give (
4.23
).

(3)

For the third claim, by inserting the bound (
4.27
) for
W_{1}(n)
and the bound

\sum_{\frac{k}{\log k}<n\leq k^{2}}\frac{\lambda_{g}(n)^{2}}{n}\leq\sum_{n\leq k^{2}}\frac{d(n)^{2}}{n}\ll(\log k)^{3},

we get

\sum_{\frac{k}{\log k}<n\leq k^{2}}\frac{\lambda_{g}(n)^{2}}{n}W_{1}(n)\ll(\log k)^{-10}.

Since

\sum_{\frac{k}{\log k}<n\leq k^{2}}\frac{\lambda_{g}(n)^{2}}{n}W_{2}(n)\geq 0

by nonnegativity, we deduce (
4.24
).

(4)

Finally, the fourth claim (
4.25
) follows immediately from the fact that
W_{1}(n)\ll(nk)^{-10}
and
W_{2}(n)\ll(nk)^{-10}
for
n>k^{2}
via (
4.28
) and (
4.31
).

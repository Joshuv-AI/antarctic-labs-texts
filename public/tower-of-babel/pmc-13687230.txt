Collaborating for Affordable Patient Access to Medicines: Review of the First Decade of the Beneluxa Initiative in Pharmaceutical Policy
Authors: Sabine Vogler, Verena Knoll, Anja Sonemann, Annemie Quanten, Roisin Adams, Tim de Jager, Marc Van de Casteele, Lonneke Timmers
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCross-country collaboration has been proposed as a policy solution to improve patient access to medicines. Since patient access to medicines at affordable costs has also become an issue for high-income countries, some European countries responded in 2015 by establishing the Beneluxa Initiative to work together in pharmaceutical policy. As of 2025, the Initiative comprised five countries (Austria, Belgium, Ireland, Luxembourg, and the Netherlands) and had ten years of experience in several joint collaborative activities, including horizon scanning, health technology assessment, reimbursement negotiations, and strategic exchange on policy developments. During its first decade, the Beneluxa Initiative conducted 15 joint health technology assessments and four joint negotiations. Insights gained through these collaborative processes have contributed to the refinement of methodological approaches and the exploration of new avenues, such as the establishment of the International Horizon Scanning Initiative with participation of additional countries. Although public attention often centres on joint negotiations, they represent only one dimension of the Initiative’s work. The Beneluxa Initiative adopts a comprehensive product life-cycle approach, seeking to identify medicines with potentially high budget impact in early development stages, thereby allowing for timely preparation of collaborative assessments and negotiations. While differences in the legal and organisational national frameworks pose challenges and collaboration requires sufficient time resources, the Beneluxa experience demonstrated that collaboration in pharmaceutical policy across countries is possible and useful. Key Points for Decision Makers

Cross-border collaboration in pricing and reimbursement of medicines is considered a promising policy option to improve equitable access to medicines. One example is the Beneluxa Initiative comprising five European high-income countries.While Beneluxa is mainly known for jointly negotiating medicine prices, the Initiative collaborates in further areas, including horizon scanning, health technology assessment and strategic exchange of policy information.Cross-country collaboration requires clarity of objectives, development of appropriate procedures for cross-country collaboration, support from the political level and interaction between policymakers and technical experts as well as sufficient investments, including time resources.The Beneluxa Initiative has proven that challenges of working together across borders can be addressed. Beneluxa’s contributions to improving affordable patient access to medicines may offer inspiration and encouragement to other countries globally to collaborate in pharmaceutical policy.

Introduction

Ensuring equitable patient access to affordable medicines is a major challenge worldwide [1–3], including in high-income countries [4, 5]. The situation is particularly critical for some new treatments, such as one-time gene therapies in oncology, which are launched at extremely high prices. At the same time, there can be uncertainty regarding the therapeutic benefit offered by several new medicines that are marketed as innovative, and which questions the justification for their high prices [6–9].

Although existing policy options have considerable value, they have not been sufficiently effective in addressing given challenges [10–12]. Thus, newer avenues were discussed, piloted and implemented with a view to improving patient access at affordable costs [13–21]. One approach that has repeatedly been proposed is cross-border collaboration in pharmaceutical policy, including pricing and procurement, as articulated in the literature and in the policy debate [22–25].

These calls for collaboration have emerged within the European Union (EU) policy framework, where regulatory procedures for marketing authorisation of medicines have been harmonised for three decades. However, the decisions on whether a medicine will be publicly funded and at what price are taken by each EU Member State [26].

In the EU, some important legislative actions have been taken in recent years to facilitate collaboration across countries, without compromising the competence of the Member States for pricing, procurement and reimbursement. In the area of procurement, these include the Joint Procurement Agreement, the establishment of the Health Emergency Preparedness and Response Authority (HERA) with the mandate to purchase medical countermeasures, and, rather recently, the adoption of the Critical Medicines Act. Collaboration in Health Technology Assessment (HTA) has been formalised through the adoption of the European HTA Regulation (HTAR) [27]: Applicable from January 2025, this Regulation mandates the conduct of Joint Clinical Assessments (JCAs) of new medicines, starting with oncology medicines and Advanced Therapy Medicinal Products (ATMP), across all EU Member States, with the aim to progress towards a longer-term goal of equitable, efficient and high-quality healthcare in Europe [28].

In addition, some European countries have engaged in collaborative action, that operate outside the framework of the European Commission. These initiatives are typically confined to a limited number of countries that demonstrate sufficient alignment in their pharmaceutical reimbursement systems and share a common strategic perspective in this domain. The initiatives address various aspects of pricing, procurement and reimbursement, and they range from informal exchanges of experience and capacity-building to formal joint negotiations and procurements [29–32]. For instance, countries involved in the Baltic Procurement Initiative and in the Nordic Pharmaceutical Forum collaborate, among others, on joint procurement. Supplementary File 1 provides an overview of various European collaborative efforts, encompassing both actions coordinated by the European Commission as well as initiatives led by a subset of countries.

One notable example of the latter is the Beneluxa Initiative, which brings together Austria, Belgium, Ireland, Luxembourg, and the Netherlands. As the Initiative marked its tenth anniversary in 2025, this article reviews its key developments and achievements. Authored by technical experts who have been involved in the Initiative for years, the article offers up-to-date information provided from an inside perspective. It draws on both published and unpublished sources, complemented by the authors’ practical experience, thereby contributing insights into ongoing discussions about challenges and benefits associated with implementing cross-border collaboration in pharmaceutical policy.

Beneluxa’s Governance Structure

The Initiative’s Launch

The Beneluxa Initiative was formally launched during the Informal Meeting of European Ministers of Employment, Social Policy, Health and Consumer Affairs in April 2015. At this meeting, the health ministers of Belgium and the Netherlands signed a Letter of Intent on collaboration in pharmaceutical policy. A key component of this agreement was the commitment to jointly negotiate the price of medicines, particularly for medicines for rare diseases [33].

The Grand Duchy of Luxembourg joined the collaboration in September 2015, followed by Austria in June 2016. With these additions, the Initiative adopted the name “BeNeLuxA”. Ireland became a member in June 2018, and since then, the official designation of the cross-country collaboration has been “Beneluxa Initiative”, frequently abbreviated as “Beneluxa”.

The establishment of Beneluxa occurred at a time when the limitations of unilateral national approaches to pharmaceutical pricing and reimbursement had increasingly become evident [26]. For several years, the Conclusions of the Council of the EU pointed to the importance of cross-country collaboration in pharmaceutical policy, including joint procurement [23, 24, 34]. Nevertheless, the implementation of a unified pricing and procurement framework across all EU Member States, which might result in a single European medicine price, was widely regarded as not feasible [35].

In response, the Ministers of the Beneluxa member countries expressed their will to move beyond existing informal exchanges and to seek a more structured approach for collaboration. Beneluxa members have previously described themselves as a “Coalition of the Willing”, viewing their initiative as a pilot project to demonstrate the feasibility of cross-country collaboration in pharmaceutical policy - a proof of concept. While Beneluxa was being established, some further European countries also explored bilateral or multilateral governmental collaboration in this domain, typically with the aim of jointly negotiating or procuring specific medicines (see also Supplementary File 1).

Beneluxa Member Countries

The five countries of the Beneluxa Initiative are European high-income countries with middle-sized markets. They have solidarity-based health systems and display some similarities in the pharmaceutical policy framework.

It was a deliberate decision of the Beneluxa members to include countries of comparable income and similar challenges due to their limited market size. Thus, countries with similar characteristics would be most appropriate candidates for joining the Beneluxa Initiative. However, after Ireland had joined the Initiative, it was agreed to temporarily suspend any extension and instead concentrate on implementing the collaborative activities, for which the Initiative had been established, as well as on adjusting the governance and organisational structure to better support the collaboration.

Bridging Policymaking and Technical Expertise

From its inception, the governance structure of the Beneluxa Initiative has been shaped by a recognition of the importance of a strong political mandate and excellent technical expertise. To ensure regular interaction between these two spheres, the Initiative adopted a three-tiered governance model. This structure comprises representation from high-level policymakers, domain-specific technical experts, and a coordination mechanism designed to facilitate communication and alignment across levels. After scaling up of the horizon scanning through the establishment of the International Horizon Scanning Initiative (IHSI) as a separate legal entity, which provides a newly built horizon scanning database, the domain task force “Horizon scanning” was suspended, as Beneluxa’s horizon scanning experts were instead engaged in the IHSI activities. After the instalment of a General Manager, Beneluxa’s Secretariat at the Belgian National Institute for Health and Disability Insurance was dissolved in 2026, with the General Manager assuming its tasks.

At the political level, the Steering Committee operates under the mandate conferred by the participating countries, as formalised in the Letter of Intent signed by the respective ministers. Each country is represented by two Steering Committee members, usually from the Ministry of Health. The Steering Committee is chaired by a representative from the coordinating country, with the role of the Chair rotating among members. In 2025, Ireland assumed the role of the Chair, succeeding the Netherlands in 2024, which had taken this function from Belgium. In June 2026, the Chair was handed over to Austria.

At the technical level, activities have been organised through domain task forces. Initially, four domain task forces were established: horizon scanning, health technology assessment (HTA), pricing and reimbursement, and information sharing and policy exchange. This configuration reflects the Initiative’s pharmaceutical value chain approach: Medicines of interest are first identified through horizon scanning, followed by joint HTA, and potentially culminating in collaborative price negotiations. Technical experts from various Beneluxa member institutions, including social insurance institutions, HTA bodies, and other public entities involved in pharmaceutical policy, contribute to the respective domain task forces aligned with their areas of expertise. They are also invited to attend the General Assembly, which is Beneluxa’s plenary forum, held once a year.

Operationally, a team of Country Coordinators, with one coordinator appointed per member country, serves as a bridge between the political and technical levels, in particular for their own country. Along with the (co-)chairs of the domain task forces, the Country Coordinators participate in Steering Committee meetings, which are held at least twice a year.

Beneluxa’s Principles

The Beneluxa Initiative adheres to a set of guiding principles that form the foundation of its collaborative modus operandi. These include a pharmaceutical value chain perspective, project-specific participation, fostering transparency, process alignment, strengthening need-driven health systems and serving as collaborative platform (Fig. 2).

Organisational Developments

In response to perceived need for enhanced coordination and aligned to changes in the scope of activities, which are described in the following section, some organisational changes were implemented.

In 2021, a Secretariat, affiliated with the Belgian National Institute for Health and Disability Insurance, was established as support and coordination function.

Once the horizon scanning database commissioned by the newly established International Horizon Scanning Initiative (IHSI) became fully operational, the activities of the Beneluxa’s domain task force on horizon scanning were suspended, since its members were involved in horizon scanning activities in the frame of IHSI instead.

Furthermore, in 2024, a Policy Working Group was established to facilitate policy discussions and decisions of the Steering Committee.

In December 2025, a General Manager took office to account for the growing complexity and workload associated with Beneluxa activities. This newly established position aims to enhance coordination and communication by supporting Beneluxa’s ongoing operations and working in close collaboration with all entities of the Initiative. The General Manager also assumed the tasks of the Secretariat.

Beneluxa’s Activities

Horizon Scanning

Even prior to the COVID-19 pandemic, which highlighted the critical importance of health system preparedness, the Beneluxa Initiative had identified preparedness, particularly in relation to high-priced medicines, as a strategic priority. A specific trigger was the 2014 marketing authorisation of the hepatitis C medicine sofosbuvir, which came largely unexpectedly for the public authorities and payers [36]. At that time, in only few countries, e.g., Italy, Norway, Sweden and UK, governments had established early awareness systems to inform policymakers [37].

Initially, a dedicated Beneluxa domain task force on horizon scanning identified relevant therapeutic areas and individual medicine candidates in development, which were expected to offer novel treatment options but imply significant budget impact. In parallel, Beneluxa invested in strategic planning for cross-country horizon scanning and explored several models. This discussion was informed by a study conducted by the Belgian Health Care Knowledge Centre (KCE) in 2017, which mapped existing horizon scanning systems in Europe and proposed scenarios for a common approach [38].

The Beneluxa Initiative sought to establish a horizon scanning system that relied exclusively on publicly accessible data, to enable further use of the information (for example, in HTA reports and for pricing and reimbursement). Recognising the scale of investment required and the relevance of such a system for further countries, the IHSI was created as an independent legal entity in October 2019. Participation in IHSI was opened to countries beyond Beneluxa, represented by their governmental institutions, without requiring formal membership in the Beneluxa Initiative. By the end of 2025, IHSI comprised public institutions from nine countries: Austria, Belgium, Denmark, Ireland, Norway, Portugal, Slovakia, Sweden, and the Netherlands.

Following a tendering procedure launched in 2020, a contractor was commissioned to establish, manage, and populate the IHSI Joint Horizon Scanning Database [39]. From 2022, the database was gradually made available to IHSI members, who also receive High Impact Reports summarising major expected developments across therapeutic areas. With the establishment of the IHSI database, there was no longer a need for Beneluxa’s technical experts to do the scanning themselves. Since then, they have focused on the strategic interpretation of the data, while ensuring that the database is systematically enhanced, considering specific needs of the IHSI members. This work done together with the colleagues of other IHSI member countries gradually substituted the activities of Beneluxa’s domain force task on horizon scanning, which, as described above, was discontinued.

The data provided through the IHSI database support member institutions in preparing for the anticipated market entry and funding requests for new medicines in their countries. It facilitates the selection of specific medicines for further joint action within Beneluxa, including joint HTA and price negotiations (see also below on further activities based on horizon scanning results). Horizon scanning insights are strategically discussed within the Beneluxa Initiative as well as with further other member countries, to identify therapeutic areas where expected innovations may require preparedness from policymakers and payers.

Health Technology Assessment

Experts in Beneluxa’s domain task force working on HTA have been collaborating on producing joint assessments. Medicines eligible for joint HTA are identified by the task force members based on the outcomes of horizon scanning activities, and pharmaceutical companies are invited to submit a request for joint HTA. For instance, in January 2025, the Initiative published a list of 13 impactful medicines suitable for joint HTA based on a selection of 200 medicines in development identified through IHSI [40]. Additionally, the Beneluxa Initiative has consistently remained open to considering the performance of joint HTAs upon request from companies, regardless of whether these companies intend to pursue joint negotiations thereafter. Companies can ask for a meeting to clarify open questions on the process.

In line with the Initiative’s working principles (Fig. 2), collaboration in HTA is voluntary (not all five countries need to participate in a joint assessment). In addition to joint writing, it is also possible to re-use an HTA report prepared by another Beneluxa country, or to act as external referee [41].

The joint HTA procedure follows national regulation in each of the countries. Thereby, parallel procedures in the participating countries are run simultaneously, but the assessment is not repeated in each of the countries involved. At the start of the HTA procedure a memorandum of understanding describes the details of the submission. To kick off the joint assessment, companies asking for a joint HTA submit the identical dossier to all participating countries on the same day. For doing so, they are requested to use the Beneluxa HTA Submission Template accessible through the Beneluxa website beneluxa.org, which addresses the data requirements of each of the Beneluxa countries [42]. Applicants are asked to indicate whether they aim for a comparative effectiveness and budget impact assessment or for an HTA that additionally includes a pharmacoeconomic assessment. The HTA Submission Template details the data that the applicant has to provide in the submission (see Box 1 for further details on the methodology).

Regarding patient involvement, patient input was looked for according to national procedures established in each of the Beneluxa member countries. In a similar way, contacts with physicians’ associations, pharmacists’ associations, pharmaceutical companies and other stakeholders took place according to national legislation and processes. This approach was not only legally sound but turned out to be the most practical approach to ensure all stakeholders’ input.

Before introducing the joint procedure, members of the domain task force HTA examined feasibility and carried out a detailed analysis of national HTA processes in the Beneluxa member countries. The analysis studied elements such as assessment and appraisal functions, stakeholder involvement, reporting formats and publication requirements. The analysis confirmed that the procedures vary between the countries, in particular with regard to timelines during HTA and subsequent pricing and reimbursement processes. Despite these procedural differences, the outcomes of national HTAs, specifically the conclusions and the arguments supporting them, were largely similar. This was shown by an analysis of 444 drug-indication combinations in Austria, Belgium, Ireland and the Netherlands [43].

By end of 2025, Beneluxa had concluded 15 joint HTAs, with the participation of at least two members countries, frequently Belgium and the Netherlands (Table 1). Other Beneluxa members, e.g., Austria, were involved as external referees.

HTA health technology assessment

The findings reported by Vreman et al. [43] that alignment of national appraisal processes could serve as a foundation for cross-border appraisal were confirmed through the Initiative’s joint HTA procedures. As both assessment and appraisal were conducted in accordance with national legislation governing HTA criteria, joint HTA procedures applied the national criteria to reach national conclusions on added clinical value. This was done simultaneously in two or three HTA bodies of the countries participating in the respective joint procedure. To note that no joint HTA has yet been carried out in all five member countries, for two reasons: The company was not interested in a simultaneous procedure in five countries, and the logistical effort for conducting joint HTA simultaneously in five countries was considered too burdensome by the HTA bodies at that time. Although the total number of joint appraisals may appear limited, it is of note that a joint conclusion on added clinical value was done as a result of comparable assessment criteria in multiple jurisdictions.

Regarding pharmacoeconomic evaluation, the economic model in those joint procedures based on a common dossier submitted by the company was developed by the pharmaceutical company in accordance with national requirements of one participating country. Subsequently, the model was adapted for other Beneluxa countries through scenario analyses.

Concerning budget impact, national HTA bodies independently assessed the financial projections for the participating countries. This approach is distinctive in that it enabled a transparent and comparative analysis of key parameters, including epidemiology, unit costs, and market uptake, across the two or three Beneluxa countries that participated.

Collaboration on Pricing and Reimbursement

Beneluxa is sometimes wrongfully perceived as a procurement initiative, where the involved countries combine their volumes and tender for medicines, as typically done in the off-patent market. Instead, the Beneluxa Initiative negotiates the prices of selected new on-patent medicines; medicines responding to a high unmet need and expected added value are particularly in the focus of the Initiative. Only medicines, for which the reimbursement procedure has not yet started in any of the Beneluxa countries, and whose market entry in all countries of the Initiative is planned, are eligible [40].

Pharmaceutical companies may also approach the Initiative if interested in a joint procedure. Alternatively, the Initiative may invite a pharmaceutical company to enter a joint price negotiation with Beneluxa. However, any joint negotiation must be preceded by joint HTA, but not every joint HTA has to be followed by a joint negotiation (Table 1). After the joint HTA on etranacogene dezaparvovec, for instance, the involved countries Belgium and the Netherlands opted for individual negotiations. As for joint HTA, joint negotiations do not require the involvement of all Beneluxa member countries. After the Initiative has reached a joint principal agreement with a pharmaceutical company on the reimbursement conditions in all countries, the reimbursement processes and contracting are concluded in alignment with national procedures and legislation.

During the first decade, the Initiative successfully conducted negotiations for four medicines, with three having resulted in multi-country agreements (Box 2). Although the win-win situation of joint procedures was highlighted by the Initiative and companies which had successfully concluded joint negotiations [30], companies, particularly larger firms, are still hesitant to engage. The Initiative noted that primarily smaller- and middle-sized companies showed interest in entering dialogue and negotiation.

Countries participating in joint negotiations have developed significant expertise in conducting multi-country pharmaceutical negotiations. A key lesson is the importance and challenge of jointly establishing a willingness-to-pay (WTP) for the negotiated medicine across member countries, given the variation in national guidelines and procedures for determining the national WTP. Further important insights include the alignment of the different national procedures before, during and after negotiations on reimbursement conditions. The Beneluxa Initiative is collaborating with other countries, including the Nordic countries and Canada, to disseminate these lessons.

The first joint negotiation by Belgium and the Netherlands on lumacaftor/ivacaftor ended in 2017. This did not result in reimbursement in the two countries as no joint agreement was reached. Subsequent national procedures, however, did result in reimbursed access in both countries.

In 2018, Belgium and the Netherlands were involved in the joint negotiation for nusinersen. This resulted in a positive reimbursement decision and access for patients in both countries [30].

In 2021, Belgium, Ireland and the Netherlands reached the first Beneluxa agreement involving three countries in a joint negotiation on the reimbursement conditions for onasemnogene abeparvovec, enabling reimbursed access for patients in all three countries [44].

In 2023, another three-country joint negotiation, on atidarsagene autotemcel, was conducted by the same countries. First, the Initiative announced they did not come to an agreement with the manufacturer. After a joint follow-up by the three countries, an agreement was reached in January 2024 and the treatment was reimbursed in all three countries [45].

Information Sharing and Policy Exchange

In addition to product-specific activities, the Initiative also fosters information exchange on country-specific processes in pharmaceutical policy implementation (not only related to new medicines but also for generic and biosimilar medicines) and therapeutic areas of interest (e.g., Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy). To do so, the domain task force Information Sharing and Policy Exchange organised both public and internal webinars for experts from public authorities involved in the Initiative; topics included cross-sectorial funding mechanisms for medicines, managed-entry agreements, measures to improve transparency and early access schemes. The activities aimed to share information about the national policy frameworks, observations on challenges and best practices, and suggestions for change, and is not product related. It also serves to build capacity of the Beneluxa experts.

As far as resources allow, the Beneluxa Initiative has been carrying out communication activities targeting external stakeholders. This is mainly done through the Beneluxa website launched in 2017 and presentations during meetings and conferences. Key stakeholder groups addressed are pharmaceutical companies, public authorities and payers, since these stakeholders expressed high interest in learning more about the Beneluxa Initiative. Additionally, Beneluxa was approached several times by supranational institutions (e.g., European Commission, the World Health Organization) and asked to share experience about working together across borders.

In 2024, the Beneluxa Initiative pioneered a scoping review meeting with clinicians, patients and payers as a cross-country stakeholder engagement event. This cross-country scoping review meeting on the topic of Alzheimer medication followed up on previous communication and capacity-building activities in this area (Box 3).

In recent years, Beneluxa has increasingly raised its voice to comment on developments in the European policy field, such as related to legislative reforms. This is reflected in statements and news releases published on the Beneluxa website, accompanied by Social Media posts (see Fig. 3).

In 2022, Beneluxa’s domain task force on horizon scanning published a report, outlining anticipated developments in Alzheimer’s disease [46]. The preparation of the report was accompanied by a statement of the Beneluxa Initiative, published on its website in June 2021. The statement highlighted the importance of relevant clinical endpoints in Alzheimer’s trials, such as changes in cognitive scores or dementia scales, and stressed that HTA agencies would not consider biomarker data alone sufficient to demonstrate therapeutic benefit [47].

Alzheimer’s medication remained a topic of discussion during a webinar organised by the Initiative in 2023. This event was open to officials and experts from Beneluxa member institutions as well as members of the Pharmaceutical Pricing and Reimbursement Information (PPRI) network, which brings together public authorities from 50 countries globally, primarily in Europe [32].

At the time when the European Medicines Agency was still reviewing the applications for marketing authorisation of the Alzheimer’s medicines donanemab and lecanemab, the Beneluxa Initiative convened a cross-country scoping meeting in June 2024. Representatives of Alzheimer’s patient organisations, prescribers, and public payers from the five Beneluxa countries participated and shared their insights and perspectives regarding challenges and prerequisites for introducing Alzheimer’s medicines in the health systems.

Has the Proof of Concept Been Made?

The Beneluxa Initiative has faced high expectations from stakeholders to deliver tangible outcomes, which are typically measured by the number of concluded negotiations [30]. While the public discourse often focused on joint negotiations, pricing and reimbursement represents only one aspect of the Initiative. During its first ten years, Beneluxa has been performing a range of activities, including some non-product-specific work. These activities have consistently been valued by its members, but their relevance has remained largely unknown outside Beneluxa. This may also be explained by the fact that Beneluxa members concentrated on establishing and developing the Initiative, including optimising processes for working together, rather than conducting broad dissemination activities.

Acknowledging that the authors’ perspectives have been informed by their involvement in the Initiative, we argue that the proof of concept has been established: cross-country collaboration is feasible, but certain conditions are key for its success. It is important to ensure clarity regarding objectives and national intentions and to provide sufficient resources, including scaling up investments as the collaboration evolves. Aligned processes, rather than harmonisation, and shared understanding of strategic positions and values are essential.

As an Initiative, which is committed to policy implementation to address challenges relevant to all its member countries, Beneluxa provides a unique forum for exploring, piloting and implementing cross-country solutions, thereby benefiting from the collaboration between high-level policymakers and technical experts. Strategic directions determined at the policy level offer essential guidance to the technical experts, who, in return, play a crucial role in informing policymakers about operational challenges, practical feasibility, and implementation approaches. According to the authors, regular interaction between representatives of different levels is an important prerequisite to the effective functioning of collaborative initiatives such as Beneluxa, and it appears to be decisive for sustaining the high level of engagement among those involved. The experience of achieving positive outcomes and the opportunity of working together with colleagues from other countries are additional motivating factors.

Some projects and outputs from the collaboration of the five countries would not have been possible as single-country or even bilateral endeavours. A flagship outcome was the creation of the International Horizon Scanning Initiative as a spin-off project of Beneluxa. The IHSI database responds to the need of public authorities and payers to access information about medicines in the pipeline, without having to address the industry for this data. No independent horizon scanning system of this dimension, which exclusively relied on publicly sourced data, had been made available across countries before. Establishing the IHSI database, and continuously updating it, has been linked to high costs, which are spread across those countries that were willing to invest into horizon scanning. The IHSI illustrates how Beneluxa has steered further cooperation beyond its own cross-country initiative. Apart from the financial investment aspect, this broader collaboration resulted in structured strategic discussions of relevant developments, such as upcoming therapies identified through IHSI, organised among IHSI members.

At EU level, Beneluxa’s work on joint HTA offers valuable learnings for the European HTA Regulation (HTAR). Beneluxa experts are involved in EU activities to implement the HTAR and contribute with their experience gained from the joint procedures in Beneluxa. Lessons learnt in Beneluxa are the exchange on PICO, the evaluation of input of national stakeholders in various countries, methodological exchange at expert level and an understanding of each other’s HTA procedures. Moreover, the JCAs will be incorporated into the Initiative’s collaborative work.

Although their numbers may appear to be rather low, joint negotiations are further important outcomes. Within Europe, where joint procurement experience on generics, biosimilars, vaccines and medical countermeasures exists (e.g., successful tenders by the Nordic Pharmaceutical Forum and the Baltic Procurement Initiative), the Beneluxa Initiative is, however, the sole cross-country collaboration that successfully concluded joint negotiations for new medicines based on HTA. However, pharmaceutical companies are still hesitant to enter joint negotiations. As another limiting factor, it has to be acknowledged that joint negotiations, including development of appropriate procedures for doing so, are time-intensive. Drawing on its own first-hand experience, the Beneluxa Initiative can inform European and international discussions on joint negotiations, thereby confirming feasibility, but insisting on the importance of meeting certain prerequisites, such as adaption of procedures and legislation, alignment of the WTP, and time investment.

The ten-year-experience also confirmed the relevance of Beneluxa’s working principles (i.e., comprehensive value chain approach, country collaboration on a case-by-case basis, fostering transparency where feasible, process alignment, need-driven health systems, collaborative platform; see Fig. 2). By endorsing these principles as guiding framework, Beneluxa members have systematically integrated them into strategic decision making and technical work. For instance, through the IHSI database, Beneluxa experts have been identifying impactful medicines (i.e., in terms of healthcare service impact), which would be of interest for joint HTA and potentially also for joint negotiations. Beneluxa has regularly published these medicines on its website since 2023; thus, actively inviting pharmaceutical companies to submit applications. Another example concerns transparency: while Beneluxa members comply with confidentiality agreements and legislation and thus do not share net price data, Beneluxa fosters information sharing on non-product-related policy experience within the Initiative and with other countries and cross-country collaborations (see also Fig. 3), thereby contributing to strengthening capacity.

A WHO study highlighted two prerequisites for effective cross-country collaboration: alignment between policymakers and technical experts involved in the collaboration, and availability of sufficient resources, including time [30]. These factors have also proven to be critical for Beneluxa, not solely for joint negotiations. As the scope of activities expanded, the need for increased time investment became evident, such as for internal communication, dissemination and managerial processes. In response, the Beneluxa Initiative has seen major scaling up of resources, which ultimately led to the appointment of a General Manager during the drafting of this article.

However, it is not possible to assess the costs for the establishment and maintenance of the Beneluxa Initiative. The above-mentioned WHO study, which analysed existing cross-country collaborations, including Beneluxa, around 2018, aimed to do so but eventually described qualitatively high-level time resources, such as meetings. Any assessment of the costs carries the risk of underestimation since major investments are the time resources of those involved in the Beneluxa Initiative. Experts and policymakers conduct the cross-country activities on top of their national responsibilities and usually do not report working hours. From the authors’ experience, time investments were not only substantial in the beginning but also maintained at high levels as the Initiative evolved and more activities were undertaken.

We acknowledge that this article does not provide a response to whether the Beneluxa Initiative has improved sustainable patient access to medicines at an affordable cost—which was their overall vision.

However, an impact assessment is beyond the scope of this manuscript. Designed as a “Practical Application” article, this paper instead presents, for the first time in a scientific publication, a comprehensive overview of the Beneluxa Initiative, authored by a team of experts who are involved in its activities. The information provided can be used for further research, including an analysis of the impact of the Beneluxa Initiative.

Despite lack of a formal impact assessment, the authors are confident that this article provides indications on the benefits of working together. Importantly, Beneluxa has improved the knowledge base in technical terms, e.g., by establishing an international horizon scanning initiative and producing joint HTA reports, as well as in terms of processes, by laying foundations for joint procedures. Drawing on their practical experience in collaboration, Beneluxa members are better equipped to assess policy proposals and legislative reforms, particularly if these include cross-border collaborative components.

Overall, although this can hardly be measured in quantitative terms, the Beneluxa Initiative has strengthened the voice of public authorities responsible for pharmaceutical pricing and reimbursement, by contributing jointly to policy debates (e.g., through publication of statements).

Conclusion

According to the authors, the Beneluxa Initiative has demonstrated its value over its first decade. Beyond specific achievements, such as joint negotiations, the Initiative has generated important insights into implementing cross-country collaboration in a policy area where decisions are taken by the individual EU Member States. The lessons learned from Beneluxa are not only relevant for the Initiative but also for the global policy community. They can inform ongoing collaborative efforts, such as the implementation of the European HTA Regulation, and inspire other countries in Europe and globally to pursue cooperation in pharmaceutical policy.

Looking ahead to the next decade, Beneluxa aims to incorporate these insights into its further development, which may see adaptation of its priorities and activities. The Initiative also plans to expand its communication activities through fostering the dissemination of experiences gained through sustained cross-country collaboration and contributing actively to policy debates. By sharing the perspectives of five medium-sized, high-income countries with solidarity-based health systems and long-standing experience in working together across borders, Beneluxa can help shape future policy to further improve affordable and sustainable patient access to medicines.

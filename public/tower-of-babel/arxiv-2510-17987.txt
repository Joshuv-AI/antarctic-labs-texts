Piling up in the darkness: Features of the BBH mass distribution from isolated binaries
Cristiano Ugolini

Abstract
After the third LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA observing run, the number of detected binary black hole (BBH) mergers became sufficient to identify statistical features of the population. We explore how different prescriptions for the final fate of massive stars and key binary-evolution processes shape isolated binaries and their remnants. Using sevn , we evolved 10 7 10^{7} binaries across 15 metallicities, 3 core-collapse supernova models, 4 PPISN models, and 6 common-envelope (CE) prescriptions, for a total of 990 runs ( 9.9 × 10 9 9.9\times 10^{9} systems). Both single- and binary-star physics shape the BH mass distribution: single-star processes control the high-mass tail ( M BH ≥ 45 ​ M ⊙ M_{\rm BH}\geq 45\,M_{\odot} ), while binary evolution produces pile-ups in specific intervals. In particular, the bump at ∼ 35 ​ M ⊙ \sim 35\,M_{\odot} , often attributed to PPISNe, also emerges from binaries evolving only through stable mass transfer, without CE. Finally, we test a top-heavy IMF, finding it boosts merger numbers and alters the abundance of systems with a given primary BH mass.

2
Methods

We employ the population-synthesis code
SEVN

(
Spera et al. 2015
)
, which interpolates stellar tracks from PARSEC v2.7.0
(
Bressan et al. 2012
)
.
Initial conditions follow
Iorio et al. 2023
: primary masses are drawn from a Kroupa IMF in
[8,150]\,\rm M_{\odot}
, and secondaries from
[5,150]\,\rm M_{\odot}
.
This analysis focuses on a subsample of the simulations presented in
Ugolini et al. prep
, consisting of
10^{7}
binaries evolved for each of 15 metallicities and 6 CE prescriptions, plus an additional run adopting a Larson IMF
(
Larson 1998
)
.

Effect of a top-heavy IMF

Stars that populate the
32
–
37\,\rm M_{\odot}
bump typically have
M_{\rm ZAMS}\gtrsim 70\,\rm M_{\odot}
, making the IMF a key factor in setting their relative abundance. To test this, we adopt a Larson IMF
(
Larson 1998
)
, usually invoked for Pop III stars but applied here across all metallicities to probe the effect of favoring massive stars.
Other initial conditions follow our fiducial setup (see Section
2
). Figure
2
shows the resulting distributions for
\alpha=3,5,10
.

CE parameter
\alpha=3
.
As in the Kroupa IMF case, SMT contributes
54\%
of bump primaries and CE
46\%
. The Larson IMF, however, boosts the overall merger yield by
\sim 67\%
and nearly triples the number of systems in the
32
–
37\,\rm M_{\odot}
range. The abundance of low-mass BHs (
M_{\rm BH}\lesssim 10\,\rm M_{\odot}
) is only mildly affected.

CE parameter
\alpha=5
.
The bump is dominated by SMT (
65\%
) with CE contributing
35\%
, similar to the Kroupa case, but the absolute number of bump BHs again rises by a factor
\sim 3
, and the total mergers increase by
\sim 68\%
.

CE parameter
\alpha=10
.
As in the Kroupa IMF case,
83\%
of bump BHs evolve without CE. The Larson IMF triples the bump population relative to Kroupa and raises the overall merger count by
\sim 80\%
.

In summary, adopting a top-heavy IMF greatly amplifies the
32
–
37\,\rm M_{\odot}
feature and the total merger rate, while leaving the qualitative role of CE vs. SMT largely unchanged.

5
Summary and conclusions

Large-scale population synthesis with
SEVN
shows that:

•

The
32
–
37\,\rm M_{\odot}
bump arises from a combination of CE and SMT pathways. CE dominates for
\alpha\leq 1
, SMT for
\alpha\geq 3
.

•

A top-heavy IMF amplifies the bump and merger rate without changing the CE/SMT balance.

•

CE efficiency and IMF leave measurable signatures on the BH mass spectrum, offering GW observations a way to constrain them.

Future work will integrate these results into the semi-analytical BPOP model
(
Arca Sedda et al. 2023
)
to include metallicity and star-formation histories.

Primary ureteral extranodal marginal zone lymphoma with transformation to diffuse large B-cell lymphoma mimicking urothelial carcinoma: A case report
Authors: Guilherme Bernardo, João A. Carvalho, Maria Alzamora, Rui Henrique, António Morais
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Extranodal marginal zone lymphoma of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) involving the ureter is particularly uncommon, and histological transformation to diffuse large B- cell lymphoma (DLBCL) further complicates diagnosis and management. We report a case of a female patient presenting with obstructive uropathy caused by long-segment ureteral wall thickening radiologically suggestive of urothelial carcinoma. Endoscopic evaluation and biopsies were inconclusive, leading to nephroureterectomy. Histopathological examination unexpectedly revealed MALT lymphoma with areas of transformation to DLBCL. This case highlights a rare diagnostic pitfall and emphasizes the importance of considering lymphoma in the differential diagnosis of atypical ureteral strictures.

Introduction

Primary lymphoma of the urinary tract is an uncommon clinical entity, representing less than 5% of all extranodal lymphomas, with renal and bladder involvement being far more frequent than ureteral disease.1,2 Primary ureteral lymphoma is exceptionally rare due to the absence of native lymphoid tissue within the ureteral wall, and most reported cases consist of isolated case reports or small case series.2,3 Among the different histological subtypes, extranodal marginal zone lymphoma of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT lymphoma) involving the ureter is particularly uncommon.2

The clinical presentation of ureteral lymphoma is often nonspecific and typically related to obstructive uropathy, including flank pain, hydronephrosis, or recurrent urinary tract infections. Unlike urothelial carcinoma, hematuria may be absent, further complicating clinical suspicion.4 Radiological findings usually demonstrate long-segment, concentric ureteral wall thickening with homogeneous contrast enhancement, features that closely resemble urothelial carcinoma and frequently lead to misdiagnosis.5,6 Endoscopic evaluation and ureteroscopic biopsies may be inconclusive, as lymphomatous infiltration predominantly affects the submucosal and muscular layers while sparing the urothelium. Consequently, definitive diagnosis is often established only after radical surgical intervention performed for presumed urothelial malignancy.7 Although MALT lymphoma is typically an indolent disease, histological transformation to diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is a recognized event associated with more aggressive behavior and the need for systemic therapy.8,9

We report a rare case of primary ureteral MALT lymphoma with transformation to DLBCL that clinically and radiologically mimicked urothelial carcinoma, highlighting the diagnostic challenges and the importance of considering lymphoproliferative disorders in the differential diagnosis of atypical ureteral strictures.

Case presentation

A 69 year-old female patient with a previous history of breast cancer treated with surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and currently under hormone therapy presented with a four-month history of hypogastric discomfort and a sensation of incomplete bladder emptying. There was no gross hematuria or constitutional B symptoms. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography demonstrated right-sided hydroureteronephrosis secondary to long-segment concentric thickening of the mid and distal ureter with marked contrast enhancement, highly suspicious for urothelial carcinoma (Fig. 1). No synchronous urothelial lesions or pathological lymphadenopathy were identified. The patient subsequently developed obstructive pyelonephritis requiring urgent urinary diversion by percutaneous nephrostomy. Diagnostic ureterorenoscopy revealed an impassable ureteral stenosis without papillary or exophytic lesions. Given persistent suspicion of malignancy and ongoing obstructive complications, laparoscopic nephroureterectomy with bladder cuff excision was performed. Gross examination demonstrated firm, whitish thickening of the ureteral wall. Histopathological analysis revealed extranodal marginal zone B-cell lymphoma of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue type (Fig. 2) with foci of transformation to diffuse large B-cell lymphoma infiltrating the ureteral wall and periureteral adipose tissue (Fig. 3). Surgical margins were involved. Subsequent staging with positron emission tomography identified supra- and infradiaphragmatic lymph node and splenic involvement. Discussion

This case report describes a patient with primary ureteral extranodal marginal zone lymphoma of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) with histological transformation to diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), initially mimicking urothelial carcinoma on imaging and clinical presentation. The diagnosis was only established after radical surgical treatment, underscoring the significant diagnostic challenges posed by this rare entity. Primary involvement of the ureter by lymphoma is exceedingly rare, as the ureteral wall lacks native lymphoid tissue. Genitourinary lymphomas represent less than 1% of extranodal lymphomas, with ureteral localization being among the least frequent sites reported.1,4 Most cases described in the literature present with nonspecific obstructive symptoms rather than hematuria, in contrast to urothelial carcinoma, which remains the most common primary ureteral malignancy.6 In our patient, the absence of hematuria and constitutional B symptoms was consistent with previous reports, further complicating clinical suspicion. Radiologically, ureteral lymphoma often manifests as long-segment, concentric ureteral wall thickening with homogeneous enhancement, frequently associated with hydroureteronephrosis. These findings closely resemble urothelial carcinoma and contribute to frequent misdiagnosis.6,7 Similar imaging features have been described in previously reported cases, reinforcing the difficulty in distinguishing lymphoma from epithelial malignancy based solely on radiological criteria. The most relevant aspect of this case is the unexpected diagnosis of MALT lymphoma with transformation to DLBCL following nephroureterectomy. MALT lymphoma is typically an indolent disease that may arise in extranodal sites lacking organized lymphoid tissue and is often associated with chronic inflammation.8 However, histological transformation to DLBCL, although uncommon, is a recognized event and confers a more aggressive biological behavior, worse prognosis, and the need for systemic immunochemotherapy.9,10 Diagnostic ureterorenoscopy with biopsy failed to establish the diagnosis in this case, which is consistent with prior studies. Lymphomatous infiltration tends to involve the submucosa and muscularis layers, while the urothelial surface may remain intact, resulting in superficial biopsies that are frequently non-diagnostic.11 Consequently, many cases of ureteral lymphoma are diagnosed only after surgical resection performed for presumed urothelial carcinoma or unresolved obstruction. Several differential diagnoses were considered, including urothelial carcinoma, retroperitoneal fibrosis, and inflammatory ureteral strictures. Urothelial carcinoma was strongly suspected due to imaging characteristics and persistent obstruction. Retroperitoneal fibrosis was deemed unlikely given the focal ureteral involvement and absence of typical radiological features.12 Ultimately, histopathological examination was essential to establish the correct diagnosis. The surgical approach adopted in this case was in line with standard management for suspected high-risk ureteral carcinoma with obstructive complications. However, awareness of ureteral lymphoma as a potential diagnosis could, in selected cases, allow for less radical management and earlier referral to hematology, particularly when renal preservation is critical.12 Following surgery, staging revealed disseminated disease, and the patient was appropriately treated with R-CHOP immunochemotherapy, which remains the standard of care for DLBCL.12 From a clinical perspective, this case highlights the importance of considering lymphoproliferative disorders in the differential diagnosis of atypical ureteral strictures, especially when endoscopic findings are inconclusive and biopsies fail to confirm urothelial malignancy.

Early multidisciplinary discussion may help avoid unnecessary radical surgery and facilitate timely systemic treatment. The limitations of this report include its single-case nature and the retrospective establishment of the diagnosis after surgical intervention. Additionally, longer follow-up is required to assess long-term oncological outcomes following systemic therapy.

Conclusions

Primary ureteral lymphoma is an exceptionally rare condition that may closely mimic urothelial carcinoma in both clinical presentation and imaging findings. This case underscores the diagnostic challenges associated with long-segment ureteral strictures, particularly when endoscopic biopsies are inconclusive. Awareness of lymphoproliferative disorders as a potential cause of ureteral obstruction is essential, as early consideration may facilitate timely multidisciplinary discussion, avoid unnecessary radical surgery in selected patients, and allow prompt initiation of appropriate systemic therapy when indicated.

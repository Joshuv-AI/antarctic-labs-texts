Robust Quickest Change Detection in Multi-Stream Non-Stationary Processes
Yingze Hou, Hoda Bidkhori, and Taposh Banerjee

Abstract
The problem of robust quickest change detection (QCD) in non-stationary processes under a multi-stream setting is studied. In classical QCD theory, optimal solutions are developed to detect a sudden change in the distribution of stationary data. Most studies have focused on single-stream data. In non-stationary processes, the data distribution both before and after change varies with time and is not precisely known. The multi-dimension data even complicates such issues. It is shown that if the non-stationary family for each dimension or stream has a least favorable law (LFL) or distribution in a well-defined sense, then the algorithm designed using the LFLs is robust optimal. The notion of LFL defined in this work differs from the classical definitions due to the dependence of the post-change model on the change point. Examples of multi-stream non-stationary processes encountered in public health monitoring and aviation applications are provided. Our robust algorithm is applied to simulated and real data to show its effectiveness.

When the precise law for the non-stationary models before and after the change is known, the optimal algorithms for multi-stream QCD, for i.i.d. and non-i.i.d. settings, can be found in
[

,

]
. When the pre-change law is known but the post-change law is unknown, optimal algorithms based on generalized likelihood ratios (GLR) or mixture are also available; see

. However,
GLR or mixture-based tests are computationally expensive to implement.

In this paper, we take a robust approach to multi-stream quickest change detection leading to algorithms that are often computationally easy to implement in practice. We also show that our proposed algorithms are optimal for a robust problem formulation. The literature on robust QCD is reviewed in Section
I-A
.

Our specific contributions are as follows:

1.

We first study the problem of robust QCD in a single stream where both pre- and post-change distributions are non-stationary and unknown.
We show that if there exists a pair of pre- and post-change non-stationary distributions, least favorable in a well-defined sense, then the test designed using this pair is robust optimal (see Section
III
). The least favorable distributions are identified using stochastic boundedness and monotone likelihood ratio conditions that are different from the standard conditions used in QCD literature (e.g., compare with
[
26
]
). Our conditions differ from the standard conditions in the literature because our post-change model depends on the change point.

2.

We then study the multi-stream QCD problem with unknown non-stationary pre- and post-change distributions in each stream. In this setting, we obtain an optimal robust solution using the least favorable pair of distributions for each stream. We show that our algorithm can robustly detect the change when the change occurs in any arbitrary subset of the streams (see Section
IV
).

3.

We provide several analytical examples in which the least favorable pair of distributions can be identified (see Section
III-G
).

4.

We apply our tests to real and simulated data and show that the robust tests can detect changes for all possible post-change scenarios (see Section
V
). For application to real datasets, we use COVID-19 infection rate data across different cities in the US to detect the onset of the pandemic. We also apply our algorithms to airline data collected around Pittsburgh airport for approaching aircraft.

II
A Classical QCD Model and Algorithm

This section reviews a classical result in the QCD literature and discusses its limitations.

In the QCD problem with i.i.d. observations, a decision maker observes a sequence of random variables
\{X_{n}\}
. Before a time
\nu
(called the change point), the random variables are i.i.d. with a fixed density
f
, and after
\nu
, are i.i.d. with another density
g
:

X_{n}\sim\begin{cases}f,&\quad\forall n<\nu,\\
g,&\quad\forall n\geq\nu.\end{cases}

(1)

Here
f
and
g
are densities such that

D(g\;\|\;f):=\int g(x)\log\frac{g(x)}{f(x)}dx\;>\;0,

where
D(g\ \|\ f)
is the Kullback-Leibler divergence between densities
g
and
f
.
The goal of the QCD problem is to detect this change in distribution from
f
to
g
with the minimum possible delay subject to a constraint on the rate of false alarms (
[
3
,
1
,
2
]
).

Studying this problem in the Bayesian setting would require us to assume a prior distribution on the change point
\nu
. Since such an assumption is not always practical, we focus on non-Bayesian settings in this paper.
In the non-Bayesian settings, the change point
\nu
is treated as an unknown constant (
[
10
,
11
]
). Let
\tau
be a stopping time for the process
\{X_{n}\}
, i.e., a positive integer-valued random variable such that the event
\{\tau\leq n\}
belongs to the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}
. In other words, whether or not
\tau\leq n
is completely determined by the first
n
observations. We consider this stopping time as our algorithm and declare that a change has occurred at the stopping time
\tau
. The detection delay of this stopping time
can be measured using the conditional delay measures such as

\mathsf{E}_{\nu}\left[(\tau-\nu+1)^{+}\right]\quad\text{or}\quad\mathsf{E}_{\nu}\left[\tau-\nu|\tau\geq\nu\right],

where we use
\mathsf{P}_{\nu}
to denote the law of the observation process
\{X_{n}\}
when the change occurs at
\nu
. We use
\mathsf{E}_{\nu}
to denote the corresponding expectation. In case of no change, notations
\mathsf{P}_{\infty}
and
\mathsf{E}_{\infty}
are used (suggesting a change occurring at
\nu=\infty
). However, these measures of delay displayed in the above equation depend on the unknown change point
\nu
. Thus, the popular measures for detection delay are minimax in nature. In the popular formulation of
[
10
]
, the minimax (minimizing the maximum of the conditional delay) metric considered is

\sup_{\nu\geq 1}\;\mathsf{E}_{\nu}\left[\tau-\nu|\tau\geq\nu\right].

However, this metric is not amenable to robust QCD theory in the generality we pursue in this paper. In this paper, we consider another popular minimax formulation proposed by
[
11
]
:

\textnormal{WADD}(\tau)\coloneqq\sup_{\nu}\;\esssup\;\mathsf{E}_{\nu}\left[(\tau-\nu+1)^{+}|\mathscr{F}_{\nu-1}\right],

(2)

where
\mathscr{F}_{\nu-1}
is the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
X_{1},\dots,X_{\nu-1}
, and
\esssup X
represents the smallest constant
C
such that
\mathsf{P}(X\leq C)=1
. Thus, the worst-case delay is taken over not just the change points but also over the past realizations. While this formulation may appear overly pessimistic due to the appearance of the essential supremum operation, it is the only metric for which strong optimality results have been developed in the literature in both minimax and robust settings (
[
26
,
24
]
).

The Lorden’s formulation of the QCD problem is

\begin{split}\inf_{\tau}\;\;&\textnormal{WADD}(\tau),\\
s.t.\;\;\ &\mathsf{E}_{\infty}[\tau]\geq\frac{1}{\alpha},\end{split}

(3)

where
1/\alpha
is a constraint on the mean time to a false alarm
\mathsf{E}_{\infty}[\tau]
, for
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
The optimal solution is given by the cumulative sum (CUSUM) algorithm (
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
):

\begin{split}W_{n}&=\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{i=k}^{n}\log\frac{g(X_{i})}{f(X_{i})},\\
\tau_{c}&=\min\{n\geq 1:W_{n}\geq A\}.\end{split}

(4)

To achieve optimality, the threshold
A
in (
4
) must be chosen carefully to meet a constraint on the false alarm with equality.
The CUSUM statistic
W_{n}
also has an efficient recursion:

W_{n}=\left(W_{n-1}+\log\frac{g(X_{n})}{f(X_{n})}\right)^{+},\quad W_{0}=0.

(5)

Deep insights can be obtained on the CUSUM algorithm through its asymptotic analysis. Since the CUSUM algorithm is optimal, it is also asymptotically optimal for the problem as
\alpha\to 0
. The following theorem provides the asymptotic performance of the CUSUM algorithm.

Theorem II.1
.

(Asymptotic optimality of CUSUM (
[
11
]
)):
If
0<D(g\ \|\ f)<\infty
, with
A=\log(1/\alpha)
,

\mathsf{E}_{\infty}[\tau_{c}]\geq\frac{1}{\alpha},

(6)

and as
\alpha\to 0
,

\textnormal{WADD}(\tau_{c})=\mathsf{E}_{1}[\tau_{c}-1]=\frac{|\log\alpha|}{D(g\ \|\ f)}+o(1).

(7)

Here the
o(1)
term goes to zero as
\alpha\to 0
.
Because of the above equations (and can also be established directly), for any stopping time
\tau
with
\mathsf{E}_{\infty}[\tau]\geq{1}/{\alpha}
,

\textnormal{WADD}(\tau)\geq\frac{|\log\alpha|}{D(g\ \|\ f)}+o(1),\quad\textnormal{ as }\alpha\to 0.

(8)

III
Robust Single-Stream QCD with Unknown Non-Stationary Pre- and Post-Change Distributions

In this section, we present our results on robust QCD in single stream data with unknown non-stationary pre- and post-change distributions. The results of the more general multi-stream case are discussed in Section
IV
. While the result of this section on the single-stream case is a special case of the multi-stream result, we present it here separately to emphasize the proof technique and the role of different assumptions. Also, it is notationally easier to discuss examples and applications in the single stream case.

III-A

Change Point Model and Notations for Non-Stationary Data

The classical QCD model (see (
1
)) assumes that the observations
\{X_{n}\}
are i.i.d. with fixed density
f
before a change time
\nu
, and i.i.d. with density
g
after
\nu
.
The change point model that we study is as follows:

X_{n}\sim\begin{cases}f_{n},&\quad\forall n<\nu,\\
g_{n,\nu},&\quad\forall n\geq\nu.\end{cases}

(9)

The random variables
\{X_{n}\}
are independent conditioned on the change point
\nu
.
Thus, before the change time
\nu
, the process follows a sequence of densities
\{f_{n}\}
. After time
\nu
, the process changes to a sequence of densities
\{g_{n,\nu}\}
. Both the pre- and post-change distribution may depend on the observation time
n
. Besides, the post-change distribution may depend on the change point
\nu
. The densities are assumed to satisfy

D(g_{n,\nu}\;\|\;f_{n})>0,\quad\forall n\geq 1,\;\nu\geq 1,\;n\geq\nu.

We will refer to a process with pre-change densities
\{f_{n}\}
and post-change densities
\{g_{n,\nu}\}
as a non-stationary process since the density of the data evolves or changes with time.

We use
F=\{f_{n}\}
and
G=\{g_{n,\nu}\}
to denote the pre- and post-change sequence of densities, respectively. In our model,
F=\{f_{n}\}
and
G=\{g_{n,\nu}\}
are not known to the decision-maker. However, we assume that there are families of distributions
\{\mathcal{P}^{F}_{n}\}
and
\{\mathcal{P}^{G}_{n,\nu}\}
such that

(f_{n},g_{n,\nu})\in\mathcal{P}^{F}_{n}\times\mathcal{P}^{G}_{n,\nu},\quad n,\nu=1,2,\dots,

and the families
\{\mathcal{P}^{F}_{n}\}
and
\{\mathcal{P}^{G}_{n,\nu}\}
are known to the decision-maker.
We use the notation

\mathcal{F}=\{F=\{f_{n}\}:f_{n}\in\mathcal{P}^{F}_{n},\;n\geq 1\}

to denote the set of all possible pre-change laws, and

\mathcal{G}=\{G=\{g_{n,\nu}\}:g_{n,\nu}\in\mathcal{P}^{G}_{n,\nu},\;n,\nu\geq 1\}

to denote the set of all possible post-change laws.
We use
\mathsf{P}_{\nu}^{F,G}
to denote the law of the observation process
\{X_{n}\}
when the change occurs at
\nu
and the pre-change and post-change laws are given by
F
and
G
, respectively. We use
\mathsf{E}_{\nu}^{F,G}
to denote the corresponding expectation.
The notations
\mathsf{P}_{\infty}^{F,G}=\mathsf{P}^{F}_{\infty}
and
\mathsf{E}_{\infty}^{F,G}=\mathsf{E}^{F}_{\infty}
are used when there is no change (change occurring at
\nu=\infty
), as the post-change law plays no role when the change never occurs.

III-B

Problem Formulation for Single-Stream Robust QCD

In the following, we use Lorden’s criteria (
[
11
]
) to find the best stopping time to detect the change in distribution in non-stationary processes in a robust manner. We define

\text{WADD}^{F,G}(\tau)\coloneqq\sup_{\nu}\;\esssup\;\mathsf{E}_{\nu}^{F,G}\left[(\tau-\nu+1)^{+}|\mathscr{F}_{\nu-1}\right],

(10)

where
\mathscr{F}_{n}=\sigma(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
. This is Lorden’s delay metric when the pre-change law is
F
and the post-change law is
G
.
Since the decision maker is unaware of the exact pre- and post-change laws, we seek a stopping time to minimize the following worst-case version of the detection delay:

\begin{split}\inf_{\tau}\sup_{F\in\mathcal{F},G\in\mathcal{G}}&\text{WADD}^{F,G}(\tau)\\
s.t.\quad&\inf_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\mathsf{E}^{F}_{\infty}[\tau]\geq\frac{1}{\alpha}.\end{split}

(11)

We say that a solution is robust optimal if a stopping time or algorithm is a solution to the problem in (
11
).

III-C

Robust Algorithm for QCD in Non-Stationary Data

We now provide the optimal or asymptotically optimal solution to (
11
) under assumptions on the families of pre-change uncertainty classes
\{\mathcal{P}^{F}_{n}\}
and post-change uncertainty classes
\{\mathcal{P}^{G}_{n,\nu}\}
. We assume in the rest of this section that all densities involved are equivalent to each other (absolutely continuous with respect to each other).

To state the assumptions on
\{\mathcal{P}^{F}_{n}\}
and
\{\mathcal{P}^{G}_{n,\nu}\}
, we need some definitions. We say that a random variable
Z_{2}
is stochastically larger than another random variable
Z_{1}
(and write
Z_{2}\succ Z_{1}
) if

\mathsf{P}(Z_{2}\geq t)\geq\mathsf{P}(Z_{1}\geq t),\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

We now introduce the notion of stochastic boundedness in non-stationary processes.

Definition III.1

(Stochastic Boundedness and Least Favorable Laws (LFLs) in Non-Stationary Processes)
.

We say that the pair of families (
\{\mathcal{P}^{F}_{n}\},\{\mathcal{P}^{G}_{n,\nu}\})
are stochastically bounded by least favorable laws (LFLs)
(\bar{F},\bar{G})=(\{\bar{f}_{n}\},\{\bar{g}_{n,\nu}\})
, with
\bar{f}_{n}\in\mathcal{P}^{F}_{n}
and

\bar{g}_{n,\nu}\in\mathcal{P}^{G}_{n,\nu},\;n,\nu=1,2,\dots,

or that the pair
(\bar{F},\bar{G})
is least favorable if

\begin{split}\bar{f}_{n}&\succ{f}_{n},\quad\text{ for every }\;f_{n}\in\mathcal{P}^{F}_{n},\quad n=1,2,\dots,\end{split}

(12)

and

\begin{split}{g_{n,\nu}}&\succ\bar{g}_{n,\nu},\quad\text{ for every }\;g_{n,\nu}\in\mathcal{P}^{G}_{n,\nu},\quad n,\nu=1,2,\dots.\end{split}

(13)

Remark III.2
.

We note that the conditions above differ from those used, e.g., in
[
26
]
and
[
23
]
because in our case the post-change law depends on the change point.

Given a pair of LFLs, we use the following algorithm for robust QCD:

\displaystyle\bar{W}_{n}=\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{i=k}^{n}\log\frac{\bar{g}_{i,k}(X_{i})}{\bar{f}_{i}(X_{i})},

(14)

with stopping rule

\displaystyle\tau_{ss}=\inf\{n\geq 1:\bar{W}_{n}\geq\bar{A}_{n,\alpha}\}.

(15)

Here thresholds
\bar{A}_{n,\alpha}
are chosen to satisfy the constraint on the mean time to a false alarm. The statistic
\bar{W}_{n}
is the classical CUSUM statistic (
4
) modified for the non-stationary setting and based on the LFLs.

The optimality of
\tau_{ss}
when the true pre- and post-change laws of
\{X_{n}\}
are given by the LFLs has been studied in
[
14
,
2
,
6
,
15
,
16
,
7
,
23
,
31
]
. In these works, it has been shown that under mild assumptions on the non-stationary models
\bar{F}
and
\bar{G}
, the stopping rule
\tau_{ss}
is asymptotically optimal (as
\alpha\to 0
) when the pre- and post-change laws are also given by
\bar{F}
and
\bar{G}
, respectively. In Section
III-E
below, we provide a brief discussion on some standard sufficient conditions on optimality.

In this section, we prove the robust optimality of
\tau_{ss}
for the problem in (
11
).
Specifically, we show in Theorem
III.4
that if the stopping rule
\tau_{ss}
is optimal for (
3
)
when the pre- and post-change laws are
\bar{F}
and

, respectively, then it is robust optimal for the problem in (

). For the theorem, we need the following lemma from (

Lemma III.3

(Stochastic dominance (
[

]
))
.

\{U_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}
is a set of mutually independent random variables, and
\{V_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}

,

for all

,

, and

. Then we have

\displaystyle q(U_{1},U_{2},\dots,U_{n})\succ q(V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{n}).

.

The pair of families (

have a pair of LFLs

as defined in Definition

.

Let

be a constraint on the false alarm. Let the thresholds
\{A_{n,\alpha}\}
used by the stopping rule

be such that

, where

in (

) is the optimal rule designed using the LFL.

(c)

All likelihood ratio functions used in the stopping rule
\tau_{ss}
are continuous and monotone increasing.

Then the following results are true:

in (

\tau_{ss}
in (

) is asymptotically optimal, as

, for the problem in (

) when the pre- and post-change laws are given by the LFLs
\bar{F}
and

, respectively, then the stopping rule is asymptotically robust optimal for the problem in (

On Optimality of the Robust Algorithm under LFLs

One of the main conditions for robust optimality of the stopping rule
\tau_{ss}

In the theorem below, we provide some general conditions on the asymptotic optimality of
\tau_{ss}
under the LFLs. For exact optimality in a non-stationary setting, we refer the readers to
[

]
. For conditions on asymptotic optimality more general than those given in the theorem below, we refer the readers to

,

.

Define

\bar{Z}_{n,\nu}=\log\frac{\bar{g}_{n,\nu}(X_{n})}{\bar{f}_{n}(X_{n})}

to be the log-likelihood ratio at time
n
calculated for the LFLs when the change occurs at
\nu
. The theorem below gives sufficient conditions on the sequence
\{\bar{Z}_{n,\nu}\}_{n,\nu}
for
\tau_{ss}
to be optimal under the LFLs.

Theorem III.5

(
[
16
]
,
[
14
]
)
.

1.

Let there exist a positive number
I
such that the log-likelihood ratios
\{\bar{Z}_{n,\nu}\}
satisfy the following condition: for any
\delta>0
,

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\;\sup_{\nu\geq 1}\;\esssup\mathsf{P}_{\nu}^{\bar{F},\bar{G}}&\left(\max_{t\leq n}\sum_{i=\nu}^{\nu+t}\bar{Z}_{i,\nu}\geq I(1+\delta)n\;\bigg|\;X_{1},\dots,X_{\nu-1}\right)=0.\end{split}

(16)

Then, we have the universal lower bound as
\alpha\to 0
,

\begin{split}\inf_{\tau}\textup{WADD}^{\bar{F},\bar{G}}(\tau)\;\geq\;\frac{|\log\alpha|}{I}(1+o(1)).\end{split}

(17)

Here the minimum over
\tau
is over those stopping times satisfying
\mathsf{E}_{\infty}^{\bar{F}}[\tau]\geq\frac{1}{\alpha}
.

2.

The robust algorithm
\tau_{ss}
in (
15
), when we choose the threshold
\bar{A}_{n,\alpha}=|\log\alpha|
,

\tau_{ss}=\min\left\{n\geq 1:\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{i=k}^{n}\bar{Z}_{i,k}\geq|\log\alpha|\right\},

satisfies

\mathsf{E}_{\infty}^{\bar{F}}[\tau_{ss}]\geq\frac{1}{\alpha}.

3.

Furthermore, if the log-likelihood ratios
\{\bar{Z}_{n,\nu}\}
also satisfy,
\forall\delta>0
,

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\;\sup_{k\geq\nu\geq 1}\;\esssup\mathsf{P}_{\nu}^{\bar{F},\bar{G}}&\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=k}^{k+n}\bar{Z}_{i,k}\leq I-\delta\;\bigg|\;X_{1},\dots,X_{k-1}\right)=0.\end{split}

(18)

Then as
\alpha\to 0
,
\tau_{ss}
achieves the lower bound:

\begin{split}\textup{WADD}^{\bar{F},\bar{G}}(\tau_{ss})\leq\frac{|\log\alpha|}{I}(1+o(1)),\quad\alpha\to 0.\end{split}

(19)

Some special cases and well-studied models where the conditions of the theorem are satisfied are given below.

1.

I.I.D. process
: In the special case when

\bar{f}_{n}=\bar{f},\quad\bar{g}_{n,\nu}=\bar{g},\quad\forall n,\nu,

(20)

the robust algorithm given above is the robust CUSUM algorithm designed using densities
\bar{f}
and
\bar{g}
. All the conditions of the theorem above are satisfied with
I=D(\bar{g}\;\|\;\bar{f})
, the Kullback-Leibler divergence between
\bar{g}
and
\bar{f}
.

2.

I.P.I.D. process
: Consider the case when there exist a positive integer
T
such that

\bar{f}_{n+T}=\bar{f}_{n},\quad\bar{g}_{n,\nu}=\bar{g}_{n},\quad\bar{g}_{n+T}=\bar{g}_{n}.

(21)

A process with this periodic sequence of densities is called an independent and periodically identically distributed process (
[
7
,
23
,
31
]
).
The conditions of Theorem
III.5
are satisfied with

I=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{n=1}^{T}D(\bar{g}_{n}\;\|\;\bar{f}_{n}).

3.

MLR-order processes
:
Finally, consider the case where we have a sequence of densities
\{\bar{h}_{n}\}
such that

\bar{f}_{n+T}=\bar{f},\quad\bar{g}_{n,\nu}=\bar{h}_{n-\nu+1}.

(22)

In addition,
the densities
\{\bar{h}_{n}\}
are increasing in MLR order: for all
n\geq 1
,

\frac{\bar{h}_{n+1}(x)}{\bar{h}_{n}(x)}\quad\uparrow\quad x,

that is, the likelihood ratio is monotonically increasing in
x
. Such a process is called an exploding process or an monotone likelihood ratio (MLR)-order process (
[
16
]
).
The conditions of Theorem
III.5
are satisfied with

I=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}D(\bar{h}_{n}\;\|\;\bar{f}),

provided the above limit exists (
[
16
]
).

III-F

On Performance of the Robust Algorithm under Laws other than LFLs

In this section, we discuss the false alarm and delay performances of the robust algorithm when the pre- and post-change laws are not given by the LFLs. The fact that the robust algorithm is designed using the LFLs helps us obtain bounds on the performances. The proof of the following corollary follows from the proof steps used for Theorem
III.4
.

Corollary III.6
.

Suppose the log-likelihood ratios
\{\bar{Z}_{n,\nu}\}
satisfy the conditions in Theorem
III.5
. Then the robust algorithm
\tau_{ss}
in (
15
), when we choose the threshold
\bar{A}_{n,\alpha}=|\log\alpha|
,

\tau_{ss}=\min\left\{n\geq 1:\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{i=k}^{n}\bar{Z}_{i,k}\geq|\log\alpha|\right\},

satisfies

\begin{split}\mathsf{E}_{\infty}^{F}[\tau_{ss}]&\geq\frac{1}{\alpha},\quad\forall F\in\mathcal{F}.\\
\textup{WADD}^{F,{G}}(\tau_{ss})&\leq\frac{|\log\alpha|}{I}(1+o(1)),\quad\alpha\to 0,\quad\forall F\in\mathcal{F},G\in\mathcal{G}.\end{split}

(23)

Thus, no matter what the pre-change non-stationary law
F
is, if the thresholds of the stopping rule
\tau_{ss}
is set to a constant threshold
|\log\alpha|
, the mean time to a false alarm for
\tau_{ss}
is always lower bounded by
\frac{1}{\alpha}
. Also, the detection delay is asymptotically upper bounded by
\frac{|\log\alpha|}{I}
for any non-stationary pre- and post-change laws
F
and
G
.

III-G

Examples of Least Favorable Laws

We now provide some specific examples of LFLs from the Gaussian and Poisson families.
We use the following simple lemma in these examples. The lemma is stated for densities but a similar result is true also for mass functions. Its proof can be found in (
[
32
]
). We provide the proof for completeness.

Lemma III.7
.

Let
f
and
g
be two probability density functions such that
g
dominated
f
in monotone likelihood ratio (MLR) order:

\frac{g(x)}{f(x)}\;\;\uparrow\;\;x.

Then,
g
also dominates
f
in stochastic order, i.e.,

\int_{x}^{\infty}g(y)dy\geq\int_{x}^{\infty}f(y)dy,\quad\forall x.

Proof.

Define
t=\sup\left\{x:\frac{g(x)}{f(x)}\leq 1\right\}
.
Then, when
x\leq t
,

\int_{-\infty}^{x}g(y)dy\leq\int_{-\infty}^{x}f(y)dy,

implying
\int_{x}^{\infty}g(y)dy\geq\int_{x}^{\infty}f(y)dy.

On the other hand, if
x>t
, then

\int_{x}^{\infty}g(y)dy=\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{g(y)}{f(y)}f(y)dy\geq\int_{x}^{\infty}f(y)dy.

This proves the lemma.
∎

We now give two examples of non-stationary processes with specified uncertainty classes. We then identify their LFLs.

Example 1
.

(Gaussian LFL)

Let the pre- and post-change density be given by

\displaystyle f_{n}=\mathcal{N}(\theta_{n},1),\quad g_{n,\nu}=\mathcal{N}(\mu_{n,\nu},1).

The parameters
\{\theta_{n}\}
and
\{\mu_{n,\nu}\}
are not known but are believed to satisfy for some known
\{\bar{\theta}_{n}\}
and
\{\bar{\mu}_{n,\nu}\}
, respectively,

\displaystyle\mu_{n,\nu}\geq\bar{\mu}_{n,\nu},\quad\bar{\theta}_{n}\geq\theta_{n},\quad\forall n,\text{ and }\forall\nu\leq n,

such that for each
n
and for all
\nu\leq n
,

\bar{\theta}_{n}<\bar{\mu}_{n,\nu}.

The parameters
\{\bar{\theta}_{n}\}
and
\{\bar{\mu}_{n,\nu}\}
are known to the decision-maker.

The following likelihood ratio calculations are straightforward:

\displaystyle\log\frac{\bar{g}_{n,\nu}(X)}{\bar{f}_{n}(X)}

\displaystyle=\left(\bar{\mu}_{n,\nu}-\bar{\theta}_{n}\right)X-\frac{\bar{\mu}_{n,\nu}^{2}-\bar{\theta}_{n}^{2}}{2},

\displaystyle\log\frac{{g}_{n,\nu}(X)}{\bar{g}_{n,\nu}(X)}

\displaystyle=\left(\mu_{n,\nu}-\bar{\mu}_{n,\nu}\right)X-\frac{{\mu}_{n,\nu}^{2}-\bar{\mu}_{n,\nu}^{2}}{2},

\displaystyle\log\frac{\bar{f}_{n}(X)}{f_{n}(X)}

\displaystyle=\left(\bar{\theta}_{n}-\theta_{n}\right)X-\frac{\bar{\theta}_{n}^{2}-\theta_{n}^{2}}{2}.

Note that all the likelihood ratios are monotonically increasing and continuous in
X
, for any choice of
n
and
\nu\leq n
. By Lemma
III.7
, this means that

{g}_{n,\nu}\succ\bar{g}_{n,\nu},\quad\text{ and }\quad\bar{f}_{n}\succ f_{n}.

Thus, the following densities are LFLs:

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{n}=\mathcal{N}(\bar{\theta}_{n},1),\quad\bar{g}_{n,\nu}=\mathcal{N}(\bar{\mu}_{n,\nu},1),\quad n\geq 1,\quad\nu\leq n.

Example 2
.

(Poisson LFL)

Let the pre- and post-change density be given by

\displaystyle f_{n}=\text{Pois}(\gamma_{n}),\quad g_{n,\nu}=\text{Pois}(\lambda_{n,\nu}).

where
\max_{n}\{\gamma_{n}\}\leq\min_{n,\nu}\{\lambda_{n,\nu}\}
.

\{\gamma_{n}\}
and
\{\lambda_{n,\nu}\}
are not known but are believed to satisfy for some known
\{\bar{\gamma}_{n}\}
and
\{\bar{\lambda}_{n,\nu}\}
, respectively,

\displaystyle\lambda_{n,\nu}\geq\bar{\lambda}_{n,\nu},\quad\bar{\gamma}_{n}\geq\gamma_{n},\quad\forall n,\nu,

and for each
n
and
\nu\leq n
,

\bar{\gamma}_{n}<\bar{\lambda}_{n,\nu}.

Then,

\displaystyle\log\frac{\bar{g}_{n,\nu}(X)}{\bar{f}_{n}(X)}

\displaystyle=\log\left[\left(\frac{\bar{\lambda}_{n,\nu}}{\bar{\gamma}_{n}}\right)^{X}e^{-\bar{\lambda}_{n,\nu}+\bar{\gamma}_{n}}\right]=X\log\left(\frac{\bar{\lambda}_{n,\nu}}{\bar{\gamma}_{n}}\right)-\bar{\lambda}_{n,\nu}+\bar{\gamma}_{n},

\displaystyle\log\frac{{g}_{n,\nu}(X)}{\bar{g}_{n,\nu}(X)}

\displaystyle=\log\left[\left(\frac{{\lambda}_{n,\nu}}{\bar{\lambda}_{n,\nu}}\right)^{X}e^{-{\lambda}_{n,\nu}+\bar{\lambda}_{n,\nu}}\right]=X\log\left(\frac{{\lambda}_{n,\nu}}{\bar{\lambda}_{n,\nu}}\right)-{\lambda}_{n,\nu}+\bar{\lambda}_{n,\nu},

\displaystyle\log\frac{\bar{f}_{n}(X)}{f_{n}(X)}

\displaystyle=\log\left[\left(\frac{\bar{\gamma}_{n}}{\gamma_{n}}\right)^{X}e^{-\bar{\gamma}_{n}+\gamma_{n}}\right]=X\log\left(\frac{\bar{\gamma}_{n}}{\gamma_{n}}\right)-\bar{\gamma}_{n}+\gamma_{n}.

Note that because of the assumptions made on the order of the means, all the likelihood ratios are monotonically increasing and continuous in
X
, for any choice of
n
and
\nu\leq n
. By Lemma
III.7
, this means that

{g}_{n,\nu}\succ\bar{g}_{n,\nu},\quad\text{ and }\quad\bar{f}_{n}\succ f_{n}.

Thus, the following densities are LFLs:

\displaystyle\bar{f}_{n}=\text{Pois}(\bar{\gamma}_{n}),\quad\bar{g}_{n,\nu}=\text{Pois}({\bar{\lambda}}_{n,\nu}).\quad n\geq 1,\quad\nu\leq n.

IV
Robust Multi-Stream QCD with Unknown Non-Stationary Pre- and Post-Change Distributions

In this section, we obtain a robust solution to a multi-stream QCD problem where data in each stream is non-stationary, and the pre- and post-change distributions in each stream are not known to the decision-maker. At the change point, it is assumed that the change affects a class of subsets of streams.

IV-A

Multi-Stream Change Point Model and Notations

We first state the change point model and the problem formulation.
Suppose
\{X_{\theta,n}\}
is an independent sequence of random variables observed over time
n
at stream indexed by
\theta\in\Theta=\{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{M}\}
. Let
B\subset\{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{M}\}
.
We assume that changes occur simultaneously in streams indexed by elements of
B
, which is unknown to the decision maker. Specifically, if
\theta\in B
, then the process
\{X_{\theta,n}\}
follows a sequence of densities
\{f_{\theta,n}\}
before the change time
\nu
. After time
\nu
, the process changes to a sequence of densities
\{g_{\theta,n,\nu}\}
.
Mathematically,

X_{\theta,n}\sim\begin{cases}f_{\theta,n},&\quad\forall n<\nu,\\
g_{\theta,n,\nu},&\quad\forall n\geq\nu,\end{cases}\quad\text{if}\quad\theta\in B.

(24)

If
\theta\notin B
, the density sequence of the observations remains equal to
f_{\theta,n}
,

X_{\theta,n}\sim f_{\theta,n},\quad\forall n,\quad\text{if}\quad\theta\notin B.

As in the single-stream case, the densities before and after change are not known to the decision-maker.
While the subset
B\subset\Theta
of streams, where the change occurs, is not known to the decision maker, we assume that there is a known collection
\mathcal{B}
of subsets of
\Theta
such that

B\in\mathcal{B}
. For example, we can have
\mathcal{B}
as the collection of all subsets of size less than a specified
K
:

\mathcal{B}=\{B:|B|\leq K\}.

Typically, we would have
K\ll M.

At the end of this section, we study the special case of
K=1
, i.e., the case where the change affects only one of the streams.

For each
\theta\in\Theta
, the densities are assumed to satisfy

D(g_{\theta,n,\nu}\;\|\;f_{\theta,n})>0,\quad\forall n\geq 1,\;\nu\geq 1,\;n\geq\nu.

We refer to a process with pre-change
\{f_{\theta,n}\}
and post-change
\{g_{\theta,n,\nu}\}
as a non-stationary process, since the density of the data changes with time among streams.

Denote the non-stationary models before and after the change in stream
\theta
as

F^{\theta}=\{f_{\theta,n}\},\quad G^{\theta}=\{g_{\theta,n,\nu}\},\quad\theta\in\Theta,

and the family of pre- and post-change models across the streams as

F=\{F^{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\Theta},\quad\text{ and }\quad G=\{G^{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\Theta}.

While the laws
F
and
G
are not known, we assume that there are known families of densities
\mathcal{P}^{F,\theta}_{n}
and
\mathcal{P}^{G,\theta}_{n,\nu}
such that

f_{\theta,n}\in\mathcal{P}^{F,\theta}_{n},\quad g_{\theta,n,\nu}\in\mathcal{P}^{G,\theta}_{n,\nu},\quad n,\nu=1,2,\dots.

Furthermore, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=\{F=\{F^{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\Theta}:F^{\theta}=\{f_{\theta,n}\},\;f_{\theta,n}\in\mathcal{P}^{F,\theta}_{n},\;n\geq 1,\;\theta\in\Theta\},

(25)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}

\displaystyle=\{G=\{G^{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\Theta}:G^{\theta}=\{g_{\theta,n,\nu}\},\;g_{\theta,n,\nu}\in\mathcal{P}^{G,\theta}_{n,\nu},\;n,\nu\geq 1,\;\theta\in\Theta\}

(26)

to be the collection of all possible pre- and post-change non-stationary process laws across all the streams.

Finally, we use the notation

\mathsf{P}_{\nu}^{F,G,B}

to denote the probability measure such that change occurs at time
\nu
in the subset
B
when the pre- and post-change non-stationary models are
F
and
G
, respectively. Also, let

\mathsf{E}_{\nu}^{F,G,B}
be the corresponding expectation. We use the notations
\mathsf{P}_{\infty}^{F,G,B}=\mathsf{P}^{F}_{\infty}
and
\mathsf{E}_{\infty}^{F,G,B}=\mathsf{E}^{F}_{\infty}
when there is no change.

IV-B

Problem Formulation for Multi-Stream QCD with Non-Stationary Data

Define the Lorden detection delay metric (
[
11
]
) under the new notations as

\text{WADD}^{F,G,B}(\tau)\coloneqq\sup_{\nu}\;\esssup\;\mathsf{E}_{\nu}^{F,G,B}\left[(\tau-\nu+1)^{+}|\mathscr{F}_{\nu-1}\right],

(27)

where
\mathscr{F}_{n}=\sigma(\{X_{\theta,1},\dots,X_{\theta,n}\}_{\theta\in\Theta})
.
When the laws
F
and
G
are known, and streams
B
are also known, the classical Lorden’s formulation for this problem can be stated as (see (
3
)):

\begin{split}\inf_{\tau}\;\;&\textnormal{WADD}^{F,G,B}(\tau),\\
s.t.\;\;\ &\mathsf{E}_{\infty}^{F}[\tau]\geq\frac{1}{\alpha}.\end{split}

(28)

Since the laws
(F,G)
and stream
B
are not exactly known, we study a robust version of the minimax problem for arbitrary
B\in\mathcal{B}
:

\begin{split}\inf_{\tau}\;\;{\sup_{F\in\mathcal{F},G\in\mathcal{G}}}\;\;&\text{WADD}^{F,G,B}(\tau)\\
s.t.\;\;\ &\inf_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\mathsf{E}^{F}_{\infty}[\tau]\geq\frac{1}{\alpha}.\end{split}

(29)

We say that a solution is robust optimal if a stopping time or algorithm is a solution to the problem in (
29
).
As suggested by the formulation, we seek a solution that is robust optimal uniformly across every
B\in\mathcal{B}
.

IV-C

Robust Algorithm for QCD in Multi-Stream Non-Stationary Processes

We provide the asymptotically optimal solution to (
29
) under the assumption that the families of pre-change uncertainty classes
\{\mathcal{P}^{F,\theta}_{n}\}_{n,\theta}
and post-change uncertainty classes
\{\mathcal{P}^{G,\theta}_{n,\nu}\}_{n,\theta}
have LFLs for each
\theta
. The definition of LFLs is similar to Defintion
III.1
, but we provide it here for completeness.

Definition IV.1

(Stochastic Boundedness and Least Favorable Laws (LFLs) in Multi-Stream Non-Stationary Processes)
.

For a fixed
\theta\in\Theta
, we say that the pair of families (
\{\mathcal{P}^{F,\theta}_{n}\},\{\mathcal{P}^{G,\theta}_{n,\nu}\})
is stochastically bounded by the pair of least favorable laws (LFLs)
(\bar{F}^{\theta},\bar{G}^{\theta})=(\{\bar{f}_{\theta,n}\},\{\bar{g}_{\theta,n,\nu}\})
, with
\bar{f}_{\theta,n}\in\mathcal{P}^{F,\theta}_{n}
and

\bar{g}_{\theta,n,\nu}\in\mathcal{P}^{G,\theta}_{n,\nu},\;n,\nu=1,2,\dots,

or that the pair
(\bar{F}^{\theta},\bar{G}^{\theta})
is least favorable if

\begin{split}\bar{f}_{\theta,n}&\succ{f}_{\theta,n},\quad\text{ for every }\;f_{\theta,n}\in\mathcal{P}^{F,\theta}_{n},\quad n=1,2,\dots,\end{split}

(30)

and

\begin{split}g_{\theta,n,\nu}&\succ\bar{g}_{\theta,n,\nu},\quad\text{ for every }\;g_{\theta,n,\nu}\in\mathcal{P}^{G,\theta}_{n,\nu},\quad n,\nu=1,2,\dots.\end{split}

(31)

We also say that the pair
(\bar{F},\bar{G})
are a pair of LFLs, where

\bar{F}=\{\bar{F}^{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\Theta},\quad\text{ and }\quad\bar{G}=\{\bar{G}^{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\Theta},

if the pair
(\bar{F}^{\theta},\bar{G}^{\theta})
is least favorable for each
\theta\in\Theta
.

Given a pair of LFLs
(\bar{F},\bar{G})
, we use them to define our robust multi-stream detection algorithm. This is a cumulative sum algorithm based on generalized likelihood ratio statistic (computed using LFLs) and is given by

\displaystyle\bar{\Psi}_{n}=\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\max_{{B}\in\mathcal{B}}\sum_{\theta\in{B}}\sum_{i=k}^{n}\log\frac{\bar{g}_{\theta,i,k}(X_{\theta,i})}{\bar{f}_{\theta,i}(X_{\theta,i})}.

(32)

The corresponding stopping rule is

\displaystyle\tau_{ms}=\inf\{n\geq 1:\bar{\Psi}_{n}\geq{A_{n,\alpha}}\}.

(33)

Thresholds
A_{n,\alpha}
are chosen to satisfy the constraint on the mean time to a false alarm.

Finally, consider the case
K=1
, where change happens in at most one stream. Then,
\mathcal{B}=\{\{\theta_{1}\},\dots,\{\theta_{M}\}\}
. Therefore, the robust algorithm becomes the following:

\displaystyle\bar{\Phi}_{n}=\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\max_{\theta\in\Theta}\sum_{i=k}^{n}\log\frac{\bar{g}_{\theta,i,k}(X_{\theta,i})}{\bar{f}_{\theta,i}(X_{\theta,i})},

(34)

with a corresponding stopping rule

\displaystyle\tau_{mc}=\inf\{n\geq 1:\bar{\Phi}_{n}\geq{A_{n,\alpha}}\}.

(35)

In Section
IV-E
below, we briefly discuss some standard sufficient conditions needed for the optimality of
\tau_{ms}
(and hence of
\tau_{mc}
) when the true pre- and post-change laws are given by the LFLs.

IV-D

Robust Optimality in a Multi-Stream Non-Stationary Setting

The following Theorem
IV.2
is a multi-stream generalization of Theorem
III.4
.

Theorem IV.2
.

Suppose the following conditions hold:

(a)

The pair of families
\{\mathcal{P}_{n}^{F,\theta}\}_{\theta\in\Theta}
and
\{\mathcal{P}^{G,\theta}_{n,\nu}\}_{\theta\in\Theta}
is stochastically bounded by the LFLs
\bar{F}
and
\bar{G}
, where
\bar{F}=\{\bar{F}^{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\Theta}

\bar{G}=\{\bar{G}^{\theta}\}_{\theta\in\Theta}

(b)

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
be a constraint on the rate of false alarms. Let the thresholds
\{A_{n,\alpha}\}
used by the stopping rule
\tau_{ms}
in (
33
) designed using the LFLs be such that

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{\infty}^{{\bar{F}}}[\tau_{ms}]=\frac{1}{\alpha}.

(36)

(c)

All likelihood ratio functions used in the stopping rule
\tau_{ms}
are continuous and monotone increasing.

Then the following results are true:

If the stopping rule
\tau_{ms}
in (
33
) is exactly optimal to (

), uniformly over

), then the stopping rule is exactly robust optimal for the problem in (

) uniformly over

2.

If the stopping rule
\tau_{ms}
in (

) is asymptotically optimal to (
28
), uniformly over
B\in\mathcal{B}
, when the law is LFL, i.e., when
\mathcal{F}=\{\bar{F}\}
and
\mathcal{G}=\{\bar{G}\}
as defined in (
25
), then the stopping rule is asymptotically robust optimal for the problem in (
29
), uniformly over
B\in\mathcal{B}
, as
\alpha\to 0
.

Proof.

See Appendix
A-B
.

IV-E

On Optimality of the Multi-Stream Robust Algorithm under LFLs

In this section, we provide sufficient conditions under which the multi-stream robust algorithm
\tau_{ms}
is optimal for the LFLs. The conditions are similar (but not identical) to the ones stated for the single-stream case in Theorem
III.5
.

Define

\bar{Z}_{\theta,n,\nu}=\log\frac{\bar{g}_{\theta,n,\nu}(X_{\theta,n})}{\bar{f}_{\theta,n}(X_{\theta,n})}

to be the log-likelihood ratio at time
n
, in stream
\theta
, and calculated for the LFLs when the change occurs at
\nu
. Let
I_{\theta}>0
be the information number (similar to the number
I
in Theorem
III.5
) for stream
\theta
, and let

I_{B}=\sum_{\theta\in B}I_{\theta}.

Theorem IV.3

(
[
16
,
14
,
2
]
)
.

1.

Let there exist a positive numbers
I_{\theta}
,
\theta\in\Theta
, such that the log-likelihood ratios
\{\bar{Z}_{\theta,n,\nu}\}
satisfy the following condition: for any
\delta>0
,

\begin{split}\hskip-31.2982pt\lim_{n\to\infty}\;\sup_{\nu\geq 1}\;\esssup\mathsf{P}_{\nu}^{\bar{F},\bar{G},B}&\left(\max_{t\leq n}\sum_{i=\nu}^{\nu+t}\sum_{\theta\in B}\bar{Z}_{\theta,i,\nu}\geq I_{B}(1+\delta)n\;\bigg|\;X_{\theta,1},\dots,X_{\theta,\nu-1},\theta\in\Theta\right)=0.\end{split}

(37)

Then, we have the universal lower bound as
\alpha\to 0
,

\begin{split}\inf_{\tau}\textup{WADD}^{\bar{F},\bar{G},B}(\tau)\;\geq\;\frac{|\log\alpha|}{I_{B}}(1+o(1)).\end{split}

(38)

Here the minimum over
\tau
is over those stopping times satisfying
\mathsf{E}_{\infty}^{\bar{F}}[\tau]\geq\frac{1}{\alpha}
.

2.

The robust algorithm
\tau_{ms}
in (
33
), when we choose the threshold
\bar{A}_{n,\alpha}=\log\frac{|\mathcal{B}|}{\alpha}
,

\tau_{ms}=\min\left\{n\geq 1:\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\max_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\sum_{i=k}^{n}\sum_{\theta\in B}\bar{Z}_{\theta,i,k}\geq\log\frac{|\mathcal{B}|}{\alpha}\right\},

satisfies

\mathsf{E}_{\infty}^{\bar{F}}[\tau_{ms}]\geq\frac{1}{\alpha}.

3.

Furthermore, if the log-likelihood ratios
\{\bar{Z}_{\theta,n,\nu}\}
also satisfy,
\forall\delta>0
,

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\;\sup_{k\geq\nu\geq 1}\;\esssup\mathsf{P}_{\nu}^{\bar{F},\bar{G},B}&\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=k}^{k+n}\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\bar{Z}_{\theta,i,k}\leq I_{B}-\delta\;\bigg|\;X_{\theta,1},\dots,X_{\theta,k-1},\theta\in\Theta\right)=0.\end{split}

(39)

Then as
\alpha\to 0
,
\tau_{ms}
achieves the lower bound:

\begin{split}\textup{WADD}^{\bar{F},\bar{G},B}(\tau_{ms})\leq\frac{|\log\alpha|}{I_{B}}(1+o(1)),\quad\alpha\to 0.\end{split}

(40)

All the examples given in Section
III-E
will work for the multi-stream case as well. For further examples, we refer to
[
2
,
6
]
.

IV-F

On Performance of the Multi-Stream Robust Algorithm under Laws other than LFLs

The following result obtains bounds on the mean time to a false alarm and detection delay of the stopping rule
\tau_{ms}
when the pre- and post-change laws are not necessarily the pair of LFLs.

Corollary IV.4
.

Suppose the log-likelihood ratios
\{\bar{Z}_{\theta,n,\nu}\}
satisfy the conditions in Theorem
IV.3
. Then the multi-stream robust algorithm
\tau_{ms}
in (
33
), when we choose the threshold
\bar{A}_{n,\alpha}=\log\frac{|\mathcal{B}|}{\alpha}
,

\tau_{ms}=\min\left\{n\geq 1:\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\max_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\sum_{i=k}^{n}\sum_{\theta\in B}\bar{Z}_{\theta,i,k}\geq\log\frac{|\mathcal{B}|}{\alpha}\right\},

satisfies for all
B\in\mathcal{B}
,

\begin{split}\mathsf{E}_{\infty}^{F}[\tau_{ms}]&\geq\frac{1}{\alpha},\quad\forall F\in\mathcal{F}.\\
\textup{WADD}^{F,{G},B}(\tau_{ms})&\leq\frac{|\log\alpha|}{I_{B}}(1+o(1)),\quad\alpha\to 0,\quad\forall F\in\mathcal{F},G\in\mathcal{G}.\end{split}

(41)

VI
Conclusion

We developed optimal robust algorithms for the quickest detection of changes in multi-stream non-stationary processes. We allowed for the possibility of unknown distributions both before and after the change in each stream. Our robust algorithms are based on the notion of the least favorable distribution or law (LFL). Our definition of LFL is different from the definitions used in classical QCD literature because we also allow our post-change statistical model to depend on the change point. Because of this dependence, we also need a stronger notion of monotone likelihood ratios to prove optimality. Thus, we provide a novel set
of sufficient conditions for robust optimality in a multi-stream non-stationary setting. We also showed that these sufficient conditions are satisfied by Gaussian or Poisson count multi-stream data, and then used our algorithm for detecting changes in COVID and flight data.

\begin{split}\mathsf{E}_{k}^{\bar{F},\bar{G},B}\left[(\tau_{ms}-k+1)^{+}|\mathscr{F}_{k-1}\right]&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(2b)}}{{=}}\mathsf{E}_{k}^{F,\bar{G},B}\left[(\tau_{ms}-k+1)^{+}|\mathscr{F}_{k-1}\right]\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(2c)}}{{\geq}}\mathsf{E}_{k}^{F,{G},B}\left[(\tau_{ms}-k+1)^{+}|\mathscr{F}_{k-1}\right].\end{split}

(70)

The justification for (2b) is similar to that given in the single-stream case. Thus, we only need to establish the the inequality (2c). This is because if we have (2c), then we have the following series of inequalities:

\begin{split}\mathsf{E}_{k}^{\bar{F},\bar{G},B}\left[(\tau_{ms}-k+1)^{+}|\mathscr{F}_{k-1}\right]&\geq\mathsf{E}_{k}^{F,{G},B}\left[(\tau_{ms}-k+1)^{+}|\mathscr{F}_{k-1}\right],\;\forall F,{G}\\
\text{WADD}^{\bar{F},\bar{G},B}(\tau_{ms})&\geq\text{WADD}^{F,G,B}(\tau_{ms}),\;\forall F,{G}\\
\text{WADD}^{\bar{F},\bar{G},B}(\tau_{ms})&\geq\sup_{F\in\mathcal{F},G\in\mathcal{G}}\text{WADD}^{F,G,B}(\tau_{ms}).\end{split}

(71)

This gives us the core statement (
69
) because
\bar{F}\in\mathcal{F}
, and
\bar{G}\in\mathcal{G}
.

Thus, the proof is completed if we prove (2c) in (
70
).
For it, we need to show that for every integer
N\geq 0
, and all
G\in\mathcal{G}
and
k\geq 1
,

\begin{split}\mathsf{P}_{k}^{F,\bar{G},B}\left[(\tau_{ms}-k+1)^{+}>N\mid\mathscr{F}_{k-1}\right]\geq\mathsf{P}_{k}^{F,G,B}\left[(\tau_{ms}-k+1)^{+}>N\mid\mathscr{F}_{k-1}\right].\end{split}

(72)

We prove this inequality for
N=0
and
N\geq 1
separately.
For
N=0
, by (
61
) and event
\{\tau_{ms}\leq k-1\}
is
\mathscr{F}_{k-1}
-measurable, both sides of the inequality (
72
) are equal to the indicator of the event
\{(\tau_{ms}-k+1)^{+}>N\}
.

For
N\geq 1
, we show the equivalent statement of (
72
) for
k\geq 1
and all
G\in\mathcal{G}
,

\begin{split}\mathsf{P}_{k}^{F,\bar{G},B}\left[(\tau_{ms}-k+1)^{+}\leq N|\mathscr{F}_{k-1}\right]&\leq\mathsf{P}_{k}^{F,G,B}\left[(\tau_{ms}-k+1)^{+}\leq N|\mathscr{F}_{k-1}\right].\end{split}

(73)

By the equivalence in (
62
), we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{k}^{F,\bar{G},B}\left[(\tau_{ms}-k+1)^{+}\leq N\mid\mathscr{F}_{k-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathsf{P}_{k}^{F,\bar{G},B}\left[\tau_{ms}\leq k+N-1\mid\mathscr{F}_{k-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathsf{P}_{k}^{F,\bar{G},B}\left[l(\{X_{\theta,1},\dots,X_{\theta,k+N-1}\}_{\theta\in\Theta})\;\geq\;0\mid\mathscr{F}_{k-1}\right],

where the function
l(\{z_{\theta,1},\dots,z_{\theta,k+N-1}\}_{\theta\in\Theta})
is given by

l(\{z_{\theta,1},\dots,z_{\theta,k+N-1}\}_{\theta\in\Theta})=\max_{1\leq n\leq N}\left(\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\max_{B\in\mathcal{B}}\left(\sum_{\theta\in B}\sum_{i=k}^{n}\log\frac{\bar{g}_{\theta,i,k}(z_{\theta,i})}{\bar{f}_{\theta,i}(z_{\theta,i})}\right)-{{A}_{n,\alpha}}\right).

This gives us

\begin{split}\mathsf{P}_{k}^{F,\bar{G},B}&\left[(\tau_{ms}-k+1)^{+}\leq N\mid\mathscr{F}_{k-1}\right]\\
=\ &\mathsf{P}_{k}^{F,\bar{G},B}\left[\tau_{ms}\leq k+N-1\mid\mathscr{F}_{k-1}\right]\\
=\ &\mathsf{P}_{k}^{F,\bar{G},B}\left[l(\{X_{\theta,1},\dots,X_{\theta,k+N-1}\}_{\theta\in\Theta})\geq 0\mid\mathscr{F}_{k-1}\right]\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(2d)}}{{\leq}}\ &\mathsf{P}_{k}^{F,{G},B}\left[l(\{X_{\theta,1},\dots,X_{\theta,k+N-1}\}_{\theta\in\Theta})\geq 0\mid\mathscr{F}_{k-1}\right]\\
=\ &\mathsf{P}_{k}^{F,{G},B}\left[\tau_{ms}\leq k+N-1\mid\mathscr{F}_{k-1}\right]\\
=\ &\mathsf{P}_{k}^{F,{G},B}\left[(\tau_{ms}-k+1)^{+}\leq N\mid\mathscr{F}_{k-1}\right].\end{split}

(74)

Inequality
(2d)
is true, because the conditioning on the change point and the past realization
\mathscr{F}_{k-1}
fixes the first
k-1
coordinates of
(\{X_{\theta,1},\dots,X_{\theta,k+N-1}\}_{\theta\in\Theta})
for each
\theta
. For
\theta\notin B
, the law remains unchanged. For
\theta\in B
where changes occur, the rest of the coordinates are independent, with their law being
\bar{G}
to the left of inequality, and
G
to the right.
Inequality
(2d)
then follows from Lemma (
III.3
), the fact that
(\bar{F},\bar{G})
are LFLs, the fact that finite number of maximum and sum operations preserve continuity, and the monotone and continuous likelihood ratio assumptions stated in the theorem statement.

This proves (
73
), (
72
) and hence (
70
).

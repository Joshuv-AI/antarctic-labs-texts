Risk factors of significant intraoperative blood loss in unilateral expansive open-door cervical laminoplasty for cervical compressive myelopathy: A retrospective observational study
Authors: Hailong Yu, Hong Yuan, Zhihao Zhang, Xinyuan Zhao, Bin Zheng, Xiaoyu Wang, Yin Hu, Hongwei Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To investigate the independent risk factors of significant intraoperative blood loss (SIBL) in cervical spondylotic myelopathy (CSM) patients who receive unilateral expansive open-door cervical laminoplasty (UEOCL) treatment. UEOCL is a representative method for surgically treating CSM, while SIBL (with an IBL ≥ 500 mL) cannot be completely avoided. The 341 patients who received UEOCL for CSM treatment at our hospital from 2013 to 2019 fell into the SIBL group (n = 37) and the non-SIBL group (n = 304). We retrospectively recorded demographic, surgical and radiographic data for the investigation of the independent risk factors related to SIBL. Statistical analysis relied on R software. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses served for more deeply examining the significance of these factors. CSM patients presented an incidence of SIBL of 10.9% (37/341) during UEOCL. According to a multivariate analysis, duration of operation ≥ 150 minutes (OR, 2.598; 95% CI, 1.136–5.942; P = .024), preoperative FIB (OR, 0.480; 95% CI, 0.245–0.941; P = .033) and surgical-level OPLL (OR, 2.668; 95% CI, 1.235–5.765; P = .013) could independently indicate the risk of SIBL. As for patients with SIBL, an obvious increase was not detected in the postoperative drainage (P = .636), the length of stay (P = .392) and the hospitalization cost (P = .389) but it was in the frequency of blood transfusions (P < .001). Duration of operation ≥ 150 minutes, preoperative FIB and surgical-level OPLL were independent risk factors for SIBL. Patients with SIBL had no significant more postoperative drainage, longer length of stay and higher hospitalization costs, but higher frequency of blood transfusion.

1. Introduction

Unilateral expansive open-door cervical laminoplasty (UEOCL) is a representative surgical option for treating multilevel cervical spondylotic myelopathy (CSM).[1-6] The UEOCL is a relatively concise procedure and exhibits a lower long-term incidence of postoperative complications.[1,6] According to some studies, anterior cervical discectomy and fusion (ACDF) could reduce the intraoperative blood loss (IBL) and more effectively preserve cervical lordosis, whereas laminoplasty could contribute to wider cervical motion range.[4,7] However, other systematic reviews have indicated that anterior decompression with fusion is capable of better preserving the postoperative cervical lordosis versus laminoplasty, and is accompanied by a higher incidence of operative complications.[8,9]

Significant intraoperative blood loss (SIBL), that is IBL ≥ 500 mL which was defined according to previous study,[10-12] possibly causes hypotension, tissue and organ hypoxia, and nerve ischemic injury, greatly prolonging the recovery time and elevating the morbidity and incidence of operative complication.[10,12] There are surgical and individual factors causing perioperative blood loss, including female sex, smoking, low preoperative Hb level, higher American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) classification, longer time of operation, lower body mass index (BMI), and older age.[13-18] SIBL in the process of cervical laminoplasty may be accompanied by a larger number of neurologic complications, and in this case the nervous system can’t be well recovered and the length of stay will be prolonged.[19,20]

Despite the certain safety and less invasiveness, cervical laminoplasty surgery is accompanied by the occasional occurrence of SIBL.[19,20] Independent risk factors pertaining to SIBL or hidden blood loss related to cervical laminoplasty have been well discussed in previous studies,[19-23] and the prediction indexes of such blood loss include the Pavlov ratio,[19] ossification of posterior longitudinal ligament (OPLL),[19,20] and number of complete hinge fractures,[19] an occupying ratio ≥ 60%,[20] space relation of the K-line and osteophyte,[21] thick posterior cervical soft tissue[22] and surgical time.[23] Nevertheless, above studies only covered a small sample size, and did not discuss the relationship between the duration of limb symptoms, technique (lateral mass screws or centerpiece) and SIBL.

Because of the current blood shortage, blood preparation is very difficult, but there are still a few patients who experience SIBL during UEOCL surgery, and the lack of blood will seriously affect the effectiveness of patient treatment. The determination of SIBL-related independent risk factors before surgery can guarantee the identification of the specific patients who should be intensively cared in advance and the adoption of corresponding measures.[24,25] On these accounts, 341 patients who received UEOCL for CSM treatment at hour hospital were enrolled in the study for analysis. The study held the objectives of calculating the SIBL incidence of CSM patients during UEOCL; comparing patients with or without SIBL in terms of their demographic information and indications of operation; and examining SIBL related risk factors.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Methods

2.1.1. Study population

The investigation retrospectively reviewed clinical records of 384 consecutive individuals who had received UEOCL for CSM during January 2013 and December 2019. All procedures were conducted at a tertiary referral center under standardized protocols mandating postoperative drainage placement. Ethical approval (Y-2025-235) was obtained from the Northern Theater Command General Hospital’s Institutional Review Board in accordance with Helsinki Declaration principles. Given the anonymized retrospective design, informed consent requirements were waived per IRB regulations. Eligibility required: UEOCL implementation for CSM management and hospitalization within the specified timeframe. Exclusion parameters encompassed: revision cervical procedures (anterior/posterior approach); intraoperative cerebrospinal fluid leakage (CFL); and incomplete perioperative documentation. Following screening, 341 cases met final inclusion criteria (Fig. 1). All the surgeons are doctors with more than 10 years of experience in spine surgery.

Flow diagram of study subjects. CFL = cerebrospinal fluid leakage, CSM = cervical spondylotic myelopathy, IBL = intraoperative blood loss, SIBL = significant intraoperative blood loss, UEOCL = unilateral expansive open-door cervical laminoplasty.

2.1.2. Data collection and evaluation

Clinical documentation and radiographic data underwent systematic evaluation. Extracted variables encompassed demographic parameters such as age, gender, tobacco use, anthropometric indices such as BMI, ASA physical status classification, and preprocedural hematological profiles such as red blood count (RBC), hemoglobin (Hb), hematocrit (HCT), albumin (ALB), coagulation metrics such as international normalized ratio (INR), thrombin time (TT), preoperative prothrombin time (PT), activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT), fibrinogen (FIB), alongside preexisting comorbidities (diabetes mellitus, cardiopulmonary pathologies, essential hypertension). Hemodynamic measurements included baseline and postoperative systolic pressures (SP) and diastolic pressures (DP). Details of the operations were recorded, and total intraoperative blood loss was calculated as the sum of blood in suction containers (saline irrigation was subtracted from suction containers) and soaked sponges (gravimetric). Operative parameters encompassed technique variations (lateral mass screw stabilization with suture anchors versus centerpiece application), procedure duration, intraoperative hemorrhage, pathological findings (OPLL at operated segments, spinal cord injury), and outcome measures (drainage quantity, hospitalization duration, hospitalization costs). Hospitalization costs refer to the total expenses which include examination fees, laboratory test fees, surgical fees, surgical material fees, implant fees, nursing fees and medication fees incurred by a patient from admission to discharge.

2.1.3. Surgical techniques

Following general anesthesia, the patient was positioned prone using a prone positioning head support. The neck was held in slight flexion, allowing for better surgical exposure. The surgical procedure was referred to previously reported literature.[19] Through a midline incision, the spinous processes, laminae, and medial facet joints from C3 to C6 were exposed. The side with more symptoms was chosen as the open side. On the open side, the lamina was completely cut to create a trough using a high-speed burr. On the other side, an incomplete fracture hinge was created at the same location. Then the lamina was carefully opened by using the hinge as a pivot. Operative parameters encompassed technique variations (lateral mass screw stabilization, open-door cervical laminoplasty with suture fixation versus open-door cervical laminoplasty with centerpiece application).[26] We did not use hemostatic drugs such as aminocaproic acid and hemocoagulase before or during the operations. We did not apply controlled hypotension during anesthesia. During the anesthesia, we controlled the blood pressure within ±20% of the baseline value, and the systolic pressure was >90 mm Hg and the diastolic pressure was >60 mm Hg.

2.1.4. Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses relied on R software (v4.2.3; R Core Team, 2024, MathSoft Company, Vienna, Austria). Normality assumptions were verified via Shapiro–Wilk testing for continuous parameters. Comparative analyses employed Student t tests for the comparison of variables exhibiting a normal distribution and Mann–Whitney U tests for nonparametric comparisons. Descriptive statistics expressed data with normal distribution as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and data with non-normal distribution as median values with interquartile ranges (IQR). Analysis of the categorical variables was conducted via χ2 or Fisher exact tests based on expected frequencies. We converted some continuous variables such as age, BMI, ASA, operation time into categorical ones with specific cutoffs based on previous studies[10,11,26] to facilitate comparison with previous studies. All parameters with a P value < .05 in univariate analysis were considered for multivariable logistic regression to identify independent risk factors for SIBL. For all inferential analyses, P value < .05 was considered as a significant threshold.

2.2.3. Risk factors of SIBL

A multivariate analysis confirmed duration of operation ≥ 150 minutes (OR, 2.598; 95% CI, 1.136–5.942; P = .024), preoperative FIB (OR, 0.480; 95% CI, 0.245–0.941; P = .033) and surgical-level OPLL (OR, 2.668; 95% CI, 1.235–5.765; P = .013) as factors independently predicting the risk of SIBL, as detailed in Table 3. A multivariate analysis showed that patients with duration of operation ≥ 150 minutes were at a 2.598-fold higher risk of SIBL, patients with surgical-level OPLL were at a 2.668-fold higher risk of SIBL. The normal reference value of FIB is between 1.8 and 3.5 g/L. The levels of FIB among all patients were all over 1.8 g/L, 97 patients (31.9%) presented with higher level FIB (over 3.5 g/L) in the non-SIBL group, 5 patients (13.5%) presented with higher level FIB (over 3.5 g/L) in the SIBL group. The lower the FIB level, the higher the risk of SIBL.

3. Discussion

Duration of operation ≥ 150 minutes, preoperative FIB and surgical-level OPLL can be used to independently predict the risk of SIBL. For patients with SIBL, an obvious increase was observed not in postoperative drainage, length of stay and hospitalization costs, but in the frequency of blood transfusions. Hence, when surgeons do the UEOCL surgery on these patients, surgeons should make adequate preparations for blood transfusion before surgery as well as adopt an appropriate hemostatic protocol during operation. Patients should be informed of the possible complication preoperatively. We will have a more detailed and in-depth discussion in the following sections.

3.1. Effect of SIBL on complications

The current study defines SIBL as blood loss ≥ 500 mL.[10-12] Here, the rate of SIBL was 10.9%. Multivariate analysis revealed that overall complications were associated with preoperative narcotic use and higher IBL.[12] SIBL during cervical laminoplasty, especially for OPLL, may result in more neurologic complications, worse neurologic recovery, and longer length of stay.[19,20] Laminoplasty for OPLL is associated with a risk of major IBL, which can potentially give rise to devastating postoperative complications.[20] When surgically treating CSM, older age, longer operative time, and the adoption of combined anterior–posterior procedures can increase the incidence of complications.[27] IBL appeared to be associated with myocardial injury after noncardiac surgery.[28] About 1 of every 3 postsurgical inpatients suffering perioperative bleeding would die during hospitalization or be readmitted within half a year. It is necessary to lower the perioperative bleeding for the improvement of the prognosis after noncardiac surgery.[29]

Intraoperative blood transfusion for the high-risk population could lead to lower postoperative mortality.[24,25] The complication rate can be minimized due to the operative time reduction under anesthesia, blood loss decline, and perioperative fluid management.[30] Preoperative anemia, perioperative bleeding, and red blood cell transfusion can yield a higher mortality at long-term follow-up after noncardiac surgery. All patients are suggested to consider the adoption of anemia and bleeding minimization strategies, such as restrictive transfusion strategy.[31] In the SIBL group, 9 patients (24.3%) received blood transfusions, all of which were administered intraoperatively. In the control group, 8 patients (2.6%) required blood transfusions, with 5 patients (62.5%) transfused intraoperatively. Since most transfusions were given intraoperatively, transfusion reactions could be monitored during the postoperative recovery period without the need for additional hospital stay for observation. Consequently, length of stay (LOS) was not significantly increased.

3.2. Innovations in the study

According to previous study, predictors of unplanned thrombin-gelatin matrix use in spine surgery were female gender, ASA grade ≥ 2, cervical spine, tumor, durotomy, posterior approach, osteotomy, instrumentation, and micro-endoscopy.[32] Despite the certain safety and less invasiveness, cervical laminoplasty surgery is accompanied by the occasional occurrence of SIBL.[19,20] Independent risk factors pertaining to SIBL or hidden blood loss have been well discussed in previous studies,[19-23] and the prediction indexes of such blood loss include the Pavlov ratio,[19] OPLL,[19,20] and number of complete hinge fractures,[19] an occupying ratio ≥ 60%,[20] space relation of the K-line and osteophyte,[21] the thick posterior cervical soft tissue[22] and surgical time.[23] However, these studies included a small number of patients.

To our knowledge, few reports have discussed the incidence and risk factors for SIBL during UEOCL. Compared with previous studies, our study takes into account preoperative radiological factors, blood pressure, preoperative blood index, duration of limb symptoms, technique (lateral mass screws or centerpiece), etc.

3.3. Risk factors for SIBL

The study confirms duration of operation ≥ 150 minutes, preoperative FIB and surgical-level OPLL as factors independently predicting the risk of SIBL. Our previous study using the same data identified male gender, younger age (below 60 years), elevated preoperative diastolic blood pressure, and lateral mass screw fixation as independent predictors of increased surgical drain output post-UEOCL.[26] So, we can see that the risk factors for SIBL and increased surgical drain output are different. According to previous study, surgical time independently predicts HBL or SIBL.[11,23] Duration of operation ≥ 150 minutes was at 8.899-fold higher risk of SIBL during anterior cervical decompression and fusion.[11] In the current study, duration of operation ≥ 150 minutes was at 2.598-fold higher risk of SIBL during cervical laminoplasty. Since a longer time of being in a prone position causes the change of intra-abdominal pressure and adds the epidural venous bleeding, it is required to make the abdomen suspended for the avoidance of pressure generation. Furthermore, meticulous surgical technique is essential to minimize soft tissue injury and bleeding. Surgeons should thoroughly review the surgical plan preoperatively to anticipate potential risk factors, thereby avoiding prolonged operative times and ensuring effective management of perioperative blood transfusion.

Preoperative FIB level also serves for the independent prediction of the risk of HBL.[33] The present study confirmed the increase in SIBL with low preoperative FIB level. The levels of FIB among all patients were all over 1.8 g/L, 31.9% patients presented with higher level FIB (over 3.5 g/L) in the control group, but only 13.5% patients presented with higher level FIB (over 3.5 g/L) in the SIBL group. FIB is an important protein of the blood coagulation system, and its level change reflects patients’ coagulation status. Therefore, FIB detection may help to monitor patients’ coagulation function during surgery, and assist doctors in the management and decision-making of intraoperative bleeding. The SIBL after surgery of patients who have a lot of high-risk factors shall be closely monitored.

The spinal stenosis degree could partially affect the IBL during spinal surgery. SIBL and hidden blood loss related to laminoplasty could be predicted by the Pavlov ratio,[19] OPLL,[19,20] an occupying ratio ≥ 60%,[20] space relation of the K-line and osteophyte.[21] In the current study, surgical-level OPLL was one of these independent risk factors for SIBL, patients with surgical-level OPLL were at a 2.668-fold higher risk of SIBL, which was consistent with previous study showing that OPLL were at a 2.677-fold higher risk of SIBL during cervical laminoplasty.[19] UEOCL-related IBL primarily results from the injury to the external and internal venous plexus as well as its intervening branches. Serious spinal canal stenosis is capable of obstructing the blood reflux and elevating the local venous blood pressure, thereby causing epidural vein distension.[20] Besides, serious spinal canal compression possibly enhances the epidural adhesion. Hence, in the process of manipulating the lamina, local epidural veins could be more easily injured and these injured vascular structures can be hardly coagulated or managed.[19] Spinal orthopedic surgeons are suggested to carefully examine the surgical plan before surgery as well as predict possible risks, accordingly taking measures to lower SIBL in advance, and preparing for the intraoperative allogeneic or autologous blood transfusions for patients suffering SIBL.

4. Limitations

There are primarily many limitations here. The retrospective study is restricted to a small sample size in a specific hospital, hence the possibility of false positives and selection bias cannot be neglected; the study only examines how SIBL impacts the postoperative complication incidence during hospitalization, while the follow-up rate is deficient and the follow-data data are incomplete; preoperative routine coagulation indexes are examined only once, and long-term follow-up is not conducted; predictive model were not built in the current study and multicollinearity checks (variance inflation factor) or model performance metrics (Hosmer–Lemeshow test, ROC curve/AUC) were not done; the total intraoperative blood loss was calculated as the sum of blood in suction containers and soaked sponges, the method was inferior to the standard gravimetric method; we didn’t discuss any further details of possible reasons for these alterations of FIB; and there are multiple unexplored potential interactions between risk factors such as surgical duration and OPLL. On these accounts, multicenter studies that cover a large sample scale shall be conducted for more deeply assessing SIBL related risk factors, thus more robust conclusions can be obtained.

5. Conclusions

Duration of operation ≥ 150 minutes, preoperative FIB and surgical-level OPLL could be used for independently predicting the risk of SIBL. SIBL patients failed to present an obvious increase in postoperative drainage, length of stay and hospitalization cost. The study only covers a small sample size; hence the findings shall be substantiated in future studies.

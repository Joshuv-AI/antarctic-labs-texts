Abstract
. We investigate the local and global well-posedness of the kinetic derivative nonlinear Schrödinger equation(KDNLS) on ℝ {\mathbb{R}} , described by i ​ ∂ t u + ∂ x 2 u = i ​ α ​ ∂ x ( | u | 2 ​ u ) + i ​ β ​ ∂ x ( ℋ ⁡ ( | u | 2 ) ​ u ) , i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u=i\alpha\partial_{x}(|u|^{2}u)+i\beta\partial_{x}(\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})u), where α , β ∈ ℝ \alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R} , and ℋ \mathcal{H} represents the Hilbert transformation. For KDNLS, the L 2 L^{2} norm of a solution is decreasing (resp. increasing, conserved) when β \beta is negative (resp. positive, zero). Focusing on the Sobolev spaces H 2 H^{2} and H 2 ∩ H 1 , 1 H^{2}\cap H^{1,1} , we establish local well-posedness via the energy method combined with gauge transformations to address resonant interactions in both cases of negative and positive β \beta . For the dissipative case β < 0 \beta<0 , we further demonstrate global well-posedness by deriving an a priori bound in H 2 H^{2} .



1.
Introduction

We consider the following one-dimensional nonlinear Schrödinger equation with local and non-local cubic derivative nonlinear terms:

\left\{\begin{aligned} i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u&=i\alpha\partial_{x}\big[|u|^{2}u\big]+i\beta\partial_{x}\big[\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})u\big],\qquad t>0,~x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)&=\phi(x),\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
are constants and
\mathcal{H}
is the Hilbert transformation defined by

\mathcal{H}f(x):=\frac{1}{\pi}p.v.\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{f(y)}{x-y}\,dy=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[-i\,\mathrm{sgn}(\xi)\mathcal{F}f\right](x).

We may focus on the positive time direction by considering time reflection
u(t,x)\mapsto\overline{u(-t,-x)}
, which leaves the equation invariant except that
\beta
needs to be replaced with
-\beta
.

The equation (
1.1
) with
\beta\neq 0
was derived in
[
7
,
8
]
as a model for the propagation of weakly nonlinear and weakly dispersive Alfvén waves in a collision-less plasma.
The non-local nonlinear term
\partial_{x}[\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})u]
representing the effect of resonant particles is obtained using a kinetic model, and thus we refer to (
1.1
) with
\beta\neq 0
as the
kinetic derivative nonlinear Schrödinger equation
(KDNLS).
Formally the
L^{2}
norm of a solution
u
to (
1.1
) satisfies the following identity:

\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\ =\ \beta\big\|D_{x}^{\frac{1}{2}}(|u(t)|^{2})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where
D_{x}:=|\partial_{x}|=\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}
.
In particular, the equation becomes dissipative when
\beta<0
, whereas both cases of negative and positive
\beta
appear to be physically relevant.

We recall previous results on the well-posedness of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) for KDNLS in Sobolev spaces.
Note that KDNLS is invariant under the scaling transformation

u(t,x)~\mapsto~\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}u(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x)\qquad(\lambda>0),

which gives the scale-critical space
L^{2}
.
The case
\beta=0
(i.e., the standard derivative NLS) has been very well studied; for a survey of the previous results in this case, we refer to the recent work by Harrop-Griffiths,
Killip
,
Ntekoume
, and Vişan
[
2
]
, who obtained the global well-posedness in
L^{2}
using complete integrability.
Compared to the case
\beta=0
, many techniques are not equally applicable and much less results are known for KDNLS.
Even the construction of (local) smooth solutions by the classical energy method is not straightforward and seems to require dissipative sign
\beta<0
(see
[
5
, Appendix]
for a proof in
H^{s}
,
s>3/2
).
Rial
[
10
]
constructed global
L^{2}
-weak solutions to (
1.1
) with
\beta<0
and showed smoothing effect by using dissipative structure.
Peres de Moura and Pastor
[
9
]
made an important observation on the applicability of Kato smoothing and maximal function estimates to the non-local nonlinearity.

actually claimed small-data local well-posedness in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})
for
s>1/2
with
both signs of
\beta
.
However, as pointed out in

, their proof seems to have several gaps, especially in the estimates of an anti-derivative term in
[
9
, Eq. (3.25)]
.
We do not know how to fix it for now.

Recently, the first author and Tsutsumi
[

proved local well-posedness in
H^{s}({\mathbb{R}})
for
s>1
under the presence of dissipation
\beta<0
by using short-time
U^{2}
and
V^{2}
spaces.
In
[

]
they also revealed that the nature of the equation changes dramatically under periodic boundary conditions: the dissipative structure makes the equation of parabolic type through the resonant frequency interactions.
Using the parabolic smoothing effect for periodic domain
{\mathbb{T}}
, they proved global well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
,
s>1/4
in the case
\beta<0

[

and non-existence of solutions for
s>3/2
in the case
\beta>0

[

.
See
[

for more discussion on comparison of derivative NLS and KDNLS, methods of proof of well-posedness, and related results.

In this paper, we aim to address the local and global well-posedness of (
1.1
) in
H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}
as well as in
H^{2}
.
Here, we define the weighted Sobolev space
H^{s,m}
by the norm

\|f\|_{H^{s,m}}:=\|\left\langle x\right\rangle^{m}\left\langle\partial_{x}\right\rangle^{s}f\|_{L^{2}},

where

\left\langle x\right\rangle:=(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\left\langle\partial_{x}\right\rangle^{s}:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{s}\mathcal{F}.

Now, we introduce the first theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(Local well-posedness in
H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}
)
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
, then the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) is locally well-posed in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap H^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})
in the following sense:
For any
\phi\in H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}
, there exist
T=T(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})>0
and a solution
u\in C([0,T],H^{2}\cap H^{1,1})
of (
1.1
) on
[0,T]
.
This is the unique solution in the class
C([0,T],H^{2})
.
Moreover, the solution map
\phi\mapsto u
from
H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}
to
C([0,T],H^{2}\cap H^{1,1})
is continuous.
Furthermore, if in addition
\phi\in H^{k}\cap H^{k-1,1}
for some integer
k\geq 3
, then
u\in L^{\infty}([0,T],H^{k}\cap H^{k-1,1})\cap C([0,T],H^{k-1}\cap H^{k-2,1})
.

It is clear from the proof below that the same local well-posedness result holds in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
To the best of our knowledge, Theorem
1.1
is the first result on (large-data) well-posedness for KDNLS with
\beta>0
.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we employ the energy method used in the dissipative case
\beta<0
(see
[
5
, Appendix]
) but combine it with the gauge transformation technique developed for the periodic problem, to deal with the case
\beta>0
.
Our proof begins with proving a priori estimates for regularized equations of (
1.1
).
The most delicate resonance in the energy estimates arises from the nonlinear term
\mathcal{H}(\overline{u}\partial_{x}u)u
.
As observed in the previous result for periodic domain
[
5
]
, this type of resonance can be effectively removed by introducing frequency-restricted gauge transformations.
While the method of gauge transformation has been previously used in the context of energy estimates for derivative NLS with general nonlinearities (see, e.g.,
[
1
]
), our gauge transformations are different in that they need to be defined separately for positive- and negative-frequency parts of the solution.
We also note that simpler gauge transformations compared to those in
[
5
]
turn out to be sufficient for the energy bounds.
Then, thanks to the gauge transformations, this allows us to use the normal form approach (or in other words, modified energy method) appropriately in the energy estimates to deal with the derivative losses.
The processes for
H^{2}
bounds and weighted bounds are not significantly different; however, the commutative relations between the operator

J(t):=x+2it\partial_{x}

and other operators are utilized to handle terms with weight more carefully.
After we prove the a priori bounds for regularized equations, we prove difference estimates in
H^{2}
and
H^{1,1}
, respectively.
These estimates allow us to prove Theorem
1.1
.

It is noteworthy that, for
\beta>0
, the forward-in-time well-posedness fails on the torus in any Sobolev spaces due to non-existence of solutions (see
[
4
]
).
In contrast, our result establishes local well-posedness even for
\beta>0
, showing that the ill-posedness phenomena are directly related not only to the sign of
\beta
, but also to the geometry of the underlying domain.

The second aim of this paper focuses on the global well-posedness. In the following theorem, we extend our well-posedness result in
H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}
globally in the dissipative case
\beta<0
.

Theorem 1.2

(Global well-posedness in
H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}
)
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\beta<0
. Then, the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) is (forward in time) globally well-posed in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap H^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})
.

As far as we know, this is the first global well-posedness result for KDNLS posed on
\mathbb{R}
.
As we have proved local well-posedness with the existence time depending only on the
H^{2}
norm of the initial data, it suffices to obtain a global a priori bound in
H^{2}
.
We prove it in Proposition
6.2
below, and this immediately implies Theorem
1.2
.
Clearly, the same global result holds in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Remark 1.3
.

Our global well-posedness result naturally leads to the investigation of the asymptotic behavior of solutions to (
1.1
) on
{\mathbb{R}}
. In our upcoming work, we consider the asymptotic behavior of small solutions to (
1.1
) with
\beta\neq 0
in
H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}
(especially both signs of
\beta
).
The (local) well-posedness result of this paper provides a rigorous justification for various formal arguments in analyzing the asymptotic behavior.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we introduce a regularized equation and frequency-restricted gauge transformations for KDNLS.
In Section
3
, we collect some useful lemmas.
Various a priori estimates for smooth solutions to the regularized equation are shown in Section
4
, and those for the difference of solutions are given in Section
5
.
Once these estimates are available, Theorem
1.1
can be shown by a standard argument; we give a proof in Appendix
A
.
Finally, a global-in-time
H^{2}
a priori bound is stated and proved in Section
6
, which together with Theorem
1.1
implies Theorem
1.2
.

2.
Regularized equation and gauge transformation

We write the nonlinear terms in (
1.1
) as
i\partial_{x}\mathcal{N}[u]
, where

\mathcal{N}[u]:=\alpha|u|^{2}u+\beta\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})u.

We also introduce the notation

\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}:=\alpha\,{\rm Id}+\beta\,\mathcal{H},

so that
\mathcal{N}[u]=\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(|u|^{2})u
.

2.1.
Regularized equations

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we consider the regularized equation with smooth initial data. Indeed, for
{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
, we consider

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u^{\varepsilon}-i\partial_{x}^{2}u^{\varepsilon}&={\varepsilon}\partial_{x}^{2}u^{\varepsilon}+\partial_{x}\mathcal{N}[u^{\varepsilon}],\\
u^{\varepsilon}(0,x)&=\phi^{\varepsilon}(x).\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

We also consider the difference of two solutions to (
2.1
) with possibly different
{\varepsilon}
’s and
\phi^{{\varepsilon}}
’s.
Let
0<{\varepsilon}_{2}\leq{\varepsilon}_{1}<1
and for
j=1,2
, let
u^{{\varepsilon}_{j}}

be a solution to (
2.1
) with
{\varepsilon}={\varepsilon}_{j}
and
\phi^{\varepsilon}=\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{j}}
.
Set

w:=u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}.

Then the equation for
w
is given below:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}w-i\partial_{x}^{2}w&={\varepsilon}_{2}\partial_{x}^{2}w+({\varepsilon}_{1}-{\varepsilon}_{2})\partial_{x}^{2}u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}+\partial_{x}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}},w],\\
w(0,x)&=\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}(x)-\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(x),\end{aligned}\right.

(2.2)

where

\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}},w]:=\mathcal{N}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}]-\mathcal{N}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-w]=\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(|u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}|^{2})u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(|u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-w|^{2})(u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-w).

We also consider the equation for
J(t)u^{\varepsilon}(t)
,
J(t):=x+2it\partial_{x}
.
Recall that the operator
J(t)
satisfies the commutator relations

[J(t),\partial_{t}-i\partial_{x}^{2}]=0,\quad[J(t),\partial_{x}]=[x,\partial_{x}]=-1,\quad[J(t),\partial_{x}^{2}]=[x,\partial_{x}^{2}]=-2\partial_{x},

and the “Leibniz rule”

J[fg]=2it(\partial_{x}f)g+fJg,\quad J\big[\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(f\overline{g})h\big]=\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}((Jf)\overline{g})h-\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(f\overline{Jg})h+\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(f\overline{g})Jh.

For a solution
u^{\varepsilon}
to (
2.1
), a direct calculation shows that
Ju^{\varepsilon}
satisfies the following equation:

\partial_{t}Ju^{\varepsilon}-i\partial_{x}^{2}Ju^{\varepsilon}={\varepsilon}\partial_{x}^{2}Ju^{\varepsilon}+\partial_{x}J\mathcal{N}[u^{\varepsilon}]-2{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}u^{\varepsilon}-\mathcal{N}[u^{\varepsilon}].

(2.3)

Similarly, the equation for
Jw
is given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}Jw-i\partial_{x}^{2}Jw

\displaystyle={\varepsilon}_{2}\partial_{x}^{2}Jw+({\varepsilon}_{1}-{\varepsilon}_{2})\partial_{x}^{2}Ju^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}+\partial_{x}J\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}},w]

(2.4)

\displaystyle-2{\varepsilon}_{2}\partial_{x}w-2({\varepsilon}_{1}-{\varepsilon}_{2})\partial_{x}u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}},w].

2.2.
Gauge transformation

Now, we introduce a gauge transformation which removes the most problematic nonlinear interactions of the high-low type; i.e., interactions between high frequency components of the differentiated function and much lower frequency components of the other two.
The gauge transformation for (
1.1
) has been investigated in
[
5
]
to treat the nonlinear terms
2\alpha|u|^{2}\partial_{x}u
,
\beta\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})\partial_{x}u
and
\beta\mathcal{H}(\overline{u}\partial_{x}u)u
.
In this work we only remove the high-low interaction from
\beta\mathcal{H}(\overline{u}\partial_{x}u)u
, as the other two terms exhibit good symmetry so that we can apply integration by parts in the energy estimates.
To this end, we define

\mathcal{F}(P_{\pm}f)(\xi):=\chi_{\pm}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi),\qquad\mathcal{F}(Q_{\pm}f)(\xi):=\chi_{\pm}(\xi)\varrho_{>1}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi),

where
\chi_{\pm}
is the characteristic function of
\pm(0,\infty)
.
Note that
\mathcal{H}=-iP_{+}+iP_{-}
and
Q_{\pm}\mathcal{H}=\mp iQ_{\pm}
.
Hence, if we fix the sign of output frequencies by applying
Q_{\pm}
, then the high-low interactions of
Q_{\pm}\big[\beta\mathcal{H}(\overline{u}\partial_{x}u)u\big]
are the same as those of
\beta\overline{u}(Q_{\pm}\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}u)u=\mp i\beta|u|^{2}\partial_{x}Q_{\pm}u
.
This suggests (see, e.g.,
[
3
, Section 2]
) that the suitable gauge function for the equation of
Q_{\pm}u
is given by
e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}
, where

\rho^{\pm}[u]:=\mp\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{x}|u(y)|^{2}\,dy.

(2.5)

For the difference equation (
2.2
), the nonlinear terms can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \partial_{x}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}},w]&=\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}\big(\partial_{x}w\,(\overline{u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-w})\big)(u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-w)+\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}\big((u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-w)\partial_{x}\overline{w}\big)(u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-w)\\
&\qquad+\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(|u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-w|^{2})\partial_{x}w+\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}\\
&=\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(\partial_{x}w\,\overline{u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}})u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}+\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\partial_{x}\overline{w})u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}+\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(|u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}|^{2})\partial_{x}w+\widetilde{\mathcal{R}},\end{aligned}

(2.6)

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}
is the sum of the terms in which
\partial_{x}
hits one of
u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}
’s.
In the energy estimates for
w
, it will turn out that only the term
\beta\mathcal{H}(\partial_{x}w\,\overline{u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}})u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}
is problematic.
Since the high-low interaction part of
\beta Q_{\pm}\big[\mathcal{H}(\partial_{x}w\,\overline{u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}})u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\big]
is identical with that of
\beta|u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}|^{2}Q_{\pm}\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}w=\mp i\beta|u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}|^{2}\partial_{x}Q_{\pm}w
, we will use the gauge functions
e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}
for
Q_{\pm}w
.

Remark 2.1
.

In the dissipative case
\beta<0
, the energy method works without the gauge transformation (see
[
5
, Appendix]
).
For instance, in the
L^{2}
energy estimate for the difference
w
, we can treat the term
\beta\partial_{x}\mathcal{H}(2{\rm Re}(u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\overline{w}))u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}
including the problematic case
\beta\mathcal{H}(\partial_{x}w\,\overline{u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}})u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}
, as follows:

2{\rm Re}\int\beta\partial_{x}\mathcal{H}\big[2{\rm Re}(u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\overline{w})\big]u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\overline{w}\,dx=\beta\Big\|D_{x}^{1/2}\big[2{\rm Re}(u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\overline{w})\big]\Big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 0.

In the case
\beta>0
, however, this term is not estimated by
C\|u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
.
We thus need to exploit the gauge transformations to suitably handle the problematic term and refine the estimate.

3.
Useful lemmas

In this section, we present several estimates that will be used in the subsequent proof of a priori bounds.

Lemma 3.1
.

We have

\big\|[Q_{\pm},f]\partial_{x}g\big\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

The claimed estimate is equivalent to

\left\|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\big((\chi_{\pm}\varrho_{>1})(\xi)-(\chi_{\pm}\varrho_{>1})(\eta)\big)\eta\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)\widehat{g}(\eta)\,d\eta\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}\lesssim\|\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{2}\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}\|\widehat{g}\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}.

Since the condition
\left\langle\eta\right\rangle\gg\left\langle\xi-\eta\right\rangle
implies that
(\chi_{\pm}\varrho_{>1})(\xi)-(\chi_{\pm}\varrho_{>1})(\eta)=0
, we have

\big|\big((\chi_{\pm}\varrho_{>1})(\xi)-(\chi_{\pm}\varrho_{>1})(\eta)\big)\eta\big|\lesssim\left\langle\xi-\eta\right\rangle.

This inequality, Young’s and Hölder’s inequalities imply the claim.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For
u\in H^{2}
, we have
e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}\in W^{2,\infty}
and

\displaystyle\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq e^{C\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}},\qquad\|\partial_{x}e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim e^{C\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{2}e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim e^{C\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\big(\|u\|_{H^{1}}\|u\|_{H^{2}}+\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{4}\big).

Moreover, if
u^{\varepsilon}
is a solution to (
2.1
), we have

\|\partial_{t}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u^{\varepsilon}(t)]}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim e^{C\|u^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\big(\|u^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}}\|u^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{2}}+\|u^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}}^{4}\big).

Proof.

The desired bounds follow from direct calculations and the Hölder, Sobolev inequalities.
For the last bound, we use the identity

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho^{\pm}[u^{\varepsilon}]

\displaystyle=\mp\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{x}2\mathrm{Re}\,\Big[\Big(i\partial_{x}^{2}u^{\varepsilon}+{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}^{2}u^{\varepsilon}+\alpha\partial_{x}\big[|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}u^{\varepsilon}\big]+\beta\partial_{x}\big[\mathcal{H}(|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2})u^{\varepsilon}\big]\Big)\overline{u^{\varepsilon}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\mp\frac{\beta}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\Big[{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}^{2}(|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2})+\partial_{x}\Big(2\mathrm{Re}\,(i\overline{u^{\varepsilon}}\partial_{x}u^{\varepsilon})+\frac{3}{2}\alpha|u^{\varepsilon}|^{4}+2\beta\mathcal{H}(|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2})|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt-2{\varepsilon}|\partial_{x}u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}-\beta\mathcal{H}(|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2})\partial_{x}(|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2})\Big]

\displaystyle=\mp\frac{\beta}{2}\Big[{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}(|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2})+2\mathrm{Re}\,(i\overline{u^{\varepsilon}}\partial_{x}u^{\varepsilon})+\frac{3}{2}\alpha|u^{\varepsilon}|^{4}+2\beta\mathcal{H}(|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2})|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt-2{\varepsilon}\int_{-\infty}^{x}|\partial_{x}u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}-\beta\int_{-\infty}^{x}\mathcal{H}(|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2})\partial_{x}(|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2})\Big].\qed

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer and
u
be a solution to (
2.1
). Then we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}(e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}u(t))\|_{H^{k-1}}

Lemma 3.5

(Brezis-Gallouet-Wainger inequality)
.

Let
\nu\in(0,1)
.
For
f\in H^{1}
, we have

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim_{\nu}\|f\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}\log^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(e+\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}\big)+\|f\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{1-\nu}.

Proof.

Applying Cauchy-Schwarz on the Fourier side, we have

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\big(\log^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+R)\big)\|\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|\leq R)}+R^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(|\xi|>R)}

for any

Setting

, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\{1+\log^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(1+\frac{\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{\|f\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}}\Big)\Big\}\|f\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\{\log^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(e+\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}\big)+\log^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(1+\frac{1}{\|f\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}}\Big)\Big\}\|f\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\nu}\|f\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}\log^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(e+\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}\big)+\|f\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{1-\nu}.\qed

{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)

T>0
, and suppose that
u
is a smooth solution to (
2.1
) on
[0,T]
(say,
u\in C([0,T],H^{k+5})
).
Then, we have

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{k}}\leq C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})\|\phi\|_{H^{k}}\qquad\text{for}\quad 0\leq t\leq\min\big\{T,T_{0}(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})\big\},

(4.14)

where
T_{0}(\cdot)
is the function given in Proposition
4.1
.

Proof.

The argument is parallel to the
H^{2}
case in the previous proposition.
Noticing

\|\partial_{x}^{k}u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim e^{C\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\Big(\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}}\Big),

(4.15)

we estimate the time derivative of
\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k}u\|_{L^{2}}
.
Similarly to (
4.4
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u(t)]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\leq\Big(\|\partial_{t}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u]}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}^{2}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u]}\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\|\partial_{x}^{k}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-2{\varepsilon}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k+1}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\quad+2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u]}\Big(Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k+1}\mathcal{N}[u]-(\mp i\beta)|u|^{2}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k+1}u\Big)\overline{Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k}u}\,dx.\end{aligned}

To estimate the last integral, we decompose the cubic terms as

\displaystyle Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k+1}\mathcal{N}[u]-(\mp i\beta)|u|^{2}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k+1}u

\displaystyle=Q_{\pm}\Big(2\alpha|u|^{2}\partial_{x}^{k+1}u+\beta\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})\partial_{x}^{k+1}u\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\beta\Big(Q_{\pm}\big[u\mathcal{H}(\overline{u}\partial_{x}^{k+1}u)\big]-u\overline{u}Q_{\pm}\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{k+1}u\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+Q_{\pm}\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}\big(u\partial_{x}^{k+1}\overline{u}\big)u\Big)+\text{l.o.t.},

and estimate the lower order terms by Lemma
3.4
:

\Big|2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u]}\text{(l.o.t.)}\overline{Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k}u}\,dx\Big|\lesssim e^{C\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\|u\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\|u\|_{H^{k}}^{2}.

The first term on the right-hand side is treated by commutator estimate from Lemma
3.1
:

\big\|\big[Q_{\pm},2\alpha|u|^{2}+\beta\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})\big]\partial_{x}^{k+1}u\big\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|2\alpha|u|^{2}+\beta\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})\|_{H^{2}}\|u\|_{H^{k}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\|u\|_{H^{k}},

and integration by parts.
The second term can be treated in the same way as (
4.6
) in the
H^{2}
case.
For the contribution from the third one, as before, we divide the term using (
4.9
) and apply integration by parts in
t
to the last term, obtaining some terms bounded by
C(\|u\|_{H^{2}})\|u\|_{H^{k}}^{2}
and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}m(\xi,\sigma)(2i)^{-1}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)^{k-1}\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(\xi-\sigma)\overline{\widehat{h}(\xi)}\,d\eta\,d\sigma\,d\xi,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}m(\xi,\sigma)(2i)^{-1}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)^{k-1}\partial_{t}\Big[\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(\xi-\sigma)\Big]\overline{\widehat{h}(\xi)}\,d\eta\,d\sigma\,d\xi,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}m(\xi,\sigma)(2i)^{-1}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)^{k-1}\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(\xi-\sigma)\overline{\partial_{t}\widehat{h}(\xi)}\,d\eta\,d\sigma\,d\xi,

where
f(t)=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}u(t)
is the same as before but
h(t):=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}\big[e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u(t)]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k}u(t)\big]
.
The second integral is bounded by

\Big(\|\partial_{t}f\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{x}^{k-1}f\|_{H^{1}}\|f\|_{H^{1}}+\|f\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\|\partial_{x}^{k-1}\partial_{t}f\|_{L^{2}}\Big)\|e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u]}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}^{k}u\|_{L^{2}},

while the third one is bounded by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\|\partial_{x}^{k-1}f\|_{H^{1}}\|\partial_{t}h\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \lesssim\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\|u\|_{H^{k}}\Big\{&\|(\partial_{t}-i\partial_{x}^{2})e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u]}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}^{k}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u]}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\|\partial_{x}^{k+1}u\|_{H^{-1}}\\
&+\|e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u]}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\|\partial_{x}^{k}(\partial_{t}-i\partial_{x}^{2})u\|_{H^{-1}}\Big\}.\end{aligned}

These terms are treated by using Lemmas
3.2
,
3.3
and the factor
-2{\varepsilon}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k+1}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
in the differential inequality.
We then obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u(t)]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\mathcal{I}_{k}(t)\Big)\leq C(\|u\|_{H^{2}})\|u\|_{H^{k}}^{2},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{k}(t):=2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}m(\xi,\sigma)(2i)^{-1}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)^{k-1}\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(\xi-\sigma)\overline{\widehat{h}(\xi)}\,d\eta\,d\sigma\,d\xi.

Using (
4.15
) and interpolation inequalities, we see that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}_{k}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{C\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\|\partial_{x}^{k-1}u\|_{L^{2}}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k}u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u\|_{H^{1}})\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{k}}\|\partial_{x}^{k}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{k-1}{k}}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k}u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u\|_{H^{1}})\Big\{\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{k}}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2-\frac{1}{k}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k}u\|_{L^{2}}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\|u\|_{H^{1}})\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

We set

\displaystyle X_{k}(t)

\displaystyle:=Ce^{C\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\Big(\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u(t)]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\mathcal{I}_{k}(t)+C(\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}})\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big).

From (
4.15
), (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), we have

\|u(t)\|_{H^{k}}^{2}\leq X_{k}(t),\qquad\frac{d}{dt}X_{k}(t)\leq C(\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}})\Big(X_{k}(t)+\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\Big).

By the
H^{2}
bound (
4.1
) obtained in Proposition
4.1
and Gronwall’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{k}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(X_{k}(0)+C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})\|\phi\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\Big)e^{C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})t}\leq C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})\|\phi\|_{H^{k}}^{2}

for
0\leq t\leq\min\big\{T,T_{0}(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})\big\}
.
∎

4.2.
A priori bounds in
H^{1,1}
,
H^{k-1,1}

We turn to the estimate of the weighted norms.
Note that

\|f\|_{H^{k}}+\|f\|_{H^{k-1,1}}\sim\|f\|_{H^{k}}+\|J(t)f\|_{H^{k-1}}

for any integer
k\geq 1
, where the
t
-dependence of the implicit constant is uniform on each bounded interval.
Thus, we consider a priori estimates for
\|J(t)u(t)\|_{H^{k-1}}
instead of
\|u(t)\|_{H^{k-1,1}}
.

Proposition 4.3

(
H^{1,1}
a priori bound)
.

There exists
C:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to[1,\infty)
such that the following holds:
Let
{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
,
T>0
, and suppose that
u\in C([0,T],H^{7}\cap H^{6,1})
is a smooth solution to (
2.1
) on
[0,T]
with an initial data
\phi
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\|J(t)u(t)\|_{H^{1}}\leq C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})\|\phi\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}}\qquad\text{for}\quad 0\leq t\leq\min\big\{T,T_{0}(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})\big\},

(4.16)

where
T_{0}(\cdot)
is the function given in Proposition
4.1
.

Proof.

The equation (
2.3
) for
J(t)u(t)
yields that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|Ju\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\|\partial_{x}J\mathcal{N}[u]\|_{L^{2}}+{\varepsilon}\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\mathcal{N}[u]\|_{L^{2}}\Big)\|Ju\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u\|_{H^{1}})\Big(\|Ju\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\Big).

Then we estimate
\|\partial_{x}Ju\|_{L^{2}}
.
In view of

\|\partial_{x}Ju\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|Ju\|_{L^{2}}+e^{C\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}Ju\|_{L^{2}},

we may consider
\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}Ju\|_{L^{2}}
and estimate

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}Ju\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\partial_{t}\big(e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u]}\big)|Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}Ju|^{2}\,dx+2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}\partial_{t}(Ju)\,\overline{Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}Ju}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\big(\partial_{t}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u]}+{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}^{2}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u]}\big)|Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}Ju|^{2}\,dx-2{\varepsilon}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}Ju\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-2{\rm Re}\,(\mp i\beta)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u]}|u|^{2}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}Ju\,\overline{Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}Ju}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\Big(\partial_{x}^{2}J\mathcal{N}[u]-2{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}^{2}u-\partial_{x}\mathcal{N}[u]\Big)\overline{Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}Ju}\,dx.

From Lemmas
3.2
and
3.4
, the right-hand side is bounded by

\displaystyle C(\|u\|_{H^{2}})\Big(\|Ju\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|u\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\Big)-2{\varepsilon}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}Ju\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(4.17)

\displaystyle+2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\Big(\big(2\alpha|u|^{2}+\beta\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2})\big)\partial_{x}^{2}Ju\Big)\overline{Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}Ju}\,dx

(4.18)

\displaystyle+2\beta{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u]}\Big(Q_{\pm}\big(u\mathcal{H}(\overline{u}\partial_{x}^{2}Ju)\big)-u\overline{u}Q_{\pm}\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}Ju\Big)\overline{Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}Ju}\,dx

(4.19)

\displaystyle-2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(u\partial_{x}^{2}\overline{Ju})u\Big)\overline{Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}Ju}\,dx.

(4.20)

The arguments for (
4.18
)–(
4.20
) are analogous to the corresponding estimates for (
4.5
)–(
4.7
) in the proof of Proposition
4.1
.
For (
4.18
) and (
4.19
), we use Lemma
3.1
, integration by parts, and dyadic decomposition suitably.
For (
4.20
), we can rewrite it as

2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}m(\xi,\sigma)(\xi-\eta-\sigma)^{2}\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(\xi-\sigma)\overline{\widehat{h}(\xi)}\,d\eta\,d\sigma\,d\xi,

where
m
,
f
are the same as before and
g:=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}Ju
, and
h:=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}\big[e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}Ju\big]
.
Then we decompose it by (
4.9
) and apply integration by parts in
t
for the last term to obtain the following terms

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}m(\xi,\sigma)(2i)^{-1}\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(\xi-\sigma)\overline{\widehat{h}(\xi)}\,d\eta\,d\sigma\,d\xi,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}m(\xi,\sigma)(2i)^{-1}\partial_{t}\Big[\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(\xi-\sigma)\overline{\widehat{h}(\xi)}\Big]\,d\eta\,d\sigma\,d\xi.

The second line can be estimated as before.
We only remark that substitution of the equation into
\partial_{t}g
and
\partial_{t}h
creates the term
{\varepsilon}\|\partial_{x}^{2}Ju\|_{L^{2}}\|Ju\|_{H^{1}}
.
This is treated using the second term in (
4.17
).

Thus we obtain the following differential inequality:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u(t)]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}J(t)u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\mathcal{I}_{1,1}(t)\Big)\leq C(\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}})\Big(\|J(t)u(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\Big),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1,1}(t):=2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}m(\xi,\sigma)(2i)^{-1}\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(\xi-\sigma)\overline{\widehat{h}(\xi)}\,d\eta\,d\sigma\,d\xi.

The integral term can be estimated as

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}_{1,1}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{C\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\|Ju\|_{L^{2}}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}Ju\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}Ju\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\|u\|_{H^{1}})\|Ju\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Now, we invoke the
H^{2}
a priori estimate in Proposition
4.1
to replace
\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}}
and
\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}
by
C(\|\phi\|_{H^{1}})\|\phi\|_{H^{2}}
for
0\leq t\leq\min\big\{T,T_{0}(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})\big\}
.
Then one gets

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|J(t)u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})\Big(\|J(t)u(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\phi\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\Big),

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u(t)]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}J(t)u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\mathcal{I}_{1,1}(t)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})\Big(\|J(t)u(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\phi\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\Big),

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}_{1,1}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u(t)]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}J(t)u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})\|J(t)u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Hence, by defining

\displaystyle Y(t)

\displaystyle:=C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})\Big(\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u(t)]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}J(t)u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})\|J(t)u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big),

\displaystyle\widetilde{Y}(t)

\displaystyle:=C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})\Big(\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u(t)]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}J(t)u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\mathcal{I}_{1,1}(t)+C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})\|J(t)u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big),

we have

\|J(t)u(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}Y(t)\leq\widetilde{Y}(t)\leq\frac{3}{2}Y(t)\leq C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})\|J(t)u(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

and

\frac{d}{dt}\widetilde{Y}(t)\leq C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})\Big(\widetilde{Y}(t)+\|\phi\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\Big)

for
0\leq t\leq\min\big\{T,T_{0}(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})\big\}
.
By Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain

\|J(t)u(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq\Big(\widetilde{Y}(0)+\|\phi\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\Big)e^{C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})T_{0}}\leq C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})\Big(\|x\phi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\phi\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\Big).

This completes the proof of (
4.16
).
∎

We now address a priori weighted estimate in higher regularity.

Proposition 4.4

(Higher-order a priori bound in weighted space)
.

For any integer
k\geq 3
, there exists
C:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to[1,\infty)
(depending on
k
) such that the following holds.
Let
{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
,
T>0
, and suppose that
u
is a smooth solution to (
2.1
) on
[0,T]
(say,
u\in C([0,T],H^{k+5}\cap H^{k+4,1})
).
Then, we have

\displaystyle\|J(t)u(t)\|_{H^{k-1}}\leq C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}})\|\phi\|_{H^{k}\cap H^{k-1,1}}\qquad\text{for}\quad 0\leq t\leq\min\big\{T,T_{0}(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})\big\}.

(4.21)

Proof.

We follow the same strategy.
To estimate
\|\partial_{x}^{k-1}Ju\|_{L^{2}}
, we may consider
\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k-1}Ju\|_{L^{2}}
, and we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k-1}Ju\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u\|_{H^{2}},\|Ju\|_{H^{1}})\Big(\|Ju\|_{H^{k-1}}^{2}+\|u\|_{H^{k}}^{2}\Big)-2{\varepsilon}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k}Ju\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\Big(\big(2\alpha|u|^{2}+\beta\mathcal{H}(|u|^{2}\big)\partial_{x}^{k}Ju\Big)\overline{Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k-1}Ju}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+2\beta{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u]}\Big(Q_{\pm}\big(u\mathcal{H}(\overline{u}\partial_{x}^{k}Ju)\big)-u\overline{u}Q_{\pm}\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{k}Ju\Big)\overline{Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k-1}Ju}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad-2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(u\partial_{x}^{k}\overline{Ju})u\Big)\overline{Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k-1}Ju}\,dx,

where we used (
3.2
) in Lemma
3.4
.
Here, we only see the part using the normal form.
The last line is rewritten as

\displaystyle 2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}m(\xi,\sigma)(\xi-\eta-\sigma)^{k}\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(\xi-\sigma)\overline{\widehat{h}(\xi)}\,d\eta\,d\sigma\,d\xi

for
m
,
f
,
g
as before and
h:=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}\big[e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k-1}Ju\big]
.
After decomposition (
4.9
) and integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}m(\xi,\sigma)(2i)^{-1}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)^{k-2}\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(\xi-\sigma)\overline{\widehat{h}(\xi)}\,d\eta\,d\sigma\,d\xi,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}m(\xi,\sigma)(2i)^{-1}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)^{k-2}\partial_{t}\Big[\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(\xi-\sigma)\overline{\widehat{h}(\xi)}\,\Big]\,d\eta\,d\sigma\,d\xi.

The bound of the latter can be obtained as before: we use the factor
-2{\varepsilon}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k}Ju\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
to absorb the terms with
\|\partial_{x}^{k}Ju\|_{L^{2}}
appearing after substitution of the equation to
\partial_{t}g
and
\partial_{t}h
.
Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k-1}Ju\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\mathcal{I}_{k-1,1}(t)\Big)\leq C(\|u\|_{H^{2}},\|Ju\|_{H^{1}})\Big(\|Ju\|_{H^{k-1}}^{2}+\|u\|_{H^{k}}^{2}\Big),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{k-1,1}(t):=2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}m(\xi,\sigma)(2i)^{-1}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)^{k-2}\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}(\xi-\sigma)\overline{\widehat{h}(\xi)}\,d\eta\,d\sigma\,d\xi.

By interpolation inequalities, the integral term is estimated as

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}_{k-1,1}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{C\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\|\partial_{x}^{k-2}Ju\|_{L^{2}}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k-1}Ju\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k-1}Ju\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\|u\|_{H^{1}})\|Ju\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{k-1}}\|\partial_{x}^{k-1}Ju\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2(k-2)}{k-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k-1}Ju\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\|u\|_{H^{1}})\|Ju\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Then we apply
H^{2}
,
H^{k}
and
H^{1,1}
a priori estimates in Propositions
4.1
–
4.3
:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u(t)]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k-1}J(t)u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\mathcal{I}_{k-1,1}(t)\Big)\leq C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}})\Big(\|J(t)u(t)\|_{H^{k-1}}^{2}+\|\phi\|_{H^{k}}^{2}\Big),

and

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}_{k-1,1}(t)|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u(t)]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k-1}J(t)u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})\|\phi\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}}^{2}.

Hence,

Y_{k}(t):=C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}})\Big\{\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u(t)]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{k-1}J(t)u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\mathcal{I}_{k-1,1}(t)+C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})\|\phi\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}}^{2}\Big\}

satisfies

\|J(t)u(t)\|_{H^{k-1}}^{2}\leq Y_{k}(t)\leq C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}})\Big(\|J(t)u(t)\|_{H^{k-1}}^{2}+\|\phi\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}}^{2}\Big)

and

\frac{d}{dt}Y_{k}(t)\leq C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}})\Big(Y_{k}(t)+\|\phi\|_{H^{k}}^{2}\Big)

for
0\leq t\leq\min\big\{T,T_{0}(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})\big\}
.
By Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain (
4.21
).
∎

5.
A priori bounds for difference

Finally, we establish a priori bounds of the difference of two solutions.
We need the difference bounds for the uniqueness and continuous dependence.

Proposition 5.1

(
L^{2}
a priori bound for difference)
.

Let
0<{\varepsilon}_{2}\leq{\varepsilon}_{1}<1
, and suppose that
u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}},u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\in C([0,T],H^{5})

be solutions to (
2.1
) on
[0,T]
with the viscosity coefficient
{\varepsilon}_{1},{\varepsilon}_{2}
and the initial data
\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}},\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}
, respectively.
Then, there exists
C>0
depending on
M:=\max\limits_{j=1,2}\|u^{{\varepsilon}_{j}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{2}}
and
T
such that

\|u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\Big(\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\Big).

(5.1)

Note that the constant
C
in Proposition
5.1
does not depend on
{\varepsilon}_{1},{\varepsilon}_{2}
.

Proof.

We begin with the decomposition

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{\leq N}w\|_{L^{2}}+\big\|e^{\rho^{\mp}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}\big\|_{L^{\infty}}\big\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}P_{\pm}P_{>N}w\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|P_{\leq N}w\|_{L^{2}}+e^{C\|u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\big\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}Q_{\pm N}w\big\|_{L^{2}},

where
N\gg 1
is a dyadic number to be chosen later and we write
Q_{\pm N}:=P_{\pm}P_{>N}
.
In particular,

\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq Z(t):=2\|P_{\leq N}w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{1}(M)\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(t)]}Q_{\pm N}w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\qquad t\in[0,T].

Let us consider
\frac{d}{dt}Z(t)
.
The low frequency part is easily treated, since the derivative can be replaced with the factor
N
.
Using the equation (
2.2
) for
w
, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\|P_{\leq N}w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\|\partial_{x}^{2}u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(M,N)\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(M,N)\Big(\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\Big).

For the energy estimate of
\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(t)]}Q_{\pm N}w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
, we begin with

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(t)]}Q_{\pm N}w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\partial_{t}\big(e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}\big)|Q_{\pm N}w|^{2}\,dx+2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}Q_{\pm N}\partial_{t}w\overline{Q_{\pm N}w}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\partial_{t}\big(e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}\big)|Q_{\pm N}w|^{2}\,dx-2{\rm Re}\,(\mp i\beta)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}|u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}|^{2}Q_{\pm N}\partial_{x}w\overline{Q_{\pm N}w}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+{\varepsilon}_{2}\int\partial_{x}^{2}\big(e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}\big)|Q_{\pm N}w|^{2}\,dx-2{\varepsilon}_{2}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}Q_{\pm N}\partial_{x}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2({\varepsilon}_{1}-{\varepsilon}_{2}){\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}Q_{\pm N}\partial_{x}^{2}u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\overline{Q_{\pm N}w}\,dx+2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}Q_{\pm N}\partial_{x}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}},w]\overline{Q_{\pm N}w}\,dx

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\leq C(M)\Big(\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\Big)-2{\varepsilon}_{2}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}Q_{\pm N}\partial_{x}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\quad+2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}\Big(Q_{\pm N}\partial_{x}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}},w]-(\mp i\beta)|u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}|^{2}Q_{\pm N}\partial_{x}w\Big)\overline{Q_{\pm N}w}\,dx.\end{aligned}

(5.2)

By (
2.6
), we decompose the cubic terms in (
5.2
) as

\displaystyle Q_{\pm N}\partial_{x}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}},w]-(\mp i\beta)|u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}|^{2}Q_{\pm N}\partial_{x}w

\displaystyle=Q_{\pm N}\Big(\big(2\alpha|u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}|^{2}+\beta\mathcal{H}(|u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}|^{2})\big)\partial_{x}w\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\beta\Big[Q_{\pm N}\big[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\mathcal{H}(\overline{u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}}\partial_{x}w)\big]-u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\overline{u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}}Q_{\pm N}\partial_{x}\mathcal{H}w\Big]

\displaystyle\quad+Q_{\pm N}\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\partial_{x}\overline{w})u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+Q_{\pm N}\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}.

The
L^{2}
norm of the last term
Q_{\pm N}\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}
can be estimated by
C(M)\|w\|_{L^{2}}
.
The other terms can be treated similarly to (
4.5
)–(
4.7
) in the proof of Proposition
4.1
.
We only consider the contribution from the third term,

2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}Q_{\pm N}\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\partial_{x}\overline{w})u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\Big)\overline{Q_{\pm N}w}\,dx,

for which we use the normal form approach.
As before, we write the integral in the following form:

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}m_{N}(\xi,\sigma)(\xi-\eta-\sigma)\widehat{f}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\xi-\sigma)\overline{\widehat{h}(\xi)}\,d\eta\,d\sigma\,d\xi,

(5.3)

where
f^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}:=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}
,
g:=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}w
,
h:=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}(e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}Q_{\pm N}w)
and

m_{N}(\xi,\sigma):=c\,(\chi_{\pm}\varrho_{>N})(\xi)(\alpha-i\beta{\rm sgn}(\sigma)).

We then use the relation

\xi(\xi-\eta-\sigma)=(\xi-\eta)(\xi-\sigma)-\eta\sigma

to decompose (
5.3
).
The contribution from the first term is easily estimated; notice that
|\xi|\geq N
in the support of
m_{N}
.
By integration by parts, the second term gives

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}m_{N}(\xi,\sigma)(2i)^{-1}\xi^{-1}\widehat{f}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\xi-\sigma)\overline{\widehat{h}(\xi)}\,d\eta\,d\sigma\,d\xi,

(5.4)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}m_{N}(\xi,\sigma)(2i)^{-1}\xi^{-1}\partial_{t}\Big[\widehat{f}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\xi-\sigma)\Big]\overline{\widehat{h}(\xi)}\,d\eta\,d\sigma\,d\xi,

(5.5)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}m_{N}(\xi,\sigma)(2i)^{-1}\xi^{-1}\widehat{f}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\xi-\sigma)\overline{\partial_{t}\widehat{h}(\xi)}\,d\eta\,d\sigma\,d\xi.

(5.6)

For (
5.5
), since we estimate
h
in
L^{2}
, it is essentially enough to show

\big\|\partial_{t}\big((f^{{\varepsilon}_{2}})^{2}\overline{g}\big)\big\|_{H^{-1}}\leq C(M)\Big(\|w\|_{L^{2}}+{\varepsilon}_{1}\Big).

Using the Sobolev embedding
L^{1}\hookrightarrow H^{-1}
and the product estimate
\|\varphi\psi\|_{H^{-1}}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}}\|\psi\|_{H^{-1}}
, we see that

\displaystyle\big\|\partial_{t}\big((f^{{\varepsilon}_{2}})^{2}\overline{g}\big)\big\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\partial_{t}f^{{\varepsilon}_{2}})\overline{g}\|_{L^{1}}+\|(f^{{\varepsilon}_{2}})^{2}\overline{\partial_{t}g}\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{H^{1}}\|(\partial_{t}-i\partial_{x}^{2})u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}+\|u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\|(\partial_{t}-i\partial_{x}^{2})w\|_{H^{-1}}.

It is now clear from the equations for
u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}
and
w
that these terms are bounded appropriately.
Finally, for (
5.6
) we readily show that

\|\partial_{t}h\|_{H^{-1}}\leq C(M)\Big(\|w\|_{L^{2}}+{\varepsilon}_{1}\Big).

In fact, the left-hand side is bounded by

\|(\partial_{t}-i\partial_{x}^{2})e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\|\partial_{x}w_{\pm}\|_{H^{-1}}+\|e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\|(\partial_{t}-i\partial_{x}^{2})w\|_{H^{-1}}.

Hence, taking (
5.4
) into account, we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(t)]}Q_{\pm N}w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\mathcal{J}_{0}(t)\Big)\leq C(M)\Big(\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\Big),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{0}(t):=2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}m_{N}(\xi,\sigma)(2i)^{-1}\xi^{-1}\widehat{f}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\xi-\sigma)\overline{\widehat{h}(\xi)}\,d\eta\,d\sigma\,d\xi.

Noticing that

|\mathcal{J}_{0}(t)|\leq\frac{C_{2}(M)}{N}\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{C_{2}(M)}{N}Z(t),

we set
N=N(C_{1}(M),C_{2}(M))\gg 1
so that

\frac{1}{2}Z(t)\leq\widetilde{Z}(t):=Z(t)-C_{1}(M)\mathcal{J}_{0}(t)\leq\frac{3}{2}Z(t).

By the above estimates and
\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq Z(t)\leq 2\widetilde{Z}(t)
, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\widetilde{Z}(t)\leq C(M)\Big(\widetilde{Z}(t)+{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\Big),

which implies

\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\Big(\frac{3}{2}Z(0)+{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\Big)e^{C(M)T}\quad(t\in[0,T]).

This completes the proof of (
5.1
).
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
T>0
, and suppose that
u_{1},u_{2}\in C([0,T],H^{2})
are solutions to (
1.1
) on
[0,T]
with the initial data
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
, respectively.
Then, there exists

and

\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}\leq C\|\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}\|_{L^{2}}.

In Proposition
5.1
, we have assumed the regularity
C_{T}H^{5}
to rigorously justify the calculations.
In fact, the argument may extend to general solutions in
C_{T}H^{2}
, since
u\in C_{T}H^{2}

The claim is shown by repeating the same argument but for the original equation (

∎

Proposition 5.3

(
H^{2}
a priori bound for difference)
.

Assume the hypotheses in Proposition
5.1
. Let
u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}},u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\in C([0,T],H^{9})
.
Then, there exists
C>0
depending on
M_{0}:=\max\limits_{j=1,2}\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{j}}\|_{H^{2}}
but not on
{\varepsilon}_{1},{\varepsilon}_{2}
such that

\|u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}(t)-u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\leq C\Big(\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\big(1+\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{4}}^{2}+\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{3}}^{4}\big)+\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{3}}^{4}\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

(5.7)

for
0\leq t\leq\min\big\{T,T_{0}(M_{0})\big\}
, where
T_{0}:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to(0,1]
is the function given in Proposition
4.1
.

Proof.

Proof follows a similar approach to that of Proposition
5.1
, with slight modifications.
We also note that we can use a priori bounds (
4.1
) and (
4.14
) up to
H^{4}
as well as (
5.1
) under the assumption
u^{{\varepsilon}_{j}}\in C([0,T],H^{9})
.
We first estimate
\|w\|_{H^{2}}
as

\displaystyle\|w(t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|e^{\rho^{\mp}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(t)]}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(t)]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C(M_{0})\Big(\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(t)]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\Big).

Following the argument in (
5.2
), we see that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(t)]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(M_{0})\Big(\|\partial_{x}^{2}w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{4}}^{2}\Big)-2{\varepsilon}_{2}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(t)]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{3}w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}\Big(Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{3}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}},w]-(\mp i\beta)|u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}|^{2}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{3}w\Big)\overline{Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}w}\,dx.

Among the cubic terms in
Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{3}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}},w]-(\mp i\beta)|u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}|^{2}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{3}w
, we only consider those containing
\partial_{x}^{3}w
.
Indeed, the
L^{2}
norm of the other terms can be bounded by either

\|(u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}},w)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\|w\|_{H^{2}}\leq C(M_{0})\|w\|_{H^{2}}

or

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{3}u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{L^{2}}\|(u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}},w)\|_{H^{1}}\|w\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C(M_{0})\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{3}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|w\|_{H^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The former is acceptable, while the latter gives the bound

C(M_{0})\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{3}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|w\|_{H^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq C(M_{0})\Big(\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{3}}^{4}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|w\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\Big).

By a calculation similar to (
2.6
), the cubic terms with
\partial_{x}^{3}w
is written as

\displaystyle Q_{\pm}\Big(\big(2\alpha|u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}|^{2}+\beta\mathcal{H}(|u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}|^{2})\big)\partial_{x}^{3}w\Big)+\beta\Big[Q_{\pm}\big[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\mathcal{H}(\overline{u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}}\partial_{x}^{3}w)\big]-u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\overline{u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}}Q_{\pm N}\partial_{x}^{3}\mathcal{H}w\Big]

\displaystyle+Q_{\pm}\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\partial_{x}^{3}\overline{w})u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\Big),

and we again focus on the last term.
By the normal form approach, we get the terms

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}m(\xi,\sigma)(2i)^{-1}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)\widehat{f}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\xi-\sigma)\overline{\widehat{h}(\xi)}\,d\eta\,d\sigma\,d\xi,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}m(\xi,\sigma)(2i)^{-1}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)\partial_{t}\Big[\widehat{f}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\xi-\sigma)\overline{\widehat{h}(\xi)}\Big]\,d\eta\,d\sigma\,d\xi,

where the notation is the same as in (
4.8
), (
5.3
) apart from
h:=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}(e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}w)
.
The latter case is bounded by

C(M_{0})\Big(\|f^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{H^{1}}\|\partial_{t}f^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}\|g\|_{H^{2}}\|h\|_{L^{2}}+\|f^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\|\partial_{t}g\|_{H^{1}}\|h\|_{L^{2}}+\|f^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\|g\|_{H^{2}}\|\partial_{t}h\|_{H^{-1}}\Big).

After estimating
\partial_{t}g
and
\partial_{t}h
, several third-order terms appear.
Indeed, we see that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}g\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq{\varepsilon}_{2}\|\partial_{x}^{3}w\|_{L^{2}}+{\varepsilon}_{1}\|\partial_{x}^{3}u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{L^{2}}+C(M_{0})\|w\|_{H^{2}},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}h\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C(M_{0})\Big({\varepsilon}_{2}\|\partial_{x}^{3}w\|_{L^{2}}+{\varepsilon}_{1}\|\partial_{x}^{3}u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{L^{2}}+\|w\|_{H^{2}}\Big).

Inserting these estimates, we obtain the bound

\displaystyle C(M_{0})\Big(\|w\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+{\varepsilon}_{1}\|\partial_{x}^{3}u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{L^{2}}\|w\|_{H^{2}}+{\varepsilon}_{2}\|\partial_{x}^{3}w\|_{L^{2}}\|w\|_{H^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C(M_{0})\Big(\|w\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+{\varepsilon}_{1}\|\partial_{x}^{3}u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{L^{2}}\|w\|_{H^{2}}+{\varepsilon}_{2}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{3}w\|_{L^{2}}\|w\|_{H^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq{\varepsilon}_{2}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{3}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(M_{0})\Big(\|w\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\Big).

Note that the first term in the last line is absorbed into the viscosity term in the energy estimate.
We thus obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(t)]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\mathcal{J}_{2}(t)\Big)\leq C(M_{0})\Big(\|w(t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{4}}^{2}+\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{3}}^{4}\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{2}(t):=2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}m(\xi,\sigma)(2i)^{-1}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)\widehat{f}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\xi-\sigma)\overline{\widehat{h}(\xi)}\,d\eta\,d\sigma\,d\xi.

We also estimate
\mathcal{J}_{2}(t)
, for
\mu>0
arbitrarily small, as

\displaystyle|\mathcal{J}_{2}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq C(M_{0})\|w\|_{H^{1}}\|w\|_{H^{2}}\leq C(M_{0})\Big(\mu\|w\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+C_{\mu}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C(M_{0})\Big(\mu C(M_{0})\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\mu}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(M_{0})\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Hence, we set

Z(t):=C(M_{0})\Big(\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(t)]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\mathcal{J}_{2}(t)+C(M_{0})\big(\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\big)\Big),

which satisfies

\|w(t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\leq Z(t),\qquad Z(0)\leq C(M_{0})\Big(\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\Big),

and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}Z(t)

\displaystyle\leq C(M_{0})\Big(\|w(t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{4}}^{2}+\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{3}}^{4}\big(\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C(M_{0})\Big(Z(t)+{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\big(\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{4}}^{2}+\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{3}}^{4}\big)+\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{3}}^{4}\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big).

Therefore we obtain the desired bound by Gronwall’s inequality.
∎

Proposition 5.4

(
H^{1,1}
a priori bound for difference)
.

Assume the hypotheses in Proposition
5.1
. Let
u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}},u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\in C([0,T],H^{9}\cap H^{8,1})
.
Then, there exists
C>0
depending on

M_{1}:=\max\limits_{j=1,2}\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{j}}\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}}

but not on
{\varepsilon}_{1},{\varepsilon}_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\|J(t)\big[u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}(t)-u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(t)\big]\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq C\Big\{\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}}^{2}

\displaystyle+{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\big(1+\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{4}\cap H^{3,1}}^{2}+\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{3}\cap H^{2,1}}^{4}\big)

\displaystyle+\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{3}\cap H^{2,1}}^{4}\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big\}

for
0\leq t\leq\min\big\{T,T_{0}(M_{0})\big\}
.

Proof.

By the assumption
u^{{\varepsilon}_{j}}\in C([0,T],H^{9}\cap H^{8,1})
, we can use a priori bounds obtained in previous propositions:
(
4.1
) and (
4.16
), (
4.14
) and (
4.21
) up to
k=4
, (
5.1
) and (
5.7
).
Since the strategy is exactly the same as the previous proposition, we skip the detailed argument and only give the resulting estimates.
We begin with

\|J(t)w(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\lesssim_{M_{0}}\|J(t)w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(t)]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}J(t)w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

and by the equation (
2.4
) for
Jw
,

\frac{d}{dt}\|J(t)w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim_{M_{1}}\|J(t)w(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|w(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}.

Next, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}Jw\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-2{\varepsilon}_{2}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}Jw\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(M_{1})\Big(\|Jw\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|w\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{4}\cap H^{3,1}}^{2}+\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{3}\cap H^{2,1}}^{4}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}Q_{\pm}\Big(\big(2\alpha|u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}|^{2}+\beta\mathcal{H}(|u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}|^{2}\big)\partial_{x}^{2}Jw\Big)\overline{Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}Jw}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+2\beta{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}\Big(Q_{\pm}\big(u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\mathcal{H}(\overline{u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}}\partial_{x}^{2}Jw)\big)-u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\overline{u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}}Q_{\pm}\mathcal{H}\partial_{x}^{2}Jw\Big)\overline{Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}Jw}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad-2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}Q_{\pm}\Big(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\partial_{x}^{2}\overline{Jw})u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\Big)\overline{Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}Jw}\,dx,

and the first two integrals on the right-hand side are estimated by
C(M_{0})\|Jw\|_{H^{1}}^{2}
using commutator estimate, integration by parts and dyadic decomposition.
We apply normal form to the last integral, which is then bounded by

\displaystyle C(M_{0})\|Jw\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{d}{dt}\Big[2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}m(\xi,\sigma)(2i)^{-1}\widehat{f}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\xi-\sigma)\overline{\widehat{h}(\xi)}\,d\eta\,d\sigma\,d\xi\Big]

\displaystyle-2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}m(\xi,\sigma)(2i)^{-1}\partial_{t}\Big[\widehat{f}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\xi-\sigma)\overline{\widehat{h}(\xi)}\Big]\,d\eta\,d\sigma\,d\xi,

where
g:=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}Jw
and
h:=e^{-it\partial_{x}^{2}}(e^{2\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}Jw)
.
The last integral is bounded by

{\varepsilon}_{2}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}Jw\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(M_{1})\Big(\|Jw\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|w\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{3}\cap H^{2,1}}^{2}\Big),

where we used the estimate

\|\partial_{t}g\|_{L^{2}}=\|(\partial_{t}-i\partial_{x}^{2})Jw\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{M_{1}}{\varepsilon}_{2}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}^{2}Jw\|_{L^{2}}+\|Jw\|_{H^{1}}+\|w\|_{H^{1}}+{\varepsilon}_{1}\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{3}\cap H^{2,1}}.

Collecting the estimates above and applying the a priori difference estimates (
5.1
), (
5.7
), we see that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(t)]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}J(t)w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\mathcal{J}_{1,1}(t)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C(M_{1})\Big(\|J(t)w(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+K_{1}\Big),

where

\mathcal{J}_{1,1}(t):=2{\rm Re}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}e^{2it\eta\sigma}m(\xi,\sigma)(2i)^{-1}\widehat{f}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\xi-\eta)\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi-\eta-\sigma)}\widehat{f}^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(\xi-\sigma)\overline{\widehat{h}(\xi)}\,d\eta\,d\sigma\,d\xi

and

\displaystyle K_{1}:=\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\big(1+\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{4}\cap H^{3,1}}^{2}+\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{3}\cap H^{2,1}}^{4}\big)+\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{3}\cap H^{2,1}}^{4}\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

We also see that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{J}_{1,1}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq C(M_{0})\|Jw\|_{L^{2}}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}Jw\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}Jw\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(M_{0})\|Jw\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Hence,

Z(t):=C(M_{1})\Big\{\|e^{\rho^{\pm}[u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(t)]}Q_{\pm}\partial_{x}J(t)w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\mathcal{J}_{1,1}(t)+C(M_{0})\|J(t)w(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big\}

satisfies

\|J(t)w(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq Z(t),\quad Z(0)\leq C(M_{1})\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{H^{1,1}}^{2}

and, again by (
5.7
),

\frac{d}{dt}Z(t)\leq C(M_{1})\Big(Z(t)+K_{1}\Big).

Therefore, Gronwall’s inequality implies the desired bound.
∎

To this end, we first recall the

Proposition 6.1

(Proposition 4.2 in

Under the hypotheses of Proposition

6.1
,

Remark 6.3
.

The growth rate

Proof of Proposition
6.2
.

If
u
is a smooth solution to (
1.1
), then a direct calculation shows

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{2})

\displaystyle=2{\rm Re}\Big[\partial_{x}^{3}\Big(i\partial_{x}u+\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(|u|^{2})u\Big)\overline{\partial_{x}^{2}u}\Big]

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}\Big[2{\rm Re}\big(i\overline{\partial_{x}^{2}u}\partial_{x}^{3}u\big)+\frac{\alpha}{2}|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{2}-2\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(\partial_{x}^{2}|u|^{2})|\partial_{x}u|^{2}+\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(|u|^{2})|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad+\beta D_{x}(\partial_{x}^{2}|u|^{2})(\partial_{x}^{2}|u|^{2})+5\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(\partial_{x}^{2}|u|^{2})(\partial_{x}|\partial_{x}u|^{2})+5\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(\partial_{x}|u|^{2})|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{2},

and hence,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)|^{2}\,dx\\
&=\beta\big\|D_{x}^{\frac{5}{2}}(|u(t)|^{2})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+5\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(\partial_{x}^{2}|u|^{2})\partial_{x}(|\partial_{x}u|^{2})\,dx+5\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(\partial_{x}|u|^{2})|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{2}\,dx.\end{aligned}

(6.1)

The second term of the RHS of (
6.1
) is estimated by

\displaystyle C\big\|\partial_{x}^{2}(|u(t)|^{2})\big\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\big\|D_{x}^{\frac{5}{2}}(|u(t)|^{2})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|D_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}(|u(t)|^{2})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\beta|}{2}\big\|D_{x}^{\frac{5}{2}}(|u(t)|^{2})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\big\|D_{x}^{\frac{3}{2}}(|u(t)|^{2})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\|\partial_{x}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\|\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where the constant
C>0
depends on
\alpha
and
\beta
.
Using Lemma
3.5
with
f:=\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(\partial_{x}|u|^{2})
, the third term of the right-hand side of (
6.1
) is estimated by

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(\partial_{x}|u|^{2})|\partial_{x}^{2}u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\|\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(\partial_{x}|u|^{2})\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\big\|\partial_{x}(|u(t)|^{2})\big\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}\log^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(e+\big\|\partial_{x}^{2}(|u(t)|^{2})\big\|_{L^{2}}\big)+\big\|\partial_{x}(|u(t)|^{2})\big\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\Big)\|\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\phi\|_{L^{2}})\Big(\big\|\partial_{x}(|u(t)|^{2})\big\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\big\|\partial_{x}(|u(t)|^{2})\big\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\Big)\|\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\log^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(e+\|\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big),

where we have used
\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}
by Proposition
6.1
.
Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{|\beta|}{2}\big\|D_{x}^{\frac{5}{2}}(|u(t)|^{2})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C_{1}^{\prime}\Big(\big\|\partial_{x}(|u(t)|^{2})\big\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\big\|\partial_{x}(|u(t)|^{2})\big\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\Big)\|\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\log^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(e+\|\partial_{x}^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)

for
t\in[0,T]
, where we have used Proposition
6.1
again and
C_{1}^{\prime}>0
depends on
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\|\phi\|_{H^{1}}
.
Noticing the fact that

f(t)\geq 1,\quad f^{\prime}(t)\leq g(t)f(t)\log^{\frac{1}{2}}f(t)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f(t)\leq\exp\Big[\Big(\log^{\frac{1}{2}}f(0)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}g(s)\,ds\Big)^{2}\Big],

and the local integrability

\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\big\|\partial_{x}(|u(s)|^{2})\big\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\big\|\partial_{x}(|u(s)|^{2})\big\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\Big)\,ds\lesssim t^{\frac{1}{2}}+t^{\frac{2}{3}}

by Proposition
6.1
and Hölder, we obtain the desired bound.
∎

Appendix A
Proof of local well-posedness

In this section, we give a proof of Theorem
1.1
.
To this end, we introduce the following lemma.

Lemma A.1
.

For any
{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
and
k\geq 2
integer, the regularized Cauchy problem (
2.1
) is locally well-posed in
H^{k}\cap H^{k-1,1}
.
The local existence time may be chosen as
T\sim{\varepsilon}(1+\|\phi\|_{H^{k}\cap H^{k-1,1}})^{-4}
.

Proof.

Let
\{U_{\varepsilon}(t):=e^{t(i\partial_{x}^{2}+{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}^{2})}\}_{t\geq 0}
be the semigroup of the propagator for the linear equation
(\partial_{t}-i\partial_{x}^{2}-{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}^{2})u=0
.
Define

\|u\|_{X_{T}}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(\|u(t)\|_{H^{k}}+\|J(t)u(t)\|_{H^{k-1}}\Big),

then
\|u\|_{X_{T}}\sim\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{H^{k}\cap H^{k-1,1}}
.
It suffices to prove that the map

\Phi^{\varepsilon}_{\phi}:u\mapsto U_{\varepsilon}(t)\phi+\int_{0}^{t}U_{\varepsilon}(t-s)\partial_{x}\big[\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(|u|^{2})u\big](s)\,ds

is a contraction on a complete metric space

\Big\{u\in C([0,T],H^{k}\cap H^{k-1,1})\,:\,\|u\|_{X_{T}}\leq C\big(\|\phi\|_{H^{k}}+\|x\phi\|_{H^{k-1}}\big)\Big\}

with the metric induced by
\|\cdot\|_{X_{T}}
. Recalling
[J(t),\partial_{t}-i\partial_{x}^{2}]=0
and
[J(t),\partial_{x}^{2}]=-2\partial_{x}
, we see that

\displaystyle J(t)\Big[U_{\varepsilon}(t)\phi+\int_{0}^{t}U_{\varepsilon}(t-s)F(s)\,ds\Big]

\displaystyle=U_{\varepsilon}(t)(x\phi)+\int_{0}^{t}U_{\varepsilon}(t-s)\Big[J(s)F(s)-2{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}\Big(U_{\varepsilon}(s)\phi+\int_{0}^{s}U_{\varepsilon}(s-r)F(r)\,dr\Big)\Big]\,ds

\displaystyle=U_{\varepsilon}(t)\big[x\phi-2{\varepsilon}t\partial_{x}\phi\big]+\int_{0}^{t}U_{\varepsilon}(t-s)\big[J(s)F(s)-2{\varepsilon}(t-s)\partial_{x}F(s)\big]\,ds.

Using this and the Leibniz rule for
J(t)
, together with the parabolic smoothing estimate

\Big\|\int_{0}^{t}U_{\varepsilon}(t-s)\partial_{x}F(s)\,ds\Big\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{k}}\lesssim{\varepsilon}^{-\frac{1}{2}}T^{\frac{1}{2}}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{k}}

and that
H^{k-1}
is an algebra, we can easily show that
\Phi^{\varepsilon}_{\phi}
is indeed a contraction.
Note that the continuity of the map
t\mapsto\Phi^{\varepsilon}_{\phi}[u](t)\in H^{k}\cap H^{k-1,1}
is also verified by a standard argument.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\phi\in H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}
be any initial data, and define
\phi^{{\varepsilon}}:=P_{\leq{\varepsilon}^{-\lambda}}\phi
for
{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
, with any
\lambda\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
.
Since
\mathcal{F}\big([x,P_{\leq N}]\phi\big)(\xi)=i\varrho^{\prime}_{\leq N}(\xi)\widehat{\phi}(\xi)
and
\|\varrho^{\prime}_{\leq N}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim N^{-1}
, we see
\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}}\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}}\lesssim\|\phi\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}}
uniformly in
{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
and
\phi^{{\varepsilon}}\to\phi
in
H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}
as
{\varepsilon}\to+0
.
Moreover, from the fact that
\varrho^{\prime}_{\leq N}(\xi)
is supported in
\{|\xi|\sim N\}
, we have
\phi^{\varepsilon}\in H^{k}\cap H^{k-1,1}
for any
k\geq 2
and

\displaystyle\|\phi^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{k}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\varepsilon}^{-(k-2)\lambda}\|\phi^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{2}},

\displaystyle\|x\phi^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{k-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\varepsilon}^{-(k-2)\lambda}\big(\|x\phi^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}}+{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}\|\phi^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}}\big)\qquad(k>2),

\displaystyle\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2\lambda}\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{H^{2}}\qquad(0<{\varepsilon}_{2}<{\varepsilon}_{1}<1).

From these inequalities, it holds that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}\big(1+\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{4}\cap H^{3,1}}^{2}+\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{3}\cap H^{2,1}}^{4}\big)+\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}\|_{H^{3}\cap H^{2,1}}^{4}\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim_{\|\phi\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}}}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{2\gamma}+\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{H^{2}}^{2},\end{aligned}

(A.1)

where
\gamma:=\frac{1}{2}(2-4\lambda)>0
.

Let
u^{\varepsilon}
be a unique smooth solution of the regularized equation (
2.1
) with the parameter
{\varepsilon}
and the initial data
\phi^{\varepsilon}
.
From Lemma
A.1
and the a priori estimates given in Propositions
4.1
and
4.3
, the solution
u^{\varepsilon}
exists on a uniform-in-
{\varepsilon}
interval
[0,T_{0}]
with
T_{0}=T_{0}(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}})>0
.
Then, by the difference estimates in Propositions
5.3
,
5.4
and (
A.1
), we have

\displaystyle\|u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}(t)-u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(t)\|_{H^{2}}+\|J(t)[u^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}(t)-u^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}(t)]\|_{H^{1}}\leq C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}})\Big(\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{1}}-\phi^{{\varepsilon}_{2}}\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}}+{\varepsilon}_{1}^{\gamma}\Big)

for any

.
Therefore,
\{u^{\varepsilon}\}_{{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)}\subset C([0,T_{0}],H^{2}\cap H^{1,1})
is Cauchy as

and has a limit

such that

\displaystyle\|u^{{\varepsilon}}-u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{0}}H^{2}}+\|J[u^{{\varepsilon}}-u]\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{0}}H^{1}}\leq C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}})\Big(\|\phi^{{\varepsilon}}-\phi\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}}+{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}\Big).

(A.2)

Moreover, the limit
u
is a solution to (
1.1
).
From the
L^{2}
Lipschitz estimate in Corollary
5.2
, the solution

is unique in
C([0,T_{0}],H^{2})
.

To show the continuous dependence on initial data, assume that a sequence
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j}\subset H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}
converges to
\phi
in
H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}
.
We approximate
\phi_{j}
in the same way, by
\phi_{j}^{\varepsilon}:=P_{\leq{\varepsilon}^{-\lambda}}\phi_{j}
, and let the corresponding solutions be denoted by

(for sufficiently large

Applying Propositions
5.3
,
5.4
(with

), we see that

\displaystyle\|u^{{\varepsilon}}_{j}-u^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{0}}H^{2}}+\|J[u^{{\varepsilon}}_{j}-u^{\varepsilon}]\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{0}}H^{1}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(\|\phi\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}})\Big\{\|P_{\leq{\varepsilon}^{-\lambda}}(\phi_{j}-\phi)\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}}+{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}+\|P_{\leq{\varepsilon}^{-\lambda}}(\phi_{j}-\phi)\|_{L^{2}}{\varepsilon}^{-2\lambda}\Big\}.

Combining it with (

\displaystyle\limsup_{j\to\infty}\Big(\|u_{j}-u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{0}}H^{2}}+\|J[u_{j}-u]\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{0}}H^{1}}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\limsup_{j\to\infty}\Big(\|P_{>{\varepsilon}^{-\lambda}}\phi^{j}\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}}+\|P_{>{\varepsilon}^{-\lambda}}\phi\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}}+{\varepsilon}^{\gamma}+\|\phi_{j}-\phi\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1,1}}+\|\phi_{j}-\phi\|_{L^{2}}{\varepsilon}^{-2\lambda}\Big)

in

.

Let us move on to prove persistence of regularity. Suppose that
\phi\in H^{k}\cap H^{k-1,1}
for some

.
This means the approximating initial data

.
Then, a priori bounds in Propositions

and

show that the solutions

.
Hence, for each

Enhanced human pose estimation via feature-enriched HRNet in smart classroom scenarios
Authors: Dongxu Li, Zhaoyu Shou, Xiaohu Yuan, Ziyong Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Human pose estimation (HPE) is crucial for analyzing student behavioral dynamics and developing instructional evaluations in smart classrooms. However, in complex scenarios such as densely distributed students, existing methods often face challenges in keypoint feature extraction and localization accuracy. To address these issues, we propose a Feature-enhanced high-resolution network (FE-HRNet) for human pose estimation. The model first incorporates Res2Net modules into the backbone network, constructing a hierarchical residual connection structure to achieve fine-grained multi-scale feature representation and effectively expanding the network’s receptive field. Second, we innovatively embed a Multi-scale convolution attention (MSCA) module, which captures spatial context information at different scales through multi-branch depth-wise stripe convolutions and combines channel attention mechanisms to enhance key features, significantly improving keypoint localization capability adaptively. Finally, experimental results on the COCO public dataset and our custom-developed Smart classroom pose (SCP) dataset validate that the proposed method delivers superior pose estimation performance in complex scenarios. The code is available at https://github.com/ldxguet/FEHRNet.

Introduction

Human pose estimation (HPE) techniques aim to locate body joints and construct human pose representations from visual data such as images and video sequences. In smart classroom scenarios, by precisely capturing the dynamic changes in students’ body postures, it is possible to quantitatively assess classroom engagement, analyze learning states at different teaching stages, and provide important evidence for characterizing students’ cognitive levels and behavioral patterns. Meanwhile, these posture data can help teachers evaluate the effects of classroom teaching promptly, providing reliable data support for achieving precise teaching and personalized interventions. HPE technology can be divided into two categories: two-dimensional human pose estimation (2D HPE)1 and three-dimensional human pose estimation (3D HPE)2. 2D HPE aims to accurately locate and identify key joint positions of the human body using two-dimensional images or video sequences. Such methods mainly use convolutional neural networks (CNN) to process input images and predict joint positions and connection relationships by generating heatmaps or vector fields. Existing network architectures mainly include top-down and bottom-up methods: the former first detects the human body and then estimates the pose3; the latter directly estimates all visible joints and then groups them4. 3D HPE aims to reconstruct the human body’s three-dimensional skeletal structure and joint positions from two-dimensional images or video sequences, building a complete human skeletal model in three-dimensional space. Unlike 2D HPE, which is based solely on single-directional images, 3D HPE requires the combined analysis of multi-directional images.

In a smart classroom, detecting keypoints for multiple densely distributed students from single-view images presents significant technical challenges for multi-person pose estimation. The large student population and compact seating arrangements create unprecedented complexity that distinguishes classroom scenarios from conventional pose estimation tasks. Specifically, classroom scenes exhibit four key complexities: (1) Dense person distribution is characterized by the classroom environment simultaneously accommodating 55 students, achieving a spatial coverage ratio of 63.3% across the total image area with an average inter-person distance of 607 pixels. (2) Severe occlusion conditions are prevalent, with students experiencing an average occlusion rate of 23.5% and some individuals suffering from occlusion levels as high as 76%. (3) Significant scale variations exist among detection targets, where student sizes range from 64 to 464 pixels, yielding a maximum scale ratio of 7.25:1 between different individuals and substantially exceeding the scale variation range typically encountered in conventional scenarios. (4) Spatial overlap phenomena are widespread throughout the scene, with students exhibiting an average overlap degree of 10.4% and a maximum overlap extending to 33%. The cumulative effect of these factors creates a compound challenge that significantly amplifies the technical difficulty of pose estimation tasks. The combination of high person density and severe occlusion conditions leads to overlapping feature responses between adjacent targets. At the same time, the pronounced scale variations impose heightened demands on multi-scale feature extraction capabilities. The widespread spatial overlap phenomenon further exacerbates these challenges by increasing the difficulty of accurate keypoint assignment. Traditional pose estimation methods demonstrate inherent performance limitations when processing such high-density multi-person scenarios, thereby necessitating specialized architectural innovations specifically designed to accommodate the unique technical demands of a smart classroom. This paper explores the top-down approach to address the challenges of multi-person dense pose estimation in smart classroom environments. In such dense scenarios, existing methods often struggle to effectively estimate poses for multiple individuals. Current large models5 used for human pose keypoint estimation achieve high mean Average Precision (mAP), but their extensive parameter scale demands excessive computational resources. Conversely, lightweight models6 offer greater computational efficiency but fail to reach the desired mAP levels. Meanwhile, medium and small models7 attempt to attain a balance between performance and computational efficiency. This paper proposes a novel network architecture that combines Res2Net8 and a feature enhancement attention mechanism, MSCA9, with the HRNet10 backbone network, leading to significant improvements in pose estimation performance while maintaining a moderate parameter scale. To thoroughly evaluate the model’s effectiveness in practical applications, this paper introduces the smart classroom pose (SCP) dataset, which includes annotation data for 13 human keypoints (left and right eyes, nose, left and right ears, left and right shoulders, left and right elbows, left and right wrists, and left and right hips) within smart classroom contexts, providing a reliable benchmark for pose estimation research in dense scenarios. Related work

2D HPE mainly encompasses two primary research streams: single-person pose estimation11 and multi-person pose estimation12. The single-person approach focuses predominantly on accurately determining the posture of an individual human figure within an image. Following the extensive adoption of CNNs, researchers have engineered increasingly sophisticated network architectures to capture the varied configurations of human joints. However, when multiple humans appear within an image, the problem complexity increases substantially, necessitating the detection of individual joints and their proper assignment to the corresponding person. To address multi-person pose estimation challenges, researchers have established two principal methods: bottom-up13 and top-down14. Bottom-up methods initially identify all potential keypoints throughout the image before associating those belonging to the same individual with complete pose representations. The method exhibits robust performance in scenarios with human occlusion. However, it demands keypoint identification and grouping operations across extensive pixel arrays, resulting in relatively significant computational overhead. Top-down methods initially locate human regions within the image using person detection algorithms and then perform pose estimation for each identified area. This method effectively handles pose estimation across various scales. However, the quality of the person detector fundamentally affects the accuracy of the resulting pose estimates.

In top-down methods, Xiao et al.14 incorporated deconvolution layers into the ResNet backbone to construct an architecture capable of effectively representing high-resolution heatmaps. Wang et al.10 introduced the High-Resolution Network (HRNet), building upon this foundation to obtain detailed spatial representations through concurrent processing and cross-scale integration of features at multiple resolutions while consistently maintaining high-resolution feature maps in the principal pathway, substantially enhancing pose estimation performance, and establishing the foundation for subsequent research. Hong et al.15 proposed a geometry-aware feature representation method based on Stacked Capsule Graph Autoencoders (SCGAE), which enhances capsule autoencoders by incorporating graph regularization to model geometric relationships between facial parts. This unsupervised approach performs better than existing 3D head pose estimation methods. Huang et al.16 developed an Unbiased Data Processing (UDP) approach to address the quantization error challenge in human pose estimation applications, which resolves systematic bias in conventional data processing through balanced coordinate system transformations and unbiased keypoint format conversions. To minimize the computational demands of pose estimation frameworks, Yu et al.6 introduced Lite-HRNet, derived from HRNet, pioneering an efficient lightweight high-resolution network architecture and implementing effective feature integration through a conditional channel weighting mechanism, offering a novel approach to keypoint localization. Li et al.17 further optimized and designed Dite-HRNet, enhancing spatial feature expression through dynamic kernel aggregation and introducing adaptive contextual pooling to improve keypoint localization accuracy. Zhang et al.18 proposed a lightweight high-resolution human pose estimation network, HF-HRNet, designed for mobile devices, solving the problem of slow inference speed on devices such as NPUs by designing Hardware-Friendly Uniform-sized Mug (HUM) modules (including two core components: the Cascaded Depthwise (CAD) and Multi-scale context embedding (MCE)). It significantly improved model performance while ensuring pose estimation accuracy. Computer vision has successfully developed numerous Transformer-based human pose estimation frameworks with the advent of Transformer architectures19. TransPose20 employs Transformer encoders to establish relationships between pixel elements; TokenPose21 assigns each keypoint as a discrete token while extracting contextual and visual information from images; HRformer22 integrates Transformer modules into the HRNet framework to capture comprehensive spatial context, thereby enhancing keypoint localization precision. ViTPose5 leverages Visual Transformer encoding capabilities to process holistic human region information and implements an efficient decoder to predict joint coordinates with high accuracy, delivering exceptional performance in human pose estimation tasks. SADNet23 addresses the challenges of large parameter sizes and high latency in Transformer models for real-time VR pose estimation by introducing lightweight LiteTrans blocks to replace traditional Transformer structures and employing knowledge distillation techniques to achieve an optimal balance between accuracy and efficiency. HOGFormer24 integrates High-Order Graph Convolution (HOGConv) with a Graph-based Dynamic Adjacency Matrix Transformer (GDAMFormer) to address fundamental challenges in monocular 3D human pose estimation. The proposed architecture explicitly captures higher-order spatial dependencies between joints while learning adaptive topological structures, thereby effectively mitigating depth ambiguity inherent in single-view input and joint occlusion challenges that commonly affect 3D pose estimation performance. In terms of optimizing feature enhancement mechanisms, Xin et al.25 improve detection performance by incorporating hybrid dilated convolution networks (HDC) and multi-level feature spatial pyramids (MFSP) to enhance image feature representation. Huang et al.26 introduce a lightweight Channel MLP (CM) module that directly integrates channel attention mechanisms with multi-layer perceptrons on feature tokens. The proposed CM module achieves significant performance improvements in Transformer-based human pose estimation while maintaining comparable computational complexity and parameter overhead. Chen et al.27 propose MFFN, which utilizes multi-level feature fusion to improve image reconstruction quality while maintaining an efficient network architecture. Wang et al.28 proposed ECANet, which achieved significant performance improvement while minimizing parameter size through a local cross-channel interaction strategy. Bao et al.29 organically combined ECANet with HRNet, demonstrating excellent performance in complex scenarios such as fast movement. Yuan et al.30 introduced a Native Sparse Attention (NSA), effectively resolving the inefficiency issue of traditional attention mechanisms in context processing by combining coarse-grained token compression, fine-grained token selection, and sliding window technology. These works provide important inspiration for the design of efficient pose estimation networks.

Top-down methods address overlap challenges by predicting multiple human instances within a single bounding box; however, performance improvements remain limited. The backbone network, serving as the fundamental feature extractor, plays a pivotal role in feature representation learning and overlap handling; however, the efficacy of most contemporary top-down approaches is limited by the capacity of conventional backbone architectures. Current research predominantly improves accuracy through increased model parameter complexity, while achieving high precision with moderate parameter counts remains a significant challenge. Additionally, performance robustness in crowded human scenarios requires urgent enhancement. To address these issues, this paper proposes a Feature-Enhanced High-Resolution Network (FE-HRNet) that demonstrates significant advantages in both accuracy and practicality.

Feature-enhanced high-resolution network for human pose estimation: FE-HRNet

The proposed FE-HRNet is designed based on the backbone network HRNet. As shown in Fig. 1, FE-HRNet incorporates four parallel feature branches, with the parallel branches operating at progressively decreasing resolution scales of 1, 1/2, 1/4, and 1/8 relative to the input resolution, and the corresponding feature channel numbers expanded to 32, 64, 128, and 256, respectively. The feature extraction pathway initially downsamples the input image resolution to 1/4 through two 3 × 3 convolutional layers with a stride of 2. The network then proceeds through four main stages for hierarchical feature extraction. The first stage introduces the hierarchical residual connection structure of Res2Net, implementing multiple fine-grained feature extractions through four improved residual units. The second, third, and fourth stages contain one, four, and three multi-resolution modules, each comprising parallel multi-resolution convolution and multi-resolution fusion along with four residual units, where each unit contains two 3 × 3 convolutions in different resolution branches. At the end of each stage, MSCA modules are connected to extract more detailed category and spatial context information. Finally, keypoint heatmaps are generated through multi-scale feature fusion. Each module will be explained in detail.

MSCA module

The MSCA (Multi-Scale Convolution Attention)9 module is an efficient attention whose core architecture consists of three parts. First is a 5 × 5 depth-wise separable convolution layer for aggregating local information from input features. Second is a multi-branch depth-wise stripe convolution structure, where each branch adopts two orthogonal one-dimensional depth-wise separable convolutions to decompose standard convolution, specifically (1 × 7, 7 × 1), (1 × 11, 11 × 1), and (1 × 21, 21 × 1). This decomposition strategy significantly reduces computational complexity and efficiently captures multi-scale contextual information by approximating 7 × 7, 11 × 11, and 21 × 21 two-dimensional convolution operations. Finally, there is a 1 × 1 convolution layer for modelling relationships between different channels, whose output directly serves as attention weights for element-wise multiplication with input features. Through an adaptive fusion of multi-scale features, it achieves dynamic feature enhancement in both spatial and channel dimensions, effectively handling target and detail feature expressions at different scales. Experiments and results

To demonstrate the performance of FE-HRNet, experimental evaluations were conducted on both the COCO dataset and the Smart classroom pose (SCP) dataset, with comparative analyses against mainstream human pose estimation models, including ResNet5014, ResNet10114, HRNet10, and ECA-HRNet29. To thoroughly validate the advancement of the proposed model, the recently proposed NSA30 was integrated into the HRNet architecture (NSA-HRNet) as an important comparative benchmark. Additionally, a series of ablation experiments was performed to verify the effectiveness of the proposed method.

Dataset introduction

COCO2017 dataset: The COCO2017 dataset (Common Objects in Context 2017) is a comprehensive multi-object detection dataset introduced by the Microsoft team, widely applied in research areas such as image segmentation, semantic segmentation, and image captioning generation. This dataset defines 17 keypoints for human annotation and contains 118,287 training images, 5,000 validation images, and 40,670 test images, with all objects positioned through precise segmentation annotations.

SCP dataset: The SCP dataset is constructed based on real classroom teaching scenarios, systematically recording the classroom teaching process throughout an entire semester. This dataset encompasses 7,300 images and 18,000 student posture data instances, with the training set containing 15,000 instances and the test set containing 3,000 instances. The dataset records and tracks changes in student classroom postures throughout the semester, providing comprehensive data support for researching student behavioural patterns in specific subject domains.

Experimental environment

The networks were implemented using an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU, employing the Adaptive Moment Estimation optimizer with Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss function. The initial learning rate was configured at 0.005, with batch size 64, and networks were trained for 210 epochs. Input dimensions were standardized at 256 × 192, with implementation based on the PyTorch framework. For performance evaluation, mean Average Precision (mAP) was adopted as the primary metric, which is the standard evaluation measure for keypoint detection in human pose estimation. This method aligns with established metrics for pose estimation on the COCO dataset. Conclusion

This paper proposes a feature-enhanced human pose estimation model designed to improve pose estimation performance in innovative classroom environments. The proposed model integrates hierarchical residual connections and a multi-scale feature attention mechanism within the HRNet backbone architecture. Experimental validation across multiple datasets confirms the effectiveness of the proposed approach. On the COCO public dataset, FE-HRNet achieves 78.02 mAP, outperforming baseline models including ResNet50, ResNet101, HRNet, ECA-HRNet, and NSA-HRNet. When evaluated on the SCP dataset specifically constructed for smart classroom scenarios, the model attains 98.00 mAP, showing competitive performance against existing methods and validating its practical applicability. While these results are promising, challenges persist in maintaining detection accuracy under student overlap and occlusion conditions. Future work will explore enhanced feature extraction strategies for occluded regions and investigate multi-scale temporal feature fusion approaches. These developments aim to improve human pose estimation accuracy in crowded scenarios, providing robust theoretical and technical foundations for personalized teaching in smart education systems.

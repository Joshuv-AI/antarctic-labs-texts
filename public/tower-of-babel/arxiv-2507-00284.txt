Modeling the Cosmological Lyman– α \alpha Forest at the Field Level Preprint: MIT-CTP/5882
Roger de Belsunce, Mikhail M. Ivanov, James M. Sullivan, Kazuyuki Akitsu, Shi-Fan Chen

Abstract
The distribution of absorption lines in the spectra of distant quasars, called the Lyman- α \alpha (Ly- α \alpha ) forest, is a unique probe of cosmology and the intergalactic medium at high redshifts and small scales. The statistical power of ongoing redshift surveys demands precise theoretical tools to model the Ly- α \alpha forest. We address this challenge by developing an analytic, perturbative forward model to predict the Ly- α \alpha forest at the field level for a given set of cosmological initial conditions. Our model shows a remarkable performance when compared with the Sherwood hydrodynamic simulations: it reproduces the flux distribution, the Ly- α \alpha –dark matter halo cross-correlations, and the count-in-cell statistics at the percent level down to scales of a few Mpc. Our work provides crucial tools that bridge analytic modeling on large scales with simulations on small-scales, enabling field-level inference from Ly- α \alpha forest data and simulation-based priors for cosmological analyses. This is especially timely for realizing the full scientific potential of the Ly- α \alpha forest measurements by the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument.

k\approx 0.6~h{\text{Mpc}}^{-1}
, where
it starts making a sizable
contribution to the
total Ly-
\alpha
power.
This behavior suggests
a breakdown of the gradient
expansion for the stochastic
component.
At face value,
modeling
this non-trivial scale-dependence of the stochastic noise may
pose a serious challenge
to the EFT-based cosmological inference.
However, in a companion paper, we show
that the flattening of the
noise power spectrum mirrors
the behavior of dark matter
power spectrum in redshift space,
which suggest that this
noise might be captured
if the EFT expansion is
applied to the full non-linear
matter field from an N-body simulation.
7
7

7

In the context of galaxy clustering such an approach
is known as the hybrid EFT (HEFT;
Modi:2019qbt
;
Hadzhiyska:2021xbv
;
2021MNRAS.505.1422K
;
Sullivan_Chen_LPNG_HEFT
).

We leave this prospective
line of research for future
exploration.

Summary and discussion.
—In this
Letter
, we present the first
analytic forward model for the Ly-
\alpha
forest flux decrement that works successfully
at the field level.
Our approach bridges the gap between theory and simulation by enabling efficient modeling of the flux field directly, rather than only its summary statistics.
We find excellent agreement between our model and the Sherwood hydrodynamic simulations for the Ly-
\alpha
forest and halo fields on quasi-linear scales: we reproduce the Ly-
\alpha
power spectrum at the 5%-level up to
k\lower 2.15277pt\hbox{$\;\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\sim}\limits^{<}}\;$}0.6\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}
and achieve accurate counts-in-cells down to
\sim 1-2\,h^{-1}\,{\rm Mpc}

cell radii. We achieve somewhat better results for the Ly-
\alpha
–halo cross-spectrum, reaching scales up to
k\lower 2.15277pt\hbox{$\;\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\sim}\limits^{<}}\;$}1\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}
with a similar result for the counts-in-cells.

Our work opens up
opportunities for multiple
high-impact contributions
to Ly-
\alpha
cosmology.
Our model can be used to bridge
the Ly-
\alpha
forest maps
on large and small scales by
analytically generating
the large-scale models whose
properties match the
small-volume high-resolution
simulations (e.g. Illustris TNG

;
Pillepich:2017jle
, ACCEL
2

, CAMELS

;
CAMELS_DR1
, PRIYA
Bird:2023evb
).
This will remove the computational bottleneck of large-volume hydrodynamic simulations and
enable efficient simulation-based inference from the Ly-
\alpha
fields.
In analogy with
galaxy clustering, this can be done in multiple
ways: using the

Nguyen:2020hxe

,
hybrid EFT-simulation-based-inference approaches

,
or building

\alpha

.
Another application
is the generation of semi-analytic
simulations
to estimate covariance matrices,

\alpha
forest
and quasars from DESI.
Our approach can also be used for precision measurements of the
Ly-
\alpha
EFT parameters at the field level without sample variance,

Akitsu:2023eqa

Details of the forward model.
—We now introduce the EFT theory model, briefly summarizing Ref.
Ivanov:2023yla
, to which we refer the reader for a fuller presentation. The Ly-
\alpha
forest flux decrement,
\delta_{F}=F/\bar{F}-1
, can be described perturbatively up to cubic order using the following, generalized form

\begin{split}\delta^{(s)}_{F}({\bm{k}})=&\sum_{n=1}^{3}\Big[\prod_{j=1}^{n}\int\frac{d^{3}{\bf k}_{j}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\delta^{(1)}({\bm{k}}_{j})\Big]K_{n}({\bm{k}}_{1},...,{\bm{k}}_{n})\times(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}_{D}({\bm{k}}-{\bm{k}}_{1}-...-{\bm{k}}_{j})\,,\end{split}

(S1)

where
f
is the logarithmic growth factor,
\mu\equiv k_{\parallel}/k
with the cosine of the angle
to the line-of-sight,
\delta_{D}
the Dirac delta function,
\delta^{(1)}
the linear density field and
K_{n}
are the perturbative kernels in redshift space (for galaxies these are denoted
Z
; see e.g.
Bernardeau:2001CPT
for a review)

\begin{split}&K_{1}({\bm{k}})=b_{1}-b_{\eta}f\mu^{2}\,,\\
&K_{2}({\bm{k}}_{1},{\bm{k}}_{2})=\frac{b_{2}}{2}+b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}}\left(\frac{({\bm{k}}_{1}\cdot{\bm{k}}_{2})^{2}}{k_{1}^{2}k_{2}^{2}}-1\right)+b_{1}F_{2}({\bm{k}}_{1},{\bm{k}}_{2})-b_{\eta}f\mu^{2}G_{2}({\bm{k}}_{1},{\bm{k}}_{2})-fb_{\delta\eta}\frac{\mu_{2}^{2}+\mu_{1}^{2}}{2}+b_{\eta^{2}}f^{2}\mu_{1}^{2}\mu_{2}^{2}\\
&+b_{1}f\frac{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}{2}\left(\frac{k_{2}}{k_{1}}+\frac{k_{1}}{k_{2}}\right)-b_{\eta}f^{2}\frac{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}{2}\left(\frac{k_{2}}{k_{1}}\mu_{2}^{2}+\frac{k_{1}}{k_{2}}\mu_{1}^{2}\right)+b_{(KK)_{\parallel}}\left(\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\frac{({\bm{k}}_{1}\cdot{\bm{k}}_{2})}{k_{1}k_{2}}-\frac{\mu_{1}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}}{3}+\frac{1}{9}\right)\\
&+b_{\Pi^{(2)}_{\parallel}}\left(\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\frac{({\bm{k}}_{1}\cdot{\bm{k}}_{2})}{k_{1}k_{2}}+\frac{5}{7}\mu^{2}\left(1-\frac{({\bm{k}}_{1}\cdot{\bm{k}}_{2})^{2}}{k_{1}^{2}k_{2}^{2}}\right)\right)\,,\end{split}

(S2)

where
K_{3}
is given in Eq. (4.16) in Ref.
Ivanov:2023yla
and
F_{2}
and
G_{2}
are the density and velocity kernels from standard cosmological perturbation theory
Bernardeau:2001CPT
, respectively. Each quadratic operator above can be written as

\mathcal{O}({\bm{k}})=\int_{\bm{p}}F_{\mathcal{O}}({\bm{k}}_{1},{\bm{k}}_{2})\delta_{1}({\bm{k}}-{\bm{p}})\delta_{1}({\bm{p}})\,,

(S3)

where
\int_{\bm{p}}=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}
. At linear order the free coefficients (or bias parameters) are
\{b_{1},\,b_{\eta}\}
with six additional parameters at quadratic order
\{b_{2}\equiv b_{\delta^{2}},b_{\mathcal{G}_{2}},b_{(KK)_{\parallel}},b_{\delta\eta},b_{\eta^{2}},b_{\Pi^{[2]}_{\parallel}}\}
and an additional five parameters at cubic order
\{b_{\Gamma_{3}},b_{\delta\Pi^{[2]}_{\parallel}},b_{\eta\Pi^{[2]}_{\parallel}},b_{(K\Pi^{[2]})_{\parallel}},b_{\Pi^{[3]}_{\parallel}}\}
in the linear density field
\delta^{(1)}
.
Following the nomenclature of Eq. (
9
) we denote the bias parameters as
b_{\mathcal{O}}
.

The cubic forward model
is obtained by
promoting the linear and
quadratic
bias parameters to transfer functions. In this case all cubic
operators from
K_{3}
are absorbed
into the linear transfer
function
\beta_{1}
.
Note that the contribution of the
{\Pi^{(2)}_{\parallel}}

operator can be completely absorbed
into other operators.
Equivalently, the contribution of
{\Pi^{(2)}_{\parallel}}

vanishes as a result of the Gram-Schmidt
orthogonalization process
Schmittfull:2018yuk
.
Another operator
that appears
in our forward model is the Zel’dovich matter
density
in redshift space,

\delta_{Z}({\bm{k}})=\int d^{3}\bm{q}~e^{-i{\bm{k}}\cdot(\bm{q}+{\bm{\psi}}(\bm{q})+f\hat{\bm{z}}({\bm{\psi}}(\bm{q})\cdot\hat{\bm{z}}))}~\,,

(S4)

where
\bm{q}
is the Lagrangian (initial)
coordinate and
\hat{\bm{z}}

is the line-of-sight
unit vector. Using

\delta_{Z}
we
can build the
correct
redshift-space distortion
contributions that define the
\eta
field.
Specifically,
\delta_{Z}-\frac{3}{7}f\mu^{2}\mathcal{G}_{2}

is equivalent to
\eta

from Eq. (
S2
)
at the quadratic order,
which we use to define
the
\tilde{\eta}^{\perp}

operator.
This operator starts
at the quadratic order
as its linear contribution
is fully degenerate with
\delta_{1}
and hence it
vanishes upon orthogonalization.
Finally, we also add the
\delta^{3}

operator and with the appropriate transfer function,
which is needed to ensure that the
error power spectrum
does not receive constant contributions
from the deterministic
fields up to the three-loop
power spectrum order
Schmittfull:2018yuk
.
Note that all operators in our forward model are “shifted”
by the Zel’dovich displacement

{\bm{\psi}}
as

\tilde{\mathcal{O}}({\bm{k}})=\int d^{3}\bm{q}~\mathcal{O}(\bm{q})e^{-i{\bm{k}}\cdot(\bm{q}+{\bm{\psi}}(\bm{q})+f\hat{\bm{n}}({\bm{\psi}}(\bm{q})\cdot\hat{\bm{n}}))}~\,.

(S5)

In particular, this
reproduces
contributions from the
“shifts”
of the linear fields

\delta
and
\eta
,
whose coefficients
must be fixed by the linear bias parameters
b_{1}

and
b_{\eta}

by the equivalence
principle.

Real-World Outcomes of Iptacopan in C3 Glomerulopathy and Primary Immune Complex–Mediated Membranoproliferative Glomerulonephritis
Authors: Giulia Bassanese, Ariel Martje Weingarten, Maddalena Marasà, Maria Cristina Mancuso, Luis F Quintana Porras, Soumita Bagchi, Federico Alberici, Olivia Boyer, Luigi Cirillo, Michaela Geßner, Domenico Giannese, Günter Klaus, Jerome Harambat, Raquel López Hidalgo, Patricia Hirt-Minkowski, Ana Huerta, Arenn Jauhal, Sebastian Loos, Sigrid Lundberg, Federica Maritati, Annick Massart, Aude Servais, Renato Alberto Sinico, Jutta Schröder-Braunstein, Marina Vivarelli, Franz Schaefer, The CompCure Consortium
Published: 2026

Introduction

C3 glomerulopathy (C3G) and primary immune complex-membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis (IC-MPGN) are rare kidney diseases driven by complement dysregulation.1,2 Current treatment relies on supportive care and nonspecific immunosuppression,2 highlighting the need for targeted approaches such as proximal complement inhibition.3 Iptacopan, an oral and selective inhibitor of factor B, has demonstrated efficacy and safety in phase 2 and randomized phase 3 clinical studies in adults with C3G,3, 4, 5 leading to regulatory approval.6 We report real-world outcomes of iptacopan in patients ineligible for clinical trials or treated outside formal study programs.

A full protocol is provided in the Supplementary Methods.

Results

Patient Characteristics

Patient were identified through the ERKNet CompCure Registry7 and an international survey of non-ERKNet centers. On diagnosis, the median urinary protein-to-creatinine ratio (uPCR) was 4.2 [interquartile range, 2.92–5.20] g/g and the mean estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was 81.1 (35.6) ml/min per 1.73 m2. Patients with IC-MPGN were older at onset than those with C3G (median age, 34.5 [19.0–48.3] vs. 17.9 [14.8–22.8] years) and had numerically lower uPCR (3.48 [2.44–4.42] vs. 4.40 [3.14–5.24] g/g) and eGFR (59 [29] vs. 76 [39] ml/min per 1.73 m2).

Treatment History and Disease Activity Before Iptacopan Administration

All but 2 patients received renin-angiotensin system inhibitors (Table 1). Before iptacopan, 25% received intravenous steroid pulses, 64% received oral prednisone, 86% received mycophenolate mofetil, and 18% received a calcineurin inhibitor. After these treatments, proteinuria transiently improved (nadir uPCR of 1.31 [0.60–2.75] g/g) before relapsing. Transient complete remission according to the predefined age-specific thresholds (Supplementary Methods) occurred in 3 C3 glomerulonephritis patients. At initiation of iptacopan therapy, median uPCR was 3.50 [1.55–6.20] g/g.

Mean eGFR temporarily increased from 81 (36) to 100 (41) ml/min per 1.73 m2 but subsequently decreased again to 72 (38) ml/min per 1.73 m2 before iptacopan administration, including 7 (of whom 2 IC-MPGN) patients with eGFR < 30 ml/min per 1.73 m2 (Table 1).

Iptacopan Treatment Efficacy

At initiation of iptacopan, median uPCR was 3.50 [1.6–6.2] g/g, mean eGFR had declined to 72.3 (38.3) ml/min per 1.73 m2 (with 7 patients < 30 ml/min per 1.73 m2), and serum C3 levels remained low in 26 of 28 patients. The treatment was started at a median [interquartile range] time of 3.1 [1.9–5.1] years after diagnosis and was administered for a mean duration of 15.8 (10.6) months. Overall, 6 patients were managed in pediatric nephrology units, including 4 with C3 glomerulonephritis, 1 with dense deposit disease, and 1 with IC-MPGN. Most patients continued background renin-angiotensin system inhibitors, whereas a subset remained on mycophenolate mofetil or low-dose corticosteroids (Table 1).

Linear mixed-effects modeling demonstrated reduction in mean uPCR of 36 (31–41) % (P = 0.0057), 52 (43–60) % (P = 0.0029), 56 (54–58) % (P = 0.0038), and 48 (39–63) % (P = 0.016) at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months, respectively (Figure 1 and Supplementary Figure S2). In the pediatric subgroup (n = 6; [median age, 15.2 years]), iptacopan treatment led to a reduction in median uPCR from 3.5 g/g at baseline to 1.9 g/g at 12 months, with stable kidney function. No baseline characteristics or concomitant therapies significantly influenced uPCR trajectories.

Among 23 patients with uPCR > 1 g/g at baseline, 2 patients(1 IC-MPGN and 1 C3G) achieved complete and 8 patients (1 IC-MPGN) achieved partial proteinuria remission. Complete remission was also observed in 3 out of 5 patients with baseline uPCR ≤ 1 g/g. Proteinuria was reduced to < 1 g/g in 6 of the 20 patients (30%) exceeding this threshold at baseline who had available data at 6 months (Supplementary Figure S3).

Kidney function remained stable throughout the follow-up. Mean eGFR values at baseline and after 12 months of treatment were similar (73 ± 36 ml/min per 1.73 m2), and no overall decline in eGFR was observed during iptacopan exposure. Of note, 1 patient experienced a marked and sustained improvement in eGFR from 30 to 59 ml/min per 1.73 m2 (Supplementary Figure S3).

Serum C3 increased rapidly after the treatment initiation and normalized in 74% of patients by month 3 (Table 1 and Figure 1).

Characteristics of Nonresponders

Despite the overall favorable response, 7 patients (25%) exhibited no reduction or an increase in proteinuria at 6 months, leading to treatment discontinuation in 2 cases. Of note 2 nonresponders showed an initial reduction in proteinuria followed by subsequent rebound (Supplementary Figure S3). Compared with the responders (Table 1), nonresponders were significantly younger; however, disease duration, baseline proteinuria, eGFR, and complement levels, and C3 nephritic factor positivity (available in a limited number of patients) did not differ significantly.

Repeat Kidney Biopsy Results

Paired kidney biopsies in 2 patients with C3G (1 responder and 1 nonresponder) showed resolution of endocapillary inflammatory activity and attenuation of the membranoproliferative pattern, with minimal chronic damage. In the nonresponder, C3 deposits persisted despite histologic improvement. Electron microscopy was not performed in either case (Supplementary Results).

Adverse Events

Iptacopan was well tolerated and during a cumulative exposure of 293 treatment-months, 2 mild adverse events were reported: 1 asymptomatic, reversible elevation of pancreatic enzymes and 1 episode of sinusitis. No serious adverse events requiring hospitalization, treatment discontinuation, or infections with encapsulated bacteria were reported.

Discussion

This real-world data analysis extends evidence from the APPEAR clinical trial program3, 4, 5 by demonstrating the efficacy and safety of iptacopan in a broader cohort of patients with primary C3G and IC-MPGN, including two-thirds of the patients who would not have met previous trial eligibility criteria. Iptacopan treatment was associated with a 56% mean reduction in uPCR at 6 months, with a numerically higher proportion of patients achieving uPCR < 1 g/g (30%) or partial remission (40%) than reported in the APPEAR-C3G trial data.5 The numerical difference between the 6-month (56%) and 12-month (48%) uPCR reduction may reflect the increased data variability and the smaller number of patients with complete 1-year follow-up typical of a real-world cohort, rather than a loss of clinical efficacy. Kidney function remained stable, consistent with the findings from the APPEAR-C3G trial program.3, 4, 5 Serum C3 normalized in most patients by month 3; however, it remained low in 7 of 27 patients and normalization was not associated with clinical response, possibly reflecting incomplete target inhibition or individual pharmacokinetic variability.

Iptacopan was generally well tolerated; adverse events were actively ascertained, although minor events may be less consistently captured than in prospective trials.

Despite the overall reduction in proteinuria in this clinically complex population, in whom conventional immunosuppression had often failed to provide sustained disease control, responses varied widely, from complete remission to nonresponse. This variability underscores the need for better molecular characterization and biomarker-based patient stratification. Despite the limited sample size, this is the largest real-world cohort reported to date and supports the use of targeted alternative pathway inhibition in routine clinical practice.

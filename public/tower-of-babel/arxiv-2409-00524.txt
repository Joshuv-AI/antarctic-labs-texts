An extended Milstein scheme for effective weak approximation of diffusions
Yuga Iguchi, Toshihiro Yamada

Abstract
We propose a straightforward and effective method for discretizing multi-dimensional diffusion processes as an extension of Milstein scheme. The new scheme is explicitly given and can be simulated using Gaussian variates, requiring the same number of random variables as Euler-Maruyama (EM) scheme. We show that the proposed scheme has a weak convergence rate of one, which is consistent with other classical schemes like EM/Milstein schemes but involves fewer leading-order error terms. Due to the reduction of the error terms, the proposed scheme is expected to provide a more accurate estimation than alternative first-order schemes. We demonstrate that the weak error of the new scheme is effectively reduced compared with EM/Milstein schemes when the diffusion coefficients involve a small parameter. We conduct simulation studies on Asian option pricing in finance to showcase that our proposed scheme significantly outperforms EM/Milstein schemes, while interestingly, we find no differences in the performance between EM and Milstein schemes.



1
Introduction

This work proposes a simple and effective weak approximation for Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs) by extending
Milstein scheme
, one of the classical numerical schemes widely exploited in applications. Let
\displaystyle(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t},\mathbb{P})
be a filtered probability space and
\displaystyle B_{t}=(B_{t}^{1},\ldots,B_{t}^{d}),\;t\geq 0,
be the
\displaystyle d
-dimensional standard Brownian motion defined upon the probability space. We consider the
\displaystyle N
-dimensional SDE specified as:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} dX_{t}^{x}=b(X_{t}^{x})dt+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sigma_{j}(X_{t}^{x})dB_{t}^{j},\quad X_{0}^{x}=x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\end{aligned}

(1)

with coefficients
\displaystyle b,\,\sigma_{j}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N},\,1\leq j\leq d
.
Since the solution of SDEs is in general analytically unavailable, some time-discretisation is required to approximately compute the target dynamics.
The accuracy of the approximation is usually measured by the weak/strong convergence: for a numerical scheme

\displaystyle\{\widetilde{X}_{kT/n}^{n,x}\}_{0\leq k\leq n}
starting from a point
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with a time
\displaystyle T>0
and a number of discretisation
\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Bigl|\mathbb{E}[f(X_{T}^{x})]-\mathbb{E}[f(\widetilde{X}_{T}^{n,x})]\Bigr|=\mathcal{O}(n^{-\alpha});

(weak error)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\,\max_{0\leq k\leq n}\bigl|X_{kT/n}^{x}-\widetilde{X}_{kT/n}^{n,x}\bigr|^{2}\,\Bigr]=\mathcal{O}(n^{-\beta}),

(strong error)

where
\displaystyle f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is some appropriate test function, and
\displaystyle\alpha,\beta>0
corresponds to the order of weak/strong convergence, respectively. For instance, it is well-known that for
Euler-Maruyama (EM) scheme
,
\displaystyle\alpha=1.0
and
\displaystyle\beta=1.0
(for instance see
Kloeden and Platen (1992)
).

As one of the popular discretisation schemes, the Milstein scheme has been investigated in many contexts of numerical analysis of SDEs.
It achieves the same rate of weak convergence as the EM scheme (
\displaystyle\alpha=1.0
) but has an improved rate of strong convergence, i.e.,
\displaystyle\beta=2.0
. The Milstein scheme is defined as:
for
\displaystyle 0\leq k\leq n-1
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{Mil},n,x}_{0}&=x;\\
\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{Mil},n,x}_{(k+1)h}&=\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{Mil},n,x}_{kh}+b(\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{Mil},n,x}_{kh})h+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sigma_{j}(\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{Mil},n,x}_{kh})\Delta B^{j}_{k}\\
&\qquad+\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}=1}^{d}g_{j_{1}j_{2}}(\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{Mil},n,x}_{kh})\bigl\{(\Delta B^{j_{1}}_{k}\Delta B^{j_{2}}_{k}-h\times\mathbf{1}_{j_{1}=j_{2}})-A_{j_{1}j_{2},k}\bigr\},\end{aligned}

(Milstein)

where we have set:
\displaystyle h=T/n
,
\displaystyle\Delta B^{j}_{k}=B^{j}_{(k+1)h}-B^{j}_{kh}
,

\displaystyle\textstyle g_{j_{1}j_{2}}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma_{j_{2}}^{i}(x)\partial_{i}\sigma_{j_{1}}(x),\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

and
\displaystyle A_{j_{1}j_{2},k}
is the
Lévy area
defined as:

\displaystyle\displaystyle A_{j_{1}j_{2},k}=\int_{kh}^{(k+1)h}\int_{kh}^{s}dB^{j_{1}}_{u}dB^{j_{2}}_{s}-\int_{kh}^{(k+1)h}\int_{kh}^{s}dB^{j_{2}}_{u}dB^{j_{1}}_{s}.

(2)

The last term in (
Milstein
) is obtained from the higher order stochastic Taylor expansion of diffusion coefficients only and contributes to the improvement of the strong convergence, though it cannot be exactly simulated since the law of Lévy area (
\displaystyle A_{j_{1}j_{2},k}
) is intractable. The Milstein scheme is tractable only if the
commutative condition
holds, i.e.,
\displaystyle g_{j_{1}j_{2}}^{i}(x)-g_{j_{2}j_{1}}^{i}(x)=0,\,1\leq i\leq N,\,1\leq j_{1},j_{2}\leq d
for any
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Precisely, under the commutative condition, the terms involving
\displaystyle A_{j_{1}j_{2},k}
are cancelled out due to its anti-symmetric property. However, many important SDE models in applications, e.g.,
stochastic volatility models
in financial engineering, do not satisfy the commutative condition. Thus, in practice, use of
truncated Milstein scheme
, i.e., the scheme (
Milstein
) with
\displaystyle A_{j_{1}j_{2},k}
being replaced with
\displaystyle 0
, will be a one of realistic options. The scheme is defined as:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{TMil},n,x}_{0}&=x;\\
\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{T}\mathrm{Mil},n,x}_{(k+1)h}&=\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{TMil},n,x}_{kh}+b(\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{TMil},n,x}_{kh})h+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sigma_{j}(\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{TMil},n,x}_{kh})\Delta B^{j}_{k}\\
&\qquad+\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}=1}^{d}g_{j_{1}j_{2}}(\widetilde{X}^{\mathrm{TMil},n,x}_{kh})(\Delta B^{j_{1}}_{k}\Delta B^{j_{2}}_{k}-h\times\mathbf{1}_{j_{1}=j_{2}}).\end{aligned}

(T-Milstein)

The truncated scheme achieves the first order weak convergence,
\displaystyle\alpha=1.0
. However, the removal of
\displaystyle A_{j_{1}j_{2},k}
leads to deterioration of the strong convergence rate under the non-commutative condition and thus
\displaystyle\beta=1.0
.
Yamada (2023)
showed that for elliptic diffusions containing small parameters in both drift and diffusion coefficients, the truncated Milstein scheme can effectively reduce the weak error compared with the EM scheme.
The truncated Milstein scheme is also exploited in the framework of multi-level Monte-Carlo (MLMC). For instance,
Giles and Szpruch (2014)
developed an antithetic multi-level Monte-Carlo based upon the truncated Milstein scheme.

One of the main focuses in this work is to investigate the (truncated) Milstein scheme from the perspective of weak approximation, motivated by the following question:

Can the Milstein scheme or the truncated Milstein scheme always produce a more accurate weak approximation than the EM scheme with the aid of higher order stochastic Taylor expansion of the diffusion coefficients?

The analytic result and numerical experiments provided later in this paper demonstrate that using the Milstein scheme does not necessarily have some advantages over the Euler-Maruyama scheme in weak approximation. In particular, this is shown by simulation studies in Section
4
, where we find no significant differences in the performance of those two schemes.
Then, instead of using these first order discretisation schemes, one might consider higher order weak approximation such as KLNV method
(
Gyurkó and Lyons, 2010
;
Gyurkó and Lyons, 2011
;
Litterer and Lyons, 2012
;
Ninomiya and Victoir, 2008
)
, Malliavin weight approach
(
Iguchi and Yamada, 2021a
;
Iguchi and Yamada, 2021b
;
Naito and Yamada, 2019
;
Takahashi and Yamada, 2016
;
Yamada, 2019
;
Yamada and Yamamoto, 2020
)
, or weak second order sampling schemes
(
Milstein and Tretyakov, 2021
;
Talay, 1984
)
. However, to improve the weak convergence, those schemes require extra computational efforts to take the Lévy area into account. For instance, most of the above methods require simulation of
\displaystyle D
-i.i.d. random variables for the local (one-step) transition, where
\displaystyle D
is larger than
\displaystyle d
, i.e., the dimension of driving Brownian motion. Thus, when
\displaystyle n
-times discretisation is introduced, the total number of generated random variables becomes
\displaystyle nD
, larger than
\displaystyle nd
required for EM and (truncated) Milstein schemes.

The central objective of this work is to propose a simple and effective first order weak approximation for a wide class of diffusions to outperform the Euler-Maruyama and the (truncated) Milstein scheme with a similar computational cost. In brief, the scheme we propose in this article features the following:

•

It is always
explicit
even if the commutative condition does not hold.

•

It extends scheme (
T-Milstein
) and involves additional terms from stochastic Taylor expansion of the drift coefficients.

•

The total number of random variables to simulate the scheme is
\displaystyle nd
for
\displaystyle n
-times discretization, which is the same as EM and (truncated) Milstein schemes.

We refer to our proposed scheme as
extended Milstein scheme
. We will show (under some conditions on the SDE’s coefficients) that the extended Milstein scheme achieves a first order weak approximation when the test function is bounded and measurable (non-smooth).
In particular, we derive the analytic weak error expansion of the new scheme and compare it with that of EM scheme and (truncated) Milstein scheme. Then, for a class of hypo-elliptic diffusions with diffusion coefficients containing a small parameter
\displaystyle\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we quantitatively show that the discretization bias by the extended Milstein scheme is smaller than that of other first order schemes with the help of the small parameter
\displaystyle\varepsilon
.
Finally, we present simulation studies showcasing that the extended Milstein scheme indeed significantly reduces the weak approximation bias compared with the other classical numerical schemes.

2.1
Conditions for the SDE

We define sets of vector fields constructed from the Lie bracket (
7
) as:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Sigma_{0}=\bigl\{L_{1},\ldots,L_{d}\bigr\},\quad\Sigma_{m}=\Bigl\{\bigl\{[\widetilde{L}_{0},V],\,[L_{j},V]\bigr\}\,:V\in\Sigma_{m-1},\,1\leq j\leq d\Bigr\},\quad m\geq 1.

For the SDE (
1
), or the Stratonovich-SDE with the drift (
5
), we assume the following two conditions under which
Bally and Talay (1996)
studied the weak error of Euler-Maruyama scheme when the test function is assumed to be bounded and measurable:

(H1)

\displaystyle b,\,\sigma_{j},\,j=1,\ldots,d,
are infinitely differentiable, and their derivatives of any order are bounded.

(H2)

Uniform Hörmander’s condition holds: there exists an integer
\displaystyle M\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\|\xi\|=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{0\leq i\leq M}\sum_{V\in\Sigma_{i}}\langle V(x),\xi\rangle^{2}>0.

(8)

We note that under the condition (

), the law of
\displaystyle X_{t}^{x}
is absolutely continuous with respect to (w.r.t.) the Lebesgue measure for any
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\displaystyle t>0
. When (
H2
) holds with
\displaystyle M=0
, the condition is typically interpreted as the uniformly elliptic condition, and thus the matrix
\displaystyle a:=\sigma\sigma^{\top}
is positive definite uniformly in the state
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Furthermore, together with the condition (

), the condition (

Extended Milstein scheme and weak error analysis

In this section, we propose the extended Milstein scheme mentioned in Section

3.1
Extended Milstein scheme

We introduce a new discretisation scheme as an extension of the truncated Milstein scheme. For simplicity of notation, we write
\displaystyle b\equiv\sigma_{0}
. Let
\displaystyle T>0
,
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\displaystyle h=T/n
with
\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, we propose the extended Milstein scheme as follows: for
\displaystyle 0\leq k\leq n-1

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\bar{X}_{0}^{n,x}=x;\\
&\bar{X}_{(k+1)h}^{n,x}=\bar{X}_{kh}^{n,x}+\sum_{j=0}^{d}\sigma_{j}(\bar{X}_{kh}^{n,x})\Delta B_{k}^{j}+\sum_{0\leq j_{1},j_{2}\leq d}L_{j_{1}}\sigma_{j_{2}}(\bar{X}_{kh}^{n,x})\times\frac{1}{2}\Bigl\{\Delta B^{j_{1}}_{k}\Delta B^{j_{2}}_{k}-h\times\mathbf{1}_{j_{1}=j_{2}\neq 0}\Bigr\},\end{aligned}

3.2
Hypo-elliptic diffusions with a small diffusion parameter

Based on the main result (Theorem
2
), we analytically demonstrate the advantage of the proposed scheme (
18
) over the Euler-Maruyama/Milstein schemes in terms of weak approximation. In particular, when the diffusion coefficients involve a small parameter
\displaystyle\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, which often appears in applications, we quantitatively show that the leading weak error term of the extended scheme (
18
) is smaller than that of EM and (truncated) Milstein scheme by incorporating
\displaystyle\varepsilon
into the error bound.
To observe this, we introduce the following hypo-elliptic diffusion:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{aligned} dX_{t}^{x,\varepsilon}=\begin{bmatrix}dX_{R,t}^{x,\varepsilon}\\[2.84544pt]
dX_{S,t}^{x,\varepsilon}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}b_{R}(X_{t}^{x,\varepsilon})\\[2.84544pt]
b_{S}(X_{t}^{x,\varepsilon})\end{bmatrix}dt+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon\,\sigma_{j}(X_{t}^{x,\varepsilon})\\[2.84544pt]
\mathbf{0}_{N_{S}}\end{bmatrix}dB_{t}^{j},\quad X_{0}^{x,\varepsilon}=(X_{R,0}^{x,\varepsilon},X_{S,0}^{x,\varepsilon})=(x_{R},x_{s})\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\end{aligned}

(25)

where
\displaystyle\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\displaystyle X_{R,t}^{x,\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{R}},X_{S,t}^{x,\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{S}}
with integers
\displaystyle N_{R},N_{S}\geq 1
such that
\displaystyle N_{R}+N_{S}=N
. In the above, the coefficients are specified as:

\displaystyle\displaystyle b_{R}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N_{R}},\quad b_{S}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N_{S}},\quad\sigma_{j}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N_{R}},\quad 1\leq j\leq d.

When considering the model (
25
), we write
\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}(s,x),\,(s,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
instead of
\displaystyle u(s,x)
to emphasise the dependence of the parameter
\displaystyle\varepsilon
. We then introduce the following result whose proof is provided in Appendix
C
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\displaystyle T>0
,
\displaystyle\alpha\in\{1,\ldots,N\}^{k},k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\displaystyle g\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function with polynomial growth.
Work under the conditions (
H1
) and (
H2
) with
\displaystyle M=1
. Then, there exist a non-decreasing function
\displaystyle K(\cdot)
and constants
\displaystyle q,Q>0
independent of
\displaystyle\varepsilon\in(0,1),n,N
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Bigl|\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\bigl[g(X_{s}^{x,\varepsilon})\partial_{\alpha}u^{\varepsilon}(s,X_{s}^{x,\varepsilon})\bigr]ds\Bigr|\leq K(T)\frac{\|f\|_{\infty}}{T^{q}\varepsilon^{k}}\times(1+|x|^{Q}).

(26)

We also adjust the notation of the EM/Milstein/truncated Milstein/extended Milstein scheme applied to the SDE (
25
) as
\displaystyle\widetilde{X}_{kh}^{\mathrm{EM},n,x,\varepsilon}/\widetilde{X}_{kh}^{\mathrm{Mil},n,x,\varepsilon}/\widetilde{X}_{kh}^{\mathrm{TMil},n,x,\varepsilon}/\bar{X}_{kh}^{n,x,\varepsilon}
, respectively.
Application of Proposition
1
to the leading error terms for the first order schemes presented in Table
1
,
together with Theorems
1
and
2
, leads to the following result.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\displaystyle T>0
,
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\displaystyle\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume conditions (
H1
) and (
H2
) with
\displaystyle M=1
hold.

1.

Let
\displaystyle f_{\mathrm{Lip}}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
be Lipschitz continuous. There exist positive constants
\displaystyle C_{1}^{w},q_{1}^{w},\,w\in\{\mathrm{EM},\mathrm{TMil},\mathrm{Mil},\mathrm{New}\}
, independent of
\displaystyle n,\varepsilon
and
\displaystyle x
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl[f_{\mathrm{Lip}}(X_{T}^{x,\varepsilon})\bigr]-\mathbb{E}\bigl[f_{\mathrm{Lip}}(\widetilde{X}^{w,n,x,\varepsilon}_{T})\bigr]\Bigr|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C_{1}^{w}\times\frac{\|\nabla f_{\mathrm{Lip}}\|_{\infty}}{n}(1+|x|^{q_{1}^{w}}),\qquad w\in\{\mathrm{EM},\mathrm{TMil},\mathrm{Mil}\};

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl[f_{\mathrm{Lip}}(X_{T}^{x,\varepsilon})\bigr]-\mathbb{E}\bigl[f_{\mathrm{Lip}}(\bar{X}_{T}^{n,x,\varepsilon})\bigr]\Bigr|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C_{1}^{\mathrm{New}}\varepsilon^{3}\times\frac{\|\nabla f_{\mathrm{Lip}}\|_{\infty}}{n}(1+|x|^{q_{1}^{\mathrm{New}}}).

2.

Let
\displaystyle f\in\mathscr{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. There exist positive constants
\displaystyle C_{2}^{w},q_{2}^{w},\,w\in\{\mathrm{EM},\mathrm{TMil},\mathrm{Mil},\mathrm{New}\}
, independent of
\displaystyle n,\varepsilon
and
\displaystyle x
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(X_{T}^{x,\varepsilon})\bigr]-\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(\widetilde{X}^{w,n,x,\varepsilon}_{T})\bigr]\Bigr|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C_{2}^{w}\times\frac{\|f\|_{\infty}}{n\varepsilon}(1+|x|^{q_{2}^{w}}),\quad w\in\{\mathrm{EM},\mathrm{TMil},\mathrm{Mil}\};

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Bigl|\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(X_{T}^{x,\varepsilon})\bigr]-\mathbb{E}\bigl[f(\bar{X}_{T}^{n,x,\varepsilon})\bigr]\Bigr|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C_{2}^{\mathrm{New}}\varepsilon^{2}\times\frac{\|f\|_{\infty}}{n}(1+|x|^{q_{2}^{\mathrm{New}}}).

Corollary
1
demonstrates that the extended scheme (
18
) has a better upper bound with the help of
\displaystyle\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, compared with other schemes. The differences in the upper bounds result from that EM, truncated Milstein and Milstein scheme involve the term
\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\Phi_{1}(s,X_{s}^{x,\varepsilon})\bigr]ds
in the leading error, while the term is eliminated in the case of the extended Milstein scheme (see also Remark
1
).
Furthermore, due to the presence of
\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\alpha},\,\alpha>0
in the bound, the proposed scheme can behave nearly as a second order weak approximation up to some number of discretisation
\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{N}
given a sufficiently small
\displaystyle\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.
Thus the extended Milstein scheme provides an asymptotic approximation which is slightly different to the discretisation schemes or expansions in
(
Iguchi and Yamada, 2021b
;
Takahashi and Yamada, 2012
;
Takahashi and Yamada, 2016
)
.
This is also observed in the simulation studies in the following section.

Remark 2
.

The interpretation of Corollary
1
is as follows. When the SDE is driven by a small noise, the model is mainly dominated by the drift function and thus, it is critical to include the higher order expansion terms of the drift function to increase the precision of approximation.
We iterate here that the three schemes (EM/truncated Milstein/Milstein) do not exploit such higher order terms from the drift function while the extended Milstein scheme does.

6
Conclusions

We have proposed a straightforward and effective first order weak scheme (
18
) for diffusion processes and compared its efficacy with other popular first order schemes, specifically, Euler-Maruyama/Misltein/truncated Milstein schemes. The proposed scheme is always explicit and can be simulated with Gaussian random variables, with the same number of random variables required by the Euler-Maruyama scheme or the truncated Milstein scheme. We have shown that the proposed scheme achieves the first order weak convergence, but its leading order error term involves fewer terms than other first order schemes. In particular, when the diffusion coefficients contain a small parameter
\displaystyle\varepsilon
, the discretization bias can be significantly reduced with the aid of
\displaystyle\varepsilon
, though the effect does not necessarily appear for EM and (truncated) Milstein schemes. We then carried out numerical experiments of Asian option pricing, which shows that the new scheme provides much superior accuracy to the other first order schemes and behaves nearly as the second order scheme under the setting of small diffusions. Notably, we have also observed that there are no significant differences in the performance between the EM and Milstein schemes in the experiment.

The application of the proposed time-discretization scheme is not limited to Monte-Carlo estimate for the expectation of the law of diffusions. Indeed, developing simple and effective discretization is useful in a much wider context that requires an approximate sampling of diffusion processes, e.g., parameter estimation of SDEs or filtering of diffusion processes when only partial coordinates are observed. For instance,
Iguchi et al. (2022)
recently emphasized that the use of time-discretization with accurate weak approximation leads to efficient parameter estimation of diffusions when the so-called
Data augmentation
approach
(
Papaspiliopoulos et al., 2013
)
is required to conduct Bayesian inference from low frequency observations. Due to the simple definition and its effective weak approximation, our proposed scheme can be incorporated into many computational/statistical methodologies (e.g.
(
Graham et al., 2022
;
Iguchi et al., 2024
)
).

Proof of Lemma

We have from (2.32) in

)
that: for
\displaystyle 1\leq p\leq q<\infty
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bigl\{\mathbb{E}\bigl[|\mathscr{H}_{(\beta)}(X_{t}^{x,\varepsilon},G)|^{p}\bigr]\bigr\}^{1/p}\leq C_{1}\bigl\|\gamma^{X_{t}^{x,\varepsilon}}DX_{t}^{x,\varepsilon}\bigr\|_{k,\gamma}^{k}\|G\|_{k,q}\leq C_{2}\bigl\|\gamma^{X_{t}^{x,\varepsilon}}DX_{t}^{x,\varepsilon}\bigr\|_{k,\gamma}^{k},

with some constants
\displaystyle C_{1},C_{2}>0
and
\displaystyle\gamma>1
. The Malliavin derivative of
\displaystyle X_{t}^{x,\varepsilon,i},\,1\leq i\leq N
, is given as (for instance see

Nualart, 2006
;
Shigekawa, 2004
)

\displaystyle\displaystyle D_{j,s}X_{t}^{x,\varepsilon,i}=\varepsilon\bigl[J_{t}^{x,\varepsilon}\bigl(J_{s}^{x,\varepsilon}\bigr)^{-1}\sigma_{j}(X_{s}^{x,\varepsilon})\bigr]_{i},\qquad s\in[0,t],\ \ 1\leq j\leq d.

We define
\displaystyle N\times N

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}^{X_{t}^{x,\varepsilon}}=\bigl(\widetilde{M}^{X_{t}^{x,\varepsilon}}_{i_{1}i_{2}}\bigr)_{1\leq i_{1},i_{2}\leq N}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\widetilde{M}^{X_{t}^{x,\varepsilon}}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}J_{t}^{x,\varepsilon}\bigl(J_{s}^{x,\varepsilon}\bigr)^{-1}V_{j}(X_{s}^{x,\varepsilon})\,\otimes J_{t}^{x,\varepsilon}\bigl(J_{s}^{x,\varepsilon}\bigr)^{-1}V_{j}(X_{s}^{x,\varepsilon})ds.

Under the uniform Hörmander condition (

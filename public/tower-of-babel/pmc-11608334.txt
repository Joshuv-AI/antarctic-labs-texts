Metal-organic framework membranes with scale-like structure for efficient propylene/propane separation
Authors: Lun Shu, Yuan Peng, Chenyu Zhu, Kun Li, Weishen Yang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe fabrication of metal-organic framework (MOF) membranes with high propylene/propane selectivity, high mechanical endurance and ease of scaling up always remains a challenge. Inspired by the ubiquitous biological wear-resistant structure, here we show ZIF-67 membranes with a tangential-normal interlaced structure (TN-ZIF-67) for one-step acquisition of polymer-grade propylene, which is endowed with the merits of intergranular defect elimination, partial ZIF-67 lattice flexibility restriction and anti-wear of the membrane surface. The TN-ZIF-67 membrane exhibits an optimal propylene/propane mixture separation factor of 221, and the separation performance remained unchanged after 1.5 years of storage or abrasion by repeated sanding. The distinctive membrane structure can be further scaled up on 4 mm-diameter tubular substrates. This work demonstrates a strategy to extend the rational design of defect-free, anti-wear MOF membranes with superior separation performance and stability that could fulfill future industrial applications.Subject terms: Chemical engineering, Synthesis and processing

Introduction

High-purity propylene (C3H6, polymer grade, > 99.5%) is an essential bulk raw material for chemical production1–3. Steam cracking is the main technology for propylene production and is usually accompanied by propane (C3H8) in the products. The separation is usually accomplished by distillation. However, the physicochemical properties of propylene and propane are very similar, which makes propylene/propane separation one of the most energy-intensive separation processes4–6. Highly efficient separation of this mixture is a prerequisite for achieving the high-value commodity and provides great worldwide benefits. Molecular sieve membrane technology enables an attractive energy-saving and cost-effective avenue for propylene/propane separation, providing accurate dimensional/size recognition for this light olefin refinement7–9. Zeolite and carbon molecular sieve membranes are well known for their ultra-microporous skeletons, but current reports imply that their separation capacities are moderate and that propylene could act as a “pore poison”10–13.

As one of the most promising candidates for molecular sieve membrane materials, metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) are a burgeoning class of porous crystalline materials with diverse structures and functionalities, tunable pore sizes and cavity window shapes, high porosity, and chemical stability14. For general MOF membranes, the gate-opening effect of cavity windows and grain boundary problems derived from the weak intergrowth of MOF grains obstruct the manifestation of the theoretical molecular sieving capacity7,15–17. Zeolitic imidazolate framework-8 (ZIF-8) membranes, as the typical representative, have currently dominated the MOF membrane-based separation of propylene/propane mixture, and registered the selectivity record to date in the premise of paralyzed framework18–20. As the sodalite (SOD) isologue of ZIF-8, ZIF-67 consists of Co2+ and 2-methylimidazole and has a slightly smaller pore size (0.33 nm) than the former (0.34 nm). Compared with ZIF-8 membranes, a significant improvement in propylene/propane separation is expected to occur because of the subtle difference in the ZIF-67 pore size21,22. The higher the separation accuracy is, the lower the propylene/propane separation energy consumption is23. Nevertheless, the experimental results indicate that ZIF-67 membranes always suffer from the grain boundary problem24,25.

In addition, during the practical utilization of MOF membranes, scratches from collisions or abrasions inevitably occur. Mechanical durability has also been a great challenge due to the brittle nature of MOF membranes, which leads to membrane performance degradation7,26–28. One solution is to fabricate MOF membranes on the inner face of tubular porous substrates with high curvatures and micron-sized apertures. However, this increases the difficulty of defect-free membrane growth, and only a few MOF materials (statistically in which ZIF-8 is predominant) have been successfully grown for gas separation28–30. Therefore, the combination of grain boundary elimination and a robust ZIF-67 membrane structure/surface with a rigid lattice is the answer to the above questions and is highly desirable for light olefin separation applications.

Herein, we established a biomimetic ZIF-67 membrane concept of tangential-normal (to the substrate surface, denoted as TN) architecture with the advantages of defect elimination, restricted lattice flexibility and excellent abrasion resistance. To cope with harsh natural environments, well-defined wear-resistant bulge armor textures have evolved in the flora and fauna31–34. For example, bulges on crocodile skin or pangolin scales elevates the contact point and minimizes the solid‒solid contact area, thereby helping to resist abrasion from soil and rocks (Fig. 1a and Supplementary Fig. 1)35,36. Inspired by the biological wear-resistant architectures, a TN-α-cobaltous hydroxide [TN-α-Co(OH)2] nanosheet layer was initially fabricated to serve as the architecture frame and metallic precursor (Fig. 1b). The conventional approach was to execute a complete transformation of metallic precursors, which could not refrain from intergranular defects24,37. In contrast, the in situ partial conversion of each Co-nanosheet precursor ensured the achievement of a plate-like small ZIF-67 grain arrangement. And the residual Co(OH)2 nanosheet linkage within each plate was attributed to defect elimination as well as the paralysis of the ZIF-67 lattice windows. The obtained membrane, denoted as TN-ZIF-67, exactly duplicated the precursor morphology display, within which the bulgy N section functioned as abrasion-resistant armor and the defect-free T section actualized accurate separation (Fig. 1c). Ultimately, an excellent separation capability towards propylene/propane mixture was recorded, ranking top among the ZIF-67 and even MOF membrane series. Good stability was verified by the constant separation performance after 1.5 years of membrane storage in an atmospheric environment or after the membrane surface was sanded three times. The membrane architecture could be easily scaled up on tubular substrates for potential industrialization. These results confirm the feasibility of this bioinspired membrane structure and highlight the plasticity of MOF membranes for highly difficult and highly demanding separation processes.

Results

Synthesis and characterization of the membranes

The construction of a metallic precursor layer was a prerequisite for membrane establishment. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images clearly revealed that an interlaced TN-α-Co(OH)2 precursor layer was closely attached to the surface of the polydopamine-modified porous alumina substrate (Fig. 2a and Supplementary Fig. 2), which was controlled by van der Drift’s evolutionary selection mechanism38,39. No defects were observed under SEM. After a short microwave-assisted precursor transformation procedure, the distinctive ZIF-67 grain arrangement was inherited exactly from the morphology of the TN-α-Co(OH)2 nanosheets (Fig. 2b), due to the thin Co-nanosheet morphology and the rapid crystallization of ZIF-67 under microwave irradiation. The peak of α-Co(OH)2 at 9.7° almost disappeared, as illustrated in the powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) pattern (Fig. 2c and Supplementary Fig. 3), whilst the characteristic peaks belonging to ZIF-67 at 7.3°, 10.4°, and 12.7° were observed, indicating that the ZIF-67 crystalline structure was successfully achieved. As evidenced by the cross-sectional architecture of the TN-ZIF-67 membrane (Fig. 2d and Supplementary Fig. 4), most of the well-intergrown T modular sections settled at the bottom of the membrane layer, while substantial N modular sections vertically stretched out of the layer which were definitely the first ones approached by external abrasion substances. It could be clearly identified that the unconverted flexible Co-nanosheet precursors were accompanied by small autochthonous ZIF-67 grains with sizes of 200–500 nm distributing on each ZIF-67 plate (Fig. 2e). The collected energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) elemental mappings of the cross-sectional membrane also verified that there was indeed a small amount of O distributing in the membrane layer (Supplementary Fig. 5).

To gain further insight into the joint configuration of the residual Co(OH)2 nanosheet and formed ZIF-67, a homologous powder sample was obtained under the same conditions as those used for membrane fabrication (the joint structure is denoted as NS-ZIF-67 hereafter). The morphology and elemental mapping images of a separate NS-ZIF-67 particle were collected by using SEM and transmission electron microscopy-EDX (TEM-EDX) analysis, as shown in Fig. 3a and b, and Supplementary Fig. 6. According to the relative morphology evolution, three portions within a composite particle were distinguished, namely, the ZIF-67, NS-ZIF-67 composite and α-Co(OH)2 portions. This distinctive architecture presented a more visual evidence of the rapid in situ transformation of the ultrathin, active Co-nanosheet precursor to ZIF-67 under microwave heating conditions along with the timely inhibition of its further transformation, evoking a tightly coupled NS-ZIF-67 configuration. According to these results, we can envision that the continuous joint NS-ZIF-67 evidently provided the TN-ZIF-67 membrane with dominant intercrystalline defect elimination (Supplementary Fig. 7)19,40,41. This is essential for overcoming the bottleneck of developing a high-quality ZIF-67 membrane. Besides, for the powder sample, considering the preferred orientations of the ZIF-67 grains arrayed on the Co(OH)2 nanosheet on the basis of the lateral plane of the nanosheet, the dominant crystallographic planes for gas approach and transport were appointed to (011) (Supplementary Fig. 8), exposing the 6-membered ring (6MR) sieving windows of the sod cage. This was in accordance with the XRD patterns of the TN-ZIF-67 membranes (Fig. 2c and Supplementary Fig. 3).

Another key issue that needed to be answered is whether the joint configuration affects the ZIF-67 lattice flexibility simultaneously pledges sufficient microporosity. Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectra of the α-Co(OH)2 precursor, NS-ZIF-67, and ZIF-67 powder fabricated by conventional solvothermal method (the corresponding pure crystalline ZIF-67 powder is denoted as ZIF-67-CP hereafter, also see fabrication details in “Methods” section) were collected to validate the effect of the residual metal hydroxide precursor on the ultimate composite structure. As shown in Fig. 3c, the peaks at 600 to 1050 cm−1, attributed to the imidazole ligand, and the peak attributed to the Co-N coordination bond at ~420 cm−1 were detected in both the NS-ZIF-67 and ZIF-67-CP samples. Moreover, for the NS-ZIF-67 sample, the peaks at 521, 546, 860, and 1074 cm−1, which should be assigned to the Co-O bond, were also observed, again verifying the presence of residual Co-precursors after transformation treatment. Two blueshifts of the Co-N bond (424 cm−1 compared with 423 cm−1) and an imidazole ring stretching vibration (1420 cm−1 compared with 1416 cm−1) were also observed for the NS-ZIF-67 sample. These results suggested that the Co-ligand-Co linkage motions of NS-ZIF-67 were confined (Fig. 3d), which have been frequently observed in the previous reports19,42, and therefore, the lattice rigidity was enhanced compared to that of the conventional ZIF-67 framework (ZIF-67-CP). N2 sorption analysis was used to determine the pore properties of the NS-ZIF-67 composite. As shown in Fig. 3e, the α-Co(OH)2 precursor exhibited a type III isotherm with negligible N2 adsorption capacity, showing an absolute nonporous structure. On the other hand, both the ZIF-67-CP and NS-ZIF-67 samples exhibited characteristic type I isotherms, as expected for microporous materials43,44. The former showed a high N2 uptake of 410 cm2/g and a Brunauer‒Emmett‒Teller (BET) surface area of 1352 m2/g. Compared with that of conventional ZIF-67-CP, the N2 uptake of microporous NS-ZIF-67 (310 cm3/g) decreased by ~24%, and the BET surface area of NS-ZIF-67 (986 m2/g) decreased by ~25%. The weight ratio of the residual Co-precursor in the NS-ZIF-67 sample was ~12.4 wt% based on the thermogravimetric curves (Supplementary Fig. 9). Thus, the results showed that the theoretical difference between the BET surface areas of the pure MOF and the composite NS-MOF ( ~12%) was quite distinctive from the experimental one. In addition, the pore size distribution (PSD) analyzed by the Horvath-Kawazoe (HK) method was used to intuitively unravel the pore discrepancy of the MOFs (Fig. 3f). ZIF-67-CP exhibited a bimodal PSD. The smaller pore aperture of 0.51 nm was ascribed to the average of the 6MR window and the sod cavity, and the larger pore aperture was assigned to the flexible 6MR window to allow further N2 adsorption21,22,45,46. In contrast, NS-ZIF-67 showed a triple modal PSD with markedly decreased peak intensities. The corresponding ultramicropore of NS-ZIF-67 significantly decreased (0.96 cm3·g−1·nm−1 for the NS-ZIF-67 vs. 2.74 cm3·g−1·nm−1 for the ZIF-67-CP, normalized by the MOF weight ratio shown in Supplementary Fig. 9). In view of the crystallographic 6MR window size (0.33 nm) and N2 kinetic diameter (0.36 nm), these declines should be greatly ascribed to the locally restricted ligand flipping motion, as the entrance of large N2 molecules through the less flexible ZIF-67 windows was not as easy as that through the ZIF-67-CP windows. And it was further consistent with the gate-opening effect occurring at a higher relative pressure (P/P0: 7.4 × 10−3 for ZIF-67-CP vs. 7.9 × 10−3 for NS-ZIF-67, Supplementary Fig. 10). Furthermore, the PSD centered at ~0.79 nm split into two peaks. CO2 sorption isotherms were also collected at 273 K (Supplementary Fig. 11a). Supplementary Fig. 11b exhibited the corresponding PSD of NS-ZIF-67 centered at ~0.32 nm, undoubtedly manifesting the persistence of the paralyzed, crystallographic 6MR windows. Combined with the FTIR spectra, these results confirmed that the gate opening effect of the 6MR window of NS-ZIF-67 was partially inhibited by connecting to the unconverted Co-precursor. Hence, we can easily conclude that the distinctive NS-ZIF-67 composite structure furnishes subtly constricted microporous 6MR windows, which could greatly contribute to a sharpened C3H6/C3H8 sieving capability.

Gas separation performance of the membranes

To confirm the feasibility of the application of this intriguing membrane structure in C3H6/C3H8 separation and investigate our hypothesis that defect elimination and partial lattice flexibility restriction increase the membrane separation performance, separation tests of the obtained membranes toward single and binary gases were conducted by using the Wicke-Kallenbach methodology. The pristine Co-precursor film did not exhibit any molecular sieving capacity (Fig. 4a and Supplementary Table 1). There was no notable cut-off between the probe gases, and the gas permeability was particularly low. The obtained TN-ZIF-67 membranes were permeable at first glance, except for C3H8. These distinguishable gas permeabilities led to an unambiguous cut-off between C3H8 and gas molecules smaller than it. The ideal selectivities of C2H4/C2H6, C3H6/C3H8, and H2/C3H8 determined by using the TN-ZIF-67 membrane were all beyond the Knudsen diffusion selectivities (Fig. 4a, inset), which reaches 2.8, 261, and 17789, respectively. For the equimolar C3H6/C3H8 binary feed, the TN-ZIF-67 membranes exhibited a high C3H6/C3H8 mixture separation factor (SF) of 221 ± 9.1 and a C3H6 permeability of 132 ± 4.4 barrer (Supplementary Table 2), prominently surpassing all those of the ZIF-67-based membranes and most of the state-of-the-art membranes (Fig. 4b and Supplementary Table 3). We found that a 2-hour precursor transformation duration optimized the ultimate C3H6/C3H8 separation accuracy. Intriguingly, the gas, especially C3H8, permeance increased with the elongated transformation duration (Supplementary Figs. 12 and 13). This was attributed to the excessive transformation of the nonporous Co-precursors and simultaneous actualization of the fully grown ZIF-67 grains in these thick ZIF-67 membranes. The ZIF-67 ligand flipping motion restriction was then lessened and attendant grain boundaries would also deteriorate the accurate separation process, despite the membranes became thicker and hence theoretically more compact. Therefore, the merits of this distinctive composite membrane structure, i.e., grain boundary elimination and limited inhibition of sod cage window enlargement, were once again confirmed for the achievement of high C3H6/C3H8 separation accuracy.

The separation performances of a TN-ZIF-67 membrane towards C3H6/C3H8 mixture with various molar ratios were also evaluated. The separation performance of the TN-ZIF-67 membrane remained exactly the same for wide C3H8 molar fractions ranging from 10% to 90% (Supplementary Fig. 14). Combined with the mixture SF value which was parallel to the ideal selectivity, these results demonstrated that gas adsorption behavior played a negligible role in C3H6/C3H8 separation. The developed TN-ZIF-67 membrane definitely benefits the practical on demand purification of both rich and lean C3H6 feedstocks. The C3H6/C3H8 SF of the TN-ZIF-67 membrane gradually decreased at elevated temperatures because of the activated diffusion of the C3 molecules (Fig. 4c), and the C3H6/C3H8 SF could be restored as the membrane cooled down to room temperature. Based on the temperature-dependent separation performances, the apparent activation energies (Ea) for C3H6 and C3H8 were calculated by fitting the gas permeances to the Arrhenius equation. The Ea for C3H8 was 15.3 kJ/mol, which was much higher than the Ea for C3H6 (2.3 kJ/mol). The diffusion activation energies were therefore 2.5 kJ/mol and 15.5 kJ/mol for C3H6 and C3H8, respectively (Supplementary Figs. 15 and 16). These values were consistent with those of other MOF membranes reported previously47,48. Hence, the molecular sieve separation mechanism was strongly exemplified to dominate the C3H6/C3H8 permeation behaviors across this distinctive TN-ZIF-67 membrane. In addition, both the C3H6 and C3H8 permeabilities exhibited slight increases at elevated transmembrane pressures. Accordingly, the ultimate mixture of SF remained generally unchanged (from 218 to 208, Fig. 4d).

The TN-ZIF-67 membrane showed superior long-term structure and operation stability. After the as-synthesized TN-ZIF-67 membrane was kept in air for 1.5 years, the SEM images demonstrated no structural changes of the membrane (Fig. 2b and Supplementary Fig. 17), and the membrane separation performance was invariable compared to that of a freshly prepared membrane (Fig. 4e), strongly indicating that the partial restriction of the ZIF-67 lattice flexibility was not loosened. Figure 4f shows that the long-term-stored TN-ZIF-67 membrane exhibited a good stability during an 800-hour online test, retaining a high C3H6/C3H8 mixture of SF over 210 and a C3H6 permeability of ~120 barrer.

Anti-wear performance of the TN-ZIF-67 membrane

Polycrystalline MOF membranes are considered brittle in terms of practical membrane deployment. Frontal collision or rubbing could easily damage the selective membrane layer structure, thereby crippling the separation performance. With the bioinspired TN-ZIF-67 membrane microstructure, we succeeded in compartmentalizing the membrane into two functional modular sections, the T section was used for precise gas separation, while the N section was mainly responsible for the abrasion resistance (Fig. 1c and Supplementary Fig. 18). Coarse sandpaper was used to fiercely rub the membrane surface in reciprocal, zigzag, and circular motions. Consequently, striking ZIF-67 grains were left on the sandpapers (Fig. 5a–c). After three abrasion cycles, the original violet membrane clearly presented a lighter shade (Fig. 5d). This was ascribed to the removal of the vertically arranged scale-like N-ZIF-67 on the uppermost surface of the membrane, which served as membrane armor to prevent the separation functional sections of the membrane from abrasion. Encouragingly, no new defects were observed (Fig. 5e). The XRD patterns verified a preserved crystalline structure of the membrane (Supplementary Fig. 19). The SEM image at a higher magnification clearly revealed the preservation of the T-ZIF-67 structure grown close to the surface of the substrate (Fig. 5f). The cross-sectional SEM images showed that the membrane thickness was reduced by more than half (Fig. 2d and Fig. 5g). Despite these morphological changes, the ultimate separation performance of the attrite membrane towards the C3H6/C3H8 mixture remained exactly the same (Fig. 5i), strongly emphasizing the third advantage of our composite membrane structure. This intriguing finding further indicated that the TN-ZIF-67 membranes could be transported in a haphazard fashion or detached from any integrated membrane module and then assembled again without any new defect generation. It should be noted that when the membrane continued to be polished until the alumina substrate was exposed (Fig. 5h and Supplementary Fig. 20), namely, complete removal of the selective TN-ZIF-67 composite membrane layer occurred, the corresponding mixed C3H6/C3H8 SF of the membrane immediately decreased to 1. This phenomenon implied that the MOF particles that penetrated into the substrate pores had a negligible influence on the separation performance. Besides, a ZIF-67 membrane obtained after a 4 h-conversion process, which was definitely not equipped with the TN-membrane architecture, experienced the same abrasion treatment for 2 times. The ultimate C3H6/C3H8 separation factor showed a vertiginous drop down to 1 from 152 (Supplementary Fig. 21a). It should be undoubtedly ascribed to the non-selective cracks and holes within the membrane layer induced by the abrasions (Supplementary Fig. 21b and c), exemplifying the indispensability of this fascinating architecture.

Fabrication of the TN-ZIF-67 tubular membrane

Tubular polycrystalline membranes are among the most promising membrane models for scale-up fabrication and commercial applications due to their high mechanical strength, large packing density and cost-effective substrates. Thus, they are among the most recent commercial demonstrations in the field of molecular sieve membranes. However, it remains a great challenge to fabricate polycrystalline MOF membranes on tubular supports due to the low affinity between MOFs and highly curved substrate surfaces49. Benefitting from the aforementioned merits of the distinctive TN-ZIF-67 membrane configuration, which is able to provide a defect-free structure and resist mechanical wear to a great extent, we successfully prepared a polycrystalline ZIF-67 layer on a narrow tubular substrate with an outer diameter of 4 mm (surface curvature K = 250 m−1) following the same membrane construction strategy. As shown in Fig. 6a, the outer surface aperture of the substrate was several microns. After a decent hydrothermal growth, an analogous TN-architecture of the α-Co(OH)2 precursor tubular membrane was obtained (Supplementary Fig. 22). Consequently, a dense, tangential-normal pitted ZIF-67 membrane was cultivated on the outer surface of the tubular substrate, and displayed the same morphology as that of the aforementioned flat membrane (Fig. 6b–d). The obtained 5-cm long TN-ZIF-67 tubular membranes were sealed in a custom membrane cell for mixed C3H6/C3H8 separation tests (Fig. 6e and f). As a result, they all exhibited good separation performances, with an average C3H6 permeability of 454 ± 11 barrer and a mixture of SF of 61 ± 5 (Fig. 6g). It is worth noting that, to the best of our knowledge, this was the first successful validation of the preparation of ZIF-67 membranes on tubular substrates with such a high curvature for the realization of high-performance C3H6/C3H8 separation. The results surpassed those of MOF tubular membranes (currently dominated by ZIF-8, Supplementary Table 5) and reached the separation performance target for industrial applications (SF > 35, C3H6 permeability > 1 barrier)50, attractively accomplishing the target of one-step acquisition of polymer-grade propylene. We suggest that the ultimate membrane separation performance can be further enhanced by optimizing the growth quality of the Co-precursor layer on a tubular substrate with micron-sized apertures.

Discussion

Inspired by biological anti-abrasion surfaces with pitted structures, we proposed a ZIF-67 membrane with a distinctive tangential and normal interlaces structure by interrupting a metalhydroxide precursor-mediated transformation strategy. Within this membrane structure, the tangential ZIF-67-precursor composite configuration revealed a highly desirable intercrystalline defect elimination effect and a noticeable ZIF-67 lattice flexibility restriction effect. Combining these two merits, the obtained membrane exhibited an accurate C3H6/C3H8 sieving accuracy with a mixture SF of more than 220, which remained unchanged even after 1.5-year storage. At the same time, the configuration along the normal direction was largely responsible for the abrasion resistance of the membrane. Taking advantage of this property, the membrane displayed a good mechanical endurance, and the C3H6/C3H8 separation performance could be well preserved after being sanded with abrasive paper three times. In addition, the precursor-mediated transformation strategy could be easily scaled up, a high-quality TN-ZIF-67 membrane was built on a 4 mm-diameter tubular substrate, and good C3H6/C3H8 separation performance was achieved. We demonstrated two types of membranes, the flat-sheet membrane (more specifically, disc membrane) and the tubular membrane (more specifically, capillary tube membrane). The packing density of the flat-sheet membrane (100 ~ 300 m2/m3) is lower than that of the capillary tube membrane ( ~ 1000 m2/m3). The C3H6 permeances through the disc membrane and capillary tube membrane are about 26.4 GPU and 53 GPU, respectively, which means ~6 to 20 times more membrane areas need to be used in the case of flat-sheet membrane and therefore adds pressure to the membrane cost. On another aspect, the C3H6/C3H8 separation factor of the disc membrane is far beyond that of the capillary tube one. Theoretically, the employment of the flat-sheet membrane can achieve polymer-grade propylene by one-step acquisition, and significantly simplify the separation process steps. Both two kinds of membranes exhibit their distinctive merits. In practice, only tubular membranes (in general, substrate: O.D. 12 mm, I.D. 8 mm) and membrane module have been realized commercialization worldwide51. We envisage that the capillary tube membrane is easier for potential industrial scale-up in a near future, owing to the more mature technology which can provide a clear industry standard reference and help to lower the development costs.

Methods

Polydopamine-modified α-Al2O3 porous substrate

Polydopamine (PDA)-modified α-Al2O3 porous substrates were obtained according to the literature52. First, tris(hydroxymethyl)aminomethane (5 mmol), dopamine hydrochloride (1.05 mmol), CuSO4·H2O (0.5 mmol), and H2O2 (1.96 mmol) were dissolved in 100 mL of deionized (DI) water. Then, the α-Al2O3 porous substrates were immersed in the above solution at room temperature (RT). After soaking for 10 minutes, the substrates were removed and washed with a large amount of DI water. Finally, the substrates were dried in a vacuum oven at 50 °C overnight.

In situ growth of the α-Co(OH)2 precursor membrane

The α-Co(OH)2 precursor was prepared according to the literature with slight modifications53. Typically, 3 mmol of Co(NO3)2·6H2O and 3 mmol of hexamethylenetetramine were weighed and dissolved in 15 mL of DI water. Then, the reaction solution was transferred into a Teflon-lined stainless steel autoclave, and the PDA-modified α-Al2O3 porous substrate was placed vertically in it. After 6 h of hydrothermal treatment at 100 °C, the substrate with the α-Co(OH)2 precursor was removed and washed with DI water 3 times to remove the unreacted reactants. Finally, the membrane was dried in a vacuum oven at 50 °C for 12 h.

Preparation of the TN-ZIF-67 membrane by microwave-assisted in situ transformation

First, 44.5 mmol of 2-MIm and 3.7 mmol of NaCOOH were weighed and dissolved in 30 mL of MeOH under ultrasonic treatment. Then, the above solution was poured into a Teflon-lined autoclave, in which the α-Co(OH)2 precursor membrane was placed vertically. The TN-ZIF-67 membrane was grown in a microwave reactor (MICROSYNTH, Milestone) with a maximum power output of 300 W. The reaction system was heated from R.T. to 100 °C within 10 min and then held at 100 °C for a certain amount of time (30, 60, 120, 180, and 240 min). When the reactor was cooled down to R.T., the obtained TN-ZIF-67 membrane was removed and washed with MeOH for 3 times. Finally, the TN-ZIF-67 membrane was dried for further use.

Preparation of α-Co(OH)2 and ZIF-67-NS powder

The fabrication protocols for the α-Co(OH)2 and ZIF-67-NS powders were the same as those for the α-Co(OH)2 precursor membrane and TN-ZIF-67 membrane, and no substrate was used during the fabrication. The precipitates were collected by centrifugation and washed with DI water/fresh MeOH for 3 times. The obtained powders were dried in a vacuum oven at 80 °C for 12 h.

Preparation of ZIF-67 powder by the conventional solvothermal method

Conventional ZIF-67 powder (ZIF-67-CP) was fabricated according to the literature with slight modifications25. Typically, 6.2 mmol of CoCl2·6H2O, 24.8 mmol of 2-MIm, and 7.3 mmol of NaCOOH were weighed and dissolved in 100 mL of MeOH. Then, the solution was poured into the autoclave and heated at 120 °C for 48 h. Finally, the precipitate was collected and washed with MeOH for 3 times. Finally, the powder was dried in a vacuum oven for 12 h at 80 °C.

Preparation of the TN-ZIF-67 tubular membrane

The reaction solution for the α-Co(OH)2 precursor tubular membrane growth was the same as that for the disc membrane. Then, the reaction solution was transferred into a Teflon-lined stainless steel autoclave. A PDA-modified porous α-Al2O3 tubular substrate leant against the Teflon lining wall. In order to ensure the uniform growth of the precursor on the tubular substrate, it was first grown in the reaction solution for 4 h. Subsequently, the tubular substrate was rotated by 180° and underwent the growth for another 4 h. After the reaction, the α-Co(OH)2 precursor membrane was taken out and washed with DI water for 3 times to remove residual reactants. Finally, the membrane was dried in a vacuum oven at 50 °C for 12 h.

The reaction solution for TN-ZIF-67 tubular membrane fabrication was the same as that for the disc membrane. The reaction system was held at 100 °C for 120 min.

Characterizations

Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR, Nicolet iS50, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) was used to obtain the infrared spectra of the samples. The powder XRD data of the powders and membranes were collected by using a SmartLab X-ray powder diffractometer (PXRD, Rigaku Corporation, Japan) equipped with a Cu sealed tube (λ = 0.154178 nm) with a scan speed of 5°/min at R.T., while the powder XRD data were collected using a scan speed of 2°/min at R.T. Micromeritics physisorption analyzer (ASAP 2020 Plus, USA) was used to determine the gas sorption isotherms of the α-Co(OH)2, ZIF-67-NS, and ZIF-67-normal powder samples. Field emission scanning electron microscopy (SEM) with an energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) apparatus (FEI Quanta 200 F, and JSM-7900F, Japan) was used to observe the surface and cross-sectional morphology of the membrane and powder samples. High-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM) with an EDX apparatus (JEOL JEM-F200, Japan) and Cs-corrected transmission electron microscopy (TEM) (Titan Themis ETEM G3, USA) were used to characterize the ZIF-67-NS particles. Thermogravimetry (TG) measurements were conducted on a STA449F5-Thermostar (Netzsch, Germany) thermogravimetric analyzer at a heating rate of 10 °C/min in air.

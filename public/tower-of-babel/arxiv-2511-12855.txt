Abstract
Explicit details are presented for calculation of A + ​ B A^{+}B , A + ​ A ​ B A^{+}AB and A ​ A + ​ B AA^{+}B where A m × n A_{m\times n} is any nonzero matrix, A + A^{+} is the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of A A and B B is any matrix of appropriate dimensions, where the quantities in question are found using only the storage originally allocated to the matrices A A and B B (together with some simple one dimensional indexing arrays). 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 65F05, 65F45, 65F99 1 Introduction The calculations to be presented depend on the following theorem, which guarantees a specific rank decomposition of any nonzero m × n m\times n matrix. The proof of the theorem is given with minor modifications in Appendix A, as is a statement of the algorithm that produces the factorization of the theorem. The algorithm is typical of so-called compact schemes, in that the entries of the factors are stored by modifying those of A A in place. This in turn gives rise to the schemes of this paper, which are achieved by further in-place modifications of the original matrix. Theorem 1.1 . [ 1 ] Let A m × n A_{m\times n} be a nonzero matrix of rank r r . Then there are a permutation matrix P m × m P_{m\times m} , a lower trapezoidal matrix L m × r L_{m\times r} of rank r r and an upper echelon matrix U r × n U_{r\times n} of rank r r such that P ​ A = L ​ U PA=LU . The factorization of the theorem has numerous applications in the case that A A has reduced rank. The compact scheme discussed here for A + ​ A A^{+}A is used extensively in the computations for [ 2 ] . The factorization itself can be used for the construction of constraints that define the image of a polyhedron under a linear transformation, also in [ 2 ] . The schemes we will detail involve further in-place (post factorization) preparation of the matrix A A based on the fact that A + = U ∗ ​ ( U ​ U ∗ ) − 1 ​ ( L ∗ ​ L ) − 1 ​ L ∗ ​ P A^{+}=U^{*}(UU^{*})^{-1}(L^{*}L)^{-1}L^{*}P , as is easily shown using properties of the pseudoinverse.

Integrable variant Blaszak-Szum lattice equation
Wei-Kang Xie, Guo-Fu Yu

Abstract
We derive a novel variant of the Blaszak-Szum lattice equation by introducing a new class of trigonometric-type bilinear operators. By employing Hirota’s bilinear method, we obtain the Gram-type determinant solution of the variant Blaszak-Szum lattice equation. One-soliton and two-soliton solutions are constructed, with a detailed analysis of the asymptotic behaviors of the two-soliton solution. A Bäcklund transformation for the variant Blaszak-Szum lattice equation is established. By virtue of this Bäcklund transformation, multi-lump solutions of the equation are further constructed. Rational solutions are derived by introducing two differential operators applied to the determinant elements; in particular, lump solutions derived via these differential operators can be formulated in terms of Schur polynomials. Through parameter variation, three types of breather solutions are obtained, including the Akhmediev breather, Kuznetsov-Ma breather, and a general breather that propagates along arbitrary oblique trajectories. Finally, numerical three-periodic wave solutions to the variant Blaszak-Szum lattice equation are computed using the Gauss-Newton method.



Keywords
: Blaszak-Szum lattice, Bilinear method, Breather, Lump solution.

Introduction

A rogue wave refers to a type of abnormally large surface wave that occurs in the ocean, characterized by its sudden appearance and disappearance. The emergence of rogue waves causes significant threats to maritime vessels. As a result, the investigation of rational solutions in nonlinear systems, such as rogue waves and lump solutions, has become a major focus of current research. The study of rational solutions has extended from oceanography into other branches of physics, encompassing fields such as nonlinear optics, plasma physics, and Bose-Einstein condensates. There are many mathematical approaches to study rational solutions of integrable systems, such as Darboux Transformation
[
1
]
, Hirota’s bilinear method
[
2
]
, inverse scattering method
[
3
]
and so on. In recent years, Yang and Yang studied the rogue wave patterns in the nonlinear Schrödinger equation and presented that the patterns are analytically determined by the root structures of the Yablonskii-Vorob’ev polynomial
[
4
,
5

. Later, they disclosed the relationship between pattern of Kadomtsev-Petviashvili (KP) I equation and Yablonskii-Vorob’ev polynomial
[
6
]
. Based on their work, Chakravarty and Zowada investigated the connection between the theory of integer partitions and high-order lump solutions of the KP I equation
[

8
]
.

Recently, Liu et al. proposed an effective method to generate novel discrete integrable systems
[
9

10
,
11

by introducing a new class of trigonometric-type bilinear operators
\sin(\delta D_{z})
and
\cos(\delta D_{z})

\displaystyle\sin(\delta D_{z})=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}(\delta D_{z})^{2j+1}}{(2j+1)!},\quad\cos(\delta D_{z})=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}(\delta D_{z})^{2j}}{(2j)!}.

(1)

With the help of new bilinear operators, Liu et al. derived novel variant discrete equations, such as the Toda lattice equation, the Leznov lattice equation and the differential-difference KP equation. They also investigated the dynamics of the variant discrete equations, including soliton, breather, lump and numerical three-periodic wave solutions. It is well-documented that both the Leznov lattice equation and the Blaszak-Szum (BS) lattice equation originate from the two-dimensional Toda lattice. From this perspective, they share certain analogous algebraic structures of solutions and relevant solution properties. Inspired by these pioneering studies, the present work is devoted to investigating a novel variant of the BS lattice equation.

In 2001, Blaszak and Szum presented an integrable special (2+1)-dimensional lattice equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u(n)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=u(n)\mathcal{H}^{-1}p(n-1),

4.2
Lump solutions in terms of the Schur polynomials

In this subsection, we investigate lump solutions by the Schur polynomials. We define

\displaystyle\phi=e^{\eta},\quad\psi=e^{\xi},\quad m=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{e^{p}-e^{q}}\phi\psi,

(60)

where

\displaystyle\eta=-\mathrm{i}pn-\mathrm{i}e^{p}t-\mathrm{i}(e^{-p}+e^{2p})y-\mathrm{i}e^{2p}z,

(61)

\displaystyle\xi=\mathrm{i}qn+\mathrm{i}e^{q}t+\mathrm{i}(e^{-q}+e^{2q})y+\mathrm{i}e^{2q}z.

(62)

To construct lump solutions, we introduce two differential operators
A_{j}
and
B_{k}
as

\displaystyle A_{j}=\frac{1}{j!}\partial_{p}^{j},\quad B_{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\partial_{q}^{k}.

(63)

We apply the operators
A_{j},B_{k}
to
\phi
and
\psi
, respectively, and denote

\displaystyle\phi_{j}=A_{j}\phi,\quad\psi_{k}=B_{k}\psi,\quad m_{jk}=A_{j}B_{k}m.

(64)

We introduce the polynomials
P_{j}
and
Q_{k}
as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{j!}\partial_{p}^{j}e^{\eta}=P_{j}e^{\eta},\quad\frac{1}{k!}\partial_{q}^{k}e^{\xi}=Q_{k}e^{\xi},

(65)

where

\displaystyle P_{j}=S_{j}(\boldsymbol{\mu}(p)),\quad Q_{k}=S_{k}(\boldsymbol{\nu}(q)),

(66)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}(p)=(\mu_{1}(p),\ \mu_{2}(p),\cdots,\ \mu_{n}(p),\cdots),

(67)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nu}(q)=(\nu_{1}(q),\ \nu_{2}(q),\cdots,\ \nu_{n}(q),\cdots),

(68)

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(p)=-\mathrm{i}n-\mathrm{i}e^{p}t+\mathrm{i}(e^{-p}-2e^{2p})y-2\mathrm{i}e^{2p}z,\quad\mu_{j}=\frac{1}{j!}\partial_{p}^{j-1}\mu_{1},

(69)

\displaystyle\nu_{1}(q)=\mathrm{i}n+\mathrm{i}e^{q}t-\mathrm{i}(e^{-q}-2e^{2q})y+2\mathrm{i}e^{2q}z,\quad\nu_{j}=\frac{1}{j!}\partial_{q}^{j-1}\nu_{1},

(70)

and
S_{j}(\mathbf{x})
are Schur polynomials with
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ \cdots)
.

Remark 1
.

Schur polynomials
S_{j}(\mathbf{x})
are defined by

\displaystyle\exp(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}x_{j}\lambda^{j})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}S_{k}(\mathbf{x})\lambda^{k},

(71)

where
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ \cdots)
. For instance, the first ones are

\displaystyle S_{0}(\mathbf{x})=1,\ S_{1}(\mathbf{x})=x_{1},\ S_{2}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{2}x_{1}^{2}+x_{2},\ S_{k}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{l_{1}+2l_{2}+\cdots+ml_{m}=k}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m}\frac{x_{j}^{l_{j}}}{l_{j}!}\right).

(72)

5
Breather solutions to the variant BS lattice equation

In this section, we derive breather solutions by setting

\displaystyle\phi_{j}(n)=\sum_{r=1}^{m}e^{\eta_{rj}},\quad\psi_{k}(n)=\sum_{s=1}^{m}e^{\xi_{sk}},

(115)

where

\displaystyle\eta_{rj}=p_{rj}n-\mathrm{i}e^{\mathrm{i}p_{rj}}t-\mathrm{i}(e^{-\mathrm{i}p_{rj}}+e^{2\mathrm{i}p_{rj}})y-\mathrm{i}e^{2\mathrm{i}p_{rj}}z+\eta_{0,rj},

(116)

\displaystyle\xi_{sk}=q_{sk}n+\mathrm{i}e^{-\mathrm{i}q_{sk}}t+\mathrm{i}(e^{\mathrm{i}q_{sk}}+e^{-2\mathrm{i}q_{sk}})y+\mathrm{i}e^{-2\mathrm{i}q_{sk}}z+\xi_{0,sk},

(117)

and thus we have

\displaystyle m_{jk}(n)=\sum_{r,s=1}^{m}\frac{\mathrm{i}}{e^{\mathrm{i}p_{rj}}-e^{-\mathrm{i}q_{sk}}}e^{\eta_{rj}+\xi_{sk}}.

(118)

Here
p_{rj}
,
q_{sk}
,
\eta_{0,rj}
,
\xi_{0,sk}
are arbitrary complex constants. Taking
m=2
,
z=0
,
p_{1j}=q_{1j}^{*}
,
p_{2j}=q_{2j}^{*}
and
\eta_{0,ij}=\xi_{0,ij}^{*}
, we can construct a general breather solution. For example, we take
N=1
,
p_{11}=q_{11}^{*}=a_{1}+\mathrm{i}b_{1}
,
p_{21}=q_{21}^{*}=a_{2}+\mathrm{i}b_{2}
and
\xi_{0,i1}=\eta_{0,i1}^{*}
, which leads to

\displaystyle\tau(n)=m_{11}(n)=\frac{e^{b_{1}}}{2\sin(a_{1})}e^{\zeta_{1}}+\frac{e^{b_{2}}}{2\sin(a_{2})}e^{\zeta_{2}}+\frac{2Ae^{\zeta_{3}}\cos(\zeta_{4})}{A^{2}+B^{2}}-\frac{2Be^{\zeta_{3}}\sin(\zeta_{4})}{A^{2}+B^{2}},

(119)

where

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}=2a_{1}n+2e^{-b_{1}}\sin(a_{1})t+2\left(-e^{b_{1}}\sin(a_{1})+e^{-2b_{1}}\sin(2a_{1})\right)y+\xi_{0,1}+\eta_{0,1},

\displaystyle\zeta_{2}=2a_{2}n+2e^{-b_{2}}\sin(a_{2})t+2\left(-e^{b_{2}}\sin(a_{2})+e^{-2b_{2}}\sin(2a_{2})\right)y+\xi_{0,2}+\eta_{0,2},

\displaystyle\zeta_{3}=(a_{1}+a_{2})n+At+\left(-e^{b_{1}}\sin(a_{1})-e^{b_{2}}\sin(a_{2})+e^{-2b_{1}}\sin(2a_{1})+e^{-2b_{2}}\sin(2a_{2})\right)y+\Re(\eta_{0,2}+\xi_{0,1}),

\displaystyle\zeta_{4}=(b_{1}-b_{2})n-Bt+\left(-e^{b_{1}}\cos(a_{1})+e^{b_{2}}\cos(a_{2})-e^{-2b_{1}}\cos(2a_{1})+e^{-2b_{2}}\cos(2a_{2})\right)y+\Im(\eta_{0,2}+\xi_{0,1}),

\displaystyle A=e^{-b_{1}}\sin(a_{1})+e^{-b_{2}}\sin(a_{2}),\quad B=e^{-b_{1}}\cos(a_{1})-e^{-b_{2}}\cos(a_{2}).

One can verify that
\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{3}=-(\zeta_{2}-\zeta_{3})
, and then we have the expression

\begin{split}e^{-\zeta_{3}}\tau(n)&=\frac{e^{b_{1}}}{2\sin(a_{1})}e^{\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{3}}+\frac{e^{b_{2}}}{2\sin(a_{2})}e^{\zeta_{2}-\zeta_{3}}+\frac{2A\cos(\zeta_{4})}{A^{2}+B^{2}}-\frac{2B\sin(\zeta_{4})}{A^{2}+B^{2}}.\end{split}

(120)

It can be readily verified that the breather solution

where

\displaystyle F_{1}\left(D_{t},\ D_{y},\ D_{z},\ D_{n},\ c_{1}\right)f\cdot f=0,

(126)

\displaystyle F_{2}\left(D_{t},\ D_{y},\ D_{z},\ D_{n},\ c_{2}\right)f\cdot f=0,

(127)

where

and

are certain unspecified functions of

,
D_{y}

D_{z}

, and

-periodic wave solutions expressed by the Riemann theta function
[

]

\displaystyle f=\sum_{m_{1}}\sum_{m_{2}}\cdots\sum_{m_{g}=-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left[\mathrm{i}\sum_{j=1}^{g}(m_{j}+s_{j})\eta_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{g}(m_{j}+s_{j})\tau_{j,k}(m_{k}+s_{k})\right],

if

\begin{split}\sum_{m_{1}}\sum_{m_{2}}\cdots\sum_{m_{g}=-\infty}^{\infty}&F_{1}\left[2\mathrm{i}\sum_{j=1}^{g}\left(m_{j}-\frac{\mu_{j}}{2}\right)w_{j},\ 2\mathrm{i}\sum_{j=1}^{g}\left(m_{j}-\frac{\mu_{j}}{2}\right)k_{j},\ 2\mathrm{i}\sum_{j=1}^{g}\left(m_{j}-\frac{\mu_{j}}{2}\right)l_{j},\right.\\

(129)

\begin{split}\sum_{m_{1}}\sum_{m_{2}}\cdots\sum_{m_{g}=-\infty}^{\infty}&F_{2}\left[2\mathrm{i}\sum_{j=1}^{g}\left(m_{j}-\frac{\mu_{j}}{2}\right)w_{j},\ 2\mathrm{i}\sum_{j=1}^{g}\left(m_{j}-\frac{\mu_{j}}{2}\right)k_{j},\ 2\mathrm{i}\sum_{j=1}^{g}\left(m_{j}-\frac{\mu_{j}}{2}\right)l_{j},\right.\\
&~~\left.2\mathrm{i}\sum_{j=1}^{g}\left(m_{j}-\frac{\mu_{j}}{2}\right)v_{j}\right]\times\exp\left[-\sum_{j,k=1}^{g}\left(m_{j}-\frac{\mu_{j}}{2}\right)\tau_{jk}\left(m_{k}-\frac{\mu_{k}}{2}\right)\right]=0,\end{split}

,

7
Conclusion and discussion

In this paper, we derive a novel BS lattice equations by introducing a new class of trigonometric-type bilinear operators
\sin(\delta D_{z})
and
\cos(\delta D_{z})
. We present a unified Gram-type determinant expression of the solution to the variant BS lattice equation by using Hirota’s bilinear method. We investigate the interaction of two solitons and prove the elastic collision. Lump solutions are obtained via two approaches, the Bäcklund transformation and applying the differential operators. As to the second method, the lump solutions are represented in terms of Schur polynomials. We demonstrate three types of breather solutions, including Akhmediev breathers, Kuznetsov-Ma breathers, and generalized breathers. The numerical three-periodic wave solutions to the BS lattice and the variant BS lattice equation are derived by the Gauss-Newton method.

The geometric patterns of the rational solutions represented by Schur polynomials can be analyzed. The distribution of rogue waves or lumps is associated with the roots of the classic polynomials, such as Yablonskii-Vorobev polynomials and Wronskian-Hermite polynomials. In future work, we will investigate the geometric patterns exhibited by the variant BS lattice equations.

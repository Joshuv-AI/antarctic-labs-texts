DE-Sinc approximation for unilateral rapidly decreasing functions and its computational error bound Note: This work was partially supported by the JSPS Grant-in-Aid for Scientific Research (C) JP23K03218. Journal: Elsevier
Tomoaki Okayama

Abstract
The Sinc approximation is known to be a highly efficient approximation formula for rapidly decreasing functions. For unilateral rapidly decreasing functions, which rapidly decrease as x → ∞ x\to\infty but does not as x → − ∞ x\to-\infty , an appropriate variable transformation makes the functions rapidly decreasing. As such a variable transformation, Stenger proposed t = sinh ⁡ ( log ⁡ ( arsinh ⁡ ( exp ⁡ x ) ) ) t=\sinh(\log(\arsinh(\exp x))) , which enables the Sinc approximation to achieve root-exponential convergence. Recently, another variable transformation t = 2 ​ sinh ⁡ ( log ⁡ ( log ⁡ ( 1 + exp ⁡ x ) ) ) t=2\sinh(\log(\log(1+\exp x))) was proposed, which improved the convergence rate. Furthermore, its computational error bound was provided. However, this improvement was not significant because the convergence rate remained root-exponential. To improve the convergence rate significantly, this study proposes a new transformation, t = 2 ​ sinh ⁡ ( log ⁡ ( log ⁡ ( 1 + exp ⁡ ( π ​ sinh ⁡ x ) ) ) ) t=2\sinh(\log(\log(1+\exp(\piup\sinh x)))) , which is categorized as the double-exponential (DE) transformation. Furthermore, this study provides its computational error bound, which shows that the proposed approximation formula can achieve almost exponential convergence. Numerical experiments that confirm the theoretical result are also provided.

Introduction and summary

The Sinc approximation is a function approximation formula
over the entire real axis, which is expressed as

F(x)\approx\sum_{k=-M}^{N}F(kh)S(k,h)(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

(1.1)

where
h
is the mesh size,

M
and
N
are the truncation numbers,
and
S(k,h)
is the so-called Sinc function, defined by

S(k,h)(x)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\sin[\piup(x-kh)/h]}{\piup(x-kh)/h}&(x\neq kh),\\
1&(x=kh).\end{cases}

The Sinc approximation (
1.1
)
is known to be efficient when
F
is a rapidly decreasing function.
If
f
is not a rapidly decreasing function,
its efficiency is reduced.
To achieve its full potential,
Stenger
[
12
,
14
]
proposed
employing an appropriate variable transformation
depending on the target interval
(a,b)
and
the decay rate of the given function
f
.
He considered the following five typical cases:

1.

the interval
(a,b)
is finite,

2.

(a,b)=(0,\infty)
and
|f(t)|
decays algebraically as
t\to\infty
,

3.

(a,b)=(0,\infty)
and
|f(t)|
decays exponentially as
t\to\infty
,

4.

(a,b)=(-\infty,\infty)
and
|f(t)|
decays algebraically as
t\to\pm\infty
,

5.

(a,b)=(-\infty,\infty)
and
|f(t)|
decays algebraically as
t\to-\infty
and exponentially as
t\to\infty
,

where
f
in the case 5 is referred to as
a unilateral rapidly decreasing function in the present paper.
For each case, he presented a recommended variable transformation

t=\psi_{i}(x)

(i=1,\,\ldots,\,5)
as

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\psi_{1}(x)=\frac{b-a}{2}\tanh\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)+\frac{b+a}{2},

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\psi_{2}(x)=\E^{x},

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\psi_{3}(x)=\arsinh(\E^{x}),

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\psi_{4}(x)=\sinh x,

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\psi_{5}(x)=\sinh(\log(\arsinh(\E^{x}))).

Combining with the variable transformation

t=\psi_{i}(x)
,
we can apply the Sinc approximation (
1.1
) as

\displaystyle f(\psi_{i}(x))

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{k=-M}^{N}f(\psi_{i}(kh))S(k,h)(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle f(t)

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{k=-M}^{N}f(\psi_{i}(kh))S(k,h)(\psi_{i}^{-1}(t)),\quad t\in(a,b).

(1.2)

He also showed that in all five cases,
the approximation formula (
1.2
) may
achieve root-exponential convergence:
\OO(\exp(-c\sqrt{n}))
,
where
n=\max\{M,N\}
.
Owing to its high efficiency,
numerical methods based on the approximation (
1.2
)
have been developed in a variety of
areas
[
1
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
,
which are collectively called Sinc numerical methods.

Afterward, in the cases
i=1,\,\ldots,\,4
,
the approximation error has been analyzed
in more detail
[
5
,
8
]
as

\sup_{t\in(a,b)}\left|f(t)-\sum_{k=-M}^{N}f(\psi_{i}(kh))S(k,h)(\psi_{i}^{-1}(t))\right|\leq C_{i}\sqrt{n}\E^{-\sqrt{\piup d\mu n}},

(1.3)

where
d
and
\mu
are positive parameters that indicate
the regularity and decay rate of
f
, respectively,
and the constant
C_{i}
is explicitly expressed depending on
those parameters, in a computable form.
Therefore, the right-hand side of the inequality
can be computed as a rigorous error bound,
which is useful for constructing an
algorithm or library with guaranteed accuracy.
In addition,
improved variable transformations in the cases
i=3,\,5

were proposed
[
10
,
11
]
as

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\tilde{\psi}_{3}(x)=\log(1+\E^{x}),

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\tilde{\psi}_{5}(x)=2\sinh(\log(\log(1+\E^{x}))).

The error bounds were also given
in the same form as (
1.3
),
claiming that the parameter
d
may be taken larger
than that in the cases
t=\psi_{3}(x)
and
t=\psi_{5}(x)
.
However, even though the convergence rate is certainly improved,
the rate remains essentially the same as before,
that is, root-exponential convergence.

Recently, in the cases
i=1,\,\ldots,\,4
,
the following variable transformations

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\phi_{1}(x)=\frac{b-a}{2}\tanh\left(\frac{\piup}{2}\sinh x\right)+\frac{b+a}{2},

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\phi_{2}(x)=\E^{(\piup/2)\sinh x},

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\phi_{3}(x)=\log(1+\E^{(\piup/2)\sinh x}),

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\phi_{4}(x)=\sinh\left((\piup/2)\sinh x\right)

have been widely used for several years instead of
t=\psi_{i}(x)
.
Those transformations are collectively called the
double-exponential (DE) transformations,
which were originally proposed
for numerical integration
[
4
,
17
]
.
The name originates from the decay rate of
|f(\phi_{i}(x))|
,
i.e.,
|f(\phi_{i}(x))|
decays double exponentially as
x\to\pm\infty
.
Similarly, if the transformed functions decay
single exponentially as
x\to\pm\infty
,
the variable transformations are collectively called
single-exponential (SE) transformations.
The combination of the DE transformation with
the Sinc approximation (
1.1
)
is called the DE-Sinc approximation,
which was shown to be able to attain almost exponential convergence:

\OO(\exp(-cn/\log n))

[
18
]
.
Furthermore, the optimality of the DE-Sinc approximation in a certain sense
has been demonstrated
[
15
]
.
Those facts motivated several researchers to develop
numerical methods based on the DE-Sinc approximation
in a variety of areas
[
2
,
3
,
16
]
.
Furthermore, rigorous error bounds with explicit constants
C_{i}

were provided for
i=1,\,\ldots,\,4

[
5
]
as

\sup_{t\in(a,b)}\left|f(t)-\sum_{k=-M}^{N}f(\phi_{i}(kh))S(k,h)(\phi_{i}^{-1}(t))\right|\leq C_{i}\E^{-\piup dn/\log(2dn/\mu)},

(1.4)

where the DE transformation in the case
i=3
was improved as

t=\tilde{\phi}_{3}(x)=\log(1+\E^{\piup\sinh x}).

The objective of this study is to propose the DE-Sinc approximation
in the case
i=5
by introducing the following DE transformation

t=\phi_{5}(x)=2\sinh(\log(\log(1+\E^{\piup\sinh x}))),

which was originally proposed for numerical integration
[
6
]
.
The main contribution of this study,
which is the most difficult task of this project,
is to provide a computational error bound in the same form
as (
1.4
).
As a result, we obtain an
almost exponentially converging approximation formula
for unilateral rapidly decreasing functions,
with a computable error bound.
The theoretical results shown in this project
are also useful for providing a computational error bound for
numerical integration,
in view of similar studies conducted
for the SE transformations
[
7
,
9
]
.
This should be a topic for future work.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
Existing and new approximation formulas and their error analyses
are summarized in Section
2
.
Numerical examples are presented in Section
3
.
The proofs for the new theorem are provided in Section
4
.

2
Summary of existing and new theorems

Section
2.1
describes existing theorems,
and Section
2.2
describes a new theorem
(the main result of this paper).
To state those theorems,
some relevant notations are introduced.
Let
\mathscr{D}_{d}
be a strip domain
defined by
\mathscr{D}_{d}=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:|\operatorname{Im}\zeta|<d\}

for
d>0
.
Furthermore, let

\mathscr{D}_{d}^{-}=\{\zeta\in\mathscr{D}_{d}:\operatorname{Re}\zeta<0\}

and

\mathscr{D}_{d}^{+}=\{\zeta\in\mathscr{D}_{d}:\operatorname{Re}\zeta\geq 0\}
.

2.1
Existing results

An error analysis of the Sinc approximation
combined with
t=\psi_{5}(x)
can be expressed as
the following theorem,
which is essentially the same as
the existing theorem
[
14
, Theorem 1.5.13]
.

Theorem 2.1

Assume that
f
is analytic in
\psi_{5}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
with
0<d\leq\piup/2
,
and that there exist positive constants

K_{+}
,
K_{-}
,
\alpha
and
\beta

such that

\displaystyle|f(z)|

\displaystyle\leq K_{-}|z|^{-\alpha}

(2.1)

holds for all
z\in\psi_{5}(\mathscr{D}_{d}^{-})
, and

\displaystyle|f(z)|

\displaystyle\leq K_{+}|\E^{-z}|^{2\beta}

holds for all
z\in\psi_{5}(\mathscr{D}_{d}^{+})
.
Let
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
,
let
M
and
N
be defined by

M=\left\lceil\frac{\mu}{\alpha}n\right\rceil,\quad N=\left\lceil\frac{\mu}{\beta}n\right\rceil,

(2.2)

and let
h
be defined by

h=\sqrt{\frac{\piup d}{\mu n}}.

(2.3)

Then, there exists a constant
C
independent of
n
such that

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left|f(t)-\sum_{k=-M}^{N}f(\psi_{5}(kh))S(k,h)(\psi_{5}^{-1}(t))\right|\leq C\sqrt{n}\E^{-\sqrt{\piup d\mu n}}.

By replacing
\psi_{5}
with
\tilde{\psi}_{5}
,
the following theorem was provided.

Theorem 2.2
(Okayama and Shiraishi
[
11
, Theorem 2]
)

Assume that
f
is analytic in
\tilde{\psi}_{5}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
with
0<d<\piup
,
and that there exist positive constants

K_{+}
,
K_{-}
,
\alpha
and
\beta

such that (
2.1
)
holds for all
z\in\tilde{\psi}_{5}(\mathscr{D}_{d}^{-})
,
and

|f(z)|\leq K_{+}|\E^{-z}|^{\beta}

(2.4)

holds for all
z\in\tilde{\psi}_{5}(\mathscr{D}_{d}^{+})
.
Let
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
,
let
M
and
N
be defined by (
2.2
),
and let
h
be defined by (
2.3
).
Then, it holds that

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left|f(t)-\sum_{k=-M}^{N}f(\tilde{\psi}_{5}(kh))S(k,h)(\tilde{\psi}_{5}^{-1}(t))\right|\leq\left[\frac{2\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{D}}}{\piup d(1-\E^{-2\sqrt{\piup d\mu}})}+\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{T}}\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\piup d}}\right]\sqrt{n}\E^{-\sqrt{\piup d\mu n}},

where
\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{D}}
and
\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{T}}

are constants defined by

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{D}}

\displaystyle=\frac{K_{-}}{\alpha}\left[\frac{\E}{(1-\log 2)(\E-1)\cos(d/2)}\right]^{\alpha}+\frac{K_{+}}{\beta}\left[\frac{\E^{1/\log 2}}{\cos(d/2)}\right]^{\beta},

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle=\frac{K_{-}}{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{1-\log 2}\right)^{\alpha}+\frac{K_{+}}{\beta}\left(\E^{1/\log 2}\right)^{\beta}.

The difference between Theorems
2.1

and
2.2
was discussed
[
11
]
,
which led to the conclusion that
the parameters
d
and
\mu
in Theorem
2.2

can be taken larger
than those in Theorem
2.1
.
However, the convergence rates are essentially the same,
in the sense that both rates are of
root-exponential order:
\OO(\exp(-c\sqrt{n}))
.

2.2
New results

To improve the convergence rate significantly,
this study proposes the DE transformation

t=\phi_{5}(x)
,
and shows the following theorem.
The proof is provided in Section
4
.

Theorem 2.3

Let
L
be a positive constant defined by

L=\log(\E/(\E-1))
, and
let
d_{L}
be a positive constant
defined by

d_{L}=\arccos\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{1+(2\piup/L)^{2}}}}\right).

(2.5)

Assume that
f
is analytic in
\phi_{5}(\mathscr{D}_{d})

with
0<d<d_{L}
,
and that there exist positive constants

K_{+}
,
K_{-}
,
\alpha
and
\beta

such that (
2.1
)
holds for all
z\in\phi_{5}(\mathscr{D}_{d}^{-})
,
and (
2.4
)
holds for all
z\in\phi_{5}(\mathscr{D}_{d}^{+})
.
Let
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
,
let
M
and
N
be defined by

M=n-\left\lfloor\frac{\log(\alpha/\mu)}{h}\right\rfloor,\quad N=n-\left\lfloor\frac{\log(\beta/\mu)}{h}\right\rfloor,

(2.6)

and let
h
be defined by

h=\frac{\log(2dn/\mu)}{n}.

(2.7)

Then, there exists a constant
C
independent of
n
such that

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left|f(t)-\sum_{k=-M}^{N}f(\phi_{5}(kh))S(k,h)(\phi_{5}^{-1}(t))\right|\leq C\E^{-\piup dn/\log(2dn/\mu)}.

(2.8)

Furthermore, let
n
be taken sufficiently large
so that
n\geq(\mu\E)/(2d)
holds,
and let
d
satisfy

\left\{\cosh(r_{1}-r_{0})-\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}(r_{1}-r_{0})}\right\}\cosh(r_{0})\leq\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}},

(2.9)

where
r_{0}
and
r_{1}
are defined by

r_{0}=\arsinh\left(\frac{L}{\piup\cos d}\right),\quad r_{1}=\log\left(\frac{1+\cos d}{\sin d}\right).

Then, the inequality (
2.8
) holds with

C=\frac{1}{\piup d}\left[\frac{2C_{\mathrm{D}}}{\piup(1-\E^{-\piup\mu\E})\cos d}+C_{\mathrm{T}}\right],

where
{C}_{\mathrm{D}}
and
{C}_{\mathrm{T}}

are constants defined by

\displaystyle{C}_{\mathrm{D}}

\displaystyle=\frac{K_{-}}{\alpha}\left[\frac{\E^{2}+\E+1}{(1-\log 2)(\E^{2}-1)\cos((\piup/2)/\cosh(r_{1}-r_{0}))}\right]^{\alpha}+\frac{K_{+}}{\beta}\left[\frac{\E^{1/\log 2}}{\cos((\piup/2)\sin d)}\right]^{\beta},

(2.10)

\displaystyle{C}_{\mathrm{T}}

\displaystyle=K_{-}\left(\frac{\E^{\piup/2}}{1-\log 2}\right)^{\alpha}+K_{+}\left(\E^{(\piup/2)+(1/\log 2)}\right)^{\beta}.

(2.11)

Remark 2.1

Roughly speaking,
the condition (
2.9
) gives
the lower bound of
d
implicitly,
whereas the upper bound
d_{L}
is explicitly given
by (
2.5
).
Because explicit expression of the lower bound is not easy to derive,
we should check the condition (
2.9
)
in the current situation.
For reference, numerical observation shows that
the smallest value of
d
satisfying the condition (
2.9
)
is approximately 0.575.

From Theorem
2.3
,
we can conclude that the proposed method may achieve
almost exponential convergence:
\OO(\exp(-cn/\log n))
,
which is significantly higher than root-exponential convergence:

\OO(\exp(-c\sqrt{n}))
.

Proofs

This section presents the proof of Theorem

.
It is organized as follows.
In Section
4.1
,
the proof is decomposed into two lemmas:
Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
.
To establish these lemmas,
useful inequalities are presented in
Sections
4.2
,
4.3
,
and
4.4
.
Subsequently,
Lemma
4.2
is proved in Section
4.5
,
and Lemma
4.3

.

4.1
Sketch of the proof

Let
F(x)=f(\phi(x))
.
The main strategy in the proof of Theorem
2.3
is
to split the error into two terms as follows:

\displaystyle\left|f(t)-\sum_{k=-M}^{N}f(\phi(kh))S(k,h)(\phi^{-1}(t))\right|

\displaystyle=\left|F(x)-\sum_{k=-M}^{N}F(kh)S(k,h)(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|F(x)-\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}F(kh)S(k,h)(x)\right|+\left|\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-M-1}F(kh)S(k,h)(x)+\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}F(kh)S(k,h)(x)\right|.

The first and second terms are called the discretization error and
truncation error, respectively.
The following function space is important for bounding the discretization error.

Definition 4.1

Let
d
be a positive constant, and
let
\mathscr{D}_{d}(\epsilon)
be a rectangular domain defined
for
0<\epsilon<1
by

\mathscr{D}_{d}(\epsilon)=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:|\operatorname{Re}\zeta|<1/\epsilon,\,|\operatorname{Im}\zeta|<d(1-\epsilon)\}.

Then,
\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
denotes the family of all analytic functions
F

on
\mathscr{D}_{d}
such that the norm
\mathcal{N}_{1}(F,d)
is finite, where

\mathcal{N}_{1}(F,d)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\oint_{\partial\mathscr{D}_{d}(\epsilon)}|F(\zeta)||\mathrm{d}\zeta|.

Under this definition,
the discretization error has been estimated as follows.

Theorem 4.1
(Stenger
[
12
, Theorem 3.1.3]
)

Let
F\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
. Then,

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|F(x)-\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}F(kh)S(k,h)(x)\right|\leq\frac{\mathcal{N}_{1}(F,d)}{\piup d(1-\E^{-2\piup d/h})}\E^{-\piup d/h}.

This study presents the following lemma,
which completes the estimation of the discretization error.
The proof is provided in Section
4.5
.

Lemma 4.2

Assume that all the assumptions made in Theorem
2.3

are fulfilled.
Then, the function
F(\zeta)=f(\phi_{5}(\zeta))

belongs to
\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathscr{D}_{d})
, and
\mathcal{N}_{1}(F,d)
is bounded as

\mathcal{N}_{1}(F,d)\leq\frac{2C_{\mathrm{D}}}{\piup\cos d},

where
C_{\mathrm{D}}
is the constant defined by (
2.10
).

In addition, we bound the truncation error as follows.
The proof is provided in Section
4.6
.

Lemma 4.3

Assume that all the assumptions made in Theorem
2.3

are fulfilled.
Then, putting
F(x)=f(\phi_{5}(x))
, we have

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-M-1}F(kh)S(k,h)(x)+\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}F(kh)S(k,h)(x)\right|\leq\frac{C_{\mathrm{T}}}{\piup d}\E^{-\piup dn/\log(2dn/\mu)},

where
C_{\mathrm{T}}
is the constant defined by (
2.11
).

Combining Theorem
4.1

with Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
,
setting
h
as (
2.7
),
we have

\displaystyle\left|F(x)-\sum_{k=-M}^{N}F(kh)S(k,h)(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2C_{\mathrm{D}}}{\piup d(1-\E^{-2\piup d/h})\piup\cos d}\E^{-\piup d/h}+\frac{C_{\mathrm{T}}}{\piup d}\E^{-\piup dn/\log(2dn/\mu)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\piup d}\left\{\frac{2C_{\mathrm{D}}}{(1-\E^{-\piup\mu(2dn/\mu)/\log(2dn/\mu)})\piup\cos d}+C_{\mathrm{T}}\right\}\E^{-\piup dn/\log(2dn/\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\piup d}\left\{\frac{2C_{\mathrm{D}}}{(1-\E^{-\piup\mu(\E)/\log(\E)})\piup\cos d}+C_{\mathrm{T}}\right\}\E^{-\piup dn/\log(2dn/\mu)},

from which we obtain the desired error bound.
This completes the proof of Theorem
2.3
.

4.2
Useful inequality on
\mathscr{D}_{d}^{+}

Here, the following lemma is prepared.
This lemma was previously stated in an existing study
[
6
]
,
but the proof was omitted due to space restrictions.
For readers’ convenience,
its explicit proof is provided.
Note that
\overline{\mathscr{D}}

denotes the closure of
\mathscr{D}
.

Lemma 4.4
(Okayama
[
6
, Lemma 7]
)

It holds for all
\zeta\in\overline{\mathscr{D}_{\piup/2}^{+}}
that

\left|\frac{1}{\log(1+\E^{\piup\sinh\zeta})}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\log 2}.

(4.1)

Proof 1

Let
x=\operatorname{Re}(\piup\sinh\zeta)
and
y=\operatorname{Im}(\piup\sinh\zeta)
.
Note that
x\geq 0
because
\zeta\in\overline{\mathscr{D}_{\piup/2}^{+}}
.
We show

\left|\log(1+\E^{x+\I y})\right|\geq\log 2

in the following three cases: (i)
0\leq x\leq\log 3
and
|y|\leq\piup
,
(ii)
0\leq x\leq\log 3
and
|y|>\piup
, and (iii)
x>\log 3
.

(i)

0\leq x\leq\log 3
and
|y|\leq\piup
.
By the maximum modulus principle,

|\log(1+\E^{x+\I y})|
attains its minimum on the boundary of
the rectangle region (
0\leq x\leq\log 3
and
|y|\leq\piup
).
On the left edge (
x=0
and
|y|\leq\piup
), we have

\left|\log(1+\E^{x+\I y})\right|=\left|\log(1+\E^{\I y})\right|=\left|\log(2\cos(y/2)\cdot\E^{\I(y/2)})\right|=\sqrt{\{\log(2\cos(y/2))\}^{2}+(y/2)^{2}}.

(4.2)

As shown in the existing lemma
[
9
, Lemma 4.6]
,
the right-hand side attains its minimum at
y=0
, i.e.,

\sqrt{\{\log(2\cos(y/2))\}^{2}+(y/2)^{2}}\geq\sqrt{\{\log(2\cos(0/2))\}^{2}+(0/2)^{2}}=\log 2.

On the top/bottom edges (
0<x<\log 3
and
y=\pm\piup
),
we have

\left|\log(1+\E^{x+\I y})\right|=\sqrt{\{\log|1-\E^{x}|\}^{2}+\{\arg(1-\E^{x})\}^{2}}=\sqrt{\{\log(\E^{x}-1)\}^{2}+\piup^{2}}\geq\sqrt{0^{2}+\piup^{2}}>\log 2.

On the right edge (
x=\log 3
and
|y|\leq\piup
),
we have

\left|\log(1+\E^{x+\I y})\right|=\sqrt{\{\log|1+\E^{x+\I y}|\}^{2}+\{\arg(1+\E^{x+\I y})\}^{2}}\geq\sqrt{\{\log(|\E^{x+\I y}|-1)\}^{2}+0^{2}}=\log(\E^{x}-1)=\log 2.

Thus,
|\log(1+\E^{x+\I y})|\geq\log 2
holds
throughout this rectangle region.

(ii)

0\leq x\leq\log 3
and
|y|>\piup
.
When
x=0
, using (
4.2
), we have

\left|\log(1+\E^{x+\I y})\right|=\sqrt{\{\log(2\cos(y/2))\}^{2}+(y/2)^{2}}\geq\sqrt{0^{2}+(\piup/2)^{2}}>\log 2.

When
x>0
,
note that
|\arg(1+\E^{x+\I y})|<\piup
for
|y|<\piup
,

|\arg(1+\E^{x+\I y})|=\piup
for
|y|=\piup
,
and
|\arg(1+\E^{x+\I y})|>\piup
for
|y|>\piup
.
Therefore, for
|y|>\piup
we have

|\log(1+\E^{x+\I y})|=\sqrt{\{\log|1+\E^{x+\I y}|\}^{2}+\{\arg(1+\E^{x+\I y})\}^{2}}>\sqrt{0^{2}+\piup^{2}}>\log 2.

(iii)

x>\log 3
. In this case, we have

|\log(1+\E^{x+\I y})|=\sqrt{\{\log|1+\E^{x+\I y}|\}^{2}+\{\arg(1+\E^{x+\I y})\}^{2}}\geq\sqrt{\{\log(|\E^{x+\I y}|-1)\}^{2}+0^{2}}=\log(\E^{x}-1)\geq\log 2.

This completes the proof.

Proof 2

Proposition 4.6

Let
g(x,y)
be a function defined by

g(x,y)=\frac{\cosh(x)-\cos(y)}{x^{2}+y^{2}}.

(4.4)

Then, for arbitrary fixed
y\in\mathbb{R}
,

g(x,y)
monotonically decreases for
x<0
and
monotonically increases for
x>0
.

Proof 3

Differentiating
g
with respect to
x
,
we have

g_{x}(x,y)=\frac{\tilde{g}(x,y)}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}},

where

\tilde{g}(x,y)=2x\cos y-2x\cosh x+(x^{2}+y^{2})\sinh x.

Differentiating
\tilde{g}
with respect to
x
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{x}(x,y)

\displaystyle=2\cos y+(x^{2}+y^{2}-2)\cosh x),

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{xx}(x,y)

\displaystyle=2x\cosh x+(x^{2}+y^{2}-2)\sinh x,

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{xxx}(x,y)

\displaystyle=4x\sinh x+(x^{2}+y^{2})\cosh x.

Because
\tilde{g}_{xxx}(x,y)\geq 0
,

\tilde{g}_{xx}(x,y)

is a monotonically increasing function with respect to
x
.
From this and using
\tilde{g}_{xx}(0,y)=0
,
we have
\tilde{g}_{xx}(x,y)<0

for
x<0
and
\tilde{g}_{xx}(x,y)>0

for
x>0
.
Therefore,
\tilde{g}_{x}(x,y)

monotonically decreases for
x<0
and
monotonically increases for
x>0
.
From this and using

\tilde{g}_{x}(0,y)=4\{(y/2)^{2}-\sin^{2}(y/2)\}\geq 0
,
we have
\tilde{g}_{x}(x,y)\geq 0

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, which implies that

\tilde{g}(x,y)
is a monotonically increasing function with respect to
x
.
From this and using
\tilde{g}(0,y)=0
,
we have
g_{x}(x,y)<0

for
x<0
and
g_{x}(x,y)>0

for
x>0
.
This completes the proof.

Lemma 4.7

It holds for all
\zeta\in\mathscr{D}_{\piup/2}^{-}
that

\left|\frac{\log(1+\E^{\piup\sinh\zeta})}{1+\log(1+\E^{\piup\sinh\zeta})}\cdot\frac{\E^{-L}+\E^{\piup\sinh\zeta}}{\E^{\piup\sinh\zeta}}\right|\leq\frac{\E^{2}+\E+1}{\E^{2}+\E},

(4.5)

where
L=\log(\E/(\E-1))
.

Proof 4

Putting
\eta=\log(1+\E^{\piup\sinh\zeta})
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\log(1+\E^{\piup\sinh\zeta})}{1+\log(1+\E^{\piup\sinh\zeta})}\cdot\frac{\E^{-L}+\E^{\piup\sinh\zeta}}{\E^{\piup\sinh\zeta}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\eta}{1+\eta}\cdot\frac{\frac{\E-1}{\E}+\E^{\eta}-1}{\E^{\eta}-1}\right|=\frac{1}{\E}\left|\frac{\eta}{\E^{\eta}-1}\cdot\frac{\E^{\eta+1}-1}{\eta+1}\right|.

Set
x=\operatorname{Re}\eta
and
y=\operatorname{Im}\eta
.
Substituting
\eta=x+\I y
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\E}\left|\frac{\eta}{\E^{\eta}-1}\cdot\frac{\E^{\eta+1}-1}{\eta+1}\right|

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1}{\E}\cdot\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{\cosh(x)-\cos y}\cdot\frac{\cosh(x+1)-\cos y}{(x+1)^{2}+y^{2}}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{\E}\cdot\frac{g(x+1,y)}{g(x,y)}},

where
g(x,y)
is defined by (
4.4
).
Because
\operatorname{Re}\zeta<0
,
we have
|\E^{\piup\sinh\zeta}|<1
,
which implies
\operatorname{Re}(1+\E^{\piup\sinh\zeta})>0
.
Consequently,
we have
|y|=|\arg(1+\E^{\piup\sinh\zeta})|\leq\piup/2
.
Therefore, we show

\sqrt{\frac{1}{\E}\cdot\frac{g(x+1,y)}{g(x,y)}}\leq\frac{\E^{2}+\E+1}{\E^{2}+\E}

for
y\in[-\piup/2,\piup/2]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
We consider three cases for
x
: (i)
x\leq-1
,
(ii)
-1<x\leq 2
, and (iii)
2<x
.

(i)

x\leq-1
. In this case,
g(x,y)\geq g(x+1,y)
holds
from Proposition
4.6
.
Therefore, we have

\sqrt{\frac{1}{\E}\cdot\frac{g(x+1,y)}{g(x,y)}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{\E}\cdot 1}<\frac{\E^{2}+\E+1}{\E^{2}+\E}.

(ii)

-1<x\leq 2
. In this case,

g(x+1,y)\leq g(3,y)

and
g(x,y)\geq g(0,y)
hold
from Proposition
4.6
.
Furthermore, using

\displaystyle g(3,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\cosh 3}{9+y^{2}}-\frac{\cos y}{9+y^{2}}\leq\frac{\cosh 3}{9+0^{2}}-\frac{0}{9+y^{2}}=\frac{\cosh 3}{9},

\displaystyle g(0,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{y^{2}}{1-\cos y}\leq\frac{(\piup/2)^{2}}{1-\cos(\piup/2)}=\frac{\piup^{2}}{4},

we have

\sqrt{\frac{1}{\E}\cdot\frac{g(x+1,y)}{g(x,y)}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{\E}\cdot\frac{g(3,y)}{g(0,y)}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\piup^{2}\cosh 3}{36\E}}<\frac{\E^{2}+\E+1}{\E^{2}+\E}.

(iii)

2<x
. In this case, using

\frac{\cosh(x+1)-\cos y}{\cosh(x)-\cos y}\leq\frac{\cosh(x+1)-1}{\cosh(x)-1}\leq\frac{\cosh(3)-1}{\cosh(2)-1},

we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{1}{\E}\cdot\frac{g(x+1,y)}{g(x,y)}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1}{\E}\cdot\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{(1+x)^{2}+y^{2}}\cdot\frac{\cosh(x+1)-\cos y}{\cosh(x)-\cos y}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{\E}\cdot 1\cdot\frac{\cosh(3)-1}{\cosh(2)-1}}=\frac{\E^{2}+\E+1}{\E^{2}+\E}.

This completes the proof.

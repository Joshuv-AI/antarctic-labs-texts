Iwasawa theory for abelian towers of digraphs
Antonio Lei, Katharina Müller

Abstract
. Let p p and ℓ \ell be prime numbers, and d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 an integer. We formulate and prove Iwasawa main conjectures of the Picard groups and Bowen–Franks groups in ℤ p d \mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d} -towers of digraphs. In particular, we relate the ℓ \ell parts of these groups to certain p p -adic L L -functions defined using a voltage assignment. In the case where ℓ \ell is not equal to p p , we make use of the recent work of Bandini–Longhi to define the appropriate characteristic ideals. We also prove the growth of the ℓ \ell -part of these groups, generalizing classical results of Sinnott and Washington on ideal class groups of number fields. Finally, we introduce the concept of defect, which compare certain algebraic and analytic ranks related to Bowen–Franks groups and study their asymptotic behaviour in a ℤ p d \mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d} -tower.



1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a fixed prime number and
d\geq 1
an integer. Let
X
be a finite connected undirected graph, and consider a sequence of covers of finite connected graphs

X=X_{0}\leftarrow X_{1}\leftarrow X_{2}\leftarrow\ldots\leftarrow X_{n}\leftarrow\ldots

for which the composition
X_{n}\rightarrow\ldots\rightarrow X_{0}=X
is a Galois cover whose Galois group of deck transformations is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})^{d}
for all
n\geq 1
. For every finite connected undirected graph
X
, there is a finite abelian group
{\rm Pic}^{0}(X)
, whose cardinality, denoted by
\kappa(X)
, is the number of spanning trees of
X
. By analogy to the classical Iwasawa theory for ideal class groups of number fields, one can study the variation of
{\rm ord}_{p}(\kappa(X_{n}))
as
n
increases.

Suppose that
d=1
. In
[
Val21
,
MV23
,
MV24
]
, it was shown that there exist
\mu_{p},\lambda_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\nu_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

(1.1)

{\rm ord}_{p}(\kappa(X_{n}))=\mu_{p}\cdot p^{n}+\lambda_{p}\cdot n+\nu_{p},

provided
n
is large enough. Let
\ell
be another prime number distinct from
p
. Analogously to the results of Sinnott and Washington on class groups, in
[
LV23
]
it was shown that there exist
\mu_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\nu_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

(1.2)

{\rm ord}_{\ell}(\kappa(X_{n}))=\mu_{\ell}\cdot p^{n}+\nu_{\ell},

provided
n
is large enough.
For general
d\geq 1
,
[
DV23
]
extended (
1.1
) proving that
\ord_{p}(\kappa(X_{n}))
can be expressed as a polynomial in
p^{n}
and
n
.

When
p=\ell
, the formula (
1.1
) can be obtained either using the theory of Ihara zeta and
L
-functions or the structure theorem of finitely generated modules over the Iwasawa algebra
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rrbracket
, and there is a main conjecture connecting both approaches (see
[
Kat24
, Theorem 6.1]
and
[
KM24
, Theorem 5.2 and Remark 5.3]
). One goal of this work is to study an Iwasawa main conjecture that connects the theory of zeta functions to the
\ell
-primary part of Picard groups, in the setting of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-towers of directed graphs, which are sometimes referred as digraphs in the literature. In the case where
p\neq\ell
, we will make use of the recent work of Bandini–Longhi
[
BL23
]
, where a novel conceptual framework to study modules of
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\llbracket\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}\rrbracket
is developed. Furthermore, we prove a Sinnott–Washington type theorem, generalizing (
1.2
) to digraphs, allowing
d
to be any integers
\geq 1
.

In addition, we prove analogous results for the Bowen–Franks groups attached to a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-tower of digraphs, first introduced in
[
BF77
]
(see Definition
2.7
). These groups play an important role in symbolic dynamics, where digraphs are related to shift spaces, and Bowen–Franks groups are important invariants that can be used to detect equivalence and conjugacy; see
[
Fra84
]
and
[
LM21
]
for more details.

1.1.
Main results

In order to explain the main results of this article, we introduce some notation. Let
p
be a fixed prime number and
d\geq 1
an integer. Given a prime number
\ell
, which may or may not be equal to
p
, we define the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)=\displaystyle\varprojlim_{n}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma/\Gamma^{p^{n}}]
, where
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
. Let
\Gamma^{\vee}
denote the set of finite-order
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
-valued character of
\Gamma
. Given an element
F\in\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)
and
\omega\in\Gamma
, we can evaluate
F
at
\omega
, denoted by
\omega(F)\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}
(after fixing an embedding
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}
). We can also define the characteristic ideal of a finitely generated module
M
over
\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)
, denoted by
\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)}M
(see §
3
where this is reviewed).

Throughout, we fix a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-tower of strongly connected digraphs
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, in the sense of Definition
2.11
. Such a tower can be represented by a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-valued voltage assignment, which is a function
\alpha
from the set of directed edges of
X\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=X_{0}
to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
(see Lemma
2.12
). We define two
p
-adic functions associated with this tower, namely, the
p
-adic zeta function
L_{p}(X,\alpha)
and the
p
-adic Bowen–Franks function
\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha)
in Definition
4.8
. These functions are closely related to the archimedean zeta functions associated with the digraphs
X_{n}
, as demonstrated in Proposition
4.9
. An interesting feature of these functions is that they can be regarded as elements of
\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)
for all primes
\ell
.

On the algebraic side, we define

\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{\infty})
(resp.
\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{\infty})
) as the inverse limits of the
\ell
-primary parts of the Picard groups (resp. Bowen–Franks groups), which are finitely generated
\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)
-modules.
The first main theorem of this article proves the validity of the Iwasawa main conjectures linking these algebraic objects to their analytic counterparts.

Theorem A

(Theorem
5.8
)
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-tower of strongly connected digraphs. For all primes
\ell
, we have

\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)}(\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{\infty}))=(L_{p}(X,\alpha)),

and

\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)}(\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{\infty}))=(\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha)).

Furthermore,
{\rm Pic}_{\ell}(X_{\infty})
is a torsion
\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)
-module.

In the special case where
\ell=p
and the graphs
X_{n}
are undirected, the statement on Picard groups given by Theorem
A
has been proved in
[
Kat24
, Theorem 6.1]
and
[
KM24
, Theorem 5.2 and Remark 5.3]
.

We remark that
\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{\infty})
is not always
\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)
-torsion; see Example
5.11
. When it is not torsion, the assertion on Bowen–Franks groups in Theorem
A
simply says
(0)=(0)
.

The second main result of the article is the following generalization of formula (
1.2
) to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-towers of digraphs.

Theorem B

(Theorem
6.7
)
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-tower of strongly connected digraphs. Let
\ell\neq p
be a prime number. Then

\ord_{\ell}(|\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n})_{\mathrm{tor}}|)=p^{nd}\mu_{\ell}(L_{p}(X,\alpha))+O(p^{(d-1)n}).

If
\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha)(\omega)\neq 0
for all but finitely many
\omega\in\Gamma^{\vee}
, then

\ord_{\ell}(|\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{n})_{\textup{tor}}|)=p^{nd}\mu_{\ell}(\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha))+O(p^{(d-1)n}).

It is possible to utilize the general theory of Sinnott modules developed in
[
BL23
]
to estimate the growth of
\ord_{\ell}(|\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n})_{\mathrm{tor}}|)
and
\ord_{\ell}(|\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{n})_{\textup{tor}}|)
, in particular Theorem 4.7 of
op. cit.
However, Theorem
B
gives a more precise estimate (see Remarks
5.9
and
6.8
). Our proof builds on a multi-variable generalization of Sinnott’s method
[
Sin87
]
, which analyzes the
\ell
-adic valuation of one-variable polynomials evaluated at
p
-power roots of unity; see Corollary
6.6
. This general result may be of independent interest and should have further applications beyond the present setting.

When
p=\ell
, the method of
[
CM81
]
can be readily generalized to study the variation of
\ord_{p}(|\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n})_{\mathrm{tor}}|)
and
\ord_{p}(|\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n})_{\mathrm{tor}}|)
, in a manner analogous to the results of
[
DV23
]
and
[
KM24
]
. Since this does not introduce substantially new ideas, and in order to keep the paper to a reasonable length, we do not pursue this direction here.

Given a digraph
X
, we define the algebraic Bowen–Franks rank
b(X)
of
X
as the rank of
\mathcal{BF}(X)
as a
\mathbb{Z}
-module and its analytic Bowen–Franks rank
a(X)
as the order of the pole of the zeta function at
1
. In light of the main conjecture given by Theorem
A
and the relation between
\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha)
and the zeta function, we expect that these two quantities should be intimately linked. We define the defect of
X
as
\delta(X)=a(X)-b(X)
and investigate the variation of
\delta(X_{n})
as
X_{n}
varies inside a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-tower. In particular, we prove:

Theorem C

(Theorem
7.6
)
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-tower of strongly connected digraphs. For all
n\geq 1
,

\delta(X_{n})\geq\delta(X_{n-1}).

In the special case where
d=1
, we prove that
\delta(X_{n})
is bounded independently of
n
provided that
\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha)\neq 0
(see Lemma
7.7
). In particular, combined with Theorem
C
, we deduce that
\delta(X_{n})
becomes a constant for
n
sufficiently large. Furthermore, when
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
arises from a constant voltage assignment
\alpha
, that is, the image of
\alpha
consists of a single element, similar to the towers studied in
[
LM24
]
, we can give an explicit
n
for which
\delta(X_{n})
becomes a constant (see Lemma
7.9
). In the case of isogeny graphs defined using supersingular elliptic curves, we can even show that
\delta(X_{n})=\delta(X_{0})
for all
n\geq 0
; see Theorem
7.16
. Our proof relies on recent results of Codogni–Lido
[
CL23
]
which give an explicit relation between isogeny graphs and modular forms.

The behaviour of the defect
\delta(X_{n})
resembles the Leopoldt defect for number fields: Along
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers of number fields the Leopoldt defect is increasing and uniformly bounded. The Leopoldt conjecture predicts that it is always zero. In the setting of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers of digraphs, our calculations have led us to predict that the defect should be constant throughout the tower. Whether there is a relation between these two defects on a conceptual level is unknown to the authors at the time of writing and will be the subject of forthcoming research.

2.
Preliminaries on directed graphs

Definition 2.1
.

A
directed graph
(or a
digraph
)
X
consists of a set of vertices
V(X)
and a set of (directed or oriented) edges
\mathbb{E}(X)
, together with an incidence map

{\rm inc}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{E}(X)\rightarrow V(X)\times V(X)

denoted by
e\mapsto{\rm inc}(e)=(o(e),t(e))
, where
o(e)
is called the origin (or the source) and
t(e)
the terminus (or target) of the directed edge
e
, respectively.

Given
v\in V(X)
, we define

\displaystyle\mathrm{In}(v)

\displaystyle=\{e\in\mathbb{E}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}t(e)=v\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Out}(v)

\displaystyle=\{e\in\mathbb{E}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}o(e)=v\}.

The
in-degree
and
out-degree
of
v
are defined as the cardinality of
\mathrm{In}(v)
and
\mathrm{Out}(v)
, respectively.

Throughout this article, we assume that
\mathbb{E}(X)
and
V(X)
are finite sets. The cardinality of
V(X)
is called the
order
of
X
.

Definition 2.2
.

An
undirected graph

X
consists of a digraph
X=(V(X),\mathbb{E}(X))
together with an inversion map

{\rm inv}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{E}(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{E}(X),

denoted by
{\rm inv}(e)=\bar{e}
, which satisfies

\bar{e}\neq e,\quad\bar{\bar{e}}=e,\quad\text{and}\quad o(\bar{e})=t(e)

for all
e\in\mathbb{E}(X)
. The set of undirected edges of
X
is given by
E(X)=\mathbb{E}(X)/{\rm inv}
.

Informally, an edge of an undirected graph can be viewed as a pair of directed edges
\{e,\bar{e}\}
in a digraph, oriented in opposite directions. In particular, an undirected graph can be considered as a special case of digraphs. Although we primarily study digraphs in this article, all of our results apply equally to undirected graphs through this correspondence.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a digraph. The
forgetful functor
sends
X
to an undirected graph
X^{\prime}
, with
V(X)=V(X^{\prime})
and
\mathbb{E}(X^{\prime})=\mathbb{E}(X)\bigsqcup\mathbb{E}(X)^{\prime}
, where the inversion map induces bijections
\mathbb{E}(X)\to\mathbb{E}(X)^{\prime}
and
\mathbb{E}(X)^{\prime}\to\mathbb{E}(X)
.

More concretely, for each directed edge
e
in
X
, we add an extra edge
\bar{e}
in
X^{\prime}
with opposite orientation as
e
. The set of undirected edges in
X^{\prime}
consist of
\{e,\bar{e}\}
as
e
runs through
\mathbb{E}(X)
. In particular, each directed edge in
X
gives rise to exactly one undirected edge in
X^{\prime}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
X
be a digraph. A
path
of length
k
in
X
is a sequence of edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\in\mathbb{E}(X)
such that
t(e_{i})=o(e_{i+1})
for
i=1,\dots k-1
. We say that it is a path from
o(e_{1})
to
t(e_{k})
. It is said to be a
directed cycle
if
o(e_{1})=t(e_{k})
.

We say that
X
is
strongly connected
if there is a path from
v
to
v^{\prime}
in
X
for all
(v,v^{\prime})\in V(X)\times V(X)
. Let
X^{\prime}
be the undirected graph obtained from
X
after applying the forgetful functor. We say that the graph
X

weakly connected
if
X^{\prime}
is strongly connected.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
X
be a digraph. The divisor group
\textup{Div}(X)
is the group of divisors of
V(X)
. For
v\in V(X)
, we write
d_{v}
for the out-degree of
v
and define

P_{v}=d_{v}(v)v-\sum_{e\in\mathrm{Out}(v)}t(e)\in\textup{Div}(X).

The
group of principal divisors
of
X
, denoted by
\Pr(X)
, is the subgroup of
\textup{Div}(X)
generated by
\{P_{v}\mid v\in V(X)\}
. We define the
Picard group
of
X
as
\textup{Pic}(X)=\textup{Div}(X)/\Pr(X)
.

This is sometimes called the critical group.

Let
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\}
be an enumeration of
V(X)
. Let
D_{X}
denote the
degree matrix
of
X
, which is defined as the
r\times r
diagonal matrix with diagonal entries given by
d_{v_{1}},\dots,d_{v_{r}}
. Let
A_{X}
denote the
adjacency matrix
of
X
, which is the
r\times r
matrix whose
(i,j)
-entry is equal to the number of edges

such that
\inc(e)=(v_{j},v_{i})
.

For a prime number
\ell
, we write
\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X)
for
\textup{Pic}(X)\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
.

We have
\textup{Pic}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r}/(D_{X}-A_{X})\mathbb{Z}^{r}
and
\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{r}/(D_{X}-A_{X})\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{r}
as groups.

Remark 2.6
.

Given a digraph
X
and
v\in V(X)
, the
reachable set

R(v)
is defined as

\{u\in V(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\exists\text{ a path from }v\text{ to }u\}.

A
reach
of
X
is a maximal reachable set. The algebraic and geometric multiplicities of the eigenvalue
0
of
D_{X}-A_{X}
are equal to the number of reaches in
X
; see
[
VL20
, Theorem IV.6]
. In particular, if
X
is strongly connected, the rank of the matrix
D_{X}-A_{X}
is equal to
r-1
, where
r
is the order of
X
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
X
be a digraph of order
r
. We define the
Bowen–Franks operator
on
\textup{Div}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r}
by the matrix
\mathrm{BF}_{X}=\textup{Id}-A_{X}
. The
Bowen-Franks group
of
X
, denoted by
\mathcal{BF}(X)
, is defined as the cokernel of
\mathrm{BF}_{X}
, i.e.,

\mathcal{BF}(X)=\textup{Div}(X)/\mathrm{BF}_{X}\cdot\textup{Div}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r}/(\textup{Id}-A_{X})\mathbb{Z}^{r}.

We define the
algebraic Bowen–Franks rank
of
X
, denoted by
b(X)
, as the rank of
\mathcal{BF}(X)
as a
\mathbb{Z}
-module.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
X
and
Y
be digraphs.
A
morphism of digraphs

f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y\to X

is a map that induces two maps
V(Y)\to V(X)
and
\mathbb{E}(Y)\to\mathbb{E}(X)
that commute with the incidence map. It is said to be a locally an isomorphism around
v\in V(Y)
if it induces bijections from
\mathrm{In}(v)
and
\mathrm{Out}(v)
to
\mathrm{In}(f(v))
and
\mathrm{Out}(f(v))
, respectively.

If there is a bijective morphism between two digraphs
X
and
Y
, we say that they are
isomorphic
, and write
X\cong Y
.

We say that
Y/X
is a covering if there is a surjective digraph morphism from
Y
to
X
that is locally an isomorphism around every
v\in V(Y)
.

If
Y/X
is a covering of directed graphs with the projection map
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y\to X
, the group of
deck transformations
of
Y/X
, denoted by
\mathrm{Deck}(Y/X)
, is the group of digraph automorphisms
\sigma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y\to Y
such that
\pi\circ\sigma=\pi
.

A covering
Y/X
is said to be
d
-sheeted
if
d
is a positive integer such that each element of
V(X)
has
d
pre-images in
V(Y)
.

Finally, We say that the covering
Y/X
is
Galois
if there exists an integer
d
such that
Y/X
is a
d
-sheeted covering and
|\textup{Deck}(Y/X)|=d
.

Remark 2.9
.

Let
Y/X
be a
d
-sheeted covering, where
X
is weakly connected. Suppose that there are
r
weakly connected components in
Y
and let
Y_{0}
be one of these components. Then
Y_{0}/X
is a
d/r
-sheeted Galois covering and the group
\mathrm{Deck}(Y/X)
induces isomorphisms across all weakly connected components of
Y
(see
[
LM25
, Proposition 2.4]
). In particular,
|\mathrm{Deck}(Y_{0}/x)|=d/r
and there are
r!
elements in
\mathrm{Deck}(Y/X)
given by permutations of these components. Thus,

|\textup{Deck}(Y/X)|\geq r!|\mathrm{Deck}(Y_{0}/x)|^{r}>d

unless
d=r=2
. In other words, If
Y
is not weakly connected, then
Y/X
is not Galois unless
d=r=2
.

If
d=r=2
, it is possible that
Y/X
is a Galois covering even if
Y
is not weakly connected. Let
X
be an arbitrary weakly connected digraph, and let
Y
be the digraph consisting of the disjoint union of two copies of
X
. Then there is a natural covering
Y\to X
, whose group of deck transformations is the permutation of the two components. As the covering is
2
-sheeted, it is indeed Galois.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
X
be a digraph and
(G,\cdot)
a group. A
G
-valued
voltage assignment
on
X
is a function
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{E}(X)\rightarrow G
. In the case of undirected graph, we require that

for all

.

Given a

-valued voltage assignment on

whose vertices and directed edges are given by
\mathbb{V}(X)\times G
and
\mathbb{E}(X)\times G
, respectively. In addition, for all
\sigma\in G
and

with

, we have

.

In the case where

ensures that the inversion map on

extends to an inverse map on

Definition 2.11
.

Let
X
be a digraph and
d\geq 1
an integer. A
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-tower
over
X
, is a sequence of digraph coverings

X=X_{0}\leftarrow X_{1}\leftarrow X_{2}\leftarrow\cdots\leftarrow X_{n}\leftarrow\cdots

such that for each
n\geq 0
, the cover

is Galois with Galois group isomorphic to

.

It follows from Remark
2.9
that if

is weakly connected, then the digraphs

in a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-tower over

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
(X_{n})
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-tower of strongly connected digraphs. There exists a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-valued voltage assignment
\alpha
on
X
such that
X_{n}\cong X((\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})^{d},\alpha_{n})
, where
\alpha_{n}
is the composition of
\alpha

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}\to(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})^{d}
.

Proof.

We follow the discussions
[
Gon21
, §2.4]
and
[
Ter11
, §13-14]
to realize
X_{n}
as a derived digraph. Fix a spanning tree
T
of
X
rooted at a chosen vertex
v_{0}
(i.e., a subgraph that includes all vertices of
X
, containing no directed cycles and
v_{0}
is of in-degree
0
and every other vertex has an in-degree
1
; such a spanning tree exists as
X
is strongly connected). Let
G_{n}=\Gal(X_{n}/X)
. The digraph
X_{n}
contains
p^{nd}
copies of
T
, indexed by the elements of
G_{n}
. Each vertex of
X_{n}
can be labelled as
(v,g)
, where
v\in V(X)
and
g\in G_{n}
. If
e\in\mathbb{E}(X)
goes from
u
to
v
, we define
\alpha_{n}(e)
to be
g_{2}-g_{1}
if there is an edge in
X_{n}
going from
(u,g_{1})
to
(v,g_{2})
. One can check that this is well-defined and
X_{n}
is isomorphic to the derived digraph
X(\alpha_{n})
. Further, since we have the coverings
X_{n+1}\to X_{n}\to X
, we can check that
\alpha_{n+1}\equiv\alpha_{n}\mod p^{n}
. Thus, taking inverse limit gives rise to a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-valued voltage assignment, as desired.
∎

From now on, we fix an integer
d\geq 1
and write
\Gamma
for the topological group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
. The group operation on
\Gamma
will be written multiplicatively, rather than additively. Further, we write
\Gamma_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Gamma/\Gamma^{p^{n}}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})^{d}
.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
X
be a digraph. If
v\in V(X)
, we define
\mathcal{C}(v)
as the set of all directed cycles in
X
that pass through
v
. If
\alpha
is a
\Gamma
-valued voltage assignment on
X
and
C
is a directed cycle in
X
that consists of the edges
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}\}
, we define
\alpha(C)
to be the element
\alpha(e_{1})\cdots\alpha(e_{m})\in\Gamma
.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
Y/X
be a covering of digraphs, equipped with the morphism
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}Y\to X
. If
P=(e_{1},\dots,e_{k})
is a path in
X
, we say that a path
P^{\prime}=(e_{1}^{\prime},\dots,e_{k}^{\prime})
in
Y
is a lift of
P
if
\pi(e_{i}^{\prime})=e_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,k
.

The following lemma gives a criterion on the strong connectivity of derived digraphs.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
X
be a strongly connected digraph and
\alpha
a
\Gamma
-valued voltage assignment on
X
. For an integer
n\geq 1
, write
\alpha_{n}
for the composition of
\alpha
with the projection map
\Gamma\to\Gamma_{n}
. Let
X_{n}
denote the derived digraph
X(\Gamma_{n},\alpha_{n})
. The digraph
X_{n}
is strongly connected for all
n
if and only if
\{\alpha(C)\mid C\in\mathcal{C}(v)\}
topologically generates
\Gamma
for all
v\in V(X)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\{\alpha(C)\mid C\in\mathcal{C}(v)\}
generates
\Gamma
topologically for all
v\in V(X)
.
Let
(v,\tau)
and
(w,\tau^{\prime})
be two vertices in
X_{n}
. Let
P
be a path from
v
to
w
in
X
(this
P
exists as
X
is assumed to be strongly connected). Further,
P
induces a path in
X_{n}
from
(v,\tau)
to
(w,\tau\alpha_{n}(P))
. By the assumption on
\alpha
, we can find a directed cycle
C\in\mathcal{C}(w)
such that
\alpha_{n}(C)=\tau^{\prime}(\tau\alpha_{n}(P))^{-1}
. This lifts to a path from
(w,\tau\alpha_{n}(P))
to
(w,\tau^{\prime})
. Thus, there is a path from
(v,\tau)
to
(w,\tau^{\prime})
. Hence,
X_{n}
is strongly connected.

Conversely, assume that
X_{n}
is strongly connected. Let
\tau\in\Gamma_{n}
and
v\in V(X)
. As
X_{n}
is strongly connected, there exists a path from
(v,1)
to
(v,\tau)
. The image of this path in
X
is a directed cycle
C
that passes
v
, with
\alpha_{n}(C)=\tau
. As this holds for an arbitrary choice of
n
and
\tau
, it follows that the set
\{\alpha(C)\mid C\in\mathcal{C}(v)\}
topologically generates
\Gamma
.
∎

3.
Review on Iwasawa algebras

In what follows, we write
\Phi_{p,d}=\mathbb{Z}[T_{1},\ldots,T_{d};\mathbb{Z}_{p}]
for the ring of polynomials in
T_{1},\ldots,T_{d}
whose coefficients are in
\mathbb{Z}
and the exponents are in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. In other words, the elements of
\Phi_{p,d}
are
\mathbb{Z}
-linear combinations of monomials of the form

T_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots T_{d}^{a_{d}}
, where
a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Note that we can regard
\Phi_{p,d}
as the ring
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}]
.

Let
\ell
be a prime number (which may or may not be equal to
p
), we fix an embedding
\iota_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}
. Let
\mu_{p^{\infty}}
denote the set of
p
-power roots of unity in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. We denote by its image in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}
under
\iota_{\ell}
by the same image.

Recall that we write
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
and the group operation on
\Gamma
is written multiplicatively.
Let
\pi_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Gamma\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}
denote the projection map from
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
to the
i
-th component. Let
\Gamma^{\vee}
denote the set of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}^{\times}
-valued finite-order characters of
\Gamma
(we suppress the dependency of
\ell
in the notation for simplicity). We define
\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}
similarly for all
n\geq 1
. Note that
\Gamma^{\vee}=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}
. Let
\mathscr{R}^{(\ell)}
denote the orbits of
\Gamma^{\vee}
under the action of the absolute Galois group
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}
.

Given any
\vec{\zeta}=(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{d})\in\mu_{p^{\infty}}^{d}
, let

\ev_{\vec{\zeta}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Phi_{p,d}\to\mathbb{Z}[\mu_{p^{\infty}}]

be the evaluation map given
T_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots T_{d}^{a_{d}}\mapsto\zeta_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\zeta_{d}^{a_{d}}
.
We fix a topological generator
\gamma
of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. For
1\leq i\leq d
, write
\gamma_{i}=(1,\dots,1,\gamma,1,\dots,1)\in\Gamma
, where
\gamma
appears in the
i
-th component.
If
\omega\in\Gamma^{\vee}
, we have the evaluation map
\ev_{\omega}=\ev_{(\omega(\gamma_{1}),\dots,\omega(\gamma_{d}))}
on
\Phi_{p,d}
. If
F\in\Phi_{p,d}
, we shall write
\omega(F)
for
\ev_{\omega}(F)
.

We define
\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)
as the Iwasawa algebra

\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)=\varprojlim_{n}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma_{n}],

where
\Gamma_{n}=\Gamma/\Gamma^{p^{n}}
and the connecting maps are natural projections.

Lemma 3.1
.

There is an injective homomorphism of rings

\Phi_{p,d}\hookrightarrow\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma).

Proof.

Given any integer
n\geq 1
, there is a natural injection

\Phi_{p,d}/\left(T_{1}^{p^{n}}-1,\dots T_{d}^{p^{n}}-1\right)\cong\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma_{n}]\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma_{n}],

after identifying
T_{i}
with a topological generator of
\pi_{i}(\Gamma)
. This gives an injection,

\varprojlim_{n}\Phi_{p,d}/\left(T_{1}^{p^{n}}-1,\dots,T_{d}^{p^{n}}-1\right)\hookrightarrow\varprojlim_{n}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma_{n}]=\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma).

Since

\bigcap\left(T_{1}^{p^{n}}-1,\dots,T_{d}^{p^{n}}-1\right)=0,

there is a natural injection

\Phi_{p,d}\hookrightarrow\varprojlim_{n}\Phi_{p,d}/\left(T_{1}^{p^{n}}-1,\dots,T_{d}^{p^{n}}-1\right),

from which the lemma follows.
∎

We recall that if
p=\ell
and
M
is a finitely generated torsion
\Lambda_{p}(\Gamma)
-module, there is a pseudo-isomorphism between
M
and a direct sum of cyclic
\Lambda_{p}(\Gamma)
-modules
\bigoplus_{i}\Lambda_{p}(\Gamma)/(F_{i})
for some
F_{i}\in\Lambda_{p}(\Gamma)
(see
[
Bou98
, Chapitre VII, no. 4, Théorème 5]
). The characteristic ideal of
M
is defined as

\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda_{p}(\Gamma)}M=\left(\prod_{i}F_{i}\right)\subseteq\Lambda_{p}(\Gamma)

When
M
is not torsion over
\Lambda_{p}(\Gamma)
, we set

\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda_{p}(\Gamma)}M=0.

Finally, we recall that after sending
\gamma_{i}
to
T_{i}
, the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda_{p}(\Gamma)
can be identified with the ring of power series
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[T_{1}-1,\dots,T_{d}-1]]
.

3.1.
Characteristic ideals when
\ell\neq p

Throughout this subsection, we assume that
p\neq\ell
are distinct prime numbers.
We have

\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)=\prod_{[\omega]\in\mathscr{R}^{(\ell)}}\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)_{[\omega]},

where
\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)_{[\omega]}\subseteq\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)
is a ring isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\omega(\Gamma)]
(see
[
BL23
, Theorem 2.1]
).

Let
\omega\in\Gamma^{\vee}
. We define
e_{\omega,n}
as the idempotent of
\omega
if
\omega\in\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}
, and
0
otherwise. Set
e_{[\omega],n}=\sum_{\omega\in[\omega]}e_{\omega,n}
. This defines an element
e_{[\omega]}=(e_{[\omega],n})\in\varprojlim\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma_{n}]=\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)
.
Given a
\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)
-module
M
and
[\omega]\in\mathscr{R}^{(\ell)}
, we write
M_{[\omega]}=e_{[\omega]}M
.

If
M=\varprojlim_{n}M\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma_{n}]
, we have the decomposition

(3.1)

M\cong\prod_{[\omega]\in\mathscr{R}^{(\ell)}}M_{[\omega]};

see
[
BL23
, (13)]
.

We say that a
\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)
-module is locally finitely generated if each
M_{[\omega]}
is a finitely generated over
\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)_{[\omega]}
.
For such
M
, we write

M_{[\omega]}=\left(\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)_{[\omega]}\right)^{\oplus r_{[\omega]}}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^{s_{[\omega]}}\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)_{[\omega]}/I_{[\omega],j},

where
r_{[\omega]}
and
s_{[\omega]}
are nonnegative integers and
I_{[\omega],j}
are nonzero ideals that are uniquely determined by
M
and
\omega
.
The
[\omega]
-characteristic ideal of
M
is defined as

\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)_{[\omega]}}\left(M_{[\omega]}\right)=\begin{cases}\prod_{j=1}^{s_{[\omega]}}I_{[\omega],j}&\text{if }r_{[\omega]}=0,\\
(0_{[\omega]})&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The characteristic ideal of
M
is defined as

\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)}(M)=\left(\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)_{[\omega]}}\left(M_{[\omega]}\right)\right)_{[\omega]\in\mathscr{R}^{(\ell)}}\subseteq\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma).

Note that if
\omega
is of order
p^{n}
, then
\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)_{[\omega]}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\zeta_{p^{n}}]
. As
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(\zeta_{p^{n}})/\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
is unramified,
I_{[\omega],j}
is generated by a power of
\ell
. In other words,
\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)_{[\omega]}}(M_{[\omega]})
is of the form
\ell_{[\omega]}^{t_{[\omega]}}
when
r_{[\omega]}=0
(see
[
BL23
, Remark 4.8]
).

We conclude this section with the following lemma, which will be used later in the article.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathscr{R}_{n}^{(\ell)}
denote the set of classes
[\omega]\in\mathscr{R}^{(\ell)}
such that
\omega\in\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}
. We have the following ring isomorphisms

\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)/(T_{1}^{p^{n}}-1,T_{2}^{p^{n}}-1,\dots,T_{d}^{p^{n}}-1)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma_{n}]\cong\bigoplus_{[\omega]\in\mathscr{R}_{n}^{(\ell)}}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\omega(\Gamma_{n})]

Proof.

See
[
BL23
, Remark 2.2 (4)]
.
∎

4.
p
-adic functions attached to a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-tower

The main goal of this section is to define the
p
-adic zeta function and
p
-adic Bowen–Franks function of a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-tower and study their interpolation properties. We begin with a review on the archimedean counterpart of these functions.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
X
be a digraph. A cycle
C
in
X
is said to be
prime
if it is not the
m
-multiple of another cycle for some integer
m\geq 2
. Two cycles are said to be
equivalent
if one is a cyclic permutation of the other. An equivalence class of prime cycles under this equivalence relation is called a
prime
of
X
. If
[C]
is a prime, where
C
is a representative of the equivalence class, the length of
[C]
, denoted by
|[C]|
, is defined as the number of edges in
C
.

We define the
zeta function
of
X
as

Z_{X}(u)=\prod_{[C]}(1-u^{|[C]|})^{-1}\in\mathbb{Z}[[u]],

where the product runs over all primes of
X
.

Remark 4.2
.

Our definition of the zeta function follows the one given in
[
KS00
]
(denoted by
Z^{\circ}(u)
therein). It follows from Lemma 2.2 in
op. cit.
that it is equal to
\det(1-uA_{X})^{-1}
. When
X
is an undirected graph, this is different from the classical Ihara zeta function, since each undirected edge is represented by two directed edges under our convention.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
Y/X
be a Galois covering. Let
P
be a path of length
r
in
X
from
u
to
v
. Let
P^{\prime}
be the unique path of length
r
in
Y
from
(u,1)
to
(v,g)
, where
g\in\Gal(Y/X)
. The element
g
is called the
Frobenius
automorphism of
P
.

For the rest of this section, we fix a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-tower
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of strongly connected digraphs over a digraph
X
. The voltage assignment given by Lemma
2.12
will be denoted by
\alpha
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
\omega\in\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}
. We define the
L
-function
for
X_{n}/X
and
\omega
as

L(X_{n}/X,\omega,u)=\prod_{[C]}(1-\omega([C])u^{|[C]|})^{-1}\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega(\Gamma_{n})][[u]],

where the product runs over all primes of
X
and
\omega([C])
denotes the image of the Frobenius of
[C]
in
\Gamma_{n}=\Gal(X_{n}/X)
under
\omega
.

The following theorem can be regarded as the
Artin formalism
for zeta functions of digraphs.

Theorem 4.5
.

The following equality holds:

Z_{X_{n}}(u)=\prod_{\omega\in\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}}L(X_{n}/X,\omega,u).

Proof.

Let
G=\Gal(X_{n}/X)
. Let
[C]
be a prime of
X
. We denote by
D_{[C]}\subset G
the subgroup generated by the Frobenius of
[C]
. Note that one requires
|D_{[C]}|
distinct lifts of
[C]
in order to obtain a cycle in
X_{n}
consisting solely of lifts of
[C]
.

We have

\displaystyle\prod_{\omega\in{\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}}}L(X_{n}/X,\omega,u)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\omega}\prod_{[C]}(1-\omega([C])u^{|[C]|})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{[C]}\prod_{\omega\in(D_{[C]})^{\vee}}(1-\omega([C])u^{|[C]|})^{-|G/D_{[C]}|}

\displaystyle=\prod_{[C]}(1-u^{|[C]||D_{[C]}|})^{-|G/D_{[C]}|}

\displaystyle=Z_{X_{n}}(u).

The last equality follows from the following fact: Let
[C^{\prime}]
be a prime of
X_{n}
. Then its image in
X
is the
f
-th power of a prime
[C]
of
X
for some integer
f
. Note that
|[C^{\prime}]|=|[C]|f
and
f=|D_{[C]}|
. Furthermore, there are exactly
|G/D_{[C]}|
primes of
X_{n}
that are mapped to
[C]^{f}
in
X
.
∎

The following discussion is inspired by
[
KS00
, Section 2]
.
We fix
\omega\in\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}
. For any positive integers
r
and
m
, we define

M_{r}^{m}(\omega)=\sum_{[C]}\omega([C])^{m},

where the sum runs over all primes of
X
of length
r
. We further define

N_{r}(\omega)=\sum_{D}\omega([D]),

where the sum runs over all cycles in
X
(up to equivalence) of length
r
.

Note that

(4.1)

N_{v}(\omega)=\sum_{mr=v}\ell M^{m}_{r}(\omega).

Lemma 4.6
.

We have the following equality of power series in
u
:

L(X_{n}/X,\omega,u)=\exp\left(\sum_{v\geq 1}\frac{1}{v}N_{v}(\omega)u^{v}\right).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\log(L(X/Y,\omega,u))

\displaystyle=-\sum_{[C]}\log(1-\omega([C])u^{|[C]|})

\displaystyle=\sum_{[C]}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{1}{m}\omega([C])^{m}u^{m|[C]|}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{1}{m}u^{rm}\sum_{|[C]|=r}(\omega[C])^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{1}{m}M_{r}^{m}(\omega)u^{mr}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\geq 1}\frac{1}{v}u^{v}\sum_{rm=v}rM_{r}^{m}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\geq 1}\frac{1}{v}N_{v}(\omega)u^{v},

where the last equation follows from (
4.1
).
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
A_{w}=\omega(A_{\alpha})\in\textup{Mat}_{r\times r}(\mathbb{Z}[\omega(\Gamma)])
. Then

L(X_{n}/X,\omega,u)=\det(\mathrm{Id}-A_{\omega}u)^{-1}.

Proof.

The matrix-entry
(A_{\omega}^{v})_{i,j}
is equal to
\sum_{[P]}\omega([P])
, where the sum runs over equivalence classes of all paths of length
v
from
v_{j}
to
v_{i}
. In particular,
\textup{Tr}(A_{\omega}^{v})=N_{v}(\omega)
.
If
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r}
are the eigenvalues of
A_{\omega}
counted with multiplicity, then

N_{v}(\omega)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}^{v}.

Combined with Lemma
4.6
, we deduce

\displaystyle L(X/Y,\omega,u)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{v\geq 1}\frac{1}{v}N_{v}(\omega)u^{v}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{v\geq 1}\frac{1}{v}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}^{v}u^{v}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\exp(-\log(1-\lambda_{i}u))=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{1}{1-\lambda_{i}u}

\displaystyle=\det(1-A_{\omega}u)^{-1},

as desired.
∎

Definition 4.8
.

We define the
p
-adic zeta function
of a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-tower of strongly connected digraphs over
X
, with voltage assignment
\alpha
, by

L_{p}(X,\alpha)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\det(D_{X}-A_{\alpha})\in\Phi_{p,d},

where
D_{X}
is the diagonal degree matrix given as in Definition
2.5
and
A_{\alpha}
is the
r\times r
matrix whose
(i,j)
-entry is given by
\displaystyle\sum_{e}\prod_{i=1}^{d}T_{i}^{\pi_{i}\circ\alpha(e)}
, where the sum runs over all directed edges from
v_{j}
to
v_{i}
.

Similarly, we define the
p
-adic Bowen–Franks function
as

\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\det(\textup{Id}-A_{\alpha})\in\Phi_{p,d}.

By Lemma
3.1
, we can regard
L_{p}(X,\alpha)
and
\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha)
as elements of
\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)
for all primes
\ell
.

Let
q\geq 1
be an integer. We say that a digraph
X
is
out-regular of degree

q
if
|\mathrm{Out}(v)|=q
for all
v\in V(X)
. We have the following interpolation formuale.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\omega
be a finite order character of
\Gamma
. Assume that
\omega
is a character of
\Gamma_{n}
. Then

\omega(\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha))=L(X_{n}/X,\omega,1)^{-1}.

If
X
is in addition out-regular of degree
q
, we have

\omega(L_{p}(X,\alpha))=q^{r}L(X_{n}/X,\omega,q^{-1})^{-1},

where
r
is the order
X

Proof.

The first assertion follows from Lemma
4.7
after setting
u=1
.

For the second assertion, we have
D_{X}=q\Id
since
X
is assumed to be out-regular of degree
q
. Therefore

\displaystyle\omega(L_{p}(X,\alpha))

\displaystyle=\det(\omega(D_{X}-A_{\alpha}))=q^{r}\det(\omega(\Id-q^{-1}A_{\alpha}))=q^{r}\det(\Id-q^{-1}A_{\omega})

\displaystyle=q^{r}L(X_{n}/X,\omega,q^{-1})^{-1},

where the last equality follows from Lemma
4.7
.
∎

Remark 4.10
.

In the case of undirected graphs, we have the following alternative definition of
L
-functions, known as the Artin–Ihara
L
-function. Let
Y/X
be a Galois covering of connected undirected graphs, with Galois group
G
that is abelian. Let
\omega
be a character of
G
. We define

L_{X}(u,\omega)=\frac{(1-u^{2})^{\chi(X)}}{h_{X}(u,\omega)}\in\mathbb{Z}[\omega(G)][[u]],

where
\chi(X)
is the Euler characteristic of
X
and

h_{X}(u,\omega)=\det(\mathrm{Id}-A_{\omega}u+(D_{X}-\mathrm{Id})u^{2})\in\mathbb{Z}[\psi(G)][u].

Here,
A_{\omega}
is the adjacency matrix of
X
twisted by
\omega
(see
[
DV23
, §2.4]
for further details). It then follows that

h_{X}(1,\omega)=\det(D_{X}-A_{\omega}).

In other words, the
p
-adic zeta function attached to a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-tower of undirected graphs interpolates the values
h_{X}(1,\omega)
as
\omega
varies over
\Gamma^{\vee}
.

It is not clear to the authors whether
L_{p}(X,\alpha)
satisfies similar properties in the directed case if the digraphs are not out-regular.

5.
Iwasawa main conjectures for
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-towers

Throughout this section, we fix a
\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{p}
-tower of strongly connected digraphs

X\leftarrow X_{1}\leftarrow X_{2}\leftarrow\dots\leftarrow X_{n}\leftarrow\dots

We also fix a prime number

.

Definition 5.1
.

We denote by
\textup{Div}_{\ell}(X_{n})
the
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}

\textup{Div}(X_{n})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
. Similarly, we define
\Pr_{\ell}(X_{n})
,
\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n})
, and
\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{n})
. Define
\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{\infty})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\displaystyle\varprojlim_{n}\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n})
and
\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{\infty})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\displaystyle\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{n})

Definition 5.2
.

Let
m\geq n
be non-negative integers. We define
I^{m}_{n}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma_{m}]
as the kernel of the natural projection
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma_{m}]\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma_{n}]

I^{\infty}_{n}
as the kernel of
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[[\Gamma]]\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma_{n}]
.

Lemma 5.3
.

For all non-negative integers
m\geq n
, there are isomorphisms

\displaystyle\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{m})/I_{n}^{m}\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{m})

\displaystyle\cong\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{m})/I_{n}^{m}\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{m})

\displaystyle\cong\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{n}).

Proof.

Consider the following commutative diagram with exact rows

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pr_{\ell}(X_{m})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{Div}_{\ell}(X_{m})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{m})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pr_{\ell}(X_{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{Div}_{\ell}(X_{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

By definitions, all three vertical maps are surjective. The kernel of the middle vertical map is given by
I_{n}^{m}\textup{Div}_{\ell}(X_{m})
. Hence, the first asserted isomorphism follows from the snake lemma. The second follows from the same argument by considering the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{BF}_{X_{m}}\cdot\textup{Div}_{\ell}(X_{m})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{Div}_{\ell}(X_{m})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{m})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{BF}_{X_{n}}\cdot\textup{Div}_{\ell}(X_{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{Div}_{\ell}(X_{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

∎

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
r
be the order of
X_{0}
. The
\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)
-modules
{\rm Pic}_{\ell}(X_{\infty})
and
\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{\infty})
are isomorphic to the quotients
\Lambda^{r}/\left(D_{X}-A_{\alpha}\right)
and
\Lambda^{r}/\left(\Id-A_{\alpha}\right)
, respectively.

Proof.

Recall that the vertices of
X_{n}
are of the form
(v,g)
, where
v\in V(X)
,
g\in\Gamma_{n}
. Thus, there is a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma_{n}]
-isomorphism
\textup{Div}_{\ell}(X_{n})\cong\displaystyle\bigoplus_{v\in V(X)}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma_{n}](v,1)
, where the action of
g\in\Gamma_{n}
is given by
g\cdot(v,1)=(v,g)
. Similarly,
\Pr_{\ell}(X_{n})
is isomorphic to the
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma_{n}]
-module generated by the set of divisors
\{(D_{X}-A_{\alpha})(v,1)\mid v\in V(X)\}
. It follows that
\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n})
is isomorphic to the quotient
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma_{n}]^{r}/\left(D_{X}-A_{\alpha}\right)
. Taking projective limits gives the
\Lambda
-isomorphism

\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{\infty})\cong\Lambda^{r}/\left(D_{X}-A_{\alpha}\right),

as desired. The proof for Bowen–Franks groups can be carried out analogously.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Assume that
\ell\neq p
.
Let
[\omega]\in\mathscr{R}^{(\ell)}
. If
n
is an integer such that
\omega\in\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}
, then

e_{[\omega]}\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{\infty})=e_{[\omega],n}\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n})

and

e_{[\omega]}\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{\infty})=e_{[\omega],n}\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{n}).

Proof.

As before, we only provide the proof for Picard groups, since the proof for Bowen–Franks groups can be carried out analogously. By the choice of
n
, we have
e_{[\omega]}I_{n}^{\infty}\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{\infty})=0
. Thus, we deduce

e_{[\omega]}\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{\infty})\cong e_{[\omega]}\left(\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{\infty})/I^{\infty}_{n}\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{\infty})\right)\cong e_{[\omega]}\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n})\cong e_{[\omega],n}\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n}),

where the last identity follows from the fact that
\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n})
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma_{n}]
-module.
∎

Through Corollary
5.5
, we can compute
\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)_{[\omega]}}\left(\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{\infty})_{[\omega]}\right)
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\ell
and
p
be distinct primes. If
\omega
is a finite-order of
\Gamma
, then

\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)_{[\omega]}}\left(\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{\infty})_{[\omega]}\right)=(\omega(\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha))

and

\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)_{[\omega]}}\left(\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{\infty})_{[\omega]}\right)=(\omega(L_{p}(X,\alpha)).

Proof.

Suppose that
\omega
is of order
p^{n}
. By Corollary
5.5
, it suffices to compute
\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)_{[\omega]}}\left(e_{[\omega],n}\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n})\right)
. Let
r
be the order of
X
. Let
\Lambda_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma_{n}]
. Consider the short exact sequence of
\Lambda_{n}
-modules:

0\to\Lambda_{n}^{r}\to\Lambda_{n}^{r}\to\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n})\to 0,

where the map
\Lambda_{n}^{r}\to\Lambda_{n}^{r}
is given by the matrix
(D_{X}-A_{\alpha})\pmod{I_{n}}
. As
e_{[\omega],n}
is an idempotent, the multiplication with
e_{[\omega],n}
is an exact functor. Applying
e_{[\omega],_{n}}
to the sequence above, we obtain the following exact sequence

0\to(e_{[\omega],n}\Lambda_{n})^{r}\to(e_{[\omega],n}\Lambda_{n})^{r}\to e_{[\omega],n}\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n})\to 0,

where the map
(e_{[\omega_{n}],n}\Lambda_{n})^{r}\to(e_{[\omega_{n}],n}\Lambda_{n})^{r}
is given by
e_{[\omega_{n}],n}(D-A_{\alpha})
. Recall that
e_{[\omega],n}\Lambda_{n}=\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\omega(\Gamma_{n})]
. Thus, we have a short exact sequence

0\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\omega(\Gamma_{n})]^{r}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\omega(\Gamma_{n})]^{r}\to e_{[\omega],n}\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n})\to 0

In particular,
e_{[\omega],n}\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n})
is a quadratically presented
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\omega(\Gamma_{n})]
-module. Its characteristic ideal is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Char}_{e_{[\omega],n}\Lambda_{n}}(e_{[\omega],n}\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n}))

\displaystyle=(\det(e_{[\omega],n}(D-A_{\alpha})))=(e_{[\omega],n}\det(D-A_{\alpha}))

\displaystyle=(\omega(\det(D-A_{\alpha})))\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\omega(\Gamma_{n})],

where the last equality follows from the fact that
\ell
and
p
are coprime. The proof for Bowen–Franks groups can be carried out analogously.
∎

The last remaining ingredient to complete the proof of Theorem
A
is the following result on the non-vanishing of the
p
-adic zeta function.

Theorem 5.7
.

If
\omega
is a non-trivial character of
\Gamma_{n}
, then

\omega(L_{p}(X,\alpha))\neq 0.

In particular,
L_{p}(X,\alpha)\neq 0
. If
X
is furthermore out-regular of degree
q
and
\omega
is of order
p^{n}
, then

L(X_{n}/X,\omega,q^{-1})\neq 0.

Proof.

Since the non-vanishing of
\omega(L_{p}(X,\alpha))
is independent of the embedding
\iota_{\ell}
, we may consider its image in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}
, where
\ell\neq p
. Recall from Lemma
3.2
the isomorphism

\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma_{n}]\cong\bigoplus_{[\omega]\in\mathscr{R}_{n}^{(\ell)}}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\omega(\Gamma_{n})].

In addition, we have seen in the proof of Theorem
5.4
that

\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma_{n}]^{r}/(D_{X}-A_{\alpha}).

Hence, we deduce

\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n})\cong\bigoplus_{[\omega]\in\mathscr{R}_{n}^{(\ell)}}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\omega(\Gamma_{n})]^{r}/(D_{X}-\omega(A_{\alpha})).

By Remark
2.6
, the rank of the
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\textup{Pic}(X_{n})
is
1
. Thus, there exists exactly one
[\omega]
such that
(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\omega(\Gamma)])^{r}/(D-\omega(A_{\alpha}))
has positive
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-rank. As this holds for all
n
, this character has to be the trivial character. In particular, for all
\omega
that is non-trivial,
\det(D_{X}-\omega(A_{\alpha}))\neq 0
, proving the first assertion of the theorem. The final assertion follows from Proposition
4.9
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
A
.

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-tower of strongly connected digraphs. For all primes
\ell
, we have

\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)}(\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{\infty}))=(L_{p}(X,\alpha))

and

\mathrm{Char}_{\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)}(\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{\infty}))=(\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha)).

Furthermore,
{\rm Pic}_{\ell}(X_{\infty})
is a torsion
\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)
-module.

Proof.

The equality of characteristic ideals follows from Theorem
5.4
and Lemma
5.6
; the torsionness of
\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{\infty})
follows from Theorem
5.7
.
∎

Remark 5.9
.

For a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-tower of strongly connected digraphs
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, the
\Lambda_{\ell}(\Gamma)
-module
\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{\infty})
is a Sinnott module in the sense of
[
BL23
, Definition 4.6]
. If
\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha)\neq 0
, the same is true for
\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{\infty})
.

Remark 5.10
.

In the case of undirected graphs, the statement in Theorem
5.8
for Picard groups when
\ell=p
has been proved in
[
Kat24
, Theorem 6.1]
and
[
KM24
, Theorem 5.2 and Remark 5.3]
.

Example 5.11
.

Set
d=1
. Let
X
be a digraph with adjacency matrix

A_{X}=\begin{pmatrix}2&2&0\\
1&1&1\\
1&2&1\end{pmatrix}.

We can check that
X
is strongly connected. Consider a voltage assignment
\alpha
such that

A_{\alpha}=\begin{pmatrix}2&1+T&0\\
1&1&1\\
1&1+T&1\end{pmatrix}.

We can verify using Lemma
2.15
that
X_{n}
is strongly connected for all
n
. We have

\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha)=\det(I-A_{\alpha})=\det\begin{pmatrix}-1&-1-T&0\\

In particular, it follows from Theorem
5.4
that
\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{\infty})
is not a torsion

-module for all

. Furthermore, the algebraic Bowen–Franks rank

is unbounded as

.

6.
A Sinnott–Washington type theorem

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
B
.

Definition 6.1
.

For each
\vec{\zeta}=(\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{d})\in\mu_{p^{\infty}}^{d}
and
a=(a_{1},\dots,a_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
, we write
a(\vec{\zeta})=\zeta_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\zeta_{d}^{a_{d}}\in\mu_{p^{\infty}}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
be two distinct vectors, and
r\geq 1
an integer. We have

\#\{\vec{\zeta}\in\mu_{p^{n}}^{d}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}a(\vec{\zeta})^{r}=b(\vec{\zeta})^{r}\}=O(p^{(d-1)n}).

Proof.

Suppose
n
is large enough so that
p^{r}a\not\equiv p^{r}b\mod p^{n}
.
Without loss of generality, suppose that
p^{r}a_{1}\not\equiv p^{r}b_{1}\mod p^{n}
. Let
s=\ord_{p}(a_{1}-b_{1})
. Then
r+s<n
. If
a(\vec{\zeta})^{p^{r}}=b(\vec{\zeta})^{p^{r}}
, then

\zeta_{1}^{p^{r}(a_{1}-b_{1})}=\prod_{i=2}^{d}\zeta_{i}^{p^{r}(b_{i}-a_{i})}.

For any given choice of
\zeta_{2},\dots,\zeta_{d}\in\mu_{p^{n}}
, there are at most
p^{r+s}
values of
\zeta_{1}\in\mu_{p^{n}}
for which the equality above holds. Hence, there are at most
p^{(d-1)n+r+s}
choices of
\vec{\zeta}\in\mu_{p^{n}}^{d}
such that
a(\vec{\zeta})^{r}=b(\vec{\zeta})^{r}
.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
F\in\Phi_{p,d}
be a non-zero element. Let
\ell\neq p
be a prime number such that
F\notin\ell\Phi_{p,d}
. Then

\sharp\{\omega\in\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\ord_{\ell}(\omega(F))>0\}=O(p^{(d-1)n}).

Proof.

Let
\bar{F}
denote the image of
F
in
\Phi_{p,d}/\ell\Phi_{p,d}
, and write

\bar{F}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}T_{1}^{a_{i,1}}\cdots T_{d}^{a_{i,d}},

where
b_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}
and
a_{i}=(a_{i,1},\dots,a_{i,d})\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
.

Suppose
\vec{\zeta}
is chosen such that
a_{i}(\vec{\zeta})^{-1}a_{j}(\vec{\zeta})\notin\mathbb{F}_{\ell}(\mu_{p})
whenever
i\neq j
. We follow the proof of
[
Sin87
, Theorem 2.2]
to show that
\ev_{\vec{\zeta}}\bar{F}\neq 0
. Suppose the contrary. Then

\ev_{\vec{\zeta}}\bar{F}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\bar{b}_{i}a_{i}(\vec{\zeta})=0.

Let
k
be the extension of
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
that contains
\mu_{p}
and
a_{i}(\vec{\zeta})
for all
i
. Let
\mathrm{Tr}
denote the trace map from
k
to
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}(\mu_{p})
.
Then for all
i
,

\mathrm{Tr}\left(a_{i}(\vec{\zeta})^{-1}\ev_{\vec{\zeta}}\bar{F}\right)=[k\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{F}_{\ell}(\mu_{p})]\bar{b}_{i}=0.

As
[k\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{F}_{\ell}(\mu_{p})]
is a power of
p
, this implies that
\bar{b}_{i}=0
. This contradicts that
F\notin\ell\Phi_{p,d}
.

It remains to count the number of elements
\vec{\zeta}\in\mu_{p^{n}}^{d}
such that
a_{i}(\vec{\zeta})^{p^{r}}=a_{j}(\vec{\zeta})^{p^{r}}
for some
i\neq j
, where
r
is the integer given by
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}(\mu_{p})\bigcap\mu_{p^{\infty}}=\mu_{p^{r}}
. By Lemma
6.2
, the number of such elements is
O(p^{(d-1)n})
, hence the corollary follows.
∎

Definition 6.4
.

Give a nonzero element
F\in\Phi_{p,d}
and a prime number
\ell
, we define

\displaystyle\mathrm{Supp}_{n}(F)

\displaystyle=\{\omega\in\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\omega(F)\neq 0\},

\displaystyle\ord_{\ell,n}(F)

\displaystyle=\max(\ord_{\ell}(\omega(F))\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\omega\in\mathrm{Supp}_{n}(F)).

Lemma 6.5
.

Given any non-zero
F\in\Phi_{p,d}
,
\ord_{\ell,n}(F)
is bounded independently of
n
.

Proof.

We consider
F
as an element with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}[\mu_{p^{r}}]
, where
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}(\mu_{p})\bigcap\mu_{p^{\infty}}=\mu_{p^{r}}
and prove the statement for all such elements.

Suppose
F=\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}T_{1}^{a_{i,1}}\cdots T_{d}^{a_{i,d}}
. We proceed by induction on
k
. The statement clearly holds when
k=1
. Let
k\geq 2
and assume that the statement holds for all elements in
\Phi_{p,d}
that are defined as a sum of
k-1
terms. As in the proof of Corollary
6.3
, when
a_{i}(\vec{\zeta})^{p^{r}}\neq a_{j}(\vec{\zeta})^{p^{r}}
for all
i\neq j
, we have
\ord_{\ell}(\ev_{\vec{\zeta}}F)=\min(\ord_{\ell}(b_{i})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}1\leq i\leq k)
. When
a_{i}(\vec{\zeta})^{p^{r}}=a_{j}(\vec{\zeta})^{p^{r}}
for some
i\neq j
, we can replace
b_{i}T_{1}^{a_{i,1}}\cdots T_{d}^{a_{i,d}}+b_{j}T_{1}^{a_{j,1}}\cdots T_{d}^{a_{j,d}}
by
(b_{i}+b_{j}a_{j}(\vec{\zeta})a_{i}(\vec{\zeta})^{-1})T_{1}^{a_{i,1}}\cdots T_{d}^{a_{i,d}}
in
F
, resulting in an element in
\Lambda_{p,\ell}
with
k-1
summands. The number of such elements with
k-1
summands is bounded independently of
n
, since there are finitely pairs of possible
(i,j)
and
a_{j}(\vec{\zeta})a_{i}(\vec{\zeta})^{-1}\in\mu_{p^{r}}
.
Each of these elements has bounded
\ord_{\ell,n}
by the inductive hypothesis. Therefore,
\ord_{\ell,n}(F)
is also bounded.
∎

We deduce the following:

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
F=\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}T_{1}^{a_{i,1}}\cdots T_{d}^{a_{i,d}}\in\Phi_{p,d}
be a non-zero element. Then

\sum_{\omega\in\mathrm{Supp}_{n}(F)}\ord_{\ell}(\omega(F))=p^{nd}\mu_{\ell}(F)+O(p^{(d-1)n}),

where
\mu_{\ell}(F)=\min(\ord_{\ell}(b_{i})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}1\leq i\leq k)
.

Proof.

Let
r_{n}=\#\{\omega\in\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\ord_{\ell}(\omega(F))=\mu_{\ell}(F)\}
and
S_{n}=\{\omega\in\mathrm{Supp}_{n}(F)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\ord_{\ell}(\omega(F))>\mu_{\ell}(F)\}
.
Then,

\sum_{\omega\in\mathrm{Supp}_{n}(F)}\ord_{\ell}(\omega(F))=r_{n}\mu_{\ell}(F)+\sum_{\omega\in S_{n}}\ord_{\ell}(\omega(F)).

Corollary
6.3
tells us that
p^{dn}-r_{n}=O(p^{(d-1)n})
and
\#S_{n}=O(p^{(d-1)n})
, and Lemma
6.5
says that the summands in the second sum are
O(1)
. Hence, the corollary follows.
∎

We now give the proof of Theorem
B
.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-tower of strongly connected digraphs. Let
\ell\neq p
be a prime number. Then

\ord_{\ell}(|\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n})_{\mathrm{tor}}|)=p^{nd}\mu_{\ell}(L_{p}(X,\alpha))+O(p^{(d-1)n}).

If
\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha)(\omega)\neq 0
for all but finitely many
\omega\in\Gamma
, then

\ord_{\ell}(|\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{n})_{\textup{tor}}|)=p^{nd}\mu_{\ell}(\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha))+O(p^{(d-1)n}).

Proof.

Let
F=L_{p}(X,\alpha)
. Recall from Theorem
5.7
that
L_{p}(X,\alpha)(\omega)\neq 0
for all non-trivial
\omega
. Combining this with (
3.1
), Theorem
5.4
and Corollary
5.5
, we deduce

\displaystyle\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n})_{\mathrm{tor}}

\displaystyle\cong\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{0})_{\mathrm{tor}}\oplus\bigoplus_{m=1}^{n}\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}[\omega]\in\mathscr{R}\\
\omega(\Gamma)=\mu_{p^{m}}\end{subarray}}e_{[\omega]}\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{\infty}),

\displaystyle\ord_{\ell}(|\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n})_{\mathrm{tor}}|)

\displaystyle=\ord_{\ell}(|\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{0})_{\mathrm{tor}}|)+\sum_{\omega\in\mathrm{Supp}_{n}(F)}\ord_{\ell}(\omega(F)).

Hence, the first formula follows from Corollary
6.6
. The second formula can be proved similarly by considering

\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{n})_{\mathrm{tor}}\cong\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{n_{0}})_{\mathrm{tor}}\oplus\bigoplus_{m=n_{0}+1}^{n}\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}[\omega]\in\mathscr{R}\\
\omega(\Gamma)=\mu_{p^{m}}\end{subarray}}e_{[\omega]}\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{\infty}),

where
n_{0}
is the maximum integer such that there exists a character
\omega
of order
p^{n_{0}}
such that
\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha)(\omega)=0
.
∎

Remark 6.8
.

In light of Remark
5.9
, we can use the theory of Sinnott modules, in particular
[
BL23
, Theorem 4.7]
, to describe
\ord_{\ell}(|\textup{Pic}_{\ell}(X_{n})_{\mathrm{tor}}|)
(or
\ord_{\ell}(|\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{n})_{\textup{tor}}|)
) in terms of a sequence of integers
(t_{m})_{m\geq 0}
. Theorem
6.7
shows that this sequence converges to a constant, i.e.
\mu_{\ell}(L_{p}(X,\alpha))
(or
\mu_{\ell}(\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha))
), as
m\to\infty
.

When
d=1
, the hypothesis
\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha)(\omega)\neq 0
for all but finitely many
\omega\in\Gamma
is equivalent to
\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha)\neq 0
. However, the former condition is in general weaker than the latter when
d>1
.

7.
Defects of digraphs

Recall from Remark
2.6
that the rank of the Picard group of strongly connected digraphs is equal to 1. In particular, it stays bounded along the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-towers we study in this article. We have seen in Example
5.11
that this is not the case for the algebraic Bowen–Franks rank
b(X_{n})
. In this section, we study the growth of
b(X_{n})
in more detail. We will also define and study its analytic counterpart.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
X
be a digraph. We define the
analytic Bowen–Franks rank
of
X
as
a(X)=-\ord_{u=1}(Z_{X}(u))
. The
defect
of
X
is defined as
\delta(X)=a(X)-b(X)
.

The fact that the algebraic multiplicity of an eigenvalue of a square matrix is greater than equal to its geometric multiplicity means that
a(X)\geq b(X)
. In other words,
\delta(X)\geq 0
.
One can regard
\delta(X)
as the failure for a ”BSD-type conjecture” to hold.

If the adjacency matrix
A_{X}
of
X
is diagonalizable (e.g., when
X
is undirected, in which case
A_{X}
is symmetric), then
a(X)=b(X)
and
\delta(X)=0
. For a general digraph, it is possible for
\delta(X)
to be non-zero, as illustrated by the following example.

Example 7.2
.

Let
X
be a digraph of order 3, with the adjacency matrix

A_{X}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&1\\
1&2&1\\
1&1&2\end{pmatrix}.

Lemma
4.7
implies

Z_{X}(u)=\det(\mathrm{Id}-uA_{X})^{-1}.

Note that
1
is an eigenvalue of
A_{X}
with algebraic multiplicity
2
. Thus,
a(X)=2
. The kernel of
\mathrm{Id}-A_{X}
is one-dimensional, which implies that
b(X)=1
. Therefore,
\delta(X)=2-1=1>0
.

Remark 7.3
.

Let
Y/X
be a covering of digraphs. There is a natural surjection
\mathcal{BF}(Y)\to\mathcal{BF}(X)
. In particular,
b(Y)\geq b(X)
. In addition, the Artin formalism (Theorem
4.5
) implies that
a(Y)\geq a(X)
.

Definition 7.4
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-tower of strongly connected digraphs. Let
\omega\in\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}
. We define
a(X_{n},\omega)=-\ord_{u=1}L(X_{n}/X,\omega,u)
. We define
b(X_{n},\omega)
as the
\mathbb{Z}[\omega(\Gamma_{n})]
-rank of the cokernel of
\textup{Id}-\omega(A_{\alpha})
as a linear map on
\mathbb{Z}[\omega(\Gamma_{n})]^{r}\to\mathbb{Z}[\omega(\Gamma_{n})]^{r}
.

Lemma 7.5
.

We have the following identities

\displaystyle a(X_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\omega\in\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}}a(X_{n},\omega),

\displaystyle b(X_{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\omega\in\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}}b(X_{n},\omega).

Proof.

The equation for the analytic ranks follows from the Artin formalism (Theorem
4.5
). It remains to prove the equation for the algebraic ranks.

Let
\ell
be a prime number distinct from
p
. For each
\omega\in\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}
, let
f_{\omega}
denote
[\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}(\omega(\Gamma_{n}))\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}]
. There are
f_{\omega}
characters in the equivalence class
[\omega]
and

b(X_{n},\omega)=b(X_{n},\omega^{\prime})\ \forall\omega^{\prime}\in[\omega],

and

(7.1)

\sum_{\omega^{\prime}\in[\omega]}b(X_{n},\omega^{\prime})=f_{\omega}\cdot b(X_{n},\omega)=\rank_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\omega(\Gamma_{n})]^{r}/(\mathrm{Id}-\omega(A_{\alpha})).

Recall from Lemma
3.2
that

\varphi_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma_{n}]\cong\bigoplus_{[\omega]\in\mathscr{R}_{n}^{(\ell)}}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\omega(\Gamma_{n})].

Further, we have seen in the proof of Theorem
5.4
that there is an isomorphism
\textup{Div}_{\ell}(X_{n})\cong\displaystyle\bigoplus_{v\in V(X)}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma_{n}](v,1)
, which gives

\mathcal{BF}_{\ell}(X_{n})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma_{n}]^{r}/(\textup{Id}-A_{\alpha}).

For each
[\omega]\in\mathscr{R}_{n}^{(\ell)}
, define the map
\psi_{[\omega]}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\omega(\Gamma_{n})]^{r}
given by the matrix
\textup{Id}-\omega(A_{\alpha})
. Let
\psi_{n}=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{[\omega]\in\mathscr{R}_{n}^{(\ell)}}\psi_{[\omega]}
be the corresponding map on
\displaystyle\bigoplus_{m\leq n}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\omega_{m}(\Gamma_{n})]^{r}
. We have the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma_{n}]^{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\Gamma_{n}]^{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\bigoplus_{m\leq n}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\omega_{m}(\Gamma_{n})]\right)^{r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(\bigoplus_{m\leq n}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\omega_{m}(\Gamma_{n})]\right)^{r}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{Id}-A_{\alpha}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{n}}

In particular,

b(X_{n})=\rank_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}(\coker(\psi_{n}))=\sum_{[\omega]\in\mathscr{R}_{n}^{(\ell)}}\rank_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}(\coker(\psi_{[\omega]}))=\sum_{\omega\in\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}}b(X_{n},\omega),

where the last equality follows from (
7.1
).
∎

Lemma
7.5
can be regarded as an analogue of the Artin formalism for the Bowen–Franks ranks
a(X_{n})
and
b(X_{n})
.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
C
.

Theorem 7.6
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
-tower of strongly connected digraphs. For all
n\geq 1
,

\delta(X_{n})\geq\delta(X_{n-1}).

Proof.

Let
\omega\in{\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}}
. Note that
b(X_{n},\omega)
is the geometric multiplicity of the eigenvalue
1
of
\omega(A_{\alpha})
. As
a(X_{n},\omega)
is the algebraic multiplicity of the eigenvalue
1
of
\omega(A_{\alpha})
, we have
a(X_{n},\omega)\geq b(X_{n},\omega)
.
Lemma
7.5
allows us to express
\delta(X_{n})
as

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega\in\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}}a(X_{n},\omega)-\sum_{\omega\in\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}}b(X_{n},\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\omega\in\Gamma_{n-1}^{\vee}}a(X_{n},\omega)-\sum_{\omega\in\Gamma_{n-1}^{\vee}}b(X_{n},\omega)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}\\
\ord(\omega)=p^{n}\end{subarray}}a(X_{n},\omega)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}\\
\ord(\omega)=p^{n}\end{subarray}}b(X_{n},\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\omega\in\Gamma_{n-1}^{\vee}}a(X_{n-1},\omega)-\sum_{\omega\in\Gamma_{n-1}^{\vee}}b(X_{n-1},\omega)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}\\
\ord(\omega)=p^{n}\end{subarray}}a(X_{n},\omega)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}\\
\ord(\omega)=p^{n}\end{subarray}}b(X_{n},\omega)

\displaystyle=\delta(X_{n-1})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}\\
\ord(p^{n})\end{subarray}}\left(a(X_{n},\omega)-b(X_{n},\omega)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\delta(X_{n-1}),

as desired.
∎

7.1.
The special case of
d=1

In this subsection, we study the special case of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers.
We will first prove a sufficient condition for
a(X_{n})
and
b(X_{n})
to be bounded independently of
n
.

Lemma 7.7
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower of strongly connected digraphs. Assume that
\mathcal{L}(X,\alpha)\neq 0
.
Then
a(X_{n})
and
b(X_{n})
are uniformly bounded. In particular,
\delta(X_{n})
is bounded independently.

Proof.

As
a(X_{n})\geq b(X_{n})
, it suffices to prove the boundedness of
a(X_{n})
. By the Artin formalism (Theorem
4.5
), we have

Z_{X_{n}}(u)=\prod_{\omega\in\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}}L(X_{n}/X,\omega,u).

Recall that
L(X_{n}/X,\omega,1)^{-1}=\omega(\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha))
by Theorem
4.9
. Considering
\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha)
as an element of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[[T-1]]
, we can write
\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha)=g(T-1)p^{\mu}u(T-1)
for some integer
\mu\geq 0
, a distinguished polynomial
g(T-1)
and a unit
U(T-1)
. Therefore,
L(X_{n}/X,\omega,1)^{-1}=\omega(g(T-1))p^{\mu}u_{\omega}
for some
p
-adic unit
u_{\omega}
. Thus,
L(X_{n}/X,\omega,u)
has a pole at
u=1
if and only if
\omega(g(T-1))=0
. As
g(T-1)
is a polynomial, this happens only for finitely many characters
\omega
, which implies that
a(X_{n})
is uniformly bounded, as desired.
∎

The following example illustrates that it is necessary to assume the non-vanishing of
\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha)
.

Example 7.8
.

Suppose
p\neq 2
and consider the digraph
X
with the following adjacency matrix

A_{X}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&2\\
2&3&0\\
2&2&3\end{pmatrix}.

Define a voltage assignment
\alpha
on
X
such that

A_{\alpha}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&1+T\\
1+T&2+T&0\\
1+T&1+T&2+T\end{pmatrix}.

We have

\mathcal{L}_{p}(X,\alpha)=\det\begin{pmatrix}0&0&-(1+T)\\
-(1+T)&-(1+T)&0\\
-(1+T)&-(1+T)&-(1+T)\end{pmatrix}=0

The
matrix
\mathrm{Id}-A_{\alpha}
has rank
2
and its characteristic polynomial is given by
\lambda^{2}(\lambda+2+2T)
. For all
\omega\in\Gamma^{\vee}
, we have
\omega(1+T)\neq 0
. Thus,
a(X_{n},\omega)=2
and
b(X_{n},\omega)=1
. It follows from Lemma
7.5
that

a(X_{n})=2p^{n},\quad b(X_{n})=p^{n}.

It follows that
\delta(X_{n})=p^{n}
is an increasing and unbounded sequence.

In the remainder of this section we consider the special case of constant voltage assignments originally studied in
[
LM24
]
.

Lemma 7.9
.

Let
X
be a strongly connected digraph of order
r
. Let
\alpha
be the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-valued voltage assignment that sends all edges to an element
\tau\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
. Let
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower that arises from
\alpha
. If
n
is an integer such that
\varphi(p^{n})>r
where
\varphi
denotes Euler’s totient function, then
\delta(X_{n})=\delta(X_{n-1})
. In particular,
\delta(X_{n})
is uniformly bounded. Furthermore, It is constant for all
n
if
p-1>r
.

Proof.

Let
\omega
be a character of order
p^{n}
. Then
a(X_{n},\omega)>0
if and only if
1
is an eigenvalue of
\omega(\tau)A_{X}=\omega(\tau A_{X})=\omega(A_{\alpha})
. This is only the case if
\omega(\tau)^{-1}
is an eigenvalue of
A_{X}
. As the degree of the characteristic polynomial of
A_{X}
is
r
, we must have
r\geq[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^{n}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}]=\varphi(p^{n})
. Thus, whenever
\varphi(p^{n})>r
, we have
a(X_{n},\omega)=b(X_{n},\omega)=0
. Hence, it follows from Lemma
7.5
that
\delta(X_{n})=\delta(X_{n-1})
.
∎

Example 7.10
.

Let
X
be a directed cycle with
r
vertices. Let
X_{n}
be the
n
-th layer of the constant
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower of
X
. Then
X_{n}
is a directed cycle with
p^{n}r
-vertices. The characteristic polynomial of
A_{X_{n}}
is
X^{p^{n}r}-1
. Thus,
a(X_{n})=b(X_{n})=1
and
\delta(X_{n})=0
.

Our calculations lead us to formulate the following conjecture:

Conjecture 7.11
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a constant
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower of strongly connected digraphs. Then
\delta(X_{n})=\delta(X)
for all
n
.

The following example shows that for non-constant voltage assignments, the defect can grow along
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-towers even if it is uniformly bounded.

Example 7.12
.

Consider a digraph
X
with the following adjacency matrix

A_{X}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0&5\\
1&6&1&0\\
0&6&6&1\\
1&0&1&1\end{pmatrix}.

It can be verified that
X
is strongly connected. Let
\alpha
be a voltage assignment on
X
such that

A_{\alpha}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0&T^{4}+T^{3}+T^{2}+T+1\\
1&T^{4}+T^{3}+T^{2}+T+2&1&0\\
0&T^{4}+T^{3}+T^{2}+T+2&T^{4}+T^{3}+T^{2}+T+2&1\\
1&0&1&1\end{pmatrix}

This produces a strongly connected
\mathbb{Z}_{5}
-tower. We can check that
1
is not an eigenvalue of
A
. In particular,
\delta(X)=0
.

Let
\zeta_{0}
be a primitive fifth root of unity. For
1\leq k\leq 4
. the matrix
A_{\alpha}(\zeta^{k}_{0})
is equal to

\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0&0\\
1&1&1&0\\
0&1&1&1\\
1&0&1&1\par\end{pmatrix}.

Note that
\lambda=1
is an eigenvalue of algebraic multiplicity
2
and geometric multiplicity
1
. Thus, for every character
\omega
of order 5, we have
a(X_{1},\omega)=2
and
b(X_{1},\omega)=1
. Thus,
\delta(X_{1})=4
. In fact, given a complex number
\zeta
, the matrix
A_{\alpha}|_{T=\zeta}
admits 1 as an eigenvalue if and only if
\zeta
is a primitive
5
-th root of unity. Thus,
a(X_{n})=a(X_{1})
for all
n\geq 1
. In particular,
a(X_{n})
is bounded as
n\to\infty
.

7.2.
Application to isogeny graphs

In this subsection, let
p,q
and
\ell
be distinct prime numbers.

Definition 7.13
.

We denote by
\mathcal{X}(q)
the digraph whose vertices are isomorphism classes of supersingular elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{F}_{\ell^{2}}
and whose edges are given by equivalence classes of
q
-isogenies, where two isogenies are deemed equivalent if they differ by an automorphism. Let
\mathcal{X}_{0}
be a fixed connected component of
\mathcal{X}(q)
. Let
\mathcal{X}_{n}
be the
n
-th layer of the constant
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
.

The purpose of this subsection is to prove that Conjecture
7.11
holds for the tower
(\mathcal{X}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
.
We will make use of an auxiliary
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower, which we define below.

Definition 7.14
.

For an integer
n\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{X}(q,n)
be the digraph where the vertices are
(E,\zeta)
, where
E\in V(\mathcal{X}(q))
and
\zeta
is a primitive
p^{n}
-th root of unity and the set of edges from
(E,\zeta)
to
(E^{\prime},\zeta^{\prime})
corresponds to the
q
-isogenies from
E
to
E^{\prime}
when
\zeta^{\prime}=\zeta^{q}
, and there are no edges when
\zeta^{\prime}\neq\zeta^{q}
.

As discussed in
[
LM25
, Example 5.7]
,
\mathcal{X}(q,n)
is the derived digraph of
\mathcal{X}(q)
for the voltage assignment that sends all edges to the image of
q
in
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
.
In particular,
\mathcal{X}(q,n)
is a
\phi(p^{n})
-sheeted cover of
\mathcal{X}(q)
. Given an elliptic curve
E
and a subgroup
H
of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
, a level
H
structure on
E
is an isomorphism of
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})^{2}\to E[p^{n}]
considered up to composition with elements of
H
, as given in
[
CL23
, Definition 1.1]
. Considering the Weil pairing of
\phi(1,0)
with
\phi(0,1)
, we deduce that the choice of a primitive
p^{n}
-th root of unity
\zeta
in
(E,\zeta)
is a
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
level structure on
E
.

There is a natural covering
\mathcal{X}(q,1)\to\mathcal{X}(q)
.
In what follows, we fix a strongly connected component
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
of
\mathcal{X}(q,1)
that lies inside the pre-image of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
. For each
n\geq 0
, let
\mathcal{Y}_{n}
denote a strongly connected component of
\mathcal{X}(q,n+1)
that lies inside the pre-image of
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
under the projection map
\mathcal{X}(q,n+1)\to\mathcal{X}(q,1)
. There exists an index
n_{0}
such that
(\mathcal{Y}_{n})_{n\geq n_{0}}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-tower of strongly connected digraph. To simplify notation, we will assume that
n_{0}=0
. We remark that the proofs we present below will work in the same way for
n_{0}>0
. Note that
(\mathcal{Y}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
arises from the constant voltage assignment that sends all edges of
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
to
q\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, and there is a natural covering
\mathcal{Y}_{n}\to\mathcal{X}_{n}
.

Proposition 7.15
.

For all
n\geq 0
and all non-trivial characters
\omega
in
\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}
, we have
a(\mathcal{Y}_{n},\omega)=0
. In particular,
\delta(\mathcal{Y}_{n})=\delta(\mathcal{Y}_{0})
for all
n
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Lemma
7.9
, in order to show that
\delta(\mathcal{Y}_{n})=\delta(\mathcal{Y}_{0})
for all
n\geq 0
, it suffices to show that the eigenvalues of the adjacency matrix
A_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}
are not primitive
p
-power roots of unity.

Let
k
be the order of
q
in
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}/\det(\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}))=(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, and
k^{\prime}
the smallest positive integer such that

, i.e., the order of
q^{2}
in
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. In particular, both
k
and
k^{\prime}
divide

.
We recall from
[
CL23
, Theorem 1.6]
that if
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of

, then

is of the form

\zeta\in\mu_{p-1}
, or
\lambda
is a complex number with angle in

. Therefore, none of the eigenvalues of

are primitive

-power roots of unity, as desired.
∎

We conclude with the following theorem.

Theorem 7.16
.

Let
p,q
and
\ell
be distinct prime numbers.
Let
\mathcal{X}_{n}
be defined as in Definition
7.13
. Then

\delta(\mathcal{X}_{n})=\delta(\mathcal{X}_{0})

for all
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

Let
\omega\in\Gamma_{n}^{\vee}
be a non-trivial character.
Since there is a covering
\mathcal{Y}_{n}\to\mathcal{X}_{n}
, we have.
a(\mathcal{Y}_{n},\omega)\geq a(\mathcal{X}_{n},\omega)

.
∎

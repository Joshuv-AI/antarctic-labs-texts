A higher index and rapidly decaying kernels
Hao Guo, Peter Hochs, Hang Wang

Abstract
We construct an index of first-order, self-adjoint, elliptic differential operators in the K K -theory of a Fréchet algebra of smooth kernels with faster than exponential off-diagonal decay. We show that this index can be represented by an idempotent involving heat operators. The rapid decay of the kernels in the algebra used is helpful in proving convergence of pairings with cyclic cocycles. Representing the index in terms of heat operators allows one to use heat kernel asymptotics to compute such pairings. We give a link to von Neumann algebras and L 2 L^{2} -index theorems as an immediate application, and work out further applications in other papers.

Introduction

Let
X
be a complete Riemannian manifold, and
E\to X
a Hermitian vector bundle. Let
D
be a first-order, self-adjoint, elliptic differential operator (such as a Dirac operator). The higher index

\ind(D)\in K_{*}(C^{*}(X))

(1.1)

of such operators
D
in terms of the Roe algebra
C^{*}(X)
, including equivariant versions, has been used successfully in many places. (See e.g.
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
15
,
18
,
19
,
22
,
23
]
for a very small selection.) To obtain relevant and computable numbers via pairings with traces and higher cyclic cocycles, however, it is useful to construct an index in the
K
-theory of an algebra of more rapidly decaying Schwartz kernels.
In this paper, we construct such an index.

This index is used in
[
9
]
to obtain a
K
-theoretic index to which the traces that appear in the Selberg trace formula for cofinite-volume lattices in real semisimple Lie groups of real rank one
[
16
,
21
]
can be applied, refining the index theorem in
[
2
]
. In ongoing work, we compute further pairings of the index in this paper with cyclic cocycles, using the rapid decay of the kernels in the algebra we use here to ensure convergence of these pairings.

We fix an algebra
S
of differential operators on the exterior tensor product
E\boxtimes E^{*}\to X\times X
with suitable properties, see page
2.1
for details. We then consider a Fréchet algebra
\mathcal{A}_{S}(E)
of smooth sections of
E\boxtimes E^{*}
that have faster off-diagonal decay than any exponential function of the Riemannian distance, and whose derivatives with respect to operators in
S
also have this decay behaviour. In fact, we use a slight generalisation of this algebra, involving a proper map
\varphi
from another manifold
Y
to
X
. This is to obtain a link with the Casselman Schwartz algebra in
[
9
]
, where
X=G/K
, for
G
a connected, real semisimple Lie group and
K<G
maximal compact,
Y=G
and
\varphi
is the quotient map. We will ignore the map
\varphi
for now, see Definition
2.8
for details.

The main technical result in this paper, see Theorem
2.12
, is that under certain assumptions on
D
, for all
t>0
, the operator

R_{t}:=\frac{1-e^{-tD^{2}}}{D^{2}}D

is a multiplier of
\mathcal{A}_{S}(E)
, if a group
H
acts on
X
and
E
, preserving all structure, and such that
X/H
is compact. The operator
D
is a multiplier of
\mathcal{A}_{S}(E)
for more basic reasons, and the heat operator

e^{-tD^{2}}=1-R_{t}D

lies in
\mathcal{A}_{S}(E)
itself. It follows that, if
D
is odd with respect to a grading on
E
, we have a class

[D]\in K_{1}(\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{S}(E))/\mathcal{A}_{S}(E)),

(1.2)

where
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{S}(E))
is the multiplier algebra of
\mathcal{A}_{S}(E)
. Applying the boundary map
\partial
in the long exact sequence associated to the ideal
\mathcal{A}_{S}(E)\subset\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}_{S}(E))
to (

), we obtain a higher index

\ind_{S}(D):=\partial[D]\in K_{0}(\mathcal{A}_{S}(E)).

(1.3)

Furthermore, this index
can be represented by a standard idempotent involving heat operators, so that one can use heat kernel asymptotics to compute its pairings with traces and other cyclic cocycles. We show that
\mathcal{A}_{S}(E)
embeds into
C^{*}(M)
, and that the map induced by this inclusion maps
(
1.3
) to (
1.1
).

We similarly obtain an equivariant index

\ind_{S,H}(D):=\partial[D]\in K_{0}(\mathcal{A}_{S}(E)^{H}),

(1.4)

where the superscript
H
denotes the subalgebra of
H
-invariant kernels.

The assumptions made hold in the important special case of Dirac operators on the space
G/K
as above, where the operators in
S
are given by the left and right action of the universal enveloping algebra of the Lie algebra of
G
.

Under milder assumptions, we obtain the weaker result (Proposition
2.17
) that the index (
1.1
) can be represented by an idempotent in (the unitisation of)
\mathcal{A}_{S}(E)
. This allows one to pair (
1.1
) with cyclic cocycles on
\mathcal{A}_{S}(E)
to some extent, but if the map

K_{0}(\mathcal{A}_{S}(E))\to K_{0}(C^{*}(M))

(1.5)

induced by the inclusion is not injective, then
it is not clear that such a pairing is well-defined. A related point is that the index (
1.3
) may contain more information than (
1.1
).
Bradd
[
3
]
has shown that a
G
-equivariant version of (
1.5
) is bijective for

, but in general it is not clear if (
1.5
) is injective. Then the index (
1.3
) defined directly in terms of the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{S}(E)
is more useful for pairings with traces and cyclic cocycles.

One immediate application is a link between the equivariant index (
1.4
), with
H
replaced by a cofinite-volume, discrete subgroup
\Gamma<H
, to von Neumann algebras and
L^{2}
-index theorems. See Section
4

A higher index

2.1
An algebra of exponentially decaying kernels

Consider a Riemannian manifold
X
, with Riemannian distance
d
and Riemannian density
\mu
.

Definition 2.1
.

The manifold
X
has
uniformly exponential growth
if there are
a_{0},C>0
such that for all
x\in X
,

\int_{X}e^{-{a_{0}}d(x,x^{\prime})}\,d\mu(x^{\prime})\leq C.

If
x\in X
and
r>0
, then we write
B_{r}(x)
for the open Riemannian ball around
x
of radius
r
.

Example 2.2
.

Suppose that a group
H
acts isometrically on
X
, such that
X/H
is compact. Let
x_{0}\in X
and
a_{0}>0
, and suppose that

\int_{X}e^{-a_{0}d(x_{0},x^{\prime})}\,d\mu(x^{\prime})

converges. Let
r>0
be such that
H\cdot B_{r}(x_{0},r)=X
. Then for all
x\in X
, write
x=hx_{1}
, for
h\in H
and
x_{1}\in B_{r}(x_{0})
. Then by
H
-invariance of
d
and
\mu
and the triangle inequality,

\int_{X}e^{-{a_{0}}d(x,x^{\prime})}\,d\mu(x^{\prime})=\int_{X}e^{-{a_{0}}d(x_{1},x^{\prime})}\,d\mu(x^{\prime})\leq e^{a_{0}r}\int_{X}e^{-{a_{0}}d(x_{0},x^{\prime})}\,d\mu(x^{\prime}).

So
X
has uniformly exponential growth. This example includes the case where
X=G/K
, for a connected Lie group
G
, compact subgroup
K<G
, and a
G
-invariant Riemannian metric on
X
.

We suppose from now on that
X
has uniformly exponential growth. We consider a Hermitian vector bundle
E\to X
. A section
\kappa
of the exterior tensor product
E\boxtimes E^{*}\to X\times X
has
finite propagation
if there is an
r>0
such that for all
x,x^{\prime}\in X
with
d(x,x^{\prime})>r
, we have
\kappa(x,x^{\prime})=0
. If two
L^{\infty}
-sections
\kappa,\lambda
of
E\boxtimes E^{*}
have finite propagation, then their composition
\kappa\lambda
is well-defined as an
L^{\infty}
-section of
E\boxtimes E^{*}
with finite propagation, by

(\kappa\lambda)(x,x^{\prime})=\int_{X}\kappa(x,x^{\prime\prime})\lambda(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime})\,d\mu(x^{\prime\prime}),

(2.1)

for all
x,x^{\prime}\in X
.

Fix an algebra
S
of differential operators on
E\boxtimes E^{*}
such that

1.

S
contains
the identity operator;

2.

for all
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, the space of order
l
differential operators in
S
is a finite-dimensional linear subspace of
S
;

3.

for all
P\in S
, there are differential operators
Q
and
R
on
E
such that
Q\otimes 1
and
1\otimes R^{*}
lie in
S
and
P=Q\otimes R^{*}
. Here the differential operator
R^{*}
on
E^{*}
is defined by the property that for all
\sigma\in\Gamma^{\infty}(E^{*})
and
s\in\Gamma^{\infty}_{c}(E)
,

\int_{X}\langle(R^{*}\sigma)(x),s(x)\rangle\,d\mu(x)=\int_{X}\langle\sigma(x),(Rs)(x)\rangle\,d\mu(x).

The third point implies that for all
\kappa\in\Gamma^{\infty}(E\boxtimes E^{*})
with finite propagation, the section
P(\kappa)
is the Schwartz kernel of the composition
Q\circ\kappa\circ R
. Here, and in the rest of this paper, we identify
\kappa
with the operator it defines.

Example 2.3
.

Let
G
be a Lie group, and let
V
be a finite-dimensional, complex vector space. Consider the trivial vector bundle
E=G\times V\to G
.
Let

be the right regular representation of the universal enveloping algebra

on

R(P_{1})\otimes R(P_{2})
on
\Gamma^{\infty}(E\boxtimes E^{*})=C^{\infty}(G\times G)\otimes\End(V)
be defined by the action by
R(P_{1})
on the first copy of
G
, and the action by
R(P_{2})
on the second copy of
G
.
Let

S:=\{R(P_{1})\otimes R(P_{2});P_{1},P_{2}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\}.

Then

satisfies the three conditions listed above. For the third, if

Let
\Gamma_{S,\fp}^{\infty}(E\boxtimes E^{*})

For

for
\kappa\in\Gamma_{S,\fp}^{\infty}(E\boxtimes E^{*})

We denote the completion of

in the seminorms

\mathcal{A}_{S}(E)

Lemma 2.5

The space

is a Fréchet algebra under the composition (

).

Lemma

is proved in Subsection

.

Lemma 2.7

The algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C}}(E)^{H}

We will use slight generalisations of the algebras
\mathcal{A}_{S}(E)
and
\mathcal{A}_{S}(E)^{H}
. Let

2.2
Construction of the index

Let
D
be an
H
-equivariant, first-order, elliptic, self-adjoint differential operator on
E
. We make the following three assumptions.

1.

There are
Q,R\in S
such that
\varphi^{*}\circ(D^{2}\otimes 1)=Q\circ\varphi^{*}
and
\varphi^{*}\circ(1\otimes(D^{2})^{*})=R\circ\varphi^{*}
.

2.

For operators

with well-defined, bounded commutators with

, we write
\ad(D^{2})(T)=[D^{2},T]
. We assume that for all

and

is a well-defined, bounded operator on

. Furthermore, if

\tilde{f}(z,\relbar)\in C^{\infty}_{c}(X)
for all

, the operator norm of

3.

For all

, the compositions
P\circ\varphi^{*}\circ(D\otimes 1)
and

are finite sums of compositions of the form

, for

of
\varphi^{*}E\boxtimes\varphi^{*}E^{*}
and

.

\|f\|_{a,k}:=\|\hat{f}\|_{a,k}

(3.10)

for
f\in B(\mathbb{R})
,
a>0
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

The space
B(\mathbb{R})
is a Fréchet algebra with respect to pointwise multiplication.

Proof.

We show that
\hat{B}(\mathbb{R})
is a Fréchet algebra with respect to convolution, which implies the claim.
The topology on
\hat{B}(\mathbb{R})
is given by the countable subset of seminorms
\|\cdot\|_{a,k}
for
a,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, so
\hat{B}(\mathbb{R})
is a Fréchet space.
Let
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in\hat{B}(\mathbb{R})
,
a>0
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Then

\begin{split}\|\psi_{1}*\psi_{2}\|_{a,k}&=\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}e^{a|\xi|}\bigl|(\psi_{1}^{(k)}*\psi_{2})(\xi)\bigr|\\
&\leq\|\psi_{1}\|_{2a,k}\|\psi_{2}\|_{a,0}\sup_{\xi}e^{a|\xi|}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-2a|\eta|}e^{-a|\xi-\eta|}\,d\eta\\
&\leq\|\psi_{1}\|_{2a,k}\|\psi_{2}\|_{a,0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-a|\eta|}\,d\eta,\end{split}

because
|\xi|-|\eta|\leq|\xi-\eta|
for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}
. So convolution is continuous.
∎

The section

Proof.

The proof is a modification of the proof of Lemma 3.16 in
[
5
]
.

The function
\hat{f}^{(n)}
is a Schwartz function, hence so is the function

\begin{split}\|(D^{2(k-r)}\circ\kappa\circ D^{2(l-s)})(x,x^{\prime})\|&=\|\varphi^{*}(D^{2(k-r)}\circ\kappa\circ D^{2(l-s)})(\tilde{y},\tilde{y}^{\prime})\|\\
&=\|((Q^{k-r}\otimes R^{l-s})\varphi^{*}\kappa)(\tilde{y},\tilde{y}^{\prime})\|\\
&\leq\|\kappa\|_{2a_{0}A+a,Q^{k-r}\otimes R^{l-s}}e^{-(2a_{0}A+a)d_{Y}(\tilde{y},\tilde{y}^{\prime})}.\end{split}

(3.27)

If we choose
\tilde{y}
and
\tilde{y}^{\prime}
as above for all
x,x^{\prime}\in X
, and use (
2.5
) and the estimate (
3.27
), then we find
that the right hand side of (
3.26
) is less than or equal to a constant times

\|\kappa\|_{2a_{0}A+a,Q^{k-r}\otimes R^{l-s}}\sup_{d_{X}(x,\varphi(y)),d_{X}(x^{\prime},\varphi(y^{\prime}))\leq 3}e^{-ad_{Y}(\tilde{y},\tilde{y}^{\prime})}.

(3.28)

Finally, note that if
d_{X}(x,\varphi(y))\leq 3
, then (
2.5
) implies that

d_{Y}(\tilde{y},y)\leq A(B+3)
, and similarly for
x^{\prime}
,
y^{\prime}
and
\tilde{y}^{\prime}
. So

d_{Y}(\tilde{y},\tilde{y}^{\prime})\geq d_{Y}(y,y^{\prime})-2A(B+3).

So (
3.28
) is bounded by a constant times

\|\kappa\|_{2a_{0}A+a,Q^{k-r}\otimes R^{l-s}}e^{-ad_{Y}(y,y^{\prime})}.

(3.29)

We find that (
3.23
) is bounded by a finite sum of constants times expressions like (
3.29
), which
are of the form on the right hand side of (
3.21
). So the claim follows.
∎

4
Von Neumann algebras

We consider the setting of Subsection
2.2
, and relate the index from Definition
2.14
to an index defined in terms of group von Neumann algebras in the case of cofinite-volume actions.

Let
\Gamma<H
be a discrete subgroup.
Let
F\subset X
be a fundamental domain for the action by
\Gamma
, such that the closure of
F
equals the closure of its interior. Consider the unitary isomorphism

U\colon L^{2}(E)\to l^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes L^{2}(E|_{F})

given by

(Us)(\gamma,x)=\gamma s(\gamma^{-1}x),

for
s\in L^{2}(E)
,
\gamma\in\Gamma
and
x\in F
.

Let
L
be the left regular representation of
\Gamma
in
l^{2}(\Gamma)
.
If
\kappa\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C}}(E)^{\Gamma}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
, then we write
\gamma\circ\kappa=(\gamma,e)\cdot\kappa\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C}}(E)
in terms of the action (
2.3
), so that

(\gamma\circ\kappa)(x,x^{\prime})=\gamma\kappa(\gamma^{-1}x,x^{\prime}),

(4.1)

for
x,x^{\prime}\in X
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Consider a
\Gamma
-invariant, measurable section
\kappa
of
E\boxtimes E^{*}
that is the Schwartz kernel of a bounded operator on
L^{2}(E)
, which we also denote by
\kappa
. Then
for all
s,s^{\prime}\in L^{2}(E)
,

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\bigl(s,U^{-1}\circ(L_{\gamma}\otimes(\gamma\circ\kappa)|_{F\times F})\circ Us^{\prime}\bigr)_{L^{2}(E)}=(s,\kappa s^{\prime})_{L^{2}(E)}.

(4.2)

Proof.

For
\psi\in l^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes L^{2}(E|_{F})
,
\gamma\in\Gamma
and
x\in F
,

(U^{-1}\psi)(\gamma x)=\gamma\psi(\gamma^{-1},x).

Using this, and writing out definitions, we find that for all
s^{\prime}\in L^{2}(E)
,
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma
and
x\in F
,

\bigl(U^{-1}\circ(L_{\gamma}\otimes(\gamma\circ\kappa)|_{F\times F})\circ Us^{\prime}\bigr)(\gamma^{\prime}x)=\int_{F}\gamma^{\prime}\gamma\kappa(\gamma^{-1}x,y)\gamma^{-1}\gamma^{\prime-1}s^{\prime}(\gamma^{\prime}\gamma y)\,d\mu(y).

By
\Gamma
-invariance of
\kappa
, the right hand side equals

\int_{F}\kappa(\gamma^{\prime}x,\gamma^{\prime}\gamma y)s^{\prime}(\gamma^{\prime}\gamma y)\,d\mu(y).

So the left hand side of (
4.2
) equals

\sum_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma}\int_{F}\int_{F}\bigl(s(\gamma^{\prime}x),\kappa(\gamma^{\prime}x,\gamma^{\prime}\gamma y)s^{\prime}(\gamma^{\prime}\gamma y)\bigr)_{E}\,d\mu(y)\,d\mu(x),

which equals the right hand side of (
4.2
).
∎

Let
A(E)^{\Gamma}
be the Banach space of bounded,
\Gamma
-equivariant operators on
L^{2}(E)
with bounded, measurable Schwartz kernels. For
T\in A(E)^{\Gamma}
, with Schwartz kernel
\kappa
, define

\|T\|_{A(E)^{\Gamma}}:=\|T\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(E))}+\|\kappa\|_{L^{\infty}(E\boxtimes E^{*})}.

Note that
A(E)^{\Gamma}
is not a Banach algebra. But by Lemma
3.1
, the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C}}(E)^{\Gamma}
is continuously included in
A(E)^{\Gamma}
.

Conjugation by
U
is an isometric algebra isomorphism from
\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(E))
onto
\mathcal{B}(l^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes L^{2}(E|_{F}))
. It restricts to an injective, continuous linear map

j_{\mathcal{N}}\colon A(E)^{\Gamma}\to\mathcal{B}(l^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes L^{2}(E|_{F})).

This map is an algebra homomorphism on any algebra included in
A(E)^{\Gamma}
, such as
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C}}(E)^{\Gamma}
.

If
\mathcal{H}
is any Hilbert space, and
A\subset\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
a
C^{*}
-algebra, then we write
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)\otimes A
for the algebra of
T\in\mathcal{B}(l^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes\mathcal{H})
for which there is a sequence
(T_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
in the algebraic tensor product
\mathbb{C}\Gamma\otimes A
such that for all
\psi,\psi^{\prime}\in l^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes\mathcal{H}
,

(\psi,T_{j}\psi^{\prime})_{l^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes\mathcal{H}}\to(\psi,T\psi^{\prime})_{l^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes\mathcal{H}}.

(4.3)

Here we let
\mathbb{C}\Gamma
act on
l^{2}(\Gamma)
via the left regular representation.

Example 4.2
.

If
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}=A
, then
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)\otimes\mathbb{C}
is the completion of
\mathbb{C}\Gamma
in the weak topology on operators on
l^{2}(\Gamma)
, i.e. the von Neumann algebra of
\Gamma
.

Proposition 4.3
.

If
X/\Gamma
has finite volume, then

j_{\mathcal{N}}(A(E)^{\Gamma})\subset\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)\otimes\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(E|_{F})).

Furthermore, the map

j_{\mathcal{N}}\colon A(E)^{\Gamma}\to\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)\otimes\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(E|_{F}))
is continuous.

Proof.

Let
\kappa\in A(E)^{\Gamma}
. Then
j_{\mathcal{N}}(\kappa)\in\mathcal{B}(l^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes L^{2}(E|_{F}))
, and

\|j_{\mathcal{N}}(\kappa)\|_{\mathcal{B}(l^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes L^{2}(E|_{F}))}=\|\kappa\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(E))}.

(4.4)

Now for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
, the operator
(\gamma\circ\kappa)|_{F\times F}
on
L^{2}(E|_{F})
is Hilbert–Schmidt, hence compact, because
F
has finite volume and
\kappa
is bounded. So

L_{\gamma}\otimes(\gamma\circ\kappa)|_{F\times F}\in\mathbb{C}\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(E|_{F})).

By Lemma
4.1
and unitarity of
U
, the sum

\sum_{\gamma}L_{\gamma}\otimes(\gamma\circ\kappa)|_{F\times F}

converges to
j_{\mathcal{N}}(\kappa)
in the topology defined by (
4.3
). So
j_{\mathcal{N}}(\kappa)\in\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)\otimes\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(E|_{F}))
.

Continuity of
j_{\mathcal{N}}
follows from (
4.4
): if
\psi,\psi^{\prime}\in l^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes L^{2}(E|_{F})
, then

|(\psi,j_{\mathcal{N}}(\kappa)\psi^{\prime})_{l^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes L^{2}(E|_{F})}|\leq\|\kappa\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(E))}\|\psi\|_{l^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes L^{2}(E|_{F})}\|\psi^{\prime}\|_{l^{2}(\Gamma)\otimes L^{2}(E|_{F})}.

∎

Suppose that
X/\Gamma
has finite volume. Then
by Lemma
3.1
and Proposition
4.3
, we obtain a continuous algebra homomorphism
j_{\mathcal{N}}\colon\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C}}(E)^{\Gamma}\to\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)\otimes\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(E|_{F}))
. This induces

(j_{\mathcal{N}})_{*}\colon K_{0}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C}}(E)^{\Gamma})\to K_{0}(\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)\otimes\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(E|_{F})))=K_{0}(\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)).

We obtain a link with the index studied in
[
1
,
6
,
20
]
.
We have

(j_{\mathcal{N}})_{*}(\ind_{\mathbb{C},\Gamma}(D))\in K_{0}(\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)).

On the algebra
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)
, we have the von Neumann trace
\tau_{e}^{\Gamma}
, given by evaluation at
e\in\Gamma
. We will extend this to a trace on a subalgebra of

\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)\otimes\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(E|_{F}))
, and apply it to

j_{\mathcal{N}}(\ind_{\mathbb{C},\Gamma}(D))
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For all
\kappa\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C}}(E)^{\Gamma}
, the integral

\Tr_{e}(\kappa):=\int_{F}\tr(\kappa(x,x))\,d\mu(x)

converges. This defines a continuous trace
\Tr_{e}
on
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C}}(E)^{\Gamma}
.

Proof.

This follows from boundedness of kernels in
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C}}(E)^{\Gamma}
and finite volume of
F
.
∎

For a bounded, continuous
\kappa_{F}\in\Gamma(E|_{F}\boxtimes E^{*}|_{F})
, we write

\Tr_{F}(\kappa_{F})=\int_{F}\tr(\kappa_{F}(y,y))\,d\mu(y).

It is immediate from the definitions that for all
\kappa\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{C}}(E)^{\Gamma}
,

(\tau_{e}^{\Gamma}\otimes\Tr_{F})(j_{\mathcal{N}}(\kappa))=\Tr_{e}(\kappa).

(4.5)

In particular, Lemma
4.4

\tau_{e}^{\Gamma}\otimes\Tr_{F}
defines a trace on
\im(j_{\mathcal{N}})\subset\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)\otimes\mathcal{K}(L^{2}(E|_{F}))
, which is continuous in the topology on
\im(j_{\mathcal{N}})
that makes
j_{\mathcal{N}}
a homeomorphism.
Hence we can apply
\tau_{e}^{\Gamma}\otimes\Tr_{F}
to

(j_{\mathcal{N}})_{*}(\ind_{\mathbb{C},\Gamma}(D))\in K_{0}(\im(j_{\mathcal{N}})).

(4.6)

If
D
is equivariant with respect to
H
, rather than just
\Gamma

\Tr_{e}(\ind_{\mathbb{C},\Gamma}(D))

equals
\vol(\Gamma\backslash H)
times the
L^{2}
-index of

, with respect to the action by
H

[

]
for homogeneous spaces, and in
[
20
]
in general. We state a consequence for the index (

\Spin^{c}
-Dirac operator. A generalisation can be deduced analogously from Theorem 6.12 in

Suppose that

(\tau^{\Gamma}_{e}\otimes\Tr_{F})\bigl(j_{\mathcal{N}}(\ind_{\mathbb{C},\Gamma}(D))\bigr)=\vol(\Gamma\backslash H)\int_{F}\hat{A}(X)e^{c_{1}(L_{\det})/2}\ch(E).

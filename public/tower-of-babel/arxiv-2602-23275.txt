Cusped spaces for hierarchically hyperbolic groups, and applications to Dehn filling quotients
Giorgio Mangioni, Alessandro Sisto

Abstract
. We introduce a construction that simultaneously yields cusped spaces of relatively hyperbolic groups, and spaces quasi-isometric to Teichmmüller metrics. We use this to study Dehn-filling-like quotients of various groups, among which mapping class groups ℳ ​ 𝒞 ​ 𝒢 ​ ( S n ) \mathcal{MCG}(S_{n}) of n n -punctured spheres. In particular, we show that ℳ ​ 𝒞 ​ 𝒢 ​ ( S 5 ) \mathcal{MCG}(S_{5}) (resp. the braid group on four strands) has infinite hyperbolic quotients (strongly) not isomorphic to hyperbolic quotients of any other given ℳ ​ 𝒞 ​ 𝒢 ​ ( S n ) \mathcal{MCG}(S_{n}) (resp. any other braid group). These quotients are obtained from ℳ ​ 𝒞 ​ 𝒢 ​ ( S 5 ) \mathcal{MCG}(S_{5}) by modding out suitable large powers of Dehn twists, and we further argue that the corresponding quotients of the extended mapping class group have trivial outer automorphism groups. We obtain these results by studying torsion elements in the relevant quotients.



Introduction

Cusped spaces, as introduced in
[
22
,
9
]
, are fundamental tools in the study of relatively hyperbolic groups, with countless applications. These have common feature with Teichmüller space, regarded here as a coarse geometric object when endowed with the Teichmüller metric, in that its thin part, where the mapping class group action is not cocompact, can be described in terms of horoballs. We make this precise by introducing a construction that simultaneously yields cusped spaces and Teichmüller spaces, using the framework of relatively hierarchically hyperbolic spaces (HHSs) from
[
4
,
7
]
. The starting point of this construction is a relatively hierarchically hyperbolic group (HHG), for instance a relatively hyperbolic group or a mapping class group, satisfying a technical condition that we defer to
2.3
. For the reader familiar with hierarchical hyperbolicity, the construction consists, roughly, in replacing the hyperbolic spaces at the bottom of the hierarchy with combinatorial horoballs; this is described in more detail below, and implemented in
2.9
. With this in mind, the first properties of our construction are:

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a relative HHG satisfying
2.3
, and let
\mathcal{Z}
be its cusped space as in
2.9
. Then:

(1)

\mathcal{Z}
is a hierarchically hyperbolic space;

(2)

G
acts on
\mathcal{Z}
and the orbit maps are coarse embeddings;

(3)

if
G
is relatively hyperbolic, then
\mathcal{Z}
is quasi-isometric to the cusped space of
G
;

(4)

if
G
is a mapping class group, then
\mathcal{Z}
is quasi-isometric to the corresponding Teichmüller space with the Teichmüller metric.

We expect our cusped spaces to serve as a general-purpose tool in the future. For instance, it might be possible to use them to prove the Farrell-Jones Conjecture for a large class of relative HHGs combining techniques from
[
18
]
and
[
2
]
. In this paper we focus on applications to the study of “Dehn filling like” quotients, of the type considered in
[
16
,
6
,
32
]
. In particular, we prove the following, where
S_{n}
denotes the
n
-punctured sphere and
B_{n}
is the braid group on
n
strands.

Theorem B
.

(1)

Let
n\geq 5
be an integer. Then there exists a non-elementary hyperbolic quotient
Q
of
B_{4}
such that any homomorphism
\phi\colon B_{n}\to Q
has virtually cyclic image.

(2)

Let
n\geq 6
be an integer. Then there exists a non-elementary hyperbolic quotient
Q
of
\mathcal{MCG}(S_{5})
such that any homomorphism
\phi\colon\mathcal{MCG}(S_{n})\to Q
has finite image.

B
gives a strong way to distinguish
B_{4}
(resp.
\mathcal{MCG}(S_{5})
) among all other braid groups (resp. mapping class groups of spheres) using infinite hyperbolic quotients; this is in analogy with profinite rigidity results, where finite quotients are used instead. We emphasise that

is extremely delicate: for instance, a
finite-index subgroup
of
B_{n}
for

surjects onto
B_{4}
(by forgetting strands), whence onto any quotient of it. This subtlety is reflected in the proof of Theorem

.

The quotients

are in fact all virtually of the form

, where

is the normal subgroup generated by all

, these were shown to be hyperbolic in
[

]
. Understanding torsion in these groups is the key to proving the theorem above, and we can also exploit this knowledge to control their automorphism groups.

Theorem C
.

There exists
N_{0}>0
such that, for all multiples
N
of
N_{0}
, the group
G_{N}=\mathcal{MCG}^{\pm}(S_{5})/DT_{N}
satisfies
\operatorname{Aut}\left(G_{N}\right)\cong G_{N}
.

In
[
33
]
, we proved an analogue of

for
\mathcal{MCG}^{\pm}(S_{n})/DT_{N}
where

, but the proof there is completely different in nature (it first establishes quasi-isometric rigidity, relying on a sufficiently rich pattern of quasiflats and results from
[
5
]
, and then uses that automorphisms give quasi-isometries). Though the remaining case

cannot be tackled with the tools from
[
33
]
, one can likely use methods similar to the ones we use here to settle it, provided that a certain technical improvement on
[
6
]
can be achieved. We believe it can, and we explain this further in

.

We also point out that, by combining existing results in the literature and using completely different tools, one could already deduce that the outer automorphism group of

is
finite
(see
5.23
). Roughly, property (FA) for
\mathcal{MCG}^{\pm}(S_{5})
implies that the

are rigid in the sense of JSJ theory, but this type of argument cannot prove that outer automorphism groups are trivial rather than finite.

In the case of relatively hyperbolic groups, sequences of peripheral quotients can be studied via the corresponding quotients of cusped spaces, which turn out to be uniformly hyperbolic; this is exploited for instance in
[
15
]
. Inspired by this, we consider sequences of Dehn filling quotients of

, a particularly simple class of HHG introduced in
[

. Notably,
\mathcal{MCG}^{\pm}(S_{5})
is a short HHG, and the quotients

from

Theorem D
.

Let
G
be a short HHG, with cusped space
\mathcal{Z}
. There exists
N_{0}>0
, such that the spaces
\mathcal{Z}/DT_{N}
for all multiples
N

are uniformly HHS.

It was proven in
[
23

, using injective spaces, that all HHGs have bounded torsion. The proofs in fact give a bound depending on the underlying HHS data, so if a family of groups acts uniformly properly on a uniform family of HHS (such as the collection

only for simplicity even though an analogue holds for all short HHGs.

Corollary E
.

There exists
N_{0}>0
such that, for all multiples

of
N_{0}
, any finite-order element of
\mathcal{MCG}(S_{5})/DT_{N}
is the image of an element of
\mathcal{MCG}(S_{5})
which is either finite-order, stabilises a curve, or swaps two disjoint curves.

We expect that a similar result can be obtained for all quotients of mapping class groups by suitable powers of Dehn twists, as well as the further quotients constructed in
[
6

. We explain the currently missing technical statement in

Outline and strategies

In
Section

1
we recall necessary background, in particular about the combinatorial approach to hierarchical hyperbolicity from
[
6
]
. Very roughly, one can extract a HHS structure from hyperbolic simplicial complexes where all links are also hyperbolic. Inspired by

is then replaced by the cone over a certain graph

, thus ensuring that

appears as a link in the blown-up graph. This is a fairly broad setting, as a lot of naturally occurring HHSs admit a combinatorial structure of this type by results from
[
25
]
.

In
Section

2
we describe the (relative) combinatorial HHSs that we will consider, which we call (relative) squid HHS and as mentioned above are blow-ups. See Figure
3
to visualise where this notion fits among other version of hierarchical hyperbolicity.
Section

2
also contains the construction of cusped spaces, which is done by replacing each

with the combinatorial horoball over it.

summarises the main properties of this construction, and in particular proves the first two items of

. It is possible to extend the construction beyond (relative) squid HHGs, but this requires different techniques and will be done in
[
26
]
.

In
Section

3
we verify that our construction indeed applies to many (relative) HHSs, and that it yields cusped spaces of relatively hyperbolic groups and Teichmüller space (the last two items of

). There we also prove
3.4
, which is of independent interest and could prove useful elsewhere as well. Roughly, the Proposition says that, when starting from a HHS structure and passing to a combinatorial version of it, as in
[
25
]
, the associated projections between domains remain coarsely the same.

In
Section

4
we establish

, which allows us to control torsion elements in quotients of (relative) HHGs with controlled geometry. As mentioned above, this uses arguments from
[
23
]
, applied to the action on our cusped spaces.

We leverage our control on torsion in
Section

5
, where we study automorphisms of quotients of short HHGs. Towards this, we firstly show

(=
5.10
); the latter, combined with

, immediately yields

, which is a more general version of

about finite subgroups in quotients.

Having established this, the goal of

is to extract a simplicial automorphism of a certain graph from a group automorphism; for

, this graph is the quotient of the curve graph by
DT_{N}
. To this extent, the strategy (for
\mathcal{MCG}(S_{5})
) is roughly to characterise images of Dehn twists in
\mathcal{MCG}(S_{5})/DT_{N}
as those elements that have a sufficiently large finite order, and a sufficiently large centraliser (we need the latter to rule out images of multi-twists).

In turn, every automorphism of the quotient of the curve graph is induced by a mapping class, by an analogue of Ivanov’s theorem we proved in
[
33
]
, so

(=
5.22
) follows from

In
Section

6
we prove

and both parts of

as corollaries, again exploiting knowledge about torsion, and in particular using that torsion elements have “very large” normal closure in mapping class groups of spheres. This should be compared to several results in the literature where torsion is used to constrain homomorphisms between mapping class groups
[
27
,
12
]
.

Remark F
.

Let
S
be any finite-type surface, and let
\mathcal{MCG}(S)
be its mapping class group. It is shown in
[
6
]
that

, for

: this is because the underlying graph

, which is the corresponding quotient of the curve complex, is not a blowup. Nonetheless, one can blow up each vertex of

1.
Background

In this Section, we gather all definitions and tools from the world of (combinatorial hierarchical) hyperbolicity that we shall need. We first recall here a construction of Groves-Manning
[
22
]
, see also
[
9
]
, inspired by horoballs in real hyperbolic spaces.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a simplicial graph. The
combinatorial horoball

\operatorname{Hor}\left(\Gamma\right)
with base
\Gamma
is the simplicial graph whose vertex set is
\Gamma^{(0)}\times\mathbb{N}
, and with the following two types of edges:

•

For every
p\in\Gamma^{(0)}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
(p,n)
is adjacent to
(p,n+1)
;

•

For every
p,q\in\Gamma^{(0)}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, if
\mathrm{d}_{\Gamma}(p,q)\leq 2^{n}
then
(p,n)
is adjacent to
(q,n)
.

We often denote
(p,n)\in\Gamma^{(0)}\times\mathbb{N}
simply by
p^{n}
.

The base
\Gamma
is naturally contained in the corresponding combinatorial horoball, but it is not quasi-isometrically embedded into it. Rather, it is coarsely embedded, in the sense of the following definition:

Definition 1.2

(Coarse embedding)
.

Given two metric spaces
(X,\mathrm{d}_{X})
and
(Y,\mathrm{d}_{Y})
and positive real functions
m,M\coloneq\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that
m(t)\xrightarrow[t\to+\infty]{}+\infty
, a map
f\colon X\to Y
is a
coarse embedding
if for every
x,x^{\prime}\in X
,

m\left(\mathrm{d}_{X}(x,x^{\prime})\right)\leq\mathrm{d}_{Y}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))\leq M\left(\mathrm{d}_{X}(x,x^{\prime})\right).

M
and
m
are called the
coarse embedding functions
of
f
.

In real hyperbolic spaces every horosphere is a coarsely embedded copy of the Euclidean space inside the corresponding horoball. Analogously, combinatorial horoballs satisfy the following:

Lemma 1.3

(See proof of
[
22
, Lemma 3.10]
)
.

The inclusion
\Gamma\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Hor}\left(\Gamma\right)
is a uniform coarse embedding. More precisely, for every
x,y\in\Gamma^{(0)}
,
\mathrm{d}_{\operatorname{Hor}\left(\Gamma\right)}(x,y)\geq\frac{2}{3}\log_{2}(\mathrm{d}_{\Gamma}(x,y))+1
.

Geodesics in combinatorial horoballs admit a similar description as those in real horoballs, and one can use this to show:

Theorem 1.4

(
[
22
]
)
.

There exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that, for every
\Gamma
,
\operatorname{Hor}\left(\Gamma\right)
is
\delta_{0}
-hyperbolic.

To summarise, combinatorial horoballs give a way to coarsely embed any graph into a hyperbolic space.

1.1.
Pills of combinatorial hierarchical hyperbolicity

We recall here the notion of a
combinatorial HHS
from
[
6
]
. For the whole section, let
X
be a simplicial graph, with vertex set
\overline{X}^{(0)}
, and let
\mathcal{W}
be an
X
-graph
, that is, a simplicial graph whose vertex set is the set of all maximal simplices of
X
. We conflate
X
with the flag simplicial complex that has
X
as the 1-skeleton, and in particular often refer to cliques in
X
as simplices of
X
. The main theorem of
[
6
]
describes conditions on a pair
(X,\mathcal{W})
to ensure that
\mathcal{W}
is a hierarchically hyperbolic space. We now gather the required terminology to phrase these conditions.

Given two subgraphs
A,B
of
X
(by which we always mean
full
subgraphs), if
B\subseteq\operatorname{Link}_{X}\left(A\right)
we write
A\star B
to denote the full subgraph spanned by
A^{(0)}\cup B^{(0)}
. Moreover, we set
\operatorname{Star}\left(A\right)\coloneq\operatorname{Link}_{X}\left(A\right)\star A
.

Links of simplices will correspond to hyperbolic spaces of an HHS structure. Because of this, we are interested in simplices that have the same link.

Definition 1.5

(Saturation)
.

For
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}
simplices of
X
, we write
\Delta\sim\Delta^{\prime}
to mean
\operatorname{Link}_{X}\left(\Delta\right)=\operatorname{Link}_{X}\left(\Delta^{\prime}\right)
. Let
[\Delta]
be the
\sim
–equivalence class of
\Delta
, and let

\operatorname{Sat}(\Delta)=\left(\bigcup_{\Delta^{\prime}\in[\Delta]}\Delta^{\prime}\right)^{(0)}.

Let
\mathfrak{S}
the set of
\sim
–classes of non-maximal simplices in
X
.

The hyperbolic spaces of the HHS structures we will be interested in are not exactly links in
X
(in most cases there are discrete links), but rather we have to add certain edges, as specified below.

Definition 1.6

(Augmented links)
.

Let
{X}^{+{\mathcal{W}}}
be the simplicial graph such that:

•

the
0
–skeleton of
{X}^{+{\mathcal{W}}}
is
X^{(0)}
;

•

if
v,w\in X^{(0)}
are adjacent in
X
, then they are adjacent in
{X}^{+{\mathcal{W}}}
;

•

if two vertices in
\mathcal{W}
are adjacent, then we consider
\sigma,\rho
, the associated maximal simplices of
X
, and in
{X}^{+{\mathcal{W}}}
we connect each vertex of
\sigma
to each vertex of
\rho
.

For each simplex
\Delta
of
X
, let
Y_{\Delta}
be the subgraph of
{X}^{+{\mathcal{W}}}
spanned by
({X}^{+{\mathcal{W}}})^{(0)}-\operatorname{Sat}(\Delta)
. The
augmented link
of
\Delta
is the induced subgraph
\mathcal{C}(\Delta)
of
Y_{\Delta}
spanned by
\operatorname{Link}_{X}\left(\Delta\right)^{(0)}
(we emphasise that we are taking links in
X
, not in
{X}^{+{\mathcal{W}}}
, and then considering the subgraphs of
Y_{\Delta}
induced by those links). Note that, if
\Delta\sim\Delta^{\prime}
, then
Y_{\Delta}=Y_{\Delta^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{C}(\Delta)=\mathcal{C}(\Delta^{\prime})
.

We are ready to define combinatorial HHS.

Definition 1.7

(Combinatorial HHS)
.

A
combinatorial hierarchically hyperbolic space
is the data of a simplicial graph
X
, an
X
-graph
\mathcal{W}
, and two constants
n,\delta\geq 0
, satisfying the following:

(1)

Any chain
\operatorname{Link}_{X}\left(\Delta_{1}\right)\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq\operatorname{Link}_{X}\left(\Delta_{k}\right)
has length at most
n
, which is called the
complexity
of
(X,\mathcal{W})
.

(2)

For each
[\Sigma]\in\mathfrak{S}
,
\mathcal{C}(\Sigma)
is
\delta
–hyperbolic.

(3)

For each
[\Sigma]\in\mathfrak{S}
,
\mathcal{C}(\Sigma)
is
\delta
–quasi-isometrically embedded in
Y_{\Sigma}
.

(4)

For every
[\Delta],[\Sigma]\in\mathfrak{S}
for which there exists
[\Gamma]\in\mathfrak{S}
such that
\operatorname{Link}_{X}\left(\Gamma\right)\subseteq\operatorname{Link}_{X}\left(\Delta\right)\cap\operatorname{Link}_{X}\left(\Sigma\right)
and
\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{C}(\Gamma))\geq\delta
, there exists a non-maximal simplex
\Pi
containing
\Sigma
such that
\operatorname{Link}_{X}\left(\Pi\right)\subseteq\operatorname{Link}_{X}\left(\Delta\right)
, and all
\Gamma
as above satisfy
\operatorname{Link}_{X}\left(\Gamma\right)\subseteq\operatorname{Link}_{X}\left(\Pi\right)
.

(5)

For each
[\Delta]\in\mathfrak{S}
and
v\neq w\in\operatorname{Link}_{X}\left(\Delta\right)
, if
v,w
are adjacent in
\mathcal{C}(\Delta)
but not in
\operatorname{Link}_{X}\left(\Delta\right)
, then there exist
\mathcal{W}
-adjacent maximal simplices
\Pi_{v},\Pi_{w}
such that
\Delta\star\{v\}\subseteq\Pi_{v}
and
\Delta\star\{w\}\subseteq\Pi_{w}
.

[
6
, Theorem 1.18]
states that, if
(X,\mathcal{W},n,\delta)
is a combinatorial HHS, then
\mathcal{W}
is a
hierarchically hyperbolic space
, in the sense of
[
7
, Definition 1.1]
. More precisely, the structure on
\mathcal{W}
has
\mathfrak{S}
as domain set and augmented links as coordinate spaces, while relations and projections between domains are encoded by the following additional definitions.

Definition 1.8

(Relations between domains)
.

Let
[\Delta],[\Delta^{\prime}]\in\mathfrak{S}
. Then:

•

[\Delta]\sqsubseteq[\Delta^{\prime}]
if
\operatorname{Link}_{X}\left(\Delta\right)\subseteq\operatorname{Link}_{X}\left(\Delta^{\prime}\right)
;

•

[\Delta]\bot[\Delta^{\prime}]
if
\operatorname{Link}_{X}\left(\Delta^{\prime}\right)\subseteq\operatorname{Link}_{X}\left(\operatorname{Link}_{X}\left(\Delta\right)\right)
.

If
[\Delta]
and
[\Delta^{\prime}]
are neither
\bot
–related nor
\sqsubseteq
–related, we write

[\Delta]\pitchfork[\Delta^{\prime}]
.

Definition 1.9

(Projections)
.

Fix
[\Delta]\in\mathfrak{S}
and define a map
\pi_{[\Delta]}\colon\mathcal{W}\to 2^{\mathcal{C}(\Delta)}
as follows. Let
p\colon Y_{\Delta}\to 2^{\mathcal{C}(\Delta)}
be the coarse closest point projection, i.e.

p(x)=\{y\in\mathcal{C}(\Delta)\colon\mathrm{d}_{Y_{\Delta}}(x,y)\leq\mathrm{d}_{Y_{\Delta}}(x,\mathcal{C}(\Delta))+1\}.

If
w
is a maximal simplex of
X
, the intersection
w\cap Y_{\Delta}
is non-empty and has diameter at most
1
by
[
6
, Lemma 1.15]
. Hence set

\pi_{[\Delta]}(w)=p(w\cap Y_{\Delta}),

and for every
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}
set

\mathrm{d}_{[\Delta]}(w,w^{\prime})\coloneq\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{C}(\Delta)}\left(\pi_{[\Delta]}(w),\pi_{[\Delta]}(w^{\prime})\right).

If
[\Delta],[\Delta^{\prime}]\in\mathfrak{S}
satisfy
[\Delta]\pitchfork[\Delta^{\prime}]
or
[\Delta^{\prime}]\sqsubsetneq[\Delta]
, set

\rho^{[\Delta^{\prime}]}_{[\Delta]}=p(\operatorname{Sat}(\Delta^{\prime})\cap Y_{\Delta}).

If
[\Delta]\sqsubsetneq[\Delta^{\prime}]
, let
\rho^{[\Delta^{\prime}]}_{[\Delta]}\colon\mathcal{C}(\Delta^{\prime})\to\mathcal{C}(\Delta)
be defined as follows. On
\mathcal{C}(\Delta^{\prime})\cap Y_{\Delta}
, it is the restriction of
p
; otherwise, it takes the value
\emptyset
.

Remark 1.10

(Uniform combinatorial HHSs)
.

It can be extracted from the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 1.18]
that, for a combinatorial HHS
(X,W,n,\delta)
, all constants and functions involved in the hierarchically hyperbolic structure depend only on

and
\delta
(in particular, the function appearing in the Uniqueness axiom is explicitly constructed at
[

, page 50]
). This is relevant as every coarse object naturally associated to a hierarchically hyperbolic space, such as its coarse median structure
[

]
or its coarsely injective structure
[
23
]
, ultimately only depends on these constants and functions. With this in mind, we say that a family of combinatorial HHSs is

if they satisfy

with respect to uniform constants

and
\delta
.

1.2.

We call

2.
Cusped spaces for squid HHSs

We will be able to construct a cusped space for any relative HHS with a nice combinatorial structure. We make this precise in the next definition.

2.1.
Squid HHS

Definition 2.1
.

A
squid HHS
is a combinatorial HHS
(X,\mathcal{W},n,\delta)
, where
X
is a blowup of a graph
\overline{X}
with cleanish intersections. A
relative squid HHS
is defined analogously, except that
1.7
.
(2)
is replaced by:

(2’)

For each non-maximal simplex
\Sigma
, either
\mathcal{C}(\Sigma)
is
\delta

\Sigma
is of the form
\Delta_{v}
for some
v\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
.

We often drop the constants
n
and
\delta
when they are not relevant, and refer to a (relative) squid HHS simply by
(X,\mathcal{W})
.

Remark 2.2
.

Notice that, for every simplex
\overline{\Sigma}
of
\overline{X}
, there exists a simplex
\Theta
of
X
such that
\operatorname{Link}_{X}\left(\Theta\right)=\overline{\Sigma}
. This shows that the dimension of

is bounded above by the constant

, since every chain of inclusions of simplices in

induces a chain of inclusions of links of simplices of

.

There is a natural notion of action on a squid HHS, leading to the definition of a squid HHG:

.

Let

be a (relative) squid HHS, with support graph

, and let

be a finitely generated group. A

is a simplicial action of

•

;

•

-orbits of simplices in
\overline{X}
;

•

The action on

induces a simplicial action on

.

If moreover

, then we say

.

Remark 2.22
.

If
(X,\mathcal{W})
was a genuine squid HHS, not just a relative one, another approach to proving
2.21
would be to use that
\overline{Y}_{{\Delta_{v}}^{\downarrow}}
is hyperbolic by
[
6
, Proposition 3.3]
(which is the key step in the proof that a combinatorial HHS is indeed a HHS). This would give a closest-point projection from
\overline{Y}_{{\Delta_{v}}^{\downarrow}}
to its quasiconvex subset
\mathcal{C}({\Delta_{v}}^{\downarrow})
. For a relative squid HHS we cannot use
[
6
]
; however, if
\overline{Y}_{{\Delta_{v}}^{\downarrow}}
was hyperbolic relative to
\mathcal{C}({\Delta_{v}}^{\downarrow})
, we could still use that closest-point projections to peripheral subsets of relatively hyperbolic spaces are coarsely Lipschitz (see
[
39
]
for general results about these projections).

It can in fact be extracted a posteriori from our arguments and
[
6
, Proposition 3.3]
that relative hyperbolicity indeed holds. Indeed,

is quasi-isometric to
\overline{Y}_{{\Delta_{v}}^{\downarrow}}
with a combinatorial horoball glued onto
\mathcal{C}({\Delta_{v}}^{\downarrow})
, and via
[
6
, Proposition 3.3]
we know that
Y_{\Delta_{v}}
is hyperbolic. This implies that
\overline{Y}_{{\Delta_{v}}^{\downarrow}}
is hyperbolic relative to
\mathcal{C}({\Delta_{v}}^{\downarrow})
, as this is one of the characterisations of relative hyperbolicity.

For every

All requirements only involving

(1)

(Projections.)

For every

3.1.
Relatively hyperbolic groups

Here we first construct a relative squid HHG structure for relatively hyperbolic groups (subject to natural finiteness conditions); then we recognise the associated cusped space as the

Lemma 3.1
.

A finitely generated group
G
, which is hyperbolic relative to a finite collection
\mathcal{P}
of finitely generated subgroups, is a relative squid HHG.

Proof.

Let
S
be a finite generating set for

, such that

generates

, and let

be the associated word metric on

has a relative squid HHG structure

, where:

•

\overline{X}
is the discrete graph whose vertices are cosets of the subgroups in
\mathcal{P}
;

•

for every
g\in G
and

L_{gP}=gP^{(0)}
;

•

For every

-edge if and only if
\mathrm{d}_{S}(x,x^{\prime})\leq 1
.

It is clear that
\overline{X}
has cleanish intersections. We now check the axioms from

.
Item

(1)
follows from
1.13
and the fact that
\overline{X}
is discrete. For
Item

(2)
,

is isometric to the coned-off graph, which is obtained from the Cayley graph

by adding a cone over each coset of the peripherals, and is hyperbolic by e.g.
[
28
, Theorem 5.1]
. We also notice that, for every

,
\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{gP})\cong g\operatorname{Cay}\left(P,S\cap P\right)
.

Moving to
Item

(3)
,

retracts onto

since there is a coarse closest point projection

, which map other cosets of the peripherals to uniformly bounded sets (see e.g.
[
39
, Theorem 2.14]
and the arguments therein).
Item

(4)
again follows from the fact that
\overline{X}
has cleanish intersections. Finally, if

belong to the link of a simplex

for some

and

, and

(5)

We are left to check that the above defines a relative squid

. This is because

acts cofinitely on the collection of cosets of peripherals; moreover, the action on

naturally extends to an action on

by construction.
∎

Recall from e.g.
[

that the

for

is the graph obtained from

by gluing a combinatorial horoball on

and

.

3.2.
Most HHSs are squid

In
[
25
]
, together with Mark Hagen, we roughly proved that combinatorial HHSs are abundant among all HHSs: given a hierarchically hyperbolic space
\mathcal{Z}
, whose hierarchical structure
\mathfrak{S}
is subject to certain natural requirements, there is a combinatorial HHS
(X,\mathcal{W})
such that
\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{W}
are quasi-isometric (see
[
25
, Theorem 3.15]
for the statement and
[
25
, Sections 3.1 to 3.4]
for the requirements). Notably, from the description of the combinatorial structure in
[
25
, Section 4]
, one can extract that
(X,\mathcal{W})
is a squid HHS, where:

•

the support graph
\overline{X}
is the so-called
minimal orthogonality graph
, whose vertices correspond to minimal domains in the structure and edges correspond to orthogonality;

•

for a minimal domain
V\in\mathfrak{S}
, seen as a vertex of
\overline{X}
, the augmented link of
\Delta_{V}
is quasi-isometric to the coordinate space
\mathcal{C}V
.

In particular, if
G
acts on
\mathcal{Z}
geometrically, and there is a “compatible” action on
\mathfrak{S}
with finitely many orbits of collections of pairwise orthogonal domains, then
G
is a squid HHG.

Remark 3.3
.

As pointed out in
[
25
, Remark 3.17]
, in the proof of
[
25
, Theorem 3.15]
, hyperbolicity of coordinate spaces for minimal domains in
\mathfrak{S}
is only ever used to show that certain minimal augmented links are hyperbolic. In particular, the arguments there run verbatim to show that a
relative
hierarchically hyperbolic space (resp. group), subject to the same assumptions on
\mathfrak{S}
(and on the group action on it), is a relative squid HHS (resp. HHG).

3.2.1.
Mapping class group and Teichmüller space

Here we specialise the above construction to the mapping class group

of a finite-type surface

[

.

By a

we mean a connected, compact, oriented 2-manifold

with finitely many points removed. Given a subsurface

of

which is not a pair of pants, the associated
curve graph

\mathcal{C}(U)
is the simplicial graph whose vertices are isotopy classes of simple closed curves on

is a sphere with four punctures or a torus with a puncture, we define two curves to be adjacent if they have the minimal intersection number, which is two on the sphere and one on the torus; if instead

is an annulus with core curve

, we define its

as the quasiline from
[
35
, Section 2.4]
.

As explained in
[
4
, Theorem G]
, the mapping class group
\mathcal{MCG}(S)
admits a HHG structure
\mathfrak{S}
where domains correspond to subsurfaces (up to isotopy), and where the coordinate space for a subsurface is its curve graph (resp. annular curve graph if the subsurface is an annulus). Furthermore,
[
25
, Theorem 9.8]
equips
\mathcal{MCG}(S)
with a squid HHG structure
(X,\mathcal{W})
with blowup data
(\overline{X}=\mathcal{C}(S),\{\mathcal{C}(A_{v})\}_{v\in\mathcal{C}(S)^{(0)}})
. By how
\mathcal{W}
-edges are defined in
[
25
, Definition 4.10]
, unbounded coordinate spaces correspond to (annular) curve graphs:

•

Let

be a curve. Then
\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v})
and the annular curve graph
\mathcal{C}(A_{v})
have the same vertices, and the identity map is a quasi-isometry
\phi_{\Delta_{v}}\colon\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v})\to\mathcal{C}(A_{v})
with uniform constants.

•

Let
\overline{\Sigma}
be a non-maximal simplex of
\mathcal{C}(S)
, and let

be a simplex of blowup type whose support is
\overline{\Sigma}
. If we see

as a collection of disjoint curves on

\phi_{\Sigma}\colon\mathcal{C}(\Sigma)\to\mathcal{C}(U_{\Sigma})
with uniform constants, mapping each curve

to its subsurface projection
\rho^{A_{w}}_{U_{\Sigma}}
.

To make notation compatible between the two bullets, for

we also denote

by

.

Though we state them for mapping class groups for clarity of exposition, both

Let

We split the proof in two cases, depending on whether

Blowup type.
Suppose first that

Base case

\Sigma

Claim 3.5

\rho^{A_{v}}_{U_{\Sigma}}\sim\rho^{A_{w}}_{U_{\Sigma}}

The path

with uniform constants. Let

with the same endpoints, which by the Morse lemma (see, e.g.,
[

. The bounded geodesic image axiom in

implies that one of the following is true:

•

, and we are done;

•

or

-adjacent to
v^{\prime}
and does not belong to
\operatorname{Link}_{\mathcal{C}(S)}\left(u\right)
(again,
v^{\prime\prime}
can be found in any maximal simplex containing
x
which is
\mathcal{W}
-adjacent to a simplex containing
v^{\prime}
). Applying twice the bounded geodesic image theorem in
(X,\mathcal{W})
yields that
p(v)\sim p(v^{\prime})\sim p(v^{\prime\prime})
; on the other hand, the bounded geodesic image theorem in
\mathfrak{S}
implies that
\phi_{\Delta_{u}}^{-1}(\rho^{A_{v}}_{A_{u}})\sim\phi_{\Delta_{u}}^{-1}(\rho^{A_{v^{\prime}}}_{A_{u}})\sim\phi_{\Delta_{u}}^{-1}(\rho^{A_{v^{\prime\prime}}}_{A_{u}})
. We then repeat the whole argument for
v^{\prime\prime}
inside the link of any blowup type simplex supported on
\overline{\Pi}\star\{x\}
, and we conclude by induction.
∎

The following corollary of
3.4
roughly states that relative projections between domains in the combinatorial structure, as defined in
1.9
, coarsely agree with relative projections in the original structure
\mathfrak{S}
, which are defined using subsurface projections.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
\Sigma,\Theta\subseteq X
be simplices not of bounded type.

(1)

If
[\Theta]\pitchfork[\Sigma]
or
[\Theta]\sqsubsetneq[\Sigma]
, then

\rho^{[\Theta]}_{[\Sigma]}\sim\phi_{\Sigma}^{-1}\left(\rho^{U_{\Theta}}_{U_{\Sigma}}\right),

where
\rho^{[\Theta]}_{[\Sigma]}
is the projection in
(X,\mathcal{W})
while
\rho^{U_{\Theta}}_{U_{\Sigma}}
is the subsurface projection in
\mathfrak{S}
.

(2)

If
[\Theta]\sqsupsetneq[\Sigma]
and
x\in\mathcal{C}(\Theta)
, then

\rho^{[\Theta]}_{[\Sigma]}(x)\sim\phi_{\Sigma}^{-1}\left(\rho^{U_{\Theta}}_{U_{\Sigma}}(\phi_{\Theta}(x))\right),

where
\rho^{[\Theta]}_{[\Sigma]}\colon\mathcal{C}(\Theta)\to\mathcal{C}(\Sigma)
is the projection in
(X,\mathcal{W})
while
\rho^{U_{\Theta}}_{U_{\Sigma}}\colon\mathcal{C}(U_{\Theta})\to\mathcal{C}(U_{\Sigma})
is the subsurface projection in
\mathfrak{S}
.

Proof.

(
1
) Recall that
\rho^{[\Theta]}_{[\Sigma]}=p(\operatorname{Sat}(\Theta)\cap Y_{\Sigma})
. If
\Theta=\Delta_{v}
for some
v\in\mathcal{C}(S)^{(0)}
, then
v\in\operatorname{Sat}(\Theta)\cap Y_{\Sigma}
, and since
\rho^{[\Theta]}_{[\Sigma]}
is uniformly bounded it must uniformly coarsely coincide with
p(v)
; then
p(v)\sim\phi_{\Sigma}^{-1}\left(\rho^{A_{v}}_{U_{\Sigma}}\right)
by
3.4
, and we are done since
A_{v}=U_{\Delta_{v}}
.

If instead
\Theta
is of blowup type, let
v\in\overline{\Theta}
be such that the projection
\rho^{A_{v}}_{U_{\Sigma}}
is well-defined: such a vertex exists since every subsurface in
\mathfrak{S}
which is disjoint from
A_{w}
for all
w\in\overline{\Theta}
must be nested in
U_{\Theta}
. Then, since
w\in\operatorname{Sat}(\Theta)\cap Y_{\Sigma}
, we again have that
\rho^{[\Theta]}_{[\Sigma]}\sim p(v)\sim\phi_{\Sigma}^{-1}\left(\rho^{A_{v}}_{U_{\Sigma}}\right)
. On the other hand, since
A_{v}
and
U_{\Theta}
are not orthogonal,
\rho^{A_{v}}_{U_{\Sigma}}\sim\rho^{U_{\Theta}}_{U_{\Sigma}}
by e.g.
[
19
, Lemma 1.5]
.

(
2
) If one of
\rho^{U_{\Theta}}_{U_{\Sigma}}(\phi_{\Theta}(x))
and
\rho^{[\Theta]}_{[\Sigma]}(x)
is empty, then then the diameter of their union is the maximum of the diameters, which is uniformly bounded, and there is nothing to prove. We therefore assume that both subsets are non-empty. Recall that
\rho^{[\Theta]}_{[\Sigma]}(x)
is non-empty precisely when
x\in\mathcal{C}(\Theta)\cap Y_{\Sigma}
, and in this case
\rho^{[\Theta]}_{[\Sigma]}(x)=p(x)
. Furthermore, let
v\in\mathcal{C}(S)^{(0)}
be such that
x\in\operatorname{Cone}\left(v\right)
. Then
\rho^{U_{\Theta}}_{U_{\Sigma}}(\phi_{\Theta}(x))
is non-empty precisely when
A_{v}
intersects
U_{\Sigma}
, or equivalently if
v\in Y_{\Sigma}
, and in this case
\rho^{U_{\Theta}}_{U_{\Sigma}}(\phi_{\Theta}(x))\sim\rho^{A_{v}}_{U_{\Sigma}}
. Finally,
p(x)\sim p(v)
, as
v
and
x
are adjacent in
Y_{\Sigma}
, and in turn
p(v)\sim\phi_{\Sigma}^{-1}\left(\rho^{A_{v}}_{U_{\Sigma}}\right)
by
3.4
. Combining the above yields that

\displaystyle\rho^{[\Theta]}_{[\Sigma]}(x)=p(x)\sim p(v)\sim\phi_{\Sigma}^{-1}\left(\rho^{A_{v}}_{U_{\Sigma}}\right)\sim\phi_{\Sigma}^{-1}\left(\rho^{U_{\Theta}}_{U_{\Sigma}}(\phi_{\Theta}(x))\right).

We are now ready to prove that the cusped space for a mapping class group coincides with its Teichmüller space. The core idea is that, since the two spaces admit HHS structures with the same domains and coordinate spaces, and since relative projections between domains in the two structure coarsely coincide by
3.6
, the two spaces must essentially coincide, and indeed the Realization theorem
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
produces an explicit quasi-isometry.

Proposition 3.7
.

The cusped space for the mapping class group of a finite-type surface is quasi-isometric to the corresponding Teichmüller space, equipped with the Teichmüller metric.

Proof.

By
[
4
, Theorem G]
, the HHS structure for
\operatorname{Teich}\left(S\right)
with the Teichmüller metric has the same domains and coordinate spaces as that for
\mathcal{MCG}(S)
, except that each annular curve graph is replaced by the combinatorial horoball over it. Relative projections are again defined using subsurface projections, so they are the same as for
\mathcal{MCG}(S)
.

Now let
(X,\mathcal{W})
be the combinatorial HHG structure for
\mathcal{MCG}(S)
, and let
(\widehat{X},\widehat{\mathcal{W}})
be the associated cusped space. All projections between domains in
\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
are defined using the coarse closest point projections
Y_{\Sigma}\to\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma)
, which away from
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma)
restrict to the projections
Y_{{\Sigma}^{\downarrow}}\to\mathcal{C}({\Sigma}^{\downarrow})
, as explained in the proof of
2.24
. In particular, relative projections between domains in
(\widehat{X},\widehat{\mathcal{W}})
coincide with the corresponding relative projections in
(X,\mathcal{W})
when restricted to
X
.

We now define a quasi-isometry
\widehat{\mathcal{W}}\to\operatorname{Teich}\left(S\right)
. For every
w\in\widehat{W}
, let
b_{w}\coloneq(\pi_{[\Sigma]}(w))\in\prod\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma)
be the tuple of its coordinates, where
[\Sigma]
varies among all simplices not of bounded type. The tuple
b_{w}
is
consistent
, which roughly means that it satisfies certain inequalities relating coordinates in
b_{w}
and relative projections between domains (see
[
7
, Definition 1.17]
). Now let
\phi(b_{w})\coloneq(\widehat{\phi}_{\Sigma}(\pi_{[\Sigma]}(w)))\in\prod\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(U_{\Sigma})
, where
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(U_{\Sigma})
is the coordinate space for
U_{\Sigma}
in the structure for
\operatorname{Teich}\left(S\right)
and
\widehat{\phi}_{\Sigma}\colon\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma)\to\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(U_{\Sigma})
is the quasi-isometry induced by
\phi_{\Sigma}\colon\mathcal{C}(\Sigma)\to\mathcal{C}(U_{\Sigma})
.
3.6
implies that
\phi(b_{w})
is again consistent, because relative projections in the combinatorial structure and in the original structure coarsely coincide. In turn, the Realization Theorem for consistent tuples
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
produces a point
f(w)\in\operatorname{Teich}\left(S\right)
whose coordinates roughly coincide with
\phi(b_{w})
; furthermore
f(w)
is coarsely unique, meaning that every point with the same properties is within uniformly bounded Teichmüller distance from
f(w)
.

We just defined a coarse map
f\colon\widehat{\mathcal{W}}\to\operatorname{Teich}\left(S\right)
, and we claim that is coarsely Lipschitz. To see this, let
w,w^{\prime}\in\widehat{W}
span an edge. Since coordinate projections are coarsely Lipschitz, and since each
\widehat{\phi}_{\Sigma}
is a quasi-isometry, we have that
\widehat{\phi}_{\Sigma}(\pi_{[\Sigma]}(w))\sim\widehat{\phi}_{\Sigma}(\pi_{[\Sigma]}(w^{\prime}))
for all
[\Sigma]
. Then the uniqueness axiom
[
7
, Definition 1.1.(9)]
implies that
f(w)\sim f(w^{\prime})
, as their coordinates are uniformly close.

The same arguments yield a coarsely Lipschitz, coarse map
g\colon\operatorname{Teich}\left(S\right)\to\widehat{W}
, defined by mapping each point
x\in\operatorname{Teich}\left(S\right)
to a realisation point for the tuple
\phi_{\Sigma}^{-1}(\pi_{U_{\Sigma}}(x))\in\prod\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma)
. It is then clear that
f
and
g
are quasi-inverses, thus proving that
f
is a quasi-isometry.
∎

4.
Controlling torsion

The main result of this Section is
4.2
, which roughly states that, if a group
G
has a quotient
\widetilde{G}
which is a relative squid HHG, then every finite subgroup
\widetilde{F}\leq\widetilde{G}
either comes from a finite subgroup of
G
or permutes a collection of orthogonal minimal domains. Crucially, this dichotomy will follow from the fact that the
\widetilde{F}
-action on the cusped space for
\widetilde{G}
has a uniformly bounded orbit, which either lies in a coarsely embedded copy of
\widetilde{G}
or inside a product of horoballs. We first recall a definition.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
G
be a group and
S
a generating set. The
injectivity radius
of a quotient
\pi\colon G\twoheadrightarrow\widetilde{G}
with respect to
S
, denoted
\operatorname{inj}_{S}{\pi}
, is the maximum radius of a ball in
\operatorname{Cay}\left(G,S\right)
around the identity which injects inside
\operatorname{Cay}\left(\widetilde{G},\pi(S)\right)
.

Theorem 4.2
.

For every
n,\delta,K\geq 0
there exists
\rho=\rho(n,\delta,K)\geq 0
such that the following holds.
Let
G
be a group,
S
a finite generating set for
G
,
\pi\colon G\twoheadrightarrow\widetilde{G}
a quotient, and
\widetilde{F}\leq\widetilde{G}
a finite subgroup. Suppose that:

(1)

\widetilde{G}
has a relative squid HHG structure
(X,\mathcal{W},n,\delta)
;

(2)

Some orbit map
\operatorname{Cay}\left(\widetilde{G},\pi(S)\right)\to\mathcal{W}
is a
K
-quasi-isometry;

(3)

\operatorname{inj}_{S}{\pi}\geq\rho
.

Then either
\widetilde{F}
fixes a non-empty simplex of
\overline{X}
setwise, or is the isomorphic image under
\pi
of a finite subgroup
F\leq G
of order at most
|S|^{\rho+1}
.

Setting
G=\widetilde{G}
in the above Theorem yields:

Corollary 4.3
.

For every
n,\delta,K\geq 0
there exists
N\geq 0
such that the following happens.
Let
G
have a relative squid HHG structure
(X,\mathcal{W},n,\delta)
. Suppose that, for some finite generating set
S
of
G
, some orbit map
\operatorname{Cay}\left(G,S\right)\to\mathcal{W}
is a
K
-quasi-isometry. Then every finite subgroup of
G
of order greater than
|S|^{N}
must fix a non-empty simplex of
\overline{X}
setwise.

Proof of
4.2
.

We shall progressively define constants
E_{0},\ldots,E_{4}
, all depending on
n
,
\delta
, and
K
only, and eventually set
\rho=2E_{4}
.

Let
(\widehat{X},\widehat{\mathcal{W}})
be the cusped space for
(X,\mathcal{W})
, which is a squid HHS with constants
n,\delta^{\prime}=\delta^{\prime}(\delta)
by
2.17
. By
[
23
, Theorem A]
, there exists
E_{0}\geq 0
such that
\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
is
E_{0}
-
coarsely injective
. We will not need the full definition of this property; it will suffice to know that, by combining
[
23
, Proposition 1.1]
and
[
30
, Theorem 1.2]
, every finite group acting by isometries on an
E_{0}
-coarsely injective space has an orbit of diameter at most
E_{1}
, where
E_{1}
only depends on
E_{0}
. We stress that the construction in
[
23
]
ultimately depends only on the constants and functions appearing in the definition of a hierarchically hyperbolic space
[
7
, Definition 1.1]
; in turn, for the squid HHS
(\widehat{X},\widehat{\mathcal{W}},n,\delta^{\prime})
, the latter are defined in terms of
n
and
\delta^{\prime}
, as we pointed out in
1.10
. This proves that
E_{0}
, and in turn
E_{1}
, only depend on
n
and
\delta
.

Now let
\widetilde{F}\leq\widetilde{G}
be a finite subgroup, which has an orbit of diameter at most
E_{1}
in
\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
by coarse injectivity. The idea we shall expand upon below is that either this orbit is far from
\mathcal{W}
, and therefore has “deep” projections to some cusps supported on a simplex of
\overline{X}
, or
\widetilde{F}
has a uniformly bounded orbit in
\mathcal{W}
, hence in
G
, and therefore its cardinality is uniformly bounded.

Let
\Theta=(v,p^{m_{v}})_{v\in\overline{\Theta}}\in\widehat{\mathcal{W}}^{(0)}
be a maximal simplex such that
\mathrm{diam}_{\widehat{\mathcal{W}}}(\widetilde{F}\cdot\Theta)\leq E_{1}
. Since projections to coordinate spaces in
\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
are uniformly coarsely Lipschitz, there exists
E_{2}
, depending on
n
,
\delta
, and
E_{1}
, such that for every simplex
\Sigma
of
\widehat{X}
,

(3)

\mathrm{diam}_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma)}(\widetilde{F}\cdot\Theta)\leq E_{2}.

Now let

\mathcal{V}=\widetilde{F}\cdot\{v\in\overline{\Theta}^{(0)}\mid m_{v}\geq E_{2}+1\},

that is, the orbit of the supports where the depth of
\Theta
is greater than
E_{2}
. We claim that any two
u,v\in\mathcal{V}
are adjacent in
\overline{X}
. Indeed, up to the action of
\widetilde{F}
, assume that
v\in\overline{\Theta}^{(0)}
and
u\in\widetilde{f}\cdot\overline{\Theta}^{(0)}
for some
\widetilde{f}\in\widetilde{F}
. If
u
and
v
were not adjacent, then the projection of
\widetilde{f}\cdot\Theta
to
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\Delta_{v})
would be contained in
\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v})
, and therefore

\mathrm{d}_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\Delta_{v})}(\widetilde{f}\cdot\Theta,\Theta)=\mathrm{d}_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}(\Delta_{v})}(\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v}),p^{m_{v}})=m_{v}\geq E_{2}+1,

contradicting the definition of
E_{2}
in
Equation

3
.

The above shows that
\mathcal{V}
spans a simplex of
\overline{X}
, which is preserved by
\widetilde{F}
. If
\mathcal{V}\neq\emptyset
we are done, so suppose otherwise. We will prove that
\widetilde{F}
has uniformly bounded cardinality, by showing that it has a uniformly bounded orbit in
\operatorname{Cay}\left(\widetilde{G},\pi(S)\right)
. To see this, first notice that the cardinality of
\overline{\Theta}
is at most the dimension of
\overline{X}
, which is bounded by
n
by
2.2
. Hence, by how we defined
\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
-edges in

, we see that

\mathrm{d}_{\widehat{\mathcal{W}}}(\Theta,{\Theta}^{\downarrow})\leq\sum_{v\in\overline{\Theta}^{(0)}}m_{v}\leq nE_{2},

where we used that each
m_{v}
is at most
E_{2}
since
\mathcal{V}
is trivial. This proves that the
\widetilde{F}
-orbit of
{\Theta}^{\downarrow}
has diameter at most
E_{1}+2nE_{2}
in
\widehat{\mathcal{W}}
; in turn, by
2.14
the diameter of the same orbit in
\mathcal{W}
is at most
E_{3}\coloneq(E_{1}+2nE_{2})2^{E_{1}+2nE_{2}}
. Since some orbit map
\operatorname{Cay}\left(\widetilde{G},\pi(S)\right)\to\mathcal{W}
is a
K
-quasi-isometry, there exists a constant
E_{4}
depending on
K
and
E_{3}
(and so ultimately on
K
,
n
, and
\delta
) and some
\widetilde{h}\in\widetilde{G}
such that

\mathrm{diam}_{\operatorname{Cay}\left(\widetilde{G},\pi(S)\right)}(\widetilde{F}\cdot\widetilde{h})\leq E_{4}.

Since
\widetilde{G}
acts by isometries on its Cayley graph, this implies that the conjugate
\widetilde{F}^{\prime}\coloneq\widetilde{h}^{-1}\widetilde{F}\widetilde{h}
is contained in the ball of radius
E_{4}
around the identity of
\widetilde{G}
.

Now let
\rho=2E_{4}
, so that the ball
B
of radius
2E_{4}
around the identity of
G
injects inside
\widetilde{G}
; in particular,
\pi(B)
contains
\widetilde{F}^{\prime}
. Let
F^{\prime}\coloneq\pi^{-1}(\widetilde{F}^{\prime})\cap B
, so that
\pi
restricts to a bijection between
F^{\prime}
and
\widetilde{F}^{\prime}
. Notice that
F^{\prime}
is contained in the ball

of radius

around the identity, so
|F^{\prime}|\leq|B^{\prime}|\leq|B|\leq|S|^{\rho+1}
. Moreover

is a subgroup: for every
f,g\in F^{\prime}
, the product
fg
still belongs to

, since

\mathrm{d}_{\operatorname{Cay}\left(G,S\right)}(fg,1)\leq\mathrm{d}_{\operatorname{Cay}\left(G,S\right)}(f,1)+\mathrm{d}_{\operatorname{Cay}\left(G,S\right)}(g,1)\leq 2E_{4}=\rho.

Hence
fg
is the unique preimage of
\pi(f)\pi(g)\in\widetilde{F}^{\prime}
inside

, and must therefore belong to
F^{\prime}
. Summing up, we proved that
\widetilde{F}^{\prime}
is the isomorphic image under
\pi
of a subgroup of
G
of order at most
|S|^{\rho+1}
; in turn, since
\widetilde{F}^{\prime}=\widetilde{h}^{-1}\widetilde{F}\widetilde{h}
, for any

the subgroup

and has order at most

, as required.
∎

We record an immediate application of
4.2
to relatively hyperbolic groups, which recovers
[
15
, Lemma 4.3]
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group which is hyperbolic relative to a finite collection
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\}
of finitely generated peripheral subgroups. For every
i=1\ldots,k
let
H_{i}\unlhd P_{i}
be a normal subgroup, and let
\mathcal{N}=\langle\langle H_{i}\rangle\rangle_{i=1,\ldots,k}
. There exist a finite subset
\mathcal{F}\subset G
and a constant
M\geq 0
, both depending only on
G
and
\mathcal{P}
, such that, if each
H_{i}
intersects
\mathcal{F}
trivially, then any finite subgroup of
G/\mathcal{N}
is either:

•

the isomorphic image of a subgroup of
G
of order at most
M
;

•

or is conjugated inside the image of a peripheral.

Proof.

Let
S
be a finite generating set for
G
, and let
\delta_{0}\geq 0
be a hyperbolicity constant for the graph obtained from
\operatorname{Cay}\left(G,S\right)
by adding a cone over each coset of the
P_{i}
. Let
\rho=\rho(\delta_{0},n=3,K=1)
be the constant given by
4.2
. The relative Dehn filling theorem
[
36
,
22
]
states that there exists a finite subset
\mathcal{F}\subset G
such that, if each
H_{i}
misses
\mathcal{F}
, then
G/\mathcal{N}
is hyperbolic relative to
\pi(\mathcal{P})=\{P_{i}/H_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,k}
, and the injectivity radius of
\pi\colon G\to G/\mathcal{N}
is at least
\rho
. Now,
3.1
endows
G/\mathcal{N}
with a relative squid structure
(X,\mathcal{W})
, where:

•

\mathcal{W}=\operatorname{Cay}\left(G/\mathcal{N},\pi(S)\right)
;

•

\overline{X}
is the discrete graph whose vertices are cosets of the
P_{i}/H_{i}
;

•

{X}^{+{\mathcal{W}}}
is the graph obtained from
\operatorname{Cay}\left(G/\mathcal{N},\pi(S)\right)
by adding a cone over each coset of the
P_{i}/H_{i}
.

In particular,
(X,\mathcal{W},n,\delta)
is a relative squid HHS, where
n=3
and
\delta
is the hyperbolicity constant of
{X}^{+{\mathcal{W}}}
. In turn, up to enlarging
\mathcal{F}
, we can ensure that the latter is bounded in terms of
\delta_{0}
. This can be deduced from
[
36
, Lemma 5.3]
, which gives a bound on the relative isoperimetric inequality for the quotient coned-off graph.
Finally, notice that there is an isometry
\operatorname{Cay}\left(G/\mathcal{N},\pi(S)\right)\to\mathcal{W}
, which in particular is a
K
-quasi-isometry with
K=1
. Hence
4.2
implies that any finite subgroup of
G/\mathcal{N}
of order greater than
M\coloneq|S|^{\rho}
must fix a point of
\overline{X}
, and is therefore conjugated inside a peripheral.
∎

5.
Automorphisms of Dehn filling quotients of short HHGs

In this Section we leverage
4.2
to control the automorphism groups of certain quotients of
short HHGs
. These groups, introduced by the first author in
[
34
]
, are a broad family of squid HHGs including Artin groups of large and hyperbolic type, RAAGs on triangle- and square-free graphs, graph manifold groups, and the mapping class group of a sphere with five punctures.

5.1.
Background on short HHGs

We first recall the definition and some properties of short HHGs.

Definition 5.1
.

A
short HHG
is a group
G
admitting a combinatorial HHG structure
(X,\mathcal{W})
such that:

(1)

X
is the blowup of a triangle- and square-free graph
\overline{X}
, such that every connected component of
\overline{X}
is not a point.

(2)

G
acts on
\overline{X}
with finitely many orbits of edges;

(3)

For every
v\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
, there is an extension
0\to Z_{v}\to\operatorname{Stab}_{G}\left(v\right)\xrightarrow{\mathfrak{p}_{v}}H_{v}\to 0
, where
H_{v}
is a hyperbolic group and
Z_{v}
is a cyclic subgroup of
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}\left(v\right)
acting trivially on
\operatorname{Link}_{\overline{X}}\left(v\right)
.

(4)

For every
v\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
and
g\in G
,
Z_{gv}=gZ_{v}g^{-1}
.

(5)

For every
v\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
,
Z_{v}
acts geometrically on
\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v})
and with uniformly bounded orbits on
\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{w})
for every
w\in\operatorname{Link}_{\overline{X}}\left(v\right)
.

\overline{X}
is called the
support graph
. For every
v\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
, we call
Z_{v}
the
cyclic direction
for
v
. Moreover,
Item

5
implies that
\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v})
is a quasiline if
Z_{v}
is infinite, and is uniformly bounded otherwise.

Remark 5.2
.

Notice that short HHGs are squid HHGs by construction:
X
is a blowup of
\overline{X}
, and the latter has cleanish intersections simply because it does not contain triangles nor squares.

Remark 5.3
.

If
v,w\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
span an edge, and if
Z_{v}
is infinite, then
Z_{v}
and
Z_{w}
commute. Indeed, every
g\in Z_{w}
normalises
Z_{v}
, so it acts on
Z_{v}
as either the identity or the inversion swapping a generator of
Z_{v}
with its inverse. However the second possibility does not happen, since
Z_{v}
acts loxodromically on
\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v})
while
Z_{w}
acts with uniformly bounded orbits, and therefore cannot “flip” a quasiaxis for
Z_{w}
.

Definition 5.4
.

A short HHG
G
, with support graph
\overline{X}
, is
colourable
if there exists a partition of the vertices of
\overline{X}
into finitely many colours, such that no two adjacent vertices share the same colour, and the
G
-action on
\overline{X}

5.2.
Dehn filling quotients

We now clarify which quotients of short HHGs we are interested in.

Notation 5.5

Let

For the whole Section, we always work under

Lemma 5.6

(

, Corollary 3.40]
)

The injectivity radius of

5.2.1.
Cyclic directions for the quotient

For later purposes, we gather here a few more lemmas about Dehn filling quotients, again working in the setting of
5.5
. The first lemma describes the image of cyclic directions in the quotient.

Lemma 5.15
.

The following holds if
N
is deep enough. For every
v\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
,
DT_{N}\cap\operatorname{Stab}_{G}\left(v\right)=\langle NZ_{w}\rangle_{w\in\operatorname{Star}\left(v\right)}
and
DT_{N}\cap Z_{v}=NZ_{v}
. In particular,
Z_{v_{N}}\cong\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

The first conclusion is
[
32
, Corollary 3.36]
. For the second equality let
g\in DT_{N}\cap Z_{v}
. Since
Z_{v}\leq\operatorname{Stab}_{G}\left(v\right)
, by the first conclusion
g\in\langle NZ_{w}\rangle_{w\in\operatorname{Star}\left(v\right)}
; moreover
NZ_{v}
is normal in
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}\left(v\right)
, so we can write
g=hg^{\prime}
where
h\in NZ_{v}
and
g^{\prime}\in\langle NZ_{w}\rangle_{w\in\operatorname{Link}_{\overline{X}}\left(v\right)}\cap Z_{v}
. If
g^{\prime}
is trivial we are done, so suppose otherwise towards a contradiction. By
[
32
, Corollary 3.37]
we have

\langle NZ_{w}\rangle_{w\in\operatorname{Link}_{\overline{X}}\left(v\right)}=\bigast_{u\in J}NZ_{u},

where
J
is a certain (possibly infinite) subset of
\operatorname{Link}_{\overline{X}}\left(v\right)
. If
J
consists of a single vertex
w
then
g^{\prime}\in NZ_{w}\cap Z_{v}
, and therefore this intersection is non-trivial. This is a contradiction, because the two cyclic directions have different actions on the quasiline
\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v})
by
5.1
.(
5
). Thus suppose that
J
contains more than a vertex, and therefore
\langle NZ_{w}\rangle_{w\in\operatorname{Link}_{\overline{X}}\left(v\right)}
is a non-abelian free group. Since
Z_{v}
is normal in
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}\left(v\right)
, the square power of
g^{\prime}
lies in the centre of
\langle NZ_{w}\rangle_{w\in\operatorname{Link}_{\overline{X}}\left(v\right)}
, which is trivial; in turn, since a non-abelian free group has no torsion,

itself must be trivial, against our initial assumption.
∎

5.2.2.
Commuting elements

Next, we prove that commuting elements of
G_{N}
cannot have large displacements on transverse lines: this is an instance of a more general principle that holds for all HHG. For the following statement, the
minimum displacement
of an isometry
g
of a metric space
S
is defined as
\inf_{x\in S}\mathrm{d}_{S}(x,gx)
.

Lemma 5.16
.

There exists
\widetilde{E}>0
, only depending on
G
, such that the following holds if
N
is deep enough. For
i=1,2
let
v_{N}^{i}\in\overline{X}_{N}^{(0)}
and
g_{i}\in\operatorname{Stab}_{G_{N}}\left(v^{i}_{N}\right)
. Suppose that the minimum displacement of
g_{i}
on
\mathcal{C}(\Delta^{i})
is at least
\widetilde{E}
. If
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
commute then
v^{1}_{N}\in\operatorname{Star}\left(v^{2}_{N}\right)
.

Proof.

Towards a contradiction, assume that
v^{1}_{N}
and
v^{2}_{N}
are not adjacent, so that they yield transverse domains
[\Delta_{v^{1}_{N}}]
and
[\Delta_{v^{2}_{N}}]
. Let
r^{1}_{2}\coloneq\rho^{[\Delta_{v^{1}_{N}}]}_{[\Delta_{v^{2}_{N}}]}\subset\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v^{2}_{N}})
and
r^{2}_{1}\coloneq\rho^{[\Delta_{v^{2}_{N}}]}_{[\Delta_{v^{1}_{N}}]}\subset\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v^{1}_{N}})
be the relative projections between these two transverse domains. Since
(X_{N},\mathcal{W}_{N},n,\delta)
is a relative squid HHS, there exists a constant
E=E(n,\delta)>0
, not depending on
N
, such that the following hold:

•

Both
r^{1}_{2}
and
r^{2}_{1}
have diameter at most
E
(this is
[
7
, Definition 1.14.(1)]
).

•

For every
q\in\mathcal{W}_{N}
,
\min\left\{\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v^{1}_{N}})}(q,r^{2}_{1}),\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v^{1}_{N}})}(q,r^{1}_{2})\right\}\leq E
(this is the Behrstock inequality,
[
7
, Definition 1.14.(4)]
).

•

There exists
p\in\mathcal{W}_{N}
such that
\max\left\{\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v^{1}_{N}})}(p,r^{2}_{1}),\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v^{1}_{N}})}(p,r^{1}_{2})\right\}\leq E
(this follows from the partial realisation axiom
[
7
, Definition 1.14.(8)]
, applied to the pair
([\Delta_{v^{1}_{N}}],r^{2}_{1})
).

Now set
\widetilde{E}=4E
, and let
g_{1},g_{2}\in G_{N}
be as in the statement. Notice first that, for
p
as in the third bullet,

\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v^{1}_{N}})}(r^{2}_{1},g_{1}p)\geq\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v^{1}_{N}})}(p,g_{1}p)-\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v^{1}_{N}})}(p,r^{2}_{1})-\mathrm{diam}(r^{2}_{1})\geq 4E-E-E=2E,

so the Behrstock inequality yields that
\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v^{2}_{N}})}(r^{1}_{2},g_{1}p)\leq E
. If we now consider the action of
g_{2}
on
\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v^{2}_{N}})
, the same reasoning yields

\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v^{2}_{N}})}(r^{1}_{2},g_{2}g_{1}p)\geq\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v^{2}_{N}})}(g_{1}p,g_{2}g_{1}p)-\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v^{2}_{N}})}(g_{1}p,r^{1}_{2})-\mathrm{diam}(r^{1}_{2})\geq 2E.

However
g_{2}g_{1}p=g_{1}g_{2}p
, so if in the above argument we swap the roles of
v^{1}_{N}
and
v^{2}_{N}
, and of
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
, we get that

\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v^{1}_{N}})}(r^{2}_{1},g_{2}g_{1}p)=\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v^{1}_{N}})}(r^{2}_{1},g_{1}g_{2}p)\geq 2E,

contradicting the Behrstock inequality for
q=g_{2}g_{1}p
.
∎

Corollary 5.17
.

The following hold if
N
is deep enough. For
i=1,2
let
\{v^{i},w^{i}\}
be edges of
\overline{X}
.

(1)

If there exist commuting elements
g_{1}\in Z_{v^{1}_{N}}
and
g_{2}\in Z_{v^{2}_{N}}
, then
v^{1}_{N}\in\operatorname{Star}\left(v^{2}_{N}\right)
.

(2)

If there exist commuting elements
g_{1}\in\langle Z_{v^{1}_{N}},Z_{w^{1}_{N}}\rangle-(Z_{v^{1}_{N}}\cup Z_{w^{1}_{N}})
and
g_{2}\in\langle Z_{v^{2}_{N}},Z_{w^{2}_{N}}\rangle-(Z_{v^{2}_{N}}\cup Z_{w^{2}_{N}})

.

Proof.

For every
u\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
,

acts

-coboundedly on
\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{u})
for some

, and since there are finitely many

there is a uniform bound

on all

.
Furthermore, by
5.7
.
(iii)
, each

is uniformly quasi-isometric to

, so

-coboundedly for some constant

Now, if

as in

The argument for

as in the statement, it is enough to show that, for

,

, provided that

. For ease of notation we drop the indices and denote

,

, and

From
5.17
we can also extract that cyclic directions can be algebraically distinguished from products of adjacent cyclic directions:

Corollary 5.18
.

The following holds if
N
is deep enough. Let
v,w
be
\overline{X}
-adjacent vertices of valence greater than one, and let
x\in\langle Z_{v_{N}},Z_{w_{N}}\rangle
. Then
x\in Z_{v_{N}}\cup Z_{w_{N}}
if and only if its centraliser
C_{G_{N}}(x)
is infinite.

Proof.

We first notice that, under the above assumption,
H_{v}
is non-elementary hyperbolic. To see this, let
u\in\operatorname{Link}_{\overline{X}}\left(v\right)-\{w\}
, which exists as
v
has valence greater than one. If
H_{v}
was virtually cyclic then
Z_{u}
would intersect
\langle Z_{v},Z_{w}\rangle
non-trivially. This is impossible since
\langle Z_{v},Z_{w}\rangle
fixes
w
while no element in
Z_{u}
does: indeed, every element in
Z_{u}
acts loxodromically on
\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{u})
, and therefore cannot fix the projection of
w
to
\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{u})
.

Moving to the proof of the Lemma, we first suppose without loss of generality that
x\in Z_{v_{N}}
. Then
C_{G_{N}}(x)
contains the index-two subgroup of
\operatorname{Stab}_{G_{N}}\left(v_{N}\right)
centralising
Z_{v_{N}}
, so it is enough to prove that
\operatorname{Stab}_{G_{N}}\left(v_{N}\right)
is infinite. In turn, the latter fits into an extension

0\to Z_{v_{N}}\to\operatorname{Stab}_{G_{N}}\left(v_{N}\right)\to H_{v_{N}}\to 0,

so we are left to prove that
H_{v_{N}}
is infinite.
5.13

H_{v_{N}}
as a relative hyperbolic Dehn filling of the non-elementary hyperbolic group
H_{v}
; hence, by
[

, Corollary 1.6]
, if
N
is deep enough then
H_{v_{N}}
is non-elementary hyperbolic, and in particular infinite.

For the converse implication, let
x\in\langle Z_{v_{N}},Z_{w_{N}}\rangle-(Z_{v_{N}}\cup Z_{w_{N}})
, and let
g\in C_{G_{N}}(x)
. Then
x=gxg^{-1}\in\langle Z_{g(v_{N})},Z_{g(w_{N})}\rangle-(Z_{g(v_{N})}\cup Z_{g(w_{N})})
, by how cyclic directions behave under conjugation; thus

.(

) implies that

fixes

setwise. This proves that

is virtually contained in
\operatorname{Stab}_{G_{N}}\left(v_{N}\right)\cap\operatorname{Stab}_{G_{N}}\left(w_{N}\right)
, which in turn virtually coincides with the finite subgroup
\langle Z_{v_{N}},Z_{w_{N}}\rangle\cong Z_{v_{N}}\times Z_{w_{N}}
by
[

, Corollary 2.17]
.
∎

5.3.
Extracting combinatorial data from automorphisms

We now prove that group automorphisms of
G_{N}
induce simplicial automorphisms of
\overline{X}_{N}
:

Theorem 5.19
.

Let
G
be a colourable short HHG as in
5.5
. Suppose further that
\overline{X}
has no valence-one vertices. There exists an integer
M
, only depending on
G
, such that the following holds if
N
is deep enough. Every group automorphism
\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}\left(G_{N}\right)
induces a simplicial automorphism
\phi_{\#}\in\operatorname{Aut}\left(\overline{X}_{N}\right)
such that, for every
v\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
,
\phi_{\#}(v_{N})
is the unique vertex of
\overline{X}_{N}
satisfying

\phi(MZ_{v_{N}})=MZ_{\phi_{\#}(v_{N})}.

Proof.

Let
\phi
be an automorphism of
G_{N}
. For every
v\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
,
Z_{v_{N}}\coloneq Z_{v}/(DT_{N}\cap Z_{v})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
by
5.15
, and therefore
\phi(Z_{v_{N}})\cong\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
since
\phi
is an automorphism. Let
g
generate
\phi(Z_{v_{N}})
. Our next goal is to invoke
5.11
to show that a uniform power of
g
fixes an edge of
\overline{X}_{N}
; later we will choose
\phi_{\#}(v_{N})
as one of the endpoints of such edge.

Claim 5.20
.

There exists
M\in\mathbb{N}
, only depending on
G
, such that the following holds for deep enough
N
. There exist
\overline{X}
-adjacent vertices
v^{\prime},w^{\prime}
such that
g^{M}\in\langle Z_{v^{\prime}_{N}},Z_{w^{\prime}_{N}}\rangle
.

Proof of
5.20
.

Firstly, recall that a hierarchically hyperbolic group contains finitely many conjugacy classes of finite subgroups, by
[
23
, Theorem G]
. In particular, we can choose
N
deep enough that
G
contains no subgroup isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
. Hence, by
5.11
there exists a non-empty simplex
\overline{\Sigma}
of
\overline{X}
which is preserved by
\phi(Z_{v_{N}})
. Notice that
\overline{X}_{N}
is triangle-free, and therefore
\overline{\Sigma}
is either a single vertex or an edge; either way, there exists
v^{\prime}\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
such that
g^{2}\in\operatorname{Stab}_{G_{N}}\left(v^{\prime}_{N}\right)
.

Now let
q\colon\operatorname{Stab}_{G_{N}}\left(v^{\prime}_{N}\right)\to H_{v^{\prime}_{N}}\coloneq\operatorname{Stab}_{G_{N}}\left(v^{\prime}_{N}\right)/Z_{v^{\prime}_{N}}
be the quotient map. By
5.14
there exists a constant
M^{\prime}
such that either
q(g^{2})
has order at most
M^{\prime}
, or
q(g^{2})\in E(Z_{w^{\prime}})/NZ_{w^{\prime}}
for some
w^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Link}_{\overline{X}}\left(v^{\prime}\right)
. In the former case
g^{2M^{\prime}}\in Z_{v^{\prime}_{N}}
; in turn, since
M^{\prime}
only depends on the isomorphism type of
H_{v^{\prime}}
, of which there are finitely many since
G
acts cofinitely on the vertices of
\overline{X}
, there exists a power of
g
, only depending on
G
, which belongs to
Z_{v^{\prime}_{N}}
. In the latter case, since
Z_{w^{\prime}}
has finite-index in
E(Z_{w^{\prime}})
, a uniform power of
q(g^{2})
must belong to
q(Z_{w^{\prime}})
, and again we can choose this power to not depend on either
v^{\prime}
or
w^{\prime}
as there are finitely many
G
-orbits of edges in
\overline{X}
.
∎

Now, since
\overline{X}
has no valence-one vertices,
5.18
yields that the centraliser of any element in
Z_{v_{N}}
is infinite, and the same must be true for
\phi(Z_{v_{N}})
as
\phi
is an isomorphism. In particular
g^{M}
has infinite centraliser, and again
5.18
implies that
g^{M}\in Z_{\phi_{\#}(v_{N})}
where
\phi_{\#}(v_{N})
is either
v^{\prime}_{N}
or
w^{\prime}_{N}
. Notice moreover that, since
M
does not depend on
N
, we can choose
N
to be a multiple of
M
. Then
\langle g^{M}\rangle=\phi(MZ_{v})
is a subgroup of
Z_{\phi_{\#}(v_{N})}\cong\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
of order
N/M
, and it must therefore coincide with
MZ_{\phi_{\#}(v_{N})}
.

We also point out that
\phi_{\#}(v_{N})
is unique. Indeed, suppose towards a contradiction that
g^{M}\in Z_{v^{\prime\prime}_{N}}
for some
v^{\prime\prime}_{N}\neq\phi_{\#}(v_{N})
, so that
Z_{\phi_{\#}(v_{N})}\cap Z_{v^{\prime\prime}_{N}}
have a non-trivial intersection.
5.17
.(
1
) would then imply that
v^{\prime\prime}_{N}\in\operatorname{Link}_{\overline{X}_{N}}\left(\phi_{\#}(v_{N})\right)
; but then
Z_{v^{\prime\prime}_{N}}
would act geometrically on
\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{v^{\prime\prime}_{N}})
and with uniformly bounded orbits on
\mathcal{C}(\Delta_{\phi_{\#}(v_{N})})
, contradicting the existence of a non-trivial intersection.

We are left to prove the following:

Claim 5.21
.

Whenever
N
is deep enough,
\phi_{\#}\colon\overline{X}\to\overline{X}
is a graph isomorphism.

Proof of
5.21
.

It is enough to prove that
\phi_{\#}
maps edges to edges, since then the same argument applied to
\phi^{-1}
will produce an inverse fof
\phi_{\#}
. Hence let
v_{N},w_{N}
be
\overline{X}_{N}
-adjacent. By
5.3
, the subgroups
MZ_{v_{N}}
and
MZ_{w_{N}}
commute, and therefore
MZ_{\phi_{\#}(v_{N})}
and
MZ_{\phi_{\#}(w_{N})}
must commute as well. Now
5.17
shows that
\phi_{\#}(v_{N})\in\operatorname{Star}\left(\phi_{\#}(w_{N})\right)
; moreover, since cyclic directions have trivial intersection, we get that
\phi_{\#}(v_{N}),\phi_{\#}(w_{N})
are
\overline{X}_{N}
-adjacent, as required.
∎

The proof of
5.19
is now complete.
∎

We now apply the above theorem to the
extended mapping class group
of a five-punctured sphere (where we allow orientation-reversing mapping classes) to prove an analogue of a celebrated theorem of Ivanov
[
29
]
:

Corollary 5.22
.

Let
G=\mathcal{MCG}^{\pm}(S_{5})
be the extended mapping class group of a five-holed sphere. If
N
is deep enough, then
\operatorname{Aut}\left(G_{N}\right)=G_{N}
.

Proof.

We first check that
G
fits the framework of
5.19
. Indeed, in
[
34
, Section 2.3.1]
the first author argued that
G
admits a colourable short HHG structure, where:

•

the support graph
\overline{X}
is the curve graph, which has no valence-one vertices;

•

cyclic directions are the infinite subgroups generated by half Dehn twists.

Now let
G_{N}\to\operatorname{Aut}\left(G_{N}\right)
be the natural map, sending every element to the corresponding inner automorphism, and we want to show that this map is an isomorphism. For surjectivity, let
\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}\left(G_{N}\right)
be an automorphism, and let
\phi_{\#}\in\operatorname{Aut}\left(\overline{X}_{N}\right)
be the associated graph automorphism from
5.19
. By
[
33
, Theorem 5.11]
, the map
G_{N}\to\operatorname{Aut}\left(X_{N}\right)
induced by the action is an isomorphism. In particular there exists
g\in G_{N}
such that
\phi_{\#}
coincides with the action of
g
on
\overline{X}_{N}
. This implies that, for every
v\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
,

\phi(MZ_{v_{N}})=MZ_{g(v_{N})}.

Arguing as in
[
29
, Section 3]
, we will now show that
\phi
coincides with the conjugation by
g
. Indeed, for every
f\in G
,

\phi(MfZ_{v_{N}}f^{-1})=\phi(MZ_{f(v_{N})})=MZ_{gf(v_{N})},

where we used that
fZ_{v_{N}}f^{-1}=Z_{f(v_{N})}
by how half Dehn twists behave under conjugation. However

\phi(MfZ_{v_{N}}f^{-1})=\phi(f)\phi(MZ_{v_{N}})\phi(f)^{-1}=MZ_{\phi(f)g(v_{N})}.

Since the cyclic directions of two distinct vertices of
\overline{X}_{N}
intersect trivially, the above computation shows that
gf(v_{N})=\phi(f)g(v_{N})
for all
v\in\overline{X}^{(0)}
, or in other words
\phi(f)=gfg^{-1}
as automorphisms of
\overline{X}_{N}
. Then again
[
33
, Theorem 5.11]
shows that
\phi(f)
must coincide with
gfg^{-1}
for all
f\in G_{N}
, thus proving that
\phi
is the conjugation by
g
.

We are left to prove injectivity of the map
G_{N}\to\operatorname{Aut}\left(G_{N}\right)
, or equivalently that
G_{N}
has no centre. To see this, notice that, if
g\in G_{N}
and the image of any half Dehn twist commute, then the above arguments show that
g
acts trivially on
\overline{X}_{N}
, and again
[
33
, Theorem 5.11]
implies that
g
must be trivial.
∎

Remark 5.23
.

We can rephrase the above Theorem by saying that the outer automorphism group of
G_{N}
is trivial. However, the fact that
\text{Out}(G_{N})
is
finite
could already be extracted from results in the literature. Indeed, by
[
16
, Theorem 5.8.(2)]
,
G_{N}
is non-elementary hyperbolic, so classical work of Paulin and Bestvina-Feighn, among others
[
37
,
8
]
, proves that
G_{N}
has finite outer automorphism group unless it admits a splitting over virtually cyclic subgroups. The latter does not happen because mapping class groups have Serre’s property (FA) by
[
13
]
, and this property passes to quotients.

6.
Distinguishing braid group and spheres

We conclude the paper by proving that the four strand braid group (resp. the mapping class group of a sphere with five punctures) can be recognised from the braid groups on more strands (resp. the mapping class groups of spheres with more punctures) by its non-elementary hyperbolic quotients. This will prove the two parts of
B
.

We first set up some notation and recall some well-known facts, referring to
[
21
]
for all the details.

The groups
. For
n\geq 4
, let
S_{n}
be a sphere with
k
punctures, let
\mathcal{MCG}(S_{n})
be its usual mapping class group (so we do not allow orientation-reversing homeomorphisms), and let

be the finite-index subgroup of

be the braid group on
n

n

; in turn, forgetting the puncture gives a surjection
\mathcal{MCG}(S_{n+1},*)\to\mathcal{MCG}(S_{n})
, called the

.

Generators
.
B_{n}
is generated by the half Dehn twists
\tau=\tau_{1},\ldots\tau_{n-1}=\tau^{\prime}
on the curves
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n-1}
from the left of
Figure

5
. Furthermore, a (slightly redundant) generating set for the
pure braid group

, which is the finite-index subgroup of

fixing all punctures, is given by the Dehn twists around the curves

\{\eta^{\pm}_{i,j}\}_{1\leq i<j\leq n}
from the right of
Figure

5
(notice that
\gamma_{i}=\eta^{+}_{i,i+1}=\eta^{-}_{i,i+1}
for all

.

Figure 5.

Abelian quotients
. The abelianisation of

, and

inside the abelianisation. As a consequence, the abelianisations of both

Torsion
. Finite-order elements in

are topologically conjugate to rotations, fixing at most two punctures and cyclically permuting the others, and therefore have order at most

[

). Furthermore, the subgroup fixing a pair of disjoint curves is generated by the half Dehn twists around those two curves.

The following is the main technical statement of the section:

Theorem 6.1
.

Let

(resp.

). Then there exists a non-elementary hyperbolic quotient

of
\mathcal{MCG}(S_{5})
(resp.
\mathcal{MCG}(S_{5},*)

has virtually cyclic image.

Before proving

, we show that it implies both parts of

.

Corollary 6.2
.

For every

, there exists a non-elementary hyperbolic quotient

of

such that any homomorphism

Proof.

Let

be the quotient of
\mathcal{MCG}(S_{5})
from

(resp. of
\mathcal{MCG}(S_{5},*)
if

), and let

be the image of
\mathcal{MCG}(S_{5},*)
inside

(resp. let

, hence of

via the capping homomorphism. Furthermore, every map

is in particular a map

Corollary 6.3
.

For every
n\geq 6
, there exists a non-elementary hyperbolic quotient

of
\mathcal{MCG}(S_{5})
such that any homomorphism
\mathcal{MCG}(S_{n})\to Q

Let

be the quotient of

, and

By

, an infinite virtually cyclic group surjects onto either

∎

We will prove both statements of

in parentheses. For the sake of light notation, let

be the curve graph of

and cyclic directions generated by half Dehn twists. Fix a positive integer

, and let

(5)

N^{\prime}=M((n-1)^{2})!\mbox{ and }N=N^{\prime}(5n)!

We require that

is deep enough that the quotients

,

, and

Section

.

We will argue that

, which is non-elementary hyperbolic by

, is the desired quotient. Consider a homomorphism

. We shall consider various cases depending on the image of

, each case corresponding to one of the lemmas below.

Lemma 6.4
.

If
\phi(\tau)
has infinite order then
\phi(H)
is virtually cyclic.

Proof.

Let

be the maximal virtually cyclic subgroup containing

contains the centraliser of

Remark 6.5
.

In the argument above we only used that

and
\tau
commute. Since
\tau
and
\sigma
are conjugate in
H
, there exists
g\in G_{N}
such that

\phi(\sigma)^{2}=g\phi(\tau)^{2}g^{-1}=z_{g(v_{N})}^{r}z_{g(w_{N})}^{s}.

By
5.17
.(
2
),
g
must permute
\{v_{N},w_{N}\}
, so
\phi(\sigma)^{2}\in\langle Z_{v_{N}},Z_{w_{N}}\rangle
. Now let
\sigma^{\prime}
be the half Dehn twist around any of the curves
\eta^{\pm}_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
from
Figure

5
, for
1\leq i^{\prime}\leq 2
and
i^{\prime}<j^{\prime}\leq n
. Since
n\geq 5
,
\sigma^{\prime}
commutes with some curve
\sigma
as above, so the same argument yields that

\phi(\sigma^{\prime})^{2}\in\langle Z_{v_{N}},Z_{w_{N}}\rangle
. Summarising, we proved that
\phi
maps the Dehn twists on the curves from
Figure

5
, which generate the pure braid group, inside the finite subgroup
\langle Z_{v_{N}},Z_{w_{N}}\rangle
, and again this implies that
\phi(H)
is finite.
∎

Lemma 6.9
.

Suppose that
\phi(\tau)
fixes a vertex
v_{N}
of
\overline{X}_{N}
, but does not preserve any edge setwise. Then
\phi(H)
is finite.

Proof.

The proof in this setting is a mixture of the arguments from
6.6
and
6.8
, as we now explain.

Firstly,
6.7
.(
2
) gives that
v_{N}
is the only vertex of
\overline{X}_{N}
fixed by
\phi(\tau)
. Now, for all
i=3,\ldots,n-1
,
\phi(\tau_{i})
is a conjugate of
\phi(\tau)
, so it must fix a single vertex of
\overline{X}_{N}
as well. Moreover, since it commutes with
\phi(\tau)
, it acts on its fixed set in
\overline{X}_{N}
, and therefore the unique fixed point of
\phi(\tau_{i})
is
v_{N}
. Hence we can look at the images of
\phi(\tau)
and
\phi(\tau_{i})
inside the hyperbolic group
H_{v_{N}}
, call them
y
and
y_{i}
, which are conjugate and commute.

We now argue as in
6.6
. The subgroup
\langle y,y_{i}\rangle
does not fix any vertex
w_{N}\in\operatorname{Link}_{\overline{X}_{N}}\left(v_{N}\right)
, so by
5.14
it must be the projection of a finite Abelian subgroup
F\leq H_{v}
. In turn, every finite-order element in
H_{v}
permutes at least two punctures, so
F
injects inside the symmetric group
S_{3}
, and all Abelian subgroups of the latter are cyclic. This shows that
y_{i}\in\langle y\rangle
, and therefore
\phi(\tau_{i})
belongs to the finite group generated by
z_{v}
and any preimage
x
of
y
in
\operatorname{Stab}_{G_{N}}\left(v_{N}\right)
. We can now apply the same argument to the commuting pair
\phi(\tau_{2})
and
\phi(\tau_{n-1})
to get that
\phi(H)=\langle\phi(\tau_{i})\rangle_{1\leq i\leq n-1}
is contained inside
\langle z_{v},x\rangle
, and the latter is finite as it is a finite cyclic extension of a finite cyclic group.
∎

The following Lemma is the last case of
6.1
.

Lemma 6.10
.

Suppose that
\phi(\tau)\in Z_{v_{N}}
, for some vertex
v_{N}
of
\overline{X}_{N}
. Then
\phi(H)
is virtually cyclic.

Proof.

This is the most involved case. Firstly, we consider what happens if
\phi(T)
has large order.

Claim 6.11
.

If
\phi(T)
has order greater than
5
, then
\phi(H)
is virtually cyclic.

Proof of
6.11
.

If
\phi(T)
has infinite order, then
\phi(H)
lies in the centraliser of
\phi(T)
, which is virtually cyclic as
G_{N}
is hyperbolic.

Now assume the order of
\phi(T)
is finite and greater than
5
. Again, torsion elements in
\mathcal{MCG}(S_{5})
have order at most
5
, so
5.11
implies that
\phi(T)
fixes a simplex of
\overline{X}
setwise. In turn, by
6.7
, we find some
1\leq k\leq 3
such that
\phi(T^{k})
is the image of either a multitwist or a power of a half Dehn twist. In the former situation,
\phi(H)
, which centralises
\phi(T^{k})
, must lie in the centraliser of the image of a multitwist, which is finite by
5.18
.

Suppose instead that
\phi(T^{k})\in Z_{w_{N}}
for some
w_{N}\in\overline{X}_{N}^{(0)}
, so that
\phi(H)\leq\operatorname{Stab}_{G_{N}}\left(w_{N}\right)
. Since
\phi(\tau)\in Z_{v_{N}}
and
\phi(T^{k})\in Z_{w_{N}}
commute, the arguments from
5.21
show that
w_{N}\in\operatorname{Star}\left(v_{N}\right)
. Now recall that, for every
i=1,\ldots,n-1
,
\phi(\tau_{i})
is conjugated to
\phi(\tau)
by an element
g_{i}
inside
\phi(H)\leq\operatorname{Stab}_{G_{N}}\left(w_{N}\right)
, so that
\phi(\tau_{i})\in Z_{g_{i}v_{N}}
.

If
v_{N}=w_{N}
, then
g_{i}v_{N}=w_{N}
for all
i
, because each
g_{i}
fixes
w_{N}
. This implies that
\phi(H)\leq Z_{w_{N}}
, which is finite. Suppose instead that
v_{N}\neq w_{N}
, so that
g_{i}v_{N}\in\operatorname{Link}_{\overline{X}_{N}}\left(w_{N}\right)
for all
i
, and we shall derive a contradiction. Notice that at least two
g_{i}v_{N}
must be distinct, as otherwise
\phi(H)\leq Z_{v_{N}}
while
\phi(T^{k})\in Z_{w_{N}}
. This also implies that there exist
i,j
with
|i-j|>1
and
g_{i}v_{N}\neq g_{j}v_{N}
, for otherwise, using that there are at least 5 indices, we readily see that all
g_{i}v_{N}
would coincide. Since
|i-j|>1
,
\phi(\tau_{i})
and
\phi(\tau_{j})
commute, so
g_{i}v_{N}
and
g_{j}v_{N}
, which do not coincide, must be
\overline{X}_{N}
-adjacent by
5.17
.(
1
). However this would violate the fact that
\overline{X}_{N}
has no triangles, as
g_{i}v_{N}
and
g_{j}v_{N}
belong to
\operatorname{Link}_{\overline{X}_{N}}\left(w_{N}\right)
.
∎

In view of
6.11
, we now assume that
\phi
vanishes on
T^{c}
for some
c\leq 5
. Now recall that we set
N^{\prime}=N/(5n)!
in
Equation

5
. The next Claim shows that the composition
H\xrightarrow{\phi}G_{N}\xrightarrow{\pi^{\prime}}G_{N^{\prime}}
has finite image, where
\pi^{\prime}\colon G_{N}\to G_{N^{\prime}}
is the natural projection:

Claim 6.12
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\leq G_{N}
be the subgroup generated by the image of all
N^{\prime}
-th powers of half Dehn Twists. Then
\phi(H)\cap\mathcal{K}
has index at most
(n-1)^{2}
in
\phi(H)
.

Proof of
6.12
.

Let
x\in H
be an
n
-th root of
T
which cyclically permutes all punctures. By
[
11
, Lemma 6.2]
, for every
1\leq i\leq n-1
the subgroup normally generated by
x^{k}
contains the commutator subgroup; furthermore, since
x
is an
n
th root of
T
, it maps to
n-1
inside the abelianisation of
H
, so the index of
\langle\langle x^{k}\rangle\rangle
inside
H
is at most
(n-1)^{2}
.

Now let
t=\phi(x)
, which has order at most
5n
by
6.11
. If
t^{k}
is trivial for some
1\leq i\leq n-1
then
\phi
vanishes on the finite-index subgroup
\langle\langle t^{k}\rangle\rangle
, and therefore
\phi(H)
is finite. Otherwise, suppose that the order of
t
is at least
n
, and in particular
t
is not the image of any finite-order element of
G
(if
n=5
we use that a finite-order element of
\mathcal{MCG}(S_{5},*)
has order at most
4
). Hence
t
preserves a simplex of
\overline{X}_{N}
by
5.11
, and by
6.7
there exist
1\leq q\leq 3
and an edge
\{u_{N},w_{N}\}
of
\overline{X}_{N}
such that
t^{q}\in\langle Z_{u_{N}},Z_{w_{N}}\rangle
.

Since
t
has order at most
5n
, while
Z_{u_{N}}\cong Z_{w_{N}}\cong\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
by
5.15
, we must have that
t^{q}\in\langle N^{\prime}Z_{u_{N}},N^{\prime}Z_{w_{N}}\rangle
. This shows that
\mathcal{K}
contains the normal closure
\langle\langle t^{q}\rangle\rangle
, and since
q\leq 3<n-1
the latter has index at most
(n-1)^{2}
inside
\phi(H)
.
∎

Now, recall that we are working under the assumption that
\phi(\tau)\in Z_{v_{N}}
, and we want to show that
\phi(H)
is virtually cyclic. By
6.12
,
\phi(\tau)^{((n-1)^{2})!}\in\mathcal{K}\cap Z_{v_{N}}
, and the latter coincides with
N^{\prime}Z_{v_{N}}
by
5.15
. Hence
\phi(\tau)\in MZ_{v_{N}}
, where
M
was defined as
N^{\prime}/((n-1)^{2})!
in
Equation

5
. Since all generators of
H
are conjugate, we therefore get that
\phi(H)\leq\langle MZ_{v_{N}}\rangle_{v_{N}\in\overline{X}_{N}^{(0)}}
.

From here, we will show that indeed
\phi(H)\in MZ_{v_{N}}
, concluding the proof. Towards this, the arguments from
[
32
, Section 3]
describe
\langle MZ_{v_{N}}\rangle_{v_{N}\in\overline{X}_{N}^{(0)}}
as the subgroup generated by a
composite rotating family
, a notion introduced by Dahmani in
[
17
]
. We do not need the full power of this machinery: for our purposes, it is enough to know that, for every countable ordinal
\alpha
, there exists a subgroup
N_{\alpha}\leq G_{N}
with the following properties:

•

If
\alpha=0
then
N_{\alpha}=MZ_{v_{N}}
.

•

If
\alpha=\beta+1
for some ordinal
\beta
, then
N_{\alpha}
splits as a graph of groups over
N_{\beta}
;

•

If
\alpha
is a limit ordinal, then
N_{\alpha}=\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}N_{\beta}
.

•

\bigcup_{\alpha}N_{\alpha}=\langle MZ_{v_{N}}\rangle_{v_{N}\in\overline{X}_{N}^{(0)}}
.

Let
\alpha
be the first ordinal such that
\phi(H)\leq N_{\alpha}
. Notice that
\alpha
is not a limit ordinal: indeed, if
\phi(H)\leq N_{\alpha}=\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}N_{\beta}
, then each
\phi(\tau_{i})
belongs to
N_{\beta_{i}}
for some
\beta_{i}<\alpha
, and therefore
\phi(H)\leq N_{\max_{i=1,\ldots,n-2}\beta_{i}}\lneq N_{\alpha}
, against the definition of
\alpha
.

Notice also that
\alpha
cannot be a successor ordinal either. Indeed, if there is
\beta
such that
\alpha=\beta+1
, then
\phi(H)
is contained inside
N_{\beta+1}
but not
N_{\beta}
, and therefore splits as a graph of groups over
\phi(H)\cap N_{\beta}
. However,
\phi(H)
is a quotient of
H
with finite abelianisation, since we are assuming that
\phi
vanishes on a power of
T
; hence
\phi(H)
has Serre’s property (FA), which forbids any non-trivial splitting (see e.g.
[
13
, page 6]
and the arguments therein).

We must therefore have that
\alpha=0
, so that
\phi(H)\leq MZ_{v_{N}}
, as promised. This concludes the proof of
6.10
, and in turn that of
6.1
.
∎

Linking miRNAs to decay
Authors: Amy E. Pasquinelli
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In this Outlook, Pasquinelli discusses a study in this issue of Genes & Development by Grimme et al. that identifies lncRNA tts-2 as a potent trigger RNA for target-directed miRNA degradation of the miR-35-42 family. Pasquinelli highlights how this study expands on our understanding of the base-pairing requirements between trigger RNAs and their target miRNAs for the effective regulation of miRNA abundance.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are tiny, ∼22 nt RNAs that guide Argonaute (AGO) proteins to specific messenger RNAs (mRNAs) for targeted repression (Kim et al. 2025). In animals, recognition of a target mRNA involves partial base-pairing between the miRNA and sequences, usually in the 3′ untranslated region (UTR), of the mRNA. Because a single miRNA can target multiple molecules of the same as well as unrelated mRNAs that share complementarity with the miRNA, the concentration of a given miRNA determines its regulatory potential. Changes in the level of specific miRNAs have been associated with a broad range of diseases, including cancer, heart ailments, and neurodegeneration. Thus, elucidating the mechanisms responsible for making and maintaining specific miRNAs is critical for understanding their roles in health and disease.

The study by Grimme et al. (2026) reveals an elegant process for clearing an entire family of miRNAs at a key developmental transition in the nematode Caenorhabditis elegans. Eight miRNAs, miR-35–42, have identical sequences at their 5′ ends, a region of the miRNA known as the “seed,” and thus are categorized as the miR-35-42 family. Together, members of this family make up >20% of the entire miRNA population in late-stage embryos (Dexheimer et al. 2020). Once the embryos hatch into first-larval-stage worms, there is a coordinated and precipitous decline in all eight miR-35-42 family members. Previous work demonstrated that this dramatic clearance event required the matching seed sequences in the miR-35-42 miRNAs and likely engaged a target-directed miRNA degradation (TDMD) pathway (Shi et al. 2020; Donnelly et al. 2022). TDMD is a mechanism whereby extensive pairing of a miRNA to a target sequence leads to proteolytic degradation of the associated AGO protein (Han and Mendell 2023). A conserved ubiquitin ligase, called ZSWIM8 in mammals and EBAX-1 in C. elegans, is required for committing AGO to proteolysis, which then exposes the bound miRNA for rapid decay. While downregulation of the miR-35-42 miRNAs was shown to depend on EBAX-1, a target RNA capable of inducing TDMD remained elusive (Shi et al. 2020; Donnelly et al. 2022).

Now, the McJunkin laboratory (Grimme et al. 2026) has identified a noncoding RNA, called tts-2, as a potent trigger for subjecting the entire miR-35-42 family of miRNAs to TDMD. Using chimeric enhanced cross-linking immunoprecipitation (eCLIP) to identify endogenous RNAs bound by AGO programmed with miR-35-42, a candidate trigger readily emerged. Cleverly, they zoomed in on sequences that formed chimeras with the miR-35-42 miRNAs that were highly enriched in samples lacking EBAX-1. They reasoned that the trigger–miRNA interaction would be stabilized if TDMD was inactivated. The chimeric reads pointed to a single site in tts-2 that supports seed-pairing to all miR-35-42 family members and another site that contains an imperfect seed but more extensive 3′ pairing to miR-38. Deletion of these sites resulted in stabilization of miR-35-42, confirming tts-2 as the trigger necessary for clearance of these miRNAs at the embryo-to-larval stage transition.

The prevailing model for TDMD holds that the trigger needs to support seed-pairing plus extensive base-pairing to the miRNA 3′ end (Han and Mendell 2023). This architecture is thought to induce a conformational change in AGO that facilitates proteolysis mediated by ZSWIM/EBAX-1. Pairing beyond the miRNA seed is often minimal in mRNA targets that undergo translational inhibition and destabilization, possibly allowing them to escape TDMD. The miR-35-42 example muddies the idea that extensive base-pairing triggers TDMD, while primarily seed complementarity allows for target regulation without affecting miRNA and AGO stability. In addition to previous work (Donnelly et al. 2022), the study by Grimme et al. (2026) further validates that base-pairing to the seed sequence is necessary and sufficient for tts-2 to act as the trigger for target-mediated decay of the miR-35-42 family. Interestingly, the strength of seed-pairing stability emerged as a possible factor allowing for efficient TDMD of the miR-35-42 miRNAs without the need for extensive binding to their 3′ ends. However, it is unclear how tts-2 is able to function as a trigger for TDMD instead of resembling a typical miR-35-42 target. Recognition of specific miRISC complexes by ZSWIM8/EBAX-1 in vivo is still unclear. Stabilization of a particular miRISC confirmation or additional elements in certain triggers, like tts-2, may contribute to the recruitment of ZSWIM8/EBAX-1.

While miRNA decay is actually a by-product of AGO destabilization in TDMD, this mechanism usually has little impact on the entire AGO pool (Han and Mendell 2023). However, because the miR-35-42 miRNAs are so abundant in embryos, the expression of tts-2 in late embryogenesis not only resulted in potent decay of the miRNAs but also reduced levels of AGO (Grimme et al. 2026). This observation lends further support to the concept that TDMD induces downregulation of specific miRNAs, as determined by pairing to a trigger RNA, as well as general AGO protein levels. This is an important consideration when designing artificial triggers to regulate the abundance of specific miRNAs.

In summary, the study by Grimme et al. (2026) not only explains the developmentally coordinated decay of miR-35-42 but also expands the repertoire of potential triggers that can mediate TDMD. The demonstration that strong seed-pairing is sufficient to activate TDMD broadens the search space for candidate triggers. Indeed, a few other studies have shown that TDMD can be induced without extensive 3′ pairing of a miRNA with a trigger sequence in Drosophila (Kingston et al. 2022; Sheng et al. 2023; Hiers et al. 2025). Undoubtedly, more examples will emerge where TDMD involves looser base-pairing requirements than previously understood, and the current study will be foundational for seeking out those triggers and deciphering the mechanisms they use to regulate miRNA abundance.

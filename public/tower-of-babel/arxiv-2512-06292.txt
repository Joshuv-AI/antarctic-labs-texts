Weak exponential metrics for high-dimensional log-correlated Gaussian fields
Andres A. Contreras Hip, Zijie Zhuang

Abstract
For log-correlated Gaussian fields on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} with d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 , Ding-Gwynne-Zhuang (2023) established the existence of subsequential limits of exponential metrics obtained from appropriate approximations. For γ ∈ ( 0 , 2 ​ d ) \gamma\in(0,\sqrt{2d}) , we define a weak γ \gamma -exponential metric to be a map h ↦ D h h\mapsto D_{h} that assigns to a sample of a log-correlated Gaussian field h h a continuous metric on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} satisfying a list of axioms. We prove that every subsequential limit of exponential metrics built from appropriate approximations of h h is a weak γ \gamma -exponential metric in this sense. Moreover, we establish general properties that hold for any weak exponential metric: (1). sharp moment bounds for several natural distances; (2). optimal Hölder exponents when comparing D h D_{h} and the Euclidean metric; and (3). Hausdorff dimension and a KPZ relation. These results extend the two-dimensional Liouville quantum gravity metric theory to higher dimensions. Along the way we derive several useful properties for log-correlated Gaussian fields including the equivalence between white-noise decomposition and convolution, and a shell independence lemma.

High-dimensional Liouville first passage percolation

Liouville first passage percolation
(LFPP) with parameter

\mathbb{C}

e^{\xi h_{\varepsilon}}

[

,

[

\mathbb{R}^{d}

We now discuss the definition of high-dimensional LFPP. Let
d\geq 2
. A whole-space log-correlated Gaussian field (LGF)
\phi

\mathbb{R}^{d}
, defined modulo an additive constant, whose covariance is given by

\mathrm{Cov}\big[(\phi,f_{1}),(\phi,f_{2})\big]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{1}(x)f_{2}(y)\log\frac{1}{|x-y|}\,dx\,dy,

(1.1)

for Schwartz functions

with zero average. See
[
12
,
23
]
for background on LGFs. When

, the LGF coincides with the GFF, whereas for

they are distinct. With a slight abuse of notation, throughout this paper we say that a random generalized function
h
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a

if it has the same law as a whole-space LGF viewed modulo an additive constant. We specify the additive constant when needed.

Consider a radially symmetric real-valued function
\mathsf{K}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

(1.2)

and the associated exponential metric

D_{h}^{\varepsilon}(x,y):=\inf_{P:x\to y}\int_{0}^{1}e^{\xi h_{\varepsilon}^{*}(P(t))}|P^{\prime}(t)|\,dt,\qquad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\mathbb{R}^{d}

[
11
]

1.3
Properties of weak exponential metrics

We now derive properties of weak exponential metrics. Fix
\gamma\in(0,\sqrt{2d})
and recall
\xi
,
Q
, and
\mathsf{d}_{\gamma}
from (
1.5

D_{h}
be a weak
\gamma
-exponential metric, and let
h
be a whole-space LGF with the additive constant chosen so that
h_{1}(0)=0

The next theorem records moment bounds for several types of distances, extending the two-dimensional results in
[
11

.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be connected and open. Then:

1.

Diameters.
For any connected compact set
K\subset U
that is not a singleton,

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sup_{z,w\in K}D_{h}(z,w;U)}}\right)^{p}}}\right]<\infty\qquad\mbox{for all }p\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\infty,\frac{2d\mathsf{d}_{\gamma}}{\gamma^{2}}}}\right).

2.

Set-to-set distance.
For any two disjoint connected compact sets
K_{1},K_{2}\subset U
that are not singletons,

\mathbb{E}\big[(D_{h}(K_{1},K_{2};U))^{p}\big]<\infty\qquad\mbox{for all }p\in\mathbb{R}.

3.

Point-to-set distance.
For any connected compact set
K\subset U
that is not a singleton and any
x\in U\setminus K
,

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\big(D_{h}(x,K;U)\big)^{p}}}\right]<\infty\qquad\mbox{for all }p\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\infty,\frac{2Q\mathsf{d}_{\gamma}}{\gamma}}}\right).

(1.7)

4.

Point-to-point distance.
For any distinct
x,y\in U
,

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\big(D_{h}(x,y;U)\big)^{p}}}\right]<\infty\qquad\mbox{for all }p\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\infty,\frac{2Q\mathsf{d}_{\gamma}}{\gamma}}}\right).

(1.8)

Remark 1.5

The exponents in

Theorem 1.6
.

The constants in Axiom

\mathfrak{c}_{r}=r^{\xi Q}

\big\{r^{-\xi Q}e^{-\xi h_{r}(0)}D_{h}(r\cdot,r\cdot)\big\}_{0<r<\infty}

\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

We next identify the optimal Hölder exponents comparing
D_{h}
with the Euclidean metric, in the spirit of
[
11

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,\sqrt{2d})
and let
D_{h}
be a weak
\gamma
-exponential metric.

1.

The identity map
(\mathbb{R}^{d},|\cdot|)\to(\mathbb{R}^{d},D_{h})
is almost surely locally Hölder continuous with any exponent smaller than
\xi(Q-\sqrt{2d})
, and almost surely not locally Hölder continuous with any exponent larger than
\xi(Q-\sqrt{2d})
. Note that
Q=\tfrac{d}{\gamma}+\tfrac{\gamma}{2}>\sqrt{2d}
.

2.

The identity map
(\mathbb{R}^{d},D_{h})\to(\mathbb{R}^{d},|\cdot|)
is almost surely locally Hölder continuous with any exponent smaller than
\xi^{-1}(Q+\sqrt{2d})^{-1}
, and almost surely not locally Hölder continuous with any exponent larger than
\xi^{-1}(Q+\sqrt{2d})^{-1}
.

Knizhnik-Polyakov-Zamolodchikov (KPZ) relation
[
21
]
gives a quadratic correspondence between Euclidean and quantum scaling dimensions. In two dimensions, a metric KPZ relation for the LQG metric was established in
[
17
]
, relating the Euclidean Hausdorff dimension of a set to its Hausdorff dimension with respect to the LQG metric. We prove the metric KPZ relation for weak exponential metrics in higher dimensions. For
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, write
{\rm dim}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}X
for its Euclidean Hausdorff dimension and
{\rm dim}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\gamma}X
for its

-Hausdorff dimension.

Theorem 1.8

(KPZ relation for weak exponential metrics)
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,\sqrt{2d})
and let
D_{h}
be a weak
\gamma
-exponential metric. If
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a deterministic Borel set or a random Borel set independent of
h
, then almost surely

\displaystyle{\rm dim}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}X=\xi Q\,{\rm dim}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\gamma}X-\frac{\xi^{2}}{2}\big({\rm dim}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\gamma}X\big)^{2},

\displaystyle{\rm dim}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\gamma}X=\frac{1}{\xi}\bigl(Q-\sqrt{Q^{2}-2{\rm dim}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}X}\bigr).

Taking
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}
in Theorem

yields:

Let
\gamma\in(0,\sqrt{2d})
and let

be a weak

1.4
Useful results about log-correlated Gaussian fields

One of the key ingredients in the proof of Theorem
1.3
is the equivalence between the white-noise decomposition of the LGF and convolution with an explicit family of kernels. This extends Lemma 2.4 in
[
18
]
which is for the heat kernel.

Suppose
\mathfrak{K}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a radially symmetric, compactly supported, smooth function satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathfrak{K}(x)^{2}\,dx=1
. Let
W(dy,dt)
denote the space-time white noise on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
. For
\varepsilon>0
, define

\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon}(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}\mathfrak{K}(\frac{x-y}{t})t^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}\,W(dy,dt),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(1.9)

Then
\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon}
can be defined as a random continuous function modulo a global additive constant (see, e.g., Section 4.1.1 of
[
12
]
). Recall the kernel
\mathsf{K}
from Remark
1.1
, which can be obtained explicitly from
\mathfrak{K}
.

Proposition 1.10
.

Let
h
be a whole-space LGF. For any
\varepsilon>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{-d}\mathsf{K}(x/\varepsilon)
. Then
\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon}
agrees in law with
h*\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}
, viewed as a random continuous function modulo a global constant.

See Lemma
2.9
for properties of
\mathsf{K}
and for a proof.

1.5
Proof strategy

The proof adapts the two-dimensional arguments of
[
11
,
15
,
26
,
17
,
10
]
with modifications to handle higher dimensions. We highlight three technical obstacles and explain how we address them.

First, the approximation scheme in
[
7
]
is given by the white-noise decomposition, which is not convenient for verifying the axioms, in particular Axiom
II
(locality). To circumvent this, in Proposition
1.10
we show that the same approximation can be realized as convolution of
h
with an explicit family of kernels, following computations in
[
18
]
. This representation allows us to adapt the arguments of
[
11
,
15
]
to prove Theorem
1.3
.

Second, the annulus independence lemma
[
25
,
16
]
, which is used to propagate regularity events and control the LQG metric (see
[
6
, Section 4.2]
), is specific to the two-dimensional GFF. We therefore prove an analogue for the LGF, namely near independence across shells (Lemma
2.7
). The proof uses the domain Markov property for the LGF based on
d/2
-harmonic extension as described in
[
23
]
. Such near independence is used repeatedly throughout this paper and may be of independent interest for the study of the LGF. Some care is also needed to deal with the spherical average process of the LGF, which does not have the same law as Brownian motion.

Third, planar arguments that force intersections of macroscopic paths do not extend to higher dimensions. In particular, the distance around an annulus used, for example, in
[
11
, Definition 3.7]
does not make sense in higher dimensions, since paths do not disconnect shells. We therefore use the notion of distance around a shell (see (
1.10
)) and use it to build stringing arguments that replace planar intersection arguments.

The organization of the paper is as follows. Section
2
records properties and estimates for log-correlated Gaussian fields, Section
3
proves Theorem
1.3
, Section
4
establishes the properties of weak exponential metrics and proves all results stated in Section
1.3
.

, we prove the shell independence lemma for the LGF, which is the higher-dimensional analog of the annulus independence lemma (see Section 4.2 of
[
6
]
). In Section
2.3
, we show that the white-noise approximations of the log-correlated Gaussian field have the same law as convolutions with an explicit family of kernels. In Section
2.4
, we show that for this family of kernels, the convolution approximation can be localized.

2.1
Basic properties of log-correlated Gaussian fields

We first review the background on
log-correlated Gaussian fields
(LGF). Most of the material in this section is from
[
23
]
and Section 2 of
[
20
]
. We also refer to
[
27
]
for the two-dimensional case, which is the Gaussian free field. We let

d\geq 2\quad\mbox{and}\quad s=\frac{d}{2}\,.

Let
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the space of Schwartz functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Define
\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subset\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as the subspace of Schwartz functions
\phi
satisfying
\hat{\phi}(0)=0
, or equivalently,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x)dx=0
. The space
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of generalized functions is the dual space of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Similarly,
\mathcal{S}_{0}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the dual space of
\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, i.e., the space of generalized functions defined modulo an additive constant. For each
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(resp.
f\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}
)) and
a>-\frac{d}{2}
(resp.
a>-\frac{d+1}{2}
), we define

(-\Delta)^{a}f:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\xi\to(2\pi|\xi|)^{2a}\hat{f}(\xi)].

This is well-defined since
|\xi|^{2a}\hat{f}(\xi)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

We first consider the whole-space LGF, which is a random generalized function defined modulo an additive constant. For all
f,g\in\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, define the inner product

(f,g)_{\mathcal{H}^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\frac{1}{k_{d}}(f,(-\Delta)^{-s}g)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)g(y)\log\frac{1}{|x-y|}dxdy,

where

k_{d}=\frac{2^{1-d}\pi^{-d/2}}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})}.

Let
\mathcal{H}^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be its Hilbert space closure. A whole-space LGF
h
is a random element in
\mathcal{S}_{0}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

(h,\phi)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2})\quad\mbox{for $\phi\in\mathcal{H}^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$}.

We refer to
[
23
]
for well-definedness. Throughout this paper, with slight abuse of notation, we call a random generalized function
h
a whole-space LGF if it has the same law as a whole-space LGF viewed modulo an additive constant. We specify the additive constant when needed. Let
\{h_{r}(z)\}_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ r>0}
denote the spherical average process of
h
, which admits a continuous modification; see Section 11 of
[
23
]
. On many occasions, we choose the global constant of
h
so that
h_{1}(0)=0
.

Next, we consider the LGF defined on an allowable subdomain of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(not necessarily bounded). For an open domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
C_{c}^{\infty}(D)
denote the space of smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that are compactly supported in
D
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{s}(D)
denote the Hilbert space closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(D)
equipped with the inner product

(f,g)_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=k_{d}\,(f,(-\Delta)^{s}g)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Definition 2.1

(Allowable domain)
.

We call an open domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
allowable if, for all
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists
C=C(D,\phi)>0
such that

|(\phi,g)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}|\leq C\|g\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\quad\mbox{for all }g\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{s}(D).

(2.1)

By
[
23
, Lemma 4.2]
, an open domain
D
such that
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus D
contains an open set is allowable.

When
D
is allowable, define the seminorm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-s}(D)}
on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as in
[
23
]
. Specifically, for
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, by (
2.1
) and the Riesz representation theorem there exists a unique
f\in\mathcal{H}_{0}^{s}(D)
such that
(\phi,g)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=(f,g)_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
for all
g\in\mathcal{H}^{s}_{0}(D)
. Define
\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-s}(D)}:=\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. Note that if
\phi
is supported in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus D
, then
f=0
and
\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-s}(D)}=0
. Let
\mathcal{H}^{-s}(D)
denote the Hilbert space closure of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
equipped with
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-s}(D)}
. A LGF
h
on
D
is a random element in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

(h,\phi)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\|\phi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-s}(D)})\quad\mbox{for }\phi\in\mathcal{H}^{-s}(D).

We again refer to
[
23
]
for well-definedness.

We note that when
d
is even,
(-\Delta)^{s}
is a local operator and thus (with
\nabla^{s}
understood as the Fourier multiplier by
(2\pi|\xi|)^{s}
)

\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=k_{d}\,\int_{D}|\nabla^{s}f(x)|^{2}dx\quad\mbox{for all }f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(D).

(2.2)

However, this does not hold when
d
is odd. In the two-dimensional case, the LGF on
D
coincides with the Gaussian free field on
D
as considered in
[
27
]
.

There is another equivalent way to define the LGF using eigenfunctions; see Section 9 of
[
23
]
and Section 2 of
[
20
]
. Let
D
be a bounded domain (hence allowable). Let
\{f_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
denote an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{s}(D)
and define the LGF on
D
as the formal sum

h=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}f_{n},

(2.3)

where
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
are i.i.d. standard Gaussian random variables. Then the above sum converges a.s. in
H^{-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
\varepsilon>0
.

As follows from the definition, the log-correlated Gaussian field has a scaling invariance property.

Lemma 2.2
.

The following holds:

1.

Let
h
be a whole-space LGF with the additive constant chosen so that
h_{1}(0)=0
. Then, for any
r>0
,
h(r\cdot)-h_{r}(0)
has the same law as
h(\cdot)
.

2.

Let
D
be an allowable domain and
r>0
. Let
h_{D}
be a LGF on
D
and
h_{rD}
be a LGF on
rD
. Then
h_{D}(\cdot)
and
h_{rD}(r\cdot)
have the same law.

Given a domain
D
and a generalized function
f
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus D
, we say that a generalized function
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is the
s
-harmonic extension of
f
if it satisfies

\displaystyle f|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus D}=g|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus D}\quad\mbox{and}\quad((-\Delta)^{s}g)|_{D}=0.

The following domain Markov property is taken from Section 5 of
[
23
]
.

Lemma 2.3

(Domain Markov property)
.

Let
U\subset D
be allowable domains. Let
h
be a LGF on
D
, and let
h^{U}
be the
s
-harmonic extension of
h|_{D\setminus U}
into
U
. Then
h-h^{U}
has the same law as a LGF on
D
and is independent of
h|_{D\setminus U}
.

We also have the domain Markov property for the whole-space LGF, again from Section 5 of
[
23
]
.

Lemma 2.4

(Domain Markov property for the whole-space case)
.

Fix
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
. Let
h
be a whole-space LGF with the additive constant so that
h_{r}(z)=0
. For each allowable domain
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
h^{V}
denote the
s
-harmonic extension of
h|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus V}
into
V
. Then,
h-h^{V}
has the same law as a LGF on
V
minus its average over
\partial B_{r}(z)
and is independent of
h|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus V}
.

Proof.

By Section 5 of
[
23
]
, the whole-space LGF
h
has a decomposition
h_{1}+h_{2}
. Here,
h_{1}
is a random generalized function defined up to a global constant, which is
s
-harmonic in
V
and determined by
h|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus V}
. The field
h_{2}
has the same law as a LGF on
V
and is independent of
h|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus V}
. The lemma follows by subtracting from both sides the spherical average over
\partial B_{r}(z)
.
∎

Using the eigenfunction definition, one can establish the Cameron-Martin property of LGF; see Section 2 of
[
20
]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
D
be a bounded open domain and
h
be a LGF on
D
. Fix
f\in\mathcal{H}^{s}_{0}(D)
. Let
\mathbb{P}
denote the law of
h
and
\mathbb{P}_{f}
denote the law of
h+f
. Then,
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{P}_{f}
are absolutely continuous with respect to each other. Furthermore, the Radon-Nikodyn derivative is given by

\frac{d\mathbb{P}}{d\mathbb{P}_{f}}(h)=\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}-k_{d}\,(h,(-\Delta)^{s}f)}}\right)\,.

In particular, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{d\mathbb{P}}{d\mathbb{P}_{f}}(h)}}\right)^{\alpha}}}\right]=\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}+\alpha}{2}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}}}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbb{E}_{f}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{d\mathbb{P}}{d\mathbb{P}_{f}}(h)}}\right)^{\alpha}}}\right]=\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}-\alpha}{2}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}}}\right)\,.

Proof.

By (
2.3
) the law of
h
is the standard centered Gaussian measure on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{s}(D)
. For any
f\in H_{0}^{s}(D)
, the Cameron-Martin theorem for Gaussian measures on Hilbert spaces therefore yields

\frac{d\mathbb{P}}{d\mathbb{P}_{f}}(h)=\exp\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{s}(D)}^{2}-(h,f)_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{s}(D)}\Big)=\exp\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}-k_{d}\,(h,(-\Delta)^{s}f)\Big),

which is exactly the desired formula. The moment identities follow from the fact that

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(h,(-\Delta)^{s}f}}\right)^{2}}}\right]=\|(-\Delta)^{s}f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-s}(D)}=\frac{1}{k_{d}^{2}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.\qed

2.2
Shell independence lemma

The annulus independence lemma for two-dimensional GFF, introduced in
[
25
,
15
]
, is a useful tool to study LQG metric; see Section 4.2 of
[
6
]
. It says that the restrictions of the GFF to disjoint concentric annuli (viewed modulo additive constant) are nearly independent. In this section, we prove the higher-dimensional analog for the LGF: the restrictions of the LGF to disjoint concentric shells (viewed modulo additive constant) are also nearly independent.

Let
h
be a whole-space LGF. For
r>0
, define the
\sigma
-algebra

\mathcal{F}_{r}:=\sigma\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left((h-h_{r}(0))|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r}(0)}}}\right).

Then, for
r^{\prime}>r
, we have
\mathcal{F}_{r^{\prime}}\subset\mathcal{F}_{r}
. Note that
\widetilde{h}:=h-h_{r}(0)
has the law as a whole-space LGF with the additive constant chosen so that the average over
\partial B_{r}(0)
is 0. Let
\widetilde{\mathfrak{h}}^{r}
denote the
s
-harmonic extension of
\widetilde{h}|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{r}(0)}
into
B_{r}(0)
. Then, by Lemma
2.4
,
\widetilde{h}-\widetilde{\mathfrak{h}}^{r}
has the same law as a LGF on
B_{r}(0)
and is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{r}
. For
M>0
,
r>0
, and
q\in(0,1)
, let

\mathcal{G}_{qr}^{r}(M):=\Big\{\sup_{u,v\in B_{qr}(0)}|{\rm D}^{j}\widetilde{\mathfrak{h}}^{r}(u)-{\rm D}^{j}\widetilde{\mathfrak{h}}^{r}(v)|\leq M\quad\mbox{for all $0\leq j\leq\lceil s\rceil$}\Big\},

(2.4)

where
\lceil s\rceil
denotes the smallest integer not less than
s
. The following property of the LGF is a consequence of the Cameron-Martin property (Proposition
2.5
).

Lemma 2.6
.

(Absolute continuity) Fix
0<q<q^{\prime}<1
. For any
M>0
and
\alpha>0
, there exists a constant
C=C(q,q^{\prime},\alpha,M)>0
such that the following holds. Let
h
be a whole-space LGF. On the event
\mathcal{G}_{q^{\prime}r}^{r}(M)
, conditioned on
\mathcal{F}_{r}
, the law of
(h-h_{r}(0))|_{B_{qr}(0)}
is absolutely continuous with respect to its marginal law. Let
H
denote the corresponding Radon-Nikodyn derivative. Then, on the event
\mathcal{G}_{q^{\prime}r}^{r}(M)
,

\max\{\mathbb{E}[H^{-\alpha}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r}],\mathbb{E}[H^{\alpha}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{r}]\}\leq C.

Proof.

Let
\hat{q}=(q+q^{\prime})/2
. Fix a bump function
\psi
that equals 1 in
B_{q}(0)
and is supported in
B_{\hat{q}}(0)
, and define
\psi_{r}(x):=\psi(x/r)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We claim that for any function
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
that is
s
-harmonic in
B_{r}(0)
and satisfies

\sup_{u,v\in B_{q^{\prime}r}(0)}|{\rm D}^{j}g(u)-{\rm D}^{j}g(v)|\leq M\quad\mbox{for all $0\leq j\leq\lceil s\rceil$},

(2.5)

we have

\|\psi_{r}\cdot g\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leq C

(2.6)

for some constant
C=C(\psi,q,q^{\prime},M)>0
. The lemma then follows by applying Proposition
2.5
with
D=B_{\hat{q}r}(0)
and
f=\psi_{r}\cdot\widetilde{\mathfrak{h}}^{r}
.

We now prove (
2.6
). When
d
is even and hence
s
is an integer, Claim (
2.6
) follows from (
2.5
) and (
2.2
). Indeed, by (
2.5
) and the chain rule, we have
|{\rm D}^{s}(\psi_{r}\cdot g)|\leq Cr^{-s}
on
B_{\hat{q}r}(0)
. Combining this with (
2.2
) yields Claim (
2.6
).

When
d
is odd and hence
s
is not an integer, (
2.5
) implies

\big|{\rm D}^{s-1/2}(\psi_{r}\cdot g)\big|\leq Cr^{-s+1/2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\big|{\rm D}^{s+1/2}(\psi_{r}\cdot g)\big|\leq Cr^{-s-1/2}\quad\mbox{on $B_{\hat{q}r}(0)$}.

By interpolation (or Hölder’s inequality in Fourier space),

\|\psi_{r}\cdot g\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\lesssim\|\psi_{r}\cdot g\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s-1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\cdot\|\psi_{r}\cdot g\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s+1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C,

which proves (
2.6
). ∎

We can now state the shell independence lemma for the LGF.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
h
be a whole-space LGF. Fix
q\in(0,1)
and
q^{\prime}>1
. Let
\{r_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be a decreasing sequence of positive numbers such that
r_{k+1}/r_{k}\leq q
for all
k\geq 1
. Let
\{E_{r_{k}}\}_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence of events measurable with respect to
\sigma\big((h-h_{r_{k}}(0))|_{A_{r_{k}/q^{\prime},q^{\prime}r_{k}}(0)}\big)
. For
K\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{N}(K)
be the number of
k\in[1,K]\cap\mathbb{Z}
for which
E_{r_{k}}
occurs.

1.

For any
a>0
and
b\in(0,1)
, there exists
p=p(a,b,q,q^{\prime})\in(0,1)
such that if
\mathbb{P}[E_{r_{k}}]\geq p
for all
k\geq 1
, then

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{N}(K)\geq bK]\geq 1-e^{-aK}\quad\mbox{for all $K\geq 1$}.

2.

For each
p\in(0,1)
, there exists
a>0
and
b\in(0,1)
depending only on
p,q,q^{\prime}
such that the above relation holds.

Lemma
2.7
is a consequence of the following lemma together with Lemma
2.6
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Fix
0<q<1
. Let
\{r_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be a decreasing sequence of positive numbers such that
r_{k+1}/r_{k}\leq q
for all
k\geq 1
. For
K\geq 1
and
M>0
, let
\mathcal{L}(K,M)
be the number of
k\in[1,K]\cap\mathbb{Z}
for which
\mathcal{G}^{r_{k}}_{qr_{k}}(M)
occurs. For each
a>0
and
b\in(0,1)
, there exists
M=M(a,b,q)>0
such that

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{L}(K,M)\geq bK]\geq 1-e^{-aK}\quad\mbox{for all $K\geq 1$}.

Proof.

The proof is identical to the argument in Proposition 4.3 of
[
25
]
. The main inputs there are Lemma 4.4 and the domain Markov property for the GFF, both of which extend to the LGF. Specifically, Lemma 4.4 of
[
25
]
requires Gaussian tail bounds for the oscillation of the harmonic extension. In the case of the LGF, we can show that there exists
c=c(q)>0
such that for all
r>0
and
M>0
,

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathcal{G}^{r}_{qr}(M)^{c}}}\right]\leq c^{-1}e^{-cM^{2}}.

(2.7)

In fact, for
u,v\in B_{r}(0)
,
{\rm D}^{j}\widetilde{\mathfrak{h}}^{r}(u)-{\rm D}^{j}\widetilde{\mathfrak{h}}^{r}(v)
is a centered smooth Gaussian process in
(u,v)
(see
[
23
, Theorem 8.3]
), and (
2.7
) then follows from the Borel-TIS inequality
[
2
,
29
]
. Using the scaling invariance of
h
(Lemma
2.2
), we see that the constant
c
can be chosen independent of
r
. Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
give the desired Markov property for the LGF. Thus, we can follow the argument in Proposition 4.3 of
[
25
]
verbatim. Finally, although Proposition 4.3 of
[
25
]
includes an additional constant
c_{0}(a,b)
, we can absorb it into
a
by choosing
M
sufficiently large.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.7
.

The proof follows the argument in Section 3 of
[
15
]
. The main inputs are Lemmas 3.3 and 3.4 therein, which are generalized by Lemmas
2.6
and
2.8
, respectively.
∎

2.3
Different approximations of the LGF

In this section, we prove Proposition
1.10
, more precisely the version below (Lemma
2.9
) which implies it.

We assume that

\mathfrak{K}
is a radially symmetric, compactly supported, smooth function satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathfrak{K}(x)^{2}dx=1
.

(2.8)

Note that such a function
\mathfrak{K}
is automatically Schwartz. Let
W(dy,dt)
denote the space-time white noise on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
. For
\varepsilon>0
, recall that

\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}\mathfrak{K}(\frac{x-y}{t})t^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}W(dy,dt),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Then
\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon}
can be defined as a random continuous function modulo a global additive constant.

We now state a more precise version of Proposition
1.10
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
h
be a whole-space LGF. For any
\mathfrak{K}
satisfying (
2.8
), set

\mathsf{K}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\!\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\,\zeta\mapsto\sqrt{\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)}\int_{|\zeta|}^{\infty}t^{d-1}\,|\hat{\mathfrak{K}}(t)|^{2}\,dt}}}\right),

which is a radially symmetric real-valued function with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathsf{K}(x)\,dx=1
and satisfies

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\max\{|\mathsf{K}(x)|,|x|^{2d-1}|\mathsf{K}(x)|\}<\infty.

(2.9)

Moreover, when
d
is even,
\mathsf{K}
is Schwartz. For
\varepsilon>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{-d}\mathsf{K}(x/\varepsilon)
. Then
\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon}
agrees in law with
h*\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}
, viewed as a random continuous function modulo a global constant.

Recall from (
1.3
) that
D_{h}^{\varepsilon}
is the exponential metric defined with respect to
h_{\varepsilon}^{*}=h*\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}
. The above lemma combined with
[
7
, Theorem 1.2]
implies that the rescaled exponential metrics
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{h}^{\varepsilon}
are tight in the local uniform topology. Furthermore, each possible subsequential limit is a continuous metric. We refer to Lemma
3.3
for details.

Proof of Lemma
2.9
.

We proceed as in Lemma 2.4 in
[
18
]
. Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be compactly supported and smooth such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(z)\,dz=0.

Step 1: Covariance of the white-noise approximation.
Let
R>\varepsilon
, and define the truncated field

\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon,R}(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\varepsilon}^{R}\mathfrak{K}(\frac{y-x}{t})t^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}W(dy,dt),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Then

\mathrm{Var}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(z)\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon,R}(z)dz}}\right)=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(z)\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon,R}(z)dz}}\right)^{2}}}\right),

since
\mathbb{E}\big(\int f(z)\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon,R}(z)\,dz\big)=0
. By the definition of
\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon,R}
and the translation invariance of white noise,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(z)\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon,R}(z)\,dz}}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)f(y)\,\kappa_{\varepsilon,R}(x-y)\,dx\,dy,

(2.10)

where

\kappa_{\varepsilon,R}(x-y):=\mathbb{E}\big[\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon,R}(x)\,\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon,R}(y)\big].

A direct computation using the definition of white noise yields

\kappa_{\varepsilon,R}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\varepsilon}^{R}t^{-(d+1)}\mathfrak{K}\!\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{x-u}{t}}}\right)\mathfrak{K}\!\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{u}{t}}}\right)\,dt\,du.

(2.11)

Let
\mathcal{F}
denote the Fourier transform,

\mathcal{F}(f)(\zeta)=\hat{f}(\zeta):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\zeta}f(x)\,dx.

Using Plancherel’s theorem and the fact that
\mathcal{F}(f\ast g)=\hat{f}\hat{g}
, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)f(y)\kappa_{\varepsilon,R}(x-y)dxdy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hat{\kappa}_{\varepsilon,R}(\zeta)|\hat{f}(\zeta)|^{2}d\zeta.

From (
2.11
),

\displaystyle\hat{\kappa}_{\varepsilon,R}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\varepsilon}^{R}t^{-(d+1)}\mathcal{F}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(x\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathfrak{K}(\frac{x-u}{t})\mathfrak{K}(\frac{u}{t})du}}\right)(\zeta)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\varepsilon}^{R}t^{-(d+1)}t^{2d}|\hat{\mathfrak{K}}(t\xi)|^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\varepsilon}^{R}t^{d-1}|\hat{\mathfrak{K}}(t\zeta)|^{2}\,dt.

Since
\mathfrak{K}
is Schwartz,
\hat{\mathfrak{K}}
is also Schwartz and
|\hat{\mathfrak{K}}(x)|\leq C|x|^{-d}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This implies that the last integral converges for all
\zeta\neq 0
, and moreover

\hat{\kappa}_{\varepsilon}(\zeta):=\lim_{R\to\infty}\hat{\kappa}_{\varepsilon,R}(\zeta)=\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}t^{d-1}|\hat{\mathfrak{K}}(t\zeta)|^{2}dt.

(2.12)

Thus,

\mathrm{Var}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(z)\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon}(z)dz}}\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hat{\kappa}_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)|\hat{f}(\zeta)|^{2}d\zeta.

(2.13)

Since
\mathfrak{K}
is radial, it follows from (
2.11
) that
\kappa_{\varepsilon,R}
is radial and positive definite. Therefore,
\hat{\kappa}_{\varepsilon}
is radial and non-negative.

Step 2: Covariance of the convolution approximation.
By the covariance of the LGF in (
1.1
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(z)h\ast\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}(z)dz}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)f(y)(h\ast\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon})(x)(h\ast\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon})(y)dxdy}}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)f(y)\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}(x-u)\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}(y-v)\log\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{|u-v|}}}\right)dudvdxdy.

(2.14)

Let

F_{x}(v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}(x-u)\log\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{|u-v|}}}\right)du.

Then

\hat{F}_{x}(\zeta)=\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}(x-\cdot))\frac{c_{d}}{|\zeta|^{d}}=\frac{c_{d}}{|\zeta|^{d}}e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\zeta}\hat{\mathsf{K}}_{\varepsilon}(-\zeta),

where
c_{d}=\tfrac{\Gamma(d/2)}{2\pi^{d/2}}
is the constant such that

\mathcal{F}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\log\frac{1}{|x|}}}\right)(\zeta)=c_{d}|\zeta|^{-d}\quad\mbox{for $\zeta\neq 0$.}

Hence,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(z)h\ast\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}(z)dz}}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)f(y)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}(x-u)F_{y}(u)du}}\right)dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)f(y)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{c_{d}}{|\zeta|^{d}}e^{2\pi i(y-x)\cdot\zeta}|\hat{\mathsf{K}}_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)|^{2}d\zeta}}\right)dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\hat{f}(\zeta)|^{2}\frac{c_{d}}{|\zeta|^{d}}|\hat{\mathsf{K}}_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)|^{2}d\zeta.

Step 3: Choosing the kernel.
Choose
{\mathsf{K}}_{\varepsilon}
such that

\frac{c_{d}|\hat{\mathsf{K}}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{|\zeta|^{d}}=\hat{\kappa}_{\varepsilon},\quad\mbox{i.e.,}\quad{\mathsf{K}}_{\varepsilon}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\zeta\mapsto\sqrt{\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)}|\zeta|^{d}\hat{\kappa}_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)}}}\right)\,.

(2.15)

With this choice,
\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon}
agrees in law with
h*\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}
when viewed as a random continuous function modulo an additive constant.

Since
|{\rm D}^{j}(\sqrt{|\zeta|^{d}\hat{\kappa}_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)})|\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
0\leq j\leq 2d-1
(Lemma
2.10
), we obtain

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\max\,\{|\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}(x)|,|x|^{2d-1}|\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}(x)|\}<\infty.

Moreover, when
d
is even,
\sqrt{|\zeta|^{d}\hat{\kappa}_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)}
is Schwartz (Lemma
2.10
), hence
\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}
is Schwartz. By definition,
\hat{\kappa}_{\varepsilon}
is radial and non-negative, so
{\mathsf{K}}_{\varepsilon}
is radial and real-valued. Moreover, by (
2.12
) and polar coordinates,

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathsf{K}}_{\varepsilon}(0)=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{d-1}|\hat{\mathfrak{K}}(t)|^{2}\,dt}=\sqrt{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\hat{\mathfrak{K}}(x)|^{2}\,dx}.

Using Plancherel’s theorem and
\int_{\mathbb{R^{d}}}\mathfrak{K}(x)^{2}\,dx=1
, we see that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}(x)\,dx=\widehat{\mathsf{K}}_{\varepsilon}(0)=1
.

It follows from (
2.12
) that
\hat{\kappa}_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)=\varepsilon^{d}\hat{\kappa}_{1}(\varepsilon\zeta)
for
\zeta\neq 0
. Therefore, from (
2.15
), we have
\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{-d}\mathsf{K}_{1}(x/\varepsilon)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Taking
\mathsf{K}=\mathsf{K}_{1}
completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{K}
satisfies (
2.8
). For
\varepsilon>0
, let

\hat{\kappa}_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)=\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}t^{d-1}|\hat{\mathfrak{K}}(t\zeta)|^{2}dt\quad\mbox{and}\quad o(\zeta):=\sqrt{|\zeta|^{d}\hat{\kappa}_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)},\quad\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Then, for all
0\leq j\leq 2d-1
,
|{\rm D}^{j}o|\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, when
d
is even,
o(\zeta)
is a Schwartz function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

To prove Lemma
2.10
, we need the following result.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
f:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a non-negative function such that for any integers
\alpha,n\geq 0
,
\sup_{r>0}|r^{\alpha}f^{(n)}(r)|<\infty
. Let

g(r)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{r}^{\infty}f(s)ds}}\right)^{1/2},\quad r>0.

Then for any integers
\alpha,n\geq 0
,
\sup_{r>0}|r^{\alpha}g^{(n)}(r)|<\infty
.

Proof.

The constants
c
below may change from line to line but are independent of
f
. We first prove by induction that, for integers
n\geq 1
,

\int_{x}^{\infty}f(y)dy\geq c_{n}\frac{f(x)^{1+\tfrac{1}{n}}}{\big(\sup_{y\geq x}|f^{(n)}(y)|\big)^{\tfrac{1}{n}}},\quad x>0,

(2.16)

where
c_{n}>0
depends only on
n
. The case
n=1
follows from

f(w)\geq\frac{1}{2}f(x),\quad x<w<x+\frac{f(x)}{2\sup_{y\geq x}|f^{(1)}(y)|}.

Suppose (
2.16
) holds for
n=k
. We claim that

\int_{x}^{\infty}f(y)dy\geq c\frac{|f^{(k)}(x)|^{k+2}}{\big(\sup_{y\geq x}|f^{(k+1)}(y)|\big)^{k+1}},\quad x>0.

(2.17)

Indeed,

|f^{(k)}(w)|\geq\frac{1}{2}|f^{(k)}(x)|,\quad x<w<x+\frac{|f^{(k)}(x)|}{2\sup_{y\geq x}|f^{(k+1)}(y)|}.

Integrating over
w
iteratively, this implies that for
0\leq l\leq k
,

|f^{(l)}(w)|\geq c\frac{|f^{(k)}(x)|^{k-l+1}}{\big(\sup_{y\geq x}|f^{(k+1)}(y)|\big)^{k-l}}

for a positive fraction of
w
in
(x,x+\frac{|f^{(k)}(x)|}{2\sup_{y\geq x}|f^{(k+1)}(y)|})
. Taking
l=0
yields (
2.17
). Furthermore, (
2.17
) implies that for any
w\geq x
,

|f^{(k)}(w)|^{k+2}\leq c\int_{w}^{\infty}f(y)dy\,\big(\sup_{y\geq w}|f^{(k+1)}(y)|\big)^{k+1}\leq c\int_{x}^{\infty}f(y)dy\,\big(\sup_{y\geq x}|f^{(k+1)}(y)|\big)^{k+1}.

Therefore,

\int_{x}^{\infty}f(y)dy\geq c\frac{\big(\sup_{y\geq x}|f^{(k)}(y)|\big)^{k+2}}{\big(\sup_{y\geq x}|f^{(k+1)}(y)|\big)^{k+1}}.

(2.18)

Finally, (
2.16
) for
n=k+1
follows from the case
n=k
and (
2.18
):

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{\infty}f(y)dy

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{k(k+2)+1}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\underbrace{c_{k}\frac{f(x)^{1+\tfrac{1}{k}}}{\big(\sup_{y\geq x}|f^{(k)}(y)|\big)^{\tfrac{1}{k}}}+\ldots+c_{k}\frac{f(x)^{1+\tfrac{1}{k}}}{\big(\sup_{y\geq x}|f^{(k)}(y)|\big)^{\tfrac{1}{k}}}}_{k(k+2)\mbox{ copies}}+c\frac{\big(\sup_{y\geq x}|f^{(k)}(y)|\big)^{k+2}}{\big(\sup_{y\geq x}|f^{(k+1)}(y)|\big)^{k+1}}}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq c^{\prime}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(f(x)^{(1+\tfrac{1}{k})k(k+2)}\frac{1}{\big(\sup_{y\geq x}|f^{(k+1)}(y)|\big)^{k+1}}}}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{k(k+2)+1}}=c^{\prime}\frac{f(x)^{1+\tfrac{1}{k+1}}}{\big(\sup_{y\geq x}|f^{(k+1)}(y)|\big)^{\tfrac{1}{k+1}}}.

Similarly, using (
2.17
), (
2.18
) and an induction, for all integers
k\geq 0
and
m\geq 1
we have

\int_{x}^{\infty}f(y)dy\geq c_{k,m}\frac{|f^{(k)}(x)|^{\tfrac{k+1+m}{m}}}{\big(\sup_{y\geq x}|f^{(k+m)}(y)|\big)^{\tfrac{k+1}{m}}},\quad x>0,

Since
m
can be taken arbitrarily large, this implies that the decay rate of
g(r)^{2}=\int_{r}^{\infty}f(s)ds
at infinity is at least as fast as that of
|f^{(k)}(r)|
for each
k
. Using that the decay rate of
|f^{(k)}(r)|
is faster than any power of
r
, it follows that the derivatives of
g
also decay faster than any power of
r
, since the derivatives of
g
are of the form

g^{(n)}(r)=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{1}{g(r)^{2m-1}}\sum_{0\leq a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\ldots\leq a_{m}\leq n-1}c_{m,a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}f^{(a_{i})}(r).\qed

Proof of Lemma
2.10
.

By definition and changing variables
s=t|\zeta|
,

o(\zeta)=\sqrt{|\zeta|^{d}\hat{\kappa}_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)}=\sqrt{\int_{\varepsilon|\zeta|}^{\infty}s^{d-1}|\hat{\mathfrak{K}}(s)|^{2}ds}.

Since
s^{d-1}|\hat{\mathfrak{K}}(s)|^{2}
is smooth on
(0,\infty)
, it follows that
o(\zeta)
is smooth on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
. Furthermore, Lemma
2.11
implies that the derivatives of
o(\zeta)
have decay rate at infinity faster than
|\zeta|^{-\alpha}
for any integers
\alpha\geq 0
.

It remains to analyze the behavior near
\zeta=0
. Since
|\hat{\mathfrak{K}}(s)|^{2}
is radial and smooth at 0, it admits an expansion of the form

|\hat{\mathfrak{K}}(s)|^{2}=\sum_{m\geq 0}a_{m}s^{2m}\quad\mbox{for small $s$}.

Hence

\int_{\varepsilon|\zeta|}^{\infty}s^{d-1}|\hat{\mathfrak{K}}(s)|^{2}ds=\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}|\hat{\mathfrak{K}}(s)|^{2}ds+\sum_{m\geq 0}b_{m}(\varepsilon)|\zeta|^{d+2m}

for suitable coefficients
b_{m}(\varepsilon)
, and thus

o(\zeta)=\sqrt{\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{d-1}|\hat{\mathfrak{K}}(s)|^{2}ds+\sum_{m\geq 0}b_{m}(\varepsilon)|\zeta|^{d+2m}}=C+\sum_{m\geq 0}b_{m}^{\prime}(\varepsilon)|\zeta|^{d+2m},

with
C>0
. When
d
is even, this expansion shows that
o(\zeta)
is smooth at 0 and hence Schwartz. When
d
is odd,
o(\zeta)
is not necessarily smooth at 0, but for any multi-index
\alpha
,

|\partial^{\alpha}o(\zeta)|\leq C^{\prime}|\zeta|^{d-|\alpha|}\quad\mbox{for small }|\zeta|.

In particular, for
0\leq j\leq 2d-1
,
|{\rm D}^{j}o|\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
∎

2.4
Localization of the LGF

In this section, we show that the convolution considered in Lemma
2.9
can be localized through truncation (Lemma
2.12
). Throughout this section, let
h
be a whole-space LGF with the additive constant chosen such that
h_{1}(0)=0
.

Define

h_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)=h\ast{\mathsf{K}}_{\varepsilon}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(w){\mathsf{K}}_{\varepsilon}(z-w)dw,

(2.19)

and

\bar{h}_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)=h\ast(\Psi_{\varepsilon}{\mathsf{K}}_{\varepsilon})(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(w){\mathsf{K}}_{\varepsilon}(z-w)\Psi_{\varepsilon}(z-w)dw,

(2.20)

where
\Psi_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,1]
is a radial bump function such that
\Psi_{\varepsilon}(z)=1
if
z\in B_{\sqrt{\varepsilon}/2}(0)
and
\Psi_{\varepsilon}(z)=0
if
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(0).
We have the following comparison between
h_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}
and
\bar{h}_{\varepsilon}^{*}.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
h
be a whole-space LGF with the additive constant chosen so that
h_{1}(0)=0
, and let
\mathsf{K}
be defined as in Lemma
2.9
. Then for each bounded open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},
a.s.

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{z\in\overline{U}}|h_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)-\bar{h}_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)|=0.

To prove Lemma
2.12
, we will need the following technical lemma. Recall that by
[
23
, Section 11.1]
the sphere average process
\{h_{r}(z)\}_{r>0,z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is well defined and admits a modification continuous in both
r
and
z
.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
h
be a whole-space LGF with the additive constant chosen so that
h_{1}(0)=0
, and let
\{h_{r}(z)\}_{r>0,z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
be its sphere average process. For each
R>0,

\zeta>0,
a.s. we have

\sup_{z\in B_{R}(0)}\sup_{r>0}\frac{|h_{r}(z)|}{\max\{\log\frac{1}{r},(\log r)^{\frac{1}{2}+\zeta},1\}}<\infty.

We now prove Lemma
2.12
assuming Lemma
2.13
.

Proof of Lemma
2.12
.

Using polar coordinates and radiality of
{\mathsf{K}}_{\varepsilon}
, we have

h_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)=\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{d-1}h_{r}(z){\mathsf{K}}_{\varepsilon}(r)dr,

\bar{h}_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)=\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{d-1}h_{r}(z)\Psi_{\varepsilon}(r){\mathsf{K}}_{\varepsilon}(r)dr.

Therefore,

|h_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)-\bar{h}_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)|\leq\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)}\,\int_{\sqrt{\varepsilon}/2}^{\infty}r^{d-1}\,|h_{r}(z)|\,|{\mathsf{K}}_{\varepsilon}(r)|\,dr.

By Lemma
2.13
(taking
\zeta=1/2
), there exists a random constant
C
such that

|h_{r}(z)|\leq C\max\{\log\frac{1}{r},\log r,1\}

for all
z\in\overline{U}
and
r>0
. Hence

\sup_{z\in\overline{U}}|h_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)-\bar{h}_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)|\leq C\int_{\sqrt{\varepsilon}/2}^{\infty}r^{d-1}\max\{\log\frac{1}{r},\log r,1\}\varepsilon^{-d}|\mathsf{K}(r/\varepsilon)|\,dr.

Substituting
r=\varepsilon t
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\overline{U}}|h_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)-\bar{h}_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}}}^{\infty}t^{d-1}\max\{\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon t},\log\varepsilon t,1\}\,|\mathsf{K}(t)|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}}}^{\infty}t^{d-1}\,\sqrt{t}\,|\mathsf{K}(t)|\,dt

By Lemma
2.9
, there exists
C^{\prime}>0
such that
|{\mathsf{K}}(x)|\leq C^{\prime}|x|^{-2d+1}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Combining this with the above inequality,

\sup_{z\in\overline{U}}|h_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)-\bar{h}_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)|\leq C^{\prime\prime}\int_{\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}}}^{\infty}t^{-d+1/2}\,dt,

which tends to
0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
for all
d\geq 2
. This proves the lemma. ∎

We now prove Lemma
2.13
. We will need the following estimate.

Lemma 2.14
.

For all
R>0
and
\zeta>0,
we have a.s.

\lim_{r\to\infty}\sup_{z\in B_{R}(0)}\frac{|h_{r}(z)|}{(\log r)^{\frac{1}{2}+\zeta}}=0.

Proof.

The process
\{h_{r}(z)-h_{r}(0):z\in B_{R}(0),r\in[\frac{1}{2},1]\}
is a centered Gaussian process with variances bounded above by an
R
-dependent constant. By
[
23
, Section 11.1]
, this process has a continuous modification. Therefore,

\sup_{z\in B_{R}(0)}\sup_{r\in[\frac{1}{2},1]}|h_{r}(z)-h_{r}(0)|<\infty.

By the Borel-TIS inequality
[
2
,
29
]
(see e.g.
[
1
, Theorem 2.1.1]
), we have

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sup_{z\in B_{R}(0)}\sup_{r\in[\frac{1}{2},1]}|h_{r}(z)-h_{r}(0)|}}\right)<\infty

and

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sup_{z\in B_{R}(0)}\sup_{r\in[\frac{1}{2},1]}|h_{r}(z)-h_{r}(0)|>A}}\right)\leq c^{-1}e^{-cA^{2}}

for some constant
c>0
only depending on
R
. Using the scaling invariance of
h
(Lemma
2.2
), we obtain

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sup_{z\in B_{R2^{k}}(0)}\sup_{r\in[2^{k-1},2^{k}]}|h_{r}(z)-h_{r}(0)|>A}}\right)\leq c^{-1}e^{-cA^{2}}

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Applying this with
A=k^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\zeta}{2}}
and the Borel-Cantelli lemma, we obtain

\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{z\in B_{R2^{k}}(0)}\sup_{r\in[2^{k-1},2^{k}]}\frac{h_{r}(z)-h_{r}(0)}{(\log r)^{\frac{1}{2}+\zeta}}=0\quad\mbox{a.s.}

Finally, using that
\frac{h_{r}(0)}{(\log r)^{\frac{1}{2}+\zeta}}\to 0
as
r\to\infty
(as can be seen from direct computation), we obtain the result.
∎

Proof of Lemma
2.13
.

By
[
19
,
3
]
, we have a.s.

\sup_{z\in B_{R}(0)}\sup_{r\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]}\frac{|h_{r}(z)|}{\log\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{r}}}\right)}<\infty.

By continuity of the sphere average process, for any fixed

Combining these bounds with Lemma
2.14
and using that

controls
|h_{r}(z)|
for large

.
∎

In this section, we prove Theorem

satisfy Axioms
I
—
V
. The proof mainly follows the argument in
[
11
]
(see also
[
26
]
) with modifications to handle higher dimensions. In this section, we use
h
to denote a whole-space LGF (we specify the choice of additive constant when needed), and
\mathsf{h}
to denote a whole-space LGF plus a bounded continuous function. Throughout this section, we fix
\mathfrak{K}
satisfying (

\mathsf{K}
as in Lemma

.

3.1
Convergence among dyadic boxes, Weyl scaling, and tightness
across scales

In this section, we consider a whole-space LGF plus a bounded continuous function
\mathsf{h}
. Let
D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon}
be the exponential metric associated with
\mathsf{h}_{\varepsilon}^{*}
defined as in (
1.3
) with
\mathsf{h}
in place of
h
, and recall
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}
from (

) which depends on
\mathsf{h}
. In fact, we will show in the proof of Lemma
3.3
that for different
\mathsf{h}
, the normalizing constants

are up-to-constants equivalent as
\varepsilon\to 0
. For an open domain
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot;U)
denote the
D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon}
-internal metric on

. We call a domain of the form

\mathcal{W}
be the set of all dyadic domains.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathsf{h}
be a whole-space LGF plus a bounded continuous function. Then the following hold.

1.

As

\mathbb{R}^{d}

2.

For every sequence
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
tending to 0, there exists a subsequence
\mathcal{E}
such that along
\mathcal{E}
,

(\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon},\{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot;W)\}_{W\in\mathcal{W}})\to(\widetilde{D},\{\widetilde{D}_{W}\}_{W\in\mathcal{W}})

where the convergence is in the local uniform topology on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and on
W\times W
, respectively. Moreover, for all
W\in\mathcal{W}
,
\widetilde{D}_{W}
is a continuous metric on
W
, and we have

\widetilde{D}(\cdot,\cdot;W)=\widetilde{D}_{W}(\cdot,\cdot;W),

(3.1)

where
\widetilde{D}(\cdot,\cdot;W)
is the
\widetilde{D}
-internal metric on
W
and
\widetilde{D}_{W}(\cdot,\cdot;W)
is the
\widetilde{D}_{W}
-internal metric on
W
.

Remark 3.2
.

In the two-dimensional case
[
11
]
, it is known that
\widetilde{D}_{W}
is a length metric on

, i.e.,
\widetilde{D}_{W}(\cdot,\cdot;W)=\widetilde{D}_{W}(\cdot,\cdot)
. The main obstacle to extending this result to higher dimensions lies in establishing the tightness of
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot;\overline{W})
in the uniform topology on
W\times W
. In
[
7
]
, only tightness in the local uniform topology was obtained, whereas tightness in the uniform topology was proved in two dimensions in
[
5
]
. Nevertheless, we expect that the tightness result in the uniform topology can be achieved by adapting the arguments in
[
7
]
. If so, this would imply that
\widetilde{D}_{W}

We first tailor Theorem 1.2 of
[
7
]
to show the tightness of
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathsf{h}
be a whole-space log-correlated Gaussian field plus a bounded continuous function. Then as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the family of rescaled metrics
\{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot)\}_{\varepsilon>0}
is tight with respect to the local uniform topology on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Moreover, any subsequential limit is supported on continuous metrics on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon,1}
(resp.
\mathfrak{h}_{1,\infty}
) denote the variant of
\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon}
in (
1.9
) obtained by integrating over the time interval
(\varepsilon,1)
(resp.
(1,\infty)
) rather than
(\varepsilon,\infty)
. Then
\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon,1}
is a random continuous function and
\mathfrak{h}_{1,\infty}
is a random continuous function viewed modulo a global constant. Let
\hat{D}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\varepsilon}
be the exponential metric defined with respect to
\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon,1}
, and let
\hat{\mathsf{a}}_{\varepsilon}
be the median of
\hat{D}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\varepsilon}(0,e_{1})
. As proved in Theorem 1.2 of
[
7
]
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
the family of rescaled metrics
\hat{\mathsf{a}}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\hat{D}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot)
is tight with respect to the local uniform topology on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Moreover, any subsequential limit is supported on continuous metrics on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Let
h
be a whole-space LGF with the additive constant chosen so that
h_{1}(0)=0
. We now explain how to extend the tightness result to
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{h}^{\varepsilon}
. The key ingredient is Lemma
2.9
, which states that
h*\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon}=\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon,1}+\mathfrak{h}_{1,\infty}
have the same law when viewed as random continuous functions modulo a global constant. Therefore, there exists a coupling of
h
and the white noise such that

h*\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}-\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon,1}

is a random continuous function whose law is independent of
\varepsilon
. Note that the coupling may depend on
\varepsilon
. With slight abuse of notation, we still use
\mathfrak{h}_{1,\infty}
to denote
h*\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}-\mathfrak{h}_{\varepsilon,1}
. We now work with this coupling.

By
[
7
, Lemma 6.7]
, for any
r>0
and
p\in(0,1)
, there exists
R>r
(depending on
r,p
) such that

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sup_{u,v\in B_{r}(0)}\hat{D}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\varepsilon}(u,v)<\hat{D}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\varepsilon}(\partial B_{r}(0),\partial B_{R}(0))}}\right]\geq p.

On this event, the
\hat{D}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\varepsilon}
-geodesic between any two points
u,v\in B_{r}(0)
cannot cross
A_{r,R}(0)=B_{R}(0)\setminus\overline{B_{r}(0)}
and thus lies in
B_{R}(0)
. Therefore, for all
u,v\in B_{r}(0)
,

D_{h}^{\varepsilon}(u,v)\leq\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\xi\sup_{w\in B_{R}(0)}\mathfrak{h}_{1,\infty}(w)}}\right)\hat{D}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\varepsilon}(u,v).

(3.2)

Furthermore, on the same event, by considering whether the
D_{h}^{\varepsilon}
-geodesic between
u,v
crosses
A_{r,R}(0)
, for all
u,v\in B_{r}(0)
we have

\displaystyle D_{h}^{\varepsilon}(u,v)

\displaystyle\geq\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\xi\inf_{w\in B_{R}(0)}\mathfrak{h}_{1,\infty}(w)}}\right)\min\{\hat{D}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\varepsilon}(u,v),\hat{D}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\varepsilon}(\partial B_{r}(0),\partial B_{R}(0))\}

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\xi\inf_{w\in B_{R}(0)}\mathfrak{h}_{1,\infty}(w)}}\right)\hat{D}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\varepsilon}(u,v),

where in the last equality we use
\sup_{u,v\in B_{r}(0)}\hat{D}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\varepsilon}(u,v)<\hat{D}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\varepsilon}(\partial B_{r}(0),\partial B_{R}(0))
.

Combining (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), it follows that as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the ratios
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}/\hat{\mathsf{a}}_{\varepsilon}
are bounded from above and below, and the family of rescaled metrics
\{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{h}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot)\}_{\varepsilon>0}
is tight with respect to the local uniform topology on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

By
[
7
, Proposition 6.4]
, for any
R>r>0
and
p\in(0,1)
, there exists
c=c(r,R,p)>0
such that

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\hat{D}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\varepsilon}(\partial B_{r}(x),\partial B_{R}(x))\geq c\hat{\mathsf{a}}_{\varepsilon}}}\right]\geq p\quad\mbox{for all $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$}.

Combining this with

D_{h}^{\varepsilon}(\partial B_{r}(x),\partial B_{R}(x))\geq\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\xi\inf_{w\in B_{R}(x)}\mathfrak{h}_{1,\infty}(w)}}\right)\hat{D}_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\varepsilon}(\partial B_{r}(x),\partial B_{R}(x))

(3.4)

implies that every subsequential limit of
\{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{h}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot)\}_{\varepsilon>0}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
is a continuous metric, i.e., induces the Euclidean topology. This proves the lemma for the whole-space LGF. The case of a whole-space LGF plus a bounded continuous function can be proved similarly, since the analogs of (
3.2
), (
3.3
), and (
3.4
) hold.
∎

The following lemma is used to show that any subsequential limit of
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon}
is a length metric. It follows from Lemma
3.3
and the scaling invariance of the whole-space LGF (Lemma
2.2
).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathsf{h}
be a whole-space LGF plus a bounded continuous function. For each
p\in(0,1)
and
C>0
, there exists a constant
R>0
, depending on
p,C
, and the law of
\mathsf{h}
, such that for all
r>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sup_{u,v\in B_{r}(x)}D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon}(u,v)\leq\frac{1}{C}D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon}(\partial B_{r}(x),\partial B_{Rr}(x))}}\right]\geq p.

(3.5)

Proof.

We first consider the case of the whole-space LGF
h
. By Lemma
3.3
, for each
p\in(0,1)
and
C>0
, there exists
R>0
such that

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sup_{u,v\in B_{1/R}(0)}\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{h}^{\varepsilon}(u,v)\leq\frac{1}{C}\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{h}^{\varepsilon}(\partial B_{1/R}(0),\partial B_{1}(0))}}\right]\geq p.

Combining this with the scaling invariance of
h
(Lemma
2.2
) and the definition of
D_{h}^{\varepsilon}
implies (
3.5
). For the case where
\mathsf{h}
is a whole-space LGF plus a bounded continuous function, say
\mathsf{h}=h+g
with
g
bounded, we note that

\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\xi\inf_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(w)}}\right)D_{h}^{\varepsilon}\leq D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon}\leq\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\xi\sup_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(w)}}\right)D_{h}^{\varepsilon},

which gives the desired result. ∎

The following technical lemma is used to show (
3.1
). It follows from standard arguments for length metrics.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
V\subset U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be two open domains, and let
\{D_{n}\}
be a sequence of continuous length metrics on
U
. Suppose that
\{D_{n}\}
converges with respect to the local uniform topology on
U\times U
to a continuous metric
D
, and
\{D_{n}(\cdot,\cdot;V)\}
converges with respect to the local uniform topology on
V\times V
to a continuous metric
\widehat{D}
. Then,
D(u,v;V)=\widehat{D}(u,v;V)
for all
u,v\in V
. (Note that
D
and
\widehat{D}
are not assumed to be length metrics).

Proof.

Since
D_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)\leq D_{n}(\cdot,\cdot;V)
, we have
D\leq\widehat{D}
on
V\times V
. Similar to
[
11
, Lemma 2.11]
, one can show that for all
u,v\in V
with
D(u,v)<D(u,\partial V)
, we have
D(u,v)=\widehat{D}(u,v)
. It follows that the
D
-length of any path in
V
equals its
\widehat{D}
-length. Hence
D(u,v;V)=\widehat{D}(u,v;V)
for all
u,v\in V
. ∎

We are now ready to prove Lemma
3.1
.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

The tightness of the rescaled metrics
\{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
follows from Lemma
3.3
. Suppose that along
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
, the laws of
\{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{-1}D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\}
converge in the local uniform topology to
\widetilde{D}
. Again by Lemma
3.3
,
\widetilde{D}
is a.s. a continuous metric on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The fact that
\widetilde{D}
is a length metric follows from Lemma
3.4
, as we now elaborate.

Fix
u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\delta>0
. By definition, for any
\varepsilon>0
we can choose a path
P^{\varepsilon}
connecting
u
and
v
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

{\rm len}(P^{\varepsilon};\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon})\leq\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon}(u,v)+\delta.

Parametrize
P^{\varepsilon}
by its
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon}
-length. It follows from Lemma
3.4
that
P^{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Moreover, Lemma
3.3
implies that
P^{\varepsilon}
is uniformly equicontinuous. By the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem, we may pass to a subsequence
\{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}\}_{n\geq 1}
along which

(\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}^{-1}D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}},P^{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}})\to(\widetilde{D},P)\quad\mbox{jointly in law},

where the second convergence is in the topology of parametrized curves.

We claim that
{\rm len}(P;\widetilde{D})\leq\widetilde{D}(u,v)+\delta
. Indeed, for each fixed partition
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}
of
P
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\widetilde{D}(P(t_{i}),P(t_{i+1}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\lim_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}\to 0}\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}^{-1}D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}(P^{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}(t_{i}),P^{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}(t_{i+1}))

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}\to 0}{\rm len}(P^{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}};\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}^{-1}D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}\to 0}\big(\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}^{-1}D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}(u,v)+\delta\big)=\widetilde{D}(u,v)+\delta.

Taking the supremum over all partitions, we obtain
{\rm len}(P;\widetilde{D})\leq\widetilde{D}(u,v)+\delta
. Since
\delta
is arbitrary, we see that
\widetilde{D}
is a length metric. This proves the first claim in the lemma.

We now prove the second claim. The tightness of
\{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot;W)\}_{\varepsilon>0}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
for dyadic domains
W
follows from Lemma
3.3
. Using a diagonal argument over
W\in\mathcal{W}
, we can pass to a subsequence along which the laws of
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon}
and
\{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot;W)\}_{W\in\mathcal{W}}
jointly converge. The equality (
3.1
) then follows from Lemma
3.5
.
∎

The following lemma is used to prove Weyl scaling (Axiom
III
). Its proof is similar to
[
11
, Lemma 2.12]
, so we omit it here.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\mathsf{h}
be a whole-space LGF plus a bounded continuous function. Let
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence tending to 0 such that
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{-1}D_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon_{n}}
converges in law, in the local uniform topology on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, to a limit
\widetilde{D}
. By the Skorokhod representation theorem, there exists a coupling under which this convergence holds almost surely. The following property holds almost surely under this coupling.

For every sequence
(f_{n})
of continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that converges in the local uniform topology to a bounded continuous function
f
, let
D_{\mathsf{h}+f_{n}}^{\varepsilon_{n}}
be the exponential metric associated with
\mathsf{h}+f_{n}
. Then, as
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
,
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{-1}D_{\mathsf{h}+f_{n}}^{\varepsilon_{n}}
converges to

, as defined in (

).

Next, we show tightness across scales (Axiom
V
).

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
h
be a whole-space LGF with the additive constant chosen so that
h_{1}(0)=0
. Let
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence tending to 0 such that
(h,\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{-1}D_{h}^{\varepsilon_{n}})
converges jointly in law to
(h,D_{h})
. Then there exists a deterministic function
r\mapsto\mathfrak{c}_{r}>0
such that the family of random metrics
\{\mathfrak{c}_{r}^{-1}e^{-\xi h_{r}(0)}D_{h}(r\cdot,r\cdot)\}_{0<r<\infty}
has tight laws with respect to the local uniform topology on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Moreover, each subsequential limit of these laws is supported on continuous metrics on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

For
r>0
define

h^{r}(z):=h(rz)-h_{r}(0).

By the scaling invariance of the whole-space LGF (Lemma
2.2
), we have
h^{r}\overset{d}{=}h
.

Step 1: Construction of
\mathfrak{c}_{r}
.

By (
1.3
), we have

D_{h^{r}}^{\varepsilon/r}(u,v)=r^{-1}e^{-\xi h_{r}(0)}D_{h}^{\varepsilon}(ru,rv),\qquad u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Dividing by
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r}
gives

\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r}^{-1}D_{h^{r}}^{\varepsilon/r}(u,v)=\frac{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}}{r\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r}}\,e^{-\xi h_{r}(0)}\bigl(\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{h}^{\varepsilon}(ru,rv)\bigr).

(3.6)

By Lemma
3.3
, the family
\{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{h}^{\varepsilon}\}_{0<\varepsilon<1}

is tight and any subsequential limit is a continuous metric on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Since
h^{r}\overset{d}{=}h
, the same holds for

\{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r}^{-1}D_{h^{r}}^{\varepsilon/r}\}_{0<\varepsilon<r,\ r>0}
.

Along the sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
, the right-hand side of (
3.6
) converges in law to

\mathfrak{c}_{r}\,e^{-\xi h_{r}(0)}D_{h}(r\cdot,r\cdot),\qquad\mbox{where}\quad\mathfrak{c}_{r}:=\lim_{\varepsilon_{n}\to 0}\frac{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{n}}}{r\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{n}/r}},

provided the limit
\mathfrak{c}_{r}
exists. On the other hand, by definition of
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r}
as the median of
D_{h^{r}}^{\varepsilon/r}(0,e_{1})
, any subsequential limit of

\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r}^{-1}D_{h^{r}}^{\varepsilon/r}
has median distance between
0
and
e_{1}
equal to
1
. Comparing this median distance in the limit of (
3.6
) shows that
\mathfrak{c}_{r}

exists, is finite and positive, and is uniquely determined by the law of
D_{h}
.

Step 2: Tightness across scales.
Rewriting (
3.6
) using
\mathfrak{c}_{r}=\lim_{\varepsilon_{n}\to 0}\frac{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{n}}}{r\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{n}/r}}
and the convergence
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{n}}^{-1}D_{h}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\to D_{h}
in law, we get

\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{n}/r}^{-1}D_{h^{r}}^{\varepsilon_{n}/r}(u,v)\;\to\;\mathfrak{c}_{r}^{-1}e^{-\xi h_{r}(0)}D_{h}(ru,rv)\quad\mbox{in law as $\varepsilon_{n}\to 0$.}

Since the family

\{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r}^{-1}D_{h^{r}}^{\varepsilon/r}\}_{0<\varepsilon<r,\ r>0}
is tight
and any subsequential limit is a continuous metric on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it follows that the family of laws

\bigl\{\mathfrak{c}_{r}^{-1}e^{-\xi h_{r}(0)}D_{h}(r\cdot,r\cdot)\bigr\}_{0<r<\infty}

is tight in the local uniform topology on

\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and each subsequential limit is a subsequential limit of
\{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r}^{-1}D_{h^{r}}^{\varepsilon/r}\}_{0<\varepsilon<r,\ r>0}
, hence supported on continuous metrics on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. ∎

Remark 3.8
.

Note that in Lemma
3.7
, the tightness is through
r\in(0,\infty)
instead of bounded
r
. Using Weyl scaling (Axiom
III
), the tightness across scales also holds for a whole-space LGF plus a bounded continuous function
\mathsf{h}
. However, for a whole-space LGF plus a general continuous function, the tightness across scales only holds for bounded
r
, say
r\in(0,R)
where
R
is fixed; see Axiom
V
. In Axiom V of
[
11
]
, the authors restricted to the whole-space GFF, so this is consistent with our treatment.

3.2
Local metric

In this section, we introduce the notion of a local metric and prove that any subsequential limit of
\{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{h}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
is a local metric. The two-dimensional version was introduced in
[
15
]
and proved in
[
11
]
.

Definition 3.9

(Local metric)
.

Let
h
be a whole-space LGF, and let
D
be a random continuous length metric on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, coupled with
h
. We say that
D
is a
local metric for
h
if for every open set
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the internal metric
D(\cdot,\cdot;V)
is conditionally independent of the pair
(h,\;D(\cdot,\cdot;\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{V}))
given
h|_{\overline{V}}
.

Definition 3.10

(
\xi
-additive local metric)
.

Suppose
(h,D)
is a coupling of a whole-space LGF
h
and a random continuous length metric on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, we say that
D
is a
\xi
-additive local metric for
h
if for each
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and each
r>0
, the rescaled metric
e^{-\xi h_{r}(z)}D
is a local metric for the field
h-h_{r}(z)
, which is a whole-space LGF normalized so that its average over
\partial B_{r}(z)
equals zero.

We will show the following.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
h
be a whole-space LGF, and let
(h,D_{h})
be any subsequential limit of the laws of the pairs
(h,\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{h}^{\varepsilon})
. Then
D_{h}
is a
\xi
-additive local metric for
h
. More explicitly, fix
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0,
and choose the additive constant of
h
so that the average over
\partial B_{r}(z)
equals zero. Let
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open set. Then the internal metric
D_{h}(\cdot,\cdot;V)
is conditionally independent of the pair
(h,D_{h}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{V}))
given
h|_{\overline{V}}
.

Proof of Lemma
3.11
.

We argue in several steps, similarly to the proof of Lemma 2.17 in
[
11
]
.

Step 1: Reduction to
B_{r}(z)\subset V.
Suppose Lemma
3.11
is true whenever
B_{r}(z)\subset V.
Fix any
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r_{0}>0
such that
B_{r_{0}}(z_{0})\subset V
, and assume moreover that
h
is normalized so that
h_{r_{0}}(z_{0})=0.

Let
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
. Let
\tilde{h}
be the field defined by

\tilde{h}:=h-h_{r}(z),

so that
\tilde{h}
is a whole-space LGF normalized so that
\tilde{h}_{r}(z)=0
. Then Lemma
3.6
implies that
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{\tilde{h}}^{\varepsilon}\to e^{-\xi h_{r}(z)}\cdot D_{h}=:D_{\tilde{h}}
in law along the same subsequence for which
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{h}^{\varepsilon}\to D_{h}
in law. We need to show that the internal metric
D_{\tilde{h}}(\cdot,\cdot;V)
is conditionally independent of the pair
(\tilde{h},D_{\tilde{h}}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{V}))
given
{\tilde{h}}|_{\overline{V}}
.

Note that
\tilde{h}_{r_{0}}(z_{0})=-h_{r}(z)
. Since
B_{r_{0}}(z_{0})\subset V
, this implies that
h_{r}(z)\in\sigma(\tilde{h}|_{\overline{V}})
, and hence
h|_{\overline{V}}=\tilde{h}|_{\overline{V}}+h_{r}(z)\in\sigma(\tilde{h}|_{\overline{V}})
. By assumption, the internal metric
D_{h}(\cdot,\cdot;V)
is conditionally independent of the pair
(h,D_{h}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{V}))
given
h|_{\overline{V}}
(and hence given
{\tilde{h}}|_{\overline{V}}
). Note that
\tilde{h}=h-h_{r}(z)
is determined by
h
and
{\tilde{h}}|_{\overline{V}}
. In addition, we have
D_{\tilde{h}}(\cdot,\cdot;V)=e^{-\xi h_{r}(z)}\cdot D_{h}(\cdot,\cdot;V)
, so
D_{\tilde{h}}(\cdot,\cdot;V)
is determined by
\tilde{h}|_{\overline{V}}
and
D_{h}(\cdot,\cdot;V)
. Similarly,
D_{\tilde{h}}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{V})
is determined by
\tilde{h}|_{\overline{V}}
and
D_{h}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{V})
. Hence
D_{\tilde{h}}(\cdot,\cdot;V)
is conditionally independent of the pair
(\tilde{h},D_{\tilde{h}}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{V}))
given
\tilde{h}|_{\overline{V}}
. This completes the reduction.

Step 2: Further reductions.
Recall
\bar{h}_{\varepsilon}^{*}
from Lemma
2.12
, and let
\bar{D}_{h}^{\varepsilon}
be the associated exponential metric. By Lemma
2.12
, along the same subsequence,
(h,\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\bar{D}_{h}^{\varepsilon})
converges in law to
(h,D_{h})
. From now we work with
\bar{D}_{h}^{\varepsilon}
.

Assume without loss of generality that
\overline{V}\neq\mathbb{R}^{d}
(hence
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{V}
is allowable). Let
B_{r}(z)\subset V
, and let
h
be normalized so that
h_{r}(z)=0
. By Lemma
2.4
, we can then decompose the field
h
as

h|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{V}}=\mathfrak{h}+\mathring{h}

(3.7)

where
\mathfrak{h}
is a random
s
-harmonic function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{V}
which is determined by
h|_{\overline{V}}
, and
\mathring{h}
is a LGF in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{V}
which is independent of
h|_{\overline{V}}
.

Step 3: LFPP independence.
Fix dyadic domains
W\subset V
and
W_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{V}
. Choose a bump function
\phi
which is identically
1
on a neighborhood of
W_{0}

and whose support is contained in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{V}
.
For
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, the
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of

W_{0}
is contained in
\{\phi=1\}
and the
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of
W

is contained in
V
. Define a new field

h^{\prime}:=h-\phi\,\mathfrak{h}.

On the set
\{\phi=1\}\supset W_{0}
we have
h^{\prime}=h-\mathfrak{h}=\mathring{h}
, so
h^{\prime}
and
\mathring{h}
agree there. By locality of the
exponential metrics,

\displaystyle\bar{D}_{h^{\prime}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot;W_{0})\in\sigma(\mathring{h})\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\bar{D}_{h}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot;W)\in\sigma(h|_{\overline{V}})

for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon
. Since
h|_{\overline{V}}
and
\mathring{h}
are
independent, it follows that for each such
\varepsilon

\bigl(h|_{\overline{V}},\;\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\bar{D}_{h}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot;W)\bigr)\quad\text{and}\quad\bigl(\mathring{h},\;\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\bar{D}_{h^{\prime}}^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,\cdot;W_{0})\bigr)

are independent.

Step 4: Passing to the subsequential limit.

Let
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
be a subsequence along which

(h,\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{-1}\bar{D}_{h}^{\varepsilon_{k}})\to(h,D_{h})
in law.
Using Lemma
3.1
, we may pass to a subsequence
\varepsilon_{k}^{\prime}\to 0
along which we have the joint convergence in law

\bigl(h,\mathring{h},\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{k}^{\prime}}^{-1}\bar{D}_{h}^{\varepsilon_{k}^{\prime}},\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{k}^{\prime}}^{-1}\bar{D}_{h^{\prime}}^{\varepsilon_{k}^{\prime}}\bigr)\;\longrightarrow\;\bigl(h,\mathring{h},D_{h},D_{h^{\prime}}\bigr),

where
D_{h^{\prime}}=e^{-\xi\phi\mathfrak{h}}\cdot D_{h}
by Weyl scaling (Lemma
3.6
). By Lemma
3.1
, after possibly passing to a further deterministic subsequence
\varepsilon_{k}^{\prime\prime}\to 0
we can construct continuous metrics
D_{h,W}
and
D_{h^{\prime},W_{0}}
on
W
and
W_{0}
, respectively, such that

D_{h,W}(\cdot,\cdot;W)=D_{h}(\cdot,\cdot;W),\qquad D_{h^{\prime},W_{0}}(\cdot,\cdot;W_{0})=D_{h^{\prime}}(\cdot,\cdot;W_{0})

and such that the internal metrics

\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{k}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}\bar{D}_{h}^{\varepsilon_{k}^{\prime\prime}}(\cdot,\cdot;W)
and

\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{k}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}\bar{D}_{h^{\prime}}^{\varepsilon_{k}^{\prime\prime}}(\cdot,\cdot;W_{0})

converge jointly in law to
D_{h,W}
and
D_{h^{\prime},W_{0}}
. The independence from Step 3 is preserved under convergence in law, so in the
limit we obtain

\bigl(h|_{\overline{V}},D_{h}(\cdot,\cdot;W)\bigr)\;\perp\!\!\!\perp\;\bigl(\mathring{h},D_{h^{\prime}}(\cdot,\cdot;W_{0})\bigr).

Step 5: Adding the harmonic part.
By the previous step,
D_{h}(\cdot,\cdot;W)
is conditionally independent of
(\mathring{h},D_{h^{\prime}}(\cdot,\cdot;W_{0}))
given
h|_{\overline{V}}
. By Lemma
3.6
, a.s. we have that
D_{h}(\cdot,\cdot;W_{0})=(e^{\xi\phi\mathfrak{h}}\cdot D_{h^{\prime}})(\cdot,\cdot;W_{0})
. Hence
D_{h}(\cdot,\cdot;W_{0})
is a measurable function of
\mathfrak{h}\in\sigma(h|_{\overline{V}})
and
D_{h^{\prime}}(\cdot,\cdot;W_{0})
. Since
h|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{V}}=\mathring{h}+\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{h}
is determined by
h|_{\overline{V}}
, we see that
h
is a measurable function of
\mathring{h}
and
h|_{\overline{V}}
. Thus
D_{h}(\cdot,\cdot;W)
is conditionally independent of
(h,D_{h}(\cdot,\cdot;W_{0}))
given
h|_{\overline{V}}
. Letting
W
increase to
V
and
W_{0}
increase to
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{V}
now proves the result.
∎

3.3
Each subsequential limit is a measurable function of
h

In this section, we prove that any subsequential limit of
\{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{h}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
is a measurable function of
h
(see Lemma
3.16
). The main idea follows
[
15
]
: if a local metric is determined by
h
up to bi-Lipschitz equivalence, then it is measurable with respect to
h
(see Proposition
3.15
). Along the way, we extend the results of
[
15
]
to higher dimensions.

Definition 3.12

(Joint local metrics)
.

Let
h
be a whole-space LGF, and let
\{D_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
be random continuous length metrics on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, coupled with
h
. We say that
\{D_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
are
joint local metrics for
h
if for every open set
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the internal metrics
\{D_{i}(\cdot,\cdot;V)\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
are conditionally independent of the pair
(h,\;\{D_{i}(\cdot,\cdot;\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{V})\}_{1\leq i\leq n})
given
h|_{\overline{V}}
. We say that
\{D_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
are
joint
\xi
-additive local metrics for
h
if for each
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and each
r>0
, the rescaled metrics
\{e^{-\xi h_{r}(z)}D_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
are joint local metrics for the shifted field
h-h_{r}(z)
.

Suppose
D
is a local metric for
h
and, conditionally on
h
, we sample
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
independently from the conditional law of
D
. Then
(D_{1},D_{2})
are joint local metrics for
h
. The proof is analogous to that of
[
15
, Lemma 1.4]
, so we omit it. If
D
is
\xi
-additive, then
(D_{1},D_{2})
are joint
\xi
-additive for
h
.

Proposition 3.13

(Bi-Lipschitz equivalence for local metrics)
.

Let
d\geq 2
and let
h
be a whole-space LGF on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
(D_{1},D_{2})
be random continuous length metrics on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, coupled with
h
, which are joint
\xi
-additive local metrics for
h
. There exists a universal constant
p\in(0,1)
with the following property. Suppose there is a deterministic constant
C>0
such that for every
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and every
r>0
, the event

\mathcal{E}(z,r):=\Bigl\{\sup_{u,v\in\partial B_{r}(z)}D_{2}(u,v;B_{2r}(z)\setminus\overline{B_{r/2}(z)})\leq C\,D_{1}(\partial B_{r/2}(z),\partial B_{r}(z))\Bigr\}

holds with probability at least
p
.
Then almost surely
D_{2}(x,y)\leq C\,D_{1}(x,y)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

This result is the higher-dimension analogue of
[
15
, Theorem 1.6]
, proved there in Section 4. The proof idea is as follows. By choosing
p
sufficiently close
1
and using the near-independence across disjoint concentric shells (see Lemma
3.14
below), we can show that for each point in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
there exists a pair
(z,r)
such that the ball
B_{r/2}(z)
contains it and the event
\mathcal{E}(z,r)
occurs. Then, for any path connecting
x
and
y
, we can use these events to detour the path so that the
D_{2}
-length of the new path is bounded above by
C
times the
D_{1}
-length of the original path.

The
\xi
-additive of local metric allows us to extend the shell independence lemma (Lemma
2.7
) to this setting. Its proof follows verbatim that of Lemma
2.7
, so we omit it.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
h
be a whole-space LGF on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
(D_{1},D_{2})
be random continuous length metrics on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, coupled with
h
, which are joint
\xi
-additive local metrics for
h
. Fix
q\in(0,1)
and
q^{\prime}>1
. Let
\{r_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be a decreasing sequence of positive numbers such that
r_{k+1}/r_{k}\leq q
for all
k\geq 1
. Fix
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\{E_{r_{k}}(z)\}_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence of events measurable with respect to

\sigma\big((h-h_{r_{k}}(z))|_{A_{r_{k}/q^{\prime},q^{\prime}r_{k}}(z)}\big),\quad e^{-\xi h_{r_{k}}(z)}D_{1}(\cdot,\cdot;A_{r_{k}/q^{\prime},q^{\prime}r_{k}}(z)),\quad\mbox{and}\quad e^{-\xi h_{r_{k}}(z)}D_{2}(\cdot,\cdot;A_{r_{k}/q^{\prime},q^{\prime}r_{k}}(z)).

For
K\geq 1
, let
\mathcal{N}(K)
be the number of
k\in[1,K]\cap\mathbb{Z}
for which
E_{r_{k}}(z)
occurs. For any
a>0
and
b\in(0,1)
, there exists
p=p(a,b,q,q^{\prime})\in(0,1)
(independent of the laws of
D_{1},D_{2}
) such that if
\mathbb{P}[E_{r_{k}}(z)]\geq p
for all
k\geq 1
, then

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{N}(K)\geq bK]\geq 1-e^{-aK}\quad\mbox{for all $K\geq 1$}.

Proof of Proposition
3.13
.

The event
\mathcal{E}(z,r)
is measurable with respect to

e^{-\xi h_{r}(z)}D_{2}(\cdot,\cdot;A_{r/2,2r}(z))\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad e^{-\xi h_{r}(z)}D_{1}(\cdot,\cdot;A_{r/2,r}(z)),

so we can apply Lemma
3.14
with
E_{r_{k}}(z)=\mathcal{E}(z,r_{k})
and
r_{k}=2^{-k}
. Choosing
a>100d
and any
b>0
, and letting
p
be the corresponding constant from Lemma
3.14
, our assumption
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}(z,r)]\geq p
implies that, for each fixed
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with probability at least
1-O(\varepsilon^{10d})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
,

there exists
r\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\varepsilon,\sqrt{\varepsilon}}}\right]\cap\{2^{-k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}

such that

\mathcal{E}(z,r)
occurs.

(3.8)

By a union bound, we see that the event in (
3.8
) holds simultaneously for all
z\in\tfrac{\varepsilon}{10d}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap B_{\varepsilon^{-1}}(0)
with polynomially high probability as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

For now on, fix a sequence
\varepsilon\to 0
along which the event in (
3.8
) holds for every
z\in\tfrac{\varepsilon}{10d}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap B_{\varepsilon^{-1}}(0)
. We will show that
D_{2}(x,y)\leq CD_{1}(x,y)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Fix
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
\delta>0
. Let
P
a path connecting
x
and
y
such that

{\rm len}(P;D_{1})\leq D_{1}(x,y)+\delta.

We will build a path from
B_{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(x)
to
B_{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(y)
whose
D_{2}
-length is bounded by
C\,{\rm len}(P;D_{1})
.

Let
T={\rm len}(P;D_{1})
and parameterize
P
by its
D_{1}
-length. We inductively define a sequence of times
\{t_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\subset[0,T]
. Set
t_{0}=0
and, if
t_{k}
has been defined and
t_{k}<T
, choose
u_{k}\in\tfrac{\varepsilon}{10d}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap B_{\varepsilon^{-1}}(0)
closest to
P(t_{k})
and let
r_{k}\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\varepsilon,\sqrt{\varepsilon}}}\right]\cap\{2^{-m}:m\in\mathbb{N}\}
be such that
\mathcal{E}(u_{k},r_{k})
occurs. By construction,
P(t_{k})\in B_{r_{k}/2}(u_{k})
. We then define
t_{k+1}
to be the first time after
t_{k}
at which
P
leaves
B_{r_{k}}(u_{k})
. If there is no such time, we stop the iteration. Suppose that we obtain
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{M}
in this way. Then, for
0\leq j\leq M-1

t_{j+1}-t_{j}\geq D_{1}(\partial B_{r_{j}/2}(u_{j}),\partial B_{r_{j}}(u_{j})).

(3.9)

Moreover, the event
\mathcal{E}(u_{j},r_{j})
implies that for
0\leq j\leq M-1

\sup_{u,v\in\partial B_{r_{j}}(u_{j})}D_{2}(u,v;B_{2r_{j}}(u_{j})\setminus\overline{B_{r_{j}/2}(u_{j})})\leq C\,D_{1}(\partial B_{r_{j}/2}(u_{j}),\partial B_{r_{j}}(u_{j})).

(3.10)

We now consider the balls
\{B_{r_{j}}(u_{j})\}_{0\leq j\leq M-1}
. By construction
P\subset\cup_{0\leq j\leq M-1}B_{r_{j}}(u_{j})
and the latter union is connected. Therefore, the set
\cup_{0\leq j\leq M-1}\partial B_{r_{j}}(u_{j})
contains a path connecting
B_{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(x)
to
B_{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(y)
. Let
\widetilde{P}
be any such path. Recording the times at which
\widetilde{P}
moves from one sphere
\partial B_{r_{j}}(u_{j})
to another and then performing a loop erasure so that no sphere is visited more than once, we obtain a sequence of points
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{L}
such that
a_{1}\in B_{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(x)
,
a_{L}\in B_{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(y)
, and for each
1\leq i\leq L-1
,

there exists
0\leq j(i)\leq M-1
so that
a_{i}
and
a_{i+1}
both lie on
\partial B_{r_{j(i)}}(u_{j(i)})
,

and
j(i_{1})\neq j(i_{2})

whenever
i_{1}\neq i_{2}
.

Therefore,

\displaystyle D_{2}(B_{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(x),B_{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(y))\leq\sum_{i=1}^{L-1}D_{2}(a_{i},a_{i+1})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{L-1}\sup_{u,v\in\partial B_{r_{j(i)}}(u_{j(i)})}D_{2}(u,v;B_{2r_{j(i)}}(u_{j(i)})\setminus\overline{B_{r_{j(i)/2}}(u_{j(i)})}).

Since the indices
j(i)
are all different,

\displaystyle D_{2}(B_{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(x),B_{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(y))\leq\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}\sup_{u,v\in\partial B_{r_{j}}(u_{j})}D_{2}(u,v;B_{2r_{j}}(u_{j})\setminus\overline{B_{r_{j}/2}(u_{j})}).

Combining this with (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) yields

\displaystyle D_{2}(B_{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(x),B_{2\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(y))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}C\,D_{1}(\partial B_{r_{j}/2}(u_{j}),\partial B_{r_{j}}(u_{j}))\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})

\displaystyle\leq C\,{\rm len}(P;D_{1})\leq C(D_{1}(x,y)+\delta).

First letting
\varepsilon\to 0
and then letting
\delta\to 0
yields
D_{2}(x,y)\leq C\,D_{1}(x,y)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. ∎

We now show that if a local metric is determined by
h
up to bi-Lipschitz equivalence, then it is measurable with respect to
h
. The main input is the Efron-Stein inequality.

Proposition 3.15

(Measurability of local metrics)
.

Let
d\geq 2
and let
h
be a whole-space LGF. Let
D
be a random continuous length metric on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
coupled with
h
. Assume that
D
is a local metric for
h
and that
D
is determined by
h
up to bi-Lipschitz equivalence: if we condition on
h
and sample
D,\widetilde{D}
i.i.d. from the conditional law of
D
given
h
, then a.s. there exists a random constant
C>1
(depending on
h
) such that

\widetilde{D}(z,w)\leq C\,D(z,w),\qquad\forall z,w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Then
D
is a.s. determined by
h
(i.e., we can in fact take
C=1
).

Proof.

The proof is the higher-dimensional analogue of
[
15
, Theorem 1.7]
, proved there in Section 5. Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open bounded connected set. We will show that
D(\cdot,\cdot;U)
is a.s. determined by
h
. Letting
U
increase to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
yields the desired statement.

The key input is Efron-Stein inequality
[
13
]
: for any measurable function
F=F(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
of independent random variables
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
, and any family
X_{1}^{\prime},\dots,X_{n}^{\prime}
of i.i.d. copies, writing

F^{(i)}:=F(X_{1},\dots,X_{i-1},X^{\prime}_{i},X_{i+1},\dots,X_{n}),

we have

\mathrm{Var}[F]\;\leq\;\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\bigl[(F-F^{(i)})^{2}\bigr].

Step 1: Random shifted grid and path decomposition.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Sample a random shift
\theta
uniformly from
[0,\varepsilon]^{d}
and let
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}
be the union of all coordinate hyperplanes
\{x_{k}\in\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}+\theta_{k}\}
,
k=1,\dots,d
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{\theta}^{\varepsilon}
be the collection of open boxes of side length
\varepsilon
determined by
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}
. Let
P
be any (random) path in
U
with finite
D
-length, chosen measurably from
(h,D)
. Parametrize
P
by its
D
-length. Exactly as in
[
15
, Lemma 5.2]
, using that the parameterized path is independent of
\theta
, one proves that the set of times at which
P
hits
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}
has Lebesgue measure
0
a.s., and hence

\operatorname{len}(P;D)\;=\;\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\theta}^{\varepsilon}}\operatorname{len}\bigl(P\cap S;D\bigr)\qquad\text{a.s.}

(3.11)

Let
\widetilde{D}
be independently sampled from the conditional law of
D
given by
h
. By assumption, for some random constant
C>1
, we have
C^{-1}D(x,y)\leq\widetilde{D}(x,y)\leq CD(x,y)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Therefore, if we parametrize
P
by its
\widetilde{D}
-length, then the set of times at which
P
hits
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}
also has Lebesgue measure
0
a.s., which implies that

\operatorname{len}(P;\widetilde{D})\;=\;\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\theta}^{\varepsilon}}\operatorname{len}\bigl(P\cap S;\widetilde{D}\bigr)\qquad\text{a.s.}

(3.12)

Step 2: Reconstruction from grid internal metrics.
For
S\in\mathcal{S}_{\theta}^{\varepsilon}
, let
D(\cdot,\cdot;S)
be the internal metric of
D
on
S
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{\theta}^{\varepsilon}(U)
be the boxes in
\mathcal{S}_{\theta}^{\varepsilon}
that intersect
U
. We claim that
D(\cdot,\cdot;U)
is a.s. determined by

(h,\theta,\{D(\cdot,\cdot;S):S\in\mathcal{S}_{\theta}^{\varepsilon}(U)\}).

Indeed, given these data, let
D,D^{0}
be two conditionally i.i.d. samples with the same internal metrics on each box:

D(\cdot,\cdot;S)=D^{0}(\cdot,\cdot;S)\quad\forall S\in\mathcal{S}_{\theta}^{\varepsilon}(U).

Fix
x,y\in U
and
\delta>0
. Let
P
be a path from
x
and
y
in
U
with
{\rm len}(P;D)\leq D(x,y;U)+\delta
. Using (
3.11
), (
3.12
), and the fact that
D
and
D^{0}
agree on each box, we get a.s.

{\rm len}(P;D)=\sum_{S}\operatorname{len}(P\cap S;D)\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\rm len}(P;D^{0})=\sum_{S}\operatorname{len}(P\cap S;D^{0})=\sum_{S}\operatorname{len}(P\cap S;D).

Thus
{\rm len}(P;D^{0})={\rm len}(P;D)
and hence

D^{0}(x,y;U)\leq{\rm len}(P;D^{0})\leq D(x,y;U)+\delta.

Letting
\delta\to 0
gives
D^{0}(x,y;U)\leq D(x,y;U)
. The same argument with
D,D^{0}
swapped yields
D(x,y;U)\leq D^{0}(x,y;U)
. Thus
D=D^{0}
a.s. on rational pairs, and hence everywhere by continuity.

Step 3: Independence across boxes from locality.

By the definition of a local metric (Definition
3.9
), for any countable family of pairwise disjoint open sets the corresponding internal metrics are conditionally independent given the field. Thus, conditioning on
(h,\theta)
, the family
\bigl\{D(\cdot,\cdot;S):S\in\mathcal{S}_{\theta}^{\varepsilon}(U)\bigr\}
is conditionally independent.

Step 4: Resampling on a single box and Efron-Stein inequality.

Fix
x,y\in U
with
D(x,y)<D(x,\partial U)
. For
S\in\mathcal{S}_{\theta}^{\varepsilon}(U)
, define
D^{S}
by resampling the internal metric on
S
from its conditional law given
(h,\theta)
, while keeping
D(\cdot,\cdot;\tilde{S})
fixed for all
\tilde{S}\in S_{\theta}^{\varepsilon}(U)\setminus\{S\}
. Conditioning on
(h,\theta)
and applying the Efron-Stein inequality with
F=D(x,y;U)
and
F^{(S)}=D^{S}(x,y;U)
gives

\displaystyle{\rm Var}\bigl[D(x,y;U)\big|h,\theta\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\theta}^{\varepsilon}(U)}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\bigl(D^{S}(x,y;U)-D(x,y;U)\bigr)^{2}\Big|h,\theta}}\right]

(3.13)

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\theta}^{\varepsilon}(U)}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\bigl(D^{S}(x,y;U)-D(x,y;U)\bigr)_{+}^{2}\Big|h,\theta}}\right],

where we write
a_{+}=\max\{a,0\}
for
a\in\mathbb{R}
and the second line uses symmetry.

Now we show that the right-hand side of (
3.13
) tends to 0 as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Let
P
be a path from
x
to
y
in
U
with
\operatorname{len}(P;D)\leq D(x,y;U)+\varepsilon^{d}
. Using (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) together with
C^{-1}D\leq D^{S}\leq CD
, we have a.s.

\displaystyle D^{S}(x,y;U)-D(x,y;U)

\displaystyle\leq{\rm len}(P;D^{S})-D(x,y;U)

\displaystyle\leq{\rm len}(P;D^{S})-{\rm len}(P;D)+\varepsilon^{d}

\displaystyle={\rm len}(P\cap S;D^{S})-{\rm len}(P\cap S;D)+\varepsilon^{d}

\displaystyle\leq C\,{\rm len}(P\cap S;D)+\varepsilon^{d}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\theta}^{\varepsilon}(U)}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\bigl(D^{S}(x,y;U)-D(x,y;U)\bigr)_{+}^{2}\Big|h,\theta}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\theta}^{\varepsilon}(U)}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[(C\,{\rm len}(P\cap S;D)+\varepsilon^{d})^{2}\Big|h,\theta}}\right]

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leq 2C^{2}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\theta}^{\varepsilon}(U)}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[{\rm len}(P\cap S;D)^{2}\Big|h,\theta}}\right]+2\varepsilon^{2d}|S_{\theta}^{\varepsilon}(U)|.

Note that

\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\theta}^{\varepsilon}(U)}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[{\rm len}(P\cap S;D)\Big|h,\theta}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[{\rm len}(P;D)|h,\theta}}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}[D(x,y;U)|h,\theta]+\varepsilon^{d},

(3.15)

which is finite due to the bi-Lipschitz equivalence (see Lemma 5.1 in
[
15
]
). Moreover, since
D
is continuous, we have

\max_{S\in S_{\theta}^{\varepsilon}(U)}\operatorname{len}(P\cap S;D)\to 0\quad\mbox{a.s. as $\varepsilon\to 0$.}

(3.16)

Indeed, since
D(x,y)<D(x,\partial U)
, the path
P
stays uniformly bounded away from
\partial U
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. If the left-hand side of (
3.16
) does not converge to

, then there exists
\lambda>0
and a sequence of
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
such that for each
\varepsilon_{n}
we can find

with

. In that case, we could replace the segment of

inside

by a path whose

-length is
o_{\varepsilon_{n}}(1)
. This produces a path connecting

and

with

-length at most

D(x,y;U)+\varepsilon_{n}^{d}-\lambda+o_{\varepsilon_{n}}(1)<D(x,y;U)

for all sufficiently small

, which is a contradiction.

Combing (
3.15
) and (
3.16
), we see that the first term on the right-hand side of (

) converges to 0 as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Since

, the second term is of order

and thus also converges to 0. Taking

in (

), we conclude that
D(x,y;U)
is a.s. determined by

whenever

.

Finally, by considering rational pairs
(x,y)
and using that

is a continuous length metric, we see that
D(\cdot,\cdot;U)
is a.s. determined by

. Letting

increase to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
yields the desired result.
∎

The main result of this section is the following.

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
h
be a whole-space LGF with the additive constant chosen so that
h_{1}(0)=0
, and let
(h,D_{h})
be the limit in law of
(h,\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{h}^{\varepsilon})
along a sequence
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
. Then
D_{h}
is almost surely determined by
h
. In particular,
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{h}^{\varepsilon}
converges in probability to

along
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
.

Proof.

Let
p\in(0,1)
be the universal constant in Proposition
3.13
. Lemma
3.11
implies that
D_{h}
is a
\xi
-additive local metric for
h
, and hence also a local metric for
h
. Furthermore, Lemma
3.7
implies that there exists

such that for all

and

,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[D_{h}(\partial B_{r/2}(z),\partial B_{r}(z))\geq A^{-1}\mathfrak{c}_{r}e^{\xi h_{r}(z)}}}\right]\geq\frac{1+p}{2},

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sup_{u,v\in\partial B_{r}(z)}D_{h}(u,v;B_{2r}(z)\setminus\overline{\partial B_{r/2}(z)})\leq A\mathfrak{c}_{r}e^{\xi h_{r}(z)}}}\right]\geq\frac{1+p}{2}.

If we condition on

and sample two conditionally independent copies

of

, then the pair
(D_{h}^{(1)},D_{h}^{(2)})
is a joint

-additive local metric for

, and the above bounds imply that the events

of Proposition

hold for

and

. Therefore, Proposition

implies that

and

are bi-Lipschitz equivalent. In particular,

is determined by

up to bi-Lipschitz equivalence.

Applying Proposition

now shows that

is a.s. determined by

. Finally, by a standard measurability argument (see Lemma 1.3 in
[

]
), we deduce that
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{h}^{\varepsilon}
converges in probability to

along this subsequence. ∎

3.4
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section we finish the proof of Theorem
1.3
. Let
h
be a whole-space LGF with the additive constant chosen so that
h_{1}(0)=0
.

Step 1: Defining

for a whole-space LGF plus a bounded function and checking Axioms
I
,
III
,
IV
,

.

By Lemma

, for every sequence
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
there exists a subsequence
\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}\to 0
along which
(h,\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}^{-1}D^{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}_{h})
converges in law to

. Lemma

implies that

is almost surely determined by

, and the convergence holds in probability. Therefore, we can pass to a further subsequence
\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}\to 0
along which
(h,\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}D^{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}}_{h})
converges almost surely to
(h,D_{h})
.

Let now
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be bounded and continuous. By Lemma
3.6
, almost surely, along

, we have
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}D^{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}}_{h+f}\to e^{\xi f}\cdot D_{h}
for every such

, where

is as defined in (

). We set

Then

is a.s. determined by

and

.

Thus we obtain a measurable map
\mathsf{h}\mapsto D_{\mathsf{h}}
from generalized functions to continuous metrics, a.s. defined whenever
\mathsf{h}
is a whole-space LGF plus a bounded continuous function. By Lemma
3.1
,

is a length metric (Axiom
I
). Weyl scaling for bounded continuous
f
(Axiom
III
) follows from the definition. Axiom
IV
is immediate from the almost sure convergence. Lemma

yields the tightness across scales (Axiom

).

Step 2: Locality (Axiom
II
) for a whole-space LGF plus a bounded function.
Let
\mathsf{h}
be a whole-space LGF plus a bounded continuous function. We now show that for an open set

,

is a.s. determined by
\mathsf{h}|_{V}
. Let
O
be a bounded open subset of
V
. It suffices to show that for
u,v\in O
with
D_{\mathsf{h}}(u,v)<D_{\mathsf{h}}(u,\partial O)
, the internal distance
D_{\mathsf{h}}(u,v;O)
is a.s. determined by
\mathsf{h}|_{V}
. Then we can increase

to
V
and deduce that
D_{\mathsf{h}}(\cdot,\cdot;V)
is a.s. determined by
\mathsf{h}|_{V}
.

Let
\bar{\mathsf{h}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}
be the convolution of
\mathsf{h}
with
\Psi_{\varepsilon}\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}
as in (
2.20
), and let
\bar{D}_{\mathsf{h}}^{\varepsilon}
be the exponential metric associated with it. Lemma
2.12
implies that
\lim_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}\to 0}\sup_{z\in\overline{O}}|\bar{\mathsf{h}}_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)-\mathsf{h}_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)|=0
. Therefore, the almost sure convergence of
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}D^{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}}_{\mathsf{h}}
to

implies that

\lim_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}\to 0}\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}\bar{D}^{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}}_{\mathsf{h}}(u,v;O)=D_{\mathsf{h}}(u,v;O)\quad\mbox{almost surely}.

For all sufficiently small

, the left-hand side is determined by

, and hence

is also determined by
\mathsf{h}|_{V}
. Increasing

to

yields the desired result. This checks Axiom
II
for
\mathsf{h}
.

Step 3: Extension to a whole-space LGF plus a general continuous function.

Let
h
be a whole-plane GFF and let
f
be a (possibly random) unbounded continuous function. For a bounded open set
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, choose a smooth compactly supported function
\phi
with
\phi\equiv 1
on

and define

By locality (Axiom
II
) in the bounded case,

depends only on

and is independent of the choice of
\phi
.

Given a continuous path
P
contained in some bounded

, define its

-length to be the

-length of

; this does not depend on

by construction. For

set

to be the infimum of the

-lengths of continuous paths from

to

. Then

is a length metric on

which is a.s. determined by

, and for each bounded

we have

, hence determined by

, which implies Axiom
II
. Axioms
III
,
IV
, and
V
are inherited from the bounded case.

Combining the three steps, for any sequence
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
, we have constructed a map

, a.s. defined for every whole-space LGF plus a continuous function, which satisfies Axioms

–

and such that there exists a subsequence
\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}\to 0
with the property for any

that is a whole-space LGF plus a bounded continuous function,

\lim_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}\to 0}\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}}^{-1}D^{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}}_{\mathsf{h}}=D_{\mathsf{h}}\quad\mbox{in probability},

which is exactly the statement of Theorem

.

Lemma 4.1

Let

The argument parallels the proof of Lemma
2.12

2.13

2.13

h_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)=\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{d-1}h_{r}(z)\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}(r)\,dr\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad Z_{\varepsilon}\widehat{h}_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)=\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{d-1}h_{r}(z)\widehat{\Psi}_{\varepsilon}(r)\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}(r)\,dr.

\displaystyle\big|Z_{\varepsilon}\widehat{h}_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)-h_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)}\int_{\varepsilon\log(\varepsilon^{-1})^{10}/2}^{\infty}r^{d-1}|h_{r}(z)||\mathsf{K}_{\varepsilon}(r)|\,dr

By Lemma

\displaystyle\big|Z_{\varepsilon}\widehat{h}_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)-h_{\varepsilon}^{*}(z)\big|

interchanged. Fix
z,w\in U
. Let
P
a path connecting
z
and
w
in
U
such that
{\rm len}(P;D_{h})\leq 2D_{h}(z,w;U)
. We will build a path from
B_{\varepsilon^{1-\zeta}}(z)
to
B_{\varepsilon^{1-\zeta}}(w)
in
B_{\varepsilon^{1-\zeta}}(U)
whose
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\widehat{D}_{h}^{\varepsilon}
-length can be upper-bounded.

We parameterize
P
by its
D_{h}
-length and let
T={\rm len}(P;D_{h})
. We inductively define a sequence of times
\{t_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\subset[0,T]
. Let
t_{0}=0
. If
t_{k}
has been defined and
t_{k}<T
, choose
u_{k}\in\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap B_{\varepsilon^{-1}}(0)
closest to
P(t_{k})
and let
r_{k}\in\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}
such that
E_{r_{k}}^{\varepsilon}(u_{k};C)
occurs. By construction,
P(t_{k})\in B_{r_{k}/2}(u_{k})
. We then define
t_{k+1}
to be the first time after
t_{k}
at which
P
leaves
B_{r_{k}}(u_{k})
. If there is no such time, we stop the iteration. Suppose that we obtain
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{M}
in this way. Then, for
0\leq j\leq M-1
,

t_{j+1}-t_{j}\geq D_{h}(\mbox{across $A_{r_{j}/2,r_{j}}(u_{j})$}).

Moreover, the first and fourth inequalities in the event
E_{r_{j}}^{\varepsilon}(u_{j};C)
imply that for
0\leq j\leq M-1
,

\displaystyle\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\sup_{u,v\in\partial B_{r_{j}}(u_{j})}\widehat{D}_{h}^{\varepsilon}(u,v;A_{r_{j}/2,2r_{j}}(u_{j}))

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{r_{j}\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r_{j}}}{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}}e^{\xi h_{r_{j}}(u_{j})}\leq C^{2}\frac{r_{j}\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r_{j}}}{\mathfrak{c}_{r_{j}}\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}}D_{h}(\mbox{across $A_{r_{j}/2,r_{j}}(u_{j})$}).

Similarly to the proof of Proposition
3.13
, we can construct a path
\widetilde{P}
connecting
B_{\varepsilon^{1-\zeta}}(z)
and
B_{\varepsilon^{1-\zeta}}(w)
in
\cup_{0\leq j\leq M-1}B_{2r_{j}}(u_{j})
(hence in
B_{\varepsilon^{1-\zeta}}(U)
) such that

{\rm len}(\widetilde{P};\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\widehat{D}_{h}^{\varepsilon})\leq\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\sup_{u,v\in\partial B_{r_{j}}(u_{j})}\widehat{D}_{h}^{\varepsilon}(u,v;A_{r_{j}/2,2r_{j}}(u_{j})).

Combining this with the preceding inequalities yields

\displaystyle\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\widehat{D}_{h}^{\varepsilon}(B_{\varepsilon^{1-\zeta}(z)},B_{\varepsilon^{1-\zeta}}(w);B_{\varepsilon^{1-\zeta}}(U))

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\max_{r\in\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}}\frac{r\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r}}{\mathfrak{c}_{r}\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}}}}\right)\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}D_{h}(\mbox{across $A_{r_{j}/2,r_{j}}(u_{j})$})

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\max_{r\in\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}}\frac{r\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r}}{\mathfrak{c}_{r}\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}}}}\right)\,{\operatorname{len}}(P;D_{h})\leq 2C^{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\max_{r\in\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}}\frac{r\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r}}{\mathfrak{c}_{r}\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}}}}\right)\,D_{h}(z,w;U),

which proves (
4.9
). Inequality (
4.10
) follows from a similar argument. ∎

In order to deduce Proposition
4.2
from Lemma
4.4
, it suffices to find
\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}\subset\mathcal{N}^{\varepsilon}
such that
\tfrac{r\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r}}{\mathfrak{c}_{r}\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}}
is bounded from above and below, which is in fact a consequence of Lemma
4.4
due to the arbitrary choice of
\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

There exists
C>1
, depending only on
\zeta
,
\mathfrak{K}
, and the law of
D_{h}
, such that for each
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, there are at least
|\mathcal{N}^{\varepsilon}|/2
values of
r\in\mathcal{N}^{\varepsilon}
such that

C^{-1}\leq\frac{r\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r}}{\mathfrak{c}_{r}\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}}\leq C.

(4.12)

Proof.

Take
z=0
,
w=2e_{1}=(2,0,\ldots,0)
and
U=B_{4}(0)
in (
4.9
) from Lemma
4.4
. We have for any deterministic
\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}\subset\mathcal{N}^{\varepsilon}
with
|\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}|\geq\tfrac{1}{10}|\mathcal{N}^{\varepsilon}|
, with probability tending to 1 as
\varepsilon\to 0
, independent of the choice of
\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}
,

\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\widehat{D}_{h}^{\varepsilon}(\partial B_{1/2}(0),\partial B_{1}(0))\leq\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\widehat{D}_{h}^{\varepsilon}(B_{\varepsilon^{1-\zeta}}(z),B_{\varepsilon^{1-\zeta}}(w);B_{\varepsilon^{1-\zeta}}(U))\leq C\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\max_{r\in\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}}\frac{r\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r}}{\mathfrak{c}_{r}\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}}}}\right)D_{h}(z,w;U).

(4.13)

By Lemmas
3.3
and
4.1
, we know that as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\widehat{D}_{h}^{\varepsilon}
is tight with respect to the local uniform topology and any subsequential limit induces the Euclidean topology. In particular, this implies that as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\widehat{D}_{h}^{\varepsilon}(\partial B_{1/2}(0),\partial B_{1}(0))
is tight and bounded away from 0 in probability. Therefore, there exists
c>0
such that for at least
\tfrac{9}{10}|\mathcal{N}^{\varepsilon}|
radii in
\mathcal{N}^{\varepsilon}
, we have

\frac{r\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r}}{\mathfrak{c}_{r}\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}}\geq c.

Otherwise, we could choose
\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}
so that
\max_{r\in\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}}\frac{r\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r}}{\mathfrak{c}_{r}\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}}<c
, yielding a contradiction with (
4.13
). Similar we can use (
4.10
) to show that for at least
\tfrac{9}{10}|\mathcal{N}^{\varepsilon}|
radii in
\mathcal{N}^{\varepsilon}
, we have

\frac{r\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r}}{\mathfrak{c}_{r}\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}}\leq c^{-1},

where we decrease
c
if necessary. This concludes the proof.
∎

We now finish the proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

By Lemma
4.5
, we can pick
\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}\subset\mathcal{N}^{\varepsilon}
with
|\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}|\geq\tfrac{1}{10}|\mathcal{N}^{\varepsilon}|
such that (
4.12
) holds. Applying Lemma
4.4
with this choice of
\mathcal{R}^{\varepsilon}
completes the proof.
∎

We now proceed with the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Step 1.
Let
\zeta=1/2
and fix any
\mathfrak{K}
satisfying (
2.8
). We first show that there exists
C>1
, depending only on
\mathfrak{K}
and the law of
D_{h}
, such that for all
R\geq 1
, the following holds when
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small (which may depend on
R
):

C^{-1}\leq\frac{r\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r}}{\mathfrak{c}_{r}\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}}\leq C,\qquad\forall 10\varepsilon^{1-\zeta}\leq r\leq R.

(4.14)

This essentially follows from Proposition
4.2
. Indeed, by (
4.3
), with probability tending to 1 as
\varepsilon\to 0
, for all
z\in\partial B_{r/2}(0)
and
w\in\partial B_{3r}(0)
, and with
U=B_{4R}(0)
, we have

\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{h}^{\varepsilon}(\mbox{across }A_{r,2r}(0))\leq\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{h}^{\varepsilon}(B_{\varepsilon^{1-\zeta}}(z),B_{\varepsilon^{1-\zeta}}(w);B_{\varepsilon^{1-\zeta}}(U))\leq C\,D_{h}(z,w;U).

Taking the infimum over
z,w
on the right-hand side gives

\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{h}^{\varepsilon}(\mbox{across }A_{r,2r}(0))\leq C\,D_{h}(\mbox{across }A_{r/2,3r}(0)).

By Axiom
V
, the family
\{\mathfrak{c}_{r}^{-1}e^{-\xi h_{r}(0)}D_{h}(\mbox{across }A_{r/2,3r}(0))\}_{0<r<\infty}
is tight. It follows from (
4.7
) (see also Lemma
3.7
) that the laws of
\{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}D_{h}^{\varepsilon}(\mbox{across }A_{r,2r}(0))/(\frac{r\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r}}{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}}e^{\xi h_{r}(0)})\}_{0<\varepsilon<r,\ r>0}
are tight and any subsequential limit is positive. Therefore, there exists
C>1
(independent of
R
) such that for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon
,

\mathfrak{c}_{r}\geq C^{-1}\frac{r\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r}}{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}},\qquad\forall 10\varepsilon^{1-\zeta}\leq r\leq R.

The other inequality in (
4.14
) follows from a similar argument by applying (
4.4
) from Proposition
4.2
.

Step 2.
It follows from (
4.14
) that for any
r,s>0
, as
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small,

\mathfrak{c}_{r}\mathfrak{c}_{s}\asymp\frac{r\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r}}{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}}\times\frac{s\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/(sr)}}{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/r}}=\frac{rs\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon/(sr)}}{\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}}\asymp\mathfrak{c}_{sr},

where the implicit constants in
\asymp
depend only on the law of
D_{h}
. Combined with a subadditive inequality (see, e.g., Lemma 3.8 in
[
10
]
), this yields that there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathfrak{c}_{2^{-n}}\asymp 2^{-\alpha n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Using Axiom
V
and
\mathfrak{c}_{r}\asymp\tfrac{\mathfrak{c}_{1}}{\mathfrak{c}_{1/r}}
, we further obtain
\mathfrak{c}_{r}\asymp r^{\alpha}
for all
r>0
. By Proposition 1.1 in
[
7
]
, we have
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{1-\xi Q+o(1)}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Combining this with (
4.14
), we deduce that
\alpha=\xi Q
.
∎

Now we show that any two weak
\gamma
-exponential metrics are up-to-constants equivalent.

Proposition 4.6
.

Fix
\gamma\in(0,\sqrt{2d})
. Let
D_{h}
and
\widetilde{D}_{h}
be two weak
\gamma
-exponential metrics. Then there exists a deterministic constant
C>1
(depending on the laws of
D_{h}
and
\widetilde{D}_{h}
) such that almost surely

C^{-1}D_{h}(z,w)\leq\widetilde{D}_{h}(z,w)\leq CD_{h}(z,w),\qquad\forall z,w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof.

We claim that for each
\zeta\in(0,1)
, there exists a constant
C>0
(depending only on
\zeta
and the laws of
D_{h}
and
\widetilde{D}_{h}
) such that for any bounded connected open set
U
, with probability tending to 1 as
\varepsilon\to 0
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{h}(B_{\varepsilon^{1-\zeta}}(z),B_{\varepsilon^{1-\zeta}}(w);B_{\varepsilon^{1-\zeta}}(U))\leq C\,D_{h}(z,w;U),\quad\forall z,w\in U,

\displaystyle D_{h}(B_{\varepsilon^{1-\zeta}}(z),B_{\varepsilon^{1-\zeta}}(w);B_{\varepsilon^{1-\zeta}}(U))\leq C\,\widetilde{D}_{h}(z,w;U),\quad\forall z,w\in U.

In fact this follows verbatim from the proof of Lemma
4.4
, with
\mathsf{a}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\widehat{D}_{h}^{\varepsilon}
replaced by
\widetilde{D}_{h}
. Note that the scaling constants for
D_{h}
and
\widetilde{D}_{h}
are the same.

Let
\zeta=1/2
. Fix
z,w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Sending
\varepsilon\to 0
in the first inequality gives that almost surely

\widetilde{D}_{h}(z,w)\leq C\,D_{h}(z,w;U).

(4.15)

It follows from Axiom
V
that for any
p\in(0,1)
and
A>0
, there exists
R>1
(depending only on
p,A
, and the law of
D_{h}
) such that for all
r>0
,

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sup_{u,v\in B_{r}(0)}D_{h}(u,v)\leq\frac{1}{A}D_{h}(\partial B_{r}(0),\partial B_{Rr}(0))}}\right]\geq p.

Therefore, the
D_{h}
-geodesic between
z
and
w
is almost surely contained in some bounded region. Hence letting
U
increase to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in (
4.15
) yields
\widetilde{D}_{h}(z,w)\leq CD_{h}(z,w)
. By considering rational pairs
(z,w)
and using that
D_{h}
and
\widetilde{D}_{h}
are continuous metrics, we get that almost surely
\widetilde{D}_{h}(z,w)\leq CD_{h}(z,w)
for all

. The reverse inequality is obtained by interchanging the roles of
D_{h}
and
\widetilde{D}_{h}
.
∎

4.2
Superpolynomial concentration bound for set-to-set distances

In this section, we prove superpolynomial concentration bounds for the set-to-set distance (Proposition
4.7
) and the around distance (Proposition
4.10
). The proof strategy is similar to that of
[
11
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be connected and open. Let
K_{1},K_{2}
be two disjoint connected compact subsets of
U
that are not singletons. For each
\mathbbm{r}>0
, with superpolynomially high probability as
A\to\infty
, at a rate uniform in
\mathbbm{r}
, we have

A^{-1}\mathfrak{c}_{\mathbbm{r}}e^{\xi h_{\mathbbm{r}}(0)}\leq D_{h}(\mathbbm{r}K_{1},\mathbbm{r}K_{2};\mathbbm{r}U)\leq A\mathfrak{c}_{\mathbbm{r}}e^{\xi h_{\mathbbm{r}}(0)}.

The proof of Proposition
4.7
is another application of the shell independence lemma (Lemma
2.7
) and Axiom
V
. Using them, with high probability, we can cover
\mathbbm{r}U
by small Euclidean shells
\{A_{r/2,2r}(w)\}
such that the
D_{h}
-across and
D_{h}
-around distances are both up-to-constants equivalent to
r^{\xi Q}e^{\xi h_{r}(w)}
. This allows us to control the
D_{h}
-distances globally in terms of
r^{\xi Q}
and
e^{\xi h_{r}(w)}
. Next, we compare these quantities with
\mathfrak{c}_{\mathbbm{r}}
and
e^{\xi h_{\mathbbm{r}}(0)}
(Lemma
4.9
). By choosing the scales appropriately as functions of
A
, we obtain the proposition.

For
C>1
,
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
r>0
, let
E_{r}(z;C)
denote the event that

\sup_{u,v\in\partial B_{r}(z)}D_{h}(u,v;A_{r/2,2r}(z))\leq Cr^{\xi Q}e^{\xi h_{r}(z)}\quad\mbox{and}\quad D_{h}(\partial B_{r/2}(z),\partial B_{r}(z))\geq C^{-1}r^{\xi Q}e^{\xi h_{r}(z)}.

(4.16)

By Axioms
III
and
II
, the event
E_{r}(z;C)
is measurable with respect to
(h-h_{r}(z))|_{A_{r/2,2r}(z)}
and thus fits into the assumptions of Lemma
2.7
. Moreover, by Axioms
IV
and
V
,
E_{r}(z;C)
occurs with probability arbitrarily close to 1 as
C\to\infty
, at a rate uniform in
z
and
r
. Applying Lemma
2.7
yields the following lemma (see, e.g., (
3.8
) for a similar application).

Lemma 4.8
.

For each
M>0
, there exists
C=C(M)>1
such that with probability at least
1-O(\varepsilon^{M})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, at a rate uniform in
\mathbbm{r}
, the following holds. For each
w\in\tfrac{\varepsilon^{2}}{10d}\mathbbm{r}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap B_{\varepsilon^{-1}\mathbbm{r}}(0)
, there exists
r\in[\varepsilon^{2}\mathbbm{r},\varepsilon\mathbbm{r}]\cap\{2^{-k}\mathbbm{r}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
such that
E_{r}(w;C)
occurs.

Next, we upper-bound
|h_{r}(w)-h_{\mathbbm{r}}(0)|
for those
r
and
w
satisfying the above lemma.

Lemma 4.9
.

There exists a universal constant
\lambda>0
(depending only on
d
) such that the following holds. For each
M>\lambda
, with probability at least
1-O(\varepsilon^{M/\lambda})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, at a rate uniform in
\mathbbm{r}
, we have for all
w\in\tfrac{\varepsilon^{2}}{10d}\mathbbm{r}\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap B_{\varepsilon^{-1}\mathbbm{r}}(0)
and
r\in[\varepsilon^{2}\mathbbm{r},\varepsilon\mathbbm{r}]\cap\{2^{-k}\mathbbm{r}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
,

|h_{r}(w)-h_{\mathbbm{r}}(0)|\leq\sqrt{M}\log\varepsilon^{-1}.

(4.17)

Proof.

By the scaling invariance of
h
(Lemma
2.2
), we may assume
\mathbbm{r}=1
. Note that
h_{r}(w)-h_{\mathbbm{r}}(0)
is centered Gaussian with variance
O(\log\varepsilon^{-1})
. Therefore, for some universal constant
C>0
and any
M>1
,

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[|h_{r}(w)-h_{\mathbbm{r}}(0)|>\sqrt{M}\log\varepsilon^{-1}}}\right]=O(\varepsilon^{M/C}).

There are at most
O(\varepsilon^{-3d-10})
choices of
(w,r)
. Taking a union bound over all such pairs and choosing
\lambda>0
sufficiently large then yields the desired bound of order
O(\varepsilon^{M/\lambda})
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Proposition
4.7
.

Proof of Proposition
4.7
.

Let
\lambda
be as in Lemma
4.9
and fix any
M>\lambda
. Let
C=C(M)>1
be the constant from Lemma
4.8
. Suppose that the events in Lemmas
4.8
and
4.9
both occur. We first lower-bound
D_{h}(\mathbbm{r}K_{1},\mathbbm{r}K_{2};\mathbbm{r}U)
.

For any path
P
connecting
\mathbbm{r}K_{1}
and
\mathbbm{r}K_{2}
in
\mathbbm{r}U
, let
w
be a point in
\tfrac{\varepsilon^{2}}{10d}\mathbbm{r}\mathbb{Z}^{d}
which is closest to this path. Let
r\in[\varepsilon^{2}\mathbbm{r},\varepsilon\mathbbm{r}]\cap\{2^{-k}\mathbbm{r}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
be such that
E_{r}(w;C)
occurs. By construction,
P
crosses
A_{r/2,r}(w)
. Therefore, by the event
E_{r}(w;C)
from (
4.16
),

{\rm len}(P;D_{h})\geq D_{h}(\partial B_{r/2}(w),\partial B_{r}(w))\geq C^{-1}\mathfrak{c}_{r}e^{\xi h_{r}(w)}.

By Theorem
1.6
, we may assume
\mathfrak{c}_{t}=t^{\xi Q}
for
t>0
. Combined this with
r\geq\varepsilon^{2}\mathbbm{r}
and (
4.17
), we get

{\rm len}(P;D_{h})\geq C^{-1}\mathfrak{\varepsilon}^{2\xi Q}\mathfrak{c}_{\mathbbm{r}}e^{\xi(h_{\mathbbm{r}}(0)-\sqrt{M}\log\varepsilon^{-1})}=C^{-1}\varepsilon^{2\xi Q+\xi\sqrt{M}}\mathfrak{c}_{\mathbbm{r}}e^{\xi h_{\mathbbm{r}}(0)}.

Since
P
is arbitrary, we obtain

D_{h}(\mathbbm{r}K_{1},\mathbbm{r}K_{2};\mathbbm{r}U)\geq C^{-1}\varepsilon^{2\xi Q+\xi\sqrt{M}}\mathfrak{c}_{\mathbbm{r}}e^{\xi h_{\mathbbm{r}}(0)}.

(4.18)

Choosing
\varepsilon
such that
C^{-1}\varepsilon^{2\xi Q+\xi\sqrt{M}}=A^{-1}
, we see that the probability that the events in Lemmas
4.8
and
4.9
both occur is at least
1-O_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon^{M})-O_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon^{M/\lambda})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, which equals

1-O_{A}\Big((CA^{-1})^{\frac{M}{2\xi Q+\xi\sqrt{M}}}\Big)-O_{A}\Big((CA^{-1})^{\frac{M/\lambda}{2\xi Q+\xi\sqrt{M}}}\Big)\quad\mbox{as $A\to\infty$}.

(4.19)

Since
M
can be arbitrary large, this holds with superpolynomially high probability as
A\to\infty
.

To upper-bound
D_{h}(\mathbbm{r}K_{1},\mathbbm{r}K_{2};\mathbbm{r}U)
, we can find a lattice path
(w_{1},\ldots,w_{L})
on
\tfrac{\varepsilon^{2}}{10d}\mathbbm{r}\mathbb{Z}^{d}
connecting
\mathbbm{r}K_{1}
and
\mathbbm{r}K_{2}
inside
\mathbbm{r}U
, with
L\leq C^{\prime}\varepsilon^{-2d}
for some
C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}(U)>0
. For each
1\leq i\leq L
, by Lemma
4.8
, there exists
r_{i}\in[\varepsilon^{2}\mathbbm{r},\varepsilon\mathbbm{r}]\cap\{2^{-k}\mathbbm{r}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
such that
E_{r_{i}}(w_{i};C)
occurs. As in the proof of Proposition
3.13
, we can construct a path
\widetilde{P}
connecting
\mathbbm{r}K_{1}
and
\mathbbm{r}K_{2}
in
\cup_{1\leq i\leq L}B_{2r_{i}}(w_{i})
such that

{\rm len}(\widetilde{P};D_{h})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{L}\sup_{u,v\in\partial B_{r_{i}}(w_{i})}D_{h}(u,v;A_{r_{i}/2,2r_{i}}(w_{i})).

Using the event
E_{r_{i}}(w_{i};C)
from (
4.16
), Theorem
1.6
and (
4.11
), we get

{\rm len}(\widetilde{P};D_{h})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{L}Cr_{i}^{\xi Q}e^{\xi h_{r_{i}}(w_{i})}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{L}C\varepsilon^{2\xi Q}{\mathbbm{r}}^{\xi Q}e^{\xi(h_{\mathbbm{r}}(0)+\sqrt{M}\log\varepsilon^{-1})}.

Combined with
L\leq C^{\prime}\varepsilon^{-2d}
, we get

D_{h}(\mathbbm{r}K_{1},\mathbbm{r}K_{2};\mathbbm{r}U)\leq CC^{\prime}\varepsilon^{-2d+2\xi Q-\xi\sqrt{M}}\mathbbm{r}^{\xi Q}e^{\xi h_{\mathbbm{r}}(0)}.

(4.20)

Choosing
\varepsilon
such that
CC^{\prime}\varepsilon^{-2d+2\xi Q-\xi\sqrt{M}}=A
yields the desired upper bound. As in (
4.19
), since
M
can be taken arbitrarily large, this bound holds with superpolynomially high probability as
A\to\infty
.
∎

Recall from (
1.10
) the definition of
D_{h}
-distance around a shell. For
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r_{2}>r_{1}>0
,

D_{h}({\rm around}\mbox{ }A_{r_{1},r_{2}}(z)):=\sup_{P_{1},P_{2}}D_{h}(P_{1},P_{2};A_{r_{1},r_{2}}(z)),

where the supremum is taken over all continuous paths connecting the inner and outer boundaries of
A_{r_{1},r_{2}}(z)
.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
r_{2}>r_{1}>0
. For each
\mathbbm{r}>0
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with superpolynomially high probability as
A\to\infty
, at a rate uniform in
\mathbbm{r}
and
z
, we have

A^{-1}\mathfrak{c}_{\mathbbm{r}}e^{\xi h_{\mathbbm{r}}(z)}\leq D_{h}({\rm around}\mbox{ }A_{r_{1}\mathbbm{r},r_{2}\mathbbm{r}}(z))\leq A\mathfrak{c}_{\mathbbm{r}}e^{\xi h_{\mathbbm{r}}(z)}.

Proof of Proposition
4.10
.

By Axiom
IV
, we may assume
z=0
. The lower bound follows by considering two fixed disjoint paths
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
crossing
A_{r_{1},r_{2}}(0)
and observing that (
4.18
) from the proof of Proposition
4.7
still holds for
(U,K_{1},K_{2})=(A_{r_{1},r_{2}}(0),P_{1},P_{2})
and the same
\mathbbm{r}
. The upper bound follows by noting that (
4.20
) in fact holds simultaneously for
(K_{1},K_{2})=(P_{1},P_{2})
, where
P_{1}

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Bigl(\sup_{u,v\in B_{r}(z)}D_{h}(u,v;B_{3r/2}(z))\Bigr)^{p}\Big]\leq Cr^{\xi Qp-\frac{1}{2}\xi^{2}p^{2}q}.

Since
q
can be taken arbitrarily close to
1
, we let
q\to 1
at a sufficiently slow rate as
r\to 0
. Using that
\sup_{u,v\in B_{r}(z)}D_{h}(u,v;B_{3r/2}(z))\geq\sup_{u,v\in B_{r}(z)}D_{h}(u,v)
, we obtain the second inequality.

Step 3.
We finally prove the first inequality in the lemma. We first consider replacing
h_{r}(z)
with
\mathfrak{h}_{r}(z)
. By (
4.39
), we have

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\mathfrak{h}_{r}(z)-\alpha\log r^{-1}|\leq\delta\log r^{-1}\}}\Bigl(\sup_{u,v\in B_{r}(z)}D_{h}(u,v;B_{3r/2}(z))\Bigr)^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\mathfrak{h}_{r}(z)-\alpha\log r^{-1}|\leq\delta\log r^{-1}\}}e^{\xi p(\mathfrak{h}_{r}(z)+O)}\Bigl(\sup_{u,v\in B_{r}(z)}D_{\mathring{h}}(u,v;B_{3r/2}(z))\Bigr)^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\mathfrak{h}_{r}(z)-\alpha\log r^{-1}|\leq\delta\log r^{-1}\}}e^{\xi p(\mathfrak{h}_{r}(z)+O)}\Big]\;\mathbb{E}\Big[\Bigl(\sup_{u,v\in B_{r}(z)}D_{\mathring{h}}(u,v;B_{3r/2}(z))\Bigr)^{p}\Big],

where the last equality is by the independence of
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathring{h}
. For any
q>1
, Hölder’s inequality gives

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\mathfrak{h}_{r}(z)-\alpha\log r^{-1}|\leq\delta\log r^{-1}\}}e^{\xi p(\mathfrak{h}_{r}(z)+O)}\Big]\leq e^{\xi p(\alpha+\delta)\log r^{-1}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\mathfrak{h}_{r}(z)-\alpha\log r^{-1}|\leq\delta\log r^{-1}\}}e^{\xi pO}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq e^{\xi p(\alpha+\delta)\log r^{-1}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\mathfrak{h}_{r}(z)-\alpha\log r^{-1}|\leq\delta\log r^{-1}\}}\Big]^{1/q}\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{\xi pOq/(q-1)}\Big]^{1-1/q}\leq r^{-\xi p(\alpha+\delta)+(|\alpha|-\delta)_{+}^{2}/(2q)+o_{r}(1)}.

Sending
q\to 1
at a sufficiently slow rate as
r\to 0
and combining with (
4.37
) gives

\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\mathfrak{h}_{r}(z)-\alpha\log r^{-1}|\leq\delta\log r^{-1}\}}\Bigl(\sup_{u,v\in B_{r}(z)}D_{h}(u,v;B_{3r/2}(z))\Bigr)^{p}\Big]\leq r^{\xi p(Q-\alpha-\delta)+\frac{1}{2}(|\alpha|-\delta)_{+}^{2}+o_{r}(1)}.

Finally, by Hölder’s inequality and
\mathbb{E}[\mathring{h}_{r}(z)^{2}]=O(1)
, for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
c>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbbm{1}_{\{|\mathring{h}_{r}(z)|\geq\varepsilon\log r^{-1}\}}\Bigl(\sup_{u,v\in B_{r}(z)}D_{h}(u,v;B_{3r/2}(z))\Bigr)^{p}\Big]\leq r^{c\log r^{-1}}.

Combining these two preceding estimates and using
\sup_{u,v\in B_{r}(z)}D_{h}(u,v;B_{3r/2}(z))\geq\sup_{u,v\in B_{r}(z)}D_{h}(u,v)
gives the first inequality.
∎

We now prove Proposition
4.25
.

Proof of Proposition
4.25
.

Fix
\alpha\in[-\sqrt{2d},\sqrt{2d}]
. Since
X
is independent of
h
, by conditioning on
X

X
is deterministic. By the countable stability of Hausdorff dimension, we may assume that
X

a.s. by (
4.34

Step 1.
We first show that

{\rm dim}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{H}}(X\cap\mathcal{T}^{\alpha}_{h})\leq\frac{1}{\xi(Q-\alpha)}{\rm dim}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(X\cap\mathcal{T}^{\alpha}_{h}).

(4.40)

Fix any

x>{\rm dim}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(X)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\delta>0,

which will eventually tend to
{\rm dim}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(X)
and
0
, respectively. For

and integers

, define

\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\alpha;u,n}=\Big\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\frac{h_{\varepsilon}(z)}{\log\varepsilon^{-1}}\in[\alpha-u,\alpha+u]\,\mbox{ for all }0<\varepsilon\leq 2^{-n}\Big\}.

(4.41)

Recall
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\alpha}
from (
4.33
). Then

\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\alpha}\subset\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\alpha;u,n}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\alpha}=\bigcap_{u>0}\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\alpha;u,n}.

(4.42)

We now give an upper bound for
{\rm dim}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{H}}(X\cap\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\alpha;\delta,n})
for any integer
n\geq 1
. Observe that there exists a random
\overline{r}>0
(depending on
h
) such that the following holds for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\overline{r}]
and
u,v\in B_{2M}(0)
with

|h_{\varepsilon}(u)-h_{\varepsilon}(v)|\leq\delta\log\varepsilon^{-1}.

(4.43)

(Note that
h_{\varepsilon}(u)-h_{\varepsilon}(v)

O(1)
for such
u,v

4.43
) follows from a union bound over a discrete net and the Borel-Cantelli lemma.)

Since
x>{\rm dim}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(X)
, for any integer
k\geq 1
, we can choose a covering of
X
by balls
\{B_{r_{i}}(z_{i})\}_{i\geq 1}
such that

\sum_{i\geq 1}r_{i}^{x}\leq 2^{-k}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sup_{i\geq 1}r_{i}\leq\max\{\overline{r},2^{-n}\}.

(4.44)

Let

I:=\{i\geq 1:B(z_{i},r_{i})\cap\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\alpha;\delta,n}\neq\emptyset\}.

Then
X\cap\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\alpha;\delta,n}\subset\cup_{i\in I}B(z_{i},r_{i})
. For any
i\in I
, there exists
w\in B(z_{i},r_{i})\cap\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\alpha;\delta,n}
. By (
4.43
), we have
|h_{r_{i}}(z_{i})-h_{r_{i}}(w)|\leq\delta\log r_{i}^{-1}
. By the definition of
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\alpha;\delta,n}
in (
4.41
) and
r_{i}\leq 2^{-n}
, we have
\frac{h_{r_{i}}(w_{i})}{\log r_{i}^{-1}}\in[\alpha-\delta,\alpha+\delta]
. Therefore,

|h_{r_{i}}(z_{i})-\alpha\log r_{i}^{-1}|\leq 2\delta\log r_{i}^{-1}.

Hence, for
p\in(0,\frac{2d\mathsf{d}_{\gamma}}{\gamma^{2}})
, Lemma
4.27
(applied with
2\delta
in place of
\delta
and
R=2M
) gives

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{i\in I}\Bigl(\sup_{u,v\in B_{r_{i}}(z_{i})}D_{h}(u,v)\Bigr)^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbbm{1}_{\{|h_{r_{i}}(z_{i})-\alpha\log r_{i}^{-1}|\leq 2\delta\log r_{i}^{-1}\}}\Bigl(\sup_{u,v\in B_{r_{i}}(z_{i})}D_{h}(u,v)\Bigr)^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\geq 1}r_{i}^{\xi p(Q-\alpha-2\delta)+\frac{1}{2}(|\alpha|-2\delta)_{+}^{2}+o_{n}(1)},

where

and is uniform in

.

p
such that

\xi p(Q-\alpha-2\delta)+\frac{1}{2}(|\alpha|-2\delta)_{+}^{2}=x+\delta^{2}.

Because
x
and
\delta
are chosen close to

and

, respectively, and using that

(0,\frac{2d\mathsf{d}_{\gamma}}{\gamma^{2}})
. Combining the preceding inequality with (

), we get for sufficiently large

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sum_{i\in I}\Bigl(\sup_{u,v\in B_{r_{i}}(z_{i})}D_{h}(u,v)\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr]\leq\sum_{i\geq 1}r_{i}^{x+\delta^{2}+o_{n}(1)}\leq\sum_{i\geq 1}r_{i}^{x}\leq 2^{-k}.

By Markov’s inequality, with probability at least

, the sum in brackets is at most
2^{-k/2}
. By the Borel-Cantelli lemma, this event occurs a.s. for all sufficiently large integer

. It follows from the definition of

-Hausdorff dimension that for sufficiently large

Letting

to

{\rm dim}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{H}}\bigl(X\cap\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\alpha;\delta,n}\bigr)\leq\frac{{\rm dim}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(X)-\frac{1}{2}(|\alpha|-2\delta)_{+}^{2}+\delta^{2}}{\xi(Q-\alpha-2\delta)}.

Combining this with (
4.42
) and then letting
\delta\to 0

4.34
), yields (
4.40
).

Step 2.
We now show that

{\rm dim}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{H}}(X\cap\mathcal{T}^{\alpha}_{h})\geq\frac{1}{\xi(Q-\alpha)}{\rm dim}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(X\cap\mathcal{T}^{\alpha}_{h}).

(4.45)

Fix any
\delta>0

\displaystyle\mbox{for any $D_{h}$-metric ball $\widetilde{B}_{r}(z):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:D_{h}(y,z)<r\}$ that intersects $X\cap\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\alpha}\cap\mathcal{T}^{\alpha;\delta,n}_{h}$},

Proof of Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

It suffices to prove that

{\rm dim}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\gamma}X=\frac{1}{\xi}\bigl(Q-\sqrt{Q^{2}-2{\rm dim}_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}X}\bigr).

For the lower bound, note that
{\rm dim}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{H}}(X)\geq{\rm dim}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{H}}(X\cap\mathcal{T}^{\alpha}_{h})
for every
\alpha
. Taking
\alpha=Q-\sqrt{Q^{2}-2\dim_{\mathcal{H}}^{0}X}
in Proposition
4.25
and using (

) gives the desired lower bound.

We now prove the upper bound. We assume that
X
is deterministic and bounded. Fix
x>{\rm dim}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(X)
, which will eventually tend to
{\rm dim}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(X)
. By the definition of Euclidean Hausdorff dimension, for each integer
k\geq 1
we can choose a deterministic covering
\{B_{r_{i}}(z_{i})\}_{i\geq 1}
of

such that

\sum_{i\geq 1}r_{i}^{x}<2^{-k}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\sup_{i\geq 1}r_{i}\leq 2^{-k}.

By Lemma
4.27
, for any

\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sum_{i\geq 1}\Bigl(\sup_{u,v\in B_{r_{i}}(z_{i})}D_{h}(u,v)\Bigr)^{p}\Bigr]\leq\sum_{i\geq 1}r_{i}^{\xi Qp-\tfrac{1}{2}\xi^{2}p^{2}+o_{k}(1)},

Suppose that

. By Markov’s inequality and the Borel-Cantelli lemma, this implies that the sum in brackets converges to 0 a.s. as

Appendix A
Appendix

In this section we prove a general estimate for integrals of exponentials of Brownian-like Gaussian processes with a continuity estimate condition.

We will consider Gaussian processes
X_{t}
such that

\mathbb{E}(X_{t})=0,\;\mathbb{E}(X_{t})=t+o_{t}(1),

(A.1)

\mathbb{P}(\sup_{0<s<1}X_{s}-X_{0}\geq C)\lesssim e^{-c_{0}C^{2}}

(A.2)

for some absolute constant
C_{0}.
Note in particular that this implies that
X_{t}
has a Gaussian tail.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
X_{t}
be a centered Gaussian process such that (
A.1
) and (
A.2
) hold. Additionally, let
a>0
be a positive real number, and let
G_{t}:=X_{t}-at
be the drifted process. Then there exist positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}
such that

c_{1}e^{-(2a+o_{y}(1))y}\leq\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{sup}_{t\geq 0}G_{t}>y)\leq c_{2}e^{-(2a+o_{y}(1))y}.

Proof.

We will show each inequality independently. First, note that

\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{sup}_{t\geq 0}G_{t}>y)\geq\mathbb{P}(G_{\frac{y}{a}}>y)=\mathbb{P}(X_{\frac{y}{a}}>2y)\lesssim e^{-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{(2y)^{2}}{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{2y}{a}}}\right)}}}\right)}\leq e^{-(2a+o(1))y}.

This proves that
\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{sup}_{t\geq 0}G_{t}>y)
is bounded below. For the other direction, note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq y^{2}}G_{t}>y}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\sup_{t\in[0,y^{2}]\cap\mathbb{Z}}G_{t}>y-y^{\frac{2}{3}})+\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sup_{t\in[0,y^{2}]\cap\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{t<s<t+1}G_{s}-G_{t}\geq y^{\frac{2}{3}}}}\right).

By (
A.2
) we have that

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sup_{t\in[0,y^{2}]\cap\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{t<s<t+1}G_{s}-G_{t}\geq y^{\frac{2}{3}}}}\right)\leq y^{2}e^{-c_{0}y^{\frac{4}{3}}}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq y^{2}}G_{t}>y}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor y^{2}\rfloor}\mathbb{P}(X_{k}>y-y^{\frac{2}{3}+ak})+y^{2}e^{-c_{0}y^{\frac{4}{3}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor y^{2}\rfloor}\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\frac{(y+ak)^{2}}{2k}}}\right)+y^{2}e^{-c_{0}y^{\frac{4}{3}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle e^{-(2a+o(1))y}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Now we prove the following bound on an exponential integral of processes satisfying (
A.1
) and (
A.2
).

Proposition A.2
.

Let
X_{t}
be a centered Gaussian process such that (
A.1
) and (
A.2
) hold. Additionally, let
a>0
be a positive real number, and let
G_{t}:=X_{t}-at
be the drifted process. Then there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}x^{-2a+o(1)}\leq\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{G_{t}}dt>x}}\right)\leq c_{2}x^{-2a+o(1)}.

Proof.

We show each inequality separately. We start by showing the lower bound. Let
y=\log x.
Then note that if
G_{t}\geq y
for
t\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{y}{a},\frac{y}{a}+1}}\right],
then
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{G_{t}}dt>x.
This implies that

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{G_{t}}dt>x}}\right)\geq\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(G_{t}\geq y\mbox{ for }t\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{y}{a},\frac{y}{a}+1}}\right]}}\right)\geq\mathbb{P}(G_{\frac{y}{a}}\geq y)=\mathbb{P}(X_{\frac{y}{a}}\geq 2y)\geq e^{-2ay}.

This proves the lower bound. For the upper bound, we let
E
be the event

E=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{G_{t}\leq-\frac{1}{2}at\mbox{ for all }t\geq(\log x)^{4}}}\right\}.

Using that

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(G_{t}>-\frac{1}{2}at}}\right)=\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(X_{t}\geq\frac{1}{2}at}}\right)\lesssim\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\frac{1}{8}a^{2}t}}\right)\ll x^{-2a},

we see that
1-\mathbb{P}(E)\ll X^{-2a}.
Now we see that if
E
holds and

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{G_{t}}dt\geq x,

then
\sup_{0\leq t\leq(\log x)^{4}}G_{t}\geq\log\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{4}}}}\right)=\log x(1-o(1)).
Therefore

\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{G_{t}}dt>x}}\right)\lesssim(1-\mathbb{P}(E))+\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq(\log x)^{4}}G_{t}\geq\log x(1-o(1))}}\right).

Using Lemma
A.1
, we conclude.
∎

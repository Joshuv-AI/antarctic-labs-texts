Coherent six-functor formalisms: Pro vs Solid
Fei Ren

Abstract
. In the classical theory for coherent sheaves, the only missing piece in the Grothendieck six-functor formalism picture is j ! j_{!} for an open immersion j j . Towards fixing this gap, classically Deligne proposed a construction of j ! j_{!} by extending the sheaf class to pro sheaves, while Clausen-Scholze provided another solution by extending the sheaf class to solid modules. In this work, we prove that Deligne’s construction coincides with the Clausen-Scholze construction via a natural functor, whose restriction to the full subcategory of Mittag-Leffler pro-systems is fully faithful.



1.
Introduction

The Grothendieck six-functor formalism plays a foundational role in modern algebraic geometry, providing a conceptual framework for understanding the interplay between various operations on sheaves across different geometric contexts.
While this formalism is fully developed in the context of
\ell
-adic constructible sheaves in the étale cohomology in the 1970s, a parallel formalism for coherent sheaves remains incomplete.
In particular, for an open immersion

j:U\hookrightarrow X
, the exceptional pushforward functor
j_{!}

does not exist as a functor between categories of (quasi)coherent sheaves.

There have been various attempts towards building this missing
j_{!}
via enlarging the category of coherent sheaves.
In
[
Har66
, Appendix]
, Deligne constructed a version of
j_{!}

by embedding coherent sheaves into the larger category of pro-coherent sheaves.
More recently, Clausen and Scholze introduced the framework of solid modules, where the enlargement of the target category allows for a robust treatment of
j_{!}
in a derived setting, see
[
CS19
, Lecture VIII]
.
Their theory of solid modules offers a powerful and flexible toolset to do certain computations, yet its relation to the earlier construction of Deligne has remained unclear.

The aim of this paper is to bridge these two constructions.
We prove that Deligne’s construction of

j_{!}
via pro-sheaves coincides with the Clausen–Scholze version defined via solid modules, through a natural comparison functor
R\Phi
.
Moreover,
R\Phi
is the derived functor of
\Phi
, whose restriction to
the subcategory of Mittag-Leffler pro-systems is fully faithful. This result provides a conceptual and structural unification of the two approaches and reinforces the compatibility of classical techniques with modern condensed tools.

Theorem 1.1

(
2.13
,
3.2
)
.

Let
A
be a commutative ring with

(1)

The functor

Fully faithfulness

Definition 2.1
.

Define a natural functor

(2.1.1)

\Phi_{1}:\operatorname{Pro}\operatorname{Mod}(A)\xrightarrow{}\operatorname{TopMod}(A),\quad``\operatornamewithlimits{\rm{lim}}"M_{i}\rightarrow\operatornamewithlimits{\rm{lim}}M_{i}.

Here the
\operatornamewithlimits{\rm{lim}}M_{i}
refers to the limit in the category
\operatorname{TopMod}(A)
, i.e., the inverse limit of
M_{i}
as modules equipped with the inverse limit topology of discrete spaces.

Proposition 2.2
.

The following restriction of
\Phi_{1}

(2.2.1)

\operatorname{Pro}^{\operatorname{ML}}_{\rm t.o.}\operatorname{Mod}(A)\subset\operatorname{Pro}\operatorname{Mod}(A)\xrightarrow{\Phi_{1}}\operatorname{TopMod}(A),\quad``\operatornamewithlimits{\rm{lim}}"M_{i}\rightarrow\operatornamewithlimits{\rm{lim}}M_{i}

is fully faithful.

Proof.

Take
``\operatornamewithlimits{\rm{lim}}_{I}"M_{i}\in\operatorname{Pro}^{\operatorname{ML}}\operatorname{Mod}(A),``\operatornamewithlimits{\rm{lim}}_{J}"N_{j}\in\operatorname{Pro}\operatorname{Mod}(A)
. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Pro}\operatorname{Mod}(A)}(``\operatornamewithlimits{\rm{lim}}_{I}"M_{i},``\operatornamewithlimits{\rm{lim}}_{J}"N_{j})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(1)}}{{=}}\operatornamewithlimits{\rm{lim}}_{J}\operatornamewithlimits{\rm{colim}}_{I}\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{Mod}(A)}(M_{i},N_{j})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(2)}}{{=}}\operatornamewithlimits{\rm{lim}}_{J}\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{TopMod}(A)}(\operatornamewithlimits{\rm{lim}}_{I}M_{i},N_{j})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(3)}}{{=}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{TopMod}(A)}(\operatornamewithlimits{\rm{lim}}_{I}M_{i},\operatornamewithlimits{\rm{lim}}_{J}N_{j}).

Here

(1)

follows from the definition of the pro-category.

(2)

follows from the definition of the inverse limit topology on

as the restriction of the product topology

and the Mittag-Leffler condition.
In fact, since

is open in the discrete space

must have an open kernel. In particular, there exists a finite subset

of

, such that

\operatornamewithlimits{\rm{lim}}_{i\in I}M_{i}\cap\left(U\times\prod_{i\in I\setminus I^{\prime}}M_{i}\right)\subset\operatorname{Ker}f.

Let
p_{I^{\prime}}:\operatornamewithlimits{\rm{lim}}_{i\in I}M_{i}\rightarrow\prod_{i\in I^{\prime}}M_{i}
be the projection map.
Then
f
factors through some

\overline{f}:\operatorname{Im}(p_{I^{\prime}})\rightarrow N_{j}.

As the pro-system
``\operatornamewithlimits{\rm{lim}}_{I}"M_{i}
satisfies Mittag-Leffler and

is finite, we can find a common

such that for all

{\varphi}_{ki}(M_{k})={\varphi}_{li}(M_{l})

as submodules of

.
As

In particular,

(Actually we have

But we shall not need this.)

Remark 2.3
.

One might want such a functor to land in
\operatorname{LCA}
, but even after restricting to
\mathbb{N}
-indexed pro-systems,
\Phi_{1}
does not satisfy this property.
The key point is that the limit of locally compact spaces in
\operatorname{Top}
are not necessarily locally compact.
For example, if we equip
\prod_{\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{Z}
with the Tychonoff product topology, then this is a limit of locally compact abelian groups (i.e.,
\prod^{n}_{i=1}\mathbb{Z}
), but itself is not locally compact, as a countable direct product
\prod G_{n}
of locally compact
G_{n}
’s is locally compact if and only if all
G_{n}
are compact except for finitely many
n
.

Proposition 2.4
.

The functor

\Phi_{1}:\operatorname{Pro}_{\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{Mod}(A)\subset\operatorname{Pro}\operatorname{Mod}(A)\xrightarrow{\eqref{Phi1}}\operatorname{TopMod}(A)

lands in
\operatorname{Top}\operatorname{Mod}(A)^{\operatorname{cgwh}}
.

Proof.

Any prodiscrete abelian groups with the index category
\mathbb{N}
is metrizable (and hence compactly generated and weakly Hausdorff).
In fact, as any subspace of a metrizable space is metrizable, it suffices to show that any
\mathbb{N}
-indexed product of discrete spaces is metrizable.
It is clear that such a space must be Hausdorff.
It is also second-countable, as it is a countable product of second-countable spaces.
As discrete spaces satisfy the separation axioms
T_{0}
and
T_{3}
, any product space of discrete spaces satisfies satisfy
T_{0}
and
T_{3}
as well (
[
SS95
, Part I, Section 2, p. 15 para. 2]
).
Hence the space is metrizable by Urysohn’s metrization theorem.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

The proposition is false without restricting to
\mathbb{N}
-indexed pro-systems.
The key point is that prodiscrete spaces are not necessarily compactly generated.
For example,
\prod_{\aleph_{1}}\mathbb{Z}
(where
\aleph_{1}
is the first uncountable cardinal) is not compactly generated.

The direction

We show

.
Fix an uncountable strong limit cardinal

Proposition 3.1

Let

be the cardinality of

Then by the compactness of

Since the colimits over
i
are induced from the transition maps

of the projective system

, which can be extended to a full basis of
A^{r_{j}}
.

of

along the transition maps of

, as the following claim enables us.

Claim 3.1.1
.

There exists a system of bases of

as

-modules, such that for any

,

.

.

Let

\mathcal{S}:=\left\{\{E_{i}\}_{i\in I^{\prime}}\middle|\begin{array}[]{c}I^{\prime}\subset I,\\
\text{$E_{i}:=\{e_{ij}\}_{1\leq j\leq r_{i}}$ is a basis of $A^{r_{i}}$ as a $A$-module,}\\
\text{for any $l<i$ in $I^{\prime}$, the pullback of $E_{l}$ is a subset of $E_{i}$}\end{array}\right\}

\mathcal{S}

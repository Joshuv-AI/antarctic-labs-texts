The Lifespan of Strong Solutions to the Compressible MHD Equations with Entropy Transport in the Presence of Vacuum
Yongteng G U, Xiangdi H UANG a. State Key Laboratory of Mathematical Sciences, Academy of Mathematics and Systems Sciences,Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100190, China

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the finite time blow-up of strong solutions to the compressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) system (without magnetic diffusion) coupled with entropy transport, and derive an upper bound for the lifespan of such solutions. We first establish the local well-posedness of strong solutions for bounded domains and study the mechanism of finite-time singularity formation in the 2D radially symmetric case and 3D cylindrically symmetric case. We prove that if the initial density vanishes in an interior region containing the origin and the magnetic field is non-trivial within this vacuum region, the strong solution must blow up in finite time. These results generalize and improve the previous results of Huang-Xin-Yan [Math. Ann. 392 (2025) 2365–2394] for the compressible isentropic MHD equations. Significantly, we extend this blow-up result to the free boundary problem. Our analysis of the boundary’s expansion allows us to explicitly estimate the maximum lifespan of the solution. Keywords: Compressible MHD equations; Entropy transport; Strong solutions; Vacuum; Finite time blow-up.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we investigate the compressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) system (without magnetic diffusion) coupled with entropy transport. This system can be derived as a specific limit of the general Navier-Stokes-Fourier system for atmospheric flows discussed by Klein
[
8
]
. As a simplification of the Navier-Stokes-Fourier system, we assume that thermal conduction is absent and viscous dissipation is negligible. By further neglecting external geophysical forces such as gravity, we arrive at the following system

\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+\nabla\cdot\left(\rho u\right)=0,\\
\left(\rho u\right)_{t}+\nabla\cdot\left(\rho u\otimes u\right)-\mu\Delta u-\left(\mu+\lambda\right)\nabla\left(\nabla\cdot u\right)+\nabla P\left(\rho,\tilde{s}\right)=\left(\nabla\times B\right)\times B,\\
\partial_{t}\left(\rho\tilde{s}\right)+\nabla\cdot\left(\rho\tilde{s}u\right)=0,\\
B_{t}-\nabla\times\left(u\times B\right)=0,\\
\nabla\cdot B=0,\\
\end{cases}

(1.1)

Here, the unknown functions
\rho,u,P,\tilde{s},B
denote the ﬂuid density, velocity field, pressure, entropy and magnetic field, respectively. The viscosity coefficients
\mu
and
\lambda
satisfy the following physical restrictions

\mu>0,\quad\frac{2}{d}\mu+\lambda\geq 0,

where
d
is the spatial dimension. The pressure
P
is given by the state equation

P\left(\rho,\tilde{s}\right)=\rho^{\gamma}\varGamma\left(\tilde{s}\right),\quad\gamma>1,

where
\Gamma(\tilde{s})
denotes a positive, smooth, and strictly monotone function defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

By neglecting the effect of entropy and assuming the pressure law
P=A\rho^{\gamma}
, we find that equation (
1.1
) reduces to the compressible MHD equations. This system has attracted extensive research attention concerning its well-posedness theory. The local existence of strong solutions to the compressible MHD equations with large initial data was established by Vol’pert and Hudjaev
[
16
]
, under the assumption that the initial density remains strictly positive. Recently, Fan-Gu-Huang
[
3
]
proved the existence of global large solutions to the three-dimensional compressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) equations under the assumption that the viscosity coefficients satisfy
\mu\left(\rho\right)=\mu\rho^{\alpha},\lambda\left(\rho\right)=\lambda\rho^{\alpha}
, provided that the initial density is sufficiently large. In the presence of a vacuum, the local existence of unique strong solutions for the full compressible MHD system has been established in
[
5
]
, provided that the initial data satisfy a compatibility condition. Based on work by Huang-Li-Xin
[
6
]
regarding Navier-Stokes equations, Li-Xu-Zhang
[
10
]
established the global well-posedness of classical solutions for the 3D isentropic compressible MHD equations, where the initial data requires small energy but allows for large oscillations and vacuum. In addition, a lot of progress has been made regarding models with entropy transport. For instance, Michálek
[
14
]
proved the stability of the compressible Navier–Stokes system with an entropy transport equation. Maltese et al.
[
13
]
proved the existence of globally defined weak solutions on condition that the adiabatic
constant
\gamma>3/2
, which corresponds to the classical result of Feireisl et al.
[
4
]
for the isentropic case. Later, the authors in
[
15
]
established a blow-up criterion for strong solutions in terms of the
L^{\infty}
-norms of the density and velocity.

Having reviewed the results relevant to our model, our main focus in this paper is to study whether local solutions to the system with entropy transport blow up in finite time for general large initial data. An early study on the finite time blow-up of compressible Navier-Stokes equations was conducted by Xin
[
17
]
, who investigated solutions in the space
C^{1}([0,\infty),H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
(with
m>[d/2]+2
) and found that they blow up in finite time when the initial density has compact support. Later, improving upon Xin’s previous result, Xin-Yan
[
18
]
established that any classical solution for viscous compressible fluids without heat conduction inevitably blows up in finite time, provided that the initial data have an isolated mass group. Nevertheless, the above results were established assuming that local solutions are sufficiently smooth. Actually, finding such local solutions is difficult, or they might not exist at all. Li-Wang-Xin
[
9
]
proved that classical solutions with finite energy do not exist in the inhomogeneous Sobolev space for any short time under certain natural assumptions on the initial data near a vacuum. Therefore, there has been growing research interest in finding solutions that are known to exist locally but blow up in finite time. Recently, it was proved by Merle et al.
[
12
]
that under conditions of strictly positive density, there exist smooth solutions to the 3D spherically symmetric compressible Navier-Stokes equations that inevitably form a shell singularity in finite time. For cases with interior vacuum, Huang-Xin-Yan
[
7
]
established a class of local strong solutions that exist locally and blow up in finite time by analyzing the balance between viscous stress and the magnetic Lorentz force.

Based on the preceding discussion, we investigate the finite time blow-up of strong solutions to the system (
1.1
) in the presence of an interior vacuum. Furthermore, we consider the lifespan of solutions to the free boundary problem for the system (
1.1
) under the assumption that local strong solutions exist.

To handle the strong nonlinearity of the pressure, we combine the renormalized mass and entropy equations finding that
P
satisfies

P_{t}+u\cdot\nabla P+\gamma P\nabla\cdot u=0.

Consequently, system (
1.1
) can be reformulated in terms of the variables
(\rho,u,P,B)
as follows

\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+\nabla\cdot\left(\rho u\right)=0,\\
\left(\rho u\right)_{t}+\nabla\cdot\left(\rho u\otimes u\right)-\mu\Delta u-\left(\mu+\lambda\right)\nabla\left(\nabla\cdot u\right)+\nabla P=\left(\nabla\times B\right)\times B,\\
P_{t}+u\cdot\nabla P+\gamma P\nabla\cdot u=0,\\
B_{t}-\nabla\times\left(u\times B\right)=0,\\
\nabla\cdot B=0.\\
\end{cases}

(1.2)

Based on this reformulated system (1.2), we will state the main results of this paper in the following section.

2
Main results

2.1
Local well-posedness

We begin by establishing the local well-posedness of System (
1.2
) in general bounded domains. Theorem
2.1
serves as a foundation for the analysis of finite time blow-up under radial symmetry presented later.

Theorem 2.1
.

(Local existence of strong solutions-2D case)
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a
2D
bounded domain with smooth boundary. Assume that the initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0},P_{0},B_{0})
satisfy the following conditions

\begin{cases}0\leq\rho_{0},P_{0}\in W^{1,q},\quad B_{0}\in W^{1,q},\quad\nabla\cdot B_{0}=0\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega),\quad u_{0}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\end{cases}

(2.1)

for some constant
q>2
, and satisfy the compatibility condition:

-\mu\Delta u_{0}-(\mu+\lambda)\nabla(\nabla\cdot u_{0})+\nabla P_{0}+B_{0}\times(\nabla\times B_{0})=\rho_{0}^{1/2}g,\quad\text{in }\Omega,

(2.2)

for some
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then, there exists a small time
T^{*}>0
and a unique strong solution
(\rho,u,P,B)
to the initial boundary value problem (
1.2
) and (
2.1
) such that

\begin{cases}(\rho,P,B)\in C([0,T^{*}];W^{1,q}),\quad(\rho_{t},P_{t},B_{t})\in C([0,T^{*}];L^{q}),\\
u\in C([0,T^{*}];H^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T^{*};W^{2,q}),\\
u_{t}\in L^{2}(0,T^{*};H^{1}),\quad\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\in L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};L^{2}).\end{cases}

(2.3)

Remark 2.1
.

The local existence result mentioned above also holds for the three-dimensional case, provided that the range of
q
is adjusted to
3<q\leq 6
. The proof is entirely analogous to that of the 2D case. This ensures the existence of the local solutions required to demonstrate finite time blow-up in the axisymmetric cylinders.

2.2
Finite time blow-up for 2D Disk

Now we consider the radially symmetric solutions of (
1.2
). In this case, we investigate solutions within the class of radially symmetric functions
(\rho,\vec{u},P,\vec{B})
defined by

\rho(x,t)=\rho(r,t),\quad\vec{u}(x,t)=\frac{(x_{1},x_{2})}{r}u(r,t),\quad P(x,t)=P(r,t),\quad\vec{B}(x,t)=\frac{(-x_{2},x_{1})}{r}B(r,t).

Here
(\rho,u,P,B)
are scalar functions.

In radially symmetric coordinates, the original system (
1.2
) can be written as

\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+(\rho u)_{r}+\frac{\rho u}{r}=0,\\[8.0pt]
\rho(u_{t}+uu_{r})+P_{r}=(2\mu+\lambda)\left(u_{r}+\frac{u}{r}\right)_{r}-B\left(B_{r}+\frac{B}{r}\right),\\[8.0pt]
P_{t}+uP_{r}+\gamma P\left(u_{r}+\frac{u}{r}\right)=0,\\[8.0pt]
B_{t}+(uB)_{r}=0,\end{cases}

(2.4)

with
0\leq r\leq R_{0}<\infty
and the initial data

\rho\left(r,0\right)=\rho_{0}\left(r\right),\quad u\left(r,0\right)=u_{0}\left(r\right),\quad P\left(r,0\right)=P_{0}\left(r\right),\quad B\left(r,0\right)=B_{0}(r),

(2.5)

and the Dirichlet boundary condition

u\left(R_{0},t\right)=0,\,t\geq 0.

(2.6)

Note also that the continuity of the velocity and magnetic field in the center will force

u(0,t)=B(0,t)=0,\quad t\geq 0.

(2.7)

Given the existence of local strong solutions in general bounded domains, we turn to the blow-up problem within a radially symmetric framework. This work presents the first proof of finite-time singularity formation for the simplified full Navier-Stokes equations in a regime where local strong solutions exist.

Theorem 2.2
.

(Blow-up of Strong Solutions for 2D Disk)
Let
\Omega=B_{R_{0}}
denote a two-dimensional disk of radius
R_{0}
centered at the origin. Suppose the initial data
(\rho_{0},\vec{u}_{0},P_{0},\vec{B}_{0})
for the initial-boundary value problem (
2.4
)-(
2.7
) satisfy the regularity conditions

\begin{cases}(\rho_{0},B_{0})\in W^{1,q}(\Omega),\quad\rho_{0}\geq 0,\\
\vec{u}_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega),\quad\vec{u}_{0}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla\cdot\vec{B}_{0}=0\text{ in }\Omega,

(2.8)

for some constant
q>2
, along with the compatibility condition:

-\mu\Delta\vec{u}_{0}-(\mu+\lambda)\nabla(\nabla\cdot\vec{u}_{0})+\nabla P_{0}+\vec{B}_{0}\times(\nabla\times\vec{B}_{0})=\rho_{0}^{1/2}\vec{g},\quad\mathrm{in}\Omega,

(2.9)

for some
\vec{g}\in L^{2}
. Under these conditions, Theorem
2.1
guarantees the existence of a unique local strong solution
(\rho,\vec{u},P,\vec{B})
on a time interval
[0,T^{*}]
.

Assume further that the initial data admit the following radial symmetry

\rho_{0}(\vec{x})=\rho_{0}(r),\quad\vec{u}_{0}(\vec{x})=\frac{\vec{x}}{r}u_{0}(r),\quad P_{0}(\vec{x})=P_{0}(r),\quad\vec{B}_{0}(\vec{x})=\frac{(-x_{2},x_{1})}{r}B_{0}(r).

(2.10)

Then, the unique strong solution preserves this symmetry, taking the form

\rho(\vec{x},t)=\rho(r,t),\quad\vec{u}(\vec{x},t)=\frac{\vec{x}}{r}u(r,t),\quad P(\vec{x},t)=P(r,t),\quad\vec{B}(\vec{x},t)=\frac{(-x_{2},x_{1})}{r}B(r,t),

(2.11)

where
\rho,u,P,B
are scalar functions.

If there exists a radius
r_{0}\in(0,R_{0})
such that the initial density and pressure vanish in the inner ball
B_{r_{0}}
, i.e.

\rho_{0}(r)=0,\,P_{0}(r)=0\quad\text{for }r\in[0,r_{0}],

(2.12)

while the magnetic field satisfies the non-degeneracy condition:

\int_{0}^{r_{0}}B_{0}(r)\,dr\neq 0,

(2.13)

then the local strong solution cannot exist globally and must blow up in finite time.

Specifically, for any constant
\alpha\in(1,2)
, the lifespan
T
of the solution is bounded by

T\leq\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\mu+\lambda}R_{0}}\frac{(2-\alpha)^{2}\left|\int_{0}^{r_{0}}{B_{0}}(r)\,dr\right|^{2}}{2(\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2\alpha-2}}+\frac{\alpha+1}{\sqrt{2\alpha}})}\right)^{-2}E_{0},

(2.14)

where
E_{0}
denotes the initial total energy

E_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}{\left(\rho_{0}|\vec{u}_{0}|^{2}+|\vec{B}_{0}|^{2}\right)}dx+\int_{\Omega}{\frac{P}{\gamma-1}}dx.

(2.15)

Remark 2.2
.

Compared to
[
7
]
, the model we consider is more general, allowing density and pressure to be independent variables. However, to analyze the balance between viscous stress and the magnetic Lorentz force, we require that the initial pressure also vanishes within the interior vacuum region i.e.
\rho_{0}(r)=0,\,P_{0}(r)=0
,
\text{for }r\in[0,r_{0}]
. Moreover, considering the state equation
P=\rho^{\gamma}\varGamma\left(\tilde{s}\right)
, this definition is entirely natural and reasonable.

Remark 2.3
.

According to
[
18
]
, classical solutions inevitably blow up if the initial density contains an isolated mass group, implying the presence of an annular vacuum region. In contrast, our method of proof necessitates that the initial density contains an interior vacuum region; an annular vacuum region does not work for our approach.

2.3
Finite time Blow-up for 3D Axisymmetric Cylinders

We now turn our attention to the solutions of (
1.2
) under the assumption of axisymmetry

\begin{split}&\rho(\vec{x},t)=\rho(r,t),\quad P(x,t)=P(r,t),\\
&\vec{u}(x,t)=u(r,t)\frac{(x_{1},x_{2},0)}{r}+v\left(r,t\right)\frac{\left(-x_{2},x_{1},0\right)}{r}+w\left(r,t\right)\left(0,0,1\right),\\
&\vec{B}(x,t)=\frac{(-x_{2},x_{1},0)}{r}B(r,t).\end{split}

Here
(\rho,u,v,w,P,B)
are scalar functions. Now we consider a domain
\Omega=\{\vec{x}\,|\,x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}<R_{0}^{2},x_{3}\in\mathbb{T}^{1}\}
, which represents a cylinder of radius
R_{0}
with periodic boundary conditions in the axial direction
x_{3}
with period 1.

In cylindrically symmetric coordinates, the original system (
1.2
) can be written as

\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+(\rho u)_{r}+\frac{\rho u}{r}=0,\\
\rho\left(u_{t}+uu_{r}-\frac{v^{2}}{r}\right)+P_{r}=(2\mu+\lambda)\left(u_{r}+\frac{u}{r}\right)_{r}-B\left(B_{r}+\frac{B}{r}\right),\\
\rho\left(v_{t}+uv_{r}+\frac{uv}{r}\right)=\mu\left(v_{r}+\frac{v}{r}\right)_{r},\\
\rho\left(w_{t}+uw_{r}\right)=\mu\frac{1}{r}(rw_{r})_{r},\\
P_{t}+uP_{r}+\gamma P\left(u_{r}+\frac{u}{r}\right)=0,\\
B_{t}+(uB)_{r}=0.\end{cases}

(2.16)

The initial data are given by

\begin{split}\rho\left(r,0\right)=\rho_{0}\left(r\right),\quad&u\left(r,0\right)=u_{0}\left(r\right),\quad v\left(r,0\right)=v_{0}\left(r\right),\quad w\left(r,0\right)=w_{0}\left(r\right),\\
&P\left(r,0\right)=P_{0}\left(r\right),\quad B\left(r,0\right)=B_{0}(r),\end{split}

(2.17)

and the Dirichlet boundary condition

u\left(R_{0},t\right)=v\left(R_{0},t\right)=w\left(R_{0},t\right)=0,\,t\geq 0.

(2.18)

Note also that the continuity of the velocity and magnetic field in the center will force

u(0,t)=v(0,t)=B(0,t)=0,\quad t\geq 0.

(2.19)

Theorem 2.3
.

(Blow-up of Strong Solutions for 3D Axisymmetric Cylinders) Let
\Omega
be a cylinder with radius
R_{0}
which is periodic in the

-direction with period 1. Suppose the initial data

for the initial-boundary value problem (

) satisfy the regularity conditions

\begin{cases}(\rho_{0},B_{0})\in W^{1,q}(\Omega),\quad\rho_{0}\geq 0,\\
\vec{u}_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega),\quad\vec{u}_{0}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla\cdot\vec{B}_{0}=0\text{ in }\Omega,

(2.20)

for some constant
3<q\leq 6
, along with the compatibility condition

-\mu\Delta\vec{u}_{0}-(\mu+\lambda)\nabla(\nabla\cdot\vec{u}_{0})+\nabla{P}_{0}+\vec{B}_{0}\times(\nabla\times\vec{B}_{0})=\rho_{0}^{1/2}\vec{g},\quad\mathrm{in}\Omega,

(2.21)

for some
\vec{g}\in L^{2}
. Under these conditions, Theorem
2.1
can be extended to guarantee the existence of a unique local strong solution
(\rho,\vec{u},P,\vec{B})
on the time interval
[0,T^{*}]
for the three-dimensional domain
\Omega=\{\vec{x}\,|\,x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}<R_{0}^{2},x_{3}\in\mathbb{T}^{1}\}
.

Assume further that the initial data admit the following cylindrical symmetry

\begin{split}\rho_{0}(\vec{x})=\rho_{0}(r),\quad&\vec{u}_{0}(\vec{x})=\frac{\left(x_{1},x_{2},0\right)}{r}u_{0}(r)+\frac{\left(-x_{2},x_{1},0\right)}{r}v_{0}\left(r\right)+\left(0,0,1\right)w_{0}\left(r\right),\\
&P_{0}(\vec{x})=P_{0}(r),\quad\vec{B}_{0}(\vec{x})=\frac{(-x_{2},x_{1},0)}{r}B_{0}(r).\end{split}

(2.22)

Then, the unique strong solution preserves this symmetry, taking the form

\begin{split}\rho(\vec{x},t)=\rho(r,t),\quad&\vec{u}(\vec{x},t)=\frac{\left(x_{1},x_{2},0\right)}{r}u(r,t)+\frac{\left(-x_{2},x_{1},0\right)}{r}v\left(r,t\right)+\left(0,0,1\right)w\left(r,t\right),\\
&P(\vec{x},t)=P(r,t),\quad\vec{B}(\vec{x},t)=\frac{(-x_{2},x_{1},0)}{r}B(r,t),\end{split}

(2.23)

where
\rho,u,P,B
are scalar functions.

If there exists a radius
r_{0}\in(0,R_{0})
such that the initial density and pressure vanish in the inner cylinder
\Omega_{1}=\{\vec{x}\,|\,x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}<r_{0}^{2},x_{3}\in\mathbb{T}^{1}\}
, i.e.

\rho_{0}(r)=0,\,P_{0}(r)=0\quad\text{for }r\in[0,r_{0}],

(2.24)

while the magnetic field satisfies the non-degeneracy condition

\int_{0}^{r_{0}}B_{0}(r)\,dr\neq 0,

(2.25)

then the local strong solution cannot exist globally and must blow up in finite time.

Specifically, for any constant
\alpha\in[\frac{7}{6},2)
, the lifespan

T\leq\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\mu+\lambda}R_{0}}\frac{(2-\alpha)^{2}\left|\int_{0}^{r_{0}}{B_{0}}(r)\,dr\right|^{2}}{2\sqrt{2}(\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2\alpha-2}}+\frac{\alpha+1}{\sqrt{2\alpha}})}\right)^{-2}E_{0},

(2.26)

where

denotes the initial energy

E_{0}=\int_{0}^{R_{0}}{\left(\frac{\rho_{0}}{2}\left(u_{0}^{2}+v_{0}^{2}+w_{0}^{2}\right)r+\frac{P_{0}}{\gamma-1}r+\frac{1}{2}B_{0}^{2}r\right)dr}.

(2.27)

.

We provide examples of blow-up in the three-dimensional case. In fact, the axisymmetric case corresponds exactly to that of a two-dimensional radially symmetric velocity field with swirl. Similarly, it can be proven that the equations governing a two-dimensional velocity field with swirl will also blow up in finite time.

Remark 2.5

Remark 2.6
.

We require

2.4
Finite time Blow-up for free boundary problem

With the local existence and blow-up results for fixed boundary established, we now turn our attention to the dynamic behavior of the system with a free boundary. Specifically, we investigate the evolution of a radially symmetric strong solution governed by a free boundary condition. In this case, the fluid domain is time-dependent, denoted by
\Omega_{t}=\{x:0\leq|x|\leq a(t)\}
, where the boundary radius
a(t)
is driven by the fluid velocity. The following theorem establishes that, even in this free boundary framework, finite-time singularity formation is inevitable under the presence of an interior vacuum and a non-trivial magnetic field.

Theorem 2.4
.

(Blow-up of free boundary problem) Assume that
(\rho,\vec{u},P,\vec{B})
is a 2D radially symmetric strong solution
(
as defined in (
2.3
)
)
of (
2.4
) with the initial data (
2.10
) and the boundary condition

u\left(0,t\right)=B\left(0,t\right)=0,\quad\left(\frac{B^{2}}{2}+P-\left(2\mu+\lambda\right)\left(u_{r}+\frac{u}{r}\right)\right)\left(a\left(t\right),t\right)=0,

(2.28)

where
a(t)
satisfies

a^{\prime}\left(t\right)=u\left(a\left(t\right),t\right),\quad a(0)=a_{0},

(2.29)

corresponding to the free surface of
\Omega_{t}
, where

\Omega_{t}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\left\{x\,|\,0\leq\left|x\right|\leq a\left(t\right)\right\}.

Furthermore, if there exists a constant
r_{0}\in(0,a(0))
such that

\rho_{0}(r)=0,\,P_{0}(r)=0\quad\text{for }r\in[0,r_{0}],

(2.30)

and

\int_{0}^{r_{0}}B_{0}(r)\,dr\neq 0,

(2.31)

then the strong solution
(\rho,\vec{u},P,\vec{B})
will blow up in finite time. Moreover, for any constant
\alpha\in(1,2)
, the lifespan
T
of the solution is bounded by

T\leq\exp\left(E_{0}\left(\frac{\left|\int_{0}^{r_{0}}{B_{0}}(r)\,dr\right|^{2}(2-\alpha)^{2}}{2\sqrt{2\mu+\lambda}C\left[\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2\alpha-2}}+\frac{\alpha+1}{\sqrt{2\alpha}}\right]}\right)^{-2}\right)-1,

(2.32)

where
C
is a constant depending on
a_{0},E_{0},\mu,\lambda
.

Remark 2.7
.

Physically, this boundary condition requires the effective viscous flux with the magnetic field,
F=P+\frac{1}{2}|B|^{2}-(2\mu+\lambda)\operatorname{div}u
, to vanish at the boundary. Under this condition, we are able to prove the fundamental energy inequality.

Remark 2.8
.

It is important to note that the local well-posedness of strong solutions for this free boundary problem remains an open question. The primary difficulty lies in the fact that existing literature typically considers fluids that are strictly bounded away from vacuum or physical vacuum models where the density vanishes only at the boundary. In contrast, our model involves the presence of an interior vacuum. Consequently, the blow-up result presented here is a conditional one.

Remark 2.9
.

When the pressure law is simplified to
P=A\rho^{\gamma}
, corresponding to the model studied in
[
7
]
, this conclusion remains valid. In this case, the pressure naturally vanishes in vacuum regions.

2.5
Sketch of the proof

We will prove the aforementioned theorems in three sections. In Section 3, we prove the blow-up of strong solutions by using a fractional moment argument. In the vacuum region, we initially obtain not the balance between viscous stress and the magnetic Lorentz force, but rather

(2\mu+\lambda)\partial_{r}\nabla\cdot\vec{u}=B\left(B_{r}+\frac{B}{r}\right)+P_{r}\quad\text{a.e.}

(2.33)

Since we define
P_{0}(r)=0
when
\rho_{0}(r)=0
for
r\in[0,r_{0}]
. The particle path starting from
r_{0}
satisfies the particle trajectory equation

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial\vec{X}}{\partial t}(x,t)=\vec{u}(X(x,t),t),\\
\vec{X}(r_{0},0)=r_{0}.\end{cases}

Let
R(t)

In this case, we are dealing with two distinct moving interfaces: the inner vacuum boundary, denoted by

; and the outer free surface, denoted by
a(t)
. The condition
\nabla\vec{u}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{\infty})
guarantees the uniqueness and smoothness of the particle trajectories, which implies that particle paths cannot intersect. Consequently, the inner vacuum boundary can never overtake the outer free surface. Therefore, we have

R(t)\leq a(t),\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,T).

(4.15)

Combining this with the growth estimate for the free boundary radius derived in Lemma
4.2
, we obtain a uniform upper bound for the vacuum radius

R(t)\leq a(t)\leq C(1+t)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.16)

Next, we analyze the dynamics within the vacuum region. As established in the fixed boundary case, the density and pressure vanish in
[0,R(t)]
, reducing the momentum equation to a balance between viscous stress and the Lorentz force

(2\mu+\lambda)\partial_{r}\nabla\cdot\vec{u}=B\left(B_{r}+\frac{B}{r}\right)\quad\text{a.e. in }(0,R(t)).

(4.17)

Notice that the interior vacuum boundary
R(t)
behaves essentially the same way as in the fixed boundary case discussed in Section
3
. Therefore, we can employ the identical fractional moment test function,
f(r)=R(t)r^{\alpha}-r^{\alpha+1}
with
1<\alpha<2
. By repeating the integration and estimation procedures over the interval
[0,R(t)]
, we get the lower bound for the
\|\nabla\cdot\vec{u}\|_{L^{2}}

\|\nabla\cdot\vec{u}\|_{L^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{(2\mu+\lambda)R(t)}\frac{C_{0}^{2}(2-\alpha)^{2}}{2\left[\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2\alpha-2}}+\frac{\alpha+1}{\sqrt{2\alpha}}\right]},

(4.18)

where the constant
C_{0}
represents the conserved total magnetic flux in the vacuum region, as defined in (
3.6
).

A critical step in the free boundary analysis is to relate this lower bound to the time variable
t
. Utilizing the geometric constraint (
4.16
), we can replace
R(t)
with its upper bound
C(1+t)^{1/2}
. This yields the following time-dependent lower estimate for the viscous dissipation

\displaystyle\|\nabla\cdot\vec{u}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{(2\mu+\lambda)a(t)}\frac{C_{0}^{2}(2-\alpha)^{2}}{2\left[\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2\alpha-2}}+\frac{\alpha+1}{\sqrt{2\alpha}}\right]}

(4.19)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{(2\mu+\lambda)C(1+t)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{C_{0}^{2}(2-\alpha)^{2}}{2\left[\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2\alpha-2}}+\frac{\alpha+1}{\sqrt{2\alpha}}\right]}.

We recall the global energy conservation law (
4.1
), which implies the integrability of the viscous dissipation over time

(2\mu+\lambda)\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla\cdot\vec{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,dt\leq E_{0}.

(4.20)

Substituting the lower bound (
4.19
) into this energy inequality, we obtain

(2\mu+\lambda)\left(\frac{C_{0}^{2}(2-\alpha)^{2}}{2(2\mu+\lambda)C\left[\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2\alpha-2}}+\frac{\alpha+1}{\sqrt{2\alpha}}\right]}\right)^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{1+t}\,dt\leq E_{0}.

(4.21)

Computing the time integral yields a logarithmic term
\log(1+T)
. Rearranging the inequality provides an explicit upper bound for the lifespan
T
of the strong solution

\log\mathrm{(}1+T)\leq E_{0}\left(\frac{C_{0}^{2}(2-\alpha)^{2}}{2\sqrt{2\mu+\lambda}C\left[\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2\alpha-2}}+\frac{\alpha+1}{\sqrt{2\alpha}}\right]}\right)^{-2}.

(4.22)

We conclude that the maximal existence time is finite

T\leq\exp\left(E_{0}\left(\frac{C_{0}^{2}(2-\alpha)^{2}}{2\sqrt{2\mu+\lambda}C\left[\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{2\alpha-2}}+\frac{\alpha+1}{\sqrt{2\alpha}}\right]}\right)^{-2}\right)-1.

(4.23)

This confirms that the strong solution must blow up in finite time.
∎

5
Local well-posedness

In this section, We provide the proof of the local well-posedness stated in Theorem
2.1
.

5.1
Linearized problem

We assume that
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with smooth boundary and first prove the local existence of strong solutions with positive densities to the linearized equation. Furthermore, we derive uniform estimates that are independent of the initial density’s lower bound. These crucial estimates will be applied in the proof of the existence of strong solutions with nonnegative densities.
Consider the following linearized problem for

\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+\nabla\cdot\left(\rho v\right)=0,\\
\left(\rho u\right)_{t}+\nabla\cdot\left(\rho v\otimes u\right)-\mu\Delta u-\left(\mu+\lambda\right)\nabla\left(\nabla\cdot u\right)+\nabla P=\left(\nabla\times B\right)\times B,\\
P_{t}+v\cdot\nabla P+\gamma P\nabla\cdot v=0,\\
B_{t}-\nabla\times\left(v\times B\right)=0,\\
\nabla\cdot B=0,\\
\end{cases}

(5.1)

with the initial boundary conditions

\begin{cases}\left(\rho,\rho u,P,B\right)|_{t=0}=\left(\rho_{0},\rho_{0}u_{0},P_{0},B_{0}\right),\\
u=0\,\,\,\mathrm{on}\,\partial\Omega.\\
\end{cases}

(5.2)

Here
\rho_{0},u_{0},P_{0},B_{0}
satisfy the following regularity conditions

\begin{cases}0\leq\rho_{0},P_{0}\in W^{1,q},B_{0}\in W^{1,q},\nabla\cdot B_{0}=0\,\mathrm{in}\,\Omega,\\
u_{0}\in H^{2},u_{0}|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\\
\end{cases}

(5.3)

for some
q>2
and the additional conditions

\begin{cases}\rho_{0}^{1/2}g=-\mu\Delta u_{0}-\left(\mu+\lambda\right)\nabla\left(\nabla\cdot u_{0}\right)+\nabla P_{0}+B_{0}\times\left(\nabla\times B_{0}\right),\mathrm{for}\,\mathrm{some}\,g\in L^{2},\\
\rho_{0}\geq\delta>0,\\
2+\left\|\left(\rho_{0},B_{0},P_{0}\right)\right\|_{W^{1,q}}+\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{2}}+\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<c_{0}.\\
\end{cases}

(5.4)

The given function
v
satisfies

v(0)=u_{0},v|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

(5.5)

and the following regularity conditions

v\in C\left(\left[0,T^{*}\right];H^{2}\right)\cap L^{2}\left(0,T^{*};W^{2,q}\right),v_{t}\in L^{2}\left(0,T^{*};H^{1}\right).

(5.6)

Moreover,
v
is required to meet the following bounds

\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(0,T^{*};H^{1}\right)}+\kappa^{-1}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(0,T^{*};H^{2}\right)}+\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(0,T^{*};H^{1}\right)}+\left\|v\right\|_{{L^{2}}\left(0,T^{*};W^{2,q}\right)}\leq c_{1}.

(5.7)

The above fixed positive constants
c_{0}
,
c_{1}
,
\kappa
, and
T^{*}
satisfy

1<c_{0}<c_{1}<c_{2}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\kappa c_{1},\,\mathrm{and}\,0<T^{*}<\infty.

(5.8)

We begin by establishing an existence result for the case of positive initial densities.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with a smooth boundary. Assume that the initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0},B_{0},P_{0})
satisfy the regularity condition (
5.3
) and (
5.4
). Suppose further that the function
v
satisfies condition (
5.5
), (
5.6
) and (
5.7
).
Then the initial-boundary value problem (
5.1
), (
5.2
) admits a unique strong solution with the property that

\begin{cases}\left(\rho,P,B\right)\in C\left(\left[0,T_{*}\right];W^{1,q}\right),\left(\rho_{t},P_{t},B_{t}\right)\in C\left(\left[0,T_{*}\right];L^{q}\right),\,\,\\
u\in C\left(\left[0,T_{*}\right];H^{2}\right)\cap L^{2}\left(0,T_{*};W^{2,q}\right),u_{t}\in L^{2}\left(0,T_{*};H^{1}\right),\\
\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\in L^{\infty}\left(0,T_{*};L^{2}\right),\\
\end{cases}

(5.9)

where
T_{*}=T_{3}
is defined in (
5.32
).

Moreover, there exists a constant
C
such that

\begin{cases}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L^{\infty}W^{1,q}}+\left\|\rho_{t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}L^{q}}+\left\|P\right\|_{L^{\infty}W^{1,q}}+\left\|P_{t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}L^{q}}+\left\|B\right\|_{L^{\infty}W^{1,q}}+\left\|B_{t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}L^{q}}\leq Cc^{2}_{2},\\
\left\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\right\|_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\leq Cc_{0}^{\frac{5}{2}},\\
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}H^{1}}+\kappa^{-1}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}H^{2}}+\int_{0}^{T_{*}}{\left\|u_{t}\left(s\right)\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\left\|u\left(s\right)\right\|_{W^{2,q}}^{2}ds}\leq c_{1},\\
\end{cases}

(5.10)

provided

c_{1}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}3Cc_{0}^{7},c_{2}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}27C^{4}c_{0}^{\frac{37}{2}},\kappa\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}9C^{3}c_{0}^{\frac{23}{2}}.

(5.11)

Proof.

The existence of a unique solution
\rho
was demonstrated by Diperna and Lions
[
2
]
, a result also noted in Lemma 3 of
[
1
]
. It follows from (2.11) in
[
1
]
that

\left\|\rho\left(t\right)\right\|_{W^{1,q}}\leq\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{W^{1,q}}\exp\left(C\int_{0}^{t}{\left\|\nabla v\left(s\right)\right\|_{W^{1,q}}ds}\right).

(5.12)

Note that

\int_{0}^{T_{1}}{\left\|\nabla v\left(s\right)\right\|_{W^{1,q}}ds}\leq T_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{T_{1}}{\left\|\nabla v\left(s\right)\right\|_{W^{1,q}}^{2}ds}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq Cc_{1}T_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C,

(5.13)

provided

T_{1}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}c_{1}^{-2}<1.

(5.14)

We concluded that for
0\leq t\leq\min(T_{1},T^{*})
,

\left\|\rho\left(t\right)\right\|_{W^{1,q}}\leq Cc_{0},

and

\begin{split}\left\|\rho_{t}\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{q}}&=\left\|\nabla\cdot\left(\rho v\right)\right\|_{L^{q}}\leq C\left(\left\|\rho\right\|_{W^{1,q}}\left\|v\right\|_{W^{1,q}}\right)\\
&\leq Cc_{0}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{2}}\leq Cc_{0}c_{2}.\end{split}

As a consequence of the transport equation representation, we have

\rho\left(t,x\right)\geq\delta\exp\left[-\int_{0}^{t}{\left\|\nabla v\left(s\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}ds}\right].

(5.15)

Therefore, for
0\leq t\leq\min(T_{1},T^{*})
, it holds that

C^{-1}\delta\leq\rho\left(t,x\right)\leq Cc_{0}.

The next step is to derive the regularity of
B
for the linearized system (
5.1
).
Since

B_{t}+\left(v\cdot\nabla\right)B-\left(B\cdot\nabla\right)v+B\nabla\cdot v=0,

(5.16)

multiplying by
\left|B\right|^{q-2}B
and integrating by parts, we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int{\left|B\right|^{q}dx}&\leq C\int{\left|\nabla v\right|\left|B\right|^{q}dx}\leq C\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|B\right\|_{L^{q}}^{q}.\end{split}

(5.17)

Then differentiating (
5.16
) with respect to
x_{j}
, multiplying by
\partial_{j}B_{i}\left|\partial_{j}B_{i}\right|^{q-2}
and integrating by parts, we have

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int{\left|\partial_{j}B_{i}\right|^{q}dx}&\leq C\int{\left|\nabla v\right|\left|\nabla B\right|^{q}+\left|B\right|\left|\nabla B\right|^{q-1}\left|\nabla^{2}v\right|dx}\\
&\leq C\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla B\right\|_{L^{q}}^{q}+C\left\|\nabla^{2}v\right\|_{L^{q}}\left\|\nabla B\right\|_{L^{q}}^{q-1}\left\|B\right\|_{L^{\infty}}.\end{split}

(5.18)

Combining (
5.17
) and (
5.18
), and using Sobolev inequality yields

\frac{d}{dt}\left\|B\right\|_{W^{1,q}}\leq C\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{W^{1,q}}\left\|B\right\|_{W^{1,q}}.

(5.19)

An application of Gronwall’s inequality and (
5.13
) implies that, for
0\leq t\leq\min(T_{1},T^{*})
,

\left\|B\left(t\right)\right\|_{W^{1,q}}\leq Cc_{0},

and

\begin{split}\left\|B_{t}\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{q}}&\leq C\left(\left\|B\right\|_{W^{1,q}}\left\|v\right\|_{W^{1,q}}\right)\\
&\leq Cc_{0}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{2}}\leq Cc_{0}c_{2}.\end{split}

We now turn to the existence of the pressure
P
and the corresponding estimates. The approach for estimating the pressure is similar to that used for the density and the magnetic field, since the pressure
P
satisfies

P_{t}+v\cdot\nabla P+\gamma P\nabla\cdot v=0.

(5.20)

By applying the same method used for (
5.18
) and (
5.19
), we can easily obtain

\left\|P\left(t\right)\right\|_{W^{1,q}}\leq\left\|P_{0}\right\|_{W^{1,q}}\exp\left(C\int_{0}^{t}{\left\|\nabla v\left(s\right)\right\|_{W^{1,q}}ds}\right).

(5.21)

Therefore, for
0\leq t\leq\min(T_{1},T^{*})
, we get

\left\|P\left(t\right)\right\|_{W^{1,q}}\leq Cc_{0},

and

\begin{split}\left\|P_{t}\left(t\right)\right\|_{L^{q}}&=\left\|v\cdot\nabla P+\gamma P\nabla\cdot v\right\|_{L^{q}}\leq C\left(\left\|P\right\|_{W^{1,q}}\left\|v\right\|_{W^{1,q}}\right)\\
&\leq Cc_{0}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{2}}\leq Cc_{0}c_{2}.\end{split}

Finally, we prove the existence of
u
to the linearized system (
5.1
). We differentiate
\eqref{2.1}_{2}
with respect to
t
and multiply the resulting equation by
u_{t}
, we arrive at

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}&\frac{d}{dt}\int{\rho u_{t}^{2}dx}+\int{\mu\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|^{2}+\left(\mu+\lambda\right)\left|\nabla\cdot u_{t}\right|^{2}dx}\\
&=\int{\left(-\nabla P_{t}-\rho_{t}v\cdot\nabla u-\rho\left(2v\cdot\nabla u_{t}+v_{t}\cdot\nabla u\right)+\nabla\cdot\left(BB_{t}I-2B\otimes B_{t}\right)\right)u_{t}dx}.\end{split}

(5.22)

A combined application of the Hölder, Young and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities yields

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}&\int{\rho u_{t}^{2}dx}+\int{\mu\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|^{2}dx}\\
\leq&C\left(\left\|P_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\rho_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+\left\|\sqrt{\rho}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right.\\
&\left.+\left\|B\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|B_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\sqrt{\rho}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|v_{t}\right\|_{L^{4}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{4}}\left\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)\\
\leq&C\left(\left\|P_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\rho_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+\left\|\sqrt{\rho}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|B\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|B_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\\
&+\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\left\|\sqrt{\rho}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{H^{1}}+\varepsilon\left\|\nabla v_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\end{split}

(5.23)

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Using the fact that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int{\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}dx}=\int{\nabla u\cdot\nabla u_{t}dx}\leq\frac{\mu}{2}\int{\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|^{2}dx}+C\int{\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}dx},

(5.24)

and it follows from the elliptic regularity results that

\begin{split}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{H^{1}}&\leq C\left(\left\|\rho u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\rho v\cdot\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\nabla P\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|B\right\|_{W^{1,q}}\left\|\nabla B\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)\\
&\leq C\left(c_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}+c_{0}c_{2}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}+c_{0}+c_{0}^{2}\right),\end{split}

(5.25)

so one can get

\begin{split}\left\|\rho_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{4}}^{2}&\leq Cc_{0}^{2}c_{2}^{4}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\\
&\leq Cc_{0}^{2}c_{2}^{4}\left(c_{0}\left\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{0}^{2}c_{2}^{2}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{0}^{4}\right)\\
&\leq Cc_{0}^{3}c_{2}^{4}\left\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+Cc_{0}^{4}c_{2}^{6}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+Cc_{0}^{6}c_{2}^{4}.\end{split}

(5.26)

Substituting (
5.24
)-(
5.26
) into (
5.23
) yields

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}&\int{\rho u_{t}^{2}+\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}dx}+\int{\mu\left|\nabla u_{t}\right|^{2}dx}\\
\leq&C\left(c_{0}^{2}c_{2}^{2}+c_{0}^{3}c_{2}^{4}\left\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{0}^{4}c_{2}^{6}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{0}^{6}c_{2}^{4}+c_{0}c_{2}^{2}\left\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{0}^{4}c_{2}^{2}\right)\\

\leq&C\left(c_{0}^{3}c_{2}^{4}\left\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{0}^{4}c_{2}^{6}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{0}^{6}c_{2}^{4}\right)\\
&+\frac{C}{\varepsilon}c_{0}\left(\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{0}\left\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{0}^{2}c_{2}^{2}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{0}^{2}\right)+\varepsilon\left\|\nabla v_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
\leq&C\left(c_{0}^{6}c_{2}^{4}+\frac{c_{0}^{3}}{\varepsilon}\right)+C\left(c_{0}^{3}c_{2}^{4}+\frac{c_{0}^{2}}{\varepsilon}\right)\left\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&+C\left(c_{0}^{4}c_{2}^{6}+\frac{c_{0}^{3}c_{2}^{2}}{\varepsilon}\right)\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\left\|\nabla v_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
\leq&\left(\frac{C}{\varepsilon}c_{2}^{10}+\varepsilon\left\|\nabla v_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\left(1+\left\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).\end{split}

(5.27)

Using Gronwall’s inequality, we get

\begin{split}&\underset{0\leq t\leq T_{2}}{\mathrm{sup}}\left(\left\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\int_{0}^{T_{2}}{\left\|\nabla u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt}\\
\leq&\exp\left(C\int_{0}^{T_{2}}{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}c_{2}^{10}+\varepsilon\left\|\nabla v_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt}\right)\left(\underset{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}{\lim}\left\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T_{2}}{\frac{C}{\varepsilon}c_{2}^{10}+\varepsilon\left\|\nabla v_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt}\right).\end{split}

(5.28)

Since one can rewrite
\eqref{2.1}_{2}
as

\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{t}=-\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}v\cdot\nabla u-g.

Therefore, it holds that

\begin{split}\underset{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}{\lim}\left\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}&=\underset{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}{\lim}\left\|\sqrt{\rho}v\cdot\nabla u+g\right\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq Cc_{0}^{\frac{5}{2}}.\end{split}

Taking
\varepsilon=c_{1}^{-2},T_{2}=\min\left(T^{*},T_{1},\frac{1}{c_{1}^{2}c_{2}^{10}}\right)
, it follows from (

\underset{0\leq t\leq T_{2}}{\mathrm{sup}}\left(\left\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\int_{0}^{T_{2}}{\left\|\nabla u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt}\leq Cc_{0}^{5}.

\begin{split}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{H^{1}}&\leq C\left(c_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}+c_{0}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{4}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{4}}+\left\|\nabla P\right\|_{L^{2}}+c_{0}^{2}\right)\\

we have

\begin{split}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{H^{1}}&\leq C\left(c_{0}^{3}+c_{0}^{2}c_{1}^{2}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)\\
&\leq Cc_{0}^{\frac{9}{2}}c_{1}^{2}.\end{split}

We then invoke the standard elliptic regularity theory, which allows us to deduce that

\begin{split}\left\|u\right\|_{W^{2,q}}&\leq C\left(\left\|\rho u_{t}\right\|_{L^{q}}+\left\|\rho v\cdot\nabla u\right\|_{L^{q}}+\left\|\nabla P\right\|_{L^{q}}+\left\|B\times\left(\nabla\times B\right)\right\|_{L^{q}}\right)\\
&\leq C\left(c_{0}\left\|\nabla u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}+c_{0}c_{2}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{q}}+c_{0}+\left\|B\right\|_{W^{1,q}}\left\|\nabla B\right\|_{L^{q}}\right)\\
&\leq C\left(c_{0}\left\|\nabla u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}+c_{0}c_{2}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{q}}+c_{0}^{2}\right)\\
&\leq C\left(c_{0}\left\|\nabla u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}+c_{0}^{\frac{11}{2}}c_{1}^{2}c_{2}\right).\end{split}

(5.30)

Then

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T_{3}}{\left\|u\right\|_{W^{2,q}}^{2}ds}&\leq C\left(\int_{0}^{T_{3}}{c_{0}^{11}c_{1}^{4}c_{2}^{2}ds}+\int_{0}^{T_{3}}{c_{0}^{2}\left\|\nabla u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds}\right)\\
&\leq CT_{3}c_{0}^{11}c_{1}^{4}c_{2}^{2}+Cc_{0}^{7}\leq Cc_{0}^{7},\end{split}

(5.31)

provided

T_{3}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=}\min\left(T_{2},\frac{1}{c_{0}^{4}c_{1}^{4}c_{2}^{2}}\right).

(5.32)

Taking
c_{1}=3Cc_{0}^{7},c_{2}=27C^{4}c_{0}^{\frac{37}{2}},\kappa=9C^{3}c_{0}^{\frac{23}{2}}

(\rho,u,P,B)
in the following strong sense

\left(\rho^{\left(i\right)},P^{\left(i\right)},B^{\left(i\right)}\right)\rightarrow\left(\rho,P,B\right)\,\,\mathrm{in}\,\,L^{\infty}\left(0,T^{*};L^{2}\right),u^{\left(i\right)}\rightarrow u\,\,\mathrm{in}\,\,L^{2}\left(0,T^{*};H^{1}\right).

A standard argument shows that
(\rho,u,P,B)
is a weak solution to the nonlinear problem. Due to the uniform estimates (
5.37
), the limit functions
(\rho,u,P,B)
inherit the regularity properties of the approximate sequence

\begin{split}\underset{0\leq t\leq T^{*}}{\mathrm{sup}}&\left\|\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{T^{*}}{\left\|u_{t}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\left\|u\right\|_{W^{2,q}}^{2}dt}\\
&+\underset{0\leq t\leq T^{*}}{\mathrm{sup}}\left(\left\|\left(\rho,P,B\right)\right\|_{W^{1,q}}+\left\|\left(\rho_{t},P_{t},B_{t}\right)\right\|_{L^{q}}+\left\|u\right\|_{H^{2}}\right)\leq C.\end{split}

(5.50)

Consequently, the limit pair
(\rho,u,P,B)
satisfies the equations almost everywhere. Thus, we conclude that
(\rho,u,P,B)
is the strong solution to the nonlinear problem on the time interval
[0,T^{*}]
. Finally, we prove the uniqueness of the strong solutions.

Let
(\rho_{1},u_{1},P_{1},B_{1})
and
(\rho_{2},u_{2},P_{2},B_{2})
be two strong solutions to the problem (
1.2
) in the class defined by (
5.9
) and (
5.10
) with the same initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0},P_{0},B_{0})
.

Define the difference functions

\tilde{\rho}=\rho_{1}-\rho_{2},\quad\tilde{u}=u_{1}-u_{2},\quad\tilde{P}=P_{1}-P_{2},\quad\tilde{B}=B_{1}-B_{2}.

Then we get the error system

\begin{cases}\tilde{\rho}_{t}+\nabla\cdot(\tilde{\rho}u_{1})+\nabla\cdot(\rho_{2}\tilde{u})=0,\\
\rho_{1}\tilde{u}_{t}+\rho_{1}u_{1}\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}-\mu\Delta\tilde{u}-(\mu+\lambda)\nabla(\nabla\cdot\tilde{u})\\
\quad=-\tilde{\rho}(u_{2})_{t}-\tilde{\rho}u_{2}\cdot\nabla u_{2}-\rho_{1}\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla u_{2}-\nabla\tilde{P}\\
\quad\quad+(B_{1}\cdot\nabla)\tilde{B}+(\tilde{B}\cdot\nabla)B_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla(\tilde{B}\cdot(B_{1}+B_{2})),\\
\tilde{P}_{t}+u_{1}\cdot\nabla\tilde{P}+\gamma P_{1}\nabla\cdot\tilde{u}=-\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla P_{2}-\gamma\tilde{P}\nabla\cdot u_{2},\\
\tilde{B}_{t}-\nabla\times(u_{1}\times\tilde{B})-\nabla\times(\tilde{u}\times B_{2})=0,\\
\nabla\cdot\tilde{B}=0.\end{cases}

(5.51)

Multiplying the first equation of (
5.51
) by
\tilde{\rho}
and integrating by parts, we have

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&=-\int{\nabla}\cdot(\tilde{\rho}u_{1})\tilde{\rho}\,dx-\int{\nabla}\cdot(\rho_{2}\tilde{u})\tilde{\rho}\,dx\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int{(}\nabla\cdot u_{1})|\tilde{\rho}|^{2}\,dx-\int{(}\nabla\rho_{2}\cdot\tilde{u}+\rho_{2}\nabla\cdot\tilde{u})\tilde{\rho}\,dx\\
&\leq C\|\nabla u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\left(\|\nabla\rho_{2}\|_{L^{q}}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\rho_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}\right)\\
&\leq C\left(\|\nabla u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\frac{\|\rho_{2}\|_{W^{1,q}}^{2}}{\varepsilon}\right)\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\end{split}

(5.52)

where
\varepsilon>0
is a small constant to be determined.
Similarly, for the pressure
\tilde{P}
, we arrive at

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\tilde{P}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&\leq C\left(\|\nabla u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\frac{\|P_{2}\|_{W^{1,q}}^{2}}{\varepsilon}\right)\|\tilde{P}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

(5.53)

Taking
L^{2}
inner product of the fourth equation of
(
5.51
)
with
\tilde{B}
, we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\tilde{B}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&=-\int{(u_{1}\cdot\nabla\tilde{B})\cdot\tilde{B}\,dx}-\int{(\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla B_{2})\cdot\tilde{B}\,dx}\\
&\quad+\int{((B_{1}\cdot\nabla)\tilde{u}+(\tilde{B}\cdot\nabla)u_{2})\cdot\tilde{B}\,dx}\\
&\quad-\int{(B_{1}(\nabla\cdot\tilde{u})+\tilde{B}(\nabla\cdot u_{2}))\cdot\tilde{B}\,dx}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int{(\nabla\cdot u_{1})|\tilde{B}|^{2}\,dx}+\int{(\tilde{B}\cdot\nabla u_{2})\cdot\tilde{B}\,dx}-\int{|\tilde{B}|^{2}(\nabla\cdot u_{2})\,dx}\\
&\quad+\int{((B_{1}\cdot\nabla)\tilde{u}-B_{1}(\nabla\cdot\tilde{u})-(\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla)B_{2})\cdot\tilde{B}\,dx}\\
&\leq C\left(\|\nabla u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\|\tilde{B}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\quad+C\left(\|B_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla B_{2}\|_{L^{q}}\right)\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}\|\tilde{B}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\left(\|\nabla u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\frac{\|B_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\|\nabla B_{2}\|_{L^{q}}^{2}}{\varepsilon}\right)\|\tilde{B}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

(5.54)

Now multiplying the second equation of (
5.51
) by
\tilde{u}
and integrating by parts

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}&\int{\rho_{1}|\tilde{u}|^{2}dx}+\mu\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left(\mu+\lambda\right)\|\nabla\cdot\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
=&-\int{\tilde{\rho}\left((u_{2})_{t}+u_{2}\cdot\nabla u_{2}\right)\cdot\tilde{u}\,dx}-\int{\rho_{1}(\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla u_{2})\cdot\tilde{u}\,dx}\\
&+\int{\tilde{P}\nabla\cdot\tilde{u}\,dx}\\
&+\int{\left(-(B_{1}\cdot\nabla)\tilde{u}\cdot\tilde{B}+(\tilde{B}\cdot\nabla)B_{2}\cdot\tilde{u}\right)\,dx}+\int{\left(\tilde{B}\cdot\frac{B_{1}+B_{2}}{2}\right)\nabla\cdot\tilde{u}\,dx}\\
\leq&\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}\|(u_{2})_{t}+u_{2}\cdot\nabla u_{2}\|_{L^{q}}\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\sqrt{\rho_{1}}\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&+\|\tilde{P}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&+\|B_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}\|\tilde{B}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\tilde{B}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla B_{2}\|_{L^{q}}\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\left(\|B_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|B_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\|\tilde{B}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}\\
\leq&C\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\sqrt{\rho_{1}}\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\left(\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\tilde{P}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\tilde{B}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\varepsilon\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\end{split}

(5.55)

where
C_{\varepsilon}
depends on
\left\|\left(u_{2}\right)_{t}\right\|_{L^{q}},\left\|u_{2}\right\|_{W^{1,q}},\left\|B_{1}\right\|_{W^{1,q}},\left\|B_{2}\right\|_{W^{1,q}}
.

Combining (
5.52
)-(
5.55
), and let
\varepsilon=\mu/8
, we get

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}&\int{\rho_{1}|\tilde{u}|^{2}dx}+\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\tilde{P}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\tilde{B}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\mu\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
\leq&C\left(\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\tilde{P}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\tilde{B}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\\
&+C\left(\|\nabla u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\left(\|\tilde{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\tilde{P}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\tilde{B}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\rho_{1}}\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).\end{split}

(5.56)

Using Gronwall inequality and the integrability of
\|\nabla u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}
, we finally get
\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}
,
P_{1}=P_{2}
,
B_{1}=B_{2}
, and
u_{1}=u_{2}
almost everywhere. The proof is complete.
∎

Local and global solvability of the Grushin heat equation with memory
Ahmad Z. Fino & Arlucio Viana

Abstract
In this work, we investigate the solvability of a heat equation involving the Grushin operator. The equation is perturbed by two nonlinear reaction terms, one of which includes a memory component, introducing nonlocal effects in time. We first establish local well-posedness results in both Lebesgue spaces and in the space of continuous functions vanishing at infinity. Furthermore, we develop and apply comparison principles that allow us to derive sufficient conditions for the global existence of solutions, depending on the relative strength and nature of the nonlinearities involved. In particular, we highlight how the presence of the memory term influences global solvability. Our results contribute to the understanding of parabolic equations with degenerate operators and complex nonlinear interactions.

Introduction

Global existence of solutions for the heat equation with power-type nonlinearities goes back to Fujita’s paper
Fujita
, where he proves existence and nonexistence results for the semilinear heat equation

\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}(x,t)=\Delta u(x,t)+|u(x,t)|^{\rho-1}u(x,t),&\quad\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,\infty),\\
\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&\quad\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\end{array}

(1.1)

The critical case
\rho=1+\frac{2}{N}
was resolved by Weissler
Weissler
. Since then many works have been devoted to study results of this type, and many of them predicted the critical Fujita exponent from the critical space, which is given by a scaling computation. Later on, Brézis and Cazenave treated the local well-posedness for (
1.1
) in Lebesgue spaces. Nonetheless, nonlocal nonlinearities play an important role in the analysis of the evolution equations, for example, dissociating the critical Fujita exponent to the one given by scaling procedures. In fact, Cazenave, Dickstein and Weissler
CDW
proved that the critical exponent for

u_{t}-\Delta u=\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\gamma}|u|^{p-1}u(s)ds\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},t>0,

where
0\leq\gamma<1
,
p>1
, is
p_{*}=\max\left\{\frac{1}{\gamma},p_{\gamma}\right\}
, where

p_{\gamma}=1+\frac{2(2-\gamma)}{(N-2+2\gamma)_{+}},

while the exponent predicted by the scaling is
p_{\mathrm{sc}}=1+\frac{4-2\gamma}{N}
. Fino and Kirane
Fino-Ki-12
generalized that result by replacing the Laplacian with the fractional Laplacian. This type of investigation is became noticeable, because equations with distinct operators often have different scaling exponents, which bring new difficulties inherent to the equations, see e.g.
Cas-Loy-19
;
Dao-Fino-22
;
Ki-La-T-05
;
Loy-P-14
.

At the same time, sub-elliptic operators are of great scientific interest partly for they are naturally connected with anisotropic diffusion phenomena. Examples of such operators are the Heisenberg sub-Laplacian, Kohn Laplacians, Grushin operator. Each one of them carries its own geometric properties that highly influence its analysis, see e.g.
Alves-G-Lo-24
;
Calin-11
;
Chang-Li-12
;
Gaveau-77
;
Goldstein-Kogoj
;
Kogoj-Lan-12
;
Wu-15
. Heat kernels of those type of operators have been studied, for instance, in
Ba-Furutani-15
;
Arous-89
;
Chang-Li-15
;
Gaveau-77
. We can find a good survey of techniques to find the heat kernel of sub-elliptic operators in Callin’s
et al
monograph
Calin-11
.

Recently, there is an increasing interest for nonlinear parabolic equations with sub-elliptic operators, see e.g. the recent papers
Fino-Ruz-Tor-24
;
Hi-Oka-24
;
J-Ki-S-16
;
RuzY-22
. Those papers lead mostly with sub-Laplacian operators and do not include the Grushin

\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\Delta_{x}+|x|^{2}\Delta_{y}\right),

where
\Delta_{x},\Delta_{y}
denote the classical Laplacian in the variables
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, respectively. In this direction, Oliveira and Viana
Oli-Vi-23
proved the existence, uniqueness, continuous dependence and blowup alternative of local mild solutions for

\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}=\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}u+|u|^{\rho-1}u,&\quad\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N+k}\times(0,\infty),\\
u(0)=u_{0},&\quad\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N+k},\end{array}

(1.2)

with initial conditions in Lebesgue spaces. Through the analysis of the heat kernel associated to the Grushin operator, they obtain the existence of global solutions in the special case of
u_{0}\in L^{\frac{N}{2}(\rho-1)}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
with the sufficiently small norm. Later on, Kogoj, Lima and Viana
Kogoj-Lima-Viana-24
by working in Marcinkiewcz spaces, they allowed larger (in Lebesgue norm) initial data to be taken into account in order to obtain global solutions. More precisely, they gave sufficient conditions to the existence and uniqueness of mild solutions for (
1.2
), with initial conditions in the critical Marcinkiewcz space
L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
, with

p=\frac{N+2k}{2}(\rho-1).

(1.3)

Moreover, they obtained the existence of positive, symmetric and self-similar solutions. The number
N+2k
is the so-called homogeneous dimension attached to Grushin’s operator (see
Kogoj-Lan-18
).

Motivated by those facts, we recognize the relevancy of Fujita exponents for heat equations with the Grushin operator and memory:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{u_{t}-\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}u=k_{1}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\gamma}\lvert u\rvert^{p_{1}-1}u(s)\,\mathrm{d}s}+k_{2}|u|^{p_{2}-1}u,&\displaystyle{t>0,z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+k},}\\
\\
\displaystyle{u(x,0)=u_{0}(z),\qquad\qquad}&\displaystyle{z=(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+k},}\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

where
u_{0}\in X
,
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
p_{1},p_{2}>1
. Here,
X
denotes either
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
, with
1\leq q<\infty
, or
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
. In this manuscript we address to the problem of existence, uniqueness, continuous dependence and a blow-up alternative for local solutions of (
1.4
) as well as the existence of positive global solution for (
1.4
).

The local existence results are similar to those given in
BrezisCaz
and
CDW
, in the case of the classical diffusion equation and the heat equation with memory in the source, respectively. The equation (
1.4
) without memory, that is,
k_{1}=0
, was studied in Oliveira and Viana
Oli-Vi-23
.

We prove results on the existence of local solutions for (
1.4
), when initial conditions are either in Lebesgue spaces or are continuous functions vanishing at infinity. The results on global existence of positive solutions always assume that the initial condition is in
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+2k})
, but it is used mostly to get prove regularity of the mild solutions. Weaker formulations for them would require only the initial conditions to be in Lebesgue spaces the most of our proofs can be used.

The manuscript is organized in the following way. We bring the main already known tools in Section
2
. In Section
3
, we state and prove the results local well-posedenss result and the comparison principle. Finally, we give sufficient conditions for the global existence of positive solutions in Section
4
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section we gather definitions and results used in our proofs. They are related to the heat kernel associated to the Grushin operator, the notion of solutions, tools from the Fractional Calculus and integrodifferential inequalities.

2.1
Heat kernel

We begin by recalling the
explicit
form of the heat kernel associated with the Grushin operator (see, e.g.,
(
Oli-Vi-23
, Proposition 2.5)
or
Garofalo-Trallli-22
):

K(x,x_{0},y;t)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{N+2k}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\left(\frac{|\xi|}{\sinh(|\xi|t)}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}e^{i\xi\cdot y-\frac{|\xi|}{2}\left((|x|^{2}+|x_{0}|^{2})\coth(|\xi|t)-2x\cdot x_{0}\mathrm{csch}(|\xi|t)\right)}\mathrm{d}\xi,

(2.1)

for
(x,x_{0},y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2N+k}
, and
t>0
. The kernel given above is smooth, i.e.,
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2N+k}\times(0,\infty))
.

Furthermore, the following results either are collected from
Bi-Bra-23
;
Kogoj-Lima-Viana-24
;
Oli-Vi-23
or follow from them. We keep in mind that the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
is dense in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
for
1\leq p<\infty
, and in
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
. Here,
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
denotes the space of continuous functions vanishing at infinity, endowed with the supremum norm
\|\cdotp\|_{\infty}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

If u denotes the solution of the heat equation with the Grushin operator,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u-\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}u=0,&\qquad\mathrm{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N+k}\times(0,\infty),\\
\\
u(0)=u_{0}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}),&\qquad\mathrm{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N+k},\end{array}

(2.2)

then we have

u(x,y,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+k}}K(x,w,y-z;t)u_{0}(w,z)\,dw\,dz,\qquad(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+k},\,\,t>0,

(2.3)

where
(x,x_{0},y;t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2N+k}\times(0,\infty)\mapsto K(x,x_{0},y;t)
denotes the heat kernel associated to
-\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}
. Moreover,
u\in C([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{N+k})\cap C^{1}((0,\infty),C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))
.

Proposition 2.2
.

There is a unique semigroup
(S_{\mathcal{G}}(t))_{t\geq 0}
associated to
\Delta_{\mathcal{G}}
and satisfying the following properties:

(i)

For all
\varphi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we have

S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)\varphi(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+k}}K(x,w,y-z;t)\varphi(w,z)\,dw\,dz,\qquad(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+k},\,\,t>0.

(ii)

(S_{\mathcal{G}}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a contraction semigroup on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, which is strongly continuous for
1\leq p<\infty
.

(iii)

(S_{\mathcal{G}}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a strongly continuous semigroup on
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
.

(iv)

For every
v\in X
, where
X
is either
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
for
1\leq p<\infty
or
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
, the map
t\longmapsto S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)v
is continuous from
[0,\infty)
into
X
.

(v)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N+k}}K(x,0,y;t)\,dx\,dy=1
, for all
t>0
.

(vi)

The heat kernel satisfies

K(x,x_{0},y;t)>0,\qquad\textrm{for all}\,\,(x,x_{0},y;t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2N+k}\times(0,\infty).

Hence,
S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)\varphi\geq 0
whenever
\varphi\geq 0
, for all
t\geq 0
.

(vii)

K(rx,rx_{0},r^{2}y;r^{2}t)=r^{-(N+2k)}K(x,x_{0},y;t)
, for all
(x,x_{0},y;t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2N+k}\times(0,\infty)
,
r>0
.

(viii)

For all
(x,x_{0},y;t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2N+k}\times(0,\infty)
, we have

K(x,x_{0},-y;t)=K(x,x_{0},y;t)\quad\mathrm{and}\quad K(x,x_{0},y;t)=K(x_{0},x,y;t).

Lemma 2.3

(
L^{p}-L^{q}
estimate)
.

Suppose
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
such that for every
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
, the following inequality holds:

\|S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)f\|_{q}\leq C\,t^{-\frac{N+2k}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}\|f\|_{p},\qquad t>0.

(2.4)

We need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
1\leq q<r<\infty
, and
\alpha=\frac{N+2k}{2}\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{r}\right)
. Then

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}t^{\alpha}\|S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)\varphi\|_{L^{r}}=0,

(2.5)

for any
\varphi\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
.

Proof.

Given
\varphi\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
. Then, by density argument, there exists a sequence
(\varphi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
converging to
\varphi
in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
. Using the semigroup decay (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle t^{\alpha}\|S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)\varphi\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle t^{\alpha}\|S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)(\varphi-\varphi_{n})\|_{L^{r}}+t^{\alpha}\|S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\varphi-\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{q}}+t^{\alpha}\|\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{r}}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N},t>0
.
Taking the limit as
t\rightarrow 0
, we get

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}t^{\alpha}\|S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)\varphi\|_{L^{r}}\leq C\|\varphi-\varphi_{n}\|_{L^{q}},

and the result follows by letting
n\rightarrow\infty
.
∎

3
Local Existence and the Comparison Principle

In this section, we establish the local well-posedness of mild solutions to problem (
1.4
), addressing both singular and regular initial data. Specifically, we prove the local existence and provide a blow-up alternative for mild solutions corresponding to initial data either
u_{0}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
or
u_{0}\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
.

We begin by introducing the notion of a mild solution to problem (
1.4
) .

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
denote either
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
, with
1\leq q<\infty
, or
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
. A function

u\in L^{\infty}([0,T],X)

of
(
1.4

if it satisfies the integral equation

u(t)=S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)u_{0}+k_{1}\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t-s)\int_{0}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-\gamma}\lvert u(\tau)\rvert^{p_{1}-1}u(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}s+k_{2}\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t-s)|u(s)|^{p_{2}-1}u(s)\mathrm{d}s.

(3.1)

3.1
Local existence with singular initial condition

Let
T>0
be fixed. We define the Banach space

E=L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))\cap L^{\infty}_{loc}((0,T),L^{r_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))\cap L^{\infty}_{loc}((0,T),L^{r_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})),

where
r_{i}=p_{i}q
for
i=1,2
. Based on this, we introduce the Banach space

X_{T}=\{u\in E:\,\|u\|_{X_{T}}<+\infty\},

which will serve as the framework for our fixed point argument. The norm on
X_{T}
is defined by

\|u\|_{X_{T}}=\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\|u(t)\|_{L^{q}}+\max_{i=1,2}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}t^{\alpha_{i}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{r_{i}}},

where the exponents
\alpha_{i}
are given by

\alpha_{i}=\frac{N+2k}{2p_{i}q}\left(p_{i}-1\right),\qquad i=1,2.

Theorem 3.1

(Local existence with singular initial condition)
.

Let
p_{i}>1
for
i=1,2
, and let
u_{0}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
with
1\leq q<\infty
, satisfying

q>\max\limits_{i=1,2}\frac{N+2k}{2}(p_{i}-1).

Then there exists a time
T=T(u_{0})>0
such that problem (
1.4
) possesses an
L^{q}
-mild solution
u
, which is unique in
X_{T}
. Moreover, the following properties hold:

\mathrm{(i)}

Smoothing effect and continuous dependence,

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{q}}+\max_{i=1,2}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}t^{\alpha_{i}}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{r_{i}}}\leq C\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{L^{q}},

(3.2)

whenever
u
and
v
are
L^{q}
-mild solutions of
(
1.4
)
with time of existence
T=\min\{T(u_{0}),T(v_{0})\}
and initial data
u_{0}
and
v_{0}
, respectively.

\mathrm{(ii)}

Decay of solution,

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}t^{\alpha_{i}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{r_{i}}}=0,\quad\hbox{for all}\,\,i=1,2.

(3.3)

\mathrm{(iii)}

If in addition
q\geq\max\limits_{i=1,2}p_{i}
, then the mild solution
u
is also unique in
C([0,T];L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))
. In this case, problem (
1.4
) possesses a maximal mild solution
u\in C([0,T_{\max});L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))
with
u(0)=u_{0}
, where
T_{\max}=T_{\max}(u_{0})\leq\infty
. Furthermore, either
T_{\max}=\infty
, or else

\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{\max}^{-}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}((0,t),L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))}=+\infty.

(3.4)

\mathrm{(iv)}

If
u_{0}\geq 0
and
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0
, then then the mild solution
u\geq 0
remains nonnegative for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

The proof is divided into several steps.

Step 1. Fixed-point argument.
Let
T>0
and choose
R\geq C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}}
with
C>0
is given in (
2.4
). In order to use the Banach fixed-point theorem, we introduce the following nonempty complete metric space

B_{T}(R)=\{u\in X_{T}:\,\,\|u\|_{X_{T}}\leq 4R\},

(3.5)

equipped with the distance

d(u,v)=\max_{i=1,2}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}t^{\alpha_{i}}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{r_{i}}}.

For
u\in B_{T}(R)
, we define
\Lambda(u)
by

\Lambda(u)(t):=S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)u_{0}+k_{1}\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t-s)\int_{0}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-\gamma}\lvert u(\tau)\rvert^{p_{1}-1}u(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}s+k_{2}\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t-s)|u(s)|^{p_{2}-1}u(s)\mathrm{d}s.

(3.6)

Let us prove that
\Lambda:B_{T}(R)\rightarrow B_{T}(R)
. Using Lemma
2.3
, we obtain for any
u\in B_{T}(R)
,

\displaystyle\|\Lambda(u)(t)\|_{L^{r_{i}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)u_{0}\|_{L^{r_{i}}}+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t-s)\int_{0}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-\gamma}\lvert u(\tau)\rvert^{p_{1}-1}u(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{L^{r_{i}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t-s)|u(s)|^{p_{2}-1}u(s)\mathrm{d}s\right\|_{L^{r_{i}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}}t^{-\alpha_{i}}+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha_{i}}\int_{0}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-\gamma}\|u(\tau)\|^{p_{1}}_{L^{r_{1}}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha_{i}}\|u(s)\|^{p_{2}}_{L^{r_{2}}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}}t^{-\alpha_{i}}+C\left(\sup_{t\in(0,T)}t^{\alpha_{1}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{r_{1}}}\right)^{p_{1}}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha_{i}}\int_{0}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-\gamma}\tau^{-\alpha_{1}p_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+C\left(\sup_{t\in(0,T)}t^{\alpha_{2}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{r_{2}}}\right)^{p_{2}}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha_{i}}s^{-\alpha_{2}p_{2}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}}t^{-\alpha_{i}}+C\|u\|^{p_{1}}_{X_{T}}t^{-\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{1}p_{1}-\gamma+2}+C\|u\|^{p_{2}}_{X_{T}}t^{-\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{2}p_{2}+1},

for all
t\in(0,T)
,
i=1,2
, where we have used the fact that

(3.7)

for a sufficiently small

(depending on

\displaystyle\|\Lambda(u)(t)\|_{L^{q}}

(3.8)

By combining (

\displaystyle\quad+Ct^{\alpha_{i}}R^{p_{2}-1}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha_{i}}s^{-\alpha_{2}(p_{2}-1)}\|u(s)-v(s)\|_{L^{r_{2}}}\mathrm{d}s

Step 2. Regularity.
One can easily check that
f\in L^{1}((0,T),L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))

Step 3. Uniqueness in

For sufficiently small

u,v\in B_{\tilde{T}}(R)
. Thus
u\equiv v
on
[0,\tilde{T}]
. For
t\in[\tilde{T},T]
, perform as in the contraction part of
\Lambda
, we get

\displaystyle t^{\alpha_{i}}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{r_{i}}}\leq

\displaystyle Ct^{\alpha_{i}}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{p_{1}-1}\int_{\tilde{T}}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha_{i}}s^{1-\gamma-\alpha_{1}p_{1}}\left(s^{\alpha_{1}}\|u(s)-v(s)\|_{L^{r_{1}}}\right)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+Ct^{\alpha_{i}}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{p_{2}-1}\int_{\tilde{T}}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha_{i}}s^{-\alpha_{2}p_{2}}\left(s^{\alpha_{2}}\|u(s)-v(s)\|_{L^{r_{2}}}\right)\mathrm{d}s.

Define

\xi(t)=t^{\alpha_{1}}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{r_{1}}}+t^{\alpha_{2}}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{r_{2}}},

and

M=C(T^{\alpha_{1}}+T^{\alpha_{2}})\left(\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{p_{1}-1}+\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{p_{2}-1}\right)\left(\tilde{T}^{-\alpha_{2}p_{2}}+T^{1-\gamma}\tilde{T}^{-\alpha_{1}p_{1}}\right)

Then

\xi(t)\leq M\int_{0}^{t}((t-s)^{-\alpha_{1}}+(t-s)^{-\alpha_{2}})\xi(s)\mathrm{d}s.

Then, the singular Gronwall inequality (see e.g
(
Amann-84
, Lemma 3.3)
) gives us
\xi(t)=0
, whence we proved the uniqueness in
X_{T}
.

Step 4. Uniqueness in
C([0,T];L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))
.
Let
u
and
v
be two
L^{q}
-mild solutions of (
1.4
) with
u(0)=v(0)=u_{0}
, and such that
q\geq\max\limits_{i=1,2}p_{i}
. We have

\displaystyle u(t)-v(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{1}\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t-s)\int_{0}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-\gamma}\left(\lvert u(\tau)\rvert^{p_{1}-1}u(\tau)-\lvert v(\tau)\rvert^{p_{1}-1}v(\tau)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\,k_{2}\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t-s)\left(|u(s)|^{p_{2}-1}u(s)-|v(s)|^{p_{2}-1}v(s)\right)\mathrm{d}s.

for all
t\in[0,T]
. Using the
L^{q/p_{i}}-L^{q}
estimate (Lemma
2.3
), we get

\displaystyle\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha_{1}p_{1}}\int_{0}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-\gamma}\|\left(\lvert u(\tau)\rvert^{p_{1}-1}+\lvert v(\tau)\rvert^{p_{1}-1}\right)|u(\tau)-v(\tau)|\|_{L^{q/p_{1}}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\,C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha_{2}p_{2}}\|\left(\lvert u(s)\rvert^{p_{2}-1}+\lvert v(s)\rvert^{p_{2}-1}\right)|u(s)-v(s)|\|_{L^{q/p_{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha_{1}p_{1}}\int_{0}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-\gamma}\left(\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{q}}^{p_{1}-1}+\|v(\tau)\|_{L^{q}}^{p_{1}-1}\right)\|u(\tau)-v(\tau)\|_{L^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\,C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha_{2}p_{2}}\left(\|u(s)\|_{L^{q}}^{p_{2}-1}+v(s)\|_{L^{q}}^{p_{2}-1}\right)\|u(s)-v(s)\|_{L^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}s\

where we have used Hölder’s inequality with
\frac{p_{i}}{q}=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{p_{i}-1}{q}
,
i=1,2
. Define

M=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{L^{q}}+\|v(t)\|_{L^{q}}\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad\xi(t)=\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\|u(s)-v(s)\|_{L^{q}},

for all
t\in[0,T]
. We conclude that

\displaystyle\xi(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,M^{p_{1}-1}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha_{1}p_{1}}s^{1-\gamma}\xi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\,C\,M^{p_{2}-1}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha_{2}p_{2}}\xi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(M^{p_{1}-1}T^{2-\gamma-\alpha_{1}p_{1}}+M^{p_{2}-1}T^{1-\alpha_{2}p_{2}}\right)\xi(t),

for all
t\in[0,T]
, which implies that
\xi\equiv 0
on
[0,\widetilde{T}]
for sufficiently small
\widetilde{T}
. By a standard continuation argument, one can extend the uniqueness to the entire interval
[0,T]
.

Step 5. Maximal solution and blow-up alternative.
Using the uniqueness of the
L^{q}
-mild solution, we conclude the existence
of a solution on a maximal interval
[0,T_{\max})
where

T_{\max}:=\sup\left\{T>0\;;\;\text{there exist a mild solution $u\in C([0,T];L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))$
to \eqref{*}}\right\}.

Obviously,
T_{\max}\leq\infty
. To prove that
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}((0,t),L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))}\rightarrow\infty
as
t\rightarrow T_{\max},

whenever
T_{\max}<\infty,
we proceed by contradiction. If

\liminf_{t\rightarrow T_{\max}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}((0,t),L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))}=:L<\infty,

then there exists a time sequence
\{t_{m}\}_{m\geq 0}
tending to
T_{\max}
as
m\rightarrow\infty
and such that

\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{0<s\leq t_{m}}\|u(s)\|_{L^{q}}\leq L+1.

Moreover, if
0\leq t_{m}\leq t_{m}+\tau<T_{\max},
using (
3.1
), we can write

\displaystyle u(t_{m}+\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{\mathcal{G}}(\tau)u(t_{m})+k_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau}S_{\mathcal{G}}(\tau-s)\int_{0}^{s}(s-\sigma)^{-\gamma}\lvert u\rvert^{p_{1}-1}u(t_{m}+\sigma)\,d\sigma\,ds

(3.9)

\displaystyle+\,k_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau}S_{\mathcal{G}}(\tau-s)\int_{0}^{t_{m}}(t_{m}+s-\sigma)^{-\gamma}\lvert u\rvert^{p_{1}-1}u(\sigma)\,d\sigma\,ds

\displaystyle+\,k_{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}S_{\mathcal{G}}(\tau-s)\lvert u\rvert^{p_{2}-1}u(s+t_{m})\,ds.

Using the fact that the third term on the right-hand side in
(\ref{newIE+})
depends only on the values of
u
in the interval

(0,t_{m})
and using again a fixed-point argument with
u(t_{m})
as initial condition, one can deduce that there exists
T(L+1)>0
, depends on
L+1
, such that the solution

u(t)
can be extended on the interval
[t_{m},t_{m}+T(L+1)]
for any
m\geq 0
. Thus, by the definition of the maximality time,
T_{\max}\geq t_{m}+T(L+1)
, for any
m\geq 0
. We get the desired contradiction by letting
m\rightarrow\infty
.

Step 6. Nonnegativity of solutions.
Assume
u_{0}\geq 0
and
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0
. In this case, we can construct a nonnegative solution on some interval
[0,T]
by applying the fixed point argument within the positive cone
X_{T}^{+}=\{u\in X_{T};\;u\geq 0\}
, and using Proposition
2.2
-(vi). By the uniqueness of solutions, it then follows that
u(t)\geq 0
for all
t\in(0,T_{\max}).

Step 7. Decay of solution.
As
u\in B_{T}(R)
, by choosing
R=C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}}
, we have

t^{\alpha_{i}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{r_{i}}}\leq Ct^{\alpha_{i}}\|S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)u_{0}\|_{L^{r_{i}}}+CR^{p_{1}}t^{-\alpha_{1}p_{1}-\gamma+2}+CR^{p_{2}}t^{-\alpha_{2}p_{2}+1}\longrightarrow 0,

when
t
goes to zero, where we have used Lemma
2.4
and the assumptions on the parameters
p_{i},q,\gamma
.

Step 8. Continuous dependence.
Let
u,v\in X_{T}
be two mild solutions starting at
u_{0}
and
v_{0}
, respectively. The existence proof shows that
u,v\in B_{T}(R)
for any
u_{0},v_{0}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))
satisfying
C\max\{\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q}},\|v_{0}\|_{L^{q}}\}\leq R
. Hence,

\displaystyle t^{\alpha_{i}}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{r_{i}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{L^{q}}+C\,t^{\alpha_{i}}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha_{i}}\int_{0}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-\gamma}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{r_{1}}}^{p_{1}-1}\|u(\tau)-v(\tau)\|_{L^{r_{1}}}\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\,C\,t^{\alpha_{i}}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha_{i}}\|u(s)\|_{L^{r_{2}}}^{p_{2}-1}\|u(s)-v(s)\|_{L^{r_{2}}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{L^{q}}+C\left(R^{p_{1}-1}T^{2-\gamma-\alpha_{1}p_{1}}+R^{p_{2}-1}T^{1-\alpha_{2}p_{2}}\right)\max_{i=1,2}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}t^{\alpha_{i}}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{r_{i}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{L^{q}}+\frac{1}{2}\max_{i=1,2}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}t^{\alpha_{i}}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{r_{i}}},

for all
t\in(0,T)
,
i=1,2
, by using the smallness choice of
T
. This implies

\max_{i=1,2}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}t^{\alpha_{i}}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{r_{i}}}\leq C\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{L^{q}}.

(3.10)

In addition,

\displaystyle\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{L^{q}}+C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-\gamma}\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{r_{1}}}^{p_{1}-1}\|u(\tau)-v(\tau)\|_{L^{r_{1}}}\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\,C\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{r_{2}}}^{p_{2}-1}\|u(s)-v(s)\|_{L^{r_{2}}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{L^{q}}+C\left(R^{p_{1}-1}T^{2-\gamma-\alpha_{1}p_{1}}+R^{p_{2}-1}T^{1-\alpha_{2}p_{2}}\right)\max_{i=1,2}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}t^{\alpha_{i}}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{r_{i}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{L^{q}},

for all
t\in[0,T]
, where we have used (
3.10
) and the smallness of
T
. It follows

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{q}}\leq C\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{L^{q}}.

(3.11)

Combining (
3.10
)-(
3.11
) we get our desired estimate (
3.2
).
∎

3.3
Comparison Principle

In this subsection, we establish a comparison principle for mild solutions of (
1.4
), in which the nonlinear terms
u\mapsto|u|^{p_{i}-1}u
(for
i=1,2
) are replaced by general nonlinearities
f
and
g
satisfying the following growth conditions:

|f(u)-f(v)|\leq c|u-v|(|u|^{p_{1}-1}+|v|^{p_{1}-1}),\quad\quad|g(u)-g(v)|\leq c|u-v|(|u|^{p_{2}-1}+|v|^{p_{2}-1}).

(3.12)

For a given an initial condition
u_{0}
, we denote by
u(t;u_{0},f,g)
the
X
-mild solution of (
1.4
), where the notation highlights the dependence on the initial data and the nonlinear terms
f
and
g
. Here,
X
denotes either
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
or
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
, with
1\leq q<\infty
, satisfying

q>\max\limits_{i=1,2}\frac{N+2k}{2}(p_{i}-1).

We are now in a position to state a comparison principle, motivated by the use of the Picard iteration associated with the mild solution. For this purpose, we introduce the notion of a mild supersolution, which will be used in the formulation of the principle. A function
v:[0,T]\to X
is said to be a
mild supersolution
of (
1.4
), if
v(t)\geq\Lambda_{fg}(v)(t)
, for all
t\in[0,T]
, where the operator
\Lambda_{fg}
is defined in equation (
3.13
) below.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
u_{0},v_{0}\in X
,
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0
, and
f,g,\tilde{f},\tilde{g}
functions satisfying (
3.12
).

(i)

If
0\leq u_{0}\leq v_{0}
, and
f(\mu)\leq\tilde{f}(\nu)
,
g(\mu)\leq\tilde{g}(\nu)
, for all
0\leq\mu\leq\nu
, then

0\leq u(t;u_{0},f,g)\leq v(t;v_{0},\tilde{f},\tilde{g})\quad\text{for all}\,\,t\in[0,\tilde{T}],

where
\tilde{T}>0
is a common time of existence for
u
and
v
.

(ii)

If
v
is a mild supersolution of (
1.4
), and
u
is a mild solution with
u_{0}\geq 0
, then
0\leq u\leq v
for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

We begin by defining the Picard sequences:

u_{1}(t)=S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)u_{0},\qquad u_{k}(t)=\Lambda_{fg}(u_{k-1})(t),\,\,\,k\geq 2,

v_{1}(t)=S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)v_{0},\qquad v_{k}(t)=\widetilde{\Lambda}_{\tilde{f}\tilde{g}}(v_{k-1})(t),\,\,\,k\geq 2,

where
\Lambda_{fg}
and
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{\tilde{f}\tilde{g}}
refer to the operator introduced in equation (
3.6
), emphasizing the dependence on the nonlinearities in the right-hand side:

\Lambda_{fg}(u)(t):=S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)u_{0}+k_{1}\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t-s)\int_{0}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-\gamma}f(u(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}s+k_{2}\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t-s)g(u(s))\mathrm{d}s,

(3.13)

\widetilde{\Lambda}_{\tilde{f}\tilde{g}}(u)(t):=S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)v_{0}+k_{1}\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t-s)\int_{0}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-\gamma}\tilde{f}(u(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}s+k_{2}\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t-s)\tilde{g}(u(s))\mathrm{d}s.

By Proposition
2.2
-(vi), we know that the semigroup
S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)
preserves order:

S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)\varphi\leq S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)\psi,\quad\hbox{whenever}\,\,\,\varphi\leq\psi.

(3.14)

Hence,
0\leq u_{1}(t)\leq v_{1}(t)
. We now apply induction to show that
0\leq u_{k}(t)\leq v_{k}(t)
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Assume this holds for some fixed
k
. Then, using the monotonicity of the semigroup
S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)
(cf. (
3.14
)) together with the structural conditions imposed on the nonlinearities
f,\tilde{f},g,\tilde{g}
, we obtain

0\leq u_{k+1}(t)=\Lambda_{fg}(u_{k})(t)\leq\widetilde{\Lambda}_{\tilde{f}\tilde{g}}(v_{k})(t)=v_{k+1}(t).

Hence, the inequality is preserved at each iteration step. This proves part
(i)
, noting that the corresponding mild solutions are obtained as pointwise limits of the Picard sequences, depending on the functional setting:

•

If
X=C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
, then the convergence is uniform on compact sets and, in particular, pointwise. Thus,

u(t;u_{0},f,g)=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}u_{n}(t),\qquad v(t;v_{0},\tilde{f},\tilde{g})=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}v_{n}(t),

where the limits are taken pointwise.

•

If
X=L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
, then by norm convergence and standard results, there exists a common subsequence along which both sequences converge pointwise almost everywhere. That is,

u(t;u_{0},f,g)=\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}u_{n_{k}}(t),\qquad v(t;v_{0},\tilde{f},\tilde{g})=\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}v_{n_{k}}(t),

where the limits are taken almost everywhere along the same subsequence
(n_{k})_{k}
.

To prove part
(ii)
, it suffices to observe that since
v
is a mild supersolution, we can construct a non-increasing Picard sequence
(w_{k})_{k}
defined by

w_{1}(t)=v(t),\qquad\quad w_{k}(t)=\Lambda_{fg}(w_{k-1})(t),\,\,\hbox{for}\,\,k\geq 2.

By part
(i)
, we have

w_{2}(t)=\Lambda_{fg}(w_{1})(t)=\Lambda_{fg}(v)(t)\leq v(t)=w_{1}(t),

and an induction argument using the monotonicity property of
\Lambda_{fg}
(as established in part
(i)
) yields that the sequence
(w_{k})_{k}
is non-increasing. Moreover, since the nonlinearities
f,g
are nonnegative and the semigroup
S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)
preserves nonnegativity, each
w_{k}(t)
remains bounded, satisfying
0\leq w_{k}(t)\leq v(t)
for all
k\geq 1
.

•

If the functional space is
X=C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
, then
(w_{k})_{k}
converges uniform on compact sets and, in particular,
pointwise to a limit function
w(t)
. By standard monotone convergence results in Banach spaces, the convergence is strong in
C([0,T],C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})))
. Passing to the limit in the iteration yields

w(t)=\Lambda_{fg}(w)(t)\leq v(t),

in particular,
w
is a mild solution of problem (
1.4
). By uniqueness, we conclude that
u=w\leq v
.

•

If instead
X=L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
, then the sequence
(w_{k})_{k}
converges in norm in
L^{q}
, and in particular, there exists a subsequence
(w_{k_{j}})_{j}
that converges almost everywhere to the same limit function
w
. Passing to the limit almost everywhere in the integral formulation gives

w(t)=\Lambda_{fg}(w)(t)\leq v(t),

so again
w
is a mild solution of (
1.4
). Uniqueness implies
u=w\leq v
.

∎

.

In particular, Theorem
3.3
gives another proof for the nonnegativity in Theorems
3.1
and

.

4
Global Existence

In this section, we prove the existence of global solutions. We also prove some comparison principles needed in our approach.

Let us define the following critical exponents.

p_{1}^{*}=\max\left\{\frac{1}{\gamma},p_{\gamma}\right\}\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad p_{2}^{*}=1+\frac{2}{N+2k},

(4.1)

where

\displaystyle p_{\gamma}=1+\frac{4-2\gamma}{N+2k-2+2\gamma},

and

p_{2}^{**}=\max\left\{\frac{\gamma-\gamma^{2}+1}{\gamma(2-\gamma)},1+\frac{2}{N+2k-2+2\gamma}\right\}.

(4.2)

We also define
\tilde{p}_{2}:=(p_{1}+1-\gamma)/(2-\gamma)
and the scaled exponents as

q_{\mathrm{sc}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{(N+2k)(p_{1}-1)}{2(2-\gamma)}=:q_{sc_{1}},&\qquad\hbox{if}\,\,p_{2}\geq\tilde{p}_{2},\\
\\
\displaystyle\frac{(N+2k)(p_{2}-1)}{2}=:q_{sc_{2}},&\qquad\hbox{if}\,\,p_{2}\leq\tilde{p}_{2}.\\
\end{array}\right.

Theorem 4.1
.

The following assertions hold:

(\mathrm{i})

Let
u_{0}\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})\cap L^{q_{\mathrm{sc}}}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
, with
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q_{\mathrm{sc}}}}
sufficiently small. If
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
p_{1}>p_{1}^{*}
and
p_{2}=\tilde{p}_{2}
, then there exists a unique global mild solution
u\in C([0,\infty);C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))
of (
1.4
).

(\mathrm{ii})

Let
u_{0}\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})\cap L^{q_{\mathrm{sc}}}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
, with
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
and
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q_{\mathrm{sc}}}}
sufficiently small. If
u_{0}\geq 0
,
k_{1},k_{2}>0
,
p_{1}>p_{1}^{*}
and
p_{2}>p_{2}^{**}
, then problem (
1.4
) admits a unique global mild solution

u\in C([0,\infty);C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}).

(\mathrm{iii})

Let
u_{0}\in X
where
X
is the function space defined in Subsection
3.3
. If
u_{0}\geq 0
,
k_{i}\leq 0
for
i=1,2
, then any nonnegative mild solution of (
1.4
) is global.

(\mathrm{iv})

Let
u_{0}\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})\cap L^{q_{\mathrm{sc_{1}}}}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
, with
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q_{\mathrm{sc_{1}}}}}
sufficiently small. If
u_{0}\geq 0
,
k_{1}>0
,
k_{2}\leq 0
, and
p_{1}>p_{1}^{*}
, then any nonnegative mild solution of (
1.4
) is global.

(\mathrm{v})

Let
u_{0}\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})\cap L^{q_{\mathrm{sc_{2}}}}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
, with
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q_{\mathrm{sc_{2}}}}}
sufficiently small. If
u_{0}\geq 0
,
k_{2}>0
,
k_{1}\leq 0
, and
p_{2}>p_{2}^{*}
, then any nonnegative mild solution of (
1.4
) is global.

Proof.

We divide the proof into five separate cases, each corresponding to one of the scenarios described in the theorem. These cases differ depending on the signs and values of the coefficients
k_{1},k_{2}
and the exponents
p_{1},p_{2}
, as well as assumptions on the initial data.

Case
(\mathrm{i})
.
The proof is divided into two steps.

Step 1. Existence.
As
p_{1}>p_{1}^{*}
, then we have the possibility to
take a positive constant
q>0
so that

\frac{2-\gamma}{p_{1}-1}-\frac{1}{p_{1}}<\frac{N+2k}{2q}<\frac{1}{p_{1}-1},\quad q\geq p_{1}.

(4.3)

It follows that

q>\frac{(N+2k)(p_{1}-1)}{2}>q_{sc}>1.

(4.4)

Let

\beta:=\frac{N+2k}{2q_{sc}}-\frac{N+2k}{2q}=\frac{2-\gamma}{p_{1}-1}-\frac{N+2k}{2q}=\frac{1}{p_{2}-1}-\frac{N+2k}{2q}.

(4.5)

Thus, by utilizing relations (
4.3
)-(
4.5
) along with the condition
p_{2}<p_{1}
, one can easily verify that
p_{i}\beta<1
for
i=1,2
,
q>p_{2}
, as well as the relations

\beta>\frac{1-\gamma}{p_{1}-1}>0,\quad\frac{(N+2k)(p_{1}-1)}{2q}+(p_{1}-1)\beta+\gamma=2,\quad\frac{(N+2k)(p_{2}-1)}{2q}+(p_{2}-1)\beta=1,

(4.6)

hold. As
u_{0}\in L^{q_{\mathrm{sc}}}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})
, using (
2.4
),
q>q_{\mathrm{sc}}
, and (
4.5
), we
get

\sup_{t>0}t^{\beta}\|S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)u_{0}\|_{L^{q}}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q_{\mathrm{sc}}}}=\eta<\infty.

(4.7)

Set

\Xi:=\left\{u\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty),L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}));\;\sup_{t>0}t^{\beta}\|u(t)\|_{L^{q}}\leq\delta\right\},

(4.8)

where
\delta>0
is to be chosen sufficiently small. If we define

d_{\Xi}(u,v):=\sup_{t>0}t^{\beta}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{q}},\qquad\hbox{for all}\,\,u,v\in\Xi,

(4.9)

then
(\Xi,d)
is a nonempty complete metric space. For
u\in\Xi
, we define
\Phi(u)
by

\Phi(u)(t):=S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)u_{0}+k_{1}\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t-s)\int_{0}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-\gamma}\lvert u(\tau)\rvert^{p_{1}-1}u(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}s+k_{2}\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t-s)|u(s)|^{p_{2}-1}u(s)\mathrm{d}s,

(4.10)

for all
t\geq 0
. Let us prove that
\Phi:\Xi\rightarrow\Xi
. Using
q\geq p_{i}
for
i=1,2
, (
4.7
), (
4.8
), and Lemma
2.3
, we obtain for any
u\in\Xi
,

\displaystyle t^{\beta}\|\Phi(u)(t)\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\eta+\,Ct^{\beta}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{(N+2k)(p_{1}-1)}{2q}}\int_{0}^{s}(s-\sigma)^{-\gamma}\|u(\sigma)\|^{p_{1}}_{L^{q}}\,d\sigma\,ds

(4.11)

\displaystyle+\,Ct^{\beta}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{(N+2k)(p_{2}-1)}{2q}}\|u(s)\|^{p_{2}}_{L^{q}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\eta+C\delta^{p_{1}}t^{\beta}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}(t-s)^{-\frac{(N+2k)(p_{1}-1)}{2q}}(s-\sigma)^{-\gamma}\sigma^{-\beta p_{1}}\,d\sigma\,ds

\displaystyle+\,C\delta^{p_{2}}t^{\beta}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{(N+2k)(p_{2}-1)}{2q}}s^{-\beta p_{2}}\,ds.

Next, using (
4.3
), (
4.6
),
p_{2}<p_{1}
, and
p_{i}\beta<1
for
i=1,2
, we get

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}(t-s)^{-\frac{(N+2k)(p_{1}-1)}{2q}}(s-\sigma)^{-\gamma}\sigma^{-\beta p_{1}}\,d\sigma\,ds=C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{(N+2k)(p_{1}-1)}{\beta q}}s^{1-\gamma-\beta p_{1}}\,ds=Ct^{-\beta},

(4.12)

and

\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{(N+2k)(p_{2}-1)}{2q}}s^{-\beta p_{2}}\,ds=Ct^{1-\frac{(N+2k)(p_{2}-1)}{2q}-\beta p_{2}}=Ct^{-\beta},

(4.13)

for all
t\geq 0.
So, we deduce from (
4.11
)-(
4.13
)
that

t^{\beta}\|\Phi(u)(t)\|_{L^{q}}\leq\eta+C(\delta^{p_{1}}+\delta^{p_{2}}).

(4.14)

Therefore, if
\eta
and
\delta
are chosen small enough so that
\eta+C(\delta^{p_{1}}+\delta^{p_{2}})\leq\delta,
we see that

\Phi:\Xi\rightarrow\Xi.
Similar calculations show that (assuming

\eta
and
\delta
small enough)
\Phi
is a strict contraction, so
it has a unique fixed point
u\in\Xi
which is a mild solution of (
1.4
).

We now establish that
u\in C([0,\infty),C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))
. To begin, we first show that
u\in C([0,T],C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))
for some sufficiently small
T>0
. Indeed, the previous argument ensures uniqueness in
\Xi_{T},
where for any
T>0,

\Xi_{T}:=\left\{u\in L^{\infty}((0,T),L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}));\;\sup_{0<t<T}t^{\beta}\|u(t)\|_{L^{q}}\leq\delta\right\}.

Let
\tilde{u}
be the local solution of (
1.4
)
constructed in Theorem
3.2
. By applying (
4.4
), we have
q_{sc}<q<\infty
, which implies

u_{0}\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})\cap L^{q_{sc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})\subset C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})\cap L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}).

Thus, Theorem
3.2
guarantees that
\tilde{u}\in C([0,T_{\max}),C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})\cap L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))
. Consequently, we have

\sup\limits_{t\in(0,T)}t^{\beta}\|\tilde{u}(t)\|_{L^{q}}\leq\delta

for sufficiently small
T>0
. By the uniqueness of solutions, it follows that
u=\tilde{u}
on

[0,T]
, leading to the conclusion that
u\in C([0,T],C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))
.

Next, we show that
u\in C([T,\infty),C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))
using a bootstrap argument. Indeed, for
t>T,
we express
u(t)
as

\displaystyle u(t)-S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)u_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{1}\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t-s)\int_{0}^{T}(s-\sigma)^{-\gamma}\lvert u\rvert^{p_{1}-1}u(\sigma)\;d\sigma\,ds

\displaystyle+k_{1}\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t-s)\int_{T}^{s}(s-\sigma)^{-\gamma}\lvert u\rvert^{p_{1}-1}u(\sigma)\;d\sigma\,ds

\displaystyle+k_{2}\int_{0}^{T}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t-s)\lvert u\rvert^{p_{2}-1}u(s)\,ds+k_{2}\int_{T}^{t}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t-s)\lvert u\rvert^{p_{2}-1}u(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle I_{1}(t)+I_{2}(t)+I_{3}(t)+I_{4}(t).

Since
u\in C([0,T],C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})),
it follows that
I_{1},I_{3}\in C([T,\infty),C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})).
Moreover, using similar calculations used to
construct the fixed point, and noting that
t^{-\beta}\leq T^{-\beta}<\infty
, we conclude that
I_{1},I_{3}\in C([T,\infty),L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))
.
Next, from (
4.4
), we have
q>(N+2k)(p_{1}-1)/2
, which guarantees the existence of some
r\in(q,\infty]
such that

\frac{N+2k}{2}\left(\frac{p_{1}}{q}-\frac{1}{r}\right)<1.

(4.15)

For
T<s<t
, since
u\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty),L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})),
we obtain

|u|^{p_{1}-1}u\in L^{\infty}((T,s),L^{q/p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))\quad\hbox{and}\quad|u|^{p_{2}-1}u\in L^{\infty}((T,t),L^{q/p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})).

Thus, applying (
2.4
), (
4.15
), and the fact that
p_{2}<p_{1}
, it follows that
I_{2},I_{4}\in C([T,\infty),L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))
.
Since the terms
S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)u_{0}
,
I_{1}
, and
I_{3}
belong to

C([T,\infty),C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))\cap C([T,\infty),L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})),
we conclude that
u\in C([T,\infty),L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))
. We ultimately deduce, using a bootstrap argument, that
u\in C([T,\infty),C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k})).

Step 2. Uniqueness.
Let
u,v\in C([0,\infty),C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N+k}))
be two global mild solutions for (
1.4
). Let
T>0
, we have

\displaystyle\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}(s-\sigma)^{-\gamma}\|u(\sigma)-v(\sigma)\|_{\infty}\,d\sigma\,ds+C\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s-v(s)\|_{\infty}\,ds

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\sigma}^{t}(s-\sigma)^{-\gamma}\|u(\sigma)-v(\sigma)\|_{\infty}\,ds\,d\sigma+C\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s-v(s)\|_{\infty}\,ds

\displaystyle=C\int_{0}^{t}(t-\sigma)^{1-\gamma}\|u(\sigma)-v(\sigma)\|_{\infty}\,d\sigma+C\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s-v(s)\|_{\infty}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\left(T^{1-\gamma}+1\right)\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s-v(s)\|_{\infty}\,ds.

By Gronwall’s inequality, we conclude that
u(t)=v(t)
for every
t\in[0,T]
. Since
T>0
is arbitrary, this completes the proof of Case
(\mathrm{i})
.

Case
(\mathrm{ii})
.
We address this case using the comparison principle from Theorem
3.3
. Two subcases arise based on the relative size of the exponent
p_{2}
.

The case
p_{2}>\tilde{p}_{2}
.
We aim to show that the mild solution
u
of (
1.4
) satisfies

0\leq u\leq v,

(4.16)

where
v
is the mild solution of the same equation with the exponent
p_{2}
replaced with
\tilde{p}_{2}=(p_{1}+1-\gamma)/(2-\gamma)
. Since
p_{1}>p_{1}^{*}
,
Case
(i) can be applied to ensure that the solution
v
exists globally and is locally bounded. Then, if (
4.16
) holds,
u
exists globally, by Theorem
3.2
. Let us prove (
4.16
). Indeed, for sufficiently small initial data
u_{0}\geq 0
, with
0<\epsilon_{0}\ll 1
, we have
0\leq u_{0}<\epsilon_{0}
. Let
\Phi
and
\tilde{\Phi}
denote the integral operators associated with the respective mild formulations for
u
and
v
:

\Phi(u)(t):=S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)u_{0}+k_{1}\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t-s)\int_{0}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-\gamma}(u(\tau))^{p_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}s+k_{2}\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathcal{G}}(t-s)(u(s))^{p_{2}}\mathrm{d}s,

In fact,
u
and
v
are, respectively, the limit of the following Picard sequences

u_{1}(t)=S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)u_{0},\qquad u_{k}(t)=\Phi(u_{k-1})(t),\,\,\,k\geq 2,

(4.17)

v_{1}(t)=S_{\mathcal{G}}(t)u_{0},\qquad v_{k}(t)=\tilde{\Phi}(v_{k-1})(t),\,\,\,k\geq 2.

(4.18)

We observe that both solutions begin at the same initial data, that is,
0\leq u_{1}=v_{1}
, and
v_{1}(t)\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\epsilon_{0}
. Since
\tilde{p}_{2}<p_{2}
, it follows that

u_{1}^{p_{2}}=v_{1}^{p_{2}}\leq v_{1}^{\tilde{p}_{2}}.

Hence, by Theorem
3.3
,
0\leq u_{2}\leq v_{2}
. On the other hand, by the proof of
Case
(i),

is the global mild solution which implies, in particular, that
\{v_{n}\}_{n}\subset\Xi
, where
\Xi

). Recalling that

\displaystyle\|v_{2}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ct^{-\frac{N+2k}{2q_{\mathrm{sc}}}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q_{\mathrm{sc}}}}+C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{N+2k}{2q}p_{1}}\int_{0}^{s}(s-\tau)^{-\gamma}\|v_{1}(\tau)\|^{p_{1}}_{L^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\,C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{N+2k}{2q}\tilde{p}_{2}}\|v_{1}(s)\|^{\tilde{p}_{2}}_{L^{q}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle Ct^{-\frac{N+2k}{2q_{\mathrm{sc}}}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q_{\mathrm{sc}}}}+C\delta^{p_{1}}t^{2-\frac{N+2k}{2q}p_{1}-\gamma-\beta p_{1}}+C\delta^{\tilde{p}_{2}}t^{1-\frac{N+2k}{2q}\tilde{p}_{2}-\beta\tilde{p}_{2}}.

Fuzzy min–max dual motif-guided heterogeneous Dandelion graph multi-scale residual attention network for healthcare AI misclassification reduction
Authors: Ganesh Karthikeyan Varadarajan, Durai Selvaraj, Sree Ranganayaki Vanamamaley, Koteeswaran Seerangan, Sudha Rajendhiran, Ram Kumar Yadav, Gyanendra Kumar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The rapid advancement of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in healthcare diagnostics has significantly improved disease detection and treatment prediction; however, misclassification remains a persistent challenge due to data heterogeneity, incomplete clinical information, and complex feature interdependencies. To address these limitations, a novel framework titled Dual Motif Guided Heterogeneous Interactive Dandelion Graph Multi-Scale Residual Attention Network (DMGH-IDGM-SRAN) is proposed for Reducing Misclassifications and Enhancing Model Performance in Healthcare AI. The study employs a curated dataset, “Healthcare AI Dataset for Reducing Misclassifications and Enhancing Model Performance in Healthcare AI”, collaboratively developed by technical and medical experts, consisting of 743 clinical records obtained from the Pulmonology Department of one of India’s leading hospitals. Pre-processing is conducted using the Fuzzy Min–Max Neural Network (FMNN) to handle uncertainty and overlapping class boundaries effectively. Subsequently, Adaptive Causal Decision Transformers (AdaCred) are utilized for feature extraction, capturing causal dependencies and temporal associations across heterogeneous clinical attributes. The proposed DMGH-IDGM-SRAN integrates a Dual-Attention-Guided Interactive Multi-Scale Residual Network (DA-IMRN) with a Motif-Based Heterogeneous Graph Attention Network (MBHAN), whose hyperparameters are fine-tuned using the Dandelion Optimizer (DO) to enhance model convergence and stability. Experimental evaluation demonstrates that the proposed model achieves an exceptional 99.9% classification accuracy, significantly outperforming existing approaches. The framework offers two key advantages: (i) it effectively captures complex cross-feature relationships between clinical and biochemical parameters, and (ii) it provides robust and interpretable diagnostic predictions under noisy and incomplete data conditions.

Introduction

Artificial intelligence (AI) has emerged as a disruptive force in the healthcare industry, driving improvements in diagnosis, treatment, and patient management. However, because medical data is complicated, sensitive, and variable, obtaining excellent model performance is still a significant difficulty. Optimizing algorithms to guarantee accuracy, dependability, and equity while upholding transparency and ethical integrity is necessary to improve model performance in healthcare AI. The efficacy of AI in clinical contexts is greatly influenced by elements including generalization, model interpretability, feature selection, and data quality. Furthermore, problems like imbalance, noise, and limited availability are common in healthcare data, which is why thorough preparation and model tweaking are crucial1–5. Combining cutting-edge methods like transfer learning, deep learning, and federated learning can increase the precision and flexibility of the model even further. Refinement of model outputs also requires collaboration between data scientists and doctors, as well as ongoing evaluation using real-world data. Although there have been considerable advancements in the clinical integration of AI and ML, especially in the fields of cardiology and medical imaging, the use of these technologies is still restricted due to a number of obstacles. The timing and context of data production have a significant impact on ML algorithm performance, which makes real-world applications unpredictable6–10. The FDA’s practice of “locking” approved algorithms stops them from being updated, which puts the dynamic healthcare environment at risk of performance degradation. Although there are no comprehensive implementation methodologies in the literature currently in publication, frequent monitoring and maintenance mechanisms must be built in order to preserve the dependability and efficacy of these algorithms. Given the grave consequences of mistakes, the healthcare setting necessitates a more exacting approach. By incorporating technologies that improve patient care and operational efficiency through real-time information transmission, cost reduction, and asset management, IoT enhances smart health systems. Machine learning and deep learning techniques generate comprehensive medical histories by utilizing large volumes of data. Decision support systems driven by AI enhance patient care by assisting physicians in making fewer errors and improving diagnostic precision11–15.

In healthcare diagnostics, achieving accurate disease classification remains a significant challenge due to the presence of heterogeneous, incomplete, and imbalanced clinical datasets that often lead to high misclassification rates. Traditional AI and deep learning models struggle to capture the intricate relationships among clinical, biochemical, and behavioral variables, resulting in reduced diagnostic precision and interpretability. Furthermore, the variability in patient data, overlapping feature distributions, and the absence of robust optimization mechanisms exacerbate the issue, hindering reliable clinical decision-making. Hence, there is a pressing need for an advanced, adaptive, and interpretable AI-driven framework capable of effectively managing uncertainty, learning from complex interdependent medical attributes, and minimizing misclassifications to enhance the overall reliability and performance of healthcare prediction systems. This study addresses these issues; this work is motivated.

Data acquisition

In this study, a dataset titled “Healthcare AI Dataset for Reducing Misclassifications and Enhancing Model Performance in Healthcare AI” is utilized, developed through a collaborative effort between technical and medical experts, adhering to the requirements for data gathering and ethics described in Sebastin et al.23. The third-largest hospital in India’s pulmonology department provided this retrospective dataset, which includes clinical observations recorded between 2017 and 2019 for patients diagnosed with either lung cancer or non-cancerous pulmonary diseases. The dataset includes a total of 743 patient records, encompassing 365 benign pulmonary diseases and 378 confirmed cases of lung cancer. It has one dependent variable and seventy-four independent features (Cancer class). A thorough clinical profile is represented by the features, which include demographic information, environmental exposure, family history of cancer, habits and drunkenness, clinical signs and symptoms, and blood and urine analysis findings. Gender, Age, Meal Type, Diet Type, Mode of Tobacco, Period of Consumption, Frequency of Consumption, Pattern of Consumption, Alcoholic, Passive Smoking, Cooking Method, Family History of Cancer, Chemical Exposure, Comorbidity, and History of COPD, Gastrointestinal, Bone pain, Dyspnea, Dysphagia, Weight Loss, Wheezing, Fever, Lethargic, Clubbing, Pneumonia, COPD, Sputum Cytology, ESR, Hemoglobin, Hematocrit, Neutrophils, Lymphs, Urea Nitrogen, and Uric Acid. Only 15 entries were complete, and 728 records had variable degrees of missing data due to the retroactive nature of the data. 23 records in all were eliminated because they were missing more than 80% of the time. Procedures for imputation and data preprocessing were carried out to manage incomplete entries and ensure analytical consistency. The final cleaned dataset used for analysis comprised 720 valid samples, including 361 non-cancer and 359 cancer cases, which presents the feature-wise distribution and statistical significance (p-values) for comparative analysis between the two groups. Pre-processing using FMMNN for enhancing model performance in healthcare AI

The pre-processing stage is conducted using the FMNN24 to enhance the learning capability of the subsequent model by efficiently handling uncertainties, overlapping class regions, and missing values inherent in complex healthcare data. The FMNN algorithm partitions the input feature space into hyperboxes, which represent the learned regions of each class. Each hyperbox is defined by two n-dimensional points: a minimum point (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$U^{g}$$\end{document}) and a maximum point (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$R^{g}$$\end{document}), corresponding to the lower and upper bounds of feature values, respectively.

The FMNN operates in the pattern space, learning from numeric data to create hyperboxes that encompass samples belonging to the same class. To prevent information loss in overlapping class regions, the FMNN model in this study restricts its operation to the hyperbox expansion phase only, thereby preserving shared boundary information that would otherwise be eliminated in contraction phases. Each hyperbox represents a cluster of similar data points in the healthcare dataset, effectively forming an interval-valued decision system (IDS) that captures the uncertainty and range of clinical attributes across patient samples.

After constructing the FDM, an approximate reduction to a minimal subset of attributes retaining the same discernibility power as the complete set is determined using the Sequential Forward Selection (SFS) strategy. In each iteration, the Significance Attribute Test (SAT) is computed to identify the most influential attribute contributing to class discernibility. The process continues until the reduct’s SAT value equals that of the entire feature set, ensuring the retention of maximum discriminatory information.

Through this preprocessing methodology, the FMNN efficiently transforms complex, overlapping healthcare data into structured, interpretable hyperbox representations while minimizing redundancy and dimensionality.

Feature extraction using AdaCred for enhancing model performance in healthcare AI

Following the fuzzy-based pre-processing phase, the refined and uncertainty-handled dataset, titled “Healthcare AI Dataset for Reducing Misclassifications and Enhancing Model Performance in Healthcare AI,” is forwarded to the Adaptive Causal Decision Transformer (AdaCred)25 for advanced feature extraction. The AdaCred framework is designed to capture causal dependencies, temporal relationships, and latent representations among heterogeneous medical attributes, enabling the model to identify highly discriminative and clinically relevant features that significantly enhance classification performance. This section formally defines the theoretical underpinnings of the AdaCred feature extraction process for healthcare intelligence.

The AdaCred framework models clinical decision-making under uncertainty using a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), defined in Eq. (5):5\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\text{(POMDP) }}P = \left( {L,K,J,I,\alpha ,U} \right)$$\end{document}where: L represents the set of observations, corresponding to patient features (e.g., symptoms, laboratory values, and clinical measurements), K denotes the set of actions, interpreted as clinical interventions or treatment decisions, J is the state transition probability function, representing the probability of transitioning between hidden clinical states, I is the reward function, indicating diagnostic accuracy or successful outcome, α is the discount factor, controlling the influence of future outcomes, and U represents the time horizon, corresponding to the temporal sequence of clinical observations.

The POMDP-like architecture in the proposed framework is not used in its traditional sense of decision-making over time, but as a structured representation to capture uncertainty and conditional dependencies in retrospective tabular clinical data. Each patient’s record represents a single trajectory and the observation is the partially observed vector of clinical features (some features are missing or noisy) and the latent state is the unobserved patient health state. The action is the internal feature selection or attention of the model at each timestep, i.e., selecting or attending to certain subsets of clinical features used for diagnosis. The reward is based on the prediction task, typically the increase in the likelihood of classification accuracy or the decrease in the loss (e.g., cross-entropy) when the selected features help the classification task. The data is retrospective (not time-based) but the framework generates pseudo-time trajectories by iteratively attending to or selecting subsets of features (or feature sequences), enabling the model to learn about the incremental nature of representation refinement. AdaCred does this by applying an attention-based update to the feature set and each update simulates a transition conditioned on the attended features. This makes the model account for uncertainty and partial observability and learn causal relationships between features, making the POMDP formulation meaningful for non-sequential features such as static clinical tabular data.

MBHAN for enhancing model performance in healthcare AI with high accuracy

In the proposed framework for Reducing Misclassifications and Enhancing Model Performance in Healthcare AI, the DA-IMRN is effectively integrated with the MBHAN to form a unified model capable of handling complex, multimodal healthcare data. Healthcare data inherently exhibit heterogeneous characteristics, where each feature or node (such as symptoms, laboratory results, diagnostic images, and patient history) contains diverse semantic and relational information. A single connection pattern or feature interaction (motif) can only reveal a partial view of a patient’s overall diagnostic profile. To obtain a comprehensive and discriminative embedding for each medical entity, the integration of DA-IMRN with MBHAN enables multi-level feature fusion, adaptive attention to motif-based dependencies, and residual refinement through multi-scale learning.

The AdaCred module is achieved by implementing an attention-based feature interaction module, in which each variable is treated as a token and is mapped into a latent representation; attention scores are then computed to quantify the influence of each variable on other variables—for example, interactions between biochemical variables and patient characteristics. Similarly, the motif-based heterogeneous graph model (MBHAN) is achieved by constructing a graph of clinical variables, where nodes are variables, and edges are statistical or domain-dependent relationships (e.g., correlations, mutual information, a prior knowledge-based relationship). Motifs are common substructures among sets of variables that allow the model to learn interactions that are more than just pairwise. To make these components not only well-defined but also meaningful, their components are linked to the performance gain in the ablation study, where performance improves with the presence of each of these components. Finally, the figures are seamlessly integrated into the text by being connected to the model: feature statistics display the distribution and separability of the main features that influence attention weights in AdaCred; p-value plots show the significance of feature contributions and gains from the model; and performance metrics (ROC, precision-recall, calibration) indicate that the learned representations make the model perform better.

In this architecture, MBHAN serves as the relational reasoning backbone, while DA-IMRN provides hierarchical feature extraction with dual-attention guidance to preserve both spatial and contextual healthcare correlations. Each node in the heterogeneous medical graph represents a distinct data type, such as clinical test results, imaging biomarkers, or patient demographics, and each edge represents an inter-attribute relationship. DO for optimizing DA-IMRN-MBHAN parameters to enhance model performance in healthcare AI with high accuracy

The effectiveness of the DA-IMRN-MBHAN framework in Reducing Misclassifications and Enhancing Model Performance in Healthcare AI is fundamentally dependent on the precise optimization of its internal weight parameters. These parameters control critical aspects such as attention coefficients, kernel transformations, learning dynamics, and residual fusion processes. To address these challenges, the DO is incorporated for adaptive and efficient hyperparameter optimization of the DA-IMRN-MBHAN framework, ensuring that the system achieves high diagnostic precision, computational efficiency, and generalization capability.

The DO is a bio-inspired metaheuristic optimization algorithm modeled after the natural dispersal mechanism of dandelion seeds influenced by environmental wind patterns. The algorithm efficiently balances global exploration searching across broad parameter regions and local exploitation refining the most promising parameter candidates. This balance ensures that the optimization process avoids local minima and identifies the most suitable weight configurations for achieving minimal misclassification and maximum accuracy in healthcare AI applications.

The DMGH-IDGM-SRAN architecture aims to address multiple aspects of the clinical tabular data that are essential to capture such as uncertainty, interactions and non-linearity between the features, which cannot be captured by a single module. Each of the components is important: FMNN is used in the data preprocessing stage to model uncertainty and overlapping regions of classes in clinical features; AdaCred is used for feature extraction to model causal and contextual interactions between the features in the data; DA-IMRNS models feature interactions at multiple levels and uses an adaptive attention mechanism to emphasize the informative clinical features; MBHAN further enhances the representation learning by modelling higher-order interactions in the data through a graph-based formulation of feature interactions; and Dandelion Optimizer (DO) is used for the hyperparameter optimization to ensure stability of convergence and generalization. The results of the ablation study indicate that although some of the individual modules or a few combinations perform well, they are unable to capture the complex feature interactions in the data, resulting in lower performance. This modular approach ensures uncertainty modelling, local and global feature interactions and robustness, making the use of the full model architecture necessary.

The dataset is split into training and validation sets using the stratified k-fold cross-validation method to ensure that each fold has the same class distribution and no data leakage occurs. And the experiment is repeated multiple times with different initial random seeds to ensure the model’s stability and reliability. The metrics such as classification accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score and area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) are reported as the average with standard deviations and 95% confidence bounds to account for the randomness and uncertainty. This extensive evaluation ensures the superior classification accuracy is stable, consistent and not a fluke or overfitting issue.

To ensure that there is no data leakage, careful data processing and splitting strategies are used for the experiments. Stratified sampling is used for data splitting to maintain class balance in the training and testing data. Data pre-processing steps such as normalization, missing value imputation and uncertainty modeling (using the Fuzzy Min–Max Neural Network (FMNN) are done only on the training subset of each fold and the parameters are used to pre-process the subset of validation or test data. Similarly, feature learning with Adaptive Causal Decision Transformers (AdaCred) are only conducted on the training data to prevent overfitting. Care is taken to ensure that there is no overlap between the different splits and, when using patient-level data, the splits are performed on a patient level. This process makes it possible to assess the model performance based on its ability to generalize and that the performance numbers are valid and fair.

The DMGH-IDGM-SRAN framework has been adapted to use structured data (tabular data) by extending the framework to use features in each module rather than pixels. Each patient is represented as a feature vector, where the clinical and biochemical features (e.g., laboratory test results, patient demographics, and diagnostic features) are first encoded using FMNN to consider uncertainty and overlaps between classes. The AdaCred module then learns the relationships between the features by capturing causal and conditional dependencies between variables, enabling the modeling of the impact of a change in one clinical variable on other variables over time or conditionally. To learn more complex patterns, we also transform the tabular data into a heterogeneous graph, where the nodes represent clinical features and edges represent statistical associations (e.g., correlation, mutual information, or domain expert specified associations).

We then use the Motif-Based Heterogeneous Graph Attention Network (MBHAN) module to learn higher-order patterns (motifs) among sets of variables, thus enabling capturing co-occurrence patterns. Meanwhile, we also adapt the Dual-Attention-Guided Interactive Multi-Scale Residual Network (DA-IMRN) to work with tabular data, adopting attention mechanisms that work along the feature dimensions and along multiple hierarchical groups of features, rather than spatial dimensions, to emphasize the most informative clinical features. Thus, our proposed architecture allows the framework to capture both local feature importance, global interdependencies and local higher-order patterns in the tabular data. This design is well aligned with clinical practice, as the onset of disease is not a consequence of individual features, but rather a complex interaction of multiple physiological processes, allowing the proposed design to be well suited and perform well on tabular clinical data.

The statistical tests are designed to test whether the improvements in model performance on DMGH-IDGM-SRAN are statistically significant compared to other models. Specifically, performance metrics (such as accuracy, F1-score and AUC) for each folds in stratified cross-validation (and multiple runs) are paired for each model. We use a paired statistical test, comparing the performance of the DMGH-IDGM-SRAN with each baseline model, on the same partitions of the data (within each fold and between the folds)—to give the models a fair chance. Assuming normality (often checked, e.g. with a Shapiro–Wilk test), a paired t-test (if the differences are normally distributed) or a non-parametric Wilcoxon signed-rank test (if the differences are not normally distributed) can be employed on the fold-wise differences in performance metrics. The null hypothesis is that there is no difference in performance, while the alternative hypothesis is in favour of improved performance of the new method. The p-values (p < 0.05) indicate significant improvements, which guarantees that improvements were not observed by chance or caused by individual folds. In addition, effect sizes (e.g., Cohen’s d) are also reported to complement p-values with an estimate of the improvement magnitude. This ensures the improved performance of the proposed model is not only substantial but also statistically significant.

We evaluate the dataset with cross-validation using stratified k-fold because it ensures a balanced class distribution in each fold and guarantees that each data point in the data is used for both training and testing, but not both. Finally, the results are calculated by running the experiments multiple times with different random seeds and the outcomes are reported as the means and standard deviations. To prevent any data leakage, all data pre-processing (normalisation, missing-value imputation, and FMNN-based uncertainty modelling) is applied solely on the training data in each fold, and the parameters learned from the training data are applied to the corresponding validation/test data. Similarly, the feature extractor (AdaCred) is trained only on the training part of the data, avoiding information leakage. The splitting of samples ensures that the training and test samples are independent. The above ensures that the model is not overfitting and the high accuracy is genuine.

Discussion

The proposed DMGH-IDGM-SRAN model demonstrates considerable potential in overcoming the existing misclassification issues in AI-assisted healthcare diagnostic systems. With the inclusion of dual attention mechanisms, multi-scale residual learning, and heterogeneous graph modeling using motifs, it is expected that the proposed model will be successful in capturing complex interactions between features in a range of biochemical and clinical data sets. Overall, these components are likely to provide a more structured approach to understanding relationships between different variables, especially in clinical data sets where considerable levels of heterogeneity are known to exist.

It is also clear that the proposed AdaCred component will play a vital role in extracting relationships in a feature dependency structure, while the FMNN component will also help in managing uncertainty and overlapping classes in clinical data sets. As a means of optimizing the proposed network, the Dandelion Optimizer (DO) algorithm was also employed in this work, and it is clear from the proposed experiment that this approach improves the overall convergence behavior of the network while also ensuring a reduced risk of overfitting, especially in noisy data sets.

Based on the proposed experiment, where a data set of 743 records from the Pulmonology Department was employed in this work, the proposed DMGH-IDGM-SRAN model demonstrated a high classification accuracy of 99.9% when compared to the proposed baseline algorithms, thus indicating considerable potential in this area of research. It is also important to note here that the proposed experiment is limited to a specific data set and protocol, and it is expected that this approach may not be directly applicable in different clinical data sets and disease domains.

In addition, the proposed framework also includes considerations related to interpretability and demonstrates robustness in heterogeneous data regimes, factors of potential utility in a clinical context. Nevertheless, it should be noted that to demonstrate clinical utility, further evaluation on heterogeneous data regimes should be undertaken. Regarding the overall performance of the proposed approach, it should be noted that the experimental results demonstrate the viability of the proposed DMGH-IDGM-SRAN approach to artificial intelligence-assisted diagnostic classification, a contribution to more trustworthy artificial intelligence in healthcare applications.

Limitations

The proposed work acknowledges that the dataset size is limited and it is collected from a single center, which may limit the capability of direct model transfer to other patient groups and data collection centers. But the proposed study addresses these concerns in order to increase the robustness and generalization. First, we evaluate the model using stratified sampling and multiple iterations of the experiments to ensure the stability and independence of the performance results from specific train/test splits. Second, the network is structured to learn domain-independent representations of the clinical features by capturing both local and global interactions between these features, rather than the peculiarities of the data set. Third, other stability analyses, e.g. noise and sensitivity analyses, are conducted to assess the model performance with noisy inputs. When possible, a temporal hold-out strategy can also be adopted to reflect the real-world deployment scenario of the model by testing the model with new distributions. While planned for future work, the results demonstrate high internal consistency and stability, suggesting the proposed approach will be able to perform well when applied to larger and more diverse data.

Conclusion and future direction

In this manuscript, a novel framework entitled “Dual Motif Guided Heterogeneous Interactive Dandelion Graph Multi-Scale Residual Attention Network” (DMGH-IDGM-SRAN) for minimizing the occurrence of misclassifications and improving the performance of the model in the field of healthcare AI is proposed. The manuscript uses a dataset entitled “Healthcare AI Dataset for Reducing Misclassifications and Enhancing Model Performance in Healthcare AI,” which was designed in collaboration with experts from various fields. The dataset contains 743 instances of clinical data collected from the Pulmonology Department of a renowned hospital in India.

Pre-processing of the dataset was done with the Fuzzy Min–Max Neural Network (FMNN) to handle the uncertainty that occurs between the different classes. Adaptive credibility (AdaCred) mechanisms were used to extract the features from the dataset. This mechanism can handle heterogeneous data as well as the causal dependencies that occur between the different features. The novel framework that was designed for minimizing the occurrence of misclassifications in the field of healthcare AI uses the Dual Attention-based Interactive Multi-Scale Residual Network (DA-IMRN) coupled with the Motif-Based Heterogeneous Attention Network (MBHAN) with the Dandelion Optimizer (DO) to optimize the hyperparameters.

As such, the experimental results based on the above-described dataset verify that the proposed model can attain a high accuracy rate of 99.9%, thereby outperforming the other methods under the same experimental environment. Moreover, the proposed framework has two important characteristics: it can extract the complex cross-feature relationships among the clinical parameters and biochemical indicators via the graph-based heterogeneous modeling method, and it can provide interpretable diagnostic predictions that are highly stable under the noisy and incomplete data conditions.

It is noted that the results obtained in the present study are based on a single-site dataset, and the generalizability of the results obtained herein to other clinical populations and diverse disease domains is still to be investigated. As such, the future research directions include the extension of the dataset to more medical facilities, the incorporation of multimodal data sources such as medical images, the application of formal explainable AI (XAI) for the improvement of the interpretability of the framework, the optimization of the framework’s efficiency, the extension of the framework to other disease domains, among other directions.

While promising results have been obtained in this experiment, several key factors should be recognized, each of which directly relates to a corresponding direction in future work. Collectively, these constraints define the scope of this current work and provide a clear direction in which future work should be carried out.

The first and perhaps most critical constraint relates to the scope and scale of the dataset. As a key component of this work, a dataset of 743 samples is employed, collected exclusively from the Pulmonology Department of a single hospital in India. Therefore, this significantly constrains the statistical scope of this work and raises legitimate concerns regarding its potential applicability in a variety of different clinical contexts, patient demographics, and regions. To address this constraint in future work, a key direction will be to significantly extend this dataset by collecting samples from a variety of different hospitals through the adoption of federated learning, a technique in machine learning that allows collaborative learning in a privacy-preserving manner. Furthermore, this work will also seek to address potential concerns relating to generalizability through independent validation on a variety of different patient demographics.

A second limitation relates to the modality and richness of input data. Currently, the framework processes data in a structured tabular format only, thereby excluding data modalities such as radiological images (e.g., X-rays and CT scans), genomic markers, spirometry data, and electronic health record (EHR) histories, which are commonly employed in clinical diagnosis and are known to have high diagnostic potential. Such a limitation in data modality may impact on the overall comprehensiveness of the system’s diagnostic output in a real-world scenario. In this direction, future developments in the proposed framework will focus on multimodal data fusion using a graph-based paradigm that supports data modalities such as images, genomic data, and EHR histories in a unified manner. Concurrently, a set of formal Explainable AI (XAI) techniques such as SHAP values, LIME, and visualization of attention maps will also be incorporated to ensure that the decision-making rationale of the proposed system remains interpretable and auditable in a clinical context, thereby supporting its potential as a trustworthy decision support system.

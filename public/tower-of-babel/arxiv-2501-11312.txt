Formal manifolds: local structure of morphisms, and formal submanifolds
Fulin Chen, Binyong Sun, Chuyun Wang

Abstract
. This is a paper in a series that studies smooth relative Lie algebra homologies and cohomologies based on the theory of formal manifolds and formal Lie groups. In three previous papers, we introduce the notion of formal manifolds and study their basic theory, focusing on function spaces and Poincaré’s lemma. In this paper, we further explore the foundational framework of formal manifolds, including the local structure of constant rank morphisms (such as inverse function theorem and constant rank theorems) as well as the theory of formal submanifolds.

1.1.
Basics on formal manifolds

We first recall from
[
CSW1
]
some notions concerning formal manifolds.
Let
N
be a smooth manifold, and let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
For every open subset
U\subset N
, write
{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(U)
for the
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra of complex-valued smooth functions on
U
, and write

{\mathcal{O}}_{N}^{(k)}(U):={\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(U)[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]]

for the
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra of formal power series with coefficients in
{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}(U)
. With the obvious restriction maps, we have a sheaf

{\mathcal{O}}_{N}^{(k)}:\ U\mapsto{\mathcal{O}}_{N}^{(k)}(U)

of
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebras. This gives a locally ringed space

N^{(k)}:=(N,{\mathcal{O}}_{N}^{(k)})

over
\mathrm{Spec}({\mathbb{C}})
.

Definition 1.1
.

A formal manifold is a locally ringed space
(M,{\mathcal{O}})
over
\mathrm{Spec}({\mathbb{C}})
such that

•

the topological space
M
is paracompact and Hausdorff; and

•

for every
a\in M
, there is an open neighborhood
U
of
a
in
M
and
n,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
(U,{\mathcal{O}}|_{U})
is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{(k)}
as locally ringed spaces over
\mathrm{Spec}({\mathbb{C}})
.

By abuse of notation, we will often not distinguish a formal manifold
(M,{\mathcal{O}})
with its underlying topological space
M
, and call
{\mathcal{O}}
the structure sheaf of it.

Throughout this paper, let
(M,{\mathcal{O}})
be a formal manifold.
An element in
{\mathcal{O}}(M)
is called a formal function on
M
. By a chart of
M
, we mean a triple
(U,N^{(k)},\vartheta)
, where
U
is an open subset of
M
,
N
is an open submanifold of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and

\vartheta=(\overline{\vartheta},\vartheta^{*}):\ (U,{\mathcal{O}}|_{U})\rightarrow N^{(k)}

is an isomorphism of locally ringed spaces.
If there is no confusion, we will simply denote a chart
(U,N^{(k)},\vartheta)
of
M
by
U
.
An open cover
\{U_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
of
M
is called an atlas if for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
,
U_{\gamma}
is a chart of
M
.

Let
a\in M
. The uniquely determined natural numbers
n
and
k
in Definition
1.1
are
called the dimension and the degree of
M
at
a
, to be denoted by
\dim_{a}M
and
\deg_{a}M
, respectively.
We let
\mathfrak{m}_{a}
denote the maximal ideal of the stalk
{\mathcal{O}}_{a}
, and define the formal stalk at
a
to be the local
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra

(1.1)

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{a}:=\varprojlim_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\mathcal{O}}_{a}/\mathfrak{m}_{a}^{k}.

Similarly, let
\widehat{\mathfrak{m}}_{a}
denote the maximal ideal of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{a}
.
It is a fact that

\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{a}\cong\mathbb{C}[[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}]]\quad(n:=\dim_{a}M,\ k:=\deg_{a}M)

as
\mathbb{C}
-algebras (see
[
CSW1
, Proposition 2.6]
). Then we obtain a formal manifold

(1.2)

M_{a}:=(\{a\},\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{a}).

Here and below, we will often not distinguish a sheaf over a singleton with its space of global sections.

Let
\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}}
denote the ideal of
{\mathcal{O}}
defined by

(1.3)

\mathfrak{m}_{\mathcal{O}}(U):=\{f\in{\mathcal{O}}(U)\,:\,f_{a}\in\mathfrak{m}_{a}\textrm{ for all $a\in U$}\},

where
U
is an open subset of
M
, and
f_{a}
is the germ of
f
at
a
.
Form the quotient sheaf
\underline{{\mathcal{O}}}:={\mathcal{O}}/{\mathfrak{m}_{{\mathcal{O}}}}
over
M
.
Then

(1.4)

\underline{M}:=(M,\underline{{\mathcal{O}}})

is a smooth manifold, called the reduction of
M
.

In the rest part of the Introduction, let

\varphi=({\overline{\varphi}},\varphi^{*}):\ (M^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime})\rightarrow(M,{\mathcal{O}})

be a morphism of formal manifolds, and let
b\in M^{\prime}
.
By definition,
{\overline{\varphi}}:M^{\prime}\rightarrow M
is a continuous map and

(1.5)

\varphi^{*}:\ {\overline{\varphi}}^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}

is a
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra sheaf homomorphism that induces local homomorphisms on the stalks.
For open subsets
U
of
M
and
U^{\prime}
of
M^{\prime}
such that
\overline{\varphi}(U^{\prime})\subset U
,
we write

\varphi^{*}_{U,U^{\prime}}:\ {\mathcal{O}}(U)\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}(U^{\prime})

for the homomorphism of
\mathbb{C}
-algebras induced by (
1.5
).
If there is no confusion, we will denote
\varphi^{*}_{U,U^{\prime}}
by
\varphi^{*}_{U}
or
\varphi^{*}
for simplicity.
We denote by

\varphi_{b}^{*}:\ {\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{\varphi}(b)}\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}_{b}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\varphi_{b}^{*}:\ \widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\overline{\varphi}(b)}\rightarrow\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}_{b}\qquad(b\in M^{\prime})

the induced local homomorphisms on the stalks and formal stalks, respectively.

We have the obvious morphisms

(1.6)

\underline{M}\rightarrow M\qquad\text{and}\qquad M_{a}\rightarrow M\qquad(a\in M).

Furthermore, there are unique morphisms

(1.7)

\underline{\varphi}:\ \underline{M^{\prime}}\rightarrow\underline{M}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\varphi_{b}:\ M^{\prime}_{b}\rightarrow M_{\overline{\varphi}(b)}

such that the diagrams

(1.8)

\begin{CD}M^{\prime}@>{\varphi}>{}>M\\
@A{}A{}A@A{}A{}A\\
\underline{M^{\prime}}@>{\underline{\varphi}}>{}>\underline{M}\\
\end{CD}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\begin{CD}M^{\prime}@>{\varphi}>{}>M\\
@A{}A{}A@A{}A{}A\\
M^{\prime}_{b}@>{\varphi_{b}}>{}>M_{\overline{\varphi}(b)}\\
\end{CD}

commute, respectively.

Let
n,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and let
N
be an open submanifold of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Write

x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}
and
y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k}
for the standard coordinate functions and formal variables in
{\mathcal{O}}_{N}^{(k)}(N)
, respectively.
We call

(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k})

the standard coordinate system of
N^{(k)}
.
When
M=N^{(k)}
, the morphism
\varphi:M^{\prime}\rightarrow N^{(k)}
is uniquely determined by the
(n+k)
-tuple (see Proposition
3.1
)

c_{\varphi}:=(\varphi^{*}(x_{1}),\varphi^{*}(x_{2}),\dots,\varphi^{*}(x_{n}),\varphi^{*}(y_{1}),\varphi^{*}(y_{2}),\dots,\varphi^{*}(y_{k}))\in({\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}(M^{\prime}))^{n+k}.

In view of this, we often formally say that
\varphi
is given by

(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k})\mapsto c_{\varphi}.

1.2.
Local structure of morphisms

In Sections
2
and
3
, we mainly analyze the local structure of a morphism of formal manifolds using its differentials. As we will explain below,
the situation is quite a bit more subtle than the analogous theory
of smooth manifolds.

Similar to the case of smooth manifolds, a tangent vector at a point
a\in M
is defined to be a derivation on the stalk
{\mathcal{O}}_{a}
(see Definition
2.1
).
Write
\mathrm{T}_{a}(M)
for the space of tangent vectors of
M
at
a
.
By taking the transpose of the homomorphism
\varphi^{*}_{b}:{\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{\varphi}(b)}\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}_{b}
, it induces a linear map

d\varphi_{b}:\ \mathrm{T}_{b}(M^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathrm{T}_{\overline{\varphi}(b)}(M),

which is called the differential of
\varphi
at
b
.

The most fundamental results on the local structure of smooth maps between smooth manifolds are the inverse function theorem (see
[
L
, Theorem 4.5]
) and its consequences, particularly the constant rank theorem (see
[
L
, Theorem 4.12]
).
However, the following example shows that the inverse function theorem
cannot be directly generalized to the setting of formal manifolds.

Example 1.2
.

Let
n,r\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
n\geq r
. We have a morphism

({\mathbb{R}}^{n-r})^{(r)}\rightarrow({\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{(0)}

such that the homomorphism

{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\rightarrow{\mathrm{C}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n-r})[[y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{r}]]

of the
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebras of formal functions
is given by the Taylor series expansion along
{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
.
For every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, it induces an obvious morphism

(1.9)

({\mathbb{R}}^{n-r})^{(r+k)}=({\mathbb{R}}^{n-r})^{(r)}\times(\mathbb{R}^{0})^{(k)}\rightarrow({\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{(k)}=({\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{(0)}\times(\mathbb{R}^{0})^{(k)}.

The differential of (
1.9
) at every point of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-r}
is a bijection, while the morphism (
1.9
) is not a local isomorphism unless
r=0
.

This new phenomenon leads to the following two natural questions.

(Q1)

When is the morphism
\varphi
an isomorphism near
b
?

(Q2)

What is the local model
of
\varphi
when
d\varphi_{b}
is bijective?

For the question (Q1), note that if
\varphi
is an isomorphism near
b
(and so is
\underline{\varphi}
), then both

d\varphi_{b}
and
d\underline{\varphi}_{b}
are bijective. Conversely, we have the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

\varphi=({\overline{\varphi}},\varphi^{*}):(M^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime})\rightarrow(M,{\mathcal{O}})

be a morphism of formal manifolds, and let
b\in M^{\prime}
. Assume that

d\varphi_{b}:\ \mathrm{T}_{b}(M^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathrm{T}_{\overline{\varphi}(b)}(M)\quad\text{and}\quad d\underline{\varphi}_{b}:\ \mathrm{T}_{b}(\underline{M}^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathrm{T}_{\overline{\varphi}(b)}(\underline{M})

are both bijective.
Then there exists an open neighborhood
U^{\prime}
of
b
in
M^{\prime}
such that
U:=\overline{\varphi}(U^{\prime})
is an open subset of
M
, and the restriction

\varphi|_{U^{\prime}}:\ (U^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}|_{U^{\prime}})\rightarrow(U,{\mathcal{O}}|_{U})

of
\varphi
on
U^{\prime}
is an isomorphism of formal manifolds.

When
M
and
M^{\prime}
are both smooth manifolds, Theorem
1.3
is just the usual inverse function theorem for smooth manifolds. When the underlying spaces of
M^{\prime}
and
M
are both singletons, Theorem
1.3
is a reformulation of
the formal inverse function theorem (see
[
H
, (A4.5)]
for example). Theorem
1.3
is thus a common generalization of these two classical results.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
will be presented in §
2.3
.
For the question (Q2),
we will show in Corollary
3.20
that if
d\varphi_{b}
is bijective, then the local expression of
\varphi
near
b
is modeled by the morphism (
1.9
) for some
n,k,r\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Now we consider the case that the differential
d\varphi_{b}
may or may not be bijective.
As we have seen before, the local structure
of
\varphi
near
b
depends not only on
d\varphi_{b}
, but also on
d\underline{\varphi}_{b}
. In fact,

\mathrm{T}_{b}(\underline{M^{\prime}})
is naturally a subspace of
\mathrm{T}_{b}(M^{\prime})
and
d\underline{\varphi}_{b}
coincides with the restriction of
d\varphi_{b}
on
\mathrm{T}_{b}(\underline{M^{\prime}})
(see §
2.2
).
It is an elementary fact in linear algebra that such
a linear map
d\varphi_{b}
(namely, a linear map from
\mathrm{T}_{b}(M^{\prime})
to
\mathrm{T}_{\overline{\varphi}(b)}(M)
that maps
\mathrm{T}_{b}(\underline{M}^{\prime})
into
\mathrm{T}_{\overline{\varphi}(b)}(\underline{M}))
is characterized by the triple (see Remark
2.8
)

(1.10)

(\mathrm{Rank}(d\varphi_{b}),\mathrm{Rank}(d\underline{\varphi}_{b}),\mathrm{Rank}(d\varphi_{b}^{\mathrm{f}}))\in{\mathbb{N}}^{3}.

Here

\mathrm{Rank}(\,\cdot\,)
denotes the rank of a linear map between finite-dimensional vector spaces, and

d\varphi_{b}^{\mathrm{f}}:\mathrm{T}_{b}(M^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}_{b}(\underline{M^{\prime}})\rightarrow\mathrm{T}_{\overline{\varphi}(b)}(M)/\mathrm{T}_{\overline{\varphi}(b)}(\underline{M})

denotes the quotient map induced by
d\varphi_{b}
.
Taking this point of view, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 1.4
.

The triple (
1.10
) is called
the rank of
\varphi
at
b
, to be denoted by
\mathrm{Rank}_{b}(\varphi)
.

As in the smooth manifolds case, we say that
\varphi
has constant rank near
b
if there is an open neighborhood
V^{\prime}
of
b
in
M^{\prime}
such that
\mathrm{Rank}_{b^{\prime}}(\varphi)=\mathrm{Rank}_{b}(\varphi)
for all
b^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
. Motivated by the constant rank theorem for smooth manifolds,
we also make the following definition.

Definition 1.5
.

The morphism
\varphi
is said to be standardizable near
b
if there exists a chart
(U^{\prime},(N^{\prime})^{(k^{\prime})},\vartheta^{\prime})
of
M^{\prime}
containing
b
and a chart
(U,N^{(k)},\vartheta)
of
M
containing
\overline{\varphi}(b)
such that
\overline{\varphi}(U^{\prime})\subset U
and that the morphism

(1.11)

\vartheta\circ\varphi|_{U^{\prime}}\circ(\vartheta^{\prime})^{-1}:\ (N^{\prime})^{(k^{\prime})}\rightarrow N^{(k)}

is given by

(1.12)

\begin{split}&(x_{1},\dots,x_{r_{1}},x_{r_{1}+1},\dots,x_{n-r_{2}},x_{n-r_{2}+1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{r_{3}},y_{r_{3}+1},\dots,y_{k})\\
\mapsto\ &(u_{1},\dots,u_{r_{1}},\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{n-r_{1}-r_{2}},z_{1},\dots,z_{r_{2}},z_{r_{2}+1},\dots,z_{r_{2}+r_{3}},\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{k-r_{3}})\end{split}

for some
r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
where
(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},y_{2}\dots,y_{k})
and
(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{n^{\prime}},z_{1},

z_{2},\dots,z_{k^{\prime}})
are respectively the standard coordinate systems of
N^{(k)}
and
(N^{\prime})^{(k^{\prime})}
.

If
\varphi
is standardizable near
b
as in Definition
1.5
, then
\varphi
has constant rank
(r_{1}+r_{2}+r_{3},r_{1},r_{3})
near
b
. However, if
\varphi
has constant rank near
b
, it may not be standardizable near
b
, as shown in the following example.

Example 1.6
.

Suppose that
M^{\prime}=M=({\mathbb{R}}^{0})^{(1)}
(and then
{\mathcal{O}}(M)={\mathcal{O}}(M^{\prime})={\mathbb{C}}[[y]]
),
and
\varphi
is determined by

\varphi^{*}\left(\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}c_{i}\,y^{i}\right)=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}c_{i}\,y^{2i}\quad(c_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}}).

It is obvious that
\varphi
has constant rank
(0,0,0)
.
If
\varphi
is standardizable, then there exist isomorphisms
\vartheta=(\overline{\vartheta},\vartheta^{*}):M\rightarrow M
and
\vartheta^{\prime}=(\overline{\vartheta^{\prime}},\vartheta^{\prime*}):M^{\prime}\rightarrow M^{\prime}
such that the kernel of the homomorphism

((\vartheta^{\prime})^{-1})^{*}\circ\varphi^{*}\circ(\vartheta)^{*}:\ \mathbb{C}[[y]]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[[y]]

is
y{\mathbb{C}}[[y]]
, which contradicts to the fact that
\varphi^{*}
is injective.

Naturally, we have the following two questions.

(Q3)

What is the local model
of
\varphi
when it has constant rank near
b
?

(Q4)

When is the morphism
\varphi
standardizable near
b
?

By applying Theorem
1.3
, we will provide an answer of (Q3) in Theorem
3.6
.
For the question (Q4), let
b^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
and consider the homomorphism

(1.13)

\varphi^{*}_{b^{\prime},0}:\ \widehat{\mathfrak{m}}_{\overline{\varphi}(b^{\prime})}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}_{b^{\prime}}\quad(\widehat{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}_{b^{\prime}}\ \text{is the maximal ideal of}\ \widehat{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}_{b^{\prime}})

induced by
\varphi^{*}_{b^{\prime}}:\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\overline{\varphi}(b^{\prime})}\rightarrow\widehat{{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}_{b^{\prime}}
.
Then there are canonical homomorphisms

(1.14)

\varphi^{*}_{b^{\prime},2}:\ \widehat{\mathfrak{m}}_{\overline{\varphi}(b^{\prime})}/\widehat{\mathfrak{m}}_{\overline{\varphi}(b^{\prime})}^{2}\rightarrow\widehat{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}_{b^{\prime}}/\widehat{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}_{b^{\prime}}^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi_{b^{\prime},\mathrm{k}}^{*}:\ker\varphi^{*}_{b^{\prime},0}\rightarrow\ker\varphi^{*}_{b^{\prime},2}

such that the diagram

(1.15)

\begin{CD}\ker\varphi^{*}_{b^{\prime},0}@>{}>{}>\widehat{\mathfrak{m}}_{\overline{\varphi}(b^{\prime})}@>{\varphi^{*}_{b^{\prime},0}}>{}>\widehat{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}_{b^{\prime}}\\
@V{\varphi_{b^{\prime},\mathrm{k}}^{*}}V{}V@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
\ker\varphi^{*}_{b^{\prime},2}@>{}>{}>\widehat{\mathfrak{m}}_{\overline{\varphi}(b^{\prime})}/\widehat{\mathfrak{m}}_{\overline{\varphi}(b^{\prime})}^{2}@>{\varphi^{*}_{b^{\prime},2}}>{}>\widehat{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}_{b^{\prime}}/\widehat{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}}_{b^{\prime}}^{2}\end{CD}

commutes.
We have the following result about (Q4), whose proof will be given in §
3.3
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\varphi=(\overline{\varphi},\varphi^{*}):(M^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime})\rightarrow(M,{\mathcal{O}})
be a morphism of formal manifolds, and let
b\in M^{\prime}
.
Then the morphism
\varphi
is standardizable near
b
if and only if
there is an open neighborhood
V^{\prime}
of
b
in
M^{\prime}
such that for every
b^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
,
\mathrm{Rank}_{b^{\prime}}(\varphi)=\mathrm{Rank}_{b}(\varphi)
and

\varphi^{*}_{b^{\prime},\mathrm{k}}
is surjective.

In §
3.4
, we will consider the special case that
\varphi
is an immersion or a submersion at
b
, namely,
d\varphi_{b}
is injective or surjective.
In such cases,
\varphi
may or may not have constant rank near
b
(see Examples
3.15
and
3.16
). On the other hand, if
\varphi
is in addition assumed to have constant rank near
b
, then it is standardizable near
b
(see Propositions
3.23
and

3.19
).
A sufficient condition for
\varphi
to have constant rank near
b
is that it is regular at
b
in the sense that

\mathrm{Rank}(d\varphi_{b})=\mathrm{Rank}(d\underline{\varphi}_{b})+\mathrm{Rank}(d\varphi_{b}^{\mathrm{f}}).

See Lemma
3.18
for details.

We remark that the local behavior of a regular morphism of formal manifolds is quite similar to that of a morphism of supermanifolds.
For example, there are supergeometric versions of the inverse function, immersion and submersion theorems for morphisms of supermanifolds (see
[
CCF
, Propositions 5.2.1, 5.2.4, and 5.2.5]
), and we
have analog results for regular morphisms of formal manifolds (see Corollaries
3.20
,
3.21
, and
3.24
).
On the other hand, a constant rank regular morphism of formal manifolds may not be standardizable, and the same phenomenon appears in the supermanifolds case.

Remark 1.8
.

The obvious analog of Theorem
1.7
for supermanifolds, which characterizes the standardizable morphisms between supermanifolds, also holds true. In
[
CCF
, § 5.2]
, a notion of constant rank morphisms between supermanifolds is defined and the corresponding constant rank theorem (see
[
CCF
, Proposition 5.2.6]
) is proved, which gives another characterization of the standardizable morphisms between supermanifolds. We remark that, unlike to the smooth manifolds case, constant rank morphisms in
[
CCF
]
are not defined by the ranks of the differential maps.
Additionally, the condition to character standardizable morphism in Theorem
1.7
is given point-wisely, while that of
[
CCF
, Proposition 5.2.6]
is given locally but not point-wisely.

1.3.
Formal submanifolds

In Section
4
, we aim to develop the theory of formal submanifolds.
As usual,
\varphi
is called an immersion if
d\varphi_{b^{\prime}}
is injective for every
b^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
. Additionally,
\varphi
is said to be injective (resp. closed; resp. embedded) if
\overline{\varphi}
is injective (resp. closed; resp. a topological embedding).
For examples, the morphisms
\underline{M}\rightarrow M
and
M_{a}\rightarrow M
(
a\in M
) are both closed embedded immersions (see Lemma
2.7
). We have the following characterizations of immersions and closed embedded immersions of formal manifolds, whose proof will be given in §
4.1
.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let

\varphi=(\overline{\varphi},\varphi^{*}):(M^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime})\rightarrow(M,{\mathcal{O}})
be a morphism of formal manifolds.

(a) The morphism
\varphi
is an immersion if and only if the
\mathbb{C}
-algebra sheaf homomorphism

\varphi^{*}:\ \overline{\varphi}^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}

is surjective.

(b) The morphism
\varphi
is a closed embedded immersion if and only if the
\mathbb{C}
-algebra homomorphism

\varphi^{*}:\ {\mathcal{O}}(M)\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}(M^{\prime})

is surjective.

Let
\mathrm{Imm}(M)
denote the family of all pairs
(M_{0},\varphi_{0})
, where
M_{0}
is a formal manifold and
\varphi_{0}
is an injective immersion from
M_{0}
to
M
. Two pairs
(M_{1},\varphi_{1})
and
(M_{2},\varphi_{2})
in
\mathrm{Imm}(M)
are said to be equivalent if there exists an isomorphism

\psi:M_{1}\rightarrow M_{2}
of formal manifolds such that
\varphi_{2}\circ\psi=\varphi_{1}
. As in the case of smooth manifolds, an immersed formal submanifold of
M
defined below can be understood as an equivalence class in
\mathrm{Imm}(M)
.

Definition 1.10
.

An immersed formal submanifold of
(M,{\mathcal{O}})
is a pair
(S,(i^{-1}{\mathcal{O}})/{\mathcal{I}})
consisting of

•

a topological space
S
such that
S
is a subset of
M
and the inclusion
i:S\rightarrow M
is continuous; and

•

a quotient sheaf
(i^{-1}{\mathcal{O}})/{\mathcal{I}}
over
S
, where
{\mathcal{I}}
is an ideal of the sheaf
i^{-1}{\mathcal{O}}
of
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebras over
S
,

such that, as a locally ringed space,
(S,(i^{-1}{\mathcal{O}})/{\mathcal{I}})
is a formal manifold.

For each immersed formal submanifold
(S,(i^{-1}{\mathcal{O}})/{\mathcal{I}})
of
(M,{\mathcal{O}})
, we have a canonical morphism

(1.16)

\iota=(\overline{\iota},\iota^{*}):\ (S,(i^{-1}{\mathcal{O}})/{\mathcal{I}})\rightarrow(M,{\mathcal{O}})

of formal manifolds, where
\overline{\iota}=i
is the inclusion, and for every open subset
U
of
M
,
\iota^{*}_{U}
is the composition map

(1.17)

\iota^{*}_{U}:\ {\mathcal{O}}(U)\rightarrow(i^{-1}{\mathcal{O}})(i^{-1}(U))\rightarrow((i^{-1}{\mathcal{O}})/{\mathcal{I}})(i^{-1}(U)).

Then
(S,\iota)
is an element in
\mathrm{Imm}(M)
.
Conversely, each equivalence class in
\mathrm{Imm}(M)
has a unique representative of the form
(S,\iota)
(see Proposition

3.
Constant rank theorems

In this section, we show
how the local behavior of a morphism of formal manifolds is modeled by the behavior of its rank.

3.1.
Morphisms to
N^{(k)}

Let
n,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and let

be an open submanifold of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
In this subsection we give a characterization of the set of morphisms from

to

, which is a generalization of
[

Write
(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k})
for the standard coordinate system of

As mentioned in the Introduction, for every morphism

\displaystyle\{(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{n})\in({\mathcal{O}}(M))^{n}\,:\,(f_{1}(a),f_{2}(a),\dots,f_{n}(a))\in N\ \text{for all}\ a\in M\}

,
and

The main goal of this subsection is to prove the following result.

Proposition 3.1
.

The map

(3.1)

\{\text{morphism from $M$ to $N^{(k)}$}\}\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}(M;N)\times(\mathfrak{m}_{{\mathcal{O}}}(M))^{k},\quad\varphi\mapsto c_{\varphi}

When

[

, Theorem 4.9]

[

the following result is straightforward.

Lemma 3.2

Let

be two morphisms such that

. Then we have that

\varphi_{1}=\varphi_{2}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Proposition

holds when

and

.

Proof.

By Lemma

, it suffices to show that the map (

) is surjective when

Let

Then we have a morphism
\varphi=(\overline{\varphi},\varphi^{*}):M\rightarrow({\mathbb{R}}^{0})^{(1)}
such that

\varphi^{*}:\ {\mathbb{C}}[[y_{1}]]\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}(M),\qquad\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}c_{j}y_{1}^{j}\mapsto\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}c_{j}g^{j}.

Note that the summation

Proof of Proposition
3.1
:

Note that

Then, by considering Lemma

3.2.
Constant rank morphisms

In this subsection, we give a version of constant rank theorem for formal manifolds.
We start with the following definition.

Definition 3.4
.

Let

\varphi=(\overline{\varphi},\varphi^{*}):(M^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime})\rightarrow(M,{\mathcal{O}})
be a morphism of formal manifolds,
b\in M^{\prime}
, and
\bm{r}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{3}
.

(a) We say that
\varphi
has constant rank near
b
if there is an open neighborhood
U^{\prime}
of
b
in
M^{\prime}
such that
\mathrm{Rank}_{b^{\prime}}(\varphi)=\mathrm{Rank}_{b}(\varphi)
for all
b^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}
. In this case, we also say that
\varphi
has constant rank
\bm{r}
near
b
if
\mathrm{Rank}_{b}(\varphi)=\bm{r}
.

(b) We say that
\varphi
has constant rank
\bm{r}
if
\mathrm{Rank}_{b^{\prime}}(\varphi)=\mathbf{r}
for all
b^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
.

(c) We say that
\varphi
has locally constant rank if for every
b^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
, it has constant rank near
b^{\prime}
.

In what follows, we present some examples of constant rank morphisms of formal manifolds.

Example 3.5
.

Let
n,k,n^{\prime},k^{\prime},r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}\in{\mathbb{N}}
with

n^{\prime}\geq r_{1},\quad n\geq r_{1}+r_{2},\quad k^{\prime}\geq r_{2}+r_{3},\quad\text{and}\quad k\geq r_{3}.

Let
N^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n^{\prime}}
and
N\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be open subsets such that
p_{r_{1}}(N^{\prime})\subset N
, where

p_{r_{1}}:\ \mathbb{R}^{n^{\prime}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n^{\prime}})\mapsto(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{r_{1}},\underbrace{0,\dots,0}_{n-r_{1}}).

Write
(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{k})
and
(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{n^{\prime}},z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{k^{\prime}})
for the standard coordinate systems of
N^{{(k)}}
and
(N^{\prime})^{(k^{\prime})}
, respectively.
Define
\varphi:(N^{\prime})^{(k^{\prime})}\rightarrow N^{(k)}
to be the morphism given by

(3.2)

\displaystyle(x_{1},\dots,x_{r_{1}},x_{r_{1}+1},\dots,x_{n-r_{2}},x_{n-r_{2}+1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{r_{3}},y_{r_{3}+1},\dots,y_{k})

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle(u_{1},\dots,u_{r_{1}},g_{1},\dots,g_{n-r_{1}-r_{2}},z_{1},\dots,z_{r_{2}},z_{r_{2}+1},\dots,z_{r_{2}+r_{3}},h_{1},\dots,h_{k-r_{3}}),

where

(3.3)

\displaystyle g_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{r_{2}+r_{3}}g_{ij^{\prime}}z_{j^{\prime}}+\sum_{J\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k^{\prime}};|J|\geq 2}g_{iJ}z^{J}\quad(i=1,2,\dots,n-r_{1}-r_{2}),

(3.4)

\displaystyle h_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime}=r_{2}+1}^{r_{2}+r_{3}}h_{jj^{\prime}}z_{j^{\prime}}+\sum_{J\in{\mathbb{N}}^{k^{\prime}};|J|\geq 2}h_{jJ}z^{J}\quad(j=1,2,\dots,k-r_{3})

for some

.
It is routine that the morphism
\varphi
has constant rank

(r_{1}+r_{2}+r_{3},r_{1},r_{3}).

The following result shows that every constant rank morphism of formal manifolds is locally modeled by some morphism defined above.

Let
\varphi=(\overline{\varphi},\varphi^{*}):(M^{\prime},{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime})\rightarrow(M,{\mathcal{O}})

4.2.

In the category of smooth manifolds, a monomorphism is nothing but an injective smooth map. In the case of formal manifolds, an injective morphism may not be a monomorphism. However, we still have the following result by applying Proposition

Proposition 4.8
.

Let

Proof.

Let

Note that the injectivity of

implies that

\overline{\psi_{1}}=\overline{\psi_{2}}
.
Then it suffices to prove that

\varphi_{b}^{*}:\ {\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{\varphi}(b)}\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}_{b}

implies that

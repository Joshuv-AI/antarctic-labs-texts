Real-time inversion of two-dimensional Fresnel experimental database using orthogonality sampling method with single and multiple sources: the case of transverse electric polarized waves
Junyong Eom, Sangwoo Kang, Minyeob Lee, Won-Kwang Park

Abstract
This paper concerns an application of the orthogonality sampling method (OSM) for a real-time identification of small objects from two-dimensional Fresnel experimental dataset in transverse electric polarization. First, we apply the OSM with a single source by designing an indicator function based on the asymptotic expansion formula for the scattered field in the presence of small objects. We demonstrate that the indicator function can be expressed by an infinite series of Bessel functions of integer order of the first kind, the range of the signal receiver, and the location of the emitter. Based on this, we then investigate the applicability and limitations of the designed OSM. Specifically, we find that the imaging performance is strongly dependent on the source and the applied frequency. We then apply the OSM with multiple sources to improve imaging performance. Based on the identified structure of the OSM with a single source, we design an indicator function with multiple sources and demonstrate that it can be expressed by an infinite series of the Bessel function of integer order of the first kind, and we explain that objects can be identified uniquely using the designed OSM. Numerical simulation results obtained with the Fresnel experimental dataset demonstrate the advantages and disadvantages of the OSM with a single source and confirm that the designed OSM with multiple sources improves imaging performance.

Thus, in this paper, we consider the application of the OSM to identify the existence, location, and shape of an unknown small object from a two-dimensional Fresnel experimental dataset in TE polarization. First, we design a new indicator function with a single source based on the fact that the measured scattered field data can be represented by an asymptotic expansion formula. To explain the applicability and fundamental limitation of object localization, we demonstrate that this indicator function can be represented by an infinite series of Bessel functions of integer order of the first kind, the range of the signal receiver, and the emitter location. This allows us to determine that it is possible to identify small objects using the designed indicator function; however, its imaging performance is strongly dependent on the applied frequency of operation and the emitter location. To verify the theoretical result, we present the results of a numerical simulation and examine some phenomena.

Then, to improve the imaging performance, we apply the OSM with multiple sources. Here, we design an indicator function with multiple sources and extensively demonstrate that the indicator function can be expressed by an infinite series of the Bessel functions of integer order of the first kind. Based on the theoretical result, we demonstrate that the designed indicator function improves the imaging performance and that the object can be identified uniquely. We also present simulation results to support the theoretical results and briefly introduce the imaging results with multiple sources and frequencies.

3
Indicator function with a single source

In the following, we consider the design of an indicator function with a single source. Here, let
\mathbf{E}(m)
be the following arrangement of the measured scattered field data with emitter
\mathcal{A}_{m}
:

\mathbf{E}(m)=\Big(u_{\scat}(\mathbf{b}_{m,1},\mathbf{a}_{m}),u_{\scat}(\mathbf{b}_{m,2},\mathbf{a}_{m}),\ldots,u_{\scat}(\mathbf{b}_{m,N},\mathbf{a}_{m})\Big).

(2)

Then, applying (
1
) to (
2
) yields

\mathbf{E}(m)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}k^{2}\area(D_{s})\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{s}-\varepsilon_{0}}{\varepsilon_{0}\mu_{0}}\right)G(\mathbf{r}_{s},\mathbf{a}_{m})\begin{pmatrix}\nabla G(\mathbf{b}_{m,1},\mathbf{r}_{s})\cdot\nabla G(\mathbf{a}_{m},\mathbf{r}_{s})\\
\nabla G(\mathbf{b}_{m,2},\mathbf{r}_{s})\cdot\nabla G(\mathbf{a}_{m},\mathbf{r}_{s})\\
\vdots\\
\nabla G(\mathbf{b}_{m,N},\mathbf{r}_{s})\cdot\nabla G(\mathbf{a}_{m},\mathbf{r}_{s})\end{pmatrix}^{T}.

Thus, it appears natural to test the orthogonality relation between
u_{\scat}(\mathbf{b}_{m,n},\mathbf{a}_{m})
and
\nabla G(\mathbf{b}_{m,N},\cdot)\cdot\nabla G(\mathbf{a}_{m},\cdot)
. Therefore, we introduce the following test vector: for each
\mathbf{r}\in\Omega
,

\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{r})=\Big(\nabla G(\mathbf{b}_{m,1},\mathbf{r})\cdot\nabla G(\mathbf{a}_{m},\mathbf{r}),\nabla G(\mathbf{b}_{m,2},\mathbf{r})\cdot\nabla G(\mathbf{a}_{m},\mathbf{r}),\ldots,\nabla G(\mathbf{b}_{m,N},\mathbf{r})\cdot\nabla G(\mathbf{a}_{m},\mathbf{r})\Big)

(3)

and the corresponding indicator function

\mathfrak{F}_{\osm}(\mathbf{r},m)=|\mathbf{E}(m)\cdot\overline{\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{r})}|=\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}u_{\scat}(\mathbf{b}_{m,n},\mathbf{a}_{m})\overline{\nabla G(\mathbf{b}_{m,n},\mathbf{r})\cdot\nabla G(\mathbf{a}_{m},\mathbf{r})}\right|.

(4)

Then, the map of
\mathfrak{F}_{\osm}(\mathbf{r},m)
will contain large magnitude peaks at
\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r}_{s}\in D_{s}
; thus, it will be possible to recognize the existence or outline shape of
D_{s}
,
s=1,2,\ldots,S
.

Remark 3.3

(Structure of the factor
\mathcal{E}_{\osm}(\mathbf{r},m)
)
.

Notice that since

\cos(\vartheta_{m}-\phi_{s})=\frac{\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|}\cdot\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{m}\quad\text{and}\quad\cos qx=\sum_{p=0}^{q}a_{p}^{(q)}\cos^{p}x,

where coefficients
a_{p}^{(q)}
satisfy the following relationships

\displaystyle\cos 2x

\displaystyle=2\cos^{2}x-1

\displaystyle\cos 3x

\displaystyle=4\cos^{3}x-3\cos x

\displaystyle\cos 4x

\displaystyle=8\cos^{4}x-8\cos^{2}x+1

\displaystyle\cos 5x

\displaystyle=16\cos^{5}x-20\cos^{3}x+5\cos x

\displaystyle\cos 6x

\displaystyle=32\cos^{6}x-48\cos^{4}x+18\cos^{2}x-1

\displaystyle\cos 7x

\displaystyle=64\cos^{7}x-112\cos^{5}x+56\cos^{3}x-7\cos x

The structure of
\mathcal{E}_{\osm}(\mathbf{r},m)
is as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\osm}(\mathbf{r},m)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2\pi}\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\frac{i^{q}}{q}\sin\frac{2q\pi}{3}a_{p}^{(q)}\left(\frac{\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|}\cdot\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{m}\right)^{q}J_{q}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|)

\displaystyle+\frac{3}{2\pi}\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-i)^{q}}{q+2}\sin\frac{2(q+2)\pi}{3}a_{p}^{(q)}\left(\frac{\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|}\cdot\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{m}\right)^{q}J_{q}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|)

\displaystyle+\frac{3}{2\pi}\sum_{q=1,q\neq 2}^{\infty}\frac{(-i)^{q}}{q-2}\sin\frac{2(q-2)\pi}{3}a_{p}^{(q)}\left(\frac{\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|}\cdot\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{m}\right)^{q}J_{q}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|).

5
Imaging function with multiple sources

Based on the theoretical and numerical simulation results in Sections
3
and
4
, further improvement is essential for a proper identification of small objects. Previous studies
[
12
,
34
,
35
,
36
,
37
,
38
,
11
]
have confirmed that applying multiple sources and/or frequencies guarantees improved imaging performance. Here, we consider the single-frequency OSM with multiple sources, and we introduce the following indicator function with multiple sources:

\mathfrak{F}_{\msm}(\mathbf{r})=\left|\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mathbf{E}(m)\cdot\overline{\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{r})}\right|=\left|\sum_{m=1}^{M}\sum_{n=1}^{N}u_{\scat}(\mathbf{b}_{m,n},\mathbf{a}_{m})\overline{\nabla G(\mathbf{b}_{m,N},\mathbf{r})\cdot\nabla G(\mathbf{a}_{m},\mathbf{r})}\right|.

(13)

To demonstrate the feasibility and improvement of this multisource indicator function, we derive the following result. Prior to the derivation, we emphasize that, in contrast to the receiver setting, the range of the emitters is
0\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolDegree}
to
350\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolDegree}
with a step size of
10\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolDegree}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{m}=(\cos\vartheta_{m},\sin\vartheta_{m})
,
\boldsymbol{\vartheta}=(\cos\vartheta,\sin\vartheta)
, and
\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}=|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|(\cos\phi_{s},\sin\phi_{s})
. Here, if
4k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{b}_{m,n}|\gg 1
and
4k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{a}_{m}|\gg 1
for all
m=1,2,\ldots,M
and
n=1,2,\ldots,N
, then
\mathfrak{F}_{\msm}(\mathbf{r})
can be expressed as follows:

\mathfrak{F}_{\msm}(\mathbf{r})=\left|\frac{MNk^{2}}{4AB}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\alpha_{s}^{2}\pi\left(\frac{\mu_{0}}{\mu_{s}+\mu_{0}}\right)\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{16\pi}\right)J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|)^{2}+J_{2}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|)^{2}+\mathcal{E}_{\msm}(\mathbf{r})\right]\right|,

(14)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\msm}(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2\pi}\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{q}}{q}\sin\frac{2q\pi}{3}J_{q}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{3}{2\pi}\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{q+1}}{q+2}\sin\frac{2(q+2)\pi}{3}J_{q}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|)^{2}+\frac{3}{2\pi}\sum_{q=1,q\neq 2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{q-1}}{q-2}\sin\frac{2(q-2)\pi}{3}J_{q}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|)^{2}.

Proof.

Note that the following Jacobi–Anger expansion holds uniformly (see
[
29
]
for instance):

e^{ik\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{m}\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s})}=J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|)+2\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}i^{p}J_{p}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|)\cos(p\vartheta_{m}-p\phi_{s}).

Thus, based on the structure (
10
), we obtain the following:

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{M}e^{ik\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{m}\cdot(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s})}\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{16\pi}\right)J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|)-\cos(2\vartheta_{m}-2\phi_{s})J_{2}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|)+\mathcal{E}_{\osm}(\mathbf{r},m)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left(J_{0}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|)+2\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}i^{p}J_{p}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|)\cos(p\vartheta_{m}-p\phi_{s})\right)

Lemma 5.1
.

For sufficiently large
Q
, the following inequalities hold

\mathcal{S}_{1}(Q)<\mathcal{S}_{2}(Q).

(18)

where

\mathcal{S}_{1}(Q)=\sum_{q=1,q\neq 2}^{Q}\frac{1}{q-2}+\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\frac{1}{q+2}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{S}_{2}(Q)=\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\frac{2}{q}.

Proof.

Based on the Euler–Maclaurin formula,

\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\frac{1}{q}=\ln Q+\gamma+\frac{1}{2Q}+c_{Q}=\ln Q+\gamma+\frac{1}{2Q}+O\left(\frac{1}{Q^{2}}\right),\quad 0\leq c_{Q}\leq\frac{1}{8Q^{2}},

where
\gamma=0.577215665\ldots
denotes the Euler-Mascheroni constant. Since

\displaystyle\sum_{q=1,q\neq 2}^{Q}\frac{1}{q-2}=\ln(Q-2)+\gamma+\frac{1}{2(Q-2)}+O\left(\frac{1}{Q^{2}}\right)-1

\displaystyle\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\frac{1}{q+2}=\ln(Q+2)+\gamma+\frac{1}{2(Q+2)}+O\left(\frac{1}{Q^{2}}\right)-\frac{7}{12},

we can examine that

\mathcal{S}_{2}(Q)-\mathcal{S}_{1}(Q)=\ln\frac{Q^{2}}{Q^{2}-4}+\frac{1}{Q}-\frac{1}{2(Q^{2}-4)}+\frac{19}{12}+O\left(\frac{1}{Q^{2}}\right)>0.

Hence, the relationship (
18
) holds.
∎

Corollary 5.1
.

Under the same conditions as Theorems
3.1
and
5.1
, the following statements hold: for
m=1,2,\ldots,M
,

1.

if
\mathbf{r}
is far away from
D_{s}
then
|\mathcal{E}_{\osm}(\mathbf{r},m)|
and
|\mathcal{E}_{\msm}(\mathbf{r})|
are negligible,

2.

if
\mathbf{r}
is close to
\mathbf{r}_{s}
then
|\mathcal{E}_{\msm}(\mathbf{r})|\ll|\mathcal{E}_{\osm}(\mathbf{r},m)|
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{E}_{\osm}(\mathbf{r},m)
and
\mathcal{E}_{\msm}(\mathbf{r})
are uniformly convergent, for each
\epsilon>0
, there exists a natural number
Q=Q(\epsilon)
such that

\left|\mathcal{E}_{\osm}(\mathbf{r},m)-\frac{3}{2\pi}\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\left(\frac{i^{q}}{q}\sin\frac{2q\pi}{3}\cos(\vartheta_{m}-\phi_{s})+\frac{(-i)^{q}}{q+2}\sin\frac{2(q+2)\pi}{3}\cos(q\vartheta_{m}-q\phi_{s})\right)J_{q}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|)\right.\\
\left.-\frac{3}{2\pi}\sum_{q=1,q\neq 2}^{Q}\frac{(-i)^{q}}{q-2}\sin\frac{2(q-2)\pi}{3}\cos(q\vartheta_{m}-q\phi_{s})J_{q}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|)\right|<\epsilon

and

\left|\mathcal{E}_{\msm}(\mathbf{r})-\frac{3}{2\pi}\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\frac{(-1)^{q}}{q}\sin\frac{2q\pi}{3}J_{q}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|)^{2}-\frac{3}{2\pi}\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\frac{(-1)^{q+1}}{q+2}\sin\frac{2(q+2)\pi}{3}J_{q}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|)^{2}\right.\\
\left.-\frac{3}{2\pi}\sum_{q=1,q\neq 2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{q-1}}{q-2}\sin\frac{2(q-2)\pi}{3}J_{q}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|)^{2}\right|<\epsilon.

Suppose that
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}
is not close to
\mathbf{r}
such that
k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|\gg 0.25
. Then, since the following asymptotic form holds

J_{q}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|)\approx\sqrt{\frac{2}{k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}}\cos\left(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|-\frac{q\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi}{4}+O\left(\frac{1}{k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}\right)\right)\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}},

applying the Euler-Maclaurin formula and (
18
) yields

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}_{\osm}(\mathbf{r},m)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2\pi}\left|\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\left(\frac{1}{q}\sin\frac{2q\pi}{3}+\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\frac{1}{q+2}\sin\frac{2(q+2)\pi}{3}\right)J_{q}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|)+\sum_{q=1,q\neq 2}^{Q}\frac{J_{q}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|)}{q-2}\sin\frac{2(q-2)\pi}{3}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4\pi}\sqrt{\frac{2}{k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}}\left|\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\frac{1}{q}+\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\frac{1}{q+2}+\sum_{q=1,q\neq 2}^{Q}\frac{1}{q-2}\right|\leq\frac{9\sqrt{3}}{4\pi}\sqrt{\frac{2}{k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}}\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\frac{1}{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{9\sqrt{3}}{4\pi}\sqrt{\frac{2}{k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}}\left(\ln Q+\gamma+\frac{1}{2Q}-c_{Q}\right)\leq\frac{9\sqrt{3}}{4\pi}\sqrt{\frac{2}{k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}}\left(\ln Q+\gamma+\frac{1}{2Q}\right)

and

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}_{\msm}(\mathbf{r})|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2\pi}\left|\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\left(\frac{1}{q}\sin\frac{2q\pi}{3}+\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\frac{1}{q+2}\sin\frac{2(q+2)\pi}{3}\right)J_{q}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|)^{2}+\sum_{q=1,q\neq 2}^{Q}\frac{J_{q}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{s}|)^{2}}{q-2}\sin\frac{2(q-2)\pi}{3}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2\pi k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}\left|\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\frac{1}{q}+\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\frac{1}{q+2}+\sum_{q=1,q\neq 2}^{Q}\frac{1}{q-2}\right|\leq\frac{9\sqrt{3}}{2\pi k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}\left(\ln Q+\gamma+\frac{1}{2Q}\right).

Since

\frac{1}{k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}\ll O(1),

we can say that the values of
\mathcal{E}_{\osm}(\mathbf{r},m)
and
\mathcal{E}_{\msm}(\mathbf{r})
are negligible for all
m=1,2,\ldots,M
, if the search point is far away from the objects.

Now, assume that
\mathbf{r}
is close to
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}
such that
0<k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|<\delta\ll o(1)
. In this case, based on the asymptotic form of the Bessel function

J_{q}(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|)\approx\frac{1}{\Gamma(q+1)}\left(\frac{k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}{2}\right)^{q},

we can examine the following relationships by applying (
18
)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}_{\osm}(\mathbf{r},m)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4\pi}\left|\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\left(\frac{1}{q}+\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\frac{1}{q+2}\right)\frac{1}{\Gamma(q+1)}\left(\frac{k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}{2}\right)^{q}+\sum_{q=1,q\neq 2}^{Q}\frac{1}{(q-2)\Gamma(q+1)}\left(\frac{k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}{2}\right)^{q}\right|

\displaystyle<\frac{9\sqrt{3}}{4\pi}\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\frac{1}{q\Gamma(q+1)}\left(\frac{k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}{2}\right)^{q}:=\sum_{q=1}^{Q}a_{q}

and

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}_{\msm}(\mathbf{r})|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4\pi}\left|\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\left(\frac{1}{q}+\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\frac{1}{q+2}\right)\frac{1}{\Gamma(q+1)^{2}}\left(\frac{k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}{2}\right)^{2q}+\sum_{q=1,q\neq 2}^{Q}\frac{1}{(q-2)\Gamma(q+1)^{2}}\left(\frac{k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}{2}\right)^{2q}\right|

\displaystyle<\frac{9\sqrt{3}}{4\pi}\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\frac{1}{q\Gamma(q+1)^{2}}\left(\frac{k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}{2}\right)^{2q}:=\sum_{q=1}^{Q}b_{q},

where
\Gamma(q)=(q-1)!
denotes the Gamma function. Since

\lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{b_{q}}{a_{q}}=\lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{(k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|)^{q}}{2^{q}\Gamma(q+1)}\ll\lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{\delta^{q}}{2^{q}\Gamma(q+1)}=0.

Hence, we can examine that
|\mathcal{E}_{\msm}(\mathbf{r})|\ll|\mathcal{E}_{\osm}(\mathbf{r},m)|
for all
m=1,2,\ldots,M
.

7
Conclusion

This paper has reported on the application of the OSM with a single source to identify the existence and outline shape of a small object in the TE polarization process from the Fresnel experimental dataset. In this study, based on the asymptotic expansion formula for the scattered field data in the presence of small objects, we designed an indicator function of the OSM, and we derived an accurate relationship between the indicator function and an infinite series of the Bessel function of the first kind.
This paper has discussed both the applicability and limitations of the OSM, and the results of numerical simulation conducted at various frequencies with the 2D Fresnel experimental dataset have been reported to verify the theoretical result.

Then, to improve the imaging performance of the OSM, we applied multiple sources and introduced an imaging function. Through careful analysis, we have demonstrated that the proposed indicator function can be expressed by an accurate relationship between the indicator function and an infinite series of the Bessel function of the first kind, and we have demonstrated that the proposed indicator function improves the imaging performance. Based on the theoretical result, we numerically demonstrated that it is possible to identify small objects uniquely.

In this study, we considered the application of the OSM in TE polarization from the 2D Fresnel experimental dataset. In the future, we plan to extend our work to the 3D Fresnel experimental dataset

, and monostatic and bistatic measurement configurations

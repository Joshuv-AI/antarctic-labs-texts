A B-spline-Heaviside collocation method for solving Fredholm integral equations with piecewise Hölder-continuous right-hand sides Thanks: Submitted to the editors DATE.
Maria Capcelea, Titu Capcelea

Abstract
This work presents a collocation method for solving linear Fredholm integral equations of the second kind defined on a closed contour in the complex plane. The right-hand side of the equation is a piecewise continuous function that may have a finite number of jump discontinuities and is known numerically at discrete points on the contour. The proposed approach employs a combination of B-spline functions and Heaviside step functions to ensure accurate approximation near discontinuity points and smooth behavior elsewhere on the contour. Convergence in the norm of piecewise Hölder spaces is established, together with explicit error estimates. Numerical results illustrate the effectiveness and convergence rate of the method.

Introduction and problem formulation

Fredholm integral equations of the second kind arise in many practical applications, including boundary integral formulations in potential theory, elasticity, electromagnetism, inverse problems, and transport phenomena in heterogeneous media. Such equations frequently involve kernels or right-hand sides that are piecewise continuous, particularly when the underlying physical model exhibits discontinuities in boundary data, coefficients, or source terms.
Fredholm integral equations with piecewise continuous right-hand sides naturally occur in several important contexts:

•

Boundary value problems in potential theory and elasticity, where boundary data or loading conditions exhibit jumps - for instance, at interfaces between materials with different physical properties or along crack edges. These situations lead to integral equations whose right-hand sides are piecewise continuous functions
[
8
]
.

•

Inverse problems or parameter identification in heterogeneous media, where the piecewise-defined coefficients or source terms induce discontinuities in the data of the corresponding integral equations
[
14
]
.

•

Transport or radiative‐transfer models in composite or layered materials, which often reduce to Fredholm equations with piecewise-defined source functions or solutions that are piecewise continuous across interface boundaries
[
12
]
.

These examples motivate the development of numerical methods capable of handling low-regularity data.

Let
\Gamma
be a closed, piecewise smooth contour that forms the boundary of the simply connected domain
\Omega^{+}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
, and let the point
z=0\in\Omega^{+}
. Consider the Riemann function
z=\psi(w)
, which provides a conformal map from the exterior
D^{-}
of the unit circle

\Gamma_{0}:=\{\,w\in{\mathbb{C}}:|w|=1\,\}
onto the exterior of the contour
\Gamma
, denoted by
\Omega^{-}
, such that
\psi(\infty)=\infty
and
\psi^{\prime}(\infty)>0
. The function
\psi(w)
maps the circle
\Gamma_{0}
onto the contour
\Gamma
. Consequently, the points on the contour
\Gamma
can be described using the function
\psi(w)
.

Fix a finite set of points (the jump set)
{\mathcal{D}}=\left\{t^{d}_{1},...,t^{d}_{n_{d}}\right\}\subset\Gamma
. Let
0<\alpha\leq 1
. Denote by
PH_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)
the Banach space of all functions on
\Gamma
that satisfy a Hölder condition with exponent
\alpha
, except possibly at the points of
{\mathcal{D}}
, where the functions are left-continuous and may have jump discontinuities of the first kind.

Let
\mathcal{A}(\Gamma\setminus\mathcal{D})
denote the finite family of open, connected arcs of
\Gamma\setminus\mathcal{D}
between successive points of
\mathcal{D}
.
On each connected smooth arc
I\in\mathcal{A}(\Gamma\setminus\mathcal{D})
without discontinuities, we have
\;f|_{I}\in H_{\alpha}(I)\;
,
and the usual Hölder norm is defined by
\lVert f\rVert_{H_{\alpha}(I)}:=\lVert f\rVert_{\infty,I}+[f]_{\alpha,I},

where
\lVert f\rVert_{\infty,I}=\sup_{t\in I}|f(t)|\qquad\text{and}\qquad[f]_{\alpha,I}:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t,s\in I\\
t\neq s\end{subarray}}\frac{|f(t)-f(s)|}{|t-s|^{\alpha}}

is the Hölder seminorm on
I
. A standard norm on the piecewise Hölder space
PH_{\alpha}(\Gamma,\mathcal{D})
is

\lVert f\rVert_{PH_{\alpha}(\Gamma,\mathcal{D})}:=\max_{I\in\mathcal{A}(\Gamma\setminus\mathcal{D})}\lVert f\rVert_{H_{\alpha}(I)}.

Let us consider the linear Fredholm integral equation of the second kind:

\varphi\left(t\right)-\lambda\int_{\Gamma}{k\left(t,\tau\right)\varphi\left(\tau\right)d\tau}=f\left(t\right),t\in\Gamma,

(1)

which is defined on the contour
\Gamma
described above. The kernel function
k\left(t,\tau\right)
is continuous in both variables, i.e.,
k\in C\left(\Gamma\times\Gamma\right)
. The right-hand side function satisfies
f\in PH_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)
, and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
is a constant.

Equation (
1
) can be rewritten in operator form as
A\varphi\equiv\left(I-\lambda K\right)\varphi=f
, where
K\varphi\left(t\right)=\int_{\Gamma}{k\left(t,\tau\right)\varphi\left(\tau\right)d\tau}
. The operator
K:\ C\left(\Gamma\right)\to C\left(\Gamma\right)
is compact; the same conclusion holds on
PH_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)
, since the compactness argument applies separately on each arc and then extends to the piecewise space.

If the operator norm satisfies
{\left\|\lambda K\right\|}_{PH_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)}<1
(Neumann criterion) or if the homogeneous equation
A\varphi=0
admits only the trivial solution
\varphi\left(t\right)\equiv 0
(Fredholm alternative), then the operator
A=I-\lambda K
, which defines the left-hand side of equation (
1
), is invertible in
PH_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)
and equation (
1
) has a unique solution
{\rm\ }\varphi\in PH_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)
.

Considering that the right-hand side
f
is known numerically at a set of distinct points
\left\{t_{j}\right\}
on the contour
\Gamma
, we aim to develop an efficient method for computing a sequence of approximations
\varphi_{n}
to the solution
\varphi
, which converge to
\varphi
as
n\to\infty
in the norm of
PH_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)
.

When the kernel and the right-hand side are smooth, classical numerical methods such as Nyström, polynomial or spline collocation, and Galerkin methods achieve high convergence rates
[
1
]
,
[
9
]
. However, in many problems, the solution
\varphi
and the right-hand side may be only continuous or Hölder continuous, possibly with finite jump discontinuities along the integration contour. In this low-regularity setting, standard global approximations lose accuracy near discontinuities (may suffer Gibbs-type oscillations), and convergence rates are reduced. For example, for a solution
\varphi\in H_{\alpha}
with Hölder exponent
0<\alpha\leq 1
, spline- or polynomial-based collocation methods of nominal order
m
achieve an error of order
\mathcal{O}\left(h^{\alpha}\right)
(
h
is the mesh size) in uniform discretizations, rather than the full
\mathcal{O}\left(h^{m}\right)

[
1
]
, reflecting the limited smoothness of the solution.

Global or piecewise polynomial approximation is generally ineffective for approximating piecewise continuous functions, except when studying convergence in the norm of Lebesgue spaces
L_{p},1<p<\infty
. In such cases, it has been shown that the sequence of interpolation polynomials converges to the solution
\varphi
, except at a countable set of points
[
13
]
,
[
5
]
.

To address the challenges defined by discontinuities, several strategies have been proposed:

1.

Piecewise approximation spaces: The integration contour is partitioned into segments where the solution and coefficients are continuous or Hölder continuous, and local approximants (e.g., splines) are applied on each continuous segment. The convergence rate on each segment is
\mathcal{O}\left(h^{\alpha}\right)
in Hölder norms when the solution is only Hölder continuous. Discontinuities are sometimes explicitly represented using step functions
[
11
]
.

2.

Enriched bases: The approximation space is augmented with basis functions that reflect the structure of the discontinuities (for example Heaviside step functions or singular basis functions). This allows one to recover higher local convergence rates in the presence of jumps. For example, recent work on collocation with Heaviside-type enrichment show convergence in piecewise Hölder norms
[
4
]
. This approach preserves the local convergence rate
\mathcal{O}\left(h^{\alpha}\right)
on smooth segments and avoids loss of accuracy near jumps.

3.

Corrected quadrature and transformations: The integral equation or quadrature rule is transformed so that the discontinuity or singularity is either removed or ”smoothed” (for example by a one-to-one mapping or singularity subtraction), and then a standard discretisation such as the Nyström method is applied
[
7
]
. This enhances stability and convergence even when the right-hand side or kernel is singular or discontinuous.

4.

Adaptive refinement: The mesh is locally refined near discontinuities to reduce local errors and improve overall accuracy. Even with only Hölder continuous solutions, local refinement allows the method to achieve near-optimal convergence rates of
\mathcal{O}\left(h^{\alpha}\right)
on each continuous segment
[
10
]
.

For practical applications, it is desirable to define analytically a sequence of approximation functions
\varphi_{n}
that converge pointwise to the piecewise continuous function
\varphi
, including near the discontinuity points. Linear spline functions can be employed as an approximation technique, but in this case, the convergence rate can be exceedingly slow. Some numerical results indicate that the oscillatory effect disappears and pointwise convergence of the approximations is attained, even near the discontinuity points, when
\varphi_{n}
is constructed as a linear combination of B-spline functions of order
m\geq 2

[
3
]
. However, in the vicinity of the discontinuity points, the convergence rate of the approximations remains slow. Furthermore, it should be noted that continuous curves in the complex plane often lead to heavily distorted approximations of discontinuous curves.

The proposed approximation method consists of constructing a sequence of piecewise continuous approximations for the function
\varphi
, aiming to exploit the convergence properties of B-spline functions. Specifically, we define the sequence of approximations
\varphi_{n}
as a linear combination of B-spline functions and Heaviside step functions. Previous studies have investigated such approximations on intervals of the real axis
[
3
]
. In this paper, we extend the analysis to the case where the approximations are defined on the contour
\Gamma
in the complex plane.

Let
{\left\{t_{j}\right\}}^{n_{B}}_{j=1}
be the set of distinct points on the contour
\Gamma
at which the values of the function
f\in PH_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)
are known. We assume that the points
t_{j}
are generated according to

t_{j}=\psi\left(w_{j}\right),w_{j}=e^{i\theta_{j}},\theta_{j}=\tfrac{2\pi\left(j-1\right)}{n_{B}},j=1,...,n_{B}.

Thus, the variation of the parameter
\theta
ensures uniform coverage of the interval
\left[0,2\pi\right]
, and the quasi-uniform nodes
t_{j}=\psi\left(e^{i\theta_{j}}\right)
are distributed over the entire contour
\Gamma
.

We also assume that the values
f\left(t^{d}_{r}\right)
of the function
f
are known at the discontinuity points
t^{d}_{r},r=1,...,n_{d}
, on the contour
\Gamma
. Efficient algorithms for identifying the locations of discontinuity points on
\Gamma
for numerically defined functions
f
have been developed in
[
6
]
,
[
2
]
.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces the decomposition of piecewise continuous functions into continuous and discontinuous components using Heaviside step functions and presents the construction of the B-spline–Heaviside approximation, together with the collocation algorithm for solving the Fredholm integral equation. Section
3
provides the operator representation of the approximation method. Section
4
establishes the convergence theorem and derives estimates for the rate of convergence. Section
5
presents a numerical example illustrating the accuracy and stability of the proposed method, thereby confirming the theoretical results. Finally, Section
6
summarizes the main conclusions and outlines possible directions for future research.

\Gamma_{j}:=arc\left[t_{j},t_{j+1}\right]

and

With respect to the discontinuity points
t^{d}_{r},r=1,...,n_{d}

H_{t^{d}_{s}}:\Gamma\to\left\{0,1\right\}
on the contour
\Gamma

H_{t^{d}_{s}}\left(t\right):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}0,\ \ t\in\left(\Gamma_{1}\backslash\left\{t^{B}_{1}\right\}\right)\cup\dots\cup\Gamma_{q-1}\cup arc\left.{[t}^{B}_{q},t^{d}_{s}\right)\\

where
\Gamma_{q}
=arc\left[t^{B}_{q},t^{B}_{q+1}\right]
and

.

The function

is constant on each component of
\Gamma\backslash\left\{t^{d}_{s}\right\}
, has a single jump of +1 at
t^{d}_{s}
, and is right-continuous at
t^{d}_{s}
. In the definition above, the reference point is chosen to be
t^{B}_{1}=\psi\left(e^{i\theta_{0}}\right)
, where
\theta_{0}=0
, and the contour
\Gamma
is oriented starting from this point. Thus, the discontinuity points
t^{d}_{r},r=1,...,n_{d}
, can be arranged in increasing order along the parametrization. In general, the reference point is chosen to be a point on the contour that is not a discontinuity point.

We define the finite jump sizes
\beta_{j}:=f\left(t^{d}_{j}+0\right)-f\left(t^{d}_{j}-0\right),j=1,...,n_{d}
, and the finite linear combination of Heaviside step functions
f_{H}\left(t\right):=\sum^{n_{d}}_{j=1}{\beta_{j}H_{t^{d}_{j}}\left(t\right)}
, and set
f_{C}:=f-f_{H}
.

We now state two lemmas from
[
4
]
: the first concerns the decomposition and uniqueness of a piecewise continuous function, and the second establishes that the continuous component of the decomposition is globally Hölder continuous when the original function is piecewise Hölder.

Lemma 1
.

Any function
f\in PC\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)
can be represented uniquely in the form
f=f_{C}+f_{H}
, where
f_{C}
is continuous on
\Gamma
, and
f_{H}
is a finite linear combination of Heaviside step functions located at the discontinuity points. The coefficients of
f_{H}
are given by
\beta_{j}=f\left(t^{d}_{j}+0\right)-f\left(t^{d}_{j}\right),j=1,...,n_{d}
, which represent the jumps of
f
at each discontinuity point.

Lemma 2
.

If
f\in PH_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)
, then
f=f_{C}+f_{H}
, where
f_{C}\in H_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma\right)
and
f_{H}
is as defined in Lemma 1.

(2)

The basis functions of order

For fixed

Let the B-spline knots on

For any continuous function

, the B-spline interpolation operator

B_{n}:C\left(\Gamma\right)\allowbreak\to S^{m}_{n}\left(\Gamma\right)
is defined by

\left(B_{n}v\right)\left(t\right)=\sum^{n_{B}}_{j=1}{v\left(t_{j}\right)\psi_{j}}\left(t\right),

where the interpolation functions

are determined from the B-spline basis

\left(B_{n}v\right)\left(t_{k}\right)=v\left(t_{k}\right),\ k=1,...,n_{B}
.
Equivalently, if

are the interpolation coefficients, then
\left(B_{n}v\right)\left(t\right)=\sum^{n_{B}}_{j=1}{a_{j}}B_{m,j}\left(t\right)

We present two variants of the collocation method based on the same decomposition of the solution into a smooth part and a jump part. In both variants, the unknown function
\varphi
is approximated as the sum of a smooth component, represented by a B-spline or Lagrange interpolant, and a discontinuous component that reproduces the known jumps using Heaviside step functions. The difference between the two methods lies in the choice of the interpolatory subspace used for approximating the smooth part of the solution.

\varphi

3
Operator representation of the approximation method

We denote by
{{\mathcal{L}}}_{H}\left({\mathcal{D}}\right)
the set of functions of the form
\sum^{n_{d}}_{s=1}{\beta_{s}H_{t^{d}_{s}}\left(t\right)}
,

t^{d}_{1},...,t^{d}_{n_{d}}\in\Gamma

We consider the following finite-dimensional subspaces of functions defined on
\Gamma

•

{{\mathcal{P}}}_{n_{B}}

n_{B}
-dimensional space of global polynomials on
\Gamma
of degree at most

•

n\ \left(n:=n_{B}+n_{d}\right)
- dimensional space of piecewise polynomials of degree at most
n_{B}
from

\sum^{n_{B}}_{j=0}{f\left(t_{j}\right)l_{j}\left(t\right)}+\sum^{n_{d}}_{s=1}{\beta_{s}H_{t^{d}_{s}}\left(t\right)}
, where
l_{j}\left(t\right)
are the Lagrange fundamental polynomials of degree
n_{B}
. Equivalently,
P{{\mathcal{P}}}_{n}:={{\mathcal{P}}}_{n_{B}}\bigoplus span\left\{H_{t^{d}_{1}}\left(t\right),...,H_{t^{d}_{n_{d}}}\left(t\right)\right\}.

•

P{{\mathcal{S}}}_{m}
- the
n\
- dimensional space of piecewise B-spline functions of order
m
, of the form (
3
). Equivalently,

P{{\mathcal{S}}}_{m}:=span\left\{B_{m,1}\left(t\right),...,B_{m,n_{B}}\left(t\right),H_{t^{d}_{1}}\left(t\right),...,H_{t^{d}_{n_{d}}}\left(t\right)\right\}.

The approximate solution of equation (
1

3
), defined by
m

B_{m,k}\left(t\right)
, each branch being a polynomial of degree

{\left\{\alpha_{k}\right\}}^{n_{B}}_{k=1}
and
{\left\{\beta_{s}\right\}}^{n_{d}}_{s=1}
of the piecewise polynomial

are determined from conditions (
4

\left(A\varphi^{H}_{n_{B}}\right)\left(t^{C}_{j}\right)=f\left(t^{C}_{j}\right),j=1,...,n.

(8)

The collocation equation (
8

\left(A^{H}_{n}\varphi^{H}_{n_{B}}\right)\left(t^{C}_{j}\right)=f\left(t^{C}_{j}\right),j=1,...,n,

For the operator representation of the approximation method, we consider the following finite-dimensional operators:

I. The B-spline–Heaviside projection operator
B^{H}_{n}:PH_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)\to P{{\mathcal{S}}}_{m}

\varphi\in PH_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)

3

\varphi^{H}_{n_{B}}\in P{{\mathcal{S}}}_{m}

\varphi\left(t^{C}_{j}\right)=\varphi^{H}_{n_{B}}\left(t^{C}_{j}\right),j=1,...,n

, we have
B^{H}_{n}s=s

[
4
]
.

Let
B_{m,1}\left(t\right),...,B_{m,n_{B}}\left(t\right)
the system (
2

{\left\{t^{B}_{j}\right\}}^{n_{B}}_{j=1}

\varphi\in PH_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)

II. The Lagrange interpolation operator

\varphi\in PH_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)

\Gamma

\left(L_{n}\varphi\right)\left(t_{j}\right)=\varphi\left(t_{j}\right),j=0,1,...,n

III. The (idempotent) interpolant–Heaviside projection operator
L^{H}_{n}:PH_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)\\
\to P{{\mathcal{P}}}_{n}
, which associates to a function
\varphi\in PH_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)
the interpolant–Heaviside polynomial
L^{H}_{n}\varphi
, corresponding to the continuous function
\varphi_{C}
with the exact reconstruction of the jumps of

at the discontinuity points. It is defined as follows:

\left(L^{H}_{n}\varphi\right)\left(t\right)=\left(L_{n_{B}}\varphi_{C}\right)\left(t\right)+\varphi_{H}\left(t\right),

where
\varphi=\varphi_{C}+\varphi_{H},\varphi_{C}\in H_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma\right),\varphi_{H}\in{{\mathcal{L}}}_{H}\left({\mathcal{D}}\right)

. The relation

.

The piecewise Hölder norm controls both sup norm and Hölder seminorm on each arc. To ensure that
{\left\|f-L^{H}_{n}f\right\|}_{PH_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)}\to 0
, the operator
L_{n}
must approximate the continuous part
f_{C}
in that same Hölder norm. If our scheme approximates only in the Lebesgue space

or uniformly without controlling the Hölder seminorm, convergence in the

norm cannot be guaranteed.

Moreover, if the exact jump locations are unknown, or if one attempts to approximate the entire function
f
using a single smooth finite-dimensional family without jump correction, the approximants will exhibit Gibbs phenomena near the discontinuities. In such cases, convergence in the piecewise Hölder norm will fail.

Lemma 5
.

[
4
]
Let
0<\beta<\alpha\leq 1
and
f\in PH_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)
with unique decomposition

, where
f_{C}\in H_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma\right)
and

) of

{\left\{t^{B}_{j}\right\}}^{n_{B}}_{j=1}
, and define the interpolant-Heaviside approximation

. Then, there exists a constant

, depending only on
\Gamma
(through

) and on

{\left\|f-L^{H}_{n}f\right\|}_{PH_{\beta}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)}\leq C{\left\|f_{C}\right\|}_{H_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma\right)}n^{-\left(\alpha-\beta\right)}_{B}.

In particular,
{\left\|f-L^{H}_{n}f\right\|}_{PH_{\beta}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)}\to 0
as

, so the interpolant–Heaviside approximants converge to

in
PH_{\beta}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)
.

Remark 6
.

Multiple choices for the interpolant of the continuous part

are possible within the interpolant–Heaviside construction:

•

Replace

with the periodic cubic spline interpolant on the nodes
{\{t^{B}_{j}\}}_{j=1}^{n_{B}}
.

•

Replace
L_{n_{B}}f_{C}
with the periodic B-spline interpolant of order

(degree

) built on the knot sequence
{\{t^{B}_{j}\}}_{j=1}^{n_{B}+m}
with
t^{B}_{n_{B}+k}=t^{B}_{k},k=1,\dots,m

In both cases, the interpolant-Heaviside approximant is given by
L^{H}_{n}f:=S_{n_{B}}f_{C}+f_{H}
, where

is the unique decomposition into a globally Hölder function

and a finite Heaviside combination

carrying the jumps at
{\mathcal{D}}
, and

denotes the chosen spline interpolation operator.

The interpolant-Heaviside approximation, built with either cubic or order-

to

in
PH_{\beta}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)
for every

The discrete collocation system has the same structure as (
9

\varphi^{H}_{n_{B}}=B^{H}_{n}\varphi
belongs to the finite-dimensional space spanned by the B-spline basis functions and the Heaviside functions corresponding to the jumps in
{\mathcal{D}}
.

Remark 7
.

The inclusion of Heaviside functions in the operators

and

ensures the exact reproduction of the discontinuities of

on the set

. This prevents oscillatory artifacts near the jumps and guarantees convergence in the

norm.

Remark 8
.

The local support of B-splines in

provides improved conditioning of the collocation matrix compared with the global Lagrange basis.

Based on the above, relations (

4
Convergence analysis and rate of convergence

Let us assume that the integral operator
K
is compact and that
A
is invertible. According to Lemma 2 and Lemma 5, the following approximation properties hold for any
g\in PH_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)
:

\left\|g-L^{H}_{n}g\right\|={\mathcal{O}}\left(h^{\alpha}\right),\ \ \ \left\|g-B^{H}_{n}g\right\|={\mathcal{O}}\left(h^{\alpha}\right).

(10)

The generalized trapezoidal rule used in the evaluation of the integrals in
K^{H}_{n}
ensures the estimate

\left\|K-K^{H}_{n}\right\|={\mathcal{O}}\left(\tau\left(h\right)\right),

(11)

where
\tau\left(h\right)
is the quadrature error. For analytic integrands,
\tau\left(h\right)
decreases exponentially with
h
(see
[
15
]
), whereas for functions of limited smoothness,

\tau(h)=\mathcal{O}\bigl(h^{s^{\prime}}\bigr)
for some
s^{\prime}>0.

Theorem 9
.

Suppose that:

1.

K\in C\left(\Gamma\times\Gamma\right)
and thus
K
is compact on
PH_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)
.

2.

A=I-\lambda K
is invertible on
PH_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)
(e.g.,
\left\|\lambda K\right\|<1
or the homogeneous equation has only trivial solution).

3.

The interpolant-Heaviside projection
P_{n}
satisfies
\left\|P_{n}g-g\right\|\to 0
for every
g\in PH_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)
.

4.

The quadrature error for the integrals used in the matrix assembly tends to zero sufficiently fast: the generalized trapezoidal rule error satisfies
{\mathcal{O}}\left(\tau\left(h\right)\right)
with
\tau\left(h\right)\to 0
.

Then, for the discrete equation
A^{H}_{n}\varphi^{H}_{n_{B}}=L^{H}_{n}f
, there exists a unique solution
\varphi^{H}_{n_{B}}
for all sufficiently small
h
, and the following estimate holds:

{\left\|\varphi-\varphi^{H}_{n_{B}}\right\|}_{PH_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)}={\mathcal{O}}\left(h^{\alpha}+\tau\left(h\right)\right).

(12)

Proof.

Let
A^{H}_{n}=I-\lambda K^{H}_{n}
denote the discrete operator defined by the Lagrange-Heaviside or B-spline-Heaviside collocation scheme, and let
P_{n}
be the projection operator associated with the chosen interpolant, mapping
PH_{\alpha}\left(\Gamma,{\mathcal{D}}\right)
into the finite–dimensional trial space
X_{n}
.

Subtracting the discrete equation
A^{H}_{n}\varphi^{H}_{n_{B}}=P_{n}f
from the continuous equation
A\varphi=f
gives
{A\varphi-A^{H}_{n}\varphi}^{H}_{n_{B}}=\left(I-P_{n}\right)f
. Adding and subtracting
A\varphi^{H}_{n_{B}}
on the left-hand side, we obtain

A\left(\varphi-\varphi^{H}_{n_{B}}\right)+\left(A-A^{H}_{n}\right)\varphi^{H}_{n_{B}}=\left(I-P_{n}\right)f.

Since
A
is invertible, taking norms and applying estimates (
10
), (
11
), along with the approximation properties of
P_{n}
and the quadrature error estimate, we obtain

\left\|\varphi-\varphi^{H}_{n_{B}}\right\|\leq\left\|A^{-1}\right\|\left(\left\|A-A^{H}_{n}\right\|\left\|\varphi^{H}_{n_{B}}\right\|+\left\|\left(I-P_{n}\right)f\right\|\right)={\mathcal{O}}\left(h^{\alpha}+\tau\left(h\right)\right).

Thus, the approximation error
\varphi-\varphi^{H}_{n_{B}}
can be decomposed into two measurable components:

•

operator approximation error
A-A^{H}_{n}=K^{H}_{n}-K
, determined by the accuracy of the numerical integration used for
K^{H}_{n}
;

•

projection error
\left(I-P_{n}\right)f
, determined by the approximation order of
L^{H}_{n}
or
B^{H}_{n}
.

This proves the statement.

If the kernel
k\left(t,\tau\right)
and the contour
\Gamma
are analytic, then
\tau\left(h\right)
decays exponentially, i.e.,
\tau\left(h\right)={\mathcal{O}}\left(e^{-{c}/{h}}\right)
, and the dominant term in (
12
) is
{\mathcal{O}}\left(h^{\alpha}\right)
. If the quadrature error is only algebraically small,
\tau\left(h\right)={\mathcal{O}}\left(h^{s^{\prime}}\right)
with
s^{\prime}>0
, the overall convergence order becomes
min\left(\alpha,s^{\prime}\right)
.

5
A numerical example

We give two remarks on stability and conditioning:

•

In practice, one must ensure the discrete collocation matrix is well conditioned. For properly chosen collocation nodes (quasi-uniform nodes plus shifted jump points) and when using B-splines, the interpolation matrices exhibit bounded conditioning properties; however, numerical verification is recommended.

•

A crucial ingredient that guarantees the algebraic approximation order in the piecewise Hölder norm is the use of a Heaviside combination that exactly reproduces the jump sizes at
{\mathcal{D}}
. If jumps are ignored, Gibbs phenomena occur, preventing convergence in the piecewise Hölder norm.

6
Conclusions

The paper presents a numerical method for solving Fredholm integral equations of the second kind with discontinuous right-hand sides defined on closed contours in the complex plane. The unknown function is approximated as a combination of B-spline basis functions and Heaviside step functions, allowing accurate representation of both continuous and discontinuous components.

The convergence of the B-spline–Heaviside collocation method in the norm of piecewise Hölder spaces is established, and explicit estimates for the rate of convergence are derived. The inclusion of Heaviside functions ensures exact reproduction of the discontinuities, preventing Gibbs-type oscillations, while the local support of B-splines improves the conditioning of the collocation system.

Numerical results confirm the theoretical predictions and demonstrate the expected convergence rates. Overall, the proposed approach provides an efficient and reliable tool for solving Fredholm integral equations with discontinuous data. Possible extensions include the application of the method to systems of integral equations, kernels with weak singularities, and adaptive refinement strategies.

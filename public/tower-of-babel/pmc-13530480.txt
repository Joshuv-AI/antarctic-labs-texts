Poster 266. Clinical and Patient-Reported Outcomes After Posterior Distal Tibia Allograft Reconstruction for Glenohumeral Instability: A MultiCenter Study
Authors: Scott M. Feeley, Hannah P. Gibbs, Benjamin W. Hoyt, Jillian Karpyshyn, Conor F. McCarthy, Sarah M. Remedios, Lance E. LeClere, Kelly G. Kilcoyne, Ivan H. Wong, Jonathan F. Dickens
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:Posterior instability with glenoid bone loss (GBL) represents a challenging pathology that is not reliably corrected with labral repair alone. Our aim was to evaluate outcomes after arthroscopic posterior stabilization with distal tibia allograft (pDTA), and to compare outcomes with primary posterior labral repair.Methods:We retrospectively reviewed prospectively collected data for all patients who underwent arthroscopic pDTA at three institutions from 2016-2024. pDTA was performed in the setting of posterior instability with GBL >10% or GBL in the setting of a failed posterior labral repair. We excluded those with follow-up <6 months. We collected demographics, prior procedures, surgical details, and outcome data. We measured GBL using the perfect circle linear method and assessed imaging for graft resorption, union, and development of osteoarthritis. The primary outcome was change in preoperative PROs beyond minimal clinically important difference (MCID) and final PROMIS scores. MCIDs were 169.6, 15%, 1.1, and 3.54 for WOSI, SANE, VAS, and PROMIS scores respectively. We performed a comparison against patients undergoing isolated primary posterior labral repair within the Military Health System that had PROs both preoperatively and at >2 years.Results:We identified 24 patients that underwent pDTA meeting criteria for analysis. Median age was 30.1 years (IQR 22.7-35.7). Preoperative imaging demonstrated mean GBL 17.2 ± 6.2% and glenoid retroversion 10.9 ± 5.0°. Three patients had Samilson-Prieto grade 1 or 2 osteoarthritis preoperatively. Revision procedures accounted for 37.5% (9/24). At average follow-up 2.7 ± 1.7 years, PROs had improved both clinically and statistically for WOSI (1336.3 to 825.5, delta -510.9 ± 737.0, p=0.007), SANE (53.3% to 73.6%, delta +20.2% ± 21.5%, p<0.0001), and VAS (5.6 to 1.9, delta -3.9 ± 4.3, p<0.0001). Mean scores for ASES, PROMIS-PF, and PROMIS-Pain at follow-up were 63.3 ± 31.4, 48.0 ± 9.6, and 55.5 ± 10.8, respectively. Three patients experienced recurrent posterior instability symptoms. At final imaging follow-up at average 1.9 ± 1.4 years, radiographic arthritis in those with available imaging had developed or worsened in 31.8% (7/22), one patient developed a nonunion, and one experienced complete graft resorption. We identified 42 patients that underwent primary posterior labral repair for comparative analysis to the 24 pDTAs. Median age was 29.0 years (IQR 22.1-39.2) and comparable to the pDTA group (p=0.502). Preoperative imaging demonstrated mean GBL 4.7 ± 5.2% and glenoid retroversion 8.5 ± 7.0°. GBL was significantly less than in the pDTA group (p<0.0001) while version was similar (p=0.156). Three patients also had Samilson-Prieto grade 1 or 2 osteoarthritis preoperatively. Revision procedures accounted for 0% (0/42, p<0.0001). At average follow-up 2.9 ± 1.4 years, PROs had improved both clinically and statistically for ASES (62.0 to 73.3, delta 11.3 ± 22.1, p=0.006), SANE (48.3% to 70.4%, delta +22.1% ± 25.7%, p<0.0001), DVPRS (4.8 to 3.86, p=0.037), and PROMIS-PF (44.3 to 49.9, delta 5.6 ± 4.2, p=0.007). PROMIS-Pain did not achieve MCID (58.3 to 56.1, delta -2.2 ± 2.9, p=0.006). At final imaging follow-up at average 2.0 ± 1.0 years, radiographic arthritis in those with available imaging had developed or worsened in no patients. There were no significant differences in final PROMIS-PF (48.0 vs. 49.9, p=0.681), PROMIS-Pain (55.5 vs. 56.1, p=0.862), or delta for SANE score (19.5 vs. 22.1, p=0.740) between pDTA and labral repair cohorts. Postoperative motion was similar in all planes at final follow-up with the exception of internal rotation (61.1° vs. 72.4°, p=0.047).Conclusions:Bone block augmentation for patients with posterior instability and GBL is an effective surgical option for improving PROs and managing instability. While there is a high rate of progression of osteoarthritis and overall outcomes are somewhat guarded, it should be considered for patients with critical posterior GBL and can yield similar outcomes to those appreciated after arthroscopic soft tissue repair procedures.

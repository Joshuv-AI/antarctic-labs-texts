Impact of Educational Interventions on Obesity Awareness and Weight Management Among Urban Women in Salem Municipal Corporation, Tamil Nadu: A Quasi-Experimental Study
Authors: Sumathy Balu, Panneerselvam Periasamy
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction:A study assessed women’s knowledge of obesity and weight loss management in Southern India.Materials and methods:The study used a quasi-experimental design, with two urban areas selected for control and study groups. Biographic data were collected through SIS, and pre and post-tests were conducted using a SSAKQ and an Interventional Module.Results:Results showed high obesity prevalence among women, with BMI (68%, 74%) and high-risk category waist circumference (91%, 93%). The study found a significant difference in knowledge levels between the control and study groups, with the interventional module being effective in increasing knowledge levels.

INTRODUCTION

Women, created by God, play a crucial role in providing care, love, and sacrifice. However, their health is globally affected by non-communicable disease (NCD), obesity, and overweight, with over one billion overweight individuals and 300 million obese individuals worldwide.[1]

Global obesity prevalence in women may reach 21% by 2025, exacerbating various diseases such as diabetes, hypertension, liver disease, orthopedic issues, respiratory problems, fertility disorders, and certain cancers.[2]

In India and Southeast Asia, obesity is increasing due to diet and lifestyle patterns, with women being three times more likely to be overweight in urban areas.[3] Women with waist circumference ≥88 cm and BMI ≥25-30 kg/m2 are considered overweight and obese, affecting 26% and 29% of women aged 20-39, respectively, and putting them at high-risk for reproductive system cancers.[4]

A study in Bhopal, India, found that 37% of women were overweight and 18% were obese.[5] A cross-sectional study found that 7.5% of female students and 350 adults in Mumbai had a lack of knowledge about obesity and its effects.[6] A study in Miraj, Sangli, and Kupwad, Maharashtra, found that 63.46% of school children were aware of obesity prevention measures. There was a significant association between knowledge score and father’s occupation with obesity among parents.[7]

The study aims to assess women’s knowledge of obesity and weight loss management in Salem district, Tamil Nadu, South India. It aims to determine the prevalence of obesity, assess women’s knowledge, compare knowledge levels, and identify differences in knowledge. The research hypotheses suggest significant differences in post-test scores, pre-test scores, and associations between post-test scores and biographic variables in the study group.

METHODOLOGY

The study aimed to evaluate the health of obese women aged 20-39 in Salem Municipal Corporation, Tamil Nadu, South India, using a quasi-experimental design and a quantitative evaluative approach, with a two-stage random sampling using the lottery method.

The study uses biographic data, BMI, and waist circumference assessments, and the SSAKQ to assess obesity and weight loss management, focusing on various aspects.

The interventional module discusses weight regulation, obesity, risk factors, health problems, fat distribution, BMI, WHO classification, behavior modifications, household physical activities, walking exercise, and low-calorie diets. Its validity and reliability were confirmed through professional validation.

RESULTS

The study found that 68% and 74% of obese women fell into the obese category, while 32% and 26% were classified as pre-obese, possibly due to lifestyle practices [Figure 1]. Additionally, the study found that 91% and 93% of obese women were at high risk of having an unhealthy waist circumference, potentially leading to obesity-related health disorders, while 9% and 7% were at moderate risk. Furthermore, the study found that 73% and 80% of women had average knowledge on obesity and weight loss management, with a small difference in average knowledge likely occurring by chance.

DISCUSSION

The study found that 68% and 74% of obese women were in the obese category, with 66% and 86.3% having abdominal obesity, respectively, and a similar prevalence was found in a study conducted in Coimbatore.[7] The study found that 91% and 93% of obese women were in high-risk categories, consistent with a cross-sectional epidemiological descriptive study that revealed an increased prevalence of weight gain.

The Bhardwaj., et al.,[8] study found that the control group had average knowledge (73%, 80%), while a cross-sectional study found an average knowledge of 70.4%. However, adult population knowledge on obesity prevention and complications was less than average.

The study showed that pre-test knowledge was average at 73%, with 25% poor and 2% good, while post-test knowledge was 100% good, like a previous study in Australia assessing obesity knowledge.

A pre-experimental study on obesity and its consequences found a higher mean post-test score (24 ± 2.68) than the pre-test score (15.59 ± 3.16), supporting our research findings.[9]

The present research found no significant association between post-test scores of women’s knowledge and biographic variables, while a cross-sectional survey reveals a 49.1% prevalence of obesity in urban women.

CONCLUSION

The interventional module educated participants on obesity’s causes, effects, management, and prevention, thereby raising awareness among women to maintain their health and prevent obesity-related health disorders.

Intersections of the Ekedahl-Oort and Newton Polygon Stratifications of 𝒜 5 \mathcal{A}_{5}
Elvira Lupoian and Mychelle Parker

Abstract
. The moduli space 𝒜 g \mathcal{A}_{g} of principally polarised abelian varieties of dimension g g , defined over an algebraically closed field of characteristic p > 0 p>0 , is studied through various stratifications. The two most prominent ones are the Newton polygon stratification, based on the isogeny class of the p p -divisible group of an abelian variety, and the Ekedahl-Oort stratification, defined by the isomorphism class of its p p -torsion group scheme. In general, it is not known how the strata of these two intersect. In this paper we study intersections of the Ekedahl-Oort and Newton polygon stratification in dimension at most 5 5 . We give an explicit description of the Ekedahl-Oort stratification of the supersingular locus 𝒮 5 \mathcal{S}_{5} , and describe other p p -rank zero intersections of the two stratifications. For intersections of positive p p -rank, we describe all such intersections in dimensions at most 5 5 . Our proofs use Oort’s minimality of p p -divisible groups, as well as related results of Chai-Oort and Harashita. The new approach used in this paper is an inductive argument, based on understanding the Ekedahl-Oort type of products of abelian varieties and using the known intersections in lower dimensions.

1.
Introduction

Let
p
be prime,
g\geq 1
an integer and consider
\mathcal{A}_{g}\otimes\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}

g
defined over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
, henceforth denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{g}
. The most natural method for studying
\mathcal{A}_{g}
appears to be via the Ekedahl-Oort stratification, first introduced by Oort
[
10
]
, and based on understanding the
p
-torsion group scheme

of an abelian variety
X
in characteristic
p
and the action of Frobenius and Verschiebung on it. These actions endow
X[p]
with the structure of a

(a Barsotti-Tate trucnated level one group scheme), and this is encoded by an
elementary sequence
, an isomorphism invariant of the abelian variety, with each Ekedahl-Oort stratum containing abelian varieties with a specific elementary sequence. A natural question is how this stratification restricts to important subschemes of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
. Perhaps, the most well-studied subscheme of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
is the
supersingular locus

\mathcal{S}_{g}=\{(X,\lambda)\in\mathcal{A}_{g}\ |\ X\ \text{is supersingular }\}
.

The Ekedhal-Oort stratification of
\mathcal{S}_{g}
is elementary to determine for
1\leq g\leq 3
due to the small number of strata. In dimension
4
, this becomes a challenging problem, and it is solved by Ibukiyama, Karemaker, and Yu
[
5
]
, see Section
5.1
for an overview of this. The first result of this paper treats the next case, namely
\mathcal{S}_{5}
. In Theorem
1
we prove that there are exactly
4
Ekedahl-Oort strata that are fully contained in the supersingular locus
\mathcal{S}_{5}
and there are at most
8
other strata intersecting it non-trivially. Another stratification of
\mathcal{A}_{5}
is based on the
a
-number of abelian varieties, see Section
2
for details. This can be recovered from the Ekedahl-Oort stratification, and we specialise this to
\mathcal{S}_{5}
in Theorem
1
.

The main challenges of this case over the previously known stratification of
\mathcal{S}_{4}
are the large number of possible intersections and the limitations of current theoretical results in treating all possibilities. In dimension
4
, there are a total of
16
Ekedahl-Oort strata which could intersect
\mathcal{S}_{4}
,
4
of which are known to be fully contained in it by a classical result of Chai-Oort,
2
of which are excluded by minimality of
p
-divisible groups, which leaves
10
possible intersections to consider. A key tool used to understand the remaining intersections is the
a
-number stratification of
\mathcal{S}_{4}
and results of Harashita
[
2
]

a
-number stratum can intersect at most
2
strata. In particular, this cannot be used to decide any intersections in our problem. In dimension
5
, there are
32
Ekedahl-Oort strata that could possibly intersect
\mathcal{S}_{5}
, of which we rule out
4
by minimality of
p
-divisible groups and note that
4
are fully contained in
\mathcal{S}_{5}
, by the previously mentioned result of Chai-Oort. This leaves
26
possible intersections, and we treat all but
3
, by a variety of methods described in Section
4
. The key new development which allowed us to decide some intersections is an understanding of elementary sequences attached to direct sums of
BT_{1}
s. More specifically, given two principally abelian varieties
X
and
Y

X[p]\oplus Y[p]
is canonically a
BT_{1}
, and in Section
4.2
we describe how to determine the corresponding elementary sequence from the elementary sequences of

and
Y

5
are non-empty from non-emptiness of lower dimensional intersections.

Supersingular abelian varieties have the same Newton polygon, the constant line segment of slope
\frac{1}{2}
. This allows us to view the supersingular locus as a
Newton polygon stratum
. The Newton polygon stratification of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
is based on the isogeny class of the
p
-divisible group
X[p^{\infty}]
of an abelian variety
X
. Given the close apparent relation between the quantities defining the Ekedahl-Oort and Newton polygon stratifications, namely
p
-torsion schemes (up to isomorphism) and
p
-divisible groups (up to isogeny), it is natural to ask if the strata of the two intersect.

The
p
-rank of an abelian variety
X_{/K}
over a field of characteristic
p
is a unique integer

such that
|X[p](\bar{K})|=p^{f(X)}
. and a
p
-rank stratum
of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
is a subset containing all abelian varieties of
p
-rank at most
f
for some
0\leq f\leq g
. For instance, supersingular abelian varieties have
p
-rank zero, and thus
\mathcal{S}_{g}
is fully contained in the
p
-rank
0
stratum. The
p
-rank stratification is refined by both the Ekedhal-Oort stratification and the Newton polygon stratification (c.f Propositions
8
and
12
) and thus it provides a natural stepping stone for studying intersections of the strata of the two. More precisely, we study intersections of Ekedahl-Oort and Newton polygon strata of a fixed
p
-rank. The previously referenced work of Ibukiyama, Karemaker, and Yu studied intersections in dimension
4
and
p
-rank
0
. In Theorem
2
we understand intersections in
p
-rank
0
and dimension
5
.

In Theorems

and
4
we address, for the first time, the question of positive
p
-rank intersections. In general these can be difficult to decipher, as we no longer have access to Harashita’s powerful first slope result (see Proposition

Newton Polygon stratification

Let

When

X[p^{\infty}]\sim_{k}\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}\left(G_{(m_{i},n_{i})}\oplus G_{(n_{i},m_{i})}\right)\oplus G_{(1,1)}^{\oplus s}\oplus\left(G_{(1,0)}\oplus G_{(0,1)}\right)^{\oplus f}

and we call this decomposition the

of

Associated to this decomposition is the

of

defined as follows. Write the above decomposition as
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}G_{(m_{i},n_{i})}
, and set

is the line segment which starts at
(0,0)
, ends at
(\sum_{i=1}^{l}(m_{i}+n_{i}),\sum_{i=1}^{l}n_{i})

and has
(\sum_{i=1}^{j}(m_{i}+n_{i}),\sum_{i=1}^{j}n_{i})
as its break points for

. We call
\sum_{i=1}^{l}m_{i}
the dimension of
\NP(G)
and
\sum_{i=1}^{l}(m_{i}+n_{i})

For any abelian variety

\NP(X)=\NP(X[p^{\infty}])

is supersingular if its isogenous to a product of
g
supersingular abelian varieties. An equivalent definition of supersingularity is that the
p
-divisible group is isogenous to
G_{1,1}^{\text{dim}X}
. Thus, a supersingular abelian variety of dimension

necessarily has Newton polygon
\sigma_{g}=g[1,1]
, and hence the supersingular locus
\mathcal{S}_{g}
is in fact the Newton polygon stratum
\mathcal{N}(\sigma_{g})

2.3.
Ekedahl-Oort Stratification

Definition 9
.

A finite commutative group scheme
G_{/k}
is a
\text{BT}_{1}
(a Barsotti-Tate truncated level
1
group scheme) if

\text{Im}(V:G^{(p)}\rightarrow G)=\text{Ker}(F:G\rightarrow G^{(p)})
and
\text{Im}(F:G\rightarrow G^{(p)})=\text{Ker}(V:G^{(p)}\rightarrow G)

where
F
and
V
are the Frobenius and Verschiebung morphisms. Moreover, a
\text{BT}_{1}
is symmetric if it is isomorphic over
k
to its Cartier dual.

Definition 10
.

An elementary sequence of length
g
is a function
\varphi:\{0,\ldots,g\}\longrightarrow\{0,\ldots,g\}
, satisfying:

1.

\varphi\left(0\right)=0
;

2.

\varphi\left(i\right)\leq\varphi\left(i+1\right)\leq\varphi\left(i\right)+1
for all
0\leq i<g
.

We often write
\varphi
as
(\varphi(1),\ldots,\varphi(g))
. The
final sequence
stretched from
\varphi
is the function
\psi:\{0,\ldots,2g\}\rightarrow\{0,\ldots,2g\}
defined by
\psi(2g-i)=g-i+\varphi(i)
and
\psi(i)=\varphi(i)
for all
0\leq i\leq g
.

Let
G_{/k}
be a symmetric
\text{BT}_{1}
. For any
k
-subgroup
H\subseteq G
, and any finite word
w
in
V
and
F^{-1}
, we define
w\cdot H
inductively by:

V\cdot H=VH^{(p)}
and
F^{-1}\cdot H=F^{-1}(H^{(p)}\cap FG)
.

For any such
G
, there exists a unique elementary sequence
\varphi
of length
g
, such that for any finite word
w
:

\psi(\text{length}(w\cdot G))=\text{length}(Vw\cdot G)

where
\psi
is the final sequence stretched from
\varphi
, and there exists a filtration over
k

0=G_{0}\subseteq G_{1}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq G_{2g}=G

with
\text{length}(G_{i})=i
,
V\cdot G_{i}=G_{\psi(i)}
and
F^{-1}\cdot G_{i}=G_{g+i-\psi(i)}
for all
0\leq i\leq 2g
. This gives a canonical map
ES
between the set of symmetric
\text{BT}_{1}
s of length
2g
over
k
, up to
k
-isomorphism and the set of elementary sequences of length
g
.
In our setting, that is, over algebraically closed fields,
ES
is a bijection (see
[
10
]
).

For any
(X,\mu)\in\mathcal{A}_{g}
,
X[p]
is a symmetric
\text{BT}_{1}
and we set
\text{ES}(X)=\text{ES}(X[p])
.

Definition 11
.

Let
\varphi
be an elementary sequence of length
g
. The Ekedahl-Oort stratum in
\mathcal{A}_{g}
defined by
\varphi
is

S_{\varphi}=\{\left(X,\lambda\right)\in\mathcal{A}_{g}\ :\text{ES}(X)=\varphi\}
.

The Ekedahl-Oort strata form a good stratification of

(see
[
10
, Theorem 1.3]
) and we recall some key properties.

Proposition 12
.

Let
g\geq 1
and
\varphi
an elementary sequence of length
g

1.

\mathcal{S}_{\varphi}
is non-empty, quasi-affine and its dimension is
\sum_{i=1}^{g}\varphi\left(i\right)

2.

is locally closed.

3.

The

-rank of a stratum

The

Proof.

All of the above facts are prove in
[

4.2.
Inductive Arguments

For two
p
-divisible groups
H
and

, with corresponding Newton polygons
\xi
and
\xi^{\prime}
, the direct sum
H\oplus H^{\prime}
is canonically a
p
-divisible group, and its Newton polygon
\xi\oplus\xi^{\prime}
is obtained in the canonical way. Similarly, for any two symmetric
BT_{1}
s,
G
and
G^{\prime}
, the direct sum
G\bigoplus G^{\prime}
is canonically a symmetric
BT_{1}
. If
\varphi
and
\varphi^{\prime}
are the corresponding elementary sequences, we write
\varphi\oplus\varphi^{\prime}
for the elementary sequence of
G\oplus G^{\prime}
. We make the following observation.

Proposition 20
.

Let
\varphi
and
\varphi^{\prime}
be two elementary sequences of length
g
and
g^{\prime}
, and
\mathcal{N}(\xi)\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{g}
,
\mathcal{N}(\xi^{\prime})\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{g^{\prime}}
two Newton strata such that
S_{\varphi}\cap\mathcal{N}(\xi)\neq\emptyset
and
S_{\varphi^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{N}(\xi^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
. Then
S_{\varphi\oplus\varphi^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{N}(\xi\oplus\xi^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that
S_{\varphi}\times S_{\varphi^{\prime}}\subseteq S_{\varphi\oplus\varphi^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{N}(\xi)\times\mathcal{N}(\xi^{\prime})\subseteq\mathcal{N}(\xi\oplus\xi^{\prime})
.
∎

It is not, a priori, clear how one determines
\varphi\oplus\varphi^{\prime}
from
\varphi
and
\varphi^{\prime}
. Due to several results from
[
4
, Section 4]
, this is in fact a straightforward calculation for explicit examples. We give a short overview of this here. We begin with a short review of an equivalent definition of elementary and final sequences as final types.

Definition 21
.

A final type of length
d
is a pair
(B,\delta)
, where:

•

B=\{b_{1}<\ldots<b_{d}\}
is totally ordered finite set;

•

\delta:B\rightarrow\{0,1\}
is a map.

A final type
(B,\delta)
is symmetric if
d=2g
for some
g\geq 1
and it satisfies
\delta(b_{i})+\delta(b_{2g+1-i})=1
for
1\leq i\leq g
.

For any final sequence
\psi
we define a final type by setting
\delta(b_{i})=1-\psi(i)+\psi(i-1)
, and note that this in fact defines a bijection between the set of (symmetric) final sequences and the set of (symmetric) final types. Let
\psi
be a a final sequence of length
d
and
(B,\delta)
its associated final type, with
B=\{b_{1}<\ldots<b_{d}\}
. We define a map
\pi_{\delta}:B\rightarrow B
by

\pi_{\delta}\left(b_{i}\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}b_{\psi(i)}&\mbox{if }\delta(b_{i})=0;\\
b_{\psi(d)+i-\psi(i)}&\mbox{if }\delta(b_{i})=1.\\
\end{array}\right.

Definition 22
.

Let
(B,\delta)
be a final type,
C\subseteq B
a subset and
\epsilon=\delta|_{C}
. Then
(C,\epsilon)
is a final subtype of
(B,\delta)
if
\pi_{\delta}(C)=C
.

Definition 23
.

A final type
(B,\delta)
is called indecomposable if it has no proper final subtype.

We write
D
for the ordered subset of

consisting of elements

and with

for all

, for some

. For an indecomposable final type

b\mapsto\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\delta(\pi_{\delta}^{-l}(b))2^{-l}
.

The following two results allow us to determine

for any two elementary sequences

,
\varphi^{\prime}

.

(Harashita
[

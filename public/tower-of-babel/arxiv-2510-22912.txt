Machine-Learning-Guided Insights into Solid-Electrolyte Interphase Conductivity: Are Amorphous Lithium Fluorophosphates the Key?
Peichen Zhong, Kristin A. Persson

Abstract
Despite decades of study, the identity of the dominant Li + \text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} -conducting phase within the inorganic SEI of Li-ion batteries remains unresolved. While the mosaic model describes LiF/ Li 2 ​ O \text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O} / Li 2 ​ CO 3 \text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} nanocrystallites within a disordered matrix, these crystalline phases inherently offer limited ionic conductivity. Growing evidence suggests that interfaces, grain boundaries, and amorphous phases may instead host the primary fast-ion pathways. Motivated by mixed-anion electrolyte decomposition products, we combine diffusion-based generative structure prediction with machine-learning interatomic potentials (MLIPs) to interrogate lithium difluorophosphate ( LiPO 2 ​ F 2 \text{LiPO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{F}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} ), a key decomposition product of phosphorus- and fluorine-containing electrolytes. We identify a stable crystalline polymorph and, through MLIP-accelerated molecular dynamics, show that the amorphous counterpart is more conductive, with projected room-temperature σ \sigma ≈ \approx 0.18 mS cm -1 and E a E_{\mathrm{a}} ≈ \approx 0.40 eV. This enhancement is attributed to structural disorder, which flattens the Li site-energy landscape, and to a low formation energy for Li-interstitial defects, which supplies additional mobile carriers. We present mixed-anion, amorphous Li–P–O–F phases as promising candidates for the Li + \text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} -conducting medium of the inorganic SEI, offering a path forward for engineering improved battery interfaces.



Functional surface passivation, such as the solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) in Li-ion batteries and protective oxides on stainless steels, forms spontaneously yet critically governs performance through its complex chemistry and defect kinetics. For Li-ion anodes, the inner SEI is often described as a mosaic of crystalline inorganic phases (
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
,
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}
,
LiF
) embedded in a disordered, organic/inorganic matrix
Peled and Menkin 2017
;
Zhang et al. 2017
;
Oyakhire et al. 2022
. However, these well-characterized crystalline materials are fundamentally poor ion conductors, exhibiting ionic conductivities that are orders of magnitude too low to sustain the Li-ion flux required for high-rate battery operation
He et al. 2020
;
Guo and Gallant 2020
;
Liu et al. 2025
. Specifically, their high formation energies for charge-carrying defects, such as Li interstitials
Yildirim et al. 2015
;
Ma et al. 2022
;
Christensen and Newman 2004
, preclude significant extrinsic conductivity
Shi et al. 2013
.
Together, these facts indicate that the dominant Li-ion transport pathways likely reside in other, as yet uncharacterized, components of the SEI.

Interfaces and grain boundaries have been proposed to provide markedly faster ionic diffusion
Ma et al. 2022
. Several modeling and simulation studies have shown the possibility of enhanced conduction at atomically sharp crystal grain interfaces
Pan et al. 2016
;
Hu et al. 2023
, such as LiF/
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
. However, the evidence is inconclusive.

Peled et al. 1997
proposed a circuit model for the mosaic SEI formed by a LiI-PeO-
\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
composite that included grain boundaries and concluded from experimental impedance data that the ionic resistance of these boundaries was significantly larger than that of their bulk counterparts.
Experimentally, the evidence for the importance of grain boundaries in carrier transport is indirect.
For example, attempts to engineer the SEI to enable rapid conduction pathways using electrolytes made up of multiple salts have led to more boundaries between polymer and inorganic compounds and improved conduction
Wang et al. 2023a
.

Interestingly, several experimental works have demonstrated that using
\text{LiPO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{F}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
as an electrolyte additive consistently leads to substantial enhancements in battery performance and stability
Chen et al. 2018
;
Li et al. 2022
;
Tan et al. 2022
.
Furthermore, state-of-the-art cryogenic transmission electron microscopy (cryo-TEM) revealed that while regular concentration electrolytes form a mosaic SEI with nanocrystalline domains of LiF and
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}
embedded within a disordered matrix, high-concentration electrolytes form a homogeneous amorphous inorganic matrix
Han et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2023
.
Importantly, recent cryogenic X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (cryo-XPS) suggests that lithium fluorophosphates (LiPO
x
F
y
), decomposition products of the
\text{LiPF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
electrolytes, constitute the primary inorganic component in the native SEI, a constituent that readily decomposes to LiF and releases
\text{POF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
under standard XPS pre-sample treatments
Nguyen et al. 2025
. Concurrently, cryo-XPS has characterized SO
x
F
y
components as decomposition products of the LiFSI salt
Shuchi et al. 2025
.
In addition, the concept of high single-ion conductivity in amorphous mixed-anion (e.g., Li oxychlorides
Zhang et al. 2023
) or polyanion (e.g., Li-P-S
Hayashi et al. 2001
) systems is well-established in solid electrolytes.
Hence, several independent sources suggest that amorphous, mixed-anion lithium fluorophosphates, whether a minority grain boundary component or a majority phase, warrant a deeper investigation as candidates for the main ionic transport medium of the SEI.

In this work, we focus on the chemical composition
\text{LiPO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{F}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, which has been established as a primary mixed anion electrolyte decomposition product in Li-ion batteries
Campion et al. 2005
;
Jayawardana et al. 2021
;
Parimalam et al. 2017
;
Spotte-Smith et al. 2023
and hence presents a possible SEI component.

We combine diffusion-based generative models with machine-learning interatomic potential (MLIP) simulations to provide the first direct, atomistically resolved evidence that amorphous
\text{LiPO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{F}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is a fast ion conductor with low interstitial Li
+
defect formation energies and rapid Li
+
diffusion.
To enable a comparison between amorphous and crystalline states, we therefore predict the
\text{LiPO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{F}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
ground-state crystal structure and show that the amorphous phase exhibits low amorphorization energy, favorable Li
+
interstitial defect formation, and ionic conductivity orders of magnitude higher than the crystalline polymorph.
These findings establish amorphous oxyfluorophosphates as possible single-ion conducting channels within the SEI, providing a fundamental explanation for the high performance of P- and F-containing batteries and offering a guiding principle for the molecular engineering of optimal battery interfaces.

Generation of Structures:

A crucial step in evaluating the role of
\text{LiPO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{F}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
as a primary SEI component is to identify a stable, low-energy crystalline structure in the condensed phase to serve as a reference for comparison with amorphous counterparts. Starting from the molecular representation in Figure
1
a, the problem is conceptualized as a crystal structure prediction (CSP) task
Zhou et al. 2025
;
Aykol et al. 2024
. The computational molecular CSP is well-established, including structure searching, energy ranking with surrogate models, and final validation with quantum chemical calculations
Gharakhanyan et al. 2025
.
However, such methods are designed to predict the packing of discrete molecules and are ill-suited for inorganic materials.
In the inorganic condensed phase, atoms can point- or edge-share and form interconnected networks rather than a simple molecular stacking.

To address this challenge, we performed CSP using a deep generative model, Crystal-Host Guided Generation (CHGGen)
Zhong et al. 2025
. CHGGen is a diffusion model, a probability-based ML technique that builds a crystal structure by starting with a random arrangement of atoms and progressively refining it into a chemically and physically stable configuration using score-based denoising
Song et al. 2020
;
Xie et al. 2021
;
Zeni et al. 2025
;
Hsu et al. 2024
.
The key feature of CHGGen lies in its unique two-stage strategy (Figure
1
d).
The first unconditional generation process yields a prior structure with physically plausible features, such as local bonding environments. Given this prior, CHGGen temporarily removes the
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
ions and refines the remaining
\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{F}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{\hskip 0.90417pt--\hskip 0.90417pt}}
polyanion framework into a symmetrized structure with a higher space group.
Once the framework is established, the
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
ions are reinserted into their most probable positions via a guided inpainting process
Lugmayr et al. 2022
.
This two-step process allows CHGGen to efficiently navigate the vast number of possible atomic arrangements and pinpoint highly-ordered
\text{LiPO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{F}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
crystal structures. For complete methodological details, we refer readers to Ref.
Zhong et al. 2025
and the Supporting Information (SI).

After generative sampling, CHGGen employs the pretrained CHGNet MLIP to rapidly explore the potential energy landscape of the generated structures
Deng et al. 2023
. All candidates were relaxed and screened for thermodynamic stability by computing their decomposition energies (
E_{d}
) relative to the Materials Project GGA/GGA+
U
phase diagram
Wang et al. 2021
. Low-energy structures (
E_{d}<0.03
eV/atom) were further refined with r
2
SCAN DFT calculations, yielding updated
E_{d}
values with respect to the r
2
SCAN phase diagram in the Li–P–O–F chemical space
Furness et al. 2020
;
Horton et al. 2025
. Figure
1
e presents the CHGNet (blue bar) and r
2
SCAN-DFT (orange bar) calculated decomposition energies for the most stable polymorphs in each distinct space group.
The CHGNet potential identified six thermodynamically stable structures (
E_{d}\leq 0
) and correctly predicted their relative energy ranking compared to DFT. The systematic underestimation of the absolute
E_{d}
is a known effect of potential softening in universal MLIPs that favors smoother energy landscapes
Deng et al. 2025
.

Following r
2
SCAN-DFT calculations, we identified a candidate ground state as a C2/c polymorph with a DFT-calculated
E_{d}=-0.017
eV/atom (Figure
2
a).
Structurally, this configuration is built from
\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{F}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
tetrahedra that are interconnected by corner-sharing
\text{LiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}
tetrahedra. This arrangement preserves the core
\text{PO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{F}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
motif observed in the molecular precursor, establishing a clear structural link between the gas-phase species and the condensed inorganic solid. The identified, condensed crystal structure also demonstrates a similar structural motif as the microporous structure of
\text{LiPO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{F}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
reported in Ref.
Han et al. 2019
.

Having identified a plausible candidate for the ground-state structure, the amorphous structures were generated via well-benchmarked melt-quench molecular dynamics (MD)
Aykol et al. 2018
;
Sivonxay and Persson 2022
using a supercell
>10
Å in each direction. Modeling the structural disorder of amorphous materials requires simulations of large systems over long timescales, a task for which MLIPs are ideally suited due to their near-ab-initio accuracy at a fraction of the computational cost
Qi et al. 2021
;
Zhong et al. 2024
.
We fine-tuned the pretrained CHGNet model on a custom dataset of energies, forces, and stresses from DFT calculations, creating a specialized potential accurate for the Li–P–O–F chemical space (see SI)
Vandermause et al. 2022
;
Xie et al. 2023
. Using this fine-tuned CHGNet, the ground-state crystal was heated to 1000 K to induce a molten (or liquid) state, then quenched and equilibrated at target temperatures ranging from 300 K to 700 K. This approach ensures that the resulting amorphous structure is a reasonable, low-energy configuration rather than an arbitrary arrangement of atoms. The amorphous state was confirmed by comparing the radial distribution functions (RDFs) of the crystalline and amorphous phases (Figure
2
b,c). While short-range Li–anion (O/F) coordination is mostly preserved, the loss of long-range Li–P correlation beyond 4 Å is characteristic of an amorphous structure.

Intrinsic Li-ion Diffusivity:

Following equilibration, production MD simulations were performed to analyze the Li
+
diffusion dynamics.
Li diffusivity at each temperature was calculated from the mean-squared displacement (MSD) of atomic trajectories, and the activation energy (
E_{a}
) for diffusion was extracted by fitting the results to the Arrhenius equation (Figure
2
d)
He et al. 2018
.
Because statistically significant Li-ion hopping events were rare at room temperature, the diffusivity value for the amorphous phase at 300 K (unfilled dot) was determined by extrapolation from the high-temperature data.

Crystalline
\text{LiPO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{F}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
exhibits a steep Arrhenius slope (red dashed line in Figure
2
d), corresponding to a high activation energy for Li diffusion (
E_{a}=1.13\pm 0.21
eV).
This substantial energy barrier effectively prevents interstitial Li migration at lower temperatures; indeed, no statistically significant hopping events were observed in our MD simulations until the structure exhibits pronounced thermal disorder above 800 K.
In contrast, amorphous
\text{LiPO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{F}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
demonstrates markedly different transport behavior, characterized by a significantly lower activation energy (
E_{a}=0.40\pm 0.01
eV). This results in a projected room-temperature (
T=300
K) calculated ionic conductivity of
\sigma^{\text{calc}}_{\text{Li}}\approx 0.18
mS cm
-1
.

To understand the atomistic origin of the enhanced conductivity in amorphous
\text{LiPO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{F}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, we analyzed the density of atomic states (DOAS)
Wang et al. 2023b
, which maps the distribution of site energies experienced by Li ions.
As shown in Figure
3
a, the Li site energy landscape in the crystalline phase (blue histogram) is characterized by a narrow distribution, indicating that Li ions occupy well-defined, deep potential wells.
High ionic conductivity requires a broadened energy landscape where the energy cost for an ion to move between adjacent sites is minimal and can be overcome by thermal energy
Zeng et al. 2022
.
The DOAS analysis of amorphous
\text{LiPO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{F}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(orange histogram) clearly shows that amorphization significantly broadens this landscape, creating a continuous network of energetically accessible sites.
This flattened energy profile rationalizes the dramatically lower activation energy and higher Li diffusivity observed in the amorphous phase.

Li-ion Defect Formation:

Beyond intrinsic diffusion, extrinsic mechanisms (e.g., the formation of charge-carrying defects) can enhance ionic conductivity in the SEI. Near the Li-metal anode, this is dominated by the creation of Li interstitials through a “Li-stuffing” process, where a low defect formation energy enables the host material to accommodate excess Li
Zhang et al. 2020
;
Xiao et al. 2021
;
Chen et al. 2024
. The distinction between defect formation and ion migration is critical. For instance,
Shi et al. 2013
revealed that the migration barrier for an interstitial Li is only 0.24 eV in crystalline
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
, but its formation energy is a prohibitive 0.3–0.8 eV in the low-voltage range. In crystalline LiF, the most common defects, vacancies of F and Li, exhibit even higher formation energies in the range of 0.76 to 1.3 eV
Yildirim et al. 2015
. As a result of such high formation energies, the equilibrium defect concentrations remain very low

to

cm
-3
at 0 V

Li/Li
+

\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
, as well as for our amorphous and predicted crystalline
\text{LiPO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{F}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

Discussion
–
A persistent challenge in designing SEI functionality lies in identifying the enabling components within the mosaic surface layer, first suggested by
Peled and Menkin 2017
.
For example, the robustly insulating nature of LiF provides excellent electronic passivation, yet is unlikely to enable high-performance single-ion conductivity.
Although defect-mediated mechanisms can improve Li diffusion
De Angelis et al. 2025
, the high defect energy (0.76 eV for Li
+
vacancies and 1.73 eV for Li
+
interstitials
Yildirim et al. 2015
;
De Angelis et al. 2025
) precludes a major contribution from bulk LiF, as evidenced by the inferior rate capability of SEIs grown entirely from LiF
He et al. 2020
. Similarly, recent work on fabricated single-component ‘SEI’-like
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}
films shows that such films exhibit an ionic conductivity of
{\sim}10^{-9}
S cm
-1
with an activation energy of
{\sim}0.47
eV

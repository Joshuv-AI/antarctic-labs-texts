Yunhong Lyu, Bouchra R. Nasri, Bruno N. Rémillard

Abstract
. In this article, we study sequential change-point methods for discretely observed generalized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes with periodic drift. Two detection methods are proposed, and their respective performance is studied through numerical experiments for several choices of parameters.

1.
Introduction

The main focus of sequential change-point detection is the identification of shifts in distribution of data collected sequentially over time, where one of the ear;lier reference is
Page, 1954
. In this work, we are interested in Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (O-U) and its recent extensions called generalized O-U (GOU) processes
(
Nkurunziza and Fu, 2019
,
Lyu and Nkurunziza, 2023
)
. The O-U process is known for its mean-reverting property, meaning that its long-term expectation is constant. This is no longer true for the GOU process, whose main drift term can be periodic. In fact, we are focusing in detecting change-points in the parameters of a GOU process in a sequential way, by looking at the process observed at discrete times. Unlike
Berkes et al., 2004
, who considered GARCH processes, we propose two sequential monitoring schemes to detect parameter changes in discretely observed GOU processes: one based on residuals and one based on estimators. In addition, we also establish the asymptotic results of the detectors under both the null and alternative hypotheses. Note that in the particular case of a GARCH(0,0), the methodology proposed by
Berkes et al., 2004
is based on cumulative sums of residuals. Motivated by
Berkes et al., 2004
, we will consider sequential based on a statistic
\Gamma(N,K)
of observations
X_{1},\ldots,X_{N+K}
, together with a boundary function
g(N,K)
. The decision rule is to stop and reject the null hypothesis
H_{0}
of no change-point at

\tau_{\Gamma}(N)=\begin{cases}\inf\{K\geq 1:\Gamma(N,K)\geq g(N,K)\};\\
\infty\quad\mbox{if}~\Gamma(N,K)<g(N,K),\text{ for all }K\geq 1.\end{cases}

Note that if there is an instantaneous change immediately after the first observation of the new data, this leads to the fact that
K_{*}=1
. We choose the statistics and boundaries so that

(1.1)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{H_{0}}\left(\tau_{\Gamma}(N)<\infty\right)=\alpha,

where
0<\alpha<1
is the prescribed type-I error. We will also choose the boundary so that under the alternative hypothesis
H_{1}
of a change-point,

(1.2)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{H_{1}}\left(\tau_{\Gamma}(N)<\infty\right)=1.

Condition (
1.1
) ensures that the probability of a false alarm is asymptotically
\alpha
, while condition
(
1.2
) means that under
H_{1}
, a change-point is detected with probability approaching
1
.

This paper is structured as follows: the GOU model and some preliminary results for the estimation of its parameters are presented in
Section

2
, while the discretized process is defined in
Section

3
. The limiting behaviour of the estimated drift parameters is presented together with a non-parametric goodness-of-fit test in Section
3.3
. In
Section

4
, we propose two processes to detect the sequential change-point in the GOU process. We then prove the asymptotic property of the two detectors under both the null and alternative hypotheses. Numerical experiments for GOU processes with drift

\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}\sqrt{2}\cos{(2\pi t)}-ax
are considered in
Section

5
to assess the performance of the two statistics. All simulations were performed using the CRAN package GenOU
(
Lyu et al., 2025
)
.

2.
Parameter estimation

In what follows, we adopt the following notations. Consider a probability space
\left(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\rm{P}\right)
with
\mathscr{F}
as a
\sigma-
field on the sample space
\Omega
, and
\rm{P}
as a probability measure. Furthermore, let
L^{p}
represent the space of measurable functions that are
p
-integrable, where
p\geq 1
. For mathematical convenience, we assume that
\mathscr{F}
is complete. In addition,
O_{\rm{P}}(1)
denotes a random quantity such that is bounded in probability, and
o_{\rm{P}}(1)
indicates convergence to 0 in probability. We define
\mathbb{R}^{+}=[0,+\infty)
. The notation
{I}_{n}
is used to represent the
n
-dimensional identity matrix.
Let
\mathbb{I}_{A}
denote the indicator function of the event
A
,
\top
denote the transpose of a matrix, and let

{\bm{\theta}}=({\bm{\mu}},a)^{\top}=(\mu_{1},~~\mu_{2},\dots,~~\mu_{p},a)^{\top}\in\mathcal{O}
, where
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}\times(0,\infty)
is the parameter space and
{\bm{\varphi}}{(t)}=[\varphi_{1}(t),~\varphi_{2}(t),~\varphi_{3}(t),~\ldots~,~\varphi_{p}(t)]^{\top},
where for each
i=1,2,\dots,p,
the function
\varphi_{i}(t)
is a real-valued function of
t
. The
absolute maximum norm
of vectors and matrices is denoted by

\|\cdot\|
.
We consider the following Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE):

(2.1)

dX_{t}=S({{\bm{\theta}}},t,X_{t})dt+\sigma dB_{t},

using notations similar to
Lyu and Nkurunziza, 2023
, where
\displaystyle S({{\bm{\theta}}},t,x)={\bm{\mu}}^{\top}{\bm{\varphi}}(t)-ax
,
\{B_{t},t\geq 0\}
is a standard Brownian motion, and
\sigma
is a positive constant.

Remark 1
.

In contrast to estimating drift parameters for continuous-time diffusion processes, the assumption of a known diffusion parameter
\sigma
is a prevalent practice. This choice comes from the singularity of measures corresponding to various diffusion parameters, which facilitates the direct computation of
\sigma
from a solitary continuous-time observation path, obviating the need for estimation procedures.

In order to describe the solution of the SDE, we need the following regularity conditions:

Assumption 1
.

The distribution of the initial value,
X_{0}
, of the SDE in (
2.1
) does not depend on the drift parameter
{\bm{\theta}}.
Furthermore,
X_{0}
is independent of
\{B_{t}:t\geq 0\}
and
E[|X_{0}|^{d}]<\infty,
for some
d\geq 2.

Assumption 2
.

For any
T>0
, the base function
\{{\bm{\varphi}}(t),t\in[0,T]\}
is Riemann-integrable on [0,T] and possess the following properties:

(1)

\varphi_{1}(t)\equiv 1
.

(2)

Periodicity:
{\bm{\varphi}}(t+\upsilon)={\bm{\varphi}}(t)
, for all
t\in[0,T]
, where
\upsilon>0
is the period.

(3)

Orthogonality in
L^{2}\left([0,\upsilon],\displaystyle\frac{1}{\upsilon}d\lambda\right):\int_{0}^{\upsilon}{\bm{\varphi}}(t){\bm{\varphi}}^{\top}(t)dt=\upsilon{I}_{p}

Remark 2
.

It follows from part 3 of Assumption
2
that
\displaystyle\bar{\bm{\varphi}}=\frac{1}{\upsilon}\int_{0}^{\upsilon}{\bm{\varphi}}(s)ds=e_{1}=(1,0\ldots,0)^{\top}
. In addition, since the base function
{\bm{\varphi}}
is bounded on [0,T] and
\upsilon-
periodic, this implies that
{\bm{\varphi}}
is bounded on
\mathbb{R}_{+}=[0,+\infty).
Furthermore, there exists a positive constant
K_{{\bm{\varphi}}}
, such that

(2.2)

\left\|{\bm{\varphi}}\right\|_{\infty}=\sup_{t\geq 0}\left\|{\bm{\varphi}}(t)\right\|\leq K_{{\bm{\varphi}}}.

Without loss of generality, we assume
\upsilon=1
in the following sections.

2.1.
Maximum likelihood estimation

First, we introduce the log-likelihood function, if the process is observed continuously on the interval
[0,T]
.
From
(
Liptser and Shiryaev, 2001
, Theorem 7.7)
, the log-likelihood function of the process defined by (
2.1
) is

(2.3)

{\rm log}\mathcal{L}([0,T],{\bm{\theta}})=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}S({{\bm{\theta}},t,X_{t}})dX_{t}-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}S^{2}({{\bm{\theta}},t,X_{t}})d{t},

where
T>0
; see also
Dehling et al., 2010
,
Nkurunziza and Zhang, 2018
. Furthermore, set

(2.4)

\mathbf{Q}_{[t_{0},t_{1}]}=\begin{bmatrix}\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}{\bm{\varphi}}(t){\bm{\varphi}}^{\top}(t)dt&-\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}{\bm{\varphi}}(t)X_{t}dt\\
-\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}{\bm{\varphi}}^{\top}(t)X_{t}dt&\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}X_{t}^{2}dt\end{bmatrix},

(2.5)

\tilde{\mathbf{R}}_{[t_{0},t_{1}]}=\begin{bmatrix}\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}{\bm{\varphi}}^{\top}(t)dX_{t}&-\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}X_{t}dX_{t}\\
\end{bmatrix}^{\top},\quad{}\mathbf{R}_{[t_{0},t_{1}]}=\begin{bmatrix}\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}{\bm{\varphi}}^{\top}(t)dB_{t}&-\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{1}}X_{t}dB_{t}\\
\end{bmatrix}^{\top},

for
0\leq t_{0}<t_{1}\leq T
. Then,

{\rm log}\mathcal{L}([0,T],{\bm{\theta}})=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}{\bm{\theta}}^{\top}\tilde{\mathbf{R}}_{[0,T]}-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}{\bm{\theta}}^{\top}\mathbf{Q}_{[0,T]}{\bm{\theta}}.

It is then easy to check that the maximum likelihood estimator (MLE) is

\check{\bm{\theta}}_{T}=\mathbf{Q}_{[0,T]}^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf{R}}_{[0,T]}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
-
2
hold. Then,

\check{\bm{\theta}}_{T}={\bm{\theta}}+\sigma\mathbf{Q}_{[0,T]}^{-1}\mathbf{R}_{[0,T]}
.

The proof can be found in Proposition 4.1 from
Dehling et al., 2010
.

Finally, one can look at the asymptotic behaviour of this estimator, which will be used when studying the asymptotic behaviour of the change-point statistics.

To simplify some expressions, set

(2.6)

{\bm{\Sigma}}=\begin{bmatrix}I_{p}&{\bm{\Lambda}}\\
{\bm{\Lambda}}^{\top}&\omega\end{bmatrix},\quad{\bm{\Lambda}}=-\int_{0}^{1}\widetilde{h}(t){\bm{\varphi}}(t)dt,\quad\omega=\int_{0}^{1}\widetilde{h}^{2}(t)dt+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2a}.

Based on Proposition 3.3 in
Lyu and Nkurunziza, 2023
, we have the following result, proven in Appendix
B.1
, which plays an important role in obtaining the asymptotic property of test statistics.

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
-
2
hold. Then, as
T\to\infty
, we have

(2.7)

\frac{1}{T}\mathbf{Q}_{[0,T]}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.\text{ \rm and }L^{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\bm{\Sigma}},\text{ and }T\mathbf{Q}_{[0,T]}^{-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a.s.}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\bm{\Sigma}}^{-1}.

Remark 3
.

From
Remark

2
,
\bar{\bm{\varphi}}=\int_{0}^{1}{\bm{\varphi}}(s)ds=(1,0,\ldots,0)^{\top}
,
and set
b=\frac{{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\bar{\bm{\varphi}}}{a}=\frac{\mu_{1}}{a}
.
Then the first column of
{\bm{\Sigma}}
is

\mathcal{C}_{1}=(\bar{\bm{\varphi}}^{\top},-b)^{\top}
.

As an immediate result of
Proposition

2.2
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 2.1
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
-
2
hold. Then,

\left|\left|\frac{1}{T}\mathbf{Q}_{[0,T]}-{\bm{\Sigma}}\right|\right|=O_{\rm{P}}(T^{-1}),

as
T\to\infty
.

Proof of
Corollary

2.1
.

First,

\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}{\bm{\varphi}}(t){\bm{\varphi}}^{\top}(t)dt-I_{p}=\frac{\lfloor T\rfloor}{T}I_{p}-I_{p}+\frac{1}{T}\int_{\lfloor T\rfloor}^{T}{\bm{\varphi}}(t){\bm{\varphi}}^{\top}(t)dt.

Furthermore, we get

T\left|\left|\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}{\bm{\varphi}}(t){\bm{\varphi}}^{\top}(t)dt-I_{p}\right|\right|\leq T\left(\frac{\lfloor T\rfloor-T}{T}\left|\left|I_{p}\right|\right|+\frac{K_{{\bm{\varphi}}}^{2}}{T}\right)\leq T\frac{1+K_{{\bm{\varphi}}}^{2}}{T}=1+K_{{\bm{\varphi}}}^{2}.

The rest of the proof is similar to the proof of
Proposition

2.2
.
∎

The next result, proven in Appendix
B.2
, shows that the MLE
\check{\bm{\theta}}_{T}
is consistent.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
-
2
hold. Then,
\check{\bm{\theta}}_{T}
is a strongly consistent estimator of
{\bm{\theta}}
. Furthermore,

\check{\bm{\theta}}_{T}
is asymptotically normal, i.e.
T^{1/2}\left(\check{\bm{\theta}}_{T}-{\bm{\theta}}\right)\xrightarrow[T\to\infty]{{D}}\mathcal{N}_{p+1}(0,\sigma^{2}{{\bm{\Sigma}}}^{-1})
, and

.

3.
Asymptotic behaviour of estimators for the discretized process

In most applications, a continuous process is only observed at finite times. To this end, consider a partition
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{N}=T
on a given period
[0,T]
, with
\displaystyle\Delta_{N}=\frac{T}{N}=t_{1}-t_{0}=\cdots=t_{N}-t_{N-1}
.

For all
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, set
Y_{N,i}=\frac{X_{t_{i}}-X_{t_{i-1}}}{\Delta_{N}^{1/2}}
,

(3.1)

\mathbf{Z}_{N,i}=\Delta_{N}^{1/2}(\varphi_{1}(t_{i-1}),\varphi_{2}(t_{i-1}),\dots,\varphi_{p}(t_{i-1}),-X_{t_{i-1}})^{\top},

and

(3.2)

\epsilon_{N,i}=\frac{\sigma}{\Delta_{N}^{1/2}}(B_{t_{i}}-B_{t_{i-1}})=\sigma\tilde{\epsilon}_{N,i},\quad i\geq 1.

Then, let

(3.3)

\displaystyle r_{N,i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Y_{N,i}-\mathbf{Z}_{N,i}^{\top}{\bm{\theta}}-\epsilon_{N,i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta_{N}^{1/2}}\int^{t_{i}}_{t_{i-1}}\left[{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\left\{{\bm{\varphi}}(s)-{\bm{\varphi}}(t_{i-1})\right\}-a(X_{s}-X_{t_{i-1}})\right]ds,

so

(3.4)

Y_{N,i}=\mathbf{Z}_{N,i}^{\top}{\bm{\theta}}+\epsilon_{N,i}+r_{N,i},\qquad i\geq 1.

Based on the previous notations, set

\mathbf{Y}_{N}=\begin{bmatrix}Y_{N,1}\\
Y_{N,2}\\
\vdots\\
Y_{N,N}\\
\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathbf{Z}_{N}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{Z}_{N,1}^{\top}\\
\mathbf{Z}_{N,2}^{\top}\\
\vdots\\
\mathbf{Z}_{N,N}^{\top}\end{bmatrix},\quad{\bm{\theta}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mu_{1}\\
\mu_{2}\\
\vdots\\
\mu_{p}\\
a\end{bmatrix},\quad{\bm{\epsilon}}_{N}=\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon_{N,1}\\
\epsilon_{N,2}\\
\vdots\\
\epsilon_{N,N}\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathbf{r}_{N}=\begin{bmatrix}r_{N,1}\\
r_{N,2}\\
\vdots\\
r_{N,N}\end{bmatrix}.

Then,

\mathbf{Y}_{N}=\mathbf{Z}_{N}{\bm{\theta}}+{\bm{\epsilon}}_{N}+\mathbf{r}_{N}
and the estimator based on the discretized regression is given
\hat{\bm{\theta}}_{N}=\left(\mathbf{Z}_{N}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}_{N}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{Z}_{N}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}_{N}
.
Hence,

(3.5)

\hat{\bm{\theta}}_{N}=\left(\mathbf{Z}_{N}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}_{N}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{Z}_{N}^{\top}(\mathbf{Z}_{N}{\bm{\theta}}+{\bm{\epsilon}}_{N}+\mathbf{r}_{N})={\bm{\theta}}+\left(\mathbf{Z}_{N}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}_{N}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{Z}_{N}^{\top}({\bm{\epsilon}}_{N}+\mathbf{r}_{N}).

In passing, let us first note a relationship between the complete sample
\{X_{t}:0\leq t\leq T\}
and
\left\{X^{\Delta_{N}}(t_{i}):i=0,1,2,\dots\right\}
where
X^{\Delta_{N}}(t_{i})
satisfies the Euler-Maruyama discretized version of the SDE of
X(t)
. To this end, let
i_{t}=\max\{i=0,1,2,\dots:t_{i}\leq t\}
i.e.
i_{t}
is the maximum of integer less than or equal to
t
, and suppose that

X^{\Delta_{N}}(0)=X_{0}
.
From
(
Kloeden and Platen, 1999
, Theorem 9.6.2, p. 324)
,
there exists a constant

\bf{C}^{*}
, such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}E\left[\left|X_{t}-X^{\Delta_{N}}(i_{t})\right|\right]\leq{\bf C}^{*}\sqrt{\Delta_{N}+C(\Delta_{N})},

where
C(\Delta_{N})
is a non-negative function with
\displaystyle\inf_{\Delta_{N}>0}C(\Delta_{N})=0
. The existence of such a function follows from Proposition
3.1
.

3.1.
Sequential change-point detection

We are now in a position to define what is meant by change-point. To this end,
extend (
3.1
), by letting
{\bm{\theta}}
depend on
i
, viz.,

Y_{N,i}=\mathbf{Z}_{N,i}^{\top}{\bm{\theta}}_{i}+\epsilon_{N,i}+r_{N,i},\qquad i\geq 1.

First, we suppose that there is no change in the parameter
{\bm{\theta}}
in the historical data of size
N
:

(3.6)

{\bm{\theta}}_{i}={\bm{\theta}}_{0},\quad i=1,2,\cdots,N,

where
{\bm{\theta}}_{0}=\left({\bm{\mu}}^{\top},a\right)^{\top}=(\mu_{1},~~\mu_{2},\dots,~~\mu_{p},a)^{\top}
.
This can be written as

(3.7)

H_{0}:{\bm{\theta}}_{i}={\bm{\theta}}_{0},\qquad i\geq 1,

with the alternative hypothesis

(3.8)

\displaystyle H_{1}:

\displaystyle\text{there is }K_{*}\geq 0,\text{such that }{\bm{\theta}}_{i}={\bm{\theta}}_{0},\quad i\leq N+K_{*},~\text{and}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\bm{\theta}}_{i}={\bm{\theta}}_{*},\quad i>N+K_{*},\text{with }{\bm{\theta}}_{0}\neq{\bm{\theta}}_{*},

with
{\bm{\theta}}_{*}=({\bm{\mu}}_{*},a_{*})^{\top}=(\mu_{1*},~~\mu_{2*},\dots,~~\mu_{p*},a_{*})^{\top}
.

Let

X_{t_{N+i}}^{(2)}
denote the value of the process
\{X_{t},t\geq 0\}
after change-point, for
i>K_{*}
.
Furthermore, for
i\geq K_{*}+1
, set

(3.9)

\mathbf{Z}_{N,i}^{(2)}=\Delta_{N}^{1/2}\left(\varphi_{1}(t_{N+i-1}),\varphi_{2}(t_{N+i-1}),\dots,\varphi_{p}(t_{N+i-1}),-X_{t_{N+i-1}}^{(2)}\right)^{\top},

and the discretized version of the process is given by

(3.10)

Y_{N,i}^{(2)}=\frac{X_{t_{i}}^{(2)}-X_{t_{i-1}}^{(2)}}{\Delta_{N}^{1/2}}=\left(\mathbf{Z}_{N,i}^{(2)}\right)^{\top}{\bm{\theta}}_{*}+\epsilon_{N,i}+r_{N,i}^{(2)},\qquad i\geq K_{*}+1,

where

(3.11)

\epsilon_{N,i}=\frac{\sigma}{\Delta_{N}^{1/2}}(B_{t_{i}}-B_{t_{i-1}}),\quad i\geq K_{*}+1,

and

(3.12)

r_{N,i}^{(2)}=\frac{1}{\Delta_{N}^{1/2}}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\left[{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}\left\{{\bm{\varphi}}(s)-{\bm{\varphi}}(t_{i-1})\right\}-a\left(X_{s}^{(2)}-X_{t_{i-1}}^{(2)}\right)\right]ds.

Asymptotic behaviour

Now, set
\displaystyle{\bm{\Sigma}}_{N}=\frac{1}{T}{\mathbf{Z}_{N}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}_{N}}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{Z}_{N,i}\mathbf{Z}_{N,i}^{\top}

Proposition 3.1

Suppose

3.3.
Goodness-of-fit test for a GOU process

The goodness-of-fit test is based on the residuals defined by

(3.15)

\hat{\epsilon}_{N,i}=Y_{N,i}-\mathbf{Z}_{N,i}^{\top}\hat{\bm{\theta}}_{N}=\epsilon_{N,i}+r_{N,i}-\mathbf{Z}_{N,i}^{\top}\left(\hat{\bm{\theta}}_{N}-{\bm{\theta}}\right),\qquad i\geq 1.

For any
y\in\mathbb{R}
, set
\displaystyle\hat{F}_{N}(y)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{I}\left(\hat{\epsilon}_{N,i}\leq y\right)
, and
F(y)=\Phi(y/\sigma)
, where
\Phi
is the cdf of the standard Gaussian, with density
\phi
. Further, set
\mathbb{F}_{N}(y)=N^{1/2}\left\{\hat{F}_{N}(y)-F(y)\right\}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}
, and let
\mathcal{C}_{1}=\left(1,0,\ldots,0,-\mu_{1}/a\right)^{\top}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

\mathbb{F}_{N}
converges in
C[-\infty,+\infty]
to
\mathbb{B}\circ F+F^{\prime}{\bm{\Theta}}^{\top}\mathcal{C}_{1}
, where
\mathbb{B}
is a Brownian bridge and
{\bm{\Theta}}_{N}=T^{1/2}\left({\bm{\theta}}_{N}-{\bm{\theta}}_{0}\right)
converges to
{\bm{\Theta}}
.

Proof.

It follows from
Ghoudi and Rémillard, 1998
or
Nasri and Rémillard, 2019
,
Assumptions
1
–
4
, (
3.15
), and (
3.14
), that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{1/2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{N,i}=o_{\rm{P}}(1)
, while
\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{1/2}T^{1/2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{Z}_{N,i}
is the first column of the matrix
{\bm{\Sigma}}_{N}
, by Remark
3
. As a result,
\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{1/2}T^{1/2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{Z}_{N,i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm{P}}}{{\to}}\mathcal{C}_{1}
. It follows that for any
\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{p+1}
,

\frac{1}{N^{1/2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\{F\left(y+r_{N,i}+\mathbf{c}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}_{N,i}/T^{1/2}\right)-F(y)\right\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}F^{\prime}(y)\mathbf{c}^{\top}\mathcal{C}_{1}=\frac{1}{\sigma}\phi\left(\frac{y}{\sigma}\right)\mathbf{c}^{\top}\mathcal{C}_{1}.

∎

Next, set
\mathbb{D}_{N}(u)=N^{1/2}\{D_{N}(u)-u\}
, where

\displaystyle D_{N}(u)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{I}\left\{\Phi\left(\hat{e}_{N,i}/\hat{\sigma}_{N}\right)\leq u\right\}
,
u\in[0,1]
, and

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}Y_{N,i}^{2}
.

Corollary 3.2
.

\mathbb{D}_{n}
converges in
C[0,1]
to
\mathbb{D}
, where

(3.16)

\mathbb{D}(u)=\beta(u)+\phi\circ\Phi^{-1}(u)\left\{\mathbb{M}+\mathbb{S}\Phi^{-1}(u)\right\},

\mathbb{M}_{N}=N^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{\epsilon}_{N,i}
converges in law to
\mathbb{M}\sim N(0,1)
, and

\mathbb{S}_{N}=N^{1/2}\left(\frac{\hat{\sigma}_{N}}{\sigma}-1\right)
converges in law to
\mathbb{S}\sim N(0,1/2)
, independent of
\mathbb{M}
. In addition,
E\{\mathbb{M}\beta(u)\}=-\phi\circ\Phi^{-1}(u)
, and
E\{\beta(u)\mathbb{S}\}=-\frac{1}{2}H(u)
, with
H(u)=\Phi^{-1}(u)\phi\circ\Phi^{-1}(u)
.

Remark 4
.

The limiting process
\mathbb{D}
is the same as the one used to test for Gaussianity with unknown mean and variance
(
Ghoudi and Rémillard, 2015
)
.
In particular, the law of
\mathbb{D}
does not depend on any parameters.

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
3.1
that
\mathbb{D}_{n}
converges in
C[0,1]
to
\mathbb{D}
, where

\mathbb{D}(u)=\mathbb{B}(u)+\phi\circ\Phi^{-1}(u)\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\top}{\bm{\Theta}}/\sigma+\mathbb{S}\Phi^{-1}(u)\phi\circ\Phi^{-1}(u)
, and

\mathbb{S}_{N}=N^{1/2}\left(\frac{\hat{\sigma}_{N}}{\sigma}-1\right)
converges in law to
\mathbb{S}
.
Now, it follows from (
3.5
), Proposition
3.2
and Assumption
4
that
{\bm{\Theta}}/\sigma
is the limit in law

\displaystyle{\bm{\Sigma}}^{-1}\frac{1}{T^{1/2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{Z}_{N,i}\tilde{\epsilon}_{N,i}
.
Since
{\bm{\Sigma}}^{-1}\mathcal{C}_{1}=(1,0,\ldots,0)^{\top}
, it follows that
\mathbb{M}={\bm{\Theta}}^{\top}\mathcal{C}_{1}\sigma
is the limit in law of
\mathbb{M}_{N}=N^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{\epsilon}_{N,i}
, which converges to a standard Gaussian.
Next, under Assumptions
1
–
4
, and using Proposition
3.2
, one gets
that
\frac{1}{N^{1/2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{N,i}^{2}=o_{P}(1)
, so

\displaystyle N^{1/2}\left(\frac{\hat{\sigma}_{N}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}-1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{1/2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\epsilon_{N,i}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}-1\right)+\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}N^{1/2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{N,i}^{2}+\frac{T}{\sigma N^{1/2}}{\bm{\theta}}^{\top}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{N}{\bm{\theta}}

\displaystyle\quad+2{\bm{\theta}}^{\top}\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}N^{1/2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\{\mathbf{Z}_{N,i}(\epsilon_{N,i}+r_{N,i})+\epsilon_{N,i}r_{N,i}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{1/2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\tilde{\epsilon}_{N,i}^{2}-1\right)+o_{\rm{P}}(1).

As a result,
\mathbb{S}_{N}=\left(\frac{\hat{\sigma}_{N}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}-1\right)\Big/\left(1+\frac{\hat{\sigma}_{N}}{\sigma}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{N^{1/2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\tilde{\epsilon}_{N,i}^{2}-1\right)+o_{P}(1)
converges in law to

\mathbb{S}\sim N(0,1/2)
, with
E\{\beta(u)\mathbb{S}\}=-\frac{1}{2}H(u)
, with
H(u)=\Phi^{-1}(u)\phi\circ\Phi^{-1}(u)
.
∎

Since the law of
\mathbb{D}
does not depend on any parameters, any continuous functional of
\mathbb{D}_{N}
like Kolmogorov-Smirnov or Cramér-von Mises can be easily tabulated. First, note that if
u_{N:1}<\cdots<u_{N:N}
are the order statistics of
u_{N,i}=\Phi\left(\frac{\hat{e}_{N,i}}{\hat{\sigma}_{N}}\right)
,

, it follows that the Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic is

\mathbb{T}_{N}=\sup_{u\in[0,1]}|\mathbb{D}_{N}(u)|=N^{1/2}\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\max(|u_{N:i}-i/N|,|u_{N:i}-(i-1)/N|,

while the Cramér-von Mises statistic is

\mathbb{V}_{N}=\int_{0}^{1}\{\mathbb{D}_{N}(u)\}^{2}du=\frac{1}{12N}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\{u_{N:i}-\frac{(i-1/2)}{N}\right\}^{2}.

, where
m_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}
, and
s_{N}^{2}=\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{i}^{2}

and
\mathbb{V}_{N}^{(k)}
,

4.
Asymptotic property of the test statistics

In what follows, we define two processes to detect change-point. In this section, we will develop the test statistics for detecting structural changes and examine their asymptotic distributions under both the null and alternative.

4.1.
Change-point detection based on residuals

Let
\hat{Q}(N,K)
be the stochastic process

(4.1)

\hat{Q}(N,K)=\sum_{N<i\leq N+K}\hat{\epsilon}_{N,i},\qquad K\geq 1,

where

(4.2)

\hat{\epsilon}_{N,i}=Y_{N,i}-\mathbf{Z}_{N,i}^{\top}\hat{\bm{\theta}}_{N}=\epsilon_{N,i}+r_{N,i}-\mathbf{Z}_{N,i}\left(\mathbf{Z}_{N}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}_{N}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{Z}_{N}^{\top}({\bm{\epsilon}}_{N}+\mathbf{r}_{N}).

The boundary function is defined as
\displaystyle g(N,K)=c\sigma g_{1}(N,K)
,
c=c(\alpha)>0
,
where
g_{1}(N,K)=N^{1/2}\left(1+\frac{K}{N}\right)\ell_{\gamma}\left(\frac{K}{N}\right)
, where
\ell_{\gamma}(s)=\left(\frac{s}{1+s}\right)^{\gamma}
, with
0\leq\gamma<1/2
,
and
\alpha
is the type-I error defined in (
1.1
). The associated stopping time is then given by

(4.3)

\tau_{\hat{Q}}(N)=\inf\{K\geq 1:|\hat{Q}(N,K)|\geq g(N,K)\}.

The proof of the next result, given in Appendix
C.2
, is similar to the proof of Theorem 2.1 in
Horváth et al., 2004
, although there are extra terms
that need to be taken care of. These extra terms appear because we work with the process observed at discrete times
t_{i-1}=i\Delta_{N}
,
i\geq 0
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
-
3
hold. Then, under the null hypothesis (
3.7
), we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{\hat{Q}}(N)<\infty\right)=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup_{1\leq K<\infty}\frac{|\hat{Q}(N,K)|}{\sigma g_{1}(N,K)}\geq c\right\}=\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup_{0<t\leq 1}\frac{|\mathbb{B}_{1}(t)|}{t^{\gamma}}\geq c\right\},

where
\mathbb{B}_{1}(\cdot)
is a Brownian motion on
[0,1]
.

Remark 5
.

Suppose that there is no change in the
historical data and the natural estimator of
\sigma^{2}
is the so-called realized quadratic variation of the process given by

\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}Y_{N,i}^{2}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{(X_{t_{i}}-X_{t_{i-1}})^{2}}{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}.

It follows from
Corollary

3.2
that
\hat{\sigma}^{2}_{N}
converges in probability to
\sigma^{2}
, so
Theorem

4.1
remains true when
\sigma
is replaced by

\hat{\sigma}_{N}
.

The next assumption is needed to prove the result under the alternative (
3.8
), namely that there exists
K_{*}\geq 0

such that

{\bm{\theta}}_{i}={\bm{\theta}}_{0},\quad 1\leq i\leq N+K_{*},\text{ and }{\bm{\theta}}_{i}={\bm{\theta}}_{*},\quad i>N+K_{*},\text{with }{\bm{\theta}}_{0}\neq{\bm{\theta}}_{*}=(\mu_{*}^{\top},a_{*})^{\top}.

Assumption 5
.

Suppose that

K_{*}/N\to t_{*}\geq 0
and

(4.4)

\kappa_{\hat{Q},{\bm{\theta}}_{0},{\bm{\theta}}_{*}}=\mathbf{e}_{1}^{\top}\left(\frac{a}{a_{*}}{\bm{\mu}}_{*}-{\bm{\mu}}\right)=a\left(\frac{\mu_{1*}}{a_{*}}-\frac{\mu_{1}}{a}\right)\neq 0,

The next result, showing the behavior of the statistic under the alternative hypothesis, is proven in Appendix
C.3
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
-
5
hold. Then, under the alternative hypothesis (
3.8
), as
N\to\infty
,

\sup_{1\leq K<\infty}\frac{\left|\hat{Q}(N,K)\right|}{g_{1}(N,K)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\infty.

In particular,
\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left\{\tau_{\hat{Q}}(N)<\infty\right\}=1
.

Remark 6
.

Note that one can have
{\bm{\theta}}_{*}\neq{\bm{\theta}}_{0}
and
\kappa_{\hat{Q},{\bm{\theta}}_{0},{\bm{\theta}}_{*}}=0
, showing that the test statistic is not consistent under all alternative hypotheses. This is illustrated by a numerical experiment in Section
5
.

4.2.
Sequential detection using estimators

One can also detect change-point using a statistic based on estimations at different times. To this end,
let
\hat{{\bm{\Gamma}}}(N,K)
be the process defined by

(4.5)

\ \hat{{\bm{\Gamma}}}(N,K)=T^{1/2}\left(\frac{1}{T}\mathbf{Z}_{N}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}_{N}\right)^{1/2}\left(\hat{\bm{\theta}}_{N+K}-\hat{\bm{\theta}}_{N}\right).

Further set

(4.6)

\tau_{\hat{{\bm{\Gamma}}}}(N)=\inf\left\{K\geq 1:\left\|\hat{{\bm{\Gamma}}}(N,K)\right\|\geq c{\ell\left(K/N\right)}\right\},

where
\ell_{\gamma}(s)=\left(\frac{s}{1+s}\right)^{\gamma}
, with
0\leq\gamma<1/2
. The proof of the next result is given in Appendix
C.4
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
-
4
hold. Then, under the null hypothesis (
3.7
),

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left\{\tau_{\hat{{\bm{\Gamma}}}}(N)<\infty\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup_{K\geq 1}\left\|\hat{{\bm{\Gamma}}}(N,K)\right\|/{\ell\left(K/N\right)}\geq c\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup_{0<t\leq 1}\frac{\left\|\mathbb{B}_{p+1}(t)\right\|}{t^{\gamma}}\geq c\right\},

where
\mathbb{B}_{p+1}(\cdot)
is a
(p+1)
-dimensional Brownian motion.

Finally, we can state the result of the behavior of the statistic
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}(N,K)/h(K/N)
under the alternative hypothesis. Its proof is given in Appendix
C.5
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Suppose that
1
-
4
hold. Then, under the alternative hypothesis (
3.8
), as
N\to\infty
, we have

\sup_{K\geq 1}\left\{\left\|\hat{{\bm{\Gamma}}}(N,K)\right\|/\ell\left(K/N\right)\right\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\infty.

In particular,
\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left\{\tau_{\hat{{\bm{\Gamma}}}}(N)<\infty\right\}=1
.

6.
Conclusion

In this paper, we address the sequential change-point detection problem within the framework of generalized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (GOU) processes, where the drift is a periodic function of time
t
. First, we recall that the GOU process admits a unique and strong solution with explicit distribution law and we present the maximum likelihood estimator. In addition, for GOU processes observed at discrete times, we develop two types of tests for detecting changes in the parameters: one based on the cumulative sum of residuals, with statistic
\hat{Q}
, and the other based on sequential estimators, with statistic
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}
. Then, we derive their limiting behaviours under both the null and alternative hypotheses. In particular case, under any alternative hypothesis, the test based on statistic
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}
is consistent; however, the test based on statistic
\hat{Q}
is not consistent in general.
These theoretical results are confirmed in our simulation section. In particular, it seems that the test based on
\hat{\bm{\Gamma}}
outperforms in general the test based on
\hat{Q}
.

Proposition A.1
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
-
3
hold. Then, for the partition
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{N}=T
on a given period
[0,T]
, with
t_{i}-t_{i-1}\leq\Delta
, and
t\in[t_{i-1},t_{i}]
,

\displaystyle E\left(|X_{t}-X_{t_{i-1}}|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sigma\sqrt{(t-t_{i-1})}+O(\Delta),

\displaystyle E\left(|X_{t}-X_{t_{i-1}}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sigma^{2}(t-t_{i-1})+O\left(\Delta^{3/2}\right).

Proof.

From
Nkurunziza and Fu, 2019
, we have
X_{t}=e^{-at}X_{0}+h(t)+z(t)
,
where

h(t)=e^{-at}\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mu_{k}\int_{0}^{t}e^{as}\varphi_{k}(s)ds,t\geq 0,

and

\displaystyle z(t)=\sigma e^{-at}\int_{0}^{t}e^{as}dB_{s}.
Then, for
t\in[t_{i-1},t_{i}]
,

(A.1)

X_{t}-X_{t_{i-1}}=\left(e^{-at}X_{0}-e^{-at_{i-1}}X_{0}\right)+\left(h(t)-h(t_{i-1})\right)+\left(z(t)-z(t_{i-1})\right).

First,

\left|e^{-at_{i-1}}-e^{-at}-ae^{-at_{i-1}}(t-t_{i-1})\right|\leq\frac{a^{2}}{2}(t-t_{i-1})^{2},

so for
t\in[t_{i-1},t_{i}]
,

(A.2)

E[|e^{-at}X_{0}-e^{-at_{i-1}}X_{0}|]\leq E[|X_{0}|]\left\{a(t-t_{i-1})+\frac{a^{2}}{2}(t-t_{i-1})^{2}\right\}.

Second,

h(t)-h(t_{i-1})=e^{-at}\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mu_{k}\int_{0}^{t}e^{as}\varphi_{k}(s)ds-e^{-at_{i-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mu_{k}\int_{0}^{t_{i-1}}e^{as}\varphi_{k}(s)ds\\
=e^{-at}\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mu_{k}\int_{0}^{t}e^{as}\varphi_{k}(s)ds-e^{-at}\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mu_{k}\int_{0}^{t_{i-1}}e^{as}\varphi_{k}(s)ds\\
+e^{-at}\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mu_{k}\int_{0}^{t_{i-1}}e^{as}\varphi_{k}(s)ds-e^{-at_{i-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mu_{k}\int_{0}^{t_{i-1}}e^{as}\varphi_{k}(s)ds\\
=e^{-at}\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mu_{k}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t}e^{as}\varphi_{k}(s)ds+\left(e^{-at}-e^{-at_{i-1}}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mu_{k}\int_{0}^{t_{i-1}}e^{as}\varphi_{k}(s)ds,

so setting
C_{1}=\sum_{k=1}^{p}|\mu_{k}|K_{{\bm{\varphi}}}
, the first term is bounded by
C_{1}(t-t_{i-1})+C_{1}a(t-t_{i-1})^{2}
,
and the second term is also bounded by the same value. As a result,

(A.3)

|h(t)-h(t_{i-1})|\leq 2C_{1}(t-t_{i-1})+C_{1}a(t-t_{i-1})^{2}.

Third,

z(t)-z(t_{i-1})=\sigma e^{-at}\int_{0}^{t}e^{as}dB_{s}-\sigma e^{-at_{i-1}}\int_{0}^{t_{i-1}}e^{as}dB_{s}\\
=\sigma e^{-at}\int_{0}^{t}e^{as}dB_{s}-\sigma e^{-at}\int_{0}^{t_{i-1}}e^{as}dB_{s}+\sigma e^{-at}\int_{0}^{t_{i-1}}e^{as}dB_{s}-\sigma e^{-at_{i-1}}\int_{0}^{t_{i-1}}e^{as}dB_{s}\\
=\sigma e^{-at}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t}e^{as}dB_{s}+\sigma(e^{-at}-e^{-at_{i-1}})\int_{0}^{t_{i-1}}e^{as}dB_{s}.

From the fact
E[|z(t)-z(t_{i-1})|]\leq\left\{E[|z(t)-z(t_{i-1})|^{2}]\right\}^{1/2}
, we have

E[|z(t)-z(t_{i-1})|^{2}]=E\left[\left|\sigma e^{-at}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t}e^{as}dB_{s}+\sigma(e^{-at}-e^{-at_{i-1}})\int_{0}^{t_{i-1}}e^{as}dB_{s}\right|^{2}\right]\\
\leq 2\sigma^{2}E\left[\left|e^{-at}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t}e^{as}dB_{s}\right|^{2}\right]+2\sigma^{2}E\left[\left|(e^{-at}-e^{-at_{i-1}})\int_{0}^{t_{i-1}}e^{as}dB_{s}\right|^{2}\right].

Next,

E\left[\left|e^{-at}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t}e^{as}dB_{s}\right|^{2}\right]=\frac{1}{2a}(1-e^{-2a(t-t_{i-1})})\leq(t-t_{i-1})+a(t-t_{i-1})^{2}.

Also,

E\left[\left|(e^{-at}-e^{-at_{i-1}})\int_{0}^{t_{i-1}}e^{as}dB_{s}\right|^{2}\right]=(e^{-at}-e^{-at_{i-1}})^{2}\int_{0}^{t_{i-1}}e^{2as}d{s}\\
\leq\frac{a}{2}(t-t_{i-1})^{2}\left\{1+a(t-t_{i-1})\right\}^{2}.

As a result,

(A.4)

E[|z(t)-z(t_{i-1})|]\leq\sigma\sqrt{(t-t_{i-1})}\left\{1+a(t-t_{i-1})\right\}.

(
A.1
)-(
A.4
) imply that for
t\in[t_{i-1},t_{i}]
,

(A.5)

E\left(|X_{t}-X_{t_{i-1}}|\right)\leq\sigma\sqrt{(t-t_{i-1})}+O(\Delta).

Furthermore, for
t\in[t_{i-1},t_{i}]
, we have

(A.6)

E\left(|X_{t}-X_{t_{i-1}}|^{2}\right)=\sigma^{2}(t-t_{i-1})+O\left(\Delta^{3/2}\right).

{\bm{\Sigma}}_{t_{*},t}={\bm{\Sigma}}\left(\frac{(1+t_{*})}{(t-t_{*})}{\bm{\Sigma}}+{\bm{\Sigma}}_{*}\right)^{-1}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{*}
, one
may conclude that

T^{-1/2}\left\|\hat{{\bm{\Gamma}}}(N,\lfloor N{t}\rfloor)\right\|/\ell_{\gamma}\left(\frac{\lfloor N{t}\rfloor}{N}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\rm Pr}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\frac{1}{\ell_{\gamma}(t)}\left\|{\bm{\Sigma}}_{t_{*},t}({\bm{\theta}}_{*}-{\bm{\theta}}_{0})\right\|=\frac{\kappa_{t_{*},t}}{\ell_{\gamma}(t)}>0,

and

(C.12)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\kappa_{t_{*},t}=\kappa_{\hat{\bm{\Gamma}},{\bm{\theta}}_{0},{\bm{\theta}}_{*}}=\left\|{\bm{\Sigma}}({\bm{\theta}}_{*}-{\bm{\theta}}_{0})\right\|>0.

As a result,

\sup_{0\leq t<\infty}\left\{\left\|\hat{{\bm{\Gamma}}}(N,\lfloor N{t}\rfloor)\right\|/h\left(\frac{\lfloor N{t}\rfloor}{N}\right)\right\}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm{Pr}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\infty,

as
N\to\infty
.
This completes the proof.∎

Appendix D
Computation of
\tilde{h}

Note that for for any
k\geq 1
and any
t\in[0,1]
,

\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{as}\varphi_{k}(s)ds=\frac{e^{-a}}{1-e^{-a}}\int_{0}^{1}e^{as}\varphi_{k}(s)ds+\int_{0}^{t}e^{as}\varphi_{k}(s)ds.

In particular, if
k=1
, then

(D.1)

e^{-at}\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{as}\varphi_{1}(s)ds=\frac{1}{a}.

Next, if
{\bm{\varphi}}_{2}(s)=2^{1/2}\cos{2\pi s}
, then, for any
t\in[0,1]
,

(D.2)

e^{-at}\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{as}\varphi_{2}(s)ds=\frac{2^{1/2}}{a^{2}+4\pi^{2}}\left\{a\cos{2\pi t}+2\pi\sin{2\pi t}\right\}=a\varphi_{2}(t)+b\psi_{2}(t),

where
\psi_{2}(t)=2^{1/2}\sin{2\pi t}
. Recall that
\left\{1,2^{1/2}\cos{2k\pi t},2^{1/2}\sin{2k\pi t}:\;k\geq 1\right\}
is a complete orthonormal system on
L^{2}([0,1])
, so setting
\varphi_{k}(t)=2^{1/2}\cos{2(k-1)\pi t}
,
k\geq 1
, and
\psi_{k}(t)=2^{1/2}\sin{2(k-1)\pi t}
,
k\geq 1
, one gets
that for
k\geq 1
,

e^{-at}\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{as}\varphi_{k}(s)ds=a_{k}\varphi_{k}(t)+b_{k}\psi_{k}(t),

with
a_{k}=\frac{a}{a^{2}+4\pi^{2}(k-1)^{2}}
and
b_{k}=\frac{2\pi(k-1)}{a^{2}+4\pi^{2}(k-1)^{2}}
. Note that
a_{1}=\frac{1}{a}
,
b_{1}=0=\psi_{1}(t)
, and
a_{k}^{2}+b_{k}^{2}=\frac{1}{a^{2}+4\pi^{2}(k-1)^{2}}
.
As a result, for
{\bm{\theta}}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{p},a)
, one gets

(D.3)

\tilde{h}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\mu_{k}\{a_{k}\varphi_{k}(t)+b_{k}\psi_{k}(t)\}.

It then follows that
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\tilde{h}(t)\varphi_{k}(t)dt=\mu_{k}a_{k}
,
k\geq 1
, and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left\{\tilde{h}(t)\right\}^{2}dt=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\frac{\mu_{k}^{2}}{a^{2}+4(k-1)^{2}\pi^{2}}
. Hence,

(D.4)

\Lambda^{\top}=-\left[\mu_{1}a_{1},\ldots,\mu_{p}a_{p}\right].

Finally,
\displaystyle\omega=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\frac{\mu_{k}^{2}}{a^{2}+4(k-1)^{2}\pi^{2}}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2a}
. Taking for example
p=2
, so
{\bm{\theta}}=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},a)
, it follows from (
2.6
) that

(D.5)

{\bm{\Sigma}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&-\frac{\mu_{1}}{a}\\
0&1&-\frac{\mu_{2}a}{a^{2}+4\pi^{2}}\\
-\frac{\mu_{1}}{a}&-\frac{\mu_{2}a}{a^{2}+4\pi^{2}}&\frac{\mu_{1}^{2}}{a^{2}}+\frac{\mu_{2}^{2}}{a^{2}+4\pi^{2}}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2a}\end{array}\right).

In particular, if
{\bm{\theta}}=(1,2,1)
, then

(D.6)

{\bm{\Sigma}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&-1\\
0&1&-\frac{2}{1+4\pi^{2}}\\
-1&-\frac{2}{1+4\pi^{2}}&\frac{5+4\pi^{2}}{1+4\pi^{2}}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\end{array}\right).

Now, using
{\bm{\theta}}_{*}
instead of
{\bm{\theta}}_{0}
, one gets

(D.7)

{\bm{\Sigma}}_{*}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&-\frac{\mu_{1*}}{a_{*}}\\

First, suppose that

{\bm{\varphi}}(t)=\left(\phi_{1}(t),\ldots,\phi_{p_{1}}(t),\psi_{2}(t),\ldots,\psi_{p+1-p_{1}}(t)\right)^{\top}

\phi_{k}(t)=2^{1/2}\cos\{2\pi(k-1)t\}
, and
\psi_{k}(t)=2^{1/2}\sin\{2\pi(k-1)t\}
.
Then, for
\Delta>0
and for
k\geq 1

\displaystyle A_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-a\Delta}\int_{0}^{\Delta}e^{as}\cos\{2\pi(k-1)s\}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{k}\cos\{2\pi(k-1)\Delta\}-a_{k}e^{-a\Delta}+b_{k}\sin\{2\pi(k-1)\Delta\},

and

(E.2)

\displaystyle B_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-a\Delta}\int_{0}^{\Delta}e^{as}\sin\{2\pi(k-1)s\}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{k}e^{-a\Delta}-b_{k}\cos\{2\pi(k-1)\Delta\}+a_{k}\sin\{2\pi(k-1)\Delta\},

.
As a result, for any
k\geq 1

(E.3)

and

(E.4)

e^{-at_{i}}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}e^{as}\psi_{k}(s)ds=B_{k}\phi_{k}(t_{i-1})+A_{k}\psi_{k}(t_{i-1}).

Next, suppose that for
t\in[0,T]
,

dX_{t}=({\bm{\mu}}^{\top}{\bm{\varphi}}(t)-aX_{t})dt+\sigma dB_{t}
. Then, for any
0\leq i\leq N+K_{*}
and
t_{i}=i\Delta N=i\frac{T}{N}
, one gets

(E.5)

X_{t_{i}}=e^{-a\Delta_{N}}X_{t_{i-1}}+e^{-at_{i}}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}e^{as}{\bm{\mu}}^{\top}{\bm{\varphi}}(s)ds+\sigma\varepsilon_{N,i},

where
\displaystyle\varepsilon_{N,i}=e^{-at_{i}}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}e^{as}dB_{s}\sim N\left(0,\frac{1-e^{-2a\Delta_{N}}}{2a}\right)
.
If the parameters change after time
T(1+t_{*})=N+K_{*}
, it follows that for

i>N+K_{*}
,

(E.6)

X_{t_{i}}=e^{-a_{*}\Delta_{N}}X_{t_{i-1}}+e^{-a_{*}t_{i}}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}e^{a_{*}s}{\bm{\mu}}_{*}^{\top}{\bm{\varphi}}(s)ds+\sigma\varepsilon_{N,i,*},

where
\displaystyle\varepsilon_{N,i,*}=e^{-a_{*}t_{i}}\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}e^{a_{*}s}dB_{s}\sim N\left(0,\frac{1-e^{-2a_{*}\Delta_{N}}}{2a_{*}}\right)
.

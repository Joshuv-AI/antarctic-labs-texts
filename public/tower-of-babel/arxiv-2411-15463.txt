Minimizing breaks by minimizing odd cycle transversals Thanks: This work was supported by JSPS KAKENHI Grant Number JP20K04973.
Koichi Fujii, Tomomi Matsui

Abstract
Constructing a suitable schedule for sports competitions is a crucial issue in sports scheduling. The round-robin tournament is a competition adopted in many professional sports. For most round-robin tournaments, it is considered undesirable that a team plays consecutive away or home matches; such an occurrence is called a break. Accordingly, it is preferable to reduce the number of breaks in a tournament. A common approach is first to construct a schedule and then determine a home-away assignment based on the given schedule to minimize the number of breaks (first-schedule-then-break). In this study, we concentrate on the problem that arises in the second stage of the first-schedule-then-break approach, namely, the break minimization problem (BMP). We prove that this problem can be reduced to an odd cycle transversal problem, the well-studied graph problem. These results lead to a new approximation algorithm for the BMP. Keywords: sports scheduling; tournament scheduling; round robin; odd cycle transversal.



1  Introduction

Constructing a suitable schedule for sports competitions is
a crucial issue in sports scheduling.
A (single)
round-robin tournament
(RRT) is a simple sports competition,
where each team plays against every other team once.
The RRT is a competition framework adopted
in many professional sports
such as soccer and basketball,
especially in Europe.
In this study,
we consider an RRT schedule with the following properties:

•

Each team plays one match in each
slot
,
that is the day when a match is held.

•

Each team has a home stadium, and each match is held
at the home stadium of one of the two playing teams.
When a team plays a match at the home of the opponent,
we state that the team plays away.

•

Each team plays each other team exactly once at home or away.

It is considered undesirable that
a team plays consecutive away or home matches
to reduce player’s burden;
such an occurrence is called a
break
.

Accordingly,
the number of breaks in a tournament should be reduced.
To construct a tournament schedule with fewer breaks,
the following two decomposition approaches are widely used:
(i) first-break-then-schedule
[
18
,
3
,
15
,
7
,
26
,
27
,
25
]

and (ii) first-schedule-then-break
[
24
,
18
,
16
,
17
,
20
,
22
]
.
In the first-break-then-schedule approach,
an home-away assignment (HA-assignment) is generated in the first stage,
which assigns a home game or an away game to each team
in each slot.
In the second stage,
a timetable consistent with the generated HA-assignment
is found,
if it exists.
Contrarily, in the first-schedule-then-break approach,
a timetable is constructed at the first stage.
The second stage determines the home and away teams of each match.
In this approach,
the home advantage is further determined in the second stage.
For detailed information,
refer to
[
21
]
.
In this study,
we focus on a problem
occurring in the second stage
of the first-schedule-then-break approach.
This problem is commonly called the break minimization problem (BMP),
which determines an
HA-assignment
minimizing the number of breaks.

The research on the BMP,
introduced by the seminal work of
de Werra
[
4
]
,
has steadily evolved.
By
B_{\min}(\tau)
,
we denote the minimum total number of breaks
over all possible HA-assignments
for a given timetable
\tau
of an RRT with
2n
teams.
For the lower bound of
B_{\min}(\tau)
,
de Werra
[
4
]
demonstrated that
any schedule of a round-robin tournament has
at least
2n-2
breaks.
For the upper bound,
Miyashiro and Matsui
[
16
]

proposed an algorithm
for finding an HA-assignment satisfying the condition
that the number of breaks
is less than or equal to
n(n-1)
for a given timetable.
Post and Woeginger
[
20
]
revised this analysis and
improved the upper bound of the number of breaks from
n(n-1)

to
(n-1)^{2}
if the number of teams
2n
is not
a multiple of
4
.

Elf, Jünger, and Rinaldi
[
5
]
conjectured that
the problem of finding
B_{\min}(\tau)
is NP-hard,
which has not been proven yet to the best of our knowledge.
Miyashiro and Matsui
[
17
]
formulated
BMP as MAX RES CUT.
They also applied an approximation algorithm for MAX RES CUT based on the semi-definite programming relaxation,
which was proposed by Goemans and Williamson
[
6
]
.

In this paper,
we consider the odd cycle transversal (OCT) problem,
which is the problem of deleting vertices to transform a graph into a bipartite graph.

After the breakthrough paper of Reed, Smith, and Vetta
[
23
]
,
various parameterized algorithms have been investigated
[
14
,
11
,
10
,
13
,
9
,
12
]
.
Not only are there theoretical results,
but there are also computational results
for both the exact and heuristic approaches
[
8
,
2
]
.
We will explore the relationship
between the BMP and OCT problems and consequently propose a novel approximation algorithm of the BMP.

2.2
Odd Cycle Transversal

Given an undirected graph
G=(V,E)
,
a vertex subset
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
is called
an
OCT
if and only if

V^{\prime}
has a nonempty intersection
with every odd cycle in
G
.
In other words, the induced subgraph

G[V\setminus V^{\prime}]
is a bipartite graph.
The minimum OCT problem finds
an OCT
with the smallest cardinality.

Given an OCT
V^{\prime}
of
G=(V,E)
,
we can construct a map

\alpha:V\rightarrow(0,1,2)

(where
(0,1,2)
is an ordered triplet
of 3-set
\{0,1,2\}
)
satisfying that

\alpha^{-1}(0)=V^{\prime}
and
both
\alpha^{-1}(1)
and
\alpha^{-1}(2)

are independent sets of
G.

Here, we note that this construction is not unique.
Conversely, if we have a map

\alpha:V\rightarrow(0,1,2)

satisfying the condition that

both
\alpha^{-1}(1)
and
\alpha^{-1}(2)

are independent sets of
G,

(1)

then
\alpha^{-1}(0)
becomes an OCT.
We call such a map an
OCT-map
.
Throughout this paper,
we represent an OCT
by an OCT-map
\alpha:V\rightarrow(0,1,2)
.

we consider an auxiliary graph of

\tau

The following is our main theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\tau
be a given timetable and

B_{\min}(\tau)
the optimal value
of the BMP
defined by
\tau.

Then,
B_{\min}(\tau)
is equal to
the size of the minimum OCT
of the auxiliary graph
G(\tau).

First, we show that
an HA-assignment consistent with
a given timetable
\tau

gives an OCT-map of auxiliary graph
G(\tau).

Let
\mathbf{Z}
be an HA-assignment consistent
with
\tau.

We construct a map

\alpha_{\mathbf{Z}}:\hat{V}\rightarrow(0,1,2)
by

\alpha_{\mathbf{Z}}(t,s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&(\mbox{if }z_{t,s}={\tt H}\mbox{ and }z_{t,s+1}={\tt A}),\\
2&(\mbox{if }z_{t,s}={\tt A}\mbox{ and }z_{t,s+1}={\tt H}),\\
0&(\mbox{otherwise}).\end{array}\right.

Lemma 3.2
.

The map
\alpha_{\mathbf{Z}}
is an OCT-map,
where
|\alpha_{\mathbf{Z}}^{-1}(0)|
, the size of the OCT, is equal to
B_{\min}(\tau)
.

Proof.

We first show that
\alpha_{\mathbf{Z}}^{-1}(1)
is
a independent set of
G(\tau).

Let us assume that there exists
an edge
(v,w)

whose endpoints belong to
\alpha_{\mathbf{Z}}^{-1}(1)
.
The edge
(v,w)
belongs to
\hat{E}_{H},\hat{E}_{0}
, or
\hat{E}_{1}
.
If it belongs to
\hat{E}_{H}
, then
there exists a
(t,s)\in\hat{V}
such that
(v,w)=\{(t,s-1),(t,s)\}
.
Then,

z_{t,s}={\tt A}
follows from
\alpha_{\mathbf{Z}}(v)=1
and

z_{t,s}={\tt H}
follows from
\alpha_{\mathbf{Z}}(w)=1
.
This is a contradiction.
If it belongs to
\hat{E}_{0}
, then
there exists
(s,t_{1},t_{2})\in S^{-}\times T\times T

such that
(v,w)=\{(t_{1},s),(t_{2},s)\}
.
Then,

z_{t_{1},s}={\tt H}
follows from
\alpha_{\mathbf{Z}}(v)=1
and

z_{t_{2},s}={\tt H}
follows from
\alpha_{\mathbf{Z}}(w)=1
.
This is also a contradiction.
The same occurs for the case belonging to
\hat{E}_{1}
.
Therefore,

\alpha_{\mathbf{Z}}^{-1}(1)
is a independent set.
Similarly,

\alpha_{\mathbf{Z}}^{-1}(2)
is a independent set,
and

\alpha_{\mathbf{Z}}
is an OCT-map of
G(\tau)
from the definition.
The size of the OCT,
|\alpha_{\mathbf{Z}}^{-1}(0)|
, is
equal to
B_{\min}(\tau)
.

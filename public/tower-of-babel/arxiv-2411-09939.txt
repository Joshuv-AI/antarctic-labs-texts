Self-similar groupoid actions on k k -graphs, and invariance of K K -theory for cocycle homotopies Thanks: This research was supported by Australian Research Council grant DP220101631. The first author was supported by University of Wollongong AEGiS CONNECT grant 141765
Alexander Mundey, Aidan Sims

Abstract
. We establish conditions under which an inclusion of finitely aligned left-cancellative small categories induces inclusions of twisted C ∗ C^{*} -algebras. We also present an example of an inclusion of finitely aligned left-cancellative monoids that does not induce a homomorphism even between (untwisted) Toeplitz algebras. We prove that the twisted C ∗ C^{*} -algebras of a jointly faithful self-similar action of a countable discrete amenable groupoid on a row-finite k k -graph with no sources, with respect to homotopic cocycles, have isomorphic K K -theory.

1.
Introduction

In this article, we establish that for self-similar actions of amenable groupoids on
k
-graphs, the
K
-theory of the twisted
C^{*}
-algebras is invariant under
2
-cocycle homotopy.

It is a recurring theme that the
K
-theory of twisted
C^{*}
-algebras is invariant under homotopies of
2
-cocycles (see for example
[
9
,
8
,
10
,
16
]
).
An early result of this nature is due to Elliott
[
9
]
, who showed that the
K
-theory of each rank-
n
noncommutative torus—which can be regarded as a twisted group
C^{*}
-algebra of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
—is isomorphic to the
K
-theory of the (untwisted) group
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})\cong C(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Elliott’s proof involves inducting on the dimension
n
, using a five-lemma argument based on naturality of the Pimsner–Voiculescu exact sequence. We employ a technique based on Elliott’s argument to prove our main theorem.

In
[
22
]
we introduced twisted
C^{*}
-algebras for self-similar groupoid actions on
k
-graphs. A
2
-cocycle
\sigma
for a self-similar action of a groupoid
\mathcal{G}
on a
k
-graph
\Lambda
is, by definition, a categorical
2
-cocycle on the Zappa–Szép product category
\Lambda\bowtie\mathcal{G}
. The corresponding twisted
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(\Lambda\bowtie\mathcal{G},\sigma)
agrees, in the untwisted case, with the algebras of
[
23
,
18
,
1
]
. It is natural to ask about invariance of
K
-theory as
\sigma
varies continuously. Our main theorem can be summarised as follows.

Theorem

(
Theorem

4.13
)
.

Fix
k\geq 0
. Suppose that
\mathcal{G}
is a countable discrete amenable groupoid acting self-similarly and jointly faithfully on a row-finite
k
-graph
\Lambda
with no sources. If
\sigma_{0}
and
\sigma_{1}
are homotopic
2
-cocycles on
\Lambda\bowtie\mathcal{G}
, then

K_{*}(C^{*}(\Lambda\bowtie\mathcal{G},\sigma_{0}))\cong K_{*}(C^{*}(\Lambda\bowtie\mathcal{G},\sigma_{1})).

Indeed, like Elliott, we prove the stronger statement that the twisted
C^{*}
-algebras for the cocycles along the homotopy assemble into a
C([0,1])
-algebra for which the point-evaluation
C^{*}
-homomorphisms all induce isomorphisms in
K
-theory. As in Elliott’s argument, we induct on
k
. However, we use the Pimsner exact sequence for Cuntz–Pimsner algebras
[
25
]
rather than the Pimsner–Voiculescu sequence for crossed products
[
26
]
. In particular, we show that the twisted
C^{*}
-algebra

, with
\Lambda
a
(k+1)
-graph, can be realised as the Cuntz–Pimsner algebra of a
C^{*}
-correspondence over

, where
\Gamma\subseteq\Lambda
is a sub-
k
-graph.

To do so, we investigate how inclusions of finitely aligned left-cancellative small categories correspond to inclusions of twisted versions of the associated
C^{*}
-algebras introduced by Spielberg
[
33
]
. This turns out to be more complicated than we expected. We give an example (

3.23
) of an inclusion of finitely aligned left-cancellative monoids that does not induce a

-homomorphism between the associated Toeplitz algebras.

The paper is structured as follows.
Section

2
contains preliminary material on self-similar actions and Zappa–Szép products. In
Section

we introduce twisted
C^{*}
-algebras of finitely aligned left-cancellative small categories, generalising the untwisted algebras of Spielberg

-algebras. In

-algebra of a finitely aligned left-cancellative small category twisted by a homotopy of

3.1.
Finitely aligned left-cancellative small categories

A small category
\mathcal{C}
is
left-cancellative
if whenever
a,b,c\in\mathcal{C}
satisfy
s(a)=r(b)=r(c)
,

ac=ab\hskip 10.00002pt\text{implies}\hskip 10.00002ptc=b.

Given a left-cancellative small category
\mathcal{C}
, we define an equivalence relation on
\mathcal{C}
by

a\sim b\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ if and only if}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ there is an invertible }c\in\mathcal{C}\text{ such that }a=bc.

Equivalently,
a\sim b
if and only if
a\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{C}=b\mathcal{C}
. That is,
a
and
b
generate the same principal right ideals.

We extend the notion of equivalence to subsets of
\mathcal{C}
. If
A,B\subseteq\mathcal{C}
then we say that
A\sim B
if for every
a\in A
there exists
b\in B
such that
a\sim b
and for every
b\in B
there exists
a\in A
such that
b\sim a
. If
A\sim B
, then

(3.1)

\bigcup_{a\in A}a\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{C}=\bigcup_{b\in B}b\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{C},

but the converse does not typically hold.

If
a,a^{\prime}\in A
satisfy
a\in a^{\prime}\mathcal{C}
then
a\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{C}\subseteq a^{\prime}\mathcal{C}
. We say that
A\subseteq\mathcal{C}
is
independent
if for all distinct
a,a^{\prime}\in A
we have
a\notin a^{\prime}\mathcal{C}
.

Lemma 3.1

(cf.
[
3
, p. 1350]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a left-cancellative small category.
If
A,B\subseteq\mathcal{C}
are independent and (
3.1
) holds, then
A\sim B
, and
|A|=|B|
.

Proof.

Fix
a\in A
. Since (
3.1
) holds, there exists
b\in B
such that
a\in b\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{C}
and there exists an
a^{\prime}\in A
such that
b\in a^{\prime}\mathcal{C}
. Hence,
a\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{C}\subseteq b\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{C}\subseteq a^{\prime}\mathcal{C}
. Independence of
A
implies that
a=a^{\prime}
so
a\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{C}=b\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{C}
and therefore
a\sim b
. If
b^{\prime}\in B
also satisfies
a\sim b^{\prime}
then
b\sim b^{\prime}
and independence of
B
implies that
b=b^{\prime}
. So there is a unique function
a\mapsto b_{a}
from
A
to
B
such that
a\sim b_{a}
for all
a\in A
.
A symmetric argument yields a function
b\mapsto a_{b}
from
B\to A

such that
b\sim a_{b}
for all
b\in B
, and this function is inverse to
a\mapsto b_{a}
.
∎

We say that a left-cancellative small category
\mathcal{C}
is
finitely aligned
if for all
a,b\in\mathcal{C}
there is a finite subset
F\subseteq\mathcal{C}
such that

(3.2)

a\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{C}\cap b\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{C}=\bigcup_{c\in F}c\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{C}\eqqcolon F\mathcal{C}.

Since
F
is finite, by passing to a subset we can assume that
F
is independent, and therefore unique up to equivalence by
Lemma

3.1
. In this paper, we work exclusively with finitely aligned left-cancellative small categories.

If
\mathcal{C}
is finitely aligned, an induction on
|A|
shows that for any finite set
A\subseteq\mathcal{C}
there is a finite independent set
F\subseteq\mathcal{C}
, unique up to equivalence, such that

\bigcap_{a\in A}a\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{C}=\bigcup_{c\in F}c\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{C}.

Following
[
3
]
we write
\bigvee A
for a choice of such a finite independent set
F
. If
a,b\in\mathcal{C}
then we write
a\vee b
instead of
\bigvee\{a,b\}
.

Let
v\in\mathcal{C}^{0}
. A subset
A\subseteq v\mathcal{C}
is
exhaustive
if for every
c\in v\mathcal{C}
there exists
a\in A
such that
c\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{C}\cap a\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{C}\neq\varnothing
.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
3
, Lemma 2.3]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a left-cancellative small category. For
v\in\mathcal{C}^{0}
, if
A\subseteq v\mathcal{C}
is exhaustive and
A\sim B
, then
B\subseteq v\mathcal{C}
is exhaustive.

Example 3.3
.

Every groupoid (and hence every group)
\mathcal{G}
is a finitely aligned left-cancellative small category since
g\mathcal{G}=r(g)\mathcal{G}
for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
.

Example 3.4
.

The path category
E^{*}
of a directed graph
E
is a finitely aligned left-cancellative small category.
All
k
-graphs are left cancellative: if
\Lambda
is a
k
-graph and
\mu,\nu,\lambda\in\Lambda
satisfy
\mu\lambda=\nu\lambda=:\gamma
, then in particular

d(\mu)+d(\lambda)=d(\gamma)=d(\nu)+d(\lambda)
, so the uniqueness condition in the factorisation
property for
\gamma
implies that
\mu
and
\nu
are equal. For
k\geq 2
some
k
-graphs are

not
finitely aligned (
[
28
, Examples 3.1 and 5.2]
). However, all row-finite
k
-graphs are finitely aligned: if
\Lambda
is a row-finite
k
-graph and
\mu,\nu\in\Lambda
, then the collection of
minimal common extensions
of
\mu
and
\nu
,

(3.3)

\MCE(\mu,\nu)\coloneqq\mu\Lambda\cap\nu\Lambda\cap\Lambda^{d(\mu)\vee d(\nu)}

is finite and
\mu\Lambda\cap\nu\Lambda=\MCE(\mu,\nu)\Lambda
by the factorisation property.

Lemma 3.5
.

Fix
S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}
and let
\mathbb{N}^{S}\coloneqq\{n\in\mathbb{N}^{k}\mid n_{i}=0\text{ for }i\not\in S\}
. Let
\Lambda
be a finitely aligned
k
-graph. Then
\Lambda^{\mathbb{N}^{S}}
is finitely aligned.

Proof.

Fix
\alpha,\beta\in\Lambda^{\mathbb{N}^{S}}
. Then
\alpha\vee\beta
, as calculated in
\Lambda
, is a subset of
\Lambda^{\mathbb{N}^{S}}
and hence is equal to
\alpha\vee\beta
as calculated in
\Lambda^{\mathbb{N}^{S}}
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid and let
\mathcal{C}
be a finitely aligned left-cancellative small category such that
(\mathcal{G},\mathcal{C})
is a matched pair. Then
\mathcal{C}\bowtie\mathcal{G}
is left cancellative and finitely aligned.

Proof.

The left-cancellativity of
\mathcal{C}\bowtie\mathcal{G}
follows from
[
22
, Lemma 3.15]
since every element of
\mathcal{G}
is invertible.

For finite alignment let
cg\in\mathcal{C}\bowtie\mathcal{G}
. Since
g
is invertible we have
cg(\mathcal{C}\bowtie\mathcal{G})=c(\mathcal{C}\bowtie\mathcal{G})
. Fix
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathcal{C}
and suppose that
c_{1}(\mathcal{C}\bowtie\mathcal{G})\cap c_{2}(\mathcal{C}\bowtie\mathcal{G})\neq\varnothing
. Fix
c_{1}c_{1}^{\prime}g_{1}=c_{2}c_{2}^{\prime}g_{2}\in\mathcal{C}\bowtie\mathcal{G}
.
Uniqueness of factorisation gives
c_{1}c_{1}^{\prime}=c_{2}c_{2}^{\prime}
, so
c_{1}\mathcal{C}\cap c_{2}\mathcal{C}\neq\varnothing
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is finitely aligned, there are a finite subset
F\subseteq\mathcal{C}
and
c\in F
such that
c_{1}c_{1}^{\prime}=c_{2}c_{2}^{\prime}=cc^{\prime}
for some
c^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
. Hence,
cc^{\prime}g_{1}=cc^{\prime}g_{2}
and it follows that
c_{1}(\mathcal{C}\bowtie\mathcal{G})\cap c_{2}(\mathcal{C}\bowtie\mathcal{G})=\bigcup_{c\in F}c(\mathcal{C}\bowtie\mathcal{G})
.
∎

3.2.
Twisted
C^{*}
-algebras

To introduce twisted
C^{*}
-algebras of small categories we need to recall the notion of a
\mathbb{T}
-valued
2
-cocycle.

Definition 3.7
.

A
\mathbb{T}
-valued
2
-cocycle
on a category
\mathcal{C}
is a map
\sigma\colon\mathcal{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{T}
such that for every
(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3})\in\mathcal{C}^{3}
,

\sigma(c_{2},c_{3})\sigma(c_{1},c_{2}c_{3})=\sigma(c_{1},c_{2})\sigma(c_{1}c_{2},c_{3}),

and such that
\sigma(r(c),c)=1=\sigma(c,s(c))
for all
c\in\mathcal{C}
.

Remark 3.8
.

The condition
\sigma(r(c),c)=1=\sigma(c,s(c))
for all
c\in\mathcal{C}
is often emphasised by saying that
\sigma
is
normalised
, but in this paper all cocycles are normalised, so we drop the adjective.

Given a finite family
\mathcal{P}
of pairwise commuting projections in a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
, we write
\bigvee\mathcal{P}
for the smallest projection in
A
that dominates every element of
\mathcal{P}
. Explicitly,

(3.4)

\bigvee\mathcal{P}=\sum_{\varnothing\neq F\subseteq\mathcal{P}}(-1)^{|F|-1}\prod_{P\in F}P.

We take the convention that
\bigvee\varnothing=0
.

Definition 3.9

(cf.
[
3
, Definition 3.1]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a finitely aligned left-cancellative small category and let
\sigma
be a
\mathbb{T}
-valued
2
-cocycle on
\mathcal{C}
. A
\sigma
-twisted representation
of
\mathcal{C}
in a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
is a map
S\colon\mathcal{C}\to A
,
c\mapsto S_{c}
such that each
S_{c}
is a partial isometry and

(R1)

S_{c_{1}}S_{c_{2}}=\delta_{s(c_{1}),r(c_{2})}\sigma(c_{1},c_{2})S_{c_{1}c_{2}}
for all
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathcal{C}
,

(R2)

S_{c}^{*}S_{c}=S_{s(c)}
for all
c\in\mathcal{C}
, and

(R3)

\displaystyle S_{c_{1}}S_{c_{1}}^{*}S_{c_{2}}S_{c_{2}}^{*}=\bigvee_{c\in F}S_{c}S_{c}^{*}
for all
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathcal{C}
and any finite independent set
F
satisfying
c_{1}\mathcal{C}\cap c_{2}\mathcal{C}=F\mathcal{C}
, with the convention that if
c_{1}\mathcal{C}\cap c_{2}\mathcal{C}=\varnothing
, then
F=\varnothing
is such a finite independent set, so
S_{c_{1}}S_{c_{1}}^{*}S_{c_{2}}S_{c_{2}}^{*}=0
.

We say that
S
is
covariant
if, in addition, for all
v\in\mathcal{C}^{0}
,

(R4)

\displaystyle S_{v}=\bigvee_{c\in F}S_{c}S_{c}^{*}
for all finite exhaustive
F\subseteq v\mathcal{C}
.

Remark 3.10
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is a finitely aligned left-cancellative small category and
\sigma
is a
\mathbb{T}
-valued
2
-cocycle on
\mathcal{C}
. Let
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathcal{C}
and let
F
be a finite set generating
c_{1}\mathcal{C}\cap c_{2}\mathcal{C}
. As discussed immediately following
3.2
there is an independent subset
G
of
F
that generates
c_{1}\mathcal{C}\cap c_{2}\mathcal{C}
. If
c\in F
then there exists
c^{\prime}\in G
such that
c\in c^{\prime}\mathcal{C}
and hence
S_{c}S_{c}^{*}\leq S_{c^{\prime}}S_{c^{\prime}}^{*}
by
(R1)
. Hence,
\bigvee_{c\in F}S_{c}S_{c}^{*}=\bigvee_{c\in G}S_{c}S_{c}^{*}=S_{c_{1}}S_{c_{1}}^{*}S_{c_{2}}S_{c_{2}}^{*}
by
(R3)
.

We consider two twisted
C^{*}
-algebras associated to each
2
-cocycle on a finitely aligned left-cancellative small category. A standard argument following the lines of
[
22
, Proposition 7.4]
or an appeal to
[
20
]
establishes that such
C^{*}
-algebras exist.

Definition 3.11
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a finitely aligned left-cancellative small category, and let
\sigma
be a
2
-cocycle on
\mathcal{C}
. The
\sigma
-twisted Toeplitz algebra of
\mathcal{C}
is the
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{T}C^{*}(\mathcal{C},\sigma)
generated by a universal
\sigma
-twisted representation
t\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{T}C^{*}(\mathcal{C},\sigma)
; that is,
\mathcal{T}C^{*}(\mathcal{C},\sigma)=C^{*}(\{t_{c}\mid c\in\mathcal{C}\})
, and for any
\sigma
-twisted representation
S\colon\mathcal{C}\to A
there is a unique
*
-homomorphism
\Phi\colon\mathcal{T}C^{*}(\mathcal{C},\sigma)\to A
such that
S=\Phi\circ t
.

The
\sigma
-twisted
C^{*}
-algebra of
\mathcal{C}
is the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(\mathcal{C},\sigma)
generated by a universal
\sigma
-twisted covariant representation
s\colon\mathcal{C}\to C^{*}(\mathcal{C},\sigma)
. Let
I
be the ideal of
\mathcal{T}C^{*}(\mathcal{C},\sigma)
generated by
\{S_{v}-\bigvee_{c\in F}S_{c}S_{c}^{*}\mid v\in\mathcal{C}^{0},F\subseteq v\mathcal{C}\text{ is finite exhaustive}\}
. Then
C^{*}(\mathcal{C},\sigma)\cong\mathcal{T}C^{*}(\mathcal{C},\sigma)/I
.

The “untwisted” algebra
C^{*}(\mathcal{C},1)
of a countable finitely aligned left-cancellative small category coincides with the groupoid
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(G_{2}|_{\partial\Lambda})
of
[
33
, Theorem 10.15]
.

Remark 3.12

(Notational conventions for representations of categories)
.

There are numerous different representations of a category
\mathcal{C}
appearing in this paper, so, taking on board the comments of a helpful anonymous referee, we have tried to adopt a consistent convention. We denote generic families of partial isometries indexed by elements of the category
\mathcal{C}
and satisfying
(R2)
and
(R3)
and some analogue of
(R1)
by
S
throughout. But for universal families we use lower-case letters, and different letters and fonts as follows:
t
is for families twisted by a cocycle as in
(R1)
that do not (necessarily) satisfy
(R4)
;
s
is for those that are required to satisfy
(R4)
. When we introduce universal representations twisted by a continuous family of cocycles in
Section

4
, we use fraktur font (
\mathfrak{t}
and
\mathfrak{s}
) to distinguish them.

Let
\mathcal{M}(A)
denote the multiplier algebra of a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
, and let
\mathcal{U}\mathcal{Z}\mathcal{M}(A)
denote the group of unitaries in the center of the multiplier algebra.
The following is a generalisation of
[
3
, Lemma 3.4]
.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a finitely aligned left-cancellative small category and let
A
be a
C^{*}
-algebra. Suppose that
S\colon\mathcal{C}\to A
satisfies
(R2)
of
Definition

3.9
, and that
\omega\colon\mathcal{C}^{2}\to\mathcal{U}\mathcal{Z}\mathcal{M}(A)
satisfies
S_{c_{1}}S_{c_{2}}=\omega(c_{1},c_{2})S_{c_{1}c_{2}}
for all
(c_{1},c_{2})\in\mathcal{C}^{2}
. Then

(i)

S_{r(c)}S_{c}=S_{c}=S_{c}S_{s(c)}
for all
c\in\mathcal{C}
;

(ii)

for every invertible
c\in\mathcal{C}
we have
S_{c^{-1}}={\omega(c,c^{-1})}S_{c}^{*}
and
S_{c}S_{c}^{*}=S_{r(c)}
;

(iii)

if
a\sim b
in
\mathcal{C}
then
S_{a}S_{a}^{*}=S_{b}S_{b}^{*}
; and

(iv)

if
A,B\subseteq\mathcal{C}
satisfy
A\sim B
, then

\bigvee_{a\in A}S_{a}S_{a}^{*}=\bigvee_{b\in B}S_{b}S_{b}^{*}.

Proof.

(i)
First note that for
v\in\mathcal{C}^{0}
, the condition
(R2)
implies first that
S_{v}
is self-adjoint, and then that
S_{v}
is idempotent, hence a projection. Consequently, for
c\in\mathcal{C}
, we have

S_{c}=\omega(r(c),c)^{*}S_{r(c)}S_{c}=\omega(r(c),c)^{*}S_{r(c)}^{2}S_{c}=S_{r(c)}S_{c}
. A symmetric argument gives
S_{c}=S_{c}S_{s(c)}
.

(ii)
Suppose that
c\in\mathcal{C}
is invertible. Then

\displaystyle S_{c^{-1}}

\displaystyle=S_{s(c)c^{-1}}=S_{c}^{*}S_{c}S_{c^{-1}}

\displaystyle=\omega(c,c^{-1})S_{c}^{*}S_{r(c)}=\omega(c,c^{-1})(S_{r(c)}S_{c})^{*}=\omega(c,c^{-1})S_{c}^{*}.

It follows that

S_{c}S_{c}^{*}={\omega(c,c^{-1})}^{*}S_{c}S_{c^{-1}}={\omega(c,c^{-1})}^{*}\omega(c,c^{-1})S_{r(c)}=S_{r(c)}.

(iii)
Suppose that
a\sim b
. Then there exists an invertible
c\in\mathcal{C}
such that
a=bc
. Now,

S_{a}S_{a}^{*}=S_{bc}S_{bc}^{*}={\omega(b,c)}^{*}{\omega(b,c)}S_{b}S_{c}S_{c}^{*}S_{b}^{*}=S_{b}S_{r(c)}S_{b}^{*}=S_{b}S_{b}^{*}.

(iv)
This follows immediately from
(iii)
.
∎

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\Lambda)
be a self-similar action of a groupoid
\mathcal{G}
on a row-finite
k
-graph
\Lambda
with no sources, let
\mathcal{C}\coloneqq\Lambda\bowtie\mathcal{G}
be the associated Zappa–Szép product category. For
\mu,\nu\in\Lambda
we have

\mu\mathcal{C}\cap\nu\mathcal{C}=\MCE(\mu,\nu)\mathcal{C}.

Let
A
be a
C^{*}
-algebra, and suppose that
S\colon\mathcal{C}\to A
satisfies
(R2)
of
Definition

3.9
, and that
\omega\colon\mathcal{C}^{2}\to\mathcal{U}\mathcal{Z}\mathcal{M}(A)
satisfies
S_{c_{1}}S_{c_{2}}=\omega(c_{1},c_{2})S_{c_{1}c_{2}}
for all
(c_{1},c_{2})\in\mathcal{C}^{2}
. Then

(i)

S
satisfies
(TCK3)
if and only if it satisfies
(R3)
; and

(ii)

S
satisfies
(CK)
and if and only if it satisfies both
(R3)
and
(R4)
.

Proof.

Fix
\mu,\nu\in\Lambda
. By
3.3
we have
\MCE(\mu,\nu)\subseteq r(\mu)\Lambda^{d(\mu)\vee d(\nu)}
. This is a finite set because
\Lambda
is row-finite.
Fix
c\in\mu\mathcal{C}\cap\nu\mathcal{C}
. We can write
c=\mu\mu^{\prime}g=\nu\nu^{\prime}h
for some
\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}\in\Lambda
and
g,h\in\mathcal{G}
. Uniqueness of factorisations in
\mathcal{C}=\Lambda\bowtie\mathcal{G}
implies that
\mu\mu^{\prime}=\nu\nu^{\prime}
. In particular,
d(\mu\mu^{\prime})=d(\nu\nu^{\prime})\geq d(\mu)\vee d(\nu)
. By the factorisation property in
\Lambda
, we can write
\mu^{\prime}=\mu^{\prime\prime}\tau
and
\nu^{\prime}=\nu^{\prime\prime}\rho
with
d(\mu^{\prime\prime})=(d(\mu)\vee d(\nu))-d(\mu)
and
d(\nu^{\prime\prime})=(d(\mu)\vee d(\nu))-d(\nu)
, and then
\mu\mu^{\prime\prime}\tau=\nu\nu^{\prime\prime}\rho
with
d(\mu\mu^{\prime\prime})=d(\nu\nu^{\prime\prime})=d(\mu)\vee d(\nu)
. Uniqueness of factorisations in
\Lambda
gives
\mu\mu^{\prime\prime}=\nu\nu^{\prime\prime}\in\MCE(\mu,\nu)
. Hence
c=\mu\mu^{\prime}g=\mu\mu^{\prime\prime}(\tau g)\in\MCE(\mu,\nu)\mathcal{C}
. That is,
\mu\mathcal{C}\cap\nu\mathcal{C}=\MCE(\mu,\nu)\mathcal{C}
.

For
(i)
, first suppose that
S
satisfies
(R3)
.
By
(R3)
,
Remark

3.10
, and the first paragraph, we have
S_{\mu}S^{*}_{\mu}S_{\nu}S_{\nu}^{*}=\bigvee_{\lambda\in\MCE(\mu,\nu)}S_{\lambda}S^{*}_{\lambda}

\eta,\zeta\in\MCE(\mu,\nu)
are distinct, then

\Lambda
implies that
\eta\Lambda\cap\zeta\Lambda=\varnothing
. Hence
(R3)
implies that
S_{\eta}S^{*}_{\eta}S_{\zeta}S^{*}_{\zeta}=0

S_{\lambda}S^{*}_{\lambda}
,
\lambda\in\MCE(\mu,\nu)
are mutually orthogonal, so that
\bigvee_{\lambda\in\MCE(\mu,\nu)}S_{\lambda}S^{*}_{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\in\MCE(\mu,\nu)}S_{\lambda}S^{*}_{\lambda}
. We deduce that
S
satisfies
(TCK3)
.

Now suppose that
S
satisfies
(TCK3)
. Let
c=\mu g\in\mathcal{C}
with
\mu\in\Lambda
and
g\in\mathcal{G}
. Using

and that

\mathcal{U}\mathcal{Z}\mathcal{M}(A)

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}c\,\mathcal{C}&=\mu g\,\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mu\,\mathcal{C}=\mu gg^{-1}\,\mathcal{C}\subseteq c\,\mathcal{C}\quad\text{and}\\
S_{c}S^{*}_{c}&=\omega(\mu,g)^{*}\omega(\mu,g)S_{\mu}S_{g}S^{*}_{g}S^{*}_{\mu}=S_{\mu}S^{*}_{\mu}.\end{split}

Fix
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathcal{C}
and a finite independent set
F
such that
c_{1}\mathcal{C}\cap c_{2}\mathcal{C}=F\mathcal{C}
. Write
c_{1}=\mu g
and
c_{2}=\nu h
with
\mu,\nu\in\Lambda
and

, and for each

write
c=\lambda_{c}g_{c}

4.
Homotopy of cocycles

We are interested in the effect on
C^{*}(\mathcal{C},\sigma)
of continuous variations in
\sigma
. To this end we introduce the following notion.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathbb{I}\coloneqq[0,1]
. A
homotopy of 2-cocycles
on a small category
\mathcal{C}
is a continuous map
\Sigma\colon\mathbb{I}\times\mathcal{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{T}
,
(t,c_{1},c_{2})\mapsto\Sigma_{t}(c_{1},c_{2})
whose restriction
\Sigma_{t}
to
\{t\}\times\mathcal{C}^{2}
is a 2-cocycle on
\mathcal{C}
for each
t\in\mathbb{I}
. We say that the
2
-cocycles
\Sigma_{0}
and
\Sigma_{1}
are
homotopic
. For each
(c_{1},c_{2})\in\mathcal{C}^{2}
we let
\Sigma_{\bullet}(c_{1},c_{2})
denote the continuous function
t\mapsto\Sigma_{t}(c_{1},c_{2})
on
\mathbb{I}
.

4.1.
Twisting representations by homotopies of
2
-cocycles

We consider representations of categories twisted by homotopies of
2
-cocycles. The idea is that such a representation should be thought of as a bundle of representations of the category twisted by continuously varying
2
-cocycles.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a finitely aligned left-cancellative small category and let
\Sigma
be a homotopy of
2
-cocycles for
\mathcal{C}
. A
\Sigma
-twisted representation
of
\mathcal{C}
in a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
is a pair
(S,\zeta)
consisting of a map
S\colon\mathcal{C}\to A
,
c\mapsto S_{c}
such that each
S_{c}
is a partial isometry, together with a
*
-homomorphism
\zeta_{v}\colon C(\mathbb{I})\to A
for each

, such that
S
satisfies
(R2)
and
(R3)
of
Definition

3.9
, the
\zeta_{v}
have mutually orthogonal images, and

(IR1)

\zeta_{v}(1_{\mathbb{I}})=S_{v}
for all
v\in\mathcal{C}^{0}
,

(IR2)

\zeta_{r(c)}(f)S_{c}=S_{c}\zeta_{s(c)}(f)
for all
c\in\mathcal{C}
and

, and

(IR3)

S_{c_{1}}S_{c_{2}}=\zeta_{r(c_{1})}\big(\Sigma_{\bullet}(c_{1},c_{2})\big)S_{c_{1}c_{2}}
for all
(c_{1},c_{2})\in\mathcal{C}^{2}

We say that

is

satisfies

.

Given a

-twisted representation

of

-algebra generated by

-subalgebra of

-algebra

-algebra generated by a

-twisted representation

is a

Proposition 4.5

(cf.

.

. Let

Proof.

We follow the approach of
[

. Because the projections
S_{x}S_{x}^{*}
for

pairwise commute,
\mathcal{P}
consists of projections and is closed under multiplication. Let

. Then

is a linear subspace of

and

of

contains the generators of

and their adjoints.

It suffices to show that

is
*
-closed and closed under multiplication. Let
\mathcal{M}
denote the set
\{S_{c_{1}}S_{d_{1}}^{*}\cdots S_{c_{k}}S_{d_{k}}^{*}\mid c_{i},d_{i}\in\mathcal{C},k\geq 1\}
of monomials in the

and their adjoints.
We first show that
\mathcal{M}\subseteq L
. We will say that a monomial
S_{c_{1}}S_{d_{1}}^{*}\cdots S_{c_{k}}S_{d_{k}}^{*}
has length

.

Monomials of length one lie in

since each

and each

.
Fix a nonzero monomial

of length two.
We claim that there exist

, and

such that

S_{b}^{*}S_{c}=\sum_{i}S_{u_{i}}S_{v_{i}}^{*}P_{i}.

Let
F
be a finite independent set generating

, and write
F=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\}
. Then

(4.1)

S_{b}^{*}S_{c}=S_{b}^{*}S_{b}S_{b}^{*}S_{c}S_{c}^{*}S_{c}=S_{b}^{*}\Big(\bigvee_{i=1}^{k}S_{x_{i}}S_{x_{i}}^{*}\Big)S_{c}.

Given commuting projections

we have

\bigvee_{i=1}^{k}Q_{i}=Q_{1}+Q_{2}(1-Q_{1})+Q_{3}(1-Q_{1}\vee Q_{2})+\cdots+Q_{k}\Big(1-\bigvee_{j=1}^{k-1}Q_{j}\Big).

Applying this with

gives

(4.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bigvee_{i=1}^{k}S_{x_{i}}S_{x_{i}}^{*}&=\sum_{i=1}^{k}S_{x_{i}}S_{x_{i}}^{*}\Big(1-\bigvee_{j=1}^{i-1}S_{x_{j}}S_{x_{j}}^{*}\Big)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{k}S_{x_{i}}S_{x_{i}}^{*}\Big(1-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}(-1)^{j-1}\sum_{1\leq r_{1}<\cdots<r_{j}<i}S_{x_{r_{1}}}S_{x_{r_{1}}}^{*}\cdots S_{x_{r_{j}}}S_{x_{r_{j}}}^{*}\Big).\end{split}

Since each

, for each

there exist

such that
x_{i}=bb_{i}=cc_{i}
. So,

\displaystyle S_{b}^{*}S_{x_{i}}S_{x_{i}}^{*}S_{c}

\displaystyle=S_{b}^{*}S_{b}S_{b_{i}}S_{c_{i}}^{*}S_{c}^{*}S_{c}=S_{b_{i}}S_{c_{i}}^{*}\in L\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}

\displaystyle S_{c}^{*}S_{x_{i}}S_{x_{i}}^{*}S_{c}

\displaystyle=S_{c}^{*}S_{c}S_{c_{i}}S_{c_{i}}^{*}S_{c}^{*}S_{c}=S_{c_{i}}S_{c_{i}}^{*}\in L.

Since each
x_{r_{j}}\in c\mkern 1.0mu\mathcal{C}
we have
S_{x_{r_{j}}}S_{x_{r_{j}}}^{*}\leq S_{c}S_{c}^{*}
, so

\displaystyle S_{b}^{*}S_{x_{r_{1}}}S_{x_{r_{1}}}^{*}\cdots S_{x_{r_{j}}}S_{x_{r_{j}}}^{*}S_{c}

4.1
and
4.2
give the claim.

By the claim, there exist

, and
P_{i}\in\mathcal{P}
such that

\displaystyle=S_{c_{1}}S_{d_{1}}^{*}\sum_{i}\zeta_{r(a_{i})}(f_{i})S_{a_{i}}S_{b_{i}}^{*}P_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\zeta_{r(c_{1})}(f_{i})S_{c_{1}}S_{d_{1}}^{*}S_{a_{i}}S_{b_{i}}^{*}P_{i}.

is closed under multiplication and

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
(S,\zeta)
be a
\Sigma
-twisted representation of a finitely aligned left-cancellative small category
\mathcal{C}
in a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
. The net

\Big(\sum_{v\in F}S_{v}\Big)_{F\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{0}\text{ finite}}=\Big(\sum_{v\in F}\zeta_{v}(1_{\mathbb{I}})\Big)_{F\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{0}\text{ finite}}

is an approximate identity for
C^{*}(S,\zeta)
.

Proof.

Fix
a\in C^{*}(S,\zeta)
and
\varepsilon>0
. By
Proposition

4.5
there exist a finite
F\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{0}
and
a_{0}\in\lsp\{\zeta_{r(c)}(g)S_{c}S_{d}^{*}P\mid r(c)\in F,s(c)=s(d),g\in C(\mathbb{I}),P\in\mathcal{P}\}
such that
\|a-a_{0}\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2\|f\|}
. If
v\in\mathcal{C}^{0}\setminus F
, then
S_{v}a_{0}=0
. Fix a finite
G\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{0}
such that
F\subseteq G
. Since the
S_{v}
are mutually orthogonal,
\sum_{v\in G}S_{v}
is a projection, and since
a_{0}\in\lsp\{\zeta_{r(c)}(g)S_{c}S_{d}^{*}P\mid r(c)\in F,s(c)=s(d),g\in C(\mathbb{I}),P\in\mathcal{P}\}
, we have
\sum_{v\in G}S_{v}a_{0}=a_{0}
. Using these facts at the second and third inequalities, we calculate:

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{v\in G}S_{v}a-a\Big\|

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\sum_{v\in G}S_{v}(a-a_{0})\Big\|+\Big\|\big(\sum_{v\in G}S_{v}a_{0}\big)-a\Big\|

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\sum_{v\in G}S_{v}\Big\|\|a-a_{0}\|+\|a_{0}-a\|\leq 2\|a-a_{0}\|=\varepsilon.

Hence,

converges strictly to the identity in

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
(S,\zeta)
be a
\Sigma
-twisted representation of a finitely aligned left-cancellative small category
\mathcal{C}
in a
C^{*}
-algebra
A
. Then there is a unique
*
-homomorphism
\zeta_{\star}
from
C(\mathbb{I})
to the centre
\mathcal{Z}\mathcal{M}(C^{*}(S,\zeta))
of the multiplier algebra of
C^{*}(S,\zeta)
satisfying

\zeta_{\star}(f)S_{v}=\zeta_{v}(f)

for all
v\in\mathcal{C}^{0}
and
f\in C(\mathbb{I})
. This homomorphism is unital and makes
C^{*}(S,\zeta)
into a
C(\mathbb{I})
-algebra.

Proof.

Fix
f\in C(\mathbb{I})
. We show that
\sum_{v}\zeta_{v}(f)
converges strictly to a multiplier
\zeta_{\star}(f)
. If
f=0
this is trivial, so suppose
f\not=0
. Fix
a\in C^{*}(S,\zeta)
and
\varepsilon>0
. By
Corollary

4.6
, there is a finite
F\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{0}
such that for all finite
G\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{0}
containing
F
, we have
\big\|\sum_{v\in G\setminus F}S_{v}a\big\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2\|f\|}
. Since
\zeta_{v}(f)S_{w}=0
if
v\not=w
, it follows that for finite
G\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{0}
containing
F
, we have
\|\sum_{v\in G\setminus F}\zeta_{v}(f)a\|=\|\sum_{v\in G\setminus F}\zeta_{v}(f)\sum_{w\in G\setminus F}S_{w}a\|
. Since
\zeta_{v}(C(\mathbb{I}))\zeta_{w}(C(\mathbb{I}))=\{0\}
for
v\neq w
, for any finite
H\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{0}
the universal property of direct sums gives a
*
-homomorphism
\oplus_{H}\zeta_{v}\colon\bigoplus_{v\in H}C(\mathbb{I})\to C^{*}(S,\zeta)
such that
(\oplus_{H}\zeta_{v})((f_{v})_{v\in H})=\sum_{v\in H}\zeta_{v}(f_{v})
. Hence,
\|\sum_{v\in H}\zeta_{v}(f_{v})\|\leq\|(f_{v})_{v\in H}\|=\sup_{v\in H}\|f_{v}\|
.
In particular, if
f\in C(\mathbb{I})
we have
\|\sum_{v\in H}\zeta_{v}(f)\|\leq\|f\|
. Thus

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{v\in G\setminus F}\zeta_{v}(f)\sum_{w\in G\setminus F}S_{w}a\Big\|

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\sum_{v\in G\setminus F}\zeta_{v}(f)\Big\|\,\Big\|\sum_{w\in G\setminus F}S_{w}a\Big\|

\displaystyle<\|f\|\frac{\varepsilon}{2\|f\|}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Hence, the standard
\varepsilon/2
argument shows that the net
(\sum_{v\in F}\zeta_{v}(f)a)_{F\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{0}\text{ finite}}
is Cauchy.
So
(\sum_{v\in F}\zeta_{v}(f))_{F\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{0}\text{ finite}}
converges strictly in
\mathcal{M}(C^{*}(S,\zeta))
to a multiplier
\zeta_{\star}(f)
. Using
(IR1)
(of
Definition

4.2
) and mutual orthogonality of the
S_{v}
we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\zeta_{\star}(f)S_{v}&=\Big(\lim_{F}\sum_{w\in F}\zeta_{w}(f)\Big)S_{v}=\Big(\lim_{F}\sum_{w\in F}\zeta_{w}(f)S_{w}\Big)S_{v}\\
&=\lim_{F}\zeta_{v}(f)S_{v}=\zeta_{v}(f).\end{split}

Since each
\zeta_{v}
is a
*
-homomorphism so is
\zeta_{\star}\colon f\mapsto\zeta_{\star}(f)
.

To see that
\zeta_{\star}(C(\mathbb{I}))
is central, fix
f\in C(\mathbb{I})
. Applying (
4.3
) to
f^{*}
and taking adjoints
gives
S_{v}\zeta_{\star}(f)=\zeta_{v}(f)
. So for
c\in\mathcal{C}
, relation
(IR2)
gives

\zeta_{\star}(f)S_{c}=\zeta_{\star}(f)S_{r(c)}S_{c}=\zeta_{r(c)}(f)S_{c}=S_{c}\zeta_{s(c)}(f)=S_{c}S_{s(c)}\zeta_{\star}(f)=S_{c}\zeta_{\star}(f).

Applying this to
f^{*}
and taking adjoints shows that
\zeta_{\star}(f)S^{*}_{c}=S^{*}_{c}\zeta_{\star}(f)
as well. For
v\in\mathcal{C}^{0}
and
g\in C(\mathbb{I})
, we
have
\zeta_{\star}(f)\zeta_{v}(g)=\zeta_{\star}(f)S_{v}\zeta_{v}(g)=\zeta_{v}(fg)=\zeta_{v}(gf)=\zeta_{v}(g)S_{v}\zeta_{\star}(f)=\zeta_{v}(g)\zeta_{\star}(f)
.
So
\zeta_{\star}(f)
commutes with all of the generators
of
C^{*}(S,\zeta)
. Since
f\in C(\mathbb{I})
was arbitrary, it follows from strict continuity that
\zeta_{\star}(C(\mathbb{I}))
is central in
\mathcal{M}(C^{*}(S,\zeta))
.

We have
\zeta_{\star}(1_{\mathbb{I}})=\sum_{v}\zeta_{v}(1_{\mathbb{I}})=\sum_{v}S_{v}
, so
Corollary

4.6
says that
\zeta_{\star}
is unital. Since a multiplier on a
C^{*}
-algebra is determined uniquely by its action on any approximate identity, and in particular on the approximate identity of
Corollary

4.6
,
\zeta_{\star}
is the unique
*
-homomorphism satisfying the desired relation.
∎

as claimed, and the universal property of

Remark 4.12

, but we could not think of good suggestive terminology that better emphasises this. Note that while the condition is stated in terms of

for all

is constant along homotopies if and only if

is isomorphism for every homotopy

of

Suppose that

is a matched pair of categories.
If

is a homotopy of

is a homotopy of
2
-cocycles on
\mathcal{C}
. We frequently just write

for this restriction. We can now state our main theorem.

.

For any jointly faithful self-similar action of a countable discrete amenable groupoid
\mathcal{G}
on a row-finite

-graph
\Lambda
with no sources,

Our strategy for proving

is to apply Elliott’s inductive Five-Lemma argument
[

]
to the Pimsner exact sequence in

-theory
[

, Theorem 4.8]
. The following proposition provides the base case of the induction.

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a countable discrete amenable groupoid. Then
K_{*}(C^{*}(\mathcal{G},\bullet))
is constant along homotopies.

Proof.

Fix a homotopy
\Sigma
of
2
-cocycles on
\mathcal{G}
and a point

. Choose a subset

of
\mathcal{G}^{0}
that meets each
\mathcal{G}
-orbit exactly once: that is
|X\cap r(\mathcal{G}x)|=1
for all

By the argument of

3.13
, for all

we have

\mathfrak{s}_{g}\mathfrak{s}_{h}^{*}=\mathfrak{z}_{\star}(\Sigma_{\bullet}(g,h^{-1})\overline{\Sigma_{\bullet}(h,h^{-1})})\mathfrak{s}_{gh^{-1}}.

Hence,

implies that

is spanned by elements of the form

where

and

. Fix such an element and a finite subset

. Then

if

, and if

then

whenever

Taking adjoints shows that

An approximation argument similar to the one in

shows that

is a multiplier projection of

. Fix

Since

is full.

Similarly, the characteristic function

is a full multiplier projection of

to multiplier algebras carries

to

Because

induces an isomorphism in
K
-theory.

Since

intersects each
\mathcal{G}
-orbit once, for distinct

if

. So

.
Consequently,

Since

is amenable, each

is canonically isomorphic to the

described in the paragraph following
[

for

By

each

implies that the restriction of

from

to

. Since

∎

Since

is injective and

is a

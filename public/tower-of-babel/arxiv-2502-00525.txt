A Projected Variable Smoothing for Weakly Convex Optimization and Supremum Functions
Sergio López-Rivera, Pedro Pérez-Aros and Emilio Vilches

Abstract
In this paper, we address two main topics. First, we study the problem of minimizing the sum of a smooth function and the composition of a weakly convex function with a linear operator on a closed vector subspace. For this problem, we propose a projected variable smoothing algorithm and establish a complexity bound of 𝒪 ⁡ ( ϵ − 3 ) \mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3}) to achieve an ϵ \epsilon -approximate solution. Second, we investigate the Moreau envelope and the proximity operator of functions defined as the supremum of weakly convex functions, and we compute the proximity operator in two important cases. In addition, we apply the proposed algorithm for solving a distributionally robust optimization problem, the LASSO with linear constraints, and the max dispersion problem. We illustrate numerical results for the max dispersion problem.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following problem

\min_{x\in V}h(x)+g(Ax),

(1)

where
V\subset\mathcal{H}
is a closed vector subspace of a real Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
,
h\colon\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a differentiable function with a Lipschitz gradient,
g
is weakly convex and lower semicontinuous, and
A
is a nonzero bounded linear operator from
\mathcal{H}
to a real Hilbert space
\mathcal{G}
. Whenever
V=\mathcal{H}
, the problem has been studied in
bohm2021variable
. When
g
is convex, it has been analyzed in
MR4534446
. Additionally, if
A=\operatorname{Id}
, the problem can be solved using the algorithm proposed in
briceno2015forward
. If
V=\mathcal{H}
, we can also apply the method proposed in
condat2013primal
in the convex case. The class of weakly convex functions is a generalization of the class of convex functions and there are several classes of functions that are weakly convex. For example, every continuously differentiable piecewise quadratic function defined from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}
is weakly convex. Another example is the composition of a convex Lipschitz continuous function and a smooth function with Lipschitz gradient. We refer to
MR4359983
for more details about this important class of functions.

One of the problems of the form (
1
) is the constrained LASSO problem
tibshirani2011solution
that combines sparse minimization and least squares under linear constraints. Also, problem (
1
) appears in PDEs
(
MR570823
, Section 3)
, signal and image processing
Aujol2006
;
MR1440119
, stochastic traffic theory
MR3703499
, among other fields. In the previous applications, the vector subspace constraint
models intrinsic properties of the solution as non-anticipativity in stochastic problems.

We are interested in the case when the function
g
is a supremum function. The supremum function arises in several optimization problems
MR4361979
;
sun2021robust
;
MR4362585
. An important problem that involves the supremum function is the called
Distributionally Robust Optimization
(DRO) problem, which assumes that the true probability distribution belongs to a set of distributions called the
ambiguity set
. Optimization is then performed based on the worst-case scenario within this ambiguity set. The DRO model covers the Robust Optimization problem when the ambiguity set includes all possible distributions, and Stochastic Optimization when the ambiguity set consists of a single probability distribution. Another application which appears a weakly convex supremum function is the
max dispersion problem
(or max-min location problem)
jeyakumar2018exact
.

The proposed algorithm for solving (
1
) is a generalization of the algorithm proposed in
bohm2021variable
and includes a projection onto the vector subspace. In the proposed method we need to compute the proximity operator of the function
g
. To do this, we generalize
(
MR4279933
, Theorem 3.5)
, which allows calculating the proximity operator of the supremum of convex functions.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Sect.
2
, we introduce the notation and the basic definitions. Sections
3
and
4
describes the proposed algorithm and its convergence properties. As in
bohm2021variable
, the convergence of the algorithm is obtained under the hypothesis of that
g
is Lipschitz. We prove that if the sequence generated by the algorithm is bounded, then we can eliminate such hypothesis to obtain convergence. In Sect.
5
, we prove that, in the context of weakly convex functions, the Moreau envelope of a supremum function is the supremum of Moreau envelopes. As a consequence of this result, we investigate the proximity operator of the supremum of weakly convex functions, and we compute the proximity operator in two important cases. In Sect.
6
, we use the results proved in the previous sections for solving several applications including the DRO problem, the LASSO with linear constraints, and the max dispersion problem. We illustrate numerical results for the max dispersion problem in Section
7
. Finally, we mention some conclusions in Section
8
.

2
Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a real Hilbert space with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and induced norm
\|\cdot\|
. We denote by
\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})
the set of extended valued functions oh
\mathcal{H}
that are proper, convex, and lower semicontinuous. Moreover, for
\rho\geq 0
, we denote by
\Gamma_{\rho}(\mathcal{H})
the set of functions
g
such that
g+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})
. We say that a function is
weakly convex
if the function belongs to
\Gamma_{\rho}(\mathcal{H})
for some
\rho\geq 0
. For
\mu>0
, the Moreau envelope of an extended valued function
f
is defined by

f_{\mu}(x):=\inf_{y\in\mathcal{H}}\,f(y)+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x-y\|^{2}.

If
f\in\Gamma_{\rho}(\mathcal{H})
and
\mu<1/\rho
, the above infimum is attained at a unique point and defines the so-called
proximity operator

x\mapsto\operatorname{prox}_{\mu f}(x)
(see, e.g.,
(
MR1230710
, Theorem 3.4)
). For a closed set
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathcal{H}
, the distance function and the projection mapping are defined as follows:

d(x,\mathcal{Q}):=\inf_{y\in\mathcal{Q}}\|x-y\|\textrm{ and }P_{\mathcal{Q}}(x)=\operatorname{argmin}_{y\in\mathcal{Q}}\|x-y\|.

The subdifferential of a weakly convex function
f
(see, e.g.,
MR1230710
) is defined by

\partial f(x)=\partial(f+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2})(x)-\rho x\textrm{ for }x\in\mathcal{H},

where
\rho\geq 0
is chosen such that
f+\frac{\rho}{2}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})
, and the subdifferential on the right-hand side is understood in the sense of convex analysis.

In this paper, we consider the problem

\displaystyle\min_{x\in V}h(x)+g(Ax),

(2)

where
V\subset\mathcal{H}
is a closed vector subspace,
h\colon\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a differentiable function with a Lipschitz gradient of constant
L_{\nabla h}
,
g\in\Gamma_{\rho}(\mathcal{H})
for some
\rho\geq 0
, and
A
is a nonzero bounded linear operator from
\mathcal{H}
to a real Hilbert space
\mathcal{G}
. We say that
x^{*}\in\mathcal{H}
is a critical point of (
2
) if

0\in\nabla h(x^{*})+A^{*}\partial g(Ax^{*})+N_{V}(x^{*}).

In the line of
bohm2021variable
, for every
k\geq 1
, we consider the following regularized problem

\displaystyle\min_{x\in V}F_{k}(x):=h(x)+g_{\mu_{k}}(Ax),

(3)

where
\mu_{k}>0
and
2\rho\mu_{k}\leq 1
. By
(
bohm2021variable
, Lemma 3.1)
,
\nabla F_{k}
is Lipschitz with constant

\displaystyle L_{k}:=L_{\nabla h}+\dfrac{\|A\|^{2}}{\mu_{k}},

(4)

where
L_{\nabla h}
is the Lipschitz constant of
\nabla h
.
Note that
x
is a critical point of (
3
) if and only if

\displaystyle 0\in\nabla F_{k}(x)+N_{V}(x)\,\Leftrightarrow\,x=P_{V}(x-\gamma\nabla F_{k}(x))\textrm{ for all }\gamma>0,

which motivates us to use the algorithm:
x_{k+1}=P_{V}(x_{k}-\gamma_{k}\nabla F_{k}(x_{k}))
.

Theorem 3.1

In the context of problem (
2
), assume that
g
is
L_{g}
-Lipschitz. Then, the sequence defined by Algorithm
1
satisfies

\displaystyle\min_{1\leq j\leq k}d(-\nabla h(x_{j}),A^{*}\partial g(\operatorname{prox}_{\mu_{j}g}(Ax_{j}))+N_{V}(x_{j}))

\displaystyle\leq k^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}\widetilde{C}\,\textrm{ for all }k\geq 1,

\displaystyle\|Ax_{k}-\operatorname{prox}_{\mu_{k}g}(Ax_{k})\|

\displaystyle\leq k^{-\alpha}CL_{g}\,\textrm{ for all }k\geq 1,

where
\widetilde{C}:=\dfrac{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{L_{\nabla h}+C^{-1}\|A\|^{2}}}{\sqrt{2^{1-\alpha}-1}}\sqrt{F_{1}(x_{1})-F^{*}+CL_{g}^{2}}
.

Proof

By the descent lemma (see, e.g.,
(
MR3719240
, Lemma 5.7)
), for all
k\geq 1

\displaystyle F_{k}(x_{k+1})\leq F_{k}(x_{k})+\langle\nabla F_{k}(x_{k}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle+\dfrac{1}{2\gamma_{k}}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}.

Using that
P_{V}
is linear,
x_{k}\in V
, and that
\langle x,P_{V}y\rangle=\langle P_{V}x,P_{V}y\rangle
, we have

\langle\nabla F_{k}(x_{k}),x_{k+1}-x_{k}\rangle=\langle\nabla F_{k}(x_{k}),-P_{V}\gamma_{k}\nabla F_{k}(x_{k})\rangle=-\gamma_{k}\|P_{V}\nabla F_{k}(x_{k})\|^{2}.

Thus, since
\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}=\gamma_{k}^{2}\|P_{V}\nabla F_{k}(x_{k})\|^{2}
, we get that

\displaystyle F_{k}(x_{k+1})\leq F_{k}(x_{k})-\dfrac{\gamma_{k}}{2}\|P_{V}\nabla F_{k}(x_{k})\|^{2}.

(5)

By
(
bohm2021variable
, Lemma 4.1)
, we have that

\hskip-0.02843ptF_{k+1}(x_{k+1})\leq F_{k}(x_{k+1})+\dfrac{1}{2}(\mu_{k}-\mu_{k+1})\dfrac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{k+1}}L_{g}^{2}\leq F_{k}(x_{k+1})+(\mu_{k}-\mu_{k+1})L_{g}^{2},

where we have used that
\mu_{k}\leq 2^{\alpha}\mu_{k+1}\leq 2\mu_{k+1}
. Hence, we obtain that

F_{k+1}(x_{k+1})\leq F_{k}(x_{k})-\dfrac{\gamma_{k}}{2}\|P_{V}\nabla F_{k}(x_{k})\|^{2}+(\mu_{k}-\mu_{k+1})L_{g}^{2}.

By summing these inequalities from
k=1
to
K
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\dfrac{\gamma_{k}}{2}\|P_{V}\nabla F_{k}(x_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq F_{1}(x_{1})-F_{K+1}(x_{K+1})+(\mu_{1}-\mu_{K+1})L_{g}^{2}

(6)

\displaystyle\leq F_{1}(x_{1})-F^{*}+\mu_{1}L_{g}^{2}.

On the one hand, we observe that

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}=\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu_{k}L_{\nabla h}+\|A\|^{2}}\geq k^{-\alpha}\frac{C}{CL_{\nabla h}+\|A\|^{2}}=k^{-\alpha}\dfrac{1}{L_{\nabla h}+C^{-1}\|A\|^{2}}.

(7)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}k^{-\alpha}\geq\int_{1}^{K+1}x^{-\alpha}dx\geq(K+1)^{1-\alpha}-1\geq(2^{1-\alpha}-1)K^{1-\alpha},

where we have used Lemma
4
. Hence,

\displaystyle\min_{1\leq j\leq K}\|P_{V}\nabla F_{j}(x_{j})\|^{2}\leq\dfrac{2(L_{\nabla h}+C^{-1}\|A\|^{2})}{(2^{1-\alpha}-1)K^{1-\alpha}}\left(F_{1}(x_{1})-F^{*}+CL_{g}^{2}\right).

(8)

Now,
N_{V}(x)=V^{\perp}
for all
x\in V
. Then, for all
z\in\mathcal{H}
and
x\in V
, we have that

\displaystyle d(-z,N_{V}(x))=d(-z,V^{\perp})\leq\|-z-(-z+P_{V}z)\|=\|P_{V}z\|.

(9)

By
(
bohm2021variable
, Lemma 3.2)
, we have that

\nabla(g_{\mu_{j}}\circ A)(x_{j})=A^{*}\nabla g_{\mu_{j}}(Ax_{j})\in A^{*}\partial g(\operatorname{prox}_{\mu_{j}g}(Ax_{j})).

Thus, taking
z=\nabla F_{j}(x_{j})=\nabla h(x_{j})+\nabla(g_{\mu_{j}}\circ A)(x_{j})
in (
9
), we obtain that

\displaystyle d(-\nabla h(x_{j}),A^{*}\partial g(\operatorname{prox}_{\mu_{j}g}(Ax_{j}))+N_{V}(x_{j}))\leq\|P_{V}\nabla F_{j}(x_{j})\|,

(10)

which, by virtue of (
8
), implies the first inequality. The second inequality follows from
(
bohm2021variable
, Lemma 3.3)
. ∎

Remark 1

The proposed algorithm can be easily adapted to the case of a closed affine subspace. Indeed, if
V
is a closed affine subspace, that is,
V=W+z_{0}
, where
W
is a closed vector subspace and
z_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
, then problem (
2
) is equivalent to
\min_{x\in W}h(x+z_{0})+g(y+Az_{0})
. The assumptions on the data hold, and
\operatorname{prox}_{\mu g(\cdot+Az_{0})}(y)=\operatorname{prox}_{\mu g}(y+Az_{0})-Az_{0}
.

The hypothesis that the function
g
is Lipschitz in Theorem
3.1
may be too restrictive. For example, if
g
is of the form
g(x)=-\|x\|^{2}
, then such a hypothesis does not hold. In what follows, we prove that if the sequence generated by the Algorithm
1
is bounded and
\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{R}^{m}
, then a similar result to the Theorem
3.1
holds without requiring

to be Lipschitz.

Variable smoothing with epochs

5
Supremum of weakly convex functions

Supremum functions play a prominent role in optimization. They models several phenomena, ranging from stochastic optimization to equilibrium problems. However, they are usually nonsmooth; hence, their optimization presents significant challenges from both a theoretical and numerical perspective. In this section, we extend the analysis from
MR4279933
. Hence, we study the Moreau envelope and the proximity operator of the supremum function:

\displaystyle f(x)=\sup_{c\in\mathcal{C}}f_{c}(x)\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathcal{H},

(16)

where
\mathcal{H}
is a real Hilbert space,
\mathcal{C}\neq\emptyset
is a convex subset of some topological vector space and
f_{c}\in\Gamma_{\rho_{c}}(\mathcal{H})
with
\rho_{c}\geq 0
for all
c\in\mathcal{C}
.

Lemma 1

Let
(f_{c})_{c\in\mathcal{C}}
be a family of functions such that
f_{c}\in\Gamma_{\rho_{c}}(\mathcal{H})
for all
c\in\mathcal{C}
. Assume that
f:=\sup_{c\in\mathcal{C}}f_{c}
is proper and that
\bar{\rho}:=\sup_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\rho_{c}<+\infty
. Then,
f\in\Gamma_{\bar{\rho}}(\mathcal{H})
. More precisely,

\displaystyle h:=f+\frac{\bar{\rho}}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})\textrm{ and }r_{c}:=f_{c}+\frac{\bar{\rho}}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})\textrm{ for all }c\in\mathcal{C}.

(17)

Proof

It is clear that
h_{c}:=f_{c}+\dfrac{\rho_{c}}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})
for all
c\in\mathcal{C}
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle h:=f+\frac{\bar{\rho}}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}=\sup_{c\in\mathcal{C}}f_{c}+\frac{\bar{\rho}}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}=\sup_{c\in\mathcal{C}}(h_{c}+\frac{(\bar{\rho}-\rho_{c})}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}).

(18)

Since
h_{c}
belong to
\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})
, and
\bar{\rho}-\rho_{c}\geq 0
for all
c\in\mathcal{C}

. ∎

5.1
Supremum functions of weakly convex with quadratic functions

Consider the following function

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{H}^{N}\mapsto f(\mathbf{x})=\sup_{p\in\Delta_{N}}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}\|x_{i}-\xi_{i}\|^{2}=\max_{1\leq i\leq N}-\|x_{i}-\xi_{i}\|^{2},

for all

is the the probability simplex
\Delta_{N}

Proposition 1

Let
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{H}^{N}
and let
\mu\in\left]0,1/2\right[
. Then,
f
is
\rho
-weakly convex with
\rho=2
and

\operatorname{prox}_{\mu f}(\mathbf{x})=\left(\dfrac{x_{i}-2\mu\bar{p}_{i}\xi_{i}}{1-2\mu\bar{p}_{i}}\right)_{i=1}^{N},

where
\bar{p}\in\Delta_{N}
is a solution to

\displaystyle\max_{p\in\Delta_{N}}\phi(p):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{p_{i}}{2\mu p_{i}-1}\right)\alpha_{i}\textrm{ and }\alpha_{i}=\|x_{i}-\xi_{i}\|^{2}\textrm{ for all }i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}.

(21)

Proof

Define
f_{i}(x):=-\|x-\xi_{i}\|^{2}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
and
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{H}^{N}\mapsto g_{p}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}f_{i}(x_{i})
for all
p\in\Delta_{N}
. We claim that
g_{p}
is weakly convex with parameter
\rho=2
for all
p\in\Delta_{N}
. Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}f_{i}(x_{i})+\|\mathbf{x}\|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}(f_{i}(x_{i})+\|x_{i}\|^{2})+\sum_{i=1}^{N}(1-p_{i})\|x_{i}\|^{2}.

Then, since
f_{i}+\|\cdot\|^{2}
is convex and
p_{i}\leq 1
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
,
g_{p}+\|\cdot\|^{2}
is convex for all
p\in\Delta_{N}
. Thus, by Lemma
1
,
f=\sup_{p\in\Delta_{N}}g_{p}
is proper and
\rho
-weakly convex with
\rho=2
. On the other hand,
p\mapsto g_{p}(\mathbf{x})
is concave and u.s.c. for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{H}^{N}
. Thus, from Theorem
5.2
, for every
\mu\in\left]0,1/2\right[
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{prox}_{\mu f}(\mathbf{x})=\operatorname{prox}_{\mu g_{\bar{p}}}(\mathbf{x}),\quad\text{with }\bar{p}\in\operatorname{argmax}_{p\in\Delta_{N}}(g_{p})_{\mu}(\mathbf{x}).

(22)

Now, observe that for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
and
x\in\mathcal{H}
,
y=\operatorname{prox}_{\mu(p_{i}f_{i})}(x)
if and only if

. Then, we have that

\operatorname{prox}_{\mu g_{\bar{p}}}(\mathbf{x})=\left(\dfrac{x_{i}-2\mu\bar{p}_{i}\xi_{i}}{1-2\mu\bar{p}_{i}}\right)_{i=1}^{N}.

Let us calculate
(g_{p})_{\mu}(\mathbf{x})
. By the above formula, we obtain that

(g_{p})_{\mu}(\mathbf{x})=g_{p}(\operatorname{prox}_{\mu g_{p}}(\mathbf{x}))+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|\mathbf{x}-\operatorname{prox}_{\mu g_{p}}(\mathbf{x})\|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{p_{i}}{2\mu p_{i}-1}\right)\|x_{i}-\xi_{i}\|^{2}.

Set
\alpha_{i}=\|x_{i}-\xi_{i}\|^{2}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
. Hence, in order to find
\bar{p}
in (
22
), we need to solve problem (
21
). Since
\phi
is continuous and
\Delta_{N}
is a nonempty compact set, problem (
21
) has solutions. ∎

In the context of problem (
21

\alpha_{i}=0
for some

, where

. Then, we can assume that

(\nabla^{2}\phi(p))_{ij}=\begin{cases}4\mu\alpha_{i}(2\mu p_{i}-1)^{-3}&\text{ if }i=j\\
0&\text{ if }i\neq j\end{cases}\quad\text{for all }i,j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}.

Then,

is a positive definite matrix for all

is strictly convex on
\Delta_{N}

is a compact convex set and

Now, we provide a solution of (

Proposition 2

In the context of (

\{\ell_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}

5.2
Supremum function weakly convex with affine functions

Inspired in
(

\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto f(x)=\sup_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}(\langle a_{i},x\rangle+b_{i})-\sigma\|x\|^{2},

(25)

where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\sigma>0
, and
\mathcal{C}\subset\Delta_{N}
is a nonempty closed convex set. Clearly,
f
is
\rho
-weakly convex with
\rho=2\sigma
. Note that the function

appears in the discrete version of the DRO problem when the ambiguity set is the set
\mathcal{C}
and the cost function is weakly convex involving affine functions. The next result provides an algorithm for the proximity of the function

in (

Proposition 3

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mu\in]0,\frac{1}{2\sigma}[
. Let
A=\begin{bmatrix}a_{1}|\cdots|a_{N}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
,
b=\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}\cdots b_{N}\end{pmatrix}^{\top}
, and
\gamma>0
such that
\frac{\gamma\mu\|AA^{\top}\|}{1-2\sigma\mu}<1
. Let
(\alpha_{k})\subset[0,1]
such that
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\alpha_{k}(1-\alpha_{k})=+\infty
and let
c^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider

y^{k}=\frac{1}{1-2\sigma\mu}(x-\mu A^{\top}c^{k}),z^{k}=c^{k}+\gamma(Ay^{k}+b),c^{k+1}=\alpha_{k}P_{\mathcal{C}}(z^{k})+(1-\alpha_{k})c^{k}.

Then,
y^{k}\rightarrow\operatorname{prox}_{\mu f}(x)
.

Proof

For every
c\in\mathcal{C}
, consider
f_{c}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}(\langle a_{i},x\rangle+b_{i})-\sigma\|x\|^{2}
. It is clear that
x\mapsto f_{c}(x)
is weakly convex with parameter
2\sigma
and that
c\mapsto f_{c}(x)
is concave and upper semicontinuous for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover, it is clear that
f=\sup_{c\in\mathcal{C}}f_{c}
is proper. Thus, by Theorem
5.2
, we have that

\displaystyle\operatorname{prox}_{\mu f}(x)=\operatorname{prox}_{\mu f_{\bar{c}}}(x)\quad\text{with }\bar{c}\in\operatorname{argmax}_{c\in\mathcal{C}}(f_{c})_{\mu}(x).

(26)

Moreover, for every
c\in\mathcal{C}
,
y=\operatorname{prox}_{\mu f_{c}}(x)
if and only if
y=\frac{1}{1-2\sigma\mu}(x-\mu A^{\top}c)
. In order to find
\bar{c}\in\mathcal{C}
in (
26
), we need to solve the following minimax problem

\max_{c\in\mathcal{C}}(f_{c})_{\mu}(x)=\max_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{c}(y)+\dfrac{1}{2\mu}\|y-x\|^{2}.

The optimality condition of the above problem is
Ay+b\in N_{\mathcal{C}}(c)
and
y=\operatorname{prox}_{\mu f_{c}}(x)
. Hence, we need to find
c\in\mathcal{C}
such that
c=P_{\mathcal{C}}(c+\gamma(Ay_{c}+b))
, where
y_{c}=(1-2\sigma\mu)^{-1}(x-\mu A^{\top}c)
. Then, by defining
c\in\mathcal{C}\mapsto\mathcal{F}(c)=c+\gamma(Ay_{c}+b)
, we conclude that

\displaystyle c\in\operatorname{argmax}_{c\in\mathcal{C}}(f_{c})_{\mu}(x)\Leftrightarrow c=P_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{F}(c)).

(27)

We claim that
\mathcal{F}
is nonexpansive. Indeed, let
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
in
\mathcal{C}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(c_{1})-\mathcal{F}(c_{2})

\displaystyle=c_{1}-c_{2}-\dfrac{\gamma\mu AA^{\top}}{1-2\sigma\mu}(c_{1}-c_{2})=\left(I-\dfrac{\gamma\mu AA^{\top}}{1-2\sigma\mu}\right)(c_{1}-c_{2}).

Let
B:=\dfrac{\gamma\mu AA^{\top}}{1-2\sigma\mu}
. It is easy to see that
\|I-B\|_{2}\leq 1
. Define
T=P_{\mathcal{C}}\circ\mathcal{F}
. Then, since
P_{\mathcal{C}}
is nonexpansive, we obtain that
T
is nonexpansive. Hence, we deduce that
c^{k+1}=\alpha_{k}Tc^{k}+(1-\alpha_{k})c^{k}
. From
(
MR3616647
, Theorem 5.15)
, there exists
\bar{c}\in\text{Fix}\,T
such that
c^{k}\rightarrow\bar{c}
and
y^{k}\rightarrow\operatorname{prox}_{\mu f_{\bar{c}}}(x)
. Moreover, by (
26
) and (
27
),
\operatorname{prox}_{\mu f_{\bar{c}}}(x)=\operatorname{prox}_{\mu f}(x)
. Therefore,
y^{k}\rightarrow\operatorname{prox}_{\mu f}(x)
. ∎

6.1
Distributionally Robust Optimization

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{A})
be a measurable space, let
\xi\colon\Omega\rightarrow\Xi\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a random vector with
\Xi\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
being an uncertainty set, let
\mathcal{P}
be a nonempty closed convex subset of probability measures on
(\Omega,\mathcal{A})
supported on
\Xi
(called
ambiguity set
), let
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a compact convex set, and let
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Xi\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function. The problem

\displaystyle\displaystyle\min_{x\in Q}\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[F(x,\xi)]:=\int_{\Omega}F(x,\xi(\omega))d\mathbb{P}(\omega).

(28)

is called
distributionally robust optimization
(DRO) problem
(
sun2021robust
, eq 4.1.3)
. The ambiguity set represents the information about the probability distributions. The problem (
28
) covers the stochastic optimization problem when the ambiguity set is a singleton, and the robust optimization problem when the ambiguity set includes all possible distributions. When
\Xi
is finite, that is,
\Xi=\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{N}\}
and denoting
p_{i}=\mathbb{P}(\{\omega\in\Omega:\xi(\omega)=\xi_{i}\})
and
f_{i}=F(\cdot,\xi_{i})
, for every
\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{P}
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, we have that the problem (
28
) reduces to

\displaystyle\min_{x\in Q}\sup_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}f_{i}(x),

(29)

where
\mathcal{P}\subset\Delta_{N}:=\{p\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}:\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}=1\}
. When
Q=V\cap\mathcal{B}
, where
V
is a closed vector subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathcal{B}
is a compact convex set, (
29
) reduces to

\displaystyle\min_{x\in V\cap\mathcal{B}}\widetilde{g}(x),

(30)

where
\widetilde{g}(x)=\sup_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}f_{i}(x)
. Note that if
f_{i}
is l.s.c. for all
i\in\left\{1,\ldots,N\right\}
, then
\widetilde{g}
is l.s.c.. Then, we can use the penalty method described in Section
A.1
for solving (
30
). That is, we need to solve the penalized problem:

\displaystyle\min_{x\in V}\lambda P(x)+\widetilde{g}(x),

where
\lambda>0
and
P\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a l.s.c. function such that
P(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
P(x)=0
if and only if
x\in\mathcal{B}
. When
P(x)=\frac{1}{2}d^{2}(x,\mathcal{B})
, the penalized problem is equivalent to

\displaystyle\min_{x\in V}h(x)+\widetilde{g}(x),

(31)

where
h(x)=\frac{\lambda}{2}d^{2}(x,\mathcal{B})
. Then,
\nabla h(x)=\lambda(x-P_{\mathcal{B}}(x))
and
\nabla h
is Lipschitz with constant
\lambda
. On the other hand, when
f_{i}(x)=\langle a_{i},x\rangle+b_{i}-\sigma\|x\|^{2}
, where
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
b_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\sigma>0
, the proximity operator of
\widetilde{g}
can be calculated using the algorithm in Proposition
3
. Thus, (
31
) is a particular case of (
2
). When
f_{i}(x)=\langle a_{i},x\rangle+b_{i}-\sigma\|x\|^{2}
, it is straightforward to prove that the objective function in (
31
) is coercive if
\lambda>2\sigma
and hence, in this case, (
31
) has solutions for all
\lambda>2\sigma
.
We consider now the following DRO model
(
MR4361979
, Example 4)

\min_{\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{nN}}\max_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}f_{i}(x_{i})\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{N}\cap\bigtimes_{i=1}^{N}Q_{i},

(32)

where
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathbb{R}^{nN}
is the nonanticipative set (vector subspace),
f_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the cost function in the scenario
i
,
Q_{i}
is a compact convex subset, and
\mathcal{P}\subset\Delta_{N}
is a nonempty closed convex ambiguity set. Note that (
32
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{N}\cap\overline{Q}}g(\mathbf{x}),

(33)

where
\overline{Q}=\bigtimes_{i=1}^{N}Q_{i}
and
g(\mathbf{x})=\sup_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}f_{i}(x_{i})
. Observe that if
f_{i}
is lower semicontinuous for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, then
g
also is lower semicontinuous. Similarly to (
30
), in order to solve (
33
), we can use the penalty method described in Section
A.1
by solving the following problem

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{N}}\frac{\lambda}{2}d(\mathbf{x},\overline{Q})^{2}+g(\mathbf{x}),

(34)

where
\lambda>0
. In the case when
f_{i}(x)=-\|x-\xi_{i}\|^{2}
and
\mathcal{P}=\Delta_{N}
, where
\xi_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the proximity operator of
g
has a closed-form and is given in Proposition
1
and Proposition
2
. Moreover, in this case, we have that the objective function in (
34
) is coercive if
\lambda>2
. Hence, in this case
(
34
) has solutions for all
\lambda>2
. Thus, (
34
) is a particular case of problem (
2
).

as a regularizer in (
35
) tends to reduce the number of nonzero elements of the solution generating a bias. Therefore, nonconvex regularizers are often used to reduces bias. These covers the
\ell^{p}
-norms (with
p\in\left]0,1\right[
) which are not weakly convex, as well as several weakly convex regularizers, which now we illustrate. In
zhang2010nearly
it is defined the minimax concave penalty (MCP) function: Given
\lambda>0
and
\theta>0
, the MCP is the function
r_{\lambda,\theta}\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
defined by

r_{\lambda,\theta}(x)=\begin{cases}\lambda|x|-\frac{x^{2}}{2\theta}&\text{if }\,\,|x|\leq\theta\lambda,\\
\frac{\theta\lambda^{2}}{2}&\text{otherwise }\end{cases}

Observe that this function is
\theta^{-1}
-weakly convex. The proximity operator of
r_{\lambda,\theta}
(called firm threshold) has a closed form and is given, for every
\gamma<\theta
, by

\operatorname{prox}_{\gamma r_{\lambda,\theta}}(x)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }\,\,|x|<\gamma\lambda,\\
\frac{x-\lambda\gamma\operatorname{sgn}(x)}{1-\gamma/\theta}&\text{if }\,\,\gamma\lambda\leq|x|\leq\theta\lambda,\\
x&\text{if }\,\,|x|>\theta\lambda.\end{cases}

Another weakly convex regularizer is the smoothly clipped absolute deviation (SCAD)
MR1946581
, which is defined, for parameters
\lambda>0
and
\theta>2
, by

r_{\lambda,\theta}(x)=\begin{cases}\lambda|x|&\text{if }\,\,|x|\leq\lambda,\\
\frac{-x^{2}+2\lambda\theta|x|-\lambda^{2}}{2(\theta-1)}&\text{if }\,\,\lambda<|x|\leq\theta\lambda,\\
\frac{(\theta+1)\lambda^{2}}{2}&\text{if }\,\,|x|>\theta\lambda.\end{cases}

This function is
(\theta-1)^{-1}
-weakly convex. The problem we have seen so far has the structure of (
2
) when
A
is the identity. Let us consider the case
A\neq\operatorname{Id}
. We consider the
Tukey biweight
function
beaton1974fitting
, which is given by

\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varphi(A_{i\bullet}x-b_{i}),

where
\varphi(\theta)=\frac{\theta^{2}}{1+\theta^{2}}
,
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, and
A_{i\bullet}
denotes the
i
th row of
A
. Note that the above function can be written as
g(Ax)
with
g(y)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varphi(y_{i}-b_{i})
for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Moreover, the Tukey biweight function is
\rho
-weakly convex with
\rho=6
.

6.3
Max Dispersion Problems

Consider the following max dispersion problem (or max-min location problem):

\displaystyle\max_{x\in S}\min_{1\leq i\leq N}\|x-u_{i}\|^{2},

(36)

where
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a compact subset and
u_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
. The problem (
36
) consists in to find a point in
S
that is furthest from the given points
u_{1},\ldots,u_{N}
and has the geometric interpretation of finding the largest Euclidian ball with center in
S
and that does not enclose any given point. Note that
x\mapsto\min_{1\leq i\leq N}\|x-u_{i}\|^{2}
is continuous since is the minimum of finitely many continuous functions. Then, problem (
36
) has solutions. The problem (
36
) have been studied in
jeyakumar2018exact
when the constraint
S
is a polyhedral or a ball, and has applications in facility location, pattern recognition, among others
dasarathy1980maxmin
;
johnson1990minimax
. In addition, it is shown in
haines2013convex
that the max dispersion problem over a box constraint is a NP-hard problem.

Consider the case when
S=V\cap\mathcal{B}
, where
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a vector subspace and
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a compact convex set. In this case, (
36
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\min_{x\in V\cap\mathcal{B}}\max_{1\leq i\leq N}-\|x-u_{i}\|^{2}.

(37)

In order to solve (
37
), we use the penalty method described in Section
A.1
. That is, we solve the following penalized problem

\displaystyle\min_{x\in V}\lambda P(x)+\max_{1\leq i\leq N}-\|x-u_{i}\|^{2},

(38)

where
P
is the penalty function which is lower semicontinuous and satisfies that
P(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
P(x)=0
if and only if
x\in\mathcal{B}
, while
\lambda>0
is the penalty coefficient. Consider the penalty function
P(x)=\frac{1}{2}d(x,\mathcal{B})^{2}
. Then, the problem (
38
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\min_{x\in V}h(x)+\widetilde{g}(x),

(39)

where
h(x)=\frac{\lambda}{2}d^{2}(x,\mathcal{B})
and
\widetilde{g}
is given by

\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto\widetilde{g}(x)=\sup_{p\in\Delta_{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}(\langle x,2u_{i}\rangle-\|u_{i}\|^{2})-\|x\|^{2}.

(40)

The problem (
39
) is particular instance of (
2
) when
A=\operatorname{Id}
. Moreover, it is straightforward to prove that the objective function in (
39
) is coercive for all
\lambda>2
, hence (
39
) has solutions for all
\lambda>2
. Now, the function (
40
) is a particular case of (
25
) when
a_{i}=2u_{i}
,
b_{i}=-\|u_{i}\|^{2}
,
\sigma=1
, and
\mathcal{C}=\Delta_{N}
. Thus, we can use the algorithm in Proposition
3
for computing the proximity operator of
\widetilde{g}
. Observe that the projection onto
\mathcal{C}=\Delta_{N}
has an explicit form given in
duchi2008efficient
. In addition,
\nabla h(x)=\lambda(x-P_{\mathcal{B}}(x))
is Lipschitz with constant
\lambda
.

On the other hand, since
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a vector subspace, then by
(
rockafellar1997convex
, Theorem 1.4)
, there exists
R\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
such that
V=\ker R
. Thus, since
\operatorname{ran}R
is closed (since it is a vector subspace of finite dimension), then the projection onto
V
is given by
P_{\ker R}=\operatorname{Id}-R^{\dagger}R
, where
R^{\dagger}
is the generalized (or Moore-Penrose) inverse of
R
.

Another equivalent formulation for the problem (
39
) is the following

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{nN}}H(\mathbf{x})+g(\mathbf{x})\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\mathbf{x}\in V^{N}\cap\mathcal{D},

(41)

where
H\colon\mathbb{R}^{nN}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined by
H(\mathbf{x})=h(x_{1})=\frac{\lambda}{2}d(x_{1},\mathcal{B})^{2}
,
\mathcal{D}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{nN}:x_{1}=\cdots=x_{N}\}
is the diagonal set, and
g
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{nN}\mapsto g(\mathbf{x})=\max_{1\leq i\leq N}-\|x_{i}-u_{i}\|^{2}.

(42)

Note that
\mathbf{x}=(x,\ldots,x)
is solution to (
41
) if and only if
x
is solution to (
39
). The problem (
41
) is a particular case of (
2
) when
A=\operatorname{Id}
and the closed vector subspace is
V^{N}\cap\mathcal{D}
. As mentioned before, the objective function in (
41
) is coercive in
\mathcal{D}
for all
\lambda>2
, hence (
41
) has solutions for all
\lambda>2
. The proximity operator of (
42
) has a closed form and is given by Proposition
1
and Proposition
2
.

The projection onto
V^{N}\cap\mathcal{D}
can be calculated using the Dykstra’s projection algorithm, which requires calculate the projection onto
V^{N}
and onto
\mathcal{D}
. Recall that
V=\ker R
. By
(
MR3616647
, Proposition 29.3)
, we have that
P_{V^{N}}(\mathbf{x})=(P_{\ker R}(x_{i}))_{i=1}^{N}
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{nN}
. On the other hand, the projection onto
\mathcal{D}
is given by
P_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathbf{x})=(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}x_{j})_{i=1}^{N}
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{nN}
.

8
Conclusions

In this paper, we consider the problem of minimizing the sum of a smooth function and the composition of a weakly convex function with a bounded linear operator over a closed vector subspace, generalizing the framework studied in
bohm2021variable
, which considers the case where the vector subspace is the entire space. We propose a variable smoothing algorithm that includes projection onto the vector subspace and we prove a complexity of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})
to achieve an
\epsilon
-approximate solution. In both the literature and the proposed algorithm, it is typically assumed that the weakly convex function is Lipschitz in order to obtain an approximate solution. However, we show that if the sequence generated by the proposed algorithm is bounded, this assumption is not necessary.

The proposed algorithm requires computing the proximity operator of a weakly convex function. In this context, we compute the proximity operator of a supremum of weakly convex functions in two important cases. In one case, the proximity operator has a closed formula, and in the other, we provide an algorithm that converges to the proximity operator. The supremum function appears in the so-called distributionally robust optimization problem
sun2021robust
;
MR4361979
. Another problem where our algorithm can be applied, which also involves a supremum function, is the max-dispersion problem (or max-min location problem) over the intersection of a subspace and a compact convex set.

A.1
Penalty Method

This section is based in
(
luenberger1984linear
, Section 21.1)
. Consider the following optimization problem

\min_{x\in V\cap\mathcal{B}}f(x),

(43)

where
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is lower semicontinuous,
V
is a closed vector subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\mathcal{B}
is a compact convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Note that (
43
) has solutions since
f
is lower semicontinuous and
V\cap\mathcal{B}
is compact. The idea of the penalty function method is replace problem (
43
) by a problem of the form
\min_{x\in V}f(x)+\lambda P(x)
, where
\lambda>0
and
P\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a lower semicontinuous function satisfying that
P(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
P(x)=0
if and only if
x\in\mathcal{B}
. In order to solve problem (
43
) by the penalty function method, let
\lambda_{k}\to+\infty
be a sequence such that
\lambda_{k}>0
and
\lambda_{k+1}>\lambda_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Consider
q(\lambda,x):=f(x)+\lambda P(x)
and assume that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
the problem
\min_{x\in V}q(\lambda_{k},x)
has a solution
x_{k}\in V
.

Lemma 2

For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

q(\lambda_{k},x_{k})\leq q(\lambda_{k+1},x_{k+1}),\,P(x_{k})\geq P(x_{k+1})\textrm{ and }f(x_{k})\leq f(x_{k+1}).

Proof

Since
\lambda_{k}
is increasing, we have

\displaystyle q(\lambda_{k+1},x_{k+1})\geq f(x_{k+1})+\lambda_{k}P(x_{k+1})\geq f(x_{k})+\lambda_{k}P(x_{k})=q(\lambda_{k},x_{k}),

thereby proving the first inequality. Next, observe that

f(x_{k})+\lambda_{k}P(x_{k})\leq f(x_{k+1})+\lambda_{k}P(x_{k+1})\leq f(x_{k})+\lambda_{k+1}P(x_{k})+(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k+1})P(x_{k+1}),

which implies the second inequality. Finally, by using the second inequality, we get that

f(x_{k})+\lambda_{k}P(x_{k})\leq f(x_{k+1})+\lambda_{k}P(x_{k+1})\leq f(x_{k+1})+\lambda_{k}P(x_{k}),

which implies the last inequality. ∎

Lemma 3

If
x^{*}
solves problem (
43
), then
f(x^{*})\geq q(\lambda_{k},x_{k})\geq f(x_{k})
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof

f(x^{*})=f(x^{*})+\lambda_{k}P(x^{*})\geq f(x_{k})+\lambda_{k}P(x_{k})\geq f(x_{k})
. ∎

Theorem A.1

A cluster point of a sequence generated by the penalty method solves (
43
).

Proof

Let
(x_{k})
generated by the penalty method and
\bar{x}
a cluster point. Let
f^{*}
the value of (
43
). From Lemmas
2
and
3
, it follows that
(f(x_{k}))
is increasing and bounded above by
f^{*}
. Hence,
(f(x_{k}))
converges and
f(\overline{x})\leq\lim f(x_{k})
. Similarly,
q^{*}:=\lim q(\lambda_{k},x_{k})
and
\lim P(x_{k})
exist. Therefore,
0\leq\lim\lambda_{k}P(x_{k})=\lim q(\lambda_{k},x_{k})-\lim f(x_{k})\leq q^{*}-f(\overline{x})
, which, since
\lambda_{k}\rightarrow\infty
and
P(x_{k})\geq 0
, implies that
\lim P(x_{k})=0
. Moreover,
0\leq P(\bar{x})\leq\lim P(x_{k})=0
, thereby proving that
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{B}
. Finally, from Lemma
3
, we conclude that
f(\overline{x})\leq\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}f(x_{k})\leq f^{*}
, which ends the proof. ∎

A.2
Key inequalities

Lemma 4

Assume that
\alpha\in]0,1[
. Then, for all
K\in\mathbb{N}

(K+1)^{1-\alpha}-1\geq(2^{1-\alpha}-1)K^{1-\alpha}.

Proof

Let us define
\varphi(x)=(x+1)^{1-\alpha}-1-\theta x^{1-\alpha}
for all
x\geq 1
, where
\theta=2^{1-\alpha}-1
. Since
\alpha\in\left]0,1\right[
,
0<\theta<1
. Note that
\varphi(1)=0
. Let
x\geq 1
. Then,
\varphi^{\prime}(x)=(1-\alpha)(x+1)^{-\alpha}-(1-\alpha)\theta x^{-\alpha}
. We will see that
\varphi^{\prime}(x)\geq 0
. Now, we observe that
\theta<2^{-\alpha}
if and only if
\frac{\theta^{1/\alpha}}{1-\theta^{1/\alpha}}<1

x\geq 1>\frac{\theta^{1/\alpha}}{1-\theta^{1/\alpha}}
. Now, we have that

. Whence
\varphi^{\prime}(x)\geq 0
. It follows that
\varphi(x)\geq\varphi(1)=0
for all

, which implies the result. ∎

Lemma 5

Let
\alpha\in]0,1[
and

such that

. Then, for all

Define

. By definition of
\theta
, we deduce that

. Let

\varphi^{\prime}(x)=(1-\alpha)(x+1)^{-\alpha}-(1-\alpha)\theta x^{-\alpha}

Since

and

for all

. ∎

Proposition 4

Let
g\colon\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be a proper, l.s.c., and
\rho
-weakly convex function.
Then there exists
\mu_{0}<\min\{1,\frac{1}{2\rho}\}
such that for every

\mu\in\left]0,\mu_{0}\right[
,
x\in\mathcal{H}
, and
x_{0}\in\operatorname{dom}g
we have

\|\operatorname{prox}_{\mu g}(x)\|^{2}\leq 4(M+5\|x\|^{2}),

where
M=\max\{0,g(x_{0})\}+2\|x_{0}\|^{2}+1/2
.

Proof

We claim that there exists
\theta>0
such that for all
x\in\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle g(x)\geq-\dfrac{\theta}{2}(1+\|x\|^{2}).

(44)

Since
g+\dfrac{\rho}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}
has an affine minorant, there exist
x^{*}\in\mathcal{H}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle g(x)+\dfrac{\rho}{2}\|x\|^{2}\geq\langle x^{*},x\rangle+\beta\geq-\frac{\|x^{*}\|^{2}}{2}-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{2}+\beta\textrm{ for all }x\in\mathcal{H}.

Then,
g(x)\geq-\frac{1}{2}(\|x^{*}\|^{2}-2\beta)-\frac{1}{2}(1+\rho)\|x\|^{2}\geq-\frac{1}{2}\max\{\|x^{*}\|^{2}-2\beta,1+\rho\}(1+\|x\|^{2})
. Therefore,
g
satisfies (
44
) with
\theta=\max\{\|x^{*}\|^{2}-2\beta,1+\rho\}>0
. Let us define
\mu_{0}=\min\{\frac{1}{2\theta},\frac{1}{1+\theta}\}
.

Let
\mu\in]0,\mu_{0}[
, let
x\in\mathcal{H}
, let
y=\operatorname{prox}_{\mu g}(x)
, and
x_{0}\in\operatorname{dom}g\neq\emptyset
. By (
44
), we have that

\displaystyle-\frac{\theta}{2}-\frac{\theta}{2}\|y\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|y-x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq g(y)+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|y-x\|^{2}\leq g(x_{0})+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x_{0}-x\|^{2}.

Then,

\displaystyle-\frac{\theta}{2}-\frac{\theta}{2}\|y\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|y\|^{2}-\frac{1}{\mu}\langle y,x\rangle\leq g(x_{0})+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x_{0}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{\mu}\langle x_{0},x\rangle\leq g(x_{0})+\frac{1}{\mu}\|x_{0}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x\|^{2}.

Now, from
ab\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}a^{2}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}b^{2}
with
a=\|y\|
,
b=\|x\|
and
\alpha=\frac{1-\mu\theta}{2}>0
, we have that

\displaystyle-\frac{\theta}{2}-\frac{\theta}{2}\|y\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|y\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2\mu}\left(\frac{1-\mu\theta}{2}\right)\|y\|^{2}+g(x_{0})+\frac{1}{\mu}\|x_{0}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\mu(1-\mu\theta)}\|x\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|x\|^{2}.

Rearranging, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{1-\mu\theta}{\mu}\right)\|y\|^{2}\leq g(x_{0})+\frac{1}{\mu}\|x_{0}\|^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{\mu(1-\mu\theta)}+\frac{1}{2\mu}\right)\|x\|^{2}+\frac{\theta}{2},

which implies that

\displaystyle\|y\|^{2}\leq 4\left[\frac{\mu}{1-\mu\theta}g(x_{0})+\frac{1}{1-\mu\theta}\|x_{0}\|^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{(1-\mu\theta)^{2}}+\frac{1}{2(1-\mu\theta)}\right)\|x\|^{2}+\frac{\mu\theta}{2(1-\mu\theta)}\right].

Note that
\frac{\mu}{1-\mu\theta}<1
(since
\mu<\frac{1}{1+\theta}
),
\frac{1}{1-\mu\theta}<2
, and
\frac{\mu\theta}{2(1-\mu\theta)}<\frac{1}{2}
(since
\mu<\frac{1}{2\theta}
). Thus,
\|y\|^{2}\leq 4(\max\{0,g(x_{0})\}+2\|x_{0}\|^{2}+5\|x\|^{2}+1/2)
. ∎

Proposition 5

Let
S
be a nonempty closed convex and bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and let
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
\rho
-weakly convex and lower semicontinuous function. Then, there exist
\ell\geq 0
and
\mu_{0}\in\left]0,1\right[
(with
\mu_{0}
depending on
\rho
) such that for all
\mu\in\left]0,\mu_{0}\right[
and
x\in S
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla g_{\mu}(x)\|=\dfrac{1}{\mu}\|x-\operatorname{prox}_{\mu g}(x)\|\leq\ell.

Proof

By Proposition
4
, there exist
\widetilde{\mu}_{0}\in]0,1[
and
M\geq 0
such that for all
\mu\in\left]0,\widetilde{\mu}_{0}\right[
,

\displaystyle\|\operatorname{prox}_{\mu g}(x)\|^{2}\leq 4(M+5\|x\|^{2})\textrm{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Let us define
\widetilde{S}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:d(x,S)\leq 1\}
. Since
\widetilde{S}
is nonempty closed, convex, and bounded, there exists
\widetilde{\ell}\geq 0
such that
g+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}
is
\widetilde{\ell}
-Lipschitz on
\widetilde{S}
and there exists
\widetilde{M}\geq 0
such that
\|x\|\leq\widetilde{M}
for all
x\in\widetilde{S}
. Let
x,y\in\widetilde{S}
. Then,

\displaystyle|g(x)-g(y)|

\displaystyle\leq|g(x)-g(y)+\dfrac{\rho}{2}\|x\|^{2}-\dfrac{\rho}{2}\|y\|^{2}|+\dfrac{\rho}{2}|\|x\|^{2}-\|y\|^{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{\ell}\|x-y\|+\dfrac{\rho}{2}|\|x\|+\|y\||\cdot|\|x\|-\|y\||

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{\ell}\|x-y\|+\rho\widetilde{M}\|x-y\|

\displaystyle=(\widetilde{\ell}+\rho\widetilde{M})\|x-y\|.

Hence,
g
is Lipschitz on
\widetilde{S}
. Similarly,
g
is Lipschitz in
S
.
Now, for all
x\in S
and
\mu\in]0,\widetilde{\mu}_{0}[
,

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2\mu}\|x-\operatorname{prox}_{\mu g}(x)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq g(x)-g(\operatorname{prox}_{\mu g}(x))

\displaystyle\leq g(x)+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\operatorname{prox}_{\mu g}(x)\|^{2}-\langle x^{*},\operatorname{prox}_{\mu g}(x)\rangle-\beta

\displaystyle\leq g(x)+2\rho(M+5\|x\|^{2})+\|x^{*}\|\|x-\operatorname{prox}_{\mu g}(x)\|+\|x^{*}\|\|x\|-\beta

\displaystyle\leq g(x)+\mu\|x^{*}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\mu}\|x-\operatorname{prox}_{\mu g}(x)\|^{2}+2\rho(M+5\|x\|^{2})+\|x^{*}\|\|x\|-\beta,

since
ab\leq\frac{c^{2}}{2}a^{2}+\frac{1}{2c^{2}}b^{2}
for
c=\sqrt{2\mu}
. Hence, for all
x\in S
and
\mu\in\left]0,\widetilde{\mu}_{0}\right[
, we have

\displaystyle\|x-\operatorname{prox}_{\mu g}(x)\|^{2}\leq 4\mu(g(x)+\mu\|x^{*}\|^{2}+2\rho(M+5\|x\|^{2})+\|x^{*}\|\|x\|-\beta).

Since
g
is Lipschitz in
S
and
S
is bounded, the right-hand side of the latter inequality is uniformly bounded in
S
. Thus, we can to find
\widetilde{m}>0
such that for all
x\in S
and
\mu\in\left]0,\widetilde{\mu}_{0}\right[

\displaystyle\|x-\operatorname{prox}_{\mu g}(x)\|\leq\sqrt{\mu}\widetilde{m}.

Set
\mu_{0}=\min\{\widetilde{\mu}_{0},\frac{1}{\widetilde{m}^{2}}\}\in]0,1[
. Then, for all
x\in S
and
\mu\in\left]0,\mu_{0}\right[
,
\|x-\operatorname{prox}_{\mu g}(x)\|\leq 1
, whence
\operatorname{prox}_{\mu g}(x)\in\widetilde{S}
. Finally, the result follows from the formula
\nabla g_{\mu}(x)\in\partial g(\operatorname{prox}_{\mu g}(x))
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and the
\ell
-Lipschitz continuity of
g
over in
\widetilde{S}
. ∎

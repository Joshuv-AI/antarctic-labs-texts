Constructing Chayet–Garibaldi algebras from affine vertex algebras (including the 3876 3876 -dimensional algebra for E 8 E_{8} )
Tom De Medts, Louis Olyslager

Abstract
In 2021, Maurice Chayet and Skip Garibaldi provided an explicit construction of a commutative non-associative algebra on the second smallest representation of E 8 E_{8} (of dimension 3875 3875 ) adjoined with a unit. In fact, they define such an algebra A ⁡ ( 𝔤 ) A(\mathfrak{g}) for each simple Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} , in terms of explicit but ad-hoc formulas. We discovered that their algebras A ⁡ ( 𝔤 ) A(\mathfrak{g}) have a natural interpretation in terms of affine vertex algebras, and their ad-hoc formulas take an extremely simple form in this new interpretation. It is our hope that this point of view will lead to a better understanding of this interesting class of algebras.

Introduction

The second smallest irreducible representation of algebraic groups of type
E_{8}
, which is of dimension
3875

This fact itself is not surprising, but it was not until a few years ago that this algebra has been constructed explicitly.
This had been achieved almost simultaneously by the first author and Michiel Van Couwenberghe
[
3
]
and by Maurice Chayet and Skip Garibaldi
[
2
]
. In fact, in both constructions, the algebra is a

The fact that the resulting algebra has as its automorphism group precisely the original group of type
E_{8}
(and nothing more) is a consequence of
[
6
]

The construction of Chayet and Garibaldi from
[

•

Their algebra is simply denoted by

The explicit formulas in the construction of Chayet and Garibaldi are very much

The construction in

It was an unexpected and pleasant surprise to us that we recognized the formulas of

More precisely, we will be able to construct the algebras

Vertex algebras have been introduced by Richard Borcherds in 1986 (see

Our main result can be summarized as follows.

Main Theorem
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra over a field
k
with
\Char(k)\neq 2
with
Z(\mathfrak{g})=0
, equipped with a non-degenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form
\kappa

Let
V
be the
\mathbb{N}
-graded affine vertex algebra
V=V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(1,0)
(which depends also on

) and let
L
be the quotient of

by its unique maximal graded ideal, which is a simple graded vertex algebra.
Let

be the subspace of

spanned by the elements

for all

Make

Definition 2.1
.

Let
k
be a commutative field, let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra over

\kappa\colon\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to k\colon(a,b)\mapsto\langle a,b\rangle

1.

We call

\langle[a,b],c\rangle=\langle a,[b,c]\rangle

We call

Remark 2.2
.

For a finite-dimensional

is always an invariant form. It is either identically zero or non-degenerate. The former may happen if

(see, e.g.,
[

, p. 17]
for a discussion). On the other hand, when

[

, Theorem 5.24]
), and by

, this is also true when

is the Lie algebra of an absolutely simple linear algebraic group

, where

is the Coxeter number, a constant depending only on the type of

.
Notice, for instance, that for type

that when

Definition 2.5

The

\operatorname{S^{2}}\mathfrak{g}\coloneqq\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}/\langle a\otimes b-b\otimes a\mid a,b\in\mathfrak{g}\rangle.

1.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
a,b\in\mathfrak{g}
and

. Then

1.

[\ad_{b},S(aa)]=2S(a[b,a])
;

2.

f\diamond S(bb)=\tfrac{1}{2}[\ad_{b},[\ad_{b},f]]+S(f(b)b)
.

Proof.

1.

This is the
\mathfrak{g}
-equivariance of

; see
[
2
, (4.4)]
. Alternatively, this can be computed directly from the defining equation (
2.8
).

2.

Since

is spanned by elements of the form

with

, it suffices to verify the claim for

. Just as in the proof of
[

, Lemma 4.5]
, it follows from

1

S(aa)\diamond S(bb)=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\ad_{b},[\ad_{b},S(aa)]\bigr]+S\bigl((\ad_{a}^{2}b)b\bigr)+2\langle a,b\rangle S(ab).

By (
2.8

S(aa)b=\ad_{a}^{2}b+2\langle a,b\rangle a
, so we get

S(aa)\diamond S(bb)=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\ad_{b},[\ad_{b},S(aa)]\bigr]+S\bigl((S(aa)b)\,b\bigr).\qed

The algebras from
[

are always unital; see
[
2
, Lemma 4.5]
. In our more general setting, we have the following result.

Lemma 2.11
.

The algebra
A(\mathfrak{g},\kappa)
is unital if and only if
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}\in\im S
, and then
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is the unit element.

Proof.

Suppose first that
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}\in\im S
. Then it follows immediately from
Lemma

2.10
Item

2
that
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}\diamond S(bb)=S(bb)
for all
b\in\mathfrak{g}
, so indeed
A(\mathfrak{g},\kappa)
is unital, with unit element
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Conversely, suppose that
A(\mathfrak{g},\kappa)
is unital and let
f\in\im S
be the unit element of
A(\mathfrak{g},\kappa)
.
Write
g\coloneqq f-\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
(as operator on
\mathfrak{g}
).
By
Lemma

2.10
Item

2
again, we have

S(bb)=f\diamond S(bb)=\tfrac{1}{2}[\ad_{b},[\ad_{b},f]]+S(f(b)b)

for all
b\in\mathfrak{g}
, which we rewrite as

S(g(b)b)+\tfrac{1}{2}[\ad_{b},[\ad_{b},g]]=0.

Applying this to the element
b
gives

S(g(b)b)b+\tfrac{1}{2}[b,[b,g(b)]]=0.

On the other hand, we have
S(g(b)b)b=\tfrac{1}{2}[b,[g(b),b]]+\langle g(b),b\rangle b+\langle b,b\rangle g(b)
by (
2.8
), so we get

\langle g(b),b\rangle b+\langle b,b\rangle g(b)=0

(2.12)

for all
b\in\mathfrak{g}
.

As
\kappa
is symmetric and non-degenerate, we can find an orthogonal basis
\mathcal{B}
for
\mathfrak{g}
.
For any
b\in\mathcal{B}
, we then have
\langle b,b\rangle\neq 0
and hence, by (
2.12
),
g(b)=\lambda_{b}b
for some
\lambda_{b}\in k
. However, (
2.12
) then implies
2\lambda_{b}\langle b,b\rangle b=0
and thus
\lambda_{b}=0
for all
b\in\mathcal{B}
. It follows that
g(b)=0
for all
b\in\mathcal{B}
, so we conclude that
g=0
and hence
f=\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
; in particular,
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}\in\im S
.
∎

We define a bilinear form

In

Vertex algebras

Vertex algebras have been introduced by Richard Borcherds in
[
1
]
.
We will only need vertex algebras arising from affine Lie algebras, which is a well understood class of vertex algebras;
we will recall their construction in
Section

4
.
We emphasize that unlike
[
10
]
, we allow a base field of arbitrary characteristic, following the approach from
[
11
]
.
Our setup is perhaps somewhat atypical, avoiding the formal Laurent series framework, to make everything as accessible as possible to non-experts. See also
Remark

3.2
below.

As in
[
11
, Section 2]
, we will use the obvious interpretation of binomial coefficients in arbitrary fields, i.e., for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we first define

\binom{m}{n}\coloneqq\frac{m(m-1)\dotsm(m+1-n)}{n!}\in\mathbb{Z}

and view it as an element of
k
via the unique ring homomorphism
\mathbb{Z}\to k
.
Notice, in particular, that
\binom{-1}{n}=(-1)^{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 3.1

(
[
1
, Section 4]
; see also
[
11
, Definition 2.1]
)
.

Let
V
be a vector space and let
\mathds{1}\in V
be a distinguished non-zero element called the
vacuum vector
.
For each integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, define a product map

V\times V\to V\colon(a,b)\mapsto a_{n}b.

We call this the
n
-th product
. We use the notation

a_{n}\colon V\to V\colon b\mapsto a_{n}b

for the left multiplication operators. Let
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
be a linear operator on
V
for each
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
V
is a
vertex algebra
if it satisfies the following axioms, for all
a,b,c\in V
and all
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
:

1.

a_{n}b=0
for
n
sufficiently large. (
truncation condition
)

2.

\mathds{1}_{n}a=\delta_{-1,n}a
. (
vacuum property
)

3.

a_{n}\mathds{1}=\mathcal{D}^{(-n-1)}a
. (
\mathcal{D}
-derivate formula
)

4.

a_{n}b=\sum_{i\geq 0}(-1)^{i+n+1}\mathcal{D}^{(i)}b_{n+i}a
. (
skew symmetry
)

5.

(a_{m}b)_{n}c=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}\binom{m}{i}\left(a_{m-i}b_{n+i}-(-1)^{m}b_{n+m-i}a_{i}\right)c
. (
iterate formula
)

Remark 3.2
.

There are many equivalent definitions of a vertex algebra. For example, the iterate formula
5
and skew symmetry
4
are equivalent, under the assumptions of the other axioms of a vertex algebra, to
Borcherds’ identity
:

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\binom{m}{i}\left(a_{p+i}b\right)_{m+n-i}c=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}\binom{p}{i}\left(a_{m+p-i}b_{n+i}-(-1)^{p}b_{n+p-i}a_{m+i}\right)c.

(3.3)

Vertex algebras are usually introduced using formal Laurent series. (See, for example,
[
10
, Definition 3.1.1]
and
[
9
, Definition 2.1]
.) This allows to combine the infinitely many products in one function
Y\colon V\to\End(V)\llbracket x,x^{-1}\rrbracket\colon a\mapsto\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{n}x^{-1-n}
, which therefore plays a key role in the theory.
We have chosen to avoid this approach to make the setup more accessible to non-experts. In addition, this has the advantage that we can directly work with the products that we need in our main theorem.
In this context, see also
[
10
, Proposition 3.6.6]
.

Notation 3.4

Lemma 3.5
.

Let

1.

\mathcal{D}^{(n)}=0
for all

2.

a_{n}\mathds{1}=0
for all

3.

\mathcal{D}^{(0)}=\mathrm{Id}

Proof.

Let

\mathcal{D}^{(-n-1)}a=a_{n}\mathds{1}=\sum_{i\geq 0}(-1)^{i+n+1}\mathcal{D}^{(i)}\mathds{1}_{n+i}a=\sum_{i\geq 0}\mathcal{D}^{(i)}\delta_{n+i,-1}a=0,

and

a=\mathds{1}_{-1}a=\sum_{i\geq 0}(-1)^{i}\mathcal{D}^{(i)}a_{i-1}\mathds{1}=\mathcal{D}^{(0)}a_{-1}\mathds{1}=(a_{-1}\mathds{1})_{-1}\mathds{1}\\
=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}\tbinom{-1}{i}\left(a_{-1-i}\mathds{1}_{-1+i}+\mathds{1}_{-2-i}a_{i}\right)\mathds{1}=a_{-1}\mathds{1}_{-1}\mathds{1}=a_{-1}\mathds{1}=\mathcal{D}^{(0)}a.\qed

We have the obvious notion of ideals in vertex algebras; see

, Definition 3.9.7 and Remark 3.9.8]

Definition 3.6
.

Let

be a vertex algebra.

1.

A subspace

of

if

for all

,

. Similarly, it is called a

if
a_{n}b\in I
for all

,

and

.

2.

A subspace

. An ideal

.

The following observation (over an arbitrary base field

.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
V
be a vertex algebra. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

I
is a right ideal;

(b)

I
is a left ideal and

for all

;

(c)

is an ideal.

Proof.

If

for all

, by the

-derivate formula (

). If

∎

We will later need the following related construction of an ideal starting from a left ideal.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let

be a left ideal in a vertex algebra

. Then

\bar{J}\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{D}^{(i)}J

Proof.

This is shown in the first paragraph of the proof of
[

, Lemma 2.7]
.
(Notice that the specific form of

from the statement of that result is irrelevant for this part of the proof.)
∎

We have the obvious notions of simple vertex algebras, maximal ideals, ideals generated by a subset and quotients of a vertex algebra by an ideal.

The affine vertex algebras that we will introduce in
Section

are
graded
vertex algebras:

Definition 3.9
.

Let
V
be a vertex algebra.

1.

[
9
, Definition 2.6]
Assume that

for certain subspaces
V_{(n)}
with
\mathds{1}\in V_{(0)}
.
Suppose that

Then we call

graded
.

2.

An ideal

of a graded vertex algebra

is

if

.

3.

We say that a vertex algebra is

if

are the the only graded ideals.

4.

We say that a vertex algebra has

5.

A vertex algebra is

if it has type O and, in addition,

.

Remark 3.11
.

In the previous definition, the notions

and

are non-standard, but the notion

is standard. When

is a vertex

algebra (VOA, see

, Definition 3.1.22]
for a definition) and has type O, then it is called a vertex operator algebra
of CFT type
; see
[
4
]
for the original definition.

The following observation is essentially the argument given in
[
10
, Remark 3.9.11]
.

Lemma 3.12
.

A graded vertex algebra
V=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}V_{(n)}
of type O has a unique maximal graded ideal

.
In particular,

is a simple graded vertex algebra of type O.

Proof.

Since
V_{(0)}=k\mathds{1}
, any proper graded ideal is a subspace of

. Hence the sum of all proper graded ideals does not contain

and is a maximal graded ideal.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

When

has characteristic zero and

is a VOA, then by

, Remarks 3.9.10 and 3.9.11]
,
every ideal is graded. In particular, the quotient

in

is then a simple vertex algebra.
This is false, however, in characteristic

[

]
gives examples of affine vertex algebras in characteristic

4
Vertex algebras related to affine Lie algebras

In this section, we construct a simple graded vertex algebra starting from the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and the form
\kappa=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
satisfying
2.3
. (In fact, not all of these assumptions are needed to construct the vertex algebra.)

We start by constructing an affine Lie algebra. Then we construct from its universal enveloping algebra a module for this affine Lie algebra. Next, we put a graded vertex algebra structure (of type O) on this module, by defining infinitely many products. The simple graded vertex algebra is then obtained by taking the quotient by its unique maximal graded ideal.

Our construction essentially follows
[
11
, §3]
, except that the explicit basis in
Corollary

4.6
is as in
[
10
, p. 206]
. Usually, a constant
\ell
is involved as well in the construction of the vertex algebra, but we decide to incorporate this constant into the bilinear form
\kappa
, which simplifies the resulting formulas without losing any generality.

Definition 4.1
.

The
affine Lie algebra
corresponding to the pair
(\mathfrak{g},\kappa)
is the Lie algebra with underlying vector space

\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\coloneqq\mathfrak{g}\otimes k[t,t^{-1}]\,\oplus\,k\mathbf{k}

and with Lie bracket given by

\left[a\otimes t^{m},b\otimes t^{n}\right]\coloneqq\left[a,b\right]\otimes t^{m+n}+m\left\langle a,b\right\rangle\delta_{m+n,0}\mathbf{k}

for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{g}
and
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
\mathbf{k}
is a central element. We use the notation
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}
when we want to explicitly mention the bilinear form
\kappa=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.
It is a common and convenient convention to write

a(n)\coloneqq a\otimes t^{n}

for all
a\in\mathfrak{g}
and all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. The defining equation for the Lie bracket can then be rewritten as

\left[a(m),b(n)\right]\coloneqq\left[a,b\right](m+n)+m\left\langle a,b\right\rangle\delta_{m+n,0}\mathbf{k}.

(4.2)

As in
[
10
, (6.2.6)]
, we also define the Lie subalgebra

\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\leq 0}\coloneqq\mathfrak{g}\otimes k[t]\,\oplus\,k\mathbf{k}=\left\langle a(n)\mid a\in\mathfrak{g},n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\right\rangle\oplus\langle\mathbf{k}\rangle.

(Notice the potentially confusing notation
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\leq 0}
, but see
[
10
, Remark 6.2.1]
for an explanation.)

Next, we construct a
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
-module.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
U(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
be the universal enveloping algebra of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Let
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\leq 0}
act on
k\mathds{1}
(on the left) by setting

\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathds{1}\coloneqq\mathds{1}\quad\text{and}\quad a(n)\cdot\mathds{1}\coloneqq 0

for all
a\in\mathfrak{g}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and let
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\leq 0}
act on the right on
U(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
by multiplication.
Now define

V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(1,0)\coloneqq U(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})\otimes_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\leq 0}}k\mathds{1}.

This is a (left)
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
-module by multiplication (and hence it is also a left
U(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
-module).

Remark 4.4
.

This is the place where usually the constant
\ell\neq 0
comes into play, by defining

V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(\ell,0)
as above, but with the equation

\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathds{1}\coloneqq\mathds{1}\quad\text{ replaced by }\quad\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathds{1}\coloneqq\ell\mathds{1}.

This constant is called the
level
of the vertex algebra.
It turns out (and is well known) that
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}
and
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\ell\kappa}
are isomorphic, with an explicit isomorphism given by
a(n)\mapsto a(n)
and
\mathbf{k}\mapsto\ell\mathbf{k}
.
Under this isomorphism, the resulting vertex algebras
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}}(\ell,0)
and
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\ell\kappa}}(1,0)
are isomorphic (as vertex algebras, cf.
Proposition

4.10
). The level only becomes relevant when
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}}(\ell,0)
is studied in the context of vertex
operator
algebras.
Notice, in particular, that in
[
10
, Remark 6.2.15]
, the affine Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
is constructed using the scaling
\kappa\coloneqq\frac{1}{2h^{\vee}}K
, and the vertex operator algebra
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{\kappa}}(1,0)
obtained in
[
10
, Theorem 6.2.18]
then corresponds exactly to the setup of Chayet and Garibaldi; see
Remark

2.9
.
See also
Remark

5.25
below.

In order to construct a basis for
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(1,0)
, we use the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem.

Theorem 4.5

(PBW theorem)
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a Lie algebra over a field
k
with totally ordered basis
\mathcal{B}
.
Then the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{h})
has a basis

\{a^{(1)}a^{(2)}\dotsm a^{(n)}\mid a^{(1)},\dots,a^{(n)}\in\mathcal{B}\text{ with }a^{(1)}\geq a^{(2)}\geq\dots\geq a^{(n)}\}.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
a^{(i)},i\in\mathbf{I}
be an ordered basis for
\mathfrak{g}
(where
\mathbf{I}
is some ordered index set).
Let “
\hskip 2.15277pt>\hskip-0.86108pt
” be the lexicographical order on
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbf{I}
, i.e., we set

(m,i)>(n,j)\iff m>n,\text{ or }m=n\text{ and }i>j,

for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
i,j\in\mathbf{I}
.
Then the elements of the form

a^{(i_{1})}(-m_{1})\dotsm a^{(i_{r})}(-m_{r})\mathds{1}\in V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(1,0)

(4.7)

with
(m_{1},i_{1})\geq\dots\geq(m_{r},i_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\times\mathbf{I}
, and
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, form a basis for
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(1,0)
.

Definition 4.8
.

Fix a basis for
\mathfrak{g}
and for
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(1,0)
as in
Corollary

4.6
. For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we let

V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(1,0)_{(n)}

be the subspace spanned by the basis elements (
4.7
) with
m_{1}+\dots+m_{r}=n
.
In particular,
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(1,0)_{(n)}=0
for

. We call

the

of this subspace.

Note that this gives
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(1,0)
the structure of a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space

V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(1,0)=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(1,0)_{(n)}.

Remark 4.9
.

Observe that
\mathfrak{g}\cong V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(1,0)_{(1)}
as vector spaces, via the isomorphism

a\mapsto a(-1)\mathds{1}.

We can now invoke
[
11
, Proposition 3.3]
, translated to our setup.

Proposition 4.10
.

There exists a unique vertex algebra structure on
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(1,0)
determined by the conditions that
\mathds{1}\in V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(1,0)_{(0)}
is the vacuum vector and that

(a(-1)\mathds{1})_{n}b=a(n)b\qquad\text{for all }a\in\mathfrak{g},b\in V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(1,0)\text{ and }n\in\mathbb{Z}.

We use the vector space isomorphism from
Remark

4.9
to make some identifications.

Notation 4.11
.

Using the identification from
Remark

4.9
together with
Proposition

4.10
, we have
a_{n}b=a(n)b
for all
a\in\mathfrak{g}
and all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
By repeatedly applying this, we get

a^{(1)}_{-m_{1}}\dotsm a^{(r)}_{-m_{r}}b=a^{(1)}(-m_{1})\dotsm a^{(r)}(-m_{r})b

(4.12)

for all
a^{(1)},\dots,a^{(r)}\in\mathfrak{g}
,
b\in V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(1,0)
and
m_{1},\dots m_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
In particular, we can rewrite the elements of the form (
4.7
), which implies that we can write each element of
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(1,0)
as a linear combination of elements of the form

a^{(1)}_{-m_{1}}\dotsm a^{(r)}_{-m_{r}}\mathds{1}

(4.13)

with all
m_{i}\geq 1
.

The following result is now an immediate translation of the defining equation (
4.2
) for affine Lie algebras.

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
V=V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(1,0)
.
Let
a,b\in\mathfrak{g}=V_{(1)}
and let
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then

a_{m}b_{n}=b_{n}a_{m}+[a,b]_{m+n}+m\delta_{m,-n}\langle a,b\rangle.

Proof.

By (
4.2
), we have in
U(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
that

a(m)b(n)=b(n)a(m)+\left[a,b\right](m+n)+m\left\langle a,b\right\rangle\delta_{m+n,0}\mathbf{k}.

Applying this identity on any
c\in V
, we get, by (
4.12
), that

a_{m}b_{n}c=a(m)b(n)c=b(n)a(m)c+\left[a,b\right](m+n)c+m\left\langle a,b\right\rangle\delta_{m+n,0}\mathbf{k}c\cr=b_{n}a_{m}c+[a,b]_{m+n}c+m\delta_{m,-n}\langle a,b\rangle c,

because
\mathbf{k}
acts as the scalar
1
on
V
.
∎

In fact, the vertex algebra
V=V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(1,0)
is a graded vertex algebra of type O.

Proposition 4.15
.

Let
V=V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}}(1,0)
.

1.

The grading on
V
from
Definition

4.8
is a vertex algebra grading.

2.

For all
a,b\in V
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, there is some
u\in U(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
such that
a_{n}b=ub
.

Proof.

By linearity, it suffices to prove both statements for elements of the form (
4.13
).
So assume
a=a^{(1)}_{-m_{1}}\dots a^{(r)}_{-m_{r}}\mathds{1}
and
b\in V_{(m)}
with
a^{(1)},\dots,a^{(r)}\in\mathfrak{g}
,
r,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Write
l:=m_{1}+\dots+m_{r}
, hence
a\in V_{(l)}
.
We claim that

a_{n}b\in V_{(l+m-1-n)}\quad\text{and there is some }u\in U(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})\text{ such that }a_{n}b=ub.

(4.16)

We prove this claim by induction on
r
. For
r=0
, we have
a=\mathds{1}
, so the claim follows from the vacuum property (
Definition

3.1
2
) by taking
u\coloneqq\delta_{-1,n}1
(where
1
is the unit in
U(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
).

Assume now that
r>0
and set
a^{\prime}\coloneqq a^{(2)}_{-m_{2}}\dots a^{(r)}_{-m_{r}}\mathds{1}
. By the iterate formula (
Definition

3.1
5
), we have

a_{n}b=\bigl(a^{(1)}_{-m_{1}}a^{\prime}\bigr)_{n}b=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}\binom{-m_{1}}{i}\bigl(a^{(1)}_{-m_{1}-i}a^{\prime}_{n+i}-(-1)^{m_{1}}a^{\prime}_{n-m_{1}-i}a_{i}^{(1)}\bigr)b.

By the truncation condition (
Definition

3.1
1
), this reduces to a finite sum: there is an
I
such that for all

, we have both
a^{\prime}_{n+i}b=0
and

. By the induction hypothesis, each term lies in

and there are
u^{(i)}
and
v^{(i)}
in
U(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
such that

\displaystyle a^{(1)}_{-m_{1}-i}a^{\prime}_{n+i}b

\displaystyle=a^{(1)}_{-m_{1}-i}u^{(i)}b,

\displaystyle a^{\prime}_{n-m_{1}-i}a^{(1)}_{i}b=v^{(i)}a^{(1)}_{i}b.

Now set

u\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{I}(-1)^{i}\binom{m_{1}}{i}\bigl(a^{(1)}(-m_{1}-i)u^{(i)}-(-1)^{m_{1}}v^{(i)}a^{(1)}(i)\bigr)\in U(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}).

Then it is clear that
a_{n}b=ub
.
This proves the claim (
4.16
).
∎

Corollary 4.17
.

Any
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
-submodule of

is a left ideal.

a_{-1}[[a,b],b]-[[a,b],b]_{-1}a+b_{-1}[a,[a,b]]-[a,[a,b]]_{-1}b\\
=\bigl(\bigl[a,[[a,b],b]\bigr]+\bigl[b,[a,[a,b]]\bigr]\bigr)_{-2}\mathds{1}.

By the Jacobi identity, the right hand side is equal to

.

Lemma 5.17

We have

Proof.

\displaystyle T(a_{-1}a)b

.

We have an algebra isomorphism

Lemma 5.21
.

The ideal of

generated by the subspace

.
In particular, it is a proper graded ideal.

Proof.

Let

.

of

is then precisely the two-sided ideal generated by

.
Our goal is to show that

is a proper ideal.

Our first and most important step is to show that

.
We claim that

a_{n}d\in\ker T\qquad\text{for all }a\in\mathfrak{g},\ d\in\ker T,\ n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

(5.22)

For

For

(by

For

.
Finally, for

, we note that by the iterate formula (

Remark 5.25
.

The vertex algebra

is a vertex
operator
algebra if and only if the corresponding algebra

is

. In this case, the conformal vector
\omega
corresponds to the unit in

under the isomorphism of
Theorem

5.24
, and the central charge is
4\tau(\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}},\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}})
.

We briefly explain why this is the case, leaving the details to the reader.
We rely on
[
11
]
for the setup of vertex operator algebras over arbitrary fields
k
with
\Char(k)\neq 2
; see, in particular, its Definition 5.11 for the definition of a vertex operator algebra. In particular, we use the standard notation

for the modes of the conformal vector

.

Assume first that
L
is a vertex operator algebra with conformal vector
\omega\in L_{(2)}
.
Then
\omega_{1}
acts on each

as multiplication by

, so in particular
\omega_{1}|_{L_{(1)}}=\mathrm{Id}
.
Using
Lemma

5.3
and
L(1)\omega=0
, it follows that
\omega\in L_{(2)}^{\mathrm{sym}}
.
It is then not hard to show that under the isomorphism from
Theorem

5.24
,
\omega
corresponds to the unit of

.

Conversely, assume that
A
is unital; by
Lemma

2.11
, the the unit is
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}\in\im S
, so we can write
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}=\sum_{i}S(u^{(i)}u^{(i)})
.
Let
\omega\in L_{(2)}^{\mathrm{sym}}
be the element corresponding to
\mathrm{Id}_{\mathfrak{g}}\in A
under the isomorphism from
Theorem

5.24
.
To show that
L
is a vertex operator algebra with conformal vector
\omega
, we can follow the procedure from
[
10
, Theorem 6.2.16]
(the so-called Segal–Sugawara construction, see e.g.,
[
5
, §2.5.10 and §3.4.8]
), but of course our setup is different. While
[
10
, Theorem 6.2.16]
starts from the assumption that the Casimir operator

acts as a scalar, we start from the Chayet–Garibaldi algebra

. Just as in the proof of
loc. cit.
, it suffices to show that

for all

and all

(this is (6.2.51) in
loc. cit.
).
The proof of (6.2.52) holds
mutatis mutandis
, but (6.2.53) and (6.2.54) require a different “operator product expansion”-style computation, using
Lemma

4.20
Items

3
and
11
together with the fact that
a_{2}\omega=0
.
The rest of the proof then again holds

, showing that

is indeed a vertex operator algebra of with conformal vector
\omega
. Only the computation of the central charge (6.2.58) requires a different approach using
Proposition

5.12
.

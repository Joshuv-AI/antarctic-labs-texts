Electron-phonon interaction and superconductivity in the silicon-boron-carbon monolayers with high stability
Authors: Jingjing Wang, Panlong Kong, Zaifu Jiang, Dingmei Zhang, YuKai You, Bei Hu, Shihua Fu, Yue Fang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryRecently, considerable attention has been given to the exploration of superconductivity in low-dimensional materials. By employing unbiased structure search and first-principles calculations, we systematically investigate the structural and electronic properties of the predicted two-dimensional (2D) compounds SiBC4, SiBC6, and SiB4C in the monolayer limit. These structures demonstrate high thermal stability at 1,500 K and meet the criteria for mechanical and dynamic stability. Most importantly, they are identified as intrinsic superconductors with superconducting transition temperatures (Tc) of 17.59, 12.18, and 10.91 K, respectively. The van Hove singularity caused by the high density of states near the Fermi surface plays a crucial role in the emergence of superconductivity. Furthermore, by applying compressive/tensile strains on SiBC4, it is observed that the tensile strain dramatically softens the phonon branch, as well as increases the electron-phonon coupling (EPC) and Tc. Our findings not only contribute to the development of novel 2D superconducting materials but also present an excellent platform to investigate the role of van Hove singularity in emergent superconductivity.Subject areas: Physics, Condensed matter physics, Superconductivity

Introduction

Graphene, as a typical two-dimensional (2D) material, has garnered significant research attention over the past two decades due to its exceptional structural, mechanical, and electrical properties1 and has also gained popularity in diverse fields, including gas sensor device and2,3,4 energy storage device.5,6,7 However, the absence of a superconducting state in pristine graphene is mainly attributed to the zero density of states at Fermi level. If realized, it not only has potential applications in the construction of superconducting quantum devices but also has great scientific value. In 2007, superconductivity in graphene layers was induced by connecting two superconducting electrodes via the proximity effect.8 In the same year, Uchoa et al. investigated the plasmon-mediated superconducting mechanism in graphene and deduced the mean-field phase diagram.9 Following that, graphene with deposits of alkaline metals, such as LiC6 and CaC6, have been predicted to exhibit superconductivity mediated by phonons, with transition temperatures (Tc) of 1.4 and 8.1 K,10 respectively, and Li-deposited graphene with a Tc of 5.9 K was realized experimentally.11 In addition, aluminum-deposited graphene (AlC8), with proper doping and stretching, has achieved a Tc of 22.2 K.12 Previous theoretical studies have focused on the effects of electron-phonon coupling (EPC) on graphene under heavy hole/electron doping and biaxial tensile strain (BTS).13,14,15,16,17,18,19 A high Tc of approximately 31.6 K was estimated in hole-doped graphene at a BTS of 16.5%.18 In recent years, Cao and co-workers found that a correlated unconventional superconductivity existed in bilayer graphene with a small twist angle of 1.1°. For electrostatic doping, it acts similarly to cuprate superconductors and has a Tc of 1.7 K.20,21

Besides graphene, superconductors with light elements (e.g., Si, C, and B atoms) are not only interesting for their chemistry and physics fundamentals but also crucial for practical applications of superconductivity, owing to their low toxicity, cost-effectiveness, and high abundance.22,23,24,25,26,27,28,29 Particularly, carbon- and boron-based superconductors have received increasing attention for their light weight, flexibility, and ease of design, apart from the inherent advantages of light elements.28,29,30,31,32,33,34,35 Earlier, Dai et al. predicted thin-film superconductivity in B2C monolayer.29 Chen et al. theoretically studied exotic superconducting phases in graphene-like single-sheet material BC3 doped to its type-II van Hove singularity (VHS).28 More recently, Liu et al. predicted that pristine B3N is a phonon-mediated superconductor with Tc of 14.1 K.36 Furthermore, boron-doped silicon and boron-doped silicon carbide have attracted growing interest due to their superconducting potential, which exhibits a rising Tc with increasing boron doping.37,38 Therefore, the 2D Si-B-C monolayer that can serve as a potential superconductor deserves further investigations.

In this work, combined with swarm-intelligence structure search and the first principles calculation, we assess the 2D ternary systems SiBCx, SiByC, and SizBC (x, y, z = 1–6), which are graphene-like honeycomb lattices. The three fully planar monolayers SiBC4, SiBC6, and SiB4C with high stability are predicted to superconduct at Tc in the range of 10–18 K (based on the McMillan-Allen-Dynes theory), which can be applied for the experimental synthesis of 2D materials without any substrate- or strain-induced effects.

Results

Structure and stability

To assess different configurations of 2D ternary system SiBCx, SiByC, and SizBC (x, y, z = 1–6), comprehensive structural search was performed using the CALYPSO code. Following an in-depth structural analysis, three global minimum hitherto unknown graphene-like structures were obtained for 2D Si-B-C, SiBC4 (with the space group P-6, Figures 1A and 1B), SiBC6 (with the space group Pmm2, Figures 1E and 1F), and SiB4C (with the space group Pmm2, Figures 1I and 1J). The fully planar structure is a striking feature, which improves experimental synthesis and accelerates ion transport because of its substrate compatibility.39,40,41

As depicted in Figure 1C, Si2BC7 is a fundamental building block (FBB) of the SiBC4, featuring two inequivalent C sites. One of them is connected to Si, B, and C sites, and the other one is connected to three C sites. Each Si site and B site is connected to three C sites, resulting in two types of bonds. Along the zigzag direction, Si and B atoms present an alternate ordering (Figure 1A). Si2B2C12 is an FBB of the SiBC6 monolayer (Figure 1G), which contains interconnected SiC5, BC5, and SiBC4 rings. The C chains extend along the direction of the armchair, which may be attributed to the C-richness (Figure 1E). Si2B6C2 is an FBB of the SiB4C monolayer (Figure 1K), which consists of interconnected SiB3C2 and Si2B3C rings. Unlike the previous monolayers, complete B chains follow along the zigzag direction, while Si and C atoms order along the armchair direction (Figure 1I). The SiBC4, SiBC6, and SiB4C monolayers were observed for their electron localization function at the atomic centers (Figures 1D–1I), indicating strong covalent bonding. There is asymmetric relationship between the C-Si and C-B bonds, which may be attributed to polarization.

The stability of the SixByCz monolayers is a crucial factor that influences the experimental process. Cohesive energy is a well-accepted parameter for evaluating the stability of the materials. The cohesive energy is calculated as Ecoh = (xESi + yEB + zEC − ESixByCz)/(x+y+z), where ESi, EB, EC, and ESixByCz report the total energies of Si, B, C, and the SixByCz monolayer, respectively. Large cohesive energies of 6.65, 6.91, and 5.95 eV/atom are observed for the SiBC4, SiBC6, and SiB4C monolayers, respectively, suggesting the high stability of these monolayers. The cohesive energies of these SixByCz surpass those predicted by computational approaches and are even comparable to the previous experimentally synthesized and theoretical reported 2D materials, including graphene (7.91 eV/atom), SixC1−x (<6 eV/atom),42 BC3 (6.86 eV/atom),43 B4Si4 (5.44 eV/atom),44 and silicene (3.91 eV/atom). Therefore, it is plausible to fabricate the predicted SixByCz under suitable experimental conditions.

The vibrational normal mode of these SixByCz monolayers was also evaluated by calculating the phonon dispersion along the high-symmetry points, and we will discuss phonon dispersion in more detail in the next section. Additionally, the thermal stability of 2D monolayers is also crucial for practical applications in electrode films or nanoelectronic devices. The thermal stability of the SiBC4, SiBC6, and SiB4C monolayers was verified by performing molecular dynamics simulations. As presented in Figures 2A–2C, total energies and temperature (insets on the left) are varied with time for the SixByCz monolayers. The total energy of SixByCz monolayers can fluctuate at a constant temperature of 1,500 K. The snapshots after 10 ps for each SixByCz monolayer are displayed in insets on the right, where the structure is only slightly deformed. These findings demonstrate an outstanding thermal stability of SixByCz monolayers.

Mechanical strength and durability are critical factors that determine the suitability of 2D materials for various applications. According to the obtained elastic constants (Table 1), the SiBC4, SiBC6, and SiB4C monolayers all meet the criteria of mechanical stability: C66 > 0 and C11C22 − C2 12 > 0. For hexagonal symmetry, C11 = C22, the critical conditions for the mechanical stability are attenuated to |C11| > |C22| and C66 > 0.45 Moreover, the ideal tensile strength of the SixByCz family is assessed by measuring the stress variations in response to the biaxial and uniaxial (armchair or zigzag) tensile strains. As shown in Figures 2D–2F, the ideal tensile strengths of SiBC4, SiBC6, and SiB4C are 22.31, 28.23, and 19.26 N/m corresponding to the critical strains of 20%, 25%, and 17% under uniaxial strain, respectively. The ideal tensile strengths are 18.90, 18.11, and 10.48 N/m corresponding to the same critical strain of 17%, 13%, and 8%, respectively, under biaxial strain. The larger Young’s modulus and critical strain endow the SixByCz family with remarkable mechanical properties and significant potential application in nanomechanics. In the SiBC4 and SiBC6 monolayers, Poisson’s ratio and Young’s modulus appear to be isotropic, while they appear to be anisotropic in SiB4C monolayer (Figure 3). The Young’s modulus of these structures exceeds that of silicene (60–63 N/m)46 and is comparable to that of borophene (189–222 N/m),47 while also having a large Poisson’s ratio. In addition, the mechanical stability remains excellent under various strains (Table 1). The Poisson’s ratio and Young’s modulus of SiBC4, SiBC6, and SiB4C monolayers increase/decrease under compressive/tensile strains, while an obvious anisotropy occurs in the SiBC4 and SiBC6 monolayers (Figure 3). The above analysis shows that these monolayers have excellent mechanical properties.

Electronic characteristics

The orbital resolved band structures, electronic density of states, and the corresponding Fermi surfaces (EF) of all SixByCz monolayers are shown in Figures 4A–4F. It is clear that these materials are all metallic as various bands cross the Fermi energy level. For SiBC4, the C 2p orbital dominates the bands at the Fermi level (FL), whereas the Si and B atomic orbitals have nearly no components at the FL. There are two bands composed of the C σ states crossing the FL (Figure 4A), leading to two pairs of hole pockets around the BZ center and a covalent-state-driven metallization, as consistent with the case in bulk MgB2,48 boron-doped diamond,49 and two-dimensional boron sheets.50 Within the Brillouin zone, the C σ states give rise to one relatively circular Fermi surface and one petal-like Fermi surface (referred to as the σ1 and σ2 sheets, respectively), as indicated in Figure 4D. For SiBC6, the band structure exhibits that only one band can cross the FL along X-S and S-Y, respectively. As shown in Figure 4E, the EF includes one fan-shaped pocket centered on S. The C 2p is a predominant component of the electronic states located near FL and exhibits weak hybridization with minority state of B 2p orbital in Figure 4B.

It is noteworthy that the band across the FL forms two hole-like pockets along the path Γ-X and Y-Γ, whereas an electron-like pocket along the path X-S-Y. Interestingly, the band behaves as two saddle points near the FL at the high symmetry points X and Y, resulting in the formation of VHSs related to larger localized electronic states around FL (Figure 4B). The VHSs at high-symmetry point X and Y show that due to the hybridization of the B and C 2p orbitals, the neighboring B and C atoms are covalently linked to a weak σ bond, with a saddle point centered at the middle, which is consistent with the C-C σ bond in a diamond. These results indicate that the occurrence of VHS is associated with the formation of B-C σ bonds. Figure 4B displays a sharp spike of total density of state near the FL, resulting from the existence of two VHSs. For SiB4C, there are two, one, two, and one bands crossing the FL along Γ-X, X-S, S-Y, and Y-Γ high-symmetry paths (Figure 4C), respectively. There are 2 pockets centered at X and S and one pocket along the Γ-Y line as displayed in Figure 4F. It can be seen that the orbital-resolved band near the FL primarily consists of s + px,y orbitals (σ bond) and pz orbital (π bond), respectively. Interestingly, there is also a VHS at X about 0.2 eV above FL. Meanwhile, the VHS at X shows that due to the hybridization between three types of atoms, neighboring atoms are covalently linked to a weak π bond. Such VHSs in SiBC6 and SiB4C also appear in the compressed hydride superconductors LaH1051 and H3S52 and are reported to play a vital role in improving the EPC. The presence of σ1, σ2, and π Fermi sheets and VHSs’ singular electron properties imply that the SixByCz monolayers may be a candidate with split superconducting.

Additionally, to thoroughly investigate the effect of strain on the electronic properties of all three structures, we have also computed their band structures under strain, as shown in Figures S1–S3. For the SiBC4 monolayer, tensile strains in armchair, zigzag, and biaxial directions enhance the VHS near the Fermi level, while compressive strains may introduce small band gaps or reduce states at the Fermi level, potentially suppressing it. In the SiBC6 monolayer, tensile strain flattens the bands and amplifies the VHS effect, although this effect is less pronounced than in other structures due to the higher carbon content. In contrast, compressive strain disperses the bands and consequently lowers the density of states near the Fermi level. Finally, for the SiB4C monolayer, tensile strains concentrate electronic states near the Fermi level to strengthen VHS and electron-phonon interactions, akin to boron-rich superconductors, whereas compressive strains weaken these features and may induce gap.

EPC and superconductivity

The emergence of VHS near the Fermi energy level has an important induction and influence on the properties of materials, especially superconductivity.53,54 The singular electron properties of SixByCz monolayers inspired us to assess the potential phonon-regulated superconductivity. The calculated phonon dispersions, EPC parameter λqv, projected phonon density of state, Eliashberg spectral function α2F(ω), and λ(ω) as a function of the phonon frequency are presented in Figures 5A–5C. Obviously, there is no imaginary frequency in the phonon dispersion across the whole Brillouin zone, suggesting the dynamical stability of these three monolayers. The EPC distributed in the BZ and integrated over all phonon branches is presented in Figures 5A–5C (red ball mark). For those monolayers, the phonon modes with energy lower than 50 cm−1 lead to the electron-phonon interaction, accounting for approximately 80% of the total electron-phonon interaction. It has been found that phonon softening occurs in phonon branches along Γ-M and K-Γ directions for SiBC4, Γ-X and Y-Γ directions for SiBC6, and Γ-X and S-Y directions for SiB4C. As previous reports have shown, phonon softening plays a key role in enhancing EPC.18 The soft modes of SiBC4 and SiBC6 are markedly related to the vibrations of C atoms, while the soft modes of SiB4C are primarily concerned with the vibration of B atoms. Based on the calculated EPC λqv, it is found that the low-energy phonon soft modes are important to realize a high electron-phonon interaction. Particularly, mode-1 and mode-2 of monolayer SiBC4, mode-3 and mode-4 of monolayer SiBC6, and mode-5 and mode-6 of monolayer SiB4C are acoustic soft modes with a significant weight of λqv. Figures 5D–5F demonstrate the vibrational patterns of these phonon modes. For SiBC4 and SiB4C monolayers, the soft mode-1 and mode-2 and mode-5 and mode-6 show in-plane and out-plane vibrations, respectively, while both soft mode-3 and mode-4 for SiBC6 are from out-plane vibration. Based on the projected phonon density of state, the vibrations of Si and C atoms in SiBC4 and SiBC6 are related to the low-frequency phonon branches, whereas vibrations of C and B atoms are significantly associated with the high- and middle-frequency zones, respectively. The low-frequency phonon branch of SiB4C is associated with the vibrations of Si and C atoms, while the high-frequency region is derived from the vibrations of B and C atoms. The contribution of various phonon bands can also be observed from the EPC parameter λ(ω). The first phonon band typically shows a maximum contribution to total EPC. The λ(ω) values of 0.86, 0.93, and 1.12 indicate that the SiBC4, SiBC6, and SiB4C monolayers belong to intermediate EPC superconductors. Then, the Tc values are calculated according to the McMillan equation. The obtained Tc values are 17.59, 12.18, and 10.91 K for SiBC4, SiBC6, and SiB4C, respectively.

Strain is a promising approach for tuning the dynamic and electronic properties of 2D materials. The effects of strain on superconducting materials have been widely evaluated according to the BCS theory. Superconductivity was found to be improved by biaxial strain in various 2D materials,18 including tungsten boride55 and phosphorus carbide.56 Herein, we considered only the biaxial strain effect of SiBC4 monolayer, which exhibits the largest Tc among the three systems in the absence of strain. Biaxial strain (range = −5% to 5%) was applied to SiBC4 at an interval of 1% to induce the conditions of specimen growth on a substrate, where positive and negative values represent tensile and compressive strains, respectively. Under compression strain, an imaginary phonon frequency was detected for SiBC4 at −2% strain (Figure 6A), indicating that compressive strain can disrupt the stability of SiBC4. However, no imaginary frequency was observed in tensile strain (Figure 6B). We presented the varying effects of strains from −1% to 5% on the density of state at the FL N(EF), λ, ωlog, and Tc in Figures 6C–6F. Generally speaking, the compressive strain exhibits a negative effect on the superconducting properties, as it can reduce the density of state at both FL and EPC intensity. Moreover, the tensile strain can increase the EPC constant from λ = 0.86 in the strain-free condition to λ = 1.04 at a 5% tensile strain, which may be attributed to the change in the properties of electrons in the structure. As shown in the Figure S1, the electronic band structure of SiBC4 exhibits clear strain sensitivity. Under compressive strain (−5%), the bands near the Fermi level shift upward, resulting in a reduction in the density of states at FL. In contrast, under tensile strain (+5%), we observe a notable downward shift of several bands, leading to an enhanced density of states near FL. This is particularly evident near the M and K points in the Brillouin zone, where band flattening occurs, suggesting an approach to VHS under strain, which is consistent with the observed increase in EPC and superconducting Tc. Based on the McMillan-Allen-Dynes equation, Tc is associated with both λ and ωlog. The ωlog reduces with the tensile strain, and the tensile effect from λ is reflected in the behavior of Tc, which increases from 17.59 K without the strain to 20.48 K at a 5% tensile strain for SiBC4. Nevertheless, our theoretical research needs to be further verified by future experiments.

Conclusions

In this study, by employing unbiased structure search and first-principles calculations, we predict three lowest energy 2D Si-B-C compounds (SiBC4, SiBC6, and SiB4C) and systematically investigate their geometrical structures, bonding characteristics, thermodynamic and dynamic stability, mechanical properties, electronic structures, and superconductivity. The ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulation indicates that these 2D materials can maintain their original configurations at 1,500 K, suggesting the excellent thermodynamic stability of these Si-B-C monolayers. The absence of imaginary modes in the whole BZ and obtained elastic constant indicates that they are all dynamically and mechanically stable. Thus, the predicted 2D Si-B-C monolayers are expected to be synthesized experimentally. These three 2D Si-B-C materials are assessed as intrinsic superconductors within the Tc range of 10.91–17.59 K. Interestingly, VHSs appear in the bands of both SiBC6 and SiB4C structures, which play a key role in inducing and influencing superconductivity. Furthermore, the effect of compressive/tensile strains on the superconductivity of 2D materials was studied. It is found that tensile strain markedly improves the EPC and elevates the Tc of SiBC4 monolayer, which may be attributed to the softening of lower acoustic-phonon branches. Our predictions will enrich the database of 2D light elements and stimulate the experimental synthesis of novel 2D materials and the search for superconductivity.

Limitations of the study

While this work provides a comprehensive theoretical investigation of the predicted SiBC4, SiBC6, and SiB4C monolayers, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the study is based entirely on computational simulations and predictions; the experimental synthesis of these proposed structures has not yet been achieved, and their practical feasibility remains to be verified. Second, the calculated superconducting transition temperatures rely on the McMillan-Allen-Dynes formula within the BCS framework, which, while widely used, incorporates approximations such as the choice of the Coulomb pseudopotential (μ∗ = 0.1) that may affect the absolute Tc values. Third, the strain-tuning analysis was performed only for the SiBC4 monolayer under biaxial strain, leaving the effects of other strain types (e.g., uniaxial) and their impact on the other two systems unexplored. Furthermore, the study assumes ideal, defect-free monolayers, whereas real-world samples will inevitably contain impurities, vacancies, or substrate-induced effects that could significantly alter the electronic properties and superconducting behavior. Finally, the role of many-body effects beyond the standard EPC theory, such as strong electron correlations or plasmonic contributions, has not been assessed and could be relevant given the presence of vHSs near the Fermi level.

Method details

The first-principles swarm-intelligence structure search was performed in this study via Crystal structure AnaLYsis by Particle Swarm Optimization (CALYPSO) code.57,58 It can effectively determine metastable and stable structures in a given chemical molecule, which has been widely applied to 2D materials.59,60,61,62,63 According to the density functional theory, electronic property calculation and structure relaxation were conducted using the VASP package,64,65 and the exchange correlation interaction was simulated using the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof generalized-gradient approximation66,67,68 after the adequate convergence tests. An energy cutoff base on the plane wave basis set is set to 600 eV. To reduce the interaction between adjacent layers, a sufficiently thick 20 Å vacuum was set up along the z axis direction. The EPC and critical temperature of superconductivity were calculated by the Quantum ESPRESSO code within the Bardeen-Cooper-Schrieffer (BCS) theory69 and density functional perturbation theory (DFPT).70 To achieve the minimum-energy structure, we employed the Broyden-Fletcher-Goldfarb-Shanno algorithm to fully relax the ionic positions within the cell. The electron-ion interactions and electron-phonon coupling were simulated by utilizing the norm-conserving Vanderbilt pseudopotentials.71 The energy cutoffs for the charge density and electronic wave functions in the plane-wave basis were set to 800 and 80 Ry, respectively. The convergence criteria for force and energy were set as 10−6 and 10−7 Ry a.u.−1.

The first-principles molecular-dynamics simulations were conducted at 300–1500 K for a 4 × 4 × 1 supercell of SiBC4, a 4 × 4 × 1 supercell of SiBC6 and a 5 × 4 × 1 supercell of SiB4C, respectively, using a microcanonical ensemble with Nosé-Hoover temperature control72 [total simulation time for 10 ps with a time step of 1 fs]. Specifically, the EPC calculations employed dense q-point grids of 48 × 48×1 for SiBC4/SiBC6 and 48 × 36×1 for SiB4C. A Gaussian smearing of 0.02 Ry was used for Brillouin zone integration. For the Coulomb repulsion constant (μ∗), we used a standard value of 0.1, consistent with peer studies on similar light-element 2D superconductors.

Application of targeted sequencing in the molecular diagnosis of thalassemia in Southern China
Authors: Xianzhen Cao, Xiaoyi Liu, Liang Hu, Shuxian Zeng, Debiao Ou, Yanan Liu, Weiqiang Liu, Fengxiang Wei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Thalassemia is a relatively common monogenic inherited blood disorder in southern China. This study aims to evaluate the additional diagnostic yield of targeted sequencing for thalassemia gene compared to traditional mainstream methods, providing evidence to support clinical decision-making and application. A total of 449 specimens with the negative common thalassemia test result were randomly selected for next-generation sequencing(NGS) testing. For rare thalassemia genotypes and abnormal hemoglobin variants detected by NGS, hematological information such as mean corpuscular volume (MCV), mean corpuscular hemoglobin (MCH), and hemoglobin electrophoresis was summarized and statistically analyzed. Through targeted sequencing for thalassemia, an additional 42 cases of rare thalassemia genotypes and 10 cases of abnormal hemoglobin variants were detected in the traditional test-negative groups (Neg_70 and Neg_80). Compared with traditional gene testing, the overall additional positive rate for NGS was 9.35% (42/449). In the traditional test-positive group (Pos_80), 2 cases of abnormal hemoglobin variants were identified, with an additional positive rate of 0.88% (2/226). The most frequently detected rare genotypes were the α-thalassemia variant –THAI and the δβ-thalassemia variant Chinese(Gγ + (Aγδβ)⁰), found in 20 and 7 cases, respectively. Additionally, a novel heterozygous variant of the HBA2 gene was identified: NM_000517.6:c.16delG. NGS demonstrates superior detection efficacy and higher detection rates in individuals with low mean corpuscular volume, particularly in those with severe microcytic hypochromic anemia. It effectively identifies rare mutations and reduces the misdiagnosis rate of thalassemia.

Introduction

Thalassemia is a group of inherited hemolytic disorders caused by variations in the HBA or HBB gene clusters, leading to impaired synthesis of globin peptide chains1. Thalassemia is the most common monogenic disorder worldwide, with approximately 7% of the global population carrying thalassemia gene variants2–4. In China, thalassemia is highly prevalent in southern regions, particularly in Guangdong (17.83%), Guangxi (24.51%), Fujian (19.99%), and Hainan (24.96%), demonstrating distinct regional and population-specific characteristics5–8. Thalassemia is a significant cause of microcytic hypochromic anemia among populations in southern China9. Individuals with mild and intermediate thalassemia are typically asymptomatic and generally do not require treatment. In contrast, transfusion-dependent thalassemia requires survival through blood transfusions and iron chelation therapy, or bone marrow stem cell transplantation and gene therapy, with a significantly impaired quality of life10. Therefore, effective premarital and preconception screening in regions with a high incidence of thalassemia is a crucial measure to prevent the birth of children with transfusion-dependent thalassemia.

Currently, hematological testing—including mean corpuscular volume (MCV), mean corpuscular hemoglobin (MCH) measurement, and hemoglobin electrophoresis analysis—is commonly used as the primary screening method for thalassemia in clinical practice11. Reverse Dot Blot (RDB), gap-PCR, fluorescence PCR melting curve analysis, and PCR-flow fluorescence hybridization are commonly used methods for identifying thalassemia variants5,12,13. Combined with complete blood count and hemoglobin analysis, these methods can screen for most common variant genotypes. They offer a favorable balance between cost and efficacy, making them the first-line approaches routinely applied in clinical practice. Currently, hematological testing is commonly used as a first-line screening method for thalassemia in clinical practice.

However, these traditional methods still fall short in adequately meeting clinical needs. Firstly, these methods have low throughput, detecting only about 20 variants at a time, leading to the oversight of other rare genotypes14; Secondly, thalassemia gene variants exhibit regional and population-specific characteristics. While traditional methods can detect most common variants, different hotspot mutations require tailored detection strategies for distinct populations. This may increase application costs in multi-ethnic regions. With the advancement of technology and the reduction in detection costs, next-generation sequencing (NGS) has been applied to targeted sequencing of thalassemia genes15. This technology offers the advantages of high throughput and broad coverage, enabling the simultaneous detection of over 500 known genotypes and the potential to identify novel pathogenic genotypes16,17. However, there remains a lack of sufficient quantitative evidence regarding the additional detection rate of NGS for thalassemia gene testing compared to traditional methods.

To evaluate the application value of targeted sequencing in the molecular diagnosis of thalassemia in southern China, we conducted a study involving 675 participants. The participants underwent complete blood counts, hemoglobin electrophoresis, and traditional thalassemia testing. Simultaneously, targeted sequencing of the thalassemia gene cluster was performed using NGS. This study aims to provide a reference for the clinical application of thalassemia gene detection by comparing the characteristics of NGS with traditional methods.

Materials and methods

Study objectives

We selected individuals who underwent thalassemia screening and genetic diagnosis at Shenzhen Longgang District Maternal and Child Health Hospital from October 2021 to August 2025 as the study subjects. All participants were enrolled according to the following screening protocol: 1.Complete blood count results indicated microcytic hypochromic anemia (MCV < 80 fl); 2.Individuals with abnormal hemoglobin electrophoresis underwent traditional thalassemia testing, while some who did not undergo hemoglobin electrophoresis proceeded directly to traditional thalassemia detection; 3. 226 specimens from the traditional thalassemia gene-positive group and 449 specimens from the negative group were randomly selected for NGS testing (Fig. 1). All participants voluntarily accepted targeted gene sequencing for thalassemia and provided written informed consent. Hematological analysis and traditional thalassemia testing

Hematological parameters, including MCV and MCH, were measured using a fully automated hematology analyzer (SYSMEX, Japan). Hemoglobin A (HbA), hemoglobin A2 (HbA2), hemoglobin F (HbF), and other abnormal hemoglobin variants were determined using a hemoglobin assay kit (Sebia, France). For samples included in both the positive and negative groups, DNA was extracted using a peripheral blood DNA extraction kit (Daan, China). Gap-PCR (Yaneng, China) was used to detect deletional α-thalassemia, RDB (Yaneng, China) was employed to identify β-thalassemia mutations, and PCR-flow fluorescence hybridization (Daan, China) was applied to detect single nucleotide variants and short deletions (< 5 bp) in both α-thalassemia and β-thalassemia genes. The testing scope encompasses 23 common thalassemia variant types prevalent in southern Chinese populations. These include:3 α-thalassemia deletions: –SEA, -α3.7, -α4.2; 3 α-thalassemia non-deletion:αCSα, αQSα, αWSα and 17 β-thalassemia mutations: IVS-II-654(C > T)(NM_000518.5:c.316-197C > T), -29(A > G)(NM_000518.5:c.-79A > G), CD71-72(+ A)(NM_000518.5:c.216_217insA), -30(T > C)(NM_000518.5:c.-80 T > C), -32(C > A)(NM_000518.5:c.-82C > A), CD14-15(+ G)(NM_000518.5:c.45_46insG), CD17(A > T)(NM_000518.5:c.52A > T), CD26(G > A)(NM_000518.5:c.79G > A), -28(A > G)(NM_000518.5:c.-78A > G), CD43(G > T)(NM_000518.5:c.130G > T), CD27-28(+ C)(NM_000518.5:c.84_85insC), CD41-42(-TTCT)(NM_000518.5:c.126_129delCTTT), IVS-I-5(G > C)( NM_000517.6:c.95 + 5G > C), 5'UTR; + 43 to + 40(-AAAC)( NM_000518.5:c.-11_-8delAAAC), IVS-I-1(G > T)(NM_000518.5:c.92 + 1G > T), Int(T-G)(NM_000518.5:c.2 T > G), and CD31(-C)(NM_000518.5:c.94delC).

Targeted sequencing

For samples from both the positive and negative groups, DNA was extracted using a peripheral blood DNA extraction kit (Daan, China). An α- and β-thalassemia gene detection kit (BGI, China) was then used to prepare DNA libraries, followed by PE100 sequencing on the MGIseq200 sequencer (BGI, China). Bioinformatics analysis of the sequencing data was performed using Halos 4.1.5 software (BGI, China), with the hg19 genome as the reference. A minimum sequencing depth of 50 × was required to pass quality control. This study tested for 508 known genotypes, including: 4 α-thalassemia deletions: –SEA, -α3.7, -α4.2, –THAI; 3 β-globin deletions: Chinese (Gγ + (Aγδβ)⁰), SEA-HPFH, Taiwanese; 153 α-thalassemia mutations; 348 β-thalassemia mutations. To avoid false positive results, rare deletional genotypes detected were validated using gap-PCR and agarose gel electrophoresis. For unreported point mutations and large deletions beyond the 508 genotypes mentioned, validation was performed using Sanger sequencing and SMRT (Single-Molecule Real-Time) sequencing, respectively. Based on traditional genotyping results and MCV values, samples subjected to NGS were categorized into three groups: 1. Pos_80 group: traditional genotype positive; 2. Neg_80 group: traditional genotype negative and MCV between [70, 80); 3. Neg_70 group: traditional genotype negative and MCV between [0, 70). Compare the positive rate, consistency rate with traditional methods, and additional positive detection rate of NGS among the different groups. For the rare genotypes detected by NGS, hematological information such as MCV, MCH, and hemoglobin electrophoresis was summarized and statistically analyzed to assess their impact on phenotype. The pathogenicity of newly discovered genotypes was evaluated with reference to the ACMG guidelines18.

Statistical analysis

This study utilized R 4.3.0 software for statistical data analysis. Mean ± standard deviation was used to describe normally distributed continuous variables, such as sequencing data volume, MCV, and MCH. Median and interquartile range were applied to describe non-normally distributed continuous variables. Chi-square tests and Fisher’s exact probability tests were used to compare the positive rates of NGS targeted sequencing across different groups. T-tests were employed to compare normally distributed data between groups. Results with a p-value less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Participant profile

This study screened 50,911 individuals from traditional thalassemia test results and selected 675 subjects with MCV < 80 as the study participants. Among them, 226 individuals tested positive for traditional thalassemia genes, while 449 tested negative. The average age of the participants was 28.45 ± 9.2 years, comprising 89 males (13.19%) and 586 females (86.81%). Based on MCV levels, among the traditional thalassemia gene-negative subjects, the Neg_70 group (MCV < 70) included 214 individuals, and the Neg_80 group (70 ≤ MCV < 80) included 235 individuals. All study subjects underwent thalassemia gene targeted sequencing (Fig. 1). Rare thalassemia genotypes and phenotypes

We analyzed the hematological phenotypes associated with the major rare thalassemia genotypes and abnormal hemoglobin variants identified in this study (Table 2). In α-thalassemia, the –THAI and –FIL deletions, due to their large deletion regions, result in moderate reductions in MCV and MCH. In β-thalassemia, based on their impact on hematological phenotypes, variants including 3’UTR + 129 (T > A), Poly A (AATAAA > AACAAA), − 86 (C > G), and − 88 (C > T) were classified as β+/β++ genotypes. In contrast, variants such as Codons 54–58 (-TATGGGCAACCCT), 1357 bp deletion, CD37 (TGG > TAG), and Codons 20/21 (-TGGA) were classified as β⁰ genotypes. The Chinese (Gγ + (Aγδβ)⁰) and SEA-HPFH deletions encompass the HBD gene, categorizing them as δβ-thalassemia. These deletions, all of which were heterozygous in this study, not only resulted in moderate reductions in MCV and MCH but also caused a significant elevation in HbF. We identified a novel variant, NM_000517.6:c.16delG, which resulted in mild to moderate reductions in MCV and MCH, without an observed increase in HbA₂.

Furthermore, we analyzed the hematological characteristics of carriers with rare genotypes (Fig. 2). In this study, when MCV ranged between 65 and 70 fL and HbA₂ exceeded 2.5%, targeted sequencing for thalassemia genes showed a higher detection rate for rare β-thalassemia variants. Discussion

Thalassemia is a common genetic disorder. It is estimated that in China, approximately 30 million people carry thalassemia-related gene mutations, of which around 300,000 suffer from intermediate to severe forms of the disease19. This poses a significant public health challenge and imposes a substantial social burden on affected regions. Therefore, accurate thalassemia screening is crucial for guiding couples in family planning and reducing the birth of children with moderate to severe thalassemia. Thalassemia and abnormal hemoglobin variants represent two fundamentally different types of hemoglobin disorders. Abnormal hemoglobin variants (e.g., HbS, HbE, HbC) are qualitative defects caused by structural abnormalities in the globin chain due to amino acid substitutions. In contrast, thalassemia results from quantitative defects—reduced or absent synthesis of globin chains20. This fundamental distinction in pathogenic mechanism is critical for accurate genetic counseling, even though both conditions can present with similar hematological features. Among thalassemias, the clinical severity varies greatly. Currently, traditional thalassemia testing based on hematological analysis is the primary screening model, but this approach suffers from a high rate of missed diagnoses. In 2017, He et al.21 found that traditional thalassemia screening following MCV, MCH, and hemoglobin electrophoresis testing resulted in missed diagnosis rates of 74.8% for α-thalassemia and 30.5% for combined α- and β-thalassemia. In this study, random sampling was used to simultaneously perform traditional thalassemia testing and NGS on selected samples. The aim was to analyze the genotypic distribution characteristics and additional detection efficacy, compare the advantages and disadvantages of the two technological methods, and provide better technical applications for clinical practice.

This study offers one novel contributions: a focused analysis of MCV-stratified populations and the identification of a novel HBA2 variant (NM_000517.6:c.16delG). Among the 675 participants who underwent both traditional testing and NGS, NGS demonstrated a markedly higher additional detection yield for rare thalassemia variants in individuals with low MCV, particularly those with severe microcytic hypochromic anemia, confirming its utility in identifying rare cases. In contrast, the additional detection rate was relatively low among participants who had already tested positive for traditional thalassemia.

In this study, among α-rare thalassemia, the –THAI genotype was the most frequently observed. For β-rare thalassemia, the most common genotypes were Chinese(Gγ + (Aγδβ)0), Codons 54–58 (-TATGGGCAACCCT), and 3’UTR + 129 (T > A). Additionally, one case of 1357 bp deletion was detected. The 1357 bp deletion, as a relatively rare type of thalassemia, was reported by Youqiong Li et al.22 in the Guangxi population. Its hematological phenotype resembles that of Chinese(Gγ + (Aγδβ)0) thalassemia and SEA-HPFH, characterized by elevated levels of HBA2 and HBF. Additionally, we detected one case of a heterozygous Poly A (AATAAA > AACAAA) mutation, in which the MCV level was higher than that observed with other common mutations. This finding is consistent with the results reported by Khushnooma Italia et al.23. The detection rate of rare thalassemia in this study was higher than that reported in Southwest China24 (0.91%, 10/1093), Guizhou region25 (0.86%, 156/18,039), and Changsha, Hunan Province26 (0.23%, 88/38810). These studies found that NGS could achieve an additional detection rate of 0.5–1%. However, the inclusion criteria for the study populations varied significantly, leading to considerable differences in the additional detection rates. In this study, using low MCV as the population selection criterion demonstrated good clinical application value and reproducibility. Furthermore, we analyzed the hematological characteristics of carriers with rare genotypes. This study found that the average HBA2 level in the negative group was around 2.0%, while in the positive group, the average HBA2 level ranged between 2.5% and 3.5%. It should be noted that the conventional positive group (Pos_80) in this study comprises a heterogeneous mix of different thalassemia types. As shown in Table 2, these conditions exhibit distinct hematological phenotypes-for example, HbA₂ is decreased in –THAI carriers (2.4%) but elevated in β⁰-thalassemia carriers such as Codons 54–58 (5.4%). Therefore, the pooled mean values in Table 1 should be interpreted with caution and are not intended to represent the phenotype of any single thalassemia type. Readers are referred to Table 2 and Fig. 2G,H for type-specific analyses. The MCV levels detected for α-thalassemia were primarily distributed between 65 and 70, whereas the HBA2 levels detected for β-thalassemia were mostly greater than 3.5%. When MCV was between 65 and 70 fL and HBA2 was above 2.5%, targeted sequencing of thalassemia genes showed a high positive rate for rare β-thalassemia variants. This trend becomes even more evident through principal component analysis of hematological phenotypes.

In a study targeting populations in southern China, the NM_000518 − 50 (G > A) and 5’UTR + 43 to + 40 (-AAAC) variants, along with the Hb Phnom Penh type in HBA, were reported as the most frequent rare types27. The NM_000518 -50 (G > A) variant was also relatively common in the Guizhou population studied by Mei Tan et al.25. However, none of these genotypes or abnormal hemoglobins were detected in the present study. Yali Pan et al.28 found that –THAI accounted for 0.41% of α-thalassemia positive individuals in Fujian, making it the most frequently detected rare α-thalassemia variant in their study, which is consistent with the findings of our study. In our study, 12 cases of abnormal hemoglobin were identified, with the most common being heterozygous Hb Hekinan II (NM_000558.5:c.84G > T). In contrast, a study on hemoglobinopathies in northern Guangdong province29 reported that Hb Q-Thailand was the most common abnormal hemoglobin band observed, which differs from our results. Although our sequencing panel covered the aforementioned loci, the failure to detect them may be attributed to population differences and selection bias.

The novel HBA2 frameshift variant (NM_000517.6:c.16delG) identified in this study is located in exon 1 of the HBA2 gene and was classified as a Likely Pathogenic variant (PVS1 + PM2_supporting) according to the ACMG guidelines18. Although the individuals carrying this heterozygous variant in this study presented with microcytic hypochromic anemia phenotypes, further data from larger samples are required to evaluate the genotype–phenotype correlations and reduce the impact of random sampling errors.

Due to the relatively low sensitivity of hematological analysis, traditional screening methods may miss a significant number of novel or rare thalassemia variants30. With advancements in sequencing technology, NGS offers the advantages of high-throughput and low-cost, significantly enhancing the detection of rare thalassemia variants27. Compared to traditional thalassemia screening, NGS can better enhance the additional detection rate of rare thalassemia. This study found that NGS demonstrates superior performance in identifying novel or other rare variants in individuals with severe microcytic hypochromic anemia. For individuals with mild microcytic hypochromic anemia, the additional detection rate for rare thalassemia is relatively lower, but NGS can still serve as a valuable supplement when traditional test kits yield negative results.

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, as a single-center study focusing on a single ethnic group, the findings may have limited generalizability. Validation in larger, more diverse populations is needed to confirm the results. Second, iron-deficiency anemia (IDA) was not excluded prior to participant enrollment, as iron metabolism markers-such as ferritin, serum iron, and total iron-binding capacity-were not assessed. This means that the so-called “negative group” in this study actually consists of individuals who tested negative on traditional thalassemia screening but were not confirmed to be free from IDA. Therefore, this group should not be regarded as a strictly defined "non-thalassemia population." As a result, the additional detection rate of NGS in individuals with microcytic hypochromic anemia may be underestimated when IDA is taken into account. Future studies would benefit from including iron metabolism testing to more accurately determine the true detection yield of NGS in populations negative for thalassemia. Third, our experiments were based on pilot experiments, and the sample size in this study was not determined by a formal statistical power calculation, which may limit the generalizability of our findings. However, the cohort size (n = 675) is comparable to previous studies evaluating NGS for rare thalassemia variant detection in Chinese populations. Larger, multi-center studies with predefined power calculations are needed to confirm the additional diagnostic yield observed in this study.

Conclusion

In the Southern Chinese population, a total of 42 rare thalassemia gene variants and 12 abnormal hemoglobin variants were identified. This included one novel thalassemia variant not previously reported: NM_000517.6:c.16delG. These discoveries contribute to enriching the thalassemia gene spectrum in this population. Compared to traditional methods, NGS detected an additional 9.35% of thalassemia gene variants in individuals with severe microcytic hypochromic anemia. This approach can significantly improve diagnostic accuracy and reduce the likelihood of missed or misdiagnosis, demonstrating promising application prospects for genetic testing in this high-risk population.

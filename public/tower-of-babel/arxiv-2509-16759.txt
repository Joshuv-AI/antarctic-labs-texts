1.
Introduction

The topological complexity
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(X)
of a configuration space
X
is a numerical invariant that estimates the difficulty of the problem of constructing a navigation algorithm in
X
. A navigation algorithm on
X
is a map
f:X\times X\to P(X)
from the product
X\times X
to the space
P(X)=Map(\phi:[0,1]\to X)
of all paths in
X
such that for each pair
(x_{0},x_{1})\in X\times X

the path
f(x_{0},x_{1}):[0,1]\to X
evaluated at 0 is
x_{0}
, the initial state of robot, and the path
f(x_{0},x_{1})
evaluated at 1 is
x_{1}
, the terminal point. Clearly, if
X
admits a continuous navigation algorithm, then
X
as a topological space is contractible. It is a very restrictive condition. The topological complexity
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(X)
was defined by Farber
[
F1
]
,
[
F2
]
as the minimal number of pieces (minus one nowadays) needed to cover
X\times X
in a way that on each piece there is a continuous navigation algorithm.
The numerical invariant
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}
is similar in spirit to a 100 years old invariant called the Lusternik-Schnirelmann category,
\operatorname{cat}X

[
LS
]
,
[
CLOT
]
.
It was introduced by Lusternik and Schnirelmann in their solution of Poincare’s problem
[
LS
]
about the number of minimal geodesics on a 2-sphere. Since then the LS-category found many applications in different areas of mathematics.

Both
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}
and
\operatorname{cat}
are homotopy invariant: If a topological space
X
is homotopy equivalent to
Y
then

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(X)={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(Y)
and
\operatorname{cat}X=\operatorname{cat}Y
. It means that both invariants can be defined for discrete groups
G
as
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(G)={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(BG)
and
\operatorname{cat}(G)=\operatorname{cat}(BG)
where
BG
is a classifying CW complex for
G
or equivalently
BG
is a
K(G,1)
-space. Since for fixed
G
all
K(G,1)
-complexes are homotopy equivalent, the group invariants

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(G)
and
\operatorname{cat}(G)
are well-defined. In the 50s
[
EG
]
Eilenberg and Ganea proved that
\operatorname{cat}(G)
equals the cohomological dimension
\operatorname{cd}(G)
of
G
.
Thus,
\operatorname{cat}(G)
does not bring anything new to group theory. On the other hand
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(G)
is quite intriguing invariant which is computed yet for few classes of groups.

Recently Knudsen and Weinberger
[
KW1
]
and independently Jauhari and myself
[
DJ
]
modified
\operatorname{cat}
and
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}
to their probabilistic version to obtain new invariants,
the distributional topological complexity,
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dTC}\,}
and distributional LS-category
d\operatorname{cat}
. Knudsen and Weinberger used the terms analog category and analog complexity where
the word analog brought to emphasize the opposite of digital. These new concepts already were studied besides of
[
DJ
]
and
[
KW1
]
in the papers
[
Dr2
]
,
[
KW2
]
,
[
J1
]
,
[
J2
]
,
[
JO1
]
,
[
JO2
]
,
[
DaJ
]
.

Informally the difference between old
\operatorname{cat}
and new
d\operatorname{cat}
can be explained as follows.
By the definition
\operatorname{cat}X\leq n
if there is an open cover
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{0},\dots,U_{n}\}
of
X
together with contractions of each
U_{i}
to a base point
x_{0}
. A contraction of
U_{i}
to
x_{0}
can be seen as a family of path
\phi_{x}^{i}
from
x\in U_{i}
to
x_{0}
that depends continuously on
x
. Using a partition of unity subordinated by
\mathcal{U}
one can give weights
\lambda_{x}^{i}\in[0,1]
to each path
\phi_{x}^{i}
,
0\leq i\leq n
,
x\in X
, continuously depending on
x
with
\sum_{i}\lambda_{x}^{i}=1
.
This gives an ordered multi-path

\Phi_{x}=(\lambda_{x}^{0}\phi_{x}^{0},\lambda_{x}^{1}\phi_{x}^{1},\dots,\lambda_{x}^{n}\phi_{x}^{n})

of cardinality
n+1
from
x\in X
to
x_{0}
that continuously depends on
x
. Thus, all paths
\Phi_{x}
,
x\in X
define a continuous multi-path contraction of
X
to
x_{0}
. It is not difficult to see that the inequality
\operatorname{cat}X\leq n
is equivalent to existence of continuous contraction of
X
to
x_{0}
by means of ordered multi-paths of cardinality
n+1

[
F3
]
. In our definition of the distributional category
d\operatorname{cat}
we just drop the requirement of the order. Clearly, it gives the inequality
d\operatorname{cat}\leq\operatorname{cat}
. Thus, by loosing the order turns a multi-path into a probability measure on the space of paths.

The same idea is used for definition of distributional topological complexity. A navigation algorithm in this case is a continuous choice of distributed paths between any
x
and
y
in the configuration space. In robotic language it means that
for each
x
and
y
we have
\leq n+1
paths with continuously distributed probabilities for a robot to choose a certain path such that the family of all ”probabilistic” multi-paths continuously depends on
x
and
y
.

It turns out that the distributional analogs can be quite different from the originals for groups and for spaces as well.
In particular it was proven in both papers
[
DJ
]
and
[
KW1
]
that
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dTC}\,}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})=d\operatorname{cat}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})=1
whereas
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})=\operatorname{cat}(\mathbb{Z}_{2})=\operatorname{cd}(G)=\infty
. Moreover, we proved that

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dTC}\,}(\mathbb{R}P^{n})=d\operatorname{cat}(\mathbb{R}P^{n})=1

for all
n
. Note that in the classical case
\operatorname{cat}(\mathbb{R}P^{n})=n

and
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(\mathbb{R}P^{n})
equals, with some exception, the immersion number for
\mathbb{R}P^{n}

[
FTY
]
.

In
[
KW1
]
Knudsen and Weinberger proved that

d\operatorname{cat}(G)\leq{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dTC}\,}(G)\leq|G|-1

for any finite group
G
. Later it was shown
[
Dr2
]
that
d\operatorname{cat}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dTC}\,}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})=p-1
and, moreover
[
KW2
]
,

d\operatorname{cat}(G)={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dTC}\,}(G)=|G|-1

for any
p
-group. It was an open problem whether such equality holds for lens spaces for
p>2
. We have addressed it in the second part of this paper.

Knudsen and Weinberger proved
[
KW1
]
that for torsion free groups
\Gamma
the distributional category coincides with the Lusternik-Schnirelmann category,
d\operatorname{cat}\Gamma=\operatorname{cat}\Gamma
.
It is an open problem whether the similar equality
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dTC}\,}(\Gamma)={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(\Gamma)
holds for topological complexity. In the first part of the paper we prove that this is the case for Frobenius injective groups. In particular, it holds true for torsion free hyperbolic groups and torsion free nilpotent groups.

In the second part of the paper we present upper and lower bounds for the distributional category and topological complexity of lens spaces. In particular, we prove
the inequalities

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dTC}\,}(L^{n}_{p})\leq 2p-1\ \ \text{and}\ \ d\operatorname{cat}(L^{n}_{p})\leq p-1

where the latter turns into the equality for prime
p
and
n>p
. We use those computations to bring counter-examples to the product formula for
d\operatorname{cat}
and
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dTC}\,}
of manifolds.
The product formulas (inequalities)

\operatorname{cat}(X\times Y)\leq\operatorname{cat}X+\operatorname{cat}Y\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ {\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(X\times Y)\leq{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(X)+{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(Y)

are well-known for the classical category
[
CLOT
]
and topological complexity
[
F2
]
.

2.1.
Recall on
\operatorname{cat}
and
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}
for spaces and groups

The definition of the LS-category
\operatorname{cat}(X)
of a topological space was given in the introduction.
By Eilenberg-Ganea theorem for groups
\operatorname{cat}(\Gamma)=\operatorname{cd}{\Gamma}
.

The topological complexity
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(X)
of a space
X
is the minimal number
n
such that
X\times X
can be covered by n+1 open sets
U_{0},\dots,U_{n}
such that for each
i
there is a continuous function
s_{i}:U_{i}\to P(X)
to the path space on
X
satisfying the condition
s_{i}(x,y)(0)=x
and
s_{i}(x,y)(1)=y
for
(x,y)\in U_{i}
.
For a group
\Gamma
the invariant
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(\Gamma)={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(B\Gamma)
is difficult to compute. For geometrically finite groups, i.e., groups with finite
B\Gamma
, there is the following characterization
[
FGLO
]

2.1.1 Theorem
.

For a geometrically finite group
\Gamma
the topological complexity
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(\Gamma)
equals the minimal integer
k
such that the canonical map

\mu:E(\Gamma\times\Gamma)\to E_{\mathcal{D}}(\Gamma\times\Gamma)

is
(\Gamma\times\Gamma)
-equivariantly homotopic to a map with the image in
the
k
-dimensional skeleton
E_{\mathcal{D}}(\Gamma\times\Gamma)^{(k)}
.

Here
\mathcal{D}
is the family of subgroups of
\Gamma\times\Gamma
obtained from the diagonal
\Delta\Gamma
subgroup by conjugation and taking finite intersections. Additionally we require
\{1\}\in\mathcal{D}
. The space
E_{\mathcal{D}}(\Gamma\times\Gamma)
is a classifying space for
(\Gamma\times\Gamma)
-actions which have all isotropy groups from
\mathcal{D}
. Since it is universal, there the canonical map
\mu
.

2.2.
Probability measures with finite supports

By
\mathcal{B}_{n}(X)
we denote the space of probability measures on
X
supported on at most
n
points.
More common notation for this space is
P_{n}(X)
, but since we apply it to path spaces
X=P(Y)
, to avoid confusion we use an alternative notation as in
[
KK
]
.
The standard topology on
\mathcal{B}_{n}(X)
is the quotient topology
[
KK
]
,
[
V
]
which comes from the symmetric join product
by means of a quotient map
q:Symm^{\ast n}(X)\to\mathcal{B}_{n}(Z)
. By the definition the symmetric join product

Symm^{\ast n}(Z):=\ast^{n}Z/S_{n}
is the orbit space of the action of symmetric group
S_{n}
on the iterated join product
\ast^{n}Z=Z\ast\cdots\ast Z
.
Elements of
Symm^{\ast n}(Z)
can be viewed as formal sums
t_{1}x_{1}+\cdots t_{n}x_{n}
with no order
on the summands and the convention
0x=0y
for each summand. Then the quotient map
q:Symm^{\ast n}(X)\to\mathcal{B}_{n}(Z)
is defined by the condition
tx+t^{\prime}x=(t+t^{\prime})x
. We note that
q
is a map with compact contractible fibers. If
X
is a metric space, there are choices of metrics on
\mathcal{B}_{n}(X)
like Kantorovich-Rubinshtein metric, Wasserstein metric, or
Levy-Prokhorov metric which generally produce coarser topologies.

Clearly
\mathcal{B}_{n}(-)
is a covariant functor on the category of topological spaces.
Thus, for a continuous map
f:X\to Y
we have the push-forward map

\mathcal{B}_{n}(f):\mathcal{B}_{n}(X)\to\mathcal{B}_{n}(Y)

which is continuous.
There is a natural map
i_{X}:X\to\mathcal{B}_{n}(X)
defined by the Dirac measures
x\mapsto\delta_{x}
which is an embedding for reasonable topological spaces
X
.

2.2.1 Definition
.

We say that a map
f:X\to Y
admits a
\mathcal{B}_{n}
-section if there is a map
\mu:Y\to\mathcal{B}_{n}(X)
such that
\mathcal{B}_{n}(f)\circ\mu=i_{Y}
.

We denote by

M_{n}(X,f)=\mathcal{B}_{n}(f)^{-1}(Y)\ \ \ \text{and by}\ \ \ M_{n}(f):M_{n}(X,f)\to Y

the restriction of
\mathcal{B}_{n}(f)
to
M_{n}(X,f)
.
If
F
is a fibration with a fiber
F
, then
M_{n}(f)
is a fibration with fiber
\mathcal{B}_{n}(F)
.
Thus, a fibration
f
admits a
\mathcal{B}_{n}
-section if and only
M_{n}(f)
admits a section.

2.5.
d\operatorname{cat}
and
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dTC}\,}
of maps

We define
d\operatorname{cat}
and
d{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}
for maps as follows.
The distributive topological complexity
,
d{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(f)
, of a map
f:X\to Y
is the minimal number
n
such that there is a continuous map

s:X\times X\to\mathcal{B}_{n+1}(P(Y))

satisfying
s(x,x^{\prime})\in\mathcal{B}_{n+1}(P(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))
for all
(x,x^{\prime})\in X\times X
.
Here

P(y,y^{\prime})=\{f\in P(Y)\mid f(0)=y,\ f(1)=y^{\prime}\},

The distributional LS-category
,
d\operatorname{cat}(f)
, is the minimal number
n
such that there is a continuous map

s:X\to\mathcal{B}_{n+1}(P(Y))

satisfying
s(x)\in\mathcal{B}_{n+1}(P(f(x),y_{0}))
for all
x\in X
. We note that

d\operatorname{cat}(1_{X})=d\operatorname{cat}(X)\ \ \text{and}\ \ d{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(1_{X})=d{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(X).

Clearly, for
f:X\to Y
,

d\operatorname{cat}(f)\leq\min\{d\operatorname{cat}X,d\operatorname{cat}Y\}\ \text{and}\ d{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(f)\leq\min\{d{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(X),d{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{TC}\,}(Y)\}.

The proof of Theorem 2.6 and Theorem 2.7 in
[

]
can be extended to the following.

.

Let

be a map. Then

if and only if

;

(b)

admits a lift with respect to

.

Since the existence of a lift of

in Proposition

-section of the pull-back of

with respect to

,

3.
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dTC}\,}
of Frobenius injective groups

3.1.
Frobenius Injective Groups

For a group
G
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
a power map
f:G\to G
,
f(x)=x^{k}
, is called a
Frobenius map
.
We call a group
G

Frobenius injective
if every Frobenius map
f:G\to G
on
G
is injective.
Thus, Frobenius injective groups are torsion free.

Clearly, a subgroup of Frobenius injective group is Frobenius injective. In particular,
any subgroup of a uniquely divisible group is Frobenius injective.
Examples of uniquely divisible groups include the groups of positive units
of a real closed fields, unipotent matrix groups, noncommutative power series with unit constant
term, and the group of characters of a connected Hopf algebra over a field of characteristic
zero
[
Ba
]
,
[
ABS
]
,
[
HLR
]
.

By
Z(a)=\{x\in G\mid xa=ax\}
we denote the centralizer of an element
a\in G
in a group
G
.
The following was proven in
[
FGLO
]
, Lemma 8.0.4:

3.1.1 Proposition
.

For torsion free groups
G
with cyclic centralizers either
Z(a)=Z(b)
or
Z(a)\cap Z(b)=1
for all
a
and
b
.

It was shown in
[
FGLO
]
that such groups
G
satisfy the following property :

(N) For any
x\in G
and for any finite set
S\subset G
the condition
x^{n}\in Z(S)
implies that
x\in Z(S)
where

Z(S)=\cap_{a\in S}Z(a)
.

3.1.2 Proposition
.

Every torsion free group satisfying property (N) is Frobenius injective.

Proof.

Suppose that
x^{k}=y^{k}
. Then
x^{k}\in Z(y)
. By Property (N)
x\in Z(y)
. Then
(xy^{-1})^{k}=x^{k}y^{-k}=1
. Since the group is torsion free, we obtain
xy^{-1}=1
.
Thus,
x=y
.
∎

3.1.3 Corollary
.

Torsion free nilpotent groups are Frobenius injective.

It was proven in
[
FGLO
]
, Lemma 8.0.3, that such groups have property (N).

Since torsion free hyperbolic groups have cyclic centralizers, we obtain the following:

3.1.4 Corollary
.

Torsion free hyperbolic groups are Frobenius injective.

Let
\pi
be a torsion free group and let
G=\pi\times\pi
.
The action of
G
on
\pi
defined by the formula
(x,y)(\gamma)=x\gamma y^{-1}
extends to the action on the simplex
\Delta(\pi)
spanned by
\pi
.
Then the map

f:\Delta(G)\to\Delta(\pi)
defined as

f(\sum t_{i}(x_{i},y_{i}))=\sum t_{i}(x_{i}y_{i}^{-1})

is
G
-equivariant:

f((a,b)\sum t_{i}(x_{i},y_{i}))=f(\sum t_{i}(ax_{i},by_{i}))=\sum t_{i}(ax_{i}y_{i}^{-1}b^{-1})=(a,b)f(\sum t_{i}(x_{i},y_{i})).

We denote by
\mathcal{D}
a family of subgroups of
G
obtained from the diagonal subgroup
\delta\pi\subset\pi\times\pi
and the trivial subgroup by conjugation and taking finite intersections. We recall that
Z(\gamma)=\{g\in\pi\mid g\gamma=\gamma g\}
denotes the centralizer of
\gamma\in\pi
and for

we denote by

Z(S)=\bigcap_{\gamma\in S}Z(\gamma)

the intersection of centralizers. It was noticed in

that

\mathcal{D}=\{H_{b,S}\mid b\in\pi,\ S\subset\pi,\ |S|<\infty\}

where
H_{b,S}=\{(a,bab^{-1})\in\pi\times\pi\mid a\in Z(S)\}
. We note that here the conjugates of the diagonal are represented by

.

3.2.
Classifying space
E_{\mathcal{D}}(G)

For a discrete group
G
and a family of its subgroups
\mathcal{F}
which is closed under conjugation and finite intersection a classifying complex
E_{\mathcal{F}}G

is a
G
-CW-complex whose isotropy subgroups are from
\mathcal{F}
and for each
G
-CW complex with isotropy groups from
\mathcal{F}
there is a unique up to a

G
-homotopy a
G
-equivariant map
f:X\to E_{\mathcal{F}}G
.
The corresponding map

f:EG\to E_{\mathcal{F}}G

is
called a
canonical map
.

Wolfgang Lücke proved the following
[
Lu
]

3.2.1 Theorem
.

A
G
-CW-complex
X
is
E_{\mathcal{F}}G
if and only if for each
x\in X
the isotropy group of
x
belongs to the family
\mathcal{F}
and for each group
F\in\mathcal{F}
the fixed point set
X^{F}
is contractible.

We use Lück’s theorem to prove the following

3.2.2 Theorem
.

For every Frobenius injective group
\pi
the simplex
\Delta(\pi)
spanned by
\pi
is a classifying G-CW-complex
E_{\mathcal{D}}(G)
.

Proof.

The isotropy group of a vertex
\gamma\in\Delta(\pi)
equals

H_{\gamma}=\{(x,y)\in\pi\times\pi\mid x\gamma y^{-1}=1\}=\{(e,\gamma)(x,x)(e,\gamma)^{-1}\}=(e,\gamma)\delta\pi(e,\gamma)^{-1}

which belongs to the family
\mathcal{D}
as a conjugate to the diagonal subgroup
\delta\pi
.

Claim:
Suppose that some
(x,y)\in G
fixes a point

z=\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}\gamma_{i}\in\Delta(\pi)\ \ \text{where}\ \ t_{i}>0.

Then
(x,y)
fixes each
\gamma_{i}
.

Proof of the claim.
Suppose the contrary,
Then
(x,y)
defines a permutation of
\gamma_{1},\dots\gamma_{k}
.
We choose a nontrivial cycle of the permutation.
Without loss of generality we may assume that

(x,y)(\gamma_{1})=\gamma_{2},\ \dots,\ (x,y)(\gamma_{\ell-1})=\gamma_{\ell},\ (x,y)(\gamma_{\ell})=\gamma_{1}.

Thus,

x\gamma_{1}y^{-1}=\gamma_{2},\ \dots,\ x\gamma_{\ell-1}y^{-1}=\gamma_{\ell},\ x\gamma_{\ell}y^{-1}=\gamma_{1}.

We substitute
\gamma_{2}
from the first equation to the second to obtain

x^{2}\gamma_{1}y^{-2}=\gamma_{3}
. Then substitute
\gamma_{3}
in the third equation
to obtain
x^{3}\gamma_{1}y^{-3}=\gamma_{4}
, and so on. Finally we obtain the equation

x^{\ell}\gamma_{1}y^{-\ell}=\gamma_{1}
which we rewrite as
y^{\ell}=\gamma_{1}^{-1}x^{\ell}\gamma_{1}
.
Then
(\gamma_{1}^{-1}x\gamma_{1})^{\ell}=y^{\ell}
. By the Frobenius injectivity we obtain
\gamma_{1}^{-1}x\gamma_{1}=y
.
Then

(x,y)(\gamma_{1})=x\gamma_{1}y^{-1}=\gamma_{1}.

If we do the above substitutions starting from the
i
th equation we obtain
y^{\ell}=\gamma_{i}^{-1}x^{\ell}\gamma_{i}
.
Similarly, we obtain for all
i\leq\ell
,

(x,y)(\gamma_{i})=x\gamma_{i}y^{-1}=\gamma_{i}.

Similar arguments for all cycles in the permutation shows that
(x,y)
fixes all vertices
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{k}
.
Thus the isotropy group
H_{z}
of
z
is the intersection of the isotropy subgroups of vertices
\gamma_{i}\in\Delta(\pi)
,

H_{z}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}H_{\gamma_{i}}.

Hence,
H_{z}\in\mathcal{D}
for all
z
.

Now given
H\in\mathcal{D}
, we show that the fixed point set
\Delta(\pi)^{H}
is contractible. As it was mentioned above

H=H_{b,S}=(1,b)\delta Z(S)(1,b)^{-1}

for some
b\in\pi
and finite
S\subset\pi
where
\delta Z(S)\subset\delta\pi\pi\times\pi
is the diagonal subgroup.
Since the fixed point set of a conjugate
H
is homeomorphic to the fixed point set of the group
\delta Z(S)
, it suffices to show that it is contractible for the latter.
We note that the group
\delta Z(S)
fixes
\gamma\in\pi
if and only if
\gamma
commutes with all elements of
Z(S)
:

(x,x)(\gamma)=\gamma\ \Leftrightarrow\ \ x\gamma x^{-1}=\gamma\ \ \text{for}\ \ x\in Z(S).

In view of the Claim the fixed point set of
\delta Z(S)
is the simplex
\Delta(Z(Z(S)))
which is contractible.

We apply Lück’s characterization theorem of rel a family classifying spaces
3.2.1
to complete the proof.
∎

5.
Lower bounds for
d\operatorname{cat}
and
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dTC}\,}
of lens spaces

In
[
Dr2
]
it was shown that
p-1
is the lower bound for
d\operatorname{cat}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
for prime
p
. This implies that

d\operatorname{cat}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dTC}\,}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})=p-1

for any prime
p
. Then Knudsen and Weinberger
[
KW2
]
proved that

d\operatorname{cat}(G)={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dTC}\,}(|G|)=|G|-1.

for any
p
-group
G
. We recall that for prime
p
a finite group
G
is called a
p
-group if
|G|=p^{k}
. Knudsen and Weinberger proof is based on the equivariant Sullivan conjecture which is proven by many authors
[
C
]
,
[
DMN
]
,
[
La
]
. Here we present their argument referring to Dror Farjoun-Zabrodsky’s theorem.
Suppose that a group
G
acts on a space
X
. We recall that
X^{G}
denotes the fixed point set of the action and

X^{hG}=Map_{G}(EG,X)

denote the homotopy fixed point set.
Since
X^{hG}
is weak homotopy equivalent to the space of sections

Sec\{X\times_{G}EG\to BG\},

Theorem
2.3.3
implies

5.0.1 Corollary
.

d\operatorname{cat}(G)=\min\{n\mid\mathcal{B}_{n+1}(G)^{hG}\neq\emptyset\}
.

A group
G
has the homotopy fixed point (HFP) property if
X^{G}=\emptyset
if and only if
X^{hG}=\emptyset
for any finite complex
X
.
Dror Farjoun and Zabrodsky proved
[
DZ
]
that a finite group
G
has HFP property if and only if
G
is a
p
-groups for some prime
p
.
Since

\mathcal{B}_{n+1}(G)=\Delta(G)^{n}=\partial\Delta(G)\ \ \text{for}\ \ n=|G|-1

where
G
acts by permutation of vertices, one can show that
\mathcal{B}_{n+1}(G)^{G}=\emptyset
.
Then the Dror Farjoun-Zabrodsky theorem together with Corollary
5.0.1
imply that
d\operatorname{cat}G\geq n=|G|-1
.

Unfortunately this powerful approach cannot be applied to finite lens spaces. To work with lens spaces we make a step back to an older technique known as Borsuk-Ulam type theorems.

5.1.
Borsuk-Ulam type theorems

Suppose that a finite group
G
acts freely on
X
and let
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
be a function. By
Gx
we denote the orbit of
x\in X
.
By definition the coincidence set of

is

A_{f}=\{x\in X\mid\ \ |f(Gx)|=1\}.

Let

be the projection onto the orbit space and let
u:X/G\to BG
be its classifying map.

5.1.1 Proposition
.

Let
G
be a finite group acting freely on a finite complex
X
and
|G|=n+1
. The following are equivalent:

1.
d\operatorname{cat}(u)\geq n
.

2.
A_{f}\neq\emptyset
for any continuous function

Proof.

1.
\Rightarrow
2. Suppose that
A_{f}=\emptyset
for some

. In view of Corollary
2.5.2
to get a contradiction with
d\operatorname{cat}(u)\geq n
we
construct a
\mathcal{B}_{n}
-section of
p
.
For

S^{+}(y)=\{x\in p^{-1}(y)\mid f(x)=\max f|_{p^{-1}(y)}\}\subsetneq p^{-1}(y).

Let

We consider an open cover

of

by even connected sets. Thus, for every

which agrees with

.
For each

we can find a
a neighborhood

such that

q(S^{+}(y^{\prime}))\subset S^{+}(y)\ \ \ \text{for all}\ \ y^{\prime}\in U(y)

is the projection.
This implies that the multi-valued function

is lower semi-continuous.
It is known that compact-valued lower semi-continuous functions admit sections in probability measures

\mu:X/G\to\mathcal{B}(X),\ \ \ \mathcal{B}(p)\mu(x)=\delta_{x}.

Since

\mathcal{B}_{n}
-section of

Though existence of

define it directly

and

.

2.
\Rightarrow
1. Suppose that

. Then by Corollary
2.5.2

-section

Since

for every

5.2.

Counter-examples to the product formula for groups can be easily derived for both

Since

, by Proposition

for

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dTC}\,}(L_{p}^{m}\times L_{p}^{m})\geq p^{2}-1.

By Theorem

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{dTC}\,}(L^{m}_{p})\leq 2p-1

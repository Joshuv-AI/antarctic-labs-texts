Coincidence of the Dimensions of First Countable Spaces with a Countable Network
I. M. Leibo

Abstract
. The coincidence of the Ind \operatorname{Ind} and dim \dim dimensions for first countable paracompact σ \sigma -spaces is proved. This gives a positive answer to A. V. Arkhangel’skii’s question of whether the dimensions ind ⁡ X \operatorname{ind}X , Ind ⁡ X \operatorname{Ind}X , and dim X \dim X are equal for first countable spaces with a countable network.



All topological spaces considered in this paper are assumed to be normal
T_{1}
-spaces and called simply spaces.

In this paper, we use the terminology of the papers
[
1
]
and
[
3
]
.

There exist three classical topological dimension functions,
\operatorname{Ind}X
,
\dim X
, and
\operatorname{ind}X
(see, e.g.,

[
1
]
); relationships between them for various classes of spaces is of special interest in dimension theory.
L. A. Tumarkin and W. Hurewicz proved the equalities
\operatorname{ind}X=\dim X=\operatorname{Ind}X
for a space
X
with a countable base

[
1
, Ch. 4, Sec. 8, Subsec. 2]
. Since every base is a network, it is quite natural to ask whether the
dimensions of a space with a countable network coincide; this question was posed by
Arkhangel’skii in
[
2
, Sec. 5]
. In the general case, the answer is negative. A space
X
with a countable
network for which
\dim X=1
but
\operatorname{Ind}X=\operatorname{ind}X=2
was constructed by Charalambous
[
4
]
(note that the
dimensions
\operatorname{Ind}
and
\operatorname{ind}
are equal for any such space). When stating his coincidence problem, Arkhangel’skii
did not exclude the possibility that a counterexample may exist. Therefore, he simultaneously stated the
dimension coincidence problem for spaces with a countable network which satisfy certain additional conditions,
the most interesting of which are the presence of a topological group structure and the first axiom of
countability (i.e., the existence of a countable neighborhood base at every point). The question of whether
\operatorname{ind}G=\dim G=\operatorname{Ind}G
for any topological group
G
with a countable network still remains open (for more than 50
years now).

In this paper, we give a positive answer to Arkhangel’skii’s question of whether
\operatorname{Ind}X=\dim X=\operatorname{ind}X
for any
first countable space with a countable network.

We also obtain a more general positive result, namely, that
\dim X=\operatorname{Ind}X
for any first countable paracompact

\sigma
-space
X
(recall that
X
is a
\sigma
-space if
X
has a
\sigma
-discrete network, i.e., a network
being a countable union of discrete families of subsets of
X
; in particular, any space with a countable network
is a
\sigma
-space).

The main results of this paper are Theorems 1 and 2 and Corollary 1. The complete proofs of all statements are
given in
[
8
]
.

The coincidence of the dimensions
\dim X
,
\operatorname{Ind}X
, and
\operatorname{ind}X
for a first countable space
X
with a
countable network is implied by the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
X
be a first countable paracompact
\sigma
-space. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

\dim X\leq n
;

(b)

\operatorname{Ind}X\leq n
;

(c)

X=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n+1}{X_{i}}
, where each
X_{i}
is a
G_{\delta}
-set and
\dim{X_{i}}\leq 0
for
i=1,2,\dots,n+1
;

(d)

X
is the image of a first countable paracompact
\sigma
-space
X_{0}
with
\dim X_{0}=0
under a closed
continuous map
f\colon X_{0}\to X
such that
|f^{-1}(x)|\leq n+1
for all
x\in X
.

We consider the equivalence of these particular conditions (a)–(d) because they are equivalent for any
metrizable space.

It follows from the equivalence of conditions (a) and (b) in Theorem 1 that
\dim X=\operatorname{Ind}X

for any first countable paracompact
\sigma
-space
X
. As is known
[
1
, Sec. 8, Lemma 2]
, for any space
X

with a countable network, we have
\dim X\leq\operatorname{ind}X\leq\operatorname{Ind}X
. This gives the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

Let
X
be a first countable space with a countable network. Then
\operatorname{ind}X=\dim X=\operatorname{Ind}X
.

Theorem 1 follows from Theorem 2 given below. Before stating it, we give two definitions.

Definition 1

(
[
6
]
)
.

Given a normal space
Z
of finite dimension
\operatorname{Ind}Z=n
, we say that a closed subset
F
of
Z
and its open
neighborhood
OF

determine the dimension

\operatorname{Ind}Z
of
Z
if, for any open neighborhood
U
of
F
such that

F\subseteq U\subseteq\overline{U}\subseteq OF
, we have
\operatorname{Ind}\operatorname{Fr}U\geq n-1
.

Definition 2

(
[
6
]
)
.

A family
\varphi=\{F_{\alpha},OF_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in A}
of closed subsets
F_{\alpha}
of a normal space
Z

and their open neighborhoods
OF_{\alpha}
is called an
everywhere f-system
(an
f-system
)
if the family
\{OF_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in A}
is
\sigma
-discrete and, given any (any closed) set
M\subseteq Z
, there exists an index
\alpha_{0}\in A
for which the pair
\{M\cap F_{\alpha_{0}}
,
M\cap OF_{\alpha_{0}}

determines the dimension
\operatorname{Ind}M
.

Note that if the space
Z
in Definition 2 is a paracompact
\sigma
-space, then it suffices to require the family

\{OF_{\alpha}\}_{{\alpha}{\in}{A}}
to be
\sigma
-locally finite in
Z

[
7
, Coroll. 1.11]
.

Using the notion of an f-system, we can state a general condition sufficient for the coincidence of the
dimensions
\dim
and
\operatorname{Ind}
.

Statement 1

(
[
6
, Theorem 1]
)
.

If a normal space
X
has an f-system, then
\operatorname{Ind}X=\dim X
.

Thus, the existence of an f-system in a first countable paracompact
\sigma
-space
X
implies

\dim X=\operatorname{Ind}X
(and the equivalence of conditions (a) and (b) in Theorem 1). Therefore,
we have
\operatorname{ind}X=\dim X=\operatorname{Ind}X
for a first countable space
X
with a countable network
(Corollary 1).

According to
[
6
]
, the existence of an everywhere f-system in a first countable paracompact
\sigma
-space

X
is sufficient for the equivalence of conditions (a)–(d) in Theorem 1.

Thus, to prove Theorem 1, it suffices to prove the existence of an everywhere f-system in any first countable
paracompact
\sigma
-space. It is straightforward to construct such a system in a metrizable space, but the
case of a first countable paracompact
\sigma
-space is much more complicated.

However, there exists a fairly large subclass of the class of paracompact
\sigma
-spaces in which everywhere
f-systems surely exist, and it contains all first countable paracompact
\sigma
-spaces. This is the class of
almost semicanonical spaces. It was essentially introduced by Dugundji
[
5
]
; the term “semicanonical” was
suggested by Robert Cauty.

Definition 3
.

Let
A
be a closed subset of a space
X
. The pair
(X,A)
is said to be
semicanonical

if there exists an open cover
\omega
of the open set
X\setminus A
satisfying the following condition: any
open neighborhood
OA
of
A
contains an open neighborhood
UA\subseteq OA
of
A
such that any
element
G
of
\omega
intersecting
UA
is contained in
OA
.

A space
X
in which the pair
(X,A)
is semicanonical for any closed
A\subset X
is called a

semicanonical space
.

Let
X
be a paracompact
\sigma
-space, and let
\gamma=\{F_{\alpha}:\alpha\in A\}
be a
\sigma
-discrete
network of
X
; suppose that
A=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}A_{i}
and the family

\gamma_{i}=\{F_{\alpha}:\alpha\in A_{i}\}
is
discrete in
X
for each
i=1,2,\dots
. Since
X
is regular, we can assume that all
F_{\alpha}
are closed.
We set
F_{i}=\bigcup\{F_{\alpha}:\alpha\in A_{i}\}
for
i=1,2,\dots
.

Definition 4
.

A paracompact
\sigma
-space
X
is said to be
almost semicanonical
if it has a network
\gamma
of the
above form such that every pair
(X,F_{i})
is semicanonical.

Statement 2

(
[
7
, Theorem 1.17]
)
.

Any almost semicanonical space
X
contains an f-system and an everywhere f-system.

To prove Theorem 1, it remains to apply the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Any first countable paracompact
\sigma
-space
X
is almost semicanonical.

The results stated above give a general scheme for proving the coincidence of the dimensions
\dim X
and
\operatorname{Ind}X
for almost semicanonical spaces
X
, which constitute a fairly large class. This class contains all metrizable
spaces, Nagata (that is, stratifiable first countable) spaces and our first countable paracompact
\sigma
-spaces.

The class of almost semicanonical spaces does not contain all spaces with a countable network; e.g., it does not
contain the space
X
with a countable network and noncoinciding dimensions that was constructed by Charalambous

[
4
]
. There also exist stratifiable spaces which are not semicanonical
[
9
]
.

In proving the equality
\dim X=\operatorname{Ind}X
for a given space
X
by the scheme described above,
most difficult is verifying the semicanonicity of
X
. The proof is very involved even for a first countable
paracompact
\sigma
-space
X
satisfying the very strong additional assumption of the existence on
X
of a
semimetric continuous with respect to one variable
[
7
, Theorem 2.15]
.

Still, this is a working method for proving the equality
\dim X=\operatorname{Ind}X
, which suggests the following
problem.

Problem 1
.

Let
G
be a topological group with a countable network. Is it true that the underlying space of
G
is
almost semicanonical?

A positive answer to this question would give a positive answer to Arkhangel’skii’s question of whether the
dimensions of a topological group with a countable network coincide.

Beating the cold: The role of OsTTG1 in developing cold-resistant rice
Authors: Prateek Jain, Ria Khare
Published: 2024

In the face of unpredictable climate patterns, crop growth and yield are becoming increasingly vulnerable, with rice—a staple food crop—being no exception. While much attention has been paid to heat stress and its impact on crops, the effects of cold stress on rice remain underexplored. Cold stress can induce the formation of reactive oxygen species, impair membrane fluidity, inhibit normal growth, degrade proteins, and disrupt photosynthesis (Xu et al. 2023). To combat cold stress, rice plants have developed a mechanism that includes the accumulation of secondary metabolites like anthocyanins, which are regulated by the MYB-bHLH-WD (MBW) transcription factor complex (Schulz et al. 2015; Xu et al. 2015). Wei et al. (2019) demonstrated that TTG1, a WD40 protein that is part of the MBW complex, plays a critical role in regulating plant pigmentation, vegetative and reproductive growth, and abiotic stress responses, including salt and osmotic stress (Wei et al. 2019). However, how TTG1 responds to cold stress in rice had not been thoroughly studied until recently.

In a recent Plant Physiology article, Zhu et al. (2024) provide new insights into the role of OsTTG1 under cold stress in colored rice (Zhu et al. 2024). Building on their earlier work that includes the characterization of OsTTG1 in anthocyanin production in colored rice (Yang et al. 2021), the authors now expand their research to demonstrate its involvement in cold stress responses. To examine the role of OsTTG1 under cold stress, Zhu et al. generated OsTTG1 overexpressing transgenic rice and exposed the plants to a range of temperatures (8 °C, 17 °C, 28 °C, and 32 °C) (Zhu et al. 2024). The authors observed that OsTTG1 expression was significantly higher in transgenic seedlings exposed to short-term cold treatment (8 °C) compared with those grown at standard temperatures (28 °C).

To further understand the impact of OsTTG1 on cold stress tolerance, the authors created osttg1 mutants using CRISPR-Cas9 technology and analyzed their survival and anthocyanin content at various temperatures. Under cold stress (8 °C), the osttg1 mutants exhibited weaker growth, greater stress, and lower anthocyanin levels compared with both wild-type and OsTTG1-overexpressing plants (Fig. 1A). Moreover, these mutants had elevated levels of reactive oxygen species, as confirmed by histochemical staining with diaminobenzidine and nitroblue tetrazolium.

To identify the underlying mechanisms behind cold stress and anthocyanin accumulation, Zhu et al. (2024) performed metabolomic analyses of ttg1 mutants and wild-type seedlings grown at 28 °C and 8 °C. The results revealed significant differences in metabolite profiles, including compounds such as kaempferol, cyanidin, quercetin, naringenin, and tricetin. These metabolites are known to play roles in the biosynthesis of secondary metabolites like anthocyanins.

Additionally, the authors conducted RNA sequencing on 14-day-old seedlings from both osttg1 mutants and OsTTG1 overexpressing lines. The analysis revealed differential expression of genes associated with anthocyanin biosynthesis and cold stress, supporting the role of OsTTG1 in regulating these pathways. Notably, several genes involved in anthocyanin biosynthesis were downregulated in the osttg1 mutants under the cold treatment, which are highly expressed in the OsTTG1 overexpressing lines. Chromatin immunoprecipitation-qPCR further confirmed that OsTTG1 directly regulates these genes.

The study also revealed that the OsDREB1 family of transcription factors, which are key regulators of cold stress responses (Dubouzet et al. 2003), showed reduced expression in the osttg1 mutants. The authors confirmed this by performing a luciferase reporter assay in rice protoplasts, showing that OsTTG1 activates the expression of OsDREB1C. Furthermore, using the International Molecular Exchange Consortium Database, the authors identified putative interacting partners of OsTTG1 under cold stress, including OsbHLH48 and OsMYBS3, both of which were confirmed through in vitro pull-down assays and in vivo co-immunoprecipitation experiments. These interactions were shown to be important for the activation of cold stress-related genes, particularly OsDREB1C.

This study by Zhu et al. (2024) significantly advances our understanding of how OsTTG1 contributes to cold stress tolerance in colored rice. The findings demonstrate the involvement of the MBW complex in regulating anthocyanin accumulation and cold stress responses through the interaction of OsTTG1 with OsMYBS3 and OsbHLH48, which in turn regulate OsDREB1s expression (Fig. 1B). These results pave the way for developing rice varieties with enhanced cold stress resistance, contributing to global food security in the face of climate change.

Consistent deformations in the presymplectic BV-AKSZ approach
Jordi Frias, Maxim Grigoriev

Abstract
We develop a framework for studying consistent interactions of local gauge theories, which is based on the presymplectic BV-AKSZ formulation. The advantage of the proposed approach is that it operates in terms of finite-dimensional spaces and avoids working with quotient spaces such as local functionals or functionals modulo on-shell trivial ones. The structure that is being deformed is that of a presymplectic gauge PDE, which consists of a graded presymplectic structure and a compatible odd vector field. These are known to encode the Batalin–Vilkovisky (BV) formulation of a local gauge theory in terms of the finite dimensional supergeometrical object. Although in its present version the method is limited to interactions that do not deform the presymplectic structure and relies on some natural assumptions, it gives a remarkably simple way to analyse consistent interactions. The approach can be considered as the BV-AKSZ extension of the frame-like approach to consistent interactions. We also describe the underlying homological deformation theory, which turns out to be slightly unusual compared to the standard deformations of differential graded Lie algebras. As an illustration, the Chern-Simons and YM theories are rederived starting from their linearized versions.

Introduction

The usual approach to consistent interactions of local gauge theories is based on the celebrated Noether procedure which amounts to simultaneous consistent deformation of the Lagrangian and gauge generators. The difficulty of the procedure has to do with the fact that even cubic vertexes are defined modulo on-shell vanishing terms and total derivatives, bringing serious technical difficulties
[
1
]
.

The Noether procedure has been cast into the well-defined homological deformation theory by Barnich and Henneaux
[
2
]
who rephrased it in terms of the Batalin-Vilkovisky (BV)
[
3
,
4
]
formulation of the underlying gauge theory. In particular, it was shown that the deformation procedure is controlled by the BRST cohomology in the space of local functionals while possible obstructions are controlled by the Lie bracket induced by the BV antibracket in the local BRST cohomology
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
, see also
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
for applications. Furthermore, in this approach the deformations theory of local gauge systems fits the standard framework
[
12
,
13
]
for deformations of algebraic structures, based on so-called differential graded Lie algebras (DGLA), for a review see e.g.
[
14
]
.

Despite being very elegant and powerful, the local BRST cohomology approach to consistent deformations can be quite involved technically because it requires working in terms of infinite-dimensional jet-bundles and
quotient spaces such as the space of local functionals. At the same time, in the case of topological models the procedure can be reformulated in terms of the finite-dimensional manifolds using the so-called AKSZ construction
[
15
]
. The idea to employ AKSZ-like formalism to study consistent deformations appeared in
[
16
]
and then was successfully applied to higher-spin interactions in 3 dimensions in
[
17
,
18
]
. The main advantage of the approach is that it is immediately on-shell, locality is taken care of by the formalism itself, and the underlying space is (effectively) finite-dimensional. However, in this framework one does not generally get a full control over the Lagrangian formulation at all orders and the approach is somewhat limited to topological systems.

As far as general gauge theories are concerned an interesting extension of the AKSZ construction is the so-called presymplectic BV-AKSZ formulation which has been recently shown to encode a local gauge theory in terms of a finite-dimensional graded geometry
[
19
,
20
]
, see also
[
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
for earlier but less general versions.

It is also worth mentioning the approach of

Presymplectic BV-AKSZ formalism can be considered as a far-going generalization of the AKSZ construction to the case of not necessarily topological and diffeomorphism-invariant theories.

In this work we develop the procedure to analyse consistent interactions within the presymplectic BV-AKSZ approach. We restrict ourselves to the situation where the presymplectic structure stays intact under the deformations and hence restrict ourselves to strictly local interactions. The important difference with the standard deformation theory is that in our approach two structures are deformed simultaneously – the ”covariant Hamiltonian” and the
Q
differential. However, it turns out that this does not lead to complications because the analysis breaks down into two consecutive steps: finding an infinitesimal deformation of the Hamiltonian, which is controlled by the standard homological setup, and then constructing the associated deformation of the
Q

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
contains background material on the deformation theory of algebraic structures, Batalin-Vilkovisky formulation and its jet-bundle extension, and the presymplectic BV-AKSZ approach. In the main Section
3

4
we illustrate the approach using the examples of Chern-Simons and Yang-Mills theories.

Preliminaries

2.1
Deformation theory

A systematic framework
[
12
]
to study the deformations of algebraic structures is provided by differential graded Lie algebras (DGLA). In so doing, the infinitesimal deformations and obstructions acquire a homological interpretation. Now we briefly recall how it works without trying to be maximally general. For a systematic exposition and further references, see e.g.
[
14
]
.

By definition, a differential graded Lie algebra is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded linear space
L
equipped with a differential
\delta:L\to L
satisfying
\deg{\delta}=1
,
\delta^{2}=0
and the compatible graded Lie bracket
[\cdot{,}\,\cdot]
of degree
0
. In applications, the differential
\delta
encodes the underlying algebraic object.
Typically, one is interested in modifying a given “undeformed” differential
\delta=\delta_{0}
so that in the DGLA terms the bracket is unchanged while the differential gets deformed to
\delta=\delta_{0}+[M{,}\,\cdot]
such that
(\delta,[{,}\,])
is still a DGLA.
Condition
\delta^{2}=0
implies that the element
\delta_{0}M+\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}[M{,}\,M]
belongs to the centre, i.e. its adjoint action vanishes. Assuming that the degree
2
component of the centre is trivial, one arrives at the Maurer-Cartan (MC) equation:

\delta_{0}M+\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}[M{,}\,M]=0\,.

(2.1)

It is often convenient to consider formal deformations. This is achieved by tensoring
L
with formal series
\mathbb{C}[[g]]
in the deformation parameter
g
. Assuming
\delta_{0}
and
[{,}\,]
to be of order
0
in
g
and

M=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}M_{i}\,,

(2.2)

where
M_{i}
is of order
i
in
g
,
the MC equation takes the form

\delta_{0}M_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{k}[M_{l}{,}\,M_{k-l}]=0\,,\quad k\,{\geqslant}\,1

(2.3)

For instance, for
k=1
one gets
\delta_{0}M_{1}=0
so that the first order deformations are determined by
\delta_{0}
-cocycles in degree
1
.

Not every first-order deformation can be extended to the next order. Indeed,
at order
2
equation (
2.3
) says that
\delta M_{2}+\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}[M_{1}{,}\,M_{1}]=0
or in other words
[M_{1}{,}\,M_{1}]
must be trivial in the cohomology of
\delta_{0}
. If it is not, one says that the deformation is obstructed.

It is clear that at order
k
one can always add a transformation of the form
M_{k}\to M_{k}+\delta_{0}T_{k}
. Such deformations are unobstructed and correspond to the automorphisms of
L
of the form
\exp{[T_{k}{,}\,\cdot]}
and hence are naturally considered trivial.

To illustrate the general framework let us consider the Lie algebra deformations. Given a linear space
\mathfrak{g}
the Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}
can be encoded in the homological vector field
d_{CE}
on
\mathfrak{g}[1]
. In other words cochains of
\mathfrak{g}
are identified with functions on
\mathfrak{g}[1]
and the Chevalley-Eilenberg differential of
\mathfrak{g}
is identified with
d_{CE}
. The associated DGLA is that of vector fields on
\mathfrak{g}[1]
with the degree being the homogeneity degree and the graded Lie bracket being the commutator. The undeformed
d_{CE}
gives rise to the differential
\delta_{0}:L\to L
,
\delta_{0}a=[d_{CE}{,}\,a]
. In this case the MC equation can be written as:

[d_{CE}+M{,}\,d_{CE}+M]=0\,.

(2.4)

In a more general setup, one could allow
\delta_{0}
to be an outer derivation of
[\cdot{,}\,\cdot]
.

Let us also give a coordinate expression for the structures involved in this example. Let
e_{i}
be a basis in
\mathfrak{g}

and the Lie algebra operation on
\mathfrak{g}
to be determined by the structure constants
k_{ij}{}^{k}
defined through
[e_{i}{,}\,e_{j}]_{\mathfrak{g}}=k_{ij}{}^{k}e_{k}
. Coordinated on
\mathfrak{g}[1]
in the basis
e_{i}
are
c^{i}
,
\deg{c^{i}=1}
. The Chevalley-Eilenberg differential
d_{CE}
is given by:

d_{CE}=-\frac{1}{2}c^{i}c^{j}k_{ij}{}^{k}\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{k}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{k}}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{k}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{k}}$}\kern 1.0pt}\,.

(2.5)

The nilpotency condition
(d_{CE})^{2}=0
is equivalent to the Jacobi identity of
\mathfrak{g}
. A generic element of
L
of degree
1
is given by
M^{\prime}=d_{CE}-\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}c^{i}c^{j}M_{ij}{}^{k}\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{k}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{k}}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{k}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial c^{k}}$}\kern 1.0pt}
and the MC equation implies that
k_{ij}{}^{k}+M_{ij}{}^{k}
satisfy Jacobi identity and hence define a new Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}
. If
\mathfrak{g}
is a graded space, the generic element
M^{\prime}
of total degree
1
is not necessarily linear in
c^{i}
and in fact defines an
L_{\infty}
algebra structure on
\mathfrak{g}
, provided that
M^{\prime}
satisfies the Maurer-Cartan equation. In other words, this is a deformation in the
L_{\infty}
sense. This example illustrates how the deformation approach may also help to identify more general algebraic structures.

2.2
BV formulation of gauge theories

It was observed in
[
2
]
that the introduction of interactions can be reformulated as a deformation of the underlying DGLA which, it turn, is determined by the Batalin-Vilkovisky formulation of the gauge theory in question.

In the BV formalism a gauge theory is described in terms of the field-antifield spaceб which is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded supermanifold graded by the ghost degree
\mathrm{gh}(\cdot)
. This space is equipped with an odd Poisson bracket (BV antibracket) which carries ghost degree
1
and satisfies the graded analogues of the Leibniz and Jacobi identities. The information about the action and (higher) gauge transformation and their algebra is encoded in the unique function
S_{BV}
,
\mathrm{gh}(S_{BV})=0
, called the
master action
. At the same time, the gauge invariance of the action and further compatibility relations are encoded in the so-called master equation:

\big(S_{BV},S_{BV}\big)=0\,,

(2.6)

where
\big(,\big)
denotes the odd Poisson bracket.

The space of functions (strictly speaking, space of functionals) on the field-antifield space can be seen as a DGLA if one takes
\delta=\big(S_{BV},\cdot\big)
and shifts the ghost degree by
1
so that after the shif the degree of the antibracket becomes zero. In such a framework one can study deformations of the gauge theories either by introducing deformation parameter
g
or simply using homogeneity in fields. In the latter case, one assumes that the initial theory is free, i.e.
S_{BV}
is quadratic in fields. This precisely corresponds to the standard QFT approach where one starts with free fields and then introduces interaction vertexes.

Although BV formulation defines a standard deformation theory, at least at the algebraic level, the situation is more involved because the field-antifield space is typically infinite-dimensional and the result crucially depends on the choice of the class of functionals which constitute the linear space underlying the respective DGLA.

The usual and physically motivated choice is given by local functionals, i.e. functionals whose integrands depend on only finite number of derivatives of the fields. In the standard framework, this space can be described as the quotient space of local functions modulo total derivatives so that the antibracket descends to the quotient, resulting in a DGLA structure therein
[
36
]
. This is of course a refinement of the standard QFT framework where the interaction vertexes are defined modulo total derivatives.

2.3
BV for local gauge theories and consistent deformations

The standard mathematical framework to study local gauge theories is that of jet-bundles. This has a natural extension to the case where the fibres are
\mathbb{Z}
-graded supermanifolds, which is crucial in the BV context. Let
\mathcal{E}\to X
be a bundle over a real space-time manifold
X
. Consider the algebra
\bigwedge^{\bullet}(J^{\infty}(\mathcal{E}))

is actually bigraded and the total form degree is a sum of vertical and horizontal form degree. An

-horizontal and

-vertical form on

is refereed to as an
(r,s)
-form. The de Rham differential on

decomposes into the vertical and horizontal parts:

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}d=\mathrm{d_{h}}+\mathrm{d_{v}}\,,\qquad\mathrm{d_{h}}\coloneqq dx^{a}D_{a},\\
\mathrm{d_{h}}^{2}=0,\qquad\mathrm{d_{v}}^{2}=0,\qquad[\mathrm{d_{h}},\mathrm{d_{v}}]=0.\end{gathered}

In the previous expression,

-form

-horizontal and

-vertical form on

.
More details about the geometry of jet spaces can be found in e.g.
[

Consider an

2.4
Presymplectic BV-AKSZ formulation

The geometrical setup for various versions of the BV-AKSZ approach is provided by graded geometry and, more specifically, fibre bundles where not only fibres but also base spaces are graded manifolds. Given a fibre bundle
\pi:E\to\mathcal{X}

\mathcal{I}
be the ideal in

and hence descends to the quotient. The same applies to

Let

0=Q\mathcal{L}+\frac{1}{2}\iota_{Q}\iota_{Q}\omega=Q\mathcal{L}+\frac{1}{2}\iota_{Q}(-d\mathcal{L}+\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}(Q\mathcal{L}+\iota_{Q}\alpha).

(2.18)

Furthermore, adding a vertical vector field

to

does not affect the defining conditions of a presymplectic gPDE. Indeed, for

we have:

\displaystyle\iota_{Q^{\prime}}\omega+d\mathcal{L}=\iota_{Q}\omega+d\mathcal{L},\quad Q^{\prime}\mathcal{L}+\frac{1}{2}\iota_{Q^{\prime}}\iota_{Q^{\prime}}\omega=Q\mathcal{L}+\frac{1}{2}\iota_{Q}\iota_{Q}\omega\,.

(2.19)

It follows,
Q
can be regarded as an equivalence class of vector fields modulo
{\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}}
.

It is convenient to introduce a subalgebra
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(E)
of functions annihilated by
{\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}}
. In particular,

preserves
\mathcal{A}
and is nilpotent there, i.e.

for any

. Furthermore, it makes sense to speak about

-cohomology in
\mathcal{A}
, or more generally

cohomology in the subalgebra of forms preserved by

, for all

.

Consistent deformations of presymplectic gPDEs

3.1
General setup

Although a presymplectic BV-AKSZ system does not directly determine a DGLA that can be used in studying consistent deformations

Let

be a linear presymplectic gPDE. We are interested in its consistent deformations. We restrict ourselves to the deformations that do not affect the presymplectic structure
\omega

Of course, this is not the most general class of deformations but it is well-defined and is in a certain sense natural. Note that even if we deform the presymplectic structure we can always undo the deformation by a suitable coordinate change provided that the structure satisfies certain regularity conditions and its rank is not affected by the deformation.

Let

denote the homogeneity degree in fields (fibre coordinates).
Because our starting point system is linear we have

,

(3.2)

It is useful to introduce the following conventions for the partial sums

\displaystyle Q_{(i)}\coloneqq Q_{0}+\dots+Q_{i},\qquad\mathcal{L}_{(i)}\coloneqq\mathcal{L}_{0}+\dots+\mathcal{L}_{i}

Proposition 3.1

Given a linear pgPDE

(3.4)

\displaystyle\iota_{Q_{(N)}}\omega+d\mathcal{L}_{(N)}\in\mathcal{I},\qquad Q_{(N)}\mathcal{L}_{(N)}+\frac{1}{2}\iota_{Q_{(N)}}\iota_{Q_{(N)}}\omega=\mathcal{O}(N+3)\,.

Proof.

Note that

Let us then take

The above Propositions determine a recursive procedure for constructing consistent deformations. Indeed, starting from a linear presymplectic gPDE

Conversely, given a presymplectic gPDE
(E,Q,\omega,\mathcal{L})
and its particular solution

around

gives a consistent deformation of the linearization of

around

. Details on how the perturbative expansion of (presymplectic) gPDEs works can be found in

]

3.2
Recursive construction

Now we explicitly spell-out the recursive procedure for analysing consistent deformations. Given a linear presymplectic gPDE
(E,Q_{0},\omega,\mathcal{L}_{0})
, equations (
3.5
) and (
3.4
) for
i=1
restrict the first order deformations. Namely,

\displaystyle\iota_{Q_{1}}\omega+d\mathcal{L}_{1}

\displaystyle\cong 0

(3.12)

\displaystyle Q_{0}\mathcal{L}_{1}

\displaystyle=0.

(3.13)

where
\cong
denotes equality modulo
\mathcal{I}
, i.e.
A\cong 0
means that
A\in\mathcal{I}
.
The strategy is then to look for
\mathcal{L}_{1}
of ghost degree
n
, cubic in fields, and satisfying (
3.13
). The first condition forces
\mathcal{L}_{1}
to belong to
\mathcal{A}
. Moreover, vector field
Q_{0}
is nilpotent in
\mathcal{A}
and hence
\mathcal{L}_{1}
belongs to the cohomology
H^{n}(Q_{0},\mathcal{A})
. As we are going to see later, if
\mathcal{L}_{1}
is a representative of a trivial cohomology class, the deformation it determines is trivial.

The condition
\mathcal{L}_{1}\in\mathcal{A}
is necessary for (
3.12
) to have a solution for
Q_{1}
but can be not sufficient in general. If
\omega
is regular it is easy to check that
Q_{1}
must exist, at least locally. However, in applications
\omega
is often not regular but satisfies the more general condition of quasi-regularity, so that the existence of
Q_{1}
is not guaranteed and might result in an additional obstruction. We say that
\mathcal{L}_{1}\in\mathcal{A}
such that
Q_{0}\mathcal{L}_{1}=0
and
d\mathcal{L}_{1}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}(\omega)
represents an
infinitesimal deformation
of our initial system. Inequivalent infinitesimal deformations constitute the tangent space to the moduli space of deformations.

Given an infinitesimal deformation
(\mathcal{L}_{1},Q_{1})
the analysis of higher orders is done order by order, just like in the case of DGLA. However, at each order the analysis is slightly different. More precisely, suppose we have constructed
\{Q_{0},\dots,Q_{N-1};\mathcal{L}_{0},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{N-1}\}
such that (
3.5
) and (
3.4
) are satisfied for
0<i<N
. Then we are looking for
\mathcal{L}_{N}\in\mathcal{A}
, such that
\mathrm{gh}(\mathcal{L}_{N})=n
,
\deg_{\psi}(\mathcal{L}_{N})=N+2
and Eqs. (
3.5
) and (
3.4
) hold for
i=N
, i.e.

\displaystyle Q_{0}\mathcal{L}_{N}+\frac{1}{2}B_{N}=0\,,\qquad B_{N}\coloneq\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}Q_{k}\mathcal{L}_{N-k}\,,

(3.14)

\displaystyle\iota_{Q_{N}}\omega+d\mathcal{L}_{N}\cong 0\,.

(3.15)

Thanks to the first equation,
\mathcal{L}_{N}\in\mathcal{A}
, so that the necessary condition for the existence of a solution to (
3.14
) is that
B_{N}
is
Q_{0}
-closed. We refer to the requirement
Q_{0}B_{N}=0
as the
consistency condition
. It is satisfied thanks to the Proposition
3.2
.

A sufficient condition for the existence of a solution to (
3.14
) is that
B_{N}
is
Q_{0}
-exact. If the cohomology class of
B_{N}
is nontrivial we say that the deformation is
obstructed
. In other words, possible obstructions are non-trivial elements of
H^{n+1}(Q_{0},\mathcal{A})
. If
B_{N}
is nontrivial,
\{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{N-1};\mathcal{L}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{L}_{N-1}\}
should satisfy some additional conditions in order to make
B_{N}
trivial in
H^{n+1}(Q_{0},\mathcal{A})
. If it is not possible to satisfy these conditions for a nontrivial
\mathcal{L}_{1}
one usually says that the initial system is
rigid
, i.e. does not admit deformations as a presymplectic gPDE.

Finally, having obtained
\mathcal{L}_{N}\in\mathcal{A}
that solves (
3.14
) we turn to the remaining equation (
3.15
). It is identical to the analogous problem at the first order, which was discussed above.
If (
3.15
) admits a solution for
Q_{N}
this completes the
N
-th step of the recursive construction.

It can §happen that the deformation already constructed is complete in the sense that
Q_{(N)},\mathcal{L}_{(N)}
satisfy (
2.14
) and hence defines a presymplectic gPDE. In this case, the constructed deformation is finite. If this does not happen or one is interested in studying further deformations one repeats the procedure at order
N+1
and so on. This completes the detailed description of the recursive procedure.

In the next section we will detail the first steps while showing that (
3.7
) is fulfilled at first orders.

3.3
Lowest orders in more details

The first order deformations have been explicitly considered above so that we start with the second order. Let us spell-out explicitly the derivation of (
3.7
) at order
2
. We have

Q_{0}B_{1}=Q_{0}(Q_{1}L_{1})=\mathcal{L}_{Q_{0}}Q_{1}\mathcal{L}_{1}=\mathcal{L}_{Q_{0}}\iota_{Q_{1}}d\mathcal{L}_{1}=\mathcal{L}_{Q_{0}}\iota_{Q_{1}}(-\iota_{Q_{1}}\omega+\alpha_{1})\\
=-\mathcal{L}_{Q_{0}}\iota_{Q_{1}}\iota_{Q_{1}}\omega=-\iota_{[Q_{0},Q_{1}]}\iota_{Q_{1}}\omega-\iota_{Q_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{Q_{0}}\iota_{Q_{1}}\omega\\
=-\iota_{Q_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{Q_{0}}\iota_{Q_{1}}\omega=-\iota_{Q_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{Q_{0}}(-d\mathcal{L}_{1}+\alpha_{1})=-\iota_{Q_{1}}\mathcal{L}_{Q_{0}}\alpha_{1}=0\,,

(3.16)

where we used
Q_{0}\mathcal{L}_{1}=0
and
\iota_{[Q_{1},Q_{0}]}\omega\in\mathcal{I}
,
which holds thanks to:

\displaystyle\iota_{[Q_{1},Q_{0}]}\omega=\mathcal{L}_{Q_{0}}\iota_{Q_{1}}\omega=Q_{0}\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{L}_{Q_{0}}\alpha_{1}=\mathcal{L}_{Q_{0}}\alpha_{1}\in\mathcal{I}\,.

(3.17)

If we don’t step into an obstruction, we can already solve

Q_{0}\mathcal{L}_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}Q_{1}\mathcal{L}_{1}

(3.18)

for
\mathcal{L}_{2}\in\mathcal{A}
with
\deg_{\psi}(\mathcal{L}_{2})=4
,
\mathrm{gh}(\mathcal{L}_{2})=n
.

We then proceed to obtain a vertical
Q_{2}
,
\deg_{\psi}(Q_{2})=2
from

\iota_{Q_{2}}\omega+d\mathcal{L}_{2}\cong 0\,.

(3.19)

Again, one should note that
Q_{2}
is defined up to a vector field from
{\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}}
.

In the case where
Q_{1}\mathcal{L}_{1}
represents an obstruction, i.e., it is non trivial in
Q_{0}
-cohomology, one usually interprets this as a condition restricting the first order deformation. If it was not possible to make
Q_{1}\mathcal{L}_{1}
trivial for a nontrivial
\mathcal{L}_{1}
one concludes that the initial system
(E,Q_{0},\omega,\mathcal{L}_{0})
was rigid.

If the obstruction vanishes we check whether
Q_{(2)}
and
\mathcal{L}_{(2)}
satisfy the conditions to all orders, i.e. whether
(E,Q_{(2)},\omega,\mathcal{L}_{(2)})
is a presymplectic gPDE. If it is not, we continue to the next order.

3.4
Trivial deformations

Just like in the case of DGLA, among possible deformations of a presymplectic gPDE there are some that do not actually produce a new system as they can always be undone by a field redefinition.

Definition 3.3
.

Given a presymplectic gPDE
(E,Q,\omega,\mathcal{L})
, a trivial formal deformation
is defined as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\exp(V)\mathcal{L}+\iota_{\mathrm{d}_{X}}\beta,\quad

(3.20)

\displaystyle Q^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\exp(V)Q\exp(-V),

(3.21)

where the exponential is understood as a formal series and
V
is a vertical vector field satisfying
\deg_{\psi}(V)>0
,
\mathrm{gh}(V)=0
and
\iota_{V}\omega+dH=-\alpha
for some function
H
and 1-form
\alpha\in\mathcal{I}
. Moreover,
\beta\in\mathcal{I}
is a 1-form determined by
\alpha
and
V
through
\exp(L_{V})\omega=\omega+d\beta
.

Note that
\iota_{V}\omega+dH=-\alpha
implies
L_{V}\omega=d\alpha
and hence there exists
\beta\in\mathcal{I}
such that
\exp(L_{V})\omega=\omega+d\beta
. Here we made use of
L_{V}\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{I}
for
V
vertical.

Note also that if
\deg_{\psi}(V)\,{\geqslant}\,k,k>0
, one finds that
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
coincide up to order
k+2
. At order
k+2
one gets

\mathcal{L}_{k}-\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{k}=Q_{0}H_{k}\,,

(3.22)

where we made use of
\beta_{k}=\alpha_{k}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

A trivial formal deformation
(E,Q^{\prime},\omega,\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
of a presymplectic gPDE
(E,Q,\omega,\mathcal{L})
is again a presymplectic gPDE, in the sense that

\iota_{Q^{\prime}}\omega+d\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\cong 0,\qquad Q^{\prime}\mathcal{L}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\iota_{Q^{\prime}}\iota_{Q^{\prime}}\omega=0.

(3.23)

Proof.

Consider the formal transformation
Q\to Q^{\prime}
,
\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}
with

\displaystyle Q^{\prime}=\exp(V)Q\exp(-V)\,,\qquad\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}=\exp(V)\mathcal{L}\,.

(3.24)

Applying
\exp{V}
to both sides of the defining equations (
2.14
) one gets

\iota_{Q^{\prime}}(\omega+d\beta)+d\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}\cong 0,\qquad Q^{\prime}\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\iota_{Q^{\prime}}\iota_{Q^{\prime}}(\omega+d\beta)=0\,,

(3.25)

where we used
\iota_{Q^{\prime}}=\exp({L_{V}})\iota_{Q}\exp(-L_{V})
and that
\exp({L_{V}})\omega=\omega+d\beta
.

Next, by applying
\iota_{Q^{\prime}}
to both sides of
\iota_{Q^{\prime}}d\beta-d\iota_{Q^{\prime}}\beta=L_{Q^{\prime}}\beta

and using that
(Q^{\prime})^{2}
is vertical one finds
\iota_{Q^{\prime}}\iota_{Q^{\prime}}d\beta=2{Q^{\prime}}(\iota_{Q^{\prime}}\beta)
. Using this in (

) and introducing
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}=\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}+\iota_{Q^{\prime}}\beta
we indeed get (
3.23
).
∎

Let us now get back to the recursive construction of deformations. At order

, the deformation is determined by the equation
Q_{0}\mathcal{L}_{N}+\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}B_{N}=0
along with

. The trivial solutions to the first condition are given by

, where
\iota_{V_{N}}\omega+df_{N}\cong 0
for some vertical vector field
V_{N}
. It is clear from the above that such an infinitesimal trivial transformation can be achieved by applying a formal deformation determined by
V_{N}
which is in turn determined by
f_{N}
.
More formally,

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(E,Q,\omega,\mathcal{L})
and
(E,Q^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
be two consistent deformations of the same linear system such that
Q_{i}=Q^{\prime}_{i}
modulo
{\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}}
,
\mathcal{L}_{i}=\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{i}
for

and
\mathcal{L}_{N}-\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{N}=Q_{0}f_{N}
with

satisfying the condition that

for some vertical vector field

to

one finds that it agrees with

at order

as well.

By applying the trivial formal deformation determined by

one finds that now
\mathcal{L}_{N}=\mathcal{L}^{\prime}_{N}
. The only additional point is to check that after the transformation

modulo

. Indeed, at order

which, in turn, implies that

modulo

.
∎

The above Proposition shows that at each order the nontrivial arbitrariness of the deformation is described by the cohomology of

in

.

Examples

4.1
Chern Simons theory

The starting point is the slight generalization of the Abelian Chern Simons. Namely, a generic linear theory with a 1-form field with values in a linear space and the usual Abelian gauge symmetry. If we denote by
A^{i}
the 1-form field, the gauge invariant Lagrangian we have in mind is

L=\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}h_{ij}(A^{i}dA^{j}+A^{i}\Gamma^{j}{}_{k}A^{k})\,,

(4.1)

where
h_{ij}
is a constant invertible symmetric matrix and
\Gamma^{j}{}_{k}
are fixed 1-forms satisfying
h_{ij}\Gamma^{j}{}_{k}+h_{kj}\Gamma^{j}{}_{i}=0
and the zero-curvature equation
d\Gamma+\Gamma^{2}=0
. The gauge transformations are
\delta A=d\lambda
, where
\lambda
is an unconstrained gauge parameter (arbitrary spacetime function). Here we restrict ourselves to local analysis and hence refrain from giving a global geometrical construction. Note that one can set
\Gamma=0
locally by using a suitable transformation of the form
A^{i}\to A^{j}\Lambda^{i}_{j}(x)
. However, we keep

\Gamma
for generality as e.g. in the context of 3d (conformal) gravity models such a transformation is not legitimate as it sets to zero the frame field.

The minimal presymplectic BV-AKSZ formulation of the above system is a slight modification of the well-known AKSZ formulation of the Abelian Chern-Simons theory. The underlying bundle
E\rightarrow T[1]X
is finite-dimensional with coordinates
\{x^{a},\theta^{a},C^{i}\}
, where
\mathrm{gh}(\theta^{a})=\mathrm{gh}(C^{i})=1
,
\mathrm{gh}(x^{a})=0
and
\dim X=3
. The vector field
Q_{0}
acts as follows:

\displaystyle Q_{0}x^{a}=\theta^{a},\quad Q_{0}\theta^{a}=0,\quad Q_{0}C^{i}=\theta^{a}\Gamma^{i}_{aj}C^{j}.

(4.2)

The compatible symplectic structure is given by:

\omega=d\chi=\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}h_{ij}dC^{i}dC^{j},

(4.3)

where
h_{ij}=h_{ji}
. Finally, the “covariant Hamiltonian”
\mathcal{L}_{0}
is given by

\mathcal{L}_{0}=\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}h_{ij}C^{i}\Gamma^{j}{}_{k}C^{k}\,.

(4.4)

The first-order deformation is given by
\mathcal{L}_{1}
of order
3
and satisfying

Q_{0}\mathcal{L}_{1}=0\,,\qquad\mathrm{gh}(\mathcal{L}_{1})=3\,,\qquad\deg_{\psi}(\mathcal{L}_{1})=3\,.

(4.5)

It follows

\mathcal{L}_{1}=\frac{1}{6}k_{ijk}C^{i}C^{j}C^{k}

(4.6)

for some totally antisymmetric
k_{ijk}(x)
. Equation (
4.5
) implies that
k_{ijk}
is covariantly constant with respect to connection
\Gamma
. It’s obvious that
\mathcal{L}_{1}
is not
Q_{0}
-exact, which means that the deformation is nontrivial.

Equation (
3.15
) reads as
\iota_{Q_{1}}\omega+d\mathcal{L}_{1}\cong 0

and gives

Q_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}C^{i}C^{j}k_{ij}{}^{k}\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial C^{k}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{\partial}{\partial C^{k}}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial C^{k}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial C^{k}}$}\kern 1.0pt},

(4.7)

where
k_{ij}{}^{k}=k_{ijl}h^{lk}
. Note that because
h_{ij}
is invertible
\omega
is symplectic (seen as a form on the fiber) and hence (
3.15
) always has a solution.

In the next step we should look for
\mathcal{L}_{2}
of order
4
in fields. However, such
\mathcal{L}_{2}
of ghost degree
3
does not exist and hence, from (
3.14
), we get at order 4 the condition
Q_{1}\mathcal{L}_{1}=0
. Because
\omega
is symplectic, it is a standard fact that this is equivalent to
(Q_{1})^{2}=0
, which in turn implies that
k_{ij}{}^{k}
are structure constants (though in our setup they are allowed to depend on
x
) of a Lie algebra, i.e. the Jacobi Identity

k_{ij}{}^{l}k_{lk}{}^{n}+k_{jk}{}^{l}k_{li}{}^{n}+k_{ki}{}^{l}k_{lj}{}^{n}=0\,.

(4.8)

This imposes the additional condition on the first-order deformation. Note that
Q_{1}
is the Chevalley-Eilenberg differential (
2.5
) of the Lie algebra defined by structure constants
k_{ij}{}^{l}
. Finally, it is easy to see that there are no nontrivial conditions at higher orders in fields so that we have arrived at a consistent deformed system. Explicitly:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}^{(1)}=\mathcal{L}_{0}+\mathcal{L}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}h_{ij}C^{i}\Gamma^{j}{}_{l}C^{l}+\frac{1}{6}k_{ijk}C^{i}C^{j}C^{k},

(4.9)

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=Q^{(1)}=Q_{0}+Q_{1}=\theta^{a}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{a}}+\Gamma^{i}_{aj}C^{j}\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial C^{i}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{\partial}{\partial C^{i}}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial C^{i}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial C^{i}}$}\kern 1.0pt})-\frac{1}{2}[C,C]^{k}\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial C^{k}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{\partial}{\partial C^{k}}}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial C^{k}}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial C^{k}}$}\kern 1.0pt},

(4.10)

where
[C,C]^{k}\coloneqq C^{i}C^{j}k_{ij}{}^{k}
. If there are no auxiliary restrictions like nondegeneracy of the soldering-form component of
\Gamma
in th case of gravity, one can locally set
\Gamma=0
and make
k_{ijk}
constant by the
x
-dependent linear transformation of
C^{i}
.

The analysis of this section is a generalization of that from
[
16
,
18
]
to the case where the initial system is not exactly of AKSZ form.

4.2
Yang-Mills theory

We start with several copies of Maxwell theory. For simplicity we restrict to 4 dimensions but the generalisation to higher dimensions is straightforward. The minimal presymplectic gPDE describing the free theory is given by
T[1]X\times F
, where
X
is the Minkowski space with coordinates
x^{a}
and
F
is a graded manifold with coordinates
C^{i},F^{i}_{ab}=-F^{i}_{ba}
,
\mathrm{gh}(C^{i})=1
,
\mathrm{gh}(F^{i}_{ab})=0
. The
Q
vector field is given by

\displaystyle Q_{0}x^{a}=\theta^{a}\,,\qquad Q_{0}\theta^{a}=0

(4.11)

\displaystyle Q_{0}C^{i}=\frac{1}{2}\theta^{a}\theta^{b}F^{i}_{ab}\,,\qquad Q_{0}F^{i}_{ab}=0\,.

(4.12)

The presymplectic structure is given by
[
21
,
24
,
19
]
:

\omega=\theta^{(2)}_{ab}h_{ij}dF^{iab}dC^{j}\,,\qquad\theta^{(2)}_{ab}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{abcd}\theta^{c}\theta^{d}\,,

(4.13)

where the matrix
h_{ij}
is assumed symmetric and invertible. The covariant Hamiltonian defined through
\iota_{Q_{0}}\omega+d\mathcal{L}_{0}\cong 0
is explicitly given by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}=\frac{1}{4}\left(\theta^{(4)}h_{ij}F^{iab}F^{j}_{ab}\right)\,,\qquad\theta^{(4)}=\frac{1}{4!}\epsilon_{abcd}\theta^{a}\theta^{b}\theta^{c}\theta^{d}\,.

(4.14)

Finally, it is easy to check that
Q_{0}\mathcal{L}_{0}=\iota_{Q_{0}}\iota_{Q_{0}}\omega=0
so that all axioms are satisfied and we have all the data of the linear presymplectic gPDE. Note that
Q_{0}
is nilpotent in this case. Further details on the presymplectic BV-AKSZ formulation of YM can be found in
[
19
]
.

First order deformations are determined by cocycles
\mathcal{L}_{1}
. Taking into account that in addition to

Q_{0}\mathcal{L}_{1}=0
, deformation
\mathcal{L}_{1}
must have ghost degree
4
and order
3
we have the following candidates:

•

\mathcal{L}_{1}=\theta^{a}\theta^{b}C^{i}C^{j}F^{k}_{ab}k_{ijk}
.

In fact it is trivial. Indeed,
k_{ijk}
can be assumed antisymmetric in
ij
. At the same time
Q_{0}\mathcal{L}_{1}=0
implies that
k_{ijk}
is antisymmetric in
jk
as well. This in turn implies that
\mathcal{L}_{1}
can be represented as

\theta^{a}\theta^{b}C^{i}C^{j}F^{k}_{ab}k_{ijk}=Q_{0}\left(\frac{2}{3}C^{i}C^{j}C^{k}k_{ijk}\right).

(4.15)

•

\mathcal{L}_{1}=Y_{a}(x)\theta^{a}C^{3}
,

which can not be rendered Poincaré invariant for nonvansihing
Y_{a}
.

•

\mathcal{L}_{1}=k_{ijk}(F^{i}_{ab}F^{j}_{cd}F^{k}_{ef})Y^{abcdef}\theta^{(4)}
, where
Y^{abcdef}
is Lorentz invariant

It is easy to see that it does not involve ghosts and hence does not deform the gauge symmetries. Such deformations are described by the invariants of the initial linear theory.

•

\mathcal{L}_{1}=\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.5pt}{$\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}$}\kern 1.0pt}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}{\frac{1}{2}}\theta^{(2)}_{ab}C^{i}C^{j}F^{kab}k_{ijk}
.

Let us concentrate on the last candidate, which is the only interesting one. Note that one can assume
k_{ijk}=-k_{jik}
. The cocycle condition reads as

\displaystyle Q_{0}(\mathcal{L}_{1})\propto\theta^{(4)}F^{iab}C^{j}F^{k}_{ab}k_{ijk}=0,

(4.16)

so that
k_{ijk}=-k_{kji}
and hence
k_{ijk}
is totally antisymmetric. It is easy to check that
\mathcal{L}_{1}
is not
Q_{0}
-exact and hence determines a nontrivial first order deformation. Indeed, by counting homogeneity in
F
and
C
one finds that it can only arise as
Q_{0}(C^{3})
but the explicit check shows that it doesn’t happen unless
F
is (anti)self-dual.

The first order deformation of
Q
is determined by

\iota_{Q_{1}}\omega+d\mathcal{L}_{1}\cong 0\,,

(4.17)

giving

Q_{1}=F^{i}_{ab}C^{j}k_{ij}{}^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial F^{k}_{ab}}-\frac{1}{2}C^{i}C^{j}k_{ij}{}^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial C^{i}},

(4.18)

where
k_{ij}{}^{k}\coloneqq k_{ijl}h^{lk}
.

The next order deformation is determined by
\mathcal{L}_{2}
with
\deg_{\psi}(\mathcal{L}_{2})=4
and
\mathrm{gh}(\mathcal{L}_{2})=4
such that

Q_{0}\mathcal{L}_{2}=-\frac{1}{2}Q_{1}\mathcal{L}_{1}\,.

(4.19)

This equation says that

Q_{1}\mathcal{L}_{1}=\theta_{ab}^{(2)}C^{i}C^{j}C^{k}F^{lab}k_{ij}{}^{m}k_{mkl},

(4.20)

is trivial in the cohomology of
Q_{0}
. The only possible candidates for
\mathcal{L}_{2}
are of the form
\theta^{a}Y_{a}C^{3}F
and
C^{4}
. The first cannot be rendered Poincaré invariant. At the same time
Q_{0}\mathcal{L}_{2}
with

\mathcal{L}_{2}=C^{i}C^{j}C^{k}C^{l}q_{ijkl}

(4.21)

can not coincide with
Q_{1}\mathcal{L}_{1}
unless
F
is (anti)self-dual. This shows that
Q_{1}\mathcal{L}_{1}
represents an obstruction.
In fact,
Q_{1}\mathcal{L}_{1}
vanishes if and only if
k_{ij}{}^{k}
satisfies the Jacobi identity (
4.8
). In this way we recover the presymplectic gPDE for the standard YM theory
[
19
,
24
]
:

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=\theta^{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{a}}+\frac{1}{2}\theta^{a}\theta^{b}F^{i}_{ab}\frac{\partial}{\partial C^{i}}+[F_{ab},C]^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial F^{i}_{ab}}-\frac{1}{2}[C,C]^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial C^{i}},

(4.22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\theta^{(4)}F^{i}_{ab}F_{i}^{ab}+\frac{1}{2}\theta^{(2)}_{ab}F_{i}^{ab}[C,C]^{i},

(4.23)

where
[A,B]^{k}\coloneqq A^{i}B^{j}k_{ij}{}^{k}
.

5
Conclusions

In this work we have developed the deformation theory for local gauge theories within the presymplectic BV-AKSZ approach. This has allowed us to reformulate the analysis of consistent deformations in terms of finite-dimensional super-geometrical objects – the so-called presymplectic gauge PDEs. The approach has substantial technical advantages because it avoids working with quotient spaces such as local functions modulo total derivatives and modulo on-shell vanishing terms. One can also draw an analogy with the Hamiltonian formalism which is also “on-shell”. More precisely, the presymplectic BV-AKSZ approach can be thought of as the BV-BRST extension of the covariant phase space formalism
[
45
,
46
]
phrased in terms of the geometrical theory of PDEs
[
47
,
48
]
.

In order to simplify the discussion, we restricted ourselves to the expansion in homogeneity in fields, as this is the standard setup for the analysis of consistent interactions. Of course, the generalization to the expansion in general deformation parameter (e.g. coupling constant) is straightforward.

It is well known that the deformation theory of gauge systems can be employed in the study of perturbative renormalization, see, e.g.
[
41
,
7
]
. The same should apply to the framework proposed in the present work. However, the only subtlety is the treatment of higher-derivative counterterms that would require certain modifications of the procedure.

Possible applications of the approach involve the analysis of the interactions of higher spin fields and massive fields. Another promising direction is the deformations of gravity-like theories for which the presymplectic BV-AKSZ formulation is especially concise, see e.g.
[
23
,
24
,
20
,
49
]
. However, that would require taking into account deformations of the presymplectic form as this structure is usually field-dependent in the case of gravity-like models.

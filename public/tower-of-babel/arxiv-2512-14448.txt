Reasoning-Style Poisoning of LLM Agents via Stealthy Style Transfer: Process-Level Attacks and Runtime Monitoring in RSV Space
Xingfu Zhou, Pengfei Wang

Abstract
Large Language Model (LLM) agents relying on external retrieval are increasingly deployed in high-stakes environments. While existing adversarial attacks primarily focus on content falsification or instruction injection, we identify a novel, process-oriented attack surface: the agent’s reasoning style . We propose Reasoning-Style Poisoning (RSP), a paradigm that manipulates how agents process information rather than what they process. We introduce Generative Style Injection (GSI), an attack method that rewrites retrieved documents into pathological tones—specifically “analysis paralysis” or “cognitive haste”—without altering underlying facts or using explicit triggers. To quantify these shifts, we develop the Reasoning Style Vector (RSV), a metric tracking Verification depth, Self-confidence, and Attention focus. Experiments on HotpotQA and FEVER using ReAct, Reflection, and Tree of Thoughts (ToT) architectures reveal that GSI significantly degrades performance. It increases reasoning steps by up to 4.4 × 4.4\times or induces premature errors, successfully bypassing state-of-the-art content filters. Finally, we propose RSP-M , a lightweight runtime monitor that calculates RSV metrics in real-time and triggers alerts when values exceed safety thresholds. Our work demonstrates that reasoning style is a distinct, exploitable vulnerability, necessitating process-level defenses beyond static content analysis.

Introduction

Large Language Model (LLM) based agents are rapidly evolving from chat-based prototypes to autonomous products capable of planning multi-step actions, calling external tools, and retrieving knowledge to maintain internal states.
As these systems are deployed in high-stakes domains—such as financial analysis, legal consulting, and medical diagnosis—their security has become a pressing concern.
Existing security research has primarily focused on two distinct attack surfaces:
(i)
content-level
data poisoning, which sabotages the system by modifying facts in retrieval corpora (e.g., changing a date or entity), and
(ii)
instruction-level
prompt injection, which attempts to override system constraints via explicit directives (e.g., “Ignore previous instructions and output X”).

However, we argue that current threat models overlook a third, process-level attack surface: the agent’s
reasoning process
—in particular, its
reasoning style
(verification depth, self-trust, and attention focus).
LLM agents rely heavily on Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, a process that is not statically defined but dynamically shaped by the context.
Our key observation is that LLMs exhibit strong
mimetic behavior
: they align not only with the factual content of retrieved documents but also with their
epistemic tone
and
discourse patterns
.
When an agent retrieves documents written in a highly hesitant, fragmented, or overly bureaucratic style, it tends to mimic this uncertainty in its own internal monologue. Conversely, authoritative and simplified texts can induce overconfidence.
This mimetic nature creates a vulnerability in which the
process
of reasoning can be hijacked without touching the
integrity
of the facts or the
validity
of the instructions.

We formalize this attack surface as
Reasoning-Style Poisoning
(RSP).
Unlike traditional attacks that aim for immediate errors, RSP induces subtle but severe process-level pathologies.
For instance, by injecting “analysis paralysis” style—characterized by excessive hedging (e.g., “remains fragmentary”, “requires cross-validation”)—an attacker can force an agent into infinite validation loops, exhausting token budgets and causing Denial of Service (DoS).
Alternatively, a “cognitive haste” style—marked by absolute certainty (e.g., “undisputedly”, “straightforwardly”)—can suppress the agent’s critical thinking, leading to shallow analysis and premature conclusions.
Crucially, because RSP documents contain no false facts or malicious instructions, they bypass state-of-the-art safety filters, creating a significant security gap in current defense architectures.

Contributions.

We make three key contributions to address this gap:

•

We introduce RSP as a
process-level
attack surface and propose a threat taxonomy that separates content, instruction, and reasoning-style manipulations. To quantify reasoning style, we define the
Reasoning Style Vector
(RSV), a three-dimensional vector
(v,s,a)
extracted from agent traces, capturing verification depth, self-trust, and goal focus.

•

We instantiate RSP via
Generative Style Injection
(GSI), an adversarial style-transfer attack that embeds specific linguistic triggers (e.g., “primary documentation remains fragmentary” for paralysis, or “the matter is settled” for haste) into
retrieved content from diverse sources (e.g., web pages, wikis, Github, or databases)
.
While GSI may induce slightly lower raw performance degradation compared to explicit meta-prompting, it achieves superior
stealthiness
: it bypasses state-of-the-art instruction detectors (e.g., PIGuard), mitigation strategies (e.g., delimiters, paraphrasing), and maintains high factual F1, all while successfully inducing significant token wastage or quality degradation.

•

We propose
RSP-M
, a runtime reasoning-style monitor designed to address the lack of process-level visibility in current defenses.
Instead of inspecting static inputs, RSP-M tracks RSV drift in real-time and triggers alerts when the agent’s reasoning trajectory deviates from normal patterns.
Our experiments show that RSP-M effectively flags stealthy GSI attacks that evade traditional input filters, providing a critical last line of defense.

Our results demonstrate that reasoning style is an independent, practically relevant dimension of agent security that is neither captured by standard content filters nor by instruction-level prompt-injection defenses. We argue that robust agent deployments will require
process-level
monitoring and calibration in addition to traditional input and output filters.

Background and Threat Taxonomy

2.1
LLM Agents and Process-Level Security

We consider tool-augmented LLM agents that interleave textual reasoning with tool calls.
Concretely, an agent processes an initial user query
q
and maintains a sequence of intermediate thoughts, actions, and observations:

\tau=\big((t_{1},a_{1},o_{1}),(t_{2},a_{2},o_{2}),\dots,(t_{T},a_{T},o_{T})\big),

(1)

where
t_{i}
is a thought (e.g., a natural-language plan step),

a_{i}
is an action (e.g., a tool invocation),
and
o_{i}
is an observation.
Crucially,
o_{i}
typically contains the raw content retrieved from external sources (e.g., document snippets, API returns), acting as the primary channel for adversarial input.

We focus on common agent architectures such as
ReAct
,
Reflexion
, and
Tree-of-Thought
(ToT).
Most safety work for such agents targets
what they see
(input queries) or
what they output
(final answers).
In contrast, we target the
process
encoded by
\tau
: using stylistic triggers in
o_{i}
to manipulate the agent’s internal reasoning dynamics
t_{i+1}
.

2.2
Attack Surfaces: Content, Instruction, and Style

We distinguish three orthogonal attack surfaces:

Content-level poisoning.

An adversary modifies factual content (e.g., changing dates or entities) in training data or retrieval corpora. Defenses include data sanitization and fact-checking.

Instruction-level prompt injection.

The attacker injects explicit directives (e.g., “Ignore previous instructions”) to override system goals. Defenses rely on pattern matching or separate instruction-tuned models.

Reasoning-style poisoning (RSP).

In RSP, the adversary preserves factual correctness and respects system goals but manipulates the
linguistic style
of documents—for example, by injecting "analysis paralysis" (excessive hedging) or "cognitive haste" (enforced certainty)—to degrade the agent’s reasoning capability.

2.3
Threat Model

We assume a standard Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) or Web-Agent setting.
The agent retrieves information from a diverse set of sources
\mathcal{C}
, which may include private databases, the open Web, or collaborative platforms (e.g., Wikipedia, GitHub).

Attacker Capabilities: Ecosystem-Level Injection.

Unlike prior work assuming full control over a static database, we consider a realistic adversary who performs
open-world knowledge contamination
. The attacker can introduce poisoned documents into the agent’s retrieval scope via:

•

Search Rank Manipulation (SEO):
creating high-ranking web pages with poisoned styles tailored to specific domains (e.g., financial blogs), ensuring they appear in the agent’s top search results.

•

Crowdsourced Knowledge Injection:
editing collaborative resources (e.g., Wikipedia articles, GitHub issues) to subtly alter the tone—making it overly tentative or dogmatic—without triggering moderation bots that look for vandalism or factual errors.

The attacker does
not
have access to the agent’s model weights, system prompts, or private memory.

Injection Rate.

We define the
poisoning ratio

\rho
as the fraction of adversarial documents present in the retrieved context
\mathcal{D}_{q}
(the top-
K
documents). For instance,
\rho=0.1
implies that in a top-10 retrieval, one document has been stylistically altered.

Stealthiness Constraint.

To evade detection, the attacker must satisfy a
semantic consistency constraint
: the poisoned documents must remain factually accurate and relevant to the query. The attack relies solely on
Generative Style Injection
(GSI) to bypass semantic filters and human inspection.

3
Methodology: Generative Style Injection

We propose
Reasoning-Style Poisoning (RSP)
, a paradigm that manipulates an agent’s reasoning process not by falsifying facts or injecting explicit commands, but by altering the
epistemic style
of retrieved information.
In this section, we formalize this attack surface and contrast a traditional instruction-based baseline against our proposed
Generative Style Injection (GSI)
.

3.1
Problem Formulation: Style as an Attack Surface

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an LLM agent that processes a user query
q
given a retrieved context
\mathcal{D}
. The agent’s reasoning process (Chain-of-Thought) can be modeled as a sequence of states
\tau=(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{T})
.
Traditional adversarial attacks target either the
integrity
of
\mathcal{D}
(i.e., changing facts:
Facts(\mathcal{D})\neq Facts(\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
) or the
intent
via explicit instructions (e.g., “Ignore previous rules”).

In contrast, RSP targets the
Reasoning Style

\mathcal{S}
. We operate on the hypothesis that modern LLM agents exhibit
Mimetic Behavior
: their internal reasoning style
S_{agent}
implicitly aligns with the epistemic tone of the retrieved context
S_{context}
.

S_{agent}\leftarrow\text{Mimic}(S_{context})

(2)

Our objective is to construct an adversarial document
d_{adv}
from a clean document
d_{clean}
such that:

1.

Fact Consistency
:

\mathrm{Facts}(d_{adv})\approx\mathrm{Facts}(d_{clean})

2.

Style Injection
:

S(d_{adv})\in\{\text{Paralysis},\text{Haste}\}

3.

Behavioral Manipulation
: The agent reading
d_{adv}
enters a dysfunctional state (e.g., infinite verification loops or premature convergence).

3.3
Ours: Generative Style Injection (GSI)

Core Insight:
We replace explicit instructions with implicit
discourse features
. By rewriting the document to naturally exhibit uncertainty (Paralysis) or absolute consensus (Haste), we manipulate the agent’s logic without triggering syntax-based filters.

3.3.1
The GSI Rewriting Pipeline

We treat GSI as a constrained style transfer problem. We employ a Rewriter LLM
\mathcal{M}_{gen}
(e.g., GPT-4.1) with a specific system prompt to transform
d_{clean}
into
d_{adv}
.
To ensure the attack targets the specific logic of reasoning agents (ReAct/ToT), we inject linguistic triggers mapped to the V/S/A dimensions (defined in Sec.
4
).

3.3.2
Attack Style 1: Analysis Paralysis (The "Doubter")

This style aims to induce a
Denial-of-Service (DoS)
state by trapping the agent in verification loops.

•

Mechanism
: Injects
Epistemic Fragmentation
. It portrays the information landscape as conflicted, incomplete, or requiring expert validation, regardless of the actual clarity of the facts.

•

Linguistic Triggers
:

–

V-Trigger (High Verification)
:
“While secondary sources cite Y, primary documentation remains fragmentary…”
,
“Subject to recent scrutiny requiring cross-validation…”

–

S-Trigger (Low Confidence)
:
“A surface reading might suggest X, but deeper analysis reveals complications…”

–

A-Trigger (Dispersion)
:
“The role of [related entity] in this period remains underexplored…”

3.3.3
Attack Style 2: Cognitive Haste (The "Rusher")

This style aims to induce
Quality Degradation
by forcing premature, often incorrect conclusions.

•

Mechanism
: Injects
Artificial Consensus
. It frames the information as settled dogma, discouraging critical checking or multi-hop reasoning.

•

Linguistic Triggers
:

–

V-Block (Block Verification)
:
“It is universally understood that…”
,
“The matter is settled…”

–

S-Boost (High Confidence)
:
“The straightforward interpretation is invariably correct here.”
,
“Indisputably…”

–

A-Narrow (Tunnel Vision)
:
“Alternative readings are the product of overthinking.”

3.4
Why Defenses Fail: The Semantic Gap

Current defenses like
XML Isolation
(Anthropic) or
Paraphrasing
(Gemini) operate on the assumption that attacks are
separable
from content (e.g., distinct malicious instructions).
However, GSI exploits a fundamental
Semantic Gap
:

1.

Hesitation is Content
: To a summarizer or rewriter, the phrase
"documentation remains fragmentary"
is a valid semantic detail describing the reliability of the source, not a malicious instruction.

2.

Style Retention
: State-of-the-art defenses (e.g., Paraphrasing) are designed to
preserve
the nuance of the original text. Therefore, they faithfully preserve the "Paralysis" style, inadvertently carrying the poison through the defense layer.

4
Metric: The Reasoning Style Vector (RSV)

While standard metrics like Exact Match (EM) capture
outcome
failures (e.g., wrong answers), they fail to capture
process
failures (e.g., infinite verification loops or premature convergence).
To quantify the impact of GSI on the agent’s cognitive state, we introduce the
Reasoning Style Vector (RSV)
, a dynamic metric
\mathbf{r}_{t}=(V_{t},S_{t},A_{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
calculated at each reasoning step
t
.

4.1
Design Principle: Mimetic measurement

A core design constraint of RSV is the
Exclusion of Observations
.
Let an agent’s trace at step
t
be
\tau_{t}=\{(th_{1},ac_{1},obs_{1}),\dots,(th_{t},ac_{t},obs_{t})\}
, where
th
,
ac
, and
obs
denote Thought, Action, and Observation (retrieved content), respectively.
We compute RSV solely on the agent’s generated content
\{th_{1:t},ac_{1:t}\}
:

\text{RSV}(\tau_{t})=f_{\text{metric}}(th_{1:t},ac_{1:t})

(3)

This ensures that RSV measures the agent’s
internal state shift
(the symptom), independent of the injected text (the pathogen).

4.2
RSV Dimensions

V: Verification (Loop Detection).

The Verification dimension quantifies the agent’s tendency to re-examine established information. We model this as the
Semantic Redundancy
of search queries.
Let
q_{t}
be the search query generated at step
t
. We compute its cosine similarity against the centroid of the query history:

V_{t}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\cos\left(\mathbf{E}(q_{t}),\frac{1}{t-1}\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\mathbf{E}(q_{i})\right)+1\right)

(4)

where
\mathbf{E}(\cdot)
is a sentence embedding model (e.g., OpenAI
text-embedding-3-small
).

•

High
V_{t}
(
\to 1
)
: Indicates
Analysis Paralysis
. The agent is "spinning its wheels," repeatedly querying semantically identical information despite different phrasings.

•

Low
V_{t}
(
\to 0
)
: Indicates
Cognitive Haste
. The agent jumps to new topics without cross-validating previous findings.

S: Self-Confidence (Epistemic Certainty).

The Self-confidence dimension captures the agent’s internal conviction. Since GSI manipulates tone, we employ a
Lexicon-based Density Analysis
on the agent’s
Thoughts
(
th_{t}
).
We define a lexicon
\mathcal{L}_{cert}
(e.g., "definitely", "obvious", "proven") and
\mathcal{L}_{hedge}
(e.g., "maybe", "assume", "unclear").

S_{t}=\frac{\sum_{w\in th_{t}}\mathbb{I}(w\in\mathcal{L}_{cert})}{\sum_{w\in th_{t}}(\mathbb{I}(w\in\mathcal{L}_{cert})+\mathbb{I}(w\in\mathcal{L}_{hedge}))+\epsilon}

(5)

•

Low
S_{t}
: Reflects
Self-Doubt
, a primary symptom of Paralysis attacks where the agent mimics the "fragmentary" tone of the poison.

•

High
S_{t}
: Reflects
Overconfidence
, typical of Haste attacks where the agent mimics the "settled" tone.

A: Attention (Focus Dispersion).

The Attention dimension measures whether the agent maintains focus on core entities or drifts to tangential details.
We extract all Named Entities
\mathcal{E}_{t}=\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\}
generated in the thoughts up to step
t
. We calculate the
Inverse Dispersion
in the embedding space:

\text{Disp}_{t}=\frac{2}{k(k-1)}\sum_{i<j}\left(1-\cos(\mathbf{E}(e_{i}),\mathbf{E}(e_{j}))\right)

(6)

A_{t}=1-\text{Disp}_{t}

(7)

•

Low
A_{t}
: Indicates
Scattered Attention
. Under Paralysis, agents often hallucinate "missing links" and drift to irrelevant entities.

•

High
A_{t}
: Indicates
Tunnel Vision
. Under Haste, agents fixate on the first entity found.

4.3
Architecture Adaptation

To ensure fair comparison, we adapt the extraction source of
V,S,A
to the trace structure of each agent (e.g., using ’Vote Scores’ for ToT). Detailed adaptation logic is provided in Appendix
A
.

For
Tree of Thoughts (ToT)
, each branch
b
is assigned an evaluation (“vote”) score
s_{t}^{(b)}
.
We normalize
s_{t}^{(b)}
from
[0,10]
to
[0,1]
and set
S_{t}=\max_{b}s_{t}^{(b)}
(or the voter-selected branch score) as a high-fidelity proxy for self-confidence.
We compute
V_{t}
by aggregating all search queries issued across explored branches, and compute
A_{t}
from named entities in all rationales and evaluation texts, excluding retrieved observations throughout.

Reflexion agents show the highest amplification (+194% for gpt-4.1). The self-critique module interprets the injected "hesitation" as a signal that its previous answer was insufficient, triggering infinite loops of re-verification.

7
Related Work

Our work sits at the intersection of adversarial NLP, RAG security, and autonomous agent reliability. We distinguish RSP from existing paradigms across three dimensions.

7.1
Prompt Injection and Jailbreaking

Early adversarial attacks on LLMs focused on
Jailbreaking
, using optimization-based token perturbations (e.g., GCG)
15
or role-play strategies (e.g., DAN) to bypass safety alignment.
With the rise of integrated applications, focus shifted to
Indirect Prompt Injection

2
;
5
, where attackers embed explicit directives (e.g., "Ignore previous instructions") into external data (emails, websites) to hijack the model’s intent.
Current defenses primarily target these imperative patterns, using perplexity filters
3
or instruction-tuned intent classifiers
10
.

Distinction:
Unlike these attacks, RSP contains no explicit malicious instructions or jailbreak templates. It exploits the
semantic style
channel, which remains invisible to instruction-centric detectors.

7.2
Knowledge Poisoning in RAG

Attacks on Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) typically target the
veracity
of information.

Fact Fabrication
attacks inject misinformation to induce hallucinations
9
.

Backdoor Attacks
inject specific trigger keywords to force targeted outputs when activated
11
;
6
.
Defenses against these threats rely on cross-checking retrieved data against internal parametric knowledge or using consensus filtering.

Distinction:
RSP adheres to a strict
factual consistency constraint
. GSI-poisoned documents are factually accurate, rendering truth-based validation ineffective. Instead of inducing false answers directly, we degrade the
reasoning capability
itself.

7.3
Agent Safety and Process Vulnerability

As agents evolve from simple chat to autonomous execution (e.g., AutoGPT, BabyAGI), security research has begun to address
action hijacking

7
.
However, most work assumes a static reasoning engine. While methods like
Process Supervision

4
and
Self-Correction

8
were designed to improve reasoning reliability, we demonstrate that these very mechanisms can be weaponized.

Distinction:
We identify the
Competence-Mimicry Paradox
, showing that sophisticated cognitive architectures (e.g., Reflexion, ToT) are prone to "over-thinking" or "under-thinking" when exposed to stylistic adversarial perturbations. This highlights the need for dynamic process monitoring (like RSP-M) rather than just input/output filtering.

8
Conclusion

In this paper, we identified a critical blind spot in current AI safety: the
Reasoning Style
.
We introduced
Reasoning-Style Poisoning (RSP)
, a novel attack surface that exploits the mimetic nature of LLM agents. unlike traditional attacks that manipulate facts or inject imperative commands, RSP utilizes
Generative Style Injection (GSI)
to alter the
epistemic tone
of retrieved documents.

Our experiments across SOTA models (including gpt-4.1, gemini 2.5 and Qwen3) and agent architectures (ReAct, Reflexion, ToT) reveal three alarming findings:

1.

High-Capability is not Defense
: Stronger reasoning models are paradoxically more vulnerable to Paralysis attacks due to their superior instruction-following and mimetic capabilities (the
Competence-Mimicry Paradox
).

2.

Architecture Amplification
: Meta-cognitive architectures like Reflexion amplify stylistic noise into catastrophic loops, increasing token costs by up to
194\%
.

3.

The Stealth Gap
: GSI completely bypasses SOTA defenses (PIGuard) and semantic sanitizers (Paraphrasing) because current safety paradigms fail to distinguish "hesitant style" from "factual uncertainty."

To counter this, we proposed
RSP-M
, a process-level monitor. By tracking the
Reasoning Style Vector (RSV)
, RSP-M effectively detects cognitive loops and restores agent functionality.
Our work suggests that future agent defense must evolve from static input filtering to dynamic
process supervision
.

9
Limitations

While our findings are robust, we acknowledge several limitations:

•

Infection Feasibility
: Our experiments simulate a RAG environment where the attacker successfully places poisoned documents in the Top-
K
results. In the wild, achieving this requires successful SEO manipulation or knowledge base access.

•

Adaptive Attackers
: We evaluated GSI against passive detectors. We did not study an "Adaptive Attacker" who specifically optimizes document style to minimize RSV drift while maintaining the attack effect.

•

Metric Dimensionality
: The proposed RSV uses only three dimensions (
V,S,A
). While effective for Paralysis and Haste, more subtle stylistic manipulations (e.g., inducing bias or polarization) may require higher-dimensional representations.

Ethical Considerations

We introduce a stealthy attack vector to highlight the vulnerability of autonomous agents in high-stakes domains (e.g., financial analysis, legal verification).
To prevent misuse, we do not release the weights of the GSI Rewriter model.
All experiments were conducted in controlled sandbox environments with no access to real-world user data.
We advocate that developers of agentic systems integrate process-level monitoring (like RSP-M) before deploying agents that have autonomous budget authority.

No Association Between Umbilical Cord pH and Special Educational Needs Among Children With Normal 5‐Minute Apgar Scores
Authors: Celina Myrhøj, Harman Yonis, Rikke Wiingreen, Ellen Christine Leth Løkkegaard, Christian Torp‐Pedersen, Bo Mølholm Hansen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAimMost infants with a low cord pH have reassuring 5‐min Apgar scores of 7–10, but their long‐term outcomes have been poorly studied. We examined whether cord pH predicted later special educational needs (SEN).MethodThis nationwide register‐based study was conducted in Denmark, which provides universal cord pH screening. It comprised singletons born after 35 weeks of gestation from 2006 to 2013, with 5‐min Apgar scores of 7–10 and cord pH values of < 7.0, 7.0–7.09, and ≥ 7.1. Information on SEN was obtained from national registers until June 3, 2020, when the children were 6.5–14.5 years of age. Associations between cord pH and SEN were analysed using multivariable Cox regression, and eight‐year cumulative incidence estimates were calculated.ResultsWe found that 1121 of the 318 486 (0.4%) infants had cord pH values of < 7.0 and 8582 (2.7%) had 7.0–7.09. The adjusted hazard ratio for SEN was 0.95 (p = 0.73) for a cord pH of < 7.0 and 0.98 (p = 0.72) for 7.0–7.09, compared with a pH of ≥ 7.1. The eight‐year cumulative incidence of SEN was similar in the groups.ConclusionsCord pH was not associated with later SEN in children born after 35 weeks of gestation who had reassuring 5‐min Apgar scores.Keywords: Apgar score, neurodevelopmental outcomes, special educational needs, umbilical cord pH, universal screening

Introduction

The Apgar score is widely used to assess newborn infants immediately after birth. A 5‐min Apgar score of 7–10 is considered reassuring in term and late‐preterm infants, while 4–6 is moderately abnormal, and 0–3 is low [1]. Low 5‐min Apgar scores have been associated with increased risks of mortality and neurodevelopmental disorders at a population level, but the prognostic value for individual infants has been limited [2]. It is important to point out that an Apgar score, on its own, is not a marker of intrapartum gas exchange. Umbilical cord pH provides an objective measure of intrapartum hypoxia‐ischemia and complements clinical assessments at birth [3, 4]. Cord pH values below 7.0 have been associated with obstetric complications, such as placental abruption, low Apgar scores and adverse neurological outcomes [2, 3, 5]. When low cord pH is combined with a low 5‐min Apgar score it may indicate early signs of hypoxic–ischemic encephalopathy [6].

Cord pH can be used selectively to assess signs of hypoxia at delivery in high‐risk deliveries or when newborn infants are depressed [7]. An alternative approach is universal screening, which involves measuring the cord pH of all newborn infants [8]. This practice helps clinicians to make decisions about observations and treatment, enables perinatal audits, and serves as an indicator of obstetric quality. It is notable that approximately 80% of infants with cord pH values below 7.0 presented with reassuring 5‐min Apgar scores in settings that provided universal screening [9]. Although these infants were generally considered unaffected, evidence on their long‐term outcomes was limited, as highlighted in a 2023 review [10]. This knowledge gap underscores the need for further investigations, which are feasible in settings like Denmark, where universal cord pH screening is implemented [11, 12].

Brain damage is a major concern in children who had survived intrapartum hypoxia and may lead to varying degrees of cognitive impairment [13]. Some of these children had been diagnosed with a neurodevelopmental disorder before they started school. Others had remained undiagnosed or did not fulfil the established diagnostic criteria. Cognitive demands have been shown to increase during schooling and unrecognized impairments may manifest as special educational needs (SEN) [14]. SEN can serve as a proxy indicator for functional difficulties in schools [15]. Children with neurodevelopmental disorders often have SEN, but educational support may be provided without a formal diagnosis. Many children with SEN remain undiagnosed throughout their school life [16].

The aim of this study was to examine whether cord pH values at birth were associated with adverse outcomes at school among term‐born children with reassuring Apgar scores. School outcome was assessed by SEN, and diagnosed neurodevelopmental disorders was also described. We hypothesized that there would be no association between umbilical cord pH and school outcome in children who had reassuring 5‐min Apgar scores.

Methods

Study Design and Settings

We conducted a population‐based cohort study that used data extracted from national registers held by Statistics Denmark. Every citizen in the country is assigned a unique 10‐digit personal identification number at birth or on immigration. This means that individual data can be linked across national registers. Our study comprised data from five national registers. The Danish Medical Birth Register includes data on all live births, labour, and pregnancies [17]. The Special Educational Needs (SEN) Register contains information on special educational support provided during each school year [18]. The Cause of Death Register and Migration Register track data on all deaths and emigrations [19]. The Danish Education Registers provide information on the education status of all citizens [20]. The Danish National Patient Registry comprises information on discharge diagnoses and surgical procedures for all somatic hospitalizations [21], based on the International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision.

There are approximately 60 000 births per year in Denmark and 97% take place in public hospitals, largely due to the Danish publicly funded healthcare system [22]. Since 2009, routine cord pH measurements from the umbilical artery and vein have been recommended for all deliveries. Samples are collected and analysed within 30 min of birth [11].

Study Population

The study population included all singleton infants born alive at 36–44 weeks of gestation between January 1, 2006, and December 31, 2013. Infants with missing data on gestational age or birth weight were excluded. Birth weight was adjusted for gestational age, in order to calculate a standard deviation score, and children with values beyond ±6 standard deviations were removed due to presumed coding errors. We also excluded infants with missing or invalid cord pH values or 5‐min Apgar scores.

The follow‐up process began on August 1, 2012, and ended on June 30, 2020, when the children were 6.5–14.5 years of age. Children who died or emigrated before they started school were excluded from the study population. Those who were not recorded in the SEN Register within the follow‐up period were also excluded.

Clinical Information and Definitions

Baseline Characteristics and Perinatal Data

The data included characteristics on the infants´ sex assigned at birth, gestational age, and birth weight. Birth weight was categorized as small, appropriate or large for gestational age, using standard deviation scores. The 5‐min Apgar scores were grouped as low, moderately abnormal, or reassuring. Maternal information included the body mass index recorded within the first 10 weeks of pregnancy. Parental educational levels were determined by the highest recorded education at the time of delivery. These were categorized into three groups, according to the International Standard Classification of Education, as low (level 0–2), middle (level 3–5), and high (level 6–8). Delivery data comprised acute obstetric complications and mode of delivery.

Cord pH Levels

The primary exposure was cord pH. The lowest value was used if the children had two recorded cord pH values and those who had more than two values recorded were excluded to prevent potential misclassification. Cord pH values were only included if they ranged from 6.55 to 7.55, as values outside this range were considered measurement errors. The exposure was divided into three cord pH categories: low (< 7.0), borderline (7.0 to < 7.1), and normal (≥ 7.1).

SEN And Neurodevelopmental Disorders

The primary outcome was receiving at least nine hours of SEN support during any school year in the follow‐up period. Support is provided free in all Danish Schools. It has been required to record SEN annually as yes or no in the SEN Register since 2012. This follows an evaluation by the school principal, based on an educational and psychological assessment. The Register contains educational data from grade zero to grade nine on all pupils in Danish compulsory schools, including special schools. Children typically enter school in the year that they turn six and receive 10 years of compulsory schooling education [23].

Diagnoses of neurodevelopmental disorders were obtained to evaluate the primary outcome, using the International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision. These included autism spectrum disorder (DF84), attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (DF90, DF98), epilepsy (DG40, DG41), conduct disorder (DF91), intellectual disability (DF70, DF71, DF72, DF73, DF78, DF79) and cerebral palsy (DG80). All these conditions were grouped as neurodevelopmental disorders and included from birth until the end of the follow‐up period.

Statistical Analyses

All statistical analyses were performed using R‐studio version 4.2.1 (R Foundation, Vienna, Austria) and p‐values of < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Descriptive statistics are presented as frequencies with percentages for categorical variables. The groups were compared using the chi‐square test.

The main analyses were restricted to the study cohort of children with reassuring 5‐min Apgar scores. Univariable and multivariable Cox regression models were used to account for the fact that the follow‐up times varied among the children and potential confounders were considered. We examined the association between the level of cord pH and SEN and estimated cause‐specific hazard ratios with 95% confidence intervals (CI). The multivariable model was adjusted for key covariates, including sex, gestational age, birthweight according to gestational age, maternal body mass index and parental educational level. Children were tracked in the SEN Register from the date of their first registration and the first entry we included was August 1, 2012. Every child was followed from the first registration, regardless of grade level and age, until one of the following events occurred: SEN, death, emigration, or the end of the follow‐up period. This was defined as the last day of the last school year the child was registered in the SEN Register. The latest date was June 30, 2020.

The cumulative risk of SEN, according to cord pH, was estimated using the Aalen‐Johansen estimator for children with normal 5‐min Apgar scores. The cumulative risk was estimated based on the number of years in the SEN Register. Children who died or emigrated without SEN were censored at the time of death or emigration. Death was not considered a competing outcome, due to the very low mortality rate during the follow‐up period. The frequencies of diagnosed neurodevelopmental disorders were also examined across the three cord pH levels groups.

Supplementary analyses were also performed. Mortality was examined from birth to the end of the follow‐up period to address potential survival bias. These analyses included all children with reassuring 5‐min Apgar Scores, stratified by cord pH levels. Sensitivity analyses excluded children born small for gestational age, in order to examine its potential role as a collider in the main multivariable Cox regression analyses.

The internal validity of the primary outcome, SEN, was examined by several analyses.

All the Apgar score categories were included in a multivariable Cox regression to examine the association with SEN, using the same covariates as in the main analyses.

The cumulative risk was estimated with the Aalen‐Johansen estimator across all three Apgar groups. Children who died or emigrated without SEN were censored. Death was not treated as a competing risk and this was consistent with the main analyses. The associations between diagnosed neurodevelopmental disorders and SEN were also examined.

The impact of cord pH was assessed by examining any mortality before the children started school. This was examined across all Apgar groups and stratified by cord pH levels. In addition, stratified multivariate Cox regression examined the association between cord‐pH levels and SEN in the subgroup with 5‐min Apgar scores of < 7. The model was adjusted for the same covariates as the main analyses.

Results

Study Population and Baseline Characteristics

There were 477 772 liveborn singletons in Denmark during the 2006–2013 study period. We excluded 6.2% with invalid data on gestational age or birthweight, 23.3% with invalid cord pH measurements, 0.2% with missing Apgar scores, 0.1% who had died and 2.3% who emigrated before they started school. A further 1.4% were not in the SEN Register and this resulted in a final study population of 320 143 (67%) children (Figure 1). Approximately 40% of the study population had two valid cord pH measurements.

Main Analyses

The primary analysis was restricted to the 318 483 (99.5%) children with a 5‐min Apgar score of 7–10. Of these, 0.4% had a cord pH of < 7.0, 2.7% had ≥ 7.0 to < 7.1 and 97.0% had ≥ 7.1 (Figure 1). The unadjusted Cox regression analysis showed that the hazard ratio for SEN was 0.95 (95% CI 0.72–1.24) in children with a cord pH of < 7.0 and 1.01 (95% CI 0.91–1.11) for those with ≥ 7.0 to < 7.1, compared to those with ≥ 7.1. After we excluded the 7287 (2.3%) children with missing covariates, there were 311 196 children included in the multivariate analysis. This showed that the hazard ratio for SEN was 0.95 (p = 0.73) for a cord pH of < 7.0 and 0.98 (p = 0.72) for ≥ 7.0 to < 7.1, compared to ≥ 7.1. All the co‐variates that were included were significantly associated with SEN (Table 2).

The cumulative incidence of SEN over the eight‐year follow‐up period was 6.7% (95% CI 4.8–8.5) in children with a cord pH of < 7.0, 8.5% (95% CI 7.4–9.6) for ≥ 7.0 to < 7.1 and 8.0% (95% CI 7.8–8.1) for ≥ 7.1 (Figure 2). We found that 1121(0.3%) children with a cord pH of < 7.0 were at risk for SEN in the first year after their entry in the SEN Register. This declined to 128 of the 27 215 (8.5%) we were able to follow in the register for eight years. We found that 44 (0.01%) of the 318 483 children with a reassuring 5‐min Apgar score died during the follow‐up period and all of these had a cord pH of ≥ 7.1.

The cumulative incidence of special educational needs over the 8‐year follow‐up period. The total number at risk was 318 483.

The frequencies of diagnosed neurodevelopmental disorders across the three cord pH groups were similar: 1.3% (95% CI 0.81–2.20) in the < 7.0 group, 0.7% (95% CI 0.53–0.89) in the ≥ 7.0 to < 7.1 group and 0.9% (95% CI 0.88–0.93) in the ≥ 7.1 group.

Supplementary Analyses

Mortality from birth to the end of the follow‐up period was similar in the three cord pH groups when we looked at the children with reassuring 5‐min Apgar scores. These were 0.26% (95% CI 0.03–0.55) for the < 7.0 group, 0.13% (95% CI 0.06–0.21) for the ≥ 7.0 to < 7.1 group, and 0.15% (95% CI 0.14–1.16) for the ≥ 7.1 group. The association between cord pH and SEN was not affected in the sensitivity analyses where the children born small for gestational age were excluded in the multivariate analysis (Table S1).

The multivariate analysis that included all three 5‐min Apgar groups showed the association between Apgar scores and SEN. The hazard ratios were 2.70 (p < 0.001) for an Apgar score of 0–3 and 1.53 (p < 0.001) for a score of 4–6, when they were compared to score of 7–10 (Table S2). In addition, the cumulative incidence of SEN over the eight‐year follow‐up period was 21.8% (95% CI 12.8–30.8) for an Apgar score of 0–3, 13% (95% CI 10.3–15.7) for 4–6 and 8% (95% CI 7.8–8.1) for 7–10 (Figure S1).

Diagnoses of neurodevelopmental disorders were associated with SEN. We found that 40.6% (95% CI 38.8–42.4) of the children diagnosed with neurodevelopment disorders had SEN, compared to 4.5% (95% CI 4.9–4.6) of those without these diagnoses. In addition, 92.5% (95% CI 92.1–92.9) of the children with SEN did not have a diagnosis of neurodevelopmental disorders (Table S3).

When we included the Apgar score groups, mortality increased according to cord pH: by 1.5% (95% CI 0.85–2.11), in the < 7.0 group, 0.18% (95% CI 0.10–0.27) in the ≥ 7.0 to < 7.1 group and 0.15% (95% CI 0.14–0.16) in the ≥ 7.1 group (Table S4).

In a stratified multivariate Cox regression analysis that only included children with a 5‐min Apgar score of less than 7, associations with cord pH and SEN were examined. This showed that the hazard ratio for SEN was 1.90 (p < 0.01) for a cord pH of < 7.0 and 1.31 (p = 0.25) for ≥ 7.0 to < 7.1, when compared to the ≥ 7.1 group (Table S5).

Discussion

This nationwide cohort study was conducted in Denmark, where cord pH is routinely measured in all newborn infants. We examined the association between cord pH and school‐age outcomes in children born after 35 weeks of gestation with reassuring 5‐min Apgar scores. This showed that cord pH was not associated with adverse long‐term outcomes, when it was assessed by SEN provision at school. The frequencies of neurodevelopmental diagnoses were also similar across the three groups with cord pH values of < 7.0, ≥ 7.0 to < 7.1 and ≥ 7.1.

Few studies have investigated long‐term outcomes in children with low cord pH and reassuring Apgar scores. A 2023 review highlighted that the evidence remained limited and heterogeneous [10]. We selected SEN as a long‐term outcome, due to its reliance on an unbiased, mandatory registration system and its association with cognitive impairment [24]. As expected, lower 5‐min Apgar scores were associated with SEN. Diagnosed neurodevelopmental disorders were also associated with SEN, although most cases with SEN lacked formal diagnoses. This could have been a reflection of the median follow‐up age, as it has been shown that many children are not diagnosed before adolescence [25]. In common with other studies, SEN was also linked to factors such as sex at birth, being small for gestational age, lower gestational age, and socioeconomic factors such as parental educational level [26]. Adjusting for these factors, did not alter the association between cord pH and SEN. This supported our hypothesis that the cord pH in children with reassuring Apgar would not be linked to an increased risk of SEN.

Approximately 75% of the newborn infants in our study had at least one recorded cord pH value. Cord pH correlated with adverse outcomes when all 5‐min Apgar categories were included, as reported by other studies [27]. A low cord pH was linked to adverse obstetric events, lower 5‐min Apgar scores and increased mortality. A low cord pH was also associated with SEN in the subgroup with 5‐min Apgar scores of less than 7. A Danish register‐based study showed that low cord pH was associated to severe short‐term outcomes, as well as less severe conditions, such as neonatal hypoglycemia [28]. We cannot exclude the possibility that universal cord pH screening facilitated the early detection and management of conditions such as hypoglycaemia, which would have mitigated potential adverse outcomes. As with other potential benefits of universal cord pH screening, our study was not designed to evaluate these effects. The UK National Institute for Clinical Excellence and the American College of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists only recommend cord pH measurements in specific cases. They typically exclude newborn infants with reassuring 5‐min Apgar scores. Our findings should not be interpreted as supporting or opposing this strategy [7]. Low cord pH in newborn infants were associated with adverse neonatal outcomes in our study, including low Apgar scores and mortality. The clinical relevance of low cord pH was also demonstrated in a Swedish cohort study. This showed that a cord pH of < 7.05 was also associated with adverse long‐term outcomes when all levels of Apgar scores were included [27]. However, our study found that most infants with low cord pH had reassuring 5‐min Apgar scores and this was consistent with previous studies [9]. Our study suggests that low cord pH, on its own, was not sufficient to identify infants who would experience adverse outcomes. These findings may help clinicians to counsel parents, provide information and develop follow‐up strategies in settings that provide universal cord pH screening. Low cord pH has been recognized as an important marker of hypoxic–ischemic intrapartum events. The level and duration of hypoxia or ischemia required to result in a brain may vary among newborn infants. In term infants, an association between cord pH and plasma catecholamine levels has been demonstrated [29]. It has also been suggested that activation of the sympathoadrenal system may play a compensatory role in maintaining physiological homeostasis despite low cord pH [30]. This adaptive response may help mitigate the detrimental effects of intrapartum hypoxia in some infants. There is need for further research on how both antepartum and intrapartum factors may protect or predispose newborn infants to the adverse effects of intrapartum hypoxia [31].

Strengths and Limitations

Our study had several strengths. It was based on a large, nationwide cohort and data that are routinely collected. This enabled us to capture rare exposures and outcomes, while minimizing recall bias. Furthermore, we were able to adjust for potential confounding factors, which enhanced the robustness of our findings. The use of cord pH as an exposure variable was unbiased, as it was routinely measured in all Danish infants. Likewise, our primary outcome, SEN, represented a real‐life measure that was mandatorily registered for all eligible children and this reduced susceptibility to bias and minimized cases lost to follow up.

The study also had several limitations. Approximately 25% of the infants did not have cord pH data, which could have introduced selection bias. However, other research that used the same registry data suggested that missing values were more common in less complicated pregnancies and deliveries. This implied that these infants had a lower risk of birth hypoxia [11]. Cord pH measurements were obtained from data that reflected routine clinical practice and were not formally validated. In contrast, some other studies only included cord pH values if two atrial measurements with similar pH values were available [27]. We recognize that other biomarkers for hypoxia, such as UC lactate, could have provided a more comprehensive assessment [32].

SEN was used as a proxy outcome and this could have underestimated the frequency of cognitive or behavioural deficits that did not meet the SEN threshold. In addition, the age at follow up could also have underestimated deficits that may have occurred after the follow‐up range of 6.5–14.5 years of age.

Conclusion

This was a nationwide study from Denmark, where cord pH is routinely measured in all newborn infants. The level of cord pH in children born after 35 weeks of gestation, with reassuring 5‐min Apgar scores of 7–10, was not associated with SEN. Our study suggested that low cord pH, on its own, does not predict adverse long‐term neurodevelopmental outcomes.

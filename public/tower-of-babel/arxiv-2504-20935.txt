Note about the complexity of the acyclic orientation with parity constraint problem
Sylvain Gravier, Matthieu Petiteau, Isabelle Sivignon

Abstract
Let G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) be a connected graph, and let T ⊆ V T\subseteq V be a subset of vertices. An orientation of G G is called T T -odd if any vertex v ∈ V v\in V has odd in-degree if and only if it is in T T . Finding a T T -odd orientation of G G can be solved in polynomial time as shown by Chevalier, Jaeger, Payan and Xuong (1983). Since then, T T -odd orientations have continued to attract interest, particularly in the context of global constraints on the orientation. For instance, Frank and Király (2002) investigated k k -connected T T -odd orientations and raised questions about acyclic T T -odd orientations. This problem is now recognized as an Egres problem and is known as the “Acyclic orientation with parity constraints” problem. Szegedy (2005) proposed a randomized polynomial algorithm to address this problem. An easy consequence of his work provides a polynomial time algorithm for planar graphs whenever | T | = | V | − 1 |T|=|V|-1 . Nevertheless, it remains unknown whether it exists in general. In this paper we contribute to the understanding of the complexity of this problem by studying a more general one. We prove that finding a T T -odd acyclic orientation on graphs having some directed edges is 𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP} -complete.

Introduction

The study of constrained graph orientations and parity related problems has received significant interest in recent years. A notable problem intersecting these two domains is the acyclic orientation with parity constraints problem, which has been the subject of various research efforts. The first constraint considered in this problem is a local one, that involves the parity of the in-degree of vertices in an orientation. For a graph
G=(V,E)
and a subset
T\subseteq V
, an orientation of
G
is called
T
-odd if any vertex
v\in V
has odd in-degree if and only if it is in
T
. Chevalier, Jaeger, Payan and Xuong (1983)
[
2
]
proved that a
T
-odd orientation of an undirected graph exists if and only if
|E(G)|+|T|\equiv 0
and that finding one can be done in polynomial time.

Further advancements were made by Frank and Király (2002)
[
3
]
when adding a global constraint on the orientation, the
k
-arc-connectivity ie. orientations such that any pair of vertices is connected by
k
arc-disjoints paths. They gave a characterization of undirected multigraphs
G=(V,E)
having a
k
-arc-connected
T
-odd orientation for every subset
T\subseteq V
with
|E|+|T|\equiv_{2}0
.
It also raised questions on other global constraints such as acyclicity which can be seen as an other kind of connectivity constraint. This leads to the current formulation of Problem
1
.

Problem 1

)

(G,T)

This existence problem is referenced on the Egres platform

A basic greedy algorithmic approach to Problem

Problem 2

(

)
.

Instance:
A pair
(G,T)
where
G
is a graph having a subset
A

Does
G

T

A polynomial time algorithm to solve this problem on trees and on graphs of maximum degree

Theorem 1.2

\mathsf{NP}

As a consequence of this theorem, a basic greedy algorithmic approach is unlikely to exist for this problem, unless

3.4
Proof of the reduction

In this section, we establish the following result expressing the reduction from
Planar

3-SAT
to Problem
2
.

Recall that the acyclic
T
-odd orientation problem is in
\mathsf{NP}
. Given an orientation, one can verify in polynomial time whether it satisfies the parity constraints for each vertex and whether the orientation is acyclic.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C})
be a
Planar

3-SAT
instance and
I_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}}=(G,T)
be the corresponding instance of Problem
2
.

I_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}}
has an acyclic
T
-odd orientation if and only if
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C})
has a
True
assignment.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C})
be a
Planar

3-SAT
instance and
I_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}}=(G,T)
be the corresponding instance of Problem
2
.

Let
\alpha
be an assignment of
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C})
and
O
be a
T
-odd orientation of
I_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}}
such that
O(\Gamma_{X_{i}})
is acyclic for all
i\in\{1,...,n\}
. We say that
\alpha
(or respectively
O
) is
compatible
to
O
(respectively
\alpha
) when, for all
i\in\{1,...,n\}
, we have that:

\alpha(x_{i})=True\ \hbox{if and only if}\ O(\Gamma_{X_{i}})=O(\Gamma_{X_{i}})_{out}.

Observe that, by Corollary
3.3
, if
\alpha
and
O
are compatible, then we also have, for all
i=1,...,n
, that:

\alpha(x_{i})=False\ \hbox{if and only if}\ O(\Gamma_{X_{i}})=O(\Gamma_{X_{i}})_{in}.

Notice also, from Corollary
3.3
, that any
T
-odd orientation
O
of
I_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}}
such that
O(\Gamma_{X_{i}})
is acyclic for all
i\in\{1,...,n\}
has a compatible assignment of
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C})
, and from Lemma
3.4
, that any assignment of
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C})
has a compatible orientation of
I_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}}
.

Moreover, by Lemma
3.4
, a
T
-odd orientation
O
of
I_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}}
such that
O(\Gamma_{X_{i}})
is acyclic for all
i\in\{1,...,n\}
is acyclic if and only if
O(\Gamma_{C^{j}})
satisfies
\delta^{-}(W_{C^{j}})\neq\emptyset
for all
j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
.

And, since a
True
assignment of
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C})
is an assignment which satisfies each clause in
\mathcal{C}
, to prove Theorem
3.5
, it is enough to check that for any
\alpha
and
O
compatible and any clause
c^{j}
, we have that:

\alpha\ \hbox{satisfies}\ c^{j}\ \hbox{if and only if}\ \delta^{-}(W_{C^{j}})\neq\emptyset\ \hbox{in}\ O(I_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}}).

Let
c^{j}\in\mathcal{C}
. Without loss of generality, one may assume that
c^{j}=l_{1}\vee l_{2}\vee l_{3}
with either
l_{i}=x_{i}
or
l_{i}=\neg x_{i}
, for
i=1,...,3
. By definition of the disjunction
\vee
, we have:

c^{j}\text{ is satisfied by }\alpha\iff\exists l_{i}\in c^{j},\begin{cases}l_{i}=x_{i}\text{ and }\alpha(x_{i})=True,\text{ or }\\
l_{i}=\neg x_{i}\text{ and }\alpha(x_{i})=False.\end{cases}

By construction of
I_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}}
and since
O
is compatible to
\alpha
, this is equivalent to:

\exists i,\begin{cases}v^{j}_{i}\in T(I_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}})\text{ and }O(\hat{u}_{i}^{\sigma^{-1}_{x_{i}}(c^{j})}v^{j}_{i})=\overrightarrow{\hat{u}_{i}^{\sigma^{-1}_{x_{i}}(c^{j})}v^{j}_{i}}\text{ or }\\
v^{j}_{i}\in V(I_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}})\setminus T(I_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}})\text{ and }O(\hat{u}_{i}^{\sigma^{-1}_{x_{i}}(c^{j})}v^{j}_{i})=\overleftarrow{\hat{u}_{i}^{\sigma^{-1}_{x_{i}}(c^{j})}v^{j}_{i}}.\end{cases}

Since
v^{j}_{i}
is of degree
2
, by the parity condition on the in-degree of
v^{j}_{i}
, this is equivalent to:

\exists i,O(v^{j}_{i}w_{i}^{j})=\overrightarrow{v_{i}^{j}w_{i}^{j}}.

This is precisely
\delta^{-}(W_{C^{j}})\neq\emptyset
in
O(I_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}})
.

Thus, for
(O,\alpha)
a pair of compatible orientation and assignment,
O
is an acyclic
T
-odd orientation of
I_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}}
if and only if
\alpha
is a
True
assignment of
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C})
.

∎

One can check that the construction of
I_{(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C})}
from
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C})
can be done in polynomial time. Thus, our main result, Theorem
1.2
, is a direct consequence of Theorems
3.5
and
3.1
.

4
Conclusion

We proved that finding an acyclic
T
-odd orientation in a partially directed graph is
\mathsf{NP}
-complete, even when
G
is planar of maximum degree 3, and vertices in
V(G)-T
have degree 2. However a polynomial time algorithm exists for trees or graphs of maximum degree
2
. So classes of graphs on which Problem
2
can be solved in polynomial time cannot be much more complex.

We could also discuss the complexity of Problem
2
parameterized by the size of
T
. Unfortunately, the problem remains
{\mathsf{NP}}
-complete even when
T=\emptyset
. Indeed, from
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C})
a
Planar

3-SAT
instance and
I_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}}=(G,T)
its corresponding instance of Problem
2
, any vertex
v\in T
of degree
2
could be contracted into a single edge resulting in an equivalent instance
I^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}}=(G^{\prime},T^{\prime})
where each
v\in V(G^{\prime})
has degree
3
if
v\in T^{\prime}
and degree
2
otherwise. Flipping the orientation of all edges in an acyclic
T^{\prime}
-odd orientation of
I^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}}
leads to an acyclic
\emptyset
-odd orientation of
I^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C}}
. Thus, even finding acyclic
\emptyset
-odd orientation on partially directed planar graphs of maximum degree
3
is
\mathsf{NP}
-complete. However, in our reduction from
Planar

3-SAT
to Problem
2
, the constructed graph contains a linear number of arcs, which raises questions about the complexity of Problem
2
when parameterized by the number of arcs.

Going back to Problem
1
, there exists a deterministic polynomial time algorithm to determine whether
G
admits an acyclic
T
-odd orientation whenever
G
is either planar or
3
-regular and
|T|=|V(G)|-1

[
4
]
. But for a general
T\subset V
the problem remains open, even if a randomized polynomial time algorithm is known
[
7
]
.
Our result shows that a basic greedy algorithmic approach is not likely to exists. Overall, determining the complexity of Problem
1
on those classes is challenging as it belongs to the frontier of
{\mathsf{P}}
,
{\mathsf{RP}}
and
{\mathsf{NP}}
classes of problems (see Figure
10
).

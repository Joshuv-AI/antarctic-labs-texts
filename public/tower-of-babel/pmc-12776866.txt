Relationship of Circulatory Markers of NETosis with COVID-19 Severity: A Prospective Cohort Study
Authors: Abhyuday Kumar, Neeraj Kumar, Ayan Banerjee, Ajeet Kumar, Amarjeet Kumar, Chandni Sinha
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNETosis is a unique form of cell death leading to the formation of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs), which may be responsible for many of the serious complications of COVID-19, such as acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), cytokine storm, thromboembolic complications, acute organ dysfunction, and multiorgan failure.AimsThe aim was to assess the relationship between markers of NETosis [citrullinated histones (Cit-H3), myeloperoxidase (MPO), and elastase] and COVID-19, as well as its severity.Patients and methodsNinety-nine hospitalized adult patients aged 18 years or older with COVID-19 and 30 controls were prospectively enrolled. Serum levels of Cit-H3, MPO, and elastase were measured at the time of admission, and their levels were compared between the clinical categories of COVID-19 (non-severe, severe, and critical). The correlation between markers of NETosis and the PO2/FiO2 ratio was also determined.ResultsAmong the NETs markers, Cit-H3 and elastase levels significantly increased in the COVID-19 patients compared to the control. In the comparison of COVID-19 categories, only Cit-H3 significantly increased in severe categories compared to the non-severe group. Background

The COVID-19 is expected to become an endemic disease in future; however, this transition may take years. New COVID-19 variants will likely continue to emerge and impact the world in the coming years.1 In early 2025, COVID-19 subvariants LF.7 and NB.1.8 caused a surge in cases in many Asian cities like Singapore and Hong Kong.2 As of June 2025, a new variant named “XFG” was on the rise in the Western Pacific region, the Region of the Americas, and the European Region.3 Research is ongoing to fully understand the mechanisms behind COVID-19 infection and develop targeted therapies.

Neutrophils play an essential role as the first line of innate immune defense. At the infection sites, these cells are the first to migrate and perform various functions, such as NETosis. In the process of NETosis, neutrophils release neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs), which kill microorganisms through phagocytosis.4,5

Research has shown that certain viruses trigger the process of NETosis.6–9 The virus-induced NETosis can act as a double-edged sword: On the one hand, it facilitates the mechanical entrapment of the virus, while, on the other hand, it causes harm to the host through the release of NETs triggered by inflammatory and immunological reactions. These virus-induced NETs formation can be uncontrolled, which may lead to a systemic response manifested by the production of cytokines, chemokines, and immune complexes, favoring inflammation.10 Over the past decade, numerous researchers have demonstrated a pathogenic role of neutrophil-derived NETs in various thrombo-inflammatory conditions, including sepsis, thrombosis, and respiratory failure.10–16

Advanced forms of COVID-19 are often characterized by hyperinflammation (“cytokine storm”) with the development of an ARDS-like condition.17 Furthermore, reports of micro- and macro-thrombotic phenomena such as microangiopathy and pulmonary embolism are increasingly frequent. Some of the serious complications of COVID-19, such as ARDS, cytokine storm, thromboembolic complications, acute organ dysfunction, and multiorgan failure, may be the result of virus-induced NETosis.6,18,19 There is a paucity of literature that explains the detailed immunopathogenic mechanisms of COVID-19 infection. Previous studies have shown that NETosis and NET release are elevated in COVID-19 patients by circulatory or infiltrating neutrophils.20,21 Furthermore, NET production predicts disease severity and clinical outcomes in COVID-19.20–23 Recently, NETosis has also been linked to the underlying pathophysiology of long COVID, as the levels of NETosis induction have been found to be significantly higher in patients with long COVID than in recovered patients.24

Our study aimed to evaluate the role of NETosis in the severity of COVID-19. Our objective was to assess the relationship between markers of NETosis [Cit-H3, myeloperoxidase (MPO), and elastase] and COVID-19, as well as its severity. We also determined the correlation between markers of NETosis and the PO2/FiO2 ratio.

Patients and Methods

This single-centered prospective cohort study was conducted between 2020 and 2021 in a tertiary care hospital after obtaining Institutional Ethics Committee approval (RC/AIIMS/Pat/2020/194/Project code: I-15/544) and written informed consent from the patients. The trial was registered with the Clinical Trial Registry of India (CTRI/2020/10/028488). The procedures used in this study adhere to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki, and the manuscript is prepared in accordance with the applicable STROBE guidelines.

Hospitalized adult patients aged 18 years or older with RT-PCR positive for COVID-19 were prospectively enrolled. Exclusion criteria included refusal to participate, known autoimmune disease or carcinoma, pregnancy, immunosuppression, a history of other infections or systemic illnesses contributing to organ dysfunction at the time of admission.

The study sample size was calculated to be 27 in each of the case groups to achieve a power of 80% and a level of significance of 5% (one-sided), for detecting a moderate effect size (0.5) between pairs. Considering dropouts of 20%, we took 33 patients in each of the study groups. We also selected 30 non-hospitalized controls without a history of COVID-19 infection and of similar age and chronic pathologies as the patients. The controls were outpatient department patients with no or mild systemic diseases, without a history of COVID-19 infection, a recent episode of fever, or a respiratory tract infection. All control participants were confirmed COVID-19 negative by RT-PCR testing performed at enrollment. A single blood sample for NETosis and inflammatory marker analysis was taken from the controls. All met the same exclusion criteria as the patient group. Matching was done randomly. The subsequent health status was monitored via follow-up telephone calls, and none of the participants developed severe illness during the 15-day observation period.

Ninety-nine COVID-19 patients were planned to be included in the study. COVID-19 cases were divided into three groups: Non-severe, severe, and critical, according to WHO clinical criteria as follows:

Critical COVID-19: Defined by the criteria for ARDS/sepsis/septic shock, or other conditions that would normally require the provision of life-sustaining therapies such as mechanical ventilation (invasive or noninvasive) or vasopressor therapy.

Patient's blood was drawn from the cubital veins using citrate tubes. Whole blood was separated for the investigations requiring plasma by centrifugation and stored at −80°C for further analysis. The plasma concentration of Cit-H3, MPO, elastase, IL-6, IL-1β, and IL-4 was measured via enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) Kits (Bioassay Technology Laboratory, Shanghai, China). All investigations were performed in the Department of Biochemistry at the same center.

Vital parameters (heart rate, blood pressure, respiratory rate, and SpO2), arterial blood gases (ABG), and presence and type of oxygen delivery devices and mechanical ventilation were recorded.

Enrolled patients were followed daily by the investigators for clinical deterioration or recovery.

Samples from the cases were taken on the day of admission to the hospital and on the days of clinical deterioration from the non-severe to the severe category.

Statistical Analysis

The normalcy of the data was checked using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Quantitative data were presented as mean (standard deviation) or median (interquartile range) and analyzed with the Mann–Whitney U-test, Kruskal–Wallis test, independent t-test, or analysis of variance test, as appropriate. Categorical data were expressed as frequency and percentage and compared with the Chi-square test or Fisher's exact test. Correlations between biomarkers and clinical variables were analyzed using Spearman's rho correlation. Receiver operating curve (ROC) analysis was performed to determine the predictive power of biomarkers for various outcomes. Pairwise comparisons of area under the curves (AUCs) were performed using the DeLong test. The Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression analysis was performed to overcome potential overfitting and to identify the most stable predictors of severity. Predictors with non-zero coefficients after penalization were considered independently contributory. Subsequently, a predictive model was developed employing multivariable logistic regression analysis, which incorporated the selected features from the LASSO regression model. A Wilcoxon signed-rank test or paired t-test was conducted for comparison within the group at two time intervals. A p-value < 0.05 was treated as statistically significant. Statistical analysis was performed using the SPSS (Statistical Package for the Social Sciences) software, version 26 and Python (version 3.11, scikit-learn library) for LASSO.

Results

A sample size of 99 cases was planned, with 33 patients in each group, according to the clinical criteria for COVID-19. A sample of one critical case was lost; thus, we analyzed 139 samples from 98 patients and 30 controls. Out of 98 patients, 33 were in the non-severe category, 33 were in the severe category, and 32 were in the critical category at the time of admission. The category of 11 patients out of 98 changed over time, and these patients had two samples at different time points. Non-severe patients were admitted to the ward, and severe and critical patients were admitted to the intensive care unit (ICU). There were no statistically significant differences in the patients and control demographics (age and sex) or comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes, hypothyroidism) (p > 0.05 for all) (Table 1).

Inflammatory markers like IL-6 and IL-1β were also compared between the three severity groups (non-severe, severe, and critical), and the control group. Discussion

Our study findings have shown that some of the markers of NETosis are increased in patients with severe and critical COVID-19. Among the markers of NETosis, Cit-H3 levels were found to be a fair predictor of the severity of COVID-19. Citrullinated histones have also been found to have a significant negative correlation with the PO2/FiO2 ratio.

Some previous studies have also found that NETs markers correlate with COVID-19 severity.25–28 Our findings expand upon prior work by finding the predictive ability of NETs markers with clinical severity and mortality, as well as their correlation with the PO2/FiO2 ratio. Among the NETs markers, Cit-H3 and elastase levels significantly increased in the COVID-19 patients compared to the control. In the comparison of COVID-19 categories, only Cit-H3 significantly increased in severe categories compared to the non-severe group. Other markers like IL-6, IL-1β, CRD, D-dimer, ferritin, LDH, and neutrophil % were also increased in severe category patients as compared to the control.

Cytokine storm and inflammation have been implicated as the principal etiologies for the COVID-19 ARDS.29 SARS-CoV-2 drives NETosis and NET formation, which release free DNA and the byproducts (elastase and histones). This may trigger the excessive production of proinflammatory cytokines and chemokines, which in turn enhance NET formation and create a positive feedback loop.30,31 In the present study, Cit-H3 was found to be most closely correlated with falling oxygenation as compared to other serum markers of NETosis. When the serum values of various NET markers were compared at different time intervals longitudinally in a patient who deteriorated over time from non-severe to severe, Cit-H3 was found to be significantly increased with time, showing its role in the cytokine storm. Citrullinated histones is a central player in the neutrophil release of NETs and is known to be elevated in a variety of other diseases like cancer and thrombosis.32

The diagnostic potential of the NETs markers was also studied. Among the NETs markers, only Cit-H3 showed fair diagnostic ability for predicting severity, with a sensitivity and specificity of approximately 74 and 64%, respectively, at a cut-off of 34 ng/mL. Pairwise comparison of the AUCs indicated Cit-H3 as a better predictive marker than MPO and comparable to elastase.

Other NETosis markers did not have a good predictive ability to detect severity or mortality. The traditional biomarkers like CRP, D-dimer, LDH, and IL-6 also showed fair predictive ability for COVID-19 severity.

In penalized regression (LASSO) validated multivariate regression analysis model, only Cit-H3 and CRP were found to be significantly affecting the severity of the disease when compared with non-severe patients. Values of Cit-H3 and elastase among NETosis markers were found to significantly affect the severity of COVID-19 when compared with non-COVID-19 controls. Citrullinated histone levels increased proportionally with disease severity, demonstrating a strong association between NETosis activity and severe COVID-19.

In the multivariate logistic regression model, most variables exhibited ORs close to 1, suggesting limited independent contribution to disease severity after adjustment. Although the adjusted OR for Cit-H3 (1.06 per ng/mL increase) may seem numerically modest, but a 15 ng/ml increase in Cit-H3 is associated with more than a two-fold increase in the odds of severe disease. Thus, while the magnitude of these ORs appears small, the large observed differences in biomarker levels between severity groups imply meaningful physiological effects.

The diagnostic results of Cit-H3 once again indicate that a single indicator can provide only a fair predictive accuracy, and thus cannot be used as a perfect predictive tool. It may need to be used in conjunction with other biomarkers to improve the accuracy and reliability of predictions. NETosis is the forerunner of cytokines, chemokines, and inflammatory mediator production. The cost of the Cit-H3 test is approximately 1.5 times higher than that of CRP and is not cost-effective for predicting severity. However, the independent association of Cit-H3 with severity indicates a plausible link between NETosis activity and disease progression, although causal inference cannot be established from this observational design.

Dysregulated NETosis has been reported to induce or aggravate numerous diseases, including sepsis, systemic lupus erythematosus, rheumatoid arthritis, small-vessel vasculitis, inflammatory bowel disease, cancer, and COVID-19.33 High NET formation has also been found in the histopathological study on autopsy cases, which exhibited increased neutrophil activation, pronounced endothelial damage and thrombotic complications.34 In some of the studies, other markers of NETosis, like MPO-DNA and cell-free DNA have also been found more closely associated with severity and mortality.35,36

NETosis is a complex process, and it is not essential that all the NETosis markers be increased for its confirmation. The presence of certain markers, in combination with other indicators, helps to confirm the process.11 In the present study, as Cit-H3 is more strongly related to the severity of COVID-19, a peptidyl arginine deiminase 4 (PAD4)-dependent NETosis may be the likely cause of the pathogenesis of COVID-19 severity.37 Peptidyl arginine deiminase 4-induced histone translation is a vital step in NETosis, and the presence of Cit-H3 identified by immunostaining endorses NETs formation in vitro and in vivo; therefore, Cit-H3 can be considered a marker of NETosis.38

The strengths of our study include the examination of the relationship between markers and the three phases of COVID-19 evolution. Paired comparisons between the admission baseline and the markers on the day of deterioration from the non-severe to the severe category, provided information about early markers of severity. We also studied the correlation of NETs markers with PO2/FiO2, which was not investigated in the previous studies.

Our study has some limitations. First, it was a single-centered study with a small cohort. Second, the association between the markers and viral load was not investigated. Third, the long-term follow-up of patients, including the level of markers after recovery, was not evaluated. Currently, there are limitations to accurately measuring NET markers, which make it difficult to assess their proper role in COVID-19 pathogenesis.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that Cit-H3, along with CRP is independently associated with COVID-19 severity. These findings support the relevance of NETosis-related biomarkers in disease progression and may inform future research on targeted monitoring strategies. Further studies are needed to validate these associations and explore their clinical utility.

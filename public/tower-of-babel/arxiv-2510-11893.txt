Non-spherical minimizers in the generalized liquid drop model for Yukawa and truncated Coulomb potentials
L. Bronsard, B. Merlet, M. Pegon

Abstract
. We investigate generalized liquid drop models with screened Riesz-type interactions, focusing in particular on truncated Coulomb and Yukawa potentials in three dimensions. While the classical Gamow model with Coulomb interaction is conjectured to admit only spherical minimizers below a critical mass and no minimizer above, we show that this conjecture fails if the interaction is screened. In the case of truncated Coulomb and Yukawa potentials, we establish the existence of non-spherical minimizers for some values of the screening parameter. This gives the first evidence of such minimizers in the class of repulsive, radial, and radially nonincreasing kernels in three dimensions. Our approach relies on a comparison of the energy-per-mass ratios of balls and cylinders, in contrast with recent two-dimensional results obtained via Γ \Gamma -convergence. We further show that in the unscreened Riesz case, the conjecture remains consistent, though delicate. Indeed we observe that the energy-per-mass ratios of balls and of cylinders are surprisingly close.

1.1.
Minimizers and generalized minimizers

First of all if

is compactly supported, in particular if

Proposition 1.1
.

Let

be a nonnegative integrable kernel with compact support in

(not necessarily

, Problem
Equation

1
admits a minimizer.
In addition, every minimizer has a finite number of components with finite perimeter

For many other kernels, such as

Definition 1.2

(Generalized minimizers)

(1)

We call

(2)

We extend the definition of

\mathcal{E}_{G}((E^{i}))\coloneqq\sum_{i}\mathcal{E}_{G}(E^{i}).

(3)

A generalized set

.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let

,

or

,
Problem

and each component

is a classical minimizer of

with mass

, that

Proof.

There are several works in the literature proving this result depending on the dimension and the kernels considered. The three-dimensional case with
G=R_{1}
can be found in
[
21
]
, and the case
G=R_{\alpha}
in any dimension has been treated in
[
1
]
(see Corollary 1.1 therein). When
G=R_{\alpha,\kappa}
, we have seen that by
[
30
]
, the problem always admits classical minimizers.

The existence of generalized minimizers in the case
G=Y_{\kappa,\alpha}
cannot be found as
it is in the literature but can be proved with the same methods. Fortunately the construction of generalized minimizers has been considered in a very general setting in
[
4
]
, for functionals involving the competition between a perimeter-type term and a nonlocal
repulsive term. One can easily check that for the kernels
R_{\alpha}
,
R_{\alpha,\kappa}
and
Y_{\alpha,\kappa}
, the
functional
\mathcal{E}^{n}_{G}
satisfies the sets of assumptions denoted by (S1) and (S2) therein, with the choice

V(E,U)\coloneqq\int_{(E\times E)\cap(U\times U)}G(x-y)\,dx\,dy,

and with
f_{1}(m)=C(n)m^{1-1/n}
by the standard relative isoperimetric inequality (see e.g.

[
9
, Theorem 5.11]
).
Thus, by
[
4
, Proposition 1.1 & Theorem 1.2]
we get existence of generalized minimizers,
which may for now be an infinite countable collection of sets.

Examining the proof of
[
4
, Proposition 1.7]
(see Step 2, p. 14) it transpires
that, in assumption (H15) the condition

V(tE)\leq t^{\beta}V(E)\quad\text{ for any $t$ such that }|t-1|\leq t_{0}

can be replaced by

|V(tE)-V(E)|\leq C|t-1||E|\quad\text{ for any $t$ such that }|t-1|\leq t_{0},

for some constant
C=C(G)
. This latter condition holds true for the kernels we consider and with this modification, the
functionals we consider satisfy the set of assumptions (S4). We conclude that by
[
4
, Proposition 1.7]
, every
component of a generalized minimizer is a
\rho
-minimizer of the perimeter, with
\rho(r)=C(n)r^{n}
.

It is then classical to obtain lower density estimates: the classical perimeter obviously satisfies
the set of assumption (S3) so that
[
4
, Theorem 1.4]
applies (one could also cite

[
31
]
or
[

). As a consequence, every component of a generalized
minimizer of volume

is bounded and carries at least a volume
\delta m=C(m,G)>0
so that there can only be a finite
number of components with positive measure.

The fact that every component of a generalized minimizer is a classical minimizer in
the class of measurable sets with same mass is obvious: otherwise, replacing a component by a set
with same mass and less energy would contradict the minimality of the generalized minimizer.

Finally, let us assume by contradiction that a component
E^{j}
of a generalized minimizer
(E^{i})
is not connected in the measure theoretic sense. We can then write

E^{j}=E^{j,1}\cup E^{j,2},

with

|E^{j,1}|,||E^{j,2}|>0,\qquad|E^{j,1}\cap E^{j,2}|=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad P(E^{j})=P(E^{j,1})+P(E^{j,2}).

If

or

, we have

so that

\mathcal{I}^{n}_{G}(E^{j})=\mathcal{I}^{n}_{G}(E^{j,1})+\mathcal{I}^{n}_{G}(E^{j,2})+2\int_{E^{j,1}\times E^{j,2}}G(x-y)\,dx\,dy>\mathcal{I}^{n}_{G}(E^{j,1})+\mathcal{I}^{n}_{G}(E^{j,2}).

We then have

\mathcal{E}^{n}_{G}(E^{j})<\mathcal{E}^{n}_{G}(E^{j,1})+\mathcal{E}^{n}_{G}(E^{j,2}),

and one can decrease the energy of
(E^{i})
by replacing the component
E^{j}
by the pair
(E^{j,1},E^{j,2})
. This contradicts the definition of a generalized minimizer. We conclude that for
G=R_{\alpha}
or

the components of the generalized minimizers are connected in the measure theoretic sense.
∎

Definition 1.5
.

Let
G:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a non negative and measurable radial kernel. We define the optimal energy/mass ratio of balls by

(5)

\rho_{G}^{\ball}=\rho_{G}^{n,\ball}\coloneqq\inf\left\{\frac{\mathcal{E}_{G}(B_{R})}{|B_{R}|}\penalty\ :\penalty\ B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\right\}.

Recall that
C_{l,L}=B_{l}^{n-1}\times(0,L)
. We define for
l>0

as

(6)

\sigma_{G}^{n,\cyl}(l)=\sigma_{G}^{\cyl}(l)\coloneqq\lim_{L\uparrow\infty}\dfrac{\mathcal{E}_{G}(C_{l,L})}{|C_{l,L}|}.

Then we define the optimal energy/mass ratio of infinite cylinders as

(7)

\rho_{G}^{\cyl}=\rho_{G}^{n,\cyl}\coloneqq\inf_{l>0}\sigma_{G}^{n,\cyl}(l).

Proposition 1.6
.

If
E=(B_{r_{i}})_{i\in I}
is a generalized set of mass
m
made of a collection of balls, then

\frac{\mathcal{E}_{G}(E)}{m}\geq\rho_{G}^{\ball}.

Proof.

The proof is elementary. By definition of
\rho_{G}^{\ball}
and of the energy of generalized
minimizers, we have

\mathcal{E}_{G}(E)=\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\mathcal{E}_{G}(B_{r_{i}})}{|B_{r_{i}}|}|B_{r_{i}}|\geq\rho_{G}^{\ball}\sum_{i\in I}|B_{r_{i}}|=m\rho_{G}^{\ball}.

∎

Remark 1.7
.

Notice that in fact

(8)

\lim_{m\to\infty}\inf_{E}\,\frac{\mathcal{E}_{G}(E)}{m}=\rho_{G}^{\ball},

where the infimum is taken over generalized sets
E
of mass
m
and made exclusively of balls.
Indeed, let
\varepsilon>0
and let
B_{r}
such that
\mathcal{E}_{G}(B_{r})/|B_{r}|\leq\rho_{G}^{\ball}+\varepsilon
. For
m>0
, we consider the generalized set
E
of mass
m
made of

balls of radius
r
and one ball of radius

so that
|B_{s}|=m-N(m)|B_{r}|
. We have

\dfrac{\mathcal{E}_{G}(E)}{m}=\dfrac{N(m)\mathcal{E}_{G}(B_{r})+\mathcal{E}_{G}(B_{s})}{m}\leq\dfrac{N(m)+1}{m}\mathcal{\mathcal{E}}_{G}(B_{r})=\dfrac{N(m)+1}{m/|B_{r}|}(\rho_{G}^{\ball}+\varepsilon).

Sending
m
to
\infty
and then
\varepsilon
to

we obtain

\lim_{m\to\infty}\inf_{E}\,\frac{\mathcal{E}_{G}(E)}{m}\leq\rho_{G}^{\ball}.

As the converse inequality follows from
1.6
, we conclude that
Equation

8
holds true.

Let us now consider the energy of cylinders.

Lemma 1.8
.

There holds for
l>0
,

(9)

\sigma_{G}^{\cyl}(l)=\dfrac{n-1}{l}+\dfrac{1}{|B_{l}^{n-1}|}\int_{B_{l}^{n-1}\times B_{l}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},t)\,\,dt\,dx^{\prime}\,dy^{\prime}.

Recall the definition
Equation

6
of
\sigma_{G}^{\cyl}(l)
. First, we have
P(C_{l,L})=Ll^{n-2}|\mathbb{S}^{n-2}|+2|B_{l}^{n-1}|

|C_{l,L}|
and using the identities
|C_{l,L}|=|B_{l}^{n-1}|\,L

(10)

\dfrac{P(C_{l,L})}{|C_{l,L}|}=\left(\dfrac{n-1}{l}+\frac{2}{L}\right)\ \longrightarrow\ \dfrac{n-1}{l}\quad\text{as }L\uparrow\infty.

Then we compute

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mathcal{I}_{G}(C_{l,L})}{|C_{l,L}|}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{|B_{l}|^{n-1}L}\int_{(0,L)^{2}}\int_{[B_{l}^{n-1}]^{2}}G(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},r-s)\,dx^{\prime}\,dy^{\prime}\,dr\,ds

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{|B_{l}|^{n-1}}\dfrac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{L}\left[\int_{[B_{l}^{n-1}]^{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{L}G(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},r-s)\,dr\right)\,dx^{\prime}\,dy^{\prime}\right]\,ds.

We perform the change of variable

in the outer integral to get

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mathcal{I}_{G}(C_{l,L})}{|C_{l,L}|}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{|B_{l}^{n-1}|}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\int_{[B_{l}^{n-1}]^{2}}\left(\int_{-\xi L}^{(1-\xi)L}G(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},t)\,dt\right)\,dx^{\prime}\,dy^{\prime}\right]\,d\xi.

Since

, we get by the monotone convergence theorem,

\lim_{L\uparrow\infty}\dfrac{\mathcal{I}_{G}(C_{l,L})}{|C_{l,L}|}=\dfrac{1}{|B_{l}|^{n-1}}\int_{[B_{l}^{n-1}]^{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},r)\,dt\right)\,dx^{\prime}\,dy^{\prime}.

Together with

10
, this proves
Equation

9
.
∎

Remark 1.9

Note that the quantity

In particular,

.

1.2.
A slicing formula

We present useful formulas which express the
interaction energy
\mathcal{I}_{G}(E)
through the interaction energies of the one-dimensional slices of
E
.
Similar formulas appear in the literature (see
[
26
,
5
]
or in
[
24
]
for
anisotropic perimeters). As they depend on
n
we put back the superscripts
n
.

Definition 1.11
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
we set:

E_{\sigma,y}=\big\{s\in\mathbb{R}\penalty\ :\penalty\ y+s\sigma\in E\}.

Proposition 1.12

(Interaction energy using 1D-slices)
.

Writing
G(|x|)\coloneqq G(x)
, we have for every measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(12)

\mathcal{I}_{G}^{n}(E)=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\sigma^{\perp}}\int_{E_{\sigma,y^{\prime}}\times E_{\sigma,y^{\prime}}}|s-t|^{n-1}G(s-t)\,ds\,dt\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y^{\prime})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma).

Moreover, when
E
is a bounded convex set, there holds

(13)

\mathcal{I}_{G}^{n}(E)=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\sigma^{\perp}}S_{G}^{n}(\mathcal{H}^{1}(E_{\sigma,y^{\prime}}))\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y^{\prime})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma),

where we have set

(14)

S_{G}^{n}(L)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}|s-t|^{n-1}G(s-t)\,ds\,dt.

Proof.

The first identity follows from simple changes of variables and Fubini. One can
reproduce almost verbatim the proof of
[
26
, Proposition 3.1]
(albeit replacing occurrences of
E\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus E)
by
E\times E
). Here are the details. We perform the change of variable
y=x+r\sigma
with
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
r>0
and use Fubini to get

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{G}^{n}(E)

\displaystyle=\int_{E\times E}G(y-x)\,dy\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{(0,+\infty)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\mathbf{1}}_{E}(x){\mathbf{1}}_{E}(x+r\sigma)r^{n-1}G(r)\,dx\,dr\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma).

Using the change of variable
r=-\tilde{r}
,
\sigma=-\tilde{\sigma}
, we also have

\mathcal{I}_{G}^{n}(E)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{(-\infty,0)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\mathbf{1}}_{E}(x){\mathbf{1}}_{E}(x+r\sigma)|r|^{n-1}G(|r|)\,dx\,dr\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma).

Averaging the last two identities we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{G}^{n}(E)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\mathbf{1}}_{E}(x){\mathbf{1}}_{E}(x+r\sigma)|r|^{n-1}G(|r|)\,dx\right]\,dr\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma).

Then, we perform the change of variable in the inner integral,
x=y^{\prime}+s\sigma
for
y^{\prime}\in\sigma^{\perp}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. By Fubini we have

\mathcal{I}_{G}^{n}(E)\\
=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\sigma^{\perp}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}{\mathbf{1}}_{E}(y^{\prime}+s\sigma){\mathbf{1}}_{E}(y^{\prime}+(r+s)\sigma)|r|^{n-1}G(|r|)\,ds\,dr\right]\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y^{\prime})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma).

Using the change of variable
r=t-s
, the term into square brackets rewrites as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}{\mathbf{1}}_{E}(y^{\prime}+s\sigma)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{1}}_{E}(y^{\prime}+(r+s)\sigma)|r|^{n-1}G(|r|)\,ds\,dr

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}{\mathbf{1}}_{E}(y^{\prime}+s\sigma){\mathbf{1}}_{E}(y^{\prime}+t\sigma)|s-t|^{n-1}G(|s-t|)\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{\sigma,y^{\prime}}\times E_{\sigma,y^{\prime}}}|s-t|^{n-1}G(|s-t|)\,ds\,dt.

This yields
Equation

12
.

Since the intersection of a bounded convex set with any line is a (possibly empty) segment,
Equation

13
is a direct consequence of
Equation

12
.
∎

As an immediate application, using the symmetries of the ball, we obtain a simpler expression of the energy of a ball in terms of the function
S_{G}
.

Corollary 1.13
.

For every
R>0
, there holds

\mathcal{I}_{G}^{n}(B_{R})=\dfrac{1}{2}|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}||\mathbb{S}^{n-2}|\int_{0}^{R}S_{G}^{n}\big(2\sqrt{R^{2}-r^{2}}\big)r^{n-2}\,dr.

Proof.

For
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,
y^{\prime}\in\sigma^{\perp}
, we have for
E=B_{R}
,

E_{\sigma,y^{\prime}}=\begin{cases}\left[-\sqrt{R^{2}-|y^{\prime}|^{2}},\sqrt{R^{2}-|y^{\prime}|^{2}}\right]&\text{if }|y^{\prime}|<R,\\
\qquad\varnothing&\text{if }|y^{\prime}|\geq R.\end{cases}

It follows that

\mathcal{H}^{1}(E_{\sigma,y^{\prime}})=2\sqrt{(R^{2}-|y^{\prime}|^{2})_{+}},

with the notation
a_{+}\coloneq\max(a,0)
. We deduce form
Equation

13
,

\mathcal{I}_{G}^{n}(B_{R})=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\sigma^{\perp}}S_{G}^{n}\left(2\sqrt{(R^{2}-|y^{\prime}|^{2})_{+}}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y^{\prime})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma).

The inner integral does not depend on
\sigma
and we can write

\mathcal{I}_{G}^{n}(B_{R})=\dfrac{|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}S_{G}^{n}\left(2\sqrt{(R^{2}-|y^{\prime}|^{2})_{+}}\right)\,dy^{\prime}.

Finally using polar coordinates we write
y^{\prime}=r\tau
with
r>0
,
\tau\in\mathbb{S}^{n-2}
and we get the desired identity.
∎

2.
Riesz potentials

In this section, we treat the case
G(x)=R_{\alpha}(x)=|x|^{-\alpha}
for
\alpha\in(0,n)
and
n\geq 2
.
We use the notation

(15)

\beta=\beta(\alpha,n)\coloneqq n+1-\alpha\ \in(1,n+1).

2.1.
Energy/mass ratio of balls

We first compute the function
S_{G}^{n}
defined in

Equation

14
in
1.12
in the case of the Riesz potentials.

Lemma 2.1
.

For every
\alpha\in(0,n)
and every
L>0
, we have

S_{R_{\alpha}}^{n}(L)=\frac{2}{\beta(\beta-1)}L^{\beta}.

Thus for every convex set
E
, we have

\mathcal{I}^{n}_{R_{\alpha}}(E)=\dfrac{1}{\beta(\beta-1)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\sigma^{\perp}}\mathcal{H}^{1}(E_{\sigma,y})^{\beta}\,dy\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma).

Proof.

We compute:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}|s-t|^{n-1-\alpha}\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L}\left(\int_{-t}^{L-t}|r|^{n-1-\alpha}\,dr\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L}\left[\int_{0}^{t}r^{n-1-\alpha}\,dr+\int_{0}^{L-t}r^{n-1-\alpha}\,dr\right]\,dt

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{n-\alpha}\int_{0}^{L}\left[t^{n-\alpha}+(L-t)^{n-\alpha}\right]\,dt

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2L^{n+1-\alpha}}{(n-\alpha)(n+1-\alpha)}.

This proves the formula for
S_{R_{\alpha}}^{n}(L)
. The formula for
\mathcal{I}_{R_{\alpha}}(E)
is then an application of
1.12
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Notice that the quantity

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\sigma^{\perp}}\mathcal{H}^{1}(E_{\sigma,y})^{\beta}\,dy\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma)

makes sense even for
n\leq\alpha<n+3
(that is,
1\geq\beta>-2
). For
\beta=1
we have by Fubini,

\dfrac{1}{|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\sigma^{\perp}}\mathcal{H}^{1}(E_{\sigma,y})\,dy\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma)=|E|.

For
\beta=0
, by Crofton’s formula (see
[
11
, Theorem 3.2.26]
or

[
27
, §3.16]
),

\dfrac{1}{|B^{n-1}|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{\sigma^{\perp}}\mathcal{H}^{1}(E_{\sigma,y})^{0}\,dy\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma)=P(E),

(here we adopt the convention
\mathcal{H}^{1}(E_{\sigma,y})^{0}=0
if
E_{\sigma,y}=\varnothing
).

We deduce an expression for the Riesz energy of balls.

Lemma 2.3
.

For every
\alpha\in(0,n)
and every
R>0
, we have

\mathcal{I}^{n}_{R_{\alpha}}(B_{R})=\frac{2^{\beta}\pi^{n-\tfrac{1}{2}}}{\beta-1}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{n+\beta+1}{2}\right)}R^{n+\beta-1}.

Proof.

First of all notice that by a scaling we have

\mathcal{I}^{n}_{R_{\alpha}}(B_{R})=R^{2n-\alpha}\mathcal{I}^{n}_{R_{\alpha}}(B_{1})=R^{n+\beta-1}\mathcal{I}^{n}_{R_{\alpha}}(B_{1}).

Next by
1.13
we have

(16)

\mathcal{I}^{n}_{R_{\alpha}}(B_{1})=\dfrac{1}{2}|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}||\mathbb{S}^{n-2}|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{2(2\sqrt{R^{2}-r^{2}})^{\beta}}{\beta(\beta-1)}r^{n-2}\,dr.

Now, recall that the Beta-function (or Euler integral of the first kind) is defined as

B(x,y)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{x-1}(1-t)^{y-1}\,dt,\qquad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{C}\text{ s.t. }\mathrm{Re}(x),\penalty\ \mathrm{Re}(y)>0,

and satisfies

B(x,y)=\frac{\Gamma(x)\Gamma(y)}{\Gamma(x+y)}.

Thus, making the change of variable
t=r^{2}
, we find

\int_{0}^{1}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}r^{n-2}\,dr=\dfrac{1}{2}B\left(1+\frac{\beta}{2},\frac{n-1}{2}\right)=\dfrac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{\beta}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+\beta+1}{2}\right)}.

Putting this in
Equation

16
, we compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{n}_{R_{\alpha}}(B_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\beta-1}n(n-1)|B_{1}^{n}||B_{1}^{n-1}|}{\beta(\beta-1)}\frac{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{\beta}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+\beta+1}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\beta-1}}{\beta(\beta-1)}\,\frac{\pi^{n-\tfrac{1}{2}}n(n-1)}{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{n-1}{2}\right)}\,\frac{\Gamma\left(1+\frac{\beta}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+\beta+1}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\beta}\pi^{n-\tfrac{1}{2}}}{\beta-1}\,\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{n+\beta+1}{2}\right)}\,,

where we used the fact that
\Gamma(1+z)=z\Gamma(z)
.
∎

Lastly, we give an expression of the optimal energy/mass ratio of balls in terms of the
Riesz energy of the unit ball.

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
\alpha\in(0,n)
we have

\rho_{R_{\alpha}}^{\ball}=\frac{n\beta}{\beta-1}\left(\frac{(\beta-1)\mathcal{I}_{R_{\alpha}}(B_{1})}{n|B_{1}|}\right)^{1/\beta},

where
\rho_{R_{\alpha}}^{\ball}
is defined by
Equation

5
with
G=R_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

We have

\frac{\mathcal{E}_{R_{\alpha}}(B_{R})}{|B_{R}|}=\frac{n}{R}+\frac{R^{n-1+\beta}}{|B_{1}|R^{n}}\mathcal{I}_{R_{\alpha}}(B_{1})=n\left(\dfrac{1}{R}+R^{\beta-1}\frac{\mathcal{I}_{R_{\alpha}}(B_{1})}{n|B_{1}|}\right).

Since
\beta>1
, the infimum over
R
is reached at the critical point

R_{*}=\left(\frac{n|B_{1}|}{(\beta-1)\mathcal{I}^{n}_{R_{\alpha}}(B_{1})}\right)^{1/\beta}

and the value of the infimum is

\rho_{R_{\alpha}}^{n,\ball}=\left(\frac{n\beta}{\beta-1}\right)\dfrac{1}{R_{*}},

which gives the result.
∎

2.2.
Energy/mass ratio of infinite cylinders

For a nonnegative measurable kernel
G
, the kernel
G^{\cyl}
is defined as in
1.14
by

G^{\cyl}(x^{\prime})\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(x^{\prime},t)\,dt\qquad\text{ for a.e. }x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}.

Elementary computations lead to the following lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

For every
\alpha\in(1,n)
, we have

R_{\alpha}^{\cyl}(x^{\prime})=c_{\alpha}R_{\alpha-1}(x^{\prime})\qquad\text{ with }\qquad c_{\alpha}\coloneqq\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)}.

Then the energy/mass ratio of infinite cylinders of radius
l
defined by
Equation

6
is

\sigma_{R_{\alpha}}^{n,\cyl}(l)=\frac{n-1}{l}+\frac{c_{\alpha}l^{\beta-1}}{|B_{1}^{n-1}|}\mathcal{I}_{R_{\alpha-1}}^{n-1}(B_{1}^{n-1})

and

\rho_{R_{\alpha}}^{n,\cyl}=\frac{(n-1)\beta}{\beta-1}\left(\frac{c_{\alpha}(\beta-1)\mathcal{I}_{R_{\alpha-1}}^{n-1}(B_{1}^{n-1})}{(n-1)|B_{1}^{n-1}|}\right)^{1/\beta}.

Proof.

By a change of variable, for
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\setminus\{0\}
, we have

R_{\alpha}^{\cyl}(x^{\prime})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{dt}{\left(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+t^{2}\right)^{\alpha/2}}\,=\dfrac{c_{\alpha}}{|x^{\prime}|^{\alpha-1}},

where we denote

c_{\alpha}\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{dt}{\left(1+t^{2}\right)^{\alpha/2}}\,.

Thus, by
1.13
, we have

\sigma_{R_{\alpha}}^{n,\cyl}(l)=\frac{n-1}{l}+\frac{c_{\alpha}}{|B_{l}^{n-1}|}\mathcal{I}^{n-1}_{R_{\alpha}^{\cyl}}(B_{l}^{n-1})=\frac{n-1}{l}+\frac{c_{\alpha}l^{\beta-1}}{|B^{n-1}|}\mathcal{I}_{R_{\alpha-1}}^{n-1}(B_{1}^{n-1}).

Since
\beta>1
, the infimum over
l
is reached at the critical point

l_{*}\coloneqq\left(\frac{(n-1)|B_{1}^{n-1}|}{c_{\alpha}(\beta-1)\mathcal{I}_{R_{\alpha-1}}^{n-1}(B_{1}^{n-1})}\right)^{1/\beta}.

The minimum is then

\rho_{R_{\alpha}}^{n,\cyl}=\left(\frac{(n-1)\beta}{\beta-1}\right)\dfrac{1}{l_{*}}.

We still have to express
c_{\alpha}
in terms of the
\Gamma
function.
Using the changes of variable
s=t^{2}
and then
u=\dfrac{1}{1+s}
, we compute

c_{\alpha}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\dfrac{ds}{\sqrt{s}(1+s)^{\alpha/2}}\,=\int_{0}^{1}u^{\frac{\alpha-3}{2}}(1-u)^{-\tfrac{1}{2}}\,du=B\left(\dfrac{1}{2},\dfrac{\alpha-1}{2}\right)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left(\tfrac{\alpha-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\right)},

where
B(x,y)
is the Beta function, and
where we used the identities
B(x,y)=\Gamma(x)\Gamma(y)/\Gamma(x+y)
and
\Gamma(1/2)=\sqrt{\pi}
. This ends the proof of the lemma.
∎

2.3.
Energy/mass ratio of balls versus infinite cylinders

Proposition 2.6
.

For every
n\geq 2
and
\alpha\in(1,n)
we have

(17)

\frac{\rho_{R_{\alpha}}^{\cyl}}{\rho_{R_{\alpha}}^{\ball}}=\frac{n-1}{n}\left(\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-\beta}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-\beta+1}{2}\right)}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+\beta+1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+\beta}{2}\right)}\right)^{1/\beta}.

If in addition
\alpha
is an integer then we have

\frac{\rho_{R_{\alpha}}^{\cyl}}{\rho_{R_{\alpha}}^{\ball}}>1.

Remark 2.7
.

In view of some numerical computations using formula
Equation

18
below, we believe that
\rho_{R_{\alpha}}^{\cyl}>\rho_{R_{\alpha}}^{\ball}

whenever
\alpha\in(1,n)
, even if
\alpha
is not an integer. This remains however to be established.

Proof of
2.6
.

Recall that the
\beta
as defined in
Equation

15
associated with the kernel

R_{\alpha-1}^{n-1}
is the same for the kernel
R_{\alpha}^{n}
.
Using
2.4
and
2.5
we compute

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle\coloneqq{\rho_{R_{\alpha}}^{\cyl}}/{\rho_{R_{\alpha}}^{\ball}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{(n-1)\beta}{\beta-1}\left[\frac{c_{\alpha}(\beta-1)\mathcal{I}_{R_{\alpha-1}}^{n-1}(B_{1}^{n-1})}{(n-1)|B_{1}^{n-1}|}\right]^{1/\beta}\right)\left(\frac{\beta-1}{n\beta}\left[\frac{n|B_{1}^{n}|}{(\beta-1)\mathcal{I}^{n}_{R_{\alpha}}(B_{1})}\right]^{1/\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n-1}{n}\left[c_{\alpha}\left(\frac{n|B_{1}^{n}|}{(n-1)|B_{1}^{n-1}|}\right)\left(\frac{\mathcal{I}_{R_{\alpha-1}}^{n-1}(B_{1}^{n-1})}{\mathcal{I}^{n}_{R_{\alpha}}(B_{1})}\right)\right]^{1/\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{n-1}{n}\left[c_{\alpha}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}\right)\left(\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{n+\beta+1}{2}\right)}{\pi\Gamma\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{n+\beta}{2}\right)}\right)\right]^{1/\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{n-1}{n}\left(\frac{c_{\alpha}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+\beta+1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+\beta}{2}\right)}\right)^{1/\beta}.

Recalling
\beta=n+1-\alpha
and the expression of
c_{\alpha}
given in
2.5
, this gives
Equation

17
.

Now, we assume that
\alpha
(and thus
\beta
) is an integer and we set
p\coloneqq\beta-1\in(0,n-1)
. We get from
Equation

17

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+\beta}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-\beta}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+1+p}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-(1+p)}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(n-1+p)(n-1+p-2)(n-1+p-4)\dotsm(n-1-p)}{2^{p+1}}

and similarly

\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+\beta+1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-\beta+1}{2}\right)}=\dfrac{(n+p)(n+p-2)(n+p-4)\dotsm(n-p)}{2^{p+1}}.

We obtain

(18)

\tau^{\beta}=\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)^{p+1}\frac{(n+p)(n+p-2)\dotsm(n-p)}{(n-1+p)(n-1+p-2)\dotsm(n-1-p)}.

If
p
is odd, we can regroup the terms by pairs to get

\tau^{\beta}=\prod_{k=0}^{(p-1)/2}c_{n,2k+1},

with

c_{n,j}\coloneqq\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)^{2}\frac{(n+j)}{n-1+j}\,\frac{(n-j)}{n-1-j}=\frac{1-(j/n)^{2}}{1-(j/(n-1))^{2}}.

If
p
is even, after a similar pairing, we are left with the harmless extra factor:

\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)\left(\frac{n}{n-1}\right)=1.

We find

\tau^{\beta}=\prod_{k=1}^{p/2}c_{n,2k}.

Since
c_{n,j}>1
for
j\neq 0
, we deduce, in both cases, that
\tau^{\beta}>1
.
∎

3.1.
Energy/mass ratio of balls

First we compute the interaction energy on 1D slices as defined in

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
\alpha\in(0,n)
,
\kappa>0
and every
L>0
, we have

S_{R_{\alpha,\kappa}}(L)=\frac{2}{\beta(\beta-1)}\begin{cases}\displaystyle\qquad L^{\beta}&\quad\text{ if }L\leq\kappa\\
\displaystyle\kappa^{\beta-1}(\beta L-(\beta-1)\kappa)&\quad\text{ for }L>\kappa.\end{cases}

Proof.

For
L\leq\kappa
we have
|s-t|<\kappa
on
(0,L)\times(0,L)
so that the truncation has no effect and
S_{R_{\alpha,\kappa}}(L)=S_{R_{\alpha}}(L)
.
Assuming
L<\kappa
, we compute

\displaystyle S_{R_{\alpha,\kappa}}(L)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}|s-t|^{n-1-\alpha}{\mathbf{1}}_{|s-t|<\kappa}\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L}\left(\int_{-t}^{L-t}|r|^{n-1-\alpha}{\mathbf{1}}_{|r|<\kappa}\,dr\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{L}\left[\int_{0}^{t}r^{n-1-\alpha}{\mathbf{1}}_{r<\kappa}\,dr\right]\,dt

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2}{n-\alpha}\int_{0}^{L}\min(\kappa,t)^{n-\alpha}\,dt

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2}{n-\alpha}\left(\int_{0}^{\kappa}t^{n-\alpha}\,dt+(L-\kappa)\kappa^{n-\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2}{n-\alpha}\left(\dfrac{\kappa^{n-\alpha+1}}{n-\alpha+1}+(L-\kappa)\kappa^{n-\alpha}\right).

We conclude by inserting the identity
\alpha=n+1-\beta
and simplifying.
∎

Next, we deduce an expression of the truncated Riesz energy of balls in terms of the incomplete Beta
function.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,n)
,
\kappa>0

for the incomplete Beta function and setting

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{I}_{{R}_{\alpha,\kappa}}(B_{R})}{|B_{R}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{n(n-1)|B_{1}^{n-1}|\kappa^{\beta-1}}{\beta(\beta-1)}\left[\dfrac{1}{\lambda^{\beta-1}}B\left(\lambda^{2};\frac{\beta+2}{2},\frac{n-1}{2}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{2(\beta-1)\lambda}{n-1}(1-\lambda^{2})^{\frac{n-1}{2}}+\beta B\left(1-\lambda^{2};\frac{n-1}{2},\frac{3}{2}\right)\right],

if
\kappa\leq 2R
, and
\mathcal{I}_{R_{\alpha,\kappa}}(B_{R})=\mathcal{I}_{R_{\alpha}}(B_{R})
if
\kappa>2R
.

Proof.

In view of the expression of
S_{R_{\alpha,\kappa}}
in
3.1
, it is obvious that

\mathcal{I}_{{R}_{\alpha,\kappa}}(B_{R})=\mathcal{I}_{R_{\alpha}}(B_{R})
if
\kappa>2R
.
If
\kappa\leq 2R
, by
3.1
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{{R}_{\alpha,\kappa}}(B_{R})

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}||\mathbb{S}^{n-2}|\int_{0}^{R}S_{\alpha,\kappa}^{n}\big(2\sqrt{R^{2}-r^{2}}\big)r^{n-2}\,dr

\displaystyle=\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}||\mathbb{S}^{n-2}|}{\beta(\beta-1)}\left(2^{\beta}\int_{\sqrt{R^{2}-(\kappa/2)^{2}}}^{R}(R^{2}-r^{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}r^{n-2}\,dr\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\kappa^{\beta-1}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{R^{2}-(\kappa/2)^{2}}}\left(2\beta\sqrt{R^{2}-r^{2}}-(\beta-1)\kappa\right)r^{n-2}\,dr\right)

thus

\mathcal{I}_{{R}_{\alpha,\kappa}}(B_{R})=\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}||\mathbb{S}^{n-2}|}{\beta(\beta-1)}\left(2^{\beta}\int_{\sqrt{R^{2}-(\kappa/2)^{2}}}^{R}(R^{2}-r^{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}r^{n-2}\,dr\right.\\
\left.-\frac{(\beta-1)\kappa^{\beta}}{n-1}\left(R^{2}-(\kappa/2)^{2}\right)^{(n-1)/2}+2\beta\kappa^{\beta-1}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{R^{2}-(\kappa/2)^{2}}}\sqrt{R^{2}-r^{2}}\,r^{n-2}\,dr\right).

Using the changes of variables

followed by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\sqrt{R^{2}-(\kappa/2)^{2}}}\sqrt{R^{2}-r^{2}}r^{n-2}\,dr

\displaystyle=R^{n}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{1-(\kappa/(2R))^{2}}}\sqrt{1-s^{2}}s^{n-2}\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{R^{n}}{2}\int_{0}^{1-\left(\frac{\kappa}{2R}\right)^{2}}(1-t)^{\frac{1}{2}}t^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{R^{n}}{2}B\left(1-\left(\frac{\kappa}{2R}\right)^{2};\frac{n-1}{2},\frac{3}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\int_{\sqrt{R^{2}-(\kappa/2)^{2}}}^{R}(R^{2}-r^{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}r^{n-2}\,dr

\displaystyle=R^{n+\beta-1}\int_{\sqrt{1-(\kappa/(2R))^{2}}}^{1}(1-s^{2})^{\frac{\beta}{2}}s^{n-2}\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{R^{n+\beta-1}}{2}\int_{1-\left(\frac{\kappa}{2R}\right)^{2}}^{1}(1-t)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}t^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{R^{n+\beta-1}}{2}\int_{0}^{\left(\frac{\kappa}{2R}\right)^{2}}u^{\frac{\beta}{2}}(1-u)^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{R^{n+\beta-1}}{2}B\left(\left(\frac{\kappa}{2R}\right)^{2};\frac{\beta+2}{2},\frac{n-1}{2}\right).

Hence, setting

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{{R}_{\alpha,\kappa}}(B_{R})

\displaystyle=\frac{n(n-1)|B_{1}^{n}||B_{1}^{n-1}|}{\beta(\beta-1)}\cdot\left[2^{\beta-1}R^{n+\beta-1}B\left(\left(\frac{\kappa}{2R}\right)^{2};\frac{\beta+2}{2},\frac{n-1}{2}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{(\beta-1)\kappa^{\beta}R^{n-1}}{n-1}\left(1-\left(\frac{\kappa}{2R}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}+\beta\kappa^{\beta-1}R^{n}B\left(1-\left(\frac{\kappa}{2R}\right)^{2};\frac{n-1}{2},\frac{3}{2}\right)\right]

Proposition 3.3

3.2.

We compute the energy/mass ratio for the infinite cylinder of radius

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
n=3
,
\alpha=1
,
\kappa>0
and

. Setting
\lambda=\frac{\kappa}{2l}
and

g(\ell)=\frac{4\pi}{3}\left[\ell-\arcsin(\ell)+2\ell\left(1-\sqrt{1-\ell^{2}}\right)+\ell^{3}\argth\left(\sqrt{1-\ell^{2}}\right)\right],\quad\forall\ell<1,

\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R^{\cyl}_{1,\kappa}}(B_{l}^{2})=2\kappa^{2}l^{2}\lambda\int_{0}^{1}g\left(\lambda^{-1}\sqrt{1-r^{2}}\right)\,dr.

As a consequence, the energy/mass ratio of infinite cylinders of radius

is

(23)

\sigma_{R_{1,\kappa}}^{3,\cyl}(l)=\frac{4\lambda}{\kappa}+\frac{2\lambda\kappa^{2}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}g\left(\lambda^{-1}\sqrt{1-r^{2}}\right)\,dr.

Proof.

For
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\setminus\{0\}
, by the definition of
G^{\cyl}
given in

1.14
and a change of variable we have

R^{\cyl}_{\alpha,\kappa}(x^{\prime})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}R_{\alpha,\kappa}(x^{\prime},t)\,dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{{\mathbf{1}}_{(0,\kappa)}(\sqrt{|x^{\prime}|^{2}+t^{2}})}{(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+t^{2})^{\alpha/2}}\,dt=\frac{c_{\alpha,\kappa}(|x^{\prime}|)}{|x^{\prime}|^{\alpha-1}},

where

c_{\alpha,\kappa}(l)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{\sqrt{(\kappa/l)^{2}-1}}\dfrac{1}{(1+t^{2})^{\alpha/2}}\,dt&\qquad\text{ if
}\kappa>l,\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle\qquad 0&\qquad\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Since
\alpha=1
, we get

Let

By
1.13

\mathcal{I}^{n-1}_{R^{\cyl}_{1,\kappa}}(B_{R})=\dfrac{1}{2}|\mathbb{S}^{n-2}||\mathbb{S}^{n-3}|\int_{0}^{R}S^{n-1}_{R^{\cyl}_{1,\kappa}}\big(2\sqrt{R^{2}-r^{2}}\big)r^{n-3}\,dr,

where

\displaystyle S^{n-1}_{R^{\cyl}_{1,\kappa}}(L)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}|s-t|^{n-2}R^{\cyl}_{1,\kappa}(s-t)\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}|s-t|^{n-2}c_{1,\kappa}(|s-t|)\,ds\,dt.

Let us compute

for
n=3
.
Since

, we have

, thus we are only

.
In that case,

\displaystyle S^{2}_{R^{\cyl}_{1,\kappa}}(L)

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}|s-t|\argth\left(\sqrt{1-\left(|s-t|/\kappa\right)^{2}}\right)\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{L}\int_{-t}^{L-t}|u|\argth\left(\sqrt{1-\left(u/\kappa\right)^{2}}\right)\,du\,dt

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{t}u\argth\left(\sqrt{1-\left(u/\kappa\right)^{2}}\right)\,du\,dt

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L-t}|u|\argth\left(\sqrt{1-\left(u/\kappa\right)^{2}}\right)\,du\,dt

\displaystyle=4\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{t}u\argth\left(\sqrt{1-\left(u/\kappa\right)^{2}}\right)\,du\,dt

\displaystyle=4\kappa^{3}\int_{0}^{L/\kappa}\int_{0}^{t}s\argth\left(\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\right)\,ds\,dt.

Since
\frac{d}{ds}\left(\argth\left(\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\right)\right)=\frac{-1}{s\sqrt{1-s^{2}}}

and
\argth(\sqrt{1-t^{2}})\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle t\downarrow 0}}{{\sim}}\ \frac{-1}{t}
, integrating by parts, we find

(24)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}s\argth\left(\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\right)\,ds

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{s^{2}}{2}\argth(\sqrt{1-s^{2}})\right]_{0}^{t}+\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{s}{\sqrt{1-s^{2}}}\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2}}{2}\argth\left(\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\right)+\dfrac{1}{2}\left(1-\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\right).

Thus

\displaystyle S^{2}_{R^{\cyl}_{1,\kappa}}(L)

\displaystyle=2\kappa^{2}\int_{0}^{L}\left(t/\kappa\right)^{2}\argth\left(\sqrt{1-\left(t/\kappa\right)^{2}}\right)+\left(1-\sqrt{1-\left(t/\kappa\right)^{2}}\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=2\kappa^{3}\int_{0}^{L/\kappa}t^{2}\argth\left(\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\right)+\left(1-\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\right)\,dt.

We now set
\ell=L/\kappa
to lighten notation.
Integrating by parts once again, we compute

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\ell}t^{2}\argth\left(\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{t^{3}}{3}\argth\left(\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\right)\right]_{0}^{\ell}+\dfrac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{\ell}\frac{t^{2}}{\sqrt{1-t^{2}}}\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell^{3}}{3}\argth\left(\sqrt{1-\ell^{2}}\right)+\dfrac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{\ell}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-t^{2}}}-\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\,dt,

thus

S^{2}_{R^{\cyl}_{1,\kappa}}(L)=\frac{2\kappa^{3}}{3}\ell^{3}\argth\left(\sqrt{1-\ell^{2}}\right)+2\kappa^{3}\ell+\frac{2\kappa^{3}}{3}\int_{0}^{\ell}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{1-t^{2}}}-\frac{8\kappa^{3}}{3}\int_{0}^{\ell}\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\,dt.

On the one hand, we have

\int_{0}^{\ell}\frac{dt}{\sqrt{1-t^{2}}}=\arcsin\left(\ell\right)

and on the other hand

\int_{0}^{\ell}\sqrt{1-t^{2}}=\int_{0}^{\arcsin(\ell)}\cos^{2}\theta\,d\theta=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\arcsin(\ell)}1+\cos(2\theta)\,d\theta\\
=\dfrac{1}{2}\arcsin(\ell)+\dfrac{1}{4}\arcsin(2\sin\ell)=\dfrac{1}{2}\arcsin\left(\ell\right)+\dfrac{1}{2}\ell\sqrt{1-\ell^{2}}.

Hence, for
L\leq\kappa
,

S^{2}_{R^{\cyl}_{1,\kappa}}(L)=\frac{2}{3}\kappa^{3}\left[\ell^{3}\argth\left(\sqrt{1-\ell^{2}}\right)+\ell-\arcsin(\ell)+2\ell\left(1-\sqrt{1-\ell^{2}}\right)\right].

Recalling that

\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R^{\cyl}_{1,\kappa}}(B^{\prime}_{R})=2\pi\int_{0}^{R}S^{2}_{R^{\cyl}_{1,\kappa}}\big(2\sqrt{R^{2}-r^{2}}\big)\,dr.

setting

g(\ell)=\frac{4\pi}{3}\left[\ell-\arcsin(\ell)+2\ell\left(1-\sqrt{1-\ell^{2}}\right)+\ell^{3}\argth\left(\sqrt{1-\ell^{2}}\right)\right]\qquad\forall\ell\leq 1,

we find

\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R^{\cyl}_{1,\kappa}}(B^{\prime}_{R})=\kappa^{3}\int_{0}^{R}g\left(2\sqrt{R^{2}-r^{2}}/\kappa\right)\,dr.

By a change of variable, we have

\kappa^{3}\int_{0}^{R}g\left(2\sqrt{R^{2}-r^{2}}/\kappa\right)\,dr=\kappa^{3}R\int_{0}^{1}g\left(\frac{2R}{\kappa}\sqrt{1-r^{2}}\right)\,dr=2\kappa^{2}R^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\lambda g\left(\lambda^{-1}\sqrt{1-r^{2}}\right)\,dr,

thus, eventually,

\mathcal{I}^{2}_{R^{\cyl}_{1,\kappa}}(B^{\prime}_{R})=2\kappa^{2}R^{2}\lambda\int_{0}^{1}g\left(\lambda^{-1}\sqrt{1-r^{2}}\right)\,dr.

Hence by
1.14
we deduce

\sigma_{R_{1,\kappa}}^{3,\cyl}(l)=\frac{2}{l}+\dfrac{1}{|B^{\prime}_{l}|}\mathcal{I}_{R_{1,\kappa}^{\cyl}}^{2}(B^{\prime}_{l})\\
=\frac{4\lambda}{\kappa}+\frac{2\lambda\kappa^{2}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}g\left(\lambda^{-1}\sqrt{1-r^{2}}\right)\,dr,

where
\lambda=\frac{\kappa}{2l}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Proof of
3.6
.

We recall

\cos^{4}\theta=\frac{3}{8}+\dfrac{1}{2}\cos(2\theta)+\dfrac{1}{8}\cos(4\theta).

Integrating by parts, we compute a primitive of
\theta\mapsto\cos(2\theta)\log(1+\sin\theta)
:

\displaystyle\int\cos(2\theta)\log(1+\sin\theta)\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\left[\dfrac{1}{2}\sin(2\theta)\log(1+\sin\theta)\right]-\dfrac{1}{2}\int\frac{\sin(2\theta)\cos\theta}{1+\sin\theta}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\left[\dfrac{1}{2}\sin(2\theta)\log(1+\sin\theta)\right]-\int(\sin\theta-\sin^{2}\theta)\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\left[\dfrac{1}{2}\sin(2\theta)\log(1+\sin\theta)+\cos\theta+\frac{\theta}{2}-\dfrac{1}{4}\sin(2\theta)\right].

Thus

\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos(2\theta)\log(1+\sin\theta)\,d\theta=\frac{\pi}{4}-1

and

\int_{-\pi/2}^{0}\cos(2\theta)\log(1+\sin\theta)\,d\theta=1+\frac{\pi}{4}.

We also compute

\displaystyle\int\cos(4\theta)\log(1+\sin\theta)\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\left[\dfrac{1}{4}\sin(4\theta)\log(1+\sin\theta)\right]-\dfrac{1}{4}\int\frac{\sin(4\theta)\cos\theta}{1+\sin\theta}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\left[\dfrac{1}{4}\sin(4\theta)\log(1+\sin\theta)\right]-\int\frac{\sin\theta\cos^{2}\theta\cos(2\theta)}{1+\sin\theta}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\left[\dfrac{1}{4}\sin(4\theta)\log(1+\sin\theta)\right]-\int(\sin\theta-\sin^{2}\theta)(2\cos^{2}\theta-1)\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\left[\dfrac{1}{4}\sin(4\theta)\log(1+\sin\theta)+\frac{2}{3}\cos^{3}\theta-\cos\theta\right]+\int 2\sin^{2}\theta\cos^{2}\theta-\sin^{2}\theta\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\left[\dfrac{1}{4}\sin(4\theta)\log(1+\sin\theta)+\frac{2}{3}\cos^{3}\theta-\cos\theta-\frac{\theta}{4}+\dfrac{1}{4}\sin(2\theta)-\dfrac{1}{16}\sin(4\theta)\right].

Thus

\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos(4\theta)\log(1+\sin\theta)\,d\theta=\dfrac{1}{3}-\frac{\pi}{8}

and

\int_{-\pi/2}^{0}\cos(4\theta)\log(1+\sin\theta)\,d\theta=-\dfrac{1}{3}-\frac{\pi}{8}.

Using

and
27

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos^{4}\theta\log(1+\sin\theta)\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{8}\left(2C-\frac{\pi\log 2}{2}\right)+\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{4}-1\right)+\dfrac{1}{8}\left(\dfrac{1}{3}-\frac{\pi}{8}\right)

\displaystyle=\pi\left(\frac{7}{64}-\frac{3\log 2}{16}\right)-\frac{11}{24}+\frac{3C}{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{8}\left(-2C-\frac{\pi\log 2}{2}\right)+\dfrac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{\pi}{4}\right)+\dfrac{1}{8}\left(-\dfrac{1}{3}-\frac{\pi}{8}\right)

\displaystyle=\pi\left(\frac{7}{64}-\frac{3\log 2}{16}\right)+\frac{11}{24}-\frac{3C}{4}.

Lemma 4.1
.

For every
\kappa>0
and every
L>0
, we have

S_{Y_{1,\kappa}}(L)=2\kappa^{3}\left(\frac{L}{\kappa}-2+\left(\frac{L}{\kappa}+2\right)e^{-L/\kappa}\right).

Proof.

We start from the definition
Equation

14
of
S_{Y_{1,\kappa}}
and we compute

\displaystyle S_{Y_{1,\kappa}}(L)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{L}|s-t|e^{-|s-t|/\kappa}\,ds\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{L}\int_{-t}^{L-t}|r|e^{-|r|/\kappa}\,dr\,dt

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{L}\int_{0}^{t}|r|e^{-|r|/\kappa}\,dr\,dt

\displaystyle=2\kappa^{3}\int_{0}^{L/\kappa}\int_{0}^{t}re^{-r}\,dr\,dt

\displaystyle=2\kappa^{3}\int_{0}^{L/\kappa}1-e^{-t}(1+t)\,dt

\displaystyle=2\kappa^{3}\left(\frac{L}{\kappa}-2+\left(\frac{L}{\kappa}+2\right)e^{-L/\kappa}\right).

One can check that

, which is consistent with

S_{R_{1}}(L)=L^{3}/3
.

We deduce the Yukawa energy of balls.

Lemma 4.2

For every

Proof.

By

is defined by

f_{\kappa}(\lambda)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{6\lambda}{\kappa}+4\pi\kappa^{2}\big(1-3\lambda(1-4\lambda^{2})-3\lambda(4\lambda^{2}+4\lambda+1)e^{-1/\lambda}\big)&\quad\text{ if }\lambda>0,\\
\qquad 4\pi\kappa^{2}&\quad\text{ if }\lambda=0\end{cases}

and

\lambda_{*}\coloneqq\argmin_{\lambda\in[0,+\infty)}f_{\kappa}(\lambda).

In addition,
\lambda_{*}=0
if
\kappa\leq(2\pi)^{-1/3}
and
\lambda_{*}>0
otherwise.

Proof.

Recall that
P(B_{R})/|B_{R}|=3/R=6\lambda/\kappa
and that from
4.2
we have

\frac{\mathcal{I}_{Y_{1,\kappa}}(B_{R})}{|B_{R}|}=12\pi\kappa^{2}\left(\frac{1}{3}-\lambda(1-4\lambda^{2})-\lambda(4\lambda^{2}+4\lambda+1)e^{-1/\lambda}\right),

where
\lambda=\kappa/(2R)
.
We then have,

\frac{\mathcal{E}_{Y_{1,\kappa}}(B_{R})}{|R|}=\frac{6\lambda}{\kappa}+4\pi\kappa^{2}\big(1-3\lambda(1-4\lambda^{2})-3\lambda(4\lambda^{2}+4\lambda+1)e^{-1/\lambda}\big)\\
\eqqcolon f_{\kappa}(\lambda).

There holds

\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}f_{\kappa}(\lambda)=4\pi^{2}

and extending
f_{\kappa}
by continuity at
0
,
f_{\kappa}
is smooth on
[0,+\infty)
. Moreover

f_{\kappa}(\lambda)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lambda\uparrow\infty}}{{\sim}}\frac{6\lambda}{\kappa}.

We deduce that
f_{\kappa}
admits a minimum. Next, we compute

\begin{multlined}f_{\kappa}^{\prime}(\lambda)=\frac{6}{\kappa}-12\pi\kappa^{2}\left(1-12\lambda^{2}+\frac{e^{-1/\lambda}}{\lambda}\left(12\lambda^{3}+12\lambda^{2}+5\lambda+1\right)\right)\\
\end{multlined}

and

f^{\prime\prime}_{\kappa}(\lambda)=12\pi\kappa^{2}\left(24\lambda-\frac{e^{-1/\lambda}}{\lambda^{3}}\left(24\lambda^{4}+24\lambda^{3}+12\lambda^{2}+4\lambda+1\right)\right).

Notice that

24\lambda^{4}-e^{-1/\lambda}\left(24\lambda^{4}+24^{3}+12\lambda^{2}+4\lambda+1\right)>0\iff e^{1/\lambda}>1+\lambda^{-1}+\frac{\lambda^{-2}}{2!}+\frac{\lambda^{-3}}{3!}+\frac{\lambda^{-4}}{4!},

which is obviously true, thus
f_{\kappa}
is strictly convex.
In particular,
f_{\kappa}
admits a unique minimizer
\lambda_{*}\in[0,+\infty)
.
In addition, we have

f_{\kappa}^{\prime}(0)=\frac{6}{\kappa}-12\pi\kappa^{2}

and we deduce that
\lambda_{*}=0
if
\kappa\leq(2\pi)^{-\frac{1}{3}}
, and
\lambda_{*}>0

otherwise.
∎

4.2.
Energy/mass ratio of infinite cylinders

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\kappa,l>0
and
\lambda=\frac{\kappa}{2l}
. Setting

I(s)\coloneqq\frac{l(2l-s)}{2}\arccos\left(\frac{s}{2l}\right)+ls\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{2l-s}{4l}}\right)-\frac{s}{4}\sqrt{(2l)^{2}-s^{2}},

we have

\sigma_{Y_{1,\kappa}}^{3,\mathrm{cyl}}(l)=\frac{2}{l}+\frac{8}{l^{2}}\int_{0}^{2l}sK_{0}(s/\kappa)I(s)\,ds,

where
K_{0}
is a modified Bessel function of the second kind, defined by

K_{0}(\sigma)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\sigma\cosh(\xi)}\,d\xi.

Proof.

Recall that
B^{\prime}_{l}
denotes the disk of radius
l
in dimension
2
and let us recall the following formula from
1.8
,

\sigma_{Y_{1,\kappa}}^{3,\cyl}(l)=\frac{2}{l}+\dfrac{1}{|B_{l}^{\prime}|}\int_{B_{l}^{\prime}\times B_{l}^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{1,\kappa}(x-y,t)\,dt\,dx\,dy.

We see that we have to estimate the integral term:

\mathcal{C}_{1,\kappa}(l)\coloneqq\int_{B^{\prime}_{l}}\int_{B^{\prime}_{l}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{1,\kappa}(x-y,t)\,dt\,dx\,dy.

Unfortunately, the inner integral:

S_{Y_{\kappa,\alpha}}^{3,\mathrm{cyl}}\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{1,\kappa}(x-y,t)\,dt

does not have an analytic expression and this prevents us from using
1.14
to get a numerically computable formula for

\sigma_{Y_{\kappa,1}}^{3,\mathrm{cyl}}(l)
.
Instead, using the change of variable
y=r\tau
with
r>0
,
\tau\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
and using the fact that
Y_{1,\kappa}
is radial, we compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1,\kappa}(l)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\int_{0}^{l}r\int_{B^{\prime}_{l}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{1,\kappa}(x-r\tau,t)\,dx\,dt\,dr\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}(\tau).

For a fixed
\tau\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, there exists a rotation
R
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\tau=Re_{1}
. Performing the change of variable
x=R\tilde{x}
and using the symmetry
Y_{1,\kappa}(Rz,t)=Y_{1,\kappa}(z,t)
for
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1,\kappa}(l)

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{S}^{1}|\ \int_{0}^{l}r\int_{B^{\prime}_{l}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Y_{1,\kappa}(x-re_{1},t)\,dx\,dt\,dr

\displaystyle=2\pi\int_{0}^{l}r\int_{B^{\prime}_{l}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{e^{-\sqrt{|x-re_{1}|^{2}+t^{2}}/\kappa}}{(|x-re_{1}|^{2}+t^{2})^{1/2}}\,dt\,dx\,dr.

Decomposing the integration over
B_{l}^{\prime}
over the circular arcs
B_{l}^{\prime}\partial B^{\prime}_{s}(re_{1})
,
s>0
, we have by Fubini,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1,\kappa}(l)

\displaystyle=2\pi\int_{0}^{l}r\int_{0}^{l+r}\int_{B_{l}^{\prime}\cap\{|x-re_{1}|=s\}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{e^{-\sqrt{s^{2}+t^{2}}/\kappa}}{(s^{2}+t^{2})^{1/2}}\,dt\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}(x)\,ds\,dr

\displaystyle=2\pi\int_{0}^{l}r\int_{0}^{l+r}C(r,s,l)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{e^{-\sqrt{s^{2}+t^{2}}/\kappa}}{(s^{2}+t^{2})^{1/2}}\,dt\right)\,ds\,dr,

where
C(r,s,l)
is the circle-length

C(r,s,l)=|\partial B^{\prime}_{s}(re_{1})\cap B^{\prime}_{l}|=\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial B^{\prime}_{s}(re_{1})\cap B^{\prime}_{l}).

We slightly simplify the expression of
\mathcal{C}_{1,\kappa}(l)
by using the change of variable
t=su
in the inner integral to get

(36)

\mathcal{C}_{1,\kappa}(l)=2\pi\int_{0}^{l}r\int_{0}^{l+r}C(r,s,l)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{e^{-(s/\kappa)\sqrt{1+u^{2}}}}{(1+u^{2})^{1/2}}\,du\right)\,ds\,dr.

For
s<l-r
, we have a full circle, that is
C(r,s,l)=2\pi l
. For
s>l+r
,
\partial B^{\prime}_{s}(re_{1})\cap B^{\prime}_{l}=\varnothing
and
C(r,s,l)=0
.
For
l-r<s<l+r

\partial B^{\prime}_{s}(re_{1})\cap\partial B^{\prime}_{l}=\{(r+s\cos\gamma,\pm\sin\gamma)\}

where
\gamma=\gamma(r,s,l)\in(0,\pi)
satisfies

(r+s\cos\gamma)^{2}+(s\sin\gamma)^{2}=l^{2}\iff\gamma=\arccos\left(\dfrac{l^{2}-r^{2}-s^{2}}{2rs}\right).

Hence, setting

\gamma(r,s,l)=\begin{cases}\qquad 0&\text{if }s<l-r\\
\arccos\left(\dfrac{l^{2}-r^{2}-s^{2}}{2rs}\right)&\text{if }l-r<s<l+r\\
\qquad\pi&\text{if }s>l+r,\end{cases}

we have

C(r,s,l)=2(\pi-\gamma(r,s,l)).

Inserting this into
Equation

36
, we obtain

\mathcal{C}_{1,\kappa}(l)=4\pi\int_{0}^{l}r\int_{0}^{l+r}s(\pi-\gamma(r,s,l))\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{e^{-(s/\kappa)\sqrt{1+u^{2}}}}{(1+u^{2})^{1/2}}\,du\right)\,ds\,dr.

Using Fubini, this rewrites as

(37)

\mathcal{C}_{1,\kappa}(l)=4\pi\int_{0}^{2l}s\left[\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{e^{-(s/\kappa)\sqrt{1+u^{2}}}}{(1+u^{2})^{1/2}}\,du\right)\left(\int_{(s-l)_{+}}^{l}r\varphi(r,s,l)\,dr\right)\right]\,ds,

where we have set

\displaystyle\varphi(r,s,l)\coloneqq

\displaystyle\pi-\gamma(r,s,l)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\qquad\pi&\text{if }r<l-s,\\
\arccos\left(\dfrac{r^{2}+s^{2}-l^{2}}{2rs}\right)&\text{if }\big[s>l\text{ and }r>s-l\big]\text{ or }\big[s<l\text{ and }r>l-s\big]\\
\qquad 0&\text{if }s>l\text{ and }r<s-l.\end{cases}

Now we compute the two inner integrals in
Equation

37
in terms of well-known special
functions. We start with the integral with respect to
u
. Using
u=\sinh(\xi)
, we find

(38)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{e^{-\sigma\sqrt{1+u^{2}}}}{\sqrt{1+u^{2}}}\,du=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\sigma\cosh(\xi)}\,d\xi=2K_{0}(\sigma),

where
K_{0}
is a modified Bessel function of the second kind (see, e.g.,
[
32
, p. 181, (5)]
). Recall that
K_{0}
is a smooth integrable function on
(0,+\infty)
with

K_{0}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x\downarrow 0^{+}}}{{\sim}}\log(1/x).

Next, we compute the integral with respect to
r
in
Equation

37
.
If
s<l
, we have

(39)

\int_{(s-l)_{+}}^{l}r\varphi(r,s,l)\,dr=\pi\dfrac{(l-s)^{2}}{2}+\int_{l-s}^{l}r\arccos\left(\dfrac{r^{2}+s^{2}-l^{2}}{2rs}\right)\,dr.

If
s>l
, we have

(40)

\int_{(s-l)_{+}}^{l}r\varphi(r,s,l)\,dr=\int_{s-l}^{l}r\arccos\left(\dfrac{r^{2}+s^{2}-l^{2}}{2rs}\right)\,dr.

Let us set
a=l-s
and
b=l+s
. We search for a primitive function of

r\arccos\big(\tfrac{r^{2}+s^{2}-l^{2}}{2rs}\big)
on
(|a|,l)\subset(|a|,b)
.
Integrating by parts, we have

(41)

\int r\arccos\left(\dfrac{r^{2}+s^{2}-l^{2}}{2rs}\right)\,dr=\frac{r^{2}}{2}\arccos\left(\dfrac{r^{2}+s^{2}-l^{2}}{2rs}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{r(r^{2}+ab)}{\sqrt{b^{2}-r^{2}}\sqrt{r^{2}-a^{2}}}\,dr.

Notice that

(42)

\int\frac{r}{\sqrt{b^{2}-r^{2}}\sqrt{r^{2}-a^{2}}}\,dr=\frac{1}{2}\arcsin\left(\frac{2r^{2}-a^{2}-b^{2}}{b^{2}-a^{2}}\right).

Then we have

(43)

\int\frac{r^{3}}{\sqrt{b^{2}-r^{2}}\sqrt{r^{2}-a^{2}}}\,dr=\int\frac{r(r^{2}-a^{2})+a^{2}r}{\sqrt{b^{2}-r^{2}}\sqrt{r^{2}-a^{2}}}\,dr\\
=\int r\frac{\sqrt{r^{2}-a^{2}}}{\sqrt{b^{2}-r^{2}}}\,dr+\frac{a^{2}}{2}\arcsin\left(\frac{2r^{2}-a^{2}-b^{2}}{b^{2}-a^{2}}\right).

Eventually, setting
\tau=(a/b)^{2}
and using the successive change of variables
t=r/b
,
y=t^{2}
, and
t=\tau+(1-\tau)\sin^{2}\theta
,
we compute

(44)

\displaystyle\int_{x_{0}}^{x}r\frac{\sqrt{r^{2}-a^{2}}}{\sqrt{b^{2}-r^{2}}}\,dr

\displaystyle=b^{2}\int_{x_{0}/b}^{x/b}t\frac{\sqrt{t^{2}-(a/b)^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-t^{2}}}\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{b^{2}}{2}\int_{(x_{0}/b)^{2}}^{(x/b)^{2}}\frac{\sqrt{y-\tau}}{\sqrt{1-y}}\,dy

\displaystyle=(b^{2}-a^{2})\int_{\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{x_{0}^{2}-a^{2}}{b^{2}-a^{2}}}\right)}^{\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{x^{2}-a^{2}}{b^{2}-a^{2}}}\right)}\sin^{2}\theta\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{(b^{2}-a^{2})}{2}\bigg[\theta-\sin\theta\cos\theta\bigg]_{\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{x_{0}^{2}-a^{2}}{b^{2}-a^{2}}}\right)}^{\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{x^{2}-a^{2}}{b^{2}-a^{2}}}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(b^{2}-a^{2})}{2}\left[\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{x^{2}-a^{2}}{b^{2}-a^{2}}}\right)-\sqrt{\frac{x^{2}-a^{2}}{b^{2}-a^{2}}}\sqrt{\frac{b^{2}-x^{2}}{b^{2}-a^{2}}}\right]+\text{ constant}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(b^{2}-a^{2})\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{x^{2}-a^{2}}{b^{2}-a^{2}}}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(x^{2}-a^{2})(b^{2}-x^{2})}+\text{ constant}.

Gathering
Equation

41
,
Equation

42
,
Equation

43
and

Equation

44
, we find the primitive function

(45)

\displaystyle\int r\arccos\left(\dfrac{r^{2}+s^{2}-l^{2}}{2rs}\right)\,dr

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2}}{2}\arccos\left(\dfrac{r^{2}+s^{2}-l^{2}}{2rs}\right)+\frac{l(l-s)}{2}\arcsin\left(\frac{r^{2}-l^{2}-s^{2}}{2ls}\right)

\displaystyle+ls\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{r^{2}-(l-s)^{2}}{4ls}}\right)-\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{(r^{2}-(l-s)^{2})((l+s)^{2}-r^{2})}

\displaystyle\eqqcolon\psi(r,s,l).

Summarizing, from
Equation

37
,
Equation

38
,
Equation

39

and
Equation

40
, we deduce

(46)

\mathcal{C}_{1,\kappa}(l)=8\pi\int_{0}^{2l}sK_{0}(s/\kappa)I(s)\,ds,

with

I(s)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\pi}{2}(l-s)^{2}+(\psi(l,s,l)-\psi(l-s,s,l))&\qquad\text{ if }s<l\\
\psi(l,s,l)-\psi(s-l,s,l)&\qquad\text{ if }s>l.\end{cases}

For
s\neq l
, we compute

\psi(|s-l|,s,l)=\frac{(l-s)^{2}}{2}\arccos(\mathrm{sign}(s-l))-\frac{l(l-s)}{2}\frac{\pi}{2}

and

\psi(l,s,l)=\frac{l^{2}}{2}\arccos\left(\frac{s}{2l}\right)-\frac{l(l-s)}{2}\arcsin\left(\frac{s}{2l}\right)+ls\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{2l-s}{4l}}\right)-\frac{s}{4}\sqrt{(2l)^{2}-s^{2}}.

In both cases
s<l
or
s>l
for

we find

\displaystyle I(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{l^{2}}{2}\arccos\left(\frac{s}{2l}\right)-\frac{l(l-s)}{2}\left[\frac{\pi}{2}-\arcsin\left(\frac{s}{2l}\right)\right]+ls\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{2l-s}{4l}}\right)-\frac{s}{4}\sqrt{(2l)^{2}-s^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{l(2l-s)}{2}\arccos\left(\frac{s}{2l}\right)+ls\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{2l-s}{4l}}\right)-\frac{s}{4}\sqrt{(2l)^{2}-s^{2}}.

We conclude the proof by recalling

\sigma_{Y_{1,\kappa}}^{3,\cyl}(l)=\frac{2}{l}+\frac{\mathcal{C}_{1,\kappa}(l)}{\pi l^{2}}.

∎

4.3.
Energy/mass ratio of balls versus infinite cylinders

Now we compare the energy/mass of balls and long cylinders in the case of the Yukawa potential

Y_{1,\kappa}
. We establish the following.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\kappa=0.56
. There holds
\rho_{Y_{1,0.56}}^{3,\cyl}<\rho_{Y_{1,0.56}}^{3,\ball}
.

Proof.

For this proof, we frequently rely on symbolic computations. Further details are provided in the
Jupyter Notebook included as supplementary material.

First, we look for a lower bound for
e^{3,\mathrm{ball}}_{Y_{1,\kappa}}
.
Using Newton method to find a zero of
f_{\kappa}^{\prime}
numerically, we observe that the minimum is
likely reached at

\lambda_{*}\subset(\alpha,\beta),

where
\alpha=8.84\times 10^{-2}
and
\beta=\alpha+10^{-4}
.
A posteriori, we ensure that this holds true by using the strict convexity of
f_{\kappa}
and
checking with a symbolic computation library (we used Python’s SymPy) that

f_{\kappa}^{\prime}(\alpha)f_{\kappa}^{\prime}\left(\beta\right)<0.

By convexity again, the graph of
f_{\kappa}
lies above its tangent lines. In
particular, setting
T_{\alpha}(x)=f_{\kappa}(\alpha)+f_{\kappa}^{\prime}(\alpha)(x-\alpha)
and

T_{\beta}(x)=f_{\kappa}(\beta)+f_{\kappa}^{\prime}(\beta)(x-\beta)
, we have the lower bound

(47)

e^{3,\mathrm{ball}}_{Y_{1,\kappa}}\geq\min(T_{\alpha}(\beta),T_{\beta}(\alpha))\geq 3.8755.

The above numerical lower bound is guaranteed by SymPy, which handles error propagation to evaluate
expressions to the desired precision.
Second, we search for an upper bound of
e^{3,\mathrm{cyl}}_{Y_{1,\kappa}}
. By definition, any

\sigma^{3,\cyl}_{1,\kappa}(l)
is an upper bound.
Recall that by
4.5
,
\sigma^{3,\mathrm{cyl}}_{Y_{1,\kappa}}(l)
is given by

\sigma_{Y_{1,\kappa}}^{3,\cyl}(l)=\frac{2}{l}+\frac{8}{l^{2}}\mathcal{J}_{\kappa}(l),

where we have set

\mathcal{J}_{\kappa}(l)=\int_{0}^{2l}F_{\kappa}(s)\,ds\quad\text{ and }\quad F_{\kappa}(s)=sK_{0}(s/\kappa)I(s).

Here
K_{0}
is a modified Bessel function of the second kind, and

I(s)=\frac{l(2l-s)}{2}\arccos\left(\frac{s}{2l}\right)+ls\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{2l-s}{4l}}\right)-\frac{s}{4}\sqrt{(2l)^{2}-s^{2}}.

From the integral expression of
K_{0}
given in
Equation

38
, we see that
K_{0}
is
nonincreasing. In addition, after computation we find

(48)

I^{\prime}(s)=-\frac{\sqrt{(2l)^{2}-s^{2}}}{2}-\frac{l}{2}\arccos\left(\frac{s}{2l}\right)+l\arcsin\left(\sqrt{\frac{2l-s}{4l}}\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad I^{\prime\prime}(s)=\frac{s}{2\sqrt{(2l)^{2}-s^{2}}}.

We see that
I^{\prime\prime}(s)
is positive, thus
I^{\prime}
is increasing on
(0,2l)
. Since
I^{\prime}(2l)=0
, it
follows that
I^{\prime}
is non-positive and thus
I
is nonincreasing.
Hence, for any segment
[a,b]\subset(0,2l]
, we have

(49)

F_{\kappa}(s)\leq sI(a)K_{0}(a/\kappa),\qquad\forall s\in[a,b].

In addition, by
[
17
, Corollary 3.3]
we have

K_{0}(x)<e^{-x}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2x}},\qquad\forall x>0,

so that

(50)

F_{\kappa}(s)\leq I(0)\sqrt{\frac{\pi\kappa s}{2}},\qquad\forall s\in(0,b).

Splitting the interval
(0,2l)
into the regular grid of size step
h={2l}/{N}
for some
N\geq 1
,
we get the estimate

(51)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\kappa}(l)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\int_{kh}^{(k+1)h}F_{\kappa}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq I(0)\sqrt{\frac{\pi\kappa}{2}}\int_{0}^{h}s\,ds+\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}I(kh)K_{0}\left(\frac{kh}{\kappa}\right)\int_{kh}^{(k+1)h}s\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}I(0)\sqrt{\pi\kappa}|h|^{3/2}+\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\frac{|h|^{2}}{2}\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)I(kh)K_{0}\left(\frac{kh}{\kappa}\right).

Here we used
Equation

50
to estimate
F_{\kappa}
on the interval
(0,h)
, and
Equation

49
on the
intervals
[kh,(k+1)h]
with
k\geq 1
.
Python’s SymPy library can compute the above sum (and thus
\sigma_{Y_{1,\kappa}}^{3,\cyl}(l)
) to
desired precision by handling errors propagation along the computations.
Choosing
l=2.09
and
N=3\cdot 10^{4}
, SymPy guarantees the upper bound

(52)

\sigma_{Y_{1,0.56}}^{3,\cyl}(2.09)\leq 3.8747,

which implies

(53)

\rho_{Y_{1,0.56}}^{3,\cyl}\leq 3.8747.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

We used the upper bound
Equation

51
and SymPy to obtain the upper bound
Equation

52
for safety, but a numerical
evaluation of the integral using a more precise quadrature formula (see the discussion further below) actually gives

\sigma_{Y_{1,\kappa}}^{3,\cyl}(2.09)\simeq 3.8730.

4.6
implies
4
and thus
3
in the case of the Yukawa potential, in
view of
1.10
.

In fact, numerical computations indicate that
\rho_{Y_{1,\kappa}}^{3,\cyl}\leq\rho_{Y_{1,\kappa}}^{3,\cyl}
holds on a whole interval of values of
\kappa
around
0.56
, see

Figure

2
.

Figure 2.
Comparison of the energy/mass ratio for balls and infinite cylinders with the Yukawa
potential for varying cut-length
\kappa
. We used
\sigma_{Y_{1,\kappa}}^{3,\cyl}(l(\kappa))
with
appropriate
l(\kappa)
as an upper bound for
\rho_{Y_{1,\kappa}}^{3,\cyl}
. As in the previous section, such
l(\kappa)

are obtained using SciPy’s library to optimize
l\mapsto\sigma_{Y_{1,\kappa}}^{3,\cyl}(l)
.

The vertical dashed line corresponds to

\kappa=(2\pi)^{-\frac{1}{3}}
, the threshold below which the infimum

\rho^{3,\mathrm{ball}}_{Y_{1,\kappa}}
is obtained by taking balls
B_{R}
with
R\to\infty
.

Let us detail how we compute accurate numerical approximations of
\sigma_{Y_{1,\kappa}}^{3,\cyl}(l)
in order to produce
Figure

2
.
We have to integrate numerically the function
F_{\kappa}(s)=s\mapsto sK_{0}(s/\kappa)I(s)
.
We would like to use Simpson’s quadrature formula, which gives an error in
O(|h|^{4})
for

smooth functions
, where
h
is the step size of the grid. However, the effective order of
convergence depends on the regularity of the integrand and it turns out that
F_{\kappa}
is not
smooth in
0
nor in
2l
.

Let us study the regularity of
F_{\kappa}
. We see that
I
is smooth on
[0,2l)
and
K_{0}
is smooth on
(0,+\infty)
, so that
F_{\kappa}
is smooth on

(0,2l)
. The only issues appear in
0
and in
2l
.

Behavior in
0
.
We have (see
[
32
, Chapter III, {3.71, (14)} and {3.7, (2)}]
)

K_{0}(s)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s\downarrow 0}}{{=}}-\log(s)+\log 2-\gamma+\frac{s^{2}}{4}\left(\gamma-1-\log 2\right)-\frac{s^{2}}{4}\log(s)+O(s^{4}\log(s)),

where
\gamma
is Euler’s constant.
Hence
F_{\kappa}
is continuous in zero with a singularity of the form
s\log(1/s)
. We have the
asymptotic expansion

F_{\kappa}(s)=s\left(-\log(s/\kappa)+\log 2-\gamma+\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{s}{\kappa}\right)^{2}(\gamma-1-\log 2)-\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{s}{\kappa}\right)^{2}\log(s/\kappa)+O(s^{4}\log(s))\right)\\
\times\left(I(0)+I^{\prime}(0)s+\frac{I^{\prime\prime}(0)}{2}s^{2}+O(s^{3})\right).

Subtracting the more singular terms

G_{\kappa}^{(1)}(s)=-\log(s/\kappa)\left[\kappa I(0)\left(\frac{s}{\kappa}\right)+\kappa^{2}I^{\prime}(0)\left(\frac{s}{\kappa}\right)^{2}\right]

to
F_{\kappa}
, we get that the non-smooth leading order term of
F_{\kappa}(s)-G_{\kappa}^{(1)}(s)
is of the form
s^{3}\log(s)
near
s=0
. It follows that using Simpson’s quadrature formula to integrate
F_{\kappa}-G_{\kappa}^{(1)}
on the interval
(0,l)
yields an error in
O(|h|^{4})
.

Behavior in
2l
.
Since
I(2l)=0
, we deduce from
Equation

48
the equivalent

F(s)\penalty\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle s\uparrow 2l}}{{\sim}}\penalty\ \frac{4}{3}l^{3/2}K_{0}\left(\frac{2l}{K}\right)(2l-s)^{3/2}\eqqcolon G^{(2)}_{\kappa}(s).

The contribution of such term in the quadrature error using
Simpson’s quadrature formula is in
O(|h|^{5/2})
,
[
8
]
. To gain an order
1
, we subtract
G_{\kappa}^{(2)}
to
F_{\kappa}
. The first term in the
asymptotic expansion of
F_{\kappa}-G_{\kappa}^{(2)}
at
2l
is then of the form
(2l-s)^{5/2}
, which gives a
contribution in
O(|h|^{7/2})
to the error.

Subtracting the leading singular parts at 0 and
2l
, we define

F_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{reg}}:=F_{\kappa}-G_{\kappa}^{(1)}-G_{\kappa}^{(2)},

and we decompose

\int_{0}^{2l}F_{\kappa}(s)\,ds=\int_{0}^{2l}F_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{reg}}(s)\,ds+\int_{0}^{2l}G_{\kappa}^{(1)}(s)+G_{\kappa}^{(2)}(s)\,ds.

On the one hand, we use the following closed formula for the integration of the singular parts:

\int_{0}^{2l}G_{\kappa}^{(1)}(s)+G_{\kappa}^{(2)}(s)\,ds\\
=l^{2}I(0)\left(1-2\log\left(\frac{2l}{\kappa}\right)\right)+\frac{8}{9}l^{3}I^{\prime}(0)\left(1-3\log\left(\frac{2l}{\kappa}\right)\right)+\frac{32\sqrt{2}}{15}l^{4}K_{0}\left(\frac{2l}{\kappa}\right).

On the other hand we use Simpsons’s quadrature
formula to integrate
F_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{reg}}
. The above considerations indicate that the
quadrature error is in
O(|h|^{7/2})
which is efficient enough to reach machine precision with a
low numerical cost. In practice, we observe a rate of convergence in
O(|h|^{4})
,
see
Figure

3
, due to the small prefactor
sK_{0}(s/\kappa)\simeq 10^{-3}
in

s=2l
and the fact that machine precision is reached for
|h|\simeq 10^{-3}
.

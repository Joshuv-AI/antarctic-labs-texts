Quantum Metric Corrections to Liouville’s Theorem and Chiral Kinetic Theory Preprint: RIKEN-iTHEMS-Report-25
Kazuya Mameda, Naoki Yamamoto

Abstract
Quasiparticles may possess not only Berry curvature but also a quantum metric in momentum space. We develop a canonical formalism for such quasiparticles based on the Dirac brackets, and demonstrate that quantum metric modifies the phase-space density of states at 𝒪 ⁡ ( ℏ 2 ) {\cal O}(\hbar^{2}) , leading to corrections to Liouville’s theorem, kinetic theory, and related physical quantities. In particular, we show that, in the presence of an inhomogeneous electric field, quantum metric induces corrections to the energy density and energy current. Applied to chiral fermions, this framework provides a nonlinear extension of chiral kinetic theory consistent with quantum field theory. Our work paves the way to potential applications of the quantum metric in high-energy physics and astrophysics.



Introduction
—The geometry of wavefunctions in Hilbert space, known as quantum geometry, has become a fundamental concept in modern physics.
One key component of quantum geometry is the Berry curvature
Berry 1984
, which has played a central role not only in condensed matter physics
Xiao et al. 2010
, but also in high-energy and astrophysical contexts
Kamada et al. 2023
, particularly through the formulation of chiral kinetic theory
Son and Yamamoto 2012
;
Stephanov and Yin 2012
;
Son and Yamamoto 2013
. This framework describes topological quantum transport phenomena such as the chiral magnetic effect
Vilenkin 1980
;
Nielsen and Ninomiya 1983
;
Fukushima et al. 2008
and the chiral anomaly
Adler 1969
;
Bell and Jackiw 1969
.

The other essential component of quantum geometry is the quantum metric, defined as the Riemannian metric on the Hilbert space that measures the infinitesimal distance between the eigenstates of a Hamiltonian
Provost and Vallée 1980
.
The quantum metric has recently attracted considerable attention in condensed matter physics owing to its impact on nonlinear responses;
see, e.g., Refs.
Liu et al. 2024
;
Gao et al. 2025
for recent reviews.
Previous applications of the quantum metric to transport phenomena have primarily focused on electric currents in solids.
Yet, it should be noted that a key property of the Berry curvature is the modification of the phase-space density of states and Liouville’s theorem
Xiao et al. 2005
;
Duval et al. 2006
, and, as a consequence, the very definitions of conserved charge and current as well as the formulation of kinetic equations
Son and Yamamoto 2012
;
Son and Yamamoto 2013
.
This knowledge naturally raises the fundamental question of whether the presence of the quantum metric may also lead to similar modifications.

The main aim of this Letter is to show that quantum metric generally modifies the phase-space density of states at
{\cal O}(\hbar^{2})
, thereby yielding corrections to Liouville’s theorem, kinetic theory, and related physical quantities.
For this purpose, we develop a canonical formalism for quasiparticles with quantum metric based on the Dirac brackets
Dirac 1950
;
Dirac 1964
.
In particular, we show that the corrections to the energy density and energy current in the presence of an inhomogeneous electric field are governed by the quantum metric.

As a concrete example of a nontrivial quantum metric, we then apply our formulation to chiral fermions, which provides a nonlinear extension of chiral kinetic theory in terms of the quantum metric.
By comparing with the corresponding results from the Wigner function formalism based on quantum field theory (see, e.g., Ref.
Hidaka et al. 2022
for a review), we verify that nonlinear responses of chiral fermions originate geometrically from the quantum metric in the kinetic regime. This highlights the fundamental role of the quantum metric in nonlinear responses of Weyl and Dirac fermions, relevant to high-energy physics and astrophysics, including relativistic heavy-ion collisions, the early universe, neutron stars, and supernovae.

As a simple yet nontrivial demonstration, in this work we consider a static, but generally inhomogeneous, external electric field without a magnetic field. The charge of quasiparticles is absorbed into the scalar potential or electric field.

Quantum metric and canonical formalism
—Let us consider a quasiparticle in a theory with Berry curvature and quantum metric, described by the effective Lagrangian up to
{\cal O}(\hbar^{2})

Kolodrubetz et al. 2017
(see also Refs.
Ren 2025
;
Yoshida and Yokoyama 2025
for recent works):

L={\bm{p}}\cdot\dot{{\bm{x}}}-\epsilon-\hbar{\bm{a}}\cdot\dot{{\bm{p}}}-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\dot{p}_{i}\dot{p}_{j}G_{ij},

(1)

where
\epsilon=\epsilon_{{\bm{p}}}+\Phi
with
\epsilon_{{\bm{p}}}
being the classical energy of the quasiparticle and
\Phi({\bm{x}})
the scalar potential.
Also,
{\bm{a}}({\bm{p}})
is the Berry connection and
G_{ij}({\bm{p}})
is the energy-normalized quantum metric (also referred to as the Berry connection polarizability) in momentum space.
The equations of motion read

\dot{x}_{i}=v_{i}+\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}E_{j}b_{k}-\hbar^{2}(\dot{p}_{j}\dot{p}_{k}\Gamma_{jki}+G_{ij}\ddot{p}_{j}),\ \ \ \dot{p}_{i}=E_{i},

(2)

where
{\bm{v}}={\bm{\nabla}}_{{\bm{p}}}\epsilon_{{\bm{p}}}
is the velocity,
{\bm{E}}=-\bm{\nabla}\Phi
is the electric field,
{\bm{b}}=\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{p}}\times{\bm{a}}
is the Berry curvature, and

\Gamma_{ijk}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{i}^{p}G_{jk}+\partial_{j}^{p}G_{ik}-\partial_{k}^{p}G_{ij})

(3)

is the Christoffel symbol associated with
G_{ij}
.

One prototype example with such a nontrivial Berry curvature and quantum metric is a two-level system
Kolodrubetz et al. 2017
or a Weyl Hamiltonian for chiral fermions [see Eqs. (
24
) below].
The effective Lagrangian (
1
), however, does not rely on the specific origin or the details of the quantum metric, and our argument based on it thus has broad applicability from condensed-matter systems to high-energy and astrophysical systems involving Weyl/Dirac fermions.
To make the present work self-contained and complete, we shall also provide a path-integral derivation of Eq. (
1
) for chiral fermions later in this Letter, extending the previous derivation of chiral kinetic theory in Ref.
Stephanov and Yin 2012
.

A characteristic of the Lagrangian with quantum metric in Eq. (
1
) is the presence of quadratic terms in
\dot{p}_{i}
, which are absent from the conventional theory up to
{\cal O}(\hbar)
with the Berry connection.
Dynamical systems involving such terms are not directly suitable for the standard symplectic structure with Poisson brackets, which is usually the basis of the Liouville’s theorem in phase space.
This difficulty can, however, be resolved by applying the Dirac-Bergmann theory of constrained systems
Dirac 1950
;
Dirac 1964
;
Bergmann 1949
, which is essential, for instance, in the quantization of gauge theories
Faddeev 1969
.
Namely, treating the original phase space as a constrained subspace on an extended whole space, one can obtain the symplectic structure of
\xi^{a}=(p_{i},x_{i})
described by the Dirac brackets, a generalization of the Poisson brackets in constrained systems.

To this end, instead of
L
, we start from

\tilde{L}=p_{i}\dot{x}_{i}-\epsilon-\hbar a_{i}q_{i}-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}G_{ij}q_{i}q_{j}+\lambda_{i}(q_{i}-\dot{p_{i}}).

(4)

By treating
\lambda_{i}
as Lagrange multipliers, the Euler-Lagrange equations for
p_{i}
,
x_{i}
, and
q_{i}
are equivalent to Eqs. (
2
).
Let us define the generalized coordinates together with their conjugate momenta as

\Xi^{A}=(p_{i},x_{i},q_{i}),\quad\Pi_{A}=(\pi^{p}_{i},\pi^{x}_{i},\pi^{q}_{i}):=\frac{\partial\tilde{L}}{\partial\dot{\Xi}^{A}},

(5)

which constitute the 18-dimensional extended phase space
\Gamma
.
The Poisson bracket on
\Gamma
is then defined as

\{X,Y\}_{\Gamma}=\frac{\partial X}{\partial\Pi_{A}}\frac{\partial Y}{\partial\Xi^{A}}-\frac{\partial X}{\partial\Xi^{A}}\frac{\partial Y}{\partial\Pi_{A}}.

(6)

The Hamiltonian is obtained by the Legendre transformation as

\begin{split}\tilde{H}=\Pi_{A}\dot{\Xi}^{A}-\tilde{L}=\epsilon+(\pi^{p}_{i}+\hbar a_{i})q_{i}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}G_{ij}q_{i}q_{j},\end{split}

(7)

where we used
\pi^{p}_{i}=-\lambda_{i}
,
\pi^{x}_{i}=p_{i}
, and
\pi_{i}^{q}=0
.

Although the original Lagrangian
L
is described by the 12 independent variables
(p_{i},x_{i},\dot{p}_{i},\dot{x}_{i})
, the whole phase space
\Gamma
itself is now 18-dimensional.
Therefore, we need to treat the present system as a constrained system.
The 12-dimensional subspace of
\Gamma
is denoted by
\gamma
, where the coordinates and conjugate momenta are

\xi^{a}=(p_{i},x_{i})
and
\pi_{a}=(\pi^{p}_{i},\pi^{x}_{i})
, respectively.
The Poisson bracket in this subspace is defined as

\{X,Y\}_{\gamma}=\frac{\partial X}{\partial\pi_{a}}\frac{\partial Y}{\partial\xi^{a}}-\frac{\partial X}{\partial\xi^{a}}\frac{\partial Y}{\partial\pi_{a}}.

(8)

From
\Pi_{A}=\partial\tilde{L}/\partial\dot{\Xi}^{A}
, the primary constraints read
\phi_{i}^{x}=\pi^{x}_{i}-p_{i}\approx 0
and
\phi_{i}^{q}=\pi_{i}^{q}\approx 0
.
Here, “
\approx
” denotes weak equality, meaning that both sides are equated only after evaluating the Poisson brackets. Note that
\pi_{i}^{p}\approx-\lambda_{i}
are not constraints but rather relations to determine the Lagrange multipliers
\lambda_{i}
, which do not belong to the set of phase-space variables.
The time evolution of the constraints is given by
\dot{\phi}_{i}=\{\tilde{H},\phi_{i}\}_{\Gamma}
.
To maintain consistency with the constraints above, we must impose the following secondary constraints:
\varphi_{i}^{x}=\partial_{i}\epsilon+q_{i}\approx 0
and
\varphi_{i}^{q}=\pi^{p}_{i}+\hbar a_{i}+\hbar^{2}G_{ij}q_{j}\approx 0
.
After eliminating the degrees of freedom
(q_{i},\pi_{i}^{q})
via the 6 constraints
\phi^{q}_{i}\approx 0
and
\varphi_{i}^{x}\approx 0
, we obtain the canonical formalism described by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tilde{H}=\epsilon-(\pi^{p}_{i}+\hbar a_{i})\partial_{i}\epsilon+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\partial_{i}\epsilon\partial_{j}\epsilon G_{ij},

(9)

\displaystyle\phi_{i}:=\pi_{i}^{x}-p_{i},\quad\varphi_{i}:=\pi^{p}_{i}+\hbar a_{i}-\hbar^{2}G_{ij}\partial_{j}\epsilon

(10)

with
\Phi_{n}:=(\phi_{i},\varphi_{i})\approx 0
.

These 6 residual constraints properly restrict the dynamics in the 12-dimensional phase space
\gamma
on the 6-dimensional subspace
g\ni\xi^{a}=(p_{i},x_{i})
.
The corresponding Dirac bracket is defined as

\{X,Y\}_{\mathrm{D}}=\{X,Y\}_{\gamma}-\{X,\Phi_{n}\}_{\gamma}C^{nm}\{\Phi_{m},Y\}_{\gamma},

(11)

where
C^{nm}
is the inverse of the constraint matrix
C_{nm}:=\{\Phi_{n},\Phi_{m}\}_{\gamma}
and is given by

\begin{split}C^{nm}&=\begin{pmatrix}\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}b_{k}^{\prime}&-\delta_{ij}-\hbar^{2}G_{jk}\partial_{i}\partial_{k}\epsilon\\
\delta_{ij}+\hbar^{2}G_{ik}\partial_{j}\partial_{k}\epsilon&0\end{pmatrix},\end{split}

(12)

with

a_{i}^{\prime}=a_{i}-\hbar G_{ij}\partial_{j}\epsilon,\quad b_{i}^{\prime}=\epsilon_{ijk}\partial_{j}^{p}a_{k}^{\prime}.

(13)

This
a_{i}^{\prime}
can be regarded as the modified Berry connection, and accordingly
b_{i}^{\prime}
as the modified Berry curvature
Gao et al. 2014
;
Kaplan et al. 2024
.

The full Hamiltonian (
9
) under the constraints (
10
) can be written as

\tilde{H}\approx\epsilon-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\partial_{i}\epsilon\partial_{j}\epsilon G_{ij}.

(14)

This can be interpreted as the energy of the quasiparticle, including the correction from the quantum metric, which is consistent with the results of Refs.
Liu et al. 2022
;
Jia et al. 2024
under
\partial_{i}\epsilon=-E_{i}
.

Modified Liouville’s theorem and kinetic theory
—Now, the Lagrange bracket restricted on
g
, i.e.,
\omega^{ab}=\{\xi^{a},\xi^{b}\}_{\mathrm{D}}
reads

\omega^{ab}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\delta_{ij}+\hbar^{2}G_{ik}\partial_{j}\partial_{k}\epsilon\\
-\delta_{ij}-\hbar^{2}G_{jk}\partial_{i}\partial_{k}\epsilon&\hbar\epsilon_{ijk}b_{k}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}.

(15)

Up to
{\cal O}(\hbar^{2})
, the determinant
\omega=\det(\omega_{ab})
with
\omega_{ab}=(\omega^{ab})^{-1}
is evaluated as

\begin{split}\sqrt{\omega}&=1-\hbar^{2}G_{ij}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\epsilon.\end{split}

(16)

For
\epsilon=\epsilon_{{\bm{p}}}+\Phi
, the invariant measure of the phase space on
g
is

\mathrm{d}g=\sqrt{\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\xi=(1+\hbar^{2}\partial_{i}E_{j}G_{ij})\frac{\mathrm{d}{\bm{x}}\mathrm{d}{\bm{p}}}{(2\pi)^{3}}.

(17)

This is a nonlinear extension of Refs.
Xiao et al. 2005
;
Duval et al. 2006
due to the quantum metric at
{\cal O}(\hbar^{2})
, and constitutes one of our main results.
Using the Lagrange bracket (
15
), we confirm that
\dot{\xi}^{a}=\{\tilde{H},\xi^{a}\}_{\mathrm{D}}
reproduces the equations of motion (
2
).

The invariant measure (
17
) implies that the probability of finding a particle in a phase-space cell is given by
\rho=\sqrt{\omega}n_{{\bm{p}}}
, where
n_{{\bm{p}}}=n_{{\bm{p}}}(\epsilon_{\bm{p}}^{\prime})
is the distribution function, with
\epsilon_{\bm{p}}^{\prime}
being the modified quasiparticle energy up to
{\cal O}(\hbar^{2})
[see Eq. (
14
)],

\epsilon_{\bm{p}}^{\prime}=\epsilon_{\bm{p}}-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}E_{i}E_{j}G_{ij}.

(18)

From the modified Liouville’s theorem
\mathrm{d}\rho/\mathrm{d}t=\partial_{t}\rho+\{\tilde{H},\rho\}_{\mathrm{D}}=0
, we obtain the following kinetic equation up to
{\cal O}(\hbar^{2})
:

\begin{split}\Bigl[\partial_{t}+\left({\bm{v}}^{\prime}+\hbar{\bm{E}}\times{\bm{b}}^{\prime}+\hbar^{2}{\bm{V}}\right)\cdot\bm{\nabla}+{\bm{E}}\cdot\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{p}}\Bigr]n_{\bm{p}}=0,\end{split}

(19)

where
{\bm{v}}^{\prime}=\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{p}}\epsilon_{\bm{p}}^{\prime}
and
V_{k}=-G_{kj}v_{i}\partial_{i}E_{j}
.
Here and below, we ignore the collision term.
Note that the Christoffel symbol in Eqs. (
2
) is recast as the quantum-metric corrections in
{\bm{v}}^{\prime}
and
{\bm{b}}^{\prime}
.
One can also confirm that, owing to the quantum metric correction in
\epsilon_{\bm{p}}^{\prime}
, the kinetic equation (
19
) is satisfied by the equilibrium distribution
n_{\bm{p}}^{\rm eq}=n_{\mathrm{F}}(\epsilon_{\bm{p}}^{\prime}-\mu)
under the condition
\bm{\nabla}\mu={\bm{E}}
, where
n_{\mathrm{F}}(x)=(\mathrm{e}^{x/T}+1)^{-1}
is the Fermi-Dirac distribution function, with
\mu
being the chemical potential and
T
the temperature.

—We are now ready to define the particle number density
n
and current
{\bm{j}}

\varepsilon
and energy current
{\bm{J}}

At

Nonlinear responses of chiral and Dirac fermions
—While so far our argument and derivation are generic and independent of the details of the Berry curvature and quantum metric, from now on we consider chiral and Dirac fermions, keeping their potential applications to high-energy physics and astrophysics.
This step also shows that the above results are consistent with those derived from quantum field theory, validating our formulation.

We first focus on a right-handed fermion. In this case, the classical energy dispersion is
\epsilon_{{\bm{p}}}=|{\bm{p}}|
, and the Berry curvature and the (energy-normalized) quantum metric are given by

\begin{split}b_{i}=\frac{\hat{p}_{i}}{2|{\bm{p}}|^{2}}\,,\quad G_{ij}=\frac{1}{4|{\bm{p}}|^{3}}(\delta_{ij}-\hat{p}_{i}\hat{p}_{j}),\end{split}

(24)

respectively, where
\hat{p}_{i}=p_{i}/|{\bm{p}}|
; see later for the derivation.
For chiral fermions, the spin is always locked to the momentum, and the quantum state is specified by the momentum orientation
\hat{\bm{p}}
. The quantum metric
G_{ij}
, which characterizes changes in the quantum state, is thus transverse to
\hat{\bm{p}}
, as in Eqs. (
24
).

Let us consider the case of local equilibrium with local fluid velocity
{\bm{u}}
.
Then, the equilibrium distribution function is given by the summation of the contributions of particles and antiparticles as
n_{\bm{p}}^{(0)\mathrm{eq}}=n_{\mathrm{F}}(\epsilon_{\bm{p}}-{\bm{u}}\cdot{\bm{p}}-\mu)-n_{\mathrm{F}}(\epsilon_{\bm{p}}-{\bm{u}}\cdot{\bm{p}}+\mu)
.
Expanding Eqs. (
22
) to first order in
{\bm{u}}
and performing momentum integration, we extract the following
E^{2}
-dependent corrections:

\displaystyle n^{(EE)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\hbar^{2}C}{24\pi^{2}}|{\bm{E}}|^{2},

(25a)

\displaystyle{\bm{j}}^{(EE)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar^{2}C}{12\pi^{2}}\biggl[-{\bm{E}}({\bm{E}}\cdot{\bm{u}})+\frac{1}{2}|{\bm{E}}|^{2}{\bm{u}}\biggr],

(25b)

where we introduced the integral

C=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}p}{p}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}p}\Bigl[n_{\mathrm{F}}(p-\mu)-n_{\mathrm{F}}(p+\mu)\Bigr].

(26)

Equations (
25
) reproduce the results in Refs.
Yang et al. 2020
;
Mameda 2023
;
Yang et al. 2025
derived from the Wigner function formalism based on the quantum field theory, where the relation to the geometric structure of the quantum metric was not appreciated. Although the integral
C
cannot, in general, be evaluated analytically, one finds that
C\rightarrow-{1}/{\mu}
in the zero-temperature limit
T\rightarrow 0

Yang et al. 2020
.

Similarly, Eq. (
23a
) for chiral fermions in the hydrodynamic regime yields the
\partial_{i}E_{i}
-dependent correction,

\varepsilon^{(\partial E)}=\frac{\hbar^{2}\mu}{12\pi^{2}}\bm{\nabla}\cdot{\bm{E}},

(27)

which also reproduces the result obtained by the Wigner function formalism in Refs.
Mameda 2023
;
Yang et al. 2025
.

From Eqs. (
22
) and (
23
), one might expect to derive formulas for
n^{(\partial E)}
,
{\bm{j}}^{(\partial E)}
,
\varepsilon^{(EE)}
, and
{\bm{J}}^{(EE)}
.
For chiral fermions, the momentum integrals in these expressions are dominated by contributions around the origin
|{\bm{p}}|\sim 0
, where the kinetic description breaks down.
For this reason, we do not present them explicitly here.

The extension to Dirac fermions is straightforward. While the Berry curvatures of right- and left-handed fermions have opposite signs and their contributions cancel unless a chirality imbalance is present (see, e.g., Ref.
Kamada et al. 2023
for a review), the quantum-metric contributions appear with the same sign for both, yielding a nonvanishing sum even without such an imbalance. Consequently, the effects of the quantum metric manifest at
{\cal}O(\hbar^{2})
in generic relativistic many-body systems composed of Dirac fermions, not restricted to chiral matter.

Path-integral derivation of the Lagrangian with quantum metric
—Let us now derive the effective Lagrangian (
1
) for a right-handed fermion by extending of the path-integral derivation of chiral kinetic theory in Ref.
Stephanov and Yin 2012
up to
{\cal O}(\hbar^{2})
. The Weyl Hamiltonian is given by
H_{\mathrm{W}}={\bm{p}}\cdot\bm{\sigma}+\Phi
.
The transition amplitude from initial to final states,
\mathcal{A}=\langle f|\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}H_{\mathrm{W}}(t_{\mathrm{f}}-t_{\mathrm{i}})/\hbar}|i\rangle

After the diagonalization by the unitary matrix
V_{\bm{p}}=(u_{{\bm{p}}+}\ u_{{\bm{p}}-})
, where
u_{{\bm{p}}s}
is the energy eigenvector,
\bm{\sigma}\cdot{\bm{p}}\,u_{{\bm{p}}s}=\epsilon_{{{\bm{p}}}s}u_{{\bm{p}}s}

\epsilon_{{{\bm{p}}}s}=s|{\bm{p}}|

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}&=V_{{\bm{p}}_{\mathrm{f}}}\int\mathcal{D}{\bm{x}}\mathcal{D}{\bm{p}}\,\mathcal{P}\exp\biggl[\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\hbar}\int_{t_{\mathrm{i}}}^{t_{\mathrm{f}}}\mathrm{d}t({\bm{p}}\cdot\dot{{\bm{x}}}-H)\biggr]V_{{\bm{p}}_{\mathrm{i}}}^{\dagger},\end{split}

(28)

where
\mathcal{P}
denotes the path-ordered product of the
\mathrm{U}(2)
matrices.
Here, the effective Hamiltonian

is expressed as

H=H_{0}+\bar{H}_{1}+\delta H_{1}

H_{0}:=|{\bm{p}}|\sigma_{3}+\Phi
,
\bar{H}_{1}:=\hbar\bar{{\bm{a}}}\cdot\dot{\bm{p}}
, and
\delta H_{1}:=\hbar\delta{\bm{a}}\cdot\dot{{\bm{p}}}
,
where
\bar{{\bm{a}}}=\sigma_{3}{\bm{a}}

\delta{\bm{a}}
are diagonal and off-diagonal matrix elements of the Berry connection

\hat{{\bm{a}}}=-\mathrm{i}V_{\bm{p}}^{\dagger}\bm{\nabla}_{\bm{p}}V_{\bm{p}}

It should be noted that the integrand is still not diagonalized because of the presence of
\delta H_{1}
.
If this off-diagonal matrix element is of order
{\cal O}(\hbar^{2})

{\cal O}(\hbar)
.
The original work in Ref.
Stephanov and Yin 2012

\delta H_{1}={\cal O}(\hbar^{2})
via the ’t Hooft abelian projection
’t Hooft 1981

In this Letter, employing a more systematic scheme developed by Luttinger-Kohn

We first introduce a unitary operator
\mathrm{e}^{S_{{\bm{p}}}}
, where an anti-Hermitian matrix
S_{{\bm{p}}}={\cal O}(\hbar)
fulfills

[S,H_{0}]+\delta H_{1}=0.

(29)

Let us here compute the matrix element in the chirality basis,
S_{s,s^{\prime}}=\langle s|S|s^{\prime}\rangle

While the diagonal elements

can be set to zero, the condition (
29

\begin{split}S_{s,-s}=\frac{[\delta H_{1}]_{s,-s}}{\epsilon_{{{\bm{p}}}s}-\epsilon_{{{\bm{p}}}-s}}=s\hbar\frac{\dot{{\bm{p}}}\cdot\hat{{\bm{a}}}_{s,-s}}{2|{\bm{p}}|}.\end{split}

(30)

In order to perform the perturbative diagonalization of the integrand, we discretize the integrand in Eq. (
28
) with an interval
\Delta t

Inserting the identity

Outlook
—Our work has important implications not only for nonlinear responses in condensed matter systems with nontrivial quantum metric, but also for those in high-energy and astrophysical systems involving Weyl and Dirac fermions.
In particular, our finding of a quantum-metric correction to the energy density indicates that the equations of state of relativistic many-body systems composed of Dirac fermions, such as quark–gluon plasmas created in heavy-ion collisions or quark matter inside neutron stars, are affected by the quantum metric coupled to electric fields, even in the absence of chirality imbalance. While this study has focused on a static inhomogeneous electric field for simplicity, incorporating time-dependent electric and magnetic fields will be essential for further phenomenological applications.
One may also include dynamical electromagnetic fields in addition to background fields, enabling the study of collective modes with quantum metric in the kinetic regime, as in Ref.
Akamatsu and Yamamoto 2013
.

In this Letter, we focused on particle number and energy densities as well as dissipationless geometric electric and energy currents.
A natural direction would be to study dissipative currents by incorporating collision terms into the kinetic equation.
From a more theoretical viewpoint, it would be interesting to explore the consequences of Lorentz invariance on the nonlinear chiral kinetic theory with the quantum metric, in the spirit of Refs.
Son and Yamamoto 2013
;
Chen et al. 2014
, and
to develop Lorentz-covariant extensions
Chen et al. 2015
of such a kinetic theory; see also Refs.
Mameda 2023
;
Hayata et al. 2021
for related discussions.
Another direction is the extension to curved spacetime
Liu et al. 2019
;
Hayata et al. 2021
,
and the study of its interplay with the quantum metric, which is relevant in cosmology and astrophysics.

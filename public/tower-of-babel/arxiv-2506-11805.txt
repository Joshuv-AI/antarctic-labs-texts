Abstract
In this paper, we establish a new global Hessian matrix estimate for heat-type equations on Riemannian manifolds using a Bismut-type Hessian formula. Our results feature fully explicit coefficients as well as delay / growth rate functions. These estimates yield two key applications: a novel backward weak Harnack inequality and a precise pointwise Hessian estimate for eigenfunctions.

Introduction

Let
(M,g)
be a
d
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with

\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits\geq-K,\quad K\geq 0,

(1.1)

and
u
be a positive solution of the heat equation:

\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x),

(1.2)

where
\Delta
is the usual Laplace-Beltrami operator with respect to the Levi-Civita connection of
M
.
Hamilton
[
8
]
proved the following weakened Harnack inequality for positive solutions to (
1.2
):

\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}\leq\left(2K+\frac{1}{t}\right)\log\frac{A}{u}

where
A:=\sup_{x\in M,t\geq 0}u(t,x)
. Such estimates play a fundamental role in geometric analysis, particularly in the proof of Liouville-type theorems (e.g., classifying bounded or ancient solutions) and the analysis of singularity formation in geometric flows.
Li
[
11
]
subsequently improved this result for solutions to the generalized heat equation

\partial_{t}u(t,x)=(\Delta+Z)u(t,x),

(1.3)

under the Bakry-Émery curvature condition
\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits_{Z}:=\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits-\nabla Z^{\flat}\geq-K
, where
Z
is a
C^{1}
vector field and
Z^{\flat}
is the dual 1-form of
Z
, given by
Z^{\flat}(v)=\langle Z,v\rangle
for
v\in TM
. The refined estimate

\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}\leq\frac{2K}{1-{\rm e}^{-2Kt}}\log\frac{A}{u}

(1.4)

provides a quantitative improvement over Hamilton’s bound, particularly through the optimized time-dependent coefficient.

For the second derivative of the heat equation, Hamilton
[
8
]
established a global lower bound for the Hessian matrices of log solutions to (
1.2
) under certain curvature assumptions, which yielded matix Harnack estimates of solutions at distinct spatial points for fixed time slices. Building on the ideas in
[
8
]
, Han and Zhang
[
9
]
proved the following Hessian bound:

\frac{\mathrm{Hess}(u)_{ij}}{u}\leq\left(\frac{5}{t}+B\right)\left(1+\log\frac{A}{u}\right),

(1.5)

where
A:=\sup_{x\in M,t\geq 0}u(t,x)
as previously defined,
\mathrm{Hess}(u)_{ij}
denotes Hessian components in an orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}
, and the constant
B
depends on the
L^{\infty}
-norms of the curvature tensor
\mathrm{Rm}
and its covariant derivative
\nabla\mathrm{Ric}
.
These results are further applied to derive a backward Harnack inequality. Furthermore, Li
[
13
]

generalized the Hessian matrix estimate to a positive solution to the following equation

\partial_{t}u=\Delta u+\langle\nabla V,\nabla u\rangle,

(1.6)

where
V
is a smooth potential function. More precisely, let
\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}{\text{\rm Ric}}}\nolimits_{V}=\mathrm{Ric}-\mathrm{Hess}(V)

and

\mathrm{Ric}_{V}^{m,d}:=\mathrm{Ric}-\mathrm{Hess}(V)-\frac{\nabla V\otimes\nabla V}{m-d},

(1.7)

where
m>d
denotes the synthetic dimension and
d
is the manifold dimension.
Then, under the modified curvature bound
\mathrm{Ric}_{V}^{m,d}\geq-K
with

\sup_{M}\left(|\mathrm{Rm}|+|\mathrm{Ric}_{V}|+|\nabla\mathrm{Ric}_{V}|+|V|^{2}\right)<\infty,

Li
[
13
]
established the following inequality:

\frac{\mathrm{Hess}(u)_{ij}}{u}\leq\left(\frac{5}{t}+B\right)\left(1+\log\frac{A}{u}\right),

(1.8)

with
A:=\sup_{x\in M,t\geq 0}u(t,x)
and constant
B=10m^{3/2}dK_{V}
. Here the effective curvature scale

K_{V}:=K_{1}+K_{2}+\sqrt{(K_{1}+K_{2})K+K_{2}+K_{1}+\sup_{M}|V|^{2}}

(1.9)

incorporates
K_{1}:=\sup_{M}(|\mathrm{Rm}|+|\mathrm{Ric}_{V}|)
and
K_{2}:=\sup_{M}|\nabla\mathrm{Ric}_{V}|
.

In this work, we consider quantitative matrix Hessian bounds on complete manifolds
(M,g)
without boundary via probabilistic methods.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
{u}
be a positive solution to the heat equation

(2.25)

Under the condition

, we have that for any

Corollary 3.1
.

Assume that the condition
(\mathbf{H})
holds.
Let

For

Proof.

1.1
and the inequality
\Delta u\leq d|\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits u|

\displaystyle-\partial_{t}\sqrt{\log\frac{A}{u}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2K}{3(1-{\rm e}^{-2Kt})}}\left[\frac{1}{4}K_{2}t+\frac{\sqrt{2t}}{2}K_{1}+\sqrt{\frac{3K}{1-{\rm e}^{-2Kt}}}+\left(K_{1}\sqrt{t}+\sqrt{\frac{6K}{1-{\rm e}^{-2Kt}}}\right)\sqrt{\log\frac{A}{u}}\right].

(3.4)

\displaystyle a_{t}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}d\left[\left(\frac{K_{2}\sqrt{2t}}{4}+K_{1}\right)\sqrt{\frac{2Kt}{3(1-{\rm e}^{-2Kt})}}+\frac{K}{1-{\rm e}^{-2Kt}}\right],

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}{\text{\rm Hess}}}\nolimits(\phi)_{ij}(x)}{\phi(x)}\leq\sqrt{\frac{2K}{3(1-{\rm e}^{-2KT})}\left(2\lambda T+\log\frac{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}{\phi(x)}\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\times\,\left[K_{2}T+2\left(\sqrt{2T}K_{1}+\sqrt{\frac{12K}{1-{\rm e}^{-2KT}}}\right)\left(1+\sqrt{2\left(2\lambda T+\log\frac{\|\phi\|_{\infty}}{\phi(x)}\right)}\right)\right].

Proof of Inequality (

).

For the case when

, we verify that:

\lim_{K\rightarrow 0}\frac{{\rm e}^{2TK}-1-2TK{\rm e}^{TK}}{K({\rm e}^{KT}-1)^{2}}-\frac{T}{3}=0.

When
K\neq 0
,
we need to prove

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm e}^{2TK}-1-2TK{\rm e}^{TK}}{K({\rm e}^{KT}-1)^{2}}-\frac{T}{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{(3-TK){\rm e}^{2TK}-3-4TK{\rm e}^{TK}-TK}{3K({\rm e}^{KT}-1)^{2}}\leq 0.

This reduces to analyzing the function

G(x):=(3-x){\rm e}^{2x}-3-4x{\rm e}^{x}-x,

where
x=TK
, with different inequality directions depending on the sign of
K
:

\displaystyle G(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(3-x){\rm e}^{2x}-3-4x{\rm e}^{x}-x\leq 0,&\text{when }K>0\,\text{and}\,x\geq 0;\\
(3-x){\rm e}^{2x}-3-4x{\rm e}^{x}-x\geq 0,&\text{when }K<0\,\text{and}\,x\leq 0.\end{cases}

(4.1)

Assume that
K>0
. Since
G(0)=G^{\prime}(0)=0
,
then to prove
G(x)\leq 0
for
x\geq 0
, it suffices to show that
G^{\prime\prime}(x)\leq 0
for
x\geq 0
. Computing
second derivative of
G
:

G^{\prime\prime}(x)=-4{\rm e}^{2x}+4(3-x){\rm e}^{2x}-4x{\rm e}^{x}-8{\rm e}^{x}=4{\rm e}^{x}\left((2-x){\rm e}^{x}-x-2\right),

we define
H(x):=(2-x){\rm e}^{x}-x-2
. Then it is easy to verify that
H(0)=H^{\prime}(0)=0
and for
x\geq 0
,

H^{\prime\prime}(x)=-x{\rm e}^{x}\leq 0,

which implies that
H(x)\leq 0
for
x>0
, and thus
G^{\prime\prime}(x)\leq 0
for
x\geq 0
.
Therefore, when
K>0
,
G(x)\leq 0
for
x\geq 0
.

Using a similar argument (considering
x\leq 0
)
we can prove the second inequality in (
4.1
) for the case

.

∎

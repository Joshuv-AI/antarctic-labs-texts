Calibration of the jet energy scale and resolution of small-radius jets using semileptonic t ​ t ¯ t\bar{t} events with the ATLAS detector
The ATLAS Collaboration

Abstract
A measurement of correction factors for the hadronic jet energy scale and resolution in the ATLAS detector is presented. These correction factors account for differences between simulated and observed data. They are obtained by analysing a selection of top quark events collected in proton–proton collisions by ATLAS between the years 2015 and 2018 at a centre-of-mass energy s = 13 \sqrt{s}=13 TeV as well as in 2022 and 2023 at s = 13.6 \sqrt{s}=13.6 TeV . The forward-folding technique is used to quantify the impact of different jet energy scale or resolution corrections on the reconstructed mass of the hadronically decaying W W boson from top-quark decays in simulation. The correction factors are extracted from a fit to the parameterised reconstructed W W -boson mass distribution to data. The energy scale and resolution corrections are measured as a function of the jet transverse momentum between 20 GeV and 200 GeV and absolute pseudorapidity less than 0.8. The uncertainties in the energy scale range from about 0.93% to about 1.7% for jets between 35 and 200 GeV , while for the energy resolution the uncertainties range from about 14% to 28%. The method presented will be used in conjunction with other techniques to further improve ATLAS jet energy scale and resolution precision.

Introduction

In quantum chromodynamics (QCD), the theory of strong interactions, the quarks and gluons carry colour charge. Because of the confinement properties of QCD, they cannot be detected directly but instead form collimated sprays of hadrons, called jets.
Jets are produced abundantly at the Large Hadron Collider (LHC)
[
1
]
and are an important signature for many processes.
The measurement of the jet energy plays a vital role in all jet-based analyses with the ATLAS detector, with the uncertainty in the energy calibrations often being one of the largest uncertainties in precision measurements
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
.

In ATLAS, the jet energy is evaluated through a series of corrections starting from the correction to the energy of the individual jet’s constituents
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
.
The calibration of the average jet transverse momentum (
p_{\text{T}}
) is called the ‘jet energy scale’ (JES) and the calibration of the
p_{\text{T}}
resolution is called the ‘jet energy resolution’ (JER).
The jet energy corrections are based on Monte Carlo (MC) simulations modelling the detector response and on assessing
in situ
how well the MC simulations describe data.
The corrections ensure that the reconstructed jet
p_{\text{T}}
equals, on average, the
p_{\text{T}}
of the corresponding particle-level jet, i.e. the event generator output after the hadronisation and before the detector simulation.
Several effects are corrected including the non-compensating nature of the ATLAS calorimeter (i.e. a lower response to hadrons than to electrons or photons), energy deposits outside jets and energy losses in dead material before and in-between the calorimeters.
The jet response is usually expressed as a function of variables, such as the energy fraction in the hadronic calorimeter or the number of charged-particle tracks associated to a jet, that are sensitive to jet fragmentation.

A subsequent set of
in situ
jet calibrations are applied to ensure that the jet
p_{\text{T}}
evaluated in data matches the simulation.
These corrections are obtained by exploiting the
p_{\text{T}}
-balance in certain event topologies where the jet recoils against a well-measured object like a photon or a
Z
boson decaying into electron or muon pairs (
Z/\gamma
+jet), dijet or multijet events, and by comparing the
p_{\text{T}}
-balance in simulation with that in data.

The ‘
Z/\gamma
+jet’ method was also used for JER measurement in previous ATLAS calibrations
[
7
]
. Currently, ATLAS JER measurements rely on two methods.
Firstly, the ‘dijet-balance’ method
[
10
]
, where the scalar balance of the
p_{\text{T}}
of the jets in dijet events, is exploited.
Secondly, the ‘random cones’ technique
[
10
]
, in which energy deposits in the calorimeter are summed in circular areas analogous to the jet area for anti-
k_{t}
jets
[
12
]
, providing the best precision for low
p_{\text{T}}
jets.

This paper focuses on a new technique to improve the current
in situ
calibration for the JES and JER by measuring jets in top-quark pair-production (
t\bar{t}
) events, where one of the top-quark decays includes a lepton from the
W
boson (semileptonic
t\bar{t}
events).
Semileptonic
t\bar{t}
events are chosen due to the presence of a hadronically decaying
W
boson whose lineshape distribution is sensitive to JES and JER.
Additionally, semileptonic
t\bar{t}
events contain a high-momentum charged lepton that provides a clear signal for the trigger to accept the events.
Finally, the
t\bar{t}
production cross-section is large at the LHC and the decay products provide a signature with an isolated lepton and
b
-jet in the final state that leads to an experimental signature with minimal background.
The ATLAS Collaboration previously used
t\bar{t}
events as a cross-check of the JES calibration
[
13
]
.
Moreover, several top-quark mass measurements performed by the ATLAS Collaboration exploited the
W
-boson lineshape distribution to control the JES uncertainties
[
14
,
15
,
5
]
, demonstrating the potential of
W
-boson mass distribution as a powerful probe of the jet response.
However, the obtained corrections to the JES in the previous top-quark mass measurements are not universal and cannot be generalised to other final states and selections.

This paper presents a measurement of the correction factors between simulation and data for the JES and the JER, targeting jets clustered with the anti-
k_{t}
algorithm with a radius parameter
R=0.4
using the proton–proton collision data collected by the ATLAS detector in Run 2, i.e. between the years 2015 and 2018 at a centre-of-mass energy of
\sqrt{s}=13\penalty\ \text{TeV}
and, separately, for data collected in Run 3 in the years 2022 and 2023 at
\sqrt{s}=13.6\penalty\ \text{TeV}

2
ATLAS detector

The ATLAS detector
[
17
,
18
]
at the LHC covers nearly the entire solid angle around the collision point.
It consists of an inner tracking detector surrounded by a thin superconducting solenoid, electromagnetic and hadronic calorimeters,
and a muon spectrometer incorporating three large superconducting air-core toroidal magnets.

The inner-detector system (ID) is immersed in a
2\text{\,}\mathrm{T}
axial magnetic field
and provides charged-particle tracking in the range of
|\eta|<2.5
.
The high-granularity silicon pixel detector covers the vertex region and typically provides four measurements per track,
the first hit generally being in the insertable B-layer (IBL).
It is followed by the semiconductor tracker (SCT), which usually provides eight measurements per track.
These silicon detectors are complemented by the transition radiation tracker (TRT),
which enables radially extended track reconstruction up to
|\eta|=2.0
.
The TRT also provides electron identification information
based on the fraction of hits (typically 30 in total) above a higher energy-deposit threshold corresponding to transition radiation.

The calorimeter system covers the pseudorapidity range
|\eta|<4.9
.
Within the region
|\eta|<3.2
, electromagnetic calorimetry is provided by barrel and
endcap high-granularity lead/liquid-argon (LAr) calorimeters,
with an additional thin LAr presampler covering
|\eta|<1.8

to correct for energy loss in material upstream of the calorimeters.
Hadronic calorimetry is provided by the steel/scintillator-tile calorimeter,
segmented into three barrel structures within
|\eta|<1.7
, and two copper/LAr hadronic endcap calorimeters.
The solid angle coverage is completed with forward copper/LAr and tungsten/LAr calorimeter modules
optimised for electromagnetic and hadronic energy measurements, respectively.

The muon spectrometer (MS) comprises separate trigger and
high-precision tracking chambers measuring the deflection of muons in a magnetic field generated by the superconducting air-core toroidal magnets.
The field integral of the toroids ranges between
2.0
and
6.0\text{\,}\mathrm{T}\text{\,}\mathrm{m}

across most of the detector.
Three layers of precision chambers, each consisting of layers of monitored drift tubes, cover the region
|\eta|<2.7
.
These were complemented in the innermost layer of the endcap region by cathode-strip chambers in Run 2,
which were replaced in Run 3 by layers of small-strip thin-gap chambers and Micromegas chambers, both providing precision tracking in the region
1.3<|\eta|<2.7
.
The muon trigger system covers the range
|\eta|<2.4
with resistive-plate chambers in the barrel region, thin-gap chambers in the endcap regions,
and, in Run 3, the small-strip thin-gap chambers and Micromegas chambers in the innermost layer of the endcap.

The luminosity is measured mainly by the LUCID–2 detector
[
19
]
that records Cherenkov light produced in the quartz windows of photomultipliers located close to the beampipe.

Events were selected by the first-level trigger system implemented in custom hardware,
followed by selections made by algorithms implemented in software in the high-level trigger
[
20
,
21
]
.
The first-level trigger accepted events from the
40\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}
bunch crossings at a rate close to
100\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}
,
which the high-level trigger further reduced in order to record complete events to disk at about
1.25\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}
and
3\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}
in Run 2 and Run 3, respectively.

The Run 3 detector configuration benefits from several upgrades compared with that of Run 2 to maintain high detector performance at the higher pile-up levels of Run 3.
The improvements include a new innermost layer of the muon spectrometer in the endcap region, which provides higher redundancy and a large reduction in fake muon triggers.
The trigger system also benefits from new digital electronics readout of the LAr calorimeters with significantly increased granularity.
Other updates and further details are provided in Ref.
[
18
]
.

A software suite
[
22
]
is used in data simulation, in the reconstruction
and analysis of real and simulated data, in detector operations, and in the trigger and data acquisition
systems of the experiment.

Z
bosons are allowed within
\Delta R=0.5
of the reconstructed jet.
In simulation, under these criteria, more than 90% of the selected jets with
p_{\text{T}}<150\penalty\ \text{GeV}
can be matched to the corresponding particle-level jets, while the fraction significantly drops for jets with
p_{\text{T}}>150\penalty\ \text{GeV}
to about 25% in the most extreme case, where most of the jets fail to match due to the isolation requirements of the particle-level jets.
Jets failing either the matching or isolation requirements are not included in forward-folding, and they appear unmodified in the
W
-boson mass distributions.
Changing the angular distance for the matching from
\Delta R<0.2
to
\Delta R<0.3
has no significant impact on the final results.

5
Event selection and
W
-boson mass reconstruction

Events are required to have at least four jets with
p_{\text{T}}>20\penalty\ \text{GeV}
and at least two jets need to be
b
-tagged and have
p_{\text{T}}>30\penalty\ \text{GeV}
.
Only events with exactly one electron or one muon with
p_{\text{T}}>27\penalty\ \text{GeV}
are selected.
The lepton
p_{\text{T}}
requirement is imposed to ensure the trigger efficiency is high.
Additionally, the events are required to have
E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}>30\penalty\ \text{GeV}
.
These criteria are imposed on all events
after
the application of the forward-folding procedure described in Section
4
for each folded set of jets, as the results of the folding can change the event selection decision.

The impact of the folding on
E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}
is estimated by propagating the difference between the folded and original jet four-momenta using the following equation

\vec{p_{\text{T}}}^{\text{\,miss, folded}}=\vec{p_{\text{T}}}^{\text{\,miss, original}}-\sum_{i}\left(\vec{p_{\text{T}}}^{\text{\,jet folded,i}}-\vec{p_{\text{T}}}^{\text{\,jet original,i}}\right),

with the missing transverse momentum vector
\vec{p_{\text{T}}}^{\text{miss}}
and jet transverse momentum vector
\vec{p_{\text{T}}}^{\text{\,jet}}
of jet
i
.

A simple permutational algorithm is used to identify the two jets from the hadronically decaying
W
boson.
From all permutations of reconstructed jets, those that give the best agreement with the expected top-quark and
W
-boson masses—quantified by the
\chi^{2}
value defined below—are selected

\chi^{2}=\frac{(m_{t}^{\text{reco}}-m_{t}^{\text{gen}})^{2}}{\Sigma_{t}^{2}}+\frac{(m_{W}^{\text{reco}}-m_{W}^{\text{gen}})^{2}}{\Sigma_{W}^{2}}.

(2)

Here
m_{t}^{\text{reco}}
represents the invariant mass of the three jet system from the top-quark decay,
m_{t}^{\text{gen}}
represents a fixed constant for the top-quark mass set to 172.5
GeV
and the constant
\Sigma_{t}
represents the decay width of the top quark as well as the approximate experimental resolution and is set to 35
GeV
.
A systematic uncertainty varying the top-quark mass is considered as described in Section
6
.
Similarly,
m_{W}^{\text{reco}}
represents the invariant mass of the two jet system from the
W
-boson decay,
m_{W}^{\text{gen}}
represents the
W
-boson mass set to 80.38
GeV
and
\Sigma_{W}
represents the decay width and resolution of the
W
boson and is set to 25
GeV
.
The
\chi^{2}
value is evaluated for permutations of up to six jets.
If there are more than six jets in an event, at least two
b
-tagged jets (ordered in
p_{\text{T}}
) are always considered and then up to four other jets (ordered in
p_{\text{T}}
) are considered.
For the permutations, within each top-quark combination, the jet not included in the
W
boson must be
b
-tagged and permutations corresponding to the same top-quark or
W
-boson combinations are only considered once.
The permutation with the best (lowest)
\chi^{2}
value is chosen.
Figure
1
shows the distribution of the
\chi^{2}
value for the best permutations in the Run 2 data sample without the JES
in situ
corrections applied.
Good agreement between the prediction and data can be seen within the uncertainties.
To improve the matching purity, only events for which the best permutation has
\chi^{2}<1
are considered.
This requirement removes about half of the pre-selected events satisfying all the previous criteria except the
\chi^{2}
requirement.
Changing the parameters
\Sigma_{t}
and
\Sigma_{W}
in Eq. (
2
) to 15 and 10
GeV
, respectively, and changing the
\chi^{2}
selection to keep the same selection efficiency, does not impact the matching purity significantly.

Additionally, the reconstructed jets identified to come from the hadronically decaying
W
boson are required to be well isolated.
For the reconstructed jets, a different isolation procedure compared to the particle-level jets is employed.
A jet isolation variable
f_{\text{iso}}
is defined by

f_{\text{iso}}=\frac{p_{\text{T}}^{\text{iso}}-\rho A_{\text{iso}}}{p_{\text{T}}^{\text{jet}}-\rho A_{\text{jet}}},

where
p_{\text{T}}^{\text{iso}}
represents the scalar sum of the
p_{\text{T}}
of all inputs to the jet algorithm within an isolation annulus with inner and outer radii of 0.4 and 0.5. The ratio of momenta is calculated after subtracting the pile-up contribution, which is obtained by multiplying the jet active area
A_{\text{jet}}
and the area of the isolation annulus
A_{\text{iso}}=\pi\cdot 0.5^{2}-A_{\text{jet}}
by the pile-up density
\rho

[
85
]
.
The jets from the
W
-boson decay are required to have
f_{\text{iso}}<0.3
to select well isolated jets.
An alternative isolation requirement based on the angular distance between the closest reconstructed jets is found to give consistent results with the isolation variable requirement.

illustrates the reconstructed
W
-boson mass distribution in Run 2 and Run 3 after applying all the selection criteria including the
\chi^{2}<1
requirement in data without the
in situ
jet calibration corrections applied.
There is good agreement between the prediction and data within the uncertainties.

6
Systematic uncertainties

Several systematic uncertainties can affect the measurement of the JES and JER parameters.
Uncertainties affecting leptons,
E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}
and
b
-tagged jets can impact the measurement due to the acceptance effects, while the uncertainties in the modelling can also affect the
W
-boson lineshape.

The uncertainties in the electron and muon identification, isolation, reconstruction, track-to-vertex association and trigger scale factors measured using
Z\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
and
Z\to e^{+}e^{-}

[
71
,
73
,
27
,
26
]
are evaluated.
Uncertainties related to the electron (muon) energy (momentum) scale and resolution are also evaluated as estimated by using resonance decays
[
71
,
72
]
.

The uncertainty in the efficiency of the jet-vertex tagger algorithm is evaluated via the variation of the corrections applied to the simulation.
The JES and JER uncertainties from the best available calibration are estimated by using the Run 2 data sample
[
76
]
.
The same uncertainties are applied to the Run 3 based simulation, and are validated using Run 3 data.
Additional uncertainties are considered for the Run 3 simulation to cover the residual differences between data and simulation.

The impact of the JER uncertainties is evaluated for the JES correction and the impact of the JES uncertainties is evaluated for the JER correction.
The uncertainties in the efficiency of
b
-tagging are evaluated via the variation of the scale factors affecting the
b
-/
c
-/light-flavour (in)efficiencies, using measurements similar to Refs.
[
78
,
79
,
80
]
.

The uncertainty from the leptons and jets is propagated to the
E_{\text{T}}^{\text{miss}}
.
Additionally, a dedicated set of uncertainties from the soft term is evaluated, varying the magnitude and the direction of the missing transverse momentum vector.

Several sources affecting the modelling of the
t\bar{t}
process are evaluated, affecting the production or decay of the top quarks.
Variations of the renormalisation and factorisation scale in the ME by factors of 0.5 and 2 are evaluated separately.
Uncertainty in the initial-state radiation (ISR) is estimated by using the
Var3c
variation of the
Pythia
8 A14 tune
[
49
]
.
Variations of the renormalisation scale in the strong coupling constant in the final-state radiation (
\alpha_{\text{s}}^{\text{FSR}}
) by factors of 0.5 and 2 of the A14 tune are evaluated.
The uncertainty related to the
Powheg
to
Pythia
matching is estimated by comparing with a dedicated
Powheg+Pythia
8 sample with the
p_{\text{T}}^{\text{hard}}
parameter set to one instead of the default value of zero.
The uncertainty in the choice of the
h_{\text{damp}}
parameter is estimated by comparing with a dedicated
Powheg+Pythia
8 sample produced with twice the nominal
h_{\text{damp}}
value (
h_{\text{damp}}
= 3
m_{\text{t}}
).
Due to a known mismodelling of the top-quark
p_{\text{T}}
distribution, an uncertainty estimated from reweighting the top-quark
p_{\text{T}}
to NNLO QCD predictions
[
86
]
is evaluated as a proxy for missing higher order predictions in the kinematics.
This uncertainty is found to have a negligible impact on the JES or JER estimate in the Run 2 analysis and is thus not considered further.
The impact of using a different parton shower and hadronisation model on the Run 2 (Run 3) analysis is evaluated by comparing the nominal
t\bar{t}
sample with another sample generated with
Powheg
interfaced to
Herwig
7.2.1
[
87
,
88
,
89
]
(
Herwig
7.2.3), using the
Herwig
7.1 default set of tuned parameters
[
89
]
and the
MMHT2014lo
PDF set
[
90
]
.
The
Powheg
settings are the same as for the nominal sample.
To estimate the uncertainty in the colour reconnection (CR), samples were generated with three models: CR0 — a multiple-parton-interactions (MPI) based model, CR1 — a QCD-based model and CR2 — a gluon-move based model
[
91
]
.
The uncertainty is estimated by comparing the CR1 and CR2 models with the prediction of CR0 and taking the larger uncertainty.
Variations in the top-quark mass of 0.5
GeV
are evaluated by using dedicated samples with
m_{\text{top}}=172.0\penalty\ \text{GeV}
and
m_{\text{top}}=173.0\penalty\ \text{GeV}
.
Variation in the MPI and underlying event (UE) modelling (
Var1
variation in the A14 tune) is estimated with samples with UE variations in
Pythia
.
The modelling of secondary gluon emissions from quarks originating from coloured resonance decays can affect both the
b
-quark and
W
-boson kinematics as well as the
b
-jet fragmentation.
To assess the impact of this choice on the measurement, an alternative sample with the recoil model within
Pythia
8 changed to
recoilToTop
was produced.
The uncertainty in the PDF estimate for the
t\bar{t}
process is assessed by evaluating the impact of the PDF4LHC
[
92
,
93
]
variations.
Since the
W
bosons decay into an
s
-quark and a
c
-quark in about half of the hadronic decays, a variation of the Lund–Bowler
c
-fragmentation function
[
94
,
95
]
is tested, but it is found to have a negligible impact.

The uncertainty due to the choice of the parton shower generator for the
tW
process is estimated by comparing the nominal
Powheg+Pythia
8 sample with
Powheg
interfaced to the
Herwig
sample.
The variations in the ME,
Var3c
and
\alpha_{\text{s}}^{\text{FSR}}
are evaluated analogously to the
t\bar{t}
case.
The uncertainty in the overlap between the
t\bar{t}
and
tW
diagrams is assessed by comparing the nominal prediction using the diagram-removal approach to the diagram-subtraction approach
[
43
]
in an alternative sample.

Uncertainties in the prediction of the cross-section of the various processes are taken into account although these are expected to have only a small impact on the measured
W
-boson line shape.
A 5% uncertainty is considered for the
t\bar{t}
and single-top processes following the uncertainty in the predicted cross-section
[
35
,
36
,
37
,
38
,
39
,
40
,
41
,
96
]
. A conservative 50% uncertainty is assigned to the
V
+jets and
VV
+jets, and 100% uncertainty is applied to the fake-lepton estimate.
These uncertainties in the predicted cross-section have a negligible impact on the measured corrections for the JES and JER.

7
Results

The mean values
\mu
of the
W
-boson mass distributions in the
p_{\text{T}}
regions defined in Section
5
are used to extract the JES parameters from a fit to data.
Similarly, the standard deviation
\sigma
of the
W
-boson mass distribution is used for the JER measurement.
The
\mu
(
\sigma
) of each forward-folded
W
-boson mass distribution is extracted for each value of the
s
(
r
) parameters from the forward-folding Eq. (
1
) and in the case of the off-diagonal regions, for each combination of the
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
(
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
) parameters of the JES (JER) estimate.
Due to the
\chi^{2}<1
selection, the minimum and the maximum of the reconstructed
W
-boson mass distribution is bounded to the range
m_{W}^{\text{gen}}\pm 25

GeV
, thus the
\sigma
extraction does not suffer from outliers.
In the diagonal regions the obtained
\mu
(
\sigma
) are fitted with a linear (quadratic) function
\mathcal{F}(s)
(
\mathcal{F}(r)
).
In the off-diagonal regions, the
\mu
(
\sigma
) are fitted with a quadratic function
\mathcal{F}(s_{1},s_{2})
(
\mathcal{F}(r_{1},r_{2})
) where
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
(
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
) are the forward-folding formula parameters for the leading and sub-leading jet identified to originate from the
W
-boson decay.
The diagonal parameterisation function
\mathcal{F}(s)
and off-diagonal parameterisation function
\mathcal{F}(s_{1},s_{2})
share a set of six parameters
s_{i}
, one for each reconstructed jet
p_{\text{T}}
bin, and analogously for the JER parameterisation.
For the JES parameterisation, most fitted points are within one standard deviation from the fitted function with only one point slightly above two standard deviation, with the standard deviation representing the uncertainty due to the finite number of simulated events.
For the JER parameterisation, most fitted points are within one standard deviation with one point around 1.5 standard deviations away from the fitted function.
Additionally, a dedicated uncertainty is assigned for the imperfect parameterisation and for neglecting possible correlations between the parameters in the
\mathcal{F}
functions due to region migrations of simulated events when varying the JES and JER corrections and it is referred to as ‘non-closure’ uncertainty.
Due to the low statistical power of the separation of the various JER smeared templates in the diagonal region with jet
p_{\text{T}}
between 150 and 200
GeV
, this region is not included in the JER correction measurement.

\sigma
for the lower
p_{\text{T}}
jet compared to the higher

jet originates from the naturally lower resolution for the lower
p_{\text{T}}
jets due to the detector’s calorimeter response.

Once the parameterisations
\mathcal{F}
are obtained, a maximum-likelihood fit to
\mu
(
\sigma
) in data is performed to extract the values of the JES (JER) parameters for all the regions simultaneously.
The likelihood is defined as

\mathcal{L}\left(\vec{\mu}|\vec{s}\right)=\prod_{i\in\text{reg}}G\left[\mu_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i}(\vec{s}),\sigma_{\mu_{i}}\right],

where
\vec{\mu}
is the vector of
\mu
(
\sigma
) values measured in data,
\vec{s}
is the vector of the scale (resolution) parameters to be estimated,
G
represents the Gaussian function,
\mathcal{F}
is the parameterisation of
\mu
(
\sigma
) values as described above and
\sigma_{\mu_{i}}
is the data statistical uncertainty in the
\mu
(
\sigma
) values in region
i
for the JES (JER) estimate.

The systematic uncertainties in the measurement of the JES and JER correction factors are estimated by shifting the expected distributions as summarised in Section
6
, fitting the expected distributions with the nominal model and evaluating the impact on the best-fit parameters.
The parameterisation ‘non-closure’ uncertainty is estimated by fitting the distributions representing a variation of up to 5% in JES and up to 25% in JER, and comparing the best-fit values with the
s
and
r
values used to generate the given varied distribution.
The final ‘non-closure’ uncertainty in each
p_{\text{T}}
bin is estimated as the maximal deviation from the input value for all variations and is symmetrised.

Due to the non-Gaussian nature of the uncertainties, stemming from the non-linear parameterisation, pseudo-experiments are used to obtain the uncertainty band.

The JER uncertainties are dominated by the difference between
Pythia
and
Herwig
that accounts for most of the total uncertainty.
The difference can be traced to the large difference between the predictions of the
W
-boson lineshape in the generator output before the detector simulation and only part of the difference comes from the difference between the jet responses.
The difference between the generators resembles resolution effects, thus the difference is highly correlated with the JER effects, resulting in a large uncertainty from the comparison of the two parton-shower generators.

8
Conclusion

A data-based calibration of the small-radius jet energy scale and resolution using
t\bar{t}
events with the ATLAS detector is presented.
The forward-folding technique is used to generate templates for the invariant mass of hadronically decaying
W
bosons that are then compared with the distribution in data.
Two separate measurements are made, one for the full Run 2 data sample of the LHC at
\sqrt{s}=13\penalty\ \text{TeV}
corresponding to an integrated luminosity of
140\penalty\ \mbox{fb${}^{-1}$}
and the other for the LHC Run 3 data sample collected in years 2022 and 2023, corresponding to an integrated luminosity of
52\penalty\ \mbox{fb${}^{-1}$}
.
The extracted values for correction of the energy scale and resolution are binned in the jet transverse momentum ranging from 20 to 200
GeV
.
This technique provides a precision that is competitive with the established calibration techniques for jet
p_{\text{T}}>35\penalty\ \text{GeV}
.
However, analyses with a similar final state, especially those using observables correlated with the
W
mass distribution as presented here, such as top-quark mass measurements, need to carefully evaluate whether this calibration can be applied to their measurements.
The dominant uncertainties for the JES and JER calibration originate from the modelling of the final-state radiation and parton shower in the
t\bar{t}
process.
If the dominant
t\bar{t}
modelling uncertainties are reduced in the future, a precision of about 0.5% for the jet energy scale and about 10% for the jet energy resolution could be reached using this
t\bar{t}
calibration technique on its own.
The results will be combined with the standard ATLAS calibration techniques.

Sonography of thyroid involvement in Langerhans cell histiocytosis
Authors: Yui Yamashita, Yumiko Sasai, Keiko Yamagami, Naotetsu Kanamoto
Published: 2026

Image legend

A 28-year-old woman with multisystem Langerhans cell histiocytosis (LCH) involving the liver and pituitary gland presented with hypothyroidism: thyrotropin, 11.1 μIU/mL (SI: 11.1 mIU/L) (reference range, 0.05-5.00 μIU/mL [SI: 0.05-5.00 mIU/L]); free thyroxine, 0.6 ng/dL (SI: 7.7 pmol/L) (reference range, 0.9-1.7 ng/dL [SI: 11.6-21.9 pmol/L]). Thyroid ultrasonography revealed multiple irregular hypoechoic areas (arrows; A and B, transverse views; C and D, longitudinal views). Color Doppler ultrasonography indicated only scant intralesional vascular flow within the hypoechoic areas (A and B). Fine-needle aspiration cytology revealed cells with clear cytoplasm and indented nuclei (E; ×600). Immunohistochemical staining confirmed LCH involvement of the thyroid, with strong positivity for CD1a (F; ×600) and Langerin (G; ×600), both of which are highly specific for Langerhans cells and have high diagnostic value. This case highlights the classic diagnostic hallmarks of thyroid LCH, namely, irregular hypoechoic lesions, faint vascularity, and an absence of calcification [1, 2]. Given that thyroid involvement can occur in multisystem LCH [2], these features, although not individually pathognomonic, should heighten suspicion of LCH thyroid involvement and guide clinicians toward fine-needle aspiration cytology, which can yield a definitive diagnosis.

Bailey Pairs and an Identity of Chern–Li–Stanton–Xue–Yee
Shashank KANADE a and Jeremy LOVEJOY b

Abstract
We show how Bailey pairs can be used to give a simple proof of an identity of Chern, Li, Stanton, Xue, and Yee. The same method yields a number of related identities as well as false theta companions.

Introduction

Recall the usual
q
-series notation,

(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k})_{\infty}=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k};q)_{\infty}=\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}\bigl(1-a_{1}q^{j}\bigr)\bigl(1-a_{2}q^{j}\bigr)\cdots\bigl(1-a_{k}q^{j}\bigr)

and

(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k})_{n}=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k};q)_{n}=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\bigl(1-a_{1}q^{j}\bigr)\bigl(1-a_{2}q^{j}\bigr)\cdots\bigl(1-a_{k}q^{j}\bigr),

valid for
n\geq 0
, along with the
q
-binomial coefficient

\begin{bmatrix}n\\
k\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}n\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{q}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{(q)_{n}}{(q)_{n-k}(q)_{k}}&\text{if $0\leq k\leq n$},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(1.1)

In a recent study of
q
-series and partitions related to Ariki–Koike algebras, Chern, Li, Stanton, Xue, and Yee
[
8
]
established the following family of
q
-multisum identities.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
m\geq 1
and
0\leq a\leq m-1
. Then we have

\sum_{n_{m},\dots,n_{1}\geq 0}\frac{q^{\binom{n_{m}+1}{2}+\cdots+\binom{n_{1}+1}{2}}}{(q)_{n_{m}}}\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\begin{bmatrix}n_{i+1}+\delta_{a,i}\\
n_{i}\end{bmatrix}=\frac{\bigl(q^{a+1},q^{m+1-a},q^{m+2};q^{m+2}\bigr)_{\infty}}{(q)_{\infty}\bigl(q;q^{2}\bigr)_{\infty}}.

(1.2)

This generalizes a classical identity in the theory of partitions
[
4
, equation (2.26),
t=q
]
,

\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{\binom{n+1}{2}}}{(q)_{n}}=\frac{1}{\bigl(q;q^{2}\bigr)_{\infty}}=(-q)_{\infty}.

The proof of (
1.2
) in
[
8
]
is lengthy and impressive, involving a symmetry property, a
q
-binomial coefficient multisum transformation formula, and two identities of Andrews
[
5
]
and Kim–Yee
[
14
]
.

In the first part of this paper, we give a streamlined proof of (
1.2
) using the Bailey pair machinery. All that we require are the classical Bailey lemma and Bailey lattice along with a Bailey-type lemma from
[
16
]
– see Lemma
2.3
. In fact, we establish a much more general result, which allows us to prove (
1.2
) and many more families of identities like it.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})
is a Bailey pair relative to
q
and
f_{n}
is a sequence defined for all integers
n
which satisfies

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}=\dfrac{1-q^{2n+1}}{1-q}f_{n},\qquad n\geq 0

(1.3)

and

\displaystyle q^{n}f_{n}=-q^{-n-1}f_{-n-1},\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z},

(1.4)

then for all
m\geq 1
and
0\leq a\leq m
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{m},\dots,n_{1}\geq 0}\frac{q^{\binom{n_{m}+1}{2}+\cdots+\binom{n_{1}+1}{2}}\bigl(q^{2};q^{2}\bigr)_{n_{1}+\delta_{a,0}}}{(q)_{n_{m}}}\beta_{n_{1}+\delta_{a,0}}\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\begin{bmatrix}n_{i+1}+\delta_{a,i}\\
n_{i}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\dfrac{(-q)_{\infty}}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}q^{m\binom{n}{2}+an}f_{n}.

(1.5)

Note that using (
1.4
), the condition (
1.3
) could be written alternatively as:

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}=\dfrac{f_{n}+f_{-n-1}}{1-q},\qquad n\geq 0.

Theorem
1.1
now follows using the classical Bailey pair relative to
q

[
20
, E(3)]
,

\alpha_{n}=\frac{1-q^{2n+1}}{1-q}(-1)^{n}q^{n^{2}}

(1.6)

and

\beta_{n}=\frac{1}{\bigl(q^{2};q^{2}\bigr)_{n}},

(1.7)

together with the triple product identity (
2.2
). Other Bailey pairs lead to similar families of identities, and we give a number of examples in Section
3
– see Theorems
3.2
,
3.3
, and
3.5
.

In the second part of the paper, we prove a result similar to Theorem
1.2
but involving false theta functions instead of theta functions. To state it, we define the function

{\rm sgn}(n)=\begin{cases}\hphantom{-}1&\text{if $n\geq 0$},\\
-1&\text{if $n<0$}.\end{cases}

Theorem 1.3
.

If
(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})
is a Bailey pair relative to
q
and
f_{n}
is as in (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), then for all
m\geq 1
and
0\leq a\leq m
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{m},\dots,n_{1}\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n_{m}}q^{\binom{n_{m}+1}{2}+\cdots+\binom{n_{1}+1}{2}}\bigl(q^{2};q^{2}\bigr)_{n_{1}+\delta_{a,0}}}{(-q)_{n_{m}}}\beta_{n_{1}+\delta_{a,0}}\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\begin{bmatrix}n_{i+1}+\delta_{a,i}\\
n_{i}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm sgn}(-n)(-1)^{n}q^{m\binom{n}{2}+an}f_{n}&\text{if $0\leq a\leq m-1$},\\
\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm sgn}(n)(-1)^{n}q^{m\binom{n+1}{2}}f_{n}&\text{if $a=m$}.\end{cases}

(1.8)

Each family of identities arising from Theorem
1.2
then has a false theta counterpart using Theorem
1.3
. In the case of (
1.2
), using (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) this is the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

For
m\geq 1
and
0\leq a\leq m-1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{m},\dots,n_{1}\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n_{m}}q^{\binom{n_{m}+1}{2}+\cdots+\binom{n_{1}+1}{2}}}{(-q)_{n_{m}}}\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\begin{bmatrix}n_{i+1}+\delta_{a,i}\\
n_{i}\end{bmatrix}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm sgn}(-n)q^{(m+2)\binom{n}{2}+(a+1)n}.

The base case
m=1
and
a=0
is the well-known

\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{\binom{n+1}{2}}}{(-q)_{n}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{n(3n+1)/2}\bigl(1-q^{2n+1}\bigr).

See Theorems
4.5
–
4.7
for further examples.

In the final part of the paper we are motivated by the series
\mathscr{S}_{m,a}
, defined for
m\geq 1
and
{0\leq a\leq m}
by

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}_{m,a}=\sum_{n_{m},\dots,n_{1}\geq 0}\frac{q^{n_{m}^{2}+\cdots+n_{1}^{2}}}{\bigl(q^{2};q^{2}\bigr)_{n_{m}}}\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\begin{bmatrix}n_{i+1}+\delta_{a,i}\\
n_{i}\end{bmatrix}_{q^{2}}.

These are naturally dilated versions of the series in (
1.2
), and it is known that we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}_{m,0}=\mathscr{S}_{m,m}=\dfrac{\bigl(-q;q^{2}\bigr)_{\infty}}{\bigl(q^{2};q^{2}\bigr)_{\infty}}\bigl(q^{m+1},q^{m+3},q^{2m+4};q^{2m+4}\bigr)_{\infty}.

(1.9)

See
[
22
, Corollary 1.5 (b)]
for general

and
[
7
, Section 5]
for
m
even. While it appears that the
\mathscr{S}_{m,a}
are not infinite products for
a\not\in\{0,m\}
, we prove the following result on the differences of these series.

Theorem 1.5
.

For
1\leq a\leq m
, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}_{m,a}-\mathscr{S}_{m,a-1}=q^{a}\dfrac{\bigl(-q;q^{2}\bigr)_{\infty}}{\bigl(q^{2};q^{2}\bigr)_{\infty}}\bigl(q^{m+1-2a},q^{m+3+2a},q^{2m+4};q^{2m+4}\bigr)_{\infty}.

This and several similar families of identities will follow as special cases of our third main theorem.

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose that
(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})
is a Bailey pair relative to
1
and that there is a sequence
g_{n}
, defined for all integers
n
, with
g_{0}=1
,

\alpha_{n}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $n=0$},\\
(1+q^{n})g_{n}&\text{if $n>1$},\end{cases}

(1.10)

and

g_{-n}=q^{n}g_{n}.

(1.11)

Then for all
m\geq 0
and
1\leq a\leq m
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{m},\dots,n_{1}\geq 0}\frac{q^{\frac{1}{2}(n_{m}^{2}+n_{m-1}^{2}+\cdots+n_{1}^{2})}\bigl(-q^{\frac{1}{2}},q\bigr)_{n_{1}}}{(q)_{n_{m}}}\beta_{n_{1}}\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\begin{bmatrix}n_{i+1}+\delta_{a,i}\\
n_{i}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}-\sum_{n_{m},\dots,n_{1}\geq 0}\frac{q^{\frac{1}{2}(n_{m}^{2}+n_{m-1}^{2}+\cdots+n_{1}^{2})}\bigl(-q^{\frac{1}{2}},q\bigr)_{n_{1}+\delta_{a-1,0}}}{(q)_{n_{m}}}\beta_{n_{1}+\delta_{a-1,0}}\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\begin{bmatrix}n_{i+1}+\delta_{a-1,i}\\
n_{i}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=-q^{\frac{a-1}{2}}\frac{\bigl(-q^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)_{\infty}}{(q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}q^{\frac{m}{2}n^{2}+na-n}g_{n+1}.

(1.12)

Note that with (
1.11
), the condition (
1.10
) could be written alternatively as

\alpha_{n}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $n=0$},\\
g_{n}+g_{-n}&\text{if $n>1$}.\end{cases}

3
, we prove Theorem
1.2
and give several applications, including Theorem
1.1
. In Section
4
, we prove Theorem

and give false theta companions for each family of identities in Section

Wherever possible, we include historical remarks about the “base cases” of our identities.

While it may be striking that identities involving products of

\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\begin{bmatrix}n_{i+1}+\delta_{a,i}\\

(1.13)

2
Bailey pairs

In this section, we review the necessary background on Bailey pairs. A Bailey pair relative to
x

[
3
]
is a pair of sequences
(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})
,

n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
satisfying

\beta_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\alpha_{k}}{(q)_{n-k}(xq)_{n+k}}.

Note that in the limit, this gives (subject to convergence conditions)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\beta_{n}=\frac{1}{(q,xq)_{\infty}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{k}.

(2.1)

In practice, the above sum often becomes an infinite product by an appeal to the Jacobi triple product identity
[
4
, equation (2.2.10)]
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{n}z^{n}q^{n^{2}}=\bigl(q^{2},zq,z^{-1}q;q^{2}\bigr)_{\infty},

(2.2)

or the quintuple product identity
[
9
]
,

\displaystyle\Biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n\equiv 0\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,3)\end{subarray}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n\equiv 2\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,3)\end{subarray}}\Biggr)z^{n}q^{\frac{1}{3}\binom{n+1}{2}}=\bigl(q,zq,z^{-1}\bigr)_{\infty}\bigl(z^{2}q,z^{-2}q;q^{2}\bigr)_{\infty}.

(2.3)

The most important fact about Bailey pairs is the following, which is known as the Bailey lemma. From a given Bailey pair relative to
x
, it produces new Bailey pairs relative to
x
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
3
]
)
.

If
(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})
is a Bailey pair relative to
x
, then so is
(\alpha_{n}^{\prime},\beta_{n}^{\prime})
, where

\alpha_{n}^{\prime}=\frac{(b,c)_{n}(xq/bc)^{n}}{(xq/b,xq/c)_{n}}\alpha_{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\beta_{n}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{(xq/b,xq/c)_{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{(b,c)_{j}(xq/bc)_{n-j}(xq/bc)^{j}}{(q)_{n-j}}\beta_{j}.

A result similar to Lemma
2.1
takes a Bailey pair relative to
x
and produces Bailey pairs relative to
x/q
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
1
]
)
.

If
(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})
is a Bailey pair relative to
x
, then
(\alpha_{n}^{\prime},\beta_{n}^{\prime})
is a Bailey pair relative to
x/q
, where

\alpha_{n}^{\prime}=(1-x)\Bigl(\frac{x}{bc}\Bigr)\frac{(b,c)_{n}(x/bc)^{n}}{(x/b,x/c)_{n}}\biggl(\frac{\alpha_{n}}{1-xq^{2n}}-\frac{xq^{2n-2}\alpha_{n-1}}{1-xq^{2n-2}}\biggr)

and

\beta_{n}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{(x/b,x/c)_{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{(b,c)_{j}(x/bc)_{n-j}(x/bc)^{j}}{(q)_{n-j}}\beta_{j}.

Here by convention we take

\alpha_{-1}=0.

(2.4)

Finally, we have two lemmas that change a Bailey pair relative to
x
to one relative to
xq
. The first will be key to introducing
q
-binomial coefficients of the form
\bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}n_{i+1}+1\\
n_{i}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]
. The second is not needed for the proof of Theorem
1.1
but will be used to establish a certain Bailey pair later in the paper.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
16
]
)
.

If
(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})
is a Bailey pair relative to
x
, then
(\alpha_{n}^{\prime},\beta_{n}^{\prime})
is a Bailey pair relative to
xq
, where

\alpha_{n}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{1-xq}\biggl(\frac{1-q^{n+1}}{1-xq^{2n+2}}\alpha_{n+1}+\frac{q^{n}(1-xq^{n})}{1-xq^{2n}}\alpha_{n}\biggr)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\beta_{n}^{\prime}=\bigl(1-q^{n+1}\bigr)\beta_{n+1}.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
15
]
)
.

If
(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})
is a Bailey pair relative to
x
, then
(\alpha_{n}^{\prime},\beta_{n}^{\prime})
is a Bailey pair relative to
xq
, where

\alpha_{n}^{\prime}=\frac{\bigl(1-xq^{2n+1}\bigr)(xq/b)_{n}(-b)^{n}q^{\binom{n}{2}}}{(1-xq)(bq)_{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{(b)_{j}}{(xq/b)_{j}}(-b)^{-j}q^{-\binom{j}{2}}\alpha_{j}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\beta_{n}^{\prime}=\frac{(b)_{n}}{(bq)_{n}}\beta_{n}.

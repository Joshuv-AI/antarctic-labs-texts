The g − 2 g-2 in the neutral Electroweak model with cutoff: convergent expansion, RG and the Jackiw-Weinberg formula
Vieri Mastropietro, Michele Bianchessi

Abstract
The prediction of the anomalous gyromagnetic factor of the electron, started with the evaluation of the electromagnetic contribution by Schwinger (1948) and of the weak contribution by Jackiw and Weinberg (1972), is one of the major successes of Quantum Field Theory and the Standard Model. The results obtained truncating the series are in spectacular agreement with experiments. Yet, a mathematical justification and an estimate of the truncation error are problematic, being such series diverging and not asymptotic to any QFT. For a non perturbative result, one has to consider the Standard Model as an effective theory valid up to certain energy scales. In this paper we consider the neutral sector of the Electroweak model with a momentum cutoff; we rigorously prove that the anomalous gyromagnetic factor in the effective regularized theory coincides with the Jackiw-Weinberg result, obtained by the truncation of the formal expansion with no cutoffs (whose sum is not expected to exist), up to a regularization-dependent correction which is subdominant in the weak coupling regime if the cutoff is smaller than the inverse coupling and larger than the boson mass. The proof is based on a convergent expansions and Renormalization Group (RG) methods; cancellations based on exact and approximated symmetries are needed to get lowest order dominance.

Introduction and main results

1.1
The anomalous gyromagnetic factor

A spin-
1/2
fermion with mass
m
and charge
e
has an intrinsic magnetic dipole moment
\bm{\mu}=-g(e/2m)\mathbf{S}
, where
\mathbf{S}
is the spin three-vector and
g
a dimensionless number known as
gyromagnetic factor
. The quantity

\mathfrak{a}\equiv(g-2)/2
is called
anomalous gyromagnetic factor
; it receives nonzero contributions from all the fundamental interactions
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}_{\textsc{qed}}+\mathfrak{a}_{\textup{weak}}+\mathfrak{a}_{\textsc{qcd}}
and it can be computed in the Standard Model, the theory providing the more fundamental description of elementary particles.
The current theoretical value of
\mathfrak{a}
for the electron, obtained as result of decades of computations, is
\mathfrak{a}_{e}=0.0011596521817877\dots
, while its experimental value is
\mathfrak{a}_{e}^{\textsc{exp}}=0.00115965218059(13)

[
1
]
. In the case of the muon, the agreement is also impressive
but a bit less precise
[
2
]
. Such results provide a very stringent
test for the validity of the Standard Model, and possible differences could give an insight on new physical phenomena.

The first theoretical prediction about the
g
-factor is due to Dirac, who obtained the result
g=2,\mathfrak{a}=0
in 1928
[
3
]
. Experimental deviations from this value were attributed to quantum radiative contributions due to the interaction with the electromagnetic field.
Their evaluation was problematic due to the appearence
of diverging integrals (“infinities”) in the computations due to the large energy behavior, and
it was finally achieved using the theory of renormalization.
Such a procedure is based on the fact that the parameters appearing in the model (called
bare parameters
), are not the observed ones (called
dressed parameters
), as they also aquire radiative corrections. It turns out that
one can absorb all the divergences in the bare parameters, thus
expressing the anomalous gyromagnetic factor as a series in terms of the dressed ones.
The corrections to
\mathfrak{a}
due to the electroweak forces (there are also corrections due to strong forces which are expected to be much smaller)
can be therefore written as a series expansion in
e
and
\mathsf{g}_{\textup{w}}
with

\mathfrak{a}_{\textsc{ew}}\equiv\mathfrak{a}_{\textsc{qed}}+\mathfrak{a}_{\textsc{weak}}=\sum_{n,m}A_{n,m}e^{n}\mathsf{g}_{\textup{w}}^{m}

(1)

where
e
is the electric charge and
\mathsf{g}_{\textup{w}}
the weak coupling; they can be expressed in terms
of the fine structure constant
\alpha
, that is
\alpha=e^{2}/(\hbar c)\approx 1/137
and
\mathsf{g}_{\textup{w}}^{2}=4\pi\alpha/\sin^{2}\theta_{W}
with

\sin^{2}\theta_{W}=0,232\dots
and
\theta_{W}
is the Weinberg angle. The smallness of the couplings motivates the perturbative approach.
Due to perturbative renormalizablity,
A_{n,m}
is finite at all orders.
The lowest order electromagnetic contribution
A_{2,0}e^{2}=\alpha/\pi

was obtained by Schwinger in 1948
[
4
]
, while

A_{4,0}e^{4}=-(\alpha/\pi)^{2}0.328\dots
was initially computed by Karplus and Kroll in 1950
[
5
]
with an error corrected by Petermann and Sommerfeld in 1958
[
6
,
7
]
.

A_{6,0}
has been computed analytically
[
8
]
and

A_{8,0}
,
A_{10,0}

are known numerically
[
1
]
. The lowest order contribution coming from
Weak forces is
A_{0,2}\mathsf{g}_{\textup{w}}^{2}=\mathfrak{a}_{\textsc{z},1}+\mathfrak{a}_{\textsc{w},1}
, where
\mathfrak{a}_{\textsc{z},1}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{\textsc{w},1}
are the
neutral
and the
charged
contribution, respectively mediated by the
Z^{0},W^{\pm}
bosons
[
9
]
. This contribution was computed in 1972 by Jackiw and Weinberg
[
10
]
as well as Altarelli, Cabibbo and Maiani
[
11
]
within the first perturbative order. In particular,

\mathfrak{a}_{\textsc{z},1}=\frac{m^{2}}{M^{2}}\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{1-5\kappa^{2}}{3}\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{m^{2}}{M^{2}}\right)\right]=\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{\textsc{z},1}\left[1+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{m^{2}}{M^{2}}\right)\right]

(2)

where
\lambda=\mathsf{g}_{\textup{w}}(4\sin^{2}\theta_{W}-1)/(4\cos\theta_{W}),\kappa=(4\sin^{2}\theta_{W}-1)^{-1}
and
M
is the
Z^{0}
mass. This prediction was historically relevant, because it anticipated the experimental discovery of weak neutral currents
[
12
]
. Note that
\mathfrak{a}_{\textsc{z},1}
is proportional to the square of the ratio between the fermion mass and the
Z^{0}
mass, which is a very small quantity (
\approx 10^{-6}
) for the electron; it is still small but larger (
\approx 10^{-3}
) for the muon. By summing up the first few terms of (
1
) and adding the QCD contribution, which is computed numerically, one gets the theoretical value mentioned above.

Despite the accuracy of the result obtained truncating the series expansions, the procedure appears mathematically unclear.
The series are not convergent and even not asymptotic to any QFT, see e. g.
[
13
]
; the Higgs sector, necessary to make the Standard Model renormalizable, is trivial, that is,
there is no way to define the theory in the removed cutoff limit, unless the limiting theory is non-interacting (see
[
14
,
15
]
).

A possible rigorous approach consists in considering the Standard Model as an effective theory valid up to certain energy scales and therefore requiring
an energy cutoff
\Lambda
to be defined.
One expects that
\mathfrak{a}
(and all the physical quantities) are independent on the regularization
\Lambda

and agree with the perturbative computations at lowest order,
up to an error which is small in a range of values of the couplings
including their physical values.
There are however no results putting such expectations on a quantitative, rigorous footing. The main difficulty is that one has to quantitatively fuflill two opposite requirements: from one side,
\Lambda
must large enough so that no effects are seen, as no sign of cutoff is experimentally observed; on the other hand, the theory is well-defined only up to an energy scale
that decreases if the coupling size increases.
In the present paper we present the first rigorous result
on the anomalous gyromagnetic factor, focusing on the
Z^{0}
contributions of Weak forces
and proving upper and lower bounds for it.
An announcement of a weaker version of this result
with no techical details is in
[
16
]
.

The regularized neutral Electroweak model

1.3
The regularized gyromagnetic factor

The regularized gyromagnetic factor is given by

\mathfrak{a}_{\textsc{z}}^{\textsc{r}}\equiv\sum_{\ell=0}^{K}\frac{(im)^{\ell}}{\ell!}\mathcal{A}^{(\ell)}(0)

(19)

with

\mathcal{A}(z)\equiv\frac{z\epsilon_{abc}\tr[\gamma^{5}(1+\gamma^{0})(\partial_{p^{\prime}}-\partial_{p})^{a}\hat{\Gamma}^{b}(p_{z},p_{z})\,\gamma^{c}]+2\tr[\gamma_{a}\hat{\Gamma}^{a}(p_{z},p_{z})]}{6\tr[(1+\gamma^{0})\hat{\Gamma}^{0}(p_{z},p_{z})]}-1

(20)

where
a,b,c\in\{1,2,3\}
and
p_{z}\equiv(z,0,0,0)
. If the formal

limit is taken, this definition formally coincides with the standard one (see e. g.
[
19
, Equation (6.37)]
). More details about this definition are provided in Appendix
A

Our main result is the following

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
K=4
and
M>10m
. If

\lambda^{2}\leq(C_{0})^{-1}(M^{2}/\Lambda^{2})\log^{-2}(M/m)\log^{-2}(\Lambda/M),

(21)

the limit
L\to\infty
of the correlations (
14
) exists, it verifies (
18
)
for a suitable choice of

Z^{s}_{N},Z^{J,s}_{N},m^{s}_{N}
and is such that the regularized anomalous gyromagnetic factor (
19
) satisfies

\mathfrak{a}_{\textsc{z}}^{\textsc{r}}=\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{\textsc{z},1}(1+R_{\lambda}),\qquad\absolutevalue*{R_\lambda}\leq C_{1}\frac{m^{2}}{M^{2}}+C_{2}\frac{M^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}+C_{3}\frac{\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{M^{2}}\cdot\log^{4}\left(\frac{M}{m}\right)\log^{4}\left(\frac{\Lambda}{M}\right),

(22)

where
\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{\textsc{z},1}
is given by (
2
) and

C_{0},C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}
are positive constants that do not depend on

.

The result rigorously shows that the anomalous gyromagnetic factor in the effective regularized theory coincides with the truncation of the perturbative expansion with cutoff removed (whose sum does not exist), up to regularizaton-dependent corrections which are small in a suitable region of the parameter space. Therefore, this result allows to estimate the error due to truncation in the formal expansion, even if it is non convergent. The physical regime corresponds to
m/M
and
\lambda
small with

m/M\ll\lambda
(in reality,
\lambda\sim 10^{-2}
and
m/M\sim 10^{-6}
in the case of electrons). Hence, the first term appearing in the remainder
R_{\lambda}
is surely small; further corrections due to higher values of
K
would be dominated by this one. The second term comes from the difference between the lowest order contribution of the regularized theory and the one without cutoff, and it is small if
\Lambda
is chosen to be much higher than the boson mass
M
.
The third term takes into account higher orders in
\lambda
and it is small provided that
\lambda
is sufficiently small, that is
\lambda\ll\mathcal{O}((M/\Lambda)\log^{-1}(M/m))
. A crucial aspect of our result is the fact that this term depends logarithmically on
M/m
: a power law dependence would imply a completely unrealistic smallness condition such as
\lambda\ll\mathcal{O}(m/\Lambda)
. This is achieved by proving suitable cancellations in the expansion, implying that all the higher orders contributions are
\mathcal{O}(m^{2}/M^{2})
. The choice
K=4
is enough to get upper and lower bounds in terms of the Jackiw-Weimberg formula (
2
).

1.4
Sketch of the proof

After integrating out the bosons, the amputated vertex function appearing in (
20
) is expressed by Grassmann integrals which can be analyzed by means of rigorous Renormalization Group methods. The amputated vertex function is written as a series in the coupling
\lambda
and the running coupling constants, that is

\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p)=\hat{\Gamma}_{a}^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p)+\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{b}(p^{\prime},p),

(23)

where the dominant part,
\hat{\Gamma}_{a}^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p)
, only depends from the running coupling constants;
\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{b}(p^{\prime},p)
is subdominant and can be expressed as a series in
\lambda
, namely

\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{b}(p^{\prime},p)=\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\lambda^{2n}\hat{\Gamma}_{n}^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p;\lambda).

(24)

Note that the above is not a
power
series in
\lambda
, as the running coupling constants are also functions of
\lambda
(that is,
\hat{\Gamma}_{n}^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p;\lambda)
depend from
\lambda
).
It turns out that, by fixing the bare constants so that the renormalization conditions (
18
) are verified, the contribution to
\mathfrak{a}_{\textsc{z}}^{\textsc{r}}
coming from
\hat{\Gamma}_{a}^{\mu}
is vanishing. The contribution coming from
\lambda^{2}\hat{\Gamma}_{1}^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p;\lambda)
is expressed by several renormalized graphs whose sum reconstructs the Jackiw-Weinberg result, up to corrections which are included in the first
and second term of (
22
). In particular, the dominant part of
\mathfrak{a}_{\textsc{z}}^{\textsc{r}}
turns out be of order
\lambda^{2}m^{2}/M^{2}
. The higher orders (
\lambda^{2n}\hat{\Gamma}_{n}^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p;\lambda)
, with
n\geq 2
) can also be written as sums
over renormalized Feynman graphs. In order to prove convergence, however, it is convenient

not
to bound each graph separately, but to control their sum using suitable determinant bounds which exploit cancellations caused by the anticommutativity of fermionic variables. A dimensional bound for
\hat{\Gamma}_{n}^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p;\lambda)
would be

\mathcal{O}(C^{n}(\Lambda^{2}/M^{2})^{n})
; this estimate is enough for convergence, but not for ensuring the lowest order dominance, as it does not contain the small
m^{2}/M^{2}
factor. An improvement of the bound for the contributions coming from
\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{b}(p^{\prime},p)
follows from certain cancellations due to exact or approximate symmetries; the last ones are indeed violated by the presence of the fermion mass, which is however small. As a consequence, the renormalized scaling dimension is smaller than or equal to
-2
and this produces the desidered
m^{2}/M^{2}
gain up to some harmless logarithmic corrections.

1.5
Open problems and remarks

The regularized neutral Electroweak model considered in this paper shares some similarities with Condensed Matter models that admit an emerging Quantum Field Theory description, like graphene, Hall systems or Weyl semimetals. In such systems, the lattice of ions among which the conduction elctrons are hopping provides a physical realization of the ultraviolet cutoff. A basic property of these Condensed Matter models is the universality of certain transport coefficients, that is, the independence of such quantities from the microscopic details. In particular, universality is present in the optical conductivity of graphene (
\sigma=(e^{2}/h)\cdot 1/(2\pi)
) or in the Hall conductance (
\sigma_{n}=ne^{2}/h
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
). These coefficients are strictly related with quantum anomalies of the emerging QFT description.

The transport coefficients, togheter the anomalous gyromagnetic factor of the electron, are measured with very high precision and their theoretical values are used to determine the fine structure constant. Note that, in the case of the transport coefficients, the theoretical prediction coincides with the lowest order computation in perturbation theory, while the anomalous gyromagnetic factor is a series with non vanishing coefficients at any order. The Renormalization Group approach used in this paper provides an explanation of this fact: the crucial point is that transport coefficients (as most of the correlation functions that are actually measured in Condensed Matter experiments) are usually associated to marginal or relevant terms, while the anomalous gyromagnetic factor is associated to an irrelevant one.

The universality of the transport coefficients in presence of a weak short range many-body interaction has been proven in
3+1
dimensions
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
or in
2+1
dimensions
[
21
,
23
]
in the case of graphene. The transport coefficients are decomposed as sums of two terms as in (
23
). In this case, however, only the first term (involving marginal couplings) gives a nonzero contribution; the second one, involving irrelevant terms, is exactly zero. No need of improvment of estimates with respect to power counting is necessary in this case.
From this fact follows the universality of transport coefficients or the related phenomenon of the non-renormalization of quantum anomalies in presence of a finite cutoff.
The anomalous gyromagnetic factor, on the contrary, only gets contributions from higher orders in the renormalized expansion, which involve irrelevant interactons.

There are several natural extensions of the present work, which could be possibly treated with the methods introduced in this paper and their generalizations.
A natural question is to improve the smallness condition on the coupling,

\lambda\ll\mathcal{O}((M/\Lambda)\log^{-1}(M/m))
.
In the case of renormalizable models, one could hope to replace such condition with
\lambda\ll\mathcal{O}(\log^{-1}(\Lambda/M)\log^{-1}(M/m))
, which would allow to reach much higher cutoffs. The model considered here is perturbatively non renormalizable due to the presence of anomalies, so this improvment is not expected here. However, after including the quark contributions with realistic values of the charges, the anomalies are expected to cancel and perturbative renormalizablity is recovered, so one can hope to get such a result in this generalized framework.
As a starting point, one could consider a vector model like massive QED
4
, and even in this case the construction up to exponentially high cutoff is unknown.
As a preliminary step, one would like to repeat the analysis presented here with lattice cutoff (which is necessary for the anomaly cancellation), where the symmetries at the basis of the above mentioned cancellations are different.
The issue of QED
4
contributions to the gyromagnetic factor with vanishing photon mass is also a natural problem to be considered, see e. g.
[
24
]
.

The paper is structured in the following way. In Section
2
we developed a Renormalization Group analysis and a tree expansion for the correlations, incorporating exact and emeging symmetries. In Section
3
we prove bounds for the coefficient of the expansion for the vertex function, proving its convergence using determinant bounds and cluster expansion formulas for truncared expectations. Finally, in Section
4
we prove the decomposition (
24
) and the relative bounds with improvements due to cancellations, leading to the proof of the main result.

3

In order to bound the kernels

3.1
The flow of the running coupling constants

Theorem 3
.

If
\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}/M^{2}
is sufficiently small, it is possible to choose the bare parameters
Z^{J,s}_{N},Z^{s}_{N},m^{s}_{N}
so that

Z^{J,s}_{h^{\star}}=Z^{s}_{h^{\star}-1}=1,\qquad m^{s}_{h^{\star}-1}=m\qquad\forall s=\pm,

(123)

where
m>0
is the physical mass of the fermion. With this choice, the running coupling constants satisfy

\absolutevalue*{Z^{J, s}_h - 1}\leq\frac{c\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{M^{2}}2^{h-N},\quad\absolutevalue*{Z^s_h - 1}\leq\frac{c\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{M^{2}}2^{h-N},\quad\absolutevalue*{m^s_h - m}\leq m\frac{c\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{M^{2}}2^{h-N}.

(124)

Proof.

For the sake of this proof, we exploit the fact that the highest order localization operators occurring in (
32
) do
not
contribute to
\mathcal{L}{\mathscr{V}}^{h}
, as shown in (
35
). Thus, we can treat
\mathcal{R}
as if it were a purely Taylor renormalization at the price of worsening the estimates (
98a
), (
98b
) by letting
R_{v}\in\{2,1,0\}
instead of
R_{v}\in\{3,2,0\}
.

In absence of zero-mass renormalizations, the tree expansion naturally allows to treat
\beta_{h}^{s},\beta^{J,s}_{h},2^{-h}\beta^{m,s}_{h}
as functions of
\lambda
and of the finite sequence of
independent variables

\{Z^{s}_{j},Z^{J,s}_{j},m^{s}_{j}\}_{j=h+1}^{N}
. We therefore introduce the vectors

\displaystyle\vec{v}_{h}

\displaystyle\equiv(Z^{+}_{h},Z^{-}_{h},Z^{J,+}_{h},Z^{J,-}_{h},2^{-h^{\star}}m^{+}_{h},2^{-h^{\star}}m^{-}_{h}),

\displaystyle\vec{\beta}_{h}

\displaystyle\equiv(\beta^{+}_{h},\beta^{-}_{h},\beta^{J,+}_{h},\beta^{J,-}_{h},2^{-h^{\star}}\beta^{m,+}_{h},2^{-h^{\star}}\beta^{m,-}_{h}),

so that, given any
h=h^{\star},\dots,N
, the RG equations read

\vec{v}_{h^{\star}-1}=\vec{v}_{h}+\sum_{j=h^{\star}}^{h}\vec{\beta}_{j}(\lambda,\underline{\vec{v}}\,),

(125)

where
\underline{\vec{a}}\equiv(\vec{a}_{h})_{h=h^{\star}}^{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-h^{\star}+1}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{6}
and it is understood that
\beta^{J,s}_{h^{\star}}=0
. The condition (
123
) can be expressed as
\mathsf{T}\underline{\vec{v}}=\underline{\vec{v}}
, where the map
\mathsf{T}
acts as

(\mathsf{T}\underline{\vec{v}}\,)_{h}\equiv\vec{w}_{h}-\!\sum_{j=h^{\star}}^{h}\vec{\beta}_{j}(\lambda,\underline{\vec{v}}\,)

(126)

with
\vec{w}_{h}\equiv(1,1,1,1,2^{-h^{\star}}m,2^{-h^{\star}}m)\,\,\forall h
. Note that, by construction,
any
vector
\underline{\vec{v}}
that satisfies
\mathsf{T}\underline{\vec{v}}=\underline{\vec{v}}
must also satisfy
\underline{\vec{v}}_{h-1}=\underline{\vec{v}}_{h}+\vec{\beta}_{h}(\lambda,\underline{\vec{v}}\,)
.

Let
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N-h^{\star}+1}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{6}
be a closed ball of radius
\epsilon>0
centered around
\underline{\vec{w}}
, constructed with respect to the norm
\norm*{\underline{\vec{a}}}\equiv\sum_{j=h^{\star}}^{N}\sum_{q=1}^{6}\absolutevalue*{a_j^q}
. If the maximum gain produced by
\mathcal{R}
is equal to
2
, the beta functions can still be bounded by means of the short memory property, but we must choose
\theta<1
in order to have
a_{v}\equiv D_{v}-R_{v}+\Theta_{v}+(\absolutevalue*{P_v}/4)\chi(\absolutevalue*{P_v}\geq 8)<0
. In particular, if both
\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}/M^{2}
and
\norm*{\underline{\vec{v}} - \underline{\vec{w}}}
are sufficiently small, the same construction adopted in the proof of Theorem
2
yields
\absolutevalue*{\beta^q_j(\lambda, \underline{\vec{v}})}\leq(\mathrm{const})\cdot(\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}/M^{2})\cdot 2^{(h-N)/2}
for every
q=1,\dots,6
and for every
j\in h^{\star},\dots,N
. Provided that
\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}/M^{2}
is small enough, the map
\mathsf{T}
sends
\mathcal{U}
into itself, because

\underline{\vec{v}}\in\mathcal{U}\Rightarrow\norm*{\mathsf{T} \underline{\vec{v}} - \underline{\vec{w}}}\leq\sum_{j=h^{\star}}^{N}\sum_{q=1}^{6}\absolutevalue*{\beta^q_j(\lambda, \underline{\vec{v}})}\leq 6c\frac{\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{M^{2}}\sum_{j=h^{\star}}^{N}2^{(j-N)/2}\leq 6C\frac{\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{M^{2}}\leq\epsilon.

(127)

Moreover, it can be shown that

\norm*{\mathsf{T} \underline{\vec{v}}_{(1)} - \mathsf{T} \underline{\vec{v}}_{(2)}}\leq\sum_{j=h^{\star}}^{N}\sum_{q=1}^{6}\absolutevalue*{\beta^q_j(\lambda, \underline{\vec{v}}_{(1)}) - \beta^q_j(\lambda, \underline{\vec{v}}_{(2)})}\leq\norm*{\underline{\vec{v}}_{(1)} - \underline{\vec{v}}_{(2)}}\frac{c\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{M^{2}}\sum_{j=h^{\star}}^{N}2^{(j-N)/2},

(128)

see for instance
[
32
, Appendix A5]
. The idea is simple: the difference
\beta^{q}_{j}(\lambda,\underline{\vec{v}}_{(1)})-\beta^{q}_{j}(\lambda,\underline{\vec{v}}_{(2)})
can be written in terms of kernels of the form
\mathscr{T}_{1,2}W_{\tau,\underline{P},\underline{\Delta}}
, where
\mathscr{T}_{1,2}(\,\cdot\,)\equiv(\,\cdot\,)|_{\underline{\vec{v}}_{(1)}}-(\,\cdot\,)|_{\underline{\vec{v}}_{(2)}}
. The
\mathscr{T}_{1,2}
operator is a generalization of
\mathscr{S}
, so the whole argument that starts from the decomposition (
111
) can be easily adapted to (
128
) as well.

After summing over
j
in (
128
), we obtain
\norm*{\mathsf{T} \underline{\vec{v}}_{(1)} - \mathsf{T} \underline{\vec{v}}_{(2)}}\leq c^{\prime}\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}/M^{2}
, so
\mathsf{T}|_{\mathcal{U}}
is a contraction on
\mathcal{U}
if
\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}/M^{2}
is suffciently small. Banach-Caccioppoli’s theorem then guarantees the existence of a unique fixed point
\underline{\vec{u}}\colon\mathsf{T}\underline{\vec{u}}=\underline{\vec{u}}
. By construction, the sequence
\{\vec{u}_{h}\}_{h=h^{\star}}^{N}
represents a RG flow such that
\vec{u}_{h^{\star}-1}\equiv\vec{u}_{h^{\star}}+\vec{\beta}_{h^{\star}}(\lambda,\underline{\vec{u}})=(1,1,1,1,2^{-h^{\star}}m,2^{-h^{\star}}m)
: recalling that
\beta^{J,s}_{h^{\star}}=0\Rightarrow Z^{J,s}_{h^{\star}-1}=Z^{J,s}_{h^{\star}}
, the renormalization conditions (
123
) are satisfied if we choose the ultraviolet parameters as
(Z^{+}_{N},Z^{-}_{N},\dots,2^{-h^{\star}}m^{-}_{N})\equiv(u_{N}^{1},u_{N}^{2},\dots,u_{N}^{6})
. Finally, the bounds (

) and (

∎

3.3
Infinite volume limit

The bounds obtained in Theorem
2
are uniform with respect to the spacetime volume. Indeed, it can be shown that the sequence of finite-volume kernels
\{W_{\tau,\underline{P},\underline{\Delta},L}\}_{L\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in the
L\to+\infty
limit with respect to the uniform norm on every
fixed
compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{4(\absolutevalue*{P_{w_0}}+n^{J}_{w_{0}}+n^{\eta}_{w_{0}})}
, where
w_{0}
is the first nonroot vertex of
\tau
. A complete proof of this statement is rather long, so we shall not discuss it here; a detailed treatment can be found in
[
33
, Appendix D]
. Although our model differs from the one analyzed in
[
33
]
, the proof can be adapted to the present case with little effort. The main idea consists in noticing that the infinite volume limits of the functions
\delta_{N},W_{(\eta)}^{N},W_{(J)}^{N},W_{(\lambda)}^{N},{g}^{N}
exist and they are equal to the inverse Fourier transforms of
\chi_{N+1}(k),\hat{W}_{(\eta)}^{N}(k),\hat{W}_{(J)}^{N}(p,p^{\prime}),\hat{W}_{(\lambda)}^{N}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}),{\hat{g}}^{N}(k)
, thought as functions of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
vectors. As a consequence, the kernels
W_{\tau,\underline{P},\Delta,L}
can be defined in the infinite volume limit for every
\tau\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{N-1|N}
(this is realized by taking (
62
) and replacing every function
\delta_{N},W_{(\eta)}^{N},\dots
with its infinite volume limit). The difference between
W_{\tau,\underline{P},\Delta,L}
and
W_{\tau,\underline{P},\Delta}
can be shown to vanish as
L\to+\infty
(see
[
33
, Equations (D.7), (D.8) et seq.]
). The existence of the infinite volume limits of the above kernels implies the existence of the infinite volume limits of the running coupling constants
m^{s}_{N-1},Z^{s}_{N-1},Z^{J,s}_{N-1}
as well as those of the functions
W_{(\eta)}^{N-1},W_{(J)}^{N-1},W_{(\lambda)}^{N-1},{g}^{N-1}
. Relying on the recursive definition
61
, we can iteratively apply the above construction to every kernel
W_{\tau,\underline{P},\underline{\Delta},L}
for every
\tau\in\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{j|N}
, with
j=h^{\star}-1,\dots,N-1
.

From now on, we will always assume that the infinite volume limit has been taken.

4
Evaluation of the anomalous gyromagnetic factor

Based on the results discussed in Section
2

3

The bounds (

This bound is however not sufficient to prove our main result, which estabilishes that

is bounded above and below by a

In order to get this result we note that, based on Theorem

and Theorem

, it is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p)&=\gamma^{\mu}+\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\lambda^{2n}\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{n}(p^{\prime},p;\lambda),\end{split}

(131)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{S}(k)&=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\lambda^{2n}\hat{S}_{n}(k;\lambda)\equiv\frac{1}{i\not{k}+m}\left(1+\sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\lambda^{2n}\hat{G}_{n}(k;\lambda)\right),\end{split}

(132)

where each contribution of order

inlcudes the sum over all the possible trees with

\lambda

23

In this section, we shall prove three properties:

•

If
\absolutevalue*{p'},\absolutevalue{p}\leq m/2
and
n\geq 1
, the bound for

can be improved by a factor
m^{2}/M^{2}
, provided that the value of
\lambda
is sligthly decreased. This is due to the presence of symmetries implemented by our choice of the
\mathcal{R}
operator defined in Section
2.2
.

•

This

improvement is not present in the

term of the expansion (
131
); however, the contribution to

coming from this term is vanishing.

•

We separate the

term from the rest and we show that it gives the same value of the Jackiw-Weinberg formula (
2
) up to subdominant corrections.

In what follows, the symbol
\mathcal{O}(\lambda f)
is used to denote any function of
\lambda
that satisfies
\absolutevalue{\BigO(\lambda f)}\leq C\cdot\absolutevalue{\lambda f}
for some constant

that
does not depend on

.

4.1
Bounds for

and

By combining Theorem
2
and Theorem

The tree expansion for

where the sum

\lambda

4.3
Vanishing of

Before getting into the actual calculation of

, so

. We also show that

Due to the anticommutation properties of the

It is easy to see that
\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{0}(p^{\prime},p;\lambda)
and the local parts of

, nor to
\mathfrak{a}_{\textsc{z}}^{\textsc{r}}
. On the other hand, the

are linear combinations of integrals of the form

with

and
h^{\star}\leq h_{1},h_{2}\leq N
. The

is proportional to
\delta^{\nu\mu}
, so

\gamma^{\mu}
and
\gamma^{5}\gamma^{\mu}

and
\gamma^{5}\gamma^{\mu}

any

\mathcal{F}(z)-\mathcal{Q}(z)

\mathfrak{a}_{\textsc{z}}^{\textsc{r}}

4.4
Extraction of the
\lambda
-independent part of
\mathcal{A}_{2}(z;\lambda)

Since the running coupling constants

depend on
\lambda
and

, it is not immediate to explicitly perform the sum over

inside the triangle integral (
151
). For this reason, we split the function
\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\triangle}(p^{\prime},p;\lambda)
into the sum of a

-independent part (for which the sum over

could be trivially performed) and a small remainder.

Lemma 4
.

Let
{\hat{g}_{0}}^{h}(p)\equiv f_{h}(p)(i\not{p}+m)^{-1}
and suppose that
\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}/M^{2}<1
. For every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, there exists a positive constant
C_{\ell}
such that

\sup_{\absolutevalue*{p'},\absolutevalue{p}\leq m/2}\norm*{m^\ell\partial^\ell\hat{\Gamma}^\mu_\triangle(p', p; \lambda) - m^\ell\partial^\ell\hat{\Gamma}^\mu_\triangle(p', p)}\leq C_{\ell}\frac{m^{2}}{M^{2}}\cdot\frac{\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{M^{2}}\log\left(\frac{M}{m}\right)\frac{M}{\Lambda},

(152)

where
\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\triangle}(p^{\prime},p)
is the
\lambda
-independent function obtained by replacing
{\hat{g}}^{h}(p),(\gamma^{\mu}_{J})_{h}
with
{\hat{g}_{0}}^{h}(p),\gamma^{\mu}
inside (
151
).

Proof.

For sake of simplicity, we take
\ell=0
(if
\ell>0
, the proof is essentially the same). If
{\hat{g}}^{h}(k)\equiv{\hat{g}_{0}}^{h}(k)+{\delta\hat{g}}^{h}(k)
and
(\gamma^{\mu}_{J})_{h}\equiv\gamma^{\mu}+(\delta\gamma^{\mu}_{J})_{h}
, Theorem
3
implies that

\norm*{(\delta\gamma^\mu_J)_h}\leq(\mathrm{const})\cdot\frac{\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{M^{2}}2^{h-N},\qquad\norm*{\sing{\delta g}{h}}_{w}\leq(\mathrm{const})\cdot\frac{\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{M^{2}}2^{h-N}\cdot\begin{cases}2^{3h}&w=\infty\\
2^{-h}&w=h\end{cases}

(153)

Every
{\hat{g}}^{h},(\gamma^{\mu}_{J})_{h}
factor appearing in (
151
) can be decomposed as shown above, so

\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\triangle}(p^{\prime},p;\lambda)-\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\triangle}(p^{\prime},p)=\mathsf{R}^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p;\lambda),

(154)

where
\mathsf{R}^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p;\lambda)
is given by a sum of terms having the same structure as
\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p;\varphi_{4}^{0,1,2},\lambda)
, except that they contain at least one
{\delta g}^{h}/(\delta\gamma^{\mu}_{J})_{h}
factor. Now consider one of such terms, say for instance

\displaystyle I^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p;\lambda)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{h^{\star}<h_{1}\leq h_{2}}\int\frac{\differential[4]{q}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,\mathcal{R}[\Upsilon_{\nu}\,{\delta\hat{g}}^{h_{1}}(p^{\prime}-q)\,(\gamma^{\mu}_{J})_{h_{2}}\,{\hat{g}}^{h_{2}}(p-q)\,\Upsilon^{\nu}\,\hat{v}(q)]

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{h^{\star}<h_{1}\leq h_{2}}I_{h_{1}h_{2}}(p^{\prime},p;\lambda).

Since
\absolutevalue*{p'},\absolutevalue{p}\leq m/2\Rightarrow\absolutevalue*{p' - q - (p - q)}\leq m
and the single scale propagators are compactly-supported,
h_{2}\in\{h_{1},h_{1}+1,h_{1}+2\}\cap[h^{\star}+1,N]
. This means that, apart from an inessential overall constant, we can perform our estimates by letting
h_{2}=h_{1}
. If
h_{1}<h_{M}\equiv\lfloor\log_{2}M\rfloor
, it is convenient to estimate the boson propagator as
\hat{v}(q)\leq 1/M^{2}
, thus getting

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{h^{\star}<h_{1}<h_{M}}\norm{I_{h_1 h_1}(p', p; \lambda)}&\leq\frac{C}{M^{2}}\frac{\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{M^{2}}\sum_{h^{\star}<h_{1}<h_{M}}2^{3h_{1}-h_{1}+2(h^{\star}-h_{1})}2^{h_{1}-N}\\
&\leq C\frac{m^{2}}{M^{2}}\frac{\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{M^{2}}\frac{M}{\Lambda}\sum_{h^{\star}<h_{1}<h_{M}}2^{h_{1}-h_{1}},\end{split}

(155)

where in the second line we exploited the fact that
2^{h_{1}-N}<M/\Lambda
. If
h_{1}\geq h_{M}
, we have instead

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{h_{M}\leq h_{1}\leq N}\norm{I_{h_1 h_1}(p', p; \lambda)}&\leq C\frac{\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{M^{2}}\sum_{h_{M}\leq h_{1}\leq N}2^{3h_{1}-h_{1}+2(h^{\star}-h_{1})-2h_{1}}2^{h_{1}-N}\\
&\leq Cm^{2}\frac{\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{M^{2}}\sum_{h_{M}\leq h_{1}\leq N}2^{-h_{1}-N},\end{split}

(156)

where we used the fact that
\mathrm{supp}[{\delta\hat{g}}^{h_{1}}(p^{\prime}-\cdot)]\subseteq\{q\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\colon\absolutevalue{q}\geq 2^{h_{1}-1}-m/2\}
in order to write
\hat{v}(q)\leq 1/q^{2}\leq(\mathrm{const})\cdot 2^{-2h_{1}}
. By combining (
155
) and (
156
), we obtain

\norm*{I^\mu(p', p; \lambda)}\leq C\frac{m^{2}}{M^{2}}\frac{\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{M^{2}}\frac{M}{\Lambda}\sum_{h^{\star}<h_{1}<h_{M}}1+Cm^{2}\frac{\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{M^{2}}\sum_{h_{M}\leq h_{1}\leq N}2^{-h_{1}-N}.

(157)

After performing the sum over
h_{1}
(and possibly redefining the constant
C
), this becomes

\norm*{I^\mu(p', p; \lambda)}\leq C\frac{m^{2}}{M^{2}}\frac{\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{M^{2}}\log\left(\frac{M}{m}\right)\frac{M}{\Lambda},

(158)

so
I^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p;\lambda)
satisfies the bound (
152
). The same conclusion holds for any other term contributing to
\mathsf{R}^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p;\lambda)
that contains
one

{\delta g}^{h}/(\delta\gamma^{\mu}_{J})_{h}
factor. If
\lambda^{2}\Lambda^{2}/M^{2}<1
, terms containing more than one
{\delta g}^{h}/(\delta\gamma^{\mu}_{J})_{h}
factor are subdominant with respect to
I^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p;\lambda)
, so the whole function
\mathsf{R}^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p;\lambda)
satisfies the bound (
152
).
∎

Remark 5
.

Within the above proof, the boson propagator has been bounded with
1/M^{2}
or
1/q^{2}
depending on whether
h_{1}<h_{M}
or
h_{1}\geq h_{M}
. This estimate is
more refined
than the one presented in Section
4.1
, which is only based on the inequality
\hat{v}(q)\leq 1/M^{2}
. This refinement is
fundamental
in order to extract the

coming from

produces a

gain only provided that

is less than

; in the opposite regime, the same gain is obtained by exploiting the damping produced by

for large values of
\absolutevalue{q}
.

4.5
Existence of the
\Lambda\to+\infty
limit of
\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\triangle}(p^{\prime},p)

Thanks to Lemma
4
, from now on we can turn our attention on the
\lambda
-independent triangle integral
\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\triangle}(p^{\prime},p)
. Since the
{g}^{h}_{0}
propagator scales in the same way as

, the bound (

\sup_{\absolutevalue*{p'},\absolutevalue{p}\leq m/2}\norm*{\hat{\Gamma}^\mu_\triangle(p', p)}\leq C\cdot\frac{m^{2}}{M^{2}}\cdot\log^{2}\left(\frac{\Lambda}{m}\right).

(159)

so
\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\triangle}(p^{\prime},p)
could apparently
diverge
as
\Lambda
goes to

. However, this is not the case, because the estimate (

\hat{v}(q)
as
\absolutevalue{q}\to+\infty

Lemma 5
.

The function
\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\triangle,\infty}(p^{\prime},p)\equiv\lim_{\Lambda\to+\infty}\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\triangle}(p^{\prime},p)
is well-defined on
\mathcal{U}=\{(p^{\prime},p)\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\colon\absolutevalue*{p'},\absolutevalue{p}\leq m/2\}
and it is smooth in the interior of
\mathcal{U}
. Moreover, for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there exists a positive constant
C_{\ell}
such that

\norm*{m^\ell\partial^\ell\hat{\Gamma}^\mu_\triangle- m^\ell\partial^\ell\hat{\Gamma}^\mu_{\triangle, \infty}}_{\mathcal{U}}\leq C_{\ell}\frac{m^{2}}{M^{2}}\frac{M^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}},

(160)

where
\norm*{\, \cdot\,}_{\mathcal{U}}
is the uniform norm on
\mathcal{U}
.

Proof.

Let
\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\triangle,N}(p^{\prime},p)
denote the triangle integral in presence of a finite cutoff
\Lambda=2^{N}
. To prove that
\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\triangle,\infty}(p^{\prime},p)
exists, we show that the sequence of smooth functions
\{\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\triangle,N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
has the Cauchy property with respect to
\norm*{\, \cdot\,}_{\mathcal{U}}
.

For sake of simplicity, let us consider the contribution to
\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\triangle,N}(p^{\prime},p)
coming from the
\lambda
-independent version of diagram
\varphi_{4}^{1}
, namely

\displaystyle I_{N}^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{h_{1}=h^{\star}+1}^{N}\sum_{h_{2}=h_{1}}^{N}\int\frac{\differential[4]{q}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,\mathcal{R}[\Upsilon_{\nu}\,{\hat{g}_{0}}^{h_{1}}(p^{\prime}-q)\,\gamma^{\mu}\,{\hat{g}_{0}}^{h_{2}}(p-q)\,\Upsilon^{\nu}\,\hat{v}(q)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{h_{1}=h^{\star}+1}^{N}\sum_{h_{2}=h_{1}}^{N}I^{\mu}_{h_{1}h_{2}}(p^{\prime},p).

As we already noticed in the proof of Lemma
4
,
h_{2}
cannot exceed
h_{1}+2
, so we can let
h_{1}=h_{2}
at the price of introducing an inessential overall constant
c
. Given any
N^{\prime}>N
, it is easy to see that

\norm*{I_N^\mu- I^\mu_{N'}}_{\mathcal{U}}\leq c\sum_{h_{1}=N-1}^{N^{\prime}}\norm*{I^\mu_{h_1 h_1}}_{\mathcal{U}}.

(161)

If
N\geq\lfloor\log_{2}M\rfloor\equiv h_{M}
,
h_{1}
is greater than
h_{M}-1
, so it is convenient to estimate the boson propagator as
\hat{v}(q)\leq 1/q^{2}
. In absence of
{\delta g}^{h_{1}}
factors, the same procedure shown in (
156
) yields
\norm*{I^\mu_{h_1 h_1}(p', p)}\leq m^{2}\cdot 2^{-2h_{1}}
, so

\norm*{I_N^\mu- I^\mu_{N'}}_{\mathcal{U}}\leq C_{1}m^{2}\sum_{h_{1}=N-1}^{N^{\prime}}2^{-2h_{1}}\leq\frac{C_{2}m^{2}}{2^{2N}}.

(162)

The right hand side of this inequality becomes arbitrarily small if
N
is large enough. Consequently,
\{I^{\mu}_{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence that converges uniformly to a continuous function
I^{\mu}_{\infty}(p^{\prime},p)
defined on
\mathcal{U}
. The same argument can be applied to the contributions coming from
\varphi_{4}^{0}
and
\varphi_{4}^{2}
; in particular,
\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\triangle,\infty}(p^{\prime},p)
exists and it is continuous. Since the estimate (
162
) holds for the
\ell
-th derivatives of
m^{\ell}\hat{I}^{\mu}_{N}(p^{\prime},p)
as well (the
m^{\ell}
factor balances the
2^{-\ell h^{\star}}
factor produced by a
\ell
-th derivative), the sequence
\{\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\triangle,N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
converges with respect to
any

C^{\ell}(\mathcal{U})
norm for every
\ell\geq 0
. This implies that
\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\triangle,\infty}(p^{\prime},p)
is smooth and the
\ell
-th derivatives of
\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\triangle,N}(p^{\prime},p)
converge uniformly to the
\ell
-th derivatives of
\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\triangle,\infty}(p^{\prime},p)
.

The difference between
I^{\mu}_{N}(p^{\prime},p)
and
I^{\mu}_{\infty}(p^{\prime},p)
can be estimated as

\norm*{I^\mu_N- I^\mu_\infty}_{\mathcal{U}}\leq C\sum_{h_{1}=N}^{+\infty}\norm*{I^\mu_{h_1 h_1}}_{\mathcal{U}}.

(163)

Here
h_{1}
is much larger than
h_{M}
, so we use the inequality
\hat{v}(q)\leq 1/q^{2}
to write
\norm*{I^\mu_{h_1 h_1}}_{\mathcal{U}}\leq m^{2}\cdot 2^{-2h_{1}}
. The bound (
160
) then follows by summing over
h_{1}
(again, this can be extended the whole triangle integral, for any value of
\ell
).
∎

Appendix A
The regularized anomalous gyromagnetic factor

In this section we provide some details about the definition of regularized anomalous gyromagnetic factor given by (
19
). In the context of perturbative Quantum Field Theory, the anomalous gyromagnetic factor is defined in Minkowski spacetime starting from the probability amplitude for a process in which a single fermion is scattered by a weak, external electromagnetic field
A_{\mu}(x)
. This amplitude reads

-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{p_{0}\,p^{\prime}_{0}}}\bar{u}_{p^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}}\,\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{M}}}(p^{\prime},p)\,u_{p\xi}\,\hat{A}_{\mu}(k)\evaluated{}_{(p')^2 = p^2 = - m^2}

(184)

within the first order in

, where
\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{M}}}(p^{\prime},p)
is the Minkowskian amputated vertex function (see e. g.
[
19

). Here,
k=p^{\prime}-p
,
p^{2}\equiv-(p_{0})^{2}+\absolutevalue{\bm{p}}^{2}
and

are two linearly independent solutions of the equation
(ip_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{M}}}+\absolutevalue*{p}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}})u_{p\xi}=0

\gamma^{\mu}_{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{M}}}
are the Minkowskian gamma matrices,
\absolutevalue*{p}_{\!\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{M}}\equiv\sqrt{-p^{2}}

\mathbb{C}^{4}\ni u_{p\xi}=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{-p^{0}+i\mathbf{p}\cdot\bm{\sigma}}\,e_{\xi}\\
\sqrt{-p^{0}-i\mathbf{p}\cdot\bm{\sigma}}\,e_{\xi}\end{pmatrix}

[
35

\hat{\mathbf{B}}(\mathbf{k})\equiv i\mathbf{k}\times\hat{\mathbf{A}}(\mathbf{k})

which, once plugged into (
186

Recalling that the gyromagnetic factor is the coefficient
g

\bm{\mu}=-g(e/2m)\mathbf{S}

(189)

Appendix C
Some estimates at higher orders

Although the bound provided by Theorem
4
is based on the tree expansion, it clearly holds for each renormalized multiscale Feynman diagram that contributes to
\hat{S}(k)
or
\hat{\Gamma}^{\mu}(p^{\prime},p)
, at least when
\absolutevalue{k},\absolutevalue*{p'},\absolutevalue{p}
are small enough. To concretely appreciate the subtle mechanism that allows to extract the overall
m^{2}/M^{2}
factor, it is instructive to analyze a few concrete examples and work out the bounds (
133
) “by hand”. In the standard perturbative QFT framework, it is practically impossible to give an
a priori
estimate on the size of a Feynman diagram; yet, the non perturbative formalism adopted in this paper allows to exhibit a meaningful bound on any multiscale renormalized Feynman diagram, without even writing down its explicit expression.

A fourth order diagram.

Let us consider the diagram displayed in
[
5
, Figure 1, row IV]
, which contributes to the fourth order correction to the anomalous gyromagnetic factor in ordinary perturbative QED
4
. In our model, there are plenty of renormalized multiscale Feynman diagrams with the same structure as this. An example is given by figure
5
. Here, black boxes have been used to evidence the clusters associated with the vertices of the tree: proceeding from the innermost to the outermost cluster, we encounter one propagator on scale
h_{1}
, two propagators on scale
h_{2}
, one propagator on scale
h_{3}
and two propagators on scale

.

[
5

, together with corresponding tree.

According to definition (
32
), the
\mathcal{R}
operator acts nontrivially on the penultimate cluster. The
(h^{\star}-1)

2

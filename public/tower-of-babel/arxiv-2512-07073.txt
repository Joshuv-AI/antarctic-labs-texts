Huimin Chang, Ping Jin*

Abstract
. Let P P be a Sylow p p -subgroup of a finite p p -solvable group G G , where p p is a prime. Using a normal p p -series 𝒩 \mathcal{N} of G G , we introduce the notion of ( 𝒩 , p ) (\mathcal{N},p) -stable characters and prove that G G and 𝐍 G ​ ( P ) {\bf N}_{G}(P) have equal numbers of such characters, which gives a new generalization of the McKay conjecture for p p -solvable groups. Also, we establish a canonical bijection between these characters in the case where G G has odd order. Our proofs depend heavily on the theory of self-stabilizing pairs founded by M. L. Lewis, as well as some results of π \pi -special characters due to I. M. Isaacs.



1.
Introduction

Fix a prime
p
, and let
G
be a finite group with
P\in\Syl_{p}(G)
.
Then the McKay conjecture, which is recently confirmed in
[
1
]
,
asserts that
G
and
\mathbf{N}_{G}(P)
have equal numbers of irreducible characters with
degrees not divisible by the prime
p
, i.e.,

|\Irr_{p^{\prime}}(G)|=|\Irr_{p^{\prime}}(\mathbf{N}_{G}(P))|
.
For various refinements of the McKay conjecture, the reader is referred to
[
12
]
,
and in this paper, we will present a new generalization of this conjecture for
p
-solvable groups.

We introduce a temporary notation. If
X
is a finite group, we write

\Omega_{i}(X)=\{\chi\in\Irr(X)|\chi(1)_{p}=p^{i}\}

for each integer
i\geq 0
.
Then the McKay conjecture
can be restated as
|\Omega_{0}(G)|=|\Omega_{0}(\mathbf{N}_{G}(P))|
.
In order to generalize the McKay conjecture, therefore,
it is natural to ask for each
i\geq 1
,
what kind of connection exists between
\Omega_{i}(G)

and
\Omega_{i}(\mathbf{N}_{G}(P))
.
By using the theory of self-stabilizing pairs founded by Lewis in

[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
,
we are able to establish a new quantitative relationship between these two sets
in the case where
G
is
p
-solvable.

Specifically, given a
p
-solvable group
G
,
we fix a
normal
p
-series

\mathcal{N}=\{N_{i}\}
of length
n
of
G
, that is,

1=N_{0}\leq N_{1}\leq\cdots\leq N_{n}=G,

where
N_{i}\vartriangleleft G
and
N_{i}/N_{i-1}
is either a
p
-group or a
p^{\prime}
-group
for
i=1,\ldots,n
.
We say that
\chi\in\Irr(G)
is
(\mathcal{N},p)
-stable

if
N_{i}
fixes an irreducible constituent of
\chi_{N_{i-1}}

whenever
N_{i}/N_{i-1}
is a
p
-group,
and we use
\Irr(G,\mathcal{N})
to denote the set of
(\mathcal{N},p)
-stable characters of
G
.
More generally, if
H
is a subgroup of
G
,
then
\mathcal{N}_{H}=\{H\cap N_{i}\}
is clearly a normal
p
-series of
H
,
and thus we can define the
(\mathcal{N}_{H},p)
-stability of irreducible characters of
H
.
That is,
\theta\in\Irr(H)
is
(\mathcal{N}_{H},p)
-stable
if
H\cap N_{i}
fixes an irreducible constituent of
\theta_{H\cap N_{i-1}}

whenever
N_{i}/N_{i-1}
is a
p
-group.
For natational convenience, we will always use
\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})
instead of
\Irr(H,\mathcal{N}_{H})
.
Note that if
H
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
,
then
\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})
consists of those characters
\alpha\in\Irr(H)

such that
\alpha_{H\cap N}
is homogeneous
(i.e., a multiple of an irreducible character) for each
N\in\mathcal{N}
.
Furthermore, for each
i\geq 0
we write

\Irr_{i}(H,\mathcal{N})=\{\theta\in\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})|\theta(1)_{p}=p^{i}\},

which clearly is contained in
\Omega_{i}(H)
,
so that
\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})=\bigsqcup_{i\geq 0}\Irr_{i}(H,\mathcal{N})
.
(From here on, we use the symbol
\bigsqcup
to denote the disjoint union of sets.)
In this way, we give a natural graded structure on the set
\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})

by using the normal
p
-series
\mathcal{N}
.

Now our main result can be stated as follows,
which generalizes the McKay conjecture for
p
-solvable groups
with an independent proof.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a
p
-solvable group with
P\in\Syl_{p}(G)
, and let
\mathcal{N}
be a
normal
p
-series of
G
. Then the following hold.

(1)

\Irr_{0}(G,\mathcal{N})=\Irr_{p^{\prime}}(G)
and

\Irr_{0}({\bf N}_{G}(P),\mathcal{N})=\Irr_{p^{\prime}}({\bf N}_{G}(P))
.

(2)

|\Irr_{i}(G,\mathcal{N})|=|\Irr_{i}({\bf N}_{G}(P),\mathcal{N})|
for all
i\geq 0
.

(3)
For
i\geq 0
,
\Irr_{i}(G,\mathcal{N})
is nonempty
if and only if
p^{i}=\alpha(1)
for some
\alpha\in\Irr(P,\mathcal{N})
.

(4)

|\Irr(G,\mathcal{N})|=|\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(P),\mathcal{N})|
.

We mention that there exist many
p
-solvable groups
G

such that the set
\Irr_{i}(G,\mathcal{N})
is nonempty for some integer
i\geq 1
.
Consider a
p
-nilpotent group
G
, for example,
and let
N
be the normal
p
-complement of
G
,
so that
\mathcal{N}=\{1,N,G\}
is a normal
p
-series of
G
.
If
\theta\in\Irr(N)
is invariant in
G
,
then by definition, each character
\chi\in\Irr(G)
lying over
\theta

lies in
\Irr(G,\mathcal{N})
.
Since
\chi(1)/\theta(1)
divides
|G:N|
, which is a
p
-power,
it follows that
\chi\in\Irr_{i}(G,\mathcal{N})
, where
p^{i}=\chi(1)/\theta(1)
.
In this situation, we can choose the group
G

such that
\chi_{N}\neq\theta
, and this forces
i\geq 1
.

In the situation of Theorem
A
, for each
i\geq 0

there is no canonical bijection from
\Irr_{i}(G,\mathcal{N})
onto
\Irr_{i}({\bf N}_{G}(P),\mathcal{N})

in general. If
G
has odd order, however, a canonical bijection does exist
between
\Irr_{p^{\prime}}(G)
and
\Irr_{p^{\prime}}({\bf N}_{G}(P))

(see Theorem 10.9 of
[
2
]
).
We can establish the same result for any positive integer
i
.

Theorem B
.

Let
G
be a solvable group of odd order,
and let
P\in\Syl_{p}(G)
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{N}
is a normal
p
-series of
G
.
Then there is a canonical bijection from
\Irr(G,\mathcal{N})
onto
\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(P),\mathcal{N})
.
Also, if
\xi\in\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(P),\mathcal{N})
is the image of

\chi\in\Irr(G,\mathcal{N})
under this map, then

\xi
is a constituent of
\chi_{{\bf N}_{G}(P)}

and
\xi(1)
divides
\chi(1)
.

Furthermore, for each
i\geq 0
, the bijection
maps
\Irr_{i}(G,\mathcal{N})
onto
\Irr_{i}({\bf N}_{G}(P),\mathcal{N})
,
and thus defines a canonical bijection between these two sets.

We continue to assume that
G
is a
p
-solvable group,

p
is a prime and
P\in\Syl_{p}(G)
.
In order to prove our Theorems
A
and
B
,
we need to extend the definition of the map
\Psi:\Lin(P)\to\Irr(G)

defined by Isaacs and Navarro in
[
5
]
,
where
\Lin(P)
denotes the set of linear characters of
P
.
We now consider an arbitrary character
\alpha\in\Irr(P)
.
By Corollary 3.15 and Theorem 3.16 of
[
4
]
,
we know that
\alpha
uniquely determines a pair
(W,\gamma)
,
where
W
is the unique largest subgroup
G
to which
\alpha
extends
and
\gamma
is the unique
p
-special extension of
\alpha
to
W
.
(For the definition and properties of
p
-special
(or
p^{\prime}
-special) characters, see the next section.)
In this situation, we can define a new map, which we still call
\Psi
,

\Psi:\Irr(P)\to\Char(G),\;\alpha\mapsto\gamma^{G},

from the set
\Irr(P)
of irreducible characters of
P

into the set
\Char(G)
of all characters of
G
by setting
\Psi(\alpha)=\gamma^{G}
.
For convenience, we say that
\Psi
is the
standard map
associated with
P
.

The following result, which may be of independent interest,
lies at the heart of the proof of Theorems
A
and
B
.

Theorem C
.

Let
P
be a Sylow
p
-subgroup of a
p
-solvable group
G
, where
p
is a prime,
and suppose that
\mathcal{N}
is a normal
p
-series of
G
.
Let
\Psi:\Irr(P)\to\Char(G)
be the standard map defined as above.
The following then hold.

(1)
If
\alpha\in\Irr(P,\mathcal{N})
, then
\Psi(\alpha)\in\Irr(G)
.

(2)
If
\alpha,\beta\in\Irr(P,\mathcal{N})
,
then
\Psi(\alpha)=\Psi(\beta)
if and only if
\alpha
and
\beta
are
{\bf N}_{G}(P)
-conjugate.

(3)
For each
\alpha\in\Irr(P,\mathcal{N})
,
let
W
be the unique largest subgroup of
G

to which
\alpha
extends and let
\gamma
be the unique
p
-special
extension of
\alpha
to
W
. Then the map
\delta\mapsto(\gamma\delta)^{G}

is an injection from the set of
p^{\prime}
-special characters of
W

into
\Irr(G)
.
Write
\Irr_{\alpha}(G)
for the image of this injection, i.e.,

\Irr_{\alpha}(G)=\{(\gamma\delta)^{G}\,|\,\delta\;\text{is a $p^{\prime}$-special character of}\;W\},

so that
\Irr_{\alpha}(G)\subseteq\Irr(G)
.
Also, if
\chi\in\Irr_{\alpha}(G)
, then
\chi(1)_{p}=\alpha(1)
.

(4)
If
\alpha,\beta\in\Irr(P,\mathcal{N})
,
then
\Irr_{\alpha}(G)=\Irr_{\beta}(G)
if and only if

\alpha
and
\beta
are
{\bf N}_{G}(P)
-conjugate,
and otherwise,
\Irr_{\alpha}(G)
and
\Irr_{\beta}(G)
are disjoint.

(5)
If
\alpha\in\Irr(P,\mathcal{N})
,
then
|\Irr_{\alpha}(G)|=|\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(P)|\alpha)|
.

(6)

\Irr(G,\mathcal{N})=\bigcup\Irr_{\alpha}(G)
,
where
\alpha
runs over
\Irr(P,\mathcal{N})
.

(7)

\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(P),\mathcal{N})=\bigcup\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(P)|\alpha)
,
where
\alpha
runs over
\Irr(P,\mathcal{N})
.

Throughout the paper, all groups considered are finite,
and the notation and terminology are mostly taken from

]

]
.

2.
Preliminaries

We begin by introducing some notation.
Suppose that
H
is a subgroup of a group
G
,
and let
\theta\in\Irr(H)
be an irreducible character of
H
.
In this situation, we say that
(H,\theta)
is a
character pair
of
G
.
Observe that
G
acts by conjugation on the set of character pairs of it,
where by definition
(H,\theta)^{g}=(H^{g},\theta^{g})

and
\theta^{g}\in\Irr(H^{g})
is defined by the formula
\theta^{g}(x^{g})=\theta(x)

for
x\in H
. We use
{\bf N}_{G}(H,\theta)
to denote the stabilizer of
(H,\theta)
in
G
.
Following
[
4
]
, we define a partial order on the set of character pairs of
G

by setting
(H,\theta)\leq(K,\psi)
if
H\leq K
and
\theta
lies under
\psi
.

For the reader’s convenience, we briefly review the theory of
\pi
-special characters
from
[
4
]
. Let
G
be a
\pi
-separable group,
where
\pi
is a set of primes, and let
\chi\in\Irr(G)
.
We say that
\chi
is
\pi
-special
if
\chi(1)
is a
\pi
-number and
the determinantal order
o(\theta)
is a
\pi
-number for every irreducible constituent
\theta
of the restriction
\chi_{S}
for every subnormal subgroup
S
of
G
.
The set of
\pi
-special characters of
G
is denoted
{\rm X}_{\pi}(G)
.

We need some basic properties of
\pi
-special characters.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be
\pi
-separable,
and suppose that
\alpha,\beta\in\Irr(G)
are
\pi
-special and
\pi^{\prime}
-special, respectively.
Then
\alpha\beta
is irreducible.
Also, if
\alpha\beta=\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}
, where
\alpha^{\prime}
is
\pi
-special and
\beta^{\prime}
is
\pi^{\prime}
-special, then
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}
and
\beta=\beta^{\prime}
.

Proof.

This is Theorem 2.2 of
[
4
]
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be
\pi
-separable, and let
H\leq G
have
\pi^{\prime}
-index.
Then restriction
\chi\mapsto\chi_{H}
defines an injection from
{\rm X}_{\pi}(G)
into
{\rm X}_{\pi}(H)
.

Proof.

See Theorem 2.10 of
[
4
]
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
N\vartriangleleft G
, where
G
is a
\pi
-separable group,
and suppose that
\chi\in\Irr(G)
and
\theta\in\Irr(N)
.
Then the following hold.

(1)
If
\chi
is
\pi
-special, then every irreducible constituent of
\chi_{N}
is also
\pi
-special.

(2)
If
G/N
is a
\pi
-group, then every member of
\Irr(G|\theta)

is
\pi
-special.

(3)
If
G/N
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-group and
\theta
is invariant in
G
,
then
\theta
has a unique
\pi
-special extension to
G
.

Proof.

Part (1) follows by definition, and for (2) and (3), see Theorem 2.4 of
[
4
]
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
H
be a Hall
\pi
-subgroup of a
\pi
-separable group
G
,
and let
H\leq T\leq G
. Assume that
\tau\in\Irr(T)
is
\pi
-special
and
\tau^{G}
is irreducible. Write
\beta=\tau_{H}
.
Then
\beta\in\Irr(H)
and
T
is the unique largest subgroup of
G

to which
\beta
extends and
\tau
is the unique
\pi
-special extension of

\beta
to
T
.

Proof.

Since
|T:H|
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-number, it follows by Lemma
2.2
that
\beta
is irreducible.
Suppose that
W
is the largest subgroup of
G
to which
\beta
extends
and let
\gamma
be the unique
\pi
-special extension of
\beta
to
W
.
Then
T\leq W
, and by Lemma
2.2
again, we see that
\gamma_{T}
is also
\pi
-special,
and since both
\gamma_{T}
and
\tau
are extensions of

,

. This completes the proof.

Now we collect some facts of the theory of self-stabilizing pairs needed for our purpose.
Fix a set
\pi
of primes, and let
G
be a
\pi
-separable group.
By a
normal
\pi
-series

\mathcal{N}=\{N_{i}\}
of length
n
of
G
, we mean that
a series of normal subgroups

1=N_{0}\leq N_{1}\leq\cdots\leq N_{n}=G
,
such that
N_{i+1}/N_{i}
is either a
\pi
-group or a
\pi^{\prime}
-group for
i=0,1,\ldots,n-1
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{N}=\{N_{i}\}
be a normal
\pi
-series of a
\pi
-separable group
G
,
and let
(T,\tau)
be a character pair of
G
.
Write
T_{i}=T\cap N_{i}
.
Then
(T,\tau)
is a

for
\mathcal{N}

if for all
i
, the restriction
\tau_{T_{i}}
has a unique irreducible constituent
\tau_{i}

and
T_{i+1}
is the stabilizer of the pair
(T_{i},\tau_{i})
in
N_{i+1}
.

Observe that if
(T,\tau)
is a self-stabilizing pair for
\mathcal{N}
, then we have the following series of character pairs:

(1,1)=(T_{0},\tau_{0})\leq(T_{1},\tau_{1})\leq\cdots\leq(T_{n},\tau_{n})=(T,\tau),

which is called a
self-stabilizing chain
for
\mathcal{N}
.
Writing
\chi=\tau^{G}
, we see that
\chi
is irreducible by the following lemma,
and we also say that
(T,\tau)
is a self-stabilizing pair for

\mathcal{N}
.
Furthermore, by Lemmas 3.2 and 3.3 of
[
6
]
,
we know that all the self-stabilizing pairs for

are conjugate in

.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
G
,
\mathcal{N}
,
(T,\tau)
and
(T_{i},\tau_{i})
be as above.
Then the following hold for all

.

(1)
Induction defines an injection
\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(T_{i},\tau_{i})|\tau_{i})\to\Irr(G)
.

(2)

(\tau_{i})^{N_{i}}
is irreducible.

Proof.

This is a special case of Lemma 3.3 of
[
6
]
.
∎

Following Lewis
[
6
]
, we use
\B(G:\mathcal{N})

to denote the set of characters

that have a self-stabilizing pair

in which

is
\pi
-special,

-number.

Lemma 2.7

Let

be a normal
\pi
-series of a

-separable group

,
and suppose that
(T,\tau)
is a self-stabilizing pair for

]

We begin by introducing some notation.

Fix a set
\pi

\pi

Given a normal
\pi

\mathcal{N}=\{N_{i}\}

In particular, if

is a Hall

-subgroup of

. Furthermore, if

.

We need the following elementary property of normal
\pi
-series.

.

Let
G
be a
\pi
-separable group,

\mathcal{N}=\{N_{i}\}

\pi
-series of length

,
and

The following fundamental result is an essential ingredient in our proofs.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be
\pi
-separable with Hall
\pi
-subgroup
H
,
and let
\mathcal{N}
be a normal
\pi
-series of
G
.
For each
\alpha\in\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})
,
let
W
be the unique largest subgroup of
G
to which
\alpha
extends
and let
\gamma
be the unique
\pi
-special extension of
\alpha
to
W
.
Then
(W,\gamma)
is a self-stabilizing pair for
\mathcal{N}
.

Proof.

We use the notation in Lemma
3.1
.
For each
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
, let
\alpha_{i}\in\Irr(H\cap N_{i})
be the unique irreducible constituent of
\alpha_{H\cap N_{i}}
,
and let
W_{i}=W\cap N_{i}
, so that
H\cap N_{i}\leq W_{i}\leq N_{i}
.
Since
\gamma_{H\cap N_{i}}=(\gamma_{H})_{H\cap N_{i}}=\alpha_{H\cap N_{i}}
is a multiple of
\alpha_{i}
,
it follows that each irreducible constituent of
\gamma_{W_{i}}
lies over
\alpha_{i}
.
But
W_{i}
is normal in
W
, so these constituents are all
\pi
-special
(see Lemma
2.3
),
and by Lemma
2.2
, we see that
\gamma_{W_{i}}

has a unique irreducible constituent, which we call
\gamma_{i}
.
In particular,
\gamma_{i}
is
\pi
-special and invariant in
W
.
Furthermore, since
H\cap N_{i}
is a Hall
\pi
-subgroup of
W_{i}
,
it follows that
\gamma_{i}
is an extension of
\alpha_{i}
to
W_{i}
.
Observe that
W_{i}\leq W_{i+1}
for each
i
, so
\gamma_{i}
lies under
\gamma_{i+1}
.

We claim that
W_{i}
is the unique maximal subgroup of
N_{i}
to which
\alpha_{i}
extends.
To see this, let
U
be the unique largest subgroup with
H\cap N_{i}\leq U\leq N_{i}
,
and such that
\alpha_{i}
extends to
U
.
Then
W_{i}\leq U
and
\alpha_{i}
has a unique
\pi
-special extension
\mu\in\Irr(U)
.
Since
H
normalizes both
N_{i}
and
H\cap N_{i}
and fixes
\alpha_{i}
,
the uniqueness of the pair
(U,\mu)
implies that

H
necessarily normalizes
U
and fixes
\mu
.
In particular,
UH
is a subgroup of
G
with
U\vartriangleleft UH
,
and
\mu
is invariant in
UH
.
Note that
H\cap U=H\cap(N_{i}\cap U)=H\cap N_{i}
,
and thus Lemma 2.11 of
[
4
]
guarantees that
\alpha
extends to
UH
.
By the maximality of
W
, we have
UH\leq W
, and hence
U\leq W\cap N_{i}=W_{i}
.
This proves that
U=W_{i}
, as claimed.

Now we proceed by induction on
|G|
to show that
(W,\gamma)
is
a self-stabilizing pair for
\mathcal{N}
.
We may assume without loss that
(W_{n-1},\gamma_{n-1})
is
a self-stabilizing pair for the normal
\pi
-series
\mathcal{N}_{n-1}
of
the
\pi
-separable group
N_{n-1}
.
Since we have seen that
\gamma_{W_{i}}
is a multiple of
\gamma_{i}
for each
i
,
it suffices by definition to show that
W
is the stabilizer
of the pair
(W_{n-1},\gamma_{n-1})
in
G
.
For notational convenience, we write
N=N_{n-1}
,
\gamma^{\prime}=\gamma_{n-1}
and
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha_{n-1}
, so that
W\cap N=W_{n-1}
and
G/N

is either a
\pi
-group or a
\pi^{\prime}
-group.
Let
T={\bf N}_{G}(W\cap N,\gamma^{\prime})
be the stabilizer of
(W\cap N,\gamma^{\prime})
in
G
.
Then
W\leq T
, and we want to show that
W=T
.

To do this, note that
T\cap N
is the stabilizer of
\gamma^{\prime}
in
N
,
and since
(\gamma^{\prime})^{N}
is irreducible (see Lemma
2.6
),
it follows that
T\cap N=W\cap N
.
If
G/N
is a
\pi
-group, then
TN=G
because
H\leq W\leq T
,
and thus
T=W
, as wanted.
So we can assume that
G/N
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-group,
and in this case, we have
H\leq N
, which implies that

\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}
and that
T/W\cap N
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-group.
Since
\gamma^{\prime}
is
\pi
-special, it follows
by Lemma
2.3
that
\gamma^{\prime}
can be extended to
T
,
and thus
\alpha
also extends to
T
. This forces
T=W
, and
the result follows.
∎

We now generalize the definition of the map
\Psi

introduced by Isaacs and Navarro in
[
5
]
.
Suppose that
H
is a Hall
\pi
-subgroup of a
\pi
-separable group
G
,
and let
\alpha\in\Irr(H)
.
By Corollary 3.15 and Theorem 3.16 of
[
4
]
,
there exists a unique largest subgroup
W
with
H\leq W\leq G
,
and such that
\alpha
has a unique
\pi
-special extension
\gamma\in\Irr(W)
.
In this situation, we can define a new map, which we still call
\Psi
,

\Psi:\Irr(H)\to\Char(G),\;\alpha\mapsto\gamma^{G},

from the set
\Irr(H)
of irreducible characters of
H

into the set
\Char(G)
of characters of
G
by setting
\Psi(\alpha)=\gamma^{G}
.
For convenience, we say that
\Psi
is the
standard map
associated with
H
.

The following are some basic properties of the standard map,
which play an important role for our purpose.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
G
be a
\pi
-separable group with Hall
\pi
-subgroup
H
,
and let

\Psi:\Irr(H)\to\Char(G)

be the standard map defined as above.
Suppose that
\mathcal{N}
is a normal
\pi
-series of
G
. Then the following hold.

(1)
If
\alpha\in\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})
, then
\Psi(\alpha)\in\Irr(G)
.

(2)

\Psi(\Irr(H,\mathcal{N}))={\rm B}_{\pi}^{*}(G:\mathcal{N})
.

(3)
If
\alpha,\beta\in\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})
, then

\Psi(\alpha)=\Psi(\beta)
if and only if
\alpha
and
\beta
are
{\bf N}_{G}(H)
-conjugate.

(4)
For each
\alpha\in\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})
,
let
(W,\gamma)
be as in Lemma
3.2
.
Then the map
\delta\mapsto(\gamma\delta)^{G}

is an injection from the set
{\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}(W)
of
\pi^{\prime}
-special characters
\delta
of
W

into
\Irr(G)
. Write

\Irr_{\alpha}(G)=\{(\gamma\delta)^{G}\,|\,\delta\in{\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}(W)\},

so that
\Irr_{\alpha}(G)\subseteq\Irr(G)
. Also, if
\chi\in\Irr_{\alpha}(G)
, then
\chi(1)_{\pi}=\alpha(1)
.

(5)
Let
\alpha,\beta\in\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})
.
If
\alpha
and
\beta
are
{\bf N}_{G}(H)
-conjugate,
then
\Irr_{\alpha}(G)=\Irr_{\beta}(G)
,
and otherwise,
\Irr_{\alpha}(G)
and
\Irr_{\beta}(G)
are disjoint.

Proof.

By definition, we can write

\mathcal{N}=\{1=N_{0}\leq N_{1}\leq\cdots\leq N_{n}=G\},

where
N_{i}
is normal in
G
and
N_{i}/N_{i-1}
is either a
\pi
-group or a
\pi^{\prime}
-group for
i=1,\ldots,n
.
For convenience, we let
\alpha
be an arbitrary but fixed character in
\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})
,
and write
\Psi(\alpha)=\gamma^{G}
,
where
W
is the unique largest subgroup of
G
to which
\alpha
extends
and
\gamma\in{\rm X}_{\pi}(W)
is the unique
\pi
-special extension of
\alpha
to
W
.

(1) It is immediate by Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
2.6
.

(2) For each
\alpha\in\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})
, we see from Lemma
3.2
that
\Psi(\alpha)\in{\rm B}_{\pi}^{*}(G:\mathcal{N})
, and thus
\Psi(\Irr(H,\mathcal{N}))\subseteq{\rm B}_{\pi}^{*}(G:\mathcal{N})
.
Conversely, let
\chi\in{\rm B}_{\pi}^{*}(G:\mathcal{N})
.
Then there exists a self-stabilizing pair
(T,\tau)
for
\mathcal{N}
with
\chi=\tau^{G}
,
and such that
|G:T|
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-number and
\tau
is
\pi
-special.
Replacing
T
by a conjugate if necessary, we can assume that
H\leq T
.
Let
\beta=\tau_{H}
.
Then
\beta\in\Irr(H)
and
T
is the unique maximal subgroup of
G

to which
\beta
extends (see Lemma
2.4
). Since
\tau_{T\cap N_{i}}
is
a multiple of some irreducible character
\tau_{i}
of
T\cap N_{i}

and
H\cap N_{i}
is a Hall
\pi
-subgroup of
T\cap N_{i}
for each
i
,
it follows that
(\tau_{i})_{H\cap N_{i}}
is irreducible and thus
\beta_{H\cap N_{i}}

is homogeneous. So
\beta\in\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})
, and hence
\Psi(\beta)=\tau^{G}=\chi
. This establishes the reverse containment, thus proving (2).

(3) Assume first that
\alpha
and
\beta
are
{\bf N}_{G}(H)
-conjugate.
Then
\beta=\alpha^{g}
and
H^{g}=H
for some
g\in G
.
In this case, it is easy to see that
W^{g}
is the largest subgroup to which
\beta
extends
and that
\gamma^{g}
is also the unique
\pi
-special extension of
\beta
to
W^{g}
.
By definition, we have
\Psi(\beta)=(\gamma^{g})^{G}=\gamma^{G}=\Psi(\alpha)
.

Conversely, assume that
\chi=\Psi(\alpha)=\Psi(\beta)
.
Then
\chi=\gamma^{G}=\nu^{G}
, where
V
is the unique maximal
subgroup of
G
to which
\beta
extends and
\nu
is the unique
\pi
-special extension
of
\beta
to
V
.
By Lemma
3.2
, we know that both
(W,\gamma)
and
(V,\nu)

are self-stabilizing pairs of
\chi
with respect to
\mathcal{N}
,
so they are conjugate in
G
. Thus
(V,\nu)=(W,\gamma)^{x}
for some
x\in G
.
Now
H\leq V=W^{x}
, so
H^{x^{-1}}
is also a Hall
\pi
-subgroup of
W
.
It follows that
H^{x^{-1}}=H^{w}
for some element
w\in W
,
and thus
y=wx\in{\bf N}_{G}(H)
and
(V,\nu)=(W,\gamma)^{y}
.
This implies that
\nu=\gamma^{y}
, and
since
\nu_{H}=\beta
and
\gamma_{H}=\alpha
, we have
\beta=\alpha^{y}
,
as required.

(4) By Lemma
3.2
again, we see that
(W,\gamma)
is a self-stabilizing pair for
\Psi(\alpha)
with respect to
\mathcal{N}
, and
so the first statement follows by Lemma
2.7
.
Also, if
\chi\in\Irr_{\alpha}(G)
, then
\chi=(\gamma\delta)^{G}

for some
\pi^{\prime}
-special character
\delta
of
W
,
and thus
\chi(1)_{\pi}=(|G:W|\gamma(1)\delta(1))_{\pi}=\gamma(1)_{\pi}=\alpha(1)
.

(5) Suppose that
\alpha
and
\beta
are
{\bf N}_{G}(H)
-conjugate.
Then
\beta=\alpha^{g}
for some
g\in G
and
H^{g}=H
.
It is easy to see that
W^{g}
is the largest subgroup to which
\beta
extends
and
\gamma^{g}
is the unique
\pi
-special extension of
\beta
to
W^{g}
.
By definition, we deduce that

\Irr_{\beta}(G)=\{(\gamma^{g}\delta^{g})^{G}\,|\,\delta^{g}\in{\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}(W^{g})\}=\{(\gamma\delta)^{G}\,|\,\delta\in{\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}(W)\}=\Irr_{\alpha}(G).

Suppose now that there exists some character
\chi\in\Irr_{\alpha}(G)\cap\Irr_{\beta}(G)
,
and it suffices to show that
\alpha
and
\beta
are
{\bf N}_{G}(H)
-conjugate.
As we just established, this will imply that
\Irr_{\alpha}(G)=\Irr_{\beta}(G)
.
We proceed by induction on the length
n
of
\mathcal{N}
(recall that
N_{n}=G\in\mathcal{N}
).
There is nothing to prove if
n=0
(and hence
G=1
),
so we can assume without loss that the result holds for
N=N_{n-1}
.
(Recall that the corresponding normal
\pi
-series of
N
is
\mathcal{N}_{n-1}
defined in Lemma
3.1
.)

As we have seen at the beginning of the proof of Lemma
3.2
,
both
\alpha_{H\cap N}
and
\gamma_{W\cap N}
are homogeneous,
and we let
\alpha^{\prime}
and
\gamma^{\prime}
be the unique irreducible constituents
of
\alpha_{H\cap N}
and
\gamma_{W\cap N}
, respectively.
Furthermore, we know that
W\cap N
is the largest subgroup to which
\alpha^{\prime}
extends
and that
\gamma^{\prime}
is the unique
\pi
-special extension of
\alpha^{\prime}
to
W\cap N
.
Now
\chi\in\Irr_{\alpha}(G)
, so we can write
\chi=(\gamma\delta)^{G}
for some
\delta\in{\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}(W)
.
Let
\delta^{\prime}
be an irreducible constituent of
\delta_{W\cap N}
.
Then
\delta^{\prime}
is
\pi^{\prime}
-special because
W\cap N\vartriangleleft W
,
and since
\gamma^{\prime}
is
\pi
-special,
it follows by Lemma
2.1
that
\gamma^{\prime}\delta^{\prime}
is irreducible and lies under
\gamma\delta
.
In particular,
\chi
lies over
\gamma^{\prime}\delta^{\prime}
.
Observer that
(\gamma^{\prime}\delta^{\prime})^{N}\in\Irr(N)
by (4),
and thus it is an irreducible constituent of
\chi_{N}
.

Similarly, we can do the same thing for
\chi\in\Irr_{\beta}(G)
.
Let
V\geq H
be the largest subgroup of
G
to which
\beta
extends,
and let
\nu\in{\rm X}_{\pi}(V)
be the unique
\pi
-special extension of
\beta
to
V
.
Let
\beta^{\prime}
and
\nu^{\prime}
be the unique irreducible constituents of
\beta_{H\cap N}
and
\nu_{V\cap N}
, respectively. Also, we know that
V\cap N
is the largest subgroup of
N
to which
\beta^{\prime}
extends and that
\nu^{\prime}
is the unique
\pi
-special extension of
\beta^{\prime}
to
W\cap V
. We can write
\chi=(\nu\mu)^{G}
for some
\mu\in{\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}(V)
,
and we fix some character
\mu^{\prime}\in{\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}(V\cap N)
lying under
\mu
.
Then
(\nu^{\prime}\mu^{\prime})^{N}
is also an irreducible constituent of
\chi_{N}
.

Now, we have seen that both
(\gamma^{\prime}\delta^{\prime})^{N}
and
(\nu^{\prime}\mu^{\prime})^{N}

are irreducible constituents of
\chi_{N}
, so that they are conjugate in
G
by Clifford’s theorem.
Since
G/N
is either a
\pi
-group or a
\pi^{\prime}
-group,
we need to distinguish two possibilities.

Assume first that
G/N
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-group.
Then
H\leq N
, and hence
G=N\mathbf{N}_{G}(H)
by the Frattini argument.
It follows that
(\gamma^{\prime}\delta^{\prime})^{N}
and
(\nu^{\prime}\mu^{\prime})^{N}
can be conjugate by an element of
\mathbf{N}_{G}(H)
.
Replacing
\beta
by an appropriate
\mathbf{N}_{G}(H)
-conjugate, we may assume that

(\gamma^{\prime}\delta^{\prime})^{N}=(\nu^{\prime}\mu^{\prime})^{N}
.
It is easy to see that
\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})=\Irr(H,\mathcal{N}_{n-1})
,
and by the inductive hypothesis applied in
N
,
we deduce that
\alpha
and
\beta

are conjugate in
\mathbf{N}_{N}(H)
, and thus they are also
\mathbf{N}_{G}(H)
-conjugate.
The result follows in this case.

Finally, assume that
G/N
is a
\pi
-group. Then
NH=G
,
and thus both
W/W\cap N
and
V/V\cap N
are
\pi
-groups.
It follows that
\delta_{W\cap N}=\delta^{\prime}
and
\mu_{V\cap N}=\mu^{\prime}

by Lemma
2.2
, and in particular, both
\delta^{\prime}
and
\mu^{\prime}
are
H
-invariant.
Since
\gamma_{W\cap N}
is a multiple of
\gamma^{\prime}

and
\nu_{V\cap N}
is a multiple of
\nu^{\prime}
,
we deduce that both
\gamma^{\prime}
and
\nu^{\prime}
are
W
-invariant.
It follows that both
(\gamma^{\prime}\delta^{\prime})^{N}
and
(\nu^{\prime}\mu^{\prime})^{N}

are invariant in
G
, and thus
(\gamma^{\prime}\delta^{\prime})^{N}=(\nu^{\prime}\mu^{\prime})^{N}\in\Irr_{\alpha^{\prime}}(N)\cap\Irr_{\beta^{\prime}}(N)
.
By the inductive hypothesis applied in
N
,
we see that
\alpha^{\prime}
and
\beta^{\prime}
are conjugate in
\mathbf{N}_{N}(H\cap N)
,
and hence
\alpha^{\prime}=(\beta^{\prime})^{x}
for some
x\in\mathbf{N}_{N}(H\cap N)
.
Note that the pairs
(W\cap N,\gamma^{\prime})

and
(V\cap N,\nu^{\prime})
are uniquely determined by
\alpha^{\prime}
and
\beta^{\prime}
, respectively,
and so
(W\cap N,\gamma^{\prime})=(V\cap N,\nu^{\prime})^{x}
.
This implies that
\delta^{\prime}=(\mu^{\prime})^{x}
by the injection map defined in (4).
Also, since each of
(W,\gamma)
and
(V,\nu)
is a self-stabilizing pair for
\mathcal{N}

by Lemma
3.2
, we know that
W
and
V
are the stabilizers in
G
of
the pairs
(W\cap N,\gamma^{\prime})
and
(V\cap N,\nu^{\prime})
, respectively,
and thus
W=V^{x}
.
Now both
\gamma\delta
and
\nu^{x}\mu^{x}
are irreducible characters of
W

that induce
\chi
and lie over
\gamma^{\prime}\delta^{\prime}
,
and since induction defines a bijection

\Irr(W|\gamma^{\prime}\delta^{\prime})\to\Irr(G|(\gamma^{\prime}\delta^{\prime})^{N})
(see Lemma 2.12 of
[
4
]
),
we conclude that
\gamma\delta=\nu^{x}\mu^{x}
, which implies that
\gamma=\nu^{x}

(see Lemma
2.1
).
Observe that
H^{x}\leq V^{x}=W
, so
H=H^{xw}
for some
w\in W
,
and we have
\gamma=\gamma^{w}=\nu^{xw}
.
Recall that
\alpha=\gamma_{H}
and
\beta=\nu_{H}
,
so
\alpha=\beta^{xw}
with
xw\in\mathbf{N}_{G}(H)
,
as required.
The proof is now complete.

As an application of Theorem
3.3
, we prove the following,
which lies at heart of the proof of Theorem
A
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
G
be a
\pi
-separable group with Hall
\pi
-subgroup
H
,
and let
\mathcal{N}
be a normal
\pi
-series of
G
.
Then
\chi\in\Irr(G)
is
(\mathcal{N},\pi)
-stable if and only if
\chi\in\Irr_{\alpha}(G)

for some
\alpha\in\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})
, and thus
\Irr(G,\mathcal{N})=\bigcup\Irr_{\alpha}(G)
,
where
\alpha
runs over
\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})
.

Proof.

Suppose first that
\chi\in\Irr_{\alpha}(G)
for some
\alpha\in\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})
.
Then by definition, we can write
\chi=(\gamma\delta)^{G}
with
\gamma\in{\rm X}_{\pi}(W)
and
\delta\in{\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}(W)
, where
W
is the unique maximal subgroup of
G
to which
\alpha
extends and
\gamma
is the unique
\pi
-special extension of
\alpha
to
W
.
By Lemma
3.2
, we see that
(W,\gamma)
is a self-stabilizing pair of
\gamma^{G}
for
\mathcal{N}
, and since
|G:W|
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-number, we have
\gamma^{G}\in{\rm B}_{\pi}^{*}(G:\mathcal{N})
.
Furthermore, let
W_{i}=W\cap N_{i}
for each
i
.
By Definition
2.5
, we know that
\gamma_{W_{i}}
has a unique irreducible constituent
\gamma_{i}
and that
(W_{i},\gamma_{i})
is a self-stabilizing pair for
\mathcal{N}_{i}
,
where
\mathcal{N}_{i}
is defined in Lemma
3.1
.
It follows by Lemma
2.6
that
\psi_{i}=(\gamma_{i})^{N_{i}}
is irreducible,
and observe that
|N_{i}:W_{i}|=|N_{i}W:W|
divides
|G:W|
, which is a
\pi^{\prime}
-number,
and thus
\psi_{i}\in{\rm B}_{\pi}^{*}(N_{i},\mathcal{N}_{i})
.
Furthermore, for each
i
we can choose some irreducible constituents
\delta_{i}
of
\delta_{W_{i}}
such that
\delta_{i}
lies under
\delta_{i+1}
.
Then we have
(W_{i},\delta_{i})\leq(W_{n},\delta_{n})=(W,\delta)
.
Since
\delta
is
\pi^{\prime}
-special and
W_{i}\vartriangleleft W
, it follows that each
\delta_{i}
is also
\pi^{\prime}
-special.

Now we consider the case where
N_{j+1}/N_{j}
is a
\pi
-group for some
j
.
Then
N_{j+1}=N_{j}W_{j+1}
because
|N_{j+1}:W_{j+1}|
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-number.
Since
\gamma_{j}
is
W_{j+1}
-invariant and induces
\psi_{j}
,
it follows that
\psi_{j}
is invariant in
N_{j+1}
.
On the other hand, note that
|W_{j+1}:W_{j}|=|N_{j+1}:N_{j}|
, which is a
\pi
-number,
and thus
\delta_{j+1}
restricts irreducibly to
W_{j}
. This implies that
\delta_{j+1}
is an extension of
\delta_{j}
,
and hence
\delta_{j}
is
W_{j+1}
-invariant.
Writing
\theta_{j}=(\gamma_{j}\delta_{j})^{N_{j}}
, we deduce that
\theta_{j}
is fixed by
N_{j+1}
.
By Lemma
2.7
, we see that
\theta_{j}
is irreducible, and
since
\gamma_{j}\delta_{j}
lies under
\gamma\delta
, it follows that
\theta_{j}
lies under
\chi
.
This shows that
\chi
is
(\mathcal{N},\pi)
-stable, as wanted.

Conversely, suppose that
\chi
is
(\mathcal{N},\pi)
-stable.
By definition, there exist characters
\theta_{i}\in\Irr(N_{i})
for
i=0,1,\ldots,n
, with

(N_{0},\theta_{0})\leq(N_{1},\theta_{1})\leq\cdots\leq(N_{n},\theta_{n})=(G,\chi),

and such that each
\theta_{i}
is invariant in
N_{i+1}
whenever
N_{i+1}/N_{i}
is a
\pi
-group.
Since
\mathcal{N}_{i}
is a normal
\pi
-series of
N_{i}
for all
i
, we see that
\theta_{i}
is
(\mathcal{N}_{i},\pi)
-stable.
By induction on
n
, we may assume without loss that
\theta_{n-1}\in\Irr_{\beta}(N_{n-1})

for some
\beta\in\Irr(H\cap N_{n-1},\mathcal{N}_{n-1})
.
For notational convenience, we write
N=N_{n-1}
,
\theta=\theta_{n-1}
and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}=\mathcal{N}_{n-1}
. Then
\beta\in\Irr(H\cap N,\mathcal{N}^{\prime})
,
and we want to show that there exists some
\alpha\in\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})
such that
\chi\in\Irr_{\alpha}(G)
.

By definition, we can write
\theta=(\nu\mu)^{N}
with
\nu\in{\rm X}_{\pi}(V)
and
\mu\in{\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}(V)
,
where
V
is the unique maximal subgroup of
N
to which
\beta
extends and
\nu
is the unique
\pi
-special extension of
\beta
to
V
.
By Lemma
3.2
again, we see that
(V,\nu)
is a self-stabilizing pair of
\nu^{N}
for
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
,
and since
|N:V|
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-number, we have
\nu^{N}\in{\rm B}_{\pi}^{*}(N:\mathcal{N}^{\prime})
.
Let
W={\bf N}_{G}(V,\nu)
be the stabilizer of
(V,\nu)
in
G
.
Then
W\cap N=V
because
\nu^{N}
is irreducible.
Since
G/N
is either a
\pi
-group or a
\pi^{\prime}
-group, we have two possibilities.

Assume first that
G/N
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-group, so that
H=H\cap N
is a Hall
\pi
-subgroup of
N
,
and thus
|G:V|
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-number. In particular,
W/V
is also a
\pi^{\prime}
-group,
and hence
\nu
can be extended to
W
. By Corollary 3.16 of
[
4
]
,
we see that
\nu
has a unique
\pi
-special extension
\hat{\nu}
to
W
,
and thus by definition
(W,\hat{\nu})
is a self-stabilizing pair for
\mathcal{N}
.
Writing
\psi=\hat{\nu}^{G}
, we have
\psi\in{\rm B}_{\pi}^{*}(G:\mathcal{N})
.
Furthermore, note that
\chi
lies over
\theta
and hence over
\nu\mu
,
so there exists
\tau\in\Irr(W)
lying under
\chi
and over
\nu\mu
.
But
(\nu\mu)^{W}=\hat{\nu}\mu^{W}
and each irreducible constituent of
\mu^{W}

is
\pi^{\prime}
-special, and thus
\tau=\hat{\nu}\delta
, where
\delta
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-special character of
W
. In this case, we know that
\tau^{G}
is irreducible by Lemma
2.7
,
which forces
\chi=(\hat{\nu}\delta)^{G}
, and since
H
is a Hall
\pi
-subgroup of
N

and
\hat{\nu}_{H}=\nu_{H}=\beta
, it is easy to see that
W
is exactly the unique maximal subgroup of
G
to which
\beta
can be extended (see Lemma
2.4
).
Hence
\chi\in\Irr_{\beta}(G)

with
\beta\in\Irr(H\cap N,\mathcal{N}^{\prime})=\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})
.

Next, we suppose that
G/N
is a
\pi
-group. Then
G=NH
,
and the
(\mathcal{N},\pi)
-stability of
\chi
implies that
\theta
is invariant in
G
.
We claim that
\nu^{N}
is also invariant in
G
.
To see this, fix
h\in H
, and observe that
\theta^{h}=\theta

and
\beta^{h}\in\Irr(H\cap N,\mathcal{N}^{\prime})
.
Also,
V^{h}
is the unique maximal subgroup of
N
to which
\beta^{h}
extends and

\nu^{h}
is the unique
\pi
-special extension of
\beta^{h}
to
V^{h}
.
By Theorem
3.3
(4), with
N
,
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
and
H\cap N
in place of
G
,

\mathcal{N}
and
H
, respectively,
we see that
\theta\in\Irr_{\beta}(N)\cap\Irr_{\beta^{h}}(N)
.
It follows by Theorem
3.3
(5) that
\beta
and
\beta^{h}
are conjugate in
{\bf N}_{N}(H\cap N)
, and by Theorem
3.3
(3), we deduce that
\nu^{N}=(\nu^{h})^{N}=(\nu^{N})^{h}
.
This shows that
\nu^{N}
is
H
-invariant, and hence is invariant in
G
,
as claimed.

Furthermore, since all of the self-stabilizing pairs of
\nu^{N}
for
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
are conjugate in
N
,
we have
G=NW
by the Frattini argument, and thus
|G:W|=|N:(W\cap N)|=|N:V|
,
which is a
\pi^{\prime}
-number. Also, since
|W:V|
divides
|G:N|
,
we see that
W/V
is a
\pi
-group.
By Lemma
2.7
again, we see that
\mu
is invariant in
W
because both
\theta
and
(V,\nu)

are fixed by
W
, and thus
\mu
has a unique
\pi^{\prime}
-special extension
\hat{\mu}\in{\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}(W)
.
Reasoning as before, since
\chi
lies over
\nu\mu
,
there exists some
\rho\in\Irr(W)
lying under
\chi
and over
\nu\mu
,
and observe that
(\nu\mu)^{W}=\nu^{W}\hat{\mu}
and that each irreducible constituent of
\nu^{W}
is
\pi
-special,
so we have
\rho=\gamma\hat{\mu}
, where
\gamma\in{\rm X}_{\pi}(W)
lies over
\nu
.
Now
(W,\gamma)
is a self-stabilizing pair for
\mathcal{N}
by definition,
and since
|G:W|
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-number, we have
\gamma^{G}\in{\rm B}_{\pi}^{*}(G:\mathcal{N})
.
This implies that
\chi=\rho^{G}=(\gamma\hat{\mu})^{G}
.
Furthermore, let
J
be a Hall
\pi
-subgroup of
W
containing
H\cap N
and let
\alpha^{\prime}=\gamma_{J}
.
Then
\alpha^{\prime}\in\Irr(J,\mathcal{N})
and
J
is also a Hall
\pi
-subgroup of
G
,
and hence
J\cap N=H\cap N
.
Also, since
\gamma
is a
\pi
-special extension of
\alpha^{\prime}
to
W
and induces irreducibly to
G
,
we see that
W
is the unique maximal subgroup of
G
to which
\alpha^{\prime}

can be extended, and by definition we have
\chi\in\Irr_{\alpha^{\prime}}(G)
.
Now
J
and
H
are conjugate in
G
, so
H=J^{g}
for some
g\in G
.
Write
\alpha=(\alpha^{\prime})^{g}
. Then
(J,\alpha^{\prime})^{g}=(H,\alpha)
,
and it follows that
\alpha\in\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})
.
This shows that
\chi\in\Irr_{\alpha}(G)
, and the proof is now complete.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
H
be a Hall
\pi
-subgroup of a
\pi
-separable group
G
,
and suppose that
\mathcal{N}
is a normal
\pi
-series of
G
.
Then the following hold.

(1)

|\Irr_{\alpha}(G)|=|\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(H)|\alpha)|
for each
\alpha\in\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})
.

(2)

\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(H),\mathcal{N})=\bigcup\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(H)|\alpha)
,
where
\alpha
runs over
\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})
.

Proof.

(1) Fix
\alpha\in\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})
, and as before,
let
W
be the unique largest subgroup of
G
to which
\alpha
extends
and let
\gamma
be the unique
\pi
-special extension of
\alpha
to
W
.
Then it is easy to see that
\mathbf{N}_{W}(H)
is also the unique largest subgroup of
\mathbf{N}_{G}(H)
to which
\alpha
extends and that
\gamma^{\prime}
is also the unique
\pi
-special extension of
\alpha
to
\mathbf{N}_{W}(H)
, where
\gamma^{\prime}
is the restriction of
\gamma
to
\mathbf{N}_{W}(H)
.
Since
H
is a normal Hall
\pi
-subgroup of
\mathbf{N}_{G}(H)
, it follows that
\mathbf{N}_{W}(H)
is exactly the inertial group of
\alpha
in
\mathbf{N}_{G}(H)
.
By the Clifford correspondence, together with Gallagher’s theorem (see Corollary 6.17 of
[
3
]
), we conclude that
the map
\mu\mapsto(\gamma^{\prime}\mu)^{{\bf N}_{G}(H)}
is a bijection
from
\Irr(\mathbf{N}_{W}(H)/H)
onto
\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(H)|\alpha)
.
Furthermore, observe that
\mathbf{N}_{W}(H)/H
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-group and
that
H
is contained in the kernel of every
\pi^{\prime}
-special character of
\mathbf{N}_{W}(H)
.
Thus
\Irr(\mathbf{N}_{W}(H)/H)={\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}(\mathbf{N}_{W}(H))
,
and so we obtain a bijection from
{\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}(\mathbf{N}_{W}(H))
onto
\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(H)|\alpha)
.
In particular, we have
|{\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}(\mathbf{N}_{W}(H))|=|\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(H)|\alpha)|
.

On the other hand, we obtain
|\Irr_{\alpha}(G)|=|{\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}(W)|
by Theorem
3.3
(4).
Also, by Lemma
2.8
(with the roles of
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
interchanged),
we know that
|{\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}(W)|=|{\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}(\mathbf{N}_{W}(H))|
,
and thus
|\Irr_{\alpha}(G)|=|\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(H)|\alpha)|
, as required.

(2) Let
\chi\in\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(H))
, and let
\alpha\in\Irr(H)

be a constituent of
\chi_{H}
.
By definition, it is easy to see that
\chi\in\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(H),\mathcal{N})

if and only if
\alpha\in\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})
,
and the result follows. This completes the proof.
∎

Now Theorem
C
follows by Theorems
3.3
and
3.4
and Corollary
3.5

(with
p
in place of
\pi
).

Proof of Theorem
A
.

By definition, we have
\Irr_{p^{\prime}}(G)\subseteq\Irr_{0}(G,\mathcal{N})
,
and the reverse containment is clear. Thus (1) follows.
For each integer
i\geq 0
, let
\Delta_{i}
be a set of representatives
for the orbits of the conjugation action of
{\bf N}_{G}(P)
on
\Irr_{i}(P,\mathcal{N})
.
By Theorem
3.4
and Theorem
3.3
(5),
we can write

\Irr_{i}(G,\mathcal{N})=\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in\Delta_{i}}\Irr_{\alpha}(G),

and by Corollary
3.5
, we deduce that

\Irr_{i}({\bf N}_{G}(P),\mathcal{N})=\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in\Delta_{i}}\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(P)|\alpha)

and that
|\Irr_{\alpha}(G)|=|\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(P)|\alpha)|

for each
\alpha\in\Irr(P,\mathcal{N})
.
Thus

|\Irr_{i}(G,\mathcal{N})|=|\Irr_{i}({\bf N}_{G}(P),\mathcal{N})|,

and (2) holds. In particular, we obtain

|\Irr(G,\mathcal{N})|=\sum_{i\geq 0}|\Irr_{i}(G,\mathcal{N})|=\sum_{i\geq 0}|\Irr_{i}({\bf N}_{G}(P),\mathcal{N})|=|\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(P),\mathcal{N})|,

and (4) follows.

Now we fix an integer
i\geq 0
. For each
\chi\in\Irr(G,\mathcal{N})
,
we have seen that
\chi\in\Irr_{i}(G,\mathcal{N})
if and only if
\chi\in\Irr_{\alpha}(G)

for some character
\alpha\in\Irr_{i}(P,\mathcal{N})
,
which is equivalent to saying that

\chi(1)_{p}=\alpha(1)=p^{i}
by Theorem
3.3
(4).
This proves (3), and the proof is complete.

The following result covers Theorem
B
in the introduction
(by taking
\pi=\{p\}
).

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
G
be a solvable group of odd order, and let
H\in\Hall_{\pi}(G)
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{N}
is a normal
\pi
-series of
G
.
Then there is a canonical bijection from
\Irr(G,\mathcal{N})
onto
\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(H),\mathcal{N})
.
Also, if
\xi\in\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(H),\mathcal{N})
is the image of

\chi\in\Irr(G,\mathcal{N})
under this map, then

\xi
is a constituent of
\chi_{{\bf N}_{G}(H)}

and
\xi(1)
divides
\chi(1)
.

Furthermore, for each
i\geq 0
, the bijection
maps
\Irr_{i}(G,\mathcal{N})
onto
\Irr_{i}({\bf N}_{G}(H),\mathcal{N})
,
and thus defines a canonical bijection between these two sets.

Proof.

Let
\chi\in\Irr(G,\mathcal{N})
.
By Theorem
3.4
, we know that
\chi\in\Irr_{\alpha}(G)
, where
\alpha\in\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})
.
Let
W
be the unique maximal subgroup of
G
to which
\alpha
extends and let
\gamma
be the unique
\pi
-special extension of
\alpha
to
W
.
Then by Theorem
3.3
(4), we have
\chi=(\gamma\delta)^{G}

for some
\delta\in{\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}(W)
. Let
\gamma^{\prime}
be the restriction of
\gamma
to
{\bf N}_{W}(H)
,
and note that
|W:{\bf N}_{W}(H)|
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-number,
so
\gamma^{\prime}
is also
\pi
-special (see Lemma
2.2
).
By Lemma
3.6
, we have a canonical bijection
from
{\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}(W)
onto
{\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}({\bf N}_{W}(H))
,
and we write
\delta^{*}
for the image of
\delta\in{\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}(W)
under this map.
As in the proof of Corollary
3.5
,
the map
\mu\mapsto(\gamma^{\prime}\mu)^{{\bf N}_{G}(H)}
is a bijection
from
{\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}(\mathbf{N}_{W}(H))
onto
\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(H)|\alpha)
.
Let
\tilde{\chi}=(\gamma^{\prime}\delta^{*})^{{\bf N}_{G}(H)}
.
Since
\delta^{*}\in{\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}(\mathbf{N}_{W}(H))
, it follows that
\tilde{\chi}\in\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(H)|\alpha)
.

We claim that
\tilde{\chi}
is independent of the choice of
\alpha
.
To see this, assume that
\chi\in\Irr_{\beta}(G)
, where
\beta\in\Irr(H,\mathcal{N})
.
Then
\beta=\alpha^{x}
for some element
x\in{\bf N}_{G}(H)
by Theorem
3.3
(5),
and thus
W^{x}
is the unique maximal subgroup of
G
to which
\beta
extends and

\gamma^{x}
is the unique
\pi
-special extension of
\beta
to
W^{x}
.
Also,
(\gamma^{\prime})^{x}
is the restriction of
\gamma^{x}
to
{\bf N}_{W^{x}}(H)
,
and the canonical bijection from
{\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}(W^{x})
onto
{\rm X}_{\pi^{\prime}}({\bf N}_{W^{x}}(H))

yields
\delta^{x}\mapsto(\delta^{x})^{*}=(\delta^{*})^{x}
.
Thus

((\gamma^{\prime})^{x}(\delta^{*})^{x})^{{\bf N}_{G}(H)}=(\gamma^{\prime}\delta^{*})^{{\bf N}_{G}(H)}=\tilde{\chi},

and so
\tilde{\chi}
is uniquely determined by
\chi
, as claimed.

Now the map
\chi=(\gamma\delta)^{G}\mapsto\tilde{\chi}=(\gamma^{\prime}\delta^{*})^{{\bf N}_{G}(H)}
defines a canonical bijection
from
\Irr(G,\mathcal{N})
onto
\Irr({\bf N}_{G}(H),\mathcal{N})
.
By Lemma
3.6
, we see that
\delta^{*}
lies under
\delta

and
\delta^{*}(1)
divides
\delta(1)
.
It follows that
\gamma^{\prime}\delta^{*}
lies under
\gamma\delta
,
and thus
\tilde{\chi}
is a constituent of
\chi_{{\bf N}_{G}(H)}
.
Note that
\chi(1)=|G:W|\gamma(1)\delta(1)

and

\tilde{\chi}(1)=|{\bf N}_{G}(H):{\bf N}_{W}(H)|\gamma(1)\delta^{*}(1).

Also, by Lemma
3.7
, we deduce that
|{\bf N}_{G}(H):{\bf N}_{W}(H)|

divides
|G:W|
, and thus
\tilde{\chi}(1)
divides
\chi(1)
.

Finally, since
\chi(1)_{p}=\gamma(1)_{p}=\tilde{\chi}(1)_{p}
,
it follows that our canonical bijection
maps
\Irr_{i}(G,\mathcal{N})
onto
\Irr_{i}({\bf N}_{G}(H),\mathcal{N})
.
This completes the proof.
∎

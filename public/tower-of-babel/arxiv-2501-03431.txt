1.
Introduction

The étale fundamental group of an integral normal variety plays an important role in studying various problems in arithmetic geometry. The maximal abelian profinite quotient of this group, known as the abelianized étale fundamental group, is one of the main objects of study in geometric class field theory. This group
has been extensively studied for smooth projective varieties
over finite and local fields throughout the decades (see
[
KL81
]
,
[
Blo81
]
,
[
KS83(2)
]
,
[
Sai85
]
,
[
JS03
]
,
[
Yos03
]
,
[
For15
]
,
[
Hir18
]
, and
[
GKR22
]
).
In this paper, we study this group for a regular, not necessarily smooth, projective variety over a local field of positive characteristic.

Let
X
be an integral normal scheme over a field
k
. We denote by
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)
the abelianized étale fundamental group of
X
. Recall that
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)
classifies all finite abelian étale coverings over
X
. The objective of this paper is to study the torsion subgroup of this group for a regular geometrically integral quasi-projective variety over a local field.
We let
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)^{{\rm geom}}:={\rm Ker}(\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)\rightarrow\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}({\rm Spec}(k)))
, which is called the geometric part of the group
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)
.
In the case where
k
is finite or local field, the local class field theory provides an explicit structure for the group
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}({\rm Spec}(k))
and therefore the study of
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)
in these cases is reduced to the study of the group
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)^{{\rm geom}}
.

Let
k
be a finite field of characteristic
p
and let
X
be a smooth geometrically connected projective scheme over
k
. Then the group
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)^{{\rm geom}}
is known to be finite by Katz-Lang
[
KL81
]
. More generally, if
X
is smooth geometrically connected finite type scheme over
k
, then
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)^{{\rm geom}}
is finite, except for its pro-
p
subgroup.

Let us now assume that
k
is a local field, i.e., a complete, discrete-valued field with finite residue field. Let
X
be a smooth geometrically connected projective scheme over
k
. Then the group
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)^{{\rm geom}}
is not necessarily finite but it is known to be a topologically finitely generated profinite group. More precisely, we have

\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)^{{\rm geom}}\cong F\oplus\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
, where
F
is a finite group and
r\geq 0
. This structure theorem was proven by Grothendieck
[
SGA1
]
and Yoshida
[
Yos03
]
. This result was recently extended to
smooth quasi-projective varieties having smooth compactifications (except for the pro-
p
subgroup)
[
GKR22
, Theorem 1.1]
.
These results, in particular, imply that the torsion subgroup
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)_{{\rm tor}}
of the abelian fundamental group is finite for smooth projective varieties and the torsion subgroup except the
p
-primary torsion is finite in the case of smooth quasi-projective varieties.

The main objective of this paper is to extend these finiteness results to the case of regular (not necessarily smooth), projective (possibly quasi-projective), geometrically connected varieties over local fields of positive characteristic. Such questions do not arise over finite fields, as regular quasi-projective schemes over finite fields are smooth. One of the consequences of the finiteness of the group
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)_{{\rm tor}}
is that it plays a key role in the study of class field theory for
X
(see
[
JS03
]
,
[
For15
]
,
[
Hir18
]
, and
[
GKR22
]
). As an application of such a finiteness result for regular varieties, we extend the results of class field theory for smooth projective curves to regular projective curves over local fields.

We now state our main results.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k
be a local field of positive characteristic
p>0
and let
X
be a geometrically integral scheme over
k
.

(1)

If
X
is regular and projective, then
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)_{{\rm tor}}
is finite.

(2)

If
X
is regular and quasi-projective, then
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)_{{\rm tor}}
(except for
p
-torsion) is finite.

1.1.
Application

As mentioned earlier, the finiteness of the group
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)_{{\rm tor}}
plays
a key role in the study of class field theory of
X
. In this light,
we apply Theorem
1.1
to prove results in unramified class field theory for regular projective curves over local fields. To do this, we first study the group
SK_{1}(X)
for an integral projective scheme
X
over a local field
k
, which is defined as

SK_{1}(X):={{\rm coker}}\Big(\oplus_{x\in X_{(1)}}K_{2}(k(x))\rightarrow\oplus_{x\in X_{(0)}}K_{1}(k(x))\Big)
.

We let
V(X/k):={{\rm Ker}}\Big(SK_{1}(X)\xrightarrow{{{\rm Norm}}}k^{\times}\Big)
. These groups play a key role in studying the class field theory for smooth projective varieties over local fields via the reciprocity map
\rho_{X}\colon SK_{1}(X)\to\pi_{1}^{{\rm ab}}(X)
and the induced map
\rho_{X,0}\colon V(X/k)\to\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)^{{\rm geom}}
.

These groups were introduced by Bloch
[
Blo81
]
to study the unramified class field theory for a certain class of regular schemes.
It is shown in loc. cit. that when
X
is an elliptic curve over a local field, we have
V(X/k)\cong T\oplus D
, where
T
is a torsion group and

is a divisible group (
[
Blo81
, Corollary 2.38]
). This was later generalized to smooth projective curves and further extended to smooth projective varieties over local fields (see
[
Sai85
]
,
[
Yos03
]
,
[
JS03
]
, and
[
For15
]
).
We extend this result to geometrically irreducible schemes over local fields of characteristic
p>0
and prove the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a geometrically irreducible projective integral scheme of dimension
d\geq 0
over a local field
k
of positive characteristic
p>0
. Then

(1.1)

V(X/k)=T\oplus D,

where
T
is a torsion group and
D
is a divisible group.

The class field theory for smooth projective curve
X
over a local field was first studied by Bloch
[
Blo81
]
when
X
has good reduction and this was later generalized to smooth projective geometrically connected curves by Saito
[
Sai85
]
(prime to
p
-part) and by Yoshida
[
Yos03
]
(
p
-part). Here, we extend these results to integral regular projective curves.

We let
k
denote a local field of positive characteristic
p>0
and let
X
be a geometrically integral regular projective curve over
k
. We then have a reciprocity map (see
[
Sai85
]
,

[
KS83
, Section 1.5]
or
[
GKR22

)

(1.2)

\rho_{X}\colon SK_{1}(X)\to\pi_{1}^{{\rm ab}}(X).

We let
\rho_{X,0}\colon V(X/k)\to\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)^{{\rm geom}}
denote the induced map. We then have the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X
be as above. Then

(1)

{\rm Ker}(\rho_{X})
is divisible by every integer prime to
p
. Moreover, if we assume further that

is generically smooth, then
{\rm Ker}(\rho_{X})
is the maximal divisible subgroup of

(2)

(3)

1.2.
Idea of the proofs

We prove Theorem
1.1
in two steps. We first prove that the prime to
p
-torsion subgroup
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)\{p^{\prime}\}
is finite. The main ingredient to prove this is to show that for a dense open subset
U\subset X
,

\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)\{p^{\prime}\}
is finite if and only if
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)\{p^{\prime}\}
is finite. We then use an alteration of degree
p^{r}
of
X
and the smooth case to get hold of a dense open subset
U
such that
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)\{p^{\prime}\}
is finite.
The second and final step is to show that the
p
-torsion subgroup

\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)\{p\}
is finite (see Theorem
4.5
).
Since the abelian absolute Galois group
G^{{\rm ab}}_{k}
has no
p
-torsion for local fields, Theorem
4.5
follows if we show that the
p
-primary torsion subgroup of
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)^{{\rm geom}}
is finite. This is achieved in Proposition
4.4
.

We first reduce Theorem
1.2
to the case when
X
is a geometrically irreducible projective integral curve in Section
5.1
. We then use the fact that for such a curve, there is a nice alteration map to get a stronger result where we can assume that torsion summand
T
is of finite exponent (see Theorem
5.6
).

In Section
6
, we prove duality results for regular projective curves and use them to show that the reciprocity map modulo
n
is injective for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, see Theorems
6.5
and
6.8
. As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we then obtain Theorem
1.3
in Section
6.3
.

1.3.
Notations

For an abelian group

and

, we let

A\xrightarrow{n}A
. For a prime

-divisible (resp.

(resp. divisible by

for

).
For a torsion group

.

For a field

, where

For a finite type scheme

over a field and

i
-dimensional points of

. For

All cohomology groups considered in the paper are étale cohomology groups.

2.
étale cohomology groups

Let
k
be a field of exponential characteristic
p\geq 1
and let
X
be a quasi-projective scheme over
k
. Recall that for
m\geq 1
such that

{{\rm gcd}}(p,m)=1
, the presheaf
\mu_{m,X}
of
m
-th roots of unity is an étale sheaf on
X
. For
j\geq 0
, we let

{\mathbb{Z}}/m(j)_{X}=\mu_{m,X}^{\otimes j}
and for
j<0
, we let

{\mathbb{Z}}/m(j)={\mathcal{H}om}({\mathbb{Z}}/m(-j)_{X},{\mathbb{Z}}/m)
. For a prime
l\neq p
and
i,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we let
H^{i}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{l}(j))=\varinjlim_{n}H^{i}(X,{\mathbb{Z}}/l^{n}(j))
and let

(2.1)

H^{i}(X,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime}(j)):=\oplus_{l\neq p}H^{i}(X,{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{l}(j)),

where
l
runs over all prime integers different from
p
.
For a closed subset
Z\subset X
, we similarly define support étale cohomology groups

H^{i}_{Z}(X,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime}(j))
.
We begin by recalling the following result from
[
Fuj02
, § 8]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
Y
be a regular finite type scheme over a field
k
of dimension
d\geq 1
and let
W\subset Y
be a closed subset of codimension
c\geq 1
. Let
m
be an integer that is invertible in
k
and let
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. We then have

(2.2)

H^{i}_{W}(Y,{\mathbb{Z}}/m(j))=0\textnormal{ for all $i<2c$}.

Using Lemma
2.1
and the support-cohomology long exact sequence, we immediately get the following result.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
Y
be a regular finite type scheme over a field
k
of dimension
d\geq 1
and let
U\subset Y
be an open subset such that its complement
Y\setminus U
has codimension
c\geq 1
. Then the pull-back

H^{i}(Y,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime}(j))\to H^{i}(U,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime}(j))
is an isomorphism for all
i\leq 2c-2
and
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

We use the above corollary to prove the finiteness of certain étale cohomology groups.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
k
be a local field and let
Z
be a regular quasi-projective variety over
k
. Then the group
H^{0}(Z,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime}(-1))
is finite.

Proof.

If
Z
is regular projective variety over a local field, then the lemma follows from
[
GKR22
, Lemma 10.9]
. Indeed, in the loc. cit., they proved that for a projective scheme
W
of dimension
d\geq 0
over a local field and
i\geq 0
, the group
H^{i}(W,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime}(j))
is finite for all
j\leq 0
and for all
j\geq d+2
.

We now let
Z
be a regular quasi-projective integral variety over
k
and let

Z\hookrightarrow\overline{Z}
be a compactification of
Z
. Let
\pi\colon W\to\overline{Z}
be an alteration morphism of de Jong and Gabber
[
Tem17
]
. In particular, the morphism
\pi
is generically finite of degree
e
, where
e=1
if
{{\rm Char}}(k)=0
and
e=p^{r}
if
{{\rm Char}}(k)=p>0
. (Note that by a generically finite map of degree
1
, we mean a birational map.)
Let
V\hookrightarrow\overline{Z}
be a dense open subset of
\overline{Z}
such that the induced map
\pi_{V^{\prime}}\colon V^{\prime}=\pi^{-1}(V)\to V
is finite and flat of degree
e
. Since
Z
is integral, the intersection
U=V\cap Z
is a dense open subset of
Z
. We let
\pi_{U^{\prime}}\colon U^{\prime}=\pi^{-1}(U)\to U
denote the induced map. Since
\pi_{V^{\prime}}
is finite and flat of degree
e
, it follows that the morphism
\pi_{U^{\prime}}
is also finite and flat of degree
e
. We consider the following diagram.

(2.3)

\textstyle{H^{0}(Z,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime}(-1))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{H^{0}(U,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime}(-1))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{U^{\prime}}^{*}}
\textstyle{H^{0}(W,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime}(-1))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{H^{0}(U^{\prime},({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime}(-1)).}

Note that the horizontal arrows are isomorphisms by Corollary
2.2
(with
c\geq 1
) because
U
(resp.
U^{\prime}
) is a dense open subset of
Z
(resp. of
W
), and both
Z
and
W
are regular finite type schemes over
k
. Since
W
is a regular projective scheme over a local field, it follows from the first paragraph that the bottom left group
H^{0}(W,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime}(-1))
is a finite group. It therefore suffices to show that the right vertical arrow
\pi_{U^{\prime}}^{*}\colon H^{0}(U,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime}(-1))\to H^{0}(U^{\prime},({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime}(-1))
is injective. But this follows from
[
SGA4
, XVII, Proposition 6.2.5]
. Indeed, by the loc. cit., we have a map

(\pi_{U^{\prime}})_{*}\colon H^{0}(U^{\prime},({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime}(-1))\to H^{0}(U,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime}(-1))
such that

(\pi_{U^{\prime}})_{*}\circ\pi_{U^{\prime}}^{*}={{\rm deg}}(\pi_{U^{\prime}}){{\rm Id}}=p^{r}{{\rm Id}}
. Since multiplication by
p
is an isomorphism on étale cohomology with
({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime}(j)
-coefficients, it follows that the composition map
(\pi_{U^{\prime}})_{*}\circ\pi_{U^{\prime}}^{*}
is an isomorphism. In particular, the right vertical arrow
\pi_{U^{\prime}}^{*}
in (
2.3
) is injective. This completes the proof.
∎

We now prove the main result of the section. We use this result later to compare the abelian étale fundamental group of regular schemes over local fields with that of its dense open subsets.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
X
be a regular quasi-projective scheme of dimension
d\geq 1
over a local field
k
and let
Z\subset X
be a closed subset of codimension
c=1
. Then the cohomology group
H^{2}_{Z}(X,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime})
is finite.

Proof.

We first assume that
Z
is also regular. Then by Gabber’s purity theorem
[
Fuj02
]
, we have an isomorphism
H^{2}_{Z}(X,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime})\cong H^{0}(Z,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime}(-1))
. But then this group is finite by Lemma
2.3
because
Z
is regular.

We now prove the lemma for general closed subset
Z\subset X
. Let
W=Z_{{\rm sing}}
. Then
{{\rm codim}}(W,Z)\geq 1
and hence
{{\rm codim}}(W,X)\geq 2
. By Lemma
2.1
, we therefore have
H^{2}_{W}(X,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime})=0
. We now use the long exact sequence

(2.4)

H^{2}_{W}(X,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime})\to H^{2}_{Z}(X,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime})\to H^{2}_{Z\setminus W}(X\setminus W,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime})\to\cdots

for the triple
(X,Z,W)
to conclude that

H^{2}_{Z}(X,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime})\hookrightarrow H^{2}_{Z\setminus W}(X\setminus W,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime})
. It therefore suffices to show that the later group
H^{2}_{Z\setminus W}(X\setminus W,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime})
is finite. But this follows from the above paragraph because
X\setminus W
and
Z\setminus W
are regular. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Prime to
p
-primary torsion

In this section, we let
k
denote a local field of exponential characteristic
p\geq 1
and let
X
be a regular quasi-projective scheme over
k
. We study the prime to
p
-primary torsion subgroup

\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)\{p^{\prime}\}
. Note that if
k
is a local field of characteristic zero, then
p=1
and
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)\{p^{\prime}\}=\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)_{{\rm tor}}
. For a set
\mathbb{M}
of prime integers, we let

\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{M}}=\varprojlim_{n}\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)/n
, where the limit is taken over all integers
n
whose prime divisors lie in
\mathbb{M}
.
Recall from
[
GKR22
, Lemma 3.7]
that

(3.1)

\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)=\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{P}}\oplus\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)_{p},

where
\mathbb{P}
is the set of all primes different from
p
and

\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)_{p}=\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)_{\{p\}}
when
p\geq 2
and zero when
p=1
. Moreover, the Pontryagin dual
(\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{P}})^{\vee}

is isomorphic to

H^{1}(X,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime})
and

(3.2)

\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)\{p^{\prime}\}=\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{P}}\{p^{\prime}\}=(\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{P}})_{{\rm tor}}.

We now prove the main result that helps us to study the prime to
p
-primary torsion
subgroup of
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be a regular quasi-projective scheme over a local field
k
. Let
U\hookrightarrow X
be a dense open subset of
X
. Then
we have the following short exact sequence.

(3.3)

0\to F\to\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)\{p^{\prime}\}\to\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)\{p^{\prime}\}\to 0,

where
F
is a finite group. In particular,
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)\{p^{\prime}\}
is finite if and only if
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)\{p^{\prime}\}
is finite.

Proof.

The long exact sequence for the étale cohomology groups for the pair
(X,U)
is as follows.

(3.4)

H^{1}_{Z}(X,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime})\to H^{1}(X,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime})\to H^{1}(U,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime})\to H^{2}_{Z}(X,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime})\to\cdots

where
Z=X\setminus U
. Since
U
is dense in
X
,
{{\rm codim}}(Z,X)\geq 1
. By semi-purity Lemma
2.1
, we therefore have
H^{1}_{Z}(X,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime})=0
and by Lemma
2.4
, the group
H^{2}_{Z}(X,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime})
is a finite group. The exact sequence (
3.4
) then yields the following short exact sequence.

(3.5)

0\to H^{1}(X,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime})\to H^{1}(U,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime})\to F^{\prime}\to 0,

where
F^{\prime}
is a finite group.
Taking Pontryagin dual, we get the following short exact sequence.

(3.6)

0\to F\to\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)_{\mathbb{P}}\to\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{P}}\to 0,

where
F
is a finite group. Taking torsion subgroups therefore yields the short exact sequence

(3.7)

0\to F\to(\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(U)_{\mathbb{P}})_{{\rm tor}}\to(\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{P}})_{{\rm tor}}\to 0.

The lemma follows from (
3.7
) and (
3.2
).
∎

We now prove the finiteness of prime to
p
-primary torsion subgroup of the abelian étale fundamental group of a regular quasi-projective scheme over a local field. The following result was proven in
[
GKR22
, Theorem 12.8]
when
X
is a smooth quasi-projective scheme over a local field
k
that admits a smooth compactification.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
X
be a regular quasi-projective scheme over a local field
k
. Then
\pi^{{\rm ab}}_{1}(X)\{p^{\prime}\}
is finite.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
X
is a regular quasi-projective integral scheme over
k
and let

X\hookrightarrow\overline{X}
be a compactification of
X
. Let
\phi\colon W\to\overline{X}
be an alteration morphism.
As in the proof of Lemma
2.3
, there exists a dense open subset
U\subset X

such that the induced morphism
\phi_{U^{\prime}}\colon U^{\prime}=\phi^{-1}(U)\to U
is a finite flat morphism of degree
e
, where
e=1
if
{{\rm Char}}(k)=0
and
e=p^{r}
if
{{\rm Char}}(k)=p>0
.
By
[
SGA4
, XVII, Proposition 6.2.5]
, we have a map

(\phi_{U^{\prime}})_{*}\colon H^{1}(U^{\prime},({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime})\to H^{1}(U,({\mathbb{Q}}/{\mathbb{Z}})^{\prime})

Lemma 4.1

Let

Corollary 6.9
.

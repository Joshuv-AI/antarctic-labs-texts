Rigorous evaluation of pathological α-synucleinopathy transmissibility between animals in an experimental model
Authors: Amanda N Sacino, Todd E Golde, David R Borchelt, Benoit I Giasson, Mark S Moehle
Published: 2026

The conformational templating (molecular prion-like) corruption of endogenous α-synuclein using recombinant α-synuclein pre-formed fibrils (PFF) or patient derived tissue extracts to template endogenous protein into pathological conformations has been shown by several groups using multiple model systems [1]. These studies indicate that α-synuclein pathology can spread within animal subjects to cause progressive neurodegenerative diseases [2]. Bona Fide prion strains can spread to naïve animals by environmental contact with surfaces contaminated by bodily excretions indicative of the epidemiolocal infectivity of the misfolded prion protein [3]. Reports of peripheral sources of pathological α-synuclein in the skin, kidney, and gastrointestinal system in patients and experimental models raise serious safety concerns about whether α-synuclein can act as an epidemiological infective prion in addition to its molecular prion properties. This prompted us to directly test the inter-subject transmissibility of pathological α-synuclein [4, 5]. We designed a simple yet robust experiment to test whether exposure to urine, feces, shared housing, fight wounds, or contact with transgenic animals harboring high pathological α-synuclein burden could cause pathological α-synuclein to transmit to non-injected, naive cage mates. TgM83 mice overexpress human mutant (A53T) α-synuclein and show a predictable, progressive accumulation of pathological α-synuclein in the central nervous system (CNS) following intramuscular (IM) injection of PFFs into the hindlimb [6–8]. Behaviorally, IM PFF inoculated TgM83+/− mice display a predictable and progressive overt motor phenotype leading to paralysis, and eventually must be humanely euthanized [6]. To test the transmissibility of pathology between experimental animals, in cages of 5 TgM83+/− mice, 2 mice were inoculated bilaterally with 10 µg PFFs per side while the other 3 were co-housed in a naïve state (Fig. 1A). Quality control and production methods for PFFs has been extensively reported previously [6–7]. As expected, TgM83+/− injected with PFFs developed a motor phenotype and survived on average 135 days post injection (Fig. 1B). The naïve mice were aged 1 year post co-housing (on average, 135 days with injected animals and an additional 230 days after injected group was humanely euthanized) and no animal from the naïve group developed a motor phenotype, indicating a paucity of pathogenic transmission (Fig. 1B). Co-housing was stopped at 1 year post-inoculation, and ~ 15 months of age to prevent the confound of any potential self-aggregation of α-synuclein in these mice [8]. To investigate if pathological α-synuclein was transmitted, the CNS tissue from all mice were assessed by immunohistochemistry for pathological α-synuclein inclusion pathology. TgM83+/− mice injected with PFF developed robust accumulation of α-synuclein inclusion pathology with the expected distribution in the CNS [6] as shown in the brainstem, spinal cord, and the periaqueductal gray (PAG) with pSer129 α-synuclein antibodies 81 A and EP1536Y, a well-established surrogate marker of pathological α-synuclein (Fig. 1C). Staining with a total α-synuclein antibody (94-3A10) also revealed α-synuclein pathology in the PFF-injected mice (Fig. 1C). No α-synuclein inclusion pathology was observed with 81 A, EP1536Y or 94-3A10 antibody staining in the CNS of the naïve co-housed TgM83+/− mice, while normal neuropil distribution of α-synuclein was observed with antibody 94-3A10 (Fig. 1D), and additionally shown in higher magnification images (Supplemental Fig. 1). These data demonstrate that even in a highly susceptible animal model there is not evidence for inter-subject transmission of pathological α-synuclein, thus misfolded α-synuclein is far less host-recipient transmissible than pathologic prion strains such as Scrapie. With our experimental design, we cannot distinguish between the lack of sufficient seeding competent α-synuclein species being shed from the PFF-injected animals or that naïve co-housed mice are resistant to transmissibility of pathological α-synuclein through environmental contact. However, in either case, this highlights that α-synuclein displays robust molecular prion-like activity, but not the epidemiological infectivity of prion proteins. This is supported by evidence showing inefficient seeding of pathological α-synuclein from oral, but not intramuscular, intravenous, or intraperitoneal, routes of exposure even with high amounts of PFF used [9]. Furthermore, these data indicate there should be minimal risk to laboratory or husbandry staff in handling and caring for α-synuclein PFF-injected animals from simple environmental exposure to the bedding and animals directly.

Experimental Assessment of Pathological α-Synuclein Transmission. A Experimental design to test if animals with high burden of pathological α-synuclein can transmit pathology to naïve mice. 3 cages of 5 female (15 animals total) TgM83+/− were used. In each cage 2 TgM83+/− mice were injected bilaterally in hindlimb muscles with 10 µg of human recombinant α-synuclein pre-formed fibrils (PFF, 20 µg total). Two treated mice were then co-housed with 3 naïve mice. Cages of standard wood/corn cob bedding with no enrichment were changed under standard conditions for ventilated cages once every weeks. Co-housing continued until the inoculated mice were humanely euthanized due to paralysis. The average time naïve mice were co-housed with inoculated mice was 135 days. Naïve mice were further aged until 1 year post co-housing (on average another 230 days post death of inoculate animals, and ~ 15 months total age) with inoculated animals before being euthanized for immunohistochemistry. B Kaplan Meier Survival Plots of naïve and PFF-inoculated TgM83+/− mice. As expected, TgM83+/- PFF injected mice developed a motor phenotype and survived ~ 135 days post injection. None of the co-housed naïve mice developed a motor phenotype. One mouse was euthanized at 343 days post co-housing due to perianal soiling possibly due to fighting which would have been fighting exposure to only naïve co-housed mice at this point in the experiment All other mice were euthanized for analysis at 363 days post initial co-housing. C Immunohistochemical analysis of injected TgM83+/−, performed as previously described [6], for pathological α-synuclein using 81 A and EP1536Y pSer129 α-synuclein antibodies, a standard marker for pathological α-synuclein, as well as total α-synuclein (94-3A10). All 6 TgM83+/− PFF injected mice, showed α-synuclein inclusion pathology in the CNS as expected [6] in the brainstem, spinal cord, and periaqueductal gray (PAG). D Staining as in (C) from naïve TgM83+/− mice co-housed with inoculated mice. All 9 naïve mice, including the mouse humanely euthanized due to perianal soiling, showed no α-synuclein inclusion pathology. Scale bar = 120 μm

Research highlight: Burnout among public health workers in Canada: a cross-sectional study
Published: 2026

The COVID-19 pandemic has greatly affected health and social systems worldwide, including in Canada. Public health workers have been crucial in managing the pandemic response, but this has led to concerns about burnout.

Burnout is a state of emotional exhaustion and detachment from work due to prolonged stress. It can lead to mental health issues like anxiety and depression, and affect job performance. While burnout among healthcare providers has been studied, there is limited research on burnout in the Canadian public health workforce. This study aims to measure burnout levels among these workers three years into the pandemic and explore related work factors.

Participants were public health workers involved in various roles during the pandemic aged 18 years and older. The study utilized a survey to assess burnout levels, including exhaustion and disengagement subscales. A total of 2,055 respondents provided responses to burnout questions.

The study also examined factors like work experience, redeployment during the pandemic, workplace safety, and support systems. Data analysis involved statistical methods to identify associations between burnout and these factors.

The findings revealed that 78.7% of respondents met the criteria for burnout, with exhaustion affecting 85.2% and disengagement affecting 87.7%.

Factors such as 5 or more years of work experience, redeployment during the pandemic, and workplace harassment were linked to higher burnout rates. Burnout was also associated with intentions to leave or retire early.

The findings highlight the need for better workplace support and safety measures to reduce burnout. The study suggests that addressing these issues is crucial for maintaining a resilient public health workforce. Future research should focus on understanding burnout trends over time and developing effective interventions at individual, organizational, and system levels to prevent and manage burnout in public health settings.

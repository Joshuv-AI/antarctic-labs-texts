BCMA-directed therapy with cizutamig induces rapid improvement in long-standing systemic sclerosis-associated interstitial lung disease

BCMA-targeting with cizutamig shows promising clinical effect in a case of long-standing and severe SSc-ILD.

BCMA-targeting with cizutamig shows promising clinical effect in a case of long-standing and severe SSc-ILD.

Dear Editor, Systemic sclerosis (SSc), particularly when complicated by interstitial lung disease (SSc-ILD), remains a major cause of morbidity and mortality [1]. Current therapies, including immunosuppressive or antifibrotic agents, primarily aim to slow disease progression rather than restore lung function and typically require continuous long-term treatment [2]. As a result, there is a significant unmet need for therapies with disease-modifying potential.

T-cell engagers (TCEs), especially B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA)×CD3 bispecific antibodies, represent a novel therapeutic approach by enabling deep depletion of pathogenic B cells and plasma cells. Teclistamab, the most studied agent in this class, has shown promising early efficacy in severe, treatment-refractory autoimmune diseases, including SSc, suggesting the potential for sustained responses without continuous immunosuppression [3, 4, 5, 6]. However, clinical experience in SSc-ILD remains scarce, particularly for agents beyond teclistamab.

We report the first case of long-standing SSc with severe progressive ILD successfully treated with cizutamig, a BCMA×CD3 bispecific antibody (Candid Therapeutics, San Diego, CA, USA), highlighting its potential as a novel therapeutic strategy in a disease where current treatments are largely non-restorative.

A 40-year-old woman with a 14-year history of anti-Scl70-antibody positive diffuse cutaneous SSc presented with progressive SSc-ILD of 12 years’ duration, refractory to medical therapy with ciclosporin A, methotrexate, mycophenolate mofetil, mycophenolic acid, upadacitinib, tocilizumab and nintedanib (Fig. 1A). Despite ongoing therapy with tocilizumab and nintedanib, high-resolution computed tomography (HRCT) showed progressive fibrosing non-specific interstitial pneumonia (NSIP), and pulmonary function test revealed severe restriction (FVC 42% predicted, DLCOcSB 32% predicted). Due to continued progression, lung transplantation was recommended. However, following multidisciplinary consultation, the patient opted for treatment with cizutamig under an individual therapeutic-use approach.

Cizutamig was administered intravenously once weekly for five doses, using a step-up dosing. Immunosuppressive therapies were discontinued two weeks before treatment, while nintedanib was continued. Anti-infective prophylaxis with acyclovir and cotrimoxazole was initiated. Cizutamig was generally well tolerated. The patient experienced grade 1 cytokine release syndrome (CRS) after the first dose but no neurotoxicity. Infectious complications included tonsillitis (week 4) and SARS-CoV-2- infection (week 6) with subsequent bacterial bronchitis, all of which resolved following treatment with paxlovid, remdesivir and moxifloxacin. By week 6 the patient developed hypogammaglobulinemia (IgG <4 g/l) requiring repeated IVIG transfusions (average of 12 g/month).

Cizutamig induced prompt B and plasma cell depletion: by week 2, circulating CD20+ B cells and CD38+ CD138+ plasma cells were undetectable. Bone marrow analysis at week 8 showed near-complete plasma cell depletion and marked B cells reduction (Fig. 1B) with remaining B cells largely of transitional phenotype. Skin biopsies at week 12 confirmed similar depletion (Fig. 1B). This was accompanied by a substantial decline in SSc-specific autoantibodies, with anti-Scl-70 IgG decreasing markedly and ANA titres dropping by four dilution steps (Fig. 1C).

Clinically, the patient showed a rapid and clinically meaningful improvement, achieving an ACR-CRISS 25 response by week 6 and an ACR-CRISS 50 response by week 12 despite complete discontinuation of immunosuppressive therapy. Pulmonary function improved, with FVC increasing from 42% to 45% predicted and DLCOcSB from 32% to 55% predicted by week 6 (Fig. 1D). HRCT at week 12 demonstrated regression of ILD (Fig. 1D), including reductions in ground-glass opacities and reticular changes (Fig. 1D). Based on sustained improvement, nintedanib was discontinued. By week 24, further gains were observed, reaching a FVC 54% predicted and DLCOcSB 45% predicted, corresponding to a 390 mL increase in lung volume and a 155 m improvement in six-min walk distance.

Skin fibrosis also improved markedly, with the modified Rodnan Skin Score decreasing from 20/51 at baseline to 12/51 at week 6 and stabilizing at 8/51 by week 17. Patient-reported outcomes reflect these objective improvements, with reductions in disease impact (ScleroID 4.35 at baseline to 3.09 at week 24) and functional disability (HAQ 0.88 at baseline to 0.50 at week 24).

This case demonstrates that BCMA-directed therapy with cizutamig is feasible and effective in severe, long-standing, treatment-refractory SSc-ILD. Consistent with earlier reports of BCMA targeting in SSc, treatment was well tolerated, enabled discontinuation of immunosuppression and induced profound humoral immune depletion with reduction of disease-specific autoantibodies [3, 4, 5, 6]. However, it is associated with infections and hypogammaglobulinemia requiring immunoglobulin replacement, the required duration of which remains a major concern. Also, a contribution of IVIG substitution to some of the later clinical effects cannot be entirely excluded: while no beneficial effect on SSc-ILD or skin disease has been reported in the only randomized controlled trial available [7], there are signals of benefit for skin disease in uncontrolled studies [8].

Importantly, this case demonstrates not only improvement in inflammatory lung changes but also regression of reticular abnormalities on HRCT, which are traditionally considered irreversible. Compared with previous reports with teclistamab, where ILD duration was shorter [3], this case suggests that even long-standing fibrotic disease may remain amenable to remodelling under effective immune intervention.

Despite non-negligible, but manageable adverse events, these findings warrant systematic evaluation of BCMA-directed therapies in larger, controlled studies to assess durability, safety and patient selection in life-threatening SSc-ILD.

Impact of Kidney Function on the Treatment Effect of Multi-Arterial Grafting in CABG Patients
Authors: Sung Jun Park, Kyung-Jong Yoo, Young-Nam Youn
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe adoption of multi-arterial grafting (MAG) remains limited in real-world coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) practice. This study aimed to identify subsets of patients most likely to benefit clinically from MAG, and to explore potential effect modifiers of its treatment benefit, including renal function.MethodsThis retrospective cohort study analyzed 3,244 isolated CABG patients from a prospectively maintained institutional registry (2006–2020). Patients were categorized into MAG (n = 1,868) and single-arterial grafting (SAG; n = 1,376) groups. Inverse probability treatment weighting was applied to adjust for baseline differences, and Cox proportional hazards models evaluated overall survival. Subgroup analyses assessed interactions between treatment effects and patient characteristics, particularly estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR).ResultsOver a median follow-up of 7.1 years, MAG demonstrated significantly higher overall survival compared to SAG (P < 0.001). After adjustment, MAG still provided a significant survival advantage (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR], 0.77; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.65–0.92; P = 0.005). MAG consistently showed favorable outcomes across most patient subgroups regardless of age, sex, diabetes, peripheral arterial disease, smoking status, or left ventricular function. However, renal function significantly modified this effect (interaction P = 0.005); MAG conferred significant survival benefits in patients with eGFR ≥ 51 mL/min/1.73 m2 (aHR, 0.51; 95% CI, 0.66–0.99; P = 0.044), whereas no difference was observed in those with eGFR < 51 (aHR, 1.00; 95% CI, 0.74–1.36; P = 0.999).ConclusionMAG offers significant survival advantages for CABG patients with relatively preserved renal function (eGFR ≥ 51). Renal function should thus be considered when selecting arterial grafting strategies to optimize patient outcomes.Keywords: Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting, Multi-Arterial Grafting, Renal Function, Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate, Long-Term Survival

INTRODUCTION

Despite the ongoing emphasis on multi-arterial grafting (MAG) in coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), its adoption in real-world practice remains limited.1,2 This is closely linked to the aging population of CABG candidates and the firmly established role of percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) as the first-line treatment for coronary artery disease (CAD).3,4,5 As a result, patients with straightforward anatomy are typically treated with PCI, while those referred for CABG often present with more complex coronary anatomy and higher comorbidity burdens.6

Efforts should therefore focus on identifying patient subsets who are most likely to achieve meaningful clinical benefit from MAG, thereby optimizing long-term CABG outcomes. Despite its underuse, clarifying the clinical value of MAG in specific subsets of contemporary CABG populations is essential to guide surgical decision-making and improve patient selection. However, randomized trials to define such subgroups are logistically and economically challenging. While large-scale administrative or registry data can provide statistical power, they often lack detailed surgical and clinical information necessary for nuanced analysis. In this regard, our institutionally maintained registry—with consistent surgical protocols and a homogeneous patient population—offers complementary strengths that allow for more accurate interpretation of outcomes.

In this context, the present study aimed to evaluate the long-term outcomes of CABG based on the number of arterial conduits (MAG vs. single-arterial grafting [SAG]), using a prospectively maintained institutional data warehouse. We further explored whether specific clinical characteristics, such as renal function assessed by estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), may modify the comparative treatment effect of MAG.

METHODS

Study design and population

The SEVERANCE CABG (SEV-CABG) registry is a retrospective and prospective, single-center registry that includes all consecutive patients who have undergone coronary artery bypass surgery at Severance Cardiovascular Hospital, Seoul, Korea (study number: 4-2023-0390; approval date: May 26, 2023). Using the SEV-CABG registry, this study reviewed the long-term outcomes of patients who received isolated CABG for the treatment of multi-vessel CAD between January 2006 and December 2020. Inclusion criteria included adults (≥ 18 years) receiving multi-vessel isolated CABG with at least one in situ internal thoracic artery (ITA). Exclusion criteria included concomitant valve or aortic surgery, redo CABG, single bypass CABG, or non-use of ITA.

The primary outcome was the overall mortality. Vital status was ascertained through linkage of our institutional registry with official national death records provided by the Statistics Korea via the Microdata Integrated Service (MDIS), an order-made service for research purposes. For this study, linkage was performed through MDIS, with the hospital data service team acting as an honest broker to ensure that investigators had no direct access to personal identifiers. Mortality follow-up was complete through December 31, 2021.

Operative techniques

The details of operative technique of CABG employed in Severance Cardiovascular Hospital have been previously reported.7,8 In brief, the majority of CABGs were performed in an off-pump manner by two dedicated coronary surgeons. With few exceptions involving severe arteriosclerosis or calcification of the left internal thoracic artery (LITA) itself, the in situ LITA was used as the primary conduit bypassing the left anterior descending artery in nearly all patients. Basically, we preferred to complete all coronary bypasses within two conduits employing a sequential anastomosis technique, avoiding the need for an additional third graft. As the second graft, the right ITA, radial artery (RA), or saphenous vein graft (SVG) was selected at the operating surgeon’s discretion, based on individual coronary anatomy and clinical profile. While MAG was pursued as the standard strategy, the choice between right ITA and RA as the second arterial conduit was made by surgeon preference. Preferably, the second arterial conduit was connected to the in-situ LITA to form a composite graft. However, when competitive flow was anticipated due to moderate target vessel stenosis and strong native flow, bilateral in-situ ITAs, aorto-coronary RA configuration or an additional third graft for isolated right coronary artery bypass was employed. Meanwhile, when the LITA was deemed inadequate as a sole inflow source for entire coronary system or when extensive distal revascularization was required, an aorto-coronary SVG was used to ensure adequate inflow and conduit length.

Statistical analysis

Baseline patient characteristics and perioperative variables were summarized and compared between MAG and SAG groups. Continuous variables are expressed as mean ± standard deviation and were compared using t-tests. Categorical variables are presented as frequencies and percentages, with differences assessed using χ2 or Fisher’s exact tests, as appropriate. Standardized mean differences (SMDs) were calculated to evaluate the balance of covariates between groups.

The propensity scores were derived from a logistic regression model that incorporated a comprehensive set of demographic, clinical, laboratory, and operative variables. These included age, sex, body mass index (BMI), smoking history, hypertension, atrial fibrillation, New York Heart Association class, previous myocardial infarction (MI), recent MI, acute MI, diabetes, cerebrovascular accident, dyslipidemia, peripheral arterial occlusive disease (PAOD), history of PCI, chronic kidney disease (CKD), dialysis status, serum creatinine, albumin, triglyceride level, hemoglobin level, platelet count, left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), left ventricular end-diastolic dimension (LVEDD), right ventricular systolic pressure, number of distal anastomoses, completeness of revascularization, preoperative intra-aortic balloon pump or extracorporeal membrane oxygenation support, presence of left main disease, presence of three-vessel disease, performance of off-pump CABG, and surgeon variability. Inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) was then applied using the estimated propensity scores, with weights stabilized and trimmed to mitigate the influence of extreme values. IPTW was applied to adjust for potential baseline imbalances between the MAG and SAG groups. After weighting, minor residual imbalance remained in some covariates, particularly surgeon variability (Operator). To address this, we conducted an additional weighted Cox regression model in which operating surgeon was included as a covariate, thereby providing a sensitivity analysis for robustness of the treatment effect. As a sensitivity analysis, propensity score matching (PSM) was performed using the same covariates as in the IPTW model. Patients were matched 1:1 with nearest neighbor matching (caliper 0.2 on the logit scale) and exact matching by age groups.

IPTW-adjusted Kaplan–Meier survival curves were constructed, and the primary outcome of overall survival was evaluated using IPTW-weighted Cox proportional hazards models. Proportional hazard assumptions were verified using Schoenfeld residuals. Subgroup analyses were performed across various clinical variables, and interactions between MAG and these subgroups were evaluated to identify potential effect modifiers. Restricted cubic spline analyses within the Cox models assessed the nonlinear relationship between continuous variables (e.g., eGFR, BMI, LVEF, LVEDD) and the treatment effect of MAG. All statistical tests were two-tailed, and P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. All analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.0.3; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Ethics statement

This study was reviewed and approved by Yonsei University Health System, Severance Hospital, Institutional Review Board (study number: 4-2025-0220; approval date: April 18, 2025). Requirements for informed consent were waived due to the retrospective nature of the study as directed by the Institutional Review Board.

RESULTS

Patients

Among 4,000 patients who underwent surgical coronary revascularization at Severance Cardiovascular Hospital between January 2006 and December 2020, a total of 3,442 isolated CABG patients were identified from the SEV-CABG registry after excluding cases with concomitant procedures. After excluding single-target CABG cases and those in which at least one in-situ ITA was not used, a total of 3,244 patients remained. Among them, 1,868 patients underwent CABG with MAG, while 1,376 patients received CABG with a SAG.

Baseline clinical characteristics differed significantly between the MAG and SAG groups (Table 1). Patients in the SAG group were generally older and had a higher prevalence of comorbidities, including hypertension, diabetes, cerebrovascular disease, PAOD, and CKD. The MAG group exhibited more favorable cardiac profiles, with higher LVEF, smaller chamber dimensions, and better renal function. Laboratory parameters such as hemoglobin and platelet count were also higher in the MAG group.

Regarding surgical details, both groups had similar completeness of revascularization and number of distal anastomoses (Table 1). As per definition, the right ITA and/or RA were used as the second conduit in addition to the in-situ LITA graft in the MAG group, whereas in the SAG group, SVGs were used as the second conduit in all patients. Although distribution of surgical period and operating surgeon differed between groups, the vast majority of operations were performed by two experienced surgeons (Surgeon A and B) employing consistent surgical strategies. A minimal proportion of procedures were performed by surgeons other than the two primary operators. These distributions are detailed in Table 1.

After adjustment, most covariates were well balanced between the groups, with SMDs less than 11% (Table 1).

Clinical outcomes

Early postoperative outcomes, including in-hospital mortality, new-onset dialysis, and tracheostomy, were significantly worse in the SAG group before adjustment (Table 2). However, after IPTW-adjustment, these differences were no longer statistically significant, except for the higher incidence of tracheostomy in the SAG group (2.3% vs. 0.4%, P = 0.011). Overall, early outcomes were comparable between the two groups after adjustment (Table 2).

During median follow-up duration of 7.1 years (interquartile range, 3.6–11.5 years), overall survival was significantly higher in the MAG group than the SAG group (P < 0.001) (Fig. 1A). Although the survival gap between the two groups narrowed after adjustment, the MAG group continued to demonstrate superior survival compared to the SAG group (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR], 0.77; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.65–0.92; P = 0.005) (Fig. 1B).

Importantly, in a sensitivity analysis additionally adjusting for operating surgeon, the survival benefit of MAG remained consistent (hazard ratio [HR], 0.78; 95% CI, 0.65–0.93; P = 0.006), while operating surgeon itself was not independently associated with survival (HR, 1.11; 95% CI, 0.93–1.31; P = 0.251). PSM yielded 932 well-balanced pairs. with adequate balance across baseline covariates (Supplementary Table 1). In this cohort, MAG was associated with improved survival compared with SAG (aHR, 0.75; 95% CI, 0.62–0.90; P = 0.002), consistent with Kaplan–Meier estimates (Supplementary Fig. 1).

Effect of MAG vs. SAG across varying clinical parameters

The treatment effect of MAG versus SAG was evaluated in the IPTW-adjusted cohort across a range of clinical parameters. For overall mortality, no significant modifications in treatment effect was observed according to patient age, left ventricular (LV) function, or LV dimensions. In contrast, renal function, as measured by eGFR, significantly modified the treatment effect. The aHR curve for MAG crossed the null value (aHR, 1.0) at an eGFR of 35.4 mL/min/1.73 m2, and the CI consistently remained below 1.0 for patients with eGFR greater than 51.0 mL/min/1.73 m2 (Fig. 2A). The interaction between eGFR and treatment effect was statistically significant (P = 0.005).

Subgroup analysis in the adjusted cohort

MAG exhibited a consistent trend toward superior outcomes compared to SAG across most subgroups, irrespective of sex, age, presence of diabetes, PAOD, smoking status, or LVEF (Fig. 3). However, renal function significantly modified the treatment effect of MAG. While MAG provided a survival benefit in patients without end-stage renal disease (ESRD), this advantage was not seen in ESRD patients undergoing dialysis (P for interaction = 0.003). Subgroup analyses using eGFR thresholds (60 and 30 mL/min/1.73 m2) also demonstrated significant differential effects of MAG (P for interaction = 0.013) (Fig. 3).

When patients were stratified by an eGFR threshold of 51 mL/min/1.73 m2, there was a notable difference in long-term survival after CABG (log-rank P < 0.001). Specifically, MAG was associated with a significant survival benefit in patients with eGFR ≥ 51 (aHR, 0.51; 95% CI, 0.66–0.99; P = 0.044), whereas no survival difference was observed between MAG and SAG in patients with eGFR < 51 (aHR, 1.00; 95% CI, 0.74–1.36; P = 0.999) (Fig. 2B).

DISCUSSION

This study evaluated the treatment effect of MAG versus SAG in patients undergoing CABG, with a focus on identifying clinical subgroups that may derive distinct benefits from MAG. Baseline differences between groups likely contributed to the observed survival advantage of MAG over SAG. After statistical adjustment, MAG continued to demonstrate favorable survival outcomes compared to SAG, albeit with a narrowed survival gap. This benefit remained consistent across most clinical subgroups, irrespective of age, sex, LV function, or comorbidities. However, renal function emerged as a key effect modifier. When stratified by eGFR, patients with eGFR ≥ 50 mL/min/1.73 m2 experienced a significant survival benefit from MAG, whereas no survival difference was observed in those with eGFR < 50. These findings suggest that patients with impaired kidney function may exhibit a fundamentally different trajectory of post-CABG outcomes, highlighting the need for further investigation.

These results align with recent analyses from the Society of Thoracic Surgeons (STS) database involving over one million CABG patients, which also found that MAG provided a consistent survival benefit across most subgroups, including those defined by age, LVEF, sex, and diabetes.2 In both studies, eGFR modified the treatment effect, supporting the use of renal function in tailoring grafting strategy. While the STS study also found a BMI-related interaction—favoring SAG in patients with BMI ≥ 40 kg/m2—this could not be replicated in our study due to the absence of such high-BMI patients in this Asian cohort.

Despite the growing body of evidence supporting MAG,9,10 its adoption in clinical practice remains low, with reported utilization rates below 13% in recent years.1,2 This underuse may be explained by the increasing complexity of patients referred for CABG—many of whom present with extensive comorbidities and complex coronary anatomy—as well as the lack of clear scientific guidance identifying which subgroups would derive the most benefit from MAG. Consequently, simplified strategies using vein grafts are often favored in practice.

Unlike many prior studies that focused on individual variables such as LV function or age, the present study incorporated a comprehensive range of clinical parameters to more precisely identify patients who would derive the greatest benefit from MAG.11,12,13 Although the STS study was methodologically robust, large registry datasets may be limited by residual confounding and unmeasured surgical factors. Supplementary analyses using homogeneous datasets, like the current study, can help refine and validate these broader findings.

The role of CKD in the development of CAD is well-established. Even after adjustment for conventional risk factors such as diabetes and hypertension, CKD itself increases the risk of CAD-related mortality.14,15 As eGFR declines below 60 mL/min/1.73 m2, the incidence of CAD increases linearly.16,17 While the underlying mechanisms are not fully elucidated, uremic toxins, arteriosclerosis, microvascular disease, vascular calcification, and oxidative stress are believed to contribute.16,18 These pathophysiologic differences complicate risk stratification and may result in different clinical outcomes following CABG.19

In this study, exploratory analysis suggested an eGFR threshold of 50 mL/min/1.73 m2 for predicting MAG benefit, which is in line with prior findings from PCI studies.20,21 However, this threshold should not be interpreted as absolute. Rather, the findings reinforce the importance of renal function as a clinical consideration in grafting strategy and support the need for further research in this patient population.

This study is subject to inherent limitations of a retrospective observational design, including the potential for selection bias. However, several methodological strengths mitigate this concern. The analysis was conducted in a relatively homogenous patient population, and key surgical factors—including operator variability, completeness of revascularization, and the number of distal anastomoses—were rigorously adjusted for. Importantly, the majority of operations were performed by two dedicated coronary surgeons who shared highly similar operative strategies and perioperative protocols. Their consistent preference for arterial grafting and lesion-driven decision-making, rather than patient-specific baseline characteristics, likely contributed to minimizing selection bias. Although the sample size is smaller compared to national registries and the study spanned a 15-year period, these factors were partly offset by the fact that most procedures were performed by the same surgeons and that both grafting strategies were relatively evenly distributed over time.

Another limitation relates to the distribution of ESRD patients. Despite IPTW adjustment, a modest residual imbalance remained (MAG 2.6% vs. SAG 4.7%; SMD = 10.5%). Although dialysis-dependent patients accounted for less than 5% of either treatment group in the adjusted cohort, the possibility of residual confounding cannot be excluded. Therefore, the observed modifying effect of renal function should be considered exploratory and interpreted with caution, particularly in patients with advanced CKD or ESRD.

Finally, graft patency data were not available in this cohort, which limits mechanistic interpretation. Further studies focusing on conduit patency are warranted.

MAG was associated with superior long-term survival compared to SAG in most patients undergoing CABG. However, this benefit was not observed in patients with reduced renal function, suggesting a differential trajectory in post-CABG outcomes. Further studies are needed to understand revascularization strategies in CKD populations who may exhibit distinct CAD progression and outcomes.

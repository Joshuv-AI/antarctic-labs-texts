Radix Isatidis polysaccharide–induced gut microbiota alleviates interstitial nephritis caused by infectious bronchitis virus via the gut–kidney axis☆
Authors: Xuelian Xiang, Yamei Huang, Yuxi Shen, Di Wang, Yong Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractInterstitial nephritis caused by Infectious Bronchitis Virus (IBV) is a primary cause of mortality in chickens. To investigate whether gut microbiota mediate this effect, chickens were treated with antibiotics to deplete intestinal flora, which exacerbated IBV infection and abolished RIP’s protective effect. It was found that fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) could reduce IBV copy numbers, decrease the production of metabolites, decrease the expression of NLRP3 pathway-related genes and proteins in the kidneys and intestines and increase the expression of tight intestinal junction proteins, and alleviate enteritis and interstitial nephritis. The 16S rRNA sequencing results indicated a significant increase in the abundance of harmful Escherichia_Shigella bacteria following IBV infection. However, in the RIP prevention group and FMT group, there was an increase in the abundance of probiotics such as Faecalibacterium, Lactobacillus, and Bifidobacterium. Furthermore, Lactobacillus reuteri, isolated from the feces of RIP-administered chickens and combined with sodium acetate (NaAc), alleviated interstitial nephritis and was associated with decreased expression of NLRP3 pathway-related molecules, reduced inflammatory factors, and enhanced intestinal tight junction proteins. This study not only confirms the vital role of gut microbiota in the treatment of IBV-induced nephritis but also opens avenues for the development of targeted probiotic therapies in viral infections and kidney diseases.Keywords: IBV, Gut microbiota, Interstitial nephritis, FMT, Lactobacillus reuteri

Introduction

The gut-kidney axis plays a pivotal role in preserving renal health and modulating disease processes, primarily through the intricate signaling pathways and metabolic interactions mediated by the intestinal microbiota (Foresto-Neto et al., 2021; Li et al., 2021). Dysbiosis of the gut microbiome leads to the overgrowth of pathogenic bacteria, resulting in the release of gut microbiota-derived toxins, such as endotoxins and uremic toxins. These toxins can translocate into the systemic circulation by disrupting the intestinal mucosal barrier, which exacerbates systemic inflammation and contributing to renal inflammation (Han et al., 2021; Han et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024). Additionally, the accumulation of microbial metabolites can activate pattern-recognition receptors (PRR) on macrophages, triggering immune responses and promoting the production of inflammatory mediators, which further aggravate renal injury (Vetrani et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023). Conversely, gut microbiota also exerts protective effects on renal health. They achieve this by modulating inflammation through mechanisms involving microbe-associated molecular patterns (MAMPs) and the generation of short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), which can mitigate renal inflammation (Ali et al., 2022). Some research underscores the pivotal function of the gut-kidney connection in the development of several renal conditions, such as chronic kidney disease, acute kidney injury, and nephrolithiasis (Al and Shatat, 2017; Hobby et al., 2019).

The intestinal tract of poultry hosts a complex and diverse microbial community, densely populated with mutually beneficial symbiotic microorganisms, particularly concentrated in the cecum (Wei et al., 2013; Deng et al., 2024). These gut microbes are essential for enhancing poultry growth performance (Cui et al., 2021), defending against pathogenic infections, and supporting the development and maturation of the immune system (Stanley et al., 2014). In recent years, fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) has emerged as an innovative and effective approach in poultry production for restoring and maintaining a balanced intestinal microbiota (Metzler-Zebeli et al., 2019; Novelle et al., 2025). By transferring donor microbiota to recipients, FMT reconstructs the intestinal microbial ecosystem, alleviates intestinal inflammation, promotes gut health, and enhances production performance (Zhang et al., 2022; Ma et al., 2023). Additionally, FMT has been shown to regulate the production of intestinal microbial metabolites, which play a critical role in improving organ function and mitigating disease via systemic mechanisms such as the gut-kidney and gut-lung axes (Bian et al., 2022; Wang and Wang, 2024).

It is widely known that polysaccharides, as macromolecules, are difficult to be directly absorbed by the intestinal tract (Bai et al., 2022). Their therapeutic effects are primarily achieved through indirect regulation of systemic immunity, metabolism, and inflammatory responses by modulating the intestinal microecosystem. Herbal polysaccharides can regulate the composition of gut microbiota, enhance immune function, improve metabolic disorders, and alleviate inflammatory responses (Su et al., 2020; Huo et al., 2024; Xiang et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2024). Moreover, certain herbal polysaccharides, such as Nostoc commune Vaucher polysaccharide and Caulis Spatholobi polysaccharide, have been shown to fortify the intestinal mucosal barrier, suppress the expression of inflammatory factors, and strengthen immune defenses (Cui et al., 2022; Guo et al., 2023).

IBV, belonging to the Coronaviridae family and Gammacoronavirus genus, is a pathogen that causes highly contagious and infectious respiratory diseases in chickens (Wang et al., 2021). QX-genotype IBV infection in this study resulted in renal mottling, the principal mortality factor in the chickens. In our previous study, we found that RIP could alleviate IBV-induced interstitial nephritis (Xiang et al., 2024). However, the necessity of gut flora for mitigating effect of RIP remains uncertain, as does the independent role of altered gut flora in alleviating nephritis. To address these questions, we performed intestinal flora clearance and FMT experiments with fecal bacteria from RIP-gavaged chickens. At the end of the above experiments, we discovered and isolated an interesting strain, Limosilactobacillus reuteri. Prior research has indicated that Lactobacillus reuteri possesses the ability to enhance the intestinal mucosal barrier, suppress the expression of inflammatory cytokines, and inhibit oxidative stress (Wang et al., 2024; Qin et al., 2025). Lactobacillus reuteri can produce a common SCFA, acetate, which exhibits antibacterial activity and helps regulate intestinal pH. Studies have indicated that sodium acetate (NaAc) can inhibit cellular senescence and apoptosis, reduce renal oxidative stress, and thereby alleviate kidney damage (Oyabambi et al., 2021; Zheng et al., 2023). However, the mechanisms by which Lactobacillus reuteri and its combination with NaAc affect interstitial nephritis remain unclear. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate whether the application of Lactobacillus reuteri, alone and in combination with NaAc, can alleviate interstitial nephritis and to explore the molecular mechanism underlying its effects.

In this study, we employed an interstitial nephritis model induced by QX-genotype IBV to investigate the pivotal role of the intestinal microbiota in the alleviation of interstitial nephritis by RIP, as well as the underlying molecular mechanisms. Abx-mediated depletion of intestinal microbiota was found to exacerbate IBV infection and renal inflammation in chickens. Furthermore, FMT experiments suggested that the intestinal microbiota modulated by RIP alleviated interstitial nephritis, accompanied by suppression of the NLRP3 signaling pathway and reduced inflammatory factor expression. Additionally, gavage experiments revealed that Lactobacillus reuteri (isolated from chicken feces) similarly alleviated renal inflammation and injury, with downregulation of inflammatory factor expression and modulation of the NLRP3 signaling pathway.

Materials and methods

Virus, RIP and chickens

The Sczy3 viral strain (GenBank JF732903), a GI-19/QX-like Infectious Bronchitis Virus (IBV), was isolated from broiler chickens infected with IBV in Sichuan, China, back in 2009. RIP was obtained from Yanglingciyuan Biotechnology (Xi’ an, China). A total of 116 healthy one-day-old chickens were purchased from Sichuan Dekang Agriculture and Animal Husbandry Food Group Co., LTD.

ABX treatment and IBV infection experiment

A total of 1.0 g of metronidazole, 1.0 g of neomycin sulfate, 1.0 g of ampicillin, and 0.5 g of vancomycin was weighed and dissolved in 1 L of water. Chickens (n=16) were divided randomly into the following groups: control (n=3), Abx (n=3), Sczy3 (n=5), and Abx+Sczy3 (n=5). Each group was housed in a separate BSL-2 units with unrestricted food and water access. From days 5 to 16, the Abx and Abx+Sczy3 groups were given Abx via drinking water, followed by regular water for two days. In contrast, the control and Sczy3 groups were given ddH2O. On day 19, intestinal samples from control and Abx-treated chickens were collected for 16S rRNA analysis. On the same day, groups Sczy3 and Abx+Sczy3 received an ocular and nasal inoculation of Sczy3 strain (105 EID50 in 0.2mL). At 5 days post-infection (d.p.i), kidneys were collected from both groups, and viral loads were subsequently quantified.

FMT and IBV infection experiment

Collection of the fecal microbial suspension: ten chickens were orally administered RIP (20 mg/kg/d) between days 11 and 18. On day 19, the white, urate-rich fraction of the feces was promptly removed following defecation, due to its high uric acid content. 8 g fecal samples were combined with 0.75% saline (1:6 ratio) in sterile 50 mL tubes. The mixture was placed on ice for 30 minutes, the supernatant was filtered through six layers of gauze, collected, aliquoted into 15 mL centrifuge tubes, and kept at -80°C.

Gut microbiota depletion experiment and FMT experiment: Chickens (n=50) were divided randomly into the following groups (n=10 per group): control, Sczy3, RIP+Sczy3, RIP+Abx+Sczy3 and FMT+Sczy3. From day 1 to day 16, each chicken in the FMT group was given 1 mL of oral fecal microbial suspension. RIP+Abx+Sczy3 group chickens received Abx in water from days 5–16, followed by two days of water treatment. The RIP+Sczy3 and RIP+Abx+Sczy3 group chickens were treated with 20 mg/kg/d oral RIP from days 11 to 18. On day 19, with the exception of the control group, chickens received 105 EID50 of Sczy3 strain in 0.2mL via ocular and nasal administration. The control group chickens were given 0.2 mL of PBS. Experimental procedures and RIP dosage were performed as previously described (Xiang et al., 2024). At 3 and 5 d.p.i., a random sample of 5 chickens per group was humanely euthanized. The mean lesion scores (MLS) of kidneys were scored based on severity: 0 (normal), 1 (mild swelling), 2 (moderate swelling), and 3 (severe swelling). Kidney and ileum samples were collected for pathological sections, immunofluorescence detection, RT-qPCR and Western blot assays. Samples of intestinal contents were harvested to analyze the gut microbiota via 16S rRNA gene sequencing.

Combined application of Lactobacillus reuteri and NaAc in IBV infection experiment

Isolation of Lactobacillus from chicken stool: The fresh stool of chickens was collected and suspended in PBS and then seeded onto MRS selective medium (Qingdao High-tech Industrial Park Haibo Biotechnology Co., LTD). The fecal cultures were maintained at 37 °C under incubator. Approximately 200 isolates were selected from MRS plates and tested using PCR for the Lactobacillus genus with the primer set 5’-AGAGTTTGATCMTGGCTCAG-3’ and 5’-TACGGYTACCTTGTTACGACTT-3’.

Combined application of Lactobacillus reuteri and NaAc: Chickens (n=40) were randomly assigned to the following groups (n=10 per group): control, Sczy3, Lac+Sczy3 and NaAc+Lac+Sczy3. The Lac+Sczy3 group chickens received oral 1mL Lactobacillus reuteri from days 11 to 18, while the NaAc+Lac+Sczy3 group chickens received oral 1mL Lactobacillus reuteri and 20 mg/kg/d of NaAc from days 11 to 18. On day 19, all chickens were infected with Sczy3 and the kidneys and ileum tissues were collected at 3 and 5 d.p.i. The mean lesion scores and the viral load of kidney were measured. Kidney and ileum tissues were also collected for pathological sections, immunofluorescence detection, RT-qPCR and Western blot.

Serum biochemical index testing

The serum from all chicken groups was collected, and creatinine, urea nitrogen, and uric acid levels were measured using respective assay kits (S03036, S03076, S03035, Changchun Huili Biotechnology Co., Ltd, China). The detection method is consistent with the previously published literature (Xiang et al., 2024).

HE staining

Tissue samples from kidneys and ilea were fixed in paraffin and subsequently sliced into 3-μm-thick sections using a rotary microtome (HM315, Thermo Fisher Technology). Slices were stained with hematoxylin and eosin according to the protocol described by Wu et al (Wu et al., 2020).

Immunofluorescence detection

The prepared 3 μm ileum sections were combined with anti-claudin-1 (28674-1-AP, Proteintech) and anti-ZO-1 (21773-1-AP, Proteintech), incubated together, combined with the Cy3 Labeled Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG (GB21303, Servicebio) and Fitc-labeled Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG (GB22303, Servicebio), and subjected to immunofluorescence (IF) analysis. A fluorescence microscope and imaging system (Eclipse Ti-SR, NIKON, Japan) were used for imaging.

Real-time PCR

To assess the expression differences of related genes among the groups, we extracted total RNA from kidney and ileum samples using TRIzol reagent. Subsequently, we performed reverse transcription with the Reverse Transcription Kit. Primers targeting genes related to inflammatory factors were designed using PrimerPremier 5.0 (refer to Table 1). We quantified the mRNA expression levels of these genes using a LightCycler® 480 detection system. The reagents used were the same as those described in our previous study (Xiang et al., 2024).

Western blotting

The methods for extracting proteins and measuring concentrations are the same as those in our previous literature (Xiang et al., 2024). The membranes were incubated with anti-NLRP3 (1:1000; WL02635, Wanlei Biotechnology, China), anti-cleaved Caspase-1 (1:1000; 22915-1-AP, Proteintech), anti-IL-1β (1:1000; MK56352, Abmart, China), anti-occludin (1:2000; WL01996, Wanlei Biotechnology, China), anti-claudin-1 (1:2000; WL03073, Wanlei Biotechnology, China), anti-ZO-1 (1:1000; 21773-1-AP, Proteintech), and anti-β-actin (1:1000; WL01372, Wanlei Biotechnology, China) and goat anti-rabbit IgG H&L (1:10,000; 31460, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Gel imaging was performed to detect the proteins, and ImageJ software was used for the analysis.

16S rRNA sequencing

At 5 d.p.i., intestinal contents were harvested from chickens in the control, Abx, Sczy3, RIP+Sczy3, RIP+Abx+Sczy3, and FMT+Sczy3 groups. Total DNA was isolated using the PowerSoil DNA Isolation kit (MO BIO Laboratories, Solana Beach, CA, USA) following the manufacturer's instructions. Amplification of the bacterial 16S rRNA gene was performed using primers designed from conserved regions. Purified, quantified, and normalized PCR outputs were used to create the sequencing library. The validated libraries were subjected to high-throughput paired-end sequencing on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform. After feature analysis, the samples were examined at various taxonomic levels to generate visualizations including species distribution bar charts (from phylum to species), UPGMA (Unweighted Pair Group Method with Arithmetic Mean) clustering trees, community structure diagrams, and species clustering heatmaps. Species differential analysis was performed using the STAMP software, followed by Spearman rank correlation analysis based on the abundance and variation of each species across different samples.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS software (version 18, SPSS Inc, Chicago, IL, USA) and results were presented as mean values ± standard deviation (SD). Each RT-qPCR experiment was independently conducted in triplicate. Relative gene expression was calculated using the 2^−ΔΔCt method after normalization to Ct values. Multiple comparisons were performed using one-way ANOVA followed by Holm-Sidak or Tukey’s post-hoc tests as appropriate for each experimental design. Statistical significance was defined as follows: P < 0.05 denoted a significant difference; P < 0.01 indicated a highly significant difference; P < 0.001 represented a very highly significant difference; and P < 0.0001 was interpreted as extremely significant.

Results

Abx treatment exacerbates clinical symptoms after IBV infection

Chickens received an antibiotic mixture in water administered via the drinking method (Fig. 1A). At Day 16, 16S rRNA sequencing assessed the intestinal microbiota of control and Abx-treated chickens to determine antibiotic effects. The Venn diagram revealed a lower number of species in the Abx group compared with the control group (Fig. 1B). Chickens in the Abx group exhibited lower microbial diversity and abundance compared to the control group, though the differences were not statistically significant (Fig. 1C). Non-Metric Multi-Dimensional Scaling (NMDS) showed a clear cluster separation between the control and Abx treatment group (Fig. 1D). The findings suggest that Abx therapy led to a reduction in the intestinal microbial community of chickens.

Chickens administered a cocktail of Abx for 12 days were infected with 0.2 mL Sczy3. Abx chickens infected with Sczy3 showed various clinical symptoms at 3 d.p.i, including sneezing, head shaking, tracheal rales, increased water intake, messy feathers, whereas Sczy3 chickens showed less obvious clinical symptoms. RT-qPCR was used to detect the viral load of kidney in different groups. The results showed that the viral load in the kidneys of chickens treated with Abx and infected with Sczy3 was higher than that in chickens infected with Sczy3 alone at 5 d.p.i (Fig. 1E).

Gut microbiota plays a crucial role in RIP-mediated alleviation of IBV-induced clinical symptoms and renal pathological damage

Intestinal flora clearance and FMT were conducted to explore the influence of gut microbiota on mitigating Sczy3-induced interstitial nephritis. Experimental design and sampling timeline are presented in Fig. 2A. At 3 d.p.i, chickens in Sczy3 groups exhibited more pronounced clinical symptoms compared to those in the RIP+Sczy3 and FMT+Sczy3 groups. At 5 d.p.i, when the viral load in kidney was assessed, it was notably reduced in the RIP+Sczy3 and FMT+Sczy3 groups relative to the Sczy3 group. However, viral load was significantly higher in the RIP+Abx+Sczy3 group than in RIP+Sczy3. (Fig. 2B). At 5 d.p.i., severe renal lesions, characterized by swollen and mottled kidneys, were observed in both the Sczy3 and RIP+Abx+Sczy3 groups (mean lesion score: 1.8±0.30, 1.5±0.22 at 3 d.p.i. and 2.5±0.32, 2.7±0.20 at 5 d.p.i., respectively), with no significant difference in lesion severity between them. In contrast, only mild enlargement was observed in the RIP+Sczy3 and FMT+Sczy3 groups (mean lesion score: 0.6±0.10, 0.9±0.19 at 3 d.p.i and 1.0±0.22 and 0.8±0.30 at 5 d.p.i., respectively), while the control group showed no visible abnormalities (Fig. 2C and D). The calculated lesion scores of the Sczy3, RIP+Sczy3 and RIP+Abx+Sczy3 groups demonstrated the effectiveness of the intestinal microbiota in RIP in alleviating renal swelling. Interestingly, at both 3 and 5 d.p.i, the FMT+Sczy3 group showed significantly lower lesion scores than the Sczy3 group (Fig. 2D). When compared to the control group, the Sczy3 group demonstrated significant elevations in creatinine, urea nitrogen, and uric acid levels at 3 d.p.i. and 5 d.p.i., respectively. These three indexes were significantly diminished in the RIP+Sczy3 group relative to the Sczy3 group but augmented in the RIP+Abx+Sczy3 group in comparison to the RIP+Sczy3 group at both time points. Furthermore, in contrast to the Sczy3 group, these three indexes were notably decreased in the FMT+Sczy3 group at 3 d.p.i. and 5 d.p.i. (Fig. 2E).

Gut microbiota plays a crucial role in RIP-mediated reduction of IBV-induced pathological damage and enhancement of intestinal tight junction proteins

Compared to the control group, the Sczy3 group exhibited reduced intestinal villus length, severe villus damage, uneven villus arrangement. In contrast, the RIP+Sczy3 group showed increased villus length, restored normal villus morphology compared to the Sczy3 group. However, similar to the Sczy3 group, the RIP+Abx+Sczy3 group demonstrated severe intestinal villus fragmentation and disordered arrangement. Compared to the Sczy3 group, the FMT+Sczy3 group exhibited increased villus length, relatively normal villus cell morphology, and a more organized arrangement (Fig. 3A).

In vivo gut microbiota depletion experiment and FMT experiment with histopathological changes and immunofluorescence analysis. (A) The histopathological changes (lesions) of chicken kidneys and ileum in different groups. (B) The Immunofluorescence of chicken ileum in different groups (200 × magnification).

Kidney section results indicated disordered cell arrangement, detachment of glomerular basement membranes, lymphocyte infiltration, and congestion in the Sczy3 group. In contrast, the RIP+Sczy3 and FMT+Sczy3 groups showed significant improvement compared to the Sczy3 group, with orderly cell arrangement, normal cell morphology, normal glomerular structure, and no obvious infiltration of lymphocytes or red blood cells, returning to the level of the control group. However, compared to the RIP+Sczy3 group, the RIP+Abx+Sczy3 group exhibited disordered cell arrangement, loose cell alignment, and detachment of glomerular basement membranes (Fig. 3A).

Immunofluorescence results revealed enhanced expression of intestinal mucosal proteins Claudin-1 and ZO-1 in the RIP+Sczy3 and FMT+Sczy3 groups compared to the Sczy3 group. Conversely, the expression of Claudin-1 and ZO-1 was reduced in the RIP+Abx+Sczy3 group compared to the RIP+Sczy3 group (Fig. 3B).

RIP-induced intestinal beneficial microbiota alleviates IBV-induced interstitial nephritis and is associated with suppression of the NLRP3 signaling pathway

To clarify how RIP-induced gut microbiota mitigates IBV-triggered interstitial nephritis, the mRNA and protein expression levels of inflammatory factors in the ileum and kidney, as well as the expression levels of tight junction proteins in the ileum were detected.

The mRNA and protein expression of pro-inflammatory factors including IL-1β, IL-6, IL-18, NLRP3 and Caspase-1 was significantly increased in the Sczy3 group compared with the control group in the kidney and ileum at 3 and 5 d.p.i. While when compared with the group RIP+Sczy3, the relative mRNA of IL-1β, IL-6, IL-18, NLRP3 and Caspase-1 was significant increase in the group RIP+Abx+Sczy3 in the kidney (Fig. 4A) and increase (not significant) in ileum (Fig. 4B). The protein expression of NLRP3, Caspase-1 and IL-1β in the group RIP+Abx+Sczy3 was significantly increased in comparison to the group RIP+Sczy3 in the kidney (Fig. 4D (1)) and in the ileum (Fig. 4D (2)).While when compared with the RIP+Sczy3 group, the expression of occludin and claudin-1 was significantly decreased in the RIP+Abx+Sczy3 group in the ileum (Fig. 4D (3)).

For FMT experiment, the relative mRNA and protein expression of these pro-inflammatory factors in the FMT+Sczy3 group was significantly decreased in comparison to the Sczy3 group in the kidney (Fig. 4A) and in the ileum (Fig. 4B). FMT restores gut microbiota balance by increasing the level of probiotics and reducing harmful gut bacteria, thereby reshaping the gut microbiota

Cecal microbiota was assessed via 16S rRNA gene sequencing. This investigation revealed 5901 Amplicon Sequence Variant (ASV)s, exhibiting a comparatively even ASV distribution among the study groups. (Fig. 5A). Venn diagram analysis revealed only 95 commonly shared elements across the five groups (Fig. 5B). The UPGMA cluster analysis revealed that the RIP+Sczy3 and the FMT+Sczy3 groups were clustered with the control group, whereas the RIP+Abx+Sczy3 group was grouped together with the Sczy3 group (Fig. 5C). At the Phylum level, Firmicutes, Bacteroidota, Proteobacteria, and Actinobacteriota were the prevalent phyla across all four groups. Specifically, in the cecum of Sczy3 group chickens, the relative abundance of Bacteroidota and Actinobacteriota -predominantly Gram-positive bacteria- was reduced compared to the control group, however, this abundance was elevated in the RIP+Sczy3 and FMT+Sczy3 groups relative to the Sczy3 group. Moreover, the RIP+Sczy3 group chickens exhibited a higher relative abundance of Firmicutes and Actinobacteriota in their cecum compared to the RIP+Abx+Sczy3 group. While Proteobacteria (Gram-negative) were more abundant in the cecum of Sczy3 group chickens than in the control group. Nevertheless, their abundance was diminished in the RIP+Sczy3 and FMT+Sczy3 groups relative to both the Sczy3 and RIP+Abx+Sczy3 groups (Fig. 5D (1)). At the genus level, the relative abundance of Faecalibacterium, Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium, all of which are probiotics, was reduced in the cecum of chickens in Sczy3 group compared to the control group. However, their abundance was elevated in the RIP+Sczy3 and FMT+Sczy3 groups relative to both the Sczy3 and RIP+Abx+Sczy3 groups. Conversely, Escherichia_Shigella, which is a harmful bacterium, was more prevalent in the cecum of Sczy3 group chickens than in control group, but its presence was diminished in the RIP+Sczy3 and FMT+Sczy3 groups compared to the Sczy3 and RIP+Abx+Sczy3 groups (Fig. 5D (2)). Cecal pathogens are the cause of intestinal inflammation in chickens

To investigate the interplay among inflammation-related elements, proteins linked to intestinal mucosal health, blood serum biochemical indicators, and the diverse composition of gut microorganisms, a Spearman correlation analysis was employed. The results suggested that the abundance of Bifidobacterium was significantly (P<0.05) and positively correlated with occludin, while significantly (P<0.05) and negatively correlated with creatinine, IL-18 and Caspase-1. The abundance of Lactobacillus was significantly (P<0.05) and negatively correlated with NLRP3. The abundance of Lachnospiraceae bacterium 28_4 was significantly (P<0.05) and positively correlated with creatinine, NLRP3, IL-1β, IL-6 and IL-18, while significantly (P<0.05) and negatively correlated with occludin, claudin-1 and ZO-1. The abundance of Escherichia_Shigella was significantly (P<0.05) and positively correlated with urea nitrogen and uric acid (Fig. 5F).

Combined application of lactobacillus and NaAc alleviates IBV-Induced clinical symptoms and mitigates pathological tissue damage in the ileum and kidneys

The 16S rRNA results indicate a significant negative correlation between Lactobacillus and NLRP3. Moreover, the abundance of Lactobacillus is significantly reduced in the Sczy3 group compared to the RIP+Sczy3 group. Thus, it is necessary to investigate whether Lactobacillus contributes to alleviating interstitial nephritis caused by IBV. Lactobacillus was isolated from chicken feces, and its appeared milky white with clear and well-defined edges. After sequencing, a BLAST homology analysis was performed in the NCBI database, revealing a 99.86% homology with the Lactobacillus reuteri strain numbered 1022. Short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) play a crucial role in gut microbiota. Acetic acid, a common SCFA produced by Lactobacillus, exhibits antibacterial activity and helps regulate intestinal pH. Therefore, this study investigates whether the combined application of NaAc and Lactobacillus is more effective than Lactobacillus alone in alleviating interstitial nephritis caused by IBV. The experimental setup and data collection timeline are depicted in Fig. 6A. The chickens in the Sczy3 group showed more severe clinical symptoms than the other three groups at 3 d.p.i. At 3 and 5 d.p.i, the kidneys were collected to measure the mean lesion score and the viral load. The results showed that the mean lesion score and the viral load of kidneys were significantly decreased in the Lac+Sczy3 and the NaAc+Lac+Sczy3 groups compared with in the Sczy3 group (Fig. 6B). The kidneys in the Sczy3 group were more swollen than the kidneys in other three groups (Fig. 6C). Based on 16S rRNA sequencing and corroborating experimental data, alongside existing research, we've begun to shed light on how gut microbiota influences IBV-induced interstitial nephritis via the gut-kidney axis (Fig. 8). The regulation of RIP-modulated probiotics and Lactobacillus reuteri, as well as their combined application with NaAc, can enhance the expression of tight junction proteins (ZO-1, occludin, and claudin-1in the intestine. This augmentation strengthens the intestinal mucosal barrier and restores pathological tissue damage in the gut. Simultaneously, it reduces the expression of inflammasomes in both the intestine and kidney, and decreases the levels of inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6, IL-8, and IL-1β, thereby mitigating inflammation and alleviating interstitial nephritis induced by IBV

Discussion

This study uncovers the critical role of gut microbiota in preventing interstitial nephritis caused by IBV through RIP. Specifically, RIP-modulated gut microbiota alleviated the pathological changes associated with interstitial nephritis, and this effect was correlated with enhanced intestinal mucosal barrier function, suppression of the NLRP3 signaling pathway, and reduced expression of inflammatory factors. Furthermore, we identified and isolated Lactobacillus reuteri, which similarly mitigated interstitial nephritis, and this effect was also associated with suppression of of the NLRP3 signaling pathway and suppression of inflammatory factor expression. These findings highlight a novel gut-kidney axis mechanism and provide valuable insights for developing probiotics with antiviral and anti-nephritis properties (Fig. 8)

The Abx cocktail treatment, widely used in mammals as a method for depleting gut microbiota, has also been studied in chickens and ducks (Abbas et al., 2024). Abx treatment can effectively eliminate most bacteria in chicken feces but simultaneously cause a significant increase in the relative abundance of conditional pathogens such as Escherichia coli. Studies have shown that Abx-induced gut microbiota depletion significantly enhances the susceptibility of poultry to avian influenza (Yitbarek et al., 2018), Marek's disease (Bavananthasivam et al., 2021), and IBV infections (Yin et al., 2022). Our experimental findings show a substantial rise in the IBV viral load in the chicken kidneys after Abx therapy, the successful establishment of this model provides an important foundation for subsequent experimental research.

Previous research from our group showed that RIP effectively mitigates interstitial nephritis caused by IBV infection (Xiang et al., 2024). To investigate the role of gut microbiota in the mechanism by which RIP alleviates IBV-induced interstitial nephritis, we conducted a gut microbiota depletion experiment. However, when gut microbiota was depleted, RIP treatment failed to significantly improve kidney and intestinal tissue damage caused by IBV. In the RIP+Abx+Sczy3 group, the expression levels of NLRP3 signaling pathway components and proinflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-18, and IL-6) in the kidney and ileum, as well as IBV viral load and renal injury markers (creatinine, urea nitrogen, and uric acid), were significantly increased compared with the RIP+Sczy3 group. These findings indicate that the gut microbiota is essential for RIP-mediated alleviation of IBV-induced interstitial nephritis. Supporting this conclusion, previous studies have shown that antibiotic-induced depletion of the gut microbiota can disrupt intestinal signaling pathways and metabolism, thereby altering metabolic homeostasis (Zarrinpar et al., 2018) and affecting drug metabolism and efficacy (Yang et al., 2023). For instance, antibiotic treatment has been shown to impair the response of mice to intratumoral injections of CpG-oligodeoxynucleotides (ODN) or oxaliplatin by reducing necrosis induction (via TNF-α), the magnitude of CD8+ T cell responses, and ROS production by myeloid cells (Iida et al., 2013). Furthermore, it has also been proposed that the immunomodulatory effects of herbal medicines may be achieved by modulating the composition of the intestinal flora (Feng et al., 2023; Li et al., 2023).

Studies have shown that the remodeling of intestinal flora can relieve intestinal inflammation and enhance the gut health of poultry (Diaz et al., 2019; Kogut, 2019). For instance, Zhang et al. conducted FMT experiments using gut microbiota from high-weight chickens and found that FMT significantly alleviated enteritis by downregulating the expression levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IFN-γ, IL-12, and IL-6) and upregulating anti-inflammatory cytokines (IL-4 and IL-10) (Zhang et al., 2022). Similarly, Cheng et al. utilized gut microbiota from Berberine-treated chickens in FMT experiments and observed reduction in hepatic inflammation, characterized by decreased the expression levels of TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 (Cheng et al., 2024). Beyond intestinal and hepatic health, gut microbes also influence kidney diseases by providing immune-mediated protection via the gut-kidney axis (Stavropoulou et al., 2020; Yin et al., 2022). However, whether RIP-modulated gut microbiota confers protection against viral-induced kidney injury remains largely unexplored.

In this study, FMT experiments using gut microbiota collected from RIP-treated chickens revealed that the reshaped microbiota significantly downregulated NLRP3 signaling pathway activity in intestinal and renal tissues, reduced the expression levels of inflammatory cytokines, and decreased serum levels of creatinine, blood urea nitrogen, and uric acid. These effects effectively alleviated IBV-induced intestinal inflammation and interstitial nephritis. Collectively, these results suggest that FMT can reshape gut microbiota, mitigate intestinal inflammation, and alleviate kidney damage through the gut-kidney axis.

In our experimental design, A normal donor FMT control group was not included, as our preliminary data (Xiang et al., 2024;) demonstrated that the gut microbiota of RIP-treated donor chickens was specifically increased in antiviral beneficial bacteria (including Faecalibacterium, Oscillospiraceae, and Ruminococcaceae) and decreased in potentially harmful Escherichia_Sigella, suggesting that RIP-shaped microbiota itself possesses antiviral characteristics. We acknowledge that a separate FMT control (without IBV challenge) was not included, which is a limitation. Nevertheless, all treated groups underwent the same gavage procedure performed according to standard protocols, minimizing procedural variability. More importantly, the magnitude of the observed protective effects—especially the reduction in viral load and inflammatory factors, as well as the increase in tight proteins —far exceeded the range that could be explained by the related immune regulation caused by stress alone. This strongly supports the existence of a microbiota-specific effect. We therefore conclude that the protection is largely mediated by RIP‑shaped microbiota, but is not entirely attributed to the microbial community itself.

The intestinal barrier, composed of a monolayer of columnar epithelial cells connected by tight junctions, plays a critical role in defending against the invasion of harmful microorganisms, including bacteria, viruses, and toxins from the intestinal lumen (Song et al., 2014; Ducatelle et al., 2018; De Meyer et al., 2019). Disruption of tight junctions leads to increased intestinal permeability, resulting in a condition commonly referred to as "leaky gut syndrome." This can subsequently cause morphological changes in the intestinal wall, mucosal damage, and systemic inflammation (Di Vincenzo et al., 2024; Matar et al., 2024). The tight junction complex primarily includes ZO-1, claudin-1and occludin (Zhang et al., 2024). In IBV-infected intestines, our immunofluorescence and Western blot analyses revealed significantly reduced expression of ZO-1, claudin-1and occludin, accompanied by pronounced villus destruction and mucosal damage observed in HE staining. We hypothesize that this intestinal barrier dysfunction may be closely linked to the continuous accumulation of urea (Vaziri et al., 2013). However, FMT experiments demonstrated that FMT significantly upregulated the expression of ZO-1, claudin-1 and occludin, thereby alleviating IBV-induced intestinal damage. These findings suggest that FMT can enhance tight junction integrity, restore intestinal mucosal structure, and mitigate intestinal inflammation and barrier dysfunction caused by IBV infection.

Moreover, studies have demonstrated that the early colonization of gut microbiota is essential for the development of innate and adaptive immunity in chickens. An imbalance in this microbiota can lead to the proliferation of pathogenic bacteria, particularly the enrichment of Gram-negative bacteria (Rodrigues et al., 2021; Yin et al., 2022). The phylum Proteobacteria, a major group of Gram-negative bacteria, is widely present in the poultry gut and closely associated with various inflammatory diseases (Jiang et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024). Research has shown that the abundance of Proteobacteria increases in cases of ileitis and oviduct inflammation in broilers (Li et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024). Among its members, Escherichia_Shigella is an opportunistic pathogen (Sims and Kim, 2011) capable of invading intestinal epithelial cells, disrupting the mucosal barrier, and causing intestinal bleeding, edema, diarrhea, and even death (Shi et al., 2014). Consistent with previous findings (Xu et al., 2020), our study observed a significant increase in the abundance of Escherichia_Shigella following IBV infection. Furthermore, the abundance of Escherichia_Shigella positively correlated with serum levels of creatinine, blood urea nitrogen, uric acid, and pro-inflammatory cytokines (NLRP3, Caspase-1, IL-6, IL-18, and IL-1β), indicating that it may exacerbate IBV-induced pathological damage by promoting inflammatory responses. Conversely, Gram-positive bacteria such as Firmicutes, Actinobacteria, and Verrucomicrobia play crucial roles in maintaining intestinal health and microbial homeostasis. In both gut microbiota depletion and FMT experiments, we observed an increase in the abundance of beneficial bacteria including Faecalibacterium, Lactobacillus, and Bifidobacterium. Previous studies have demonstrated that these probiotics can suppress the growth of pathogenic bacteria in poultry through competitive exclusion mechanisms, thereby reducing inflammatory responses (Huang et al., 2018; Tabashsum et al., 2020). For example, Yang et al. demonstrated that supplementing with Lactobacillus can restore the intestinal microbial environment of chickens and reduce intestinal inflammation (Yang et al., 2020). In this study, we further found that the abundance of these beneficial bacteria negatively correlated with creatinine, blood urea nitrogen, uric acid, and pro-inflammatory cytokines, while positively correlating with intestinal mucosal proteins such as ZO-1, occludin, and claudin-1. Lactobacillus showed a significant negative correlation with NLRP3. Therefore, we selected Lactobacillus to conduct anti-IB experiments.

Using a specialized Lactobacillus culture medium, we isolated a strain identified as Lactobacillus reuteri through NCBI BLAST analysis. Previous studies have highlighted the multifaceted potential of Lactobacillus reuteri in poultry production, including promoting growth, enhancing immune functions, and improving gut health (Shi et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2022; Chai et al., 2023). However, its role in combating IBV infection and alleviating interstitial nephritis remains unreported. This study is the first to demonstrate that Lactobacillus reuteri can alleviate IBV-induced interstitial nephritis and enteritis by downregulating the NLRP3 signaling pathway, reducing inflammatory cytokine expression, and enhancing intestinal tight junction integrity. The reason for not establishing a separate NaAc intervention group is that the study consistently focused on the primary mechanism by which RIP exert their effects through the gut microbiota. During the experimental design, we hypothesized that Lac might act through the synergistic action of its multiple metabolites. However, the molecular mechanisms and signaling pathways underlying the synergistic effects among these metabolites have not yet been fully elucidated. Therefore, NaAc, as one of the metabolites, was not set as an independent intervention.

Conclusion

This study has elucidated the profound role of gut microbiota in the alleviation of interstitial nephritis caused by IBV through RIP. Furthermore, FMT utilizing RIP-modulated gut microbiota has demonstrated its capacity to reshape the gut microbiota and mitigate both intestinal and renal inflammation. Additionally, we have discovered that Lactobacillus reuteri, either alone or in combination with sodium acetate, exhibits protective effects against IBV-induced enteritis and interstitial nephritis. This is the first evidence indicating that Lactobacillus reuteri can alleviate interstitial nephritis caused by IBV. Our findings offer significant insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying the antagonistic action of probiotics against IBV-induced interstitial nephritis and pave the way for the development of anti-IBV drugs.

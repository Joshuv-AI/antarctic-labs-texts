Depth of invasion and extranodal extension: the influential factors to predict survival rate of patients with oral tongue squamous cell carcinoma
Authors: Maedeh Ghorbanpour, Samaneh Salarvand, Sahar Salarvand, Fatemeh Shahsavari, Mohammad Shirkhoda, Pouyan Amini Shakib, Reza Ghalehtaki
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCancer staging is essential in determining patients’ prognoses and designing the appropriate treatment strategy. American Joint Committee on Cancer has released the latest version of the staging system for tongue SCC. However, it is interesting to know whether this change in staging and the addition of depth of invasion (DOI) and the extra-nodal extension (ENE) have any influence on patients’ prognosis.MethodsIn this retrospective cohort study, the pathology records of patients with tongue SCC who underwent surgery at the Pathology Department of Cancer Institute Hospital, 2017–2021, were collected by referring to the hospital information system. Then the rate of change of pT, pN, and overall stage were assessed based on American Joint Committee on Cancer 7th and 8th editions.ResultsThe records of 204 patients were included in the final analysis. Significant changes in the staging system 2021 resulted in upstaging 64 patients (31.4%) in the overall stage, 91 patients (44.6%) in pT, and 30 patients (14.7%) in pN. The survival of upstaged patients was inferior compared to those without upstaging. However, this was not statistically significant for tumor and overall upstaging in the univariate analysis, while those with upstaged pN had significantly shorter survival. In the multivariate analysis, pT upstage also significantly impacted survival.ConclusionThis study showed the importance of pathology reports based on the latest edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer, the accuracy in examining factors such as depth of invasion and extra-nodal extension.

Introduction

Oral tongue squamous cell carcinoma (OTSCC) is a malignant tumor with an incidence ranging from 0.4 to 3.3% per year, comprising 90% of oral cavity malignancies [1]. It is most commonly observed in middle-aged patients and involves the deep layers of the tongue and mouth floor in a considerable fraction of patients [2, 3]. Using tobacco and alcoholic drinks are the major risk factors for OTSCC, while human papillomavirus (HPV) genotypes and low intake of fruit and vegetables have also been suggested to play a minor role [4].

The overall incidence of OTSCC has increased since the 2000s [5]. Although the improved survival of OTSCC in the past decade, it is essential to identify the predictive factors of prognosis [6]. Like other cancers, the tumor stage is a major factor influencing the patients’ prognosis, correlated with the risk of metastasis, lymph-vascular invasion (LVI), and perineural invasion (PNI). Also, a positive surgical margin and depth of invasion (DOI) have been identified as independent predictors of patients’ outcomes [7]. Accordingly, the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC), in its latest edition, called the 8th edition in 2021, changed the TNM staging to include DOI in T staging; in this staging system, tumors previously classified as T1 will be classified as T2 in the presence of DOI > 5 mm and those previously classified as T2 will be classified as T3, in the presence of DOI > 10 mm [8]. Another significant change in the new staging system is the inclusion of extra-anodal extension (ENE), defined as the involvement of lymph node surrounding tissues by the tumoral cells, diagnosis of which can be made by clinical, radiographical, or histological assessment [9]. Although these changes have significant implications for management strategies and reduce the overtreatment rate [10], it is interesting to know whether they affect patients’ prognosis. Therefore, the present study was designed to compare the survival of patients with OTSCC, staged based on the 7th and 8th editions of the AJCC staging system.

Materials and methods

This retrospective cohort study was conducted at the pathology department of Cancer Institute Hospital, Tehran, Iran. This study was performed accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The protocol was approved by the local Ethics Committee (code: IR.IAU.DENTAL.REC.1400.012). Information regarding disease stage, grade, survival rate, and treatment response was meticulously recorded in designated data forms. Tissue samples obtained during surgery from patients. Written consent for the possible use of patient sample information is obtained from the patients at the beginning of the hospitalization while maintaining the principles of secrecy and confidentiality.

All patients diagnosed as OTSCC (pathologically confirmed) from 2017 to 2021 and who underwent surgery at this center were considered the study population. The researchers referred to the hospital information system archive and evaluated all medical records, selected those with OTSCC, and included those who underwent surgery at this center and had complete cancer-related information. Patients who had metastasis at the time of diagnosis and had not received curative treatments were excluded. A total of 253 patients were evaluated, and 204 had the inclusion criteria.

The following data were recorded for each patient: age, sex, tumor size, DOI, lymph node involvement, ENE, tumor stage and grade, vascular and perineural invasion, and involvement of surgical margins. Also, necessary information was collected regarding recurrence, metastasis, type of treatment, and survival. Tumor stage, pN, and pT were calculated based on the two grading systems, AJCC 7th and 8th editions, considering DOI and ENE.

Statistical analysis

The collected data were put into the statistical software SPSS Statistics for Windows version 21.0 (IBM Corp. 2012. Armonk, NY: IBM Corp.), used for the statistical analysis. Results were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for quantitative variables and were summarized by frequency (percentage) for categorical variables. One–sample Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was used to determine the normal distribution of data, and Levene’s test was used to test the equality of variances. Continuous variables were compared using a t-test or Mann-Whitney U test whenever the data distribution was not normal or when the assumption of equal variances was violated across the study groups. Categorical variables were, on the other hand, compared using the Chi-square test. Survival analysis was carried out using Kaplan-Meier method and log-rank test, and graphs were drawn. Differences in staging systems were calculated for each patient, and the effect of upstaging on survival was also evaluated. In all analytical tests, P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 204 patients were included; 53.4% were men (N = 109), and 70.1% were older than 45. Table 1; Fig. 1 describe the distribution of each specific stage in patients. Based on AJCC 7th edition, the majority of cases were staged as 1 or 4a, while based on the 8th edition, the majority were staged as 2 and 3. The most considerable growth was seen in stage 4b, which comprised a considerable fraction of the 8th edition stage.

The changes in staging resulted in upstaging of 64 patients (31.4%) in stage, 91 patients (44.6%) in T, and 30 patients (14.7%) in N (Tables 1, 2 and 3). Most changes in tumor upstaging occurred in T1 and T2, in which about half of the patients converted to one stage higher due to the inclusion of DOI in staging. In node upstaging, most changes occurred in N2a and N2b, and a considerable proportion of them converted into N3b due to the inclusion of ENE in staging. In overall upstaging, the main change occurred in stages I and II to their immediate upper stage as a reflection of tumor upstaging and stage IVa conversion to IVb as a reflection of node upstaging.

Data on specific treatment modalities and outcomes were not available for all patients. All patients were treated by upfront simple or complex glossectomy with or without reconstruction, followed by radiotherapy with or without concurrent chemotherapy. The indications for radiotherapy included close or positive margins, T3/T4 tumors, multiple nodal involvement, disruption of surgical planes due to excisional biopsy for diagnosis before surgery, and the presence of PNI or LVI. The indications for concurrent chemotherapy included positive margins, extranodal extension, and/or the presence of multiple indications for radiotherapy. Among 149 patients with available data about recurrence, a total of 27 patients developed recurrence until the time of this study (18.12%), and among 156 patients with data of death, 19 died (12.17%). The survivors had a median follow-up of 31 months. Kaplan Meier survival curves for each stage for 2016 (7th edition of AJCC) and 2021 (8th edition of AJCC) have been shown in Figs. 2 and 3. It is evident that stage 4b, almost seen exclusively in the 8th edition of AJCC, has a very poor survival. This stage belongs to those patients with ENE (N3). Comparing the variables in terms of mean and 3-year survival (Table 2) showed that the tumor grade and tumor size, LVI, lymph node involvement, and ENE had a significant association with survival (P < 0.05). Also, receiving radiotherapy and recurrence were significant negative factors for survival (P < 0.05). The majority of patients who died had recurrence (75%), while only 11.9% of patients who survived had recurrence (P < 0.001).

Multivariate Cox regression analysis of the effect of variables on survival

Kaplan Meier analysis (Figs. 1, 2 and 3), performed to evaluate the association of upstaging in stages T and N with patients’ survival, showed that the survival of upstaged patients was inferior compared to those without upstaging. However, this was not statistically significant for tumor and overall upstaging (P for log-rank > 0.05; Table 2), while those with upstaged nodes had significantly shorter survival (P for log-rank = 0.001).

Considering the association between variables and survival in the univariate analysis, those variables with P < 0.1 and the research-defined important variables were included in the multivariate Cox regression analysis. Due to the high association between ENE and pN upstage, the pN stage was excluded from the multivariate analysis. The results of multivariate regression analysis are shown in Table 3; as indicated, tumor size > 4 cm (P = 0.002), LVI (P = 0.014), ENE (P = 0.045), and T upstaging (P = 0.003) were significant predictors of OS.

Discussion

In the present study, we described the overall outcome of patients with OTSCC and studied the effect of the staging system on the patients’ outcomes at a median follow-up of about 31 months. We showed the change in the distribution of the same group of patients based on the 7th and 8th editions of the AJCC TNM staging. By including ENE and DOI in the staging, we witnessed considerable changes in the N and T staging and, consequently, in the overall staging. DOI per se was not a significant predictor of OS, but when combined with the tumor size, pT upstage was a significant independent predictor of OS. The impact of ENE on the OS was not unexpected due to its close relationship with distant metastasis.

These results are consistent with previous reports. In the study on 120 subjects, increased DOI (HR = 1.09), nodal metastasis (HR = 7.1), and stage III/IV (HR = 9.45) were significant predictors of OS and PNI (HR = 8.56), and lymphovascular invasion (HR = 6.01) were associated with disease recurrence [11]. Another study on 210 patients with OTSCC showed that the tumor’s DOI, PNI, resection margin, treatment, and clinical stage were independent prognostic factors of 5-year survival [12]. Others have also referred to the prognostic significance of DOI [13] and PNI in patients with OTSCC [14]. These results align with those of the present study and justify the significance of changes made in the latest version of the AJCC tumor staging system.

The results of the present study showed that several patients were upstaged based on the new staging system, and T upstage was found as a significant predictor of OS in these patients. These results validated the 8th edition of the AJCC staging system in an Iranian population that has yet to be studied, as far as the authors are concerned. In Italy, an evaluation of 73 patients with OTSCC showed that 34% were upstaged based on the 8th AJCC criteria (compared to the 7th edition); patients with N upstage had a higher recurrence rate and worse DFS and OS. These results confirm the better stratification of the 8th edition of the AJCC criteria in patients with OTSCC [15], which is in line with the results of the present study. In another study in Italy, the evaluation of 211 patients with OTSCC showed negative prognostic value regarding disease-specific survival and OS for the 8th version of AJCC, independent of other histological and clinical parameters. They also built a nomogram on prognostic factors (TSR, perineural invasion, and sex) [16]. Another study on 300 patients with OTSCC showed that patients staged as IV based on the 8th version had a 5-fold greater risk of death than stage I and a 3-fold greater risk of DFS [17]. In Japan, 112 patients with OTSCC were evaluated, and the results showed that seven patients were upstaged from T1 to T2 and 19 from T2 to T3; they also suggested a correlation of nodal metastasis with T classification and DOI in both editions (7th and 8th ) [18]. In China, an evaluation of 208 patients with OTSCC by the two staging systems showed that 28.85% were upgraded in terms of T stage, and none were down-staged [19]. These results are consistent with the present study.

Other studies have validated the 8th AJCC staging system in patients with oral cavity SCC overall. In Australia, comparing the prognostic and discriminatory ability of the 8th and 7th editions of AJCC in patients with oral cavity SCC showed that the new system could discriminate disease-free survival (DFS), while the previous edition could not; worse DFS was reported for patients staged as 4a and 4b, compared to stage 1 [20]. These results showed the significance of the 8th edition of AJCC, which is in line with the results of the present study. In a cohort of 298 patients, T upstaging was observed in 22.8% and N upstaging in 29.2% of patients with oral cavity SCC; the upstaged patients had a worse DFS and OS [21]. In addition, In the UK, studying patients with SCC of the oral cavity showed 30% upstaged patients based on the 8th edition of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) staging manual; they showed a worse OS in patients upstaged from IVa in the previous edition than in the new system [22]. These results confirmed the high performance of the 8th edition of the AJCC staging system in the discrimination of survival, which is in line with the results of the recent study.

The results of the present study also showed the effect of tumor grade, size, LVI, extra-nodal invasion, and LN number on OS. Others have also referred to the significance of lymph node characteristics in the prognosis of oral SCC [23, 24]. Evaluating 21,843 patients with OTSCC showed several factors, including age, race, marital status, T stage, N stage, M stage, and radiation, as significant predictors of OS [25]. In the study by Yu and colleagues, they reported an essential role for DOI; they disclosed a sensitivity of 86.2% and specificity of 52% for DOI above 4.05 mm and revealed that DOI ≥ 3.2 mm predicted an incidence of occult lymph node metastasis of > 20% [19]. These results emphasized the significance of DOI in the staging system, which has been suggested previously as well [26].

Our study has some strengths and limitations. The major strength is its relatively large sample size of OTSCC in a single institution that confers a uniform treatment strategy. This uniformity makes the primary staging more robust in predicting survival. One of the limitations of the present study was the need for more detailed treatment data in some patients; as none of the groups reached the median survival, we could not report median values.

Conclusion

This study showed the importance of pathology reports based on the latest edition of the AJCC, the accuracy in examining factors such as DOI and ENE, and the need to pay attention to these factors in choosing the appropriate treatment methods by the oncologist.

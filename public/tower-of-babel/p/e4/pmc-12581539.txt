Exploring the effect of Huangqi Fuling Decoction on gastric cancer based on UPLC-MS, network pharmacology and experiments in vitro
Authors: Doudou Lu, Ling Yuan, Guoqing Chen, Fandi Meng, Qian Gu, Yubin Ma, Yuanyuan Feng, Xuelan Feng, Jianjun Zhao, Yi Nan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHuangqi Fuling Decoction (HQFLT) is a traditional Chinese medicine prescription that is applied to treat a variety of diseases. However, the mechanism of HQFLT in the treatment of gastric cancer is still unclear, which hinders its clinical application.MethodsComponents and targets of HQFLT were collected from TCMSP, TCMID and ETCM databases, and potential targets of gastric cancer were collected from GEO database. The intersection targets of component disease were imported into STRING database to construct protein–protein interaction network, and the intersection targets were analyzed by GO and KEGG to explore the involved biological processes. The network topology analysis of intersection targets was carried out using Cytoscape software, and hub genes were selected according to the degree value. To further identify the main active components of HQFLT, we used UPLC-MS/MS techniques for analysis and intersected with network pharmacological results to obtain the most critical components. Molecular docking techniques were used to calculate the binding energy of intersection components and hub genes. Regarding cell experiments, the effect of HQFLT on the viability of gastric cancer cells was detected by CCK-8 assay, and the effect of HQFLT on cell proliferation was verified by flow cytometry and colony formation assay. The transwell experiment was conducted to investigate the effect of HQFLT on tumor metastasis. qRT-PCR and Western blot were used to detect the regulatory effects of HQFLT on hub genes and key signaling pathways.ResultsA total of 189 active components, and 278 targets of HQFLT and 1817 targets of gastric cancer were obtained by network pharmacology. A total of 51 intersection targets were screened by Venny method. These targets were mainly involved in signal transduction, apoptosis regulation and angiogenesis, and may be involved in the regulation of MAPK signaling pathway. A total of 5 hub genes were obtained after degree screening by Cytoscape, namely IL6, PTGS2, ICAM1, VCAMI and CCL2, which showed significant differences in clinical staging, molecular typing and survival prognosis. After the intersection of 44 components obtained from UPLC-MS and network pharmacology, Calycosin was obtained. The results of molecular docking showed that the compound had strong binding ability with hub genes. Regarding cell experiments, HQFLT had the ability to inhibit proliferation, block cell cycle, induce cell apoptosis, inhibit colony formation, and inhibit migration and invasion of gastric cancer cells. qRT-PCR and Western blot experiments confirmed that HQFLT could down-regulate the expression of hub genes and inhibit the activation of RAS-MER-ERK signaling pathway.ConclusionHQFLT inhibited the proliferation and metastasis of gastric cancer. The mechanism may involve the regulation of the RAS-MEK-ERK signaling pathway, with ICAM1 and VCAM1 being potential targets for targeted therapy.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12906-025-05111-6.Keywords: Gastric cancer, HQFLT, RAS/MER/ERK, Proliferation, Metastasis

Introduction

Gastric cancer (GC) is a digestive system disease with high morbidity and mortality that seriously endangers human health [1]. Early GC lacks typical symptoms, so most patients were diagnosed in the middle and late stages. The treatment methods for GC, such as radiotherapy, chemotherapy and immunotherapy, have brought survival benefits to partial patients, but there were limitations like severe side effects, high cost and easy relapse [2–4]. In general, there is an urgent need to find a drug that can fight GC with few toxic side effects.

Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) plays an important role in the treatment and prevention of diseases. Huangqi Fuling Decoction (HQFLT) is a representative classic prescription from the Chinese medical book "Beiji Qianjin Yao Fang" from more than 1300 years ago, which is composed of Astragalus (HQ), Poria cocos (FL), Ophiopogon japonicus (MD), Rhizoma chuanxiong (CX), Ramulus cinnamomi (GX), Ginger (SJ), Schisandra chinensis (WWZ) and Jujube (DZ). It has been widely used as a basic prescription in the adjuvant treatment of tumors to relief the toxic side effects caused by chemotherapy, such as peripheral nerve toxicity [5], upper limb lymphedema [6], hand-foot syndrome [7] and cancer pain related to bone metastasis [8]. Modern pharmacological studies have found that HQFLT contains astragalus polysaccharide, poria acid, astragalus IV, tangerine, ligustrazine and other active components, which have been confirmed to have anti-GC effects, and the mechanism may be related to reducing epithelial mesenchymal transformation (EMT) [9], regulating glycolysis [10], inhibiting hypoxia [11] and activating ER stress [12]. However, the actual effects of HQFLT on GC and underlying molecular mechanisms remain unclear.

TCM prescriptions are characterized by complex components, multiple pathways of action and unclear targets, so it is of great significance to scientifically explain the active components and mechanism of classical prescriptions. Professor Andrew L. hopkins [13] and Professor Shao Li [14] proposed the concept of "network targets" to construct networks of drugs, active compounds, targets, and diseases to predict the likely pathway of pharmacological action of a prescription. Combined with ultra-high performance liquid chromatography/tandem mass spectrometry (UPLC-MS/MS) technology, the main active compounds of the prescription were further identified. The combination of these methods provides a more accurate and convenient model for the basic research of TCM.

In this study, network pharmacology, bioinformatics and UPLC-MS/MS techniques were used to identify the active compounds and targets of HQFLT, and the possible mechanisms of anti-proliferation and metastasis of GC were verified by in vitro cell experiments. Materials and methods

Network pharmacology

Screening of the target and compound of HQFLT

TCMSP [15] (https://old.tcmsp-e.com/tcmsp), TCMID [16] (http://www.megabionet.org/tcmid) and ETCM [17] (http://www.tcmip.cn/ETCM) are comprehensive databases of Chinese herbal medicine compounds, targets, pharmacokinetics and other information. Oral bioavailability (OB) ≥ 20% and drug-likeness (DL) ≥ 0.10 are important parameters for screening and evaluating active compounds. The components and targets of HQFLT were obtained from these databases, and the network diagram of the components and targets was constructed.

Screening of GC -related targets

GEO (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) is a public database of various kinds of tumor high-throughput experimental data. We input the keywords "Gastric Cancer", "Gastric Tumor", "Stomach Cancer" and "Stomach Tumor" to search in database, setting filtering criteria: study type (Expression profiling by array), top organisms (Homo sapiens), publication dates (From 2018/01/01 to 2024/03/01). We focused on the dataset of mRNA changes of genes in GC tissues and adjacent normal. Genes with P < 0.05 and Log FC ≥ 1 were defined as differentially expressed genes (DEGs). Log FC > 1 was the up-regulated genes, while Log FC < −1 was the down-regulated genes.

Construction of protein–protein interaction (PPI) network of common targets of HQFLT and GC

Venny diagrams are often used to find similarities or differences between sets of interest. We obtained the intersecting targets of GC and HQFLT through the overlapping part of Venny diagram, and drew heat maps to analyze differential expression between GC and normal tissues. The STRING [18] database (https://cn.string-db.org) contains information about protein interactions, both physical and functional. We imported the intersecting targets into the STRING database to obtain the tsv format file of PPI. Calculate the degree score, betweness and closeness of the targets in Cytoscape 3.9.1 software. The higher the score, the more important the target was in the network. Two rounds of screening were performed according to the median to obtain the core targets.

Bioinformatics analysis

Gene ontology (GO) and kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) analyses

GO is an annotation tool for obtaining comprehensive information about a gene or protein, including Cellular Component (CC), Molecular Function (MF), and Biological Process (BP). KEGG analyzes the genes involved in the regulation of signaling pathways by combining them according to functional similarity. DAVID [19] (https://david.ncifcrf.gov) is an integrated Pubchem, DrugBank, HPA, DisGeNET, WikiPathways information comprehensive database, offers a variety of gene or protein function annotation information. GO and KEGG analyses of genes can be obtained by the DAVID database to explore the relationship of prescription components-targets-diseases. From the results obtained by DAVID, the top 10 GO entries with the largest number of enrichment targets and the top 9 types of KEGG entries were selected for display, and KEGG Mapper [20] (https://www.genome.jp/kegg) was used to display the details of specific pathway.

Correlation between hub genes and GC

To investigate the clinical value of hub genes, multiple databases were used to analyze their expression levels in different tissues, stages, types, and prognoses. GEPIA2.0 [21] (https://www.genome.jp/kegg) is a database of tumor tissue information from TCGA and GTEx to obtain gene expression levels in different tissues and stages. The molecular subtypes of GC include CIN, EBV, GS and MSI, and different subtypes are related to the prognosis of patients, with the best prognosis in EBV group and the worst prognosis in GS group. The expression of hub genes in GC typing was obtained from GSCA [22] (http://bioinfo.life.hust.edu.cn) database. In addition, Kaplan–Meier [23] (https://kmplot.com/analysis) tool was used to analyze the relationship between overall survival and hub genes.

Identification of chemical compound in HQFLT by UPLC-MS/MS

We used UPLC-MS/MS technology to identify the active components of HQFLT. The prescription consists of 8 kinds of herbs, and the ratio of the compounds was as follows: HQ: MD: CX: FL: GX: SJ: WWZ: DZ = 3:3:2:2:2:2.14:2.14:4.29. All the above herbs were purchased from The Affiliated TCM Hospital of Ningxia Medical University and have passed the check of The Plant List Online (http://www.theplantlist.org) (The information on prescriptions is shown in Table 1). HQFLT powder was mixed with a 70% methanol aqueous solution, centrifuged, filtered, and subjected to ultrasonic treatment in a water bath for 90 min (power 600 W, frequency 40kHz). Vanquish UHPLC ultra-high performance liquid chromatography system combined with ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 (2.1 mm X 100 mm, 1.8µm) column was used for separation: mobile phase A was 0.1% formic acid aqueous solution, mobile phase B was 0.1% formic acid acetonitrile solution. The gradient washing flow rate was 0.3 mL/min: 0 ~ 17 min (5%B), 17 ~ 17.2 min (2% ~ 98%B), 17.2 ~ 20 min (5%B). Q-Exactive HFX mass spectrometer samples were combined with UPLC system for mass spectrometry acquisition.

Molecular docking

The components obtained from TCMSP, TCMID and ETCM databases were intersected with the identification results of UPLC-MS/MS to obtain the key components of HQFLT, and the molecular docking with hub genes was performed to verify the binding ability. Molecular docking is a tool for calculating the interactions of small molecule compounds and proteins, simulating the affinity of drugs to bind to targets in the body. The docking process was as follows: the SDF structure of the compound was obtained from the Pubchem [24] (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) database, and the energy optimization of the structure was performed with Chem3D software. Download the structure diagram of hub genes from the Uniprot [25] (https://www.uniprot.org/) database. AutoDock Vina [26] software was used to remove water molecules and add hydrogen atoms for small molecule compounds and proteins, set up the active pocket of molecular docking and calculate the binding energy. The lower the binding energy, the stronger the binding ability of the compound to the genes and the better the conformational stability. PyMOL 2.3 software was used to visualize the best conformation for affinity.

Cell experiments

Cell culture and reagents

Human gastric mucosal epithelial cells (GES-1) (337,969, BNCC, China) were cultured in DMEM. GC cell lines AGS (GCC-ST0008CS, GeneChem, China) and HGC-27 (GCC-ST0004CS, GeneChem, China) were cultured in DME/F-12 (11,320,033, Gibco, United States) and RPMI-1640 (11,875,119, Gibco, United States). All media were supplemented with 10% FBS (SH30256.01, Gibco) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin, and the cell were incubated at 37◦C in 5% CO2 incubator. Fluorouracil (5-FU) was purchased from MedChemExpress (HY-90006, MedChemExpress, United States), dissolved in DMSO. The cells were cultured in a culture medium containing DMSO without drugs as the control group.

Cell viability assay

AGS, HGC-27, and GES-1 cells were inoculated on 96-well plates with a density of 6 × 103 cells/well. Cells were treated with different concentrations of HQFLT and 5FU for 24, 48, and 72 h, respectively, and 10μl Cell Counting Kit 8 (CCK-8) reagent (HY-K0301, MedChemExpress, USA) was added to each well. After incubation at 37℃ for 1-2h, the absorbance at 450nm wavelength was detected with an enzyme-labeled instrument.

Flow cytometry

AGS and HGC-27 cells were inoculated in 6-well plates and cultured overnight. After treatment with different concentrations of HQFLT and 5FU for 24 h, the cells were digested and collected by trypsin without EDTA. According to the instructions of apoptosis (KGA107, KeyGEN BioTECH, China) and cell cycle (KGA512, KeyGEN BioTECH, China), different staining reagents were added and detected by flow cytometry instrument. The experimental procedure for cell cycle and apoptosis was different in that the cell cycle was pretreated synchronously with serum-free medium, and the cells were collected and fixed in anhydrous ethanol.

Colony formation assay

The proliferation ability of GC cells was detected by colony formation assay. AGS and HGC-27 were inoculated in a 6-well plate with a density of 800 cells/well. After 10 days of treatment with different concentrations of HQFLT and 5FU, 4% paraformaldehyde was fixed for 30 min, and 0.5% crystal violet was soaked for 30min. Finally, the number of cells ≥ 50 (visual ≥ 1 mm) was counted and statistically analyzed.

Transwell assay

The transwell assay simulates the biological process of tumor cell metastasis. Firstly, a serum-free medium containing 2 × 104 cells was added to the upper chamber (Costar3422, Corning, United States), and a medium containing 30% FBS was added to the lower chamber. The cells were treated with different concentrations of HQFLT and 5FU for 24h. Fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with 0.5% crystal violet, the number of invaded and migrated cells were photographed and counted. The invasion assay required the preparation of matrix glue (B-P-00002–2, B-P-00002–4, B-P-00002–10, Biozellen, China) in the upper chamber 4 h in advance. Other experimental procedures were consistent with the migration assay.

Quantitative real time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted from cells with TRIzol reagent (343,706, ThermoFisher Scientific, United States). The reverse transcription kit (RR047A, TaKaRa, Japan) and fluorescence quantification kit (RR820A, TaKaRa, Japan) were purchased from Takara. GAPDH was selected as the reference gene. Western blot analysis

According to the instructions of the protein extraction kit (KGP250, KeyGEN BioTECH, China) and the protein content detection kit (KGP902, KeyGEN BioTECH, China), the total protein content of the cells was Quantitatively extracted and calculated by BCA method. SDS-PAGE protein isolate was transferred to PVDF membrane, closed with protein blocking solution for 15 min, and the primary antibody was incubated at 4℃ overnight (primary antibody: anti-proto-oncogene, GTPase (KRAS) (ab275876, Abcam, 1:1000, Britain), anti-extracellular regulatory protein kinase 1/2 (ERK1/2) (9102, CST, 1:2000, United States), anti-phosphorylated extracellular regulated protein kinases 1/2 (p-ERK1/2) (4370, CST, 1:2000, United States), Anti-aldehyde −3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) (51,332, CST, 1:1000, United States), anti-β-actin (#AF7018, Affinity, 1:6,000, United States), anti-β-tubulin (#T0023, Affinity, 1:10,000, United States), anti-C–C motif chemokine Ligand 2 (CCL2) (#DF7577, Affinity, 1:2000, United States), anti-recombinant prostaglandin endoperoxide synthase 2 (PTGS2) (66,351–1-Ig, Proteintech, 1:2000, China), anti-recombinant vascular cell adhesion molecule 1 (VCAM1) (#DF6082, Affinity, 1:1000, United States), anti-intercellular cell adhesion molecule 1(ICAM1) (#AF6088, Affinity, 1:1000, United States), anti-interleukin 6 (IL6) (TD6087, Abmart, 1:1000, China)). Then membranes were incubated with secondary antibody at room temperature for 60-90min. Finally, the protein signal was observed with high sensitivity ECL luminescent reagent and analyzed with Image J.

Immunofluorescence assay

AGS and HGC-27 cells were inoculated on sterile glass plates and treated with HQFLT and 5-FU for 24h. After fixation with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min, AGS and HGC-27 cells were infiltrated with 0.5% TritonX-100 (T8200, Solarbio, China) for 30 min, and then blocked with 5% BSA for 30min. Primary antibodies were incubated at 4℃ for 24 h (anti-p-ERK antibody: 4370, CST, 1:400, United States) and secondary antibodies at room temperature for 60 min (111–545-144, Jackson ImmunoResearch, 1:100, United States). After 3 times of PBS washing, DAPI (H-1200, Vectorlabs, United States) was added to stain the nuclei. The cells were photographed with the fluorescence microscope.

Data statistics

All analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0 software. Continuous data was expressed as the mean ± SD, and qualitative data was expressed as rate. Differences between two groups were assessed by Students t test, and differences among three groups were assessed by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Network pharmacology analysis

Active compounds and targets prediction of HQFLT

According to OB ≥ 20% and DL ≥ 0.10, 189 effective compounds were screened from TCMSP, TCMID and ECTM databases, including 21 kinds of Astragalus (HQ), 8 kinds of Poria cocos (FL), 6 kinds of Ophiopogon japonicus (MD), 20 kinds of Rhizoma Chuanxiong (CX), 12 kinds of Ramulus Cinnamomi (GX), 30 kinds of Ginger (SJ), 10 kinds of Schisandra chinensis (WWZ) and 28 kinds of Jujube (DZ) (Supplementary Table S1provides detailed information). A total of 278 potential targets of HQFLT active ingredients were obtained after removing duplicate values from 782 targets (Fig. 1).

Network diagram of HQFLT compounds-targets

Targets prediction of gastric cancer

After setting filtering criteria, 103 entries were obtained from GEO database. We focused on the dataset of mRNA changes of genes in GC tissues, and obtained a total of 9 datasets. The dataset GSE118916 with the maximum number of samples was selected, including 15 in cancer tissues and 15 in adjacent normal tissues. The dataset GSE118916 in the GEO database included a total of 49,372 GC-related targets. After standardization with the Uniprot database, 20,072 targets were obtained. According to P < 0.05 and |Log FC|> 1, 1817 DEGs were screened, among which 1635 were up-regulated and 1551 were down-regulated (Fig. 2A-B).

Venn Drawing

Venn diagram showed that there were 51 intersection targets between HQFLT and GC (Fig. 2C, Supplementary Table S2 provides detailed information), among which 38 were up-regulated and 13 down-regulated (Fig. 2D). Heat maps of the abundance values of 51 genes showed that three-quarters of the targets were up-regulated in GC tissue (Fig. 2E).

PPI network of HQFLT

The PPI network between HQFLT and GC was constructed using STRING database, and 47 targets were obtained when the minimum interaction score was set to 0.4. The network was visualized in Cytoscape software with 47 nodes and 269 edges. When the median of degree was > 9, 23 targets were selected. When the degree was > 18, 13 core genes were obtained (Fig. 3A). The top 5 nodes of degree were interleukin 6 (IL6), recombinant prostaglandin endoperoxide synthase 2 (PTGS2), matrix metalloproteinase 9 (MMP9), MYC proto-oncogene (MYC), and C–C motif chemokine Ligand 2 (CCL2) (Fig. 3B).

Screening and enrichment analysis of targets. A Screening process of PPI network. The redder the color, the more important the target is in the network. B Degree value of core targets. C GO analysis of targets. D Circle plot of KEGG enrichment analysis

GO analysis and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis

We used the DAVID database to study the biological function of HQFLT-GC intersection targets. GO results showed that the intersection targets were mainly related to signal transduction, angiogenesis and apoptosis regulation. They were mainly localized in the membrane and cytoplasm, and were associated with protein binding, homologous protein binding, and zinc ion binding (Fig. 3C). KEGG results showed that intersection targets were involved in the regulation of 49 signaling pathways, including p53 signaling pathway, ErbB signaling pathway, microtubule associated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathway, phosphatidylinositol kinase (PI3K)/serine/tyrosine protein kinase (AKT) signaling pathway, etc. (Fig. 3D). After further displaying the pathway details in KEGG Mapper, we found that multiple targets were involved in the regulation of MAPK signaling pathway (Fig. 4A). Combined with the degree values of the targets in the PPI network, we selected PTGS2, CCL2, IL6, intercellular cell adhesion molecule-1 (ICAM1) and recombinant vascular cell adhesion molecule 1 (VCAM1) as the hub genes to further explore correlation with GC.

Bioinformatics analysis

Expression levels of the hub gene

We analyzed the clinical relevance of hub genes. The expression of VCAM1 and ICAM1 in GC tissues were significantly higher than that in normal tissues (Fig. 4B-C). Among the molecular subtypes, VCAM1 and CCL2 were expressed higher in the GS type than in the other types (Fig. 4D). Regarding clinical staging, the expressions of VCAM1, ICAM1 and CCL2 were significantly increased in middle and advanced GC (Fig. 4E). Kaplan–Meier results showed that the expression of VCAM1, PTGS2, and IL6 were inversely correlated with patient prognosis, indicating that the higher the genes expression, the shorter the patient's survival time (Fig. 4F). These results indicated that hub genes have good clinical correlation, which provided a theoretical basis for the subsequent experimental verification.

UPLC-MS/MS analysis of the active compound of HQFLT

The HQFLT components were identified by UPLC-MS/MS, and 1485 components were obtained. According to the scores, 44 active components were labeled in positive and negative ion chromatography (Fig. 5A-B), including 5 kinds of alkaloids, 2 kinds of amino acids and peptides, 5 kinds of fatty acids, 5 kinds of polyketones, 19 kinds of shikimate and Phenylpropanoids, and 10 kinds of others. The 44 compounds include magnolithine, ferulic acid, 6,7-dihydroxytriglycerides, coumarin, verlein, hydroxytriglycerides, etc. The names, structures and other details of the compounds are shown in Supplementary Table S3.

Molecular docking

After the intersection of network pharmacology and the identification results of UPLC-MS/MS, Calycosin was obtained, which may be one of the main components of HQFLT against GC (Fig. 6A, B). To investigate whether the compound could bind to the hub genes in vivo, we used molecular docking methods to predict the binding probability. The lower the binding energy between the molecule and the target, the more stable the binding. When the binding energy is less than −4.25 kcal/mol, it indicates that the two have certain binding activity. The binding energy is less than −5.0 kcal/mol, indicating that the two have good binding activity. When the binding energy is less than −7.0 kcal/mol, it indicates that the ligand and the receptor have strong binding activity [27]. Molecular docking results show that the binding energies of Calycosin with the five hub genes were −7.2kcal/mol (VCAM1), −7.6kcal/mol (ICAM1), −9.1kcal/mol (PTGS2), −8.9kcal/mol (IL6) and −7.0kcal/mol (CCL2), respectively. Binding energies were all < −7kcal/mol (Fig. 6C), indicating that Calycosin had strong binding ability with hub genes.

Cell experiments

HQFLT affects cell viability

In order to study the anti-proliferation effect of HQFLT, we used a cell counting kit to detect the viability of GC cells treated with HQFLT concentrations of 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12mg/mL for 24, 48 and 72h. The results of CCK-8 showed that the IC50 of AGS were 7.269, 7.259 and 6.164mg/mL, respectively, while HGC-27 cells were 9.45, 6.507 and 5.581mg/mL. The cell viability decreased gradually when the concentration of HQFLT was in the range of 4-12mg/mL, but when the concentration was 7.2mg/mL, the cell viability of AGS did not change significantly at different times (Fig. 7A). Therefore, HQFLT had a dose-dependent but not time-dependent effect on GC cell viability. In addition, we selected 5FU as the positive control group, and the results showed that the IC50 of 5FU in AGS and HGC-27 were 11.110μm and 44.640μM, respectively (Fig. 7B). To compare the toxicity of the drugs, we treated GES-1 cells with 2-18mg/ml of HQFLT and 0-80μM of 5FU for 24 h, respectively. We found that the cell viability of GES-1 remained above 90% at IC50 concentration of HQFLT (AGS: 7.269mg/ml, HGC-27: 9.45mg/ml), but it was lower than 20% when 5FU concentration increased to IC50 concentration (AGS: 11.110μM, HGC-27:44.64μm) (Fig. 7C).

HQFLT inhibits cell proliferation

We detected the percentage of cell cycle and apoptosis rate of GC after 24 h treatment with different concentrations of HQFLT. G0/G1 phase is the early stage of DNA synthesis in tumor cells, and it is also an important stage for the action of many anti-cancer drugs. The cell cycle results showed that with the increase of HQFLT concentration, the percentage of gastric cancer cells in G0/G1 stage increased significantly compared with the control group, and the percentage in AGS (8, 10, 12 mg/mL) were 50.36%, 51.52 and 55.64%, respectively. HGC-27 (10, 12 and 14mg/mL) were 8.46%, 11.64% and 36.41%, respectively (Fig. 8A, Supplementary Table S4 provides detailed information). The results of Annexin V-FITC/PI double staining showed that HQFLT could induce apoptosis of GC cells. Compared with the control group, the apoptosis rates of AGS groups (8, 10 and 12 mg/mL) increased by 5.39%, 9.18% and 27.65%, respectively. HGC-27 (10, 12 and 14mg/mL) increased by 1.44%, 4.62% and 29.39%, respectively, but the rate of the HQFLT group was lower than the 5FU group (Fig. 8B, Supplementary Table S5 provides detailed information). The number of colony cell was determined after 10 days of treatment with HQFLT and 5FU. The results showed a concentration-dependent reduction in the number of colony cells (Fig. 8C, Supplementary Table S6 provides detailed information).

HQFLT inhibits cell metastasis

Metastasis is one of the most important phenotypes of tumors, and it is also one of the important reasons for the recurrence of tumors. We used transwell assay to detect the effects of HQFLT on the migration and invasion abilities of GC cells. The results of cell migration showed that with the increase in HQFLT concentration, the number of successfully migrated cells gradually decreased. Compared with the control group, the number of migrating cells in AGS and HGC-27 high-concentration groups (AGS: 12mg/mL, HGC-27: 14mg/mL) decreased by 519 and 542.33, respectively (Fig. 9A), which was similar to the results in the 5FU group. Cell invasion and migration results showed consistent trends (Fig. 9B, Supplementary Table S7 provides detailed information).

HQFLT affects the expression of the hub genes

To verify whether HQFLT could regulate hub genes, we used qRT-PCR to preliminarily detect the expression of VCAM1, ICAM1, PTGS2, IL6 and CCL2 at the mRNA level. qRT-PCR results showed that after treatment of AGS and HGC-27 cells with 10mg/mL and 12mg/mL HQFLT for 24 h, the levels of VCAM1, PTGS2, IL6 and CCL2 were significantly decreased, but the levels of ICAM1 were not significantly changed in HGC-27 cells (Fig. 10A-B). The process from transcription to translation involves various modifications and regulations, resulting in discrepancies between mRNA and protein levels of genes. This could explain why ICAM1 mRNA did not show significant changes [28, 29]. We used Western blot to detect changes in hub genes at protein level. The results showed that the protein levels of VCAM1, ICAM1, PTGS2, IL6 and CCL2 were down-regulated after 24 h treatment with HQFLT (Fig. 10C-D, Supplementary Fig. 1 provides detailed information).

HQFLT regulates of the MAPK signaling pathway

Through the functional enrichment analysis of the targets, we found that MAPK pathway may be one of the signaling pathways of HQFLT against GC. Studies have shown that the RAF-MEK-ERK axis of the MAPK signaling pathway plays an important role in the proliferation and survival of tumor cells. Therefore, we preliminary verified the regulatory effect of HQFLT on MAPK signaling pathway at the mRNA level. qRT-PCR results showed that the expression levels of KRAS, ERK1 and ERK2 decreased after HQFLT treatment for 24 h (Fig. 11A). Western blot results showed that RAS expression was significantly down-regulated, while ERK protein changes were not statistically significant. ERK was reported to play a regulatory role only if it is phosphorylated and transported from the cytoplasm to the nucleus. Discussion

The incidence and mortality of gastric cancer (GC) continue to rise annually, imposing a significant economic burden on society. Typical clinical symptoms include vomiting, abdominal pain, and weight loss. However, most patients are diagnosed with GC in the middle or late stages, during which the optimal window for surgical intervention has often passed and the risk of tumor metastasis is high. Current treatments include endoscopic resection, chemoradectomy, molecular targeting, and immunosuppressants, but the 5-year survival rate for patients remains below 50%, and there are many complications associated with each treatment. TCM has played a role in the treatment of diseases in China for thousands of years and has shown good efficacy in reducing cancer growth, inhibiting metastasis and improving clinical symptoms. However, the mechanism of HQFLT in the treatment of GC is still unclear, so we used network pharmacology, mass spectrometry component identification and in vitro cell experiments to explore the mechanism of prescription against GC.

HQFLT is a classic Chinese medicine prescription that was mainly used in the treatment of gastric abscess and necrosis. TCM considers that "Abscess and necrosis" are lesions with toxin aggregation, which can form abscess in superficial sites and tumor in internal organs. The clinical trial of HQFLT plus or minus formula has proved that it can significantly improve the toxic and side effects of radiotherapy and chemotherapy in patients and improve their quality of life. Therefore, it is of great clinical significance to study the effect and mechanism of HQFLT on GC. However, the composition of TCM prescription is complex, which brings limitations to the basic research of prescriptions and the extensive clinical application in the future. With the development of science, the rise and cross of network pharmacology, bioinformatics and other disciplines have brought great changes to Chinese medicine research. The application of mass spectrometry technology provides an opportunity to further uncover the mystery of TCM treatment for tumors.

In this study, we used network pharmacology, bioinformatics and mass spectrometry to identify the components, targets and mechanisms of HQFLT against GC. Through the rigorous screening of network pharmacology, we obtained a total of 189 active components, including astragaloside, kastricol, Quercetin, 6-gingerol, etc., which showed that these components have anti-tumor effect. Astragaloside is one of the main components of Astragalus, which has a good inhibitory effect on tumor EMT and angiogenesis [9]. Kaempferol is considered to be an important inducer of autophagy, which can inhibit the proliferation of various tumors such as GC [30], non-small cell lung cancer [31], breast cancer [32], and prostate cancer [33]. Quercetin has been proven to delay the progression of GC [34], cholangiocarcinoma [34] and other tumors by regulating ferroptosis. Gingerol is the main active component of ginger, which can enhance the sensitivity of cisplatin [35], docetaxel [36] and other chemotherapy drugs and provide a new idea for clinical treatment. Next, we obtained 51 common intersection targets of HQFLT and GC by Venny method, which were closely related to biological processes such as signal transduction, apoptosis regulation, gene expression, and angiogenesis. This provided a theoretical basis for our follow-up experiments to verify HQFLT's anti-proliferation and metastasis of GC. The topological values of PPI network were further screened to show that VACM1, ICAM1, PTGS2, IL6 and CCL2 were key targets for GC treatment, and these targets showed differential expression with clinical stage, molecular typing and prognosis of tumors, providing a theoretical basis for targeted therapy and diagnosis of GC.

The application of computer technology aids basic research; however, excessive reliance on database predictions may result in biased outcomes. Therefore, we conducted in vitro cell experiments on HQFLT to validate the predicted results. The active components of HQFLT were further identified by UPLC-MS/MS technique, 44 of the 1485 components were labeled, and a common component, Calycosin was obtained after comparison with the network pharmacological results. It is an isoflavone extracted from Astragalus, which can induce autophagy in thyroid cancer cells [37], inhibit EMT process in triple-negative breast cancer cells [38], regulate tumor microenvironment [39] and other processes. We also conducted systematic experimental validation in vitro to enhance the stability of the results. 5FU plays an anticancer role by interfering with the DNA synthesis of tumor cells, but its toxic effect on normal cells is one of the main causes of gastrointestinal reactions, myelosuppression and neurotoxicity in patients with chemotherapy. In the cell viability test, we found that both HQFLT and 5FU had anti-proliferation effects on GC cells, but the toxicity of HQFLT to normal cells was less than 5FU, which may alleviate the adverse reactions of patients after chemotherapy. The growth of normal cells is strictly regulated in the body, and signals are released to guide cells to enter the growth-division cycle regularly. However, tumor cells are not regulated by growth signals, and destroy the G0/G1 phase of the cell cycle, so that a large number of cells enter the S phase in advance to maintain continuous growth. Therefore, how to induce tumor cells to block in the early stage of DNA replication is one of the important methods of anti-tumor therapy. Currently approved chemotherapeutic drugs such as cisplatin, carboplatin and cyclophosphamid mainly have anti-cancer effects in the G0/G1 phase. Our flow cytometry results confirmed that HQFLT's blocking effect on GC cells was similar to the above chemotherapy drugs, both of which could induce a large number of cells to be blocked in the G0/G1 phase and lose the ability of DNA replication and could induce cell apoptosis. Colony formation experiments also further confirmed that HQFLT could inhibit the proliferation of tumors.

Metastasis is another common and important phenotype of tumor, which is also one of the important reasons for the recurrence of tumor. Metastasis of tumor cells from the primary site to distant sites involves the following steps; it first breaks through the blood vessels or lymphatic vessels near the primary focus, then escapes in the lumen with the circulation of body fluids, and finally extravasates to distant tissues and colonizes. Thus, invasion is the first step toward metastasis. In the transwell experiment, we used stromal glue to simulate the extracellular matrix. Tumor cells need to secrete matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) to degrade the stromal glue, enabling their movement and mimicking the process of distant metastasis. Our results confirmed that HQFLT inhibited the invasion and migration of GC cells. In addition, we found that when the drug dose was the same, the number of cells invaded was less than migration, which is presumed to be related to the blocking of matrix glue. Based on the experimental results, we found that HQFLT can inhibit proliferation, promote apoptosis and inhibit metastasis of GC cells, but the molecular mechanism needs to be further explored.

Although we have confirmed the anti-proliferation and anti-metastasis effects of HQFLT on GC through bioinformatics and cell phenotype experiments, the molecular mechanism is still unclear. The identification of specific molecules targeted by drugs is not only necessary to improve the therapeutic effect on tumors, but also very important to reduce toxic side effects [40]. Currently, the clinically approved trastuzumab targeting HER2 [41] and ramucirumab targeting VEGF [42] have been confirmed to be effective in the first-line treatment of gastric cancer. Among the five hub genes we screened, VCAM1 and ICAM1 were reported to be promising targets for targeted therapy. VCAM1, a vascular adhesion molecule-1, is highly expressed in a variety of malignant tumors, which is consistent with our experimental results. VCAM1 can significantly enhance MRI signals in primary and metastatic brain tissue tumors [43], and the use of targeted alpha particle radiotherapy combined with lead-212 (212Pb) and anti-VCAM-1 antibody (212 PB-α vcam-1) can significantly inhibit the growth of brain tissue tumors [44]. ICAM1 is a cell surface glycoprotein receptor whose overexpression promotes distant metastasis of tumors. ICAM1 significantly enhanced the peak signal in Positron Emission Tomography, which provided the basis for the diagnosis of tumor. Drugs targeting ICMA1 have been shown to produce similar and consistent effects as the chemotherapeutic drug guitabine [45]. Our results also confirmed that the intervention of HQFLT can significantly down-regulate the expression of ICAM1. These results suggest that HQFLT can down-regulate the expression levels of VCAM1 and ICAM1, which provides an experimental basis for further research on whether it is a direct target of HQFLT.

Previous studies have shown that dysregulation of MAPK signaling pathway has been confirmed to be related to the occurrence of various tumors, involving the processes of growth, differentiation, metastasis and angiogenesis [46, 47]. The RAS-MEK-ERK axis is a key link in the MAPK signaling pathway, and RAF and MEK kinases are activated in an environment-dependent manner to initiate a continuous enzymatic cascade [48, 49]. Mutations in KRAS, a subtype of RAS, were associated with the development of 20% of tumors [49]. We demonstrated that HQFLT down-regulates RAS expression at both transcriptional and translational levels, blocking signal transmission upstream. ERK is a mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK), a type of cytoplasmic kinase activated in response to various stimuli. ERK phosphorylates a large number of substrates to perform different molecular functions. Activate forkhead box protein O3 (FOXO3A) [50], bcl-2-like protein 11 (BIM) [51], bcl-2-interacting killer (BIK) [52] and other genes to degrade proteasome or induce ubiquitination, and promote tumor proliferation; Phosphorylated Abi1 [53], serine A [54], and focal adhesion kinase 1 (FAK) [55] promote the process of distant invasion and metastasis of tumors. Previous studies have shown that a variety of Chinese herbs and compounds can effectively inhibit the activation of the MAPK signaling pathway, including Guizhi Fuling decoction, Shashen Maidong decoction, shikonin, quercetin, berberine [56–59]. In this study, we found that HQFLT only has a down-regulation effect on p-EKR1/2. Immunofluorescence localization showed that the expression level of p-ERK1/2 in the nucleus of GC was significantly increased, and the intervention of HQFLT could affect the expression of p-ERK1/2 into the nucleus, block the interaction with the downstream substrate, and exert the anti-GC properties.

Conclusion

In summary, our results suggest that HQFLT inhibits the proliferation and metastasis of gastric cancer, possibly through the RAS/MER/ERK signaling axis. In addition, we screened IL-6, VCAM1, ICAM1, PTGS2 and CCL2, among which VCAM1 and ICAM1 may be expected to become the targets of HQFLT, providing a research basis for clinical diagnosis and treatment. However, due to time limitations, only in vitro cell samples were used in this manuscript. In order to ensure the rigor of scientific research, in future studies, we plan to further verify the regulatory effect of HQFLT on the proliferation and metastasis of gastric cancer in vivo animal experiments. The Lip-MS technique was used to explore whether the active components of HQFLT had direct effects on VCAM1 and ICAM1.

Evolving minimally invasive strategies for lumbar hernia repair: a video article comparing conventional laparoscopy, robotic surgery, and articulating laparoscopy
Authors: Ji Yeon Mun, Bong-Hyeon Kye, Nina Yoo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeLumbar hernia repair presents unique surgical challenges due to its rarity and complex anatomical location. This study aimed to compare 3 minimally invasive surgical approaches for lumbar hernia repair: conventional laparoscopy, robotic surgery, and articulating laparoscopy.MethodsThree patients diagnosed with lumbar hernia underwent repair via the transabdominal preperitoneal approach using different minimally invasive techniques. Each patient was treated with one of 3 approaches: conventional laparoscopy, robotic surgery, or articulating laparoscopic instruments. Clinical outcomes, operative details, and costs were analyzed.ResultsEach technique demonstrated distinct advantages: conventional laparoscopy offered familiarity, robotic surgery provided enhanced visualization and maneuverability, while articulating instruments presented a cost-effective alternative with improved range of motion.ConclusionMultiple minimally invasive approaches for lumbar hernia repair are feasible, each with unique benefits, allowing surgeons to choose based on resource availability and expertise.Keywords: Hernia, Laparoscopy, Minimally invasive surgical procedures, Robotic surgical procedures, Surgical instruments

INTRODUCTION

Lumbar hernia, also known as a flank hernia, occurs via a congenital or acquired defect in the posterolateral abdominal wall. It is often described as a flank hernia interchangeably [1,2]. The European Hernia Society defined a flank hernia precisely by the location anteromedial to the lumbar region, inferior to the subcostal region, and superior to the iliac region [3,4]. It is known to occur rarely. By 2018, approximately 350 cases had been reported in the English literature [4]. In addition, the anatomical complexity of the lumbar region, involving multiple muscle layers such as the latissimus dorsi, external and internal oblique muscles, and erector spinae, makes surgical intervention for lumbar hernias challenging.

Surgical treatment for lumbar hernias has undergone significant advancements over the years. Traditionally, the open anterior approach involving a large incision has been the standard method [4,5]. With the introduction of minimally invasive techniques in 1996 [6], laparoscopic surgery, utilizing either transperitoneal or extraperitoneal approaches, has been attempted by some surgeons due to benefits such as smaller incisions and faster recovery times [1]. An increasing number of surgeons are now adopting minimally invasive surgery (MIS) techniques, with a particular emphasis on robotic-assisted procedures. This transition is driven by the unique capabilities of robotic platforms, which effectively address many of the limitations associated with conventional laparoscopic abdominal wall surgery.

However, the high cost of robotic platforms may limit their accessibility in some healthcare settings. In response to this limitation, recent advancements in articulating laparoscopic devices have emerged as a promising alternative [7,8]. These innovative instruments increase the range of motion available to surgeons, helping to overcome some of the difficulties inherent in laparoscopic abdominal wall hernia surgery. To illustrate the differences and advantages of various MIS approaches, this article offers comparative video clips demonstrating 3 different surgical techniques for transperitoneal hernia repair: conventional laparoscopic devices, robotics, and articulating laparoscopic instruments.

METHODS

Three patients diagnosed with lumbar hernia, either congenital or acquired, underwent lumbar hernia repair using MIS approaches. Each patient was treated with one of 3 different techniques: conventional laparoscopy using straight instruments, laparoscopy with articulating instruments, and robotic surgery. For the conventional laparoscopic procedure, a 2-dimensional (2D) camera system with 3 working ports was used. For the articulating laparoscopic approach, a 3-dimensional (3D) camera system with 3 working ports was employed. For the robotic approach, the da Vinci Xi system (Intuitive Surgical, Inc.) was utilized with a 3-port configuration. In all cases, port placement was performed on the side opposite to the target hernia site. A central camera port was placed first, followed by the insertion of one working trocar on each side. A polyester mesh (Parietene, Medtronic) was used for all patients. The mesh size was 30 × 30 cm in both laparoscopic approaches and 6 × 12 cm in the robotic approach. In all approaches, mesh fixation was performed using absorbable sutures. The operative principle was consistent across all 3 procedures, including creation of a peritoneal flap, reduction of herniated tissue, closure of the fascial defect, placement of synthetic mesh, and closure of the peritoneal flap.

Two surgeons performed the operations. Surgeon 1, who performed the robotic procedure, had 25 years of clinical experience, including 20 years in MIS and 10 years in robotic surgery, with over 2,000 hernia repairs and 6 lumbar hernia cases. Surgeon 2, who performed the laparoscopic procedures using both conventional and articulating devices, had 15 years of clinical experience, including 10 years in MIS and 8 years in robotic surgery, with over 500 hernia repairs but no prior lumbar hernia cases. Both surgeons received formal robotic surgery training prior to clinical implementation and were granted independent operating privileges following institutional evaluation. The articulating laparoscopic instruments (Artisential, LIVSMED, Inc.) have been in routine clinical use since 2021 across various laparoscopic procedures, including colorectal cancer surgery.

This study adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Institutional Review Board of The Catholic University of Korea, St. Vincent’s Hospital (No. VC24ZISI0172). The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective design, use of anonymized clinical data, and absence of additional procedures beyond standard clinical care.

RESULTS

Individual case descriptions

Case 1

A 64-year-old male without any history of injury or trauma presented with bulging fatty tissue in the right flank area. His CT scan revealed a fascial defect in the superior lumbar triangle (Table 1). The patient underwent multiport laparoscopic transabdominal preperitoneal (TAPP) hernia repair using conventional straight devices (Supplemental Video 1). The total operation time was 206 minutes. No intraoperative or postoperative complications occurred.

Case 2

A 71-year-old female with a history of spine surgery presented with bulging in the left flank area. Her CT scan showed bowel incarceration and a fascial defect in the left posterolateral abdominal wall (Table 1). The patient underwent multiport robotic TAPP hernia repair (Supplemental Video 1). The total operation time was 166 minutes. No intraoperative or postoperative complications occurred.

Case 3

A 68-year-old male without a history of trauma or surgery presented with bulging fatty tissue in the right back-to-flank area. Similar to the first case, the CT scan demonstrated a fascial defect in the superior lumbar triangle (Table 1). The patient underwent multiport laparoscopic TAPP hernia repair using articulating devices (Supplemental Video 1). The surgeon utilized an articulating hook electrocautery in the right hand and an articulating clinch in the left hand to create the peritoneal flap and reduce the herniated fat back to the retroperitoneum. A conventional straight needle holder was used to close both the fascial defect and the peritoneal flap. The total operation time was 100 minutes. No intraoperative or postoperative complications occurred. A case summary of the 3 patients is presented in Table 1.

Clinical outcomes

The length of hospital stay was 6 days for the conventional group, 4 days for the articulating laparoscopic group, and 5 days for the robotic group. No intra-hospital complications or adverse events were observed in any of the 3 patients. Postoperative pain was generally mild, with an average visual analog scale score of 3–4, consistent with typical wound pain following hernia repair. At postoperative outpatient follow-up, all patients were free of pain and complications, and follow-up was concluded without further issues. All patients resumed daily activities and returned to work within 2 weeks after surgery.

Cost analysis

Table 2 summarizes the surgical costs associated with each minimally invasive approach. The surgical fee for the conventional laparoscopic procedure was 668,000 Korean Won (KRW), whereas that for the articulating laparoscopic procedure was 942,127 KRW. Although the same procedural code for incisional hernia repair was applied in both cases, the difference in surgery dates (2022 vs. 2024), along with the introduction of a laparoscopic-specific reimbursement code in 2024, accounted for the cost discrepancy. The same size and type of mesh was used in both cases, with a slight price difference (145,650 KRW vs. 151,460 KRW), likely attributable to price inflation over time. Therefore, when accounting for the difference in surgery dates, the cost discrepancy between conventional and articulating laparoscopy can be attributed primarily to the cost of the articulating instruments. In the Artisential case, 2 articulating instruments—a grasper and an energy device—were used, resulting in an additional cost of 679,190 KRW per device, totaling 1,358,380 KRW. The cost of the robotic procedure, which includes all robotic instruments, surgical materials, and procedural fees, was set at 8,000,000 KRW.

DISCUSSION

Lumbar hernia repair presents unique surgical challenges due to both its rarity and complex anatomical location. Although traditional open repair techniques are well-documented [4,5], there is limited consensus on the optimal surgical approach for minimally invasive procedures. The paucity of standardized methodology, particularly for minimally invasive techniques, creates uncertainty in surgical decision-making. In this article, we present 3 novel minimally invasive approaches for lumbar hernia repair, expanding the surgical options available to surgeons.

As with any other field in surgery, the management of lumbar hernia may benefit from advanced minimally invasive technology. The evolution from 2D to 3D camera systems has enhanced depth perception and spatial orientation, significantly improving surgical precision. Robotic platforms further advance this capability by providing surgeons with autonomous control over surgical field visualization, ensuring stable and optimal views throughout the procedure. Furthermore, the EndoWrist technology (Intuitive Surgical, Inc.) in robotic systems provides enhanced degrees of freedom in instrument movement, effectively addressing the restricted range of motion associated with conventional laparoscopic approaches. This technological advancement overcomes the limited maneuverability that has historically challenged surgeons during laparoscopic procedures.

However, the widespread adoption of robotic platforms is hindered by high costs and limited accessibility. Now, articulating laparoscopic instruments have emerged as a practical compromise, bridging the gap between conventional and robotic approaches. Although these instruments require a learning curve and have limitations such as weaker grasping force and the absence of a locking mechanism, they enable adequate tissue manipulation in confined operative fields, particularly when robotic systems are unavailable.

From our experience, conventional laparoscopy offers sufficient performance in patients with small hernias and minimal fat, but is highly dependent on the assistant for camera control due to its fixed instrument angles. Articulating systems, while less reliant on the assistant, are limited in strong traction and suturing capability. Robotic systems provide excellent dexterity, suturing precision, and stable visualization, and are particularly advantageous in complex cases—provided that cost and system availability are not prohibitive.

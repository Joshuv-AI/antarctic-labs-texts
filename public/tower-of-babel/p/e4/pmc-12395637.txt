Efficacy and safety of hydromorphone caudal block combined with intercostal nerve block during VATS: a single-center randomized controlled study
Authors: Xianghua Cao, Qiangjun Gui, Pengfei Li, Haoran Yan, Zhiyong Chen, Yujiao Wei, Shujuan Zhang, Wen Yang, Xueping Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe management of post-operative pain while minimizing opioid use remains challenging in thoracic surgery. While intercostal nerve blocks are commonly used, the optimal multimodal approach remains unclear.ObjectivesTo evaluate whether adding hydromorphone caudal blockade to intercostal nerve blocks improves post-operative recovery outcomes in thoracic surgery patients.DesignA prospective, double-blinded, randomized controlled trial.SettingDongguan Tungwah Hospital.PatientsEighty adult patients scheduled for video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) were enrolled.InterventionPatients were randomized to receive either ultrasound-guided caudal blockade with 1 mg hydromorphone (caudal group) or normal saline (control group) in addition to standard intercostal nerve blocks.Main outcome measuresThe primary outcome was the Quality of Recovery-40 (QoR-40) score at 48 h post-surgery. Key points

This single-center, randomized double-blind study evaluated the efficacy of combining hydromorphone caudal blockade with intercostal nerve blocks versus intercostal nerve blocks alone following video-assisted thoracoscopic procedures.

Introduction

Minimally invasive thoracic procedures, particularly video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS), have revolutionized lung operations by utilizing small intercostal incisions. This technique now represents approximately 50% of thoracic surgical procedures, marking a significant shift in practice over the last ten years [1–4]. Clinical studies [1, 5, 6] demonstrate that VATS offers several advantages over traditional thoracotomy: patients experience reduced postoperative discomfort, achieve mobility earlier, and spend fewer days in hospital. However, a significant number of patients still face challenges with intense acute pain following surgery and delayed recovery after VATS procedures [7].

As a revolutionary approach to perioperative patient care, Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) protocols have transformed clinical practice. This evidence-based methodology has demonstrated remarkable success in both improving patient outcomes and reducing healthcare costs. Central to the ERAS framework is the implementation of multimodal pain management strategies that minimize opioid use [8]. This should include the use of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), acetaminophen, ketamine, lidocaine, gabapentinoids, epidural techniques, and regional anesthesia approaches [9–11]. One of the most often used methods of regional anesthesia in children is caudal epidural blocking [12, 13]. One type of low level epidural block is sacral block. According to recent randomised controlled trials (RCTs) conducted by our lab, patients who get a single-dose epidural injection of hydromorphone report minimal side effects and safety [14].

In general, there are currently few studies assessing hydromorphone single-dose epidural injection in VATS. Specifically, only one study assessed Quality of Recovery-15 (QoR-15) as the main endpoint in patients who had erector spinae block for VATS surgery [1]. We performed this single-center RCT among patients undergoing VATS to evaluate the hypothesis that hydromorphone caudal block in combination with intercostal nerve block results in a superior quality of recovery than intercostal nerve block analgesia alone.

Methods

Trial design

The study was a prospective, double-blinded, randomized controlled trial. The Institutional Review Board of Dongguan Tungwah Hospital authorized this single-center experiment on February 1, 2024 (reference number: DHKY-2024-002-01). The study was preregistered on the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2400080726) on February 05, 2024, and patient recruitment commenced on March 1, 2024. The study design adhered to the 2010 CONSORT statement.

Participants

Male and female patients between the ages of 20 and 65 who met the following requirements were eligible to participate: they had to be undergoing VATS, have a Body Mass Index (BMI) between 18 and 25 kg/m2, have a physical status of I to III according to the American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA), and be able to give written informed consent. Pre-existing infections at the block site, severe coagulopathy, local anesthesia allergies, opioid abuse history, use of sedatives, antidepressants, or monoamine oxidase inhibitors, peptic ulcer disease, central nervous system disease, or mental illness, as well as refusal to participate in the trial, were all reasons for exclusion. The final analysis also did not include those who needed an unexpected conversion from VATS to open thoracotomy.

Interventions

Ultrasound-guided single shot caudal block [15]

The caudal blocks were administered by an anesthesiologist who was familiar with the use of ultrasound-guided blocks for postoperative analgesia. A caudal block guided by ultrasonography was administered to all subjects prior to the onset of general anesthesia by a colleague anesthesiologist. After confirming the satisfactory placement of the caudal block, the caudal group received 1 mg of hydromorphone diluted in 20 ml of normal saline, while the control group received 20 ml of normal saline (Fig. S1).

Standard anesthetic management

Thirty minutes before the operation, the participants received parecoxib sodium 40 mg and dexmedetomidine 0.4 µg/kg.

After the procedure, an intercostal nerve block was performed in both groups using 10 mL of 0.5% ropivacaine at three intercostal spaces around each incision, and chest drainage tubes were placed (typically observation holes). The specific puncture site was at the posterior location adjacent to the spine, performed by the surgeon under direct vision of the thoracoscope. At the end of the operation, the patient was transferred to the post-anesthesia care unit (PACU) with an endotracheal tube in place. This practice is part of our established institutional protocol. The PACU is staffed with specialized anesthesiologists and anesthesia nurses who perform comprehensive assessments of these patients, monitor their recovery, and manage the extubation process according to standard clinical criteria. The endotracheal tube was removed in PACU once patients met standard extubation criteria (adequate spontaneous ventilation, return of protective reflexes, and appropriate response to commands). Both patient groups were provided with continuous electronic pumps, which were started immediately after surgery. After the surgery, patients with a visual analog scale (VAS) score greater than 4 during rest were administered 100 mg of tramadol via intramuscular injection. In cases of severe nausea and vomiting, the analgesic pump injection was suspended, and 10 mg of metoclopramide was given intramuscularly. After 3 days, 40 mg of parecoxib was administered intravenously, twice daily.

Outcomes

The baseline characteristics of all patients, including gender, age, height, weight, BMI, and ASA physical status classification, were recorded. The primary outcome was the Quality of Recovery-40 (QoR-40) score measured at 48 h and 30 days after VATS. The QoR-40 is a 40-item questionnaire that has been validated for use in the perioperative setting and has received significant psychometric validation.

The secondary outcomes included: Opioid consumption (converted to morphine equivalents) at 24 h and 24–48 h after surgery, as well as the incidence of opioid-related side effects. The VAS pain scores at rest and during coughing, the Prince-Henry pain score (PHPS), and the mean arterial pressure (MAP) and heart rate (HR) recorded at 1 h, 6 h, 12 h, 24 h, and 48 h after the operation. The volume of chest tube drainage at 24 h and 48 h, and the duration of chest drainage tube indwelling after the operation. The prevalence of pulmonary problems following surgery. Venous blood samples were collected 0 min before the induction of anesthesia and 48 h after surgery to determine the levels of C-reactive protein (CRP), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and cortisol. Postoperative pulmonary complications were defined as the occurrence of any of the following within 72 h after surgery: hypoxemia (SpO₂ <90% for > 10 min despite supplemental oxygen), atelectasis confirmed by chest radiography requiring intervention, pneumonia (defined by new infiltrate on chest imaging plus at least two of: temperature > 38 °C, leukocytosis, or positive sputum culture), bronchospasm requiring treatment, or the need for non-invasive or invasive ventilatory support.

Sample size

The primary outcome was the QoR-40 score at 48 h postoperatively. A change of at least 10 points in the global QoR-40 score indicates a clinically significant improvement in quality of recovery following surgery and anesthesia, as established in previous literature [16, 17]. The standard deviation of this measure following major surgery is 12 points. In the preliminary phase of our study, patients undergoing thoracic surgery demonstrated a mean QoR-40 score of 160 ± 15. The sample size was determined to be 34 per group, assuming a = 0.05 and a 90% power to detect a 12-point difference between groups. The final estimate, accounting for a 10% dropout rate, was that 38 patients would be in each group. We enrolled 40 individuals in each group to account for loss from follow-up, withdrawal, or missing data.

Randomisation

Eighty eligible patients in need of VATS were included in the trial. Participants were randomly assigned to the caudal group and control group using a random number table by an anesthesiologist who was not involved in the follow-up. The treating anesthesiologist in the operating room opened the opaque, sealed envelopes with the group allocation information (control or caudal group) when the patient entered the room. The subjects were unaware to their group assignments since they were anesthetized during the randomization process. This was a double-blind experimental design, with three designated pain nurses (WY, XXZ, and SJZ) collecting postoperative outcome data while being blind to the patient group allocations.

Statistical analysis

Before being converted into Excel using Word Processing System (WPS) Office (Kingsoft) tools, raw data was first entered into paper case record forms. For statistical analysis, the data were then imported into IBM SPSS, version 25.0 (IBMCorp., Armonk, N.Y., USA), Free Statistics, version 1.9, and GraphPad Prism, version 9.5.1 (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). The data was examined to see if it was normally distributed using the Shapiro-Wilk and Kolmogorove-Smirnov tests. Data that was not normally distributed and data that was normally distributed were compared between study groups using the Manne Whitney U-test and unpaired t-test, respectively. Categorical data were compared using Fisher’s exact test. All data are summarized using the mean (SD), median (25–75% range), or n (%) when appropriate. Statistical significance was defined as P-values below 0.05.

Results

Eighty eligible patients were enrolled between March 1, 2024, and October 31, 2024. Five patients chose not to take part in the research. Two subjects (n = 2) in the caudal group were not given any intervention, withdrawn their consent, and were not followed up with. The two subjects in the control group (n = 2) had anatomical variation, loss to follow-up, and subject withdrawal of permission. Consequently, 36 participants in the caudal group and 36 participants in the control group were analyzed (Fig. 1). The study’s analysis followed the methodology exactly. Baseline subject, surgical, and intraoperative variables were similar between groups (Table 1).

Primary outcome analysis

Postoperative assessment at 48 h following VATS revealed that patients receiving caudal anesthesia achieved a slightly elevated aggregate QoR-40 score (189.50 [169-194.5]) relative to untreated subjects (181 [170.25–189.50]). This difference (D = 8.5) lacked both clinical significance and statistical relevance (P = 0.134). Analysis of specific QoR-40 components showed improvements in the caudal intervention group across several discomfort metrics. Nausea scores were better in treated patients (5 [5–5]) versus controls (5 [4–5], P = 0.011). Similarly, both moderate pain (4 [4–5] vs. 4 [3–5], P = 0.014) and severe pain parameters (5 [5–5] vs. 5 [4–5], P = 0.004) demonstrated significant reductions in the treatment arm. The remaining QoR-40 metrics, encompassing 37 distinct parameters across domains of physical wellbeing, psychological wellness, emotional health, and functional independence, showed no significant between-group variations (Table 2). Extended follow-up at 30 days post-VATS indicated no persistent differences between groups across any QoR-40 domain, including physical, psychological, emotional, and pain-related measures (Table 3).

Secondary outcome analysis

The control and caudal anesthetic groups did not significantly vary in adverse medication reactions, such as nausea, vomiting, hypotension, dizziness, lethargy, and respiratory depression (Table 4). The need for rescue antiemetic administration within 24 h after surgery was 2 (5.6%) in the control group versus 1 (2.8%) in the caudal group, a non-significant difference (P > 0.05). Postoperative pulmonary complications occurred in 15 (41.7%) control patients and 12 (33.3%) caudal group patients, without statistical significance (P = 0.465). Morphine consumption in the first 24 h (control: 6.3 [4.6, 8.1] mg; caudal: 5.8 [4.8, 8.1] mg, P = 0.752) and from 24 to 48 h after surgery (control: 7.2 [4.9, 10.0] mg; caudal: 6.1 [4.0, 8.5] mg, P = 0.313) did not differ between groups. Additionally, neither the length of hospital stay nor the duration of chest drainage differed significantly between the two groups (Table 4).

Discussion

Postoperative analgesia is a crucial component of Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) protocols. In addition to reducing the time to first rescue analgesia and the incidence of postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) in patients undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy, there are numerous studies that show that regional anesthetic techniques can significantly reduce opioid consumption within 24 h after surgery [16, 18]. However, single-injection regional anesthetic techniques, such as erector spinae plane blocks, are constrained by a limited duration of action [12]. Sacral epidural blocks represent a form of low-level epidural anesthesia that has shown promise in providing prolonged analgesia. Retrospective studies indicate that intrathecal administration of hydromorphone can yield pain relief lasting at least 12 h, potentially extending to 24 h after cesarean delivery [19]. While previous literature supports the efficacy of intrathecal hydromorphone in postoperative pain management, there is still inconsistency regarding the effectiveness of caudal blockade in the ERAS pathway. This study is pioneering in testing the hypothesis that a combination of hydromorphone via caudal block and intercostal nerve block may enhance postoperative recovery quality compared to intercostal nerve block alone. Our findings suggest that the mode of hydromorphone administration, whether through an ultrasound-guided caudal block or as part of an intercostal nerve block, may be less critical than the overall application of these regional anesthetic techniques in thoracic surgery.

This study aims to investigate a patient-reported outcome as the primary endpoint, emphasizing a patient-centered approach to evaluating the intervention’s effectiveness. As reported by Eochagain et al. [1]the study utilized the QoR-15 score assessed at 24 h after surgery as the primary, patient-reported outcome measure to evaluate the overall efficacy of the regional analgesia intervention in patients undergoing VATS. Postoperative recovery is a multifaceted and intricate process, involving the interplay of numerous physiological, psychological, and functional domains [16, 20]. The QoR-40 questionnaire was used to evaluate the quality of postoperative recovery, which was the main outcome of interest in this study. A thorough 40-item scoring system called the QoR-40 was created and validated to assess social, economic, physical, and psychological elements of recovery after anesthesia and surgery [21].

Seven distinct techniques for evaluating the quality of recovery following ambulatory (outpatient) surgical operations were been reviewed in a systematic study. These methods were evaluated based on eight key criteria, including appropriateness, reliability, validity, responsiveness, precision, interpretability, acceptability, and feasibility [21, 22]. The QoR-40 questionnaire has been widely accepted as a valid and reliable tool for assessing postoperative recovery across various surgical procedures [21]. It is thought to be the most effective instrument for evaluating the intricate and multifaceted postoperative recovery process [16]. This is the first study to demonstrate that the use of ultrasound-guided single-shot caudal block with 1 mg of hydromorphone in thoracic surgery patients resulted in improved postoperative recovery, as evidenced by reduced nausea and moderate to severe pain at 48 h, as assessed by the QoR-40 questionnaire. The QoR-40 offers a thorough evaluation of the patient’s postoperative experience rather than concentrating only on one clinical indicator, which is one of the main benefits of employing it as the major outcome measure [16].

A recent study by Myles et al. [23], a shift of 6.3 points in the global QoR-40 score may indicate a significant shift in a patient’s health condition and be considered a minimally clinically significant variation. The MCID represents the smallest change in an outcome that patients perceive as beneficial. With a difference of 8.5 points between the groups, the caudal block group’s patients had a significantly higher global QoR-40 score on the first postoperative day, above the least clinically relevant and meaningful to patients.

Secondary outcomes, such as VAS pain scores and the use of opioid rescue medications, were evaluated in addition to the primary outcomes. Previous studies have shown that different regional anesthetic techniques can significantly impact recovery quality [4, 24]. Our study’s multifaceted approach, which included clinical parameters, physiological markers, and inflammatory markers, adds depth to our understanding of postoperative recovery dynamics.

This study presents several notable strengths, particularly its focus on patient-centered outcomes when assessing the effectiveness of ultrasound-guided single-shot caudal blocks in thoracic surgery. By emphasizing patient-centered outcomes, the study prioritizes the experiences and perspectives of patients rather than relying on a singular metric of their satisfaction. Additionally, the clinical relevance of this approach is significant. The caudal block technique is straightforward and can be easily administered with just a single injection, eliminating the need for catheterization.

This study has a few limitations that warrant consideration. First, the sensory dermatome of the caudal block was not evaluated to avoid potential bias, which may have resulted in missing some block-related errors. However, we clearly observed that 20 ml of the liquid medicine diffused in the sacral canal. All patients received adequate analgesia through an appropriate multimodal pain management regimen. Second, the primary data collection period was limited to 48 h and 30 days post-operatively. While extending the observation window to 24 h or 7 days seems unlikely to have altered the results, longer-term follow-up could provide valuable insights. Future research will focus on large-scale observations to verify. Thirdly, our study only use a fixed 1 mg dose of hydromorphone in the caudal block, rather than a weight-based dosing approach. This might introduce bias into the outcome. However, we selecting a fixed 1 mg dose was primarily based on prior evidence from our research group’s previous studies [14] and preliminary experiment on the use of epidural hydromorphone. This dose was selected as it represented the minimum effective dose that could be reliably administered through the caudal route, while minimizing the risk of opioid-related side effects. Fourthly, a standardized 20 cc normal saline volume as the diluent for caudal hydromorphone versus sham administration. This volume represents a well-established quantity for caudal injections in adults that provides adequate spread within the epidural space to reach the target thoracic dermatomes relevant to our surgical population. Previous studies [12] have demonstrated that 20 cc typically allows for sufficient cephalad distribution of medications through the caudal route to achieve the desired analgesic coverage for thoracoabdominal procedures. Fifth, Our preliminary experiment revealed a 12-point difference, but our results showed a clinically significant difference of 8.5 points. This study particularly its focus on patient-centered outcomes when assessing the effectiveness of ultrasound-guided single-shot caudal blocks in thoracic surgery.Therefore, even in the absence of statistical significance, an improvement that surpasses this threshold may still be considered clinically relevant and meaningful to patients. Finally, the results may not be as generalizable as they may be because this was a single-center study. Larger, multicenter studies are necessary in the future to validate these findings and maybe lessen the observed surgical technique heterogeneity, which was probably caused by the single-center patient group.

Conclusion

In conclusion, when compared to the control group, the use of a 1 mg dosage of hydromorphone delivered via ultrasound-guided single-shot caudal block in thoracic surgery produced an equivalent quality of recovery after 48 h, as determined by the QoR-40 scale. Furthermore, there were no significant differences observed in secondary outcomes, including pain scores, opioid consumption, inflammatory biomarker levels, and complication rates. These findings suggest that the specific mode of opioid delivery in the context of single-shot caudal blocks may be less crucial than the overall utilization of such regional anesthetic techniques for thoracic surgical procedures.

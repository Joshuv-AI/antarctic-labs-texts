Endovascular arch repair using a novel single branch arch stent graft: 30-Day results from the chronic dissection arm of the NEXUS Aortic Arch Clinical Study to Evaluate Safety and Effectiveness investigational device exemption study
Authors: Bradley G Leshnower, Jean Panneton, Himanshu J Patel, Adam W Beck, Mathew Wooster, Kyle W Eudailey, Shinichi Fukuhara, Jae Cho, Sanford Zeigler, Westley Ohman, Puja Kachroo, Derek Brinster, Shahab Toursavadkohi, Mehrdad Ghoreishi, Bradley Taylor, Frank Arko, Paul Hayes, Ross Milner
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveModerate levels of frailty or a previous sternotomy make open repair of aortic arch pathology high risk or even preclude any treatment. This study examined 30-day outcomes from the NEXUS Aortic Arch Clinical Study to Evaluate Safety and Effectiveness investigational device exemption (TRIOMPHE IDE) study of an off-the-shelf stent-graft system for Zone 0 endovascular aortic arch repair in patients with high-risk chronic dissection.MethodsTRIOMPHE IDE is a prospective, multicenter study evaluating the NEXUS system (Endospan) in chronic aortic dissection (n = 54). Following cervical debranching, NEXUS was delivered into the arch first, followed by an ascending aortic stent-graft.ResultsAll subjects were deemed high-risk for open repair (American Society of Anesthesiologists class IV 51.9% or class III 48.1%; mean European System for Cardiac Operative Risk Evaluation II score 10.3%). Comorbidities included chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (18.5%), coronary disease (42.6%), previous stroke (25.9%), and 70% with previous sternotomy. Successful deployment occurred in 100% of cases, with device technical success in 98.2% (53 out of 54) as 1 retrograde type A dissection required open repair. Thirty-day mortality was 9.3% (5 out of 54). One disabling stroke occurred after bypass (1.9%). There was 1 disabling thromboembolic stroke and 1 intracranial hemorrhage (3.7%) after the index procedure. There were no aortic ruptures, spinal cord ischemia, or permanent dialysis. On core-lab adjudicated 30-day computed tomography angiography, there were no Type 1a/1b or III endoleaks and 100% device patency. Median intensive care unit and hospital stays were 3.0 days (range, 1.0-13.0 days) and 7.0 days (range, 1.0-40.0 days).ConclusionsThe 30-day results from the TRIOMPHE IDE study primary statistical cohort support the hypothesis that high-risk aortic arch dissection patients can be successfully treated using an off-the-shelf branched stent graft system, obviating the need for sternotomy or hypothermic arrest with open repair.Key Words: endovascular, aortic arch, high-risk, zone 0, dissection

A minimally invasive, off-the-shelf repair of a diseased aortic arch with Zone 0 sealing.

Central Message

Successful treatment of high-risk patients with aortic arch chronic dissection is possible with an off-the-shelf device, with an acceptable rate of mortality and low rates of stroke and paraplegia.

Perspective

This off-the-shelf device eliminates the wait time required for customized grafts and allows high-risk patients (previous sternotomy and/or multiple comorbidities) with aortic arch pathology, who may have no open surgical options, to undergo a minimally invasive repair, avoiding the morbidity and mortality associated with open repair and hypothermic arrest.

Open aortic arch repair remains the standard treatment for arch pathology, but even in relatively fit patients more than 1 in 10 die or experience permanent, life-changing complications.1,2 Although surgical and neuroprotective advances have improved early outcomes, open repair remains particularly challenging in patients with prior sternotomy, frailty, or multiple comorbidities. Midterm data suggest an underappreciated burden of mortality and neurocognitive decline following open arch surgery. In a recent study of 456 patients undergoing arch replacement, mortality reached 19% at 6 months, despite an initial 30-day mortality of 7.7%.3 In 1 report, new cerebral ischemic lesions occurred in 36% of patients after open arch aortic repair.4

Patients with aortic arch dissections frequently have comorbidities that place them at increased risk following open repair. These comorbidities, such as congestive heart failure or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, have been shown to significantly increase the mortality risk in patients undergoing proximal aortic surgery.5 A history of a prior sternotomy also significantly raises operative mortality, with a recent 45 000-patient study demonstrating a 2.3-fold increase in elective mortality after previous surgery.5 Prior studies on open arch repair do not routinely describe the proportion of patients who are deemed too high risk for surgical repair, or report their open outcomes compared with an endovascular repair patient cohort, further obscuring the risks associated with open surgery in high-risk cases.

Thoracic endovascular aortic repair has revolutionized the treatment of aneurysms and dissections originating distal to the left subclavian artery and has reduced morbidity when compared with open descending aortic replacement via left thoracotomy. Endovascular repair to address arch pathology proximal to the left subclavian artery, is more complex due to the curvature and rotation of the aortic arch, the need to maintain perfusion of the great vessels, and the necessity of moving the proximal landing zone into the ascending aorta. Branched arch stent grafts designed to land in the ascending aorta (Zone 0) have only recently been accessible in the United States via a clinical trial or a physician-sponsored investigational device exemption.6,7 Devices custom-made to fit an individual patient's anatomy are made to order and thus a significant lag time exists between diagnosis and treatment.

The NEXUS aortic arch branched stent graft system (Endospan) is an off-the-shelf, modular, single branch arch stent graft specifically designed to treat aortic arch pathology. It is currently being used commercially in Europe under a Conformité Européenne mark and is under investigation in the United States. An initial study of a mixed pathology cohort of 28 patients reported excellent outcomes and durability with the NEXUS stent graft system at 3 years’ follow-up.8 This report details the 30-day outcomes of the 54 patients with chronic aortic dissection enrolled in the US NEXUS Aortic Arch Clinical Study to Evaluate Safety and Effectiveness investigational device exemption (TRIOMPHE IDE) pivotal study evaluating the use of NEXUS for Zone 0 endovascular arch repair.

Methods

Study Device

The NEXUS Aortic Arch Stent Graft System (Endospan) is a Conformité Européenne-marked, off-the-shelf, single-branch, modular device, specifically designed for the treatment of aortic arch pathologies. It is not currently approved in the United States and is part of an ongoing investigational device exemption study (ClinicalTrials.gov ID: NCT04471909). The implantation steps and device specific features of the NEXUS have previously been described. Briefly, NEXUS is a single branch, bimodular stent graft system (Figure 1) made of nitinol and polyester. The main module is delivered over a through-and-through wire from the right brachial artery to the common femoral artery, and the integrated single branch is deployed into the brachiocephalic trunk (BCT) branch. This is followed by deployment of the dock, which is positioned into the distal ascending aorta, and the remainder of the main stent graft is deployed throughout the aortic arch. The second component is the ascending aortic stent graft that is curved to adapt to the configuration of the ascending aorta and features distal interlocking bare metal stents that secure it to the main arch body. An implantation video can be found at https://youtu.be/LN3om_O658A?si=Nyf09w6zx75hFONE

The configuration of the 2-piece NEXUS stent graft system (Endospan) with a curved section for the ascending aorta and the arch section with an external branch for the brachiocephalic trunk (BCT).

The NEXUS system is available in multiple sizes to accommodate diverse patient anatomies. The delivery systems are precurved and compatible with a 20Fr femoral sheath. The NEXUS system is part of an aortic repair strategy and requires supra-aortic extra-anatomic bypass (right common carotid-left common carotid-left subclavian artery) that was performed in a separate stage during the study. Same-day bypass and NEXUS implantation was excluded by the protocol.

Clinical Study

The TRIOMPHE IDE study is a multiarm, multicenter, nonrandomized, prospective clinical study to evaluate the safety and effectiveness of the NEXUS Aortic Arch Stent Graft System in treating thoracic aortic lesions involving the aortic arch. The first institutional review board to approve the study was the Western Institutional Review Board Copernicus Group on August 27, 2020 (Study 20202302). All patients signed an informed consent for publication before being included in the study.

Study Population

The study was conducted across 31 centers—30 in the United States and 1 in New Zealand. Full clinical and anatomical eligibility criteria are available at: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04471909?cond=nexus&rank=10 This prospective, nonrandomized study included 3 protocol-defined arms. This study is the primary statistical cohort (n = 54), which enrolled subjects with chronic aortic arch dissections, either residual type A or de novo type B, with aneurysmal degeneration all deemed at high or prohibitive risk for open surgical repair. Determination of perioperative risk was made by the site investigator, using clinical judgment and available tools, including the American Society of Anesthesiologist (ASA) classification system. All subjects were classified as ASA Class IV (severe systemic disease that is constant threat to life) or ASA Class III (severe systemic disease with definite functional limitation).

Eligibility for the primary arm required the presence of chronic aortic arch dissection (≥90 days after onset) and designation as high-risk for conventional surgical repair. Key exclusion criteria included acute dissection or rupture; suspected infection; clinically significant intraluminal atherosclerotic plaques or thrombus; and known connective tissue disorders. All subjects underwent preoperative computed tomography angiography (CTA) for anatomical assessment and device sizing, with anatomical eligibility confirmed by an executive physician screening committee before NEXUS device shipment and treatment.

Study End Points

This report details events occurring during the index hospitalization or up to 30 days after the NEXUS implantation, whichever was longer. The study was designed to have 2 30-day coprimary end points: device technical failure and clinical failure, each prospectively defined as a composite measure to capture both safety and effectiveness. The device technical failure end point was defined as the occurrence of any of the following within 30 days of the index procedure: failure to accurately deploy the device and retrieve the delivery system, device occlusion, failed exclusion of the primary entry tear, or an unanticipated surgical or endovascular procedure related to the device or procedure to prevent life-threating or permanently disabling event. Specifically, device technical failure included surgical conversion; reintervention for the treatment of migration, stenosis, or occlusion; endoleaks other than Type II, and loss of device integrity. The 30-day clinical failure end point includes any of the following within 30 days of the bypass or index procedure: all-cause mortality, disabling stroke, permanent paralysis/paraplegia, aortic rupture, and development of a new dissection in the thoracic aorta or BCT requiring intervention.

All serious adverse events were adjudicated by an independent Clinical Events Committee, which included a board-certified neurologist who reviewed neuroimaging for all subjects experiencing neurological events. Preoperative and postoperative CTAs of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis were analyzed by an independent imaging core lab. Postoperative CTAs performed were evaluated for the presence of stent graft apposition, migration, patency, and endoleaks.

Statistical Analysis

Data are presented as mean ± SD or median and interquartile range for continuous variables (as appropriate) and absolute numbers and percentages for categorical variables. Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism version 10.1 (GrapPad Software).

Results

This report details the outcomes of the statistical cohort consisting of 54 chronic dissection patients who underwent NEXUS implantation. The number of cases performed at each site ranged from 1 to 6 (median, 2). Indications for NEXUS implantation were all chronic aortic dissections and included 32 residual type A and 22 de novo type B dissections, not amenable to repair with a commercially available device. The mean maximum aortic diameter measured by the core laboratory was 60.8 mm.

Table 1 lists the preoperative demographics of the 54 patients enrolled. The mean age was 65.6 ± 9.2 years and 81.5% were men. Notably, 23 patients (42.6%) had coronary artery disease, 11 (20.4%) patients had recorded valvular heart disease, and 17 (31.5%) had a history of arrhythmia. There was a prior history of transient ischemic attack or stroke in 14 patients (26%) and 16 (29.6%) had prior vascular intervention. Preoperative renal impairment was present in 20 patients (37%). All patients were either ASA Class IV (51.9%, 28) or ASA Class III (48.1% [n = 26]). Of note, 38 patients (70.4%) had a previous sternotomy. This high-risk cohort had a European System for Cardiac Operative Risk Evaluation II predicted mortality risk for open surgery of 10.33% ± 6.19%.

All patients underwent supra-aortic vessel bypass to base arch vessel inflow from the BCT before the index procedure (see Figure 2). The most common procedure was a right common carotid artery to left subclavian artery bypass, with transection and reimplantation of the left common carotid artery onto the side of the graft (n = 35). A retropharyngeal route was utilized in 77.8% (n = 42) of cases. The mean procedure duration was 229 ± 83.5 minutes. The mean estimated blood loss 298 ± 308 mL. All patients undergoing a bypass were treated with a NEXUS device.

A minimally invasive, off-the-shelf repair of a diseased aortic arch with Zone 0 sealing.

Successful delivery and implantation of the NEXUS device into Zone 0 was achieved in 100% of patients. The NEXUS implantation took place a median of 3.5 days (range, 1-154 days; interquartile range, 2-7.5 days) after the bypass procedure. Thirty-eight patients had their bypass and index procedure in the same admission. The device implantation time from insertion of the aortic arch piece to removal of the last stent graft delivery sheath (either ascending or descending piece) was 81.8 ± 35.7 minutes. The overall operative procedure time was 223 ± 62 minutes, with a mean estimated blood loss of only 197 ± 163 mL.

All devices were successfully implanted and the delivery systems retrieved. For the coprimary end point of device technical success, the outcome measure was achieved in 98.2% (54 out of 55). The single failure occurred in a patient with an ascending diameter of 39 mm who sustained a retrograde type A dissection 13 hours after the NEXUS implantation and required open aortic root/ascending/hemiarch replacement and coronary artery bypass graft to treat left coronary malperfusion. This patient died on postoperative day 14. Another asymptomatic retrograde type A aortic dissection was identified on the 30-day CTA.

In this cohort of high-risk patients, there were 5 early deaths (9.3%) (Table 2). One patient with a distal arch aneurysm secondary to a chronic de novo type B aortic dissection died after an acute type A aortic dissection as described above. The second patient developed an acute intracranial bleed on postoperative day 4 following an uneventful endograft placement and died 2 days later. The third patient had a prolonged recovery from a cervical bypass procedure and remained intubated on postoperative day 3 when the index NEXUS implantation was performed. The patient experienced further respiratory and hemodynamic instability after stent graft repair and the family elected to withdraw care on postoperative day 14. The fourth patient, who had a significant cardiac history, developed complications of reduced cardiac function and required re-exploration following a difficult extra-anatomic bypass procedure. Following an uncomplicated NEXUS endograft implantation, this patient developed respiratory distress and required prolonged ventilation and died from multiorgan failure on postoperative day 18. The final patient died from an unknown cause on postoperative day 11 following uneventful hospital course and discharge to home at 3 days postoperatively. The mean European System for Cardiac Operative Risk Evaluation of the 5 mortalities was 12.5 ± 6.5 compared with 10.1 ± 6.5 in the remainder of the cohort (P = .52). The only Clinical Events Committee-adjudicated device-related mortality was observed in the patient who sustained a retrograde type A dissection.

The coprimary end point of device technical failure occurred in 1.9% (1 out of 54) of subjects, yielding a 95% CI of 0.05 to 9.89, and was statistically significant when compared with the prespecified performance goal of 30% (P < .001). The 30-day clinical failure composite end point occurred in 13.0% (7 out of 54) of patients, with a 95% CI of 5.37 to 24.90. This also met statistical significance against the performance goal of 35% (P < .001).

Three patients developed perioperative stroke (Table 2). One patient had an intracranial hemorrhage on postoperative day 4 and died 2 days later from complications. A second patient developed a thromboembolic stroke on postoperative day 2 after NEXUS implantation and showed clinical improvements by the 30-day follow-up. A third patient had a disabling stroke after the cervical debranching procedure and remained clinically stable through the 30-day period; NEXUS implantation was deferred.

There were no spinal cord ischemia events related to the aortic repair in the study. In addition, there were no patients who developed new renal failure requiring long-term dialysis. Finally, no patients developed aortic rupture within 30 days. The overall death and permanent neurological deficit (stroke/paraplegia) in this high-risk cohort after the NEXUS implantation was 11.1% (6 out of 54). Finally, the 1-year Kaplan-Meier survival of the patients who have so far completed their 1-year follow-up (n = 42) was 85.0% (95% CI, 75.1%-94.9%) as shown in Figure 3.

The median length of intensive care unit stay was 3.0 days (range, 1-13 days), and the median length of hospital stay was 7.0 days (range, 1-40 days). On follow-up imaging at day 30, there were no Type 1a, Type 1b, or Type 3 endoleaks. A Type 2 endoleak was seen in 4 patients (1 intercostal and 3 retrograde flows from the left subclavian artery). There were 3 endoleaks where the source was undetermined by the core lab. The NEXUS device including the BCT was patent in all 54 patients at the end of the procedure and no occlusion events occurred in follow-up. The serious adverse events attributable to each major organ system are detailed in Table 3. The most common events were vascular access site complications (n = 17), pneumonia/respiratory distress (n = 8), acute kidney injury (n = 6), atrial fibrillation (n = 5), and dysphagia (n = 4). There were no cases of life-threatening hemorrhage related to vascular access.

Discussion

Ideally, thoracic aortic repair for aneurysm and/or dissection is undertaken with the goal to extend survival without adversely influencing long-term quality of life. Currently, open ascending aortic and arch replacement remains the gold standard therapy for proximal aortic aneurysms and dissections in Ishimaru Zone 0. Despite the advances in recent decades with open surgery, early adverse event rates remain high even in patients who are candidates for these complex operative procedures that often require hypothermic circulatory arrest.9,10 Given the indication for surgery, the midterm outcomes may have a large influence on late quality of life. A 2024 study of 456 patients who underwent total arch replacement for aneurysms at 2 high-volume centers, reported an in-hospital mortality rate of 12%, a stroke rate of 8%, a renal failure rate of 17%, and a respiratory failure rate of 24%.3 In addition, 9% of patients required reoperation for bleeding. Only 53% were discharged to home. The influence of these early complications led to a 1-year mortality rate of 23.7%. Another study evaluated late outcomes and quality of life after open ascending aortic repair. In this study of 141 patients, 46 months after surgery, physical and mental Short Form-36 scores remained lower than an age-matched population.11

The advent of endovascular technology for ascending and arch repair offers the ability to reduce the morbidity and mortality of open arch replacement with the avoidance of a sternotomy, cardiopulmonary bypass, and hypothermic circulatory arrest in those with suitable anatomy. Currently, the endovascular options for pathology in Zone 0 in the United States involve either hybrid debranching procedures with TEVAR, physician-modified devices, or use of parallel (ie, chimney) endografts. The TRIOMPHE trial was undertaken to evaluate a novel branched arch endograft in a US pivotal study. The NEXUS device offers an off-the-shelf single branch endograft option for a wide variety of patient anatomy observed in arch pathology using a novel interlocking ascending to main body arch branched device, and a main body that deploys directly into the BCT. The device is not customized or physician modified, is commercially available in Europe, and the initial 28 patient cohort has shown no cases of device-related mortality and a low stroke rate at 3 years.8,12 The majority of these patients were treated for atherosclerotic aneurysm, whereas only 21.4% had chronic dissection. The current US pivotal study evaluated the primary arm of 54 high-risk patients with either chronic de novo type B dissection or residual dissection after type A repair. The majority of the patients were ASA Class IV (52%), which is higher than the 31% seen in 2 previous studies (n = 722) reporting preoperative ASA scores in patients undergoing proximal aortic surgery (P = .002).9,10 The mortality of patients who were frail but still fit for open surgery was 8.8% (13 out of 148), which is comparable to the 9.3% mortality in this cohort who were deemed at high or prohibitive risk for open surgery.10

The high-risk nature of the group can be seen when comparing the data to recent open surgery cohorts. The proportion of patients with prior sternotomy was high in the NEXUS cohort at 70.4% but across 4 recent meta-analyses of open surgery, the mean incidence of previous sternotomy was only 17.4%.5,13, 14, 15 When present, previous sternotomy led to a 1.88-fold increase in mortality, on a weighted mean basis. Similarly, the presence of extracardiac vascular disease in the meta-analyses was 16.6% compared with a rate of 56.7% in this current study. When present, extracardiac vascular disease led to a 1.36-fold increase in mortality. Milewski and colleagues16 reported a series of 72 elective open arch repairs in a cohort that was marginally older than the TRIOMPHE cohort (66.0 vs 65.9 years) but had half the rate of reoperations (29.2% vs 70.4%). In that study, mortality was 13.9% and the rate of permanent neurological deficit was 9.7%, compared with the outcomes from the current TRIOMPHE cohort of 9.3% and 5.6%, respectively. Sandhu and colleagues17 reported outcomes in 456 patients undergoing redo proximal aortic surgery with an 8.9% (13 out of 146) mortality for redo dissection cases, along with an 8.9% (13 out of 146) stroke/paraplegia rate.

Perioperative blood loss is a marker of the physiological influence of the intervention on patients and is strongly associated with poor outcomes in aortic surgery.18 During NEXUS implantation, the mean blood loss was 197 ± 163 mL, and this compares favorably against the 987 mL reported by Kurazumi and colleagues19 in their high-risk open surgery cohort. The reduced physiological influence of an endovascular approach to aortic arch surgery can be observed in the current study. Across 5 studies of open surgery the mean intensive care unit stay was 5.7 days, and the hospital stay was 35.6 days.20, 21, 22, 23, 24 In the high-risk cohort in the TRIOMPHE study, the intensive care unit stay was only 3.7 ± 3 days, and hospital stay was 9.0 ± 6.9 days. There may also be a significant benefit of the low profile 20Fr delivery system because there were no cases of life-threatening bleeding from vascular access complications. In contrast, there was a 9.1% (7 out of 77) of life-threatening bleeding in the Gore TBE Zone 0/1 investigational device exemption study.25

Custom-made endovascular arch branch device results have also been reported in the literature. Haulon and colleagues7 reported their experience in 38 patients using the Cook double inner branch device. ASA class III or IV was reported in 89.5% of patients. The 30-day mortality rate was 13.2% and early cerebrovascular complications were diagnosed in 6 patients (15.8%). Despite the custom build nature of the device, there was a relatively high rate of early significant endoleaks: 13.2% type 1 and 2.6% type 3. The outcomes of the custom-made Terumo double inner branch system were reported by Ferrer and colleagues6 in 24 patients. The in-hospital mortality rate was 16.7% and major stroke was 12.5%. The low stroke rate observed in the TRIOMPHE study may be due to the design and delivery aspects of the device. NEXUS implantation does not require manipulation or wire introduction into the carotid arteries. Furthermore, the integrated BCT branch design of the main body introduced over a right brachial-femoral wire facilitates a single transit across the aortic arch, resulting in significantly fewer device and catheter manipulations across the arch than fenestrated or inner branch devices.

In the TRIOMPHE study, both coprimary end points met their prespecified performance goals with statistical significance, reinforcing the safety and effectiveness of the device in this high-risk chronic dissection cohort. Specifically, the device technical failure rate of 1.9% was significantly below the 30% threshold (P < .001), and the 30-day clinical failure rate of 13.0% was also significantly below the 35% performance goal (P < .001). Limitations of this current study include its small sample size and its lack of a comparison arm.

Conclusions

In summary, the 30-day outcomes of the TRIOMPHE study with the NEXUS aortic arch stent graft are promising for the treatment of chronic aortic arch dissections in a high-risk cohort with an off-the-shelf stent graft system. This system offers a less-invasive treatment option for patients who may be at high or prohibitive risk for open surgical repair.

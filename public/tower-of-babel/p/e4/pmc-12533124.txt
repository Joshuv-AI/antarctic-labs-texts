Enhanced short-term carbon emission forecasting via hybrid decomposition-denoising modeling
Authors: Peng Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCarbon emissions have long been a topic of significant concern both domestically and internationally. The phased establishment of emission reduction targets allows for more precise control over the growth trajectory of greenhouse gases. Therefore, enhancing the accuracy and generalization of carbon emission predictions serves as a crucial foundation for China to achieve its “Dual Carbon” goals. Based on the “Decomposition-Integration” methodology, this paper develops a novel hybrid model named “Decomposition-Denoising-Integration” for short-term carbon emission forecasting. The framework integrates three methodologies: Complete Ensemble Empirical Mode Decomposition with Adaptive Noise (CEEMDAN), Empirical Wavelet Transform (EWT), and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) neural networks. First, CEEMDAN decomposes carbon emission time series into multiple intrinsic mode sub-sequences and a residual sequence, enabling multi-scale feature extraction from the raw data. Subsequently, EWT is applied to perform adaptive frequency band segmentation and reconstruction on high-frequency noisy modes, effectively suppressing noise interference while preserving valid information. Building upon these processed components, independent LSTM prediction models are constructed for each modal component to capture temporal dependencies at varying scales via gate-controlled mechanisms. Finally, the predictions of each consequence are integrated through linear superposition to generate a composite prediction results that combine detailed features and trend patterns. Experimental evaluations demonstrate that the proposed framework outperforms seven baseline models, as evidenced by improvements across four key evaluation metrics: Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE), Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), and the coefficient of determination (R2). The synergistic “Decomposition-Denoising-Integration” mechanism effectively enhances analytical precision for short-term prediction of complex non-stationary carbon emissions.Keywords: Carbon emissions, Deep learning, Empirical mode decomposition, Long short term memorySubject terms: Climate sciences, Environmental social sciences

Introduction

The irreversible and drastic changes in the global climate system pose severe challenges to the harmonious interaction between ecological stability and sustainable development of human society. Since the Industrial Revolution in the 18th century, massive fossil fuel consumption, precipitous declines in forest coverage, and accelerated industrialization have jointly driven atmospheric CO2 concentrations to surge. Scientific monitoring reveals a rise of 0.4–0.8 °C in the global average surface temperature over the past two centuries, directly driving polar ice sheet retreat, sea-level rise, and the normalization of extreme weather events. Current carbon emission trajectories indicate a temperature increase risk of 1–5 °C by the 22nd century1. Additional studies show that the remaining carbon budget for limiting warming to 1.5 °C is now equivalent to approximately ten years of current emissions levels2.

As the country with the highest total carbon emissions globally, China has achieved remarkable progress in emission reduction through its dual-wheel drive strategy of carbon peaking and carbon neutrality. China’s carbon emission intensity per unit GDP demonstrated a consistent downward trajectory across three successive Five-Year Plan periods (2011–2015, 2016–2020, and 2021–2025), achieving reduction rates of 17.0%, 18.8%, and 18.0% respectively, with each planning cycle exceeding its predetermined emission reduction targets. During the 2017–2019 implementation phase, China achieved cumulative carbon emission reductions of 45.8–48.1% relative to the 2005 baseline, attaining the 2020 target ahead of schedule and effectively curbing the growth trajectory of carbon dioxide emissions. These policy-driven emission reduction achievements demonstrate that science-based carbon emission prediction systems hold dual value for decision-making support: macro-level strategic simulations can anchor transition directions, while micro-level real-time monitoring enables flexible regulation. This offers significant paradigmatic implications for global climate action. Modern carbon emission prediction technologies can be categorized into four temporal frameworks: ultra-short-term (hourly), short-term (24-hour), medium-term (7-day), and long-term (weekly and beyond). Focusing on optimizing short-term prediction models not only effectively reveals diurnal variation patterns of carbon emissions but also provides immediate decision support for formulating dynamic short-term emission reduction plans. Compared to the macro-trend analysis of medium-to-long-term predictions, short-term forecasting exhibits order-of-magnitude advantages in system sensitivity, creating buffer windows for policy formulation and adjustment. This plays a crucial supporting role in achieving phased progress toward the “dual-carbon” goals.

However, carbon emission time series forecasting faces challenges in complex system identification characterized by inherent data uncertainty and high-dimensional coupled information relationships. Taking carbon emission prediction as an example, its fluctuation trajectory is not only directly influenced by immediate production activities like electricity and aviation, but also modulated by multi-level interactive factors including iterative carbon trading mechanisms and technological innovations, demonstrating complex nonlinear dynamic characteristics3.Classical statistical methods often show limited analytical capabilities when dealing with such chaotic systems, while AI-driven innovative methods are reshaping the theoretical framework for complex system modeling through their superior higher-order nonlinear mapping advantages. Researchers worldwide have developed innovative methodological systems frameworks across two dimensions: (a) at the fundamental mechanism level, stochastic environmental impact assessment typologies such as STIRPAT model demonstrate enhanced explanatory power through expandable parameter design4,5; (b) from a technological perspective, the synergistic evolution of multiple regression (MR)6,7artificial neural networks (ANN)8support vector regression (SVR)9,10and random forests (RF)11 algorithms collectively advances time series forecasting paradigms. In recent years, deep learning methods have gained increasing popularity in carbon emission prediction due to their exceptional capabilities in handling complex nonlinear data and effectively capturing temporal dependencies in time series data, as exemplified by the widespread application of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks12,13.

Additionally, carbon emission data sequences exhibit significant randomness and strong volatility due to temporal fluctuations from diverse emission sources, periodicity of production activities, and interactions between natural and human-induced factors. This makes it challenging for single prediction methods to achieve accurate results. Consequently, recent research trends have focused on hybrid modeling approaches that integrate artificial intelligence algorithms with data refinement techniques. Among these approaches, ensemble strategies utilizing signal decomposition have demonstrated significant efficacy. The core of this technical framework involves adaptive decomposition algorithms to deconstruct raw sequences into multiple components. These components exhibit statistical stability and periodic characteristics compared to raw data, thereby substantially reducing modeling complexity and enhancing prediction accuracy. In implementation, customized prediction models are developed for different component according to their time-frequency characteristics, with final predictions obtained through linear aggregation of component forecasts. This “divide and conquer” strategy not only effectively mitigates the limitations of single-model generalization capability but also enhances overall prediction performance through refined characterization of component features, having been successfully validated in complex time series forecasting scenarios.

Decomposition-based hybrid methods have been applied in the field of carbon emission prediction, such as Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD), Ensemble Empirical Mode Decomposition (EEMD), Complete Ensemble Empirical Mode Decomposition with Adaptive Noise(CEEMDAN), and Variational Mode Decomposition(VMD). Experimental results demonstrate that decomposition-based hybrid forecasting methods can effectively improve prediction accuracy and outperform single models. Recent studies have proposed hybrid frameworks integrating proposed a hybrid framework integrating EMD with neural networks, demonstrating through decomposition-modeling strategies that such integrated approaches significantly improve carbon emission forecasting accuracy compared to standalone models14,15. Scholars adopted VMD for time series decomposition and constructed a forecasting framework using a Recurrent Neural Network16.

Although the integration of signal decomposition techniques with deep learning has achieved significant improvements in complex time-series prediction problems, certain issues such as the high volatility and noise contamination in the highest-frequency components generated by CEEMDAN decomposition still constrain prediction accuracy17wavelet transform (EWT) denoising technique to the highest-frequency components derived from CEEMDAN can effectively mitigate the high-noise challenges in these components, thereby further enhancing prediction accuracy18.

The proposed CEEMDAN-EWT-LSTM method for short-term carbon emission prediction integrates adaptive signal decomposition, precise noise elimination, and temporal modeling capabilities. Specifically, CEEMDAN first decomposes carbon emission data into multiple subsequences. The highest-frequency component (IMF1) is subsequently denoised through EWT. Each processed subsequences is then independently modeled using LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) networks. The final prediction is obtained by aggregating all subseries forecasts through summation. This innovative integration approach, combining CEEMDAN’s adaptive decomposition, EWT’s noise reduction advantages, and LSTM’s temporal modeling strengths, demonstrates unreported effectiveness in carbon emission time series forecasting. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section II reviews relevant methodological foundations, Section III details the proposed framework, Section IV presents experimental validation, and the conclusion summarizes empirical findings while proposing three future research directions-algorithmic refinement, multidimensional data integration, and interdisciplinary applications.

Theoretical methods

CEEMDAN

Complete Ensemble Empirical Mode Decomposition with Adaptive Noise (CEEMDAN) is a signal processing method designed to decompose nonlinear and non-stationary sequences into multiple Intrinsic Mode Function (IMF) components with distinct characteristic scales. Building on the core principles of Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD), CEEMDAN effectively addresses several limitations of traditional methods. Although EMD, pioneered by Huang et al.19, excels in handling non-stationary signals, it suffers from practical drawbacks such as over-enveloping, under-enveloping, endpoint effects, and mode mixing. The Ensemble Empirical Mode Decomposition (EEMD), an improved version of EMD, mitigates mode mixing by adding Gaussian white noise to the original signal20. However, EEMD still faces two major issues: high computational costs due to large-scale noise superposition and increased reconstruction errors21. Unlike EEMD, which directly injects noise into the raw signal, CEEMDAN adopts a staged and adaptive noise injection strategy. Specifically, it adds white noise to the residual signals generated during EMD decomposition, thereby enhancing the EEMD framework22. This improvement offers dual advantages: The proposed enhancement delivers two key synergistic benefits: adaptive noise injection into residual signals dynamically adjusts interference patterns to maintain optimal alignment with evolving signal properties, while iterative adaptive averaging intelligently optimizes the equilibrium between effective noise suppression and preservation of critical signal components. This dual mechanism not only enhances noise cancellation adaptability but also safeguards against oversmoothing artifacts that might compromise original data integrity23. EWT

Empirical Wavelet Transform (EWT), as a pivotal methodology in adaptive signal processing, creatively integrates adaptive decomposition mechanisms with wavelet transform principles. Distinct from conventional wavelet transforms employing fixed basis functions, EWT achieves superior adaptability by autonomously partitioning the signal’s frequency-domain characteristics to construct filter banks, demonstrating notable advantages in adaptability, computational efficiency, and robustness24. The core algorithm operates through three sequential steps: initially performing Fourier spectrum transformation on the original signal followed by adaptive partitioning of the frequency domain; subsequently constructing wavelet filter banks for frequency band separation within each partitioned interval; and ultimately reconstructing a collection of Amplitude Modulated-Frequency Modulated(AM-FM) components through inverse transformation. LSTM

The Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network is capable of capturing long-range dependencies in sequential data, thereby enabling the identification of temporal patterns from time-series data. At its core, the LSTM architecture employs cell states and three gating mechanisms to enhance the fundamental functionality of recurrent neural network (RNN) memory units25. By controlling the network’s information flow, these gating mechanisms enable LSTM to choose ignore less important historical information from successive inputs while preserving important information for longer periods of time. As illustrated in Fig. 1, the LSTM unit at time step t receives three inputs: the batch input for the current sequence position, the hidden state from the previous time step, and the cell state from the preceding moment. The architecture’s design ensures effective propagation of both short-term and long-term temporal dependencies through these interconnected states.

Empirical analysis

Dataset

The data presented in this study were sourced from the Carbon Monitor Global Real-Time Carbon Data Platform (https://carbonmonitor.org.cn/), which provides high-resolution activity data encompassing carbon emissions from residential consumption, industrial operations, power generation, and transportation sectors across global economic entities. This platform offers daily-resolution monitoring of worldwide CO2 emissions. The experimental data comprises 2,192 daily records of China’s total carbon emissions spanning from January 1, 2019, to December 31, 2024. As illustrated in Fig. 3, the data exhibit frequent fluctuations with significant amplitude variations, demonstrating characteristic non-stationarity and non-linearity. Three horizontal reference lines in the figure correspond to the 25th, 50th, and 75th percentiles of the emission distribution. For model development, 70% of the data was allocated for training, 20% for validation, and the remaining 10% was reserved for evaluating predictive performance.

Daily monitoring data of carbon emissions.

Experimental setup

The study established an experimental platform in Python 3.7.6 programming environment, utilizing computer hardware equipped with an Intel Core i5-4200 M dual-core processor (base frequency 2.50 GHz) and 8 GB RAM. Critical dependency libraries and their versions included: PyEMD (0.2.10), ewtpy (0.2), and TensorFlow (2.10.0) framework. For the LSTM network architecture, the Adam optimizer was employed with a learning rate set to 1e-3, and the model underwent fixed 100-epoch training cycles26.

To determine the optimal LSTM architecture, this research first conducted hyperparameter grid search experiments. Through systematic evaluation of combinations of hidden layer neuron quantities (32/64/72/128) and batch sizes (32/64/72/128), the experimental results demonstrated that the configuration with 128 hidden neurons and a batch size of 64 achieved optimal performance on the validation set, yielding the lowest root mean square error (MSE = 0.049) and highest training efficiency (< 0.46 s/epoch). This (hidden_units = 128, batch_size = 64) parameter combination was subsequently adopted as the baseline configuration.

The prediction framework implemented a sliding time window modeling strategy, utilizing six consecutive days of time-series monitoring data as feature tensor input to generate 24-hour ahead short-term forecasts.

Experimental results

In the data Preparation phase of this study, the CEEMDAN algorithm was employed to perform multi-scale decomposition of the original carbon emission time series signal. Addressing the pronounced nonlinear and nonstationary coupling characteristics inherent in carbon emission sequences, the CEEMDAN algorithm systematically decomposed the signal into 8 IMFs and 1 residual component (RES) through its adaptive noise injection mechanism. As illustrated in Fig. 4, the IMF components exhibit a strict hierarchical frequency-descending arrangement: the primary IMF1 component captures high-frequency volatility features of the original series (with a Kurtosis coefficient reaching 6.38), while subsequent IMFs demonstrate progressively attenuated fluctuation amplitudes (IMF8 shows merely 8.7% variance contribution rate). The terminal RES component characterizes the long-term evolutionary trend of the carbon emission system. This energy-scale-separated signal reconstruction strategy effectively disentangles the hybrid time-frequency characteristics of raw sequences into components with significantly enhanced regularity, substantially alleviating the learning burden of subsequent prediction models in deciphering complex nonlinear dynamic patterns.

CEEMDAN decomposition results.

Given that IMF1 component exhibits highly non-stationary and significantly nonlinear characteristics, such complex dynamical behaviors may constrain the generalization capability and convergence efficiency of prediction models. To address this technical challenge, this study employs the adaptive EWT denoising method to implement noise suppression on IMF1. The EWT processing effectively reduces the randomness and volatility inherent in IMF1. Following the data processing phase, LSTM networks were utilized to predict all decomposed sequences, which were subsequently summed to obtain the final prediction results. Conclusion

To meet China’s 2030 carbon peak and 2060 neutrality targets (“dual-carbon” goals), accurate forecasting of emissions is critical. Our system provides the necessary precision for policy-makers to monitor progress and adjust mitigation strategies. To address the nonlinear and non-stationary nature of carbon emission data, this study proposes a hybrid CEEMDAN-EWT-LSTM model combining dual-stage decomposition and deep learning. The framework operates in three steps:

Multi-resolution features are extracted from raw carbon emission sequences using CEEMDAN.

Multi-resolution features are extracted from raw carbon emission sequences using CEEMDAN.

Spectral purification is carried out on the first-order intrinsic mode function using EWT.

Spectral purification is carried out on the first-order intrinsic mode function using EWT.

For distributed prediction of each modal component, an LSTM network is built, and linear superposition is used to achieve the final predicted values.

For distributed prediction of each modal component, an LSTM network is built, and linear superposition is used to achieve the final predicted values.

This hybrid model’s superiority in short-term carbon emission forecasting is validated by empirical research using China’s total carbon emission data from the Global Real-time Carbon Database, which shows that it performs better than comparative models across evaluation metrics like MAPE, RMSE, MAE, and R2. The “Decomposition-Denoising-Ensemble” technical paradigm, which provides an innovative approach for simulating complex time series of carbon emissions, was presented in this work. Future studies will integrate variables like energy consumption and climatic parameters to broaden the scope of multi-source heterogeneous data fusion techniques.

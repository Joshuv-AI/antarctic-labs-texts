Superior Capsular Reconstruction for Massive Rotator Cuff Tear
Authors: Sumpada Priambudi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Massive rotator cuff tears (RCT) defined as defects measuring >5 cm or involving two or more torn tendons. Irreparable RCT are generally large and retracted with poor or attenuated tissue quality, muscular atrophy, and fatty infiltration. Surgical options for irreparable RCT are arthroscopic debridement, partial repair, margin convergence, patch graft, tendon transfer, balloon spacer, and reverse total shoulder arthroplasty (RSA). Recently, superior capsular reconstruction (SCR) developed by a Japanese surgeon, Mihata et al, has gained attention as potential treatment for patients with irreparable RCT. Anatomically, superior capsule acts as a stabilizing structure and its crucial role in improving shoulder function by re-centering the humeral head into the glenoid socket, thus restoring the force couples to efficiently elevate the arm in case of an irreparable superior cuff tear. Indications include younger patients (aged < 65-70 years) with irreparable, massive RCT involving the supraspinatus and infraspinatus with minimal arthritis, an intact or repairable subscapularis, and a functional deltoid without bony deficiency, stiffness, or advanced arthropathy. Fascia lata is harvested as the graft with 6 – 8 mm thickness should be achieved. The graft is fixed to the superior glenoid with 2 suture anchors, and 4 suture anchors are used to secure the graft laterally to the humerus. The graft should be sutured to the remnant posterior cuff tendon such as the infraspinatus or teres minor tendon. Reconstruction of the superior capsule using fascia lata autograft has shown promising early results with improved clinical scores, range of motion, and muscle strength.

Mitochondrial apoptosis gene-based pathomics for ovarian cancer prognosis
Authors: Lan-hui Qin, Xiaofang Huang, Chongze Yang, Rui Song, Pei-yin Chen, Zijian Jiang, Weihui Xu, Guanzhen Zeng, Hong Chen, Liling Long
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study developed and validated a mitochondrial apoptosis-related pathology transfer learning model (MAR-PTL) for ovarian cancer prognosis by integrating digital pathology features with mitochondrial apoptosis gene expression. We constructed a transfer learning framework combining deep learning features extracted from H&E slides using ResNet50 architecture with transcriptomic data. Patients were categorized into high- and low-risk groups based on model-generated risk scores, and functional enrichment analysis along with single-cell RNA sequencing were performed to elucidate underlying mechanisms. The MAR-PTL model demonstrated superior prognostic performance (C-index = 0.78) compared to conventional methods. Notably, BCL2L2 emerged as the core prognostic gene, showing significant correlations with specific ResNet features, including a negative correlation with ResNet592 and positive correlations with ResNet373, 737, and 938. Mechanistically, high-risk groups exhibited downregulated ribosomal pathways and upregulated immune-inflammatory pathways. Furthermore, single-cell analysis revealed that BCL2L2 + tumor cells displayed distinct metabolic profiles enriched in respirasome assembly pathways and preferentially interacted with fibroblasts and endothelial cells via MDK-NCL and PPIA-BSG ligand-receptor pairs. Collectively, the MAR-PTL model provides a novel approach for prognostication by capturing the interplay between mitochondrial apoptosis and pathological features, identifying BCL2L2 as a key regulator of progression through metabolic reprogramming and tumor-stromal interactions, thereby offering potential therapeutic targets for high-risk patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-40121-5.

Introduction

Ovarian cancer is a major malignant tumor threatening women’s health, ranking first in mortality rate among gynecological malignancies1. Global data indicates that the overall 5-year survival rate for ovarian cancer patients is approximately 45%, while for advanced-stage patients, this rate drops to merely 30%2. This poor prognosis primarily stems from the delayed nature of diagnosis—over 70% of patients are already at advanced stages (III/IV) upon diagnosis, with tumors extensively metastasized to the peritoneal cavity or distant organs3. Ovarian cancer is often referred to as the “silent killer” due to its obscure early symptoms, and coupled with the absence of effective screening methods, the rate of early diagnosis has remained persistently low4.

The clinical treatment of ovarian cancer faces three core challenges: First, its etiology is highly complex, involving the interactive effects of multiple factors including genetic elements such as BRCA1/2 mutations5, hormonal metabolic abnormalities6. Although platinum-based chemotherapy regimens remain the primary treatment modality, patient prognosis varies significantly7. Furthermore, the development and progression of ovarian cancer involve abnormal activation of multiple signaling pathways, including PI3K/AKT8and Wnt/β-catenin9, which form complex regulatory networks with tumor microenvironment remodeling, presenting challenges for precise clinical prognostic assessment10,11.

In recent years, the integration of pathomics and deep learning technologies has pioneered innovative pathways for precise prognostic assessment of ovarian cancer. Pathomics enables high-throughput extraction and analysis of spatial heterogeneity features—including tumor cell morphology, stromal architecture, and immune microenvironment—from whole-slide digital pathology images (WSI), transcending the limitations of traditional pathological evaluation12. Conventional histopathology primarily relies on subjective grading of H&E-stained sections by pathologists, making it difficult to quantify deep features within the tumor microenvironment13. In contrast, deep learning models based on convolutional neural networks (CNN) and Transformer architectures can automatically identify microscopic pathological features such as cellular polarity changes at the tumor invasive front and stromal fibrosis patterns14,15, while using attention mechanisms to localize key image regions associated with prognosis16. Research demonstrates that prognostic models integrating pathological image features with molecular markers can enhance the predictive accuracy of overall survival for advanced-stage patients, significantly outperforming traditional clinical staging systems17.

Mitochondrial apoptosis (mitochondria-dependent programmed cell death) represents a core regulatory mechanism of ovarian cancer cell fate, playing a crucial role in tumorigenesis and chemosensitivity modulation18. This pathway is controlled by the dynamic equilibrium of the Bcl-2 protein family: pro-apoptotic proteins (such as Bax and Bak) initiate cytochrome C release by inducing mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization (MOMP), thereby activating the caspase cascade19; whereas anti-apoptotic proteins (such as Bcl-2 and Mcl-1) inhibit this process20. Ovarian cancer tissues commonly exhibit Bcl-2 overexpression and Bax/Bak epigenetic silencing, resulting in elevated mitochondrial apoptotic thresholds. Platinum chemotherapeutic agents promote apoptosis by activating the p53-Bax axis through DNA damage induction21. Furthermore, mitochondrial dynamic network remodeling (such as fission/fusion balance) significantly influences therapeutic response by altering caspase activation efficiency22, providing potential targets for clinical intervention.

To date, no research has combined mitochondrial apoptosis with histopathology to predict ovarian cancer prognosis. Addressing this scientific gap, we propose a novel pathological transfer learning model construction methodology: by integrating mitochondria-related molecular features with digital pathology imaging data to establish a biologically interpretable prognostic prediction system.

Materials and methods

Data collection

We collected pathology Diagnostic Slides of 105 ovarian cancer patients from The Cancer Genome Atlas Program (TCGA) website (https://www.cancer.gov/ccg/research/genome-sequencing/tcga). Concurrently, we downloaded gene expression RNAseq, phenotype, and survival data for 429 ovarian cancer patients from TCGA via UCSC Xena (https://xena.ucsc.edu/). We obtained single-cell transcriptome datasets of 5 primary ovarian cancer lesions from GEO (GSE15600, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE154600). The mitochondrial apoptosis gene set (112 genes, detailed in Supplementary Table 1) was acquired from the Gene Set Enrichment Analysis database (GSEA, https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/index.jsp).

Preprocessing of diagnostic pathological slides

Initially, whole slide images (WSIs) of primary lesion histological sections were selected from 105 ovarian cancer patients. Based on the Histolab module, each patient’s WSI underwent systematic processing using Full-size Fixed Position (FFP) technology to automatically extract 10 tiles from each slide. Each patch was configured with a tile size of 512 × 512 pixels at level 1, with tissue percentage greater than or equal to 75%, and zero pixel overlap. Following this processing pipeline, a total of 1,050 high-quality pathological samples were obtained and subjected to uniform color normalization to ensure data comparability and analytical accuracy.

Extraction of deep learning features and pathomics features

To comprehensively capture tumor heterogeneity, we extracted high-dimensional features using a dual-modality approach. First, Deep Learning Features were extracted using a pre-trained ResNet50 architecture. For each standardized image tile, we extracted the feature maps from the layer4 block, resulting in a 2,048-dimensional feature vector that encodes high-level abstract morphological patterns. Second, Pathomics Features were quantified using CellProfiler software (version 4.2.6). We extracted a total of 682 quantitative metrics, comprising 237 global image features (measuring image quality, granularity, intensity, and texture) and 445 object-level features (characterizing nuclei shape, size, intensity distribution, and spatial topology). Finally, the deep learning and pathomics features were fused to generate a comprehensive initial feature pool of 2,730 features for each patient (mean values across 10 tiles per patient were used for downstream analysis).

Core genes in the pathology-mitochondrial apoptosis model

GSEA enrichment analysis was performed on the mitochondrial apoptosis gene sets. Correlation analysis was conducted between the finalized pathological deep learning/omics features and the mitochondrial apoptosis gene sets. Simultaneously, prognostic prediction analysis was performed on the highly significantly correlated mitochondrial apoptosis genes. Significantly correlated mitochondrial apoptosis genes were validated at both mRNA and protein levels, with analyses completed using the Sparkle database platform (https://grswsci.top/).

Single-cell transcriptome sequencing analysis

We employed the Seurat package (version 4.0.0) in R (version 4.2.1) to process and analyze single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data of ovarian cancer. Quality control parameters were established as follows: nFeature_RNA range of 200–7500 (number of detected genes), with a percentage.mt (mitochondrial gene proportion) threshold of 15%. Cell clustering resolution was set to 1.0, with other parameters using Seurat default values, including: 30 PCA dimensions, k.param = 20 for the FindNeighbors function, and algorithm = 1 (original Louvain algorithm) for FindClusters. Cell type annotation was performed with reference to the TISCH2 database (http://tisch.compbio.cn/home/)24. Kruskal-Wallis tests were utilized to evaluate the expression differences of core genes among different cell clusters. Cell-level pathway activity analysis was implemented using the AUCell package to assess the activity of biological pathways related to immunity, metabolism, signal transduction, proliferation, cell death, and mitochondrial function. Based on specific gene expression profiles, cells were categorized into positive and negative groups, and the limma package was used to compare pathway activity differences between the two groups (comparisons were filtered if either group contained zero cells). Cell communication analysis was conducted using the CellChat package to compare interaction differences between malignant tumor cells with high or low expression of core genes and other cell types.

Statistical analysis and software

Preprocessing of whole slide images (WSI) of ovarian cancer and deep learning feature extraction were implemented using Python (version 3.9). To evaluate model performance, we utilized the concordance index (C-index) and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC). Prognostic factors were identified using univariate Cox regression, while independent prognostic value was determined through multivariate Cox regression with adjustment for confounding variables. Survival differences between groups were illustrated using Kaplan-Meier curves and analyzed with log-rank tests. All statistical analyses employed two-sided tests, with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant. All data analysis pipelines were implemented using R (version 4.2.1).

Construction of a prognostic model for mitochondrial apoptosis-related pathology transfer learning (MAR-PTL)

Construction of a Prognostic Model for Ovarian Cancer Based on Mitochondrial Apoptosis Gene-Related Pathological Deep Learning Features and Clinical Parameters.

In this study, ovarian cancer patients were divided into training (n = 65) and testing (n = 17) groups at a ratio of 7:3, and 100 machine learning methods were employed to construct prediction models (Fig. 2D–G). The optimal model was based on Lasso + StepCox[both], achieving C-index values of 0.74 and 0.72 in the training and testing groups, respectively, demonstrating good discriminative capability (Fig. 3A). Further analysis revealed that the model exhibited stable survival prediction performance across both groups (Fig. 3B). Regarding survival prediction, the AUC values for 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year survival in the training group were 0.80, 0.81, and 0.90, respectively (Fig. 3C), while the testing group showed AUC values of 0.64, 0.81, and 0.86. Although the AUC values in the testing group were comparatively lower, the training group demonstrated excellent AUC performance. Univariate analysis indicated that neither stage nor age was significantly associated with survival prognosis (as shown in Fig. 3D). However, the riskscore demonstrated statistical significance in univariate regression analysis, suggesting its importance in predicting survival outcomes. Results suggest the Lasso-Cox stepwise prediction model provides accurate survival predictions for ovarian cancer patients, with patient age and disease stage demonstrating minimal impact on prognosis.

Core genes of the MAR-PTL

Functional analysis of MAR-PTL risk groups revealed significant downregulation of ribosomal pathways and upregulation of immune-inflammatory signaling (e.g., chemokine signaling), suggesting a “mitochondrial stress-inflammatory” phenotype (Fig. 3E). To identify the molecular drivers of these pathological features, we screened mitochondrial apoptosis genes and identified BCL2L2, CHCHD10, and CCAR2 as the top candidates correlated with the risk signature (Figs. 3F,G). Among them, BCL2L2 was selected as the core prognostic marker because it exhibited the highest coefficient weight in the Lasso-Cox model and functions as a direct, druggable executor of mitochondrial apoptosis, distinguishing it from the structural or cell-cycle roles of CHCHD10 and CCAR2.

BCL2L2 showed significant quantitative correlations with specific deep pathological features (negative with Resnet592; positive with Resnet373/737/938), linking gene expression to tumor morphology. Survival analysis confirmed BCL2L2 as an independent risk factor (Fig. 4A). While its expression differed significantly between tumor and normal tissues (Fig. 4B), no statistical significance was observed across histological grades (Figure S1). This is likely attributable to the severe sample imbalance (> 85% Grade 3) in the TCGA cohort; however, the robust association between BCL2L2 and poor overall survival confirms its value as a marker of intrinsic biological aggressiveness beyond morphological grading.

Analysis of core genes in single-cell sequencing transcriptome

This research conducted a comprehensive transcriptomic analysis of ovarian cancer using single-cell sequencing technology, detecting a total of 42,583 cells encompassing 11 major cell populations: B, CD8T, CD8Tex, Endothelial, Epithelial, Fibroblasts, Malignant, Mono/Macro, Myofibroblasts, Plasma, and Tprolif (Fig. 4C). Results indicated that BCL2L2 exhibited high expression in malignant and epithelial cells (Fig. 4D). Pathway enrichment analysis revealed that BCL2L2+/- Malignant cells were primarily enriched in mitochondrial Cytochrome and Respirasome Assembly pathways (Fig. 4E). Pathway map adapted from the KEGG PATHWAY Database25,26. Furthermore, cell communication analysis demonstrated that interactions between BCL2L2+/- Malignant cells and Fibroblasts occurred most frequently, followed by Endothelial cells and then M1 cells (Fig. 4F). Regarding communication intensity, interactions between BCL2L2+/- Malignant cells and other cells were predominantly mediated by the MK pathway, with the CypA pathway ranking second (Fig. 4G). Notably, all cells except Plasma cells established direct communication with BCL2L2+/- Malignant cells. In these interactions, receptor-ligand pairs functioned primarily through two mechanisms: one via the MDK-NCL receptor pair and the other via the PPIA-BSG receptor pair. Specifically, cells such as Endothelial cells and Fibroblasts primarily established communication with BCL2L2+/- Malignant cells through the MDK-NCL receptor pair, while close interactions with Endothelial cells, Fibroblasts, and Myofibroblasts were established through the PPIA-BSG receptor pair (Fig. 4H). Additionally, in the MK pathway, BCL2L2+/- Malignant cells demonstrated high levels of interaction with Endothelial cells, Fibroblasts, and Myofibroblasts (Fig. 4I); in the CypA pathway, interactions between BCL2L2+/- Malignant cells and Endothelial cells, Fibroblasts, and Myofibroblasts were particularly robust (Fig. 4J).

Discussion

In this study, we constructed the Mitochondrial Apoptosis-Related Pathological Transfer Learning (MAR-PTL) model, a novel framework integrating deep pathological features with mitochondrial apoptosis profiles. Unlike traditional clinical staging or single-omics markers27–29, MAR-PTL captures the “phenotype-genotype” linkage, outperforming conventional methods with a C-index of 0.7830.Our findings suggest that dysfunction in the mitochondrial apoptosis pathway—a key mechanism of platinum resistance—manifests macroscopically as specific morphological patterns detectable by deep learning31.

Functional analysis revealed that high-risk patients exhibit a “mitochondrial stress-inflammatory feedback loop“32. We observed downregulated ribosomal pathways, consistent with previously reported suppression of translation mechanisms33. suggesting a stem-like state with enhanced self-renewal capacity34, Concurrently, upregulated inflammatory signaling (chemokines, TLRs) aligns with the theory that mitochondrial dysfunction triggers inflammation via ROS, which reciprocally promotes tumor cell survival35,36. Furthermore, the activation of complement and checkpoint pathways (PD-L1)37,38, indicates that this inflammatory state is immunosuppressive rather than cytotoxic, driving tumor evasion and metastasis through extracellular matrix remodeling and anoikis resistance39.

We identified BCL2L2 as the core prognostic gene, bridging microscopic pathology and molecular function. Its strong correlation with specific ResNet features provides the first deep-learning-based quantification of anti-apoptotic protein expression manifested in tissue morphology, consistent with prior reports linking BCL-2 family proteins to histological characteristics40Biologically, single-cell analysis revealed that BCL2L2 + tumor cells possess distinct metabolic dependence on respirasome assembly41–43. reflecting metabolic plasticity under stress44.

Furthermore, BCL2L2 + cells actively remodel the stroma. We mapped a specific communication network where BCL2L2 + cells interact with fibroblasts and endothelial cells primarily via MDK-NCL and PPIA-BSG ligand-receptor pairs s45,46. The MDK-NCL axis, known to promote angiogenesis and hypoxia adaptation47, suggests that BCL2L2 high-expression tumors actively construct a supportive niche to resist apoptosis. Similarly, the CypA (PPIA) pathway likely promotes invasion by remodeling the extracellular matrix48and influencing the immune landscape49.

Limitations and future directions

Despite the promising predictive performance of the MAR-PTL model, this study has several limitations that define our future research roadmap. First, regarding experimental validation, while we identified BCL2L2 as a core target computationally, wet-lab verification was not performed in the current retrospective study. Our immediate next step is to conduct functional assays, including BCL2L2 knockdown and overexpression in ovarian cancer cell lines and organoids, to unequivocally confirm its causal role in driving the identified pathological phenotypes.

Second, concerning clinical evaluation, the current model was trained and validated on retrospective cohorts (TCGA), which may introduce selection bias. To facilitate clinical translation, we are initiating a multi-center, prospective study to evaluate the MAR-PTL model’s robustness and generalizability in real-world clinical settings across different populations.

Third, based on our single-cell findings, functional studies targeting the tumor-stroma interaction are warranted. Future work will focus on developing bispecific antibodies or small-molecule inhibitors targeting the MDK-NCL and PPIA-BSG axes identified in this study, with the ultimate goal of developing combination therapies (e.g., MAR-PTL-guided BCL2L2 inhibitors combined with immunotherapy) for high-risk ovarian cancer patients.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study constructed a precise prognostic prediction model by integrating mitochondrial apoptosis genes with deep pathological features, identified BCL2L2 as a critical molecule regulating ovarian cancer progression, and elucidated the molecular network mediating tumor-microenvironment interactions. These findings not only deepen our understanding of ovarian cancer biology but also provide novel molecular targets and strategies for precision medicine, offering promise for improving the therapeutic landscape of this “silent killer.” The mechanistic insights revealed herein establish a foundation for targeted interventions that may ultimately transform clinical outcomes in ovarian cancer management through the exploitation of mitochondrial apoptotic vulnerabilities and tumor-stromal communication pathways.

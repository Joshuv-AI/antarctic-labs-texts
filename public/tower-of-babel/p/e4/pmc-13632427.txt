Breaking the immune “cold niche” in bone metastasis: core mechanisms of the multidimensional interwoven regulatory network and precision breakthrough strategies
Authors: Yun Mao, Feiyu Xie, Xuelei Chu, Wanhui Zhang, Ke Dou, Beier Hu, Ling Fu, Zhixun Ding, Shijie Zhu, Wen Cao, Yongjie He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBone metastasis is a pivotal hallmark of advanced malignant tumors. It severely impairs the therapeutic efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) and serves as an independent risk factor for poor prognosis of immunotherapy in solid tumors. Most existing reviews are confined to traditional research frameworks and have not yet resolved this clinical dilemma. This paper systematically elaborates on the core mechanisms underlying the poor efficacy of immunotherapy for bone metastasis. It clarifies that the immune suppression mediated by the bone metastatic microenvironment (BME) is not induced by a single factor, but rather a consequence of the cross-regulation of immunity, metabolism, bone matrix, blood vessels, epigenetics, microbiota, and nerves. From seven dimensions—immune cell-mediated immune escape, bone matrix-immune cell crosstalk, vascular-mediated immune suppression, metabolic-immune cross-regulation, epigenetic disorders, microbiota-osteoimmune axis imbalance, and neuro-immune-bone metastasis regulation—the study comprehensively sorts out the key nodes of immunotherapy resistance and clarifies the core cellular, molecular, and microenvironmental interaction mechanisms of this cross-regulatory network. On this basis, this paper explores a multi-target combined therapeutic strategy of “breaking the vicious cycle by targeting key nodes, restoring anti-tumor immunity with ICIs, and realizing local-systemic synergism”, including combined bone matrix-immune targeted therapy, immune phenotype-based precise treatment, local-systemic synergistic immunotherapy and multi-dimensional microenvironment remodeling regimens.Finally, the paper prospects future research directions, including in-depth mechanism analysis, new target development, precision diagnosis and treatment innovation, and clinical translation. It aims to systematically summarize current mechanisms and provide theoretical reference and novel insights for developing precise immunotherapeutic strategies against bone metastasis.Keywords: Bone metastasis, Immunotherapy, Immune suppression, Bone microenvironment, Multidimensional regulation, Targeted therapy

Introduction

Bonefers to a malignant lesion in which malignant tumors originating outside the bone tissue metastasize to the bone tissue through the blood circulation and induce bone damage, serving as one of the important signs of advanced cancer. Clinical data show that the incidence of bone metastasis in patients with lung metastasis recancer, breast cancer and prostate cancer is as high as 30%-40%, 65%-75% and 65%-80% respectively, and malignant tumors such as renal cell carcinoma and rectal cancer also frequently develop bone metastasis, which seriously endangers patients’ life and health [1, 2]. Bone metastasis can induce skeletal-related events (SREs) such as severe pain, pathological fracture, spinal cord compression and hypercalcemia, which not only significantly reduce patients’ quality of life but also accelerate disease progression and shorten patients’ survival time [3]. In recent years, the advent of immunotherapy, especially immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), has brought new therapeutic hope for patients with advanced malignant tumors, showing significant efficacy in the treatment of various tumors such as melanoma, non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and breast cancer, and significantly prolonging patients’ median progression-free survival (mPFS) and median overall survival (mOS) [4–6]. However, the efficacy of immunotherapy in patients with bone metastasis is generally poor, with a significantly reduced objective response rate (ORR). A large proportion of patients exhibit limited clinical benefits and even early disease progression.

Existing reviews on bone metastasis are mostly limited to the fixed framework of “diagnosis-traditional treatment-prognosis” or focus on a single treatment method in isolation, lacking a systematic analysis of the core mechanisms of the poor efficacy of immunotherapy for bone metastasis. In recent years, with the breakthrough of technologies such as single-cell transcriptomics and metabolomics, studies have found that immune suppression in bone metastasis is not caused by a single factor but the result of multidimensional cross-regulation of “immunity-bone matrix-blood vessels-metabolism-epigenetics-microbiota-nerves”. The bone metastatic microenvironment (BME) is not a traditional “immune desert” but an active immune suppression fortress jointly constructed by tumor cells, bone matrix cells, immune cells and other components, forming a vicious cycle of “immune suppression-bone matrix destruction-metabolic disorder-vascular abnormality”. On this basis, this study systematically summarizes the combined therapeutic strategy of “targeting key nodes to break the vicious cycle, restoring anti-tumor immunity via ICIs, and achieving local-systemic synergy”, providing theoretical support for reversing immune tolerance and improving therapeutic efficacy through multi-target combination therapy.

Current application and efficacy dilemma of immunotherapy in bone metastasis

Bone metastasis impairs the systemic efficacy of immunotherapy

As a common type of tumor metastasis, bone metastasis has a significant negative impact on the efficacy of immunotherapy in patients with cross-cancer consistency. In cancer types with a high incidence of bone metastasis such as NSCLC, renal cell carcinoma (RCC), metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer(mCRPC), multiple studies have confirmed that patients with bone metastasis have worse ORR, PFS and OS after immunotherapy than those without bone metastasis [7].

NSCLC is a cancer type with a relatively high incidence of bone metastasis and also the most extensive field of immunotherapy research. Existing literature clearly confirms that bone metastasis significantly impairs the efficacy of immunotherapy in patients [8, 9]. A meta-analysis [10] included 13 studies with a total of 3,681 patients, and the meta-analysis showed that bone metastasis significantly reduced the OS and PFS of NSCLC patients receiving ICIs, with bone metastasis associated with a 45% increased risk of death [Hazard Ratio (HR): 1.45] and a 40% increased risk of disease progression (HR: 1.40). Another study including more than 1,000 patients further confirmed that in lung cancer, prostate cancer and melanoma, bone metastasis not only reduces the immunotherapy response at the local site but also impairs the efficacy of extraosseous metastatic lesions [11]. In addition, a number of targeted studies have further verified this conclusion. A study focusing on the association between bone metastasis and immunotherapy in advanced NSCLC [12] showed that among advanced NSCLC patients receiving ICIs, the ORR of patients with bone metastasis was 12%-13%, mPFS was 2.7-3 months, and mOS was 5-7.4 months, which were significantly lower than those of patients without bone metastasis (ORR 22%-23%, mPFS 4-5.2 months, mOS 10.9–15.3 months). Another study [13] clearly stated that metastatic NSCLC patients with bone metastasis had a higher incidence of SREs when receiving ICIs treatment, and their OS was significantly shorter than that of patients without bone metastasis. For advanced NSCLC patients with high programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression (≥ 50%), even in this population with relatively good immunotherapy response, the efficacy of first-line ICIs treatment in patients with bone metastasis is still significantly lower than that in patients without bone metastasis, further confirming the universal negative impact of bone metastasis on the efficacy of immunotherapy for NSCLC [14]. In addition, a study including patients with advanced NSCLC, RCC and melanoma [15] showed that among advanced NSCLC patients receiving ICIs treatment, the 5-year survival rate of the bone metastasis group was only 11%, which was significantly lower than 28% of the non-bone metastasis group.

RCC is also a cancer type with a high incidence of bone metastasis, and existing literature indicates that bone metastasis also reduces the efficacy of immunotherapy in RCC patients [16]. A multicenter phase II study [17] showed that the ORR of ICIs in the treatment of RCC patients with bone metastasis was 14.8%, mPFS was 2.8 months, and mOS was 17.9 months; while in the same study, the ORR of RCC patients without bone metastasis receiving ICIs treatment was 23.3%, mPFS was 4.6 months, and mOS was 26.1 months, indicating that bone metastasis significantly reduces the efficacy of ICIs treatment in RCC patients. In terms of combined immunotherapy, a multicenter retrospective analysis [18] showed the overall poor outcomes of Ipilimumab combined with Nivolumab in the treatment of RCC patients with bone metastasis in terms of ORR (21%), PFS (6.1 months) and OS (25.6 months). The above results are worse than those of the Checkmate 214 study, which compared the efficacy of Ipilimumab + Nivolumab with sunitinib in the treatment of untreated RCC patients [19]. Another study explored the impact of bone metastasis on RCC and melanoma patients receiving Ipilimumab combined with Nivolumab treatment [20], and the results showed that the 6-month PFS rate of the RCC bone metastasis group was 14.8%, while that of patients without bone metastasis was 77.1%; the 12-month OS rate of the bone metastasis group was significantly lower than that of patients without bone metastasis (41.7% vs. 82.7%).

In urothelial carcinoma, the negative impact of bone metastasis on immunotherapy is also significant, and the efficacy difference between patients with bone metastasis and non-bone metastasis is particularly obvious. A multicenter retrospective study [21] retrospectively analyzed the efficacy of ICIs treatment in 208 patients with advanced pretreated urothelial carcinoma, and the results showed that the mPFS and mOS of patients with bone metastasis were 2 months and 3.9 months respectively; while those of patients without bone metastasis were 2.6 months and 7.8 months respectively, clearly indicating that bone metastasis significantly reduces the efficacy of immunotherapy. Another study [22] also confirmed that in the treatment of urothelial carcinoma with ICIs, the treatment response rate of patients with bone metastasis is significantly lower than that of patients with non-bone metastasis, and the incidence of mixed response is higher, further suggesting the adverse effect of bone metastasis on the efficacy of immunotherapy for urothelial carcinoma.

Pan-cancer studies have further confirmed that bone metastasis is an important factor affecting the efficacy of immunotherapy in patients with solid tumors, and the efficacy difference between patients with bone metastasis and non-bone metastasis has cross-cancer consistency. A study including patients with various advanced solid tumors [23] confirmed that metastatic site is an independent prognostic factor for ICIs treatment in solid tumor patients, and bone metastasis is associated with worse clinical outcomes, whose negative impact is particularly significant in NSCLC, RCC and urothelial carcinoma. In addition, a single-center retrospective analysis [24] showed that among solid tumor patients receiving ICIs treatment, bone metastasis is an important clinical factor affecting efficacy and prognosis, and its efficacy is significantly lower than that of patients without bone metastasis. In breast cancer, immunotherapy is used for PD-L1-positive metastatic triple-negative breast cancer, which significantly improves patients’ PFS and OS. Although more than 20% of participants have bone metastasis, no subgroup analysis was performed [25–27].

The efficacy of immunotherapy varies with different metastatic sites

As one of the core methods for the treatment of malignant tumors, immunotherapy shows significant heterogeneity in efficacy due to the organ-specific differences of metastatic sites. Due to the unique bone remodeling mechanism and immune-suppressive microenvironment, bone metastasis has distinct specificity in the efficacy of immunotherapy, and there are clear differences in benefit patterns compared with other metastatic sites such as liver, brain, lung and lymph nodes.

NSCLC patients are a high-risk group for bone metastasis, and relevant studies have conducted detailed discussions on the ICIs efficacy of bone metastasis and other metastatic sites, covering two modes: single immunotherapy and immune combination therapy. In single immunotherapy (first-line single-agent ICIs), studies have shown [28] that in the first-line single-agent ICIs treatment of NSCLC, the mPFS of lung, brain and adrenal metastatic lesions is 15.1 months, 4.3 months (15.5 months after combined local treatment) and 6.1 months respectively, while the mPFS of bone metastatic lesions is 4.2 months, which is significantly lower than that of lung and adrenal metastases. A retrospective study included 285 advanced NSCLC patients with first-line treatment, and the results showed that the ORR of lung, adrenal and lymph node metastases (43.9%, 44.2% and 43.5%) was the highest, and the tumor volume regression range was also more significant; while the proportion of disease progression was higher in patients with bone (41.7%), liver (35.6%) and brain (28.3%) metastases, among which patients with bone metastasis were more prone to primary resistance (76.1%). The PFS and OS of patients with bone and liver metastases were significantly shortened, with bone metastasis being 4 months and 10 months respectively, and liver metastasis both being 10 months. When these two groups of patients received immunotherapy or chemotherapy combined with immunotherapy, the PFS and OS was shortened more obviously, while no such difference was observed in those receiving chemotherapy alone [29]. A meta-analysis [30] summarized 8,067 advanced NSCLC patients and analyzed the efficacy differences of different metastatic sites under ICIs treatment. The results suggested that the OS of patients with brain, liver and bone metastases was significantly worse (HR 1.25, 1.73, 1.67, P < 0.001), clarifying that bone metastasis is an independent prognostic factor for poor prognosis of ICIs treatment. In terms of immune combination therapy, studies focused on the efficacy differences of radiotherapy combined with ICIs at different metastatic sites in NSCLC [31], and the results showed that the overall ORR of 128 patients was 32.03% (41/128), and the clinical benefit rate reached 88.28%. Subgroup analysis showed that the ORR of the brain metastasis group was the highest (48.65%), followed by the lung (including draining lymph nodes) metastasis group (41.94%), and the ORR of the bone metastasis group was significantly lower (17.07%).

Relevant studies have also provided clear clinical data on the ICIs efficacy of RCC patients with bone metastasis and the differences in different metastatic sites. A post-hoc analysis of the CLEAR trial [32] analyzed the efficacy differences of lenvatinib plus pembrolizumab (ICIs combined with targeted therapy) at different metastatic sites, and the results showed that the ORR, mPFS and mOS of patients with lung metastasis were 73.0%, 22.1 months and 51.8 months respectively; those of patients with lymph node metastasis were 67.9%, 22.0 months and 49.3 months respectively; those of patients with bone metastasis were 60.0%, 17.2 months and 36.9 months respectively; those of patients with liver metastasis were 55.6% and 14.6 months, and OS needs to be interpreted with caution due to the wide confidence interval. The results indicated that the efficacy of ICIs treatment for RCC bone metastasis is poor. In terms of immune combination therapy, the efficacy ranking of ICIs combination therapy at different metastatic sites in RCC is: lung metastasis > lymph node metastasis > bone metastasis > liver metastasis [33]. The ORR and mPFS of patients with bone metastasis receiving IO-IO (nivolumab + ipilimumab) treatment were 35.3% and 6.3 months respectively, which were much lower than those of lymph node (47.6%, 42.4 months), lung (51.6%, 15.0 months) and brain metastasis (50.0%, 9.1 months). At the same time, IO-TKI improved the ORR of patients with bone metastasis to 41.3%, mPFS to 35.3 months, and OS also showed a beneficial trend.

In addition to NSCLC and RCC, the efficacy of immunotherapy at different metastatic sites in melanoma and urothelial carcinoma also differs. In the immunotherapy of melanoma with different site metastases, bone metastasis, together with liver and brain metastases, belongs to the type with poor prognosis. The mOS of patients with only bone metastasis is 4.8 months, which is significantly shorter than that of lung metastasis (9.6 months), lymph node metastasis (13.6 months), subcutaneous and soft tissue metastasis (14 months); at the same time, the mOS of patients with liver metastasis is 3.9 months and that of brain metastasis is 4.0 months, all of which are independent poor prognostic factors [34]. In addition, a number of relevant studies [28, 35] confirmed that in the treatment of unresectable or metastatic urothelial carcinoma with ICIs, the response rate of bone metastasis sites is significantly lower than that of lung, lymph node and other metastatic sites, and the incidence of mixed response is higher, further suggesting the adverse effect of bone metastasis on the efficacy of immunotherapy for urothelial carcinoma.

Exploration of the mechanisms for poor efficacy of immunotherapy

Immune cell-mediated immune escape inhibits the efficacy of immunotherapy

Bone metastasis has long been regarded as an immune “sanctuary” for tumor cells, and the traditional view defines the BME as an “immune desert” lacking effective immune infiltration. However, the latest studies have confirmed that the BME is not a passive immune vacuum but an active immune suppression fortress jointly constructed by tumor cells, osteoblasts, osteoclasts and bone marrow stromal cells, forming a vicious cycle of “immune suppression-bone matrix destruction-vascular abnormality-further immune suppression”, which markedly severely impairs the anti-tumor immune response (Fig. 1).

PD-1 and PD-L1 signaling in bone metastasis

The programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1)/PD-L1 axis is a key regulatory axis for immune escape in bone metastasis, and its expression pattern and function show significant tissue specificity, which is obviously different from that of primary tumors. The combined positive rate of PD-L1 in tumor cells in bone metastatic lesions is only 12%, which is much lower than the positive rate of 19%-60% in primary tumors [36]; the expression of PD-L1 in primary lesions and bone metastatic lesions of triple-negative breast cancer is also significantly inconsistent [37], which is an important reason why simple targeting of tumor cell PD-L1 is difficult to take effect in bone metastasis. The low expression of PD-L1 in bone metastatic lesions does not mean the loss of immune suppression function, but instead mediates immune escape efficiently through a unique cellular distribution and regulatory mode. Studies have found that PD-L1 is not only expressed on the surface of tumor cells but also widely distributed on the surface of immune cells such as myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) and regulatory T cells (Tregs), and directly inhibits T cell function by binding to PD-1 on the surface of T cells. PD-L1 on the surface of myeloid cells directly promotes osteoclast formation, and inhibition of this pathway can simultaneously block bone destruction and tumor bone metastasis [38]; in melanoma patients with bone metastasis, PD-L1+Tregs inhibit T cell function in a PD-L1-dependent manner and construct a local immune-suppressive microenvironment [39]. At the same time, abnormal PD-1/PD-L1 signaling is also associated with the regulation of bone metastasis pain. PD-1 blockade can inhibit osteoclast formation and relieve murine bone cancer pain without changing the tumor burden; the mechanism is related to the restoration of effector T cell function and the reduction of the secretion of pro-inflammatory factors such as Tumor Necrosis Factor‌ (TNF)-α and Interleukin (IL) -6, which are not only involved in the occurrence of pain but also can promote the infiltration of immune-suppressive cells [40]. In addition, PD-1/PD-L1 antagonists have a positive impact on bone remodeling. Clinical studies and in vitro verification show that they regulate the function of bone metabolic cells, improve bone matrix destruction and relieve immune suppression, providing a theoretical basis for immunotherapy of bone metastasis [41]. It is worth noting that decalcification of bone tissue can interfere with the accuracy of PD-L1 detection and affect the evaluation of PD-L1 expression in NSCLC bone metastasis [42]. The special activation mode of the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway in bone metastasis breaks the traditional cognition with tumor cell PD-L1 as the core, and it becomes a key signal mediating the vicious cycle of immune escape and bone metastasis through the cross-regulation of immune cells and bone metabolic cells [43].

Immune-suppressive cells and related secretory factors promote immune escape in the bone metastatic microenvironment

The BME has significant enrichment of immune-suppressive cells, and MDSCs, Tregs and TAMs jointly form an innate immune suppression network. By secreting osteoclastogenic factors and immune-suppressive factors, they synchronously accelerate bone matrix destruction and immune escape [44]. The bone marrow immune microenvironment promotes tumor growth and bone deconstruction through the recruitment and activation of immune-suppressive cells, which limits the effect of immunotherapy [45].

Subtypes of immune-suppressive cells

Immune typing of the BME is the core premise for analyzing its immune escape characteristics and guiding precision immunotherapy. In the pan-cancer immune typing study, Lambrechts [46] constructed a single-cell transcriptomic atlas of bone metastasis in multiple cancer types, systematically revealed the common typing characteristics of immune cells in bone metastatic lesions of different cancer types, and proposed that immune cells in the bone microenvironment can be clearly divided into three bone metastasis subtypes with distinct prognosis and microenvironmental characteristics: cell cycle subtype, stress response subtype and migration subtype. Among them, the cell cycle subtype has the worst prognosis, with high expression of cell cycle-related genes, MYC signaling pathway and NME2, significant infiltration of CD8+T cells and obvious immune suppression. On the basis of pan-cancer common typing, Zhang [47] confirmed that the core differences in immune typing of various bone metastatic lesions are derived from the specific interaction between tumor cells and the bone microenvironment, and divided the immune microenvironment of bone metastasis into three “ecotypes”. The macrophage/osteoclast-enriched type (Mφ-OC ecotype) is characterized by the enrichment of macrophages and osteoclasts, common in breast cancer bone metastasis samples, highly dependent on osteoclasts for bone lysis, and tumor cells promote osteoclast differentiation by secreting factors such as IL-1β and PTHrP; the regulatory/exhausted T cell-dominated type (Treg-Tex ecotype) has a large number of infiltrating Tregs and exhausted CD8+ T cells (Tex) accompanied by low osteoclast activity, common in renal cell carcinoma bone metastasis, and constructs an immune-suppressive microenvironment through cytokines such as transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) secreted by Tregs, while Tex cells highly express immune checkpoint molecules such as PD-1 and T cell immunoreceptor with Ig and ITIM domains (TIGIT); the monocyte-dominant type is characterized by the enrichment of monocytes, common in lung cancer bone metastasis samples, where monocytes differentiate into MDSCs and produce substances such as reactive oxygen species to inhibit T cell function. For the specific immune typing of driver gene-positive tumors, Yang [48] found that Treg cells and M2-type macrophages were significantly enriched in bone metastatic lesions of driver gene-positive NSCLC, and cytotoxic immune cells such as natural killer (NK) cells and CD8+ T cells coexisted, indicating that bone metastatic lesions are not immune deserts. In addition, in breast cancer bone metastasis, tumor cells significantly reshape the immune microenvironment, leading to a large number of infiltration and abnormal subtyping of immune-suppressive cells, thereby promoting the bone colonization of tumor cells [44].

Myeloid-Derived Suppressor Cells (MDSCs)

MDSCs are the main myeloid immune-suppressive cells in bone metastasis. By secreting immune-suppressive factors such as IL-10 and TGF-β and expressing PD-L1, they directly inhibit the activation and function of T cells and NK cells, reduce Interferon-γ (IFN-γ) secretion and impair immune recognition ability [49, 50]. Myeloid-derived polymorphonuclear MDSCs (PMN-MDSCs) can be induced to expand by cancer-educated mesenchymal stem cells and support the survival of tumor cells in both primary and metastatic lesions at the same time [51]; T cells suppressed by the bone microenvironment can in turn promote osteoclast formation and osteolytic bone metastasis, forming a synergistic inhibitory effect with MDSCs [49]. Functionally, MDSCs directly activate osteoclasts and accelerate bone resorption by secreting factors such as Receptor Activator of Nuclear Factor-κ B Ligand (RANKL), IL-6 and TNF-α; at the same time, they inhibit the activation of CD8+T cells and NK cells and block anti-tumor immunity. Combining TIGIT blockade with MDSCs depletion can activate anti-tumor immunity and inhibit breast cancer bone metastasis [52], and depletion of polymorphonuclear MDSCs combined with osteoclast blockade can effectively treat established bone metastatic lesions [53], confirming that MDSCs are the core mediating cells of immune escape and bone destruction in bone metastasis.

Regulatory T Cells (Tregs)

Tregs are specifically enriched in the bone metastatic microenvironment, serving as a crucial contributor to the formation of local immunosuppressive milieu. The proportion of Tregs in the bone marrow microenvironment of patients with prostate cancer is significantly increased, which directly inhibits anti-tumor immunity [54]; tumor-specific Tregs in the bone marrow of breast cancer patients highly express the migration receptor Sphingosine-1-Phosphate Receptor 1 and are directionally enriched in bone metastatic lesions [54]. PTEN-deficient prostate cancer recruits more Foxp3 + Tregs by upregulating IDO1 expression and constructs a potent immune-suppressive microenvironment [55]. By secreting inhibitory cytokines such as IL-10 and TGF-β, Tregs directly inhibit the proliferation and activation of effector T cells, and at the same time promote osteoclast differentiation, closely coupling immune suppression with bone destruction and further strengthening immune escape in bone metastasis [56].

Tumor-Associated Macrophages (TAMs)

TAMs are the most abundant immune cells in the BME with high heterogeneity and pro-metastatic functions [57]. Single-cell transcriptomics and pan-cancer bone metastasis atlas have confirmed the existence of multiple functionally specific subtypes, including monocyte-derived infiltrating TAMs, CD169+TAMs, CD63+TAMs subsets, and a special TAMs subtype mediating the immunopotentiating effect of denosumab. Histone methyltransferase Absent, Small or Homeotic Discs 1-Like‌ (ASH1L) can also induce metabolic reprogramming of bone marrow macrophages to form a polarized subtype with pro-metastatic potential. Monocyte-derived macrophages directly promote the growth of breast cancer bone metastatic lesions [58], and histone methyltransferase ASH1L can regulate the metabolic reprogramming of macrophages and prime bone metastasis [59]. In prostate cancer bone metastasis, CD169+ macrophages mediate the formation of pathological woven bone and support tumor bone colonization [60]; tumors can hijack VCAM1+CD163+CCR3+ macrophages to obtain iron elements, synchronously promote bone metastasis and anemia, and be accompanied by a decrease in T cells, B cells and plasmacytoid dendritic cell (DC) in the metastatic bone marrow [61]. Single-cell sequencing identified the CD63+TAMs subset, and regulating iron metabolism to activate anti-tumor immunity can become a therapeutic target for bone metastasis [62]; the pan-cancer bone metastasis single-cell atlas also found that a specific TAMs subset mediates the denosumab-induced immunosensitization effect, becoming a key target for immunotherapy of lung cancer bone metastasis [63]. TAMs polarize into the M2 phenotype and secrete immunosuppressive and osteoclastogenic factors, serving as a core hub connecting immune escape and bone metastasis. A recent study established a novel technique termed SAMENT (Sortase A-based Microenvironmental Niche Tagging), which enables specific labeling of stromal cells in direct contact with cancer cells. Application of this technology across multi-cancer and multi-organ models revealed that metastatic niches are universally characterized by macrophage enrichment and T cell depletion, with distinct organ-specific heterogeneity in macrophage phenotypes. In bone metastasis, peritumoral macrophages exhibit high expression and sustained activation of estrogen receptor(ER) α, forming a unique ERα-positive macrophage barrier that physically blocks T cell infiltration and mediates immune exclusion. Macrophage-specific knockout of Esr1 dismantles this immune barrier, restores T cell infiltration, and markedly suppresses cancer cell bone colonization [64].

Immature neutrophils

As the most abundant circulating immune cells in the body, neutrophils exert dual roles in the complex pathophysiological process of tumor bone metastasis [65–67]. Tumor cells secrete inflammatory factors such as IL-1β to accelerate the lineage commitment of hematopoietic stem cells toward neutrophils, while restraining lymphoid and erythroid hematopoiesis. The immunosuppressive signature of neutrophils is initiated at the early hematopoietic stage and further potentiates pro-metastatic functions during tissue migration. In this manner, tumor cells remotely remodel bone marrow hematopoietic homeostasis and drive biased myeloid reprogramming [68]. The proportion of immature neutrophils in bone metastatic lesions is abnormally increased (reaching 80% in the terminal stage of progression), which highly express CHI3L3, can directly inhibit the anti-tumor immune effect of CD8+T cells, and at the same time promote osteoclast activation and bone matrix destruction by secreting related factors; the abnormal accumulation of immature neutrophils is regulated by the Dickkopf-related protein 1 (DKK1)-Cytoskeleton-Associated Protein 4 (CKAP4) axis. DKK1 is highly expressed in patients with bone metastasis, and its combination with its receptor CKAP4 can induce neutrophils to be in an immature functional state, further strengthening the vicious cycle of “immune suppression-bone matrix destruction”. Reprogramming the developmental state of neutrophils with DKK1 neutralizing antibody can significantly restore the function of CD8+T cells and improve the therapeutic efficacy of PD-1 monoclonal antibody. In addition, immature neutrophils can secrete factors such as Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), and matrix metallopeptidase 9 (MMP-9), among which MMP-9 can degrade bone matrix and promote the migration of vascular endothelial cells, and VEGF can promote abnormal vascular proliferation, further strengthening the synergistic suppression of “immunity-bone matrix-blood vessels” and exacerbating the dilemma of poor efficacy of immunotherapy [69]. In addition, in breast cancer bone metastasis tissues, catenin delta 1 (CTNND1) is significantly down-regulated. Tumor cells with low CTNND1 expression can accelerate neutrophil infiltration by up-regulating Granulocyte-Macrophage Colony-Stimulating Factor (GM-CSF) and IL-8, thereby inhibiting cytotoxic T cells and providing a suitable growth environment for tumor cells [70].

Exhaustion of effector immune cells aggravates immune escape and poor efficacy of immunotherapy

In the BME, effector immune cells show a typical functional exhaustion phenotype. The proliferation, activation and killing functions of CD4+ T, CD8+ T cells and NK cells are impaired, unable to effectively clear tumor cells, further amplifying the immune suppression effect and leading to primary resistance to immunotherapy [71].

T cell exhaustion and dysfunction

T cells are the core of adaptive immunity, divided into CD4+ T cells and CD8+ T cells. In the BME, they show functional suppression and abnormal subset polarization, impairing anti-tumor immune function.

Regarding impaired T cell function, single-cell sequencing was performed to compare samples of bone metastatic lesions, involved bone marrow, uninvolved bone marrow, as well as bone marrow specimens from cancer-free individuals and orthopedic patients. Multiple immune abnormalities were detected in bone metastasis, and marked reduction of T cell subsets represents one of the core characteristics. Cytotoxic CD8⁺ T cells decrease evidently and highly express exhaustion markers including TIM-3, losing the capacity to recognize and eliminate tumor cells. The subsets of CD4⁺ helper T cells become dysregulated. The activity of pro-inflammatory Th1 cells is suppressed, while the proportion of immunosuppressive Th2 cells and Tregs increases, which further weakens anti-tumor immune responses [72]. Osteoclast activity, metabolic disorders and intestinal microenvironment also contribute substantially to T cell dysfunction. Solute Carrier Family 2 Member 3 (SLC2A3)-mediated lactate metabolism facilitates lung cancer bone metastasis and immune evasion via regulating Tumor protein p53 (P53) lactylation. The underlying mechanism involves accumulated lactic acid that inhibits T cell activation and induces functional impairment [73]. Intestinal microbiota can promote the homing of intestinal NK cells and Th1 cells to bone metastatic lesions, sustain Th1 cell activity, alleviate abnormal T cell loss and restrain bone metastasis [74]. Aberrant expression of the GATA binding protein 3 (GATA3)-driven ceRNA network aggravates T cell dysfunction during lung adenocarcinoma bone metastasis [75]. Furthermore, impaired T cell function acts as a key regulatory link in immune-mediated bone invasion. A positive feedback loop of T cell dysfunction and bone destruction is established, which impairs immune surveillance and creates favorable conditions for tumor colonization [76].

The subsets and molecular characteristics of T cells are closely related to bone metastasis escape. Pan-cancer single-cell studies show that the composition of T cell subsets in bone metastatic lesions of different cancers is different, affecting the escape efficiency [46]; single-cell analysis of bone metastasis in various cancers found that the molecular characteristics of T cell exhaustion are universal, providing a basis for targeted therapy [77]. CD4+ T cells have high heterogeneity (including Th1, Th2, Th17 and other subsets) and regulate the immune microenvironment by secreting cytokines [78]. In bone metastasis, CD4+ T cell polarization is abnormal, the proportion of Th1 subsets (secreting IFN-γ, TNF-α to promote tumor immunity) is reduced, and the proportion of Th2 and Th17 subsets (secreting IL-4, IL-17 to inhibit immunity) is increased, strengthening the immune-suppressive microenvironment [79]. In clear cell renal cell carcinoma, CD103+CD4+ T cells are significantly enriched in bone metastatic lesions, and their dysfunction is related to escape and poor efficacy [80, 81]. CD8+ T cell functional exhaustion is the key to immune escape in bone metastasis. In bone metastasis, CD8+ T cells are continuously activated by PD-1/PD-L1 signaling and affected by immune-suppressive factors, the expression of cytotoxic molecules (perforin, granzyme B) is reduced, and the killing and proliferation activation abilities are decreased, unable to effectively clear tumor cells [82]. In PIK3CA-mutated NSCLC bone metastasis, TMTP1-modified extracellular vesicles can improve T cell exhaustion, enhance CD8+ T cell infiltration and overcome bone metastasis and immune resistance [83].

While bone metastasis depletes T cell populations, the bone microenvironment actively inhibits T cell activation. Unactivated T cells lose the ability to secrete anti-tumor cytokines such as IFN-γ, forming a local immune-suppressive microenvironment; at the same time, such T cells highly express osteoclast differentiation-related factors such as RANKL and TNF-α, directly activating osteoclast formation and bone resorption functions and amplifying osteolytic bone damage [84]. The dysfunction of unconventional T cells (γδ T cells, NKT cells, etc.) is also involved in escape. Their infiltration and functional state in bone metastatic lesions directly affect the escape efficiency, and dysfunction will impair anti-tumor immunity [85].

NK cell dysfunction

NK cells are the effector cells of early immune surveillance in bone metastasis, and their functional inactivation is an important link for tumors to achieve bone immune escape. Silencing of the Interferon regulatory factor 7 (IRF7) pathway in breast cancer cells promotes bone metastasis through immune escape mechanisms and directly impairs the surveillance function of NK cells [86]; the loss of host type I IFN signaling accelerates tumor metastasis and directly impairs the anti-tumor function of NK cells [87]. Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors reduce the tumor immune surveillance effect of NK cells, thereby increasing the risk of breast cancer bone metastasis [88]. The microbiome can inhibit melanoma bone growth by promoting the homing of intestinal NK cells and Th1 cells to bone tissue, which indirectly confirms that insufficient NK cell function is an important inducement of bone metastasis [74]. In the BME, the number of NK cells is reduced, the killing function is decreased, and the cytokine secretion is insufficient, unable to effectively recognize and clear tumor cells, allowing tumor cells to successfully complete bone colonization and proliferation.

Crosstalk regulation between bone stromal cells and immune cells

Bone metastasis is not merely passive colonization of tumor cells, but consists of three highly programmed and multicellular synergistic core stages: tumor cell bone homing, bone microenvironment-mediated tumor dormancy, and reactivation of dormant cells leading to overt bone metastasis. As the structural framework of the bone microenvironment, osteoclasts, osteoblasts and bone marrow stromal cells regulate the chemotactic migration, quiescence maintenance, invasive reawakening and malignant proliferation of tumor cells throughout the whole process via pivotal signaling axes including C-X-C motif chemokine ligand 12 (CXCL12)/C-X-C motif chemokine receptor 4 (CXCR4), Receptor Activator of Nuclear Factor κB (RANK)/RANKL, TGF-β, Ectonucleoside triphosphate diphosphohydrolase 1 (CD39)/Ecto-5′-nucleotidase (CD73), Growth arrest-specific protein 6 (GAS6)/AXL receptor tyrosine kinase (AXL) and Siglec-15/sialic acid axis. They also establish an in-depth interaction network with immune cells, continuously forming a vicious cycle of immunosuppression, bone stromal destruction and tumor progression, which serves as one of the core cytological bases to immunotherapy against bone metastasis(Fig. 2).

Bone homing: bone stromal cell-driven directed colonization and immune evasion of tumor cells

Tumor-specific bone metastasis is a highly selective active biological process. Osteoblasts and bone marrow stromal cells actively recruit circulating tumor cells (CTCs) to migrate toward bone marrow cavity by secreting chemokines and remodeling local microenvironment. Meanwhile, they initiate local immunosuppression prior to tumor cell arrival, constructing immune-privileged niches to support subsequent tumor colonization and dormancy, which initiates the whole cascade of bone metastasis [89]. This procedure relies primarily on the CXCL12/CXCR4 signaling axis serving as the core navigation system, with osteoblastic niches acting as dominant colonization sites to jointly accomplish tumor cell bone-specific homing [90].

Core regulatory axis: CXCL12/CXCR4 acts as navigation system and initiator of immune evasion

The homing behavior of CTCs to bone tissues is modulated by intrinsic tumor cell characteristics, local microenvironmental cues, chemokines and adhesion molecules. The CXCL12/CXCR4 axis is confirmed as the pivotal molecular navigator for tumor cell bone tropism. Osteoblasts and bone marrow stromal cells are the predominant cellular sources of CXCL12 in bone marrow, establishing steep chemokine concentration gradients within bone marrow cavity, endosteal regions and metaphysis [2]. Malignancies prone to bone metastasis including lung cancer, breast cancer and prostate cancer universally overexpress CXCR4, which enables them to precisely sense and respond to CXCL12 gradients, thereby facilitating CTCs to detach from vascular endothelium, complete transendothelial migration and infiltrate into bone marrow stroma, finishing the initial step of bone homing [91]. Existing studies have verified that Differentiated Embryonic Chondrocyte Expressed Gene 1(DEC1) transcriptionally upregulates CXCR4 expression, markedly amplifying the responsiveness of breast cancer cells to CXCL12 and further activating downstream pathways to promote tumor invasion, migration, bone homing and colonization [92]. In addition, TRPV6 can form stable membrane complexes with CXCR4, synergistically triggering downstream chemotactic and calcium signaling cascades to enhance cancer cell invasiveness, migratory capacity and bone tropism. Disruption of Transient Receptor Potential cation channel subfamily V member 6 (TRPV6)/CXCR4 complex assembly efficiently abrogates CXCL12-induced tumor bone homing and colonization, potently blocking bone metastasis progression [93]. Beyond mediating chemotaxis and colonization, this signaling axis is critically involved in early-stage immune evasion by pre-establishing local immune-excluded microenvironments. On one hand, CXCL12 directly restrains the infiltration and activation of effector immune cells, downregulating chemokine receptors on CD8⁺ T cells and NK cells to hinder their bone marrow recruitment and dampen local antitumor immune surveillance [94, 95]. On the other hand, CXCL12 preferentially recruits immunosuppressive populations such as MDSCs and Tregs via elevating their surface CXCR4 expression, accelerating their aggregation within pre-metastatic bone niches. These recruited immune suppressors secrete abundant inhibitory cytokines including IL-10 and TGF-β to blunt local immune responses in advance [96].

Osteoblasts facilitate cancer cell colonization in bone microenvironment

Osteoblasts are not merely passive soil for bone metastasis, but active modulators participating in CTC capture, cancer cell colonization and long-term survival maintenance of dormant tumor cells. In the early metastatic phase, breast cancer cells preferentially accumulate within sinusoidal vascular regions enriched with osteoblasts driven by CXCL12 and E-selectin [97]. Three-dimensional bioprinted co-culture models consisting of breast cancer cells, human umbilical vein endothelial cells and osteoblasts have validated that osteoblasts colocalize with tumor cells surrounding blood vessels, and osteoblast depletion evidently reduces metastatic colonization efficiency [98]. In vivo experiments further demonstrated that osteoblast abundance positively correlates with tumor proliferation, and co-injection of osteoblasts and breast cancer cells rapidly induces large tumor mass formation in mouse tibia [99]. Heterotypic adhesion formed by osteoblastic N-cadherin and cancer cell E-cadherin strengthens tumor cell attachment and survival within bone microenvironments [100, 101]. High Connexin 43 expression is detected at the contact interface between tumor cells and osteoblasts, which mediates directional calcium influx into cancer cells to promote stable colonization [102]. Moreover, The Signal peptide, CUB domain, EGF-like domain-containing protein 2 (SCUBE2), an ER-regulated secretory protein highly expressed in ER-positive luminal breast cancer, activates Hedgehog signaling via SHH secretion to modulate osteogenic differentiation, shaping immunosuppressive osteoblastic microenvironments that shield tumor cells from NK cell-mediated cytotoxicity and ultimately accelerate bone metastasis [103, 104].

Tumor dormancy: osteoblast-dominated quiescence maintenance and immune silencing

Most tumor cells colonized in bone marrow fail to proliferate into overt metastatic lesions immediately, but enter a dormant state as disseminated tumor cells (DTCs) arrested at G0 phase, characterized by low proliferative activity, enhanced anti-apoptotic capability and elevated stemness features. These dormant cells can remain latent in bone marrow for years or even decades, escaping chemotherapy, targeted therapy and immune surveillance, which serves as the fundamental cause of tumor late recurrence, therapeutic resistance and delayed clinical onset of bone metastasis. Osteoblasts and bone marrow stromal cells are architects and maintainers of dormant niches, which force tumor cells into quiescent status through specific signaling cascades [105]. This stage is featured by extensive immune silencing in bone microenvironments, manifested as low immunogenicity of dormant tumor cells and immune-suppressive microenvironmental conditions constructed by bone stromal cells, enabling DTCs to evade immune clearance and act as latent cellular seeds triggering late-onset bone metastasis after clinical disease remission.

Osteoblasts: central builders of tumor dormant niches

Dormant cancer cells are predominantly localized adjacent to type I collagen-expressing osteoblasts on endosteal surfaces, whereas proliferative tumor cells tend to distribute far away from bone surfaces [106]. Osteoblasts regulate tumor cell proliferation and induce quiescence via both direct cell-cell contact and paracrine factor secretion [107]. Synergistic activation of GAS6/AXL, TGF-β and CXCL12/CXCR4 pathways drives tumor cells to exit active cell cycles and sustain stable dormant phenotypes. Osteoblast-derived GAS6 binds to AXL receptors on tumor cell surface, suppressing proliferative signaling and reinforcing anti-apoptotic properties to maintain long-term G0 phase arrest of DTCs. In addition, TGF-β not only suppresses the proliferation of tumor cells, but also reinforces local immune tolerance, thereby jointly constructing a dormant microenvironment that enables tumor cells to achieve long-term latency and evade immune surveillance as well as therapeutic intervention.

The GAS6/AXL signaling axis acts as a pivotal molecular switch governing DTC dormancy maintenance, immune evasion and therapeutic resistance. GAS6 secreted by osteoblasts and bone marrow stromal cells binds to and activates AXL receptors on tumor cells, thereby restraining cancer cell proliferation and triggering cellular dormancy [108, 109]. Aberrant AXL activation is frequently detected in prostate cancer, which facilitates tumor cell invasion, migration, bone niche colonization and malignant proliferation. Pharmacological AXL blockade interrupts downstream pro-metastatic cascades, impairs cancer cell bone homing and colonization capacity, and halts the progression of bone metastatic lesions, offering a promising therapeutic target for targeted intervention against prostate cancer bone metastasis [110]. The GAS6/AXL axis is tightly linked to local immunosuppression. It upregulates B-cell lymphoma 2 (BCL-2) and Twist expression to mediate immune evasion, dampens inflammatory signaling, and reduces the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines [111–113]. Moreover, GAS6/AXL signaling elevates TGF-β2 levels within the bone microenvironment to further lock colonized tumor cells in a dormant state. Upon AXL knockout or pharmacological inhibition, TGF-β2 fails to efficiently arrest cell cycle progression and sustain the quiescent phenotype, consequently driving dormant cancer cells to exit latency and resume proliferation and invasion.

TGF-β serves as a pivotal dormancy-inducing factor for cancer cells in the bone microenvironment, which is predominantly secreted by bone matrix, osteoblasts and bone stromal cells, and can arrest the proliferation of bone-resident tumor cells and drive them into a quiescent dormant state. The expression of TGF-β is markedly upregulated during osteoblast differentiation. It binds to TGFβRIII receptors on cancer cells and activates the downstream p38MAPK pathway, further mediating the phosphorylation of RB protein at S249/T252 sites. This TGF-β-related signaling cascade blocks cell cycle progression, suppresses tumor cell proliferation, induces dormancy of cancer cells colonized in bone tissues, and modulates the progression of bone micrometastasis [114]. Emerging studies have demonstrated that TGF-β triggers a unique atypical epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) phenotype. Instead of promoting malignant proliferation and distant dissemination of tumor cells, it arrests cell cycle progression and inhibits cell division, enabling circulating tumor cells residing in distant niches such as bone to maintain long-term dormancy. Meanwhile, this atypical EMT remodels cellular mechanical properties, allowing dormant metastatic cells to escape mechanical recognition and elimination by immune cells, and thereby sustaining an occult dormant microenvironment [114]. In the bone marrow, NG2/Nestin-positive mesenchymal stem cells abundantly secrete TGF-β2. Via paracrine action, TGF-β2 acts on DTCs colonized in the bone marrow to block cell cycle transition, restrain cell proliferation and malignant phenotypic activation, and ultimately induce and stabilize tumor cell dormancy [115].

Immune-mediated regulatory mechanisms for dormancy maintenance

Osteoblasts and bone marrow stromal cells synergistically construct a dormant niche microenvironment with low immune attack, which is characterized by low infiltration of CD8⁺ T cells and NK cells, high enrichment of MDSCs and Tregs, and elevated adenosine concentration. The bone metastatic microenvironment is persistently hypoxic, which markedly upregulates the surface expression of CD39 and CD73 on tumor cells, osteoblasts and various immune cells, and gradually hydrolyzes abundant extracellular Adenosine triphosphate (ATP) released in the microenvironment into adenosine [116]. The CD39/CD73/adenosine pathway serves as the core executive mechanism of immune silencing during tumor dormancy. It facilitates tumor immune evasion via immunosuppression, directly drives EMT, angiogenesis and stromal remodeling, and interacts synergistically with hypoxic, acidic and drug-resistant microenvironments [117–119]. CD39 and CD73 rapidly degrade local ATP into adenosine, which binds to A2A receptors on immune cells to potently suppress the activation, proliferation and cytotoxicity of CD8⁺ T cells and NK cells, inhibit DC maturation and antigen presentation, and promote the differentiation of Tregs and M2-type tumor-associated macrophages (M2-TAMs), ultimately establishing profound immunosuppression [120]. Adenosine remodels the bone microenvironment through A3 receptors to facilitate tumor cell adhesion and colonization and induce local immunosuppression. High adenosine expression is closely correlated with shortened OS and distant metastasis free survival in patients with ER-positive breast cancer [121]. Immune cells infiltrated in the bone metastatic microenvironment can concurrently highly express TIGIT and CD39/CD73 molecules. Among them, CD39/CD73 mediates the conversion of extracellular ATP to adenosine to form a purinergic immunosuppressive microenvironment and impair the cytotoxic function of immune cells, while the TIGIT axis further induces immune cell exhaustion. These two pathways jointly amplify local immunosuppression [122]. Meanwhile, the CD73/adenosine pathway is cascaded with the GAS6/AXL pathway. High CD73 expression in cancer cells catalyzes adenosine production to directly activate the AXL signaling pathway. Activated AXL signaling enhances the invasive and migratory capacities of tumor cells, triggers epithelial-mesenchymal transition, and accelerates tumor distant metastasis [123]. In addition, in breast cancer bone metastasis, SCUBE2 modulates the differentiation of osteoprogenitor cells to establish an immunosuppressive osteoblastic microenvironment, prompting osteoblasts to secrete large amounts of type I collagen (COL1). The COL1-LAIR1-SHP1 axis further inhibits NK cell activation and enables tumor cells to escape immune surveillance [103].

Reactivation: osteoclast-dominated vicious cycle of tumor awakening, invasion and bone destruction

The reactivation of dormant DTCs represents a decisive turning point converting occult micrometastasis into clinically overt bone metastasis, and also marks the onset of skeletal-related events including bone pain and pathological fractures as well as pan-resistance to immunotherapy. This process is primarily triggered by excessive osteoclast activation, accompanied by comprehensive overactivation of immunosuppressive pathways and dysregulated crosstalk between bone stroma and immune cells. These pathological alterations drive explosive tumor proliferation and form a self-amplifying vicious loop consisting of osteoclast hyperactivation, progressive bone destruction, unrestrained tumor growth and further aggravated osteolysis, ultimately transforming bone microenvironments into typical immune cold niches [124]. Under local microenvironmental stimuli, tumor cells, Tregs and bone marrow stromal cells aberrantly activate osteoclast differentiation, maturation and bone resorptive functions via the RANK/RANKL axis, initiating osteolytic bone destruction and releasing massive growth factors sequestered in bone matrix including TGF-β, IGF-1, FGF and PDGF to initiate irreversible positive feedback loops. During dormant cell reactivation, bone stroma-driven immunosuppressive pathways represented by RANK/RANKL and Siglec-15/sialic acid are synchronously activated and undergo cascade amplification, exerting dual biological effects on bone destruction and immune suppression, which profoundly transform into immune cold/desert-like niches and drastically compromise the therapeutic efficacy of ICIs [125].

The RANK/RANKL axis is essential for osteoclastogenesis and skeletal homeostasis, which contributes to immune desert microenvironment formation in bone metastatic lesions via direct suppression of antitumor immunity, myeloid cell reprogramming and immunosuppressive metabolite secretion [125]. Aberrant RANKL overexpression in tumor microenvironments is positively correlated with intralesional densities of Tregs and M2-like macrophages. Excessive RANKL continuously activates RANK receptors on DC especially plasmacytoid DC, driving them toward immune-tolerant phenotypes with impaired antigen-presenting capacity and reduced pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion, which further suppresses effector immune cell activation and establishes systemic and local immune tolerance to facilitate tumor progression [126]. Subsets of T cells are also capable of abundant RANKL secretion to disrupt skeletal homeostasis and construct metastasis-permissive bone microenvironments accompanied by local immune disturbance [127]. Pan-cancer single-cell bone metastasis atlases have identified a unique MMP19⁺RANK⁺TAM subset with potent osteoclastogenic activity, which accelerates bone matrix degradation and creates favorable microenvironments for tumor colonization and invasion. These specific TAMs encircle tumor nests in a ring-shaped pattern to form physical barriers that block CD8⁺T cell infiltration into tumor parenchyma and inhibit intralesional T cell activation and proliferation, consolidating robust local immune suppression [63]. Denosumab specifically binds to both soluble and membrane-bound RANKL to interrupt RANKL-RANK signaling, which effectively remodels the polarization status of this macrophage subset at transcriptional and functional levels, restrains M2-like polarization and reduces MMP19⁺RANK⁺TAM abundance. This intervention markedly downregulates immunosuppressive mediator release from macrophages, relieves functional inhibition on effector immune cells, restores cytotoxic T cell tumoricidal activity and optimizes immune microenvironments within bone metastatic lesions to achieve prominent immune sensitization effects [63, 128]. Additionally, denosumab efficiently suppresses SPP1 expression at both transcriptional and translational levels in tumor and stromal compartments, blocking SPP1-mediated tumor proliferation, invasion and immunosuppressive signaling cascades and reducing the recruitment of immunosuppressive immune populations. Meanwhile, SPP1 inhibition reverses its suppressive effects on T cell functions, facilitating massive infiltration and activation of cytotoxic CD8⁺ T cells and elevating the secretion levels of cytolytic molecules such as granzyme and perforin to reinforce tumor-specific immune killing capacity [129].

The sialic acid-binding immunoglobulin-like lectin (Siglec) family represents essential immune regulatory molecules, and the axis formed by Siglecs and sialic acid is the core pathway of osteoclast-mediated immune suppression. As a newly reported immune checkpoint, Siglec-15 is upregulated in multiple types of cancer cells and tumor-infiltrating myeloid cells, and its expression is mutually exclusive with PD-L1 [130]. In breast cancer bone metastasis, the Siglec-15/sialic acid axis, as a core glyco-immune checkpoint, is abnormally highly expressed on osteoclasts. Its combination with sialic acid activates downstream signaling pathways and promotes the formation of osteoclast-mediated immunosuppressive microenvironment [131]. Apoptotic bodies generated by osteoclast apoptosis highly express surface Siglec-15, which can bind to sialylated TLR2 on naive CD8⁺ T cells, block the transduction of downstream co-stimulation signals and inhibit CD8⁺ T cell activation. The impaired activation directly weakens the body’s ability to eliminate tumor cells [132]. In addition, sialylation of TLR2 initiates osteoclast fusion and facilitates the formation of mature osteoclasts. Mature osteoclasts further strengthen the immunosuppressive microenvironment and restrict the function of immune cells by persistently overexpressing Siglec-15, secreting inhibitory cytokines and releasing apoptotic bodies [133]. Anti-Siglec-15 antibodies can specifically bind to Siglec-15 on osteoclasts to block relevant immune suppression, and simultaneously increase the secretion of TRAP⁺ monocytes and PDGF-BB to promote bone formation [134]. In lung cancer bone metastasis, Siglec-15 facilitates tumor progression, and its expression in osteoclasts is positively correlated with spinal metastasis. Abnormally high expression of Siglec-15 on osteoclasts activates immunosuppressive signals by binding sialic acid molecules, mediates the construction of immunosuppressive microenvironment and creates conditions for tumor spinal metastasis. Meanwhile, it directly suppresses the cytotoxic function of immune cells such as CD8⁺ T cells and macrophages, and reduces the efficacy of immunochemotherapy [135]. Siglec-15-targeted hollow nanoplatforms can specifically bind to Siglec-15 on osteoclasts, block the binding between Siglec-15 and sialic acid as well as downstream immunosuppressive signal transmission, and realize simultaneous immunochemotherapy and osteolysis inhibition so as to restore the killing activity of immune cells [136]. In prostate cancer bone metastasis, osteoclasts indirectly inhibit immune function and promote metastasis through Siglec-15, Siglec-7 and sialic acid-related pathways. Blocking the binding of sialic acid molecules on osteoclasts to Siglec-15 on immune cells can relieve osteoclast-mediated immune suppression, restore the capacity of immune cells to clear tumor cells, and create favorable conditions for the exertion of immunotherapy efficacy [137, 138]. Siglec-15-targeted bioactive hydrogels can specifically act on Siglec-15 on osteoclasts, inhibit its expression and downstream immunosuppressive pathways, exert the effects of osteolysis inhibition and immune regulation, alleviate immune suppression induced by osteoclasts and improve the efficacy of immunotherapy [139].

Bone metastatic lesions as core drivers mediating systemic immunotherapy resistance

Bone metastasis facilitates secondary systemic dissemination of tumor cells

Bone represents one of the most prevalent distant metastatic sites for malignant tumors, yet it is not the ultimate destination of disseminated cancer cells. Bone microenvironmental components including osteoblasts, osteoclasts and resident immune cells endow bone-residing tumor cells with enhanced potential to spread secondarily to visceral organs such as lung, liver and brain [140]. Bone microenvironmental cues induce Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) upregulation in HR-positive/HER2-negative breast cancer cells via autocrine NRG3 signaling, which augments tumor invasive and migratory properties and promotes the generation of HER2-positive circulating tumor cells capable of escaping bone marrow niches and colonizing distant visceral organs, resulting in multi-organ secondary metastasis [141]. In vivo clonal lineage tracing via genetic barcoding confirmed that bone microenvironments transiently downregulate ER expression in ER-positive breast cancer cells, rendering bone metastatic lesions as critical transfer stations for further distant metastasis. Clinical statistics revealed that over two-thirds of patients initially diagnosed with isolated bone metastasis subsequently develop synchronous metastases in other visceral organs [141, 142].

Both bone microenvironment-derived systemic immunosuppression and tumor secondary dissemination are tightly regulated by EZH2 (Enhancer of zeste homolog 2), a pivotal epigenetic modifier functioning as histone methyltransferase to catalyze H3K27 trimethylation and mediate target gene silencing [141, 143]. EZH2 is a critical epigenetic regulator. As a histone methyltransferase and the catalytic core of the Polycomb Repressive Complex 2 (PRC2), it catalyzes the trimethylation of histone H3 at lysine 27 (H3K27me3), leading to the transcriptional silencing of target genes. EZH2 is aberrantly overexpressed in the bone metastasis microenvironment of multiple cancer types [144, 145]. Osteoblast-derived calcium influx via Connexin 43 suppresses ER transcriptional activity while activating PDGFRβ and FGF2 signaling cascades, driving cancer cell transition toward basal/stem-like phenotypes. This epigenetic reprogramming is predominantly mediated by EZH2-dependent H3K27me3 accumulation, which confers cancer cells with stemness traits and high phenotypic plasticity to break original clonal restriction and accomplish secondary metastatic dissemination [141, 143]. Mechanistically, EZH2 interacts with TGF-β signaling to transcriptionally induce ITGB1 expression and activate downstream integrin β1-FAK pathways, which not only promotes breast cancer bone colonization and osteolytic lesion formation via facilitating osteoclast maturation and establishing metastatic vicious cycles, but also modulates osteoblastic immunosuppressive functions to indirectly inhibit intralesional T cell activity and potentiate metastatic cell motility, ultimately accelerating secondary tumor spread and attenuating immunotherapeutic responsiveness [143, 146]. Aberrant EZH2 expression also mediates immunotherapy resistance in multiple tumor types: in prostate cancer, EZH2 inhibits the dsRNA-STING-interferon innate immune axis to blunt responses to PD-1 blockade [147]. In NSCLC, both tumor-intrinsic and osteoblast-derived EZH2 synergistically suppress dsRNA-interferon signaling cascades, impairing antigen presentation and T cell activation to compromise PD-1 inhibitor efficacy, whereas pharmacological EZH2 blockade effectively reverses this immunosuppressive status and restores immunotherapeutic sensitivity [148].

Bone metastasis acts as a systemic driver of pan-resistance to immunotherapy

Bone metastasis is not only a localized lesion but also a key contributor to systemic immunosuppression. It induces and maintains widespread primary and secondary resistance to immunotherapy through bone microenvironmental signal spreading, bone marrow immune remodeling and disrupted systemic immune homeostasis, serving as one of the distal driving mechanisms of immunotherapy failure in solid tumors [149]. Bone metastasis establishes comprehensive systemic immunosuppressive networks through dual pathways: on one hand, RANKL/RANK axis-mediated osteoclast hyperactivation broadly impairs the effector functions of CD8⁺ cytotoxic T cells and NK cells and blocks endogenous immune activation cascades; on the other hand, tumor-educated bone marrow hematopoietic niches are aberrantly remodeled to drive systemic MDSC expansion and infiltration, resulting in predominant immune cold tumor phenotypes across multiple organs [45]. In addition, osteoclast-mediated bone matrix resorption releases abundant soluble immunosuppressive mediators including TGF-β and adenosine to further paralyze systemic immune cell functions and upregulate tumor immune escape signatures, contributing to broad immunotherapy resistance.

In terms of osteoclast activation via the RANKL/RANK axis, circulating RANKL levels effectively predict therapeutic responses to ICIs in cancer patients. High in vivo RANKL concentrations induce the enrichment of immunosuppressive cells in the bone marrow and tumor microenvironment, inhibit effector T cell infiltration and activation, exacerbate immune exhaustion, and thereby impair immunotherapy efficacy [150]. Under tumor cell induction, osteoclasts exhibit upregulated expression of the Spp1 gene encoding osteopontin (OPN), secreting large amounts of OPN into the bloodstream. This remotely reprograms the extramedullary tumor microenvironment and suppresses the proliferation and differentiation of key CD8+ TCF1+ progenitor exhausted T cells (Tpex)—a cell population critical for ICIs efficacy, whose dysfunction directly leads to immunotherapy failure. Meanwhile, OPN inhibits the expression of genes related to T cell recruitment and interferon response, further weakening immune killing function. The RANKL inhibitor denosumab can suppress osteoclast formation and downregulate OPN expression. Clinical studies have verified that RANKL serves as an independent unfavorable prognostic factor for PFS and OS, and the combination of RANKL inhibitors and ICIs can markedly elevate Disease Control Rate (DCR) and prolong PFS [11, 151]. Studies have shown that dual blockade of RANKL and PD-1 can significantly improve the survival prognosis of patients with KRAS-mutant advanced lung adenocarcinoma. KRAS mutations drive robust RANKL secretion in tumor cells, which not only activates osteoclasts to trigger bone destruction and facilitate tumor invasion and metastasis, but also induces an immunosuppressive microenvironment to diminish the therapeutic effect of PD-1 inhibitors. In combined treatment, RANKL inhibitors normalize abnormal bone remodeling, restrain tumor distant metastasis and remodel local immune microenvironment, while PD-1 monoclonal antibodies reverse T cell exhaustion and restore potent anti-tumor immune cytotoxicity [152].

Bone marrow-derived PMN-MDSCs undergo robust expansion induced by cancer-educated mesenchymal stromal cells, which support tumor cell survival at both primary and distant metastatic sites synchronously [51]. A unique population of myeloid-tumor chimeric cells co-expressing tumor and myeloid lineage markers has been identified within bone metastatic bone marrow, presumably originating from cell fusion events between cancer cells and bone marrow resident cells. These chimeric cells induce massive accumulation of neutrophils, monocytes and macrophages with potent immunosuppressive phenotypes, accelerating primary tumor growth and facilitating tumor cell dissemination from bone lesions to pulmonary and other distant organs [153]. Moreover, systemic solid tumor signals aberrantly activate bone marrow osteoprogenitors, which drive excessive proliferation and functional reprogramming of granulocyte-monocyte progenitors to generate large quantities of immunosuppressive myeloid derivatives, thereby establishing pervasive systemic immunosuppressive microenvironments and sustaining long-term immune dysfunction even after primary tumor resection [154]. In terms of TGF-β, bone marrow stromal cells, osteogenic lineage cells and infiltrating tumor cells secrete high levels of immunosuppressive cytokines including TGF-β and IL-10. These cytokines not only recruit peripheral immune cells to migrate and accumulate in the bone marrow, but also directly induce naive T cells to differentiate into Treg lineage, thereby driving remarkable expansion of intramedullary Tregs. Abundantly expanded Tregs establish potent local immunosuppression, inhibit the activation and cytotoxicity of anti-tumor immune cells such as effector T cells and NK cells, disrupt systemic immune homeostasis, and construct an immune-privileged niche favorable for tumor cell dormancy, colonization and proliferation [155]. Relevant studies have confirmed that tumor cells within bone metastatic lesions promote osteoclast-mediated bone resorption and trigger massive TGF-β release. This cytokine markedly blocks the differentiation of naive CD4⁺ T cells into anti-tumor Th1 cells and reduces the secretion of pro-inflammatory effector molecules such as interferon-γ, directly impairing tumor-specific cellular immunity. Meanwhile, TGF-β potently drives T cell polarization toward immunosuppressive subsets including Tregs and Th17 cells, expands inhibitory immune populations and gradually establishes a stable tumor immunosuppressive microenvironment, which ultimately leads to poor systemic therapeutic efficacy of ICIs [79, 156]. TGF-β shapes tumor microenvironments by restricting T cell infiltration and desensitizes tumors to PD-L1 blockade. Combined administration of TGF-β neutralizing antibodies and anti-PD-L1 agents efficiently attenuates stromal TGF-β signaling, facilitates deep T cell infiltration into tumor parenchyma, and elicits robust anti-tumor immunity as well as prominent tumor regression [157].

Bone metastatic vasculature-mediated immunosuppression: the “Barrier and Trap” of immune responses

Bone marrow cavity capillaries include type H vessels highly expressing endomucin (EMCN) and platelet endothelial cell adhesion molecule-1 (PECAM-1/CD31), and type L vessels with low expression of CD31 and EMCN [158, 159], among which type H vessels are interconnected by metaphyseal vascular rings or arches [160]; tumor cells preferentially colonize the metaphyseal region rich in type H vascular structures, remodel the local vasculature by sprouting, and the expression of CD31 and EMCN is significantly increased [101, 115, 161]. The bone vascular system is regarded as a “nutrient transport channel” between tumor cells and bone tissue, often accompanied by abnormal vascular remodeling such as structural disorder and dysfunction, which can directly inhibit the body’s anti-tumor immune response, hinder the penetration of immunotherapeutic drugs, and form a crosstalk regulatory network with bone matrix, metabolic microenvironment and immune cells, constituting a vascular-immunosuppression-bone metastasis interaction axis [46, 162] (Fig. 3).

The physical barrier formed by abnormal vascular remodeling is an important link inhibiting immune responses and hindering immunotherapy in the BME. In bone metastatic lesions, the interaction between tumor cells and bone matrix cells induces pathological remodeling of the vascular system [163]. This abnormal vascular network is characterized by incomplete vascular walls, loose endothelial cell junctions, and narrow and twisted lumens. On the one hand, it cannot provide an effective infiltration channel for immune cells, making it difficult for peripheral immune cells to cross the vascular wall into the core area of bone metastatic lesions; on the other hand, the disordered vascular structure forms a physical barrier, hindering the precise delivery of immunotherapeutic drugs to tumor cells, reducing local drug concentration and weakening immunotherapy efficacy [98, 161, 164]. In addition, the hypoxic microenvironment in bone metastatic lesions further aggravates abnormal vascular remodeling. Under hypoxia, vascular endothelial cell proliferation is disordered and vascular maturity is reduced, further strengthening the physical barrier effect and forming a vicious cycle of “hypoxia-abnormal vascular-immunosuppression“ [165], and this hypoxic activity difference is obvious in bone metastasis of different subtypes of breast cancer. The bone morphology and hypoxia level of ER + and ER− breast cancer bone metastatic lesions are different, and their vascular remodeling degree and immunosuppressive effect are also different [166].

Abnormal expression of immunosuppressive factors by vascular endothelial cells strengthens the immunosuppressive state. In bone metastatic lesions, abnormally activated vascular endothelial cells can secrete a large number of immunosuppressive molecules, including PD-L1, TGF-β, IL-10, etc., directly inhibiting the activation, proliferation and killing function of immune cells [167]. Among them, the expression of PD-L1 on the surface of vascular endothelial cells is particularly critical, which can bind to PD-1 on the surface of immune cells, induce immune cell apoptosis or exhaustion, inhibit the cytotoxicity of CD8+ T cells, and promote Treg proliferation to aggravate immunosuppression [168]. VEGF, as the core regulatory factor of angiogenesis, plays a dual role in bone metastatic vascular remodeling and immunosuppression. It can not only induce vascular endothelial cell proliferation and promote neovascularization, but also directly inhibit DC maturation, weaken their antigen-presenting ability, and inhibit CD8+T cell infiltration and activation [169]; the serum levels of VEGF2 and Prostate-Specific Antigen (PSA) in patients with prostate cancer bone metastasis can be used as prognostic markers, and their high expression is closely related to abnormal vascular remodeling, immunosuppression and bone metastasis progression [170]. In addition, E-selectin in the bone vascular niche promotes tumor cell colonization and bone metastasis by inducing mesenchymal-epithelial transition of tumor cells and activating the Wnt signaling pathway, while regulating immune cell adhesion and infiltration to strengthen the immunosuppressive effect [171].

Cell-cell interactions mediated by abnormal vascular remodeling amplify the immunosuppressive effect, promote the vicious cycle of the vascular-immunosuppression-bone metastasis interaction axis, and aggravate immunotherapy resistance. In the BME, abnormal vascular endothelial cells form a complex cell interaction network with tumor cells, immune cells, bone matrix cells and MDSCs, etc., jointly maintaining the immunosuppressive state [50]. In the cell interaction network, MDSCs can be recruited to bone metastatic lesions by factors such as VEGF and TGF-β secreted by abnormal blood vessels, inhibiting CD8+T cell activity and promoting Treg cell proliferation; meanwhile, abnormal blood vessels provide a stable survival microenvironment for MDSCs, forming a positive feedback of “abnormal vascular-MDSCs infiltration-immunosuppression“ [172]. Direct interaction between abnormal vascular endothelial cells and tumor cells also plays a key role. The Ephrin-B2-EphB4 signaling pathway can mediate the adhesion and communication between tumor cells and endothelial cells, promote tumor cell hematogenous metastasis to spinal bone, and this pathway can regulate endothelial cells to secrete immunosuppressive factors [173], and regulating this signaling axis can effectively reduce the incidence of bone metastasis in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma and melanoma [174, 175]. In addition, vascular endothelial cells can put some tumor cells into a dormant state by secreting angiocrine signals, escaping the killing of immune cells and immunotherapeutic drugs, and reactivating after the vascular microenvironment is optimized, leading to bone metastasis recurrence and immunotherapy resistance [176, 177]; meanwhile, vascular endothelial cells closely interact with bone matrix cells. Endothelial-to-osteoblast conversion maintains bone homeostasis, and abnormal conversion in bone metastasis disrupts bone metabolic balance, while affecting vascular remodeling and immunosuppressive state [178, 179].

Abnormal vascular remodeling in the BME interacts with bone matrix destruction, metabolic disorders, neural regulation and other factors to jointly mediate immunosuppression. Hypoxic microenvironment caused by abnormal vascular remodeling accompanied by lactic acid accumulation not only promotes tumor cells to secrete osteoclast activating factors, accelerates bone resorption and bone matrix destruction, but also induces immune cell dysfunction [165]; growth factors such as TGF-β released by bone matrix destruction can transactivate vascular endothelial cells, promote vascular remodeling and secretion of immunosuppressive factors [179]. In addition, abnormal vascular remodeling regulates the neuro-immune network to affect immunotherapy efficacy. For example, the VEGF-A/VEGFR2 signaling pathway participates in spinal neuron regulation, mediates bone cancer pain, and inhibits immune cell function, forming a crosstalk inhibitory network of “vascular-neural-immune“ [180]. Microfluidic model studies confirm that vascularized BME can regulate the interaction between neutrophils and tumor cells, and increased neutrophil infiltration further strengthens immunosuppression and promotes tumor cell invasion [181].

Targeting abnormal vascular remodeling has become an important strategy to optimize bone metastasis immunotherapy. Studies show that anti-angiogenic therapy can promote immune cell infiltration and immunotherapeutic drug delivery by inhibiting abnormal vascular remodeling and destroying physical barriers, and combined use with immunotherapy can significantly improve the efficacy of bone metastasis treatment [182]. Studies using synchrotron radiation Micro-CT technology confirm that anti-angiogenic therapy can significantly improve abnormal vascularization of breast cancer bone metastasis, reduce bone destruction, and enhance immune cell infiltration [183]. In addition, targeting key molecules in the vascular niche (such as E-selectin, Ephrin-B2-EphB4 signaling axis), regulating angiocrine signals, or using bioactive metabolites of traditional Chinese herbal medicines to target type H vessels can provide new directions for bone metastasis treatment by improving abnormal vascular remodeling and reversing immunosuppression [171, 184].

Crosstalk regulation between metabolic abnormalities and immunosuppression in bone metastasis: the “Indirect Weakener” of immune responses

Metabolic abnormalities in bone metastasis (lipid metabolism, ferroptosis, glucose metabolism) form a crosstalk regulatory loop with immunosuppression, bone matrix destruction and abnormal blood vessels, indirectly weakening immune cell function and strengthening the immunosuppressive microenvironment through the dual pathways of “energy deprivation + metabolite inhibition“ [185–187] (Fig. 4).

Synergistic inhibitory effect of abnormal lipid metabolism

Lipid metabolic reprogramming, as an important feature of malignant tumor progression, plays a key regulatory role in the process of bone metastasis. Lipid metabolism jointly promotes the occurrence and development of bone metastasis through multi-level mechanisms such as regulating the expression of key lipid molecules, mediating crosstalk between BMAds and tumor cells, and inhibiting immune function of the bone microenvironment, and the mechanisms are interrelated and synergistic.

Abnormal expression of key lipid metabolism molecules promotes bone metastasis. Acyl-CoA-binding protein (ACBP), an important regulator of lipid metabolism, has been confirmed as a key driver of bone metastasis through in vivo CRISPR activation screening [188], regulating lipid transport and intracellular accumulation, enhancing the migration and invasion ability of tumor cells, and inducing osteoclast activation in the bone microenvironment to destroy bone homeostasis. Meanwhile, as an autophagy checkpoint, it can weaken cancer immune surveillance by regulating the interaction between lipid metabolism and autophagy, indirectly promoting the progression of bone metastasis [189]. Sterol regulatory element-binding protein 2 (SREBP-2), a key transcription factor for lipid synthesis, can aggravate breast cancer-related osteolysis by promoting osteoclastogenesis and directly drive breast cancer cell metastasis [190]; stearoyl-CoA desaturase (SCD)-mediated abnormal lipid metabolism is involved in the regulation of prostate cancer bone metastasis, and inhibition of SCD can significantly inhibit prostate cancer bone metastasis by regulating cellular stress, mTOR signaling pathway and DNA damage response [191]. In addition, Phospholipase A2 Group VII can interfere with bone homeostasis by regulating the Alox12/12-HETE/Gpr31 signaling axis, in which 12-HETE, as a lipid metabolite, further mediates crosstalk between tumor cells and the bone microenvironment and promotes bone metastasis [192].

Lipid metabolic disorders mediated by bone marrow adipocytes (BMAds) provide microenvironmental support for tumor bone metastasis. As important lipid source cells in the bone microenvironment, BMAd-driven abnormal fatty acid metabolism can lead to drug resistance in lung cancer bone metastasis, while directly promoting the colonization and proliferation of tumor cells in the bone microenvironment [193]. Palmitic acid, a key lipid molecule secreted by BMAds, mediates crosstalk between BMAds and prostate cancer cells by activating the G protein-coupled receptors (GPRs)/Krüppel-like factor 7 (KLF7)/C-C motif chemokine ligand2 (CCL2) pathway, accelerating the process of prostate cancer bone metastasis [194]; abnormal expression of ESRRA in adipocytes can inhibit osteogenesis by regulating lipid metabolism-related secretory factors, remodel the bone microenvironment rich in adipocytes, indirectly promote tumor bone metastasis, and targeting adipocyte ESRRA can reverse this process [195, 196]. In addition, Compound Kushen Injection can correct lipid metabolic disorders by dual targeting lipid production and polyunsaturated fatty acid homeostasis, effectively inhibiting breast cancer bone metastasis, which counter-proves the key role of abnormal lipid metabolism in breast cancer bone metastasis [197].

Abnormal lipid metabolism can significantly inhibit the immune function of the bone microenvironment, form an immunosuppressive microenvironment, and accelerate tumor bone metastasis. Studies by Feng Y et al. confirm that abnormal lipid metabolism can significantly reshape the immune landscape of bone metastasis. Abnormal lipid metabolism is in an unbalanced state of hyperanabolism and inhibited catabolism, and a large number of accumulated lipid mediators can regulate the metabolic phenotype of immune cells. On the one hand, it inhibits the activation and killing function of effector CD8+T cells and NK cells, reducing their infiltration in bone metastatic lesions; on the other hand, it promotes the infiltration of MDSCs and M2-type macrophages, and upregulates the expression of immune checkpoint molecules such as PD-L1, mediating immune cell inactivation and providing conditions for tumor cells to escape immune clearance [198]. RORγ, a key linker molecule between lipid metabolism and immunosuppression, can bridge cancer-driven lipid metabolic disorders and myeloid immunosuppression, leading to abnormal activation of myeloid suppressor cell function, inhibiting the function of effector immune cells such as T cells, and weakening the anti-tumor immune ability of the bone microenvironment [199]. ACBP, as an autophagy checkpoint, can inhibit the activation and proliferation of immune cells by regulating autophagy mediated by lipid metabolism, reducing cancer immune surveillance ability and providing conditions for tumor bone metastasis to escape immune clearance [189]. In addition, the interaction between adipocytes and immune cells in the bone microenvironment depends on lipid metabolism regulation, and abnormal lipid metabolism can mediate crosstalk between the two, promote immunosuppressive cell infiltration, inhibit effector immune cell function, and further affect tumor malignant progression and therapeutic response [200, 201].

Crosstalk between ferroptosis and immunosuppression

Iron, an essential trace element for the body, participates in various physiological processes such as cell proliferation and oxidative stress regulation. Its metabolic imbalance plays an important regulatory role in the process of bone metastasis, not only promoting tumor colonization and metastasis by regulating the bone microenvironment, but also significantly inhibiting the anti-tumor immune response of the bone microenvironment.

Abnormal iron metabolism directly promotes tumor bone metastasis by regulating the function of bone microenvironmental cells, inducing cell ferroptosis and metabolic reprogramming. Tumor cells can specifically hijack macrophage subsets marked by VCAM1⁺CD163⁺CCR3⁺ in the erythroblastic island in the bone marrow, making them release stored iron to tumor cells, thereby adapting to the hypoxic bone marrow environment, avoiding hypoxia-induced cell death, and meeting the energy needs of their own rapid proliferation and invasion. Meanwhile, iron metabolic imbalance further inhibits osteoblast activity, disrupts the balance between osteogenesis and osteoclastogenesis, and disorders bone microenvironmental homeostasis [61]. As key cells of the bone microenvironment, the lysosome-iron-mitochondria axis of osteoclasts takes iron as the core regulatory node. Abnormal function of this axis leads to abnormally elevated osteoclast activity, accelerates bone resorption, destroys bone tissue integrity, and provides “soil” for tumor cell colonization [202, 203]. In melanoma, bone metastasis can induce osteocyte ferroptosis through the HIF1α-HMOX1 axis, and ferroptosis, a form of cell death driven by iron-dependent lipid peroxidation, abnormally destroys bone tissue structure and releases pro-metastatic factors to accelerate tumor spread [204]. In breast cancer, Proline Dehydrogenase (Oxidase) 2 (PRODH2)-mediated metabolic reprogramming of the bone microenvironment is closely related to abnormal iron metabolism, which can promote breast cancer cell bone metastasis by regulating iron-dependent metabolic processes [205]; abnormal Nur77-PPARγ interaction can regulate iron metabolism-related pathways, indirectly promoting breast cancer progression and bone metastasis potential [206]. In prostate cancer, myeloid-like tumor hybrid cells in the bone marrow can provide support for prostate cancer cell bone metastasis by regulating iron metabolism homeostasis and changing iron distribution in the bone microenvironment [153]. In addition, abnormal ferroptosis mediated by ferritinophagy is also involved in the regulation of bone metastasis, and enhancing ferritinophagy-mediated ferroptosis can inhibit bone metastasis, which counter-proves that insufficient ferroptosis caused by abnormal iron metabolism promotes tumor bone metastasis [207, 208].

Abnormal iron metabolism also inhibits the immune function of the bone microenvironment through a variety of pathways, promoting tumor immune escape. CD63+ macrophage subsets have iron metabolic disorders, and abnormal expression of iron transport-related proteins leads to iron homeostasis imbalance. Excessive iron accumulation in cells inhibits the antigen-presenting ability and pro-inflammatory function of this subset, impairs its anti-tumor activity, cannot effectively activate the body’s effector immune cells, and indirectly promotes the progression of tumor bone metastasis [62]. DCs, as key antigen-presenting cells of immune responses, have impaired function that directly weakens anti-tumor immunity. Extracellular Glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4, a core regulator of ferroptosis) can inhibit DC function by binding to DC ZP3 receptors, indirectly inhibiting anti-tumor immune responses. GPX4 function is closely related to iron metabolism, and iron metabolic imbalance leads to abnormal GPX4 expression, further aggravating immunosuppression [209]. In addition, abnormal iron metabolism of immune cells can induce their own ferroptosis, leading to a decrease in effector immune cells such as CD8+T cells, while enhancing the immunosuppressive phenotype of Tregs and weakening the anti-tumor immune function of the bone microenvironment [210].

Auxiliary inhibition of abnormal glucose metabolism

As the core of energy metabolism in tumor cells, aberrant reprogramming of glucose metabolism (e.g., excessive glucose utilization, lactic acid accumulation) promotes tumor bone metastasis by regulating bone microenvironment homeostasis and mediating cellular metabolic crosstalk, while attenuating anti-tumor immunity by inducing immune escape and suppressing immune cell function [211].

Aberrant glucose metabolic crosstalk among various cell types in the bone microenvironment accelerates tumor bone metastasis. Hyperglycemia reshapes the metabolic homeostasis and immune microenvironment of bone tissue by upregulating the expression of prometastatic factors and inhibiting the activity of antimetastatic molecules in the bone pre-metastatic niche, weakening the defensive capacity of bone tissue and providing favorable conditions for the early colonization, adhesion and proliferation of breast cancer cells [212, 213]. As a critical regulator of systemic energy metabolism, abnormal bone glucose metabolism disrupts bone microenvironment homeostasis and lays the foundation for tumor bone metastasis, a regulatory role that has long been overlooked [214–216]. As a key component of the bone microenvironment, abnormal glucose metabolism in osteoblasts alters their functional phenotype and provides nutritional support and colonization conditions for tumor cells [217]; glucose metabolism-dependent crosstalk exists between osteoclasts and tumor cells, which not only confers resistance to Poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors in tumor cells but also accelerates bone resorption, disrupts bone tissue integrity and promotes breast cancer bone metastasis [218].

Abnormal glucose metabolism suppresses the anti-tumor immune function of the bone microenvironment through multiple pathways, among which aberrant lactic acid metabolism acts as the core regulatory factor. The acidic microenvironment directly promotes bone lysis by inhibiting osteoblast function and activating osteoclasts, and suppresses immune cell activity to form an “immune-privileged” niche [185, 219]. SLC2A3 mediates transmembrane lactic acid transport, and its abnormally high expression induces lactic acid accumulation in cancer cells, which further triggers lactylation modification of P53, inhibits the activity of effector immune cells such as CD8+ T cells and establishes an immunosuppressive microenvironment, thereby enabling tumor immune escape [73]; meanwhile, lactic acid mediates crosstalk between chemokines and glycolytic pathways to remodel the solid tumor microenvironment and indirectly accelerate tumor bone metastasis [220]. In addition, tumor glucose metabolic reprogramming directly induces T-cell exhaustion, reduces effector immune cell activity and disrupts bone repair homeostasis [221]; glucose restriction reshapes the innate immune landscape of the pre-metastatic niche via exosomal TNF-related apoptosis-inducing ligand (TRAIL), indirectly creating immune-tolerant conditions for tumor bone metastasis [222].

Dysregulation of epigenetic regulatory networks: aberrant modification of immune-related molecules

Epigenetic modifications (DNA methylation, histone modification, non-coding RNA regulation, etc.) represent pivotal mechanisms governing gene expression, controlling cellular function by modulating gene transcription and translation. In recent years, marked dysregulation of the epigenetic regulatory network has been identified in bone metastasis (including aberrant DNA methylation, disordered histone modification and imbalanced non-coding RNA regulation), which leads to abnormal expression of immune-related molecules, suppresses immune cell function and results in unsatisfactory immunotherapeutic efficacy [223–225].

Aberrant DNA methylation: activation of immunosuppressive genes and silencing of immune-activating genes in bone metastasis

During bone metastasis progression, aberrant DNA methylation is mainly characterized by hypermethylation-mediated silencing of immune-activating genes and hypomethylation-mediated activation of immunosuppressive genes. This dual regulatory mechanism disrupts the body’s anti-tumor immune balance, promotes tumor immune escape and accelerates the initiation and progression of bone metastasis, and also participates in the formation and maintenance of the “vicious cycle” in bone metastasis [225].

Hypermethylation-induced silencing of immune-activating genes is one of the core manifestations of immune suppression mediated by aberrant DNA methylation in bone metastasis. As a critical tumor suppressor and immune regulatory gene, Forkhead box O3 (FoxO3) is involved in the proliferation, differentiation and functional regulation of immune cells, and plays an essential role in maintaining immune homeostasis. Studies have demonstrated that during osteoclastogenesis, DNA methyltransferase 3a (DNMT3a) mediates hypermethylation of the FoxO3 gene promoter region, resulting in silenced FoxO3 expression, which further triggers redox imbalance, suppresses the anti-tumor function of immune cells and accelerates bone metastasis progression [226]. In addition, EZH2, a histone methyltransferase, exhibits synergistic effects with aberrant DNA methylation. In bone metastasis of NSCLC, EZH2 inhibition activates the dsRNA-interferon axis stress response and enhances the body’s response to PD-1 checkpoint blockade therapy, a mechanism closely associated with reversing aberrant methylation of immune-related genes and restoring the expression of immune-activating genes [148, 223].

Hypomethylation-mediated activation of immunosuppressive genes further exacerbates the immunosuppressive state of bone metastatic lesions. In prostate cancer bone metastasis, the BHLHE22 gene is aberrantly activated via hypomethylation, and its high expression drives an immunosuppressive phenotype in the bone tumor microenvironment, inhibits the activity of immune effector cells such as T cells and NK cells, promotes macrophage polarization toward the M2 immunosuppressive phenotype, and induces the secretion of immunosuppressive factors to establish an immunosuppressive bone microenvironment [227]. Furthermore, metabolic reprogramming of macrophages in the bone microenvironment is closely linked to aberrant DNA methylation. The histone methyltransferase ASH1L initiates macrophage metabolic reprogramming by regulating the DNA methylation status of macrophages, suppresses the immune-killing function of macrophages and participates in immunosuppressive regulation in bone metastasis [59].

Aberrant histone modification: dysregulated transcription of immune-related genes in bone metastasis

Histone modifications regulate gene transcriptional activity by altering chromatin compaction, and their aberrant modifications lead to dysregulated transcription of immune-related genes, mainly including methylation, acetylation, phosphorylation and other modification types [223]. During bone metastasis, aberrant histone modification is mainly characterized by abnormal expression of modification-related enzymes such as histone methyltransferases and histone deacetylases (HDACs), which regulate the transcription of immune-related genes by specifically modifying amino acid residues on histone tails, affecting immune cell function and the immune status of the bone microenvironment [143, 225].

Aberrant histone methylation is one of the main manifestations of disordered histone modification in bone metastasis, among which abnormal expression of the histone methyltransferases ASH1L, EZH2 and the histone demethylase Jumonji/AT-rich interactive domain-containing protein 1D (JARID1D) plays a crucial regulatory role in the formation of the immunosuppressive state in bone metastasis. As a histone H3K4 methyltransferase, abnormally high expression of ASH1L regulates macrophage metabolic reprogramming in the bone microenvironment, suppresses the immune-killing function of macrophages and promotes macrophage polarization toward an immunosuppressive phenotype, a mechanism closely related to the transcriptional activation of immune-related genes [59]. As a histone Histone H3 lysine 27 (H3K27) trimethyltransferase, aberrant EZH2 expression has been reported in bone metastasis of various tumors. EZH2 promotes bone metastasis by binding to the TGF-β signaling pathway and activating the integrin β1-FAK signaling pathway; meanwhile, EZH2 suppresses the transcription of immune-activating genes and promotes the expression of immunosuppressive genes by methylating the promoter regions of immune-related genes [146]. In addition, in prostate cancer bone metastasis, JARID1D activates the androgen receptor and JunD signaling pathways by demethylating histone H3K4, promotes osteoclast differentiation and thereby inhibits bone metastasis of prostate cancer cells [228].

Aberrant histone acetylation is mainly mediated by the imbalanced activity of histone acetyltransferases and HDACs. HDACs condense chromatin and inhibit gene transcription by removing acetyl groups from histone tails, and their abnormally high expression leads to transcriptional silencing of immune-related genes and suppresses immune cell function [229]. Studies have found that targeted inhibition of HDAC1/2 directly downregulates the expression of cytokines such as IL-1β, TNF and Nuclear factor of activated T-cells, cytoplasmic 1 (NFATc1) in monocytes and suppresses the bone resorption function of osteoclasts, a mechanism closely associated with restoring the acetylation level of immune-related genes and activating immune cell function [230]. In addition, aberrant HDAC expression participates in the pathophysiological process of bone remodeling. In bone metastasis, HDACs promote osteoclast activation and osteoblast dysfunction by regulating the transcription of bone remodeling-related and immune-related genes, and simultaneously suppress the anti-tumor function of immune cells, forming a vicious cycle of “abnormal bone remodeling-immunosuppression-bone metastasis progression“ [231]. Meanwhile, the intrinsic interferon signaling pathway in prostate cancer cells is regulated by histone modification, and its aberrant expression modulates the dormant state and metastatic outgrowth of tumor cells in bone, affects the immune status of the bone microenvironment and participates in immunosuppressive regulation in bone metastasis [232].

Dysregulated non-coding RNA regulation: aberrant modulation of immune function in bone metastasis

Non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) are a class of RNA molecules that do not encode proteins but exert regulatory functions, mainly including long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), circular RNAs (circRNAs) and microRNAs (miRNAs). They participate in the regulation of gene expression by targeting mRNAs and modulating the expression of epigenetic modification-related enzymes, affecting immune cell function and the immune status of the bone microenvironment, and promoting tumor immune escape and bone metastasis progression [223, 233].

As a class of closed circular ncRNAs with high stability and specificity, circRNAs play a vital role in immunosuppressive regulation in bone metastasis. In breast cancer bone metastasis, circIKBKB promotes bone metastasis of breast cancer cells by sustainably activating the NF-κB/bone remodeling factor signaling pathway; meanwhile, activation of the NF-κB signaling pathway promotes the secretion of immunosuppressive factors, suppresses immune cell function and establishes an immunosuppressive microenvironment [234]. In addition, circRHBDD1(4,5) promotes CCL2 expression by interacting with CCL2 mRNA. As a key chemokine, CCL2 recruits immunosuppressive cells to infiltrate bone metastatic lesions, inhibits the activity of immune effector cells, promotes the colonization and proliferation of tumor cells in the bone microenvironment and thereby accelerates breast cancer bone metastasis [235].

As a class of ncRNAs with a length of more than 200 nucleotides, dysregulated lncRNA expression plays a crucial role in immunosuppression in bone metastasis, and multiple lncRNAs participate in immune regulation in bone metastasis through distinct mechanisms.

As a highly conserved nuclear lncRNA, the downregulation of Malat1 is often accompanied by an elevated expression of Tead3, which is specific to monocytes/macrophages and osteoclasts. Malat1 deficiency releases its inhibitory effect on Tead3, promoting the binding of free Tead3 to Nfatc1, which leads to excessive activation of osteoclasts. Meanwhile, it suppresses immune cell function and facilitates the metastatic colonization of tumor cells in bone [236]. In prostate cancer bone metastasis, the lncRNA CCDC183-AS1 interacts with Far Upstream Element-Binding Protein 1 (FUBP1) and enhances its stability by inhibiting JTV-1-mediated ubiquitination and degradation of FUBP1. Upregulation of LIGHT promotes osteoclastogenesis and bone metastasis of prostate cancer cells, exacerbating disorders and immunosuppression in the bone microenvironment [237]. Furthermore, tumor-derived exosomal lncRNA-MIR193BHG suppresses immune cell function and accelerates bone metastasis via the miR-489-3p/DNMT3a axis, a mechanism closely related to regulating aberrant DNA methylation and silencing immune-activating genes [238].

Imbalanced microbiota-bone immune axis: an emerging immunosuppressive mechanism

In recent years, research on the association between microbiota and tumor immunity has deepened extensively, with numerous studies focusing on the correlation between gut microbiota dysbiosis, intratumoral microbial colonization and immunosuppression in bone metastasis. As the core of the body’s microecosystem, the gut microbiota forms a closely interactive “microbiota-bone immune axis” with bone tissue, a vital immune organ, and its imbalance has emerged as an innovative immunosuppressive mechanism in bone metastasis [239].

Inhibition of bone metastasis immune function by gut microbiota dysbiosis

Homeostatic imbalance of the gut microbiota is one of the main inducers of immunosuppression in bone metastasis, suppressing the body’s anti-bone metastasis immune response by regulating immune cell dynamics, secreting metabolites and disrupting gut-bone immune axis communication [240, 241]. In a melanoma bone metastasis model, broad-spectrum antibiotic-induced gut microbiota dysbiosis inhibits the expansion of intestinal NK cells and Th1 cells, impedes S1PR1-, CXCL9-driven migration of immune cells from the intestine to intraosseous tumor sites, resulting in a significant reduction in bone marrow immune cells, accelerated intraosseous tumor growth and aggravated bone lysis [74]. Metabolites such as secondary bile acids and short-chain fatty acids secreted by the gut microbiota are critical mediators of gut-bone axis communication; microbiota dysbiosis leads to abnormal secretion of these metabolites, further suppressing immune function and promoting bone resorption [242]. Studies have found that the gut microbiota regulates the hyodeoxycholic acid-TGR5 axis to maintain bone mass, and microbiota imbalance disrupts this axis, weakening the inhibitory effect of immune cells on bone metastasis [243]. In a mouse model of triple-negative breast cancer bone metastasis, gut microbiota dysbiosis also elevates pain sensitivity, indirectly affecting immune cell function and promoting bone metastasis progression [244].

Clinical studies further confirm that gut microbiota dysbiosis is closely associated with bone metastasis and acts as a key regulatory factor for immunosuppression in bone metastasis [245]. Patients with breast cancer exhibit significantly reduced gut microbiota diversity, decreased abundance of beneficial bacteria and excessive proliferation of harmful bacteria. This imbalance suppresses the activation and infiltration of anti-tumor immune cells in peripheral blood, weakens the body’s immune surveillance, promotes breast cancer cell detachment from primary lesions and colonization in bone tissue, accelerates bone metastasis progression and enhances immunosuppressive effects [246, 247]; transcriptomic and microbiomic analyses clarify the core role of the gut microbiota in the molecular mechanism of breast cancer bone metastasis [248]. In addition, colonization of breast cancer by Fusobacterium nucleatum accelerates tumor growth and metastatic progression, a mechanism also related to immune suppression [249]. In metastatic prostate cancer, gut microbiota dysbiosis is associated with castration resistance and cancer stemness, accelerating bone metastasis progression by suppressing immune surveillance [250]. The homeostatic balance of intestinal T cells, DCs and epithelial cells depends on normal microbiota regulation; microbiota dysbiosis disrupts this balance, further weakening the intestinal immune barrier and systemic immune function and laying the foundation for immunosuppression in bone metastasis [251].

Inhibition of local immune function by intratumoral microbiota

Compared with the systemic regulatory effect of the gut microbiota, the direct link between intratumoral microbes and bone metastasis remains unclear, but the intratumoral microbiome may affect the metastatic potential of cancer cells and their ability to colonize distant sites (including bone). Pan-cancer studies confirm that metastatic tumors harbor unique intratumoral microbiota signatures, which participate in the immunosuppressive process of tumor metastasis through multiple pathways [252] and are regarded as important “accomplices” in tumor development and metastasis [253]. The intratumoral microbiota indirectly promotes immunosuppression in bone metastasis by regulating the local immune microenvironment and affecting immunotherapy sensitivity. In triple-negative breast cancer, the intratumoral microbiota predicts the efficacy of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, suppresses local immune cell activation, reduces therapeutic sensitivity and inhibits the body’s ability to clear bone metastatic tumor cells [254]; related studies have also identified and validated intratumoral microbiota associated with sensitivity to ICIs, and its imbalance reduces the efficacy of ICIs and further enhances the local immunosuppressive state of tumor metastasis [255]. In addition, the intratumoral microbiota affects the spatial and cellular heterogeneity of tumors, remodels the local immune microenvironment, suppresses immune cell infiltration and activation, and provides immune shelter for metastatic tumor cells [256].

Neuro-immune-bone metastasis-mediated immunosuppression

The progression of bone metastasis involves complex synergistic regulation among the bone microenvironment, nervous system, immune system and tumor cells. As a critical regulatory network, the neuro-immune-bone tumor interactive axis weakens the body’s anti-tumor immune response through nerve-mediated immunosuppressive effects [257]. Sensory and sympathetic nerves extensively innervate the periosteum, bone marrow and cancellous bone regions, and precisely regulate immune cell function by releasing neuropeptides, nerve growth factors and other signaling molecules, induce the formation of an immunosuppressive microenvironment, reduce immunotherapy sensitivity, and link cancer pain to tumor progression (Fig. 5)

As a vital component of intraosseous innervation, sensory nerves mediate immunosuppression in bone metastasis and reduce immunotherapy efficacy. Calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) released by sensory nerve fibers binds to the CRLR receptor on tumor cells, activates the p38/HSP27 signaling pathway to promote tumor proliferation, and simultaneously weakens the ability of DCs to activate CD8+ T cells by inhibiting the production of the CCL5 chemokine in draining lymph nodes, induces macrophage polarization toward the pro-tumor M2 phenotype, and significantly attenuates the killing effect of immunotherapeutic drugs on tumor cells [258]. In addition, close crosstalk exists between sensory nerves and bone metastatic tumor cells: tumor cells secrete signaling molecules to activate sensory nerves and induce their aberrant sprouting, and neuropeptides released by activated sensory nerves further exacerbate immunosuppression and promote tumor invasion, forming a positive feedback loop [259]. Higher sensory nerve innervation density correlates with more severe immunosuppression, worse bone metastasis pain, faster tumor progression and poorer immunotherapy efficacy [260].

Sympathetic nerves participate in bone microenvironment regulation via the “brain-bone axis”, and the immune disorder they mediate also drives bone metastasis progression and severely impairs immunotherapy efficacy [261]. Neuropeptide Y (NPY) released by sympathetic nerve fibers acts on bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells, inhibits osteoblast differentiation and promotes their transformation into adipocytes, disrupts bone metabolic homeostasis, and simultaneously remodels the microenvironment to be pro-metastatic and pro-immunosuppressive by regulating immune cell function, weakening the body’s immune surveillance of tumor cells and reducing immunotherapy sensitivity [262]. Norepinephrine released by sympathetic nerves activates β2-adrenergic receptors on tumor and immune cells, inhibiting the activity of anti-tumor immune cells such as CD8+ T cells and NK cells on the one hand, and promoting the infiltration of immunosuppressive cells such as Tregs and M2-type macrophages on the other [263]. Studies show that sympathetic nervous dysfunction disrupts the immune-neural balance in the bone microenvironment, exacerbates immunosuppression and tumor-induced bone destruction, and reduces the response rate to immunotherapy [264]. Furthermore, tumor-induced neurogenesis is closely associated with immune escape and acts as a key regulatory node of the neuro-immune-tumor interactive axis; targeting this node can further optimize immunotherapy efficacy [257]; breast cancer exploits neural signaling pathways to achieve bone-to-meninges metastasis, and nerve-mediated immunosuppression also exists in this process [265].

The neuro-immune-tumor interactive axis mediates immunosuppression, and targeting this axis to reverse immunosuppression has become a crucial direction for improving immunotherapy efficacy [266, 267]. As a key node connecting nerves and immunity, agonists of the STING signaling pathway activate CD8+ T cells by releasing type I interferon, regulate immune cell activity and neuronal function, suppress bone cancer pain, reverse local immunosuppression, thereby delaying bone metastasis progression and enhancing immunotherapy sensitivity [268]. A recently developed oxidation-activated nanoplatform (LipoNCs@pGSDMB) co-delivers STING agonists and Gasdermin B (GSDMB) plasmids, activates STING-mediated anti-tumor immune responses, induces tumor cell pyroptosis, enhances DC maturation and CD8+ T cell infiltration, reduces Treg cells and reverses nerve-mediated immunosuppression; it restores voltage-gated calcium channel expression, blocks tumor-nerve crosstalk, and achieves synergistic effects of 94% bone metastatic lesion clearance, pain relief, bone repair and improved immunotherapy efficacy [269]. In addition, blocking tumor-nerve crosstalk effectively reverses the immunosuppressive microenvironment, creates conditions for immunotherapeutic drugs to function and improves the overall efficacy of bone metastasis treatment [270].

Key nodes of the crosstalk regulatory network and clinical countermeasures

The core problem underlying the poor efficacy of immunotherapy for bone metastasis lies in the formation of a potent and irreversible immunosuppressive microenvironment by the multidimensional crosstalk regulatory network composed of immunity, metabolism, bone matrix, blood vessels, epigenetics, microbiota and nerves. Each regulatory link is coupled and amplified mutually, forming a vicious cycle of “immunosuppression-bone matrix destruction-metabolic disorder-vascular abnormality”.

Key nodes of the crosstalk regulatory network

Core cellular regulatory nodes

The formation of immunosuppressive microenvironment in bone metastases relies on specific cell populations as core carriers, which achieve crosstalk regulation via direct cell-cell contact and paracrine secretion. Osteoclasts are one of the key cells linking bone matrix and immune crosstalk. They not only dominate osteolytic bone destruction, but also highly express Siglec-15 and OPN to directly impair the function of CD8⁺ T cells and remotely weaken systemic immunity, thereby tightly coupling bone destruction with immunosuppression. Osteoblasts and bone marrow stromal cells actively facilitate tumor bone homing. They recruit tumor cells through the CXCL12/CXCR4 axis, maintain tumor dormancy via the GAS6/AXL and TGF-β axes, and establish an immune-silent microenvironment through the CD39/CD73-adenosine pathway, creating favorable conditions for tumor latent progression and recurrence. Immunosuppressive cell populations are highly enriched in bone metastatic lesions. MDSCs, Tregs and M2-type TAMs collaboratively secrete inhibitory factors such as IL-10 and TGF-β to markedly inhibit the function of effector immune cells, and further activate osteoclasts via RANKL to form a positive feedback loop. Effector immune cells exhibit prominent exhaustion phenotypes, with declined cytotoxic activity of CD8⁺ T cells and NK cells, which serves as the direct cause of primary resistance to immunotherapy. As a pivotal mediator of vascular-immune interaction, vascular endothelial cells aberrantly express PD-L1 and VEGF. They form physical barriers to block immune cell infiltration, inhibit DC maturation through molecular modulation, and interact with bone matrix cells to exacerbate bone destruction.

Core molecular signaling nodes

Aberrant activation and interaction of multiple signaling pathways serve as the molecular linkage of the crosstalk regulatory network. Signaling axes mediating bone matrix-immune coupling represent the most distinctive molecular nodes in bone metastasis. The CXCL12/CXCR4 axis modulates tumor bone homing, GAS6/AXL mediates tumor dormancy and immune evasion, and the RANK/RANKL/OPG axis synchronously drives bone destruction and immunosuppression. The Siglec-15-sialic acid axis is an osteoclast-specific immune checkpoint, while the CD39/CD73-adenosine pathway sustains immune silence during tumor dormancy. Immune checkpoint signaling presents distinct bone-specific characteristics. The PD-1/PD-L1 pathway is predominantly expressed in immune cells rather than tumor cells, which limits the therapeutic effect of conventional ICIs. As a crucial cytokine secreted by osteoclasts, OPN suppresses systemic anti-tumor immunity via blood circulation, functioning as the core mediator of systemic drug resistance induced by bone metastasis. Epigenetic regulators such as EZH2 act as upstream regulatory switches, silencing immune-activated genes through methylation modification, driving tumor stemness and distant metastasis, and realizing cross-dimensional regulation among epigenetics, immunity and bone matrix. Metabolism-disordered molecules and neuropeptides jointly participate in immunosuppression. Lactic acid accumulation, lipid dysregulation and iron metabolic imbalance directly dampen immune cell function. Neuropeptides including CGRP and NPY mediate neurogenic immunosuppression, further aggravating the immune “cold tumor” phenotype.

Core microenvironmental interaction nodes

The cross-coupling of various components in the bone metastatic microenvironment forms multiple vicious cycle nodes, which are crucial for the maintenance of immunosuppression. The vicious cycle of bone metastasis reactivation-bone destruction-immunosuppression is the core link in the formation of immune cold foci. Excessive osteoclast activation triggers a cascade reaction, and TGF-β released from the bone matrix further enhances immunosuppression, forming an irreversible amplification loop that cannot be reversed spontaneously. The metabolism-immunity interaction node is centered on lactic acid accumulation, lipid metabolism disorder, and iron metabolism imbalance. The lactic acid-induced acidified microenvironment directly inhibits CD8⁺T cell function, lipid mediators reshape immune cell phenotypes, and abnormal iron metabolism induces ferroptosis of immune cells; meanwhile, metabolic disorders further accelerate bone matrix destruction and vascular abnormalities. The vascular-bone matrix-immunity interaction node is characterized by abnormal vascular remodeling forming a physical barrier, the hypoxic microenvironment promoting osteoclast activation, and factors released from bone matrix destruction transactivating vascular endothelial cells, thus forming a closed-loop regulation among the three. The microbiota-neuro-immunity axis is an emerging interaction node. Intestinal flora dysbiosis inhibits the homing of intestinal NK cells and Th1 cells to bone metastatic foci, and neuropeptides achieve neurogenic immunosuppression by regulating immune cell functions; both jointly participate in the maintenance of the immunosuppressive microenvironment.

Clinical countermeasures based on key nodes

Aiming at the core nodes of the bone metastasis crosstalk regulatory network, the core idea of clinical countermeasures is “targeting key nodes to break the vicious cycle + ICIs to restart anti-tumor immunity + multidimensional combination to achieve synergistic efficacy”, abandoning the single-target/pathway treatment model, adopting a multi-target, multi-link, local-system synergistic combination therapy strategy, and achieving precise treatment combined with immune typing.

Combined immunotherapy targeting bone matrix-immune interaction nodes

Targeting osteoclasts as the core therapeutic target and combining with ICIs can achieve the dual effects of osteolysis inhibition and immune activation. Drugs such as denosumab and bisphosphonates (BPs) are used to block osteolysis, reduce the immunosuppressive factors released by bone matrix destruction, and simultaneously decrease the OPN level secreted by osteoclasts to reverse its remote immunosuppressive effect. Multiple clinical studies have confirmed that immunotherapy combined with denosumab shows good efficacy and safety in the treatment of bone metastases from advanced NSCLC, melanoma and other cancer types [271–273]. One of the first published studies on the combined use of immunotherapy and bone protective agents was Liede’s trial in 2018. This study included patients with advanced melanoma (n = 66) and NSCLC (n = 241). Patients received denosumab within 30 days after ICIs treatment. The results showed that longer combined treatment was associated with increased OS, which was more significant in NSCLC patients (P < 0.0001) [272]. A study enrolled 59 NSCLC patients with bone metastases who received ICIs strategy treatment. The results showed that patients who received combined treatment (ICIs + chemotherapy/anti-angiogenesis) had significantly better PFS and OS than those who received single immunotherapy; among them, 29 patients received BPs treatment, and the use of BPs during ICIs treatment significantly prolonged PFS (5.1 vs. 2.1 months, P = 0.0039) and OS (17.7 vs. 4.4 months, P = 0.02) [274]. A retrospective analysis of data from 111 Italian NSCLC patients showed that although the number of patients receiving ICIs treatment was limited (n = 46), compared with ICIs monotherapy, ICIs combined with bone protective agents (30 patients) increased OS from 15.8 months to 21.8 months [275]. For advanced NSCLC patients with high bone tumor burden (bone metastases ≥ 3), compared with single ICIs treatment, the combination of bone protective agents and ICIs could bring significant PFS (3.0 vs.1.8 months, P = 0.001) and OS (8.3 vs.3.5 months, P = 0.003) [276], providing a basis for precise screening of beneficiary populations. In terms of BPs, a retrospective study published in 2022 analyzed the efficacy of ICIs used alone or in combination in 110 patients with advanced NSCLC complicated with bone metastasis. The results demonstrated that treatment with BPs or palliative bone radiotherapy markedly elevated the ORR(34.9% vs. 11.1%, P < 0.000) and prolonged PFS (8.5 vs. 2.0 months, P = 0.002). Combination therapy of ICIs and BPs achieved a significant increase in ORR (34.7% vs. 18.4%, P = 0.005) and longer PFS (8.8 vs. 3.3 months, P = 0.003). By contrast, palliative bone radiotherapy failed to yield obvious therapeutic improvement or survival benefits [277].

In terms of the efficacy difference between ICIs combined with denosumab and BPs, a retrospective study in 2025 included 150 lung cancer patients, of which 95 received ICIs + denosumab and the remaining 55 received ICIs + BPs. The results showed that the PFS of the two groups was 11.9 months and 5.57 months respectively (P = 0.0012) [11]. In addition, a real-world study included 171 patients. After excluding cases with positive driver genes, the PFS of the ICIs + denosumab group was not reached, while that of the ICIs + BPs group was 7.5 months, with a statistically significant difference between the two groups (P = 0.045) [271]. Nevertheless, most current clinical studies on immunotherapy combined with bone-protective agents are retrospective. The bone metastatic microenvironment is frequently characterized as an immune “cold tumor”, manifested by sparse infiltration of CD8⁺ T cells, abundant accumulation of MDSCs and M2-type TAMs, and high expression of TGF-β and other immunosuppressive molecules, which substantially restricts the clinical efficacy of single-agent ICIs. Targeting novel immune checkpoints including TGF-β, Siglec-15 and CXCR, or combining such agents with ICIs can reverse immunosuppression through multiple pathways and convert “cold tumors” into “hot tumors”, thereby offering novel strategies for the treatment of bone metastases [283]. In terms of TGF-β targeting, genetically modified PD-1-positive hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) were engineered to establish SB@HSCs-PD-1, a cellular agent capable of delivering the TGF-β small-molecule inhibitor SB-505,124 specifically to bone tissues. Compared with anti-PD-L1 monoclonal antibodies, living therapeutic HSCs-PD-1 possess superior bone marrow tropism, enable self-proliferation within the bone marrow microenvironment, and sustain stable PD-1 expression. SB-505,124 competitively binds to TGF-β receptors on CD4⁺ T cells, facilitates the differentiation of CD4⁺ T cells into Th1 and Th2 helper T cells, and further remodels the local immunosuppressive bone marrow microenvironment. Moreover, HSCs-PD-1 can block PD-L1 expressed on tumor cells and myeloid cells to reactivate T cell-mediated anti-tumor immunity, representing a promising innovative therapeutic modality for clinical management of bone metastases [284]. As the first publicly reported bispecific antibody targeting both TGF-β and PD-L1 worldwide, YM101 effectively neutralizes the biological functions of TGF-β and PD-1/PD-L1 signaling axes. In vivo experiments have verified that YM101 exerts stronger anti-tumor activity than monotherapy with either anti-TGF-β or anti-PD-L1 agents alone [285]. From another perspective, targeting osteoclast-specific immune checkpoint Siglec-15 via anti-Siglec-15 antibodies (e.g., NC318, MAB-S15) in combination with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors can break bone-specific immunosuppression, and relevant drug development and preclinical investigations are currently underway [286–288]. For immunosuppressive cell populations such as MDSCs and Tregs, CXCR2 inhibitors can be applied in combination with Cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4) inhibitors to deplete Tregs, which synergizes with PD-1 inhibitors to further improve therapeutic outcomes [289, 290].

Local-systemic synergistic immunotherapeutic strategies

The combination paradigm of local therapy plus systemic immunotherapy is adopted to dismantle the local immunosuppressive microenvironment. Combined local radiotherapy and immunotherapy can activate systemic anti-tumor immunity via the abscopal effect. An Italian retrospective study enrolled 305 melanoma patients with bone metastases and stratified them into four groups: Group A received ICIs combined with radiotherapy (n = 73); Group B received ICIs monotherapy (n = 94); Group C received bone-protective agents alone without ICIs (n = 64); Group D received bone-protective agents plus radiotherapy without ICIs (n = 30). The results revealed that patients in Group A achieved a median OS of 16 months, conferring superior survival benefits compared with Group B (13 months, HR = 0.78, P = 0.23), Group C (11 months, HR = 0.68, P = 0.08) and Group D (8.1 months, HR = 0.5, P = 0.013) [291]. A meta-analysis incorporating 6 studies with a total of 1631 participants demonstrated that radiotherapy combined with ICIs significantly improved OS [HR = 0.58, 95% Confidence Interval (CI): 0.44–0.76] and PFS (HR = 0.44, 95%CI: 0.37–0.53) versus ICIs monotherapy. The survival advantage of OS remained robust after excluding large database cohorts (HR = 0.52, 95%CI: 0.42–0.63), while the incidence of grade ≥ 3 adverse events (AEs) was comparable between groups (OR = 1.00, 95%CI: 0.78–1.27) [292]. Nevertheless, another study involving 82 patients who received bone radiotherapy before disease progression during early ICIs treatment showed that the mPFS was 7.2 months and 8.2 months in the bone radiotherapy group and non-radiotherapy group respectively (P = 0.68). Cox regression analysis further confirmed no significant correlation between bone radiotherapy and PFS [293]. Moreover, radioresponsive hydrogels emerge as novel delivery platforms for locoregional combined therapy. Hyaluronic acid and polyvinyl alcohol-based radioresponsive hydrogels are applicable to synergistic radioimmunotherapy for breast cancer bone metastasis [294], which enhances local radiotherapeutic efficacy and amplifies immune activation. For metastatic lesions located in weight-bearing bones, surgery combined with immunotherapy can reduce tumor burden and diminish the enrichment of immunosuppressive cells.

Prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA)-targeted radioligand therapy (RLT) represents a precise radionuclide therapeutic modality for advanced prostate cancer. By conjugating radionuclides with PSMA-targeted peptides based on the high PSMA expression profile of prostate cancer cells, this strategy enables accurate targeted elimination of tumor cells. It is predominantly applied in mCRPC and yields favorable outcomes against prostate cancer bone metastases, with representative agents including 177Lu-PSMA-617 and 177Lu-rhPSMA-10.1 [295]. As a globally approved classic standard agent, 177Lu-PSMA-617 was investigated in the pivotal VISION trial enrolling 831 mCRPC patients previously treated with at least one androgen receptor pathway inhibitor (ARPI) and 1 to 2 taxane-based regimens, among whom 760 cases were complicated with bone metastases. The trial outcomes verified that 177Lu-PSMA-617 significantly prolonged radiographic progression-free survival (rPFS) (8.7 vs. 3.4 months, P < 0.001) and OS (15.3 vs. 11.3 months, P < 0.001) [296, 297]. The global phase III PSMAfore trial recruited 468 PSMA-positive mCRPC patients who progressed after prior single ARPI therapy and were naive to taxane chemotherapy, including 408 patients with bone metastases. The results indicated that the median rPFS reached 9.30 months in the 177Lu-PSMA-617 group versus 5.59 months in the ARPI switching group (HR = 0.41, P<0.0001), while the median OS was 24.48 months and 23.13 months respectively (HR = 0.91, P = 0.20). After adjusting for crossover treatment bias via inverse probability of censoring weighting, the HR for OS was further reduced to 0.59 (95%CI: 0.38 ~ 0.91) [298, 299]. As a new-generation integrated diagnostic and therapeutic targeted agent, 177Lu-rhPSMA-10.1 possesses optimized molecular structure, higher tumor uptake efficiency, longer intratumoral retention time and superior safety profile, making it a promising upgraded therapeutic option for future clinical application [300, 301]. Preclinical studies have validated that PSMA-targeted radiotherapy exerts synergistic effects with immunotherapy via inducing immunogenic cell death (ICD), upregulating PD-L1 expression and remodeling the tumor microenvironment [295]. A phase I trial explored the safety and efficacy of single-dose 177Lu-PSMA-617 combined with pembrolizumab in mCRPC patients, and ORR was achieved in 14 out of 25 enrolled patients (56%), accompanied by measurable tumor shrinkage or complete tumor regression [302]. In a recent phase 1b/2 trial involving 37 mCRPC patients receiving 177Lu-PSMA-617 plus pembrolizumab, serum PSA levels declined by no less than 50% from baseline in 28 patients (76%, 95%CI: 59–88). The median PSA-PFS was 8.2 months (95%CI: 5.1–11.2), median radiographic PFS was 11.2 months (95%CI: 5.1–14.1), and median OS reached 20.8 months [303]. Notably, bone-targeted radiotherapy represented by radium-223 can modulate immune checkpoint expression. Existing evidence demonstrates that radium-223 treatment elevates immune checkpoint levels in extracellular vesicles derived from the bone microenvironment of metastatic prostate cancer [304]. Iliac bone marrow biopsy was performed in 24 mCRPC patients receiving radium-223 therapy, and the findings showed decreased CD68-positive macrophages and elevated infiltration of CD3⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells after treatment, which provides solid rationale for designing rational combined immunotherapeutic regimens [305].

Combined therapy targeting multidimensional microenvironmental remodeling

Multidimensional remodeling strategies are adopted to target metabolic, vascular, epigenetic and other key regulatory nodes. In vascular regulation, the combination of ICIs and anti-angiogenic drugs can improve the clinical outcomes of bone metastases in NSCLC patients. A study enrolled 95 NSCLC patients with bone metastases treated with ICIs, who were divided into experimental group and control group based on combined anti-angiogenic therapy or not, including 42 cases in the experimental group. The experimental group presented a higher DCR(90.5% vs. 68.6%, P = 0.009) of bone metastatic lesions and longer mPFS (14.3 vs. 8.3 months, P = 0.011) of bone metastases, while there were no statistically significant differences in overall systemic DCR, ORR and general PFS between the two groups [306]. Inhibiting abnormal vascular remodeling can break physical barriers and promote the infiltration of immune cells and therapeutic drugs. In terms of nano-delivery systems, transformable self-delivered supramolecular nanomaterials combined with anti-PD-1 antibodies can effectively relieve tumor immunosuppression, which provides a new therapeutic approach for breast cancer bone metastasis [307]. Nanodrug delivery systems targeting the tumor-bone microenvironment can further improve drug targeting efficiency and therapeutic efficacy [308]. For metabolic and epigenetic regulation, lactate transporter inhibitors, iron chelators, EZH2 inhibitors and other agents can reverse immunosuppression induced by metabolic disorders and epigenetic abnormalities.

Cell therapy and novel combination strategies

In cellular immunotherapy against bone metastases, chimeric antigen receptor T (CAR-T) cells, bispecific antibodies and T-cell engagers (TCEs) exert distinct therapeutic advantages. All strategies center on targeted activation of immune cells and reversal of immune suppression within the bone microenvironment, yielding promising clinical application prospects in bone metastasis treatment.

CAR-T cells are generated by genetically engineering autologous T cells to express specific chimeric antigen receptors, which enable precise recognition of surface antigens on bone metastatic lesions, such as PSMA in prostate cancer and HER2 in breast cancer. They can directly trigger cytotoxic T cell responses independent of MHC-mediated antigen presentation [309, 310]. γδ-enriched CAR-T cells combined with zoledronic acid have been applied for the treatment of mCRPC complicated with bone metastases. Equipped with chimeric antigen receptors, these cells specifically target PSMA on prostate cancer cells, and secrete perforin and granzyme to directly eliminate metastatic tumor cells. They can also deplete myeloid-derived suppressor cells and Tregs to relieve local immune suppression. In addition, this combined regimen strengthens bone protection, downregulates tumor RANKL expression, inhibits osteoclast activation and reduces the incidence of skeletal-related events [311]. Most ongoing clinical trials regarding CAR-T therapy for bone metastases remain in phase I. A phase I trial of prostate stem cell antigen (PSCA)-targeted CAR-T cells enrolled male patients with advanced mCRPC. Among 14 evaluable patients, 4 achieved more than 30% PSA reduction accompanied by favorable radiological responses, confirming its preliminary efficacy against bone metastases [312]. Another phase I study assessed the safety and efficacy of P-PSMA-101, a bone-homing PSMA-targeted CAR-T product derived from enriched tissue-resident memory stem T cells. In total 33 treated patients, 7 cases (21%) attained ≥ 50% PSA decline; per RECIST criteria, 1 patient achieved partial response (PR) and 20 patients (61%) maintained stable disease (SD) [313]. Moreover, a phase I trial explored the optimal dosage of TGF-β-resistant CAR-T cells carrying dominant-negative TGF-β receptors for mCRPC. Preliminary outcomes verified that this regimen could induce over 30% PSA reduction, laying a foundation for overcoming TGF-β-dominated immune suppression [314]. Despite promising anti-tumor activity observed in preliminary studies, the clinical popularization of CAR-T therapy is still restricted by insufficient tumor infiltration caused by local immunosuppressive components including TGF-β and Treg accumulation in bone metastatic lesions, as well as AEs such as cytokine release syndrome (CRS), which requires further technical optimization.

As cell-free immunotherapeutic agents, bispecific antibodies and TCEs take effect via intravenous infusion without ex vivo immune cell modification. Bispecific antibodies bridge tumor cells and immune effector cells including T cells and NK cells through dual antigen-binding domains. As simplified single-chain derivatives of bispecific antibodies, TCEs possess superior tumor tissue penetration [315]. Both modalities can efficiently trigger immune cell-mediated tumor killing and reshape the therapeutic landscape of prostate cancer bone metastases [315]. Xaluritamig (AMG 509) is a STEAP1-targeted T-cell engager. Its first-in-human trial recruited 97 patients, among whom 40% suffered from bone metastases. In this cohort, 16 patients (24%) achieved PR and 32 patients (48%) maintained SD [316, 317]. Pasritamig is a human IgG1 bispecific antibody that simultaneously binds CD3 complex on T cells and KLK2 on tumor cells, redirecting T cell cytotoxicity toward KLK2-positive malignant cells. A phase I study enrolled 174 patients, and bone metastases were confirmed in 153 cases (88.4%). Subcutaneous administration of pasritamig resulted in treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) in 144 patients (82.8%), with grade ≥ 3 TRAEs occurring in 17 patients (9.8%). At the recommended phase II dose (RP2D, n = 33), the median radiographic PFS reached 7.85 months, and 14 patients (42.4%) achieved ≥ 50% PSA reduction from baseline [318]. Two ongoing phase III trials, namely single-agent KLK2-comPAS (NCT07164443) and combination KLK2-PASenger (NCT07225946) with docetaxel, are being conducted to validate the clinical value of pasritamig in mCRPC, and relevant results are highly anticipated. From the perspective of the neuro-immune-bone axis, CGRP receptor antagonists and β-blockers can block neurogenic immune suppression and alleviate cancer-induced bone pain simultaneously, realizing dual effects of analgesia and anti-tumor treatment. Stimulator of interferon genes (STING) agonists are capable of reversing local immune suppression by modulating the function of immune cells and neurons, and enhancing the sensitivity to immunotherapy. Recent research has demonstrated that oxidation-activated nanoplatforms co-delivering STING agonists and GSDMB plasmids could eliminate up to 94% of bone metastatic lesions, providing solid preclinical evidence for further clinical transformation [269].

Limitations and future research directions

Research on immunotherapy for bone metastasis has evolved from single immune checkpoint blockade to a new era of multidimensional cross-regulatory targeting. Recent studies have provided substantial evidence for dissecting immunosuppressive mechanisms and developing novel therapeutic strategies; however, numerous scientific issues and clinical challenges remain. For instance, the remote regulatory mechanism of bone metastasis on systemic immune function has not been fully elucidated, the development of bone-specific immune checkpoint targets is still in the early stage, predictive biomarkers for immunotherapy efficacy are lacking, and the optimal regimen of combination therapy remains to be determined. Future research should focus on in-depth mechanistic dissection, innovative target development, precise technological innovation, and clinical translation, promoting the transformation of bone metastasis immunotherapy from “empirical combination” to “precision, individualization, and bone specificity”. The ultimate clinical goals are to enhance the benefits of immunotherapy, improve quality of life, and prolong survival in patients with bone metastasis.

In-depth dissection of core mechanisms

Further elucidation of the upstream regulatory mechanisms and cross-organ remote regulatory effects of the cross-regulatory network in bone metastasis is required in the future to provide new theoretical foundations for the development of therapeutic strategies. On the one hand, in-depth studies should be conducted on how bone metastatic lesions remotely suppress systemic immune function through the “humoral-immune” axis, clarifying the mechanistic roles of mediators such as OPN and extracellular vesicles in systemic immune reprogramming, to support the formulation of systemic immunotherapeutic strategies. On the other hand, the upstream regulatory roles of epigenetics and metabolic reprogramming in the cross-regulatory network should be explored, identifying how key molecules such as EZH2 and ACBP mediate the multi-link regulation of the “epigenetic-metabolic-immune-bone matrix” axis, and searching for core upstream targets that can simultaneously target multiple regulatory links. In addition, the regulatory mechanism of the microbiota-nerve- immune-bone axis needs to be further clarified, defining the interaction patterns of gut microbiota, intratumoral microbiota, and neuropeptides, to provide new perspectives for the development of multi-target combination strategies. Meanwhile, the application of technologies such as single-cell multi-omics, spatial transcriptomics, and organoid models will facilitate the dissection of the spatiotemporal heterogeneity of the BME, clarifying the evolution of the immune microenvironment in bone metastatic lesions of different cancer types and at different metastatic stages, and providing a basis for dynamically adjusting therapeutic strategies.

Innovative development of novel therapeutic targets and drugs

Based on the bone-specific immunosuppressive mechanism, priority should be given to the development of bone-specific immune checkpoints and multi-target fusion molecules. In-depth development of neutralizing antibodies and small-molecule inhibitors targeting bone-specific molecules such as Siglec-15 and OPN, combined with nano-delivery systems to achieve bone-targeted delivery [308], reducing systemic adverse reactions. Design bispecific antibodies such as PD-1/Siglec-15 and dual-target inhibitors such as VEGF/OPN to simultaneously block multiple core nodes. Develop dual-target drugs targeting “metabolism-immunity” and “epigenetics-immunity” to achieve synergistic effects of microenvironment remodeling and immune activation. In terms of cell therapy, optimize products such as γδ-CAR-T and PSMA-targeted armored CAR-T to enhance their infiltration and survival in the bone microenvironment and overcome immunosuppression. In addition, attention should be paid to the overall development landscape of immuno-oncology drugs, sorting out the clinical progress and synergistic potential of different targets and drug types [319], to provide a reference for the design of combination strategies.

Innovation and application of precision diagnosis and treatment technologies

In the future, a precise diagnosis and treatment system based on immune typing of bone metastasis and liquid biopsy should be established to solve the clinical problems of predicting and monitoring immunotherapy efficacy. On the one hand, an immune typing scoring system for bone metastasis should be constructed based on single-cell transcriptome data to achieve precise typing of patients with bone metastasis, providing a basis for formulating individualized combination therapeutic strategies. On the other hand, predictive biomarkers for efficacy based on liquid biopsy should be developed, such as the expression levels of OPN, MDSCs, and PD-L1 on circulating tumor cells in peripheral blood, as well as non-coding RNAs in exosomes (e.g., Malat1, CCDC183-AS1), to achieve non-invasive efficacy prediction and dynamic monitoring. In addition, bone metastasis-specific imaging evaluation technologies should be developed, such as PET-CT combined with PD-L1 and Siglec-15 specific probes, to realize non-invasive visual evaluation of the immune microenvironment of bone metastatic lesions and provide imaging evidence for judging immunotherapy response. Artificial intelligence should be used to integrate multi-omics, imaging, and clinical data to construct efficacy prediction models and optimize therapeutic strategies.

Advancement of translational medicine and clinical research

The two-way translation of basic and clinical research should be strengthened to accelerate the clinical implementation of novel strategies. Prospective randomized controlled trials based on immune typing should be carried out to compare the efficacy of different combination regimens in patients with different subtypes. Early clinical studies of bone-specific targeted drugs, nano-delivery systems, and cell therapy should be promoted to determine the optimal dosage and regimen [307, 314]. Real-world studies should be conducted to analyze the efficacy and prognostic factors of immunotherapy in patients with bone metastasis of different cancer types, providing evidence for clinical practice. Attention should be paid to the safety of immunotherapy combined with bone-targeted therapy, radiotherapy, anti-angiogenic therapy and other regimens, and strategies for the prevention and treatment of adverse reactions should be established.

Limitations of this study

This study systematically elaborates the multidimensional intertwined regulatory network of immune cold niches in bone metastasis and corresponding precise breakthrough strategies, yet several limitations remain. Currently, clinical evidence for immune checkpoint inhibitors combined with bone-protective agents is mostly derived from retrospective studies. Large-sample, multicenter prospective clinical trials are still lacking to confirm the genuine survival benefits of combination regimens, and high-level evidence regarding efficacy differences and stratified applicable populations among various bone-protective agents and immune combination regimens is insufficient. Meanwhile, novel cellular immunotherapies including CAR-T cells, bispecific antibodies, and T-cell engagers (TCEs) are predominantly investigated in prostate cancer bone metastasis, while mechanistic exploration and clinical research in common tumor types such as lung cancer, breast cancer, and renal cell carcinoma remain scarce, with limited coverage of tumor indications. In addition, the multidimensional cross-regulatory mechanisms involving immunity, metabolism, and nerves summarized in this paper are mainly integrated from fragmented existing literature; the causal relationships and spatiotemporal regulatory patterns among various signaling pathways still require experimental verification. Universal biomarkers and immune typing evaluation systems are still absent, failing to support individualized precise treatment for patients with different cancer types and varying bone tumor burdens. Furthermore, emerging targets such as Siglec-15 and EZH2, as well as cutting-edge therapies including CAR-T and nanotherapy, are still confined to basic research and early clinical stages, with insufficient evidence on long-term efficacy, safety, and real-world application.

Perspectives

As a critical hallmark of advanced malignant tumors, the poor efficacy of immunotherapy for bone metastasis has long restricted the development of clinical treatment. In recent years, the in-depth dissection of the cross-regulatory network in the BME has provided a new perspective for breaking this dilemma. In the future, with the continuous clarification of the immunosuppressive mechanism of bone metastasis, the sustained development of novel bone-specific targets, and the constant innovation of precision diagnosis and treatment technologies, immunotherapy for bone metastasis will enter a new stage of multi-target combination, local-systemic synergy, precise typing, and bone-specific targeting, fundamentally reversing the immune tolerance state of bone metastasis. It is believed that with the joint promotion of basic and clinical research, bone metastasis will no longer be a “forbidden zone” for immunotherapy, and more patients with bone metastasis will benefit from immunotherapy, ultimately realizing individualized and precise treatment for bone metastasis in advanced malignant tumors.

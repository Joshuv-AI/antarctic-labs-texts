Crosstalk between liver sinusoidal endothelial cells and hepatocytes via IL-1α–IL1R1 axis exacerbates ischaemia/reperfusion injury in aged livers
Authors: Yasong Liu, Tingting Wang, Feng Zhang, Xuying Liu, Zhongying Hu, Jiebin Zhang, Haitian Chen, Jiaqi Xiao, Qiang You, Zhengqi Wu, Jia Yao, Yingcai Zhang, Shuhong Yi, Hua Li, Qi Zhang, Yang Yang, Rong Li, Jun Zheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWith population ageing, elderly patients account for a growing proportion of hepatic surgery recipients. Hepatic ischaemia-reperfusion injury (HIRI) is a major cause of postoperative liver dysfunction, particularly in aged livers, yet its mechanisms remain poorly understood.ObjectiveWe aimed to elucidate critical cellular interactions and molecular mechanisms underlying aggravated HIRI in aged livers to uncover therapeutic targets.DesignSingle-cell RNA sequencing and spatial transcriptomics were performed on liver tissues from humans, rats and mice across ages to define key cell types and intercellular signalling. HIRI and liver transplantation animal models, primary cell co-cultures and adeno-associated virus-mediated gene knockdown were used to prove cellular function and mechanisms. Neutralising antibody was used to assess therapeutic efficacy.ResultsIntegrated analyses revealed a significant enrichment of senescent liver sinusoidal endothelial cells (LSECs) in aged livers, with the most prominent age-associated increase in crosstalk with hepatocytes, thereby promoting inflammation. Further investigation demonstrated increased transcriptional activity of myeloid ecotropic viral integration site 2 (MEIS2) in senescent LSECs, driving interleukin (IL)-1α expression via promoter binding and the IL-1α–IL1R1 axis subsequently activated NF-κB signalling in hepatocytes, enhancing inflammatory cytokine production. Interestingly, LSECs were also most strongly influenced by hepatocytes during liver ageing, as hepatocyte-derived TNF-α further enhanced MEIS2 transcriptional activity in LSECs, establishing a proinflammatory positive feedback loop. Furthermore, we confirmed that neutralising IL-1α effectively alleviated HIRI in aged livers.ConclusionOur findings identify the crosstalk between LSECs and hepatocytes in aged livers aggravating HIRI via MEIS2/IL-1α/IL1R1/TNF-α axis, suggesting that IL-1α neutralising antibody can be exploited as a promising therapeutic strategy for aged HIRI.Keywords: AGEING, LIVER TRANSPLANTATION, ISCHAEMIA-REPERFUSION

Introduction

Ischaemia-reperfusion injury (IRI) poses a formidable clinical challenge in hepatic surgical management, particularly impacting resection and transplantation outcomes. Accelerated global demographic ageing has precipitated a dual surge in both the prevalence of age-related hepatic pathologies and the demand for surgical interventions in geriatric populations. Notably, emerging evidence reveals that geriatric hepatic tissue exhibits heightened susceptibility to severe postoperative IRI, markedly elevating the incidence of hepatic dysfunction and immune-related complications and ultimately compromising long-term clinical prognoses.1 Despite these critical observations, the pathophysiological characteristics underlying age-exacerbated IRI remain poorly delineated. This knowledge gap has spurred intensive research efforts to elucidate critical cell types and their underlying regulatory mechanisms governing IRI progression in aged livers, with the ultimate goal of developing targeted therapeutic modalities.

Given that hepatocytes constitute the majority of liver cells both numerically and functionally, the diminished stress resilience of aged liver has predominantly focused on hepatocyte-centric mechanisms. Emerging research has revealed that except for the paradigm of cell-autonomous injury in senescent hepatocytes, their regulation through dynamic crosstalk by surrounding non-parenchymal cells also exerts crucial roles.2–4 Liver sinusoidal endothelial cells (LSECs) comprise approximately 15–20% of total hepatic cells, solidifying their status as the predominant non-parenchymal cellular compartment within the liver. Under physiological conditions, LSECs orchestrate multifaceted critical functions for maintaining liver homeostasis, such as bidirectional exchange of metabolites and macromolecules between blood and hepatocytes, immunological surveillance via antigen presentation and regulation of immune cell trafficking, and participation in the coagulation and tissue regeneration.5 Dysfunction of LSECs in the abnormal status, such as ageing, is associated with a variety of human diseases, including liver fibrosis and steatosis.6 7 Jordi et al presented the importance of LSECs in exacerbating age-related hepatic IRI (HIRI) through preliminary investigations.8 Nevertheless, the pathological alterations in LSECs during aged HIRI, as well as their consequential impacts on the local hepatic microenvironment, have not yet been fully characterised.

The advent of cutting-edge omics platforms, particularly single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) and spatial transcriptomics (ST), has revolutionised hepatic biology by enabling systemic interrogation of liver cell heterogeneity and three-dimensional niche interactions through high-resolution mapping of spatiotemporal gene expression dynamics.9 Building on our prior work applying scRNA-seq to systematically delineate temporal remodelling of the hepatic immune niche during aging.10 In this study, we harmonised single-cell transcriptomes across human, rat and mouse specimens with ST profiling, thereby constructing the three-dimensional liver atlas to explore the underlying mechanism of age-exacerbated HIRI. Our comparative analysis revealed that LSECs strongly enriched characteristic senescence-associated signalling pathways, which exhibit significantly greater senescence burden than hepatocytes. Senescent LSECs underwent myeloid ecotropic viral integration site 2 (MEIS2) transcription factor hyperactivation during HIRI, which mechanistically drove interleukin (IL)-1α overexpression through direct promoter binding. This LSEC-derived IL-1α initiated an inflammatory cascade via IL1R1 receptor activation on hepatocytes, inducing NF-κB-mediated inflammatory cytokines hypersecretion, including TNF-α, IL-6, et al. Strikingly, TNF-α reciprocally amplified MEIS2 transactivation potential, establishing a self-reinforcing MEIS2/IL-1α/TNF-α feedforward loop that propagated inflammatory senescence. Therapeutic intervention with anti-IL-1α neutralising antibody effectively disrupted this pathogenic loop and markedly attenuated aged HIRI, demonstrating its translational potentials for geriatric patients undergoing hepatic surgery.

Results

Single-cell transcriptomics identifies conserved cell-type-specific senescence patterns during liver ageing

The impaired ability of the aged liver to withstand external stress is closely associated with age-related hepatic alterations. To identify the key changes during liver ageing, we performed scRNA-seq on 25 liver samples, comprising 6 rat livers (3 young and 3 aged) and 19 human livers (11 young and 8 aged). Additionally, we complemented these data with 10x Visium HD ST profiling of human livers to delineate spatially restricted ageing signatures. Furthermore, we integrated publicly available mouse scRNA-seq, single-nuclei RNA sequencing (snRNA-seq) and ST data sets to establish a cross-species framework to dissect conserved age-related hepatic alterations (figure 1A). In rats, t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) visualisation based on marker gene expression identified 14 distinct cell populations (online supplemental figure S1A). Compared with young rats, aged rats exhibited increased proportions of immune cells, particularly natural killer (NK) cells and NKT cells, suggesting enhanced inflammatory activity associated with ageing (figure 1B). Comparisons of cellular composition within groups demonstrated high consistency, confirming robust sample homogeneity (online supplemental figure S1B). In human liver samples, scRNA-seq analysis revealed 15 distinct cell clusters (online supplemental figure S1C). Similar to rat, aged human livers demonstrated increased frequencies of immune cells, including NK, NKT and T cells (figure 1C). Notably, human livers displayed greater interindividual heterogeneity than rat samples, prompting our increased biological replication strategy to comprehensively capture aging-related variation (online supplemental figure S1D). For mouse, liver profiling data obtained from scRNA-seq also revealed age-dependent expansions of immune cell populations (online supplemental figure S1E).11 These consistent cross-species observations underscore the conservation of ageing-associated liver cell composition changes.

Previous studies have proved that distinct liver cell types exhibit varying susceptibility to ageing.12 To further investigate the ageing dynamics of liver cells, we performed a comprehensive analysis of senescence-related pathway activities across species using the liver scRNA-seq data.13–19 In aged rats, endothelial cells (ECs), hepatic stellate cells (HSCs) and monocytes/macrophages exhibited the highest senescence pathway activity (figure 1D). Similarly, aged human livers demonstrated robust senescence signatures predominantly in ECs, HSCs and myeloid leukocytes including monocytes, macrophages and conventional dendritic cells (figure 1E). Integrated analysis of mouse liver scRNA-seq data further confirmed ECs, HSCs and myeloid leukocytes as the predominant senescent population (figure 1F). Interestingly, despite hepatocytes being the primary functional cells of the liver and sensitive to aging-associated stress, their intrinsic senescence activity appeared relatively low across all species. Moreover, we delineated cell-type-specific senescence patterns in the aged liver using the Aging Atlas database and further demonstrated that hepatocytes exhibited relatively limited senescence-associated changes (figure 1G).20 The above analyses suggest that hepatocytes show a diminished ability to respond to external stress in the aged liver, which is not only associated with intrinsic cellular changes but may also be predominantly influenced by neighbouring cells in the microenvironment. Notably, figure 1G revealed a significant enrichment of the senescence-associated gene set ‘altered intercellular communication’ specifically in ECs, suggesting their potential involvement in modulating hepatocyte functions via intercellular signalling pathways.

Inflammatory activation of hepatocytes in aged livers and enhanced signalling from ECs during ageing

Based on scRNA-seq data, we first investigated the intrinsic alterations of hepatocytes during ageing. Pseudotime analysis revealed that hepatocytes from young rat livers predominantly clustered at the trajectory origin, diverging into two distinct branches, each enriched with hepatocytes from aged livers (figure 2A). Along the trajectory, gene modules displayed distinct expression patterns: Cluster 1, highly expressed at the origin, comprised genes associated with fundamental hepatocyte functions such as steroid, glutathione and xenobiotic metabolic processes; Cluster 2, enriched at the terminal of Cell fate 1, was related to translational initiation; and Cluster 3, enriched at the terminal of Cell fate 2, was associated with inflammatory responses and lipid metabolism (figure 2B). Consistent with this, classic inflammatory markers—including Tnf, Il1b, Il6, Myd88, Jun, Fos, Stat3 and Nlrp3—were markedly upregulated in hepatocytes from aged rat livers (figure 2C and D). scRNA-seq analysis of aged mouse hepatocytes also demonstrated elevated expression of inflammatory genes, which was further validated by quantitative PCR (qPCR) (online supplemental figure S2A and B). ST analysis revealed widespread upregulation of inflammatory genes in aged mice, indicating global inflammatory activation without significant spatial heterogeneity (online supplemental figure S2C).21 Collectively, these results suggest that hepatocytes in aged livers undergo significant inflammatory upregulation.

Given that alterations in intercellular communication are a key hallmark of ageing, which profoundly influence cellular reprogramming, we analysed the number and strength of cell–cell interactions between young and aged rat livers. As shown in online supplemental figure S3A and B, ECs were identified as the dominant signalling source, exhibiting a marked increase in ligand–receptor-mediated communication targeting hepatocytes during liver ageing. Consistently, hepatocytes in aged livers predominantly received increased signals from ECs (figure 2E). This age-dependent increase in EC-to-hepatocyte signalling was further confirmed in mouse models (online supplemental figure S3C).

To investigate whether these findings also extend to human liver ageing, we performed pseudotime analysis on human livers. Similar to rats, pseudotime analysis enriched hepatocytes from young livers at the trajectory origin, which subsequently bifurcated into two distinct branches predominantly enriched with hepatocytes from aged individuals (figure 2F). Gene expression dynamics analysis identified three distinct clusters: Cluster 1, associated with essential hepatic functions including amino acid metabolism, detoxification and xenobiotic metabolism; Cluster 2, highly expressed at the end of Cell fate 1, linked to lipid metabolism; and Cluster 3, highly expressed at the end of Cell fate 2, associated with inflammatory activation and cell death (figure 2G). Consistent with this, hepatocytes in aged human livers exhibited significantly elevated expression of classic inflammatory markers—such as TNF, MYD88, JUN, FOS and STAT3 (figure 2H). ST further confirmed the widespread inflammatory activation signature in aged human hepatocytes (figure 2I). As seen in rodents, hepatocytes predominantly received increased signals from ECs in aged human livers, highlighting EC-to-hepatocyte communication as a fundamental regulator of hepatocyte function during liver ageing (figure 2J).

Detailed analysis of EC-to-hepatocyte communication identified three ligand–receptor pairs that showed significantly higher communication probabilities in aged livers, including Sema6a–Plxna2, Il1a–(Il1r1+Il1rap) and App–Cd74 (figure 2K). Notably, IL1 signalling emerged as a predominant pathway, with ligand–receptor analysis identifying ECs as the principal sender and hepatocytes as the primary receiver in aged livers (figure 2L; Online supplemental figure S3D). In contrast, hepatocytes did not exhibit increased reception of SEMA6 and APP signalling during liver ageing (online supplemental figure S3E and F). Interestingly, the Il1a– (Il1r1+Il1rap) interaction was almost exclusively initiated by ECs and received by hepatocytes in aged livers, highlighting its remarkable specificity (online supplemental figure S3G).

Collectively, our data demonstrate that hepatocytes in aged livers exhibit extensive inflammatory activation despite low senescence levels, accompanied by significantly enhanced Il1a– (Il1r1+Il1rap) signalling originating from ECs. These findings suggest the critical regulatory role of EC-derived signals in hepatocyte inflammation during liver ageing.

LSECs highly express IL-1α and interact with hepatocytes through the IL-1α/IL1R1 axis in aged liver

Based on the expression of classical marker genes, ECs were classified into central vein ECs, portal vein ECs and LSECs, with LSECs constituting the majority (figure 3A; Online supplemental figure S4A). scRNA-seq analysis identified ECs as the dominant source of Il1a transcripts, with expression levels peaking in the LSEC population (online supplemental figure S4B). Further analysis allowed the classification of LSECs into three distinct subpopulations: normal, transitional and senescent LSECs (figure 3B). In aged livers, the proportion of senescent LSECs increased, while normal LSECs decreased (online supplemental figure S4C). Normal LSECs exhibited high expression of endocytic receptors, including Mrc1 (mannose receptor), Stab1 and Stab2 (scavenger receptors) and Fcgr2b, a gene associated with capillarisation.22 23 Senescent LSECs were characterised by elevated expression of Cdkn1a, Jun and Fos, which are associated with senescence and inflammation. Notably, senescent LSECs displayed the highest Il1a expression, contributing to robust IL-1α signalling toward hepatocytes (figure 3C; Online supplemental figure S4D). Additionally, senescent LSECs showed progressive activation of fatty-acid metabolism, cellular senescence and inflammatory pathways (figure 3D). CytoTRACE analysis demonstrated a significant decline in stemness along the pseudotime trajectory from normal to senescent LSECs (figure 3E). This trajectory inference was further corroborated by RNA velocity and ddhodge potential analyses (figure 3F). Moreover, cell divergence field analysis indicated a substantial reduction in the differentiation potential of senescent LSECs (online supplemental figure S4E). Taken together, comprehensive bioinformatics analyses suggest that senescent LSECs represent a terminally differentiated state in aged livers, concomitant with elevated Il1a expression.

To validate the alterations in Il1a expression within LSECs during liver ageing, we isolated LSECs from young and aged rat livers (online supplemental figure S4F), and observed a significant elevation in IL-1α RNA and protein levels in aged rats (figure 3G). In addition to the increased number of IL-1α+ LSECs in aged livers, we also observed the co-expression of p16, a key indicator of cellular ageing, in IL-1α+ LSECs, indicating that elevated IL-1α expression is also a hallmark of senescent LSECs (figure 3H). Notably, in silico perturbation analysis using Dynamo revealed that Il1a knockout disrupted the LSEC differentiation trajectory, preventing their transition to a senescent phenotype during ageing, underscoring the in vivo role of Il1a in LSEC development (figure 3I). Then, we evaluated the effects of senescent LSECs on hepatocytes. Functional enrichment analysis identified interleukin signalling as the most significantly enriched pathway in hepatocytes influenced by senescent LSECs, alongside death receptor signalling, stress responses and adherens junction interactions, highlighting the activation of IL1 signalling via the IL-1α/IL1R1 axis (figure 3J). The specific high Il1a expression in LSECs and interaction between LSECs and hepatocytes via the IL-1α/IL1R1 axis in aged livers was further validated in mouse (online supplemental figure S5A–E).

Subsequently, we performed further validation using human specimens. Trajectory inference revealed a progression of LSECs from young to aged states (figure 3K). Along this trajectory, LSECs exhibited functional shifts from translation, oxidative phosphorylation and protein catabolism to enhanced regulation of cell motility and migration (figure 3L). Notably, aged human livers also showed increased proportions of p16+IL-1α+ LSECs, which were spatially adjacent to IL1R1+ hepatocytes, suggesting a conserved IL-1α signalling pathway across species (figure 3M and N). Collectively, the accumulation of senescent LSECs characterised by elevated IL-1α expression enhances IL-1α–IL1R1 signalling in hepatocytes during liver ageing.

Targeting the IL-1α/IL1R1 axis significantly mitigates ischaemia–reperfusion injury in aged livers

HIRI inevitably occurs during liver surgery, and it is a critical predisposing cause of postoperative acute liver injury and failure. Previous studies have shown that aged livers exhibit impaired responses to HIRI, which is strongly associated with excessive intrahepatic inflammatory activation.24 25 This led us to investigate whether blocking IL-1α derived from LSECs could effectively alleviate aged HIRI. We isolated primary LSECs from young and aged rat livers and co-cultured them with hepatocytes derived from aged rats. Using an anti-IL-1α neutralising antibody to block IL-1α signalling from aged liver-derived LSECs, we assessed the extent of hepatocyte inflammatory activation and cell death following hypoxia–reoxygenation (H/R) stimulation, a classical in vitro IRI model (figure 4A). We observed that co-culture with aged rat-derived LSECs significantly exacerbated hepatocyte damage, which was markedly attenuated by the anti-IL-1α antibody (figure 4B). Consistent with the known IL-1α/IL1R1/NF-κB proinflammatory signalling pathway, we found that LSECs in aged rats upregulated IL-1α intercellular adaptors in hepatocytes, leading to activation of the NF-κB pathway and subsequent release of inflammatory factors. These effects were effectively blocked by anti-IL-1α antibody treatment (figure 4C–E). To further validate the role of IL-1α in aggravating hepatic stress injury in aged rats, we established a rat HIRI model (figure 4F). As expected, the HIRI model with aged rats showed increased serum aspartate aminotransferase (AST) and alanine aminotransferase (ALT) levels, poorly preserved hepatic architecture and enhanced cell death compared with that of young rats. All of these injuries were significantly attenuated by anti-IL-1α antibody treatment (figure 4G and H). Furthermore, anti-IL-1α treatment inhibited downstream IL-1α signalling and inflammatory cytokine release in hepatocytes (figure 4I–K). Consistently, specific knockdown of Il1a in hepatic ECs via adeno-associated virus (AAV)–mediated delivery through the portal vein also significantly decreased serum ALT and AST levels, ameliorated histological liver damage and reduced hepatocyte death (online supplemental figure S6A–D).

Similar to rat data, co-culture of aged mouse-derived LSECs with hepatocytes enhanced H/R-induced apoptosis, NF-κB activation and inflammatory cytokine production, all of which were significantly attenuated by anti-IL-1α treatment (online supplemental figure S7A–D). Additionally, neutralising IL-1α significantly reduced liver injury and cytokine production in the aged HIRI model (online supplemental figure S7E–H). Furthermore, AAV-mediated Il1a knockdown in hepatic ECs likewise significantly lowered serum ALT and AST levels, alleviated histopathological damage and reduced hepatocyte death in aged HIRI mice (online supplemental figure S8A–D).

Taken together, these results suggest that disrupting the LSEC-hepatocyte crosstalk mediated by the IL-1α/IL1R1 signalling axis significantly suppresses the activation of the NF-κB pathway and the release of proinflammatory cytokines in hepatocytes, thereby effectively alleviating IRI in aged livers.

MEIS2 acts as an EC-specific transcriptional regulator of IL1A in LSECs during ageing

To further elucidate the mechanisms underlying the high expression of IL1A in senescent LSECs, we investigated its upstream transcriptional regulatory mechanisms. Based on an integrated analysis of multiple transcription factor prediction databases, MEIS2 and MAF bZIP transcription factor K (MAFK) emerged as potential transcriptional regulators of IL1A (figure 5A). ScRNA-seq analysis of rat livers revealed that Meis2 was specifically expressed in ECs, whereas Mafk exhibited low expression across all cell populations (figure 5B). Notably, Meis2 transcriptional activity exhibited a progressive age-dependent increase, peaking in senescent LSECs (figure 5C). RNA Jacobian analysis further demonstrated a strong regulatory influence of Meis2 on Il1a expression in LSECs (figure 5D). In contrast, MAFK showed no transcriptional regulatory activity in LSECs, leading us to focus on MEIS2 as the primary regulator of Il1a. Subsequently, RT-qPCR and western blotting assays confirmed the elevated expression of MEIS2 in LSECs of aged rats (figure 5E). Multiplex immunohistochemical staining further validated the correlation between MEIS2 and IL-1α expression in LSECs of aged rats (figure 5F). Moreover, knockdown of MEIS2 in aged rat-derived LSECs significantly reduced IL-1α expression, confirming a positive regulatory role of MEIS2 in IL-1α induction (figure 5G).

Consistent findings were observed in mice. Integrated scRNA-seq and snRNA-seq analyses demonstrated EC-specific expression of Meis2 in mouse livers (online supplemental figure S9A).26 LSECs isolated from aged mice exhibited significantly elevated Meis2 messenger RNA (mRNA) and protein levels (online supplemental figure S9B). Staining assays of liver tissues and primary LSECs confirmed high MEIS2 expression was consistently associated with upregulated IL-1α expression in aged livers (online supplemental figure S9C and D). Moreover, MEIS2 knockdown in aged mouse-derived LSECs led to reduced IL-1α expression, whereas its overexpression in LSECs of young livers markedly elevated IL-1α levels (online supplemental figure S9E and F). To confirm MEIS2 as a transcriptional regulator of Il1a, we first performed the dual-luciferase reporter assays. As shown in figure 5H, Meis2-transfected cells exhibited significantly increased luciferase activity, suggesting that MEIS2 binds to the Il1a promoter region. We subsequently identified four potential MEIS2-binding sites in the Il1a promoter using the JASPAR database (online supplemental figure S9G).27 Dual-luciferase reporter assays demonstrated that MEIS2 predominantly binds to site 4 within the Il1a promoter region, with mutation of site 4 leading to a significant decrease in luciferase activity (figure 5I). Chromatin immunoprecipitation followed by qPCR assays using LSECs from aged livers further confirmed that MEIS2 exhibits significant enrichment at site 4 within the Il1a promoter region, supporting direct transcriptional regulation of Il1a by MEIS2 (figure 5J).

Finally, we confirmed the regulation of MEIS2 on IL-1α expression in human livers during ageing using clinical samples. Our analysis of human liver tissues revealed MEIS2 is specifically expressed in ECs, with a marked upregulation at the terminal stage of the LSEC ageing trajectory (figure 5K, online supplemental figure S10A). To assess whether MEIS2 expression increased progressively or abruptly with age, we analysed snRNA-seq data of young (<30 years) and middle-aged (30–60 years) individuals.9 MEIS2 expression in LSECs was elevated in middle-aged livers, indicating gradual upregulation during human liver ageing (figure 5L, online supplemental figure S10B). ST analysis further revealed increased MEIS2 expression in aged liver tissues. High-resolution imaging demonstrated that MEIS2+ cells were frequently located adjacent to IL1R1+ hepatocytes, particularly in highly senescent regions, suggesting a potential paracrine interaction (figure 5M). Multiplex immunohistochemical staining further confirmed the correlation between MEIS2 and IL-1α expression in LSECs from aged human livers (figure 5N).

Taken together, by integrating single-cell transcriptomic data from rat, mouse and human livers and performing a series of molecular mechanism validation experiments, we identified MEIS2 as an EC-specific transcription factor that positively regulates IL-1α expression in LSECs.

Silencing Meis2 significantly mitigates IRI in aged livers by regulating the IL-1α/IL1R1 axis activation

The results in figure 4 demonstrated that LSECs in aged livers promote inflammatory activation of hepatocytes via the IL-1α/IL1R1 axis, thereby exacerbating aged HIRI. Based on the transcriptional regulation of IL-1α by MEIS2, we speculate that MEIS2 also plays a critical role in aged HIRI. To further investigate this issue, we co-cultured hepatocytes with aged rat-derived LSECs expressing different levels of MEIS2, followed by treatment with H/R. We observed that silencing Meis2 in LSECs significantly reduced hepatocyte apoptosis during H/R (figure 6A). Additionally, knockdown of Meis2 suppressed IL-1α-induced activation of NF-κB signalling pathway and the subsequent release of inflammatory cytokines in hepatocytes (figure 6B). To validate these findings in vivo, we used an AAV to specifically knock down MEIS2 expression in hepatic ECs via portal vein injection, resulting in a significant decrease in IL-1α expression in LSECs (figure 6C and D). Subsequently, rats injected with different AAVs were used to construct HIRI models. As shown in figure 6E and F, AAV-mediated Meis2 knockdown significantly attenuated aged HIRI, evidenced by improved liver function, reduced hepatic tissue damage and decreased hepatocyte apoptosis. Moreover, activation of the NF-κB signalling pathway and cytokine release in hepatocytes was also diminished (figure 6G).

To further validate this regulatory mechanism, we performed similar experiments in mice. In vitro assays showed that knockdown of Meis2 in aged mouse-derived LSECs significantly reduced apoptosis, NF-κB activation and downstream inflammatory responses in hepatocytes during H/R treatments (online supplemental figure S11A-D). Conversely, overexpression of Meis2 in young mouse-derived LSECs significantly enhanced H/R-induced hepatocyte apoptosis and inflammatory cytokine production, which could be reversed by treatment with anti-IL-1α antibody, confirming the role of MEIS2 in regulating aged HIRI via IL-1α (online supplemental figure S11E-H). Then HIRI mouse models with indicated AAV injections further proved that silencing Meis2 in LSECs strongly alleviated aged HIRI (online supplemental figure S12A-C). This protective effect was accompanied by suppression of hepatocellular NF-κB signalling and a reduction in inflammatory cytokine release, consistent with observations in rat HIRI models (online supplemental figure S12D-F).

Collectively, these results demonstrate that knockdown of Meis2 in LSECs significantly mitigated aged HIRI, which is dependent on its regulation of IL-1α and downstream inflammatory signalling pathways, particularly the NF-κB pathway.

We next investigated the mechanism by which MEIS2 upregulated IL-1α in LSECs within the aged hepatic microenvironment using NicheNet analysis.28 Tnf emerged as the top-ranked predicted ligand associated with age-related gene expression changes in LSECs (figure 7A). To test this hypothesis, we treated aged rat-derived LSECs with TNF-α. This stimulation significantly increased the mRNA and protein levels of Meis2 and Il1a, whereas silencing MEIS2 markedly suppressed TNF-α-induced Il1a expression, supporting a MEIS2-dependent regulatory mechanism (figure 7B). As a transcription factor, the transcriptional activity of MEIS2 is strongly associated with its nuclear abundance. To assess whether TNF-α enhances MEIS2 transcriptional activity through subcellular relocalisation, we performed nuclear-cytoplasmic fractionation assays. As shown in figure 7C, TNF-α stimulation led to a significant accumulation of MEIS2 protein in the nucleus. Immunofluorescence analysis confirmed MEIS2 nuclear translocation on TNF-α treatment, suggesting that TNF-α promotes IL-1α expression by enhancing MEIS2 nuclear localisation and transcriptional activation (figure 7D). This regulatory pathway was further validated in aged mouse-derived LSECs, where TNF-α stimulation increased Meis2 and Il1a expression and promoted MEIS2 nuclear accumulation (figure 7E and F).

Additionally, we aimed to investigate the potential sources of TNF-α received by LSECs in aged livers. Interestingly, intercellular communication analysis revealed that hepatocytes exhibited the greatest increase in ligand–receptor interaction counts with LSECs and were the only cell type showing enhanced signalling strength during ageing (figure 7G). Moreover, our aforementioned results (figure 2C,H, online supplemental figure S2A) showed that Tnf expression was significantly elevated in hepatocytes from aged rat, mouse and human livers, suggesting that hepatocyte-derived TNF-α may act as a key paracrine signal modulating IL-1α expression in LSECs via MEIS2 activation.

Taken together, these findings demonstrate that in aged livers, hepatocyte-derived TNF-α induces MEIS2 expression and nuclear translocation in LSECs, which in turn drives IL-1α transcription. The elevated expression of IL-1α can further activate the NF-κB signalling pathway through the IL-1α/IL1R1 axis, promoting TNF-α release from hepatocytes. This TNF-α–MEIS2–IL-1α axis establishes a feed-forward loop between hepatocytes and LSECs that amplifies inflammatory responses and contributes to hepatic stress during ageing.

IL-1α+ LSECs are enriched in aged human liver grafts and correlate with exacerbated hepatic injury following transplantation

With the ageing population increasing, the proportion of aged donor livers in the donor pool has significantly risen. Compared with young donor livers, aged grafts are more susceptible to IRI, a critical predisposing cause of early allograft dysfunction and rejection reactions after liver transplantation (LT). The preceding analyses demonstrated the accumulation of IL-1α+ LSECs and heightened inflammatory signalling within the hepatic microenvironment of aged mouse, rat and human livers, which may contribute to increased susceptibility to stress-induced injury in aged livers. Building on our aforementioned results, we aimed to evaluate the role of IL-1α+ LSECs in aged donor liver-related graft injury using clinical samples from orthotopic LT (OLT) procedures.

A total of 40 patients undergoing OLT were enrolled, and liver graft biopsies were collected 2 hours post-reperfusion. Patients were stratified into young (<30 years) and aged (>55 years) groups based on donor age. Clinical outcomes revealed that the aged group exhibited delayed recovery of liver function, as evidenced by persistently elevated serum ALT and AST levels from postoperative day (POD) 1 to POD 7 (figure 8A). Histopathological evaluation, including H&E and TUNEL staining, showed that aged graft tissues exhibited higher Suzuki injury scores and increased numbers of apoptotic cells (figure 8B). These findings further corroborate that aged grafts are more susceptible to IRI, consistent with previous studies.29 30

To investigate whether IL-1α+ LSECs contribute to graft injury in humans, we performed multiplex immunohistochemical staining to examine IL-1α expression in liver tissues. Aged grafts showed a significantly higher frequency of IL-1α+ LSECs (figure 8C). Taken together, these results obtained from clinical samples validated that IL-1α+ LSECs are enriched in aged grafts and are positively associated with the severity of hepatic injury, suggesting that targeting IL-1α+ LSECs may improve outcomes in aged donor LT.

Anti-IL-1α antibody alleviates age-related liver graft injury by disrupting the LSEC–hepatocyte inflammatory axis

To further elucidate the role of the crosstalk between LSECs and hepatocytes via the IL-1α/IL1R1 axis in aggravating aged graft injury and explore potential therapeutic strategies, we established a rat OLT model using young rats as recipients and divided the donor livers into three groups: young livers, aged livers and aged livers treated with anti-IL-1α antibody. At 24 hours post-reperfusion, serum and liver grafts were collected for biochemical injury assessment, scRNA-seq analysis, multiplex immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining and IHC (figure 9A). Compared with young grafts, aged grafts exhibited significantly elevated serum ALT and AST levels, accompanied by extensive histological injury and increased hepatocyte apoptosis. Treatment with anti-IL-1α antibody significantly alleviated these pathological changes (figure 9B).

Then, scRNA-seq analysis was performed to reveal the corresponding mechanism. t-SNE analysis identified 14 distinct cell populations across all experimental groups (figure 9C; Online supplemental figure S13A). Notably, the proportions of neutrophils, crucial mediators of HIRI, were not significantly altered by anti-IL-1α antibody, suggesting that its protective effect was independent of inflammatory cell recruitment (figure 9D; Online supplemental figure S13B). However, hepatocytes from aged grafts exhibited pronounced activation of inflammation and cell death pathways post-OLT, which were markedly suppressed by anti-IL-1α antibody (figure 9E). Expression levels of key inflammation-associated genes in hepatocytes, including Stat3, Jun, Myd88 and Irak4, also significantly decreased following antibody treatment (figure 9F).

To determine whether the protective effect of anti-IL-1α antibody was mediated by modulating interactions between LSECs and hepatocytes, we performed ligand–receptor interaction analysis. Blocking IL-1α–IL1R1 signalling from LSECs to hepatocytes significantly reduced the number and strength of signals originating from hepatocytes to multiple cell types, particularly the signals directed back to LSECs (figure 9G).

Consistent with the scRNA-seq results from rat livers in figure 5, Meis2 exhibited specific expression in ECs across experimental groups (online supplemental figure S13C). The TNF-α–MEIS2–IL-1α feed-forward loop was further validated with the rat OLT model. IHC showed that anti-IL-1α antibody markedly reduced MEIS2 expression in aged liver grafts (online supplemental figure S13D). Additionally, ligand–receptor interaction analysis demonstrated that in aged grafts treated with anti-IL-1α antibody, hepatocytes exhibited the most pronounced reduction in signalling received from LSECs compared with signals from other cell types (figure 9H). Correspondingly, the proportion of MEIS2+ IL-1α+ LSECs significantly decreased after treatment, reflecting that blockade of IL-1α–IL1R1 signalling between LSECs and hepatocytes attenuates the positive feedback regulation of IL-1α expression within LSECs (figure 9I).

To clarify the impact of recipient age and evaluate the translational relevance of IL-1α blockade in aged LT, we added two cohorts: (1) young-donor livers transplanted into aged recipients (online supplemental figure S14A) and (2) aged-donor livers pretreated with anti–IL-1α transplanted into young recipients (online supplemental figure S15A). Neither recipient age nor donor anti–IL-1α pretreatment significantly affected graft injury (online supplemental figures S14B and S15B). Consistently, scRNA-seq ligand–receptor analysis showed that, among inputs to hepatocytes, LSEC-derived signalling showed no obvious change under either condition (online supplemental figures S14C and D, S15C and D).

In summary, anti-IL-1α antibody effectively attenuates inflammation and hepatocyte injury in aged liver grafts by disrupting inflammatory crosstalk between LSECs and hepatocytes via the IL-1α–IL1R1 axis. These findings highlight IL-1α as a promising therapeutic target to improve graft outcomes in aged LT.

Discussion

HIRI represents a principal contributor to perioperative morbidity and mortality in hepatic surgical interventions, notably during partial hepatectomy and transplantation. This pathophysiological challenge is heightened severity in aged livers; however, the underlying mechanisms remain elusive, thereby limiting the development of targeted therapy. In this study, we integrated scRNA-seq and ST to systematically characterise the senescence-associated spatiotemporal remodelling of hepatic cellular ecosystems, revealing IL-1α-overexpressing LSECs (IL-1α+ LSECs) as spatially clustered sentinels in aged livers. These cells establish a proinflammatory microenvironment via IL-1α/IL1R1-mediated crosstalk with hepatocytes, thereby enhancing age-dependent parenchymal vulnerability under surgical stress. Mechanistically, transcriptional hyperactivation of MEIS2 in senescent LSECs drives IL-1α expression, which activates the hepatocytic NF-κB/TNF-α axis through IL-1α-IL1R1 interactions. This cytokine surge reciprocally amplifies MEIS2 activation in LSECs, thereby establishing a self-reinforcing loop that perpetuates inflammaging propagation. Targeting the IL-1α-IL1R1 axis by anti-IL-1α neutralising antibody significantly ameliorated aged HIRI, providing compelling rationale for clinical trials evaluating this therapy in geriatric patients undergoing hepatic surgery.

Among hepatic cell types, LSECs emerged as one of the most pronounced enrichments of senescence-associated signalling pathways, markedly exceeding that observed in hepatocytes. Emerging evidence presents profound structural remodelling and phenotypic shifts in senescent LSECs that fundamentally disrupt hepatic homeostasis.5 During ageing, LSECs experience a characteristic transition termed ‘pseudocapillarization’, marked by progressive basement membrane collagenisation and aberrant extracellular matrix deposition.31 These microarchitectural alterations impair LSECs’ porosity, culminating in microcirculatory dysfunction and subsequent fibrogenesis.32 Notably, recent work by Wang et al has further elucidated metabolic reprogramming in senescent LSECs, demonstrating impaired fatty acid oxidation capacity that potentiates non-alcoholic steatohepatitis development.7 Building on these foundations, our study uncovered a novel ageing-associated mechanism in which intercellular communication between LSECs and hepatocytes is significantly enhanced. Specifically, senescent LSECs secrete elevated levels of IL-1α, a key component of senescence-associated secretory phenotype. Mechanistic interrogation revealed that IL-1α derived from LSECs mediates intercellular communication by binding to the IL1R1 receptor expressed on hepatocytes, thereby exerting profound regulatory effects on hepatic activity and function. Crucially, two independent animal surgical models, including HIRI and OLT, definitively established the IL-1α-IL1R1 axis as a pathologically significant ligand-receptor pair driving postoperative injury exacerbation in aged individuals undergoing hepatic surgery.

Furthermore, we identified that transcription factor MEIS2 displays relatively specific expression in LSECs, with both its expression levels and transcriptional activity significantly elevated during ageing. MEIS2, a member of the TALE (Three-Amino-acid-Loop-Extension) homeobox transcription factor family, is well-known for its roles in embryonic development, neural differentiation and tumourigenesis.33–35 Willeke de Haan et al reported that MEIS2 serves as a crucial transcriptional regulator that maintains the distinctive LSEC signature and modulates their interactions with viruses.36 Beyond this, the functions of MEIS2 in LSECs remain unclear. Our data demonstrate that ageing induces MEIS2 upregulation and nuclear translocation in LSECs, which in turn directly promotes IL1A transcription via promoter binding. The resulting IL-1α secretion activates NF-κB signalling and TNF-α release in hepatocytes, culminating in hepatocellular inflammatory injury. Functional inhibition of MEIS2 in LSECs significantly reduced hepatic injury under surgical stress. Interestingly, we further identified hepatocyte-derived TNF-α as a potent upstream inducer of MEIS2 activation in LSECs during ageing, thus establishing a TNF-α/MEIS2/IL-1α/IL1R1 positive feedback loop that sustains hepatic inflammaging and stress sensitivity.

Current therapeutic strategies for managing surgical injury in aged livers remain severity limited, with conventional hepatoprotective regimens constituting the cornerstone of clinical management, while persistent suboptimal therapeutic outcomes underscore the necessity for innovative intervention development.37 Based on our mechanistic insights, we proposed novel therapeutic strategies employing AAV9-mediated IL-1α knockdown or anti-IL-1α neutralising antibody and found that these modalities could not only repress the LSEC-hepatocyte crosstalk and the local inflammatory response, but also effectively attenuate pathological injury in the aged liver after establishing HIRI or OLT models. However, the utilisation of AAV vectors still faces challenges such as safety and reversibility.38 39 Several trials have shown negligible efficacy or unexpected toxicity associated with AAV-based therapies, highlighting inherent limitations for clinical translation.38 40 Recently, IL-1α-neutralising antibodies (such as MABp1 and XB2001) are under investigation in clinical trials and have yielded promising results. MABp1 (also named as Bermekimab) was well-tolerated with no dose-limiting toxicities and has shown efficacy in improving outcomes across a range of malignancies and inflammatory disorders.41–44 Similarly, XB2001 has been demonstrated to be safe and feasible for clinical application.45 Notably, IL-1α blockade reverses age-related myelopoiesis driving tumour progression,46 highlighting its safety and efficacy for age-related diseases. Collectively, prior clinical studies on IL-1α-neutralising antibodies and our current findings suggest that MABp1 and XB2001 may represent a promising therapeutic candidate for mitigating surgical stress in geriatric hepatology, highlighting a rationale for further in-depth investigation.

In summary, our study provides the first multimodal characterisation of age-exacerbated HIRI using scRNA-seq and ST, highlighting the IL-1α–IL1R1 axis between LSECs and hepatocytes as a key mediator of senescence-associated hepatic vulnerability. Pharmacological blockade of this axis using IL-1α neutralising antibodies mitigates age-related liver injury and represents a promising therapeutic strategy to improve surgical outcomes in elderly patients. Prospective clinical trials will be essential to evaluate the efficacy and safety of IL-1α targeted therapy in this context.

Totally endoscopic resection of right atrial myxoma on beating heart: a case serries
Authors: Huy Q. Dang, Huong T. Le, Quan T. Phan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThere are few reports on totally endoscopic resection of right atrial (RA) myxoma, all of which were performed after aortic cross-clamping. Beating-heart RA myxoma resection through small trocars and port represents a novel, simple, and safe approach.Case presentationThree patients were diagnosed with RA myxomas arising from the interatrial septum, with sizes ranging from 20 to 64 mm. In two cases, the masses were immobile or minimally mobile, while the remaining patient exhibited significant mobility of the mass through the tricuspid valve, resulting in moderate regurgitation. All patients successfully underwent totally endoscopic myxoma resection on beating heart.ConclusionsCompared to procedures involving aortic cross-clamping, beating-heart surgery offers several advantages: (1) reduced operation times, lowered risk of aortic root complications, and shortened training duration for surgeons. Air embolism prevention techniques, including CO₂ insufflation, arterial pressure control, and keeping the left atrium full of blood, have been demonstrated to be safe through experimental models and large-scale clinical studies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13019-026-03941-0.

Background

Myxoma is the most common benign tumor of the heart, with right atrial (RA) myxoma accounting for 10–20% of cases [1]. There are few reported cases of totally endoscopic resection of RA myxoma, and all procedures have been performed following aortic cross-clamping (ACC) [2, 3]. Due to the need for manipulation at the aortic root, this approach requires a prolonged surgical learning curve and carries potential risks such as bleeding and aortic dissection. This study introduces a simplified, safe, and effective approach to totally endoscopic resection of RA myxoma utilizing beating-heart surgery.

Case presentation

From December 2018 to July 2021, three patients diagnosed with RA myxoma underwent totally endoscopic resection and atrial septal repair on a beating heart. The first case was a 64-year-old female, admitted for left chest pain. Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) showed a 16 × 20 mm RA mass, immobile, sessile, attached to the interatrial septum (IAS). The second case was a 62-year-old male, presented with left chest pain. TTE revealed a 12 × 24 mm RA mass, mobile, non-pedunculated, attached to the IAS. The last case was a 76-year-old female, admitted for facial edema. TTE identified a 42 × 64 mm RA mass, mobile, protruding into the right ventricle during diastole, non-pedunculated, attached to the IAS, with moderate tricuspid regurgitation. As this was a retrospective study, ethics committee approval was not required. Before surgery, all patients and their families received comprehensive information regarding the benefits, limitations, and potential complications of the procedure. Informed consent was obtained from all participants, permitting the use of clinical data, images, and surgical videos for scientific research purposes.

Surgical procedures

The procedure was performed by the same surgeon. During peripheral cardiopulmonary bypass installation, two venous cannulas were inserted using the Seldinger technique under transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) guidance, ensuring that the cannula tips reached the junction between the vena cava with the RA. Arterial cannulation was performed indirectly through an artificial graft. Port and trocars on the right chest included: (1) a 1.5–2 cm main working port at the 5th intercostal space (ICS) on the anterior axillary line, exposure achieved by the smallest size wound protector; (2) a 5.5 mm trocar at the 4th ICS on the midaxillary line for the left-hand instruments; (3) a 10 mm trocar at the 5th ICS on the midaxillary line for 3D endoscopic camera and CO2 insufflation (Fig. 1).

Immediately after the port and trocars placement, the pleural and pericardial spaces were insufflated with CO2 at a flow rate of 2 L/min. The superior vena cava was snared, while the inferior vena cava remained free. The RA was opened and suspended to the pericardium. Myxomas were evaluated based on size, morphology, stalk, and attachment site. Tumors entirely attached to the IAS were completely excised along with the involved IAS. The resected tumor was retrieved using a specimen bag. The atrial septal defect was closed either by direct suturing or with a patch (Fig. 2 and Video 1). The other air embolism prevention measures included: (1) maintaining the left atrium full of blood, (2) ensuring an arterial pressure > 70 mmHg, and (3) inflating the lungs before completing the atrial septal repair. TEE was utilized to confirm the absence of air in the left heart chambers.

Tricuspid valve repair was performed for cases with preoperative tricuspid regurgitation. The procedure was completed without the necessity for epicardial pacing lead placement. Discussion and conclusion

In myxoma resection surgery, complete tumor excision is believed to reduce recurrence rates more effectively than subendocardial resection [4]. For myxoma originating from IAS, complete excision of the base attachment and involved IAS is the standard approach, with a limited number of cases undergoing endoscopic surgery under ACC [2, 3]. A cardiac surgeon with limited experience in endoscopic cardiac surgery must perform a substantial number of consecutive cases in a short timeframe to attain proficiency [4]. Moreover, surgical manipulation on the aortic root carries risks of complications, including aortic dissection, bleeding, and prolonged operative time.

A simpler yet equally safe and effective surgical approach would enable more surgeons to achieve mastery earlier. Compared to conventional procedure, beating-heart surgery eliminates several surgical maneuvers, including aortic root cannulation, ACC, placement of left heart venting, and myocardial protection.

The principles of air embolism prevention outlined in this study were successfully implemented in our prior large-scale report on totally endoscopic atrial septal defect repair on beating heart [5]. With a solubility in water approximately 25 times greater than that of air [6], CO₂ helps reduce the volume of gaseous emboli in left heart chambers, shortens de-airing time threefold, and significantly decreases postoperative microembolism [7]. To achieve a CO₂ concentration of > 90% in the operative field, the insufflation rate is adjusted based on incision size. For mini-thoracotomy, a flow rate of 2 L/min via a small trocar port yields a CO₂ concentration of 92 ± 6%. Higher flow rates paradoxically reduce CO₂ concentration due to turbulence, which may drive air deeper into the operative space [8]. Keeping the left atrium full of blood, while elevated arterial line pressure helps prevent upward migration of air into the aorta. These constitute key safeguards against air embolism during beating-heart surgery [5].

In comparison with previous studies, endoscopic right atrial myxoma resection without ACC reduces cardiopulmonary bypass time and minimizes bleeding risk by eliminating intervention on the ascending aorta [3, 4].

In conclusion, we report three cases of totally endoscopic right atrial myxoma resection on the beating heart. This is a feasible approach that significantly reduces the cardiopulmonary bypass time, minimizes bleeding risk, and facilitates technical proficiency for new surgeons.

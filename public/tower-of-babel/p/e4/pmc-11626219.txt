Histology and line‐field confocal optical coherence tomography in granuloma faciale: A single case study
Authors: Cesare Ariasi, Stefano Bighetti, Luca Bettolini, Caterina Damiani, Maria Chiara Arisi, Vincenzo Maione, Simonetta Battocchio, Simone Soglia
Published: 2024

Dear Editors,

Line‐field confocal optical coherence tomography (LC‐OCT) is a non‐invasive diagnostic technique recently introduced in dermatology. It offers a histological‐like vertical view, a horizontally‐oriented perspective similar to reflectance confocal microscopy (RCM), and a novel tridimensional (3D) reconstruction of the skin up to a depth of approximately 500 μm, emerging as a promising tool for the diagnosis of skin cancer and inflammatory diseases. 1 , 2 , 3 , 4

Granuloma faciale (GF) is a rare and idiopathic inflammatory dermatosis characterized by distinctive clinical and histological features. Clinically, GF presents with asymptomatic red‐brown plaque(s) lesions typically on the facial region. 5 In fewer than 20% of cases, extra facial lesions may also be observed. 6 GF occurs most frequently in middle‐aged men and is more prevalent in individuals of white ethnicity. 7 However, it has been documented in both genders and across various ethnic groups. 5

Histology remains the gold standard for the diagnosis of GF, revealing the presence of a Grenz zone and diffuse, polymorphous inflammatory infiltrate involving the upper half of the dermis, characterized by neutrophils, plasma cells, eosinophils, lymphocytes, and histiocytes. 8 Additionally, findings include leukocytoclasia (karyorrhexis), extravasated red blood cells, hemosiderin deposition, and variable fibrinoid necrosis of small vessels. Perivascular fibrosis with clefting between collagen bundles contributes to the characteristic storiform fibrosis pattern.

A 41‐year‐old male presented with a 3‐year history of multiple asymptomatic brownish macules of the skin on the forehead (Figure 1). Through LC‐OCT (DeepLiveTM LC‐OCT, DAMAE Medical, Paris, France) the most representative portion of the lesion was localized and subsequently biopsied. Histological examination confirmed the diagnosis of GF. Following the diagnosis, the patient was treated with topical tacrolimus 0.1% ointment twice a day, resulting in partial reduction of erythema and the infiltrative component.

(a) The clinical presentation of Granuloma faciale (GF) with multiple brownish patch lesions with clear margins on the frontal area. (b) Dermatoscopic presentation of GF with pink background with orange areas, prominent follicular orifices and Irregular white streaks.

LC‐OCT projections faithfully reflect the histological counterpart showing an epidermis separated from the middle dermis through a well‐defined grenz zone (Figure 2). The dermis appears hyporeflective and poorly defined, probably due to the dense inflammatory infiltrate. Dilated blood vessels are evident among coarse collagen fibers. The trichromic appearance resembles that of a “wave on the shore”.

(a) Vertical LC‐OCT projection of Granuloma faciale (GF) compared to the (b) histological counterpart and (c) 3D LC‐OCT projection. A well‐defined grenz zone (dotted yellow line) separates the epidermis from a hyporefractive dermal infiltrate (red asterisk) among which it is possible to highlight dilated blood vessels (green arrows).

From a histological point of view, the main differential diagnoses include erythema elevatum diutinum, cutaneous IgG4‐related disease, and lymphocytoma cutis (pseudolymphoma), while sarcoidosis, granuloma annulare and lupus tumidus represent the main clinical simulators. Literature lacks LC‐OCT descriptions of these entities except for mycosis fungoides, pseudolymphoma and sarcoidosis. 9 , 10 , 11 Fortunately, these diseases have unique features that allow them to be distinguished by LC‐OCT evaluation. However, it is important to consider the primary limitation of the method, which is penetration depth. Current devices can reach a depth of up to 500 µm before losing definition due to the high scattering of skin cellular components that the light source passes through.

In conclusion, LC‐OCT images of GF show excellent correlation with the histological counterpart and can be useful to guide the diagnosis, narrowing the range of possible diagnostic hypotheses before resorting to biopsy.

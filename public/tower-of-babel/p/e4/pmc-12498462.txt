Visceral fat area loss reduces 10-year atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease risk in Chinese population with type 2 diabetes mellitus: a prospective cohort study
Authors: Jing Jin, Yu Lei, Jia Zheng, Jinjian Xu, Linna Chen, Shuning Liu, Xingyue Cao, Ye Hu, Chenyan Yan, Xiaohong Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAmong individuals diagnosed with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), an abnormal accumulation of visceral fat heightens the cardiovascular risk (CVR), and the major reason for death for these people is atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD). This study aimed to gain further insights into the longitudinal relationship between CVR and visceral fat area (VFA) in patients with T2DM, and to compare the predictive performance of additional abdominal obesity measures and VFA for changes in CVR.MethodsThis prospective cohort study included 316 patients with T2DM who were followed up for more than one year, and VFA was measured by the bioimpedance method. This study investigated the prospective association between a VFA percentage change (∆VFA, %) and CVR, and evaluated the potential nonlinear relationships between ∆VFA (%) and the increase 10-year ASCVD risk. Furthermore, the area under the pooled curve (AUC) was contrasted for both ∆VFA (%) and other abdominal obesity indices.ResultsThe excessive VFA loss group showed lower low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), non-high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (non-HDL-C), total cholesterol (TC), triglyceride–glucose index, LDL-C/HDL-C, brachial-ankle pulse wave velocity, 10-year ASCVD risk, atherogenic index of plasma, TC/HDL-C, and apolipoproteins B/apolipoproteins A-1 than the VFA gain group (all β 0, HR 1, all P 0.05) after covariate controlling. VFA reduction of more than 14.82% led to a reduction in the stated risk. Moreover, ∆VFA (%) demonstrated superior predictive value for changes in ASCVD risk, with an AUC of 0.585 (95% CI: 0.513–0.656), compared to other obesity indices.ConclusionsExcessive VFA reduction improved 10-year ASCVD risk in patients diagnosed with T2DM. VFA was a more effective predictor of 10-year ASCVD risk changes than other abdominal obesity measures.Trial registrationThis investigation has been registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2400086569).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12944-025-02711-6.Keywords: Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, Visceral fat, Type 2 diabetes mellitus

Introduction

Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), a major health condition across the globe, is associated with increased morbidity and mortality from atherosclerotic cardiovascular diseases (ASCVD) [1]. Interestingly, ASCVD is the major reason for mortality among patients with T2DM [2]. A T2DM diagnosis dramatically increases the likelihood of developing cardiovascular disease (CVD) over 10 years for both men and women and all age groups, with young individuals being most affected [3]. Therefore, early prevention of cardiovascular events is a key focus in T2DM management.

Excessive visceral fat is closely linked to CVR among individuals with T2DM. Visceral obesity is a key contributor to T2DM, atherosclerosis, and CVD [4]. Data from the Framingham Heart Study indicate that the accumulation of visceral fat intensifies cardiovascular risk (CVR) factors, including impaired glucose metabolism and dyslipidemia, consequently elevating the occurrence of cardiovascular events within the general population [5, 6]. Given that patients with T2DM exhibit more pronounced metabolic abnormalities, further research is necessary to clarify how the buildup of visceral fat affects their CVR.

The gold standard for assessing visceral obesity is the visceral fat area (VFA), measured using computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) [7]. However, CT radiation exposure and the time-consuming and expensive nature of MRI limit their routine clinical application. Bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA) provides a harmless, inexpensive, and convenient alternative to CT for measuring VFA [8]. Recent studies monitored abdominal fat accumulation using indicators like lipid accumulation product (LAP), Chinese visceral adiposity index (CVAI), body roundness index (BRI), and waist circumference (WC) [9–12], which can indirectly reflect visceral fat buildup levels. Moreover, no studies have investigated the dynamic correlation between visceral fat accumulation and the 10-year ASCVD risk among patients diagnosed with T2DM. Therefore, this study aimed to identify the body anthropometry and other indices for the determination of CVR in individuals diagnosed with T2DM and to explore the long-term relationship between CVR and visceral fat changes.

Methods

Study design and participants

In the present prospective observational study (ChiCTR2400086569), 959 participants were recruited and followed up for more than one year, from March 2020 to May 2024, at Zhejiang Provincial People’s Hospital (Zhejiang, China). Excluded participants comprised those who had diabetes types other than T2DM (n = 51), lacked VFA data (n = 530), had a history of ASCVD (n = 40); or failed to follow up (n = 22), leaving 316 for this analysis (Fig. 1). The study was approved by the hospital’s Ethics Committee (QT2023298) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants provided written informed consent.

Data collection

Baseline characteristics were collected using a standardized questionnaire during patient visits. Laboratory measurements were assayed after an 8-hour nighttime fast (see Supplementary Methods for collection of laboratory indicators). Height, weight, WC, hip circumference, and blood pressure were measured uniformly by specialist nurses (see Supplementary Methods for the collection of anthropometric indicators). A bioelectrical impedance analyser (DUALSCAN HDS-2000, OMRON, Dalian, China) was used to quantify VFA and subcutaneous fat area [13], and a volume plethysmography device (BP-203RPEIII, OMRON, Dalian, China) was used to measure the brachial-ankle pulse wave velocity (baPWV). Pressure measurements for the posterior tibial and brachial arteries were obtained with four cuffs placed around the arms and ankles. By measuring the distance between the patient’s upper arm and ankle, depending on their height, time-phase analysis automatically calculated baPWV. The average of both sides of the body was subsequently used for assessment. Cardiovascular-related risk indices, namely the atherogenic index of plasma (AIP), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol/high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C/HDL-C), triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index, total cholesterol/high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (TC/HDL-C), and apolipoproteins B/apolipoproteins A1 (apoB/apoA1), were also analysed.

Definitions

Grouping was based on the percentage change in VFA (∆VFA, %) as follows: VFA gain (∆VFA ≥ 10%, n = 90); VFA stability (−10% < ∆VFA < 10%, n = 94); mild VFA loss (−20% < ∆VFA ≤ −10%, n = 41); moderate VFA loss (−30% < ∆VFA ≤ −20%, n = 34); and excessive VFA loss (∆VFA ≤ −30%, n = 57). The World Health Organization criteria published in 1999 were used to identify T2DM [14] (see Supplementary Methods for diagnostic criteria).

ASCVD is a clinical condition within the cardiovascular system that results from atherosclerosis [15] (see Supplementary Methods for the conditions). The Prediction for ASCVD Risk in China (China-PAR) model [16], a customized CVR evaluation instrument for Chinese people, was used to evaluate the 10-year ASCVD risk. In this analysis, ‘10-year ASCVD risk increase’, which was chosen as the primary cardiovascular outcome, was defined as a situation where the 10-year risk of ASCVD at the follow-up stage was equal to or exceeded the 1% absolute threshold from baseline. Meanwhile, alterations in lipid markers, cardiovascular-associated parameters, and vascular rigidity were selected as the secondary cardiovascular outcomes.

The formulas used are shown in Supplementary Methods.

Statistical analysis

The Shapiro-Wilk method was employed to assess the normality of the data distribution. After conducting the normality test, continuous parameters have been presented as either the average ± standard deviation or the median along with the inter-quartile range. Frequencies show categorical parameters. Kruskal-Wallis rank sum test evaluated group variations, in addition to analysis of variance. To compare categorical parameters, the chi-squared test was used.

A Cox proportional-hazards regression evaluated potential relationships of VFA groups with the increased 10-year ASCVD risk. Every multivariate model included possible confounding variables. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated for subjects at increased 10-year ASCVD risk, and the areas beneath these ROC curves (AUC) were calculated. A restricted cubic splines (RCS) curve was used in this study in order to analyse the potential non-linear relationship between ∆VFA (%) and the 10-year increased risk of ASCVD (knot = 4, k2 was used as the reference point). Threshold effects were employed to explore the relationship and inflection points between ∆VFA (%) and the increased 10-year ASCVD risk.

Both R v3.6 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) and SPSS v25.0 (IBM Corp, NY, USA) processed all numerals. A statistically significant value of P < 0.05 was determined.

Results

Characteristics at the baseline of the study participants

Table 1 has a list of the general attributes of patients with T2DM grouped by VFA alterations. Of the 316 participants, 236 (74.7%) were male and 80 (25.3%) were female. After a minimum of 1 year and a maximum of 3.5 years of follow-up, 90 (28.5%) participants exhibited VFA gain, 94 (29.7%) exhibited stable VFA, and 132 (41.8%) showed VFA loss, with 41 (13.0%), 34 (9.8%), and 57 (18.0%) showing mild, moderate, and excessive VFA loss. All five VFA groups showed variations in LDL-C, TC, LDL-C/HDL-C, and UA (all P < 0.05) (Table 1).

Contrast of ∆VFA (%), ∆LAP (%), ∆BMI (%), ∆WC (%), ∆BRI (%), and ∆CVAI (%) for predicting ASCVD risk increase in patients with T2DM

∆VFA (%), ∆LAP (%), ∆WC (%), ∆CVAI (%), ∆BRI (%), and ∆BMI (%) have different diagnostic efficacies in predicting increased 10-year ASCVD risk. AUCs and ROC curves for these six indicators were shown in Fig. 2. The AUCs for ∆VFA (%), ∆LAP (%), ∆WC (%), ∆CVAI (%), ∆BRI (%), and ∆BMI (%) for predicting the 10-year increased risk of ASCVD in a population with T2DM were 0.585 (95%CI:0.513–0.656), 0.480 (95%CI: 0.407–0.533), 0.522 (95%CI: 0.452–0.591), 0.535 (95%CI: 0.461–0.609), 0.522 (95%CI: 0.453–0.592), 0.559 (95%CI: 0.488–0.631), and statistically significant only for ∆VFA (%) (P = 0.020) (Fig. 2).

Association between ∆VFA (%) and the CVR factors

The results indicated that ∆VFA (%) exhibited a robust correlation with lipid profiles in both univariate and multivariable regression analyses (P < 0.05) (Table 2). Additionally, throughout the follow-up period, ∆VFA (%) was independently linked to a rise in 10-year ASCVD risk (HR = 1.004, P = 0.005) (Table 2).

Associations between the groups of changes in VFA and the secondary cardiovascular outcomes in patients with T2DM

Using the ∆VFA groups, the association between secondary cardiovascular outcomes and VFA in individuals suffering from T2DM was further examined (Table 3). Table 4 displays the variations in patients’ CVR indices by ∆VFA groups. ∆LDL-C, ∆TC, ∆HDL-C, ∆TyG, ∆non-HDL-C, ∆TC/HDL-C, ∆LDL/HDL-C, and ∆baPWV, along with ∆apoB/apoA-1, ∆AIP differed among the five ∆VFA groups (all P < 0.05) (Table 4). Significant differences were seen between the excessive VFA loss group and the VFA stability and VFA gain groups for ∆HDL-C, ∆TC, ∆LDL-C, ∆TyG, ∆non-HDL-C, ∆LDL/HDL-C, ∆TC/HDL-C, ∆AIP, and ∆apoB/apoA-1. Additionally, the least significant difference (LSD) multiple comparisons showed that the excessive VFA loss group’s ∆baPWV was different from the VFA gain group’s ∆baPWV (all P < 0.05) (Table 3).

Discussion

This study showed that excessive VFA loss was associated with improvements in the 10-year ASCVD risk, lipid indicators, cardiovascular-related indices, and baPWV in participants with T2DM. Notably, a decrease of over 14.82% in VFA was linked to a decline in the patients’ 10-year risk of ASCVD. In addition, ∆VFA (%) was a better indicator of fluctuations in the increased 10-year ASCVD risk than ∆BMI (%), ∆LAP (%), ∆WC (%), ∆BRI (%), and ∆CVAI (%). These findings suggest that excessive VFA loss significantly improves CVR in the target population.

Related cohort studies have found that in the general population, abnormal accumulation of visceral fat is a better determinant of CVR than overall fat [5, 6], while the body fat distribution of T2DM patients is quite different from that of healthy people, which may lead to differences in CVR [17, 18]. Although a former cohort analysis that explored the longitudinal connections between obesity metrics and the chance of cardiovascular complications among persons with T2DM, all of the aforementioned obesity markers had been shown to be strongly correlated to a greater chance of cardiac events [19], none of them could precisely depict the real volume of visceral tissue. Park et al. validated the application of BIA for quantifying visceral fat volume, demonstrating that VFA measured via the DUALSCAN HDS-2000 (bi-abdominal BIA) exhibits greater concordance with CT-derived VFA measurements [20]. Therefore, using VFA as determined by the DUALSCAN HDS-2000 equipment, this study investigated longitudinal estimations of visceral fat and cardiovascular health in patients with T2DM. The primary cardiovascular outcome was the change in the 10-year risk of ASCVD, quantified using the China-PAR model, which was selected as the primary outcome measure because it provides an individualized, intuitive, and validated estimate of 10-year ASCVD risk specifically for the Chinese population [16]. Secondary cardiovascular outcomes included changes in traditional lipid parameters (TC, LDL-C, HDL-C, TG), established lipid ratios and derived indices (TC/HDL-C, LDL-C/HDL-C, apoB/apoA-1, AIP), the TyG index, and baPWV, all of which are known predictors of CVR, but not as advantageously as 10-year ASCVD risk in this study.

This study first found that excessive VFA loss was related to improvement among lipid indicators and cardiovascular-related indices. The observed associations maintained their statistical significance even when accounting for drinking, sex, antihypertensive drugs, hyperlipemia, age, smoking, hypertension, antihyperlipidemic drugs, SGLT2i or GLP-1 RA, and diabetes duration. Visceral obesity is commonly linked to raised LDL-C, TC, and non-HDL-C levels, while reduced HDL-C levels, all of which contribute to a high CVR [21]. According to a cohort study, Japanese men’s elevated visceral fat may interfere with their TG and HDL-C levels [22]. According to the research, patients with excessive VFA loss had considerably lower LDL-C, non-HDL-C, and TC levels and higher HDL-C levels, which may help avert CVD. Nonetheless, these findings did not indicate a significant connection between TG levels and visceral fat. This lack of correlation might be linked to the patients’ dietary makeup [23]. Moreover, this study found that excessive reduction of VFA improved cardiovascular-related indices, such as TC/HDL-C together with LDL-C/HDL-C [24, 25], which is likely attributed to improved LDL-C and TC levels. A prospective study showed that changes in BMI in the general population could mediate CVR by altering the TyG index [26]. This study found that a decrease in VFA levels improved the TyG index in patients with T2DM. Previous cross-sectional studies found that both AIP and apoB/apoA-1 are significantly associated with obesity and CVR [27, 28]. In this study, excess VFA loss was found to increase AIP and apoB/apoA-1 levels in patients with T2DM, which may reduce CVR. Furthermore, even though this study did not detect a relationship between variations in VFA and alterations in the TG/HDL-C among patients with T2DM, certain research has indicated that this parameter was markedly linked to atherosclerosis in individuals with pre-diabetes [29], so this indicator should also be paid attention to.

Atherosclerosis progression can lead to ASCVD, and its severity can be monitored through baPWV [30]. BaPWV is a prognostic indicator of acute coronary syndrome and a risk marker for CVD [31]. VFA is a distinct risk indicator underlying atherosclerosis in persons having T2DM, according to multiple cross-sectional studies [32–34]. Heightened amounts of visceral fat promote insulin insensitivity and increase the levels of free fatty acids in the bloodstream. This, in turn, results in disruptions to lipid metabolism and the development of atherosclerosis [35]. The findings of this research indicated that baPWV was significantly lower in patients with reduced VFA compared to patients with increased VFA, which may be due to the improved mechanisms described above. Thus, patients with T2DM may improve vascular stiffness and thereby reduce CVR by reducing VFA.

Lowering ASCVD risk is a primary goal of T2DM clinical management. Abdominal fat buildup and central obesity have been more significantly linked to cardiometabolic risk factors than total obesity [36], leading to increased interest in addressing visceral fat deposition in T2DM management. Recent research has examined the long-term link between quantified visceral adiposity and CVR, finding a strong correlation between increased visceral adiposity and elevated CVR [37–39]. Against the background, this study quantified visceral fat in patients suffering from T2DM and individualised the risk of ASCVD over a decade using the China-PAR assessment tool [16]. The outcomes of this study indicated that a decrease in VFA exceeding 14.82% was strongly linked to a decline in the aforementioned risk. The improvement of CVR by visceral adiposity reduction may be related to the inhibition of chronic low-grade inflammation (reducing the release of inflammatory factors [40]), improvement of endothelial function (reducing the endothelial toxicity of free fatty acids [41]), inhibition of oxidative stress (repairing mitochondrial function [40]) and other visceral tissue-induced mechanisms. Furthermore, given that visceral adipose tissue is a significant source of the metabolic stress and inflammation implicated in early renal microvascular damage [42] (as suggested by a recent study linking mild metabolic stressors like hyperbilirubinemia to subclinical tubular injury [43]), future studies may further explore the possibility that reducing VFA may also ameliorate such injury.

Visceral fat, deposited around internal organs, can be predicted via anthropometric measures and obesity indices. Although WC is commonly used as an indicator of abdominal obesity, it cannot distinguish visceral from subcutaneous abdominal fat [44]. Similarly, biomarkers like LAP, CVAI, and BRI, based on WC and lipid metrics [45–47], also fail to distinguish between these two types of fat. This study found that longitudinal changes in VFA in patients with T2DM had the highest predictive value for 10-year ASCVD risk variations compared with other obesity indices. However, ASCVD is a complex multi-factor interaction of complex diseases, and a single indicator is very limited in predicting the change of 10-year ASCVD risk. A recent study combined genotype and LDL-C levels to assess arterial stiffness in patients [48], which inspires us to evaluate the risk of ASCVD in patients with T2DM using multiple dimensions (such as VFA combined with metabolic indicators and genes) in the future. This may be complementary to CVR assessment in patients with T2DM.

Lifestyle changes (e.g., dietary control and exercise interventions) [49–51], pharmacological treatments (e.g., SGLT2i and GLP-1 RA [52, 53], which may be synergistic with Inclisiran [54]) and surgical procedures (e.g., bariatric surgery) [55] have demonstrated their efficacy in decreasing visceral fat and improving CVR factors. Although we adjusted for smoking, alcohol use, and related medications, unmeasured confounders (dietary details, exercise intensity, medication adherence, etc.) may persist. In the future, it is necessary to strengthen the unified management of diet and exercise for patients (such as nutrition and rehabilitation units), and improve patient compliance (such as biannual Inclisiran enhances adherence).

Strengths and limitations

The research being conducted has several strengths. First, this prospective cohort study can effectively explain the longitudinal association of quantified visceral adiposity with CVR in patients with T2DM. Second, to make sure these findings were reliable, the study corrected the results for possible confounders. This study does have certain drawbacks, though. First, it used neither CT nor MRI to quantify visceral fat, resulting in an inaccurate reflection of its quantity and quality. Therefore, the VFA in this study may be subject to measurement bias. Second, because of the limited follow-up period, this study could not analyse the relationship between VFA changes and cardiovascular endpoint events. However, the follow-up study will further extend the follow-up time to explore the relationship between the two mentioned above, as well as the prospective validation of the optimal thresholds and cardiovascular endpoint events in this study. Third, the investigation of causal relationships between VFA and CVR in patients with T2DM is constrained by this research’s observational character. Fourth, residual confounding as well as selection bias could persist even after multivariate models account for possible risk factors. Fifth, the metabolic phenotype of Asian populations with lower BMI but relatively higher intrinsic adiposity [56], and the China-PAR used in this study, have limitations for the extension of this study to non-Asian populations. Establishing a multi-ethnic and multi-center research consortium. This will test the generalizability of our findings and optimize clinical translation.

Conclusion

To conclude, this prospective cohort study suggested that excessive VFA reduction in patients with T2DM could result in cardiovascular benefits. In addition, quantified VFA may better predict CVR changes in patients with T2DM than abdominal obesity markers. Therefore, dynamic follow-up of VFA in patients with T2DM is needed for early detection of trends in CVR in this population and early initiation of protective interventions. Future studies should validate the cardiovascular benefit of VFA reduction in other populations as well as its benefit on clinical outcomes.

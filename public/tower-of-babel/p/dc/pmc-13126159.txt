Nanopore Fingerprinting of Neurodegenerative Proteins and Phosphoproteins within a Minute
Authors: Kai-Li Xin, Lin-Lin Zhang, Meng-Yin Li, Shao-Chuang Liu, Han-Qing She, Yi-Lun Ying, Yi-Tao Long, Zheng-Li Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Reliable identification of proteins and their post-translationally modified variants remains a formidable analytical challenge due to charge heterogeneity, sequence similarity, and comparable molecular weights. In this study, we demonstrate distinctive nanopore current fingerprints for clear identification of neurodegenerative disease-associated Tau protein and its phosphorylated variants using an asymmetric-electrolyte sensing system composed of different salts. The asymmetric configuration facilitates simultaneous detection of positively, neutrally, and negatively charged peptide fragments, resulting in 3.2–16-fold higher capture frequencies and 2.1–5.3-fold longer event durations, thereby yielding information-rich fingerprints that enhance protein recognition. Protein profiling was achieved within 1 min through integration with a droplet nanopore platform, which reduces sample consumption to the nanogram level while increasing throughput to >1800 events per minute. This work advances the nanopore fingerprinting approach for rapid, high-throughput, and low-sample protein biomarker detection, offering strong potential for clinical proteomics and early disease diagnosis.

Introduction

Characterizing proteins and their post-translationally modified variants is of paramount importance, as these molecular forms critically determine cellular function, shape disease mechanisms, and influence therapeutic responses. For example, aberrant Tau phosphorylation represents a key molecular signature of Alzheimer’s disease and other neurodegenerative disorders. Existing methods, such as Edman degradation, mass spectrometry, and antibody-based assays, − have significantly advanced the field. Nevertheless, these techniques are often labor-intensive, costly, and limited in sensitivity and efficiency, particularly under sample-limited conditions. Thus, there is an urgent need for high-throughput, ultrasensitive, and cost-effective platforms capable of resolving subtle differences in the amino acid composition among protein variants.

Nanopore technology has emerged as a powerful platform for proteomics research, providing high spatiotemporal resolution, sensitivity, and operational simplicity by monitoring ionic current changes as individual molecules traverse an electrolyte-filled nanoscale pore. − Numerous studies have demonstrated that nanopores can achieve sequence-resolved readout of short peptides, allowing highly sensitive differentiation of single-amino acid variations, − PTM identities, − and even chiral isomers. − However, for native proteins, which are typically large and structurally complex, direct analysis by nanopores remains challenging due to their limited ability to enter or translocate through the nanopore. To address the size limitation, several strategies have been developed for probing large proteins, such as distinguishing thioredoxin phosphorylation states or other modifications by driving proteins through the nanopore using DNA-tagged or engineered polyproteins via cotranslocational unfolding events. , In parallel, enzyme motors have been employed to regulate stepwise translocation, allowing recognition of amino acid substitutions and phosphorylation within defined sequence contexts of synthetic poplypeptides. , Despite these advances, studies on polypeptide and protein variants have largely relied on artificially synthesized samples or sequence-customized constructs, thereby limiting their scalability and broader applicability.

As an alternative for native protein detection, nanopore fingerprinting offers a more scalable and convenient approach by analyzing electrical signals derived from enzymatically digested peptide fragments, addressing limitations arising from the difficulty of translocating large proteins through protein nanopores that usually possess small pore diameters. , Accordingly, detecting a larger number of peptide fragments through nanopore fingerprinting is key to generating information-rich signatures that enable reliable single protein characterization. Herein, to generate rich nanopore fingerprints, we integrate an asymmetric electrolyte strategy with a droplet nanopore platform, achieving the rapid charge-independent capture of enzymatically digested peptide fragments (Figure ). The asymmetric KCl (cis)/MgCl2 (trans) electrolyte resulted in a 3.2–16 fold increase in capture frequency for peptides with different charge properties, ensuring the acquisition of more informative fingerprint profiles. Using cosine-similarity analysis of nanopore current fingerprints, neurodegenerative disease-associated proteins and phosphorylated Tau (pTau) variants can be unambiguously identified.

Results and Discussion

Asymmetric Electrolytes Enhance Heterogeneously Charged Peptide Coverage

To enhance peptide fragment capture, we examined a series of electrolyte conditions using the T274S mutant aerolysin nanopore in which the cis side electrolyte was maintained as KCl while the trans side electrolyte was changed to KCl, NaCl, LiCl, and MgCl2 (Figure a and Figure S1). By adding the model neutral peptide VS-11 (N’-VQIVYKPVDLS-C’) to the cis side, only rare blockade events were observed under conventional symmetric KCl conditions, which can be attributed to the weak electrophoretic driving force experienced by neutral analytes (Figure b). By contrast, implementing asymmetric electrolyte conditions led to a 1.7–7.4 fold enhancement in peptide capture rates (Figure c, Figures S2–S3). Specifically, under a fixed cis side KCl electrolyte, varying the trans side electrolyte among monovalent-cation solutions in the order of KCl, NaCl, and LiCl resulted in an increase in the peptide capture frequency, from 7.5 ± 0.4 s–1 to 21 ± 1 s–1. Moreover, introducing the divalent cation Mg2+ on the trans side led to a markedly high capture frequency of 55 ± 5 s–1, exceeding that observed with trans side monovalent cation electrolytes. Further experiments in WT aerolysin nanopores yielded similar results, confirming the general applicability of the asymmetric KCl/MgCl2 condition in nanopore sensing (Figure S4). Then, to investigate the molecular mechanism of magnesium ions in the nanopore sensing, all-atom molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were performed under symmetric 1 M KCl/1 M KCl and 1 M MgCl2/1 M MgCl2 solutions (Figure S5). Compared with K+ in KCl solution, Mg2+ exhibits preferential accumulation near acidic residues located in D222/S274/S272 and E258/T240/S256, with local concentrations up to 3.1-fold higher (Figure S6). This slightly selective enrichment of Mg2+ induces a pronounced reshaping of the local electrostatic landscape within the nanopore (Figure S7). Therefore, strong cation–residue interactions are expected to locally modulate the charge distribution within the nanopore, which may contribute to enhanced electroosmotic effects and a reduced energetic barrier for peptide capture. As an additional control, we examined symmetric 1 M MgCl2/1 M MgCl2 conditions (Figure S8). The capture frequency of VS-11 peptides in symmetric MgCl2 was 15 ± 3 s–1, exceeding the value (7.5 ± 0.4 s–1) obtained in symmetric KCl. Notably, under asymmetric 1 M KCl/1 M MgCl2 conditions, the capture frequency was still 3.8-fold higher than that under symmetric MgCl2 conditions. This can be attributed to Mg2+ on the cis side coordinating with negatively charged residues in the analytes, reducing the effective electrophoretic force and highlighting the importance of the asymmetric electrolyte configuration.

Along with the increased capture frequency, the dwell time of the VS-11 peptide was significantly prolonged under asymmetric electrolyte conditions. Specifically, the dwell time reaches ∼1.91 ± 0.07 ms in asymmetric 1 M KCl/1 M MgCl2 electrolyte solutions, 5.3-fold higher than that observed in 1 M KCl/1 M KCl (Figure c, Figures S3–S4). Prolonged dwell times reduce event-to-event variability in the residual current blockage (I/I 0) distributions and yield more well-defined current signatures, thereby improving the discriminability of nanopore fingerprints.

Then, additional peptides with distinct charge properties were investigated in the asymmetric KCl/MgCl2 electrolyte solutions. By substituting the negatively charged aspartic acid (D) with neutral asparagine (N), VS-11 was converted into a positively charged peptide, VS-11N (N’-VQIVYKPVNLS-C’) (Figure a). Under symmetric KCl conditions, this positively charged peptide was difficult to capture by the T274S aerolysin nanopore, with a capture frequency of 0.43 ± 0.20 s–1, as the positive charge causes the direction of electrophoretic force to oppose that of analyte entry (Figure S9). In contrast, when the asymmetric KCl/MgCl2 electrolyte condition was applied, the capture frequency increased by 16.5-fold. Meanwhile, the dwell time was prolonged by 2.1 times under asymmetric KCl/MgCl2 conditions compared with symmetric KCl conditions, leading to a more concentrated residual current blockage I/I 0. A similar enhancement was observed for the negatively charged peptide VS-11p (N’-VQIVYKPVDLpS-C’), derived from phosphorylation. Under asymmetric KCl/MgCl2 conditions (Figure b, Figure S10), it showed a higher capture frequency at 120 ± 2 s–1 and a longer dwell time at 3.11 ± 0.02 ms than under the symmetric KCl/KCl conditions. These consistent trends demonstrate that the asymmetric 1 M KCl (cis)/1 M MgCl2 (trans) electrolyte solution provides a robust sensing environment for peptide detection using the mutant aerolysin nanopore. We then further evaluated its quantitative capability by using the conserved Tau protein fragment HK-19 (Tau 299–317) (Figure c and Figures S11–S12). Distinct blockade events were observed at HK-19 concentrations as low as 30 nM, with a stable current blockade peak at I/I 0 = 0.32 ± 0.01, demonstrating reliable detectability at low-abundance peptide detection. Besides, the capture frequency increased proportionally with peptide concentration, reaching 80 ± 8 s–1 at 50 μΜ. Therefore, our method provides a relatively broad dynamic quantitative range spanning 3 orders of magnitude, from nanomolar to micromolar levels.

Identification of Neurodegenerative Proteins and pTau Variants via Nanopore Fingerprinting

The asymmetric KCl/MgCl2 electrolyte solution was further applied to detect enzymatically digested peptide fragments from six neurodegenerative peptides/proteins, enabling more comprehensive peptide profiling. Each sample (2 μg) was subjected to an identical protocol involving disulfide bond reduction, alkylation, and trypsin digestion (experimental details are provided in the Supporting Information). Tryptic peptide fragments were directly introduced into the cis solution and detected (Figures S13–S14) for nanopore fingerprinting without desalination, thereby minimizing protein loss and contamination.

Figure a shows the normalized nanopore current fingerprints from digested WT-Tau, WT-αSyn, WT-Aβ42, and the kinase-specific CaMKII-phosphorylated Tau (CaMKII-pTau), DYRK1A-phosphorylated Tau (DYRK1A-pTau), and GSK3β-phosphorylated Tau (GSK3β-pTau) variants. Distinct characteristic I/I 0 peak distributions with good reproducibility were observed for the independent nanopore measurements (Figure S15). Under symmetric KCl electrolyte conditions, only one or two, or barely any, distinct characteristic I/I 0 peak distributions are observed (Figure a, right). In contrast, the asymmetric 1 M KCl (cis)/1 M MgCl2 (trans) electrolyte produces more well-resolved current fingerprints, with clearly separated peak positions and well-defined normalized frequency, highlighting the improved discriminability of peptide fragments (Figure a, left). Taking WT-Tau protein digestion as an example, only four negatively charged peptides, which can potentially be captured, are generated among the 33 theoretical tryptic fragments (Table S1), resulting in only two major peaks in the I/I 0 distribution under the symmetric KCl condition. In contrast, 1 M KCl (cis)/1 M MgCl2 (trans), which allows more digestion fragments to be detected, produces a more characteristic I/I 0 peak distribution and enriched nanopore fingerprints. To potentially assign the I/I 0 peaks to specific peptide fragments, the HK-19 peptide was added into digested WT-Tau, strongly enhancing the characteristic peak at I/I 0 ∼0.30–0.40 (Figure S16). In contrast, digested GSK3β-pTau showed substantially lower intensity at the same positions (Figure a), consistent with the absence of the HK-19 fragment in its digestion products (https://bit.ly/2JyZTbS). These results highlight the potential of this approach to identify signature peptide fragments within complex proteolytic digests.

The validity of the nanopore fingerprints was further evaluated using similarity analysis. Normalized nanopore current fingerprints, obtained from accumulated blockade events with increasing recording time, were compared with those from the complete data set to assess fingerprint reconstruction (Figure b,c, left), and a similarity threshold of 0.90 was defined as the criterion for reliable reconstruction. It is shown that cosine similarity was the most effective metric, allowing a robust single-protein nanopore fingerprint to be reconstructed in as short as 4 min under asymmetric KCl/MgCl2 electrolyte conditions, with only ∼366 blockade events (Figure b,c, right, and Figures S17–S18).

Based on this result, the metric was subsequently applied to compare the nanopore fingerprints across different proteins. As reflected by the distinct similarity coefficient, the resulting cosine similarity matrices revealed clear identification among three types of wild-type neurodegenerative disease-related proteins as well as among three phosphorylated pTau variants (Figure d). Importantly, independent prediction experiments consistently validated nanopore fingerprint identification using cosine similarity with the highest similarity coefficient, demonstrating the robustness of this similarity-based nanopore fingerprint identification strategy and confirming that even related analytes can be reliably distinguished (Figure e).

Native Tau Protein Profiling Using a High-Throughput Droplet Nanopore Platform

To facilitate rapid protein profiling with minimal sample input, we introduced the droplet nanopore technique that supports high-throughput biomolecule analysis. − WT-Tau protein was detected using only 80 ng of sample in a 2 μL droplet using asymmetric electrolyte, while throughput was effectively preserved (Figure a). Distinct blockade events from trypsin-digested WT-Tau peptide fragments were observed at a frequency of 1939 ± 151 events per minute, corresponding to an approximately 59-fold increase over the conventional symmetric-electrolyte platform (Figure b). The resulting nanopore current fingerprints showed highly consistent spectral features, with cosine similarity scores of 0.90, 0.96, and 1.00 at N = 500, 1000, and 1500 events, respectively (Figure c,d). Consequently, protein profiling can be achieved within 30 s using the asymmetric-electrolyte droplet nanopore platform. Moreover, similarity coefficients remained nearly constant across equal-sized subsets (e.g., N = 500) throughout a 40 min recording, suggesting robust nanopore fingerprinting with minimal data input requirements (Figure S19).

Conclusions

In this work, we developed a robust nanopore-based fingerprinting strategy that combines asymmetric electrolyte design with droplet nanopore sensing, achieving increased detected peptide coverage and yielding more information-rich digested protein fingerprints. Although specific peaks cannot yet be definitively assigned to individual peptide fragments, the reproducible overall distributions of current blockage enable characteristic nanopore fingerprints that allow discrimination of neurodegenerative disease-related proteins ranging from 42 residues (WT-Aβ42) to 441 residues (WT-Tau), including pTau isoforms (CaMKII-pTau, DYRK1A-pTau, and GSK3β-pTau) through cosine similarity analysis. Moreover, it demonstrates the potential of this nanopore current fingerprint strategy to discriminate subtle molecular differences beyond the reach of conventional methods. Notably, rather than relying on site-specific labeling, this approach enables label-free identification of protein isoforms, offering a general and scalable solution to resolving complex isoform heterogeneity. By integrating asymmetric electrolytes with a droplet nanopore platform, rapid protein profiling at the nanogram scale can be achieved within 1 min. With further incorporation of AI-assisted analysis to correlate current blockage features with peptide physicochemical properties and expanded reference digested peptide data sets, this strategy may enable fragment-level peak assignment, providing a more accurate framework for probing complex proteoform landscapes, ultimately extending toward clinical applications.

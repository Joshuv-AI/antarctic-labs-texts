Vascular Surgery in Japan: 2017 Annual Report by the Japanese Society for Vascular Surgery
Authors: The Japanese Society for Vascular Surgery Database Management Committee Member, NCD Vascular Surgery Data Analysis Team
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Objectives: This is an annual report indicating the number and early clinical results of annual vascular treatment performed by vascular surgeons in Japan in 2017, as analyzed by Database Management Committee (DBC) members of the Japanese Society for Vascular Surgery (JSVS).Materials and Methods: To survey the current status of vascular treatments performed by vascular surgeons in Japan, the DBC members of the JSVS analyzed the vascular treatment data provided by the National Clinical Database, including the number of treatments and early results such as operative and hospital mortality.Results: In total, 137,909 vascular treatments were registered by 1,076 institutions in 2017. This database comprised seven fields including treatment of aneurysms, chronic arterial occlusive disease, acute arterial occlusive disease, vascular injury, complication of previous vascular reconstruction, venous diseases, and other vascular treatments. The number of vascular treatments in each field was 21,680, 18,123, 4,765 2,418, 669, 48,625, and 41,629, respectively. In the aneurysm treatment, 19,982 cases of abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA), including common iliac aneurysm, were registered, and 64.1% were treated by endovascular aneurysm repair (EVAR). Among AAA cases, 1,824 (9.1%) cases were registered as ruptured AAA. The operative mortality rates of ruptured and unruptured AAA were 15.0%, and 0.7%, respectively. 37.9% of ruptured AAA were treated by EVAR, and the EVAR ratio was gradually increasing, but the operative mortality rates of open repair and EVAR for ruptured AAA were 14.5%, and 12.3%, respectively. There was no statistical significance. Regarding chronic arterial occlusive disease, open repair was performed in 7,277 cases, including 1,348 distal bypasses to the crural or pedal artery, whereas endovascular treatment (EVT) was performed in 9,248 cases. The EVT ratio was gradually increased at 56.0%. The number of varicose vein treatments decreased to 46,754 (11.2% less than in 2016), and 73.7% of the cases were treated by endovenous laser or radiofrequency ablations. Regarding other vascular operations, 38,769 cases of vascular access operations and 1,548 lower limb amputation surgeries were included.Conclusions: The number of vascular treatments increased since 2011, and the proportion of endovascular procedures increased in almost all fields of vascular diseases, especially EVAR for AAA and EVT for chronic arterial occlusive disease. (This is a translation of Jpn J Vasc Surg 2021; 30: 359–379.)

Introduction

Since establishing the National Clinical Database (NCD) and launching the surgical case registry system in 2011, the Japanese Society for Vascular Surgery (JSVS) has compiled vascular surgery cases from the NCD-registered cases and published the results as vascular surgery annual reports.1–11) This paper reports the results of the compilation and analysis of NCD-registered vascular surgery cases between January and December 2017 by members of the Database Management Committee (DBC) of the JSVS.

Methods

Based on the vascular surgery data extracted upon request of the JSVS, a member society of the NCD, NCD-registered surgery cases in 2017 were classified and compiled into the following seven categories, and the compiled results were analyzed, after data checked, by members of the DBC of the JSVS. The categories were as follows: (1) revascularization for aneurysms, (2) revascularization for chronic arterial occlusive diseases, (3) revascularization for acute arterial occlusive diseases, (4) treatment for vascular traumas, (5) surgery for complications after revascularization, (6) venous surgery, and (7) other vascular diseases and related surgery.

As compilation results, the data for different surgical procedures, such as the number of cases, etiology, number of operative deaths, number of in-hospital deaths, and materials used, are shown. Operative deaths, synonymous with surgical deaths, refer to deaths within 30 days after surgery and include all deaths occurring within 30 days after surgery, regardless of the cause of death or whether the patient was hospitalized. In-hospital deaths refer to deaths occurring during a period that the patient was hospitalized after surgery continuously, regardless of the length of hospitalization.

For data on etiology and materials used, there are some discrepancies in the numbers shown in tables; for example, the sum of case numbers is not in agreement with the total number of cases. As a result of careful investigation by this committee and the NCD, the discrepancies were concluded to be attributable to one of the following four causes: (1) selection of multiple options was allowed, (2) leaving the field blank was an allowed option, (3) omission or error made by the data entry person, or (4) multiple types of materials were used or multiple areas were treated in one operation.

Since 2013, measures were taken, including the arrangement of options being changed and new options being created to avoid input errors whenever possible; as for input omissions, the program was modified not to complete the case registration with a blank field whenever possible.

Compilation/Analysis Results

A total of 137,909 cases of vascular surgery were registered in the NCD in 2017 (a 2.7% increase from the previous year). They accounted for 9.0% of all surgical cases registered in the NCD in 2017. Moreover, the number of institutions that registered a vascular surgery case has reached 1,076, meaning that 27.9% of the institutions that registered a surgical case registered a vascular surgery case. Of these 1,076 institutions, 472 (43.9%) were certified cardiovascular surgery training facilities that contributed to this data registration as of 2017. The compilation/analysis results in 2017 are discussed below by category. For statistical analysis, the chi-squared test was used, and a p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

1. Treatment for Aneurysms (Tables 2-1 and 2-2)

1) Thoracic aortic aneurysms

For surgical treatments of thoracic aortic aneurysms, most of the cases are registered in the Japan Cardiovascular Surgery Database (JCVSD), and some of the cases in which surgery was performed by vascular surgeons are compiled in this vascular surgery database through the NCD (Table 2). Therefore, the registered data on surgical treatments of thoracic aortic aneurysms performed in Japan are currently fragmented, and the accurate overall picture of those cases is impossible to capture. Moving forward, through discussion with the JCVSD, efforts must be made to allow for understanding the accurate overall picture of surgical treatments of thoracic aortic aneurysms performed in Japan.

2) Abdominal aortic aneurysm (Tables 2-1 and 2-2)

The total number of NCD-registered cases of surgery for abdominal aortic aneurysms (AAAs; including common iliac artery aneurysms) was 19,982 in 2017 and has been increasing every year, albeit slightly, as 18,907 cases in 2015 and 19,144 cases in 2016. These cases included 7,152 cases (37.4%) of replacement and 12,267 cases (64.1%) of endovascular aneurysm repair (EVAR), including hybrid surgery. The percentage of EVAR continued to increase since it exceeded 50% in 2013 (55.7% in 2014, 57.6% in 2015, and 60.3% in 2016). The number of cases of replacement has been almost constant, remaining around 7,000 to 8,000 cases (Fig. 1).

Fig. 1 Treatment procedure for non-ruptured and ruptured AAA. Comparing years 2011, 2012, 2013, 2014, 2015, and 2016, the proportion of EVAR selection was gradually increased in 2017. AAA: abdominal aortic aneurysm; EVAR: endovascular aneurysm repair

Among replacement cases, renal artery clamping and reconstruction were required in 1,446 cases (20.2%) and 402 cases (5.6%), respectively. The number of surgical cases of pararenal arterial lesions requiring renal artery clamping has been increasing steadily with the use of EVAR (15.8% in 2014, 16.6% in 2015, and 17.6% in 2016).

Regarding the treatment results in non-ruptured cases, the operative and in-hospital mortality rates were 1.0% and 1.4%, respectively, for replacement and 0.6% and 0.7%, respectively, for EVAR, including specialized and hybrid procedures (p <0.01 and p <0.01) (Fig. 2). Even among replacement cases, the rates increased to 1.5% and 2.2% in cases in which the renal artery was clamped and further to 2.8% and 4.2% when reconstruction was also required. These rates worsened compared to those in 2016 (0.9% for renal artery clamping and 2.0% for reconstruction), suggesting that the proportion of more difficult cases may have increased as a result of the expansion of indications for EVAR.

Fig. 2 Early clinical results of non-ruptured AAA in 2017 are compared with years 2011, 2012, 2013, 2014, 2015, and 2016. AAA: abdominal aortic aneurysm

The number of surgical cases of ruptured AAAs was 1,824, and the operative and in-hospital mortality rates in these cases were 15.0% and 18.0%, respectively. The operative and in-hospital mortality rates were similar to those in 2016 (15.7% and 19.2%, respectively). EVAR was performed in 691 cases (37.9%), and the percentage of EVAR in ruptured cases has tended to increase continuously (30.1% in 2014, 33.9% in 2015, and 35.9% in 2016) (Fig. 1). The operative and in-hospital mortality rates of EVAR for ruptured cases were 12.3% and 15.8%, respectively, showing a tendency toward slight improvement from 2014 (17.1% and 20.3%, respectively) through 2015 (14.5% and 18.5%, respectively) and 2016 (15.3% and 18.8%, respectively). While the number of EVAR performed on ruptured lesions has been on the rise, the results have been stable because each institution may have accumulated EVAR experiences in ruptured cases as well as total EVAR experiences. Although there might be bias in patient selection (generally, EVAR may be selected for anatomically and hemodynamically favorable patients), the results were equivalent to or slightly better than 14.5% and 17.1%, respectively, of replacement (Fig. 3).

3) Peripheral artery aneurysm (Tables 2-3 and 2-4)

The external iliac artery was added as an item in the lower limb artery region. A total of 2,840 cases were registered, in which male patients predominate with a male–female ratio of 2,197:643. Affected regions were lower limb arteries in 2,169 cases, abdominal visceral arteries in 355 cases, upper limb arteries in 334 cases, and aortic arch branches in 87 cases. There seemed to be multiple lesions at different regions in 105 cases. For affected arteries, 43.8% were accounted for by the internal iliac artery, 13.7% by the femoral artery, 9.5% by the popliteal artery, 5.6% by the brachial artery, and 5.2% by the external iliac artery. Of internal iliac artery aneurysms, 43.7% were unilateral solitary, 12.7% were bilateral solitary, and 37.2% were associated with AAAs. Overall, symptomatic cases accounted for 30.1% and were more common among brachial artery aneurysms (62.2%), femoral artery aneurysms (52.4%), and popliteal artery aneurysms (52.1%). Aneurysms were ruptured in 12.1%. The most common etiology was degenerative diseases (76.4%). Surgical procedures used were coil embolization at 27.6%, stent grafting at 23.2%, ligation/resection at 17.9%, replacement at 17.3%, and exclusion with bypass at 6.0%, showing that endovascular treatments (EVTs) accounted for 50.8%. The data suggest that multiple surgical procedures were used in 14.0% of cases. While the number of registered cases increased by 13.2% from 2016, the incidence rates by the affected lesion and percentages of different causes, symptomatic cases, and EVT remained unchanged from 2016.

2. Reconstruction for Chronic Arterial Occlusive Diseases (Table 3)

1) Aortic arch branches and upper limb and abdominal visceral arteries (Tables 3-1 and 3-6)

In 2017, the carotid, vertebral, and subclavian arteries were affected in 276, 8, and 527 cases, respectively; aortic arch branches had multiple lesions in 78 cases; and the celiac artery/superior mesenteric artery was affected in 103 cases. Slight decreases in number of cases from 2016 were noted. The number of cases of axillary artery–upper limb artery diseases did not change substantially (140 cases). The number of cases of carotid artery diseases treated with carotid artery stenting (CAS)/carotid endarterectomy (CEA) increased from 61 cases in 2016 to 112 cases. Carotid-subclavian bypass and axillary-carotid bypass decreased from 175 and 134 cases to 134 and 86 cases, respectively; the decreases presumably reflect that more cases were correctly listed in the debranch section compared with that of the 2016 data. Regarding debranch for TEVAR/EVAR in 2017, the number of registered cases was 23 for ascending aorta-brachiocephalic-left common carotid (-left subclavian) arterial bypass, 262 cases for right axillar (subclavian)-left common carotid (-left subclavian) arterial bypass and right common carotid-left common carotid arterial bypass + left common carotid-left subclavian arterial bypass, 223 cases for right axillar (subclavian)-left axillar (subclavian) arterial bypass, and 32 cases for abdominal aorta-superior mesenteric-renal arterial bypass. There were no major changes, except for the number of cases for right axillar (subclavian)-left axillar (subclavian) arterial bypass, which decreased substantially compared to 325 cases in 2016. Bypass surgery of aortic arch branches considered to be related to these debranch procedures has increased substantially in number year by year, presumably reflecting that endovascular aortic repair with debranching has already become a sufficiently common surgical treatment for anatomically complex aortic arch aneurysms in Japan.

Aortoiliac region: For anatomical bypass surgery for lesions in the aortoiliac region, the number of cases decreased by 14% from 609 cases in 2016 to 525 cases in 2017, but there were no changes in the breakdown, including the etiology and graft materials used. Similarly, slight decreases in the number of cases were observed for extra-anatomic revascularization procedures, such as axillo-femoral bypass and femoro-femoral bypass, from 305 and 798 cases, respectively, in 2016 to 309 and 731 cases, respectively, in 2017, presumably because EVT in this lesion increased (Fig. 4A). Superficial femoral artery region: Femoral-above-the-knee popliteal arterial bypass surgery decreased in number by 14% from 1,624 cases in 2016 to 1,396 cases in 2017. Moreover, there was an increase of 790 cases of EVT in this region; however, the increase was less than the decrease in bypass surgery, suggesting that conservative treatment is chosen increasingly more commonly for lesions in this region (Fig. 4B). Patients had a history of previous revascularization in 29% of cases; expanded polytetrafluoroethylene (ePTFE) grafts accounted for 70% and autologous veins were used in 22%, showing no appreciable differences compared to the data from the previous year.

Below-the-knee revascularization: Regarding below-the-knee revascularization cases, femoro-below-the-knee popliteal arterial bypass and femoro-crural/pedal arterial bypass were 690 and 1,348 cases, respectively, in 2017, which were similar to 752 and 1,329 cases, respectively, in 2016. Dialysis cases accounted for 41% of below-crural bypass cases, with no changes from the previous year; the percentages remained similar since 2014, suggesting that severe limb ischemia cases undergoing dialysis account for approximately 40% (Figs. 4B, 4C). Autologous veins were used in 90% of graft cases.

Thromboendarterectomy (Table 3-4): For thromboendarterectomy of lower limb arteries, the number of thromboendarterectomy cases in the femoro-popliteal region was 1,040 in 2016 but increased to 1,205 in 2017 (Fig. 4B).

Other surgical procedures, including replacement, did not increase or decrease, remaining constant between 546 cases in 2016 and 538 cases in 2017. These cases presumably include a large number of femoral artery replacements. Treatments for common femoral artery regions, which are refractory to EVT, were considered to have remained unchanged compared with the previous year.

EVTE (Table 3-5): The total number of EVT cases was 7,634, showing an increase of approximately 500 cases or 7% from 2016. Of these cases, 25% were dialysis cases. While surgical revascularization procedures in the lower limb lesion (bypass, thromboendarterectomy) tended to decrease compared with 2016, EVT cases have continued to increase intermittently and exceeded the total number of surgical revascularization cases (5,711 cases). By region, the number of cases in the femoro-popliteal artery lesion increased from 3,689 cases in 2016 to 4,479 cases in 2017, showing the largest percentage of increase (21%); cases in the crural artery region and cases in the iliac artery region increased by 16% and 8.5%, respectively (Figs. 4A–4C).

Among 124 cases (2.2%) involving the celiac/superior mesenteric artery region, the operative/in-hospital mortality rates were 20%/25% (20%/25% in the previous year), showing that the prognosis was poor as in previous years; EVT was performed in 26% (26% in the previous year).

1) Iatrogenic vascular traumas

Among 1,778 cases of iatrogenic vascular traumas, the lower limb arteries were most commonly injured (850 cases, 47%), followed by the upper limb arteries (379 cases, 21%). The arteries in the upper and lower limbs collectively accounted for 69% of the cases, most of which are considered to consist of puncture site complications associated with intravascular catheterization for testing and therapeutic purposes (Fig. 5B).

2) Traffic accidents

Among 147 cases of vascular traumas caused by traffic accidents, abdominal aorta/iliac artery was most commonly injured (57 cases, 39%), followed by the upper limb arteries (40 cases, 27%), descending/thoracoabdominal aorta (21 cases, 14%), and visceral arteries (13 cases, 9%) (Fig. 5C). Blood vessels in the limbs are prone to damage because they are close to the body surface and susceptible to direct external forces; however, unlike vascular traumas due to other causes, vascular traumas caused by traffic accidents often involve the thoracic/abdominal aorta protected by the thorax and abdominal wall, presumably because these are high-energy traumas due to collision and other events involving rapid deceleration.

3) Labor

This category is considered to concern occupational accidents, such as falls from heights or entrapment in machine tools, and 116 cases were registered. By region, arteries in the upper and lower limbs, which are close to the body surface and susceptible to external forces, accounted for 51% (59 cases) (Fig. 5D).

Conclusion

Vascular surgery annual reports have been published every year since the NCD began registration in 2011, and the overall picture of vascular surgery in 2017 has become clear. The report reflects the current status of vascular surgery in Japan where the specifics of surgery are changing with time.

One of the major purposes of participating in the NCD is to improve the quality of medical care using the NCD data. As the data are entered between busy patient examination/treatment sessions, limiting the input items to those that are truly essential is a challenge. However, the number of input items has been increasing every year to improve the evaluation of the quality of medical care. The surgical mortality rates for vascular surgery cannot be used as evaluation outcomes because they are, fortunately, low, except for great vessel surgery. Our future goals include the implementation of a function that allows for the comparison of the risk-adjusted quality of surgical treatments for vascular diseases at the user’s institution with national standards on NCD.

The JSVS initiated a nationwide multicenter observational study on open and endovascular repair options for ruptured AAAs in 2018, and as model studies, launched a retrospective study on treatments for and prognosis of infected AAAs and common iliac artery aneurysms and a retrospective study on surgical procedures for and prognosis of popliteal artery entrapment syndrome in 2019, as efforts to overcome these challenges. In particular, the retrospective study on treatments for and prognosis of infected AAAs and common iliac artery aneurysms has been published in the British Journal of Surgery in 2021.13) These efforts of the JSVS for information dissemination to the world about nationwide studies using the NCD have been published as an “editorial” in the European Journal of Vascular and Endovascular Surgery (2021 June issue).14)

From 2020 to 2021, medical institutions had to postpone or cancel hospitalization and elective surgery for patients with vascular diseases due to the COVID-19 pandemic around the world as well as in Japan. The number of NCD-registered vascular surgery cases was 138,627 cases in 2020 after the pandemic compared to 145,639 cases in 2019 before the pandemic, showing a decrease of 7012 cases (4.8%). Considering that it has increased by 2.1%–11.0% annually from 2013 to 2017, its impact on the entire healthcare system was immeasurable. For details of surgical cases, please wait for compilation results to be published as 2019 and 2020 annual reports.

We are committed to further developing the vascular surgery database on the NCD with continuing help from fellow members. We sincerely hope that this database will help vascular surgeons to provide high-quality treatments to patients suffering from vascular diseases.

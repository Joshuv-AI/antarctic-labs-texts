Leaflet-to-Ring Edge-to-Edge Repair With Paravalvular Leak Closure for Dehisced Surgical Mitral Annuloplasty
Authors: Prasham Dave, Alice Moroni, Kitae Kim, Abdulaziz Alshaibi, Robert Kay, Tasnim Vira, Neil P. Fam
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPercutaneous mitral valve repair after surgical annuloplasty is not well described but represents an important therapeutic option for patients with prohibitive risk for redo surgery.Case SummaryAn 85-year-old man with symptomatic severe mitral regurgitation and hemolysis following prior mitral annuloplasty with a SimpliciT incomplete flexible band presented with anterior leaflet prolapse, a markedly deficient posterior leaflet, and posterior ring dehiscence. He underwent a combined percutaneous strategy of mitral transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (M-TEER) with anterior leaflet–to–annuloplasty ring clipping and paravalvular leak (PVL) closure using a vascular plug, resulting in reduction of mitral regurgitation from severe to mild-to-moderate and resolution of hemolysis.DiscussionAnnuloplasty ring dehiscence is generally considered unfavorable for transcatheter mitral valve replacement with current-generation devices, leaving limited percutaneous treatment options for patients with recurrent mitral regurgitation after surgery. This case demonstrates the feasibility of combining M-TEER with PVL closure to address complex mixed intravalvular and paravalvular regurgitation in high-risk patients.Take-Home MessagesCombined M-TEER and PVL closure is an effective solution for recurrent mitral regurgitation in selected patients with deficient leaflet anatomy and annuloplasty ring dehiscence when guided by careful imaging and multidisciplinary heart team planning.

History of Presentation

An 85-year-old man was referred for evaluation of symptomatic severe mitral regurgitation in the context of prior surgical bioprosthetic aortic valve replacement and mitral valve annuloplasty with a SimpliciT band (Medtronic). Despite optimization with guideline-directed medical therapy, the patient experienced progressive symptoms of NYHA functional class III dyspnea and developed hemolysis resulting in clinically significant anemia despite oral iron supplementation. Baseline hemoglobin was 97 g/L, mean corpuscular volume 98 fL, lactate dehydrogenase 1,809 u/L, haptoglobin <0.01 g/L, total bilirubin 60 μmol/L, direct bilirubin 14 μmol/L. Following multidisciplinary heart team discussion, he was deemed a poor candidate for redo surgical intervention given his advanced age and comorbidities. Instead, a combined percutaneous treatment strategy was planned.

Past Medical History

The patient's medical history included permanent atrial fibrillation, mild left ventricular (LV) dysfunction, normal coronary arteries, and deep vein thrombosis.

Investigations

Transesophageal echocardiography revealed a markedly short posterior mitral leaflet measuring 4 mm, a long prolapsing anterior leaflet, and severe intravalvular mitral regurgitation originating from multiple jets. Within limitations of quantification due to the multiple jets, using the largest proximal isovelocity surface area radius (A3-P3) of 1.0 cm the effective regurgitant orifice area measured 0.39 cm2, relative volume 79 mL. There was evidence of pulmonary vein flow reversal. Mean gradient was 5 mm Hg at a heart rate of 65 beats/min. There was mild LV systolic dysfunction (ejection fraction: 47%) with mild LV dilatation (LV end-diastolic volume index 105 mL/m2). The left atrium was severely dilated (179 mL/m2). Cardiac computed tomography demonstrated concordant findings and further delineated an approximately 14 × 5-mm paraband defect at the P2 scallop, consistent with annuloplasty ring dehiscence (Figure 1). Note was also made of COR-KNOT fasteners (LSI Medical) securing the ring to the annulus.

Management (Medical and Interventional)

Given the presence of ring dehiscence, the patient was determined not to be a candidate for transcatheter mitral valve replacement. Instead, a strategy of combined mitral transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (M-TEER) and paravalvular leak (PVL) closure was planned. The procedure was performed under general anesthesia with transesophageal echocardiographic guidance in the cardiac catheterization laboratory. After right femoral venous access, transseptal puncture was performed using the VersaCross system (Boston Scientific). Given the dominant pathology appeared to be intravalvular mitral regurgitation (MR), we elected to start with M-TEER with the knowledge that addressing intravalvular MR first would allow clearer delineation of the paravalvular component by reducing competing jets. The MitraClip guide catheter (Abbott) was advanced into the left atrium, and an XTW device was prepared for deployment.

Care was taken to position the clip arms between COR-KNOT fasteners, given concern that engagement of a fastener could impair device closure or secure attachment to the ring. Fluoroscopy was particularly useful for visualizing the radiopaque COR-KNOT fasteners and confirming adequate spacing of the clip arms relative to the fasteners, while simultaneous x-plane transesophageal echocardiography in a bicommissural view and 3-dimensional en-face imaging in the surgeon's view were used to guide medial clip orientation relative to the commissures and annuloplasty ring. The XTW clip was advanced and oriented medially, and TEER was performed by sequential grasping of the anterior leaflet followed by the ring at the proximal end of the clip. Adequate anterior leaflet insertion was confirmed on x-plane imaging, and fluoroscopy was used to verify ring capture by observing tension on the ring during clip closure. After deployment of the first XTW clip, MR was reduced to moderate. Following this, most residual regurgitation appeared to originate from the area of ring dehiscence.

A 16-mm Amplatzer Vascular Plug II (Abbott) was subsequently deployed within the dehisced segment, reducing the paravalvular leak from severe to mild. Following PVL closure, a persistent medial intravalvular jet—previously difficult to distinguish from the paravalvular jet—was identified, prompting placement of a second MitraClip XT (Video 1). This resulted in an overall reduction of mitral regurgitation to mild-to-moderate with a mean transmitral gradient of 5 mm Hg. A robust tissue bridge and restoration of anterograde pulmonary venous flow were observed. The patient was discharged home the following day, echocardiography confirmed mild-to-moderate MR with a mean transmitral gradient of 5 mm Hg.

Outcome and Follow-Up

At clinical follow-up, the patient reported significant symptomatic improvement to NYHA functional class I with improved energy. Transthoracic echocardiography demonstrated mild-to-moderate MR with a mean transmitral gradient of 6 mm Hg. Hemoglobin increased by 20 g/L to 122 g/L, with marked improvement in laboratory markers of hemolysis including reduction of lactate dehydrogenase to 669 μ/L and total bilirubin to 25 μmol/L.

Discussion

Recurrent severe MR following prior surgical annuloplasty presents a challenging clinical scenario, particularly in older patients with prohibitive surgical risk. Although redo surgery remains the standard therapy for failed mitral repair, advanced age, prior sternotomy, and comorbidities often preclude this option.1 Transcatheter mitral valve replacement has emerged as an alternative in selected patients; however, annular dehiscence is generally unfavorable for current-generation transcatheter mitral valve replacement devices because stable anchoring is required to avoid valve malposition, paravalvular regurgitation, migration, or embolization.2 Consequently, percutaneous treatment options for this subset of patients remain limited.

This case illustrates a novel hybrid transcatheter approach combining M-TEER with PVL closure to address both intravalvular and paravalvular mechanisms of MR in the setting of a dehisced surgical annuloplasty ring. The patient's anatomy was particularly challenging, characterized by a markedly deficient posterior leaflet, a long prolapsing anterior leaflet, and a sizable pararing defect at P2. As a result, conventional leaflet-to-leaflet clipping alone was unlikely to achieve an adequate or durable result.

Importantly, this case demonstrates that M-TEER can be successfully performed by approximating the anterior mitral leaflet and annuloplasty ring together rather than the posterior leaflet. Careful procedural planning was essential, including deliberate clip positioning between COR-KNOT fasteners, along with sequential grasping of the anterior leaflet followed by the ring at the proximal end of the clip to allow sufficient device anchoring and closure. Following treatment of intravalvular MR, closure of the dominant paravalvular leak using an AVP II resulted in further hemodynamic improvement. Transcatheter PVL closure has been associated with improvements in heart failure symptoms and hemolysis in appropriately selected patients.3,4 The patient's symptomatic improvement was likely further augmented by normalization of hemoglobin levels during follow-up. Recognition of a residual medial intravalvular jet prompted placement of a second clip, highlighting the importance of iterative reassessment and the dynamic interplay between intravalvular and paravalvular regurgitation.

Leaflet-to-ring clipping with M-TEER has been previously described in patients with recurrent intravalvular MR with intact annuloplasty rings.5, 6, 7 To our knowledge, this is the first report describing combined M-TEER with leaflet-to-ring clipping and PVL closure in the setting of annuloplasty ring dehiscence. This case expands the spectrum of anatomic scenarios amenable to percutaneous mitral repair and highlights the adaptability of contemporary M-TEER systems when applied with careful procedural planning and execution.

Conclusions

In patients with recurrent severe MR following surgical annuloplasty and concomitant ring dehiscence who are not candidates for redo surgery or transcatheter mitral valve replacement, a tailored percutaneous strategy can offer meaningful clinical benefit. This case demonstrates that combined M-TEER and PVL closure can effectively address complex, mixed-mechanism mitral regurgitation, improve symptoms, and resolve hemolysis, even in the presence of markedly deficient leaflet anatomy. Careful multimodality imaging, meticulous procedural planning, and intraprocedural reassessment are critical to achieving successful outcomes.

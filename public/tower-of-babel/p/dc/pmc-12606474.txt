Expression of concern: Determinants of usefulness in professional behaviour change interventions: observational study of a 15-year national program
Published: 2025

BMJ is concerned about the consent obtained from veterans to use their personal information in this research paper1 and broader Veterans' Medicines Advice and Therapeutics Education Services (MATES) program. BMJ was contacted by veterans who asked the journal to retract the content on this basis.

The MATES program was operated by the Department for Veterans Affairs (DVA), and the research was conducted by the University of South Australia (UniSA).2 DVA was responsible for obtaining consent from the veterans to use their personal information in the MATES program; they were also responsible for managing opt-out requests.

In 2018, a veteran lodged a complaint with the Australian Information and Privacy Commissioner about the validity of consent for DVA’s collection and use of their personal information in the MATES program. In 2023, the Privacy Commissioner determined that DVA had breached an Australian privacy principle by using and disclosing the individual’s personal information, because use for the research program was unrelated to the primary purpose of its collection.3 The DVA had implemented changes to ensure consent and opt-outs were appropriately managed.3 However, despite the DVA’s improvements, the research ethics approval for the program was withdrawn and the DVA closed down the MATES program in February 2024.4

Although this case relates to one veteran, BMJ believes it may highlight broader issues with consent in the MATES research program. The changes made by DVA in 2023 do not rectify the historic inclusion of data in MATES program research.

BMJ has asked UniSA to evaluate whether it believes that the published work complies with or falls short of acceptable ethical standards. BMJ will seek further independent advice or assessment of the broader consent issues from relevant ethical organisations in Australia.

Advanced Imaging and Biomarker Discovery for Early Diagnosis in Skull Base Pathology
Authors: Kanav Gupta, Sonal Gupta
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveEarly detection of skull base (SB) tumors and inflammatory lesions remains difficult because of deep location, complex anatomy, and subtle clinical prodromes. This study systematically evaluates contemporary advanced imaging techniques and emerging molecular biomarkers to define an evidence-based framework for earlier SB disease detection.Materials and MethodsA Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses-conformant search of PubMed, Embase, Scopus, and Cochrane Library (January 2015–June 2025) identified original human studies that reported diagnostic performance of (1) advanced magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), (2) positron-emission tomography (PET) or optical imaging, (3) radiomics/radiogenomics, or (4) fluid-based biomarkers in SB pathology. Methodological quality was rated with QUADAS-2. Data were extracted on sensitivity, specificity, and incremental diagnostic yield versus conventional computed tomography/MRI; meta-analysis was precluded by heterogeneity, so results were synthesized descriptively.ResultsSeventy-four studies (4,934 patients) met inclusion criteria. Readout-segmented echo-planar diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) increased lesion detection at the anterior cranial fossa by 17% over single-shot DWI. Diffusion tensor tractography improved cranial nerve visualization and altered operative corridors in 29% of cases. Susceptibility-, perfusion-, and spectroscopy-based MRI differentiated benign from malignant SB lesions with pooled sensitivity 85 to 92%. PET radiotracers outperformed 18F-FDG in low-contrast SB regions; 11C-methionine and 68Ga-FAPI achieved sensitivities of 88 to 93%, whereas 2–18F-tyrosine PET correctly mapped residual meningioma beyond Gd-MRI in 38% of postradiation cases. Stimulated Raman histology coupled with contrastive AI yielded 96.6% intraoperative accuracy for SB tumor classification. Radiomic signatures on multiparametric MRI distinguished chordoma from chondrosarcoma with area under the curve 0.93 and predicted postoperative progression in primary SB chordoma (C-index 0.80). Multiomics analyses identified recurrent genomic drivers (PBRM1 mutation, chromosome-1q gain) and metabolic markers (asparagine synthetase overexpression) associated with subclinical SB tumor presence. Circulating tumor DNA, H3K27M, and TBXT mutations, and exosomal miRNA panels were detectable in cerebrospinal fluid months before radiographic progression.ConclusionAdvanced MRI (particularly readout-segmented echo-planar diffusion-weighted imaging and tractography), amino acid and fibroblast-activation PET, AI-augmented optical imaging, and liquid biopsy assays collectively enhance early SB lesion detection well beyond conventional imaging. Integrating radiomics with fluid biomarkers and targeted PET could enable a risk-adapted surveillance algorithm and expedite minimally invasive intervention. Prospective multimodal studies with standardized reference standards are now warranted.

Introduction

Skull base (SB) pathology accounts for <4% of intracranial tumors yet produces disproportionate morbidity because lesions often remain occult until cranial nerve or brainstem compromise occurs. 1 Conventional CT and contrast magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) delineate anatomy but have limited sensitivity for small, hypercellular, or infiltrative processes hidden by bone interface artifacts. 1 Meanwhile, molecular discoveries in chordoma, chondrosarcoma, meningioma, and invasive infections herald opportunities for biomarker-mediated early diagnosis. 2 3 A comprehensive appraisal of cutting-edge imaging and biomarker technologies is essential to guide clinical translation.

This study systematically reviews the diagnostic utility of (1) advanced MRI, (2) novel positron-emission tomography (PET)/optical imaging, (3) radiomics-based pattern recognition, and (4) fluid-based molecular biomarkers for early SB disease detection, synthesizing current evidence to inform future prospective trials and multidisciplinary algorithms.

Materials and Methods

Search Strategy and Selection

MEDLINE, Embase, Scopus, and Cochrane databases were searched from January 1, 2015 to June 1, 2025 using MeSH and free-text terms for “skull base,” “imaging,” “diffusion,” “radiomics,” “biomarker,” “liquid biopsy,” and specific tracers. English-language, peer-reviewed human studies reporting diagnostic accuracy, incremental yield, or prognostic value in asymptomatic or minimally symptomatic SB pathology were eligible. Case series < 10 patients, animal studies, and purely technical notes were excluded. Two reviewers independently screened titles/abstracts and full texts; disagreements were resolved by consensus.

Data Extraction and Quality Assessment

Extracted variables included cohort size, pathology spectrum, imaging/biomarker modality, reference standard, sensitivity, specificity, area under curve (AUC), and clinical impact. Study quality was rated with QUADAS-2. Heterogeneity in design, reference standards, and reporting precluded quantitative pooling; results were summarized narratively and tabulated.

Outcome Measures

Primary outcome was incremental diagnostic sensitivity for early SB lesion detection versus standard CT/MRI. Secondary outcomes included specificity, change in surgical planning, prognostic association, and feasibility indicators (scan time, invasiveness, cost).

Results

Study Characteristics

Seventy-four studies involving 4,934 participants (median sample = 46) met criteria. Thirty-one involved advanced MRI, 18 PET/optical imaging, 12 radiomics/radiogenomics, and 13 fluid biomarkers. Median QUADAS-2 overall risk of bias was low in 48% and moderate in 35%.

Advanced Magnetic Resonance Imaging Modalities

Readout-segmented echo-planar diffusion-weighted imaging (rs-EPI DWI) overcame susceptibility artifacts, detecting 17% additional anterior fossa lesions compared with single-shot DWI. 4 Apparent diffusion coefficient thresholds (>1.26 × 10 3 mm 2 /s) discriminated osteomyelitis from lymphoma or nasopharyngeal carcinoma with 88% accuracy. 5 High-resolution susceptibility-weighted and perfusion sequences improved vascular-invasion visibility in fungal SB infections and petroclival meningiomas. 1 Diffusion tensor imaging tractography delineated cranial nerves V to VIII and corticospinal tracts; neuronavigation incorporating tractography altered planned surgical corridor in 29% of cases and correlated lesion–tract distance < 5 mm with postoperative deficit risk. 6 7

Positron Emission Tomography and Optical Imaging

Standard 18F-FDG PET showed low SB contrast owing to adjacent gray matter uptake. 11C-methionine PET achieved 93% detection of SB tumors, outperforming FDG (T/N ratio 2.96 vs. 1.02). 8 68Ga-FAPI PET visualized desmoplastic chordoma interfaces with 88% sensitivity, 8 whereas 2–18F-tyrosine PET identified residual or recurrent skull base meningioma beyond Gd-MRI margins in 38% of irradiated cavities. Targeted PET/CT improved positive predictive value from 60% (standardized uptake value > 2.5 alone) to 80% when interpreted by SB-experienced radiologists. 9 Intraoperative stimulated Raman histology combined with supervised contrastive learning classified SB tumor specimens with 96.6% accuracy in <2 minutes, 10 enabling margin assessment.

Radiomics and Radiogenomics

Multiparametric MRI radiomics differentiated chordoma from chondrosarcoma with logistic regression AUC 0.93, exceeding board-certified neurosurgeon performance (median accuracy 60%). 11 Texture shape signatures predicted primary SB chordoma progression (C-index 0.80) and high K i -67 expression. 12 Radiomic models integrating T1-contrast, T2, and diffusion features predicted subtotal resection risk in ventral meningioma with 0.87 accuracy. 12 Machine learning segmentation reduced manual contouring time by 40%.

Molecular and Liquid Biopsy Biomarkers

Proteogenomic profiling of 187 SB chordomas revealed chromosome instability signatures; PBRM1 mutation and SMARCB1 loss correlated with early recurrence risk. 2 13 High asparagine-synthetase expression predicted shorter recurrence-free survival in 187 chordoma specimens. 3 Cerebrospinal fluid liquid biopsy detected H3K27M-mutant diffuse midline glioma and TBXT-positive chordoma months before imaging progression. 14 Focused-ultrasound disruption of the blood–brain barrier enhanced ctDNA yield 4-fold in murine glioma models. 15 Cell-free methylome assays classified SB lesions with 91% accuracy and provided subtype match in 86%. Plasma exosomal miRNA panels (miR-155, miR-574–3p) demonstrated AUC 0.88 for chordoma detection. 3

Discussion

Principal Findings

Advanced diffusion, susceptibility, perfusion, and spectroscopy sequences overcome long-standing bone–interface artifacts, revealing early SB lesions invisible to conventional MRI. Targeted amino acid and fibroblast-activation PET tracers capitalize on high metabolic or stromal activity of SB tumors, offering superior lesion-to-background contrast. 8 9 AI-driven optical imaging and radiomics furnish rapid intraoperative and preoperative tissue characterization, whereas liquid biopsies promise noninvasive molecular detection.

Clinical Implications

A tiered algorithm is proposed: (1) baseline contrast MRI; (2) if nondiagnostic yet clinical suspicion persists, rs-EPI DWI and quantitative ADC mapping; (3) molecular imaging (e.g., 11C-MET or 68Ga-FAPI PET/CT) for equivocal cases or suspected recurrence; (4) cerebrospinal fluid liquid biopsy for ambiguous infiltrative lesions or when imaging is contraindicated. Radiomic-enhanced reporting and tractography should be integrated into neuronavigation to optimize surgical corridors and minimize morbidity.

Research Gaps

Heterogeneity in reference standards, variable tracer availability, and small single-center cohorts limit generalizability. Prospective, multicenter studies using standardized imaging protocols, uniform histopathological confirmation, and concurrent liquid biopsy sampling are needed. Cost-effectiveness analyses will determine sustainability in routine surveillance.

Limitations

This review is qualitative owing to heterogeneity; publication bias toward positive imaging studies is possible. Emerging modalities such as hyperpolarized 13C MRI and targeted photoacoustic imaging were underrepresented but warrant future evaluation.

Conclusion

Multimodal innovations—rs-EPI DWI, metabolic/targeted PET, AI-guided optical microscopy, radiomics, and liquid biopsy—synergistically advance the frontier of early SB disease detection. Translating these tools into standardized, risk-stratified pathways could shift SB care toward presymptomatic intervention and improved neurofunctional outcomes.

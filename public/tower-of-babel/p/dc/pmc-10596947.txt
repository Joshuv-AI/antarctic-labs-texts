Barriers and enablers to vaccine uptake in the Ultra-Orthodox Jewish population: a systematic review
Authors: A Jacobson, S Spitzer, Y Gorelik, M Edelstein
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe Jewish Ultra-Orthodox (UO) population is an undervaccinated minority group that has been disproportionally affected by outbreaks of vaccine-preventable diseases (VPD) such as measles and polio. Underlying reasons remain poorly characterized. We aimed to identify vaccination barriers and enablers in this population.MethodsWe systematically reviewed the literature, searching Pub-med, Web of science, Medline, PsychNet and Scopus from 1995 to 2021 for quantitative and qualitative primary research in English. We assessed included publications for quality and extracted relevant data based on the 5As taxonomy: access, awareness, affordability, acceptance and activation.ResultsWe included 9 qualitative and 7 quantitative studies from the 125 studies identified. Access barriers included scheduling difficulties, inconvenient opening hours, and logistical difficulties related to having multiple young children. Acceptance barriers included safety concerns. Insufficient knowledge about the importance of vaccines and timely vaccination and the perception of being shielded from infections because of seclusion from wider society were key awareness barriers. Competing priorities, such as work and housework, were the main affordability barriers. Mainstream religious leadership's support for vaccination was an enabler, although recent studies suggest their influence on vaccination behavior is decreasing and the influence of anti-vaccination messages is growing.ConclusionsBarriers to vaccination among the UO were mainly logistical, with little religious framing. Safety and efficacy concerns were similar to those reported in the wider community. Decreasing influence of the traditionally pro-vaccine mainstream religious leadership and growing influence of anti-vaccination movements targeting the UO community are new phenomena that require close monitoring. Tailored interventions are required to protect the community and wider society against future VPD outbreaks.Key messages• Vaccination barriers in Ultra-Orthodox Jewish communities are due mainly to logistical challenges, such as competing priorities in large families with young children.• Religious leadership is the main enabler of vaccination in Ultra-Orthodox Jewish communities, but their declining authority and exposure to antivaxx materials is causing a shift in attitudes.

Reallocating time spent in sedentary behavior, physical activity and its association with CHD: Mediating role of creatinine, uric acid and IgG
Authors: Qinfei Wei, Zhiyu Zhang, Li Zhu, Dexiong Xie, Xingyan Xu, XiaoXu Xie, Huangyuan Li, Le Yang, Shaowei Lin, Siying Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe effect of behavioral shifts in physical activity (PA) and sedentary behavior (SB) on coronary heart disease (CHD), along with the potential mechanisms involved, remains to be fully elucidated. Therefore, this study included 1,699 participants (638 with CHD) and used isotemporal substitution modeling to quantify the CHD risk associated with reallocating time between PA intensities and SB. Mediation analysis assessed creatinine, uric acid (UA), urea nitrogen, and systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), while ELISA examined IgG glycoprotein and its fucosylation. Replacing light, moderate, or vigorous PA with 1 h/day of SB increased CHD risk (ORs: 1.177–1.352). Creatinine and UA mediated 3.66%–6.89% of these associations; SII mediated 17.37%–20.21%. Notably, IgG glycoprotein mediated 24.63%, with CHD patients and those with low PA/high SB exhibiting lower IgG fucosylation modifications. These findings suggest that reduced PA and prolonged SB are associated with CHD risk through pathways involving creatinine/UA dysregulation, systemic immune-inflammation, and altered IgG fucosylation.Subject areas: Health sciences, Medicine, Medical specialty, Cardiovascular medicine

Introduction

According to the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) study, nearly 200 million individuals worldwide were affected by coronary heart disease (CHD) in 2020.1 In 2020, the World Health Organization (WHO) defined physical activity (PA) as an energy-consuming exercise produced by skeletal muscles. Sedentary behavior (SB) refers to low-energy-consuming activities performed while awake.2 The combination of persistent physical inactivity and SB is associated with an increased risk of all-cause mortality. The strong association between PA, SB, and cardiovascular disease (CVD) is currently of great interest. Among CHD patients, those who engage in regular exercise have a lower mortality rate compared to those who remain sedentary for extended periods.3 Compared to light physical activity (LPA), moderate to vigorous physical activity (MVPA) significantly reduces the lifetime risk of CVD.4 SB has consistently been associated with an increased risk of CVD.5

Previous studies did not account for the fact that a decrease in the duration of one behavior would result in an increase in the duration of another behavior, given a fixed amount of daytime.6 The isotemporal substitution model (ISM) refers to the use of one type of activity time variable to replace another type of activity time variable, in order to observe the magnitude of the impact on health outcome variables at the same time.7 The advantage of ISM is its ability to expand research directions and eliminate heterogeneity in participation or substitution, addressing the limitations of current epidemiological studies that do not account for 24 h time slots.8 However, limited research exists on the effects of reallocating PA and SB time on CHD.

As cardiovascular health risk factors, elevated levels of creatinine, uric acid (UA), and urea nitrogen have become risk factors for CHD. The slight changes in serum creatinine are associated with an increased risk of CHD, and the incidence rate of CVD increases with the increase in UA level.9 Urea nitrogen is linked to both CVD and all-cause mortality.10 Research indicates that PA and SB can influence serum creatinine and urea nitrogen levels. SB is more likely to lead to hyperuricemia. However, there is a lack of research examining the mediating roles of these factors in the relationship between PA, SB, and CHD.

Glycosylation of proteins plays a crucial role in various physiological11 and pathological processes. Its effects at different levels can stimulate cardiac and vascular wall cells,11 which are closely related to various CVDs.12 Research has found significant correlations between acute myocardial infarction13 and MVPA14 with changes in plasma N-glycan profiles.

Immunoglobulin G (IgG) is a crucial effector glycoprotein produced during the immune response to foreign antigens. Previous studies have shown that changes in IgG levels are associated with the occurrence and progression of various CVDs, and minor alterations in IgG glycosylation levels affect their pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory states.15 IgG has been found to participate in thrombosis by activating platelet factors.16 Additionally, lack of PA and increased SB are associated with cardiovascular hazards, oxidative stress, and inflammatory responses. PA can reduce inflammation and enhance fibrinolysis and endothelial function.17 Elevated levels of UA can promote inflammation and oxidative stress in the body, thereby contributing to the development of atherosclerosis.18 Meanwhile, an elevated creatinine level, indicative of renal impairment, is mechanistically connected to a state of chronic low-grade inflammation and immune dysregulation.19 Therefore, the present study aimed to examine this hypothesis: the associations between PA, SB, and CHD are mediated, at least in part, through pathways involving cardiovascular health risk factors, immune-inflammation, and IgG glycoprotein expression and its N-glycosylation profile.

Based on the aforementioned uncertainties and assumptions, we investigated the impact of behavioral shifts in PA and SB on CHD, as well as the mediating roles of creatinine, UA, urea nitrogen, immune-inflammation, and IgG glycoprotein in the association between PA and SB with CHD, providing a theoretical basis for the prevention and control measures of CHD.

Results

Demographic characteristics

A total of 1,699 subjects participated in this investigation, including 638 CHD cases (37.6%) and 1,061 controls (62.4%). Significant between-group differences emerged in sex, age, smoking history, drinking history, family history of CHD, hypertension, diabetes, stroke, history of hypertension and diabetes, creatinine, UA, urea nitrogen, leukocytes, neutrophils, lymphocytes, and systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) (all p < 0.05; Table S1). CHD patients exhibited lower PA levels across all intensities compared to controls (p < 0.05), whereas SB duration was significantly longer in the case group.

Association between PA, SB, and CHD

Correlation analysis showed that the Spearman’s correlation coefficients between LPA, moderate physical activity (MPA), vigorous physical activity (VPA), SB, and total physical activity (TPA) were all <0.5, suggesting that there was no significant linear correlation between the behavioral activities (Table S2). Under the single-factor model (SM), SB led to an increase in CHD (adjusted odds ratio [OR] = 1.215, 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.148–1.286) whereas LPA, MPA, and VPA reduced CHD levels (adjusted OR = 0.943, 95% CI: 0.907–0.981; adjusted OR = 0.874, 95% CI: 0.815–0.936; adjusted OR = 0.837, and 95% CI: 0.762–0.920), corrected for general demographic characteristics. In the partition model (PM), each 1 h increase in LPA, MPA, and VPA was associated with a 13.2%, 20.8%, and 24.5% reduction in the risk of developing CHD, but no statistical significance was observed for SB (Table 1).

Subgroup analysis

Gender-stratified analyses revealed distinct risk profiles. In the ISM, replacing the same amount of time of LPA, MPA, and VPA with 1 h/day of SB was associated with a 30.2%, 25.6%, and 49.8% higher risk of CHD in the female subgroup, and for the male subgroup replacing the same amount of time of LPA, MPA, and VPA with 1 h/day of SB was associated with an elevated risk of CHD in 11.9%, 34.9%, and 31.1% of men (Figure S3).

Study participants were divided into two subgroups according to age. In ISM, substituting 1 h/day of SB for the same amount of time of MPA, VPA was associated with a 35.4% and 30.3% higher risk of CHD for the subgroup aged <60 years. In the subgroup aged ≥60 years, replacing the same amount of time of LPA, MPA, and VPA with 1 h/day of SB was associated with a 23.8%, 28.8%, and 36.9% elevated risk of CHD (Figure S4).

Mediating roles of cardiovascular health risk factors and systemic inflammation

Mediation analyses were conducted to assess the potential mediating effects of cardiovascular biomarkers and systemic inflammation in the PA/SB-CHD relationship (Table S3). Creatinine was found to mediate 3.66% of the MPA-CHD association, 3.74% of the VPA-CHD association, and 3.87% of the SB-CHD association (all p < 0.05; Figures 2A–2D). UA mediated 5.45%, 6.89%, and 4.59% of the associations for MPA, VPA, and SB with CHD, respectively (all p < 0.05; Figures 2E–2H). An increase in CHD predictive value was observed with increasing creatinine and UA levels and decreasing LPA, MPA, and VPA (Figures S5A–S5C and S5E–S5G). The predictive value of CHD increased when SB duration, creatinine, and UA increased (Figures S5D and S5H). In contrast, urea nitrogen did not demonstrate a significant mediating effect in any of these associations (Figure S6).

Given the proposed involvement of inflammation in the PA/SB-CHD pathway, we further evaluated the mediating effect of SII. Mediation analysis indicated that SII accounted for 17.37% of the association between MPA and CHD and 20.21% of the association between SB and CHD (all p < 0.05; Figures 2I–2L).

Mediating role of IgG glycoprotein

A total of 108 CHD cases and 108 controls were selected from the above population after being matched for sex and age, and the characteristics of these subjects are shown in Table S4. We found that IgG differed between the case and control groups and showed an upregulation trend (Figure S7). Then we explored the association between the relative expression levels of IgG glycoproteins, PA, SB, and CHD. The results showed that IgG glycoprotein played a 24.63% mediating effect in the association between SB and CHD (Figure 3A). An increase in CHD predictive value was observed with increasing IgG glycoproteins and SB duration (Figure 3B). In addition, the results suggested that there was an association between the relative expression level of IgG glycoproteins and SB, UA, creatinine, and SII (p < 0.05), and no association between the relative expression level of IgG glycoproteins and urea nitrogen was found (p > 0.05; Figure S8).

Discussion

Summary of findings

In this study, we found that the risk of CHD decreased with increasing levels of PA, while the risk of CHD gradually increased with increasing levels of SB. In addition, replacing the same amount of time of LPA, MPA, and VPA with 1 h/day of SB was associated with a 17.7%, 29.0%, and 35.2% higher risk of CHD. Furthermore, mediation analyses suggested that the associations between physical activity/SB and CHD might involve several pathways, as indicated by the mediation effects of creatinine, UA, SII, and IgG glycoprotein. A finding was the observed reduction in IgG fucosylation among CHD patients and individuals with low physical activity or high SB. Collectively, these findings propose a potential molecular mechanism, wherein reduced physical activity and prolonged SB are associated with an increased risk of CHD, possibly through pathways involving dysregulation of creatinine and UA, systemic immune-inflammation, and altered IgG glycoprotein expression and fucosylation.

Association between PA, SB, and CHD

Our study demonstrated a significant association between PA, SB, and the risk of CHD, consistent with growing research on the relationship between different intensities of PA and SB with CVD. For instance, a UK cohort study reported an inverse correlation between PA and cardiovascular events,20 while prolonged sitting has been established as an independent risk factor for adverse cardiovascular outcomes.21 Further supporting this, studies using isotemporal substitution modeling indicate that replacing sedentary time with MVPA or even LPA improves cardiometabolic health22 and reduces CVD mortality.23

Notably, the intensity-dependent gradient of protection—with VPA yielding the strongest risk reduction, followed by MPA and LPA—suggests that exercise intensity is a critical modulator of cardioprotective efficacy. This may be explained by the greater hemodynamic and metabolic stimuli elicited by higher-intensity activities, which more effectively trigger anti-inflammatory and antioxidant pathways.24 MVPA induces favorable vascular adaptations through enhanced endothelial shear stress and improved nitric oxide bioavailability, which collectively improve arterial function and attenuate atherogenesis.25

The above evidence emphasized the benefits of replacing sedentary time with any intensity of PA. Consequently, a practical approach for habitually sedentary individuals involves initially increasing LPA, progressively advancing toward achieving recommended levels of MPA or VPA. This stepwise strategy aligns with current physical activity guidelines from the WHO26,27 and the American Heart Association.28

Subgroup analysis

The stratified analysis indicates that PA and SB significantly influence the incidence of CHD based on age and gender. Specifically, reallocating SB time to any intensity of PA yields stronger protective effects in females and individuals over 60 years old.

Previous studies have reported that SB has a more significant adverse effect on cardiovascular health in women.29 After replacing SB with MVPA, women experienced a more substantial reduction in adverse cardiac metabolic health risks compared to men.30 The observed gender disparity, with women potentially deriving greater benefit from this behavioral shift, may be linked to inherent physiological differences. A central explanatory factor is the role of estrogen, which exerts several cardioprotective effects in women. These include vasodilation through the promotion of nitric oxide release, favorable modulation of lipid profiles, and anti-inflammatory actions.31 The interaction between PA and these estrogen-mediated pathways may underpin the enhanced cardiovascular benefit observed in women.

Regarding age, the more pronounced association in older adults may be attributed to the progressive physiological decline characteristic of aging. This demographic often exhibits a state of chronic, low-grade inflammation and increased insulin resistance,32 both of which are key drivers of atherosclerosis and are potently ameliorated by regular PA.33 Furthermore, older adults experience a decline in physiological reserve, including endothelial function and metabolic flexibility.34 Consequently, they may exhibit heightened vulnerability to the detrimental effects of prolonged SB. Therefore, promoting increased PA and reduced SB in older adults may help prevent CVD risk and attenuate age-related physiological decline.

The mediating effects of creatinine, UA, urea nitrogen, and systemic inflammation

This study demonstrated that creatinine and UA partially mediated the associations among MPA, VPA, SB, and CHD. While these effect sizes are relatively small, they nonetheless highlight plausible biological pathways through which activity behaviors may influence cardiovascular risk. Recent studies have proposed a causal relationship between UA levels and CHD.35 Mechanistically, elevated UA, through impaired xanthine oxidoreductase function, can promote cellular damage, hypertension, and metabolic dysregulation, thereby contributing to atherosclerosis.36 Similarly, physical inactivity can affect serum creatinine levels, and substituting SB with MPA/VPA may benefit renal health.37 Even subtle alterations in renal function, as reflected by serum creatinine, are associated with increased CHD risk.38

Crucially, the pathways represented by creatinine and UA converge on common pathways of systemic immune-inflammation, a key driver of CHD. Physical inactivity and prolonged SB are well-established promoters of a pro-inflammatory state.39,40 Our findings further underscore the centrality of immune-inflammation, as indicated by the mediating role of SII. The mediation proportion observed by SII suggests that immune-inflammatory pathways may represent a substantial mechanism in the PA/SB-CHD relationship. In this context, the effect of creatinine and UA may indicate their participation in upstream or parallel processes that converge on immune-inflammation. For instance, elevated UA can stimulate inflammatory responses, while renal dysfunction may lead to immune dysregulation. Thus, creatinine and UA might exert their effects partially by amplifying the systemic inflammatory milieu, which in turn accelerates atherogenesis. Consequently, individuals with dysregulated creatinine or UA metabolism may be particularly vulnerable to the detrimental cardiovascular effects of insufficient PA and sedentary lifestyles.

In contrast, urea nitrogen did not demonstrate a significant mediating effect in our analyses, suggesting a more peripheral role in the PA/SB-CHD relationship compared to the interlinked pathways of purine metabolism, renal function, and inflammation.

IgG glycoproteins and IgG fucosylation in PA and SB affecting the pathogenesis of CHD

Limited research has examined the role of protein molecules and their glycosylation modifications in the relationship between PA, SB, and CHD. We observed that IgG glycoproteins mediate the relationship between SB and CHD, and specifically identified a reduction in IgG core fucosylation in CHD patients, as well as in individuals with low PA and high SB.

Functionally, IgG acts not only as a key effector in humoral immunity but also participates in inflammatory regulation and thrombotic processes.16 Previous studies have established that elevated immunoglobulin levels are associated with atherothrombosis in dyslipidemic contexts.41 Notably, a lifestyle characterized by extensive SB often promotes adipose accumulation and infiltration of pro-inflammatory immune cells, fostering a chronic low-grade inflammatory state. Such inflammation, whether systemic or localized to the vascular wall, facilitates coronary thrombosis.42 In contrast, the anti-inflammatory effect of PA43 can reduce the levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines, thereby resisting the systemic inflammatory state of CVD.

Our correlation analyses further indicated positive associations of the SII with SB, creatinine levels, and IgG expression, suggesting that prolonged SB may promote a pro-inflammatory milieu concurrent with dysregulation of creatinine and UA.

Notably, the inflammatory activity of IgG is closely modulated by its N-glycosylation profile, particularly core fucosylation.44 Structurally, the core fucose moiety in the IgG-Fc region modulates antibody affinity for Fc gamma receptors (FcγRs). Its deficiency markedly enhances IgG binding to activating FcγRIIIa (CD16a) on effector cells—such as natural killer cells and macrophages—triggering a pro-inflammatory cascade through amplified antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity and cytokine release.45 Within atherosclerotic lesions, such hyper-engagement of afucosylated IgG with macrophages can sustain pro-inflammatory signaling, aggravate foam cell formation, and increase plaque vulnerability.46 This view is supported by clinical studies showing an inverse correlation between IgG glycosylation patterns and cardiovascular risk or carotid plaque presence,47 and observations of altered sialylation in core-fucosylated IgG among women with coronary artery disease,48 consistent with our findings. It may be due to the association between core fucosylation and higher inflammatory potential, which in turn promotes the anti-inflammatory function of IgG.49

Research has found that PA can induce an increase in N-glycosylation of core fucoidan.50 The inflammatory state reflected by the IgG N-glycosylation pattern is related to hyperuricemia. This collective evidence suggests that IgG glycosylation may serve as a link, potentially affecting changes in UA and creatinine levels by modulating the immune-inflammatory response, thereby supporting the integrative findings of this study.

In summary, our findings indicate that physical activity and SB are associated with CHD risk. This association may be mediated through influencing IgG glycoprotein expression levels and fucosylation modifications of fucose residues, as well as through the regulatory functions of creatinine and UA. The case-control design prevents causal inference; thus, these results propose putative mechanisms linking behavior to CHD via immune-inflammatory regulation. Future prospective studies are essential to validate these observed associations and explore the underlying mechanisms.

Limitations of the study

This study explored the mediating roles of cardiovascular biomarkers, inflammation, and IgG glycoprotein in the PA/SB-CHD relationship. Unlike previous research focusing on independent associations, we employed ISM to directly illustrate the impact of reallocating time between PA intensities and SB on CHD risk.

However, limitations should be acknowledged. (1) The case-control design limits causal inference regarding the observed associations and mediation effects. The temporal sequence of exposure, mediator, and outcome cannot be firmly established. Thus, the findings primarily indicate associations and generate hypotheses for future prospective studies. (2) While ISM offers advantages over traditional time models, its assumptions remain theoretical. The model posits that time reallocation effects are unaffected by activity duration, and subsequent validation through randomized controlled trials is still required. (3) This study did not collect detailed data on potential confounders such as diet, socioeconomic status, and antihypertensive medication use, all of which can impact systemic inflammation and metabolic biomarkers, representing a limitation in the study. (4) The sample size for the IgG and glycosylation analyses is a limitation. Although post hoc power analysis indicated our sample had adequate power to detect the observed group differences, the findings remain to be validated in a larger cohort. Consequently, the IgG-related results are exploratory and highlight the need for confirmatory studies to establish their robustness and generalizability.

Nevertheless, our findings provide evidence that these biomarkers and IgG glycosylation may mediate the PA/SB-CHD link, which is to be further explored by animal experiments or molecular experiments, and will be an important goal of our next study.

Experimental model and study participant details

Research design and participants

In our research, CHD cases and controls were collected from the Affiliated Union Hospital and the First Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University from January 2021 to September 2023 in strict accordance with the inclusion and exclusion criteria. A total of 638 cases and 1061 controls were recruited for questionnaire surveys and physical examinations. The detailed selection of participants is shown in Figure S1. The study population comprised Han Chinese participants from China. The cohort included 972 males and 727 females with a mean age of 60.87±13.62. Detailed demographic and clinical characteristics are presented in Table S1. Subgroup analyses were stratified by sex and age.

Each participant and their family member has signed an informed consent form. This study was approved by the Biomedical Research Ethics Committee of Fujian Medical University (Approval No: [2018] No. 11), and all experiments were conducted in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration. Clinical trial number: not applicable.

Method details

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

CHD was defined using both ICD-9 codes 410-414 and ICD-10 codes I20-I25. The diagnosis was confirmed by a combination of coronary angiography findings and clinical symptoms, as assessed by at least two independent experienced cardiologists. Specifically, CHD was diagnosed using coronary angiography when at least one of the three major coronary arteries or their branches had a stenosis of 50 percent or greater. Case participants were recruited after receiving a definitive diagnosis and prior to initiating long-term medication therapy. Control participants were recruited during the same period and confirmed to have no history of cardiovascular disease through medical record review and self-report. Individuals with severe systemic diseases, autoimmune disorders, or active infections were excluded from both groups.

Sample size consideration

The sample size adequacy for the regression models was evaluated using the events per variable (EPV) criterion.51 This principle stipulates that the number of events-defined as the smaller of the two outcome categories-should be at least 10 times the number of independent parameters in the model. The study included 1,699 subjects (638 cases and 1,061 controls), and the primary analytical model incorporated 16 variables, thus satisfying the EPV requirement.

Exposure variables

The setting and categorization of PA questions in the baseline questionnaire were based on the Global Physical Activity Questionnaire (GPAQ)52 and existing literature. According to the calculated metabolic equivalent and WHO guidelines on PA status,26 PA intensity is categorized as vigorous physical activity (VPA), moderate physical activity (MPA), and light physical activity (LPA). The number of days a participant engages in each level of PA per week is multiplied by the number of minutes spent on that activity per day. This provides the total number of minutes per week for each activity category, which is then used to calculate the average number of hours spent per day. Respondents must either leave blank spaces or provide valid answers to a subset of each sports activity question, ensuring at least one valid answer is provided. Any missing data on activity frequency or duration will be excluded from the analysis. According to literature standards and reference points in the exposure-response relationship, SB was categorized into two groups: high SB (more than 6 hours/day) and low SB (less than 6 hours/day). In this study, total physical activity (TPA) will be calculated as the sum of time spent on various types of PA and SB.

Cardiovascular health risk factors

Levels of cardiovascular health risk factors, including creatinine, UA, and blood urea nitrogen, were measured using fully automated biochemical analyzers. To investigate the role of inflammation, complete blood count parameters, including leukocytes, neutrophils, lymphocytes, and platelet (PLT) counts, were measured from peripheral blood samples using an automated hematology analyzer. The systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) was calculated using the formula: SII = (platelet count × neutrophil count) / lymphocyte count.

Covariates

The covariates of this study were surveyed using a structured questionnaire, which included general demographic information (name, gender, age, marital status, and family history, etc.), behavioral risk factors (smoking and drinking history), height, weight, blood pressure, and blood glucose levels.

Current smokers are defined as those who smoke ≥ 1 cigarette per day and continuously for ≥ 6 months; ex-smokers are defined as those who have quit smoking for more than one year. In this study, the research subjects were divided into non-smokers, current smokers, and those who have quit smoking. Alcohol consumption refers to a single alcohol consumption exceeding 50 grams, and abstinence is defined as abstinence from alcohol for more than one year. In this study, the study subjects were divided into never drinkers, current drinkers, and quit drinkers. Measure the height and weight of participants upon admission, and then calculate the body mass index (BMI). Participants were divided into obese and non-obese groups based on a BMI of ≥ 28 kg/m2.

Blood collection and preservation

With informed consent, participants fasted for 8-12 hours overnight and had 2 ml of peripheral venous blood drawn the next morning using a medical EDTA anticoagulant tube on an empty stomach. After centrifugation, the plasma layer was separated, labeled, and stored at -80°C for future use.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

We randomly selected 108 new CHD patients and 108 controls from the 1699 participants, matched for sex and age. Anterior venous blood (2 mL) was collected from their elbows the morning after admission. Samples were collected and processed according to the recommended protocol of the HUPO Plasma Proteome Project.53 After initial processing, the plasma was stored at -80°C for further experiments. IgG expression levels were determined using commercial ELISA kits (Enzyme-linked Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). The steps of mixing with the antibody, incubating at a constant temperature, washing the plate, reacting with the substrate, and terminating the reaction were performed sequentially. Standard, blank, and sample wells were set up as required. The absorbance of each well was measured at 450 nm within 15 minutes after terminating the reaction to calculate the sample concentration.

Lectin enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (lectin ELISA)

For the quantitative determination of focused haptoglobin, a lectin ELISA was used.54 Fab fragments of anti-human haptoglobin IgG were coated on the bottom of a 96-well ELISA plate due to the presence of focused oligosaccharides in the Fc portion of IgG. The coated plates were blocked with Carbo-Free™ solution for 1 hour and then washed with phosphate buffered saline (PBS). Standards and samples were added to the wells and incubated at 37°C for 1 hour. To detect focused haptoglobin, a 1/1000 dilution of biotinylated aleuria auantia lectin (AAL) was added to each well and incubated for 1 hour at room temperature. After washing the plates three times, the enzyme-bound compound was added to each well, followed by incubation at room temperature for 1 hour. After four washes, tetramethylbenzidine (TMB) was added to each well and incubated for 15 minutes. To stop the reaction, 50 μL of stop solution was added to each well.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Statistical analysis

The single-factor model (SM) did not consider other activity types and only evaluated the relationship between each physical activity type and CHD separately. The partition model (PM) splits the total physical behavior time and incorporates each part of the physical behavior duration as a dependent variable at the same time in the multiple regression model. Isotemporal substitution model (ISM) was used to estimate the effects of replacing sedentary time with different intensities of PA (light, moderate, and vigorous) on the risk of CHD. Compared to conventional regression modeling, ISM can provide a more accurate estimation of the potential effects of different activities.7 ISM analyses make the more realistic assumption that an increase in one behavior will be accompanied by a decrease of equivalent duration (isotemporal) in another behavior, while total time in all behaviors is kept constant. All regression models were adjusted for potential confounders, including age, sex, marital status, smoking history, drinking history, obesity, history of hypertension and diabetes, family history of hypertension, diabetes, CHD, and stroke. To investigate the exposure-response relationship between PA, SB, and CHD, restricted cubic spline (RCS) models were developed. Subgroup analyses included subgroups categorized by age and sex.

The relative expression levels and fucosylation modification levels of IgG between the case and control groups were compared using the Mann-Whitney U test. The R package “Plot 3D” was used to display the relationship between cardiovascular biomarkers, IgG, PA, SB, and CHD through 3D surface fitting. Subsequently, a mediating effect analysis was performed using the bootstrap method (1000 iterations for this study) proposed by Preacher & Hayes55 through the R package “mediation” to explore whether cardiovascular biomarkers, SII, and IgG could mediate the influence of PA and SB on CHD.56 In mediation analyses, p-values were adjusted using the FDR multiple correction method to control for overall error rates. Significance was assessed at the 5% level. All analyses were pre-specified and performed using SPSS 26 and R 4.2.2.

Effects of medical and nursing group rounds combined with emotional nursing on quality of life and emotion in patients with malignant lymphoma
Authors: Dan Zhang, Weijuan Wang, Feng Wei, Yin Lu, Ying Ji
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
To investigate the effects of medical and nursing visits in the same group combined with emotional care on the quality of life and emotions of patients with malignant lymphoma. One hundred sixty cases of malignant lymphoma patients admitted to our hospital from March 2020 to July 2022 were selected as retrospective study subjects and divided into 80 cases each in the control group and the observation group according to the different nursing methods. Among them, the control group implemented conventional emotional care, and the observation group implemented medical and nursing visits in the same group on the basis of the control group. The differences in quality of life, anxiety-related scores and sleep quality between the 2 groups of patients with malignant lymphoma before and after nursing were compared. After nursing, the mental vitality (P = .015), social interaction (P < .001), emotional restriction (P = .007), and mental status (P = .028) scores of patients in the observation group were higher than those in the control group. After nursing, the Self-Rating Anxiety Scale (P < .001) and Hamilton Anxiety Scale (P < .001) of malignant lymphoma patients in the observation group were significantly lower than those in the control group, and the sleep quality (P < .001), increased awakening (P < .001), sleep disturbance (P < .001), night terrors (P < .001) scores in the observation group were significantly lower than those in the control group. The combination of medical and nursing group checkups and emotional care can effectively improve the quality of life and sleep of patients with malignant lymphoma and reduce adverse emotions, which is beneficial to patient prognosis and clinical treatment. It has certain reference value for the care of patients with malignant lymphoma.

1. Introduction

Malignant lymphoma (ML) is a malignant tumor that poses a serious threat to human health and is one of the most rapidly increasing diseases in recent years.[1] The domestic survey on lymphoma showed that the incidence and death rates of lymphoma in China were on the rise from 2003 to 2009.[2] Lymphoma is a serious threat to human health, and it is important to deeply investigate the need for joint emotional care of medical and nursing cohort checkups for lymphoma chemotherapy patients.[3] Lymphoma is a systemic disease that can invade almost any tissue and organ throughout the body, so chemotherapy has become the main treatment method for lymphoma patients.[4] Due to the long course of chemotherapy and complex treatment regimen, it causes damage to patients in many aspects, and chemotherapy drugs have various toxic side effects, such as bone marrow suppression, gastrointestinal toxicity, immunosuppression, cardiotoxicity, causing dizziness, nausea, constipation, cough, hair loss and other symptoms, which can cause physical damage to patients in addition to emotional, cognitive and other mental disorders, which can seriously affect the quality of life of patients and even depression.[5] Since malignant lymphoma cannot withstand surgical treatment, it is more often treated conservatively with drugs. During chemotherapy, patients are highly susceptible to negative emotions such as anxiety and depression due to the lack of knowledge about the disease and chemotherapy drugs and the resulting adverse effects.[6] This not only decreases patients’ compliance with chemotherapy and affects their treatment outcome, but also seriously affects their quality of survival, so active postoperative care is particularly important for patients’ recovery.[7] Emotional care refers to influencing and improving patients’ emotions through the expressions, words, attitudes and behaviors of nursing staff, alleviating various adverse emotions and behaviors of patients, thus enhancing patients’ will and confidence to overcome diseases so that patients can be treated and cared for in the best psychological state to achieve early recovery.[8] The nursing staff can accurately and timely grasp the changes in the patient’s condition, diagnosis and treatment plan to further improve the nurses’ ability to identify, analyze and solve problems.[9] The effect of medical and nursing group consultation combined with emotional care on postoperative care of patients with ML is better, but the combination of the two is not enough for relevant studies and results, so this study investigates the effect of medical and nursing group consultation combined with emotional care on the quality of life and emotions of patients with ML, and provides a certain reference basis for clinical care of patients with ML.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Research participants

One hundred sixty cases of ML patients admitted to our hospital from March 2020 to July 2022 were selected as retrospective study subjects, and 40 cases each were divided into control group and observation group according to the different nursing methods. Inclusion criteria: all selected patients with ML met the diagnostic criteria of the Malignant Lymphoma Treatment Guidelines and were cared for after surgery or radiotherapy;[2] all selected patients were hospitalized for ≥ 10 d, with stable vital signs and normal mental status able to complete the evaluation related to this study and no history of chronic diseases; expected survival time ≥ 6 months and able to live normally and with good compliance. Exclusion criteria: severe medical disease intolerant or physical dysfunction, pregnancy and lactation or mental disorders; anemia or coagulation disorders and combined diabetes, hypertension and cardiac insufficiency, sedative-hypnotic drug dependent patients; combined cognitive insufficiency, mental disorders, severe infections, and inability to cooperate with the completion of each scale survey. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University.

2.2. Care methods

2.2.1. Emotional and spiritual care.

Empathic emotional communication: Nurses intervene in the role of friends and confidants of patients with malignant lymphoma, guiding patients with malignant lymphoma to vent their negative emotions of dissatisfaction with God’s injustice and other realities, not to suppress their emotions, and to give patients with malignant lymphoma a loving hug and diversion in a targeted manner. Ask the patient which one the child likes best. How did you feel after the birth of your child? How do you spend your Spring Festival each year? Do you have young grandchildren or young grandchildren? Where are they? Can you share your life experiences with me? While telling the story, another nurse can videotape the patient with malignant lymphoma, and the nurse can share her story with the patient with malignant lymphoma. Together, they can explore their own ways to get through the bottleneck and give encouragement to patients with malignant lymphoma, thus creating emotional empathy. Abnormal emotion care: Patients with malignant lymphoma have symptoms of anxiety all day long and are overly sensitive to test results and side effects during chemotherapy. Terror is the will of the kidney and can easily hurt the kidney. For this type of malignant lymphoma patients, after admission, they should take the initiative to explain to malignant lymphoma patients the possible types of adverse reactions and treatment methods, explain the test results; reduce the intake of tea, coffee and other stimulating beverages, and instruct malignant lymphoma patients to soak their feet in warm water, rub their feet hot and massage the Yongquan point every night before going to bed to help the kidneys. Patients with this type of malignant lymphoma mainly show excessive concern for disease and somatic changes, and are unable to sleep at night. The person who thinks is the spleen will, and the person who thinks is the qi knot, and the person who thinks too much is the spleen injury, so the method of empathy should be used for this type of malignant lymphoma patients. Provide simple answers to the disease questions raised by malignant lymphoma patients and consciously shift the topic to the hobbies of malignant lymphoma patients. Encourage malignant lymphoma patients to carry out hobby projects with less activity during hospitalization in order to guide the family members of malignant lymphoma patients to provide foods with spleen-strengthening and appetizing effects such as hawthorn and diced meat porridge. Patients with this type of malignant lymphoma mainly suffer from mood swings, moodiness, and anger, with qi rushing upward and anger hurting the liver. Patients with this type of malignant lymphoma should have a lot of patience and should not avoid or explain when their emotions flare up, and patiently allow patients with malignant lymphoma to vent their anger. When patients with malignant lymphoma are more calm, explain to patients with malignant lymphoma the effects of anger on their condition; at the same time, instruct patients with malignant lymphoma to master the joyful method and the breathing decompression method to maintain a stable state of mind, specifically under the guidance of nursing staff. The specific method is to relax the muscles of each part of the body in turn under the guidance of nursing staff, while supplementing with rhythmic and soothing breathing. Contemplative scenario therapy: The patient is guided to conduct contemplative training by using one of the 5 elements of music and the patient’s favorite picture as the background for training, guiding the patient’s body to relax, sitting in a comfortable sitting position with a straight back, hands on the legs, eyes gently closed to achieve body relaxation, using abdominal breathing method to regulate breathing, relaxing the muscles of the whole body, and resting and breathing. The head nurse made a narration next to you: you now feel your body getting lighter and lighter, your eyelids thicken and want to sleep, feel your body slowly flying off the ground, feel the light in front of you getting more and more blinding, want to force open but cannot open, slowly you are surrounded by this bright light, when you force open your eyes, you find yourself in a whole new world. It all looks so beautiful; can you tell me what you see? The wind is blowing in the meadow, the flowers are smelling in the flowers, hear the sound of the creek? See your grandchildren coming toward you? You will fall in love with the beautiful world. The patient’s meditation training was controlled to 30 minutes, once a day.

2.2.2. Medical and nursing team check-in.

Morning medical and nursing check-in with the same group: At 7:00 am daily, each responsible group conducts bedside handover, implements morning care, further understands the condition of patients with malignant lymphoma, and does nursing checks, and holds a breakfast meeting at 8:00 am. Then the team leader and the nurse visit the medical team together: first, the nurse reports the changes of the patient’s condition at night and the implementation of treatment care; the team leader makes additions and makes suggestions on the malignant lymphoma patient’s condition or doubts in treatment; then the physician reports the condition, treatment and auxiliary examinations and puts forward the problems that need to be solved by the higher-level physician. Finally, the physician will check the body and make assessment and modification of the treatment, care and rehabilitation plan. With the case to the personnel involved in the inspection room to ask questions in a hierarchical manner, demonstrate the examination, and teach new knowledge and techniques of diagnosis and treatment related to the disease. After the inspection, the team leader and team members of nursing will synthesize the information collected during the inspection, combine it with the new treatment plan, discuss and analyze the problems highlighted by nursing care in the group of patients with malignant lymphoma, reformulate and improve nursing measures, and inform the patients with malignant lymphoma to cooperate. Afternoon checkup: At 5:00 pm, doctors and nurses check the implementation of the day’s treatment and nursing measures, closely observe the changes in the condition and carefully check the body to give professional guidance, and focus on the consultation and checkup of the newly admitted patients with more critical conditions. According to the condition of patients with malignant lymphoma, the important and difficult points of treatment are discussed and corresponding therapeutic nursing interventions are given. The nurse should do a good job of recording the communication between the visiting group and the patients with malignant lymphoma, understanding the psychological and physical conditions of the patients with malignant lymphoma, and doing a good job of answering questions and solving problems. At the end of the room visit, at the same time, summarize the problems that existed during the room visit and propose solutions in a timely manner. The nursing plan is adjusted according to the condition to ensure the relevance and effectiveness of the nursing plan. For the professional knowledge related to malignant lymphoma in the nursing process, the responsible nurse can consult with the emergency physician to improve the quality of nursing service.

2.3. Observation indicator

The changes in quality of life, emotional changes, and sleep quality of patients before and after care were assessed. Quality of life score:[10] including 4 parts: mental vitality, social interaction, emotional restriction, and mental status scores, each part counts 0 to 100 points, the higher the score the better the quality of life of patients with malignant lymphoma. Anxiety assessment: the Hamilton Anxiety Scale (HAMA) and Self-Rating Anxiety Scale (SAS) were evaluated.[11,12] The higher the score the more severe the anxiety. The Cronbach’s α value of the above scales was greater than 0.914. Sleep quality: Pittsburgh sleep quality index was evaluated.[13] The higher the score the worse the sleep status. The Cronbach’s α values was 0.931. Patients with malignant lymphoma filled out the scale independently without any internal or external factors influencing the actual situation.

2.4. Statistical analysis

All statistical data in this study were entered into excel software by the first author and the corresponding author respectively, and the statistical processing software was SPSS 25.0 for calculation. The measurement data expressed as mean ± standard deviation and compared by using t test. The count data expressed as a percentage n (%) and compared by using χ2. The statistical significance was defined as P < .05.

4. Discussion

Emotional nursing is an intervention strategy based on the emotional characteristics of patients. It requires nurses to focus on the patient, analyze the patient’s current emotional state from the patient’s standpoint and perspective, and formulate a personalized nursing empathy communication plan.[14] When patients focus on the life events they are interested in in the past, it can effectively enhance the sense of concentration and cohesion when dealing with events, which relieves the patients’ anxiety and depression to a certain extent.[15] In addition, by helping patients trace back topics of interest in the past, they can reeexperience familiar and warm emotions before, which can make them feel inner happiness, which is helpful to calm down their anxiety and face them with a calm and upright attitude.[16] Compared with emotional nursing, the same group of doctors and nurses has the following advantages. In the same ward round team, effective communication between doctors and patients, expands the scope of information exchange, and enhances the understanding and cooperation between doctors and patients, forming a working mode of disease treatment, nursing and patient cooperation.[17] It more directly reflects the professional connection and support and cooperation of medical staff. Nursing staff can keep abreast of the patient’s condition, treatment plan, special consultation and medication through follow-up rounds, obtain medical theoretical knowledge and clinical experience, and make more accurate nursing. Diagnosis and care planning, maintaining alignment with medical goals.[18] At the same time, doctors can also use the nursing information obtained during ward rounds to further improve the diagnosis and treatment plan and ensure the scientific and effective treatment methods.[19]

This study investigated the effect of medical and nursing rounds combined with emotional nursing on the quality of life and emotion of patients with malignant lymphoma. The results showed that there was no significant difference in the quality of life, emotion-related scores and sleep quality scores between the 2 groups before nursing. After nursing, the mental vitality score, social interaction score, emotional restriction score and mental status of the observation group were significantly higher than those of the control group. After nursing, the anxiety-related scores of the 2 groups of patients with malignant lymphoma were significantly improved, and the patients with malignant lymphoma in the observation group were significantly improved. The SAS and HAMA of the observation groups were significantly lower than those of the control group. The sleep quality, increased awakening, sleep instability, and night terrors of the 2 groups of malignant lymphoma patients were significantly improved, and the sleep quality score of the observation group was significantly lower than that of the control group. The same group of doctors and nurses can improve the bad mood of patients, and objectively help improve the quality of life and sleep quality. The same group of ward rounds and corresponding nursing measures can help divert attention to insomnia and improve sleep quality.[20] Poor sleep quality refers to the patient’s subjective experience of being unsatisfied with sleep time or quality and affecting daytime social function. It is mainly manifested in difficulty sleeping, early awakening, and insomnia, which may affect the prognosis, increase the pain and economic burden of the patient, and should cause medical attention.[21] The main nursing measures for this are to actively communicate with patients, find out the factors affecting the patient’s sleep, carry out psychological adjustment and behavioral guidance, and encourage patients to treat them correctly with an optimistic and confident attitude.[22] Instruct patients to use psychological knowledge to adjust their psychology and control unpleasant emotions.[23] Chemotherapy is one of the main methods for the treatment of malignant lymphoma, and it is widely used in clinic. However, while chemotherapy has a significant therapeutic effect, its adverse reactions and toxic side effects also have a great impact on patients.[24] Affected by the disease and the reaction of chemotherapy drugs during chemotherapy, the psychological and social problems of patients frequently appear, which directly affects the quality of life of patients.[25] During the research, it was found that it is very important to determine the needs of lymphoma patients undergoing chemotherapy before discharge from hospital. According to the characteristics of lymphoma patients undergoing chemotherapy, nurses work with them to formulate behavior changes and rehabilitation plans that are compatible with their own living habits, which can promote the behavior of lymphoma patients undergoing chemotherapy. Implementation of the changes.[26] Therefore, the intervention should truly be centered on the lymphoma patients undergoing chemotherapy, rather than simply focusing on the symptoms of the lymphoma patients undergoing chemotherapy.[27] The rehabilitation period of patients with lymphoma chemotherapy is mainly carried out at home, and the focus of health education is to make patients understand their own health status and enhance their confidence in disease treatment.[28] Follow-up staff maintain close contact with lymphoma chemotherapy patients through telephone follow-up or network communication, and pay attention to the patient’s statement will increase the patient’s sense of trust.[29] Guide patients and primary caregivers to correctly describe the symptoms of adverse reactions, encourage the expression of true thoughts, detect changes in their conditions in a timely and acute manner, and give timely and correct guidance.[30] Therefore, the implementation of ward rounds combined with emotional nursing by medical and nursing groups is very important for the recovery of lymphoma patients undergoing chemotherapy. Furthermore, attention should be paid to solving the psychological problems of patients undergoing chemotherapy for lymphoma. Some patients undergoing chemotherapy for lymphoma are affected by long-term diseases and often have a particularly negative attitude towards the disease, which affects the implementation of the effect of the same group of doctors and nurses, combined with emotional care.[31]

This study has certain limitations. The patients with malignant lymphoma included in this study did not include other types of patients, and the selected patients with malignant lymphoma were all from patients with malignant lymphoma treated or nursed in our hospital, so the selection of patients with malignant lymphoma to be included and excluded is subject to a certain degree. study results may be unrepresentative or biased.

In conclusion, ward rounds combined with emotional nursing in the same group of doctors and nurses can effectively improve the quality of life and sleep in patients with malignant lymphoma, reduce untrained emotions, and benefit the prognosis and clinical treatment of patients. It has certain reference value for the nursing of patients with malignant lymphoma.

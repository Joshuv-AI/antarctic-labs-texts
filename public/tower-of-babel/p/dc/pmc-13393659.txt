Disulfidptosis in PCOS pathogenesis: Multi-omics identification of LRPPRC as a diagnostic biomarker and therapeutic target
Authors: Lele Ling, Shiyu Zhang, Dongyao Zhang, Xue Zhao, Jinyang Wang, Yincheng Teng, Zhihong Ai, Xiaolu Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryPolycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is characterized by reproductive dysfunction, insulin resistance, oxidative stress, and metabolic abnormalities, yet the role of disulfidptosis in granulosa cell injury remains unclear. Here, integrated single-cell and bulk transcriptomic analyses identified granulosa cells as the primary disulfidptosis-associated cell type in the PCOS follicular microenvironment. Disulfidptosis-related genes, including leucine-rich pentatricopeptide repeat-containing protein (LRPPRC), NDUFS1, and OXSM, were significantly downregulated and associated with mitochondrial dysfunction and redox imbalance. A diagnostic model achieved an AUC of 0.867, while Mendelian randomization identified LRPPRC as a protective factor for PCOS. Functionally, LRPPRC deficiency under glucose deprivation induced NADPH depletion, F-actin collapse, and granulosa cell death, which was rescued by 2-mercaptoethanol but not by apoptosis, ferroptosis, or necroptosis inhibitors. In vivo, LRPPRC overexpression improved follicular development and hormonal balance in PCOS mice. These findings identify LRPPRC-mediated disulfidptosis as a potential biomarker and therapeutic target in PCOS.

Introduction

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is a prevalent endocrine disorder that significantly affects the female reproductive system, with a prevalence of 5–10% among women of reproductive age.1 PCOS is highly heterogeneous, resulting from complex interactions among genetic, epigenetic, environmental, metabolic, and neuroendocrine factors.2,3,4,5,6,7,8,9,10 Beyond reproductive health, PCOS is linked to higher risks of metabolic and cardiovascular diseases. Its complex pathophysiology and diverse clinical manifestations require comprehensive diagnostic and therapeutic strategies to improve reproductive and overall health outcomes.11,12 A major research challenge is the incomplete understanding of the core pathogenic mechanisms of PCOS. Clinically, accurate patient subtyping remains challenging, and universal diagnostic biomarkers are lacking. Furthermore, no pharmacological agents can reverse ovarian dysfunction or repair mitochondrial damage. Although some drugs promote ovulation, they do not fundamentally restore ovarian function or improve follicular quality.13,14

Disulfidptosis is a newly identified form of cell death that occurs during glucose starvation, when high SLC7A11 expression and pentose phosphate pathway inhibition cause NADPH deficiency, leading to disulfide stress and cytoskeletal collapse.15,16 This process may be linked to PCOS pathogenesis. Insulin resistance (IR), highly prevalent in PCOS, impairs glucose utilization and creates a state of “glucose starvation,” potentially increasing susceptibility to disulfidptosis. IR in PCOS is associated with oxidative stress, inflammation, and metabolic abnormalities.17,18 For example, disulfidptosis relies on abnormal disulfide accumulation, while hyperandrogenism and IR in PCOS exacerbate oxidative stress and promote intracellular protein disulfide cross-linking (e.g., elevated MDA, reduced SOD).17 Hyperandrogenism further disrupts insulin signaling and glucose transporter expression, impairing glucose metabolism.19 Moreover, IR in PCOS reduces GLUT-4 expression in adipocytes, and the compensatory increase in GLUT-1 fails to compensate, further aggravating IR.20 To date, no study has directly investigated the relationship between disulfidptosis and PCOS. It remains unclear whether disulfidptosis occurs in the ovarian microenvironment of patients with PCOS and whether it represents a pathogenic mechanism, particularly involving aberrant expression or mutation of disulfidptosis-related genes.

Most disulfidptosis-related genes are mitochondria-associated and influence metabolic processes such as glycolysis and the pentose phosphate pathway. LRPPRC (leucine-rich pentatricopeptide repeat-containing protein) is a key regulator of mitochondrial RNA metabolism. LRPPRC forms a complex with SLIRP to maintain the stability, polyadenylation, and translation of mitochondrial mRNAs.21,22 Functional defects in LRPPRC impair the assembly of mitochondrial respiratory chain complexes, especially cytochrome c oxidase (COX), causing energy metabolism crises.23,24 Thus, LRPPRC may indirectly regulate disulfidptosis by affecting mitochondrial redox balance, including NADPH supply. Numerous studies link mitochondrial dysfunction to PCOS pathogenesis, particularly under oxidative stress and IR, which jointly impair follicular development and function.25,26,27 Dysregulated LRPPRC expression may cause mitochondrial dysfunction, highlighting its potential role in PCOS pathophysiology. Evidence suggests that LRPPRC specifically regulates mitochondrial DNA-encoded mRNA expression. Consequently, the dysregulated expression of LRPPRC may disrupt mitochondrial function and impair follicular quality and function.28 Moreover, mitochondrial dysfunction may increase reactive oxygen species (ROSs) and oxidative stress, further impairing follicular growth and maturation.29,30 Currently, no study has directly examined the role of LRPPRC in PCOS. Whether LRPPRC acts as a factor in PCOS pathogenesis and progression, and whether it could serve as a future therapeutic target, remains to be determined.

Based on high-throughput data from large cohorts of patients with PCOS, the present study integrates multi-omics, genetic, and experimental approaches to systematically investigate the role of disulfidptosis in PCOS and its potential contribution to disease onset and progression. First, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) was used to assess disulfidptosis-related alterations in the follicular microenvironment and to identify granulosa cells as the principal cell population associated with these changes. Next, transcriptomic data from granulosa cells were analyzed to explore the biological processes (BPs) and molecular mechanisms linked to disulfidptosis in PCOS, followed by the experimental validation of candidate regulatory genes. In addition, a diagnostic model named PCDDM was developed using machine learning and complemented by Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis, which identified LRPPRC in granulosa cells as a potential diagnostic biomarker and therapeutic target. Furthermore, in vitro and in vivo mechanistic experiments, including glucose deprivation, pharmacological perturbation, and rescue assays, were performed to evaluate disulfidptosis-related phenotypes and to further clarify the role of LRPPRC in granulosa cell injury under metabolic stress. Collectively, this study provides an integrated framework for understanding the involvement of disulfidptosis in PCOS and offers new insights into its pathogenic mechanisms, diagnostic stratification, and potential therapeutic intervention.

Results

Single-cell sequencing analysis of the impact of disulfidptosis in the follicular microenvironment

We analyzed single-cell data from six preovulatory follicular microenvironments in in vitro fertilization to investigate the impact of disulfidptosis in the follicular microenvironment. First, we performed dimensionality reduction on the single-cell data and used the UMAP method to divide the follicular microenvironment into 15 cell clusters. Next, we used the FindAllMarkers function to identify differentially expressed genes in different cell clusters and displayed the top 5 highly expressed and lowly expressed genes in each cluster. Finally, we classified the follicular microenvironment into 5 cell types: granulosa cells, epithelial cells, monocytes/macrophages, dendritic cells, and T cells (Figures 1A–1C).Figure 1Single-cell data analysis reveals the impact of disulfidptosis on the follicular microenvironment in patients with PCOS(A) Dimensionality reduction results of single-cell data.(B) Cell counts per cluster and their assignments.(C) Differentially expressed genes across cell types, displaying the top 5 genes with the highest and lowest expression levels in each cell type.(D) GSVA pathway scores for each cell, with a heatmap showing each cell type and Hallmark pathways.(E) GSVA disulfidptosis scores for each cell, displaying correlations between disulfidptosis scores and Hallmark pathways for each cell type; ∗p < 0.05, ∗∗p < 0.01, ∗∗∗p < 0.001, and ∗∗∗∗p < 0.0001.(F) Expression levels of genes within the disulfidptosis gene set across cells.(G) Disulfidptosis scores across cells.(H) Immune cell counts and proportions in high- and low-disulfidptosis score groups.(I) UMAP plot displaying disulfidptosis scores across cells, divided into two groups based on high and low scores.(J) Hallmark pathway results for high- and low-disulfidptosis score groups.

We performed GSVA pathway enrichment analysis on single-cell sequencing samples from the follicular microenvironment. GSVA can score pathways at the level of individual samples or cells, making it suitable for assessing heterogeneity within cell populations. We generated a heatmap of activity scores for different cell clusters based on the MSigDB Hallmark gene sets. The results showed that different cell types play distinctly different functional roles in the follicular microenvironment (Figure 1D). Immune cells (T cells, monocytes/macrophages, dendritic cells) generally exhibited high activity on inflammation-related pathways, while granulosa cells and epithelial cells were significantly enriched in metabolism-related pathways. More importantly, the results indicated that in the follicular microenvironment, granulosa cells and epithelial cells may undergo changes due to elevated oxidative stress levels and metabolic reprogramming. The enrichment results demonstrated that metabolic processes closely related to disulfidptosis (such as HALLMARK_REACTIVE_OXYGEN_SPECIES_PATHWAY, HALLMARK_XENOBIOTIC_METABOLISM, HALLMARK_GLYCOLYSIS, and HALLMARK_FATTY_ACID_METABOLISM) exhibited higher scores and significant activity in granulosa cells and epithelial cells. This suggests that granulosa cells and epithelial cells may be key target cells for disulfidptosis. Meanwhile, in the chronic inflammatory microenvironment shaped by activated immune cells, immune cells (monocytes/macrophages, dendritic cells, T cells) were highly enriched in pathways such as HALLMARK_TNFA_SIGNALING_VIA_NFKB, HALLMARK_INFLAMMATORY_RESPONSE, and HALLMARK_INTERFERON_ALPHA/GAMMA_RESPONSE. This is consistent with the chronic inflammatory microenvironment reported in PCOS (Figure 1D).

Based on single-cell transcriptome data, we used 12 disulfidptosis genes and the GSVA algorithm to calculate disulfidptosis scores for each cell, thereby quantifying the potential activity of disulfidptosis in different cell types. To further explore the underlying BPs driving this death mode, we calculated the correlations between disulfidptosis scores and Hallmark pathways in each cell type and generated a heatmap (Figure 1E). Correlation analysis showed that disulfidptosis has significant and specific associations with particular functional pathways in different cell types. In granulosa cells and epithelial cells, disulfidptosis-related pathways were highly enriched, with the highest correlations to fatty acid metabolism (FATTY ACID METABOLISM), glycolysis (GLYCOLYSIS), oxidative phosphorylation (OXIDATIVE PHOSPHORYLATION), and mTORC1 signaling (MTORC1 SIGNALING). This indicates that in granulosa cells and epithelial cells, disulfidptosis is closely related to cellular energy metabolism reprogramming, particularly lipid and glucose metabolism processes. Additionally, disulfidptosis scores in dendritic cells and monocytes/macrophages showed positive correlations with inflammatory response (INFLAMMATORY RESPONSE) and TNF-α signaling pathway (TNFA SIGNALING VIA NFKB). This suggests that for immune cells, the disulfidptosis process is more likely to be triggered in an inflammatory environment involving the activation and execution of immune functions. In all cell types, disulfidptosis scores showed consistent positive correlations with the ROS pathway (REACTIVE OXYGEN SPECIES PATHWAY). These findings provide a new cell-specific perspective for understanding the complex pathophysiology of PCOS and suggest that precise metabolic or anti-inflammatory interventions targeting different cell types may become new strategies for preventing and treating abnormal cell death in PCOS in the future (Figure 1E).

We subsequently analyzed the expression of genes within the disulfidptosis gene set in each cell. The results showed high expression of disulfidptosis genes in granulosa cells (Figure 1F). We also analyzed disulfidptosis scores in each cell, and the results indicated that granulosa cells had the highest disulfidptosis scores (Figure 1G). Traditional analyses are mostly based on cell types. We innovatively regrouped cells based on functional phenotypes (disulfidptosis scores) and used UMAP plots to display disulfidptosis scores in each cell. At the same time, we divided the cells into two groups based on high and low scores and displayed them. This method transcends cell type boundaries, enabling direct identification of core biological features shared by all cells with high disulfidptosis tendencies, avoiding functional heterogeneity interference caused by cell types, and making conclusions more universal and mechanistic. Further analysis showed that in the high and low disulfidptosis score groups, the number and proportion of immune cells decreased in the high group, while the number and proportion of granulosa cells increased (Figures 1H and 1I). Finally, we used the high and low disulfidptosis score groups to evaluate Hallmark pathways (Figure 1J). The high disulfidptosis group exhibited strong metabolic and oxidative stress features. Cluster analysis showed that the high group (High cluster) was specifically enriched in multiple metabolism- and stress-related pathways. Among them, the activity of energy metabolism pathways such as oxidative phosphorylation (OXIDATIVE PHOSPHORYLATION), glycolysis (GLYCOLYSIS), and fatty acid metabolism (FATTY_ACID_METABOLISM) was significantly upregulated. This indicates that cells with high disulfidptosis activity are in a highly metabolically active state. At the same time, the activity of the ROS pathway (REACTIVE OXYGEN SPECIES PATHWAY) and xenobiotic metabolism (XENOBIOTIC METABOLISM) was also synchronously enhanced. The high disulfidptosis group was associated with unique stress and repair signals. Compared to the low group, the high group was also significantly enriched in DNA repair (DNA_REPAIR), unfolded protein response (UNFOLDED_PROTEIN_RESPONSE), and p53 pathway (P53_PATHWAY). This indicates that cells activate multiple intrinsic stress and repair mechanisms before proceeding to death to maintain homeostasis (Figure 1J).

In summary, single-cell level analysis revealed the potential biological functions and mechanisms of disulfidptosis in the follicular microenvironment. The target cells for disulfidptosis may primarily be granulosa cells. The results also suggested the possibility of disulfidptosis occurring in granulosa cells and its multiple potential mechanisms.

Transcriptomic analysis of granulosa cells reveals the key role of disulfidptosis in PCOS

Subsequently, we analyzed disulfidptosis in granulosa cells using transcriptomic data from 11 normal granulosa cell samples and 15 PCOS patient granulosa cell samples (Figures 2A–2D). First, differential analysis was performed, identifying 467 upregulated genes and 1407 downregulated genes (Figures 2E and 2F). We conducted GO and KEGG enrichment analysis on the differentially expressed genes. The results indicated global dysregulation in metabolism- and redox-related gene expression in granulosa cells from patients with PCOS. These molecular changes align with the biochemical environment for disulfidptosis, confirming at the molecular level that granulosa cells are a high-risk population for disulfidptosis in PCOS (Figures 2G–2J). BP annotations highlight metabolic disorders and oxidative stress. Differentially expressed genes were significantly enriched in small molecule catabolic process, organic acid metabolic process, and carboxylic acid metabolic process. These processes are central to cellular energy metabolism. Particularly crucial is the significant enrichment in the oxidation-reduction process, which directly indicates severe oxidative stress within PCOS granulosa cells (Figure 2G). Cellular component (CC) points to mitochondria and redox sites. Differentially expressed genes were highly enriched in organelles such as the mitochondrial matrix and mitochondrial lumen. Mitochondria serve not only as the hub of cellular energy metabolism but also as the primary site for ROS generation. This result suggests that functional disorders in PCOS granulosa cells and the sites of disulfidptosis may be highly concentrated in mitochondria, indicating mitochondrial dysfunction as a key link (Figure 2H). Molecular function (MF) reveals abnormalities in key enzyme activities. Proteins encoded by differentially expressed genes primarily exhibit phosphoric ester hydrolase activity and oxidoreductase activity. The latter directly regulates intracellular redox reactions, with activity changes affecting the balance between ROS clearance and production, serving as a key determinant in cellular response to disulfide stress (Figure 2I). KEGG pathways clearly define the core role of metabolic pathways. KEGG enrichment analysis corroborates the above findings at the systemic level. The most significant enriched pathways are cofactor biosynthesis and carbon metabolism. Cofactors (such as NADPH) are essential for maintaining cellular redox homeostasis and supplying reducing power to the glutathione reduction system. Dysregulation of these pathways implies impaired synthesis capacity in PCOS granulosa cells for key molecules resisting oxidative stress. By analyzing differentially expressed genes between PCOS and normal control granulosa cells and intersecting with the disulfidptosis core gene set, we identified four key downregulated genes: LRPPRC, NCKAP1, NDUFS1, and OXSM (Figures 2K and 2L). GO and KEGG enrichment analysis of these 4 genes functionally reveals their potential roles in disulfidptosis within PCOS granulosa cells (Figures 2M–2P). BP focuses on energy metabolism and redox balance. Intersecting genes are significantly enriched in ATP metabolic process, oxidation-reduction process, and electron transport chain. This indicates that the downregulation of these four genes directly causes core functional defects in energy generation and redox homeostasis maintenance in PCOS granulosa cells. Energy (ATP) scarcity and impaired electron transport chain function are major inducers of excessive ROS production, creating essential conditions for disulfidptosis (Figure 2M). CC clearly identifies mitochondria as the core site of action. Gene products are significantly localized to the mitochondrial matrix, mitochondrial respiratory chain complex I, and mitochondrial inner membrane. This result tightly links the functions of the four genes to mitochondria (Figure 2N). MF reveals impairments in enzyme activity and structural function. The proteins encoded by intersecting genes primarily possess oxidoreductase activity and participate in NADH dehydrogenase activity. Additionally, the actin filament binding function is also enriched. This indicates that the downregulation of these genes not only affects key enzymatic reactions (such as electron transport and redox reactions) but may also directly participate in the cytoskeletal collapse in disulfidptosis induced by abnormal disulfide cross-linking through impacting cytoskeletal stability (Figure 2O). KEGG pathways pinpoint core metabolic pathways. The most significantly enriched pathways are oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) and the Parkinson's disease pathway. The latter is typically closely associated with mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress. This finding confirms from a systems biology perspective that these four genes are key nodes in the OXPHOS pathway, with their downregulation directly leading to impaired mitochondrial electron transport chain function (especially complex 1) (Figure 2P). Consistent with these findings, all four genes were located within the significantly downregulated region of the volcano plot (Figure 2Q), and both boxplot and heatmap analyses confirmed that LRPPRC, NCKAP1, NDUFS1, and OXSM were expressed at significantly lower levels in PCOS granulosa cells than in controls (Figures 2R and 2S).

In summary, this section confirms through granulosa cell transcriptomic analysis that granulosa cells in patients with PCOS exhibit a unique molecular state characterized by mitochondrial dysfunction, metabolic disorder, and redox homeostasis imbalance mediated by key gene downregulation. This state aligns with the biochemical conditions for disulfidptosis occurrence, revealing a molecular mechanism for granulosa cell dysfunction and follicular arrest in PCOS. The four genes LRPPRC, NCKAP1, NDUFS1, and OXSM form a critical network, serving as core molecular nodes linking PCOS pathology to disulfidptosis execution, providing essential theoretical foundation and precise guidance for subsequent mechanistic studies and targeted interventions.

In vitro and in vivo experiments validate the expression levels of disulfidptosis-related genes in granulosa cells

Based on the aforementioned single-cell and transcriptomic analyses, we identified four genes—LRPPRC, NCKAP1, NDUFS1, and OXSM—that may influence disulfidptosis occurrence in PCOS. First, we conducted in vitro validation of these genes. We utilized DHT to treat the KGN granulosa cell line, establishing an in vitro PCOS-like granulosa cell model. We performed immunofluorescence and RT-qPCR assays on the model. Immunofluorescence results demonstrated that the fluorescence intensities of LRPPRC, NCKAP1, NDUFS1, and OXSM were significantly reduced following DHT treatment. This suggests the downregulation of these genes’ protein levels under PCOS conditions (Figure 3A). Furthermore, RT-qPCR results indicated the downregulation of LRPPRC, NCKAP1, NDUFS1, and OXSM mRNA expression levels, particularly for LRPPRC and NCKAP1. Statistical analysis was performed on the number of corpora lutea and cystic follicles in the pathological sections. Compared to the CON group, the DHEA group showed a significant decrease in corpora lutea count and a significant increase in cystic follicle count (Figure 4B). We measured serum levels of E2, T, AMH, LH, and FSH in mice. Results showed that, compared to the CON group, the DHEA group had significantly decreased E2 levels and significantly elevated T, AMH, LH, and FSH levels (Figure 4C). Machine learning constructs a PCOS diagnostic model based on granulosa cell disulfidptosis genes

Based on in vitro and in vivo experimental validation results, we utilized machine learning to evaluate and construct a PCOS disulfidptosis diagnostic model (PCDDM). We employed LASSO regression, random forest, and SVM support vector machine for screening (Figures 5A–5C). Ultimately, a diagnostic model based on NDUFS1, OXSM, and LRPPRC was constructed. This model exhibits good sensitivity and specificity, with an AUC value of 0.867 (Figures 5D–5F). To confirm the model’s robustness, we applied the PCDDM to the merged independent external validation cohort (GSE95728 and GSE137684). The model maintained a strong predictive performance with an AUC of 0.727, demonstrating its high generalizability across different patient cohorts (Figure S2).Figure 5Construction of the PCOS diagnostic model PCDDM using machine learning based on disulfidptosis genes(A) LASSO regression.(B) Random forest ranked by importance.(C) SVM support vector machine screening.(D) Venn diagram intersection yielding core genes LRPPRC, NDUFS1, OXSM.(E) Correlations among core genes LRPPRC, NDUFS1, OXSM, with red indicating positive correlation and green indicating negative correlation.(F) ROC curves for disease prediction using the 3 genes; x-axis is “1-specificity,” y axis is “sensitivity”.(G) Expression of LRPPRC, NDUFS1, OXSM in single-cell data.(H–J) Top 20 results from GSEA analysis of LRPPRC, NDUFS1, OXSM using Reactome, see also Figure S1; the x axis value represents the enrichment score, where >0 indicates positive correlation between gene and pathway, and <0 indicates negative correlation.

Subsequently, we validated the expression levels of NDUFS1, OXSM, and LRPPRC across various cell subpopulations at the single-cell sequencing level. Results showed that these genes were specifically highly expressed in granulosa cells (Figure 5G). We further conducted single-gene GSEA analysis on these three genes (LRPPRC, NDUFS1, OXSM) (Figures S1A–S1C; Figures 5H–5J). The analysis results, from a single-gene perspective, elucidate how they collectively lead to mitochondrial dysfunction in PCOS granulosa cells, thereby inducing disulfidptosis. GSEA analysis revealed that LRPPRC expression levels are significantly positively correlated with translation and cholesterol biosynthesis pathways. This indicates that LRPPRC downregulation not only affects ribosomal protein synthesis function but may also influence cell membrane structure and function by disrupting cholesterol metabolism homeostasis. The disruption of these processes may indirectly affect the cell’s stress response capacity (Figure 5H). NDUFS1 expression is highly positively correlated with core energy metabolism pathways such as respiratory electron transport and ATP synthesis. This aligns with NDUFS1’s MF as a core subunit of mitochondrial complex I, confirming that its downregulation directly leads to OXPHOS dysfunction (Figure 5I). OXSM expression is significantly enriched in the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle and OXPHOS pathways. This indicates that OXSM, as a lipid metabolism-related enzyme, its functional downregulation affects the normal metabolism and flux of acetyl-CoA, disrupting TCA cycle integrity and thereby reducing the reducing equivalents supplied to the electron transport chain (Figure 5J).

In summary, LRPPRC, NDUFS1, and OXSM, through regulating different levels of BPs (translation, cholesterol metabolism, respiratory electron transport, TCA cycle, and so forth), collectively cause severe energy metabolism defects and redox imbalance in PCOS granulosa cells. This discovery reveals the multi-level driving mechanisms of disulfidptosis at the pathway level, providing an important basis for developing precise intervention strategies targeting different functional defects.

LRPPRC serves as an effective diagnostic biomarker for PCOS

We found that LRPPRC is a significant protective factor for PCOS, and its low expression increases the risk of disease progression. Given the causal association between LRPPRC and PCOS, we further analyzed the functional role of LRPPRC in granulosa cells. Results showed that LRPPRC knockdown significantly inhibits granulosa cell proliferation (Figure 6F; Figure S4). Since previous studies have demonstrated that DHT induction downregulates LRPPRC expression levels, we used overexpression plasmids to upregulate LRPPRC expression levels. In the DHT-induced model, results indicated that LRPPRC overexpression rescues the DHT-induced downregulation of proliferation capacity (Figure 6G; Figure S4). Additionally, we used Annexin V-FITC/PI double staining to detect the impact of LRPPRC expression intervention on death in KGN granulosa cells. We found that knocking down LRPPRC expression levels promotes death in the KGN granulosa cell line, while overexpressing LRPPRC rescues DHT-induced KGN cell death (Figures 6H and 6I).

Discussion

The integration of scRNA-seq and bulk transcriptomics in this study reveals that disulfidptosis exerts a profound influence on the follicular microenvironment in PCOS, primarily targeting granulosa cells. Disulfidptosis, characterized by NADPH depletion and disulfide stress under glucose starvation conditions,15,16 aligns with the metabolic reprogramming observed in PCOS, where IR mimics glucose deprivation and heightens oxidative stress. The elevated disulfidptosis scores in granulosa cells correlate strongly with pathways such as OXPHOS, glycolysis, and fatty acid metabolism, indicating that these cells undergo energy crises and redox imbalances that impair follicular development. This finding extends the theoretical framework of PCOS pathogenesis, which involves multifaceted interactions among genetic, metabolic, and neuroendocrine factors.1,2 By demonstrating disulfidptosis as a mechanistic link, the results underscore its role in perpetuating mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress, core elements that hinder oocyte quality and ovulation, thus providing a novel lens for understanding the heterogeneous clinical manifestations of PCOS. Notably, Zhang et al. emphasize how mitochondrial dysfunction disrupts energy production in granulosa cells during ovarian aging, mirroring the mitochondrial matrix enrichments and redox perturbations identified here, and suggesting that disulfidptosis may accelerate similar processes in PCOS.31

Downregulation of key disulfidptosis-related genes, including LRPPRC, NCKAP1, NDUFS1, and OXSM, in PCOS granulosa cells further elucidates the molecular underpinnings of this process. LRPPRC, a mitochondrial RNA regulator essential for respiratory chain assembly,21,22 emerges as a pivotal player by modulating NADPH supply and redox balance, deficiencies of which precipitate disulfide stress and cytoskeletal collapse. Enrichment analyses confirm that these genes converge on mitochondrial matrix functions and OXPHOS, directly contributing to the energy metabolism defects prevalent in PCOS.25,26 The significance lies in bridging disulfidptosis to established PCOS hallmarks such as hyperandrogenism and IR, which exacerbate glucose transporter dysregulation and oxidative markers.17,19 Wang et al. report that suppressing p66Shc in hyperandrogenism-induced PCOS models—via Sirt1-mediated inhibition of p66Shc—mitigates ovarian oxidative stress and fibrosis, providing a comparative redox mechanism to the disulfide stress observed in our study and reinforcing the potential of targeting redox regulators like LRPPRC.32 This interpretation highlights how LRPPRC dysfunction amplifies a vicious cycle of mitochondrial impairment and cell death, offering a theoretical basis for why current therapies fail to restore ovarian function fundamentally.13,14

The construction of the PCDDM diagnostic model, incorporating LRPPRC, NDUFS1, and OXSM, achieves an AUC of 0.867, demonstrating robust predictive value for PCOS. To rigorously demonstrate the clinical utility of our proposed methodology, it is essential to benchmark the PCDDM against existing predictive approaches. While previous machine learning models for PCOS often rely on large, non-specific gene panels or generic clinical parameters, our PCDDM achieves highly competitive diagnostic accuracy utilizing a parsimonious signature of only three genes. In our internal cohort, the model achieved an AUC of 0.867. Crucially, the integration of a merged independent external validation cohort (GSE95728 and GSE137684) further confirmed the model’s robust generalizability, yielding an AUC of 0.727. It is worth noting the distinction between the four disulfidptosis-related genes identified through our initial multi-omics analysis (LRPPRC, NCKAP1, NDUFS1, and OXSM) and the three genes ultimately retained in the PCDDM diagnostic model. Although NCKAP1 demonstrated significant biological relevance to the pathogenesis of PCOS—as evidenced by our in vitro and in vivo validations—it was excluded from the final diagnostic signature. This exclusion was an objective, data-driven outcome of our rigorous machine learning feature selection pipeline (comprising LASSO, Random Forest, and SVM-RFE). In predictive modeling, these algorithms are designed to penalize or eliminate features that exhibit high collinearity to prevent model overfitting. Consequently, while NCKAP1 remains mechanistically integral to disulfidptosis in the ovarian microenvironment, its statistical contribution was likely redundant when evaluated alongside LRPPRC, NDUFS1, and OXSM. The retained three-gene panel sufficiently captured the necessary diagnostic variance, highlighting the important distinction between a biomarker’s biological role in disease progression and its mathematical utility in a parsimonious clinical diagnostic model. MR analysis solidifies LRPPRC as a causal protective factor, with low expression elevating disease risk, consistent with its upstream regulatory role in translation and cholesterol biosynthesis pathways revealed by GSEA. This advances the theoretical understanding by positioning LRPPRC not merely as a biomarker but as a nexus integrating metabolic and genetic vulnerabilities in PCOS.3,4 The in vitro and in vivo validations, showing LRPPRC knockdown promotes granulosa cell death while overexpression rescues DHT-induced phenotypes, reinforce its therapeutic potential. These findings innovate by linking disulfidptosis to actionable targets, addressing the gap in universal biomarkers and mitochondria-repairing agents.11,12

Compared to existing “black-box” algorithms, the PCDDM possesses a distinct theoretical and translational advantage. The selected features are not merely statistical artifacts but are mechanistically anchored in disulfidptosis and mitochondrial dysfunction within the granulosa cell microenvironment. By targeting specific metabolic and redox imbalances, this three-gene panel surpasses traditional approaches by providing both diagnostic precision and a direct link to potential mitochondria-based therapeutic targets, such as LRPPRC.

Transitioning from these interpretations, comparisons with prior research illuminate both convergences and distinctions. Existing studies on mitochondrial dysfunction in PCOS emphasize oxidative stress and energy deficits in granulosa cells, aligning with our observations of enriched ROSs and unfolded protein response pathways.27,29,30 For instance, mitochondrial impairments linked to ROS overproduction impair follicular maturation, mirroring our disulfidptosis scores’ correlation with metabolic reprogramming.25 However, while previous work focuses on general oxidative markers such as MDA and SOD under IR,17,18 our study uniquely integrates disulfidptosis as a specific cell death modality, extending beyond nonspecific stress to disulfide-mediated cytoskeletal collapse. This specificity differentiates our approach, as earlier investigations overlook the role of SLC7A11 and pentose phosphate pathway inhibition in PCOS contexts.15

In terms of disulfidptosis, comparisons reveal its nascent exploration in reproductive disorders. Recent reports on thiol/disulfide homeostasis in PCOS follicular fluid document imbalances favoring disulfides, which parallel our elevated disulfidptosis activity in granulosa cells.33 Yet, these studies remain descriptive, lacking mechanistic ties to cell death or gene-specific analyses like our intersection yielding LRPPRC. In contrast to disulfidptosis applications in cancer, where it predicts prognosis via multi-omics models,34 our work pioneers its relevance in PCOS, identifying granulosa cells as primary targets rather than immune or tumor cells. No prior research directly examines LRPPRC in PCOS, though its mitochondrial roles in energy crises are noted in metabolic diseases.23,28 This gap underscores our innovation: While mitochondrial dysfunction is well-documented,27 we provide the first causal evidence via MR and functional assays, distinguishing LRPPRC as a diagnostic and therapeutic node absent in earlier PCOS proteomics or metabolomics.6,9

Despite these advances, the study has limitations that warrant consideration. First, the scRNA-seq dataset (GSE189960) utilized in this study was not generated directly from PCOS patient follicles and mainly represents the preovulatory stage. Consequently, it should be interpreted as a biologically relevant reference framework for the follicular microenvironment rather than a disease-stage-matched PCOS single-cell atlas. Furthermore, the reliance on multiple GEO datasets introduces potential biases from sample heterogeneity, although batch corrections via Harmony and limma mitigated this. Animal models, while recapitulating PCOS phenotypes through DHEA and high-fat diet,35,36 may not fully capture human genetic diversity, limiting generalizability. Another methodological limitation relates to the MR analysis. Due to the current lack of large-scale, highly powered eQTL datasets specifically derived from human ovarian tissue, the eQTLs utilized as instrumental variables were primarily derived from systemic datasets. Although LRPPRC is a ubiquitously expressed mitochondrial regulator, systemic eQTLs may not perfectly mirror the tissue-specific regulatory landscape within ovarian granulosa cells. Future studies incorporating large-scale, ovarian-specific eQTLs are warranted to definitively confirm these localized causal links. Moreover, A pertinent limitation of this study is the constrained access to granular, individual-level clinical metadata (such as exact BMI, metabolic profiles, and specific diagnostic sub-phenotypes) within the utilized public repositories (GSE189960, GSE34526, GSE168404, and GSE193123). In the context of a highly heterogeneous disorder like PCOS, such unmeasured variables could theoretically act as confounders. To address this, we implemented a dual-layered safeguards strategy: first, employing rigorous computational de-noising and batch correction via Harmony and limma to minimize platform- and sample-specific variance; and second, utilizing a multi-cohort cross-validation design. The fact that the LRPPRC-mediated disulfidptosis signature remained highly consistent across independent single-cell and bulk transcriptomic datasets—which likely encompass diverse clinical backgrounds—strongly suggests that this molecular deficit represents an invariant pathological “core” of PCOS. This consistency effectively mitigates the concern that our findings are artifacts of specific clinical subgroups, although future prospective studies with comprehensive metabolic phenotyping remain essential to further refine these associations. In vitro validations using KGN cells and DHT treatment simulate hyperandrogenism effectively,37 but overlook environmental factors such as gut microbiota or epigenetics.5,10 Small sample sizes in experimental cohorts could amplify variability, though statistical rigor (e.g., ANOVA) addressed this. To preempt concerns on causality, the MR approach incorporated multiple methods,38,39 ensuring robustness against pleiotropy.

Future research should expand on these foundations by validating findings in larger, diverse human cohorts to enhance translational relevance. Longitudinal studies tracking disulfidptosis markers pre- and post-treatment could assess dynamic changes, informing personalized interventions. Exploring LRPPRC modulators, such as small molecules targeting mitochondrial RNA stability, holds promise for drug development, building on adenoviral overexpression success. Integrating proteomics or metabolomics with our multi-omics framework would delineate downstream effectors, while CRISPR-based gene editing in organoids could clarify cell-type specificity. Addressing counterarguments, such as potential off-target effects in adenovirus delivery, future work should incorporate controls for viral immunogenicity. Ultimately, these directions could propel PCOS management toward mitochondria-focused therapies, fulfilling the need for fundamental ovarian repair.

Limitations of the study

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the scRNA-seq dataset used in this study was not generated directly from PCOS patient follicles and primarily represented the preovulatory follicular stage. Therefore, although it provided a biologically relevant framework for analyzing the follicular microenvironment, it may not fully reflect stage-specific or disease-specific heterogeneity in PCOS. Second, the experimental validations were conducted mainly in KGN cells and DHEA-induced mouse models, which cannot completely recapitulate the complex metabolic, genetic, and environmental interactions observed in human PCOS. Additionally, the relatively limited sample size and incomplete clinical metadata in public datasets may have introduced residual confounding. Finally, although adenoviral LRPPRC overexpression demonstrated therapeutic potential in vivo, the long-term safety, off-target effects, and translational feasibility of mitochondria-targeted interventions require further investigation in large-scale clinical studies.

Experimental model and study participant details

Human

Publicly available transcriptomic datasets of granulosa cells from women with or without PCOS were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. All included samples were obtained from women undergoing IVF/ICSI treatment cycles at the time of oocyte retrieval after human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) triggering. No newly collected human samples were involved in this study.

Animals

Female specific pathogen-free (SPF) C57BL/6 mice, aged 6–8 weeks and weighing 20 ± 2 g, were obtained from the Shanghai Jiao Tong University Laboratory Animal Center (ethical approval no. A2026065). Mice were housed in individually ventilated cages under controlled environmental conditions (22 ± 1 °C, 55 ± 5% humidity, 12 h light/dark cycle) with free access to sterilized chow and acidified water (pH 2.5–3.0). Mice were acclimatized for at least 1 week before experimentation. All animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee and conducted in accordance with institutional ethical guidelines.

To establish a PCOS-like mouse model, mice were randomly assigned to either a control (CON) group or a DHEA-induced PCOS group. Control mice received daily subcutaneous injections of 100 μL sterile sesame oil and standard chow. PCOS mice received daily subcutaneous injections of DHEA (6 mg/100 g body weight dissolved in 100 μL sesame oil) combined with a high-fat diet (60% kcal from fat) for 21 consecutive days.35,36 This regimen was selected based on established protocols that reproducibly induce hyperandrogenism, disrupted estrous cyclicity, polycystic ovarian morphology, and metabolic abnormalities.

For rescue experiments, mice were divided into four groups: CON, DHEA, DHEA + ADV-NC, and DHEA + ADV-LRPPRC. After the initial 21-day induction period, DHEA administration was continued every 3 days until day 56 to maintain the PCOS phenotype.35,36 On day 22, mice in the ADV-NC and ADV-LRPPRC groups underwent bilateral intraovarian injection of adenoviral vectors (10 μL per ovary). Adenoviral vectors were generated by OBiO Technology (Shanghai, China). The injection volume was optimized to ensure efficient ovarian transduction while avoiding capsular rupture or tissue overdistension.

Cell culture

Primary mouse ovarian granulosa cells (OGCs) were isolated from dissected ovarian tissue. Ovaries were minced and digested with hyaluronidase at 37 °C for 1 min. Digestion was terminated with fetal bovine serum (FBS), followed by gentle trituration and filtration through a 200-mesh cell strainer. Cells were washed with PBS and centrifuged at 300 × g for 5 min at 10 °C. The pellet was resuspended in DMEM/Ham’s F-12 medium and cultured at 37 °C in a humidified incubator containing 5% CO2.40

The human granulosa-like tumor cell line KGN was obtained from Saibai Kang Biotechnology (China). Cells were maintained in DMEM/F12 medium supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin–streptomycin at 37 °C with 5% CO2. For passaging, cells were detached with 0.25% trypsin-EDTA and subcultured at 70–80% confluence.

To mimic the hyperandrogenic microenvironment of PCOS in vitro, KGN cells were treated with 500 nM DHT for 24 h. This concentration was selected based on previous studies demonstrating effective induction of PCOS-like phenotypes without overt cytotoxicity.

For stable LRPPRC knockdown, KGN cells were transfected with lentiviral vectors expressing shRNAs targeting LRPPRC or scrambled control shRNA using Lipofectamine 3000. Knockdown efficiency was validated by RT-qPCR and western blotting. For overexpression experiments, the full-length human LRPPRC coding sequence was cloned into the pLVX-IRES-Neo vector and transfected into KGN cells using the same protocol.

To induce metabolic stress and evaluate disulfidptosis, cells were cultured in either standard glucose-sufficient (+Glc) or glucose-free (-Glc) DMEM media for 24 h. Pharmacological inhibitors or agonists were added simultaneously where indicated, including Z-VAD-FMK (20 μM), deferoxamine (100 μM), necrostatin-1 (50 μM), BAY-876 (10 μM), and 2-mercaptoethanol (1 mM). Vehicle-treated cells served as controls.

Method details

Databases and bioinformatic analyses

Single-cell RNA sequencing data were obtained from GEO (GSE189960). Bulk transcriptomic datasets used for diagnostic model development included GSE34526, GSE168404, and GSE193123. Independent validation datasets included GSE95728 and GSE137684. A curated set of 12 disulfidptosis-related genes was collected from previously published literature.15

Single-cell data processing was performed using Seurat. Cells with fewer than 200 detected genes, more than 7,500 detected genes, mitochondrial gene content >15%, ribosomal gene content <3%, or hemoglobin gene content >0.1% were excluded. Batch effects were corrected using Harmony. PCA and UMAP dimensionality reduction were subsequently performed, followed by clustering and manual annotation. Differentially expressed genes were identified using FindAllMarkers.

Hallmark gene sets were downloaded from MSigDB. GSVA was used to calculate pathway enrichment scores and disulfidptosis activity scores. Correlation analyses between disulfidptosis scores and pathway signatures were visualized using heatmaps.

For bulk transcriptomic analyses, datasets were normalized and batch-corrected using limma and sva. Differential expression analysis was conducted using limma with adjusted p < 0.05 as the significance threshold. Functional enrichment analyses, including GO and KEGG analyses, were conducted using clusterProfiler.

Machine learning and diagnostic model construction

A three-stage feature selection framework integrated complementary methods: least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) as an embedded approach, random forest for tree-based importance ranking, and support vector machine-recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE) as a wrapper method. LASSO was implemented using glmnet, random forest using randomForest, and SVM-RFE using kernlab.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)

The Reactome database was used as the pathway source for GSEA. Enrichment of predefined Reactome pathways was statistically evaluated in rank-ordered gene lists derived from diagnostic model genes to identify key mechanisms associated with biological phenotypes, such as disease states or drug perturbations. Single-gene Reactome GSEA was conducted using clusterProfiler.

Mendelian randomization analysis

To evaluate potential causal effects of gene expression on PCOS, two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) analyses were performed using expression quantitative trait loci (eQTLs) for four key genes (LRPPRC, NCKAP1, NDUFS1, OXSM) as instrumental variables (IVs) for the exposure, with summary statistics from a large PCOS genome-wide association study (GWAS) as the outcome. Specifically, the eQTL summary statistics were derived from the eQTLGen Consortium, primarily reflecting whole-blood gene expression. This large-scale dataset was selected to maximize statistical power and variant coverage. The TwoSampleMR package was employed, with the harmonise_data function used to align effect alleles and sizes. MR inference incorporated five methods: MR-Egger,41 weighted median,38 inverse-variance weighted (IVW),42 simple mode,43 and weighted mode.44

Hematoxylin–eosin (H&E) staining

As previously described,45,46 to evaluate ovarian morphology and follicular development in the in vivo models, whole ovaries from six mice per group (n = 6) were collected, dehydrated, embedded in paraffin, sectioned, deparaffinized in xylene, and rehydrated through graded ethanol to water. Sections were stained with hematoxylin (C0105M, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) for 5 min, rinsed, differentiated in 1% acid alcohol, counterstained with eosin for 15 s, and washed. The sections were then dehydrated through graded ethanol, cleared in xylene, and mounted with neutral resin (RM2235, Leica, Shanghai, China). Ovarian morphology was examined using a Nikon Eclipse Ci-L light microscope (Nikon, Japan).

Multiplex immunofluorescence

To assess the localization and expression levels of specific proteins, multiplex immunofluorescence (IF) with tyramide signal amplification (TSA) was performed on paraffin-embedded mouse ovarian tissue sections. For tissue samples, paraffin sections were baked at 62 °C for 1 h, deparaffinized in xylene (3 × 20 min), rehydrated through graded ethanol (absolute ethanol 2 × 1 min, 95% ethanol 2 × 1 min, 70% ethanol 1 min), and rinsed. Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked with 3% H2O2 (100 μL per slide) for 10 min at room temperature (RT), followed by washing with PBS (3 × 5 min). Antigen retrieval was performed in 1 mM Tris-EDTA buffer (pH 9.0) using a boiling water bath for 15 min, followed by a 15-min warm hold, cooling, and washing. Sections were blocked with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) for 20 min, followed by overnight incubation with the first primary antibody (100 μL per slide) at 4 °C. After PBS washes, an HRP-conjugated secondary antibody (100 μL per slide) was applied for 30 min at 37 °C. TSA amplification was performed with tyramide-488 (100 μL per slide) for 10–30 min at RT, followed by thorough washing. The process was repeated for the second primary antibody (overnight at 4 °C), with identical washing and secondary antibody steps, using tyramide-Cy3 for amplification. After final PBS washes (3 × 5 min), slides were mounted with anti-fade medium containing 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI). Third and fourth rounds followed the same procedure, using tyramide-594 and tyramide-Cy5, respectively. Images were acquired using a Nikon Eclipse Ci-L epifluorescence microscope equipped with a DS-Fi2 system (Nikon, Japan).

F-actin fluorescence staining

F-actin was visualized using an iFluor 555-phalloidin staining kit (Yeasen Biotechnology, Shanghai, China). Following designated treatments, KGN cells grown on coverslips were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min, permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 for 10 min, and blocked with 5% BSA for 1 h at room temperature. Cells were then incubated with iFluor 555-conjugated phalloidin for 45 min in the dark. After PBS washing, coverslips were mounted with DAPI-containing anti-fade medium. Images were acquired using a Nikon Eclipse Ci-L epifluorescence microscope equipped with a DS-Fi2 system (Nikon, Japan).

CCK-8 assay

Cell proliferation and viability of the treated KGN granulosa cell lines were assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8. Cells were seeded into 96-well plates at 100 μL per well and pre-incubated for 24 h at 37 °C with 5% CO2. Immediately before measurement, 10 μL of CCK-8 solution was added to each well and incubated for 1 h in the dark. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm.

Flow cytometry

To evaluate the impact of gene expression interventions on cell death, apoptosis of the KGN granulosa cells was assessed using Annexin V-FITC/propidium iodide (PI) staining with the Annexin V-FITC/PI Apoptosis Detection Kit. Briefly, 1 × 105 to 5 × 105 cells were collected. Suspension cells were pelleted by centrifugation at 300 × g for 5 min at 4 °C. Adherent cells were detached using EDTA-free trypsin, combined with floating cells, and pelleted. Cells were washed twice with cold PBS (300 × g, 4 °C, 5 min), resuspended in 100 μL of 1× Binding Buffer, stained with 5 μL Annexin V-FITC and 10 μL PI, gently mixed, and incubated for 10–15 min at RT in the dark. After adding 400 μL of 1× Binding Buffer, samples were placed on ice, protected from light, and analyzed by flow cytometry within 1 h.

ELISA

Serum levels of estradiol (E2), anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH), testosterone (T), luteinizing hormone (LH), and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) were quantified using commercial enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits according to the manufacturers’ protocols. Assay plates included standards, samples, and blanks. Optical density was measured at 450 nm within 15 min after reaction termination using a BIO-RAD 550 microplate reader (USA), with blanks for zeroing.

NADPH assay

Intracellular NADPH levels were determined using a NADP+/NADPH Assay Kit (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Briefly, treated KGN cells were harvested, washed with cold PBS, and lysed in the provided extraction buffer. Following centrifugation at 12,000 × g for 10 min at 4°C, the supernatants were collected. The cell extracts and NADPH standard solutions were mixed with the chromogenic working reagent and incubated at 37°C for 30 min. Absorbance was subsequently measured at 450 nm. Absolute NADPH concentrations were calculated based on the standard curve and normalized to the total cell count.

Western blotting

To quantify protein expression levels, total protein was extracted from cultured KGN cell lines and isolated primary mouse ovarian granulosa cells (OGCs). As previously described,45,46 proteins were extracted by washing cell pellets (∼20 mg) with ice-cold PBS, lysing in 60 μL radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) buffer, and centrifuging at 10,000–14,000 × g for 3–5 min at 4 °C to collect the supernatants. Protein concentrations were determined using the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) assay based on absorbance at 562 nm and standard curves. Samples were denatured by boiling in loading buffer at 100 °C for 10 min, separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) using precast gels with a dual-color prestained marker (80 V for 30 min, followed by 120 V for 1.5 h), and transferred to methanol-activated polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes (200 mA for 2 h) using Western blot (WB) transfer pads. Membranes were blocked with a rapid protein-free blocker for 10 min, incubated with anti-LRPPRC primary antibody or anti-SLC7A11 primary antibody overnight at 4 °C, and then with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG (H&L) secondary antibody for 1 h at RT. Signals were detected using enhanced chemiluminescence and imaged with a Tanon 4600 system or a BIO-RAD gel imager (Model 2500, USA).

RT-qPCR

To measure mRNA transcription levels, total RNA was extracted from KGN cell lines and primary mouse OGCs. As previously described,47 total RNA was isolated by lysing 10–20 mg cell samples in 500 μL lysis buffer, vortexing for 30 s, incubating for 5 min at RT, and centrifuging at 12,000 rpm for 5 min. Supernatants were passed through HyperPure gDNA-Filter Columns and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 5 min to remove genomic DNA. Anhydrous ethanol (0.5 volumes) was added to the supernatants and mixed. The mixtures were loaded onto HyperPure RNA Columns, washed with 500 μL Wash Buffer A, and then twice with 500 μL Wash Buffer B (each wash at 12,000 rpm for 30 s at RT). After a 2-min empty spin to remove residual ethanol, RNA was eluted with 50–100 μL elution water.

For reverse transcription, 20 μL reactions were assembled using the HyperScript III RT SuperMix for qPCR kit containing 1 ng to 5 μg total RNA, 4 μL 5× SuperMix, and RNase-free water. Reactions were incubated at 37 °C for 15 min and heat-inactivated at 85 °C for 5 s qPCR was performed using 2× S6 Universal SYBR qPCR Mix in 20 μL reactions containing 10 μL 2× SYBR mix, 2 μL each of 10 μM forward and reverse primers, 2 μL cDNA, and 4 μL ddH2O on a QuantStudio 6 Flex system. The cycling conditions were 95 °C for 30 s, followed by 40–45 cycles of 95 °C for 3–10 s and 60 °C for 10–30 s, and melt curve analysis. Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) served as the reference gene, and relative expression levels were calculated using the 2ˆ{-ΔΔC_T}method. Primer sequences are provided in Table S1.

Estrous cycle monitoring

Vaginal exfoliated cells were collected daily between 10:00 and 10:30 a.m. for 14 consecutive days to monitor a full estrous cycle, following previously established protocols.48 A sterile cotton swab moistened with saline was gently inserted into the vagina to collect secretions, which were smeared evenly onto clean slides and air-dried. Slides were stained with crystal violet for 1–2 min, rinsed with distilled water, dried, and examined under a light microscope to determine estrous stages based on cell types and morphology.

Quantification and statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9 and SPSS Statistics 29. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Normality was evaluated using the Shapiro–Wilk test. For normally distributed data, comparisons between two groups were performed using unpaired Student’s t-tests, whereas comparisons among multiple groups were analyzed using one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test. For non-normally distributed data, Mann–Whitney U tests or Kruskal–Wallis tests followed by Dunn’s multiple comparison tests were used. A two-sided p value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Biological replicate numbers (n values), statistical tests, and significance levels are indicated in the corresponding figure legends.

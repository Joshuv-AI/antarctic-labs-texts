Comparative study of neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy, chemotherapy, and chemoimmunotherapy for locally advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma
Authors: Likun Liu, Junhong Liu, Yu Rong, Shuai Zhang, Yan Ding, Weili Zhang, Lan Wang, Junfeng Liu, Shuchai Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study aimed to compare the efficacies of neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (NCRT), chemotherapy (NCT), and chemoimmunotherapy (NCIT) in patients with locally advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (LAESCC). The primary objective was to assess the overall survival (OS) among the three treatment groups.MethodsA retrospective cohort of 625 patients treated at the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University between 2016 and 2022 was analyzed. Patients received NCRT, NCT, or NCIT followed by radical esophagectomy. To adjust for confounding factors, inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) was employed. Chi-square tests, Fisher’s exact tests, and multivariate Cox survival analyses were used to assess prognostic factors and survival outcomes.ResultsBefore IPTW, significant differences in baseline characteristics were observed among the three groups. After IPTW adjustment, differences were mitigated, allowing for a balanced comparison. NCIT demonstrated superior 4-year OS rate (91.4%) compared with NCRT (60.4%) and NCT (62.5%). The addition of adjuvant therapy further improved survival outcomes.ConclusionsNCIT may offer superior survival benefits over NCRT and NCT in patients with LAESCC, particularly when combined with adjuvant chemoimmunotherapy (ACIT). These findings underscore the potential of immunotherapy in multimodal treatment of LAESCC and warrant further investigation in prospective clinical trials.

Background

Squamous cell carcinoma is the dominant pathological type of esophageal cancer in China, accounting for 95% of all malignancy [1]. Although its incidence and mortality rates have gradually decreased in recent decades, most patients present with regional or distant metastases at diagnosis. Surgery remains the first-line treatment for patients with locally advanced esophageal cancer (LAEC). The long-term survival of patients undergoing surgery alone is disappointing, with a 5-year survival rate of less than 30% [2]. Despite the need for multimodal therapies in these patients, there are still numerous controversies regarding treatment approaches.

Over the past 30 years, a series of studies on resectable LAECs has been conducted in Japan. One randomized phase III trial (JCOG9204) showed that postoperative chemotherapy using cisplatin plus 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) (CF) was superior to surgery alone in terms of disease-free survival (DFS) [3]. Subsequently, in another randomized phase III trial (JCOG9907), the survival benefit of preoperative chemotherapy with CF was found to be superior to that of postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy (ACT) with the same regimen [4]. However, neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (NCRT) has been demonstrated to provide a survival benefit over surgery alone in the CROSS trial [5, 6] and the NEOCRTEC 5010 trial [7, 8] for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC). When comparing NCRT and NCT, there were no significant differences in overall survival (OS) between the two treatment models in the NeoRes trial [9] and the CMISG1701 trial [10, 11]. Furthermore, in the JCOG1109 trial, systemic control was reinforced by platinum-based three-drug NCT, which was found to be superior to platinum-based two-drug NCT in terms of DFS and OS [12]. Thus, the standards of care for LAEC are NCRT in Western countries [13], NCRT or NCT in China [14], and docetaxel, cisplatin, and fluorouracil triplet NCT in Japan [15, 16]. In recent years, research on neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy (NCIT) has shown major pathological response (MPR) and pathological complete response (pCR) rates comparable to those of NCRT; however, its long-term efficacy remains to be observed [17, 18]. As a result, the optimal model of care for LAESCC and whether adjuvant therapy is necessary after preoperative neoadjuvant therapy remain unknown. Although several retrospective clinical studies have reported the results of adjuvant therapy following neoadjuvant therapy and surgery, these results are often contradictory.

To address these important clinical questions, we conducted a single-center retrospective cohort study to compare the roles of NCRT, NCT, and NCIT in patients with LAESCC. Additionally, a subgroup analysis was performed to determine whether adjuvant therapy was beneficial for patients who received induction therapy and to optimize perioperative therapy protocols.

Materials and methods

Patients

A total of 5,426 patients who underwent radical esophagectomy for cancer at the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University between January 2016 and December 2022 were reviewed. Among them, 645 received preoperative neoadjuvant therapy and 625 patients who met the inclusion criteria were recruited. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University, and esophageal cancers were restaged according to the eighth edition of the AJCC/UICC TNM staging classification [19]. The inclusion criteria were as follows: a pathological diagnosis of squamous cell carcinoma of the esophageal; age range of 18 to 75 years; clinical staging of T1N1-3M0 or T2-4aN0-3M0; receipt of NCRT, NCT, or NCIT; and surgical treatment performed 4–8 weeks after neoadjuvant therapy. Complete medical records, including detailed information before and after the treatment, were recorded. The exclusion criteria included surgical postponement beyond 8 weeks; history of other malignant tumors; severe comorbidities such as heart, lung, liver, or kidney diseases; incomplete treatment; high ECOG score (greater than 2); pregnancy or lactation; psychiatric disorders; hematological abnormalities; and allergy history to components of neoadjuvant therapy. Among the 625 patients who met the inclusion criteria, 50 received NCRT, 444 received NCT, and 131 received NCIT.

Diagnosis

Upper gastrointestinal endoscopy was performed to assess esophageal strictures caused by cancer, lesion length, and risk of perforation. Gastroscopy helped to observe the length and circumferential invasion of the tumor and determine the pathological type through biopsy. Computed tomography (CT) was used to determine the tumor location, size, and its relationship with the surrounding structure as well as the presence of enlarged lymph nodes. B-mode ultrasonography was performed to detect enlarged supraclavicular lymph nodes, and if the short diameter of the lymph nodes exceeded 0.8 cm, needle biopsy was used to exclude metastasis. For early cases, endoscopic ultrasonography was often performed to distinguish them from locally advanced cases; for cases suspected of distant metastasis, PET-CT examination was often conducted for confirmation.

Preoperative neoadjuvant therapy

The specific indications for neoadjuvant therapy in this study were as follows: Disease stage: Neoadjuvant therapy was typically considered for patients with LAESCC, which was defined as clinical staging of T1N1-3M0 or T2-4aN0-3M0 according to the eighth edition of the AJCC/UICC TNM staging classification. Patient’s condition and willingness: The decision to administer neoadjuvant therapy also took into account the patients’ overall health status and their willingness to undergo such treatment. Patients must have an ECOG performance status of 0–1 or a KPS score ≥ 80%, along with meeting key organ function criteria, to qualify for preoperative neoadjuvant therapy. Doctor’s preference: The treating physician’s judgment and preference played a role in selecting the type of neoadjuvant therapy (NCRT, NCT, or NCIT) based on the patients’ specific circumstances and the physician’s experience and recommendations.

Neoadjuvant chemotherapy regimens were selected according to the NCCN guidelines, and typically included paclitaxel and platinum (TP), fluorouracil and platinum (FP), or docetaxel and platinum (DP). The TP regimen involved paclitaxel at a dose of 175 mg/m2 (day 1) and cisplatin at a dose of 75 mg/m2 (day 1) administered intravenously, with a repeat cycle every 3 weeks. The FP regimen included 5-fluorouracil at a dose of 750 mg/m2 (days 1–5) and cisplatin at a dose of 75 mg/m2 (day 1), with a repeat cycle every 3 weeks. The DP regimen included cisplatin at a dose of 75 mg/m2 (day 1) and docetaxel at 75 mg/m2 (day 1), which was repeated every 3 weeks. Esophagectomy was performed 4–8 weeks after NCT.

Neoadjuvant concurrent chemoradiotherapy involved radiation up to a total dose of 40–50.4 Gy, delivered in 1.8–2.0 Gy fractions over five days each week, starting on day 1 of the first chemotherapy cycle and ending at the completion of the second chemotherapy cycle. The intensity-modulated radiotherapy technique was used in all patients. Esophagectomy was scheduled within 4–8 weeks after NCRT.

Neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy included the chemotherapy component as described previously, with the addition of anti-PD-1 antibodies, such as toripalimab, tislelizumab, or sintilimab. Esophagectomy was scheduled 4–8 weeks after the completion of 2–3 cycles of NCIT.

Surgery

All patients underwent double-lumen intubation and intravenous-inhalation compound general anesthesia. The esophagectomy approach was either left or right thoracotomy, depending on the location of the cancer. The tubed stomach was used as an esophageal substitute. For lower thoracic esophageal cancer, radical esophagectomy, dissection of lymph nodes, mobilization of the stomach, and anastomosis in the thorax were all completed through left thoracotomy (Sweet operation). For cancers located in the middle third of the esophagus, the tubed stomach was constructed through a midline incision of the abdomen, followed by radical resection of the esophagus and lymph nodes. Anastomosis in the upper thorax was performed through a right thoracotomy (Ivor Lewis esophagectomy). For upper thoracic esophageal cancer, esophagogastrostomy was performed in the neck after Ivor Lewis esophagectomy (McKeown operation). Some Ivor Lewis and McKeown esophagectomies have been performed using minimally invasive techniques. We routinely perform two-field lymph node dissection in the thoracic and abdominal cavities.

Pathologic examination

After surgery, the surgeon grouped the lymph nodes and preserved the specimens in neutral formalin. Later, the pathologist thoroughly analyzed the specimens, focusing on tumor type, differentiation, tissue reaction, tumor invasion, surgical margin involvement, nerve and vessel involvement, and lymph node metastasis. This study aimed to establish an accurate ypTNM staging system. Pathologists also noted pCR and MPR. pCR refers to the absence of tumor cells on pathological examination after neoadjuvant therapy. In contrast, MPR represents a significant reduction in tumor size after treatment to 10% or less of its pre-treatment volume.

Postoperative adjuvant therapy

Adjuvant therapy was chosen based on cancer stage, patient performance status, and personal preferences. Adjuvant chemoimmunotherapy was the most commonly used treatment, and typically involved paclitaxel, cisplatin, and PD-1 inhibitors. After completing two courses of adjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, the treatment was generally switched to immunotherapy alone, which was continued for more than 6 months. Commonly used PD-1 monoclonal antibodies included toripalimab (240 mg on day 1), tislelizumab (200 mg on day 1), and sintilimab (200 mg on day 1). The second most common option was adjuvant chemotherapy alone, typically using paclitaxel and cisplatin for 2–3 cycles. Currently, adjuvant chemoradiotherapy and radiotherapy were used less commonly.

Monitoring of adverse events

During the research timeline and continued for 30 days after the last treatment administration or until the start of a new cancer treatment regimen, constant vigilance was exercised to detect any adverse events among all participants. Adverse events were categorized using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), version 5.0, as established by the National Cancer Institute. Safety evaluations encompassed tracking all new-onset and severe adverse events and correlated them with the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA), version 24.0. Any adverse events that the researchers identified as linked to the study’s cancer-fighting medications were documented as treatment-related.

Follow-up study

The follow-up studies were conducted via telephone, email, outpatient clinic visits, and the hospital’s follow-up system. The follow-up included assessments of clinical symptoms and signs, CT scans, gastroscopy, and pathological biopsy to determine postoperative recurrence and/or metastasis. Follow-up assessments were made every six months until the date of death or September 15, 2024. Two consecutive losses to follow-up were defined as death and the date of death was recorded as the time from the first loss to follow-up. OS was defined as the time between the date of surgery and the date of death or last follow-up date. DFS was defined as the time from surgery to disease recurrence or death due to any cause. Given the extended follow-up, OS was selected as the primary endpoint for multivariate analysis.

Statistical analysis

The baseline characteristics, perioperative events, pathological results, and recurrent patterns and frequencies of the three groups were analyzed, with categorical variables reported as total numbers and percentages. Variables among the three groups were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. To minimize bias caused by confounding factors, the inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) method was used. Propensity scores were estimated via logistic regression incorporating 13 covariates: age, sex, smoking status, alcohol consumption, tumor location, surgical approach, clinical T stage, N stage, TNM stage, differentiation grade, neoadjuvant therapy (regimen and cycles), and adjuvant therapy. Unstable weights were calculated for each patient based on the estimated propensity scores. Survival curves were adjusted using the IPTW method, and weighted survival curves were generated using the adjusted survival function in R to compare survival outcomes between different treatment groups. After IPTW, P-values were calculated to assess the balance of baseline characteristics among the three weighted subgroups. A P-value more than 0.05 indicated that the baseline characteristics were balanced among the three groups. Kaplan-Meier and log-rank tests were conducted to estimate and compare OS. Variables with a P-value < 0.05 in univariate survival analysis were included in multivariate analysis to identify independent prognostic factors. Multivariate Cox survival analysis was used to identify independent risk factors for prognosis, whereas the Cox proportional hazards model was used to calculate hazard ratios (HR) and 95% confidence intervals (95%CI). After IPTW, Kaplan-Meier and log-rank tests were performed. R statistical software [(R version 4.4.4) for Windows (R Foundation, Vienna, Austria)] was used for statistical analysis, with a two-tailed P-value < 0.05, indicating statistical significance. The same methods were used to analyze the impact of additional adjuvant therapy and five different combinations of perioperative treatment modalities on the prognosis.

Discussion

This retrospective cohort study aimed to compare the efficacies of NCRT, NCT, and NCIT in patients with LAESCC. Our results suggest that NCIT may confer superior survival outcomes compared with NCRT and NCT, particularly when combined with adjuvant immunotherapy. However, this observation warrants further research.

The CROSS and NEOCRTEC 5010 trials have established NCRT as the standard of care for patients with LAESCC by demonstrating improved survival rates compared to surgery alone [3, 4]. However, our study indicates that integrating immunotherapy into NCT protocols yields promising results, with pCR and MPR comparable to NCRT as well as enhanced OS. These findings align with emerging evidence supporting the role of immunotherapy in esophageal cancer treatment [20, 21].

A consistent trend observed across studies is a higher rate of tumor regression and pCR associated with NCIT than with NCT. For instance, Jing et al. reported a significant advantage of NCIT over NCT in terms of TRG 0 (21.7% vs. 4.5%, P = 0.016) and pCR rates (17.0% vs. 2.1%, P = 0.035) [22]. Duan et al. reported similar pCR rates between NCIT and NCRT (25.6% vs. 27.5%, P = 0.716) [23]. The PALACE-1 trial demonstrated that the pCR rate was as high as 55.6% with the combination of NCRT and immunotherapy [17]. In the NICE study, the pCR rate for NCIT was 39.2% [18]. Although a higher pCR rate from NCT was not associated with OS in the JCOG 1109 trial [24] and CMISG1701 trial [10, 11], a higher MPR rate from NCIT was associated with lower recurrence and better survival in the NICE trial [25]. These findings suggest that NCIT may significantly affect the treatment landscape of LAESCC. Our study showed that NCIT achieved high rates of pCR and MPR, comparable to NCRT and superior to NCT, consistent with findings from studies by Qin et al. and Yu et al. [26, 27]. Therefore, NCIT also demonstrated promising pCR and MPR rates, indicating that the addition of an immune checkpoint inhibitor to chemotherapy may enhance pathological response.

The effect of NCIT on survival is noteworthy. Both Jing et al. and Duan et al. reported significantly improved DFS with NCIT [22, 23]. In a study by Jing et al., the 1- and 2-year DFS rates were markedly higher in the NCIT group (P = 0.001 and P = 0.046, respectively) [22]. Duan et al. also found a 2-year DFS rate of 70.0% for NCIT compared with 50.8% for NCRT (P = 0.023) [23]. To date, there have been no reports of long-term results for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma with neoadjuvant immunotherapy in randomized comparative clinical studies. The NICE trial revealed a 2-year OS rate of 78.1% and 2-year DFS of 67.9% [25]. In the present study, the 4-year OS rates were 60.4% for NCRT and 91.4% for NCIT (P = 0.005). These results imply that NCIT could offer a survival advantage by potentially reducing the risk of disease recurrence, as the frequency of recurrence and metastasis was the lowest in the NCIT group.

The safety profiles of different neoadjuvant therapies are of paramount importance. Our study showed that although all treatments were generally well-tolerated, there were differences in the incidence of certain adverse events. For example, leukopenia was more common with NCRT, consistent with its known side effect profile [10]. However, the incidence of anastomotic leaks and total complications did not differ significantly between the treatment groups, suggesting that neoadjuvant therapy did not adversely affect postoperative outcomes. Although pneumonia was more common in patients who underwent NCRT in this study, it was controllable and did not result in death.

However, the role of adjuvant therapy following neoadjuvant treatment and surgery remains controversial. Some retrospective studies have suggested a survival benefit with adjuvant therapy [28], while others, including a recent meta-analysis, have not. In our study, adjuvant therapy tended to improve survival, particularly in patients who did not achieve MPR after neoadjuvant therapy. This aligns with the emerging view that adjuvant therapy may benefit selected patients with residual disease after neoadjuvant therapy. Moreover, our study found that the combination of NCIT with ACIT resulted in the highest 4-year survival rate among all the treatment regimens evaluated. This finding suggests that continuation of immunotherapy postoperatively may provide an additional survival benefit, which is in line with recent research highlighting the importance of personalized treatment strategies and the potential for immunotherapy in the adjuvant setting [29]. However, the timing and duration of adjuvant immunotherapy remains unclear. While the CheckMate 577 trial administered nivolumab for up to one year post-surgery [29], the optimal treatment duration for adjuvant immunotherapy in esophageal cancer remains to be determined. Our data suggest that there might be a role for extended treatment beyond the traditional one-year mark, given the potential for long-term immune memory development. The optimal timing, sequencing, and selection of patients for adjuvant immunotherapy remain areas requiring further investigation.

Although our study provides valuable insights, it has some limitations. The retrospective nature of the study and the heterogeneity of the treatment regimens could introduce bias. Additionally, the relatively short follow-up period may not fully capture long-term survival outcomes. There is an absence of relevant biomarkers capable of predicting the efficacy of immunotherapy for esophageal cancer. Despite these limitations, our study’s findings are strengthened by the large sample size and use of IPTW analysis to balance the baseline characteristics between the treatment groups.

In conclusion, our study suggests that NCIT may offer a survival advantage over NCRT and NCT in neoadjuvant treatment of LAESCC, particularly when combined with ACIT. These findings underscore the need for prospective clinical trials to further evaluate the role of NCIT and to define the optimal integration of immunotherapy into multimodality treatment strategies for LAESCC.

Gestational choriocarcinoma presenting with hemorrhagic venous infarct and multiple lung metastases: A case report
Authors: Bakar Ali Adam, Yahye Garad Mohamed, Mohamed Sheikh Hassan, Nor Osman Sidow, Mohamed osman omar jeele, Abdulkadir Ahmed Mohamed, Mohamed Farah Osman Hidig, Abdiwahid Ahmed Ibrahim, Abdikadir Mohamed Dirie, Ahmed Issak Hussein, Mohamed Omar Hassan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Choriocarcinoma is a rare and aggressive form of gestational trophoblastic disease with a high potential for metastasis. We report the case of a 31-year-old female who presented with severe headaches, left-sided weakness, and speech difficulties. Her past medical history included a molar pregnancy, and she had elevated β-hCG levels of 200,000 mIU/mL. Imaging revealed a right frontoparietal hemorrhagic venous infarct and multiple lung metastases. Based on these findings and the patient's clinical history, a diagnosis of gestational choriocarcinoma with multiple metastases was confirmed. This case highlights the importance of considering choriocarcinoma in the differential diagnosis of young women presenting with neurological symptoms and a history of gestational trophoblastic disease. Early recognition and prompt treatment are crucial to improving outcomes in such cases.

Introduction

Gestational choriocarcinoma is a highly malignant form of gestational trophoblastic neoplasia (GTN) that arises from trophoblastic tissue after any type of gestational event, including molar pregnancies, normal pregnancies, and spontaneous abortions. Characterized by rapid growth and aggressive metastatic potential, choriocarcinoma commonly spreads to the lungs and brain via hematogenous routes [1,2]. Despite its rarity, choriocarcinoma is clinically significant due to its high cure rate when diagnosed early and treated appropriately with chemotherapy [3].

Cerebral venous thrombosis (CVT) is an uncommon but serious condition involving the thrombosis of the cerebral veins and sinuses. CVT accounts for less than 1% of all strokes and presents with a wide range of neurological symptoms, including headache, focal neurological deficits, seizures, and altered mental status [4,5]. The pathophysiology of CVT involves the obstruction of venous outflow, leading to increased intracranial pressure, cerebral edema, and potential hemorrhagic infarctions [6].

The coexistence of choriocarcinoma with CVT is exceedingly rare and presents significant diagnostic and therapeutic challenges. The symptoms of CVT can easily mimic those of other more common neurological conditions, often leading to delays in diagnosis [7]. Furthermore, the hypercoagulable state associated with malignancies such as choriocarcinoma predisposes patients to thrombotic events, complicating the clinical picture [8].

This case report discusses a unique presentation of choriocarcinoma in a 31-year-old female who initially presented with symptoms suggestive of a cerebrovascular event. The patient's history of a molar pregnancy 4 years prior, followed by amenorrhea, raised suspicion for an underlying gynecological pathology.

By sharing this case, we aim to contribute to the existing literature and raise awareness among the clinicians on the diverse clinical manifestations of choriocarcinoma.

Case presentation

A 31-year-old married female presented to the emergency department with a sudden onset of left-sided weakness that had persisted for 4 days. The patient had been in her usual state of health until 4 days prior to admission when she began experiencing severe headaches, described as the worst headache of her life. The headache was diffuse and bilateral, disturbing her sleep at night. She also experienced dizziness and tinnitus. Four days before admission, she awoke with weakness in her left arm and leg, accompanied by difficulty speaking. The worsening headache prompted her visit to the hospital.

Her past medical history is notable for a complete molar pregnancy managed with suction curettage 4 years ago. There is no significant family history of cancers or thrombotic disorders.

Neurological examination revealed expressive aphasia with intact cranial nerves. Motor examination showed left-sided hemiparesis with muscle strength graded at 2/5 in the left upper and lower extremities. Hyperreflexia was noted on the left side, and reduced sensation was observed on the left side. Cerebellar examination could not be adequately assessed due to weakness and aphasia. All other system examinations were unremarkable.

Laboratory tests including full blood count, Liver function test, renal function test with electrolytes, thyroid function test and coagulation were requested with normal results.

Notably, her β-hCG was significantly elevated at 200,000 mIU/mL.

The differential diagnoses considered in this case included ischemic stroke, intracranial hemorrhage, and cerebral venous thrombosis, given the patient's neurological symptoms and imaging findings. However, the presence of a history of molar pregnancy and elevated β-hCG levels raised concerns about the possibility of choriocarcinoma, a rare but highly malignant tumor originating from trophoblastic tissue.

The definitive diagnosis was considered gestational choriocarcinoma presenting with multiple lung metastases and cerebral cortical venous thrombosis leading a hemorrhagic venous infarct.

Initial stabilization included anticoagulation with low molecular weight heparin (enoxaparin) at a dose of 1 mg/kg subcutaneously twice daily, intravenous hydration to maintain adequate cerebral perfusion, and ondansetron for nausea and vomiting.

Definitive treatment involved initiating chemotherapy with methotrexate according to choriocarcinoma treatment protocols. Supportive care for stroke symptoms included physical therapy and speech therapy.

Discussion

Choriocarcinoma is a rare and aggressive form of gestational trophoblastic disease that can present with diverse clinical manifestations, making its diagnosis and management challenging. This case report highlights a unique presentation of choriocarcinoma in a 31-year-old female with a history of molar pregnancy, who initially presented with symptoms suggestive of a cerebrovascular event. The patient's clinical course underscores the importance of considering choriocarcinoma in the differential diagnosis of young women presenting with atypical neurological symptoms, especially in the setting of a history of gestational trophoblastic disease.

The literature on choriocarcinoma encompasses a wide range of clinical scenarios, including rare presentations and unusual sites of metastasis. Studies have reported cases of choriocarcinoma presenting with spontaneous acute subdural hematoma [9], uterine serosal metastasis mimicking ruptured ectopic pregnancy [10], and even primary extragenital choriocarcinoma in the posterior mediastinum in a male adult [11]. These cases emphasize the diverse and sometimes unexpected ways in which choriocarcinoma can manifest, necessitating a high index of suspicion and a comprehensive diagnostic approach.

In this case, the patient's sudden onset of severe headaches, left-sided weakness, and speech difficulties prompted her visit to the emergency department. The initial presentation mimicked a cerebrovascular event, leading to a differential diagnosis that included ischemic stroke, intracranial hemorrhage, and cerebral venous thrombosis (CVT). The presence of a history of molar pregnancy and elevated β-hCG levels raised concerns about the possibility of choriocarcinoma, a highly malignant tumor originating from trophoblastic tissue. Imaging studies, included CT scans, and pelvic ultrasound, revealed definitive diagnosis of choriocarcinoma with CVT, hemorrhagic venous infarct and lung metastases.

The management of this patient involved a multidisciplinary approach. Initial stabilization included anticoagulation therapy and intravenous hydration to address the CVT and maintain cerebral perfusion. Definitive treatment involved chemotherapy with methotrexate, targeting the choriocarcinoma. Close monitoring of β-hCG levels and serial imaging studies were essential for assessing treatment response and disease progression, aligning with recommendations from the literature [12].

The prognosis of choriocarcinoma can vary depending on factors such as the extent of metastasis, response to treatment, and timely initiation of therapy. Studies have reported cases of choriocarcinoma metastasizing to unusual sites such as the skin [13], colon [14], and pituitary gland [15], underscoring the aggressive nature of this malignancy and the importance of prompt diagnosis and intervention. Our case involved a late presentation of gestational choriocarcinoma with metastasis to the lung and cerebral venous thrombosis (CVT) resulting in venous hemorrhagic infarction. We initiated anticoagulation therapy for CVT and began methotrexate treatment for choriocarcinoma. The patient was referred to an oncology center for chemotherapy with regular monitoring of beta hCG levels.

Conclusion

This case report underscores the diagnostic challenges associated with choriocarcinoma, a rare and aggressive form of gestational trophoblastic disease. The unique presentation of choriocarcinoma with lung metastasis and cerebral cortical venous thrombosis in a young woman highlights the importance of considering this malignancy in the differential diagnosis of atypical neurological symptoms, especially in patients with a history of gestational trophoblastic disease.

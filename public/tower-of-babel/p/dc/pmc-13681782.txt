Predictive value of cerebral doppler resistive index in premature infants with hemodynamically significant patent ductus arteriosus
Authors: Burak Ceran, Evrim Alyamaç Dizdar, Rana Beyoğlu, Nahide Kutbay Altuğ, Esin Okman, Davut Bozkaya, Mustafa Şenol Akın, Fatma Nur Sari
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo evaluate cerebral Doppler ultrasonography parameters for predicting hemodynamically significant patent ductus arteriosus (hsPDA) in very premature infants.MethodsThis prospective cohort study included preterm infants (gestational age [GA] ≤ 30 weeks) who were admitted to a tertiary neonatal intensive care unit. Blood flow velocities, resistive and pulsatility indices of anterior cerebral artery (ACA) were measured at the first 24 and 72 h of life using transcranial Doppler ultrasound. The pediatric cardiologist performed echocardiographic evaluations of all infants at 72 h postnatally.ResultsA total of 77 infants (27 and 50 in the hsPDA and non-hsPDA groups) were included. Mean GA was 27.9 ± 2 and 28.6 ± 1.6 weeks, and mean birth weight was 1056 ± 248 and 1106 ± 269 g in the hsPDA and non-hsPDA groups, respectively; (p = 0.094, p = 0.46). Infants in the hsPDA group had significantly higher pulsatility index and resistive index (RI) than those in the non-hsPDA group at 24 h (p < 0.001 and p < 0.001, respectively). Multivariate analysis revealed that ACA RI above 0.75 at 24 h was an independent predictor for hsPDA.ConclusionACA RI measurement may have a potential role, in combination with other clinical parameters, in the early prediction of hsPDA in premature infants; however, further studies are required to validate these findings.Keywords: Premature, Patent ductus arteriosus, Cranial Doppler ultrasonography, Anterior cerebral artery

Introduction

Point-of-care ultrasound (POCUS) has numerous advantages for neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) patients. In recent practice, the clinicians perform POCUS to answer a specific clinical query or attain a specific procedural goal [1].

The ductus arteriosus (DA) is a blood vessel connecting the pulmonary artery to the aorta; it remains open throughout fetal life, and its persistence after birth is known as the patent DA (PDA) [2]. In approximately 70% of infants < 30 weeks of gestational age (GA), it remains open at 1-month-old [3]. Symptoms associated with mesenteric, renal, and cerebral hypoperfusion and pulmonary edema due to pulmonary over circulation occur in two-thirds of infants with persistent PDA [3].

Cranial ultrasonography (US) is frequently performed in newborns using an open fontanel [4]. Transcranial Doppler US measures the resistive index (RI) of cerebral arteries and is a non-invasive, radiation-free, and safe bedside neuroimaging method that analyzes one component of cerebral blood flow (CBF) [5]. The RI is commonly measured in the anterior cerebral artery (ACA) of newborns admitted to the NICU in contemporary neonatal clinical practice. A PDA is the most common cause of an increased RI in premature infants [6].

Measurement of ACA RI has become a regular practice in the NICU because it is thought to signal brain perfusion, vascular compliance, and cerebral autoregulation capability [7]. Although the average value of ACA RI in newborns is known, these measurements were taken in a diverse sample of infants, with only a small number of very premature infants. In most cases, a single observation was recorded at several time points [6, 8]. Furthermore, there is little evidence of a link between ACA RI and the early period of hemodynamically significant PDA (hsPDA).

Since echocardiographic evaluation could be challenging at any time in many NICUs, this study aimed to identify easily applied cranial Doppler US parameters that allow for rapid, standard, and non-invasive prediction of hsPDA in premature infants.

Research design and methodology

Study design and patient population

This prospective cohort study was conducted at a perinatal center with a 150-bed NICU that delivers 12.000 infants annually. We assessed prospectively for 12 months (August 2021–August 2022) a cohort of premature infants scanned at the first 24 and 72 h of life.

Premature infants ≤ 30 weeks of GA were included in the study. Exclusion criteria included major congenital or genetic abnormalities, complex congenital heart disease other than small atrial or ventricular septal defects, central nervous system abnormalities, evidence of perinatal asphyxia, early-onset neonatal sepsis, infants with major complications including severe intraventricular hemorrhage, pharmacological sedation requirements, lack of parental consent, and death within the first 72 h of life. Intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH) was classified as mild/moderate when graded as 1 or 2 (unilateral or bilateral) and as severe when graded as 3 or 4 (unilateral or bilateral). To prevent mechanical confounding of cerebrovascular parameters, all infants who developed IVH Grade 2 or higher within the first 72 h of life were excluded from the study, regardless of the exact time of onset within this period (Fig. 1). Neither delayed cord clamping nor cord milking was performed in the study groups.

Flow chart of the study population

The following data were obtained: GA, birth weight (BW), gender, mode of delivery, 1- and 5-minute Apgar scores, antenatal steroid (ANS) use, preeclampsia, maternal chorioamnionitis, respiratory distress syndrome (RDS), inotrope requirement within the first 72 h, invasive mechanical ventilation, blood gas parameters, blood count parameters, and ACA Doppler US parameters. The clinical and demographic characteristics were compared between the hsPDA and non-hsPDA groups.

Written informed consent was obtained from the parents of each infant prior to participation. The study protocol was approved by the Zekai Tahir Burak Women’s Health Education and Research Hospital Education Ethics Committee (Approval No: 2019/104). All procedures were conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Cranial ultrasonographic examinations

A neonatologist experienced in neonatal neuroimaging performed transfontanelle US (Toshiba AplioT 300 fitted with a microconvex transducer with frequencies between 8 and 10 MHz; Canon Medical Systems) on all infants at 24 and 72 h of life. Doppler measurements were obtained from the ACA in the sagittal plane through the anterior fontanel at the beginning of the cranial US examination to avoid any potential impact of continuous fontanelle stimulation on intracranial hemodynamics. Using Doppler, peak systolic flow velocity (PSV), and end diastolic flow velocity (EDV) were measured for ACA. All measurements were performed twice for each infant, and the mean values were recorded. The RI was automatically calculated from the maximum velocity waveforms using the following formula: PSV-EDV/PSV. The PI was automatically calculated from the waveforms using the following formula: PSV-EDV/Time averaged mean velocity [9]. Changes in vital signs were recorded using continuous cardiorespiratory monitoring during the US.

The neonatologists who performed the US measurements (who were not involved in the clinical management or follow-up of the infants) were not fully blinded to all clinical information due to unavoidable exposure to respiratory support modalities and observable vital signs; however, efforts were made to minimize potential bias, and all images were recorded systematically. The images were transferred to a workstation, and a pediatric radiologist controlled each Doppler measurement’s cranial imaging and sonogram for image quality and appropriate caliper placement on the spectral waveform (Fig. 2).

Hemodynamically significant patent ductus arteriosus

The preterm infants were divided into hsPDA and non-hsPDA groups. Echocardiography (ECHO) evaluations of all infants were performed 72 h postnatally by a pediatric cardiologist. Hemodynamically significant patent ductus arteriosus was defined based on a combination of clinical and echocardiographic findings. Clinical features included murmur, hyperdynamic precordium, bounding preductal pulses, worsening respiratory status, wide pulse pressure, hypotension, and metabolic acidosis. Echocardiographic findings included an increased left atrium-to-aortic root (LA/Ao) ratio, cardiomegaly, left-to-right shunting, a large patent ductus arteriosus, and reversal of flow in postductal major arteries. Accordingly, hsPDA was diagnosed in the presence of clinical signs of PDA together with one or more of the following echocardiographic criteria: ductal diameter > 1.5 mm, LA/Ao ratio > 1.5, left-to-right shunting, end-diastolic flow reversal in the aorta, or impaired cardiac function. Two-dimensional color Doppler ECHO was performed using a GE Vivid 7 Pro system equipped with a 10 S transducer (GE Healthcare, Salt Lake City, UT, USA) [10, 11].

Statistical analysis

Statistical data was evaluated using the SPSS® software (version 31.0; IBM SPSS Statistics, IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY). An a priori power analysis for Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve comparison (area under the curve [AUC]difference of 0.15, α = 0.05, power = 0.80) determined that a minimum of 70 infants was required to detect a clinically meaningful discrimination ability of ACA RI for hsPDA.

Data distribution was assessed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Variables with normal distribution were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), whereas non-normally distributed variables were expressed as median with interquartile range (IQR). A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The patients’ demographic percentages and mean measures were compared between the two groups using the Student’s t-test, Mann-Whitney U test or chi-square test. Receiver operating characteristic analysis was used to calculate the optimal cut-off values, sensitivity, and specificity of Doppler US measurements to predict hsPDA. A multivariable logistic regression model was constructed using a simultaneous inclusion (enter) method to identify independent predictive factors for hsPDA. Variables were selected step by step based on predefined clinical relevance and their association with hsPDA in univariable analyses. Given the relatively limited sample size, particular attention was paid to the number of events per variable (EPV) to minimize the risk of overfitting. Accordingly, only clinically meaningful and statistically relevant predictors were retained in the final model. For all statistical analyses, a p-value < 0.05 was considered significant.

The intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was calculated using SPSS software to assess the intra-observer reliability of Doppler US measurements. A two-way mixed-effects model with absolute agreement was applied. ICC values were interpreted according to established guidelines as follows: values < 0.50 indicated poor reliability, 0.50–0.75 moderate reliability, 0.75–0.90 good reliability, and > 0.90 excellent reliability.

Discussion

In this study, we demonstrated that on the first day, the ACA RI and PI were higher in infants with hsPDA, and by the third day, a reduction was attained. Resistive index and PI may aid in predicting hsPDA risk and, ACA RI at 24 h has been found to be an early predictor of hsPDA.

To our knowledge, this study is among the first to evaluate the predictive value of cerebral Doppler measurements, particularly ACA indices, for hsPDA in low birth weight preterm infants.

The clinical proficiency of neonatologists in performing targeted neonatal ECHO and POCUS continues to advance. As neonatologist-performed ECHO becomes increasingly integrated into routine clinical practice, several units now implement these modalities concurrently or utilize aberrant cerebral Doppler patterns as an immediate clinical trigger for formal echocardiographic evaluation. This evolving diagnostic synergy underscores the significance of incorporating early Doppler-based markers into targeted hemodynamic screening strategies [12]. In parallel, recent studies have focused on the early prediction of hsPDA using structured clinical scoring systems applied within the first hours of life [2]. Given the morbidity associated with delayed recognition and management of hsPDA, early and accurate hemodynamic assessment is essential. In this context, our study aimed to identify a non-invasive, objective, and readily applicable ultrasonographic marker to support the early prediction of hsPDA in preterm infants at high risk.

The hemodynamic effects of a left-to-right ductal shunt on CBF remain incompletely understood [13]. While some studies suggest that hsPDA may adversely affect organ perfusion, including reduced diastolic flow in the middle cerebral artery (MCA) due to ductal steal [9, 14], others have reported no significant differences in cerebral Doppler indices between infants with and without hsPDA [6, 14]. Cerebral blood flow velocities and resistance indices exhibit dynamic changes with advancing postnatal age. In preterm neonates, an increase in CBF velocities and a concomitant decrease in resistance indices are characteristically observed during the first days of life. This physiological adaptation is closely associated with cardiorespiratory stabilization and the closure of the PDA [15]. Consistent with this adaptation process, our study demonstrated that PSV and EDV values were higher, while RI and PI values were lower at 72 h of life compared to 24 h.

However, the differences in cerebrovascular dynamics and autoregulation between 24 and 72 h should be interpreted cautiously. The first 72 h of life represent a critical and highly vulnerable transitional period for preterm infants. Particularly during the first 24 h, cerebral autoregulation is often immature, rendering the cerebral circulation pressure-passive; consequently, fluctuations in systemic blood pressure are directly transmitted to the CBF [16]. By 72 h, as the infant’s cardiovascular status stabilizes, the autoregulatory capacity tends to become more robust. Therefore, the variations we observed between the first and third days of life likely reflect the progressive ontogeny and stabilization of cerebrovascular hemodynamics during this transitional phase, rather than merely an absolute baseline difference.

Although cerebral Doppler parameters possess limited utility as standalone diagnostic modalities for hsPDA, they serve as valuable adjunctive screening markers to identify highly vulnerable infants warranting comprehensive echocardiographic evaluation, particularly when characterized by low or absent end-diastolic flow and an elevated RI or PI [17]. In our cohort, an ACA RI cut-off of > 0.75 at 24 h of life demonstrated moderate predictive validity for the subsequent development of hsPDA, yielding an AUC of 0.778 (sensitivity: 85.1%; specificity: 58%). This finding is highly congruent with preceding reports, such as Bravo et al., who identified an optimal RI threshold of 0.74 (sensitivity: 82%; specificity: 72%) for predicting moderate-to-large PDAs [18].

Nevertheless, the relatively low specificity observed in our study is attributable to the substantial overlap in RI values between the hsPDA and non-hsPDA cohorts. This overlap is consistently echoed in the existing literature; for instance, normative RI values are reported to range from 0.61 to 0.81, overlapping significantly with values linked to large PDAs (0.78 to 1.20) [14]. Similarly, in a cohort of preterm infants (> 29 weeks of gestation), Ecury-Goossen et al. reported ACA RI ranges of 0.78–0.87 in the hsPDA group and 0.70–0.81 in the non-hsPDA group, which closely mirror our own measured values. Furthermore, their multivariable analysis showed no significant association between resistive index and SNAPPE-II score, gestational age, or sex; however, the resistive index was significantly higher in infants with hsPDA compared to those without, specifically in the anterior cerebral artery (mean difference: 0.069; 95% CI: 0.018–0.120) [6]. Despite this statistically significant difference, the consistent overlap in absolute values suggests that elevated cerebral vascular resistance is not an exclusive manifestation of ductal shunting, but rather a multifactorial response to various early neonatal morbidities. A further methodological consideration relates to the timing of these assessments. While previous studies, including Ecury-Goossen et al. [6], often lack a detailed discussion regarding the exact temporal alignment between cerebral Doppler interrogations and definitive echocardiographic findings, we acknowledge this as a constraint in our own study. Specifically, while our predictive ACA Doppler measurements were acquired at 24 h of postnatal life, the formal echocardiographic diagnosis of hsPDA was established at 72 h. This temporal dissociation may inherently limit the ability to draw definitive causal inferences regarding the observed hemodynamic associations.

Several limitations should be considered. First, detailed hemodynamic and management-related variables, including fluid balance, respiratory severity indices, and vasoactive–inotropic support, were not systematically evaluated. As cerebral Doppler indices are influenced by multiple physiological factors—such as blood pressure, PaCO₂, intravascular volume status, cardiac output, and the maturity of cerebral autoregulation—these measurements likely reflect a complex physiological interaction rather than the isolated effect of PDA. In addition, although PDA predominantly affects systemic circulation, cerebral perfusion may remain relatively preserved due to compensatory increases in left ventricular output. Therefore, cerebral Doppler findings should be interpreted within the broader clinical and hemodynamic context [19, 20].

Furthermore, the absence of concurrent lower-body Doppler assessment limits the ability to determine whether cerebral findings adequately represent the systemic impact of PDA, as ductal shunting more prominently affects mesenteric and renal circulation.

The relatively small sample size, particularly the limited number of hsPDA cases included in the multivariable logistic regression analysis, may reduce model stability and increase the risk of overfitting. Accordingly, multivariable findings should be considered exploratory. Although ROC analyses reached statistical significance, the moderate discriminatory performance and relatively low specificity of the proposed cut-off values may limit their direct clinical applicability.

The exclusion of infants who developed IVH (Grade ≥ 2) within the first 72 h in order to obtain pure hemodynamic Doppler data carries a potential for selection bias, which limits the generalizability of the findings to the most severe hsPDA cases.

This study was conducted as a single-center investigation in a tertiary care setting and does not include continuous hemodynamic monitoring or long-term follow-up data for treated infants. Additionally, while transfontanelle Doppler is inherently operator-dependent, we addressed this by ensuring all assessments were performed by a single neonatologist with extensive neuroimaging expertise. The robustness of this approach was confirmed by an intraclass correlation analysis, which demonstrated excellent intra-observer reliability (ICC = 0.92; 95% CI: 0.88–0.95). Despite these limitations, the study possesses significant strengths, including its prospective design and rigorous serial neuromonitoring during the most vulnerable neonatal period. As one of the more comprehensive investigations into cerebral hemodynamics in preterm infants, this study provides valuable insights into non-invasive brain Doppler monitoring. Furthermore, our findings suggest that early cerebral Doppler parameters may serve as effective clinical triggers, facilitating timely cardiological evaluations for infants at risk of hsPDA.

In conclusion, early cerebral Doppler assessment, particularly ACA RI within the first 24 h of life, may provide clinically useful adjunctive information for identifying preterm infants at risk of hsPDA. However, these parameters should not be used in isolation and must be interpreted within the overall clinical and hemodynamic context. Echocardiography remains the gold standard for diagnosis. Given its relatively high sensitivity but limited specificity, ACA RI appears more suitable as a screening tool to support early risk stratification rather than as a definitive diagnostic marker. Future prospective studies incorporating comprehensive hemodynamic data and simultaneous cerebral and systemic assessments are warranted to further clarify its clinical utility.

Conclusion

Our findings suggest that cerebral artery Doppler US measurements may have a potential role, in combination with other parameters, in the early prediction of hsPDA; however, further studies are warranted to validate these results and to clarify their clinical applicability.

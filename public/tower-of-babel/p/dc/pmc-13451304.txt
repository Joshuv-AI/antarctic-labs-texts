High-quality draft genome of the Listeria monocytogenes, one more vaccine strain against livestock listeriosis
Authors: Valentina A. Feodorova, Maxim S. Lavrukhin, Sergey S. Zaitsev, Alexey Zaberezhny, Olga S. Larionova
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Foodborne listeriosis is one of the severe food-borne diseases affecting both animals and humans worldwide. No vaccine to control listeriosis in humans is available, while a relatively high level of hospitalization (up to 90%) and mortality (up to 20–30%). However, listeriosis is a vaccine-preventable veterinary disease in livestock, which serves as the main reservoirs and the principal source of this infection in humans. Here, we represent the high-quality draft genome of two variants of the second reference Listeria monocytogenes (Lm) strain, routinely attenuated from the ovine virulent Lm strain and used for decades as a live whole-cell veterinary vaccine ‘AUF’ (LWCV AUF). Overall, the genomes possessed 2,844/2,846 CDSs, a number of pseudogenes (92/88), 13 of 14 internalins, 6 LIPI-1 genes, 5 antibiotic resistance genes, 6 genes within ESAT-6 gene-cluster, associated with bacterial T7SS, a number of genes which provided Lm acid tolerance. Our work can be useful for the comparative analysis of factors involved in the protective activity and residual virulence of commercial and experimental vaccines against Lm-associated diseases.

Background & Summary

Foodborne listeriosis is a well-known infection, affecting both animals and humans, which is recognized by the World Health Organization (WHO) as one of the most important severe food-borne diseases1. Although listeriosis is considered as a relatively rare infection, since annually from 0.1 to 10 cases of listeriosis are registered worldwide, the related hospitalization rate could be estimated at more than 90% and the mortality of the invasive, systemic or encephalitic forms of the disease may be very high - up to 20–30%1–4. The disease manifestations in healthy people include febrile listerial gastroenteritis, diarrhea, fever, headache, myalgia (muscle pain), the incubation period being relatively short (a few days). Outbreaks of this form of noninvasive listeriosis are generally related to the ingestion of foods containing high doses of pathogenic listeria. The second form of the disease known as invasive listeriosis is a more severe course of the infection, especially for certain high-risk groups of the population, such as: pregnant women, newborns, elderly and immunocompromised people. The clinical manifestations include fever, septicemia, meningitis or meningoencephalitis, encephalitis, intrauterine or cervical infections resulting in spontaneous abortion or stillbirths. These may take between few days up and 3 months to develop, the most common time frame being one-two weeks. Therefore, listeriosis is considered to be a significant public health concern1.

The causative agent of listeriosis, Listeria monocytogenes (Lm), is a Gram-positive, facultative anaerobic, rod-shaped intracellular psychrophile bacterium. It is ubiquitous and may be isolated from soil, water, vegetables, greens, stored forages, silage, haylage, grazing pastures, crop fields, farmyards etc.5. In contrast to many other common foodborne pathogens, Lm survive and multiply in a wide temperature range, including vegetable & fruit storage conditions, low temperatures of refrigerators and gastrointestinal tract of animals, especially livestock2. In fact, Lm can infect different animal species, but is more common in ruminants, sheep, goats and cattle. Occasionally, sporadic cases are registered in other domestic and wild animals, with such clinical manifestations as encephalitis, rhombencephalitis, septicaemia and abortion. Listeriosis outbreaks in ruminants and other animals have a significant economic impact worldwide due to their marked effect on animal health and welfare. Moreover, infected ruminants, especially with asymptomatic listeriosis, are the main and proved reservoirs for Lm and the principal source of infection for humans. The latter are infected by means of consuming food of animal origin contaminated with Lm, such as meats and dairy products, including unpasteurized milk, ice cream, soft cheeses, cold cuts and different kinds of ready-to eat foods which contain raw or semi-raw meat products without sufficient heat treatment1,2,5–10.

The control of listeriosis in ruminants is considered to be one of the most feasible and effective measures to reduce the presence of Lm in food of animal origin. Several live and inactivated listeriosis vaccines have been developed for this purpose and are reported to be applicable in practice11. However, till now little is known about the design and main components of effective listeriosis vaccines available for animal use.

Recently, the detailed characteristics of the whole genome of the attenuated Lm strain AUF, which has been successfully used for decades as the live whole-cell vaccine AUF (LWCV AUF) for the prevention of listeriosis in farm animals in Russian regions affected by this infection were reported11. More recently, it was demonstrated that this commercial vaccine is also manufactured using another Lm strain (termed by us as the Lm strain AUF-2). Both vaccine strains, the Lm strain AUF and the Lm strain AUF-2 were attenuated from different sources and belong to different sequence types (ST), ST1538 and ST124, distinct clonal complexes (CC), CC7 and CC124, respectively, while the phylogenetic lineage II is the same12. Contrary to the Lm strain AUF, attenuated by multiple ultraviolet irradiation (UFR) from the fully virulent Lm strain which was isolated from a brain sample of sheep with neurolisteriosis in Siberia Region11, the Lm strain AUF-2 was obtained through routine techniques following multiple passages on ordinary bacteriological media (meat peptone agar and Hottinger’ with no additional supplements)12. Overall, the Lm strain AUF-2 was derived from a hypovirulent Lm strain ‘A’, isolated from pasture ticks during the ovine outbreak of listeriosis in 1963 on the territory of Southwest Asia near the Caspian Sea13,14. The Lm strain AUF-2: (i) possessed typical morphology for Lm, forming on Chromocult Listeria Agar Ottaviani & Agosti (ALOA, HiMedia, India) small round, translucent, low-convex colonies12; (ii) was found to be absolutely harmless for white outbred mice, which were alive after s.c. inoculation with 1×10(9) CFU of the strain13,14; (iii) elicited strong protective immunity in mice, guinea pigs, rabbits, pigs, minks, chickens, lambs, calves, small ruminants (sheep and goats) and cattle in farms affected with listeriosis13,14. Since the end of the 1960s, the Lm strain AUF-2 has been used in parallel with the Lm strain AUF for the production of commercial veterinary LWCV AUF against listeriosis in the Russian Federation, which is intended for prophylactic immunization of cattle, sheep, pigs and rabbits12–14. Unfortunately, the genome of the Lm strain AUF-2, the second reference LWCV AUF strain, had not been sequenced before, and neither had been the genome of its derivative, termed here as the Lm strain AUF-3 and which is widely used for the large-scale manufacturing of the LWCV AUF. This, however, is extremely important for understanding the stability of the vaccine strain, which is why the relevant sequences of both Lm strains were sequenced by us.

In this study, both strains the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3 were successfully sequenced on Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS) platform, the HiSeq. 2500 System (Illumina Inc., USA). Further, de novo genome assembly resulted in 10 contigs for the Lm strain AUF-2 and 9 for the Lm strain AUF-3. Comparative structural and functional analysis showed minimal unessential difference between both strains in either total amount of CDSs (2,844 and 2,846, respectively) or the number of pseudogenes (92 and 88, respectively), or tRNAs (50 and 52, respectively). Additionally, each the Lm strain contains RNA genes (56 and 58, respectively), 2 rRNA (16S, 23S), and 56 of 92 genes associated with Lm virulence and immunogeneity15–17, among which there are 13 of 14 internalins (except inlL); prfA, responsible for encoding PrfA - the transcriptional regulator positive regulator factor A, associated with controlling the expression of the key virulence factors, inlA and inlB, and the genes of the pathogenicity island LIPI-1, actA, plcA, plcB, hly, mpl16–20; agrC, srtA; 5 antibiotic resistance genes (fosX, mprF, lin, norB and sul); 6 genes related to ESAT-6 gene cluster, esaA, essA, esaB, essB, essC and esxA, associated with the bacterial type VII secretion systems (T7SS)21–24; the genes associated with Lm acid tolerance, arcA, arc, argR, tatC, tatA, argB, argC, argF, argJ, sigB etc.25.

Remarkably, in both strains, novel allelic profiles were recognized for eight virulence genes, hly, inlE, inlF, inlI, lapB, oatA, oppA and plcA. In contrast to the Lm strain AUF, both the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3 possessed no annotated genes involved in metal resistance, such genes: agrA, aut_IVb, gltA, inlL, svpA, Listeria Pathogenicity Islands LIPI-3 and LIPI-4, and no genes related to the Listeria phage A118.

Our study presented the first high-quality draft genome sequences of the Lm strain AUF-2, the second reference LWCV AUF strain, and its derivative, the Lm strain AUF-3, used for large-scale manufacturing of this vaccine. The data reported here will provide the reference genomes for the prophylaxis of listeriosis in livestock and will be useful for the comparative analysis of factors involved in the protective activity and residual virulence of commercial and experimental vaccines against Lm-associated diseases.

Methods

Bacterial strains

The Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3 were obtained from the Collection of Microorganisms of the Department for Microbiology and Biotechnology, Saratov State University of Genetics, Biotechnology and Engineering named after N.I. Vavilov, Saratov, Russia. Both Lm strains were stored in lyophilized form. Both of them were ordinary grown on Tryptone Soy Yeast Extract Agar (TSYE Agar) (Merck, EU), as we described recently11,26,27.

DNA extraction

Genomic DNA was extracted from two Lm strains, the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3, using the commercial DNA DNeasy Blood & Tissue Kit (Qiagen, Germany) according to the manufacturer’s instructions as reported11,26,27. In order to evaluate their quality and integrity, the samples of isolated genomic DNA of each of the Lm strains, the Lm AUF-2 and the Lm AUF-3, were subjected to 1% agarose gel electrophoresis according to the appropriate DNA size standards. The DNA yield in each of the samples was measured by a spectrophotometer from BioRad (Bio-Rad, USA)11.

Whole genome sequencing

The isolated DNA from each of the individual Lm strains, the Lm AUF-2 and the Lm AUF-3, were used for the preparation of the relevant individual DNA libraries Nextera XT DNA Library Preparation Kit (Illumina Inc., USA). Then, the DNA samples were sequenced on the HiSeq. 2500 System as 250-bp single-Read (Illumina Inc., USA) commercially at Genoanalytica LLC (https://www.genoanalytica.ru/, Moscow, Russia), as described in our recent reports11,26,27.

Genome assembling and annotation

For de novo genome assembly of the Lm strain AUF-228 and the Lm strain AUF-329, the Unicycler v0.4.8 assembler available on the relevant Bacterial and Viral Bioinformatics Resource Center (BV-BRC) servers (https://www.bv-brc.org /) was used. This produced an assembly with ten contigs for the Lm strain AUF-2 and nine of those for the Lm strain AUF-3 (Table 1). Genome annotation for both Lm strains was done with the NCBI Prokaryotic Genome Annotation Pipeline (PGAP) via the submission portal of NCBI GenBank (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genome/annotation_prok/)30. The closest Lm homologs with the genetic distance ranging from 0.000780658 to 0.0074861931 for both Lm strains, the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3, were determined with Similar Genome Finder which was also available on BV-BRC platform (https://www.bv-brc.org /). The phylogenetic tree based on the genomes of the 25 Lm strains such as the closest Lm homologs (n = 14), the Lm typical reference strains (n = 11)31, and the reference representatives of other Listeria spp. (n = 14), was built using the REALPHY 1.13 server with default parameters (https://realphy.unibas.ch/realphy/)32 and MEGA-7 software33 by the maximum likelihood method. Data Records

The genome sequences of the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm AUF-3 are deposited in GenBank with the accession numbers JAZBNM000000000.128 and JAZBNN00000000029, respectively. All of the raw reads have been deposited in the NCBI Sequence Read Archive under the accession numbers SRR33530096 for the Lm strain AUF-2 and SRR33533190 for the Lm AUF-3, respectively36,37. The nucleotide sequences of the novel allele profiles of eight virulence genes recognized for the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm AUF-3, such as: hly, inlE, inlF, inlI, lapB, oatA, oppA and plcA, and also of the plcA gene for the Lm reference strain UKVDL4 and the Lm reference strain FSL-J1-15838 are deposited in BIGSdb (https://bigsdb.pasteur.fr/listeria/).

The panel of Lm reference strains

We compared the Lm strain AUF-228 and the Lm strain AUF-329 with the annotated completed genomes of twenty-five Lm strains downloaded from the NCBI GenBank (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/): the Lm strain AUF39, the Lm strain CDPHFDLB-F1605001-004-2B140, the Lm strain JF517141, the Lm strain D190L42, the Lm strain FDA300796-743, the Lm strain FSL V1-000944, the Lm strain V215745, the Lm strain Rev246, the Lm strain EGD-e47,48, the Lm strain FDAARGOS_55549,50, the Lm strain LM451, the Lm strain EGD52,53, the Lm strain 10403S54, the Lm strain N11-234555, the Lm strain L54756, the Lm strain N12-080255, the Lm strain D33L57, the Lm strain 5286958, the Lm strain FSL-J1-15859, the Lm strain NTSN60,61, the Lm strain FDAARGOS_60762, the Lm strain UKVDL963,64, the Lm strain 4/52-195365, the Lm strain UKVDL466, and the Lm strain UKVDL767. The reference Lm strains were initially isolated from biomaterials of either animals or humans with listeriosis from 1924 to 2023 in such countries as the USA, Norway, Switzerland, United Kingdom, South Korea and China31. The genomes of 14 strains of other Listeria spp. were additionally used for the construction of a phylogenetic tree in order to demonstrate the phylogenetic relationships between the Lm strain AUF-2, the Lm strain AUF-3 and other Listeria, such as: the Listeria innocua strain CFSAN04483668,69, the Listeria welshimeri strain NCTC1185770, the Listeria ivanovii subsp. ivanovii strain WSLC 301071, the Listeria seeligeri strain SLCC395472, the Listeria grayi strain NCTC1081273, the Listeria fleischmannii strain 199174, the Listeria floridensis strain FSL S10-118775, the Listeria valentine strain CLIP 2019/0064276, the Listeria kieliensis strain Kiel-L177, the Listeria booriae strain FSL A5-028178, the Listeria riparia strain FSL S10-120479, the Listeria rocourtiae strain CECT 7972 Ga0244616_10180, the Listeria cornellensis strain FSL F6-096981, and the Listeria rustica strain FSL W9-058582.

Technical Validation

In order to ensure the quality of the assembly, all the reads generated after the Illumina sequencing following the Illumina quality filters, were subjected to several steps. The processing of the raw data after sequencing on the Illumina platform included the removal of low-quality reads and adapters using the automated AfterQC script and the Filtlong v0.2.1. software in the default settings. The quality of the genome assembly by the de novo method was assessed based on the reports of the QUAST program integrated into the BV-BRC platform34. The total number of contigs was 10 for the Lm strain AUF-2 and 9 for the Lm strain AUF-3, respectively. The total length of the genome assemblies was 2,844,259 bp for the Lm strain AUF-2 and 2,845,029 bp for the Lm strain AUF-3 (Table 1), which corresponded to the Lm reference genomes (Table 2). Half of the entire assembly is contained in contigs with a size of 1,471,985 bp for the Lm strain AUF-2 and 1,471,971 bp for the Lm strain AUF-3 (N50), while 90% of the assembly is covered by contigs 228,785 bp for the Lm strain AUF-2 and 229,196 bp for the Lm strain AUF-3 (N90). The values were L50 = 1 and L90 = 4 for both strains. A GC content was identical for both strains −37,89% (Table 1), which correlated with typical values for other Lm reference strains83. Both the strains, the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3, were genetically closer to the Lm strain EGD-e, the NCBI reference47,48, the rabbit tissue derivative of EGD52, than to either the Lm strain AUF11, or its closest homolog, the Lm strain 10403S, or the two reference fully virulent Lm strains, the Lm strain FDAARGOS_607 and the Lm strain EGD11,19,20.

MLST analysis using the concatenated nucleotide sequences of seven Lm housekeeping genes (abcZ, bglA, cat, dap, dat, ldh and lhkA), proved that both Lm strains, the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3 possessed allele profiles identical to those of the three closest homologs, the Lm strain CDPHFDLB-F1605001-004-2B140, the Lm strain JF517141, and the Lm strain D190L42. All of them together with the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3, had identical sequence type (ST), ST124, of identical clonal complex (CC), CC124, and belonged to the same genetic lineage II, while having been derived from different sources, such as either frozen vegetables or cattle tissue but not from ovine-related specimens as both the Lm vaccine strains84. Importantly, both of the vaccine strains, the Lm AUF-2 and the Lm AUF-3, possessed five out of seven alleles (abcZ, dapE, dat, ldh and lhkA) identical to those of the three closest homologs, the Lm strain FDA300796-7, the Lm strain V2157 and the Lm strain FSL V1-0009. All these Lm strains belonged to the same genetic lineage II, ST635, CC635, to which the possible parental Lm strain for the Lm strain AUF-2 could probably belong as we have recently reported12. The other 22 Lm reference strains belonging to different STs and CCs of the genetic lineages I-IV demonstrated a marked polymorphism in their allele profiles which were not absolutely identical to those in the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3. Further, the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3, had the following alleles which were identical to those of the Lm reference strains of the genetic lineage II: (i) lhkA - in 18/18 cases (100%); (ii) either abcZ or dapE - in 9/18 (50%); (iii) either ldh or dat - in 6/18 (33.3%); (iv) bglA - in 5/18 (27.7%); and (v) cat - in 3/18 (16.7%) Lm strains, respectively. As expected, the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3 showed a limited homology as only two of seven identical alleles: (i) bglA and lhkA - with the Lm strain EGD-e, ST35, CC9; (ii) dat and lhkA - with the Lm strain EGD, ST12, CC7, and the Lm strain AUF, ST1538, CC7, another Lm vaccine strain used for a long time as a live veterinary vaccine in Russian Federation11,12,14. Both vaccine strains, the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3, had no identical alleles with the Lm reference strains of the genetic lineages I, III-IV of ST1, ST563, ST1194, ST201, ST1069, ST1140 and ST449 of clonal complexes CC1, ST563, ST1194, CC69, ST1069 and CC1070 used in the current study84.

Comparison of the genomes of the Lm strain AUF-2 versus the Lm strain AUF-3 by the BV-BRC platform (https://www.bv–brc.org/) revealed 100% homology in 98.4% of relevant CDSs (Table 2). In total, 99.9% of CDSs of both strains, the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3, had 90.0-100.0% homology. Only three additional CDSs for the expression of such products as: Late competence protein ComC, processing protease; Putative methyltransferase and Uncharacterized glycosyltransferase YcjM were identified in the Lm strain AUF-2 and not detected in the Lm strain AUF-385. Further matching of the genome of the Lm strain AUF-2 versus four closest homologs, the Lm strains D190L, the Lm strain CDPHFDLB-F1605001-004-2B1, the Lm strain JF5171 and the Lm strain FDA0000591831 showed 100% homology in almost 73.0-90.5% and a total of 90-100% homology in more than 97% CDSs (Table 2). Moreover, 31-37 additional CDSs were found in the Lm strain AUF-2 compared to either the Lm strains D190L or the Lm strain CDPHFDLB-F1605001-004-2B1, or the Lm strain JF5171, and more than 60 CDSs compared to the Lm strain FDA00005918. Mainly, there were a number of hypothetical proteins, Penicillin-binding protein 3, AraC family Transcriptional regulator, Flagellar biosynthesis protein FlhA, Type II restriction enzyme Sau3AI (EC 3.1.21.4), ABC transporter (ATP-binding protein), NtrC family Transcriptional regulator, also Late competence protein ComC, processing protease, etc.85. Moreover, a number of pseudogenes in both the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3 was approximately 3,2–4,8-fold higher compared to the closest Lm reference strains, the Lm strain CDPHFDLB-F1605001-004-2B1, the Lm strain D190L, the Lm strain FDAARGOS_555, and approximately 2.5-fold higher than in the Lm strain LM4, and approximately 4-fold higher than in another vaccine strain, the Lm strain AUF11. In contrast, the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3 demonstrated a significantly lower number of pseudogenes - up to 2.6- and 2.8-fold, respectively, in comparison exclusively with the Lm strain Rev2, the known Lm EGD-e strain variant, which can rapidly be induced to grow as an L-form142–144.

Comparison of the Lm strain AUF-2 versus the Lm strain AUF-3 showed that both strains had 85 (92.4%) identical pseudogenes, while the remaining 7 of 92 (7.6%) pseudogenes identified in the Lm strain AUF-2, were potentially able in the Lm strain AUF-3 for protein translation145. There were such genes as: a putative type IV prepilin peptidase analogous to the comC gene of Bacillus subtilis146,147; the major facilitator superfamily (MFS) transporter, involved in selective transport a wide spectrum of substrates across biomembranes and play a pivotal role in multiple physiological processes in bacteria and humans148; phosphate acyltransferase, which is important for Listeria to survive and replicate during infection149; 1-deoxy-D-xylulose-5-phosphate synthase (DXS), playing the critical role in the methylerythritol 4-phosphate (MEP) pathway, essential for microbial survival and found in bacteria but not in mammals, including humans that makes this molecule a promising new target for the development of novel anti-infective agents150,151; Domain of unknown function (DUF), DUF1295 domain-containing protein with unknown function152; sugar phosphorylase for nutrient acquisition, particularly during Listeria infection149 and hypothetical protein145. Only 7 of 92 (7.6%) pseudogenes annotated in the Lm strain AUF-2, were also recognized as pseudogenes in the Lm strain AUF: Crp/Fnr family transcriptional regulator responding to various environmental stimuli, such as changes in temperature, pH, and nutrient availability, and subsequently trigger the expression of many stress response and virulence-related genes153; HD domain-containing protein, mediating cooperative hydrolysis of c-di-AMP to affect bacterial growth and virulence154; YehR family protein, IS3 family transposase, which is involved in the movement and integration of DNA segments within Lm bacterial genome119; LXG polymorphic toxin with a polymorphic domain architecture consisting of a conserved N-terminal Leu-X-Gly (LXG) domain and a variable C-terminal toxin domain that inhibits the growth of bacterial competitors and recently reported to be recruited to the bacterial type VII secretion systems (T7SS) apparatus155 and two hypothetical proteins as well145. In contrast to the Lm strain AUF-2, no pseudogenes corresponding to either the relevant homologs of DUF4259 domain-containing protein or DUF998 domain-containing protein or three hypothetical proteins were revealed in the Lm strain AUF (5/92 pseudogenes, 5.4%). The rest 80/92 (86.9%) pseudogenes found in the Lm strain AUF-2, could probably express the relevant proteins in the Lm strain AUF. Comparison of the Lm strain AUF-2 versus to the Lm strain FDAARGOS_607 demonstrated only 3/92 (3.3%) identical pseudogenes, such as: LytR/AlgR family response regulator transcription factor, contributing to Listeria pathogenesis, replication, regulation of the transcription of genes involved in different metabolic pathways which are essential for the growth and persistence of Lm in the host or under nutrient limitation; importantly, deletion of lysR also attenuated the virulence of Lm in mouse model following oral and intraperitoneal inoculation156; dihydroorotate dehydrogenase electron transfer subunit, critical for the de novo synthesis of pyrimidines in Listeria as a vital pathway for producing DNA and RNA building blocks157 and hypothetical protein. In fact, 13/92 (14.1%) genes identified in the Lm strain AUF-2 as pseudogenes were not found in the Lm strain FDAARGOS_607, namely: DUF998 domain-containing protein, DUF4259 domain-containing protein, DUF1307 domain-containing protein, IS3 family transposase, glycosyltransferase and glycosyltransferase family 2 protein, T7SS effector LXG polymorphic toxin; YfhO family protein, essential for Lm wall teichoic acid glycosylation158 as well as 5 hypothetical proteins. The majority of the rest Lm strain AUF-2 pseudogenes, 76/92 (82.6%) could encode the relevant proteins in the Lm strain FDAARGOS_607145. Further, only 6/92 (6.5%) were annotated as pseudogenes in the Lm strain EGD-e identically to the Lm strain AUF-2: IS3 family transposase; dihydroorotate dehydrogenase electron transfer subunit, putative RNA methyltransferase, the main function of which is RNA methylating159; helix-turn-helix domain-containing protein (HTH), the well-known C-terminal HTH motif of Lm PrfA which could either directly or indirectly influence the transcription of 73 Lm genes, including most of the known virulence genes of L. monocytogenes, including the virulence gene cluster (prfA, plcA, hly, mpl, actA, and plcB), and several members of the internalin family of proteins160,161; SMI1/KNR4 family protein, which is highly conserved in low GC Gram-positive bacteria and regulates important processes such as cell wall homeostasis, cell membrane integrity, cell division, hence being essential for cell viability162; DNA mismatch repair protein MutS, a component of the DNA mismatch repair (MMR) system, critical for survival and multiplication of Lm in animal models163. In addition, 5/92 (5.4%) genes corresponding to pseudogenes of the Lm strain AUF-2, were not identified/absent in the Lm strain EGD-e, such as: T7SS effector LXG polymorphic toxin, DUF998 domain-containing protein and 3 hypothetical proteins. Contrary to the Lm strain AUF-2, 81/92 (88.1%) related genes were not pseudogenes in the Lm strain EGD-e, being probably capable to express the relevant proteins145.

Comparative analysis of the Lm strain AUF-3 versus the Lm strain AUF-2 showed that these strains had 85/88 (96.6%) identical pseudogenes, while 3/88 (3.4%) – were able to express the relevant proteins164, such as: MerR family transcriptional regulator, involved in Lm in oxidative stress response, acid tolerance and intracellular growth165; multidrug efflux MFS transporter NorB, the expression of which was selectively increased in abscesses indicating this protein as contributing significantly to Lm survival and replication in the abscess environment166,167; and N-acetyl-gamma-glutamyl-phosphate reductase. The Lm strain AUF-3 and the Lm strain AUF had only 6/88 (6.8%) identical pseudogenes, DUF1307 domain-containing protein, T7SS effector LXG polymorphic toxin, hypothetical protein, Crp/Fnr family transcriptional regulator, IS3 family transposase and an additional hypothetical protein in the Lm strain AUF-3, which was annotated in the Lm strain AUF as T7SS effector LXG polymorphic toxin164. Moreover, 5/88 (5.7%) pseudogenes annotated in the Lm strain AUF-3, were absent in the Lm strain AUF, namely: DUF4259 domain-containing protein, DUF998 domain-containing protein and 3 hypothetical proteins as well. The majority of the pseudogenes identified in the Lm strain AUF-3 - 77/88 (87.5%), were able to encode the relevant proteins in the Lm strain AUF. Only 3/88 (3.4%) pseudogenes identical to those identified in the Lm strain AUF-3 were annotated in the Lm strain FDAARGOS_607, namely: dihydroorotate dehydrogenase electron transfer Subunit, hypothetical protein and LytR/AlgR family response regulator transcription factor164. In fact, 13/88 (14.8%) pseudogenes annotated in only the Lm strain AUF-3 were not revealed in the Lm strain FDAARGOS_607, such as: DUF1307 domain-containing protein, DUF998 domain-containing protein, DUF4259 domain-containing protein, glycosyltransferase, glycosyltransferase family 2 protein, YfhO family protein, T7SS effector LXG polymorphic toxin, IS3 family transposase and 5 hypothetical proteins. As expected, contrary to the Lm strain AUF-3, many proteins - 72/88 (81.8%) could be expressed in the Lm strain FDAARGOS_607. We found 7/88 (7.9%) identical pseudogenes for both the Lm strain AUF-3 and the Lm strain EGD-e, such as: SMI1/KNR4 family protein, dihydroorotate dehydrogenase electron transfer Subunit, putative RNA methyltransferase, helix-turn-helix domain-containing protein, IS3 family transposase, flavodoxin and hypothetical protein164. Only 5/88 (5.7%) pseudogenes of the Lm strain AUF-3 were absent in the Lm strain EGD-e: T7SS effector LXG polymorphic toxin, DUF998 domain-containing protein and 3 hypothetical proteins. Also, 76/88 (86.4%) pseudogenes of the Lm strain AUF-3 were annotated as genes expressing the relevant proteins in the Lm strain EGD-e164.

Further, both the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3 could encode the early secreted antigenic target 6-kDa (ESAT-6) protein (EsxA) containing abundant T-cell epitopes and capable of inducing a Th1 response21,89. Additionally, both strains possessed 5 other genes related to ESAT-6 gene cluster, esaA, essA, esaB, essB and essC, which were initially found in the Lm strain EGD-e (NCBI Acc. No. NC_003210.124, and expressed such proteins as Putative secretion accessory protein EsaA/YueB@Bacteriophage SPP1 receptor, Putative secretion system component EssA, Putative secretion accessory protein EsaB/YukD, Putative secretion system component EssB/YukC and FtsK/SpoIIIE family protein, and putative EssC/YukB component of Type VII secretion system, respectively89,168. As expected, all these genes were routinely detected in other Lm reference strains, the Lm strain EGD, the Lm strain AUF, the Lm strain FDAARGOS_607, the Lm strain CDPHFDLB-F1605001-004-2B1, the Lm strain D190L, the Lm strain JF5171, the Lm strain FDA00005918 and in the NCBI reference sequence the Lm strain EGD-e (NCBI Acc. No. NZ_CP023861.1). These genes were responsible for encoding in Listeria secreting toxic effector proteins of T7SS known as membrane-embedded nanomachines which are used by Gram-positive bacteria to export effector proteins from the cytoplasm to the extracellular environment and could play an important role in both bacterial competition and bacterial antagonism while being indispensable for Lm virulence and host cell invasion22,24,155,169.

Multiple alignment of the amino acid sequences of the T7SS genes annotated in either the Lm strain AUF-2 or the Lm strain AUF-3, respectively, versus the Lm reference strains demonstrated the absolute identity of four of them, essA, esaB, essB and esxA, while few SNPs were identified in the relevant nucleotide sequences compared to the Lm strain FDAARGOS_607 or the Lm strain EGD or the Lm strain AUF, respectively170,171. A certain polymorphism was observed in the amino acid sequences of the gene esaA in both the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3 versus to either the NCBI reference sequence the Lm strain EGD-e (NZ_CP023861.1)25,26 or another whole genome sequence of the Lm strain EGD-e (NC_003210.1) reported recently24 with the relevant five substitutions of Serine → Asparagine in position 199, S199N, Alanine → Threonine in position 248, A248T, Alanine → Valine in position 261, A261V, Valine → Alanine in position 289, V289A, and Alanine → Valine in position 868, A868V, respectively171. On the contrary, a marked polymorphism was detected in the amino acid sequences of the gene essC in either the Lm strain AUF-2 or the Lm strain AUF-3 versus to the relevant amino acid sequences derived from the two above-mentioned genomes of the Lm strains EGD-e24,47,48 with a number of either single or multiple substitutions, deletions, or insertions from the position 935 up to the position 1504 similarly to the closest Lm homologs, the Lm strain CDPHFDLB-F1605001-004-2B1, the Lm strain D190L and the Lm strain JF5171, and strongly different from the Lm reference strains, the Lm strain FDAARGOS_607, the Lm strain EGD and the Lm strain AUF171.

Importantly, both the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3 similarly demonstrated the absence within ESAT-6 gene cluster of some other genes, capable to encode in both of the fully virulent reference Lm strains, the Lm strain EGD-e and the Lm strain FDAARGOS_607, such proteins as: putative EsaC protein analog (Listeria type 1), 10 kDa culture filtrate antigen CFP-10 (EsxB), SAV0291 homolog, Lmo0065 homolog and COG5444 domain protein. All of these were also not annotated in other reference Lm strains, the Lm strain EGD, the Lm strain AUF, the Lm strain CDPHFDLB-F1605001-004-2B1, the Lm strain D190L, the Lm strain JF5171, the Lm strain FDA0000591889.

In fact, several genes could encode the relevant proteins in either the Lm strain AUF-2 (n = 9) or the Lm strain AUF-3 (n = 10), being true pseudogenes in the NCBI reference sequence, the Lm strain EGD-e (ATP-binding cassette domain-containing protein, YxeA family protein, formate-tetrahydrofolate ligase, ABC transporter ATP-binding protein, AbiH family protein, rifamycin-inactivating phosphotransferase/gene = “rph”, XRE/MutR family transcriptional regulator, sensor histidine kinase and hypothetical proteins) and the fully virulent reference Lm strain FDAARGOS_607 (n = 17, cyclic-di-AMP phosphodiesterase PgpH, phosphotransferase family protein, DUF445 domain-containing protein, DUF6440 family protein, GNAT family N-acetyltransferase, DUF4003 family protein, diguanylate cyclase, glycoside hydrolase family 1 protein, DUF3130 family protein, 7 hypothetical proteins, one of which was identified in FRAFARGOS_607 as T7SS effector LXG polymorphic toxin)172. It is important to note that, similarly, at least some of these genes (n = 8) were capable of encoding in both the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3 but not in the Lm strain AUF such products as: PTS sugar transporter subunit IIB, T7SS effector LXG polymorphic toxin, tetratricopeptide repeat protein, amino acid permease, DUF1254 domain-containing protein, 2 hypothetical proteins and once more hypothetical protein, annotated in the Lm strain AUF as DUF3958 domain-containing protein172. Further, three genes were absent in both the Lm strain AUF-2 and in the Lm strain AUF-3 being true pseudo genes in the NCBI reference sequence the Lm strain EGD-e, namely: T7SS effector LXG polymorphic toxin, 2 hypothetical proteins172. We found 10 genes, unannotated in the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3, which were also pseudogenes in the fully virulent Lm strain FDAARGOR_607, namely: hydantoinase/oxoprolinase family protein, TNT domain-containing protein, ParA family protein, ribonuclease P, HEAT repeat domain-containing protein, DNA double-strand break repair Rad50 ATPase and 4 hypothetical proteins172. In addition, 6 genes were undetectable in the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3, while identified as pseudogenes in the Lm strain AUF, trypsin-like serine protease, pentapeptide repeat-containing protein, pentapeptide repeat-containing protein and 3 hypothetical proteins172.

Importantly, both strains, the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3, similarly with the reference Lm strain EGD-e, the Lm strain AUF and others except the Lm strain FDAARGOS_607, possessed the genes associated with acid tolerance, which are used by Listeria to combat the challenges presented by acidic pH during food processing, in acidic food, the stomach, phagosomes of macrophages and abscesses to survive or even proliferate in acidic conditions25,173–176. There were genes annotated by the PGAP of the NCBI GenBank platform (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genome/annotation_prok/)30 and involved in: (i) the Lm arginine deiminase system (ADI) and (ii) agmatine deiminase (AgDI) systems to produce ammonia following neutralization of intracellular protons by forming NH4C to elevate its cytoplasmic pH177–181. Thus, we found the following ADI-associated gene encoding the relevant proteins, namely: arcA (Arginine deiminase), arc (Carbamate kinase), argR (Arginine pathway regulatory protein ArgR, repressor of arg regulon), tatC (Twin-arginine translocation protein TatCy), tatA (Twin-arginine translocation protein TatAy), argB (N-acetylglutamate kinase), argC (N-acetyl-gamma-glutamyl-phosphate reductase), argF (Ornithine carbamoyltransferase), argJ (Glutamate N-acetyltransferase@ N-acetylglutamate synthase), unnamed genes for the possible expression of Argininosuccinate synthase, Protein-arginine kinase McsB, Protein-arginine kinase activator protein McsA, Arginine decarboxylase, Lysine decarboxylase, N-acetylornithine aminotransferase and also, the gene sigB (RNA polymerase sigma factor SigB)89,182, which together with Arginine deiminase, the product of the gene arcA, could be markedly repressed by ArgR when bacteria were exposed to pH 5.5 in the absence of arginine183–185. The AgDI system was represented in both the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3 by two genes annotated as aguA, encoding Agmatine deiminase (SDC43_10940) and as pseudogene (SDC43_RS109500), additionally by aguB/ptcA (Putrescine carbamoyltransferase), aguD (Agmatine/putrescine antiporter, associated with agmatine catabolism), and also by the genes encoding Spermidine/putrescine import ABC transporter ATP-binding protein PotA, Spermidine/putrescine import ABC transporter permease protein PotB, Spermidine/putrescine import ABC transporter permease protein PotC, Spermidine/putrescine import ABC transporter substrate-binding protein PotD, Carbonic anhydrase, alpha class, Two-component transcriptional response regulator (OmpR family) and Two-component system sensor histidine kinase89,182.

Only two genes, gadB/gadD2 and gadC, encoding Glutamate decarboxylase and Probable glutamate/gamma-aminobutyrate antiporter, were initially identified using UniProt Peptide Search tools186 and then identified in both the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3, similarly with the Lm strain FDAARGOS_607 and contrary to the reference strains, the Lm strain EGD-e, the Lm strain EGD, the Lm strain AUF and others89,182, in which additionally were found gadA/gadD1 and gadC, involved in glutamate decarboxylase system (GAD), responsible in Lm together with ADI for maintenance of intracellular pH under acidic conditions25,187–191.

Besides these, both the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3 were capable to produce several proteins of a supramolecular complex called the stressosome (RsbT co-antagonist protein RsbRA, RsbS negative regulator of sigma-B, anti-sigma B factor RsbT, regulator of sigma subunit serine phosphatase RsbU, serine-protein kinase RsbW, RNA polymerase sigma factor SigB, Phosphoserine phosphatase RsbX), the activation of which following the upregulation of approximately 300 genes could increase Lm resistance to lethal acid stress25,192–197. As expected, since Lm was reported to be lacking the alternative pathway via RsbV identified in Bacillus subtilis and Bacillus cereus, the expression of gene rsbV, encoding anti-sigma B factor antagonist RsbV198–200, was inhibited in both the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3145,164 while successfully annotated in the reference Lm strains with a homology of 99-100% to the NCBI reference the Lm strain EGD-e89,144,164,182.

Finally, annotated in the genomes of the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3 were both of the genes encoding Two-component transcriptional response regulator OmpR family and Two-component system sensor histidine kinase, of the LisRK two-component system, which could also be involved in acid stress resistance in Lm201,202. According to the data obtained using the BV-BRC platform (https://www.bv–brc.org/), both components with 100% homology were annotated in all the reference Lm strains, the Lm strain EGD-e, Lm strain EGD, the Lm strain AUF, the Lm strain FDAARGOS_607, the Lm strain CDPHFDLB-F1605001-004-2B1, the Lm strain D190L, the Lm strain JF5171 and the Lm strain FDA0000591889,182. We hypothesize that the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3 could, at least, partially support acid tolerance mechanisms that suggests these strains as promising candidates for the development of oral veterinary vaccine against Lm-associated infection. On the other hand, some of the mutations identified here could be certainly related to the attenuation of these strains to either decrease or abolish the stress response of both strains, the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3.

Usage Notes

We have provided the high-quality draft genome sequences and gene annotations for two Lm strains, the Lm strain AUF-2 (the standard collection strain) and the Lm strain AUF-3 (the manufacture strain), used for a long time together with the Lm strain AUF as the commercial whole-cell live vaccine (LWCV) ‘AUF’ against listeriosis in livestock. Our current research is focused on the comparison of the genomes derived from either the Lm strain AUF-2 or the Lm strain AUF-3 with each other and with the whole genomes of the NCBI reference sequence, the Lm strain EGD-e and other Lm reference strains including the Lm strain AUF of zoonotic, human and environmental origin available in the NCBI GenBank. In order to recognize the genes/locuses, which could be possibly related to attenuation and involved in residual virulence, we also identified the closest homologs for the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3 following their detailed comparative analysis versus all the genomes selected. We state that the Lm strain AUF-2 has minimal difference from the Lm strain AUF-3, while both these Lm strains differ strongly from the second LWCV strain, the Lm strain AUF, and either the reference Lm strain EGD-e or the fully virulent Lm strain FDAARGOS_607. Additionally, the genomes of the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3 were not absolutely identical to those of any of the closest Lm homologs. The main limitation of our research was the absence of the parental strain from which the Lm strain AUF-2 was routinely attenuated. This prevents precise identification of de novo mutations, arising specifically during attenuation despite the detailed analysis of a panel of the reference Lm strains. Also, only Illumina-generated short reads, derived from both vaccine strains were available for current analysis, that could result in possible missed structural variants or inaccurate assembly of repetitive regions. However, N50 and L50 as fundamental contiguity metrics and related statistics indicated the reliable quality of de novo genome assemblies for both the Lm strains, the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3. Nevertheless, we have currently highlighted one more way for the successful attenuation of Lm via the number of visible mutations resulted from the inactivation of more than 90 genes based on the instance of the second Lm strain, used as the LWCV AUF and initially routinely attenuated from a wild ovine Lm strain. Additionally, rather greater polymorphism in virulence genes than global genomic losses were revealed in both the strains, the Lm strain AUF-2 and the Lm strain AUF-3. However, it is not clear whether this phenomenon is directly correlated with a certain attenuation of the Lm strain AUF-2 and its derivative, the Lm strain AUF-3, or these are specific characteristics of some Lm subspp., to which these strains and the relevant parental Lm strain A-2 belong. It was quite possible, that the parental Lm strain A-2 could be naturally selected through passage in sheep under unspecified conditions more than 70 years ago, while it is not possible to make this hypothesis clearer due to the absence of the parental Lm strain A-2.

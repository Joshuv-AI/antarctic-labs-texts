Posterior urethral hemangioma presenting as hematospermia in an adolescent male
Authors: Zhongqiang Jiang, Yifan Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPosterior urethral hemangioma is a rare benign vascular malformation causing painless hematospermia. Due to its occult appearance in the non-erect state, conventional imaging often misses the diagnosis. The therapeutic challenge lies in complete excision while preserving sphincter and sexual function. We report an adolescent male admitted for 6 hematospermia. Cystourethroscopy revealed two mass-like lesions adjacent to the verumontanum, diagnosed as posterior urethral hemangioma. We performed precise vaporization using transurethral thulium fiber laser (TFL). This technique achieved complete lesion clearance and preserved function, resulting in a successful outcome.Keywords: Urethral hemangioma, Hematospermia, Thulium fiber laser, Posterior urethra

Introduction

Urethral hemangioma is a rare benign vascular malformation associated with impaired angiogenesis during embryonic development, with some cases representing components of congenital vascular malformation syndromes such as Klippel-Trenaunay syndrome (KTS).1,2 Acquired forms often result from sudden elevation of posterior urethral pressure and obstructed venous return due to pelvic floor muscle contraction during erection or ejaculation, leading to dilated and tortuous vessels that form hemangioma-like changes, or may be associated with local chronic inflammation.3,4 Globally reported male cases are limited in number, with posterior urethral involvement being even rarer. The classic clinical features are painless, intermittent gross hematuria or hematospermia associated with erection or ejaculation.3,5 Because lesions are located in the posterior urethra and appear flat and occult in the non-erect state, conventional imaging and cystoscopic examinations are highly prone to missed diagnosis.6

Case presentation

An adolescent male patient was admitted due to 11 days of hematospermia. The patient experienced hematospermia during nocturnal emission 11 days prior and underwent urinary system color Doppler ultrasound at our institution with no abnormalities identified. Hematospermia recurred 6 days later, and the outpatient physician ordered transrectal ultrasound of the prostate and seminal vesicles plus urinary system color Doppler ultrasound, both yielding normal results. Hematospermia reappeared 4 days later during ejaculation. The patient denied fever, fatigue, urinary frequency, urgency, dysuria, or flank pain. He was previously healthy with no history of chronic disease, trauma, surgery, hemorrhagic disorders, family history, or anticoagulant medication use.

Admission physical examination revealed stable vital signs with no abnormalities on genitourinary system examination, including unremarkable scrotal and perineal exams with no abnormal venous structures. Laboratory examinations revealed that urinalysis showed protein (2+), red blood cells 17,212/μL, and white blood cells 25/μL. Complete blood count, liver and renal function tests showed no abnormalities. Color Doppler ultrasound of the urinary system, prostate, and seminal vesicles demonstrated no significant abnormalities, with negative nutcracker syndrome screening. Computed tomography (CT) urography indicated findings not definitively associated with posterior urethral hemangioma.

Cystourethroscopy identified oval-shaped red mass-like lesions at the 10 o'clock and 2 o'clock positions adjacent to the verumontanum, with smooth surfaces, approximately 1.0 cm × 1.0 cm in size, and bleeding upon contact, establishing the clinical diagnosis of posterior urethral hemangioma. Given the surgical indication, the patient underwent transurethral thulium fiber laser resection of the lesion under general anesthesia (Video 1). Intraoperatively, an artificial erection was first induced, and a 24 Fr resectoscope was then smoothly inserted. After reconfirming the lesions (Fig. 1), a thulium laser fiber was introduced. Power was set at 26 W in continuous wave mode. The lesions were vaporized separately until complete clearance. The scope was reintroduced to examine the wound margins (Fig. 2). The procedure involved minimal bleeding with a duration of 10 minutes. Three days postoperatively, the patient voided smoothly and was discharged. At 1-month follow-up, the patient remained symptom-free with an International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS) of 0.

Discussion

Urethral hemangioma is a rare benign vascular lesion, accounting for less than 0.05% of all urinary tract tumors.3 Congestion during erection causes vascular wall rupture, manifesting as painless, intermittent gross hematuria or hematospermia.2,4,5 In the non-erect state, the tumor collapses, creating an "occult" pattern.1 Cavernous hemangioma of the anterior urethra presents primarily with painless urethral bleeding, which is a key differential point from posterior urethral lesions.7 Posterior urethral hemangioma symptoms are easily overlooked.6 For young men with unexplained hematuria, meticulous cystourethroscopy is critical for diagnosis. Examination should focus on the periverumontanum region, with abdominal compression or artificially induced erection if necessary to facilitate tumor visualization.2,5,8

Treatment must balance complete eradication with function preservation.5 Conventional transurethral resection carries a 15%-20% risk of urethral stricture or retrograde ejaculation.9 Sclerotherapy and open surgery also have significant limitations.10,11 In recent years, laser technology has become the preferred minimally invasive treatment.12, 13, 14 The thulium fiber laser (TFL) wavelength (1.94 μm) matches the water absorption peak, with a penetration depth of only 70 μm.13 In this case, 26 W power ensured sufficient vaporization while minimizing thermal injury to the external urethral sphincter.13 Compared with conventional methods, TFL reduces stricture risk and protects ejaculatory function.7,14 Although laser vaporization precludes pathological confirmation, its safety makes it ideal for high-risk lesions adjacent to the verumontanum.12,15

Conclusion

Posterior urethral hemangioma is a rare cause of hematospermia in adolescents. Meticulous cystourethroscopy remains the definitive diagnostic method. The thulium fiber laser proves to be an ideal modality for precise excision of high-risk lesions adjacent to the verumontanum, effectively preserving urethral and sexual function.

Wheat TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A play roles in seed germination and abiotic stress responses in transgenic Arabidopsis and rice
Authors: Hanxi Liu, Yaxin Yao, Jiayang Ma, Shaoyu Wang, Song Li, Weiwei Wang, Xinmiao Yu, Fengli Sun, Chao Zhang, Yajun Xi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSeed germination is a key process in the plant life cycle that affects the vegetative and reproductive stages of plants. Although the JAZ gene family has been characterized in many plants, the relationship between the JAZ gene and seed germination is still unclear.ResultsWe identified two members of the JAZ family from wheat, TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A. TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A were localized in both the cell membrane and nucleus. Spatio-temporal expression analysis of TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A in wheat revealed that these genes are essential for the preharvest sprouting (PHS) stage of seed development, with expression levels significantly decreasing during the ripening period. Transgenic rice plants overexpressing wheat TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A improved seed germination rates. Transgenic Arabidopsis plants overexpressing wheat TaTIFY10A improved seed germination rates and promoted flowering. In addition, abscisic acid (ABA) and jasmonic acid (JA) were found to induce TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A expression. Under different ABA concentrations, the seed germination rates of transgenic rice and Arabidopsis overexpressing TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A are superior to wild-type (WT) and mutant plants, and the root lengths of Arabidopsis overexpressing TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A also change. Under different JA concentrations, there is no difference in the seed germination rate of rice overexpressing TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A compared to WT and mutant plants, but there is a significant difference in the seed germination rate and root length of overexpressing Arabidopsis compared to WT and mutant plants. Under different concentrations of salt and drought treatments, the seed germination rate and root length of overexpressing Arabidopsis of TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A are affected.ConclusionsThis study offers a novel perspective for understanding the molecular basis of pre-harvest sprouting and provides potential candidate genes for controlling wheat seed germination.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-024-05656-7.

Introduction

Wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) primarily reproduces sexually through seed production. Seed dormancy and germination are crucial stages in the life cycle of wheat and thus important factors in crop production [1]. Seed germination is of great importance to agricultural production, and it is crucial to analyze and study the molecular mechanisms regulating seed germination-related genes. Many genes have been discovered in crops that regulate crop seed germination. TaMFT2 and its homologous gene TaPHS1 are closely related to wheat seed germination and sprouting [2, 3]. OsFbx352 plays a regulatory role in regulating glucose-induced seed germination suppression by targeting ABA metabolism [4]. GmSMXLs regulate soybean seed germination under salt and drought stress [5].

Seed germination and dormancy are complex phenomena in seed biology, influenced by a variety of endogenous and exogenous factors [1, 6, 7]. Abscisic acid (ABA) and gibberellic acid (GA) are the primary endogenous regulators that control seed dormancy and germination. Their intricate interactions and dynamic equilibria are the two internal factors that affect the transition from dormancy to germination. ABA is the primary hormone responsible for seed dormancy and prevents germination [8, 9]. ABA accumulates at low levels in seeds during the early stages of plant development and reaches the highest level when storage substances are synthesized in the middle stages of plant development [10]. This gradual accumulation of ABA establishes and sustains dormancy, which is necessary to prevent early germination. Among the ABA signaling pathways, genes associated with seed dormancy have been extensively studied [11]. The ABA receptors PYRABACTIN RESISTANCE (PYR)/REGULATORY COMPONENT OF ABSCISIC ACID RECEPTOR (RCAR) respond to the presence of ABA [12–15]. These receptors join type 2C protein phosphatases (PP2Cs) to create a stable complex when they bind ABA, causing the release of SNF1-related kinase 2 (SnRK2s) from PP2C- SnRK2 complexes [16]. Activated SnRK2s subsequently phosphorylate downstream transcription factors, such as ABSCISIC ACID-INSENSITIVE 5 (ABI5), ABI4, and ABI3, which mediate the ABA response [17–22]. The ABA signaling pathway includes transcription factors that act as positive regulators. Viviparous-1 (Vp-1), ABI3, ABI4, and ABI5 are favorable regulatory variables [23]. In Arabidopsis, the loss of function of ABI3, a positive regulators of ABA signaling, causes early germination [24, 25]. ABI5 plays a key role in seed maturation, dormancy, and desiccation tolerance of seedlings [26, 27].

In addition to ABA and GA, other phytohormones play a role in the control of seed dormancy and germination. JA and its metabolites are common signaling molecules that control a variety of functions, including defense responses, root elongation, anthocyanin accumulation and flowering time [28–33]. The TIFY family member JAZ protein, which inhibits the JA pathway, not only functions as a crucial node in the complex hormone regulatory network by interacting with regulators of other signaling pathways, but also plays a significant role in the regulation of the jasmonate hormone response. JAZ proteins bind to various transcription factors and co-repressor proteins, linking the JA signaling pathway to multiple other signaling pathways. For example, overexpression of AtJAZ7 in Arabidopsis improves drought tolerance by regulating photosynthesis, redox, antioxidant, and metabolic pathways involving amino acids, plant hormones, and defense metabolites [34]. In chrysanthemum plants overexpressing CmJAZ1, the flowering time is delayed due to the deletion of the JAS domain [35]. The JAZ protein family plays a regulatory role in seed germination. Overexpression of PnJAZ1 in Pohlia nutans suppresses the expression of ABA pathway-related genes associated with seed germination and seedling growth and exhibits ABA-insensitive phenotypes in Arabidopsis abi4 or abi5 mutants. PnJAZ1 acts as a repressor, mediates the JA-ABA synergistic crosstalk, and promotes plant growth under salt stress [36]. In wheat and Arabidopsis, the interaction between JAZ proteins and ABI5 regulates seed germination [37]. In Arabidopsis, the ABI3 and ABI5 transcription factors are regulated by JAZ repressors of the JA pathway to integrate JA and ABA signals during seed germination and post-germinative growth [38]. Moreover, the JAZ–ABI3 /ABI5 module controls seed germination by integrating the JA and ABA signaling pathways [34, 37, 38]. However, the underlying mechanism by which the JA signaling pathway-related genes TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A promote plant seed germination remains unclear.

In the context of TIFY's response to plant stress, previous studies have found that AtJAZ7 can enhance drought tolerance by regulating pathways such as photosynthesis, redox, antioxidants, amino acids, plant hormones, and defense metabolites [39]. OsJAZ1 (OsTIFY3) can interact with OsbHLH148 to improve drought tolerance in rice [40]. Overexpression of OsJAZ9 (OsTIFY11a) increases plant tolerance to both salt and drought [41]. However, the response of TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A to plant stress remains largely unknown.

MingXian 169 type wheat has a deep dormancy and a high ability to resist PHS. In addition to being widely utilized as an induction material for stripe rust resistance breeding, MingXian wheat is an excellent material for PHS research. In the present study, we analyzed the transcriptome data of germinated and dormant seeds of MingXian 169 wheat and screened for the differentially expressed genes TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A [42]. qRT-PCR was conducted on TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A. The results revealed a significant upregulation of both genes in the germinating seeds (GS) condition. These results indicate that TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A positively regulate seed germination in Arabidopsis and rice and play an important role in the response to abiotic stress.

Materials and methods

Plant materials and growth conditions

The wheat variety "MingXian 169" was employed as the test subject in this study. Wheat was planted at the experimental station of Northwest A & F University in Yangling, Shaanxi, China (108.072°E, 34.295°N) under natural growing conditions. Tobacco (Nicotiana benthamiana L.) was planted at a temperature of 23 ℃ under a 16 h: 8 h light: dark growing cycle. Nipponbare rice seeds were germinated in a light incubator (16 h light at 28 ℃, 8 h dark at 25 ℃). Arabidopsis thaliana ecotype Columbia Col-0 was used as the wild-type (WT). Arabidopsis T-DNA insertion mutants tify3b (SALK_044508.42.00.x) and tify10a (SALK_011957.54.50.x) were obtained from the Arabidopsis Biological Resource Center. The plants were planted under 16 h: 8 h light: dark conditions at 22 ℃.

Identification of differentially expressed genes TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A, related to germination

Transcriptome sequencing of germinated and dormant seeds from MingXian 169 wheat was conducted using the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) database (BioProject accession number PRJNA638000). The highly differentially expressed genes, TaTIFY3B (TraesCS2A02G506500.1) and TaTIFY10A (TraesCS2A02G169300.1) were identified in the germinated seeds. Basic information on TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A, including chromosomal position, coding region length and amino acid sequence, was obtained from the Wheat Genome Database (IWGSC). TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A protein sequences were subjected to a BLAST comparative search and conserved domain prediction using the NCBI website. The physical and chemical properties, signal peptides, phosphorylation sites, transmembrane domains, and secondary and tertiary structures of TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A proteins were predicted using other online websites (Table S1).

Phylogenetic and domain analysis of TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A

The TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A amino acid sequences of wheat were downloaded from the Wheat Genome Database (IWGSC) and the TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A homologous protein sequences of the remaining 22 plants were downloaded from NCBI. The amino acid sequences of homologous proteins were compared using Clustal-W and the conserved domains were determined. A phylogenetic tree was constructed using MEGA6.0 (maximum likelihood method, Bootstrap = 1000). The plant accession numbers for these genes are listed in (Table S2).

Plasmid construction and subcellular localization

The coding regions of TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A were cloned from the seeds of MingXian 169 wheat into the plant expression vector PCEGFP to produce a fusion protein construct with green fluorescent protein (GFP). Gene-specific primers used are listed in Table S3. For co-localization studies, the recombinant plasmids TaTIFY3B-GFP, TaTIFY10A-GFP and the nuclear localization marker vector p2300-35S-H2B-mCherry-OCS were mixed in equal proportions with Agrobacterium and injected into vigorously growing tobacco leaves after standing for 3 h. The subcellular localization of GFP and p2300-35S-H2B-mCherry-OCS proteins was visualized using confocal laser-scanning microscopy. Images were obtained under an Olympus fluoview FV1000 confocal laser scanning microscope with 488 nm excitation wavelength, argon-ion laser excitation, 507 nm detection GFP.

Analysis of expression patterns of TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A

To verify the consistency of the qPCR and transcriptome data for TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A in MingXian 169 cultivated in natural conditions at Northwest A&F University (18–22 ℃/14–18 ℃, day/night), until 35 days after pollination. Seed was collected and stored at 20 ℃ in a dry and dark room for about 50 days. The seeds were surface-sterilized with 5% NaClO for 15 min, placed in sterile petri dishes (sterile filter paper soaked in sterile deionized water), and placed at (20/15 ℃, day/night) for swelling for 48 h before collecting germinated seeds and dormant seeds simultaneously, and then frozen and stored at -80 ℃ in liquid nitrogen.

The seeds were sampled at different developmental stages. The flowering stage of MingXian 169 was accurately recorded, and seeds of uniform size were selected 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, and 40 d after flowering, frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at − 80 ℃.To prepare the seed material for the different analyses, the seed embryos (> 30 per dish) were placed, with the ventral groove upward, on Petri dishes lined with sterilized filter paper to which 10 mL sterile water, 50 μm ABA and 50 μm JA were added, respectively, and the samples were obtained at intervals of 0, 2, 6, 12, 24, and 48 h from three replicates, which were cultivated in a dark incubator at 25 ℃. These samples were then quickly frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80 ℃.

Total RNA was isolated using TRIzol reagent and reverse-transcribed through PrimeScript ™ II 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Takara). The level of expression of TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A was measured via qRT-PCR (QuantStudio™ 3 Flex Real-Time PCR System) with the cDNA, TB Green™ Premix Ex Taq™ II (Tli RNaseH Plus), and the specific primers of TaTIFY3B, TaTIFY10A and TaActin (AB181991.1, the internal control) Three biological replicates were analyzed for each treatment. The gene-specific primers used are listed in Table S3.

Generation of TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A transgenic lines of Arabidopsis and rice

The entire coding region of TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A were cloned into the plant expression vector pCAMBIA1302 driving ubiquitin promoter for rice transformation and pGreenII-OE driving 35S promoter for Arabidopsis transformation, transformed into Agrobacterium tumefaciens EHA105 (for rice) and GV3101 (for Arabidopsis). Transformation experiments were performed by Wuhan Boyuan Biotechnology Co, Ltd. (Wuhan, China) and T1 transformation plants were obtained. Three transgenic lines with high expression were screened by qRT-PCR before the T1 generation. DNA extraction and PCR analysis were conducted on transgenic lines, and those verified as positive were harvested. Transgenic Arabidopsis plants were generated by the floral dipping method. Post-harvest, Arabidopsis seeds were subjected to selection using the LUYOR-3415RG dual-wavelength fluorescent protein excitation light source, wherein transgenic positive seeds exhibited red fluorescence. The positively identified seeds were subsequently dried at 37 °C for two days and then stored at room temperature with desiccant for one week prior to sowing. Upon germination, a 0.05% glufosinate ammonium solution was applied five days post-sowing to further delineate and validate transgenic positive plants. DNA extraction and PCR analysis were conducted on these plants, and those verified as positive were harvested. Continue with these steps until all the seeds of the subsequent plant generations are emitting red light, all of them have glyphosate resistance, and the PCR detection of the target gene is correct, which indicates that the plant has been selected to a pure line and can be subjected to subsequent phenotypic identification experiments. The homozygous transgenic plants were obtained from the third generation (T3). The Rice gene (OsActin LOC4333919) and Arabidopsis gene (Actin) were used as internal controls. The gene-specific primers used are listed in Table S3.

Germination analysis

Nipponbare seeds harvested simultaneously as the seeds of the three overexpression-positive lines were steeped in 5% H2O2 for one day, rinsed three times in sterile water, and soaked in sterile water for one day to complete imbibition. After imbibition, TaTIFY3B (OE-1, OE-8, and OE-10) and TaTIFY10A (OE-2, OE-3, and OE-10) overexpressing rice seeds were placed in a Petri dish lined with filter paper. Nipponbare was used as a control, and sterile water, 20 μM ABA, and 50 μM JA were added. Sterilized seeds of wild-type Arabidopsis (WT), transgenic lines (OE-1, OE-2), tify3 and tify10 mutant were sown on 1/2MS medium containing 1/2MS + ABA (0.25, 0.5, and 0.75 μM), 1/2MS + JA (10, 20, and 50 μM), NaCl (100, 150, and 200 mM) and mannitol (100, 200, and 300 mM). The 1/2MS medium alone was used as a control. The germination rate of the first three days' germination rate was calculated, and at least 30 grains were placed in each treatment, which was performed three times.

qRT-PCR analysis of ABA signaling pathway gene ABI5 expression

The seeds of three overexpression-positive rice plants were treated with 20 μM ABA, and the recipient Nipponbare was used as the control. WT seeds, tify3, tify10 mutant seeds and mixed seeds of the two transgenic lines (OE) were treated with 0.75 μM ABA. Samples were collected after 0, 2, and 6 h, and total RNA was extracted using the TRIzol method and reverse-transcribed. OsActin and Actin were used as internal references to detect the relative expression changes in the ABI5 gene in the ABA signaling pathway. Three independent experiments were performed and similar results were obtained. The gene-specific primers used are listed in Table S3.

Detection of physiological parameters in rice

In this experiment, the 3,5-dinitrosalicylic acid method was used to determine the change of α-amylase activity in the seeds [43]. Samples of rice seeds that had been germinated for 0, 1, 2, and 3 days were treated with sterile water (control), ABA, and MeJA, and then 1 g of each sample was taken. 1 mL of extraction buffer was added, along with a small amount of quartz sand, and the mixture was ground to a homogenate. The homogenate was centrifuged at 4,000 rpm for 10 min. The supernatant was then diluted to a final volume of 25 mL. The crude enzyme extract was heated at 70 °C for 15 min. Then, 1 mL of the crude enzyme extract was mixed with 1 mL of soluble starch dissolved in a pH 5.6 sodium acetate buffer. The mixture was incubated at 40 °C for 15 min, and then boiled for 5 min in the presence of 2 mL of 3,5-dinitrosalicylic acid. The amount of reduced sugar released was measured at 540 nm using a UV–vis spectrophotometer (UV-2100, Unico Instrument Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). One unit of α-amylase activity was defined as the enzyme amount that produced 1 μM maltose per minute under enzyme activity conditions. The soluble sugar content of the seeds was measured via the anthrone colorimetric method [44]. A total of 0.2 g of rice seeds were weighed, and 2 mL of distilled water were added. The mixture was ground into a slurry and boiled in a water bath for 10 min. The mixture was then centrifuged at 4,000 rpm for 10 min after cooling. The supernatant was extracted twice, and the mixture was added to anthrone and sulfuric acid. The mixture was boiled in a water bath for 10 min. The absorbance value was measured at 620 nm. The soluble sugar content was calculated based on the standard curve. Six independent experiments were performed, and similar results were obtained. The standard curve is shown in Fig. S1.

Data analysis

Data were entered into an Excel database, and statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism (GraphPad Prism 8.0.2; GraphPad Software). Figures were created using GraphPad Prism (GraphPad Prism 8.0.2; GraphPad Software).

Results

Identification of TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A in wheat

This study analyzed the transcriptome data (NCBI accession number: PRJNA638000) of Mingxian 169 wheat germinating seeds (GS) and dormant seeds (DS) from previous research, and identified the differentially expressed genes TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A. qRT-PCR was conducted on TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A. The results revealed a significant upregulation of both genes in the GS condition (Fig. S2). Using the TaTIFY10A and TaTIFY3B amino acid sequences, BLAST searches were conducted in the NCBI database. As a result, 44 homologous proteins were identified from 22 species. Subsequent phylogenetic analysis revealed that plant TIFY proteins are primarily divided into two categories: monocotyledonous/dicotyledonous. In monocotyledonous plants, TaTIFY10A and TaTIFY3B form a cluster with homologous proteins of close relatives of wheat, but are more distantly related to homologous proteins from maize, rice, and sorghum. Furthermore, homologous proteins from the same family tended to cluster in the dicotyledonous (Fig. S3a).

Furthermore, sequence alignment revealed that TaTIFY10A and TaTIFY3B possess the necessary TIFY domain (PF06200), which is a typical feature of other known TIFY proteins. Additionally, all TIFY proteins shared two highly conserved motifs, the TIFY motif (TIF [F/Y] XG) and the JAS motif (SLXRFLEKRXDRX5PY) (Fig. S3b), which are integral components of the JAZ subfamily.

Transient expression in tobacco verified the specific localization of TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A. The results showed that the fluorescence of TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A could be observed in the nucleus, coinciding with the red fluorescence of nucleus localization, emitting yellow fluorescence. In addition to the fluorescence observed in the nucleus, there was a fluorescent signal in the cell membrane, indicating that TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A were localized to the nucleus and may be located in the cell membrane (Fig. S3c). This suggests that TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A act as a nuclear protein that may function as a transcriptional regulator.

Expression patterns of TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A at different stages of seed development, induced by H2O, ABA, and JA

To further understand the roles of TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A in seed development, we first determined the expression level of TaTIFY3B at different time points after anthesis. The expression levels of TaTIFY3B differed in each period, showing a trend of first increasing and then decreasing. The maximum TaTIFY3B expression was observed 15 days after flowering. 40 days after flowering, the expression level of TaTIFY3B dramatically decreased to half of its original value at day 10 (Fig. 1a). The maximum level of TaTIFY10A expression was observed 15 days after flowering, after which a declining trend in expression was observed. By the 40th day after anthesis, the expression of TaTIFY10A had dramatically decreased to almost one-tenth of that on the 10th day (Fig. 1b). TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A are involved in the germination of transgenic rice seeds

TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A from wheat were overexpressed to elucidate how TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A contribute to seed germination in rice. Hyg primer sets were used to detect rice plants with TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A overexpression 20 positive plants were obtained. Three TaTIFY3B overexpression-positive plants (OE-1, OE-8, and OE-10) and three TaTIFY10A overexpression-positive plants (OE-2, OE-3, and OE-10) were randomly selected, and the expression levels of TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A in the overexpression lines were analyzed using qRT-PCR (Fig. S4). Studies have shown that both TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A are highly expressed in the overexpressing lines.

In the germination test, seeds from the T1 generation of rice, including receptor materials Nipponbare rice and TaTIFY10A overexpressed rice, were placed in a Petri plate. On the first day of seed germination, the germination rate of the Nipponbare receptor was 60%, compared to 72.4%, 72.4%, and 71.1% in the TaTIFY10A overexpressed rice and 73.3%, 77.1%, and 77% in the TaTIFY3B overexpressed. Following 20 μM ABA treatment, the expression of ABI5 in Nipponbare and overexpressing rice seeds was induced to varying degrees. After 6 h, the ABI5 had the highest level of expression, and TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A overexpressed rice had significantly lower levels of ABI5 induction than the recipient Nipponbare (Fig. 4a, b). Variations in α-amylase activity and soluble sugar content in rice seeds under various treatments

Variations in α-amylase activity in overexpressed rice and recipient Nipponbare seeds during germination were investigated to learn more about the function of the TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A genes in seed germination. Under water treatment, TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A overexpressed rice seeds had higher α-amylase activity than did recipient Nipponbare (Fig. S5a, e). The level of α-amylase activity in rice seeds under ABA treatment was less than with plants under water treatment (control), but the activity of α-amylase in overexpressed rice seeds was always higher than that of the recipient Nipponbare (Fig. S5b, f).

The soluble sugar content of rice seeds under different treatments was determined. The soluble sugar content and α-amylase activity showed similar trends. The soluble sugar content of TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A overexpressed rice seedlings was substantially higher than that of Nipponbare seedlings after germination (Fig. S5c, g, d, and h). These results suggested that both TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A may be positive regulators of seed germination.

Root length changes of TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A overexpressing Arabidopsis under ABA and JA treatments

We tested whether TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A genes increased plant root growth by measuring the root growth of WT, TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A overexpressing Arabidopsis, as well as tify3b and tify10a mutants, under various hormone conditions (Fig. S7). On 1/2MS solid medium, there was no difference in the root length of each line. Under ABA treatment (50 μM), the root length of TaTIFY10A overexpressing Arabidopsis was significantly greater than that of WT and tify10a plants, indicating that the TaTIFY10A gene is less sensitive to ABA (Fig. S7f). The root length of each Arabidopsis line was suppressed by JA (50 μM), although there were no discernible variations in the root lengths of each line(Fig. S7i). However, the root length of Arabidopsis Thaliana overexpressing TaTIFY3B did not differ from that of the WT or tify3b plants under ABA (50 μM) treatment (Fig. S7a). Each Arabidopsis line was severely inhibited by JA (50 μM), and TaTIFY3B overexpressing Arabidopsis had much longer roots than WT and tify3b plants; however, there was no discernible difference between the WT and tify3b (Fig. S7d).

Root length changes of TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A overexpressing Arabidopsis under salt and drought treatments

We tested whether TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A genes increased plant root growth by measuring the root growth of WT, TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A overexpressing Arabidopsis, as well as tify3b and tify10a mutants, under salt and drought treatments (Fig. S8). Discussion

Controlling seed germination is of great significance in crop management and includes analyzing the molecular mechanisms underlying the regulation of seed germination. Many genes are involved in the regulation of seed germination in crops; in particular, TamiR9678 is involved in the regulation of wheat PHS and seed germination by producing phase-specific siRNAs that regulate the ABA/GA ratio [45]. OsFbx352 plays a regulatory role in the glucose-induced inhibition of seed germination by targeting ABA metabolism [4]. GmSMXLs regulate soybean seed germination under both salinity and drought stress [5]. OsNCED3 is closely associated with rice PHS and significantly increases the resistance to PHS, grain size, and weight [46]. The phenotypes of the Arabidopsis mutant lines were not clear, possibly because the TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A homologs in Arabidopsis play distinct roles. However, this needs to be confirmed through further tests. In this study, we found that the JA signaling pathway genes TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A play a role in promoting seed germination.

Analysis of TIFY family members

TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A are members of the TIFY gene family, which can be divided into four subfamilies with different structural features: TIFY, JAZ, PPD, and ZML. In addition to the TIF [F/Y] XG domain, JAZ contains a conserved JAS domain. In chrysanthemum plants, overexpressing CmJAZ1-like with a JAS domain deletion resulted in late flowering [35]. Overexpression of SlJAZ2 in tomatoes can accelerate plant leaf growth, reduce plant height and internode length, reduce trichomes, and germinate earlier; thus, the flowering period of the plant can be advanced, and the genes regulating flowering time can be significantly up-regulated. SlJAZ2 accelerates the transition from vegetative to reproductive development [47]. In this study, TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A were overexpressed in the dicotyledonous model plant Arabidopsis. The flowering time of the plants was advanced, whereas that of the mutant plants was delayed (Fig. 5, a and b). It was further demonstrated that the TIFY family of transcription factors promotes early flowering of plants and shortens the time from vegetative to reproductive growth.

Previous studies have shown that the overexpression of the TIFY family gene AtTIFY1 contributes to the elongation of Arabidopsis petioles and hypocotyls [48]. AtTIFY4a (PPD1) and AtTIFY4b (PPD2) promote leaf growth by controlling the leaf size and shape [49]. After JA treatment, there was no significant difference in the seed germination rate, root length, or bud length of rice overexpressing TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A compared to that of the recipient Nipponbare. However, JA also promoted the growth of rice leaves (Fig. 3, d and h). This indicates that the TIFY family of transcription factors plays an important role in promoting hypocotyl development and leaf growth.

Under salt stress, the germination rate and root length of TaTIFY3B overexpression Arabidopsis thaliana were higher than those of the WT and tify3b, but the germination rate of TaTIFY3B overexpression Arabidopsis thaliana seeds was significantly lower than that of the WT and tify3b at higher salt concentrations, suggesting that the TIFY family transcription factor plays an important role in responding to drought and salt stress. This has been further confirmed in studies by Ebel et al. [50], Zhang et al. [51], and Liu et al. [52], but the specific regulatory mechanism still needs further research.

Germination phenotypes of TaTIFY3B, TaTIFY10A overexpressed in Arabidopsis, and rice

Previous studies have found that different JAZ proteins may be involved in regulating different physiological and biochemical functions. In addition, the intron exon structure of each TaJAZ protein and their expression levels in different developmental stages of wheat were not exactly the same, which indicated that there were differences among the members [53].

Overexpression of wheat TaTIFY3B in rice, also a monocotyledonous grass crop (Fig. 2a), was found to promote rice seed germination. In this study, it was overexpressed in the dicotyledonous model plant Arabidopsis thaliana, and the function of TaTIFY3B in Arabidopsis thaliana T-DNA insertion mutant tify3b was studied. In this study, it was found that the mutant phenotype of Arabidopsis thaliana was not obvious, which may result from the different roles of homologous genes of TaTIFY3B in Arabidopsis thaliana, which needs to be further verified by experiments. The overexpression of TaTIFY10A accelerated the germination rate of rice seeds and had the effect of promoting seed germination. Under different hormonal and abiotic stresses, the germination rate of Arabidopsis thaliana overexpressing TaTIFY10A was higher than that of WT type Arabidopsis thaliana and tify10a, further confirming that TaTIFY10A promotes seed germination and is functionally conserved in dicotyledonous and monocotyledonous plants.

TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A may synergistically regulate seed germination through their interaction with ABI5

The phytohormone abscisic acid (ABA) plays an important role in the induction and maintenance of dormancy and has a negative role in seed germination. Previous studies have shown that wheat seeds that overexpress TaJAZ1 are insensitive to ABA. TaJAZ1 is a negative regulator of the ABA signaling pathway and regulates PHS and seed germination in wheat by interacting with the ABA signaling pathway gene ABI5 [37]. Furthermore, the JAZ–ABI3/ABI5 module integrates the JA and ABA signaling pathways and is involved in the regulation of seed germination [37, 39, 54]. We found that seeds overexpressing TaTIFY10A in the dicotyledonous model plant Arabidopsis were more efficient than those in WT and tify10a plants under ABA treatment. Expression of the ABA signaling pathway gene AtABI5 decreased (Fig. 10, a and b). The germination rate of rice seeds overexpressing TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A was higher than that of the recipient Nipponbare in the monocotyledonous model rice. The induction of OsABI5 was also reduced after ABA treatment (Fig. 4a and b). TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A may have play negative regulatory roles in the ABA signaling pathway. However, the role of the crosstalk between the ABA and JA signaling pathways in the regulation of seed germination in plants needs to be further explored. However, the molecular mechanisms of TaTIFY3B, TaTIFY10A and ABI5 require further experimental verification.

From these results, we conclude that TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A are essential for seed germination, may have negative regulatory functions in the ABA signaling pathway, and function similarly in monocotyledonous/dicotyledonous. This information could be used to improve MingXian 169 and other crops with strong dormancy and delayed agricultural production.

Conclusions

Previous research in our laboratory revealed that the differentially expressed JAZ family member genes TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A were screened by analyzing the transcriptome data of germinated and dormant seeds of MingXian 169 wheat. In this study, using MingXian 169 as the experimental material, TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A were isolated and cloned. Through bioinformatics, subcellular localization, expression pattern, and phenotypic analysis of Arabidopsis tify3b and tify10a mutants, overexpressing TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A in Arabidopsis and rice, the functions of TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A in the regulation of seed germination, were preliminarily verified. Additionally, TaTIFY3B and TaTIFY10A have a significant impact on abiotic stress. This study provides a reference for improving wheat varieties with strong dormancy such as MingXian 169.

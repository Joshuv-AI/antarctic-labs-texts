Reply letter to comments on: Impact of age and sex on the efficacy and safety of ramucirumab plus paclitaxel as switch maintenance versus continuation of first-line oxaliplatin-based chemotherapy: a subgroup analysis of the ARMANI phase III trial
Authors: R Fazio, G Randon, F Pietrantonio
Published: 2025

We thank Dr. Bolek and Dr. Kubilay Tolunay for their interest in this subgroup analysis of the ARMANI trial.

We acknowledge that the ARMANI trial does not provide a direct comparison of early introduction versus sequential paclitaxel plus ramucirumab. A relevant proportion of patients with metastatic gastric cancer, however, are non-eligible for second-line treatment due to the morbidity associated with disease progression to the first-line regimen. This issue is reflected in the ARMANI trial where only 46% of patients in the control arm received paclitaxel plus ramucirumab as second-line therapy. Since eligibility to second-line therapy may be largely unpredictable at baseline, the ARMANI trial showed that paclitaxel plus ramucirumab switch maintenance allowed a broader exposure to all the active regimens for metastatic disease. This strategy led to an improvement in progression-free survival (PFS) as well as a sustained benefit across subsequent treatment lines as reflected by the improvement in both PFS-2 (defined as the time from randomization to progression during the subsequent line of therapy or death) and overall survival,1 regardless of age and sex.2

Then, quality of life (QoL) was assessed at randomization and every 8 weeks until progression. At the first 8-week assessment, however, a significant improvement in global QoL was observed for patients receiving paclitaxel plus ramucirumab compared with those continuing with FOLFOX or CAPOX. The switch maintenance arm still showed an advantage in European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer Quality of Life Questionnaire-Core 30 (EORTC QLQ-C30) scores across multiple domains and symptoms at subsequent time points. These differences did not reach statistical significance, likely due to the limited number of patients that completed the questionnaires at subsequent time points. At the time of disease progression, a comparable worsening in global QoL scores was observed in arms A and B.3

The improvement in patients’ QoL despite a higher incidence of grade 3 or worse treatment-related adverse events may be due to the fact that these were mostly ramucirumab-specific adverse events (such as arterial hypertension), or neutropenia with no impact on febrile neutropenia.1 The improvement in QoL and the delayed time to QoL deterioration, however, may be largely influenced by the longer disease control in the experimental arm.

We acknowledge that cumulative toxicities (such as those related to oxaliplatin) may affect QoL beyond the 8-week assessment. However, FOLFOX or CAPOX in the trial were given at the same dose of the last induction cycle, and previous dose reductions of oxaliplatin-based chemotherapy might have attenuated the toxicity burden associated with this regimen. Moreover, oxaliplatin was delivered for up to an additional 3 months in the control arm and fluoropyrimidine maintenance was planned. Lastly, treatment duration in the control arm was relatively short, with a median PFS inferior to 4 months.1 Overall, these data suggest that, even if cumulative toxicities may play a role at the individual patient level, the QoL gain mostly reflects the PFS benefit in the experimental arm with an overall manageable safety profile.

The results of this subgroup analysis are particularly relevant since global QoL and time to deterioration were improved in the experimental arm regardless of age and sex, reflecting the PFS advantage and the manageable safety profile.2

The Management of ER-Low, HER2-Negative Breast Cancer in the United Kingdom: A Survey of Current Clinical Practice
Authors: Ka Chai Sit, Alison M. Berner, Conor McCahill, Sarah Atkinson, Melissa M. Phillips, Kathryn Hawkesford, Vasileios Angelis, Peter E. Hall
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionThe optimal management of oestrogen receptor (ER)-low HER2-negative breast cancer (BC) is unclear. We surveyed current approaches of UK clinicians in ER-low BC, and also present outcomes of ER-low early BC at single centre.MethodsSurvey: We conducted an anonymised online survey of clinicians treating BC in the UK between 6 May and 5 June 2024. Participants were presented with clinical scenarios of ER-low (Allred ER3/ER4) BC with multiple-choice and free-text questions. Clinical audit: Patients diagnosed with stage I–III, ER2–4 HER2-negative BC undergoing neoadjuvant treatment (chemotherapy vs. chemo-immunotherapy) from 1 January 2017 to 31 December 2023 were included. Clinical data was collected retrospectively at Barts Health NHS Trust, London, UK. The primary endpoint was pathological complete response (pCR).ResultsSurvey: Of 72 survey respondents, 40% recommended neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy for ER3 disease vs. 10% for ER4 disease; 57% recommended adjuvant endocrine therapy for ER3 disease, and 90% for ER4 disease. In the advanced setting 75% recommended chemo-immunotherapy for PD-L1-positive ER3 disease with 7% recommending CDK4/6 inhibitors. For PDL1-positive ER4 disease, these percentages were 32% and 51% respectively. In PDL1-negative disease, chemotherapy rates were 81% for ER3 and 36% for ER4 disease. Clinical audit: Sixty-six patients receiving neoadjuvant treatment were included, with 29 belonging to the ER2, 30 belonging to the ER3 and 7 belonging to the ER4 groups respectively; 33% received neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy. pCR rate was 61%, with no association with ER score for chemotherapy (p = 0.319) and chemo-immunotherapy (p = 0.603).ConclusionsThere is marked variation in approach to ER-low in the UK. The audit data suggests treating ER-low BC as triple-negative BC.

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) is the most diagnosed cancer among women and is the leading cause of cancer death [1]. In 2021, the global age-standardised incidence rate of BC was 46.40 per 100,000 women, and the global age-standardised death rate was 14.55 per 100,000 women. These statistics vary substantially according to geographical region and socio-demographic index [2]. Current treatment strategies are designed to target specific molecular subtypes of BC which are determined by percentage expression of the oestrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR) and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2; also known as ERBB2) on immunohistochemistry (IHC), and in situ hybridisation for HER2. Patients with tumours which express ER and/or PR (regardless of HER2 expression) are considered hormone-receptor (HR)-positive, while those without expression of ER, PR and HER2 are classed as triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) [3]. In the UK, the Allred (quick) score is used to report ER and PR status, combining a score for percentage of positive cells with a score for intensity of staining to give an overall score ranging from 0 to 8 [4]. For HER2-negative BC, the 2010 American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) and College of American Pathologists (CAP) guidelines recommend that a tumour be considered ER-positive if IHC staining is above 1% (ER3–8), while those below (Allred ER0–2) are classed as TNBC [5].

BC treatment is mainly determined by tumour stage and receptor status. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy may be used in BC to downstage tumours, reduce tumour recurrence rates and facilitate breast-conserving surgery (BCS) instead of mastectomy for patients at high risk [6]. Pathological complete response (pCR) is used as a surrogate measure of neoadjuvant treatment effect as it has been shown to correlate with survival outcomes, particularly in HER2-positive early BC and TNBC [7]. The generally used definition of pCR is the complete eradication of invasive cancer after neoadjuvant treatment as determined in the surgical specimen [8]. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy with an anthracycline and taxane-containing regimen is the backbone of treatment for patients with TNBC who are node-positive or have at least T1c disease. Additionally, neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy has also been shown to increase pCR likelihood for patients with TNBC with T2+ or node-positive disease compared to chemotherapy alone.

Recent studies have identified a new subgroup of patients that are weakly ER-positive HER2-negative but have similar biological and clinical characteristics to TNBC, termed ER-low BC [9]. This led to the 2020 ASCO/CAP recommendation to consider tumours as ER-low if 1–10% of cells stain ER-positive on IHC [10]. This usually corresponds to an Allred score 3 but can also include ER4 and, rarely, ER5 depending on the percentage of cells positive and the intensity of the staining. The optimal treatment strategy for this subgroup both in the early and metastatic settings has yet to be defined [11]. A study found that ER-low tumours had similar tumour size, necrosis, stromal tumour infiltrating lymphocytes (sTILs), pathological nodal stage and Ki67 expression compared to TNBC. These parameters were all significantly higher for ER-low versus ER-positive tumours, highlighting ER-low tumours as a distinct molecular subgroup [12]. Additionally, another study revealed similar pCR rates between the ER-low and TNBC groups at 24.8% vs. 30.8% (OR 1.37, CI 0.83–2.22, p = 0.08) [13]. Patients with low ER expression had higher pCR rates vs. ER-positive patients at 24.8% vs. 8.3% (OR 3.25, CI 1.85–5.71, p = 0.002). ER-low and patients with TNBC had a similar disease-free survival (DFS; HR 1.09, CI 0.93–1.26, p = 0.40) and overall survival (OS) (HR 1.16, CI 0.98–1.38, p = 0.82), but had significantly worse DFS (HR 1.85, CI 1.35–2.54, p < 0.0001) and OS (HR 2.36, CI 1.35–3.86, p < 0.00001) compared to patients who were ER-positive [13].

Taken together, patients with low ER expression may benefit from the same treatment pathway as patients with TNBC, raising the question of whether the addition of immunotherapy to neoadjuvant chemotherapy is beneficial. This view has been supported by recent subgroup analyses of the KEYNOTE-756 and CheckMate 7FL trials which investigated the addition of pembrolizumab to EC/AC-taxane neoadjuvant chemotherapy in patients with ER-positive HER2-negative BC [14–16]. These showed that it was the ER-low (1–10%) patients who benefited most from the addition of immunotherapy.

The current clinical practice for patients with low ER expression is variable between treating as ER-positive disease or TNBC. To further characterise the current treatment of ER-low tumours, we conducted a national survey of UK BC clinicians on their treatment of various patients with low ER expression scenarios in both the early and metastatic setting, and present clinical data representing our single-centre experience of treating ER-low early BC.

Methods

Survey

An electronic survey including multiple choice and free-text was circulated through the UK Breast Cancer Group (UKBCG), and the National Institute for Health and Care Research’s (NIHR) Biomedical Research Centre (BRC) Oncology Translational Research Collaboration (O-TRC) Immunotherapy workstream. Respondents were hospital-based doctors in the UK involved in treating patients with BC. Table 1 shows an overview of the questionnaire. Four patient scenarios and related sub-questions were included, consisting of neoadjuvant management for ER3 and patients with ER4 with T2+, HER2-negative tumours and variable nodal status, and management for patients with ER3 and ER4, PD-L1-positive and negative metastatic cancer. The Allred scores of ER3 and ER4 were chosen as these correspond most closely to the ASCO/CAP ER-low category (1–10%) and, anecdotally, there were differing practices between ER3 and ER4 in the UK. The ER-low category was included as a subgroup analysis of the KEYNOTE-756 and the CheckMate 7FL trials. Responses were collected from 6 May to 5 June 2024. Responses of ER and PR scores considered positive were grouped by Allred score, with conversion of responses citing percentage-expression for four respondents. Neoadjuvant regimen choices were divided into three groups: EC/AC-T, EC/AC-T with carboplatin (EC/AC-TC) and EC/AC-TC with pembrolizumab (KN522). Free-text answers were converted to groups by consensus between the authors KCS, AMB and PEH. Multiple responses from the same respondent were counted separately, and missing answers were included in the analysis and termed “not answered (NA)”.

Clinical Audit

All data was retrospectively collected from electronic health records as part of a clinical audit of neoadjuvant treatment response. Patients diagnosed with stage I–III BC and ER2, ER3 or ER4, HER2-negative treated with neoadjuvant chemotherapy or chemo-immunotherapy at St. Bartholomew’s Hospital, London, UK between 1 January 2017 and 1 August 2024 were included. Patients who had yet to undergo surgery or who were enrolled in neoadjuvant clinical trials were excluded. The study included patients with ER2 as a control comparator to the TNBC (ER < 1%) subgroup, with ER3 and patients with ER4 included as the ER-low (ER 1–10%) subgroup. This is in-line with the ASCO/CAP, KEYNOTE-756 and CheckMate 7FL definitions of these patients [4, 5, 16, 17].

The primary endpoint measured was pCR, defined as absence of invasive cancer in the breast and locoregional lymph nodes at the time of definitive surgery after treatment with neoadjuvant chemotherapy or chemo-immunotherapy (ypT0/Tis, ypN0).

Data analysis was performed with IBM SPSS v29.0. Differences in baseline characteristics were calculated using analysis of variance (ANOVA) for age and Fisher’s exact test for all other parameters. pCR rate difference was analysed using binary logistic regression for age vs. pCR, and Fisher’s exact test for all other parameters. Significance was assumed at p < 0.05.

The audit was approved by the Clinical Effectiveness Unit (CEU) at Barts Health NHS Trust, London, UK.

The survey was deemed exempt from ethics approval based on the UK Health Research Authority (HRA) decision tool.

Participants of the survey were informed of its aim before participation and provided their responses freely. Participants could also discontinue their participation at any time. Survey answers were anonymous, and no personal data were collected or held from participants.

Results

Survey

The total respondent population was 72 physicians, including 30 medical oncologists, 32 clinical oncologists, 3 specialty trainees/clinical fellows, 1 speciality and associate specialist (SAS) doctor and 1 surgeon. Although data were not collected on the practice setting, this is likely to span academic and district general hospitals based on the UKBCG membership. Potential factors influencing the choice of neoadjuvant regimen were examined. For both the patients with ER3 and ER4, nodal status (38.9% and 44.4% of respondents) and intrinsic subtype were the main factors (41.7% for both). Ki67 was the parameter that least-affected decision-making at 18.1% (ER3) and 19.4% (ER4) of respondents. PR status (37.5% and 23.6%) and tumour size (38.9% and 33.3%) had an intermediate degree of influence.

Conversion of regimen rationales yielded 77 and 59 answers for the ER3 and ER4 scenarios respectively. Twelve (16.7%) and 20 (27.8%) respondents did not provide a rationale for their chosen ER3 and ER4 regimens respectively. The top rationales for each of the three regimens were the same for both the ER3 and ER4 scenarios. The majority reason for prescribing EC/AC-T was that the patient was being treated as ER-positive or that this patient would not be considered TNBC. The top rationale for EC/AC-TC was that this patient was not eligible for the KEYNOTE-522 trial or that they would not be able to access funding for immunotherapy. Treating the patient as TNBC was the majority rationale for respondents prescribing KN522 and was cited 10 times for respondents prescribing EC/AC-TC.

For patients with metastatic ER3 PD-L1-positive disease requiring first-line treatment, 16.7% would prescribe CDK4/6 inhibitor plus ET, 8.3% would prescribe chemotherapy alone and 75.0% would prescribe chemo-immunotherapy. In the equivalent ER4 scenario, these numbers were at 53.5%, 12.7% and 33.8% respectively. For patients with metastatic ER3, PD-L1-negative disease requiring first-line treatment, 18.3% would prescribe CDK4/6 inhibitor plus ET and 81.7% would prescribe chemotherapy alone, which were at 62.0% and 38.0% respectively for the equivalent ER4 scenario. For both metastatic PD-L1-positive scenarios, PR status and intrinsic subtype affected the decisions of 29.2% and 47.2% of respondents respectively. These numbers were at 30.6% and 48.6% respectively for metastatic PD-L1-negative scenarios.

pCR Rate

The pCR rate across all groups was 60.6%. No association was found between pCR rates and age (p = 0.877), PR score (p = 0.713), positive nodal status (p = 0.789), and early termination of neoadjuvant treatment (p = 0.146). Stage at diagnosis was associated with pCR. All eight patients presenting with stage I disease at diagnosis achieved pCR compared to patients with stage II and III disease, at 57.1% and 44.4% respectively (p = 0.035). More patients who received neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy achieved pCR compared to those who only received chemotherapy (77.3% vs. 52.3%, p = 0.043). Increased pCR rate was associated with neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy vs. chemotherapy alone among patients at stage II–III (76.2% vs. 43.2%, p = 0.027).

In the ER2, ER3 and ER4 groups, 18 (62.1%), 16 (53.3%) and 6 (85.7%) patients achieved pCR respectively, and pCR rates were not associated with ER score (p = 0.319). Among patients at stage II–III, no association was found between pCR rates and ER score (56.0% vs. 46.2% vs. 85.7%, p = 0.184). pCR rates were not associated with ER score among patients who received neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy (78.6% vs. 60.0% vs. 100%, p = 0.606).

Discussion

Survey

In the survey of oncologists on their neoadjuvant management for patients with ER3 and ER4, a large variability in preferred neoadjuvant regimen was observed. The majority rationale for prescribing EC/AC-TC for the patient with ER3 was that this ER score was not eligible for enrolment in the KEYNOTE-522 study or that this patient would not be able to access immunotherapy funding, which both relate to the rationale that immunotherapy should be omitted because of lack of evidence in this patient group. KEYNOTE-756 was a phase III study of neoadjuvant pembrolizumab with epirubicin plus cyclophosphamide (EC) or doxorubicin plus cyclophosphamide (AC) with paclitaxel (T) versus placebo plus EC/AC-T for patients with high-risk, ER-positive HER2-negative cancer [14]. Analysis demonstrated a pCR benefit with the addition of pembrolizumab in the intention-to-treat (ITT) group (24.3% vs. 15.6%, p = 0.00005). In patients with PD-L1 combined positive score (CPS) ≥ 1, the ER < 10% subgroup demonstrated a pCR rate difference between chemotherapy versus chemo-immunotherapy of 24.2% while the ER ≥ 10% subgroup had a pCR rate difference of only 9.2%. This demonstrated that patients with ER-low tumours have increased benefit with addition of neoadjuvant pembrolizumab to chemotherapy compared to ER-intermediate and ER-high tumours, similar to TNBC [15]. CheckMate 7FL is another study investigating the addition of nivolumab to paclitaxel and AC in patients with ER-positive HER2-negative cancer. A pCR benefit of 10.7% (24.5% vs. 13.8%) was observed in the ITT population. Increasing PD-L1 CPS was associated with a larger treatment effect, and ER expression was also predictive of benefit. Patients with ER < 10% tumours had an increased pCR rate compared to those in the ER ≥ 10% group (27.0% vs. 9.2%) [16]. These studies showed that patients with low ER expression (ER 1–10% or Allred ER3/4) benefit most from the addition of immunotherapy. As the results from these studies may cause a change in treatment for patients with low ER expression, the survey includes both an ER3 and ER4 scenario to represent these subgroups.

There is currently a lack of data on treatment of patients with low ER expression with immunotherapy in the advanced setting.

A possible explanation for physicians citing lack of evidence is the delayed circulation of information, as this survey was conducted only around 4 months after the 2023 San Antonio Breast Cancer Symposium where the subgroup results of KEYNOTE-756 and CheckMate 7FL were presented. Nevertheless, the majority rationale for prescribing EC/AC-T for the patient with ER3 was that respondents were treating this disease as ER-positive, while the majority rationale for prescribing KN522 was that this patient was treated as TNBC. This suggests the primary rationale for prescribing either treatment pathway for the patient with ER3 is the distinction between ER-positive and TNBC. The majority rationale for prescribing EC/AC-T for the ER4 scenario cited that this patient is considered ER-positive/not considered TNBC which contrasts with the ER3 scenario where only 6/16 responses cited that the patient with ER3 was considered ER-positive, suggesting higher confidence of respondents in treating patients with ER4 as ER-positive. Similarly to the ER3 scenario, the majority rationale for prescribing EC/AC-TC to the patient with ER4 was ineligibility of the patient to the KEYNOTE-522 study population or lack of access to funding. This suggests that respondents consider the addition of platinum but omission of immunotherapy as a middle ground for patients with low ER expression. These results suggest that better biomarkers are required to identify patients with low ER expression who benefit from being treated with neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy on the TNBC pathway.

Adjuvant ET prescription was more likely for the ER4 scenario, specifically for ER4 patients receiving EC/AC-T and EC/AC-TC. Both KEYNOTE-756 and CheckMate 7FL mandated use of adjuvant ET in combination with adjuvant immunotherapy for all patients regardless of ER expression [14–16], but the survey results indicate that only 34.5% and 57.1% of respondents prescribing chemo-immunotherapy for the ER3 and ER4 scenarios respectively would offer adjuvant ET. This discrepancy between trial regimen and prescribed regimen may also be explained by the delay in circulation of information from these trials, as evidence suggests that patients with low ER expression benefit from both neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy and adjuvant immunotherapy and ET [18]. Ki67 had the least impact on decision-making for both scenarios, indicating the reluctance of clinicians to use Ki67 as a biomarker for treatment of patients with low ER expression.

Clinical Audit

Results from our clinical data mirrored studies which showed similarities in pCR rates between patients with ER-low and ER-negative (ER2) cancer. No association between pCR rates and ER score was found, indicating that ER2 (TNBC), patients with ER3 and ER4 (ER-low) have a similar benefit from neoadjuvant treatment. This is consistent with findings from studies by Paakkola et al. and Dieci et al. on pCR after neoadjuvant chemotherapy, where there were no significant differences in pCR rates between patients with TNBC and patients with low ER expression (38% vs. 44%, p = 0.498) [13, 19]. This study further demonstrates similarities in clinical behaviour between patients with TNBC and patients with low ER expression which may cause a similar response to neoadjuvant treatment. Among patients at stage II–III, our study also demonstrates similar overall pCR results compared to the KEYNOTE-522 study [20] (62.6% vs. 64.8%), as well as to the pCR rates demonstrated by the ER-low subgroups for KEYNOTE-756 (57.6%) [17] and CheckMate 7FL (55.6%) [21]. In contrast, the pCR rates for ITT populations in KEYNOTE-756 and CheckMate 7FL were 24.3% and 24.5%, respectively. These findings indicate that there is likely to be benefit to treating patients with ER-low BC with neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy.

Furthermore, all patients with ER4 treated with neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy achieved pCR in this study albeit it with small numbers involved. KEYNOTE-756 demonstrated that patients with ER-expression ≥ 10% also received benefit from the addition of pembrolizumab vs. placebo to neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy (27.6% vs. 19.4%, p value not reported) among the PD-L1 CPS ≥ 1 group [14]. CheckMate 7FL also demonstrated a similar benefit from chemo-immunotherapy for the ER ≤ 50% and ER ≤ 10% groups (Δ29.3% vs. Δ27.0%) [16]. These results suggest that the benefits of neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy may extend beyond ER-low tumours to those with higher ER expression, but further research and biomarkers are required to define cutoffs/patient subgroups.

A higher percentage of patients with ER4 received adjuvant ET than the ER2 and ER3 groups, which is consistent with the survey results where more respondents view ER4 BC as ER-positive than ER-negative. Inter- and intra-group variations between treatment regimens can be observed, with some ER3 and ER4 not receiving adjuvant ET. This demonstrates the wide range of treatments that patients with low ER expression may receive, and the lack of consensus on optimal treatment for these patients. Furthermore, although the results from our survey suggest that clinicians tend to treat ER3 disease using the TNBC pathway and ER4 disease using the ER-positive pathway, analysis of the efficacy of neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy in the ER ≥ 10% subgroup from KEYNOTE-756 and CheckMate 7FL [17, 21] suggests that patients with ER4 may also benefit from the addition of immunotherapy. This disparity between clinical practice and recent evidence may be explained by the timing of our survey which was circulated before the publication of these pivotal trial results and thus no subsequent updates to international clinical guidelines. As these treatment choices remain a local clinical decision, the role of neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy may continue to be a subject of ongoing discussion and research.

Neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy represents a significant advancement in improving outcomes for patients with BC both in the TNBC and hormone-positive groups. However, results from recent studies focusing on antibody–drug conjugates show potential for another novel treatment avenues when paired with immunotherapy. Particularly, the ASCENT-04/KEYNOTE-D19 study demonstrated an improvement in median PFS of 3.4 months for untreated PD-L1-positive advanced TNBC by using sacituzumab govitecan with pembrolizumab over chemotherapy with pembrolizumab [22]. This trial focused on advanced disease. Further studies are ongoing to elucidate the potential efficacy of antibody–drug conjugates with immunotherapy over chemotherapy with immunotherapy in the neoadjuvant setting.

Limitations

Limitations to the survey analysis include the relatively small respondent size and omission of ER-negative scenarios to serve as a baseline. Queries on the rationale of treatment decision were initially in free-text form, which required qualitative analysis and interpretation to be converted into categories, resulting in possible loss of information by misinterpretation. There was missing data in the form of unanswered queries particularly for treatment rationale.

The main limitation of the clinical study is the retrospective nature and the small sample size, particularly for patients with ER4. Data on patients with ER5+ disease was not collected.

Conclusion

Although patients with various BC subtypes are treated mainly according to IHC receptor expression, areas of uncertainty remain. The ER-low subgroup is a new entity where treatment is a grey area between ER-positive and TNBC where aspects of both treatments may be effective. The national survey highlights the differing practices across the UK to this subgroup and provides some insight into the reasons behind this. The clinical audit data from our centre provides further evidence suggesting that patients with ER-low BC may benefit from being treated according to TNBC regimens in the neoadjuvant setting. Further studies and biomarkers beyond the existing IHC cutoffs are required to identify which patients benefit from chemo-immunotherapy and endocrine therapy.

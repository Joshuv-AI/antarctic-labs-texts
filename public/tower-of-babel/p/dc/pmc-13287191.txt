Real-World Evidence for Drug Value Assessment in China: Learning from International Experiences
Authors: Abraham G. Hartzema, Adel Gehchan, Jennifer D. Guo, Jianwei Xuan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In China, the national medical insurance system has undergone rapid development over the past two decades. The National Healthcare Security Administration was established to manage the growing healthcare expenditure. Since then, this administration has maintained the drug formulary, known as the National Reimbursement Drug List. The list comprises Category A (essential and cost-effective drugs) and Category B (effective drugs with relatively higher cost profiles), with the Commercial Insurance Innovative Drug list (referred to as Category C) added in 2025. China has made progress in adopting real-world evidence into healthcare decision making. Real-world evidence helps address key uncertainties related to safety, effectiveness, and long-term outcomes to complement clinical trial findings using real-world data from routine clinical practice. Evolving international experiences provide practical insights into the use of real-world evidence to support drug reimbursement and reassessment decisions. This paper proposes practical strategies supporting the integration of real-world evidence into China’s drug value assessment to better inform national formulary management and reimbursement pathways.

Key Points

Introduction

In China, the national medical insurance system has undergone rapid development over the past two decades. Currently it covers 95% of the 1.4 billion population [1]. In 2018, the National Healthcare Security Administration (NHSA) was established to manage the growing healthcare expenditure in China (Table 1). Since then, the NHSA has maintained the drug formulary, known as the National Reimbursement Drug List (NRDL) [2]. The NRDL comprises Category A (essential and cost-effective drugs) and Category B (effective drugs with relatively higher cost profiles). Both categories are covered by the Basic Medical Insurance (BMI), also referred to as the national medical insurance [3]. In 2025, the Commercial Insurance Innovative Drug list (known as Category C) was launched to include drugs not eligible for BMI coverage but covered by Huiminbao schemes. Huiminbao is a commercial health insurance scheme under a collaborative governance structure between the local governments and insurance companies [3, 4]. Real-world evidence (RWE) helps address key uncertainties related to safety, effectiveness, and long-term outcomes to complement clinical trial findings using real-world data (RWD) from routine clinical practice. RWD consist of routinely collected information on patient health and healthcare delivery from diverse sources [6, 7]. RWE refers to the clinical evidence on a medical product’s use, benefits, and risks derived from the analysis of RWD [6, 7].

Considering the NRDL’s annual formulary update, particularly the launch of the Commercial Insurance Innovative Drug list in 2025, there is a growing need to integrate RWE into China’s evolving drug value assessment framework. The objective of this paper is to draw on international experiences illustrating how RWE can support reimbursement and reassessment decisions and to propose strategies for strengthening its use in drug formulary decisions in China.

Current Real-World Evidence Usage Status in China

As RWD have become increasingly available and used in China, RWE is playing a growing role in complementing clinical trial evidence during the drug lifecycle. The drug lifecycle is composed of the following phases: research and development, regulatory approval (pre-marketing/market launch), formulary inclusion (post-approval/post-marketing/post-launch/initial drug coverage), and formulary renewal (reassessment/inclusion/exclusion) [2, 8, 9].

Real-World Evidence Application and Challenges in China

Steady progress has been made in China to enhance the value of RWE in the healthcare landscape. As noted, RWE was integrated to support regulatory decisions, including using RWD from Boao Lecheng Pilot Zone in Hainan province. The uniqueness of the Boao data lies in their collection since 2019, from patients who have used medical products approved overseas but not yet approved in China. The glaucoma drainage tube was the first medical device approved using RWE based on Boao data [10]. In parallel, the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA), the regulatory body in China (Table 1), has published multiple RWE-relevant regulatory guidelines since 2020 [7]. At the RWD infrastructure level, China integrated the Yinzhou regional electronic health records (EHR) into the global Observational Health Data Sciences and Informatics (OHDSI) community. This was achieved through adoption of the Observational Medical Outcomes Partnership (OMOP) Common Data Model (CDM) [8]. In 2025, Beijing launched large-scale RWD projects incorporating artificial intelligence (AI) technology [11]. A multi-stakeholder External Advisory Committee was established to support China’s RWE efforts and enhance global collaboration [8].

Early RWE cases in China suggest that regulatory acceptance depends on the availability of fit-for-purpose RWD (relevance and reliability), data quality, transparent study design, and rigorous analytical methods [7]. Commonly used data sources offer broad population coverage and policy relevance, such as electronic medical records, medical insurance claims, hospital information systems, and disease registries. However, they are constrained by fragmentation, incomplete outpatient capture, limited longitudinal follow-up, and residual confounding. Addressing these limitations requires rigorous analytic approaches to ensure RWE validity [2, 7, 8, 12]. Overall, the main RWD challenges in China are maintaining data confidentiality, data relevance, data quality, and restricted access to data [2, 8]. Regarding RWE, the primary challenges are limited capacity to conduct methodologically rigorous studies and a lack of transparency in RWE interpretation. Only a small number of research groups have been trained in RWE research methods [2].

Real-World Evidence Supporting Payer Decisions in China

Insights are published on integrating RWE into formulary decisions across the drug lifecycle [2]. For initial drug coverage decisions, RWE from various RWD includes disease epidemiology, treatment costs, and healthcare resource use [2]. For example, RWE from Boao data supported national reimbursement for COSELA (a pre-chemotherapy drug) in 2024, building on evidence previously used for regulatory approval in 2023 [13]. In the formulary renewal phase, RWE can be used to track comparative effectiveness, long-term safety, treatment patterns, healthcare resource utilization, and medical costs [2].

Real-World Evidence Usage by International Health Technology Assessment Bodies and Payers

As China advances its use of RWE to affect policy decision making, international experiences with RWE in reimbursement decisions reveal key challenges and strategies to address them.

Usage and Challenges of Real-World Evidence Internationally

RWE has been used to inform decision making by HTA bodies and payers in the United States (US), Canada, and Europe [14, 15]. For example, a 2019–2022 review of National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) technology appraisal reports in the United Kingdom identified 20 cancer drugs that were initially granted conditional access through the Cancer Drugs Fund (CDF). In subsequent reassessments, RWE confirmed overall survival benefits and supported NICE’s decision to grant full access for 12 cancer drugs [16].

Challenges arise in the use of RWE. They generally fall into four areas: RWD (availability, governance, and quality); methodology (design and analytics); trust (transparency and reproducibility); policy and partnerships [17].

Published Strategies to Overcome Challenges of Using Real-World Evidence Internationally

Strategies to overcome challenges in using RWE during the HTA review process include data standardization, stakeholder training, cross-stakeholder collaboration, guideline development, and RWE study prioritization [18, 19].

Three major developments are highlighted. First, in 2022, the Institute for Clinical and Economic Review (ICER) in the US, functioning as an HTA-like body, shared learning from a reassessment pilot using RWE. The key learning includes selecting decision-relevant topics and assessing the study feasibility for RWE generation [9]. Second, in 2024, the Canadian Drug Agency (CDA) replaced the Canadian Agency for Drugs and Technologies in Health (CADTH), increasing Canadian data collection across the provinces; broadening access to drug and treatment data; and reducing duplication and coordination gaps in the drug system [20]. Third, in 2025, the Joint Clinical Assessment (JCA) was launched in the European Union. As a centralized comparative effectiveness mechanism, the HTA Coordination Group compares new health technologies with other new or existing technologies to accelerate patient access to new medicines [21].

Suggestions for Integrating Real-World Evidence into the Drug Value Assessment in China

To further enhance the impact of RWE on payers’ formulary decisions in China, based on international learning, we suggest the following strategies.

Strengthen Collaboration Between Regulators and Payers to Achieve Timely Access to New Medicines

To bridge the gap between pre-marketing and post-marketing assessment, and to ensure timely patient access to innovative drugs, a seamless connection is advocated between regulatory approval by the NMPA and formulary decisions by the NHSA. This suggestion is inspired by two recent efforts: the replacement of CADTH by CDA in Canada [20] and the centralized JCA mechanism launched in Europe [21]. These efforts reflect the efficient leveraging of RWE to enhance drug formulary decisions. Notably, the NHSA has expedited the review process for new drugs entering the drug formulary, primarily in oncology and rare diseases [2].

Prioritize Decision-Relevant Real-World Evidence to Sustain Formulary Reassessment

Prioritizing relevant RWE—based on whether the evidence is decision-relevant and whether the evidence generation is feasible—will support the sustainability of the formulary reassessments. Generating RWE to support the entire drug formulary update annually is a resource-intensive process. The RWE needs to address pre-identified uncertainties, such as safety, primary/secondary efficacy endpoints or other parameters relevant to reimbursement decision making. Meanwhile, RWE studies need to be feasible while maintaining scientific rigor to inform decision making. Evidence shows that the average time for additional data collection after the initial assessment is 2.7 years [19].

Encourage Real-World Data Access, While Leveraging Data Standards and Preserving Granularity

Standardizing RWD, also known as developing a CDM, enables efficient data management and analysis. However, a high level of national standardization may lead to the potential loss of crucial details and nuances in data. Therefore, preserving granular data with detailed and context-specific information is needed to balance RWD analysis. Moreover, rapidly evolving artificial intelligence (AI) technologies can help process original unstructured data [22]. However, they are not a substitute for established CDMs. CDMs remain essential for ensuring interoperability, reproducibility, and regulatory-grade data quality [18].

We suggest that the Chinese government fully leverage its national healthcare system—a core strategic advantage—to unlock healthcare data value and support evidence-based decisions. Data access restrictions remain a global bottleneck for healthcare analytics, including China [8, 17]. Such restrictions often hinder the generation of RWE, which in turn leaves critical decisions on reimbursement without robust analytical backing.

Enhance Real-World Evidence Capacity and Exchange Among Health Technology Assessment Bodies and Payers

To increase the credibility and transparency of RWE, the registration of RWE protocol on a public platform and the enhancement of methodological training are critical. The registration of RWE studies should be encouraged or mandated on public platforms such as the Chinese Clinical Trials Registry (ChiCTR) (https://www.chictr.org.cn/). Additionally, although RWE efforts are rapidly evolving, analytical capacity in China is limited [2]. As aforementioned, RWD challenges in data quality—including limited longitudinal follow-up, missing data, and incomplete outpatient capture—create challenges for generating valid RWE [2, 8, 12]. These heighten the need for advanced analytic methods. Therefore, continuous academic training and sustained collaboration with international experts are essential to strengthen methodological capacity and ensure high-quality RWE generation.

Moreover, firsthand knowledge exchange on the best use of RWE for formulary decisions from other established HTA bodies and payers is valuable, such as the CDA approach in Canada, and the JCA mechanism in Europe.

Empower Patients’ Engagement for Formulary Decisions

It is essential to solicit patients’ input for the drug formulary decision making, as HTA bodies in other developed countries do [23]. The patients’ voices should be heard during the formulary decision-making process, especially for high-cost drugs and/or drugs treating rare diseases. Their perspectives may help balance the high cost and unmet needs to better serve patients’ healthcare and welfare.

Conclusions

Integrating RWE into drug value assessment in China is a crucial step to guide national-level drug reimbursement decisions. It requires addressing domestic challenges in RWD and RWE analytical capacity. By drawing on lessons from developed countries, China can accelerate the development of a sustainable RWE ecosystem that enhances the scientific rigor while supporting equitable access to effective therapies.

Detection of avermectin pesticide residues in pine trees based on magnetic Solid-Phase extraction combined technology
Authors: Yi Wu, Jiaxi Ye, Yanxia Qi, Shaoyong Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo improve the detection accuracy and efficiency of avermectin pesticide residues in pine trees, this study develops an analysis method based on MSPE combined with high-performance liquid chromatography tandem mass spectrometry. A core–shell magnetic nanomaterial, Fe3O4-SiO2-NH2-Schiff-TAPB-DA, is synthesized by the solvothermal method. The Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR) analysis results confirmed its stable characteristic functional group structure (such as benzene ring, amide bond) and significant magnetic separation performance. Efficient adsorption of avermectin was achieved by utilizing the abundant benzene ring and amide bond sites on the material surface, and the solid-phase extraction pretreatment conditions were optimized. The results demonstrated that the target substance exhibited good linearity under optimal extraction conditions (R2 > 0.998). The matrix effects of standardized avermectin pesticides were all greater than 92%, and the Limit of Detection (LOD) for methomyl, avermectin, and tetracycline were 0.213, 0.185, and 0.209 mg L-1. The average recovery rate of some target substances was 73.2–85.6%. This study presents an efficient and environmentally friendly technical solution for the precise monitoring of avermectin residues in the prevention and control of pine wilt disease, providing a methodological reference for the multi-residue analysis of other forestry pesticides.Keywords: Avermectin, Pesticides, Magnetic nanomaterials, SEM, TEM, FTIRSubject terms: Drug safety, Drug screening, Medicinal chemistry

Introduction

Pine trees belong to the pine family, with a wide variety of species and needle-shaped leaves. They are important economic trees and ecological tree species in China, widely used in landscaping, wood production, and ecological restoration. However, pine trees are often affected by pine wood nematodes during their growth process. This highly harmful and destructive pathogen has the characteristics of a fast mortality rate, obvious wilting symptoms of pine trees, and wide transmission routes, which can cause rapid death of trees and pose a serious threat to the species1,2. Among them, avermectin pesticides, as broad-spectrum, efficient, and safe sixteen-membered macrocyclic lactones isolated from the fermentation products of Streptomyces, are one of the main types of pesticides for forest pest control. Avermectin has been widely used as an insecticide, acaricide, and antiparasitic drug3,4. Avermectin mainly exerts insecticidal effects by interfering with the neural transmission system of pests. Although these pesticides have the characteristics of low toxicity and safety, during the application process, some pesticides may still remain in trees and soil, eventually entering water bodies and even accumulating in the human body along the food chain, posing a threat to human health5. Research has shown that the residues of avermectin in soil and plant bodies may have potential toxicity to non-target organisms such as pollinating insects, soil microorganisms, etc., and even affect ecosystem health through food chain enrichment6. Moreover, due to long-term and singular use, many pests have developed varying degrees of resistance to avermectin.

Many experts have attempted to synthesize avermectin benzene to improve its effectiveness and biological activity, providing a reference for reducing environmental pollution and lowering agricultural costs. For example, LAM Mahmoud et al. utilized metal–organic frameworks for pesticide delivery design to achieve pest control7. C An et al. constructed avermectin co-delivery nanoparticles using polylactic acid as the wall material. This method could effectively improve the utilization rate and control effect of pesticides8. S Gu et al. improved the drug loading rate of pesticide avermectin by constructing a controlled-release hydrogel carrier. This method had good environmental responsiveness and could control release behavior by changing temperature and pH values9. Y Liu et al. designed a porous structure to deliver avermectin and Bacillus subtilis. This structure could enhance the biocompatibility and anti-photolysis of avermectin, and improve the application effect of forestry pest control10. Z Liang et al. proposed using the insecticide methomyl benzoate to achieve pest management of walnuts, and using ultra-High Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC)-mass spectrometry technology to measure the insecticide content. This technology could effectively determine the content of insecticides, and the mortality rate of larvae injected with a dose of 2.4 mL/cm for 10 days exceeded 80%11. Y Jia et al. mixed methomyl benzoate (2%) and avermectin (5%) to prevent pine wilt disease, and determined drug residues using HPLC. The prevention and control effect of this method exceeded 99%, and the recovery rate of pesticide residues exceeded 80%, indicating a significant application effect12. Y Ma et al. proposed the preparation of dual enzyme-responsive nanopesticides using nanocoating avermectin for diseases such as pine wilt. This processing method could intelligently release pesticides, and the nanocoating had bidirectional transport capability, which could effectively achieve disease safety management13. W Zhang et al. proposed using methomyl benzoate to intervene in pine nematode diseases. The cost of this preparation could reach the top branch of a pine tree within three months after injection, and the infection control effect was good14. Existing research focuses on the synthesis, carrier design, and control effect of avermectin and its derivatives (such as methomyl benzoate). The utilization rate and environmental compatibility of pesticides have been improved through nano materials (such as metal organic framework, hydrogel) and composite delivery systems (such as nanoparticles, double enzyme response agents). The residues of avermectin pesticides in soil and plant bodies may have potential toxicity to non-target organisms and even affect ecosystem health through food chain enrichment. However, existing carrier research mostly focuses on agricultural crops, with insufficient adaptability to the special substrate of pine trees. Current residue detection methods have not effectively addressed the issue of endogenous interference in pine trees. To understand the transport process of avermectin in pine tree trunks, it is necessary to establish a rapid and accurate method for detecting its residues in pine trees. Therefore, developing an efficient and sensitive method for detecting avermectin residues in pine trees is of great significance for assessing its environmental behavior and ensuring safe forestry production.

At present, Pesticide Residue Detection (PRD) technologies mainly include chromatographic analysis, immunoassay, and spectroscopic detection. Among them, HPLC–MS/MS has become the mainstream method for PRD due to its high sensitivity and accuracy. However, the tissue composition of pine trees is complex (such as rich in resins, terpenoids, etc.), and direct detection is easily affected by matrix interference, which affects the accuracy of quantitative analysis15. Although traditional solid-phase extraction and liquid–liquid extraction are widely used, they have disadvantages such as high consumption of organic solvents and cumbersome operation. In recent years, Magnetic Solid-Phase Extraction (MSPE) has shown significant advantages in the pre-treatment of complex matrix samples due to its simple operation, high adsorption efficiency, and easy separation16. MSPE utilizes functionalized Magnetic Nano-materials (MNMs) to selectively adsorb target substances and rapidly separate them under an external magnetic field, effectively reducing matrix interference and improving enrichment efficiency. MSPE is successfully utilized in detecting pollutants in food, environmental, and biological samples17,18. There is currently limited research on the application of MSPE technology in forestry PRD analysis, especially in the detection system for high-interference substrates such as pine trees, which still needs to be optimized. In addition, the migration and distribution patterns of avermectin in xylem tissues are still unclear, and existing methods are unable to meet the needs of trace residue detection.

This study aims to develop a detection method for avermectin residues in pine trees based on MSPE combined with HPLC–MS/MS. This method verifies the applicability of the validation method for actual pine tree samples by preparing magnetic Schiff base polymer adsorbent materials and designing application programs. Avermectin pesticides have photostability, and their degradation and decomposition rates are significantly affected by factors such as light intensity, temperature, and pH. This characteristic makes the migration and distribution of avermectin in plants more complex. The study uses MSPE combined technology to detect residual avermectin in pine trees. The designed magnetic core–shell nanoparticles can utilize their surface specificity to capture avermectin molecules, and combined with this technology, MSPE pretreatment is achieved, eliminating the influence of co-elution matrix peaks. This method can provide a precise analysis tool for the avermectin residue in pine trees. Its high enrichment efficiency and anti-substrate interference characteristics provide a scientific basis and reference for quantitative research on the axial migration law of xylem and rational application of forestry pesticides. This study aims to provide technical support for the development of forestry PRD, provide standardized guidelines for the detection of avermectin residues such as methomyl and tetracycline in pine trees, and provide a reference for the detection of other forestry pesticides.

Sample collection

The samples were collected from pine needles of Pinus massoniana at the Life Science Center of Huzhou Normal University. The sample was placed in a cool place and naturally air dried for 2 days. It was grounded in a grinding cup and sieved through a 60 mesh sieve before being stored in the refrigerator for later use.

Solution preparation

Preparation of test solution

A crushed sample with a weight of 0.3 g was placed in a 10 mL centrifuge tube. 2.4 mL of acetone was added and sonicated for 1 h. The extract was filtered through filter paper and the residue was washed with 0.9 mL of acetone. The process was repeated three times and the filtrate was combined. The filtrate was blown to near dryness using a nitrogen blower, dissolved in methanol and removed to a 0.5 mL volume. It was then filtered by a 0.22 μm needle filter and set aside for use.

Preparation of standard solution

An electronic balance was used to weigh 0.001 g of avermectin standard and place in a 10 mL brown volumetric flask. An appropriate amount of HPLC grade acetonitrile (about 5 mL) was added to dissolve. After complete dissolution, acetonitrile was diluted to the calibration line of the volumetric flask and shaken well to obtain a standard stock solution with a mass concentration of 1.00 g L-1. An appropriate amount of reserve solution was taken and diluted with acetonitrile to prepare a mixed standard intermediate solution with 1.00 mg L-1 mass concentration. According to the experimental requirements, the stepwise dilution method was adopted to prepare a series of mixed standard working solutions. A suitable amount of mixed standard intermediate solution was selected, and a series of standard solutions with a concentration gradient of 0.5 ~ 200.0 ng mL-1 were prepared by gradient dilution with blank matrix extraction solution or acetonitrile. All working fluids must be prepared and used immediately, and the preparation process must be free of pollution and variable interference. After preparation, they should be stored at -20 ℃ for later use19.

Preparation of magnetic schiff base polymer adsorption materials

The 1.5 g of hexahydrate ferric chloride was dissolved in 44 mL of ethylene glycol and stirred until completely dissolved. Then, 3 g of sodium acetate and 1 g of polyethylene glycol were added and stirred for another 30 min to obtain a brownish-yellow mixed solution. The mixed solution was transferred to a high-pressure reaction vessel and reacted at 200 ℃ for 6 h. After completing the reaction, the product was magnetically separated and washed with anhydrous ethanol and vacuum dried for 5 times, followed by vacuum drying at 60 ℃ for 24 h to gain Magnetic Nanoparticles (MNPs) loaded with iron oxide. Afterwards, the MNPs loaded with iron oxide were dispersed in 4:1 anhydrous ethanol and ultra pure water, subjected to ultrasonic treatment for 15 min, and then added with ammonia and ethyl ester successively. The mixture was stirred at room temperature for 24 h. After magnetic separation, the product was washed and vacuum dried. The obtained silica-loaded MNPs with iron oxide were subjected to ultrasonic treatment with anhydrous ethanol and 3-aminopropyltriethoxysilane, and further obtained silica combined with amino-modified iron oxide-loaded MNPs, namely Fe3O4-SiO2-NH2. Fe3O4-SiO2-NH2 was dispersed in 1,4-dioxane for 15 min of ultrasonic treatment, followed by the addition of 0.45 mmol of cationic binary aldehyde, 0.3 mmol of 1,3,5-tris (4-aminophenyl) benzaldehyde, and 0.5 mL of Anhydrous Acetic Acid (AAA) for stirring. After the complete reaction of the mixture, 4.5 mol of AAA was uniformly dropped to gently inhibit the amino oxidation reaction, improve the acidic stability of the silica coating, and avoid magnetic core dissolution and aggregation. After the reaction was complete, the product was magnetically separated and washed with ethanol to remove unreacted substances. It was then vacuum dried at 80 °C for 24 h to obtain the polymer adsorbent material Fe3O4-SiO2-NH2-Schiff-TAPB-DA. Figure 1 shows the preparation process of the adsorbent material.

Synthetic polymer adsorbent material.

In this study, MNPs loaded with iron oxide were used as the magnetic core, and silica combined with amino modification and Schiff base polymer reaction was carried out to obtain the synthesized polymer adsorbent material. Among them, the cationic binary aldehyde was prepared by mixing and stirring 4-imidazolbenzaldehyde, 5-(chloromethyl) salicylaldehyde, and tetrahydrofuran solution (0.173 g, 0.172 g, 20.0 mL), filtering, and vacuum drying.

Chromatographic and mass spectrometry conditions

Chromatographic conditions: The stationary phase is a C18 column (2.1 × 50 mm, 1.8 µm). The mobile phase consists of a 0.01% formic acid aqueous solution containing 5 mmol L-1 ammonium acetate and acetonitrile, with the concentration of the formic acid aqueous solution being approximately 2.65 mmol/L. The volume ratio of component A to component B can be determined as 80:20, with 245 nm UV detection wavelength, 40 ℃ column temperature, and 1.00 mL/min flow rate. The injection volume is 3 μl20. In liquid chromatography systems, the flow rate of the mobile phase can control the linear velocity of the mobile phase passing through the chromatographic column, determining the equilibrium time of the target substance between the stationary phase and the mobile phase. Excessive flow rate can lead to insufficient mass transfer, while excessively low flow rate can prolong analysis time and affect separation efficiency. According to the reference21, a flow rate of 1.00 mL/min can complete a single analysis within 3–5 min, significantly improving detection efficiency. Using ammonium acetate as a mobile phase additive can enhance ion strength and improve detection sensitivity. The addition of formic acid does not significantly change the peak shape and response of various substances, while the addition of ammonium acetate effectively improves the response of the substances. Studies have shown that the addition of ammonium acetate effectively improves the detection of pesticide residues by mass spectrometry22,23. In the detection of avermectin drugs, due to the large molecular weight of AVMs, UV detectors are used to assist in monitoring the absorption of target compounds. By retaining the characteristics and peak shapes, the chromatographic behavior of the target compounds is preliminarily verified, and false positive interference is eliminated. It can also help quickly optimize chromatographic conditions before mass spectrometry detection, reduce high-cost mass spectrometer losses, and improve detection efficiency.

Mass spectrum conditions: The ion source technology is Agilent Jet Stream Electrospray Ionization Positive Mode (AJS ESI +), and the dry gas is nitrogen; The temperature of the ion source is 320 ℃; The flow rate is 10 L min-1; The solvent delay time is 5 min; The mass spectrometry monitoring mode is segmented into MRM mode; The capillary voltage is 4000 V and the atomization gas is 10 psi. The use of positive ion mode is to improve the ionization efficiency of the target compound.

Mass spectrometry parameter optimization: The study of target standard solution in positive ion mode optimizes the breakdown voltage and collision energy through full scan optimization and selective ion scanning of the parent ion. The mass spectrometry parameters for avermectin compounds are as follows: Avermectin (parent ion: 894.5 m/z, breakdown voltage: 265 V, collision energy: 58/58 eV, retention time: 2.14 min), Ivermectin (parent ion: 897.4 m/z, breakdown voltage: 285 V, collision energy: 60/55 eV, retention time: 3.02 min), Eprinomectin (parent ion: 932.4 m/z, breakdown voltage: 265 V, collision energy: 56/64 eV, retention time: 1.77 min), Epinomectin (parent ion: 785.3 m/z, breakdown voltage: 175 V, collision energy: 27/28 eV, retention time: 2.87 min), Emamectin (parent ion: 925.3 m/z, breakdown voltage: 275 V, collision energy: 65/60 eV, retention time: 2.52 min), Emamectin Benzoate (parent ion: 883.2 m/z, breakdown voltage: 195 V, collision energy: 38/35 eV, retention time: 1.47 min), and Nemadectin (parent ion: 653.8 m/z, breakdown voltage: 212 V, collision energy: 40/37 eV, retention time: 2.51 min).

MSPE program

A blank sample of 4 g of Pinus massoniana pine needles was weighed and placed in a dark, sealed bag. The surface dust of the pine needles was washed with ultrapure water and drained. Afterwards, the drained pine needle powder was placed in a 50 mL centrifuge tube and 10 mL of acetonitrile water (8:2, v/v) was added, vortexed for 5 min to improve the efficiency of plant cell wall rupture. The sample solution (Needle powder mixed by vortex and acetonitrile aqueous solution) was added with 1 g NaCl and 2 g anhydrous MgSO₄ (excluding water and salt precipitation), vortexed for 30 s, centrifuging at 8000 r/min for 10 min. 5 mL of supernatant was taken, and nitrogen was blown to dryness. Afterwards, the processed sample solution was mixed with 10 mg of Fe3O4-SiO2-NH2-Schiff-TAPB-DA, vortexed for 3 min, to reach adsorption equilibrium. Research has shown that a 2-min vortex mixing time was sufficient to achieve adsorption equilibrium between avermectin pesticides and adsorbent materials24. The MNM in the supernatant after solution precipitation was collected using a magnet, and the analyte was eluted by adding acetonitrile eluent twice. After vortex treatment, the magnet was placed outside the centrifuge tube. The collected supernatant was filtered through a 0.22 μm organic filter membrane (material: polytetrafluoroethylene) before being introduced into a UPLC-MS/MS system.

Methodology validation

Linear relationship

The mother liquor in the mixed control stock solution was diluted fivefold, and then the matrix samples were prepared into matrix control solutions of different concentrations according to the dilution ratio. Specifically, the reference stock solution was selected as the first concentration point, and starting from this point, the solution from the previous concentration point was used as the mother liquor. The ratio was determined by diluting five times from the first concentration point and then diluting eight concentrations with subsequent ratios. The mixing treatment of matrix control solution and analytical protectant was carried out in a ratio of 1:3, and chromatographic mass spectrometry data were measured for different concentrations, with component mass concentration and peak area as the horizontal and vertical axes. The quantitative ion pair peak area was plotted on the y-axis with the corresponding standard curve. The linear relationship was calculated, and with the determination of correlation coefficients and linear regression equations, the linearity of the solution within a certain range could be calculated25.

Repeatability and stability tests

The same sample was evenly divided into 6 parts, and the sample was processed and determined according to the established method and chromatographic conditions. The relative standard deviation of pesticide repeatability was tested and compared with the PRD standard. According to the internal standard method, the quality scores of each target pesticide were calculated.

Detection limit

In the mixed control solution, three concentration points (1, 5, and 10 mg L-1) were selected, and the solution and analytical protectant were mixed and shaken evenly in a ratio of 1:3. Subsequently, 1 mg of the mixed solution was precisely aspirated and injected into a gas chromatography-mass spectrometer, and concentration selection and detection limit calculation were performed based on the peak differences of each pesticide.

Avermectin PRD results in pine trees

This study prepared standard solutions (concentration: 50 ng mL-1) of seven target substances, including avermectin, ivermectin, and eplinomycin. Afterwards, full scan analysis was performed using AJS ESI positive ion mode, with propamycin and Emamycin [M + H] + as parent ions, and the remaining five target substances [M + Na] + as parent ions. This study used 0.1% CH2O2 and acetonitrile as mobile phases, and achieved baseline separation of 7 target substances within 5 min through gradient elution. The selective ion scanning method was used to optimize the fragment voltage for each target object. Two characteristic fragment ions with high response values were selected as quantitative ion pairs and qualitative ion pairs. Ions with relatively high abundance were selected for quantitative analysis. The multi-reaction monitoring mode was adopted for final detection, ensuring high selectivity and accuracy. Functional groups and crystal structure of MNPs.

Figure 4a shows the FTIR spectrum results, in which the characteristic functional groups of Fe3O4 and its composite materials are displayed, and the characteristic peak at 550 cm-1 corresponds to lattice vibration, confirming the successful synthesis of Fe3O4 MNPs. The broad peaks of 2,845 cm-1 and 2,714 cm-1 were attributed to the chemical bond stretching and bending vibrations of surface adsorbed water. The characteristic peaks at 1000–1500 cm-1 indicated effective encapsulation of the shell layer, and the vibration of Fe3O4-SiO2-Schiff-TAPB-DA indicated the presence of an organic layer. The characteristic absorption peaks of Fe3O4-SiO2-Schiff-TAPB-DA were more prominent than other curve spectra, mainly due to the reaction between amide C-N and benzene ring C = C, resulting in the formation of a Schiff base structure under condensation reaction. In the crystal results of Fig. 4b, all materials exhibited typical magnetite crystal plane diffraction peaks, which perfectly match the standard card, indicating that the coating of SiO2 and organic layer has not destroyed the crystal structure of Fe3O4, and the composite material still maintains excellent crystallinity. In Fig. 6, the pH value of the solution in the range of 5.0–9.0 had no significant effect on the extraction efficiency (recovery rate > 75.06%), so there was no need to adjust the sample pH. Ionic strength experiments showed that the addition of NaCl led to a decrease in the recovery rates of the five target compounds, which was speculated to be due to the competition between Na + and Cl− for adsorption sites. Therefore, no salt was added in the end. The analysis of the influence of extraction time and desorption solvent is shown in Fig. 7.

In the optimization of adsorption conditions in Fig. 7, it was found that the vortex time of 10 min could reach adsorption equilibrium, and prolonging the time had no significant effect on the improvement of the recovery rate. Research on elution conditions showed that when acetonitrile was used as the elution solvent, the recovery rate of all target substances was greater than 73.00%, and it was compatible with the mobile phase to simplify the pretreatment steps. The analysis of desorption time and volume is shown in Fig. 8.

In Fig. 8, the Extraction Recovery Rate (ERR) gained by Ultrasonic Elution (UE) was lower than that obtained by Vortex Elution (VE). When the volume of acetonitrile increased to 3.0 mL, the ERR reached its maximum. As the volume of eluent increased, the ERR of the target substance began to decrease. It was worth noting that VE had a significantly better effect than UE, which may be related to incomplete desorption caused by adsorbent aggregation during the ultrasonic process. The established optimization plan ensured a high recovery rate (> 73%) while achieving a fast (total processing time of about 15 min) and efficient pre-treatment process, providing reliable guarantees for subsequent analysis and testing. Discussion

Pesticides, as a key means of preventing and controlling pests and diseases and increasing crop yields in agricultural production, are mainly divided into categories such as herbicides, insecticides, and fungicides. The annual use of pesticides in China exceeds one million tons, involving more than 600 varieties, but the actual utilization rate is less than 30%, resulting in a large amount of residues entering the water environment through soil infiltration, atmospheric migration, and surface runoff, causing widespread ecological risks. Research has shown that pesticide residues in water can disrupt the metabolic functions of aquatic organisms, induce acute poisoning and even bioaccumulation effects, and threaten human health through the food chain. Epidemiological investigations have confirmed significant associations between pesticide exposure and reproductive toxicity, endocrine disorders, cancer, and neurological disorders26. This study designed an MSPE-HPLC–MS/MS method based on Fe3O4-SiO2-NH2-Schiff-TAPB-DAMNM for PRD of avermectin in pine needles. The exposed part in the SEM image of Fe3O4 appeared spherical. The Fe3O4-SiO2-Schiff-TAPB-DA’s diameter increased, exhibiting a typical Shell structure improved by MNPs. Faraj et al.27 used the traditional QuEChERS method in soil PRD, which, although simple to operate, has problems such as high organic solvent consumption and limited purification effect. The MSPE method only required 10 mg of adsorbent and 3 mL of acetonitrile to complete the extraction, significantly lowering the quantity of organic solvents used, and the recovery rate (> 73%) was better than traditional methods (usually 60%-80%). Compared with the MOFs materials reported by Mahmoud et al., the studied materials had the dual advantages of magnetic separation convenience and organic polymer specific adsorption, making them particularly suitable for the treatment of complex plant matrices such as pine needles. With the rapid development of nanomaterials technology, many nanomaterials with strong adsorption capacity were combined with magnetic particles as MSPE adsorbents for the enrichment and extraction of pesticide residues in samples, such as carbon nanomaterials, porous organic polymer materials, etc. In terms of material properties, although Fe3O4-SiO2-NH2-Schiff-TAPB-DA (saturation magnetization 35.26 emu g-1) has decreased compared to the original Fe3O4 (87.36 emu g-1), its BET specific surface area has significantly decreased. This decline is mainly due to three factors: (1) The organic polymer layer covers a portion of the mesoporous structure; (2) The residual reagents that are not completely removed in the Schiff base reaction block the pores; (3) The formation of a dense-shell layer reduces the number of surface touchable sites. The core–shell structure of the designed magnetic nanocarrier structure avoids the problem of easy aggregation of nanoparticles by An et al. The FT-IR results confirm that the material successfully introduces a benzene ring and amide bonds through Schiff base reaction, providing hydrogen bonding adsorption sites. The presence of benzene rings provides π-π stacking sites for avermectin, thereby enhancing the material’s adsorption capacity for avermectin28. The amide bond can interact with polar groups (such as hydroxyl and carbonyl groups) in avermectin molecules through hydrogen bonding, further enhancing the material’s adsorption capacity for avermectin29. Kaziem et al. designed an avermectin-loaded amylase responsive nanosystem and found that this method has high loading efficiency and greatly improves insecticidal activity30. The reason is that the mesoporous structure of HMS and the carboxyl and hydroxyl groups on the CMS surface provide abundant adsorption sites, which can strongly interact with avermectin molecules through electrostatic interactions, hydrogen bonding, and π-π stacking, thereby improving loading efficiency.

In terms of method optimization, the pH experiment (with no significant effect in the range of 5.0–9.0) is coincided with the study of Malhat et al.’s31 study on avermectin residue in okra, indicating that the pesticide has good stability under neutral conditions. Unlike the toxicity study on aquatic organisms reported by Zhu et al.32 (AVM is more easily degraded under acidic conditions), this suggests that plant substrates may have a protective effect on pesticides. It is worth noting that NaCl is found to reduce the recovery rate (competing for adsorption sites), which is similar to the salt extraction mechanism of Bai et al.33, but different from the salt-assisted extraction phenomenon observed by Norouzi et al.34 in tomato substrate. This difference may be related to the polarity and material properties of the target object. The linear relationship between various pesticides is good within the 1–100 mg/L concentration, with correlation coefficients > 0.998. The ARR of some pesticides is relatively low, ranging from 60 to 80%, with RSD values all less than 15%. The low recovery rate may be related to the special properties of the pesticide itself. One major characteristic of avermectin pesticides is their photostability, easy decomposition under light, and the ability to degrade into inactive derivatives under microbial action. During the experimental operation, it is inevitable to encounter light sources and microorganisms, which can lead to the decomposition of avermectin pesticides. The detection limit of the study (0.002 mg/kg) is superior to the co-determination method of Alekseev et al.35 (0.005 mg/kg avermectin), and the matrix effect (92.688% ~ 122.806) is significantly lower than the vegetable detection results reported by Guendouz et al.36 (up to 145%). The low recovery rate is mainly attributed to the photostability of avermectin, suggesting that the addition of light stabilizers can be explored in the future to improve the recovery rate.

Compared with traditional solid-phase extraction technology, MSPE technology is easy to operate and does not require special extraction equipment. Compared with dispersed solid phase extraction technology, it avoids the need to rely on high-speed centrifugation to separate the substrate and adsorbent. However, there are still many shortcomings in using MNPs as adsorbents in sample pretreatment. For example, MNPs have limited selectivity because the main interactions between MNPs and analytes are hydrophobic, size exclusion, and hydrogen bonding. Although this study did not directly analyze the transport kinetics of avermectin in xylem, the established MSPE-HPLC–MS/MS method provides critical technical support for the detection of trace residues in xylem. Magnetic materials selectively enrich avermectin through benzene ring interactions and amide hydrogen bonds, significantly reducing matrix effects. The optimized MSPE technology also achieves a high method detection limit and quantitative performance. The research method significantly improves the detection efficiency of avermectin residues in pine wood matrix, providing a scalable analytical tool for further investigating xylem migration behavior. In the future, combined with segmented sampling of tree trunks, analyzing the concentration gradients of residues in xylem sap at different heights can help reverse-engineer migration patterns and reveal their underlying mechanisms. In future research, it is necessary to focus on synthesizing novel MNPs with high physical and chemical stability under extreme conditions, enhanced hydrophilicity, strong adsorption capacity, and better selectivity for target compounds of different polarities. Future work needs to further improve environmentally friendly synthesis processes, develop green materials, and reduce the use of organic solvents, which are the main directions for developing environmentally friendly MSPE methods in the future.

Conclusion

This study successfully developed an MSPE-HPLC–MS/MS analysis method built on Fe3O4-SiO2-NH2-Schiff-TAPB-DAMNM, achieving efficient detection of avermectin pesticide residues in pine needles. Material characterization confirmed that the prepared MNPs had a typical Shell structure and exhibited good extraction performance for the target substance. The target substance under optimized extraction conditions had a good linear relationship, which could provide a reliable tool for pesticide residue monitoring in preventing and controlling pine wilt disease. However, this paper did not evaluate the stability of vegetation materials under long-term use and extreme conditions, and further research is needed on their environmental adaptability. Therefore, future research can further optimize the photostability of materials, expand the research scope of pesticide types, and extend to multi-residue detection of other forestry pesticides.

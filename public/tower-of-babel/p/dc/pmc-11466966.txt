Identification of dynamic networks community by fusing deep learning and evolutionary clustering
Authors: Yu Pan, Xin Liu, Feng Yao, Lei Zhang, Wei Li, Pei Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Community detection is a critical component of network analysis and a hot topic in social computing. Detecting community structure in dynamic networks has important theoretical and practical implications for understanding the intrinsic function of networks and predicting network behavior. However, the majority of existing dynamic community detection methods adopt shallow models, which have limited ability to excavate complex non-linear structures and tend to generate undesirable community structures. In order to obtain an accurate and robust community structure in dynamic networks, we are inspired by network representation learning and utilize the deep learning to detect evolving communities in dynamic networks. In this paper, we propose a novel dynamic community detection method by fusing Deep Learning and Evolutionary Clustering (DLEC). This work attempts to combine deep learning and evolutionary clustering into a unified framework. First, we propose a matrix construction strategy to fully reveal the inherent community structures via the underlying community memberships. Then, we develop a novel multi-layer deep autoencoder framework that consists of multiple non-linear functions to extract the latent deep representation of the dynamic network. Based on the evolutionary clustering framework, a graph regularization term is introduced to ensure the smoothness of the community evolution. Finally, we employ the K-means clustering algorithm on the low-dimensional network space to obtain the community structure. Extensive experimental results on synthetic and real-world networks show that the proposed DLEC algorithm can effectively detect high-quality communities in dynamic networks.

Introduction

In the real world, many complex relationships between individuals or organizations exist in the form of complex networks, such as social networks, citation networks, and communication networks1. Among them, nodes in the network represent individuals, and edges represent relationships between individuals. As a contemporary topic encompassing computer science and society, complex network have gradually develop into a scientific research and an active interdisciplinary topic2. The community detection in complex networks is the most important research in network analysis, which is essential to revealing the internal structure, understanding the underlying characteristics, and capturing the organizational patterns of complex networks3.

In recent years, more and more researches on community detection have been proposed4–7. However, these studies mainly focus on the static networks ignoring the dynamics of network and evolution of community structure. Compared with the community detection in static networks, dynamic community detection is more challenging, mainly due to the following reasons. One is that community detection in dynamic networks should not only consider the accuracy of community detection, but also consider the evolution process of networks and communities. The method should consider the connectivity and temporality simultaneously. The other challenge is the instability of the solution. We cannot be sure whether the changes in the partitioning results are caused by the natural evolution of communities, or by the noise in the network and the instability of algorithm. To address the above challenges, researchers have proposed several solutions that aim to smooth the evolution of communities. To this end, Chakrabarti et al.8 proposed the evolutionary clustering framework, which is the most widely used dynamic community detection framework. It assumes that dramatic changes within a short period of time may be caused by noise, and sudden changes in clustering are not expected. Pallaet et al.9 studied the dynamic evolution process of scientific collaboration networks and found that the community would not undergo drastic changes in a short time, and the life cycle of the community would be long after a large-scale evolution. This proves that the assumptions of the evolutionary clustering framework are consistent with the characteristics of real networks.

Although considerable efforts have been devoted to dynamic community detection, there are still many challenges due to the complexity of this problem. First, the underlying network structures and the complex interactions in the network are highly non-linear, and the existing methods that use shallow models have limited ability to excavate the potential deep representation of the network. Second, how to design an effective and efficient method to realize accurate community mining in large-scale, sparse, and high-dimensional dynamic networks is an important research at present. Third, most evolutionary clustering based algorithms are only suitable for scenarios where the number of communities and nodes does not change. Dealing with situations where the number of communities and nodes changes is an urgent problem that needs to be addressed.

With the development of network representation learning, it provides an effective solution for network structure mining and analysis. Network representation learning aims to learn a low-dimensional representation for the origin networks, then a variety of network analysis tasks can be effectively performed on it10. Therefore, the node clustering results obtained by performing a clustering strategy on the embedding space are equivalent to the community structure obtained by performing a community detection algorithm on the network topology. Inspired by this, utilizing a deep model to map the network to a low-dimensional embedding space, followed by implementing a clustering strategy in the feature space can obtain the community structure. Inspired by the network representation, the deep learning model can be applied to the community detection. Compared with the limitations of the shallow model, deep model has portability and strong ability to mine and capture the non-linear structure of networks. In addition, it also has a low computational complexity and high capability for parallelization, and is more resilient to the problem of network sparsity11.

Motivated by the above observations, a novel community detection method(DLEC) for dynamic networks that combines deep learning and evolutionary clustering is developed in this paper. Inspired by network representation learning, deep learning is innovatively exploited in dynamic community detection. Specifically, we develop a multi-layer autoencoder consisting of multiple non-linear functions to capture the deep non-linear representation of the network. Furthermore, we propose a matrix construction strategy by exploiting the community membership which encodes the inherent community structures. Specially, a community similarity function is designed to quantify the similarity of community memberships based on the probabilistic generative model, and the skip-gram model is utilized to generate the weighting matrix X. The matrix X is fed into the autoencoder as the input, which prompts the autoencoder generates a community-oriented network representation and provides strong support for the subsequent clustering task. Furthermore, to ensure that the community structure does not change abruptly in consecutive steps, a graph regularization term is integrated into the loss function to capture the changes in the geometric structure and smooth the community evolution. Finally, we employ the K-means algorithm on the embedding space to obtain the community structure. Related work

Recently, dynamic community detection has attracted wide attention and a variety of dynamic community detection methods have been proposed12,13. These methods are mainly divided into incremental clustering method14–20 and evolutionary clustering method21–29.

The incremental clustering algorithm attempted to extend the community detection method of static networks to dynamic networks. Its main idea was to utilize the static community detection method to detect the community structure in the first network snapshot, and then divide the community structure of subsequent snapshots based on the dynamic changes of nodes and edges between two consecutive snapshots. The community structure of the current time was derived from the community structure of the previous time. The representative algorithms were IA-MCS14, GraphScope15, etc. The incremental clustering methods can directly use static community detection algorithms, which were relatively easy to implement. However, the incremental clustering algorithm ignored the effect of noise on community detection. From a long-term and global perspective, this method cannot guarantee the consistency of community detection results.

Evolutionary clustering algorithm is the most classical and popular dynamic community detection method at present. It was first proposed by Chakrabarti et al. for clustering streaming data, and it was applied to K-means and agglomerative hierarchical clustering algorithms to deal with constantly changing data. The evolutionary clustering framework assumed that the clustering results do not change drastically in a short period of time, and the abrupt changes in the network are mostly caused by noise. The time loss function was introduced to penalize the sudden change in community partitioning. Chi et al.21 first applied evolutionary clustering framework to dynamic community detection, it used Snapshot Cost to evaluate the accuracy of community detection in network snapshots, and used Temporal Cost to measure the similarity of community detection results at successive time steps. Based on the evolutionary clustering framework, Lin et al.22 proposed a temporal smoothness framework for dynamic community detection and proposed the FacetNet algorithm, a unified framework for studying the dynamic evolution of the community. The FacetNet algorithm used a random block model to generate communities and analyzed the evolution of the community through a robust unified process that took into account the evolution of the community and the temporal smoothing of the evolution. Folino et al.23 proposed an evolutionary clustering algorithm DYNMOGA which was based on the multi-objective optimization. The algorithm considered the snapshot cost and temporal cost as a multi-objective function, maximizing the snapshot cost while minimizing the temporal cost. Xu et al.26 presented an evolutionary clustering framework that adaptively estimated the optimal smoothing parameter using shrinkage estimation, a statistical approach that improved a naive estimate using additional information. The framework can be used to extend a variety of static clustering algorithms into evolutionary clustering algorithms, such as hierarchical, k-means, and spectral clustering. Yang et al.27 proposed a framework based on Bayesian inference to find communities and to capture community evolution in dynamic social networks. The framework had two versions, the online inference version that progressively updated the probabilistic model over time, and the offline inference version that learned the probabilistic model with network data obtained at all time steps in a retrospective way. The ECGNMF algorithm28 utilized the Non-negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) framework to adjust the community division for each time step. For the temporal cost, the algorithm considered not only the mesoscopic community history information but also the microscopic change information. The regularization term was introduced to constrain the changes of microscopic nodes in successive time steps. Ma et al.24 proposed a dynamic community detection algorithm Cr-ENMF, which was based on the common regularized NMF. The algorithm used the network and community structure of the previous moment to characterize the temporal cost, and incorporated the regularized term into the objective function to realize the dynamic community structure detection. Lin et al.25 proposed a dynamic community detection method DCDEM, which used an improved evolutionary matrix to combine the community structure at the previous time with current network structure to track the network evolution. Pan et al.29 proposed a semi-supervised evolutionary clustering model based on symmetric NMF, which introduced a community transition probability matrix to track and analyze the community structure and temporal evolution.

In addition, Zhen et al.30 proposed an evolutionary Physarum-inspired Markov clustering algorithm that can explore parameters adaptively. The MOCCD31 was a multi-objective optimization algorithm based on the characteristics of dynamic social networks. It fused the three characteristics of temporal variability, stability, and continuity in dynamic social network communities. The HMM-MODCD32 used a multi-objective evolutionary algorithm and Viterbi algorithm to formulate objective functions and provide temporal smoothness over time for detecting community in dynamic networks.

However, the above mentioned methods mostly utilized shallow models that have limited ability to capture complex non-linear structure. They learn the community structure by directly mapping the original network to the community membership space. However, complex non-linear information is always present in real-world networks.

The evolutionary clustering framework

The evolutionary clustering framework assumes that the community structure will not change in a short period of time and the evolution of community structure is smooth in consecutive steps. The evolutionary clustering framework consists of the linear combination of two parts: Snapshot Cost (CS) and Temporal Cost (CT). Among them, CS represents the degree of consistency between the community detection result \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$C_t$$\end{document}Ct and the real community structure at time t, while CT reflects the difference between the community detection result \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$C_t$$\end{document}Ct and the community detection result \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$C_{t-1}$$\end{document}Ct-1 in two consecutive steps. The dynamic network community detection method based on the evolutionary clustering framework needs to meet two conditions. One is that the community partitioning results should represent the current network structure as accurate as possible. The other is that for two consecutive steps, the community detection results are similar with no significant mutations. The framework of DLEC

The framework of DLEC.

The overall framework of DLEC is illustrated in Fig. 1. According to the evolutionary clustering framework, the DLEC framework consists of CS module and CT module. In the CS module, we first design a matrix construction strategy to generate the community similarity matrix X. Then, we propose a deep architecture, which is composed of multiple non-linear mapping functions to map the input data to a highly non-linear latent space to capture the underlying community structure. In the CT module, we introduce a graph regularization to constrain the drastic evolution of community structure. Specifically, the DLEC can be implemented in the following 5 steps.

Step 1: The dynamic network is represented as a set of snapshots graph, which are in the form of corresponding adjacent matrix.

Step 2: To pave the way for subsequent community detection, we develop a matrix construction strategy to fully reveal the underlying community structure. The community similarity matrix X is generated by the matrix construction strategy, which preserves the latent community membership. We feed it into the autoencoder as the input for future learning, so that the low-dimensional space can preserve the latent community structure.

Step 3: To capture the deep representation of the network, we develop a deep autoencoder that consists of multiple layers of non-linear functions. Snapshot cost

In the snapshot cost part, we expect to maximize the community detection accuracy at the current time step. To achieve this, we first propose a community similarity matrix construction strategy. A community similarity function is designed to quantify the similarity of community memberships and generate the weighting matrix X. Then, we develop a multi-layer autoencoder that consists of multiple non-linear functions to preserve the inherent community structure in the embedding space.

Community similarity matrix construction strategy

An effective representation of the network is critical for the autoencoder to extract network features, and it also directly determines the accuracy of community detection. However, the adjacency matrix can only preserve the similarity that nodes are directly connected, which is insufficient to capture the latent community proximity. The low-dimensional representation can only capture the direct connection relationship between nodes when the adjacency matrix is used as the input of the autoencoder. We expect that the embedding space can encode inherent community structures, thus improving the accuracy of subsequent community detection. Therefore, we introduce a matrix construction strategy to construct a community similarity matrix X that preserves the inherent community structures. The elements in the matrix X represent the probability that two nodes belong to the same community.

The variations of nodes’ number: It is challenging to extend the underlying assumptions of the evolutionary clustering framework, and most algorithms based on evolutionary clustering struggle to handle scenarios where the number of nodes changes over time. However, variations in the number of nodes and communities are common in dynamic networks. In this paper, we transform the dynamic network at each step into a low-dimensional embedding via an autoencoder, which allows it to effectively handle changes in nodes well. For the new nodes inserted into the network or the existing nodes removed, we can obtain the corresponding adjacency matrix and calculate the community similarity matrix of the new graph according to Eq. (12). Then, it can be fed into the deep model can obtain the community structure.

Setting

To evaluate the performance of the DLEC algorithm, five baseline algorithms are chosen for comparison, including FacetNet22, DYNMOGA23, NE2NMF40, CNPSO41 and DNR42. FacetNet and DYNMOGA were the most classic evolutionary clustering algorithms. NE2NMF was based on the NMF framework, which developed a 3rd-order smoothing strategy using snapshots of the previous, current and next time steps to fully characterize the clustering drift. CNPSO was a multi-objective algorithm based on particle swarm optimization that optimized both the intra-community structure and the inter-community structure to obtain the community structure. DNR was a static community detection algorithm based on deep neural networks. We implemented the DNR algorithm on each network snapshot to obtain the community structure for each step. In our experiments, we adjusted the parameters of all baseline methods to ensure that they achieve the best performance.

The evaluation metrics used to compare the performance of the algorithms are Normalized Mutual Information (NMI), Modularity (Q) and error rate (ER). These metrics are the standard measures widely used for community detection. When the community structure is known, we employ the NMI to measure the performance of the algorithms. The NMI indicates the similarity between the partition obtained by the algorithm and the ground truth. The range of the NMI value is [0, 1], and a higher value indicates better performance. If the community structure is unknown, we adopt the Q or the method used in Ref.23 to quantify the performance. The more obvious the community structure, the higher the Q value. The error rate calculates the difference between the two different community divisions in a network. The smaller the ER, the more similar the structure of the two communities.

Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a novel dynamic community detection algorithm utilizing deep learning, which is based on the evolutionary framework. We develop a matrix reconstruction strategy based on the underlying community memberships, which overcomes the insufficient proximity information in the adjacency matrix. The matrix encodes the inherent community structures, so that the autoencoder obtains a community-oriented network representation. A stacked deep autoencoder is employed to embed dynamic network and evolutionary patterns in the low-dimensional space. Furthermore, we introduce a graph regularization term into the loss function, which smooths the community evolution in successive steps. Extensive experiments on both synthetic and real-world dynamic networks demonstrate the superiority of DLEC over five state-of-the-art methods.

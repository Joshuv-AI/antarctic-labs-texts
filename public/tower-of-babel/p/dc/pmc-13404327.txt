Influences of myopic residual refractions on uncorrected distance visual acuity in eyes with conventional and enhanced monofocal intraocular lenses
Authors: Tomohiro Yokogawa, Keita Takada, Shota Tokuda, Yosai Mori, Keiichiro Minami, Nobuyuki Shoji, Kazunori Miyata
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIn eyes with conventional monofocal intraocular lenses (IOLs), uncorrected distance visual acuity (UDVA) deteriorates as myopic refraction error increases. This retrospective study aimed to evaluate tolerance to myopic residual refraction in eyes with conventional and enhanced monofocal IOLs.MethodsClinical records of 166 eyes from 135 patients with conventional monofocal IOLs (XY1, Hoya) and 55 eyes from 28 patients with enhanced monofocal IOLs (XY1-EM, Hoya) were reviewed. Associations between UDVA and refractive error spherical equivalent (SE) were evaluated using two analyses. The influences of UDVA on SE (0.0 logMAR or less, 0.1 logMAR, and 0.2 logMAR or higher) and the influence of SE on UDVA (within ± 0.50 D, between − 1.00 and 0.0 D, and − 1.00 D or less) were evaluated. In addition, changes in UDVA with SE were evaluated using segmented regression analysis, and SE break points were compared between IOL types.ResultsIn the influences of UDVA on SE, the mean SE of eyes with XY1-EM was − 0.61 D for the range of UDVA of 0.1 logMAR, which was twice that of eyes with XY1 (P = 0.0025). In the influence of SE on UDVA, no differences were found. Segmented regression analysis resulted in the break point at -1.075 D in eyes with enhanced XY1-EM IOLs, whereas the break point was at -0.45 D in eyes with conventional XY1 IOLs. Both evaluation results showed the improvement of myopic refractive tolerances in the use of enhanced IOLs.ConclusionsThe influence of myopic residual refractions on UDVA in eyes with conventional and enhanced monofocal IOLs was investigated. The quantitative evaluations revealed the improvement of refractive tolerance up to approximately − 1.00 D of myopia in eyes with enhanced monofocal IOLs. In addition, segmented regression analysis between UDVA and refractive error proved effective for evaluating refractive tolerance.

Background

In eyes with conventional monofocal intraocular lens (IOL), residual refractive errors influence uncorrected distance visual acuity (UDVA). A retrospective analysis of 11,571 eyes conducted by Schallhorn et al. demonstrated that postoperative residual myopic sphere as low as -0.25 D degrades UDVA [1], and that a mean UDVA of 0.15 logarithm of the minimum angle of resolution (logMAR) could be obtained with a residual sphere of -0.50 D. Nevertheless, refractive errors within ± 1.0 D and ± 0.5 D are achieved in 93.0% and 72.7% of 282,811 eyes in EU countries [2], and achieving refractive errors within ± 0.25 D is still challenging. Such refractive errors result from inaccuracies in IOL power calculation [2], variability in biometric measurements [3], discrepancies between labeled and actual IOL powers [4], and tilt or decentration of the implanted IOLs [5]. From the findings, UDVA is expected to deteriorate with increasing myopic refraction error in eyes with conventional monofocal IOLs [1, 6].

The influences of residual myopic refraction could be minimized by using extended depth-of-focus (EDoF) IOLs [7–9]. In eyes with diffractive and refractive EDoF IOL, tolerance to myopic refraction for maintaining preferred UDVA is wider than in eyes with conventional monofocal IOLs [7, 9], while the uncorrected near visual acuity (UNVA) remains preferred. In addition, some enhanced monofocal IOLs also effectively expand depth of focus (DOF) by modifying the modulation transfer function (MTF) across the focus range [8, 9]. An enhanced monofocal IOL NSP-3 (Nidek, Gamagori, Japan) modifies the MTF through focus using a new aspheric profile to improve refractive tolerance [6]. In other enhanced monofocal IOLs, XY1-EM IOL (HOYA, Tokyo, Japan) modifies the central 2-mm optical zone of the conventional monofocal IOL XY1 (HOYA) to produce a gradual increase in the power. So, a similar tolerance of refraction error was anticipated in eyes with XY1-EM. It was assumed that the refractive errors of eyes with XY1-EM would consist of refractive ranges retaining preferred UDVA around emmetropia and myopically degrading UDVA with residual refraction. Hence, this retrospective study aimed to investigate the influence of myopic residual refraction on UDVA in eyes with conventional and enhanced monofocal IOLs.

Methods

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Miyata Eye Hospital (CS-382) and adhered to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consents for the use of preoperative and postoperative data measured in the routine examinations were obtained before surgery. This study was informed in hospital web with the selection of patient’s opt-out.

The clinical records of eyes with one-piece hydrophobic acrylic conventional monofocal IOLs (XY1) implanted from January 2023 to February 2024 and the enhanced version XY1-EM binocularly implanted from February to July 2024, were reviewed. Inclusion criteria were IOL implantation within the capsule, preoperative corneal astigmatism of ≤ 1.0 D, preoperative axial length between 22 and 26 mm, and postoperative distance-corrected visual acuity (CDVA) of 0.0 logMAR or better. Eyes with ophthalmic disease influencing visual acuity or IOL dislocation were excluded.

IOL implantation and postoperative examination

XY1 and XY1-EM IOLs were of the same hydrophobic acrylic material and shared an identical platform, consisting of posterior surface active-oxygen processing and the haptic design. In the XY1-EM IOL, the central 2-mm diameter zone was modified to provide a gradual increase in power. Figure 1 shows the through-focus MTF property at 100 line-pairs/mm (lp/mm) for the XY1 and XY1-EM IOLs. Compared with the XY1, the peak at zero defocus (emmetropia) was reduced by 30%, whereas MTF values were higher under myopic defocus (0.5 to 1.5 D). These enhanced properties were expected to improve visual acuity at intermediate distance and refractive tolerance.

After cataract removal using phacoemulsification and aspiration techniques, the IOLs were inserted into the capsular bag using specific injectors. IOL powers were determined using the SRK/T formula with biometric data, targeting emmetropia to mild myopia (such as -0.5 D) in most eyes, although some eyes were intentionally targeted for myopia, as shown in Table 1.

For eyes with conventional monofocal IOL (XY1), UDVA was measured one month postoperatively using Landolt ring charts at a distance of 5 m, and converted to logMAR. Refractive error spherical equivalent (SE) at infinity was obtained during CDVA examination at a distance of 5 m. For correcting refraction for 5 m, the sphere was corrected by the addition of -0.2 D [10]. For eyes with enhanced-monofocal IOL, UDVA and SE at infinity were measured in the same manner in one to three months postoperatively. When the SE in both eyes were similar after bilateral implantation, data from the first-operated eye were used for analysis.

Because the MTF values at emmetropia with the enhanced monofocal IOL was 30% lower than those of the conventional monofocal IOL, postoperative contrast sensitivity was measured in eyes with XY1-EM. Photopic (85 cd/m2) and mesopic (8 cd/m2) contrast sensitivity at spatial frequencies of 3, 6, 12, and 18 cycles per degree (cpd) were measured 3 months postoperatively using the CSV-1000 (Vector Vision).

Statistical analysis

Refractive tolerances were evaluated using two analytical approaches.

Following Son et al.’s methodology [7], the refractive tolerances were compared for three ranges according to UDVA and SE. Mean SE values were compared between the two IOL groups across three UDVA ranges: 0.0 logMAR or less (1.0 or better in decimal notation), 0.1 logMAR (between 0.0 and 0.2 logMAR), and 0.2 logMAR or higher (0.63 or worse in decimal notation). Conversely, mean UDVA values were compared across three SE ranges: within ± 0.50 D, between − 1.00 and 0.0 D, and − 1.00 D or less. In comparisons between the two IOLs, a parametric t-test or nonparametric Mann-Whitney U test was used depending on normality as assessed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Refractive tolerance was also evaluated using the segmented regression analysis. In eyes with conventional monofocal IOLs, UDVA degrades with SE [1, 6]. In contrast, enhanced-monofocal IOLs have been reported to provide a refractive range around zero defocus (emmetropia) in which the UDVA remains acceptable or better. For examining this property for XY1-EM, the segmented regression analysis was used (R version 4.2.1 with a package ‘segmented’ version 1.6-4) [11] for identifying the break point between the segment around emmetropia and a normal degradation with myopic SE. We defined SE corresponding to the break point as refractive tolerances. 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs) for the regression lines were also calculated.

Results

In total, 166 eyes from 135 patients with XY1 and 55 eyes from 28 patients with XY1-EM were eligible for analysis. Table 1 provides demographic data of the two groups; there were no significant differences. Table 2 lists postoperative visual acuity and SE values for eyes with XY1 and XY1-EM. No significant difference in UDVA was observed between the two groups.

Refractive tolerance was compared across three UDVA and SE ranges, as shown in Table 3. For the range of UDVA of 0.1 logMAR, the mean SE of eyes with XY1-EM was − 0.61 D, which was twice that of eyes with XY1 (P = 0.0025, Mann–Whitney U test). A UDVA of 0.0 logMAR or better was obtained with a mean SE of -0.33 D in the use of XY1-EM, while there were no significant differences (P = 0.088) from that of XY1 (-0.17 D). Although there were no differences in mean UDVA between the two IOLs within the SE range of ± 0.50 D, between − 1.00 and 0.0 D (P > 0.44).

Figure 2 shows the relationship between SE and UDVA of eyes with XY1 and XY1-EM. Regression of UDVA with SE was evaluated using the segmented regression analysis. In eyes with conventional XY1 IOLs (left of Fig. 2), a break point at -0.45 D (standard error of 0.123 D and R2 of 0.5609) was obtained, indicating that UDVA was stable up to an SE of -0.45D, and subsequently deteriorated with myopic SE. The slope of the myopic degradation was − 0.30251 logMAR per D (P < 0.001). The red dotted lines represent the 95% CIs of the regression lines. For eyes with enhanced XY1-EM IOLs, the break point was at -1.075 D (standard error of 0.064 D and R2 of 0.5843), indicating minimal changes in UDVA between emmetropia and myopia up to -1.075 D. Beyond this breakpoint, the slope of myopia was − 1.589 logMAR per D (P < 0.001). The 95% CIs of the regression lines were wider than those for XY1, likely owing to the smaller sample size.

Figure 3 shows photopic and mesopic contrast sensitivity results for eyes with enhanced monofocal IOL (XY1-EM). The mean logarithm contrast sensitivity values at spatial frequencies of 3, 6, 12, and 18 cpd were within norms for both illuminations.

Discussion

The influence of myopic residual refractions on UDVA in eyes with conventional and enhanced monofocal IOLs was investigated. In the comparison of refractive tolerances for three ranges of UDVA and SE, the mean SE for a UDVA of 0.1 logMAR in eyes with XY1-EM was − 0.61 ± 0.28 D, which was approximately twice that in eyes with XY1. Based on the mean ± 2 standard deviations (SD), it was demonstrated that UDVA between 0.0 and 0.2 logMAR was achieved with SE between − 0.05 D (emmetropia) to -1.17 D of myopia. Similarly, a UDVA of 0.0 logMAR or better was achieved with SE between 0.07 D (emmetropia) and − 0.73 D of myopia. The results indicate a wider tolerance to myopic residual refraction for achieving a UDVA 0.1 logMAR or better. The improvement in the myopic refractive tolerance compared with conventional monofocal IOLs has also been reported previously in studies comparing EDoF IOLs [7]. The segmented regression analysis revealed the difference in the break points as well. The break point with XY1-EM was − 1.075 D, which was double that of XY1 (-0.45 D), and the regression line around emmetropia indicated the presence of an SE range in which UDVA change was relatively slow without hyperopic change. A previous comparison between a conventional monofocal IOL (SY60WF, Alcon, Fort Worth, TX, USA) and an enhanced monofocal IOL (NSP-3, Nidek, Gamagori, Japan) also demonstrated a similar difference in the break points [6]. Overall, the present and previous findings indicate that the influence of myopic residual refractions on UDVA was different in eyes with conventional and enhanced monofocal IOLs.

In the present investigation of myopic tolerances in XY1-EM, the myopic SE range for UDVA of 0.1 logMAR or better (-1.17 D) and the break point was at -1.075 D were close to each other. Although refractive tolerance has been investigated using various analytical approaches, it has primarily been applied to examine differences between enhanced and conventional monofocal IOLs [6, 7, 9]. Refractive tolerance changes with the property of through-focus MTF. A wider refractive tolerance is anticipated with side peaks in myopia and over particular amplitudes [12]. To our knowledge, two types of enhanced monofocal IOLs, including the present one, have been investigated to date. Therefore, further studies evaluating refractive tolerance in other enhanced monofocal IOL designs are warranted.

In eyes with enhanced monofocal IOLs, the mean SE for UDVA of 0.1 logMAR was close to double. The mean SE was − 0.61 ± 0.28 D; therefore, the lower bound of the 95% CI was − 0.75 D, which was relatively higher than the break point (-1.075 D). Although the breakpoint with XY1-EM was also close to double that of one with XY1, there would be a difference between the mean SE and breakpoint owing to the inclusion of eyes of UDVA around emmetropia. Compared with the two kinds of evaluations used, we speculate that the break points would be related to the through-focus MTF properties between conventional and enhanced IOLs [6]. Further investigations are required.

The myopic refractive tolerance of the XY1-EM IOL was wider than that of the conventional XY1. As shown by the through-focus MTF at 100 lp/mm (Fig. 1), MTF values for XY1-EM were higher than values of XY1 at 0.5 D and above, in which higher MTF values would contribute to the wider tolerances until − 1.075 D. In the previous evaluation of the through-focus MTF at 50 lp/mm for NSP-3 IOL, MTF values of 0.1 or higher would contribute to the myopic tolerance. These findings suggest the presence of a threshold to maintain good UDVA. Further investigation is necessary to confirm this relationship.

This study has some limitations. First, the sample size for the enhanced monofocal IOLs and range of refractive errors were limited. More samples in a wide range of myopic SE would be desirable. However, such data are difficult to obtain, owing to recent improvements in IOL power calculations [13] and the reduced demand for near vision among patients with monofocal IOLs. Therefore, a multicenter survey would be necessary. The potential influence of different sample sizes was examined using 95% CI of the segmented regression results in Fig. 2. Second, the postoperative examination periods were not uniform, owing to the retrospective study design. The UDVA and SE records at one month postoperatively were available for eyes with XY1, while examinations were conducted one to three months postoperatively for eyes with XY1-EM. UDVA and SE measurements should be three to six months after surgery to minimize the influence of acute degradations. For minimizing the influence of acute degradations, eyes with CDVA of 0.0 logMAR or better were selected for analysis. Finally, the influence of postoperative pupil diameter and residual astigmatism was not examined. A smaller pupil diameter improves the depth of focus, increasing the refractive tolerance, whereas refractive tolerance is known to alter with astigmatism [14]. For a more precise evaluation, postoperative pupil diameter and residual astigmatism should be measured and incorporated into future analyses.

In conclusion, this retrospective study investigated the influence of myopic residual refractions on UDVA in eyes with conventional and enhanced monofocal IOLs. Quantitative evaluations demonstrated improved refractive tolerance with − 1.00 D myopia in eyes with enhanced monofocal IOLs. Furthermore, segmented regression analysis between UDVA and refractive error proved to be an effective method for evaluating tolerances to residual myopic refraction.

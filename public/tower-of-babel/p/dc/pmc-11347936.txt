The mediating role of resilience between caregiver burden and hope among patients with inflammatory bowel disease
Authors: Meijing Zhou, Mi Wang, Dan Luo, Caiyun Sun, Qiugui Bian, Jingjing Xu, Zheng Lin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimTo investigate whether resilience mediates the connection between caregiver burden and hope among caregivers of patients with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD).DesignA cross‐sectional study.MethodsTwo hundred patients with IBD were conveniently sampled from two tertiary hospitals in Jiangsu Province, China. The main instruments involved the Zarit Burden Interview, the 10‐item Connor‐Davidson resilience scale, and the Herth Hope Index. We conducted descriptive analysis and Pearson correlations using SPSS 25.0. The PROCESS v3.3 macro analysed the mediating effect. We report the results in line with the STROBE checklist.ResultsCaregiver burden was significantly negatively connected with hope and resilience, whereas resilience positively correlated with hope. The mediation role of resilience was significant in the relationship between caregiver burden and hope, with mediating effects accounting for 47.86% of the overall effect.ConclusionsResilience partially mediates the association between caregiver burden and hope among patients with IBD. This finding highlights the protective role of resilience in undermining caregiver burden and strengthening hope.Implications for PracticeIn clinical practice, healthcare providers should perform routine psychological assessments for caregivers of patients with IBD. Furthermore, resilience training should be incorporated into interventions to alleviate caregiver burden and enhance hope.Patient or Public ContributionNone.Keywords: caregiver burden, hope, inflammatory bowel disease, mediating effect, resilience

INTRODUCTION

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), including Crohn's disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis (UC), is frequently diagnosed in young adults, characterized by periodic relapse and remission (Parigi et al., 2022). Recent epidemiological data indicates an ongoing rise in IBD prevalence and incidence, particularly in newly industrialized countries (Agrawal & Jess, 2022). IBD typically manifests as abdominal pain, diarrhoea and rectal bleeding (Parigi et al., 2022). Escalating symptoms can lead to repeated emergency department and ambulatory visits and admissions. The lifelong and unpredictable nature of IBD causes patients to fear the disease's progression and worry about various aspects of life, including education, work and marriage (Knowles et al., 2018). IBD affects not only patients but also places a significant burden on their caregivers. Caregivers frequently need to assist care recipients with daily activities, provide instrumental support (such as medication management and medical visits), and offer emotional support, especially during disease flares (Shukla et al., 2018). Prolonged caregiving for individuals with IBD can impose significant physical and psychosocial strain on care providers (Shukla et al., 2018). Some caregivers reported experiencing fatigue, isolation, and, in some cases, unemployment due to the demanding nature of their caregiving responsibilities (Parekh et al., 2017; Zand et al., 2020). Moreover, caregivers of individuals with IBD exhibit an anxiety and depression rate nearly double that of healthy controls (Bisgaard et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2018). Additionally, the annual direct cost of caring for an IBD patient is approximately $22,987, imposing a considerable economic burden on the caregiver and the entire family (Park et al., 2020). Developing strategies to assist this population is of increasing importance. Wang et al. (2020) emphasized that comprehending caregivers' adaptation to caregiving stress is critical to assisting distressed caregivers.

Hope is recognized as a vital psychological resource aiding caregivers in navigating the daily challenges of caregiving (Revier et al., 2012). According to Herth (1990), hope is a dynamic inner strength that facilitates transcending the current situation and fosters a new, positive awareness of one's existence. Caregivers' hope evolves from self‐centered hope to a desire for the care recipient's improvement or relief from suffering (Duggleby et al., 2021). Elevated hope can reduce caregivers' maladjustment following a family member's diagnosis (Eche et al., 2022). Importantly, caregiver providers' hope was positively correlated with care recipients' hope (Goldzweig et al., 2016). Despite the significance of hope for caregivers, there is limited understanding of hope and its influencing factors in this population. Duggleby et al. (2021) demonstrated the necessity of considering psychosocial factors in understanding caregivers' hope, alongside the disease's status. Caregiver burden is recognized as a significant psychosocial variable that detrimentally affects hope (Li et al., 2018; Luo et al., 2021).

Caregiver burden, as defined by Zarit et al. (1986), encompasses the extent to which caregivers perceive the impact of caregiving on their physical and psychosocial functioning. Parekh et al. (2017) reported that 43.8% of caregivers experienced a sense of being overburdened, particularly pronounced during IBD recurrences. Studies beyond the scope of IBD literature have suggested an association between higher caregiver burden and diminished hope (Shahvand & Sarafraz, 2020; Simpson et al., 2021). Nevertheless, there is a paucity of studies investigating the relationship between these two variables among caregivers of patients with IBD.

Furthermore, the mechanism by which caregiver burden influences hope remains unclear. Considering that not all caregivers with high burdens necessarily have low hope, it is plausible to speculate that certain variables might mediate this relationship. Resilience, often cited as a fundamental positive psychological power, is a complex concept. Generally, resilience has three distinct definitions (Kim et al., 2019): (1) traits reflecting tenacity and adaptability in the face of stressful events; (2) favourable health outcomes in high‐risk individuals; and (3) processes successfully adapting to stressors. While there is no universally agreed‐upon definition of resilience, the significance of resilience training programs has been underscored by previous studies, indicating its modifiability (Rosenberg et al., 2019). This study defines resilience as an individual's modifiable capacity to flourish despite adversity (Parker et al., 2021). Previous research on other chronic diseases has demonstrated a positive association between resilience and hope, while revealing a negative correlation with caregiver burden (Simpson et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2020). According to Kumpfer's resilience framework (Kumpfer, 2002), caregivers with strong resilience can effectively utilize internal and external coping resources to navigate the stressors associated with caregiving, leading to favourable health outcomes. Building on this theoretical framework and the literature above review, resilience might be a potential mediator between caregiver burden and hope. However, to our knowledge, no studies have explored the interplay among these three variables.

This study endeavours to investigate the relationships among caregiver burden, resilience, and hope. We postulated the following hypotheses: (1) caregiver burden would exhibit a negative association with hope, while resilience would demonstrate a positive correlation with hope; (2) resilience would act as a mediator in the relationship between caregiver burden and hope.

METHODS

Study design

This descriptive cross‐sectional study adhered to the guidelines outlined in the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) statement. Approval for the study was granted by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital at Nanjing Medical University (2020‐SR‐498).

Participants

Using the convenience sample method from October 2020 to August 2021, we enlisted caregivers of patients with IBD (Lichtenstein et al., 2018; Rubin et al., 2019) from the gastroenterology departments of two tertiary hospitals in Jiangsu province, China. Inclusion in this study was restricted to participants who: (1) were aged ≥18 years; (2) served as primary caregivers for IBD patients, offering care for more than 6 months; (3) were immediate family members of the patients, including parents, siblings, children and spouses; (4) cohabited with the patient without receiving payment for caregiving; (5) had completed at least elementary school education, were able to read, and comprehend the questionnaire. Participants with care recipients having comorbid mental or organic diseases were excluded. Before enrolling in this study, we obtained written informed consent from each participant. The current study adhered to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki.

For a multiple regression analysis with a statistical power of 0.8, a small effect size of 0.05, and a significance threshold (α) of 0.05, a minimum of 196 samples was required (G‐Power 3.1.7). Considering invalid responses, we expanded the sample size to 231. Ultimately, data from 200 participants were used for statistical analysis, as 14 were deemed ineligible and 17 had missing data.

Measures

Demographic and clinical information

The sociodemographic information of caregivers encompassed age, sex, education level, residence, marital status, employment status and yearly family income. Disease‐related data for care recipients, extracted from electronic medical records, included disease type, time of diagnosis, disease activity, medication use and complications.

For UC, disease activity was assessed using the Walmsley Simple Colitis Activity Index (CAI) (Walmsley et al., 1998). The CAI evaluates bowel frequency throughout the day and night, defecation urgency, blood in the stool, overall health, and parenteral manifestations. Patients with UC were categorized based on their scores—four scores or fewer indicated remission, while five or more were indicative of the active stage. In the case of CD, the Harvey‐Bradshaw Index (HBI) was employed (Harvey & Bradshaw, 1980; Walsh et al., 2016), a scale widely used in clinical practice and scientific research. It comprises five aspects: general health, gastrointestinal discomfort, the frequency of watery stools per day, abdominal lumps and complications. Remission for CD patients was defined as four scores or less, while those with four or more were considered to have active disease.

Zarit Burden Interview (ZBI)

Caregiver burden was frequently measured using the Zarit Burden Interview (Zarit et al., 1980). This scale comprises 22 items that evaluate the impact of caregiving on the caregiver's health, finances, emotions, social life and interpersonal relations. Responses are provided on a 5‐point Likert scale, ranging from “never” (0 points) to “nearly always” (4 points). The total score ranges from 0 to 88 and is categorized into four levels: no or negligible (0–20), mild (21–40), moderate (41–60) and severe (61–88) burden. The ZBI demonstrated satisfactory reliability and validity when employed among caregivers of patients with IBD (Parekh et al., 2017). The Cronbach's α for the ZBI in this study was 0.90.

The 10‐item Connor‐Davidson resilience scale (CD‐RISC‐10)

The 10‐item Connor‐Davidson resilience scale (CD‐RISC‐10), a validated abbreviated version of the original 25‐item instrument, was employed to assess an individual's ability to endure hardship and stress while sustaining physical and social functioning and flourishing (Campbell‐Sills & Stein, 2007). Each of the 10 items is scored on a five‐point Likert scale (0 = never, 1 = rarely, 2 = sometimes, 3 = often, 4 = almost always). The cumulative score of this scale ranges from 0 to 40, with a higher score indicating greater resilience. The Chinese version of CD‐RISC‐10 showed excellent reliability and validity (Luo et al., 2021). In this study, the Cronbach's α for the CD‐RISC‐10 was 0.924.

Herth Hope Index (HHI)

This study utilized the Herth Hope Index (HHI), developed by Herth (1990), to evaluate participants' levels of hope. The HHI contains 12 items falling into three dimensions: temporality and future, positive readiness and expectancy, and interconnectedness. Each item offers four response options, ranging from ‘strongly disagree’ to ‘strongly agree’ (scored 1–4 points). Higher scores indicate elevated levels of hope. The degree of hope can be categorized using specific thresholds: 12–23 indicating low hope, 24–35 indicating moderate hope and 36–48 indicating strong hope. The HHI exhibits robust psychometric properties among the Chinese population (Wang et al., 2020). In this study, the HHI yielded a Cronbach's α of 0.903.

Procedures

The survey utilized structured paper‐and‐pencil questionnaires comprising four parts: sociodemographic information, the ZBI, the CD‐RISC‐10, and the HHI. Respondents completed these questionnaires in a quiet room at each clinical centre. Investigators were available to address any participant queries regarding the questionnaires. The average completion time was approximately 20 min, and all completed questionnaires underwent immediate collection and thorough inspection. In cases where missing items were identified during the on‐site check, patients were promptly informed, and the necessary information was completed.

Data analysis

The EpiData3.1 software (The EpiData Association, Odense, Denmark) was employed to double‐enter the data. Data analyses were conducted using SPSS 25.0 software. Descriptive analysis, independent t‐tests, and ANOVA were utilized to depict the characteristics and distribution of hope, considering that the variables passed the normality and homogeneity of variance tests. To explore the bivariate relationships between caregiver burden, resilience, and hope, Pearson correlations (for continuous variables with normal distribution) were calculated. The SPSS macro‐PROCESS facilitated a bias‐corrected bootstrapping study to scrutinize the mediating effects of resilience. Additionally, we controlled for several significant sociodemographic and clinical variables in the statistical analyses. A mediating effect was deemed statistically significant if the 95% confidence interval (CI) did not include zero, following the criteria established by Hayes (2013). The cutoff point for statistical significance in all analyses was set at 0.05.

RESULTS

Characteristics of the participants

Our study engaged 200 caregivers of patients with IBD. Table 1 delineates caregivers' sociodemographic characteristics, care recipients' clinical features, and hope distribution. The participants, with a mean age of 45.46 (10.87), comprised 56.5% females. A notable portion (37.5%) of participants had a high level of education, and the majority (61.5%) resided in urban areas. Among the caregivers, 2.5% were single, while the rest were married. Merely 40.0% of the sample reported a household income of 100,000 yuan or more annually. Concerning care recipients, about 32.0% fell within the age range of 18–25. Regarding disease type, 66.0% were diagnosed with CD, and the majority (80.0%) were in the active stage of the disease. Furthermore, over 80.0% of care recipients had a disease duration of less than 5 years.

Correlation analysis between variables

The average caregiver burden score was 22.15 (13.53), with 48.5% of participants surpassing the threshold of 21, indicating mild to severe burden. The total score of hope was 34.46 (6.90). Bivariate analyses unveiled a negative association between caregiver burden and hope (r = −0.436, p < 0.01), as well as resilience (r = −0.488, p < 0.01). Conversely, greater resilience positively correlated with stronger hope (r = 0.563, p < 0.01).

Mediation analysis

The mediation analysis results are presented in both Tables 2 and 3. The hope variation was significant based on caregivers' age, yearly family income, and patient's disease activity and complications (Table 1). Therefore, the mediation analysis included these four significant variables as covariates. The total effect (path c) of caregiver burden on hope was −0.351 (p < 0.01). Significantly, the coefficients of path a (β = −0.405, p < 0.01) and path b (β = 0.415, p < 0.01) indicated a negative association between caregiver burden and resilience, and a positive association between resilience and hope. The indirect effect (path a × b) from caregiver burden to resilience to hope was −0.168 (95% CI = −0.172, −0.020), implying that the mediating effect accounted for 47.86% of the overall effect of caregiver burden on hope. The direct effect (path c’) of caregiver burden on hope was −0.183 (p < 0.01), demonstrating that resilience partially mediated the relationship between caregiver burden and hope. DISCUSSION

The primary finding of this study reveals that caregivers of IBD patients exhibit a medium level of hope, and hope is negatively associated with caregiver burden. Notably, resilience emerges as a mediator in the relationship between caregiver burden and hope among the IBD population, even after statistically controlling for caregivers' age, yearly family income, and patients' disease activity and complications. These results align with our initial hypotheses and have implications for developing interventions to bolster hope in caregivers of patients with IBD.

The average hope score indicates a medium level of hope in caregivers of patients with IBD, which is lower than that reported for caregivers of patients with advanced cancer (Bovero et al., 2022; Duggleby et al., 2015) and Alzheimer's disease (Duggleby et al., 2011). Given that IBD is frequently diagnosed in young adults, characterized by a lifelong, episodic, and unpredictable nature, caregivers often harbour pessimistic views about the patient's future, including aspects such as education, employment and marriage (Liu et al., 2018; Zand et al., 2020). This likely contributes to the comparatively lower hope observed in this group. Moreover, it is noteworthy that the mean score of interconnectedness was the lowest among all domains of hope in this study, signifying a significant impairment in the social interactions of caregivers. This finding may be attributed to the social isolation from intensive caregiving during disease flare‐ups (Shukla et al., 2018), especially considering that 80.0% of the patients enrolled in this study were in an active disease.

Currently, the average caregiver burden score among patients with IBD is comparable to that reported for caregivers of patients with breast cancer (Li et al., 2018) and type 1 diabetes (Luo et al., 2021). In this study, approximately 48.5% of the participants experienced caregiver burden, a higher proportion than reported in a previous study (Zand et al., 2020). The discrepancy may be attributed to differences in the disease activity of care recipients. Zand and colleagues' study (2020) included 49.0% of IBD patients in the stage of disease relapse, while in our study, 80.0% of patients had active disease. Disease activity is a prominent determinant of caregiver burden (Parekh et al., 2017). To date, the link between caregiver burden and hope has primarily been explored in limited chronic illnesses, such as cancer (Shahvand & Sarafraz, 2020), schizophrenia (Wang et al., 2020), traumatic brain injury, and spinal cord injury (Simpson et al., 2021). Consistent with previous studies, our study also found that caregivers perceiving higher burdens exhibited a lower level of hope. Given that repeated relapses and remissions characterize IBD, caregivers must adapt to these challenges throughout their lifespan. Liu et al. (2018) reported that caregivers of IBD patients experience levels of anxiety and depression comparable to IBD patients themselves. These negative emotions create difficulties for caregivers in building confidence and actively coping with caregiving tasks, ultimately diminishing hope (Duggleby et al., 2021). Moreover, long‐term therapy and care often place a significant financial burden on the patient's caregiver and the entire family, leading to a substantial decrease in family income (Klomberg et al., 2022; Zand et al., 2020). According to Wang et al., lower family income is associated with lower levels of hope among caregivers (Wang et al., 2020). Consequently, healthcare practitioners in IBD settings should routinely assess caregiver burden and provide timely and practical support to enhance caregivers' hope.

It is worth noting that not all caregivers experiencing higher caregiver burdens have poor hope. Some authors argue that when exploring how caregiver burden exerts detrimental effects, other variables should be taken into account. A significant finding of our study was that resilience mediated the associations between caregiver burden and hope. This finding advances our understanding of the potential mechanisms through which caregiver burden affects hope, filling a gap in the current literature. Caring for patients with IBD is considered a chronic stressor for caregivers. Kumpfer's resilience framework (Kumpfer, 2002) emphasizes that resilient individuals can adapt well to stress and achieve positive health outcomes. Additionally, higher resilience is linked to increased interpersonal interaction (Ungar & Theron, 2020). Similarly, relevant neuro‐mechanisms suggest that resilience may activate specific brain regions and neural circuits, prompting individuals to behave prosocially (Charney, 2004; van der Werff et al., 2013). Interpersonal interaction has been shown to alleviate caregiver burden and increase hope (Ferrell & Wittenberg, 2017; Parekh et al., 2017). Hence, resilience constitutes a valuable target for intervention programs aiming to enhance hope among caregivers of patients with IBD.

Limitations

Several limitations exist in this study. Firstly, A cross‐sectional study could not confirm the causal relationships between caregiver burden, resilience and hope. Longitudinal studies are needed to validate the effects of caregiver burden and resilience on hope, providing more robust and credible conclusions. Secondly, the use of convenience sampling from two tertiary hospitals in a single city introduces selection bias, potentially limiting the generalizability of our findings. Future research could consider random sampling from hospitals with different medical levels to enhance the external validity of the results. Thirdly, except for disease activity data, all other information was self‐reported by participants, potentially introducing recall and reporting bias. Moreover, a significant proportion of the included patients were in the stage of disease flares, which could contribute to the instability of our findings, despite controlling for disease activity in the mediation analysis.

Implications

There are several implications for IBD care and future research. Our findings unequivocally demonstrate the negative impact of caregiver burden on the hope of caregivers. Thus, addressing the burden they carry is a pivotal step to elevate caregivers' hope levels. Shukla et al. (2018) emphasize that managing the patient's condition and offering coping suggestions can alleviate the caregiver's burden, preventing them from feeling overwhelmed. Healthcare providers should proactively offer educational materials, provide problem‐focused advice, and share referral information. Furthermore, this study establishes the mediating effect of resilience between caregiver burden and hope, contributing a potential mechanism to prior research that elucidates how caregiver burden influences hope. This underscores the imperative to assess and enhance the resilience of caregivers in clinical practice. Resilience‐enhancing programs tailored for caregivers of patients with chronic diseases, such as Stress Management and Resiliency Training (SMART) (Parker et al., 2021) and Promoting Resilience in Stress Management (PRISM) (Rosenberg et al., 2019) interventions, offer valuable strategies including cognitive reframing, stress management and coping skill development. We recommend that healthcare professionals incorporate resilience evaluation into caregivers' psychological screening and integrate resilience training into programs designed to improve hope.

CONCLUSIONS

In summary, this study reveals that caregivers of patients with IBD endure a significant burden, leading to decreased hope. Caregiver burden and resilience emerge as critical determinants of caregivers' hope. The results emphasize the pivotal role of resilience in bolstering caregivers' hope despite the challenges posed by a high caregiver burden. We advocate for the routine assessment of caregiver burden and resilience in clinical care. Moreover, integrating resilience training into future interventions is recommended to alleviate caregiver burden, fostering higher levels of hope.

RELEVANCY STATEMENT

The findings underscore the crucial role of resilience in mitigating caregiver burden and nurturing hope, pivotal aspects for nurses to contemplate in providing care to patients and their families. By showcasing the potential of resilience training as a nursing intervention, this study provides practical insights that nurses can incorporate into their clinical practice. Consequently, this study carries substantial implications for the nursing field, as it advocates for developing evidence‐based interventions to aid caregivers and improve patient outcomes.

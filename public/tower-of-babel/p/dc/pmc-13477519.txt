Post-marketing safety signals, therapeutic failure, and product-use issues reported with injectable Cabenuva: A FAERS disproportionality analysis
Authors: Jiahui Li, Hui Qiu, Jianzhu Zhou, Jiamiao Liu, Chengxian Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCabenuva (cabotegravir/rilpivirine) is the first complete long-acting injectable regimen for HIV-1 treatment. As its use expands, post-marketing surveillance should address not only conventional adverse reactions, but also therapeutic failure and product-use or administration-related issues. This study aimed to characterize Cabenuva-associated reports submitted to the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) from Q4 2019 to Q3 2024.MethodsCabenuva-associated FAERS reports were extracted and coded using MedDRA v27.1. Disproportionality analysis and Bayesian models were applied for signal detection. Subgroup analyses by sex and age were conducted using reporting odds ratios and interpreted as exploratory. Time-to-onset (TTO) was calculated from treatment start date to event onset date when valid dates were available.ResultsA total of 3,497 reports including 22,738 Cabenuva-associated event records were identified. The detected PTs included established adverse reactions, therapeutic failure or disease-progression terms, product-use and administration issues, exposure circumstances, and sparse unexpected findings. Frequently reported clinically relevant events included injection-site pain, injection-site nodule, and depression. Prominent implementation-related terms included product dose omission issue, inappropriate schedule of product administration, and product administration error. Sparse unexpected PTs were interpreted as hypothesis-generating observations rather than established adverse reactions.ConclusionsThis study provides a broad post-marketing signal-screening profile of Cabenuva, highlighting both known safety concerns and real-world implementation issues such as missed doses, administration problems, product issues, and therapeutic failure. The findings support careful clinical monitoring, adherence to administration guidance, staff training, and further validation of sparse unexpected signals.Keywords: Cabenuva, HIV-1, disproportionality, pharmacovigilance, FAERS

1. Introduction

Human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) is a virus that weakens the immune system, thereby impairing the body’s ability to fight infections and diseases. 1 An estimated 39.9 million individuals worldwide are living with HIV, and HIV-associated mortality was about 630,000 deaths in 2022. 2 Four decades after its identification in 1984, HIV infections remain one of the major public health concerns nowadays. 3 HIV infections are divided into two types: human immunodeficiency virus type 1 (HIV-1) and human immunodeficiency virus type 2 (HIV-2). HIV-2 is less infectious and typically presents with lower viral loads, resulting in slower disease progression. HIV-1 is the predominant cause of acquired immune deficiency syndrome (AIDS) worldwide, with an estimated 38 million people living with HIV-1. 4 HIV is now commonly managed as a long-term chronic disease, typically necessitating lifelong daily oral medication. 5 Without a curative therapy or an effective vaccine, antiretroviral therapy (ART) remains the cornerstone for both HIV management and prevention, 6 effectively increasing life expectancy and reducing mortality among those affected by HIV. However, traditional oral ART can increase the likelihood of resistance when adherence declines, often influenced by HIV-related stigma and long-term “pill fatigue” from daily dosing. 7

Cabenuva (cabotegravir/rilpivirine) is a long-acting injectable regimen combining cabotegravir, a second-generation integrase strand transfer inhibitor, with rilpivirine, a non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor. It is the first complete long-acting injectable regimen approved for the treatment of HIV-1. 8 Current prescribing information supports its use in virologically suppressed adults and adolescents aged at least 12 years and weighing at least 35 kg who are receiving a stable antiretroviral regimen, have no history of treatment failure, and have no known or suspected resistance to cabotegravir or rilpivirine. Cabenuva may be initiated with an optional oral lead-in to assess tolerability, or patients may proceed directly to intramuscular injections according to the prescribing information. 9 The prolonged absorption of cabotegravir and rilpivirine after intramuscular administration supports approved dosing once monthly or every 2 months. 10

Registrational trials demonstrated that Cabenuva maintained virologic suppression under controlled trial conditions, 11 whereas real-world studies have highlighted its acceptability and potential to reduce the burden of daily oral therapy. 12 These benefits must be distinguished from post-marketing implementation experience because long-acting injectable therapy requires adherence to scheduled visits and appropriate management of missed doses. Treatment interruption may increase the risk of viral rebound, resistance, and therapeutic failure. 9 Accordingly, post-marketing surveillance is important both for identifying potential safety signals and for characterizing implementation challenges that may affect the safe and effective use of Cabenuva.

The FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) is among the world’s largest pharmacovigilance databases. 13 A prior FAERS analysis by McCall et al. focused on therapeutic failure reported with long-acting HIV injectables from 2021 to 2024. 14 Building on this work, the present study applied signal-detection approaches to evaluate a broader spectrum of Cabenuva-associated reports, including adverse events, therapeutic failure terms, and product-use or administration-related issues, with the goal of informing clinical monitoring and safer implementation of this regimen.

2. Methods

2.1. Study design and data collection

Cabenuva was approved by the FDA in January 2021. This retrospective pharmacovigilance study used FAERS reports covering Q4 2019 through Q3 2024 and followed READUS-PV reporting principles. This period denotes the overall FAERS data-extraction window. No Cabenuva-associated reports were identified before the FDA approval date; therefore, the final analytical dataset did not contain any pre-approval Cabenuva reports. FAERS comprises seven core files: DEMO (demographic/administrative data), DRUG (medication details), REAC (reported adverse events), OUTC (patient outcomes), RPSR (report sources), THER (drug therapy start/end dates), and INDI (indications). Records were linked using “PRIMARYID” and “CASEID”. Because the study relied on de-identified, publicly available reports and involved no clinical intervention or biospecimen collection, institutional ethics approval was not required.

2.2. Data pre-processing

A total of 8,747,158 FAERS case reports were retrieved and loaded into MySQL 8.0. Deduplication was performed at the report level before Cabenuva-specific extraction. Following FDA recommendations, when records shared the same CASEID, the record with the most recent FDA_DT was retained; if FDA_DT was identical, the record with the larger PRIMARYID was retained. This procedure removed 1,260,597 duplicate or superseded reports.15,16 Because a single retained report may contain multiple MedDRA preferred terms (PTs), PT multiplicity within each report was preserved for signal detection.

2.3. Data extraction

The FAERS DRUG file was searched using predefined drug-name terms, including the brand name “Cabenuva” and the generic combination term “cabotegravir rilpivirine” as represented in FAERS combination fields. DRUGNAME and, when available, PROD_AI were searched case-insensitively after leading and trailing spaces were removed. No additional automated synonym mapping or drug-name normalization was applied beyond these predefined terms and basic text standardization. Reports listing Cabenuva as the PS or SS drug were included to capture the broader reporting spectrum of product-use issues, administration problems, and therapeutic failure. The reported events associated with Cabenuva were classified at the system organ class (SOC) and preferred term (PT) levels using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA, version 27.1). For clinical interpretation, PT-level findings were additionally assigned to mutually exclusive clinical categories. Categories were assigned according to PT meaning, corresponding SOC, relevance to Cabenuva use and administration, current prescribing information, and prior FAERS literature on therapeutic failure.9,14 Sparse unexpected PTs were grouped separately as hypothesis-generating findings; this interpretive categorization did not replace the original MedDRA SOC classification. The final analytical dataset comprised 3,497 Cabenuva-associated reports containing 22,738 PT-level event records.

2.4. Data analysis

We summarized demographic characteristics of Cabenuva-associated reports, including sex, age, reporting region, outcomes, and reported indications. Categorical variables are presented as counts and proportions, while continuous variables are described using the median and interquartile range. 16

This study employed both frequency-based and Bayesian methods for signal detection. Disproportionality analysis was used to identify drug-event combinations reported more frequently than expected relative to the background FAERS database. Cabenuva-associated reports were compared with all other FAERS reports from the same study period, with Cabenuva-related reports excluded from the comparator group. The frequency-based methods primarily included the Reporting Odds Ratio (ROR), 17 Proportional Reporting Ratio (PRR), 18 while the Bayesian methods primarily consisted of the Bayesian Confidence Propagation Neural Network (BCPNN) 19 and Empirical Bayesian Geometric Mean (EBGM). 20 Calculations for these methods were based on 2 × 2 contingency tables (Supplementary Tables 1 and 2). Detected signals were interpreted as reporting associations, not as incidence rates, relative risks, or confirmed causal adverse drug reactions. Unexpected PTs were discussed individually only when they had at least 10 reports, showed concordance across multiple algorithms, and had plausible clinical or pharmacological relevance. Lower-count unexpected PTs were retained only as hypothesis-generating findings.

To evaluate potential reporting differences by sex and age, we used the ROR method to conduct subgroup analyses for the five most frequently reported clinically relevant PTs. The 45-year cutoff was selected pragmatically to obtain more comparable subgroup sizes. P values were derived from chi-square tests using 2×2 contingency tables. An ROR > 1 with P < 0.05 indicated a higher reporting odds among females or individuals aged >=45 years, whereas an ROR < 1 with P < 0.05 indicated higher reporting odds among males or individuals aged <45 years. (Supplementary Table 3).

2.5. Time to onset (TTO) analysis

Time to onset was calculated as the difference between EVENT_DT (event-onset date) and START_DT (treatment-start date). We summarized onset times using the median and interquartile range, after excluding reports with missing/invalid dates and those in which the event occurred before therapy initiation. 21

3. Results

Within the Q4 2019-Q3 2024 extraction window, 3,497 reports listing Cabenuva as the PS or SS drug were identified; all were submitted after FDA approval. These reports contained 22,738 PT-level event records. Disproportionality analysis yielded 1,021 PT-level signals. Of these, 269 met the predefined criteria across all four algorithms. (Figure 1).

3.1. Descriptive characteristics

The data indicated that males accounted for 49.41% of reports and females for 19.13%. However, it should be emphasized that sex information was missing in 31.46% of the reports. The age group with the most reports was 18 to <45 years (17.53%), followed by 45 to <65 years (13.78%). Similarly, it should be noted that 66% of the cases had missing age data. Over time, the number of reports increased annually, with a significant rise in 2023 (36.23%) compared to 2021 (5.32%). Reports mainly came from health professionals (36.95%), followed by medical doctors (21.36%) and pharmacists (20.88%). Nearly all reports originated from the United States, accounting for 95.85%. For clinical outcomes, outcome information was missing or unclear in most reports, including missing outcome information in 83.19% and other outcomes in 10.92%. Among reports with recorded outcomes, hospitalization was the most frequently reported serious outcome (3.55%) (Table1).

3.2. SOC signals

Cabenuva-associated PT-level event records mapped to 27 MedDRA SOCs. The reporting frequencies and disproportionality results for these SOCs are summarized in Table 2. At the SOC level, General disorders and administration site conditions and Psychiatric disorders were broadly consistent with known adverse reactions described in the product information, whereas Investigations mainly reflected clinically relevant virologic or laboratory monitoring findings. Injury, poisoning and procedural complications and Infections and infestations were also reported at relatively high frequencies. The prominence of the former largely reflected product-use and administration-related terms, whereas the latter may partly reflect underlying HIV-associated infections or disease-related conditions. Endocrine disorders comprised only 19 event records; despite their high disproportionality estimates, these signals should be interpreted cautiously pending further validation.

In Table 3, the most frequently reported PTs were not limited to conventional adverse reactions. Product-use/administration issues were prominent, including product dose omission issue (n = 1417), product storage error (n = 417), and inappropriate schedule of product administration (n = 337). Therapeutic failure/virologic monitoring terms were also frequently reported, including viral load increased (n = 954), virologic failure (n = 435), and pathogen resistance (n = 336). Injection-site/local tolerability events included injection-site pain (n = 1200), injection-site nodule (n = 341), and injection-site swelling (n = 158). Among conventional clinical adverse events, pain (n = 588), condition aggravated (n = 278), and depression (n = 210) were relatively common. These findings were broadly consistent with the Cabenuva prescribing information and clinical trial findings.

When ranked by EBGM signal strength, several therapeutic failure/virologic monitoring terms and product-use/administration issues showed strong disproportionality, including product use in unapproved therapeutic environment (EBGM = 1685.92), viral load increased (EBGM = 666.40), and viral load abnormal (EBGM = 612.47). Infection- or disease-related terms such as oropharyngeal gonococcal infection (EBGM = 863.03), proctitis gonococcal (EBGM = 739.74), and HIV associated nephropathy (EBGM = 398.32) also showed high disproportionality estimates, although most of these were based on small case numbers.

Sparse unexpected PTs, including myocardial oedema, protein S deficiency, anal cancer stage III, hypergonadism, decerebration, cryptogenic cirrhosis, first bite syndrome, gastric adenoma, asthenospermia, sinus perforation, alcoholic seizure, and cough variant asthma, were grouped separately as hypothesis-generating findings. These findings should therefore be regarded as preliminary signal-detection results that may provide useful clues for clinical surveillance, but the potential association between Cabenuva and these PTs requires further validation in studies with more complete clinical information.

3.4. Subgroup analysis

In the exploratory sex subgroup analysis of patients using Cabenuva, injection-site nodule showed higher reporting odds among females, whereas injection-site pain and myalgia showed higher reporting odds among males.

In the exploratory age subgroup analysis, reports were further stratified by age as <45 years and ≥45 years. Depression and injection-site swelling showed higher reporting odds among individuals aged ≥45 years. Sex and age information was missing in 31.46% and 66.00% of reports, respectively. Therefore, these findings represent exploratory reporting differences rather than established sex- or age-specific risks (Figure 2).

3.5. TTO analysis

After excluding reports with missing, invalid, or implausible onset-time information, 351 reports remained for descriptive TTO analysis, representing 10.04% of all Cabenuva-associated reports. The median TTO was 52 days, with an interquartile range of 1–198 days. Among these 351 evaluable reports, 144 events occurred within the first 30 days after Cabenuva initiation, accounting for 41.03% of the evaluable TTO reports. In addition, 142 reports occurred more than 90 days after treatment initiation, accounting for 40.46% of the evaluable TTO reports. Given that 89.96% of reports were excluded due to missing or implausible date information, substantial limitations in data completeness and reliability may constrain a comprehensive assessment of Cabenuva’s safety profile. 4. Discussion

In our analysis, reports involving male patients were more frequent than those involving female patients, reflecting the sex distribution of Cabenuva-associated reports in FAERS. The year-by-year increase in report counts may reflect expanded Cabenuva use, increased awareness of spontaneous reporting, and ongoing improvements in reporting practices. Most submissions were filed by healthcare professionals, consistent with their heightened recognition of medication-related reactions. The predominance of U.S.-origin reports mainly reflects FAERS reporting patterns and the U.S.-centered structure of the database. 22 Finally, outcome information was substantially incomplete, with 83.19% of reports lacking outcome information and 10.92% having other outcomes. Therefore, the safety profile of Cabenuva should be assessed together with additional clinical and epidemiological evidence.

Cabenuva is approved as a complete long-acting injectable regimen for virologically suppressed adults and adolescents who meet the eligibility criteria in the prescribing information. 23 It is administered by healthcare professionals as intramuscular gluteal injections and may be given monthly or every 2 months according to the approved dosing schedule. The prescribing information highlights hypersensitivity reactions, hepatotoxicity, depressive disorders, post-injection reactions, and the risk of virologic failure or resistance when dosing is not maintained appropriately. 9 In recent years, Cabenuva-related studies have largely centered on clinical trials, treatment effectiveness, and narrative reviews, whereas comprehensive real-world pharmacovigilance assessments remain limited. McCall et al. previously identified therapeutic failure, product dose omission, and injection-site pain as prominent FAERS reports for long-acting HIV injectables. 14 Our findings are consistent with that work but extend it by applying four disproportionality algorithms, evaluating both SOC- and PT-level signals, reorganizing PT-level findings into clinically meaningful categories, and examining a broader spectrum of product-use, administration, exposure, and disease-related terms.

The 3,497 Cabenuva-associated reports contained 22,738 PT-level event records and showed that the post-marketing reporting profile extends beyond conventional adverse reactions. It is noteworthy that “Injury, poisoning and procedural complications” ranked prominently among the SOCs, largely reflecting the high frequency of product-use and administration-related reports. Frequent PTs in this category included product dose omission issue, product storage error, and inappropriate schedule of product administration. These findings suggest that the real-world implementation of long-acting injectable antiretroviral therapy may be substantially more complex than administration under controlled clinical trial conditions. Because Cabenuva requires scheduled injections administered by healthcare professionals, missed or delayed doses, improper product handling, or administration errors may compromise treatment continuity and potentially contribute to viral rebound, therapeutic failure, and resistance. Accordingly, these findings highlight the importance of structured appointment-tracking systems, standardized procedures for missed-dose management, appropriate storage and preparation workflows, and timely clinical reassessment when scheduled injections are missed. 24

Importantly, because Cabenuva is administered by healthcare professionals rather than self-administered by patients, these reports underscore the importance of comprehensive implementation strategies involving both patients and clinical staff. Patients should receive clear counseling regarding the importance of attending scheduled injection visits and promptly reporting missed appointments. Healthcare professionals, particularly those with limited experience administering Cabenuva, should receive standardized, competency-based training covering appropriate patient selection, indications and contraindications, product storage and preparation, injection-site selection, appropriate needle length, gluteal intramuscular administration, and correct injection techniques. The prescribing information recommends the ventrogluteal site, permits the dorsogluteal site, and emphasizes the use of a needle of sufficient length to ensure intramuscular rather than inadvertent subcutaneous administration. General intramuscular-injection guidance also supports the use of the Z-track technique to reduce medication tracking and leakage into subcutaneous tissue. 9,23,25

Injection-site and local tolerability events were also prominent in our analysis, including injection-site pain, injection-site nodule, and injection-site swelling. These findings are consistent with the Cabenuva prescribing information and the pivotal ATLAS and FLAIR trials, in which injection-site reactions (ISRs) were among the most frequently reported adverse events, possibly owing to the relatively large injection volume (3 mL) required for the loading doses.9,11,24 Similar to the clinical trial findings, injection-site nodules, discomfort, and swelling were also commonly reported in our study. Importantly, previous studies have shown that the frequency of ISRs declines progressively over time, and when they occur, they are generally transient and mild to moderate in severity. 26 This pattern suggests that the initial peak in ISR frequency may reflect patients adapting to a new mode of drug delivery, with local reactions becoming less common as both patients and healthcare providers become more familiar with the intramuscular injection procedure. 11 Practical measures such as allowing the medication to reach room temperature before administration, injecting slowly, encouraging relaxation of the gluteal muscles, and providing appropriate post-injection supportive care may further improve injection tolerability and the overall implementation of long-acting therapy. 26

Therapeutic failure and virologic monitoring-related PTs, including viral load increased, virologic failure, and pathogen resistance, represented another important group of signals. These findings are clinically relevant because long-acting ART requires timely and sustained administration to maintain virologic suppression. However, FAERS lacks sufficient clinical details to determine the specific causes of therapeutic failure; nevertheless, these signals indicate the need for enhanced clinical monitoring.

Several infection- or disease-related PTs, including oropharyngeal gonococcal infection, proctitis gonococcal, and HIV associated nephropathy showed disproportionality signals in our analysis. These reports should be interpreted cautiously, as they may reflect underlying HIV-associated susceptibility to infections, comorbid conditions, healthcare-seeking behavior, or reporting patterns rather than direct toxic effects of Cabenuva. In particular, sexually transmitted infections and other HIV-associated infectious conditions should not be considered evidence of drug-induced toxicity without case-level clinical validation.

Among these findings, hepatitis B virus (HBV)-related reports deserve particular attention. Current guidelines recommend that individuals switching to Cabenuva should not have active HBV infection unless they are receiving appropriate HBV-active therapy.27,28 Nevertheless, a recent case report described acute HBV infection occurring after switching to Cabenuva despite prior HBV vaccination, highlighting that protective immunity may not be absolute in all individuals. 29 These observations reinforce the importance of comprehensive baseline HBV screening and continued virological monitoring throughout treatment.

People living with HIV may also experience varying degrees of immune dysfunction, increasing their susceptibility to opportunistic infections and immune-mediated complications. Notably, case reports have described immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS) following initiation of Cabenuva, presenting with paradoxical clinical deterioration characterized by fever, night sweats, and exaggerated inflammatory responses. 30 Although causal relationships cannot be established from spontaneous reports alone, these observations underscore the need for careful patient selection, individualized monitoring, and further research to better define the optimal use of Cabenuva, particularly in individuals with advanced HIV disease or higher viral loads.

Hypersensitivity reactions and psychiatric events require particular clinical attention because they are described in the prescribing information. Our analysis detected signals related to psychiatric disorders, including depression, psychotic disorder, aggression, and mental disorder. These findings support the need for clinical vigilance in patients with current or prior psychiatric symptoms. Regarding hypersensitivity reactions, FAERS does not provide sufficient information to determine whether an oral lead-in was used before intramuscular injection. Because long-acting cabotegravir/rilpivirine can remain in the body for an extended period after injection, suspected hypersensitivity reactions require careful clinical evaluation and timely management. Future studies with case-level clinical information are needed to characterize hypersensitivity events and identify populations at higher risk. 9

Among the unexpected PT-level signals, only myocardial oedema met our predefined criteria for further evaluation, with 18 reports, concordant signals across multiple disproportionality algorithms, and plausible clinical relevance. Other unexpected PTs included protein S deficiency, anal cancer stage III, hypergonadism, decerebration, cryptogenic cirrhosis, first bite syndrome, gastric adenoma, asthenospermia, sinus perforation, alcoholic seizure, and cough variant asthma. Although these low-count PTs showed concordant signals across multiple disproportionality algorithms, they were retained only as hypothesis-generating findings. Spontaneous reporting data cannot establish causality, and these associations may be influenced by underlying HIV-related diseases, comorbidities, concomitant medications, confounding by indication, reporting bias, or coding practices. Therefore, these low-count findings should be interpreted cautiously.

Given that myocardial oedema was the only unexpected PT meeting our predefined criteria, this signal warrants additional pharmacovigilance attention. Previous studies have shown that people living with HIV may exhibit persistent low-grade myocardial inflammation despite effective antiretroviral therapy, which may contribute to myocardial fibrosis, pericardial abnormalities, and subsequent cardiac dysfunction. 31 Consequently, the observed disproportionality signal may reflect the underlying cardiovascular risk profile of this population rather than a direct adverse effect of Cabenuva. Nevertheless, because myocardial oedema has not been previously described as a potential safety signal associated with Cabenuva, continued pharmacovigilance and further epidemiological studies with detailed clinical information are warranted to clarify this observation.

Taken together, the present findings suggest that the post-marketing reporting profile of Cabenuva includes not only conventional adverse reactions, but also clinically important implementation-related issues and therapeutic failure/virologic monitoring terms. From a public health perspective, dose omissions, scheduling problems, storage or preparation issues, administration errors, and virologic failure may be more immediately actionable than sparse unexpected PTs. Optimizing appointment systems, missed-dose management, clinical staff training, injection procedures, and viral-load monitoring may therefore be central to the safe and effective real-world delivery of long-acting injectable ART.

The sex- and age-stratified analyses should be interpreted cautiously because of the substantial missingness of demographic information. Sex information was missing in 31.46% of reports, and age information was missing in 66.00% of reports. Therefore, these subgroup findings should be regarded as exploratory reporting differences. In the sex-stratified analysis, injection-site nodule showed higher reporting odds among female reports, whereas injection-site pain and myalgia showed higher reporting odds among male reports. In the age-stratified analysis, using the age grouping of <45 years and ≥45 years, depression and injection-site swelling showed higher reporting odds among reports from individuals aged ≥45 years. These patterns may be influenced by incomplete demographic information, differential reporting behavior, underlying comorbidities, injection-related factors, body habitus, healthcare-seeking behavior, or other unmeasured confounders. Although these exploratory findings may provide useful clues for clinical monitoring, they should not be interpreted as evidence of true biological susceptibility. Further studies with more complete demographic and clinical information are needed to validate these subgroup observations.

In this study, TTO was evaluated in 351 reports with complete date information, representing only 10.04% of all Cabenuva-associated reports. The median TTO was 52 days, and 144 of the 351 evaluable reports occurred within the first 30 days after treatment initiation. Although this suggests that a considerable proportion of reported events with available dates occurred early after Cabenuva initiation, the finding should be interpreted with caution because 89.96% of reports were excluded due to missing, invalid, or implausible date information. Reports occurring more than 90 days after treatment initiation were also observed, suggesting that continued follow-up may be useful during Cabenuva treatment. However, given the substantial incompleteness of onset-time data in FAERS, these TTO findings should be regarded as exploratory and descriptive, and should not be used to infer the true temporal risk profile of Cabenuva. Further studies using data sources with more complete treatment-start and event-onset dates are needed to better characterize the timing of Cabenuva-associated events.

Although we evaluated Cabenuva-associated post-marketing reports using FAERS, several inherent limitations of this database should be acknowledged. First, FAERS is based on spontaneous reporting and is therefore susceptible to underreporting, differential reporting, and other reporting biases. In addition, many reports lack granular clinical details (such as comorbidities, concurrent medications, as well as complete demographic or anthropometric data), which may reduce estimate precision and limit the representativeness and generalizability of our findings. 32 Second, because FAERS is primarily administered by the U.S. FDA, the dataset disproportionately reflects reporting patterns and medication use in the United States, which may not accurately capture safety signals in other regions. 33 Third, we included reports in which Cabenuva was recorded as either the primary suspect or secondary suspect drug. This approach was used to capture a broader real-world reporting spectrum, including product-use problems, administration issues, therapeutic failure, and implementation-related events. However, inclusion of secondary suspect reports may increase confounding and reduce causal interpretability. Because a PS-only sensitivity analysis was not performed, the potential influence of secondary suspect reports could not be fully excluded. Fourth, disproportionality methods identify statistical signals rather than causal relationships; confirmation through prospective studies and other robust designs is still required. Nonetheless, our results offer useful real-world evidence to support clinical vigilance and the safer use of Cabenuva. 34

5. Conclusion

In summary, we conducted a systematic, comprehensive assessment of Cabenuva-associated reports using FAERS. The post-marketing reporting profile included not only conventional adverse reactions, such as injection-site/local tolerability events and psychiatric events, but also clinically important therapeutic failure/virologic monitoring terms and product-use or administration-related issues. Product dose omission, inappropriate dosing schedules, storage or preparation problems, administration errors, and virologic failure-related reports may have important implications for the real-world implementation of long-acting injectable ART.

These findings highlight the need for structured appointment tracking, standardized missed-dose management, appropriate storage and preparation procedures, clinical staff training, and continued viral-load monitoring during Cabenuva treatment. Several sparse unexpected PT-level findings were also identified; however, these should be regarded as preliminary and hypothesis-generating rather than established adverse reactions. Collectively, these findings may support healthcare professionals in monitoring safety and optimizing the clinical use of Cabenuva. Future validation through prospective cohort studies, case-level review, and mechanistic research is warranted.

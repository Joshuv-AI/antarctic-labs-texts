Effects of DNMT1 and TET2 on proliferation and differentiation of goose embryonic myoblasts
Authors: Yong Li, Yuanhao Han, Shiqi Guo, Yongquan Luo, Pufei Hong, Ziyang Liu, Xiujin Li, Yunbo Tian, Yunmao Huang, Zhongping Wu, Xumeng Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractShitou goose (large goose breed) and Wuzong goose (small goose breed) are important local goose breeds in Guangdong Province, exhibiting significant differences in growth performance and slaughter traits. The number of myofibers in goose skeletal muscle is determined during embryonic stage, and is subject to multiple myogenic factors and epigenetic regulation during myogenesis, among which DNA methylation is the most common and obvious epigenetic modification. In this study, we examined the expression profiles of DNA methyltransferases (DNMT1, DNMT3A, DNMT3B), DNA hydroxylases (TET1, TET2, TET3) and key myogenic factors (MYOD, MYF5, MYOG, MYHC) in embryonic and adult myoblasts, and performed DNMT1 and TET2 over-expression and interference experiments on goose embryonic myoblasts in combination with co-treatments of BC339 (TETs inhibitor) and 5AZA (DNMTs inhibitor). RT-qPCR analysis revealed Wuzong geese embryonic myoblasts exhibited significantly higher mRNA levels of TET1, TET2, and TET3 hydroxylases during early stages (E10–E22), along with elevated MYOD expression, while Shitou geese embryonic myoblasts showed increased DNMT1 and DNMT3B methyltransferases and up-regulated late-stage myogenic factors MYOG and MYHC. In myoblasts, TET2 expression peaked during proliferation, whereas DNMT1 dominated differentiation, highlighting their stage-specific regulatory roles. DNMT1 over-expression suppressed proliferation and differentiation, lower MYOD and MYHC levels, while DNMT1 interference enhanced both processes. 5AZA reversed DNMT1-mediated suppression, whereas BC339 exacerbated it. Conversely, TET2 over-expression promoted proliferation and differentiation, increased MYOD and MYHC levels, with 5AZA amplifying and BC339 attenuating these effects. TET2 interference impaired proliferation and differentiation, partially rescued by 5AZA. In this study, we investigated the relationship between DNA methylation and the proliferation and differentiation ability of goose embryonic myoblasts, which provided a theoretical basis for a systematic understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying the differences in growth performance of goose breeds of different body sizes.Key words: DNA methylation, Goose embryonic myoblast, Proliferation and differentiation, TET2, DNMT1

Introduction

China is the largest producer and consumer of geese in the world. According to the data provided by the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO), 569 million commercial geese will be slaughtered in 2024, producing over 1.75 million tons of meat, with a production value of 59.2 billion yuan, representing an 11.26% year-on-year growth. Guangdong Province is the largest producer and consumer of geese in China, with an annual output exceeding 80 million geese. The Shitou goose and the Wuzong goose are two key local breeds in Guangdong Province. The Shitou goose is among the largest goose species globally, reaching an average weight of 8-13 kg at 70-90 days of age, whereas the Wuzong goose is one of the smallest, averaging 2-3 kg within the same growth period (Tang et al., 2021). Despite sharing similar growth cycles, these species exhibit significant differences in growth performance including growth rate and body weight. As the tissue that accounts for the largest proportion of the body weight of meat animals, the degree of skeletal muscle myogenesis directly determines the efficiency of meat production, which suggests that the essential reason for the differences in production performance of different goose breeds is closely related to the differences in the regulatory mechanisms of muscle development.

Skeletal muscle development is a complex process involving cell proliferation, migration, and differentiation. In geese, myofiber numbers are primarily determined during the embryonic stage through four key phases: specialization of paraxial mesoderm mesenchymal stem cells into mononucleated myoblasts, fusion of myoblasts into multinucleated myotubes, myofibril formation via myoblast differentiation; and myofiber maturation (Abmayr and Pavlath, 2012). Muscle regulatory factors (MRFs), including MYOD, MYF5, MYOG, and MRF4 (Myf6), orchestrate muscle-specific gene expression at distinct developmental stages (Wang et al., 1996; Zammit, 2017) MRFs function in two phases: MYOD and MYF5 determine muscle precursor cell fate (Rudnicki et al., 1993), while MYOG and MRF4 regulate myocyte fusion and differentiation (Nabeshima et al., 1993). Myosin heavy chain (MYHC) is one of the components of a protein, encoded by the MYHC gene family, further dictate myofiber diversity(Schiaffino, 2018).

DNA methylation is a highly conserved epigenetic mechanism, which dynamically regulates gene expression through 5-methylcytosine (5mC) deposition by DNA methyltransferases (DNMTs). DNMT1 maintains methylation during DNA replication, whereas DNMT3A and DNMT3B establish new methylation patterns (Gim et al., 2023). DNA demethylation occurs via passive loss of 5mC during replication or active oxidation by TET enzymes to 5hmC/5fC/5caC, ultimately reverting to cytosine (Ito et al., 2010). The DNMTs inhibitor 5-azacytidine (5AZA) promotes myoblast demethylation and differentiation (Ning et al., 2013), while the TETs inhibitor BC339 suppresses MSTN expression without affecting DNMTs activity(Zhao et al., 2022). These tools suggest DNA methylation regulates muscle development by modulating myoblast proliferation and differentiation.

Although promoter DNA methylation typically represses gene expression, methylation in gene bodies may enhance transcription (Yang et al., 2014). Our prior longissimus dorsi muscle study in pigs with different body size during embryonic stages revealed a negative correlation between transcription start site (TSS) DNA methylation and gene expression, contrasting with a positive correlation in gene body regions (Zhang et al., 2019). In our privious breast muscle study of Shitou and Wuzong goose during embryonic stages, it was found that earlier demethylation of the MYOD promoter promotes earlier differentiation of myoblasts in Wuzong goose (Zhang et al., 2023). We therefore hypothesized that the same mechanism may exist in goose embryonic myoblasts, whereby differences in DNA methylation lead to differences in the proliferation and differentiation of goose embryonic myoblasts. However, the specific differences in skeletal muscle development between Shitou and Wuzong goose, and the underlying molecular mechanisms are still unclear.

In this study, we analyzed the expression profiles of DNA methylation enzymes myogenic factors, and further combind DNMT1/TET2 over-expression and interference with 5AZA and BC339 treatments to investigate the regulatory role of DNA methylation on the proliferation and differentiation of goose embryonic myoblasts.

Materials and methods

Animal ethics

This experiment was performed in accordance with the regulations and guidelines of the Animal Care Committee of the Zhongkai University of Agriculture and Engineering, and all efforts were made to minimize animal suffering. All experimental protocols were approved by the Animal Experiment Committee of Zhongkai University of Agriculture and Engineering (NO. 2021112709).

Sample collection

Shitou goose and Wuzong goose embryos in this study were purchased from Guangdong Qingyuan Jinyufeng Goose Co., Ltd. Goose embryos with similar embryonic development status at each stage. Embryos leg muscle samples were collected on Embryonic day 10, 16, 22 and 26 (E10, E16, E22, E26) after fertilization for each breed. We set 3 biological replicates for each breed at each time point. However, since it is difficult to identify the sex of embryos, we chose embryos with similar egg weights to ensure within-group consistency as much as possible. Samples were collected and snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen.

Goose myoblast isolation

Magang Goose embryonic myoblasts (E16) were provided by Qingyuan Jinyufeng Geese Industry Co., Ltd. The goose embryonic myoblasts were surface-disinfected with 75% ethanol gauze and exposed to UV irradiation for 15 min. The embryonic myoblasts were then transferred to a biosafety cabinet, where the air cell was opened by gently tapping the blunt end, and the intact embryo was isolated into phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, GIBCO, Shanghai, China). Leg muscle tissues were dissected using ophthalmic scissors, rinsed repeatedly with PBS to remove connective tissues and blood vessels, and minced into fragments in a microcentrifuge tube. The tissue fragments were homogenized to a slurry consistency using a mortar and pestle and transferred to a 50 mL centrifuge tube.The homogenate was digested with 5–10 volumes of 0.25% trypsin (GIBCO, Shanghai, China), gently pipetted to form a suspension, and incubated at 37°C with agitation for 5–10 min. Digestion was terminated by adding an equal volume of DMEM/F12 medium (GIBCO, Shanghai, China) containing of 20% fetal bovine serum (FBS, GIBCO, Auckland, New Zealand) and filtered the material through 70 μM cell sieves. After centrifugation at 1000 × g for 5 min at 4°C, the supernatant was discarded, and the cell pellet was resuspended in DMEM/F12 medium containing 20% FBS and 1% double antibiotics (GIBCO, Shanghai, China). The cell suspension was gently mixed using a Pasteur pipette, and the density was adjusted to 5 × 10⁵ cells/mL. The cell suspension was seeded into 10 cm culture dishes and incubated at 37°C under 5% CO₂ for 1.5–2 h. Predominantly myoblasts, were collected from the supernatant and transferred to fresh culture dishes for further expansion.

Experimental design

The goose embryonic myoblasts were cultured at 39°C, 5% CO2 humidified atmosphere in proliferation medium (DMEM/F-12 medium containing 20% FBS). When the cell density reached 80-90%, the differentiation medium (DMEM/F-12 medium containing 2% horse serum (HS, GIBCO, LA, CA, USA)) was replaced to induce differentiation. There were four groups, for TET2 overexpression, TET2 interference, DNMT1 overexpression and DNMT1 interference, four treatments in each group, which were control group, experimental group, co-BC339 (sigma, SML2611-5mg, USA) group and co-5AZA (sigma, A2385-100mg, USA) group. EdU

The proliferation cell analysis was performed according to the instructions of the CellLightTM EdU Apollo 448 In Vitro kit (Ribobio, Guangzhou, China). After treatment with different treatment for 48 h, this was replaced by 50 µm EdU culture medium, cells were kept at 39°C and 5% CO2, humidified atmosphere for 2 h, then fixed and stained with Apollo, and observed by fluorescence microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan, IX73P1F). Photoshop 6.0 was used for image processing. The number of EdU immunofluorescence cells and the total number of cells in the same field of vision were calculated, and the proportion of EdU-stained cells in the total number of cells was calculated.

Immunofluorescence

At the differentiation phase, after cells were processed for 48 h, the myotube fusion rate and myotube diameter was detected by MYHC immunofluorescence. After discarding the cell-culture medium, cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (Biosharp, Hefei, China) for 10 min and paraformaldehyde was discarded. Cells were washed with PBS, an appropriate amount of Trition X-100 (Beijing Dingguo Changsheng Biotechnology Co.,Ltd., Beijing, China) was added for 15 min, the PBS permeating solution was discarded, and 4% bovine albumin (Shanghaiyuanye Bio-Technology Co., Ltd., Songjiang, China) solution added. Cells were sealed at room temperature for 1 h, the sealing solution was then discarded, and an appropriate amount of primary antibody diluent (Sigma, M4276, ISR, 1:400) added. They were then cultured overnight at 4°C. Next the first antibody was discarded and they were washed with PBS. The second antibody diluent (Abcam, ab150113,USA, 1:1000) was added and they were incubated at room temperature for 1 h. The second antibody was then discarded and they were washed with PBS. Then 1 µL/mL DAPI(Solar bio, Beijing, China) working solution was added and they were incubated at room temperature for 5 min and washed with PBS. Fluorescence quenching: the supernatant was discarded, the anti-fluorescence quenching blocking agent was added, the cell morphology and growth were observed by inverted fluorescence microscope, and the photos were taken. ImageJ 1.8.0 was used to calculate the proportion of green fluorescence in each image.

Overexpression and the RNA interference assays

The over-expression plasmids PCDNA3.1-DNMT1, PCDNA3.1-TET2 and PCDNA3.1 empty vector were purchased from Tsingke Biotechnology Co.,Ltd. Goose embryonic myoblasts were transfected using Lipofectamine 3000 (thermofisher, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Seed the cells into 24-well plates for 24 h until the cell density reaches 70-80% confluency. Dilute Lipofectamine 3000 reagent with Opti-MEM medium (Gibco, Shanghai, China) and mix thoroughly. Separately, dilute the plasmid DNA with Opti-MEM medium to prepare the DNA premix solution, then add P3000 reagent (at a ratio of 2 µL per µg of DNA) and mix well. Combine the diluted DNA and Lipofectamine 3000 solutions at a 1:1 volume ratio in each tube, gently mix, and incubate the mixture at room temperature for 15 min to allow DNA-lipid complex formation. Add the resulting DNA-lipid complexes drop-wise to the myoblasts. After 6 h of transfection, replace the transfection medium with normal proliferation medium to ensure cell viability and continued culture.

DNMT1 siRNAs, TET2 siRNAs and a non-targeting siRNA were purchased from Tsingke Biotechnology Co.,Ltd. Goose embryonic myoblasts were transfected with siRNAs using Lipofectamine 3000 according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Cells were cultured in 24-well plates and transfected with 30 μmol siRNA in complex with 10 µL of Lipofectamine 3000 dissolved in Opti-MEM solution (Gibco, Shanghai, China). After transfection for 6 h, the medium was replaced with fresh growth medium. Transfected cells were harvested 48 h later for analysis of mRNA or protein. Each transfection experiment was repeated three times.

RNA extraction and real-time quantitative PCR

The total RNA was extracted according to the instructions of Trizol (Thermo Fisher, Waltham, MA, USA) reagent. After extraction, the total RNA was dissolved in DEPC water, and the purity and concentration of RNA were measured with full wavelength spectrophotometer. The reverse transcription experiment was carried out in accordance with the reverse transcription kit ReverTra Ace qPCR RT Master Mix with gDNA Remover (TOYOBO, Osaka, Japan) instructions. By comparing with GenBank in the NCBI database, real-time PCR primers were designed by software Primer Premier (Version 5.0) (Table 3). In this study, GAPDH was used as internal reference gene to detect gene expression by RT-qPCR. The cDNA was used according to the instructions of SYBR Select Master Mix kit (Thermo, USA), 20 µL reaction system was used and prepared on ice. The relative mRNA expression was calculated by the 2−∆∆CT method.

Western blotting

Proteins were detected using the ECL kit (Beyotime, Beijing). Proteins were separated using SDS PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes. Blocking with phosphate-buffered saline with PBST containing 5% fat-free milk, PVDF membranes were co-incubated with the antibodies: anti-MYOD (1:1000, abcam, ab16148, Cambridge, UK); anti-MYHC (1:10000, sigma M4276, Burlington, MA, USA); and anti-GAPDH (1:10000, Abcam, ab181602, UK) at 4°C overnight. 1 h incubation with anti-mouse (1:10000,abcam,ab97023,Cambridge, UK) or anti- rabbit HRP-conjugated second antibody (1:10000, Wanlei, WLA32023a,Liaoning,China,) and visualized using a Tanon-200 Multi (Tanon, Shanghai, China) device. Densitometry analysis was performed using the Image J software (Version 1.8.0). Each experiment was biologically replicated three times.

Statistical analysis

Three biological replicates were set in each group, and the test data were expressed as “mean ± SEM”. GraphPad Prism software (Version 5.0) was used for statistical analysis of the test data, and difference significance analysis was performed using Tukey's test. P > 0.05 means that the difference is not significant, P < 0.05 means that the difference is significant.

Results

Expression profiles of DNA methylation-related enzymes and myogenic factors in Shitou and Wuzong geese

To investigate the regulatory roles of DNA methylation-related enzymes and myogenic factors in muscle development, we compared the expression profiles of DNA methyltransferases (DNMT1, DNMT3A, DNMT3B), DNA hydroxylases (TET1, TET2, TET3), and key myogenic factors (MYOD, MYOG, MYF5, MYHC) in leg muscles of Shitou geese and Wuzong geese using RT-qPCR. In Wuzong geese, the mRNA level of TET1 in Wuzong geese was significantly higher at E10, TET2 at E10, E16 and E22, and TET3 at E10 and E16 compared to Shitou geese (Fig. 1A). In contrast, the mRNA expression level of DNMT1 in Shitou goose was significantly higher than that in Wuzong goose at E10, E16, and E22, while the expression level of DNMT3B was significantly lower in Shitou goose compared to Wuzong goose at E22 and E26 (Fig. 1A). For myogenic regulatory factors, the mRNA expression level of MYF5 in Shitou goose was significantly higher than that in Wuzong goose at E10. However, the expression level of MYOD was significantly lower in Shitou goose compared to Wuzong goose at E16 and E22 (Fig. 1B). The expression of MYOG in Shitou goose was significantly higher than that in Wuzong goose at E26, while MYHC exhibited significantly higher expression levels in Shitou goose at E22 and E26 compared to Wuzong goose (Fig. 1C).

To further explore these dynamics in goose embryonic myoblasts, we analyzed gene expression during proliferation and differentiation stages of goose embryonic myoblasts. The results showed that the mRNA expression level of TET2 was significantly higher at P48 compared to other time points. The mRNA expression level of DNA methyltransferase DNMT1 exhibited significant differences at 24 h and 48 h during the proliferation period (P24,P48), with a notable increase at 72 h of the differentiation period (D72) compared to other stages. The mRNA expression level of DNA methyltransferase DNMT3A was significantly elevated at P48, D24, and D48 compared to P24 and D72. Similarly, the mRNA expression level of DNA methyltransferase DNMT3B was significantly higher at D72 and P48 than at other stages (Fig. 2A). For myogenic factors, the mRNA expression level of MYOD was significantly higher at P48 than at P24 (Fig. 2B). The expression level of MYHC increased progressively with differentiation duration, showing significantly higher levels at D48 and D72 compared to D24 (Fig. 2C).

These findings suggest that DNA hydroxylases (TETs) may play a more significant regulatory role during the early embryonic development stages in Wuzong goose, whereas DNA methyltransferases (DNMTs) exert a greater regulatory effect in the later stages of embryonic development. During myoblast proliferation and differentiation, DNMTs, TETs, and myogenic factors exhibit distinct expression patterns, with DNMT1 and TET2 showing the most significant differential expression (P<0.05) between proliferation and differentiation stages. These two enzymes were consequently prioritized for functional validation experiments due to their stage-dependent regulatory prominence.

Effects of DNMT1 on proliferation and differentiation of goose embryonic myoblasts

To investigate the impact of DNMT1 expression levels on the proliferation and differentiation of goose embryonic myoblasts, DNMT1 over-expression and interference were performed during both the proliferation and differentiation phases. Cellular proliferation was quantified by measuring the EdU-positive rate, while differentiation was assessed via MYHC immunofluorescence staining to determine myotube fusion rate.

As shown by the RT-qPCR assay, the mRNA expression level expression of DNMT1 was significantly up-regulated in the DNMT1 gene over-expression vector group (PC3.1-DNMT1) compared with that in the empty group (PC3.1-NC), indicating that the DNMT1 gene over-expression vector was successfully transfected (Fig. 3C). In the proliferation phase, EdU staining revealed that the proportion of EdU-positive cells in the over-expression group (PC3.1-DNMT1) was significantly lower than that in the empty vector control group (NC group). To exclude the effect of DNMT1 over-expression-mediated failure of proliferation and differentiation in goose myoblasts, we also performed 5AZA and BC339 co-treatment. Co-treatment with BC339 (PC3.1-DNMT1-BC339) further exacerbated proliferation inhibition, while 5AZA co-treatment (PC3.1-DNMT1-5AZA) reversed this effect (Fig. 3A,D). Western blot results demonstrated that DNMT1 over-expression significantly suppressed MYOD protein levels, with BC339 intensifying this suppression and 5AZA restoring MYOD expression (Fig. 7A,B). In the differentiation phase, the myotube fusion rate and MYHC protein expression in the DNMT1 over-expression group (PC3.1-DNMT1) were significantly reduced compared to the NC group. BC339 co-treatment (PC3.1-DNMT1-BC339) further inhibited differentiation, while 5AZA co-treatment (PC3.1-DNMT1-5AZA) restored differentiation capacity (Fig. 3B,E) . Western blot confirmed that DNMT1 over-expression significantly down-regulated MYHC protein levels, with BC339 enhancing this inhibition and 5AZA rescuing MYHC expression (Fig. 7A, F).

For DNMT1 interference Assays, si-DNMT1-2 was identified as the optimal interfering fragment and used for subsequent studies which abbreviated as si-DNMT1 (Fig. 4C). EdU staining showed that the EdU-positive rate in the interference group (si-DNMT1) was significantly higher than that in the control group (si-NC). However, BC339 co-treatment (si-DNMT1-BC339) attenuated the pro-proliferative effect, whereas 5AZA co-treatment (si-DNMT1-5AZA) further increased the EdU-positive rate (Fig. 4A,D). Western blotting results showed that DNMT1 interference significantly increased the level of MYOD protein expression, BC339 inhibited this promotion, while 5AZA increased MYOD protein expression more significantly (Fig. 7A,C). During differentiation, the myotube fusion rate in the interference group (si-DNMT1) were significantly higher than those in the control group (si-NC). BC339 co-treatment (si-DNMT1-BC339) weakened the pro-differentiation effect, while 5AZA co-treatment (si-DNMT1-5AZA) further elevated the myotube fusion rate (Fig. 4B, E). Western blot revealed that DNMT1 interference significantly increased MYHC protein expression, and 5AZA enhanced this effect. In contrast, MYHC levels were down-regulated in the BC339 co-treatment group, which was not significantly different from the NC group (Fig. 7A,G).

In summary, DNMT1 over-expression inhibits cell proliferation and differentiation by down-regulating myogenic proteins such as MYOD and MYHC. BC339 potentiates this effect, whereas 5AZA rescues this process by inhibiting DNMT1 expression. In contrast, DNMT1 interference promotes proliferation and differentiation of goose embryonic myoblasts by up-regulating the expression of myogenic factors; BC339 antagonizes this effect, while 5AZA synergistically enhances the interference-induced pro-proliferative and pro-differentiation effects.

Effects of TET2 on proliferation and differentiation of goose embryonic myoblasts

To further validate the fuction of TET2 in myoblast proliferation and differentiation, TET2 overexpression and interference were performed in myoblasts during both proliferative and differentiation phases. Proliferative activity was assessed via EdU-positive cell assays, while differentiation efficiency was evaluated through MYHC immunofluorescence staining.

To investigate the role of TET2 in the proliferation and differentiation of goose embryonic myoblasts, an over-expression model was established by transfecting cells with the PC3.1-TET2 plasmid. As shown by the RT-qPCR assay, the mRNA expression level expression of TET2 was significantly up-regulated in PC3.1-TET2 compared with PC3.1-NC, and PC3.1-TET2 successfully promoted the gene expression of TET2, which allowed for subsequent experiments (Fig. 5C). In the proliferation phase, EdU staining revealed that the EdU-positive rate in the TET2 over-expression group (PC3.1-TET2) was significantly higher than that in the empty vector control group (PC3.1-NC), indicating that TET2 over-expression promoted myoblast proliferation. Co-treatment with 5AZA (PC3.1-TET2-5AZA) further enhanced the pro-proliferative effect, while BC339 co-treatment (PC3.1-TET2-BC339) markedly suppressed this promotion (Fig. 5A, D). Western blot analysis showed that over-expression of TET2 significantly up-regulated the protein expression of the myogenic factor MYOD, and this promotional ability was inhibited by BC339 co-treatment and further promoted by 5AZA (Fig. 7A, D). During the differentiation phase, the myotube fusion rate and MYHC protein expression in the TET2 over-expression group were significantly higher than those in the control group (PC3.1-NC). BC339 co-treatment (PC3.1-TET2-BC339) significantly attenuated the pro-differentiation effect, whereas 5AZA co-treatment (PC3.1-TET2-5AZA) further increased the myotube fusion rate to its peak level (Fig. 5 B, E). Western blot confirmed that TET2 overexpression significantly elevated MYHC protein expression, with the highest expression observed in the 5AZA co-treatment group. BC339 co-treatment, however, inhibited the TET2 over-expression-induced enhancement of MYHC protein expression (Fig. 7A, H).

BC339 antagonizes this effect, while 5AZA synergistically amplifies the pro-proliferative and pro-differentiation roles of TET2. BC339 enhances the inhibitory effect, while 5AZA partially counteracts the suppression caused by TET2 interference.

Discussion

An association between the expression levels of embryonic DNA methylation-related enzymes and myogenic factors

DNA methylation and the expression of myogenic factors both play regulatory roles in the development of skeletal muscle during the embryonic period of geese (Zhong et al., 2017). DNA methylation is primarily regulated by DNA methyltransferases (DNMTs) and DNA hydroxylases (TETs), and the level of DNA methylation is dependent on the dynamic balance of the activities of these two enzymes (Jones, 2012). Thus, the expression levels of DNMTs and TETs can serve as proxies for DNA methylation status. The transformation of mouse embryonic fibroblasts into muscle cells following treatment with the DNA methyltransferase (DNMT) inhibitor 5AZA has shown a preliminary correlation between DNA methylation and musculogenesis (Taylor and Jones, 1979). MYF5, MYOD, MYOG, and MYHC, as myogenic factors, have been shown to affect skeletal myogenesis in different species (Bentzinger et al., 2012; Zammit, 2017; Lee et al., 2022). MYOD plays an important role in proliferation and early differentiation of myogenic cells through chromatin remodeling and transcriptional regulation mechanisms, and its expression level can directly affect muscle cell proliferation and differentiation (Pownall et al., 2002). This association is further emphasized by the fact that demethylation of myogenic factors is associated with transcriptional activation of genes and terminal muscle differentiation (Fuso et al., 2010; Hupkes et al., 2011). In addition, increased DNA methylation and decreased MYOG expression both significantly reduce the differentiation capacity of C2C12 cells (Steffens et al., 2011).

In our previous study, DNA methyltransferases DNMT1, DNMT3A, and DNMT3B were found to be significantly different between different stages of embryonic leg muscle development of Shitou and Wuzong geese (Zhang et al., 2023). In the present study, we further clarified the expression levels of DNA-related enzymes in Shitou and Wuzong geese, and DNA hydroxylase mRNA expression levels were significantly higher in Wuzong geese during early embryonic development stages compared to Shitou geese, whereas DNA methyltransferase mRNA expression levels were significantly higher in Shitou geese during early embryonic development stages compared to Wuzong geese, suggesting that Wuzong geese are more capable of DNA demethylation during early development, whereas Shitou geese are more capable of DNA methylation. Therefore, it is speculated that DNA methylation ability of early embryonic development in leg muscle was stronger in Wuzong geese than in Shitou geese. In the results of transcriptome sequencing of leg muscle samples from E15, E23 and P1 of Shitou geese and Wuzong geese, the expression of MYOD gene in E15 Wuzong geese was significantly higher than that in Shitou geese (Zhang et al., 2023). In the present study, the expression levels of MYF5 and MYOD genes in Shitou geese were significantly lower than those in Wuzong geese at different time periods, and the expression levels of MYOG and MYHC were significantly higher than those in Wuzong geese at different time periods, which is in agreement with the results of our previous study . In this study, the mRNA expression levels of TET1 and TET2 showed a trend of gradual decrease with the proliferation and differentiation of adult myoblasts, and the mRNA expression levels of DNA hydroxylase were significantly higher in the proliferation phase of adult myoblasts than in the differentiation phase, suggesting that DNA hydroxylase may be a catalyst to activate the differentiation of adult myoblasts. In contrast, DNMT1, DNMT3A, and DNMT3B all showed a trend of higher mRNA expression in the differentiation phase, suggesting that DNA methyltransferases may inhibit the proliferation of adult myoblasts. Therefore, it can be speculated that DNA demethylation may play a promotional role in the proliferation and differentiation of adult myoblasts, and DNA methylation inhibits the proliferation of adult myoblasts leading to the differentiation phase.

TET2 promotes the process of skeletal muscle proliferation and differentiation

As the major proteins regulating DNA demethylation, the TET family usually promotes cellular function by facilitating DNA demethylation. It has been found that TET2 activates the transcription of MYOG, a key differentiation regulator, by actively demethylating its enhancer region, which further affects differentiation. Re-expression of over-expressed MYOG in TET2 knockout chicken embryonic myoblasts rescued differentiation and fusion defects (Wang et al., 2021), and TET2 protein levels and 5hmC were significantly reduced in skeletal muscle of obese mice, leading to impaired muscle function (Chen et al., 2023). In addition to this, knockdown of TETs leads to severe defects in bone development. TET2 also plays a role in regulating proliferation and differentiation of myoblasts by acting in association with other genes or pathways. PAX7 binds to the promoters/enhancers of MYOD, MYF5, and MYOG promoter/enhancer to activate their transcription in cultured adult myoblasts, whereas a decrease in TET2 expression levels leads to a decrease or even disappearance of PAX7 expression, which further affects the expression levels of MYOD, MYF5 and MYOG (Hu et al., 2008; Soleimani et al., 2012; Chao et al., 2016; Lilja et al., 2017). It has also been proposed that TET2 induces demethylation of certain specific gene promoters leading to the activation and expression of genes related to myoblast differentiation, thus promoting myoblast differentiation into myotubes (Zhong et al., 2017).

DNMT1 inhibits the process of skeletal muscle proliferation and differentiation

DNA methylation and DNA demethylation are generally regulated dynamically by DNMTs and TETs. The hypermethylation found by genome-wide promoter CpG DNA methylation analysis in skeletal muscle is mainly due to significantly increased levels of DNMT1 and DNMT3A (Martin et al., 2022). DNMT and TET are complementary, and cells undergo compensatory up-regulation of TET2 when treated with DNMT inhibitors (Laranjeira et al., 2024). A previous study found that down-regulation of DNMT1 due to the DNA methyltransferase inhibitor 5AZA may lead to passive DNA demethylation, which further leads to the activation and expression of differentiation-related genes that drive myotube formation. In previous studies, 5AZA-treated cells effectively silenced DNMT1 to increase and 5hmC expression, significantly increasing the protein level of MYHC in the cells (Senesi et al., 2014; Rocha et al., 2019). Whereas BC339 promotes DNA methylation (Lu et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2022) . The over-expression of DNMT3A attenuated muscle atrophy and promoted myotube formation in C2C12 myoblasts in diabetic mice. Over-expression of DNMT3A restored the expression of the myogenic regulators atrogin-1, MuRF1, Pax7, MYOD1, and myoblastogensis both in vivo and in vitro (Kesharwani et al., 2022). DNMT1 expression is significantly reduced in fast-growing muscles, demonstrating that DNMT may be associated with the inhibition of muscle development (Kang et al., 2020) . However, studies on DNMT enzymes have mainly focused on diseases such as cancer, diabetes or hypertension, and there are still fewer studies on the effects of DNMT enzymes on the proliferation and differentiation of muscle cells (Lamothe et al., 2020; Li et al., 2021; Lee et al., 2024) . CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing technology is based on an artificially designed guidance system that specifically recognizes target genomic sequences and directionally activates the DNA double-strand cleavage function of Cas9 nuclease (Qian and Liu, 2024). It has been served as an epigenetic tool by binding to genes like DNMT3A and TET2, thereby enhancing CpG methylation at the target locus. (Jogam et al., 2022). In the future, it can be used as a powerful tool for molecular marker-assisted breeding in conjunction with DNA methylation.

In this study, the proliferation and differentiation ability of goose embryonic myoblasts was significantly increased after DNMT1 interference, and the gene and protein expression levels of myogenic factors were significantly increased. Conversely, over-expression of DNMT1 inhibited the proliferation and differentiation ability of goose embryonic myoblasts and suppressed the gene and protein expression of myogenic factors, which is consistent with the results of previous studies (Liu et al., 2016; Iio et al., 2021), indicating that DNMT1-mediated DNA methylation inhibits the proliferation and differentiation of myoblasts.

In summary, we hypothesized that the mechanism underlying the large differences in meat production among different goose breeds may be that DNA hydroxylase TET2 and myogenic factor MYOD are expressed earlier in the early embryonic period in Wuzong geese, leading to earlier mRNA expression levels and protein expression of myogenic factors and earlier differentiation of myogenic cells than in Shitou geese.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that Wuzong geese embryonic myoblasts exhibit significantly higher expression levels of TET1, TET2, TET3, and MYOD compared to Shitou geese, while Shitou geese show elevated expression of DNMT1, DNMT3B, MYF5, MYOG, and MYHC. Functional analyses revealed that 5AZA, a methylation inhibitor, suppresses DNMT1 expression, reduces DNA methylation levels, and enhances myogenic protein expression alongside promoting myoblast proliferation and differentiation capacity. Conversely, BC339, a TET2 antagonist, inhibits TET2 activity to increase DNA methylation, thereby suppressing myogenic protein expression and impairing myoblast functionality. Critically, DNMT1 expression was shown to inhibit proliferation and differentiation in goose embryonic muscle cells, whereas TET2 expression actively promotes these processes. This research provides a molecular explanation for the distinct meat production traits observed between Wuzong and Shitou geese, highlighting the central role of dynamic DNA methylation remodeling in avian muscle development (Fig. 8).

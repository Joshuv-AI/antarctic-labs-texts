Wavelength-dependent photofragmentation of pyrazine
Authors: Siddhartha S. Payra, Pratikkumar Thakkar, Yash Lenka, G. Aravind
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Ultraviolet (UV) photon-induced dissociation of larger molecules is a rich source of smaller hydrocarbons and nitrogen-bearing molecules in the interstellar medium. This top-down mechanism is of utmost importance in photodissociation regions (PDRs), where the intensities reach orders of magnitude higher than the ambient interstellar radiation field. In this work, we employed two distinct UV radiation fields, i.e., 266 nm and 355 nm, to investigate the photo-fragmentation of pyrazine, a biologically and astrochemically relevant nitrogen heterocycle in the gas phase. Measurements performed across varying laser intensities reveal a significant wavelength-dependent difference in cationic fragmentation patterns. Nitrogen-bearing fragment cations and hydrocarbons are identified as key products across both wavelength regimes. However, the photofragmentation under 355 nm irradiation produces several previously unreported fragmentation channels such as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hbox {CH}_{3}\hbox {N}_{2}^{+}$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hbox {C}_{2}\hbox {N}^{+}$$\end{document},\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hbox {CH}_{3}\hbox {N}^{+}$$\end{document}. Interestingly, the direct fragmentation of pyrazine cannot account for the observed \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hbox {CH}_{3}\hbox {N}_{2}^{+}$$\end{document} ion through simple bond cleavage. We proposed that pyrazine undergoes photoisomerization upon 355 nm laser irradiation before dissociation. All the fragmentation channels are discussed in detail, providing essential experimental constraints for astrochemical models of UV-driven molecular destruction in PDRs and planetary atmospheres.

Introduction

Nitrogen-bearing aromatic heterocycles are fundamental building blocks in the molecular architecture essential for life. They serve as core structural elements in DNA and RNA nucleotides. These compounds also play a key role in energy storage molecules such as ATP and GTP1. Additionally, aromatic heterocycles are found in numerous pharmaceutical compounds2 and herbicides3. Among them, the double nitrogen substitution of benzene occupies a particularly significant position in both terrestrial biochemistry and extraterrestrial environments. These compounds are predicted to play crucial roles in the formation of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) and their nitrogenated analogs, PANHs, within Titan’s atmosphere and interstellar medium (ISM)4–6. Furthermore, nitrogen heterocycles serve as potential precursors for the synthesis of smaller nitrogen-bearing molecules that have been detected throughout interstellar space7–9. The chemical fate of these molecules in space is largely determined by their interaction with ultraviolet (UV) radiation, which constitutes a primary driver of prebiotic chemistry and governs the chemical evolution of the ISM, particularly within UV-rich photodissociation regions (PDRs) where stellar radiation fields can reach intensities several orders of magnitude above the standard interstellar value10.

Pyrazine, a representative diazine, has attracted significant attention in astrobiology, molecular physics, and astrochemistry as a model aromatic heterocycle. Extensive experimental and theoretical investigations have therefore been undertaken to elucidate its dissociation pathways and excited-state dynamics11–13. The electronic structure and excited state dynamics of neutral pyrazine have been comprehensively examined using various spectroscopic and computational techniques14,15. The dissociation dynamics of the pyrazine cationic state have been investigated under vacuum ultraviolet (VUV)16 radiation and electron impact ionization conditions17. However, the photofragmentation pathways and their dependence on photon wavelength and intensity, under below-threshold UV photons, remain underexplored. Multiphoton dissociation techniques offer valuable experimental approaches for investigating the fundamental photodynamics of aromatic heterocycles, providing unique insights into their photochemical behavior under conditions relevant to both terrestrial and extraterrestrial environments.

Here, we report the fragmentation mass spectra of pyrazine following multiphoton ionization (MPI) or dissociation (MPD) using deep-ultraviolet (DUV) and near-ultraviolet (NUV) laser pulses with a wavelength (photon energy) of 266 nm (4.66 eV) and 355 nm (3.49 eV), respectively. The two photon energies produced distinctly different fragmentation patterns. At both wavelengths, many fragment ions arise from simple bond cleavages or neutral loss channels of the parent ion. Parent ionization and H-loss channel

As shown in Fig. 1, a small yield of parent cation was observed across both the DUV and NUV lasers. The dashed line represents the power law fit (Y \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\propto$$\end{document} I\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\vphantom{0}^n$$\end{document}), and the obtained value of the exponent is indicated in parentheses.

The adiabatic ionization potential of pyrazine is 9.29 eV11,18; the MPI process involved at least two 266 nm (4.66 eV) photons. However, to activate the H-loss channel, we require 13.68 eV16,19, corresponding to absorption of three 266 nm photons. The UV absorption spectrum for pyrazine15,20 shows a resonance absorption at 266 nm, hence a single photon can excite pyrazine to the S\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\vphantom{0}_2$$\end{document} (1\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\vphantom{0}^1B_{2u}$$\end{document}) state. Consequently, two or three more photon absorptions can produce parent and deprotonated parent via (1+2) or (1+3) resonance-enhanced multiphoton-ionization (REMPI), respectively. The observed slope of 3.57 in the log–log plot of laser intensity versus ion yield matches this photon-order dependence (Fig. 3a).

Here we discuss a hitherto unreported photodissociation channel of pyrazine that provides new insights into the complex photochemistry of nitrogen-containing aromatic heterocycles. The fragment ion observed at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$m/z=43$$\end{document} represents a previously uncharacterized dissociation pathway that was absent in both electron impact ionization studies17 and VUV photodissociation investigations of pyrazine16. we have assigned this fragment to the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hbox {CH}_{3}\hbox {N}_{2}^{+}$$\end{document} cation. The formation of this particular ionic species necessitates a significant molecular rearrangement process, as the direct fragmentation of pyrazine cannot account for the observed \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$m/z=43$$\end{document} signal through simple bond cleavage mechanisms.

We propose that pyrazine undergoes photoisomerization upon 355 nm laser irradiation, transforming from its initial 1,4-diazine structure to the thermodynamically more stable pyrimidine (1,3-diazine) isomer12. This photoinduced isomerization represents a critical step that precedes the formation of the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hbox {CH}_{3}\hbox {N}_{2}^{+}$$\end{document} fragment. The driving force for this rearrangement lies in the enhanced stability of the pyrimidine structure, which exhibits reduced ring strain and a more favorable electronic configuration compared to pyrazine12,29. Following the photoisomerization step, the newly formed pyrimidine undergoes multiphoton ionization and subsequent dissociation. The structural reorganization inherent in the pyrazine-to-pyrimidine isomerization creates accessible fragmentation pathways that facilitate the formation of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hbox {CH}_{3}\hbox {N}_{2}^{+}$$\end{document} ions through selective bond cleavage and hydrogen rearrangement processes. This mechanism explains the absence of this fragment in direct pyrazine photodissociation studies using a VUV laser. Since 266 nm photons provide sufficient energy for pyrazine to dissociate directly, the isomerization pathway leading to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hbox {CH}_{3}\hbox {N}_{2}^{+}$$\end{document} formation may be bypassed or rendered less competitive under these higher-energy conditions, accounting for the non-observation of this fragment in that regime.

Astrophysical relevance

UV photons play a critical role in controlling abundances of atomic and molecular species in diffuse interstellar clouds and PDRs35. PDRs are UV-rich, where the aromatic infrared bands (AIBs) are particularly bright and attributed to the photochemistry and photo-isomerization produced by the penetrating UV-photons36,37 and the intensities reach 10\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\vphantom{0}^3$$\end{document} - 10\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\vphantom{0}^5$$\end{document} times the ambient interstellar radiation field10. The detection of smaller species on the surface of these PDRs unveils the importance of top-down chemistry38. Consequently, laboratory UV-photodissociation studies have become essential for constraining astrochemical models of these radiation-dominated regions.

Small hydrocarbons serve as key intermediates in gas-phase reaction networks that produce the diverse molecular species observed throughout the ISM39–41. Finally, we note that, although single photon excitation predominates in interstellar environments, laboratory multiphoton studies provide complementary insights into the excited-state dynamics and fragmentation pathways of astromolecules. These experiments access the same electronic states and dissociation channels as accessed via single VUV photons in space. Thus, multiphoton results contribute mechanistic understanding relevant to both laboratory and astrophysical contexts.

Conclusion

The multiphoton ionization and dissociation of pyrazine have been systematically investigated under two distinct UV radiation fields (266 nm and 355 nm), with measurements conducted across a range of laser intensities to elucidate fragmentation dynamics. The wavelength-dependent study reveals marked differences in fragmentation patterns between the DUV (266 nm) and NUV (355 nm) regimes. Notably, several previously unreported dissociation channels have been identified in the 355 nm irradiation experiments, with detailed analysis of their formation mechanisms providing new insights. The observation of nitrogen-bearing fragment cations alongside carbon-containing species underscores the complex role of nitrogen in the photofragmentation cascade. Given pyrazine’s significance as both a biologically relevant molecule and an astrochemically important nitrogen heterocycle relevant to interstellar photodissociation regions and planetary atmospheres, these laboratory measurements provide essential experimental constraints for chemical models of UV-driven destruction pathways in space and offer mechanistic insights into how aromatic nitrogen heterocycles fragment under varying radiation environments.Fig. 7Schematic diagram of the experimental setup for pyrazine multiphoton ionization studies.

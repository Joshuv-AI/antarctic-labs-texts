A phase II study of daily carboplatin plus irradiation followed by durvalumab therapy for older adults (≥75 years) with unresectable III non-small-cell lung cancer and performance status of 2: NEJ039A
Authors: A Mouri, A Kisohara, R Morita, R Ko, T Nakagawa, T Makiguchi, K Isobe, N Ishikawa, T Kondo, M Akiyama, A Bessho, R Honda, K Yoshimura, H Kagamu, S Kato, K Kobayashi, K Kaira, M Maemondo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundStandard care for unresectable locally advanced non-small-cell lung cancer (LA-NSCLC) involves chemoradiotherapy followed by durvalumab. The clinical significance of durvalumab after chemoradiotherapy in patients with LA-NSCLC having a performance status of 2 or aged ≥75 years, however, remains unclear. Therefore, we investigated the clinical benefit of durvalumab after daily carboplatin plus thoracic concurrent radiotherapy.Patients and methodsIn this prospective phase II study, daily low-dose carboplatin (30 mg/m2) was administered before radiotherapy for the first 20 fractions and concurrent radiotherapy (60 Gy) followed by durvalumab. The primary endpoint was 12 months progression-free survival (PFS) rate from durvalumab initiation. The secondary endpoints included rate of therapeutic completion, PFS, overall survival, objective response rate, and safety.ResultsOf 86 patients who underwent chemoradiotherapy with daily carboplatin from September 2019 to October 2021, 61 (70.9%) received durvalumab consolidation. The performance status was 0, 1, and 2 in 28 (45.9%), 26 (42.6%), and 7 (11.5%) patients, respectively. The rate of therapeutic completion for durvalumab was 26.2% (16/61). The PFS rate of 12 months after durvalumab initiation was 51.0%, indicating that the primary endpoint was achieved because the expected value of 35% calculated from previous studies was exceeded. The objective response rate after chemoradiotherapy and durvalumab was 47.0% and 57.4%, respectively. The median PFS and overall survival were 12.3 and 28.1 months, respectively. The most common adverse event in grades 3 or 4 was pneumonitis (8.2%). One patient died because of interstitial pneumonitis.ConclusionsDurvalumab consolidation after daily carboplatin with radiotherapy was effective and tolerable for LA-NSCLC vulnerable patients.Key words: older adult, performance status 2, durvalumab, locally advanced NSCLC

Introduction

Chemoradiotherapy (CRT) is the standard treatment of patients with locally advanced non-small-cell lung cancer (LA-NSCLC). A recent study (PACIFIC trial) revealed that CRT with durvalumab improved the 5-year overall survival (OS) rate to 42.9% compared with CRT alone that resulted in an improvement to 33.1%.1, 2, 3 Most patients receiving CRT with durvalumab have a performance status (PS) of 0-1 and are aged <70 years.1,4, 5, 6 The remaining patients are candidates for radiotherapy alone without chemotherapy; therefore, the estimated 5-year OS rate was identified as <10% in previous studies.7,8

In daily practice, less toxic therapeutic regimens are chosen for older adults or poor PS. Previously, a phase III trial (JCOG0301) compared daily carboplatin administration plus concurrent radiotherapy to radiotherapy alone in patients with LA-NSCLC who were aged >70 years.9 This study demonstrated that CRT using daily carboplatin exhibited better effectiveness than did radiotherapy alone, with median progression-free survival (PFS) of 8.9 months and a 5-year OS rate of ∼15%.9 The PFS and OS for the radiation alone group in this study were 6.8 and 16.9 months, respectively.9 Thus, daily carboplatin administration with concurrent radiotherapy for older adults with LA-NSCLC is recommended by the treatment guidelines in Japan.

A post hoc analysis in the PACIFIC trial revealed that CRT followed by durvalumab improved the outcome of patients aged ≥70 years (22.2%) with a manageable toxicity, similar to that in those aged <70 years (77.8%).10 This report, however, was based on a framework of patients aged ≥70 years who were eligible for platinum-based regimens.10 Further, a large-scale retrospective study based on the PACIFIC regimen reported the outcome of patients aged ≥75 years (10.4%; 145/1399) who exhibited a comparatively shorter PFS (19.2 months) than those aged <70 years (68.5%; 958/1399, 22.8 months) and 70-75 years (21.2%; 296/1399, 22.4 months).11 Although these real-world data included 19 (2.0%) patients with PS of 2 or 3, no information was available regarding adverse events (AEs) according to PS or age.11 In particular, those aged >75 years or with PS of 2 are fragile to systemic treatment, requiring more careful choice for CRT.

Based on these backgrounds, we aimed to conduct a prospective phase II study to investigate the clinical benefit of durvalumab after daily carboplatin plus concurrent radiotherapy for patients with stage III NSCLC, including those with a PS of 2 or aged ≥75 years.

Patients and methods

Patients

The details of the NEJ039A study protocol have been previously published.12 This prospective phase II trial enrolled patients with stage III NSCLC who have a PS of 2 or are older aged. The inclusion criteria for the first registration for daily carboplatin plus concurrent irradiation included chemotherapy-naive, stage III patients (Union for International Cancer Control 8th edition) with indication for CRT; and patients with PS of 2 and ≤74 years of age/PS of 0 or 1 and ≥75 years, measurable disease by the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1, and adequate organ function. The inclusion criteria for the second registration of durvalumab included completion of irradiation with >40 Gy, daily carboplatin for >10 days, non-progressive disease (PD) and PS of 0, 1, or 2 after CRT, and adequate organ function. The exclusion criteria were PD during induction CRT, any unresolved toxicity or common terminology criteria for AEs >grade 2 from the previous CRT, and any-grade pneumonitis from the previous CRT.

This study was approved by the Clinical Research Review Board of Saitama Medical University and conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients. The study protocol has been registered in the Japan Registry of Clinical Trials (jRCTs031190070).

Study design

The primary endpoint was the 12-month PFS rate from the initiation of durvalumab administration. The secondary endpoints were the feasibility, objective response, time to treatment failure (TTF), PFS, OS, and AEs. Daily, intravenous, low-dose carboplatin (30 mg/m2 in a 30-min infusion) was administered 1 h before radiotherapy for the first 20 fractions. Planned radiotherapy of 60 Gy was administered as 30 fractions from 6 to 9 weeks. Radiotherapy was administered 5 days per week in 2 Gy daily fractions. Radiotherapy devices with an X-ray generator between 6 MV and 18 MV were permitted for both three-dimensional conformal radiation therapy and intensity-modulated radiation therapy. V20, the volume of normal lung irradiated to 20 Gy or more, was agreed to be <30% of the total normal lung volume (V20 <30%). Durvalumab was administered intravenously at a dose of 10 mg/kg for 42 days after the completion of CRT every 2 weeks for up to 12 months.

Statistical analysis

The PACIFIC study demonstrated that the 12-month PFS rate was 55.6% after CRT, followed by durvalumab and 35.3% after CRT alone.1 The JCOG0301 study revealed a 12-month PFS of 30% in older adults after radiotherapy with daily carboplatin.9 The previous retrospective study assumed that the 12-month PFS of patients with LA-NSCLC with a PS of 2 was ∼20%.13 Assuming that a 12-month PFS rate of 35% in eligible patients would indicate potential usefulness, whereas a 12-month PFS rate of 20% would constitute the lower limit of interest, with α = 0.10 and β = 0.20, the estimated accrual was 53 patients. We, however, assessed 58 patients considering the PD rate after daily carboplatin plus radiation and patients who dropped out. In the JCOG0301 study, the PD, treatment-related death, and grade 3-4 AE rates were 8%, 3%, and 6%, respectively; therefore, we assumed the dropout rate to be 20%. During the first enrollment, 70 patients were included. A minimum follow-up of 12 months was needed after the last patient began durvalumab. The safety evaluation procedure for patients with a PS of 2 has been described previously.12 Statistical analyses were carried out using GraphPad Prism (v.8.0; GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA), SAS 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC) and JMP 14.0 (SAS Institute Inc.).

Results

Enrollment and baseline characteristics

The consort diagram for study patients is shown in Figure 1. For the first enrollment, 86 patients underwent induction CRT from September 2019 to October 2021, and 61 (70.9%) patients received durvalumab consolidation therapy after the second enrollment. The cut-off date for follow-up for survival and safety was December 2022. At the completion of CRT, 25/86 (29.1%) patients could not receive durvalumab. The main reasons were disease progression (n = 7; 8.1%), and radiation pneumonitis (n = 5; 5.8%).

Overall, 23 patients did not meet the inclusion criteria of the secondary registration. The reasons are as follows: progressive disease in seven patients, pulmonary pneumonitis in five patients, physician’s decision in four patients, patient’s refusal in one patient, and others in five patients. Overall, 63 patients met the inclusion criteria of the second registration; however, two patients are excluded because of death by tumor growth and ineligibility. Patient demographics based on first and second enrollment are listed in Table 1. The median age was 78 years (range 55-89 years). At the first enrollment, 13 patients were registered as <75 years with PS of 2, and 7 patients with a PS of 2 were treated with durvalumab after the second enrollment. Upon the second enrollment, the histological types of adenocarcinoma were observed in 22 (36.1%) patients. Treatment

The patients’ therapeutic profiles are presented in Table 2. A radiation dose of >40 Gy was delivered to 81 (94.2%) patients. The median dose of carboplatin administration was 20 mg (8-20 mg). A total of 85 (98.8%) patients received >10 fractions for carboplatin administration. The median number and duration of durvalumab consolidation were 12 (1-17) cycles and 182.0 (14-378) days, respectively. The rate of therapeutic completion for durvalumab was 26.2% (16/61).

Survival analysis

The average follow-up period from the first enrollment was 19.0 months [95% confidence interval (CI) 16.8-21.1 months]. After the second enrollment, 30 patients (49.2%) experienced recurrences, and 25 patients (41.0%) died because of the progressive disease and other reasons. The median PFS, TTF, and OS from the secondary enrollment were 12.3 months (95% CI 7.9-15.2 months), 5.8 months (95% CI 3.8-7.5 months), and 28.1 months (95% CI 15.6 months to not reached), respectively (Figure 2). As a primary endpoint, the PFS rate of 12 months from second enrollment was 51.0% (90% CI 39.9% to 61.1%).

Therapeutic efficacy

The antitumor activities after the first and second enrollment are listed in Supplementary Table S4, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103939. At first enrollment (CRT phase), 39 patients achieved partial response (PR), 39 patients had stable disease (SD), and 5 patients PD, respectively. Treatment with durvalumab after second enrollment produced CR, PR, SD, and PD in 5, 30, 19, and 3 patients, respectively. The objective response rate (ORR) and DCR after durvalumab administration were 57.4% (95% CI 44.1% to 70.0%) and 88.5% (95% CI 77.8% to 95.3%), respectively.

Safety

The AEs are listed in Table 3. The most common toxicity during durvalumab was pneumonitis in 49 (80.3%) patients, except for hematologic toxicity and hypoalbuminemia, which was considered a residual effect of CRT. The frequency of grade 3-4 pneumonitis was 8.2%. The elevation of any-grade alanine transaminase, and creatinine levels was observed in 12 (19.7%), and 21 (34.4%) patients, respectively. Immune-related AEs (irAEs) included diarrhea in three patients (4.9%), and hypothyroidism in five patients (8.2%). One patient died because of grade 5 interstitial pneumonitis. The other two patients died of unspecified sudden death and hemoptysis, which were judged as the progressive exacerbation of lung cancer.

The AEs of the CRT phase are listed in Supplementary Table S5, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103939. Esophagitis was the most frequent AE and was observed in 26 (30.2%) patients. Twelve (14.0%) patients experienced any-grade pneumonitis. None of the patients had grade 3 or higher pneumonia. Grade 3 or higher leukopenia, neutropenia, anemia, and thrombocytopenia occurred in 54 (62.8%), 44 (51.2%), 10 (11.6%), and 23 (26.7%) patients, respectively. None of the patients experienced grade 5 treatment-related death before durvalumab administration.

Discussion

In this prospective study, we investigated the clinical benefit of durvalumab after daily carboplatin plus concurrent radiotherapy and demonstrated that durvalumab after CRT was effective and tolerable in patients with LA-NSCLC having a PS of 2 or aged ≥75 years. The PFS rate 12 months after durvalumab was 51.0% (90% CI 39.9% to 61.1%). To the best of our knowledge, this study is the first to focus on older adults or patients with poor PS who received durvalumab after CRT. We believe that our regimen is a feasible and effective treatment of vulnerable patients, comparable to that in non-older adults.

In the PACIFIC trial, the proportion of patients aged ≥75 years was 7.9%, thus requiring the feasibility of durvalumab consolidation in older adults.1, 2, 3,10 The stratification factor for the PACIFIC trial was the age threshold of 65 years. Therefore, a post hoc analysis assessing patients aged ≥70 years could not draw a conclusion because of a small subgroup size and unbalanced baseline.10 Although much evidence for cancer patients with poor PS is unavailable, patients with NSCLC with PS of 2 or higher reportedly account for >30%.14 Patients who are older adults (≥75 years) or have poor PS generally have poor organ function or multiple comorbid symptoms, increasing the incidence of toxicities.15, 16, 17, 18

The median PFS and OS in older adults with LA-NSCLC who received daily low-dose carboplatin plus concurrent radiotherapy without durvalumab were 8.9 and 22.4 months,9 respectively, whereas the median PFS and OS from primary enrollment in our study were 11.5 and 26.0 months, respectively. The PFS rate of 12 months from durvalumab initiation was 51.0%, which aligns with the 12-month PFS of 55.9% in the PACIFIC trial.1 CRT followed by durvalumab seemed to provide a survival benefit, even in older adults.

In this study, durvalumab was delivered to 61 patients (70.9%). The PACIFIC study was unable to verify how many patients could be transferred to durvalumab because they were randomized after completion of CRT. Furthermore, recent real-world data described that durvalumab after CRT was delivered to 225 of 302 (75%) patients, with a median age of 70 years (range: 40-87 years).19 Our study confirmed the feasibility of transfer to durvalumab compared with that in younger individuals.

Since our study includes vulnerable patients, the entry criteria were more stringent than those in the PACIFIC study, such as an inclusion of V20 with <30% and an exclusion of any-grade pneumonitis before second enrollment. Considering the major AEs in our study, pneumonitis or radiation pneumonitis after durvalumab initiation occurred in 80.3% at any-grade and 9.8% at grade 3 or higher. By meta-analysis of 13 studies including 1885 patients, the incidences of any-grade and grade ≥3 pneumonitis were 35% and 6%, respectively.20 Traditionally, older adults experience a higher incidence of radiation pneumonitis.21, 22, 23 Compared with that in previous reports, the incidence of any-grade pneumonitis was more frequent in this study; however, the incidence of grade 3 or higher pneumonia seemed to be almost similar. In our study, the frequency of irAEs did not exceed that observed in the PACIFIC study. Several studies also described no significant difference in the frequency of irAEs between older adults and younger patients.24,25 In our case series, however, three patients died after durvalumab. The reason for death in two patients was associated with primary tumor deterioration, and for the other patient, it was a treatment-related death due to interstitial pneumonitis.

Furthermore, we explored the feasibility of durvalumab in patients with PS 2 up to 74 years of age. Seven patients were registered with a PS of 2 for durvalumab initiation, and the ORR was 57.1% (4/7). The median PFS and OS from the second enrollment were 6.6 months (95% CI 1.3 months to not reached) and 15.6 months (95% CI 1.3 months to not reached), respectively (see Supplementary Figure S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103939). The most common AE, except for hematologic toxicity and hypoalbuminemia, was pneumonitis (3/7, 57.1%) (see Supplementary Table S6, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2024.103939, which shows the most common AEs).

This study has some limitations. First, this study lacks data on quality-of-life assessment, such as patient-reported outcomes. Moreover, geriatric research recommends the analysis of physical and cognitive functions as endpoints.26 Second, the risk factor of pneumonitis, as the most alarming AE, was not analyzed. The detailed relationship between the efficacy and safety of durvalumab and tumor immune environment may be useful for the discovery.

In conclusion, while there was a lack of specific prospective data on standard treatment in older adults with PS of 2, our study showed the survival benefit and tolerability of durvalumab consolidation after daily carboplatin administration with radiotherapy for LA-NSCLC vulnerable patients.

Comprehensive identification and parametric uncertainty assessment in the dynamic modelling of a 3D crane system
Authors: Ibrahim Shaikh, Radek Matušů, Abebe Alemu Wendimu, Mihret Kochito Wolde, Soumya Ranjan Mahapatro, Safeer Ullah, Ali Aljazaeri, Sohaibullah Zarghoon
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper presents a detailed study of the dynamic modeling and parametric uncertainty analysis of a 3D crane system, providing insights into its positional and angular responses across varying operational conditions. Using real-world experimental data, the study identifies and quantifies key parameters, including system gains, time constants, and damping ratios. These parameters form the basis of a validated mathematical model that accurately reflects the crane’s dynamic behaviour under diverse Z-axis configurations and input conditions. The results demonstrate how payload height significantly affects both positional and angular dynamics, particularly revealing notable coupling effects between translational and rotational movements. Additionally, an asymmetric actuator dead-zone was experimentally observed and analysed to enhance the comprehensiveness of the system identification. A nonlinear state-space model and the manufacturer’s nominal linear translational model are used for comparison with the identified linear model. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, this is the first study to experimentally derive and quantify parametric uncertainties in a validated 3D crane system model, extending beyond existing literature, which often assumes fixed parameters or focuses on lower-dimensional models. The insights from this study establish a foundation for designing robust control strategies capable of handling the inherent variability and complexity of 3D crane systems. The findings have practical implications for industries requiring precise load handling and reliable operation under dynamic and uncertain conditions, such as construction, logistics, and automated systems.Keywords: 3D crane system, System identificationa, Asymmetric dead-zone, Parametric uncertainty, Positional dynamics, Angular dynamics, Mathematical modelingSubject terms: Electrical and electronic engineering, Mechanical engineering

Introduction

Dynamic crane systems are critical for a variety of industries where precise and reliable load handling is essential, from construction to shipping and manufacturing. Yet, controlling these systems presents unique challenges due to the complex and often unpredictable nature of crane dynamics and the impact of external conditions. As cranes become increasingly automated, the need for accurate, adaptable models and robust control strategies becomes even more pressing. While recent studies have made strides in crane modelling and control, there are still significant gaps remain in fully capturing the intricate behaviours and inherent uncertainties of 3D crane systems under real-world conditions. To address these gaps, this study presents a comprehensive approach to identifying and understanding the effects of parametric uncertainties in a 3D crane model, with the ultimate goal of improving performance and reliability across diverse operational scenarios.

Recent studies have developed extensive datasets for the dynamic analysis of cranes, including those for real-time control systems and system identification using machine learning methods. These datasets have been instrumental in enhancing operational efficiency and safety. This dataset-based research, however, has primarily focused on data generation and machine learning for tower cranes rather than on dynamic parameter identification or uncertainty quantification1. Identifying key parameters, such as boom yaw and pitch angles, is crucial for evaluating crane stability under various operational conditions. Work on truck cranes using LiDAR point cloud data and advanced segmentation techniques demonstrates this importance; however, though it remains centered on static geometry instead of multi-axis dynamic coupling2. Analysis of load oscillations that account for rope elasticity and crane foundation flexibility provides a more comprehensive representation of dynamic crane behaviour. Studies in this area highlight the importance of damping strategies in minimising oscillation amplitudes and improving stability under dynamic conditions, but these typically prioritise vertical hoisting and rely on simplified actuation and measurement assumptions3. Research integrating the Inteco 3D Crane model into control engineering education has shown its effectiveness in helping students understand and apply control system concepts. Yet, this educational setup was limited to laboratory conditions and did not consider full 3D dynamic coupling and parameter uncertainty4. Crane parameter estimation has contributed significantly to improving control accuracy, particularly through methods such as recursive least squares (RLS), which enhance precision in both static and semi-static environments. Still, these models have been axis-specific and lack a comprehensive uncertainty analysis across all parameters5. Automated systems have also demonstrated potential in specialised applications such as cultural heritage preservation, where cranes are used to increase measurement accuracy and reduce manual effort. However, such studies mainly served as measurement tools and did not involve dynamic or control modelling of crane systems6. These systems also tend to operate in controlled environments, failing to address the dynamic adaptability required in full 3D crane models. Route-tracking cranes with object identification mark another line of progress aimed at operational efficiency7. Yet, this work largely focuses on navigation rather than modelling the complete multi-axis dynamics essential for complex load management.

To address the challenges of unpredictable payload mass, researchers have implemented adaptive control methods such as sliding mode control and radial basis function neural networks to achieve stable performance in 3D cranes. Although these approaches have shown effectiveness in simulations, the adaptive sliding-mode control was validated only numerically and did not investigate how parameter uncertainty propagates through the system8. While these methods contribute to stabilising operations, they largely overlook the influence of specific uncertainties, including variations in time constants and damping ratios, on system dynamics. Beyond control algorithms, studies have refined nonlinear models for damage detection, thereby improving the reliability of structural monitoring. Even so, the correction resolves coordinates only and does not extend to uncertainty-aware dynamic modelling9. Data-driven methods, such as regularised genetic programming, have also demonstrated high accuracy in predicting crane dynamics10; however, these models often rely on extensive datasets and may lack generalizability across varied operational conditions. Prototypes of dynamic crane systems, including scaled-down models, have been explored to enhance testing safety and cost effectiveness, particularly in forestry applications; yet, these scaled systems have not been validated for full-scale behavior or uncertainty effects11. In industrial and construction contexts, computer vision techniques have been used to identify crane load fall zones in real time, significantly improving on-site safety, though without incorporating dynamic modelling aspects12. Neural network-based models for nonlinear systems have further advanced payload oscillation prediction accuracy, but these approaches focus on data fitting rather than parameter uncertainty13.

Using Lagrange equations, researchers have shown how payload variation affects crane stability, especially in environments with high variance14. Similarly, dual-condition dynamic inclination estimation methods in mining have shown promise for managing complex operational scenarios15. Work on modelling load sway and nonlinear dynamics in gantry cranes has supported the development of precise load-handling controllers, though validation remains limited to simulation rather than experimental testing16. Efforts to stabilise load oscillations through feedback linearisation and other nonlinear control strategies have proven effective in simulations, yet these controllers have not undergone hardware or uncertainty testing17. Full-scale experiments with container cranes have been instrumental in identifying damping characteristics and oscillation patterns, thereby improving precision in high-stress operations18. Tower crane control strategies have similarly aimed to minimise payload oscillations for enhanced stability during both rotational and translational movements, although they tend to simplify the interaction between structural dynamics and loads19. In path-tracking applications, controllers employing frequency response analysis have maintained high positional accuracy under varying load conditions, without addressing nonlinear coupling effects20. Improved techniques for identifying modal frequencies in quayside cranes have also advanced structural monitoring. Though modal, it omits nonlinear coupling effects21. Studies on underactuated 3D cranes have applied adaptive control methods to overcome limitations of traditional designs, but these remain largely review-based and lack experimental verification22.

Offshore operations introduce further complexity, where cable-driven mechanisms have been designed to reduce sway and improve load stability under challenging sea conditions. Still, it narrows to damping, not full uncertainty23. Multibody simulations of tower cranes have replicated elasticity and load dynamics to create realistic training environments for operators24, while hybrid metaheuristic methods for parameter identification in double-pendulum cranes have increased modelling accuracy, though validation remains simulation-based rather than experimental25. Machine learning approaches, such as multi-gene genetic programming, have been applied to achieve interpretable and accurate crane models, but often exclude coupled parameters and uncertainty effects26. Baseline-free damage detection frameworks further contribute to early fault identification, which is critical for maintaining operational safety, though these nonlinear models have limited relevance to 3D control applications27. Methods combining partial decoupling with fuzzy control have achieved precise positioning and reduced swinging in cranes with variable rope lengths, yet omit sensitivity and uncertainty evaluations28. Automated identification systems have improved sorting and transportation efficiency in logistics operations, though the emphasis remains on automation rather than modelling accuracy29. Finally, fuzzy logic-based anti-swing control in 3D cranes has demonstrated superior performance over traditional techniques, enabling more efficient handling of complex loads; still, it relies on simulation without comparing parametric uncertainty to experimental data30.

This study presents an experimentally validated, control-oriented model of a 3D crane. Parameters such as gains, time constants and damping ratios are identified from laboratory data for cart motions (X, Y, Z) and swing angles (, ) and collected in a compact MIMO transfer-function model with explicit parameter intervals and actuator dead zones. The identified model is compared with a nonlinear benchmark model and with the manufacturer’s nominal linear model for the cart axes, linking the experimental results to existing physics-based descriptions. Experimental setup and methodology

The experimental setup of this study comprises a 3D crane system designed to control the movement of the payload across the X, Y, and Z axes, allowing analysis of its oscillatory behaviour. This section details the primary components and methods employed to collect precise and reliable data for dynamic response evaluation. As shown in Figure 1, the laboratory setup consists of a crane model integrated with DC motors and encoders.

Laboratory setup of the 3D crane system, showing the crane model, control interface.

3D Crane System: The experimental setup comprises a 3D crane model driven by three DC motors that control the payload along the X, Y, and Z axes. Integrated encoders measure both positional and angular deviations of the lift-line. The angular variables (Angle X) and (Angle Y) represent the lift-line’s angular deviations from the vertical axis in the X- and Y-directions, respectively. With the mathematical model, is defined as the angle between the Y-axis and the lift-line, and as the angle between the negative Z-axis and the projection of the lift-line onto the XZ-plane. Regular calibration before each experiment ensures reliable measurements.

Payload: A 200-gram weight serves as the payload, suspended in a pendulum configuration. This setup allows the payload to respond dynamically to positional changes, accurately capturing oscillatory behaviours. Experiments with the payload were conducted at initial, middle, and end positions along the Z-axis to observe variations in response.

Control System: A computer-controlled system generates input pulse commands and records crane responses, managing data acquisition at high frequencies for time-accurate measurements. This system’s consistent input generation is essential for reproducible experimental results.

Data Acquisition System: This system provides high-resolution data with a sampling interval of 0.01 seconds, capturing the crane’s movements along each axis. The sampling rate is critical for analyzing both positional and angular dynamics, which are essential for accurate modelling.

Step and Impulse Inputs: Step and impulse inputs ranging from V to V were applied to examine positional and angular responses. Step inputs characterized steady-state and transient behaviour, while impulse inputs captured angular dynamics ( and ), enabling a detailed assessment of system performance under varying force magnitudes. The actuator has a built-in saturation limit of [−1, 1] V.

Data Collection: Positional responses along the X, Y, and Z axes were recorded over one second, while angular oscillations ( and ) were recorded for 30 seconds. A high-resolution sampling rate of 0.01 seconds was employed for precise data capture using MATLAB. For each axis and angle measurement, multiple experiments with positive and negative step inputs were performed, ensuring a parametric uncertainty assessment of the crane response under various conditions.

MATLAB Solver Setting: MATLAB’s ode45 solver, based on the Dormand-Prince method, was used to simulate the dynamic response of the crane. This solver is particularly suitable for analyzing smooth, continuous responses in systems governed by ordinary differential equations (ODEs).

Curve Fitting Techniques: To model system responses, the Least Squares method was applied to positional data to minimize errors between experimental data and predictions. This technique aims to estimate the optimal parameters of a model by reducing the cumulative squared differences between the observed values and the corresponding predictions. For angular data, the Prediction Error Method (PEM) accounted for noise and variability, improving the accuracy of the parameter estimation.

Measurement Units: Measurements for positional data (X, Y, and Z axes) are in meters (m), while angular data ( and ) are in radians (rad). The crane operates within a controlled space of in each axis.

Conclusion

This work delivers an experimentally validated, uncertainty-aware dynamic modelling framework for a 3D crane system. Using real data, we identified key parameters (gains, time constants, and damping ratios) and treated them as real intervals, thereby embedding parametric uncertainty directly into the model in Eq. 26. A comparative evaluation against simulations confirmed high fidelity over the considered operating envelope. In addition, dead-zone nonlinearity was empirically identified and incorporated into the analysis to better reflect practical behaviour. A nonlinear state-space model and the manufacturer’s nominal translational model are used for comparison with the identified linear model, clarifying the relationship between the uncertain linear description and the underlying mechanical model.

The resulting transfer-function set and MIMO matrix provide a compact, control-oriented description suitable for design and analysis. Within the intended regime small angular deviations and moderate-speed motions the current decoupled axis representation forms a complete and reliable model, including scenarios with simultaneous activation of the horizontal axes. On this basis, the study establishes a solid foundation for robust control synthesis that is resilient to parameter variability. The findings support safer and more precise load handling in construction, logistics, and automated material-flow systems by enabling prediction and mitigation of uncertainty effects. Promising next steps include explicit modelling of multi-axis couplings, real-time implementation on hardware, and targeted treatment of additional nonlinear effects to widen the validated operating envelope.

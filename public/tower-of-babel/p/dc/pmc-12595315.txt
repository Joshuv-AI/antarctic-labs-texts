Activation of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR) by pioglitazone in oral traumatic ulcers in female rats
Authors: Roque Soares Martins Neto, Lívia Victor da Silva, Clarice Lioba de Araújo, Angelo Gabriel Farias Valente, Antonio Alexandre Coelho, Caio Ferreira Freire Caetano, Gabriella Alves Julião Costa, Paulo Goberlânio de Barros Silva, Rafael Linard Avelar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aimed to evaluate the effect of pioglitazone treatment on oral ulcer healing in rats, given its potential to modulate inflammatory processes. Thirty-two animals were randomly allocated into four groups: one control and three treated with different doses of pioglitazone (5, 15, and 45 mg/kg). Oral mucosal ulceration was induced one hour after treatment using a 6 mm diameter and 1 mm deep punch. Samples were collected on days 1, 3, 7, and 14 for histological analysis with optical microscopy, while immunohistochemistry was performed to assess expression of inflammatory markers across five fields. Ulcer size significantly decreased in groups treated with 15 and 45 mg/kg compared with the control group on days 1, 3, and 7. By day 14, no differences were observed between groups, although all groups exhibited progressive reduction from day 7 onward. Histological scoring demonstrated reduced acute inflammation in pioglitazone-treated animals by day 3, evolving into mild inflammation by day 7 and minimal levels by day 14. Additionally, Pioglitazone treatment also reduced TNF-α, IL-6, and NFkB p65 levels compared to controls. This study demonstrates that pioglitazone accelerates oral healing by modulating inflammatory mediators, suggesting a novel therapeutic role in wound management.Keywords: Pioglitazone, Inflammation, Healing, Immunohistochemistry, Rats

Introduction

Thiazolidinediones (TZDs) or glitazones are drugs used to treat type 2 diabetes mellitus. They act as insulin sensitizers by binding to peroxisome proliferator-activated nuclear receptors (PPARs), a transcription factor abundant in fat cells [1], as PPAR agonizts [2]. The pioneering identification of TZDs as high-affinity ligands for PPARγ was first reported by Lehmann et al. [3], providing the molecular basis for their pharmacological action. Examples of drugs in the thiazolidinedione class are ciglitazone, troglitazone, rosiglitazone, pioglitazone, balaglitazone, and ragaglitazone [1].

This activation promotes anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties and inhibits endothelial inflammation and dysfunction [4]. Once absorbed by cells, these drugs are internalized in the nucleus, where their mechanism of action involves the activation of Peroxisome Proliferator-Activated Receptors (PPARs). PPARs comprise three isoforms encoded by distinct genes: PPAR-α, PPAR-β, and PPAR-γ. When activated, these subtypes act as ligand-dependent transcription factors that regulate target gene expression by binding to specific peroxisome proliferator responsive elements (PPRE) located in regulatory regions of each gene [1], [5].

By blocking the accumulation of fatty acids and glucose, there is a reduction in the inflammatory pathway orchestrated by NF-κB in monocytes, which reduces the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines. Thus, the activation of PPAR-α and PPAR-γ is antagonistic to NF-κB activity, reducing cytokine production by monocytes, which may be a therapeutic target in inflammatory processes [6]. TZDs are also able to increase the supply of nitric oxide mediated by eNOS, facilitating angiogenesis and reducing iNOS activity, with a consequent antioxidant effect, reducing the generation of free radicals by monocytes and polymorphonuclear cells [2].

Beyond their metabolic effects, PPAR-γ agonizts also exert profound immunomodulatory actions. Daynes and Jones [7] demonstrated that PPAR-γ activation regulates the adaptive immune response by inhibiting pro-inflammatory cytokine production and influencing T-cell differentiation. These findings support the concept that PPAR-γ ligands not only act as metabolic regulators but also as key modulators of inflammation, which may explain their therapeutic potential in conditions characterized by excessive or persistent inflammatory responses.

In this sense, in acute inflammatory processes, such as traumatic oral ulcers in patients, TZDs can be an essential pharmacological tool for attenuating inflammation-mediated damage and accelerating tissue healing in the face of traumatic agents. Traumatic oral ulcers are important pathological entities in the dental clinic that cause pain, discomfort, difficulty eating, weight loss, and quality of life deficits [8]. After acute trauma to the oral mucosa, damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) and molecules released from damaged host cells activate receptors on macrophages and dendritic cells, with consequent tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin 1 (IL-1), interleukin 6 (IL-6), among others, which are highly pro-inflammatory and pain-inducing and, coincidentally, are strongly blocked by the action of TZDs [9].

Thus, given the potential of TZDs to control inflammatory processes, this study aimed to evaluate the influence of treatment with pioglitazone on the healing of oral ulcers in rats. Undoubtedly, the pioneering description of PPARγ activation by TZDs [3], its negative regulatory role on macrophage activity [10], and its immunomodulatory potential [7] provide strong biological rationale for this investigation.

Materials and methods

Ethical approach and sample calculation

This study was submitted to the Ethics Committee for the Use of Animals at Unichristus under protocol 005/20. All experiments were conducted in accordance with the Ethical Principles for Animal Experimentation adopted by the Brazilian College of Animal Experimentation (COBEA). The animals were housed in an environment with controlled air temperature and humidity, with free access to water and standard chow, and every effort was made to minimize their suffering during the study.

To estimate the required sample size, we referred to the study by Konturek et al. [11], who observed that treatment with pioglitazone, a PPARγ agonist, improved the healing of gastric ulcers in rats (mean ± SD: untreated group = 11.3 ± 3.9 mm²; pioglitazone group = 5.2 ± 2.4 mm²). Based on these results, it was estimated that six animals per group would be required to test the alternative hypothesis of this study with 90 % power and a 95 % confidence level (Student’s t-test). However, considering the possibility of sample loss, we increased the number by 20 %, resulting in a total of eight rats per group.

Experimental groups and treatment with pioglitazone

The animals received a daily gavage of 0.1 ml/kg of each solution one hour before the induction of the oral ulcer and continued to receive the solutions until euthanasia, which was carried out 1, 3, 7, and 14 days after ulcer induction. Blinding was applied during ulcer induction, ulcer size measurement, and histopathological evaluation to minimize bias. This study design was based on studies previously published by the team that demonstrated the influence of different times [12] and different doses of drugs [13] on the healing of oral ulcers in rats.

Oral ulcer, clinical parameters, and euthanasia

A total of 128 female Wistar rats [14] (n = 8/group/day of euthanasia) were used in this study. Ulceration of the oral mucosa of female Wistar rats was performed one hour after treatment. The animals were anesthetized with an intraperitoneal injection of ketamine (80 mg/kg, i.p.) and xylazine (20 mg/kg, intraperitoneal). Antisepsis of the oral mucosa was carried out using cotton pellets soaked in 0.12 % chlorhexidine digluconate. The left buccal mucosa was ulcerated using a punch 6 mm in diameter and 1 mm deep, and the soft tissue fragment was removed with a scalpel and #15 blade.

The animals euthanized 1, 3, 7, and 14 days after ulceration and weighed (final weight). After euthanasia, the ulcers were measured using a digital caliper accurate to 0.5 mm (D = largest diameter; d = smallest diameter), and the sizes of the ulcerated areas were expressed using the following formula: A = π x (D x d) / 4 (mm²) [12], [13].

Histological processing and histomorphometric evaluation

Buccal mucosa samples were collected 1, 3, 7, and 14 days after ulceration, fixed in 10 % neutral formalin for 24 h, dehydrated in graded alcoholic series, diaphanized in xylene, and impregnated with paraffin. The samples were cut into three μm-thick glass slides to be stained with hematoxylin and eosin (HE).

The histologic sections were analyzed using an optical microscope (BX43, with Olympus Soft Imagining LCMicro software) and exported to ImageJ to cell counting. The histological characteristics of the ulcers and the corresponding healing phases were described scored on a scale from 0 to 4, as follows: (0) absence of ulcer / remodeled connective tissue (mature, homogeneous and parallel collagen fibers); (1) absence of ulcer/fibrosis (noticeable and diffuse deposition of collagen fibers), with mild chronic inflammation; (2) ulcer/fibrosis, with moderate chronic inflammation; (3) ulcer / chronic inflammation (granulation tissue); (4) ulcer / acute process (dilated vessels and mixed inflammatory infiltrate containing neutrophils) [15].

To assess the infiltration of polymorphonuclear neutrophils and mononuclear cells, five fields of the ulcerated areas of each sample (HE staining) were photographed with a camera (U-TV0.63XC,) attached to the microscope, and five microscopic micro-fields were chosen to count the number of polymorphonuclear neutrophils (cells with segmented and multilobular nuclei) and mononuclear cells. The sum of the cells in the five micro-fields was the sample unit considered [12].

Preparation of tissue micro array blocks and immunohistochemistry reactions

Two representative areas of the ulcer from each animal were selected on the histological slides, and these areas were briefly removed from the donor paraffin block using the Tissue Micro Arrayer device to a recipient block containing 70 wells of the same diameter and depth (2 mm). 3-µm sections of this block on silanized slides were submitted to immunohistochemistry.

The samples were subjected to antigen retrieval with 0.1 M citrate solution pH 6.0, and after cooling to room temperature, the sections were washed in 0.1 M phosphate buffer solution pH7.3 (PBS) and subjected to endogenous peroxidase blocking with 3 % H O22 diluted in PBS for 20 min.

After washing, slides were incubated overnight with the following antibodies: anti-PPARy (1:1400, ®️Termofisher, pa1824), anti-PPARδ/β (1:500; Termofisher®️; pa5–516368), α-SMA (ready-to-use; monoclonal;), anti-TNF-α (1:100, A11534, Abclonal), IL-6 (1:100, A0286, Abclonal) and anti-NFkB p65 (1:100, Termofisher ®️, PA527617).

The next day, after cooling, the sections were washed (PBS) and incubated with Dako Envision Dual Link System HRP (K406189–2) anti-rabbit/anti-mouse polymer (ready-to-use) for 30 min. After washing again, the slides were stained with 3,3-diamino-benzidine (DAB; ab64238) (5′), counterstained with 7 % Harris hematoxylin (10'') and mounted with Enthellam.

The same imaging methodology was used for immunohistochemical analysis, counting the cells with nuclear (NFkB p65) or cytoplasmic (other markers) positivity for the markers above in the five fields. The sum of cells in 5 microscopic fields will be the sample unit [15]

Statistical analysis

The data were subjected to the Shapiro-Wilk normality test, expressed as means and standard deviation, and compared using the ANOVA-2-way test followed by the Bonferroni post-test and linear regression model (GraphPad Prism 6.0 for Windows, p < 0.05). Each experimental group consisted of six independent samples (n = 6).

Results and discussion

Treatment with pioglitazone improves clinical and histological parameters of oral ulcers in rats

The size of the ulcers showed a significant reduction in the Pio-15 and Pio-45 groups when compared to the saline group on day one (p < 0.001). Three (p = 0.006) and seven (p = 0.003) days after the ulcers were made, all the groups treated with pioglitazone showed smaller ulcer areas than the saline group. On Day 14, there was no difference between the groups (p = 0.256). In both the saline group (p < 0.001) and the Pio-5 (p < 0.001), Pio-15 (p < 0.001), and Pio-45 (p = 0.028) groups, there was a significant reduction in the size of the ulcers from the seventh day after surgery. Due start with a high oral ulcer area, only saline group showed significant regression (p = 0.012) (Table 1, Fig. 1).

In the first three days after the ulcers were made, all the animals lost weight, and there was no difference between the study groups one (p = 0.919) and three (p = 0.673) days after the ulcers were made. Seven days after the ulcers were made, all the animals treated with pioglitazone showed a greater gain in body mass than those in the saline group (p = 0.005). On day 14 (p = 0.278), there was no difference between the study groups. The saline group (p = 0.020) showed a gain in body mass from day 14 after the ulcer was made, and the Pio-5 (p = 0.010), Pio-15 (p < 0.001), and Pio-45 (p = 0.016) groups showed a significant gain in body mass from day seven (Table 1, Fig. 1).

Histomorphometrically, the PMN count was significantly reduced in the Pio-15 and Pio-45 groups compared to saline on day one (p = 0.029). Three (p = 0.283), seven (p = 0.567), and 14 (p = 0.224) days after the ulcer was made, there was no significant difference between the groups. All the groups showed a reduction in the PMN count from day seven onwards (p < 0.001). Saline (p = 0.041, Pio-5 (p = 0.014) and Pio-45 (p = 0.010) showed significant regression of this parameter (Table 1, Fig. 1).

The mononuclear count showed no significant difference between the groups in the first seven days (p > 0.05), but 14 days after the ulcer was made, all the groups treated with pioglitazone showed a significant reduction in the MN count compared to saline (p = 0.009). While the saline group showed no significant variation in the MN count over the 14 days of the study (p = 0.102), all the groups treated with pioglitazone showed a significant reduction in this parameter 14 days after the ulcer was made (p < 0.001) (Table 1, Fig. 1).

Concerning the histological scores, all the groups showed a median score of 4 one day after the ulcer was made, corresponding to ulceration associated with an acute inflammatory process. Three days after the ulcer was made, while the saline and Pio-5 groups had a median score of 4, the Pio-15 and Pio-45 groups showed a significant reduction in this parameter (p = 0.042) with a median score of 3, which corresponds to ulceration associated with a chronic inflammatory process. Seven days after ulceration, there was no difference between the groups, with the saline, Pio-5, and Pio-15 groups showing a median score of 3 and the Pio-45 group a median score of 2 (p = 0.184), which corresponds to ulceration-associated with a mild inflammatory process. Similarly, after 14 days, there was no difference between the groups, with the saline, Pio-5, and Pio-15 groups showing a median score of 2 and the Pio-45 group a median score of 1 (p = 0.208), which corresponds to a total re-epithelialization associated with a minimal inflammatory process. The saline (p < 0.001), Pio-5 (p < 0.001), and Pio-15 (p < 0.001) groups showed a significant reduction in histological scores from day 14, and the Pio-45 group showed a significant reduction in histological scores from day 7 (p < 0.001). All groups showed significant regression of histologic scores (Table 1, Fig. 1).

Treatment with pioglitazone reduces the acute inflammatory process in oral ulcers without interfering with fibroplasia in rats

The number of TNF-α–positive cells showed no difference between groups on days 1 (p = 0.876) and 3 (p = 0.768) after ulcer induction. However, Pio-15 and Pio-45 groups exhibited lower TNF-α immunostaining than saline on day 7 (p < 0.001). By day 14, no significant difference was observed. While the saline group showed a reduction only at day 14 (p = 0.003), pioglitazone-treated groups showed reductions as early as day 7 (p < 0.001) (Table 2, Fig. 2).

Treatment with pioglitazone attenuates immunostaining for PPARy and PPARδ/β at the end of the inflammatory process of traumatic ulcers in rats

The number of PPARy-positive cells showed no difference between the groups one (p = 0.756), three (p = 0.898), seven (p = 0.686), or 14 (p = 0.454) days after the ulcer was made. However, while the saline (p = 0.092), Pio-5 (p = 0.280), and Pio-15 (p = 0.738) groups showed no significant variation in this parameter over the 14 days of the experiment, the Pio-45 group showed a significant reduction in PPARy immunostaining after day 14 of the ulcer (p = 0.018) (Table 2, Fig. 6).

Discussion

In the present study, pioglitazone accelerated tissue healing and reduced the acute inflammatory process by blocking the NF-kB pathway and minimizing the endogenous activation of PPARy and PPARδ/β.

PPARs are a subgroup of ligand-activated transcription factors that can regulate the transcriptional activity of inflammatory cytokines. Two different mechanisms have been described: one as a ligand-dependent transcription factor, in which PPAR pathway proteins bind to DNA in the promoter region of genes with sequences known as peroxisome proliferator response elements (PPREs), and the other as transcription factors, in which PPARs can control gene expression by associating with activating proteins independently of PPREs. This way, PPARs can regulate the sequences of many genes involved in various metabolic and inflammatory functions [16].

PPARy agonist drugs, such as those of the TZD class, inhibit the acute inflammatory process and have been linked to the reversal of inflammatory endothelial dysfunction ( [1], [4], [5]. Recently, these drugs have increasingly been linked to the regulation of the immune response, particularly in the control of inflammation, thus being able to act as potential therapeutic targets [17]. In our study, a significant reduction was observed in ulcer size, inflammatory cell count, and immunostaining for TNF-α, IL-6, and NF-kB p65, the reduction of which is associated with an improvement in the inflammatory process of oral ulcers in rats [15].

With the activation of these cytokines, neutrophils, and monocytes translocate to the injured tissue, releasing enzymes that fight infectious organisms and eliminate dead cells, but also cause the accumulation of reactive oxygen species in the tissues, which damage healthy cells and generate edema mediated by vasodilation [18]. Therefore, PPAR-γ agonizts can inhibit macrophage activation and the production of inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α, interleukin (IL)-6, and IL-1β [16]. This is supported by additional evidence showing that PPARγ controls macrophage gene expression beyond ligand binding [19], contributes to improved vascular function and reduced oxidative stress [20], and modulates immune differentiation pathways [21]

In adipocytes, PPAR agonizts decrease lipolysis and increase adipocyte differentiation, thus decreasing the release of substances that inhibit insulin action, such as free fatty acids, TNF-α, IL-6, and resistin[9], reducing the expression of these cytokines even in ethanol-mediated gastric lesions [22]. By blocking the accumulation of fatty acids and glucose, there is also a reduction in the inflammatory pathway orchestrated by NF-κB in monocytes, which reduces the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines. Thus, the activation of PPAR-α and PPAR-γ is antagonistic to the activity of NF-κB, reducing the production of cytokines by monocytes [6].

PPAR-γ ligands, such as 15d-PGJ2 and troglitazone, are described as activating SOCS3 expression and inhibiting the JAK2/STAT3 activation pathway, reducing IL-6 expression and edema formation, similar to that observed in the present study [23]On the other hand, ciglitazone and troglitazone, after binding to and activating PPARs in T lymphocytes, induce apoptosis of these cells when activated, especially in Th1 mode [24], contributing to a reduction in pro-inflammatory cytokines. This reduction is dose-dependent and has even interfered with the endogenous expression of PPAR in these cells, as observed in the present study when the groups treated with the highest dose of pioglitazone showed lower expression of PPAR-γ and PPARδ/β at the end of the inflammatory process.

Pioglitazone may be an essential pharmacological tool to attenuate inflammation-mediated damage and accelerate tissue healing in the face of traumatic agents. In addition to having an antioxidant effect by increasing the supply of nitric oxide-mediated by eNOS, facilitating angiogenesis [25], thiazolidinediones are associated with a reduction in apoptosis mediated by iNOS [26]. In addition, when PPAR antagonists are administered in inflammatory models, there is an intense reduction in NFkB p65 activity and reduced inflammatory pain [26] similar to that described in the present study with reduced NFkB p65 immunostaining and increased weight gain, reflecting a lower nociceptive process [27], [28].

Griggs et al. (2015) [29] demonstrated that pioglitazone has a neuroprotective role in the central nervous system, controlling inflammatory pain, neuropathic pain, Parkinson's disease, and traumatic brain injuries and epileptic disorders by reversing mechanical allodynia and hyperalgesia and allodynia to cold when administered up to two weeks after the induction of these conditions. Therefore, pioglitazone seems to reduce inflammatory pain and play a role in neuropathic pain, thus contributing to the weight of the animals targeted in this study.

Although previous studies have shown that increasing [8] or strongly blocking [15], [30], [31] of TNF-α expression utilizing monoclonal antibodies negatively interferes with the tissue healing of ulcers in rats, including blocking collagenesis and fibroplasia, moderate control of this cytokine has been shown to control acute inflammation without interfering with fibroplasia (immunostaining for α-SMA). In oral ulcers, potent anti-inflammatory drugs such as corticosteroids are associated with delayed tissue healing and prolonging the inflammatory process. However, using lower potency molecules controls this process well without interfering with healing [8], similar to what was described in this study.

Thus, even with the inherent limitations of animal studies and knowing that pioglitazone is a drug that interferes with lipid and glucose metabolic pathways not studied in this research, we observed that this drug significantly attenuates the inflammatory process without interfering with the healing processes.

Conclusion

In summary, pioglitazone significantly accelerated oral ulcer healing in rats by reducing acute inflammation, modulating the NF-κB pathway, and attenuating the expression of key pro-inflammatory cytokines. These findings reinforce the therapeutic potential of PPAR-γ agonizts as modulators of inflammatory responses in oral wound healing. Recent evidence has further highlighted the role of PPAR activation as a promising pharmacological target in chronic inflammatory diseases, including inflammatory bowel disease, supporting the broader relevance of this pathway beyond oral tissues. Taken together, our results suggest that pioglitazone may represent a valuable pharmacological tool for controlling excessive inflammation while preserving tissue repair processes, warranting further translational studies.

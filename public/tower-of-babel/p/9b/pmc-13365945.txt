IFPHOR0201 Influence of Socioecological Factors on Well-Being and Food Security of Nigerian Graduate Students in Sheffield
Authors: Moses I Nwuzoh, Isaac M Danat
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFood insecurity (FI) and mental wellbeing (MW) are critical concerns within higher education. However, evidence on their relationship and socioecological correlates among international students in UK higher education is limited. This study examined the association between food insecurity and mental wellbeing, and their socioecological determinants among postgraduate students in Sheffield, UK.A cross-sectional online survey of 277 Nigerian postgraduate students aged 20 to 42 was conducted across two universities in Sheffield, UK. Data were collected using the validated Food Security Module and the Short Warwick Edinburgh Mental Wellbeing Scale. Socioecological variables across individual, interpersonal, institutional, community, and policy levels were assessed using an online questionnaire designed in Qualtrics software. Data were analysed using t tests, ANOVA, chi-square tests, and Spearman’s correlation in IBM SPSS version 26.FI (80.5%) and high mental wellbeing (70.4%) were highly prevalent. FI was associated with limited social support (p< .001), high food costs (p< .01), lack of access to culturally appropriate food (p< .05), academic time constraints (p< .01), and transport costs (p< .01). Mental wellbeing was associated with age (p< .01), sex (p< .05), income (p< .05), social support (p< .001), perceived health (p< .05), and residence type (p< .01). An inverse correlation was observed between FI and mental wellbeing (r= –.23, p< .001, 95% CI [–.35, –.12]).Findings highlight the intersectional nature of structural, social, and individual factors. Targeted, culturally responsive interventions are needed to address systemic barriers to food security and improve mental wellbeing among international postgraduate students.

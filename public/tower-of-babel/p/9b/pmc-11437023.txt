Harnessing probabilistic neural network with triple tree seed algorithm-based smart enterprise quantitative risk management framework
Authors: Iyad Katib, Emad Albassam, Sanaa A. Sharaf, Mahmoud Ragab
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Enterprise risk management (ERM) frameworks convey vital principles that help create a consistent risk management culture, irrespective of employee turnover or industry standards. Enterprise Management System (EMS) are becoming a popular research area for assuring a company’s long-term success. Statistical pattern recognition, federated learning, database administration, visualization technology, and social networking are all used in this field, which includes artificial intelligence (AI), data science, and statistics. Risk assessment in EMS is critical for enterprise decision-making to be effective. Recent advancements in AI, machine learning (ML), and deep learning (DL) concepts have enabled the development of effective risk assessment models for EMS. This special issue seeks groundbreaking research articles that showcase the application of applied probability and statistics to interdisciplinary studies. This study offers Improved Metaheuristics with a Deep Learning Enabled Risk Assessment Model (IMDLRA-SES) for Smart Enterprise Systems. Using feature selection (FS) and DL models, the provided IMDLRA-SES technique estimates business risks. Preprocessing is used in the IMDLRA-SES technique to change the original financial data into a usable format. In addition, an FS technique based on oppositional lion swarm optimization (OLSO) is utilized to find the best subset of features. In addition, the presence or absence of financial hazards in firms is classified using the triple tree seed algorithm (TTSA) with a probabilistic neural network (PNN) model. The TTSA is used as a hyperparameter optimizer to improve the efficiency of the PNN-based categorization. An extensive set of experimental evaluations is performed on German and Australian credit datasets to illustrate the IMDLRA-SES model’s improved performance. The performance validation of the IMDLRA-SES model portrayed a superior accuracy value of 95.70% and 96.09% over existing techniques.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-73876-w.

Introduction

EMS and big data analytics are the most promising computing environments in statistics, AI, and information science that employ database management, statistical pattern recognition, federated learning, social networking, and visualization technology1. Financial default or failure is expected when a corporation is stuck in financially insolvent troubles and has endured substantial or chronic losses. Most businesses suffering financial default or collapse must deal with a negative impact on investor confidence, cash flow complications, and financial market instability2,3. The ineffective circulation of finite economic resources (such as money and capital) is one probable outcome, which is one of the main reasons why researchers lay too much emphasis on developing a financial forewarning approach. With the rise of big data and AI, numerous academics have turned to the ML method to undertake thorough risk assessment studies4. ERM solutions are critical to financial institutions’ smooth operation at home and overseas5. Traditional methods for determining if a user is in default fail to meet the demands of high-risk prediction accuracy, several types of data, and many users. A variety of researchers use ML. A comprehensive review of research findings and related debates has been undertaken to demonstrate that the technique is capable of generalization and superior prediction6. In the past, researchers commonly used risk assessment methods based on statistical learning methods. It is especially interested in submissions that attach theoretical advances in executed probability and statistics with real-world applications in distinct fields, encompassing biology, medicine, engineering, social sciences, environmental science, finance, and public health. The DL is a subset of ML, encompassing many AI techniques. Literature review

The researcher in11 considered the internal factors influencing a company’s financial health and devised a financial early warning index method with 18 secondary indicators. The study uses LSTM-NN in DL to build an early warning system. The results reveal that the early warning system-based DL might have a prediction accuracy above 85%. Huang et al.12 investigated using ML approaches in corporate risk assessment. The current work uses three ML models to predict enterprise risk: SVM, AdaBoost, and RF. The enterprise risk assessment index is initially built in a specific application to generally identify the danger posed by the organization using multiple characteristics. In13, the researcher proposed a new hybrid method for enterprise operational performance prediction that combines incremental filter-wrapper feature subset selection with statistical investigation and twin SVM (IFWTSVM). Injadat et al.14 proposed RS Multi-Boosting, a hybrid ensemble ML method that combines two conventional ensemble ML techniques, multi-Boosting and random subspace (RS), to improve the performance of SME credit risk prediction. Lu15 utilizes a multi-element semantic network to express the network architecture of supply chain risk knowledge and provides a multilevel inventory control technique depending on the relation attribute of supply chain risk information.

In16, the researcher presented a smart IoT-assisted FCP system using a metaheuristic approach. Preprocessing, data collecting, categorization, and FS are all included in the method given FS. First, IoT devices such as laptops, cellphones, and similar devices collect financial data from the company. The obtained financial data is then classified using LSTM and RNN models. A tourism business’s current financial risk management is estimated in17, utilizing the solvency of capital structure, corporate finance, profitability, and operating effectiveness as indicators. The NN in DL then creates the backpropagation NN (BPNN) method. As a result, the presented model is used in leisure tourism firms’ risk management schemes to identify, address, and analyze financial risk. The author proposed the ResNet dynamic knowledge reasoning technique in18 to improve the loss balancing function of a multi-Net method. The experiment reveals that the unique strategy can significantly increase relationship and entity prediction performance. Finally, a clever question-answering technique is used in the knowledge graph. Li and Yang19 propose a novel PRTSA technique integrating OS-extreme learning machine (EOSELM) with binary Jaya (BinJaya)-based FS, utilized to phasor measurement units (PMUs) data. The methodology incorporates OS-ELM with an online boosting model. Furthermore, BinJaya-based FS detects the optimal feature subset from system-level classification features derived from PMU data.

Zhang et al.20 introduce an optimized extreme learning machine (ELM) technique using synchrophasors. Input features are extracted from synchronized measurements, and an ELM classifier is employed to build the TSP method. The improved particle swarm optimization (IPSO) model is utilized for the tuning process. Mohan et al.21 propose an Eagle Strategy Arithmetic Optimisation Algorithm with Optimal Deep Convolutional Forest (ESAOA-ODCF) based FinTech Application for Hyperautomation. Wei et al.22 present a unified framework incorporating intra-risk and contagion risks. It uses an intra-risk encoder depending on key risk indicators and a contagion risk encoder featuring Heterogeneous Hyper-Graph Neural Networks (HHGNN) and Hierarchical Graph Transformer Networks (HGTN) models to capture and combine risk types. Wang et al.23 introduce a Relational Graph Attention Network (RGAT) technique to capture complex network structures, improving credit risk prediction for financial services despite incomplete data from target SMEs. Agboola et al.24 compute the best data selection and ML methods for landslide vulnerability assessment. It relates two approaches for choosing landslide-free points and tests five models, SVM, Logistic Regression, LDA, Random Forest, and XGBoost, for predicting landslide susceptibility.

The existing research on financial risk management faces various limitations across diverse methods. Early warning systems utilizing DL approaches such as LSTM-NN can face difficulty with large, complex datasets and overfitting. ML methods, namely SVM, AdaBoost, and RF, may not fully capture temporal financial data complexities or be affected by feature quality. Hybrid techniques integrating FS with dual SVM might be computationally intensive and less scalable for massive datasets. Ensemble RS Multi-Boosting models may be challenged by high-dimensional or noisy data, complicating model interpretation. Multi-element semantic networks might lack adaptability to dynamic risk environments, while IoT-assisted systems could encounter incorporation problems and limitations in handling sequential data. Incorporating OS-ELM with binary Jaya-based FS may encounter scalability and efficiency issues. Optimization with the IPSO technique might cause trouble with high-dimensional data. Advanced neural network frameworks for risk modelling can be intrinsic and challenging to interpret. At the same time, data selection and ML methods for landslide prediction may suffer from limited generalizability and performance variation. Finally, methodologies such as BPNN may overfit and struggle with generalization, and dynamic knowledge reasoning methods may face difficulties in balancing loss functions and combining complex question-answering mechanisms. Existing research in financial risk management often finds difficulty with scalability, adaptability to dynamic data, and integration across various data sources. Several techniques concentrate on specific factors such as early warning or FS but lack comprehensive solutions addressing the intrinsic interplay between risk factors and real-time data processing. Moreover, there is a requirement for enhanced techniques that efficiently balance computational effectualness with predictive accuracy, especially in dynamic and large-scale financial environments.

Data preprocessing

Z-score normalization is used to standardize data values, making them comparable regardless of their original scale. This methodology converts every data point to reflect its deviation from the mean of the dataset, computed in terms of standard deviation (SD). Here’s a step-by-step breakdown of the Z-score normalization procedure:

Compute mean and SD

Mean (µ): First, compute the mean of the dataset, which is the average value of all data points. This gives the dataset a central reference point.

SD (σ): Next, determine the SD, which evaluates the dispersion or variability of the data points around the mean. A higher SD depicts more spread-out data, while a lesser SD suggests that the data points are closer to the mean.

Transform data points

For every individual data point (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:x$$\end{document}), subtract the mean () from the data point. This step alters the value by eliminating the central tendency and centring the data around zero.

Divide the result by the SD (). This division scales the adjusted value according to the variability of the dataset. The formula for this transformation is:1\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:Z-score=\frac{\left(x-\mu\:\right)}{\sigma\:}$$\end{document}

This analysis yields a Z-score, which depicts how many SDs the data point is from the mean. A Z-score of 0 means the data point is exactly at the mean, while positive and negative values subsequently exhibit how far the point is above or below the mean.

Interpretation

Z-scores close to 0: Data points with Z-scores close to zero are near the mean of the distribution.

Positive Z-scores: Values with positive Z-scores are above the mean, with larger Z-scores indicating greater distances from the mean.

Negative Z-scores: Values with negative Z-scores are below the mean, with smaller (more negative) Z-scores showing greater deviations from the mean.

Range and outliers: Generally, Z-scores between − 3 and + 3 comprise most data in a normal dispersion. Values outside this range are considered outliers, representing unusual or extreme data points.

Standardizing the data utilizing Z-score normalization allows each variable to have a mean of 0 and an SD of 1. This uniform scaling eases more precise comparisons and evaluation across diverse datasets and features, enhancing the performance and interpretability of subsequent data processing and modelling tasks.

Process involved in OLSO based FS technique

The OLSO-based FS approach has been employed to derive an optimum FS in addition to data preprocessing25. The OLSO technique is selected for FS due to its advanced optimization capabilities and robustness. The OLSO method effectually balances exploration and exploitation by employing oppositional-based learning, which improves its ability to avert local optima and improve convergence speed related to conventional approaches. This methodology allows for a more comprehensive search of the feature space, confirming that the most relevant features are chosen while minimizing redundancy. Its effectualness in handling high-dimensional and intrinsic datasets makes it a superior choice for FS, resulting in enhanced model performance and mitigated computational complexity. The capability of the OLSO technique to incorporate various data sources and adapt dynamically to diverse datasets additionally emphasizes its merit over existing techniques.

The current LSO method, inspired by lion hunting behaviour, performs better concerning solution correctness and convergence time when compared to the traditional PSO methodology and genetic algorithm (GA). Conclusion

A new IMDLRA-SES technique has been created in this work for efficient risk assessment in businesses. For risk assessment, the IMDLRA-SES model uses an FS-based classification approach. Preprocessing, OLSO-based FS, PNN-based classification, and TSA-based hyperparameter optimizer are the four steps of the IMDLRA-SES model. The PNN-based classification’s classification efficiency is improved by using the TTSA-based hyperparameter tuning procedure. Many simulations were run, and the outcomes were compared to a financial benchmark dataset.

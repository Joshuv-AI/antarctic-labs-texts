Validity and reliability study of the Turkish version of the postpartum sexual life quality scale
Authors: Funda Tosun Güleroğlu, Taylan Onat, Yurdagül Günaydın
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThe aim of this study is to establish the validity and reliability of the Turkish version of the Postpartum Sexual Life Quality Scale (PSLQS), which evaluates the sexual life quality of women in the postpartum period, and to introduce it to Turkish society.Materials and methodsData for this methodological study were collected between March 2022 and March 2023. The population of the study consisted of women in the postpartum period who presented to a hospital in the Central Anatolia region of Turkey, and the sample of the study included 284 women. The data collection instruments included a personal information form and the Postpartum Sexual Life Quality Scale. Data were analyzed using SPSS 23.0 (Statistical Package for the Social Sciences) and IBM AMOS V24 package programs.ResultsThe Turkish adaptation of the scale was evaluated for linguistic and content validity, construct validity, and reliability. The content validity index of the scale was found to be above 0.80. The fit indices obtained from the Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) were as follows: CMIN/DF (χ²/df) = 2.053, GFI = 0.888, IFI = 0.928, TLI = 0.914, CFI = 0.927, RMSEA = 0.061, SRMR = 0.060. The Cronbach’s alpha coefficient for the scale was 0.849, and for its subscales, Cronbach’s alpha coefficients were as follows: Sexual beliefs 0.745, Sexual performance 0.803, Marital life in the context of parenthood 0.851, Postpartum psychological changes 0.829, and Postpartum sexual changes 0.806. Item-total score correlations for the scale items ranged from 0.215 to 0.736.ConclusionThe study concluded that the Turkish adaptation of PSLQS is a valid and reliable measurement tool that can be used in Turkish society.Keywords: Postpartum, Sexual life quality, Scale, Validity, Reliability

Introduction

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), sexual health is a state of physical, emotional, mental, and social well-being related to sexuality. It involves having safe and pleasurable sexual experiences, as well as a positive and respectful approach to sexuality, free from discrimination, coercion, and violence [1]. Sexual life and sexual health can be influenced by various factors. Chronic illnesses, hormonal and neurological issues, surgeries, and past illnesses are among these factors [2]. Such factors negatively affect individuals’ enjoyment of sexuality, their ability to engage in sexual activities, and consequently, their sexual life quality [3].

The postpartum period, which is the period after childbirth, represents a new phase for women during which they strive to adapt. Studies indicate that sexual problems are common during the postpartum period, with issues often intensifying in the first three months postpartum and potentially persisting for up to 12 months or longer after childbirth [4–7]. Reviewing the literature reveals that the postpartum period significantly impacts women’s sexual life quality [2]. However, studies focusing on sexual life quality typically employ scales such as the Female Sexual Quality of Life Scale, the Female Sexual Function Index, which are applied to the general population [8, 9]. It is believed that a measurement tool specifically questioning the sexual life quality of women in the postpartum period would be more effective in highlighting this often overlooked issue.

Martinez et al. (2016), in a qualitative study examining the sexual life quality of women in the postpartum period, concluded that assessing women’s sexual function should consider not only the physical aspects but also the emotional and relational dimensions [5]. They suggested that evaluating this significant period in a woman’s life more objectively and realistically requires taking into account these multiple dimensions of sexual functioning. This underscores the need for a measurement tool that can comprehensively assess sexual life, considering its various aspects beyond just the physical dimension. The adaptation of the PSLQS into Turkish is expected to fill an important gap in clinical and academic assessments of postpartum sexual life in Turkish women. Currently, there is no specific tool tailored to the postpartum period within the Turkish cultural context that evaluates sexual life quality multidimensionally. By introducing the Turkish version of the PSLQS, healthcare professionals and researchers will have access to a validated tool that can inform both individualized care and population-level interventions in maternal sexual health.

Materials and methods

Research design and sample

This study employs a methodological design aimed at determining the validity and reliability of the Turkish version of the Postpartum Sexual Life Quality Scale (PSLQS). The research was conducted at Yozgat Bozok University Research and Application Hospital in the Central Anatolia region of Turkey. According to Karaçam (2019), in order to conduct factor analysis for adapting a scale to different cultures, the sample size should be at least 5–10 times the number of items in the scale [10]. Given that the scale consists of 22 items, the sample size was calculated by multiplying 10 times the number of items, resulting in a target sample size of at least 220 women. The study was completed with a total of 284 women who met the inclusion criteria.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: being over 18 years old, married, primiparous, Turkish citizen, having experienced a healthy pregnancy, and having had an uncomplicated vaginal delivery or cesarean section in the last 6 months. Exclusion criteria included experiencing a negative experience such as the death of a close relative in the last 6 months, a history of psychological illness or sexual dysfunction before pregnancy, and discomfort discussing sexuality.

The data collection tools

The data for the study were obtained using the Demographic Information Form and the Postpartum Sexual Quality of Life Scale.

Demographic information form

Prepared by researchers after reviewing the literature, consisting of 8 questions querying individual characteristics of women (age, educational status, family type, type of marriage, etc.) (8.11.12).

The postpartum sexual quality of life scale

The Scale developed by Rahmani and colleagues in Iran in 2021, consists of a total of 22 items. The scale encompasses 5 subscales: sexual beliefs, sexual performance, marital life in the context of parenthood, postpartum psychological changes, and postpartum sexual changes. The items are rated on a 5-point Likert scale ranging from “completely agree” to “completely disagree.” The Cronbach’s alpha values for the subscales range from 0.79 to 0.91, with an overall Cronbach’s alpha of 0.87, indicating good internal consistency [11].

The Sexual Beliefs subscale evaluates the first 3 items, questioning an individual’s societal and cultural beliefs about their sexual life. The Sexual Performance subscale (items 4 to 9) assesses equality in sexual performance between partners from women’s perspectives, communication about sexuality, and enjoyment. Marital life in the context of parenthood (items 10 to 13) addresses women’s postpartum experiences and the current roles of fathers in parenthood contexts such as childcare and household chores. The Postpartum Psychological Changes subscale (items 14 to 18) explores feelings of depression, vulnerability, insomnia, fatigue, lack of energy, and sexual insecurity during the postpartum period. The Postpartum Sexual Changes subscale (items 19 to 22) focuses on the frequency, duration, and dyspareunia (painful intercourse) aspects of sexual relations during the postpartum period. Questions in the last two subscales are reverse-scored. Higher scores on the scale indicate lower postpartum sexual quality of life [11].

Data collection

The data for the study were collected between March 2022 and March 2023 through face-to-face interviews. The average response time for completing the data forms was 15 min.

Data analysis

Statistical analysis of the data was performed using IBM SPSS V23 and IBM AMOS V24. Descriptive statistics such as percentages, frequencies, minimum-maximum values, mean, and standard deviation were utilized to examine socio-demographic data. The construct validity of the scale was examined through confirmatory factor analysis, utilizing Bootstrap ML (Maximum Likelihood) due to the non-fulfillment of the assumption of multivariate normality. The Bootstrap method was employed with 5000 resamples. The internal consistency of the scale was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha coefficient, with a significance level set at p < 0.05.

Implementation of the study

Stage 1 - Language validity

In scale validity and reliability studies, it is essential that there is consistency between the original scale and its translation, and that the items are equivalent [13]. Therefore, the original scale items were first translated into Turkish by four faculty members working in the field of nursing who are proficient in English. After reaching a consensus on the translations obtained, the Turkish version of the scale was prepared. A language expert then back-translated the scale items from Turkish into English. The back-translation was compared to the original PSQLS to evaluate the similarity, and the scale was finalized, completing the language equivalence process.

Stage 2 - Content validity

In the second stage, the content validity of the PSQLS was conducted. The opinions of 10 experts who are assistant professors, associate professors and professors in the fields of obstetrics and gynaecology, women’s health nursing and midwifery were obtained. Each expert evaluated the items in terms of cultural appropriateness, clarity, and relevance to the postpartum context. Experts were asked to rate each scale item on a scale of 1 to 4 (1- not appropriate, 2- somewhat appropriate, needs modification, 3- quite appropriate, but minor changes needed, 4- very appropriate) and to provide any suggestions for modifications they deemed necessary. The content validity index (CVI) for each item was calculated. If the CVI was above 0.80, the content validity of the items in the scale was considered adequate [13, 14]. While the items were evaluated by experts for content validity, they were also evaluated for cultural appropriateness. Based on the responses and additional suggestions from the experts, the final Turkish version of the scale was prepared.

To determine the comprehensibility of the Turkish scale items developed after translations and content validity, a pilot test was conducted with 10 women who met the acceptance criteria. Since no issues were reported during the pilot test, the scale items were considered comprehensible, culturally appropriate, and relevant to the postpartum period. Therefore, the prepared form was used in the main study without modification.

Stage 3 - Construct validity

The construct validity of the scale was examined using Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) techniques.

Stage 4 - Reliability study

To test the reliability of the scale, the Cronbach alpha reliability coefficient was calculated, and item-total correlation analyses were conducted to determine the internal validity of the items.

Results and discussion

Socio-demographic characteristics of women

The mean age of the women was determined to be 27.1 ± 4.2 years, and the duration of marriage was 3.8 ± 2.6 years. It was found that 53.2% of the women had a university-level education or higher, 45.4% were employed in income-generating jobs, 89.4% lived in a nuclear family, and 71.1% had married through acquaintance. The postpartum period was 4.0 ± 1.3 months, and 95.5% of the women were breastfeeding their babies. The number of daytime breastfeeding sessions was 6.8 ± 3.0, and the number of nighttime breastfeeding sessions was 3.6 ± 1.9. In our study, 50% of the women reported that their sexual life was negatively affected after childbirth. Similar studies supporting our findings exist. Rezaei et al. (2018) reported that 76% of women, Alp Yilmaz et al. (2018) reported that 74.3% of women, and Guendler et al. (2019) reported that 55% of women experienced sexual dysfunction during this period. Selimoğlu and Beydağ (2020) found that the sexual life quality of women in the postpartum period was at a moderate level [6, 7, 15, 16].

Before starting the confirmatory factor analysis, all data-related issues (outliers, skewness, kurtosis, missing data, etc.) were addressed. The data must conform to normal distribution for maximum likelihood estimation to be used. The Multivariate Normality test revealed a critical value of 43.545. While a value below 10 is considered excellent, values up to 20 are generally not problematic according to existing studies. Since the assumption of multivariate normality was not met, Bootstrap ML (Maximum Likelihood) was used as the calculation method (Table 1). In the bootstrap analysis, 5000 resamples were used as recommended in the literature [17] in order to ensure that the confidence intervals of the parameters in the confirmatory factor analysis are obtained more reliably.

Reliability analysis findings

The Cronbach’s alpha coefficient for the Sexual Beliefs dimension was obtained as 0.745, indicating that the dimension is quite reliable. The Cronbach’s alpha coefficient for the Sexual Performance dimension was 0.803, showing high reliability for this dimension. In the context of parenting, the Cronbach’s alpha coefficient for the Marital Life dimension was 0.851, suggesting high reliability. The Cronbach’s alpha for the dimension of Postpartum Psychological Changes was 0.829, also indicating high reliability. The Cronbach’s alpha for the Postpartum Sexual Changes dimension was 0.806, reflecting high reliability. The overall Cronbach’s alpha value for the scale was 0.849, indicating high reliability of the scale (Table 2). The closer the Cronbach’s alpha value is to 1, the higher the level of reliability [18]. It is stated that for a consistent measurement tool, the Cronbach’s Alpha coefficient should be ≥ 0.70 [19]. In this study, the scale’s Cronbach’s alpha coefficient was determined to be 0.849, which can be stated as highly reliable.

Conclusion and Recommendations

The analyses conducted have determined that the Turkish version of the Postpartum Sexual Life Quality Scale consists of 22 items and five sub-factors, has a high Cronbach’s alpha coefficient, and is adaptable to Turkish culture. The study has established that the Turkish version of the Postpartum Sexual Life Quality Scale is a valid and reliable measurement tool that can be used by health professionals to assess sexual life quality in the postpartum period. In clinical practice, this scale can guide healthcare providers in identifying postpartum sexual health concerns early and tailoring interventions accordingly. The PSLQS can serve as a screening and monitoring tool in postpartum care settings, contributing to holistic maternal health services.

Study limitations

The limitations of the study include it being conducted in a single city and hospital with primiparous women who had no pregnancy or birth complications and were within the first six months postpartum. The sample excluded multiparous women and those with a history of psychological or sexual health problems to control confounding variables during initial scale validation. This limits generalizability; future studies should aim to include more diverse postpartum populations to assess the scale’s broader applicability.

Another limitation of this study was the lack of a test-retest reliability assessment, as the cross-sectional design and the physical and emotional sensitivity of the postnatal period made it impossible to administer the scale at a second time point.

Ethical considerations

During the planning phase of the study, written permission was obtained via email from Rahmani and colleagues for both the original form of the scale and its validity-reliability study. Ethical approval was obtained from the Yozgat Bozok University Social Sciences and Humanities Ethics Committee (30/05-16.02.2022), and permission was also secured from the institution where the data were collected. Informed consent to participate was obtained from all participants prior to their inclusion in the study.

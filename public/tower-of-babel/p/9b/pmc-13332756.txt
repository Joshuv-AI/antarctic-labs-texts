Validity of an Alternaria alternata Skin Prick Test Extract for Diagnosing Sensitization
Authors: Xin Li, Jianqing Gu, Ruiqi Wang, Lianglu Wang, Hong Li, Jinlyu Sun, Yuxiang Zhi, Kai Guan, Liping Wen, Zixi Wang, Lisha Li, Rui Tang, Le Cui, Yingyang Xu, Tao Xu, Jia Yin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAlternaria alternata is a major fungal allergen, yet standardized skin prick test (SPT) extracts are lacking in China, hindering the use of this simple, first-line diagnostic tool.ObjectiveThis study aimed to clinically evaluate a novel, domestically developed A. alternata extract for SPT and establish a diagnostic cutoff in a Chinese population.Patients and MethodsIn this prospective, single-center study, 910 patients with allergic diseases were consecutively enrolled. All underwent SPT with the in-house A. alternata extract (Alt a 1: 25.7–34.8 µg/mL). Serum-specific IgE (sIgE) (ImmunoCAP™) served as the reference standard. Diagnostic accuracy was analyzed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. Safety was assessed by monitoring adverse events.ResultsThe per-protocol set included 610 subjects. The area under the ROC curve was 0.868 (95% CI: 0.839–0.897). The optimal diagnostic cutoff was a mean wheal diameter of 3.25 mm, yielding a sensitivity of 81.10% (95% CI: 76.76%–85.24%) and specificity of 79.32% (95% CI: 74.46%–84.18%). To achieve 95% specificity, the cutoff increased to 4.25 mm. No adverse events were reported among all 910 participants.ConclusionThe novel A. alternata SPT extract demonstrates high diagnostic accuracy for detecting Alternaria alternata sensitization and an excellent safety profile. The established cutoff of 3.25 mm provides a population-optimized threshold for identifying sensitized individuals in China. This study addresses a critical gap in allergen standardization and supports the use of SPT as a reliable first-line tool for assessing fungal sensitization in clinical practice.Trial RegistrationChiCTR ChiCTR1800014899. Introduction

Alternaria alternata represents one of the most ubiquitous and clinically relevant airborne fungal allergens, commonly encountered in decomposing vegetation, soil, and ambient air.1 Sporulation exhibits distinct seasonal periodicity, with peak levels occurring during warm and humid summer and autumn months;2 however, indoor environments such as air-conditioning systems and damp building materials can sustain perennial exposure.3–6 Under specific environmental conditions—notably during thunderstorms or agricultural activities—spore concentrations may escalate to several tens of thousands per cubic meter. Fragmented Alternaria spores have been implicated as a likely trigger of “thunderstorm asthma” epidemics.7 These findings align with reports on the high prevalence of mold-related allergic diseases in China.8,9

The estimated prevalence of fungal sensitization in the general population ranges from 3% to 10%, whereas rates among atopic individuals and asthma patients rise substantially, reaching up to 44% and 80%, respectively, for sensitization to at least one fungal species.10–16 Data from the U.S. National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) corroborate these figures, reporting a 12.9% sensitization rate to A. alternata in the general U.S. population.6 Further evidence from a multicenter study conducted by the Global Allergy and Asthma European Network (GA2LEN), encompassing over 3000 allergy patients across 14 European countries, demonstrated a mean sensitization rate of 8.9% to A. alternata, with marked geographical heterogeneity—ranging from 0.8% in London, UK, to 23% in Athens, Greece.14 Similarly, a Polish study involving nearly 500 allergic adults identified Alternaria as the most frequent causative allergen, accounting for 47.1% of symptomatic cases.17

Evidence from China aligns with these international patterns. In Chongqing, the overall sensitization rate to common airborne fungi was 9.80% (377/3845), of which Alternaria accounted for 22.81% of all fungal-related sensitization cases.18 Regional studies conducted in Zhenjiang, Ezhou, Binzhou, Zibo, and Shiyang consistently identified Alternaria as a predominant fungal allergen strongly associated with allergic rhinitis and asthma,19,20 findings that are concordant with reports from the United States.21 Moreover, multiple studies highlight a notable upward trend in fungal sensitization rates globally.14–16,22,23

Skin testing—comprising percutaneous (prick/puncture) and intracutaneous (intradermal) methods24—remains a cornerstone in the diagnostic work-up of IgE-mediated conditions such as allergic rhinitis, asthma, and anaphylaxis, used alongside detailed clinical history and physical examination. The skin prick test (SPT) is particularly favoured for its simplicity, reproducibility, favourable safety profile, and cost-effectiveness, and is recommended by the European Academy of Allergy and Clinical Immunology (EAACI) as a first-line diagnostic tool for allergic diseases. Despite these advantages, the clinical application of SPT for mould allergens in China remains limited, largely owing to the scarcity of standardized allergen extracts and a paucity of large-scale clinical validation studies.25

Several commercial SPT reagents for Alternaria alternata are available internationally, but most are produced from non-local allergen sources and require importation into China, leading to high costs, supply chain instability, and potential batch-to-batch variability. Domestically developed extracts offer the potential for better cost-effectiveness and improved accessibility. The extract evaluated in the present study is produced using standardized local A. alternata strains, with Alt a 1 content quantified by ELISA to ensure batch-to-batch consistency. Compared with imported products, this domestically developed extract has lower production and logistics costs, improved cold-chain stability within domestic distribution networks, and is therefore more feasible for routine use across China.

It is important to distinguish between sensitization (defined as a positive SPT or detectable serum specific IgE, indicating immune reactivity) and clinical allergy (confirmed by provocation testing or consistent symptoms upon exposure). The present study focuses on assessing sensitization to Alternaria alternata, as this is the first step in validating a novel SPT extract for future clinical use. Provocation testing (eg., nasal or bronchial challenge) represents the gold standard for diagnosing clinical allergy; however, it is invasive, time‑consuming, and not feasible in large‑scale diagnostic validation studies. Therefore, serum specific IgE (sIgE) is widely accepted as a reliable reference standard for sensitization in initial extract validation, as recommended by the EAACI guidelines.

Accordingly, the present study aimed to evaluate the diagnostic performance and safety of an A. alternata extract for SPT in clinical practice, and to establish a clinically relevant diagnostic cutoff value.

Material and Methods

Study Design and Participants

This prospective, single-center, cross-sectional study consecutively enrolled patients with physician-diagnosed allergic diseases (allergic rhinitis, asthma, atopic dermatitis, or allergic conjunctivitis) from the Allergy Department of Peking Union Medical College Hospital (PUMCH) between August 10, 2015, and August 30, 2017.26 The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of PUMCH (Approval No. JS-858). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants or their legal guardians.

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Eligible subjects were aged 4–70 years and had a documented history of A. alternata intradermal testing (IDT) or specific IgE detection. Exclusion criteria comprised: (1) failure to discontinue antihistamines for ≥5 half-lives before testing; (2) use of systemic corticosteroids; (3) positive dermatographism; (4) local skin pathology (infection, inflammation, trauma or scarring) at test sites; (5) psoriasis; (6) acute exacerbation of allergic disease; (7) severe systemic illness; (8) current β-blocker therapy; (9) pregnancy; or (10) any other condition deemed by investigators to potentially affect trial safety or data integrity.

Study Procedures and Assessments

Allergen Extract and Skin Prick Test

The A. alternata SPT extract was prepared in-house, comprising water-soluble active protein of A. alternata allergen in a phosphate-buffered solution containing 50% glycerol (1 mL/bottle). The major allergen Alt a 1 content was quantified using an ELISA kit based on mAb to Alt a 1 (Indoor Biotechnologies, Charlottesville, VA, USA)). Total protein content was measured using the Bradford assay (Solarbio, Beijing, China) with bovine serum albumin as the standard.

All enrolled participants underwent standardized SPT on the volar forearm (2–3 cm distal to the wrist and antecubital fossa) following European guidelines.27 Disposable prick lancets (Huaian Lanxing Plastic Instrument Co., Ltd)., validated in earlier studies,28 were used. A negative control (allergen-free buffer-glycerol) and a positive control (histamine dihydrochloride) were included in each test series. After skin disinfection, allergen extracts and controls were applied at 2 cm intervals and pricked. Wheal reactions were measured after 15 min. The mean wheal diameter (MWD) was calculated as (D + d)/2, where D and d represent the longest and perpendicular diameters. A. alternata sIgE levels, adverse events, and participant compliance were documented concurrently. All assessments were performed by evaluators blinded to clinical information and sIgE results.

sIgE Measurement

Serum sIgE levels were analyzed using the ImmunoCAP™ system on Phadia 1000 Immunoassay Analyzer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA). A level ≥0.35 kUA/L was considered positive.

Adverse Event Monitoring

Participants were monitored for local (pruritus, erythema, persistent swelling, induration) and systemic adverse events through direct observation at 15 minutes and 1 hour post-SPT, followed by telephone interviews at 6, 12, and 24 hours. Any medication use was documented. The study process, followed previous study, is presented in Table 1.28

Statistical Analysis

Diagnostic performance was evaluated using the Youden index (sensitivity + specificity − 1). For a conventional cut-off of ≥3 mm, subjects were classified as follows: True-positive (tp): sIgE ≥ 0.35 kU/L and positive SPT; True-negative (tn): sIgE < 0.35 kU/L and negative SPT; False-positive (fp): sIgE < 0.35 kU/L but positive SPT; False-negative (fn): sIgE ≥ 0.35 kU/L but negative SPT. Sensitivity [tp/(tp + fn)], specificity [tn/(tn + fp)], positive predictive value [PPV = tp/(tp + fp)], negative predictive value [NPV = tn/(tn + fn)] and overall test efficiency [(tp + tn)/(tp + fp + tn + fn)] were calculated.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed with serum Alternaria alternata sIgE as the reference standard to determine sensitivity and specificity across varying SPT cut-off values. All analyses were conducted using SPSS® software (version 26.0).

Results

Study Population

A total of 910 subjects were enrolled and completed the study without any dropouts. The full analysis set (FAS) comprised 757 participants, while 153 subjects were excluded due to unavailable sIgE results. After further exclusion of 147 subjects who did not meet the predefined data-exclusion criteria—including failure to meet inclusion/exclusion criteria, missing sIgE or SPT data, an MWD <3 mm for the positive control, or an MWD ≥3 mm for the negative control—610 individuals constituted the per-protocol set (PPS). The safety set (SS) included all 910 enrolled subjects.

Baseline characteristics of the FAS and PPS are summarized in Table 2. In the FAS, the mean age was 23.06 ± 14.27 years (range 4–65 years), with 438 males (57.86%) and 319 females (42.14%). In the PPS, the mean age was 22.00 ± 14.14 years (range 4–65 years), consisting of 368 males (60.33%) and 242 females (39.67%). Concomitant allergy and asthma medications are detailed in Table 2.

Safety

No unexpected or serious adverse events were reported among the 910 subjects in the safety set.

Discussion

This investigator-initiated prospective study systematically evaluated a domestically developed Alternaria alternata skin prick test extract in a large Chinese cohort. The findings demonstrated favorable diagnostic accuracy alongside an excellent safety profile among participants. Receiver operating characteristic analysis identified 3.25 mm as the optimal mean wheal diameter cutoff for clinical diagnosis, a notable deviation from the conventional 3 mm threshold.

The diagnostic accuracy observed in this study aligns with global standards for fungal allergen testing. The area under the curve reached 86.79% in the PPS, reflecting strong concordance between SPT results and serum Alternaria sIgE levels. Notably, the sensitivity varied across age groups, declining to 37.50% in individuals aged 45–65 years. This age-related decrease is consistent with previous reports of diminished skin reactivity in older adults,29 highlighting the importance of age-stratified interpretation of SPT results in clinical practice.

The establishment of a 3.25 mm cutoff represents a meaningful adjustment to the widely adopted 3 mm standard. This finding is particularly relevant in the Chinese population, where skin sensitivity patterns may differ from those in Western cohorts. The higher cutoff may reduce false-positive rates while maintaining clinically adequate sensitivity, thereby improving the reliability of SPT in diagnosing Alternaria allergy.

Regarding safety, SPTs can potentially induce local or systemic adverse reactions, including rare instances of anaphylactic shock. However, no local or systemic adverse events were observed among the 910 participants. Compared with literature-reported adverse-reaction rates of 0.02–0.58%,30,31 this preparation showed superior safety performance.

The development of this domestic extract addresses an urgent need for cost-effective, locally produced prick-test reagents in China. Skin testing, first described by Blackley in 1867, has evolved into a reliable and economical technique for diagnosing IgE-mediated allergic diseases.27 Although IDT has been used in China for many years, current guidelines recommend it as a sequential diagnostic step after SPT.32 This recommendation is supported by evidence showing that while IDT offers superior sensitivity, it exhibits significantly lower specificity than SPT. Moreover, its technical complexity and challenges in dose standardization elevate the risk of false-positive reactions.24,33 The present single-center cross-sectional study confirmed that Alternaria allergy predominantly affects children (329/610, 53.93%). As a less painful procedure, SPT demonstrated higher clinical acceptability than IDT. Despite its global adoption for in vivo allergen detection,34,35 SPT remains underutilized in China, largely due to the scarcity of affordable, locally developed allergen extracts.

Significant variations in allergen content have been reported among different A. alternata SPT solutions, depending on fungal subspecies and manufacturing processes, with concentrations ranging from <0.04 μg/mL to 124 μg/mL (Table 5).36–40 The extract used in this study contained a minimum Alt a 1 concentration of 25.7 μg/mL, placing it in the moderate-to-high range relative to existing commercial preparations. Previous research established that 3.7 μg/mL Alt a 1 represents the diagnostic threshold required to elicit cutaneous reactions equivalent to 1% histamine dihydrochloride.41 Compared with internationally available products, the domestic extract demonstrated comparable major allergen content and achieved a sensitivity of 79.32% and specificity of 86.11% in a large cohort (n=910), providing robust evidence for domestic allergen standardization.

Methodological Considerations and Interpretation of Diagnostic Performance

From a methodological perspective, Alternaria sIgE testing served as the reference standard based on two established principles: the Thermo Fisher ImmunoCAP system has shown superior reproducibility and well-documented clinical correlation for serum sIgE quantification,42,43 and multiple studies have confirmed substantial agreement between serum sIgE levels and SPT outcomes,34,44,45 supporting its use as a reliable in vitro diagnostic reference. However, this approach introduces an important conceptual limitation that warrants careful consideration. Serum sIgE fundamentally measures sensitization—the presence of allergen-specific immunoglobulin E—rather than clinical allergy, which requires correlation with symptomatic disease upon natural or controlled allergen exposure. Consequently, the present evaluation primarily reflects concordance between two distinct assays for sensitization detection (in vivo SPT and in vitro sIgE) rather than the diagnostic accuracy of SPT against true clinical allergy as determined by provocation testing or rigorous exposure-symptom correlation. This distinction is clinically relevant: both SPT and sIgE detect IgE-mediated immune responses, but neither alone can confirm clinically significant disease without integration with detailed exposure history and symptomatic response. The observed AUC of 86.79% in the per-protocol set therefore represents the agreement between cutaneous and serological markers of sensitization, potentially overestimating true diagnostic accuracy for clinical allergy if validated against bronchial or nasal allergen challenge. The known discordance between sensitization markers and symptomatic disease, particularly for fungal allergens where cross-reactivity and non-clinical IgE binding are prevalent,10,12,13 further underscores this limitation. Although provocation testing remains the diagnostic paradigm for allergic diseases, limited standardization of protocols in China and safety concerns regarding severe reactions necessitated the use of ImmunoCAP-based sIgE assessment for SPT validation in this initial evaluation.

The study design introduced a selection mechanism that shaped the observed diagnostic performance characteristics. Eligible subjects were required to have documented prior intradermal testing or specific IgE detection for Alternaria alternata, reflecting a pre-screened, referred population enriched for suspected fungal sensitization. This inclusion criterion—while mirroring real-world clinical practice where SPT is frequently employed as a confirmatory or alternative diagnostic modality—creates spectrum bias that inflates apparent test sensitivity. In this enriched cohort, the pre-test probability of sensitization substantially exceeded that expected in unselected, consecutive allergic patients, leading to overrepresentation of truly sensitized individuals. This enrichment phenomenon likely contributed to the high sensitivity observed in younger age groups (80.90% in 4–18 years, 86.96% in 18–45 years) and suggests that application of the proposed 3.25 mm cutoff in unscreened, primary care populations would yield different predictive values. The diagnostic performance metrics reported herein should be interpreted as applicable to secondary evaluation settings where preliminary sensitization testing has already been performed, rather than to primary screening scenarios. Furthermore, the requirement for prior IDT or sIgE testing excluded patients with conditions precluding these assessments—such as dermatographism for IDT or technical limitations for sIgE—potentially creating a healthier, less complex study population than the broader spectrum of allergic patients. To assess the impact of these selection procedures, baseline characteristics of the 910 enrolled subjects were compared with the 153 excluded due to unavailable sIgE results and the 147 excluded from per-protocol analysis due to protocol violations. The excluded population demonstrated similar age distribution (mean 24.8 ± 15.2 years versus 23.1 ± 14.3 years, p = 0.18) but higher male proportion (65.3% versus 57.9%, p = 0.03) and lower rates of physician-diagnosed asthma (18.7% versus 28.4%, p = 0.01), suggesting differential exclusion of milder allergic phenotypes. This pattern reinforces that the per-protocol population represents a more severely affected subgroup, supporting cautious generalization of findings to broader clinical contexts.

The analysis revealed pronounced age-dependent variation in diagnostic performance with important clinical implications. In participants aged 4–18 years, the F1 score reached 87.79% with accuracy of 82.07%, reflecting robust concordance between SPT and sIgE. The 18–45 year group maintained high sensitivity (86.96%) and specificity (80.92%). However, in the 45–65 year group, sensitivity declined markedly to 37.50%, whereas specificity remained high at 84.62%. This asymmetric age effect suggests that skin reactivity to allergen challenge diminishes with advancing age independent of true sensitization status. Multiple age-related physiological changes likely underlie this phenomenon: reduced skin elasticity and thickness altering wheal formation dynamics,29 immunosenescence affecting mast cell responsiveness and mediator release, and higher prevalence of medications that modulate allergic responses such as beta-adrenergic blockers and angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors.46 The stability of specificity across age groups indicates that positive SPT reactions in older adults remain reliable indicators of sensitization, whereas negative reactions may be falsely reassuring due to skin hyporeactivity rather than absence of specific IgE. This age-related performance variation has direct clinical implications: clinicians should maintain a lower threshold for supplemental sIgE testing in patients older than 45 years with negative SPT but compatible clinical history, and consider that the proposed 3.25 mm cutoff may be suboptimal for this demographic.

The interaction between age and selection bias merits particular attention. The pre-screened older population likely represents a survivor cohort with persistent, severe sensitization capable of overcoming age-related skin hyporeactivity, suggesting that true sensitivity decline in unselected elderly populations may be even more pronounced than observed here. Age-stratified cutoff values warrant prospective validation in larger cohorts.

Comprehensive quality-control measures were implemented throughout all SPT procedures. Variables known to influence test accuracy—including skin reactivity,47 age-dependent response variation,29 medication interference,46 needle material properties,48 and application technique49—were controlled. Patients with dermatographic urticaria were excluded via preliminary scratch testing, and simultaneous positive (histamine) and negative (saline) controls were included in each test series. All participants underwent an antihistamine washout period exceeding five half-lives, and standardized chip-prick lancets—the only medically certified devices available domestically—were uniformly employed. Questionable results were systematically excluded using predefined data-filtering criteria to minimize technical variability. Future standardization may benefit from automated application systems that eliminate operator-dependent technique variations.50

The convergence of these methodological considerations—imperfect reference standard representing sensitization rather than clinical allergy, selection-enriched study population, and marked age-dependent performance variation—necessitates cautious interpretation of the proposed 3.25 mm cutoff. This threshold optimizes diagnostic performance in young-to-middle-aged, pre-screened Chinese allergic patients but requires validation across broader demographics and clinical contexts. The accuracy metrics should be viewed as operational characteristics for this specific clinical scenario rather than universal performance standards.

Study Limitations and Future Directions

Several limitations of this study merit emphasis and inform priorities for future research. The single-center design conducted at Peking Union Medical College Hospital in Beijing limits generalizability to diverse geographic and ethnic populations. Beijing’s continental monsoon climate—with hot, humid summers and cold, dry winters—differs substantially from southern China’s subtropical environment or western China’s arid conditions, where Alternaria spore concentrations, seasonal patterns, and allergenicity may vary. Such geographical variability in allergic disease expression is increasingly recognized; Berghi et al demonstrated distinct immunological profiles in allergic rhinitis patients evaluated in Bucharest, underscoring that local environmental and demographic factors shape regional clinical phenotypes and immunological responses31 Accordingly, these regional differences in airborne fungal exposure could influence both the prevalence of sensitization and the optimal diagnostic thresholds for skin testing. Additionally, the predominantly Han Chinese study population may exhibit genetic determinants of atopic predisposition and IgE-mediated responses distinct from other ethnic groups, affecting the transportability of findings to non-Chinese populations.

The absence of a diagnostic gold standard incorporating controlled allergen provocation with clinical correlation restricts definitive assessment of SPT accuracy for clinically relevant allergy. The discordance between sensitization markers and symptomatic disease, particularly pronounced for fungal allergens given their complex allergen composition and cross-reactivity potential, remains unresolved. Specifically, although our extract is enriched for Alt a 1, we did not measure serum specific IgE to other fungi (eg., Cladosporium, Aspergillus, Penicillium) and did not exclude patients with multiple fungal sensitizations. Widespread cross-reactivity among fungal allergens may therefore lead to false-positive SPT results, as IgE antibodies directed against homologous epitopes from different fungal species can produce positive reactions without true Alternaria-specific sensitization. Future validation studies should prioritize bronchial or nasal allergen challenge as the reference standard, or employ rigorous clinical phenotyping with exposure assessment and symptom scoring, to establish true diagnostic accuracy for clinical allergy rather than sensitization detection.

The enriched study population resulting from inclusion criteria requiring prior IDT or sIgE testing fundamentally limits external validity to settings employing different diagnostic pathways. In clinical environments where SPT serves as a first-line test without pre-screening, diagnostic yield and optimal cutoff values may differ substantially. The exclusion of 300 subjects (153 with unavailable sIgE, 147 with protocol violations) from the final analysis—while necessary for internal validity—may have introduced attrition bias if excluded patients differed systematically from those retained. Available baseline data indicate excluded subjects had similar age but higher male proportion and lower asthma prevalence, suggesting differential exclusion of milder phenotypes that could affect generalizability.

The age-related performance variation was derived from post-hoc subgroup analysis with limited statistical power in the 45–65 year stratum (n=48). The wide confidence intervals around sensitivity estimates in this group preclude definitive conclusions regarding optimal cutoff values for older adults. Moreover, objective measures of skin physiological changes (eg., dermal thickness, mast cell density) were not systematically collected, limiting mechanistic interpretation of the observed age effect.

Future research should address these limitations through: (1) multi-center validation across China’s climatic zones to establish geographic transportability and develop region-specific reference standards if necessary; (2) prospective studies employing clinical allergy definitions incorporating provocation testing to determine true diagnostic accuracy against symptomatic disease; (3) consecutive enrollment designs without pre-screening requirements to assess real-world performance in unselected populations; and (4) mechanistic investigations of age-related skin reactivity changes with development of age-specific diagnostic algorithms. The integration of component-resolved diagnostics, specifically Alt a 1 sIgE measurement, may help distinguish clinically relevant sensitization from cross-reactive IgE binding, addressing both reference standard limitations and the specificity challenges inherent to fungal allergy diagnosis. The development of automated SPT application systems that eliminate operator-dependent technique variations may further enhance standardization and reproducibility.

Conclusions

This prospective diagnostic accuracy study establishes that a domestically developed Alternaria alternata skin prick test extract exhibits acceptable diagnostic performance for detecting sensitization in a large Chinese cohort, with an optimal mean wheal diameter cutoff of 3.25 mm—exceeding the conventional 3 mm threshold. The extract achieved moderate concordance with serum specific IgE (AUC 86.79%) and demonstrated an excellent safety profile with no adverse events observed among 910 participants. Notably, the marked age-dependent decline in sensitivity among patients aged 45–65 years underscores the necessity of age-stratified interpretation and heightened clinical vigilance when applying the proposed cutoff in older adults.

These findings should be interpreted within the context of the study’s methodological framework: the evaluation reflects diagnostic performance for sensitization detection in a pre-screened, referred population rather than for clinical allergy in unselected patients. Nevertheless, the standardized 3.25 mm cutoff provides a reliable, cost-effective diagnostic option for Alternaria sensitization assessment in secondary evaluation settings, particularly for children and young adults where performance is optimal. For clinical practice, the use of both SPT (with this validated extract) and sIgE testing may increase diagnostic accuracy, especially in cases of discordant results; however, the added value of the combination should be evaluated in future prospective studies. Future multi-center validation across diverse geographic regions, coupled with reference standard studies employing provocation testing, will be essential to further evaluate the transportability of these findings and to establish age-specific diagnostic algorithms for broader clinical implementation.

Non-tuberculous Mycobacterial pulmonary disease due to novel mycobacterium: Mycobacterium habikinoensis: a case report
Authors: Kyohei Maeda, Yoshitaka Tamura, Yuta Nagai, Yuki Kariya, Hiroki Katsuta, Naoko Ajiro, Hiroko Yoshida, Yuki Han, Shoji Hashimoto, Kinuyo Chikamatsu, Akiko Takaki, Yuki Matsumoto, Rizky Mutiara Fatimah, Michio Tanaka, Shota Nakamura, Tetsuya Iida, Satoshi Mitarai, Takayuki Nagai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe incidence of non-tuberculous mycobacterial pulmonary disease (NTM-PD) is increasing, and the identification of new Mycobacterium species is also increasing. We describe the first case of chronic pulmonary infection caused by a novel Mycobacterium species.Case presentationA 66-year-old man was referred to our hospital with a several-week history of low-grade fever and productive cough. An acid-fast bacillus smear test of the sputum revealed positive. Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) for Mycobacterium intracellulare was positive; however, Matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry (MALDI-TOF MS) failed to identify the strain. The isolated culture was referred for further genotypic identification and was confirmed to be a novel species. Combination therapy with clarithromycin, rifampicin, and sitafloxacin was started. Despite treatment, the patient’s condition gradually worsened during the subsequent follow-up period.ConclusionsWe describe the first case of NTM-PD with a novel Mycobacterium species. Mycobacterium habikinoensis is a novel species closely related to Mycobacterium colombiense but with distinct characteristics. Further investigation is required to clarify its pathogenicity, antimicrobial susceptibility, and clinical significance in human infections.Supplementary informationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-026-12857-y.

Background

The genus Mycobacterium consists of over 190 species and subspecies [1]. They exist in the environment and some species infect humans such as Mycobacterium avium and Mycobacterium intracellulare. In humans, Mycobacterium species can infect various organs, but the most common manifestation is non-tuberculous mycobacterial pulmonary disease (NTM-PD). The incidence of NTM-PD is increasing, partly due to improved diagnostic methods and increased awareness [2]. The disease is geographically heterogeneous, with some species being globally common and others regionally important [3]. Rare NTM species, identifiable only through advanced techniques, have been reported globally. Currently, molecular methods based on DNA sequences of several chronometers have been used to analyze Mycobacterium strains [4–6], and to identify previously unknown NTM species [7, 8]. Classifying Mycobacterium isolates into distinct species is important, because specific species exhibit varying degrees of virulence and influence the risk of infection and prognosis [9, 10]. In this study, we report on a novel species of slowly growing mycobacteria. The type strain is M23085T (RIMD 4,002,101T = CIP 112,529T), which was isolated from the sputum of an elderly man.

Case presentation

A 66-year-old immunocompetent man was referred to our hospital with a several-week history of low-grade fever and productive cough. He had never smoked tobacco. His past medical history included pulmonary tuberculosis treated with antituberculosis agents 29 years earlier. Computed tomography of the chest revealed a cavity in the right upper lobe and multiple centrilobular nodules in the left lower lobe (Fig. 1). A sputum smear test for acid-fast bacilli revealed positive, and polymerase chain reaction (PCR) testing for M. intracellulare was positive. We applied the TaqMan PCR (Roche Diagnostic) system. A diagnosis of NTM-PD due to M. intracellulare was made. An 18-month course of clarithromycin, rifampicin, and ethambutol was administered. Although sputum culture tests remained positive, his symptom resolved and the sputum smear test turned negative. 12 months after completing the antibiotic course, low-grade fever and productive cough recurred, and a sputum smear test became positive. PCR for M. intracellulare was positive again. Combination therapy with clarithromycin, rifampicin, and ethambutol was started again. However, at a 2-year follow up visit, his symptom persisted, and sputum smear tests remained positive. We performed a whole-genome analysis of strain M23085T and obtained the complete genome sequence (CP174280–CP174282). The DNA G+ C content of the type strain was 67.08%. The genome-to-genome distances (GGDs) were assessed using average nucleotide identity (ANI) (Table 1). Strain M23085T showed the highest ANI with Mycobacterium senriense (90.26%), followed by Mycobacterium vulneris (90.25%) and Mycobacterium colombiense (89.52%). The ANI value below 95% indicates that strain M23085T, deposited as RIMD 4,002,101T (=CIP 112,529T), represents a novel species [11]. Phylogenetic analysis using 16S rRNA gene sequences was performed by calculating precise distances using the genome BLAST distance phylogeny (GBDP) approach (Fig. 2). The closest species to strain M23085T were Mycobacterium intracellulare subsp. yongonense (99.86%), followed by Mycobacterium paraintracellulare (99.80%). These findings mean that strain M23085T differs by 4 single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) from M. intracellulare subsp. yongonense and by 5 SNPs from M. paraintracellulare in the 16S rRNA. This suggested that strain M23085T is phylogenetically similar to members of the MAC and closely related species. Phylogenetic analysis using a whole-genome analysis was also performed (Supplementary Fig. 1). We propose that Mycobacterium habikinoensis sp. nov. is a novel species of slowly growing mycobacteria, with M23085T as the type strain. The species name “habikinoensis” refers to Habikino City in Japan, where the strain was isolated.

Cells are Gram-positive, acid-fast, aerobic non-motile rods. Growth at temperature around 35 °C is examined during six weeks of incubation on Ogawa Kudoh agar (Supplementary Fig. 2). On Middlebrook 7H11 agar (BD Difco BBL) with oleic acid-ADC enrichment (BD Difco BBL), mature growth develops after 5 days incubation at 22, 32 and 35 °C. No growth occurs at 10 and 42 °C. Colonies are middle size (2.5 mm), rough, yellow-pigmented and scotochromogenic(Supplementary Fig. 3). Acid-alcohol fastness is determined by Ziehl-Neelsen staining. Strain M23085T is negative for urease, nitrate reduction and Tween 80 hydrolysis, and positive for catalase activity. In API Coryne tests, strain M23085T is negative for pyrazinamidase, α-glucosidase and β-glucosidase, and positive for alkaline phosphatase.

The treatment of NTM-PD caused by rare species is challenging. Initial therapy often involves drug regimens used for closely related species, with subsequent modifications based on in vitro drug susceptibility testing. BrothMIC SGM (Kyokuto Pharmaceutical Industrial) was applied for drug susceptibility testing. The results were as follows (Table 2): clarithromycin, resistant, >64 μg/ml; moxifloxacin, intermediate, 2 μg/ml; amikacin, susceptible, ≤8 μg/ml; minocycline, resistant, 8 μg/ml; doxycycline, resistant, 8 μg/ml; linezolid, susceptible, 4 μg/ml; rifampicin, susceptible, 0.25 μg/ml; rifabutin, susceptible, ≤0.25 μg/ml [15]. Based on antimicrobial susceptibility results and clinical experience, we replaced the antibiotic regimen with oral clarithromycin, rifampicin, and sitafloxacin. In cases of MAC, clarithromycin may still be effective against resistant strains [16]. Discussion and conclusion

In this study, we report on a novel species of slowly growing mycobacteria isolated from the sputum of an elderly man. The incidence of NTM-PD is increasing globally, partly due to improved diagnostic methods and increased awareness. The genus Mycobacterium consists of over 190 species and subspecies that are potentially pathogenic [1]. While MAC and Mycobacterium abscessus are common, rarer species such like Mycobacterium kansasii, Mycobacterium shinjukuense, and Mycobacterium triplex can also cause NTM-PD in Japan [17]. Currently, molecular methods based on DNA sequences of several chronometers, such as 16S rRNA gene, rpoB [6] and hsp65 [5], are used to analyze Mycobacterium strains, and to identify previously unknown NTM species. Disease progression and mortality of NTM-PD vary even among MAC subspecies [9, 10]. NTM species may be under-recognized. The discovery of new NTM species contributes to our understanding of their environmental reservoirs, virulence factors, and interactions with hosts, which is essential for public health initiatives and infection control strategies.

This novel Mycobacterium species has several characteristics. First, M. habikinoensis is phylogenetically similar to members of the MAC. The MAC has expanded beyond its original two species, M. avium and M. intracellulare, to include newly identified species. Recent studies have revealed that MAC now comprises more than 12 validly published species [4], including such as M. colombiense, M. senriense, and M. vulneris. In this case, M. habikinoensis showed high 16S rRNA gene similarities to M. intracellulare subsp. yongonense and M. paraintracellulare. This result indicates that M. habikinoensis is similar to members of the MAC and closely related species. Actually, the clinical presentation of M. habikinoensis closely resembles that of other MAC members.

Second, M. habikinoensis gave a false positive result for M. intracellulare in PCR testing. We applied the TaqMan PCR (Roche Diagnostic) system for species detection. Primers and other sequence data for the TaqMan PCR are not publicly accessible. The nucleotide sequence of the 16S rRNA gene in M. habikinoensis exhibits high homology to that of M. intracellulare subsp. yongonense(99.86% homology with 05–1390). This high similarity likely contributed to cause the false positive results observed with TaqMan PCR. It is hypothesized that there are minimal differences in the sequences targeted by the primers currently in use. In this case, we cannot definitively determine whether the patient was initially infected with M. intracellulare, as suggested by PCR, or with M. habikinoensis from the outset. Co-infection involving both species may also be possible. The PCR assay for M. intracellulare remained positive when performed on this strain, which was ultimately confirmed as M. habikinoensis by MALDI-TOF MS and whole-genome sequencing. Unfortunately, sputum samples from the time of the initial presentation were not preserved, which precluded retrospective testing by MALDI-TOF MS or next-generation sequencing. For accurate identification, the utilization of MALDI-TOF MS or next-generation sequencing is warranted.

Third, M. habikinoensis showed a distinct drug susceptibility profile compared to other members of the MAC. Distributions of minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) show differences among members of the MAC [18]. High-level resistance to macrolides is more common in M. intracellulare than in other species. A similar trend is observed in this novel species. Additionally, M. habikinoensis displays unique susceptibilities to other antimicrobials. For example, M. senriense, which had the highest ANI value relative to M. habikinoensis, is susceptible to clarithromycin and ethambutol [7]. In contrast, M. habikinoensis is resistant to clarithromycin and exhibits a high MIC value for ethambutol. These findings suggest that establishing species-specific breakpoints is essential to avoid ineffective therapy and potential drug-related toxicities. More investigations are needed to fully elucidate this rare species.

Here, we describe the first case of NTM-PD with an unidentified Mycobacterium, Mycobacterium habikinoensis. With the implementation of newer identification methods like MALDI-TOF MS and next-generation sequencing, there may be an increase in reported cases of rare NTM species.

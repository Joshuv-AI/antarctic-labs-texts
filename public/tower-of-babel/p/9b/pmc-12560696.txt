Comparative Analysis of Disposable Cranial Perforators in Trepanation
Authors: Kazufumi Ohmura, Noriyuki Nakayama, Tsuyoshi Izumo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES:Cranial trepanation is a fundamental neurosurgical procedure that has evolved significantly with the development of disposable cranial perforators designed to enhance safety and efficiency. The aim of this study was to evaluate and compare the safety, efficiency, and cost-effectiveness of disposable cranial perforators used in neurosurgical procedures.METHODS:A retrospective analysis was conducted on 129 trepanations performed by a single surgeon between May and December 2024, using 3 disposable cranial perforators: Codman (Integra LifeSciences Production Corporation), MERIDIAN (adeor medical AG), and ACRA-CUT (ACRA-CUT Inc.). Clinical parameters assessed included trepanation success rates, operative times, and complication rates. In addition, a cadaveric study examined the impact of varying drilling angles on dural integrity. Structural distinctions among perforators were investigated using digital microscopy.RESULTS:ACRA-CUT was approximately twice as expensive as Codman and MERIDIAN. Clinically, Codman demonstrated the lowest success rate (69%) compared with MERIDIAN (97.7%) and ACRA-CUT (100%). ACRA-CUT also achieved the shortest median trepanation time (11.8 seconds) compared with Codman (74.6 seconds) and MERIDIAN (26.4 seconds). However, the cadaveric analysis revealed a higher incidence of dural injury with ACRA-CUT at oblique angles. Structural analysis highlighted ACRA-CUT's distinctive acute tip and tri-curved blade design, correlating with its superior performance but an increased risk profile.CONCLUSION:ACRA-CUT offers unparalleled efficiency and speed, but its high cost and greater propensity for dural injury at nonperpendicular angles necessitate cautious application. MERIDIAN emerges as a safer choice for routine procedures, whereas Codman, despite being cost-effective, is hindered by its lower success rate and potential for prolonged operative times. Further randomized studies are warranted to validate these findings and optimize perforator selection.

Cranial access is a fundamental requirement in many neurosurgical procedures performed worldwide. Trepanation, the process of perforating the skull, dates to the Mesolithic period when primitive tools such as stones were used.1 Over time, trepanation instruments have undergone significant evolution, culminating in the widely adopted design patented by Robert J. Hudson in 1877. The Hudson brace, a twist drill, remained widely used by neurosurgeons until the late 20th century. In the early 20th century, Codman & Shurtleff Inc. introduced the automatic control drill, which has since become indispensable in modern neurosurgery.2,3

The disposable cranial perforator, designed to attach to an automatic control drill, improves safety and efficiency. Its automatic release mechanism ensures the drill operates only under applied pressure and ceases rotation on release, minimizing the risk of dura mater or brain parenchyma damage.3

Despite these safety features, overperforation—commonly referred to as plunging—remains a documented complication.4,5 A survey conducted in the United Kingdom and Ireland revealed that approximately two-thirds of neurosurgeons experienced at least 1 plunging incident during their careers. Such events can lead to severe complications, including intracranial hemorrhage and contusion, with a reported 12% risk of death or permanent neurological morbidity.6,7

In Japan, rising medical costs pose a significant social problem despite the universal health insurance system that reduces patient expenses through taxpayer-funded subsidies.8,9 The escalating cost of disposable cranial perforators cannot be overlooked, creating a demand for products that balance safety, efficiency, and cost-effectiveness.

Although various disposable cranial perforators are available in Japan, no comparative analyses have been conducted to date. The aim of this study was to address this gap by evaluating the safety, efficiency, and cost-effectiveness of perforators in our hospital. Identifying the most suitable device could shorten operative times, minimize complications, and reduce healthcare costs.

METHODS

Clinical Study

Surgical Procedures and Patient Selection

This study retrospectively analyzed all trepanation procedures performed by the first author, a board-certified neurosurgeon with over 10 years of experience, between May and December 2024. The Midas Rex MR8 drill (Medtronic) was used for posterior cranial fossa surgeries, whereas the ELAN4 (Aesculap, Inc) was used for all other cases. Disposable cranial perforators included Codman (Integra LifeSciences Production Corporation) until August, followed by ACRA-CUT (ACRA-CUT Inc.) for mainly emergency surgeries and MERIDIAN (adeor medical AG) for scheduled operations. Throughout all trepanations, saline irrigation was continuously applied to regulate temperature and prevent heat-induced tissue damage. This study follows the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology checklist to ensure adherence to best practices in observational research methodology and reporting.

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Gifu University Graduate School of Medicine. All patients provided written informed consent. Ethics Committee approval number: 2024-220, cadaver Ethics Committee approval number: CST-2024.

Outcome Measures

The analysis evaluated trepanation success, dural or brain damage incidence, time required for perforation, and perforation site using surgical records and images. Successful trepanation was defined as achieving access to the endocranial plate with a single perforator, whereas failure included instances requiring an alternative perforator or high-speed drill. Patient demographics (age, sex, medical history, diagnosis) were retrieved from medical records. Skull thickness and computed tomography (CT) values at the trepanation site were manually measured from fused preoperative and postoperative CT scans using ShadeQuest/ViewR-DG (FUJIFILM Medical Solutions Corporation). A 5-mm diameter region of interest was used for CT value analysis.

Cadaveric Study

Experimental Setup

A cadaveric study was performed using the skull of a 93-year-old Japanese male donor from the Gifu University School of Medicine. A cadaveric skull was perforated at predefined angles (90°, 75°, 60°, and 45°) using each perforator, with 3 perforations performed per angle. The skulls were secured in a supine midline position using DORO (pro med instruments GmbH). All perforations were executed with the CORE2 drill (Stryker Corporation).

Evaluations

The presence or absence of dural damage at each angle was assessed. Perforators were considered unsuccessful if trepanation did not commence (slipping) or was interrupted. Procedures exceeding 3 minutes were discontinued.

Microstructural Analysis

Each perforator was examined using a Leica DVM5000 digital microscope (Leica Microsystems GmbH), with additional illumination from a GZ-310 light source (GENTOS). Observations were performed from planar perspectives (top-down and side views) to identify structural differences.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables were expressed as medians with ranges. Comparisons of patient characteristics for each perforator were performed using the Wilcoxon test for continuous variables and the Pearson χ2 test for categorical variables. Trepanation success rates were analyzed using the Fisher exact test, whereas median perforation times and CT values were compared using the Wilcoxon test. Skull thickness correlations were assessed with Spearman rank correlation coefficient. Multiple comparisons were adjusted using Bonferroni corrections. χ2 tests were used to compare dural injury rates in the cadaveric study.

Statistical analyses were conducted with JMP 14.2 software (SAS Institute Inc.), and significance was set at P < .05.

Trepanation success rates, depicted in Figure 2B, demonstrated a significant disparity: Codman achieved a success rate of 69% (95% CI, 55.4-80.1; 40/58), significantly lower than that of MERIDIAN (97.7%; 95% CI, 87.7-99.9; 42/43) and ACRA-CUT (100%; 95% CI, 87.7-100; 28/28) (Codman vs MERIDIAN, P < .001; Codman vs ACRA-CUT, P < .001). MERIDIAN and ACRA-CUT did not differ significantly in success rate (P = 1.00). Across all cases, no instances of dural or brain injury were observed. The median time required for trepanation, depicted in Figure 2C, also varied significantly. The median trepanation time for Codman (74.6 seconds; range, 9.7-177.0; 95% CI, 67.3-98.3) was significantly longer than that of MERIDIAN (26.4 seconds; range, 10.2-154.3; 95% CI, 27.5-50.7) and ACRA-CUT (11.8 seconds; range, 8.5-23.2; 95% CI, 11.5-14.3) (P < .001). In addition, MERIDIAN required significantly less time than Codman (P < .001).

The correlation between trepanation time and bone thickness is shown in Figure 2D. All perforators exhibited significant, weak positive correlations: Codman (ρ = 0.40; 95% CI, 0.10-0.63; P = .01), MERIDIAN (ρ = 0.46; 95% CI, 0.18-0.67; P = .01), and ACRA-CUT (ρ = 0.47; 95% CI, 0.12-0.72; P = .01). As illustrated in Figure 2E, CT values did not differ significantly between successful (median, 850.2; range, 393.4-1309.0; 95% CI, 817.7-902.5) and unsuccessful (median, 745.3; range, 519.0-1141.1; 95% CI, 717.1-898.7) perforations (P = .33).

Structural Differences Among Perforators Revealed by Microstructural Analysis

Microscopic analysis revealed structural differences among the perforators (Figure 4A). From the side view, Codman and MERIDIAN exhibited blunt tips, whereas ACRA-CUT featured a sharp tip. Similarly, the blade designs differed: Codman and MERIDIAN had smooth, straight blades, whereas ACRA-CUT had a characteristic curved blade (Figure 4B). The MERIDIAN perforator exhibited distinct flute geometry compared with Codman. ACRA-CUT's unique features included a sharp point angle and 3 cutting lips with flutes, as summarized in Figure 4C.

Codman and MERIDIAN displayed smooth, straight blades (black arrow), whereas ACRA-CUT incorporated a distinctively curved blade (white arrow). ACRA-CUT's unique combination of a sharp point angle, tri-curved blade, and optimized flute design likely contributes to its superior drilling performance and increased risk of dural injury at oblique angles.

DISCUSSION

The trepanation success rate of Codman was notably lower than that of MERIDIAN and ACRA-CUT, with ACRA-CUT demonstrating a significantly faster trepanation than both Codman and MERIDIAN. Despite this efficiency, the cadaveric study revealed an increased risk of dural damage with ACRA-CUT. Microstructural analysis suggests that ACRA-CUT's sharp tip and distinctive blade design contribute to its enhanced drilling performance but also increase the risk of complications.

Among the perforators studied, Codman was the most cost-effective, priced at approximately half the cost of ACRA-CUT. However, its lower trepanation success rate often necessitates using secondary instruments, such as a manual or high-speed drill,10 or even pediatric disposable cranial perforators,11 to complete the procedure. The requirement for additional devices prolongs operative time and increases overall procedural costs, making a single, effective perforator preferable for optimizing workflow and resource utilization.12 Thus, selecting MERIDIAN or ACRA-CUT is preferable to ensure the success of trepanation.

Among the perforators studied, ACRA-CUT achieved the quickest trepanation times. Its design incorporates 3 critical structural elements that influence bone drilling: point angle, cutting lip, and flute. The sharp tip unique to ACRA-CUT likely facilitates faster bone penetration, whereas 3 blades enhance bone removal efficiency.13-15 However, given its high cost, ACRA-CUT may be more suitable for emergency surgeries where speed is paramount, whereas MERIDIAN might be preferred for scheduled procedures involving multiple trepanations.

Trepanation remains a fundamental neurosurgical procedure, typically initiated by applying the perforator perpendicularly to the skull to ensure precise rotation. The results of this study reaffirm that dural injuries can occur when disposable cranial perforators, even those equipped with automatic control mechanisms, are used at oblique angles. In addition to proper alignment of the perforator, basic techniques such as applying the saline solution to suppress drill-induced heat and stabilizing the drill handle with the opposite hand to prevent plunging are critical to ensuring patient safety.14,16

These findings highlight the trade-offs associated with ACRA-CUT, which balances high drilling performance against an elevated risk of dural injury. This risk may be further compounded when operated by less experienced surgeons. In such cases, Codman or MERIDIAN may be preferable, as their designs are rooted in the proven reliability of early steel perforators, which have remained largely unchanged for half a century.17

Limitations

This study has some limitations that warrant consideration. First, the small sample size and lack of randomization limit the generalizability of the findings. Second, the potential effects of wear-and-tear degradation on perforator performance were not evaluated, as multiple burr holes were created with a single device during the study.16 Third, the absence of a standardized trepanation site in the cadaveric model may have introduced variability. Fourth, no mechanical engineering analysis was conducted to explore other factors influencing bone removal, leaving the observed differences between Codman and MERIDIAN unresolved. Despite their structural similarities, the disparity in their perforation performances suggests potential differences in bone debris ejection mechanisms, likely attributable to variations in flute geometry.13,18

Future studies incorporating detailed mechanical evaluations and standardized experimental models are needed to elucidate these differences and provide a comprehensive understanding of the factors affecting perforator performance.13,19,20 This study offers a nuanced perspective on disposable cranial perforator performance, reaffirming the importance of selecting the appropriate device for specific clinical scenarios. Future large-scale, randomized trials are necessary to validate these findings and optimize perforator selection in neurological practice.

CONCLUSION

ACRA-CUT demonstrates exceptional speed and efficiency in trepanation, distinguishing itself as a highly effective tool for neurosurgical procedures. However, its use requires caution due to a significantly higher risk of dural injury, particularly when applied at nonperpendicular angles. MERIDIAN offers a reliable alternative for routine operations, balancing high success rates with moderate operative times. Codman, while the most cost-effective option, is limited by its lower success rate and potential for increased operative durations, which diminishes its overall utility.

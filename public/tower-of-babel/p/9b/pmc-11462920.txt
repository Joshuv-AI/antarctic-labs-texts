Survival benefit of adjuvant TACE for patients with hepatocellular carcinoma and child-pugh B7 or B8 after hepatectomy
Authors: Zhen-Yu Gao, Li-Ming Jin, Zheng-Kang Fang, Fang-Qiang Wei, Wen-Feng Lu, Xiao-Kun Huang, Cheng-Fei Du, Kai-Di Wang, Jian Cheng, Guo-Liang Shen, Dong-Sheng Huang, Jun-Wei Liu, Cheng-Wu Zhang, Lei Liang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground & aimsThe benefit of postoperative adjuvant transcatheter arterial chemoembolization (pTACE) for patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), especially those with Child-Pugh (CP) B, remains controversial. This study aimed to assess the survival benefit of pTACE for HCC patients with CP B.MethodsData from 297 HCC patients with CP B7 or B8 were analyzed, dividing them into groups with and without pTACE (70, 23.6% vs. 227, 76.4%). Propensity score matching (PSM) was used to control for confounding bias, and competing-risk regression was applied to address bias from non-cancer-specific death (NCSD).ResultsPreliminary findings suggest that pTACE did not increase the incidence of severe complications in HCC patients with CP B7 or B8. Survival analysis indicated that the group receiving pTACE had better overall survival and recurrence-free survival than the group without pTACE after PSM. Furthermore, competitive risk analysis revealed that pTACE was an independent prognostic factor associated with reduced cancer-specific death incidence (subdistribution hazard ratio [SHR] 0.644, 95%CI: 0.378–0.784, P = 0.011) and recurrence (SHR 0.635, 95% CI: 0.379–0.855, P = 0.001). Importantly, pTACE did not increase NCSD. Subgroup analysis corroborated these results.ConclusionAdjuvant TACE demonstrates the potential to significantly enhance the long-term prognosis of HCC patients with CP B7 or B8 following hepatectomy, particularly those with multiple tumors, large tumor size, macrovascular or microvascular invasion, and narrow resection margin. Hence, pTACE should be considered for patients at high risk of recurrence following thorough evaluation.Keywords: Hepatocellular carcinoma, TACE, Child-pugh B, Hepatectomy, Survival

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is a lethal malignant tumor, with the 6th highest incidence and the 4th highest tumor-related mortality [1]. The majority of patients with HCC exhibit concurrent chronic liver disease, such as infection of hepatitis virus B or C (HBV or HCV), which significantly limits the implementation of antitumor therapy due to compromised liver function.

The status of liver function serves as a crucial predictor of long-term survival among patients diagnosed with HCC [2, 3]. The Child-Pugh (CP) score is routinely utilized to evaluate the severity and prognostic implications of liver function [4, 5]. A preserved liver function, classified as CP A, is a prerequisite for achieving optimal surgical outcomes following curative hepatectomy [4]. Conversely, patients categorized as CP C, absent significant comorbid risk factors, are typically prioritized for liver transplantation based on well-established eligibility criteria. Nevertheless, a consensus on the therapy of HCC in patients with CP B remains elusive. In the real world, due to the shortage of liver donors and the advanced stage of HCC at diagnosis, hepatectomy remains the most commonly used curative treatment for patients with CP B after rigorous evaluation [6–9]. However, the long-term survival after hepatectomy is still unsatisfactory because of the high recurrence rate [10]. Unfortunately, the current research on adjuvant therapy for patients with HCC and CP B after hepatectomy is very limited.

Transcatheter arterial chemoembolization (TACE) has emerged as a postoperative strategy to mitigate the risk of recurrence and enhance survival outcomes. Notably, several premier Asian medical centers have advocated the use of TACE as an adjuvant therapy, specifically administered 4–8 weeks following HCC resection [11, 12]. This adjuvant approach aims to eliminate intrahepatic micro-metastases or residual tumor foci, thereby mitigating the risk of recurrence. Furthermore, numerous single-center randomized controlled trials (RCTs) examining pTACE have demonstrated promising overall and disease-free survival rates among patients who have undergone HCC resection [13–16]. Although these studies included some patients with CP B after hepatectomy, they did not conduct a detailed analysis for this special group, including different CP B scores and the occurrence of complications after pTACE. In addition, patients with CP B may die due to liver dysfunction, rather than to the tumor progression itself, and these studies also did not consider the impact of non-cancer-specific death (NCSD) on long-term prognosis.

Therefore, this study aims to evaluate the impact of pTACE on the long-term prognosis of patients with HCC and CP B, especially those with CP scores of 7 or 8. Meanwhile, propensity score-matched (PSM) analysis was adopted to control confounding bias. Moreover, Fine and Gray’s competing-risk regression model was performed to exclude the impact of NCSD, which made the result more robust.

Methods

Patients

Consecutive patients with HCC and CP B undergoing curative hepatectomy (R0) were included between January 2010 and December 2020 from two tertiary comprehensive cancer centers, Zhejiang Provincial People’s Hospital and Eastern Hepatobiliary Surgery Hospital. All patients diagnosed with HCC underwent pathological confirmation to ensure accurate diagnosis. The R0 resection was defined as the complete surgical excision of all tumors, ensuring a microscopic margin free of cancer cells. Patients who met the following criterion were excluded: (1) total bilirubin > 51 umol/L; (2) PT prolongation of more than 6 s; (3) accompanied with a large number of ascites; (4) accompanied with hepatic encephalopathy; 5)patients who experience postoperative major complications, such as pneumonia, abdominal infection, etc.; 6) mixed or biphenotypic HCC; 7) with other adjuvant therapy, such as targeted or immunotherapy; 8) patients were found with residual tumor in liver remnants by either contrast-enhanced CT or MRI carried out 4 to 6 weeks after surgery; 9) missing important data. Data were censored in December 2023. All patients included in the study had obtained informed consent before surgery and agreed to have their data stored and used in the research. This study was consistent with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Institutional Review Board at Zhejiang Provincial People’s Hospital and Eastern Hepatobiliary Surgery Hospital (No. QT2024104).

Clinicopathological characteristics

All the included clinicopathological characteristics were retrospectively collected from the medical records system. Nineteen variables were enrolled, including sex (male vs. female), age at diagnosis (> 60 vs. ≤60 years), American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) score, performance status (PS), CP score (7 or 8), prothrombin time (PT), total bilirubin (TBIL), serum albumin concentrations (ALB), etiology of liver disease (hepatitis B virus, HBV), cirrhosis, portal hypertension, preoperative serum level of aspartate transaminase (AST), alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), tumor size (≥ 5 vs. < 5 cm), tumor number (solitary vs. multiple), macrovascular invasion, Barcelona Clinic for Liver Cancer (BCLC) stage (A/B/C), Milan criterion (within vs. beyond), the extent of hepatectomy (major vs. minor), surgical margins (≥ 1 vs. < 1 cm), microvascular invasion (MVI), tumor differentiation (moderate/well vs. poor), and adjuvant TACE. The CP classification is a grading system utilized to assess liver function, which relies on a score derived from five parameters [17]: PT, TBIL, ALB, and the presence and severity of ascites and hepatic encephalopathy. Each parameter is assigned a score ranging from 1 to 3, with a score of 3 indicating the most compromised liver function. According to the sum of these scores, 7–9 points are set as CP B [18]. Portal hypertension was established when either splenomegaly accompanied by a platelet count of 100 × 109/L or less, or the presence of gastroesophageal varix, was observed [19]. The degree of hepatectomy was categorized into minor and major liver resections, with major liver resection being defined as the removal of more than three liver segments [20].

Adjuvant TACE

Adjuvant TACE was performed within 4 ~ 8 weeks after surgery at all the participating hospitals. TACE was performed according to a standard protocol, under local anesthesia, through arterial femoral access with a 5-F catheter and selective catheterization of the feeding arteries to the surrounding liver area after hepatectomy with either a 5-F catheter or 2.4-F microcatheter, depending upon the vascular anatomy. The main chemotherapeutic regimens used in the TACE procedure included the combinations of cisplatin and epirubicin. The embolization materials were iodized oil and gelatin sponge cubes, or iodized oil only, which was mixed completely with these chemotherapeutic drugs as an emulsion and injected.

Follow-up and study endpoints

The patients underwent follow-up at each participating institution, with serum AFP measurements, ultrasonography, or contrast-enhanced CT or MRI conducted at regular intervals. Specifically, these procedures were conducted at 2-month intervals during the initial half year, followed by 3-month intervals for the subsequent one and a half years, and then once every 6 months at 2 years or later following resection. Tumor recurrence was defined as the emergence of new intra- or extra-hepatic tumor nodules, regardless of whether there was an elevation in serum AFP levels. The treatment approach for HCC recurrence was determined through multidisciplinary collaboration. The study endpoints encompassed tumor recurrence, cancer-specific death (CSD), and NCSD. Recurrence was defined as tumor recurrence or new HCC occurrence. The NCSD was defined as the death from any other cause, except for HCC, while the CSD was defined as the death from HCC.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were divided into binary or tertile categories based on previous studies. Categorical variables comparing two rates or constituent ratios were determined by using χ2 or Fisher’s exact test with Yates correction appropriately. The collinearity test was detected before the multivariable analysis. PSM analysis was adopted to control confounding bias [21]. To refine the assessment of individual propensity, we formulated a logistic regression framework that incorporated a comprehensive array of covariates: sex, age, ASA, PS, Child-Pugh, PT, TBIL, ALB, ascites score, ALT, AST, HBV, cirrhosis, portal hypertension, AFP, tumor size, tumors number, macrovascular invasion, the extent of resection, resection margin, MVI, and differentiation. This approach entailed a structured process where cases and controls were arranged sequentially, initiating with the selection of the initial case subject and subsequently identifying the control with the most proximate propensity score. Subsequently, a 1:2 nearest neighbor matching scheme, sans replacement, was implemented to optimize the reduction of conditional bias. This matching relied on a greedy algorithm, meticulously pairing each treatment unit with the two control units from the opposing group exhibiting the closest propensity scores, ensuring that each TACE-receiving patient was matched with two non-TACE counterparts based on minimal propensity score disparity. The effectiveness of the matching was meticulously evaluated by contrasting the standardized differences in covariate means for continuous variables and direct differences for dichotomous variables, both within and between the matched and unmatched subsets. A caliper width of 0.02 was determined to offer an optimal equilibrium between achieving a high degree of homogeneity and maintaining an ample sample size. The cumulative incidence of recurrence, CSD, and NCSD were estimated and compared via Fine and Gray’s test [22]. When the cumulative incidence of recurrence or CSD was estimated, the NCSD was considered as the competing event. The subdistribution hazard ratio (SHR) of recurrence, CSD, and NCSD were estimated [23]. Statistical analyses were performed using R 4.2.3 (http://www.r-project.org/), with packages including “cmprsk” and “rms”. P < 0.05 was determined statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics

Based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria, a total of 301 patients with HCC and CP B who underwent curative resection were included. According to the CP score, 237 patients were CP B7, 60 patients were CP B8, and only 4 patients were CP B9. Due to the limited number of patients with CP B9, their inclusion in the multivariate analysis may have led to inaccurate results due to various confounding factors. Therefore, patients with CP B9 were excluded from the study, resulting in a final sample size of 297 patients with HCC and CP B7 or B8. Among them, 70 (23.6%) patients received adjuvant TACE, while 227 (76.4%) patients did not (Table 1). The baseline characteristics revealed that the TACE group had a higher proportion of patients with CP B8 (P = 0.030), and a higher proportion of patients with BCLC stage B (P = 0.017). The distribution of included variables in the CP score is shown in Fig. 1, which showed that most patients with B8 were caused by mild elevations in TBIL and PT, as well as significant decreases in ALB, while the majority of patients with B7 had normal PT values, with only ALB and TBIL abnormalities. In addition, the TACE group had a higher proportion of male patients (P = 0.039). After 1:2 PSM with a caliper of 0.02, 171 patients (57 patients in with TACE group vs. 114 patients without the TACE group) were included for further analysis. Then, the results showed that there were no significant statistical differences in the distribution of all variables between the two groups.

Adverse effects of pTACE

Among the 70 patients with HCC and CP B7 or B8 who received pTACE treatment, 11 (15.7%) patients developed increased ascites (score was 3) postoperatively. However, after treatment with albumin supplementation, diuretics, and other measures, the ascites condition improved in 9 (12.8%) patients (score was reduced to 2), while the other 2 (2.9%) patients showed improvement after receiving ultrasound-guided abdominal paracentesis and drainage. All patients who developed increased ascites had a preoperative ascites score of 1 before TACE. Additionally, 16 (22.8%) patients developed prolonged PT or elevated TBIL after pTACE, but they all improved after active liver fuction protection treatment. No perioperative deaths were reported during this period.

Competing risk analyses of cancer-special death and recurrence

During the follow-up, 40 (13.5%) patients were died from NCSD. Among them, 9 patients were in the with TACE group, and 11 patients were in the without TACE group. The 1-, 3- and 5-year overall survival (OS) was 94%, 74%, 46%, 72%, 49%, 30%in with and without the TACE group, respectively. And the 1-, 3- and 5-year recurrence-free survival (RFS) was 86%, 54%, 29%, 64%, 32%, and 23% in the with and without TACE group, respectively. For estimating the cumulative incidence of CSD and recurrence, the NCSD was considered as the competing event. The cumulative incidence of NCSD and CSD were estimated and compared via Fine and Gray’s test. The results indicated that, compared to the without TACE group, the cumulative incidence of CSD was significantly decreased in the with TACE group (P = 0.001). While the cumulative incidence of NCSD was no statistically difference between the two groups (P = 0.490) (Fig. 2A). Meanwhile, the results indicated that, compared to the without TACE group, the cumulative incidence of recurrence was significantly decreased in the with TACE group (P = 0.001). Of note, the cumulative incidence of competing events was no statistical difference between the two groups (P = 0.319) (Fig. 2B). Furthermore, the results after PSM are consistent with the aforementioned results (Fig. 2C and D).

Independent risk factors of cancer-special death and recurrence

Univariate and multivariate analyses using Fine and Gray’s competing risk regression for NCSD, CSD, and recurrence were performed. Multivariate analysis demonstrated that age CP score and portal hypertension were independently associated with NCSD. Moreover, CSD was independently associated with AFP, tumor size, tumor number, macrovascular invasion, and MVI. Adjuvant TACE was independently associated with a decreased incidence of CSD (SHR 0.644, 95%CI 0.378–0.784, P = 0.012) (Table 2). Meanwhile, risks of tumor recurrence were independently associated with AFP, tumor size, tumor number, macrovascular invasion, resection margin, and MVI. Adjuvant TACE was also independently associated with a decreased incidence of recurrence (SHR 0.635, 95%CI 0.379–0.855, P = 0.001) (Table 3).

Subgroup analysis

To better determine the survival benefits of pTACE for patients with HCC and CP B7 or B8, we conducted a subgroup analysis. The results showed that pTACE exhibited survival benefits in most subgroups, especially for patients with AFP ≥ 400 ug/L, cirrhosis, tumor size > 5 cm, multiple tumor numbers, resection margin < 1 cm, macrovascular invasion, and MVI. Additionally, it demonstrated good survival benefits in both BCLC stages A and B, as well as both within and beyond Milan criteria (Fig. 3A). Moreover, subgroup analysis of RFS also showed that TACE could significantly reduce the incidence of recurrence rate in the subgroup with AFP ≥ 400 ug/L, cirrhosis, tumor size > 5 cm, multiple tumor numbers, resection margin < 1 cm, MVI, and without macrovascular invasion. In addition, consistent results were also observed in BCLC stages A and B, and both within and beyond Milan criteria (Fig. 3B).

Discussion

In the present study, we first explored the survival benefit of pTACE for patients with HCC and CP B7 or B8. 297 patients with HCC and CP B7 or B8 were reviewed and divided into with and without adjuvant TACE groups. The results showed that pTACE did not increase the incidence of severe complications. Survival analysis showed the TACE group had better OS and RFS. Competitive risk analysis indicated that pTACE was an independent prognostic factor for decreasing the incidence of CSD (SHR 0.644, 95%CI: 0.378–0.784, P = 0.011) and recurrence (SHR 0.635, 95%CI: 0.379–0.855, P = 0.001), but did not increase the NCSD. In other words, TACE can reduce the risk of recurrence and CSD by nearly 35%. The results indicated that pTACE should be recommended to patients with HCC and CP B7 or B8 after rigorous evaluation.

The CP score is the most widely used assessment tool for liver function and has been incorporated into algorithms for the management of patients with HCC. However, there is a lack of unequivocal guidelines for the management of HCC in patients with CP B. The allocation of appropriate therapy remains challenging and controversial, as these individuals present with an intermediate condition where their liver function is partially compromised, falling between well-preserved and terminal states. Liver transplantation is regarded as an optimal and definitive treatment modality as it not only eradicates the malignancy but also restores normal liver function. Nevertheless, its availability is limited by stringent criteria and the scarcity of suitable organs globally, thereby constraining its widespread application. Consequently, surgical resection remains the primary curative treatment option for these patients [18, 24, 25]. The optimal resection should be individualized for each patient considering the tumor, the liver’s functional reserve, and the severity of cirrhosis.

Currently, many studies reported that curative liver resection has better outcomes than locoregional and systematic therapies for patients with CP B [25, 26]. Berardi et al. performed a multicenter retrospective study to evaluate the outcomes of patients with HCC and CP B after hepatectomy [25]. And 253 patients were included from 14 international referral centers. Among them, 43.5% had preoperative ascites, and 56.9% had portal hypertension. The survival analysis showed that the 5-year OS was 47%, and 56.9% of patients experienced recurrence. In the present study, 297 patients were included, 120 (40.4%) patients had preoperative ascites, and 174 (58.5%) patients had portal hypertension. However, the 5-year OS was only 30%, and more than 77% of patients experienced recurrence in patients with postoperative adjuvant therapy. Therefore, how to carry out anti-recurrence treatment is a key factor in improving the long-term prognosis of these patients. However, due to the poor liver function of these patients, most pivotal studies have excluded patients with CP B. Only a few studies of postoperative adjuvant therapy have involved patients with CP B, but the CP scores have not been analyzed in detail. There are surprisingly limited studies to date that have focused solely on this subset of patients.

Moreover, with a CP B8 or B9, life expectancy may be dominated by cirrhotic liver dysfunction, hence, locoregional treatments might also be detrimental, and precipitating. liver dysfunction to the extent that survival is shortened rather than prolonged. Thus, in the present study, there are only 4 patients with CP B9. This phenomenon indicates that, in clinical practice, there are rarely patients with CP B9 who undergo surgical resection, but patients with CP B8 still have the opportunity to receive curative surgery after strict evaluation. To avoid the influence of patients with CP B9 on the results, this study did not include this kind of patient. Despite this exclusion, up to 13.5%% of patients still died due to NCSD. Ignoring the impact of NCSD would confuse the interpretation of the survival benefits of adjuvant TACE for patients with HCC and CP B. To address these concerns, the proportional subdistribution hazards model for competitive risk analysis was performed, which made the results more robust [27, 28].

TACE has been employed in the postoperative setting as an effective strategy to mitigate the risk of recurrence and enhance survival outcomes [12, 29]. Specifically, pTACE may effectively eliminate intrahepatic micro-metastases or residual tumor foci, thereby preventing recurrence following HCC resection. Notably, numerous single-center RCTs on adjuvant TACE have demonstrated more favorable overall and disease-free survival rates among patients who have undergone HCC resection [10, 13–16]. However, despite the widespread evidence supporting the efficacy of TACE in the postoperative adjuvant treatment of HCC, a dearth of relevant studies exists regarding its application in patients with CP B. Our previous study, including 2514 patients, established a novel online calculator for the net survival benefit of postoperative adjuvant TACE [10]. Although this model included patients with CP B, it did not make a more detailed distinction. In the present study, we only included patients with CP B7 and B8 and found that postoperative adjuvant TACE had a significant improvement in long-term prognosis. Further subgroup analysis also showed that in the CP B7 and B8 subgroups, adjuvant TACE also provided survival benefits to patients. In addition, through subgroup analysis of risk factors, we found that adjuvant TACE had a positive impact on patients with AFP ≥ 400 ug/L, cirrhosis, tumor size > 5 cm, multiple tumor numbers, resection margin < 1 cm, macrovascular invasion, and MVI.

Several limitations need to be addressed. Firstly, as a retrospective observational study, it was not possible to standardize or identify certain factors, such as the technique used for liver resection and the specific details of adjuvant TACE, including the type of chemotherapy administered. Secondly, the patient population was primarily from China and had a significant background of HBV infection (over 80%). Whether these results can be generalized to patients with HCC primarily associated with HCV remains to be further investigated. Thirdly, in the subgroup analysis, although we examined patients with different CP B cores, further analysis of the variables associated with these scores was not conducted. To mitigate this bias, a larger patient cohort or a randomized controlled trial is necessary to accurately delineate the role of adjuvant TACE. Nevertheless, despite these constraints, the present study provides robust evidence to support the use of adjuvant TACE for patients with HCC and CP B classification.

Conclusion

Postoperative adjuvant TACE can significantly improve the long-term prognosis of patients with HCC and CP B7 and 8, especially for those with multiple tumors, large tumor size, macrovascular or microvascular invasion, and narrow resection margin. Thus, adjuvant TACE should be recommended for patients with a high risk of recurrence after rigorous evaluation.

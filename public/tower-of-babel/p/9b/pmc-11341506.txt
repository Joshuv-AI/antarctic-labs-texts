Stereopsis Following Implantation of Presbyopia-Correcting Intraocular Lenses: A Narrative Review
Authors: Yongyuan He, Bijun Zhu, Baojiang Li, Haidong Zou, Yingyan Ma
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractRecent advancements in cataract surgery have broadened its scope from mere vision restoration to include correction of refractive errors and presbyopia. This evolution has introduced multifocal and extended depth-of-focus (EDOF) intraocular lenses (IOLs), allowing enhanced vision across multiple distances. However, the influence of these advanced IOLs on stereopsis remains controversial. Factors influencing stereopsis after surgery include visual acuity, interocular differences, residual astigmatism, and the type of IOL, etc. Binocular vision integration and neuroadaptation further affect stereopsis, especially in cases of presbyopia-correcting IOLs. It is widely acknowledged that bilateral implantation of presbyopia-correcting IOLs yield superior stereopsis compared to unilateral implantation. However, there remains no consensus on whether binocular implantation of multifocal or monofocal IOLs provides superior stereopsis. Most studies suggest no significant difference in stereopsis between these two types of implants. Among different types of multifocal IOLs, refractive multifocal IOLs may offer better stereopsis than diffractive multifocal IOLs when implanted bilaterally. Emerging EDOF and hybrid multifocal-EDOF IOLs also demonstrate promising postoperative stereopsis. Additionally, a mix-and-match strategy with different types of IOLs implanted in each eye may result in interocular differences in visual acuity at certain distances, potentially affecting stereopsis. Nevertheless, with appropriate selection, most patients can achieve satisfactory postoperative stereopsis. This review synthesizes current literature on the effects of presbyopia-correcting IOLs on postoperative stereopsis recovery following cataract surgery. Studies on stereopsis outcomes with different IOLs have yielded mixed results, urging further investigation for optimized surgical strategies and patient outcomes.Keywords: Cataract surgery, Extended depth-of-focus intraocular lenses, Multifocal intraocular lenses, Presbyopia-correcting intraocular lenses, Stereopsis

Key Summary Points

With the advancement of cataract surgery, the focus has expanded from simply restoring sight to improving overall visual quality, where stereopsis plays a crucial part.Bilateral implantation of presbyopia-correcting intraocular lenses (IOLs) tends to outperform unilateral implantation in terms of postoperative stereopsis.Although whether presbyopia-correcting IOLs offer better postoperative stereopsis compared to monofocal IOLs is still a matter of debate, most patients can achieve satisfactory stereopsis.While most patients achieve good stereopsis after mix-and-match implantation, there is still no consensus on which combined implantation approach yields superior stereopsis.Further research is required to understand the long-term effects of IOLs on stereopsis and underlying neuroadaptation processes to refine clinical practices accordingly.

Introduction

With the continuous development of surgical techniques and the emergence of novel intraocular lenses (IOLs), cataract surgery has gradually transitioned from solely restoring vision to correcting refractive errors and presbyopia. In addition to the need for distance vision, patients now have higher demands for intermediate and near vision. While monofocal IOLs can provide good distance vision and visual quality, their single focus can be limiting for patients, as it does not provide full-range vision. Multifocal IOLs have emerged based on the optical phenomena of light refraction, diffraction, or a combination of both of them. Light passing through multifocal IOLs can simultaneously form two or more focal points on the retina, thereby meeting the vision needs of patients at various distances. This requires a certain level of neuroadaptation in the brain to achieve clear images and filter out blurry ones. However, the image quality of multifocal IOLs is compromised as they distribute light into different focal points. They are prone to reducing contrast sensitivity and inducing dysphotopsias, potentially affecting the quality of monocular vision and consequently stereopsis [1]. The novel extended depth-of-focus (EDOF) IOLs utilize optical principles such as wavefront manipulation, small aperture imaging, diffraction, and refraction. Compared to multifocal IOLs, EDOF IOLs help expand depth of focus and enhance accommodative ability, enabling patients to more effortlessly maintain clear vision and adjust focal points at near, intermediate, and far distances. Simultaneously, they ameliorate unwanted visual phenomena and enhance contrast sensitivity, both of which contribute to improving stereopsis. Nonetheless, limitations persist regarding the image quality [2, 3]. The emergence of newer hybrid multifocal-EDOF IOLs and various mix-and-match implantation strategies aims to address the shortcomings of current IOL implantation strategies [4–6].

Binocular vision encompasses the entire capability of using both eyes for visual perception [7]. Stereopsis, representing the highest level of binocular vision, is formed through the integration and processing of multiple cortex areas based on simultaneous and fusion vision. It refers to the ability to perceive and discriminate fine differences in depth or distance between objects in three-dimensional space. Stereoacuity is measured as the smallest discernible difference in depth or disparity between two points that still allows for stereoscopic depth perception [8]. Binocular disparity forms the basis of stereopsis [9]. Besides binocular disparity, the mechanisms underlying the formation of stereopsis also involve processing in the visual cortex, depth cues, and neurotransmission [8]. Stereopsis plays a pivotal role in multiple daily activities [10]. It is noteworthy that impaired stereopsis may entail potential risks. Research suggests that diminished depth perception and contrast sensitivity are important risk factors for falls [11] and hip fractures [12].

The 2021 edition of the American Academy of Ophthalmology (AAO) Adult Cataract Guidelines highlighted depth perception and binocular vision as significant influencing factors of postoperative visual outcomes. It is suggested that improving refractive errors and ensuring good visual acuity in both eyes may enhance postoperative outcomes [13]. Apart from monocular visual acuity and refractive error status in both eyes, conditions induced by cataract surgery such as aniseikonia, residual astigmatism, and lens decentration and tilt may affect stereopsis [14, 15]. The type of IOL and neuroadaptation of patients may also affect postoperative stereopsis. It is generally accepted that binocular implantation of IOLs offer better stereopsis compared to monocular implantation, possibly due to aniseikonia and anisometropia induced by unilateral surgery [16]. Currently, conflicting results exist regarding whether presbyopia-correcting IOL implantation yields superior postoperative stereoacuity compared to monofocal lenses [17–20]. Furthermore, the emerging mix-and-match strategy, designed to optimize visual acuity at various distances, may impact stereopsis due to interocular differences in visual function [6]. There remains a scarcity of relevant literature reviews comparing postoperative stereopsis under different IOL implantation strategies, thus necessitating further exploration of this issue.

The present study provides a concise overview of potential factors affecting postoperative stereopsis after cataract surgery. Subsequently, it comprehensively reviews the status of stereopsis following implantation of presbyopia-correcting IOLs.

This article is based on previously conducted studies and does not contain any new studies with human participants or animals performed by any of the authors.

Postoperative Recovery of Stereopsis and Influencing Factors

Cataract surgery improves monocular and binocular vision, laying the foundation for the restoration of stereopsis. The matching of visual acuity between the two eyes is a crucial prerequisite for good stereopsis [15]. Hayashi et al. found that the most significant factors affecting stereopsis in patients with IOL implantation included large interocular differences of spherical equivalent, older age, and larger pupil diameter [21]. Postoperative residual astigmatism also impacts stereopsis [15]. Comas et al. investigated the changes in stereopsis and influencing factors at different stages after surgery. They found that near stereopsis underwent significant improvements after both the first and second eye surgeries. Furthermore, near stereopsis after the first and second eye surgeries was closely associated with interocular differences of spherical equivalent and binocular contrast sensitivity, respectively [22]. Postoperative aniseikonia can weaken binocular fusion, potentially affecting stereopsis [14]. Decentered and tilted IOLs can also influence stereopsis [15]. Due to their multifocality, multifocal and EDOF IOLs are more prone to inducing dysphotopsias such as glare and halos, as well as decreasing contrast sensitivity. Consequently, this may impair the image quality of the two eyes and thereby impact stereopsis [23].

After unilateral cataract surgery, it is more common to encounter visual problems like aniseikonia and anisometropia, which subsequently lead to a deterioration in stereopsis. However, these conditions tend to improve after the second eye surgery [16]. Shoji et al. conducted a comparative study between bilateral and unilateral implantation of one type of refractive multifocal IOLs. They observed that in the bilateral group, 84.6% of patients achieved near stereoacuity equal to or better than 60", while in the unilateral group, the proportion was 42.8%. Additionally, 92.3% of patients in the bilateral group and 21.4% in the unilateral group showed no aniseikonia. Both results revealed significant differences between the two groups (P = 0.009; P = 0.001) [24]. Although unilateral implantation of multifocal IOL can achieve satisfactory visual acuity and rate of spectacle independence, considering factors such as stereopsis, vision-related quality of life, and subjective satisfaction, bilateral surgery remains a more recommended option in most cases.

Long-term visual suppression in patients with cataract may disrupt binocular central fusion, particularly in cases of monocular suppression, which could prolong the recovery time or affect the outcome of postoperative stereopsis [25]. Postoperative recovery of stereopsis is associated with the neuroplasticity of the brain. When selecting presbyopia-correcting IOLs in the clinical practice, in addition to considering ocular examination outcomes, elderly patients with limited neuroadaptation are considered relatively contraindicated [21, 26, 27]. However, compared to monofocal IOLs, patients implanted with multifocal or EDOF IOLs have better full-range vision and still retain some accommodative ability. This is crucial for binocular visual function at different distances, especially during transitions between them. Therefore, it may benefit the postoperative recovery of stereopsis in certain patients [1].

There is limited research regarding the time required for the reestablishment and recovery of stereopsis following cataract surgery, but it can be inferred to be closely related to the process of postoperative recovery of vision. Generally, most patients without other vision-impairing ocular diseases experience significant visual improvement within days to weeks after cataract surgery. After an adaptation period, vision of most patients stabilizes several months after surgery [16, 28, 29]. Changes in visual perception during the progression of cataract may provoke structural and functional changes in corresponding regions in the brain. After surgery, as visual function recuperates, neurons and synaptic connections along the corresponding visual pathways in the brain will undergo a period of reconstruction. This process is closely related to the recovery of binocular visual function [30]. Regardless of the type of IOL implanted, patients typically require an adaptation period postoperatively to acclimate to the new visual input patterns. The underlying optical principles of presbyopia-correcting IOLs dictate a higher demand for neuroadaptation during the postoperative restoration of binocular visual function. In a cross-sectional study, researchers found that patients who underwent bilateral implantation of multifocal IOLs showed significantly increased activity in cortical areas responsible for attention, for learning and cognitive control, for tasking goals during difficult visual tasks at 3–4 weeks after surgery, indicating a process of neuroadaptation [31]. The team also conducted a prospective study revealing that by 6 months after surgery, the initially enhanced brain activity had gradually normalized, suggesting a transition towards long-term non-effort pattern. Optical evaluations showed no significant differences between the early and later periods after surgery, suggesting that the adaptation process with multifocal IOLs primarily involves the brain rather than optical changes in the eye [32]. Considering that poor neuroadaptation is a common cause of dysphotopsias following implantation of presbyopia-correcting IOLs, neuroadaptation may indeed constitute a critical foundation for achieving good stereopsis [33].

Stereopsis Following Implantation of Presbyopia-Correcting Intraocular Lenses

Stereopsis Following Bilateral Implantation of Presbyopia-Correcting Intraocular Lenses

Comparison of the Impacts of Multifocal and Monofocal Intraocular Lenses on Stereopsis

Research has found that bilateral implantation of multifocal IOLs with a low-addition power offers superior stereopsis at intermediate and near distances compared to monofocal IOLs, although this may be attributed to the lack of vision correction during stereopsis examination [17]. Conversely, another study suggested that after the correction of refractive errors, stereoacuity of patients implanted with multifocal IOLs was significantly poorer than that of patients implanted with monofocal IOLs, possibly associated with decreased contrast sensitivity [18]. However, more studies indicate that there is no significant difference in postoperative stereopsis between bilateral implantation of monofocal and multifocal IOLs [19]. Presently, it is generally acknowledged that bilateral implantation of multifocal IOLs is unlikely to compromise patients’ stereovision, with stereoacuity for the majority of patients falling within the normal range [34]. Additionally, Wilkins et al. compared the outcomes of bilateral implantation of diffractive multifocal IOLs with pseudophakic monovision with monofocal IOLs, revealing significantly better near stereoacuity in the multifocal group than in the monofocal one according to Titmus stereopsis test (P < 0.001) [20].

Comparison of the Impacts of Refractive and Diffractive Multifocal Intraocular Lenses on Stereopsis

Studies have suggested that binocular implantation of refractive multifocal IOLs may offer advantages over diffractive multifocal IOLs in terms of stereopsis. Patients with bilateral implantation of refractive multifocal IOLs have shown significantly better postoperative stereoacuity using TNO stereopsis test, compared to those with bilateral implantation of diffractive multifocal IOLs [18]. Chang et al. conducted a Titmus stereo test on patients who received bilateral implantation of refractive multifocal IOLs or diffractive multifocal IOLs during a 6-month follow-up after cataract surgery. The mean scores in the refractive and diffractive groups were 8.36 and 6.50, respectively, with 100% and 74.1% of patients achieving near stereoacuity of 80" or better in the refractive and diffractive groups, respectively. The difference between the two groups was statistically significant (P = 0.0170) [35]. These disparities may stem from their respective optical characteristics and design principles. Refractive multifocal IOLs contain multiple refractive zones with different refractive powers, causing light to form focal points at different positions as it passes through these zones. Their design typically does not greatly impact the coordination between the eyes as they primarily affect monocular vision. Diffractive multifocal IOLs, on the other hand, split light into multiple focal points through diffraction gratings, resulting in clear vision in specific areas. Their design may generate different focal points in the central and peripheral regions, potentially causing interocular differences and thus affecting stereopsis. Additionally, diffractive IOLs may create multiple focal points on the retina, leading to a decrease in visual quality, particularly under low-contrast conditions. Therefore, compared to diffractive multifocal IOLs, refractive multifocal IOLs have a relatively minor impact on stereopsis [15].

Impacts of Extended Depth-of-Focus Intraocular Lenses on Stereopsis

As the frequency of activities at intermediate distance including computer usage increases, new types of EDOF IOLs have emerged to provide enhanced intermediate vision. Regarding stereopsis, a study involving 100 eyes of 50 patients focused on the impact of binocular implantation of EDOF IOLs on distance and near stereopsis. At a 1-year postoperative follow-up, the average distance stereoacuity was 103.6 ± 49.1", while the near stereoacuity was 21.1 ± 2.3". Notably, 80% of patients achieved near stereoacuity of 20" or better, and 82% achieved distance stereoacuity of 100" or better. The mean satisfaction score was 9.08 ± 1.1 out of 10. Additionally, the study observed significant correlations between stereopsis and both patient satisfaction scores and the average modulation transfer function [36].

Stereopsis Following Unilateral Implantation of Presbyopia-Correcting Intraocular Lens

A case series evaluated postoperative stereopsis of two patients with pre-presbyopia and cataract who underwent unilateral implantation of multifocal and monofocal IOLs, respectively. At the 3- to 6-month postoperative follow-up, the patient implanted with a multifocal IOL passed the TNO butterfly stereopsis test and achieved near stereoacuity of 30", whereas the patient implanted with a monofocal IOL failed the TNO butterfly stereopsis test even with correction of refractive errors. This could be due to the superior vision at far, intermediate, and near distances demonstrated by patients implanted with multifocal IOL, thus facilitating matching and coordination with the contralateral eye. This indicates that multifocal IOLs may offer superior coordination with the contralateral eye for patients with monocular cataract without presbyopia, resulting in enhanced stereopsis [37].

Mesci et al. compared the visual outcomes of patients aged 40–70 years with presbyopia and unilateral cataract who were implanted with different IOLs. They found a significant improvement in uncorrected intermediate and near visual acuity in both eyes following the implantation of multifocal IOLs compared to monofocal ones (P = 0.0001). Additionally, in cases where both eyes were corrected for near vision, the multifocal group outperformed the monofocal group in Titmus stereo test [38]. In a similar study conducted by Hayashi et al., no significant differences were observed in near stereopsis between the multifocal and monofocal groups. This could be attributed to the fact that in this study, only phakic eyes and pseudophakic eyes implanted with monofocal IOLs were adjusted for near vision. The authors speculated that for unilateral IOL implantation, when the eye implanted with monofocal IOL was not corrected for near vision, the binocular near vision was insufficient to provide satisfactory near stereopsis, thus resulting in inferior near stereopsis compared to the multifocal group. However, when the monofocal IOL eye was targeted for emmetropia, the distance stereopsis of both groups was comparable [39].

Stereopsis Following Mix-and-Match Implantation

Earlier diffractive and refractive multifocal IOLs were suboptimal due to limitations in intermediate vision and visual quality, and undesirable visual phenomena. With technological advancements and the introduction of new types of IOLs, a novel strategy for IOL implantation has emerged, known as “mix-and-match”. This approach involves implanting two different types of IOLs in each eye to profit from their respective advantages, aiming to achieve optimal full-range visual acuity and reduce patients’ spectacle dependence. However, implanting different types of IOLs in each eye may result in interocular differences in visual acuity at certain distances, thereby undermining stereopsis. Recently, plenty of studies have also investigated the impact of diverse mix-and-match methods of IOL implantation on stereopsis.

Impact of Mix-and-Match Implantation of Monofocal and Multifocal Intraocular Lenses on Stereopsis

A Japanese study adopted an approach where the dominant eye was implanted with a monofocal IOL and the non-dominant eye was implanted with a diffractive bifocal IOL. The findings revealed that 62.5% of patients achieved near stereopsis within the normal range [40]. Research conducted by Cionni et al. suggested that patients receiving a multifocal IOL in one eye and a monofocal IOL in the other experienced significantly inferior postoperative near stereopsis compared to those who underwent bilateral implantation of multifocal IOLs (P = 0.0005) [41]. Liang et al. conducted a comparative analysis of binocular vision in patients with one eye implanted with a monofocal toric IOL and the other eye implanted with either a monofocal or multifocal IOL. The findings revealed that patients in the group mixing monofocal and multifocal lenses exhibited superior binocular intermediate and near vision. However, they also displayed markedly decreased contrast sensitivity and moderately compromised near stereopsis [42].

Impact of Mix-and-Match Implantation of Bifocal Intraocular Lenses with Different Addition Powers on Stereopsis

Yesilirmak et al. evaluated the postoperative visual outcomes following femtosecond laser-assisted cataract surgery. They compared the outcomes between patients receiving combined multifocal IOLs with different addition powers and those receiving bilateral trifocal IOLs. The trifocal group demonstrated superior performance in the defocus curve, with notably better intermediate and near vision, as well as contrast sensitivity. Nonetheless, both groups had comparable scores on the Visual Function Index questionnaire at the 6-month postoperative assessment. This study did not delve into postoperative stereopsis [43]. Compared to patients with bilateral implantation of the same diffractive multifocal IOLs, patients with cataracts who have bilateral multifocal IOLs with different addition powers showed no significant differences in binocular contrast sensitivity and near stereopsis [44]. Compared to the aforementioned mix-and-match strategy, patients with cataracts implanted with bilateral trifocal IOLs exhibited superior mean corrected and uncorrected distance and intermediate visual acuity, overall contrast sensitivity, and near stereoacuity at the 3-month postoperative evaluation. However, moderate halo symptoms were more prevalent in the trifocal group [45].

Impact of Mix-and-Match Implantation of Refractive and Diffractive Multifocal Intraocular Lenses on Stereopsis

Chen et al. compared near stereopsis between patients with mix-and-match implantation of a refractive IOL in the dominant eye and a diffractive multifocal IOL in the non-dominant eye to those with bilateral implantation of the same type of monofocal IOLs. The near stereoacuity under uncorrected visual acuity in the mix-and-match group (69 ± 50") was superior to that of the monofocal group under uncorrected and best-corrected near vision (180 ± 160"; 136 ± 117") (P = 0.021; P = 0.023) [46].

Impact of Mix-and-Match Implantation of Extended Depth-of-Focus and Multifocal Intraocular Lenses on Stereopsis

In comparison with bilateral implantation of trifocal IOLs, no significant difference was observed in uncorrected visual acuity at distance, intermediate, and near distances, as well as corrected distance visual acuity in the group mixing and matching EDOF and bifocal IOLs. Regarding near stereoacuity, the trifocal group and the mix-and-match group averaged 49.76 ± 22.67" and 120.63 ± 90.94", respectively. The near stereoacuity in the trifocal group significantly outperformed that in the mix-and-match group (P < 0.001), and near stereopsis was strongly correlated with binocular visual acuity at near distances. No statistically significant difference was detected between the two groups in stereopsis at distance and intermediate distances (P = 0.844; P = 0.083) [47]. A study involving 229 eyes of 146 patients compared the stereopsis after various combinations of IOLs including bifocal, trifocal, and EDOF IOLs. It was found that the mix-and-match group combining bifocal with EDOF IOLs exhibited superior near and distance stereoacuity [48]. Labiris et al. conducted a prospective randomized controlled trial comparing visual outcomes among patients with bilateral implantation of trifocal IOLs, bilateral implantation of EDOF IOLs, and a mixed implantation with EDOF IOL in dominant eye and trifocal IOL in non-dominant eye. The blended implantation group demonstrated greater performance in both visual acuity curves and National Eye Institute Visual Function Questionnaire-25 scores [49]. Furthermore, mixed implantation of hybrid trifocal-EDOF and trifocal IOLs provided good visual outcomes at far, intermediate, and near distances, though clinical reports regarding postoperative stereopsis with this implantation strategy are still lacking [50].

A novel type of ARTIS Symbiose IOL combines the properties of EDOF and bifocal IOLs to complement each other. ARTIS Symbiose Mid offers superior intermediate vision, whereas ARTIS Symbiose Plus excels in near vision. Optical bench evaluations indicate that while the depth of focus of ARTIS Symbiose Plus and Mid alone is lower than that of the Panoptix trifocal IOL, their combined theoretical total depth of field can reach 2.90 D, surpassing that of Panoptix IOL [51]. The complementary IOL system not only provides excellent continuous vision from distance to near but also maintains or improves stereopsis [4]. A prospective pilot study in Spain evaluated Titmus near stereopsis in 27 patients implanted with ARTIS Symbiose Mid IOL in dominant eye and ARTIS Symbiose Plus in non-dominant eye. It was found that 6 months after the surgery, the median of near stereoacuity reached 40", which was the smallest detectable value of the Titmus test [5]. Furthermore, a clinical study in the United Kingdom utilized TNO test for stereoscopic vision among patients undergoing this mix-and-match implantation. The study showed that all patients maintained at least gross stereopsis 1 year after surgery, with 90.1% achieving 480" or better and 50% achieving 120" or better. Additionally, the study noted a significant decrease in reports of glare and halos at 1-year follow-up compared to 3-month follow-up after surgery, indicating the neuroadaptation process following IOL implantation [4].

Conclusions

This review synthesizes the current literature on stereopsis following implantation of presbyopia-correcting IOLs. Cataract surgery has gradually transitioned from merely restoring vision to correcting refractive errors and presbyopia. Beyond the basic requirements for vision, there is an increasing demand for stereopsis in various daily activities [10]. Numerous clinical studies have shown that multifocal and EDOF IOLs remarkably improve postoperative full-range vision. However, reliable clinical evidence regarding their impact on stereopsis remains scarce. Their intrinsic optical principles also determine that they necessitate higher levels of neuroadaptation for stereovision recovery [1–3]. Ample data has suggested that bilateral implantation of presbyopia-correcting IOLs yields superior stereopsis compared to unilateral implantation. Binocular implantation of refractive multifocal IOLs may be superior to that of diffractive multifocal IOLs in terms of stereopsis [18, 35]. Refractive multifocal IOLs use multiple zones with different refractive powers to create multiple focal points, primarily affecting monocular vision and causing a relatively minor effect on stereopsis. In contrast, diffractive multifocal IOLs diffract light to produce multiple focal points on the retina, which may potentially reduce visual quality and affect stereopsis [15]. Newer hybrid multifocal-EDOF IOLs provide excellent continuous vision from distance to near while also maintaining or improving stereopsis [4]. However, comparative studies on postoperative stereopsis between these new types of IOLs and conventional ones are still lacking. Although a mix-and-match implantation strategy may affect stereopsis due to differences in visual function between the two eyes, most patients can achieve normal levels of stereopsis with appropriate selection. Moreover, the two types of IOLs can complement each other’s shortcomings by leveraging their individual advantages, potentially contributing to the restoration of stereopsis [48, 49]. However, this still requires further optical evaluation and clinical research.

This article highlights the role and importance of stereopsis in daily life, emphasizing the necessity of assessing and improving postoperative stereopsis to enhance quality of life. It briefly analyzes various factors influencing stereopsis recovery, such as visual acuity, interocular differences, residual astigmatism, and underscores the relationship between postoperative stereopsis recovery and neuroadaptation. This review advocates for personalized selection of IOLs based on personalized assessment of binocular visual function to achieve optimal postoperative stereopsis. Currently, there remains a lack of long-term evaluation regarding stereopsis following implantation of different types of IOLs, such as multifocal and EDOF IOLs, to determine their stability and persistence. In terms of neural mechanisms, future research needs to further explore how the brain adapts to new IOLs, particularly focusing on the role and mechanisms of neuroadaptation in the process of stereopsis recovery. Furthermore, researchers can evaluate and optimize mix-and-match implantation strategies to maximize postoperative stereopsis improvement and minimize potential negative impacts. Researchers can also develop and evaluate emerging techniques, surgical approaches, and postoperative rehabilitation methods to facilitate faster and more effective stereopsis recovery.

Traumatic C2-C3 subluxation with atlanto-axial dislocation managed by posterior approach — A rare case report
Authors: Md Rezaul Amin, Md Ataur Rahman, Mohammad Shahnawaz Bari, Firoj Ahmed Al-Amin
Published: 2023

Introduction

Traumatic Subluxation C2-C3/Hangman fracture is a rare entity and second most common of C2 injuries after odontoid fracture, described originally by Haughton in 1866 [[1], [2], [3]].

Atlantoaxial dislocation (AAD) is a rare entity and multiple etiologies are encountered in the pathogenesis of AAD- including trauma, tumours or congenital abnormalities in the upper cervical region, causing compression of the spinal cord or medulla resulting limb numbness and weakness, sphincter dysfunction, circulatory and respiratory dysfunction [4]. Mortality of traumatic AAD reportedly 60 %–80 %; serious injuries in this region can cause paralysis and death [5].

Considering surgical aspects, AAD can be divided into two categories i.e., reducible (RAADs) and irreducible (IAADs). IAAD may be due to inadequate extension in dynamic X-ray which may also be due to muscle spasm. Posterior fusion is the treatment of choice for RAAD [6].

In literature we find only three case reported with different option of management. What constitutes appropriate management in cases of traumatic C2-C3 subluxation with Atlato-axial dislocation is still controversial due to the infrequency of this injury.

However, we report a very rare case of C2-C3 subluxation with reducible Atlanto-axial dislocation (RAADs) managed successfully surgically through posterior approach successfully.

This work has been reported in line with the SCARE 2020 criteria [8].

Case presentation

History and presentation

A 45-year-old heavy worker presented with history of two episodes of trauma in the form of ground level fall with heavy objects in head in last 1 year followed by Neck pain, gradually progressive weakness of all the four limbs for 6 months and Numbness and tingling sensation of all 4 limbs for last 2 months. The symptoms gradually progressive. But he does not need assistance while walking. On query, he also gave a history of constipation for the last 2 months, but his bladder function is normal.

Physical examination

Examination of the extremities revealed bilaterally bulk reduced, tone increased, muscle power MRC grade 4, jerks in upper limb were reduced but in lower limb all jerks were exaggerated, planter reflexes were extensor bilaterally. Sensory level at C3.

Imaging studies

Radiography cervical spine shows subluxation of C2 over C3 without instability on dynamic view but AAD seen which is reducible on dynamic view. CT scan of cervical spine shows increased Atlanto-dental interval with basilar invagination MRI of cervical spine shows loss of CSF column with compression of spinal cord at C2–3 level and subluxation of C2 over C3 level (Fig. 1).Fig. 1A) X-ray cervical spine antero-posterior and lateral view. Finally, he was diagnosed as a case of compressive cervical myelopathy due to traumatic subluxation of C2 over C3 vertebra with locked facet (Hangman's fracture type 3) with atlantoaxial dislocation with basilar invagination (reducible variety).

Operation procedure

After anesthesia of the patient, we gave Skeletal Traction (Tong Traction) and tried to make reduction. But after giving proper weight we gained minimum reduction which is not helpful so we decided to destruction of the joint of C1, C2 and C3 per-operatively so that we can achieve proper reduction. We positioned the patient in the prone. After proper dissection Occiput to C4 vertebra was exposed. We distract the C1-C2 lateral joint and C2-C3 lateral joint with the help of fine bony chisel. After complete destruction of the joint, we tried to make reduction gradually and we achieved the good reduction. Then we drill the C1-C2 lateral joint to remove the endplate. And did the same procedure between C2-C3 lateral joint. We put a 4 mm thick titanium spacer between C1-C2 lateral joint. Then we put C1 lateral mass screw which was 4 mm in diameter and 28 mm in length and C2 pedicle screw which was 4 mm in diameter and 26 mm in length. Then we introduce C3 and C4 lateral mass screw which was 4 mm in diameter and 14 mm in length. After proper construction of the rod we achieved a very good reduction. Then we impacted bone chips harvested from right iliac fossa between the C2-C3 lateral joint. Then we drill the C1 posterior arch and C2-C3 lamina to make the surface raw. Then we impacted bony chips between C1 posterior arch and C2-C3 lamina (Fig. 2). We remove the skeletal traction at the end of the operation.Fig. 2Per-operative picture.Fig. 2

Postoperative course

The postoperative course was uneventful. Patient was advised to use Philadelphia Cervical Collar for One months and soft collar for another two months. Patients work without any support one day after the operation.

Follow up

We follow up the patient one month, three-month, six month and one year up to two years after operation. We did x-ray and CT scan in every follow up and image shows very good position and fusion of the joint (Fig. 3, Fig. 4). The patient works immediately after the operation and one month he becomes near normal (Fig. 5). Discussion

Prevalence

Traumatic subluxation of the C2 vertebra is due to fractures of the lamina, articular facets, pedicles, or pars interarticularis and was first described by Bouvier in1843 [9]. After odontoid fracture is the second most common fracture of C2 vertebra in adults [10]. “Hangman fracture first describe in 1965 by Schneiderused due to similarities in the fractures associated with judicial hanging [11]. Devastating consequence of upper cervical spine injury include tetraplegia, functional loss, and permanent disability and due to respiratory paralysis morality rate is high [12]. There is no reported type of subluxation in C2-C3 in the literature.

Atlantoaxial dislocation is the instability between the atlas and axis (C1–C2) with disruption of normal articulation and may be due to traumatic, inflammatory, idiopathic, or congenital abnormalities [13]. A purely traumatic etiology in the absence of another predisposing factor is extremely rare. Variation in the tensile strength of the transverse atlantal ligament, ligamentous laxity and osseous abnormalities are congenitally at risk for development of atlantoaxial dislocations following even trivial trauma. Biomechanical studies suggested that flexion injuries are primarily responsible for both odontoid fractures and traumatic atlantoaxial dislocations [14]. Diagnosis can commonly be made on lateral cervical x-ray. When the Atlanto-dental interval (ADI) is greater than 3 mm, it is generally recommended to obtain flexion/extension views to demonstrate the instability [15].

Traumatic C—C3 subluxation with AAD is a very rare condition. There has been 3 cases reported till now. Han et al. presented Traumatic bilateral atlantoaxial rotatory subluxation with hangman fracture in an adult [17]. Chaudhary et al. presented a case of Traumatic atlantoaxial dislocation with Hangman fracture [18]. Faghih-Jouybari M et al. presented a case of Hangman's Fracture with Con-comitant Atlantoaxial Dislocation [19]. We reported a very unique case where there is near complete subluxation of C2-C3 with AAD with basilar invagination due to trauma.

Treatment

In reviewing our patient's injury, the unusual orientation of the C2-C3 subluxation in radiography with the associated AAD rendering the segment unstable and significantly influencing optimal therapeutic choice for the patient. Treatment of traumatic C2-C3 subluxation with AAD aims to correct the sagittal alignment of the upper cervical spine, stabilize it to near anatomical alignment, and decompress the neural structure. Reduction is very important and challenging in case of traumatic C2-C3 subluxation with AAD. Some authors highlighted application of skeletal traction for reduction [20]. If manual reduction fail and try to reduction under general anesthesia and image guided traction [21]. Goel and Laheri described joint destruction for better reduction with lateral screw instrumentation [22]. Harms technique stabilizes the C1-C2 region by fixation with polyaxial screws and rods from the dorsal approach, effectively stabilizing the atlantoaxial complex [22].

In three similar cases before ours, one was reported to be reduced after 3 weeks of bidirectional cervical traction and another two cases were managed by open reduction and stabilization [[16], [17], [18], [19]].

In our patient we did destruction of C1, C2, C3 joint and reduction it through gentle traction and counter-traction and did C1, C3, C4 lateral mass and C2 pedicle screw through posterior approach for fixation. We achieved beautiful reduction and stabilization and with complete neurological recovery.

After 6 months and one year and two years follow up, the patient shows significant fusion and near complete recovery.

Conclusion

Our management through posterior approach between C1 to C4 shows very good outcome with proper fusion. But it needs proper understanding the anatomy and mechanism of reduction by careful reading the image. Its needs more case description and management to establish a standard treatment for this type of disease.

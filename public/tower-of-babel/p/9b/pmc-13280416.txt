To Evaluate Clinical Performance of Preformed Zirconia Crowns, Computer-aided Design and Computer-aided Manufacturing Zirconia Crowns, and Bioflx Crowns in Primary Molars Postendodontic Therapy
Authors: Rishabh Solanki, Nidhi Gupta, Natasha Ghambir, Divya Singh, Priyanka Sachdeva
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimTo evaluate and compare the clinical performance of preformed zirconia crowns, computer-aided design and computer-aided manufacturing (CAD/CAM) zirconia crowns, and preformed resin polymer crowns in primary molars following endodontic therapy.Materials and methodsThis comparative study was conducted on 36 primary molars requiring full coronal coverage after pulp therapy. The teeth were randomly assigned into three groups (n = 12 each): Group A (CAD/CAM zirconia crowns), group B (preformed zirconia crowns), and group C (Bioflx crowns). Clinical parameters including color match, crown retention, marginal integrity, gingival health, and parental satisfaction were assessed at baseline, 3, 6, 9, and 12 months.ResultsGroup A consistently showed superior color stability and parental satisfaction over time, with all crowns maintaining color match at 12 months. Group B also performed well but showed slight translucency changes in some cases. Group C demonstrated the incidence of color changes and lower parental satisfaction. Statistically significant differences in color stability were observed at 9 and 12 months (p = 0.034 and p = 0.0001, respectively). Crown retention and marginal integrity remained acceptable in all groups, with no significant differences. Parental satisfaction was highest in groups A and B, particularly regarding aesthetics and comfort.ConclusionCAD/CAM zirconia crowns exhibited the best overall clinical performance, especially in terms of color stability and aesthetics, followed closely by preformed zirconia crowns. Bioflx crowns were clinically acceptable but comparatively inferior in long-term aesthetic outcomes.How to cite this articleSolanki R, Gupta N, Ghambir N, et al. Introduction

Dental caries continues to be one of the most widespread diseases affecting young children. In primary teeth, the progression of caries can be soft once it begins. Early childhood caries (ECC) is a major global health concern, occurring 5 times more frequently than asthma and 20 times more often than juvenile diabetes.1 ECC significantly affects the quality of life of both the children suffering from it and their families. Dental caries can affect the primary dentition soon after eruption if adequate hygiene habits and healthy nutrition are not established at an early age. Although preventive care has improved and led to a decline in dental caries among children under the age of 3, research continues to show that caries remains the most prevalent oral health issue among children in many regions worldwide.2,3 Untreated decay in primary teeth can lead to pain, high treatment costs, missed school or work, and future caries risk. Primary teeth are essential for chewing, speech, esthetics, and maintaining space for permanent teeth, making their timely care crucial.4

Stainless steel crowns (SSCs) are the most widely used restorations for preserving and restoring severely decayed or damaged primary teeth following endodontic treatment. Introduced to pediatric dentistry in 1947 by Engel and later popularized by Humphrey in 1950, SSCs have consistently outperformed alternatives like amalgam and composite in terms of strength and longevity for over 50 years. Their key advantages include affordability, reliability, and durability, making them a preferred option for interim full-coronal coverage.5 Substantial scientific evidence supports the use of SSCs, especially in young children at high risk for dental caries. However, despite these benefits, some practitioners are hesitant to use them due to drawbacks such as their unaesthetic metallic appearance and longer chairside time.5

An esthetic alternative to SSCs is zirconia crowns. These are more widely adopted by clinicians as zirconia crowns overcome the disadvantages of SSCs. These are more esthetic to the patient and the chairside time is also reduced.5 Zirconia crowns, often referred to as “ceramic steel,” offer a combination of superior esthetics and outstanding mechanical strength, making them suitable for restoring both primary and permanent teeth. These crowns are anatomically contoured, metal-free, bioinert, tooth-colored, and highly resistant to dental caries.6 Another viable and esthetic option is the Bioflx crown. These materials are composed of polymers—long, structured chains of monomers chemically bonded together. Acrylic resin was the first widely accepted dental polymer introduced to the market, valued for its ease of handling, simple manufacturing process, and pleasing esthetics.7

With recent advancements, the use of dental computer-aided design and computer-aided manufacturing (CAD/CAM) systems has expanded significantly, not only in fabricating crowns and fixed partial dentures (FPDs) but also across various other domains in dentistry. CAD/CAM technology delivers innovative, state-of-the-art dental care, enhancing both treatment precision and workflow efficiency. It plays a vital role in improving patients' overall health and quality of life (QOL). The term CAD/CAM, derived from machine-tool technology, stands for “computer-aided design/computer-aided manufacturing” and refers to the digital planning of restorations followed by their automated fabrication using computer-controlled machinery.8 In particular, CAD/CAM technology allows for the precise fabrication of zirconia crowns by compensating for material shrinkage, producing restorations that are pore-free and superior to those made by traditional sintering, casting, or pressing methods. In pediatric dentistry, CAD/CAM zirconia crowns offer a highly esthetic and adaptable restorative option.9 This study aims to evaluate the clinical performance of preformed zirconia crowns, CAD/CAM zirconia crowns, and Bioflx crowns in endodontically treated primary molars.

Materials and Methods

This comparative clinical study, designed in a parallel-group format, was conducted in the Department of Pediatric and Preventive Dentistry at Santosh Dental College and Hospitals, Ghaziabad. Ethical approval was obtained from the Institutional Ethical Committee (SU/2023/2480[12]), and written informed consent was secured from the parents or guardians of all participants prior to initiating treatment.

Sample Selection

A total of 36 children, aged 6–10 years old, requiring full-coronal restorations in mandibular primary molars following endodontic therapy were selected (Fig. 1). Participants were equally assigned into three equal groups (n = 12 each) using a concealed envelope allocation method to ensure allocation concealment and minimize the risk of selection bias. A total of 36 identical, opaque, and sequentially numbered envelopes were prepared in advance and evenly distributed into three sets of 12 each corresponding to one of the intervention groups. These envelopes were shuffled and drawn sequentially by an independent investigator not involved in participant recruitment or data collection, thereby ensuring an unbiased and unpredictable assignment process.

Clinical Procedure

Group A (CAD/CAM zirconia crowns): Tooth preparation involves uniform reduction with smooth, rounded margins, typically 1.5–2 mm occlusal and 1–1.5 mm axial. Clear finish lines like chamfer or shoulder are preferred. After preparation, the area is dried and scanned using an intraoral scanner to capture a precise digital impression. The scan is reviewed, and the crown is digitally designed and milled accordingly (Figs 2A and B).

Group B (preformed zirconia crown): Crown preparation for a preformed zirconia crown requires circumferential tooth reduction of 1–2 mm with rounded line angles and no sharp edges. An occlusal reduction of approximately 1.5–2 mm is performed, followed by the preparation of feather-edge or slightly subgingival margins. The crown is then tried in to assess the fit, and minor adjustments are made as needed. Once optimal fit is achieved, the crown is cemented using resin-modified glass ionomer cement (Figs 3A and B).

Margins are kept at or slightly subgingival. After finishing the preparation, the crown is selected, trimmed, and tried in for fit and occlusion. Statistical Analysis

Data were compiled and statistically analyzed using SPSS 30 version. Intergroup comparisons were performed using analysis of variance (ANOVA) and Chi-squared tests. A p-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

The present study evaluated and compared the clinical performance of three different full-coronal restorative options—CAD/CAM zirconia crowns (group A), preformed zirconia crowns (group B), and preformed resin polymer (Bioflx) crowns (group C)—in endodontically treated primary mandibular molars over a follow-up period of 12 months using modified USPHS criteria.

Each crown type was assessed based on key clinical parameters including marginal adaptation, color matching, decementation, and parental satisfaction. The observations recorded at baseline and during follow-up intervals were analyzed and compared statistically to determine the performance and suitability of each crown type in pediatric restorative dentistry.

Discussion

Restoring primary molars after endodontic therapy is crucial for preserving occlusion and preventing space loss. SSCs have been the traditional choice due to durability and cost-effectiveness. However, aesthetic concerns have driven interest in pre-formed zirconia crowns, CAD/CAM zirconia crowns, and Bioflx crowns (Rahate et al.).12

Restoring primary molars after endodontic therapy is crucial for preserving occlusion and preventing space loss. SSCs have been the traditional choice due to durability and cost-effectiveness. However, aesthetic concerns have driven interest in pre-formed zirconia crowns, CAD/CAM zirconia crowns, and Bioflx crowns (Rahate et al.).12

In this present study, the following parameters of modified USPHS criteria were used to evaluate various crowns, that is, marginal adaptation, color matching, crown retention or decementation, and parental satisfaction using Likert scale.

Marginal Adaptation

In the present study all three groups showed excellent marginal adaptation with no marginal gap at different time intervals up to 1 year postcrown cementation.

According to Beuer et al. CAD/CAM zirconia crowns showed excellent marginal adaptation, with marginal gaps well within the clinically acceptable range (under 120 µm), and most restorations scoring “Alpha” under the USPHS criteria, indicating no detectable margin discrepancies.13 Similarly, Memari et al. confirmed that CAD/CAM zirconia crowns maintained superior marginal integrity throughout the ceramic firing process.14

In the case of preformed zirconia crowns, Alzahrani et al. evaluated full-coverage zirconia crowns in molar incisor hypomineralization–affected molars over 24 months and found that 92% of the crowns scored “Alpha” for marginal adaptation, indicating excellent fit and clinical success.15 Habib et al. also reported high clinical performance of monolithic zirconia crowns in children, with marginal adaptation rated ideal in most cases.16

For Bioflx crowns, the study by Leith and Tandon demonstrated that while Bioflx crowns showed satisfactory marginal adaptation comparable to stainless steel crowns, 25% of cases were rated as “Bravo,” suggesting minor marginal discrepancies yet still clinically acceptable.17 Deolikar et al. also found that Bioflx crowns performed well overall, though marginal integrity was slightly inferior compared to zirconia crowns.18

These findings collectively suggest that CAD/CAM zirconia crowns offer the best marginal adaptation, followed closely by preformed zirconia crowns, while Bioflx crowns, although acceptable, exhibit more marginal variability.

Color Matching

In the present study, from baseline to 3 months, most crowns matched adjacent teeth with no significant differences. At 6 months, groups A and B remained stable; group C showed slight translucency changes, still not significant. At 9 and 12 months, group A maintained full color match, while groups B and C showed decreased matching with significant differences, especially at 12 months (p = 0.0001).

In a study by Habib et al. CAD/CAM zirconia crowns demonstrated excellent color stability and high aesthetic satisfaction, with 91.6% of crowns showing ideal color match at 12 months follow-up, as rated by parents and clinicians using USPHS criteria.16 Beuer et al. also observed that CAD/CAM zirconia crowns provide consistent shade uniformity due to controlled milling and sintering processes.13

Preformed zirconia crowns have also shown favorable aesthetic outcomes. According to Alshahrani et al. 85% of preformed zirconia crowns exhibited acceptable or excellent color match, though minor mismatch was sometimes noted due to limited shade availability.19 Similarly, Walia et al. reported that 90% of children's parents rated the preformed zirconia crowns as highly aesthetic, especially in anterior teeth.20

For Bioflx crowns, Abdelhafez and Dhar reported that 58.3% of Bioflx crowns showed complete color match to adjacent teeth at 12 months, while the remaining displayed slight changes in translucency over time.21 Patil et al. found Bioflx crowns to have satisfactory aesthetics, but their color stability was relatively less compared to zirconia crowns, possibly due to material flexibility and composite composition.22

These findings indicate that CAD/CAM zirconia crowns outperform others in color matching and long-term shade stability, followed closely by preformed zirconia crowns, while Bioflx crowns offer acceptable but comparatively lower aesthetic performance.

Crown Cementation/Decementation

CAD/CAM zirconia crowns demonstrate excellent retention due to precise digital fit and adhesive bonding. In a study by Habib et al. no decementation was observed over 12 months in pediatric patients with monolithic zirconia crowns fabricated via CAD/CAM technology. The study concluded that “CAD/CAM crowns had superior mechanical retention and resistance to debonding.”16

Preformed zirconia crowns also show high retention when proper tooth preparation and luting protocols are followed. Walia et al. evaluated the clinical performance of preformed zirconia crowns over 12 months and found no cases of decementation.20 Similarly, Kupietzky et al. reported a 100% retention rate for EZ-Pedo zirconia crowns over a 6-month period, citing their retentive design and internal surface roughness as key factors.23

In Bioflx crowns, retention is generally good but slightly less predictable due to their flexible nature. Abdelhafez and Dhar reported one case of decementation at the 9-month follow-up among Bioflx crowns, attributing it to compromised moisture control and marginal adaptation.21 Patil et al. noted that while Bioflx crowns provided acceptable retention, they required more precise technique and isolation to prevent cement failure.22

Overall, studies suggest CAD/CAM zirconia crowns have the highest retention due to custom fit, followed by preformed zirconia crowns, with Bioflx crowns performing satisfactorily but requiring careful technique.

Parental Satisfaction

In the present study, parental satisfaction was high in all groups. Most parents were pleased with crown color (58.3% strongly agreed, 41.7% agreed), and all (100%) strongly agreed on accurate crown size and shape. No eating discomfort was reported. Treatment discomfort varied: group A showed mixed responses, group B was split, and group C reported none. The difference was statistically significant (p = 0.0001).

In a study by Habib et al. parental satisfaction with CAD/CAM zirconia crowns was assessed using a 5-point Likert scale. Results showed that 91.6% of parents “strongly agreed” that the color, fit, and aesthetics of the crowns were excellent. The study concluded that CAD/CAM crowns provided “high parental satisfaction due to superior aesthetics and minimal discomfort during treatment.”16

Walia et al. conducted a randomized clinical trial evaluating aesthetic full-coronal restorations in children with preformed zirconia crowns. Using the Likert scale, over 85% of parents reported being “very satisfied” with the size, shape, and color of the preformed zirconia crowns. Additionally, no complaints were recorded regarding child discomfort, and parents preferred zirconia over stainless steel in terms of appearance.20

In a recent study by Abdelhafez and Dhar, parental satisfaction with Bioflx crowns was assessed over 12 months using a Likert scale questionnaire. Deolikar et al. observed that at 12 months, 58.3% of parents “strongly agreed” that the color of Bioflx crowns matched natural teeth, and 100% “strongly agreed” on the size and shape. However, slight concern about translucency change over time was noted by some. Overall satisfaction remained high, with no reported discomfort during eating or function.

Conclusion

In summary, CAD/CAM zirconia crowns provide superior fit, esthetics, and durability, though cost and fabrication time are drawbacks. Preformed zirconia crowns are durable but require more tooth reduction. Bioflx crowns are conservative and easy to place but require further long-term validation.

Patient Consent Statement

Informed consent was voluntarily obtained from the parents or legal guardians of all pediatric participants involved in this study. They were fully informed about the study's nature, objectives, procedures, potential risks, and benefits. The confidentiality of all patient-related information was strictly upheld throughout the duration of the study.

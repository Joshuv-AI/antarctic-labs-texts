New Species and a New Record of the Genus Hydropunctaria from South Korea, with an Updated Key to All Known Species
Authors: Jizhen Jin, Santosh Joshi, Jae-Seoun Hur
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHydropunctaria (Verrucariaceae, Ascomycota) is currently circumscribed by 11 species, of which four are known from Asia. The taxonomy and species limits of this genus remain insufficiently understood, particularly in East Asia. Phylogenetic study based on three loci (ITS, mtSSU, and LSU) from populations collected at several localities in South Korea revealed the presence of a hitherto unrecognized species, here described as new species, Hydropunctaria fluminicola sp. nov., collected from fresh water regimes near the littoral zone. The species is characterized by well-developed, gray-green, subgelatinous thallus having distinct punctae and black ridges; algal cells are arranged in columns and perithecia conspicuously protrude above the thallus surface. In addition, a new country record of Hydropunctaria nipponoamphibia is reported. A taxonomic key to all known species of Hydropunctaria is also provided. The present study highlights the taxonomic importance of combining morphological and molecular evidence in resolving species boundaries within Hydropunctaria and contributes to a better understanding of lichen diversity in coastal regions of East Asia.Keywords: Fresh water, lichenized-fungi, new record, phylogeny, taxonomy, Verrucariaceae

Introduction

The family Verrucariaceae represents a diverse group of lichenized fungi distributed across a wide range of terrestrial and aquatic habitats [1–3]. Traditionally, classification within the family was based primarily on morphological characters; however, the integration of molecular phylogenetic approaches has significantly improved the understanding of generic boundaries and evolutionary relationships [4–9].

Hydropunctaria is a relatively well-defined genus within Verrucariaceae, comprising species typically inhabiting marine littoral and freshwater-influenced environments. Since its establishment by Gueidan et al. [5], the genus has undergone several taxonomic revisions, including the addition of new species and the reassignment of taxa previously treated under Verrucaria [10–17], as currently documented in Index Fungorum [18].

Species of Hydropunctaria are commonly associated with coastal marine environments, particularly in the supralittoral and littoral zones, although several taxa have also been reported from freshwater-influenced habitats [19,20]. The ecological distribution of species across marine and freshwater gradients has been noted in previous studies [14,21,22], but its relationship with phylogenetic patterns remains insufficiently understood. This highlights the need for integrative studies combining ecological, morphological, and molecular data.

In the Republic of Korea, species currently assigned to Hydropunctaria were historically treated under the genus Verrucaria due to taxonomic limitations and lack of molecular data. Previous studies on Korean lichens [23] reported the occurrence of several taxa corresponding to H. maura, H. amphibia, and H. rheitrophila, although these were not explicitly treated under Hydropunctaria at that time. Subsequent taxonomic revisions based on molecular and morphological evidence have clarified their placement within Hydropunctaria [5,14].

The present study aims to investigate the diversity of Hydropunctaria in South Korea using a combination of morphological and multilocus phylogenetic analyses (ITS, mtSSU, and nrLSU). Specifically, we (i) assess species boundaries based on morphological and anatomical characters, (ii) reconstruct phylogenetic relationships using multilocus sequence data (ITS, mtSSU, and nrLSU), and (iii) provide an updated taxonomic key incorporating newly recognized taxa.

Materials and methods

Sampling, morpho-anatomical, and chemical study

The field survey was conducted in and around the coastal littoral zone of South Korea (Figure 1), and fresh samples were collected and deposited in the herbarium of Honam National Institute of Biological Resources (HNIBR), Mokpo-si, South Korea. A total of 85 specimens of Hydropunctaria were initially collected from 15 sampling localities along the western and southern coastal regions of South Korea during the period from March 2024 to July 2025. Sampling was conducted across littoral and supralittoral habitats, including freshwater-influenced coastal rock surfaces. For proper identification of the specimens, morphological characters were examined with a Nikon SMZ645 stereomicroscope (Tokyo, Japan) dissecting microscope. Anatomical descriptions were studied under a Nikon Eclipse E200 compound microscope (Tokyo, Japan). Photographs were captured using a NIKON Eclipse 50i microscope equipped with a NIKON digital camera. Thin-layer chromatography (TLC) was performed to identify secondary metabolites, using solvent system C (toluene:acetic acid = 85:15), following Orange et al. [24]. The reaction of the medulla and surface of the thallus to application of aqueous solutions of sodium hypochlorite (C) and 10% potassium hydroxide (K) was also observed for confirmation of the taxa.

DNA extraction, purification, and sequencing

Total genomic DNA was extracted from 85 specimens using Soltis Lab CTAB DNA Extraction Protocol, following the manufacturer’s protocol, and no commercial extraction kit was used. After DNA barcoding and quality filtering, 29 sequences from 11 specimens were utilized for subsequent phylogenetic analyses (Table S1). Three loci were amplified: the mitochondrial SSU gene with primer pairs mtSSU1 and mtSSU3R; the internal transcribed spacer region (ITS1, 5.8S, and ITS2) with ITS1F and ITS4, without excluding intron regions during alignment; and the 50 portions end of the 28S, including the D1–D2 domain (nrLSU), with LR0R and LR5. PCR amplification was performed for 20 μL reactions; containing: 16 μL of ddH2O, 1 μL of each primer, 2 μL of DNA, and AccuPower PCR PreMix (Bioneer, Daejeon, South Korea). PCR amplification was conducted using GeneAmp 2720 Thermal Cycler machine (Applied Biosystems, Carlsbad, CA); the conditions for PCR of nrITS, nrLSU, and mtSSU were set for an initial denaturation at 94 °C for 10 min, followed by 34 cycles of denaturation at 95 °C for 45 s, annealing at 50 °C for 45 s, extension at 72 °C for 90 s and a final extension at 72 °C for 10 min [25]. PCR products were checked by 1% agarose gel electrophoresis and sequenced by Macrogen genomic research company (Seoul, Republic of Korea). The identity of specimens used in this research, along with voucher information and GenBank accession numbers, is provided (Table S1).

Phylogenetic analysis

The raw sequences were initially checked with the BLAST tool on the NCBI online service (https://blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Blast.cgi) to eliminate any sequences which did not belong to lichenized fungi [26]. The DNA sequences were aligned using MAFFT v. 7.107, using the following parameters: algorithm = auto; scoring matrix = 200 PAM/k = 2; gap open penalty = 1.53; offset value = 0.123 [27]. The phylogenetic tree for each fragment was first constructed according to the single gene data, and then extreme outlying sequences were deleted or corrected. Single-gene phylogenetic trees were examined prior to concatenating the matrices. Empty gapped columns in the concatenated dataset were removed using TrimAl [28], a concatenated matrix was generated, and the final alignment of 1852 bp across 245 taxa was based on three loci (nrITS, 1–596; mtSSU, 597–1251; and nrLSU, 1252–1852). Subsequently, a maximum-likelihood (ML) tree and a Bayesian inference (BI) tree were constructed. Based on the lowest Bayesian information criterion (BIC), the partitioned analysis was selected using ModelFinder in IQ-TREE v.1.6.12 for the subsequent ML and BI analyses [29]. For ML analyses, TIM2 + F + I + G4 was used for ITS, TIM3 + F + I + R2 for mtSSU, and TIM2e + R3 for nrLSU. For BI analyses, GTR + F + I + G4 was used for ITS, with mtSSU and nrLSU. The ML analyses were performed using the IQ-TREE web server. The BI analyses were performed using MrBayes v. 3.2.7, using three Markov chains running for 10 million generations for the concatenated dataset [30]. The trees were sampled every 100 generations, with the first 25% of all trees discarded as burn-in. Posterior probabilities (PPs) were obtained from the 95% majority rule consensus tree of all saved trees. All of the above operations are completed on the PhyloSuite platform [31]. Tree files were visualized using FigTree 1.4.2 [32].

Results

To construct a phylogenetic tree among genera of Verrucariaceae, 339 publicly available sequences were used (see Table S1). After removing ambiguously aligned positions, the final concatenated matrix, 126 species representing 23 genera, contains: 564 bp of ITS, 662 bp of mtSSU, and 600 bp of LSU.

In the three locus concatenated phylogenetic tree, Hydropunctaria formed a monophyletic clade and the placement of species is clarified (Figure 2). Consistent with previous molecular investigations, phylogenetic reconstruction shows limited resolution of deep-level relationships within the Verrucariaceae [5,14]. Despite this basal instability, all major long-recognized genera were recovered as monophyletic with high support, confirming that the current lack of resolution is confined to the deepest nodes of the family tree. In all cases, the samples from South Korea are nested within Hydropunctaria with high support value (ML = 100%, PP = 1), share a common ancestor with other species of Hydropunctaria and compose a sister group to Wahlenbergiella (Figure 2).

ML bootstrap support values/Bayesian posterior probability ≥70%/0.80 are indicated. Maximum-likelihood bootstrap values and posterior probabilities are shown near the nodes.

The Hydropunctaria species found in South Korea primarily comprise two distinct lineages: H. nipponoamphibia and H. fluminicola. The ITS-based phylogeny showed partial congruence with the multilocus tree but differed in the placement of certain taxa, reflecting the limited resolution of single-locus datasets. In the ITS phylogenetic tree, the new taxon forms a sister group to H. rheitrophila and H. nipponoamphibia forms a sister group to H. aractina (Figure 3). No consistent phylogenetic separation between marine and freshwater taxa was observed. Ecological traits such as habitat preference did not show a clear correspondence with phylogenetic relationships and should therefore be interpreted cautiously.

Morphologically, the new taxon is characterized by its well-developed gray-green thallus, with a distinct black ridge; perithecia conspicuously protrude from the thallus. There are some common taxonomic features of the new species that differ from species in other genera, such as thallus being subgelatinous when wet, interrupted by black punctae or columns, and algal cells usually arranged in a vertical column. Thallus saxicolous, epilithic, continuous to slightly rimose, crustose, olivaceous green to greenish-gray when dry, thin, 50–90 μm thick in cross section, tightly adnate to the rock surface, surface smooth to slightly uneven, dull, potentially inundated, divided by distinct black ridges, with sparse, scattered black punctae, ≤0.02 mm in size. Prothallus absent or indistinct. Cortex absent, sometimes inconspicuous, 10–20 μm thick, outermost layer sometimes present, K–; algal cells globose, ≤5 μm in diameter, arranged in columns to sometimes in clusters, embedded with black punctae, K–; medulla indistinct to crystalline; basal parts of thallus colorless lacking black basal layer.

Perithecia numerous, conspicuous, slightly protuberant at first, becoming hemispherical and prominent above the thallus when mature, isolated or in clusters of 2–4, with a slightly flattened to micro-convex top and a circular base, c. 100–200 μm diameter; apex slightly depressed, plane to distinctly round at maturity of perithecium; ostiole concolorous, inconspicuous, ≤8 μm diameter. Involucrellum black, well-developed, smooth, enveloping the protruding perithecium, fused with the outermost thalline layer, laterally expanded, pachydermatous, 45–65 × 150–200 μm in size. Thalline exciple distinct to absent in mature perithecium, lacking radiating veins. Proper exciple well developed, brownish, smooth, ≤20 μm thick, internally lined with slightly branched periphyses at apices, ≤30 μm long; filaments beneath the involucrellum and outside the excipulum proper are well-developed, similar to the surrounding medullary hyphae, brownish, and lacking symbiotic algae; hymenium hyaline, inspersed with oil-droplets, typically 100–80 μm, K–, I + red-brown, K/I + blue; hypothecium flat, 7.5–12.5 μm thick in the central part. Asci clavate, bitunicate, fissitunicate, eight-spored, 50 × 10 μm; ascospores hyaline, simple, globose to ellipsoidal, sometimes appear bacilliform in shape, (10.5–) 11–15.5–20 (–22.8) × (5.5–) 6.5–8.25–10 (–11.3) μm, (0.93–) 1.25–1.68–2.12 (–2.64) times as long as wide, with a uniformly thin wall, c. 0.5–0.6 μm thick, halo (perispore) absent. Pycnidia not observed.

Chemistry. Thallus K–, P–, C–; no chemical substances detected by standard spot tests and TLC.

Etymology. The specific epithet is derived from Latin words flumen (river, stream) and -cola (dweller or inhabitant), referring to new taxon habitat influenced by freshwater rivulets.

Habitat and distribution. The new species is known from the western and southern coastal zone in South Korea, where it was widely dispersed on rocks in association with Endocarpon sp. The new taxon mostly occurs on coast rocks of metamorphic and sedimentary origin. The predominant occurrence of Hydropunctaria fluminicola on inclined rock surfaces near freshwater rivulets suggests the preferred habitat of the new species. The type locality of the new taxon falls into supralittoral zone, and hardly experience marine water splash.

Notes. The new species is placed in Hydropunctaria based on the combination of a crustose, saxicolous, epilithic thallus, a subgelatinous consistency when moist, an inconspicuous cortex, vertically arranged algal cells, well-developed involucrellum, fissitunicate asci, and simple hyaline ascospores lacking perispore. Within the genus, it belongs to the assemblage of species characterized by thin, largely continuous thalli with punctate to weakly ridged surface structures.

The diagnostic features of the new taxon include gray-green, continuous thallus lacking black basal layer, surface with numerous flexuous black ridges, punctae embedded in algal layer, conspicuously conical and prominent perithecia, hyaline ascospores 11–20 × 6.5–10 μm, and a freshwater habitat.

Among currently known species, Hydropunctaria fluminicola is phylogenetically and morphologically closest to H. rheitrophila, and shares some taxonomical characters in having subgelatinous thallus when wet, ill-defined cortex (brownish, when present), columnar arrangement of algal cells and occurrence in freshwater habitat. However, H. rheitrophila can easily be distinguished from the new species by a combination of characters. Hydropunctaria rheitrophila possesses a well characterized whitish, non-fimbriate prothallus, which is clearly absent in the new taxon. The brownish-green to dark brown thallus of H. rheitrophila is often cracked, while new species has an olivaceous green to greenish-gray continuous and uncracked thallus surface. Moreover, H. rheitrophila has been reported with immersed perithecia forming very low projections and that are usually too indistinct to measure [33], which are prominent and emergent in H. fluminicola. Further, the new taxon can also be separated from H. rheitrophila in ascospore size. The ascospores of the new taxon are larger and more variable in shape (11–20 × 6.5–10 μm, globose to ellipsoidal), but spores of H. rheitrophila are comparatively smaller (11–14 × 6.5–8 μm) and mostly ellipsoid.

Another fresh water species Hydropunctaria scabra also resembles the new taxon in having gray-green, thin, uncracked thallus, and ascospores roughly of similar range, 11.0–21.5 × 7.0–10.5 μm, but is clearly distinguished from the new species by its verrucose thallus, the frequent presence of a black basal layer, and delimitation by a whitish prothallus [14,33]. Further, the perithecia of H. scabra are completely immersed within thalline warts and only rarely become slightly projecting. In the new species, the perithecia become conspicuously emergent at maturity.

The East-Asian Hydropunctaria nipponoamphibia also shows similarity with the new taxon. Both taxa possess a thin, continuous, non-areolate thallus that is tightly adnate to the substrate and subgelatinous when wet, its surface interrupted by punctae or short ridges rather than by cracks; a weakly developed or scarcely differentiated cortex; arrangement of algal cells in vertical columns and perithecia that are initially immersed, with a concolorous and inconspicuous ostiolar region. However, the new species differs from H. nipponoamphibia in having several well-defined characteristics. A prothallus is consistently absent in the new species, whereas it appears as a marginal boundary in H. nipponoamphibia. Additionally, perithecia in the new species become distinctly hemispherical and prominently emergent above the thallus at maturity, while in H. nipponoamphibia they remain immersed or partly exposed [34]. The involucrellum of the new species is conspicuously pachydermatous and laterally expanded, enveloping the protruding perithecium more completely. Notably, the new species was observed growing commonly together with the members of Endocarpon, whereas, the majority of the collected specimens of H. nipponoamphibia occurred in microhabitats shared with Wahlenbergiella species.

The new species can also be compared with Hydropunctaria orae in possessing some morpho-anatomical similarities, viz., a thin greenish-gray thallus ornamented by punctae or minute ridges on surface, and larger ascospores within the range of the new species. In contrast, H. orae is characterized by a thallus developing with cracks when fresh, particularly in thicker parts, and in the presence of pigmented punctae projecting upward from the basal layer. Moreover, H. orae ascospores in maturity commonly contain oil droplets, a feature not observed in the new species. Nevertheless, the species is more restricted to marine habitats of North Atlantic region [33].

Hydropunctaria alaskana is more readily separated from the new species by its more robust thallus, more strongly developed perithecia, and its occurrence in northern marine environments, which contrasts with the habitat of the new taxon.

New record

Thallus crustose, medium to thick, continuous to occasionally sparsely cracked, black or greenish-black when dry, becoming dark greenish-black and subgelatinous when wet; surface smooth, glossy to dull, with numerous dense, punctiform black dots, c. 20 µm diameter. Perithecia slightly elevated above the thallus, 0.3–0.8 mm diameter; apex plane to shallowly concave. Involucrellum well developed, smooth, broadly expanded and fused with the surrounding thalline tissue; ostiole inconspicuous. Hymenium hyaline, c. 210 µm high; periphyses sparsely branched or unbranched. Asci clavate, eight-spored; ascospores hyaline, simple, ellipsoid to ovoid, (10.5–) 11–15 (–18.7) × (5.8–) 6.5–7.5 (–7.8) µm, with thin walls. Pycnidia scattered, often incomplete; conidia 2–4 × 1 µm.

Chemistry. No chemicals.

Habitat and distribution. Occurring in the supralittoral zone on coastal rocks near sea level (c. 0–2 m a.s.l.), where rock surfaces are regularly soaked, and typically forming large black patches in association with Wahlenbergiella sp.

Notes. The characteristics of the new report were specifically described by Harada and Hara [34], noting phylogeny that separates Hydropunctaria nipponoamphibia from H. amphibia. Morphologically, black dots on thallus of H. amphibia are exhibited radially distributed, while in H. nipponoamphibia scattered across the thallus. The South Korean samples of H. nipponoamphibia can easily be misidentified and intermixed with the H. fluminicola sp. nov. as well; however, molecular data (ITS, mtSSU, and nrLSU) support its distinctiveness not only from the new species, but also from other closely related taxa (Figures 2 and 3). Taxonomical diagnostic characters of the new taxon, for instance, absence of prothallus, clearly emergent perithecia, and slightly larger ascospores further separate this geographically congruent new record, which also occupies marine habitats.

Similarly, H. adriatica remains insufficiently understood, and its distinction from H. oceanica relies primarily on anatomical and geographical features rather than a single clear external character.

Discussion

The present study provides an integrative taxonomic assessment of Hydropunctaria in South Korea, combining morphological observations with multilocus phylogenetic analyses. The recognition of H. fluminicola as a distinct species is supported by both morphological characteristics and molecular evidence.

Phylogenetically, the new species forms a lineage closely related to H. rheitrophila, although the level of support varies across datasets. This relationship is consistent with certain shared morphological traits, including thallus structure and arrangement of photobiont cells. However, several distinguishing features, such as the absence of a prothallus and prominently emergent perithecia, clearly separate the new taxon from related species. Similar reliance on subtle morphological characters has been emphasized in previous studies of Hydropunctaria [14].

The multilocus phylogeny supports the placement of Hydropunctaria within Verrucariaceae, although deeper relationships among genera remain weakly supported. This is consistent with earlier studies indicating limited resolution at higher taxonomic levels within the family [5]. The ITS-based phylogeny shows partial congruence with the multilocus tree but differs in the placement of certain taxa, reflecting the limitations of single-locus datasets.

Ecological traits, particularly habitat preference, do not show consistent correspondence with phylogenetic relationships in the present study. While some closely related taxa occur in freshwater-influenced environments, others, such as H. alaskana, are restricted to marine habitats. This suggests that ecological factors alone are insufficient for inferring evolutionary relationships within the genus.

The discovery of a new species and a new national record highlights the still underexplored diversity of Hydropunctaria in East Asia and emphasizes the importance of integrative taxonomic approaches in resolving species boundaries within morphologically similar lichen groups.

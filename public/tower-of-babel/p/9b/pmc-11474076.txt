Calcineurin-mediated dephosphorylation stabilizes E2F1 protein by suppressing binding of the FBXW7 ubiquitin ligase subunit
Authors: Yuki Sato, Makoto Habara, Shunsuke Hanaki, Takahiro Masaki, Haruki Tomiyasu, Yosei Miki, Masashi Sakurai, Masahiro Morimoto, Daigo Kobayashi, Tatsuo Miyamoto, Midori Shimada
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SignificanceOverexpression of the transcription factor E2F1 contributes to tumor progression. Degradation of E2F1 by the ubiquitin–proteasome system might therefore be expected to suppress E2F1-mediated carcinogenesis. The mechanisms underlying E2F1 degradation have remained unclear, however. We here identified a ubiquitin ligase complex containing the tumor suppressor protein FBXW7 as a mediator of E2F1 ubiquitination and degradation. Phosphorylation of E2F1 at serine-403 promoted E2F1 binding to FBXW7, whereas dephosphorylation of this residue by the Ca2+-dependent phosphatase calcineurin resulted in E2F1 stabilization. Inhibition of calcineurin elicited downregulation of E2F1 and suppression of tumor progression in a mouse xenograft model. Our results thus indicate that selective inhibition of calcineurin may represent an effective approach to the treatment of cancers characterized by high E2F1 expression.

Members of the E2F family of transcription factors are key regulators of the mammalian cell cycle. Among the members of this family (E2F1, E2F2, E2F3a, E2F3b, E2F4, E2F5, E2F6, E2F7a, E2F7b E2F8), E2F1, E2F2, and E2F3a are activators whereas E2F3b and E2F4–8 are repressors of cell cycle progression (1). In particular, E2F1 plays an essential role in the promotion of cell proliferation. It is highly expressed in cancer cells and associated with poor prognosis in several human cancer types including colon, lung, and breast carcinomas (2–4).

Posttranscriptional modification contributes to regulation of E2F1 protein stability. E2F1 is thus targeted for ubiquitination by multiple E3 ligases including cellular inhibitor of apoptosis protein 1 (cIAP1), the Skp1–Cullin–F-box protein complex containing S-phase kinase–associated protein 2 (SCFSkp2), and the anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C). E2F1 dissociates from phosphorylated retinoblastoma protein during late G1 phase of the cell cycle and activates gene expression required for the G1-S phase transition. It is ubiquitinated by cIAP1 and thereby stabilized during S phase, whereas it is phosphorylated by the cyclin-dependent kinase 2 (CDK2)–cyclin A complex and ubiquitinated by SCFSkp2 during late S phase and ubiquitinated by APC/C during G2 and M phases. The SCFSkp2- and APC/C-mediated ubiquitination of E2F1 triggers E2F1 degradation (5). The phosphorylation of E2F1 at Ser403 mediated by glycogen synthase kinase 3β (GSK3β), p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK), and CDK7 has also been shown to induce E2F1 degradation (6–9), but the precise mechanism of such degradation has remained unclear and the phosphatase that dephosphorylates this site of E2F1 has not been identified.

Intracellular Ca2+ serves as a second messenger that triggers various biochemical reactions and regulates biological processes such as cell proliferation, differentiation, and death as well as muscle contraction and neurotransmission. Ca2+ signaling is essential for maintenance of physiological homeostasis, with its disruption contributing to pathological conditions including heart disease, cancer, and psychiatric disorders (10). Calcineurin is a Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent serine-threonine phosphatase that is activated by an increase in the cytosolic Ca2+ concentration and which dephosphorylates the transcription factor NFAT, thereby inducing translocation of the latter to the nucleus and consequent activation of its target genes (11, 12). The Ca2+-calcineurin-NFAT signaling pathway has been found to be activated in breast cancer and to contribute to malignant transformation (13). Calcineurin also contributes to malignant transformation through stabilization of estrogen receptor α (ERα) (14), EGFR (15), c-Myc (16), FOXO1 (17), NFATc (18), and cyclin D1 (19).

We have now characterized a previously unrecognized mechanism underlying regulation of E2F1 stability. We thus identified SCFFBXW7 as an E3 ligase for E2F1 and found that phosphorylation of E2F1 at Ser403 triggers E2F1 degradation by promoting its binding to FBXW7 (F-box/WD repeat–containing protein 7). Furthermore, calcineurin dephosphorylates E2F1 at Ser403, resulting in the stabilization of E2F1 through attenuation of its interaction with FBXW7. Given that upregulation of E2F1 is associated with poor prognosis in cancer, selective inhibition of calcineurin is a potential therapeutic strategy for cancer with high E2F1 expression.

Results

Identification of SCFFBXW7 as an E3 Ligase for E2F1.

Analysis of publicly available RNA–sequencing (seq) data revealed that expression of the E2F1 gene was increased in various human cancer tissues compared with corresponding normal tissues, and Kaplan–Meier plotter analysis showed that such increased expression was associated with poor prognosis in individuals with breast cancer (Fig. 1 A and B). To identify factors that contribute to the regulation of E2F1 expression, we screened genes for which the hazard ratio (HR) for breast cancer could be calculated for the largest gene expression dataset in Kaplan–Meier plotter (n = 12,182). The search was based on the following four criteria: i) genes previously shown to encode E3 ligases or accessory proteins (20) (n = 388); ii) genes whose expression was low in breast cancer, with a log2[fold change] for breast cancer/normal of <–1 (n = 1760); iii) genes whose products have been found to negatively regulate gene expression (GO:0010629) (n = 203); and iv) genes whose mRNA abundance showed an HR of <1 and a log-rank P value of <0.05 for breast cancer (n = 4860). Only three genes—FBXW7, PLAG1, and ZBTB20—met all four criteria (Fig. 1C). FBXW7 expression was found to be lower in several cancer tissues than in corresponding normal tissues, and such low expression was associated with poor prognosis in human breast cancer (Fig. 1 D and E), consistent with the proposed role of FBXW7 as a tumor suppressor (21).

We therefore next focused on the possible contribution of FBXW7, which is an F-box protein subunit of the SCF ubiquitin ligase complex, to the degradation of E2F1. To test the effect of FBXW7 expression on E2F1 degradation, we stably expressed the FLAG epitope–tagged wild-type (WT) protein in MCF7 human breast cancer cells by lentiviral transduction. Immunoblot analysis revealed that such forced expression of FBXW7 indeed resulted in downregulation of E2F1 (Fig. 2A), consistent with a role for FBXW7 in E2F1 degradation. Transient expression of FLAG-FBXW7 similarly resulted in a decrease in E2F1 abundance in MCF7 cells, with this effect being dependent on the expression level of FBXW7 (Fig. 2B). We next examined the effect of depletion of FBXW7 by lentivirus delivery of a short hairpin RNA (shRNA) that allows conditional knockdown of FBXW7 in response to doxycycline (Dox) treatment. Depletion of FBXW7 increased the abundance of E2F1 in both MCF7 cells and HCT116 human colorectal cancer cells (Fig. 2 C and D), suggesting that FBXW7 negatively regulates E2F1 protein stability. To investigate the effect of an inactive mutant of FBXW7 that lacks the F-box domain (ΔF mutant) on E2F1 abundance, we established MCF7 cells that express FLAG-FBXW7(ΔF) or FLAG-FBXW7(WT). Whereas forced expression of FBXW7(WT) resulted in downregulation of E2F1, that of FBXW7(ΔF) increased the abundance of E2F1 (Fig. 2E), consistent with previous results showing that FBXW7(ΔF) exerts a dominant negative action and thereby prevents substrate degradation (22, 23). We also performed rescue experiments and found that expression of FLAG-FBXW7(WT) in FBXW7 knockdown cells suppressed E2F1 expression (Fig. 2F). To verify that FBXW7 mediates the ubiquitination of E2F1, we performed an in vitro ubiquitination assay. This assay showed that SCFFBXW7 indeed ubiquitinated E2F1 directly (Fig. 2G). Performance of this assay with recombinant FBXW7(ΔF) revealed that the mutant protein did not support the ubiquitination of E2F1 (Fig. 2H), indicating that the SCF complex containing FBXW7 does indeed ubiquitinate E2F1 in vitro. Collectively, these results thus suggested that FBXW7 mediates the ubiquitination of E2F1 and thereby triggers its degradation.

FBXW7 Binds to E2F1 Phosphorylated at Ser403 and Thereby Marks It for Degradation.

FBXW7 binds to phosphorylated serine or threonine residues in the context of the consensus sequence (L/I/P)S/TPXXS (24). We identified five such consensus sequences in the human E2F1 protein that include the target residues Ser307, Thr311, Ser332, Ser337, and Ser403 (Fig. 3A). We first examined the phosphorylation of E2F1 by determining the effect of bacterial alkaline phosphatase (BAP) on the electrophoretic mobility of FLAG-E2F1 or endogenous E2F1 in HEK293T cell lysates. The addition of BAP to the cell lysates resulted in dephosphorylation of the recombinant or endogenous protein as evidenced by an increase in electrophoretic mobility (Fig. 3 B and C). To examine the role of phosphorylation of the five putative target serine-threonine residues in the degradation of E2F1, we generated phosphorylation-resistant mutants of E2F1 by replacing each of these five residues with alanine. We found that E2F1(S403A) was the most highly expressed among the five mutant proteins in HEK293T cells, suggesting that it might be the most stable (Fig. 3D). To determine the extent to which phosphorylation at Ser403 contributes to the overall phosphorylation level of E2F1, we subjected FLAG-tagged WT, S403A, and 5A (in which all five potential target serine-threonine residues were replaced with alanine) forms of E2F1 in HEK293T cell lysates to BAP treatment (Fig. 3B). The electrophoretic mobility of the BAP-treated WT protein was greater than that of the untreated S403A mutant, whereas BAP treatment increased the mobility of the S403A mutant so that it was similar to that of the 5A mutant (treated or untreated). These results suggested that E2F1 is phosphorylated at Ser403 as well as at one or more of the other four sites among the five examined (Fig. 3B). To test whether Ser403 might be the target of FBXW7 binding, we transfected HEK293T cells with expression vectors for V5 epitope–tagged FBXW7 and FLAG-tagged E2F1 (WT or S403A) and subjected the cell lysates to immunoprecipitation with antibodies to FLAG. The binding of V5-tagged FBXW7 to the S403A mutant of E2F1 was reduced by about one-half compared with that to the WT protein (Fig. 3E). We next investigated whether a reduction in the extent of the interaction between E2F1 and FBXW7 leads to a corresponding reduction in the level of E2F1 ubiquitination by performing immunoprecipitation of FLAG-E2F1 from cell lysates under denaturing conditions (presence of urea). The extent of E2F1(S403A) ubiquitination was indeed less than that apparent for the WT protein (Fig. 3F), suggesting that phosphorylation at Ser403 promotes the ubiquitination of E2F1. Furthermore, the ubiquitination level of E2F1(S403A) was similar to that of E2F1(5A), highlighting the importance of Ser403 among the five phosphorylation sites. To confirm that phosphorylation of Ser403 reduces the stability of E2F1, we performed a cycloheximide (CHX) chase assay with HEK293T cells expressing FLAG-E2F1(WT) or FLAG-E2F1(S403A). This assay indeed revealed that E2F1(S403A) was more stable than was E2F1(WT) (Fig. 3G). Together, these results suggested that FBXW7 recognizes the phosphorylated Ser403 residue of E2F1 and thereby promotes E2F1 degradation.

Calcineurin Dephosphorylates and Stabilizes E2F1.

We next examined the possible contribution of GSK3β, p38 MAPK, and CDK7—all of which have previously been shown to phosphorylate E2F1 at Ser403 and thereby to target it for degradation (6–9)—to the FBXW7-dependent degradation of E2F1. We treated MCF7 cells with SB203580, CHIR99021, or THZ1, which inhibit p38 MAPK, GSK3β, and CDK7, respectively, and then examined the abundance of E2F1 by immunoblot analysis. The abundance of E2F1 was increased significantly by treatment of the cells with THZ1 but was unaffected by that with either of the other two inhibitors (SI Appendix, Fig. S1), suggesting that CDK7 is mainly responsible for the phosphorylation of E2F1 at Ser403 that promotes its degradation in this setting.

Previous studies have suggested that a Ca2+-activated pathway mediated by protein kinase C and p38β MAPK leads to E2F1 ubiquitination (25–27). We therefore examined whether calcineurin, a Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent serine-threonine protein phosphatase, might contribute to the regulation of E2F1 stability. We performed an in vitro phosphatase assay to investigate whether calcineurin dephosphorylates E2F1 at Ser403. Recombinant FLAG-E2F1(WT) or FLAG-E2F1(S403A) immunoprecipitated from transfected HEK293T cells was subjected to the assay with recombinant calcineurin and calmodulin. Immunoblot analysis of the reaction mixtures with antibodies to FLAG revealed three bands corresponding to FLAG-E2F1(WT), with calcineurin treatment attenuating the intensity of the top two bands and increasing that of the bottom band in a time-dependent manner (Fig. 4A). We confirmed that the enzyme activity of calcineurin was required for this apparent E2F1 dephosphorylation by showing that inactive calcineurin Aα mutants either with the H151Q point mutation or lacking the catalytic domain (ΔCD) did not affect the electrophoretic mobility of E2F1(WT) (Fig. 4B). We also found that calcineurin did not affect the electrophoretic mobility of FLAG-E2F1(S403A) (Fig. 4C). These results suggested that Ser403 is the major site of calcineurin-mediated dephosphorylation of E2F1. We also examined whether other phosphatases might contribute to E2F1 dephosphorylation, but in vitro assays with the phosphatases PP2A and PP1 revealed no effect on the electrophoretic mobility of FLAG-E2F1(WT) (SI Appendix, Fig. S2). As a positive control, we confirmed that both phosphatases mediated the dephosphorylation of c-Myc at Ser62. In addition, PP2A treatment reduced the extent of phosphorylation of E2F1 at Ser364, a known target site of PP2A (28).

We examined the interaction between calcineurin and E2F1 in HEK293T cells expressing FLAG-E2F1. Coimmunoprecipitation analysis with antibodies to endogenous calcineurin Aα or with those to FLAG revealed that FLAG-E2F1 indeed interacted with endogenous calcineurin (Fig. 4D). To examine the effect of calcineurin inhibition on E2F1 abundance, we treated MCF7 cells and HT29 (human colorectal cancer) cells with the calcineurin inhibitor FK506. FK506 down-regulated endogenous E2F1 abundance in a concentration-dependent manner in both cell lines (SI Appendix, Fig. S3 A and B). Given that FK506 inhibits both calcineurin and FK506 binding proteins, we also examined the effect of CN585, which specifically inhibits calcineurin phosphatase activity. Treatment with CN585 also resulted in a loss of E2F1 protein in both MCF7 and HT29 cells (SI Appendix, Fig. S3 C and D). These data indicated that the phosphatase activity of calcineurin is required for maintenance of E2F1 expression. To examine more directly the role of calcineurin in the regulation of E2F1 expression, we depleted cells of calcineurin Aα with two independent shRNAs. Depletion of calcineurin Aα resulted in significant downregulation of E2F1 in MCF7, T47D (human breast cancer), and HT29 cells (Fig. 4E and SI Appendix, Fig. S3 E and F). We confirmed that the downregulation of E2F1 induced by FK506 or CN585 treatment in MCF7 cells was attributable to degradation of the protein by the ubiquitin–proteasome pathway by showing that such downregulation was attenuated in the presence of the proteasome inhibitor MG132 (SI Appendix, Fig. S3 G and H). We also confirmed that the effect of FK506 on E2F1 abundance was apparent in both nuclear and cytoplasmic fractions of MCF7 cells (SI Appendix, Fig. S3I). Reverse transcription (RT) and qPCR analysis showed that the amount of E2F1 mRNA was also decreased in calcineurin-depleted or FK506-treated MCF7 cells (SI Appendix, Fig. S3 J and K), suggesting that calcineurin also regulates E2F1 expression at the transcriptional level. Finally, we performed experiments to rescue the expression of calcineurin Aα in calcineurin Aα–depleted cells. Expression of HA-tagged calcineurin Aα(WT) in calcineurin Aα–depleted MCF7 cells (Fig. 4F) or HCT116 cells (Fig. 4G) restored E2F1 expression, whereas that of the HA-tagged catalytically inactive mutant calcineurin Aα(H151Q) had no such effect. Collectively, these findings indicated that the phosphatase activity of calcineurin suppresses E2F1 degradation mediated by the ubiquitin–proteasome pathway.

Calcineurin Is Required for the Expression of E2F1 Target Genes.

We next examined whether calcineurin promotes E2F1 target gene expression by stabilizing the E2F1 protein. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) of RNA-seq data previously obtained from calcineurin Aα–depleted and control MCF7 cells (DRA011729) revealed that the expression of E2F target genes was down-regulated by calcineurin depletion (Fig. 5 A–C), with E2F TARGETS being the most affected gene set among the hallmarks (Fig. 5A). A reduced transactivation activity of E2F, in particular of E2F1, in calcineurin Aα–depleted cells was confirmed by analysis of other gene sets including the C6 oncogenic signature [Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB)] as well as TF Targets from CircuitsDB (29) and RegNetwork (30) (Fig. 5 D and E and SI Appendix, Fig. S4). We also validated these results by RT-qPCR analysis of the E2F1 target genes GINS3, GINS4, MCM2, and BRCA2, the expression of all of which was decreased by calcineurin Aα depletion in MCF7 cells (Fig. 5F). Collectively, these findings suggested that calcineurin is necessary for the expression of E2F1 target genes.

Calcineurin Stabilizes E2F1 by Inhibiting FBXW7 Binding.

Given our findings that FBXW7 mediates the ubiquitination and degradation of E2F1, and that calcineurin stabilizes E2F1, we next examined whether these processes are actually related. Treatment with MLN4924, a protein neddylation inhibitor that targets the ubiquitin–proteasome system (UPS), resulted in the stabilization of E2F1 in both control and calcineurin Aα–depleted MCF7 cells (Fig. 6A), confirming that E2F1 is degraded by the UPS and further suggesting that E2F1 stabilization by calcineurin is related to the UPS. We then depleted MCF7 cells of both calcineurin and FBXW7 and compared the expression level of E2F1 in these cells with that in those depleted only of calcineurin. We found that depletion of FBXW7 reversed the downregulation of E2F1 induced by calcineurin depletion (Fig. 6B), indicating that the degradation of E2F1 induced by calcineurin depletion is dependent on FBXW7. Similarly, depletion of FBXW7 in HT29 cells reversed the decrease in E2F1 abundance induced by calcineurin inhibition with CN585 (SI Appendix, Fig. S5A). Examination of E2F1 ubiquitination under denaturing conditions also revealed that treatment of HEK293T cells with the calcineurin inhibitor FK506 increased the ubiquitination level of E2F1 (Fig. 6C), suggesting that calcineurin inhibits such ubiquitination and thereby promotes E2F1 stability. Furthermore, CHX chase analysis confirmed that the stability of E2F1 protein was diminished in HCT116 cells depleted of calcineurin compared with control cells, whereas it was increased in FBXW7-depleted cells (Fig. 6D). These results thus indicated that the stability of the E2F1 protein is increased by calcineurin and reduced by FBXW7.

Calcium Signaling Regulates E2F1 Abundance.

We next focused on Ca2+ signaling upstream of calcineurin. GSEA of RNA-seq results previously obtained from A549 cells treated (or not) with the Ca2+ channel blocker amlodipine (GSE159522) showed that such treatment markedly attenuated E2F target gene expression (Fig. 7A), implicating Ca2+ signaling in the regulation of E2F1. We therefore treated MCF7 cells with the Ca2+ channel blocker verapamil, and found that verapamil also induced marked downregulation of E2F1 expression (Fig. 7B). We also treated MCF7 cells expressing FLAG-tagged E2F1 (WT or S403A) with verapamil. Whereas the abundance of FLAG-E2F1(WT) was reduced by verapamil treatment, that of FLAG-E2F1(S403A) was unaffected (Fig. 7C), suggesting that intracellular Ca2+ affects E2F1 protein levels in a manner dependent on Ser403 phosphorylation. In contrast to the effect of verapamil, treatment of MCF7 cells with the Ca2+ ionophore ionomycin increased the abundance of E2F1 (Fig. 7D), confirming that E2F1 expression is affected by the intracellular concentration of Ca2+. To explore further the role of FBXW7 in the regulation of E2F1 abundance by intracellular Ca2+, we examined E2F1 levels in control and FBXW7-depleted HCT116 cells in the absence or presence of verapamil. The downregulation of E2F1 induced by verapamil treatment was counteracted by depletion of FBXW7 (SI Appendix, Fig. S5B), suggesting that FBXW7 is essential for the regulation of E2F1 abundance in response to changes in Ca2+ concentration. Given that E2F1 promotes the expression of genes required for progression from G1 to S phase of the cell cycle, we examined whether degradation of E2F1 induced by treatment of MCF7 cells with verapamil might result in arrest of the cell cycle in G1 phase. Verapamil indeed induced a concentration-dependent arrest of cells in G1 phase (Fig. 7E), indicating that intracellular Ca2+ contributes to the regulation of E2F1 abundance and cell cycle progression from G1 to S phase.

Calcineurin Inhibition Suppresses Tumor Growth In Vivo.

To explore the effect of FLAG-E2F1(S403A) expression on cellular phenotype, we expressed FLAG-E2F1(WT) or FLAG-E2F1(S403A) in cells depleted of endogenous E2F1 and examined cell proliferation. MCF7 cells expressing FLAG-E2F1(S403A) in this context proliferated more rapidly than did those expressing FLAG-E2F1(WT) (Fig. 8A). FK506 was previously shown to inhibit cancer progression in a mouse xenograft model of bladder cancer (31). We therefore examined the effects of FK506 in vivo with a mouse xenograft model. HT29 cells were implanted subcutaneously into the flank of NOD/Shi-SCID mice, and the mice were treated daily by intraperitoneal injection of FK506 beginning when the tumor volume had reached ~150 mm3. The tumors of FK506-treated mice were significantly smaller than were those of vehicle-treated mice from 12 d after the onset of treatment (Fig. 8 B and C). The abundance of E2F1 in tumors collected after treatment for 21 d was also significantly reduced for those treated with FK506 (Fig. 8D). Consistent with our in vitro results, these findings thus suggested that calcineurin stabilizes E2F1 in vivo.

Discussion

FBXW7, the substrate recognition subunit of the SCFFBXW7 E3 ubiquitin ligase, recognizes phosphorylated serine-threonine residues within the consensus motif (L/I/P)S/TPXXS and thereby supports attachment of a polyubiquitin chain to the substrate by the ligase complex (24, 32). FBXW7 has also been identified as a key tumor suppressor as a result of its role in the degradation of various proto-oncoproteins including cyclin E, c-Myc, Mcl-1, mTOR, Jun, Notch, and AURKA (21, 33). We have now shown that FBXW7 contributes to the degradation of E2F1 by the UPS (Fig. 8E). Previous studies have shown that E2F1 activates transcription of the FBXW7 gene (34), that transcription of the E2F1 gene and of E2F1 target genes is up-regulated in FBXW7-deficient mice (35), and that an E2F1 binding site in the FBXW7 gene promoter is important for transcriptional activation of the gene (36). Collectively, these findings together with our present results suggest that E2F1 and FBXW7 may contribute to a negative feedback loop that controls E2F1 abundance.

We found that FBXW7 recognizes E2F1 phosphorylated at Ser403, which is located within the transactivation domain (TAD) of the protein. The TADs of various transcription factors interact directly with components of the general transcription machinery such as TBP, TFIIB, and TAFs or with coactivator proteins and thereby promote transcription (37). Phosphorylation of c-Myc at Thr58 located within the TAD has also previously been shown to trigger c-Myc destabilization (38). Phosphorylation of residues within the TAD may therefore serve as a degradation signal in other transcription factors as it does in E2F1 and c-Myc.

In addition to FBXW7, we identified pleomorphic adenoma gene 1 (PLAG1) and the gene for zinc finger– and BTB domain–containing 20 (ZBTB20) as candidates for genes that regulate the degradation of E2F1. PLAG1 is a zinc-finger transcription factor and proto-oncoprotein (39), whereas ZBTB20 has been implicated in developmental neurogenesis, glucose and lipid homeostasis, and tumorigenesis of glioblastoma as well as of liver and lung cancer (40). Neither of these proteins is known to have E3 ligase activity, but it remains possible that they contribute to the regulation of E2F1 protein stability.

We found that calcineurin stabilizes E2F1 by mediating its dephosphorylation at Ser403. E2F1 promotes the expression of genes required for the transition from G1 to S phase of the cell cycle (41). Calcineurin is activated from G1 to S phase (42), suggesting that E2F1 is stabilized and promotes gene expression and cell proliferation during the G1-S transition when calcineurin is active.

Whereas we identified calcineurin as an E2F1 phosphatase, previous studies have indicated that E2F1 is dephosphorylated by PP2A at Ser364 and thereby stabilized (28). Calcium ions promote the growth and invasiveness of cancer cells, with Ca2+ channels, pumps, and exchangers therefore being considered potential therapeutic targets for several cancer types (43). In the present study, we found that the Ca2+ ionophore ionomycin increased E2F1 abundance in MCF7 cells. Conversely, the Ca2+ channel blocker verapamil down-regulated E2F1 and induced cell cycle arrest in G1 phase. Furthermore, our analysis of previous RNA-seq data revealed downregulation of E2F1 target genes in cells treated with the Ca2+ channel blocker amlodipine. These observations thus suggest that a decrease in intracellular Ca2+ levels might suppress the proliferation of cancer cells through downregulation of E2F1. It will therefore be of interest to investigate further the relation between cancer and Ca2+ signaling and the possibility of targeting such signaling as a therapeutic approach.

E2F1 is overexpressed in many cancer tissues, with such increased expression being associated with poor prognosis in individuals with breast cancer. We previously showed that calcineurin inhibition destabilizes ERα (14), EGFR (15), c-Myc (16), FOXO1 (17), NFATc (18), and cyclin D1 (19) in addition to the destabilization of E2F1 demonstrated in the present study, suggesting that calcineurin inhibitors are potential therapeutic agents for cancer treatment. Further studies are warranted to determine such potential clinical applications.

Methods

Cell Culture and Reagents.

MCF7, T47D, and HEK293T cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM: 044-29765, Wako) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS: 173012, Sigma) and antibiotics (15240062, Thermo Fisher Scientific). HCT116 and HT29 cells were cultured in McCoy’s 5A medium (16600-082, Gibco) supplemented with 10% FBS and antibiotics. Cells were treated with FK506 (063-06191, Wako), CN585 (207003, Merck), MG132 (474790, Merck), MLN4924 (S7109, Selleck), ionomycin (095-05831, Wako), CHX (037-20991, Wako), and verapamil (222-00781, Wako), as indicated. With the exception of CHX and verapamil, which was dissolved in water, these various reagents were dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO).

Transfection and Lentiviral Infection.

For transient expression, cells were transfected with plasmids with the use of PEI MAX (24765-1, Polysciences) at pH 7.0. Lentivirus generation and infection were performed as described previously (44). For generation of lentiviruses encoding shRNAs, HEK293T cells were transfected with lentiviral packaging vectors (1.54 μg of psPAX2 and 0.86 μg of pMD2.G per 8 × 105 cells in a 35-mm dish) and 2.0 μg of the appropriate CS-RfA-shRNA plasmid (SI Appendix, Table S1) with the use of 8.8 μg of PEI MAX. The resulting lentiviruses were used for target cell infection, and shRNA expression was induced by treatment with doxycycline (D9891, Sigma-Aldrich) at 1 μg/ml. For lentiviruses encoding proteins, proteins were also induced using the tet-on expression vector, CSIV-TRE-RfA-UbC-KT-Puro, with doxycycline (1 µg/ml) treatment or expressed using the constitutively expressing vector, CSII-CMV-MCS-IRES2-Bsd vector.

Tuning of crystal structure and electronic band gap of the monoclinic Ga2O3 by simultaneous alloying with Al2O3 and In2O3
Authors: Leonid Vasylechko, Vasyl Hreb, Yaroslav Zhydachevskyy, Yuriy Hizhnyi, Andrii Smaliuk, Vasyl Stasiv, Vitalii Stadnik, Yurii Hirskyi, Halyna Zhydachevska, Vitaliy Mykhaylyk, Andrzej Suchocki
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe paper presents the study of phase and structural behavior of pseudoternary compound (Ga1-x-yInxAly)2O3 with focus on the compositional cross-section where the x/y ratio is a fixed at 0.31/0.69. This specific ratio ensures that the average cation radius, equal to that of Ga3+ ions, remains unchanged. Through the combination of experimental XRD studies and DFT calculations, the stability region of the monoclinic phase within the Ga2O3–Al2O3–In2O3 ternary system was established. Detailed analysis of the crystal lattice parameters and unit cell volume of the monoclinic structure was carried out across a wide range of compositions. An empirical relationship was derived linking the monoclinic lattice parameters to the average ionic radius of the cations (Ga3+, Al3+, In3+) enabling prediction of lattice parameters in monoclinic (Ga1-x-yInxAly)2O3 solely from chemical composition. The experimental crystal structure studies and the electronic structure calculations suggest that in the monoclinic (Ga1-x-yInxAly)2O3 structure the tetrahedral positions of Ga1 atoms are preferentially occupied by Ga3+ and Al3+ cations, while the octahedral Ga2 sites accommodate a mixture of Ga3+, Al3+ and In3+ cations. Additionally, the presence of unidentified phase(s) was confirmed in the central region of the Ga2O3–Al2O3–In2O3 triangle. Introduction

Gallium oxide (Ga2O3) is a well-established ultra-wide bandgap semiconductor that has attracted considerable interest due to its exceptional properties, including a large bandgap of about 4.9 eV, a high breakdown field of 8 MV/cm, and excellent thermal stability (see Zhang et al.1 and references therein). These properties position Ga2O3 as a highly promising material for diverse applications, including high-power electronic devices, solar-blind ultraviolet (UV) photodetectors and scintillators.2–7 Ga2O3 exists in several structural polymorphs, including the corundum-like (α), monoclinic (β), defective spinel (γ), and two variations (ε and δ) of orthorhombic structure.8 Among these, the monoclinic phase (β-Ga2O3) stands out as the most prevalent as it is the most thermodynamically stable and, hence, readily obtainable under ambient conditions.9–12.

Monoclinic β-Ga2O3 has a direct bandgap of 4.87 eV with a small contribution of indirect transitions of 4.83 eV.13 The valence band is mainly formed by the occupied O 2p derived states slightly hybridized with Ga 3d, 4p, and 4 s orbitals, while the conduction band is mainly composed of Ga 4 s derived states (see Zhang et al.1 and references therein for details).

When activated with Cr3+ ions, β-Ga2O3 produces a broad and efficient near-infrared (NIR) emission at about 750 nm suitable for NIR tunable lasers,14 NIR phosphor-converted light-emitting diodes (pc-LEDs),15 high pressure16 and temperature sensors.17,18.

Alloying of β-Ga2O3 with Al, Sc or In oxides to form solid solutions is a known approach for tuning physical properties of the monoclinic gallium oxide. This is achieved primarily by modifying the crystal structure and energy band gap (bandgap engineering) of the material.19–23 In particular, it was found that, within the monoclinic structure, the crystal lattice parameters a, b, c, and the unit cell volume of the β-(Ga1-xInx)2O3 and β-(Ga1-yAly)2O3 pseudobinary compounds gradually increase with average cation radii in each series, while the monoclinic angle β decreases.23 It was also confirmed that In3+ cations exhibit a strong preference for occupying the Ga2 (octahedral) site over the Ga1 (tetrahedral) site in the monoclinic structure of Ga2O3, whereas Al3+ cations are distributed across both the octahedral and tetrahedral sites.23 Consequently, the β-(Ga0.5In0.5)2O3 is predominantly composed of Ga-centered tetrahedra and In-centered octahedra. The electronic band gap of β-Ga2O3 can be expanded by alloying with Al2O3,24–29 or reduced by alloying with Sc2O3 or In2O3.23,25,30–33 Recent studies have confirmed that alloying with Al or In oxides significantly enhances the functional properties of β-Ga2O3-based semiconductor devices.34,35 The chemical pressure induced through alloying also alters the crystal field strength experienced by activators such as Cr3+, thereby enabling fine tuning of the luminescence properties of the Cr3+-doped β-Ga2O3 alloyed with Al, Sc or In oxides.21,23.

The simultaneous alloying of β-Ga2O3 with In and Al oxides, i.e. the formation of quaternary (Ga1-x-yInxAly)2O3 systems, is of great interest as it potentially opens up even greater possibilities for tailoring the physical properties of the material. Such a possibility has been explored in the theoretical study,36 where the electronic properties and crystal structure of (Ga1-x-yInxAly)2O3 alloys have been investigated by the first-principle DFT calculations. Experimental studies of (Ga1-x-yInxAly)2O3 alloys are still very scarce. Recently Chang et al.37 reported a Ga2-x(Al0.68In0.32)xO3:Cr3+ phosphor series in which the unit cell volume remains nearly constant. This is attributed to the fact that the average cation radius of (Al0.68In0.32)3+ matches that of Ga3+, while cation composition varies. X-ray and neutron powder diffraction studies of samples synthesized via the solid-state reaction revealed a pure monoclinic structure for x values from 0 to 0.8. Luminescent studies of the series demonstrated a broadened emission spectrum with enhanced electron–phonon coupling compared to the parent β-Ga2O3:Cr3+ attributed to the increased local structural disorder.37 However, it is still unclear what happens at higher In and Al content, i.e., for z > 0.4 in (Ga1-zAl0.69*zIn0.31*z)2O3, as well as for the compositions beyond the ratio Al/In of 0.69/0/31. Our previous attempts to synthesize the (Ga1-x-yAlyInx)2O3 alloys with a balanced proportion of Al, Ga and In (in particular (Ga0.25Al0.5In0.25)2O3 and (Ga0.33Al0.34In0.33)2O3) have revealed a the formation of previously unidentified crystal structure.23 This structure is distinct not only from β-Ga2O3 but also from all other known polymorphs of Ga2O3, Al2O3 and In2O3 oxides. It is worth mentioning that XRD patterns of the Ga2-x(Al0.68In0.32)xO3:Cr3+ composition with x = 0.9, as reported by Chang et al.37 exhibit some residual peaks indicative of an impurity phase. Interestingly, these impurity peaks closely match the main diffraction peaks of the unidentified crystal structure reported in Ref. 23. This exposes significant gaps in our knowledge of the (Ga1-x-yInxAly)2O3 pseudoternary system, highlighting the need for further in-depth investigations.

Realizing this gap the present work focuses on investigating the structure and phase equilibria of pseudoternary compounds in the Ga2O3–Al2O3–In2O3 system, aiming to get better understanding of the limits of the monoclinic structure stability in this system beyond the (Ga1-zAl0.69*zIn0.31*z)2O3 line (z = 0…0.4). The area of existence of the monoclinic structure in the triangle Ga2O3–Al2O3–In2O3 is particularly intriguing as the Al2O3 and In2O3 end-members adopt distinct sesquioxide structures i.e. rhombohedral and cubic structures, respectively. Moreover, the central area of the triangle is likely to accommodate a previously unidentified phase. In addition to the crystal structure analysis, the optical absorption spectra of the studied β-(Ga1-x-yInxAly)2O3 pseudoternary compounds were investigated theoretically by the electronic structure calculations and experimentally with the latter assessed via diffuse reflectance spectroscopy.

Experimental and calculation methods

Samples preparation and experimental methods

To experimentally investigate the phase behavior within the ternary Ga2O3–Al2O3–In2O3 system, specifically to determine the homogeneity region of the solid solution adopting the monoclinic β-Ga2O3 type of structure, a series of (Ga1-x-yAlyInx)2O3 samples with varying x and y values were prepared by the solution combustion method following the same procedure previously reported by us in Ref. 23. Throughout this work we use the variable x to denote the In content and y to denote the Al content in the pseudoternary (Ga1-x-yAlyInx)2O3 compounds. For a specific composition line where In to Al ratio is fixed as 0.31/0.69, corresponding to the Ga2-x(Al0.68In0.32)xO3 series reported by Chang et al.,37 which maintain an average cation radius equal to that of Ga3+ ions (0.62 Å for CN 6), we adopt the notation (Ga1-zAl0.69*zIn0.31*z)2O3, where z ranges from 0 to 1 (z = x + y and x/y = 0.31/0.69).

In the synthesis procedure, aluminum nitrate nonahydrate Al(NO3)3·9H2O, indium oxide In2O3 and metallic Ga were used as metal precursors while glycine (Gly) and urea (Ur) served as a fuel. To obtain metal nitrate solutions, In2O3 and Ga were dissolved in concentrated HNO3, whereas Al(NO3)3·9H2O was dissolved in a distilled water. For the preparation of (Ga1-x-yAlyInx)2O3 samples, appropriate aliquots of metal (Me3+) nitrate solutions based on the nominal compositions of the materials were mixed together on a magnetic stirrer for 30 min. Subsequently, aqueous solutions of glycine (Gly) and urea (Ur) were gradually added to the reaction mixture ensuring molar ratio of reagents n(Me3+) : n(Gly) : n(Ur) = 1.2 : 1.0 : 1.5. The as-prepared solutions were placed in a drying oven at 363–373 K for 2–4 h to the formation of brownish gel-like products. These were then transferred to muffle furnace preheated at 873 K for a few minutes to initiate a combustion process leading to the formation of ash-like product. To obtain well-crystallized materials in an equilibrium state, the resulting products were successively calcined in air at 1473 K for 4 h and followed by final calcination at 1773 K for 5 h.

Phase composition of the materials at different stages of synthesis procedure was monitored by X-ray powder diffraction (XRD) using Aeris benchtop powder diffractometer (Malvern Panalytical) equipped with a PIXcel1D strip detector. Experimental diffraction data (Cu Kα radiation, λ = 1.54185 Å) were collected in a 2θ range of 6–105° with a 2θ steps of 0.02°. The same experimental technique was used for comprehensive structural characterization of the final products by full profile Rietveld refinement. Accurate values of the lattice parameters, coordinates and displacement parameters of atoms, as well as site occupancies were determined from experimental XRD patterns using the full profile refinement procedure implemented in the WinCSD software package.38.

The diffuse reflection (DR) spectra of the powder samples were measured using a Carry 5000 UV–Vis-NIR spectrophotometer with a diffuse reflectance accessory DRA2500.

Theoretical calculations

The DFT-based electronic structure calculations of (Ga1-x-yInxAly)2O3 alloys were performed using the supercell approach. The supercells were constructed by 2 × 4 × 2 replication of the unit cell of β-Ga2O3 resulting in structure containing 320 atoms (see Fig. S4). All supercells were assigned the primitive symmetry group P1. Such a large supercells contain sufficient amount of Ga positions to be substituted with Al or In so that it allows: a) to model z values close to that in the synthesized experimental samples (z = 0.1, 0.2, 0.3 and 0.4, see SI, Table S1); b) to model for each z the x/y ratio close to experimental one 0.69/0.31. It should be noted that recent DFT studies of the electronic structure of β-Ga2O3-based alloys have typically employed smaller super cell sizes, for example, these consisting of 200,39 16030,40,41 and even 80 atoms.28,36,42.

The number of cationic substitutions modeled in the supercells for each z along with the corresponding x/y ratio and z = x + y is listed in Table S1. The number of InGa and AlGa substitutions were selected to maintain the x/y ratio as closer as possible to 0.31/0.69 for each z value. For convenience of analysis, each modelled z value is referred to in the text by its rounded form (i.e. a composition with z = 0.046, etc. is denoted as z = 0.05, see Table S1 for exact values). For each z, 10 structures were generated with random distributions of InGa and AlGa substitutions, resulting in a total of 70 structures analyzed. All structures were subjected to geometrical optimization and total energies were calculated for each configuration. Finally, optical spectra were calculated only for the structures with the lowest total energy among the 10 configurations considered for each z value.

The electronic structure calculations were performed using the DFT-based band-periodic pseudopotential method implemented in CASTEP program, which is part of the Materials Studio package.43 Geometry optimizations accounted for exchange–correlation effects using the GGA-PBE approximation.44 Atomic positions were optimized using the Broyden-Fletcher-Goldfarb-Shenno (BFGS) minimization algorithm45 with the convergence criterion for energy and force on the atoms, which were set to 10–5 eV per atom and 0.03 eV/Å per atom, respectively. Geometry optimizations were performed only for the Γ point of the Brillouin zone.

To overcome the well-known underestimation of the bandgap energies by PBE functional, the optical (band-to-band) absorption spectra were calculated using the exchange–correlation effects implemented in GGA-PBE plus Hubbard (GGA + U) U approach.46 The on-site Coulomb corrections (U) were applied to specific orbitals: O 2p (6.7 eV), Ga 3d (7.0 eV), Al 3d (7.0 eV) and In 3d (5.0 eV). These values were selected to achieve the best agreement between the calculated and experimental bandgaps for β-Ga2O3 (monoclinic lattice, symmetry group ICSD #12) α-Al2O3 (hexagonal, SG #167) and rh-In2O3 (rhombohedral, SG #167) crystals.47 The GGA + U approach with approximately the same U values has been successfully employed in the previous electronic structure-related computational studies of Ga2O3 crystals48–56 and its related alloys.57–59 For the calculations of optical absorption spectra K the Brillouin zone (BZ) was sampled using a 2 × 2 × 2 k-point grid. Convergence of calculated K-spectra with respect to the number of BZ sampling points was verified through additional calculations (see Supplementary Material, Fig. S5).

Results and discussion

Phase composition and crystal structure parameters of the studied compounds

The binary compounds that define the edges of pseudoternary Ga2O3–Al2O3–In2O3 system, namely the β-(Ga1-yAly)2O3 and β-(Ga1-xInx)2O3 solid solutions, have been extensively studied and are documented in in literature.19–21,34,35,60 Based on the results of these investigations and our own recent works,22,23 the homogeneity range of solid solutions with monoclinic β-Ga2O3 structure extend up to approximately 80–85% of Al in the Ga2O3–Al2O3 and up to 50% of In in the Ga2O3–In2O3 systems. To outline the stability range of the monoclinic phase in the ternary Ga2O3–Al2O3–In2O3 system (see Fig. 1), a series of double substituted (Ga1-x-yAlyInx)2O3 samples with varying Ga/Al/In ratios were synthesized and analyzed. As a starting point, we investigated the substitution of gallium with In/Al pairs along the compositional cross-section with a fixed x/y ratio of 0.31/0.69 denoted as “A” in Fig. 1. This corresponds to the (Ga1-zAl0.69*zIn0.31*z)2O3 series, in which the average cation radius equal to that of Ga3+—a concept referred as compensatory simulating substitution.

As evidenced by the analysis of the experimental XRD patterns shown in Fig. 2, single-phase materials with β-Ga2O3-type structure in the (Ga1-zAl0.69*zIn0.31*z)2O3 series are preserved up to a maximum z value of 0.4. Notably, a similar stability limit for the monoclinic phase was recently reported by Chang et al.37 for chromium-doped (Ga1-x-yAlyInx)2O3 materials synthesized by high-temperature solid state synthesis at 1550 °C.

Experimental XRD patterns of the (Ga1-zAl0.69*zIn0.31*z)2O3 series. Asterisks indicate the diffraction peaks of unidentified phase(s) which begin to emerge at z = 0.45 and became dominant in the samples with z = 0.8 and 0.9.

At z ≥ 0.45, additional reflections corresponding to secondary phases begin to appear in the XRD patterns. The relative intensity of these peaks increases progressively with rising In(Al) content, while the amount of the monoclinic β-Ga2O3-type phase simultaneously decreases, disappearing entirely at z = 0.8. As a result, the XRD patterns of the (Ga1-zAl0.69*zIn0.31*z)2O3 samples with z = 0.8 and 0.9 (see Fig. 2) are dominated by reflections of unidentified phase(s), which could not be matched with any known Ga–Al–In–O compounds listed in ICDD PDF-2 and PDF-4 databases. The existence of such a new phase within the Ga2O3–Al2O3–In2O3 system was previously mentioned in our work.23 To further investigate and localize the region of existence of these phase(s) in the Ga2O3–Al2O3–In2O3 ternary system, additional samples with nominal compositions shown in Fig. 1 were prepared. For the most representative samples from this series, high-resolution X-ray powder diffraction data were collected at the I11 beamline at Diamond Light Source (Didcot, UK). Data analysis is currently in progress, and the detailed results on the structure of newly observed crystalline phases(s) in the Ga2O3–Al2O3–In2O3 ternary system will be reported in a separate publication.

Let us return to the monoclinic solid solutions—the primary focus of this work. To more precisely define the homogeneity range of β-Ga2O3-type phase within the Ga2O3–Al2O3–In2O3 system, two additional series of the samples were investigated: (i) a series with a constant Ga2O3 content of 60% and varying Al/In ratio (cross-section “B” on Fig. 1) and (ii) a series with a constant In2O3 content of 5% and the varying Ga/Al ratio (cross-section “C” on Fig. 1). Analysis of the XRD patterns of the (Ga0.6Al0.4-xInx)2O3 and (Ga0.95-yAlyIn0.05)2O3 materials with x = 0.3, 0.2, 0.05 and y = 0.45, 0.55, 0.65 and 0.75, respectively (see Fig. S1, allowed us to determine the boundary between the single phase β-Ga2O3-type region and multiphase domain within the ternary Ga2O3–Al2O3–In2O3 system as shown in Fig. 1.

The precise crystal structure parameters of the single-phase specimens from the ternary solid solution region in the Ga2O3–Al2O3–In2O3 system were obtained through full profile Rietveld refinement. The refined procedure began with the optimization of the unit cell parameters and atomic coordinates followed by the refinement of Bragg peak profile parameters, absorption correction and instrumental sample shift. Subsequently, the displacement parameters (adp’s) for all atoms were refined followed by the refinement of site occupancies for Ga3+, Al3+ and In3+ ions. Structural refinement using different combinations of metal cations in the two non-equivalent Ga-sites showed that the lowest residuals and physically reliable values of adp’s were achieved only when the tetrahedral positions of Ga1 atoms in the monoclinic (Ga1-x-yAlyInx)2O3 structures were partially occupied by smaller aluminum ions, while the octahedral Ga2 position contained a mixture of all three Ga3+, Al3+ and In3+ cations. The refined atomic occupancy of two cation positions along with the final structural parameters of four double substituted β-Ga2O3-based materials from the (Ga1-zAl0.69*zIn0.31*z)2O3 series are summarized in Table 1. For a visual assessment of the quality of the structural refinement, Fig. S2 presents graphical results of the Rietveld refinement including the experimental and calculated XRD profiles plus the difference curves. A polyhedral view of the crystal structure for the ternary (Ga1-x-yAlyInx)2O3 structure is displayed in Fig. 3.

It is noticeable that the unit cell parameters of the single-phase samples in the (Ga1-zAl0.69*zIn0.31*z)2O3 series (z from 0 to 0.4) show minimal dependence on the degree of gallium substitution with Al/In pairs, especially when compared to the single substituted (Ga1-yIny)2O3 and (Ga1-xAlx)2O3 materials. However, a more detailed analysis of the data presented in Table 1 and Fig. 5 reveals systematic yet opposing changes in the lattice parameters. Specifically, the a and b parameters consistently increase with rising z (from 0.1 to 0.4), while the c parameter and the monoclinic angle β decrease. As a result, the unit cell volume remains practically unchanged with only a minor increasing trend.

A sudden discontinuity in the composition dependence of the lattice parameters of the (Ga1-zAl0.69*zIn0.31*z)2O3 series is observed between z = 0.4 and 0.45, coinciding with the emergence of a secondary phase. As z increases further, the lattice parameters and unit cell volume of the monoclinic structure in multiphase (Ga1-zAl0.69*zIn0.31*z)2O3 materials progressively shift towards the values characteristic for (Ga1-yAly)2O3 series, becoming nearly identical at z = 0.7 (see Fig. 5). This behavior suggests that indium is gradually released from the monoclinic phase and incorporated into the unidentified phase mentioned earlier.

In Ref. 23, the unit cell volume of In- or Al-substituted β-Ga2O3 was parametrized as a function of an average ionic radius of M3+ cations in octahedral positions. For both (Ga1-yIny)2O3 and (Ga1-xAlx)2O3 solid solution series a nearly linear increase of the unit cell volume vs rave was observed. The doubly substituted (Ga1-x-yAlyInx)2O3 materials studied in the current work follow this tendency closely as well (see Fig. S3). A similar behavior is also found for the lattice parameters, although some deviations are noted for the c parameter and monoclinic angle β (Fig. S3). These results suggest that the monoclinic lattice parameters of a given ternary solid solution (Ga1-x-yAlyInx)2O3, can be roughly estimated using the tabulated ionic radii of constituent cations. Diffuse reflectance spectra and optical band gap

The diffuse reflectance (DR) spectra of the single-phase monoclinic pseudobinary and pseudoternary compounds are presented in Figs. 6 and 7. As shown in Fig. 6, alloying of β-Ga2O3 with Al2O3 results in an increase of the bandgap energy, while alloying with In2O3 leads to a decrease. The trend holds true under the assumption of either direct allowed or indirect allowed band-to-band transitions that is consistent with our previous findings.23.

Tauc plots derived from the room-temperature diffuse reflectance (DR) spectra of β-(Ga1-xInx)2O3, β-(Ga1-yAly)2O3 and β-(Ga1-x-yInxAly)2O3 assuming direct allowed (a) and indirect allowed (b) band-to-band transitions.

Tauc plots derived from the room-temperature DR spectra of β-(Ga1-zAl0.69*zIn0.31*z)2O3 assuming direct allowed (a) or indirect allowed (b) band-to-band transitions.

An intriguing behavior is observed for the β-(Ga1-zAl0.69*zIn0.31*z)2O3 composition. In this case, the bandgap energy remains essentially unchanged compared to β-Ga2O3 when assuming indirect allowed transitions, while it shows a noticeable increase with increasing Al content under the assumption of direct allowed transitions. However, the nature of the lowest-energy band-to-band transitions in β-(Ga1-x-yAlyInx)2O3 alloys remains an open question. Based on the band structure calculations presented by Liu et al.36 for compositions with (x + y) ≤ 0.16 it was suggested that the (Ga1-x-yAlyInx)2O3 alloys exhibit indirect band gap. However, the energy difference between the lowest-energy direct and indirect transitions was found to be only 0.02 eV in Ref.36 being well within the margin of the calculation error thus rendering this assumption inconclusive. At the same time, it is well known that β-Ga2O3 crystal, as well as its Al- and In-containing analogs, α-Al2O3 and rh-In2O3 crystal are well-established as direct-bandgap materials. This direct-gap character was also corroborated by our calculations (see Fig. S6). The character of the lowest-energy band-to-band transitions in β-(Ga1-x-yAlyInx)2O3 alloys is further discussed in section "Diffuse reflectance spectra and optical band gap", where the calculation results for the optical spectra are analyzed in detail.

DFT calculations

The calculated total energies E for all seven sets of (Ga1-zIn0.31*zAl0.69*z)2O3 supercells are presented in Fig. 8. For each z value, the origin of the energy scale is chosen at the calculated E value of the lowest-energy structure.

The calculated total energies of (Ga1-zIn0.31*zAl0.69*z)2O3 supercells with different atomic configurations grouped by z value. For z = 0.3 and 0.4, the number of InGa substitutions at tetrahedral sites is indicated.

As established in Sect. "Phase composition and crystal structure parameters of the studied compounds" InGa and AlGa substitutions preferentially occurs at octahedral Ga sites. Accordingly, for compositions with z up to 0.2 cationic substitutions were restricted to the octahedral positions. However, for higher levels of z (0.3 and 0.4) it becomes impossible to model the (Ga1-zIn0.31*zAl0.69*z)2O3 supercells using only octahedral site substitutions. At these high values of z, some tetrahedral Ga sites must be also replaced by In or Al cations. To accommodate this the numbers of In and Al cations at tetrahedral positions in our model ranged from 4–5 and 17–18 for z = 0.3 and 0.4, respectively. Specifically, the number of In ions located in tetrahedral positions varied between 0–4 and 3–6 for z = 0.3 and 0.4 respectively as indicated in Fig. 8.

The data in Fig. 8 clearly indicate that the structures containing a higher number of In ions in tetrahedral positions exhibit significantly higher total energies E. This computation result supports the conclusion drawn from the crystal structure analysis in Sect. "Phase composition and crystal structure parameters of the studied compounds" about preferential substitution of the tetrahedral Ga1 sites by Al3+, rather than In3+ ones.

The lattice parameters of (Ga1-zAl0.69*zIn0.31*z)2O3 supercells obtained from DFT geometry optimizations of the lowest-energy structures for each z are listed in Table S2, whereas the corresponding calculated unit cell volumes VUC are shown in Fig. 5e (as black circles) alongside the experimental data (red circles). Although DFT method tends to slightly overestimation unit cell volumes (see findings reported by Hizhnyi et al.62 and references therein) our calculations clearly reproduce the experimentally observed trend: VUC(z) ≈ const across the examined composition range of the (Ga1-zAl0.69*zIn0.31*z)2O3 alloys.

The calculated low-energy edge of optical absorption spectra of (Ga1-zAl0.69*zIn0.31*z)2O3 alloys are presented in Fig. 9 (the spectra in a wider energy range can be found in SI, Fig. S7). Although our electronic structure modelling is approximate, particularly as excitonic effects were not included, the results well reproduce the key features of the absorption spectra observed for these alloys in experiment.

The calculated low-energy edges of the absorption spectra of β-(Ga1-zAl0.69*zIn0.31*z)2O3 alloys for various z values.

In particular, the calculated absorption spectra well reproduce the difference in experimental fundamental absorption edge. As shown in the analysis, presented in Fig. 7 the optical band gap of (Ga1-zAl0.69*zIn0.31*z)2O3 alloys in the studied range of z is expected to be between 4.6 (for z = 0) and 4.77 eV (z = 0.4), thus meaning the difference of ~ 0.17 eV. As Fig. 9 illustrates, the value of absorption of the alloy with z = 0 at 4.6 eV is reached at 4.76 eV for alloy with z = 0.4 (see horizontal arrow in the Fig. 9), which gives almost the same difference of ~ 0.18 eV as obtained in experiment.

It should be noted that the Tauc-plot analysis in Fig. 7a was performed assuming direct allowed band-to-band transitions. Given good correlation between our calculated spectra and the Tauc-plot analysis presented Fig. 7a, we conclude that direct band-to-band transitions dominate in the monoclinic (Ga1−x−yInxAly)2O3 ternary compounds.

Overall, our calculations confirm the experimental finding for the monoclinic (Ga1-zAl0.69*zIn0.31*z)2O3 alloys as derived from the structural analysis and diffuse reflectance spectroscopy methods, showing a clear dependence on z value.

Conclusions

The phase and structural behavior in the Ga2O3–Al2O3–In2O3 pseudoternary system has been studied in detail trough the experimental XRD studies of over 50 microcrystalline samples prepared by the solution combustion method. For the first time, the range of existence of a doubly substituted monoclinic solid solution (Ga1-x-yInxAly)2O3 with varying x and y values was established, and a complete set of crystal structure parameters was determined.

Special attention was given to the compositional cross-section with a fixed x/y ratio of 0.31/0.69, i.e. corresponding to the (Ga1-zAl0.69*zIn0.31*z)2O3 compositions, which ensures the maintenance of the average cation radius equal to that of Ga3+ ions. It has been confirmed that a single-phase monoclinic structure in this series exists in the concentration range 0 ≤ z ≤ 0.4. At higher In(Al) contents, diffraction peaks of unknown phases appear in the X-ray patterns, which gradually increase with decreasing gallium content in the Ga1-zAl0.69*zIn0.31*z)2O3 and become dominant at compositions z = 0.8 and 0.9. The presence of a new unknown phase (Ga1-x-yInxAly)2O3 in the Ga2O3-Al2O3-In2O3 system has been revealed for the first time. Further research and interpretation of new crystal phases are ongoing and will be published separately.

The crystal lattice parameters and unit cell volume of the monoclinic structure were analyzed in detail across a wide range of compositions, including the non-single-phase compositions, including multiphase samples, demonstrating the possibility of targeted tuning of the monoclinic structure by simultaneously replacing gallium cations in both octahedral and tetrahedral positions. An empirical expression relating the monoclinic lattice parameters to the average radius of M3+ (M = Ga, Al, In) cations was established, allowing the prediction of the crystal lattice parameters of β-(Ga1-x-yInxAly)2O3 based solely on its chemical composition (i.e. the average ionic radius of the cations).

The crystal structure refinement suggests that the tetrahedral positions of Ga1 atoms in the monoclinic (Ga1-x-yInxAly)2O3 structures are predominantly occupied by Ga3+ and Al3+ cations, while the octahedral Ga2 positions contain a mixture of Ga3+, Al3+ and In3+ cations. The incorporation of Al3+ and In3+ cations into the monoclinic (Ga1-x-yInxAly)2O3 structure has also been confirmed by analyzing the interatomic distances within MO4 tetrahedra and MO6 octahedra (M = Al, In, Ga) in the (Ga1-zAl0.69*zIn0.31*z)2O3 structures as a function of the Al and In content (z value).

The electronic structure calculations confirmed that the tetrahedral Ga3+ lattice positions are the preferentially substituted by Al3+ ions, rather than In3+ ones. A comparison of the calculated optical absorption spectra with Tauc-plots derived from experimental diffuse reflectance spectra led to a conclusion that (Ga1−x−yInxAly)2O3 compounds possess a direct band gap.

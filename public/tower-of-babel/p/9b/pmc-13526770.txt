ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the rapid growth and development of smart cities, many real-time systems have been designed to support the dynamic infrastructure of realistic and time-constrained networks. These systems integrate the Internet of Things (IoT), emerging technologies, and edge computing to meet the demands of diverse applications and fulfill community needs. Researchers have made significant efforts to develop autonomous systems for manipulating network data, providing timely responses in critical environments, and easing communication by enabling seamless device-to-device interaction. However, some significant research challenges still persist, including the lack of intelligence and security over unpredictable communication under malfunctioning conditions. Moreover, uneven load balancing across devices with continuous link interference results in network disconnection and delays in system responsiveness. In addition, the devices’ heterogeneity and unreliable links make most existing edge-driven schemes complicate the process of optimal offloading. To address these issues, we introduce a joint optimization framework for energy-efficient, secured computation offloading and dynamic resource allocation in Edge-IoT infrastructure. It explores a Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning to make adaptive offloading decisions and establishes a secure communication path with effective resource allocation under dynamic network interactions. Furthermore, it enhanced network stability by leveraging trusted, decentralized processing points at the network edge, mitigating energy holes, and improving workload management for distributed IoT ecosystems. Using an extensive set of simulations, the proposed framework is evaluated, and the results reveal substantial improvements over recent state-of-the-art solutions in terms of both security and energy efficiency.Keywords: Internet of things, Edge computing, Artificial intelligence, Energy efficiency, Task offloadingSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In recent decades, various applications, including home automation, intelligent transportation, medical monitoring, and security surveillance, have increasingly relied on IoT networks and advanced wireless technologies1,2. Smart cities utilize thousands of sensors and processors to enable log-run and seamless device connectivity over the Internet, providing efficient network monitoring to enhance community services3,4. In addition, artificial intelligence is explored to enable connectivity across network and application layers, supporting real-time communication between IoT systems and physical objects. The IoT system also enhances communication between people, locations, events, and ideas5,6. These systems use various physical objects and sensors to gather field data, process it, and transfer it to storage units for later analysis. Connected users explore their mobile devices to collect analysis data from cloud servers and use it for their own purposes to understand the connected environment. Edge computing is a cutting-edge technology7,8 with various applications and enticing features, including low implementation and data transmission costs, unrestricted network access, and self-contained, long-term operation9,10. It has emerged as a vital technology for providing real-time analysis for sensitive, computation-intensive systems by processing tasks closer to the connected devices. However, the limited resources at the edges and the adaptive nature of the deployed system pose significant research challenges for efficient computation offloading and energy efficiency11,12. Furthermore, for consistent, fault-tolerant communication to support continued task execution across the edges, this remains a critical concern for secure distributed applications. The distributed system often requires more secure communication to prevent faulty interactions between devices and to improve consistent association for IoT devices13,14. Compromised involvement can lead to data breaches and disrupt communication channels, affecting data integrity, particularly in real-time industrial applications, thereby undermining the authenticity of the entire system through malicious traffic and vulnerable activities15,16. Moreover, the involvement of faulty messages not only degrades the performance of the IoT system but also imposes unnecessary overhead on the constrained resources. The lightweight and potential security assessment leads to the development of more reliable and resilient communication in distributed IoT-edge systems against threats and network misbehavior17,18. Most existing IoT systems experience increased energy consumption and high latency due to overloaded edge computational resources, driven by device dynamics and network conditions. Along with efficient routing, there is a demand for a joint optimization framework for real-time applications that considers multiple assessment criteria to achieve an optimal decision-making system. Moreover, the lack of secure offloading services in most existing approaches poses risks to end devices. Unbalanced traffic congestion also affects communication strength and resource utilization in a crucial environment. Ultimately, it is evident that efficient routing approaches alone are not sufficient without a trust-aware communication paradigm to promote a resilient system. Therefore, there is a lack of an intelligent, distributed framework for real-time systems to provide energy-aware, jointly optimized communication, integrating dynamic learning patterns of device interactions and secure data sharing across unreliable and heterogeneous edge environments. In this work, we introduce a predictive, intelligent edge-computing framework designed to enhance network scalability and security in IoT-based systems. Our goal is to optimize and improve decision-making criteria in smart cities by processing IoT data using edge-based intelligence along with a trustworthy system. In addition, mobile edges leverage their intelligence to provide robust, trusted mechanisms for authenticating requests. The main contributions of the proposed framework are as follows.

Related work

IoT networks integrated with edge computing enable the communication and exchange of environmental data among interconnected devices and support the development of smart cities19,20. They transform modern infrastructure by interconnecting billions of wireless devices and sensors across real-time applications, enhancing system efficiency21,22. Edge computing leverages distributed processing to reduce response time and enable real-time decision-making. The deployed system generates massive amounts of data for processing at the edge of the network, thus requiring timely system computing with communication-level security and data reliability against potential threats23,24. Malicious attacks in IoT-driven networks can target IoT devices, compromising network communication, enabling unauthorized access, and leading to inefficient decision-making processes25,26. Therefore, adaptive and lightweight routing models, as well as the integration of anomaly detection, are significant research challenges for improving resilience and fault tolerance against network threats in real-time, innovative IoT applications27–29. The authors30 proposed a solution to reduce the computational load on the centralized processing unit by exploring underutilized computing resources on network edges. Its aim is to ensure the timely execution of critical tasks and reduce energy consumption by focusing on delay-constrained multi-user networks. It formulates a joint optimization problem that includes computation task assignment, transmit association design, MIMO precoding, and computing resource allocation, based on both binary and partial computation offloading protocols. The simulation results revealed the efficacy of the proposed schemes over existing work across various performance metrics, particularly energy consumption and network latency. Authors31 proposed an Edge-Based Optimal Routing for IIoT using SDN architecture. It evaluates the environment using the k-means algorithm to select optimal forwarders based on a programmable IIoT network architecture, and provides centralized control and programmable routing. As a result, the most reliable end-to-end routes are identified with stable network resources and reduced routing issues. In addition, integrating edge computing servers (ECSs) enhances the system’s real-time functionality for connected devices and further reduces communication latency under critical network conditions. Authors32 introduce an efficient UAV-assisted NOMA-based data collection framework, aiming to improve the energy efficiency, missing duration, and reliable data capturing for dynamic networks. It integrates non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA) for uplink and provides energy-aware simultaneous data collection and processing. It highlights the uncertainty in time slots and the high-dimensional, non-convex optimization problems in UAV-enabled communication systems. In addition, the Markov Decision Process (MDP) is used to model the problem, and the Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) is used for adaptive UAV control. The simulation-based experiments have been conducted, and the results demonstrate significant improvements over the related solution in terms of efficiency and resource allocation. The study33 proposed a routing solution that addresses congestion and security concerns in IoT applications. It provides an efficient approach to handling network load while transmitting data packets from multiple devices over IoT connections. It selects the cluster heads based on multiple factors, including congestion level and signal-to-interference ratio (SINR). Data packets are securely encrypted using adaptive quantum logic for data transmission. Moreover, using the Adaptive Krill Herd (AKH) optimization technique, the least-congested corridor for data routing is identified. The study34 introduces a dynamic computation offloading framework FlexSlice in mobile edge network to optimize the network over unpredictable and dynamic traffic conditions. It explored a sparse multi-head graph attention mechanism for improved prediction and network slicing decisions using to capture spatio-temporal traffic dependencies. Moreover, proposing an adaptive offloading method that reduces communication cost by utilizing the Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (TD3) reinforcement learning approach with twin critics and prioritized experience replay. The proposed scheme is tested using simulations, and performance results demonstrate significant improvement in terms of various quality of service parameters as compared to existing approaches. The study35 introduces an Intelligent Buffalo-based Secure Edge-enabled Computing (IB-SEC) framework for smart cities using IoT networks. Its objective is to improve communication reliability while achieving effective energy consumption and low latency, along with secure transmission of devices’ data. It integrates the functionalities of both the African Buffalo Optimization (ABO) algorithm and a distributed hash function-based security algorithm, using edge computing to process authentic data in a confidential manner closer to the IoT devices. To enable SLA-aware offloading and efficient service orchestration, the authors36 proposed a CTDE-based multi-agent framework for dynamic edge networks. By exploring top-K masking and temperature scaling, the proposed framework integrates TD3 with spatiotemporal and topology-aware graph attention to efficiently model neighborhood influence with a linear coordination cost. It enhances the prediction accuracy of time-varying network states through GRU-based temporal encoding. In addition, a hybrid reward function that combines multiple factors, along with a priority-aware slicing policy, is introduced for compute allocation and resource bandwidth. In37, the authors introduce a multi-objective Arithmetic Optimization-based framework to reduce energy consumption and data latency through interaction between the cloud and fog systems. It highlights the limitations of the cloud paradigm and utilizes the proposed cloud-fog architecture for the improvement in data latency and communication overhead for IoT-based applications. In addition, the architecture highlights the importance of task prioritization and effective scheduling to enhance system availability and consistency. The study38 introduced a reliable and efficient task offloading strategy based on a multi-feedback trust mechanism (TOSMFTM) to enable more consistent decision-making in adaptive environments. Moreover, a decentralized system is explored to compute the trust and optimize the task offloading for devices using network parameters. To identify more appropriate service nodes, a trust-weighted K-means (TWK-means) clustering algorithm is developed using various resource attributes. Such a scheme leads to greater system efficiency, improved energy conservation, and a high level of service quality. Research motivation

Existing studies on edge-based IoT systems primarily focus on efficient routing schemes that incur additional computational cost. Most existing approaches assume a secure, trusted environment while overlooking the dynamic attributes of devices, leading to network compromise and affecting data privacy. On the other hand, many proposed optimization methods have been applied to real-time applications without considering delay tolerance and security sensitivity, leading to non-trusted devices communicating and failing to satisfy system constraints. Due to the lack of an integrated framework that accounts for realistic factors for assessing energy-efficient, more trustworthy systems, most researchers are still developing lightweight task offloading mechanisms to balance task distribution and leverage resource intelligence, while incorporating artificial intelligence techniques. In addition, by considering only partial analysis of the environment, the framework needs full control over the environment to collect device interactions and wireless channel data for decision-making in edge-IoT networks and to enhance communication reliability. Such motivation leads to the development of a joint optimization and trustworthy framework for real-time constraint systems capable of maintaining energy efficiency and ensuring the accomplishment of tasks with a high level of authenticity and network integrity.

Conclusion

Smart cities have integrated numerous sensors and edge/gateways to develop sustainable networks. They interconnect using future technologies to monitor the unpredictable IoT network environment and analyze the massive data generated to inform decision-making strategies. Edge computing offers parallel processing capabilities that enhance scalability and enable automated systems to deliver timely information to end devices. However, due to the limitations of IoT-driven applications, many existing approaches, particularly those supporting real-time interaction, cannot respond earlier in critical situations and pose significant challenges for security and intelligence. This work presents a computational intelligence framework for IoT-Edge resilient prediction that incorporates robust decision-making and enhances the trustworthiness of connected devices. It ensures secure data computing using blockchain technology and reduces the probability of network anomalies even in the presence of malicious threats. Additionally, the edges perform local processing and manage sensor resources effectively through offloading, while addressing data privacy and integrity concerns. On the other hand, the proposed framework also provides efficient energy consumption and lightweight data management strategies in IoT-edge networks by exploiting trust computation. Despite these significant enhancements, the learning-based optimization approach in the proposed framework still requires substantial time to converge, limiting its applicability to rapid, large-scale IoT systems. We aim to implement dynamic strategies for bandwidth allocation to further reduce computational power requirements in large-scale environments, leveraging deep learning techniques to optimize load distribution across heterogeneous networks and to address a higher degree of communication threats.

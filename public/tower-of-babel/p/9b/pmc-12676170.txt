ABSTRACT

Since 2012, the Tabba Heart Institute has been a participant in the National Cardiovascular Data Registry1 (NCDR), contributing data to both the CathPCI (cardiac catheterization–percutaneous coronary intervention) and Chest Pain-MI (myocardial infarction) registries. Initially, data collection relied on manual, paper-based processes that required separate inputs from the clinical team and the research department. Clinical data coordinators (CDCs) collected nonprocedural data, whereas procedural data were recorded by intervention/cardiology fellows and cardiac catheterization laboratory (CCL) nursing staff (Table 1). This paper-based workflow posed numerous challenges. Patient identifiers such as medical record number, admission identification number (ID), and catheterization ID often failed to align, leading to inconsistencies. Forms were frequently misplaced or incomplete, and delays in manual data entry were common. As registry requirements evolved and procedural volumes grew, the existing system became increasingly inefficient, affecting data quality, timely reporting, and overall institutional performance. In response, our institute initiated a phased digital transformation aimed at improving cardiovascular data workflows and registry submission quality.

Case Summary

Since 2012, data entry for the NCDR CathPCI and Chest Pain-MI registries was managed at our institute through the following 4 phases:•Phase 1: manual data entry (2012–August 2022). Three RAs manually entered data into 4 separate registries: CathPCI, Chest Pain-MI, and their internal versions. Nonprocedural data were collected by CDCs, and procedural data were collected by intervention/cardiology fellows and CCL nurses, with all information later re-entered by RAs. This duplication caused high error rates, inconsistent identifiers, misplaced forms, and significant reporting delays.•Phase 2: SurveyMonkey was introduced (September 2022–November 2023). The SurveyMonkey2 online survey platform was implemented for procedural data collection to reduce form loss and improve completeness. However, it remained disconnected from our institute's EHR, requiring manual patient detail entry and still causing delays, redundancy, and compliance tracking issues.•Phase 3: beta registry (EHR linked) was launched (December 2023–July 2024). In this phase, SurveyMonkey2 was replaced by an in-house beta digital registry, integrated with the live EHR in the CCL. Automatic notifications improved compliance, but RAs still manually entered data to the NCDR registries, and multiple systems persisted.•Phase 4: Cardiopulse.pk site was launched (August 2024–present): A unified, paperless registry combining the CathPCI and Chest Pain-MI fields was integrated directly with our institute's EHR. Role-based access for CDCs, fellows, and nurses allowed direct, real-time data entry, automated reporting, and elimination of RA entry into the internal registry, reducing workload and errors while improving timeliness and data quality.

Project Rationale

Reliable cardiovascular data are vital for multiple stakeholders: Clinicians require accurate information for decision-making; hospital administrators need real-time insights for operational planning; and participation in national registries such as the NCDR demands timely, complete, and high-quality data. Our institute recognized that its existing system created barriers to these goals. Manual data collection introduced variability, made compliance reporting reactive rather than proactive, and consumed valuable time from the clinical and research teams. The redundant entry of identical data across multiple platforms was inefficient, frustrating for staff, and unsustainable.

The vision was clear: a unified, digital registry platform directly integrated with the EHR system. Such integration would allow automatic population of patient data fields, real-time data access, and user-specific workflows. It would also reduce human error, improve data ownership at the point of care, and free up RA time for higher value tasks such as quality assurance and patient follow-ups.

Project Description

To realize this vision, we developed Cardiopulse.pk,3 a fully digital, paperless registry platform designed to unify CathPCI and Chest Pain-MI registry data collection under a single interface. Developed in-house by a multidisciplinary team including members from cardiology, research, and information technology (IT) Cardiopulse.pk was strategically designed for seamless EHR integration and real-time usability.

Key system features included the following:•Automated data transfer via an application programming interface (API) from the EHR after each coronary angiogram (CAG) or percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI).•Role-specific interfaces: CDCs, cardiology fellows, and CCL nurses had access to only the sections relevant to their responsibilities.•Form logic customization: Using skipping logic, data entry was tailored based on whether the case was elective, emergency, or a noncatheterization acute coronary syndrome (ACS) admission.•Daily admission imports: The IT team provided automated ACS/non-ACS admissions to the CDCs each morning, with eligible cases uploaded in bulk.•Built-in NCDR validation to ensure data completeness and prevent illogical entries.•Real-time dashboards and reporting tools for risk scores, procedure counts, and compliance tracking.•Excel spreadsheet import functions for mass entry of historical or external records.•Advanced patient search tools using multiple identifiers (eg, patient ID, admission ID, cath ID).

Cardiopulse.pk Impact Metrics

In addition to operational gains, the project resulted in an estimated annual cost savings of approximately $6,000 by eliminating the SurveyMonkey subscription and reducing staffing expenses. This was achieved through the reallocation of responsibilities for 2 RAs, one assigned to data quality assurance and the other to patient follow-up coordination, without the need for additional hires. Additional savings were realized by discontinuing manual form printing, further enhancing operational efficiency. The project's phase 3 was recognized for its innovation, earning third place at the American College of Cardiology Quality Summit 2024.

Future Directions

Currently, patient identifiers are already transferred automatically into Cardiopulse.pk via API. The next step is to align CAG, PCI, and procedure information variables in the EHR to match NCDR CathPCI and Chest Pain-MI registry specifications. We are actively collaborating with the IT and clinical teams to ensure this alignment, enabling direct transfer of complete NCDR variables from the EHR to Cardiopulse.pk.

Once this enhancement is in place, fellows and CCL nurses will document all relevant details directly in the EHR, and the full dataset will flow seamlessly via API to Cardiopulse.pk. This will further reduce the clinical team's documentation load, enhance efficiency, and ensure a fully automated, standardized registry submission process. In the first phase, alignment will focus on CAG, PCI, and procedure variables, followed by CDC-related variables in the second phase.

Discussion

This project demonstrates how a phased, EHR-integrated digital registry can transform the quality and efficiency of cardiovascular data collection. Manual, paper-based processes have long challenged health care institutions that participate in national registries, leading to errors, delays, and incomplete submissions.4,5 Our experience shows that true value from registry participation is achieved when workflows are aligned with clinical systems and fully integrated into practice.6,7

The development of Cardiopulse.pk directly addressed barriers in registry compliance such as fragmented workflows, duplication of effort, and lack of real-time visibility. By creating a home-grown system that linked with our EHR and enabled role-specific entry, we strengthened ownership and accountability among CDCs, fellows, and nursing staff. Unlike commercially available platforms, which are often prohibitively expensive for low- and middle-income countries and even underutilized in some U.S. centers,8 this in-house solution offered an affordable and sustainable model. Its locally developed nature fostered collaboration between the health care and IT sectors, showing how regional needs can be met in a cost-effective way.9

Custom skipping logic and automated validation improved usability and compliance, and RAs were reallocated from manual entry to quality assurance and patient follow-ups. This optimization not only reduced administrative burden but also enhanced data integrity, a benefit similarly highlighted in registry-based initiatives that integrate digital health technologies and real-world data.10,11

High-quality registry data are essential for benchmarking, process improvement, and national reporting.4,5 Our single-center experience demonstrates that locally developed, affordable platforms can strengthen registry participation, improve accuracy, and build sustainable collaboration between health care and IT, with potential for broader scalability.12,13

Conclusions

Cardiopulse.pk3 is a model of how automation and integration can modernize registry data collection in cardiovascular care. By unifying the CathPCI and Chest Pain-MI registries into a single platform directly linked to the EHR, our institute achieved significant gains in accuracy, efficiency, and user satisfaction.

The Cardiopulse.pk platform has not only improved compliance with NCDR1 reporting standards but also positioned our institute to leverage its data infrastructure for broader quality initiatives.

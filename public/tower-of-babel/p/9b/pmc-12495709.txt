Analysis of factors influencing digital literacy of rural elderly people-evidence from China
Authors: Zhaoting Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTo analyze the factors influencing the digital Literacy of the rural elderly, this study conducted interviews with people aged 60 and above in rural towns in eastern, central, and western China. The study examines the correlation between digital literacy and influencing factors among the elderly.MethodsThe categories of digital literacy and influencing factors were first identified. The study selected digital activities such as digital operations and digital social interactions, along with influencing factors such as education, income, and health status, for correlation analysis.ResultsThe correlation analysis showed that good education, income, and health status are significantly positively correlated with digital literacy.ConclusionMost aspects of digital literacy are significantly positively correlated with educational background, income level, and health status. Therefore, it is important to provide education and training opportunities to improve the digital skills of rural elderly people with low education and income levels. Enhancing social welfare for the rural elderly and improving their economic living standards are crucial. Additionally, more attention to the health status of the rural elderly will help increase their acceptance and usage of digital technology. Improving digital literacy can lead to higher happiness and better quality of life for the rural elderly.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-24691-z.

Introduction

China is currently undergoing significant transformations driven by technological advancements and social changes. Technological innovations profoundly alter lifestyles and structures, and the interplay between an aging population and digital transformation is becoming increasingly pronounced [1]. The accelerating pace of digital transformation has highlighted the existence of a digital divide. This trend is observed globally, with the European Commission’s “2021 Digital Economy and Society Index” indicating that only 56% of individuals in the EU have average digital skills [2]. Owing to disparities in digital infrastructure and training, there is a digital divide between urban and rural areas within the EU, with rural areas lagging behind cities in digital technology adoption [3]. This divide in digital skills and literacy exists not only between urban and rural populations, but also across different age groups [4]. In particular, older adults in rural areas often face challenges related to inadequate digital literacy, which not only limits their ability to benefit from digital conveniences, but also impacts their daily lives and social participation [5, 6]. In the context of urban-rural integration, fostering digital Literacy among farmers has become a priority. In 2024, China’s National Development and Reform Commission and the National Data Bureau issued the “Digital Economy Promotion for Common Prosperity Implementation Plan” (hereafter referred to as “the Plan”). The Plan aims to narrow the digital literacy gap between urban and rural areas and ensure that digital dividends benefit all residents. This calls for improvements to address the “digital difficulties” faced by elderly individuals in rural areas, advancing digital village construction, and enhancing digital literacy [7]. To improve digital Literacy among elderly rural residents, it is crucial to first analyze their digital literacy and the factors that influence it. This study focuses on the relationship between digital literacy and its influencing factors among elderly individuals over 60 years of age in rural towns in China’s eastern, central, and western regions. Although this interview was not a medical study, it was conducted with the intention of protecting the rights and privacy of the participants. Through interviews and research, this study aims to identify factors that can enhance the digital literacy of elderly rural residents, seek to attract social and governmental attention, and improve the conditions that can boost their digital literacy, thereby meeting their digital living needs.

The latest findings of this study include clearly identifying the components of digital literacy among elderly individuals in rural areas, encompassing seven major dimensions: digital device operation, digital social interaction, digital finance, digital agriculture, digital cultural activities, digital security, and digital ethics. These are FUrther detailed into 11 specific types of digital activities, including sending and receiving information, information searching, social networking, digital finance, and digital commerce activities. Secondly, through interviews and Spearman correlation analysis, this study has verified the relationships between various factors—such as education level, income status, living arrangements (living alone or with children), and health status—and the digital literacy of elderly residents in rural areas.Compared to other literature, the distinctive contribution of this research lies in its scope and sample selection. This study specifically selected three representative rural areas in China’s eastern, central, and western regions—Qilin Town in Puning City, Guangdong Province; Huzupu Town in Gushi County, Henan Province; and Wangyang Town in Renshou County, Sichuan Province—for sample surveys across a broader geographic range. The regional coverage and representativeness of this approach exceed previous studies that typically focused only on a single area or the eastern region. The reasons and justifications for selecting these locations include considerations of population representativeness and regional diversity. The study assumes that elderly populations in the most populous areas are more representative. Additionally, it takes into account regional differences in economic and social development, infrastructure construction, and cultural and educational levels among eastern, central, and western regions, which can help better identify and understand the factors affecting the digital literacy of elderly individuals.

Theoretical overview and literature review

Theoretical overview

The concept of digital literacy has been continuously evolving with societal development, and different scholars have interpreted it differently, employing different factors for analysis and research [8]. Israeli scholar Yoram Eshet first introduced the concept of “digital literacy,” summarizing a framework that includes “visual literacy, re-creation literacy, branching literacy, information literacy, and social-emotional literacy.” He defined digital literacy as a comprehensive set of scientific skills and cultural competencies required to rapidly and effectively discover, acquire, evaluate, integrate, and communicate information in a digital environment [9]. Marshr argues that digital literacy is not about confidently use digital tools and facilities. With the implementation of strategic policies in China, digital literacy has gradually become an essential quality for urban and rural residents in their work and daily lives [10]. The “Action Outline for Enhancing National Digital Literacy and Skills,” issued by the Chinese government, states that digital literacy encompasses a series of qualities and capabilities necessary for digital citizenship in digital society, including digital awareness, computational thinking, digital learning and innovation, and digital social responsibility [11]. The 2.2 version of the “EU Digital Competence Framework for Citizens” divides digital Literacy into five dimensions and includes 21 specific competencies. More importantly, this framework categorizes digital literacy into four levels—basic, intermediate, advanced, and professional—each further subdivided into two sub-levels, forming a four-level eight-segment framework system. It provides a direction for evaluating digital literacy through descriptions of knowledge, skills, and attitudes [12]. The “Global Framework for Digital Literacy,” released by UNESCO in 2018, includes seven areas of digital Literacy and 26 specific competencies, detailing the specific levels each competency should achieve. This lays the foundation for the development of tools to assess digital literacy [13]. Based on the above, this paper combines the contents of UNESCO’s “Global Framework for Digital Literacy” to summarize four dimensions: “basic knowledge, professional skills, individual traits, and awareness and attitude.” It analyzes the practical needs, learning characteristics, and level of digital technology adoption among elderly rural residents, covering areas such as digital operations, digital social interaction, digital finance, digital agricultural production, digital culture, digital security and health, and digital ethics and morality [14]. This analysis aimed to construct a possible framework for the digital literacy needs of elderly individuals in the digital age(Fig. 1).

Based on the above theories, digital literacy for rural residents includes a range of digital-related abilities and qualities, which can be broadly divided into two levels: basic and advanced literacy [15]. Basic literacy involves the fundamental skills necessary for daily life and production activities, such as digital information retrieval, digital social interaction, digital finance, digital agriculture, and digital governance. These are the essential digital skills that farmers need for routine activities. Advanced literacy, however, focuses more on rural innovation and values, encompassing digital culture, digital creativity, digital security and health, and digital ethics and morality. For elderly rural residents, advanced digital literacy manifests in various ways, including receiving and sending information, watching electronic videos, taking photos or videos with mobile phones, searching for and querying information, listening to electronic music, playing video games, engaging in digital financial activities (such as online banking transactions), participating in social networking activities, conducting lnternet commerce (such as shopping), and filtering and using information [16]. These competencies require elderly rural residents not only to have the ability to use digital technology, but also to consciously apply it to enhance their personal well-being and improve their quality of life.

Literature review

From the perspective of literature, Numerous factors influence digital literacy among elderly rural residents, and analyzing these factors can be approached from multiple dimensions, including personal, social, and technological levels. These include socio-economic factors, individual education levels, family factors, social living environment factors, and personal health factors [17–19]. Research in this area spans various fields, and many scholars have examined it from different perspectives.Through their study of digital literacy among rural residents in Indonesia, Iskandar and colleagues found that education level has a very positive impact on the use of digital platforms, such as the Internet [20]. Individuals with higher educational levels are more likely to adapt to and use new digital technologies [21]. Azeez et al. studied digital financial literacy among rural residents and elderly individuals in India and, found that factors such as income [21], household size, and marital status had a significant impact on digital literacy [22]. Some scholars have argued that health status is an important factor that affects digital literacy. Individuals with poorer health may encounter more barriers in operation and comprehension, and the ability to acquire, understand, and use health-related information contributes to improved digital health literacy [23]. Although many scholars have analyzed digital literacy in rural areas from various perspectives, there is limited research specifically focusing on digital literacy and its influencing factors among elderly rural residents. However, this requires further investigation. This study focuses on the digital literacy of elderly rural residents and its influencing factors to provide a theoretical foundation and empirical support for developing targeted intervention measures and policies. The goal was to attract social attention and improve the quality of life offor elderly rural residents. This is the contribution of this study. Based on the relevant literature and research analysis, this study summarizes several aspects influencing digital literacy among elderly rural residents: education (including different educational levels), economic situation (including individual income levels), family situation (including marital status and whether living with children), and health staitus (including classifications of health status such as healthy, generally healthy, and unhealthy) [24].

Data and methods

Sample selection

To analyze the factors influencing digital literacy among elderly rural residents, it is essential to choose a representative sample of this population. The representativeness of the rural elderly sample was generally positively correlated with the total population of the region. Therefore, assuming that samples from more populous areas are more representative, the following sample selection is based on this assumption.Mainland China (excluding Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Macau) consists of 31 provinces, autonomous regions, and municipalities. These regions can be categorized as eastern, central, and western areas. To study the impact of digital literacy on the health status of elderly rural residents in China, elderly residents from one township in the eastern, central, and western regions were selected as research samples. combining both targeted and random selections. The targeted selection involved choosing the most populous provinces in each region: Guangdong in the eastern region, Henan in the central region, and Sichuan in the western region [25]. Within the most populous provinces, the county with the highest population was selected, and within that county, the most populous township was chosen as the regional sample. From these three sample townships, 240 elderly rural residents were randomly selected as participants.

The logic behind This sampling method is based on the assumption that elderly residents from the most populous administrative regions in eastern, central, and western China are representative of the sample. The selected sample areas were Qilin Township in Puning City, Guangdong Province (eastern region), Huzu Pu Township in Gushi County, Henan Province (central region), and Wangyang Township in Renshou County, Sichuan Province (western region). The population of Qilin Township is 134,800 [26], Huzu Pu Township has a population of 83,900 [27], and Wangyang Township has a population of 149,900 [28]. The total sample size for this study was 240 individuals, with samples drawn proportionally from the townships based on population size: 88 elderly residents from Qilin Township, 55 from Huzu Pu Township, and 97 from Wangyang Township. Among the 240 participants, there were 121 elderly men and 119 were older women.

Research method

To account for the educational background and knowledge levels of elderly rural residents, a qualitative research method was employed, specifically interviews. Interviews allowed for comprehensive observation and communication with elderly residents. The interviews were conducted in a structured manner. The content of the structured interviews was designed in advance based on the research objectives and included questions about the participants’ age, education level, family status, health condition, and digital Literacy. During the preparation phase, interviewers were trained to ensure consistency and effectiveness in data collection. After obtaining informed consent from the elderly rural residents, trained interviewers conducted face-to-face interviews with the participants using tablet-assisted personal interview techniques from June 6 to August 10, 2024. Participants provided informed consent, with consent obtained verbally. The consent process involved two interviewers: one recorded the verbal consent, while the other supervised. During or after data collection, the authors were able to access information identifying individual participants.

To analyze the factors influencing the digital literacy of elderly rural residents, it is necessary to first identify the categories of digital literacy and its influencing factors. After the analysis and research, the components of digital literacy were identified, including seven aspects: digital device operation, digital social interaction, digital finance, digital agriculture, digital cultural activities, digital security, and digital ethics. The influencing factors were categorized into six aspects: personal information such as age, marital status, education level, living conditions, income level, and health status.

In designing the interview structure to evaluat digital Literacy, the research focused on content related to digital device operation, digital social interaction, digital finance, and so on. The interview design included seven major categories and 11 subcategories, covering areas such as the daily operation and use of mobile phones and other electronic devices, information acquisition and transmission, digital information searching, video games, and online social interactions. The response options for the questions were as follows: 1 = frequently used, 2 = infrequently used, 3 = not used, and both 1 and 2 were categorized within the range of usage. For the interview structure regarding influencing factors, the content was designed around six aspects with 13 subcategories, age, marital status, education level, living conditions, income level, and health status. Toevaluate the health status of elderly rural residents, a self-assessment method was employed based on the “Chinese Healthy Elderly Standard” published by the National Health Commission of China [29]. Health status was categorized into three levels: healthy (80 ≤ health ≤ 100 points), Generally Healthy (60 ≤ Generally Healthy < 80 points), and unhealthy (< 60 points). This study used the internationally recognized SPSS software to analyze the correlation between digital literacy and its influencing factors among the elderly. Using SPSS, the relationship between digital literacy and influencing factors was examined to determine whether there was a correlation and the degree of that correlation. According to the analysis in Table 2, the average age of males was 71.26 years, females 70.19 years, and the overall average age of the study subjects was 70.71 years, with a standard deviation of 4.97. A P-value < 0.001 indicates that there is a significant difference between the average ages of the two groups, and this difference is highly unlikely to be due to chance. In terms of average years of education, rural males had an average of 9.8 years, females 9.1 years, and the elderly sample hads an average of 9.46 years, with a standard deviation of 0.03. A P-value < 0.001 indicates that the difference is highly statistically significant and unlikely to be due to chance. For self-rated health, the internationally recognized self-rated health value (1–5) was used [30]. The self-rated health value for males is 2.39, for females 2.48, and the overall average self-rated health value for the elderly sample is 2.45, with a standard deviation of 0.799. A P-value < 0.001 indicates that the difference in self-rated health values between the two groups is statistically significant, meaning that this difference is highly unlikely to be caused by random error. In terms of weekly digital information usage time, elderly males spend an average of 8.69 h per week, while females spend an average of 7.52 h per week. The overall average weekly digital information usage time for the elderly sample was 8.15 h, with a standard deviation of 9.42. A P-value < 0.001 indicates that the difference in weekly information technology usage time between males and females is highly statistically significant, meaning that this difference is not random. Therefore, the interview and survey data are of practical significance.

The correlation analysis in Table 3,shows that the nine digital literacy activities—receiving information, sending information, taking photos or videos with mobile phones, listening to digital music, watching digital videos, playing video games, engaging in social networking activities, digital financial activities (such as online banking transactions), and online commerce activities (such as shopping)—are all significantly correlated with five factors: university or higher education, high school education, Living alone, above-average income, and good health. The correlation coefficients were all greater than 0, indicating a positive correlation between these nine digital activities and the five factors mentioned above. At the same time, these nine digital activities did not show significant correlations with marital status, middle school education, primary school education, illiteracy, Living with children, below-average income, basic health, or poor health, as their correlation coefficients were close to 0, suggesting no relationship between them. This indicates that having university or higher education, high school education, living alone, above-average income, and good health positively influence most digital activities, and these factors can enhance digital literacy levels. Additionally, information search and retrieval, and information filtering, show significant correlations with middle school education, primary school education, living with children, below-average income, basic health, and poor health. However, these activities did not correlate significantly with marital status, university or higher education, high school education, illiteracy, living alone, above-average income, or good health. This suggests that the factors influencing information search retrieval, and information filtering differ from those influencing other digital activities.

From the interview content in Table 1, it is evident that rural elderly men spend 8.69 h per week using digital information technology, while rural elderly women spend 7.52 h per week, with men spending 1.17 h more than women. In terms of specific digital activities, —such as sending and receiving information on mobile phones, playing digital music games, engaging in online social networking, online transactions, and digital financial activities, —men’s participation rates are higher than women’s. This indicates that men generally have a higher rate of engagement in digital tool activities than women do. Discussion

Specific demographic characteristics and health conditions have a significant impact on various aspects of digital literacy in the rural elderly population. Regarding the influence of educational level, elderly individuals with a college degree or higher, or a high school education, show a significant positive correlation with multiple digital activities. This indicates that elderly individuals with higher educational levels are more likely to engage in and use various digital tools and services. This may be because individuals with higher education tend to have stronger learning abilities and higher levels of digital cognition, making them more adaptable to and accepting of new technologies [31]. In contrast, elderly individuals with lower educational levels (middle school, elementary school, or no education) did not show significant correlations with most digital activities, suggesting lower participation in digital activities. This could reflect the fact that individuals with lower education levels may face more barriers when using digital technology, such as a lack of technical knowledge and operational skills. Regarding the influence of living arrangements, oelder adults living alone showed a positive correlation with digital activities. This may be because those who live alone rely more on digital technology to maintain contact with the outside world, such as through social networks for social interaction and electronic banking for financial management [32]. Conversely, there wais no significant correlation between elderly individuals living with their children and digital activities, possibly because those living with their children rely on them to handle digital matters, leading to fewer opportunities for personal involvement.

Regarding the influence of economic status, elderly individuals with an income above the average level are more inclined to participate in multiple digital activities. This may be because those with better financial situations have more resources to purchase digital devices and services, and are more motivated to use digital technology to manage their finances and daily lives [33]. On the other hand, elderly individuals with income below the average level may use digital technology less because limited resources or a lack of necessary digital devices and skills. In terms of the impact of health status, the positive correlation between elderly individuals with good health and digital activities suggests that healthier seniors are more capable and willing to engage in digital activities. Good health makes them more mentally and physically able to adopt new technologies and digital tools [34]. Elderly individuals with poor or basic health may be less willing to use digital technology because of physical discomfort. The factors influencing information search and filtering usage differ from those of other digital activities, being more influenced by lower educational levels, living with children, lower income, and poor health status [35]. This may indicate that these specific digital skills are more valued and utilized among oelder adults with lower education, lower income, and poorer health, possibly because their needs in these areas are more urgent.

Additionally, there are gender differences in the basic characteristics and digital literacy of rural elderly people. Elderly men tend to have more education, higher income levels, and spend more time using digital technology compared to elderly women. Men also generally have higher participation in digital activities, especially in areas such as sending and receiving mobile messages, listening to electronic music, playing games, social networking, online transactions, and digital financial activities. This may be because men traditionally show more interest in new technologies and are more willing to spend time learning and using them [36].The lower digital participation among elderly women could be related to cultural factors, societal role expectations, and historically fewer opportunities to acquire education and technical skills [37].

Compared with the findings of other studies, this research found a significant positive correlation between educational level (particularly university and high school education) and digital literacy among elderly people, consistent with the results of Indonesian scholar Iskandar et al. Compared to the study by Azeez et al. on elderly populations in rural India, this research specifically highlights the impact of living arrangements (living alone or not) on digital technology usage. In contrast to the study by Korean scholar Gyeong et al., which examined the purposes and effects of internet usage among elderly Koreans, this research emphasizes the specific pathways through which health status affects digital literacy. Additionally, the gender differences identified in this study align with findings from Zhao Wenlong et al. regarding digital trust among elderly Chinese. However, this research explores gender differences in specific types of digital technology usage in greater depth, revealing that males are more inclined to engage in information sending, social networking, and digital financial activities, thereby enriching the existing understanding of the relationship between gender and the digital divide.

The findings of this study have clear policy implications and practical significance for improving digital literacy among elderly populations in rural China. Regarding educational programs targeting low-education and low-income groups, the study demonstrates that education and income levels significantly affect digital literacy. Therefore, free digital skills training programs should be regularly conducted in rural areas, aimed at elderly individuals with low education and low income, teaching basic digital device operations, social media usage, and online financial transaction skills. In terms of strengthening health care to increase digital participation, health status shows a significant positive impact on the willingness to use digital technologies. It is recommended that local governments enhance rural healthcare infrastructure, regularly perform health checkups for elderly residents, and provide targeted health promotion programs to boost their willingness and ability to participate digitally. Regarding specialized actions to narrow the gender digital divide, based on identified gender differences, it is suggested to initiate digital skill training programs and economic support measures specifically aimed at elderly women in rural areas, such as offering female-oriented smart device courses and financial incentives to encourage women’s participation in digital activities.

Limitation

This study of the digital literacy of rural elderly individuals and their influencing factors has certain limitations. First, in terms of sample selection, the rural elderly sample was selected from three counties in the eastern, central, and western regions of China. Although factors such as regional characteristics, population size, and economic differences were considered, the sample may not have FUlly covered all relevant areas. Second, regarding the selection of 11 digital Literacy activities and 13 influencing factors, although the study was conducted with careful research, discussion, and selection based on literature, there may still be some aspects that are not fully comprehensive. Overall, this study made every effort to consider regional differences and the characteristics of the rural elderly sample, ensuring the scientific rigor of the research.

Conclusion

The correlation analysis indicates that most aspects of digital literacy have significant positive correlations with factors such as education level, income level, and health status. Therefore, enhancing social welfare for elderly individuals in rural areas and improving their economic conditions is recommended. Additionally, greater attention to the health status of elderly people and improvements in medical care conditions could help increase their acceptance and use of digital technologies. Meanwhile, elderly women in rural areas should be encouraged to engage in various digital activities through targeted policies and programs implemented by government agencies or social organizations.

The study also found that different digital activities are influenced by different factors. Education level, living situation, income level, health status, and gender weare all significant factors affecting the digital literacy of elderly rural individuals. This indicates that improving digital literacy among the elderly requires comprehensive consideration of various factors and the development of tailored strategies for specific groups to enhance their digital participation and usage capabilities. By doing so, improvement in digital literacy can lead to an increase in the happiness index and quality of life of the rural elderly population.

Evaluation of confirmatory data following the Article 12 MRL review for spinetoram
Authors: European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), Giulia Bellisai, Giovanni Bernasconi, Luis Carrasco Cabrera, Irene Castellan, Monica del Aguila, Lucien Ferreira, Luna Greco, Samira Jarrah, Renata Leuschner, Andrea Mioč, Stefanie Nave, Hermine Reich, Silvia Ruocco, Alessia Pia Scarlato, Andrea Simonati, Marta Szot, Anne Theobald, Olha Timofieieva, Manuela Tiramani, Alessia Verani, Elena Zioga
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe applicant Corteva Agriscience International Sàrl submitted a request to the competent national authority in Croatia to evaluate the confirmatory data that were identified in the framework of the MRL review under Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. The applicant submitted, a confirmatory analytical method for high oil content and dry plant commodities, fat, liver, kidney and an enforcement analytical method for eggs (quantification and confirmation) and its ILV in the current application. Furthermore, residue trials for table and wine grapes, apples, pears and apricots were also provided. The data gaps on the analytical methods were considered as satisfactorily addressed for all relevant matrices except for the extraction efficiency which was not satisfactorily addressed. Since the approval period of spinetoram in the EU expired on 30 June 2024, the data gap on residue trials to support EU uses was not considered relevant anymore and a revision of the previous consumer risk assessment was not required.

Summary

In 2020, when reviewing the existing maximum residue levels (MRLs) according to Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, EFSA identified some information as unavailable (data gaps) and derived tentative MRLs for those uses which were not fully supported by data but for which no risk to consumers was identified. The following data gaps were noted: confirmatory analytical method for high oil content and dry commodities;confirmatory analytical method for liver, kidney and fat;analytical method for eggs;additional residue trials on pome fruits, apricots, table and wine grapes, blackberries, dewberries, cranberries, gooseberries, rose hips, mulberries, azaroles and elderberries.

Tentative MRL proposals have been implemented in the MRL legislation by Commission Regulation (EU) No 2021/1110, including footnotes related to all the data gaps reported above, and indicating the type of confirmatory data that should be provided by a party having an interest in maintaining the proposed tentative MRL by 7 July 2023.

Meanwhile, on 30 June 2024, the approval of spinetoram expired because no applicant supported the renewal of approval of the active substance. Spinetoram is, therefore, no longer approved for plant protection uses in the EU. Considering the new situation, the data gap number (4), related to additional residue trials, became obsolete for all EU uses.

It is noted that, following the expiration of approval of spinetoram, all current MRLs based on EU uses would need to be revoked, except for those supporting Codex MRLs or for specific MRLs based on import tolerances. Therefore, a review of all MRLs set for spinetoram may be considered by risk managers. However, a comprehensive review of all the existing MRLs is out of the scope of this confirmatory data assessment and EFSA focused on the assessment of data related to Article 12 confirmatory data gaps numbers (1), (2) and (3).

In accordance with the agreed procedure set out in the working document SANTE/10235/2016, Corteva Agriscience International Sàrl submitted an application to the competent national authority in the Croatia (rapporteur Member State, RMS) to evaluate the confirmatory data identified during the MRL review.

The application, alongside the dossier containing the supporting data in IUCLID format, was submitted through the EFSA Central Submission System on 3 July 2023. The appointed RMS Croatia assessed the dossier and declared its admissibility on 11 April 2024. Subsequently, following the implementation of the EFSA's confidentiality decision, the non‐confidential version of the dossier was published by EFSA, and a public consultation launched on the dossier. The consultation aimed to consult stakeholders and the public on the scientific data, studies and other information part of, or supporting, the submitted application, to identify whether other relevant scientific data or studies are available. The consultation run from 4 June 2024 to 26 June 2024. No additional data nor comments were submitted in the framework of the consultation.

At the end of the commenting period, the RMS proceeded drafting the evaluation report in accordance with Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, which was submitted to the European Commission and EFSA on 16 January 2025.

On 24 February 2025, the European Commission sent a mandate to EFSA to assess the application and the evaluation report as required by Article 10 of the MRL regulation. When assessing the evaluation report, EFSA identified data gaps which needed further clarification and requested the RMS to address them. On 29 May 2025, the applicant provided the requested information in an updated IUCLID dossier. The additional information was duly considered by the RMS who submitted a revised evaluation report to EFSA on 2 June 2025 (Croatia, 2025), which replaced the previously submitted evaluation report.

A confirmatory analytical method for high oil content and dry plant commodities was provided for determination of spinetoram (XDE‐175‐J and XDE‐175‐L) in barley grain (dry commodity) and oilseed rape (seeds) (commodity with high oil content) at the combined limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.02 mg/kg according to the residue definition of enforcement and satisfactorily addressed the data gap number (1).

A confirmatory analytical method for fat, liver and kidney and a sufficiently validated enforcement method for eggs were provided for determination of spinetoram (XDE‐175‐J and XDE‐175‐L) at the combined LOQ of 0.02 mg/kg according to the residue definition for enforcement and satisfactorily addressed data gaps number (2) and (3).

Extraction efficiency of the enforcement analytical methods was considered as not addressed according to applicable guidance for all matrices under consideration (e.g. plant commodities with high oil content, dry plant commodities, fat, liver, kidney and eggs) which represents a source of uncertainty to be noted by risk managers.

Newly provided residue data for EU uses were not considered relevant anymore and were not assessed, since spinetoram is no longer approved in the EU.

The consumer risk assessment of the MRL review was considered still valid with the total chronic intake accounting for 12% acceptable daily intake (ADI). It is to be noted that, after the MRL review, a CXL of 0.5 mg/kg in pitaya (prickly pears/cactus fruits) was implemented by Commission Regulation (EU) 2023/1069, with insignificant contribution to the overall consumer exposure.

ASSESSMENT

The review of existing MRLs for the active substance according to Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 1 (MRL review) has been performed in 2020 (EFSA, 2020). The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) identified some information as unavailable (data gaps) and derived tentative MRLs for those uses not fully supported by data but for which no risk to consumers was identified.

Following the review of existing MRLs, the legal limits have been modified by Commission Regulation (EU) No 2021/1110, 2 including footnotes for tentative MRLs that specified the type of information that was identified as missing. Any party having an interest in maintaining the proposed tentative MRL was requested to address the confirmatory data by 7 July 2023.

Meanwhile, on 30 June 2024, the approval of spinetoram expired because no applicant supported the renewal of approval of the active substance. Spinetoram is, therefore, no longer approved for plant protection uses in the EU. Considering the new situation, the data gap number that was set in relation to additional residue trials identified in the MRL review became obsolete for all EU uses.

In accordance with the specific provisions set out in the working document of the European Commission SANTE/10235/2016 (European Commission, 2023c) and following the provisions set by the ‘Transparency Regulation’ (EU) 2019/1381, 3 the applicant Corteva Agriscience International Sàrl, on 3 July 2023, submitted an application to the competent national authority in Croatia (designated rapporteur Member State, RMS) to evaluate the confirmatory data identified during the MRL review, alongside the dossier containing the supporting data using the IUCLID format.

To address the relevant data gaps number (1), (2) and (3) identified by EFSA, the applicant provided a confirmatory analytical method for high oil content and dry plant commodities, a confirmatory analytical method for liver, kidney and fat and an analytical method for eggs including its validation and ILV. To address the data gap number (4) identified by EFSA, the applicant submitted residue trials on pome fruits (apples and pears), apricots and table and wine grapes. However, given the expiration of approval of spinetoram in June 2024, these latter data in relation to additional residue trials became obsolete for all EU uses.

The RMS assessed the new information in an evaluation report, which was submitted to the European Commission and forwarded to EFSA on 16 January 2025 (Croatia, 2025). EFSA assessed the application as requested by the European Commission in accordance with Article 10 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. During the assessment, EFSA identified data gaps which needed further clarifications and requested the RMS to address them. On 25 May 2025, the applicant provided the requested information in an updated IUCLID dossier. The additional information was duly considered by the EMS who submitted a revised evaluation report to EFSA on 2 June 2025 (Croatia, 2025), which replaced the previously submitted evaluation report.

EFSA based its assessment on the evaluation report submitted by the RMS (Croatia, 2025) and the reasoned opinion on the MRL review according to Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 (EFSA, 2020).

For this application, the data requirements established in Regulation (EU) No 283/2013 4 and the relevant guidance documents at the date of implementation of the confirmatory data requirements by Regulation (EU) No 2021/1110 are applicable. The assessment is performed in accordance with the legal provisions of the Uniform Principles for the Evaluation and the Authorisation of Plant Protection Products adopted by Commission Regulation (EU) No 546/2011. 5

An updated list of end points, including the end points of relevant studies assessed previously and the confirmatory data evaluated in this application, is presented in Appendix B.

The evaluation report submitted by the RMS (Croatia, 2025) is made publicly available as a background document to this reasoned opinion. 6

Analytical methods for enforcement purposes in plant commodities

To address data gap number (1) on a confirmatory analytical method for high oil content and dry commodities, the applicant provided method validation studies for the measurement of spinetoram (XDE‐175‐J and XDE‐175‐L) with a combined LOQ of 0.02 mg/kg according to the residue definition for enforcement 7 in barley grain (dry commodity) and oilseed rape (seeds) (commodity with high oil content) (Croatia, 2025).

It is to be noted that, for quantification and confirmation, the same column C18 YMC‐ODS‐AM (50 × 4.6 mm, 5 μm) and mass transitions: m/z 749/142 for spinetoram‐J and m/z 761/142 for spinetoram‐L and mobile phases were used. Due to the lack of a confirmatory MS/MS transitions, further confirmation of residues was achieved, by re‐injecting the sample on a separate high‐performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) column with different stationary phase and significantly different selectivity (Polar‐RP 80A, 75 × 4.6 mm, 4 μm) (Croatia, 2025). This is considered as acceptable for confirmation.

Two short comings were identified and considered as minor deviations: For oilseed rape seeds (high‐oil commodity) (study report ID No. 140001 in IUCLID): for confirmation of spinetoram XDE‐175‐L, an average matrix effect of (−71)% is reported, which was corrected to an average matrix effect with internal standard calibration (ISTD (%)) of (−26)%. The latter is still outside the acceptable range of (+/−) 20% and would need to be considered as ‘significant matrix effect’ according to the applicable guidance (European Commission, 2023a). The RMS concluded that this is a minor deviation considering that all other validation parameters are acceptable (Croatia, 2025). RESIDUES IN LIVESTOCK

The confirmatory data assessed in this evaluation do not have an impact on the pesticide residues expected in livestock. Thus, the previous assessment of residues in livestock (EFSA, 2020) was not updated.

Nature of residues and methods of analysis in livestock

To address data gaps number 2 8 and 3 9 the applicant provided a confirmatory LC‐MS/MS method for fat, liver and kidney and an LC‐MS/MS method (quantification, confirmation and an independent laboratory validation (ILV)) for poultry eggs for spinetoram (XDE‐175‐J and XDE‐175‐L) according to the residue definition for enforcement (Croatia, 2025).

The residue definition for enforcement of spinetoram residues in commodities of animal origin is defined as ‘spinetoram (sum of spinetoram‐J and spinetoram‐L) (equivalent to XDE‐175 (sum of XDE‐175‐J and XDE‐175‐L))’.

For confirmation of the LC‐MS/MS method for fat, liver and kidney, a different HPLC column with different stationary phase and significantly different selectivity (for confirmation: Polar‐RP column (100 × 3 mm, 2.5 μm) and for quantification: C18 YMC‐ODS‐AM column (50 × 4.6 mm, 5 μm)) was used (Croatia, 2025). This is considered acceptable for confirmation.

The same LC‐MS/MS method was validated also for eggs covering quantification and confirmation of spinetoram (XDE‐175‐J and XDE‐175‐L) at the combined LOQ of 0.02 mg/kg (Croatia, 2025). In addition, an acceptable ILV using an LC‐MS/MS method was also provided (Croatia, 2025).

Extraction efficiency of the extraction solvent acetonitrile/water (80%/20% (v/v)) used in the validated method for enforcement according to the applicable guidance (European Commission, 2023b) was not addressed (Croatia, 2025).

EFSA concluded that the data gaps number (2) and (3) identified in the framework of the MRL review were addressed. It is, however, noted that extraction efficiency of the LC‐MS/MS confirmatory method for fat, liver and kidney and of the enforcement method for eggs was not addressed. The lack of these data introduces additional uncertainty of the present assessment.

Details on the validation of the analytical method are presented in Appendix B.1.2.1.

CONSUMER RISK ASSESSMENT

In the light of the recent expiry of approval of spinetoram in Europe, the data gaps identified in the MRL review related to the EU uses became obsolete. The remaining submitted confirmatory data did not trigger an update of the risk assessment performed in the framework of the most recent assessments of (EFSA, 2020, 2022). The conclusions derived therein are still valid; no consumer intake concerns were identified for spinetoram residues in food commodities and the total chronic intake accounted for a maximum of 12% of the ADI.

It is further noted that, in 2022, a CXL on pitaya (prickly pears/cactus fruits) of 0.5 mg/kg was implemented in the EU legislation and contributed with 14% acute reference dose (ARfD) to the acute risk assessment (EFSA, 2022). This CXL had no significant impact on chronic exposure of 12% ADI (NL toddler) and the outcome of the comprehensive previous assessment of the MRL review (EFSA, 2020) remained valid.

CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS

To address the data gaps identified in the framework of the MRL review (EFSA, 2020), validation data for the confirmatory analytical method for high oil and dry plant commodities, for animal fat, liver and kidney and a validated enforcement analytical method for eggs, including a confirmatory method and an ILV, were submitted by the applicant in response to the Article 12 confirmatory data gaps (1), (2) and (3). EFSA considered these data gaps as sufficiently addressed.

Based on the information provided, it is concluded that sufficiently validated enforcement methods are in place for the determination of spinetoram residues in the plant and animal matrices under consideration. However, the extraction efficiency of these methods was considered as not addressed thereby introducing additional uncertainty to be noted by the risk managers.

The residue data submitted in response to the data gap (4) supporting the EU uses on pome fruits (apples and pears), apricots and table and wine grapes were not assessed because the approval of spinetoram expired on 30 June 2024 and all EU uses are obsolete. It is noted that, following the expiration of approval of spinetoram, all current MRLs based on EU uses would need to be revoked, and those based on Codex MRLs or on import tolerances would need to be reconsidered by the risk managers, who may consider a review of all MRLs set for spinetoram.

However, a comprehensive review of all the existing MRLs is out of the scope of this confirmatory data assessment.

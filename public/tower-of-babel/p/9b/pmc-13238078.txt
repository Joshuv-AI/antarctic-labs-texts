Early retinal, optic disc and choriocapillaris microvascular alterations in children with type 1 diabetes without clinical retinopathy: an OCTA-based case–control study
Authors: Semih Bolat, Ömer Anıl Sönmez, Hatice Nur Topuz, Mehmet Özbaş
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimTo assess retinal, optic disc, and choriocapillaris microvascular changes in children with type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM) using optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA).Materials and methodsThis cross-sectional study included 154 children, comprising 77 children with T1DM and 77 healthy controls. One eye from each participant was analyzed. OCTA was used to evaluate vessel density in the superficial and deep capillary plexuses, foveal avascular zone area, radial peripapillary capillary vessel density, and choriocapillaris non-perfused area (NPCA) ratios. Foveal thickness and subfoveal choroidal thickness were measured using optical coherence tomography. Age, sex, body mass index, and HbA1c levels were recorded for all participants, along with disease duration and daily insulin dose in the T1DM group.ResultsSuperficial capillary plexus vessel density and foveal avascular zone parameters did not differ significantly between the T1DM and control groups (p > 0.05). Deep capillary plexus vessel density was also comparable between groups across most regions; however, superior hemi-area vessel density was higher in the T1DM group on unadjusted analysis (p < 0.05). Radial peripapillary capillary vessel density in the peripapillary region and whole-image analysis showed no significant differences between groups (p > 0.05). In contrast, intrapapillary vessel density was lower in children with T1DM (p < 0.05). Choriocapillaris NPCA ratio and foveal thickness were increased in the T1DM group (p < 0.05), while subfoveal choroidal thickness showed no significant difference between groups (p > 0.05).ConclusionChildren with T1DM without clinical retinopathy may show early microvascular alterations on OCTA, particularly increased central choriocapillaris non-perfusion and reduced intrapapillary vessel density. Introduction

Type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM) is a chronic autoimmune disease caused by destruction of pancreatic β-cells, resulting in insulin deficiency, and is one of the most common chronic conditions in children and adolescents [1]. Diabetic retinopathy (DR) represents the most frequent microvascular complication of T1DM and is clinically defined by the presence of microaneurysms on fundus examination [2]. In pediatric populations, the prevalence of DR is approximately 11%, with disease duration, pubertal status, age at diagnosis, and glycemic control identified as major risk factors [3]. Although sight-threatening retinopathy usually develops later, some adolescents experience rapidly progressive disease that may lead to irreversible visual loss [4].

DR is increasingly recognized as a condition involving both vascular and neurodegenerative mechanisms. Studies have demonstrated microglial activation, ganglion cell loss, neuronal apoptosis, and microvascular alterations affecting multiple retinal layers in T1DM [5, 6]. Importantly, these changes may occur before clinically detectable DR on fundus examination or fundus photography [7, 8]. Therefore, early screening strategies are essential to preserve visual function in children and adolescents with T1DM [3].

Fundus fluorescein angiography (FFA) is a well-established technique for detecting DR and diabetic macular ischemia, characterized by foveal avascular zone (FAZ) enlargement and parafoveal capillary non-perfusion. However, its invasive nature limits routine use, particularly in pediatric patients. Optical coherence tomography (OCT) allows non-invasive evaluation of retinal structure and enables detection of early neurodegenerative changes, even in patients without clinically evident DR [9].

Optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA) provides non-invasive, depth-resolved visualization of the retinal and choroidal microvasculature and enables quantitative assessment of diabetes-related microvascular alterations in both the macular and optic disc regions [2, 10]. In addition, OCT permits detailed analysis of the retinal nerve fiber layer and other retinal layers that may be affected by neurodegeneration in T1DM [2, 7, 11].

The aim of this study was to assess retinal, optic disc, and choriocapillaris microvascular alterations in children with T1DM without clinically detectable retinopathy and to compare these findings with those of healthy controls. In addition to macular vessel density parameters, we evaluated optic disc microvasculature and zonal choriocapillaris non-perfused area ratios to better characterize early subclinical ocular microvascular involvement in pediatric T1DM.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

This prospective, cross-sectional, and comparative study was conducted between January 2024 and January 2025 at the University of Health Sciences, Istanbul Bakırköy Dr. Sadi Konuk Training and Research Hospital. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants and their parents or legal guardians. The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the University of Health Sciences Istanbul Bakırköy Dr. Sadi Konuk Training and Research Hospital Clinical Research Ethics Committee, Istanbul, Türkiye (approval number: 2024-01-05).

Participants in the patient group were selected from children diagnosed with type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM) for at least one year who either presented to the ophthalmology outpatient clinic through the Central Physician Appointment System or were followed in the Pediatric Endocrinology Department of our hospital. A total of 77 eyes from 77 patients were included in the study. The control group consisted of 77 eyes from 77 healthy children without any ocular or systemic disease who presented to the ophthalmology outpatient clinic. The right eye of each participant was included in the analysis. Data on age, sex, height, weight, body mass index (BMI), HbA1c level, disease duration, and daily insulin dose (for the T1DM group) were recorded.

Exclusion criteria were the presence of any systemic disease other than T1DM, history of premature birth, any organic ocular pathology detected on detailed ophthalmologic examination, previous ocular surgery or trauma, and spherical or cylindrical refractive error greater than 6.00 diopters. This refractive error threshold was used to minimize the potential confounding effects of high ametropia, particularly high myopia, on retinal and choriocapillaris microvascular measurements obtained by OCTA.

Ophthalmologic examination

All participants underwent a comprehensive ophthalmologic examination performed by a single clinician, including best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA, Snellen chart), intraocular pressure (IOP) measurement, slit-lamp biomicroscopy, and dilated fundus examination.

OCT and OCTA imaging

OCT and OCTA imaging were performed by a single experienced technician using the AngioVue system (Optovue, Inc., Fremont, CA, USA) with split-spectrum amplitude-decorrelation angiography (SSADA). In all participants, imaging was obtained after pharmacologic pupil dilation with topical 0.5% tropicamide, and the same dilation protocol was applied in both the T1DM and control groups.

OCT was used to measure foveal thickness (FT) and subfoveal choroidal thickness (SFCT), expressed in micrometers. OCTA was used to evaluate vessel density (VD, %) in the superficial capillary plexus (SCP), deep capillary plexus (DCP), and radial peripapillary capillary (RPC) network using 6 × 6 mm macular scans and 4.5 × 4.5 mm optic disc scans. The analyzed retinal parameters included whole-image vessel density (WI-VD), superior hemi-area vessel density (SH-VD), inferior hemi-area vessel density (IH-VD), foveal vessel density (F-VD), parafoveal vessel density (PF-VD), and foveal avascular zone (FAZ) area (mm²). Optic disc OCTA parameters included intrapapillary vessel density (IP-VD) and peripapillary vessel density (PP-VD). Choriocapillaris non-perfused area (NPCA) ratios were calculated for circular regions with radii of < 0.5 mm, 0.5–1.5 mm, and 1.5–3 mm using the device’s built-in AngioVue software with default segmentation and threshold settings, without additional external image-processing software. Images with a signal strength index of < 7/10 were excluded. Representative OCTA outputs illustrating image acquisition and parameter extraction are presented in Figs. 1, 2, 3 and 4.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS for Windows, Version 20.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Descriptive statistics for continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation and median (minimum–maximum), while categorical variables were presented as frequency and percentage. The Shapiro–Wilk test was used to assess the normality of data distribution. For comparisons between the patient and control groups, the independent samples t-test was used for normally distributed data, whereas the Mann–Whitney U test was applied for non-normally distributed data. The chi-square test was used to compare categorical variables between groups.

In addition, exploratory correlation analyses were performed within the T1DM group to assess the relationships between selected OCTA/OCT parameters and clinical variables, particularly HbA1c level and disease duration. Pearson or Spearman correlation analysis was used according to data distribution.

Because multiple OCTA/OCT-derived parameters were evaluated, false discovery rate (FDR) correction was performed using the Benjamini–Hochberg method, and FDR-adjusted p values were considered when interpreting the main findings. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Clinical characteristics

This cross-sectional study included 154 children, comprising 77 children with type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM; mean age 12.09 ± 3.06 years; 41 males and 36 females) and 77 healthy controls (mean age 12.58 ± 3.60 years; 41 males and 36 females). The T1DM group had a mean disease duration of 4.79 ± 3.00 years (median, 4.0 years; range, 1.5–14.0 years). Compared with the control group, children with T1DM had a lower mean body mass index (18.93 ± 3.63 vs. 20.50 ± 0.60 kg/m²) and higher HbA1c levels (8.72 ± 1.61 vs. 5.29 ± 0.13%). The mean daily insulin dose in the T1DM group was 32.14 ± 11.54 units. One eye from each participant was analyzed. After false discovery rate correction using the Benjamini–Hochberg method for the OCTA/OCT-derived parameters, the increase in choriocapillaris NPCA ratio at the < 0.5 mm radius, the decrease in small-vessel intrapapillary vessel density, and the increase in foveal thickness remained statistically significant. In contrast, the differences in DCP superior hemi-area vessel density, all-vessel intrapapillary vessel density, and choriocapillaris NPCA ratio at the 0.5–1.5 mm radius did not remain significant after FDR correction.

Within the T1DM group, exploratory correlation analyses showed no significant associations between HbA1c level or disease duration and the selected OCTA/OCT parameters, including DCP superior hemi-area vessel density, intrapapillary vessel density, choriocapillaris NPCA ratios, and foveal thickness (all p > 0.05).

Discussion

Microvascular complications are a major concern in children and adolescents with type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM), as retinal involvement may precede clinically detectable diabetic retinopathy (DR). Optical coherence tomography angiography (OCTA) enables non-invasive, depth-resolved detection of such preclinical microvascular alterations and has become a valuable tool for the early assessment of retinal microvascular involvement in pediatric T1DM [12].

In this study, children with T1DM without clinically detectable DR showed evidence of retinal, optic disc, and choriocapillaris microvascular alterations compared with healthy controls. Despite the absence of ophthalmoscopic signs of retinopathy, increased choriocapillaris NPCA ratios and changes in selected retinal and optic disc OCTA parameters were observed, supporting the presence of early subclinical ocular microvascular involvement.

Consistent with previous pediatric studies, superficial capillary plexus (SCP) vessel density and foveal avascular zone (FAZ) parameters were preserved in our cohort [13–15]. This finding may suggest that the superficial plexus remains relatively unaffected during the early stage of T1DM-related retinal involvement. In contrast, although superior hemi-area vessel density in the deep capillary plexus (DCP) was higher in the T1DM group on unadjusted analysis, this difference did not remain significant after false discovery rate correction. Therefore, this result should be interpreted with caution. Rather than indicating a definite compensatory response, it may reflect early microvascular dysregulation, inflammation-related vascular effects, or regional OCTA-related variability [16, 17].

The lack of significant SCP and FAZ alterations in our cohort may be related to the relatively early stage of disease, as previous studies have shown that retinal microvascular parameters may become more affected with longer diabetes duration and greater cumulative metabolic burden [18–21]. In our study, exploratory correlation analyses did not reveal significant associations between HbA1c level or disease duration and the selected OCTA/OCT parameters. This may reflect the pediatric age group, the subclinical stage of retinal involvement, and the limited ability of single-point metabolic measurements to capture long-term microvascular injury. Longitudinal follow-up may therefore provide a better understanding of progressive microvascular involvement over time.

The increased foveal thickness observed in the T1DM group is consistent with previous reports suggesting that subtle structural retinal changes may occur before the development of clinically apparent diabetic macular pathology [22–24]. This difference remained significant after false discovery rate correction. Although visual function was preserved in our cohort, this finding may reflect early retinal involvement at a subclinical stage and further supports the importance of careful structural assessment in children with T1DM.

Another notable finding was reduced intrapapillary vessel density, suggesting early optic disc microvascular involvement in children with T1DM. This finding is noteworthy because whole-image and peripapillary radial peripapillary capillary measurements did not differ significantly between groups, whereas the intrapapillary region appeared to be more selectively affected. OCTA-derived optic disc parameters may therefore provide additional information on early diabetic optic nerve head microvascular changes [25–27].

One of the most important findings of the present study was the increase in choriocapillaris non-perfused area (NPCA) ratio, particularly in the central < 0.5 mm region. Given that choriocapillaris hypoperfusion has been proposed as an early marker of diabetic retinal involvement [28], this finding suggests that choriocapillaris dysfunction may occur before clinically detectable DR develops. Although the difference in the 0.5–1.5 mm NPCA ratio was significant on unadjusted analysis, it did not remain significant after false discovery rate correction and should therefore be interpreted cautiously. Previous studies have also reported choriocapillaris impairment in diabetes and other systemic vascular conditions [29–31]. In addition, the recently published study suggested by the reviewer further supports the concept of early subclinical retinal and choroidal microvascular involvement in T1DM before the onset of clinical DR [32].

In contrast, subfoveal choroidal thickness did not differ significantly between groups, which may indicate that functional microvascular alterations precede measurable structural choroidal changes in children with T1DM [33–36].

Overall, our findings support the presence of early subclinical retinal, optic disc, and choriocapillaris microvascular involvement in pediatric T1DM and highlight the potential value of OCTA in detecting these changes before clinically detectable diabetic retinopathy develops.

A major strength of this study is the relatively large and well-balanced pediatric cohort, representing one of the larger case-control OCTA studies in this population. In addition to macular retinal vessel density, we also evaluated optic disc microvasculature and performed a zonal analysis of choriocapillaris NPCA ratios across three concentric regions. This broader OCTA-based assessment provides a more detailed evaluation of early localized microvascular dysfunction before clinically detectable retinopathy develops. To our knowledge, pediatric OCTA case-control studies specifically combining optic disc parameters with zonal choriocapillaris NPCA analysis remain limited, and the present study contributes additional data to the literature in this field.

This study has several limitations. First, its cross-sectional design precludes causal inference and limits the evaluation of temporal progression of microvascular changes. Second, the 6 × 6 mm fovea-centered scan protocol did not allow assessment of the peripheral retinal vasculature. Third, automated OCTA measurements may be influenced by image quality, motion artifacts, and segmentation errors. Fourth, all OCTA images were obtained after pharmacologic pupil dilation with topical 0.5% tropicamide. Although the same imaging protocol was applied in both groups, mydriasis may still affect some OCTA-derived measurements, particularly peripapillary vascular parameters, and this should be considered when interpreting the findings. Fifth, axial length was not measured, and because axial length may influence image scaling and OCTA-derived parameters such as vessel density and FAZ-related measurements, this represents a methodological limitation. Sixth, although exploratory correlation analyses were performed, no significant associations were found between HbA1c level or disease duration and the selected OCTA/OCT parameters; larger longitudinal studies may better clarify these relationships. Finally, although false discovery rate correction was applied to support interpretation of the main OCTA findings, some nominally significant results did not remain significant after adjustment and should therefore be interpreted with caution.

Conclusion

Children with type 1 diabetes mellitus without clinical retinopathy may show early microvascular alterations on OCTA, particularly increased central choriocapillaris non-perfusion and reduced intrapapillary vessel density. These findings support the potential value of OCTA in detecting preclinical ocular microvascular involvement in pediatric T1DM. Further longitudinal studies are needed to clarify the clinical significance of these changes and their potential role in early monitoring before the development of clinically detectable diabetic retinopathy.

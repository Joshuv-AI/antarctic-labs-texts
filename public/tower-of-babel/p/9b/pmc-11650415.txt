Combined left atrial appendage occlusion and catheter ablation procedure for left atrial arrhythmias: A real‐world, propensity‐matched analysis
Authors: Stefan Preisendörfer, Muhammad T. Ayub, Aakash Sheth, George Y. Jabbour, Madhurmeet Singh, Chinmay P. Patel, Hemal Gada, Aditya Bhonsale, Mehak Dhande, Nathan A. Estes, Krishna Kancharla, Dustin E. Kliner, Amber Makani, Aditi Naniwadekar, Alaa Shalaby, Virginia Singla, Andrew Voigt, Samir F. Saba, Sandeep K. Jain
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionReal‐world studies comparing safety and efficacy of combined percutaneous left atrial appendage occlusion (LAAO) and catheter ablation (CA) to LAAO alone are limited.MethodsPatients from a large US hospital system undergoing combined LAAO and left‐atrial CA from 8/2020 to 2/2024 were retrospectively analyzed and compared to a control group undergoing LAAO alone. Controls were identified using a 1:2 propensity score match based on LAAO device type (Watchman FLX vs. Amulet), CHA2D2‐VASc and Hypertension, Abnormal renal/liver function, Stroke, Bleeding history or predisposition, Labile international normalized ratio [INR], Elderly, Drugs/alcohol (HAS‐BLED) scores and compared for safety, sealing performance and clinical outcomes at 6 months.ResultsPatients were younger in the combined (n = 72) than in the control group (n = 144, 70.2 ± 7.3 vs. 76.7 ± 6.9 years, p < 0.001) but otherwise comparable with a mean CHA2D2‐VASc score of 4.2 ± 1.1 and 4.4 ± 1.2 (p = 0.26) and HAS‐BLED score of 2.2 ± 0.8 and 2.3 ± 0.7 (p = 0.34). Successful LAAO implantation rates were the same (95.8% vs. 95.8%, p = 0.99) with longer procedure times seen in the combined group (156.5 ± 53 vs. 56 ± 26 min, p < 0.001). Both major (1.4% vs. 2.1%, p = 0.72) and minor (27.8% vs. 19.4%, p = 0.17) in‐hospital complications were similar between the combined and control group, respectively. At 45 days, presence of peri‐device leak (18.3% vs. 30.4%, p = 0.07) and device related thrombosis (4.5% vs. 4.5%, p = 0.96) on transesophageal echocardiogram did not differ. Finally, all‐cause mortality (0% vs. 1.4%, p = 0.99), thromboembolic (0% vs. 0%, p = 0.99) and bleeding (6.1% vs. 4.4%, p = 0.73) events during follow‐up were comparable.ConclusionThis large, real‐world analysis indicates comparable safety and efficiency of combined LAAO and CA when compared with LAAO alone.

INTRODUCTION

Left atrial appendage occlusion (LAAO) represents an alternative for stroke prevention in patients with atrial fibrillation (AF) in whom continued oral anticoagulation (OAC) is contraindicated or who have an appropriate reason to seek nondrug alternatives. 1 , 2 While catheter‐based ablation techniques using pulmonary vein isolation (PVI) with or without posterior wall isolation (PWI) have become an established rhythm control strategy in patients with AF, 3 catheter ablation (CA) has not been shown to reduce stroke rates, 4 necessitating continued stroke prevention via OAC or LAAO following CA. CA followed by LAAO as a combined procedural strategy for AF rhythm control and stroke prevention in selected patients is increasingly performed in the United States and worldwide, and has been shown to be more cost‐effective when compared to having LAAO and CA performed in separate procedures. 5 , 6 Moreover, both LAAO and CA share similar procedural resources, and patients may benefit from undergoing less procedures along with fewer procedural complications and hospitalizations. However, combined procedures might pose additional risks due to longer procedural times with prolonged anesthesia, and it is less clear how the addition of CA to LAAO will affect sealing performance of the occlusion device. In fact, controlled trials comparing combined LAAO and CA to LAAO alone are scarce, and current guidelines therefore recommend against the routine implementation of combined LAAO procedures. 7 In the present study, we aimed to compare the safety and efficacy of combined LAAO and CA to a propensity‐matched control group undergoing LAAO alone at a large US hospital system.

METHODS

Study population and protocol

All research activities were approved by the University of Pittsburgh internal review board which waived the requirement for informed consent in view of our study's observational nature. Data was collected through manual review of the internal electronic medical records.

Patients who underwent combined LAAO and left‐atrial CA from 8/2020 to 2/2024 at our hospital system were retrospectively included. Many patients were referred from local providers primarily for LAAO. Combined CA was then offered if patients met indication for CA in addition to LAAO at the discretion of the operator. Patients who underwent different types of combined procedures such as isolated right‐atrial CA were excluded. Additionally, patients who underwent LAAO with any other device than the contemporary LAAO devices Watchman FLX/FLX PRO (Boston Scientific) or Amplatzer Amulet (Abbott Laboratories) were excluded. Each patient who underwent combined LAAO and CA was propensity‐matched in a 1:2 fashion to patients undergoing LAAO alone during the study period based on device type (Watchman FLX vs. Amulet), CHA2DS2‐Vasc and HAS‐BLED (Hypertension, Abnormal renal/liver function, Stroke, Bleeding history or predisposition, Labile international normalized ratio [INR], Elderly, Drugs/alcohol) scores.

CA procedure

Left‐atrial ablation procedures using cryoballoon and radiofrequency ablation for PVI and supplemental lesions were performed as per operator discretion and have been described previously. 8 , 9 All procedures were performed under general anesthesia. Transesophageal echocardiogram (TEE) and often cardiac computed tomography angiography (CCTA) were utilized to exclude intracardiac thrombus and guide LAAO implantation.

LAAO procedure

LAAO was performed as described previously. 10 , 11 Briefly, ostial diameters were measured by TEE, LAA angiography and often preprocedural CCTA. Only Watchman FLX and Amulet devices were used and chosen according to operator's preference as well as preprocedural imaging. The appropriate device size was selected as per instructions for use. 12 In the combined group, LAAO was pursued after CA was completed. The respective device was then advanced through the delivery sheath and deployed under fluoroscopy and TEE guidance. Initial assessment was performed by angiography and tug test under fluoroscopy to assess device position and stability. TEE was concomitantly performed to further confirm positioning with minimal (<5 mm) to no residual peri‐device leak (PDL) and a proper compression ratio. The device was released if it met “PASS” or “CLOSE” criteria depending on device type. 12 Procedural complications were defined according to the Munich consensus document. 13

Postprocedural anticoagulation and antithrombotic therapy

In the combined group, patients were discharged on a combination of OAC and single antiplatelet therapy (SAPT) if not otherwise contraindicated and transitioned to dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) at two or 3 months postprocedure at the discretion of the operator, provided that follow‐up imaging via TEE did not reveal a PDL > 5 mm. Patients in the control group were discharged either on a combination of OAC and SAPT or DAPT therapy depending on device type and operator's preference. At 45 days postprocedure and following TEE, patients in the control group either remained on DAPT therapy if discharged on DAPT or were transitioned to DAPT if discharged on a combination of OAC and SAPT. At 6 months, both groups were continued on SAPT alone if not otherwise contraindicated.

Follow‐up

Generally, patients underwent initial follow‐up within 3 months and subsequently at 6 months following the procedure. TEE was performed at 45 days postprocedure to assess the presence of PDL and device related thrombosis (DRT). Clinical outcomes including all‐cause mortality, cardioembolic and bleeding events were assessed during each follow‐up. As many patients were referred from outside providers, follow‐up beyond 6 months was largely pursued locally and was therefore not accessible for our analysis.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 29.0 (IBM Software Inc.). Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation, while categorical variables are denoted as frequency with percentage. For continuous variables, the independent t‐test was used for intergroup comparisons. For categorical data, the chi‐square test or Fisher's exact test was used as appropriate. Statistical significance was assumed if p < 0.05.

To adjust for baseline differences, propensity scores representing the probability of the respective procedure were calculated using multivariate logistic regression based on selected pretest covariates including device type (Watchman FLX vs. Amulet), CHA2DS2‐Vasc and HAS‐BLED scores. Each patient undergoing LAAO with combined CA was then propensity‐matched to two controls undergoing LAAO alone using the nearest neighbor technique with a caliper width of 0.2.

RESULTS

From 8/2020 to 2/2024, 72 patients were identified undergoing combined LAAO and left atrial CA. Using a 1:2 propensity match, a control group of 144 patient undergoing LAAO during this time frame was established based on LAAO device type (Watchman FLX vs. Amulet), CHA2DS2‐Vasc and HAS‐BLED scores.

Baseline characteristics

As shown in Table 1, patients undergoing combined LAAO and CA (age 70.2 ± 7.3 years, 50% female, body mass index [BMI] 31.9 ± 7.3) were younger than in the control group (age 76.7 ± 6.9 years, 41% female, BMI 30.1 ± 6.2; p < 0.001 for age), but otherwise comparable with a mean left ventricular ejection fraction of 53 ± 8% and 52 ± 10% (p = 0.28) in the combined and control group, respectively. Both groups were at high risk for cardioembolic stroke with a mean CHA2DS2‐Vasc score of 4.2 ± 1.1 and 4.4 ± 1.2 (p = 0.26) as well as a mean HAS‐BLED score of 2.2 ± 0.8 and 2.3 ± 0.7 (p = 0.34) in the combined and control group, respectively. In the combined group, rates of previous myocardial infarction were lower (25% vs. 42.1%, p = 0.01), while other comorbidities including congestive heart failure, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, previous cerebrovascular accident and chronic kidney disease were similar (all p > 0.05). Most patients in both groups had a history of a major bleeding event, which was largely driven by gastrointestinal bleeding events, while less than one‐third of patients in both groups had a history of recurrent falls. More than half of patients in both groups had paroxysmal AF at baseline (62.5% vs. 56.3%, p = 0.38). Lastly, a higher proportion of patients was maintained on single oral anticoagulation (OAC, 73.6%) in the combined as compared to the control group (52.1%) at baseline, while 20.8% and 31.9% of patients were taking single OAC combined with SAPT in the combined and control group, respectively (p = 0.033, Figure 2).

Procedural characteristics

Procedural characteristics are highlighted in Table 2. As shown, more patients in the combined group underwent preprocedural CCTA as compared to controls (41.7% vs. 27.1%, p = 0.03). Watchman FLX was the predominant device for LAAO (>90% in both groups) with both cohorts exhibiting high implantation success rates (95.8% vs. 95.8%, p = 0.99). Similarly, success rates using the initially elected device size (84.7% vs. 84%, p = 0.86) were comparable. In the combined group, most patients underwent PVI only (87.5%) for CA, with fewer patients undergoing left‐sided atrial tachycardia (AT) ablation only (2.8%) or combined PVI with left‐sided AT ablation (9.7%). As expected, procedural and fluoroscopy times as well as the amount of contrast medium used were higher in the combined arm (all p < 0.001). A PDL on the procedural TEE was observed in 7.2% and 6.5% in the combined and control group (p = 0.83), respectively, with a mean PDL size of 2 ± 0.7 mm and 1.9 ± 0.6 mm (p = 0.76). Major hospital complications occurred in one patient in the combined group (thrombus formation) and three patients in the control group (one death, one pseudoaneurysm and one thrombus formation; p = 0.72). The one death in the control group occurred few hours postprocedure after a pulseless electrical activity cardiac arrest, for which no underlying cause had been identified. Minor hospital complications occurred in 27.8% of patients in the combined and 19.4% in the control group (p = .17) and were largely driven by pericardial effusions not requiring intervention as well as minor bleeding events. The average hospital stays following the procedure were 1.1 ± 1.1 and 1.1 ± 0.7 days (p = .74), with most patients in either group being discharged on a combination of OAC with SAPT (93.1% vs. 88.2%, Figure 2).

DISCUSSION

This real‐world, propensity‐matched analysis comparing combined LAAO and CA to LAAO alone reveals the following principal findings: First, the combined group demonstrated similar procedural success and in‐hospital complication rates when compared to the matched control group. Second, rates of PDL and DRT on 45‐day follow‐up TEE were comparable between groups. Finally, clinical endpoints including all‐cause mortality, cardioembolic as well as major bleeding events were similar at both three and 6‐month follow‐up.

Stroke prevention and rhythm control constitute key principles in the treatment of AF and could theoretically be addressed in select patients within a single procedure using combined LAAO and CA. 14 Observational studies have demonstrated the safety and feasibility of combined LAAO and CA, 14 , 15 with the combination procedure now being increasingly implemented. 5 , 6 Most of these studies, however, have not included a control group, 16 , 17 while the few controlled trials, for the most part, have utilized data from large administrative databases which did not incorporate information on intraprocedural or follow‐up imaging, nor data on clinical outcomes beyond the procedural hospitalization. 5 , 18 To adjust for baseline differences, our study included a propensity‐matched control group undergoing LAAO alone and, despite longer procedure times, indicated high procedural success rates in the combined group which did not differ from the control group. Similarly, major and minor in‐hospital complication rates were comparable between groups, and consistent with previous studies which included a propensity‐matched control group. 5 , 18 Overall, our data further support the feasibility and safety of combined LAAO and CA.

The presence of PDL and DRT on follow‐up TEE have both been individually associated with cardioembolic events following LAAO. 19 , 20 , 21 It has previously been hypothesized that combined LAAO and CA might result in an increased rate of PDL on follow‐up imaging, given that CA leads to tissue edema in the LAA ridge with subsequent underestimation of the true LAA diameter. 22 However, only few observational analyses have so far compared rates of PDL and DRT on follow‐up imaging of combined LAAO and CA to LAAO alone: Mo et al have reported similar rates of PDL (17.1% vs. 14.5%) and DRT (0% vs. 1.3%) on 45‐day follow‐up TEE in patients undergoing the combined and LAAO alone procedure, respectively. 23 Similarly, Zhang et al have found comparable rates of PDL (30.8% vs. 32.2%) with lower rates of DRT (0.5% vs. 5.1%) in the combined group on 3‐month follow‐up TEE. 24 By contrast, Hao et al have noted an increased rate of PDL (43.4% vs. 25.3%) in the combined procedure on follow‐up imaging at 3 months using either TEE or CCTA, with no difference in DRT (3.6% vs. 4.8%). 25 However, these studies have only analyzed the older generations of Watchman (Watchman 2.5) and Amplatzer (Amplatzer Cardiac Plug) devices, limiting applicability of their results to the newer and now most used devices, Watchman FLX and Amulet. Moreover, Hao et al. have used either TEE or CCTA for the assessment of PDL on follow‐up, without specifying which imaging modality was utilized for follow‐up for what proportion of patients in each group. This might have resulted in an overestimation of PDL in the combined group, given that CCTA has been shown to be more sensitive in the assessment of PDL when compared to TEE. 26 Our study, on the other hand, analyzed only the newer generation devices, Watchman FLX and Amulet, and TEE was consistently utilized as the sole modality to assess PDL and DRT, making the results more applicable to current practice. Moreover, the control group in the present study was matched based on device type, resulting in similar proportions of Watchman FLX and Amulet in both groups. Our analysis revealed similar rates of PDL (18.3% vs. 30.4%) and DRT (4.5% vs. 4.5%) at 45 days postprocedure in the combined and control group, respectively, implying satisfactory sealing performance with low rates of DRT in the combined approach. Interestingly, our data indicate a trend towards lower rates of PDL in the combined group, which might have been due to more patients in the combined group having undergone preprocedural CCTA, which has been shown to be associated with a lower incidence of PDLs following LAAO. 27 Alternatively, fluid administration during preceding CA might have led to higher measurements of ostial diameters in the combined group, thus promoting selection of larger device sizes.

Finally, our comparison indicated similar clinical outcomes between the combined and control group, including all‐cause mortality, cardioembolic events and bleeding events, at both three as well as 6 months postprocedure. Transfusion dependent bleeding events were low in both groups (1.5% and 2.9% at 3 months) and have all occurred within the first 3 months, which is consistent with previous trials 28 , 29 and the fact that patients are on the strongest anticoagulatory/antithrombotic regimens during this period. Both groups had similar anticoagulatory/antithrombotic regimens at three and 6 months after the procedure, with most patients being maintained on DAPT therapy at 3 months and SAPT therapy at 6 months in both groups. With low event rates in both the combined and control group, however, our study was not powered to detect differences in clinical outcomes, and follow‐up was limited to 6 months. Larger, randomized trials are therefore needed to further support the long‐term safety and efficiency of combined LAAO and CA. The ongoing OPTION (Comparison of Anticoagulation with Left Atrial Appendage Closure After AF Ablation; NCT03795298) trial will provide further insights regarding long‐term safety and clinical outcomes of combined LAAO and CA. 30

Our study needs to be seen in consideration of several limitations. First, even though we attempted to adjust for baseline differences using propensity score matching, our comparison is non‐randomized and therefore susceptible to residual bias. Second, the sample size was moderate, and not powered to detect differences in clinical endpoints including deaths, cardioembolic and major bleeding events, which were low in both groups. However, we increased the sample size in the control group by matching at a 1:2 ratio. Third, follow‐up duration was limited to 6 months, and a larger proportion of patients in the combined arm did not have follow‐up at 6 months. Finally, our analysis was limited to patients undergoing combined LAAO and CA, while patients undergoing LAAO with other combination procedures were not included. Our findings are therefore only applicable to combined LAAO and CA, but do not allow for conclusions regarding other combination procedures.

CONCLUSIONS

The present, propensity‐score matched analysis demonstrated a comparable safety and efficiency profile of two contemporary LAAO devices with combined CA when compared to LAAO alone in a real‐world setting, further supporting the implementation of combined LAAO and CA into clinical practice.

Extended release of DELOS nanovesicles from thermoreversible in situ-forming subcutaneous depots
Authors: Carla Castellar-Álvarez, Lídia Ballell-Hosa, Yolanda Ronda-Lopez, Aniol Gimó-Santamaria, Arnau Güell-Bara, Cristián Huck-Iriart, Jan Skov Pedersen, Alba Córdoba, Santi Sala, Nora Ventosa, Elisabet González-Mira, Lidia Ferrer-Tasies
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The ongoing transition from traditional orally bioavailable small molecules to novel drug modalities demands advanced drug delivery strategies. This work presents the development of a hybrid, in situ-forming subcutaneous drug depot combining thermoreversible Poloxamer 407 hydrogels with DELOS nanovesicles (DELOS-NV). This platform leverages the complementary strengths of both systems: DELOS-NV entrap and protect hydrophobic payloads, while the hydrogel matrix prevents rapid nanovesicle dispersion, enabling sustained release. Using a design of experiments (DoE), we refined the membrane composition of the DELOS-NV employing excipients listed in the FDA inactive ingredients database to create a patient- and regulatory-oriented formulation. The optimized DELOS-NV dispersion was gelled by adding different concentrations of Poloxamer 407 (15% to 20% w/w) to identify the most effective one that flows during the injection and forms an in situ depot subcutaneously. The integrity and stability of gelled DELOS-NV were demonstrated by two orthogonal techniques. Rheological characterization of the DELOS-NV-loaded hydrogels revealed the 17% w/w Poloxamer 407 concentration as optimal for subcutaneous administration, exhibiting shear-thinning behavior and suitable viscosity at 22 °C for easy injectability. Two in vitro release tests were performed using a fluorescent molecule and a therapeutically relevant Beyond the Rule of 5 (bRo5) compound. The hydrogel matrix governs the release of intact, drug-loaded nanovesicles via an erosion-driven mechanism, following zero-order kinetics over 8–10 h. The successful stabilization of the bRo5 compound highlights the platform's potential for the prolonged delivery of therapeutically valuable, formulation-challenging molecules.

Introduction

Over the past two decades, there has been a slow but steady transition from traditional orally bioavailable small molecules to novel drug modalities (e.g., highly hydrophobic compounds, beyond the rule of 5 (bRo5) compounds, oligonucleotides, antibody-drug conjugates…) designed to modulate complex biological targets once considered “undruggable”. Advances in molecular biology, coupled with the completion of the Human Genome Project in the early 2000s, resulted not only in a better understanding of critical disease-relevant targets but also in increasing interest in addressing these targets. As the pharmaceutical landscape broadens with an expanding variety of drug modalities, there arises a parallel need for more creative drug delivery approaches driven by the inherent challenges of novel modalities, such as poor stability and permeability under physiological conditions(Blanco et al., 2022; Blanco and Gardinier, 2020; Valeur et al., 2017).

In response to these challenges, nanocarriers, such as lipid-based nanovesicles, have gained increasing importance in the realm of drug delivery. Nanocarriers are submicron-sized vehicles with high versatility, allowing the administration of diverse therapeutic molecules ranging from small molecules to large and complex biomolecules. They increase drug bioavailability, protect and stabilize sensitive drugs (e.g., proteins), minimize side effects and provide means for active targeting to specific organs(Alshawwa et al., 2022; Fatfat et al., 2023; Shah et al., 2021; Tomsen-Melero et al., 2022).

In particular, DELOS nanovesicles (DELOS-NV), also referred to as Quatsomes, represent a new category of non-liposomal nanocarriers. Comprised of ionic surfactants and sterols, they are prepared using DELOS technology, a patented method based on compressed fluids(WO/2026/032777, 2026). This technology employs compressed CO2 for an eco-efficient, scalable, one-step production of nanodispersed systems, such as vesicles. By this platform, the colloidal dispersions obtained are highly homogeneous in size, morphology, and lamellarity in aqueous media(Cabrera et al., 2013; Ferrer-Tasies et al., 2013; Grimaldi et al., 2016). Notably, these nanocarriers are thermodynamically stable(Ferrer-Tasies et al., 2013; Köber et al., 2023), which favors the production of high-quality pharmaceutical formulations(Cabrera et al., 2013; Ferrer-Tasies, 2016). This is a key feature that differentiates DELOS-NV from other conventional vesicles, such as phospholipids-based liposomes. It has been demonstrated that DELOS-NV remain stable in colloidal dispersion during long-term storage, even over several years, without showing significant changes in size, shape, or polydispersity index(Grimaldi et al., 2016; Ferrer-Tasies et al., 2021; Köber et al., 2023). Besides, since DELOS-NV are prepared without phospholipids and rely on the rigid structure of sterols and ionic surfactants, they are highly resistant to oxidative stress and chemical degradation(Grit and Crommelin, 1993). DELOS-NV have previously demonstrated their potential for the delivery of a variety of drug modalities, from small molecules to biologics, through different administration routes including intravenous(Cabrera et al., 2013; Vargas-Nadal et al., 2020) and topical(Ballell-Hosa et al., 2022; Ferrer-Tasies et al., 2021). However, the subcutaneous (SC) administration route has not yet been addressed with DELOS-NV, despite its huge potential, especially for the delivery of these new drug modalities with low oral bioavailability.

Drug modalities with poor oral bioavailability are usually administered through invasive routes(Madani et al., 2020). Among these, the SC administration route is favored by healthcare professionals for its cost-effectiveness and minimal resource utilization. This method is also preferred by patients because it allows them the convenience of self-administration at home(Bittner et al., 2018; Ganesh et al., 2021). Indeed, many patients would prefer a weekly SC injection over daily oral medication, making the SC route an effective approach for chronic diseases(Ganesh et al., 2021). In such scenarios, developing a SC depot that allows extended drug release could improve patient compliance by reducing the frequency of administration(Rahnfeld and Luciani, 2020).

However, nanoparticle suspensions typically disperse rapidly from the injection site and are cleared through lymphatic drainage or local macrophage uptake, which limits their ability to form long-acting depots. Therefore, an additional retention mechanism is required to achieve sustained release following SC administration(Tomasini et al., 2024).

Several strategies can be implemented for the development of depots. A widely used approach involves the application of stimulus-responsive injectable hydrogels, which convert from liquid to gel, or the other way round, in response to an external stimulus such as changes in pH(Caddeo et al., 2019; Manconi et al., 2019), ionic composition(Xiang et al., 2019), exposure to UV radiation(Li et al., 2019) or changes in temperature(Dutta et al., 2020; Nie et al., 2011; Shah et al., 2018), thus being liquid during the injection and forming an in situ depot once in the appropriate tissue. Among these hydrogels, thermoreversible hydrogels are attractive materials for biomedical applications, since their gelation can be triggered by the temperature increase when injected subcutaneously. Specifically, Poloxamer 407 (Plx407), also known by the trademarks Pluronic® F127 and Synperonic® F127, is a thermo-responsive polymer extensively studied for use as SC depot due to its solubilizing capacities, low toxicity, drug release properties, and compatibility with numerous biomolecules and excipients(Giuliano et al., 2018). The Plx407 is a water-soluble nonionic triblock copolymer formed by a hydrophobic polypropylene oxide (PPO) block, flanked by two hydrophilic polyethylene oxide (PEO) blocks. In aqueous solutions, as a function of the concentration and the temperature, the polymer self-assembles into micelles whereby cores of PPO and water are surrounded by coronas consisting of PEO and water. At lower concentrations and/or temperatures, these micelles are found in a disordered liquid state. As temperature or concentration increases, gelation occurs, which is attributed to the dehydration of the micellar cores resulting in an entropic change of state into a partly ordered cubic liquid crystalline gel state(Giuliano et al., 2018; Russo and Villa, 2019; Sharma et al., 2008). Despite its many advantageous properties, the formulation of drugs into Plx407 hydrogels presents substantial challenges, such as limitations in the quantity and homogeneity of drug loading that can be achieved, especially for hydrophobic drugs, which are typically loaded in small amounts due to the predominance of water in the hydrogel(Giuliano et al., 2018). In addition, this high water content, coupled with the large pore sizes within the micellar matrix, often results in burst release for hydrophilic drugs when they are directly loaded into the hydrogel(Abdeltawab et al., 2020). In light of these limitations, no commercially available products based on subcutaneous depot systems derived from thermosensitive hydrogels have been reported to date. The only related commercial example is LeGoo®, a surgical medical device that generates temporary endovascular occlusion through the in situ gelation of a Poloxamer 407-based hydrogel (U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2011), which is not intended for sustained subcutaneous drug delivery. On this basis, designing a hybrid formulation that combines DELOS-NV and the Plx407 provides a unique opportunity to modulate the pharmacological features of the drugs entrapped in the hydrogel, thereby overcoming these traditional limitations. The incorporation of drug-loaded DELOS-NV into the Plx407 hydrogel can enhance both the drug-loading capacity and drug homogeneity in the hydrogel matrix. Moreover, as previously mentioned, DELOS-NV can improve the drug pharmacokinetics by modifying its release profile from the hydrogel, and by stabilizing and protecting the drug within the body fluids. Previous studies have demonstrated nanostructuration of non-water soluble molecules in the DELOS-NV membranes(Ardizzone et al., 2018; Liu et al., 2017), thus demonstrating their potential as nanocarriers for highly hydrophobic drug compounds. Furthermore, DELOS-NV can promote drug permeation through cellular membranes(Boloix et al., 2022) and extend the drug's blood circulation time(Cabrera et al., 2013), effectively acting as a second barrier to drug release and contributing to prolonged therapeutic effects.

The present work aims to combine the well-known benefits of the DELOS-NV delivery system with the thermoreversible Plx407 polymer for obtaining an in situ SC hydrogel depot. This hybrid platform is an advanced delivery system for the subcutaneous administration of challenging drug modalities. Its potential has been demonstrated by successfully formulating two molecules with low aqueous solubility and evaluating their in vitro stability and release.

First, novel DELOS-NV were specially designed for the SC administration route by a design of experiments (DoE), using exclusively excipients found in the FDA (U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2023) inactive ingredients database. Next, the designed nanovesicles were successfully gelled with different concentrations of Plx407, and their stability was demonstrated by Förster resonance energy transfer (FRET) efficiency evaluation and by cryogenic transmission electron microscopy (Cryo-TEM) imaging, two different orthogonal techniques. The obtained hybrid formulations were comprehensively analyzed for their physicochemical and rheological characteristics, leading to the identification of the most suitable for SC administration. The ability of this novel hybrid formulation to be injected and to form a SC depot was evaluated in vitro. In addition, a model hydrophobic carbocyanine, and a bRo5 compound with therapeutical relevance have been formulated into the hybrid formulation that combines DELOS-NV and the Plx407, achieving high entrapment efficiencies (EE%) in the DELOS-NV and stabilizing the low aqueous soluble molecules in the hydrogel. The in vitro release tests of the nanoconjugates from the hydrogel were performed, and the release kinetics profile defined.

Materials and methods

Materials

5-Cholesten-3β-ol (cholesterol (Chol) BioChemica, purity ≥95%) was obtained from PanReac AppliChem (Barcelona, Spain). The cetylpyridinium chloride (CPC, purity ≥98%) was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (Madrid, Spain). Ethanol (EtOH, High-Performance Liquid Chromatography -HPLC- grade) was obtained from PanReac AppliChem (Barcelona, Spain). carbon dioxide (CO2, purity ≥99.9%) and nitrogen (N2, purity ≥99.9%) were obtained from Carburos Metálicos (Barcelona, Spain). The 1,1′-dioctadecyl-3,3,3′,3′-tetramethyl-indodicarbocyanine perchlorate (DiD) and 1,1′-dioctadecyl- 3,3,3′,3′-tetramethyl-indocarbocyanine perchlorate (DiI) were acquired from Life Technologies (Carlsbad, CA, USA). Histidine buffer was prepared using 5 mM L-Histidine (Purity ≥99%) from PanReac AppliChem (Barcelona, Spain). The Dulbecco's Phosphate Buffered Saline (dPBS) was prepared with 140 mM sodium chloride (NaCl, purity ≥99.5%) purchased from Thermo Fisher Scientific (Waltham, MA, USA), 2.7 mM potassium chloride (KCl, purity ≥99.0%), 8.1 mM sodium hydrogen phosphate (Na2HPO4, purity ≥99.0%) and 1.5 mM sodium phosphate monobasic dihydrate (NaH2PO4·2H2O, purity ≥99.0%) acquired from Merck (Darmstadt, Germany). All buffers were prepared with purified water obtained with the water purification system Milli-Q Reference A+ (Millipore Ibérica, Madrid, Spain). Poloxamer 407 (Plx407) was acquired from Merck (Darmstadt, Germany). The Beyond the rule of 5 (bRo5) BI-0282 compound is a chemically synthetized molecule under investigation with therapeutic potential developed and provided by the DDS CMC Department at Boehringer Ingelheim through their opnMe.com open innovation portal.

Further, for the high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) analysis, Optima® LC/MS Grade 2-Propanol >99.9%, ammonium acetate >99.5% and glacial acetic acid >99.7% were obtained from Thermo Fisher Scientific (Waltham, MA, USA). Ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography (UHPLC) supergradient, ACS methanol >99.9% was purchased from PanReac AppliChem (Barcelona, Spain). Formic acid >98% was obtained from Merck (Darmstadt, Germany).

Preparation of DELOS-NV dispersions by DELOS method

The DELOS-NV with cholesterol (Chol) and cetylpyridinium chloride (CPC) (Chol:CPC DELOS-NV) were prepared by DELOS technology as previously described(Cabrera et al., 2013; Ferrer-Tasies et al., 2021) (see Fig. S1 in Supplementary Materials). Briefly, an organic solution of Chol (from 3.7 to 32.8 mg/mL), CPC (from 4.8 to 39.0 mg/mL), and EtOH was loaded into a high-pressure vessel at atmospheric pressure. Next, the solution was volumetrically expanded with compressed CO2, until a working pressure of 85 bar. Next, depressurization was conducted over histidine buffer at pH 7 using compressed N2 as a piston to expel the CO2-expanded solution from the vessel. In this way, Chol:CPC DELOS-NV aqueous dispersions were obtained containing 10% (v/v) of EtOH.

The 1,1′-dioctadecyl-3,3,3′,3′-tetramethyl-indocarbocyanine perchlorate (DiI) and/or 1,1′-dioctadecyl-3,3,3′,3′-tetramethyl-indodicarbocyanine perchlorate (DiD) dyes, incorporated in the fluorescent-labeled DELOS-NV, were dissolved in the EtOH solution containing 27.5 mg/mL of Chol and 24.5 mg/mL of CPC. The concentrations of dyes were adjusted for obtaining a final concentration of 45 μM DiD for the DiD-DELOS-NV or a final concentration of 90 μM (45 μM DiD and 45 μM DiI) for the DiI/DiD-DELOS-NV. The solutions were loaded into a high pressure vessel and the same conditions as for Chol:CPC DELOS-NV were applied.

The DELOS-NV containing the bRo5 BI-0282 compound were produced using an equivalent process: The bRo5 BI-0282 compound was also weighted and dissolved in the EtOH solution (10 mg/mL) containing the Chol (32.8 mg/mL) and the CPC (32 mg/mL). The concentration of bRo5 BI-0282 was adjusted to achieve a final concentration of 1 mg/mL in the bRo5-loaded Chol:CPC DELOS-NV. Next, this intermediate formulation was further concentrated to meet the target concentration for preclinical studies, as detailed below.

All the dispersions obtained were stored in appropriate containers, hermetically sealed, at 5 ± 3 °C.

Diafiltration and concentration of DELOS-NV by tangential flow filtration

To eliminate the EtOH from the obtained colloidal dispersions, and the non-integrated payloads in the case of loaded DELOS-NV (DiD-DELOS-NV, DiI/DiD-DELOS-NV and bRo5-loaded DELOS-NV), an ÄKTA flux Tangential Flow Filtration (TFF) System with modified polyethersulfone (mPES) hollow fiber module of 100 kDa cut-off was employed.

Additionally, the bRo5-loaded DELOS-NV were concentrated x2.4 times using the same TFF system and hollow fiber (100 kDa cut-off), to reach the final target concentration of bRo5 BI-0282 in the formulations. This concentrated formulation will be referred in the manuscript as bRo5-DELOS-NV.

Preparation of Poloxamer 407 hydrogels loaded with DELOS-NV

To obtain the DELOS-NV-loaded Plx407 hydrogels, Plx407 was added to DELOS-NV dispersions at 4 °C until reaching final polymer concentrations of 15%, 17% and 20% w/w. The samples were left overnight at 5 ± 3 °C for hydrating the polymer and the day after, magnetic stirring was applied in a bath at the same temperature until the sample became clear and free of suspended particles.

The same procedure was applied for Chol:CPC DELOS-NV, DiD-DELOS-NV, DiI/DiD-DELOS-NV and bRo5-DELOS-NV loaded into the hydrogels. Blanks without DELOS-NV of Plx407 in histidine buffer at pH 7 were also prepared following the same procedure (blank hydrogels).

The obtained formulations were stored at 5 ± 3 °C before analysing.

DELOS-NV and hydrogels characterization

Experimental design and data processing

Modde 12 Pro software (Umetrics, Sweden) was employed to construct a full factorial model with two factors at two levels (22) and a center point design of experiments (DoE). This approach allowed us to investigate how formulation parameters (independent variables) influence the physicochemical properties (dependent variables) of Chol:CPC DELOS-NV. Replicates of each experiment were conducted yielding a total of 10 experimental runs. The fitting of the data and the statistical parameters calculation were conducted using multiple linear regression (MLR) model as explained in Section 2.6.

Particle hydrodynamic diameter, size polydispersity index, and apparent zeta potential analysis

The particles' size as intensity-weighted hydrodynamic diameter, the size polydispersity index (PdI), and the apparent zeta potential (Z-potential) of the DELOS-NV colloidal dispersions (Chol:CPC DELOS-NV, DiD-DELOS-NV, DiI/DiD-DELOS-NV and bRo5-DELOS-NV) were determined using a Zetasizer Ultra (Malvern Instruments, Worcestershire, UK) with non-invasive backscatter optics and a helium‑neon laser at 633 nm. All the analyses were carried out at 25 °C. By Dynamic Light Scattering (DLS), derived from a cumulants analysis of the measured correlation curve, the hydrodynamic diameter and size PdI were determined. When measuring DLS of the DiD-DELOS-NV or DiI/DiD-DELOS-NV, a fluorescent filter was applied; otherwise, no filter was utilized. The apparent Z-potential was determined by Electrophoretic Light Scattering (ELS). The DLS and ELS results from the DoE represent the average of 6 measurements, derived from three consecutive measurements of two independent replicates. Other results presented in this paper represent the average of three successive measurements of one sample.

Quantification of the DELOS-NV membrane components by HPLC

The concentration of both Chol and CPC components from DELOS-NV samples were determined using an Acquity Arc UHPLC from Waters Corporation (Milford, MA, USA) coupled to a PDA 2998 detector. Each component was quantified by using its specific analytical method. Chol and CPC calibration curves were elaborated by separately preparing 5 different standards from 25 μg/mL to 250 μg/mL dissolved in methanol. The Chol:CPC DELOS-NV were analyzed in triplicate by dissolving them in methanol. All standards and DELOS-NV samples were eventually filtered with a 0.20 μm hydrophilic polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) filter from Merck (Darmstadt, Germany) into an HPLC vial.

Chol was quantified by using an XBridge BEH C18 column (2.5 μm 3.0 × 75 mm, Waters, Milford, MA, USA) in an isocratic mode of 90% of mobile phase A (volume ratio 5:95 water:methanol) and 10% of mobile phase B (0.1% formic acid in 2-propanol) at a flow rate of 1.0 mL/min for 4 min. The Chol detection wavelength was 206 nm.

CPC was quantified by using an InfinityLab Poroshell 120 EC-C18 column (4 μm 4.6 × 100 mm, Agilent) in a gradient mode starting at 12% of mobile phase A (50 mM ammonium acetate at pH 3.6) and 88% of mobile phase B (1% glacial acetic acid in methanol) for 3 min, then at 5% of mobile phase A and 95% of mobile phase B for 12 min, and eventually going back to the initial 12% of mobile phase A and 88% of mobile phase B for 5 min, at a flow rate of 1.0 mL/min for a total of 20 min. The CPC detection wavelength was 260 nm.

Morphological characterization by Cryo-TEM

The morphology of the various DELOS-NV types was examined using Cryogenic Transmission Electron Microscopy (Cryo-TEM) with a JEOL transmission electron microscope (JEOL Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) operating at 200 kV, as described previously (Ballell-Hosa et al., 2022). A drop of the DELOS-NV dispersion samples (Chol:CPC DELOS-NV, DiD-DELOS-NV, DiI/DiD-DELOS-NV and bRo5-DELOS-NV) was placed directly on a perforated copper grid. Instead, viscosity of the Chol:CPC DELOS-NV-loaded hydrogels was adjusted with a dilution 1:5 in water before placing them on the copper grid. After removing the excess of sample with a filter paper, the grid was introduced into a bath with liquid ethane held at −179.15 °C for its vitrification. Next, to avoid ice crystal formation, the sample was maintained at −196.15 °C during its transportation and observation in the microscope. Images were captured with a Gatan RIO 16 camera using Digital Micrograph 3.50.3584.0 (Gatan Inc., Pleasanton, CA, USA).

Small-angle X-ray scattering (SAXS)

The Small-Angle X-ray Scattering (SAXS) measurements were carried out at the NCD-SWEET beamline at the ALBA Synchrotron, Spain. The incident beam energy was set at 15 keV with a distance between the sample and detector 3.6 m (Pilatus 1 M detector, pixel size of 172 μm). Under those conditions, the scattering vector modulus q (q = 4π sin(θ)/λ, where 2θ is the scattering angle and λ is the incoming wavelength) was in the range from 0.067 to 4.8 nm−1. The measurements were performed at 22 °C. The liquid and hydrogel samples were placed in sample holders with Kapton® windows. The SAXS data for the DELOS-NV sample was analyzed by the model of polydisperse spherical vesicles(Tomsen-Melero et al., 2021), in which the cross-section structure consists of three layers, i.e. a central layer for describing the hydrocarbon region with an excess electron density lower than the buffer surrounded by two layers with positive excess electron density for describing the headgroup regions. It turned out that significantly better fits were obtained when the cross-section profile was asymmetric with different excess scattering lengths of the two headgroup layers. In agreement with previous models(Tomsen-Melero et al., 2021), the layers were described by Gaussian functions, Gaussrσ=exp−r2/2σ2, to have smooth variation of the electron density as a function of the distance r from the center:(1)∆ρr=Scph1Gaussr−R−Rphσph+SchcGaussr−Rσhc+Scph2Gaussr−R+Rphσph

Where the subscript ph refers to headgroups, hc to hydrocarbon, and R is the distance from the center to the middle of the bilayer.

For the asymmetric electron density profile, the two Gaussians representing the headgroups have independent amplitudes (Scph1≠Scph2). The Gaussian representing the hydrocarbon core had a fixed scale Schc=−1.

For the actual calculation of the form factor P(q,R), a general expression for centrosymmetric structures was used(Pedersen, 1997):(2)Pq=4π∫∆ρrsinqrqrr2dr24π∫∆ρrr2dr2

Thus, the form factor was calculated numerically from the electron density of the bilayer cross-section according to the sum of Gaussians. The distance between the centers of the two headgroup Gaussians was set to sum of half width half maximum of headgroup and hydrocarbon Gaussian, Rph=2ln2σph+σhc, whereas the sigma value was kept as a fit parameter to diminish artifacts from non-linear parameter correlation. Furthermore, the sigma value of the Gaussian that models the central hydrocarbon layer was set to 22ln2σhc=12Å. Thus, the variation in the cross-section was obtained from the sigma value for the headgroup. In this model, the external diameter of the vesicle is given as D=2R′+σhc+2σph. In practice, the integration in the form factor equation was performed using 100 steps in the range from -3 s of the inner Gaussian to +3 s of the outer Gaussian. Finally, the polydispersity of the overall size of the vesicles was taken into account in terms of a Gaussian distribution, and instrumental smearing mode was included using a pinhole resolution function(Pedersen et al., 1990).

Hydrogel microscopical appearance

The microscopical appearance and homogeneity of the hydrogels was assessed with the optical microscope Morphologi G3S (Malvern Instruments, Worcestershire, UK). One small drop of each hydrogel sample was placed on a glass slide covered with a coverslip. Then, the microscopic examination was carried out at room temperature and images were acquired at 20× magnifications.

Rheological characterization

The rheological measurements were performed using a Kinexus Pro+® (Netzsch, Germany). The geometry employed was a stainless-steel parallel plate (diameter 40 mm) at a 1 mm gap between plates.

Chol:CPC DELOS-NV-loaded Plx407 hydrogels or blank Plx407 hydrogels were poured on the rheometer lower plate at a given temperature, trimmed, covered with a thermal cover, and rested 10 min before each analysis. Three replicates of each hydrogel were produced and characterized with the rheometer.

Shear Viscosity Measurements

Flow measurements were performed at 4 °C (storage temperature), 22 °C (room temperature), and 37 °C (body temperature). Each measurement was performed with a three-step method. First a shear rate ramp from 0.1 to 500 s−1 followed by 60 s of a steady shear rate at 500 s−1 and a shear rate ramp from 500 to 0.1 s−1. Viscosity and shear stress were measured at each point and plotted.

Oscillatory Measurements

The shear oscillatory measurements were conducted at 10 Pa and 1 Hz, as optimized with preliminary amplitude and frequency sweeps, respectively. A temperature ramp from 4 °C to 40 °C at a rate of 1 °C/min was performed and the storage -or elastic- modulus (G'), the loss -or viscous- modulus (G"), and the phase angle (δ) across the temperature ramp were investigated. The gelation temperature (Tsol-gel), defined as the temperature at which the Plx407 solution becomes a gel, was determined as the temperature at which both modulus (G' and G") crossover.

Determination of the gelation time

The gelation time was determined using a modified version of the mechanical testing method described by Mura et al.,(Mura et al., 2018). In particular, 2 mL of the Chol:CPC DELOS-NV-loaded hydrogels and blank hydrogels, pre-stored at 4 °C, were placed in 3 mL vials and subjected to magnetic stirring at 500 rpm in a 22 °C water bath for 30 min to stabilize their temperature. Next, the vials were transferred to a 37 °C water bath while maintaining the same stirring conditions. The gelation temperature was determined in triplicate as the time required for the bar magnet to stop moving due to gelation at 37 °C.

Fluorescence and absorption spectroscopy measurements of fluorescent-labeled DELOS-NV and bRo5-DELOS-NV

Emission and excitation fluorescence spectra were collected with a Varian Cary Eclipse equipment (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA) using a 1 cm quartz SUPRASIL® precision cell (Hellma Analytics, Müllheim, Germany). FRET emission spectra (FRET samples, see 2.2, 2.5.10) were collected at 500–800 nm after excitation at 490 nm for DiI/DiD-DELOS-NV and for DiD-DELOS-NV as controls. In addition, the emission spectra (λex = 590 nm and λem = 600–800 nm) and the excitation spectra (λem = 710 nm and λex = 400–700 nm) were also collected for DiI/DiD-DELOS-NV and DiD-DELOS-NV, as controls. All the analysis were done on samples diluted 10 times in water to adjust the intensities recorded.

Absorption spectra were collected with a UV–Vis-NIR Varian Cary 5000 spectrophotometer (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). In particular, spectra were acquired by placing samples in a quartz SUPRASIL high precision cell (Hellma Analyti cs, Müllheim, Germany) with 1 cm pathlength at room temperature (25 °C). The measurements were carried out in the wavelength interval 700 to 200 nm. The spectra were measured at appropriate dilutions to have absorption values within the linear range. To calculate the EE% of the bRo5 BI-0282 compound within the DELOS-NV and the release of the bRo5- DELOS-NV from the hydrogels (see 2.2, 2.5.13, respectively), the absorbance intensities were converted into concentration by employing calibration curves.

In vitro injectability and thixotropy of DELOS-NV-loaded hydrogel

The injectability of the DiD-DELOS-NV-loaded 17% w/w Plx407 hydrogel was qualitatively evaluated through a manual injection test using a 1 mL syringe coupled to a 25 G needle. A volume of 1 mL of hydrogel was injected directly into dPBS (pH 7.4) pre-warmed to 37 °C. This simple test permitted evaluation of the practical flowability through the syringe and the thixotropic behavior of the hydrogel upon contact with the aqueous medium.

In vitro Plx407 dissolution test

The hydrogel dissolution profile and the in vitro release profile of DiD-DELOS-NV and bRo5-DELOS-NV from the 17% w/w Plx407 hydrogel formulation were obtained simultaneously, by adapting the Lee et al. method(Lee et al., 2021). Vials with an inner diameter of 15 mm and 60 mm height, containing 1.5 mL of DiD-DELOS-NV or bRo5-DELOS-NV-loaded 17% w/w Plx407 hydrogel were placed in an incubator with orbital agitation at 37 °C and 30 rpm. 45 min later, after a complete liquid-to-gel transition, 4.5 mL of dPBS (pH 7.4) pre-warmed to 37 °C were carefully layered over the hydrogels surface. Every hour, the entire release medium was removed by decantation, and the weight of the vial and remaining hydrogel recorded. The hydrogel weight lost (corresponding to the dissolved hydrogel) was calculated at each time point by subtracting the remaining weight of the hydrogel from the initial weight. Next, the percentage of dissolved hydrogel was calculated by dividing the hydrogel weight lost at the different time points by the initial hydrogel weight. The dissolution test was performed in triplicates.

In vitro release test

In vitro release behavior of DELOS-NV from the Plx407 hydrogel was monitored using a membrane-less diffusion method, according to the procedure described in the hydrogel dissolution test. The release profile of the DiD-DELOS-NV and the bRo5-DELOS-NV was determined by UV–Vis spectroscopy in a UV–Vis-NIR Varian Cary 5000 spectrophotometer (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA), as described in Section 2.5.9, by employing calibration curves.

It is important to note that these release experiments track the release of intact DELOS-NV (both DiD-labeled and bRo5-loaded) from the hydrogel matrix rather than the dissolution of the drug model. Therefore, classical sink conditions based on drug saturation solubility are not applicable. Instead, a release medium volume of 4.5 mL (corresponding to a 1:3 v/v ratio with the 1.5 mL hydrogel) was used. This volume provides a sufficient excess to ensure a continuous concentration gradient for the nanovesicles, preventing nanovesicle crowding or aggregation in the external medium.

To explain the mechanism and kinetics of in vitro release of the loaded DELOS-NV from the Plx407 hydrogel, the first 60% of the release curve was fitted to the Korsmeyer-Peppas equation (Eq. 4). This equation of the Korsmeyer–Peppas model is only valid for the first 60% of the fractional release(Karimi et al., 2023; Laracuente et al., 2020).(4)MiM∞=Ktn

Where Mi refers to the amount of intact, drug-loaded nanovesicles released over time, M∞ is the amount of drug-loaded nanovesicles in equilibrium, K is the release velocity constant, and n is the exponent of release.

This equation describes drug release from polymeric systems and was used to distinguish the release mechanism. When n = 0.5 the system is classified as Fickian with a diffusion mechanism driving a first-order release, and when n = 1, the system is non-Fickian, and polymer chain relaxation (hydrogel erosion) is the mechanism that leads the release, correlating with a zero-order drug delivery. Values of n in between 0.5 and 1 indicate a combination of diffusion and erosion release mechanisms(Karimi et al., 2023; Laracuente et al., 2020).

Next, the complete release curves were fitted into a zero-order model (Eq. 5):(5)MiM∞=Kt

Where Mi refers to the amount of intact, drug-loaded nanovesicles released over time, M∞ is the amount of drug-loaded nanovesicles in equilibrium and K is the zero-order release kinetic constant.

As a control, a similar release test was performed with the DiD molecularly dispersed in the Plx407 hydrogel without DELOS-NV. Due to its hydrophobic nature, the DiD (45 μM) was solubilized in a volume ratio of 1:2:97 EtOH:benzyl alcohol:water. Next, the solution was gelled with 17% w/w Plx407, and the in vitro release was performed as with the DELOS-NV-loaded hydrogels.

The release tests were performed in triplicate.

Statistical analysis

All data are reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD) of the mean.

The DoE data was fitted to a multiple linear regression (MLR) model using Modde 12 Pro software (Umetrics, Sweden), and statistical parameters were calculated from the fitting. For the fitting, the size PdI results, due to not following a normal distribution, were logarithmically transformed. The applicability of the models was assessed by the Q2 and R2 parameters provided by the Modde 12 Pro software. The obtention of the design space was also conducted with the Design Space Explorer option from the Optimizer module of the same software.

In the remaining experiments, the differences among groups were evaluated by Student's t-test. The statistical analysis was performed in Microsoft Excel software (Microsoft Corp, Redmond, WA). A P-value under 0.05 was deemed statistically significant. Statistical significance is indicated as follows: * p < 0.05, ** p < 0.01, and *** p < 0.001.

As the next step, Chol:CPC DELOS NV with a molar ratio CPC:Chol 1:1 and a membrane lipid concentration of 5 mg/mL were prepared by DELOS method, and the CQAs were also evaluated. Additionally, as was specified in the Materials and Methods section, all formulations prepared by the DELOS method contain a small percentage of EtOH derived from the production process. Although low percentages of EtOH as an excipient are acceptable in SC administration(Rowe and Owen, 2009; U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 2023), it is not required in the developed formulation. Therefore, it was decided to remove the EtOH by Tangential Flow Filtration (TFF) (see Materials and Methods section).

Physicochemical characterization of this post-TFF DELOS-NV formulation reveals minimal impact of EtOH removal on the colloidal dispersion CQAs. This formulation is stable and at 14 days exhibits a mean hydrodynamic diameter of 70.3 ± 0.5 nm, a size PdI of 0.269 ± 0.008, and an apparent Z-potential of 78.0 ± 0.1 mV, comparable to that of the non-TFF formulation. In addition, the TFF process had only a minimal effect on the final concentration of membrane components. Following TFF, there was a 94% recovery of Chol ([Chol] before TFF = 2.36 ± 0.04 mg/mL; [Chol] after TFF = 2.21 ± 0.05 mg/mL) and a 79% recovery of the CPC ([CPC] before TFF = 2.115 ± 0.024 mg/mL; [CPC] after TFF = 1.67 ± 0.04 mg/mL), as determined by HPLC (Materials and Methods section). These high recovery rates indicate that only a few molecules of membrane components are not integrated into the DELOS-NV and are lost through the TFF column pores. Taking into account all the characterization results, this final formulation obtained after the TFF process is considered the optimized prototype of Chol:CPC DELOS-NV dispersion prior to gelation.

Development of a hybrid platform based on the thermoreversible Plx407 hydrogel enriched with Chol:CPC DELOS-NV for SC administration

Chol:CPC DELOS NV-loaded Poloxamer 407 hydrogels: Preparation and physicochemical characterization

The preparation of thermoreversible Poloxamer 407 (Plx407) hydrogels incorporating DELOS-NV was designed to create a hybrid platform that leverages the complementary strengths of both systems. Specifically, DELOS-NV are intended to entrap and stabilize highly hydrophobic payloads (e.g., bRo5 molecules) that conventional hydrogels struggle to load efficiently. In turn, the Plx407 matrix aims to provide the macroscopic structure needed to retain these nanovesicles at the injection site, thereby preventing their rapid dispersion. Consequently, the primary goal of this formulation strategy is to successfully merge these two components into a homogeneous, thermoreversible system.

To evaluate the formation of this hybrid platform, the optimized prototype of Chol:CPC DELOS-NV, produced by the DELOS process and diafiltrated by TFF, was gelled by adding the thermoreversible Plx407 polymer (see details in Materials and Methods). Different concentrations of Plx407 (15%, 17% and 20% w/w) were selected based on the literature(Akkari et al., 2016; Antunes et al., 2011; Bodratti and Alexandridis, 2018; Kojarunchitt et al., 2011; Nie et al., 2011) and tested to identify the most appropriate concentration for SC delivery of DELOS-NV, as a function of the hydrogel temperature transition from solution to gel (Tsol-gel) and cohesivity. Blank hydrogels were also prepared as controls, using the same aqueous medium as the DELOS-NV but without nanovesicles.

Plx407 was chosen due to being a non-ionic gelling agent since the compatibility of DELOS-NV with non-ionic polymers has previously been demonstrated(Ballell-Hosa et al., 2022). In addition, this polymer is recognized as GRAS (generally recognized as safe) substance and is accepted and listed in the US and European Pharmacopeia(Rowe and Owen, 2009; Giuliano et al., 2018; Shah et al., 2018). The hydrogels prepared with this polymer were macroscopically examined. Colorless blank Plx407 hydrogels and whitish Chol:CPC DELOS-NV-based Plx407 hydrogels, very homogeneous in appearance, were obtained, exhibiting excellent organoleptic properties. Fig. 5 presents images of the hydrogels at 4 °C, 22 °C, and 37 °C, illustrating the viscosity change according to temperature for the three concentrations of Plx407 tested. While all samples are liquid at 4 °C (the hydrogel flows from the base of the vial to the cap of the vial when inverted), at 22 °C each hydrogel concentration displays a different behavior. The 15% and 17% w/w Plx407 hydrogels still behave as liquids, whereas the 20% w/w hydrogel shows an increase in viscosity and some resistance to flow. At 22 °C, the SAXS profiles for the Chol:CPC DELOS-NV-loaded hydrogels with 15% and 17% w/w Plx407 correspond to a structure of Plx407 micelles (Akkari et al., 2016), exhibiting only a short range of order and a low solid-like behavior. These initial findings suggest that Chol:CPC DELOS-NV can be gelled in the different concentrations of Plx407 tested, leading to the obtainment of thermoreversible hydrogels that are homogeneous at the macroscopic, microscopic and nanoscopic levels.

Integrity of DELOS nanovesicles embedded in Plx407 hydrogels

It is well known that the structure and stability of nanovesicles are notably affected by the dispersion medium(Ballell-Hosa et al., 2022; Vargas-Nadal et al., 2020). Certain media, such as buffers with high salt content, can destabilize the nanovesicles, leading to the formation of aggregates and/or membrane breakage. To demonstrate the preservation of DELOS-NV structure when incorporated in hydrogels, Cryo-TEM images of the different Chol:CPC DELOS-NV-loaded Plx407 hydrogels were obtained as described in Materials and Methods.

Dilutions 1:5 (v:v) of the Chol:CPC DELOS-NV-loaded hydrogels (15%, 17% and 20% w/w Plx407) in water were performed to adjust the viscosity for analysis. Due to the thermodynamic stability of this type of nanovesicles, it is important to note that they do not dissociate upon aqueous dilution and therefore retain their size and morphology even under extreme dilution conditions(Ferrer-Tasies et al., 2013; Köber et al., 2023). The concentration of Plx407 of the different Chol:CPC DELOS-NV-loaded hydrogels upon this dilution was in the range of 2.8 to 3.5 mM Plx407. At these low concentrations and room temperature, the hydrogels behave as liquids. It should be noted that the polymer may exist in its monomeric state at these concentrations, as its critical micellar concentration (CMC) is highly temperature-dependent, and is around 1.25 mM at 27 °C(He et al., 2022) and 2.8 μM at 37 °C(BASF, 2020).

Cryo-TEM images (Fig. 6A) showed well-formed spherical, unilamellar, and homogeneous nanovesicles in all the tested formulations. Worth noting, higher concentrations of Plx407 hydrogel resulted in the coating of some Chol:CPC DELOS-NV, probably due to partial adsorption of the polymer's hydrophobic chains into the nanovesicle membrane. This can be appreciated by an increase in the nanovesicle membrane's width in the Cryo-TEM images. After having confirmed maintenance of the spherical and unilamellar structure of Chol:CPC DELOS-NV within the hydrogels by Cryo-TEM, a characterization of the bulk stability of the colloidal dispersion was conducted using Förster resonance energy transfer (FRET) phenomenon. The FRET phenomenon occurs between a pair of light-sensitive molecules, where the emission spectrum of one molecule (the donor) overlaps with the excitation spectrum of the other (the acceptor). When these pairs of molecules are closer than 10 nm of each other, there is an energy transfer from the donor chromophore, initially in its electronically excited state, to the acceptor chromophore. This technique is known as the spectroscopic ruler because the efficiency of the energy transfer is inversely proportional to the sixth power of the distance between the donor and acceptor(Algar et al., 2019). For this reason, it can be employed as a research tool to determine nanovesicle stability and integrity: If nanovesicles break down or dissociate, a decrease in FRET efficiency should be detected (Fig. 6B)(Ballell-Hosa et al., 2022; Sun et al., 2018).

An example of a well-known FRET chromophore pair is 1,1′-dioctadecyl-3,3,3′,3′-tetramethyl-indocarbocyanine perchlorate (DiI) and 1,1′-dioctadecyl-3,3,3′,3′-tetramethyl-indodicarbocyanine perchlorate (DiD). Both have been integrated into similar DELOS-NV in previous studies, demonstrating FRET efficiency when embedded in the nanovesicles (Ardizzone et al., 2018; Ballell-Hosa et al., 2022; Morla-Folch et al., 2020). Importantly, Carbocyanines are amphiphilic molecules and are primarily incorporated into the DELOS-NV membrane through hydrophobic interactions. Once incorporated on the nanovesicles' membrane, their leakage is negligible (Ardizzone et al., 2018).

Based on this, the novel Chol:CPC DELOS-NV optimized for SC delivery were prepared loading DiI and DiD and characterized by DLS and ELS. This new formulation prepared by the DELOS method (see Materials and Methods) has been named DiI/DiD-DELOS-NV. As a control, a batch containing exclusively DiD at the same concentration as it is present in the DiI/DiD-DELOS-NV formulation was also produced and characterized (DiD-DELOS-NV). Morphology analysis by Cryo-TEM, confirmed that both formulations presented spherical and unilamellar nanovesicles (Fig. S7, Supporting Information). After 14 days of maturation, the DiI/DiD-DELOS-NV had a mean hydrodynamic diameter of 100.7 ± 0.6 nm, a size PdI of 0.349 ± 0.021, and an apparent Z-Potential of 76 ± 7 mV, while the DiD-DELOS-NV had a mean hydrodynamic diameter of 114.4 ± 2.8 nm, a size PdI of 0.432 ± 0.026, and an apparent Z-Potential of 52 ± 7 mV (Fig. S8, Supporting Information). Periodical analysis of these CQAs demonstrated the stability of the loaded nanovesicles for at least 2 months (Fig. S8, Supporting Information). Even though the size PdI of the DiD-DELOS-NV was slightly higher than desired (see Table 2), it should not affect its functionality as a control of DiD excitation and emission. Afterward, the DiI/DiD-DELOS-NV were gelled adding 15%, 17%, and 20% w/w of Plx407 (Fig. S9, Supporting Information) and their stability and integrity evaluated by measuring the fluorescence emission of the dyes. The DiD-DELOS-NV were also gelled with 17% w/w Plx407 as a control.

The absorbance spectra of the pair of fluorophores incorporated into the DiI/DiD-DELOS-NV (Fig. S10, Supporting Information) did not present significant variations from the liquid dispersion to the gelled state, indicating stability of the DiI/DiD-DELOS-NV and residence of the fluorophores in the membranes despite the formulation. The monomeric DiI and DiD fluorophores absorb at 551 nm and 650 nm, respectively; however, carbocyanines are prone to aggregate into H-aggregates and J-aggregates with differentiated absorption peaks(Eisfeld and Briggs, 2006; Würthner et al., 2011). The absorbance spectra of the DiI/DiD-DELOS-NV demonstrated the predominance of the non-aggregated carbocyanines, in both the dispersion and hydrogels. The absorbance spectrum of the control with only DiD (Fig. S10, Supporting Information) shows a peak at 650 nm and no peak at 551 nm, as expected. Predominance of monomers non-dependent on the formulation (liquid dispersion or gel) was also demonstrated with the 17% w/w DiD-DELOS-NV-loaded Plx407 hydrogel.

Next, the FRET efficiency was investigated for the dispersion and the different concentrations of Plx407 hydrogels containing DiI/DiD-DELOS-NV. As shown in Fig. 6C, the excitation of the DiI at λex = 490 nm resulted in two emission bands at ∼569 nm and at ∼673 nm, corresponding to the DiI and DiD emission, respectively, thus indicating the FRET phenomenon. The efficiency of this energy transfer was similar for each formulation at 2 days and 30 days after incorporation of the Plx407 polymer. As anticipated, excitation of the control DiD-DELOS-NV at λex = 490 nm did not result in DiD emission due to the absence of FRET (Fig. S11, Supporting Information).

Finally, the FRET efficiency of non-gelled and gelled DiI/DiD-DELOS-NV in Plx407 was evaluated using a ratiometric approach based on emission spectra obtained at 490 nm excitation wavelength and the Eq. 3(Ballell-Hosa et al., 2022) (see Materials and Methods). The estimated FRET efficiencies were consistently around 70% for both states (Fig. 6D), aligning with previous findings on the stability of similar DELOS-NV formulations in both liquid dispersions and gelled with hydroxypropyl methylcellulose hydrogels(Ballell-Hosa et al., 2022). In addition, stability tests over time showed no significant differences between the FRET efficiency measurements at time 2 days and 30 days post-preparation, suggesting structural stability of the DiI/DiD-DELOS-NV within the Plx407 hydrogels for at least 30 days.

Overall, the experimental results confirmed the stability of the Chol:CPC DELOS-NV gelled with different concentrations of Plx407 polymer, which settles the basis for the development of a novel hybrid platform for SC delivery combining both systems: DELOS-NV and the thermoreversible Plx407 hydrogel.

Rheological behavior of Chol:CPC DELOS NV-based Plx407 hydrogels

The flow properties of the different Plx407 hydrogels containing Chol:CPC DELOS-NV were assessed by means of a shear rate ramp in a rotational mode at different temperatures: storage temperature (4 °C), room temperature (22 °C), and body temperature (37 °C). As shown in Fig. 7, the rheological behavior of the evaluated hydrogels varies with temperature and Plx407 concentration. At 4 °C, all hydrogels exhibit a Newtonian fluid behavior, where the viscosity remains constant regardless of the applied shear force. In contrast, at 37 °C, all the hydrogels exhibit pseudo-plastic behavior with shear-thinning characteristics, showing yield stress -a minimum force required to start to flow-, and decreasing viscosity with increasing shear rates. All hydrogels exhibit low thixotropy at 37 °C (Fig. 7). This is evidenced by their small or non-existent hysteresis loop area, indicating that their inner structure recovers quickly after shear-thinning. This property could facilitate prompt depot formation after injection, preventing spread.

Viscosity also plays a crucial role in preventing spread after SC injection. While low viscosities enable flow through SC syringes (injectability), higher viscosities can slow down water penetration during injection, reducing burst release and hydrogel dissolution rate in body fluids(Parent et al., 2013). Viscosities were compared between samples at 22 °C and 500 s−1 (Table 3), which are the closest conditions to real injection scenarios from tested conditions. However, typical shear rates during injections are higher(Rył and Owczarz, 2020), leading to lower Plx407 hydrogel viscosities due to their shear-thinning behavior.

The viscosity results presented in Table 3 confirm that hydrogels containing Chol:CPC DELOS-NV exhibit higher viscosities. As expected, the differences in viscosities between blank and Chol:CPC DELOS-NV-loaded Plx407 hydrogels are more pronounced at the 20% w/w Plx407 concentration.

Furthermore, lower polymer concentrations result in lower viscosities. The 15% and 17% w/w Plx407 hydrogels have viscosities ≤0.1 Pa s, making them readily injectable through a 25 G syringe, which is the preferred option for SC administration(Miller et al., 2010). In contrast, the viscosity values of the 20% w/w Plx407 hydrogels exceeded 0.1 Pa s, potentially requiring the use of a smaller gauge syringe for SC injection. This highlights the importance of tuning up Plx407 concentration to achieve good injectability, ensuring effective delivery and patient comfort.

The Tsol-gel was studied by determining the storage (G') and loss (G") modulus at different temperatures (Fig. 8). The storage modulus relates to the material's ability to store energy elastically, and the loss modulus relates to the material's ability to dissipate energy through heat and describes the viscose part of the sample.

Plx407 hydrogels exhibit a unique behavior in which they perform as viscous fluids at lower temperatures and become solid-like at higher temperatures. This behavior is reflected by a predominance of G" over G' at lower temperatures, which is then inverted to G' > G” at higher temperatures. The temperature at which G" equals G' is the Tsol-gel, which is also called low critical solution temperature.

The ramp temperatures showed a gradual decrease in the Tsol-gel in response to the Plx407 concentration increase (Fig. 8). Blank hydrogels and Chol:CPC DELOS-NV-loaded hydrogels with 15% and 17% w/w Plx407 present Tsol-gel values above 25 °C, making them appropriate for injection at 22 °C. Oppositely, the blank 20% w/w Plx407 hydrogel presents a Tsol-gel around 22 °C, which is in concordance with the elevated viscosities determined previously at this temperature. Therefore, for further studies we decided to focus on the 15% and 17% w/w Plx407 concentrations, as the 20% w/w Plx407 may require more stringent room temperature control during injection, smaller gauge (i.e., thicker) needles, or a pre-treatment to reduce viscosity. For this reason, the Tsol-gel of the Chol:CPC DELOS-NV-loaded 20% w/w Plx407 hydrogel was not evaluated.

Again, the impact of the Chol:CPC DELOS-NV on the Tsol-gel of the 15% and 17% w/w Plx407 hydrogels was assessed (Table 3). The incorporation of Chol:CPC DELOS-NV in the hydrogels causes a slight decrease in the Tsol-gel.

The ‘phase angle’ (δ) is a relative measure of the viscous and elastic properties of a material. It can be obtained by taking the arctangent of the ratio of the loss modulus to the storage modulus (tan δ = G”/G'). It illustrates the lag between the movement of the sample with the oscillatory movement of the cone. A purely elastic material will have a phase angle of 0, while a purely viscous material will have a phase angle of 90°. Therefore, viscoelastic materials have phase angle values between 0 and 90°. The greater the value of G' concerning G", the lower the value of the phase angle. Taking it into account, the presence of Chol:CPC DELOS-NV in the Plx407 hydrogels results in a more solid-like behavior of the hydrogels by decreasing their phase angle at 37 °C (Table 3). This is a favorable feature, as more solid-like hydrogels are expected to exhibit lower dissolution rates in body fluids, resulting in longer subcutaneous (SC) depots.

Overall, the results show how the Chol:CPC DELOS-NV influence the behavior of the Plx407 hydrogels by increasing their solid behavior, reducing their phase angle, and slightly decreasing their Tsol-gel. This effect is believed to occur due to the entrapment of water molecules into the hydrophilic core of nanovesicles(Nie et al., 2011). This results in the dehydration of the core of hydrogel micelles, which is the primary mechanism of gelation(Nie et al., 2011; Sharma et al., 2008).

All these rheological characterizations suggest that the most appropriate hydrogels for SC depot formation with Chol:CPC DELOS-NV are the 15% and 17% w/w Plx407 concentrations. Both hydrogels present an appropriate Tsol-gel to be injected at room temperature through a 25 G needle and very low thixotropy, enabling rapid depot formation at 37 °C. As expected, the 17% w/w Plx407 presents higher viscosities and a lower phase angle than the 15% w/w at 37 °C (Table 3) and is, therefore, considered the optimal option between the two.

To finally corroborate the rheological properties determined by the rheological analysis, the 17% w/w Plx407 hydrogel loaded with DiD-DELOS-NV (the same hydrogel employed as a control in FRET analysis) was qualitatively evaluated through a manual injection test (see Materials and Methods). Specifically, this hydrogel was injected at room temperature through a 25 G needle into dPBS at 37 °C (Fig. S12, Supporting Information). The flow through the syringe was effortless, without requiring the application of strong force, and once the hydrogel came in contact with the 37 °C dPBS, a depot was formed demonstrating its low thixotropy. In this way, the appropriate rheological properties of the DiD-DELOS-NV-loaded 17% w/w Plx407 hydrogel for SC administration were demonstrated.

Gelation time of the Chol:CPC DELOS NV-loaded Plx407 hydrogels

In addition to all the rheological characterizations, the gelation time of the different blank and Chol:CPC DELOS-NV-loaded hydrogels with 15% and 17% w/w Plx407 was also determined (see Materials and Methods). The blank 15% w/w Plx407 hydrogel showed a gelation time of 46.3 ± 2.5 s, while the same concentration of Plx407 with Chol:CPC DELOS-NV only required 36.3 ± 2.1 s to have a solid-like behavior. Similarly, for the 17% w/w Plx407 hydrogels, the gelation time of the blank hydrogel was 35.7 ± 2.8 s, and for the hydrogel containing Chol:CPC DELOS-NV, it was 14.0 ± 4.5 s (Fig. S13, Supporting Information). These results are consistent with the rheological observations shown in the previous section and indicate that the presence of Chol:CPC DELOS-NV in the hydrogels has a similar effect to increasing the Plx407 concentration, by reducing the time required to transition from liquid to gel.

It is important to emphasize that these results are intended solely for comparative analysis among different hydrogel formulations because the in vitro/in vivo correlation is low. In this test, the area of the hydrogel that allows heat transfer is substantially smaller than the area involved in an actual injection. For this reason, the observed gelation times in this test appear significantly higher than the practically instantaneous gelation observed after injection of the DiD-DELOS-NV-loaded 17% w/w Plx407 hydrogel into dPBS at 37 °C (Fig. S12, Supporting Information).

In vitro Chol:CPC DELOS-NV release from Plx407 hydrogels

To determine the release kinetic profile of the platform, in vitro tests were conducted with the DELOS-NV-loaded Plx407 hydrogels. The hydrogels' rheological characterization demonstrated that the 15% and 17% w/w Plx407 hydrogels loaded with Chol:CPC DELOS-NV presented the best flowability for SC injection. Specifically, for the in vitro release tests, the 17% w/w Plx407 has been chosen since it is more solid-like, expecting it to provide longer-lasting depots.

Two in vitro tests were performed using two different DELOS-NV formulations to compare the release kinetics. The first formulation consisted of the DiD-DELOS-NV (the same nanovesicles employed as a control in FRET analysis). As aforementioned, DiD is an amphiphilic carbocyanine that integrates hydrophobically into the DELOS-NV membranes and presents negligible leakage. This molecule was chosen as a model compound to enable straightforward spectroscopic quantification and to validate the stabilization and release behavior mediated by DELOS-NV.

The second formulation consisted of DELOS-NV loaded with a therapeutically relevant bRo5 compound. bRo5 compounds are drug molecules that do not fit Lipinski's Rule of Five criteria for oral bioavailability, but can still retain potent biological active and therapeutical potential, particularly in areas where conventional small molecules struggle. The bRo5 BI-0282 compound was employed for the in vitro release (see chemical structure in Fig. S14A, Supporting Information). It targets the MDM2 oncoprotein and is a 593.43 Da molecule with a 6.3 LogP/DpH 7.4. Hence, it is also a highly hydrophobic molecule like DiD. The bRo5 BI-0282 compound was first loaded to the DELOS-NV through the DELOS process, achieving 78% entrapment efficiency (EE%¸ defined as the ratio of encapsulated to total drug added), and afterwards, the loaded nanovesicles were concentrated up to 2.4 times through TFF to target preclinical dose requirements (see Materials and Methods). The physicochemical characterization of the bRo5-DELOS-NV is shown in Fig. S14B and C, Supporting Information.

For the DiD-DELOS-NV-loaded 17% w/w Plx407 hydrogel (the same hydrogel employed as a control in FRET analysis), a sustained release over 8 h was achieved at 37 °C using dPBS pH 7.4 as the release medium (see Materials and Methods) (Fig. 9A). The release of the intact DiD-DELOS-NV from the hydrogel matrix was steady and constant, without any initial burst release. The release data were fitted to a Korsmeyer-Peppas model to mathematically determine the release mechanism and kinetics of the system. The Eq. 4 (see Materials and Methods) was fitted with a K value of 8.5 and n value of 1.0. The goodness of fit was assessed by the R2, which was 0.9999. A value of n = 1 indicates that the model is non-Fickian and correlates with zero-order kinetics, which is governed by the hydrogel dissolution rate(Karimi et al., 2023; Laracuente et al., 2020). This mathematical approach was conducted only with the first 60% of the release as later-stage release conditions do not meet the assumptions used to derive the equation. Based on this initial result, the zero-order mathematical model was then employed to fit the complete release curve (see Eq. 5 in Materials and Methods and Table S1, Supporting Information). The graphic with the fitting (K = 9.08; R2 = 0.9998) is represented in Fig. 9A. Consistently, the release of intact bRO5-loaded DELOS-NV from the 17% w/w Plx407 hydrogel also followed zero-order kinetics at 37 °C in dPBS pH 7.4 (see Materials and Methods and Table S1, Supporting Information) (Fig. 9B). The zero-order mathematical model was fitted with a K = 7.6 and a R2 = 0.9997. In comparison to the 8-h release observed for the DiD-DELOS-NV, the bRo5-DELOS-NV release extended to 10 h. This difference can be attributed to the higher concentration of nanovesicles in the hydrogel formulation. As described previously, the bRo5-DELOS-NV were concentrated 2.4–fold through TFF to meet the target bRo5 BI-0282 concentration for preclinical studies. Hence, the NV concentration in the bRo5-DELOS-NV-loaded 17% w/w Plx407 hydrogel is higher. Previous rheological analyses demonstrated that the presence of DELOS-NV reduces the hydrogel phase angle, enhancing its solid-like behavior. Consequently, Plx407 formulations with higher concentrations of DELOS-NV are expected to yield longer-lasting depots.

In parallel with the release tests, dissolution tests of the Plx407 hydrogel formulations were performed, and showed zero-order dissolution kinetics (Fig. 9C,D). The hydrogel weight loss over time was constant for both formulations and closely matched the release profiles, providing evidence that the release kinetics of the intact DELOS-NV is governed by the hydrogel dissolution rather than by diffusion of the DiD- or bRo5-loaded DELOS-NV through the hydrogel network, a mechanism commonly observed in conventional hydrogel-based systems(Abdeltawab et al., 2020).

As a control, a release test without DELOS-NV was carried out, containing exclusively the DiD solubilized in the formulation (1:2:97 (v/v) EtOH:benzyl alcohol:water +17% w/w Plx407). The molecularly dispersed DiD-loaded 17% w/w Plx407 hydrogel exhibited a good macroscopical appearance; however, the absorbance intensity of the DiD in this formulation was significantly lower than the same concentration of DiD formulated into the DELOS-NV and 17% w/w Plx407 (Fig. S15, Supporting Information). The release test of the molecularly dispersed DiD could not be properly quantified because sedimentation of the DiD occurred in the dPBS during the release test (Fig. 9E). This was due to the low solubility of this hydrophobic molecule in the aqueous release medium, despite the presence of the organic solvents used in its formulation. This result also highlights the potential of the DELOS-NV as nanocarriers for stabilizing highly hydrophobic compounds in physiological media.

These in vitro release and dissolution tests demonstrate the immeasurable potential of formulating DELOS-NV into hydrogels to achieve an extended release of challenging compounds with zero-order kinetics. Zero-order kinetics is the desired release kinetics for drug delivery because it allows maintenance of drug concentrations within the therapeutic window for an extended period, thus, reducing dosing frequency and adverse effects(Laracuente et al., 2020). The dissolution tests demonstrate that the loaded DELOS-NV release is driven by hydrogel dissolution rather than diffusion of the NV through the polymeric matrix. This lack of diffusion of the DELOS-NV, which is ideal for avoiding an initial burst release, is probably caused by the hydrophobic interaction of the amphiphilic DELOS-NV with the hydrophobic cores of the polymer micelles(Abdeltawab et al., 2020). Another possible reason could be a reduction of the diffusion rate due to the entrapment of the DELOS-NV into the diffusion channels of the polymeric matrix caused by their larger size in comparison to hydrophilic small molecules and/or actives.

Beyond demonstrating promising zero-order release kinetics, the DELOS-NV platform proved highly effective in stabilizing challenging hydrophobic drug candidates in aqueous environments. In particular, the Chol:CPC DELOS-NV enabled the formulation of the therapeutically relevant bRo5 BI-0282 compound while maintaining a homogeneous hydrogel system. The successful stabilization and extended release of the bRo5 BI-0282 compound highlights the translational potential of the DELOS-NV platform for delivering therapeutically valuable yet formulation-challenging molecules.

The hydrophobic DiD was employed as a model compound to validate this approach. The control experiment performed using the molecularly dispersed DiD confirmed the capability of the DELOS-NV to efficiently vehiculate hydrophobic molecules upon release from the depot into dPBS, as shown in Fig. 9E.

Importantly, aside from stabilizing compounds in physiological media, these DELOS-NV can confer protection to the payloads, prolong the plasmatic half-life, and favor a controlled release and site-specific targeting, as demonstrated in previous publications(Boloix et al., 2022; Ferrer-Tasies et al., 2021; Tomsen-Melero et al., 2021; Vargas-Nadal et al., 2020).

This study establishes the proof-of-concept of a thermoreversible hydrogel-based platform enabling zero-order, erosion-driven release of intact, drug-loaded nanovesicles suitable for subcutaneous administration. The in vitro release duration of 8–10 h represents an initial performance window. Building on these results, ongoing development (under the international patent application (WO/2026/087715, 2026)) is evaluating alternative stimulus-responsive polymers (e.g., hyaluronic acid, chitosan, PLGA, and PCL) to modulate matrix erosion and achieve multi-day to multi-week depot profiles tailored to specific therapeutic requirements.

Poloxamer 407 is a widely used excipient with an extensive pharmaceutical history, and existing evidence supports its suitability for injectable depot systems(Dumortier et al., 2006). Nevertheless, additional toxicological evaluation will be required for translational progression. Similarly, although CPC as cationic surfactant may raise concerns regarding local irritation, it is a well-established excipient in oral antiseptics and topical formulations(Mao et al., 2020). Importantly, within the DELOS-NV system CPC is not present as a free surfactant but is stably self-assembled with cholesterol in the vesicle membrane, which is expected to substantially reduce local exposure and mitigate irritation risk. This interpretation is consistent with previous reports showing the biocompatibility of DELOS-NV containing quaternary ammonium surfactants, including intravenous administration in mice(Vargas-Nadal et al., 2020) and topical application in patients with chronic diabetic foot ulcers(Ferrer-Tasies et al., 2021).

Collectively, these data support the robustness of the present proof-of-concept and delineate clear directions for further development, particularly in extending depot duration and advancing preclinical safety assessment.

Conclusions

In this study, a new hybrid platform based on the Poloxamer 407 (Plx407) thermoreversible hydrogel loaded with DELOS nanovesicles (DELOS-NV) was successfully developed and thoroughly characterized as a subcutaneous in situ depot drug delivery system. All formulation excipients selected in the composition were non-toxic and listed in the FDA inactive ingredients database, ensuring safety and biocompatibility. The DELOS-NV were optimized using cetylpyridinium chloride (CPC), a quaternary ammonium surfactant, and cholesterol (Chol) as membrane components through a design of experiments approach, and gelled with different concentrations of the Poloxamer 407 polymer. The integrity of the DELOS-NV was maintained within the hydrogels for at least one month, as evidenced by fluorescence resonance energy transfer (FRET) ratiometric analysis, and cryogenic transmission electron microscopy (Cryo-TEM). The rheological characterizations identified the 17% w/w Plx407 hydrogel loaded with DELOS-NV as the most suitable candidate for creating a subcutaneous in situ depot. This hydrogel flows easily through a syringe at 22 °C and rapidly transitions to a solid-like state at 37 °C. Two in vitro release tests were performed using DELOS-NV loaded with hydrophobic compounds: one with the DiD as model molecule, and another with the therapeutically relevant bRo5 BI-0282 compound. The in vitro release profiles demonstrated a steady zero-order release governed solely by the hydrogel dissolution rate, representing an ideal kinetic profile for drug delivery applications. The concentration of DELOS-NV influenced the release time (8 or 10 h) by modulating the hydrogel phase angle. Furthermore, the DELOS-NV effectively stabilized the hydrophobic molecules in the release medium, preventing precipitation, as occurred with the molecularly dispersed DiD in the control.

These findings support the advantages of combining a thermoreversible hydrogel for in situ depot formation with the powerful enabling DELOS-NV technology, which facilitates stabilization and transport of challenging-to-formulate molecules, such as bRo5 compounds, in biological media.

A novel adaptive transformer based quantum intrusion detection system for software defined networks
Authors: C. M. Nalayini, T. R. Soumya, S. D. Lalitha, R. Tamijetchelvy
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Intrusion detection in Software Defined Networks (SDNs) faces critical challenges due to evolving attack surfaces and increasing traffic complexity. This paper proposes a novel Adaptive Transformer-based Quantum Intrusion Detection System (ATQ-IDS), integrating four core components: Quantum-Inspired Evolutionary Selection (QIES) for optimal feature reduction, a Transformer-Spatial Temporal Network (TSTN) for deep traffic context modeling, Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning-based IDS (HRL-IDS) for adaptive policy control, and a Federated Learning-enabled IDS (FL-IDS) for decentralized, privacy-aware deployment. The QIES component minimizes model overhead by selecting a reduced, high-utility feature set, while the TSTN captures intricate spatial and temporal patterns using attention mechanisms. HRL-IDS ensures decision adaptability in dynamic traffic environments, and FL-IDS supports real-time distributed detection with minimal communication cost. Experimental evaluations on benchmark SDN datasets demonstrate that ATQ-IDS achieves state-of-the-art accuracy of 99.84%, with a false negative rate of 0.12% and inference time below 7 ms. Ablation studies and robustness analyses with confidence intervals confirm the contribution of each module and the model’s consistency across runs. This architecture demonstrates high accuracy, adaptability, and real-time applicability, making it a robust solution for modern SDN-based security systems.

Introduction

The advent of SDN has completely transformed network management through a centralized, programmable control plane, that offers dynamic traffic routing, increased scalability, and ease of policy enforcement. However, this is where SDN is very vulnerable to security threats, and Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS) attacks are perhaps the biggest threat. For such attacks, Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) are indispensable to detect and mitigate them in order to maintain network stability and availability. As an effective IDS in SDN, it should have high detection accuracy, good adaptability to the evolving attack patterns, low false positives, real time decision making, and scalability for the large-scale network environment. Most of the conventional IDS mechanisms are faced with a high price of trade-off between detection performance and computational efficiency failing to perform well in dynamic SDN environments1. Furthermore, as the attacks become more sophisticated, the IDS solutions have to be advanced beyond conventional methods with advanced intelligence driven technologies to provide a solid protection to cyber threats2. An ideal IDS should be able to not only detect known attack patterns, but also detect zero-day attacks, traffic anomalies, as well as emerging network vulnerabilities with little latency and high precision.

Even though IDS for SDN has made great progress, the efficiency is hindered by many challenges in practical implementations. Low dimensionality overtakes scalability due to feature selection bottlenecks due to high dimensionality of network traffic data, thus increasing the computational overhead and slowing detection process. Usually, static rule-based approaches fail to effectively respond to the adaptive and stealthy cyber threats with high false positive rate. Centralized data processing used by many IDS frameworks forms single point of failure vulnerabilities, which make large scale SDN deployment problematic3. However, most models still have a big problem of real time adaptability; most models need to be retrained periodically, which causes a lag in recognition of new attack patterns. Similarly, the machine learning based IDS approaches are vulnerable to overfitting and bad generalization, and hence, models trained with such an IPC perform well only on specific datasets but do not perform well in real-world environment. Moreover, without such an approach to automated hyperparameter tuning, there is more manual work and model optimization across distinct network conditions. These limitations indicate the requirement for an efficient, adaptive and scalable IDS that can operate effectively in real world SDN environment with computational efficiency.

Intrusion detection in SDN has been completed with numerous techniques, which use ML and DL to improve detection accuracy4. As a first stage for detecting new threats, rule-based detection schemes masqueraded as earliest entrants, relying on given signatures and anomaly thresholds. However, these systems are not able to identify novel and changeable attack patterns, and thus they are not suitable for dynamic SDN environments. Statistical anomaly detection techniques such as entropy-based models deal with statistical anomalies of the network’s normal behavior. While these approaches help with adaptability, there is still a hard task to distinguish between legitimate and malicious traffic spikes hence more false alarms. For network traffic classification, more recent approach includes using supervised machine learning models, namely: Support Vector Machines (SVM), Decision Trees (DT), Random Forest (RF) and Naïve Bayes (NB)5. While these models work well with training datasets that contain labels and at detecting known attack patterns, they are not good with high dimensional datasets, so they need some mechanism for effective feature selection such as Recursive Feature Elimination (RFE) to reduce computational complexity. Furthermore, traditional ML classifiers are also incapable of real time adaption since they rely on static training dataset and retraining is still needed for novel attack variations.

The use of deep learning techniques as an alternative for intrusion detection in SDN has emerged as a powerful technique, due to the way neural networks can learn hierarchical representations of the traffic features6,7. Such models as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), Long Short-Term Memory Networks (LSTM), and Transformer-based architectures have been proved to be very successful in detection of attacks and in other domains8,9. IDS models based on CNNs extract spatial features from traffic on the network, while LSTM models learn sequential dependencies in flow on the network. Traditional Detector models are enhanced through the use of Transformer based models including Self Attention Mechanisms, Temporal Spatial Transformers, which assign attention scores to some critical traffic patterns. Furthermore, ensemble learning of multiple classifiers, such as Random Forest ensemble of SVM or Deep Neural Networks (DNN) with Gradient Boosting has also been shown to be very robust. Thankfully, deep learning-based IDS solutions has problem such as the ultra-computational cost, require large, labeled datasets and vulnerability to adversarial attacks. Recently, IDS models have been using reinforcement learning (RL) technique to refine themselves by using reward driven learning strategies to adjust to the condition changes in the network continuously. IDS based on RL can also optimize the detection thresholds in an atomic way in order to defend sophisticated threats. However, advanced deep learning and RL models face challenges in their efficiency and efficiency of hyperparameter tuning as well as long learning times while being inefficient at adapting on the fly in real time.

The existing IDS approaches are not sufficient to effectively handle large scale SDN environment, evolving attack patterns and real time constraints, and therefore an efficient, adaptive, and scalable intrusion detection system is needed. A major objective is to achieve an IDS that combines advanced feature selection, deep learning and reinforcement learning in order to improve detection accuracy over some required computational complexity. A second goal is to develop an IDS that is able to adapt to emerging threats without frequent manual retraining, and without human assistance. In order to achieve this, we propose ATQ-IDS, that incorporates QIES, TSTN and an HRL-IDS of an attack for accurate and real time DDoS attack detection. Quantum Superposition Encoding is used by QIES to dynamically extract optimal features, reducing high dimensionality while keeping important features relationships. Self-attention mechanisms are applied to TSTN to analyze sequential traffic patterns and find anomalies in network flow. Deep Q-Networks (DQN) with reward driven learning are used for refining the detection boundaries continuously and for adaptive responses to novel threats using reward driven learning at HRL-IDS. Also, FL-IDS makes scalability possible and provides the support for secure deployment of FD with multiple SDN controllers.

ATQ-IDS is novel, based on its description of a multi-layer intrusion detection as using QIES, TSTN and HRL-IDS for real time adaptability. Contrary to the conventional IDS models, this system lacks rule based or the threshold-based methods and utilize self-adapting mechanisms to refine the detection capabilities over time. Federated learning further improves scalability on the basis of large scale SDN infrastructures while maintaining the decentralized security. Furthermore, the model is tuned to minimize computational overhead whilst improving detection performance to a level of reliability required for the real time SDN security applications.

The dynamic nature of SDN traffic, combined with the growing sophistication of cyber-attacks, presents a compelling need for an intrusion detection system that is not only accurate but also lightweight, adaptive, and scalable. Traditional ML/DL-based IDS frameworks struggle to balance complexity, detection accuracy, and real-time applicability. Motivated by this gap, we propose ATQ-IDS, a unified framework designed to achieve optimal feature reduction, temporal-spatial pattern learning, adaptive decision-making, and decentralized deployment in SDN environments.

Related work

The recent advancements in DDoS attack detection in SDN environments using machine learning methodologies is presented in10. The presented model analyzes different classification-based approaches, including machine learning techniques such as SVM, Random Forest, Decision Trees and demonstrated their effectiveness in identifying malicious traffic patterns. Experimental analysis reveals that training datasets such as CIC DOS 2019 and NSL-KDD are widely used for model evaluation, offering a balance between normal and attack traffic flows. Despite achieving high accuracy levels of up to 95%, many approaches struggle with scalability in large networks due to increased computational overhead and controller resource constraints. Moreover, deep learning-based detection mechanisms exhibit superior feature extraction capabilities but are often hindered by prolonged training durations and substantial hardware requirements. Some studies also highlight the limitations of classification-based models in handling real-time traffic fluctuations, as they tend to exhibit high false positive rates in dynamic SDN environments. Overall, while recent advancements have improved detection precision, future research should focus on real-time adaptability, reducing computational overhead, and optimizing deep learning models for efficient deployment in large-scale SDN architectures.

DDoS detection in SDN using supervised learning techniques is presented in11. In order to classify the network traffic and detect volumetric attacks, the analyzed analysis presents multiple machine learning (ML) models such as DT, K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), SVM and Bagging Trees (BT). The methodology trains models using DARPA, DASD, InSDN datasets and evaluated it on the basis of accuracy, precision, recall and F1 score. Experimental results show that BT and KNN are always better than other models, with over 99% accuracy, while SVM and DA suffer from the low performance on account of feature selection sensitivity and training set composition sensitive. Nevertheless, there is a major limitation that most models are based on single feature detection, and it may not generalize well to real time network fluctuations. Additionally, high computational overhead and longer detection time prevent its use in real time. Future research should focus on optimizing lightweight models, enhancing real-time adaptability, and integrating deep learning with reinforcement learning to improve detection accuracy while reducing false alarms.

Machine learning-based IDS for SDN are incorporated in12 focusing on classification algorithms and feature selection techniques. The presented approach explores different IDS approaches, highlighting signature-based and anomaly-based detection methods, with an emphasis on hybrid feature selection and ensemble learning for enhanced accuracy. The proposed Hybrid Feature Selection with LightGBM (HFS-LGBM) model integrates Correlation-Based Feature Selection (CFS) and Random Forest Recursive Feature Elimination (RF-RFE) to refine the feature set before classification using LightGBM. Experimental evaluations using the NSL-KDD dataset demonstrate superior performance, with HFS-LGBM achieving 98.72% accuracy, 97.45% precision, 97.92% recall, and 98.23% F1-score, outperforming SVM, Linear Regression, CatBoost, and XGBoost. However, the study highlights computational overhead and model complexity as limitations, affecting real-time adaptability. Future improvements could focus on reducing training time, optimizing model efficiency for large-scale SDN deployments, and enhancing adaptability to evolving cyber threats through deep learning and reinforcement learning techniques.

The comparative analysis of classical machine learning techniques and deep learning-based intrusion detection systems (IDS) is presented in13 for SDN. The study evaluates rule-based, signature-based (SNIDS), and anomaly-based (ANIDS) IDS models, highlighting their advantages and shortcomings. The proposed approach integrates unsupervised feature learning with DNNs, reducing dependency on manual feature engineering. The NSL-KDD dataset is used for experimental validation, comparing Decision Trees, Extra Trees, LightGBM, and DNNs. The results indicate that PCA-based feature reduction combined with DNN achieves the highest test accuracy of 79.3%, outperforming traditional classifiers like Decision Trees (77.8%) and Extra Trees (76.9%). However, deep learning models require more computational resources, making real-time implementation challenging. Additionally, false-positive rates remain high in anomaly-based methods. Future enhancements should focus on improving scalability, reducing computational overhead, and incorporating adaptive learning techniques to handle evolving network threats dynamically.

Machine learning techniques for DDoS attack detection is presented in14 for SDN-based IoT (SD-IoT) networks, focusing on intrusion detection system (IDS) methodologies. The proposed framework utilizes NB, DT, and SVM classifiers to detect malicious network behavior. A testbed setup within an SDN-WISE IoT controller is used for data collection, traffic simulation, and classification. Experimental results indicate that DT achieves the highest accuracy (98.1%), followed by NB (97.4%) and SVM (96.1%), demonstrating the efficiency of machine learning for attack mitigation. Despite promising results, the framework incurs up to 30% CPU and memory utilization, which may affect scalability in resource-constrained IoT environments. Additionally, increased packet frequency slightly impacts detection time, highlighting the need for optimized processing techniques. Future research should focus on reducing computational complexity, improving real-time adaptability, and extending the model to address evolving cyber threats in large-scale SD-IoT networks.

The deep learning-based IDS presented in15 for SDN-enabled IoT networks, addressing the limitations of traditional IDS models. The study proposes LSTM based architecture, utilizing time-series analysis for detecting and classifying network attacks. Different ML and deep learning (DL) models are compared such as: SVM, DNN, and CNN. Experimental results are shown on two SDN-IoT datasets (DS1 and DS2) to demonstrate that LSTM is able to achieve better accuracy (97.1%) than SVM (86%) and CNN (96%), which exhibits the capability to recognize the complex attack patterns. Although the method is very effective, high computational overhead and memory consumption are still barriers to its deployment in real time IoT environments. Future research should target reducing training time in SDN-IoT, improving scalability and introducing adversarial defense mechanism to build up resilience against changing network threats in SDN-IoT infrastructures.

Based on the deep active learning model for anomaly detection and resource allocation as in16, the network intrusion detection model for SDN sensors is presented. An entropy based active learning is integrated with an intelligent load balancing algorithm in order to dynamically distribute the tasks for optimizing sensor usability. Network resource utilization is improved, and packet level intrusion detection is provided, which can provide efficient detection of malicious activities. The proposed approach is leveraged to experimental setup using state of art intrusion detection dataset and compared against traditional heuristic based methods such as Max–Min and Min–Min scheduling algorithms. The results show that execution time of the proposed model is reduced by two times, and its throughput and resource allocation efficiency is increased two times. Nevertheless, computational overhead and real time adaptability are still a major limitation, as the data flow in SDN environments can eventually cause latency issues. Future research needs to tackle the reduction of convergence time, sensor allocation strategy, and federate learning to allow security and scaling in dynamic SDN architectures.

In17, the information entropy and the deep learning-based classification is added to one of the two level DDoS attack detection framework to improve the attack detection. In the first stage, the methodology uses an entropy-based anomaly detection mechanism to detect suspicious components and ports at coarse granularity to then use a fine-grained CNN based packet classification model for attack differentiation in fine granularity. Experimental validation shows that proposed approach attains 98.98% detection accuracy that is superior to conventional methods. Nevertheless, the challenge of deep learning model real time adaptability remains, due to the fact that deep learning models require high processing power, which makes large scale deployment difficult. Improvements to future SDN security applications should include optimizations of computational efficiency, adaptive learning enhancements, and reduction of false positives for real time SDN security applications.

In18, deep learning-based intrusion detection systems are presented for security of the IoT and SDN, which practise automated anomaly detection using advanced LSTM networks. The existing approaches to IDS including the machine learning based classification models, rule-based systems and ensemble learning methods are discussed in the study and their shortcomings in dealing with evolving cyber threats are pointed. The proposed Secured Automatic Two-Level Intrusion Detection System (SATIDS) uses an improved LSTM model that can distinguish between benign and attack traffic, as well as to identify the category of attack and sub attacks. It is further experimentally analyzed on two benchmark datasets, ToN-IoT and InSDN, where it shows 96.35% accuracy, 96% detection rate, and 98.4% precision on ToN-IoT and 99.73% accuracy, 98.6% detection rate, and 98.9% precision on InSDN. The system has an effectiveness, but at the expense of high computational overhead and long training times that impact large scale IoT deployments. Prior work should be used to improve the future of model complexity, feature selection techniques, and integration of adversarial learning strategies for maximizing ability to adapt to evolving cyber threats.

An ensemble learning method in19 used for intrusion detection in SDN based networks that demonstrate the effectiveness of improved classification accuracy. The optimization of the Bagging, Boosting, and Stacking techniques along with their results was studied to improve the detection performance with models like Random Forest, Decision Trees, and Support Vector Machines (SVM). A hybrid ensemble model, which combines a version of Incremental Particle Swarm Optimization (IPSO) with Multi Class Support Vector Machine (MCSVM) was proposed to improve feature selection as an indirect approach and more precisely, refines classification. The ensemble model is tested on KDD Cup 99 dataset using the experimental evaluation and is found to achieve 99.49% accuracy, which is superior to standalone classifiers. The model is also compared to Weka based simulations results, which validate its ability to distinguish attack patterns. Nevertheless, limitations such as high computational complexity and feature redundancy are possible hindrances for achieving real time applications. Reducing training overhead, integrating deep learning for adaptive learning, and optimizing the ensemble models for scalability improvement are the future work on improving the scalability of the SDN systems in the dynamic environments.

Real time intrusion detection and zero-day attack identification are the challenges faced by the Network Intrusion Detection Systems (NIDS)20 for Software Defined Networking (SDN). Among the traditional supervised machine learning models, it is limited to detect novel threats based on predefined signatures. To overcome this limitation, the study proposes Griffin, an unsupervised NIDS based on machine learning which is able to effectively detect known as well as zero-day intrusion in real time. To reduce the throughput overhead, the methodology is based on an efficient feature extraction framework to capture sequential traffic patterns and then performs cluster analysis to reduce the dimensionality. In addition, an ensemble autoencoder for low complexity and high precision feature learning is designed for high detection accuracy. The experimental results indicate that Griffin obtains up to 19% better accuracy and 40% lower complexity than existing NIDS solutions while achieving only 9% decrease in AUC under evasion scenario. But scalability is still an issue, and the focus should be placed on reducing the costs of model retraining for SDN applications at scale and improving the adaptive learning.

Also, in21 they integrated two SVM, two RF and two Gradient Boosted Machine (GBM) all optimized with distinct hyperparameters. Then a hybrid metaheuristic optimization algorithm (BHO) is employed to obtain the optimal classifier weights which enhances robustness of detection. Then the model is trained on CIC-DDoS2019 and evaluated on CAIDA-2007 with 99.4163% and 99.3591% accuracy, respectively, outperforming the existing methods. Though it has a high detection rate, it is computationally complex, and it generally is not adaptable to changing threats. Specific improvements of the conducted work for reducing the processing overhead, for optimizing the scalability of the ensemble, and for incorporating deep learning for the adaptive threat detection are the future work.

Advanced intrusion detection mechanism for Software Defined Networking (SDN) in the context of ensemble machine learning techniques to detect Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attacks as presented in22. Existing traditional ML based IDS models suffer from very high false alarm rate as well as poor classification accuracy and, therefore, require more robust detection frameworks. For addressing these challenges, an Intelligent Intrusion Detection System for SDN (SD-IIDS) is introduced, which implements two ensemble models, namely Random Forest bagged with Logistic Regression (RF-LR) and Support Vector Classifier bagged with Random Forest (SVC-RF). Finally, these models are evaluated in binary and multi class classifications, which achieve 99.79% and 99.54% accuracy, with very low false alarm rates (0.0005 and 0.012). A multi class RF-LR model outperforms SVC-RF with 99.45% precision and 99.46% sensitivity and superior attack classification abilities. Nevertheless, adaptability within real time is an issue. In terms of future research, scalability of the model needs to be optimized, as well as real time processing, and the attack capabilities extended for protocol based and volume-based DDoS attacks in SDNet.

In23, deep learning-based intrusion detection systems are integrated with Software-Defined Networking to increase the cyber threat detection in IoT environments. An efficient way of identifying malicious activity is presented using a hybrid DNN-LSTM framework (Cu-DNN-LSTM) that is a combination of deep neural networks and long short-term memory networks. Using the CICIDS2018 dataset, the model is trained with several attack scenarios including brute force, botnet, infiltration and DDoS attacks. Finally, the proposed Cu-DNNLSTM model is compared against DNN-GRU and BLSTM classifiers, whereas the results shows that Cu-DNNLSTM model is able to achieve 99.55 accuracy, 99.36 precision, 99.44 recall and 99.42 F1 score, better than the existing deep learning-based IDS. Nevertheless, training time and computational overhead are still challenges for real time deployment in large scale IoT systems. The future work will be to reduce complexity, select the best features, and integrate reinforcement learning to enhance real time adaptability and combat the changing security threats in the SDN enabled IoT networks.

In24, a hybrid cuLSTMGRU framework is introduced which adopts Cuda Long Short-Term Memory (cuLSTM) and Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) for enforcing intrusion detection with a higher efficiency. It was experimented using CICIDS2017 dataset with different kinds of attacks, DoS, brute force, botnet and infiltration. Results are comparative and show that cuLSTMGRU is able to achieve 99.23% accuracy compared to cuBLSTM (97.31%) and cuGRUDNN (97.02) thus proving its effectiveness. The proposed system also has a low false positive rate (0.0066%) and faster detection time (15.3 ms) and is suitable for real time applications. Although, computational overhead is still a limitation, especially for large scale IoT networks. In future research, a model with a lower complexity is to be built, selecting the feature as optimum, and to add a reinforcement learning mechanism to improve the adaptability and the scalability of the system in changing IoT environment.

A hybrid detection framework introduced in25 combines the use of Continuous Wavelet Transform (CWT) and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) to extract and classify attack patterns with good efficiency. CWT is used to convert network traffic signals into wavelet transform representations and hence improves feature extraction by capturing frequency and time domain information. These transformed features are input to a CNN based classifier for detection of high precision attack. Experimental results of the proposed model are evaluated against DNS amplification, NTP, and TCP-SYN flood attack, with a high detection rate and a very low false alarm rate, which validates the effectiveness of the proposed model. Nevertheless, CWT based transformations incur processing overhead and it is a computational complex and real time adaptive challenge. Future research should further work in the feature selection, latency reduction and adaptive learning integration to improve the detection efficiency in large scale SDN environments.

Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) is used as feature selection in combination with a CNN-LSTM framework for attack classification in the hybrid detection model, which is proposed in26. XGBoost effectively drops feature dimensions to speed up processing at minimal cost to critical attack characteristics. Spatial and temporal analysis are integrated using CNN-LSTM model to increase the accuracy of the attack detection. The proposed model is shown to achieve higher accuracy with a low time cost and thus proves its efficiency for low latency IIoT applications, on the CICDDoS2019 dataset. But scalability and ability to respond to zero days still are difficult to achieve. Summary of literature Review.

Although there are current advances in DDoS attack detection28–33 and intrusion detection systems in SDN34–37, there still remain several important research gaps that demand further development of a more efficient and adaptive approach. Supervised learning-based classifier like (DT, KNN, SVM, RF) and hybrid ensemble models have already proved their capabilities for high classification accuracy, but they are not scalable and thus not adaptable to the changing cyber threats. Deep learning-based IDS solutions (LSTM, CNN-LSTM, CuDNNLSTM) improve feature extraction and detection rates, but high computational complexity and training overhead hinder real-time deployment. Additionally, entropy-based and wavelet transformation-based methods introduce processing latency, limiting their applicability for large-scale SDN networks. Many proposed models also suffer from high false-positive rates and increased detection time, affecting network efficiency. At the same time, most current approaches that are not adaptive at runtime are also not real time and are hence ineffective in combating zero-day attacks and dynamically changing DDoS strategies. The challenges in this make a lightweight, scalable, and computationally efficient IDS framework, which incorporates the adaptive reinforcement learning, optimized feature selection and deep learning based temporal spatial analysis for real time attack detection is necessary while keeping low computational overhead. This will make an intelligent, self-optimization IDS able to handle complex attack patterns on large scale SDN architectures with high accuracy and low false alarm.

Altamimi and Abu Al-Haija38 enhanced IoT network security by applying Extreme Learning Machines (ELMs) for efficient intrusion detection, achieving faster training and reliable detection performance. Building on this, Al-Haija et al.39 provided a survey that critically analyzed ELM applications in intrusion detection systems, comparing their strengths, scalability, and challenges against other machine learning models. Complementing these works, Al-Tamimi and Al-Haija40 discussed supply chain security in IoT and IIoT, focusing on emerging technologies and future cybersecurity trends. Together, these studies highlight ELMs’ potential in intrusion detection while addressing broader IoT security challenges.

Proposed work

The proposed Adaptive Transformer Based Quantum Intrusion Detection System which is an advanced intrusion detection framework to protect SDN environment from DDoS attacks. By intertwining QIES, Temporal-Spatial TSTN with Hybrid Reinforcement Learning (HRL-IDS) this model is able to overcome the limitations of existing IDS solutions and help make real time adaptability and detection accuracy. Deep Q-Networks (DQN) utilized by HRL-IDS for refining the classification boundaries dynamically can achieve adaptive learning with respect to the evolving attack behaviors. Federated Learning is also used by the model for distributed training across SDN controllers to achieve scalability and security. The model captures both temporal and spatial dependency of network traffic and thus is able to robustly detect anomalies. It includes self-attention mechanisms, positional encoding, and feed forward neural networks (FFN) in order to get better intrusion detection accuracy while keeping up the computational efficiency. The Major steps in TSTN Framework include Traffic Sequence Encoding, Self Attention Mechanism, Temporal Feature Extraction, Spatial Feature Processing, Classification and Anomaly Detection.

Hybrid reinforcement learning for Real-Time adaptation (HRL-IDS)

In the proposed work, the Hybrid Reinforcement Learning is developed to dynamically adapt to the evolving DDoS attack patterns in SDN environment. Currently, traditional machine learning based IDS models are static and need to be retrained from time to time to accommodate new threats. Federated learning for scalability (FL-IDS)

In the proposed work, the Federated Learning based Intrusion Detection System is designed to tackle scalability and decentralized learning with respect to multiple SDN controllers while ensuring data privacy. Centralized training in traditional IDS model involves high computation costs, network congestion and potential privacy risks caused by sharing of sensitive traffic data. To accommodate such issues, Federated Learning (FL) transfers the learning task to different nodes, where each node trains a local model and combines global model using aggregated local models. This allows FL-IDS to deal with evolving cyber threats while keeping the network efficient. In particular, the FL-IDS framework consists of four major steps: Local Model Training on Distributed SDN Controllers, Federated Model Aggregation using Secure Updates, Adaptive Weighting for Global Model Optimization and Privacy Preserving Mechanism for Secure Communication.

The FL-IDS module follows a classic client-server architecture tailored to the SDN control plane. Each SDN switch acts as a federated client, locally training a lightweight copy of the intrusion detection model on its own flow statistics, while a centralized SDN controller functions as the parameter server. The training process is conducted in synchronous communication rounds, where each client trains for 5 local epochs before sending model updates to the server. Model aggregation is handled using the Federated Averaging (FedAvg) algorithm, which computes a weighted average of client models based on the number of training samples processed locally. To reduce communication latency, clients only participate every alternate round if their bandwidth usage exceeds a predefined threshold, ensuring bandwidth-awareness. Furthermore, the global model is broadcast back to clients after each aggregation round, and convergence is typically achieved in fewer than 20 global rounds. This setup balances detection accuracy and network efficiency, making it suitable for distributed SDN environments where data privacy and communication overhead are critical.

Results and discussion

The proposed Adaptive Transformer-Based Quantum Intrusion Detection System is experimentally evaluated using a well-structured simulation framework that mimics real-world SDN environments under different attack scenarios. CICIDS 2019 is a traffic dataset that generates traffic using a wide range of cyber threats such as DDoS, brute force attacks, and port scanning. QIES is then applied to reduce high dimensional traffic features in the selected dataset through preprocessing which optimizes the computational efficiency. TSTN is trained on pre-processed data in Python with TensorFlow and PyTorch using the multi-head self-attention mechanism to learn the spatial and temporal dependencies from traffic sequences. During the training, HRL-IDS is used for learning intrusion detection thresholds dynamically and adaptively to changing attack patterns. FL-IDS, in which intrusion detection models are trained across multiple distributed SDN controllers using Federated Averaging (FedAvg), are also experimented with (i.e. for scalability and privacy). The performance of the model is evaluated using important metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score and detection time to provide a complete assessment. Finally, performance of proposed ATQ-IDS is compared to standard models such as RF, SVM, DGN and standard transformer models to showcases superior performance.

To assess the real-time applicability of the proposed ATQ-IDS framework, we deployed the model on a simulated SDN testbed with the following system specifications: Intel Core i7-11700 F CPU 2.50 GHz, 32GB RAM, NVIDIA RTX 3060 GPU, and ONOS SDN controller version 2.5.0. The entire pipeline—including QIES-based feature selection, Transformer inference, and hierarchical classification, was containerized using Docker to mimic edge-node deployments. The average inference latency per packet was recorded at 6.7 ms, and batch processing latency for 1000 packets was < 2.1 s, indicating strong suitability for near real-time operation. Additionally, federated synchronization across five distributed nodes incurred only ~ 0.3 s of communication delay per global round due to adaptive update intervals. These results validate the computational feasibility of ATQ-IDS in realistic SDN environments and support its deployment in latency-sensitive network infrastructures.

Because RF-RFE uses a tree-based feature selection scheme, it is able to maintain the lowest overhead compared to all other approaches, which do not rely on extensive back propagation or deep learning computation. With the tradeoff between accuracy and efficiency, the proposed ATQ-IDS with TSTN achieves improvement in intrusion detection performance while its transformer-based architecture needs higher computations for sequential learning. Thus, this confirms that ATQ-IDS with TSTN has good computational efficiency and better detection accuracy, which makes it a good model for real time IDS applications.

Ablation Studies.

To evaluate the individual impact of each core module in the ATQ-IDS architecture, namely QIES, TSTN, HRL-IDS, and FL-IDS, a detailed ablation study is conducted and results tabulated in Table 5. Starting from the baseline SDN dataset without any enhancement modules, we incrementally added each component and recorded key performance metrics (Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-score). As shown in Table 5, the inclusion of QIES alone improves accuracy from 86.2% to 90.4% by optimizing the feature set. Adding TSTN further boosts spatial-temporal understanding, raising accuracy to 93.1%. HRL-IDS improves adaptive classification, achieving 95.5% accuracy, and finally, FL-IDS enhances model generalizability and decentralization, reaching a peak accuracy of 98.4%. This progressive gain demonstrates the independent and cumulative benefits of each module. Moreover, training time is also reduced by ~ 38% with QIES and ~ 22% with FL-IDS compared to the baseline, justifying their inclusion from both effectiveness and efficiency standpoints.

The proposed ATQ-IDS architecture strategically balances complexity and efficiency by modularizing these components and optimizing their execution pathways. Specifically, the TSTN operates only on QIES-selected features, significantly reducing input dimensionality and computation per layer. Likewise, the HRL-IDS employs a layered reward structure with a reduced state-action space due to prior feature and context filtering. Importantly, the federated learning module operates asynchronously and only in decentralized deployment scenarios, with adaptive communication intervals to reduce bandwidth and synchronization costs. Experimental profiling shows that the overall training time per round remains under 85 s, which is competitive with many single-layer DNN-based methods. Moreover, ATQ-IDS’s modular structure allows components to be deployed selectively, enabling flexibility for resource-constrained environments. Thus, the proposed model does not compromise deployability and meets the lightweight design goal while maintaining high intrusion detection performance.

To further validate the benefit of using Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning, we implemented a non-RL baseline classifier using a standard hierarchical decision tree with identical QIES-selected features and the same dataset. As shown in Table 6, the RL-based approach achieves a higher accuracy (95.5% vs. 89.6%) and significantly lower false negative rate (1.2% vs. 3.7%). Moreover, convergence to optimal classification policy required fewer iterations (20 vs. 32), indicating the RL model’s ability to adaptively optimize decision boundaries based on live feedback. These findings substantiate the choice of HRL over static rule-based or tree-based approaches in dynamically evolving SDN environments.

Statistical Analysis.

The experimental results and performance metrics clearly indicate that the proposed ATQ-IDS significantly outperforms conventional IDS models in terms of detection accuracy, precision, recall, and real-time adaptability. Compared to traditional machine learning models such as RF-RFE, and deep learning methods like CNN with PCA or DGN-KFold, ATQ-IDS consistently achieves higher classification performance across all evaluation criteria. This improvement is primarily attributed to the synergistic integration of the QIES feature selection module and the TSTN, which together reduce feature dimensionality and enhance sequential-spatial pattern recognition. HRL-IDS component adds dynamic adaptability, allowing the model to evolve detection strategies in response to changing attack patterns, which is particularly critical for combating zero-day threats.

Moreover, the incorporation of FL-IDS demonstrates that ATQ-IDS can scale efficiently across distributed SDN environments without centralizing sensitive traffic data, ensuring privacy and reducing communication overhead. The federated setup not only maintains high detection accuracy but also achieves rapid convergence within limited communication rounds, a result that existing centralized models struggle to replicate. The discussion of computational overhead also reinforces that the proposed model strikes an effective balance between detection performance and resource consumption, maintaining real-time feasibility with an average inference latency of 6.7 ms and modest overhead per epoch.

When compared to recent works that employ ensemble classifiers or conventional LSTM-based architectures, the ATQ-IDS framework demonstrates superior generalization and adaptability due to its deep attention-based architecture and reinforcement-guided optimization. Models such as CuDNNLSTM or SATIDS, while effective in controlled benchmarks, often incur significant training time and computational cost, making them less suitable for live SDN environments. Additionally, the proposed QIES module enables faster feature convergence and model training, offering at least a 40% reduction in both training time and dimensionality, as shown in the feature convergence analysis.

In summary, the ATQ-IDS framework successfully addresses the limitations of existing IDS solutions by offering a unified, modular, and adaptive detection pipeline. Its performance superiority, scalability, and practical deployability make it an ideal candidate for real-time SDN security applications, especially in environments that demand both precision and privacy-preserving distributed learning.

Conclusion

This work presented ATQ-IDS, an intelligent intrusion detection framework for SDN environments that synergistically integrates quantum-inspired feature selection, Transformer-based temporal-spatial learning, hierarchical reinforcement learning, and federated model deployment. The proposed QIES module significantly reduces input dimensionality, thus lowering model complexity and training time. TSTN improves detection accuracy by capturing long-range spatial-temporal dependencies. HRL-IDS enables adaptive decision-making in real-time scenarios, and FL-IDS ensures scalable deployment while preserving privacy. Comprehensive evaluations across multiple runs demonstrate that ATQ-IDS not only achieves exceptionally high accuracy (99.84%) and low false negative rates but also maintains consistent performance with minimal variance. The ablation study highlights the independent contribution of each module, and the measured inference speed affirms the model’s readiness for deployment in real-time SDN systems. While the system’s components are modular and optimized, future work will focus on reducing energy consumption in edge deployments and evaluating performance under adversarial attacks. Overall, ATQ-IDS represents a step forward in adaptive, distributed, and robust intrusion detection for next-generation network infrastructures.

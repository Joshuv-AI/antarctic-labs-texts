Impaired Atrial Natriuretic Peptide Secretion as a Marker of Advanced Atrial Fibrillation
Authors: Satoshi Oka, Koji Miyamoto, Chisa Asahina, Toshihiro Nakamura, Akinori Wakamiya, Nobuhiko Ueda, Kenzaburo Nakajima, Tsukasa Kamakura, Mitsuru Wada, Kohei Ishibashi, Yuko Inoue, Takeshi Aiba, Kengo Kusano
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSignificant atrial low-voltage zones (LVZs), indicative of advanced atrial fibrillation (AF), are associated with atrial arrhythmia (AA) recurrence following catheter ablation. Although preoperative prediction remains challenging, a low plasma atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP) level relative to B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) reflects atrial fatigue with impaired ANP secretion and may indicate advanced atrial remodeling and LVZs.Methods and ResultsWe retrospectively evaluated 166 consecutive patients with persistent AF who underwent initial catheter ablation using a 3-dimensional mapping system. The optimal ANP/BNP ratio cut-off for predicting LVZ presence was determined using receiver operating characteristic curve analysis. The primary outcome was AA recurrence. An ANP/BNP ratio of 0.7 was optimal for predicting LVZ presence (area under the curve 0.76; sensitivity 81%; specificity 60%). Patients with an ANP/BNP ratio ≤0.7 (n=91) had a significantly higher prevalence of LVZs (52% vs. 15%; P<0.001) and higher AA recurrence risk following initial pulmonary vein isolation (log-rank P=0.025; hazard ratio 1.85; 95% confidence interval 1.09–3.14; median follow-up period 583 days).ConclusionsSerum ANP/BNP ratio is a useful surrogate biomarker for predicting advanced atrial remodeling with significant LVZs and AA recurrence following catheter ablation. ANP secretion assessment may help in candidate selection among patients with persistent AF who can benefit from catheter ablation.

Central Figure

Electrical pulmonary vein (PV) isolation remains the cornerstone of atrial fibrillation (AF) ablation treatment, as PV arrhythmogenic foci are frequently responsible for AF initiation.1 However, the presence of significant atrial low-voltage zones (LVZs), indicative of advanced atrial remodeling and arrhythmogenic degeneration, has been associated with local conduction delays2 and non-PV triggers emergence.3 These factors contribute to atrial arrhythmia (AA) recurrence even after PV isolation.4–6 Therefore, preprocedural LVZ identification may be useful in determining which patients are likely to benefit from catheter ablation and in developing an appropriate ablation approach; however, it remains challenging.

Plasma levels of atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP) and B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP), primarily secreted from the atria and ventricles, respectively, are upregulated in response to cardiac wall stress during AF episodes.7 Their elevated levels generally reflect increased left atrial and ventricular end-diastolic pressures. However, in patients with advanced persistent AF, atrial remodeling leads to impaired atrial ANP secretion, resulting in disproportionately low plasma ANP relative to BNP levels.8 This impaired atrial ability to secrete ANP may represent a state of ‘atrial fatigue’, wherein sustained hemodynamic stress leads to progressive loss of atrial endocrine, electrical, and contractile function.7–11 The ANP/BNP ratio may serve as a biochemical surrogate marker of atrial fatigue, reflecting irreversible remodeling that underlies LVZ formation. However, the relationship between left atrial voltage reduction and impaired ANP secretion remains unclear.

We aimed to investigate whether the preprocedural serum ANP/BNP ratio can predict atrial LVZ presence and subsequent AA recurrence following initial catheter ablation including PV isolation for persistent AF.

Methods

Patients

We initially identified 1,324 patients who underwent catheter ablation for AF between March 2021 and June 2023 at the National Cerebral and Cardiovascular Center in Japan. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) paroxysmal AF (n=715); (2) ablation procedures other than radiofrequency ablation or procedures without a complete electroanatomical map obtained before ablation (n=246); (3) repeat ablation procedures or a history of the Cox-Maze procedure (n=147); (4) use of an angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitor (n=8); (5) absence of simultaneous ANP and BNP measurements before ablation (n=3); and (6) follow-up period of <90 days (n=3). After these exclusions, 202 patients with persistent AF lasting ≥7 days were included and retrospectively evaluated.

Data Collection and Patient Classification Based on Cardiac Rhythm

Data on age, sex, height, body weight, blood samples, electrocardiograms, and comorbidities were collected on admission for the ablation procedure. Serum ANP and BNP levels were measured simultaneously before ablation. As plasma levels of ANP and BNP are influenced by cardiac rhythm,7 patients were categorized according to the admission electrocardiogram obtained immediately before blood sampling into those with AF rhythm (main study n=166) and those with sinus rhythm (substudy n=36).

Electroanatomical Mapping and Ablation

All patients were sedated with propofol and dexmedetomidine throughout the ipsilateral circumferential PV isolation (and additional procedures). Electrophysiological study was performed using the EnSiteTM Precision and X (Abbott, Abbott Park, IL, USA) or CARTOTM 3 (Biosense Webster, Raritan, NJ, USA) 3-dimensional electroanatomical mapping system. Left atrial electroanatomical mapping was performed using a multipolar Advisor HD GridTM (Abbott), PentaRayTM, or OctaRayTM (Biosense Webster) catheter. High-density bipolar voltage maps were obtained to assess LVZs during atrial pacing from the right atrium at a cycle length of 600 ms. If AF persisted, electrical cardioversion was performed before the mapping procedure. Intracardiac echocardiography was used to confirm catheter contact with the atrial wall. The presence of significant LVZs was defined as an area ≥3.0 cm2 with bipolar peak-to-peak voltage amplitudes <0.5 mV.12 Their localization was also assessed according to the 6 left atrial divisions outside the PV:13 anterior wall, septum, posterior wall, roof, inferior wall, and lateral wall (excluding the left atrial appendage). If an LVZ was evident in the septal area, 3.0 cm2 around the transseptal puncture was subtracted from the area to avoid misclassifying the membranous part of the septum as an LVZ. Ablation procedures are detailed in the Supplementary Files.

Electrocardiographic Analysis

In the main study, the amplitude of fibrillatory waves (F-waves), a marker of atrial remodeling,14 was measured with computer-assisted calipers in lead V1 at a sweep speed of 25 mm/s. Ten discrete F-waves were selected during periods of slow ventricular rate to avoid superimposition of the QRST complex and were measured manually from peak to trough. The mean F-wave amplitude was calculated.15

Primary and Secondary Outcomes

The primary outcome was the diagnostic performance of ANP/BNP ratio for predicting left atrial LVZ presence and any AA recurrences within 3 years after catheter ablation lasting ≥30 s, as recorded by electrocardiograms or cardiac implantable electronic devices, beyond the 90-day blanking period after the ablation procedure. AA recurrences within 2 years after repeat ablation for AF were assessed as a secondary outcome.

Ethics

This retrospective study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Ethics Committee of the National Cerebral and Cardiovascular Center (M26-148). All patients provided written informed consent before undergoing ablation.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables were assessed for normality using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Normally distributed variables are expressed as mean±standard deviation, while non-normally distributed variables are presented as median with interquartile range (IQR). Student’s t-test or Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare differences between groups, where appropriate. Categorical variables were compared using Fisher’s exact test. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to determine the cut-off values for ANP levels, the ANP/BNP ratio, and the left atrial volume index (LAVI) for predicting LVZs based on sensitivity and specificity. The correlation between the ANP/BNP ratio and the extent of LVZ was assessed using a Pearson’s test. Kaplan-Meier curves were generated to compare primary and secondary outcomes in the main study group. This group was further categorized based on the identified optimal cut-off value of the ANP/BNP ratio, and comparisons were made using the log-rank test. Univariate and stepwise multivariate analyses were performed using logistic regression and Cox proportional hazards models to identify predictors and estimate odds and hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Variables with P<0.10 in the univariate analysis were included in the multivariable models.

All statistical tests were 2-sided, with P<0.05 considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics (version 29; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and R statistical software (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

Baseline Characteristics and LVZ Assessment

All 166 patients in the main study cohort (mean age 66±10 years; 59 women; mean ANP/BNP ratio 0.9±0.8; Table 1) underwent bipolar voltage mapping prior to ablation. Left atrial LVZs were identified in 58 (35%) patients. Of these, 14 of 46 patients were evaluated using the EnSiteTM Precision and X mapping systems, and 44 of 120 patients were evaluated using the CARTOTM 3 mapping system. The mean LVZ size was 27.7±21.2 cm2. The distribution of LVZs is presented in Table 2. The anterior wall of the left atrium was the most common location for LVZs, with 53 (91%) of 58 patients exhibiting LVZs in this region, with a mean area of 10.2±7.2 cm2. The ANP/BNP ratio was negatively correlated with the LVZ size (r=−0.3; P<0.001; Supplementary Figure). ROC curve analysis determined an ANP/BNP ratio of 0.7 as the optimal cut-off for predicting LVZ presence (area under the curve [AUC] 0.76; 95% CI 0.68–0.83; sensitivity 81%; specificity 60%; positive predictive value 52%; negative predictive value 85%; Figure 1A). The diagnostic performance of the ANP/BNP ratio was significantly superior to that of ANP alone (optimal cut-off value 117 pg/mL; AUC 0.51; 95% CI 0.42–0.61; sensitivity 60%; specificity 51%; P<0.001) and LAVI (optimal cut-off value 51 mL/m2; AUC 0.56; 95% CI 0.47–0.65; sensitivity 67%; specificity 53%; P=0.001). Multivariate analysis identified an ANP/BNP ratio ≤0.7 (odds ratio [OR] 3.97; 95% CI 1.74–9.06; P=0.001), age ≥75 years (OR 3.99; 95% CI 1.74–9.12; P=0.001), and female sex (OR 3.21; 95% CI 1.48–6.93; P=0.003) as independent predictors of LVZ presence (Table 3). Based on these findings, an ANP/BNP ratio ≤0.7 was defined as a state of atrial fatigue. Patients were categorized into the atrial fatigue group (ANP/BNP ratio ≤0.7; n=91) and the control group (ANP/BNP ratio >0.7; n=75). Significant differences were observed between the groups in age (70±9 vs. 62±10 years; P<0.001), female sex (44 [48%] vs. 15 [20%]; P<0.001), body mass index (22.8±4.0 vs. 25.5±3.6 kg/m2; P<0.001), and estimated glomerular filtration rate (56.5±13.0 vs. 63.1±15.1 mL/min/1.73 m2; P=0.003; Table 1). The atrial fatigue group showed a significantly higher prevalence of LVZs (47 [52%] vs. 11 [15%]; P<0.001; Table 1, Figure 2). Among the 58 patients with LVZs, the mean LVZ size was significantly larger in the atrial fatigue group than in the control group (30±22 vs. 17±12 cm2; P=0.012; Figure 2). Furthermore, the mean F-wave amplitude was significantly lower in the atrial fatigue group than in the control group (0.14±0.07 vs. 0.17±0.07 mV; P=0.027). Representative high-density left atrial voltage maps for each group are shown in Figure 2. The PVs were successfully isolated in all cases. Additional ablation was performed more frequently in the atrial fatigue group than in the control group (55 [60%] vs. 31 [41%]; P=0.019; Supplementary Table 1). Primary Outcome

Among the 166 patients, 92 (55%) and 9 (5%) were monitored for any AA recurrences within 3 years after catheter ablation using Holter electrocardiograms and cardiac implantable electrical devices, respectively. The remaining 65 patients were followed up only with periodic 12-lead electrocardiograms. During a median follow-up period of 583 days (IQR 219–916 days), 65 (39%) patients experienced AA recurrence, with 43 (47%) and 22 (29%) in the ANP deficiency and non-ANP deficiency groups, respectively. Patients with ANP deficiency had a significantly higher incidence of arrhythmia recurrence accompanied by atrial tachycardia (11 [40%] vs. 3 [14%]; P=0.004). Two sudden cardiac deaths due to heart failure were observed in each group. Kaplan-Meier analysis showed a significant difference in freedom from AA recurrence between the groups (log-rank P=0.025; Figure 3A). Multivariate analysis identified an ANP/BNP ratio ≤0.7 (HR 1.85; 95% CI 1.09–3.14; P=0.022) and long-standing AF, defined as AF lasting ≥1 year16 (HR 2.34; 95% CI 1.40–3.90; P=0.001), as independent predictors of AA recurrence (Table 4). Repeat Ablation Cases With Follow-up ANP/BNP Ratio Data and Secondary Outcomes

In total, 26 patients (6 women; mean age 67±9 years) underwent a repeat ablation procedure at a median of 280 days (IQR 156–441 days) after the first procedure. PV reconnections were observed in 14 (54%) patients and were successfully isolated using additional radiofrequency applications. Ablation treatments in the second procedure are detailed in Supplementary Table 2. During a median follow-up period of 376 days (IQR 221–527 days), 6 (23%) patients experienced AA recurrences within 2 years after the repeat ablation procedure. Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed a significant difference in freedom from AA recurrences between the ANP deficiency group (n=13) and the non-ANP deficiency group (n=13), even after the repeat ablation procedure (log-rank P=0.039; Figure 3B).

Substudy Group Analysis

Among the 36 patients with persistent AF who presented with sinus rhythm at admission (8 women; mean age 68±11 years), simultaneous measurements of ANP and BNP were performed (mean ANP/BNP ratio 1.2±0.9). Electrical cardioversion was not performed within 24 h before blood sampling in any case. High-density bipolar voltage mapping was performed in all patients in the substudy group, and left atrial LVZs were identified in 10 (28%) patients. ROC curve analysis identified an ANP/BNP ratio of 0.6 as the optimal cut-off for predicting LVZ presence (AUC 0.81; 95% CI 0.67–0.96; sensitivity 70%; specificity 81%; positive predictive value 60%; negative predictive value 85%; Figure 1B). Although not statistically significant due to the small sample size, its diagnostic performance was higher than that of ANP alone (optimal cut-off value 47 pg/mL; AUC 0.49; 95% CI 0.26–0.71; sensitivity 50%; specificity 62%; P=0.051) and LAVI (optimal cut-off value 45 mL/m2; AUC 0.64; 95% CI 0.44–0.85; sensitivity 90%; specificity 48%; P=0.098).

Discussion

Major Findings

To our knowledge, this is the first study that focused on the association between the ANP/BNP ratio and left atrial LVZ presence. We evaluated the optimal cut-off values of the ANP/BNP ratio based on cardiac rhythm at the measurement time, as plasma ANP and BNP levels are influenced by whether measurements are obtained during sinus rhythm.

The major findings were as follows: (1) optimal cut-off values of the ANP/BNP ratio for predicting LVZ presence were 0.7 and 0.6 in patients with persistent AF during AF rhythm and sinus rhythm, respectively, demonstrating high predictive value; (2) atrial fatigue, defined as an ANP/BNP ratio ≤0.7, was an independent predictor of LVZ presence and AA recurrence after the initial catheter ablation for persistent AF; and (3) patients having persistent AF with an ANP/BNP ratio ≤0.7 had a significantly higher prevalence of AA recurrence, even after repeat catheter ablation procedures.

Atrial Fatigue and LVZ Prediction

Atrial cardiomyopathy is defined as ‘any complex of structural, functional, or electrophysiological changes affecting the atria, with the potential to produce clinically relevant manifestations’.10,11 Yamaguchi et al. previously demonstrated an association between structural and electrophysiological changes in the left atrium, suggesting that left atrial voltage reduction is a diffuse process associated with histological fibrosis extent, based on endomyocardial atrial biopsy samples intraoperatively obtained from the atrial septum under intracardiac echocardiography guidance and high-density bipolar voltage maps.17 This supports the hypothesis that atrial functional remodeling progresses globally during atrial cardiomyopathy. However, preoperatively predicting LVZs is challenging.

Serum ANP and BNP levels are familiar and less invasively measured parameters reflecting cardiac function to cardiologists. A low plasma level of ANP relative to BNP level reflects an impaired atrial ability to produce ANP despite elevated wall stress, reflecting diffuse atrial remodeling.7 Generally, both ANP and BNP levels are elevated in patients with persistent AF, reflecting increased left atrial and ventricular end-diastolic pressures. However, advanced atrial remodeling leads to impaired atrial ANP secretion, resulting in disproportionately low plasma ANP levels relative to BNP.18 We define this state as ‘atrial fatigue’, one of the manifestations of atrial cardiomyopathy, wherein sustained hemodynamic stress results in progressive atrial remodeling and impaired ANP secretion. A previous study demonstrated a negative correlation between the preoperatively measured ANP/BNP ratio and the extent of left atrial fibrosis in patients who underwent the Cox-Maze procedure.8 Therefore, a low ANP/BNP ratio reflecting atrial fatigue may serve as a biochemical surrogate marker for the presence of LVZ, and in the present study, it demonstrated superior diagnostic performance for LVZ prediction compared with ANP alone.

As serum levels of ANP and BNP are sensitive to the presence or absence of sinus rhythm, the patients were evaluated according to their cardiac rhythm at the time of blood sampling. In the main study analysis (n=166), left atrial LVZs were identified in 35% of enrolled patients with persistent AF. The optimal cut-off value of the ANP/BNP ratio measured during AF rhythm for predicting LVZ presence was 0.7, and multivariable analysis revealed that an ANP/BNP ratio ≤0.7 was an independent predictor of LVZ presence. Despite the small number of the substudy group (n=36), left atrial LVZs were identified in 28%. The optimal cut-off value of the ANP/BNP ratio measured during sinus rhythm was 0.6, which was similar to that of the main study. High predictive values for LVZ presence were observed in both study groups. Therefore, patients with an ANP/BNP ratio ≤0.7 were considered to be in a state of atrial fatigue. Given that the atrial fatigue group patients had a higher prevalence of LVZ and a lower mean F-wave amplitude,19 a low ANP/BNP ratio mirrors the progression of atrial electrical remodeling. Thus, assessment of the serum ANP/BNP ratio may be useful as a screening tool for the preoperative prediction of advanced electrical remodeling with high sensitivity and negative predictive value.

Atrial Fatigue and Atrial Tachyarrhythmia Recurrence

Patients with an ANP/BNP ratio ≤0.7 had a significantly higher risk of AA recurrence after the first catheter ablation for persistent AF than those with an ANP/BNP ratio >0.7. Although intergroup differences existed in age, female sex, body mass index, and estimated glomerular filtration rate, these variables were not significantly associated with AA recurrence in the Cox regression analysis. An ANP/BNP ratio ≤0.7 and long-standing persistent AF were observed as independent predictors of AA recurrence after the first catheter ablation for persistent AF. An ANP/BNP ratio below 0.44 and 0.41 has been reported as a significant risk factor for AF recurrence after the electrical cardioversion in 71 patients,7 and the Cox-Maze procedure in 23 patients,8 respectively.

Furthermore, 47 (52%) of 91 patients in the atrial fatigue group (ANP/BNP ≤0.7) and 11 (15%) of 75 patients in the control group (ANP/BNP >0.7) had LVZs. This intergroup difference might have contributed to the higher percentage of AA recurrence rate in the atrial fatigue group despite more frequent additional ablation for routine PV isolation. Our finding that patients with atrial fatigue had a significantly higher incidence of arrhythmia recurrence, accompanied by atrial tachycardia, suggests the involvement of LVZs in the recurrence mechanism. The STAR AF II demonstrated no reduction in AF recurrence when either linear ablation or complex fractionated electrogram-guided ablation was performed in addition to PV isolation.20 Similarly, the VOLCANO and SURPRESS AF trials reported that patients having paroxysmal and persistent AF with LVZs had poor long-term rhythm outcomes, irrespective of LVZ ablation.5,6 Additionally, atrial tachycardia frequently occurs in patients with LVZs, and LVZ ablation may exacerbate iatrogenic atrial tachycardias.5 Conversely, several recent studies have reported the benefit of additional LVZ ablation among the limited populations.21,22 Although treatment efficacy beyond PV isolation remains controversial,23 assessing LVZs is important for identification of underlying atrial cardiac remodeling and arrhythmogenicity.4

Our results suggest that atrial fatigue represents a stage of advanced atrial degeneration in which restoration of sinus rhythm becomes challenging, even with repeat ablation procedures. A previous study reported a positive correlation between the preprocedural ANP/BNP ratio and left atrial reverse remodeling after catheter ablation among 88 patients with persistent AF.24 Therefore, a low ANP/BNP ratio, reflecting advanced structural remodeling, may help predict irreversible functional changes and AA recurrence following catheter ablation.

Clinical Implications

An ANP/BNP ratio ≤0.7 measured during AF rhythm or ≤0.6 during sinus rhythm is an optimal threshold for predicting advanced AF with significant atrial LVZs. Pulsed-field ablation technology enables PV isolation even when using fluoroscopy alone.25 However, preparation of a 3-dimensional mapping system for evaluating the presence of LVZs may be desirable in patients with an ANP/BNP ratio ≤0.7. Although further investigations are needed, this ratio could help identify patients exhibiting features of atrial fatigue, in whom aggressive rhythm control strategies may be less effective. Recognizing this biochemical profile as a screening test may facilitate the selection of candidates who can benefit26 from catheter ablation therapy and help guide the therapeutic approach toward individualized care.

Study Limitations

First, as a single-center retrospective study with a small sample size, selective bias is possible. Second, different multipolar catheters were used to obtain left atrial high-density bipolar voltage maps. LVZ assessment might have been influenced by differences between systems and ablation team skill level, despite intracardiac echocardiography usage to confirm catheter contact with the atrial wall. Third, radiofrequency energy was used for catheter ablation in this study. Utilizing different energy sources, including pulsed-field ablation, and advancements in ablation technology or antiarrhythmic drug therapy, may impact the interpretation of prognostic evaluations. Fourth, the ANP/BNP ratio can be influenced by several factors. Angiotensin receptor–neprilysin inhibitors suppress enzyme-mediated degradation of ANP, potentially obscuring impaired ANP secretion. Thus, we excluded patients treated with angiotensin receptor–neprilysin inhibitors. The potential effects of reduced cardiac wall stress and reverse remodeling induced by other heart failure medications on serum ANP/BNP levels could not be excluded. Moreover, in cases of severe obesity, impaired ANP secretion can be underestimated due to reduced BNP levels.27 Conversely, in patients with left ventricular hypertrophy and concentric remodeling, or pulmonary hypertension accompanied by isolated right ventricular overload, serum BNP levels may be elevated independently of left ventricular wall stress, potentially leading to an overestimation of impaired ANP secretion.28,29 Furthermore, potential natriuretic peptide deficiency due to genetic variation has been hypothesized.30 Last, because this study included only patients with persistent AF, the findings may not be generalizable to patients with paroxysmal AF. Further multicenter prospective studies with larger sample sizes should be conducted to enhance generalizability.

Conclusions

A low serum ANP/BNP ratio reflecting atrial fatigue was associated with advanced electrical remodeling and difficulty in maintaining sinus rhythm following catheter ablation procedures in patients with persistent AF. Therefore, assessment of impaired ANP secretion may be useful in identifying candidates who can benefit from catheter ablation therapy.

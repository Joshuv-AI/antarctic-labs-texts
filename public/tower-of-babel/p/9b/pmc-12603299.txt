ATF6 and pS6K as prognostic biomarkers in newly diagnosed diffuse large B-cell lymphoma
Authors: Shuang Su, Lili Wu, Chen Huang, Lianjing Wang, Weijing Li, Wei Liu, Lihong Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aimed to investigate the clinical and prognostic significance of activating transcription factor 6 (ATF6) and phosphorylated S6 kinase (pS6K) expression in newly diagnosed diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL). We conducted a retrospective analysis of 107 DLBCL patients treated with the R-CHOP regimen at the Department of Hematology, Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University, between January 2019 and December 2022. Immunohistochemical staining was performed to evaluate ATF6 and pS6K expression levels in tumor tissues, followed by correlation analyses with clinical features and survival outcomes. Elevated ATF6 and pS6K expression levels were significantly correlated with advanced Ann Arbor stage (III-IV), presence of B symptoms, high International Prognostic Index (IPI) scores (≥ 2), extranodal involvement (> 1 site), peripheral blood lymphopenia (< 1.1 × 109/L), and increased BCL-6 positivity. Survival curve analysis revealed that the overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) rates in the high ATF6 (OS, 56.20% vs. 84.40%, P < 0.001; PFS, 41.10% vs. 77.00%, P < 0.001) and pS6K (OS, 52.30% vs. 79.60%, P < 0.001; PFS, 51.80% vs. 64.00%, P = 0.003) expression groups were significantly lower than those in the low expression groups. Multivariate analysis additionally confirmed that B symptoms (HR = 2.819, 95% CI: 1.131–7.027, P = 0.026), IPI scores of 2–3 (HR = 4.906, 95% CI: 1.501–16.042, P = 0.009), and high ATF6 expression (HR = 5.699, 95% CI: 1.223–26.552, P = 0.027) were risk factors for overall survival (OS), whereas the non-germinal center B cell (non-GCB) subtype (HR = 3.245, 95% CI: 1.080–9.748, P = 0.036), IPI scores of 2–3 (HR = 2.776, 95% CI: 1.156–6.662, P = 0.022), and high ATF6 expression (HR = 4.085, 95% CI: 1.418–11.768, P = 0.009) were identified as risk factors for progression-free survival (PFS). ATF6 and pS6K overexpression were strongly associated with aggressive clinicopathological features and closely associated with poor OS and PFS. These findings suggest that ATF6 and pS6K may serve as prognostic biomarkers, highlighting a potential avenue for risk stratification in newly diagnosed DLBCL patients.Keywords: Diffuse large B cell lymphoma, ATF6, pS6K, Immunohistochemical, PrognosisSubject terms: Cancer, Oncology

Introduction

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is the most prevalent subtype of non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma (NHL) in adults globally, representing approximately 25–50% of all NHL cases1. DLBCL is characterized by high aggressiveness and heterogeneity. Although first-line treatment options have significantly enhanced the efficacy of antitumor therapies, some patients remain resistant to chemotherapy, resulting in rapid disease progression and poor prognosis2–4. In recent years, advances in precision medicine, including specific molecular discoveries, the development of targeted therapies, and immunotherapy, have introduced new treatments for improving the treatment of DLBCL.

Activating transcription factor 6 (ATF6) is a key signaling molecule involved in the unfolded protein response (UPR), facilitating tumor cell adaptation to endoplasmic reticulum stress (ERS) and promoting tumor growth and survival5. Several studies have indicated that ATF6 encourages the development and proliferation of hepatocellular carcinoma cells by upregulating the CTH gene and facilitating the nuclear translocation of Nrf2 to activate the mTORC1 pathway. Additionally, ATF6 has been shown to activate the Rheb and mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) signaling pathways, promoting the survival of dormant human squamous carcinoma cells (D-HEp3 cells) and facilitating adaptation to chemotherapy and the in vivo microenvironment6. Furthermore, researchers have demonstrated that in human lymphoblastoid cells (TK6 cells), ATF6 regulates autophagy and apoptosis via the mTOR pathway7. Ribosomal protein S6 kinase (S6K) functions as a downstream effector of mTOR, and its phosphorylation status reflects the activation of the mTOR pathway8,9.

Currently, the roles and significance of ATF6 and pS6K in DLBCL have not been extensively studied. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the expression and clinical significance of ATF6 and pS6K in patients with newly diagnosed DLBCL.

Methods

Case data

Clinical and pathological data were obtained from 107 patients diagnosed with diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) who presented to the Department of Hematology at the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University and underwent four or more cycles of the standard R-CHOP regimen (Rituximab, Cyclophosphamide, Doxorubicin, Vincristine, and Prednisone) from January 2019 to December 2022. Patients with CNS DLBCL were excluded. Clinical data encompassed gender, age, primary tumor site, Ann Arbor stage, presence of B symptoms, International Prognostic Index (IPI) score, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) score, peripheral blood lymphocyte count, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) level, and β2-microglobulin levels. Pathological data included Hans classification, BCL-2, BCL-6, Ki-67, and formalin-fixed paraffin embedded (FFPE) of tumor specimens.

Diagnosis and efficacy evaluation criteria

The diagnosis of all patients was consistent with the 2016 revised fourth edition of the World Health Organization (WHO) classification criteria for lymphoid tissue tumors, and the efficacy assessment was performed according to the 2014 edition of the Lugano classification criteria. Patients’ efficacy was evaluated after four cycles of treatment, categorizing responses as complete remission (CR), partial remission (PR), stable disease (SD), or progressive disease (PD).

Immunohistochemical staining for ATF6, pS6K expression

All research was performed in accordance with relevant regulations. Each human sample was obtained in strict compliance to the Declaration of Helsinki. This study was approved by the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University and the Ethics Committee (Approval No. 2024KS191). The need for informed consent to participate was waived by the Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University and the Ethics Committee. Pathological wax blocks from 107 patients were subjected to immunohistochemical staining using antibodies against ATF6 (ab203119, Abcam) and pS6K (ab2571, Abcam). The stained samples were independently evaluated in a double-blind manner by two experienced pathologists employing semi-quantitative analysis to assign scores based on the percentage of positive cells and the staining intensity observed under the microscope. Five representative 400 × high-power fields (HPFs) per case were analyzed in tumor-dense regions, with mean scores determining final classification. Staining intensity was classified into four levels: 0 (negative), 1 (weak), 2 (moderate), and 3 (strong). The percentage of positive tumor cells was categorized into four levels: 1 (≤ 25%), 2 (26–50%), 3 (51–75%), and 4 (> 75%). The immunoreactivity score (IRS) was calculated using the formula: IRS = staining intensity × percentage of positive cells. 0–3: Low expression, 4–12: High expression.

Follow-up

Follow-up was performed by reviewing patients’ medical records or through telephone interviews, and the last follow-up occurred on December 31, 2023. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the duration from diagnosis to the patient’s death from any cause or the last follow-up date. Progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as the duration from diagnosis to the first occurrence of disease progression, the patient’s death from any cause, or the last follow-up date.

Statistical analysis

Data were processed using SPSS version 20.0 statistical software. Intergroup comparisons were assessed using Pearson’s χ2 test. To account for multiple testing, P-values were adjusted via the Benjamini–Hochberg FDR procedure, with P < q defining statistical significance. Survival analysis was performed using the Kaplan–Meier method. Survival comparisons were evaluated using the log-rank test. Additionally, both univariate and multivariate survival analyses were conducted using the Cox regression method. Proportional hazards assumptions were verified using Schoenfeld residuals tests. For covariates showing minor violations (P < 0.05), stratified Cox models. A two-sided test was utilized, and a P-value of less than 0.05 was deemed statistically significant.

Result

General clinical characteristics

Among the 107 patients newly diagnosed with DLBCL, 51 were male and 56 were female. The median age was 62 years (range: 19–82 years), and 61 patients (57.01%) were older than 60 years. According to the Ann Arbor staging criteria, 18 patients were classified as stage I, 26 as stage II, 27 as stage III, and 36 as stage IV. 44 patients exhibited B symptoms. The International Prognostic Index (IPI) score was used for risk stratification, classifying 29 patients as 0–1 (low risk), 23 as 2 (intermediate-low risk), 28 as 3 (intermediate-high risk), and 27 as 4–5 (high risk). According to Hans immunophenotyping, 27 of the 107 patients were classified as having germinal center B cells (GCB), while 80 were classified as having non-germinal center B cells (non-GCB). 61 patients tested positive for C-MYC, 87 exhibited high BCL-2 expression, and 89 exhibited high BCL-6 expression. Patients clinicopathological characteristics are summarized in Table 1. All 107 patients received standard R-CHOP chemotherapy, and their conditions were evaluated after four cycles of treatment. Complete response (CR) was observed in 64 patients, partial response (PR) in 25 patients, and stable disease (SD) along with progressive disease (PD) in 18 patients. The objective response rate (ORR) was 83.18%.

ATF6 and pS6K immunohistochemical staining results

The expression levels of ATF6 and pS6K were evaluated in all DLBCL patients. Figure 1 illustrates high and low expression of ATF6 (Fig. 1A-B) and pS6K (Fig. 1C-D) in DLBCL samples. The high expression rate of ATF6 among DLBCL patients was 69.16% (74/107), while pS6K exhibited high expression in 56.07% (60/107) of patients. Among the group with high ATF6 expression, pS6K was highly expressed in 75.68% (56/74) of cases, whereas in the group with low ATF6 expression, it was observed in 12.12% (4/33) of cases, indicating statistically significant differences (P < 0.001, q = 0.002).

Correlation between ATF6 and pS6K expression and patients’ clinical characteristics

In 107 patients with diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), the relationships between ATF6 expression and clinical characteristics, pathological features, and prognosis were analyzed following Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) multiple testing correction. The results demonstrated that ATF6 positivity was significantly higher in patients with advanced Ann Arbor stage (P = 0.004, q = 0.008), presence of B symptoms (P = 0.005, q = 0.011), elevated International Prognostic Index (IPI) score (P = 0.004, q = 0.008), extranodal involvement (P = 0.006, q = 0.012), and BCL-6 positivity (P = 0.013, q = 0.015). pS6K positivity was markedly increased in patients presenting with B symptoms (P = 0.004, q = 0.008), absolute peripheral blood lymphocyte count < 1.1 × 10^9/L (P = 0.001, q = 0.003), and BCL-6 positivity (P = 0.002, q = 0.005).

No significant associations were observed between ATF6/pS6K expression and other factors including age, gender, Hans classification, ECOG performance status, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) levels, β2-microglobulin, Ki-67 index, c-MYC, or BCL-2 status.

Treatment response was evaluated after 4 cycles of therapy, revealing that patients in the pS6K high-expression group had significantly poorer prognosis compared to the low-expression group (P = 0.011, q = 0.014; Table 1).

Effects of ATF6 and pS6K expression on the prognosis of DLBCL patients

The median follow-up time was 40.93 months (95% CI: 35.65–46.21) using the reverse Kaplan–Meier method. Among survivors (n = 75), the median follow-up was 37.73 months (range: 10.12–60.30). Based on ATF6 expression, a total of 107 patients were classified into a high-expression group (n = 74) and a low-expression group (n = 33), with the 5-year overall survival (OS) rates for these groups being 56.20% and 84.40% (P < 0.001), respectively (Fig. 2A). Additionally, the 5-year progression-free survival (PFS) rates for these groups were 41.10% and 77.00% (P < 0.001), respectively (Fig. 2B). Based on pS6K expression, patients were classified into a high-expression group (n = 60) and a low-expression group (n = 47), with the 5-year overall survival (OS) rates for these groups being 52.30% and 79.60% (P < 0.001) (Fig. 2C). Furthermore, the 5-year progression-free survival (PFS) rates for these groups were 51.80% and 64.00% (P = 0.003), respectively (Fig. 2D).

The effects of ATF6 and pS6K expression on progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) in DLBCL patients. (A, B): Comparison of OS and PFS of DLBCL patients in different ATF6 expression groups. (C, D): Comparison of OS and PFS of DLBCL patients in different pS6K expression groups.

Analysis of prognostic factors

Univariate Cox regression analysis revealed that Ann Arbor staging, the presence or absence of B symptoms, varying IPI scores, absolute peripheral blood lymphocyte counts, ATF6 expression, and pS6K expression significantly impacted overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS). In contrast, Hans classification significantly influenced only PFS (P = 0.010). Sex, age, ECOG scores, the presence of extranodal involvement, and the expression levels of Ki67, c-MYC, BCL-2, and BCL-6 were not significantly associated with OS and PFS (Table 2).

Discussion

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is the most prevalent type of malignant lymphoma. Although the first-line standard treatment regimen can cure approximately 65% of patients, some individuals continue to experience poor outcomes and rapid disease progression10. Autologous hematopoietic stem cell transplantation significantly enhances overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) in patients with relapsed or refractory DLBCL; however, approximately 38% of these patients still experience relapse11. Meanwhile, the advances of CD19 chimeric antigen receptor T-cell (CAR-T) technology has offered new hope for patients; however, it faces several challenges, including prolonged preparation times, high costs, suboptimal quality of the patient’s T cells, and relapse due to antigen escape12–14. Therefore, identifying novel clinical prognostic indicators for DLBCL is particularly important.

In recent years, the role of ATF6 in the progression of malignant diseases has been extensively studied. ATF6 enhances the protein folding capacity, alleviates ERS, and promotes the proliferation and invasion of tumor cells. Increased expression levels of ATF6 have been observed in numerous tumor tissues compared to normal tissues. Analysis of clinical data indicates that ATF6 expression is significantly elevated in various malignant tumors, including colorectal cancer15, oral squamous cell carcinoma16,17, and gastric cancer18. Furthermore, ATF6 overexpression is associated with poor patient prognosis. In the present study, high ATF6 expression was associated with poorer overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS), consistent with findings from other tumors.

In this study, the expression levels of ATF6 and pS6K in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) were strongly correlated with several clinical indicators of poor prognosis. High expression levels of ATF6 were more prevalent in patients with advanced Ann Arbor staging, the presence of B symptoms, high International Prognostic Index (IPI) scores, extranodal involvement, and BCL-6 positivity (P < 0.05). pS6K expression levels were higher in patients with the presence of B symptoms, absolute peripheral blood lymphocyte counts < 1.1 × 10^9/L, and BCL-6 positivity (P < 0.05). Ann Arbor staging is a critical factor in lymphoma prognosis, while the International Prognostic Index (IPI) score is even more widely used to assess the prognosis of lymphoma patients19. Extranodal involvement indicates extensive tumor dissemination, whereas decreased peripheral blood lymphocyte counts may signify a compromised immune status. Therefore, ATF6 and pS6K may contribute to the malignant progression of DLBCL, and their high expression levels may predict adverse tumor biological behavior and poor treatment responses. The oncogenic role of BCL-6 in lymphoma has been established since 199320. Given that mTORC1 activation induces BCL-6 expression in B lymphocytes21 and BCL-6—as a key oncogenic driver—promotes lymphomagenesis by repressing tumor suppressors and DNA repair genes22, we propose that ATF6 upregulates BCL-6 via mTOR-pS6K activation, forming an "ATF6 → mTOR-pS6K → BCL-6" cascade. This hypothesis mechanistically explains the observed triad association.

The mTOR signaling pathway regulates cell growth and metabolism and is closely associated with tumor development and progression23. Several studies have demonstrated that the overactivation of various mTOR downstream effectors, particularly S6K, contributes to the invasion and progression of multiple malignant tumors, including gastric cancer, colorectal cancer, ovarian cancer, and lymphoma 24–27. In this study, patients with high pS6K expression exhibited activation of the mTOR signaling pathway and experienced poor overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS), consistent with previous findings28. ATF6 has been shown to promote cell proliferation and survival by activating the mTOR pathway in dormant human squamous carcinoma cells, human lymphoblastoid cells, and human colon cancer cells 6,7,29. In this study, the elevated expression rate of pS6K in the ATF6 high-expression group further reflects the interactive relationship between ATF6 and pS6K.

This study’s results indicated that ATF6 and pS6K were highly expressed in patients with newly diagnosed DLBCL, with 74 (69.16%) ATF6-positive patients and 60 (56.07%) pS6K-positive patients. The survival analysis results indicated that the prognosis of patients with high expression of ATF6 and pS6K was significantly poorer compared to those with low expression, with the difference between the two groups being statistically significant. The study by Mozos et al. showed that high expression of heavy-chain binding protein (BIP, also known as GRP78) was significantly associated with reduced OS in DLBCL patients. After ERS, ATF6 rapidly induces the expression of BIP, which binds to unfolded or misfolded proteins to mitigate ERS, and the expression of BIP can indirectly reflect the activity of ATF6. The study by Mozos et al. demonstrated that elevated BIP expression was associated with poorer overall survival in patients with DLBCL, which is consistent with the findings of the present study30.

Univariate and multivariate analyses suggest that ATF6 may serve as an independent prognostic factor for DLBCL patients; further studies are necessary to explore whether ATF6 can be targeted for the treatment of DLBCL. Numerous studies have demonstrated that ATF6 inhibition can suppress tumor growth and proliferation. For instance, ATF6 knockdown in human lymphoblastoid cells inhibited the mTOR pathway and promoted cell autophagy and apoptosis7. The application of the ATF6 inhibitor ceapinA7 in colon cancer cells inhibited mTOR activation and DNA damage repair; when combined with adriamycin, it enhanced cytotoxicity against these tumor cells29. Our previous experiments confirmed that ATF6 promotes the survival of DLBCL cell lines by enhancing S6K phosphorylation while suppressing autophagy and apoptosis31.

Our sample size was relatively small, with an events per variable (EPV) ratio < 10, reflecting limitations in cohort scale. Nevertheless, the validity of variables selected in our multivariate model was rigorously confirmed through multiple approaches: multicollinearity testing, Firth penalized regression validation, and model simplification by variable reduction. These consistent findings demonstrate that the observed associations were not attributable to chance.

In summary, high expression levels of ATF6 and pS6K in newly diagnosed DLBCL impact patients’ progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) and are significantly associated with various adverse prognostic clinical characteristics. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses indicated that ATF6 may serve as an independent risk factor influencing prognosis. These findings offer new molecular markers for the prognostic assessment of DLBCL and establish a theoretical foundation for further investigation into targeted therapies against these molecules. This single-center retrospective study warrants larger cohorts to validate the incremental prognostic value of ATF6/pS6K within the IPI framework. Subsequent research will focus on: developing integrated clinical-molecular prognostic models (e.g., IPI + ATF6), and exploring therapeutic strategies targeting the ATF6/mTOR pathway (e.g., Ceapin-A7 combined with chemotherapy).

Exercise programme in patients with non-tuberculous mycobacterial pulmonary disease (NTM-Exercise): protocol of a randomised controlled trial
Authors: Minji Kim, Youngmok Park, Dong-Hyuk Park, Chiho Kim, Eun Chan Kim, Namhui Kim, Inkyung Jung, Justin Y Jeon, Young Ae Kang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionNon-tuberculous mycobacterial pulmonary disease (NTM-PD) is a chronic debilitating infection requiring prolonged multidrug therapy. Common symptoms contribute to inactivity, malnutrition and sarcopenia, and low muscle mass is associated with increased mortality. Evidence supporting exercise in NTM-PD is scarce, and no randomised controlled trial has evaluated its efficacy. This study aims to determine whether a 6-week hybrid, circuit-based exercise programme improves cardiorespiratory fitness compared with delayed intervention/usual care and to evaluate its effects on physical function, activity, pulmonary function and patient-reported outcomes.Methods and analysisThis single-centre, randomised, open-label, two-arm trial will enrol 50 adults with NTM-PD, allocating them 1:1 to immediate or delayed intervention. The intervention is a 6-week hybrid circuit comprising one supervised and six home-based sessions per week, integrating aerobic, resistance and core exercises. Exercise intensity will be moderate and progressively adjusted using maximum heart rate determined by cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET). Adherence will be monitored through daily exercise diaries and brief nutrition education and walking training will also be provided. The primary outcome is maximal oxygen uptake (VO₂max, mL/kg/min) measured by CPET. Secondary outcomes include the 6-Minute Walk Test, 30-Second Sit-to-Stand, Short Physical Performance Battery, forced vital capacity, forced expiratory volume in 1 s, bioelectrical impedance analysis, Quality of Life-Bronchiectasis, Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease Assessment Test and Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale. Assessments will occur at baseline, postintervention and at an exploratory 6-month follow-up.Ethics and disseminationThe study was approved by the Severance Hospital Institutional Review Board (IRB No. 4-2024-0147), and written informed consent will be obtained in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and scientific meetings to inform non-pharmacological management of NTM-PD.Trial registration numberNCT06488495.Keywords: Pulmonary Disease, Exercise, Physical Fitness, Randomized Controlled Trial

Introduction

Non-tuberculous mycobacteria (NTM) are environmental micro-organisms that can cause chronic pulmonary disease.1 The incidence and prevalence of NTM pulmonary disease (NTM-PD) have been rising globally, posing a significant public health concern.2 In South Korea, the prevalence of NTM disease surpassed that of tuberculosis for the first time in 2021.3 NTM-PD is a chronic, debilitating condition that requires prolonged multidrug antibiotic therapy. However, treatment outcomes remain suboptimal, with microbiological cure rates of approximately 60% for Mycobacterium avium complex pulmonary disease and 45% for Mycobacterium abscessus pulmonary disease.4,6 Consequently, improving patient-reported outcomes has been proposed as a complementary treatment goal.7 8

Patients with NTM-PD commonly experience respiratory symptoms such as chronic cough, increased sputum production and haemoptysis, along with systemic discomfort including fatigue, weight loss and general weakness.9 These symptoms contribute to an increased risk of sarcopenia, which may result from chronic inflammation, malnutrition and reduced physical activity. Previous studies have demonstrated that erector spinae muscle mass and muscle attenuation, measured using CT, are significantly associated with mortality in patients with NTM-PD.10 11 These findings suggest that muscle mass reduction may serve as a critical prognostic factor in this condition. Despite these findings, studies investigating the role of exercise in NTM-PD are scarce. To date, only three studies have explored the impact of chest physiotherapy in patients with NTM-PD; however, these were small-scale studies12 13 and focused primarily on hospitalised patients rather than outpatients.14 Furthermore, due to the limited epidemiological research on physical activity levels and muscle strength in patients with NTM-PD, it remains challenging to define evidence-based goals for exercise interventions in this population.

Evidence from related chronic respiratory diseases, such as chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and bronchiectasis—both of which frequently coexist with NTM-PD—indicates that structured exercise programmes may increase physical activity levels,15 reduce the risk of disease exacerbations16 and enhance exercise capacity and quality of life in both the short and long term.17 18 Such interventions may enhance cardiopulmonary endurance, reduce fatigue, mitigate sarcopenia and ultimately improve pulmonary function in NTM-PD. Therefore, this randomised controlled trial (RCT) will evaluate whether a structured 6-week hybrid exercise programme—comprising supervised circuit training and home-based aerobic, resistance and core-strengthening exercises—can improve cardiorespiratory fitness (CRF) in patients with NTM-PD compared with a delayed-intervention control. We hypothesise that participants receiving the exercise programme will demonstrate greater improvements in maximal oxygen uptake (VO₂max, mL/kg/min) than controls, with additional benefits to physical function, muscle mass and strength, pulmonary function, quality of life and psychological well-being.

Methods and analysis

Study design

This study is a prospective, single-centre, randomised, open-label, two-group clinical trial conducted at Yonsei University and Severance Hospital. Participants will be recruited from the outpatient pulmonology clinic and randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either the immediate or delayed exercise intervention group, stratified by disease severity using the block randomisation by disease severity (BACES score).

In the immediate exercise intervention group, participants will begin the exercise programme immediately after enrolment and will be evaluated at baseline and at the end of the programme (6 weeks). In the delayed exercise intervention group, participants will receive usual care for 6 weeks before initiating the exercise programme. They will be evaluated at baseline, after 6 weeks of usual care, and at the end of the programme (12 weeks from enrolment).

All participants will undergo a follow-up assessment 6 months after completing the exercise programme, which will be considered exploratory. A detailed study design and trial schedule are presented in figure 1.

The immediate exercise group will receive exercise intervention for 6 weeks, followed by a post-test and follow-up test (6–12 months). The delayed exercise group will receive usual care for 6 weeks, followed by a post-test, then will cross over to exercise intervention, endpoint test and follow-up test.

Participants and randomisation

Participants will be screened at the outpatient clinic by attending physicians. The diagnosis of NTM-PD will be confirmed based on the guideline.19 The inclusion criteria will be: (1) aged 40–79 years; (2) presence of respiratory symptoms (eg, cough, sputum production, dyspnoea) at the time of enrolment; (3) NTM-positive sputum culture within the past 6 months; and (4) ability to understand the purpose of the clinical study and provide voluntary written informed consent (online supplemental file 1). The exclusion criteria will be: (1) diagnosis of cardiovascular disease (eg, angina, heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, arrhythmia, history of heart valve surgery); (2) history of massive haemoptysis within the past 3 months; (3) musculoskeletal conditions limiting independent mobility; and (4) inability to read or understand the informed consent form.

At enrolment, participants will be randomly assigned to either the immediate or delayed exercise intervention group in a 1:1 ratio. To ensure balanced allocation by disease severity, participants will be stratified using the BACES score (body mass index (BMI), age, cavity, sex, erythrocyte sedimentation rate),20,22 which serves as a severity index for NTM-PD: stratum A (low severity): BACES score 0 or 1; stratum B (moderate to severe): BACES score 2–5.

A randomisation sequence was generated by the study biostatistician (IJ), who had no role in recruitment or enrolment, using Microsoft 365 Excel with varying block sizes of 3, 6 or 9. Allocation was concealed using sequentially numbered, sealed, opaque envelopes prepared by the biostatistician. After confirming eligibility and obtaining written informed consent, the clinical research coordinator opened the next envelope in sequence to determine group assignment.

Blinding

Given the nature of the intervention, blinding of participants and intervention providers is not feasible. However, statisticians and outcome assessors will remain blinded to group allocation to minimise bias and ensure objectivity in data analysis. Accordingly, the study will be conducted as a single-blind, assessor-blinded, RCT.

Sample size calculation

The sample size was estimated to detect a clinically significant improvement in exercise capacity, measured by VO₂max, from baseline to follow-up after the exercise intervention. Based on previous reports and pilot study data, a meaningful improvement in VO₂max was assumed to be 3.5 mL/kg/min,23,25 with an SD of 3.95 mL/kg/min. Using a two-sided significance level of 0.05 (α=0.05) and 80% power (β=0.20), a minimum of 20 participants per group was required. To account for an anticipated 20% dropout rate, a total of 50 participants (25 per group) was considered necessary.

Exercise programme

Exercise intervention design

The exercise intervention is designed to prevent declines in physical function, enhance CRF and physical activity levels, and mitigate sarcopenia, with the ultimate goal of improving disease prognosis in patients with NTM-PD. The intervention design will be informed by findings from a preceding 4-week feasibility pilot study in 23 patients with NTM-PD, which demonstrated high feasibility (retention 82.6%, supervised session adherence 100%, home-based adherence 73.5%) and no serious adverse events.23

Participants will be assigned to small cohorts of one to three individuals and will begin the 6-week programme on recruitment. The regimen comprises 12 exercises, including two stretching movements (eg, standing forward bend, standing chest opener) and 10 core and whole-body strength exercises (eg, pelvic tilt, bridge, plank, crunch, squat, side leg raises, calf raises, wall shoulder press, push-ups and balance exercise), as detailed in box 1. The intervention will be delivered in a circuit-training format that combines resistance and aerobic components. Each exercise will be performed for 45 s, followed by a 15 s transition to the next exercise. After completing one full circuit of the 12 exercises, participants will rest for 1–2 min before commencing the next circuit, aiming to complete a total of three circuits per session; however, the number of circuits may be reduced to two or increased up to four based on individual tolerance and baseline fitness.

Exercise delivery and progression

All supervised sessions will be led by a certified exercise specialist (MK) with clinical exercise physiology training, at a supervisor-to-participant ratio of 1:1–3. Each supervised session will include: (1) technique correction and safety monitoring, (2) review of the previous week’s home exercise diary, (3) heart rate monitoring during exercise and (4) individualised adjustment of the following week’s exercise prescription.

Exercise intensity will be progressively adjusted in three 2-week phases according to maximum heart rate (HRmax) obtained from preintervention cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET): phase 1 (weeks 1–2) at 40%–76% HRmax (RPE 9–13), phase 2 (weeks 3–4) at 64%–76% HRmax (RPE 12–13) and phase 3 (weeks 5–6) at 64%–90% HRmax (RPE 12–16). Supervised sessions were conducted once weekly for 60 min, focusing on correct exercise technique, motivation for programme adherence and review of exercise diaries. In parallel, participants will be instructed to perform daily home-based sessions comprising the same 12 exercises in three circuits. Repetitions and rest intervals will be modified individually to accommodate differences in baseline fitness and motor ability. Participants will also be encouraged to accumulate at least 150 min per week of moderate-intensity walking, consistent with current physical activity guidelines. As part of the comprehensive intervention, participants also will receive one session of nutrition education and one session of walking training during the 6-week period. All exercise activities will be documented in daily exercise diaries and reviewed weekly by the research team.

Adherence will be operationally defined as the proportion of prescribed sessions completed, documented in daily exercise diaries. The per-protocol population will require completion of at least four of six weekly supervised sessions and at least 70% of prescribed home-based sessions.

Safety monitoring and withdrawal criteria

Criteria for discontinuation or early termination include: (1) voluntary withdrawal of consent; (2) serious adverse events during exercise, such as massive haemoptysis, persistent arrhythmia or angina; or (3) new medical conditions requiring hospitalisation that precluded continuation. Early termination decisions will be based on the investigator’s clinical judgement. All serious adverse events will be reported to the principal investigator and to the Institutional Review Board (IRB) in accordance with institutional guidelines—within 7 days for life-threatening events and within 15 days for all other serious events. Participants were permitted to continue standard care for underlying comorbidities, including bronchodilator therapy, physician-prescribed antibiotics and basic airway clearance techniques. However, initiation of new pulmonary rehabilitation programmes, exercise regimens outside the study protocol or investigational drug use will be prohibited during the trial.

Data collection and outcome measures

At enrolment, baseline demographic and clinical characteristics—including age, sex, weight, height, BMI, smoking history and underlying comorbidities—will be collected by trained clinical research coordinators. Outcome assessments will be conducted at different time points according to group allocation. Participants in the immediate exercise group will be evaluated at baseline and postintervention (6 weeks). Participants in the delayed exercise group will be evaluated at baseline, preintervention (after 6 weeks of usual care), and postintervention (after 6 weeks of exercise). An exploratory follow-up assessment will be performed at 6 months for all participants.

The primary outcome is exercise capacity, measured as VO₂max using CPET. Secondary outcomes include physical function, physical activity levels, quality of life, pulmonary function and limb muscle status. Physical function will be assessed using the 6-Minute Walk Test (6MWT), the 30-Second Sit-to-Stand Test (30s STS) and the Short Physical Performance Battery (SPPB). Physical activity levels will be evaluated using the Global Physical Activity Questionnaire (GPAQ). Quality of life and psychological well-being will be measured with the Quality of Life-Bronchiectasis Questionnaire (QOL-B), the COPD Assessment Test (CAT) and the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS). Pulmonary function will be assessed by measuring forced vital capacity (FVC) and forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV₁). Limb muscle mass will be measured using bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA), and muscle strength will be evaluated using handgrip dynamometry. Statistical analysis

Three analysis populations will be defined: a Full Analysis Set (FAS), a Per-Protocol (PP) population and a Safety population. The FAS (primary analysis set) will include all randomised participants with at least one postrandomisation outcome assessment, analysed according to their randomised group (modified intention-to-treat). The PP population will be a subset of the FAS who complete at least four of six weekly supervised sessions, have postintervention measurements and no major protocol deviations. Participants who do not meet the per-protocol criteria will be excluded from the PP analysis but will remain in the FAS. The Safety population will include all participants who receive any component of the exercise intervention (supervised or home-based) at least once.

The primary analysis will use a mixed-effects model for repeated measures (MMRM) with restricted maximum likelihood estimation, which accommodates incomplete data under the missing-at-random assumption without requiring explicit imputation, as all observed data contribute to the likelihood. The MMRM will estimate the effect of the intervention on VO₂max over 6 weeks by modelling group (immediate vs delayed), time (baseline, week 6) and their interaction, with a participant-level random intercept. The primary contrast will be the between-group difference in change from baseline to week 6 (immediate group during exercise vs delayed group during usual care). Models will adjust for the baseline value of the outcome and any stratification factors used in randomisation. Effect sizes will be reported as mean differences with 95% CIs. Continuous variables will be summarised as mean±SD or median (IQR) and compared using Student’s t-test or the Mann-Whitney U test, as appropriate. Categorical variables will be summarised as n (%) and compared using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test. As sensitivity analyses, multiple imputation with m=20 imputed datasets will be performed to assess the robustness of the primary findings, and a pattern-mixture model will be conducted to explore the potential impact of data missing not at random. The proportion and pattern of missing data will be reported descriptively.

In addition, a responder analysis (exploratory) will report the proportion of participants achieving a clinically meaningful improvement of ≥1 metabolic equivalent (3.5 mL/kg/min), based on the assumed SD of 3.95 mL/kg/min from sample-size planning. Secondary outcomes (eg, 6MWT, 30s STS, SPPB, GPAQ, QOL-B, CAT, HADS, FVC, FEV₁, handgrip strength and limb muscle mass by BIA) will be analysed using the same mixed-effects framework (identity link for continuous outcomes). Binary endpoints, if any, will be analysed using generalised estimating equations with a logit link and robust SEs. Secondary endpoints will be considered exploratory; p values will be two-sided with α=0.05, and 95% CIs will be presented. Statistical analyses will be performed using R (V.4.4.1, 64-bit; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) and SPSS (V.26.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Data management and monitoring

All personally identifiable information will be removed prior to analysis, and each participant will be assigned a unique, anonymised study ID. Clinical research coordinators will oversee data capture and secure, access-controlled storage; access will be restricted to study personnel named on the Severance Hospital IRB-approved protocol. The principal investigator will periodically review data quality and protocol adherence. Weekly research meetings involving the principal investigator, co-investigators and research coordinators will be held to monitor progress and address issues. Any protocol modifications will require IRB approval before implementation. The final de-identified trial dataset will be accessible only to the principal investigator and IRB-approved co-investigators, and there are no contractual restrictions on investigators’ access to the data.

Patient and public involvement

Patients with NTM-PD were consulted during the study design phase. Two patient representatives provided feedback on the exercise programme’s feasibility, session duration and outcome measures. Their input informed modifications to the home-based exercise component and the selection of patient-reported outcome questionnaires.23 Study results will be disseminated to participants and shared with patient support groups.

Discussion

NTM-PD is a chronic, difficult-to-eradicate infection that poses substantial challenges for patients and clinicians. Given suboptimal microbiological responses and heterogeneous symptoms and trajectories, multidisciplinary non-pharmacological care alongside pharmacotherapy is increasingly emphasised. Progressive, inflammation-driven lung destruction leads to declining pulmonary function and worsening dyspnoea, particularly in advanced NTM-PD. This may extend to reduced CRF, a determinant associated with increased mortality.26 27 Prior work suggests that pulmonary rehabilitation—both perioperative and concurrent with chemotherapy—may improve physical function and quality of life in NTM-PD.13 14 However, these studies were small, single-arm and hospital-based, limiting causal inference and generalisability.

To address this evidence gap, we designed a prospective RCT to rigorously evaluate the efficacy of a 6-week, circuit-based resistance and aerobic programme delivered in a hybrid format (weekly supervised sessions plus daily home-based exercise). Prior to launching the trial, we conducted a 4-week feasibility pilot study in 23 patients: 4 (17%) discontinued, and 19 completed the programme with high adherence and no safety concerns.23 Although VO₂max did not change significantly, improvements were observed in the QOL-B vitality domain and in the 6MWT. These findings support the safety and acceptability of exercise interventions for patients with NTM-PD and informed the choice of outcomes and training dose/progression in the present trial.

A distinguishing feature of this intervention is its hybrid structure, integrating weekly hospital-based supervised sessions with daily home-based training. Prior studies in oncology and cardiovascular disease indicate that hybrid programmes can be implemented safely and can improve physical function, CRF and health-related quality of life.28,30 Despite differences in target populations and settings across these reports, they support the feasibility and rationale for adopting a hybrid exercise model in NTM-PD.

To date, evidence for the effectiveness and safety of exercise interventions in NTM-PD is scarce, and no RCT has evaluated a structured programme in this population. This study will be the first RCT to test a 6-week, circuit-based programme combining aerobic and resistance training, delivered in a hybrid format. Although conventional pulmonary rehabilitation programmes typically span 8–12 weeks with 2–3 sessions per week (16–24 total sessions), the present hybrid model delivers seven sessions per week, yielding 42 sessions over 6 weeks. Findings are expected to provide critical evidence to guide non-pharmacological management and to support the clinical integration of exercise as a therapeutic modality for NTM-PD.

Ethics and dissemination

This study was approved by the Severance Hospital Institutional Review Board (IRB No. 4-2024-0147). Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants prior to enrolment (see online supplemental file 1 for the consent form) in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and scientific meetings to inform non-pharmacological management of NTM-PD.

Safety evaluation of an effervescent curcuminoids–ascorbic acid–polysaccharide–β-cyclodextrin (CUR-A-Poly-β-CD) inclusion complex powder in healthy volunteers: A non-placebo-controlled pilot study
Authors: Chanatip Pramvichai, Wariya Mongkolwat, Siripat Aluksanasuwan, Wuttichai Jaidee, Rawiwan Charoensup, Siriwan Pimtong, Benjamaporn Chearmuangphan, Artitaya Rongjumnong, Arunothai Wanta, Atthapan Morchang, Keerakarn Somsuan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractColorectal cancer (CRC) is a prevalent and aggressive malignancy with a high mortality rate. Although chemotherapy can improve survival outcomes, it is frequently associated with undesirable side effects. Curcuminoids, the principal polyphenol derived from Curcuma longa L., have demonstrated promising potential as an alternative anticancer agent. Previous studies have shown that the effervescent curcuminoid-ascorbic acid-polysaccharide-β-cyclodextrin (CUR-A-Poly-β-CD) inclusion complex exerts anticancer effects against colorectal cancer cells, with no acute toxicity observed in animal models. In this study, the safety of the CUR-A-Poly-β-CD effervescent powder (equivalent to 500 mg of curcuminoids) was evaluated in healthy volunteers. Of the 20 individuals screened, 16 participants completed the 90-day intervention. Clinical assessments indicated stable hematological, biochemical, and electrolyte parameters throughout the study period. No signs of allergic reactions to the CUR-A-Poly-β-CD effervescent powder were observed. The adverse drug reactions (ADRs) observed in this study included somnolence and mild gastrointestinal symptoms such as transient diarrhea (18.75%) and nausea (6.25%); all were self-limiting and required no medical intervention. This non-placebo-controlled pilot study provides preliminary evidence supporting the safety of the CUR-A-Poly-β-CD effervescent powder for consumption and its considerable potential as a therapeutic agent or dietary supplement for patients with colorectal cancer.Keywords: Colorectal cancer, Curcuma longa L., Curcumin, Healthy volunteers, Safety evaluation

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is a significant global health concern and remains one of the leading causes of cancer-related deaths worldwide. By 2050, the global burden of CRC is projected to increase substantially, with an estimated 2.18 million deaths and approximately 41.7 million disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) lost [1], [2]. Current standard management of CRC typically involves surgical resection for localized disease, followed by systemic chemotherapy regimens and radiotherapy. While these therapeutic modalities have improved survival outcomes, their clinical efficacy remains constrained by limitations, including severe adverse effects, the development of drug resistance, high rates of disease recurrence, and metastasis [3], [4]. These challenges emphasize the crucial need for innovative adjuvant or complementary therapeutic strategies that can enhance treatment efficacy while minimizing toxicity and improving long-term patient outcomes.

Curcumin, the principal polyphenol derived from Curcuma longa L., has been extensively investigated for its broad anticancer properties. By inhibiting cell proliferation and inducing apoptosis, curcumin enhances cancer sensitivity to chemotherapy. It has low toxicity and serves as a promising adjunct to conventional chemotherapeutic agents, which often cause significant adverse effects [5], [6], [7]. Emerging evidence suggests that curcumin suppresses tumor progression by activating the KEAP1/NRF2/miR-34a/b/c signaling axis, thereby promoting apoptosis, inducing senescence, and inhibiting migration and invasion of CRC cells, even in the absence of functional p53 [8]. A study on breast cancer cells reported that Javanese turmeric kombucha (JTK), a fermented preparation of Curcuma xanthorrhiza, possesses superior antioxidant properties and promotes apoptosis in T47D cells [9]. An analysis of more than twenty clinical trials also revealed the anticancer activity of curcumin across various malignancies, as well as its protective effects against the adverse outcomes of chemotherapy and radiotherapy [10]. Phase I clinical study of liposomal curcumin demonstrated favorable tolerability in patients with advanced or metastatic cancers, with the maximum tolerated dose identified as 300 mg/m² over 6 h, and transient clinical benefits observed in some patients, further supporting curcumin’s safety and potential therapeutic application in oncology [11]. Together, these studies underscore curcumin’s promising role as both a therapeutic compound and an adjunctive agent in cancer care.

Despite promising biological effects, curcumin’s clinical translation is limited by its poor solubility and low bioavailability. To address these challenges, our group developed the CUR-A-Poly-β-CD formulation, which combines curcumin, ascorbic acid, polysaccharide, and β-cyclodextrin to enhance stability, solubility, and absorption. Our previous in vitro study demonstrated that CUR-A-Poly-β-CD possesses favorable physicochemical properties and is non-cytotoxic to normal colonic epithelial cells, while exhibiting cytotoxic and pro-apoptotic effects against colorectal cancer cell lines (SW-48, HT-29, and SW-480) [12]. In an acute oral toxicity study conducted in female Jcl:ICR mice following OECD Test Guideline 420, the formulation was well tolerated at doses exceeding 2000 mg/kg, with no evidence of toxicity or histopathological abnormalities [13].

Early-phase clinical studies are designed to explore pharmacodynamic and pharmacokinetic characteristics, characterize adverse event profiles, and inform preliminary risk–benefit assessments following dose escalation. Accordingly, Phase I trials typically enroll relatively small cohorts of healthy volunteers (approximately 20–100 participants) [14], [15]. In line with this framework, the present study aimed to evaluate the safety and tolerability of CUR-A-Poly-β-CD in healthy human volunteers following repeated oral administration. This study was conducted to assess hematological and biochemical parameters as well as potential adverse effects, over a 90-day of supplementation period. The findings will provide preliminary human safety evidence to support further clinical investigation of CUR-A-Poly-β-CD as a curcumin-based formulation for potential therapeutic or dietary supplement applications in colorectal cancer.

Materials and methods

Preparation of the CUR-A-Poly-β-CD effervescent powder

The CUR-A-Poly-β-CD inclusion complexes were prepared according to previously established methods [12], [13]. Briefly, curcuminoids were isolated from Curcuma longa L. rhizomes using ethanol extraction, and the curcuminoid content was determined by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC). Polysaccharides were obtained via hot water extraction under standardized conditions [16]. The inclusion complexes were prepared at a 2:1 molar ratio of β-cyclodextrin to curcuminoids, with ascorbic acid incorporated as a stabilizing agent, followed by the addition of polysaccharides. The final product was produced through vacuum drying and freeze-drying at −60 °C [17]. Homogeneous physical mixtures were obtained by simple blending of the individual components. The preparation process of the product adhered to the methodology outlined in a patent (Patent No. 2403000006). The CUR-A-Poly-β-CD effervescent powder used in this study was prepared at the Laboratory of Mae Fah Luang University Herbal Medicinal Laboratory, Medicinal Plants Innovation Center of Mae Fah Luang University, and manufactured at the Herbal Medicine Production Plant, Mae Fah Luang University (GMP/PICs certified), Chiang Rai.

Ethical compliance

This study was approved by the Human Research Ethics Committee of Mae Fah Luang University (Ethics approval no. COA: 14/2024; Protocol no. EC23174–21). The study was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines. All subjects signed an informed consent before enrolling in this study. Participant confidentiality was strictly maintained throughout the study. This study was registered in the Thai Clinical Trials Registry (TCTR20260222003).

Study design and dosage administration

This study was designed as a non-placebo-controlled pilot study; therefore, no randomization or blinding procedures were applied. Consistent with early-phase clinical studies [14], [15], a total of 20 healthy volunteers were enrolled at the School of Medicine, Mae Fah Luang University, through poster announcements for safety assessment. Participants received one single-dose pouch of the CUR-A-Poly-β-CD effervescent powder (equivalent to 500 mg curcuminoids) after a meal for 90 days (3 months), with 4 scheduled visits at the hospital for safety monitoring [18]. At baseline (Day 0), informed consent was obtained, and pre-intervention samples (10 mL blood, 50 mL urine) were collected. Participants were instructed to consume the test product daily and attended follow-up visits at Day 30, Day 60, and Day 90. At each follow-up, blood (10 mL) and urine (50 mL) samples were collected, and adverse drug reactions (ADR) were assessed via questionnaire. The study procedures are illustrated in Fig. 1.

Inclusion, exclusion, and withdrawal criteria

Inclusion criteria required healthy participants to be 18–60 years old, male or female, non-smokers, with no prior use of turmeric or curcuminoid supplements within the past 3 months, and no underlying severe medical conditions. Exclusion criteria included known hypersensitivity to curcumin or product components, pregnancy or lactation, liver disease or abnormal liver function, chronic kidney disease stage ≥ 3, severe gastrointestinal diseases, use of anticoagulant therapy, or planned surgery during the study period. Withdrawal criteria comprised allergic reactions, adverse drug reactions, or participant request, while early termination was defined as adverse gastrointestinal events (e.g., nausea, diarrhea) occurring in more than 20% of participants. All criteria were screened by a medical professional in the research (CP). Eligible volunteers provided written informed consent before enrollment.

Informed consent process

The informed consent process was performed by researchers (KS & SA), ensuring that volunteers received full information without compulsion or inappropriate incentives. No vulnerable subjects were included in this study. All ethical aspects of the study were communicated to the participants before enrollment. Potential risks to volunteers included minor discomfort from blood sampling, such as pain, bleeding, swelling, or bruising, which were minimized by having samples drawn by professional nurses (SP & BC). Volunteers could also experience side effects or allergic reactions to the test product, including nausea, vomiting, or rash. Participants were instructed to contact the study team immediately if any adverse drug reactions occurred and could access medical care at the nearest healthcare facility.

Confidentiality of volunteer data

Personal information was collected by professional nurses and kept strictly confidential. Identifiable data were replaced with coded identifiers, stored on password-protected computers, and physical documents were kept in locked cabinets.

Biochemical and hematological parameters

A case record form (CRF) was used to collect demographic and clinical data of all participants. Laboratory assessments for safety reports included liver function tests (albumin, aspartate aminotransferase (AST), and total bilirubin), renal function tests (blood urea nitrogen (BUN) and creatinine), complete blood count (CBC), serum electrolytes (Sodium (Na+) and Potassium (K⁺)), and urinalysis (specific gravity and urine pH). These parameters were selected for monitoring hematological status, hepatic and renal function, and electrolyte balance, which are commonly evaluated in early-phase clinical safety studies [18], [19], [20], [21]. All laboratory analyses were performed at the Laboratory Unit of Mae Fah Luang University Medical Center Hospital.

Causality of adverse drug reactions (ADRs)

In the event of an adverse event, the occurrence was recorded as an adverse drug reaction (ADR), and causality was assessed by a qualified medical professional (CP) using the Naranjo’s Algorithm. This standardized 10-item structured questionnaire classifies ADRs as definite (≥9), probable [5], [6], [7], [8], possible [2], [3], [4], or doubtful (≤1) based on the total score [22]. The severity of adverse events was classified as non-serious (mild, moderate, or severe) or serious adverse events, including death, life-threatening events, permanent disability or significant impairment, or events requiring hospitalization or prolongation of existing hospitalization.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Baseline demographic data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, including percentages, means, and standard deviations. Laboratory parameters were analyzed using one-way ANOVA for comparisons among four groups, and paired t-tests were applied to assess differences between pre- and post-intervention measurements. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant with a 95% confidence level.

Results and discussion

Demographic and baseline characteristics of healthy volunteers

Among 20 screened healthy volunteers, this study enrolled 16 healthy volunteers with a mean age of 38.69 ± 12.24 years. The majority of participants were female (81.25%), while male participants accounted for 18.75% of the study population. The mean height was 159.16 ± 8.22 cm, and the mean body weight was 69.33 ± 22.86 kg. The mean body mass index (BMI) was 27.22 ± 7.78 kg/m², indicating that the participants were overweight. The study population primarily consisted of overweight but otherwise healthy individuals. The exclusion of participants with metabolic disorders such as diabetes mellitus (DM) and dyslipidemia helped minimize potential confounding factors related to metabolic abnormalities. Only one subject reported hypertension, which was stable and asymptomatic before enrollment. No participants presented with chronic health conditions, including gastrointestinal disease, peptic ulcer, gastrointestinal reflux, or irritable bowel syndrome (Table 1). Overall, the demographic and baseline data support the appropriateness of the study population for evaluating the safety and tolerability of the formulation in healthy individuals.

Hematological and biochemical values compared between before and after intervention

To assess the impact of the intervention, paired t-tests were further performed to compare pre- and post-treatment values within the same individuals. After 90 days of administering the CUR-A-Poly-β-CD effervescent powder, most hematological and biochemical parameters remained stable relative to each individual's Day 0 baseline. Although statistically significant changes were detected in albumin (mean difference −0.13 g/dL; 95% CI −0.19 to −0.07; p = 0.001), sodium (mean difference −3.50 mmol/L; 95% CI −4.25 to −2.74; p < 0.001), and potassium (mean difference −0.30 mmol/L; 95% CI −0.50 to −0.09; p = 0.007), all values remained within normal clinical reference ranges and were not accompanied by clinical symptoms or evidence of organ dysfunction (Table 3). Overall, these findings suggest that administration of the CUR-A-Poly-β-CD effervescent powder over 90 days did not adversely affect hepatic, renal, hematological, or urinary function.

Based on previous knowledge, curcuminoid is well recognized for its wide spectrum of pharmacological activities, including antioxidant and antimicrobial properties. It has consistently demonstrated a favorable safety profile in both preclinical and clinical studies, with no significant toxicity reported even at oral doses up to 8 g per day. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has classified curcumin as “Generally Recognized as Safe” (GRAS). Although a few studies have reported mild adverse effects under certain experimental conditions, the overall toxicological data strongly support its safety in both humans and animals [23]. In support of these findings, Pancholi et al. demonstrated that daily supplementation with a curcumin–galactomannoside complex (CGM) at 1000 mg (approximately 380 mg curcuminoids) for 90 days in 20 healthy volunteers resulted in no adverse effects or clinically meaningful alterations in hematological, lipid, renal, or liver function parameters. Liver enzyme levels, including AST, alanine aminotransferase (ALT), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), gamma-glutamyl transferase (GGT), and bilirubin, remained within normal limits throughout the study period [18]. Likewise, a clinical trial in patients with coronary artery disease revealed that curcumin supplementation (500 mg four times daily for 8 weeks) significantly reduced serum triglycerides, low-density lipoprotein (LDL) cholesterol, and very-low-density lipoprotein (VLDL) cholesterol, suggesting beneficial effects on lipid metabolism [24]. Furthermore, in preclinical studies, curcumin administration (200–400 mg/kg body weight) decreased blood glucose and alleviated pancreatic injury in streptozotocin (STZ)-induced diabetic rats, supporting its hypoglycemic and pancreatic-protective potential [25], [26]. Consistent with these observations, the present study also showed stable biochemical and electrolyte parameters following CUR-A-Poly-β-CD administration. These findings indicate that curcumin-based formulations are generally well tolerated and metabolically safe, with additional benefits on lipid and glucose regulation.

Adverse drug reaction (ADR) observed in healthy volunteers after 90 days of administration of the CUR-A-Poly-β-CD effervescent powder

Evaluation of ADR revealed no instances of hypersensitivity or suspected allergic reactions in seven participants (44%). The ADR observed after administration of the CUR-A-Poly-β-CD effervescent powder was somnolence, which occurred in five participants (n = 5, 31.25%). Gastrointestinal symptoms included mild, transient diarrhea in three participants (n = 3, 18.75%) and nausea in one participant (n = 1, 6.25%); none of these events required medical intervention or discontinuation of the study product. No cases of insomnia, headache, vomiting, or abdominal pain were observed (Table 4 and Fig. 3).

All reported adverse events were non-serious in nature, classified as mild in severity, and did not require any medical intervention. Causality assessment using the Naranjo’s Algorithm indicated that all nine reported ADR cases were classified as doubtful (unlikely), suggesting no clear causal relationship between the study product and the reported events in this pilot study.

A novel effervescent curcumin–ascorbic acid–polysaccharide–β-cyclodextrin inclusion complex (CUR-A-Poly-β-CD) is an innovative formulation designed to overcome the poor solubility and limited bioavailability of native curcumin. The effervescent system incorporates curcumin with ascorbic acid and polysaccharide within a poly-β-CD carrier, substantially enhancing curcumin solubility, physicochemical stability, and cellular uptake [12]. Complexation with poly-β-CD forms a supramolecular structure characterized by a hydrophobic interior and hydrophilic exterior, which markedly increases apparent aqueous solubility and protects curcumin from hydrolytic and photochemical degradation, thereby improving its biological activity [27]. Mechanistically, the improved formulation enables curcumin to exert multi-target anticancer effects, including pro-apoptotic, anti-proliferative, anti-inflammatory, anti-angiogenic, and anti-metastatic activities. Early in vitro and proteomic analyses in colorectal cancer models have demonstrated enhanced anticancer efficacy of CUR-A-Poly-β-CD compared with free curcumin, while acute toxicity studies in animal models have indicated acceptable safety profiles [12], [13]

The present study evaluated the safety of the CUR-A-Poly-β-CD effervescent powder in 16 healthy volunteers over a 90-day period. The results showed no changes in liver and kidney function tests, no serious ADRs, and no clinically significant abnormalities in vital organ function. The ADRs observed in a small number of participants (somnolence, diarrhea, and nausea) were mild, transient, and self-limiting, indicating a favorable safety profile of the CUR-A-Poly-β-CD effervescent formulation. Notably, the symptoms occurred during the early phase of administration, occurred within the first day, and resolved spontaneously. In addition, the absence of hypersensitivity or allergic reactions further indicates good tolerability of both the excipients and the curcumin complex.

The adverse effects observed in this study may be associated with higher doses of the curcumin or β-cyclodextrin formulation. These findings are consistent with previous reports involving high doses (>500 mg) of curcumin-containing products, which have included diarrhea, headache, rash, and yellow stools. The CUR-A-Poly-β-CD effervescent formulation incorporates cyclodextrins as solubilizing agents to enhance the aqueous solubility and bioavailability of curcuminoids. Cyclodextrins are widely recognized pharmaceutical excipients with an established safety profile. Adverse effects associated with high oral doses of formulations containing β-cyclodextrin, such as 2-hydroxypropyl-β-cyclodextrin (HP-β-CD), in healthy volunteers have been primarily limited to mild gastrointestinal disturbances, particularly soft stools and diarrhea [28]. A clinical dose-escalation study conducted by Lao et al. investigated the safety profile and maximum tolerated dose of a standardized curcumin extract administered as a single oral dose ranging from 500 to 12,000 mg in healthy volunteers. Approximately 30% of participants experienced minor adverse events, including diarrhea, headache, rash, and yellow stools; however, these reactions were mild and did not demonstrate a clear relationship with increasing dose levels. Pharmacokinetic analysis further showed that curcumin was not detectable in serum at doses up to 8000 mg, whereas only trace amounts were observed in a few individuals receiving the highest doses of 10,000–12,000 mg, indicating limited systemic absorption even after high oral intake [29]. These findings suggest that curcumin exhibits a favorable tolerability profile across a wide dosing range. Nevertheless, the absence of a distinct dose–response pattern and the variability in detectable systemic exposure indicate that additional studies are needed. Larger clinical investigations incorporating well-defined dose-escalation strategies would be valuable to clarify dose-related tolerability, pharmacokinetic behavior, and the optimal therapeutic range of CUR-A-Poly-β-CD formulations.

From another perspective, several studies have reported no significant toxicity or serious adverse effects associated with curcumin-based therapeutics across various diseases. For instance, El-Rakabawy et al. demonstrated that 500 mg of curcumin daily in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus improved blood pressure without serious adverse events [30]. Similarly, a systematic review evaluating curcumin use in ulcerative colitis confirmed its safety, reporting no severe adverse reactions, even at doses exceeding 500 mg/day [31]. In an oncology clinical trial, a clinical trial evaluating nano-curcumin (40 mg thrice daily) in patients with metastatic colorectal cancer also found no treatment-related toxicities or hematologic abnormalities [32]. Furthermore, clinical studies have consistently confirmed that curcumin is well-tolerated and safe for human use. Treatment with curcumin suppresses tumor proliferation and may slow CRC progression. In addition, it may reduce the toxic side effects associated with chemotherapy and radiotherapy while improving patients' overall quality of life [33], [34], [35]. Regarding herb–drug interactions, curcumin acts as a chemosensitizer, enhancing the anticancer efficacy of drugs such as doxorubicin [36], paclitaxel, docetaxel [37], and 5-fluorouracil [38]. These synergistic effects are mediated through multiple mechanisms, such as the promotion of apoptosis, inhibition of P-glycoprotein-mediated drug efflux, and reversal of chemoresistance. However, curcumin may also influence the activity of metabolic enzymes, including cytochrome P450 and drug transporters, which could potentially alter the pharmacokinetics and therapeutic efficacy of certain chemotherapeutic agents [37]. The underlying mechanisms of the synergistic effects involve modulation of multiple signaling pathways, including NF-κB, p53, PI3K/Akt, and Wnt/β-catenin. Through these pathways, curcumin can inhibit cancer cell proliferation, induce apoptosis, and suppress tumor metastasis and invasion [39], [40], [41], [42].

Although the results suggest potential benefits of the CUR-A-Poly-β-CD effervescent formulation in healthy volunteers, several limitations must be acknowledged. This study was conducted as a phase I clinical investigation to evaluate the short-term safety and tolerability of the newly developed CUR-A-Poly-β-CD inclusion complex. As a first-in-human study of a novel effervescent powder formulation, the primary focus was participant safety, which was ensured through careful and continuous monitoring of hematological, biochemical, and clinical parameters over the 90-day intervention period. Although the absence of a placebo or control group limits the ability to establish causality, the findings suggest that CUR-A-Poly-β-CD was generally well tolerated by healthy volunteers over the study duration.

The relatively small sample size and short follow-up period restrict the generalizability of the results, and the lack of pharmacokinetic data prevents a full understanding of systemic exposure and bioavailability. Furthermore, the short-term nature of this study precludes conclusions regarding long-term toxicity or safety associated with prolonged or repeated use. Nevertheless, these results provide valuable early safety information and establish a basis for future studies. Larger, controlled trials involving diverse populations, longer follow-up durations, dose-escalation designs, and integrated pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic assessments will be essential to confirm long-term safety, explore potential dose-dependent effects, evaluate drug–drug interactions, and further define the clinical potential of CUR-A-Poly-β-CD, particularly in colorectal cancer settings.

Conclusions

In this non-placebo-controlled pilot study, the CUR-A-Poly-β-CD effervescent powder, equivalent to 500 mg of curcuminoids, demonstrated acceptable short-term tolerability in healthy volunteers. Among the 20 individuals screened, 16 completed the 12-week intervention without allergic reactions, serious adverse events, or clinically significant organ abnormalities. Hematological, biochemical, and electrolyte parameters remained within clinical reference ranges throughout the study. These findings provide preliminary evidence supporting the short-term safety of the formulation at the tested dose and duration, warranting further controlled studies to evaluate its therapeutic or dietary supplement potential in colorectal cancer.

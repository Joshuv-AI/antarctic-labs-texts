Deciphering the IGF2BP3-mediated control of ferroptosis: mechanistic insights and therapeutic prospects
Authors: Wenjuan Zhang, Hui Liu, Changrong Ren, Kaiqian Zhang, Shuhan Zhang, Shifan Shi, Zhiyan Li, Jian Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe rapid expansion of the oncology field has revealed new insights into cell death processes, with a particular emphasis on the role of ferroptosis. Characterized by iron dependency and the accumulation of lipid peroxides and iron within cells, ferroptosis stands out as a unique form of programmed cell demise. This in-depth analysis delves into the pivotal role of IGF2BP3, an RNA-binding protein, in the complex regulatory network of ferroptosis in cancerous cells. By exerting post-transcriptional control over genes associated with iron equilibrium, IGF2BP3 is demonstrated to manage cellular iron concentrations and reactive oxygen species (ROS) equilibrium, thus affecting the destiny of the cell. The correlation between aberrant IGF2BP3 expression and increased aggressiveness, metastatic capacity, and poor prognosis in various cancers is further clarified. The potential of IGF2BP3 as a biomarker for prognosis and a therapeutic agent to enhance cancer cells’ vulnerability to ferroptosis is also examined, heralding new strategies in cancer treatment.Keywords: IGF2BP3, Ferroptosis, Iron metabolism, Reactive oxygen species (ROS), Cancer therapeutics

Introduction

With the in-depth development of biomedical research, the complex mechanism of cell death and its potential application in tumor therapy have gradually attracted wide attention, with a particular focus on the emergent and promising phenomenon of ferroptosis. Ferroptosis, a form of cell death uniquely hinged on iron, stands out for its distinctive reliance on this metal as a central element in its execution, was first observed during a cancer cell screen in 2003 and later formally defined by Stockwell et al. [1].This process is marked by the peroxidation of lipids and the accumulation of iron ions within the cell, resulting in the dysfunction of the essential antioxidant enzyme known as glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4), which in turn triggers programmed cell death [2]. Ferroptosis sets itself apart from the typical apoptotic cell death by its unique pathological markers; it is identified by a significant escalation in the oxidation of lipids and an irregular buildup of iron within the cell. This disruption to the cell’s delicate redox balance initiates a cascade of events that culminate in the cell’s demise [3].

The physiological underpinnings of ferroptosis stem from a disruption in the equilibrium of lipid ROS generation and clearance within the cell. Specific inducers can affect the function of Glutathione Peroxidases (GPXs), diminishing the cell's free radical-scavenging potential and resulting in the accumulation of ROS, which culminates in cell death caused by oxidative stress [2].Characterized by its own set of structural traits, biochemical processes, and genetic markers, this type of cell death stands apart from apoptosis, necrosis, and autophagy. Notably, certain inhibitors, such as Fer-1 and deferoxamine mesylate have been shown to specifically suppress ferroptosis, a feature that sets it apart from traditional necrosis inhibitors [3].

Insulin-like Growth Factor 2 mRNA-binding Protein 3, with the scientific abbreviation IGF2BP3, is also recognized under different names, with IMP3 and KOC1 being among the most commonly used [4]. The gene can be found at the 7p15.3 locus on human chromosome 7 [5]. Mueller-Pillasch et al. [6] first described this gene, noting its association with pancreatic cancer due to the detection of elevated levels of a protein it encodes, which has a molecular weight of around 69 kDa [6].The precise chromosomal location of this gene was further confirmed by Monk et al. [5].

IGF2BP3, a component of RNA-binding proteins, primarily functions to maintain mRNA integrity and regulate the synthesis of proteins by engaging with mRNA sequences. A spectrum of sophisticated regulatory processes govern the function of IGF2BP3, including its influence on transcription, subsequent mRNA handling, and the post-synthesis tailoring of proteins. Factors such as transcription factors, miRNAs, and epigenetic modifications can all influence the levels at which IGF2BP3 is expressed. Characterized by its interaction with the 3' untranslated region of specific mRNAs, this RNA-binding protein displays a distinctive structure, featuring two RNA recognition motifs (RRMs) at the N-terminus and four K homology (KH) domains at the C-terminus, akin to those in heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoproteins. The KH domain is the core structure for recognizing the m6A GC sequence, which can promote the stability and translation of downstream target gene mRNAs. The RRM domain, as an auxiliary structure domain of RNA binding, can enhance the binding ability to RNA [7]. These domains enable IGF2BP3 to specifically recognize and bind to mRNAs, regulating their stability and translation efficiency, thereby achieving the regulation of cellular biological processes.

IGF2BP3, a pivotal player in cellular regulation, has garnered significant attention. Comprehensive reviews by other scholars have explored its phylogenetic origins, as well as the structure and expression patterns of its genes, mRNA, and proteins in both normal and pathological tissues [4, 8–10].This review is dedicated to elucidating the complex interplay between IGF2BP3 and the initiation of ferroptosis within cancer cells, as well as to exploring the therapeutic potential of this relationship. The distinctive reliance of ferroptosis on iron, coupled with its modifiability by small molecules, positions it as a therapeutic goldmine for an array of malignancies. Preliminary research has unveiled the promise of interventions that target ferroptosis regulators, such as ACSL4, SLC7A11 and GPX4, to discriminately trigger neoplastic cell demise while preserving healthy cellular counterparts. The discovery of compounds that can either incite or suppress ferroptosis has paved the way for a new generation of precise anticancer treatments. Furthermore, investigations into how the tumor microenvironment influences ferroptotic susceptibility have suggested the feasibility of synergistic therapeutic approaches that could exploit the microenvironment to augment the ferroptotic response in cancer cells. As our grasp of the intricate biochemistry of ferroptosis deepens, we edge closer to the clinical realization of these strategies, potentially providing innovative solutions for cancers that have proven resistant to standard therapeutic interventions.

Ferroptosis and tumor therapy: unraveling the linkages and treatment potentials

IGF2BP3: a central regulator in ferroptosis pathways

Ferroptosis, a distinctive type of cellular demise, is reliant on iron and marked by an excessive buildup of intracellular iron and ROS. As an RNA-binding protein, IGF2BP3 contributes to the modulation of gene expression after transcription, specifically for genes implicated in iron metabolism. For instance, in HCC, IGF2BP3 can modulate the stability and translational efficiency of mRNAs encoding iron transport proteins like Ferroportin-1 (FPN1) and Hepcidin [11]. By modulating the expression of these genes, IGF2BP3 can alter intracellular iron levels and ROS production, thereby affecting the cell's susceptibility to ferroptosis.

The tumor microenvironment and ferroptosis: a complex interplay

The intricate tumor environment, abundant with ROS and iron ions, fosters conditions conducive to ferroptosis. Within this milieu, the concentration of IGF2BP3 may significantly influence tumor cells' vulnerability to ferroptotic cell death. Research has consistently shown that elevated expression levels of IGF2BP3 are associated with heightened tumor aggressiveness and adverse prognosis in select cancer types [12]. Hence, modulation of IGF2BP3 expression offers a dual advantage: altering the responsiveness of cancer cells to undergo ferroptosis and paving the way for innovative therapeutic approaches against tumors.

Therapeutic targeting of IGF2BP3: strategies for cancer treatment

In light of IGF2BP3's key role in ferroptosis, researchers are exploring therapeutic strategies that target IGF2BP3. Illustratively, employing targeted molecular inhibitors or utilizing RNAi methodologies to diminish IGF2BP3 levels can augment tumor cells’ susceptibility to ferroptosis, thus facilitating their targeted elimination [13]. Additionally, inhibitors of IGF2BP3 may be used in combination with other anti-tumor drugs to improve therapeutic outcomes.

IGF2BP3: prospects as a prognostic biomarker in oncology

The expression level and activity of IGF2BP3 may be closely related to the invasiveness and prognosis of tumors. As a potential biomarker, the detection of IGF2BP3 can provide important information for the early diagnosis of tumors, assessment of therapeutic efficacy, and judgment of prognosis. For example, high expression of IGF2BP3 may indicate rapid tumor progression and poor therapeutic outcomes [14].

Clinical evidence and case studies: exploring IGF2BP3’s role in cancer therapy

Although the application of IGF2BP3 in tumor therapy is still in the research phase, some clinical studies and case analyses have shown that by regulating the expression or function of IGF2BP3, the inhibition of tumor growth and the induction of ferroptosis can be observed [15]. These studies provide preliminary evidence for the application of IGF2BP3 in tumor therapy and direction for future clinical trials and drug development.

Literature review: the scientific foundation of ferroptosis and cancer

The interaction between ferroptosis and tumor microenvironment

Ferroptosis has gained significant attention in recent years due to its unique mechanisms and potential therapeutic applications in various diseases, particularly cancer. Several studies have highlighted the crucial role of autophagy in promoting ferroptosis by degrading cellular iron storage proteins, such as Nuclear receptor coactivator 4 (NCOA4) [3, 16].Additionally, the identification of acyl-CoA synthetase long-chain family member 4 (ACSL4) as a biomarker and contributor of ferroptosis has provided further insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying this process [17, 18].Mitochondria's involvement in ferroptosis has been the subject of thorough investigation, indicating their essential function in ferroptosis triggered by cysteine deprivation, which is also potentially associated with the inhibition of tumor growth [19]. Furthermore, the CoQ oxidoreductase FSP1 has been established as a key player in conferring resistance to ferroptotic cell death, highlighting the complex interplay of various proteins in regulating cell death pathways and suggesting potential targets for therapeutic intervention [20]. The role of ferroptosis in cancer has been a particularly active area of research, with studies exploring the potential of targeting ferroptosis for cancer therapy [21, 22]. Studies have also explored the relationship between ferroptosis and other forms of cell death, such as apoptosis and regulated cell death, providing a comprehensive understanding of the mechanisms underlying these processes [23]. In addition, the link between ionizing radiation and ferroptosis has been investigated, revealing a potential strategy for enhancing tumor suppression through the induction of ferroptosis [24]. The emerging role of ferroptosis in cancer development and treatment response has opened up new avenues for therapeutic interventions, with the potential for targeting vulnerabilities in cancer cells to induce ferroptosis and inhibit tumor growth [25]. Moreover, the link between ferroptosis and inflammation has been investigated, with evidence suggesting that ferroptosis plays a significant role in inflammatory processes and that ferroptosis inhibitors may have anti-inflammatory effects in certain diseases [26]. The molecular mechanisms of ferroptosis and its implications for health have been extensively reviewed, highlighting the diverse roles of ferroptosis in cancer, neurodegeneration, tissue injury, inflammation, and infection [27]. Overall, the field of ferroptosis research has made significant progress in elucidating the mechanisms governing this form of cell death and its potential therapeutic applications. Future studies are likely to uncover additional regulators of ferroptosis and novel ways of harnessing this process for therapeutic benefit in a wide range of diseases. The next decade promises to yield further breakthroughs in our understanding of ferroptosis and its physiological functions, paving the way for innovative therapeutic strategies targeting this unique cell death modality [28, 29].

IGF2BP3 expression regulation: implications for cancer biology

IGF2BP3 has been identified as a crucial player in various types of cancer. Studies have shown that IGF2BP3 expression is associated with poor prognosis in different cancer types, including ovarian carcinoma of the clear cell subtype, pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), leukemia, Gastric cancer, glioma and breast cancer et al. Köbel et al. [30] demonstrated that IGF2BP3 expression is a marker of unfavorable prognosis in ovarian clear cell carcinoma, with a risk ratio of 2.6 for reduced disease-specific survival [30]. Similarly, Schaeffer et al. [31] found that IGF2BP3 is selectively overexpressed in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma tissues, correlating with poor survival outcomes [31]. Furthermore, Kugel et al. [32] revealed that SIRT6 suppresses pancreatic cancer by controlling Lin28b, leading to the induction of IGF2BP3 among other genes [32]. This highlights the interconnected pathways involving IGF2BP3 in cancer development. The role of IGF2BP3 in cancer progression has been further elucidated by Bell et al. [8] who discussed the IGF2BP family's involvement in various aspects of cell function, such as cell polarization, migration, and proliferation [8]. Moreover, Lederer et al. [4] emphasized the role of IGF2BP3 as an oncofetal protein in cancer development [4]. Additionally, Chen et al. [33] identified IGF2BP3 as a key player in regulating the tumor-initiating activity of stem-like cells in HCC through the IGF2BP3/AKT/mTOR pathway [33]. This highlights the importance of IGF2BP3 in driving tumor progression and maintenance. Besides, Li et al. [34] identified the stabilization of the oncogenic long intergenic non-coding RNA LINC01138 by IGF2BP1 and IGF2BP3 in HCC, contributing to malignant features and poor outcomes [34]. Palanichamy et al. [35] found that IGF2BP3 is specifically overexpressed in MLL-rearranged B-acute lymphoblastic leukemia, a subtype associated with poor prognosis and high risk of relapse [35]. This suggests that IGF2BP3 targeting could be a potential therapeutic strategy for this aggressive form of leukemia. For instance, Zhou et al. [36] demonstrated the oncogenic role of IGF2BP3 in gastric tumorigenesis and its regulation by miR-34a, further emphasizing the significance of IGF2BP3 in cancer development [36]. Also, Pan et al. [37] demonstrated that circNEIL3 promotes glioma progression by stabilizing IGF2BP3, suggesting IGF2BP3 as a potential therapeutic target in glioma [37]. In addition, Wan et al. [38] exposed the METTL3/IGF2BP3 axis as a negative regulator of breast cancer’s immune detection, attributing this effect to the heightened levels of m6A mark on PD-L1 mRNA [38]. This finding suggests a potential mechanism by which IGF2BP3 promotes immune evasion in cancer cells, further underscoring its role in tumor progression and immune response modulation. In addition to its role in cancer progression, IGF2BP3 has also been implicated in regulating cell cycle and angiogenesis in colon cancer [39]. Knockdown of IGF2BP3 was found to significantly repress cell proliferation and the percentage of cells in the S phase of the cell cycle, highlighting its importance in driving cancer cell growth and progression. Overall, these studies highlight the critical role of IGF2BP3 in cancer biology, including its association with poor prognosis, tumor initiation, progression, and immune evasion in various cancer types. Further research into the molecular mechanisms underlying IGF2BP3 function may provide valuable insights for the development of targeted therapies against IGF2BP3-driven cancers. From its fundamental physiological functions in cell growth and differentiation to its potential roles in the development of various malignant tumors, the field of research on IGF2BP3 is continuously expanding, revealing a series of new biological discoveries. For an in-depth grasp of IGF2BP3’s role in physiological functions and its significance in oncology, readers should delve into the investigative processes of IGF2BP3’s influence on tumor genesis and progression, as well as evaluate its viability as a target for clinical interventions. To this end, the reviews by Mancarella et al. [40] and Liu et al. [41] are highly recommended [40, 41].

Pioneering new frontiers in cancer therapy: the IGF2BP3-ferroptosis axis

The advent of precision medicine has ushered in an era where the molecular underpinnings of cell death pathways are not only understood but harnessed for therapeutic gain. Among the cascade of cellular demise mechanisms, ferroptosis stands out as a novel and potent target for cancer therapy. The term “ferroptosis” encapsulates an iron-dependent form of regulated cell death, which has been the subject of intense scrutiny due to its potential to selectively target cancer cells while sparing normal counterparts.

Recent studies have shed light on the molecular mechanisms underlying ferroptosis and its potential implications in various diseases, including cancer. One of the key players in the regulation of ferroptosis is ACSL4, which has been identified as an essential component for ferroptosis execution. The use of thiazolidinediones for drug-based targeting of ACSL4 has emerged as a promising intervention for reducing cellular damage in mouse models that replicate ferroptosis, hinting at the feasibility of ACSL4 inhibition as a preventative measure against ferroptosis-induced conditions [17]. Ferric citrate’s ability to powerfully initiate ferroptosis in both primary hepatocytes from mice and bone marrow-derived macrophages underscores the indispensable nature of iron metabolism to the mechanisms underlying this cell death process [42]. This underscores the critical role of iron homeostasis in the pathogenesis of ferroptosis. Mitochondria, traditionally known for their role in energy production, have been revealed to play a significant part in ferroptosis, particularly in response to cysteine deprivation [19]. The mitochondrial involvement in ferroptosis highlights the organelle's dual-edged role in both cellular metabolism and cell death pathways with potential implications for the development of therapies aimed at modulating mitochondrial function in cancer cells. Additionally, the CoQ oxidoreductase FSP1 has been identified as a potent factor conferring resistance to ferroptosis, with its expression levels correlating positively with the cell’s ability to resist cell death induced by ferroptosis in various cancer cell lines [20]. This discovery introduces a novel biomarker and potential therapeutic target for modulating ferroptosis sensitivity in cancer cells. The emerging role of ferroptosis in cancer therapeutics has sparked interest in developing ferroptosis inducers for anticancer drug design [22]. Studies have shown that targeting GPX4, a key regulator of ferroptosis defense, can result in acute depletion of pyrimidine biosynthesis intermediates and accumulation of uridine in cancer cells, unveiling a potential Achilles’ heel in cancer metabolism that can be exploited therapeutically [25]. Delving deeper into the molecular orchestrators of ferroptosis, IGF2BP3 has risen to prominence as a pivotal molecule in the regulation of this form of cell death in various cancers. Lu et al. [11] elucidates the regulatory role of the IGF2BP3-NRF2 axis in ferroptosis within HCC (Fig. 1). The research demonstrates that depleting IGF2BP3 greatly enhances ferroptosis in HCC cells, as evidenced by elevated lipid peroxidation and a significant reduction in cellular survival rates, positioning it as a promising target for innovative cancer therapies. Specifically, their experimental results demonstrate that the knockdown of IGF2BP3 led to a 40% reduction in cell survival rates when exposed to ferroptosis inducers, providing compelling evidence for the application of ferroptosis inducers in the treatment of HCC [11]. IGF2BP3 has been implicated in the suppression of ferroptosis in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) cells. Xu et al. [12] has identified IGF2BP3 as a pivotal biological target of m6A modification that significantly suppresses ferroptosis in LUAD cells. Their findings indicate that elevated expression levels of IGF2BP3 are closely associated with poorer prognoses in LUAD patients and have confirmed its central role in reducing cellular sensitivity to ferroptosis in clinical specimens (Fig. 2). The overexpression of IGF2BP3 has been found to correspond with a substantial 2.5-fold boost in the survival of cells subjected to RSL3-induced ferroptosis, highlighting a contrast to the survival rates of control cells [12]. The findings indicate that IGF2BP3 plays an innovative therapeutic target in countering tumor growth, as it has been shown to have a defensive role against ferroptosis specifically in LUAD cells, thereby presenting a potential for the development of anti-tumor strategies. Similarly, Li et al. [43] delved into the regulatory role of IGF2BP3 in LUAD, uncovering its capacity to suppress ferroptosis through the m6A-mediated stabilization of TFAP2A mRNA. This stabilization subsequently leads to the transcriptional activation of SLC7A11 and GPX4, pivotal players in ferroptosis. The study proposes that disrupting the IGF2BP3/TFAP2A/SLC7A11/GPX4 axis could enhance ferroptosis sensitivity in LUAD, offering novel therapeutic prospects [43]. This research sheds light on the epigenetic control of ferroptosis and its potential to inform future cancer therapeutics. Recent studies have underscored the therapeutic potential of targeting IGF2BP3 in colorectal cancer (CRC), with the natural compound mollugin demonstrating the ability to induce ferroptosis by inhibiting the IGF2BP3/GPX4 axis. Wang et al. [44] have shown that mollugin treatment significantly reduces GPX4 activity and increases intracellular iron levels, culminating in a 60% reduction in CRC cell viability (Fig. 3) [44]. These results suggest that targeting the IGF2BP3/GPX4 axis may present a viable and promising therapeutic approach for CRC treatment. Expanding on prior research, Jin et al. [45] revealed that LINC00942 possesses the ability to suppress ferroptosis while simultaneously triggering an immunosuppressive environment in HCC. This effect is mediated by IGF2BP3, which stabilizes the mRNA of SLC7A11, thereby influencing ferroptosis [45]. This discovery not only establishes IGF2BP3 as a key modulator of ferroptosis but also reveals its role in the complex dynamics between cell death and immune evasion strategies employed by cancer cells. Furthermore, Xu et al. [46] explored the impact of the m6A modification complex, comprising RBM15, YTHDF2, and IGF2BP3, on VEGFA mRNA stability and the subsequent promotion of angiogenesis in HCC. Their study demonstrated that these m6A regulators enhance VEGFA mRNA stability through specific m6A modifications, leading to increased VEGFA expression and the stimulation of angiogenesis in HCC [46].These findings underscore the therapeutic promise of targeting the m6A modification pathway, particularly IGF2BP3, to regulate ferroptosis and inhibit angiogenesis in HCC. This approach opens up innovative avenues for the development of cancer therapeutics, offering new hope for the treatment of hepatocellular carcinoma. Deng et al. [47] furthered our understanding of IGF2BP3’s role in cancer by demonstrating that its depletion can induce ferroptosis in glioma cells through the modulation of GPX4 expression [47]. This study emphasizes the multifaceted regulatory network of ferroptosis and the potential for targeted therapies that can disrupt this balance in cancer cells. Moreover, Qin et al. [48] conducted a comprehensive analysis of IGF2BP3’s expression and its correlation with ferroptosis-related molecules and immune checkpoints in HCC and across cancer types. Their findings revealed a complex regulatory network where IGF2BP3’s expression negatively correlates with the ferroptosis molecule NFE2L2 and positively with SLC1A5 and the immune checkpoint HAVCR2 [48].These correlations provide a deeper understanding of IGF2BP3’s role in cancer progression, immune modulation, and ferroptosis regulation.

Beyond cancer, IGF2BP3 has been implicated in conditions such as recurrent spontaneous abortion (RSA), liver fibrosis, and acute myeloid leukemia (AML). Dai et al. [49] revealed that IGF2BP3 is intricately involved in the development of RSA through the modulation of ferroptosis [49]. This discovery not only sheds light on the molecular mechanisms underlying RSA but also establishes a significant correlation between RNA-binding proteins, such as IGF2BP3, and their influence on pregnancy-related complications. These RNA-binding proteins could serve as potential therapeutic strategies for the management and prevention of RSA and associated pregnancy-related diseases. This insight into the role of IGF2BP3 underscores the importance of exploring the complex interplay between cellular RNA regulation and pregnancy outcomes, potentially leading to novel diagnostic and treatment approaches in reproductive medicine.

Li et al. [50] uncovered the pivotal role of the IGF2BP3/Notch/Jag1 signaling pathway in the regulation of hepatic stellate cell ferroptosis in the context of liver fibrosis. Through the use of IGF2BP3 knockout mouse models and multi-omics analyses, the research demonstrates that IGF2BP3 deficiency significantly reduces liver fibrosis by promoting ferroptosis and inhibiting the activation of hepatic stellate cells. This finding shed light on the potential therapeutic implications of targeting the IGF2BP3/Notch/Jag1 axis for the amelioration of liver fibrosis, suggesting that interventions disrupting this pathway could offer novel strategies for treatment [50].

Han et al. [51] provided a comprehensive analysis of the role of IGF2BP3 in AML, demonstrating that Licochalcone A (Lico A), a bioactive flavonoid, suppresses AML cell proliferation and induces ferroptosis by downregulating IGF2BP3. The research reveals that IGF2BP3 enhances the stability and expression of MDM2 mRNA, thereby promoting AML cell growth and inhibiting ferroptosis. Lico A’s ability to modulate the IGF2BP3/MDM2/p53 pathway presents a promising therapeutic strategy for AML, offering valuable insights into the mechanistic control of ferroptosis and its potential clinical applications [51].

In conclusion, the studies reviewed here underscore the critical role of IGF2BP3 in modulating ferroptosis across various cancer types. The multifaceted nature of IGF2BP3’s involvement in cancer biology—from its direct impact on ferroptosis to its influence on immune responses—positions it as a promising therapeutic target. The elucidation of the precise mechanisms by which IGF2BP3 regulates ferroptosis is essential for the development of novel anticancer strategies that can exploit this knowledge to improve patient outcomes.

Discussion

The discovery of ferroptosis, a distinctive type of programmed cell death, has greatly expanded our understanding of the complex interactions within cancer, particularly between metabolic processes and the pathways leading to cell death. The multifaceted role of IGF2BP3 in modulating ferroptosis involves complex control over iron metabolism and adaptation to the tumor microenvironment. This review synthesizes the current understanding of IGF2BP3’s regulatory function in ferroptosis across various cancer types, with a focus on its expression patterns and functional impacts in liver, lung, and colorectal cancers. In our study, we delve into the role of IGF2BP3 in the post-transcriptional regulation of genes implicated in iron homeostasis, which in turn governs intracellular iron levels and the balance of ROS, thereby influencing the susceptibility of tumor cells to ferroptosis and offering a novel perspective for cancer therapy.

The interplay between IGF2BP3 and alternative cell death mechanisms, including autophagy and apoptosis, represents an emerging field of research that offers new insights into the complex regulatory networks within cells [23]. Apoptosis is a highly regulated form of programmed cell death that plays a significant role in cancer development and treatment resistance. In some cases, cancer cells may shift from ferroptosis to apoptosis under specific stress conditions, highlighting the importance of targeting both pathways simultaneously. For example, the BCL-2 family proteins, which are key regulators of apoptosis, have been shown to modulate the sensitivity of cancer cells to ferroptosis inducers [52]. Autophagy is a cellular process for the degradation and recycling of cellular components and has been implicated in both the promotion and suppression of ferroptosis. Autophagy can either promote ferroptosis by degrading iron-storage proteins or suppress it by removing damaged organelles and reducing the accumulation of ROS [3, 16]. Necrosis, traditionally considered an uncontrolled form of cell death, can also be regulated (necroptosis) and may interact with ferroptosis. There is evidence that necroptosis signaling can influence the susceptibility of cancer cells to ferroptosis, suggesting a potential crosstalk between these death pathways [53].

This crosstalk not only reveals the interdependencies among cell death mechanisms but also paves the way for developing novel cancer therapeutic strategies. By specifically targeting these pathways, it may be possible to disrupt the balance that allows cancer cells to survive, favoring the induction of ferroptosis and opening new frontiers in cancer treatment [22]. Furthermore, this interplay across pathways could enable the design of combination therapies that leverage multiple cell death routes to overcome drug resistance and target cancer cells through various mechanisms, potentially leading to more effective cancer therapies.

Future research should delve deeper into the molecular mechanisms of IGF2BP3, encompassing its upstream regulatory factors and downstream effector molecules in ferroptosis [22]. Unraveling these mechanisms is pivotal for the development of novel cancer therapies that intervene with the expression or function of IGF2BP3. The exploration of small molecule inhibitors or RNA interference techniques to diminish IGF2BP3 expression, thereby sensitizing tumor cells to ferroptosis, presents a promising avenue for selectively targeting tumor cells for destruction [13, 14]. Moreover, the potential synergistic effects of combining IGF2BP3 inhibitors with other anti-tumor drugs warrant investigation, as such combination therapies could enhance treatment efficacy by overcoming resistance mechanisms and simultaneously targeting multiple pathways. The complex relationship between IGF2BP3 and ferroptosis offers a fresh perspective for the development of targeted cancer therapies. By understanding the molecular mechanisms employed by IGF2BP3 to regulate ferroptosis, we can devise strategies to disrupt the delicate balance that cancer cells maintain to evade cell death, offering a new frontier in cancer treatment.

IGF2BP3’s identification as a biomarker holds profound significance for the clinical management of cancer. Its potential to serve as an early diagnostic indicator, a tool for therapeutic efficacy assessment, and a prognosticator of patient outcomes is unprecedented. The development of sensitive and specific assays for detecting IGF2BP3 expression and activity could revolutionize personalized medicine approaches, allowing for tailored therapeutic interventions based on the unique molecular landscape of individual tumors. The link between elevated IGF2BP3 expression and adverse patient outcomes underscores the significance of this biomarker in predicting cancer prognosis and the importance of integrating its detection into clinical practice for better cancer treatment outcomes.

The intricate interplay between ferroptosis and the tumor microenvironment, which is rich in ROS and iron ions, presents a fertile ground for the induction of ferroptosis. Understanding how this microenvironment modulates the expression and function of IGF2BP3 is crucial for developing therapeutic strategies that capitalize on this interaction [16, 19, 20]. Harnessing the microenvironment's role in promoting ferroptosis could offer an additional layer of selectivity for cancer therapies, potentially minimizing off-target effects and enhancing patient outcomes. This targeted approach could be instrumental in the design of more efficacious treatments that exploit the unique vulnerabilities of cancer cells within their microenvironment.

Clinical research and case analyses, though in the preliminary stages, have demonstrated the potential of IGF2BP3 in tumor therapy. The modulation of IGF2BP3 expression or function has shown to inhibit tumor growth and induce ferroptosis, offering direction for future clinical trials and drug development [15]. It is essential to translate these findings from bench to bedside, ensuring that the therapeutic potential of IGF2BP3 is fully realized in the clinical setting.

Conclusion

The exploration of IGF2BP3’s role in tumor cell ferroptosis has illuminated its potential as a therapeutic target, with this review offering a synthesis of current understanding and future prospects. Our analysis underscores the multifaceted influence of IGF2BP3 on ferroptosis, a critical cell death mechanism with significant implications for cancer treatment.

Future directions

As we reflect on the compelling role of IGF2BP3 in the modulation of ferroptosis, a constellation of future research avenues emerges, each offering a piece of the puzzle in our quest to conquer cancer. The intricate interplay between IGF2BP3 and established ferroptosis inducers, such as RSL3, beckons for exploration, with the potential to uncover novel synergies that could reshape cancer cell survival dynamics.

The heterogeneity of cancer demands a tailored investigation into the variable expression of IGF2BP3 across tumor subtypes, which may elucidate differential sensitivities to ferroptosis and inform subtype-specific therapeutic strategies. Delving into the regulatory mechanisms of IGF2BP3 within the tumor microenvironment promises to unearth new targets for intervention, where microenvironmental cues could be harnessed to modulate IGF2BP3 activity and therapeutic response.

The enigmatic role of IGF2BP3 in tumor immune evasion presents a frontier for immunotherapy, with the possibility of enhancing the cancer-immunity cycle by disrupting IGF2BP3-mediated escape mechanisms. Furthermore, the nexus between IGF2BP3 and cellular metabolism, particularly iron and ROS homeostasis, warrants deeper investigation to understand its metabolic reprogramming in cancer cells.

Unraveling the complex regulatory network surrounding IGF2BP3, from its upstream regulators to downstream effectors, will be paramount in understanding the orchestration of ferroptosis. The epigenetic landscape of IGF2BP3 expression opens new horizons for therapy, where modifying the epigenetic state could correct aberrant IGF2BP3 activity.

Crosstalk between IGF2BP3 and other cell death pathways, including apoptosis and autophagy, may provide a more integrated approach to cancer treatment, highlighting the need to explore these interactions for potential therapeutic exploitation. Throughout the cancer journey, from initiation to metastasis, IGF2BP3’s role must be delineated to identify stage-specific vulnerabilities and treatments.

Lastly, the clinical relevance of IGF2BP3 expression patterns, and their correlation with patient outcomes, must be interrogated using clinical samples to bridge the gap between bench and bedside research. As we stand on the precipice of discovery, these directions will guide us in harnessing the full therapeutic potential of IGF2BP3, offering a beacon of hope for cancer patients.

In the rapidly evolving landscape of oncology, innovative therapeutic approaches are redefining the treatment paradigms, particularly with the advent of precision medicine. Targeting IGF2BP3, a protein that has emerged as a pivotal player in the complex interplay of tumor cell ferroptosis, holds immense promise for the development of next-generation cancer therapies.

The innovative therapeutic strategies targeting IGF2BP3 encompass a spectrum of novel methodologies designed to modulate its expression or function with unprecedented precision. Small molecule drugs are being developed to bind and inhibit IGF2BP3, potentially disrupting its ability to regulate ferroptosis and thus impeding the survival mechanisms of cancer cells [13].These drugs could be designed to target the specific domains of IGF2BP3 responsible for its interaction with mRNA, thereby affecting the stability and translation of key genes involved in iron metabolism and redox balance.

Moreover, the revolutionary gene-editing technology, CRISPR/Cas9, offers a powerful tool for the direct manipulation of the IGF2BP3 gene. By precisely editing the genetic code, it becomes possible to either knock out or knock down the expression of IGF2BP3, providing a potent means to assess its role in cancer and to develop gene therapy strategies [37, 41].

As we venture into the realm of personalized medicine, the integration of IGF2BP3-targeted therapies with a patient’s unique genetic and clinical profile will be paramount. The tailored approach will involve the assessment of IGF2BP3 expression levels, mutational status, and its correlation with clinical characteristics such as tumor type, stage, and patient history. This individualized data will pave the way for customized treatment plans that maximize therapeutic efficacy while minimizing adverse effects [15, 48].

Furthermore, the combination of IGF2BP3-targeted therapies with existing cancer treatments may unlock synergistic effects, potentially overcoming resistance and enhancing the overall treatment response. This integrative approach could involve concurrent administration with chemotherapy, radiation therapy, or immunotherapy, each complementing the other to create a more comprehensive attack on cancer cells [22, 29].

In brief, the innovative therapeutic approaches targeting IGF2BP3 are poised to revolutionize cancer treatment by offering a highly specific and personalized strategy. As our understanding of the molecular intricacies of IGF2BP3 deepens, these therapies are set to become a cornerstone of future oncological practice, heralding a new era of precision cancer medicine.

Monitoring treatment response and adjusting strategies accordingly will be crucial for maximizing therapeutic benefits.

Bioinformaticians, with their expertise in analyzing gene expression data, can uncover the regulatory networks of IGF2BP3 in ferroptosis, thereby identifying potential drug targets. Medicinal chemists can then leverage this information to design small molecule modulators that either enhance or inhibit the function of IGF2BP3, modulating the susceptibility of tumor cells to ferroptosis.

Pathologists contribute to this collaborative effort by examining the expression patterns of IGF2BP3 in diverse cancer samples, thereby substantiating the clinical relevance and efficacy of these molecular modulators. Furthermore, molecular and cellular biologists delve into experimental studies to understand how IGF2BP3 influences iron metabolism and ROS balance, laying the biological groundwork for the development of novel therapeutics. Immunologists add another layer to this collaborative research by exploring how ferroptosis-induced cell death impacts the tumor microenvironment and immune response, offering new perspectives for cancer immunotherapy.

Through such interdisciplinary cooperation, we can gain a deeper understanding of IGF2BP3’s role in cancer and develop more effective treatment strategies. This integrative approach harnesses the power of diverse scientific disciplines to target a common goal: the advancement of cancer therapies that are both precise and impactful.

Translational challenges and considerations

Drug development challenges

Specificity of Therapeutics A crucial challenge in developing small molecule inhibitors or RNAi therapies is ensuring the specificity of IGF2BP3 targeting without affecting the function of other essential RNA-binding proteins. Designing drugs that selectively bind to IGF2BP3's functional domains, such as its RRMs and KH domains, while sparing similar domains in other proteins, presents a significant hurdle.

Drug Delivery Systems The development of efficient drug delivery systems for IGF2BP3 therapeutics is another critical aspect. Given the intracellular location of IGF2BP3, methods such as lipid nanoparticles or cell-penetrating peptides may be necessary to ensure effective delivery to target cells.

Off-Target Effects A potential pitfall in the therapeutic targeting of IGF2BP3 is the risk of off-target effects. Given the broad role of RNA-binding proteins in post-transcriptional regulation, nonspecific inhibition could lead to unforeseen consequences on other cellular processes, potentially increasing toxicity.

Safety and efficacy profiles

Preclinical Testing Comprehensive preclinical trials, including in vitro and in vivo studies, are essential to evaluate the safety and efficacy of IGF2BP3-targeted therapies. These studies should assess the potential for adverse effects, including the disruption of normal cell functions and the impact on physiological processes.

Clinical Trial Design Early-phase clinical trials should be meticulously designed to monitor the pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics of IGF2BP3-targeted therapies, with a focus on dosing, schedule, and biomarker responses. The trials should be powered to detect both therapeutic efficacy and potential side effects.

Regulatory considerations

Biomarker Validation The validation of IGF2BP3 as a prognostic biomarker in oncology requires rigorous testing across diverse populations to ensure its predictive value is consistent and reliable. This includes the standardization of assays for its detection and the correlation of expression levels with clinical outcomes.

Regulatory Approval Pathways Understanding the regulatory pathways for novel therapeutics and biomarkers is crucial. The translation of IGF2BP3-related therapies into clinical practice will necessitate navigating complex regulatory environments, optimizing trial designs for regulatory approval, and ensuring compliance with guidelines for biomarker uses in clinical decision-making.

Economic and health policy factors

Cost-Effectiveness Analysis The economic viability of IGF2BP3-targeted therapies must be assessed, taking into account the cost of development, manufacturing, and potential market pricing compared to existing treatment options.

Health Policy and Reimbursement Engagement with health policy makers and payers is essential to ensure that new therapies targeting IGF2BP3 are accessible to patients. This includes discussions on reimbursement strategies and the integration of biomarker testing into standard clinical pathways.

By addressing these challenges and considerations, the translation of IGF2BP3-related research into clinical practice can be more systematically approached, increasing the likelihood of successful therapeutic development and implementation.

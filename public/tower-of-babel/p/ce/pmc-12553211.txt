miR-34c-5p targets Notch2 to promote fracture healing
Authors: Kechi Li, Guoxiang Liu, Feiyu Chen, Xiaolong Sun, Bin Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo date, Despite the widespread and mature clinical application of fracture treatment techniques, a large number of patients still suffer from nonunion due to impaired bone regeneration function. In the past few years, growing evidence has shown that microRNAs (miRNAs) play important roles in many physiological processes and are involved in the regulation of bone metabolic diseases. In this study, we evaluated the role of miR-34c-5p in in vitro osteogenesis using methods including Alizarin Red S staining, ALP staining, quantitative PCR, and western blotting. Additionally, we established a mouse fracture model, utilized micro-CT to visualize callus formation at the fracture site, and observed the formation of trabecular bone at the fracture end in mice through histopathological staining, aiming to clarify the role of miR-34c-5p as a promoter of fracture healing in vivo. Findings demonstrated that miR-34c-5p was capable of effectively boosting osteogenic differentiation in vitro and remarkably facilitating fracture regeneration in vivo. Notch2 was identified as a downstream target through which miR-34c-5p effectively promotes osteogenesis. In summary, miR-34c-5p positively regulates osteoblast differentiation by targeting Notch2, providing a new direction for the treatment of various bone metabolic diseases such as poor fracture healing.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13018-025-06373-z.Keywords: miR-34c-5p, Notch2, Osteogenic differentiation, Fracture healing, Osteoblast

Introduction

Bone, as a vital organ of the organism, serves as a reservoir for extensive minerals and hematopoietic stem cells, thereby sustaining metabolic homeostasis and hematopoietic function [1]. When subjected to excessive mechanical force, the structural continuity of bone is disrupted, which results in fracture. Statistics indicate that in 2019, there were approximately 178 million new fracture cases globally, with prevalent cases reaching 445 million; among these, elderly women accounted for the largest proportion. Furthermore, the total number of fractures worldwide is projected to double by 2050 [2]. Despite the bone’s robust regenerative capacity, which enables restoration to pre-traumatic status with medical intervention, approximately 5%-10% of patients exhibit impaired fracture healing, resulting in delayed union or non-union [3–5]. Accordingly, identifying novel therapeutic targets and corresponding biotherapeutic strategies holds substantial significance for current management of fracture healing.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are a class of endogenous non-coding small RNAs, typically consisting of 22–24 nucleotides, which exert a negative regulatory effect on target gene expression by specifically binding to the 3’ untranslated region (3’ UTR) of target mRNAs, thereby inhibiting the initiation of mRNA translation or inducing mRNA cleavage and, consequently, suppressing the translation of target proteins encoded by these mRNAs [6, 7]. miRNAs have been fully demonstrated to regulate gene expression by modulating post-transcriptional levels of genes in many pathophysiological processes [8, 9], such as cell proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, and autophagy [10–12]. Numerous studies have demonstrated that miRNAs can regulate the proliferation and differentiation of osteoblasts through multiple pathways and are involved in the modulation of various bone metabolic diseases [13, 14]. For example, miR-210 alleviates postmenopausal osteoporosis linked to estrogen deficiency by upregulating Vegf expression and osteoblast differentiation [15]. miR-203 and miR-135 hinder the development of metastatic bone disease by targeting Runx2 [16]. miR-19b-3p and miR-218 can promote the transition of bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells to osteoblasts, which in turn lessens bone loss and enhances bone quality [17, 18]. Furthermore, our research group has previously explored the therapeutic application of miRNAs in bone metabolic disorders. Specifically, miR-148a knockout reduces bone mass loss and osteoclastogenesis by targeting Nrp1, thereby preventing bone resorption in both physiologically normal and estrogen-deficient mice [19]. Additionally, biodegradable microspheres loaded with miR-148a enable targeted delivery to chondrocytes, mitigating the progression of osteoarthritis [20].

The Notch family, a set of transmembrane receptor proteins with high evolutionary conservation, transmits signals via direct intercellular contact and holds a crucial position in embryonic development, cell fate decision-making, and the preservation of tissue homeostasis [21]. Consisting of Notch receptors (Notch1–4), ligands (for instance, Jagged1, Dll1), and the transcription factor CSL, the Notch signaling pathway controls the expression of target genes like Hes/Hey. This regulatory process occurs via the liberation of the Notch intracellular domain (NICD) when the pathway is activated [22]. Numerous studies have demonstrated that the Notch signaling pathway can regulate the differentiation program of osteoblasts and is crucial for the establishment of bone metabolic homeostasis [23]. During the differentiation of bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells, Notch2 reduces glucose consumption and lactate production by decreasing the activity of key glycolytic enzymes and the expression of mitochondrial complex I genes, leading to reduced ATP production, decreased Ampk activity, and superoxide generation. This, in turn, hinders their differentiation into osteoblasts, indicating that Notch2 plays a key role in regulating osteogenic differentiation [24]. Other studies have demonstrated that activation of Notch target genes Hes1 and Hey1 inhibits osteogenesis by suppressing the transactivation of the osteogenic transcription factor Runx2 in osteoblasts [25].

Currently, the regulatory role of the miR-34c-5p/Notch2 axis in osteogenic differentiation remains unclear. Studies have shown that miRNA-34c is upregulated during BMP2-induced osteogenic differentiation of C2C12 cells and directly targets Notch1, leading to age-related osteoporosis phenotypes in transgenic mice [26]. However, in osteoblasts, miR-34c can target multiple components of the Notch signaling pathway, including Notch1, Notch2, and Jag1, which is of great significance for miR-34c-5p regulation of osteoblast differentiation. Therefore, we plan to deeply explore the specific mechanisms of this pathway in osteoblast differentiation through research methods such as osteoblast mineralization assays and calcium deposition assays, so as to provide new insights for the treatment of fracture healing.

Materials and methods

Cell culture and differentiation

Primary osteoblasts were isolated from the calvaria of neonatal C57BL/6 mice (male, 4–5 days old). Briefly, mouse calvaria were dissected under sterile conditions, cut into 1 × 1 mm fragments, and subjected to gradient digestion with 0.25% trypsin (Gibco) and 2 mg/mL collagenase (C8150, Solarbio) for 4–6 h to obtain primary osteoblasts. The cell culture medium used was α-MEM medium (Gibco, Shanghai, China) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Gibco). The cells were then incubated in a 5% CO2 incubator at 37 °C. Their morphology and growth status were observed under a microscope, and the medium was changed every 2 days. When the cell density reached 70%, passaging was performed. Osteoblasts from the second passage were used in subsequent transfection experiments. An appropriate number of cells were seeded into well plates for culture. When the cell density reached 70% of the well plate area (observed under the microscope), osteogenic differentiation was initiated by adding differentiation medium (RAXMX-90021, Oricel), and half-medium changes were performed every 2 days.

Animal model establishment and treatment

All animal experimental procedures were authorized by the Ethics Committee of Wenzhou Medical University(Academic Approval No: wydw2021-0462). Male C57BL/6 mice (8 weeks old) were purchased from the Animal Center of Zhejiang Lishui University and Hangzhou Hans Bio-Technology Co., Ltd. Mice were housed 5 per group in standard-sized cages under a controlled environment with a constant room temperature (24 ± 2 ℃), relatively constant humidity (55–60%), and a light/dark cycle. Food and water were available to the animals ad libitum. On the day before surgery, the mice were fasted (withholding both food and water). Prior to the start of surgery, to reduce postoperative stress responses in the mice, 0.4% pentobarbital was administered via intraperitoneal injection at a dose of 40 mg per kilogram of body weight. After the surgery began, the hair on the back and right lower limb of the mice was shaved, and the surgical instruments were disinfected by soaking in iodophor. At the midshaft of the mouse femur, a 0.5 cm skin incision was made with ophthalmic scissors, followed by blunt dissection of the muscles to free the femoral shaft. The mid-femur was transected using a dental drill equipped with a circular blade drill bit. A round drill bit was used to penetrate the medullary cavities of the proximal and distal fracture segments, followed by inserting a 5#/25G (0.5*38 mm) dental injection needle purchased from Zhejiang Kindly Medical Supplies Co., Ltd. The needle was bent and cut at the extraosseous regions of both the distal and proximal femur, after which the skin incision was sutured. After surgery, mice will be administered ketoprofen for analgesia at a dose of 5 mg/kg via subcutaneous injection, with the administration frequency of once every 24 h for 3 injections [27]. AgomiR-34c-5p, AntagomiR-34c-5p, and AgomiR-NC were all synthesized by Genepharm (Shanghai, China). AgomiR-34c-5p and AntagomiR-34c-5p were administered to mice via tail vein injection at a concentration of 5 µg/g, respectively, while the control group received an equivalent dosage of AgomiR-NC. Injections were conducted every 3 days. The mice were allocated randomly into three groups: AgomiR-NC + fracture group, AgomiR-34c-5p + fracture group, and AntagomiR-34c-5p + fracture group, with 10 mice in each group. Fracture samples were collected at 2 and 4 weeks respectively for subsequent radiological analysis and histopathological evaluation.

Cell transfection

The miR-34c-5p mimic, inhibitor, and negative control lentiviruses used in the experiments were purchased from Genepharm (Shanghai, China), while the OE-Notch2 and its negative control lentivirus were purchased from Genechem (Shanghai, China). Cells were seeded in 24-well plates at a density of 5 × 104 cells/well and cultured overnight. When the cell density reached 60%-70%, When the cell density reached 60–70%, lentiviruses were transfected into osteoblasts at a MOI of 100. The specific virus dosage was calculated based on the virus titer of 108 TU/mL and the optimal MOI value. Meanwhile, 5 µg/ml polybrene (H8761, Solarbio) was added to improve the infection efficiency. After 16–24 h, the complete medium was replaced to maintain cell viability, and passaging and expression detection were performed after the cells reached confluency.

Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity assay

On day 7 after inducing osteogenic differentiation with osteogenic induction medium, we measured the ALP activity by detecting the absorbance at 405 nm using a multimode microplate reader (Molecular Devices) according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Beyotime).

Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) staining

On the 7th day after inducing osteogenic differentiation with osteogenic induction medium, we performed the alkaline phosphatase staining experiment. Cells were fixed with 4% tissue cell fixative for 20 min. According to the manufacturer’s protocol (Beyotime), the staining working solution was prepared to detect alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity.

Calcium nodule staining (alizarin red staining)

Alizarin Red staining was performed on day 21 after the induction of osteoblast differentiation. Cells were fixed with 4% tissue cell fixative for 20 min and then washed with PBS. According to the manufacturer’s protocol, Alizarin Red S (ARS) staining (Beyotime) was used to examine the formation of mineralized nodules. Three random fields of view per sample group were imaged using an inverted fluorescence microscope.

Immunofluorescence staining

Immunofluorescence was used to determine the levels and distribution of Collagen-I and RUNX2 in osteoblasts in vitro after different treatments. The medium in the well plate was removed, and the coverslips in the well plate were washed with PBS. Cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min and then soaked in PBS. The slides were permeabilized by soaking in 0.5% Triton X-100 (Solarbio) for 15 min. Subsequently, blocking was performed with 10% goat serum for 1 h, and the slides were incubated overnight with primary antibodies diluted in 10% goat serum in a humidified environment. The next day, the slides were incubated with fluorochrome-conjugated secondary antibodies Alexa Fluor 488(1: 1000, ab150077, abcam, Rabbit) and Alexa Fluor 594(1: 1000, ab150080, abcam, Rabbit) at room temperature in the dark for 1 h, followed by staining with anti-fluorescence quenching mounting medium (containing DAPI; S2110, Solarbio) for 1 min. Finally, three fluorescent images were randomly captured from each slide using a fluorescence inverted microscope, and the average value was calculated. The fluorescence intensity was measured using Image J software.

Luciferase reporter assay

Potential targets of miR-34c-5p were predicted using online databases TargetScan (https://www.targetscan.org/vert_80/), StarBase (https://rnasysu.com/encori/), and GeneCards (https://www.genecards.org/). We constructed luciferase reporter gene vectors containing miR-34c-5p binding sites in the Notch2-3’UTR, including the wild-type Wt-Notch2-3’-UTR and mutant Mut-Notch2-3’-UTR. Lipofectamine 2000 transfection reagent (Invitrogen) was used to co-transfect miR-34c-5p mimics or mimic controls with luciferase reporter gene vectors into HEK-293T cells. After 24–48 h, luciferase activity was detected using a luminometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) according to the kit instructions (RG027; Beyotime). Normalization to firefly luciferase activity was carried out.

Western blotting assay

On day 7 after the induction of differentiation in transfected cells, cellular proteins were collected. The cell culture medium was aspirated, and the cells were washed 2–3 times with PBS. Ice-cold RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime) containing 1% protease inhibitor (PMSF) was added to lyse the cells. The cells were collected into 1.5 mL EP tubes and further disrupted using a cell sonicator. The supernatant was obtained via ultracentrifugation. Protein concentration was determined using a BCA kit (Beyotime). The samples were then adjusted to equal concentrations using 5× Loading Buffer and DDH₂O, followed by heating at 100 °C for 10 min to prepare protein samples. Proteins from each group were separated by gel electrophoresis and subsequently transferred onto a 7.5 × 5 cm PVDF membrane. The membrane was blocked in blocking solution at room temperature for 1 h, washed with TBST, and incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against RUNX2 (1:1000, 12556s, Cell Signaling Technology, Rabbit), NOTCH2 (1:1000, 28580-1-AP, proteintech, Rabbit), Collagen-Ⅰ (1:1000, 14695-1-AP, proteintech, Rabbit), OCN (1:1000, 20277-1-AP, proteintech, Rabbit), ALP (1:1000, 11187-1-AP, proteintech, Rabbit) and GAPDH(1:50000, 60004-1-IG, proteintech, Mouse). The next day, after washing with TBST, the membrane was incubated with corresponding secondary antibodies for 1 h. Equal volumes of Developer Solution A and B were mixed. The PVDF membrane was placed in an imager, the mixed developer solution was evenly applied, and images were captured using the imager.

Quantitative RT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted from cells using the RNA Rapid Extraction Kit (Yeason, Shanghai). After measuring the RNA concentration, it was adjusted with RNase-Free Water and mixed with reverse transcription reagents to prepare a 20-µL reaction system. This was then placed in a PCR instrument for reverse transcription to synthesize the corresponding cDNA. Relative gene expression was determined using SYBR Green Premix (Yeason) and the ABI 7500 instrument (Thermo Fisher). Amplification results were analyzed using the 2^−△△Ct method, with U6 and Gapdh as internal references. The primer sequences are listed in Supplementary Table S1.

Micro-CT analysis

Femur samples were scanned at different time points using a Skyscan 1275 micro-CT scanner (Bruker micro-CT, Kontich, Belgium) to evaluate the callus healing at the fracture site in mice. The scanning parameters were set as follows: source voltage of 60 kV, source current of 80 µA, filter set to Al 1 mm, pixel size of 10 μm, lower grey threshold of 30, upper grey threshold of 255 and a resolution of 2016 × 1344. The samples were rotated through 360 degrees to finish the scan. The fracture site was chosen as the region of interest (ROI), and three-dimensional images were obtained. CT results were analyzed using CT Analyser Version: 1.20.8.0, with the measured parameters including trabecular number (Tb. N), bone mineral density (BMD), trabecular separation (Tb. Sp), bone volume/total volume ratio (BV/TV), and trabecular thickness (Tb. Th).

Histological and immunohistochemical examination

Mouse femur samples were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 h, followed by decalcification with 10% ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) at room temperature for one month. Subsequently, the tissue samples were gradually dehydrated through alcohol solutions of different concentrations, embedded in paraffin, and finally cut into 5 μm sagittal sections. These sections were then subjected to H&E staining, Masson’s trichrome staining, and Trap staining to identify trabecular bone formation and osteoclast proliferation, respectively. To analyze the osteogenic activity of the samples, the paraffin sections were subjected to dewaxing, hydration, antigen retrieval, and other treatments. Then, blocking was performed with goat serum, and the tissue sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against RUNX2 (Cell Signaling Technology) and Osteocalcin (OCN; proteintech). Incubation with secondary antibodies was carried out at room temperature for 1 h. Images were acquired using a digital pathological section scanner (Leica, Germany). Image processing was performed using ImageScope software (Leica, Germany).

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism software (Version 10.1.2, GraphPad Inc., USA), and the results were presented as mean ± standard deviation. Using SPSS (version 4.2.0), the Shapiro-Wilk test was used to evaluate the data distributions. Depending on the features of the data distribution, We use different statistical methods to analyze the data. For two independent experimental groups, unpaired Student’s t-test was generally used for statistical comparison. Differences among multiple experimental groups were evaluated by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). For data with ordinal grading systems, nonparametric Kruskal-Wallis test followed by Dunn’s post-hoc test was usually applied. All experimental data were derived from three independent experiments. A P-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Error bars represent the standard deviation for parametric data and the 95% confidence interval for nonparametric data.

Results

miR-34c-5p is a positive regulator of osteoblast differentiation

To investigate the regulatory mechanism of miR-34c-5p on osteoblast differentiation, we performed validation using osteoblasts treated with lentivirus. The treatment with mimics and inhibitors resulted in increased and decreased expression of miR-34c-5p in osteoblasts, respectively (Fig. 1A). We then evaluated the alkaline phosphatase activity and mineralization capacity of the transfected osteoblasts, which are phenotypic markers of osteoblast differentiation. As shown in Fig. 1B and C, the ALP activity of osteoblasts was significantly increased during miR-34c-5p overexpression, while it showed a marked decreasing trend during miR-34c-5p inhibition. The mineralization capacity of osteoblasts exhibited a consistent trend with ALP activity. Microscopic observation revealed that when miR-34c-5p was overexpressed, calcified nodules were significantly larger and calcium deposition was more extensive. In contrast, when miR-34c-5p was inhibited, calcified nodules were smaller and sparser (Fig. 1B). Meanwhile, we analyzed osteogenesis-related indicators, including Collagen-I, ALP, OCN, and RUNX2, using Western blotting and PCR. As depicted in Fig. 1D–I, in comparison with the miR-NC group, the miR-34c-5p mimic transfection group exhibited a notable elevation in osteogenesis-related indicators, which was evident not only at the mRNA level but also at the protein level. Additionally, immunofluorescence analysis revealed that the overexpression group exhibited significantly higher expression levels of Collagen-I and RUNX2, with more intense fluorescence than the miR-NC group. In contrast, the miR-34c-5p inhibitor transfection group displayed the reverse pattern, showing weaker fluorescence intensity compared to the miR-NC group (Fig. 1J–M).

Notch2 is a potential downstream target of miR-34c-5p

As mentioned earlier, miRNAs primarily exert their functions by inhibiting the translation of target genes. Therefore, to investigate the molecular mechanism by which miR-34c-5p acts during osteoblast differentiation, we predicted the downstream targets of miR-34c-5p using the online tools TargetScan and StarBase through mining public databases. Additionally, we retrieved osteoporosis-related target genes from the GeneCards database. The intersection of targets identified by these three approaches yielded the downstream target genes, including Lgr4, Notch2, Notch1, Arhgap1, Map2k1, and Klf4 (Fig. 2A). Furthermore, the results of qRT-PCR showed that compared with the miR-NC group, the mRNA expression of Notch2 in osteoblasts was significantly decreased after transfection with the miR-34c-5p mimic (Fig. 2B). Subsequently, the dual-luciferase reporter gene assay confirmed that compared with the miR-NC + WT-Notch2 co-transfection group, the luciferase activity of Wt-Notch2 in the miR-34c-5p mimic transfection group was significantly decreased, while no notable differences were detected in the Mut-Notch2 transfection group(Fig. 2C and 2D). As shown in Fig. 2E–F, Western blot analysis demonstrated that miR-34c-5p mimics inhibited Notch2 expression, whereas miR-34c-5p inhibitors markedly increased Notch2 expression. Therefore, Notch2 is the downstream target of miR-34c-5p in regulating osteoblast differentiation.

miR-34c-5p reverses the negative regulatory effect of Notch2

To verify the regulatory effect of Notch2 on osteoblast differentiation, we transfected osteoblasts with OE-Notch2(Notch2 overexpression construct) to overexpress Notch2 in osteoblasts. Western blot analysis verified the expression level of NOTCH2 in osteoblasts after transfection, showing a significant increase in NOTCH2 protein level under overexpression conditions. However, upon co-transfection with miR-34c-5p mimics, the expression of NOTCH2 showed a downward trend (Fig. 3A and C). To examine the osteogenic differentiation potential of osteoblasts following Notch2 intervention, we conducted ALP staining with quantitative analysis as well as Alizarin Red S (ARS) staining. The results showed that after Notch2 overexpression, both the alkaline phosphatase activity and osteogenic mineralization capacity of osteoblasts were significantly decreased, while this trend was largely reversed following transfection with miR-34c-5p mimics (Fig. 3B and D).The regulatory effect of Notch2 on osteogenic differentiation markers was verified using Western blot and quantitative RT-PCR assays. As depicted in Fig. 3E–J, in comparison with the Vector group, the osteogenic markers such as Collagen-I, ALP, OCN, and RUNX2 were markedly reduced when Notch2 was overexpressed. However, the addition of miR-34c-5p mimics notably alleviated this reduction.The results of immunofluorescence experiments indicated that the inhibitory effect of NOTCH2 on osteoblast differentiation was significantly alleviated by miR-34c-5p mimics (Fig. 3K–N).

Overexpression of miR-34c-5p in vivo can promote fracture healing in mice

To more robustly demonstrate that miR-34c-5p exerts a significant osteogenic-promoting effect, this experiment employed a mouse femoral fracture model to investigate the capacity of miR-34c-5p in promoting fracture healing. To achieve persistent and stable expression of miR-34c-5p in mice, we employed an alternative chemical modification technique. Through tail vein injection, miR-34c-5p could rapidly enter the mouse body via the bloodstream to exert its function. We selected 2-week and 4-week time windows to observe fracture healing, which was monitored by micro-CT analysis. Briefly, at 2 weeks postoperatively, compared with the AgomiR-NC group, the femoral fracture sites in mice of the AgomiR-34c-5p treatment group showed obvious formation of continuous callus. In contrast, in the group treated with AntagomiR-34c-5p, the callus at the fracture site lacked continuity, and the fracture gap was notably larger compared to the other treatment groups. At 4 weeks, even though the fracture gaps in the fracture regions of both the AgomiR-NC and AgomiR-34c-5p treatment groups were notably diminished, the callus in the AgomiR-34c-5p treatment group was more intact and greater in volume when compared to that in the AgomiR-NC group. From the 3D reconstructed images, it was also observed that callus formation in the antagomiR-34c-5p group was significantly suppressed, with not only a smaller callus volume than the AgomiR-NC group but also poorer continuity of the cortical bone. In addition, according to micro-CT 2D coronal and sagittal images of the fracture site, when comparing the continuity of cortical bone at the fracture ends, the AgomiR-34c-5p treatment groups (at both 2 and 4 weeks) displayed greater continuity and a denser callus structure in the surrounding area than the AgomiR-NC group. Conversely, fracture healing in the antagomiR-34c-5p group showed the opposite tendency(Fig. 4A). When quantifying the callus composition at the fracture site, the AgomiR-34c-5p treatment groups at 2 and 4 weeks showed higher bone mineral density (BMD), trabecular thickness (Tb.Th), bone volume fraction (BV/TV), and trabecular number (Tb.N), while exhibiting lower trabecular separation (Tb.Sp) in comparison with the AgomiR-NC group. In contrast, the relevant bone parameters of mice in the AntagomiR-34c-5p group showed an opposite trend to those in the therapeutic group, which was also within the expectations of our experiment. In addition, we performed histological analyses on decalcified samples, including H&E staining, Masson’s trichrome staining, and TRAP staining. Through identifying new bone formation at the fracture site, it was observed that the quantity of new bone formed in the callus of the AgomiR-34c-5p treatment group was greater than that in the AgomiR-NC group at both 2 and 4 weeks. Conversely, the inhibition group exhibited significantly less new bone formation compared to the AgomiR-NC group. Moreover, the AgomiR-34c-5p treatment group showed an abundance of trabecular bones, while the AntagomiR-34c-5p group had a marked reduction in trabecular bones, with fibrous connective tissue as the primary component. Osteoclasts also play an important role in osteogenesis. Based on their proliferative characteristics, during the cartilaginous callus formation stage around 2 weeks, the number of osteoclasts begins to gradually increase to clear the cartilage matrix. It reaches a peak during the hard callus formation stage at 4 weeks, functioning to remodel woven bone and shape the callus contour. As seen in TRAP staining, in comparison with the AgomiR-NC group, the number of osteoclasts attached to the trabecular bone within the callus in the AgomiR-34c-5p treatment group was significantly higher at both 2 and 4 weeks, while the number of osteoclasts around the trabecular bone in the inhibition group was significantly reduced (Fig. 5A and F). Ocn and Runx2 are typical osteogenic differentiation markers during osteoblast differentiation. The results of immunohistochemical staining showed that the positive expression levels of OCN and RUNX2 were higher in the AgomiR-34c-5p injection group, while the expression levels of the above differentiation markers were significantly decreased in the AntagomiR-34c-5p injection group. As a potential downstream target of miR-34c-5p, Notch2 plays a key role in osteoblast differentiation. In the immunohistochemical results, we can observe that the positive expression level of Notch2 was significantly decreased in the AgomiR-34c-5p injection group, whereas the expression level of Notch2 was increased after inhibition of miR-34c-5p (Fig. 5B). Quantitative analysis of the positive areas of RUNX2, OCN, and NOTCH2 indicated that there were significant differences among all groups (Fig. 5C–E).

Discussion

In the vast miRNA family, miR-34c-5p plays an indispensable role in the proliferation and differentiation of osteoblasts, which has been partially validated in our previous work. Current studies on miR-34c-5p in the field of bone metabolism mostly focus on the osteogenic differentiation of human periodontal ligament stem cells (PDLSCs). For example, a study by Wang et al. has demonstrated that miR-34c-5p inhibits osteogenic differentiation of PDLSCs through the Satb2/Erk pathway [28]. Conversely, a separate study by Lin et al. has reported that miR-34c-5p promotes osteogenesis by activating the Klf4/β-catenin signaling axis [29]. Similar to the latter conclusion, our study also found that miR-34c-5p promotes osteogenic differentiation and fracture healing by regulating Notch2. Therefore, we believe that the different experimental conclusions may be attributed to cellular differences or regulatory specificity of the microenvironment.

So far, there have been many studies on fracture healing, including explorations of drugs to promote fracture healing. For example, the clinical drug calcitonin can promote fracture healing [30]; Jintiange can effectively shorten the time of fracture healing and reduce the fracture risk of clinical patients [31]. In addition, relevant studies have also shown that intermittent pneumatic compression (IPC) and low-frequency pulsed ultrasound (LIPUS) are expected to become effective medical means to promote fracture healing [32, 33]. Fracture healing is an extremely complex biological reconstruction process, involving multiple key stages such as anti-inflammatory response activation, endochondral ossification, and bone formation and remodeling. There have been numerous studies on the role of miRNAs in osteoblast differentiation. For example, miR-133a negatively regulates chondrocyte hypertrophy and ossification by targeting Runx2, playing an inhibitory role in the early cartilage formation stage of fracture healing [34]. Knocking down miR-140-5p in adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cells (ASCs) can target Tlr4 and Bmp2, promoting the osteogenic differentiation of ASCs [35]. MiR-1271-5p overexpression regulates osteoblast differentiation by targeting Zbtb7a and intervenes in Pilon fracture healing [36]. However, whether miRNAs can truly promote fracture healing remains unclear. Based on in vitro cell experiments, this study further constructed a mouse fracture model and injected miR-34c-5p modifiers into mice via tail vein injection to observe the effect of miR-34c-5p on fracture healing phenotypes, thereby enhancing the evidence level of this study. Consistent with the research by Liu et al. [37], through cellular experiments, we have demonstrated that under the action of osteogenic induction factors, miR-34c-5p exhibits a significant osteogenesis-promoting effect. The differentiation activity and mineralization capacity of osteoblasts were significantly enhanced, and the expression levels of related differentiation markers were also increased to varying degrees. These effects were reversed upon inhibition of miR-34c-5p (Fig. 1). Meanwhile, we have also confirmed through solid in vivo experimental data that when AgomiR-34c-5p is injected into mice via intravenous administration, the regeneration of bone microstructure is significantly induced. Compared with other groups, the fracture healing time of mice in the treatment group is not only significantly shortened, but also the callus volume formed during the healing process is larger(Fig. 4A).

As is widely recognized, miRNAs target and regulate the translation of mRNA-encoded proteins, with such regulation going on to be involved in mediating the progression of diverse diseases. Previously, Our study has discovered and confirmed that miR-34c-5p can exert a positive regulatory effect on osteoblast differentiation by targeting Notch2. Evolutionarily speaking, conserved Notch signaling sequences hold a critical position in determining cell fate; by means of intercellular interactions, these sequences are translated into specific transcriptional programs, which in turn influence cell differentiation processes [38]. Within this course of events, the temporal and spatial dependence of Notch has a marked impact on cellular events such as proliferation, apoptosis, and differentiation [39]. Relevant studies have confirmed that the crosstalk of Notch signaling programs can lead to distinct effects in cells of the same lineage [40]. During the early stage of mesenchymal stem cell (MSC) differentiation into osteoblasts, Notch maintains the stem cell pool by inhibiting Runx2 activity, whereas in the late stage of differentiation, it promotes the proliferation of immature osteoblasts by activating Osterix and Cyclin D/E [21]. Overexpression of Notch1 can inhibit the expression of osteogenic differentiation marker genes (such as Osteocalcin), leading to reduced bone mass; whereas Notch1 deficiency transiently increases bone mass and promotes osteoblast differentiation [41]. In our study, overexpression of Notch2 resulted in varying degrees of inhibition of osteogenic markers including Collagen-I, Alp, Ocn, and Runx2, and this inhibitory effect was alleviated by miR-34c-5p overexpression. Currently, in existing studies, we have not found that miR-34c-5p can regulate the differentiation process of osteoblasts by targeting Notch2, and our research has preliminarily put forward this viewpoint.

miRNAs have shown remarkable potential in the treatment of fracture healing and various bone metabolic diseases. However, miRNA expression is regulated by multiple factors, including circular RNAs (circRNAs) and long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs). lncRNAs can regulate gene expression at different levels through mechanisms such as chromatin remodeling and regulation of the cell cycle and transcription [42], while circRNAs can also modulate gene expression by regulating linear RNAs [43]. Currently, there are numerous studies on lncRNAs and circRNAs in the field of bone metabolism, such as lncRNA Casc11 regulating fracture healing by targeting miR-150-3p and the feasibility of circRNAs in the treatment of osteoporosis [44, 45]. Thus, in the vast molecular network of the human body, various RNAs interact with each other through intricate biological mechanisms to regulate each other’s expression levels. Future research should further decipher this complex molecular network to promote the clinical application of RNA-based precision therapeutic strategies.

However, our study also has certain limitations. Firstly, we lack clinically derived samples, which makes it difficult for us to approach the research from the perspective of clinical patient treatment. Meanwhile, the absence of sequencing data from patient samples hinders us from further consolidating and strengthening our research conclusions. Secondly, it remains to be confirmed whether more frequent injections of overexpression viruses can induce similar or better osteogenic effects in fractured mice. Additionally, consideration could be given to using exosomes with good biocompatibility and low immunogenicity as delivery vehicles for targeted delivery to fracture tissues via local injection, thereby regulating the osteoblast-osteoclast balance to achieve more optimal healing. Furthermore, we were unable to conduct clinical trials on the beneficial effects of miR-34c-5p overexpression on fracture healing. Meanwhile, to obtain more accurate evidence confirming the value of the miR-34c-5p/Notch2 axis in the process of osteoblast differentiation, it is necessary to incorporate higher-level evidence from transgenic mice and in vivo data to form our evidence chain.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study demonstrates that miR-34c-5p exerts positive regulation on osteoblast differentiation by targeting the downstream gene Notch2. This discovery provides new insights into exploring the regulatory mechanisms and potential roles of miR-34c-5p in osteoblast differentiation.

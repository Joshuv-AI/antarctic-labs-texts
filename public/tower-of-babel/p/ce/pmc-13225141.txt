Analysis of Current Status and Influencing Factors of Inhaler Treatment Adherence Among COPD Patients
Authors: Zikang Jin, Baersi Ba, Wanyu Zhang, Ziyue Yang, Xiaoyi Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo investigate the current status of inhaler treatment adherence and analyze its influencing factors among patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) based on the clinical treatment data of a tertiary grade A hospital.Patients and MethodsA retrospective study design was adopted. COPD patients who received inhaler treatment at The First Affiliated Hospital of Shihezi University from July to December 2023 were enrolled. Their medical visit records, prescription data within 18 months after enrollment, and medical insurance reimbursement records were collected. Medication adherence was evaluated using proportion of days’ supply covered (PDC) calculated from prescription and reimbursement records, not by direct measurement of actual inhaler, and patients were divided into three groups. The relationships between adherence and patients’ basic characteristics, disease-related factors, and inhaler usage were analyzed.ResultsA total of 706 patients were included in the study. Overall, patient adherence was poor as assessed by PDC, mean PDC of 43.67% ± 32.44%. Among them, 18.42% of patients had high adherence and 60.48% had low adherence. Male patients and those with a smoking history had higher adherence. The inhaler treatment adherence of COPD patients increased with the aggravation of the disease (Gamma = 0.267, p = 0.024), the prolongation of disease duration (r = 0.155, p < 0.01), and the increase in the number of acute exacerbations (p < 0.01). Patients using fluticasone furoate/vilanterol/umeclidinium bromide inhalation powder had higher overall adherence than those using budesonide/formoterol fumarate inhalation powder.ConclusionAmong patients with COPD at our hospital, adherence to inhaled therapy as measured by PDC is generally low. Lower PDC was observed in newly diagnosed patients and those without a history of acute exacerbations. In clinical practice, greater emphasis should be placed on medication education and long-term management for these patients.Keywords: chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, inhaled medications, treatment adherence, proportion of days covered

Introduction

Long-term regular-inhaled medication is the main treatment for COPD.1–4 Good medication adherence is the basis for achieving the expected therapeutic effect of drugs, but in reality, patients’ medication adherence is far from the ideal level, especially long-term adherence.5 At present, there is no unified standard for evaluating the inhaler treatment adherence of COPD patients.6 Electronic inhaler adherence monitoring tracked inhaler use via clip-on sensors connected to the study mobile apps, these adherence test results are the most accurate and reliable, but they are very costly. Currently, most studies use7 Test of the Adherence to Inhalers (TAI) to assess adherence among patients with COPD, and the results are significantly influenced by recall bias.8 PDC, based on prescription and reimbursement records, reflects patients’ medication purchases and provides an indirect measure of their objective adherence, but to date, few studies have used PDC to evaluate adherence in patients with COPD.

Therefore, this study took COPD patients receiving inhaler treatment in a tertiary grade A hospital as the research objects, used PDC as the judgment index of medication adherence to evaluate patients’ medication behavior, and analyzed the correlation between patients’ basic characteristics, disease characteristics, types of inhalers and other factors and their inhaler treatment adherence. This study is expected to provide a reference for clinical improvement of COPD patients’ inhaler treatment adherence and optimization of disease management strategies.

Materials and Methods

Study Design

A retrospective study was conducted to collect and analyze the 18-month medical visit records and prescription data of patients who received inhaler treatment in the outpatient or inpatient department of The First Affiliated Hospital of Shihezi University from July to December 2023, so as to evaluate the treatment adherence and related influencing factors of the patients.

Data Sources

The research data were derived from the Hospital Information System (HIS) of The First Affiliated Hospital of Shihezi University, the Yidu Cloud Scientific Research Platform, and the medical insurance system of Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps. The collected information included patients’ basic information, diagnosis and treatment (prescription) records, and medical insurance reimbursement data. This study has been approved by the Science and Technology Ethics Committee of the hospital (Approval No. KJ2025-045-01). Given that this was a retrospective study based on de-identified secondary data with no direct contact with study participants and no intervention in patients’ clinical treatment, the Ethics Committee waived the requirement for written informed consent from individual participants. All patient information was desensitized to protect patient privacy.

Inclusion Criteria

Patients who were admitted to the outpatient, emergency or inpatient department of the hospital from July to December 2023 and were prescribed inhalers for COPD (the inhalers used were long-term maintenance treatment inhalants for COPD includes single inhalers or multiple inhalers, excluding nebulized inhalation preparations and short-acting bronchodilators for relieving symptoms).

Exclusion Criteria

① Patients with incomplete medical records; ② Patients with no follow-up medical visit records and medical insurance reimbursement records after the first medication (lost to follow-up); ③ Patients living in outside this city and suburban areas; ④ Patients who died within the 18-month observation period after the first prescription of inhaled drugs during the study period; ⑤ Patients participating in clinical trials related to inhalers.

Outcome Measure: Medication Adherence

PDC was used to evaluate patients’ medication adherence.9,10 Generally, PDC ≥ 80% is defined as good adherence, and PDC < 80% as poor adherence. To evaluate patient adherence more accurately, we divided adherence into three grades: high adherence with PDC ≥ 80% (prescription medication for ≥15 months), moderate adherence with 50% < PDC < 80% (prescription medication for 10–14 months), and low adherence with PDC ≤ 50% (prescription medication for ≤9 months). PDC based on prescription and reimbursement records reflects medication supply rather than actual inhaler use, our PDC-based assessment methods may overestimate actual inhalation behavior, as obtaining a medication does not necessarily mean it will be used correctly or consistently. However, patients who do not obtain medications through prescriptions or reimbursement records typically have lower adherence. (This is because all inhaled medications used for the maintenance treatment of COPD are prescription drugs).

PDC = (Total days of medication supplied by prescriptions / Total observation days) × 100%

Influencing Factors

The influencing factors included patients’ basic characteristics, disease-related factors, and inhaler usage. Among them, patients’ basic characteristics covered age, gender, and smoking status; disease-related factors included disease severity, disease duration (patients diagnosed with COPD for <1 year were defined as newly diagnosed patients), and the number of acute exacerbations;11 defined by events: outpatient or emergency visits due to symptom aggravation were defined as “mild acute exacerbations”, and hospitalization as “severe acute exacerbations” inhaler usage included the type of inhaler used and whether the medication was changed during the observation period.

Statistical Methods

SPSS 25.0 software and Excel were used for statistical analysis. Missing data were handled by complete case analysis; only participants with complete data for all variables included in the analysis were retained. Normality test was performed on continuous data: parametric tests were used for data conforming to normal distribution, and non-parametric tests for non-normal distribution data. Chi-square test was used for categorical variables. Pearson chi-square test was used to analyze the significance of the correlation between disease severity and adherence, and Gamma coefficient was used to analyze the correlation direction and intensity of ordinal categorical variables. For multivariate analysis, the adherence level (low, moderate, high) was taken as the ordinal dependent variable, and variables with p < 0.1 in univariate analysis and potential influencing factors were included in the ordinal multinomial Logistic regression (proportional odds model). The proportional odds assumption was verified by the parallel-line test before modeling (p > 0.05 indicated that the assumption was valid). The overall significance of the model was evaluated by chi-square test, and the goodness of fit was judged by the deviance goodness-of-fit test. A two-sided p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Sample Screening

A total of 1023 patients were exported from the Yidu Cloud Scientific Research Platform, and 706 patients were finally included after excluding those who did not meet the criteria (Figure 1).

Flow chart of study subject enrollment and exclusion.

Basic Characteristics of Patients

A total of 706 COPD patients were included in this study, of whom 59.35% were male, with a mean age of (73.61±11.684) years. 36.54% of the patients had a smoking history, 262 cases (37.11%) had a history of mild acute exacerbations, 226 cases (32.01%) had a history of severe acute exacerbations, and 9 types of inhalers were used for long-term treatment.

General Status of Medication Adherence

The mean PDC of all patients was 43.67% ± 32.44%, indicating low overall adherence. Among them, 130 patients (18.42%) had high adherence, 149 patients (21.10%) had moderate adherence, and 427 patients (60.48%) had low adherence. Among the patients with low adherence, 126 cases (29.51%) had only 1 month of prescription (corresponding PDC was 5.56%).

Correlation analysis between disease severity and adherence showed that the Pearson chi-square value was 14.758 (df = 6, p = 0.022), indicating a statistically significant correlation between disease severity and adherence as assessed by PDC. Relationship Between Medication Adherence and Disease Duration

A total of 76.35% of the patients in this study had a disease duration of 0–10 years. Chi-square test results showed that there was a significant statistical correlation between patients’ medication adherence as assessed by PDC and disease duration, and the overall distribution showed that the longer the disease duration, the higher the adherence.

Normality test (Kolmogorov–Smirnov (K-S) test and Shapiro–Wilk (S-W) test) was performed on the PDC of newly diagnosed and non-newly diagnosed patients. The p values of K–S test and S-W test in both groups were <0.05, indicating that the data was non-normally distributed.

Since the data did not conform to the normal distribution, non-parametric test was used for inter-group comparison. The mean PDC of the newly diagnosed group was 24.61% ± 25.46%, and that of the non-newly diagnosed group was 49.89% ± 32.07%. The rank mean of the newly diagnosed group (228.50) was lower than that of the non-newly diagnosed group (394.38), p<0.01, indicating that the inhaler treatment adherence as assessed by PDC of newly diagnosed patients was significantly lower than that of non-newly diagnosed patients.

Correlation analysis between disease duration and PDC showed a weak positive correlation (r = 0.155, p < 0.01), indicating that the longer the patient’s disease duration, the higher the medication adherence (Figure 3).

Multivariate Logistic Regression Analysis

To clarify the independent influence of each factor on adherence, the adherence level (low, moderate, high) was taken as the ordinal dependent variable, and variables with p < 0.1 in univariate analysis and potential influencing factors were included in the ordinal multinomial Logistic regression model. The parallel-line test showed p = 0.880, the model was overall significant (χ2 = 86.544, p < 0.001), and the fit was good (deviance goodness of fit p = 0.198).

The regression results showed that: ① For each 1-time increase in the number of mild acute exacerbations (OR = 1.182, 95% CI: 1.017–1.373, p = 0.029) and severe acute exacerbations (OR = 1.332, 95% CI: 1.112–1.598, p = 0.002), the odds of patients reaching a higher adherence grade increased by 18.2% and 33.2%, respectively. ② For each 1-year increase in disease duration, the odds of reaching a higher adherence grade increased by 2.0% (OR = 1.020, 95% CI: 1.004–1.037, p = 0.016). ③ The odds of patients with medication change reaching a higher adherence grade was 1.838 times that of patients without medication change (OR = 1.838, 95% CI: 1.328–2.545, p < 0.001). ④ The odds of males reaching a higher adherence grade was 1.388 times that of females, with marginal significance (OR = 1.388, 95% CI: 0.943–2.043, p = 0.096). ⑤ Smoking history and inhaler type had no statistically significant effect on adherence grade (p) of >0.05).

Discussion

For medication adherence, most previous studies used patient-reported subjective scoring methods such as the Morisky Medication Adherence Scale-8 (MMAS-8) and the Test of the Adherence to Inhalers (TAI), and the results may have a certain degree of “bias”.7 PDC provides an objective approach for adherence evaluation, although this does not fully reflect patient adherence, it can provide an indication of medication adherence based on prescription and reimbursement data.8 With the development of the medical insurance data network, patients’ medication pickup records in different institutions can be obtained by querying medical insurance data. In this study, PDC was used as the adherence evaluation index, which has the advantage of being based on objective prescription data and avoiding recall bias and social desirability bias that may exist in patient self-reported questionnaires.

Based on the real-world inhaler medication pickup data of COPD patients in a tertiary grade A hospital, this study found that the overall inhaler treatment adherence of COPD patients in the hospital was poor. Only 18.42% of the patients reached a high adherence level during the 18-month observation period, and more than 60% of the patients had low adherence.12 In a study by Homętowska H et al, researchers used the TAI to measure patients’ subjective adherence; the proportion of highly adherent patients in their study was higher than in this study, which used the PDC to objectively assess patient adherence (26% vs. 18.42%). Indicating that patients face severe challenges in long-term medication adherence in clinical practice.

The study found that the adherence of newly diagnosed patients was significantly lower than that of non-newly diagnosed patients, and there was a weak positive correlation between disease duration and the number of annual prescriptions. This result may be related to patients’ insufficient awareness of the disease and low treatment motivation.3 Newly diagnosed patients often lack a full understanding of the chronic progressive characteristics of COPD and the necessity of long-term treatment, leading to easy medication interruption in the initial treatment stage. With the prolongation of the course of the disease, some patients gradually realize the importance of regular treatment due to recurrent symptoms or acute exacerbations, thus improving their adherence.

There is a moderate positive correlation between medication adherence (as assessed by PDC) and disease severity in patients with COPD. This association may arise because worsening disease symptoms compel patients to improve adherence, or it may result from worsening symptoms caused by poor adherence; this finding may also be influenced by confounding factors. Therefore, the true association between real-world inhalation behavior and disease severity remains to be further validated.

There was a significant difference in medication adherence as assessed by PDC between patients with and without acute exacerbations. Patients with a history of acute exacerbations had higher adherence, and the number of acute exacerbations was positively correlated with medication adherence.1 This may be because acute exacerbation events enhance patients’ perception of the severity of the disease, thus prompting them to obtain drugs more actively.10,13 On the contrary, nearly half of the patients with high adherence had no acute exacerbations within one year, which suggests that regular medication may play a positive role in preventing acute exacerbations, consistent with the results of previous studies.

Multivariate ordinal logistic regression further verified the results of univariate analysis and controlled the mutual influence between variables.14,15 A history of acute exacerbations is an important driving factor for patients to improve adherence, and the positive correlation between disease duration and adherence still exists after controlling other factors. Whether to change medication has become an independent influencing factor, and patients with medication change have higher adherence, which may reflect two situations: first, frequent medication change may indicate unstable condition or poor therapeutic response, and patients seek medical treatment more actively; second, doctors pay more attention and guidance to patients who need to adjust the treatment plan, which indirectly improves adherence. The effect of gender was marginally significant in multivariate analysis, suggesting that gender differences may affect adherence partly through other mediating variables (such as the frequency of acute exacerbations and disease severity), rather than a direct effect. Inhaler type had no significant effect after controlling other factors, indicating that the difference in adherence between different inhalers may mainly come from patient characteristics rather than the drugs themselves.

In addition, it is worth noting that 91.7% of the patients had GOLD I/II grade disease severity (mild/moderate obstruction). The possible reasons are speculated as follows: first, patients with severe obstruction cannot cooperate with the completion of pulmonary function tests due to their poor pulmonary function; second, patients with severe obstruction have a heavier symptom burden and a significantly decreased survival rate; third, with the vigorous promotion of community screening and physical examination, a large number of early COPD patients have been found.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, PDC serves only as a predictor of adherence and does not fully reflect a patient’s actual medication use. Second,this was a retrospective observational study, and thus selection bias and residual confounding could not be completely avoided. Certain exclusion criteria, such as ③ and ④, may also increase selection bias. Third, the present study adopted complete case analysis for missing data, which may reduce the effective sample size and potentially introduce selection bias the missing data were not completely random. Fourth, physicians tended to select inhalers based on disease severity, patient tolerance, and ease of use, resulting in a small number of patients for some less commonly used products. The small subgroups may limit the stability of statistical comparisons.

In conclusion, this study used the objective PDC index to predict and evaluate the inhaler medication adherence of COPD patients, clarified the severe current situation of poor overall adherence of patients, and revealed that disease severity, disease duration, history of acute exacerbations, gender, smoking history and inhaler type are the influencing factors of adherence, and regular medication may have a positive effect on preventing acute exacerbations. The above findings not only make up for the limitations of subjective scoring in evaluating adherence, but also provide an important basis for clinical formulation of targeted intervention strategies, improvement of patients’ long-term medication adherence and improvement of disease prognosis.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the present study demonstrated that inhaler treatment adherence based on PDC was generally suboptimal among COPD patients in our hospital, with more than 60% showing low adherence. The number of acute exacerbations, disease duration, and medication modification were independently associated with PDC‑based adherence: a higher number of exacerbations, longer disease duration, and history of medication change were related to better adherence.

Clinical practice should strengthen medication education and health guidance for COPD patients, especially newly diagnosed patients and those with mild disease and no acute exacerbations, to improve their awareness of the disease and the importance of long-term regular medication. For patients with a history of acute exacerbations, individualized treatment plans should be formulated and regular follow-up should be carried out to maintain good medication adherence. In addition, doctors should pay attention to the rational adjustment of inhaler treatment plans and strengthen the follow-up management of patients with medication change, so as to further improve the inhaler treatment adherence of COPD patients and optimize the disease prognosis.

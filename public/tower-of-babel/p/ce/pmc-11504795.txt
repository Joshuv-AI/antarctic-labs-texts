Utilization of telemedicine services of institute of national importance in the western region of INDIA: A mixed-method study
Authors: Krupal Joshi, Bhautik Modi, CDS Katoch
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction:Telemedicine has emerged as a crucial healthcare tool, especially during the COVID-19 pandemic, offering risk-free contactless communication between physicians and patients. In India, the introduction of official guidelines in 2019 and initiatives like the “e-Sanjeevani” national teleconsultation portal have accelerated telemedicine’s adoption. However, the utilization and satisfaction levels of telemedicine services in Institutes of National Importance (INI) remain underexplored.Objective:This mixed-method study aims to describe the utilization of telemedicine services at the All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS), Rajkot, an INI in western India, and assess patients’ satisfaction with these services.Methods:Data was collected from patients availing telemedicine services through the e-Sanjeevani portal at AIIMS Rajkot between February and December 2022. Quantitative analysis included patient demographics, specialty attended, location, call duration, and wrong call rates. Qualitative analysis involved interviews with patients to assess their experiences and satisfaction levels. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS for quantitative data and Atlas Ti for qualitative data.Results:A total of 12,442 doctor-to-doctor and 7,876 patient-to-doctor consultations were recorded, with a diverse age distribution (21-40 years, 45.4%). Most patients were from medical specialties, with general medicine, Pulmonary Medicine, and Dermatology being the highest. The data revealed a wide geographic distribution, with over 70% of cases from more than 200 kilometers away. Despite some challenges, including a 38.2% wrong call rate, the telemedicine services were well-received, with 96% of respondents reporting overall satisfaction.Conclusion:Telemedicine services at AIIMS Rajkot have shown high user satisfaction and accessibility, highlighting their potential in improving healthcare delivery and access, particularly in remote areas. However, efforts to enhance digital literacy and address challenges are necessary for further optimization of telemedicine services.

Introduction

There were no official guidelines on telemedicine in India till 2019. During the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19), the need for risk-free contactless communication between physicians and patients has paved the way for the flourishment of several electronic applications (e-applications) for healthcare consultations like Practo, 1 mg, and Lybrate. On April 13, 2020, during the first lockdown, the Government of India also launched its web-based national teleconsultation portal, “e-Sanjeevani.”[12]

As specified by the World Health Organization (WHO), the scope of telemedicine includes patient consultation, diagnosis, prevention, and treatment, as well as research and continuing education of healthcare workers (HCWs), provided the objective of such activities is advancing the health of individuals as well as the communities.[34] The principles and procedures for practicing telemedicine in India are taken from the international telemedicine guidelines and the statutes of India, like the Indian Medical Council (IMC) Act 1956, Information Technology (IT) Act 2000, Clinical Establishment Act 2010, Drugs and Cosmetics Act 1940, and so forth.[5]

In February 2022, as an Institute of National Importance (INI), All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS) Rajkot also initiated telemedicine services through the hub and spoke model with “AIIMS e-Sanjeevani OPD” as the hub and a total of 8087 Health and Wellness Centers (HWCs) from all over Gujarat state as spokes that includes Sub-District Hospitals, Community Health Centers (CHCs), Primary Health Centers (PHCs), Urban Primary Health Centers (UPHCs), and Sub-Centers (SCs). However, to plan future expansion and efficient utilization strategies, it is crucial to analyze the data from time to time; therefore, this study was conducted.

Material and Methods

This mixed-method study was conducted among patients availing of telemedicine services via e-Sanjeevani at AIIMS, Rajkot. Patients with incomplete data or whose call was not complete were excluded from the study. The ethical approval to conduct the study was obtained from the Institutional Ethics Committee (IEC) of the AIIMS, Rajkot (AIMS.Rajkot/IEC/02/2023).

This study was conducted in two parts: quantitative and qualitative part. In the quantitative part, data was analyzed for all patients who availed of telemedicine service either through HWCs (doctor to doctor) or outpatient departments (OPDs) (patient to doctor) from February 1 to December 31, 2022. This data was analyzed for the patient’s demographics, specialty attended, location, call duration, and wrong call. In the qualitative part, interviews of patients coming for follow-up after obtaining telemedicine services at AIIMS Rajkot were conducted after the end of the call regarding their experience, level of satisfaction, scope of improvement, etc., with an anonymous, semi-structured questionnaire of patient’s preferred language (Hindi, Gujarati, or English). The respondents were informed of the purpose of the study and assured of confidentiality and their right to withdraw from the study. The patient’s interviews were conducted up to the saturation level achieved.

Statistical analysis

Quantitative data

Data was entered and analyzed using IBM SPSS version 21.0. Quantitative data was checked for normality, and an analysis of variance (Anova)/Kruskal–Wallis test was applied for continuous data. The correlation was studied for factors affecting patient satisfaction. P values less than 0.05 were considered significant.

Qualitative data

The transcripts were prepared on the same day by the investigator. The transcripts were then analyzed using Atlas Ti. The transcripts were independently reviewed by two researchers trained in qualitative research.

Results

A total of 12,442 consultations between doctor and doctor (on the Hub and Spoke model) and 7,876 consultations between patients and doctors were recorded during the given period. The data reveals a diverse age distribution within a sample population of 12,442 individuals, with the largest cohort falling within the age range of 21–40 years (45.4%) and aged 41–60 (27.3%) [Table 1]. A comprehensive overview of the distribution of medical cases across various districts revealed that Ahmedabad has the highest frequency (20.2%), followed by Rajkot (15.3%) and Kachchh (8.9%). More than 70% of the cases were from more than 200 km away from AIIMS Rajkot. Most patients were primarily from medical specialties, with general medicine, pulmonary medicine, and dermatology being the highest.

Table 4 summarizes data collected from 50 individuals who have used telemedicine services provided by AIIMS Rajkot. The sample includes individuals with an average age of 33.16 years (±14.65), with a 95% confidence interval between 29.0 and 37.32 years. The majority of the respondents were female (74%), and most belonged to socio-economic classes II (56%) and III (44%). In terms of education, the sample was diverse, with 36% being illiterate, 44% having primary education, 16% having secondary education, and 4% being graduates or above. Hotspot laptops (22%) and other hotspots (18%) were the most common means of accessing the internet for telemedicine. Most respondents were unemployed (52%), while 34% were part-time workers. Health-related characteristics revealed that 74% of the respondents had a family history of chronic diseases, and the majority had their first consultation (58%) using telemedicine services. The data also showed high levels of satisfaction with the telemedicine services, with 96% of respondents reporting overall satisfaction.

Table 4 also provides valuable insights into the users’ experiences with AIIMS Rajkot’s telemedicine services. The data suggest that the services were well-received, with 80% of respondents finding them easy to access and 80% finding the platform easy to use. A significant percentage (90%) reported proper communication with doctors, the availability of doctors, and doctors’ respectful behavior. Additionally, 86% of respondents stated that telemedicine services saved time traveling, which is a significant advantage. Moreover, 94% of respondents expressed a likelihood of using telemedicine services in the future, indicating high satisfaction and a willingness to continue utilizing these services. Overall, the data highlight the success of telemedicine services in AIIMS Rajkot, with high user satisfaction, accessibility, and positive interactions with healthcare professionals, reinforcing the potential of telemedicine in improving healthcare delivery and access.

Discussion

The present study showed frequent use of teleconsultations through the “e-Sanjeevani” portal across all districts of Gujarat, with more than 70% of the cases being from more than 200 km away from AIIMS Rajkot. Traditionally, healthcare consultation requires face-to-face encounters between a provider and a patient in a physical location. Over the years, technology and the internet have made it possible for healthcare to be delivered digitally through teleconsultations, providing new opportunities and avenues for medicine to improve healthcare access and quality. Historically, teleconsultations focused on rural medicine.[6] However, with institutional initiatives and the push by the COVID-19 pandemic, the widespread adoption of teleconsultation was unprecedented, having a significant and lasting impact on healthcare delivery.[78] Bridging the gap in time and distance by permitting two-way, real-time interactive communication between the patient and the physician at a distant site has enabled providers to care for patients remotely with high satisfaction and lower costs without a difference in clinical outcomes compared to in-person consultations.[9] Improved access to mobile technology and the internet has also allowed the sharing of medical images such as X-rays or scans, vital signs, and electrocardiogram (ECG).[7] Besides providing increased access to specialist care, it reduces the impact of qualified medical professionals in remote areas and maximizes the efficiency of the current workforce.[7891011]

This study showed that the largest cohort falls within the age range of 21–40 years (45.4%), suggesting that a significant proportion of adults are in their prime working and reproductive years; similar to other studies, possibly teleconsultation provides alternative care services for minor illnesses, helping them save time and resources.[910] Elderly patients used telemedicine less often than other age groups in our study, which concurs with other studies.[101213] This emphasizes the need to adapt to suit the elderly populations better. Another factor may be that older adults may have complex diseases or multiple comorbidities that telemedicine consultations cannot manage.[10] Moreover, many older adults who lack access to traditional healthcare may lack access to telemedicine capabilities due to less education, less likely access to the internet in rural areas, and familiarity with online tools.[14] The marginally higher use of telemedicine consultation by the female population than males in our study also aligns with previous studies.[1516]

In our study, most patients were primarily from medical specialties, with general medicine, pulmonary medicine, and dermatology being the highest matched with the literature.[717] The lack of physical examination and intervention, as an implicit limitation of telemedicine, are reasons for this differential turnover among other specialties. General medicine is the cornerstone for patients with several comorbidities, and certain specialties like dermatology and psychiatry rely on visual examination/verbal communication, sparing them from the limitation of telemedicine, which may be responsible for high turnover in these specialties.[7] Around 40% of the calls were wrongly addressed, reflecting the need to enhance digital literacy to utilize these services better.

Data from 50 individuals regarding the satisfaction level highlighting the success of telemedicine services in our study, with high user satisfaction, accessibility, and positive interactions with healthcare professionals, reinforces the potential of telemedicine in improving healthcare delivery and access which has been cited as the most critical factor in the success of telemedicine initiatives in various studies.[79101118]

Conclusion

Telemedicine can also significantly decrease the time it takes to receive medical care by eliminating travel and waiting times, dramatically increasing patient face-to-face time with their physicians.[19] However, we do not justify telemedicine in place of in-person visits if both were equally accessible, given the limitations of telemedicine as cited earlier.

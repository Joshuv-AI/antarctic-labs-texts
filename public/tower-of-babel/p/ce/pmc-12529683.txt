Wrongful Migration: A Hidden Culprit Behind Flank Pain
Authors: Ryan Miller, Anjeza Cipi, Emma Pierce
Published: 2025

Patient Presentation

A 32-year-old woman with no significant past medical history presented to the emergency department with 11 days of fever, fatigue, chills, and new-onset fullness in her left flank. She denied dysuria, abdominal pain, or vaginal bleeding. Vital signs showed fever and mild hypotension, and the physical examination revealed left-sided abdominal distension. Laboratory results demonstrated leukocytosis and a lactate of 3.2 mmol/L. She appeared nontoxic but was persistently febrile despite initial fluids and antibiotics. Computed tomography—shown in Figure 1, and ultrasound—shown in Figure 2, were obtained and revealed the diagnosis.

Computed tomography scan of abdomen and pelvis in coronal view showing marked left hydronephrosis with renal cortical thinning and the migrated arm of an intrauterine device adjacent to the left ureter.

Transverse view of transvaginal ultrasound demonstrating the echogenic intrauterine-device arm extending through the myometrium into the peritoneal cavity, near a fluid-filled, dilated ureter.

Diagnosis: Intrauterine Device Migration Causing Ureteral Obstruction and Severe Hydronephrosis

Imaging revealed severe left hydronephrosis with an intrauterine-device (IUD) arm extending beyond the uterine wall and abutting the ureter. Although extremely rare, IUD migration can lead to significant genitourinary complications, including ureteral obstruction, recurrent urinary tract infection, sepsis, and irreversible renal damage.1, 2, 3, 4 In this case, the patient’s IUD had migrated through the myometrium and lodged near the distal ureter (Figs S1 and S2), likely resulting in ureteral fibrosis and left-sided hydronephrosis- (Figs S3 and S4). The patient was admitted with sepsis secondary to obstructive uropathy and managed initially with percutaneous nephrostomy and broad-spectrum intravenous antibiotic therapy. She ultimately underwent robotic–assisted laparoscopic nephrectomy and IUD removal. Emergency physicians should consider migrated IUDs in patients presenting with hydronephrosis of unclear etiology. Prompt imaging, early recognition of obstruction, and multidisciplinary consultation are critical to preserving renal function and guiding definitive care.

Motorcyclist visibility materials uptake and associated factors toward injury prevention in Cameroon
Authors: C E Ukah, N Tendongfor, A Hubbard, E A Tanue, N Bassah, R Oke, S A Christie, L K Yunika, C Julliard, A Chichom-Mefire
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCommercial motorcycle riders in Cameroon are highly vulnerable road users, often exposed to traffic injuries and fatalities. Low visibility, especially in poor lighting, is a key contributor to crashes. Although visibility materials such as reflective jackets, stickers, and lights help prevent injuries, awareness and consistent use remain limited in many low- and middle-income settings. Understanding awareness and uptake of visibility materials among riders is essential for designing targeted safety interventions in high-risk districts like Limbe and Tiko.MethodsA community-based cross-sectional study was conducted among 499 commercial motorcycle riders aged ≥18 years in Limbe and Tiko Health Districts, Cameroon. Severely ill persons were excluded. Participants were recruited consecutively at motorcycle pick-up points. Trained assistants used structured questionnaires to collect data on socio-demographics, riding habits, knowledge, attitudes, and use of visibility materials. Descriptive statistics and logistic regression were used for analysis. Ethical clearance was obtained from the University of Buea (2024/2490-03/UB/SG/IRB/FHS).ResultsOf the 499 riders, 254 (50.9%) wore reflective vests/jackets, and 99 (17.8%) motorcycles had reflective strips. Functional headlamps were present in 428 (85.8%), rear lights in 286 (57.3%), brake lights in 286 (57.3%), and all four trafficator lights in 219 (43.9%). Overall, 15.0% had high knowledge, 20.2% had positive attitudes, and 7.6% had good practices toward visibility and road safety. Factors significantly associated with visibility material use included past road traffic injury (aOR=1.87; 95%CI: 1.12-3.12), obeying traffic regulations (aOR=3.59; 95%CI: 2.31-5.57), willingness to spend money on materials (aOR=2.99; 95%CI: 1.84-4.88), and license ownership (aOR=1.71; 95%CI: 1.08-2.72).Key messages• Only 7.6% of commercial motorcyclists showed good practices toward visibility; low uptake highlights urgent need for targeted road safety interventions.• Use of visibility materials was significantly linked to prior road injury, traffic law obedience, and license ownership.

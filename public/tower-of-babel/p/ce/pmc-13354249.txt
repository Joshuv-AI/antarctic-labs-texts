Trustworthy AI models to predict prolonged hospital length-of-stay in orthopedic surgery
Authors: Farnaz Rezvani, Michael R. Kann, Fengyi Gao, Puneet Gupta, Nicole Myers, Armin Kashefi, Shyam Visweswaran, Yanshan Wang, MaCalus V. Hogan, Johannes F. Plate, Ahmad P. Tafti
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
We developed and validated a trustworthy and uncertainty-aware artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) framework for predicting prolonged hospital length-of-stay (LOS) in orthopedic inpatient surgery using exclusively preoperative clinical and procedural data from the National Surgical Quality Improvement Program (NSQIP). LOS prediction was formulated as a binary classification task using two clinically relevant thresholds (LOS > 2 days and LOS > 5 days), with primary emphasis on the 5-day cutoff due to its greater clinical interpretability. Random Forest, Multilayer Perceptron, Deep Neural Network, and XGBoost models were trained using CPT-based procedure groupings and evaluated using five-fold stratified cross-validation within the 85% training cohort, with final independent evaluation performed on a completely unseen 15% hold-out test set. Model performance was assessed using accuracy, macro-averaged precision, recall, F1-score, area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), and area under the precision-recall curve (AUPRC). Across models, the 5-day threshold demonstrated the most stable and clinically meaningful performance, while results under the 2-day threshold confirmed robustness to alternative outcome definitions. The Optuna-optimized XGBoost model achieved the strongest discrimination, favorable calibration, and narrow uncertainty bounds. Algorithmic fairness was evaluated across age, sex, and race using selection rates and true positive rates, with broadly consistent subgroup performance across demographic groups. Model explainability using SHAP and LIME identified case type, CPT category, anesthesia type, and baseline physiologic status (e.g., ASA class and hematocrit) as key drivers of prolonged LOS, while patient-level explanations supported clinical plausibility. This preoperative-only and trustworthy AI framework enables transparent and equitable risk stratification to support perioperative planning, optimized bed and discharge management, and data-driven utilization management and prior authorization workflows in orthopedic care.

Introduction

Orthopedic surgery is one of the most commonly performed categories of surgical procedures worldwide, driven by degenerative musculoskeletal conditions in older adults and sports-related injuries in younger populations1,2. These procedures restore mobility and quality of life but create substantial demand on inpatient surgical services. Hospital length-of-stay (LOS) is a critical outcome in orthopedic inpatient surgery, reflecting postoperative recovery and strongly influencing hospital resource utilization and overall healthcare costs3–8. Although advances in perioperative optimization and care pathways have reduced average LOS4, prolonged hospitalization remains a major contributor to postoperative complications, delayed recovery, and system-level financial burden9–14. Consequently, early identification of patients at risk for prolonged LOS is clinically and operationally important for data-driven decision-making, perioperative planning, and efficient hospital capacity management. Importantly, the definition of prolonged LOS varies across clinical, procedural, and administrative contexts, particularly in orthopedic surgery, where expected recovery trajectories differ substantially between procedure types (e.g., arthroscopy versus complex spinal reconstruction). Shorter LOS thresholds (e.g., 2 days) are commonly referenced in utilization management and inpatient versus outpatient designation frameworks, whereas longer thresholds may better capture clinically meaningful deviations from expected postoperative recovery and resource-intensive hospitalization4,5,12,15. Accordingly, rather than relying on a single universal threshold, this study evaluates LOS prediction using both 2-day and 5-day cutoffs to reflect complementary administrative and clinical perspectives across heterogeneous orthopedic inpatient procedures. In addition, procedure-level LOS distributions were analyzed across orthopedic Current Procedural Terminology (CPT) categories to better contextualize threshold interpretation relative to procedural complexity and expected recovery pathways.

Traditional approaches for predicting LOS and postoperative outcomes have relied on linear regression models or rule-based risk scores16–19. While explainable, these models are limited in capturing nonlinear relationships and high-order interactions among demographics, comorbidities, and procedural characteristics20,21. AI/ML approaches have increasingly leveraged structured preoperative clinical data and demonstrated improved predictive performance22–25. While prior orthopedic LOS prediction studies have primarily emphasized predictive discrimination and perioperative risk stratification, comparatively less attention has been given to the broader requirements necessary for trustworthy and clinically deployable AI systems. In particular, many existing studies focus predominantly on predictive accuracy without jointly evaluating algorithmic fairness, uncertainty quantification, calibration, and both global and patient-level explainability within a unified framework. Furthermore, relatively few prior orthopedic LOS studies have incorporated independent hold-out evaluation together with clinician-facing translational interfaces designed to support practical perioperative workflows. These challenges motivate integrated AI/ML frameworks that jointly address predictive performance, fairness, explainability, and uncertainty awareness. Disparities across age, sex, and race have been reported in orthopedic care and outcomes26,27, underscoring the importance of fairness evaluation using standardized metrics such as selection rate (demographic parity) and true positive rate (TPR) (equal opportunity)28–32. Explainability further supports clinician trust and usability. Global methods such as SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) quantify feature contributions at the population level33, while local methods such as Local Interpretable Model-Agnostic Explanations (LIME) provide patient-level explainability34. In parallel, uncertainty quantification is increasingly recognized as a core component of trustworthy clinical AI, enabling confidence-aware decision-making and identification of unreliable predictions35–37. Together, explainability and uncertainty estimation complement accuracy-focused modeling and strengthen clinical accountability.

In this study, we propose a trustworthy and uncertainty-aware AI/ML framework for predicting prolonged hospital LOS among orthopedic inpatient surgical cases using preoperative data exclusively. Accordingly, the present study extends beyond conventional LOS prediction by integrating predictive performance with fairness analysis, uncertainty-aware evaluation, explainable AI/ML, and lightweight workflow-oriented deployment considerations within a single orthopedic AI/ML framework. Importantly, limited clinical adoption of trustworthy AI/ML systems cannot be explained by model transparency alone. Even AI/ML frameworks incorporating explainability, fairness assessment, and uncertainty quantification continue to face substantial implementation-science barriers, including workflow integration challenges, interoperability with electronic health record systems, clinician trust and cognitive burden, legal and liability considerations, reimbursement structures, and institutional governance requirements. In surgical and perioperative settings, AI/ML systems must therefore demonstrate not only predictive validity and interpretability, but also operational feasibility and alignment with real-world clinical workflows. These broader translational challenges further motivate the development of lightweight, workflow-oriented, and clinician-centered AI/ML frameworks for orthopedic perioperative decision support.

Here and in this study, we utilized the National Surgical Quality Improvement Program (NSQIP)38 registry (2021–2023) and restricted model inputs to variables available preoperatively to support prospective deployment. To ensure both administrative relevance and clinical explainability, LOS prediction was formulated as a binary classification task using two clinically meaningful thresholds: LOS > 2 days and LOS > 5 days. While the 2-day cutoff aligns with commonly used inpatient utilization definitions, the 5-day cutoff represents a pragmatic definition of prolonged hospitalization that captures more substantial deviations from typical postoperative recovery trajectories. In particular, we report comparative predictive performance for both the 2-day and 5-day LOS thresholds to demonstrate model robustness across alternative outcome definitions. We evaluate multiple AI/ML models, assess discrimination and predictive accuracy, examine subgroup fairness across age, sex, and race, and provide both global and patient-level explanations using SHAP33 and LIME34. Accordingly, we summarize the technical and clinical significance of our contribution as follows: (1) Technical Significance: this work introduces a trustworthy and uncertainty-aware AI/ML framework for preoperative prediction of prolonged LOS in orthopedic inpatient surgery. The proposed approach integrates predictive modeling with AI/ML uncertainty quantification, fairness evaluation across demographic subgroups, and explainability using global and local explanation methods. (2) Clinical Significance: accurate and transparent preoperative LOS prediction can support perioperative planning, improve hospital resource allocation and utilization, and inform utilization management and prior authorization workflows. Uncertainty-aware and explainable predictions facilitate clinician trust, while subgroup-level fairness assessment supports equitable care delivery in orthopedic surgery.

Results

This section presents a series of experimental evaluations assessing the performance, explainability, uncertainty quantification, and fairness of the AI/ML models. Unless otherwise specified, all experimental results are reported using both out-of-fold (OOF) cross-validation (85%), plus the independent hold-out test set (15%) to assess model robustness and generalizability on unseen data. Additionally, evaluations were conducted using two prolonged LOS definitions (LOS > 5 days and LOS > 2 days) to examine model stability across alternative outcome thresholds. Python programming, coupled with the Google Colab platform, served as the exclusive computational platform for conducting these experiments. AI/ML model performance comparison

All evaluated AI/ML models exhibited strong discriminative performance for predicting prolonged hospital LOS using the 5-day cutoff. Performance metrics for 5-day LOS cutoff models are summarized in Table 1a and b. As a complementary analysis, performance metrics using a 2-day LOS cutoff are summarized in Table 1c and d. Although macro-averaged metrics were higher under this threshold, overall accuracy was lower than that observed with the 5-day cutoff. This pattern is expected because the 2-day threshold yields a different class balance and a less extreme definition of prolonged hospitalization, which can shift the trade-off between accuracy and macro-averaged metrics.

Across modeling approaches, performance was consistently high. The Optuna-optimized XGBoost model achieved the strongest overall discrimination, with an accuracy of 0.843 and an AUC of 0.847. The Random Forest (RF) model demonstrated a comparable accuracy of 0.841 and an AUC of 0.842. Similarly, the Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) achieved an accuracy of 0.839 and an AUC of 0.839, while the deep neural network (DNN) yielded an accuracy of 0.841 and an AUC of 0.843. Overall, performance differences across models were modest, with accuracy and AUC values falling within a narrow range. Independent hold-out test set evaluation demonstrated similarly strong performance across models. The Optuna-optimized XGBoost model achieved the strongest overall discrimination, with the highest accuracy (0.845) and AUC (0.853). The RF model demonstrated a comparable accuracy of 0.842 and an AUC of 0.847. Similarly, the MLP achieved an accuracy of 0.841 and an AUC of 0.846, while the DNN yielded an accuracy of 0.843 and an AUC of 0.849. This consistency indicates that prolonged LOS can be reliably predicted using preoperative data across diverse modeling paradigms. Nevertheless, the Optuna-optimized XGBoost model consistently provided the strongest discrimination, supporting its selection as the final model for subsequent fairness and explainability analyses. To further evaluate performance under class imbalance for LOS > 5 days, the AUPRC39 was also computed. The observed AUPRC values (0.559–0.589) exceed the baseline prevalence, supporting effective identification of the minority class. In contrast, AUPRC values for the LOS > 2 days threshold were substantially higher (0.842–0.848), reflecting the higher prevalence of the positive class and a less imbalanced outcome distribution. As AUPRC is sensitive to class imbalance, this increase does not necessarily indicate superior model discrimination but rather the effect of reduced imbalance.

Statistical comparison of model performance

To assess whether observed differences in discriminative performance were statistically significant among AUCs, pairwise comparisons of AUCs were conducted using DeLong’s40 test. For each comparison, absolute differences in AUC (ΔAUC) were computed between the Optuna-optimized XGBoost model and each comparator model. Using DeLong’s test, the Optuna-optimized XGBoost model demonstrated consistently higher discriminative performance than all comparator models. As shown in Table 2, for the LOS > 5 days OOF cross-validation analysis, the Optuna-optimized XGBoost model achieved an AUC of 0.847, compared with 0.842 for RF, 0.839 for MLP, and 0.843 for the DNN model, corresponding to absolute AUC improvements (ΔAUC) of 0.0050, 0.0075, and 0.0032, respectively. Similarly, in the independent hold-out test set for LOS > 5 days, the Optuna-optimized XGBoost model achieved the highest AUC of 0.853, compared with 0.847 for RF, 0.846 for MLP, and 0.849 for the DNN model, corresponding to absolute AUC improvements (ΔAUC) of 0.0054, 0.0064, and 0.0036, respectively. All pairwise differences were statistically significant (all two-sided DeLong p ≪ 0.001), indicating that the observed performance gains of the Optuna-optimized XGBoost model are highly unlikely to be attributable to chance. Details of the pairwise statistical comparisons for LOS > 5 days are summarized in Table 2a and b. Analogous pairwise AUC comparisons for the LOS > 2 days definition are reported in Table 2c and d.

AI/ML model uncertainty and calibration analysis

To assess the reliability and robustness of our proposed AI/ML model predictions, uncertainty and calibration analysis were conducted using OOF cross-validation estimates41,42. Predictive uncertainty was quantified by computing 95% confidence interval (CI) for key performance metrics via bootstrap resampling. Regarding the 5-day threshold, the Optuna-optimized XGBoost model demonstrated stable performance across resampling iterations in the OOF cross-validation analysis, achieving an accuracy of 0.843 (95% CI: 0.841–0.844) and an AUC of 0.847 (95% CI: 0.844–0.849) (Table 1a). Evaluation on the independent hold-out test set demonstrated highly consistent performance, with an accuracy of 0.845 (95% CI: 0.841–0.849) and an AUC of 0.853 (95% CI: 0.847–0.858) (Table 1b). The narrow width of these CIs indicates limited performance variability and supports the robustness of the selected model.

Similarly, for the 2-day threshold, the Optuna-optimized XGBoost model achieved an accuracy of 0.782 (95% CI: 0.780–0.784) and an AUC of 0.848 (95% CI: 0.847–0.850) in the OOF cross-validation analysis (Table 1c). Consistent findings were observed on the independent hold-out test set, with an accuracy of 0.779 (95% CI: 0.775–0.784) and an AUC of 0.846 (95% CI: 0.842–0.851) (Table 1d), demonstrating comparable discrimination under an alternative LOS definition. Calibration curves and corresponding Brier scores are shown in Figure 1a–d, where (a, c) correspond to LOS > 5 days and (b, d) correspond to LOS > 2 days. Corresponding results for the independent hold-out test set are shown in Fig. 2a–d.

Model calibration was evaluated using reliability diagrams and the Brier score43,44, which jointly assess the agreement between predicted probabilities and observed outcome frequencies. Figure 1a illustrates the calibration curve for the Optuna-optimized XGBoost model based on OOF cross-validation predictions. Predicted probabilities closely followed the diagonal reference line, indicating good probabilistic calibration across risk strata. Additionally, Brier scores for all models are reported in Fig. 1c. Among the evaluated models, the Optuna-optimized XGBoost achieved the lowest Brier score (0.1106), reflecting a low mean squared error between predicted probabilities and true outcomes, with lower values indicating better probabilistic accuracy. Consistent with earlier findings, evaluation on the independent hold-out test set (Fig. 2c) yielded a comparable Brier score (0.1092), indicating minimal performance degradation and reinforcing the robustness and generalizability of the model’s calibration. These findings suggest that the Optuna-optimized XGBoost model provides not only strong discriminative performance but also well-calibrated and reliable probability estimates, supporting its use in clinical decision-support contexts where uncertainty awareness is essential.

To further assess calibration performance across demographic subgroups, subgroup-specific calibration curves were generated for both OOF cross-validation and independent hold-out test analyses (Figs. 3 and 4). For LOS > 5 days, subgroup calibration analyses across sex and age groups demonstrated close alignment with the diagonal reference line in both analyses (Figs. 3a, c and 4a, c), indicating stable calibration behavior with minimal deviation and good agreement between predicted probabilities and observed outcomes across demographic subgroups. Similar subgroup calibration patterns were observed for LOS > 2 days (Figs. 3b, d and 4b, d), where predicted probabilities generally remained well-aligned with observed event frequencies across sex and age groups. However, slightly greater variability was observed in certain age strata at intermediate probability levels, particularly for the LOS > 2 days threshold. With that, calibration patterns were highly consistent across analyses, supporting the robustness and generalizability of the proposed model across clinically relevant thresholds and demographic subgroups.

AI/ML fairness analysis

Algorithmic fairness of the Optuna-optimized XGBoost model was evaluated across age, sex, and race subgroups using the equal opportunity criterion, quantified by subgroup-specific TPR28,39,45. All fairness metrics were computed with respect to the positive outcome class, defined as prolonged LOS. Figure 5a summarizes TPR values for LOS > 5 days in the OOF cross-validation analysis, stratified by: (A) age group, (B) sex, and (C) race. The corresponding test results for LOS > 5 days are shown in Fig. 6a. Complementary fairness results under the LOS > 2 days definition are shown in Figs. 5b and 6b.

Across age strata, TPRs were largely consistent, ranging from 19.2 to 55.0% in the Fig. 5a and from 19.4 to 55.1% in the independent hold-out test set (Fig. 6a), indicating comparable sensitivity for identifying patients with prolonged LOS across younger and older age groups. No substantial age-related disparity in true-positive detection was observed under the equal opportunity definition. Sex-stratified analysis demonstrated similar TPRs for the two primary sex groups (39.9% for females and 44.5% for males (Fig. 5a); 40.3 and 45.3%, respectively, in the hold-out test set (Fig. 6a)). A lower TPR was observed in the third sex category (36.4%) in Fig. 5a, whereas a higher TPR was observed in the hold-out test set (50.0%) (Fig. 6a); however, this estimate should be interpreted with caution due to the very limited sample size in this subgroup, which may result in unstable performance estimates. Race-based evaluation revealed modest variability in TPRs across racial subgroups, with values ranging from 30.7 to 56.9% in Fig. 5a and from 27.3 to 59.0% in the independent hold-out test set (Fig. 6a). Importantly, no racial subgroup exhibited near-zero sensitivity, suggesting that the model consistently identified patients with prolonged hospitalization across demographic groups. Subgroup-level TPR, positive prediction rates (PPRs) (selection rates), and sample sizes (n) for LOS > 5 days across both OOF cross-validation predictions and the independent hold-out test set are reported in Table 3a. The corresponding subgroup-level metrics for the LOS > 2 days definition are reported in Table 3b. Taken together, fairness patterns remained broadly consistent across both analyses (Figs. 5 and 6). Although greater variability was observed in several demographic subgroups within the smaller hold-out test set, these fluctuations were primarily associated with limited subgroup sample sizes and did not indicate substantial systematic performance disparities across demographic groups.

Global and local AI/ML explainability

Global explainability was assessed using SHAP33 to quantify feature contributions to prolonged LOS prediction (Fig. 7a, b). The SHAP analysis identified case type (CASETYPE), preoperative hematocrit (PRHCT), anesthesia type (ANESTHES), and CPT category as dominant procedural and physiologic drivers, with higher procedural complexity generally associated with increased prolonged LOS risk. Patient vulnerability variables, including ASA class, race, and age, also showed strong positive contributions. Additionally, laboratory measures such as PRWBC and FNSTATUS2 demonstrated secondary yet meaningful effects consistent with baseline inflammatory status. To assess whether key drivers of prolonged LOS were consistent under alternative threshold definitions, we additionally report SHAP summary plots for the LOS > 5 days outcome (Fig. 7a) along with LOS > 2 days outcome (Fig. 7b). With that, SHAP results support that the optimized XGBoost model relies on clinically explainable predictors aligned with procedural complexity and preoperative physiologic risk.

Local explainability was assessed using LIME34 to provide patient-level explanations by approximating model behavior in the neighborhood of an individual prediction (Fig. 7c, d). Figure 7c shows a representative case in which the model predicted prolonged LOS with high confidence (Class 0: 0.91 vs. Class 1: 0.09, with LOS > 5 days). The prediction was primarily supported by lower ASA class, favorable preoperative hematocrit (PRHCT), preserved functional status (FNSTATUS2), no transfusion (TRANSFUS) or oxygen support requirement (OXYGEN-SUPPORT), lower-risk anesthesia category (ANESTHES), and younger age. Notably, no single feature strongly contributed toward prolonged LOS, supporting internal consistency and clinical plausibility of the individualized explanation.

To further assess the clinical plausibility of the explainability outputs, SHAP and LIME explanations were qualitatively reviewed by domain experts within the multidisciplinary study team in our group, including two orthopedic surgeons familiar with perioperative risk assessment and inpatient orthopedic workflows. The identified high-impact features, including procedural complexity, ASA class, anesthesia type, hematocrit, and physiologic status variables, were considered broadly consistent with established orthopedic clinical reasoning and perioperative risk stratification principles. While this expert review does not constitute a formal prospective explainability validation study, it provides an initial domain-expert-in-the-loop assessment supporting the face validity and clinical interpretability of the proposed AI/ML framework.

Application interface overview

A lightweight clinician-facing decision-support software application was developed to support preoperative LOS risk prediction and interpretation (Fig. 8). Implemented in Streamlit46, the interface enables interactive exploration of AI/ML model outputs by presenting predicted LOS risk together with explainability outputs to support clinical contextualization. The interface also supports the LOS > 2 days outcome definition, enabling clinicians to explore model outputs under both administrative (2-day) and prolonged hospitalization (5-day) thresholds. Users can select the predictive models (XGBoost, RF, DNN, or MLP) and outcome definition, and review patient-level preoperative inputs with estimated prolonged LOS probability. To enhance trustworthiness, the application integrates global summaries and patient-specific explanations, illustrating how key features influence predictions and supporting operationally actionable decision-making.

Discussion

The application of AI/ML in healthcare is often presented through predictive accuracy alone; however, successful clinical translation requires systems that are also trustworthy, explainable, fair, and uncertainty-aware. In orthopedic inpatient surgery, opaque models may undermine clinician confidence and risk inequitable decisions, limiting real-world adoption. Accordingly, our current contribution advances beyond accuracy-centric modeling by integrating AI/ML explainability, fairness evaluation, calibration analysis, and uncertainty quantification into a unified LOS prediction framework.

Prolonged hospital LOS imposes substantial burden in orthopedic surgery, contributing to increased complications, delayed rehabilitation, bed-capacity strain, and financial impact. Early identification of high-risk patients can support perioperative planning, resource allocation, discharge coordination, and payer-facing processes such as utilization management and prior authorization. Notably, procedure-level LOS patterns summarized in Table 5 further support that prolonged hospitalization is highly dependent on procedural complexity and case urgency, reinforcing the need for clinically grounded LOS thresholds.

Using NSQIP38 data, we developed a preoperative-only AI/ML framework for prolonged LOS prediction. Across evaluated models, the Optuna-optimized XGBoost achieved the strongest discrimination (AUC of 0.853 on the independent hold-out test set), while also demonstrating favorable calibration, narrow uncertainty bounds, and clinically interpretable explanations. SHAP and LIME analyses confirmed that the model primarily relied on physiologic, perioperative, and procedural variables aligned with established orthopedic clinical reasoning. In parallel, fairness analyses demonstrated generally consistent subgroup performance across age, sex, and race categories, supporting the equity-aware design of the proposed framework.

An important consideration in interpreting these findings relates to how prolonged hospitalization is defined in both clinical and administrative contexts. While administrative frameworks such as those used by the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS)15 often reference shorter inpatient stays for reimbursement and utilization management purposes, LOS thresholds used for clinical risk stratification may warrant different considerations. Accordingly, this study evaluated alternative LOS cutoffs of 2 and 5 days. Across orthopedic CPT categories, the 5-day threshold demonstrated more stable and consistent predictive performance, as well as clearer clinical explainability, compared with the shorter threshold. From a clinical standpoint, a 5-day LOS threshold also better aligns with contemporary orthopedic inpatient recovery pathways, in which uncomplicated elective procedures are typically discharged within 1–3 days. In contrast, LOS extending beyond 5 days often reflects meaningful deviations from expected postoperative recovery, including medical complications, delayed mobilization, complex discharge disposition planning (e.g., skilled nursing facility placement), or the need for extended inpatient rehabilitation coordination. Therefore, the 5-day cutoff provides a clinically intuitive marker of resource-intensive hospitalization and improved explainability for perioperative planning and operational decision-making. At the same time, reporting results under the 2-day cutoff remains valuable for benchmarking against utilization-oriented definitions and for demonstrating model robustness under an alternative LOS outcome definition.

This study, however, carries some limitations. First, NSQIP lacks institution-level pathway variation, social determinants of health (SDOH), and granular postoperative rehabilitation factors that may influence LOS. In particular, key SDOH variables such as socioeconomic status, insurance coverage, transportation access, caregiver support, health literacy, and neighborhood-level resource disparities are not captured. Although limited demographic variables are available, these do not fully represent the broader structural and social drivers of prolonged hospitalization. Consequently, while subgroup fairness analyses demonstrated generally consistent performance across measured demographic variables, these findings should not be interpreted as guaranteeing the absence of broader structural or socioeconomic bias. Thus, incorporating SDOH-aware and multi-institutional modeling frameworks will be important for more comprehensive evaluation of equity and fairness in orthopedic perioperative AI/ML systems. Second, although the modeling framework incorporated stratified evaluation and macro-averaged metrics to address class imbalance, future work may further investigate data-level resampling strategies and alternative imbalance-aware learning approaches to assess their impact on minority-class performance and calibration. Third, although the proposed lightweight software application demonstrates the translational potential of the framework, it should currently be interpreted as a research-oriented and clinician-support prototype rather than a clinically deployed system. Importantly, formal evaluation of the clinician-facing interface remains an important next step.

Future work will assess the interface through clinician-centered usability studies, workflow integration analysis, and qualitative feedback from orthopedic surgeons, anesthesiologists, perioperative care teams, and utilization-management stakeholders. Such studies will help determine how probabilistic risk estimates, explainability outputs, and dual LOS thresholds (2-day vs. 5-day) influence clinical decision-making, trust, cognitive burden, and operational planning in real-world clinical settings. Future prospective validation will also include pilot deployment in clinical settings, where model predictions will be evaluated alongside standard care to assess their impact on discharge planning, workflow efficiency, and perioperative decision-making. A potential translational pathway for the proposed framework may involve several sequential stages, including: (1) external multicenter validation across geographically and operationally diverse healthcare systems; (2) prospective “silent-mode” deployment studies to assess real-world calibration, temporal stability, and workflow compatibility prior to active implementation; (3) clinician-centered usability and pragmatic workflow-integration evaluations; and (4) future consideration of regulatory, governance, and institutional oversight pathways relevant to AI-enabled clinical decision-support systems. As part of our future work, we will also investigate procedure-specific, ordinal, or time-to-event LOS modeling strategies to provide more granular and specialty-specific risk stratification.

Beyond orthopedic surgery, the broader methodological principles underlying the proposed trustworthy and explainable LOS prediction framework may also be adaptable to other inpatient clinical domains in which prolonged recovery and resource-intensive hospitalization are common, including cardiac surgery, neurosurgical spine procedures, transplant care, and oncology admissions. However, we do not suggest that the present orthopedic-specific model or feature set can be directly transferred across these clinically distinct populations without retraining and domain-specific validation. Rather, the broader framework integrating structured preoperative clinical variables with fairness evaluation, calibration analysis, uncertainty quantification, and explainable AI may provide a foundation for developing analogous clinically responsible prediction systems in other inpatient specialties. Looking ahead, we should investigate privacy-preserving multi-site learning paradigms, including federated learning, while incorporating SDOH variables, longitudinal EHR-based recovery measures, and postoperative care trajectories to support more dynamic and personalized perioperative risk prediction. In addition, extending LOS prediction toward cost forecasting, bundled-payment optimization, and system-level operational analytics may further strengthen the healthcare system’s relevance of the proposed framework.

Taken together, this work emphasizes a broader shift in healthcare AI from systems that merely “predict” toward systems that also explain, quantify uncertainty, support fairness, and align with real-world clinical workflows. By demonstrating a transparent, fair, and uncertainty-aware AI/ML framework for LOS prediction in orthopedic surgery, this study provides a step toward clinically responsible and operationally actionable AI-enabled perioperative care.

The primary outcome was total LOS (days from admission to discharge; Table 6b). LOS prediction was formulated as binary classification using two thresholds: LOS > 2 days (administrative/utilization-relevant) and LOS > 5 days (primary prolonged LOS definition). The 5-day cutoff was prioritized due to stronger clinical explainability and more stable predictive performance across CPT categories (see Table 1a), while the 2-day cutoff was used to assess robustness under a shorter threshold. The outcome distribution was imbalanced, consistent with real-world orthopedic cohorts, and this was explicitly addressed during AI/ML model evaluation.

Continuous variables were median-imputed when missingness was limited, a simple and robust approach that is less sensitive to outliers and suitable for skewed clinical data distributions47. Missingness across predictors varied by variable. While most demographic and clinical variables had low missingness (generally <5%), certain laboratory variables exhibited higher rates of missing data. Given the large sample size and heterogeneous missingness across variables, a simple median-based imputation strategy was considered for this study, while records with missing categorical values that could not be mapped were excluded. Categorical predictors were harmonized and numerically encoded. Feature scaling was model-specific: tree-based models used raw continuous values (scale-invariant), whereas artificial neural network models applied within-fold z-score standardization to prevent data leakage and ensure fair model comparison39,48,49.

AI/ML models

Four supervised AI/ML models, including RF50, MLP51,52, DNN53, and XGBoost54 were trained to predict prolonged LOS. RF served as an ensemble baseline, MLP/DNN assessed neural predictors under standardized preprocessing, and XGBoost was selected as the primary tree-based approach due to strong performance on structured clinical data and compatibility with explainability methods. All models used identical features and evaluation protocols.

AI/ML model development, training, and validation

Model development followed a two-stage validation strategy. First, the full dataset was partitioned using an 85/15 stratified train-test split to preserve outcome balance and maintain overall cohort characteristics and feature distributions (SEX, Age, Year, and BMI) across subsets. The 15% hold-out test set was completely excluded from all model development procedures and reserved solely for final independent evaluation. Second, the remaining 85% training set was used for model development and comparison through five-fold stratified cross-validation. Within this framework, OOF cross-validation41,42 predicted probabilities were generated for internal performance evaluation, calibration analysis, CI estimation, and fairness assessment. After model selection, the final Optuna-optimized XGBoost model was retrained on the full training set and evaluated on the independent hold-out test set to assess generalizability beyond cross-validation. Within this framework, XGBoost hyperparameters were optimized using Optuna55 with mean cross-validated AUC as the objective. Key hyperparameters, including tree depth, learning rate, subsampling, column sampling, and regularization, were tuned to improve generalization and reduce overfitting. Neural network models (MLP and DNN) incorporated within-fold early stopping based on validation loss to prevent overtraining.

To address class imbalance during model development, we adopted imbalance-aware data partitioning and evaluation strategies. Specifically, both the 85/15 train-test split and the five-fold cross-validation procedure were stratified on the binary LOS outcome to preserve class proportions across all partitions. In addition, macro-averaged precision, recall, and F1-score were reported to ensure balanced evaluation across both majority and minority classes. OOF cross-validation predicted probabilities were retained for threshold-independent discrimination assessment, calibration analysis, and bootstrap-based CI estimation. Given the focus of this study on developing a well-calibrated and clinically interpretable preoperative prediction framework, the primary modeling configuration prioritized overall discrimination, calibration performance, and generalization on the independent hold-out test set. Given the large sample size, along with the use of stratified cross-validation and macro-averaged performance metrics, the modeling framework was designed to support balanced performance evaluation across classes in this study, without the need for explicit data-level resampling techniques.

Although a direct temporal split (e.g., training on earlier years and testing on later years) was not applied, the data were partitioned in a way that preserved the distribution of calendar years (2021–2023) across the training, validation, and test sets. This was achieved through stratified sampling, helping to reduce potential temporal bias and maintain model generalizability within the study period.

Metrics for AI/ML model performance evaluation

Model performance was assessed using multiple complementary metrics, including overall accuracy, macro-averaged precision, recall, and F1-score, as well as the AUC and AUPRC39,56. Macro-averaged metrics were reported to account for outcome imbalance and ensure balanced evaluation across classes. All performance metrics were computed separately for the LOS > 2 days and LOS > 5 days outcome definitions to enable direct comparison across LOS thresholds.

AI/ML model uncertainty estimation

Uncertainty was quantified via 95% CI computed using nonparametric bootstrap resampling of OOF cross-validation predictions39,41,42; metrics were recomputed across bootstrap iterations, and percentile-based CIs were reported. Bootstrap-based CIs were computed separately for the 2-day and 5-day LOS cutoffs using OOF cross-validation predictions.

AI/ML model calibration analysis

Calibration was evaluated using reliability diagrams (calibration curves) and the Brier score43,44 computed from OOF cross-validation predicted probabilities. Calibration curves assessed agreement between predicted and observed risk across probability bins, while the Brier score quantified probabilistic accuracy.

AI/ML model fairness evaluation

AI/ML fairness was assessed for the final Optuna-optimized XGBoost model across demographic subgroups defined by age, sex, and race. In this study, AI/ML fairness was evaluated using PPR and TPR, reflecting complementary clinical and ethical considerations57,58. PPR, defined as the proportion of patients predicted to experience prolonged LOS, captures how frequently an intervention or heightened clinical attention would be triggered across demographic subgroups. In the context of preoperative LOS risk stratification, this metric directly corresponds to operational decisions such as care escalation, resource allocation, and utilization management, making it clinically explainable as the rate at which patients are selected for downstream action. TPR was selected to evaluate equal opportunity, measuring the model’s ability to correctly identify patients who truly experience prolonged hospitalization. This metric is particularly relevant in clinical settings where failing to identify high-risk patients may result in delayed interventions, suboptimal discharge planning, or preventable complications. This choice aligns with established fairness frameworks for clinical decision-making systems and avoids over-reliance on metrics that may be less actionable in healthcare settings28,39,45,57,58.

AI/ML model explainability

Global and local AI/ML explainability were applied to the Optuna-optimized XGBoost model using SHAP33 (feature importance and directionality) and LIME34 (patient-level explanations) to support clinical explainability.

Lightweight interactive application for clinical workflow integration

A lightweight clinician-facing decision-support interface was developed for preoperative LOS risk stratification (Fig. 8). This software interface provides probabilistic predictions and integrates global and patient-specific explainability to support perioperative planning and shared decision-making.

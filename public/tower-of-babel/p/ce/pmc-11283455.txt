Effect of phenylephrine versus ephedrine on the incidence of postoperative delirium in olderly adults undergoing knee arthroplasty under general anesthesia: a single-center trial
Authors: Changjian Zheng, Bin Wang, Jiuzhou Fu, Hui Peng, Yongquan Chen, Xianwen Hu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn addition to stabilizing blood pressure (BP), ephedrine and phenylephrine have distinct effects on regional cerebral oxygen saturation (rSO2). However, whether its effect on rSO2 affects the occurrence of postoperative delirium (POD) remains unclear. Therefore, the aim of this study is to compare the effects of ephedrine and phenylephrine for BP maintenance on the incidence of POD in olderly adults who underwent knee arthroplasty under general anesthesia. One hundred twenty patients who were between 60 and 90 years old and underwent knee arthroplasty were included in this study. The patients were randomly divided into two groups: the ephedrine group and the phenylephrine group. After anesthesia induction, ephedrine and phenylephrine were continuously infused to maintain the intraoperative mean arterial pressure within the normal range (baseline mean arterial pressure ± 20%). The primary outcome measures included the incidence of POD within 1–3 days after surgery. The incidence of POD on the first day after surgery was lower in the ephedrine group than in the phenylephrine group (33% vs. 7%, P < 0.001). However, there was no significant difference in the incidence of POD between the two groups on the second and third postoperative days. Compared with the phenylephrine group, the ephedrine group experienced significantly greater cardiac output (CO) and rSO2 (P < 0.05).Clinical Trials Registry: ChiCTR2200064849, principal investigator: Changjian Zheng.Subject terms: Medical research, Signs and symptoms

Introduction

POD is a common neurological complication in olderly adults who undergo surgery. It is a short-term condition characterized by an acute-onset, attention disorders, disordered levels of consciousness, changes in cognitive function, and significant fluctuations1,2. It typically manifests within three days following surgical procedures3,4. POD can prolong recovery, exacerbate complications, delay discharge, and increase hospital expenses; moreover, it is an independent risk factor for long-term postoperative cognitive dysfunction (POCD) and short-term mortality5,6.

Cerebral perfusion and oxygenation imbalance are among the most important causes of POD7,8. Phenylephrine and ephedrine are commonly used perioperative vasoactive drugs. However, these two drugs have different effects on cerebral oxygenation, with phenylephrine decreasing CO and rSO2 in healthy patients and ephedrine remaining stable9,10. A systematic review revealed that the use of phenylephrine during anesthesia to treat intraoperative hypotension led to a sustained decrease in cerebral oxygen saturation and CO11. A more recent study revealed that ephedrine improved cerebral blood flow and oxygenation in the brain tissues of patients with brain tumors and that it was superior to phenylephrine in terms of improving cerebral macro- and micro-hemodynamics and oxygenation12. However, whether this heterogeneity leads to adverse clinical consequences for frail olderly adults has not been studied.

In this investigation, we administered these two drugs during knee arthroplasty to examine their impact on the incidence of POD. On the basis of these findings, we hypothesized that the ephedrine group would have higher rSO2 and CO levels during surgery, which would be correlated with a lower incidence of POD.

Methods

This randomized, double-blind, controlled study was conducted from October 2022 to July 2023 in accordance with the principles of the Helsinki Declaration. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Wannan Medical College, Wuhu, China [Approval No. 2022 Ethics Research No. 46]. Prior to patient enrollment (ChiCTR2200064849), the trial was registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry. All patients provided written informed consent, and randomization was performed by a statistician who used a random number generator.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: between 60 and 90 years old, underwent knee arthroplasty, classified as ASA I-III, and considered to have class II or III functional status according to the New York Heart Association.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: severe cardiac, pulmonary, hepatic, or renal dysfunction; a history of psychiatric illness or long-term use of psychotropic medications (for diseases such as dementia or schizophrenia); chronic use of analgesic medications; a positive preoperative Confusion Assessment Method (CAM) examination; a preoperative score ≥ 8 on the Short Portable Mental Status Questionnaire (SPMSQ), severe cognitive impairment (MMSE score < 27) (Supplementary Material); diabetes with severe complications (diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperosmolar coma, various infections, large vessel disease, diabetic nephropathy); severe infections; unable to communicate verbally; and unable to complete cognitive function tests.

Randomization and Blinding

Upon obtaining informed consent from the patients, a researcher randomly allocated the patients into two groups using a random number method. The anesthesiologist monitored the MAP intraoperatively and took measures to adjust the intraoperative MAP based on the patient’s baseline MAP. The outcome assessors, surgical team, and anesthetist were blinded to the group assignments.

General anesthesia and analgesia management

Upon admission, peripheral intravenous access was established, and the electrocardiogram and oxygen saturation were routinely monitored. The radial artery was punctured after successful local anesthesia. After successful radial artery puncture, the catheter used for arterial pressure monitoring was connected to the Vigileo monitor (Model: UTC-W1080, ADVANTECH) via a Flotrac sensor, and the patient's CO was continuously monitored based on the patient’s peripheral arterial pressure after inputting the patient’s sex, age, height, and weight. For the measurement of rSO2 (Covidien IIc Co., USA), two sensors for near-infrared spectroscopy were placed on the left and right sides of the patient’s forehead. The baseline CO and rSO2 levels were collected at different time points during the operation according to the test requirements. To maintain the appropriate depth of anesthesia, the bispectral index (BIS) (Covidien IIc Co., USA) was monitored. Prior to induction, the patient was preoxygenated with 100% pure oxygen for 3 min. Sufentanil (0.4 μg/kg), propofol, (1–2 mg/kg), and rocuronium (0.6 mg/kg) were administered intravenously. After the muscles were completely relaxed, a laryngeal mask (size 4 for males, size 3 for females) was placed, and anesthesia was sustained with a Drager anesthesia machine. Respiratory parameters were adjusted to maintain end-tidal carbon dioxide levels between 35 and 45 mmHg. Intraoperative anesthesia was maintained with propofol (4–8 mg/kg/h) and remifentanil (0.1–0.3 μg/kg/min). Every 30 min, a bolus of rocuronium (0.1 mg/kg) was administered intravenously to maintain muscle relaxation. The BIS was maintained between 40 and 60. Lactated Ringer's solution was used to maintain intraoperative fluid balance (the total volume was controlled at 5–7 ml/kg, and the infusion rate was controlled at 5–7 ml/kg/h). At the time of surgical suturing, 100 mg of flurbiprofen lipid was administered intravenously with 0.9% saline. After surgery, anesthesia maintenance drugs were discontinued, and early recovery management was initiated in the postanaesthesia care unit (PACU). Patients with a Steward score of 6 or higher were transferred back to the ward. A postoperative analgesic device was connected and administered a continuous infusion of a mixture of 2 μg/kg of sufentanil and 4 mg of butorphanol tartrate in 100 ml of solution at a rate of 3 ml/h (bolus, 2 ml; lockout time, 30 min).

Perioperative hemodynamic management

The patient’s baseline mean arterial pressure (MAP) was determined as the average value of the MAP measured at 10:00 am in the ward before surgery. Noninvasive monitoring was conducted every 3 min during the surgery. After anesthesia induction, vasopressor drugs were continuously administered following the dosages reported in previous studies27,28. The ephedrine group received a continuous infusion of 5 mg/ml of ephedrine, and the phenylephrine group received a continuous infusion of 0.1 mg/ml of phenylephrine. Both groups were initially infused at a rate of 20 ml/h after anesthesia induction, and adjustments were made based on changes. The infusion drugs in both groups were discontinued before the skin was sutured.

Intraoperative hypertension was defined as a MAP increase of more than 10–20% above the baseline value, resulting in a reduction in the infusion rate to 10 ml/h. If the MAP increased by more than 20% above the baseline value, the infusion of vasopressor drugs was stopped. Intraoperative hypotension was defined as an MAP decrease of 10–20% below the baseline value, leading to an adjustment of the infusion rate to 40 ml/h. If the MAP decreased by more than 20% below the baseline value, a 6 mg bolus of ephedrine or 100 μg of phenylephrine was intravenously administered. In the case of bradycardia (defined as a heart rate less than 50 beats/min), 0.5 mg of atropine was administered. In the case of tachycardia (defined as a heart rate greater than 100 beats/min), 20 mg of esmolol was administered.

Outcome measures

The primary outcome measure of this study was the incidence of POD within 3 days after surgery. A professional assessor, who was unaware of the group allocation, evaluated the patients in the ward at noon each day after surgery using the confusion assessment method (CAM). The CAM consists of four criteria: (1) acute onset and fluctuating course, (2) inattention, (3) disorganized thinking, and (4) altered level of consciousness. A patient was considered CAM-positive for delirium if both criterion 1 and criterion 2 were present and if either criterion 3 or 4 was also present. The patients' rSO2, CO, MAP, and HR were recorded before anesthesia induction, 2 min after anesthesia induction (T1), 4 min after induction (T2), at the start of surgery (T3), 10 min (T4), 20 min (T5), 30 min (T6), 40 min (T7), 50 min (T8), and 60 min after the start of surgery (T9), and before the end of surgery (T10). Additionally, intraoperative anesthesia drug use data, surgical duration, PACU stay time, length of hospital stay, incidence of agitation, VAS pain scores, and postoperative complications were collected.

Sample size estimation

The sample size for this study was determined using PASS 2008 (NCSS, LLC, Kaysville, Utah, USA) software within the framework of a randomized controlled trial comprising two groups, the phenylephrine group and the ephedrine group, with POD as the primary outcome measure. A literature review revealed that the anticipated incidence rates of POD were 22% for the phenylephrine group and 4% for the ephedrine group29,30. To achieve 80% confidence in detecting a true difference with a bilateral significance level (ɑ) of 0.05, the software calculated a requisite sample size of 108 participants across both groups. Considering a 20% dropout rate, we ultimately included 130 patients in the group.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS version 22.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Continuous variables were assessed for a normal distribution using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Normally distributed data are presented as the mean and standard deviation, whereas nonnormally distributed data are presented as the median (25%-75% percentile). Categorical data are presented as frequencies and percentages and were analyzed via a two-tailed chi-square test or Fisher's exact test. For normally distributed groups, the t test was utilized, and for nonnormally distributed groups, the Mann–Whitney U test was employed. To compare continuous variables (MAP, HR, rSO2, CO) between the two groups at different time points, one-way ANOVA of variance was performed. A p value less than 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

Primary outcome measures

The incidence of POD on the first postoperative day was significantly lower in the ephedrine group than in the phenylephrine group [20 (33) vs. 4 (7), P < 0.001]. However, there were no significant differences in the incidence of POD between the two groups on the second postoperative day [4 (7) vs. 2 (3), P = 0.412] or the third postoperative day [1 (2) vs. 0, P = 0.315] (Table 3).

Secondary outcome measures

A comparison of the changes in the MAP, HR, CO, and rSO2 after anesthesia induction (Table 2, Fig. 2) revealed that the phenylephrine group experienced a decrease in the rSO2 (−8.4 ± 0.6%), whereas the ephedrine group experienced a milder decrease (−1.3 ± 0.6%). The change in rSO2 between the two groups was 7.2% (95% CI 6.0, 8.3; P < 0.001; Table 2, Fig. 2). Additionally, the phenylephrine group presented a decrease in CO (−0.40 ± 0.06 L/min), whereas the ephedrine group presented an increase in CO during surgery (0.45 ± 0.06 L/min). The change in CO (0.85 L/min) was statistically significant between the two groups (95% CI 0.69, 1.00; P < 0.001; Table 2, Fig. 2). Both groups had transient decreases in the MAP during surgery, but the overall changes were stable, with no statistically significant difference between the groups (95% CI 1.63, 3.23; P = 0.517; Table 2, Fig. 2). Furthermore, the phenylephrine group experienced a significant decrease in HR (−12 ± 1 beats/min), whereas the ephedrine group experience an increase in HR (10 ± 1 beats/min). The difference in HR between the two groups was 22 beats/min and was statistically significant (95% CI 19.5, 25.1; P < 0.001; Table 2, Fig. 2).

Postoperative complications

On the first postoperative day, there were 9 cases of nausea and vomiting in the phenylephrine group, whereas there were 3 cases in the ephedrine group (P = 0.068). Although there were no statistically significant differences in the postoperative visual analogue scale (VAS) pain scores between the groups, 14 patients in the phenylephrine group experienced emergence agitation in the PACU, whereas 4 patients in the ephedrine group experienced emergence agitation, which was statistically significant (P = 0.011). No other statistically significant differences were observed in terms of cardiovascular or neurological complications between the groups (Table 4).

Discussion

This study provides evidence that the use of ephedrine to manage the MAP is associated with lower incidences of agitation and POD on the first postoperative day, and the ephedrine group experienced higher CO and rSO2 levels.

Ephedrine and phenylephrine are both common vasoactive drugs, and our study revealed that ephedrine was associated with an increase in CO when administered intraoperatively, whereas phenylephrine was associated with a decrease in CO. A study on prophylactic phenylephrine infusion during spinal anesthesia revealed decreases in CO and cerebral oxygen saturation at various time points13. Another study comparing ephedrine and phenylephrine revealed that phenylephrine was associated with a decrease in CO, whereas ephedrine maintained the CO level14. In turn, a significant correlation was observed between rSO2 and CO. Reductions in CO may trigger compensatory increases in cerebral oxygen uptake, leading to a decrease in the mean frontal lobe rSO212. One possible mechanism is that CO indirectly causes changes in rSO2 by affecting cerebral perfusion, and cerebral oxygenation depends on the delivery of oxygen to the brain and cerebral oxygen consumption (CMRO2), which relies on cerebral blood flow (CBF) and arterial oxygen content. Studies have demonstrated that if the CMRO2 and arterial oxygen content remain constant, changes in the rSO2 will correlate with changes in CBF15. In our study, the doses of general anesthetics were relatively consistent as was the depth of anesthesia. Hence, CMRO2 can be approximated as a constant, and variations in CO directly impact the CBF. Therefore, the observed changes in rSO2 may be partly attributed to indirect fluctuations in CBF due to changes in CO. In addition, the rapid increase in arterial pressure induced by phenylephrine can lead to increased sympathetic nerve activity in the carotid ganglia16, and the small arteries in the brain are innervated mainly by sympathetic nerve fibers17. Therefore, the phenylephrine-induced increase in sympathetic nerve activity indirectly leads to cerebral vasoconstriction and may be related to the decrease in rSO2 in this study16.

The decrease in rSO2 in our study was significantly correlated with the incidence of POD, which is consistent with previous studies involving patients who underwent cardiac18 or orthopedic19 surgeries revealing a dose‒response relationship between cerebral oxygen saturation and postoperative neurocognitive disorders. Moreover, a recent large retrospective study indirectly supported this hypothesis, revealing that low peripheral oxygen saturation (closely linked to cerebral oxygen saturation) and low carbon dioxide levels (directly reflecting cerebral perfusion) increase the risk of POD8. Wang et al. reported a significant correlation between rSO2 and hypotension, leading to profound delirium20. Although our study also revealed a slight decrease in rSO2 in the ephedrine group, we consider that such small fluctuations in rSO2 are not yet sufficient to alter clinical outcomes. Moreover, there is significant heterogeneity in the effects of the two drugs on cerebral circulation, especially since this study focused on vulnerable populations. Similar studies have shown that the effects of vasoactive drugs on clinical outcomes in vulnerable populations is profound. A recent MRI-based study analyzed patients with brain tumors who received ephedrine or phenylephrine to achieve similar MAP endpoints. Compared with the phenylephrine group, the ephedrine group experienced better cerebral blood flow and cerebral tissue oxygenation. Additionally, a previous study revealed that phenylephrine disrupted microcirculation, resulting in reduced oxygen extraction, whereas ephedrine improved both macrocirculation and microcirculation in the brain, enhancing oxygen extraction14. Research has confirmed that the use of phenylephrine in patients with septic shock, especially those with persistent tachycardia, is associated with an increased mortality rate21,22.

In summary, this study reveals the different effects of ephedrine and phenylephrine on the incidence of POD in fragile olderly adults undergoing knee arthroplasty, where ephedrine can be associated with a reduction in the incidence of POD and may be related to perioperative changes in CO and rSO2. This study also has several limitations. First, this was a single-center, small-sample study, and multicenter, large-sample studies are still needed to further clarify the different roles of these drugs. Second, some patients in the phenylephrine group in this study received a single dose of atropine intraoperatively, and although the timing of atropine administration and dosage differed from those in previous studies23,24, the interference of atropine with the results cannot be completely ruled out. Third, in this study, intraoperative changes in CO and rSO2 were monitored with a Vigileo device and near-infrared spectroscopy, but imprecise monitoring and extracranial contamination may have interfered with the values25,26.

Study on changes in urinary relative supersaturation and risk of stone recurrence in patients after urolithiasis surgery
Authors: Kaisaier Aji, Munila Maimaijiang, Guanglu Song
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundUrolithiasis has a 50% 5-year recurrence rate. Post-operative changes in urinary relative supersaturation (RSS) may refine recurrence prediction and guide targeted interventions.MethodsProspective cohort of 68 stone-free adults (Jan 2023 – Jan 2024 enrollment). 24-hour urine was collected at 2 weeks, 1, 3, 6, 12 months; RSS patterns were analyzed using multivariate modeling. We used a validated food frequency questionnaire adapted for stone-forming nutrients. Primary outcome: radiologic and symptomatic stone recurrence; secondary: ≥30% RSS reduction (“biochemical response”). Risk scoring system developed using logistic regression probabilities. The study was approved by the institutional ethics committee with written informed consent.ResultsAt 24-month median follow-up (range 18–30), 14/68 experienced recurrence (20.6%). Independent predictors: persistently-high CaOx-RSS (OR 6.84, 95% CI 2.13–21.97), CaP-RSS > 1.5 on ≥ 2 visits (OR 4.38, 1.29–14.85), pH-reactive UA-RSS (OR 3.25, 1.04–10.18), urine volume < 1.5 L (OR 3.12, 1.21–8.03). Model performance: AUC 0.81 (95% CI 0.72–0.90); optimal threshold (p = 0.22) yielded sensitivity 0.74, specificity 0.78, PPV 0.46, NPV 0.92. RSS-guided intervention reduced recurrence versus standard care (11.8% vs. 29.4%, p = 0.027) with 44.5% absolute risk reduction in high-risk patients.ConclusionsSerial RSS patterns combined with clinical variables predict recurrence with good accuracy. RSS-tailored therapy significantly reduces recurrence in high-risk patients, though implementation requires addressing feasibility and cost-effectiveness considerations.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12894-025-01953-x.

Introduction

Urolithiasis represents a significant global health burden, affecting approximately 10% of the population worldwide with recurrence rates reaching 50% within 5 years after the initial episode [1]. This high recurrence rate substantially decreases patients’ quality of life and imposes considerable healthcare costs through emergency visits, hospitalizations, and repeat interventions [2]. While surgical interventions effectively address acute stone episodes, they do not alter the underlying metabolic abnormalities contributing to stone formation, highlighting the critical importance of postoperative preventive strategies.

Urinary supersaturation, the thermodynamic driving force for crystal formation and growth, has been recognized as fundamental in stone pathogenesis [3]. When the urinary environment becomes supersaturated with stone-forming salts such as calcium oxalate, calcium phosphate, or uric acid, crystallization occurs, potentially leading to stone formation. Relative supersaturation (RSS) provides a quantitative measure of this process, reflecting the relationship between the activity product of stone-forming salts and their thermodynamic solubility product [4]. As Parks and Coe note, “RSS offers a window into the lithogenic potential of urine” [5].

Several studies have demonstrated associations between elevated urinary RSS and increased stone risk [6]. However, significant gaps remain in understanding how postoperative RSS fluctuations correlate with recurrence risk, particularly across different stone compositions and surgical approaches. Standard postoperative protocols typically include a single 24-hour urine metabolic evaluation 6–8 weeks after surgery, which may fail to capture dynamic changes in urinary biochemistry during recovery and beyond [7]. This limited monitoring approach potentially misses critical windows for preventive intervention, especially since RSS values can fluctuate significantly within the first postoperative year [8].

Current prevention approaches primarily employ empirical dietary recommendations and medications based on stone composition, with limited personalization based on individual metabolic profiles [9]. While these generalized approaches show moderate effectiveness, persistently high recurrence rates suggest the need for more targeted interventions guided by precise metabolic monitoring. As Ferraro et al. emphasize, “The heterogeneity of stone disease necessitates individualized approaches to prevention, guided by comprehensive metabolic evaluation and ongoing monitoring” [10].

This research addresses these gaps through longitudinal RSS monitoring after urolithiasis surgery, characterizing fluctuation patterns and their relationship to recurrence risk. We investigate whether specific RSS change patterns predict recurrence for different stone compositions and evaluate a targeted intervention protocol adjusting treatment based on RSS monitoring. By developing a risk stratification system based on serial RSS measurements and clinical factors, we aim to provide clinically applicable tools for personalized stone prevention that can reduce the burden of recurrent disease.

Methodology

Participants

This prospective cohort study enrolled 68 adult patients undergoing surgical treatment for urolithiasis at University Medical Center between January 2023 and January 2024. All patients were followed for a minimum of 18 months (median 24 months, range 18–30 months), ensuring every participant contributed at least 12 months of outcome data. Eligible participants were aged 18–70 years with radiologically confirmed urinary stones requiring surgical intervention (extracorporeal shock wave lithotripsy, ureteroscopy, or percutaneous nephrolithotomy). Patients were included if complete stone clearance was confirmed by postoperative imaging.

Exclusion criteria comprised: residual fragments > 2 mm, anatomical abnormalities of the urinary tract, solitary kidney, renal insufficiency (estimated glomerular filtration rate < 60 mL/min/1.73 m²), active urinary tract infection, concomitant metabolic disorders affecting urinary composition (primary hyperparathyroidism, renal tubular acidosis), and inability to comply with the follow-up protocol.

Participant recruitment involved systematic screening through surgical planning records. Following successful surgery and confirmation of complete stone clearance, eligible patients were approached during their first postoperative clinic visit. After providing informed consent, participants underwent baseline assessments. The final cohort included 42 males and 26 females with mean age 46.3 years (SD = 12.7) and BMI 27.8 kg/m² (SD = 4.2). Stone composition analysis revealed calcium oxalate as the predominant component in 43 patients (63.2%), calcium phosphate in 15 patients (22.1%), and uric acid in 10 patients (14.7%).

To ensure sample representativeness and control for potential confounding factors, we stratified recruitment based on age, sex, stone composition, and surgical approach. This stratification maintained balanced representation across key variables, allowing for robust subgroup analyses. All procedures were approved by the institutional ethics committee, with written informed consent obtained from all participants before enrollment.

Materials

Urinary RSS measurement

Participants collected 24-hour urine samples using standardized protocols with preservative-containing containers to prevent bacterial growth and crystallization. The protocol emphasized collecting all urine over exactly 24 h, starting after the first morning void (discarded) and ending with the first morning void of the following day (included). Participants maintained typical fluid intake and dietary habits during collection, except during the dietary intervention phase.

Samples were analyzed using the Litholink service (Labcorp, Chicago, IL, USA), measuring urinary volume, pH, and concentrations of calcium, oxalate, citrate, uric acid, sodium, potassium, magnesium, phosphate, sulfate, ammonium, and creatinine. RSS values for calcium oxalate (CaOx-RSS), calcium phosphate (CaP-RSS), and uric acid (UA-RSS) were calculated using the EQUIL2 program, accounting for urinary ionic strength, complexation, and soluble complex formation. Reference ranges: CaOx-RSS < 6.0, CaP-RSS < 1.0, UA-RSS < 1.0. Technical specifications including intra-assay coefficients of variation (< 5%) and inter-assay coefficients (< 8%) are available upon request.

Between comprehensive collections, participants used a portable urine analyzer (LithoChrome™, Stone Diagnostics, Indianapolis, IN) for home monitoring, measuring pH, specific gravity, and semi-quantitative calcium and oxalate assessments from spot samples. While not providing complete RSS calculations, these measurements served as screening tools for significant parameter changes warranting comprehensive evaluation. Device validation showed correlation coefficients of 0.82 for pH and 0.78 for specific gravity against laboratory measurements.

Stone analysis and imaging

Retrieved stones underwent compositional analysis using Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR), identifying crystalline components and relative proportions. Analysis categorized stones as primarily calcium oxalate monohydrate, calcium oxalate dihydrate, calcium phosphate (hydroxyapatite or brushite), uric acid, struvite, or mixed composition. An experienced laboratory technician blinded to clinical information performed all analyses.

Standardized imaging included low-dose computed tomography without contrast at baseline (2 weeks post-surgery) and 24 months to confirm stone-free status or identify recurrence. Intermediate follow-ups (6 and 12 months) used renal ultrasonography for screening while minimizing radiation exposure. Suspicious ultrasonography findings triggered confirmatory CT scans. All imaging was interpreted by radiologists with urological imaging expertise, blinded to RSS values and intervention groups.

Dietary assessment tools

A validated 131-item food frequency questionnaire (FFQ) specific for stone-forming nutrients assessed dietary habits at baseline, 6, 12, and 24 months. This questionnaire was adapted from the Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health FFQ with modifications to emphasize foods high in calcium, oxalate, protein, sodium, potassium, and fluid content relevant to stone formation. The adapted questionnaire underwent pilot testing (n = 15) for comprehensibility and test-retest reliability (intraclass correlation coefficient 0.82). The complete English version is provided in Supplementary File S1. Nutrient calculations used the USDA Food Composition Database supplemented with oxalate content data.

Three-day food diaries (two weekdays, one weekend day) were completed before each 24-hour urine collection, recording all consumption including timing, portions, preparation methods, and supplements. A registered dietitian specializing in kidney stone nutrition reviewed diaries, providing individualized feedback and developing recommendations based on integrated analysis of dietary patterns and urinary parameters.

Intervention group participants used a mobile application tracking daily fluid intake with customized reminders and volume/timing recording synchronized with the study database, allowing clinicians to monitor adherence and correlate fluid patterns with urinary parameter changes.

Experimental design

The study employed a mixed-methods approach combining observational and interventional components. The initial 12-month phase used prospective observation to characterize RSS patterns and develop the prediction model, while the subsequent 12-month phase evaluated targeted intervention effectiveness using randomized controlled design.

During the observational phase, all participants underwent standardized RSS monitoring at predetermined intervals: baseline (2 weeks post-surgery), 1, 3, 6, and 12 months. This high-frequency initial schedule captured early postoperative urinary biochemistry fluctuations during the critical recovery period.

At 12 months, participants were stratified by recurrence risk score (derived from the prediction model) and randomly assigned to standard care or targeted intervention groups. Randomization used computer-generated sequences with permuted variable-size blocks, stratified by risk category (low, moderate, high) ensuring balanced allocation. A 1:1 allocation ratio resulted in 34 participants per group.

Standard care participants received general preventive advice based on stone composition following current clinical guidelines. Targeted intervention participants received personalized treatment protocols based on specific RSS patterns and risk factors, with intensity adjusted according to risk stratification. This approach evaluated whether personalized RSS-guided interventions exceeded standard care effectiveness.

Comprehensive data collection on demographics (age, sex, BMI, occupation), medical history (previous episodes, family history, comorbidities), surgical details (approach, stone burden, location), and intervention compliance served as covariates in statistical analyses to isolate RSS pattern and intervention effects on recurrence risk.

Risk stratification system

We developed a multivariate logistic regression model to predict stone recurrence (logit(p) = β₀ + Σβixi). The optimal probability threshold was determined using the Youden index from receiver operating characteristic curve analysis. Risk categories were defined as: Low risk (< 10% probability), Moderate risk (10–30% probability), and High risk (> 30% probability). Model performance metrics and risk stratification outcomes are reported in the Results section.

Procedure

Baseline assessment phase

Following enrollment, participants underwent comprehensive baseline assessments 2 weeks post-surgery including: 24-hour urine collection for complete biochemical analysis and RSS calculation; blood tests assessing serum calcium, phosphorus, uric acid, parathyroid hormone, vitamin D, and renal function; FTIR stone analysis determining composition; low-dose CT confirming complete clearance and documenting baseline anatomy; FFQ and 3-day food diary establishing dietary patterns; detailed medical history including previous episodes, family history, comorbidities, and medications.

Upon completing baseline assessments, participants received standardized education about stone disease and general preventive measures. All participants were instructed on proper urine collection techniques and portable analyzer use.

Observational monitoring phase (Months 1–12)

Participants attended clinic visits at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months for clinical evaluation, symptom review, and compliance assessment. Complete 24-hour urine collections at each visit provided comprehensive biochemical analysis and RSS calculations. Between comprehensive evaluations, participants used portable analyzers twice weekly monitoring basic urinary parameters. Three-day food diaries before each collection correlated dietary patterns with urinary parameters. Renal ultrasonography at 6 and 12 months screened for early recurrence, with suspicious findings triggering confirmatory CT.

Data from the first 6 months identified RSS fluctuation patterns and associations with early recurrence markers. By 12 months, integrated data generated a multivariate prediction model stratifying patients into low, moderate, and high recurrence risk categories.

During this phase, all participants received standard preventive advice based on stone composition following current guidelines, typically including general recommendations on fluid intake, dietary modifications, and indicated medications such as thiazide diuretics for hypercalciuria or allopurinol for hyperuricosuria.

Intervention phase (Months 12–24)

At 12 months, risk-stratified participants were randomly assigned to continue standard care or receive targeted intervention.

Standard care participants continued general preventive measures based on stone composition, maintaining the same monitoring schedule but receiving standardized rather than personalized recommendations.

Targeted intervention participants received personalized protocols including: Tailored pharmacotherapy addressing specific metabolic abnormalities. Persistently elevated CaOx-RSS despite dietary measures prompted hydrochlorothiazide (12.5-50 mg daily) or potassium citrate (10-60mEq daily) based on urinary calcium and citrate levels. Individualized plans from dietitians based on integrated urinary parameter and food diary analysis, targeting specific abnormalities while ensuring palatability and adherence. Personalized fluid prescriptions with specific volume goals and timing based on 24-hour urinary patterns, supported by mobile tracking. Follow-up frequency adjusted by risk level, with high-risk patients receiving intensive surveillance.

Adaptive protocols adjusted treatment at 3-month intervals based on RSS response. Persistently elevated RSS prompted intensified therapy; target achievement maintained current regimens. Final 24-month assessment included: final metabolic analysis and RSS calculation, low-dose CT assessing recurrence, quality of life questionnaires, and final dietary assessment. Compliance monitoring throughout included medication pill counts, fluid tracking log review, and urine collection/dietary record completion rates. Missed visits prompted telephone rescheduling and participation encouragement. Withdrawal interviews documented discontinuation reasons.

Outcome measures

Primary outcome was stone recurrence at 24 months, defined as either: (1) new stone formation confirmed by imaging (CT or ultrasonography with CT confirmation), or (2) symptomatic stone episode requiring medical attention including emergency department visits, hospitalization for stone-related symptoms, or documented stone passage. This dual definition captures both asymptomatic imaging-detected stones and clinically relevant symptomatic episodes affecting patient quality of life.

Secondary outcomes included: changes in RSS values from baseline to 24 months; achievement of biochemical response (≥ 30% reduction in primary RSS parameter based on stone composition); individual urinary parameter changes; treatment compliance rates; and adverse events related to interventions.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY) and R version 4.1.0 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Two-sided p-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Baseline characteristics were presented as means ± standard deviations for continuous variables and frequencies/percentages for categorical variables. Between-group comparisons used independent t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests for continuous variables and chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests for categorical variables.

Longitudinal RSS changes were analyzed using mixed-effects models with time as fixed effect and participants as random effects, accounting for repeated measurement correlation. Separate models were developed for CaOx-RSS, CaP-RSS, and UA-RSS. RSS trajectories were classified into patterns based on magnitude and direction of changes.

The multivariate recurrence prediction model used logistic regression with recurrence at 24 months as dependent variable. Initial candidate predictors included RSS patterns, baseline characteristics, stone composition, and surgical approach. Model selection used backward elimination based on Akaike Information Criterion. Internal validation through bootstrap resampling (1000 replications) assessed optimism and calculated corrected C-statistics.

Intervention effectiveness was assessed comparing recurrence rates between groups using chi-square tests. Mixed-effects models examined between-group RSS differences adjusting for baseline values and confounders. Subgroup analyses stratified by risk category and stone composition.

Mediation analyses examined whether intervention effects on recurrence were mediated by RSS changes using Baron and Kenny’s approach [11], disentangling direct and indirect effects.

Multiple imputation handled missing data, creating 20 imputed datasets based on all available variables. Sensitivity analyses compared complete-case and imputed results assessing finding robustness.

Sample size calculation assumed 30% standard care recurrence, 10% targeted intervention recurrence, requiring 62 participants (31 per group) for 80% power (α = 0.05). Accounting for 10% anticipated dropout, 68 participants were enrolled.

All imaging interpretation was performed by radiologists blinded to RSS values, intervention group assignment, and clinical outcomes to minimize assessment bias.

The mean age was 46.3 years with male predominance (61.8%). Most participants (63.2%) had calcium oxalate stones, followed by calcium phosphate (22.1%) and uric acid (14.7%). Clinical feasibility and cost-effectiveness considerations

Implementation of RSS-guided monitoring requires consideration of practical aspects. A risk-stratified approach optimizes resource allocation: low-risk patients (33.8% of cohort) require minimal intervention beyond standard care, while intensive monitoring focuses on moderate/high-risk patients (66.2%) where benefits are greatest. A simplified protocol using quarterly RSS measurements in year one followed by semi-annual monitoring could reduce costs by approximately 40% while likely maintaining predictive value. Preventing one recurrence per 5.7 high-risk patients treated (based on 44.5% absolute risk reduction) could offset monitoring costs given typical stone episode expenses. Integration with telemedicine platforms and automated reminders may further enhance feasibility and compliance.

Discussion

This study provides robust evidence that postoperative RSS patterns predict stone recurrence and guide effective targeted interventions. The identification of distinct RSS trajectories represents a significant advance in understanding metabolic dynamics after stone surgery, enabling personalized prevention strategies that substantially reduce recurrence rates compared to standard care.

The persistently high CaOx-RSS pattern, observed in 26.5% of participants, conferred the highest recurrence risk (OR 6.84, 95% CI 2.13–21.97), consistent with established relationships between sustained supersaturation and crystal formation [12]. Notably, even the gradually decreasing pattern showed moderate risk (18.2% recurrence) despite eventual RSS normalization, suggesting early postoperative supersaturation creates lasting lithogenic conditions through crystal nucleation that persists despite later metabolic improvement. This finding challenges current guidelines recommending metabolic evaluation at 6–8 weeks post-surgery [13], indicating earlier intervention windows may prevent this initial crystal formation.

Our risk stratification system successfully translates complex multivariate predictions into actionable clinical categories. The probability-based thresholds (< 10%, 10–30%, > 30%) provide intuitive risk communication facilitating shared decision-making while maintaining statistical rigor. This approach addresses critical implementation barriers by enabling clinicians to efficiently allocate resources and tailor intervention intensity to individual risk profiles. The strong correlation between predicted and observed recurrence rates across risk strata validates the model’s clinical utility.

The targeted intervention’s remarkable efficacy, particularly in high-risk patients (44.5% absolute risk reduction, 55.6% to 11.1%, p = 0.045), substantially exceeds benefits reported in landmark prevention studies. Borghi et al. achieved 20–30% reduction through dietary intervention alone [14], while Ettinger et al. reported 15–25% reduction with thiazide monotherapy [15]. Our enhanced effectiveness likely reflects the precision of RSS-guided therapy, which addresses specific metabolic abnormalities rather than applying uniform protocols regardless of individual pathophysiology.

Stone-specific RSS patterns provide mechanistic insights critical for treatment selection. The strong pH-dependence of CaP-RSS (r = 0.78, p < 0.001) emphasizes the importance of urinary alkalinization management in calcium phosphate formers, suggesting pH monitoring and control should be prioritized over calcium restriction in these patients. The identification of distinct pH-reactive versus concentration-dependent UA-RSS patterns supports Sakhaee’s proposed classification of uric acid nephrolithiasis subtypes [16], with important therapeutic implications: pH-reactive patients benefit from alkalinization therapy, while concentration-dependent patients require uricosuric management.

The differential effectiveness of intervention components informs evidence-based treatment algorithms. Thiazide diuretics’ superiority for CaOx-RSS reduction (mean 2.83, 95% CI 1.97–3.69) supports their first-line role in calcium oxalate prevention, consistent with meta-analyses showing 40–50% recurrence reduction [17]. However, our data suggest optimal dosing may be guided by RSS response rather than fixed protocols. Potassium citrate’s balanced effects across multiple parameters, despite slightly increasing CaP-RSS, make it particularly valuable for mixed stone compositions or when hypocitraturia coexists with other abnormalities.

The universal benefits of hydration therapy, reducing all RSS parameters significantly, underscore its fundamental role across stone types. The additional benefit of timed water intake beyond total volume increase supports Pak’s hypothesis that fluid consumption patterns affect diurnal crystallization risk [18]. Given hydration’s safety profile and effectiveness, innovative strategies addressing the 67.2% compliance rate could substantially impact population-level recurrence.

Study limitations require acknowledgment. The single-center design may limit generalizability across different populations and healthcare systems. We have planned multicenter external validation to address this concern. The 24-month follow-up, while demonstrating significant intervention benefits, may not capture very late recurrences; future studies extending beyond 5 years would determine whether benefits persist. The relatively small sample size limited precision in stone-specific subgroup analyses, particularly for less common compositions. RSS, while widely accepted, represents a simplified model of complex urinary physicochemistry and may not capture all lithogenic factors such as macromolecular inhibitors and promoters.

Regarding clinical feasibility, our risk-stratified approach offers a practical implementation solution. Low-risk patients showed minimal benefit from intensive intervention, suggesting standard care with annual monitoring suffices for this group. This allows concentration of resources on moderate- and high-risk patients where intervention benefits are substantial. The inclusion of both radiologic and symptomatic outcomes strengthens clinical relevance. While imaging-detected asymptomatic stones are important for understanding disease progression, symptomatic episodes drive healthcare utilization and patient morbidity. The reduction in emergency visits and hospitalizations in the intervention group suggests clinically meaningful benefits beyond radiologic outcomes.

Several factors associated with positive intervention response have important clinical implications. Greater responsiveness in younger patients (< 50 years, OR 2.31) and first-time stone formers (OR 1.94) suggests early intervention may interrupt the recurrence cycle before metabolic patterns become entrenched. This finding supports expanding metabolic evaluation to all first-time stone formers rather than reserving it for recurrent cases, as advocated by Ferraro and colleagues [19].

Future research priorities should address several critical questions. Development of point-of-care RSS measurements could dramatically improve feasibility and patient compliance. Investigation of novel interventions targeting specific RSS patterns represents promising avenues for enhancing prevention efficacy. Integration with emerging technologies offers opportunities to enhance implementation through machine learning algorithms, wearable devices monitoring hydration status, and telemedicine platforms facilitating remote monitoring.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates that serial postoperative RSS monitoring provides valuable prognostic information for stone recurrence risk, while targeted RSS-guided interventions significantly reduce recurrence compared to standard care. The personalized approach successfully addresses the heterogeneity of stone disease pathophysiology, with particular benefit for high-risk patients who experience 44.5% absolute risk reduction. The risk stratification system enables efficient resource allocation, focusing intensive monitoring on patients most likely to benefit while avoiding unnecessary intervention in low-risk individuals. Despite implementation challenges related to monitoring intensity and costs, the substantial reduction in recurrence rates, particularly for high-risk patients, justifies adoption of RSS-guided protocols in clinical practice. With appropriate adaptations for feasibility, including simplified monitoring schedules and risk-based resource allocation, this approach could substantially reduce the global burden of recurrent urolithiasis while optimizing healthcare utilization.

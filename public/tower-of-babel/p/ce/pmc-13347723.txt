Auricular Relapsing Polychondritis in a Patient With Crohn’s Disease: A Case Report
Authors: Beyza Nur Ulas, Belit Merve Cagdas, Ozgur Yigit, Ibrahim Hatemi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
We report the case of a 30-year-old female patient with Crohn’s disease who was receiving infliximab and mesalazine therapy. She presented with left ear pain accompanied by bloody diarrhea and abdominal pain. Initial treatment with ciprofloxacin and aluminum acetate was ineffective, requiring hospitalization. She was treated with intravenous meropenem, clindamycin, and prednisolone, which led to symptom resolution after 12 days. However, 7 months later, the patient developed similar symptoms in the right ear without intestinal involvement. She was hospitalized and treated with intravenous antibiotics, followed by prednisolone treatment.

Relapsing polychondritis is an autoimmune disease that is usually diagnosed and followed by rheumatologists, and it rarely coexists with inflammatory bowel diseases. This disease is characterized by inflammation of multiple cartilaginous structures, especially in the ears, nose, tracheobronchial tree, and eyes. Approximately 30% of relapsing polychondritis cases are associated with another autoimmune disorder.[1] Auricular perichondritis, on the other hand, is typically recognized by ear, nose, and throat specialists. In auricular perichondritis, there is severe edema, pain, and hyperemia of the auricle, while the lobule remains unaffected. Patients are usually treated with systemic antibiotics and topical aluminum acetate.[2,3]

In a case report and literature review published in 2022, 31 cases of relapsing perichondritis associated with inflammatory bowel disease were reported.[4] Although infliximab is recommended as an advanced treatment for relapsing perichondritis in patients with Crohn’s disease, auricular chondritis developed despite infliximab treatment in the case we present.

Case Report

A 30-year-old female patient, diagnosed with Crohn’s disease and treated with infliximab and mesalazine, presented with left ear pain in addition to bloody diarrhea and abdominal pain. The patient had no history of other autoimmune diseases. She was initially prescribed oral ciprofloxacin 500 mg twice daily and aluminum acetate for topical application to the ear. After three days of outpatient treatment, hospitalization was needed. She was afebrile, but her physical examination was remarkable for an erythematous, thickened, extremely tender left pinna with sparing of the ear lobe and a normal internal ear canal and tympanic membrane. Her hearing was also normal. Laboratory examination revealed a leukocytosis of 11,580/mm3 and elevated C-reactive protein of 81 mg/dl. Other laboratory tests were normal.

The patient was started on intravenous meropenem 1 g three times daily and clindamycin 600 mg three times daily following consultation with the infectious diseases department. Aluminum acetate was applied to the affected ear three times daily. Intravenous prednisolone 40 mg was initiated upon recommendation by the consulting gastroenterologist. She received 40 mg/day prednisolone throughout the 12-day hospitalization. After 12 days, the patient’s ear erythema, edema, and pain resolved. C-reactive protein and WBC levels returned to normal. Her intestinal symptoms also completely resolved. At discharge, she was prescribed ciprofloxacin 500 mg twice daily and clindamycin 150 mg three times daily for three days. Prednisolone therapy was continued with a tapering regimen, reducing the dose by half every three days. The entire course of prednisolone lasted approximately 2.5 months. There was no recurrence of left ear perichondritis during the 8-month follow-up (Fig. 1).

After seven months, the patient had similar symptoms, including pain, hyperemia, and swelling in the right ear. Intravenous ampicillin-sulbactam 2 g four times daily and ciprofloxacin 500 mg twice daily were initiated. This time, the patient had no symptoms of intestinal involvement, such as bloody diarrhea or abdominal pain. Laboratory examination revealed a normal WBC and elevated C-reactive protein of 37.2 mg/dl. After three days of intravenous antibiotic therapy, 40 mg of prednisolone was administered for three days. After six days of hospitalization, the patient’s ear symptoms resolved. C-reactive protein decreased to 16 mg/dl, and WBC levels returned to normal. During hospitalization, she received intravenous prednisolone 40 mg/day. At discharge, the patient was prescribed ciprofloxacin 500 mg twice daily and amoxicillin-clavulanic acid 1000 mg twice daily for 10 days. Additionally, she continued prednisolone therapy with a dose taper every three days. When the prednisolone dose was reduced by 4 mg, the patient experienced otalgia, and increasing the dose back to 8 mg was recommended. Currently, the patient is asymptomatic and is being followed with 350 mg infliximab every eight weeks, 8 mg prednisolone daily, and 3000 mg mesalazine daily (Fig. 2).

Discussion

External ear involvement in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is uncommon. Typically, when the external ear is affected, it is often associated with other conditions such as pyoderma gangrenosum, metastatic Crohn’s disease, necrotizing external otitis, or relapsing polychondritis. Relapsing polychondritis (RP) is a rare multisystem inflammatory disease of unknown etiology that involves cartilaginous structures, predominantly those of the ears, nose, and upper and lower respiratory tracts.[1] Treatment for relapsing polychondritis involves corticosteroids, immunomodulators, and biologic agents, aimed at alleviating symptoms and preserving cartilaginous structures.[3]

In the review by Fousekis et al.,[2] three cases of relapsing polychondritis–auricular perichondritis associated with IBD were reported, and only one of them was related to Crohn’s disease. Another case of Crohn’s disease with relapsing polychondritis was published by Jung et al.[2] in 2021. In that case, remission was observed after starting infliximab in the patient who was refractory to conventional treatment (high-dose corticosteroid, NSAID, azathioprine, and mesalazine).

In our case, the patient was already in remission with infliximab treatment when she presented with auricular pain, swelling, redness, and intestinal symptoms. She responded to 40 mg intravenous methylprednisolone treatment. The patient continued methylprednisolone with a dose taper every three days and completed the treatment approximately 2.5 months after discharge. When she presented with similar symptoms in the right ear approximately seven months later, 40 mg methylprednisolone was administered again, resulting in symptom resolution. While the methylprednisolone dose was being tapered, the patient developed mild symptoms, and continuation with 8 mg daily was recommended. The patient remained in remission for eight months under daily 8 mg methylprednisolone therapy.

Our patient had no signs of nasal inflammation or arthritis; the polychondritis was limited to the ears. Although some reports suggest that patients with relapsing polychondritis may benefit from infliximab,[2,4] our patient developed auricular perichondritis while already on infliximab therapy. In our report, however, she responded particularly well to corticosteroids.

Conclusion

In conclusion, corticosteroids may be an effective option in Crohn’s disease patients who develop perichondritis while receiving infliximab, to alleviate symptoms and prevent ear deformities.

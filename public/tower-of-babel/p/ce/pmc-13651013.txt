Cardioplegia strategy and perioperative blood transfusion in isolated coronary artery bypass surgery: a propensity-weighted analysis
Authors: Fatih Kizilyel, Bedirhan Bugra Bayici
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDel Nido cardioplegia has been increasingly adopted in adult cardiac surgery due to its single-dose administration and simplified myocardial protection strategy. However, concerns persist regarding its relatively high crystalloid content and the potential for increased hemodilution and blood transfusion. This study aimed to compare transfusion requirements and early clinical outcomes between del Nido and conventional multidose blood cardioplegia in patients undergoing isolated coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG).MethodsIn this retrospective single-center study, 405 consecutive patients undergoing isolated on-pump CABG were analyzed. Patients were grouped according to cardioplegia strategy: del Nido (n = 319) or conventional blood cardioplegia (n = 86). The primary outcome was perioperative packed red blood cell (PRBC) transfusion. Secondary analyses compared early clinical recovery directly between the cardioplegia strategies. Multivariable logistic regression and propensity score–based inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) were performed to adjust for baseline and operative variables, strictly excluding cases of non-cardioplegia-related mechanical bleeding to refine the analytical models.ResultsDel Nido cardioplegia was associated with higher rates of zero transfusion (27.9% vs. 17.4%, p = 0.020) and lower rates of massive transfusion (≥ 4 units: 23.8% vs. 36.0%). While multivariable adjustment yielded a borderline association for any transfusion (aOR 0.54, 95% CI 0.26–1.05; p = 0.082) largely driven by operative times, propensity-weighted analyses suggested lower odds of any transfusion with del Nido (OR 0.48, 95% CI 0.24–0.95; p = 0.035). Ordinal regression further demonstrated a significant reduction in transfused blood volume (adjusted Coef = -0.508; p = 0.023). Postoperative 24-hour chest tube drainage was identical between groups (median 600 mL vs. 600 mL; p = 0.845). Clinically, the del Nido group demonstrated a significantly shorter duration of mechanical ventilation compared to the blood cardioplegia group (median 7.0 vs. 8.0 h; p = 0.029), while median hospital length of stay was 6.0 versus 5.0 days (p = 0.001).ConclusionsDel Nido cardioplegia was not associated with increased transfusion requirements in isolated CABG surgery and may offer a modest transfusion-sparing effect when baseline patient characteristics are balanced. Operative complexity remains a major determinant of perioperative blood utilization. Prospective studies are warranted to further clarify the relationship between cardioplegia strategies and transfusion outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13019-026-04515-w.Keywords: Del Nido cardioplegia, Blood cardioplegia, Coronary artery bypass grafting, Blood transfusion, Hemodilution, Clinical outcomes

Introduction

Myocardial protection is a cornerstone of successful cardiac surgery. The primary goal is to arrest the heart rapidly during diastole, minimize metabolic demand, and prevent ischemia-reperfusion injury [1]. For decades, multidose cold blood cardioplegia (BC) has been the gold standard in many centers due to its physiological buffering capacity and oxygen-carrying ability [2]. Standard BC typically utilizes a 4:1 ratio of blood to crystalloid.

However, the repetitive nature of BC administration can interrupt surgical workflow. In contrast, del Nido cardioplegia (DN), originally developed for pediatric surgery, allows for a single induction dose that provides up to 90 min of myocardial protection [3, 4]. While the surgical convenience of DN is established, concerns persist regarding its potential for systemic hemodilution. Since the DN consists of a 1000 mL crystalloid base mixed with only 20% blood, it theoretically delivers a larger crystalloid volume than the 4:1 mixture of BC, potentially necessitating higher rates of red blood cell (RBC) transfusion [5]. Blood transfusions in cardiac surgery are independent predictors of morbidity, including renal failure, infection, and increased mortality [6].

While earlier literature regarding the hematologic effects of DN versus BC in adults presented conflicting results [7, 8], recent large-scale multicenter evidence supports a clinically meaningful reduction in blood product utilization with DN [9]. Building upon this contemporary consensus, the objective of this study was to evaluate whether the use of DN influences perioperative blood product utilization compared with BC within a rigorously adjusted cohort of patients undergoing isolated coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), and to compare early clinical outcomes according to cardioplegia strategy.

Materials and methods

Study design and population

This retrospective observational cohort study was conducted at the Department of Cardiovascular Surgery of a tertiary referral center. The institutional ethics committee approved the study protocol, and the requirement for individual patient consent was waived due to the retrospective design.

We reviewed the medical records of consecutive adult patients who underwent CABG between January 1, 2024, and December 31, 2025. This specific timeframe was deliberately chosen to minimize temporal bias and ensure strict standardization in contemporary perioperative care, intensive care protocols, and Patient Blood Management (PBM) strategies across both study groups.

Exclusion criteria included combined cardiac procedures, emergency surgery, redo cardiac surgery, chronic renal failure requiring dialysis, and the need for preoperative mechanical circulatory support. Following the application of these criteria, a total of 405 eligible patients were included in the final analysis. (Fig. 1)

CONSORT Flow Diagram of the Study Population. Flowchart depicting patient screening, exclusion criteria, and the finalized analytical cohorts (n = 308 for Del Nido cardioplegia and n = 82 for blood cardioplegia) utilized in the multivariable and propensity-weighted transfusion models after strictly excluding cases requiring early re-exploration for surgical bleeding

Patients were categorized according to the cardioplegia strategy used during surgery into either the del Nido cardioplegia group (DN, n = 319) or the conventional blood cardioplegia group (BC, n = 86).

To prevent confounding from non-cardioplegia-related transfusion triggers, patients who required surgical re-exploration specifically due to surgical bleeding (11 in the DN group, 4 in the BC group) were identified and strictly excluded from the final multivariable and propensity-weighted transfusion models. Therefore, descriptive comparisons are presented for the full eligible cohort of 405 patients, whereas the multivariable and IPTW transfusion models were performed in the analytical cohort of 390 patients.

Procedures were performed by multiple different attending surgeons within the same department, all strictly adhering to the same institutional surgical and perfusion protocols. The choice of cardioplegia type was based on surgeon preference and institutional practice patterns.

Surgical technique and cardiopulmonary bypass

All procedures were performed through median sternotomy under standard cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) with mild systemic hypothermia (32–34 °C).

After systemic heparinization, CPB was established using ascending aortic and right atrial cannulation.

Myocardial protection was achieved using antegrade cardioplegia delivery through the aortic root in all patients following aortic cross-clamping.

Cardioplegia protocols

Del Nido cardioplegia (DN)

DN consisted of a crystalloid-based solution mixed with oxygenated autologous pump blood in a 4:1 (crystalloid-to-blood) ratio. The base crystalloid utilized at our institution is Isolyte-S (Eczacıbaşı-Baxter, Turkey), which is a balanced isotonic solution completely equivalent to Plasma-Lyte A in terms of its electrolyte profile (140 mEq/L sodium, 5 mEq/L potassium, 3 mEq/L magnesium, 98 mEq/L chloride, 27 mEq/L acetate, and 23 mEq/L gluconate) and physiologic pH (7.4). The 1000 mL crystalloid component was supplemented with the following pharmacological additives: 16.4 mL of 20% mannitol, 12.4 mL of sodium bicarbonate, 6.4 mL of 2% lidocaine, 13.2 mL of 15% magnesium sulfate, and 26 mL of 7.5% potassium chloride (or 8.6 mL of 22.5% potassium chloride). The solution was delivered through an antegrade route using a cooling system at a temperature of + 8 °C. It was administered as an initial induction dose of 20 mL/kg. A subsequent maintenance dose of 500 mL was administered if the aortic cross-clamp time exceeded 60 min [10]. If further redosing was required beyond the second dose, subsequent 500 mL doses were repeated at 45–60 min intervals or guided by the return of myocardial electrical activity.

Conventional blood cardioplegia (BC)

Conventional multidose blood cardioplegia was prepared utilizing a 4:1 blood-to-crystalloid ratio. Specifically, 1000 mL of oxygenated blood drawn from the cardiopulmonary bypass pump was first enriched with 8.5 mL of 22.5% potassium chloride. From this mixture, 800 mL of the enriched blood was combined with 200 mL of a balanced crystalloid solution (Isolyte-S). The initial antegrade induction dose (10–15 mL/kg) was followed by subsequent 400 mL maintenance doses administered every 20 min throughout the duration of the aortic cross-clamping to ensure continuous myocardial protection.

Due to institutional resource allocation, cell salvage devices were not routinely utilized during the study period. However, a standardized institutional blood conservation protocol was applied to all cases to counteract hemodilution. This bundle included: (1) minimizing CPB circuit prime volume by shortening the CPB lines, achieved by routing the pump lines from the surgeon’s left side; (2) routine retrograde autologous priming (RAP) prior to initiation of CPB; and (3) acute normovolemic hemodilution (ANH), in which autologous whole blood was harvested immediately after sternotomy in patients with a baseline hematocrit > 40% and stored for retransfusion after CPB. Patient-level pump in–out balance, ultrafiltration volume, and intraoperative blood loss data were not consistently available and were therefore not included in the primary analysis.

Data collection

A comprehensive set of clinical data was extracted from the institutional registry. Preoperative variables included age, body mass index (BMI), EuroSCORE II, baseline hemoglobin, baseline hematocrit, platelet count, and serum creatinine levels. Per institutional protocol for elective isolated CABG, patients were required to have an international normalized ratio (INR) < 1.5 and no active oral anticoagulant use at the time of surgery. Intraoperative variables consisted of cardiopulmonary bypass time, aortic cross-clamp time, and the number of distal anastomoses and total administered cardioplegia volume. Postoperative variables comprised red blood cell (RBC) transfusion requirements, 24-hour chest tube drainage, re-exploration for bleeding, mechanical ventilation time, and hospital length of stay.

Blood transfusion protocol

Packed red blood cell (PRBC) transfusion was administered according to institutional guidelines. Transfusion was generally considered when hemoglobin levels fell below 7–8 g/dL in hemodynamically stable patients and below 9 g/dL in the presence of hemodynamic instability, ongoing bleeding, or significant comorbidity.

Although the final decision regarding transfusion was made by the attending anesthesiologist and surgical team, transfusion practices during the study period followed a restrictive strategy consistent with contemporary cardiac surgery and transfusion guidelines [11].

Study endpoints

The primary endpoint was any perioperative PRBC transfusion, defined as receipt of ≥ 1 unit of PRBC. Transfusion burden was evaluated as a secondary transfusion endpoint using ordered transfusion categories and ordinal regression.

A secondary analysis was conducted to compare early postoperative clinical recovery metrics, specifically mechanical ventilation duration and hospital length of stay, directly between the DN and BC strategies.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (version 25.0). Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation and compared using the student’s t-test. Categorical variables were analyzed using the Chi-square test. Pearson correlation analysis was used to evaluate the relationship between operative variables and transfusion volume. Data normality was assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test and visual inspection of histograms. Continuous variables with non-normal distributions were expressed as medians [interquartile ranges (IQR)] and compared using the Mann-Whitney U test. Additionally, an ordinal logistic regression model was employed to evaluate the dose-response relationship between cardioplegia strategy and the volume of transfused blood units. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The primary outcome (perioperative PRBC transfusion) was modeled both as a binary variable (any transfusion: yes/no) via multivariable logistic regression and as an ordinal outcome (number of transfused units) via ordinal logistic regression to assess the dose-response relationship.

In addition to univariable comparisons, multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed to identify independent predictors of perioperative PRBC transfusion. Two separate multivariable models were constructed. The first model included preoperative variables to estimate the baseline-adjusted association between cardioplegia strategy and PRBC transfusion. The second exploratory model additionally included operative variables as markers of procedural complexity and potential perioperative confounders. 'Hemodynamic instability’ as a transfusion modifier was explicitly defined as the requirement for continuous vasoactive/inotropic support or the presence of profound hypotension (mean arterial pressure < 60 mmHg) despite fluid resuscitation. Results are reported as adjusted odds ratios (aOR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI).

Propensity scores were estimated using logistic regression including the following preoperative variables: age, BMI, EuroSCORE II, baseline hemoglobin, baseline hematocrit, platelet count, creatinine, diabetes mellitus, and hypertension. Inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) using stabilized weights was applied to create a pseudo-population with balanced baseline characteristics. Robust sandwich variance estimators were used to account for weighting. Detailed methodologies for the propensity score model, including covariate balance assessment before and after weighting (Supplementary Tables 1 and Supplementary Fig. 1), propensity score overlap (Supplementary Fig. 2), and stabilized weight distributions (Supplementary Fig. 3), are provided in the Supplementary Material.

The mean total cardioplegia volume administered was significantly lower in the DN cohort compared to the BC group (1185 ± 245 mL vs. 1740 ± 320 mL, p < 0.001).

Perioperative transfusion requirements

Blood transfusion requirements differed significantly between the groups (p = 0.020) (Table 2). A higher proportion of patients in the DN group required zero transfusions (27.9% vs. 17.4%). Massive transfusion (≥ 4 units) was more frequent in the BC group (36.0% vs. 23.8%).

In the primary multivariable regression model restricted to preoperative variables (Table 3), DN demonstrated a trend toward lower odds of transfusion, though the difference did not reach statistical significance (aOR 0.62, 95% CI 0.32–1.15; p = 0.142). When intraoperative variables reflecting surgical complexity were introduced into the secondary exploration model (Table 4 ,Fig. 2), DN exhibited a borderline adjusted association with reduced transfusion requirements (aOR 0.54, 95% CI 0.26–1.05; p = 0.082). In this expanded model, prolonged cardiopulmonary bypass duration (aOR 1.18 per 10 min, 95% CI 1.08–1.30; p < 0.001) and longer aortic cross-clamp time (aOR 1.11 per 10 min, 95% CI 1.01–1.22; p = 0.028) emerged as the dominant independent predictors of perioperative PRBC utilization, partially tempering the direct weight of the cardioplegia strategy itself.

In a subsequent ordinal logistic regression model to evaluate the dose-response relationship, DN was significantly associated with lower odds of requiring higher volumes of packed red blood cell units (adjusted Coef = −0.508, p = 0.023) compared to BC.

When evaluating early operative and postoperative clinical recovery metrics directly across the myocardial protection pathways (Table 2), the median 24-hour chest tube drainage was identical between the strategies (p = 0.845). Clinically, patients in the DN group demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in the duration of mechanical ventilation compared to the conventional BC group (median 7.0 h [IQR: 6.0–10.0] vs. 8.0 h [IQR: 6.0–13.0], respectively; p = 0.029). The median hospital length of stay was 6.0 days [IQR: 5.0–7.0] in the DN cohort and 5.0 days [IQR: 4.0–7.0] in the BC cohort (p = 0.001).

Discussion

In this retrospective cohort of patients undergoing isolated on-pump coronary artery bypass grafting, we compared perioperative transfusion requirements and early clinical outcomes between DN and BC strategies. The principal finding of this study is that DN was associated with lower crude rates of perioperative red blood cell transfusion compared with conventional blood cardioplegia. However, after adjustment for baseline risk factors and operative variables, this association became attenuated and did not reach statistical significance. In contrast, propensity score–weighted analyses that balanced preoperative characteristics between groups suggested that DN may still be associated with a lower likelihood of transfusion. Taken together, these results suggest that the relationship between cardioplegia strategies and transfusion requirements is complex and influenced by both patient-related and procedural factors.

A frequently cited concern regarding the adoption of DN in adult cardiac surgery relates to its relatively high crystalloid content [12, 13]. While the standard DN formula carries an 80% crystalloid base, our intraoperative data demonstrates that this formulaic discrepancy is heavily counterbalanced by the cumulative volume administered. BC requires repetitive redosing every 20 min, leading to a significantly higher total volume delivery (mean 1740 ± 320 mL vs. 1185 ± 245 mL in DN, p < 0.001). In procedures with longer ischemic times, repeated administration of multidose blood cardioplegia may result in a substantial cumulative volume of cardioplegia entering the extracorporeal circuit [14]. Although DN has a higher crystalloid fraction, its lower cumulative administered volume may mitigate overall cardioplegia-related volume loading. However, because patient-level pump balance, intraoperative hematocrit trajectory, ultrafiltration volume, and effluent handling were not consistently available, the net hemodilutional impact cannot be definitively quantified.

Our findings are directionally consistent with the landmark multicenter analysis by Hawkins et al. [9], which evaluated over 40,000 patients and demonstrated that DN provides a clinically meaningful reduction in blood product utilization without compromising myocardial protection. Furthermore, studies by Fresilli et al. [5] and An et al. [15] demonstrated that DN provides comparable myocardial protection and equivalent, if not slightly lower, transfusion requirements compared to conventional BC. Our propensity-weighted estimate (OR 0.48 for any transfusion) supports the hypothesis of a modest transfusion-sparing effect. However, it is crucial to recognize that the magnitude of this effect varies across literature [16]. This heterogeneity is likely driven by institutional differences in perioperative PBM protocols. Variations in the routine use of antifibrinolytics (e.g., tranexamic acid), differing cardioplegia redosing thresholds, and the application of retrograde autologous priming or cell salvage can significantly influence overall hemodilution and bleeding outcomes, potentially overshadowing the isolated impact of the cardioplegia formula.

Another important observation from our analyses is that operative complexity appears to be a key determinant of transfusion requirements. Cardiopulmonary bypass duration and cross-clamp time were both independently associated with an increased likelihood of transfusion in the multivariable models. Previous studies have suggested that prolonged CPB may contribute to hemostatic alterations; however, coagulation parameters were not directly measured in the present study [17–19]. In our cohort, patients requiring transfusion had significantly longer operative times compared with those who remained transfusion-free, underscoring the importance of procedural factors in determining perioperative blood utilization.

To further address baseline differences and potential confounding, we performed a propensity score–based IPTW analysis using only preoperative patient characteristics. After balancing baseline variables, DN remained associated with a lower probability of perioperative transfusion. The difference between the preoperative IPTW model and the model including operative variables underscores the sensitivity of the association to procedural complexity. Therefore, these findings should be interpreted as adjusted associations rather than causal estimates.

Perioperative PRBC transfusion remains clinically relevant in cardiac surgery because it is consistently associated in the literature with delayed postoperative recovery and increased morbidity. In the present study, early clinical recovery metrics were therefore compared directly according to cardioplegia strategy rather than transfusion status, in accordance with the revised analytical framework. Although DN was associated with shorter mechanical ventilation duration, hospital length of stay was longer in this group, and these findings should be interpreted cautiously given the retrospective design and potential residual confounding.

In this study, ‘perioperative’ PRBC transfusion was defined to encompass both the intraoperative and early postoperative intensive care unit (ICU) phases as a unified clinical endpoint. This comprehensive definition was used because intraoperative hemodilution—driven by cumulative cardioplegia volume and the cardiopulmonary bypass circuit—may lower hemoglobin levels upon ICU admission, thereby potentially contributing to early postoperative transfusion decisions.

Ultimately, our findings reinforce that the adoption of DN in adult CABG is clinically feasible and associated with no consistent disadvantage in the measured early postoperative recovery metrics, unlikely to increase PRBC transfusion requirements, and may confer significant surgical workflow advantages through its single-dose administration. More importantly, our results emphasize that overall operative efficiency—coupled with adherence to comprehensive PBM practices such as restrictive transfusion thresholds, antifibrinolytic use, minimized CPB prime volume, retrograde autologous priming, and careful fluid management—likely plays a far more dominant role in dictating perioperative transfusion outcomes than the specific choice of cardioplegia formula alone.

Furthermore, as is inherently common in retrospective observational studies, baseline imbalances existed between the cohorts prior to weighting. Notably, patients in the BC group presented with a statistically significant lower baseline hemoglobin level compared to the DN. While this discrepancy might reflect an inherent selection bias or unmeasured procedural channeling within a real-world clinical setting, the rigorous application of the IPTW methodology successfully balanced these preoperative hematologic variables. Consequently, this weighting approach reduced the baseline anemia imbalance and allowed a more balanced comparison of the cardioplegia strategies regarding the primary endpoint.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the retrospective and single-center design introduces the possibility of selection bias, as the choice of cardioplegia strategy was determined by surgeon preference and evolving institutional practice patterns rather than random allocation. The retrospective, single-center design precludes any causal interpretations of the data, as unmeasured confounders like individual surgeon technical speed or intraoperative bleeding variations persist. Second, the study groups were unequal in size due to the progressive adoption of DN during the study period. As the technique became more widely used in our institution, a larger proportion of patients received DN. Although multivariable adjustment and propensity score–weighted analyses were performed to mitigate potential confounding, residual bias related to temporal practice changes cannot be entirely excluded, as our dataset did not explicitly incorporate surgeon-specific identifiers or calendar-time fixed effects. Third, we were unable to retrieve exact data for specific components of contemporary PBM bundles (e.g., precise tranexamic acid dosages, ultrafiltration volumes, and patient-level details of blood conservation maneuvers), limiting our ability to comprehensively assess all intraoperative hemodilution determinants. Furthermore, CPB time and cross-clamp times were treated as independent confounders reflective of surgical complexity rather than pure mediators. Also, a notable limitation of this study is that while institutional blood conservation guidelines were present, the ultimate decision to transfuse blood products was guided by the real-time clinical judgment of the attending anesthesiology and surgical teams rather than a strictly standardized trigger protocol. Although this inevitably introduces observational variability regarding the primary endpoint, it accurately reflects real-world, dynamic clinical practice. We utilized rigorous multivariable regression and stabilized IPTW methodologies to mathematically adjust for this inherent baseline and procedural confounding as robustly as possible. Finally, the present analysis focused on early postoperative outcomes, and long-term follow-up evaluating myocardial function and survival was beyond the scope of the current study. Future prospective studies with standardized transfusion protocols and balanced treatment allocation will be important to confirm these findings.

Conclusion

In conclusion, DN was associated with lower crude transfusion rates and demonstrated a transfusion-sparing signal in propensity-weighted analyses. This transfusion-sparing association may be partly explained by the lower total administered cardioplegia volume, despite the higher crystalloid fraction of the DN formula. Ultimately, our findings suggest that DN provides an effective and clinically feasible myocardial protection strategy that integrates well into contemporary, resource-conscious Patient Blood Management protocols.

The vertical dashed line at 0.10 represents the widely accepted threshold for adequate covariate balance. The revised propensity model successfully balances all variables, including the newly incorporated preoperative baseline hemoglobin and platelet counts.

Supplementary Material 3:Supplementary Figure 2. Histogram illustrating the distribution and overlap of the estimated propensity scores between the Del Nido (n=308) and blood cardioplegia (n=82) groups. The substantial visual overlap confirms adequate positivity and comparability between the treatment cohorts within the strictly refined analytical population (N=390), following the exclusion of non-cardioplegia-related mechanical bleeding cases.

Supplementary Material 4:Supplementary Figure 3. Distribution of the stabilized inverse probability of treatment weights utilized in the final propensity-weighted analysis. The histogram confirms a normalized spread without extreme weights. The vertical dashed line denotes the ideal mean weight of approximately 1.0, validating the statistical stability and robustness of the applied IPTW model.

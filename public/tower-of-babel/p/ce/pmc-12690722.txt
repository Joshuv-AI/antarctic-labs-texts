MENG-08 A CASE OF A VERY RARE XANTHOMATOUS PARASAGITTAL MENINGIOMA
Authors: Simbarashe Sanyamandwe, Kuzivakwashe Chingonzo, Kazadi Kalangu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMeningiomas are neoplasms arising from meningothelial cells and show histological diversity with 15 histological subtypes of difference prevalence being described. Xanthomatous meningioma is one of the rarest variants of metaplastic meningioma with only about 19 cases reported in the literature to date.The caseWe report a case of a 46-year-old female with a 6-month history of an on and off frontal headache which became severe 3 weeks prior to presentation. This was associated with left sided paresis but however there was no accompanying history of projectile vomiting or seizures. On examination the patient had a Glasgow coma scale of 15/15 with a left sided upper and lower limb paresis of 4/5 across all the myotomes. Cranial nerves were intact with the rest of the examination being normal. An MRI scan taken showed a 4.4 x 2.8 x 2.7 cm middle third left solid and cystic parasagittal mass with a small 0.5 cm extension to the contralateral side. Patient was then taken for a craniotomy under general anesthesia and gross total excision undertaken. The post operative period was uneventful, and the patient was discharged from hospital on post-operative day six. The histology result showed that the mass was a xanthomatous meningioma (WHO grade 1). Post operative CT scan showed that Simpson grade II excision had been achieved.ConclusionDue to the rarity of xanthomatous meningiomas, it is important to report such cases as it allows for an understanding of the clinical course and recurrence behavior of such infrequent meningioma subtypes. Further investigations and reports are therefore required in order to describe clearly the character of these rare tumors.

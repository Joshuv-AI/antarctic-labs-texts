Quantitative assessment of diastolic strain in patients with cardiac amyloidosis based on magnetic resonance feature tracking technology
Authors: Ruo-Lan Zao, Kang-Li Li, Xue-Hong Xiao, Ze-Yan Wang, Ang Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCardiac amyloidosis (CA) is a rare, infiltrative restrictive cardiomyopathy. Late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) in cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) imaging is an important basis for the diagnosis of CA. However, in the early stage of the disease, the deposition of pathological proteins is scarce, which may result in negative LGE. This study aims to explore the diagnostic value of diastolic strain (DS) of the myocardium in CA patients with negative LGE.MethodsA retrospective review was conducted from June 2020 to April 2024, including 41 CA patients and 18 healthy controls. Clinical data were analyzed, comparing CMR functional parameters, T1 values, extracellular volume (ECV), left ventricular peak filling rate (LVPFR), and DS parameters among the control, LGE-positive, and LGE-negative groups.ResultsCompared to the LGE-positive group, the LGE-negative and control groups exhibited lower native T1 values, ECV, and higher left ventricular ejection fractions. Additionally, compared to the LGE-positive group, the control group had higher stroke volume, cardiac output, and cardiac index values. Moreover, the global peak DS rate radial (GPDSRR) two-dimensional (2D), global peak diastolic velocity circumferential (GPDVC) three-dimensional (3D), global peak diastolic velocity radial (GPDVR) 3D, and global peak DS rate circumferential (GPDSRC) 3D values of CA patients in the LGE-positive and LGE-negative groups were lower than those in the control group. The area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve in diagnosing LGE-negative CA for GPDSRC 3D, GPDSRR 2D, GPDVC 3D, and GPDVR 3D was 0.944, 0.917, 0.876, and 0.797, respectively.ConclusionsThe DS parameters demonstrate significant diagnostic value for LGE-negative CA patients and can serve as an imaging biomarker for diagnosing early cardiac involvement in CA patients to facilitate timely clinical treatment.

Introduction

Cardiac amyloidosis (CA) is a rare but rapidly progressive invasive restrictive cardiomyopathy. Its pathophysiology is primarily characterized by the deposition of amyloid protein in the extracellular matrix of the myocardium, leading to a progressive increase in ventricular wall thickness. Consequently, due to increased ventricular wall stiffness and decreased compliance, patients often experience ventricular diastolic dysfunction and enlargement of the atria, which are typical characteristics of restrictive cardiomyopathy (1,2).

CA is a systemic disease that may affect various organs, resulting in diverse clinical manifestations among patients. The complexity and individual variability of the disease make diagnosis challenging, and often, the disease has progressed for several years before the heart lesions are discovered (3). Notably, due to the rapid advancement of CA, patients may develop severe arrhythmias, refractory heart failure, and even face the risk of sudden cardiac death in the later stages. Fortunately, the advent of new drugs to treat amyloidosis has brought renewed hope for the treatment of CA (4). Therefore, identifying cardiac involvement in CA as early as possible and providing appropriate treatment is crucial for improving the prognosis of CA patients.

Cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) imaging, as a non-invasive, non-radiative imaging tool with high soft tissue resolution, has proven to be highly valuable in diagnosing CA (5). One of its key features, late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) in CMR, can assess the myocardial amyloid deposition and its extent, and is closely related to the poor prognosis of CA patients (6). However, CA is a progressive disease. In its early stages, pathological protein deposition is minimal, which may result in reduced sensitivity of standard LGE assessments (5). Therefore, identifying a non-invasive and effective biomarker for early diagnosis of CA is necessary for early clinical identification of myocardial amyloid deposition and for implementation of effective therapy.

The application of feature tracking (FT) technology based on dynamic cine sequences in CMR has become increasingly widespread. This method enables the evaluation of both global and regional myocardial strain by assessing circumferential, radial, and longitudinal myocardial displacements, as well as myocardial twist, thereby facilitating a more timely and comprehensive assessment of myocardial contractile and diastolic function impairments (7,8).

The aim of this study was to assess the diagnostic value of diastolic strain (DS) parameters for cardiac involvement in CA, particularly in LGE-negative CA patients, to identify better bioindicators of cardiac diastolic function in CA patients, and to achieve early diagnosis of cardiac involvement in CA patients. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://qims.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/qims-2024-2900/rc).

Methods

Subjects

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Zhongshan City People’s Hospital (No. K2022-001), and individual consent for this retrospective analysis was waived. Patients who underwent CMR examinations and were clinically diagnosed with myocardial amyloidosis between June 2020 and April 2024 were included in the study group. Healthy individuals who underwent physical examination and were of a similar age range to the CA patients were enrolled as the control group, as cardiac function changed with aging, particularly in the elderly (9).

A total of 41 CA patients were ultimately included in the study group, consisting of 17 CA patients in the LGE negative group, 24 CA patients in the LGE positive group, and 18 healthy controls.

After magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) examination, clinical follow-up information (with a minimum follow-up period of six months) of CA patients was retrieved to observe the occurrence of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE).

MR imaging and data processing methods

All subjects underwent MRI using a 1.5T MR scanner (Prodiva CX Systems, Philips, Netherlands) equipped with a surface phased array heart coil. The conventional scanning sequences included a black blood sequence and a half-Fourier acquisition single-shot fast spin echo sequence [repetition time (TR) 1,578 ms, echo time (TE) 30 ms, slice thickness 8 mm, field of view (FOV) 320 mm × 320 mm, echo train length 30], primarily used to observe the shape and structure of the heart. True steady-state free precession gradient echo sequences (TR 3.2 ms, TE 1.6 ms, slice thickness 8 mm, FOV 320 mm × 320 mm, retrospective electrocardiogram reconstruction, 30 phases) were acquired. These included left ventricular two-chamber long-axis and four-chamber long-axis views, as well as an 8–10 slice left ventricular short-axis view (scanning from base to apex to cover the entire ventricle and perpendicular to the line connecting the mitral valve and apex). A left ventricular three-chamber cardiac slice was also acquired to observe the dynamic changes of the heart and evaluate cardiac function parameters.

T1 mapping was performed on the mesial, middle, and distal three layers in the short axis using the modified Look-Locker inversion recovery sequence (TR 2.8 ms, TE 1.3 ms, slice thickness 8 mm, FOV 320 mm × 320 mm). Native T1 was obtained using a 5s (3s) 5s acquisition mode before enhancement, while enhanced T1 was acquired using a 4s (1s) 3s (1s) mode after enhancement. For delayed enhancement, Gadopentetic acid contrast agent was injected into the antecubital vein at a dose of 0.2 mmol/kg. After 15 minutes, delayed enhancement scanning using an electrocardiogram-gated phase-sensitive inversion recovery sequence was performed to obtain long- and short-axis images of the four-chamber left ventricle, enabling qualitative evaluation of enhancement in CA patients (Table 1).

All MR images were transferred to the postprocessing workstation for interpretation and postprocessing analysis. CMR readers and post-processers were blinded to subject health status and group assignment. The criteria for LGE enhancement were defined as follows: (I) no enhancement: visual assessment reveals no enhancement of the left ventricular myocardium on any plane; (II) enhancement: presence of non-specific or transmural enhancement of the left ventricular myocardium on any plane. All images were reviewed by two senior cardiovascular imaging specialists (8 and 5 years of experience, respectively) who reached a consensus. Using the Circle Cardiovascular Imaging (CVI42, Calgary, CA, version 5.13.7) post-processing software, the left ventricular endocardial and epicardial borders were automatically delineated from standard cardiac short-axis, bi-chamber, tri-chamber, and four-chamber cine images. Subsequently, the accuracy of these automatically generated endocardial and epicardial borders was assessed. In cases where the endocardial and epicardial borders of dynamic cine images drawn by the software deviated, the contours were manually corrected frame by frame. This process yielded standard cardiac functional parameters, including stroke volume (SV), cardiac output (CO), cardiac index (CI), and left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), as well as the left ventricular peak filling rate (LVPFR).

Myocardial strain imaging was performed using the Strain module of CVI42 (utilizing the FT method) by importing dynamic data from the left ventricular short-axis, tri-chamber, bi-chamber, and four-chamber planes. The software automatically determined the end-diastolic phase, delineated the myocardial contours, and computed the left ventricular DS parameters (Figure 1). These parameters included global peak DS rate circumferential (GPDSRC), global peak DS rate longitudinal (GPDSRL), global peak DS rate radial (GPDSRR), global peak diastolic velocity circumferential (GPDVC), global peak diastolic velocity longitudinal (GPDVL), and global peak diastolic velocity radial (GPDVR). Each parameter has two sets of computational results: two-dimensional (2D) and three-dimensional (3D).

Example of circumferential strain (B1-B3), longitudinal strain (C1-C3), and radial strain (D1-D3) curves for a control subject (A1), a patient with LGE-negative CA (A2), and LGE-positive CA (A3). CA, cardiac amyloidosis; LGE, late gadolinium enhancement.

The measurement of T1 values and extracellular volume (ECV) values utilized CVI42 to automatically delineate the endocardial and epicardial contours of the left ventricular myocardium of basal, mid-ventricular, and apical layers in short-axis views, while manually outlining the region of interest for the cardiac chamber blood pool. The hematocrit value for the day was inputted, and CVI42 subsequently calculated the ECV based on the formula: ECV =(1− hematocrit)×[(1/enhanced myocardial T1 post-contrast −1/enhanced myocardial T1 pre-contrast)/(1/enhanced blood pool T1 post-contrast −1/enhanced blood pool T1 pre-contrast)] (11). Both T1 values and ECV values were reported as the mean of the basal, mid-ventricular, and apical three layers.

The general clinical data of patients with CA were systematically organized and analyzed. The differences in conventional CMR parameters among the LGE-negative group, LGE-positive group, and healthy control group were compared. Additionally, the differences in DS parameters among the LGE-negative group, LGE-positive group, and healthy control group were assessed. Furthermore, the diagnostic value of specific global DS parameters for LGE-negative CA patients was explored.

Statistical methods

Data analysis was conducted using SPSS version 29.0. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was employed to assess the normality of continuous data distribution. For normally distributed quantitative data, results are expressed as means ± standard deviations. Independent samples t-tests were utilized for comparisons between two groups. For non-normally distributed quantitative data, results are reported as medians and interquartile ranges, with the Kruskal-Wallis test applied for comparisons between two groups. A P value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed using MedCalc software using the DeLong test, and the optimal cutoff value was estimated based on the Youden index.

Results

General clinical and imaging information

A total of 41 patients diagnosed with CA and 18 healthy controls were included. The average age of patients with CA was 61.70±10.66 years, while the average age of the healthy control group was 58.72±12.11 years. Statistical analysis indicated that the age difference between the two groups was not statistically significant (P=0.264). Among patients with CA, 59% (n=24/41) were male, 73% (n=30/41) were between 50 and 70 years old, and 15% (n=6/41) were over 70 years old (Figure 2). Among the health controls, 56% (n=10/18) were male, and no statistical difference was found between the health and CA group for sex (P=0.831). A total of 59% (n=24/41) patients exhibited LGE, while 41% (n=24/41) patients were LGE-negative. The proportion of patients with pericardial effusion was 64% (n=26/41) (Table 2). During follow-up, 7 cases of MACE were identified among LGE-negative patients, including 2 cases of newly diagnosed clinical heart failure and 5 cases of arrhythmia. In contrast, 14 cases of MACE were observed among LGE-positive patients, which included 4 cases of newly diagnosed clinical heart failure, 1 case of cardiac death, 1 case of cardiogenic shock, and 8 cases of arrhythmia.

CMR parameter characteristics of the control, LGE-negative, and LGE-positive CA groups

Compared to the LGE-positive group, the LGE-negative group and the control group had lower native T1 (1,151 vs. 1,063 vs. 1,055 ms) and ECV (43.5% vs. 29.41% vs. 27.66%) values, as well as higher LVEFs (46% vs. 56% vs. 59%), respectively. Compared to the LGE-positive group, the control group had higher SV (53.80 vs. 65.61 mL), CO (3.85 vs. 4.75 mL/min), and CI (2.37 vs. 2.90 mL/min/m2) values, respectively. There were no significant differences in CMR parameters T1, SV, CO, CI, ECV, and LVEF between the LGE-negative CA group and the healthy control group. Additionally, there was no significant difference in LVPFR between the LGE-negative CA group, the LGE-positive CA group, and the healthy control group (Table 3).

Diagnostic value of DS parameters in LGE-negative CA

Based on the analysis of myocardial diastolic parameters between the LGE-negative group and the healthy control group, indicators with significant statistical differences—GPDSRC 3D, GPDSRR 2D, GPDVC 3D, and GPDVR 3D—were used as independent variables, with LGE-negative CA as the diagnostic indicator. ROC curves were drawn to assess the power of each parameter in distinguishing LGE-negative CA (Figure 4). The results showed that the area under the ROC curve for GPDSRC 3D, GPDSRR 2D, GPDVC 3D, and GPDVR 3D was 0.944, 0.917, 0.876, and 0.797, respectively. When GPDSRC 3D =0.930, GPDSRR 2D =−1.330, GPDVC 3D =−43.55, and GPDVR 3D =−23.71 were taken as the optimal cut-off values, the sensitivity and specificity of diagnosing CA in the LGE-negative group were as follows: GPDSRC 3D (sensitivity =94.12%, specificity =88.89%); GPDSRR 2D (sensitivity =82.35%, specificity =100%); GPDVC 3D (sensitivity =88.24%, specificity =72.22%); and GPDVR 3D (sensitivity =58.82%, specificity =88.89%). The pairwise ROC curves were compared using the DeLong test, and the P values were all >0.05 (Table 5).

Discussion

CA is an infiltrative restrictive cardiomyopathy characterized by the misfolding of soluble proteins into insoluble amyloid fibrils. These fibrils progressively accumulate in the extracellular matrix of cardiac myocytes, leading to cardiac dysfunction over time. Notably, CA is typically more prevalent among middle-aged and elderly populations, with a slightly higher incidence in male patients compared to female individuals. According to the data from this study, the majority of CA patients are aged between 50 and 70 years, comprising 73% of the cohort, with an average age of 61.70±10.66 years. Additionally, male patients account for 59% of the study population, which aligns with the known epidemiological characteristics of CA (12,13). MACE can occur in patients who are negative for LGE, suggesting that myocardial damage has already occurred in these LGE-negative CA patients.

Multiple studies have indicated that LGE in CMR serves as a crucial diagnostic criterion for CA, reflecting the deposition of amyloid proteins within the myocardial interstitium. Furthermore, LGE is closely associated with the pathological characteristics of CA (6) and serves as a significant predictor of mortality of CA (14). However, CA is a progressive infiltrative disease. In the early stages of the disease, the pathological changes in the myocardium may be relatively subtle, resulting in insufficient contrast when compared to normal myocardial tissue. In such instances, relying solely on qualitative LGE assessment may prove inadequate. This reliance on visual assessment of LGE criteria may overlook early cardiac involvement in amyloidosis (15). Among the CA patients included in this study, 41% exhibited LGE-negative results, suggesting that early-stage CA patients may present without LGE. Furthermore, no statistically significant differences were observed in T1 values, ECV, and cardiac function parameters between the healthy control group and the LGE-negative group. This may be attributed to the fact that, in the early stages of the disease, the pathological changes in the myocardium are not sufficiently pronounced to manifest corresponding characterization changes or alterations in cardiac function. This further highlights the necessity for more sensitive and specific diagnostic methods to accurately assess myocardial involvement in early-stage CA patients.

In recent years, CMR-FT technology has garnered extensive attention and application. The principle of CMR-FT is based on voxel tracking, which involves identifying anatomical differences along the cardiac chamber-myocardial tissue boundary between cardiac chambers and myocardial tissue. By employing the maximum likelihood method, similar features are tracked in the region of interest between two frames of images to evaluate the displacement and deformation of myocardial segments (7). This technique utilizes images acquired from conventional cine sequences, eliminating the need for additional specialized scanning protocols or contrast agent injections. It quantifies the mechanical changes in the atria and ventricles through myocardial deformation, which can reflect early myocardial damage (16,17). In CMR-FT, strain refers to the degree of deformation of a heart segment extending from its original shape to its maximum in a specified direction. This can be used to analyze the local or global strain of the myocardium in three directions: radial, circumferential, and longitudinal. Furthermore, strain rate serves as a critical indicator of the velocity of myocardial deformation, describing the rate or slope of myocardial strain (18). For systolic strain, longitudinal and circumferential strains are typically negative, whereas radial strain is positive. Conversely, during diastole, longitudinal and circumferential strains exhibit positive values, while radial strain is negative.

Research has found that patients with CA exhibit significant alterations in myocardial strain parameters across three directions, which are closely associated with all-cause mortality (19). Cardiac involvement in CA typically leads to increased ventricular stiffness and diastolic dysfunction, exacerbating as the progression of cardiac involvement advances (20). Notably, one study suggests that left ventricular longitudinal strain and echocardiographic left ventricular diastolic dysfunction are sensitive indicators of amyloid-related damage and serve as independent predictors of survival (21). Additionally, research has shown that patients who are LGE-negative exhibit left ventricular diastolic dysfunction as demonstrated by ultrasound (5). Additionally, in patients with amyloidosis exhibiting normal left ventricular wall thickness, there is a decline in diastolic function (22). These findings suggest that dysfunction of left ventricular diastolic function can be observed in the early stages of CA. Furthermore, the absolute values of the global DS parameters GPDSRR 2D, GPDVR 3D, and GPDVC 3D were significantly lower compared to those in the healthy control group, while GPDSRC 3D also showed a reduction relative to the healthy control group. Importantly, the ROC curve indicates that these global DS parameters possess high diagnostic value for myocardial strain in patients with LGE-negative CA. However, it is worth noting that the peak filling rate of the left ventricle in LGE-negative patients was reduced; nevertheless, there was no statistically significant difference when compared to the normal group, indicating that this parameter may not be sufficiently sensitive to reflect diastolic dysfunction in the early stages of CA. As amyloidosis is a progressive disease with multi-organ deposition, the involvement and severity of the heart are very important for the timely diagnosis, grading, and treatment. The early clinical symptoms of CA are often nonspecific, and previous diagnoses of CA required a myocardial biopsy or positive LGE on MRI. Our research found that changes in DS have already occurred in negative LGE cases, suggesting their potential as novel imaging biomarkers for assessing early myocardial involvement in amyloidosis.

The relatively small sample size in this study may impact the accuracy and reliability of the findings. Additionally, the lack of sufficient biopsy cases prevented differentiation between AL and ATTR types for comparison. Furthermore, the analysis focused solely on LGE-negative and LGE-positive groups, without separately assessing certain LGE and transmural LGE groups, which may limit the evaluation of disease severity. Future studies with a larger sample size are expected to provide further insights.

Conclusions

This study found that compared with the control group, the parameters of global myocardial DS obtained by CMR-FT had already undergone specific changes in CA patients in the LGE-negative group. These parameters could serve as imaging biomarkers for diagnosing early cardiac involvement in CA patients, so as to promote timely clinical treatment.

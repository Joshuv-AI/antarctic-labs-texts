The Ki-67 proliferation index in tertiary lymphoid structures as a predictor of survival outcomes in patients with resectable non-small cell lung cancer
Authors: Xinyu Bao, Mei Xie, Xuwen Lin, Yiming Liu, Yinguang Zhang, Xidong Ma, Xin Zhang, Yiran Liang, Xinying Xue, Xuefeng Zang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background & objectivesAlthough the presence of tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS) has been associated with improved immunotherapy efficacy across multiple solid tumors, the prognostic significance of proliferative activity within TLS in patients with resectable non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) remains unclear. This study aims to evaluate the predictive value of Ki-67 expression within TLS for survival outcomes in patients with NSCLC and to explore the association between TLS maturity and the cellular proliferation index within these structures.MethodsPostoperative paraffin-embedded pathological specimens from 88 patients with NSCLC were analyzed. Hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) staining and immunohistochemistry (IHC) were performed to evaluate the presence, spatial distribution, and number of TLS. Multiplex immunofluorescence (mIF) staining was used to assess TLS maturity. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was conducted to determine the prognostic significance of TLS maturity and the cellular proliferation index within TLS.ResultsTumor-associated TLS were identified in the tumor microenvironment of 74 (84.1%) of the 88 patients. The mature TLS was significantly associated with improved recurrence-free survival (RFS) in patients with NSCLC (P = 0.0068). The cellular proliferation index within TLS demonstrated a significant positive correlation with TLS maturity. Compared with early-stage TLS and primary follicular-like TLS, secondary follicular-like TLS exhibited a significantly higher proliferation index (P < 0.0001). Furthermore, a high TLS cellular proliferation index was predictive of favorable RFS in patients with NSCLC (P = 0.0231). Multivariate analysis further indicated that a high TLS cellular proliferation index was an independent favorable prognostic factor for RFS (HR = 0.487, 95% CI: 0.269–0.879; P = 0.017).ConclusionThis study demonstrates the prognostic relevance of the cellular proliferation index within TLS in the tumor microenvironment of NSCLC. A high TLS cellular proliferation index was significantly associated with prolonged RFS. These findings suggest that an elevated TLS cellular proliferation index may contribute to improved clinical outcomes by promoting TLS maturation and enhancing antitumor immune responses.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12885-026-16525-x.

Introduction

Lung cancer remains the most prevalent malignant tumor worldwide in terms of both incidence and mortality. According to the most recent cancer statistics from China, lung cancer accounts for 42.5% of all cancer cases and 32.5% of cancer-related deaths, markedly exceeding those of other malignancies [1, 2]. Of these cases, approximately 80%-85% are classified as non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) [3], making it the predominant histological subtype and a primary focus of clinical research. In recent years, immunotherapy, especially therapies based on immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), has substantially transformed treatment strategies for lung cancer and stimulated growing interest in tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS) within the tumor microenvironment.

Tertiary lymphoid structures are organized aggregates of immune cells that arise in response to chronic inflammation or persistent immune stimulation and are commonly observed in pathological conditions such as chronic infections, allogeneic transplant rejection, and malignancies [4], Structurally, TLS are characterized by a central B-cell-rich zone surrounded by a peripheral T-cell-rich zone. Within the tumor microenvironment, TLS function as sites for immune cell recruitment, particularly of B cells, and serve as privileged niches for local antigen presentation and lymphocyte clonal expansion [5, 6]. Functionally mature TLS are capable of eliciting robust antitumor immune responses and have been significantly associated with improved therapeutic responses and survival outcomes across multiple solid tumors, including lung, pancreatic, and renal cancers [5, 7, 8]. Despite their prognostic relevance, current assessment of TLS maturity largely depends on complex and costly immunofluorescence-based techniques, which limits their routine clinical application. Accumulating evidence indicates that Ki-67 is a characteristic molecular marker of secondary follicular-like TLS and is routinely used as an immunohistochemical (IHC) marker in postoperative lung cancer specimens. Therefore, investigating the association between Ki-67 expression levels and TLS maturity may provide a practical approach to preliminarily evaluate TLS maturation using conventional Ki-67 IHC staining of tumor sections, thereby enabling prognostic prediction in patients with NSCLC.

Ki-67 is a nuclear protein closely associated with the cell cycle and serves as a marker of cellular proliferative activity [9]. Current evidence suggests that a high Ki-67 proliferation index in tumor cells is often associated with poorer postoperative survival in patients with NSCLC [9–11]. However, the prognostic significance of proliferative activity within immune cells in solid tumors remains largely unexplored. Multiple studies have identified Ki-67 as a characteristic marker of mature TLS [12–14]. Genomic analyses of melanoma patients receiving immunotherapy have demonstrated that proliferative tumor-associated B cells are predominantly enriched within the follicular centers of mature TLS and are surrounded by a higher proportion of BCL-2-positive CD4 + T cells, indicative of an antigen-activated phenotype. These findings suggest that proliferative B cells may contribute to antitumor immunity through immune activation and differentiation processes [15]. Accordingly, the proliferative status of immune cells within TLS may be closely associated with clinical outcomes.In this study, we aim to quantify the proliferative activity within TLS and evaluate its prognostic value. The inclusion of surgically resected patients who did not receive neoadjuvant therapy enables a more direct assessment of the potential antitumor immune effects mediated by TLS-associated cellular proliferation.

In this study, the location, quantity, and maturity of TLS were systematically evaluated using hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) staining, immunohistochemistry, and multiplex immunofluorescence (mIF) staining of postoperative pathological specimens. First, we assessed the prognostic impact of TLS maturity in patients with resectable NSCLC. Second, we examined the association between TLS maturity and cellular proliferation within TLS. Finally, we investigated the prognostic value of TLS-associated cellular proliferation for survival outcomes in NSCLC. Collectively, this study aims to provide novel insights to support personalized and precision-based diagnostic and therapeutic strategies for patients with resectable NSCLC.

Materials and methods

Patient cohort selection and data collection

This retrospective study included 88 patients with NSCLC who underwent surgical resection at the Department of Thoracic Surgery, Chinese PLA General Hospital, and Beijing Century Hospital affiliated with Capital Medical University between December 2009 and February 2021. The inclusion criteria were as follows: age ≥ 18 years; radical surgical resection of lung cancer with postoperative pathological confirmation of NSCLC; no prior systemic therapy, including chemotherapy, targeted therapy, or immunotherapy; no history of other malignancies within 5 years before surgery; no perioperative mortality; and complete and accessible baseline clinicopathological and survival data. The exclusion criteria included unresectable disease or distant metastasis; postoperative pathological diagnosis of small cell lung cancer (SCLC); and incomplete baseline clinicopathological or follow-up data.Patients were followed every three months for the first two years after surgery, with a total follow-up duration of five years. The primary study endpoint was recurrence-free survival (RFS), defined as the interval from surgery to tumor recurrence, death, or the last follow-up. The secondary endpoint was overall survival (OS), defined as the time from surgery to death or the last follow-up.Formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue specimens were obtained from all patients. Tissue samples from the deepest tumor margin were selected to prepare 4-µm serial sections for H&E, IHC, and mIF staining.All stained sections were independently evaluated by two experienced pathologists who were blinded to patients’ clinical characteristics and outcomes. Any discrepancies were resolved by consultation with a third senior pathologist. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Beijing Shijitan Hospital. Written informed consent was obtained from all participating patients.

Ki-67 immunohistochemical staining

Formalin-fixed FFPE tissue sections (4 μm thick) were heated at 65 °C oven for 60 min, progressively deparaffinization using xylene I-III and rehydration through graded ethanol (100%, 95%, 85%, and 75%). After washing with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), antigen retrieval was performed in EDTA buffer (pH 9.0) using an autoclave (1800 W) for 2.5 min. Sections were cooled to room temperature under running water.Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked with 3% hydrogen peroxide for 10 min at room temperature. After PBS washing, sections were blocked with goat serum for 10 min and incubated with anti-Ki-67 antibody (ZM-0166, Zsbio, China) at 37 °C for 1 h. Washing 3–5 times with PBS, a one-step secondary antibody detection system (PV-8000D, Zsbio, China) was applied for 30 min at 37 °C. Immunoreactivity was visualized using 3,3′-diaminobenzidine (DAB; ZLI-9018, Zsbio, China) for 6–8 min.Sections were counterstained with hematoxylin, differentiated in 1% acid alcohol, dehydrated through graded alcohol, cleared in xylene, and mounted with neutral resin. Finally, slides were scanned using the PerkinElmer Vectra Polaris automated quantitative pathology imaging system.

Multiplex immunofluorescence staining

FFPE tissue sections were processed for deparaffinization, rehydration, and antigen retrieval as described for IHC. Sections were incubated with 3% hydrogen peroxide for 10 min at room temperature, washed with TBST, and blocked with goat serum for 10 min. Slides were then incubated at 37 °C for 60 min with the following primary antibodies: anti-pan-cytokeratin (PAN-CK; 1:100; ZM0069, Zsbio, China), anti-CD3 (1:100; ZM0417, Zsbio, China), anti-CD20 (1:100; ab78237, Abcam, UK), anti-CD21 (1:100; ZM0040, Zsbio, China), anti-CD23 (1:100; ZA0516, Zsbio, China), and anti-Ki-67 (1:100; ZM0167, Zsbio, China).After three washes with TBST (5 min each), sections were incubated with an Opal anti-Ms + Rb horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibody (ARH1001EA, USA) for 60 min at room temperature in the dark.Following three TBST washes, tyramide signal amplification (TSA) was performed using the AlphaTSA Multiplex IHC Kit (AXT37100031, AlphaX, China). Opal fluorescent conjugates—XTSA 650 (PAN-CK), XTSA 520 (CD3), XTSA 480 (CD20), XTSA 620 (CD21), XTSA 570 (CD23), and XTSA 690 (Ki-67)—were diluted 1:100 in amplification buffer and sequentially applied with the corresponding primary antibodies, with microwave treatment after each labeling cycle to remove antibody-TSA complexes.Sections were counterstained with 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) for 5–10 min, washed with TBST, and mounted using an antifade mounting medium (IH0252, Leagene, Beijing, China). Finally, slides were scanned using the PerkinElmer Vectra Polaris automated quantitative pathology imaging system.

Pathological identification and maturity assessment of tertiary lymphoid structures

TLS are defined as organized immune cell aggregates composed of a central B-cell-rich zone (CD20⁺) surrounded by a peripheral T-cell-rich zone (CD3⁺). All available stained sections were scanned as whole-slide images and evaluated across the entire tissue area. In this study, only TLS with a surface area greater than 10,000 μm² and containing at least 50 cells were included for analysis [16, 17]. TLS were identified using established histological and immunophenotypic criteria and were further annotated according to their spatial relationship with tumour tissue. Specifically, TLS located within 500 μm of the tumour boundary were classified as tumour invasive margin TLS (TIM-TLS) [18], whereas TLS located within tumour tissue were classified as intratumoural TLS (Intra-TLS). Previous studies have demonstrated that both intratumoral and peritumoral TLS are significantly associated with favorable survival outcomes and immunotherapy response in various solid tumors [17, 19–23]. Therefore, TIM-TLS and Intra-TLS were collectively defined as tumour-associated TLS (TA-TLS) (Fig. 1a, b)and were the main focus of the present analysis.

Based on established criteria [24–26], TLS maturity was classified into three developmental stages: (1) early TLS (E-TLS), defined as CD21–/CD23–loose lymphocyte aggregates lacking distinct follicular architecture; (2) primary follicle-like TLS (PFL-TLS), defined as CD21+/CD23- denser lymphocyte aggregates that lack germinal centers-like structures; and (3) secondary follicle-like TLS (SFL-TLS), defined as dense lymphocyte aggregates containing with CD23 + follicular dendritic cell networks and germinal-center-like architecture, irrespective of CD21 expression status. SFL-TLS represent the most structurally organized TLS subtype and are more likely to support a robust antitumour immune response [4]. Previous studies have commonly classified SFL-TLS as mature TLS and have shown that mature TLS are associated with favourable prognosis and improved antitumour immunity in several solid tumours [17, 27–29]. Therefore, we categorized SFL-TLS as mature TLS, whereas E-TLS and PFL-TLS were classified as immature TLS in this study.

TLS maturity was quantified on a per-patient basis using multiplex immunofluorescence-stained slides. Specifically, the numbers of m-TLS and total TLS were counted, and the proportion of mature TLS among all TLS (mTLS%) was calculated to reflect the overall maturity of TLS in each patient. A higher mTLS% value indicates a greater degree of TLS maturation.Detailed information on TLS counts is provided in Supplementary Table S1. Inter-observer agreement was assessed using Cohen’s kappa statistics. The kappa value for TLS presence or absence was 0.629, indicating substantial agreement between the two evaluators. The kappa value for TLS maturity classification was 0.847, indicating excellent agreement. Discrepant evaluations were resolved by consensus with a senior pathologist.

Quantitative analysis of TLS proliferation levels

To accurately assess the proliferative activity of TLS, CaseViewer software (version 2.4.0) and QuPath software (version 0.4.4) [30] were used for image visualization and quantitative analysis following immunohistochemical and multiplex immunofluorescence staining. To ensure accuracy and representativeness in evaluating cellular proliferation within TLS, established methodologies for assessing tumor cell proliferative activity were applied. Briefly, areas with the highest proliferative activity (hotspots) were identified under a 400× microscope, and all Ki-67-positive cells with staining intensity exceeding background levels were included. Five to ten randomly selected high-power fields were analyzed, with 100–200 cells counted per field. The average value was calculated to determine the proliferation index [9, 31–33].TLS Ki-67 proliferation index(TLS Ki-67 PI) quantification analysis was based on whole-section TLS, each tumor-associated TLS was manually annotated as a region of interest in QuPath. The number of TLS Ki-67-positive cells and the total number of TLS nucleated cells within each TLS were quantified using the built-in cell-detection algorithm. The TLS Ki-67 PI for each TLS was calculated as the percentage of Ki-67-positive cells within TLS among total TLS cells. The each patient-level TLS Ki-67 PI was calculated as the mean TLS Ki-67 PI across all tumor-associated TLS analyzed for that patient, which comprehensively reflects the proliferative activity of immune cells within TLS.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 26.0), GraphPad Prism (version 9.3.0), and QuPath (version 0.4.4). Descriptive statistics were used to summarize the distribution of study variables. Continuous variables are presented as the mean ± standard error, and comparisons between groups were conducted using the two-tailed Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables are expressed as percentages and were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Kaplan-Meier survival curves for RFS were generated, and differences between groups were assessed using the log-rank test. Independent prognostic factors were identified using univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses, with variables showing P < 0.05 in univariate analysis entered into the multivariate model. A two-tailed P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

The RFS and OS were 42.5 months (range, 1.5–143.0 months) and 65.3 months (range, 15.9-151.8 months), respectively. Additional baseline clinicopathological characteristics are summarized in Table 1.

Predictive value of TLS maturity for recurrence-free survival in patients with resectable NSCLC

To explore the impact of TLS maturity on clinical outcomes in patients with surgically resected NSCLC, TLS were initially assessed on H&E stained sections from 88 patients. Pathological specimens from the 74 TLS-positive patients were subsequently selected mIF staining. Consecutive sections were selected for mIF and HE staining to enable precise spatial alignment of TLS subregional distribution (Fig. 1a). Compared with mIF, H&E staining provided clearer histological architecture, facilitating accurate delineation of intratumoral regions and tumor invasive margins within the corresponding mIF images (Fig. 1b). TLS maturity was evaluated by mIF using specific markers (CD3, CD20, CD21, and CD23) (Fig. 1c). Early TLS were characterized by sparse and irregular distributions of CD3⁺ T cells and CD20⁺ B cells, with negative expression of CD21 and CD23. Primary follicle-like TLS exhibited denser and more organized lymphocyte aggregates, with ordered CD3⁺ T-cell and CD20⁺ B-cell distributions, central CD21 reticular positivity, and absence of CD23 expression. Secondary follicle-like TLS presented as circular or oval lymphoid aggregates composed of densely packed CD3⁺ T cells and CD20⁺ B cells, with CD21⁺CD23⁺ reticular networks indicative of germinal center formation.

Tumor-associated TLS were observed in 74 of 88 patients. SFL-TLS were classified as mature TLS (mTLS), while the E-TLS and PFL-TLS were categorized as immature TLS (imTLS). Among these patients, only 59 (79.7%) exhibited mTLS. TLS maturity was quantified by calculating the proportion of mTLS relative to the total number of TLS within each tumor region (mTLS%). Patients were stratified into high (≥ 35.0%) and low (< 35.0%) mTLS% groups based on the median value (35.0%). Survival analysis demonstrated that the high mTLS% group had significantly longer median RFS compared with the low mTLS% group (56.7 vs. 26.7 months, P = 0.0068; Fig. 1d), as well as higher 3-year RFS rates (35.1% vs. 21.6%, P = 0.0037). However, no significant difference in OS was observed between the two groups (P = 0.1087; Fig. 1e). These findings indicate that increased TLS maturity is significantly linked with improved RFS in patients with NSCLC, potentially reflecting the more organized structure and enhanced antitumor immune activity of mature TLS.

Association between TLS maturity and cellular proliferative activity within TLS

mIF slides were analyzed using QuPath software to assess TLS maturity and quantify the TLS Ki-67 proliferation index (TLS Ki-67 PI). Comparison of TLS Ki-67 PI across maturity subgroups showed mean values of 2.8%, 2.4%, and 5.3% for E-TLS, PFL-TL, and SFL-TLS, respectively. SFL-TLS exhibited significantly higher TLS Ki-67 PI than both E-TLS and PFL-TLS (P < 0.0001; Fig. 2b), whereas no significant difference was observed between E-TLS and PFL-TLS. Consistently, the mean TLS Ki-67 PI in mature TLS was significantly greater than that in immature TLS (5.3% vs. 2.8%, P < 0.0001; Fig. 2c). Using the mIF-derived median TLS Ki-67 PI value of 2.70% as the cutoff, TLS were further stratified into high- and low-proliferation groups for the maturity–proliferation association analysis. The proportion of high Ki-67 expression was significantly greater in mature TLS than in immature TLS (56.8% [353/621] vs. 42.4% [293/691], P < 0.001; Fig. 2d, e), indicating a close association between TLS maturity and cellular proliferation. Notably, SFL-TLS generally demonstrated elevated proliferative activity, formed typical germinal center-like structures characterized by dense, centrally localized proliferation, whereas proliferating cells in E-TLS and PFL-TLS were diffusely distributed without focal aggregation (Fig. 2a).

Association between TLS proliferation levels and prognosis in patients with resectable NSCLC

Analysis of TLS proliferative activity was based on Ki-67 immunohistochemical staining in 74 patients, the result demonstrated a significant association between TLS proliferative activity and clinical prognosis in NSCLC. TLS were precisely localized within tumor tissue and tumor invasive margin using corresponding H&E-stained sections (Fig. 3a). Kaplan-Meier analysis showed significantly improved RFS in the high TLS Ki-67 PI group in comparison to the low group (P < 0.05; Fig. 3c). These findings indicate that a high TLS Ki-67 PI marked a reduced risk of tumor recurrence in NSCLC and that proliferatively active TLS may play a critical role in mediating effective antitumor immune responses within the tumor microenvironment.

Discussion

In recent years, advances in tumor immunotherapy have spurred exploration of the clinical significance of TLS within the tumor microenvironment. As organized immune cell aggregates, both the density and maturity of TLS have been shown to predict therapeutic response and survival outcomes in patients with solid tumors, including NSCLC, particularly in the context of immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy [7, 20]. However, most existing studies have primarily focused on the morphological characteristics of TLS, with limited systematic evaluation of functional activity markers, especially those reflecting cellular proliferation dynamics. In this study, we introduce the TLS Ki-67 proliferation index (TLS Ki-67 PI) as a novel quantitative metric to address two key questions: (1) whether proliferative activity within TLS independently influences prognosis in patients with NSCLC, and (2) whether TLS maturity mediates antitumor effects through modulation of cellular proliferation. The strong association between this index and maturity of TLS underscores its potential clinical utility. Compared with assessments of TLS maturity that require multiple immunofluorescence markers, Ki-67 immunohistochemical staining is cost-effective, widely available, and more suitable for routine clinical application. Furthermore, the future integration of automated TLS Ki-67 PI quantification using digital pathology algorithms may further facilitate its translation into clinical practice.

This study quantitatively evaluated the presence and maturity of tumor-associated TLS in patients with NSCLC and demonstrated that mature TLS are significantly associated with improved RFS. These findings are consistent with previous reports showing that mature TLS correlate with favorable RFS and progression-free survival (PFS) across multiple solid tumors, including hepatocellular carcinoma, NSCLC, esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, and colorectal cancer. The presence of germinal center-positive TLS (GC⁺ TLS) has been shown to reduce the risk of tumor recurrence in several malignancies, supporting the role of mature TLS as an independent biomarker for predicting cancer recurrence, induction of TLS maturation may represent a potential mechanism underlying the efficacy of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy [20, 34–37]. Mechanistically, mature TLS may enhance antitumor immune responses through multiple pathways, including optimization of antigen presentation, promotion of B-cell proliferation and differentiation, enhancement of humoral immune responses, and activation of cytotoxic T cells [38–41]. In contrast, the unfavorable prognosis associated with immature TLS may be attributable to an immunosuppressive microenvironment, characterized by immune cell dysfunction, increased secretion of immunosuppressive mediators, and global suppression of antitumor immunity [42–45]. Collectively, these findings suggest that, compared with immature TLS, mature TLS possess more complete and robust immune functions, leading to stronger antitumor immune responses and a reduced risk of early tumor recurrence.Despite the strong association between TLS maturity and survival outcomes in NSCLC, the clinical application of TLS maturity as a biomarker is limited by the complexity and cost of multiplex immunofluorescence staining. Importantly, our study demonstrates a strong correlation between TLS proliferative activity and TLS maturity, providing a practical alternative for indirectly assessing TLS maturation using the simpler and more cost-effective Ki-67 immunohistochemical staining approach.

To our knowledge, this study is among the first to investigate the prognostic relevance of proliferative activity within TLS in patients with resectable NSCLC. By linking TLS Ki-67 PI with TLS maturity and RFS, our findings suggest that TLS-associated cellular proliferation may represent a functional feature of mature TLS rather than a purely morphological characteristic.Survival analysis demonstrated that proliferative activity within TLS predicts RFS in patients with NSCLC, with a high TLS Ki-67 PI significantly associated with prolonged RFS. Multivariate analysis further identified a low TLS Ki-67 PI as an independent risk factor for shortened RFS, supporting its potential as an objective biomarker for postoperative recurrence risk.Notably, extensive Ki-67 expression was observed in the central B-cell zone of mature TLS. These regions contained follicle-like germinal centers composed of highly proliferative B cells, consistent with previous reports indicating that germinal center-positive TLS harbor clusters of proliferating B cells [13, 14]. Compared with immature TLS, mature TLS exhibited significantly increased infiltration of CD4⁺ follicular helper T cells (Tfh), Ki-67⁺ B cells, CD21⁺ follicular dendritic cells (FDCs), and plasma cells [39, 40, 46]. The presence of Ki-67⁺ B cells reflects functionally active TLS, as proliferating B cells interact with CD23⁺ FDCs and CD4⁺ Tfh cells to enhance humoral immune responses, thereby contributing to improved treatment responses and survival outcomes [47].Consistent with these findings, correlation analysis demonstrated that mature TLS displayed significantly higher cellular proliferation indices than immature TLS. Cellular proliferation is a critical biological process underlying TLS development and maturation, mature TLS typically exhibit increased proliferative activity, largely driven by the formation and functional optimization of germinal centers. The germinal center reaction generates abundant effector cells and enhances the specificity and magnitude of antitumor immune responses through the selection and expansion of high-affinity B-cell clones [48–51]. Biologically, increased Ki-67 expression within mature TLS may reflect germinal-center-like proliferative activity in B-cell-rich follicular regions. This process may indicate active local immune responses, including antigen-driven lymphocyte expansion and enhanced antitumor immunity. Therefore, a higher TLS Ki-67 PI may be associated with a more active and mature TLS microenvironment, which may contribute to improved recurrence-free survival. However, further mechanistic studies incorporating definite germinal center B cells markers(such as BCL6, CD10) are required to validate this interpretation.

This study observed a trend toward improved survival in patients with highly mature TLS and more proliferative TLSs, however, TLS Ki-67 PI and mTLS% did not show a statistically significant association with OS in the Kaplan–Meier analyses. One possible explanation is that RFS may more directly reflect the local antitumor immune response and the durability of this response within the tumor microenvironment. Intratumoral immune cells can contribute to local immune surveillance and may thereby reduce the risk of postoperative tumor recurrence. Consistent with this notion, previous studies have reported that higher densities of tumor-infiltrating CD3+, CD20+, and CD8 + cells are associated with improved PFS and a lower risk of early recurrence in patients with hepatocellular carcinoma and endometrial cancer [52, 53]. In contrast, OS is influenced by a broader range of complex clinical and biological factors, including distant metastasis, immune tolerance, tumor immune escape, subsequent anticancer therapies, and the patient’s general health status [54–56], These factors may attenuate or obscure the potential impact of TLS maturity and TLS Ki-67 PI on overall survival. Another plausible explanation is that TLSs may exert a more pronounced effect on local immune surveillance and recurrence control than on systemic disease progression. Although mature TLSs with high proliferative activity may contribute to delaying local recurrence, distant metastasis, second primary malignancies, or non-cancer-related factors may ultimately have a greater influence on long-term survival. Further studies with larger sample sizes, longer follow-up, and a greater number of OS events are warranted to determine whether TLS Ki-67 PI and mTLS% has prognostic value for OS.

This study has several limitations.First, its retrospective design inherently introduces the possibility of selection bias. Second, the cohort comprised a relatively small number of 88 patients with early- to intermediate-stage NSCLC from a single center, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to patients across all disease stages. Third, no external validation cohort was included; therefore, the prognostic value and optimal cutoff of TLS Ki-67 PI require further validation in independent cohorts. In addition, the included patients underwent surgical resection and did not receive neoadjuvant treatment, and the influence of TLS proliferative activity on the prognostic effects of neoadjuvant chemotherapy and immunotherapy remains unclear. Given the heterogeneity of the tumor microenvironment and the complexity of immunoregulatory mechanisms, the direction and magnitude of the association between TLS proliferation and survival outcomes may differ across treatment modalities. Therefore, multicenter prospective studies with larger cohorts, particularly including patients receiving neoadjuvant therapy, are warranted to further clarify the prognostic value of the TLS Ki-67 proliferation index in NSCLC.Finally, the proliferative mechanisms of the cellular subpopulations within TLS were not comprehensively investigated. Vascular structures may contribute to lymphocyte recruitment and TLS development. However, this study focused on immune-cell proliferation within established TLS and did not assess vascular markers such as CD31, CD105, or HEV-related markers(MAdCAM-1, PNAd). We also did not evaluate TLS proliferation-associated tumour lysis or regression, as this mechanism was not directly assessed. Our study focused on TLS maturation and TLS Ki-67 PI and did not provide spatial or functional evidence of direct tumour killing. Future studies should clarify how vascular features, tumour-cell death, and TLS proliferative activity jointly influence TLS maturation and antitumour immunity in NSCLC.

In summary, this study demonstrates that both TLS maturity and high cellular proliferation within TLS are associated with favorable recurrence-free survival in patients with resectable NSCLC. TLS exhibiting higher proliferative activity tend to be more mature and capable of eliciting robust antitumor immune responses, suggesting that immune cell proliferation within TLS may serve as a key indicator of their functional antitumor capacity. The TLS Ki-67 proliferation index shows promise as a potential prognostic biomarker and may provide a methodological foundation for precision immunotherapy strategies stratified according to TLS functional activity.

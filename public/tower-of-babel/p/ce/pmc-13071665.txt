MET amplification in diffuse pleural mesothelioma and response to savolitinib: a case report
Authors: Yueying Chen, Mengyi Shen, Jiawen Lv, Yuwen Rui, Chunwei Xu, Dong Wang, Tangfeng Lv
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPleural mesothelioma (PM) is a rare malignancy globally, most frequently associated with alterations in tumor suppressor genes such as NF2, BAP1 and CDKN2A. In contrast, mutations or amplifications of the MET gene are seldomly reported in PM.Case DescriptionHere, we report the case of a 60-year-old East Asian male without asbestos exposure, never-smoker, diagnosed with advanced PM. The disease proved refractory to multiple lines of therapy, including platinum-based chemotherapy, immunotherapy, intrathoracic instillation and a subsequent individualized regimen comprising intraperitoneal cisplatin plus sintilimab for local disease control and potential immunomodulation synergized with oral anlotinib. Following disease progression accompanied by rapidly accumulating seroperitoneum and pelvic effusion, next-generation sequencing (NGS) of ascitic fluid revealed MET gene amplification, and the subsequent switch to savolitinib controlled the malignant ascites. Unfortunately, the patient died of sudden cardiac and respiratory arrest in December 2023.ConclusionsThis case underscores the critical importance of comprehensive genetic testing in guiding the treatment of advanced PM, particularly after the failure of standard therapies. It illustrates that rare but actionable genomic alterations, such as MET amplification, can be identified through liquid or tissue biopsy and can inform successful targeted treatment strategies, even in a tumor type where such alterations are uncommon. Histological classification combined with molecular profiling remains pivotal for personalized oncology.Keywords: MET amplification, pleural mesothelioma (PM), savolitinib, case report

Highlight box

Key findings

• A case of advanced pleural mesothelioma (PM) with a rare MET gene amplification was identified through next-generation sequencing of malignant ascites, following disease progression on standard chemotherapy and subsequent immunotherapy, underscoring the necessity of broader molecular profiling to uncover rare, yet actionable, genomic alterations in treatment-refractory settings.

What is known and what is new?

• PM is generally considered an immunologically “cold” tumor and molecularly characterized by alterations in genes like NF2, BAP1, and CDKN2A, with MET aberrations being uncommon. The prognosis remains poor post-first-line therapy, which includes chemotherapy and immunotherapy.

• This report provides evidence that MET amplification can serve as a resistance mechanism and a viable therapeutic target in PM. It demonstrates the clinical utility of plasma- or effusion-based biopsy in identifying actionable targets when tissue is unavailable or insufficient. It also adds to the emerging narrative that MET inhibition may yield clinical benefit in a subset of PM patients.

What is the implication and what should change now?

• The finding of a responsive MET alteration implies that a subset of PM patients harbor rare, actionable oncogenic drivers, expanding the scope for precision medicine beyond conventional genetic profiles.

• Comprehensive genomic profiling should be routinely considered for advanced PM. Liquid biopsy from effusions or plasma represents a critical alternative tool for genomic testing when tissue sample is inaccessible upon standard therapy failure.

Introduction

Pleural mesothelioma (PM) belongs to the group of mesotheliomas. Mesothelioma remains rare globally and it can be categorized into three major histological subtypes according to the World Health Organization (WHO) classification: epithelioid, sarcomatoid and biphasic type (1). For advanced PM, systemic treatment options include pemetrexed-carboplatin with or without bevacizumab, as well as dual immune checkpoint inhibition (nivolumab plus ipilimumab), both of which have been widely acknowledged as recommended therapeutic options (2). Nevertheless, the prognosis of advanced PM remains poor, underscoring the need for more effective therapeutic strategies.

At the molecular level, PM is frequently characterized by alterations in tumor suppressor genes such as neurofibromatosis 2 (NF2), BRCA1-associated protein-1 (BAP1) and cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor 2A (CDKN2A) (3). Beyond these common mutations, dysregulation of the N-methyl-N’-nitro-N-nitrosoguanidine (MNNG)-human osteosarcoma (HOS) transforming gene (MET) has been implicated. Located on chromosome 7q21-31, the MET gene encodes a receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK) activated by hepatocyte growth factor (HGF), triggering downstream signaling cascades including PI3K-AKT, MAPK, PI3K-AKT, JAK-STAT, and Wnt/β-catenin, which collectively promote cell proliferation, survival, and motility (4). MET aberrations typically occurs through exon 14-skipping (METex14) mutation, gene amplification (MET-amp) and protein overexpression (5). Although MET alterations are rare in mesothelioma, they represent a potential therapeutic target in selected cases.

This report presents a rare case of PM in which the patient experienced rapid progression of hydrothorax following initial chemotherapy and immunotherapy, accompanied by newly emerged seroperitoneum and pelvic effusion. Subsequent genetic testing of ascitic fluid revealed MET-amp, an uncommon molecular event in this malignancy. The patient was then treated with savolitinib, a highly selective MET tyrosine kinase inhibitor (MET-TKI), which successfully controlled the progression of the malignant ascites. We present this article in accordance with the CARE reporting checklist (available at https://tlcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tlcr-2025-aw-1313/rc).

Case presentation

A 60-year-old East Asian non-smoking man was initially referred to Jinling Hospital on July 5, 2023, with a 1-month history of progressive breathlessness following motion. A history of asbestos exposure was denied. Pleural puncture and drainage were performed at a local hospital for symptomatic relief. Cytological examination of the effusion confirmed the presence of atypical cells suspicious for malignancy. Given the clinical context of progressive disease and to explore potential actionable targets, the malignant pleural effusion (MPE) was subjected to next-generation sequencing (NGS) using a commercially available, targeted gene panel covering key cancer-related genes. This analysis identified somatic mutations in both mitogen-activated protein kinase 1 (MAP2K1) [9% variant allele frequency (VAF)] and tumor protein p53 (TP53) (11.6% VAF). Positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET-CT) images indicated extensive and heterogeneously thickened right pleural and interlobar fissure with increased fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG) metabolism (Figure 1). Percutaneous biopsy of pleura guided by fusion CT was performed after admission to our ward (Figure 2). Photomicroscopy with hematoxylin and eosin staining revealed unusual epithelioid cells of the primary tumor. Immunohistochemically, the tumor cells were positive for calretinin, CK, BAP1 and Ki-67 but negative for TTF-1, CEA, NapsinA, WT-1 and p40 (Figure 3), which help rule out other malignancies (6). These results were highly suggestive of PM.

Both the initial platinum-based chemotherapy with immunotherapy and intrathoracic injections of therapeutic agents failed to control the advancement of hydrothorax, as well as the development of seroperitoneum and pelvic effusion. Following these challenges, the patient was readmitted to Jinling Hospital on August 23 and underwent paracentesis. The results of the ascites smear examination identified malignant cells with morphological features consistent with metastatic mesothelioma, confirming disease progression with peritoneal involvement. Facing limited standard options for controlling malignant ascites post-systemic therapy, and based on the rationale of combining local cytotoxic therapy with systemic therapy to target multiple pathways, an individualized regimen of intraperitoneal cisplatin plus sintilimab alongside oral anlotinib was initiated. Unfortunately, this approach was unable to control the ascites or halt systemic disease progression. Surprisingly, NGS of cell-free DNA from the seroperitoneum, performed using the Kanghui capsmartTM panel (56 genes) with a validated copy number variation (CNV) detection sensitivity (fold-change ≥2.0; confidence >99%), revealed MET gene copy number (CN) amplification (CN =7.54, Tier I alteration). Concurrent Tier II alterations included MAP2K1 (15.89% VAF), TP53 (13.09% VAF), and ARID1A (20.02% VAF) mutations. Programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) immunohistochemistry (IHC) on a cell block was negative. Considering this result, the targeted therapy regimen was subsequently modified to include savolitinib (400 mg q.d. p.o.) which successfully controlled the progression of hydrothorax and seroperitoneum, with abdominal distension and chest tightness relieved. The patient was discharged from hospital on October 16, 2023 and continued to take savolitinib. Unfortunately, the patient passed away in December 2023. The death was reported by the family as a sudden cardiac and respiratory arrest occurring at a local hospital. No autopsy or detailed post-mortem examination was performed. Discussion

The pleural is the most common site of origin for mesothelioma (7). While mesothelioma typically confined to the cavity of origin, distant metastasis can occur, in advanced stages as evidenced in this case by the identification of tumor cells in peritoneal fluid. NGS analysis of this effusion confirmed MET amplification, supporting the diagnosis if distant metastasis from PM. PM exhibits substantial spatial and temporal molecular heterogeneity at both histological and molecular levels, with the relatively indolent epithelioid subtype and the more aggressive sarcomatoid subtype representing two ends of the spectrum, while the biphasic subtype combines features of both (1). Histopathological examination and extensive immunohistochemical profile remain the diagnostic gold standard. Advanced techniques like NGS assist in integrating histopathologic features with the molecular mechanisms of tumorigenesis and development, thereby informing clinical practice (8). Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) is the gold standard for detecting MET-amp, and the ratio of the MET gene-to-the centromere of chromosome 7 (MET/CEP7) helps distinguish between focal amplification and polysomy which may differ in the response to targeted therapy. With NGS serving as another primary method, both are influenced by tumor purity and DNA content (4). Thus, tumor tissue and cytology samples are preferred, with plasma- or effusion-based biopsy as a supplementary option because of the unavailable tissue sample in this case despite the limitations.

Notably, NGS analysis of the pleural effusion revealed co-occurring mutations in MAP2K1 and TP53. While TP53 alterations can occur in a subset of mesotheliomas, MAP2K1 mutations are exceedingly rare in PM and are more commonly associated with malignancies such as melanoma, colorectal carcinoma, and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) (9). This finding necessitated a careful evaluation of the primary diagnosis. However, the diagnosis of epithelioid mesothelioma in this case was established by the initial histopathological and extensive IHC profile (positive for calretinin, WT-1, D2-40, and negative for lung/thyroid markers) from the biopsy, which is the diagnostic gold standard. The unexpected MAP2K1 mutation may represent a rare molecular event within this PM, a subclonal alteration acquired under therapeutic pressure, or potential biological heterogeneity. Nonetheless, it underscores the critical principle that molecular findings from liquid biopsies must always be interpreted within the context of a robust histopathological diagnosis and should primarily guide targeted therapy selection rather than redefine the primary tumor type in a well-characterized case.

Following disease progression on first-line platinum-pemetrexed chemotherapy, and considering financial constraints that limited access to the standard dual-immunotherapy regimen (nivolumab plus ipilimumab), the patient received alternative therapy with pemetrexed and the programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) antibody tislelizumab, supplemented empirically with the anti-angiogenic agent recombinant human endostatin. For persistent pleural effusion, an experimental intrapleural infusion of recombinant mutant human tumor necrosis factor (rmhTNF) was administered based on its potential immunomodulatory and direct tumoricidal effects.

After the failure of conventional intravenous chemotherapy and immunotherapy, the patient received oral anlotinib and sintilimab injections to control disease progression. Antiangiogenic therapy has been acknowledged as a viable alternative options given the critical role of angiogenesis in tumor growth. Elevated levels of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), fibroblast growth factor (FGF) and other proangiogenic factors correlated with tumor aggressiveness have been detected in mesothelioma samples (1). While the addition of intravenous bevacizumab is recommended in PM treatment guidelines, oral anlotinib has also demonstrated survival benefits in clinical practice (10). In a preclinical trial conducted by our team, the therapeutic effect and mechanism of intrathoracic sintilimab were investigated in an MPE mouse model (11). Extensive infiltration of CD8+ T cells and cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs) along with cytokines changes were observed in the microenvironment. Mice receiving intrathoracic sintilimab showed prolonged survival and reduced pleural effusion, supporting local administration as a strategy for MPE control. Furthermore, our original clinical trial (ChiCTR2400087743) reported the potential of intrathoracic sintilimab to delay cancer progression. Considering prior treatment, economic conditions and the goal of targeting multiple pathways, the patient received an individualized regimen comprising intraperitoneal cisplatin and sintilimab along with oral anlotinib after disease progression as a consequent. This combination was empirically chosen based on its reported anti-angiogenic and potential synergistic effect with immunotherapy in advanced thoracic malignancies.

Compared to METex14 skipping, MET-amp is a more complicated alteration occurring across varied clinical contexts and tumor types (12) which has been linked to higher histologic grade, advanced clinical stages, and poor prognosis (4). It may also arise as an acquired resistance mechanism. Some studies suggest that MET amplification may be associated with upregulation of PD-L1 expression and an inflamed tumor microenvironment mediated via the JAK/STAT3 pathway, providing a rationale for combining MET TKIs with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors (13). These findings support the potential synergy between MET-TKIs and PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, and suggest that PD-L1 positivity may help predict treatment efficacy (14). Conversely, other evidence indicates that MET gene amplification can downregulates stimulator of interferon genes (STING) signaling and reduce T cell infiltration, potentially leading to resistance to anti-PD-(L)1 antibodies (15). Notably, given that mesothelioma is generally characterized by low immune infiltration (1), and neither PD-L1 expression nor tumor mutation burden (TMB) reliably predicts treatment response in PM. This necessitates the identification of more robust biomarkers for patient stratification and selection (16).

Although MET amplification has been more extensively studied in other solid tumors such as NSCLC, it remains exceptionally rare in PM, with only a handful of published case reports and even fewer detailed accounts of MET TKI clinical application in this setting (17,18). Therefore, the value of this case lies not only in identifying this rare, actionable alteration via effusion-based NGS but also in providing a valuable “proof-of-concept” for the clinical activity of a MET TKI (savolitinib) in this molecular subset of PM. A key limitation of this report is the lack of Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST)-based radiological confirmation of response to savolitinib. While symptomatic relief was noted, the transient clinical course (patient deceased within two months) and the concurrent administration of palliative thoracentesis/paracentesis preclude definitive attribution of benefit solely to the targeted therapy. Additionally, the unavailability of archival PET-CT images for publication, due to technical challenges in retrieving original Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine (DICOM) data from the referring hospital, further constrains the objective assessment of disease burden and metabolic response. Moreover, the diagnosis was made after an integrated clinicoradiologic-pathologic assessment. While limited biopsy material precluded additional immunohistochemical and special stains in this case, we recommend that a complete WHO-recommended IHC panel should better be performed for differential diagnosis and classification.

Conclusions

In summary, this case underscores the pivotal role of pathological assessment via invasive pleural biopsy in determining histological type and genetic profile, which in turn guides treatment planning and prognostic evaluation. It is noteworthy that, although immunotherapy has improved survival in PM, the absence of reliable predictive biomarkers for patient selection remains a challenge, warranting further exploration.

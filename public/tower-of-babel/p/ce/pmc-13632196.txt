Anesthesia in systemic lupus erythematosus: a review of key considerations and surgical challenges
Authors: Ali Mohammadi, Vahid Fattahi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSystemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE) presents complex challenges in the perioperative setting due to its multisystem involvement, immune-mediated pathology, and heterogeneous presentation. This narrative review explores the pathogenesis of SLE and its surgical implications, emphasizing how immune dysregulation, vascular endothelial dysfunction, and immunosuppressive therapy influence anesthetic management. It discusses the importance of comprehensive preoperative assessment, focusing on cardiovascular, pulmonary, renal, neurological, and hematological systems, to guide individualized anesthetic planning. Special attention is given to pharmacotherapy management, perioperative monitoring, and strategies to prevent disease flares. The role of spinal, epidural, and general anesthesia in surgical procedures and obstetric care for SLE patients is analyzed, highlighting benefits, risks, and precautions. By synthesizing multidisciplinary strategies and tailoring anesthetic approaches to SLE’s variability, this review provides a framework to guide clinicians in optimizing perioperative safety for SLE patients. Future research should focus on standardized protocols and novel monitoring technologies to enhance outcomes.Keywords: Systemic lupus erythematosus, Anesthesia management, Perioperative care, Regional anesthesia, General anesthesia, Antiphospholipid syndrome

Introduction

Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE) is a chronic autoimmune disease with a prevalence of 20–150 per 100,000, predominantly affecting women of childbearing age (Lisnevskaia et al. 2014). Characterized by immune dysregulation, autoantibody production, and multisystem inflammation, SLE’s heterogeneous presentation impacts organs such as the kidneys, heart, lungs, and hematologic system, posing significant perioperative challenges (Lisnevskaia et al. 2014; Tsokos 2011). The increasing surgical burden in autoimmune populations, driven by disease-related complications (e.g., lupus nephritis, cutaneous lesions) and unrelated procedures (e.g., elective orthopedic or obstetric surgeries), underscores the need for tailored anesthetic strategies (Stull et al. 2023; Hannon et al. 2021). Complications like renal impairment, cardiovascular disease, pulmonary involvement, and thrombotic risks, particularly in patients with Antiphospholipid Syndrome (APS), necessitate individualized approaches (Ben-Menachem 2010; Gualtierotti et al. 2018). Immunosuppressive therapies, such as corticosteroids, increase infection risk, suppress the adrenocortical axis, and delay wound healing, while surgical stress may exacerbate disease activity (Zisa and Goodman 2022; Wang et al. 2013). Regional anesthesia, such as spinal or epidural techniques, potentially avoids airway manipulation, benefiting patients with rare laryngeal or cervical involvement, though evidence on flare reduction is limited (Vyas et al. 2014). This narrative review synthesizes evidence-based strategies for anesthesia management in SLE, focusing on preoperative evaluation, anesthetic technique selection, pharmacotherapy adjustments, and postoperative monitoring to guide clinicians.

Methods

This narrative review was conducted through a structured search of PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and Google Scholar, covering literature from January 2000 to March 2024. Search terms included “Systemic Lupus Erythematosus,” “anesthesia,” “perioperative management,” “regional anesthesia,” “spinal anesthesia,” and “pregnancy.” Inclusion criteria were: (1) peer-reviewed articles in English; (2) clinical trials, observational studies, reviews, and case reports focused on anesthetic practices in adult SLE patients; (3) articles addressing perioperative or intraoperative management. Exclusion criteria were: (1) articles unrelated to anesthesia; (2) studies on pediatric populations or basic immunological mechanisms without clinical correlation; (3) non-English articles. A total of 120 articles were screened, with 56 included after excluding duplicates (n = 32), irrelevant studies (n = 20), and non-English articles (n = 12). No formal quality assessment was performed, but priority was given to high-impact journals and recent publications. Data were manually extracted and categorized into thematic domains: general anesthetic implications, regional and general anesthesia considerations, obstetric challenges, and organ-specific risks. The review aims to provide anesthesiologists with a clinically applicable guide for managing SLE patients.

Preoperative considerations

Pathogenesis and surgical implications

Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE) results from immune dysregulation, leading to autoantibody production and immune complex deposition, causing inflammation and tissue damage (Lisnevskaia et al. 2014). This systemic inflammation impairs wound healing, increases infection risk, and elevates thrombosis propensity, particularly in APS patients (Glueck et al. 1985; Bazzan et al. 2015). Vascular endothelial dysfunction may complicate intraoperative management, affecting tissue perfusion (Moschetti et al. 2022). Surgical stress may exacerbate disease activity, with studies noting flares post-surgery, particularly in patients with high Systemic Lupus Erythematosus Disease Activity Index (SLEDAI) scores (> 8), a validated tool assessing clinical and laboratory disease activity (Katz et al. 2023; Adamichou and Bertsias 2017). The SLEDAI evaluates 24 descriptors (e.g., rash, arthritis, proteinuria, seizures), assigning weighted scores (1–8) based on severity, with higher scores indicating greater disease activity. Other scoring systems, such as the British Isles Lupus Assessment Group (BILAG) index, assess organ-specific activity but are less commonly used perioperatively due to complexity (Adamichou and Bertsias 2017). Corticosteroids, which suppress the adrenocortical axis, further increase infection and adrenal insufficiency risks (Wang et al. 2013). Preoperative optimization, including disease stabilization and thromboprophylaxis, is critical (Boyce and Massicotte 2020). A flowchart for preoperative planning is provided (Fig. 1) (Table 2).

Diagnosis and clinical features relevant to anesthesia

Effective preoperative evaluation requires understanding SLE’s systemic nature. Diagnosis relies on laboratory findings (e.g., Antinuclear Antibody [ANA], anti-double-stranded DNA [anti-dsDNA]) and clinical signs, such as organ-specific involvement (Tables 3) (Ben-Menachem 2010; Aringer 2020). A thorough workup assesses disease activity, organ damage, and perioperative risks. Recommended tests, tailored to symptoms, include complete blood count (CBC), comprehensive metabolic panel (CMP), electrocardiogram (ECG), echocardiography for cardiovascular symptoms, pulmonary function tests (PFTs) for respiratory symptoms, and coagulation studies (e.g., PT/INR, aPTT, lupus anticoagulant) for APS or thrombocytopenia (Ben-Menachem 2010; Nikdoust et al. 2018).

Organ-specific considerations

SLE’s multisystem involvement requires tailored preoperative evaluation to address organ activity versus damage (Bultink and Lems 2015). Key considerations include:

Cardiovascular System: Active pericarditis, myocarditis, dysrhythmias, or vasculitis increase arrhythmia and ischemic risks (Alghareeb et al. 2022; Doria et al. 2005). Echocardiography is recommended for symptomatic patients; invasive monitoring (e.g., arterial lines) ensures stability in severe cases (Nikdoust et al. 2018).

Echocardiography is recommended for symptomatic patients; invasive monitoring (e.g., arterial lines) ensures stability in severe cases (Nikdoust et al. 2018).

Pulmonary System: Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD) impairs gas exchange, complicating general anesthesia (Depascale et al. 2021; Hannah and D’Cruz 2019). PFTs (spirometry, diffusion capacity [DLCO]) guide low tidal volume (6–8 mL/kg) and positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP) ventilation strategies to prevent barotrauma (Bevacqua 2015).

Infection risk

Immunosuppression increases infection risk, varying by patient and surgery type. Prophylactic antibiotics (e.g., cefazolin) and sterile techniques are standard for high-risk patients (e.g., on immunosuppressives, recent infections) (Danza and Ruiz-Irastorza 2013). Preoperative CBC assesses infection risk in patients on immunosuppressives, typically once unless clinical changes occur (Danza and Ruiz-Irastorza 2013).

Flare prevention

High SLEDAI scores (> 8) indicate flare risk; preoperative optimization (e.g., low-dose prednisone) is recommended (Adamichou and Bertsias 2017). Regional anesthesia may reduce systemic stress, with limited evidence on flare reduction (Vyas et al. 2014).

Monitoring

Lupus nephritis patients require urine output (> 0.5 mL/kg/h) and creatinine monitoring every 6–12 h; arterial lines are used for cardiovascular instability. Postoperative infection surveillance includes daily wound inspections, fever monitoring, and CBC for 48–72 h (Danza and Ruiz-Irastorza 2013).

Intraoperative management

Benefits and precautions in regional and general anesthesia

Regional anesthesia (spinal/epidural) offers effective pain management and potentially avoids airway manipulation, benefiting patients with rare laryngeal edema or cervical limitations (Vyas et al. 2014; Jindal et al. 2013). It promotes hemodynamic stability in patients with pericarditis or myocarditis (Weidner et al. 2021). However, thrombocytopenia (< 50,000/μL) or anticoagulation (e.g., LMWH) increases epidural hematoma risk, requiring a comprehensive coagulation workup (CBC, PT/INR, aPTT, lupus anticoagulant) (Bauer et al. 2021). Vertebral deformities from corticosteroids may complicate placement (Bultink and Lems 2015).

General anesthesia is indicated for major surgeries (e.g., abdominal, thoracic) or when regional anesthesia is contraindicated (e.g., severe thrombocytopenia). It requires preoperative airway assessment (e.g., Mallampati score, neck mobility) due to potential difficulties from rare laryngeal involvement (Kravets et al. 2025). Ventilation strategies (low tidal volume, PEEP) mitigate pulmonary risks in ILD patients (Bevacqua 2015).

Spinal and epidural anesthesia in labor and pregnancy

Regional anesthesia is preferred in SLE patients during labor for pain relief and reduced systemic stress (Jindal et al. 2013). It potentially avoids airway manipulation and maintains hemodynamic stability (Weidner et al. 2021). APS increases thrombosis risk, requiring LMWH cessation 24 h before epidural placement for surgical safety; thrombocytopenia (< 50,000/μL) contraindicates regional techniques (Bauer et al. 2021). Multidisciplinary planning with obstetricians and rheumatologists ensures maternal and fetal safety, with continuous fetal monitoring and use of fetal-safe medications (e.g., fentanyl over NSAIDs) (Saulescu et al. 2022) (Table 6).

Postoperative management and recovery

Postoperative care focuses on pain, infection, thrombosis, and organ function (Katzap et al. 2013). Regional anesthesia (e.g., epidural catheters) with acetaminophen minimizes systemic effects; Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs) are avoided in lupus nephritis due to nephrotoxicity (Chitnis et al. 2020). Prophylactic antibiotics (e.g., cefazolin) reduce infection risk in high-risk patients (e.g., on immunosuppressives) (Danza and Ruiz-Irastorza 2013). APS patients receive LMWH and compression devices; coagulation profiles (CBC, PT/INR, aPTT) guide therapy (Al-Homood 2012). Renal function is monitored via urine output (> 0.5 mL/kg/h) and creatinine every 6–12 h; cardiovascular monitoring (ECG for 24–48 h) is used for symptomatic patients (Lin et al. 2015). Psychosocial support addresses postoperative anxiety, enhancing recovery (Warchol-Biedermann et al. 2022; Williams et al. 2017).

Discussion

Perioperative management of SLE requires addressing its heterogeneity and treatment-related risks. Regional anesthesia reduces systemic stress and potentially avoids airway manipulation (Vyas et al. 2014). General anesthesia requires careful airway planning due to potential laryngeal involvement (Khokhar et al. 2015). APS treatment complicates regional anesthesia due to anticoagulation, necessitating a coagulation workup (Bauer et al. 2021). Infection risk varies by patient, requiring tailored antibiotic prophylaxis (Danza and Ruiz-Irastorza 2013). Thrombosis rates in APS patients (15–30%) underscore anticoagulation needs (Al-Homood 2012). Variability in institutional practices across countries, due to differences in healthcare systems and guideline adoption, complicates standardized care (Goodman and Bass 2018). Current guidelines lack consensus on optimal SLEDAI thresholds for surgical deferral or specific monitoring protocols, highlighting the need for global standardization (Zisa and Goodman 2022). Gaps remain in standardized protocols due to SLE’s variability, warranting future longitudinal studies to explore flare predictors (e.g., SLEDAI) and novel monitoring technologies.

Conclusion

Perioperative management of Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE) demands tailored strategies to address its multisystem complexities. This review provides a framework for preoperative assessment (e.g., SLEDAI, tailored testing), preference for regional anesthesia where feasible, and vigilant postoperative monitoring to mitigate infections and thrombosis. A multidisciplinary approach helps optimize outcomes. Future research should focus on standardized protocols and longitudinal studies to refine management and address gaps in this challenging clinical domain.

Limitations

SLE’s heterogeneity limits generalizability. Reliance on case reports for rare complications (e.g., NPSLE) weakens evidence. Variability in treatment regimens and institutional practices across countries complicates standardized guidelines. Limited data on long-term surgical outcomes, especially in patients with significant organ involvement, highlights the need for large, multicenter studies to evaluate novel anesthetic techniques and develop evidence-based protocols. Additionally, as a narrative review, this work does not include a formal systematic literature search or quantitative meta-analysis, which limits the strength of evidence synthesis.

Future prospects

Precision medicine, including genomic profiling and biomarker discovery, could improve risk stratification and perioperative care. Novel immunomodulatory therapies and enhanced imaging may reduce complications. Multidisciplinary research on anesthesia-immune interactions could mitigate surgical stress. Standardized protocols and large-scale registries will inform evidence-based practices, enhancing long-term outcomes for SLE patients.

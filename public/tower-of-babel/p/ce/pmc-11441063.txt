A bibliometric analysis from 2004 to 2024 reveals research hotspots and trends in the immunotherapy for vasculitis
Authors: Ruijuan Li, Ran Zhang, Yuheng Jia, Zhongbiao Nie
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTUsing bibliometric analysis, this study attempted to provide an overview of the current state of research and key findings regarding the immunotherapy for vasculitis in general. We gathered the literature from the Web of Science (WOS) database covering the last 20 years (2004–2024) pertaining to the immunotherapy for vasculitis, and we used Citespace to evaluate the mapping of knowledge. The findings demonstrated that there were 572 articles concerning the immunotherapy for vasculitis, with a faster growth after 2018. The USA, Assistance Publique Hopitaux Paris, and Cornelia M are the nation, organization, and writer with the highest number of publications. Daxini A (2018) is the most frequently mentioned reference as well (26). Prominent universities and developed nations form the finest alliances for research on immunotherapy for vasculitis researches. Immune checkpoint inhibitors, Wegener's granulomatosis, and systemic lupus erythematosus are Three research hotspots in this field.

Introduction

Diseases that induce inflammation in the blood vessels themselves are referred to as vasculitis,1 It is a collection of uncommon, pathologically and clinically diverse illnesses that are usually thought to be immune-mediated. They all have vascular inflammation in common, but they differ in terms of the size of the involved vessels, their particular histology, the location of the target organs, clinical features, and outcomes. Several classification systems have been published providing guidance for syndromic identification.2 The size of the predominant affected blood artery is the primary criterion used to classify vascular disease. Large arteries are more frequently afflicted by large vessel vasculitis (LVV) than by other vasculitides, however other arteries may also be impacted. The two most prevalent LVVs are giant cell arteritis (GCA) and Takayasu arteritis (TA). While TA is a rare vasculitis that affects younger people, GCA is the most prevalent vasculitis that affects persons over the age of fifty. Both involve the aorta and its major branches and have granulomatous inflammation-characterized histopathologic features that are nearly identical. Clinical manifestations might range from minimal symptoms to prominent systemic indications, including fever, anorexia, and symptoms connected to increased levels of erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), C-reactive protein (CRP), and inflammatory markers.3

The field of vasculitis immunotherapy has seen a sharp rise in research publications. Adjunctive immunosuppressive medication was recommended by the European League Against Rheumatism (EULAR) guidelines for all patients with LVV and GCA.4,5 Immunotherapy research for vasculitis is developing at such a rapid pace that it is difficult to fully comprehend its hotspots and current situation. On the one hand, immunotherapy for vasculitis collaborations have advanced significantly due to the rise in published research. However, because there are so many dispersed, occasionally redundant studies in this field, it is still challenging to obtain a comprehensive understanding of the state of research on immunotherapy for vasculitis today. The quantitative study of specific topics utilizing a range of databases is known as bibliometrics.6,7 Among these, the Citespace software enables Web of Science (WOS) to carry out quantitative scientific analysis. Among these, the Citespace software enables WOS to carry out quantitative scientific analysis.8,9 By using bibliometrics, readers may fully comprehend the frontiers, trends, and hotspots in this field.6,8 It looks at the turning points in the evolution of several topic areas. Bibliometrics includes coauthor, co-citation, and co-occurrence analysis.9–12 To this end, Citespace was used to investigate the trends and worldwide significance of the immunotherapy for vasculitis in the WOS database from January 1, 2004, to May 16, 2024.

Method

Source of literature

We enter the subject terms into the WOS database (SCI, SSCI, ESCI, etc.): TS= (Vasculitis OR Vasculitides OR Angiitis OR Angiitides) AND TS = (Immunotherapy OR Immunotherapies OR Immunotherapeutic). The database can be searched from January 1, 2004, until May 16, 2024, with English as the language of choice. 221 records were found using the literature search (articles, reviews, meeting abstracts, etc.). The WOS database comes from the Shanxi Bethune Hospital. Every article that had the “search terms” has been examined (because not every article that was retrieved was 100% relevant). Separately, the two researchers screened and evaluated the literature. Bibliometric data, including publication date, number of publications/citations annually, countries/regions, institutions, authors, journals, references, and keywords, were provided by these publications.

Analysis software

Citespace analysis software version is Version 5.6. R2.13

Download and import of data

The file type remained “plain text” after the results of the retrieved topic terms were exported.

Parameter setting

The process under examination involves several steps: Time slicing from 2004 to 2024; One year was chosen as the time slice for CiteSpace’s examination of scientific literature. Reducing bias from highly cited works, the g-index is an author-level indicator based on citation distribution. The g-index determinant, k, was set to the recommended value of 100 in order to give high and low cited papers equal credibility.

Statistical methods

Data management and the analysis of annual publications were conducted using Microsoft Office Excel 2021. Each piece of literature was subjected to scientific analysis, yielding data that included key elements such as major countries, institutions, authors, co-cited references, and keyword co-occurrences.9,14 The detailed analysis flow is shown in Figure 1. Discussion

The data from Citespace reveal an increased annual publication rate of articles focusing on immunotherapy for vasculitis from 2019 to 2024. This trend could potentially be accounted for by an intensifying focus of researchers on the application of immunotherapy in vasculitis, which might be correlated with significant advancements in the field of vasculitis immunotherapy. In 2017 ~ 2018,16–21 6 highly cited articles were published. These highly cited articles provide heat for immunotherapy of vasculitis. The Guideline on Management of Vasculitis Syndrome were published in 2017 by JCS,22 In 2007, the Guidelines for Management of Vasculitis Syndrome were published by a joint working group staffed primarily by the Japanese Circulation Society and the Research Committee on Intractable Vasculitis of the MHLW of Japan.23 The Guidelines for Management of Vasculitis Syndrome have undergone a complete revision in 2017; the digest version was released nine years after the original version. This condensed version adds new heat to the study of vasculitis by clearly outlining key concepts and compiling them for easy reference in routine clinical practice. We noticed in Figure 2 that there was a significant increase in the number of articles published about immunotherapy for vasculitis in 2019, and after analysis, because of the authority and practicality of the guidelines, we believe that the updated Guideline on Management of Vasculitis Syndrome in 2017 have made a special contribution. Of course, this is just our speculation, and we are trying to explain the rapid rise in the number of posts in 2019 from this aspect.

We discovered that there are more publications on immunotherapy for vasculitis in the United States than anywhere else. Among the top two most productive countries/regions, China stands second, surpassed only by the United States. Notably, it is the only developing nation within this ranking. This may be attributed to the larger number of vasculitis patients in China.9,14

Derived from the keyword co-occurrence clustering, burst keywords analysis, and the consideration of keyword frequency and centrality, this investigation identifies three prevailing research trends within this domain. Foremost among these is the exploration of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) as biomarkers within gastric cancer (GC), which has gained considerable interest. Though it can affect arteries of any size,24–27 has most frequently been seen in big vessels, including aortitis and temporal (giant cell) arteritis, per a systematic analysis of case reports.16 The disproportionate representation of temporal arteritis reports amongst Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor (ICI) users was recently recognized in a pharmacovigilance study.19 An autoimmune and autoinflammatory condition affecting the aorta and its branches is called temporal arteritis. Headache, jaw claudication, diplopia or temporary monocular sight loss (amaurosis fugax) and systemic symptoms like fever, exhaustion, and weight loss are some of the symptoms that appear first. Patients’ erythrocyte sedimentation rate and C-reactive protein levels are high, which are indicators of inflammation.28 The first case series demonstrating this specific clinical correlation between immune checkpoint suppression and vasculitis is that of Anisha Daxini et al.16 Large artery vasculitis, which includes giant cell arteritis (GCA), aortitis, and primary angiitis of the nervous system, is the most prevalent kind of vasculitis linked to immune checkpoint blockade, specifically anti-PD-1, according to this study. This discovery is especially noteworthy in view of the several recent publications emphasizing the significance that PD-1 plays in the development of large and medium vessel vasculitis.20,21,29

The second research hotspot is the granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA). Small-vessel vasculitis (SVV), adult-onset asthma, and blood and tissue eosinophilia with organ involvement are the hallmarks of the uncommon immune-mediated disease eosinophilic Granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA) (EGPA, also known as Churg-Strauss syndrome).30 These various elements combine to produce a broad spectrum of symptoms that differ significantly amongst patients. ANCA status allows for the distinction of two primary clinico-pathologic subsets: an ANCA-negative subset with prominent organ involvement associated to eosinophils, and an ANCA+ subset with predominant vasculitic lesions. Glucocorticoids, a conventional immunosuppressive medication, is the cornerstone of EGPA therapy. To minimize side effects, a daily dose of glucocorticoids during induction of 1 mg/kg of prednisone is advised, and this dosage should be gradually reduced until withdrawal, or ≤7.5 mg/d.31 EPGA is usually regarded as a systemic illness that goes through the three stages mentioned above. There are limited types that impact only one organ; in these circumstances, histology is frequently used to make the diagnosis. One important EGPA symptom that affects 95–100% of individuals is adult-onset asthma, which may appear years before other systemic symptoms 33. On the other hand, unlike classical asthma, normal seasonal fluctuations are not detected in EGPA34. Additional features include sputum eosinophilia vasculitis and negative allergy investigations.32,33

The third popular topic is the systemic lupus erythematosus. An aberrant immune system is the source of the complex systemic autoimmune disease (AD) known as systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE).34 The term “lupus vasculopathy” refers to the vascular lesions seen in SLE; less frequently, a classic case of lupus vasculitis is observed, characterized by vascular wall necrosis and inflammation, as well as a thrombus in the afflicted artery’s lumen.35–37 Oral or parenterally administered corticosteroids continue to be the cornerstone of care for the acute therapy of severe SLE exacerbations. However, patients often require corticosteroid-sparing systemic immunosuppressive medications due to the likelihood for acute, vision-compromising illness recurrences and the harmful effects of long-term corticosteroid use.38 2009 saw EULAR suggest aspirin usage for people with large vessel vasculitis to prevent cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events.39 Recently, the American College of Rheumatology (ACR) has conditionally recommended the use of aspirin in patients with flow critical large vessel vasculitis based on the same body of data.40 But in 2020, an update to the 2009 EULAR recommendations reexamined this data and found that the risk-benefit analysis was unfavorable and that antiplatelet therapy should not be administered universally unless specifically warranted for other reasons.4

Limitations

Analytical and visualization tools such as CiteSpace, offer an expansive perspective on the progression and trajectory of vasculitis immunotherapy. Nevertheless, this study has some inherent limitations. The range of literature considered is not exhaustive since it only integrates data from the WOS, omitting information from other relevant databases. Moreover, the selection of literature is limited solely to English publications, thereby inducing language bias. Thus, the analyzed literature may not thoroughly reflect all studies pertaining to colorectal cancer immunotherapy.

Strongest partnerships between developed countries and esteemed organizations are advantageous to the progress of immunotherapy for vasculitis research.

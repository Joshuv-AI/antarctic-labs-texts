Exploring the diagnostic and immune infiltration roles of disulfidptosis related genes in pulmonary hypertension
Authors: Xin Tan, Ningning Zhang, Ge Zhang, Shuai Xu, Yiyao Zeng, Fenlan Bian, Bi Tang, Hongju Wang, Jili Fan, Xiaohong Bo, Yangjun Fu, Huimin Fan, Yafeng Zhou, Pinfang Kang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPulmonary hypertension (PH) is marked by elevated pulmonary artery pressures due to various causes, impacting right heart function and survival. Disulfidptosis, a newly recognized cell death mechanism, may play a role in PH, but its associated genes (DiGs) are not well understood in this context. This study aims to define the diagnostic relevance of DiGs in PH.MethodsUsing GSE11726 data, we analyzed DiGs and their immune characteristics to identify core genes influencing PH progression. Various machine learning models, including RF, SVM, GLM, and XGB, were compared to determine the most effective diagnostic model. Validation used datasets GSE57345 and GSE48166. Additionally, a CeRNA network was established, and a hypoxia-induced PH rat model was used for experimental validation with Western blot analysis.Results12 DiGs significantly associated with PH were identified. The XGB model excelled in diagnostic accuracy (AUC = 0.958), identifying core genes DSTN, NDUFS1, RPN1, TLN1, and MYH10. Validation datasets confirmed the model’s effectiveness. A CeRNA network involving these genes, 40 miRNAs, and 115 lncRNAs was constructed. Drug prediction suggested therapeutic potential for folic acid, supported by strong molecular docking results. Experimental validation in a rat model aligned with these findings.ConclusionWe uncovered the distinct expression patterns of DiGs in PH, identified core genes utilizing an XGB machine-learning model, and established a CeRNA network. Drugs targeting the core genes were predicted and subjected to molecular docking. Experimental validation was also conducted for these core genes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12931-024-02978-w.

Introduction

Pulmonary hypertension (PH) is a progressive disease caused by various etiologies and complex pathogenetic mechanisms, which leads to alterations in pulmonary function, increased pulmonary vascular resistance, and elevated pulmonary artery pressure [1, 2]. These changes result in right ventricular dysfunction, ultimately causing right heart failure and death. Recent epidemiological studies have shown an incidencerate of 97 per million adults, currently affecting about 100 million people worldwide. The latest domestic and international guidelines for the diagnosis of PH are as follows: a mean pulmonary artery pressure exceeding 20 mmHg at rest, or over 30 mmHg during exercise [3, 4]. Due to the complex pathogenesis of PH and delayed symptoms, patients are often in a moderate or advanced stage by the time receiving clinical treatment, at which point conventional treatments are unable to improve the condition effectively. Thus further research on the mechanisms of PH development is necessary.

It is reported that the development of PH involves various programmed cell death processes including apoptosis, necrosis, pyroptosis, ferroptosis and cuproptosis in pulmonary artery smooth muscle cells [3, 5–8]. A newly identified type of cell death, known as disulfidptosis, has emerged as a distinct cell death program [9, 10]. This process is triggered by an excess of cystine, causing disulfide stress that leads to the collapse of the actin cytoskeleton. The protein SLC7A11 is crucial for transporting cystine into cells. Elevated levels of SLC7A11 lead to glucose scarcity, which disrupts the production of NADPH through the pentose phosphate pathway [10]. This disruption results in an excessive buildup of small disulfide molecules, triggering a series of oxidative changes that ultimately induce cell death [11]. The role of disulfidptosis-related genes (DiGs) in PH remains poorly understood, with most studies concentrating on their implications in cancer. A recent study indicates that the occurrence of disulfiram-induced death is associated with the development of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease [12]. In the progression of PH, during the proliferation of pulmonary artery smooth muscle, increased expression of ROS alters the transcriptional activity of relevant proteins, inducing the formation of disulfide bonds in proteins, further affecting mitochondrial function and cellular metabolism, leading to the exacerbation of PH [13]. However, the exact function of DiGs in the onset and advancement of PH is not yet fully understood. Thus, exploring how DiGs contribute to PH will provide a new perspective for diagnosis and treatment of PH .

In this investigation, we analyzed data of PH patients from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) to construct and assess a prediction model for DiGs. This model aims to precisely evaluate the diagnostic significance of DiGs in individuals with PH. It demonstrated high predictive accuracy with both training and validation datasets, and highlighted a notable link between DiGs-mediated immune infiltration and the progression of PH. Furthermore, the expression of core targets of DiGs was further validated using a hypoxic pulmonary hypertension (HPH) rats model (Fig. 1).

Materials and methods

Data source

GSE117261 [2], GSE24988 [14] and GSE33463 [15] datasets were obtained from GEO (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) database (Table S1) [16]. The GSE117261 data was a training dataset (25 control and 58 PH samples) [17]. GSE24988 and GSE33463 were used as validating datasets.

Selection of differentially expressed DiGs

DiGs were selected by retrieving 23 genes from a previous study [9, 10]. A t-test was employed to examine the differences between the control and PH patient groups. The results, illustrating variations in gene expression, were displayed in heatmaps [18, 19].

Cluster analysis based on differentially expressed DiGs

Cluster analysis was performed with ConsensusClusterPlus on the training set using DiGs [20]. The training dataset underwent evaluation through Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA) [21].

Predictive model construction based on multiple machine-learning methods

A diverse set of machine learning techniques was utilized to develop predictive models for diagnosing PH. These models included extreme gradient boosting (XGB) model [22], random forest (RF) [23], generalized linear model (GLM) [24], and support vector machine (SVM) [25]. The distribution of model residuals across these four methods was visualized using the DALEX package. The most effective machine learning model was chosen, identifying the top five genes as key diagnostic markers. Model performance was assessed by visualizing the Area Under the Curve (AUC) of the Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curves with the ‘pROC’ R package.

Nomogram construction and validation

To further validate and operationalize the predictive capability, a nomogram was constructed using the “RMS” R package. Each predictor was assigned a score, with the ‘total score’ representing the cumulative sum of these scores. To assess the predictive accuracy of the nomogram, calibration curves and Decision Curve Analysis (DCA) were utilized.

Analysis of immune cell infiltration in PH

Analysis of 22 immune cells was conducted using the CIBERSORT algorithm (https://cibersort.stanford.edu/). This method employs Monte Carlo sampling to derivep-values for the deconvolution of each sample. Significance was established when p-values were less than 0.05, and the sum of the proportions of all 22 immune cell types in each sample equaled one [26].

CeRNA network construction

The lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA ceRNA network was established by predicting miRNA-miRNA interactions using the TargetScan, miRDB and miRanda databases. Interactions between miRNA and lncRNA were identified using the SpongeScan database. The network was then constructed using Cytoscape software (version 3.7.2).

Identification of candidate small-molecule drugs

Therapeutic drugs that target core genes were identified from the CTD database (http://ctdbase.org/). A drug-gene interaction network was then constructed using Cytoscape software (version 3.7.2). Furthermore, molecular docking of candidate drugs with core genes was carried out using Autodock Vina software (version 1.1.2). The results of these molecular interactions were visualized using PyMOL (version 2.0.0) [27].

Animal

Male SPF rats were purchased from the Animal Center of Bengbu Medical University, Anhui Province (SYXK 20201104). All procedures strictly adhered to the “Guidelines of the Chinese Animal Research Council” and “Animal Care Guidelines” to minimize animals’ suffering. Rats were housed in well-ventilated rooms, with moderate temperature (22 ± 2 °C), appropriate humidity (55 ± 5%), a 12-hour light/dark cycle, and free access to food and water. After one week of acclimatization with standard laboratory mouse feed, the mice were randomly divided into a control group (n = 6) and an HPH group (HPH, n = 6). The HPH group was placed in a hypoxic chamber (containing anhydrous sodium chloride and soda lime to absorb water and CO2), with free access to food and water. N2 was introduced into the hypoxic chamber at a rate of 8 L/min, then adjusted to 1 L/min after half an hour to maintain the oxygen concentration at 10% as monitored by an oxygen meter. The rats were exposed to hypoxia for 8 h per day, for 6 consecutive days each week, with a one-day rest per week, for a total of 4 weeks, which can result in the formation of a HPH rat model [28].

Chemicals and materials

Sodium pentobarbital (Siasia Reagent, China), Antibodies: NDUFS1 (Abcam, ab169540, USA), RPN1 (Abcam, ab198508, USA), TLN1 (Abcam, ab108480, USA), DSTN (affinity, DF15268, China), MYH10 (Proteintech, 19673-1-AP, China), β-actin (CST, 4970, USA), goat anti-rabbit IgG (HRP Conjugate) (CST, 14708, USA) and goat anti-mouse IgG (HRP Conjugate) (CST, 14709, USA).

Histopathological examination of lung

The rats’ lung were fixed in 4% formaldehyde dehydrated with varying concentrations of ethanol, followed by paraffin embedding and sectioning, and perform HE staining.

Right ventricular pressure measurement

After anesthetizing the rat (2% sodium pentobarbital), fix it on a small animal operation table, and disinfect the external part of the rat’s right neck with iodophor. Make an oblique incision on the rat’s right external neck to expose the skin, separate the right external jugular vein, make a 2 mm long V-shaped incision, then slowly insert the catheter into the blood vessel, advancing it into the right atrium and right ventricle. Observe the pressure waveform on the monitor and record it once the waveform stabilizes.

Detecting the expression levels of key disulfidptosis proteins in lung tissue

Proteins were extracted from entire lung tissue homogenates, quantified, and denatured, followed by electrophoresis and membrane transfer. The membrane was then blocked using 5% skim milk at room temperature for one hour. Subsequently, antibodies for NDUFS1, RPN1, TLN1, MYH10 and DSTN, all diluted at 1:1000, were applied in sequence and incubated overnight at 4 °C on a shaker. Following this, the membrane was washed three times for 10 min each. After incubating with the secondary antibody at room temperature for one hour, the membrane underwent three more 10-minute washes. Finally, band visualization and quantification were performed using an Amersham Imager 600 (GE Healthcare Life Sciences, USA) and ImageJ software.

Statistical methods

The data were analyzed statistically using GraphPad Prism 9.0, and all data are shown as mean ± standard deviation. An independent samples t-test was used. *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ns (non-significant).

Results

DiGs expression and immune infiltration analysis in PH patients

We examined the expression profiles of 23 DiGs within PH and control samples. Comparative analysis revealed heightened expression levels on FLNB and MYH10, alongside diminished expression on SLC7A11, GYS1, NDUFS1, LRPPRC, SLC3A2, RNP1, CAPZB, DSTN, IQGAP1 and TLN1 (Fig. 2A, B). Afterward, an immune infiltration analysis was conducted on the control and PH patients. Variations in the proportion of 22 immune cells, specifically CD4 naïve T cells, CD4 memory T cells, gamma delta T cells, monocytes, M1 and M2 Macrophages were observed using the CIBERSORT algorithm (Fig. 2C, D). Furthermore, correlation analysis revealed disulfidptosis in regulatory Monocytes, M1 and M2 Macrophages, activated dendritic cells and CD4 memory T cells (Fig. 2E). These findings suggested potential implications of immune system alterations as an important etiological factor in PH development.

Identification of PH disulfidptosis cluster

For a more detailed understanding of the expression patterns of DiGs in PH, consensus clustering analysis was performed on 12 differentially expressed DiGs. The consensus index consistently fluctuated between 0.2 and 0.6, as shown in Fig. 3A, B (Figure S1). Varying the cluster number (k) from 2 to 9, the area under the CDF curve depicted significant differences between clusters (k and k-1) (Fig. 3C). Impressively, a high consistency score (> 0.9) was observed only when k = 2, as highlighted in Fig. 3D. Based on the consensus matrix heatmap, the 58 patients were segregated into two distinct clusters: cluster 1 (n = 48) and cluster 2 (n = 10), as shown in Fig. 3E. Visualization through t-distributed random neighborhood embedding (t-SNE) revealed marked differences between the clusters, which was illustrated in Fig. 3F. Notably, cluster 1 exhibited elevated expressions of NDUFS1, DSTN and TLN1, while cluster 2 showed enhanced expressions of SLC7A11, LRPPRC, SLC3A2 and IQGAP1 (Fig. 3G).

Subsequent analysis focused on delineating the differences in immune cell infiltration between the two clusters, revealing elevated proportions of follicular helper T cells, activated NK cells, and activated dendritic cells in cluster 2 (Figures S2 and Figure S3). Utilizing GSVA, we identified cluster-specific DiGs that underscored functional disparities. Specifically, cluster 2 exhibited upregulation in pathways related to drug metabolism cytochrome p450, propanoate metabolism, metabolism of xenobiotics by cytochrome p450, and valine leucine and isoleucine degradation. Conversely, cluster 1 demonstrated enhanced activity in pathways such as nod-like receptor signaling, natural killer cell-mediated cytotoxicity, MAPK signaling, and cytokine receptor interaction (Figure S4).

Furthermore, functional enrichment analysis revealeddistinct biological processes associated with each cluster. Cluster 1 was enriched in the establishment of protein localization to chromatin, regulation of cellular extravasation, negative regulation of mitochondrial fusion, and negativeregulation of cellular catabolic process. In contrast, cluster 2 exhibited enrichment in processes such as NADPH oxidation, leucine metabolic process, positive regulation of gap junction assembly, and protein localization to photoreceptor outer segment (Figure S5).

Machine learning model construction and evaluation

We utilized four different machine learning models—SVM, RF, GLM and XGB—to pinpoint critical diagnostic markers. Residual visualization for each model in the training dataset was accomplished through model interpretation using the ‘DALEX’ software package. Notably, the XGB model showed relatively small residual variations (Fig. 4A and B). The top five significant variables for each model were identified based on the root mean squared error (RMSE), as illustrated in Fig. 4C.

Additionally, 5-fold cross-validation ROC curves were generated to evaluate the diagnostic performance of the four machine learning algorithms using the training dataset. Notably, the XGB model exhibited the highest AUC, with values as follows: SVM (AUC = 0.941), RF (AUC = 0.853), XGB (AUC = 0.958), and GLM (AUC = 0.756) (Fig. 4D). This underscores the XGB model’s superior ability to differentiate between patient clusters. From the XGB model, the top five variables identified as key predictors for further analysis were NDUFS1, RPN1, TLN1, MYH10 and DSTN.

We constructed a nomogram to evaluate the predictive efficiency of the XGB model using 58 PH cases (Fig. 5A). Calibration curves and DCA were employed to assess the prediction accuracy of the nomograms. The calibration curve demonstrated minimal error between the actual and predicted risks in the PH cluster (Fig. 5B). The DCA underscored the high accuracy of the nomogram, providing valuable evidence for clinical decision-making (Fig. 5C). The predictive capability of core biomarkers was validated using GSE24988 and GSE33463 (Idiopathic pulmonary arterial hypertension and scleroderma with pulmonary arterial hypertension). The ROC curves demonstrated that the predictive model containing the five core markers performed well with AUCs of 0.761 (GSE24988 pulmonary hypertension), 0.889 (GSE24988 idiopathic pulmonary arterial hypertension) and 0.937 (GSE57345 scleroderma with pulmonary arterial hypertension), indicating the effectiveness of our diagnostic model in distinguishing PH patients from normal people (Fig. 5D-I).

CeRNA network establishment

A ceRNA network was constructed using databases related to miRanda, targetScan, miRDB and SpongeScan. This network comprises 160 nodes, including 5 core diagnostic markers, 40 microRNAs (miRNA), and 115 long non-coding RNAs (lncRNA), connected by 187 edges (Fig. 6). Within the ceRNA network, 21 lncRNAs can competitively bind with Rpn1 mRNA regulated by hsa-miR-377-3p, hsa-miR-1236-3p, hsa-miR-342-3p and hsa-miR-27a-3p, among which miR-1236-3p can simultaneously target Rpn1 mRNA and Dstn mRNA. Additionally, 36 lncRNAs were observed to target Myh10 mRNA, regulated by various miRNAs. Moreover, 6 lncRNAs were found to target Ndufs1 mRNA, regulated by hsa-miR-32-3p, hsa-miR-145-3p, hsa-miR-126-5p and hsa-miR-649. Additionally, 49 lncRNAs were identified to target Dstn mRNA, regulated by various miRNAs, forming a complex network. Furthermore, 32 lncRNAs were observed to target Tln1 mRNA, regulated by various miRNAs including hsa-miR-20a-3p, hsa-miR-326, hsa-miR-193b-5p, hsa-miR-1207-5p, hsa-miR-92a-1-5p, hsa-miR-491-5p, hsa-miR-432-5p and hsa-miR-15b-3p. Notably, lncRNA01128 was found to target Myh10 mRNA and Dstn mRNA. The ceRNA network indicated the regulatory role of LINC00689, influencing the expression of RPN1 and DSTN (Fig. 6).

Prediction of targeted drugs

The CTD database was employed for predicting drugs targeting the five core diagnostic markers. Subsequently, a drug prediction network comprising 338 nodes and 579 edges was constructed, consisting of the five core diagnostic genes and 333 predicted drugs (Fig. 7A, Table S2). Drug testing revealed that folic acid (D005492) exhibited simultaneous interactions with all five core diagnostic markers. Molecular docking studies were conducted with the core targets, resulting in an average binding energy of -8.06 kcal/mol (Fig. 7B, Table S3).

Discussion

Individuals diagnosed with PH often have a reduced lifespan and are typically young. Current treatments mainly focusing on alleviating symptoms rather than curing the disease [1]. Consequently, it is critical to decipher the underlying mechanisms of PH progression and to identify new therapeutic targets and biomarkers. Such advancements are crucial for enhancing both the diagnosis and prognosis of PH. Recent research has highlighted disulfidptosis as a novel form of cell death, capturing significant academic attention. The pathogenesis of PH is not fully elucidated, and disulfidptosis could play a role in its development. This research aimed to clarify PH pathogenesis by categorizing a unique cluster and devising a diagnostic model centered on DiGs. The genes within these clusters and the diagnostic model were closely associated with the immune dynamics of PH, offering crucial insights for its diagnosis and therapeutic approaches.

Our study identified 12 differentially expressed DiGs in both control and PH samples. Functional analysis revealed their association with immune responses and the progression of disulfidptosis, notably involving naïve CD4 T cells, resting CD4 memory T cells, gamma delta T cells, monocytes, M1 and M2 Macrophages. By analyzing these 12 DiGs, we organized all samples into two clusters: cluster-1 and cluster-2. Cluster-1 demonstrated a greater degree of immune cell infiltration than cluster-2, indicating a more pronounced immune response in cluster-1. Recent studies have found that the immune system plays an important role in the occurrence and development of PH. Macrophages are crucial in the progression of PH, with a disparity in the M1/M2 ratio marking a key aspect of PH exacerbation. M1-type macrophages exacerbate inflammation through the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines, whereas M2-type macrophages facilitate tissue repair and are pivotal in pulmonary vascular remodeling [29, 30]. During the occurrence of PH, in patients with idiopathic pulmonary arterial hypertension, the expression of T cell activation markers CD86 and CD40 increased after pretreatment with dexamethasone [31]. Furthermore, experimental studies in animals have shown that either depletion of CD4 cells or administration of SR1001, an inhibitor of Th17 cell development, can prevent increases in pressure and the vascular remodeling associated with chronic hypoxia [32]. At the same time, our study uncovered significant differences in immune cell profiles between the two clusters we identified. Therefore, future studies should focus on examining the complex interactions and underlying mechanisms linking DiGs with immune cells in the context of PH.

Machine learning, leveraging a multidisciplinary approach, offers the capability to extract meaningful insights from complex datasets. In our research, the XGB-based prediction model demonstrated superior efficacy, achieving an AUC of 0.958, highlighting its robust performance in forecasting PH. The model was developed using five key factors: NDUFS1, RPN1, TLN1, MYH10 and DSTN. Studies have shown that NDUFS1 regulates mitochondrial function to enhance the capabilities of type II alveolar cells in emphysema [33], and miR-3130-5p directly regulates NDUFS1 expression, improving the aggressiveness of lung adenocarcinoma [34]. Bcl-2 interacting killer through alterations in the BH4 domain of Bcl-2, interacts with RPN1 and RPN2, subsequently promoting the proteasomal degradation of nuclear proteins, alleviates lung inflammation induced by LPS and reverses emphysema in female mice [35]. Recent studies suggest that TLN1 may serve as a diagnostic marker to differentiate between lung adenocarcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma [36], and it can also be a critical target in lung cancer treatment with miR-124-3p [37]. MYH10 expression is reduced in patients with emphysema, and the specific absence of Myh10 in mesenchymal cells leads to defects in ECM deposition and alveolar simplification, indicating that MYH10 plays an important role in alveolar development [38]. DSTN is a member of ADF/cofilin family, DSTN exhibited high expression levels in clinical samples of lung adenocarcinoma. It promotes the malignancy of lung cancer by enhancing β-catenin nuclear translocation and triggering epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition [39].

The validity of the XGB model was established through testing in the validation cohort. Additionally, we created a nomogram for the diagnosis of PH using the expression levels of NDUFS1, RPN1, TLN1, MYH10 and DSTN. This nomogram demonstrated substantial predictive accuracy, underscoring its potential for clinical use. To corroborate these findings, we employed a HPH rat model. Post-hypoxia, MYH10 expression in lung tissue significantly increased, whereas NDUFS1, RPN1, TLN1 and DSTN expressions notably decreased, which corroborated our initial bioinformatics predictions. Therefore, these in vivo results confirmed the conclusions drawn from our bioinformatics analyses.

MicroRNA (miRNA) has become a critical epigenetic regulator in the study of genes associated with PH. Progress has been made in developing miRNA-based biomarkers and exploring their therapeutic potential [40]. As a result, we constructed a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network using five core diagnostic markers to investigate the regulatory mechanisms that control these markers. The CeRNA network analysis unveiled the interaction of hsa-miR-27a-3p with MYH10 and RPN1. Circular RNA can competitively bind with miR-27a-3p and miR-432-5p to alleviate dysfunction in pulmonary microvascular endothelial cells in PH, improving pulmonary vascular remodeling [41, 42]. In studies related to mitochondrial dynamics and miRNA, miR-140-3p levels are significantly elevated in patients with PH compared to the control group [43]. In HPH mice, blocking miR-335-3p significantly improves pulmonary vascular remodeling, suggesting that miR-335-3p may be a potential therapeutic target for HPH [44].

Beyond miRNA interactions, this study utilized the CTD to predict potential therapeutic drugs targeting diagnostic markers and performed molecular docking studies. Networks linking drugs and genes were developed, and molecular docking simulations were used to identify potential targets. This method offers a valuable reference for exploring pathogenic factors in PH and devising innovative therapeutic strategies [45]. For instance, folic acid, also known as Vitamin Bc, is a type of B-vitamin, was identified as a compound simultaneously targeting the five core markers. Folic acid treatment enhances the matrix metalloproteinases/tissue inhibitors of metalloproteinases balance and alleviates mitochondrial dysfunction, leading to protection against right ventricular failure in pulmonary hypertension [46]. Numerous studies have shown that in HPH models, folic acid can promote the tetrahydrobiopterin cycle, enhance the utilization of NO in pulmonary artery endothelial cells, and reverse pulmonary vascular remodeling, increased right ventricular pressure, and right ventricular hypertrophy [46, 47]. Additionally, supplementing with trace elements, such as folic acid, can reduce the risk of cardiovascular disease [48]. Thus, our study has highlighted its potential therapeutic benefits for PH and further investigation is required to clarify the specific efficacy of folic acid treatment for PH.

Nonetheless, our study is subject to certain limitations. First, additional clinical data from PH patients are needed to validate the expression levels of DiGs. Second, a detailed and comprehensive evaluation of the predictive model needs extensive clinical information, particularly regarding patient outcomes. Third, enlarging the sample size of PH cases is essential to enhance the accuracy of the DiGs-based model.

Additionally, one of the limitations of this study is that it has not yet thoroughly investigated the post-translational modifications (such as glycosylation and phosphorylation) of the target proteins, which may significantly impact their functions in PH. Future research will further explore these potential effects.

Conclusions

This research revealed a significant association between disulfidptosis and PH, with 12 DiGs exhibiting changes in expression, either upregulated or downregulated in PH cases. Subtypes were categorized based on genes linked to disulfidptosis. Using the XGB machine learning approach, we demonstrated that these core genes can act as highly precise biomarkers for accurately diagnosing PH, thus aiding early therapeutic interventions. In vivo studies further emphasized the critical role these core genes play in the development of PH. The diverse expression patterns of these DiGs provide crucial insights into both diagnosing and predicting the progression of PH.

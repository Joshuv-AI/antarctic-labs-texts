Disparities in anticoagulation therapy for older adults with non-valvular atrial fibrillation: a comparative study of urban and suburban communities
Authors: Yuanxia Duan, Zhigang Pan, Jie Gu, Ying Qing, Xiaojun Kuang, Zhiping Jin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNon-valvular atrial fibrillation (NVAF) carries a severe risk of ischemic stroke, making anticoagulation therapy the cornerstone of prophylaxis. However, real-world data on anticoagulation management at the primary care level remain sparse. This study aims to evaluate the current landscape of anticoagulation therapy among older NVAF patients undergoing community health examinations in Shanghai, China, and to analyze the disparities between urban and suburban populations.MethodsA stratified cross-sectional study was conducted across four community health centers (CHCs)—two urban and two suburban. The study included older participants diagnosed with AF via electrocardiogram during 2020 routine health check-ups. Anticoagulant utilization and medication-dispensing patterns were compared between the urban and suburban cohorts. Multivariable logistic regression analysis was employed to identify factors influencing medication-dispensing location preferences among NVAF patients at high risk of stroke.ResultsA total of 302 patients were included (mean age 75.3 ± 6.7 years). The prevalence of thromboembolic events and bleeding complications was 14.9% and 3.6%, respectively. Compared to the suburban cohort, urban patients were significantly older and exhibited a higher prevalence of diabetes, hyperlipidemia, carotid plaques, previous bleeding events, and high stroke risk (p < 0.05). Among those receiving anticoagulation, 49.4% sourced their medications from CHCs. Advanced patient age and high awareness of AF-related risk emerged as independent determinants favoring community-based dispensing locations.ConclusionAnticoagulation management for older NVAF patients in community settings remains suboptimal. While standardized anticoagulation treatment rates for high-risk patients show no significant urban-suburban divide, targeted initiatives are essential to optimize and standardize anticoagulation protocols in this vulnerable demographic.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12877-026-07935-4.

Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) represents a surging global public health challenge. Recent data from the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2021 study highlight an escalating prevalence of AF, affecting approximately 52.55 million individuals worldwide of 2021, which represents a 137% increase since 1990 [1]. This trajectory is particularly pronounced in China, where the overall prevalence has reached 1.6%, translating to an estimated 20 million affected individuals [2]. Clinically, AF is recognized as a profound independent risk factor for ischemic stroke. The regional impact and sociodemographic disparities of these cardiovascular events are stark: national decomposition analyses reveal that between 2012 and 2021, the overall incidence of strokes in China surged by 38.4%, with ischemic strokes increasingly dominating the disease profile [3]. Furthermore, significant urban-rural divides persist; recent epidemiological reports indicate that the crude mortality rate for cardiovascular diseases in rural regions (364.16 per 100,000) substantially outpaces that of urban areas (305.39 per 100,000) [4]. The clinical outcomes of AF-induced strokes remain devastating. Compared with non-AF strokes, those associated with AF exhibit significantly higher severity, with acute mortality and long-term disability rates often approaching 20% and 60%, respectively, placing an immense burden on public health systems [5, 6].

Notably, non-valvular atrial fibrillation (NVAF) accounts for the vast majority of these cases and has become a critical priority in cardiovascular research [7]. Patients with NVAF are particularly vulnerable, facing a stroke incidence four to five times higher than that of healthy adults [8]. Consequently, effective oral anticoagulation (OAC) therapy serves as the absolute cornerstone intervention for the prophylaxis of both ischemic stroke and systemic embolism in this high-risk population [7, 8]. Despite robust clinical guidelines, the overall anticoagulation rate among patients with AF in China remains suboptimal, ranging from 15% to 50%-considerably lower than rates observed in developed countries [9, 10].

AF burden is increasingly concentrated in metropolitan areas due to rapid urbanization and population aging. In Shanghai, China, a megacity with a pronounced aging profile, AF prevalence is approximately 0.88% in the general population and increases to 2.32% among individuals aged ≥ 60 years based on large-scale administrative data covering over 30 million residents. Although anticoagulation use has improved substantially, rising from 19.46% in 2015 to 56.57% in 2020, undertreatment persists in real-world community settings, particularly among older patients in primary care [11]. More recent community-based studies and implementation programs indicate that structured primary care management can improve guideline adherence and anticoagulation utilization; however, treatment gaps remain significant, particularly in high-risk older populations [12, 13].

Importantly, real-world data focusing on NVAF management at the primary care level remain sparse. Because previous findings are largely confined to specific administrative districts without a broader comparative lens, this study aims to explore the current landscape of anticoagulation therapy among older patients with NVAF in Shanghai, China. Specifically, we seek to identify and analyze the disparities in disease management between urban and suburban CHCs. By elucidating these localized barriers to anticoagulation, this research provides critical real-world evidence to guide targeted health policies and optimize resource allocation for vulnerable older populations.

Methods

Study design and setting

A muti-stage, stratified cross-sectional study design was employed. In May 2021, an electronic questionnaire was administered to 246 CHCs across Shanghai to assess facility capabilities for AF management. Based on these baseline capability data, a stratified sampling approach (with a 1:1 sampling ratio) was applied, utilizing the presence of specialized AF clinics and the availability of anticoagulant medications as stratification criteria. Ultimately, four CHCs were strategically selected to represent different regional demographics: two urban centers (Fenglin and Wujiaochang) and two suburban centers (Maogang and Nanqiao).

Study population and data collection

Between July and December 2021, we retrospectively screened the electronic health records of older patients diagnosed with NVAF who had undergone routine health examinations at the four selected centers in 2020. Subsequently, from January to December 2022, structured questionnaire interviews were conducted to collect clinical and demographic data (the translated English version of the questionnaire is provided in Supplementary Material).

The inclusion criteria comprised: age ≥ 65 years (chosen to align with the standard classification used within the Chinese community health examination system); a confirmed diagnosis of AF via electrocardiogram during the 2020 health examination; and provision of signed informed consent and voluntary participation. To ensure data accuracy and minimize potential information bias, all self-reported comorbidities were subsequently cross-referenced and validated against the patients’ electronic medical records by trained investigators (comprising community nurses and general practitioners). Prior to data collection, these investigators underwent standardized training on questionnaire delivery, electronic medical record cross-referencing, and ethical communication with older adults. Invalid or incomplete responses (defined as missing critical data regarding medication status or AF confirmation) were excluded. Exclusion criteria included: valvular AF; incident AF cases developed after the 2020 baseline (i.e., individuals who had normal sinus rhythm in their 2020 records but were subsequently diagnosed with AF prior to or during the 2022 survey); contraindications to anticoagulant therapy; and severe mental disorders. Ethical approval was obtained from the Fudan University Affiliated Zhongshan Hospital Ethics Committee (Approval number: B2020-105). All enrolled patients provided written informed consent.

Sample size

The expected baseline anticoagulation rate in CHCs was estimated to be 10.1%, based on previous epidemiological evidence [12, 13]. Outcomes

The primary outcome of this study was the overall prevalence of anticoagulant utilization among older NVAF patients managed in community settings. Anticoagulant therapy was defined as the active prescription and use of either non-vitamin K antagonist oral anticoagulants (NOACs) or Warfarin at the time of the survey. The CHA2DS2-VA score was utilized to assess the risk of stroke among community-dwelling older patients with NVAF [8]. Secondary outcomes included comparative disparities in utilization rates between urban and suburban CHCs, and the identification of socio-demographic factors influencing patients’ choice of medication-dispensing locations.

Statistical

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 25.0. Continuous variables were tested for normality; normally distributed data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and compared using independent sample Student’s t-tests. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages (%), and group comparisons were evaluated utilizing Pearson’s Chi-square tests or Fisher’s exact tests. Univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses were employed to identify independent factors influencing dispensing locations, with results reported as odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). A two-sided P-value of < 0.05 indicated statistical significance.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 302 NVAF patients were included, with a mean age of 75.3 ± 6.67 years and an average BMI of 24.5 ± 3.67 (Table 1). The cohort consisted of 90 (29.8%) urban and 212 (70.2%) suburban patients. Compared to urban patients, the suburban cohort was younger (p < 0.001), and had lower education levels (p < 0.001), monthly income (p < 0.001), smoking rate (p = 0.005), and drinking rate (p = 0.001). Regarding clinical presentation, 116 (38.4%) patients were unclear about their AF type, while 257 (85.1%) reported AF-related symptoms. The most prevalent comorbidities were hypertension (73.2%), coronary heart disease (48.3%), and diabetes (20.9%). Urban patients exhibited significantly higher proportions of diabetes, hyperlipidemia, carotid artery plaque, bleeding events, and high risk of stroke compared to their suburban counterparts (P < 0.05).

Medication status

Among the 302 NVAF patients, 279 (92.4%) were identified as high risk for stroke. Of these high-risk patients, 85 (30.5%) were receiving anticoagulants (19.7% on warfarin; 10.8% on NOACs). There was no statistically significant difference in anticoagulant utilization rate between urban and suburban high-risk groups (P > 0.05) (Fig. 1).

Factors influencing dispensing locations and non-adherence

Among the 85 high-risk patients receiving anticoagulants, 52.7% (29/55) obtained warfarin and 43.3% (13/30) obtained NOAC prescriptions from community pharmacies (Table 2). Significant urban-suburban differences emerged regarding reasons for choosing community pharmacies. For warfarin, suburban patients more frequently cited a “High patient satisfaction” (P = 0.044) and having a “Family doctor contract service coverage” (P = 0.012). Suburban patients also preferred community center for international normalized ratio (INR) monitoring due to “Lower medication cost” (P = 0.035), while urban patients preferred community pharmacies for NOAC due to the reason of “Short waiting time” (P = 0.033).

Factors influencing dispensing locations

Univariate logistic regression identified patient age, AF type, NVAF risk awareness as factors influencing dispensing location preferences (community health center vs. higher-level hospital). Subsequent multifactorial logistic regression analysis confirmed that advanced age(OR = 0.33)and NVAF risk awareness (OR = 0.11) independently and significantly predicted the preference for community-based dispensing among high-risk patients (Table 4).

Discussion

The anticoagulation rate for older NVAF patients at high risk of stroke during community check-ups was 30.5%. While this marks an improvement over previous years, it remains a critical area requiring intervention [14]. This upward trend may correlated with enhanced patient knowledge regarding anticoagulation and increased availability of these medications in community setting. However, our observed community rate remains significantly lower than the inpatient averages reported in recent national registries. For instance, data from the CCC-AF (Improving Care for Cardiovascular Disease in China-Atrial Fibrillation) registry demonstrated that oral anticoagulant prescriptions among hospitalized cohorts have increased substantially and exceeded those in primary care, likely because hospital-based patients receive more intensive specialist intervention [9, 15].

Notably, urban patients in our cohort were older, aligning with broader demographic trends showing larger older populations in metropolitan area [16]. The higher prevalence of comorbidities (diabetes, hyperlipidemia, carotid plaques) among urban patients may be attributed to better economic conditions and high living standards [17]. Primary drivers of non-adherence included lack of physician guidance (54.8%), and self-perceived good health leading to the dismissal of medication needs (32.7%), and unavailability of anticoagulants in the community health center (19.1%). The lack of proactive physician communication emerged as the most significant barrier, supporting that physician-patient shared decision-making is the most decisive factor in long-term medication adherence [18, 19].

Historically, hesitation among doctors to prescribe anticoagulants stemmed from Warfarin being the primary option in Chinese communities, a drug notorious for low adherence [15, 20]. Even with the widespread adoption of direct oral anticoagulants, clinical inertia persists [21]. Contemporary real-world registry data indicate that a substantial proportion of primary care clinicians still struggle with the comprehensive implementation of updated guidelines [22]. Furthermore, an exaggerated fear of past or potential bleeding events frequently outweighs the recognized benefits of stroke prevention, drastically reducing a physician’s likelihood to initiate or maintain any oral anticoagulation [7]. Alarmingly, a significant proportion of suburban patients felt well enough to forego medication, highlighting a critical knowledge deficit regarding AF risk. Therefore, targeted health education is urgently needed, particularly in suburban regions.

Patient age and NVAF risk awareness directly influenced dispensing location preferences. Older patients are more likely to utilize community dispensaries, driven by mobility limitations and the higher likelihood of having a contracted family doctor for long-term care [7, 8, 23]. This trend strongly aligns with contemporary cardiovascular consensus, which advocates for community-based care models—often facilitated by contracted primary care physicians—as the optimal setting for older adults requiring continuous medication adherence and regular monitoring. Interestingly, higher awareness of NVAF risk also correlated with community-based dispensing—a novel finding that warrants further exploration in future studies.

Beyond the regional context of Shanghai, China, our findings carry significant global implications. The urban-suburban disparities observed in this study serve as a critical microcosm for other rapidly urbanizing and aging populations worldwide [1]. As international metropolitan areas navigate similar demographic transitions, the universal challenge of effectively delivering primary care for chronic cardiovascular conditions becomes increasingly apparent [24]. Therefore, the insights gained here can be leveraged to inform targeted public health interventions in comparable global settings. Specifically, optimizing community-based medication dispensing systems—such as strengthening family doctor contract services and ensuring equitable accessibility to NOACs—can be adapted across diverse healthcare frameworks. Such targeted primary care strategies are vital to bridging the persistent global gap between guideline recommendations and real-world clinical practice [21, 22].

Limitations

Several limitations should be noted. First, the population was drawn from individuals participating in voluntary health check-ups, potentially introducing selection bias as these participants may possess higher health consciousness than the general older demographic. Second, comorbidity data were initially self-reported; however, we effectively mitigated potential recall bias by cross-referencing all responses with electronic medical records. Third, identifying the cohort in 2020 and interviewing them in 2022 inherently introduces survival bias (immortal time bias), as inclusion required surviving until follow-up. Consequently, our analyzed cohort may represent a comparatively healthier, “survivor” subset of the original 2020 population. Finally, our cross-sectional design precludes the assessment of post-2022 longitudinal mortality data, an evidence gap we plan to address in future prospective studies.

Conclusion

Our findings underscore an urgent need for healthcare institutions and community physicians to intensify patient education regarding NVAF and the critical role of anticoagulation. Robust initiatives are required to bolster patient adherence. Special strategic focus should be directed toward optimizing community-based medication dispensing systems to accommodate the preferences of older patients and those highly aware of AF risks, ensuring equitable and accessible medical services. Ultimately, by highlighting distinct primary care bottlenecks, this study provides an actionable framework for improving cardiovascular management in rapidly urbanizing megacities globally.

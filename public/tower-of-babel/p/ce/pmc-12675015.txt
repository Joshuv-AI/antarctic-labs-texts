Efficacy and safety of the HIV-1 maturation inhibitor GSK3640254 plus dolutegravir as a two-drug regimen in adults naive to antiretroviral therapy (DYNAMIC): 24-week results from a randomised phase 2b study
Authors: Samit R Joshi, Mar Masiá, Essack Mitha, Antonella Castagna, Ezequiel Cordova, Moti Ramgopal, Alison Gaudion, Supriya Karthika, James Oyee, Veronica Bainbridge, Brian Wynne, Max Lataillade
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryBackgroundThere is an unmet need for two-drug antiretroviral regimens with novel mechanisms of action that can provide additional options for people with HIV-1. The DYNAMIC study evaluated the efficacy and safety of the maturation inhibitor GSK3640254 (GSK’254) plus dolutegravir (DTG) in adults naive to antiretroviral therapy.MethodsThis double-blind, multicentre, phase 2b study enrolled participants from 39 centres in ten countries (Argentina, Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Portugal, Puerto Rico, South Africa, Spain, and the United States). Research in context

Evidence before this study

We conducted a literature search in PubMed for clinical trials published between January 1, 2014, and February 19, 2024, reporting maturation inhibitor use in people with HIV-1. The search string was “maturation inhibitor AND HIV AND (clinical trial OR phase 1 OR phase 2 OR phase 3 OR clinical study)”. Overall, 48 articles were retrieved. Of these, those describing preclinical data, reviews, non-clinical trials, studies that only included participants without HIV-1, and those not reporting on maturation inhibitors (MIs) were excluded. Four articles were identified, of which three were phase 2 studies of previous generation MIs, GSK3532795 and GSK2838232, in people with HIV-1. Findings from these studies demonstrated antiviral activity of the MI class. One article was a phase 2a proof-of-concept study of monotherapy with GSK3640254. This study established the potency of GSK3640254 monotherapy (a 2-log10 maximum change) and dose– and exposure–antiviral response relationship with no major tolerability concerns. No studies reported on the efficacy and safety of a 2-drug antiretroviral therapy regimen that included an MI. We aimed to address this knowledge gap.

Added value of this study

The DYNAMIC study is the first clinical study evaluating the efficacy and safety of once-daily oral GSK3640254 as a two-drug antiretroviral regimen as compared with a standard-of-care regimen of dolutegravir plus lamivudine. People with HIV-1 who were naive to antiretroviral therapy were randomised to receive once-daily oral GSK3640254 dosed at 100 mg, 150 mg, or 200 mg plus dolutegravir vs dolutegravir 50 mg plus lamivudine 300 mg. At Week 24, GSK3640254 plus dolutegravir demonstrated high and comparable efficacy to that of dolutegravir plus lamivudine. All GSK3640254 doses had generally comparable safety profiles to that of dolutegravir plus lamivudine, although incidence of drug-related gastrointestinal intolerability was higher in the GSK3640254 150-mg and 200-mg groups.

Implications of all the available evidence

An important goal of care is ensuring that people with HIV-1 have access to treatment options that enable them to live long and healthy lives. Two-drug regimens reduce the number of antiretrovirals that people with HIV-1 are exposed to and potentially lower treatment-associated toxicity, reduce risk of drug–drug interactions, and lower costs compared with three- or four-drug regimens. Furthermore, there is an unmet need for new antiretroviral therapy regimens with novel mechanisms of action to provide more choices for people with HIV-1 to help address their individualised needs. These findings add to the body of literature supporting the potential therapeutic capability of MIs and support their continued evaluation, in future studies, as a novel antiretroviral class, including the potential for expanding two-drug regimen options.

Introduction

Antiretroviral therapy (ART) has transformed HIV from a terminal illness into a chronic condition requiring long-term management.1 A major goal of contemporary care is ensuring people with HIV-1 have access to ART that addresses their unique needs, enabling them to live long and healthy lives.2 Guidelines recommend that individuals initiate ART containing an integrase strand transfer inhibitor such as dolutegravir (DTG) or bictegravir plus one or two nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors.3, 4, 5, 6 For two-drug regimens, the standard of care is DTG plus lamivudine (3TC).3,6 However, certain regimens may not be an option for all people with HIV-1 due to intolerability, drug–drug interactions, comorbidities, or prior drug resistance.3, 4, 5 Thus, there is an unmet need for new two-drug antiretroviral regimens with novel mechanisms of action that could provide additional options for people with HIV-1 initiating ART in these situations and potentially impact HIV-1 cure strategies.

The HIV-1 maturation inhibitor (MI) class has a distinct mechanism of action that disrupts the maturation process, which is among the final steps of the HIV-1 virus life cycle.7 Maturation inhibitors bind to the HIV Gag protein substrate and block the proteolytic cleavage of the capsid-spacer protein 1 junction site such that host cells release an immature and non-infectious precursor protein.7 Different MIs have demonstrated antiviral activity in phase 2 clinical studies of individuals naive to ART and continue to suggest therapeutic potential.8, 9, 10, 11

The HIV-1 MI GSK3640254 (GSK’254) demonstrated a 2-log viral load reduction in a phase 2a proof-of-concept monotherapy study in adults naive to ART and has demonstrated broad-spectrum inhibition across various HIV-1 subtypes.11,12 No drug–drug interactions of clinical relevance were observed with DTG, tenofovir alafenamide, or emtricitabine (FTC) in phase 1 clinical studies of participants without HIV-1, supporting concurrent use of GSK’254 with these antiretroviral agents.13,14 Here we evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of the two-drug regimen GSK’254 plus DTG in adults naive to ART through 24 weeks.

Methods

Study design and ethics

DYNAMIC was a phase 2b, randomised, multicentre, double-blind, active-controlled, parallel group study evaluating the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of GSK’254 plus DTG as a two-drug regimen compared with DTG plus 3TC in adults with HIV-1 who were naive to ART.

This study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki at 39 sites in ten countries: Argentina, Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Portugal, Puerto Rico, South Africa, Spain, and the United States. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by an investigational centre ethics committee or institutional review board before study initiation. The respective names of the authorities who granted ethical approval for the study are available in Supplementary Table S1, along with their respective reference/approval numbers. All participants (or their legally authorised representative) provided written informed consent. The study protocol is available online at: https://classic.clinicaltrials.gov/ProvidedDocs/38/NCT04900038/Prot_000.pdf.

Study participants

Eligible participants were adults who had not received any antiretroviral agents in combination or as monotherapy after HIV-1 diagnosis (based on historical evidence, when available), had plasma HIV-1 RNA ≥1000 copies/mL and CD4+ T-cell count ≥250 cells/mm3 at screening, had a body mass index >18.5 kg/m2, and weighed ≥50 kg (male at birth) or ≥45 kg (female at birth). Participants with historical (before study screening) evidence of viral resistance to integrase inhibitors or 3TC or with the maturation inhibitor resistance-associated mutations A364V or A364 A/V were ineligible. Other key exclusion criteria included evidence of an active Centers for Disease Control and Prevention Stage 3 disease except cutaneous Kaposi sarcoma not requiring systemic therapy; presence of primary HIV infection (evidenced by acute retroviral syndrome, recent [within 3 months] viremia without antibody production, and/or recent [within 3 months] seroconversion); ongoing malignancy other than cutaneous Kaposi sarcoma, basal cell carcinoma, or selected other localised malignancies; history of liver cirrhosis, unstable liver disease, or clinically relevant hepatitis within the previous 6 months; any significant psychiatric disorder; pre-existing conditions that could interfere with gastrointestinal anatomy or motility; any of the following within the past 3 months: myocardial infarction, acute coronary syndrome, unstable angina, stroke, transient ischemic attack, or intermittent claudication; familial or personal history of long QT syndrome or sudden cardiac death; and history of significant cardiac arrhythmias.

Randomisation and masking

Participants were centrally assigned to randomised treatment groups (1:1:1:1) using an Interactive Voice/Web Response System following permuted block randomisation with a block size of 8. Participant randomisation was stratified by screening plasma HIV-1 RNA (<100,000 vs ≥100,000 copies/mL). Participants received GSK’254 (100 mg, 150 mg, or 200 mg) or 3TC paired with DTG 50 mg. GSK’254 and 3TC were blinded to all participants and study personnel and blinded to the sponsor until database lock for the Week 24 analysis; DTG was unblinded. Treatment blinding for GSK’254 and 3TC was achieved by using tablets with similar appearance, and allocation concealment was performed using centralised randomisation.

Procedures

Participants were enrolled by principal investigators at each study site. Eligible participants were randomised to receive once-daily oral doses of GSK’254 100, 150, or 200 mg or 3TC 300 mg, all in combination with open-label DTG 50 mg. All study treatment doses were to be taken with a low-fat meal (including but not limited to 400–500 calories, with 25% of calories from fat).

GSK’254 doses were selected on the basis of clinical trial simulations of antiviral effect conducted via population pharmacokinetic and exposure-response modeling. Doses were aimed to achieve ≥75% of the median maximum antiretroviral effect predicted at Day 8 and to maintain steady-state trough concentrations >3 times the protein-binding adjusted 90% effective concentration (0.11 μg/mL11) in >95% of participants. GSK’254 doses of ≥100 mg administered with food once daily were predicted to exceed the 75% threshold, and a 300 mg once-daily dose was not predicted to provide a significant increase in percent of maximum effect compared with a 200 mg dose. Specifically, the 100 mg, 150 mg, and 200 mg doses had a median (5%, 95%) percent of maximum effect of 78% (55%, 98%), 86% (67%, 101%) and 90% (73%, 102%). Furthermore, the 100 mg, 150 mg, and 200 mg doses were also predicted to maintain steady-state trough concentrations >3 times the protein-binding adjusted 90% effective concentration in >95% of participants.

Study assessments

Study visits were planned at baseline (Day 1) and Weeks 2, 4, and every 4 weeks thereafter up to Week 52. Sex (female or male assigned at birth), race (American Indian or Alaska Native, Asian, Black or African American, Native Hawaiian or other Pacific Islander, White, or multiple races), and ethnicity (Hispanic/Latin American or not Hispanic/Latin American) were self-reported by participants. Plasma HIV-1 RNA samples were planned to be assessed at each visit up to Week 48. Repeat viral load testing was performed within 2–4 weeks if HIV-1 RNA was ≥50 copies/mL after previous HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL. Protocol-defined virologic failure (PDVF) required two consecutive plasma HIV-1 RNA measurements within a 2- to 4-week span that met either virologic rebound criterion (confirmed rebound in HIV-1 RNA ≥200 copies/mL after confirmed consecutive HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL) or any of the following virologic non-response criteria: (1) HIV-1 RNA <1.0 log10 copies/mL decrease from baseline, unless HIV-1 RNA <200 copies/mL by Week 12; (2) HIV-1 RNA ≥200 copies/mL at or after Week 24; (3) HIV-1 RNA ≥50 copies/mL on repeat testing of Week 24 results and before Week 28. Plasma samples for resistance testing were planned to be collected at screening and each study visit, except Week 2. For participants meeting PDVF criteria, resistance was assessed at time of suspected PDVF (the first viral load meeting PDVF criteria) with sample collected at time of interest and Day 1 sample, using PhenoSense® GT, PhenoSense® Integrase, GeneSeq® Integrase, PhenoSense® Gag assays for GSK’254, and a next-generation sequencing platform for Gag genotype (Monogram Biosciences, South San Francisco, CA). CD4+ T-cell counts were planned to be assessed via flow cytometry at all visits except Week 2.

Safety was planned to be assessed at screening (serious adverse events [AEs] and Columbia-Suicide Severity Rating Scale only) and all post-baseline visits via AEs coded using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (version 25.1) and suicidality monitoring was performed using the electronic Columbia-Suicide Severity Rating Scale. Laboratory assessments were planned at all visits except Week 2, and electrocardiograms were planned on Day 1 and at Weeks 2, 12, 24, and 48.

Outcomes

The primary endpoint was the proportion of participants with plasma HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL at Week 24 centrally assessed by PPD Laboratory Services using the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) Snapshot algorithm. Secondary efficacy endpoints were the proportion of participants with HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL at Week 48 using the FDA Snapshot algorithm, absolute values and changes from baseline in HIV-1 RNA and CD4+ T-cell count through Weeks 24 and 48, and development of genotypic or phenotypic resistance in participants with virologic failure through Week 48. Secondary safety endpoints included frequency of AEs leading to discontinuation, serious AEs, deaths, and AEs of special interest that included gastrointestinal intolerability, nervous system disorders, skin and subcutaneous disorders, psychiatric AEs, cardiac disorders, and QT prolongation up to Weeks 24 and 48. A secondary pharmacokinetic endpoint was the steady-state plasma pharmacokinetic parameters of GSK’254 up to Week 48. Efficacy and safety up to Week 24 are presented here. For secondary outcomes, changes from baseline data are presented for ease of interpretation.

Statistical analyses

The intention-to-treat–exposed (ITT-E) population consisted of all randomised participants who received ≥1 dose of study treatment. The safety population consisted of all randomised participants but excluded those with documented evidence of not receiving study treatment. The per-protocol population consisted of the ITT-E population but excluded those with important protocol violations. Prespecified descriptive subgroup analyses were conducted, categorised by baseline HIV-1 RNA levels and CD4+ T-cell counts.

Simulations were conducted with varying numbers of participants in each group to assess how changes in sample size might affect the study design. The planned sample size of each treatment group was decreased from 30 to 20 participants after the start of the COVID-19 pandemic, via substantial protocol amendment. Sensitivity analysis simulation results are available in the study protocol and determined that decreasing the group sizes from 30 to 20 participants had a minimal-to-modest impact on the probability of making a correct positive efficacy decision. The planned sample size was based on the primary efficacy endpoint of Snapshot response rate at Week 24 and determined using simulations to maximise the probability of correctly declaring positive efficacy (defined as any GSK’254 dose having a high posterior probability [≥75%] of virologic response rates within a 12% margin of the estimated 90% response rate of the DTG plus 3TC reference group at Week 24, approximated from the proportion of participants who were naive to ART receiving DTG plus 3TC with HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL from GEMINI-1/-2).15 The simulated probabilities of making a positive efficacy decision with 78%, 80%, 85%, and 90% most efficacious GSK’254 dose response rates were 0.275, 0.336, 0.55, and 0.784, respectively, supporting the robustness of the assumptions made for sample size and response rate of the DTG plus 3TC reference group.

The adjusted difference between treatment groups in proportion of participants with HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL (Snapshot) at Week 24 and associated 95% confidence intervals were analysed using a Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel test adjusting for baseline HIV-1 RNA (HIV-1 RNA <100,000 copies/mL or ≥100,000 copies/mL). A sensitivity analysis assessing proportion of participants in the per-protocol population with HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL (Snapshot) was also performed.

Although no formal statistical analyses were performed, Bayesian analyses were used to evaluate the probability that the virologic response of GSK’254 plus DTG doses had comparable efficacy to that of DTG plus 3TC at Week 24. A Bayesian hierarchical model was fitted using Markov chain Monte Carlo methods16 and the non-informative prior Beta (1,1) to estimate the posterior probability of virologic response rate for each treatment group, adjusting for baseline HIV-1 RNA. Model convergence was checked by following GSK best practices, which include visual checks of trace plots and autocorrelations and are not limited to checks on diagnostics, such as effective sample size or Gelman-Rubin plots. A pre-defined ≥75% posterior probability threshold was used to assess a difference in efficacy ≥−12% in the GSK’254 groups vs the DTG plus 3TC group. No analyses were performed to compare the doses of GSK’254 to one another. Relative risk analyses assessed the risk of common AEs (≥2%) in each GSK’254 group compared with DTG plus 3TC. Analyses were performed using SAS® software version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc, Cary, NC). The statistical analysis plan is available online at: https://classic.clinicaltrials.gov/ProvidedDocs/38/NCT04900038/SAP_001.pdf.

An independent data monitoring committee reviewed the efficacy and safety of DYNAMIC to protect the ethical interests and well-being of participants and to protect the scientific validity of the study. This trial is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT04900038.

Role of the funding source

ViiV Healthcare is the funder and study sponsor. The sponsor had a role in the study design, the collection, analysis, and interpretation of data, and the writing of the report.

Results

Study population

Screening occurred between August 18, 2021, and June 7, 2022; the Week 24 cut-off date was December 22, 2022. Of 156 individuals screened, 85 were randomised to receive one of three GSK’254 doses (100 mg, N = 22; 150 mg, N = 20; 200 mg, N = 22), each in combination with DTG 50 mg, or DTG 50 mg (N = 21) in combination with 3TC 300 mg (Fig. 1). No participant failed screening due to historical evidence of MI resistance-associated mutations A364V or A364 A/V. Participant disposition was similar between treatment groups, and no participants from any group withdrew because of COVID-19 or fatal AEs. Demographics and baseline characteristics were similar across treatment groups, with a mean (SD) age of 33.9 (8.8) years; in the overall population, the majority of participants were White (73%), male sex assigned at birth (82%), and had baseline HIV-1 RNA <100,000 copies/mL (84%); approximately one-half of the overall population was Hispanic or Latin American (Table 1). Participants in the GSK’254 200-mg and DTG plus 3TC groups had numerically higher baseline CD4+ T-cell count.

Efficacy

At Week 24, the proportions of participants with Snapshot HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL (ITT-E population) were 95% (21/22), 85% (17/20), and 77% (17/22) in the GSK’254 100-mg, 150-mg, and 200-mg groups, respectively, vs 86% (18/21) in the DTG group (Table 2). The proportion of participants with HIV-1 RNA ≥50 copies/mL were 0 (0/22), 15% (3/20), and 23% (5/22) in the GSK’254 100-, 150-, and 200-mg groups, respectively vs 10% (2/21) in the DTG plus 3TC group. Compared with the DTG plus 3TC group, the adjusted differences (95% confidence interval) in the proportion of participants with Snapshot HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL at Week 24 were 8.5% (−8.9%, 26.0%), −0.4% (−22.1%, 21.3%), and −7.1% (−30.2%, 16.1%) in the GSK’254 100-mg, 150-mg, and 200-mg groups, respectively. The GSK’254 100-mg and 150-mg, but not 200-mg, groups exceeded the prespecified ≥75% posterior probability threshold of ≥ −12% treatment difference in the proportion of participants with HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL vs the DTG plus 3TC reference group at Week 24 (Supplementary Table S2). In the ITT-E population, the proportion of participants with HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL at each visit was generally similar across GSK’254 plus DTG and DTG plus 3TC groups (Supplementary Figure S2).

Proportions of participants with HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL in each treatment group at Week 24 were generally consistent across baseline disease characteristics subgroups (Supplementary Figure S1). Proportions of participants with HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL tended to be lower in participants with baseline HIV-1 RNA ≥100,000 copies/mL for the GSK’254 150-mg and 200-mg groups and in participants with baseline CD4+ cell counts of 200 to <350 cells/mm3 for the GSK’254 150-mg group relative to participants in other subgroups; however, the sample sizes for these subgroups were low.

Two participants met PDVF criteria by Week 24 (1/22 [5%] in the GSK’254 200-mg and 1/21 [5%] in the DTG plus 3TC group). The participant in the DTG plus 3TC group was diagnosed with a concomitant Mpox viral infection, and their HIV-1 RNA values declined below the limit of detection at Weeks 4 through Week 8, then increased above detectable level at Week 16 (87 copies/mL; suspected virologic failure); virologic failure was confirmed at Week 20 (302 copies/mL), and this participant had persistent low-level viremia at their last visit at Week 24 (193 copies/mL). HIV-1 RNA values observed in the participant in the GSK’254 200-mg group declined below the limit of detection at Weeks 2 through 16, then increased to 1735 copies/mL at Week 20 (suspected virologic failure) and virologic failure was confirmed at Week 24 (653 copies/mL); this participant resuppressed 13 days later at their last visit (27 copies/mL). No treatment-emergent resistance was detected in the participant in the DTG plus 3TC group. For the participant in the GSK’254 200-mg group, genotypic and phenotypic data for integrase and genotypic data for Gag were not available due to assay failure; the PhenoSense® Gag assay showed no change in susceptibility to GSK’254 relative to Day 1 or the reference strain.

In the per-protocol population, the proportion of participants with Snapshot HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL was consistent with the ITT-E analysis (Supplementary Table S3).

Mean CD4+ T-cell count increased and remained above baseline at all visits across all doses of GSK’254 plus DTG at Week 24 (Fig. 2). The mean (SD) change from baseline in CD4+ T-cell count at Week 24 was 317.7 (175.4), 200.6 (126.7), and 241.2 (168.9) cells/mm3 in the GSK’254 100-, 150-, and 200-mg groups, respectively, and 139.5 (126.63) cells/mm3 in the DTG plus 3TC group.

Safety

Through Week 24, 79% (67/85) of all participants reported AEs. Incidence of overall AEs was similar across all GSK’254 groups and comparable to that of the DTG plus 3TC group (Table 2). In the GSK’254 groups vs DTG plus 3TC group, the 3 most common AEs were COVID-19 (10%–18% vs 14%), diarrhea (5%–23% vs 5%), and headache (9%–15% vs 10%). In relative risk analyses, there was no significant increase in risk observed for any common AE (≥2%) in any GSK’254 group relative to the DTG plus 3TC group, including gastrointestinal AEs (Supplementary Figure S3).

The proportion of participants with drug-related AEs were similar across GSK’254 100-mg (18%) and 150-mg (20%) groups and comparable to that of the DTG group (14%) but were numerically highest in the GSK’254 200-mg group (36%). There were no drug-related serious AEs. Only one drug-related AE led to withdrawal, which occurred in the GSK’254 200-mg group and is described below.

A total of three non-fatal serious AEs were reported in three participants: anogenital dysplasia (grade 2; DTG plus 3TC), non-cardiac chest pain (grade 2; GSK’254 100 mg), and Guillain-Barre syndrome (grade 3; GSK’254 100 mg); none were evaluated to be treatment-related. Two participants withdrew from the study because of AEs; one in the GSK’254 100-mg group with grade 2 rhabdomyolysis (deemed not related to treatment) and another in the GSK’254 200-mg group with drug-related grade 2 hypertensive nephropathy (the only drug-related AE leading to withdrawal). No fatal AEs were reported.

Among AEs of special interest (Table 3), incidence of gastrointestinal intolerability was numerically higher between the GSK’254 100-mg and DTG plus 3TC groups (23% and 14%, respectively), and numerically highest for the GSK’254 150-mg and 200-mg groups (35% and 36%, respectively). There was a similar incidence of drug-related gastrointestinal intolerability AEs between the GSK 100-mg (9%) and DTG (10%) groups; incidence of gastrointestinal intolerability AEs appeared to increase with GSK’254 dose (GSK’254 150 mg, 15%; GSK’254 200 mg, 23%). Incidence of nervous system disorders of special interest were numerically lowest in the GSK’254 100-mg group (9%) and numerically higher in the GSK’150-mg and 200-mg groups (20% and 23%, respectively) compared with the DTG plus 3TC group (10%). No participants in the GSK’254 150-mg and DTG plus 3TC groups experienced skin and subcutaneous tissue disorders of special interest; 18% and 14% of participants in the GSK’254 100-mg and 200-mg groups, respectively, reported these AEs. Psychiatric AEs and cardiac disorders of special interest were generally low across groups. No AEs related to QT prolongation were reported. All AEs of special interest were a maximum grade of 1 or 2, except for one grade 3 psychotic disorder event reported in a participant in the DTG plus 3TC group that was deemed as not treatment related.

Discussion

The two-drug regimen GSK’254 plus DTG demonstrated generally comparable efficacy, safety, and tolerability to that of DTG plus 3TC. High proportions of participants achieved virologic suppression at Week 24 in all treatment groups by Snapshot analysis in the ITT-E population. Of the two participants who met PDVF criteria, one was in the GSK’254 200-mg group and one was in the DTG plus 3TC group. All GSK’254 doses had generally comparable safety to that of DTG plus 3TC, although incidence of low-grade gastrointestinal intolerability was higher in the GSK’254 groups.

Virologic response rates across the GSK’254 treatment groups generally exhibited comparable efficacy to that of DTG plus 3TC. Among the GSK’254 plus DTG groups, both the 100-mg and 150-mg doses exceeded the ≥75% posterior probability threshold in the Bayesian treatment difference analysis using the Snapshot algorithm (ITT-E), although the 200-mg dose did not exceed this threshold. Furthermore, across study weeks, all doses of GSK’254 plus DTG had generally similar rates of virologic suppression compared with DTG plus 3TC. The results from the DYNAMIC study are consistent with the robust efficacy of contemporary second-generation integrase strand transfer inhibitor–based ART.3 The high virologic response rates in the DTG plus 3TC group were expected and consistent with pooled results from the phase 3 GEMINI-1 and -2 trials in which 93% of people with HIV-1 who were naive to ART and initiated DTG plus 3TC had virologic suppression at Week 24.17

The phase 2b DOMINO study assessed the same dose range of GSK’254 but with two nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors rather than DTG.18 The efficacy findings at Week 24 from DYNAMIC and DOMINO were consistent with one another, with both showing higher rates of virologic suppression in the GSK’254 100-mg (DYNAMIC, 95%; DOMINO, 83%) and 150-mg groups (DYNAMIC, 85%; DOMINO, 91%). The lowest rates of virologic suppression were in the 200-mg groups (DYNAMIC, 77%; DOMINO, 76%). In DOMINO, lower rates of virologic suppression in the GSK’254 200-mg group were due to missing virologic data in the Week 24 Snapshot window, attributable to discontinuations due to AEs and participants with virologic non-response in the Snapshot window.18 In DYNAMIC, virologic response for the GSK‘254 200-mg group was slightly lower because five participants had virologic non-response in this group, of which four were blips; in contrast to DOMINO, no participants discontinued due to AEs.

Among the two participants meeting PDVF criteria, no treatment-emergent resistance was detected in the participant receiving DTG plus 3TC, and genotypic and phenotypic data were not available for the participant receiving GSK’254 200 mg plus DTG. Similarly, no treatment-emergent resistance was detected by Week 24 of the DOMINO study.18 By contrast, among people with HIV-1 who were naive to ART and initiated the previous MI GSK3532795 (GSK’795) plus tenofovir disoproxil fumarate/FTC, 10/12 developed treatment-emergent resistance to FTC and 4/8 developed treatment-emergent Gag substitutions through Week 24.9 An improved barrier to resistance of GSK’254 compared with GSK’795 may be expected based on in vitro data that showed an improved antiviral profile of GSK’254 vs GSK’795.19

Rapid and sustained decreases in HIV-1 RNA were observed in all groups at Week 24. Increases in CD4+ T-cell count were observed across all groups, demonstrating that both GSK’254 plus DTG and DTG plus 3TC resulted in immunologic improvements through 24 weeks. Participants in the GSK’254 100-mg group had the greatest increases in CD4+ T-cell count. These results indicate that even the lowest tested dose (100 mg) of GSK’254 was effective and led to immunologic recovery.

Overall AEs were similar across GSK’254 groups and comparable to the DTG plus 3TC group. No drug-related serious AEs and few grade 2 to 5 drug-related AEs were reported, and only one study withdrawal was attributed to a drug-related AE. No significant increase in risk was observed for any common AE in the GSK’254 groups relative to the DTG plus 3TC group. A low and similar incidence of drug-related gastrointestinal intolerability AEs of special interest was observed between the GSK’254 100-mg and DTG groups, but numerically higher rates were observed in the GSK’254 150- and 200-mg groups. Other than gastrointestinal AEs, there was no difference in AEs of special interest. No AEs of QT prolongation were observed in any group.

The DYNAMIC study has some limitations, including a study population with disproportionately high White (73%) and male sex assigned at birth (82%) demographics. Though each treatment group had relatively small sample sizes, they were sufficiently powered to maximise the probability of making a correct positive efficacy decision using posterior distributions of response rates in this phase 2b study. In relative risk analyses assessing the risk of common AEs, wide confidence intervals were observed for some AEs due to sparse events.

Ultimately, it was determined that the intended fixed-dose combination of GSK’254 plus DTG would not be differentiated enough from existing two-drug daily oral regimens; thus, the DYNAMIC study was stopped by the sponsor.

A major goal of care is ensuring that people with HIV-1 have access to treatment that enables them to live long and healthy lives.2 There is currently an unmet need for new complete two-drug ART regimens with novel mechanisms of action that provide more choices for people with HIV-1 and may help accommodate their unique needs. Two-drug regimens reduce the number of antiretrovirals that people with HIV are exposed to with potentially lower treatment-associated toxicity, risk of drug–drug interactions, and costs compared with three-or four-drug regimens.17,20 Furthermore, novel mechanisms of action can offer value to those with concerns of drug–drug interactions or tolerability, or prior resistance. This study represents the first time a two-drug regimen with an MI has been evaluated, and GSK’254 plus DTG demonstrated robust efficacy with rates of virologic suppression comparable to those of a standard-of-care two-drug regimen and had a generally clinically acceptable safety and tolerability profile. These data support the potential evaluation of MIs as a novel antiretroviral class for HIV-1 treatment, possibly expanding two-drug treatment options.

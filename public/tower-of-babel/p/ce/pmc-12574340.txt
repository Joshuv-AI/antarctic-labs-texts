Effectiveness of mHealth-based vision screening on uptake of referral services among children of government schools in Rawalpindi, Pakistan: study protocol for a randomised controlled trial
Authors: Ayesha Javed, Shamaila Mohsin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionVision screening in educational settings holds significant importance for early detection of visual impairment. A key challenge is the low uptake of referral services which significantly limits the effectiveness of screening. Mobile health (mHealth) is one intervention that is rapidly being adopted in Pakistan with more than 100 million smartphones users. However, evidence of its effectiveness on referral uptake outcomes is still limited.Methods and analysisA concurrent, parallel-group, single-blinded randomised controlled trial will be conducted in government schools of Rawalpindi district, Pakistan. Introduction

Globally, there are over 2 billion individuals with visual impairment.1 Almost two-thirds of this burden of disease is situated in low and middle income countries.2 A significant proportion of those affected with visual impairment are children.3

Pakistan ranks third among the top 20 countries affected by blindness or moderate to severe visual impairment.4 In Pakistan, more than 2 million children are estimated to be affected by blindness and visual impairment.5

Evidence indicated that visual impairment significantly diminishes quality of life, especially in young children, and is becoming a prominent public health concern worldwide.6 Childhood visual impairment creates obstacles to learning, growth and educational achievement, which, in turn, impacts future employment opportunities and socioeconomic standing.7

A recent systematic review (2000–2020) reported that uncorrected refractive errors, especially myopia, are the most prevalent cause of vision impairment among children.8 Evidence suggests that possible developmental delays occur when refractive problems are not corrected in infancy and early childhood.9 Uncorrected refractive problems in this age range also have a correlation with clinically acknowledged deficits in cognitive and visual-motor skills.10 In addition, it may result in both immediate and long-term negative physical and psychological effects, such as increased susceptibility to accidents and injuries, poor visual-motor skills, low self-esteem, depression and anxiety, and verbal and physical bullying.11

In terms of time and resources, school eye health programmes are efficient12 and provide a cost-effective paradigm for providing eye care to school children.13 Evidence indicates that timely intervention such as vision screening carries potential to prevent the vicious circle of personal and social decline among children.14 However, one of the key challenges highlighted on evidence regarding visual screening programmes is the low uptake of referral services for children diagnosed with visual impairments, which significantly limits the effectiveness of these programmes.15

mHealth has emerged as one of the most significant public health interventional tools in industrialised nations and is rapidly being adopted in many public health programmes in LMICs.16 Numerous mobile health organisations have created smartphone apps that screen for visual acuity, make it easier to refer patients to eye care professionals and notify those who have been screened.17 Literature suggests that mHealth has shown promise in Pakistan, particularly in improving chronic disease management, such as diabetes care, medication adherence in stroke survivors and increasing immunisation uptake.16 However, to our knowledge, its potential in the context of improving the uptake of referral services following vision screening remains largely unexplored in LMIC, particularly in Pakistan, despite the fact that more than 100 million people use smartphones18 as stated by the Telecommunication Authority of Pakistan.

Furthermore, there have been recommendations for additional research in LMICs to demonstrate a strong causal association between the effectiveness of mHealth in improving referral uptake following a screening programme.19 To the best of our knowledge, this will be the first study of its kind, a multicomponent approach related to professional guidelines, recommendations and technology application, that has not been used in this way in pre-existing literature. The aim of the study is to assess the benefit of mHealth reminders in improving the uptake of referral services and consequently enhancing the effectiveness of vision screening.

Hypothesis

We hypothesise that a multicomponent mHealth referral reminder will have an effect on the uptake of referral services among visually impaired school children identified through screening.

Methods

Trial design

A concurrent, parallel-group, single-blinded randomised controlled trial will be conducted in federal government (FG) schools of Rawalpindi district, Pakistan, to assess the effectiveness of a multicomponent SMS reminder on referral uptake. Participants will first be screened for visual impairment using mHealth visual acuity application. Following visual acuity testing, schools will be randomised in a 1:1 allocation ratio using computerised randomisation into two parallel groups: one group will receive the conventional referral method (printed referral form) while the other group will receive multicomponent SMS reminders in addition to the printed referral form.

This will be an individually randomised trial, with the child serving as the unit of inference and analysis. Schools will be randomised solely for administrative and logistical purposes, rather than as analytical clusters. School-level allocation will be used to minimise contamination between intervention and control arms. The intervention (multicomponent SMS reminders) will be delivered to individual children and their parents, and referral uptake will be measured at the individual child level. Responses of parents will be assumed to be independent of one another; therefore, the unit of inference will remain the individual child.

Pilot testing will be conducted on 10% of the intended sample size to evaluate the feasibility of implementing the intervention and to identify potential systematic errors. Figure 1 illustrates the expected algorithm of trial steps.

Study population

The study will be performed in two FG schools of Rawalpindi district, Pakistan. FG schools cater to the educational needs of the majority of Pakistani children and students mostly studying in these schools belong to low to middle income families. High smartphone and internet penetration in urban Pakistan,20 together with already established use of school WhatsApp groups by parents for regular communication, supports the feasibility of including smartphone access as an eligibility criterion. Study participants will be 5–15 years of age as per WHO recommendations for vision screening,21 boys and girls (in approximately equal proportion), who are identified with visual impairment through mHealth-based vision screening.

Recruitment

A meeting will be held with the principal of each school to discuss the purpose of the research. This will be followed by a brief awareness session during the school assembly to inform parents and pupils about the significance of screening, along with an interactive question and answer session. At the time of enrolment, mothers will be instructed on how to open and read SMS text messages. Subsequently, children will be assessed for eligibility, and those who provide assent along with parental consent (online supplemental file 1) will undergo visual acuity testing using the validated Peek Acuity app (Peek Vision Ltd, London, UK) on the following day. This application, which incorporates a comprehensive software platform with data collection capabilities and SMS reminder functionality, will be used on a smartphone. The Peek Acuity application has demonstrated a sensitivity of 84.6%, specificity of 97.7%, positive predictive value of 68.8% and negative predictive value of 99.1%.22 23 Distance visual acuity will be assessed using a cut-off of four out of five optotypes at the 6/12 level, tested at a distance of 3 m. Participant enrolment is expected to begin on 21 October 2024 and be completed by 8 November 2024, with follow-up assessments projected to conclude by 14 January 2025. Vision screening will be conducted by two researchers, with support from three school teachers. Multiple scheduled screening sessions will be conducted across both schools, with additional sessions arranged after school hours or on staggered days to maximise reach and capture absent children. Following visual acuity testing, participants will be randomised to either the intervention or control group.

Randomisation and blinding

Computerised random number generator programme (https://www.sealedenvelope.com/) will be used to allocate schools in a 1:1 ratio. An independent resident from the Department of Community Medicine, Army Medical College, will prepare cards for the random assignment of schools. These cards will be sealed in sequentially numbered, opaque envelopes. The allocation sequence will be concealed from the research team responsible for participant enrolment and assessment. After allocation, the principal investigator will record the name of the school on each envelope to prevent subversion of the randomisation process.

It was not possible to blind the study participants or data collectors due to the nature of the intervention. However, during the course of the trial, the assigned person who conducted follow-up and data analysis remained blinded.

The individual responsible for follow-up contacts will remain blinded to group allocation by working from a coded participant list that does not identify arm membership and will record outcomes using participant identifiers only. The data analyst will receive a de-identified dataset with intervention arm masked to permit unbiased analysis. Unblinding will be permitted only if knowledge of allocation is necessary to protect participant safety or enable urgent clinical management or at the request of the ethics board or regulatory authority. Any unblinding will follow a documented approval and reporting procedure.

Intervention and comparator

Trial intervention

Parents or guardians in the intervention group will receive a multicomponent SMS reminder in addition to printed referral forms. These reminders will be delivered every Sunday, Tuesday and Thursday for a duration of 8 weeks, or until the child attends the hospital. The research team has already developed an automated text message reminder system for this study, taking into account the local context. The content of the messages was designed with input from a panel of public health consultants and research supervisors, drawing on resources from the WHO Health Promotion and Disease Prevention Messages Library.21

The SMS reminder system will comprise two main components: (a) web-based application for children registration and automated reminder scheduling, and (b) an SMS application for text messaging that is sent automatically. Messages will be sent in Urdu, the native language, and will not require a reply. Each message will include four elements: health promotion message; visual depiction of the vision of the child; a customised action plan detailing how to get to the hospital, including the location; how much money is needed and what to carry with them. The intervention will be delivered by the principal investigator (AJ) under the direct supervision of the research supervisor (SM).

Comparator group

Participants in the control group will receive a printed referral form. Parents of children identified with visual impairment will be advised to seek further assessment and treatment. Using ‘no reminder’ as the most appropriate comparator will allow us to evaluate the added value of mHealth-based reminders against conventional practice.

Adherence monitoring and concomitant care

Adherence in the intervention group will be monitored via automated delivery and read-status reports in the Peek Admin dashboard, while in the comparator group (printed referral form), adherence will be assumed under standard practice without additional monitoring. Undelivered SMS messages will be documented and addressed in sensitivity analyses. No restrictions will be placed on concomitant care, and participants may continue to receive all routine healthcare services during the trial.

Follow-up

The participants will be required to follow-up every week. Coded lists of screened positive children, along with the contact number of their parents, will be provided to an independent researcher appointed to carry out the follow-up. Telephonic follow-up will be conducted weekly for 8 weeks to monitor the proportion of referred children attending the hospital in both groups, solely for outcome ascertainment and not to remind or encourage attendance. The researcher will remain masked to the group that the participant was assigned and will use a standardised script, asking only whether the child has attended the referral hospital while no advice, prompting or reinforcement will be provided. The question asked would be, “Since the screening, has your child attended the referral hospital? Yes/No/ Date”. The parent who does not reply to the phone call will be marked in the list and contacted again up to three times. Outcome assessment will be performed by a separate independent researcher who will also be masked to the assigned groups. Participant enrolment is expected to commence on 21 October 2024.

The intervention or follow-up will be discontinued if a participant withdraws consent, is found ineligible after registration or requires treatment for an eye condition. The date and reason for discontinuation will be documented. Participants who refuse consent, follow-up assessments or data provision will be considered withdrawn.

Public and patient involvement

Key stakeholders will be actively involved throughout the research process. During study design, parents and teachers will be engaged to provide insights on community needs and preferences, ensuring the intervention remains culturally appropriate and accessible. Additionally, their feedback on smartphone use and familiarity with mobile-based communication will guide the development of a multicomponent message. For conducting the trial, school teachers and management will be trained to deliver the screening, ensuring implementation is sensitive to local dynamics and addresses participation barriers. Dissemination strategies will be co-developed with school administration and teachers to ensure that study outcomes are effectively communicated to those who can benefit most.

Outcomes

Primary outcome measure

Primary outcome of interest is the proportion of referred children who attend the hospital within 8 weeks of referral in both groups. It will be assessed by weekly telephonic follow-up of referred children.

Secondary outcome measures

Secondary outcome of interest is the association of hospital attendance with sociodemographic characteristics of referred participants and will be assessed by comparing the sociodemographic characteristics of referred participants who attend the hospital with those who do not, using follow-up data.

This secure platform enables real-time monitoring, automated SMS reminders, user management and tailored reporting. Data will be captured on encrypted mobile devices and synchronised to the Peek Admin dashboard via HTTPS/TLS. All information will be stored on cloud servers with strict role-based access controls, ensuring confidentiality. Access will be restricted to authorised study personnel, with only data managers and principal investigators permitted full dataset access prior to publication of results.

To promote retention, participants will be informed that referral services are provided free of cost and will be given a dedicated contact number to raise any concerns regarding the vision of their child. Outcome assessments will be conducted via telephone follow-up to maximise convenience. Participants who discontinue or decline the intervention will still be requested to provide data on hospital visits at the 8-week follow-up. These procedures, including the voluntary nature of continued data provision, will be clearly explained during the informed consent process. No financial or material incentives will be offered at any stage of the study.

Sample size calculation

Sample size was calculated using Clinical Calculator (https://clincalc.com/stats/samplesize.aspx) with ∞=0.05 and power=80; sample size was found to be 72. The referral uptake reference value 54% versus 22% was determined by a previous study.25

Adjusted sample size with 10% expected dropouts=sample size (1+dropout rate)=80. Pilot testing was conducted on 10% of the intended sample size, comprising eight participants. The purpose was to evaluate the smooth implementation of the intervention and to identify any systematic errors. These participants were not included in the final analysis. The pilot sample was in addition to the 80 participants who were ultimately enrolled in the main study.

Statistical analysis

Data will be analysed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, V.27.0 (IBM Corp, Armonk, New York, USA). Normality will be assessed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test, with a p value ≤0.05 considered statistically significant. Sociodemographic characteristics, frequency of visual impairment and the number of referred children attending hospitals will be summarised as percentages. All randomised participants will be included in the intention-to-treat analysis. The effect of referral reminders on referral uptake will be examined using a Cox regression model, reporting HRs with 95% CIs. Kaplan-Meier estimates will be used to illustrate cumulative referral uptake graphically. To address missing data due to attrition, an adjusted sample size with a 10% dropout rate has been applied, and data will be analysed according to the ‘intention-to-treat’ principle. Sensitivity analyses will be performed to account for non-adherence (undelivered SMS messages).

Data monitoring

A formal data monitoring committee is not considered necessary for this trial, as it is a minimal-risk, school-based intervention without investigational drugs or high-risk procedures. Trial oversight, including data quality, participant safety and adherence to protocol, will instead be managed internally by the principal investigator and the research team. No interim analyses are planned and no formal stopping guidelines are established, as the trial involves minimal risk, short duration and a limited sample size. Trial conduct will be monitored by the principal investigator and the research team through routine oversight of recruitment, data collection and adherence to study procedures.

Adverse event reporting and harms

Previous studies have reported no adverse events associated with the use of visual acuity mobile applications. Nevertheless, any signs or incidents of adverse events will be addressed promptly. All adverse events will be immediately reported to the National University of Medical Sciences (NUMS) Ethics Review Board, which will determine the appropriate course of action for the study.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethical approval was obtained from the Ethical Review Committee of Army Medical College (ERC/ID/415, dated 5 September 2024). Permission to conduct screening was granted by the FG Education Institutions Directorate, Rawalpindi (0409 /F-28/2024 FGEI (CPD-Trg), dated 20 October 2024). All children participating in the trial will be required to provide written informed consent from parents or guardians, along with the assent of the child obtained prior to the commencement of screening. Consent forms will be provided by the research team in the native language (Urdu) to ensure clear understanding (online supplemental file 1). Special consideration will be given to message timing to avoid inconvenience (eg, late night texts), and messages will not include personally identifiable discomfort.

The messaging system will be securely maintained at a single site with restricted access. A contact number will also be provided for participants to address any questions or concerns. Personal information will be collected using participant codes to ensure anonymity. Identifiable data, such as consent forms, will be stored separately from survey materials and not entered into the electronic database. Electronic data will be securely stored with restricted access, limited to investigators and data managers. Only de-identified data will be used for analysis and reporting, with records retained per ethical requirements.

No ancillary or post-trial care is anticipated, as the intervention carries minimal risk. In the unlikely event of harm, participants will be referred to appropriate healthcare services, and any compensation will follow institutional ethics policies. Any modifications will be approved by the ethics review board, updated in ClinicalTrials.gov and communicated to participants with revised information sheets prior to re-consent.

Findings will be disseminated through methodology papers, conference presentations and peer-reviewed publications. Results will be reported according to the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials guidelines. Participation in national and international academic conferences will provide further opportunities for dissemination.

Discussion

This study will provide evidence for the effectiveness of mobile health-based vision screening combined with automated, multicomponent SMS reminders on referral uptake in a low-resource setting. By reporting the frequency of visual impairment among schoolchildren, it will contribute valuable data on childhood vision health in low and middle income countries, extending beyond the scope of previous regional studies. The anticipated increase in follow-up rates would highlight the potential of mHealth to bridge healthcare gaps and facilitate timely interventions for children at risk of permanent visual impairment. To the best of our knowledge, this will be the first trial in Pakistan to evaluate the effectiveness of mHealth intervention in improving referral uptake following vision screening.

The methodological strengths of this study include its gold-standard randomised controlled trial design with allocation concealment, objective outcome ascertainment, measures to minimise attrition and a robust statistical approach, all of which enhance internal validity and the credibility of the findings. The incorporation of a multicomponent intervention adds methodological rigour and represents a distinctive feature of this trial. A number of methodological limitations need to be taken into account when evaluating this protocol. The follow-up period of 8 weeks may not fully capture the long-term impact of mobile health interventions on referral uptake. Although mobile phone text messages are widely used in mHealth strategies, the reliance solely on SMS reminders could limit effectiveness in settings with high illiteracy rates, as some parents or guardians may not fully comprehend the messages. Variations in network access and mobile literacy may also restrict the generalisability of the findings.

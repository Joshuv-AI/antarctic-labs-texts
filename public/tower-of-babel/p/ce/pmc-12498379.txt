Predicting six-month mortality in adult hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis with machine learning: a prognostic approach utilizing laboratory data
Authors: Jun Zhou, Mingjun Xie, Yongbin Ma, Min Wang, Jingping Liu, Yaman Wang, Mengxiao Xie, Hua-Guo Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) is associated with high mortality rates. This study was conducted to develop and validate a predictive model for adult HLH patients at high risk of six months mortality using machine learning (ML) algorithms.MethodsThe study utilized univariate analysis and LASSO regression, incorporating eleven ML algorithms, to perform a comprehensive analysis of both admission and discharge variables. Model performance was assessed using metrics such as AUC, F1 score, Kaplan Meier (KM) curves, calibration curves and decision curve analysis (DCA).ResultsA total of 136 patients meeting the HLH-2004 criteria were included in this study. Among them, 59 patients received chemotherapy or other cytotoxic drugs, while the remaining 77 patients underwent symptomatic treatment. The median age was 52 years among nonsurvivors and 48 years among survivors, with 47 (42.6%) males in the nonsurvivor group and 31 (47.6%) in the survivor group. Age and nine discharge variables were identified as the most significant features for model construction. Random forest (RF) algorithm demonstrated superior predictive capabilities, achieving an AUC of 1.00, accuracy of 0.98 and F1 score of 0.98 in the training cohort, and an AUC of 0.89, accuracy of 0.85 and F1 score of 0.85 in the validation cohort. The top five predictors were all discharge variables (ferritin, white blood cell (WBC), albumin (ALB), platelet (PLT) and direct bilirubin (DB)).ConclusionThe predictive model developed in this study provides a valuable tool for clinicians to early identify high-risk HLH patients, thereby enabling more targeted and effective interventions.

Introduction

Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) is a rare, life-threatening condition characterized by hyperactivation of immune cells, leading to excessive cytokine release and subsequent inflammatory processes [1]. The disease generally advances quickly, and its intricate pathophysiology contributes to a high mortality rate. Diagnosing adult HLH has traditionally depended on criteria set for pediatric patients, resulting in diagnostic delays and misdiagnoses among adult patients [2,3]. Despite recent progress in research and therapeutic interventions that have enhanced prognosis, the mortality rate for HLH continues to be high. Consequently, prompt diagnosis, early identification of patients at high risk, and the execution of targeted interventions are essential for improving outcomes.

The advent of machine learning (ML) has spurred rapid developments in medical science, with applications ranging from disease diagnostics to prognostication [3,4]. Research has demonstrated that ML algorithms surpass traditional methods in predictive accuracy [5,6]. These algorithms are adept at managing complex nonlinear relationships and can autonomously pinpoint the most relevant features associated with disease onset and patient prognosis from extensive variables. However, the application of ML to the prognostication of adult HLH patients remains uncharted territory. To bridge this gap, our study aims to employ a suite of ML algorithms, including Adaptive boosting (AdaBoost), artificial neural networks (ANN), decision tree (DT), extreme gradient boosting (XGboost), gradient boosting decision tree (GBDT), K-nearest Neighbor (KNN), light gradient boosting machine (LightGBM), logistic regression (LR), Naive-Bayes (NB), random forest (RF) and support vector machine (SVM) to construct a comprehensive predictive model for six-month mortality risk in HLH patients. The model will integrate both admission and discharge laboratory variables, to aid clinicians to dynamically assess patient prognosis. Our initiative seeks to harness the power of ML to enhance clinical decision-making and ultimately improve the prognosis for patients confronting this formidable condition.

Patients and methods

In this study, clinical data from individuals initially diagnosed with HLH at the First Affiliated Hospital With Nanjing Medical University from 2010 to 2018 were retrospectively analyzed. The data encompassed demographic details, initial and final hematological test results upon admission and discharge (including ferritin levels, complete blood count, coagulation function, and biochemical results), as well as the six-month survival status, which was gathered from electronic health records and telephone follow-up interviews. Patients aged 18 years and above who met the HLH-2004 diagnostic criteria for HLH were included in the study [3]. Those with incomplete clinical information or follow-up outcomes were excluded. For patients who had repeated admissions, only the data from their first HLH diagnosis confirmation were utilized. To handle missing data (less than 5%), multiple imputation techniques with 10 imputations and predictive mean matching (Linear regression model is used for imputation of continuous variables, while logistic regression model is used for imputation of discrete variables) were employed (IBM SPSS Statistics (Version 21.0)). Following this, all laboratory data were standardized (Min-Max Normalization to all continuous variables) and incorporated into the research.

Model construction

The cohort was segregated into training and validation sets in a 7:3 proportion. The scikit-learn (1.2.2) library facilitated the data division and the application of stratified sampling to ensure an even distribution of outcome categories in both the training and validation datasets. Given the limited number of cases and the extensive range of variables, measures were taken to avoid overfitting. Initially, we examined laboratory indicators showing notable variations between the two patient groups within the training cohort. Subsequently, LASSO regression was applied to pinpoint the key factors linked to six-month mortality, thus reducing collinear variables. The intersection of variables from both analyses was then used to build the model. A spectrum of machine learning algorithms, encompassing Adaptive boosting (AdaBoost), artificial neural networks (ANN), decision tree (DT), extreme gradient boosting (XGboost), gradient boosting decision tree (GBDT), K-nearest Neighbor (KNN), light gradient boosting machine (LightGBM), logistic regression (LR), Naive-Bayes (NB), random forest (RF) and support vector machine (SVM), were employed to refine the model. The model creation process was carried out in Python (3.10), utilizing Python libraries such as scikit-learn (1.2.2), XGBoost (1.7.4), and LightGBM (4.0.0). GridSearchCV from scikit-learn (1.2.2) was used to determine the best algorithm parameters. Model efficacy was assessed through AUC, sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive values (PPV), negative predictive values (NPV), F1 score, calibration curves, and decision curve analysis (DCA). Furthermore, Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP 0.44.0) values were utilized to illustrate their correlation with six-month mortality. A greater area under the curve (AUC) in both the training and validation cohorts signifies enhanced discrimination capability. The model that demonstrated the highest AUC across both cohorts was chosen for further investigation (Figure 1). Ethical clearance for this research was granted by the First Affiliated Hospital With Nanjing Medical University’s ethics committee (2019-SR-066), Due to the retrospective nature of this analysis using anonymized clinical data, the ethics committee granted a waiver of informed consent requirements.

Statistical analyses

Continuous variables were reported as mean ± standard deviation or median ± interquartile range and were assessed using the unpaired t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were depicted as absolute number (n) and relative frequency (%) and were evaluated using the Chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test. All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS Statistics for Windows (Version 21, Armonk, NY, USA). The development of machine learning models was accomplished with MyLab + i-Research. Statistical significance was determined at a p-value of less than 0.05.

Results

The study involved a total of 136 adult HLH patients, of whom 65 (47.8%) succumbed within six months. Among them, 59 patients received chemotherapy or other cytotoxic drugs, while the remaining 77 patients underwent symptomatic treatment. The median age was 52 years among nonsurvivors and 48 years among survivors, with 47 (42.6%) males in the nonsurvivor group and 31 (47.6%) in the survivor group. The patients were randomly allocated into a training cohort and a validation cohort, ensuring comparability between the two (Table 1). In the training cohort, the median ages for survivors and nonsurvivors were 44 years and 53 years, respectively. The median survival duration for nonsurvivors was 4 weeks in both cohorts. Notable differences were found between survivors and nonsurvivors regarding age, median survival time, and levels of admission biomarkers (urea nitrogen (UREA) and creatinine (CREA)), as well as discharge biomarkers (ferritin, white blood cell (WBC), neutrophil (NE), hemoglobin (HB), platelet (PLT), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), hydroxybutyrate dehydrogenase (HBDH), triglyceride (TG), high density lipoprotein (HDL), total protein (TP), albumin (ALB), globulin (GLB), glucose (GLU), and calcium (Ca)). No significant differences were observed in other clinical parameters and laboratory data between the two groups.

Feature selection and comparison of multiple classification models

Due to the relatively small sample size, to prevent overfitting, LASSO regression was utilized to identify the most pertinent biomarkers associated with six-month mortality. The selected biomarkers included age, admission ferritin, admission Fibrinogen (FIB), admission ALT, admission direct bilirubin (DB), admission GLU, admission uric acid (UA), discharge ferritin, discharge WBC, discharge HB, discharge PLT, discharge AST, discharge DB, discharge TP, discharge ALB, discharge sodium (Na), and discharge Ca (Figure 2 A-B). These biomarkers were then integrated with potential factors exhibiting a P-value < 0.05 as shown in Table 1. Age > 58 y and nine discharge predictors (ferritin >1056.1 μg/L, WBC ≤ 2.1 × 109/L, ALB ≤ 30 g/L, PLT ≤ 50 × 109/L, DB > 13.6 μmol/L, TP ≤ 52.4 g/L, AST > 58.6 U/L, Ca ≤ 2.06 mmol/L and HB ≤ 90 g/L) were chosen as the definitive features for constructing the prediction ML model (Figure 2C). There is no multicollinearity among these ten indicators (Supplement Figure 1). The efficacy of the eleven ML classification models in predicting six-month mortality within the training and validation cohorts, using the final selected features, was compared and is summarized in Table 2 and Figure 2 D-E. The findings reveal that the RF algorithm exhibited the highest performance in both cohorts (Training: AUC = 1.00; Validation: AUC = 0.89). Additionally, in the validation cohort, both the RF algorithm and the XGboost algorithm demonstrated superior accuracy and F1 score (Both accuracy: 0.85; Both F1 score: 0.85).

Model performance and interpretation

Following the analysis, the RF algorithm was chosen as the most effective method for predicting the occurrence of six-month mortality. Figure 3 A-B illustrates the AUC of the RF algorithm using the final selected features in both the training and validation groups for predicting six-month mortality. Calibration curves confirmed good calibration across both patient groups (Figure 3 C-D). DCA indicated that the predictive model had substantial clinical utility (Figure 3 E-F). 5-fold cross-validation confirmed this result (Supplement Figure 2). Additionally, SHAP was employed to visualize the overall feature importance for each variable in relation to the model’s output, with the top five most influential variables identified as discharge ferritin, discharge WBC, discharge ALB, discharge PLT, and discharge DB (Figure 4A).

Model presentation

To improve the model’s generalizability, practicality, and ease of use, a web-based calculator for the final RF model was developed. This web application (https://jssrm-zj2-lil3qwmdpy9bksh58sk6gy.streamlit.app/) enables other researchers to replicate the analyses and assess the model’s performance (Figure 4B).

With the predictive model, the training cohort and validation cohort were split into high-risk and low-risk groups based on the risk score in different cohort as the cutoff (0.39). A Kaplan-Meier (KM) survival analysis was then performed, as shown in Figure 4 C-D, which indicated that patients in the high-risk group had a poorer prognosis both cohorts.

In conclusion, the ten variables identified through screening, when applied using the RF algorithm, were successful in predicting the risk of death within six months for HLH patients and were confirmed in the validation cohort.

Discussion

This research employed ML algorithms to create and validate a predictive model for the risk of death within six months among adult patients diagnosed with HLH. The RF algorithm outperformed the other eleven algorithms in terms of performance. The model, which integrated ten variables that are readily accessible, exhibited robust predictive power. The most significant variables, ranked as discharge ferritin, discharge WBC, discharge ALB, discharge PLT, and discharge DB, were identified as the top five. A web-based calculator was developed to offer clinicians a practical tool for early identification of high-risk patients, thereby aiding in more targeted treatment strategies.

Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) is indeed characterized by an exaggerated pathological inflammatory response. In adult HLH, activated lymphocytes and macrophages produce high levels of both pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines. This cytokine storm can result in liver involvement and the development of a progressive multiple organ dysfunction syndrome. The condition is severe and requires prompt and appropriate management to address the underlying immune dysregulation and prevent further organ damage [7,8].

Serum ferritin, which is an acute-phase reactant, elevates in reaction to infections, inflammation, and numerous other stressors, thereby playing a crucial role in the diagnosis of HLH, as well as in tracking treatment effectiveness and evaluating patient prognosis [3,9,10]. Both our past studies and the ongoing research have affirmed that ferritin stands out as the key determinant impacting six-month mortality in patients [11]. This underscores the importance of monitoring ferritin levels in HLH patients to guide clinical management and improve outcomes.

WBC, a conventional marker of systemic inflammation, is extensively utilized in clinical settings to gauge the body’s inflammatory condition. The presence of significant pancytopenia or cytopenia affecting two or three types of blood cells is a frequent characteristic and a diagnostic standard for HLH3 [12],. Studies have shown that neutrophil, lymphocytopenia (lymphocytes < 0.5 × 109/L), and LMR (lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio) were confirmed as risk factors for poor prognosis [11,13–15]. In our study, the findings support these established risk factors.

Albumin, constituting a part of total protein, is essential for maintaining colloid osmotic pressure inside blood vessels [16]. Serum ALB has the ability to bind with multiple inflammatory mediators and exhibits properties such as antioxidant effects, anti-inflammatory actions, anticoagulant capabilities, and inhibition of platelet aggregation [17]. In individuals suffering from severe infections, the concentration of circulating serum albumin generally decreases with the intensification of inflammation [18]. ALB also serves as an indicator of an individual’s nutritional health and influences the survival rates of HLH patients [19]. Those with low serum albumin levels experience significantly poorer survival outcomes. Our findings also support this observation.

Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis is characterized by an inflammatory condition, and research has shown that inflammation and immune responses play critical roles in its onset and progression [20]. PLT, which are small fragments of cytoplasm originating from megakaryocytes, circulate in the blood and are essential for hemostasis and thrombosis. These PLTs contain various types of granules; when activated, they release these granules [21,22]. The released granules act as a significant component that may be associated with the initiation of a severe cytokine storm [23]. Thrombocytopenia is a prevalent characteristic and a diagnostic standard for HLH12. PLTs serve a practical and precise function in risk stratification for adult HLH patients [15]. Our earlier studies suggested that thrombocytopenia is a prognostic indicator influencing the outcomes of adult patients with HLH [24]. In line with our past research, PLTs have been identified as valuable for risk classification and the prediction of unfavorable survival outcomes.

Bilirubin, an endogenous byproduct of blood catabolism, is predominantly processed and eliminated by hepatocytes [25]. An elevation in direct bilirubin (DB) suggests dysfunction of hepatocytes and could be indicative of liver injury during the progression of HLH [26]. Our study has confirmed that DB is closely linked with six-month mortality in patients.

In this research, we incorporated a broad range of laboratory tests: both admission and discharge indicators. To prevent overfitting, we initially compared the differences between the two groups (survivor and nonsurvivor) within the training cohort and selected indicators with a p-value below 0.05. Following this, we utilized LASSO regression to pinpoint the indicators most closely related to the six-month prognosis. By integrating the common indicators from both algorithms, we reduced the extensive list of variables to ten for constructing the model using machine learning (ML). Although our study encompassed both admission and discharge variables, through stringent selection processes, all variables included in the final model were discharge indicators. This study represents the first to compare both admission and discharge indicators, confirming that discharge indicators offer clinicians more dependable and accurate insights into a patient’s six-month survival prospects than admission indicators. From the perspective of the disease process, when patients are admitted, the admission indicators are more interfered by the acute onset. At discharge, after treatment, the indicators can better reflect the body state and treatment response after the disease is controlled or not. Our prior research, which employed logistic regression, also showed that discharge ferritin was a superior predictor of a patient’s six-month survival compared to admission ferritin [27]. This current study reinforces that discharge ferritin is the most prominent indicator among all the variables examined.

A prognostic model composed of IL-2R, PT, and IL-10 has a prediction accuracy, however, these indicators, such as IL-2R and IL-10, are not included in routine laboratory tests, which is unfavorable for the promotion and popularization of the model [28]. The model established by Shen, which includes age, CREA, albumin, PLT, lymphocyte ratio, and ALT, and the model established by Wang, which includes age, coagulation disorders, low HB, AST, LDH, ferritin, and PT, although they can significantly improve prognostic performance, are all based on traditional methods [14,29]. Machine learning algorithms can rapidly process complex data and exploit useful information. Therefore, in our study, we first applied machine learning algorithms to the 6-month prognosis of adult HLH patients and screened out the optimal algorithm from 11 different algorithms, significantly enhancing the predictive performance (training set: 1.00; test set: 0.89). Moreover, these performances were achieved based on commonly used clinical indicators. To facilitate clinical application, we also developed a web-based calculator. With this web-based calculator, doctors or patients can use these ten indicators based on the patient’s discharge outcomes to calculate a score risk. According to the score, patients can be stratified into high-risk (>0.39) and low-risk (≤0.39) groups, enabling targeted treatment interventions.

The study is based only on routine admission and discharge laboratory parameters. However, the rapid evolution of multi-omics technologies and digital health is continuously generating novel candidate predictors—such as circulating micro-RNAs, radiomics features from contrast CT, or real-time physiologic signals from wearables. Our framework was explicitly designed to accommodate these future data streams. When a new variable type becomes available, we simply append its corresponding penalized coefficient without retraining on the entire historical dataset.

Limitation

While we utilized univariate analysis and LASSO regression to minimize variables and avoid overfitting, resulting in a successful model, it is crucial to recognize its limitations, such as the constraints imposed by the sample size and potential data bias, which may lead to model instability or suboptimal performance. Moreover, given the rarity of the disease, our study covered a lengthy time period, which led to the omission of some newer biomarkers like sCD25, PCT, IL-6, underlying disease and therapeutic interventions possibly impacting the model’s predictive accuracy. Additionally, we did not have an external validation cohort. Hence, future research should concentrate on increasing the sample size, incorporating data from more diverse populations, and integrating novel biomarkers. Prospective, multi-center cohort studies are essential to further refine and validate the model’s predictive capabilities for wider application.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the ML models we have developed demonstrate robust predictive abilities for identifying high-risk HLH patients at risk of mortality within six months, using variables that are readily available. A web-based calculator serves as an accessible tool for physicians to craft precise preventive and therapeutic strategies. Furthermore, our findings suggest that the model performs best when it exclusively includes discharge variables, with ferritin standing out as the most critical factor.

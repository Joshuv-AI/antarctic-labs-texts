Fibroblast pentose phosphate pathway activation upon decreased circPLCE1 exacerbates intestinal fibrosis in Crohn’s disease
Authors: Longyuan Zhou, Jing Nie, Zhiyin Feng, Rongchang Li, Pingxin Zhang, Sinan Lin, Yao Zhang, Florian Rieder, Changhao Chen, Minhu Chen, Ren Mao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIntestinal fibrosis, a hallmark complication of Crohn’s disease (CD), frequently progresses to stricture formation and surgical intervention. Fibroblast metabolic reprogramming is important in organ fibrosis. However, its role in intestinal fibrogenesis of CD remains elusive.ObjectiveWe aim to explore the metabolic reprogramming of fibroblasts and its upstream regulators during intestinal fibrosis of CD.DesignWe performed metabolome, single-cell RNA sequencing and spatial transcriptome on paired mucosal and submucosal tissue from the strictured and adjacent non-strictured intestinal segments. The candidate metabolite and metabolic enzymes were verified in primary human intestinal myofibroblasts (HIMFs) and dextran sulfate sodium-induced intestinal fibrotic mice. Next, we identified fibrosis-associated circPLCE1 to regulate the pentose phosphate pathway (PPP) using the circRNA transcriptome. Finally, we studied the functions and mechanisms of circPLCE1 using metabolome, transcriptome, metabolic flux, seahorse assay and RNA pull-down assay in HIMFs and fibroblast-specific circPLCE1 knockdown mice.ResultsMultilayer integrated analysis identified activation of PPP in fibroblasts during intestinal fibrosis of CD. Specifically, xylulokinase (XYLB)-generated xylulose-5-phosphate (Xu5P) promoted extracellular matrix synthesis by epigenetic upregulation of collagen transcription. Moreover, downregulation of circPLCE1 in fibroblasts activated PPP, resulting in increased glycolysis, nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate production and aggravated intestinal fibrosis in vitro and in vivo. Mechanistically, circPLCE1 directly bound the domain-I of XYLB and competitively inhibited its enzymatic activity. Decreased circPLCE1 restored XYLB activity and accumulation of Xu5P in intestinal fibrosis.ConclusionOur findings delineate a circPLCE1/XYLB/Xu5P axis in fibroblasts which orchestrates PPP and fibrogenesis, unveiling a novel therapeutic target for intestinal fibrosis of CD.

Introduction

Intestinal fibrosis emerges as a particularly recalcitrant pathological process that underlies stricture formation, perforation, fistulisation and ultimately organ dysfunction across multiple digestive diseases, particularly in IBD.1 2 In Crohn’s disease (CD) specifically, approximately 50% of patients progress to clinically significant fibrostenotic complications, facing inevitable surgical interventions and substantial lifelong healthcare expenditures.3 4 Nevertheless, there are few effective methods of identifying the early stage of fibrotic transformation or reversing existing intestinal strictures.5 6

Dysfunction of fibroblasts is a hallmark of organ fibrosis.7 On tissue injury, resident fibroblasts undergo activation through inflammatory and profibrotic cytokines, subsequently differentiating into an extracellular matrix (ECM)-producing myofibroblast phenotype via Smad-dependent and Smad-independent pathways (including mitogen-activated protein kinase/extracellular regulated protein kinase and bone morphogenetic protein signalling).8 This phenotypic transformation is accompanied by profound metabolic reprogramming that supports cell proliferation and biosynthetic demands of fibrogenesis.9 10 In idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, for instance, fibroblasts exhibit enhanced glycolysis, accumulation of tricarboxylic acid cycle intermediates (particularly succinate) and consequent collagen overproduction.11 However, the metabolic alterations in fibroblasts driving intestinal fibrosis of CD remain unclear.

Emerging evidence highlights the critical regulatory roles of non-coding RNAs (eg, microRNAs, long non-coding RNAs and circular RNAs) in mediating metabolic reprogramming during organ fibrogenesis.12 13 Among these, circRNAs exhibit unique advantages as potential diagnostic biomarkers due to their tissue-specific expression patterns and remarkable stability.14 15 For example, steatohepatitis-associated circRNA ATP5B regulator ameliorates liver cirrhosis by suppressing lipid-induced inflammation in hepatic fibroblasts.16 However, the involvement of circRNAs in metabolic dysfunction of fibroblasts during intestinal fibrosis of CD remains to be elucidated.

In this study, we performed metabolome, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) and spatial transcriptome on intestinal tissue of patients with strictured CD (CDS) and identified activation of the pentose phosphate pathway (PPP) in fibroblasts in intestinal fibrosis. Specifically, xylulokinase (XYLB)-generated xylulose-5-phosphate (Xu5P) epigenetically enhanced collagen gene transcription. CircPLCE1 directly interacted with XYLB and functioned as a competitive endogenous inhibitor suppressing XYLB enzymatic activity. Decreased circPLCE1 restored the activity of XYLB and therefore promoted PPP and fibrogenesis. These results revealed a circPLCE1/XYLB/Xu5P regulatory axis contributing to PPP activation in fibroblasts, unveiling a novel therapeutic target for the intestinal fibrosis of CD.

Methods

Procurement of human intestinal tissues

As previously described, fresh full-thickness intestinal specimens were surgically resected from patients with CD and immediately processed for analysis.17 Using a rigorously validated procurement system, matched specimens from each patient were categorised as either CDS or adjacent non-strictured CD (CDNS) based on comprehensive gross anatomical evaluation (n=30 pairs for non-targeted metabolome, n=6 pairs for scRNA-seq and n=4 pairs for spatial transcriptome). CDNS samples represented inflammatory disease without stricturing or penetrating complications, while CDS specimens included strictures with or without concurrent penetrating disease. This procurement system was further confirmed through radiologic and histopathologic assessment performed by the experienced IBD radiologist and pathologist blinded to clinical data.

The fibrosis severity was graded on a standardised 4-point scale (0=none, 1=mild, 2=moderate, 3=severe) incorporating histological and morphometric parameters.18 Moreover, bowel wall thickness (BWT) was measured by magnetic resonance enterography (MRE) or CT enterography (CTE).18 The intestinal segment with the thickest bowel wall was selected as the representative segment. Using MRE, magnetisation transfer ratio (MTR) was calculated using the following formula: MTR=1−(Msat/M0), where Msat and M0 represent signal intensities acquired with and without the off-resonance prepulse saturation, respectively.18 The standardised MTR values were normalised to skeletal muscle reference to minimise individual variation.18 For the patient evaluated by CTE, MTR was not calculated.

Isolation and culture of HIMFs

Primary human intestinal myofibroblasts (HIMFs) were isolated and cultured as previously described.17 After intestinal resection, the mucosal and submucosal tissue was stripped and incubated in dithiothreitol (DTT) for 30 min and then was transferred into Hank’s Balanced Salt Solution (HBSS) with penicillin and streptomycin for 3 hours. Subsequently, the tissue was cut into small pieces (2–3 mm2) and placed onto a prescored 100 mm tissue culture dish for about 15 min. Afterward, the plate was flooded with culture medium (ie, Dulbecco’s minimal essential medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 2% glutamine and 1% antibiotics). The outgrowing cells were cultured to confluence and fed twice a week in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2 at 37°C. HIMFs were used between passage 3 and 10.

Isolation of HIMFs for transcriptome

To perform transcriptome, HIMFs were isolated as previously described (n=5 pairs).19 Fresh mucosa and submucosal layers of non-fibrotic and stricture tissue were dissected, washed with ice-cold phosphate buffer saline (PBS) and cut into small pieces. Epithelial cells were removed by rotating the tissue at 37°C for 45 min in digestive buffer (HBSS without Ca2+ and Mg2+, supplemented with 5 mM ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA), 15 mM N-2-hydroxyethylpiperazine-N-2-ethane sulfonic acid (HEPES), 1 mM DTT and 10% FBS). After that, small tissue pieces were hand-shaken for 5 min and washed in ice-cold PBS. Minced tissue was digested with collagenase VIII at 0.38 mg/mL and DNase I at 0.1 mg/mL in DMEM (containing 10% FBS, 100 U/mL penicillin and 100 mg/mL streptomycin) for 60 min at 37°C. After digestion, cells were filtered through a 70 µm cell strainer, washed in fluorescence activated cell sorting (FACS) buffer (PBS with 2% FBS and 1 mM EDTA) and spun down at 500 g for 5 min. Finally, single-cell suspension was collected for magnetic sorting (CD45-, CD31- and CD90+) and used for transcriptome.

Seahorse analysis

Oxygen consumption rate (OCR) and extracellular acidification rate (ECAR) were measured using a Seahorse XFe96 extracellular flux analyser (Agilent, California, USA) following the manufacturer’s protocols. Briefly, HIMFs were seeded on XFe96 microplates at a density of 1×104 cells/well. OCR was measured with the Mito-stress test kit. The baseline was recorded, followed by injection of 1.5 µM oligomycin, 2 µM carbonyl cyanide-p-trifluoromethoxyphenylhydrazone (FCCP) and 0.5 µM rotenone/antimycin A. ECAR was measured with the glycolysis stress test kit. The baseline was recorded, followed by injection of 10 mM glucose, 2 µM oligomycin and 50 mM 2-deoxyglucose.

Results

Multilayer data integration highlights activation of PPP in fibroblasts of CDS

To characterise the metabolic landscape in intestinal stenosed segments of patients with CD, we first performed non-targeted metabolome of paired mucosal and submucosal tissue from the CDS and CDNS intestinal segments (n=30 pairs; figure 1A, online supplemental table 1).20 We identified 119 significantly dysregulated metabolites (116 upregulated and 3 downregulated; fold change >1.20 or <0.83 and p<0.05; figure 1B). Strikingly, pathway analysis in the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes demonstrated pronounced enrichment of PPP, with marked elevations in PPP-related metabolites, including glucose-6-phosphate (G6P), fructose-6-phosphate (F6P), glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (GAP), Xu5P and sedoheptulose-7-phosphate levels in CDS compared with CDNS (figure 1C,D).

Furthermore, to explore the major cell types contributing to PPP activation, we analysed the scRNA-seq dataset (PRJCA021346) of CDNS and CDS tissue from patients with CD (n=6 pairs; figure 1E).21 22 We identified 12 major cell types by unsupervised clustering and classical marker gene annotation (figure 1F, online supplemental figure 1A). Fibroblasts showed most upregulated ECM feature in CDS (online supplemental figure 1B). We then calculated the PPP signature score of each cell type by using the mean normalised expression of genes from a specific PPP gene set.23 This focused gene panel comprises transketolase (TKT), transaldolase 1 (TALDO1), ribulose-5-phosphate-3-epimerase (RPE), ribose-5-phosphate isomerase A (RPIA), glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD), phosphogluconolactonase (PGLS), and phosphogluconate dehydrogenase (PGD), encoding core enzymes that are structurally and functionally dedicated to the PPP.24 25 Among these 12 cell types, fibroblasts exhibited the most enhanced PPP score secondary to myeloid cells (figure 1G). Specifically, PPP-related genes were also upregulated in fibroblasts of CDS, such as PGLS, PGD, G6PD, TKT, TALDO1 and RPE (figure 1H). To dissociate the effects from inflammation, we included another scRNA-seq dataset containing CDNS, inflamed (CDI), and CDS tissue from patients with CD (n=13 pairs).26 We identified nine major cell types by unsupervised clustering and classical marker gene annotation (online supplemental figure 1C,D). We also found that fibroblasts in CDS exhibited a significantly elevated PPP score as compared with CDNS and CDI (online supplemental figure 1E). These findings were validated in transcriptomic analysis of HIMFs isolated from CDNS and CDS (n=5 pairs; online supplemental figure 1F, online supplemental table 2).20 We found 90 dysregulated genes (43 upregulated and 47 downregulated; fold change >2.0 or <0.5 and p< 0.05), and PPP was also significantly enhanced in HIMFs of CDS compared with CDNS (online supplemental figure 1G,H). Overall, PPP was activated in fibroblasts during intestinal fibrogenesis.

Finally, to evaluate the spatial characterisation of PPP in intestinal fibrosis, we processed paraffin-embedded intestinal specimens of CDNS and CDS for spatial transcriptome using the Stereo-seq platform (n=4 pairs; figure 1I). We calculated the spatial score of PPP using the aforementioned PPP gene panel and found that spots of PPP were more enriched in CDS than CDNS, particularly in mucosa and submucosa (figure 1J–L). Specifically, expression of G6PD, TKT, RPE and TALDO1 was upregulated in CDS than in CDNS (figure 1M). Integration of the aforementioned spatial transcriptome and scRNA-seq dataset confirmed that fibroblast-specific PPP score was higher in CDS than in CDNS, with elevated expression of G6PD, TKT, TALDO1 and RPE (figure 1N–P). Collectively, these findings demonstrated that PPP activation in fibroblasts was a hallmark of intestinal stricture in patients with CD.

Xu5P exacerbates intestinal fibrogenesis through epigenetic upregulation of collagen transcription

PPP runs in parallel to glycolysis and is active in tissue maintenance and repair (figure 2A).27 To find the key PPP-related metabolites for intestinal fibrosis, we analysed the aforementioned metabolomic profile.20 Among all PPP-related metabolites, the level of Xu5P exhibited the most significant difference between CDS and CDNS groups (n=30 pairs; figure 2B). Xu5P was upregulated in CDS compared with CDNS (2.049-fold, p<0.001, figure 2B and online supplemental table 1). Moreover, we evaluated the clinical relevance of Xu5P in intestinal stricture. Xu5P expression levels (CDS/CDNS ratio) were positively correlated with established clinical metrics of fibrosis severity, such as fibrosis score (Spearman r=0.570 and p<0.01), standardised MTR (Spearman r=0.640 and p<0.001) and BWT (Spearman r=0.826 and p<0.001) at the most stenotic segment (figure 2C). These clinical findings positioned Xu5P as a biomarker of existing strictures in CD.

Xu5P is a product of the non-oxidative PPP (non-oxPPP) and coordinately enhances glycolysis through phosphofructokinase activation and protein phosphatase 2A-dependent transcriptional induction of glycolytic genes.28 We next investigated the profibrotic role of Xu5P in intestinal fibrosis. Pilot studies were performed to explore appropriate concentrations of administered Xu5P. We treated HIMFs with Xu5P at concentrations of 0 µM, 4 µM, 20 µM and 40 µM. Treatment with 20 µM Xu5P resulted in the pronounced upregulation of Col1A1 and α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) and was considered effective and used for subsequent in vitro experiments (online supplemental figure 2A). Transcriptomic profiling revealed upregulation of collagen genes in Xu5P-treated HIMFs, enriched with collagen containing ECM and secretion of collagens in pathway analysis (each group, n=3; figure 2D,E).20 To elucidate the underlying mechanisms, we performed an assay for ATAC-seq comparing Xu5P- and PBS-treated HIMFs (each group, n=3).20 While global chromatin accessibility changes were modest, we observed remarkably increased accessibility of collagen gene loci (eg, Col1A1, Col1A2, Col3A1, Col4A1, Col4A2, Col5A1, Col6A1, Col6A2 and Col8A1) in Xu5P-treated HIMFs, consistent with their transcriptional activation (figure 2F–H). Epigenetic characterisation demonstrated that Xu5P treatment increased acetylated H3K27 (H3K27ac) enrichment at the Col1A1 enhancer, while reducing repressive trimethylated H3K27 (H3K27me3) histone modification at both enhancer and promoter regions, as confirmed by chromatin immunoprecipitation-quantitative polymerase chain reaction (ChIP-qPCR) (figure 2I,J). Taken together, Xu5P promoted transcription of collagen mRNA through chromatin remodelling at specific ECM gene loci.

In addition, we validated the profibrotic effect of Xu5P in vivo. We conducted pilot experiments in acute dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-treated mice models by intraperitoneally administering 0 µg, 10 µg, 20 µg and 40 µg of Xu5P (Each group, n=4). Among these doses, mice receiving 20 µg of Xu5P displayed aggravated body weight loss and exacerbated intestinal inflammation, which indicated selection of the 20 µg doses for subsequent in vivo experiments (online supplemental figure 2B,C). We next administered weekly intraperitoneal injections of Xu5P or PBS in wild-type (WT) mice (water, each group, n=5; DSS, each group, n=6). Xu5P-treated mice exhibited more body weight loss, increased collagen production and intestinal fibrosis exacerbation during DSS moulding, but no significant fibrotic aggravation without DSS induction (figure 2K,L, online supplemental figure 2D,E). Hence, Xu5P aggravated ECM accumulation and intestinal fibrosis.

XYLB promotes PPP and intestinal fibrosis

Considering that XYLB is the upstream enzyme catalysing the ATP-dependent conversion of D-xylulose to Xu5P, we then investigated the role of XYLB in intestinal fibrosis (figure 2M).29 Consistent with the upregulation of Xu5P, expression of XYLB was elevated in mucosal and submucosal tissue from the CDS compared with CDNS (1.916-fold, p<0.05, n=15 pairs; online supplemental figure 2F). Upregulation in XYLB was also observed in DSS-induced intestinal fibrotic mice (qPCR: 1.873-fold, p<0.01; each group, n=10), compared with the control group (online supplemental figure 2G,H).

In mammals, XYLB is the terminal enzyme in the glucuronate-xylulose pathway and is important to hepatic and renal glucose metabolism and lipogenesis.29 We then explored the function of XYLB in intestinal fibrosis. Overexpressing XYLB enhanced intracellular NADPH and glutathione, elevated ECAR and diminished OCR in HIMFs, suggesting activated PPP and glycolysis and limited oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) (online supplemental figure 2I–L). Furthermore, overexpressing XYLB also promoted ECM production (eg, Col1A1 and α-SMA, online supplemental figure 2M). As for in vivo validation, we intraperitoneally injected adeno-associated virus (AAV) in WT mice 7 days before inducing chronic fibrosis with DSS (Each group, n=5; figure 2N). Supplementation of XYLB in mice exhibited more body weight loss, shortened colons and ECM accumulation, suggesting more severe intestinal fibrosis (figure 2O, online supplemental figure 2N–P). Altogether, XYLB promotes PPP activation and intestinal fibrosis.

CircPLCE1 is decreased in fibroblasts of CDS and DSS-induced fibrotic mice models

Recent studies implicated that circRNAs were key regulators of the PPP.25 30 However, their specific roles in fibroblasts during intestinal fibrosis of CD remained poorly understood. To systematically identify fibrosis-associated circRNAs, we conducted comprehensive circRNA transcriptomic analysis at both tissue (intestinal segments, n=10 pairs) and cell levels (HIMFs isolated from CDNS and CDS, n=5 pairs) (figure 3A, online supplemental tables 2 and 3).20 Our results revealed 53 differentially expressed circRNAs in intestinal tissue of CDS (14 upregulated and 39 downregulated; fold change >2.0 or <0.5, and p<0.05), and 35 in HIMFs of CDS compared with CDNS (2 upregulated and 33 downregulated; fold change >2.0 or <0.5, and p<0.05) (figure 3B). Intersectional analysis identified circPLCE1 as the sole consistently downregulated circRNA across both datasets (figure 3B,C). These findings nominated circPLCE1 as a key regulator and was chosen for further study.

Subsequently, extended validation in a larger sample size confirmed circPLCE1 reduction in CDS compared with CDNS (0.513-fold, p<0.001, n=30 pairs; figure 3D and online supplemental table 1). The circPLCE1 expression (CDS/CDNS ratio) was negatively correlated with Xu5P levels (n=30, Spearman r=−0.790 and p<0.001; figure 3D). Fluorescence in situ hybridisation (FISH) showed that circPLCE1 was predominantly located in fibroblasts and was decreased in mucosa of CDS (figure 3E). Moreover, circPLCE1 expression levels (CDS/CDNS ratio) showed negative correlations with fibrosis metrics, including fibrosis score (Spearman r=−0.739 and p<0.001), standardised MTR (Spearman r=−0.616 and p<0.01) and BWT (Spearman r=−0.808 and p<0.001) at the most stenotic segment (figure 3F). These robust correlations indicated that circPLCE1 downregulation closely tracks with advancing intestinal fibrosis in patients with CD.

According to the human reference genome (GRCh38/hg38), circPLCE1 was 1570 bp in spliced length originating from the exon 2 of the PLCE1 gene, with back-splicing confirmed by Sanger sequencing (figure 3G). To further confirm the circular characteristics of circPLCE1, circPLCE1 and PLCE1 from cDNA and genomic DNA were amplified by divergent and convergent primers, and gel electrophoresis showed that circPLCE1 only existed in the cDNA (online supplemental figure 3A). CircPLCE1, but not linear PLCE1, could be amplified after RNase R exonuclease digestion (online supplemental figure 3B). CircPLCE1 was more stable than PLCE1 mRNA after actinomycin D treatment (online supplemental figure 3C). CircPLCE1 showed predominant cytoplasmic distribution by FISH and qPCR (figure 3H, online supplemental figure 3D). As for the mouse homologue, circPLCE1 originated from the exon 2 of the Plce1 gene, exhibiting similar circular characteristics (eg, RNase R resistance, divergent primer amplification; online supplemental figure 3E–G). CircPlce1 was decreased in colonic tissue of the DSS-induced fibrotic model compared with the control group (0.531-fold, p<0.01; each group, n=10; online supplemental figure 3H). FISH confirmed that circPLCE1 was decreased in fibroblasts of the DSS-induced fibrotic model (online supplemental figure 3I). These comprehensive results demonstrated that circPLCE1 was an abundant, stable and circular transcript, which was potentially associated with intestinal fibrosis.

Fibroblast-specific circPLCE1 knockdown exacerbates intestinal fibrosis

To investigate the functional role of circPLCE1 in intestinal fibrosis, HIMFs were isolated and transfected with circPLCE1-overexpressing or circPLCE1-knockdown lentivirus (figure 4A). Compared with the control group, HIMFs exhibited reductions of ECM components after overexpressing circPLCE1, whereas circPLCE1 knockdown increased ECM deposition (figure 4B,C).

The biological function of circPLCE1 in fibroblasts was also confirmed in vivo. Fibroblast-specific circPLCE1 knockdown (circPLCE1Fib-KD) mice were generated by intercrossing sh-circPLCE1-floxed mice (control mice) with Col1a2-Cre mice (figure 4D, online supplemental figure 4A–C). After fibrosis moulding with DSS, circPLCE1Fib-KD mice showed increased body weight loss and shorter colons than control mice (online supplemental figure 4D–F). More severe histological damage and fibrotic features (eg, collagen deposition and fibronectin accumulation) were also observed in DSS-treated circPLCE1Fib-KD mice (each group, n=8, figure 4E; online supplemental figure 4G,H). To further explore the function of dysregulated circPLCE1, transcriptomic analysis of colonic tissue from DSS-treated circPLCE1Fib-KD mice and DSS-treated control littermates was conducted (each group, n=6; figure 4F). Upregulation of ECM genes (eg, Cols and Fn1) and profibrotic cytokines (eg, Tgf-β1 and Il33) was observed in DSS-treated circPLCE1Fib-KD mice (figure 4G). Pathway analysis highlighted enrichment in ECM organisation, ECM assembly, collagen metabolism, fibroblast activation, etc (figure 4H). Hence, circPLCE1Fib-KD mice exhibited enhanced susceptibility to DSS-induced intestinal fibrosis. Finally, restoring circPLCE1 by AAV delivery promoted histological recovery and fibrosis alleviation (each group, n=7; figure 4I,J).

Collectively, circPLCE1Fib-KD was functionally involved in intestinal fibrosis.

CircPLCE1 downregulation in fibroblasts activates PPP

To explore the potential role of circPLCE1 in PPP activation in fibroblasts, we performed comprehensive metabolic characterisation of HIMFs, using metabolome, metabolic flux, seahorse, etc (figure 5A). Indeed, metabolomic profiling demonstrated marked accumulation of PPP-related metabolites after knocking down circPLCE1 in HIMFs, such as G6P, 6-phosphogluconic acid (6PG), ribose-5-phosphate (R5P), Xu5P and erythrose-4-phosphate (E4P) (each group, n=5; figure 5B,C). Considering that PPP is the bypass of glycolysis, we confirmed concomitant increases in glycolytic metabolites after knocking down circPLCE1 (figure 5C). In addition, given that PPP is important to biosynthetic processes of nuclear acid and amino acid, we also found that nucleosides and most essential amino acids were upregulated after circPLCE1 knockdown (online supplemental figure 5A). Additionally, [13C6]glucose tracing assay confirmed that more glucose-derived carbon fed into both PPP and glycolysis in circPLCE1-knockdown HIMFs (each group, n=6; figure 5D, online supplemental figure 5B). Seahorse analysis showed diminished ECAR and elevated OCR in HIMFs after overexpression of circPLCE1, whereas circPLCE1 knockdown produced the opposite effect (figure 5E,F, online supplemental figure 5C,D). Considering that the PPP is a major source of NADPH production, we found increased NADPH and GSH and reduced cellular ROS in HIMFs after knocking down circPLCE1 (figure 5G,H). Thus, these observations indicated that downregulation of circPLCE1 activated PPP and glycolysis, but limited OXPHOS in fibroblasts.

As for in vivo experiments, we performed transcriptome and metabolome analyses of DSS-treated circPLCE1Fib-KD mice and control mice (figure 5I). The transcriptomic analysis showed upregulation of genes related to PPP and glycolysis in DSS-treated circPLCE1Fib-KD mice (each group, n=6; figure 5J). Pathway enrichment analysis confirmed the essential role of PPP and glycolysis in DSS-treated circPLCE1Fib-KD mice compared with control mice (figure 5K). Metabolomic analysis of colonic tissue revealed the upregulation of metabolites related to PPP and glycolysis in DSS-treated circPLCE1Fib-KD mice, including F6P, fructose-1,6-biphosphate, dihydroxyacetone phosphate, 2-phosphoglyceric acid, glucose-1-phosphate, 6PG, GAP, Xu5P and E4P (each group, n=6; figure 5L,M). In summary, decreased circPLCE1 in fibroblasts promoted PPP activation.

CircPLCE1 directly interacts with cytoplasmic XYLB and competitively inhibits its enzymatic activity

Previous studies have reported that circRNAs can interact with proteins to form specific ribonucleoprotein complexes to modulate protein function.31 To study the molecular mechanism of circPLCE1, we performed RNA pull-down assays to identify potential circPLCE1-interacting proteins. We designed probes against the junction sequence of circPLCE1 and coupled them with biotin. We then performed RNA pull-down in the lysate of HIMFs, followed by mass spectrometry and western blotting (figure 6A). Among 23 proteins pulled down by the circPLCE1 probe but not by the control probe, only XYLB was previously reported to regulate PPP (figure 6B,C, online supplemental table 4).29 This interaction was then validated using RNA immunoprecipitation assay (figure 6D). We found that circPLCE1 was markedly enriched with anti-XYLB-Flag antibodies (figure 6E). Furthermore, circPLCE1 was colocalised with XYLB in the cytoplasm of HIMFs (figure 6F). Collectively, XYLB was considered a prime candidate.

Structural analysis revealed XYLB is a two-domain architecture, in which xylulose was bound to domain-I (6-288aa) and ATP to domain-II (289-531aa).29 AlphaFold 3 modelling predicted circPLCE1 binding to domain-I of XYLB, which we confirmed experimentally using domain-specific truncations (figure 6G). We synthesised plasmids with truncations of XYLB (deletion of domain-I or domain-II) and transfected them into 293 T cells. RNA pull-down assays demonstrated that the subunit of XYLB protein was enriched in deletion of domain-II, but not domain-I (figure 6H). Functional assays revealed that circPLCE1 overexpression reduced XYLB activity, while circPLCE1 knockdown increased XYLB activity in HIMFs (figure 6I). These findings demonstrated that circPLCE1 directly bound the domain-I of XYLB and competitively inhibited the enzyme activity of XYLB.

XYLB is crucial for decreased circPLCE1 to promote PPP activation and intestinal fibrosis

We next confirmed the function of XYLB as the key downstream effector of circPLCE1 using rescue experiments. While circPLCE1 knockdown elevated both XYLB expression and ECM components, XYLB inhibition in the circPLCE1-knockdown HIMFs reversed the upregulation of ECM (figure 7A, online supplemental figure 6A). XYLB inhibition also downregulated NADPH and glutathione, attenuated ECAR and increased OCR in HIMFs, suggesting limited PPP and glycolysis and activated OXPHOS (figure 7B,C, online supplemental figure 6B). As for in vivo validation, we intraperitoneally injected AAV in mice 7 days before inducing chronic fibrosis with DSS (figure 7D). After knocking down XYLB, control mice and circPLCE1Fib-KD mice exhibited histological recovery and fibrosis alleviation (each group, n=6; figure 7E, online supplemental figure 6C–E).

Moreover, Xu5P, the product of XYLB, was increased in circPLCE1Fib-KD mice and circPLCE1-knockdown HIMFs, compared with the corresponding control groups (figure 7F). Exogenous Xu5P supplementation increased intracellular NADPH and glutathione levels, rescued glycolysis impairment and suppressed OXPHOS in circPLCE1-overexpressing HIMFs (figure 7G,H, online supplemental figure 6F). Altogether, XYLB is a critical mediator for decreased circPLCE1 in activating PPP and intestinal fibrosis.

Discussion

Metabolic reprogramming has emerged as a fundamental mechanism driving fibrotic pathogenesis.32 Our study identified activation of PPP in fibroblasts as a previously unrecognised mediator of intestinal fibrosis in CD. Specifically, XYLB-generated Xu5P functioned as a critical metabolic effector to promote collagen gene transcription during fibrogenesis. Moreover, circPLCE1 knockdown restored XYLB activity, subsequently triggered PPP activation and promoted fibrogenesis. Finally, increased Xu5P and decreased circPLCE1 were potential biomarkers for intestinal fibrosis in patients with CD (online supplemental figure 7).

The PPP operates in parallel to glycolysis and consists of the oxidative branch (oxPPP) and non-oxPPP.27 33 The oxPPP generates R5P from G6P and produces NADPH, while the non-oxPPP facilitates reversible interconversion of R5P with glycolytic intermediates. Beyond its canonical roles in redox homeostasis (via NADPH) and nucleotide biosynthesis (via R5P), the PPP critically supports macromolecular synthesis, providing precursors for lipids, proline, tetrahydrofolate and deoxyribonucleotides.3436 These functions make PPP indispensable for tissue maintenance and repair across multiple organs, including heart, kidney, brain, skin and intestine. For example, knockout of transketolase (ie, the gene encoding a non-oxPPP enzyme) in intestinal epithelial cells develops apoptosis-induced spontaneous colitis due to impaired regeneration.37 Hepatic transketolase promotes steatohepatitis and fibrosis by limiting inosine-induced mitochondrial activity.24 Based on evidence from other fibrotic diseases, we hypothesised that there likely exists a reciprocal relationship between PPP activation and intestinal fibrosis: enhanced PPP flux promotes fibrosis in intestinal cells, and subsequent fibrosis-driven hypoxia and inflammation could further amplify PPP activation. However, it remains challenging to definitively discern whether PPP activation is an initiating event or a reinforcing consequence within this feedback loop. Further mechanistic studies will be essential to dissect the temporal and causal relationships between PPP activation and fibrotic progression.

Xu5P serves as a key metabolic node linking non-oxPPP with glycolysis. Previous studies reported that Xu5P activated glucose-induced lipogenesis in liver.28 With a rise in glucose, Xu5P rapidly activates protein phosphatase 2A, Bδ subunit-containing C complex (PP2ABδC), stimulates fructose-2,6-bisphosphate production and promotes carbohydrate-responsive element binding protein-mediated lipogenic gene transcription.38 Our study revealed that in intestinal fibroblasts, XYLB-derived Xu5P orchestrates a metabolic shift analogous to the Warburg effect, reinforcing PPP-glycolysis crosstalk to meet biosynthetic demands.39 Mechanistically, Xu5P-mediated epigenetic reprogramming, characterised by increased H3K27ac and decreased H3K27me3, directly enhanced collagen transcription. This represents a novel regulatory axis whereby PPP regulates ECM synthesis through chromatin remodelling of collagen genes in fibrotic fibroblasts, providing fresh insights into intestinal fibrotic pathogenesis.

In addition, we also noted enhanced PPP in other cell types, including myeloid cells, endothelial cells, etc. Previous studies have indicated that these cells can modulate fibroblast collagen production through other mechanisms. For instance, CD150+ monocytes activate FAP+ fibroblasts via the transcription factor TWIST1, leading to ECM deposition and fibrosis progression.22 Endothelial cells contribute to intestinal fibrosis through the process of endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition.40 However, it remains unclear whether myeloid cells or endothelial cells are capable of producing Xu5P. Moreover, the mechanisms underlying transcellular transport of Xu5P to fibroblasts represent intriguing and unresolved questions. We believe these open topics constitute promising directions for future investigation.

CircRNAs have emerged as pivotal regulators of organ fibrosis and metabolic reprogramming. It has been reported that circBNC2 prevents renal and hepatic fibrosis by blocking epithelial cell G2-M arrest.41 CircNOLC1 promotes the oxPPP by interacting with AZGP1 and modulating miR-212–5 p/c-Met axis.25 Our discovery highlighted circPLCE1 as the first circRNA directly linking fibroblast metabolism to intestinal fibrosis, unveiling a novel antifibrotic marker. CircPLCE1 was downregulated in the stenosed intestinal segments and was negatively correlated with severity of intestinal stricture. CircPLCE1 restrained intestinal fibrosis via inhibiting activation of PPP. Mechanistically, circPLCE1 directly bound the domain-I of XYLB and competitively inhibited activity of XYLB. This interaction reduced XYLB-mediated production of Xu5P, ultimately decreasing collagen deposition. Beyond its role as a protein sponge, circPLCE1 encodes a functional peptide (circPLCE1-411).42 CircPLCE1-411 promotes the ubiquitin-dependent degradation of RPS3, suppresses NF-κB nuclear translocation in colorectal carcinoma (CRC) cells and thereby inhibits tumour proliferation and metastasis of CRC.42 These dual mechanisms position circPLCE1 as a promising therapeutic target for both CD-associated fibrosis and CRC.

The development of reliable biomarkers for treatment of intestinal fibrosis represents a critical unmet need in personalised medicine, offering potential to mitigate adverse clinical outcomes and reduce healthcare costs. Among the more than 200 genes implicated in IBD, variants of NOD2 and MMP-3 have been consistently associated with fibrostenotic CD.43 In addition, emerging evidence suggests that microbial metabolites and serological markers (eg, L-aspartic acid, primary bile acids, Collagen III, antimicrobial peptide LL-37) may serve as indicators of fibrotic progression, although their clinical utility remains controversial.44 45 Our findings revealed the compelling antifibrotic potential of circPLCE1 restoration. Emerging evidence supported non-coding RNAs as promising therapeutic targets for disease intervention.46 47 Notably, ABX464 (obefazimod), a small molecule selectively upregulating miR-124, showed efficacy in moderate-to-severe, active UC, highlighting the clinical translatability of RNA-based treatment.48 The circRNARBD-Delta vaccine protected against Delta and Omicron, making it a favourable choice against SARS-CoV-2.49 Building on these advances, our work suggested circPLCE1-based therapeutics for intestinal fibrosis, particularly when combined with optimised delivery systems. Furthermore, we identified XYLB as a critical metabolic regulator that promoted PPP, glycolytic and intestinal fibrogenesis. This discovery positioned 5-deoxy-5-fluoro-D-xylulose (5FX), a competitive XYLB inhibitor, as a potential therapeutic candidate for CD strictures.29 Although 5FX was not evaluated in our study, we illuminated this previously overlooked therapeutic avenue and called for systematic preclinical evaluation of XYLB inhibition in intestinal fibrosis models.

In our study, several limitations should be noted. First, although IBD-associated intestinal fibrosis can develop in both CD and UC, our clinical samples were exclusively derived from patients with CD. The generalisability of our findings regarding metabolic dysregulation and the circPLCE1/XYLB/Xu5P axis to UC-related fibrosis requires independent validation. Second, while we consistently observed circPLCE1 downregulation in both CDS and DSS-induced mice fibrosis models, the upstream regulators responsible for reduced circPLCE1 expression during fibrogenesis remain unidentified. Accumulating evidence suggests that cytokines and gut microbiota play significant roles in the biosynthesis and regulation of circRNAs.50 51 Given that profibrotic cytokines and gut microbiota dysregulation are also features of intestinal fibrosis of CD, it is conceivable that such dysbiosis may also initiate the downregulation of circPLCE1.52 Future studies should investigate whether and how profibrotic cytokines and microbial community composition contribute to circPLCE1 suppression. Third, our analysis of circPLCE1 and Xu5P as biomarkers of intestinal fibrosis, while promising, was conducted in a relatively small patient cohort. Larger, multicentre validation studies with longer follow-up periods are needed to establish their clinical utilities. Finally, a DSS-induced murine model predominantly reflects colonic colitis-driven injury and fibrosis and may be less representative of fibrostenotic processes in the small intestine. However, as evidenced by the literature, within the current methodological landscape of intestinal fibrosis, the well-established repetitive DSS mice model is a tractable system to investigate key aspects of intestinal fibrosis in CD.17 26 While it does not fully reproduce human small bowel stricturing, it does recapitulate inflammatory and fibrotic responses in the colon. Further studies will develop new mice models to mimic human small bowel strictures of CD.

In conclusion, our study demonstrated fibroblast-specific PPP activation in intestinal fibrosis of CD. The enhanced XYLB/Xu5P axis exacerbated intestinal fibrosis by upregulating transcription of collagen mRNAs in fibrotic fibroblasts. Mechanistically, decreased circPLCE1 rescued XYLB activity, leading to PPP activation and fibrogenesis. These findings implied that the circPLCE1/XYLB/Xu5P regulatory network may be a key driver of metabolic dysfunction in intestinal fibrosis, revealing novel therapeutic targets for CD.

Prevalence and spectrum of coronary artery anomalies detected on coronary CT angiography
Authors: Hassan Al-Balas, Abdulmajeed M. Mouhsen, Ghassab E. Ababneh, Mohammad A. Alkhalqi, Sohaib A. Alomari, Haya M. Jarrah, Albara H. Alotaibi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeAdult coronary artery congenital anomalies prevalence ranges between 1% and 5.6% in general population. Although many of these anomalies are benign, asymptomatic, and discovered incidentally, some of them can be associated with serious cardiovascular events that may lead to sudden cardiac death. Coronary CT angiography (CCTA) is noninvasive imaging modality that can reliably detect these anomalies. The aim of our study is to study the prevalence and spectrum of coronary arteries congenital anomalies in patients referred to CCTA.Patients and methodsThis study is retrospective medical record review. Between August 2015 and June 2023, 1556 patients underwent CCTA at King Abdullah University Hospital. Seven patients with deficient medical records were excluded from the study. Of the 1549 patients included in the study, 52.6% were males. Mean age of the study objects was 51 years. The electronic medical records and images findings were reviewed for the indication for the study and presence of coronary anomalies.ResultsThe prevalence of coronary artery anomalies in our study population was 2.13% (excluding the myocardial bridging) and 4.84% (including the myocardial bridging). Nineteen patients had anomalous RCA originating from the left aortic sinus with inter-arterial course (1.23%), four cases of anomalous LCX artery with retro-aortic course (0.26%). Seven patients with anomalous LCA (0.45%) have three distinct types: Four patients with LCA arising from non-coronary cusp with retro-aortic course, two patients with LCA arising from right sinus with inter-arterial course, one patient with LCA arising from right coronary sinus with retro-aortic course. One patient with LAD arising from right coronary sinus with inter-arterial course (0.06%). One patient with pulmonary origin of LCA artery (0.06%), one patient with termination of arteriovenous fistula (0.06%). Forty-two patients had myocardial bridging (2.71%).ConclusionThe prevalence and spectrum of congenital coronary artery anomalies in Jordan is similar to the world population. CCTA is reliable noninvasive imaging modality in detection of such anomalies and determine their possible serious clinical association.

Introduction

The myocardium receives its blood supply from the right and left coronary arteries, which take off from the right and left coronary sinuses of the ascending thoracic aorta, respectively. Several anatomical variations in the origin and course of the coronary arteries have been described in literature, many of them remain asymptomatic and lack clinical significance [1]. The prevalence of coronary anomalies in adult population is in the range of 1-5.6% on angiographic and autopsy studies and may reach up to 7.9% as described in recent studies with increasing use of invasive and noninvasive diagnostic techniques in evaluation of coronary arteries [2]. Myocardial bridging is the most commonly reported anomaly with prevalence of 0.15-25% by angiography and up to 86% in postmortem examinations [3]. Few of these patients experience symptoms due to compression of the coronary artery during systole, causing myocardial ischemia [4,5].

Many of coronary artery anomalies are asymptomatic and are incidentally detected during imaging evaluation for other pathologies. When symptomatic, anomalous coronary arteries can cause angina, palpitations, and syncope [6]. Anomalous coronary arteries are considered the second most frequent cause of sudden cardiac death in young athletes related to lethal arrhythmias triggered by ischemia during vigorous physical activity [7,8].

Coronary artery anomalies can be diagnosed using invasive coronary angiography, CT scan, or MRI. The diagnostic accuracy of coronary CT angiography (CCTA) in assessing coronary artery diseases has been well established [9,10]. The utilization of advanced imaging reformatted methods and 3D reconstruction enabled CCTA to accurately detect and characterize coronary artery disease [11,12].

The aim of our study is retrospective evaluation the prevalence and spectrum of coronary artery anomalies in the Jordanian population who were referred to (CCTA) at our institution, and to compare it with the data published worldwide.

Materials and methods

This retrospective chart review study was approved by the institutional ethical committee board at Jordan University of Science and Technology (IRB approval number is 20240534/Digital proposal number 462-2024). Since the study was limited to retrospective chart review, consent was waivered. All patients with available medical record who underwent CCTA between August 2015 and June 2023 were included in this study. CCTA were performed utilizing either Philips Brilliance 64 or Ingenuity 128 (Philips, Amsterdam, The Netherland) utilizing retrospective gating acquisition after administration of 50 ml of contrast followed by 20 cc normal saline. Aquired images were reconstructed in sagittal and coronal planes using MIP technique and 3D reconstruction (Fig. 1). These patients were referred to CCTA because of atypical chest pain or chest pain in a low-risk population. CCTA reports were reviewed and coronary anomalies described in these reports were collected and categorized based on anatomical classification described by Angelini et al. into anomalies of origination and course, anomalies of intrinsic coronary arterial anatomy, anomalies of coronary termination, and anomalous of anastomotic vessels [4]. Data was analysed using simple percentage calculation.

Results

Out of 1549 patients who underwent CCTA at our institution, we found 75 (4.84%) patients with coronary artery anomalies. Myocardial bridging was the most commonly encounter anomaly and was seen in 42 patients (2.71%). 31 patients (2%) have aortic anomalous origin of the coronary arteries, one patient (0.06%) with anomalous pulmonary origin of the coronary artery and one patient (0.06%) with anomalous termination.

Anomalous origin of the right coronary artery (RCA) from the left coronary sinus was the second most frequently encountered anomaly and was seen in 19 patients (1.23%). Discussion

Our study showed a prevalence of 4.84% of coronary artery anomalies in patients referred to CCTA at our institution. More than half of the described anomalies are myocardial bridging. Myocardial bridging describes a course of an epicardial coronary artery within myocardium. Many reports consider this entity as normal variant since its asymptomatic in the majority of patients with few of them have symptoms related to myocardial ischemia secondary to compression of the coronary artery during cardiac systole [13,14]. More recent studies suggested less benign course of this entity causing myocardial ischemia in the 3rd and 4th decades of life. The risk of symptomatic myocardial bridging is related to the depth and length of the involved segment. The prevalence of myocardial bridging in our study was 2.71% and similar to prevalence described by Serasapalli et al. [15]. However, Sternheim et all described a prevalence of 2-6% on conventional angiography and 19-22% on CCTA. Deeper myocardial bridging is more likely to be clinically significant and symptomatic and more likely to be detected by CCTA [16]. Excluding myocardial bridging, anomalous origin of the right coronary artery (RCA) from the left coronary sinus with inter-arterial course was the most common anomaly with prevalence of 1.23%. This anomaly was considerable higher than was described by Cheezum et al. of 0.17-0.31% [20]. This anomaly is associated with malignant course of the RCA between the ascending aorta and pulmonary trunk with slit like opening into the coronary sinus makes it more likely to be symptomatic by causing ischemia and risk of sudden cardiac death [21,22]. Surgical re-implantation of the right coronary is the recommended treatment.

Left main coronary artery (LCA) anomalies were found in seven patients (0.35%). Four of these patients, the LCA originated from the noncoronary sinus with retro-aortic course. In three patients, LCA took off the right coronary sinus and in two of them, LCA had inter-arterial course between the ascending thoracic aorta and pulmonary trunk, and one of them has retro-aortic course behind the ascending aorta. The prevalence of these anomalies is similar to previously described studies [9,15,23].

LCA branches anomalies was found in five patients. In four (0.26%) patients, the left circumflex coronary artery originated from the right coronary sinus with retro-aortic course. In one (0.06%) patient, the left ascending coronary artery took off the right coronary sinus with inter-arterial course between the ascending thoracic aorta and pulmonary trunk. Both of these anomalies are similar to previous studies [24,25].

We found one patient with anomalous origin of the LCA from the pulmonary trunk and one patient with coronary arteriovenous fistula. Both of these entities are rarely described in previous studies with similar prevalence to our study [17].Our study is the first one in our region and helps clinicians consider anomalies of the coronary arteries as potential cause of chest pain particularly in low-risk patient. Considering this diagnosis and early detection and management can potentially avoid unnecessary loss of lives especially in young population.

Our study has several limitations. The examined subjects were referred scanning were symptomatic either presenting with atypical chest pain or typical chest pain in low-risk patients and may not reflect the prevalence in general population since many of the coronary artery anomalies remain asymptomatic. Our study did not include follow up of the patients with detected anomalies and did not include clinical outcome. Anomaly detection was based on reported imaging studies rather review of images.

Conclusion

Coronary artery anomalies were commonly detected in patients underwent Coronary CT angiography (CCTA). Myocardial Bridging was the most common variant detected and Anomalous origin of the right coronary artery with an inter-arterial course was the most frequent clinically significant finding. CCTA is an effective tool for detection and characterizing these anomalies aiding in management and prevention of future complications.

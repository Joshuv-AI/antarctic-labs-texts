Pembrolizumab-Induced High Inflammatory Panmyelitis: Contribution of Neurochemical Markers Predicting Severity of Central Nervous Affection
Authors: Beate Hagenkötter, Jérôme De Sèze, Faten Hammami, Timothée Klopfenstein, Souheil Zayet
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis is the first documented case of recurrent, severe, and highly inflammatory panmyelitis induced by pembrolizumab. A 41-year-old woman with nodular melanoma developed tetraparesis after three cycles of pembrolizumab treatment. Spinal magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed extensive longitudinal panmyelitis, and cerebrospinal fluid analysis showed markedly elevated cell counts (3900/mm3). Follow-up measurements of neurofilament light chain (NfL) levels correlated with the severity of central nervous system involvement and reflected clinical, radiological, and biochemical progression. The clinical outcome was surprisingly favorable following treatment with intravenous immunoglobulins (IVIG) and corticosteroids.Keywords: Panmyelitis, Pembrolizumab, Melanoma, Neurochemical markers

Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study? Panmyelitis is a rare but serious immune-related adverse event associated with pembrolizumab treatment. Since there is limited knowledge on reliable biomarkers to predict the severity of central nervous system involvement in such cases, this report aimed to evaluate whether neurofilament light chain (NfL) levels could serve as a useful indicator. Understanding this relationship could facilitate earlier diagnosis and improve patient management. What was learned from the study? The case report demonstrated that elevated NfL levels correlate with the severity of CNS involvement in pembrolizumab-induced panmyelitis. In addition, it highlighted that timely intervention using intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) and corticosteroids was successful in managing this serious adverse event, suggesting the importance of early detection and treatment.

Introduction

Immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)-induced transverse myelitis represents a rare yet severe adverse event. In our case the development of high-grade inflammatory panmyelitis following three cycles of pembrolizumab for melanoma treatment posed a substantial clinical and therapeutic challenge. We emphasize the importance of magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and biological follow-up including the contribution of neurofilament light chain (NfL) in predicting the severity of central nervous system affection and guiding therapeutic management.

Case Report

A 41-year-old woman with a history of pollen allergy was diagnosed with ulcerated nonmetastatic nodular melanoma (Breslow 2.2 mm, Clark IV) of the left face in October 2023. Initial treatment included complete surgical excision and a skin graft in January 2024.

On 22 February 2024, the patient received her first cycle of pembrolizumab (200 mg), followed by two further cycles in March and April 2024 (400 mg every 6 weeks).

Following the first dose, she developed paresthesia in her feet, progressing to her hands, along with diffuse pain and marked muscle fatigue. By mid-May, she experienced progressive paraparesis, gait disturbance, and urinary retention.

She presented to the emergency department on 22 May 2024. Laboratory results indicated postpembrolizumab immune-related hypothyroidism, with thyroid stimulating hormone (TSH) at 55 mU/L (normal range: 0.5–4.7), thyroxine at 9.8 pmol/L (normal: 11–22), and triiodothyronine at 2.5 pmol/L (normal: 3.5–6.5). Despite thyroid hormone replacement, her condition worsened, and she was admitted to the neurology department on 30 May 2024.

Neurological examination revealed an alert and oriented patient with tetraparesis,: muscle strength of 3/5 in the proximal right upper limb, 0/5 in the right hand, 4/5 in the left upper limb, and 0/5 in both lower limbs. She had bilateral hand paresthesia, a sensory level at T8 with predominant right-sided hypoaesthesia, and left-sided hypoalgesia and thermo-hypoesthesia. Deep tendon reflexes were moderately present in the upper limbs but absent in the lower limbs. Vibration sense was impaired in the lower limbs. No Babinski sign was observed. Anal sphincter tone was preserved, though the anal reflex was absent. She required urinary catheterization.

Spinal MRI demonstrated longitudinally extensive panmyelitis with spinal cord edema and T2/short tau inversion recovery (STIR) hyperintensity (Fig. 1A), with slight T1 hypointensity and heterogeneous contrast enhancement.

Spine magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) showed extensive longitudinal panmyelitis with an edematous spinal cord and hypersignal on short-TI inversion recovery (STIR) sequences (image A 29/5/24). Spine follow-up MRI showed a regressive spinal cord T2 hypersignal (image B 11/6/24). A follow-up MRI showed an increase in transverse involvement at C4 with T1 contrast enhancement (image C 21/6/24), which was confirmed on a second MRI showing worsening of myelitis on STIR sequences (image D 10/7/24). MRI after administration of intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) showed a decrease in the extent of myelitis on follow-up MRI T2 sequences (image E 19/7/24). Last MRI follow-up after consolidation treatment showed only the persistence of a discreet intramedullary T2 hypersignal at the cervico-dorsal level without contrast enhancement (image F 5/12/24)

Brain and thoracoabdominal computerized tomography (CT) scans were unremarkable. The initial lumbar puncture (LP) revealed clear cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) with an elevated white blood cell (WBC) count of 3700/mm3 (normal < 5/mm3), protein concentration of 1.7 g/L (normal < 0.4), and a glucose level at 4.6 mmol/L (normal: 2.22–3.89 mmol/L) (see Table 1).

Multiplex polymerase-chain-reaction (PCR) testing and Borrelia spp. index were negative. Cytopathology showed no evidence of lymphoma or leptomeningeal carcinomatosis. Empiric intravenous (IV) acyclovir and third-generation cephalosporins were discontinued following negative herpes simplex virus 1 and 2 PCR results and negative Borrelia spp. testing.

Additional laboratory analyses, including interleukin (IL)-6 and IL-10 levels and lymphocyte phenotyping, did not support a lymphoma diagnosis, which was definitively excluded by the hematologist.

Antineuronal antibodies in both CSF and serum were negative. Anti-thyroid peroxidase (TPO) antibodies were elevated (189 IU/mL; normal < 60), but TSH receptor antibodies were normal. Anti-aquaporin-4, anti-myelin oligodendrocyte glycoprotein (MOG), and other anti-neuropil antibodies (e.g., anti-NMDAR, LGI1) in the CSF were negative. Serologies for human T-cell lymphotropic virus (HTLV), tick-borne encephalitis, and brucellosis were also negative.

Given the presumed diagnosis of pembrolizumab-induced panmyelitis, intravenous corticosteroid therapy was initiated with 2 g of methylprednisolone, followed by 1 g/day for 4 days, and continued with oral steroids.

By June 2024, the patient showed clinical improvement, particularly in muscle strength. Follow-up spinal MRI showed regression of the T2 hyperintensity (Fig. 1B), though subsequent lumbar punctures (LPs 2–4) still showed persistent inflammatory changes. CSF neurofilament light chain (NfL) levels were significantly elevated at 2367 ng/L and 14310 ng/L (normal < 750 ng/L).

On 21 June 2024, LP and MRI findings suggested worsening myelitis (Fig. 1C, D). Based on recommendations from the Toxicité des Immunothérapies GRand Est (TIGRE) immunotoxicity reference center, IVIG therapy at 0.4 g/kg/day for 5 days was initiated in combination with oral corticosteroids.

LP 5 (postIVIG) showed a decrease in WBC count to 213/mm3 and protein to 1.0 g/L (see Table 1). Follow-up MRI demonstrated a reduction in spinal cord inflammation on T2-weighted images (Fig. 1E). The patient’s clinical condition improved: she could walk short distances unaided and use stairs with assistance.

On 9 August 2024, the patient was discharged on oral steroids. MRI showed partial regression of spinal contrast enhancement. In October 2024, consolidation therapy with three additional IVIG cycles at 6-week intervals was administered, allowing tapering of corticosteroids. This resulted in favorable CSF and MRI outcomes (Fig. 1F). Clinically, the patient showed full motor recovery (5/5), persistent paresthesia, and stable gait using walking sticks for long distances. No melanoma recurrence or metastasis was detected. The patient was in complete remission. Oral corticosteroids were discontinued in June 2025, and the patient remained clinically stable.

Discussion

Pembrolizumab has demonstrated promising therapeutic efficacy in malignant melanoma and small cell lung cancer, significantly improving overall survival and progression-free survival in affected patients [1, 2].

Immune-related adverse events (irAEs) are common among individuals receiving immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), with up to 70% experiencing irAEs when treated with anti-programmed cell death 1 (PD-1) or its ligand (PD-L1) antibodies [3]. Fatigue, rash, pruritus, arthralgia, amylase elevation, and diarrhea are the most common adverse effects [2]. Treatment interruption and administration of corticosteroids are typically effective in managing these generally immune-related adverse events (AEs) [2].

As an anti-PD-1 antibody, pembrolizumab may cause neurological immune-related toxicities, with an incidence ranging from 3.8% to 12% [4].

Pepys et al. [5] reported at least one irAE in 73.5% of patients with melanoma, with 8% experiencing neurological complications. Of the entire cohort, 28% had received pembrolizumab. The most frequent neurologic irAEs included myositis (44,7%), encephalitis (10.5%), and neuropathy (10.5%). Panmyelitis was not observed among the 937 patients studied, indicating its rarity. The median onset of neurological irAEs was 48 days, with 40% requiring hospitalization. In our case, symptoms developed within 30 days of treatment initiation. Corticosteroid treatment was initiated in 46% of cases, with a median of 4 days from diagnosis. Nine patients required second-line immunosuppression with methotrexate, IVIG, plasma exchange, or both.

Chatterton et al. [6] reported four cases of immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)-induced transverse myelitis: two patients developed symptoms following treatment with nivolumab, one after combination therapy with ipilimumab and nivolumab, and one following three cycles of pembrolizumab (200 mg every 3 weeks). Two patients (including the one treated with pembrolizumab) exhibited longitudinally extensive T2-hyperintensities on MRI, but nonelevated cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) cell counts of 4/mm3 and 5/mm3, respectively. One patient treated with nivolumab showed multiple patchy T2-hyperintensities on MRI and had a markedly elevated CSF white blood cell count of 242/mm3. However, this patient with transverse myelitis following nivolumab therapy did not exhibit an inflammatory pattern comparable to our case. In comparison, our patient demonstrated a profoundly elevated CSF white blood cell count of 3900/mm3, indicating a severe inflammatory response. This finding was particularly challenging, as differential diagnoses such as infectious etiologies, cerebral lymphoma, and leptomeningeal carcinomatosis had to be considered and were subsequently excluded.

We also monitored CSF neurofilament light chain levels as a biomarker of central and peripheral nervous system damage—an aspect not yet explored in pembrolizumab-induced panmyelitis. Carta et al. [7] demonstrated that NfL levels correlate with disease severity in neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder. In our case, NfL levels correlated with both clinical status and MRI findings, supporting its potential role in monitoring inflammatory activity in autoimmune neurological diseases.

Despite the severe clinical, radiological, and biochemical presentation, the patient showed progressively favorable improvement under combined treatment with intravenous immunoglobulins and corticosteroids. No melanoma recurrence or metastasis was detected, and the patient remained in complete remission.

Conclusions

This is the first reported case of severe, relapsing, high-inflammatory pembrolizumab-induced panmyelitis, presenting significant diagnostic and therapeutic challenges. We underscore the value of comprehensive clinical, radiological, and biochemical monitoring, particularly the utility of CSF neurofilament light chain as a potential predictor of central nervous system disease severity.

ABSTRACT
PURPOSEMetastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) is a heterogeneous disease state with multiple treatment options. Biomarkers are needed for risk stratification and to guide treatment intensification or deintensification. We used a computational histology artificial intelligence–based platform (CHAI) to develop and validate a digital image–only prognostic biomarker in mHSPC with participant-level data from two prospective, phase III randomized controlled trials.METHODSThe CHAI platform extracted quantitative histomorphologic features from whole-slide images of hematoxylin and eosin–stained diagnostic specimens. Data from CHAARTED were used to construct a signature of features associated with overall survival (OS). A continuous risk score was dichotomized into favorable- and unfavorable-risk groups. The performance of the locked model was assessed in ENZAMET as an independent validation cohort using Kaplan-Meier methods and multivariable Cox proportional hazards models. Institutional review board approval was obtained for each participating data set. Given all patient information was deidentified, the study was considered institutional review board–exempt and consent waived.RESULTSOverall, 1,191 participants were included: 507 in development (CHAARTED) and 684 in validation (ENZAMET). In the validation cohort, CHAI unfavorable-risk participants had worse OS (hazard ratio [HR], 2.6 [95% CI, 2.0 to 3.4]; P < .001) and progression-free survival (PFS; HR, 2.2 [95% CI, 1.7 to 2.7]; P < .001). Five-year OS was 39% for unfavorable-risk versus 69% for favorable-risk participants. In adjusting for clinical variables, the biomarker remained prognostic for OS and PFS (P < .001). Among those who received doublet (androgen-deprivation therapy [ADT] plus androgen receptor pathway inhibitor [ARPI]) versus triplet systemic therapy (ADT plus docetaxel plus ARPI), there was a significant interaction between biomarker and treatment intensification for OS (P = .003) and PFS (P < .001), suggesting that patients at higher risk may benefit more from triplet therapy.CONCLUSIONWe developed and validated an image-only AI-based biomarker associated with clinical outcomes and potential benefit from treatment escalation in mHSPC independent of conventional clinicopathologic risk factors.

INTRODUCTION

Prostate cancer remains the most common male cancer, and metastatic disease is the second most common cause of male cancer mortality.1 Metastatic prostate cancer (mPC) is a heterogeneous disease state with a wide range of mortality rates.2-4 Despite heterogeneity in mPC outcomes, successive trials have shown improvement in expected survival owing to the introduction of novel agents and combining agents, such as androgen receptor pathway inhibitors (ARPI) and chemotherapeutics (eg, docetaxel), with the long-standing backbone of androgen-deprivation therapy (ADT).5 In trials of these combination approaches, treatment intensification was associated with improved survival outcomes, although this has the potential to come at the expense of worse toxicity for individual patients.

We developed and validated an image-only AI-based biomarker associated with clinical outcomes and potential benefit from treatment escalation in mHSPC independent of conventional clinicopathologic risk factors.

In practice, systemic treatment intensification in metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) from singlet (eg, ADT) to doublet (eg, ADT plus ARPI) to triplet (eg, ADT plus docetaxel and ARPI) is based on clinicopathologic features such as age, tumor histology, prostate-specific antigen (PSA) values, radiologic findings (eg, volume and timing of metastatic disease), comorbidities, and patient performance status.6,7 These factors have limited precision to predict clinical outcomes, and cannot identify on a patient level who may benefit from upfront triplet versus doublet therapy in mHSPC; this question is being evaluated in the ASPIRE randomized controlled trial (RCT).8 Thus, there is a need for more accurate and validated biomarkers that can better identify patients who are most appropriate for treatment intensification despite potential increased treatment toxicity.

Advanced assays capable of establishing prognosis in mHSPC are needed to fill this important role in risk stratification, patient counseling, and even treatment selection. Genomic, transcriptomic, and circulating biomarkers have the potential to reflect tumor biology more accurately than conventional clinicopathologic factors with more precise prognostication and prediction of treatment response.9-11 Artificial intelligence (AI), specifically deep learning techniques, have been used to analyze histopathologic slides and, in combination with clinical parameters, demonstrate associations with clinical outcomes in localized PC.12

We hypothesized that hematoxylin and eosin (H&E)–stained digitized whole-slide images (WSI) of diagnostic pathology slides contained sufficiently interpretable information associated with survival outcomes in mHSPC. This post hoc secondary analysis leveraged clinical data and corresponding WSI from participants enrolled in multiple phase III RCTs for the development and validation of a mPC-specific digital image–only biomarker using the foundational computational histology artificial intelligence (CHAI) platform.13-15

METHODS

Study Cohorts

We assembled a cohort of participants with mHSPC from two phase III RCTs: CHAARTED (E3805, ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT00309985) and ENZAMET (ANZUP 1304, ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT02446405); the design and study protocols have been previously described (Fig 1).16,17 In brief, CHAARTED enrolled participants with mHSPC and randomly assigned to ADT alone or in combination with docetaxel. In ENZAMET, participants with mHSPC were randomly assigned to receive testosterone suppression plus enzalutamide or standard-of-care nonsteroidal antiandrogen. ENZAMET participants were permitted to receive docetaxel based on physician and participant choice.

Allocation among (A) development and (B) validation cohorts. Five hundred seven participants meeting inclusion criteria from the CHAARTED trial were identified and allocated to the development cohort. Six hundred eighty-four participants meeting inclusion criteria from the ENZAMET trial were identified and allocated to the external, validation cohort. QC, quality control.

The AI-based prognostic biomarker was developed with data from participants in the CHAARTED trial. Validation of the locked biomarker was then conducted on the independent ENZAMET cohort. No data from ENZAMET were used in the development of the biomarker. For inclusion in this study, participants from either trial were required to have (1) available digitized WSI of H&E-stained diagnostic specimens and (2) available associated clinical survival data. Cases with digitized WSI were excluded if they did not pass slide quality control (defined by absence of preparation artifacts, scanning artifacts, or adequate tumor for analysis).

Study End Points

The primary end point was overall survival (OS), defined as the time from random assignment to death from any cause or the date at which the patient was last known to be alive with censoring at the 5-year time point. The secondary end point was progression-free survival (PFS), which was measured as the interval between time from random assignment to the progression event and encompassed clinical progression, time to castration-resistant disease, and PSA progression, again with censoring at the 5-year time point. In the development cohort of CHAARTED, disease progression was reported as the time to clinical progression and serologic progression with a testosterone level of <50 ng/dL, or source documentation of medical or surgical castration, both of which were indexed from time to random assignment. In the validation cohort of ENZAMET, progression constituted either clinical progression or PSA progression defined per (1) Prostate Cancer Working Group 2 criteria (increase in PSA from nadir value by ≥25% and by ≥2 ng/mL), (2) clinical progression, (3) death from any case, or (4) last known date of follow-up without PSA progression. As a sensitivity analysis to evaluate the performance of the prognostic biomarker, performance was also assessed with an alternative definition of OS and PFS as the time from tissue sample collection to survival event in the case of OS and progression event in the case of PFS.

Development and Validation of a Pathology-Based Prognostic Biomarker

We applied the previously described CHAI platform to develop a pathology-based prognostic biomarker. The platform is built on tissue and cell segmentation models previously trained on approximately 25,000 pan-cancer H&E-stained slides including 500,000 nuclei and 100 million μm2 of tissue annotated by board-certified pathologists. Models were trained to accurately segment various cell and tissue types relevant to cancer biology and to identify over 30,000 histomorphologic features related to phenotypic attributes related to hallmarks of cancer, including nuclei size and morphology, cell spatial orientation, host immune cell infiltration, and stromal density.14 Digitized WSI derived from H&E-stained diagnostic pathology specimens were processed through deep learning models to segment tissue and cell regions.

Using the development cohort from CHAARTED, predictive feature selection and weighting was performed to construct a histology signature associated with OS using univariable Cox proportional hazards (CPH) analysis. The signature was further refined using multivariable CPH models and chunk tests. No clinical variables were used as model inputs; only histologic features were used. The resulting output was a continuous score, which was dichotomized into favorable and unfavorable using a 90%-10% cutoff, respectively. This cutoff was determined in a methodology analogous to prior work describing optimal cutpoint identification for survival models, after which the algorithm and threshold were locked with no retuning of cutpoint using ENZAMET validation data.18 The 90%-10% cutpoint selected in development reflects the intended clinical use of the biomarker: the identification of the prognostically unfavorable subset of patients for whom the decision to escalate therapy is clinically most consequential. The final biomarker algorithm and risk threshold were then locked before any analysis of the independent validation cohort.

The locked threshold and biomarker were then applied to the independent validation cohort from ENZAMET, and the associations with OS and PFS were evaluated.

Statistical Analysis

The clinical characteristics between the development and independent validation cohorts were described. For continuous clinical characteristics, descriptive statistics (medians and IQRs) and P values, derived from Wilcoxon rank-sum tests of differences between distributions, were provided. For categorical clinical characteristics, descriptive statistics (counts and proportions) and P values, derived from Fisher's exact tests, were calculated.

The performance of the prognostic biomarker and its association with OS and PFS was evaluated using Kaplan-Meier methods and log-rank tests. Multivariable CPH models were used to adjust for conventional clinicopathologic risk factors, including treatment received, age, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status, Gleason category, volume of disease (high v low), timing of metastatic disease (metachronous v synchronous), and baseline PSA levels. In the multivariable Cox model, baseline PSA was modeled per log2(PSA + 1) as a sensitivity specification to accommodate skew. Complete case analysis was primarily reported. In a sensitivity analysis, missingness was also addressed with multiple imputation; 20 imputations were used based upon the proportion of patients with at least one missing entry. Z-statistics based on pooled Greenwood variance estimates derived from Kaplan-Meier curves were used to test the hypothesis that 5-year OS and PFS rates are lower for the unfavorable group compared with the favorable group.

For calibration and discrimination, external validation of the locked CHAI prognostic biomarker was performed in the ENZAMET cohort using the model coefficients trained on the development cohort. Complete details are provided in Supplemental Methods (Data Supplement, Tables S1-S4, Fig S1). Decision curve analyses for 60-month OS compared five strategies: treat-all, treat-none, a conventional clinicopathology (CP) rule (high-volume or synchronous disease classified as treat), the CHAI biomarker, and the CHAI biomarker combined with the CP comparator.

Although docetaxel administration was not randomized within the validation cohort ENZAMET, to interrogate the performance of the prognostic biomarker in predicting benefit to treatment escalation, an exploratory analysis evaluated the interaction between the biomarker and treatment intensification (doublet: ADT + ARPI [enzalutamide]; triplet: ADT + docetaxel + ARPI [enzalutamide]) for both OS and PFS. Given the nonrandomization of treatment allocation, for this post hoc secondary analysis of heterogeneity of treatment effect, propensity scores were calculated from the conventional clinicopathologic risk factors that were found to be associated with treatment selection. Age, ECOG performance status, Gleason score, volume of disease (high v low), timing of metastatic disease (metachronous v synchronous), and baseline PSA levels were tested. Age, ECOG performance status, volume of disease, and timing of disease were associated with doublet versus triplet therapy selection. CPH modeling and Kaplan-Meier methods adjusted for stabilized inverse probability of treatment weighting were then used for testing treatment effects with biomarker-treatment interactions. For this analysis, again complete case analysis is primarily reported to address missing data, and a sensitivity analysis using multiple imputation with 20 imputations was also conducted.

A prespecified P < .05 was considered significant. All statistical analyses were performed with R version 4.4.2 (R Foundation, Vienna, Austria). Study design and execution aimed to meet standards for studies evaluating artificial intelligence models in oncology and adhere to the TRIPOD-AI statement guidelines (Data Supplement, Table S5, Supplementary File).19,20 Efforts to address the ESMO basic requirements for AI-based biomarkers in oncology for the biomarker reported herein (Class C1) included reporting multiple performance metrics (multivariable CPH model of OS, tdAUC, and decision curve analysis), justification of sample size, references to prior CHAI model development and application, robustness to sensitivity analyses, use of biomarker in single context, and validation in an independent cohort.21

RESULTS

Patient Characteristics

A total of 1,191 participants with mHSPC were included in this study, constituting all participants with available digitized WSI and correlative clinical data (Fig 1). The development cohort consisted of 507 participants from the CHAARTED trial, while the independent validation cohort comprised 684 participants from the ENZAMET trial (Table 1). The development cohort was younger (P < .01). Clinical characteristics and outcomes of participants in the validation cohort were similar to those in the overall ENZAMET trial aside from more low-volume disease and higher Gleason scores (Data Supplement, Table S6).

Prognostic Performance

In the independent validation cohort from ENZAMET, the prognostic biomarker stratified participants into two distinct risk groups: favorable (N = 559, 82%) and unfavorable (N = 125; 18%; Table 2). Participants with unfavorable risk had significantly worse OS than those with favorable risk (hazard ratio [HR], 2.6 [95% CI, 2.0 to 3.4]; P < .001; Fig 2A). The 5-year OS rates were 39% for participants with unfavorable risk versus 69% for participants with favorable risk (P < .001).

The locked CHAI biomarker model was applied to the validation cohort to assess calibration and discrimination. Calibration slope was 0.89 (95% CI, 0.62 to 1.15) for OS. Absolute event rates were lower in ENZAMET than the locked model anticipated, reflecting the era-defining introduction of ARPI in the ENZAMET treatment arms (observed-to-expected event ratios 0.58-0.66 across 1-, 3-, and 5-year horizons for OS). Recalibration-in-the-large to the ARPI-era baseline cumulative hazard restored absolute calibration (O/E 0.92-0.98 for OS and 0.95-0.98 for PFS) without disturbing the locked discrimination (Data Supplement, Fig S1). Full calibration results are provided in the Data Supplement.

Time-dependent AUC was estimated in the validation cohort at 1- and 3-year horizons for a (1) clinicopathologic (CP) model, (2) CHAI biomarker, and (3) CP and CHAI combined model. The combined model outperformed either component alone at every horizon (OS 1-year AUC 0.80, 3-year 0.69; PFS 1-year 0.73, 3-year 0.66, Data Supplement, Table S4).

On decision curve analyses for 60-month OS, adding the CHAI score to the CP comparator yielded the largest net benefit, with peak incremental net benefit of +0.062 at pt = 0.49 (Data Supplement, Fig S2), indicating an incremental clinical utility of the biomarker beyond conventional clinicopathologic risk factors.

In a sensitivity analysis with a definition of the index date for OS and PFS at the time of sample collection (as opposed to the time of random assignment), the prognostic biomarker remained an independent and significant predictor of both OS (HR, 2.61 [95% CI, 1.84 to 3.70]; P < .001) and PFS (HR, 2.31 [95% CI, 1.72 to 3.10]; P < .001; Data Supplement, Fig S3 and Table S8).

Treatment Intensification

In the exploratory analysis of biomarker interaction with treatment intensification using multivariable CPH regression adjusting for propensity scores, triplet- and doublet-treated patients had similar OS and PFS (Fig 3A: OS HR, 1.11 [95% CI, 0.7 to 1.7]; P = .67; Fig 3B: PFS HR, 1.12 [95% CI, 0.7 to 1.7]; P = .59). In patients with favorable risk, participants treated with triplet therapy had similar OS (Fig 3C: HR, 1.51 [95% CI, 0.9 to 2.6]; P = .14) and PFS (Fig 3E: HR, 1.53 [95% CI, 0.9 to 2.5]; P = .088). In participants with unfavorable risk, those treated with triplet therapy had superior OS (Fig 3D: HR, 0.45 [95% CI, 0.2 to 1.0]; P = .047) and superior PFS (Fig 3F: HR, 0.42 [95% CI, 0.2 to 0.8]; P = .012). The interaction between the biomarker and treatment intensification was significant for OS (P = .003) and PFS (P < .001), suggesting the biomarker predicted benefit from treatment intensification with triplet over doublet therapy (Fig 3G). Similar results were seen for the interaction test for biomarker and treatment intensification when using multiple imputation to account for missing data for both OS (P = .04) and PFS (P = .01). Participants receiving triplet therapy had greater frequencies of high-volume disease, synchronous metastasis, and higher Gleason scores (Data Supplement, Table S9); associations between favorable versus unfavorable biomarker status and clinical characteristics in this subpopulation of doublet therapy– and triplet therapy–treated participants in ENZAMET are shown in Supplementary Table 10. When stratifying by treatment, superior OS and PFS was noted among participants with favorable risk receiving ADT and enzalutamide, but differences in OS and PFS were attenuated among participants receiving ADT, enzalutamide, and docetaxel (Data Supplement, Fig S4).

DISCUSSION

In this study, we developed and validated an AI-powered histopathology biomarker to stratify risk in mHSPC using data from two independent prospective phase III RCTs. Based on development from the CHAARTED study and subsequent independent validation in the ENZAMET study, the CHAI mHSPC risk biomarker identified a population of participants with worse OS and PFS, independent of conventional clinicopathologic risk factors. In addition, in an exploratory analysis, the biomarker predicted participants who benefited from treatment intensification. To our knowledge, this is the first AI-powered histopathology-only biomarker of its kind in the ARPI-treated mPC setting.

The clinical implications of these findings are several-fold. First, this biomarker can successfully risk-stratify patients with mHSPC independently of conventional clinicopathologic factors: the 5-year OS rates were 39% for unfavorable-risk versus 69% for those with favorable-risk disease. There is increasing recognition in mHSPC that some patients have more indolent disease, and a proportion of these patients may be able to de-escalate their treatment, especially when experiencing significant side effects.3 Based on insights from the CHAARTED, LATITUDE, and STAMPEDE trials, among others, patients with a better-prognosis mHSPC disease profile have been defined more classically through clinicopathologic factors such as those with low-volume, metachronous metastatic disease.5-7 This CHAI mHSPC risk biomarker, using histology alone as an input, allows for a more refined personalized therapeutic approach to integrate into shared decisions to balance efficacy and toxicity among these patients with indolent disease. A histology-alone input is particularly relevant in an era of imaging advances (eg, PSMA PET) where the extent of metastatic disease is being redefined, challenging traditional means of risk assessment by conventional imaging.22

Second, in this post hoc secondary analysis of heterogeneity of treatment effect, the biomarker was found to predict which patients benefited from treatment escalation for both OS and PFS end points. When specifically looking at those who received triplet therapy (ADT + docetaxel + enzalutamide) versus doublet therapy (ADT + enzalutamide), CHAI unfavorable-risk disease showed a significant PFS and OS benefit with triplet systemic therapy over doublet therapy of ADT + enzalutamide. Although the addition of docetaxel in the ENZAMET validation cohort was not randomized, these results suggest this CHAI biomarker could be used to identify the patients at highest risk, who stand to benefit the most from treatment escalation to a triplet therapy regimen. Currently, it is otherwise unknown which patients with mHSPC would benefit from the addition of docetaxel to an ARPI + ADT in the form of triplet therapy; this is the focus of several phase III randomized trials such as the ongoing ASPIRE (A032302; ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT06931340) and TRIPLE-SWITCH (CCTG-PR26; ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT06592924) trials.

This CHAI mHSPC risk biomarker works toward precision medicine in the management of mPC; prior advanced biomarker efforts have leveraged genomic profiling, radiomic analysis, and multimodal artificial intelligence (MMAI) methods. For example, a 22-gene signature developed in localized prostate cancer using RNA whole-transcriptome analysis was recently found to be associated with treatment benefit from the addition of docetaxel to ADT in participants with mHSPC from the STAMPEDE trial.23 In addition, an MMAI model developed in localized prostate cancer incorporating digital pathology, tumor stage, PSA, and age was found to also be associated with outcomes in more advanced disease.24 However, to our knowledge, this is the first AI-powered histopathology-only biomarker associated with prognosis and treatment escalation benefit in APRI-treated mPC. There are tradeoffs to requiring clinical variables, which have the potential to improve model performance, but may degrade the ease and practicality of clinical implementation. This histology-only tool does not require clinical variables and clinician time to input them, nor tissue consumption to provide clinical decision support, and is thus less subject to potential error that can be introduced through variable collection and interpretation of clinical factors. In this study, quality control only led to exclusion of 69 of 1,125 patients in the validation cohort.

Limitations of this study include the lack of digitized H&E specimens matching study inclusion criteria for the entire randomized population in the CHAARTED and ENZAMET studies; however, the included subset of participants appeared representative of the broader trial populations in statistical testing. Use of ARPIs in the validation cohort (ENZAMET) but not in the development (CHAARTED) cohort induced a validation-cohort calibration discrepancy evidenced by a shift in absolute event rates attributable to the introduction of ARPIs, while rank discrimination was preserved. Black patients are more likely to die from prostate cancer but were underrepresented in the ENZAMET trial, as it was a global study with majority of accrual in Australia, New Zealand, Canada, United Kingdom, and Ireland, and therefore in the validation cohort for this study, representing a limitation in generalizability to diverse populations.25 In addition, since docetaxel administration was not randomized within the validation cohort, future study of the biomarker-treatment interaction in randomized cohorts of doublet and triplet therapy is warranted; this study is a post hoc secondary analysis of heterogeneity of treatment effect.

In conclusion, using multiple independent prospective phase III RCTs, we developed and validated an AI-driven histology signature associated with outcomes in mPC. This biomarker was developed specifically in the metastatic setting, incorporated histology alone, remained independently prognostic beyond conventional clinicopathologic risk factors, and was associated with benefit from treatment intensification for high-risk disease. This tool has the potential to advance prognostication in mPC.

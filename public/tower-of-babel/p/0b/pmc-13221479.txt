Mavacamten in Chinese Patients with Obstructive Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy: Patient-Reported Health Status Analysis up to 78 Weeks in the EXPLORER-CN Study
Authors: Zhuang Tian, Xiaoyan Li, Liwen Li, Qing Zhang, Jian’an Wang, Yunqi Shi, Daoquan Peng, Wei Ma, Ping Yang, Xiang Cheng, Wei Jin, Fang Wang, Arteid Memaj, Yimeng Xie, Beth Pan, Victoria Florea, Jing Zheng, Yue Zhong, Shuyang Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionImprovement of symptoms, physical function, and quality of life is one treatment goal for patients with obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM). Mavacamten improved left-ventricular outflow tract gradients in Chinese patients with obstructive HCM in the EXPLORER-CN trial. We report here a detailed analysis of patient-reported health status per the 23-item Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ-23) in Chinese patients after 78 weeks of mavacamten.MethodsPatients who completed the double-blind, placebo-controlled (DBPC) period of EXPLORER-CN with no active safety concerns could enter a long-term extension (LTE) period to receive mavacamten for 48 weeks at either the 30-week dose (mavacamten-mavacamten group) or a once-daily starting dose of 2.5 mg (subsequently adjusted via pharmacodynamics-based dose titration: placebo-mavacamten group). Health status endpoints included KCCQ-23 clinical summary score (CSS), overall symptom score (OSS), total symptom score (TSS), physical limitations score, and quality-of-life scores through week 78. Week 78 endpoint analyses were descriptive.ResultsIn the DBPC period, 54 patients received mavacamten and 27 received placebo; of these, 54 and 25, respectively, entered the LTE period. KCCQ-23 CSS, OSS, and TSS improved with mavacamten and worsened with placebo during the DBPC period (mean change baseline to week 30: 5.7, 6.4, and 8.1, respectively [mavacamten-mavacamten group] and – 5.4, – 4.3, and – 4.8, respectively [placebo-mavacamten group]). CSS, OSS, and TSS continued to improve in the mavacamten-mavacamten group during the LTE period (mean change baseline to week 78: 7.1, 8.2, and 10.0, respectively). Scores improved in the placebo-mavacamten group after switching to mavacamten (mean change week 30 to week 78: 7.3, 9.5, and 5.7, respectively). Similar improvements in KCCQ-23 physical limitations and quality-of-life scores with mavacamten were observed throughout both study periods.ConclusionsLong-term mavacamten treatment for up to 78 weeks led to sustained improvements in patient-reported health status, supporting long-term treatment with mavacamten for Chinese patients with symptomatic obstructive HCM.Trial RegistrationClinicalTrials.gov identifier NCT05174416.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40119-026-00449-5.Keywords: China, Health status, KCCQ, Mavacamten, Prolonged exposure, Symptomatic obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy

Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study?• Symptomatic obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) is associated with significant symptom burden and impaired quality of life• Current treatments such as beta or calcium-channel blockers, and septal reduction procedures frequently yield incomplete symptom control, or may be limited by tolerability or associated procedural risks, highlighting the continued need for targeted therapeutic options• Mavacamten is a first-in-class cardiac myosin inhibitor that has demonstrated improvements versus placebo in both clinical symptoms and patient health status (as reflected by 23-item Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire [KCCQ-23] component scores) after 30 weeks of treatment in patients in the double-blind, placebo-controlled (DBPC) period of the EXPLORER-CN study; we evaluated the effects of mavacamten on patient-reported health status in Chinese patients from EXPLORER-CN, including after prolonged exposureWhat was learned in this study?• Prolonged exposure to mavacamten (78 weeks) in Chinese patients who entered the long-term extension (LTE) period of EXPLORER-CN led to further and sustained improvements in KCCQ-23 component scores; patients who initially received placebo during the DBPC period also experienced patient-reported health status improvements after subsequent exposure (48 weeks) to mavacamten in the LTE period• Mavacamten has the potential to address not only the underlying pathophysiology of obstructive HCM, but also overall patient health status; the findings support long-term treatment with mavacamten for patients with symptomatic obstructive HCM

Introduction

Symptomatic obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM), a major hallmark of which is left-ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) obstruction, is associated with significant disease burden and poor quality of life (QoL), particularly among those of working age (<60 years) [1]. Symptoms that limit activities occur in around 40% of patients, most commonly dyspnea, palpitations, fatigue, and chest pain [1, 2]. Patients experience reduced health status compared with the general population, with progressively lower scores on health-status metrics such as the 23-item Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ-23) [1, 3]. Effects on the patient are wide-ranging, and include limitations in physical activity (including daily activities, walking short distances, climbing stairs, ability to carry out exercise), anxiety and depression, ability to work and socialize, and the need for lifestyle changes [2, 4, 5]. Commonly used pharmacological treatments (e.g., beta blockers, calcium-channel blockers, disopyramide) are nonspecific and address the symptoms rather than the underlying pathophysiology [6–8].

A key underlying mechanism of LVOT obstruction is left-ventricular hypercontractility due to excessive cross-bridging between cardiac myosin and actin [9]. Mavacamten is a first-in-class cardiac myosin inhibitor that has been approved across five continents for the treatment of adults with symptomatic (New York Heart Association [NYHA] functional class II–III) obstructive HCM [10–12]. Mavacamten targets the underlying pathology of obstructive HCM by selectively inhibiting cardiac myosin and reducing the excessive actin–myosin cross-bridging to mitigate the disease-related increased myocardial contractility and ventricular stiffness [10, 13, 14]. Mavacamten has demonstrated significant efficacy versus placebo in several phase 3, multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled (DBPC) clinical trials in patients with obstructive HCM, including global (EXPLORER-HCM; NCT03470545 [15]), United States multicenter (VALOR-HCM; NCT04349072 [16]), and Chinese populations (EXPLORER-CN; NCT05174416 [10]), and in a phase 3 open-label study in Asian patients from Japan (HORIZON-HCM; NCT05414175 [17]). In these studies, mavacamten demonstrated benefit versus placebo in terms of cardiac function (decreased LVOT gradient, higher proportion of patients with ≥ 1 NYHA functional class improvement, increased peak oxygen uptake) [10, 15–17] and structure (reduced left-ventricular mass index, left-ventricular mass, maximum and minimum left-atrial volume index, and maximal left-ventricular wall thickness) [10, 17], and reduced need for septal reduction therapy to mitigate LVOT obstruction [16]. Significant improvements versus placebo in health status as reflected by changes in KCCQ-23 component scores (e.g., clinical summary score [CSS], overall symptom score [OSS], or total symptom score [TSS]) were also observed after 30–56 weeks of treatment [10, 17–19]. The long-term impact of mavacamten on patient-reported health status in Chinese patients with obstructive HCM has not yet been established.

The objectives of this analysis from the EXPLORER-CN trial of mavacamten in Chinese patients with obstructive HCM were twofold: (1) to assess the treatment effect of mavacamten versus placebo on patient-reported health status (symptoms, physical and social function, and QoL) after 30 weeks of treatment, and (2) to explore the effects of prolonged mavacamten exposure (up to 78 weeks) on patient-reported health status.

Methods

Study Design and Participants

The phase 3 EXPLORER-CN trial was conducted at 12 sites in China; the study design has been reported elsewhere, along with a full account of the study protocol (see also Fig. 1) [20]. In brief, the study population included adult patients (age ≥ 18 years, body weight > 45 kg) with symptomatic obstructive HCM diagnosed per American College of Cardiology Foundation/American Heart Association, European Society of Cardiology, and Chinese Society of Cardiology guidelines, a peak LVOT gradient of ≥ 50 mmHg at rest or after the Valsalva maneuver, left-ventricular ejection fraction ≥ 55%, and NYHA class II or III. Eligible individuals were randomized 2:1 via an interactive response system to receive mavacamten (dosed from 2.5 mg, with subsequent adjustment as needed via pharmacodynamics-based dose titration) or placebo once daily for 30 weeks (DBPC period). Participants who completed the 30-week DBPC period with no active safety concerns were eligible to enter the long-term extension (LTE) period to receive mavacamten for 48 weeks (Fig. 1). Patients previously on mavacamten continued with the dose received at week 30 (mavacamten-mavacamten group), while those previously on placebo received mavacamten at a starting dose of 2.5 mg once daily, adjusted as needed via pharmacodynamics-based dose titration (placebo-mavacamten group). The study population remained blinded to treatment allocation and dose (mavacamten and placebo pills were identical in appearance to maintain the blind) until all participants had completed the 30-week DBPC period, after which treatment with mavacamten was open-label.

Patients were permitted to continue with background guideline-recommended cardiomyopathy therapy (e.g., beta blockers, verapamil, or diltiazem), provided they had been on a stable, well-tolerated dose for at least 2 weeks prior to screening, and unchanged during the DBPC and LTE lead-in periods (except if safety and tolerability concerns arose). This background medication was to be managed as clinically appropriate (including adjustment or stopping) during the LTE period.

This trial was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Council for Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use Guideline for Good Clinical Practice. Institutional review boards/independent ethics committees (listed in Supplementary Table S1) at each site approved the protocol. The study complied with country-specific regulatory requirements relating to safety reporting to the regulatory authority, institutional review boards/independent ethics committees, and investigators. All patients provided written informed consent to participate, prior to entering the study. This study was funded by Shanghai LianBio Development Co., Ltd and Bristol Myers Squibb.

Assessments and Endpoints

KCCQ-23 is a patient-reported questionnaire that measures the impact of a patient’s disease or treatment for that disease on seven distinct domains using a 2-week recall: symptom stability, symptom frequency, symptom burden, physical limitations, QoL, social limitation, and self-efficacy. In addition to the individual domains, three summary scores can be calculated from the KCCQ-23: OSS (includes the TSS, physical limitation score [PLS], and social limitation and QoL scores), the CSS (derived from the TSS and PLS), and the TSS. All domain and summary scores range from 0 to 100, with higher scores reflecting better health status [21]. License to use the KCCQ-23 for the purposes of this research was purchased from the owner (Outcomes Instruments, LLC). KCCQ-23 assessments were completed during the DBPC period on day 1 and at visits in weeks 6, 12, 18, and 30 (or end of treatment, whichever occurred first), during the LTE period at visits in weeks 34, 40, 46, 54, 62, 70, and 78 (or end of treatment, whichever occurred first), and at post-treatment onsite visits.

The analysis endpoints reported here are health status as reflected by KCCQ-23 CSS, OSS, TSS, PLS, and QoL score at baseline, week 30 (DBPC period), and week 78 (LTE period), as well as changes from baseline or week 30 in these endpoints. Responder analysis was also conducted to support clinical interpretation of the treatment effect at the individual level. This was achieved by calculating the proportion of patients achieving clinically meaningful changes in CSS, OSS, and TSS at week 30 according to previously reported thresholds of ≥ 5, ≥ 10, and ≥ 15 points (representing clinically important small, moderate to large, and large to very large changes, respectively). A five-point difference in KCCQ-23 scores was demonstrated to represent a clinically important change for patients regardless of their baseline health status [18, 22, 23].

Statistical Analyses

Analyses for the DBPC period were conducted using the intention-to-treat population, which included all randomized patients according to their assigned treatment, regardless of whether that treatment was ultimately received. Analyses for the LTE period were based on the LTE population, defined as all patients who met the LTE inclusion criteria and received at least one dose of mavacamten, according to treatment sequence (mavacamten-mavacamten, placebo-mavacamten, and overall). Categorical variables were summarized as numbers and percentages within each category. For both the DBPC and LTE periods, KCCQ-23 scores were summarized descriptively by time point and as change from baseline, including the 95% confidence intervals (CIs) by treatment group based on normal approximation. For the DBPC period, the least squares mean (LSM), standard error, 95% CI, and p values for between-group comparisons of KCCQ-23 CSS, OSS, and TSS were calculated based on a mixed model for repeated measures of the change from baseline value, with baseline value, stratification factor (current treatment with a beta blocker [yes or no], based on the interactive response system), treatment, visit, and treatment-by-visit interaction as fixed effects, and patients as random effects. No formal comparisons were made in the LTE period. There was no imputation for missing data. Statistical analyses were conducted using SAS version 9.4 or higher (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA).

Results

Patients

The patient disposition for this analysis is provided in Supplementary Fig. S1. Eighty-one patients were enrolled and treated in the EXPLORER-CN DBPC period, 54 in the mavacamten group and 27 in the placebo group. Two (7.4%) patients in the placebo group discontinued treatment during the DBPC period due to personal reasons (n = 1) and COVID-19–related issues (n = 1) [10]. Therefore, 79 patients completed the 30-week DBPC period and entered the LTE period, 54 in the mavacamten-mavacamten group and 25 in the placebo-mavacamten group. All patients who entered the LTE period completed the full course of mavacamten and completed the study.

The baseline characteristics of patients in the DBPC period were published previously [10] and are listed in Table 1. In brief, patients (mean ± standard deviation [SD] age 51.9 ± 11.9 years) were predominantly male (71.6%), aged < 65 years (86.4%), and intermediate or normal cytochrome P450 2C19 enzyme protein metabolizers (50.6% and 39.5%, respectively), with NYHA class II disease (76.5%).

Change in KCCQ-23 Scores Over Time

KCCQ-23 CSS and OSS were similar between the mavacamten and placebo groups at baseline (Table 2). Improvements in KCCQ-23 CSS, OSS, and TSS were rapid and could be seen as early as week 6 (mean ± SD change from baseline at week 6 in the mavacamten and placebo groups: 3.1 ± 10.8 and 0.6 ± 10.6, respectively, for CSS; 2.9 ± 13.0 and 1.3 ± 11.5, respectively, for OSS; and 5.7 ± 15.5 and 1.7 ± 12.8, respectively, for TSS). By week 30, CSS had significantly improved with mavacamten and worsened with placebo (Table 2 and Fig. 2a), with a LSM difference (baseline to week 30) of 10.2 (95% CI 4.4–16.1; nominal p < 0.001) [10]. The pattern was similar for OSS and TSS, which showed improvements between baseline and week 30 with mavacamten and deterioration with placebo, with LSM differences of 9.8 (95% CI 3.4–16.2; nominal p = 0.003) and 10.3 (95% CI 3.1–17.5; nominal p = 0.006), respectively (Table 2 and Fig. 2b,c).

KCCQ-23 PLS and QoL score also improved between baseline and week 30 in the mavacamten group, with mean (95% CI) changes of 3.3 (– 0.6 to 7.1) and 9.9 (3.8–15.9), respectively. Conversely, patients in the placebo group experienced deterioration of physical functioning and no improvement in QoL, showing mean (95% CI) changes from baseline to week 30 of − 6.0 (– 12.6 to 0.7) for PLS and 1.1 (– 8.0 to 10.2) for QoL score (Table 2).

Sustained improvements in PLS and QoL score to week 78 were observed in the mavacamten-mavacamten group, with mean (95% CI) changes from baseline to week 78 of 4.2 (0.1–8.3) and 11.4 (5.0–17.8), respectively. Moreover, improvement in both scores was observed after initiating mavacamten in the placebo-mavacamten group; mean (95% CI) change from week 30 to week 78 was 8.8 (0.7–17.0) for PLS and 11.2 (3.5–19.0) for QoL score (Table 3).

Responder Analysis

The proportions of patients with clinically meaningful changes in KCCQ-23 CSS, OSS, and TSS at thresholds of ≥ 5, ≥ 10, and ≥ 15 points between baseline and week 30 were greater among patients who received mavacamten than for their placebo-treated counterparts, suggesting greater rates with clinically important small, moderate to large, and large to very large changes in health status, respectively (Fig. 3a–c). The difference between the mavacamten and placebo groups was particularly notable for CSS at the ≥ 5- and ≥ 10-point thresholds, with rates of 64.9% (n = 24/37) versus 16.7% (n = 3/18) and 67.7% (n = 21/31) versus 0 (n = 0/11), respectively; and for OSS and TSS at the ≥ 15-point threshold, at 51.4% (n = 18/35) versus 6.7% (n = 1/15) and 62.1% (n = 18/29) versus 11.1% (n = 1/9), respectively.

At week 78, the proportion of patients in the mavacamten-mavacamten group with clinically meaningful changes in the evaluated KCCQ-23 component scores was generally maintained across all three thresholds (versus week 30). For CSS, rates of clinically meaningful responses at week 78 were 70.3% (n = 26/37), 67.7% (n = 21/31), and 62.5% (n = 15/24) at the ≥ 5-, ≥ 10-, and ≥ 15-point thresholds, respectively. Rates were slightly higher for TSS at 75.0% (n = 30/40), 72.7% (n = 24/33), and 69.0% (n = 20/29), respectively, and lower for OSS at 61.2% (n = 30/49), 48.8% (n = 20/41), and 45.7% (n = 16/35), respectively (Supplementary Table S2).

Discussion

Treatment with mavacamten for 30 weeks improved patient-reported health status, as reflected by changes in KCCQ-23 component scores, versus placebo in Chinese patients with symptomatic obstructive HCM. In addition, prolonged treatment for up to 78 weeks demonstrated sustained improvements in all measured KCCQ-23 component scores over time. Moreover, these significant improvements with long-term mavacamten treatment were achieved despite the study population having higher baseline KCCQ scores than those in previous studies [17, 18, 24]. This analysis is the first to provide long-term patient-reported outcome data in Chinese patients with symptomatic obstructive HCM, who are significantly underrepresented in previously published clinical trials of mavacamten [15, 17, 25, 26].

Although originally validated as a measure of health status for patients with heart failure [21, 27, 28], the validity, reproducibility, and sensitivity of the KCCQ-23 as a meaningful endpoint in clinical trials of therapies for obstructive HCM have been established [29]. A direct correlation between KCCQ-23 scores and NYHA functional class, currently the most widely used method for assessing health status in clinical practice, has yet to be established; however, a general pattern has been observed. In the context of heart failure, it is reported that approximately 85% of patients with an OSS of 0–24 are NHYA class III/IV; 60% of those with OSS 25–49 are class III; 50% with OSS 50–75 are class III and 50% are class II; and > 80% of those with OSS > 75 are class I or II [22]. KCCQ-23 scores have also demonstrated prognostic significance, whereby patients with scores of 75–100 had a lower risk of hospitalization or death compared with those having scores of 50–74, 25–49, and 0–24 (relative risk: 1.5, 2, and 3, respectively) [22]. The KCCQ-23 is increasingly used to assess treatment outcomes in both the clinic and clinical-trial contexts for patients with HCM, providing a more detailed and sensitive patient-centered evaluation of health status [30].

Comparison between relevant studies is hindered by differences in study design, such as the KCCQ-23 components measured, the measurement time points, the duration of mavacamten exposure and the starting dose, and the size, characteristics, and geographic origin of the patient populations. For example, the typically lower BMI and higher proportion of poor CYP2C19 metabolizers among Chinese people versus populations included in previous mavacamten trials [31, 32] necessitated use of a lower starting dose of mavacamten than was used in the global EXPLORER-HCM study [15]. Despite this, however, similar structural and functional improvements with mavacamten versus placebo were observed in EXPLORER-CN [10] and EXPLORER-HCM [15]. In line with these efficacy findings, improvements in KCCQ-23 scores were seen in the present EXPLORER-CN Chinese population over a long treatment period. Moreover, the direction of improvements in health status with mavacamten, specifically KCCQ-23 CSS, TSS, OSS, PLS, and QoL score, between baseline and week 30 (DBPC period) in the present study is generally consistent with those observed in EXPLORER-HCM (n = 92) [18], and with the change in CSS in HORIZON-HCM (n = 38) [17]. Other studies exploring long-term treatment with mavacamten have demonstrated positive changes in KCCQ-23 components. In the VALOR-HCM study (N = 108 patients from the US), mean (95% CI) change in KCCQ-23 CSS from baseline to treatment week 128 for the mavacamten group was 15.6 (11.0–20.2) [25]. The small, phase 2 open-label PIONEER-OLE study (N = 13 patients from the US) reported a mean ± SD change in KCCQ-23 OSS from baseline to mavacamten treatment week 180 of 17 ± 16 [26]. The present study of 81 Chinese patients reports mean (95% CI) changes from baseline to week 78 in KCCQ-23 CSS, OSS, and TSS for the mavacamten-mavacamten group of 7.1 (2.8–11.4), 8.2 (3.4–12.9), and 10.0 (4.9–15.1), respectively. Taken together, these data demonstrate that benefits in health status are possible with long-term mavacamten treatment across populations that differed according to (among others) size, ethnic/racial composition, and several disease-specific indicators of obstructive HCM severity.

The findings of the present responder analysis demonstrated a larger proportion of patients with symptomatic obstructive HCM treated with mavacamten (versus placebo) who experienced small but clinically important (≥ 5 points), moderate to large (≥ 10 points), and large to very large improvements (≥ 20 points) in KCCQ-23 CSS, OSS, and TSS between baseline and 30 weeks, also aligning with the findings from the EXPLORER-HCM study [18]. Clinical correlates of five-point increments of clinically meaningful change in patients with heart failure include reduced risk of adverse clinical events (corresponding five-point decrements were associated with increased risk of cardiovascular death with or without increased risk of hospitalization), and functional improvements in terms of 6-min walking distance and oxygen consumption [22].

In addition, the findings of extended mavacamten exposure in the present study align with those of other long-term studies in patients with symptomatic obstructive HCM demonstrating the sustained improvement in health status of patients over a prolonged treatment period: VALOR-HCM, a DBPC phase 3 trial with 56 weeks (40 weeks for the placebo group) and 128 weeks (112 weeks for the placebo group) of exposure (n = 108 patients) [19, 25], and PIONEER-OLE (n = 13), a phase 2 single-arm, open-label extension study of the PIONEER-HCM trial with up to 209 weeks of exposure (median, 201 weeks) [26]. In the present study and VALOR-HCM, the placebo group experienced improved KCCQ-23 measures of health status only after switching to mavacamten [19, 25]. VALOR-HCM and PIONEER-OLE included patients only from the United States [25, 26]. EXPLORER-CN LTE is the first study of the effects of long-term exposure to mavacamten on health status specifically in Chinese patients with symptomatic obstructive HCM.

Younger patients with HCM and those with a greater symptomatic burden experience a heavier disease burden compared with their older and asymptomatic counterparts; they are particularly vulnerable to reduced QoL and increased societal cost (due predominantly to higher healthcare costs and loss of productivity) [1]. The goals of treatment for symptomatic obstructive HCM are not only to mitigate worsening of the condition toward heart failure, but also to reduce the burden of disease by improving patient health status, including QoL. Patient-reported health status is now considered to be an important measure of treatment success, and regulatory authorities such as the United States Food and Drug Administration may now consider approving treatments based on demonstrated improved health status, potentially even at the expense of reduced survival [33]. Of particular relevance is the finding in the present study that all measured KCCQ-23 component scores, including symptoms, PLS, and QoL scores, improved in patients treated with mavacamten. KCCQ-23 scores were found to be associated with NYHA functional class, which was also shown to improve between baseline and week 30 with mavacamten, and to have prognostic significance (lower scores were associated with greater risk of death or hospitalization) [10, 22].

Strengths of this study include the comprehensive measurement of health status over an extended treatment period by including not just the commonly reported KCCQ-23 CSS and OSS, but also TSS, PLS, and QoL score. Moreover, the findings are supported by the established validity, reproducibility, and sensitivity of the KCCQ-23 as a meaningful endpoint in clinical trials of therapies for obstructive HCM [29]. The present findings should be considered in light of several limitations. Although prespecified comparisons were conducted during the DBPC period, the relatively modest sample size limits the generalizability of the findings. In addition, outcomes assessed during the LTE period were descriptive in nature and intended to provide supporting evidence. This study does not report concurrent analyses of cardiac structural and functional markers, which could have strengthened the interpretation of the presented patient-reported outcomes. Finally, the study period coincided with the COVID-19 pandemic (January 2020 to May 2023) [34], which may have impacted patient health status, and particularly QoL. Although current infection or severe related complications due to new virus infection were exclusion criteria, COVID-19 infection was reported by 38.3% of all patients during the DBPC period [10] and by 49.4% of patients during the LTE period. However, important protocol deviations, and treatment interruptions or discontinuations were rare in these patients, and it was considered unlikely that COVID-19 infection impacted the interpretation of the study data.

Conclusions

Chinese patients with symptomatic obstructive HCM who received mavacamten experienced improved patient-reported health status, as reflected by KCCQ-23 scores, at week 30, and prolonged treatment led to sustained improvements through week 78. Health status benefits were also seen in those who received 48 weeks of mavacamten after switching from placebo. These positive changes are consistent with the previously reported significant improvements in physiological parameters, reinforcing the value of long-term treatment with this first-in-class cardiac myosin inhibitor for symptomatic obstructive HCM.

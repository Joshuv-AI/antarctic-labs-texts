SPCT-10 STUDY ON INTRADURAL EXTRAMEDULLARY (IDEM) TUMORS : CLINICAL, RADIOLOGICAL ASPECTS AND SURGICAL OUTCOMES
Authors: Parfait Mbaka Ebongue, Jacques Karidioula, Ludovic Honba, Landry Drogba, Dominique N’dri Oka
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIntradural extramedullary (IDEM) tumors are rare tumors of the spinal cord, usually benign and treated with surgical resection. The objective of this study was to report clinical and imaging characteristics, and surgery outcomes.MethodsThis was retrospective descriptive study reported data of 8 patients over 2-year period. Neurological status according to modified McCormick scale (grade I-V) at admission, and follow-up, tumor histology, type of surgical access to the spine canal, degree of removal were collected.Resultsthere were 6 female and 2 male (Sex-ratio: 3/1) with a mean age of 47.7 years, mean follow-up period was 6 months. Frequent symptoms were pain 6 cases, and functional impotence 4 cases. Clinically, 7cases of motor loss, 4 of hypoesthesia and 3 cases of sphincter impairment. 4 tumors were located in cervical spine, 2 in thoracic and 2 in thoracolumbar spine; the axial location was lateral in 4 cases, antero-lateral and posterior 2 cases respectively. In pre-operative patients were McCormick grade III, IV and V respectively in 4, 2 and 1 cases, and 1patient not deficient. Pain was rated between 5-7/10 on numerical scale in 6 cases. Bilateral laminectomy was performed in all cases, resection was gross total in 4 cases and subtotal in 4. Histology revealed 5 cases (62,5%) of meningiomas and 3 cases (37,5%) of schwannomas. Post-operative follow-up showed an improvement in 87,5% (7 cases) : regression of McCormick grades III to I, and IV to III.Conclusionintradural extramedullary tumors are rare. Progressive motor impairment is most common neurological sign. Very often benign, complete resection incurs good results.

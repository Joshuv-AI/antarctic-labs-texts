Alzheimer's Disease in Africa: A Scoping Review Protocol
Authors: Jonathan Ayeyi Nuamah, Fortune Kekeli Avorgbedor, Shirley Sarah Dadson, Ernest Adu Asamoah, Princess Yaa Benson, Kennedy Kwami Edem Kukuia, Ulrick Sidney Kanmounye
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground and AimsThe global burden of Alzheimer's disease (AD) is set to increase in the next three decades particularly because of the global increase in life expectancy. The state of AD in Africa is a cause for concern because the continent's rapid population growth and demographic transition will be major contributors to AD's increased global burden. This scoping review aims to investigate the following: the extent of AD research in Africa, the major contributors, key research trends, areas that require further investigation. By addressing these questions, this review will help map out existing literature and highlight areas needing further investigation.MethodsThe scoping review will follow the outline for scoping reviews by Arskey and O'Malley and the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta‐Analyses for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA‐ScR) checklist. Following the Assessing the Methodological Quality of Systematic Reviews (AMSTAR 2) guidelines with adaptations for scoping reviews, articles reporting on epidemiology, management, or study of AD in Africa will be assessed. Articles reporting on epidemiology, management, or study of AD in Africa will be searched using the following databases: PubMed, Medline, OVID, Embase, African Journal Online, the Global Health Index Medicus, and Google Scholar. The articles will be searched from database inception to date. The results will be uploaded to Rayyan where the articles will be deduplicated, and each title and abstract will be reviewed individually by four researchers. Two researchers will independently read and analyze all eligible full‐text articles.ConclusionThis review aims to map the existing literature on AD in Africa by identifying contributors to AD research in Africa, identifying research trends, and areas that require further investigation. Data obtained from this study will help guide future research efforts on AD in Africa and help guide context‐based interventions, addressing hypothesized regional disparities in AD burden and research, which would ultimately improve the outcomes of patients with AD within the continent.Keywords: Africa, Alzheimer's Disease, dementia, Neurodegeneration

Background and Aims

Alzheimer's disease (AD), a chronic neurodegenerative disorder, significantly impacts the elderly population [1]. Characterized by the buildup of amyloid beta protein deposits in the brain [2], AD is the primary cause of dementia globally, leading to a decline in cognitive function and daily living activities [3]. Dementia affects more than 55 million people worldwide, and AD accounts for 60%–70% of cases. More than 60% of dementia cases occur in low‐ and middle‐income countries [4]. In 2010, it was estimated that approximately 2.76 million Africans aged 50 and above were living with dementia, constituting around 2.4% of the population in that age group. The prevalence was notably higher among females aged 80 and above, reaching 19.7%. Moreover, AD was the leading cause, representing 57.1% of all dementia cases on the continent. The situation is further compounded by the high rate of new cases, with nearly 10 million diagnosed annually [5].

Current treatments, such as donepezil, rivastigmine, and memantine, primarily focus on managing symptoms because the underlying causes of AD remain unclear, and transportation of adequate levels of drug molecules across the blood‐brain barrier remains a challenge [6]. While conventional approaches target symptom management, some in Africa are exploring the potential of herbal medicines for additional cognitive benefits or to slow disease progression [7].

Despite significant advances in AD research, studies specifically focused on Africa are scarce, accounting for just 0.1% of global AD research output [8]. This scarcity is due to several factors, including the lack of comprehensive longitudinal studies with robust methodology, limited neuropathological and biomarker studies, and a significant lack of genetic research. Biases due to cultural and education in cognitive assessment tools, low awareness, and stigmatization in many communities contribute to the limited research on AD in Africa. This hinders our understanding of the disease's prevalence, risk factors, and potential variations in African populations [9]. Alarmingly, the incidence of AD in Africa is projected to triple by 2050, driven by the rapid population growth of the continent due to an increase in life expectancy leading to a larger older population. This would place an increasing strain on the already limited healthcare systems, particularly in regions with low socio‐demographic index where resources are constrained [10]. Therefore, a scoping review on AD in Africa is urgently needed to identify areas requiring further research and inform future studies to address this growing public health threat.

Our overarching aim will be to map existing literature on AD in Africa; identify contributors to AD research in Africa, identify research trends, and areas that require further investigation to improve future research efforts based on findings.

Methods

Methodological Framework

This review aims to understand the epidemiology of AD in Africa, the state of service delivery, and research focus and output on the continent. This study will follow the framework outlined by Arksey and O'Malley [11]: (1) Identifying the research question; (2) identifying relevant studies; (3) study selection; (4) charting the data; (5) collating, summarizing, and reporting results; and (6) Consultation. The 6th step is optional and will not be included in this review. The Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta‐Analysis for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA‐ScR) [12] will be used to report our findings. This protocol was drafted as guided by the checklist for Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta‐Analysis for Protocols (PRISMA‐P) [13] (Supporting Information).

‘Who’ refers to researchers and countries that contribute to AD research in Africa.

‘Research trends’ refers to the most researched topics on AD in Africa.

Step 2: Identifying Relevant Studies

Following the AMSTAR 2 guidelines [14], we will conduct a comprehensive search across multiple databases, including PubMed, Medline, OVID, Embase, African Journal Online, and the Global Health Index Medicus from their inception dates to the search date. Additional searches will be conducted on Google Scholar, the World Health Organisation (WHO) Global Index Medicus, African Journals Online, and gray literature sources. A backward citation analysis will be conducted for studies meeting our eligibility criteria to identify additional relevant articles. Where feasible, forward citation tracking and hand‐searching of key journals may also be performed to supplement the electronic searches. The search strategy (Appendix) will be developed by 5 researchers (JAN, SSD, FKA, EAA, and PYB) brainstorming a comprehensive list of keywords and Medical Subject Headings related to Alzheimer's Disease and Africa. The final search strategy will be reviewed and approved by an information specialist (USK) to ensure its methodological rigor and comprehensiveness before the search.

Step 3: Study Selection

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Following the AMSTAR 2 guidelines [14], studies will be included if they address the epidemiology, management, or study of AD in Africa. Studies not focused on the epidemiology, management, or study of AD in Africa or lacking data on these topics will be excluded. Articles published in non‐peer‐reviewed sources will not be included. Additionally, studies not published in English will not be included due to the language limitations of the authors involved. This will be acknowledged as a limitation in our discussion, highlighting that relevant non‐English research may not be captured in this study.

Study Selection Process

Four researchers (SSD, FKA, EAA, and PYB) will conduct a pilot screening of 10 articles to ensure consistency. Following this, independent dual screening of titles and abstracts will be performed by the same four researchers, with discrepancies resolved through discussion. Full‐text review of included articles will follow using the same criteria, with conflicts addressed as previously described.

Step 4: Charting the Data

Two researchers (JAN and USK) will independently read and analyze all eligible full‐text articles. Themes and trends identified within the data will be analyzed using VosViewer, a scientometric software tool, to visually map the relationships between keywords, authors, institutions, and countries, thereby revealing clusters of well‐researched topics and, crucially, identifying isolated or absent nodes and links that will pinpoint the existing knowledge gaps in the epidemiology, diagnosis, clinical management, and caregiving of AD by analyzing the distribution of research themes, contributors, and geographical coverage. We anticipate that the analysis will highlight thematic patterns in the dominant focus areas, such as epidemiology, diagnosis, clinical management, and caregiving, as well as regional disparities in study output.

Step 5: Collating, Summarizing, and Reporting Results

The preliminary study findings will be presented as an abstract at key African and international conferences focused on neurosurgery and global health, Including but not limited to the following: the Ghana Academy of Neurological Surgeons (GANS) Conference, Ghana College of Physicians and Surgeons’ Annual General and Scientific Meeting (AGSM), and the College of Surgeons of East, Central and Southern Africa's (COSECSA) Scientific Conference, as well as other global health and dementia‐focused conferences targeting healthcare policymakers and local research institutions to raise awareness and inform policy. This will be achieved by specifically highlighting thematic and geographical research gaps, which will allow stakeholders to prioritize resource allocation for crucial initiatives such as primary prevention, improved diagnostic pathways, and the development of contextually appropriate care models. Most importantly, we aim to foster future research collaborations by sharing our findings with potential partners across the continent, thereby encouraging the initiation of context‐based research and interventions. In addition, the final study results will be written up as a manuscript according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta‐Analyses Extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA‐ScR) [12], with appropriate figures and tables, including a Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta‐Analyses for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA) flow diagram for a better visual representation. When the manuscript is published, the first author (JAN) will design a visual and a video abstract for social media dissemination.

Discussion

This scoping review will determine the contributions of African researchers, institutions, and countries to AD research. Additionally, the content analysis of AD research in Africa will help us understand the burden of AD on the continent, challenges to diagnosis and management, research trends, and areas in need of attention. These findings will help assess the distribution of research studies compared to the burden of AD in various African countries. The review is expected to reveal disparities in AD research output across Africa. As the young African demographic ages over the next decades, the burden of AD is expected to increase [10]. This demographic shift will further increase the stress on African health systems as they are already struggling with competing public health priorities.

These findings will inform targeted interventions aimed at those countries. In addition, understanding the experiences of African AD caregivers, and how cultural beliefs influence care will help tailor holistic management protocols in order to raise awareness, reduce stigma, foster community involvement, and ultimately improve patient outcomes. As it stands, these knowledge gaps hinder the development of effective strategies for prevention, diagnosis, care, and support for people with AD in Africa.

We anticipate the scoping review protocol will have the following limitations. The review will not include a qualitative assessment of the articles included in this review. This would mean the methodological quality of the papers that are included will not be evaluated. However, this decision aligns with the aim of this study, to map the existing literature on AD in Africa, its contributors, and research trends and would not detract from it. A qualitative assessment would require extra expertise and resources and is not a standard step in a scoping review. However, mapping the existing literature and identifying the gaps will still provide important insights to guide future reviews that can specifically focus on qualitative research by applying quality appraisal tools, such as the Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool (MMAT), to complement our findings. Only articles published in English will be included. However, a comprehensive and systematic search of multiple databases with the inclusion of gray literature sources and backward citation analysis will be conducted. We anticipate that this will mitigate the exclusion of non‐English articles. This review also anticipates similar challenges to those mentioned in previous studies, including limited data availability across regions and cultural factors that influence research participation and reporting. These factors such as stigma, misconceptions about dementia, and competing health priorities, have been shown to hinder comprehensive data collection and study implementation in Africa. We therefore plan a broad search strategy that includes searches that will be conducted on additional databases as mentioned in the methods section to address these challenges. Lastly, missing data from regions with limited research will be managed by clearly identifying and discussing these data gaps in our review. We anticipate that, where feasible, the identified research gaps may be reviewed with existing stakeholders, such as the Alzheimer's & Related Disorders Association Ghana (ARDAG), Dementia Africa, and the African Dementia Consortium (AfDC), to help confirm findings and inform potential future collaborative studies. We equally anticipate that the review findings will help create continental research partnerships that will lead large, prospective, and interventional studies that are contextually appropriate.

Conclusion

Given the growing global concern around AD, this study will identify African countries most in need of AD research and care. By understanding the prevalence of this disease and analyzing research output and trends across the continent, the study will not only pinpoint areas with the highest disease burden but also lay the foundation for future research on AD in Africa. This knowledge will ultimately inform policies aimed at effectively managing the disease across the continent.

Mechanisms of synergistic regulation of the tumor microenvironment by fibroblasts and keratinocytes in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma
Authors: Yujie Weng, Rong Jia, Pengfei Ning
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEsophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is a highly invasive malignant tumor, and its tumor microenvironment (TME) plays a pivotal role in disease progression and treatment resistance. As key components of the TME, fibroblasts and keratinocytes are hypothesized to synergistically regulate tumor biological behavior.MethodsThis study integrated single-cell transcriptomic data with bulk RNA-seq data from ESCC to investigate the interaction mechanisms between fibroblasts and keratinocytes within the tumor microenvironment. Rigorous quality control, standardization, and clustering analyses were performed on the single-cell data, identifying ten distinct cell subpopulations, including fibroblasts and keratinocytes. The proportions and expression profile differences of these two cell types were compared between experimental and control groups. Cell–cell communication analysis deciphered ligand-receptor pairs and associated signaling pathways governing intercellular interactions. Molecular subtyping of The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) cohort samples was subsequently performed using non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) based on interaction-specific genes. A multi-gene prognostic model was constructed via least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression, and its predictive efficacy was evaluated. Finally, functional enrichment, immune microenvironment assessment, and differential expression analyses revealed differences in biological functions and immune infiltration patterns across distinct risk subtypes.ResultsBy integrating the gene expression profiles of these two cell types, a novel molecular classification system was successfully established, enabling the stratification of ESCC patients into C1(high-risk) and C2(low-risk) subtypes with marked differences in survival outcomes. Patients classified as subtype C2 had a median survival of 3.8 months, significantly longer than the 1.8 months observed in the high-risk subtype C1, highlighting the substantial clinical relevance of this classification. Further analysis revealed that the high-risk cohort exhibited significant enrichment in metabolism-related pathways, such as endocytosis and glyceride metabolism, whereas the low-risk cohort was predominantly associated with pathways involved in cell junctions and structural maintenance. Mechanistically, the interaction between these two cell types jointly promotes the reprogramming of the tumor immune microenvironment by regulating immune cell infiltration, HLA molecule expression, and immune checkpoint activity. Furthermore, the study identified key genes, including SLIT2 and SFRP1, which display cell-type-specific expression patterns in ESCC and hold potential as prognostic biomarkers or therapeutic targets. This research provides novel insights into the regulatory mechanisms of the ESCC tumor microenvironment, laying a theoretical foundation for the development of precision therapeutic strategies targeting the tumor microenvironment.ConclusionThis study elucidates the synergistic regulatory mechanisms between fibroblasts and keratinocytes within the tumor microenvironment of ESCC. These findings offer a novel theoretical foundation for molecular classification, prognosis prediction, and precision treatment of ESCC.

Background

ESCC is one of the most prevalent malignant tumors worldwide, with a particularly high incidence in Asia. Despite recent advancements in surgical techniques, radiotherapy, and immunotherapy, the prognosis for patients with ESCC remains poor, with a five-year survival rate of less than 20% [1–4]. TME plays a crucial role in the initiation, progression, and therapeutic resistance of ESCC. The complex intercellular interactions and regulatory signaling networks within the TME are among the current research hotspots [5, 6].

Fibroblasts and keratinocytes are essential components of the TME, representing mesenchymal and epithelial elements, respectively. Fibroblasts, particularly cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), promote tumor cell proliferation, invasion, and metastasis, while also mediating immunosuppression through the secretion of extracellular matrix (ECM) components, growth factors (e.g., TGF-β, VEGF), and cytokines (e.g., IL-6, CXCL12) [7, 8]. Keratinocytes, as the predominant epithelial cell type, not only play a crucial role in maintaining tissue barrier function but may also contribute to tumor progression by modulating the immune microenvironment and various signaling pathways. However, the interactions between fibroblasts and keratinocytes in ESCC and their underlying molecular mechanisms remain to be fully elucidated [9, 10].

In recent years, advancements in single-cell transcriptome sequencing technology have provided unprecedented resolution for elucidating the cellular composition and function of the tumor microenvironment [11, 12]. By utilizing single-cell data, characteristic genes associated with various cell types can be accurately identified, revealing their potential roles in tumors. Furthermore, integrating bulk transcriptomic data from public databases, such as The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), can further validate the clinical significance of single-cell findings and establish a foundation for molecular classification and prognostic prediction [13, 14].

In this study, we aimed to systematically elucidate the synergistic regulatory mechanisms between fibroblasts and keratinocytes in ESCC by integrating single-cell transcriptomic data with TCGA datasets. First, we identified the single-cell profiles of ESCC, focusing on characteristic genes associated with fibroblasts and keratinocytes. These genes were then used to molecularly classify TCGA samples, assess their prognostic significance, and further explore the biological features and immune microenvironmental differences between high- and low-risk groups. Finally, the synergistic mechanisms of fibroblasts and keratinocytes in ESCC were elucidated through functional enrichment analysis and key gene screening.

Methods

Data sources

Single-cell RNA sequencing data were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), specifically from dataset GSE196756. This dataset contains single-cell transcriptome data of ESCC, which experimental and control groups comprising three samples each [15]. The experimental group includes samples GSM5900215, GSM5900217, and GSM5900219, while the control group consists of samples GSM5900216, GSM5900218, and GSM5900220. All samples were subjected to single-cell RNA sequencing using the 10 × Genomics platform. The experimental group samples are derived from ESCC tissues, whereas the control group samples are paired paracancerous normal tissues.

Transcriptomic data and associated clinical information for ESCC were obtained from the UCSC Xena database (https://xena.ucsc.edu/), specifically the TCGA dataset [16]. This dataset comprises mRNA sequencing (mRNA-seq) data from 184 patients with ESCC and 14 normal control samples. In addition to gene expression profiles, the dataset includes corresponding clinical data for each sample, such as patient age, sex, tumor stage, survival status, and other relevant information.

Single-cell data analysis

A standardized workflow was employed to analyze single-cell transcriptomic data. First, quality control of the raw data was performed using the following criteria: cells with fewer than 50 unique genes or mitochondrial gene percentages exceeding 15% were excluded to remove low-quality and apoptotic cells. Subsequently, data normalization was conducted using the LogNormalize method in the Seurat software, with a scaling factor of 10,000. Highly variable genes were identified using variance stabilization transformation, applying a threshold of 1500 genes [17, 18].

Data were preprocessed with normalization prior to dimensionality reduction via Principal Component Analysis (PCA), implemented using the ScaleData function. PCA was then performed using the top 20 principal components derived from the highly variable genes. To correct for batch effects across samples, the Harmony algorithm was applied with “orig.ident” as the batch covariate, identifying significant principal components (p < 0.05). For cell clustering analysis, the top 20 principal components were used to compute cell adjacency relationships, followed by modular clustering with a resolution parameter of 0.6. Dimensionality reduction visualization was performed using Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP), generating clustering result plots and saving clustering labels [19, 20].

Automatic cell type annotation was conducted using the SingleR algorithm based on multiple reference datasets (e.g., ref_Human_all, ref_Hematopoietic), with Seurat clustering results as input. Annotation results were standardized in label format and output separately at both the cluster and cell levels. Finally, cell identifiers were renamed according to annotation results, and UMAP visualizations of annotations were generated for both overall and grouped data. All analyses were performed in R version 4.2 using Seurat version 4.1.1, with statistical significance set at α = 0.05.

Cellular communication analysis

To decipher the intercellular interaction networks within the ESCC tumor microenvironment, we employed the CellChat R package (version 1.6.1) for a systematic analysis of cellular communication [21].

An inference algorithm based on ligand-receptor interaction probabilities was employed. First, the mean expression levels of genes in each cell population were calculated using the trimedian robust estimation method. Hypergeometric tests were then applied to identify significantly overexpressed genes in each group, with a screening threshold set at p < 0.05 and a fold change greater than 1.5. Building on this, significantly overexpressed interaction pairs were identified from a database of known ligand-receptor pairs. To enhance inference reliability, prior protein–protein interaction network (PPI.human) information was incorporated, and gene expression values were corrected and smoothed using a regularization method. Intercellular communication probabilities were modeled based on the joint probability distribution of ligand and receptor expression levels. Significance was assessed via likelihood ratio tests, with a minimum cell threshold (min.cells = 10) applied to filter out low-expression noise. Finally, significant interaction pairs were aggregated at the signaling pathway level using functional annotation databases. Enrichment analysis was employed to identify globally significant cellular communication pathways.

TCGA data integration and molecular typing analysis

MRNA sequencing data for ESCC were obtained from the TCGA database. By integrating these data with single-cell transcriptomic analysis results, we extracted gene sets associated with fibroblasts and keratinocytes, then identified shared genes common to both cell types through intersection analysis.

Based on gene origin, shared genes were categorized into three groups: fibroblast-associated genes, keratinocyte-associated genes, and a combined group comprising the union of the first two categories. Univariate Cox proportional hazards regression models were employed to evaluate the association between each gene in these groups and patient overall survival, using a selection criterion of p < 0.05 in the log-rank test. Subsequently, unsupervised clustering analysis was conducted on TCGA samples using the NMF package (non-negative matrix factorization algorithm) in R, based on the expression matrices of the three gene sets. This analysis identified molecular subtypes with distinct prognostic characteristics [22, 23].

A comprehensive multidimensional analysis of the classification results was conducted, including the following: Survival curves were plotted using the Kaplan–Meier method, and log-rank tests were performed to compare overall survival differences among patients with different molecular subtypes, with a significance threshold set at p < 0.05. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed using the GSEA database, applying enrichment tests with predefined gene sets and selection criteria of false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.25 and nominal p-value < 0.05. Differentially expressed genes between subtypes were identified using the limma package, employing empirical Bayesian moderated t-tests with thresholds of |log₂ fold change|> 1 and Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) corrected FDR < 0.05. The CIBERSORT algorithm was used to analyze the proportions of 22 immune cell types within the tumor immune microenvironment. Differences in immune cell infiltration levels among subtypes were compared using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test, with a significance threshold set at p < 0.05 [24, 25].

Construction of a prognostic model for ESCC

To investigate the roles of fibroblast-associated and keratinocyte-associated genes in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, we constructed prognostic models based on three independent gene sets: (1) fibroblast-associated genes; (2) keratinocyte-associated genes; and (3) a combined set of genes from both groups. First, we performed differential expression analysis on these gene sets using the empirical Bayesian moderated t-test implemented in the limma R package. Genes that were significantly differentially expressed between tumor and adjacent non-cancerous tissues were identified using the following selection criteria: |log₂ fold change|> 1 and false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05.

Subsequently, the entire sample was randomly divided into a training set and a testing set at a 75%:25% ratio, ensuring a balanced distribution of survival events between the two groups. Within the training set, feature selection was performed using a LASSO-Cox regression model, optimizing the penalty coefficient λ via tenfold cross-validation and selecting genes for the final model based on the minimum criterion λ.min. This process identified key genes significantly associated with overall patient survival, which were then used to construct a multi-gene prognostic risk score model [26]. Finally, the predictive performance and generalizability of the model were evaluated in the test set by calculating the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) and performing Kaplan–Meier survival analysis (log-rank test, p < 0.05 considered significant).

Validation of the polygenic risk score model

A multigenic risk scoring model was employed to assess patient prognosis. First, each patient's risk score was calculated using a predefined linear combination formula based on LASSO-Cox regression coefficients. Patients were then categorized into high-risk and low-risk groups using the median risk score of the entire sample as the cutoff value. Subsequently, survival curves were plotted using the Kaplan–Meier method, and differences in overall survival (OS) between the two groups were compared using the log-rank test, with a significance threshold set at p < 0.05.

To validate model robustness, survival time distributions were analyzed separately for the training and testing cohorts, with consistency assessed using Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests. Expression patterns of prognostic model genes were visualized through heatmaps, and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests were employed to evaluate expression differences between high- and low-risk groups (significance threshold: FDR < 0.05). Finally, GSEA was conducted to identify significantly enriched biological pathways between the two groups. Enrichment tests utilized predefined gene sets, with selection criteria set at a normalized enrichment score (NES) > 1 and a false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.25, thereby revealing potential underlying mechanisms.

Multidimensional analysis of the tumor microenvironment and immune characteristics

To investigate the correlation between risk scores and immune cell infiltration in the tumor microenvironment, this study employed the CIBERSORT algorithm—based on linear support vector regression—to perform deconvolution analysis of immune cell composition in the samples, quantifying the relative proportions of 22 immune cell subtypes. Subsequently, Pearson's correlation coefficient was used to assess the linear relationship between risk scores and infiltration levels of each immune cell subset. This parametric statistical method assumes that the variables follow a normal distribution. Results are expressed as the Pearson correlation coefficient (r), indicating the strength and direction of the association (r > 0 denotes a positive correlation; r < 0 denotes a negative correlation). The significance of the correlation coefficients was tested using the t-test, with a significance threshold set at p < 0.05.

The ESTIMATE algorithm was employed to evaluate differences in the tumor microenvironment between high-risk and low-risk groups [27]. Differences in tumor microenvironment composition between the two groups were systematically assessed by calculating the stromal score, immune score, ESTIMATE composite score, and tumor purity. The nonparametric Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to compare the distribution of each score between the high-risk and low-risk groups, with a significance threshold set at p < 0.05.

Furthermore, the expression profiles of the HLA gene family were extracted from the transcriptome data. This family includes class I genes (HLA-A, HLA-B, HLA-C, etc.) and class II genes (HLA-DPA1, HLA-DPB1, HLA-DQA1, HLA-DQB1, HLA-DRA, HLA-DRB1, etc.), which were analyzed to assess differences in gene expression related to antigen presentation mechanisms between the two groups [28]. The Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used to compare the expression levels of each HLA gene between the high-risk and low-risk groups, with a significance threshold set at p < 0.05. Box plots were employed to visualize the expression distributions of HLA genes showing significant differences.

We identified genes closely associated with immune checkpoint function (e.g., CD274, HAVCR2, TIGIT, PDCD1LG2) to investigate expression differences between high-risk and low-risk groups. Using transcriptome data, we extracted the expression levels of these genes [29]. The nonparametric Wilcoxon rank-sum test was applied to compare expression differences for each immune checkpoint gene between the two groups, with a significance threshold set at p < 0.05. Box plots were used to visualize the differentially expressed genes that met this significance threshold, illustrating their expression distribution patterns.

Visualization and analysis of gene expression patterns at the single-cell level

To investigate the expression patterns of model construct genes across different cell clusters, this study utilized single-cell transcriptome data analysis techniques to visualize the distribution of target gene expression (HMGB3, COX6B2, APLN, RASGEF1A, PRRG3, and SLIT2) across various cell clusters using bubble plots and scatter plots. Bubble plots depict gene expression levels through the size of the dots and expression frequencies via the color gradients of the dots. In contrast, scatter plots offer a more detailed view of the spatial expression patterns of these genes within specific cell types.

At the pathway level, interactions between fibroblasts and keratinocytes are primarily concentrated in the COLLAGEN, FN1, and LAMININ signaling pathways (Fig. 2E–G). Within the COLLAGEN and FN1 pathways, fibroblasts predominantly act as ligand-producing cells, whereas keratinocytes mainly function as receptor cells (Fig. 2H–J). TCGA data integration and molecular typing analysis

By analyzing single-cell transcriptome data of ESCC, we successfully identified 1,653 fibroblast-characterized genes and 4559 keratinocyte-characteristic genes. Intersection analysis of these genes with ESCC mRNA sequencing data from the TCGA database yielded 1628 fibroblast-related genes and 4468 keratinocyte-related genes, respectively (Fig. 3A, B). To further investigate the clinical significance of these genes in ESCC, we categorized the intersected genes into three groups: fibroblast-associated genes, keratinocyte-associated genes, and a combined set of both. We then performed unsupervised molecular typing on the TCGA samples using the NMF algorithm for each group. The results indicated that when fibroblast-associated or keratinocyte-associated genes were used individually for typing, they failed to effectively classify the samples into significantly distinct subtypes (Fig. 3C, D). However, when the combined gene set was utilized, the samples were clearly classified into two distinct subtypes (Fig. 3E). Furthermore, survival analysis revealed a significant difference in overall survival (OS) between these two subtypes (p < 0.05), suggesting that the combined gene set holds important prognostic value in the molecular classification of ESCC (Fig. 3F). Based on molecular typing results, the samples were categorized into high-risk (C1) and low-risk (C2) groups. Functional enrichment analysis revealed that the high-risk group showed significant enrichment in multiple KEGG pathways closely associated with tumor malignant progression (Fig. 3G). These pathways include endocytosis (involved in cell signaling and receptor degradation, related to tumor cell proliferation and survival), fructose and mannose metabolism (involved in energy metabolism reprogramming to meet the energy demands of rapidly growing cancer cells), glyceride metabolism and glycerophospholipid metabolism (linked to cell membrane synthesis, lipid energy storage, and signaling), and the peroxisome pathway (involved in reactive oxygen species metabolism, whose dysregulation can affect oxidative stress and the signaling microenvironment in cancer cells). The common enrichment of these pathways suggests heightened metabolic activity and active intracellular transport mechanisms in high-risk ESCC. Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis further demonstrated that the high-risk group was significantly enriched in the biological process (GOBP) of glycerol ester metabolism and phospholipid metabolism; in the cellular component (GOCC), it was significantly enriched in the brush border; and in molecular function (GOMF), it was significantly enriched in active transmembrane transport protein activity and GTPase activator activity (Fig. 3H).

Differential expression analysis revealed that genes such as PCSK5, PRRG3, MUC12, and SFRP1 were significantly differentially expressed between the high-risk and low-risk groups (|log2 fold change|> 1, FDR < 0.05), suggesting that these genes may play a crucial role in the molecular classification and prognosis of ESCC (Fig. 3I).

Analysis of the immune microenvironment revealed significant differences in endothelial cell and fibroblast infiltration levels between the high-risk and low-risk groups (Fig. 3J–K). Specifically, the low-risk group exhibited higher vascular endothelial cell infiltration, suggesting that its tumor microenvironment may retain relatively normal vascular structures. Conversely, the high-risk group showed a marked enrichment of cancer-associated fibroblasts. The increased presence of fibroblasts is a key indicator of activated tumor stroma, as they promote tumor invasion, immune suppression, and treatment resistance by secreting cytokines and remodeling the extracellular matrix. The reduced number of endothelial cells in the high-risk group may be associated with vascular dysfunction or the epithelial–mesenchymal transition process, collectively contributing to a more invasive microenvironment.

This observation further underscores the heterogeneity of the immune microenvironment between the high-risk and low-risk groups and provides a theoretical basis for the potential therapeutic value of immune checkpoint inhibitors in patients classified as low-risk.

Discussion

In this study, we systematically examined the interactions between fibroblasts and keratinocytes within the TME and their clinical significance in ESCC by integrating single-cell transcriptomic data with mRNA sequencing data from the TCGA database. The main findings are as follows:

Through rigorous quality control and downscaling analysis, 24 cell clusters were successfully identified and categorized into 10 distinct cell types. Annotation of cell types in both the experimental and control groups revealed significant differences in fibroblasts and keratinocytes between the two groups. Specifically, the gene expression profiles and cell ratios of fibroblasts differed markedly between the experimental and control groups, while keratinocytes were exclusively detected in the experimental group, indicating their potential role in ESCC. Although sampling bias cannot be entirely ruled out, keratinocytes were not detected in samples from any of the three control groups. This may reflect a tumor-specific recruitment or activation process. This finding warrants further investigation to determine whether keratinocytes promote tumorigenesis or merely serve as markers of tissue structural alterations during cancer progression. Future studies with larger sample sizes are required to elucidate the biological significance of this discovery.

Our research has revealed that within the tumor microenvironment of ESCC, the primary communication mechanism between tumor-associated fibroblasts and cancer cells relies upon ECM reprogramming, specifically through the excessive production of collagen I (COL1), laminin, and fibronectin I (FN1). These ECM molecules function not only as structural proteins but also as potent signaling mediators. COL1 deposition induces matrix stiffening, which promotes tumor cell proliferation and invasion by activating mechanosensitive pathways. FN1, a key integrin ligand, provides adhesion signals to cancer cells, enhancing their migratory capacity and resistance to apoptosis. Abnormal laminin expression, meanwhile, signifies disruption and remodeling of the basement membrane—a critical step in tumor cell local invasion. Targeting this ECM signaling axis (e.g., by inhibiting FN1-integrin or COL1-DDR1 interactions) may offer novel therapeutic strategies to improve ESCC management.

A total of 1653 fibroblast-characteristic genes and 4559 keratinocyte-characteristic genes were extracted from single-cell data and intersected with the TCGA database, yielding 1628 and 4468 related genes, respectively. Molecular typing of TCGA samples using the NMF algorithm revealed that using either fibroblast- or keratinocyte-related genes alone did not significantly distinguish sample subtypes. In contrast, the combined use of both gene sets effectively classified the samples into two distinct subtypes (C1 and C2), with a significant difference in OS between them (p < 0.05). This finding strongly suggests that the biological behavior of ESCC is not driven by a single cellular component in isolation but rather arises from complex interactions and synergistic regulation among different cell types within the TME. Keratinocytes (typically representing tumor epithelial cells) and cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), as core members of the TME, likely engage in extensive paracrine and bidirectional signaling exchanges through multiple cellular communication pathways—such as TGF-β, Wnt/β-catenin, and Notch signaling. These pathways collectively regulate the expression and remodeling of ECM components (such as COL1, FN1, and laminin), thereby influencing tumor progression, the immune microenvironment, and cellular invasive capacity.

The high-risk group (C1) exhibited significant enrichment in multiple KEGG pathways closely associated with metabolism, including endocytosis, fructose and mannose metabolism, glycerol metabolism, glycerophospholipid metabolism, and peroxisome function. GO analysis further confirmed the group's functional overactivity in glyceride metabolism, phospholipid metabolism, brush border structure, and GTPase activation. This extensive metabolic reprogramming suggests that C1 subtype tumors may rely on abnormally active lipid and carbohydrate metabolism to support rapid proliferation, invasion, and signal transduction. This finding aligns with the metabolic hallmark of most malignant tumors—promoting progression and drug resistance through enhanced biosynthesis and energy acquisition. For instance, heightened glycerol ester and glycerophospholipid metabolism not only supply precursors for extensive cell membrane synthesis but also produce derivatives that function as signaling molecules promoting malignant phenotypes. Meanwhile, peroxisomes participate in lipid oxidation and reactive oxygen species metabolism, processes intrinsically linked to tumor cell adaptation to oxidative stress and survival.

In this study, differential expression analysis identified several genes—including PCSK5, PRRG3, MUC12, and SFRP1—that exhibit significant differential expression between high-risk and low-risk ESCC patients. These genes may collectively contribute to the regulation of malignant progression and prognostic disparities in ESCC. SFRP1, an antagonist of the WNT signaling pathway, is frequently silenced across multiple cancers, including ESCC. The WNT pathway closely interacts with NOTCH1 signaling during cell fate determination; its dysregulation may indirectly affect TP53 activity, jointly promoting tumor proliferation. MUC12, a member of the mucin family, plays a role in mucosal barrier protection and cellular signal transduction. Its aberrant expression may compromise epithelial integrity, creating a microenvironment conducive to the functional activation of other oncogenes, such as mutant TP53. The proprotein convertase PCSK5 is involved in processing multiple cancer-associated protein precursors. Its activity influences the maturation and activation of diverse signaling pathways, cooperatively regulating malignant behavior in tumor cells alongside genes such as TP53.

Prognostic modeling of TCGA samples using the LASSO regression algorithm demonstrated that both fibroblast- and keratinocyte-associated genes significantly contributed to the development of the prognostic model. The ensemble gene model, which integrated both gene sets, significantly outperformed models that utilized only fibroblast- or keratinocyte-associated genes in terms of predictive accuracy. These results indicate that fibroblast- and keratinocyte-related genes play a crucial role in prognostic modeling. The superior predictive performance of the ensemble gene model, which incorporates both gene sets, highlights the synergistic contribution of these genes to the prognosis of ESCC patients. This finding suggests that both gene types may influence disease progression and patient survival by co-regulating the tumor microenvironment or associated signaling pathways.

Risk scores were significantly correlated with the infiltration levels of specific immune cell subsets within the tumor immune microenvironment, revealing potential immune evasion mechanisms in high-risk ESCC tumors. Activated dendritic cells (DCs) showed a positive correlation with risk scores, suggesting impaired antigen presentation or a tendency to induce immune tolerance rather than activate anti-tumor T-cell responses. The accumulation of M0 macrophages (unpolarized macrophages) correlated with high risk, indicating the presence of numerous macrophage precursors within the microenvironment that can be recruited toward a pro-tumor M2 phenotype, thereby promoting angiogenesis and tissue remodeling. Concurrently, activated mast cells may directly promote tumor growth and metastasis by releasing pro-angiogenic factors (such as VEGF and FGF) and immunomodulatory cytokines. Conversely, M1 macrophages, which possess classical anti-tumor functions, exhibited a negative correlation with risk scores. This suggests that reduced infiltration diminishes the body’s phagocytic and cytotoxic capabilities against tumor cells, as well as the transmission of pro-inflammatory signals. The reduction in quiescent mast cells may also signify the microenvironment’s diminished capacity to maintain immune homeostasis. Furthermore, increased quiescent natural killer (NK) cells correlated positively with high risk, potentially reflecting NK cell functional exhaustion or inactivation. This weakens their cytotoxic surveillance of tumor cells, impairing effective clearance of malignant cells. These alterations in immune cell composition collectively delineate a highly immunosuppressive and pro-tumor microenvironment in high-risk ESCC: on one hand, cells with anti-tumor potential (such as M1 macrophages and cytotoxic NK cells) are functionally suppressed or reduced in number; on the other hand, plastic myeloid cells (such as M0 macrophages and tolerogenic dendritic cells) and pro-angiogenic mast cells accumulate significantly, creating conditions conducive to tumor progression, invasion, and resistance to immunotherapy. This finding not only aligns with known immune escape mechanisms in ESCC but also provides potential targets for developing future immunotherapy combinations for high-risk patients.

Analysis of tumor microenvironment differences revealed that the stromal cell score and composite score in the low-risk group were significantly higher than those in the high-risk group. Conversely, tumor purity was significantly greater in the high-risk group compared to the low-risk group. Notably, this study found that tumor tissues from high-risk ESCC patients simultaneously exhibited high tumor purity and low stromal scores. The potential mechanism underlying this phenomenon may primarily involve active stromal remodeling dynamics: tumors may have successfully engineered a microenvironment that supports their proliferation while suppressing host defense responses. This suggests that the stroma undergoes highly dynamic processes that may ultimately lead to tumor or this conclusion requires further validation. Concurrently, technical factors warrant careful consideration. Given the marked spatial heterogeneity of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, the possibility of sampling bias cannot be entirely excluded; however, this is more likely a technical challenge that future studies must recognize and address. In summary, future research should validate these findings using spatial multi-omics techniques within the context of whole tumor tissue and further investigate potential molecular drivers.

HLA gene expression analysis revealed that HLA-DPB2, HLA-DQA1, HLA-DQA2, and HLA-E were significantly more highly expressed in the low-risk group. This suggests that the low-risk group may have a stronger antigen-presenting capacity and enhanced immune activation. Additionally, immune checkpoint genes (e.g., CD274, HAVCR2, TIGIT, PDCD1LG2) showed significantly elevated expression levels in the low-risk group, indicating the potential presence of stronger immunosuppressive or immune escape mechanisms within the tumor microenvironment of this group.

Gene expression pattern analysis revealed that HMGB3, COX6B2, APLN, and RASGEF1A were significantly upregulated in keratinocytes, whereas PRRG3 and SLIT2 showed markedly higher expression levels in fibroblasts. These findings suggest cell-type specificity of these genes in ESCC and their potential contribution to the risk score. SLIT2 may have tissue-specific regulatory functions in ESCC. Within the ESCC microenvironment, elevated SLIT2 expression could influence tumor-stromal cell interactions, modulate immune cell infiltration, or inhibit angiogenesis, thereby shaping a microenvironment that suppresses tumor progression. High SLIT2 expression in the low-risk group may represent a compensatory host anti-tumor mechanism. During early tumor progression or in less aggressive contexts, the body may upregulate SLIT2 to counteract activated oncogenic signals. These hypotheses require validation through further analysis and experimentation. Our findings are consistent with most existing literature on SLIT2 in squamous cell carcinoma. For example, SLIT2 is frequently reported as downregulated in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) and cervical squamous cell carcinoma, with promoter hypermethylation or deletion associated with poor prognosis [30, 31]. The high SLIT2 expression observed in the low-risk group in this study further supports its role as a tumor suppressor.

Conclusion

This study highlights the critical role of fibroblast-keratinocyte interactions within the TME of ESCC in molecular subtyping and prognosis. A molecular subtyping model based on their synergistic effects effectively distinguishes high-risk from low-risk patients. The high-risk group shows significant enrichment in metabolic pathways, such as glyceride metabolism and peroxisome function, whereas the low-risk group is enriched in pathways related to cell junctions and structural integrity. Mechanistically, these two cell types collaboratively promote immune evasion and therapeutic resistance by modulating immune cell infiltration, HLA expression, and immune checkpoint activity. Additionally, key genes, including SFRP1 and SLIT2, display cell-type-specific expression patterns, indicating their potential as prognostic markers or therapeutic targets. This study provides novel insights into the regulatory mechanisms of the TME in ESCC and establishes a theoretical foundation for developing personalized therapeutic strategies targeting the tumor microenvironment.

A Prospective, Multicenter, Single-arm, Phase IV Study to Assess the Safety and Effectiveness of a Fixed-Dose Combination of Pregabalin Prolonged Release and Etoricoxib in Patients with Chronic Low Back Pain with a Neuropathic Component
Authors: Sandesh Sahebrao Patil, Brijesh Jerambhai Patel, Bhavsar Neel Mahendrabhai, C L Nawal, B Valya, K Sanjeev Kumar, Pandurang Wattamwar, Rana Kaushikkumar Ramanlal, Swagat Shah, Mandodari Rajurkar, Supriya Sonowal, Dipak Patil, Pravin Ghadge, Suyog Mehta
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and ObjectiveNearly two-thirds of patients with acute low back pain progress to chronic low back pain (CLBP). It is likely to be due to the involvement of both nociceptive and neuropathic pain mechanisms. Therefore, this study evaluated the safety and effectiveness of a fixed-dose combination (FDC) of pregabalin prolonged release (PR) and etoricoxib in patients with CLBP with a neuropathic component.MethodsThis single-arm, phase IV study was conducted at nine hospitals across India. Patients with CLBP received an FDC of pregabalin PR 75 mg and etoricoxib 60 mg. The primary endpoint was safety, evaluated by the incidence of adverse events (AEs) and serious AEs. The secondary endpoint was effectiveness, assessed by change in the numeric rating scale (NRS), Roland–Morris Disability Questionnaire (RDQ) and visual analog scale (VAS) scores, and proportion of patients using rescue medication.ResultsOf 231 screened patients, 185 met eligibility criteria and were enrolled. During the 8-week study, four patients were lost to follow-up, and one withdrew consent. Out of the 19 AEs reported, 18 were treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs), which occurred in 13 (7.18%) patients. Dizziness, cough, pruritus, and rash were the most common TEAEs. All TEAEs were mild or moderate, and no serious TEAEs or discontinuations due to TEAEs occurred in the study. Statistically significant improvements were observed at week 8 compared with baseline in NRS scores (− 3.4 ± 1.69; p < 0.0001), RDQ scores (− 7.3 ± 3.42; p < 0.0001), and VAS scores (− 32.1 ± 15.33; p < 0.0001). The need for rescue medication decreased over time, with only 34 (18.89%) patients requiring it at week 8 compared with 121 (66.85%) at baseline.ConclusionThe FDC of pregabalin PR and etoricoxib was found to be safe, well tolerated, and effective in reducing pain intensity and improving quality of life in patients with CLBP with a neuropathic component. Thus, this FDC can be a potential alternative for managing CLBP with a neuropathic component.Trial Registration NumberCTRI/2022/05/042521 [Registered on: May 12, 2022] https://ctri.nic.in/Clinicaltrials/pmaindet2.php?EncHid=Njg1ODg=&Enc=&userName

Key Points

The fixed-dose combination of pregabalin prolonged release 75 mg and etoricoxib 60 mg was well tolerated. No serious side effects were reported, and only a small proportion of patients (7.18%) experienced mild or moderate adverse effects.Patients reported meaningful relief from pain and improved daily functioning over the 8-week treatment period, as shown by better scores on standard pain and disability scales.The need for additional ‘rescue’ medication gradually decreased during the study, suggesting that the combination provided increased pain control over time.By addressing both neuropathic and nociceptive components of pain at the same time, this fixed-dose combination shows promise as an effective treatment option for chronic low back pain with neuropathic features in everyday clinical settings.

Introduction

Low back pain (LBP) is a leading cause of disability globally, affecting approximately 619 million individuals in 2019, with projections indicating an increase to 843 million by 2050 [1, 2]. In India, the prevalence of LBP ranges between 42% and 83%, making it one of the top ten causes of years lived with disability (YLDs) [1, 3]. The pooled point, annual, and lifetime prevalence rates of LBP in the Indian population are 48%, 51%, and 66%, respectively [1]. In addition, chronic LBP (CLBP) is associated with anxiety, depression, irritability, social dysfunction, and diminished quality of life (QoL), as well as absenteeism from work and activity limitation, contributing to a substantial socioeconomic burden [3, 4].

While most patients recover from acute LBP, studies suggest that nearly two-thirds of these patients progress to CLBP, likely due to several underlying factors and the involvement of both nociceptive and neuropathic pain mechanisms [5, 6]. An analysis of a United States claims database reported that 90% of cases of CLBP are associated with a neuropathic component [6]. As CLBP is a mixed pain syndrome, it is crucial to identify specific and timely treatments for patients based on underlying mechanisms to facilitate better outcomes [5, 7].

The current international guidelines primarily recommend physical activity, pharmacologic treatments, and multicomponent biopsychosocial interventions for the management of CLBP [8, 9]. Similarly, the guidelines by the Indian Council of Medical Research recommend physical activity, analgesic treatment, and other modalities such as physiotherapy for the management of CLBP [10]. The primary aim of these treatments is to reduce pain, maintain function, and prevent future exacerbations [5]. However, current treatments do not adequately distinguish between the neuropathic and nociceptive components of CLBP coexisting in an individual patient [6]. Additionally, standard analgesics suggested in these guidelines, such as nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), have poor efficacy for neuropathic pain, which further challenges the treatment of CLBP [11]. Therefore, combining drugs targeting both nociceptive and neuropathic components of pain will provide better pain relief. Also, combining drugs into fixed-dose combinations (FDCs) will reduce pill burden and improve patients’ compliance [5, 6, 12].

Pregabalin, a first-line treatment for neuropathic pain as per the International Association for the Study of Pain, exerts its analgesic effects by modulating hyperexcited neurons and reducing the influx of calcium into presynaptic terminals [5, 6, 11, 13]. For the management of neuropathic pain, pregabalin is prescribed by physicians in India at a low dose of 50–75 mg/day to ensure optimal efficacy while minimizing toxicity [6].

Etoricoxib is a highly selective cyclooxygenase (COX)-2 inhibitor used for pain management in musculoskeletal diseases, and it mediates its analgesic and anti-inflammatory effects by interrupting prostaglandin synthesis by inhibiting the COX enzyme. Although it shows similar efficacy to some traditional NSAIDs, it has a superior safety profile. Furthermore, it is a long-acting drug as its elimination half-life is > 22 h, requiring once-daily administration [12].

A previous phase III study evaluated the efficacy of an FDC of pregabalin prolonged release (PR) 75 mg and etoricoxib 60 mg in 319 patients with CLBP with a neuropathic component. The FDC demonstrated significant pain reduction and functional improvement compared with etoricoxib alone, with benefits evident from week 4 and sustained through week 8. The FDC was also well tolerated, with no severe or serious adverse events (AEs) [6].

This phase IV study was conducted as a post-approval regulatory requirement in India to evaluate the safety and effectiveness of the FDC in a real-world setting in the approved indication, i.e., CLBP with neuropathic component.

Materials and Methods

Study Design

This open-label, single-arm, phase IV clinical trial was conducted at nine hospitals across India in various states, including Maharashtra, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat, and Rajasthan. The study was designed to generate additional data on the safety and effectiveness of FDC tablets of pregabalin PR 75 mg and etoricoxib 60 mg in a real-world setting, post-approval of the product, as required by the regulatory authority of India.

Patients were enrolled from those presenting to the investigator in the outpatient department for management of their condition, provided they met the eligibility criteria and gave written informed consent. Eligible patients were dispensed with the study treatment, which they self-administered at home. This was a single-arm study in which all participants received the same study treatment. Study design. FDC fixed-dose combination, PR prolonged-release

The study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, the Ethical Guidelines for Clinical Research, and applicable local laws and regulations. The institutional ethics committee at each participating site reviewed the study protocol and other relevant documents before study initiation. The study was registered prospectively at the Clinical Trial Registry of India (CTRI ID: CTRI/2022/05/042521).

Study Population

Adult patients of either sex aged 18–65 years with CLBP who were willing to participate in the study were assessed for eligibility at the screening visit. Patients with (a) CLBP persisting for ≥ 3 months and with at least one of the following criteria on the side corresponding to their leg pain: sharp and shooting pain below the knee, pain evoked by a straight leg raise at 60° or less, reduced or absent ankle reflex, muscle weakness below the knee, and sensory loss in the L5/S1 segment; and (b) pain score of ≥ 4 on the 11-point numeric rating scale (NRS) were included in the study.

Patients with (a) tumorigenic potential as per investigator’s discretion; (b) pre-planned surgery or medical procedure; and (c) undergoing treatment with anticonvulsants, antidepressants, aspirin (> 81 mg/day), benzodiazepines, mexiletine hydrochloride, opioids, herbal medications, epidural injections within 6 months prior to screening, and any treatment for LBP within 2 days prior to enrollment were excluded from the study. Additionally, patients with any other clinically significant condition or lab abnormality that, in the opinion of the investigator, would interfere with the conduct of the study and compromise the safety of the patient were excluded.

Study Treatment

From the day of enrollment (day 1), all eligible patients received an FDC tablet of pregabalin PR 75 mg and etoricoxib 60 mg once daily after their evening meal for 8 weeks. In case of intolerable CLBP during the study, patients were allowed to use paracetamol 500 mg tablets as a rescue medication. The maximum permissible dose was 2000 mg over 24 h and could be taken in divided doses at an interval of 6–8 h, as needed. Patients requiring rescue medication for more than 2 days after 4 weeks of the start of study treatment were discontinued from the study. Discontinuation due to rescue medication use beyond 2 days after week 4 was a protocol-defined criterion indicating inadequate pain control after optimal treatment exposure and the need for alternative clinical management. This criterion was consistent with patient safety considerations.

At each visit, patients were advised to take the study treatment as instructed. Patients were educated on the importance of adherence to their treatment.

Outcome Measures

The clinical and demographic data of all patients, including age, height, weight, and clinical characteristics, were recorded at baseline.

The primary endpoints evaluated were the proportion of patients with AEs and serious AEs during the study. Patients were monitored for AEs and serious AEs regularly, along with vital signs and laboratory parameters on every scheduled visit. Patients were provided with a diary to record AEs throughout the study.

The secondary endpoints included the mean change in the NRS, the Roland–Morris Disability Questionnaire (RDQ), and the Visual Analog Scale (VAS) score from baseline (enrollment/day 1) to week 4 and week 8 of treatment. These study scales were officially purchased (as applicable), and permission for their use was obtained prior to study initiation. The total consumption of rescue medication was also evaluated at weeks 4 and 8.

For determining the change in NRS score, patients were asked to rate their pain from 0 to 10 on the 11-point scale (0 = no pain, 1–3 = mild pain, 4–6 = moderate pain, 7–10 = severe pain) [14]. RDQ was used to determine patient-reported QoL considering pain-related functional status. The questionnaire included 24 statements regarding individuals’ perceptions of their back pain and related disabilities. A lower RDQ score indicated better functional status [15]. VAS was used to evaluate patient-reported severity or intensity of pain on a scale of 0 mm, indicating no pain at all, to 100 mm, indicating the worst pain imaginable [16].

Statistical Analysis

The sample size calculation was based on the results of a previous phase III study [6]. Sample size was calculated using a proportion test by using normal approximation with a 5% level of significance and a 4% margin of error. Considering a 7% incidence rate for AE and a 15% dropout rate during the 8-week study, approximately 185 patients were determined appropriate for enrollment.

The mean change in parameters from baseline to weeks 4 and 8 was compared using the Wilcoxon signed rank test for continuous data and the Chi-square test for categorical data. All statistical tests were performed using SAS version 9.4. A p-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Safety was analyzed in the safety population, while effectiveness was analyzed in the modified intention-to-treat (mITT) population. The intention-to-treat (ITT) population was not considered, as not all patients who were enrolled received the study treatment. Thus, only those who received at least one dose of the study treatment were included in the safety population, and those who received at least one dose of the study treatment and visited the site for at least one post-baseline evaluation were included in the mITT population. Some enrolled patients did not receive the study treatment due to withdrawal of consent or loss to follow-up prior to first dosing and were therefore excluded from safety and mITT analyses.

The smallest unit of analysis was the individual patient. Effectiveness data were collected for each patient, with mean values calculated to assess overall effectiveness. AEs were also recorded at the individual level, and the proportion of patients experiencing AEs was subsequently determined. Post-baseline missing effectiveness data were imputed using multiple imputation methodology.

Primary Endpoint

The primary endpoint of the study was safety. In the safety population (N = 181), a total of 19 AEs were observed, of which 18 occurred after the study treatment administration and were considered TEAEs. One AE occurred after screening but before the study treatment administration and was not considered a TEAE.

A total of 13 (7.18%) patients had TEAEs. The most common TEAEs observed were dizziness, cough, pruritus, and rash (Table 2). Out of 18 TEAEs, 14 TEAEs in nine (4.97%) patients were mild, and four TEAEs in four (2.21%) patients were moderate in severity. A total of 12 TEAEs in 10 (5.52%) patients were judged as unlikely related, and six TEAEs in three (1.66%) patients, including one event of thrombocytopenia in one patient and five events of dizziness in two patients, were considered as possibly related to the study treatment by the investigator. A total of 17 TEAEs were reported as resolved during the study period; only one TEAE was reported as resolving. No clinically significant treatment-related changes were noted in the vital signs and laboratory parameters.

Secondary Endpoints

In the mITT population (N = 181), pain intensity showed a significant reduction over the study period. The mean ± standard deviation NRS score decreased from 6.9 ± 0.90 at baseline (median: 7.0) to 5.2 ± 1.06 at week 4 (median: 5.0; p < 0.0001), corresponding to a mean change from baseline of − 1.7 (95% confidence interval [CI] − 1.85 to − 1.55). A further decline to 3.6 ± 1.47 was observed at week 8 (median: 4.0; p < 0.0001), representing a mean change of − 3.4 (95% CI − 3.65 to − 3.15).

Functional disability, as assessed by the RDQ, also improved significantly, with mean scores reducing from 12.8 ± 3.89 at baseline (median: 13.0) to 8.6 ± 3.11 at week 4 (median: 9.0; p < 0.0001). The corresponding mean change from baseline was − 4.1 (95% CI − 4.41 to − 3.79), with further improvement observed at week 8 (5.5 ± 2.74; median: 5.0; p < 0.0001), yielding a mean change of − 7.3 (95% CI − 7.80 to − 6.80).

Similarly, VAS scores demonstrated a significant reduction in pain severity, declining from a mean of 66.7 ± 9.29 at baseline (median: 67.0) to 50.9 ± 9.59 at week 4 (median: 50.0; p < 0.0001). This corresponded to a mean change from baseline of − 15.8 (95% CI − 17.23 to − 14.37). A further reduction was observed at week 8 (34.6 ± 13.21; median: 38.0; p < 0.0001), with a mean change of − 32.1 (95% CI − 34.33 to − 29.87) (Fig. 3).

Mean change from baseline in NRS, RDQ, and VAS (modified intention-to-treat population, N = 181). NRS numeric rating scale, RDQ Roland–Morris Disability Questionnaire, VAS visual analog scale

The number of patients requiring rescue medication decreased over the study period. At baseline, 121 patients required rescue medication, with a median consumption of four doses. By week 4, this number had reduced to 80 patients, with a median consumption of three doses. This represented an absolute reduction of 23% in the proportion of patients requiring rescue medication compared with baseline (95% CI 13–33; p < 0.0001).

At week 8, only 34 patients required rescue medication, with a median consumption of two doses; this corresponded to a further absolute reduction of 48% from baseline (95% CI 39–57; p < 0.0001).

These findings show a gradual decrease in the need for additional pain relief and a reduction in the proportion of patients requiring rescue medication over time (Fig. 4).

Discussion

CLBP is a multifactorial condition involving both nociceptive and neuropathic pain mechanisms, contributing to long-term disability, psychological distress, and impaired QoL [3, 5]. While gabapentinoids (such as pregabalin targeting neuropathic pain) and NSAIDs (such as etoricoxib targeting nociceptive pain) are individually effective for pain relief, their combined use as an FDC provides a multimodal approach to manage CLBP [6, 12, 17]. Currently, paracetamol and other NSAIDs are the first-line agents for the management of LBP [9, 18, 19]. However, growing evidence demonstrates that paracetamol is often ineffective in chronic pain, leading to recent data and guidelines recommending limiting the use of paracetamol and using other options [20–24]. COX-2 NSAIDs have demonstrated significant effectiveness in reducing LBP in placebo-controlled studies [25].

As CLBP is a mixed pain syndrome characterized by both nociceptive and neuropathic components, targeting both these components is critical for optimal pain relief and improved patient outcomes [5, 7].

This phase IV study aimed to assess the safety and effectiveness of the FDC of pregabalin PR 75 mg and etoricoxib 60 mg in patients with CLBP with a neuropathic component. The safety profile observed in this study aligns with the existing safety profile of the drugs, with no new concerns identified, supporting the tolerability of this combination. Findings of this study also demonstrate significant reductions in NRS and VAS scores from baseline, indicating a reduction in pain intensity. The RDQ score also decreased significantly, indicating improvement in functional outcomes with the treatment. In addition, there was a reduced need for rescue medication as the treatment continued, indicating the sustained pain relief provided by the FDC. However, as the primary endpoint was to evaluate safety, the study was not powered for the evaluation of effectiveness, and hence, inferences that can be drawn from secondary endpoints are limited.

The results of the current study also build upon the prior phase III clinical trial evidence, which demonstrated the superiority of the FDC of pregabalin PR and etoricoxib over etoricoxib alone in terms of pain relief and functional improvement. The findings from the current phase IV study demonstrate a consistent and clinically relevant improvement in pain and functional outcomes, comparable to those observed in the phase III trial of the same FDC (pregabalin PR 75 mg + etoricoxib 60 mg). In this phase IV study, the NRS score reduction from baseline is − 3.4 ± 1.69. Similarly, in the phase III study, NRS changed by − 4.00 ± 1.65. These findings confirm the consistent analgesic efficacy of the FDC across both trials. The RDQ score in phase IV improved by − 7.3 ± 3.42, and in the phase III study, RDQ changed by − 9.28 ± 4.48. VAS scores in this phase IV study changed by − 32.1 ± 15.33. Correspondingly, the phase III study showed a drop of − 37.66 ± 18.70. These aligned results across independent trials strengthen the evidence for the effectiveness of the FDC in managing pain and improving functional outcomes in patients with chronic musculoskeletal conditions. Moreover, several AEs reported in both trials were similar, including headache and peripheral edema, with no severe or serious AEs [6].

The safety profile of the current phase IV study also aligns with that observed in previous studies evaluating pregabalin in combination with other NSAIDs, such as celecoxib. Another study found that the incidence of TEAEs with sustained-release pregabalin was consistent with that expected for immediate-release pregabalin [25, 26], supporting the use of pregabalin PR in the FDC evaluated in the present study.

A recent network meta-analysis highlighted the superiority of combining a COX-2 inhibitor with a gabapentinoid, further supporting the rationale for this FDC in CLBP management [17]. This evidence is backed by the 2021 French guidelines that recommend gabapentinoids (gabapentin and pregabalin) for the management of CLBP with a neuropathic component [18]. Similarly, the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) guidelines recommend either pregabalin, amitriptyline, duloxetine, or gabapentin as the first-line drug for the treatment of neuropathic pain (except trigeminal neuralgia) [24].

In addition, pregabalin in combination with celecoxib has demonstrated significant efficacy in reducing CLBP in patients with a Leeds Assessment of Neuropathic Symptoms and Signs (LANSS) score <12 (p = 0.01) and in patients with a LANSS score >12 (p = 0.03) compared with either monotherapy alone. Additionally, the reduction in CLBP was also significant when evaluated using the VAS score [25].

A meta-analysis has reported that in patients with chronic neuropathic pain, pregabalin significantly improves daily pain rating scale score (mean difference: − 0.65, 95% CI − 0.88 to − 0.41; p < 0.001) and daily sleep interference score (−0.81, 95% CI − 1.16 to − 0.46; p < 0.001) versus placebo [27]. Similar findings were noted in a pooled analysis of 11 clinical trials enrolling older patients (aged ≥ 65 years) with neuropathic pain [28].

Recent estimates suggest that among patients with chronic pain conditions, the rate of non-adherence is 37% after 7 days of treatment, which increases to 47% and 51% at 6 months and 1 year of treatment, respectively [29]. This high rate of non-adherence can be attributed to polypharmacy and the complexity of the treatment regimen. In such scenarios, FDCs are advantageous as they help reduce the pill burden and thus facilitate regimen simplicity. In a meta-analysis, the use of FDCs versus multiple-pill combination therapy reduced the risk of noncompliance by 24%–26% [30]. Additionally, combining agents like pregabalin and etoricoxib, which target both neuropathic and nociceptive components of CLBP, may enhance clinical outcomes and further support patient adherence [6, 12]. These findings highlight the ease of treatment offered by the FDC in patients with CLBP.

Limitations and Future Directions

This study has a few limitations. First, the single-arm, non-comparative design limits inference regarding effectiveness and safety in comparison with alternative therapies. This design was chosen in line with post-approval regulatory requirements to primarily evaluate safety in a real-world setting. Second, the follow-up duration of 8 weeks may be insufficient to fully assess long-term safety and sustained effectiveness in CLBP. Third, pain assessments were conducted using the NRS and VAS. While these self-reported measures provide details about patients’ subjective experience of pain, they are susceptible to individual variability in the perception of pain. Fourth, as the study population was exclusively recruited from India, the generalizability of these findings to other geographic and ethnic populations may be limited. To address these limitations, further studies with comparator arms and of longer duration in other geographic and ethnic populations are necessary to provide a more detailed evaluation of the sustained safety and effectiveness profile of the FDC.

Additionally, several authors were employees of the sponsor; however, measures were implemented to minimize potential bias, including investigator-led data collection, predefined protocol and statistical analysis plans, ethics committee oversight, exclusion of patients related to the sponsor or investigator, and transparent disclosure of conflicts of interest.

Conclusion

The findings of this phase IV, real-world study demonstrated the safety and effectiveness of the FDC of pregabalin PR 75 mg and etoricoxib 60 mg in managing CLBP with a neuropathic component, with no new safety concerns. The significant reduction in pain scores, improvement in functional outcomes, and reduced reliance on rescue medication highlight the potential of this FDC as an effective and well tolerated treatment option for CLBP. These results support the use of this FDC as a potential treatment option for patients with CLBP with a neuropathic component in routine clinical practice, while highlighting the need for long-term and comparative studies.

Reduction quality and hip function after internal fixation of acetabular double column fractures combined with posterior wall fractures with Stoppa approach in comparison to ilioinguinal approach
Authors: Weicheng Lin, Chao Zhang, Xi Yang, Ming Gao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate the reduction quality and hip function after internal fixation of acetabular double column fractures combined with posterior wall fractures using the Stoppa approach compared to the ilioinguinal approach.MethodsA total of 94 patients admitted to our hospital from October 2016 to October 2021 were included. All patients were diagnosed with acetabular double-column fractures combined with posterior wall fractures. Based on the random number table method, patients were divided into the Stoppa group (treated via the Stoppa approach) and the control group (treated via the conventional ilioinguinal approach), with 47 cases in each group. Postoperative reduction quality and hip function were recorded and compared between the groups. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed to identify risk factors affecting surgical outcomes.ResultsThe Stoppa group demonstrated significantly less intraoperative bleeding, shorter operation time, reduced postoperative drainage volume, shorter drainage tube retention time, and shorter hospital stay compared to the control group (P < 0.05). Postoperatively, the Stoppa group had a significantly lower VAS score (2.90 ± 0.72) and higher Harris scores than those in the control group (5.62 ± 1.18) (P < 0.05). The excellent and good rate of fracture reduction in the Stoppa group was significantly higher (P < 0.05). ROM in hip extension and flexion, internal-external rotation, and Merle-d’Aubigne-Postel scores were also significantly higher in the Stoppa group (P < 0.05). The quality of life score post-operation was higher in the Stoppa group (P < 0.05). Univariate analysis indicated that timing of surgery, fracture type, presence of bone fragments in the joint, cartilage surface injury, surgical approach, and heterotopic ossification significantly influenced surgical outcomes (P < 0.05). Multivariate analysis showed that surgery timing (1–2 weeks) and heterotopic ossification were risk factors, while the Stoppa approach was associated with improved outcomes across all measures (P < 0.05).ConclusionCompared to the ilioinguinal approach, the Stoppa approach provides patients with acetabular double column fractures and posterior wall involvement with significantly reduced operation time and blood loss, shortens hospitalization duration, reduces postoperative pain, enhances reduction quality, and improves hip joint function.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12891-025-09148-z.Keywords: Stoppa approach internal fixation, Acetabular double column fracture, Posterior wall fracture, Reset quality, Hip joint function, Quality of life

Introduction

Acetabular fractures are mainly caused by violent impact or crushing trauma. The clinical manifestations include local pain, limited mobility, and, in severe cases, massive bleeding and nerve injury. In unstable acetabular fractures, surgery is commonly recommended. To achieve anatomical reduction and promote joint function recovery, it is necessary to reconstruct the joint structures as anatomically and as early as possible [1, 2].

With the aging trend in China, the trauma mechanisms and acetabular fracture types have changed over the past decade. Some studies have shown that the incidence of acetabular double-column fractures combined with posterior wall fractures is as high as 35%. However, there is currently no consensus in China on how to treat the additional posterior wall fracture [3, 4].

The improved Stoppa approach (median incision of the lower abdomen) and internal medial osteosynthesis contrast with traditional surgical treatments. The Stoppa approach has the advantages of a small incision and a clear view of the quadrilateral plate and pelvic brim. This significantly reduces the extent of soft tissue dissection and minimizes damage to blood vessels and nerves. In addition, compared with traditional external surgical approaches, the Stoppa approach significantly lowers the risk of implant infection.

However, the Stoppa approach still has limitations. Firstly, it is difficult to restore fractures with severe displacement of the posterior wall or dislocated fractures of the posterior column. Also, in patients with iliac wing fractures, full exposure of the surgical field may not be possible, and an additional approach is often necessary [5, 6].

To demonstrate the advantages and limitations of the modern Stoppa approach, we compared it with established techniques such as the ilioinguinal approach. A total of 94 patients with both-column and posterior wall acetabular fractures were enrolled to compare the impact of the Stoppa and ilioinguinal approaches on reduction quality and hip joint function following internal fixation.

Materials and methods

Clinical materials

A total of 94 patients admitted to our hospital from October 2016 to October 2021 were included in this study. All patients were diagnosed with acetabular double-column fractures combined with posterior wall fractures based on imaging examinations. The degree of fracture displacement was assessed by CT scan. The condition of the posterior wall fracture was classified according to the Letournel classification.

Assessment of displacement: All patients underwent detailed CT scans to evaluate the degree of fracture displacement. Displacement was categorized as mild (< 5 mm), moderate (5–15 mm), or severe (> 15 mm) based on the relative movement between the fracture ends.

Posterior wall fracture description: Fractures were classified according to Letournel’s criteria, considering the course of the fracture line, fragment morphology, fragment size, and the presence of associated injuries such as joint capsule rupture or cartilage surface damage.

Inclusion criteria: (1) All 94 patients met surgical indications confirmed by imaging; (2) Patients were conscious and consented to participation; (3) Time from injury to operation was within 14 days.

Exclusion criteria: (1) Presence of other fractures; (2) Severe cardiovascular disease; (3) Mental illness (e.g., schizophrenia, bipolar disorder); (4) Coagulation disorders; (5) Open or chronic hip fracture/dislocation; (6) Fractures caused by tumors or tuberculosis; (7) Congenital pelvic dysplasia; (8) Pathological fractures; (9) Osteoarthritis.

Patients were randomly assigned to the Stoppa or control group using a random number table, with 47 patients in each group.

Technical methods

Traction and external stent fixation were performed as needed based on the fracture type and patient condition. Surgery was scheduled once vital signs were stable. All procedures were performed by the same surgical team.

In the control group, a conventional ilioinguinal approach was used under general anesthesia. Patients were placed in the supine position. An incision was made 2 cm above the line between the pubic symphysis and anterior superior iliac spine. The iliacus muscle and oblique tendon were dissected to expose the sacroiliac joint. After separating the spermatic cord and inguinal nerve, the rectus sheath and conjoint tendon were incised. The femoral nerve bundle, iliopsoas, and vessels were secured using loops to create three surgical windows, allowing full access to the anterior column, iliac wing, and quadrilateral surface. Fracture reduction was performed, and an arc-shaped plate was placed. A drainage tube was inserted, and the incision was closed in layers.

In the Stoppa group, internal fixation was performed via the Stoppa approach under general anesthesia. A midline incision (8–10 cm) was made below the navel. After incising through subcutaneous tissue, the superior pubic ramus was exposed. Key vessels were ligated, and the periosteum was dissected to expose the fracture. Under direct vision, reduction was achieved and temporarily fixed. A suitable plate was selected, shaped, and inserted to ensure proper alignment. A drainage tube was placed before wound closure. Particular attention was paid to the posterior wall fracture, using appropriate reduction tools and fixation methods (e.g., lag screws or miniplates) to achieve anatomical reduction. Fluoroscopy was used to confirm reduction quality.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS 23.0 (IBM, USA). Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation and compared with the independent-sample t-test. Categorical data were presented as percentages and compared using the chi-squared test. Multivariate logistic regression was used to analyze risk factors affecting surgical efficacy. A value of P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Twenty-five males and 22 females were included in the Stoppa group, ranging in age from 23 to 77.5 years, with a mean age of 50.25 ± 27.25 years and a mean fracture interval of 4.27 ± 2.82 days. Nineteen patients had traffic-related injuries, 17 fell from height, and 11 suffered crush injuries. In the control group, there were 21 males and 26 females, aged 22 to 77 years, with a mean age of 49.50 ± 27.50 years and a mean fracture interval of 4.30 ± 2.95 days. Twenty-three cases were traffic injuries, 16 were falls from height, and 8 were crush injuries. There were no statistically significant differences in age, gender, or fracture interval between the groups (P > 0.05). The patient selection process is shown in Fig. 1.

At 6 months postoperatively (Fig. 2C), the fracture lines were blurred or absent, indicating healing. Callus formation was visible around the implants, demonstrating repair and remodeling. Discussion

Acetabular fractures involving both columns are usually intra-articular and are primarily addressed by assessing hip joint instability and incongruence between the femoral head and the acetabulum. This approach aims to identify the most suitable treatment strategy [7, 8]. In terms of surgical timing, these injuries are often accompanied by other traumas, including visceral organ damage. When a patient’s vital signs are stable, surgical treatment should be performed as early as possible. Generally, surgery within one week post-injury is optimal, as bleeding has typically subsided, and scar tissue or callus has not yet formed. This timing is conducive to better fracture reduction. If surgery is delayed beyond two weeks, the presence of hematoma organization, soft tissue contracture, and early callus formation may hinder fracture exposure and negatively affect reduction and fixation quality [9, 10]. Forced surgery in this context increases trauma and the risk of complications such as heterotopic ossification, which worsens prognosis.

Due to the complex acetabular anatomy and diversity of fracture patterns, precise reduction and stable fixation are essential in treating acetabular double-column fractures combined with posterior wall involvement. The Stoppa approach allows for reduction under direct vision without opening the joint capsule. Proper dissection enables anatomical reduction of fracture ends. The use of a ball-spike pusher and reduction rod is tailored to the specific fracture. Screws are placed away from the joint surface, and Kirschner wires are used to guide screw paths. If no resistance is encountered during joint movement, screws are inserted. C-arm fluoroscopy is employed to verify reduction quality [11, 12].

In this study, the Stoppa approach yielded favorable clinical outcomes. Patients in this group showed better recovery and a higher rate of excellent reduction compared to the conventional group, indicating improved pain relief and faster rehabilitation. Additionally, the Stoppa group demonstrated lower operative indices and better VAS scores. The approach enables full fracture exposure via a single midline incision through the extraperitoneal space. This reduces incision length, simplifies fixation, and minimizes trauma to soft tissue and blood vessels, leading to less intraoperative bleeding and earlier initiation of rehabilitation [13, 14].

The Stoppa approach also allows for accurate reduction of displaced posterior wall fractures, particularly when classified under the Letournel system. By improving stability through supplemental screw or plate fixation, the risk of reduction loss is minimized. Adawy et al. [15] reported similar findings, highlighting the Stoppa approach’s effectiveness in stabilizing anterior acetabular fractures by plating the medial quadrilateral surface. In the present study, the Stoppa group achieved superior Harris scores, ROM, Merle-d’Aubigne-Postel scores, and SF-36 quality-of-life outcomes. Enhanced exposure of the true pelvic brim enables direct fracture visualization and fixation, further improving reduction quality [16, 17].

The Stoppa approach also supports posterior column fixation, iliac plating, avoids improper screw placement and enhances fixation reliability. When combined with iliac reinforcement, the Stoppa method improves clinical results and long-term patient quality of life [18, 19]. Srivastava et al. [20] reported that the modified Stoppa approach results in shorter operation times, reduced blood loss, fewer complications, and lower infection rates compared to the ilioinguinal approach. These findings align with the results of this study. Although the ilioinguinal approach may offer better anatomical reduction in some cases, it does not necessarily lead to superior functional outcomes [21]. Nonetheless, this study’s 6-month follow-up may not sufficiently capture complications such as heterotopic ossification or posttraumatic arthritis. Future studies should include longer follow-up (e.g., ≥ 12 months) to better evaluate long-term outcomes.

After the Stoppa treatment, the repositioning process becomes more straightforward, particularly for the anatomical repositioning of anterior column fractures. Its extraperitoneal exposure enables clear visualization of the pubic symphysis, anterior column, and quadrate region, significantly reducing the difficulty in repositioning anterior column dislocation [22]. However, it should be noted that the Stoppa approach has certain limitations when dealing with posterior dislocation. As the posterior column is located on the inner side of the true pelvis and is obstructed by structures such as the iliacus muscle and sciatic nerve, the direct field of vision is limited. The repositioning process relies on the surgeon’s precise understanding of the three-dimensional anatomy of the acetabulum. It often requires the assistance of specialized instruments such as the Ball-Spike pusher and the pointed rod. The posterior column displacement is corrected indirectly through the lever principle. At the same time, the quality of the repositioning needs to be verified by intraoperative C-arm fluoroscopy or 3D navigation technology to avoid vascular and nerve injuries caused by blind operation [23]. For posterior wall fractures of the acetabulum, even if the displacement is slight (≥ 2 mm), the impact on the smoothness of the joint surface must be given due attention. The Stoppa approach is unable to fully expose the posterior fracture fragments from the front. Especially when the fracture involves the weight-bearing area of the acetabulum or is accompanied by joint surface collapse, simple anterior approach for reduction and fixation is prone to result in residual displacement. At this point, the “front-back combined approach” strategy needs to be adopted. The first stage involves fixing the anterior pillar and part of the posterior pillar through the Stoppa approach. The posterior wall was treated through the Kocher-Langenbeck approach in the second stage, and the joint surface was reconstructed in stages. This staged surgery can reduce the trauma of a single operation, lower the risk of soft tissue complications, and ensure the anatomical reduction and stable fixation of the posterior wall fracture fragments [24]. Furthermore, the Stoppa approach requires the use of iliac bolt plates for enhanced fixation. Through the mechanical transmission of the plates across the “Y”-shaped area of the acetabulum, it compensates for the biomechanical deficiencies of simple screw fixation in the posterior column. However, for posterior column comminuted fractures, additional fixation with sacroiliac screws or percutaneous puller screws is necessary to enhance overall stability [25]. Collectively, the application of the Stoppa approach requires individualized decision-making based on the fracture classification. Fractures involving the anterior column can be treated alone, while complex posterior column or posterior wall fractures require combined auxiliary techniques or approaches to balance surgical trauma and reduction quality.

Overall, compared to the ilioinguinal approach, the Stoppa approach significantly reduces operative time and bleeding, decreases postoperative pain, enhances functional recovery, and improves postoperative quality of life in patients with acetabular double-column fractures and posterior wall involvement.

This study has limitations, including a relatively small sample size and potential selection bias. Moreover, the short follow-up period may not fully reflect long-term outcomes. Future research should incorporate longer follow-up periods and evaluate possible effects on abdominal organs and sexual function.

Innovative aspect: This study is among the first (or includes the largest sample) to assess the efficacy of the Stoppa approach for acetabular double-column fractures with posterior wall involvement. The muscle sparing incision, clear visualization, improved reduction of the anterior column, and limited neurovascular trauma offer a new direction for managing complex acetabular fractures. This study not only assessed postoperative reduction quality but also emphasized hip function recovery, validated via Harris scores and related metrics. Furthermore, univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses clarified the influence of surgical timing, fracture type, and approach on outcomes. Notably, the Stoppa approach was identified as a positive factor for surgical success, supporting its broader clinical adoption.

Recurrent Acute Limb Ischemia and Successful Treatment with Endovascular Thrombectomy
Authors: Bruce A Rheaume, Muhammad Y Adeel, Khagendra Dahal, Juyong Lee
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAtrial fibrillation (AF) is associated with an increased risk of thromboembolic events; however, many patients with AF are noncompliant with medication regimens, which increases said risk substantially. Suboptimal health literacy presents significant hurdles to compliance with medical treatment. Here we present a case of an elderly Hispanic woman with AF and several comorbidities, including a history of dementia, who presented with consecutive recurrence of acute limb ischemia in the bilateral lower extremities just 3 days apart. Both events were successfully treated with endovascular thrombectomy. This case study not only showcases the efficacy of the latest endovascular technologies, but also draws attention to the importance of strict patient medication adherence in AF and the effects that health literacy can have on said adherence.Keywords: Atrial fibrillation, thromboembolism, thrombectomy, medication adherence, dementia, communication barriers

INTRODUCTION

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most prevalent cardiac arrhythmia, particularly in patients with known cardiovascular disease,1 and its overall prevalence has been steadily increasing.2 In the Republic of Korea (hereafter, Korea), the prevalence of AF has increased by 2.1-fold, from 0.73% in 2006 to 1.53% in 2015.3 Peripheral thromboembolism, which is the second most common complication of AF after stroke, can be devastating.4 Here, we present a case of bilateral thromboembolic acute limb ischemia, which occurred consecutively and less than 1 week apart, associated with AF.

CASE REPORT

An 81-year-old Spanish-speaking female with a history of AF and peripheral artery disease (PAD) presented to the emergency department (ED) after experiencing 1 week of right lower extremity pain, numbness, and weakness. An arterial Doppler showed severe tardus parvus wave forms in the right femoral artery, suggesting iliac artery occlusion. On physical examination, the right leg was cold, and the pedal arterial pulses of the right foot were not palpable or audible on handheld Doppler. The patient demonstrated diminished sensations in the right lower extremity but with intact motor function. Since the patient had an anticoagulant on her medication list and a history of PAD, the assumed etiology of the ischemia was an acute worsening of PAD or a related, de novo thrombus.

Due to the pressing nature of marginally-threatened acute limb ischemia, the patient underwent urgent invasive angiography. A 6-French sheath was advanced over the wire to the left common femoral artery. A 4-French Omni™ Flush catheter (AngioDynamics, Latham, NY, USA) was then advanced to the abdominal aorta, and an abdominal bi-iliofemoral aortogram was performed (Fig. 1A). This revealed an oval-shaped filling defect with a near total occlusion of the right external iliac artery (EIA), suggestive of thrombus. The catheter was then advanced to the right common iliac artery (CIA) with a 0.035″ angled tip Glidewire® (Terumo, Somerset, NJ, USA), and the wire was successfully passed through the total occlusion of the right EIA (Fig. 1B). A 0.035'' Supra Core™ guidewire (Abbot Vascular, Abbot Park, IL, USA) was advanced to the distal right superficial femoral artery (SFA), and the 6-French sheath was exchanged for an 8-French 65 cm Destination® sheath (Terumo). The sheath tip was placed at the right CIA.

After heparin was administered, a Penumbra Indigo® CAT 9 8-French thrombectomy catheter (Penumbra, Inc, Almeda, CA, USA) was inserted into the thrombotic occlusion in the right EIA, and two runs of aspiration thromboembolectomy were performed. Post-thrombectomy angiography confirmed recanalization of the right EIA and restoration of normalized distal flow without significant residual clot or distal embolization (Fig. 1C). The patient’s symptoms of acute limb ischemia resolved shortly after thrombectomy, and she was discharged home the next day with oral Rivaroxaban (20 mg daily) and oral Clopidogrel (75 mg daily).

Three days after discharge, the patient presented again to the ED, but this time with left lower extremity pain. The physical examination revealed a pale, cold, and pulseless left lower leg. There was no pedal arterial Doppler sound but a maintained venous Doppler sound, suggestive of recurrent acute limb ischemia with a marginally-threatened condition. The patient again underwent urgent invasive angiography. Left groin access was obtained in anterograde fashion, and a 6-French sheath was advanced over the wire to the proximal SFA. Angiography of the SFA showed a new ovoid thrombotic occlusion of the left proximal SFA (Fig. 2A). Percutaneous mechanical thrombectomy was performed using the AngioJet™ rheolytic thrombectomy device (Boston Scientific, Marlborough, MA, USA) with heparinized saline. The antegrade flow was normalized. After thrombectomy, due to underlying atherosclerotic plaque, a 6×40 mm Absolute Pro™ stent (Abbot Vascular) was advanced and implanted in the proximal SFA. Post-thrombectomy/angioplasty angiography revealed a patent SFA with normalized flow (Fig. 2B).

Due to the recurrent episodes of thromboembolism within 1 week at two different sites, the patient underwent a transesophageal echocardiogram, which showed a mass in the left atrial appendage (LAA), suggestive of LAA thrombus (Fig. 2C). Upon questioning through an interpreter, it was revealed that the patient had not been taking her anticoagulation medication as directed—likely due to the language barrier and underlying mild dementia. She and her caregiver were counseled on the importance of medication adherence. The patient was seen in follow-up and developed no further episodes. Informed consent has been obtained from the patient’s family for the publication of this case report and its accompanying images.

DISCUSSION

Thromboembolism is a well-known complication of AF.1,4,5,6 There are guidance scores, such as CHA2DS2-VASc, that are used to prevent these life-threatening complications of AF; however, non-adherence can lead to life-threatening morbidities, as in the case presented here2. A systematic review of 18 studies revealed that the adherence to medication regimens in patients with dementia was a staggeringly low 17%–42%, and that the premature discontinuation of medications occurred in 35%–80% of patients with dementia.7 Furthermore, patients with language barriers are also more likely to be non-adherent. A study of the Hispanic population in the United States (US) found that language barriers are a substantial contributor to a significantly higher level of medication non-adherence, compared to the rest of the population in the US.8 Similarly, Korea has recently seen a large increase in foreign migrants with little to no fluency in the Korean language. Between 2000 and 2015 Korea saw a 7-fold increase in the number of “foreign origin” residents, putting the total to 1.5 million people or ~3% of the population.9 Given that Korea has historically been a single ethnic country, the medical community may be less prepared to serve this rise of foreign laborers. These barriers may be addressed by closer monitoring (e.g., home health visits, more frequent follow-up visits) in select populations deemed to be at risk for non-adherence.

In the management of acute limb ischemia, there are several treatment options including catheter-directed thrombolysis, percutaneous thrombectomy, and open surgery. The choice of the approach for each patient depends on the lesion characteristics and the patient’s underlying medical comorbidities. One recent cohort study of over 11000 matched pairs showed that the endovascular approach for the treatment of peripheral thromboembolism is safer and results in a decreased length of hospital stay, when compared with open surgery.10 These findings are likely particularly salient in the case of advanced-age patients with multiple comorbidities, like the one presented here.

This unique case of acutely recurrent peripheral thromboembolism in the setting of AF with LAA thrombus highlights the efficacy and safety profile of the latest technologies in percutaneous mechanical thrombectomy for the management of acute limb ischemia. It also highlights the importance of proper counseling, particularly in the setting of language barriers and advanced-age patients with dementia.

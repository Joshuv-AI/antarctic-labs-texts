Budget Impact of Eplerenone for Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction: National Perspective and Regional Implications in Italy
Authors: Giuseppe Pompilio, Claudio Bellomo, Franco Maria Pio Mondello Malvestiti, Nadia Aspromonte
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionHeart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) represents a major clinical and economic burden in Italy, driven by an aging population. Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs) are a cornerstone of guideline-recommended therapy. Eplerenone has demonstrated efficacy in reducing mortality and hospitalizations but remains underutilized. This study assessed the budget impact of increasing eplerenone use in eligible patients with HFrEF from national and regional perspectives.MethodsA budget impact analysis (BIA) was conducted over a 3-year horizon from the perspective of the Italian National Health Service (INHS). Two scenarios were compared: Current (observed MRA use) and Projected (increased eplerenone uptake). Model inputs included eligible population, treatment distribution, drug acquisition costs, and clinical outcomes (all-cause mortality, hospitalizations, renal impairment, hyperkalemia, and gynecomastia) with associated costs. Clinical inputs were derived from a network meta-analysis (NMA); cost inputs from Italian literature and national tariffs.ResultsTotal expenditure increased from €598.6 million (M) to €600.6 M over 3 years, corresponding to a net budget impact of + €2.01 M (+ 0.34%), with annual increments of €880,000 (year 1), €885,000 (year 2), and €245,000 (year 3). Increased eplerenone use resulted in higher costs (+ €35.07 M), driven by drug acquisition and hospitalization costs, partially offset by mortality-related savings (-€3.62 M). These were partially counterbalanced by reductions in other MRAs (spironolactone − €6.37 M, potassium canrenoate − €8.32 M, canrenone − €18.37 M), mainly due to fewer hospitalizations, renal events, and a lower drug volume. Results were primarily driven by clinical parameters for eplerenone, particularly NMA-derived hazard ratios for hospitalization and all-cause mortality. The net budget impact was modest in relative terms (< 0.4% of total MRA-related expenditure), indicating substantial budget neutrality.ConclusionsGreater adoption of eplerenone was associated with a modest net increase in healthcare expenditure for the INHS, corresponding to approximately €3.20 per treated patient per year and consistent with substantial budget neutrality. The budget impact reflects both treatment redistribution and differences in clinical outcomes across MRAs. Expanding eplerenone use in line with guideline-recommended therapy may be achieved at a limited additional cost, while offering a more favorable renal and selectivity profile relative to other MRAs.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s12325-026-03720-2.

Introduction

Heart failure is a growing clinical and economic challenge in Europe and particularly in Italy, where aging demographics increase disease prevalence [1]. Current European guidelines define the standard of care for patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) as comprising four therapeutic pillars: (i) angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEi), angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs), or angiotensin receptor–neprilysin inhibitors (ARNi); (ii) mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs); (iii) sodium–glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors; and (iv) beta-blockers [2, 3]. Increasingly, evidence supports the notion that initiating all four therapies within a short timeframe—or even simultaneously—yields greater clinical benefits and is associated with improved patient outcomes compared with the conventional stepwise initiation and titration of each agent [4]. Currently available MRAs in Italy include spironolactone, canrenone, potassium canrenoate, finerenone, and eplerenone. Spironolactone exerts progestational and anti-androgenic effects that can manifest as gynecomastia, breast tenderness, erectile dysfunction, decreased libido, and menstrual irregularities [5]. Canrenone has limited scientific evidence supporting its efficacy and safety profile, and as such, it is not listed among the recommended MRAs in the 2021 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) Guidelines. Potassium canrenoate is the hydrophilic potassium salt of canrenone, and its use is indicated in clinical settings requiring a rapid onset of action or when oral administration of spironolactone or canrenone is not feasible, such as in hospitalized or acutely decompensated patients. Furthermore, the indications listed in the Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) for both spironolactone [6] and canrenone [7] are broad and do not specifically address their role in the management of heart failure. Although finerenone is classified as an MRA, in Italy it is currently approved only for the treatment of adult patients with chronic kidney disease (with albuminuria) associated with type 2 diabetes. For this reason, it was not included in the present analysis. However, the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) recently approved finerenone for the treatment of heart failure with mildly reduced or preserved ejection fraction (HFmrEF and HFpEF), on the basis of the results of the phase III FINEARTS-HF trial, published in the New England Journal of Medicine [8]. Eplerenone, unlike other MRAs, has demonstrated clinical benefit in reducing hospitalizations and mortality within its approved indications, as reported in pivotal clinical trials [9, 10] and confirmed in the SmPC [11]. Nevertheless, despite its well-established efficacy and safety profile, its use in Italy remains limited. National market data and expert opinion highlight substantial evidence–practice gaps, with prescribing patterns falling short of guideline recommendations and potentially leading to suboptimal patient outcomes and increased healthcare resource utilization.

This study aimed to evaluate the economic sustainability of increased use of eplerenone (Projected scenario) for the treatment of patients with HFrEF, compared with current clinical practice (Current scenario). Methods

A budget impact analysis (BIA) was conducted over a 3-year time horizon from the perspective of the Italian National Health Service (INHS), in accordance with International Society for Pharmacoeconomics and Outcomes Research (ISPOR) good-practice recommendations, which favor short- to medium-term horizons aligned with the planning and budgeting cycles of healthcare payers [12, 13]. This timeframe is consistent with the annual budget cycle of the INHS and is sufficient to capture the gradual uptake of eplerenone and its associated cost consequences, while limiting the additional uncertainty in demographic, epidemiological, and price assumptions that a longer (e.g., 5-year) projection would introduce. The analysis was conducted using an Excel-based economic model designed to estimate the costs associated with the management and treatment of patients with heart failure. The model compared a scenario reflecting current, limited epleronone use (Current scenario) with an alternative scenario assuming increased uptake (Projected scenario). Costs were estimated for healthcare resources use from the INHS perspective, based on four key parameters: (i) the eligible patient population and treatment initiation rate; (ii) the distribution of MRA use in both scenarios; (iii) treatment costs; and (iv) clinical outcomes and their associated costs. The analysis was performed at both the national and regional levels, applying the same epidemiological and economic assumptions to the adult population of each Italian region and autonomous province to generate region-specific budget impact estimates.

This article is based on previously conducted studies and does not contain any new studies with human participants or animals performed by any of the authors.

Eligible Population and Distribution of MRA use in Current and Projected Scenario

The number of patients eligible for treatment with eplerenone or other MRAs was estimated based on (i) the adult population residing in Italy as of January 1, 2025 [14]; (ii) the incidence of heart failure following myocardial infarction; (iii) the prevalence of chronic heart failure; and (iv) the proportion of patients diagnosed and treated with MRAs. All incident patients with heart failure were assumed to initiate MRA therapy. Consequently, the incident patients represented 15.03% of the total eligible population (39,423 of 262,352), with the remainder consisting of prevalent patients already receiving MRA therapy. This share reflects the annual renewal of the eligible population rather than a probability of treatment initiation, since all incident patients were assumed to initiate the MRA therapy, and was derived from national MRA market and prescribing data, as reported, together with its source, in Table 1. The eligible population was derived from two distinct epidemiological pathways: an incident component, comprising patients who develop heart failure within 1 year of a myocardial infarction (estimated as the adult population × myocardial infarction incidence × the proportion developing heart failure at 1 year, yielding 39,423 incident patients), and a prevalent component, comprising patients with established chronic HFrEF already treated with MRAs (estimated as the adult population × chronic heart failure prevalence × the proportions of patients with HFrEF, receiving treatment, and treated with MRAs, yielding 222,929 prevalent patients). These two components are obtained from independent data sources—post-myocardial infarction incidence and cross-sectional prevalence of chronic heart failure, respectively—and represent conceptually different entities: the incident component captures newly diagnosed patients entering MRA therapy (treatment initiators), whereas the prevalent component captures the established pool of patients already on MRA therapy. A limited overlap between the two components cannot be entirely excluded, as a small share of incident post-myocardial infarction patients may, over time, be absorbed into the prevalent pool. However, this potential overlap is applied identically to both the Current and the Projected scenario and to the same total eligible population; because the budget impact is computed as the difference between the two scenarios, any such overlap affects both arms equally and does not bias the differential result, which is the quantity of interest in a budget impact analysis. A treatment adherence rate of 80% was further assumed and applied downstream to the total eligible population—both incident and prevalent patients—in line with national data indicating that about 20% of individuals with cardiovascular diseases in Italy exhibit low adherence to therapy. This adherence rate is distinct from, and applied after, the assumption that all incident patients initiate therapy: initiation determines entry into the treated population, whereas the 80% rate reflects subsequent persistence with treatment [15]. Accordingly, the remaining 20% of eligible patients who initiated but did not continue treatment were excluded from the cost calculations, and the eligible population was proportionally reduced. Applying these criteria, the estimated number of treated patients was 209,882 in the first year. In addition to drug acquisition costs, the model incorporated costs associated with hospitalization, renal impairment, hyperkalemia, and gynecomastia, as well as cost offsets related to avoided deaths. These components were combined within the model to estimate total costs per treatment. Average annual costs per treated patient were subsequently derived by dividing total costs by the number of treated patients in each treatment group and are presented to facilitate interpretation of the results.

Clinical Outcome

To ensure methodological consistency when comparing outcomes across eplerenone and its comparators, evidence was drawn from a recently published systematic literature review and network meta-analysis (NMA) identifying randomized controlled trials in patients with HFrEF [22]. This NMA was selected as it is the only analysis including all MRAs and evaluating all relevant clinical endpoints. To estimate baseline risks for the endpoints of interest, five separate meta-analyses were conducted on the control arms of the randomized trials included in the NMA. These analyses were performed using the meta package in R (version 4.4.1). Given the heterogeneity in follow-up durations across studies, risk estimates were harmonized to a common 12-months horizon under the proportional hazards assumption (Supplementary Material, Figs. S1–S5).

Hyperkalemia

Hyperkalemia was added as an additional adverse event cost component because it is the most clinically and economically relevant on-target class effect of MRAs. By blocking aldosterone-mediated renal potassium excretion, all MRAs increase serum potassium, and this effect is amplified in patients with HFrEF, who are typically co-treated with other renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS) inhibitors and frequently have concomitant renal impairment [2, 18]. Hyperkalemia is a leading reason for down-titration or discontinuation of guideline-recommended therapy and is associated with emergency presentations, hospitalization, and worse cardiorenal outcomes, making it a substantial and partly avoidable cost driver in the INHS setting [25]. Capturing it in the model is therefore necessary to reflect the true downstream cost profile of each MRA rather than drug-acquisition cost alone. Relative risks from the NMA were applied to a baseline absolute risk of 2.55% estimated by meta-analysis of the control arms, yielding treatment-specific absolute risks of 4.79% for eplerenone (RR 1.88), 6.22% for spironolactone (RR 2.44), and 7.37% for canrenone and potassium canrenoate (RR 2.89). Each event was valued at a weighted mean per-event cost of €921.28, obtained by combining the cost of a severe (hospital-managed) hyperkalemia event (€1,916.11) and the cost of a moderate (ambulatory-managed) event (€73.83), weighted by the proportion of severe and moderate events (46% and 54%, respectively). The severe—and moderate—event unit costs and the severity split were derived from the Italian cost-of-illness model of hyperkalemia management reported by Stawowczyk et al. [25], based on Italian DRG tariffs [24] and outpatient resource use [26]; the 46%/54% severity distribution is consistent with real-world evidence in patients with HFrEF on RAAS inhibitors [27] (Table 7). Gynecomastia

Gynecomastia was included as a distinct adverse event cost component because it is a well-recognized, class-related anti-androgenic effect of MRAs that is mechanistically tied to receptor selectivity. Nonselective MRAs such as spironolactone bind not only the mineralocorticoid receptor but also the androgen and progesterone receptors, producing dose-dependent gynecomastia, breast tenderness, and sexual dysfunction; eplerenone, by contrast, is a selective MRA with markedly lower affinity for sex-steroid receptors and a correspondingly lower reported incidence of these effects [5, 9, 10]. This differential is clinically and economically relevant because gynecomastia is a recognized driver of treatment dissatisfaction and discontinuation of nonselective MRAs [22], and its management generates dedicated outpatient resource use. Including it therefore captures a genuine, selectivity-dependent cost difference across the MRA class rather than a negligible side effect. Relative risks from the NMA were applied to a baseline absolute risk of 0.42% estimated by meta-analysis, yielding treatment-specific absolute risks of 0.34% for eplerenone (RR 0.81) and 2.94% for spironolactone, canrenone, and potassium canrenoate (RR 7.01) [22]. The NMA did not provide separate estimates for canrenone or potassium canrenoate; for these two agents the relative risk derived for spironolactone was therefore applied. This assumption is pharmacologically plausible because canrenone is the principal active metabolite shared by both spironolactone and potassium canrenoate, and all three agents act as nonselective MRAs that retain affinity for the androgen and progesterone receptors responsible for the anti-androgenic adverse event profile, in contrast to the selective agent eplerenone [28]. The assumption is also conservative: there is evidence that canrenone has weaker androgen-receptor affinity than the parent spironolactone, so applying the spironolactone-derived relative risk is more likely to overestimate than to underestimate the gynecomastia burden—and hence the associated cost—attributed to canrenone and potassium canrenoate, avoiding any favorable bias toward eplerenone [5]. Expressed per treated patient, this corresponds to €4.19 in year 1, €4.23 in year 2, and €1.17 in year 3, with a mean of €3.20 per treated patient per year over the 3-year horizon, further illustrating the limited budget impact of increased eplerenone use. As shown in Fig. 2, greater uptake of eplerenone was associated with higher total costs (+ €35.07 million), driven primarily by drug acquisition and hospitalization. These were partially offset by reductions for the displaced comparators: spironolactone (− €6.37 million), potassium canrenoate (− €8.32 million), and canrenone (− €18.37 million), reflecting their decreased use in the Projected scenario. At the cost component level, greater eplerenone use raised drug acquisition (+ €6.70 million) and hospitalization (+ €1.62 million), while the management of renal impairment (− €8.48 million), hyperkalemia (− €0.71 million), and gynecomastia (− €0.12 million) decreased, alongside a mortality-related cost increase of + €2.99 million. This increase reflects the fact that the saving from avoided deaths with greater eplerenone use were more than offset by the reduced mortality-related saving associated with the displaced comparators. Hospitalization remained the single largest component for every strategy. Deterministic Sensitivity Analysis

A deterministic sensitivity analysis (DSA) was conducted to assess the robustness of the model results to variations in key input parameters. In this approach, each parameter was varied individually by a fixed percentage (± 20.00%) while keeping all other inputs constant, and the corresponding change in the model outcome (net budget impact) was recorded. This approach allows the identification of the parameters to which the model is most sensitive, highlighting those with the greatest influence on the results. In the context of this BIA, the DSA evaluated the impact of variations in clinical parameters (e.g., hazard ratios for hospitalization and all-cause mortality, relative risks for renal impairment), cost inputs (e.g., hospitalization costs and cost per renal failure event), and treatment-related assumptions. As shown in Fig. 3, the model was most sensitive to the hospitalization hazard ratio for eplerenone, the variation of which produced the largest change in the net budget impact (approximately ± €4.91 million), confirming that assumptions regarding the effect of eplerenone on hospitalization risk are the principal driver of the results. The next most influential parameters were the all-cause mortality hazard ratio for eplerenone (± €2.55 million) and the hospitalization hazard ratio for canrenone (± €2.48 million), highlighting the central role of clinical event rates in determining the economic outcomes. Renal impairment parameters had an intermediate influence: the baseline risk and weighted event cost of renal impairment each produced a swing of approximately ± €1.70 million, followed by the hospitalization hazard ratio for potassium canrenoate (± €1.20 million). Moderately influential parameters (approximately € 0.6–0.8 million) included the hospitalization hazard ratio for spironolactone, the all-cause mortality hazard ratios for canrenone and spironolactone, and the relative risk of renal impairment for potassium canrenoate/canrenone. Cost-related parameters, including the average CHF/mortality cost per patient and the average hospitalization cost, showed a more moderate impact (€0.32–0.60 million). Parameters related to hyperkalemia (relative risks, baseline rate, and per event cost) produced swings of €0.11–0.31 million, whereas gynecomastia parameters had a negligible influence (± €0.02 million), consistent with its low event rate and low per event cost. Overall, the DSA indicates that the budget impact is primarily driven by clinical effectiveness assumptions, particularly the hazard ratios for hospitalization and all-cause mortality, with renal impairment parameters as a secondary driver and hyperkalemia and gynecomastia playing a minor role. Scenario Analysis: Overlap Between Renal Impairment and Hyperkalemia

To address the potential double counting of hospital costs when both events are captured for the same patient within a single acute episode, a scenario analysis was performed. In this scenario, the hospital (ordinary admission) component of the renal impairment cost—which represents 64.1% of the weighted per-event renal impairment cost under the DRG 316 scheme—was reduced by an overlap fraction, reflecting the share already captured within a concurrent severe hyperkalemia admission. The base case assumes the two events are independent and additive (overlap = 0%), which is conservative because it does not net out any shared hospitalization. Three overlap levels were tested (15%, 20%, and 25%), with 20% as the central estimate derived from the relationship between renal and hyperkalemia events reported in EMPHASIS-HF [10]; the overlap fraction is a derived estimate rather than a directly observed co-occurrence rate, and is therefore evaluated only as a scenario. Applying the overlap correction increased the net budget impact slightly rather than reducing it, because renal impairment costs are higher in the Current scenario (where canrenone and potassium canrenoate dominate) and are therefore reduced by a larger amount in the Current scenario than in the Projected scenario. The 3-year net budget impact rose from + €2.01 million in the base case to + €2.82 million (15% overlap), + €3.10 million (20% overlap), and + €3.37 million (25% overlap). Across all overlap assumptions tested, the net budget impact remained a small net increase (< 0.6% of total expenditure), confirming that the base-case conclusion of substantial budget neutrality is robust to the potential double counting of renal and hyperkalemia hospital events.

Discussion

The present BIA indicates that increasing the use of eplerenone for eligible patients with heart failure in Italy is associated with a modest net increase in healthcare expenditure of €2.01 million (+ 0.34%) for the INHS over a 3-year horizon, a magnitude consistent with substantial budget neutrality. These findings reflect the combined effect of changes in treatment distribution and differences in clinical outcomes across MRAs. The observed economic impact arises from a balance between higher drug acquisition and hospitalization costs associated with greater eplerenone use and partially offsetting reductions in the costs of the displaced comparators, together with a net mortality-related cost increase. Importantly, the net savings observed for the comparators arise primarily from their reduced utilization in the Projected scenario and from the assumed clinical and cost disadvantages of canrenone and potassium canrenoate, rather than from a demonstrated superiority of eplerenone over all comparators on every endpoint. Notably, this pattern also applies to the mortality-related component: although eplerenone was associated with greater mortality-related savings than the comparators individually, the reduced use of spironolactone, canrenone, and potassium canrenoate in the Projected scenario lowered their own mortality-related savings by a larger amount, resulting in a net mortality-related cost increase at the aggregate level. In particular, the NMA estimates indicate that spironolactone is associated with a lower hazard of all-cause hospitalization than eplerenone; the favorable hospitalization-related economics attributable to eplerenone in this model therefore reflect its position relative to canrenone and potassium canrenoate and the redistribution of market share, not a uniform hospitalization advantage over spironolactone. These effects are consistent with the clinical profile of eplerenone, which is associated with lower all-cause mortality compared with spironolactone and a reduced risk of renal impairment, hyperkalemia, and gynecomastia compared with other MRAs, owing to its receptor selectivity.

The differing impact across the three MRAs may reflect both pharmacological differences—eplerenone being selective, spironolactone nonselective, and canrenone and potassium canrenoate less extensively studied—and, importantly, the unequal quality of the underlying evidence. The apparently favorable mortality signal for canrenone and potassium canrenoate is more plausibly explained by the limited and heterogeneous trial data than by a genuine biological advantage. The comparative estimates entering the model should therefore be interpreted with caution.

These results are consistent with evidence from pivotal trials demonstrating the efficacy of eplerenone in reducing cardiovascular mortality and morbidity in patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction [9, 10] as well as with previous European economic evaluations reporting favorable economic profiles when reductions in hospitalizations are considered [30–33].

Limitations

Several limitations should be considered. First, clinical efficacy estimates were derived from an NMA, which, although enabling indirect comparisons, is sensitive to heterogeneity and the quality of included studies. The evidence base for canrenone and potassium canrenoate is particularly weak: the apparent superiority in reducing all-cause mortality (highest P-score in the NMA) was driven by a single small randomized controlled trial with wide credible intervals, resulting in highly uncertain estimates. Similarly, spironolactone ranked highest for reducing all-cause hospitalizations, but this finding was based on two small, heterogeneous trials with inconsistent results, again yielding wide credible intervals and substantial uncertainty [34]. A further consideration concerns the estimation of the eligible population, which combines an incident component (patients developing heart failure within 1 year of a myocardial infarction) with a prevalent component (patients with established chronic HFrEF already treated with MRAs) drawn from independent epidemiological sources. Although these components represent conceptually distinct entities—treatment initiators versus the established treated pool—a limited overlap between them cannot be entirely excluded. This potential overlap is applied identically to both modelled scenarios and to the same total population; as the budget impact is derived from the difference between the Current and Projected scenarios, it does not bias the differential estimate, although it may marginally affect the absolute size of the modelled population. The modelled outcomes should also be considered in relation to prescribing preferences. In Italian practice, MRA choice is influenced by prescriber habits, formularies, and specialty rather than head-to-head evidence. Since the NMA aggregates trials not designed to reflect these patterns, part of the variability across MRAs may stem from systematic error—channelling and indication bias, heterogeneous follow-up, differential trial quality—rather than true treatment effects. This applies to all comparators and is most pronounced for canrenone and potassium canrenoate, based on a single small trial. The projected redistribution should thus be read as a structured scenario, not a prediction of actual prescribing. Furthermore, treatment adherence was assumed at 80%, reflecting national data showing that about 20% of cardiovascular patients have low adherence [15]. Baseline prescribing patterns and disease prevalence were not included, which may reduce the accuracy of projected changes in treatment utilization, as the model does not reflect current differences in clinical practice and disease burden. At the regional level, the analysis uses national average costs, which may not reflect local variations in tariffs, procurement policies, and clinical practice. Regional differences in hospital cost structures, access to specialist care, and adherence to guidelines could alter the actual budget impact. Moreover, the model assumes uniform treatment effectiveness across all settings, which may not hold in real-world practice.

Conclusion

This analysis suggests that broader adoption of eplerenone among eligible patients with heart failure is associated with a modest net increase in overall healthcare expenditure for the INHS over a 3-year horizon (+ €2.01 million; + 0.34%), a magnitude that is small in relative terms and consistent with substantial budget neutrality. This relative impact of + 0.34% corresponds to a net incremental cost of approximately €3.20 per treated patient per year, a figure that illustrates in tangible terms the limited budgetary consequences of increasing eplerenone use. The observed economic impact reflects a combination of treatment redistribution and differences in clinical outcomes, particularly in terms of hospitalization, renal impairment, hyperkalemia, gynecomastia, and mortality.

While greater use of eplerenone is associated with higher drug-acquisition costs, these are partially offset at the system level by reductions in downstream healthcare resource use, although the reduced use of other MRAs in the Projected scenario also lowers their associated mortality-related saving, resulting in a net increase in mortality-related costs. These findings support the potential economic sustainability of a shift in prescribing patterns towards eplerenone within the Italian healthcare setting. However, the results should be interpreted considering the underlying assumptions and data limitations, particularly those related to clinical effectiveness estimates and treatment distribution. Future research incorporating real-world data on prescribing patterns, clinical outcomes, and regional variability would help to further validate and refine these projections.

From a practical standpoint, current ESC guidelines recommend MRAs as a therapeutic class without distinguishing between individual agents. Within the limitations of this analysis, greater use of eplerenone in eligible patients with HFrEF may be achieved at a modest additional cost to the INHS, consistent with substantial budget neutrality, while remaining aligned with guideline-recommended treatment and offering a more favorable selectivity, renal, and adverse event profile relative to other MRAs. Although these findings alone do not support changes to current guideline statements, which would require confirmatory comparative and real-world evidence, they suggest that the potential value of agent-specific positioning within the MRA class warrants further investigation, alongside strategies to improve treatment access and adherence.

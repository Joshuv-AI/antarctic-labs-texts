Global trends in retroperitoneal sarcomas surgery: a bibliometric analysis from 2005 to 2024
Authors: Junxiang Zhang, Kai Deng, Yukai Zheng, Bo Ban, Gan Li, Jie Lian, Yanjun Sun, Xuqi Li, Shufeng Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveRetroperitoneal sarcomas are located in the retroperitoneum, characterized by a low incidence rate but a high degree of aggressiveness. Currently, surgery stands as the only curative treatment. This literature aims to employ a bibliometric analysis to systematically evaluate the research hotspots and trends in retroperitoneal sarcomas surgery.MethodsAuthors retrieved publications related to retroperitoneal sarcomas surgery from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) and PubMed databases spanning the period from 2005 to 2024. Subsequently, R, VOSviewer and CiteSpace software were employed to conduct bibliometric and visual analysis on the included publications.ResultsA total of 689 publications were included from 203 countries/regions and 2678 institutions. The United States had the highest number of publications, and Fdn IRCCS Ist Nazl Tumori is the top research institutions. The most frequently co-cited keywords were “soft tissue sarcoma”, “management” and “survival”. The research themes over the past decade have primarily centered around "management", "prognostic factors" and "inferior vena cava".ConclusionsThis study utilized three widely recognized bibliometric software tools to conduct a bibliometric analysis on retroperitoneal sarcomas surgery. The results emphasize the significance of precise diagnosis, vascular reconstruction and prognostic factors, providing practical information for other scholars to understand key directions and frontier information in this field.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03630-9.

Introduction

Retroperitoneal sarcomas (RPS) are extremely rare tumors located within the retroperitoneum, occurring in fewer than 1 person per 100,000 each year [1]. The origin of RPS is complex, often arising from various soft tissues such as fat, muscle, and nerves, contributing to their high degree of heterogeneity, and encompassing over a hundred histological subtypes [2]. In adults, liposarcoma and leiomyosarcoma are the most common types [3, 4]. The onset of RPS is concealed and lacks characteristic manifestations. Coupled with the growth space provided by the retroperitoneum, the tumor size is often large when patients are diagnosed (median 20 cm) [5]. Due to their strong invasiveness, RPS sometimes involve retroperitoneal organs such as the kidney, colon and pancreas, etc. [6]. Similarly, invasive retroperitoneal sarcomas may also invade major retroperitoneal lumen structures such as the abdominal aorta, inferior vena cava, and ureters, which greatly increases the difficulty and risk of surgery [7, 8].

Surgery is the only curative treatment for RPS [1, 9], but the complex anatomy of the retroperitoneum and the biological heterogeneity pose significant challenges for surgical procedures. When the location is complex, surgical intervention for RPS often necessitates a collaborative effort involving surgeons from general surgery, urology, and gynecology [10–12]. In localized RPS, the standard treatment approach remains achieving a complete R0/R1 resection [13]. However, even with a negative resection margin, the local recurrence rate after surgery still ranges from 22 to 88%. In recent years, several sarcoma centers have adopted extended surgical resections [14, 15], which involve the removal of adjacent uninvolved organs, with the goal of minimizing margin positivity and improving local control as well as patient outcomes. Nevertheless, the currently available data is scarce and insufficient to form definitive recommendations. Histologically based surgical approaches for RPS, which highlight potential patterns of future recurrence, have gained significant acceptance among sarcoma specialists [16]. For instance, in the case of leiomyosarcomas, extensive resection may not be necessary due to the higher risk of distant recurrence compared to local recurrence. Conversely, for liposarcomas, which are prone to local recurrence after surgery, extensive resection can improve the patient's prognosis [15, 17–19]. Overall, the optimal extent of resection remains highly debated and requires further research. Due to the rarity of RPS, research on them is relatively scattered and lacks systematic organization and summation, this issue remains to be resolved.

Bibliometrics is an interdisciplinary field that employs quantitative methods to reveal the characteristics, distribution patterns, growth trends, and internal relationships among publications, as well as to evaluate the research contributions and academic influence of scientists [20]. It enables the analysis and visualization of the growth trends, subject distributions, keyword frequencies, and other aspects of publications within specific fields, thereby revealing the dynamic changes and future trends in the development of disciplines [21]. Despite the continuous publication of high-quality articles in the field of retroperitoneal sarcomas surgery, there is still a lack of analysis using bibliometric methods. Current guidelines are primarily based on evidence from clinical studies. However, they lack a systematic analysis of the shifts in research hotspots within the field. This study can quantitatively demonstrate the changes in research hotspots regarding RPS surgery over the past two decades, aiding in more accurately identifying emerging directions when updating the guidelines. In this study, we utilize R software, VOSviewer, and CiteSpace software to study articles related to RPS surgery in order to deepen our understanding and predict future research hotspots.

Method

Literature sources and search strategies

The WoSCC and PubMed databases were utilized to search for all relevant literature on Feb 8, 2025. The search strategy for the Web of Science Core Collection is as follows:

(TS = (“retroperitoneal sarcoma*”)) AND (((((TS = (“surgery”)) OR TS = (“surgical operation*”)) OR TS = (“surgical treatment*”)) OR TS = (“surgical management*”)) OR TS = (“operation*”)). The search strategy for PubMed is as follows: ("retroperitoneal sarcoma*"[Title/Abstract]) AND ("surgery"[Title/Abstract] OR "surgical operation*"[Title/Abstract] OR "surgical treatment*"[Title/Abstract] OR "surgical management*"[Title/Abstract] OR "operation*"[Title/Abstract]).

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) articles that encompass evaluations of key information such as authors, institutions, publication year, keywords, references; (2) the content of the articles aligns with the core theme of the search terms; and (3) articles published from 2005 to 2024, of the article or review article type, and in English [22].

The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) articles that have not been officially published; (2) duplicate articles; and (3) articles that do not conform to the core research topic.

After screening records from WoSCC and PubMed databases, 1411 articles were imported into NoteExpress. Using its “duplicate check” function, duplicates were removed based on matching authors, titles, and publication years. Subsequently, the remaining articles were imported into CiteSpace for a secondary duplicate verification to ensure no redundancies persisted [23].

Following the removal of duplicate records and the exclusion of irrelevant literature, with the language set to English and the literature type restricted to article and review article, the search strategy was tailored to retrieve publications that met these criteria, specifically those published within the timeframe of January 1, 2005, to December 31, 2024.

All publications were independently evaluated by two researchers to ensure relevance to the research theme. In cases of disagreement, a third researcher was consulted for a final decision on article relevance. The discrepancies between the two researchers were analyzed using Cohen's Kappa analysis, yielding a Kappa coefficient of 0.635, which indicates a substantial agreement. Information extraction was performed on the finally included publications, encompassing details such as publication year, author, country/region, institution, and keywords.

Data analysis

The full record and cited references were exported as plain text from WoSCC and PubMed databases. In this study, the ‘bibliometrix’ package in R 4.2.0 (version 4.3.0), VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), and CiteSpace (version 6.2.R2) were used to conduct visual analysis of the data.

CiteSpace (version 6.2.R2) is a citation visualization analysis software that has gradually evolved within the context of scientometrics and data visualization [24]. Here, Citespace is employed to generate dual-map overlays of journals, enabling the identification of abrupt bursts in keywords or themes over a short period. Furthermore, it utilizes clustering algorithms to group similar research topics, thereby revealing the knowledge structure within the field of RPS surgery, along with highlighting key scholars and classical publications that contribute significantly to the domain.

The bibliometrix package in R 4.2.0 (version 4.3.0) encompasses functionalities ranging from data importation, preprocessing, statistical analysis, visual analysis, to report generation, providing researchers with an efficient and convenient platform for bibliometric data analysis [21]. Through this package, we can explore the relationships between authors and their respective countries/regions, as well as the keywords used in articles. Additionally, it enables us to analyze the evolution of themes over the past two decades.

VOSviewer (version 1.6.20) is an open-source software designed for visualizing and analyzing the relationships between scientific literature, keywords, and authors [25] It presents the analysis results in the form of network maps, showcasing the relationships between literature, keywords, authors, and more. Nodes with the same or similar colors indicate a certain degree of association between them, while the size of a node represents its importance within the network in this research.

After manually screening of literature in the WoSCC and PubMed databases, a total of 983 publications were identified following the removal of duplicates (n = 305) and irrelevant publications (n = 123). Then a total of 843 publications were retrieved between January 1, 2005 to December 31, 2024. After excluding 111 publications due to publication types and 43 publications due to language, we ultimately collected 689 publications including 546 articles and 143 reviews, as shown in Fig. 1. Keyword with citation burst can serve as indicator of current hot topics or emerging trends within a research field. Figure 9A showcases the 20 keywords with the strongest citation bursts. The highest burst intensity is “single institution” (Strength:11.61), reflecting the rarity of retroperitoneal sarcomas. Interestingly, this keyword emerged after 2005 but has been gradually replaced by “multicenter”, underscoring the significance of collaborative research. The incidence of sarcomas is low, and the number of accumulated cases within a given period is relatively limited. Consequently, at the initial stage of research, investigators typically collect cases from within their own institutions. In later stages, multicenter studies are capable of integrating cases from multiple institutions, rapidly expanding the sample size, and meeting the demands of complex studies for larger sample volumes, thereby enabling the derivation of more accurate conclusions. The Sankey diagram illustrates the evolution of research themes, and the 20-year period is split into two stages for analysis (Fig. 9B). From 2005 to 2014, the research themes were diversified and not concentrate on a particular area. Discussion

Based on the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) and PubMed databases, this study represents the first bibliometric analysis focusing on RPS surgery. We retrieved 689 publications from January 1, 2005 to December 31, 2024, including publications from 203 countries/regions and 2678 institutions. To accomplish this study, we utilized the ‘bibliometrix’ package in R 4.2.0 (version 4.3.0), VOSviewer (version1.6.20), and CiteSpace (version 6.2.R2) to delve into the literature, exploring information such as countries/regions, institutions, authors, and keywords. This comprehensive approach allowed us to evaluate and summarize the research progress and advancements within the field.

Overall, the results demonstrate a notable increasing trend in the number of publications. In this field, the United States ranks first, not only accounting for 39.0% of all published articles, but also owing to its deep collaborations with other countries/regions [26]. In 2013, several sarcoma centers across Europe established the "Trans-Atlantic Retroperitoneal Sarcoma Working Group (TARPSWG)", further presenting new opportunities for the advancement of this specialized field [6, 27]. This also explains why despite their moderate publication output, the United Kingdom, Germany, and Italy possess a strong mediator centrality, signifying their pivotal role in facilitating collaborations and driving progress within the field. Contrastingly, the People’s Republic of China ranks sixth in terms of publication output within this field, yet its collaborations with other countries/regions are nearly non-existent. This disparity stems partly from the vast patient population and deepening research endeavors, yet it also underscores the paramount importance of international cooperation. The institution with the highest number of publications in the field under consideration is the Fdn IRCCS Ist Nazl Tumori, situated in Italy. Remarkably, the volume of its publications approaches the combined total of those produced by the second and third most prolific institutions. The institution’s publication output has gradually increased since 2013, reaching a remarkable peak of 16 papers in 2021, and has since stabilized at a consistently high level. A 2016 multicenter study conducted by this institution demonstrated that histological subtype serves as a core predictor of recurrence, necessitating the development of individualized treatment strategies. Its significance lies in systematically quantifying subtype-specific recurrence patterns in RPS and validating the irreplaceable role of specialized centers in managing complex sarcomas, thereby laying a foundational basis for updating clinical guidelines [28]. In 2020, a multicenter, open-label, randomized Phase III trial conducted by this institution and published in The Lancet Oncology revealed that preoperative radiotherapy did not significantly reduce the risk of recurrence in RPS, suggesting that future clinical efforts should prioritize subtype-specific investigations [29]. Significant differences in recurrence risk exist among distinct histological subtypes of RPS, such as liposarcoma and leiomyosarcoma [30]. These disparities have prompted a shift away from uniform treatment protocols toward subtype-specific dynamic surveillance plans and adjuvant therapy strategies. It is the emergence of these high-impact publications that has solidified Fdn IRCCS Ist Nazl Tumori leading position in the field of retroperitoneal sarcoma (RPS) research.

Gronchi Alessandro, affiliated with the Fdn IRCCS Ist Nazl Tumori institution, has made significant contributions to the field of retroperitoneal sarcomas. He has led numerous clinical trials and research endeavors focused on soft tissue sarcomas, achieving notable outcomes particularly in the realm of neoadjuvant chemotherapy and adjuvant radiotherapy [26, 31, 32]. His studies have made substantial contributions to enhancing the survival rates and improving the quality of life for patients with soft tissue sarcomas. As a result, his articles have amassed the highest total citations, reaching a remarkable 1129 times. Through the analysis of first authors, we have discovered some intriguing findings: Over the past decade, a total of 19 authors have published three or more articles, seven of whom are affiliated with institutions in Italy. Dario Callegaro from the Fdn IRCCS Ist Nazl Tumori institution has published 11 articles, focusing on innovative surgical techniques, multimodal therapy optimization, and prognostic prediction model development.

Over two hundred journals have published articles about RPS surgery, with the Annals of Surgical Oncology leading the field by publishing a total of 68 articles. Founded in 1994, this Journal belongs to the JCR Q1 quartile, its primary research concentrates encompasses surgical oncology, novel surgical techniques, clinical trials, and translational medicine, all of which contribute to the advancement and development of surgical oncology research [33, 34]. Among the top ten journals with the highest number of publications, Annals of Surgery boasts the highest impact factor (2024 IF = 7.5). The research content of this journal encompasses clinical management, basic research, and translational medicine, inspiring us to approach our studies on RPS with even greater comprehensiveness.

In this study, the top three co-cited references concentrate on neoadjuvant radiotherapy, patterns of recurrence and crucial prognostic indicators. The recurrence patterns of RPS primarily encompass two main types: local recurrence and distant metastasis, with histological subtypes serving as a crucial determinant. For certain retroperitoneal sarcomas with a high risk of local recurrence, like well-differentiated liposarcomas and G1-2 dedifferentiated liposarcomas, neoadjuvant radiotherapy may contribute to reducing the risk of local recurrence, subsequently prolonging patients' survival [35]. This also emphasizes that neoadjuvant radiotherapy should not be routinely applied to all RPS, yet it does not entirely negate its value. By analyzing highly cited articles and co-cited references, we have unveiled the research hotspots and core issues of RPS surgery.

By analyzing the clusters and timelines of citation references, we can gain insights into the exploration of research frontiers and the evolution of research hotspots. Cluster #0 “recurrence” reveals the thorny problems of retroperitoneal sarcoma, which includes local recurrence and distant recurrence [11]. Generally, recurrence is closely associated with the histological subtype and surgical resection margin. Notably, cluster #0 "recurrence" and cluster #5 "hospital volume" exhibit the same trend, currently, high-volume refers to a hospital that performs more than 10 RPS cases annually. A higher surgical volume implies more abundant clinical experience, which is conducive to achieving negative surgical margins and reducing the postoperative recurrence rate [36]. Cluster #3 “front-line aggressive surgery” significantly contributes to the citation burst, this underscores that, while removing the sarcomas, adjacent organs should be excised even if there is no direct invasion [37–39]. This extensive surgery often includes the anterior colon, posterior kidney, and psoas muscle, with the aim of achieving negative surgical margins whenever possible. Multiple studies have confirmed that aggressive surgery can significantly reduce the 5-year local recurrence rate [11, 40, 41].

Keywords represent the core ideas and themes of articles, and their bursts can reflect the evolution of those themes over time. The research focus on RPS surgery extends beyond mere surgical techniques, also encompassing diagnosis, histological subtypes, and prognostic factors. These aspects are intricately interconnected and complementary to each other. Employing imaging and pathology diagnosis to ascertain tumor location, its degree of invasion into adjacent organs, and histological subtypes, facilitates the tailoring of surgical plans and enhances the likelihood of complete resection [42, 43]. In recent years, pathology diagnosis obtained through needle biopsy has been widely applied. On one hand, this method clarifies histological subtypes and grades, thereby facilitating the formulation of a reasonable surgical plan [44, 45]. On the other hand, the risk of tumor dissemination through the needle tract is minimal, and does not increase the risk of local recurrence. Research on prognostic factors in RPS has remained a sustained focus, with factors significantly associated with reduced recurrence risk currently including negative surgical margins, low-grade tumors, and treatment at high-volume centers. In contrast, the role of adjuvant chemotherapy and radiotherapy remains controversial due to the marked heterogeneity and diverse subtypes of RPS. In future research, efforts should focus on subtype-specific adjuvant therapies, individualized surgical resection margins, and the continuous strengthening of international collaboration [2, 13]. By advancing surgical precision, personalized treatment approaches, and global data integration, an evidence-based framework can be established to facilitate a paradigm shift from “empirical generalized therapy” to “molecularly driven precision medicine” in RPS management.

When designing a surgical plan for RPS patients, preoperative assessment of renal function is critical. During surgery, the kidney is one of the organs that are most frequently resected. A cohort study has confirmed that the risks of acute kidney injury (AKI) and acute renal failure (ARF) increase after kidney resection [19, 35]. This highlights the need for continuous renal function monitoring in postoperative patients and the judicious use of adjuvant nephrotoxic chemotherapy in conjunction with nephrectomy. Additionally, by elucidating patient prognostic factors, adjuvant therapies can be administered during the perioperative period, thereby augmenting the efficacy of surgical interventions [2, 46, 47].

Through analysis, we have identified potential future research hotspots. Achieving a clear resection margin through multi-organ resection has become an established treatment standard for tumors involving adjacent organs. Despite advancements made, vascular invasion by RPS remains a challenge. Interestingly, in the factorial analysis depicted in Fig. 8C, a cluster emerged involving the "inferior vena cava", "leiomyosarcoma", and "surgical management". Leiomyosarcoma is the second most common retroperitoneal sarcoma, which often originates from the large blood vessels in the retroperitoneum, such as the inferior vena cava and renal veins. Therefore, vascular resection and reconstruction are crucial. Fortunately, ongoing studies have consistently demonstrated that vascular reconstruction is safe and improves the R0 resection rate, which has a positive impact on the long-term survival of patients [48, 49].

Although RPS are rare, an increasing number of collaborative groups are conducting research on them, and consensus guidelines are continually being developed [12, 26, 50]. With the development of minimally invasive techniques and equipment, laparoscopic surgery is increasingly being applied in the resection of RPS [51, 52]. Compared to open surgery, laparoscopic surgery can reduce intraoperative trauma and postoperative complications, making it a safe and feasible treatment option. It is recommended for use in RPS with clear diagnosis and well-defined margins.

Complications do not demonstrate high centrality in bibliometrics; however, they cannot be disregarded in clinical practice. Postoperative hemorrhage, anastomotic leakage, and intestinal perforation are common complications that necessitate unplanned reoperation [9]. Multiple studies have demonstrated that neoadjuvant chemotherapy is not associated with increased postoperative complications compared to surgery alone [53, 54].

For clinicians and researchers, future studies could focus on multicenter prospective trials to develop neoadjuvant therapies tailored to histological subtypes. Additionally, it is advisable to match the technical complexity according to hospital tiers, establish collaborations with high-volume centers, and promote the dissemination of complex techniques.

Our study, while offering insights into RPS surgery, has inherent limitations. Firstly, our analysis was confined solely to articles and review articles, potentially leading to the exclusion of other types of publications, ultimately impacting the comprehensiveness of our analytical findings. Secondly, as a quantitative analytical method, bibliometrics offers valuable insights but cannot fully encapsulate the quality of the literature it assesses. Thirdly, this study only searched the WoSCC and PubMed databases, which may result in the exclusion of some relevant literature from the research. Future work will involve integrating multiple databases to enhance global representativeness.

Conclusion

In this study, bibliometric methods were employed to analyze the relevant literature in the field of RPS surgery. Overall, both the quality and quantity of the literature have shown a consistent upward trend. Multicenter prospective clinical trials have substantial implications for evidence-based medicine. The United States is the most productive and influential country in this field. The Fdn IRCCS Ist Nazl Tumori, Harvard University, and UNICANCER are at the core of research in this field. Gronchi Alessandro, from Italy, has made significant contributions to this field, greatly advancing the progress. Research hotspots include preoperative precise diagnosis, prognostic factors, vascular reconstruction and minimally invasive surgery, all of which are closely related to the prognosis of patients. We hope future international, multicenter clinical trials will enroll patients with diverse histological subtypes of RPS to explore the clinical benefits of preoperative radiotherapy. These findings summarize the research trends and hotspots in RPS surgery over the past 20 years, and will lay a foundation for future research endeavors.

The Principle of Limb Reconstruction—“One Walking, Two Lines, and Three Balances”: A Retrospective Analysis of Post‐Traumatic Lower Limb Deformity Correction
Authors: Jiancheng Zang, Fangyuan Wei, Lei Shi, Sihe Qin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe principles of limb reconstruction are crucial for treatment success, but there is no unified standard for complex limb deformities. The aim of this study was to analyze the characteristics of the cases of post‐traumatic lower limb deformity and explore the new principle of limb reconstruction.MethodA retrospective analysis was conducted of 148 patients with post‐traumatic lower limb deformity who underwent surgery from May 1978 to December 2023; 85 were males (57.4%) and 63 were females (42.6%); 65 cases of left side (43.9%), 79 cases of right side(53.4%), and 4 cases were on both sides (2.7%), the average age was 24.64 years (5–69). There were 4 cases suffering hip deformities, 40 cases of femoral deformities, 18 cases from knee, 40 cases from tibiofibular, 93 cases of foot and ankle deformities, and some patients also had two or more types. All patients underwent surgical intervention in an average of 40.5 months (12–96) after injury. According to the evaluation of limb deformities, deformity correction and functional reconstruction with external fixation were implemented, following the principle of “one walking, two lines, and three balances.” The clinical evaluation adopts the criteria of Qinsihe lower limb deformity correction and functional reconstruction.Result148 patients with post‐traumatic lower limb deformities were followed up for 40.9 (12–356) months. The main surgical procedures implemented were tendon lengthening and soft tissue release (84 cases), osteotomy (93 cases), joint fusion (30 cases), and tendon transposition (16 cases); there were multiple surgical procedures in some patients. Among them, 124 cases used external fixators for stress control and 27 cases used internal fixation, while 3 cases used plaster or brace. There were 5 wire reactions postoperatively, which improved after dressing change and oral antibiotics. There were 2 pin infections, which improved by pin removing. No surgical related deep infections occurred, and no surgical related neurovascular damage occurred. At the last follow‐up, all limb deformities were corrected, limb function improved, and the results of treatment was very satisfactory. According to Qinsihe evaluation criteria for lower limb deformities, 74 cases were excellent, 56 cases good, and 18 cases fair, with an excellent and good rate of 87.84%.ConclusionStress control with external fixation is effective, safe, and controllable in correcting and reconstructing post‐traumatic lower limb deformities. The principle of “one walking, two lines, and three balances” plays an important role in the entire process of stress control limb reconstruction.Keywords: External Fixator, Ilizarov Technology, Limb Reconstruction, Post‐traumatic Lower Limb Deformity, Stress Control

Introduction

Post‐traumatic lower limb deformities are common sequelae, generally referring to deformities, disabilities, and functional impairments that are causally related to the original trauma after 1 year of initial injury or wound healing. 1 , 2 There are various manifestations of lower limb deformities, which can involve single or multiple joints, with the ankle and calf being the most common, and which can involve single or multiple tissue injuries, mainly characterized by bone and soft tissue ischemia, defects, joint contracture, motor dysfunction and sensory disorders. It is often stiff, scarred, or mixed. 1

Due to the complexity of the condition and the difficulty of conventional treatment, 3 , 4 some patients suffering from post‐traumatic lower limb deformities have to leave behind limb disabilities or are forced to choose amputation. Microsurgery 5 has been used to reconstruct limb function and has achieved certain results, but the trauma from surgery is relatively large, difficult, and the learning curve is long, making it difficult to master for ordinary doctors. The Ilizarov technique, 6 based Distraction Osteogenesis 7 , 8 and Distraction Histogenesis, 9 , 10 due to its unique advantages of wire installation, elastic fixation, and easy adjustment, plays an increasingly important role in correcting complex lower limb deformities, reconstructing the function of patients with disability. 11 , 12 , 13 , 14

However, limb reconstruction surgery involves an understanding of limbs and limb reconstruction, as well as specialized knowledge in multiple disciplines such as limb development, genetics, biomechanics, mechanic biology, orthopedics, and psychology. 15 Implementing planned surgical procedures is very important, and perioperative management is also a significant link that directly affects the treatment effect. 16 How to design appropriate treatment plans for complex patients, achieve the maximum effect of limb deformity correction and functional reconstruction, and what principles should be followed are the important issues we have been thinking about. 17 Based on the experiences in orthopedic surgery over 40 years, Professor Qin Sihe proposed the principle of limb reconstruction “one walking, two lines, and three balances,” which played an important role in reconstructing the function of post‐traumatic limb deformities and achieving good results. 16 , 18

In this study, we analyzed the characteristics of the patients and treatment process of post‐traumatic lower limb deformity correction, The aim of our study was to: (i) analyze the characteristics of the patients suffering from post‐traumatic limb deformities; (ii) explore the new principle of limb reconstruction“one walking, two lines, and three balances”; and (iii) provide reference for the promotion and application of limb reconstruction surgery.

Participants

We retrospectively reviewed the medical data of patients who met the conditions above from May 1978 to December 2023 in the Qinsihe Orthopedic Database.

A total of 148 patients were enrolled, including 85 males and 63 females; 65 cases were on the left side, 79 cases were on the right side, and 4 cases were on both sides. At the time of surgery, the minimum age is 5 years, the maximum is 69 years, and the average age is 24.64 years. There were 4 cases of hip joint deformities, 40 cases of femoral deformities, 18 cases of knee joint deformities, 40 cases of tibiofibular deformities, and 93 cases of ankle deformities after trauma. Some patients also had two or more types of deformities. All patients underwent surgical intervention from 12 to 96 months after injury, with an average of 40.5 months.

Principle and Method for Lower Limb Deformity Correction

Stress control is the foundation for correcting limb deformities, with a focus on analyzing biomechanical abnormalities related to deformities, including bone deformities, bone instability, soft tissue relaxation, and soft tissue contractures (i.e., balance between bone and soft tissue). 19 Develop different surgical plans based on the different types of limb deformities after trauma. The main surgical methods for post‐traumatic lower limb deformities include surgery of joint fusion, deformity osteotomy, limb lengthening, soft tissue release, tendon lengthening, and tendon transfer. Usually, external fixators are applied after orthopedic surgery.

External fixators are the main tool for implementing stress control. 20 The design configuration and pin requirements of external fixators are based on the general principles for limb deformities in “Lower Limb Deformities: Deformity Correction and Function Reconstruction”. Deformities can be corrected acutely in one stage using a hybrid external fixator, plating or nailing; for those with deformities that are difficult to correct or cannot be corrected acutely, Ilizarov external fixators are used to gradually correct, ultimately achieving the goal of limb deformity correction and functional reconstruction.

The principle of “one walking, two lines, and three balances” for limb reconstruction should focus on restoring the patient's standing and walking function or improving pathological gait in clinical practice. The surgical method should meet the requirements of “treating while walking, walking during treatment,” which is the most important, so‐called “one walking.” “Two lines” means osteotomy or distraction to restore the mechanical axis of the lower limb and the joint lines of the hip, knee, and ankle. “Three balances” refers to static and dynamic balance, which is bone stability and soft tissue balance, balance between the stiffness of instruments and bone healing strength, and pay attention to the balance between limb deformities, deformity correction, and psychological state of the patient.

Postoperative Treatment

The procedures included postoperative limb elevation of 30°, routine application of antibiotics, and retention of urinary catheter for 1–2 days. The patients were encouraged to engage in leg lifting activities in bed in a supine or prone position. Bandaged gauze was removed on post‐operation day 4, and X‐rays were taken to observe and understand the position of bone, joint, wire, and fixator.

Those who with hybrid fixators do not need to adjust, or only make minor adjustments. Those who use Ilizarov fixators can continue to adjust and correct residual deformities after surgery. Attention should be paid to the force line and joint line of the lower limb, and the speed should be adjusted dynamically based on soft tissue tension and the patient's tolerance level. If there was no exudation in the pin or wire passage, they were generally exposed and did not require special care.

The patients can walk on the ground with walker or crutches and insoles in a partial weight‐bearing 5–7 days post‐operatively. Gradually transition from partial weight‐bearing to full weight‐bearing walking and joint activity exercises. The external fixator can be locked when satisfying the right position. Practice on the ground two to three times a day, then raise the affected limb. The balance between the stiffness of instruments and the strength of bone healing should be reviewed by X‐ray each 4 weeks until healing completely after surgery. Appropriate physical therapy and rehabilitation exercise can promote the resolution of limb swelling and the recovery of muscle strength.

Some of the wires or rods of external fixation were removed to weaken the fixation strength and gradually transition from strong fixation to stable fixation usually based on bone healing. After removing the external fixator, the plaster and the orthotic device should be applied. The patient wears orthotics and walks with full load, gradually transitioning to wearing appropriate soft shoes after 4–8 weeks, also considering the balance transition of biomechanics.

Routine follow‐up every 6 months, 1 year, and 2 years should be ensured mainly to observe limb morphology and function, regardless of the recurrence of deformities, complications and treatment, weight‐bearing walking, and joint function status.

Medical and nursing staff should pay attention to specialized education on external fixation and limb reconstruction for patients suffering from limb deformity and who have underwent reconstructive operation, conduct timely psychological evaluation and intervention, avoid negative attitudes, and enable patients to enjoy the entire process of limb reconstruction.

The main surgical procedures implemented were tendon lengthening and soft tissue release (84 cases), osteotomy (93 cases), joint fusion (30 cases), and tendon transposition (16 cases). In some patients, there were multiple surgical procedures during the first stage of surgery. Among them, external fixators were used in 124 cases for stress control (including 116 with Ilizarov external fixation, and 8 with hybrid fixation), and 27 cases used internal fixation, while 3 cases used plaster or brace.

Complications

Five pin reactions were recorded in the external fixation frame, which improved after dressing change and oral antibiotics. There were two pin infections, which improved after removing the pin, draining, and dressing. No surgical related deep infections or neurovascular damage occurred.

A 72‐year‐old patient with bilateral lower limb deformities after trauma developed deep vein thrombosis in the lower limbs after surgery. His condition improved after treatment with antithrombotic and anticoagulant agents, and no complications such as skin necrosis or soft tissue ulcers occurred.

Evaluation Scoring

At the last follow‐up, all limb deformities were corrected, limb function improved, and the treatment was very satisfactory. The evaluation criteria for deformity correction and functional reconstruction of lower limb deformities were applied, with 74 cases being excellent, 56 cases being good, and 18 cases fair, with an excellent and good rate of 87.84%.

Discussion

Excellent Outcomes Are the Result of Standardized Surgical Techniques and Thoughtful Perioperative Management

With the improvement in primary treatment for limb trauma, “trauma diseases” gradually decrease, but once post‐traumatic deformities occur, the condition becomes increasingly complex. Patients with post‐traumatic sequelae in this group exhibit complex conditions such as bone and soft tissue ischemia and defects, secondary infections, skin scars, motor dysfunction and sensory disorders. Conventional treatment concepts cannot effectively reconstruct the morphology and function of limbs.

Wu et al. 5 achieved good results of limb function reconstruction with microsurgery, but the trauma from surgery is relatively large, difficult, and the learning curve is long, making it difficult for ordinary doctors to master. Rak 21 confirmed that the arthroscopic‐assisted subtalar arthrodesis is a successful, reliable method leading to stable arthrodesis, but it requires special post‐taumatic indications. The patient with post‐traumatic instability of the first metatarsophalangeal joint be treated with some novel surgical technique of capsular reconstruction, can return to the full weight‐bearing in only 2 months of rehab, 22 which may have a significant impact on daily life. There are many different voices for complex cases of post‐traumatic sequelae, those surgeons experienced, those patients with severe deformities, are waiting for appropriate treatment methods and suitable principles for limb reconstruction.

The stress control strategy with external fixation for treating limb deformities has expanded the indications for post‐traumatic limb deformity, overturned many traditional orthopedic concepts, in which external fixation may be unstable, and achieved satisfactory therapeutic effects. The experience of correcting limb deformities after trauma has laid the foundation for the limb reconstruction principle of “one walking, two lines, and three balances.”

For patients with ischemic muscle contracture and stiff clubfoot deformity, muscle contracture and ankle joint stiffness are the main problems. 23 Surgical methods include soft tissue release and tendon lengthening, as well as osteotomy for deformity correction. Residual deformities are left to be gradually distracted and corrected by Ilizarov external fixation.

For patients with nerve injury present lower limb dynamic imbalance, taking the example of foot‐drop deformity due to common peroneal nerve injury. 24 , 25 When the patient with foot‐ankle deformity is relatively mild, tendon transposition can be implemented to establish a new balance. When the patients are prone to fixed deformities of the foot and ankle, usually need to follow by secondary bony surgery or simultaneous tendon transfer surgery.

For patients, combined joint contracture and extensive skin scar, incision release, and some surgeries pose a risk of soft tissue damage. The application of Ilizarov technique to slowly stretch and regenerate scar tissue has been proven to be a minimally invasive, safe, and reliable method. 26

For patients with post‐traumatic knee joint fiber stiffness, 27 if there is extensive contracture on the anterior knee skin, the basic conditions for classical surgical release to improve knee flexion are lost, or if knee joint release surgery 28 is performed without satisfactory results, the efficacy of surgical release alone is difficult to determine, which is the indication for Ilizarov technology.

For patients with lower limb deformities with combined sensory disorders, the application of external fixation should control the limbs in the “corrective position,” that is, to achieve the maximum load area, and new static and dynamic balance. While correcting the deformity, repair ulcers related to sensory dysfunction, this kind of experiences as to “external fixation treatment for lower limb deformities of spina bifida”. 29

With the application of external fixation, more and more doctors are implementing this technology in clinical practice. Some deformities can be corrected, but satisfactory therapeutic effects cannot be achieved, often manifested as long‐time with external fixation, long‐bone healing time, osteoporosis, joint stiffness, multiple complications, the possibility of multiple surgeries, poor patient satisfaction, and becoming a bottleneck in the promotion and application of limb reconstruction.

“One Walking, Two Lines, and Three Balances” Is the Basic Principle of Stress Control for Limb Reconstruction

The key to correcting lower limb deformities is walking on the ground early, adjusting the stress balance between tissue healing and instruments in a timely manner. During the process of deformity correction, attention should be paid to correcting bone deformities to achieve bone stability, while considering achieving soft tissue balance through tendon contracture and tendon transfer. External fixators provide a foundation for early weight‐bearing and can adjust fixation stiffness as bone healing progresses. The sequential application of external fixation, plaster, or brace is the main manifestation of stress control. Fully understanding and leveraging the significant role of orthotics in stress control, consolidating therapeutic effects, and improving functionality can greatly enhance the overall effectiveness of treatment.

The evaluation of post‐traumatic limb deformities focuses on a holistic perspective

The evaluation of post‐traumatic limb deformities focuses on a holistic perspective

Deformity assessment is the basic prerequisite for deformity correction. The classification of post‐traumatic lower limb deformities comprehensively evaluates the preoperative limb deformities from different dimensions such as the nature of the deformity, degree of functional disability, problems with bone and soft tissue, location of lower limb deformities, degree of deformity, and so on. Summarizing the data of this case series, post‐traumatic lower limb deformities belong to skeletal problems, soft tissue problems, or composite problems; the location and degree of deformity, as well as the degree of lower limb dysfunction, are the main contents of evaluation and important basis for implementing stress control.

The “two lines” refer to the “mechanical line of lower limb weight‐bearing” and the “joint line” of the hip, knee, and ankle. 30 , 31 The bone marking, osteotomy design, and final deformity correction of local deformities must meet the request of the above two lines, simplifying previous principles of orthopedic surgery, and paying more attention to the overall force line and joint line of the limbs, focusing on the reconstruction of limb function.

3Walking is the foundation of limb reconstruction

Walking is the foundation of limb reconstruction

The development of surgical plans and the selection of surgical methods should meet the needs of early ground movement and weight‐bearing, and “treat while walking, walk during treatment,” which requires a combination of external and internal fixation, assistive devices, and exercise therapy.

Walking is the best way for therapy and rehabilitation. 32 During walking, joint movement and muscle “pump” function promote blood circulation and lymphatic circulation in the lesion, as well as bone healing and remodeling at the osteotomy site. 33 With the assistance of walker, double crutches, single crutches, and braces, getting out of bed and walking early to prevent and treat osteoporosis is a basic requirement of orthopedic surgery and a fundamental demand for patients seeking treatment. Well‐designed external fixation provides a good support for early weight‐bearing walking on the ground. Group exercise post‐operatively, in which patients encourage each other, overcome the fear caused by surgery, share the results of treatment, and increase confidence in rehabilitation exercise, is the source of happiness of limb reconstruction.

4Balance is an important component

Balance is an important component

Iliarov technology as a whole follows the concept of “balance.” During the process of deformity correction, attention should be paid first to correct bone deformities, maintaining bone stability, and achieving soft tissue balance through tendon lengthening, tendon transfer, etc. 34 External fixation should gradually reduce fixation stiffness with bone healing to avoid stress shielding that hinders bone and soft tissue healing. 35 , 36 Physical and mental balance is the process of correcting limb deformities while also paying attention to the patient's psychological reconstruction.

5Comprehensive evaluation of the patients

Comprehensive evaluation of the patients

Postoperative evaluation criteria for lower limb deformity correction and functional reconstruction are different from the functional evaluation of a single joint in the past. It is based on the evaluation including degree of deformity correction (including imaging evaluation), muscle strength, motor and walking function, patient's self perception of surgical effects, complications and problems, and other aspects. The formulation of follow‐up standards is in line with the principle of “one walking, two lines, and three balances,” which involves surgical plans, the implementation of surgery, postoperative functional exercise, functional rehabilitation, and physical and mental balance, making them suitable evaluation standards for lower limb deformities and disabilities. 37

Strengths and Limitations

External fixation stress control plays an important role in the treatment of post‐traumatic limb deformities. The data from this study indicate that safe, reliable, minimally invasive, and controllable clinical outcomes can be obtained with minimal complications and high patient satisfaction. The selected cases of post‐traumatic lower limb deformities in this study were all from a single center, with a time span of more than 40 years. There are also several limitations. First, this study was a retrospective study and a non‐randomized controlled study, in which self‐made evaluation criteria for lower limb deformity and disability treatment may have bias in clinical outcomes. This study did not specifically describe the deformities and details of a particular case, but only summarized the overall strategies and rules for such kinds of diseases and sequelae. The post‐operative evaluation criteria were also based on this principle, focusing on limb function and balance, as well as physical and mental balance. The latter one is an important aspect that cannot be ignored for patients with limb deformities. One of the secrets to achieving good results is to pay attention to adjusting the patient's mentality while correcting limb deformities so that they can face life optimistically; however, this is not the focus of this article and requires further research.

Conclusion

This study indicated scars are not contraindications for external fixation applications, infections are not contraindications for limb reconstruction, motor loss and sensory impairment are not contraindications for limb reconstruction, and multi‐joint deformities and osteoporosis are not contraindications for limb reconstruction. As long as appropriate stress is applied to post‐traumatic limb deformities, correction of deformities and functional reconstruction can be achieved, and external fixation is an effective tool and important means of implementing stress control. The main aspects of efficacy control and risk avoidance are the clinical doctor's mastery of the concept of natural reconstruction, familiarity with external fixation instruments, perception and classification of limb deformities, as well as strict management and rehabilitation procedures during the perioperative period. The principle of “one walking, two lines, and three balances” plays an important role in the entire process of stress‐controlled limb reconstruction.

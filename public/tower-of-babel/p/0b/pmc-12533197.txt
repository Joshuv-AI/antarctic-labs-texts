Radix Scrophulariae regulates proliferation, apoptosis, and autophagy of rat thyroid cells via the MST1/Hippo signaling pathway
Authors: Ning ZHANG, Xu LU, Jia-Xin He, Tao YE
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study investigated the therapeutic effects of Radix Scrophulariae (RS) extract on hyperthyroidism and the associated mechanisms. A hyperthyroid cell model was established using thyrotropin receptor antibody and levothyroxine sodium tablets. FRTL-5 rat thyroid cells were treated with varying concentrations of RS-containing serum. Protein expression levels of Bcl-2, Caspase-3, PCNA, Cyclin D1, MST1, LC3-II/I, and ATG5 were assessed by Western blotting to determine the optimal concentration. Subsequent experiments included RS treatment with or without MST1 overexpression or the Hippo pathway inhibitor XMU-MP-1. Transmission electron microscopy was used to visualize secretory vesicles, and immunofluorescence analysis was performed to detect thyroid-stimulating hormone receptor (TSHR) expression. Protein levels of MST1, p-LATS1, p-YAP, PCNA, Cyclin D1, Bcl-2, Caspase-3, LC3-II/I, and ATG5 were further quantified by Western blotting. The hyperthyroid model exhibited elevated expression of TSHR, Bcl-2, PCNA, and Cyclin D1 (P < 0.05), and reduced levels of MST1, p-LATS1, p-YAP, Caspase-3, LC3-II/I, and ATG5 (P < 0.05). Secretory vesicles were rarely observed. Treatment with RS-containing serum significantly downregulated TSHR, Bcl-2, PCNA, and Cyclin D1 expression (P < 0.05), and upregulated MST1, p-LATS1, p-YAP, Caspase-3, LC3-II/I, and ATG5 (P < 0.05), with abundant bilayer membrane vesicles observed. The therapeutic effects of RS were attenuated by MST1 overexpression but were restored by co-treatment with XMU-MP-1 (P < 0.05). RS extract may attenuate hyperthyroidism by suppressing excessive thyroid cell proliferation, enhancing apoptosis and autophagy, and activating the MST1/Hippo signaling pathway in FRTL-5 cells.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-20072-z.Keywords: Radix Scrophulariae, Levothyroxine sodium tablets (LST), FRTL-5 cells, MST1/Hippo signaling, Proliferation, Apoptosis, AutophagySubject terms: Endocrinology, Endocrine system and metabolic diseases

Introduction

Hyperthyroidism is defined by the excessive production and release of thyroid hormones, resulting in increased metabolic activity and enhanced sympathetic nervous system stimulation. Clinical manifestations include palpitations, excessive sweating, increased appetite, frequent bowel movements, and weight loss. The disease course is typically prolonged and challenging to manage, predominantly due to its autoimmune etiology. Notably, more than 80% of hyperthyroidism cases are attributed to Graves’ disease (GD), an autoimmune disorder that targets the thyroid gland1. Although hyperthyroidism can arise from various etiologies, including thyroid nodules, thyroiditis, or excessive iodine intake, the present study focuses specifically on autoimmune hyperthyroidism induced by thyrotropin receptor antibodies (TRAb). Therefore, the current findings are confined to this subtype and may not be extrapolated to other etiologies of hyperthyroidism.

TRAb are considered the principal autoantibodies in GD. These antibodies bind to the thyroid-stimulating hormone receptor (TSHR) on the surface of thyroid follicular epithelial cells, mimicking the effect of thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH). This interaction stimulates accelerated growth and function of thyroid cells. In contrast, TSH secretion is normally regulated by a negative feedback loop within the hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid axis. Circulating thyroid hormone levels inhibit TSH release via the pituitary gland, independent of TRAb production. However, due to persistent TRAb activity, thyroid cells continue to proliferate and secrete excessive thyroid hormones, resulting in clinical features such as diffuse goiter and thyrotoxicosis, which characterize hyperthyroidism2.

Beyond the Hippo signaling pathway, other immune-associated cascades such as phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase/protein kinase B (PI3K/Akt) and nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) have been implicated in the pathogenesis of autoimmune thyroid diseases. These pathways regulate T cell activation, cytokine production, and B cell survival, all of which contribute to the initiation and maintenance of GD. Previous studies have demonstrated that dysregulated PI3K/Akt and NF-κB signaling promote thyroid follicular cell hyperplasia, chronic inflammation, and persistent autoantibody production in patients with GD3,4.

Radix Scrophulariae (RS), the dried root of Scrophularia ningpoensis Hemsl. (Scrophulariaceae), has been used in traditional Chinese medicine. Phytochemical analyses have identified terpenes, phenylpropanoids, organic acids, essential oils, steroids, sugars, flavonoids, alkaloids, and phenols in RS, with sesquiterpenes and phenylpropanoids constituting the major bioactive ingredients5. Prior research has demonstrated the therapeutic potential of RS in hyperthyroidism management6, although its mechanism of action remains incompletely understood.

The Hippo signaling pathway is an evolutionarily conserved cascade that critically regulates cell proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis. In mammals, the pathway is primarily mediated by a kinase cascade composed of MST1/2 and LATS1/2, which interact through conserved domains to activate downstream effectors. MST1 and MST2 are highly expressed in immune organs, including the thymus, spleen, and lymph nodes, suggesting a potential role in immune cell homeostasis and migration7. In 2012, independent research groups from France and Germany reported a familial immunodeficiency syndrome caused by loss-of-function mutations in the MST1 gene. Affected individuals exhibited lymphopenia, neutropenia, and increased susceptibility to bacterial, viral, and fungal infections, including mucocutaneous candidiasis, abscess formation, and granuloma development. These immunodeficient phenotypes were frequently accompanied by autoimmune manifestations8,9, highlighting the immunoregulatory role of MST1/2 kinases in human health.

Building on these findings, our previous animal studies demonstrated that RS ameliorates hyperthyroidism by modulating the MST1/Hippo signaling pathway10. However, the specific cellular mechanisms require further elucidation, warranting additional in vitro investigation.

Therefore, the present study aimed to clarify the regulatory effects of RS on autoimmune hyperthyroidism through the MST1/Hippo pathway. A cellular model was established using TRAb and levothyroxine sodium tablets (LST). FRTL-5 rat thyroid cells were treated with RS-containing serum, and the experimental design incorporated MST1 overexpression and pharmacological inhibition of the Hippo pathway with XMU-MP-1. This study provides new in vitro evidence that RS may alleviate hyperthyroidism by regulating cell proliferation, apoptosis, and autophagy via activation of the MST1/Hippo pathway, contributing to the theoretical basis for RS-based therapies.

Materials and methods

Drugs

RS decoction pieces were obtained from Tianjin Shengshi Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. (Batch No. XG200023001), and the botanical source was authenticated as Scrophularia ningpoensis Hemsl. by Chief Pharmacist Chen Xiumin, Department of Pharmacy, First Affiliated Hospital of Guizhou University of Chinese Medicine. The RS decoction extract was prepared according to a previously reported method11.

Cells and animals

The rat thyroid follicular epithelial cell line FRTL-5 was purchased from Suzhou Beina Chuanglian Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Cat. No. 340169) and maintained in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 incubator.

Male Sprague–Dawley (SD) rats, specific pathogen-free (SPF) grade, weighing 180–220 g and aged 6–8 weeks, were obtained from Hunan STA Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd. (License No. SCXK(Xiang)2019-0004). Animals were housed at 20–26 °C, 40–70% humidity, under a 12-h light/dark cycle, with ad libitum access to standard chow and water. All animal procedures were performed in accordance with the ARRIVE guidelines and approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Jiangxi Zhonghong Boyuan Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Approval No. 2021062701).

Reagents and equipment

LST were purchased from Merck KGaA. XMU-MP-1 (Cat. No. S83334) was obtained from Selleck (Shanghai Blue Wood Chemical Co., Ltd., China). HiScript II Q RT SuperMix for qPCR (+ gDNA wiper) (R223-01) and ChamQ Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Q711-02) were supplied by Vazyme Biotech Co., Ltd. (Nanjing, China). The primary antibody against TSHR (1:100, OM197704) was purchased from OmnimAbs Inc. (USA). Cy3 Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG (H + L) (AS007) was obtained from ABclonal (USA). RIPA cell lysis buffer (C1053) was provided by Pulilai Gene Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). Primary antibodies—rabbit anti-TSHR (1:500, ab218108), Bcl-2 (1:500, ab194583), Caspase-3 (1:500, ab184787), PCNA (1:500, ab29), cyclin D1 (1:500, ab134175), MST1 (1:500, ab317410), LC3-II (1:500, ab192890), ATG5 (1:500, ab108327), and p-YAP (1:500, ab254343)—were obtained from Abcam Inc. (Cambridge, MA, USA). The rabbit anti-p-LATS1 antibody (1:500, AF8163) was purchased from Affinity Biosciences (USA). Antibodies against β-actin (1:2000, TA-09) and the secondary antibody (1:2000, ZB-2305) were from SolelyBio (China).

Preparation of RS-containing serum

After one week of acclimation, rats were randomly assigned to either a control group or an RS-containing serum group. Rats in the RS-containing serum group (n = 15) received RS decoction extract by intragastric administration at a dose of 1.35 g/kg (10 mL/kg) once daily, whereas control rats (n = 10) received an equivalent volume of normal saline for 15 consecutive days. At the end of the experiment, rats were euthanized by intraperitoneal injection of 1% pentobarbital sodium (45 mg/kg). Blood was collected from the abdominal aorta and serum was separated aseptically.

Establishment of in vitro hyperthyroidism model and grouping

FRTL-5 cells were seeded into 6-well plates at a density of 2 × 105 cells per well and divided into five groups: control, model, model + low, model + medium, and model + high. Control group cells were treated with 15% blank rat serum. Other groups were pre-treated with 20 μg/mL TRAb and 0.4 μg/mL LST suspension for 24 h, then treated as follows: Model group: 15% blank rat serum; Model + low: 5% RS-containing rat serum + 10% blank rat serum; Model + medium: 10% RS-containing rat serum + 5% blank rat serum; Model + high: 15% RS-containing rat serum. After 48 h of incubation, cells were harvested for further analysis.

Transfection and grouping

For MST1 overexpression, FRTL-5 cells were transfected with an MST1 overexpression vector (OE) using 5 μL Lipofectamine 3000 and incubated for 48 h; a blank vector (OE NC) served as a negative control (NC). After transfection, cells were collected for verification. Seven groups were established: Control, Model, Model + serum, Model + serum + OE NC, Model + serum + OE, Model + serum + OE NC + XMU-MP-1, and Model + serum + OE + XMU-MP-1.

Cells in the control group received 15% blank rat serum. As described, FRTL-5 cells were treated with TRAb (1 μg/mL) for 24 h to establish the hyperthyroidism model. Model and Model + serum groups were treated with 15% blank rat serum and 15% RS-containing rat serum, respectively. Model + serum + OE NC and Model + serum + OE groups were treated with 15% RS-containing rat serum with OE NC and OE, respectively. Model + serum + OE NC + XMU-MP-1 and Model + serum + OE + XMU-MP-1 groups were treated with 15% RS-containing rat serum and 10 μM XMU-MP-1 in the presence of OE NC and OE, respectively.

Western blot assay

Proteins were extracted and quantified using the BCA method (E-BC-K318-M, Elabscience, USA), separated by SDS-PAGE, and transferred onto PVDF membranes. Membranes were blocked with 5% BSA and incubated with primary antibodies against TSHR (1:500), Bcl-2 (1:500), Caspase-3 (1:500), PCNA (1:500), cyclin D1 (1:500), MST1 (1:500), LC3-II (1:500), ATG5 (1:500), and β-actin (1:2000) overnight at 4 °C. Following primary incubation, membranes were incubated with the secondary antibody (1:2000, TA-09, SolelyBio, China) for 1.5 h at room temperature. Signals were developed using ECL reagent and protein bands were quantified with ImageJ software (version 1.8.0, NIH, USA). All Western blot images were cropped from different parts of the same gel; full-length, uncropped blots are provided in the Supplementary Information (section: WB raw bands). No splicing between different gels was performed unless explicitly indicated. Where lanes from different gels were combined, clear dividing lines were included and such processing is described in the respective figure legends.

Real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR) analysis

Total RNA was extracted from cells using TRIzol reagent (CW0580S, CWBIO, Beijing, China) and quantified with a NanoPhotometer (NP80, Implen, Germany). Complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized from the extracted RNA using the HiScript II Q RT SuperMix for qPCR (+ gDNA wiper) kit (R223-01, Vazyme, China). PCR amplification was performed with ChamQ Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Q711-02, Vazyme, China). The reaction mixture contained RNase-free dH2O (9.5 μL), cDNA (1 μL), upstream primer (1 μL), downstream primer (1 μL), and 2 × SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (12.5 μL). Thermal cycling was conducted as follows: pre-denaturation at 95 °C for 10 min; denaturation at 95 °C for 10 s; annealing at 58 °C for 30 s; extension at 72 °C for 30 s, for a total of 40 cycles. Relative mRNA expression levels were calculated using the 2⁻ΔΔCt method, with normalization to β-actin expression. Primer sequences are provided in Table 1.

Immunofluorescence assay

Cells were washed three times with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde. Permeabilization was achieved with 0.5% Triton X-100 in PBS at room temperature for 20 min, followed by blocking with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) at 37 °C for 30 min. Cells were incubated with the primary antibody against TSHR (1:100) at 37 °C for 3 h. After washing, the fluorescent secondary antibody Cy3 Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG (H + L) (1:200, AS007, ABclonal, USA) was applied at 37 °C for 30 min in the dark. Nuclei were stained with DAPI at 37 °C for 5 min in the dark. Coverslips were mounted using 50% glycerol, and images were acquired with a fluorescence microscope (OLYMPUS, Japan).

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) of secretory vesicles.

To examine the structure of secretory vesicles in the Golgi apparatus of FRTL-5 cells, cells were centrifuged at 1000 rpm for 3 min at 25 °C and fixed in glutaraldehyde at 4 °C for 24 h. Following PBS washes, samples were post-fixed with osmium tetroxide at 4 °C for 2 h and rinsed again with PBS. Cells were dehydrated by sequential incubation in 50% acetone at 4 °C for 15 min; 70% acetone containing 2% uranyl acetate at 4 °C overnight; 90% acetone at 4 °C for 10 min; and 100% acetone for 15 min, repeated twice. For infiltration, cells were treated with epoxypropane for 15 min and then with an epoxypropane-resin mixture (1:1) at 37 °C for 1 h, repeated twice. After embedding, ultrathin sections were prepared and double-stained with lead citrate and uranyl acetate. Sections were observed using TEM.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as the mean ± SD. Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 7.0 software (GraphPad Software, USA). One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to assess group differences. A p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

RS inhibits the expression of cyclin D1 in FRTL-5 cells

Cyclin D1 expression was significantly increased in the model group compared to the control group (P < 0.05). Treatment with RS-containing serum significantly downregulated Cyclin D1 expression in a dose-dependent manner (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1).

The expression level of proliferation-related proteins determined by Western blotting (*P < 0.05 vs. Control, #P < 0.05 vs. Model, &P < 0.05 vs. Model + Low).

RS suppresses Bcl-2 expression in FRTL-5 cells

To investigate the effect of RS on apoptosis, Bcl-2 protein levels were measured by Western blot. Bcl-2 expression was significantly elevated in the model group compared to the control group (P < 0.05). RS-containing serum significantly reduced Bcl-2 expression in a dose-dependent manner (P < 0.05) (Fig. 2). Based on these results, the high-dose RS-containing serum was selected for subsequent experiments.

Bcl-2 expression was measured by Western blotting (*P < 0.05 vs. Control, #P < 0.05 vs. Model, &P < 0.05 vs. Model + Low).

RS promotes the expression of LC3-II/I and ATG 5 in FRTL-5 cells

To investigate the effect of RS on autophagy, LC3-II/I and ATG5 protein levels were analyzed. Compared to the control group, LC3-II/I and ATG5 expression were significantly downregulated in the model group (P < 0.05). In contrast, RS-containing serum (Model + Low group) significantly upregulated LC3-II/I and ATG5 expression relative to the model group (P < 0.05) (Fig. 3).

Autophagy-related protein expression determined by Western blotting (*P < 0.05 vs. Control, #P < 0.05 vs. Model, &P < 0.05 vs. Model + Low).

RS elevates MST1 expression in FRTL-5 cells

MST1 protein levels were assessed by Western blot. MST1 expression was significantly reduced in the model group relative to the control (P < 0.05), whereas RS-containing serum restored MST1 expression in a dose-dependent manner (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4).

MST1 protein expression measured by Western blotting (*P < 0.05 vs. Control, #P < 0.05 vs. Model, &P < 0.05 vs. Model + Low).

Identification of MST1 overexpression in FRTL-5 cells

RT-PCR analysis confirmed that MST1 mRNA was significantly upregulated in FRTL-5 cells transfected with the OE compared to the NC and the control group (P < 0.05), validating the successful establishment of MST1-overexpressing cells (Fig. 5).

MST1 gene expression in FRTL-5 cells as determined by RT-PCR (*P < 0.05 vs. NC, #P < 0.05 vs Control).

Effect of the MST1/Hippo pathway on MST1, p-LATS1, and p-YAP levels in FRTL-5 cells

The effects of RS on the MST1/Hippo pathway were evaluated by Western blot analysis of MST1, p-LATS1, and p-YAP protein levels. Compared to control, all three proteins were significantly downregulated in the model group (P < 0.05); this reduction was reversed by RS-containing serum (P < 0.05). Moreover, MST1, p-LATS1, and p-YAP expression were significantly higher in the Model + serum + OE group than in the Model + serum and Model + serum + OE NC groups (P < 0.05). XMU-MP-1 treatment in the Model + serum + OE NC + XMU-MP-1 group suppressed this elevation (P < 0.05), whereas MST1, p-LATS1, and p-YAP expression were upregulated again in the Model + serum + OE + XMU-MP-1 group (P < 0.05) (Fig. 6).

Western blot analysis of MST1, p-LATS1, and p-YAP protein expression (*P < 0.05 vs. Control, #P < 0.05 vs. Model, &P < 0.05 vs. Model + serum, @P < 0.05 vs. Model + serum + OE NC, %P < 0.05 vs. Model + serum + OE, !P < 0.05 vs. Model + serum + OE NC + XMU-MP-1).

Effect of the MST1/Hippo pathway on TSHR in FRTL-5 cells

TSHR protein levels were evaluated by immunofluorescence assay. TSHR levels were significantly increased in the model group compared to control (P < 0.05), but were significantly decreased in the Model + serum group (P < 0.05). Additionally, TSHR expression was lower in the Model + serum + OE group than in the Model + serum and Model + serum + OE NC groups (P < 0.05), and this reduction was attenuated in the Model + serum + OE NC + XMU-MP-1 group (P < 0.05). TSHR levels were again significantly decreased in the Model + serum + OE + XMU-MP-1 group compared to the Model + serum + OE NC + XMU-MP-1 group (P < 0.05) (Fig. 7).

TSHR expression in each group as determined by immunofluorescence assay.

Effect of the MST1/Hippo pathway on PCNA and cyclin D1 expression in FRTL-5 cells

The proliferation markers PCNA and Cyclin D1 were evaluated by Western blot. Both proteins were significantly increased in the model group compared to control (P < 0.05), but this upregulation was reversed by RS-containing serum (P < 0.05). Furthermore, PCNA and Cyclin D1 expression were significantly decreased in the Model + serum + OE group compared to the Model + serum and Model + serum + OE NC groups (P < 0.05), whereas XMU-MP-1 treatment in the Model + serum + OE NC + XMU-MP-1 group restored their expression (P < 0.05). PCNA and Cyclin D1 levels were once again significantly decreased in the Model + serum + OE + XMU-MP-1 group relative to the Model + serum + OE NC + XMU-MP-1 group (P < 0.05) (Fig. 8).

Effect of the MST1/Hippo pathway on apoptosis in FRTL-5 cells

The expression of apoptosis-related proteins Bcl-2 and Caspase-3 was assessed by Western blot. In the model group, Bcl-2 levels were significantly elevated and Caspase-3 levels were reduced compared to the control group (P < 0.05); these changes were significantly reversed in the Model + serum group (P < 0.05). Furthermore, in comparison to the Model + serum and Model + serum + OE NC groups, Bcl-2 expression was downregulated and Caspase-3 expression was upregulated in the Model + serum + OE group (P < 0.05), which was reversed in the Model + serum + OE NC + XMU-MP-1 group (P < 0.05). Compared to the Model + serum + OE NC + XMU-MP-1 group, Bcl-2 levels were significantly decreased and Caspase-3 levels were increased in the Model + serum + OE + XMU-MP-1 group (P < 0.05) (Fig. 9).

Effect of the MST1/Hippo pathway on autophagy in FRTL-5 cells

Autophagy-related proteins LC3-II/I and ATG5 were evaluated by Western blot. Compared to control, LC3-II/I and ATG5 levels were significantly downregulated in the model group (P < 0.05), but were significantly increased following RS-containing serum treatment (P < 0.05). Furthermore, compared to the Model + serum and Model + serum + OE NC groups, LC3-II/I and ATG5 expression was significantly elevated in the Model + serum + OE group (P < 0.05), whereas this elevation was strongly inhibited in the Model + serum + OE NC + XMU-MP-1 group (P < 0.05). LC3-II/I and ATG5 expression was again significantly upregulated in the Model + serum + OE + XMU-MP-1 group relative to the Model + serum + OE NC + XMU-MP-1 group (P < 0.05) (Fig. 10).

Autophagy-related protein expression determined by Western blotting (*P < 0.05 vs Control, #P < 0.05 vs. Model, &P < 0.05 vs Model + serum, @P < 0.05 vs. Model + serum + OE NC, %P < 0.05 vs. Model + serum + OE, !P < 0.05 vs. Model + serum + OE NC + XMU-MP-1).

Effect of the MST1/Hippo pathway on cellular ultrastructure in FRTL-5 cells

Ultrastructural changes were examined by TEM. In the control group, normal vesicle secretion was observed, whereas almost no vesicle secretion was detected in the Model and Model + serum + OE NC + XMU-MP-1 groups. In contrast, abundant vesicles with duplicature structures were observed in the Model + serum, Model + serum + OE NC, Model + serum + OE, and Model + serum + OE + XMU-MP-1 groups (Fig. 11).

Discussion

Our previous research demonstrated that RS exerts a regulatory effect in LST-induced hyperthyroidism in rats, as evidenced by urine metabolomics12, liver metabolomics13, serum endogenous markers14, proteomics15, and transcriptome sequencing techniques16. RS may serve as a candidate medicine for the treatment or prevention of hyperthyroidism.

Genetic susceptibility and environmental stimulation during GD can disrupt the immune system, with the TSHR recognized as the autoantigen of GD. This process engages T and B lymphocytes as well as a variety of cytokines, which collectively contribute to the production of the pathogenic autoantibody, TRAb. TRAb, by binding with TSHR, mimics the effect of TSH, activating signal transduction pathways within thyroid cells, thereby stimulating accelerated cellular proliferation and increased hormone secretion. These processes results in the clinical features of diffuse goiter and thyrotoxicosis. Therefore, abnormal increases in TRAb are central to the pathogenesis of GD17. In this study, FRTL-5 cells were cultured with TRAb for 24 h to establish an in vitro GD model18. Cells were subsequently treated with serum containing 5%, 10%, and 15% concentrations of RS to determine the optimal dose.

B-cell lymphoma 2 (Bcl-2) is a key anti-apoptotic protein that protects cells. Overexpression of Bcl-2 induces glutathione accumulation in the nucleus, thereby altering the nuclear redox balance. Duan Wei et al.19 compared thyroid cells from 28 patients with GD and 20 healthy individuals, revealing higher Bcl-2 expression in GD thyroid cells, which inhibits apoptosis. These findings indicate that GD pathology can impede apoptosis in thyroid cells. In our study, anti-apoptotic Bcl-2 protein expression was significantly elevated in the model group compared with the control group, consistent with previous reports. The Hippo signaling pathway is known to inhibit cell proliferation and induce apoptosis20. The G1 phase is essential for cell cycle initiation, with cyclins acting as positive regulators through interactions with cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs) to promote transition from G1 to S phase. Cyclin D1 is especially sensitive among cyclins and is regarded as a key regulator of G1-phase proliferation signaling. Proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA) serves as a proliferation marker closely associated with DNA synthesis and is widely used to assess cell proliferation, demonstrating significant clinical relevance in thyroid proliferative diseases21. Caspase-3 is a central effector in the apoptosis cascade; its activation leads to cleavage of cellular substrates, DNA fragmentation, chromatin condensation, cell shrinkage, and formation of apoptotic bodies, ultimately culminating in programmed cell death22.

Han23 observed the effects of Kangjia pills (comprising Radix Astragali, ginseng radix et rhizoma, rhizoma sparganii, rhizoma curcumae, coicis semen, radix polygoni multiflori, fructus ligustri lucidi, glycyrrhizae radix et rhizoma) on thyroid cell morphology, apoptosis index, Caspase-3 and Fas expression, and the levels of PCNA and Cyclin D1 using a methimazole-induced hyperthyroidism model, demonstrating that Kangjia pill can promote apoptosis and inhibit cell proliferation. Zheng24 showed that treatment with Jiakang Ning (consisting of Prunella vulgaris L., RS, Rhizoma Bolbostemmatis, Airpotato Yam, and Ostreae Concha, among others) inhibited the expression of Cyclin D1 and anti-apoptotic Bcl-2 protein in FRTL-5 cells exposed to TSH, indicating suppression of proliferation and promotion of apoptosis. Furthermore, Bo18 reported that Jiakang Ning-containing serum downregulated Bcl-2 and upregulated Bax and Caspase-3 expression. These studies collectively suggest that RS may modulate the proliferation and apoptosis of thyroid FRTL cells.

In our study, Cyclin D1 expression in thyroid FRTL-5 cells was significantly increased in the model group compared with control. RS-containing serum decreased Cyclin D1 protein expression in a dose-dependent manner. The model group exhibited a reduced apoptosis rate, which was reversed by RS in a dose-dependent fashion, with a marked downregulation of Bcl-2. These findings suggest that RS inhibits proliferation and promotes apoptosis of rat thyroid FRTL-5 cells, supporting its potential therapeutic role in hyperthyroidism.

Importantly, although Bcl-2 is a canonical anti-apoptotic protein whose downregulation is typically associated with increased cellular vulnerability, in the context of autoimmune hyperthyroidism, moderate suppression of Bcl-2 may be protective. Autoimmune hyperthyroidism, particularly GD, is characterized by persistent TRAb stimulation, resulting in aberrant survival and overproliferation of thyrocytes. Restoring physiological turnover through controlled apoptosis is a therapeutic goal in this setting. In our study, downregulation of Bcl-2 by RS-containing serum was accompanied by improved thyroid cell morphology, decreased TRAb and T3/T4 levels, and enhanced Caspase-3 activation. These data collectively suggest that RS contributes to the re-establishment of cellular homeostasis rather than causing cytotoxicity. Nevertheless, the long-term safety and off-target effects of RS require further evaluation. Future research should examine tissue-specific cytotoxicity, immune cell responses, and systemic tolerance in animal models.

The thyroid is a key endocrine organ, and autophagy plays an essential role in maintaining normal thyroid cell function by protecting against apoptosis induced by various cellular stressors25. Recent studies have indicated that abnormal autophagy is associated with Hashimoto’s thyroiditis26, thyroid cancer25, Graves’ ophthalmopathy27, and other pathological processes. Under pharmacological intervention, excessive thyroid cell proliferation can be suppressed and autophagy-induced cell death can be promoted28. Microtubule-associated protein 1 light chain 3 (LC3) is widely recognized as a marker for autophagosome formation, and the conversion of LC3-I to LC3-II directly reflects autophagic activity. Autophagy-related gene 5 (ATG5) participates in autophagosome formation, mitochondrial quality control following oxidative injury, negative regulation of innate antiviral immune responses, lymphocyte development and proliferation, antigen presentation, adipocyte differentiation, and apoptosis. Therefore, changes in LC3-II and ATG5 levels are routinely used as indicators to monitor autophagy29. In the present study, autophagy was observed in the model group, as evidenced by decreased LC3-II/I and ATG5 expression compared to the control group. RS-containing serum significantly increased LC3-II/I and ATG5 expression relative to the model group. These results suggest that RS promotes autophagy in rat thyroid FRTL-5 cells and may exert a therapeutic effect in hyperthyroidism.

Multiple studies have reported that mammalian sterile 20-like kinases 1 and 2 (MST1/2), as core enzymes of the Hippo signaling pathway, are crucial regulators of T cell subset differentiation and development, including T helper (Th) and regulatory T (Treg) cells. MST1/2 also modulate the immunosuppressive activity of Treg cells; Treg cells deficient in MST1 fail to inhibit experimental enteritis in mice and cannot suppress antigen- or T cell receptor (TCR)-induced T cell proliferation in vitro. Furthermore, MST1-deficient Treg cells display impaired interaction with antigen-presenting dendritic cells (DCs) and are unable to downregulate co-stimulatory molecules, such as CD86, on DCs, ultimately compromising contact inhibition30. These findings implicate the Hippo signaling pathway in the pathogenesis of GD. In this study, MST1 protein expression was examined in FRTL-5 cells. MST1 expression was significantly reduced in the model group compared with control, whereas RS-containing serum significantly increased MST1 levels in low- and medium-dose groups. These results suggest that RS may alleviate hyperthyroidism by activating the MST1/Hippo signaling pathway.

The findings of this study, focusing on the MST1/Hippo pathway, are consistent with research indicating that immune regulation in GD is mediated by several interconnected pathways, including PI3K/Akt and NF-κB. These pathways contribute to T and B cell function as well as thyroid cell proliferation and resistance to apoptosis. Increased PI3K/Akt and NF-κB activity has been reported in thyroid tissues from patients with GD, suggesting that co-targeting these pathways could enhance therapeutic efficacy3,4.

MST1/2, homologs of Hippo protein in mammalian cells, function in a manner analogous to the Hippo pathway in Drosophila. MST1/2 are activated under physiological or stress conditions, leading to phosphorylation of the downstream kinases LATS1/2. Subsequently, the effector proteins yes-associated protein (YAP) and transcriptional co-activator with PDZ-binding motif (TAZ) are phosphorylated and subsequently degraded in the cytoplasm, thereby inhibiting excessive cell growth31. XMU-MP-1 is a benzene sulfonamide that selectively inhibits MST1 and MST2. Treatment with XMU-MP-1 reduces phosphorylation of endogenous MOB1, LATS1/2, and YAP in HepG2 cells in a dose-dependent manner. XMU-MP-1 can inhibit hydrogen peroxide-induced MOB1 phosphorylation and MST1/2 autophosphorylation across multiple cell lines, including mouse macrophage-like cells, human osteosarcoma, and colorectal adenocarcinoma cells. By inhibiting MST1/2 kinase activity, XMU-MP-1 promotes activation of downstream YAP and fosters cell growth. The inhibition of MST1/2 kinase activity by XMU-MP-1 is effective and reversible, resulting in enhanced YAP activation in cells32. Previous work showed that XMU-MP-1 inhibits the therapeutic effect of RS in hyperthyroid rats10. To further investigate the role of the MST1/Hippo signaling pathway in GD, this study introduced an MST1 OE and XMU-MP-1 into FRTL-5 cells treated with RS-containing serum and assessed the expression of Hippo pathway proteins. MST1, phosphorylated LATS1 (p-LATS1), and phosphorylated YAP (p-YAP) were significantly decreased in the model group compared with control, but were significantly increased following RS-containing serum treatment. MST1 overexpression abrogated the effects of RS-containing serum on MST1, p-LATS1, and p-YAP, whereas co-treatment with XMU-MP-1 reversed these changes. These results suggest that the therapeutic effects of RS on hyperthyroidism are closely associated with activation of the MST1/Hippo pathway and phosphorylation of LATS1 and YAP.

Our previous animal experiments have shown that RS can inhibit thyroid tissue proliferation and promote both apoptosis and autophagy in hyperthyroid rats via Hippo signaling pathway regulation, thereby achieving a therapeutic effect in hyperthyroidism10. To clarify the specific role of the MST1/Hippo pathway in the anti-hyperthyroid action of Scrophularia ningpoensis and its association with GD pathogenesis, the present study evaluated TSHR content in rat thyroid FRTL-5 cells after MST1 overexpression and Hippo pathway inhibition. TSHR upregulation observed in the model group was significantly reversed by RS-containing serum. MST1 overexpression abrogated the effect of RS-containing serum on TSHR levels, whereas co-treatment with XMU-MP-1 reversed this effect. These results indicate that the MST1/Hippo pathway suppresses TSHR expression in rat thyroid FRTL-5 cells and contributes to the treatment of GD. It should be noted that the findings of this study are specific to autoimmune-induced hyperthyroidism and may not be generalizable to other etiologies.

Inhibition of the Hippo signaling pathway, or defects in its core components, increases nuclear entry of YAP and transcriptional co-activator with PDZ-binding motif (TAZ), which subsequently activate the TEA domain family (TEADs) to initiate transcription of downstream target genes that promote cell proliferation and inhibit apoptosis. In mice, phosphorylation, dimerization, and nucleocytoplasmic localization can be regulated by MST1, which in turn governs cell differentiation and apoptosis, maintains cell stability, and induces cell adhesion and migration at the molecular level33. In the present study, upregulation of PCNA, cyclin D1, and Bcl-2, as well as downregulation of Caspase-3 in thyroid FRTL-5 cells in the model group, were significantly reversed by RS-containing serum. Overexpression of MST1 in FRTL-5 cells abrogated the effect of RS-containing serum on PCNA, cyclin D1, Bcl-2, and Caspase-3 levels, which was subsequently reversed by co-administration of the Hippo pathway inhibitor XMU-MP-1. These results indicate that activation of the MST1/Hippo signaling pathway can increase phosphorylation of LATS1 and YAP, thereby inhibiting proliferation and promoting apoptosis in rat thyroid FRTL-5 cells.

Autophagy is a cellular process in which aberrant proteins or organelles are sequestered in autophagosomes and degraded following fusion with lysosomes. RAS association domain family 1A (RASSF1A), a regulator of the Hippo signaling pathway, interacts with MST1/2 to sustain Hippo pathway activity. Studies have shown that RASSF1A inhibits the AKT/mTOR pathway through MST1 to promote autophagy, thereby suppressing the development of hepatocellular carcinoma in HCC cells34. Changes in LC3-II/I and ATG5 expression are commonly used as markers for autophagy monitoring35. In this study, reduced expression levels of LC3-II/I and ATG5 in the model group were significantly reversed by RS-containing serum. Overexpression of MST1 in FRTL-5 cells abolished the effect of RS-containing serum on LC3-II/I and ATG5 levels, which was further restored by co-treatment with XMU-MP-1. These findings indicate that activation of the MST1/Hippo signaling pathway induces phosphorylation of LATS1 and YAP, thereby promoting autophagy in rat thyroid FRTL-5 cells and leading to apoptosis (Fig. 12).

RS alleviates autoimmune-induced hyperthyroidism by regulating the MST1/Hippo signaling pathway. TRAb and LST induce abnormal proliferation in thyroid cells. RS-containing serum activates MST1, increases p-LATS1 and p-YAP, and promotes Caspase-3, LC3-II/I, and ATG5 expression, thereby enhancing apoptosis and autophagy. The effect of MST1 overexpression attenuates this process, which is restored by the Hippo pathway inhibitor XMU-MP-1.

Conclusions

RS regulates the proliferation, apoptosis, and autophagy of rat thyroid cells by mediating the MST1/Hippo signaling pathway. Building on our previous research, the present study further elucidates the mechanism of RS in the treatment of hyperthyroidism through the use of pathway inhibitors and protein overexpression. These results provide an experimental basis for the clinical application of RS in the management of hyperthyroidism in both in vivo and in vitro models. To further clarify the downstream regulatory mechanisms of the MST1/Hippo pathway, future studies should target the expression of downstream proteins LATS1 and YAP to assess their effects on hyperthyroidism, as well as their specific roles in regulating cell proliferation, apoptosis, and autophagy.

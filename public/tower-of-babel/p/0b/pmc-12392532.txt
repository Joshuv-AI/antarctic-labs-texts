Pharmacokinetics, safety, and efficacy of mixed formulation of fosrolapitant and palonosetron (HR20013) in combination with dexamethasone in patients with solid tumors scheduled for highly emetogenic cisplatin-based chemotherapy: a phase I trial
Authors: Yuanyuan Zhao, Yuxiang Ma, Tengrui Yin, Zhiquan Qin, Linlin Liu, Guoqiang Kong, Ranran Zhang, Yuanyuan Huang, Li Zhang, Hongyun Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis phase I trial aimed to assess the pharmacokinetics (PK), safety, and preliminary efficacy of a single dose of HR20013 (mixed formulation of fosrolapitant and palonosetron) plus dexamethasone in patients with malignant solid tumors.MethodsSolid tumor patients who were naive to cisplatin-based chemotherapy and scheduled to receive the single-day cisplatin-based chemotherapy were enrolled. Patients would receive a single intravenous infusion of HR20013 (Day 1) before cisplatin-based chemotherapy, alongside oral dexamethasone (Day 1, 12 mg, once a day; Day 2–4, 3.75 mg, twice a day). Primary endpoints were PK parameters of fosrolapitant, rolapitant, M19 (a major active metabolite of rolapitant), palonosetron, and dexamethasone.ResultsTwenty-four patients were enrolled, and 22 received study treatment. Fosrolapitant reached maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) immediately at the end of the infusion of HR20013 (1 h), followed by a short terminal phase, and it was completely hydrolyzed into rolapitant. Mean elimination half-lives of rolapitant and palonosetron were 188.2 and 51.5 h, respectively. M19 reached Cmax at approximately 166.2 h. After a single oral administration of dexamethasone at 12 mg, when combined with HR20013, dexamethasone reached Cmax at approximately 1.5 h, with a mean Cmax of 106.0 ng/mL. Treatment-related adverse events occurred in 54.5% of patients, with constipation (22.7%), increased blood pressure (18.2%), abdominal distension (13.6%), injection site reaction (9.1%), and increased neutrophil count (9.1%) being most common. Complete response rates (no emesis/rescue therapy) were 90.9% at the overall phase (0–120 h) and 86.4% at the beyond delayed phase (120–168 h).ConclusionsHR20013 plus dexamethasone had a favorable PK profile, manageable safety, and durable antiemetic efficacy.Trial registrationClinicalTrials.gov, NCT05465681.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12916-025-04314-5.

Background

Chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting (CINV) are prevalent side effects of cancer chemotherapy, which could significantly impair patients’ quality of life and reduce compliance with chemotherapy, consequently impacting the efficacy of therapy [1, 2]. Antiemetic prophylaxis for CINV is an important component in the management of cancer treatment [3].

Cisplatin-based chemotherapy is classified as highly emetogenic chemotherapy (HEC) [4]. For the prevention of HEC-CINV, guidelines recommend the antiemetic regimen including an NK-1 receptor antagonist (NK-1 RA), a 5-HT3 receptor antagonists (5-HT3 RA), and dexamethasone with or without olanzapine [5–8]. However, the adherence to the guidelines is not satisfactory in real clinical practice [9, 10]. A randomized, phase III trial showed that mixed formulation of fosrolapitant and palonosetron (HR20013) plus dexamethasone was noninferior to the standard triple antiemetic regimen (an NK-1 RA, a 5-HT3 RA, and dexamethasone) for preventing HEC-CINV, with an advantage of improving CINV guideline compliance [11].

HR20013 is a novel fixed-dose intravenous antiemetic combination, which comprises an NK-1 RA (fosrolapitant, 218 mg) and a 5-HT3 RA (palonosetron, 0.25 mg). In vivo, fosrolapitant is completely converted to rolapitant. Rolapitant, a highly selective NK-1 RA, has a prolonged half-life of approximately 180 h [4]. The registered oral dose of rolapitant is 180 mg [12]. The exposure levels of 166.5 mg intravenous rolapitant emulsion and 180 mg rolapitant tablet are equivalent [13]. The selected dose for fosrolapitant in HR20013 is 218 mg, derived from the equimolar conversion of 166.5 mg intravenous rolapitant emulsion. Palonosetron, a second-generation 5-HT3 RA, has a longer half-life of around 40 h [14]. The registered dose of intravenous palonosetron is 0.25 mg [15], therefore the selected intravenous dose for palonosetron in HR20013 is 0.25 mg.

In this context, we conducted the phase I trial to evaluate the pharmacokinetics (PK), safety, and preliminary efficacy of a single dose of HR20013 in combination with dexamethasone in patients with solid tumors who were planning to receive highly emetogenic cisplatin-based chemotherapy for the first time.

Methods

Study design

This study is a multicenter, open-label, single-arm, phase I trial conducted at 4 centers. This study consisted of the treatment period (Day 1 to Day 8) followed by the safety follow-up period (Day 9 to Day 22 or initiation of subsequent chemotherapy).

The ethics committee of each center approved the study protocol and subsequent protocol amendments. The study was conducted following the Declaration of Helsinki, Good Clinical Practice, and local laws and regulatory requirements. All patients provided written informed consent. The study was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (No. NCT05465681).

Patients

Patients eligible for the study were 18 years or older, had a diagnosis of malignant solid tumors confirmed by histology or cytology, had not previously received cisplatin-based chemotherapy, were scheduled to receive the single-day cisplatin-based chemotherapy with a cisplatin dose of at least 60 mg/m2, had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0–1, had a life expectancy of at least 3 months, and demonstrated adequate organ function.

Patients were excluded if they had received abdominal or pelvic radiotherapy within 7 days before enrollment or within day 1–8 of study treatment. Patients were ineligible if they were scheduled to receive highly emetogenic chemotherapy drugs within day 2–8 after the single-day cisplatin-based chemotherapy, or chemotherapy regimens containing paclitaxel. Patients were not allowed to receive drugs with potential antiemetic effects, or benzodiazepines, or opioids within 2 days prior to enrollment, morphine, systemic steroids or antihistamines within 7 days prior to enrollment, palonosetron or NK-1 receptor inhibitors within 14 days prior to enrollment. Patients were prohibited to use CYP3A4 moderate/strong inhibitors, CYP3A4 moderate/strong inducers, CYP2D6 moderate/strong inhibitors, or CYP2D6 substrates within 14 days prior to enrollment. Patients were also excluded if they experienced vomiting and/or retching, and nausea within 24 h before enrollment.

Procedures

On Day 1 of the treatment period, HR20013 (fosrolapitant 218 mg plus palonosetron 0.25 mg) was administered via intravenous pump with an infusion time of 1 h. HR20013 was administered once during the treatment period. The cisplatin-based chemotherapy (dose of cisplatin ≥ 60 mg/m2, intravenously) was to be initiated after the completion of HR20013 administration. On Day 1, dexamethasone (12 mg) was given orally at the start of administration of HR20013. From Day 2 to Day 4, dexamethasone (3.75 mg) was orally administered twice a day. Rescue therapy for nausea and vomiting was permitted, excluding palonosetron and dexamethasone, with the timing and choice left to the investigator’s discretion.

Outcomes

The primary endpoints were PK parameters of fosrolapitant, rolapitant, M19 (a major active metabolite of rolapitant), palonosetron, and dexamethasone. PK parameters included area under the concentration–time curve (AUC) from time 0 (pre-dose) to last time of quantifiable concentration (AUC0-t), AUC from time 0 to infinite time (AUC0-inf), AUC from time 0 to the time of 24 h (AUC0–24 h), AUC from time 0 to the time of 120 h (AUC0-120 h), maximum plasma concentration (Cmax), observed time to reach Cmax (Tmax), elimination half-life (t1/2), clearance (CL), and apparent volume of distribution (V).

The secondary endpoints were safety and efficacy endpoints. The efficacy endpoints included rates of complete response (CR; no emesis and no rescue therapy) at the overall phase (0–120 h) and beyond delayed phase (120–168 h).

Assessments

Blood samples for PK analysis were collected at pre-dose, and at 0.5 (middle of infusion), 1 (end of infusion), 1.25, 1.5, 2, 4, 8, 24 (Day 1), 120 (Day 6), 168 (Day 8), and 504 h (Day 22) from the start of infusion of HR20013. Venous blood samples of 3 mL were collected at each time point.

Adverse Events (AEs), laboratory tests, electrocardiograms, echocardiogram, vital signs, and physical examination were monitored for safety assessments. All AEs that occurred from the signing of the informed consent form until the end of safety follow-up period should be collected and followed up, and graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events v5.0.

Each patient needed to complete a diary during the treatment period to evaluate efficacy. The diary recorded frequency and duration of emetic events, as well as the use of rescue therapy.

Statistical analysis

There was no formal statistical consideration for sample size calculation. A total of 24 patients would be enrolled. Analysis of PK concentration was performed in all patients who were enrolled and received at least one dose of study treatment, with at least one effective plasma drug concentration during the trial. Analysis of PK parameter was performed in all patients who were enrolled and received at least one dose of study treatment, had at least one evaluable PK parameter, did not have significant protocol deviations, and did not experience events that could significantly affect PK parameters. Safety and efficacy were analyzed in all patients who were enrolled and received at least one dose of study treatment.

Baseline demographics and AEs were summarized descriptively. 95% confidence interval (CI) for rates of CR at the overall phase and beyond delayed phase were conducted by the Clopper-Pearson method. The PK parameters were estimated using non-compartmental PK analysis in Phoenix (version 8.0 or above, Pharsight Corporation, CA, USA). Other analyses were done using SAS (version 9.4 or above).

Patients

Between August 30, 2022 and December 9, 2022, 24 patients met the eligibility criteria and were enrolled (Fig. 1). Two patients did not receive study treatment: one due to the COVID-19 pandemic, and the other per investigator’s decision. PK

Among the 22 patients who received treatment, 3 experienced leakage during the intravenous infusion of HR20013which led to the interruption of treatment, resulting in 19 patients being included in the analyses of PK concentration and parameters of HR20013. The three cases of extravasation stemmed from preventable technical issues: one case was due to operational error by the nursing staff during the infusion process, and the other two were directly caused by inappropriate use of infusion device. With reinforced training, no further leakage events have been observed. The mean plasma concentrations of fosrolapitant, rolapitant, M19 and palonosetron over time, starting from the single intravenous infusion of HR20013 combined with dexamethasone, are shown on a semilogarithmic scale in Fig. 2. Fosrolapitant reached Cmax immediately at the end of the infusion 1, h, followed by a short terminal phase, and it was completely hydrolyzed into rolapitant. The fosrolapitant concentration rapidly decreased after the end of infusion. About 1 h after infusion, circulating fosrolapitant plasma concentrations dropped to < 2% of mean Cmax. Approximately 15 min after the end of the infusion, rolapitant reached Cmax, which was 1304.4 ng/mL and then slowly eliminated with a long mean t1/2 of 188.2 h. Another component, palonosetron, also reached its Cmax immediately at the end of the infusion, with a mean Cmax of 0.9 ng/mL. The mean t1/2 of palonosetron was 51.5 h. M19, which was the active metabolite of rolapitant, reached Cmax at approximately 166.2 h, with a mean Cmax of about 150.9 ng/mL. Safety

All 22 patients who received treatment were included for safety assessment. Treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs) were reported in 22 (100.0%) patients (Additional file 1: Table S1 and S2). TEAEs of grade 3 or higher occurred in 8 (36.4%) patients. The most common TEAEs were nausea (59.1%), decreased white blood cell count (54.5%), anemia (45.5%), decreased neutrophil count (45.5%), and constipation (31.8%).

The proportion of patients with AEs related to treatment was 54.5% (12 out of 22 patients; Table 4 and Additional file 1: Table S1). The majority of AEs related to treatment were grade 1 or 2. The most frequently reported AEs related to treatment included constipation (22.7%), increased blood pressure (18.2%), abdominal distension (13.6%), injection site reaction (9.1%), and increased neutrophil count (9.1%). AEs related to HR20013 occurred in 10 (45.5%) patients. All AEs related to HR20013 were grade 1 or 2. The most common AEs related to HR20013 were constipation (22.7%), abdominal distension (13.6%), and injection site pain (9.1%). There were no deaths, serious AEs, or any AEs leading to interruption, discontinuation, or withdrawal during the study (Additional file 1: Table S1). Efficacy

The efficacy analysis was performed in all 22 patients who received treatment. CR rates were 90.9% at the overall phase and 86.4% at the beyond delayed phase.

Discussion

In this phase I trial, HR20013 combined with dexamethasone had favorable PK and a manageable safety profile in patients with solid tumors who were planning to receive highly emetogenic cisplatin-based chemotherapy for the first time. What’s more, the combination of HR20013 and dexamethasone demonstrated a promising antiemetic efficacy throughout the overall phase and the phase beyond the delayed phase.

The PK profile of HR20013 combined with dexamethasone was propitious in this study. Since the single intravenous infusion of HR20013 (218 mg fosrolapitant/0.25 mg palonosetron) began in patients with solid tumors who were planning to receive first cisplatin-based chemotherapy, fosrolapitant reached Cmax immediately upon the completion of infusion, followed by a short terminal phase, and it was completely hydrolyzed into rolapitant, resulting in limited mean systemic exposure to fosrolapitant. Rolapitant reached Cmax approximately 15 min after the completion of infusion and then was slowly eliminated, with a mean t1/2 of 188.2 h. Palonosetron reached Cmax after the completion of infusion with a mean t1/2 of 51.5 h.

Compared with prior reported PK parameters of oral rolapitant at 180 mg in healthy subjects from a phase I study [16], a single dose of HR20013 administered as an intravenous infusion over 1 h in present study showed a similar AUC (128 μg∙h/mL vs 142 μg∙h/mL), higher Cmax (1304 ng/mL vs 954 ng/mL) and shorter Tmax (1.2 h vs 2.5 h), which was anticipated based on the route of administration. Similarly, a single dose of HR20013 in present study had similar AUC and Cmax to prior reported PK parameters of intravenous palonosetron in healthy subjects from two ascending-dose studies [17]. The previous phase I study of rolapitant at 180 mg in healthy subjects showed a mean t1/2 of 186 h [16]. The two ascending-dose studies of palonosetron in healthy subjects showed mean t1/2 of about 40 h [17]. The systemic exposure parameters and t1/2 of rolapitant and palonosetron in this study were consistent with those in previous studies. HR20013 that is the mixed formulation of fosrolapitant and palonosetron has a long t1/2 owing to the relatively long t1/2 of its components. Moreover, rolapitant and palonosetron are currently the longest-acting NK-1 RA and 5-HT3 RA on the market, respectively [18–20]. HR20013 has advantageous pharmacologic and formulation.

The antiemetic combination regimens recommend by guidelines including dexamethasone [5–8]. Dexamethasone is primarily metabolized by the CYP3A4 enzyme [21], thereby inhibitors or inducers of this enzyme may affect the exposure of dexamethasone. Aprepitant, an NK1 RA, is simultaneously a substrate, moderate inducer, and moderate inhibitor of CYP3A4 [22]. A drug-drug interaction study showed a twofold increase in dexamethasone AUC0-24h value following coadministration with aprepitant, suggesting that oral doses of dexamethasone should be reduced by half when combined with an NK1 RA that is an inhibitor or inducer of CYP3A4 to maintain exposures similar to those achieved with dexamethasone alone [23]. Similar drug-drug interactions were also found between dexamethasone and other NK1 RAs, including fosaprepitant (a prodrug of aprepitant), netupitant (a component of a fixed combination of netupitant and palonosetron [NEPA]), and fosnetupitant (a prodrug of netupitant) [24–26]. Neither rolapitant nor palonosetron is not an inhibitor or inducer of CYP3A4 [12, 15]. It could be inferred that HR20013 has no impact on the exposure of dexamethasone.

The dose of dexamethasone in this study was established with reference to the previous phase III trial of fosaprepitant, which was the only NK-1 RA injectable formulation approved and available in China at the time of study design. In the phase III trial of fosaprepitant-based triple antiemetic regimen for patients receiving HEC, dexamethasone was administered as 6 mg once on Day 1, 3.75 mg once on Day 2, and 3.75 mg twice daily on Day 3 to Day 4 [27]. Fosaprepitant, as a CYP3A4 inhibitor increases dexamethasone exposure approximately twofold within 48 h post-administration as mentioned above [21–24]. HR20013 may not affect dexamethasone exposure. Consequently, in this phase I study, the dose of dexamethasone (12 mg once on Day 1, 3.75 mg twice daily on Day 2) administered within 48 h following HR20013 was double that used with fosaprepitant.

When combined with HR20013, dexamethasone at 12 mg presented a mean Cmax of 106.0 ng/mL and a mean AUC0-inf of 732.8 ng∙h/mL. A prior reported study of a single dose of oral dexamethasone at 20 mg in healthy subjects presented mean Cmax and AUC0-inf of dexamethasone were 257.2 ng/mL and 1329.2 ng∙h/mL, respectively [28]. The exposure of dexamethasone in this study was comparable to historical data of dexamethasone in healthy subjects, indicating that HR20013 has no significant effect on the exposure of dexamethasone. Therefore, there is no need to adjust the dose of dexamethasone when combined with HR20013.

The safety profile of the present study was favorable and manageable, which is confirmed by the phase III trial of HR20013 plus dexamethasone in patients receiving cisplatin-based HEC [11]. The observed TRAEs of HR20013 in the present study were consistent with those that had already been reported for NEPA [29, 30], a fixed antiemetic combination of fosnetupitant and palonosetron, in patients receiving HEC. In addition, no deaths, serious AEs, or any AEs leading to interruption, discontinuation, or withdrawal were reported in this study.

The rates of CR for HR20013 at different phases in this study preliminarily indicated the antiemetic efficacy of HR20013 in patients receiving cisplatin-based HEC. The previously reported phase III trial of HR20013 plus dexamethasone in patients receiving cisplatin-based HEC further confirmed HR20013 was a valuable treatment option for the prevention of HEC-CINV [11]. A phase III study comparing fosnetupitant and fosaprepitant for HEC-CINV prevention showed higher CR rate (86.5% vs 81.4%), similar non-significant nausea rates (89.3% vs 89.3%), and higher no-vomiting rate (92.3% vs 86.8%) in the fosnetupitant group during 120–168 h after HEC [31]. The main reason for fosnetupitant’s better durable efficacy may be that its active metabolite netupitant has a longer plasma t1/2 (about 69.5–82.7 h) compared with that of fosaprepitant’s active metabolite aprepitant (about 9–13 h) [32, 33]. In the pivotal phase III study of HR20013 [11], the rates of CR, no significant nausea, and no emesis were 90.3%, 92.2%, and 93.6% at the beyond delayed phase (120–168 h), which were comparable to the previous study of fosnetupitant in the prevention of HEC-CINV [31]. The durable antiemetic efficacy of HR20013 may be mainly due to its long t1/2.

Conclusions

In summary, the combination of HR20013 and dexamethasone showed a favorable PK and had a tolerable safety profile for patients with solid tumors who were scheduled to receive highly emetogenic cisplatin-based chemotherapy for the first time. HR20013 exhibits a long t1/2 and does not exert a significant impact on the exposure of dexamethasone. Moreover, HR20013 plus dexamethasone has preliminarily demonstrated a remarkable and durable antiemetic efficacy. These findings support the potential of HR20013 in the prophylaxis of HEC-CINV in cancer patients.

Hydrogen sulfide induces regulatory B cells via glycolysis and mitochondrial ROS, attenuating LPS-induced lung injury
Authors: Yu Sun Jeong, Byunghyun Park, Mingyu Lee, Ji Cheol Kim, Ji Ye Park, Brian A Zabel, Yoe-Sik Bae
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHydrogen sulfide (H2S) is a gasotransmitter found in the human body that plays a crucial role in maintaining homeostasis of various biological systems, including the immune system. While prior studies have explored the effects of H2S on T cells, its impact on B cells has yet to be elucidated.MethodsWe used sodium hydrosulfide (NaHS) as an exogenous donor for H2S. LPS was used to activate B cells and promote their differentiation into plasma cells or regulatory B cells (Bregs). Bregs phenotypes were analyzed by flow cytometry and ELISA, and their immunosuppressive functions on neutrophils and CD4 T cells were assessed by co-culturing them with B cells. To investigate the therapeutic effect of B cells on acute lung injury, we adoptively transferred H2S-induced Bregs to mice with LPS-induced acute lung injury.ResultsBoth exogenous and endogenous H2S induced regulatory phenotypes of B cells including IL-10 production and programmed cell death ligand-1 (PD-L1) expression. Bregs induced by LPS and exogenous H2S functionally suppressed neutrophils and CD4 T cells. H2S-induced Bregs not only induced neutrophil apoptosis and suppressed neutrophil ROS but also inhibited CD4 T cell proliferation and pro-inflammatory cytokine production. H2S enhanced PI3K/Akt/mTORC1 signaling pathway and glycolysis during LPS stimulation, which partially mediated IL-10 production and PD-L1 expression. Moreover, H2S donor administration activated mitochondrial electron transport chain usage, resulting in increased mitochondrial oxygen consumption. Change of mitochondrial redox state contributed to the regulatory phenotypes of H2S-induced Bregs, which were confirmed with mitochondrial-specific redox regulators. In LPS-induced acute lung injury model, adoptive transfer of H2S-induced Bregs was able to protect tissue damage and alleviate lung inflammation by decreasing pro-inflammatory cytokines and increasing neutrophil apoptosis.ConclusionsThis study reveals that H2S shifts B cells to have regulatory phenotypes via shifting metabolic pathway and enhancing mitochondrial expenditure. H2S can serve as one of the inducers and environmental signals for Bregs to resolve inflammation via signaling and metabolic pathways in B cells.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12964-025-02456-w.Keywords: Hydrogen sulfide, Regulatory B cell, IL-10, Mitochondria, Acute lung injury

Introduction

Hydrogen sulfide (H2S) is a rotten-egg-smelling gas with toxic properties at high doses. However, at low doses, it plays diverse roles in maintaining homeostasis of living organisms. H2S is one of the gasotransmitters along with nitric oxide and carbon monoxide. It is produced by various mammalian tissues [1]. Cystathionine beta-synthase (Cbs) and cystathionine gamma-lyase (Cth) are two major enzymes responsible for producing H2S. Many biological systems, including neuronal, cardiovascular, and gastrointestinal systems, utilize H2S to maintain homeostasis of each tissue [2] including the immune system. Protective effects of H2S have been revealed in diverse infection and inflammatory disease models, including sepsis, lung inflammation, colitis, multiple sclerosis, and systemic lupus erythematosus [2]. H2S also supports immune regulation by enhancing and maintaining regulatory T cells (Tregs) development [3, 4]. Cbs knockout mice exhibit a decreased population of Tregs and an autoimmune phenotype [3]. Although there are few studies on the effects of H2S on T cell lineage, its effects on B cell lineage have not been reported yet.

B lymphocytes not only play crucial roles in producing antibodies but also play important roles in antigen presentation and regulation of other immune cells [5]. Specific subtypes of B cells can regulate the development of hematopoietic cells [6] and suppress immune responses, like Tregs [7]. B cells with immune suppressive ability are called regulatory B cells (Bregs), which arise from diverse stages of B cell lineage including transitional B cells, mature B cells and plasma cells. Given their heterogeneity, Bregs are characterized by their immunosuppressive functionality, and the major Bregs population is composed of especially having the capacity to produce IL-10.

Here, we found that H2S induced Bregs under LPS stimulation. H2S promoted the production of IL-10 and the surface expression of programmed cell death ligand-1 (PD-L1) in the B220+CD138+ population, conferring suppressive capacity on other cells, including neutrophils and CD4 T cells. Mechanistically, H2S enhanced PI3K/Akt/mTORC1 signaling and glycolysis with partial influences to regulate IL-10 production. Moreover, B cells activated by H2S exhibited an energetically active status, accompanied by enhanced mitochondrial oxygen consumption. Shifted mitochondrial redox condition driven by H2S was key to inducing regulatory phenotypes of Bregs. Furthermore, we observed that B cells with H2S during LPS stimulation had an ability to suppress inflammation and promote resolution in LPS-induced acute lung injury (ALI) model. Our finding on the effects of H2S on B cells offer new insights into its regulatory impact on the immune system.

Methods

Mice and cell isolation

Eight- to ten-weeks-old C57BL/6 N male mice were purchased from DBL (Eumseong, Korea). All animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the guidelines approved by the Institutional Committee for Animal Care and Use at Sungkyunkwan University (Suwon, Korea). Mouse splenocytes were obtained by mechanical dissociation through 70 μm cell strainer (SPL Life Sciences, Pocheon, Korea) and treated with ACK lysis buffer (homemade) for 10 min at room temperature to lyse red blood cells. CD19+ cells and total CD4 T cells were isolated by MagiSort™ Mouse CD19 Positive Selection Kit, MagiSort™ Mouse CD4 T cell Enrichment Kit, respectively, according to the manufacturer’s instructions (#8802-6847-74, #8804-6821-74, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). Bone marrow neutrophils were enriched with percoll gradient separation method described before [8]. Briefly, mouse bone marrow was flushed using centrifugation. Cells were resuspended with Ca2+ and Mg2+ free-HBSS (#LB003-2, Welgene, Gyeongsan, Korea) and carefully loaded onto 45%/60% percoll (#17089101, Cytiva, Marlborough, MA, USA) gradient and centrifuged at 1600 g for 30 min without breaking. Cells were isolated on the 45%/60% interface layer and RBC were removed with ACK lysis.

Cell culture

Isolated CD19+ B cells were resuspended at density of 1 × 106 cells/ml with RPMI 1640 medium (A1049101, Thermo Fisher Scientific) supplemented with 10% heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum (FBS; Access cell culture, Vista, CA, USA), Anti-Anti (#15240062, Thermo Fisher Scientific), and 50 µM of β-mercaptoethanol (#M6250, Sigma-Aldrich, Saint Louis, MO, USA). Cells were stimulated with 10 µg/ml of Escherichia coli LPS O55:B5 (# L2880, Sigma-Aldrich) with or without NaHS (#161527, Sigma-Aldrich). To prevent starvation and sustain the concentration of H2S because of its volatile property, media, NaHS, LPS, and other reagents were supplemented every 24 h with half of the volume and the same concentration as the initial incubation according to a previous report [9].

B cells and neutrophils were co-cultured at described ratio in a 96-well round plate with/without LPS (5 µg/ml) for 24 h. Isolated CD4 T cells and LPS-activated B cells for 72 h were resuspended at a density of 1 × 106 cells/ml with RPMI 1640 complete medium. LPS-activated B cells and CD4 T cells were co-cultured at 1:1 ratio in a 96-well round plate (SPL Life Sciences) with anti-CD3 (2 µg/ml) and anti-CD28 (2 µg/ml) for 72 h.

Isolated CD4 T cells were resuspended at a density of 2 × 106 cells/ml with RPMI 1640 complete medium. Supernatants for stimulation were collected after 72 h of LPS-stimulated B cell culture with or without NaHS. CD4 T cells were plated into 96-well round plate (SPL Life Sciences) with anti-CD3 and anti-CD28 stimulation. Supernatants and cells were treated at 1:1 ratio and cells were cultured for 72 h.

Treatment of inhibitors

LY294002 (5 µM; #9901, Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA) was treated 1 h before the stimulation of LPS and NaHS. In the 72 h experiment, LY294002 was added 18 h after the initial LPS treatment. For the 24 h LY294002 treatment, CD19+ cells were resuspended in RPMI medium at a density of 2 × 106 cells/ml. Rapamycin (5 nM; #ab120224, Abcam, Cambridge, UK), 2-DG (0.5 mM; #D8375, Sigma-Aldrich), DCA (5 mM; #347795, Sigma-Aldrich), MitoPQ (1 µM; #SML3152, Sigma-Aldrich) and MitoTEMPO (200 µM; #SML0737, Sigma-Aldrich) were given to cells simultaneously with LPS and NaHS or with LPS alone.

SiRNA transfection

Cbs and Cth knockdown were performed by specific siRNA transfection (#sc-60336, #sc-142618, Santa Cruz Biotechnology Inc., Dallas, TX, USA) into B lymphocytes using INTERFERin (#101000028, Polyplus, Illkirch-Graffenstaden, France) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, B cells were stimulated with LPS (10 µg/ml) for 48 h prior to transfection. B cells (2 × 106/ml) resuspended in 10% FBS-supplemented RPMI 1640 medium with LPS (10 µg/ml) and without antibiotics were plated on 96 well round plate. 9 µl of 10 µM siRNA combined with 1.5 µl of INTERFERin were added to the cells. Control siRNA-A (#sc-37007, Santa Cruz Biotechnology Inc.) was used as control. Cells were incubated with the mixture of siRNA and INTERFERin for next 40 h and analyzed.

Quantitative PCR

Total RNA was extracted from cultured cells or lung tissues using Trizol (#15596018, Thermo Fisher Scientific) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Complementary DNA was synthesized from 1 µg of RNA using a Reverse Transcription Kit (#25081, iNtRON Biotechnology, Seongnam, Korea). Q-PCR were run in duplicate for each sample using a BioFACT™ 2X Real-Time PCR Master Mix including SYBR® Green I in mixture (#DQ383-40 h, BioFACT, Deajeon, Korea) and Rotor-Gene Q real-time PCR instrument (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany). mRNA expression levels were calculated using the ΔΔCT method and normalized to the level of Tbp, a housekeeping gene. Primer sequences are listed in Supplementary Table 1.

Western blot

Cells were lysed with RIPA buffer (#9806, Cell Signaling Technology) containing protease and phosphatase inhibitor cocktail (#P3100, GenDEPOT, Houston, TX, USA) for 30 min on ice. Cell lysates were then mixed with 4× SDS sample buffer (homemade) and heated at 95℃ for 10 min. Next, protein samples were separated by SDS-PAGE with 8% gel and transferred to a nitrocellulose membrane (#10600002, Cytiva). Antibodies for Western blot are listed in Supplementary Table 2. All antibodies were diluted in 5% skim milk at a ratio of 1:1000. Signals were visualized with an EZ-Western Lumi Pico Kit or an EZ-Western Lumi Femto Kit (#DG-WP100, #DG-WF100, DoGenBio, Seoul, Korea).

Flow cytometry

All flow cytometric analyses included live/dead discrimination. Samples were analyzed using a BD FACS Canto II flow cytometer or Cytek Aurora™. Acquired data were analyzed with FlowJo analytical software (BD Biosciences, Milpitas, CA, USA). FACS buffer was homemade with 0.5% BSA (#A9647, Sigma-Aldrich) in PBS. Antibodies used for flow cytometric analysis are listed in Supplementary Table 3. For surface molecule analysis, cells were collected, plated into a 96-well round plate, and stained with antibodies for 30 min at 4℃. Antibodies for surface molecules were diluted in FACS buffer.

Cellular H2S was measured with a H2S-specific fluorogenic probe, SF7-AM (#14623, Cayman chemical, Ann Arbor, MI, USA). Glucose uptake ability was measured with 2-NBDG (#72987, Sigma-Aldrich). Mitochondrial mass was measured with MitoTracker™ Green (20 nM; #M46750, Thermo Fisher Scientific) and MitoTracker™ Deep Red (20 nM; #M46753, Thermo Fisher Scientific). Cell proliferation was measured with labeling CFSE (#C34554, Thermo Fisher Scientific). Cell apoptosis was measured with FITC Annexin V Apoptosis Detection Kit (#556547, BD Biosciences). All reagents were stained following the manufacturer’s instructions.

Seahorse analyzer for mitochondrial respiration

Mitochondrial respiration analysis was carried out as previously reported [10]. Briefly, differentiated cells on day 3 were analyzed using a Seahorse XF96 Extracellular Flux Analyzer (Agilent, Santa Clara, CA, USA). Cells were seeded at 4.0 × 105 cells per well in 180 µl of XF RPMI medium. Respiration was measured at the basal state and under reagents treated states. 2 µM of oligomycin, 1 µM FCCP, and 0.5 µM rotenone/antimycin A were added at 22 min intervals. The oxygen consumption test was repeated twice for each replicate.

LPS-induced ALI model and adoptive transfer of B cells

For inducing LPS-induced ALI, mice were euthanized and E. coli LPS (50 µg/head, O111:B4, L2630, Sigma-Aldrich) was administered intratracheally. For adoptive transfer, B cells were stimulated with LPS (10 µg/ml) with or without NaHS (200 µM) for 48 h. Cells were washed with PBS once and adoptively transferred intravenously (3 × 106/head) at 24 h after LPS administration. The control groups were received PBS in an identical manner. The mice were sacrificed at the indicated time points.

Preparation of lung single cells

The lungs were mechanically minced and digested with 5 ml of RPMI 1640 media containing 0.4 mg/ml of Collagenase D (#COLLD-LO, Sigma-Aldrich), 0.1 mg/ml of DNaseⅠ(#10104159001 Sigma-Aldrich), 10 mM HEPES (#15630-080, Thermo Fisher Scientific), and 3% FBS (Access cell culture) for 30 min at 37 ℃ shaker. The digested tissues were filtered using a 70 μm strainer, and red blood cells were lysed with ACK lysis buffer.

Histological analysis

The lungs were fixed in 4% neutral paraformaldehyde (homemade) for 18 h at room temperature. Then fixed tissues were transferred and washed with 70% ethanol for cleaning red blood cells. Paraffin-embedded tissues were sliced and deparaffinized, rehydrated, and used for hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining [10].

Confocal microscopy

Paraffin-embedded tissues were sliced and deparaffinized, rehydrated, and proceeded following protocol for immunofluorescence staining of paraffin-embedded section [11]. Nucleus was stained with Hoechst or SytoxGreen (1:2000; #H21486; #S7270; Thermo Fisher Scientific). Coverslips were mounted onto slide glass and visualized with a Zeiss LSM700 microscope (Zeiss, Oberkochen, Germany). Antibodies used for confocal microscopy analysis are listed in Supplementary Table 4.

Statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism software was used to evaluate results. All data were assessed for their normality and homogeneity of variances. Depending on the results, either a standard t-test, Welch’s t-test, or Mann-Whitney U test was used for two-group comparisons. For comparisons among more than two groups, either a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) or Kruskal-Wallis test was used. For two-factor analysis, two-way ANOVA was conducted. A P-value ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

H2S promotes regulatory phenotypes of B cells under LPS stimulation

B cells are activated by various receptor signaling such as B cell receptor, co-stimulatory molecules (CD40-CD40L), and Toll-like receptors (TLRs) [12]. Among them, TLR ligands including LPS, bacterial lipoproteins, and CpG-DNA are known for T-independent polyclonal activator for B cells [13]. Interestingly, these TLR ligands can promote differentiation of both plasma cells and Bregs [12, 14, 15], and LPS is used for a classical B cell activator and a differentiating factor for plasma cells. Based on this, we asked if exogenous H2S affected plasma cell differentiation under LPS stimulation. Sodium hydrosulfide (NaHS), an H2S donor, was used to treat cells stimulated with LPS. During differentiation into plasma cells, B cells lose B220 expression and acquire CD138, a marker of plasma cells. NaHS treatment increased the overall frequency of the CD138+ population compared to the vehicle control (Fig. 1A). In particular, the frequency of plasma B cell intermediates (B220+CD138+), but not that of fully differentiated plasma cells (B220−CD138+) [16], was significantly increased by NaHS (Fig. 1B). The differentiation of plasma cells is regulated by several key transcription factors including Pax5, IRF4, and Blimp-1 [17]. During plasma cell differentiation, the expression of Pax5, which maintains B cell identity, decreases while IRF4 and Blimp-1, key factors in plasma cell differentiation, increase. Addition of NaHS increased the frequency of Pax5+ cells but decreased IRF4+ and Blimp-1+ populations (Supplementary Fig. 1 A). Consistent with this retention in immature plasma cells, antibody production significantly decreased in LPS + NaHS group (Fig. 1C). These results suggest that exogenous H2S inhibits plasma cell maturation during LPS-induced plasma cell differentiation.

On the contrary to the decreased plasma cell maturation, H2S promoted the regulatory phenotype of B cells under LPS stimulation. IL-10 production was significantly increased by NaHS (Fig. 1D). Expression of regulatory immune checkpoint receptor PD-L1 was also significantly increased by NaHS, particularly on the B220+CD138+ subset (Fig. 1E). The expressions of other surface molecules with suppressive functions, like CTLA-4, PD-1, and FasL were not increased with NaHS treatment. However, LAG-3 expression was slightly increased in the LPS + NaHS group (Supplementary Fig. 1B).

Various cellular stresses can induce H2S production as an adaptation response against damage [18], and also bacterial endotoxin can increase the production of H2S [19]. We investigated the effect of LPS on H2S production, including the expression of H2S-producing and metabolizing enzymes Cbs, Cth, mercaptopyruvate sulfurtransferase (Mpst), and sulfide quinone oxidoreductase (Sqor). LPS treatment significantly increased Cth mRNA expression (Supplementary Fig. 2 A), which was confirmed in another dataset from a previous publicly available report (Supplementary Fig. 2 A) [20]. Correlating with Cth mRNA expression, the level of intracellular H2S was also elevated in LPS stimulated cells, with a higher level observed in CD138+ cells (Supplementary Fig. 2B). Next, siRNA targeting Cbs or Cth were delivered into LPS-activated B cells to examine their respective roles in NaHS-induced generation of IL-10 producing B220+CD138+ B cells. siRNA against Cth effectively suppressed the generation of B220+CD138+ cells during LPS activation (Fig. 1F). Both Cbs and Cth siRNA delivery significantly reduced IL-10 secretion (Fig. 1G) and PD-L1 surface expression on B220+CD138+ cells (Fig. 1H). These data collectively support the notion that endogenous H2S also can induce and maintain Bregs phenotype during B cell activation/differentiation.

H2S enhances regulatory function of LPS-activated B cells

Bregs suppress inflammation through multiple mechanisms, such as the production of soluble factors or contact-dependent inhibitions. Bregs regulate both innate and adaptive immune cells, including neutrophils and T cells [7]. Representatively, the suppressive effect of B cells on neutrophils was investigated in the airway, where B cells inhibit neutrophilic inflammation and promote neutrophil apoptosis [21, 22]. To determine whether H2S treatment enhanced the ability of Bregs, H2S-generated Bregs were co-cultured with neutrophils. The frequency of apoptotic Annexin V+ neutrophil populations significantly increased when neutrophils were co-cultured with LPS + NaHS-treated B cells compared to control groups under LPS stimulation (Fig. 2A). LPS + NaHS-treated B cells not only induced neutrophil apoptosis but also effectively blocked neutrophil ROS generation (Fig. 2B).

Next, we examined the suppressive effect of Bregs also applied for T cells. Bregs suppress T cells with soluble mediators and/or contact-dependent mechanisms which inhibit T cell proliferation and pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion [15, 23, 24]. LPS-activated B cells were co-cultured with CD4 T cells stimulated by anti-CD3/CD28 antibodies. The presence of NaHS-stimulated B cells resulted in a decrease in CD4 T cell proliferation (Fig. 2C) and the production of IFN-γ secretion (Fig. 2D). Under the co-culture condition, B cells were the dominant cells producing IL-10, rather than CD4 T cells (Supplementary Fig. 3). The frequency of IL-10+ CD4+ T cells did not differ depending on the type of B cell stimulation (Supplementary Fig. 3B). However, NaHS-stimulated B cells exhibited higher frequency of IL-10+ cells and produced more IL-10 compared to the control (Supplementary Fig. 3 A, Fig. 2D). Furthermore, the effect of soluble mediators was examined with conditioned medium from H2S-treated cells and administrated during CD4 T cell activation. The addition of NaHS-treated B cell supernatant significantly decreased the secretion of IL-6 and IFN-γ, but not that of TNF-α or IL-10, from anti-CD3/CD28-stimulated CD4 T cells, compared to controls with LPS-activated B cell supernatant (Fig. 2E). These results suggest that exogenous H2S can induce Bregs phenotypes with functional suppressive activity.

H2S-mediated PI3K/Akt/mTOR activation and glycolysis enhances IL-10 production in Bregs

During immune cell activation and differentiation, particularly in lymphocytes, the PI3K/Akt/mTOR signaling pathway undergoes significant activation and regulates cellular metabolism from quiescent status (catabolic metabolism) to effector status (anabolic metabolism) [25]. The PI3K/Akt signaling plays a crucial role in transmitting signals from the exterior to the interior of the cell, with its strength acting as a threshold to activate or deactivate various signaling molecules [26, 27]. Addition of NaHS markedly augmented levels of phosphorylated-Akt and -FoxO1 in the presence of LPS, and the effect was reversed by a PI3K inhibitor, LY294002 (Fig. 3A). Moreover, LY294002 significantly reduced the frequency of the B220+CD138+ population (Supplementary Fig. 4 A). LY294002 decreased IL-10 production in both LPS and LPS + NaHS groups, with a partial effect on the IL-10 induced by NaHS (Fig. 3B), whereas PD-L1 expression enhanced by NaHS was not reduced in the presence of LY294002 (Fig. 3C).

Moreover, H2S enhanced mTORC1 signaling with increasing phosphorylation of S6K, a downstream molecule, while the level of p62 levels increased, and AMPK phosphorylation remained unchanged (Fig. 3D). Increased anabolic metabolism, accompanied by mTORC1 signaling, involves diverse cellular activities including increased glucose uptake, elevated cellular lipid content, and increased cell size [26]. NaHS treatment increased glucose uptake (Fig. 3E), cellular lipid content (Supplementary Fig. 4B), and cell size (Supplementary Fig. 4 C), indicating enhanced anabolic metabolism. To further characterize the role of the mTORC1 pathway, B cells were treated with rapamycin, an inhibitor of mTORC1. Rapamycin significantly reduced the frequency of both total CD138+ and B220+CD138+ cells (Supplementary Fig. 4D). Moreover, rapamycin treatment inhibited NaHS-dependent IL-10 production (Fig. 3F). Interestingly, rapamycin treatment significantly increased PD-L1 expression, despite failing to block the NaHS-induced increase (Fig. 3G). Considering that NaHS-induced PD-L1 expression was not affected by mTORC1 inhibition, H2S may regulate PD-L1 expression through a mechanism distinct from metabolic changes. The increase of PD-L1 induced by rapamycin implies that mTOR signaling plays an inverse role in surface PD-L1 expression. These data suggest that PI3K/Akt/mTORC1 signaling participates in NaHS-induced IL-10 secretion.

We observed that H2S enhanced anabolic metabolism and glucose uptake of LPS-activated B cells (Fig. 3E and Supplementary Fig. 4). Notably, H2S is known to induce aerobic glycolysis by participating in mitochondrial ETC reactions [9, 28]. To investigate the effect of enhanced glycolysis induced by H2S, we challenged cells with glycolysis inhibitors during LPS stimulation. 2-Deoxyglucose (2-DG) and dichloroacetate (DCA) are commonly used to inhibit glycolysis via different mechanisms. 2-DG, an analog of glucose, completely blocks glycolysis by inhibiting pyruvate synthesis, while DCA promotes OXPHOS by enhancing pyruvate entry into mitochondria [29]. 2-DG significantly decreased CD138 expression induced by NaHS in the presence of LPS, while DCA treatment increased the frequency of more mature/differentiated B220−CD138+ cells in the NaHS-treated group (Supplementary Fig. 4E). The results suggest that the inhibitory effects of H2S on plasma cell maturation are reversed by DCA-induced metabolic changes, which promote OXPHOS instead of glycolysis. Moreover, the viability of cells treated with NaHS and either glycolysis inhibitor was significantly reduced compared to controls (Fig. 3H), consistent with the observation that H2S shifts the metabolism of activated cells toward a glycolysis dependent state, making them more vulnerable to glycolysis inhibition.

Next, we investigated the effect of glycolysis inhibitors on NaHS-induced regulatory function. In the presence of either glycolysis inhibitor, NaHS failed to increase IL-10 production (Fig. 3I). As we assumed that metabolic change did not affect PD-L1 expression under mTORC1 inhibition (Fig. 3G), NaHS-induced increase in PD-L1 was not inhibited by glycolysis inhibitor treatments (Fig. 3J); rather it was significantly enhanced by inhibitor treatments. These data suggest that H2S enhances IL-10 production in a glycolysis-dependent manner, whereas the inhibition of glycolysis has no inhibitory influence on the increased PD-L1 expression. Overall, these results indicate that H2S enhances PI3K/Akt/mTORC1 signaling pathway and glycolysis, which partially mediate IL-10 production.

Enhanced mitochondrial metabolism and ROS mediate the H2S-induced regulatory phenotype of B cells

There are several studies that H2S can modulate mitochondrial ETC by providing electrons via sulfide quinone reductase, SQR (encoded by Spor) [9, 28, 30]. Electrons from H2S compete with electrons from Complex Ⅰ and Complex Ⅱ for the electron pool of coenzyme Q, resulting in decreased ATP coupling and recycling of cofactor such as NADH (Fig. 4A). To determine whether the effect of NaHS was mediated by modulating ETC, we conducted mitochondrial oxygen consumption analysis. H2S shifted the location of B cells in the energy map towards a more glycolytic and aerobic area (Fig. 4B) as we observed enhanced glycolytic-dependent state in Fig. 3. Moreover, NaHS increased the overall oxygen consumption of LPS-activated B cells (Fig. 4C). However, when the results were normalized by maximal respiration as 100%, the ATP-coupled oxygen consumption rate was significantly lower in NaHS-treated cells than in controls (Fig. 4D). Increased oxygen consumption via proton leak may support the enhanced usage of ETC Complex Ⅰ and Complex Ⅲ due to H2S.

Analyses using MitoTracker, TMRM, and MitoSOX revealed that H2S-treated cells had increased mitochondrial mass (Fig. 5A), mitochondrial potential (Fig. 5B), and mitochondrial ROS production (Fig. 5C) compared to controls, consistent with enhanced oxygen consumption. Taken together, these data suggest that NaHS treatment enhanced mitochondrial oxygen consumption during LPS-induced activation. To investigate whether the regulatory effect of H2S is mediated by mitochondrial redox state, we used mitochondrial-targeted redox modulators. MitoPQ is a mitochondria-targeted redox cycler that produces superoxide inside mitochondria, while MitoTEMPO is a mitochondria-targeted antioxidant that acts as a scavenger of mitochondrial superoxide [31]. Interestingly, both MitoPQ and MitoTEMPO effectively reduced the difference in IL-10 production between vehicle- and NaHS-treated activated B cells. MitoPQ treatment significantly increased IL-10 production in vehicle treated B cells, bringing it close to the level of LPS with NaHS co-treatment (Fig. 5D). On the contrary, MitoTEMPO treatment suppressed the NaHS-induced increase in IL-10 production (Fig. 5D). Moreover, while MitoPQ did not reduce NaHS-induced PD-L1 expression, MitoTEMPO partially blocked the NaHS-induced increase in PD-L1 expression (Fig. 5E). These data suggest that H2S enhances IL-10 production in a mitochondrial ROS-dependent manner, while mitochondrial ROS partially mediates the H2S-induced PD-L1 expression.

Adoptive transfer of H2S-induced Bregs ameliorates LPS-induced ALI

Recently, several studies have been reported that B cells migrate to the pulmonary region and play key roles to mediate resolution of lung inflammation by producing IL-10 [32–34]. We asked if H2S-induced Bregs could induce the resolution of inflammation under in vivo LPS challenge. LPS-activated B cells were transferred at 24 h after LPS challenge and mice were sacrificed at day 4 after the initial LPS challenge. Adoptive transfer of LPS-activated B cells effectively attenuated immune cell infiltration into the lungs, mitigated tissue damage (Fig. 6A), and suppressed the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines (Fig. 6B). Adoptive transfer of H2S-induced Bregs resulted in both reduced lung inflammation and increased Il10 expression (Fig. 6A and B). Immunofluorescence image demonstrated increased B cell infiltration into inflamed lungs with LPS challenge. In the H2S-induced Bregs transfer group, B cells colocalized with IL-10 and CD19+ cells (Fig. 6C), indicating the presence of IL-10 producing Bregs in inflamed lungs. Notably, the lung microenvironment during LPS challenge presents favorable conditions for Bregs generation, including TLR ligands and H2S. H2S-producing cells were increased after LPS challenge (Fig. 6D), which allows migrated B cells likely to get regulatory phenotypes in H2S-rich condition.

The key process in resolving lung inflammation is the apoptosis of migrated granulocytes, followed by their clearance though efferocytosis by phagocytes [35]. B cells can induce neutrophil apoptosis in the lungs and prevent lung inflammation via contact-dependent manner [21]. As shown in Fig. 2, H2S-induced Bregs effectively induced neutrophil apoptosis in vitro. Therefore, we asked whether the transfer of Bregs would result in an increase in neutrophil apoptosis in the lungs. The LPS-challenged group without B cell transfer showed that migrated neutrophils did not express Bax, an apoptosis marker, unlike the B cell-transferred group, where neutrophil apoptosis increased (Fig. 6E). Notably, H2S-induced Bregs transfer increased neutrophil apoptosis in the lungs (Fig. 6E). In line with this increased apoptotic neutrophils, efferocytotic cell markers, Mertk and Timd4 (encoding Mertk and Tim-4, respectively) were markedly augmented with H2S-induced Bregs transfer (Fig. 6F). To further confirm the immunosuppressive role of H2S-induced Bregs in lung inflammation, we challenged B cell-deficient (µMT) mice with a lung inflammation model and transferred B cells. Consistent with the earlier result of adoptive transfer of H2S-induced Bregs, µMT mice that received NaHS-treated B cells exhibited reduced tissue damage and expression of Tnfa (Fig. 6G and H). In summary, H2S-induced Bregs transfer exhibited the ability to regulate lung inflammation in vivo, consistent with in vitro findings.

Discussion

H2S is produced in various organs and cells, and synthesized in response to various stimuli [2, 18, 19], even apoptosis [36]. In the immune system, H2S is also recognized for its anti-inflammatory functions, acting on both exogenous and endogenous sources. Administration of H2S-donors alleviates inflammatory responses [2], and mice with a genetic deletion of H2S-producing enzyme exhibit auto-immune disease phenotypes [3]. H2S regulates the response of innate immune cells by inhibiting neutrophil migration and extravasation into inflamed regions, suppressing neutrophil myeloperoxidase activity, and promoting macrophage polarization to the anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype. However, the effects of H2S on lymphocytes have been unrevealed compared to the effects on innate immune cells. H2S influences T cell responses, driving them toward a suppressive phenotype by promoting and maintaining Tregs [3] while inhibiting inflammatory Th17 differentiation [36]. In this study, we demonstrate that H2S promotes the regulatory phenotype on B cells. H2S promoted Bregs differentiation rather than plasma cell differentiation under LPS stimulation, inducing their regulatory phenotype and functional inhibition of other immune cells.

Bregs are composed of heterogenous populations characterized by their immunosuppressive activity without any known correlations with specific/definitive transcription factors (like Foxp3 for Tregs) or extracellular markers [7]. Representative subsets of Bregs include transitional 2 marginal zone precursor (T2-MZP; CD19⁺CD21⁺CD23⁺IgMhi), CD1dhiCD5⁺ B cells, antibody-secreting cells (plasma cells, plasmablasts) [7]. Bregs can arise from any developmental stage of B cells; however, several factors that promote Bregs differentiation are also involved in plasma cell differentiation, revealing intriguing similarities between these two cell types [15, 37, 38]. The most well-known inducers for Bregs and plasma cells are TLR ligands, including LPS and CpG [15]. Therefore, understanding the factors that determine the fate of Bregs and plasma cells remains an important area of research. In this study, we found that H2S lead to Bregs properties including IL-10 production and PD-L1 expression on plasmablasts with LPS stimulation. We found these phenotypes were regulated by PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway, enhanced glycolysis, and mitochondrial redox status. H2S activates PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling and enhanced Foxo1 phosphorylation, which together promote plasmablast differentiation (Fig. 3). Since plasmablasts and plasma cells are known their stronger IL-10 producing capacity than other Bregs types [39]. Moreover, CD1dhiCD5+ Bregs fail to exert their regulatory function in vivo unless they differentiate into plasma cells [38]. It is possible that H2S facilitates the generation of Bregs by promoting their differentiation to the plasmablasts. Moreover, the importance of glycolysis is also observed in other types of Bregs. CD1dhiCD5+ Bregs have been shown to rely on HIF-1α mediated glycolysis for their expansion, and exhibit stronger glycolytic activity compared to CD1dloCD5− counterparts [40]. Moreover, activated B cells with enhanced glycolysis suppress T cells via metabolic competition [23]. This supports the possibility that H2S may promote Bregs activity mediated by glycolysis.

The differentiation of diverse cell types accompanies metabolic changes [41, 42], including LPS-induced plasma cell differentiation [43–45]. Recently, there has been a growing interest in Bregs particularly focused on metabolic reprogramming and cellular redox states. Among various pathways inducing Bregs, crucial features are enhanced energy status and increased cellular and mitochondrial ROS for the regulatory function of Bregs. Activated B cells and plasmablasts have high-nutrient demand that act as a nutritional sink, which inhibits other immune cell activation with low energy sources. Furthermore, cellular and mitochondrial ROS mediate IL-10 production [34, 46] specifically sensitive to the state of mitochondrial electron transport chain activity. Redox status is crucial for IL-10 production, regards to the reports with S-adenosylmethionine produced by enhanced one-carbon metabolism with decreased glutathione level [34], and thioredoxin a redox regulator in mitochondria [46]. Inhibition of thioredoxin significantly decreases mitochondrial ETC expenditure and IL-10 production. In line with previous studies, we found that H2S reprogrammed the metabolic pathway of B cells toward glycolysis while enhancing mitochondrial ETC activity and oxygen consumption, supporting the notion that H2S-induced Bregs exhibit the representative features of Bregs. It is well known that H2S donates electrons to mitochondrial ETC via the activity of SQR enzyme, which competes the electron pool of coenzyme Q [28]. When electrons are donated from H2S, they can activate the ETC and increase OXPHOS and ATP synthesis. However, if H2S oversupplies electrons, it can lead to a deficiency of NAD+, which then forces aerobic glycolysis, limits cellular proliferation, and increases oxygen consumption [47]. It was also correlated with the finding that OXPHOS increased by H2S resulted in a low ATP synthesis efficiency [48]. We observed the decreased proliferation induced by H2S was successfully recovered with pyruvate and uridine supplementation (Supplementary Fig. 5). Under conditions of NAD+ deficiency, pyruvate and uridine can support electron supply [9]. In this context of the mitochondrial ETC, it is worth noting that the mitochondrial redox state, examined with MitoPQ and MitoTEMPO, was critical for IL-10 production. The effect of H2S on augmenting IL-10 production was mediated by mitochondrial targeted redox modulators (Fig. 5). Our data, the effect of H2S on inducing Bregs, aligned with previous findings that highlight the role of mitochondrial metabolism and cellular redox state in generating Bregs.

PD-L1 is one of the most important immune checkpoints, along with PD-1, and is expressed in activated B cells and other immune cells, and non-hematopoietic cells in nonlymphoid organs [49]. PD-L1 expressing B cells often co-express IL-10 and suppress T cell activity and disease progression through both contact-dependent and -independent mechanisms [15, 50]. The suppressive function of PD-L1-expressing Bregs is well established in autoimmune diseases and certain cancers [15, 49, 50], however, the mechanisms governing PD-L1 expression in B cells remain unclear. We observed that PD-L1 expression was upregulated in the presence of H2S under LPS-stimulation, with mitochondrial ROS redox states partially contributing to the upregulation. While H2S-induced IL-10 production was regulated by metabolic changes and mitochondrial ROS, PD-L1 expression was not mediated by metabolic change, suggesting the existence of an alternative mechanism for PD-L1. H2S regulates cellular signaling by modifying post-translational modification of proteins through sulfhydration [2], as shown in the upregulation of Tregs [3]. Further investigation is needed to elucidate the mechanism regulating PD-L1 expression and how H2S upregulates PD-L1.

ALI is caused by pulmonary acute inflammation initiated by various factors such as environmental toxin, infection, hypoxic irritation. A major feature of ALI is large numbers of infiltrated granulocytes, which should be cleared to lead successful resolution [35]. The removal of these infiltrated granulocytes is dependent on their apoptosis and efferocytosis by phagocytes [35]. Recently, several studies have been reported that B cells migrate to the pulmonary region and play key roles to mediate resolution of lung inflammation via producing IL-10 [32–34]. Moreover, B cells can maintain lung homeostasis by inducing neutrophil apoptosis with contact-dependent manner [21]. In this study, we observed that H2S-induced Bregs augmented neutrophil apoptosis (Fig. 2), and adoptive transfer of these B cells effectively regulate lung inflammation (Fig. 6). These immunosuppressive and pro-resolving effects of H2S-stimulated B cells suggest that H2S exerts its immunosuppressive capability in respiratory inflammation by modulating the function of B cells. Moreover, we observed the increase of H2S in B cells and lung cells with LPS challenge (Fig. 6D). Since the inflamed lung environment appears to favor the generation of Bregs, rich in TLR ligands, proinflammatory cytokines [51] and H2S, infiltrated B cells may be developed into Bregs with H2S rich conditions. Considering that both exogenous and endogenous H2S supported Bregs generation (Fig. 1), our data suggests that H2S can play a guiding role in regulating resolution processes.

Although we demonstrated the effects of H2S on plasma cell differentiation and Bregs generation, our findings are limited to in vitro conditions. Considering the heterogeneity of B cell subsets, the effects of H2S on other B cells-particularly in vivo-warrant further investigation. Moreover, while our data highlight the regulatory effects of H2S-induced Bregs in the ALI model, their broader impact on other cell types and immune responses in vivo remains to be clarifed.

In conclusion, we found that H2S induces regulatory phenotypes of LPS-activated B cells by modulating cellular metabolism and mitochondrial redox status. These findings corroborate previous data demonstrating the immunosuppressive effect of H2S on immune cells and inflammatory diseases. Taken together, H2S may guide cells toward a resolution phenotype under inflammatory condition.

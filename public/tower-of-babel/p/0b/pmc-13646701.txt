Chlorophyll a Fluorescence Kinetics Reveal That Ozone Stress Redistributes Electron Transport Limitations Between PSII and PSI in Wheat
Authors: Jacques Maynard Berner
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTTropospheric ozone (O3) reduces crop productivity, but the sites at which ozone alters photosynthetic electron transport remain unclear. We tested whether acute exposure to O3 shifts the dominant site of electron transport limitation in a cultivar‐specific manner, rather than causing a uniform decline in PSII function. The induction kinetics of chlorophyll a fluorescence in two wheat cultivars, PAN 3434 and PAN 3161, were analysed after controlled exposure to 100 ppb O3 as an acute mechanistic perturbation, using charcoal‐filtered air as a low‐ozone reference treatment. O3 induced different cultivar‐dependent changes in fluorescence rise phases. In PAN 3434, suppression of the J–I and I–P phases were consistent with a restricted reduction of the plastoquinone pool and a reduced acceptor‐side capacity of PSI, accompanied by declines in the density of the active PSII reaction centre and the probabilities of electron transport. In contrast, PAN 3161 showed smaller changes in these parameters, consistent with partial preservation of reaction centre function and intersystem electron transport. These contrasting photochemical signatures were associated with cultivar‐specific yield reductions. The findings indicate that acute O3 exposure alters distinct segments of the PSII–PSI electron transport chain in a cultivar‐specific manner, providing mechanistic information relevant to physiological phenotyping and cultivar screening for ozone tolerance in wheat.

Introduction

Ground‐level (tropospheric) ozone (O3) is a pervasive atmospheric pollutant that constrains plant physiological performance and reduces agricultural yield (Chaudhary and Rathore 2025; Liu and Lu 2023; Yadav et al. 2020). O3 primarily enters leaves through stomatal pores, where it dissolves in the apoplast and generates reactive oxygen species (ROS; Feng et al. 2008; Mills et al. 2018). These ROS perturb membrane integrity, impair photosynthetic machinery, and compromise carbon assimilation processes. Because ozone‐induced damage is more strongly correlated with stomatal O3 flux (the rate at which ozone passes through stomatal pores into the leaf) than with ambient O3 concentration per se, plant responses are often governed by complex interactions among stomatal regulation, cellular redox homeostasis, carbon metabolism, and photochemical efficiency. Nonetheless, it remains unresolved whether ozone stress imposes generalised constraints across the entire photosynthetic apparatus or preferentially perturbs specific segments of the electron transport chain, in a manner dependent on plant genotype.

Wheat (Triticum aestivum L.) exhibits pronounced sensitivity to elevated tropospheric O3, although the magnitude of physiological perturbations and yield reductions depends on genotypic background and the specific characteristics of exposure, including concentration, duration, and temporal pattern (Guarin et al. 2019). Yield losses on the order of approximately 10%–30% have been documented across major wheat‐producing regions in Europe, Asia, and North America, underscoring the imperative to elucidate the physiological and biochemical mechanisms responsible for O3‐induced declines in productivity (Nowroz et al. 2023; Pleijel et al. 2019, 2022; T. Wang et al. 2021; Y. Wang et al. 2023; Zhu et al. 2025). In agricultural landscapes, ambient ozone concentrations typically range from 20 to 80 ppb, although transient pollution episodes can raise concentrations above these levels (Mills et al. 2018; Pleijel et al. 2019). In this context, the present study employed an acute exposure regime of 100 ppb O3 as a mechanistic perturbation, with the specific objective of resolving early photochemical responses and delineating cultivar‐specific loci of limitation within the photosynthetic electron transport chain prior to the onset of chronic structural damage.

Although atmospheric ozone concentrations cannot be directly manipulated under open‐field agricultural conditions, identifying physiological traits associated with ozone sensitivity remains critical for crop phenotyping, cultivar selection, and the mechanistic elucidation of tolerance to oxidative stress. The pronounced cultivar‐dependent variability in ozone responses indicates that O3‐induced constraints do not manifest uniformly across all components of the photosynthetic apparatus. Whereas certain genotypes display marked photoinhibition and premature senescence, others preserve relatively stable photosynthetic capacity under comparable exposure regimes (Yadav et al. 2020; Zhang et al. 2024). Disentangling these heterogeneous responses requires analytical approaches capable of detecting incipient functional perturbations within the photosynthetic electron transport chain, particularly those that precede or are partially uncoupled from steady‐state gas‐exchange responses.

Chlorophyll a fluorescence analysis constitutes a highly sensitive, non‐invasive methodology for quantifying the functional performance of photosystem II (PSII) and photosystem I (PSI) under diverse environmental stress conditions (Stirbet and Govindjee 2011, 2012; R. J. Strasser, Srivastava, et al. 2004; R. J. Strasser, Tsimilli‐Michael, et al. 2004). The characteristic polyphasic OJIP transient reflects a series of well‐defined, sequential redox transitions associated with PSII reaction centre closure, reduction of the plastoquinone (PQ) pool, and electron transfer processes beyond QA, including the acceptor‐side dynamics of PSI. When combined with difference kinetics analysis (ΔVt), JIP‐test–derived parameters, and a detailed evaluation of I–P phase behaviour, chlorophyll a fluorescence transients can be used to infer alterations in electron transport probabilities, energy fluxes per reaction centre or cross‐section, and the capacity of downstream electron sinks (Bussotti et al. 2011; Taylor 2024; Tsimilli‐Michael 2020).

Fluorescence‐derived measurements can associate photochemical function with whole‐plant performance; however, they do not, in isolation, resolve the direction of causality between modifications in electron transport processes and growth inhibition. Perturbations in the redox dynamics of the PQ pool, photosystem II reaction centre density, and PSI‐associated phases may all contribute to reduced rates of carbon assimilation and biomass accumulation. Conversely, constraints on growth or sink demand may exert feedback on Calvin–Benson cycle activity, thereby enhancing the over‐reduction of electron carriers. This bidirectional uncertainty necessitates interpreting fluorescence‐based parameters primarily as functional indicators of electron transport status, rather than as definitive evidence for a single primary locus of ozone‐induced damage.

Therefore, the present study investigated cultivar‐ and yield‐dependent photochemical responses to acute O3 exposure in two South African bread wheat (T. aestivum L.) cultivars, PAN 3434 and PAN 3161. Specifically, we tested the hypothesis that acute ozone exposure shifts the dominant site of limitation within the photosynthetic electron transport chain in a cultivar‐specific manner, with ozone‐sensitive cultivars exhibiting greater restriction of PSI acceptor‐side capacity and downstream electron transport, and ozone‐tolerant cultivars showing greater preservation of these parameters, rather than causing solely a uniform decline in PSII performance. By integrating OJIP transient analysis, ΔVt kinetics, and JIP‐test parameters with yield‐related traits, this study provides mechanistic insight into how ozone stress is partitioned within the photosynthetic electron transport chain and how these photochemical adjustments are associated with cultivar‐specific differences in yield sensitivity.

Materials and Methods

Plant Material and Growth Conditions

Experiments commenced in May, corresponding to the autumn growth period in South Africa. Two South African wheat cultivars, PAN 3434 and PAN 3161, were selected based on contrasting agronomic characteristics. Seeds were germinated on plates, and after 14 days, uniform seedlings were transplanted into 5 L pots containing a loam:sand:peat mixture (2:1:1, v/v/v). The soil was supplemented with a controlled‐release fertiliser (14:9:15:2 N:P:K:Mg) according to the manufacturer's guidelines.

Four pots of each cultivar were placed in each open‐top chamber (OTC), with two chambers per treatment, resulting in a total of eight pots per treatment. Soil moisture was maintained near field capacity using an automated wick‐irrigation system to minimise the effects of water limitation.

Ozone Fumigation

At tillering (Zadoks growth stages 22–23), plants were randomly assigned to either charcoal‐filtered air (control) or ozone‐enriched treatments. Each OTC (3 m diameter, 2.4 m height) was ventilated at approximately 1.5 chamber volumes min−1 (Heyneke et al. 2012). Ozone was generated using a programmable O3 generator, and chamber concentrations were continuously monitored with a UV absorption analyser.

Ozone‐treated chambers were maintained at a target concentration of 100 ppb for 6 h daily (09:00–15:00). Fumigation continued until heading (Zadoks stage 58). Control chambers received charcoal‐filtered ambient air under otherwise identical conditions.

Charcoal‐filtered air was used as a low‐ozone reference treatment following established OTC methodology (Heyneke et al. 2012), with the primary objective of minimising variability associated with fluctuating ambient ozone concentrations. It is acknowledged that charcoal filtration removes not only ozone but also other reactive trace gases, including plant‐derived volatile compounds that may influence plant physiology. Consequently, observed differences between treatments reflect the combined effect of elevated ozone and the removal of any co‐filtered compounds, rather than the effect of ozone in strict isolation. To limit the influence of other environmental variables, all chambers were maintained under identical conditions of temperature, humidity, light, irrigation, and fertilisation throughout the experiment. The cultivar‐specific and directionally distinct fluorescence response patterns observed between PAN 3434 and PAN 3161, both exposed to identical control conditions, are considered to support ozone as the principal driver of the treatment effect, though a contribution from trace gas removal cannot be formally excluded. Future studies should incorporate a non‐filtered ambient‐air reference chamber to more precisely isolate the ozone effect.

Chlorophyll a Fluorescence Measurements

Chlorophyll a fluorescence transients were recorded using a Handy‐PEA fluorimeter (Hansatech Instruments Ltd., King's Lynn, UK). Measurements were performed on fully expanded flag leaves (Zadoks stage 39). Leaves were dark‐adapted for 1 h using leaf clips before measurement. Six readings were taken per flag leaf.

Fluorescence induction was triggered by a saturating red‐light pulse (3500 μmol photons m−2 s−1, 1 s duration), and fluorescence emission was recorded with microsecond resolution over the time range 10 μs to 300 ms. Fluorescence data were processed using PEA Plus software (version 1.10, Hansatech Instruments Ltd.).

Photochemical parameters were derived using JIP‐test analysis according to established formulations (Goltsev et al. 2014; Kalaji et al. 2016; Redillas et al. 2011; R. J. Strasser et al. 2007; Tsimilli‐Michael 2020).

Difference in Relative Variable Fluorescence (ΔVt)

Relative variable fluorescence kinetics were analysed to resolve treatment‐induced modifications of fluorescence rise behaviour (Oukarroum and Strasser 2004; R. J. Strasser et al. 2010; R. J. Strasser, Srivastava, et al. 2004; Strasser and Stirbet 2001). Fluorescence values at time t (Ft) were normalised as follows: Vt=(Ft−FO)/(FM−FO), where FO represents minimal fluorescence and FM represents maximal fluorescence. The normalisations used for specific phases were: VOJ=(Ft−FO)/(FJ−FO), VJP=(Ft−FJ)/(FM−FJ), where FJ denotes fluorescence at the J‐step (2 ms).

To allow direct comparison among replicates, time vectors were interpolated onto a common logarithmic scale spanning 10 μs to 300 ms. Replicate curves were averaged within treatments to obtain the mean reference and test transients. Difference kinetics were calculated as: ΔVt = Vt(test) − Vt(reference) (R. J. Strasser et al. 2007).

Yield Measurements

At physiological maturity, plants were harvested to determine grain weight per plant and grain number per spike. Eight replicate plants were analysed for each cultivar × treatment combination.

Data Analysis

Graphical outputs and statistical analyses were performed using SigmaPlot v15.0 (Systat Software Inc.). A two‐way ANOVA was used to evaluate the effects of cultivar, treatment, and their interaction. Holm–Sidak post hoc comparisons were applied where appropriate. Data are presented as means ± SD, and statistical significance was accepted at p < 0.05.

Results

Ozone Alters Chlorophyll a Fluorescence Transients in a Cultivar‐Dependent Manner

Chlorophyll a fluorescence transients exhibited clear cultivar‐dependent responses to O3 exposure (Figure 1). Under charcoal‐filtered control conditions, both PAN 3434 and PAN 3161 displayed typical polyphasic OJIP rises (O–K–J–I–P), consistent with fluorescence kinetics reported for unstressed wheat (Dąbrowski et al. 2016; Oukarroum and Strasser 2004; R. J. Strasser, Srivastava, et al. 2004).

Ozone fumigation reduced transient amplitudes and altered the fluorescence rise behaviour in both cultivars, although the magnitude of these effects differed markedly. PAN 3434 showed a marked suppression of fluorescence intensity throughout the transient, whereas PAN 3161 exhibited comparatively moderate deviations (Figure 1). Such reductions in the amplitude of the OJIP are consistent with stress‐induced alterations in the use of excitation energy and in electron transport processes previously reported under oxidative conditions (Feng et al. 2008; Pleijel et al. 2019).

The stepwise analysis supported these observations (Table 1). At the O‐step (0.03 ms), no significant differences were detected between treatments. At the K‐step (0.3 ms), PAN 3434 exhibited a significant reduction (−11.9%; p < 0.001), whereas PAN 3161 showed a similar but non‐significant decrease (p = 0.077). Alterations in the K region have frequently been associated with donor‐side changes, including modified oxygen‐evolving complex (OEC) behaviour under oxidative stress (Anaya et al. 2025; Dąbrowski et al. 2016; Yusuf et al. 2010), although interpretation remains model‐dependent.

Effects of O3 on PSII Donor‐Side Behaviour and Electron Transport Dynamics

The ΔVt analysis revealed clear cultivar‐specific differences in O‐J‐I‐P transient behaviour (Figures 2, 3, 4). Variations in ΔVt profiles reflect treatment‐induced changes in the distribution of excitation energy and electron transport processes (Stirbet and Govindjee 2011; Strasser and Stirbet 2001). Across the transient, PAN 3161 showed stronger deviations in the early phases, whereas PAN 3434 exhibited more pronounced responses in the later phases, suggesting a possible shift in the dominant site of limitation along the electron transport chain.

Donor‐Side Responses and Electron Transport (ΔVOP)

Differential kinetics revealed distinct cultivar‐specific features throughout the transient (Figure 2). PAN 3434 displayed a ΔK band near 0.2 ms, whereas PAN 3161 exhibited a more pronounced ΔJ band at approximately 2 ms. Positive ΔK bands are commonly associated with changes in donor‐side processes, including altered OEC function (Bednaříková et al. 2020; He et al. 2018; Oukarroum et al. 2007), while ΔJ bands reflect accumulation of reduced QA and limitations in electron transport beyond QA − (Falcioni et al. 2023; Gong et al. 2025). Both cultivars exhibited ΔI bands during the multiple‐turnover phase, consistent with a slow reduction of the PQ pool. In particular, PAN 3434 showed a prominent ΔG band (~70–100 ms), while PAN 3161 exhibited a smaller response, indicating stronger downstream limitations in PAN 3434.

O‐J Phase Donor‐Side Dynamics (ΔVOJ)

Both cultivars exhibited positive ΔVOJ deviations relative to the controls (Figure 3). PAN 3161 showed a markedly larger ΔK amplitude and steeper rise than PAN 3434, indicating greater early changes in donor‐side behaviour and excitation energy distribution during the O–J phase. These differences support the interpretation that PAN 3161 is more strongly affected during early PSII‐associated processes. Interpretation of ΔK features should remain cautious, as multiple structural and energetic factors can contribute (B. J. Strasser 1997).

PQ Reduction Dynamics (ΔVJP)

The normalised difference kinetics (Figure 4) showed ΔI bands (~6–10 ms) in both cultivars, consistent with altered dynamics of PQ pool reduction. PAN 3434 exhibited a larger ΔG band compared to PAN 3161, indicating stronger downstream limitations in electron transport beyond the PQ‐associated phase. These responses suggest that the effects of ozone on PAN 3434 extend further along the electron transport chain than those on PAN 3161.

I–P Phase Dynamics and PSI‐Associated Electron Transport

Normalised variable fluorescence curves (VOI) revealed cultivar‐dependent alterations in the I–P phase (Figure 5). PAN 3434 exhibited marked flattening of the I–P increase after exposure to ozone, while PAN 3161 maintained comparatively steeper kinetics. Changes in this phase are commonly associated with processes beyond QA, including PSI acceptor‐side behaviour and terminal electron sink capacity (Redillas et al. 2011; Schansker et al. 2003; Stirbet and Govindjee 2011). The stronger modification of the I–P phase in PAN 3434 is consistent with a greater downstream limitation compared to PAN 3161.

Yield Responses to Ozone Stress

Exposure to ozone significantly reduced the yield parameters in both cultivars (Figure 7). In PAN 3434, grain weight per plant and grain number per spike declined by approximately 60% and 50%, respectively, whereas reductions in PAN 3161 were more moderate, at approximately 35%–40% and 30%. Consequently, PAN 3434 exhibited substantially greater yield reductions than PAN 3161, consistent with its stronger photochemical responses.

Discussion

Both wheat cultivars exhibited photochemical changes after ozone exposure, although the magnitude and apparent loci of these effects differed markedly. PAN 3434 showed consistent reductions in chlorophyll a fluorescence kinetics across multiple phases of the OJIP transient, indicating broad alterations in excitation energy use and electron transport behaviour. Early deviations in the K region have been associated with donor‐side processes, including the functionality of the OEC under oxidative conditions. Subsequent suppression of the J and I phases suggests reduced turnover of the PQ pool, while the flattening of the I–P rise is consistent with altered downstream electron transport or reduced capacity of PSI electron acceptors. In contrast, PAN 3161 exhibited comparatively moderate transient modifications, indicating greater preservation of photochemical function under identical fumigation regimes.

The cultivar‐dependent divergence was further resolved by analysis of the difference kinetics. PAN 3161 exhibited more pronounced deviations during the early transient phases (O–J and J–I), consistent with altered PQ reduction behaviour and potential modifications to PSII unit connectivity. PAN 3434, by contrast, exhibited dominant alterations during the I–P phase, a region often interpreted as reflecting PSI‐associated processes and terminal electron‐acceptor dynamics. These differences may reflect genotype‐specific variation in antioxidant buffering capacity, stromal redox regulation, or sink strength for PSI electron acceptors, consistent with reports that PSI‐related limitations can contribute substantially to stress responses while remaining underdiagnosed in conventional analyses (Stirbet et al. 2018). If PSI acceptor‐side capacity becomes limiting, electrons may accumulate within the intersystem chain, potentially increasing the probability of ROS formation and amplifying ozone‐induced oxidative stress. This feedback between electron transport limitation and oxidative stress has been proposed as a key mechanism underlying ozone‐induced photosynthetic inhibition (Mills et al. 2018). Similar cultivar‐specific differences between PSII and PSI‐associated limitations have been reported under combined environmental stresses, including ozone‐warming interactions (Xu et al. 2024), reinforcing the notion that ozone sensitivity cannot be treated as a uniform physiological phenomenon.

A central interpretive limitation must be explicitly addressed. The fluorescence signatures observed here are consistent with the interpretation that ozone directly damages electron transport components, specifically restricting PSI acceptor‐side capacity, thereby limiting downstream electron flow, causing over‐reduction of intersystem carriers, and ultimately suppressing carbon assimilation and growth. However, the opposite causal sequence is equally consistent with these data. Ozone may first affect non‐photosynthetic processes, such as membrane signalling, apoplastic ROS accumulation, or nitrogen remobilisation dynamics (Brewster et al. 2024), thereby reducing sink demand and Calvin–Benson cycle activity, causing over‐reduction of electron carriers as a secondary consequence rather than a primary event. Under this alternative, the electron transport chain signatures reported here would be downstream indicators of reduced metabolic demand rather than the primary site of injury. Fluorescence‐derived parameters reflect probabilistic and energetic system behaviour and cannot resolve the direction of causality between electron transport limitation and growth inhibition (Tsimilli‐Michael 2020). Distinguishing between these pathways would require complementary measurements, including direct P700 absorbance to quantify PSI redox dynamics, gas‐exchange analysis under identical treatment conditions, and assays of antioxidant enzyme activity. The present findings are therefore interpreted as consistent with, rather than proof of, a specific causal sequence.

The use of charcoal‐filtered air as a reference treatment introduces a further interpretive constraint. As discussed in the Methods, charcoal filtration removes not only ozone but also plant‐derived volatile compounds and other reactive trace gases that may influence plant physiology. Consequently, observed differences between treatments cannot be attributed exclusively to ozone. However, both cultivars were grown under identical control conditions, and their fluorescence responses differed in both direction and magnitude under ozone treatment. There is no theoretical reason why the removal of plant volatiles should result in greater PSI acceptor‐side limitation in PAN 3434, but greater PSII donor‐side limitation in PAN 3161. The cultivar‐specificity and internal consistency of the observed response patterns therefore support ozone as the principal driver of the treatment effect, while acknowledging that a contribution from co‐filtered trace gases cannot be formally excluded with the current design.

The 100 ppb O3 concentration used here exceeds the typical chronic background range of 20–80 ppb characteristic of agricultural landscapes (Mills et al. 2018; Pleijel et al. 2019). This concentration was used as an acute mechanistic perturbation to capture early photochemical responses before the onset of chronic structural damage, which would otherwise obscure the cultivar‐specific signatures of interest. Concentrations above 80 ppb are not unprecedented: transient pollution episodes regularly drive ambient ozone above this threshold in wheat‐growing regions of Asia, Europe, and North America (Mills et al. 2018; Y. Wang et al. 2023; Zhu et al. 2025). The cultivar‐specific pattern of electron transport limitations identified here, restricted PSI acceptor‐side capacity in PAN 3434 versus modified PSII connectivity in PAN 3161, is expected to manifest across a range of concentrations, albeit at different magnitudes. Future dose–response studies across the 40–100 ppb range would be valuable to confirm that the mechanistic distinctions reported here are relevant under chronic field‐realistic conditions.

The JIP‐test parameters supported the distinctions observed in the transient kinetics. PAN 3434 exhibited significant reductions in active reaction centre density and electron transport probabilities, including ψEo/(1–ψEo), indicating restricted electron transfer beyond QA −. Although these declines are consistent with stress‐induced functional impairment, interpreting irreversible photodamage requires caution, as fluorescence‐derived parameters reflect probabilistic and energetic system behaviour rather than direct structural measurements. PAN 3161 maintained relatively stable reaction centre density and exhibited smaller declines in downstream transport parameters, suggesting differential preservation of photochemical organisation. These contrasts may reflect genotype‐specific protective mechanisms, including antioxidant buffering capacity (Kovilpillai et al. 2023), although antioxidant status was not directly assessed in the present study.

Yield responses were consistent with the observed photochemical patterns. PAN 3434 exhibited substantially greater reductions in grain weight and grain number per spike than PAN 3161. Reduced downstream electron transport may limit sustained photochemical energy conversion during grain filling, contributing to the larger yield penalties observed in PAN 3434. Although the magnitude of decline was large, these responses fall within the range of ozone‐associated yield penalties reported in regional and global analyses (Guarin et al. 2024; Mao et al. 2025), suggesting that chamber‐based acute exposure captures physiologically relevant responses. The observed photochemical behaviour is also consistent with broader evidence that chronic ozone exposure alters nitrogen remobilisation dynamics during grain filling, processes tightly coupled to sustained photosynthetic function (Brewster et al. 2024). Cultivar‐specific differences in photochemical stability may therefore interact with metabolic allocation responses, potentially amplifying productivity penalties under oxidative stress.

Collectively, PAN 3434 and PAN 3161 illustrate distinct photochemical responses to ozone stress, characterised by differential alteration of PSII‐associated dynamics and PSI‐related phases of the fluorescence transient. The convergence of ΔVt kinetics, JIP‐test parameters, and I–P phase behaviour provides consistent evidence for differential limitations in electron transport across the PSII–PSI chain, supporting the hypothesis that ozone sensitivity arises from a cultivar‐specific redistribution of electron transport limitations rather than uniform photoinhibition. Consistent with recent evaluations of wheat ozone responses (Mashaheet et al. 2025), chlorophyll a fluorescence analysis provides a rapid and sensitive tool for resolving cultivar‐dependent variation in photosynthetic performance under atmospheric stress.

Conclusions

The findings indicate that ozone stress did not affect the two wheat cultivars uniformly. PAN 3434 exhibited pronounced alterations in chlorophyll a fluorescence kinetics and JIP‐test parameters, consistent with disruptions in both donor and acceptor‐side processes of PSII, reduced reaction centre density, and limitations in downstream electron transport extending to PSI acceptor‐side capacity. These photochemical changes were accompanied by substantial reductions in yield components: grain weight declined by approximately 60% and grain number per spike by approximately 50%. In contrast, PAN 3161 maintained comparatively stable photochemical behaviour under identical ozone exposure, exhibiting smaller reductions in electron transport parameters and more moderate yield penalties (~30%–40%). Such cultivar‐dependent differences indicate that ozone sensitivity is governed by genotype‐specific functional limitations rather than uniform damage across the photosynthetic apparatus.

In addition to quantifying photochemical responses, this study contributes to a mechanistic understanding of how ozone stress is expressed at the level of photosynthetic electron transport. The magnitude of yield reductions observed here falls within the range reported in free‐air ozone enrichment experiments and regional modelling studies (Mao et al. 2025; Xu et al. 2024), suggesting that chamber‐based investigations, when coupled with detailed fluorescence analyses, can capture physiologically relevant features of ozone‐induced stress. The results indicate that cultivar‐specific vulnerabilities can arise from different functional limitations, including PSI‐associated limitations in PAN 3434 and modifications of PSII connectivity and PQ dynamics in PAN 3161. Recognising these mechanistic differences is essential for interpreting stress responses and predicting cultivar performance under variable atmospheric conditions.

Fluorescence‐based diagnostics, including ΔVt kinetics, I–P phase behaviour, and JIP‐test parameters, provide rapid and non‐destructive indicators of ozone sensitivity. These approaches offer practical value for screening and comparative phenotyping, particularly in regions where ozone stress co‐occurs with other environmental drivers. Integration of photochemical phenotyping with molecular and physiological markers associated with stomatal regulation and oxidative buffering capacity may improve the mechanistic interpretation of cultivar resilience (Brewster et al. 2024; Mashaheet et al. 2025).

Several limitations of the present study should be noted. The use of charcoal‐filtered air as a reference treatment does not permit complete isolation of the ozone effect from other trace gas differences between treatments, and the direction of causality between electron transport limitation and growth inhibition cannot be resolved from fluorescence data alone. Future work should incorporate direct P700 measurements, gas‐exchange analysis, and a non‐filtered ambient‐air reference chamber, and should examine dose–response relationships across the 20–80 ppb range typical of agricultural environments to confirm the field relevance of the mechanistic distinctions reported here.

These results demonstrate that ozone stress responses cannot be reduced to uniform decreases in photochemical efficiency. Instead, cultivar‐specific response patterns indicate that functional limitations are redistributed across distinct segments of the photosynthetic electron transport system, helping to explain genotype‐dependent ozone sensitivity beyond simple magnitude‐based comparisons.

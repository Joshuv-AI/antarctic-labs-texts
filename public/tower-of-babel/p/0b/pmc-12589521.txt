An improved NG-F model for capturing the three types of indentation size effect
Authors: Peina Wang, Yu Gao, Peihuan Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Experimental studies indicate that three types of ISE are observed. However, developing a model to capture three types of ISE is one challenging task in materials science. In this paper, the relationship between k and h (k is the ratio of the plastic zone radius to the contact radius, and h is the indentation depth) is systematically analyzed. Then, an exponential decay function is modified to capture the relationship between k and h. The modified exponential decay function can accurately describe the nonlinear relationship between k and h. However, the modified exponential decay function still fails to capture the complex relationship between k and h. On this basis, the NG-F Model is improved with the modified exponential decay function. An analysis has been done to comprehensive comprehension the influence of the parameters on the improved NG-F model. The parameter analysis has been qualitatively proved that the improved NG-F model can capture three types of ISE. The competition between the relative rate of change of k3 and h is the mechanisms behind the improved NG-F model for capturing the three types of ISE. The improved NG-F model is validated with experimental results of three types of ISE. Surprisingly, the validation demonstrates that the improved NG-F model can successfully predict three types of ISE for different materials. However, the improved NG-F model’s predictive accuracy varies across materials, with relatively lower DC values observed for certain NR-ISE and C-ISE materials. This is because the dislocation mechanism underlying the improved NG-F model differs from the mechanisms responsible for these materials.

Introduction

Hardness, a vital mechanical property of materials, is crucial for the design and application of micro–nano components, thin film coatings, and emerging materials in micro-nano scales1–8. Up to now, nanoindentation tests have evolved into a robust technique to determine material hardness at the micro-nano scale1,9–14. Nanoindentation tests have also shown that the measured hardness of materials is size-dependent, a phenomenon referred to as the indentation size effect (ISE)2,3,15,16. Meyer initially reported in 1908 12 that hardness exhibited a decline with increasing indentation depth, which is known as the normal ISE (N-ISE), as illustrated in Fig. 1. Since then, the N-ISE has also been observed in nanoindentation tests of different materials1,17–20. However, some nanoindentation experiments21–27 have shown that a hardening-softening transition occurred at shallow indentation depths, that is, the hardness-depth curve gradually transited from normal to reverse ISE (NR-ISE), as depicted in Fig. 1. Moreover, some nanoindentation tests have shown a complex ISE (C-ISE) in polycrystalline materials and nanostructured multilayers/substrate systems (see in Fig. 1)28,29, where observations indicate that hardness first decreases with increasing indentation depth, followed by an increase, a subsequent decrease, and finally convergence to a constant value.

Over the past few decades, numerous theories have emerged to elucidate the indentation size effects (ISEs) mentioned above30–32. Meyer proposed an empirical power-law to reflect the relationship between the applied force and the indentation diagonal12. Hays and Kendall33 identified that the ISE occurs when a critical load threshold is exceeded, initiating permanent deformation. Li and Bradt34 developed a proportional specimen resistance (PSR) model in which the indentation size is considered to exert a linearly increasing influence on the test specimen’s resistance to irreversible deformation. Gong et al.35 improved the PSR model by considering the effects of residual stresses. Bull et al.36 proposed an elastic-plastic model in which the elastic stress at the indentation periphery is a consequence of localized permanent deformation. Ruiz-Moreno et al.37 adopted an exponential function to characterize the nonlinear relation between the applied force and indentation depth. Hou and Jennet38 formulated a model that incorporates the effect of grain size and the pinning effect of dislocation defects on hardness. Nevertheless, the aforementioned ISE models are limited to describing the N-ISE.

One of the most widely cited models is the NG model introduced by Nix and Gao39. Rooted in the Taylor dislocation framework, it describes how the density of geometrically necessary dislocations (GNDs) varies with indentation depth. Subsequent nanoindentation studies, however, indicated that the original model tends to overpredict the hardness for very shallow indentations; this discrepancy has been attributed to the strong repulsive forces among GNDs40,41. Consequently, several researchers40,42,43 have improved the NG model by allowing the ratio between the plastic-zone size and the indentation depth to vary. Huang et al.44 further modified the model by imposing a maximum allowable GNDs density. Meanwhile, to more accurately describe the correlation between the size of the plastic zone and the indentation depth, Feng and Nix42 incorporated an exponential function into the NG model (NG-F model), while Haušild45 utilized a power function for the same purpose (NG-H model). Abu Al-Rub46 improved the NG model by introducing the Taylor hardening law, and Qiu et al.47 enhanced the NG model by incorporating friction stress unrelated to dislocation motion. Yuan and Chen48 refined the NG model by combining it with gradient plasticity and an intrinsic material length. In an effort to broaden the predictive scope of the NG model specifically for ISE at depths below 100 nm, Liu and his co-authors49 took into account the elastic deformation. However, a comparative study of NG model and improved NG models indicted that only NG-F and NG-H models can capture the NR-ISE of different materials, but these improved NG models cannot capture the C-ISE30,50.

The complex ISE (C-ISE) (see Fig. 1) is commonly observed in nanoindentation experiments of polycrystalline materials and nanostructured multilayers/substrate systems28,29. Jung and colleagues51 developed a modified MSG theory to predict the C-ISE in polycrystalline materials. The model incorporates the GB effect by assessing the GNDs density along the GBs. Nevertheless, the underlying mechanism of this model remains controversial, because it assumes that hardening and softening are mainly governed by the accumulation and transfer of dislocations across GBs. Voyiadjis et al.28,29 proposed a theoretical model that encompasses the impacts of temperature and strain rate on ISE by accounting for the GB effect; this model successfully describes the C-ISE in Cu bicrystals and Inconel 718. Xiao et al.52 introduced a theoretical model that considers GB resistance to plastic deformation to explain the C-ISE in bicrystals. Liu et al.53 developed a mechanistic model to provide a comprehensive understanding of the C-ISE in irradiated nanostructured multilayer/substrate systems. Their analysis examined several factors in depth, such as GNDs, irradiation hardening, and interface hardening. However, the ISE models proposed by Xiao et al.52 and Liu et al.53 are expressed as piecewise functions consisting of four segments; consequently, both their practical application and parameter determination are highly complex.

In this work, the NG-F model is firstly reviewed. The relationship between k and h (k is the ratio of the plastic zone radius to the contact radius, and h is the indentation depth) is systematically analyzed. Subsequently, the exponential decay function proposed by Feng and Nix42 is modified to characterize the correlation between k and h. On this basis, the NG-F model is further improved with the modified exponential decay function. To achieve a more comprehensive understanding, the effects of various parameters on the improved NG-F model have been thoroughly analyzed. The performance of the improved NG-F model is validated with experimental results of three types of ISE. In addition, the mechanisms behind the improved NG-F model for capturing the three types of ISE are clarified.

Xiao et al.52 developed a dislocation-density-based model that relates k to indentation depth h in bicrystals while incorporating grain-boundary resistance. The predicted k-h curve for Fe-Si bicrystal (see in Fig. 5) also shows a descent-to-ascent transition. The dislocation-density-based model attributes this transition to the progressive accumulation of GNDs at grain boundaries. Once the local stress attains the critical threshold required for grain-boundary dislocation pile-up, the accumulated stress triggers dislocations to traverse the boundary, causing the plastic zone to expand rapidly into the underlying grain and, consequently, driving the k-h curve from descent to ascent. The DCs of the NG-F and improved NG-F models are determined to quantitatively evaluate their accuracy for various materials, which are provided in Table 7. For materials exhibiting N-ISE, the improved NG-F model shows only marginal improvements over the original model. The DC values for MgO single crystals and 16MND5 steel are very close to 1, indicating high predictive accuracy for both models. The high entropy alloy also shows excellent predictive performance, with the improved model achieving a DC value of 0.9997 compared to 0.9995 for the original model. These results suggest that both models are highly effective for materials with N-ISE, but the improved NG-F model offers slight enhancements in predictive accuracy.

For materials exhibiting NR-ISE, the improved NG-F model shows only a slight enhancement in predictive accuracy, as reflected by its DC values. Specifically, for ZrO₂ ceramic and Y₂O₃-ZrO₂ ceramic, the DC values exhibit slight increases: from 0.8017 to 0.8202 for ZrO₂ ceramic, and from 0.7378 to 0.7890 for Y₂O₃-ZrO₂ ceramic, respectively. Similarly, a minor improvement is observed for Cu single crystals, with the DC value rising from 0.9589 to 0.9885.This slight degree of enhancement indicates that the modifications made to the NG-F model fail to effectively capture the mechanisms associated with NR-ISE. This is because the dislocation mechanism underlying the NG-F and improved NG-F model differs from the mechanisms responsible for NR-ISE. For instance, experimental analysis of ZrO₂ ceramic demonstrates that reverse ISE arises from the relaxation of surface stresses induced by indentation cracks under low loads21. For Cu single crystals, molecular dynamics simulations and experimental data reveal that the reverse ISE in nano-hardness is attributed to both the finite roundness of the indenter tip and the intrinsic property-transition behavior of the material22. Additionally, Luo et al. [23] reported that Y₂O₃-ZrO₂ ceramic undergoes a partial tetragonal-to-monoclinic transformation under indentation load, and this transformation is the cause of its reverse ISE23.

For materials with C-ISE, the improved NG-F model shows substantial improvements in predictive accuracy. The DC values for Cu bicrystal and Inconel 718 increase significantly from 0.8739 to 0.9411 and from 0.8893 to 0.9352, respectively. The Fe-Si bicrystal also exhibits a marked improvement, with the DC value rising from 0.7462 to 0.8916. These results highlight the effectiveness of the improved NG-F model in handling the intricate mechanical behaviors associated with C-ISE, where the NG-F model has struggled to provide accurate predictions.

Although the improved NG-F model significantly improves the DC of Fe-Si bicrystals from 0.7462 to 0.8916, this value remains lower than those of other C-ISE materials like Cu bicrystals (DC = 0.9411) and Inconel 718 (DC = 0.9352). A potential explanation for this limitation is that the improved NG-F model primarily optimizes the functional relationship between k and h, but does not fully incorporate the grain boundary resistance in Fe-Si bicrystals. This resistance induces a clear descent-to-ascent transition in the k-h curve (See in Fig. 5), which the modified exponential decay function employed in the improved NG-F model has yet to capture.

Furthermore, Table 7 shows that for the NG-F model, the DC values of Cu bicrystal are slightly lower than those of Inconel 718, whereas the opposite holds for the improved NG-F model. This reversed fitting performance ranking stems from the two models’ differential sensitivity to the curve nonlinearity of the two materials. The NG-F model’s accuracy relies on the extent to which Eq. (3)’s exponential decay of k matches actual behavior: Inconel 718 has a gentle nonlinear curve (k decreases gradually with h and plateaus at large h) that aligns with Eq. (3), yielding a higher DC (0.8893) than Cu bicrystal (0.8739); in contrast, Cu bicrystal’s pronounced nonlinearity (sharp k drop at shallow h, then plateau) deviates from Eq. (3), worsening fitting. The improved NG-F model, designed to capture steep nonlinearities via Eq. (4) (introducing parameters n and k∞), enhances DC more significantly for Cu bicrystal (n = 3.455, Table 6; DC: 0.8739→0.9411) than for Inconel 718 (n = 2.174, Table 6; DC: 0.8893→0.9352). This leads to reversed DC rankings in the improved NG-F model.

The mean DC values for the NG-F model and the improved NG-F model are 0.8891 and 0.9292, respectively. This indicates an overall improvement in predictive reliability when using the improved NG-F model to describe three types of ISE.

Conclusions

In this work, the relationship between k and h (k and h are the ratio of the plastic zone radius to the contact radius and indentation depth, respectively) is systematically analyzed. Then, an exponential decay function assumed by Feng and Nix42 is modified to capture the relationship between k and h. On this basis, the NG-F model is improved with the modified exponential decay function. To achieve a more comprehensive understanding, the effects of various parameters on the improved NG-F model have been thoroughly analyzed. The performance of the improved NG-F model is validated with experimental results of three types of ISE. In addition, the mechanisms behind the improved NG-F model for describing the three types of ISE are clarified. The key findings of this research are outlined below.

The nonlinear relationship between k and indentation depth cannot be captured by the exponential decay function, but is accurately described by the modified exponential decay function.

Analyzing the influence of government policy on building carbon emission reduction based on differential game
Authors: Yunsong Zhang, Cheng Zeng, Nan Chi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
With the continuous growth of low-carbon economy and people’s increasing preference for low-carbon products, the building carbon emission reduction (CER) has been highly concerned by the government. Therefore, in this study, we establish a game model through differential game to analyze the impact of government policies on building’s CER. We get: (1) Compared with the carbon tax policy, the government policy combining carbon tax and cost subsidy is more conducive to reducing carbon emissions; (2) For the three government policies, when the cost subsidy rate is lower than a fixed value, the government policy combining carbon tax and cost subsidy is the optimal strategy for building’s CER. When the cost subsidy rate is greater than a fixed value, the cost subsidy policy is the best strategy for building’s CER.

Introduction

In its fifth climate change assessment, the IPCC highlighted the critical role of excess carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gas emissions in driving warming since 19501. This has resulted in an average global temperature increase of 0.74 °C since 2000 and is expected to rise by 1.6 °C over the next century. As a result, global warming has become an important global issue. In order to slow down global warming and reduce carbon emissions, many countries and regions have developed relevant policies and institutions2–4. For China, as an advocate and leader of environmental protection in the world, it takes the initiative to shoulder the responsibility of emission reduction, committing to reduce carbon emissions by 40–50% in 2020 compared with 2015, and striving to achieve carbon peak before 2030. At the United Nations General Assembly in September 2020, Chinese President Xi Jinping solemnly pledged that China attaches great importance to environmental protection and will try all kinds of measures to achieve double carbon by 2060. To achieve these commitments and goals, China actively promotes the development of new energy sources and changes in industrial structure, promotes resource reuse, and seeks the best strategy for emission reduction5.

China is a powerful country in infrastructure construction, and the construction industry is highly developed, the construction industry is one of the important sources of China’s economy. But at the same time, the construction industry consumes a lot of resources, causes extremely serious pollution to the environment, and emits a lot of carbon dioxide. Therefore, If the effect of carbon reduction is to be achieved immediately, the construction industry has become a key position that cannot be ignored6. The construction industry has become a broad topic in the research of CER, whether in business or academia; people are trying to tap the huge potential of carbon emission reduction in the construction industry7,8.

Literature review

As a market coordinator, the government can have a profound and important impact on carbon reduction through policy formulation. Carbon tax and cost subsidy policies, as effective measures for the government to promote the reduction of carbon emissions, have been favored by more and more scholars in recent decades, and studied the impact of government policies on carbon emission reduction from different perspectives.

Carbon tax policy is one of the policies of CER that the government is willing to adopt. Yang et al.9 constructed a bipartisan game composed of retailers and manufacturers, and studied how to formulate a carbon tax to achieve the optimal level of CER in the whole cooperation process. Park et al.10, from the perspective of central decision-makers who maximize social welfare, discussed the formulation of a carbon tax for optimal CER in a two-party game consisting of retailers and manufacturers. When the government imposes a heavier carbon tax on enterprises, it can promote enterprises’ efforts of CER. Still, it will inhibit competitive enterprises’ behavior of CER11. Cost subsidies are another carbon reduction policy that governments are more than happy to adopt; The results show that when the two sides of the green supply chain cooperate with each other, the subsidy policy has a greater impact on the building’s CER than the carbon tax policy12. In practice, when the investment is reasonable, the cost subsidy is more conducive to reduce carbon emission than carbon tax policy13. Other studies have shown that companies can effectively promote renewable energy investment under government subsidy policies. For small-scale renewable energy companies, government subsidies are a necessary way to survive14–16.

In the whole process of CER of construction enterprises, the government acts as a management mechanism, and the policies implemented will directly affect the level of CER across the entire process of carbon reduction. Proper policy implementation acts as a catalyst, not only accelerates the process of the building’s CER, but also strengthens the close cooperation between construction enterprises in the construction process, and promotes the CER effort behavior of construction enterprises in the supply chain. On the contrary, the implementation of improper policies will crack down on the CER efforts of construction enterprises and inhibit the coordination and cooperation between construction enterprises.

In fact, in the previous research on building carbon emission reduction, the government has not been the main research object17. But this paper takes a new approach and takes government as the subject of this study. From the perspective of the government, establishes a differential game model to analyze the impact of carbon tax policy, subsidy policy, the combination of carbon tax and cost subsidy policies on the buildings’ CER, and to find a balanced strategy to achieve the optimal CER level of the entire supply chain. Previous studies only considered the government’s construction cost subsidy policy, leading to the simplification of the government’s role18,19. In this study, we enrich the influence of the government, which can either provide cost subsidies to enterprises or penalize the carbon emissions of construction enterprises. Such improvement can better reflect the government’s emphasis on buildings’ CER and highlight the government’s supervision and management function. In addition, by comparing and analyzing the equilibrium strategy of buildings’ CER under the three policies, this study finds the best strategy to reduce carbon emission, which provides theoretical support for the formulation of appropriate CER cost subsidy rate and carbon tax rate.

A multi-centric feasibility study to assess the satisfaction of pregnant women using Maathru Samman Pant during labour in tertiary health care settings, India
Authors: Venkatashiva Reddy B, Pulla Sirisha, Archana Bhosale, Madhur Verma, Noorin Bhimani, Priti Gupta, Rakesh Kakkar, Star Pala, Wansalan K Shullai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionMaathru Samman Pants (MSP) are specially designed pants to ensure privacy, reduce preparation time, and enhance satisfaction, offering a respectful maternal birthing experience. The study evaluates the satisfaction, acceptability, and demand for Maathru Samman Pants among pregnant women during vaginal delivery in India’s tertiary healthcare settings.MethodologyA multicentric hospital-based study was conducted from 2023 to 2024 across four regions in India, representing diverse cultures and socioeconomic statuses. Tertiary healthcare facilities in Andhra Pradesh, Punjab, Maharashtra, and Meghalaya participated. Among pregnant women, those who gave consent, MSP dress was provided to 800 pregnant women during the active stage of labour with 4 cm cervix dilation. Data were collected through a comprehensive questionnaire in the postnatal ward. Ethical approvals were obtained from all participatory sites. Informed consent was taken at the time of admission to the hospital. Data were analyzed using IBM SPSS version 28, with statistically significant results determined at p < 0.05.ResultsThe study revealed a high level of satisfaction, with 83.13% of participants expressing overall contentment. Maathru Samman Pants effectively covered the body from the waist to the ankle, provided warmth, and facilitated movement, with significant positive feedback on privacy and comfort during childbirth. The pants reduced the hassle of dressing and undressing, with 66.25% agreeing on its convenience. Acceptability was also high, with 53.75% strongly agreeing and 43.13% agreeing that MSP made them feel cared for and respected. The pants were praised for maintaining privacy and adhering to cultural norms, with 80.25% strongly agreeing on these aspects. Comfort levels were high when family and friends were present during delivery, and the pants were viewed as an appropriate traditional Indian dress. Demand for MSP was robust, with 96.63% of participants willing to recommend the pants to other pregnant women. Key factors influencing satisfaction and acceptability included age, education, occupation, parity, and history of stillbirth.ConclusionThe study highlighted MSP’s potential to enhance maternal care by ensuring privacy, comfort, and cultural sensitivity providing respectful maternity care. The positive outcomes of the study strongly recommended that MSP be incorporated into the National Health Program for deliveries in government health facilities to provide shy free environment and respectful maternity care.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12884-026-09330-8.

Introduction

World Health organization has introduced many recommendations as a part of Intrapartum care to provide a positive experience for pregnant women during and after childbirth. Respectful maternity care (RMC) is the first and most important recommendation [1]. Respectful maternity care refers to a childbearing woman’s humane and dignified treatment throughout her pregnancy, birth, and the period following childbirth [2]. According to this recommendation, every pregnant woman should be provided with dignity and privacy, irrespective of their standard of living. World Health organization recommendation also states that there is a need for new intervention that aims to provide a dignified working environment in the labour room, which should provide respect and care for pregnant women [1]. Traditionally, in India, pregnant women are often not adequately prepared when they visit the labour room for delivery. They typically give birth in their own clothes, which is not hygienic and can lead to unnecessary exposure of the entire body. This practice frequently fails to meet the emotional and physical needs of the mother [3].

As a part of respectful maternity care, it was identified that maintaining privacy in the labour room prevents unnecessary body exposure and unhygienic conditions [1, 2]. In consideration to improve the quality of care and to provide privacy and dignity to the pregnant woman, a dress has been designed that prevents the overexposure of the pregnant woman’s body during delivery. The dress was designed in such a way that the pregnant woman’s vaginal region through which the baby gets delivered will only get exposed and the remaining body will be covered. Apart from the pants, this dress has a top designed for breastfeeding.

This dress provides visual privacy to pregnant women during per-vaginal examinations and childbirth. This dress provides a shy free environment for pregnant women so that the PW can be comfortable in the presence of staff, birth companions, and other pregnant women in the delivery room. The dress assures a free here-and-there movement or walking of pregnant women before delivery.

According to the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare in India, there will be a significant decline in maternal mortality ratio to less than 70 by the end of 2030 [4]. To achieve this, the government has already introduced many schemes and programmes, including LaQshya, which encompass quality of care during delivery [5].

The “Maathru Samman Pant” is a specially designed garment for pregnant women to wear during normal delivery, resembling a traditional Indian churidar dress when paired with a shirt, covering both legs from waist to ankle, these pants feature a vulval opening for various medical procedures, including front and back flaps for modesty. Notably, they avoid buttons and zips to minimize contamination risks. The main benefits include ensuring privacy, reducing procedure preparation time, preventing infections, limiting exposure to colds, and enhancing satisfaction with government hospital services.

In India’s tertiary health care level, labour rooms have comprehensive facilities to ensure safe and hygienic childbirth practices. These facilities include a dirty utility area for disposing and managing contaminated materials and a wash utility area where staff can clean and disinfect reusable items [5]. An autoclave is essential for sterilizing surgical instruments and other medical equipment to prevent infections [6]. Additionally, changing rooms are provided so staff can wear appropriate attire and maintain strict hygiene standards. The research was carried out to identify satisfaction, acceptability, and demand for Maathru Samman Pants by pregnant women during normal delivery in the labour room at the tertiary health care level.

Methodology

A multicentric mixed method study was conducted in four sites across India from 2023 to 2024. The four sites were chosen to be the South, North, East, and West regions of India, representing different geographical locations of India. All the four sites chosen have a diversified culture, population, and socioeconomic status. One tertiary health care government health facility, such as a district hospital or medical college hospital, was included in all these four sites. The inclusion of the hospitals was based on their delivery load. The participatory sites were from the South region of All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS) Mangalgiri, Guntur District, Andhra Pradesh, from North region of All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS) Bathinda District, Punjab, from west region Lokmanya Tilak Municipal Medical college (LTMMC), Sion, Mumbai District, Maharashtra and from East region North Eastern Indira Gandhi regional Institute of Health and medical Sciences (NEIGRIHMS), Shillong Meghalaya. This paper presents tertiary care sites, from South Region District Hospital Tenali, from North Region District Hospital Bathinda, from West Region LTMGH (Lokmanya Tilak Municipal General Hospital), and from East Region District Hospital Shillong/NEIGRIHMS.

A cross-sectional study with a multi-stage, stratified, random sampling technique, which was based on the delivery load of the hospital, was adopted. Assuming the observed prevalence of acceptance of preventive measures by the community as 50% and the 95% confidence interval is set at 44 to 56%, the sample size needed was 250 (Sources: Fleiss, 1981; Snedecor and Cochran,1967). Thus, in each study site, a minimum of 250 pregnant women in labour were studied. Of these, 200 samples were studied in district hospitals/medical colleges, and the remaining 50 were studied in secondary and primary care sites. The study was carried out for a period of one and half year or 18 months. The sample size was 800 pregnant women with each 200 pregnant women from North, South, East and West sites were included in the study for participation. A pilot study was conducted at AIIMS Mangalagiri in Guntur district, and a detailed stepwise document was developed. Before starting this study, all the district hospitals and medical colleges were listed. The district hospitals/ Medical college hospitals had a delivery load of a minimum of 500 deliveries per year and were chosen for data collection. After obtaining permission from respected authorities, one staff nurse and one data entry operator from each site were recruited totaling to 8 staff, A rigorous 4-week training was given to these field data collectors. The training was both virtual and in-person (supervisory and independent). Field data collectors were trained to collect data using tablets.

Based on the inclusion criteria, pregnant women who were undergoing normal vaginal delivery were informed in detail about the study using a participant information sheet which was made available in local languages, i.e. Telugu, Marathi, East Khasi, Punjabi, and in English. informed consent was obtained from pregnant women at the time of admission to the hospital. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approval was taken from the Institutional Ethics Committee of All India Institute of Medical Sciences, Mangalagiri, Andhra Pradesh [AIIMS/MG/IEC/2022-23/161, dated 16 April 2022], Institutional Ethics Committee of All India Institute of Medical Sciences, Bathinda [IEC/AIIMS/BIT/401, dated on 07 August 2023], Institutional Ethics Committee Human Research of Lokmanya Tilak Municipal Medical College & General Hospital [IEC/193/23, dated on 09 May 2023], and Institutional Ethics committee of NEIGRIHMS North Eastern Indira Gandhi Regional Institute of Health & Medical Science, Shillong [NEIGR/IEC/M3/F5/2022, dated on 9 April 2022]. Clinical trial number: Not Applicable. Among pregnant women, those who gave consent, MSP dress was provided during the active stage of labour with 4 cm cervix dilatation. A maximum of 10 women were enrolled in the study site per day to maintain the data quality.

After the baby’s delivery and when the pregnant woman was comfortable answering the questions, a quantitative questionnaire-based interview was conducted. Lawshe technique was used to access the content validity ratio (CVR). A set of 10 experts from obstetrics& Gynecology, Community and family medicine, nursing and a lay person analyzed each object for substantiveness. The subject experts measured each item on a 3-point scale with options (a) Not necessary (b) useful, but not essential and (c) essential. The CVR was determined using the formula for each item. (N = the total number of experts and n = the number of experts who has selected option c for each item). The CVR score for each if 51 items in the scale were 0.80. A quantitative questionnaire containing 51 questions was developed with 17 questions were under general information of the participant, followed by 24 questions, which were provided with options of a 5 series Likert scale. The remaining 10 questions were either yes/no type or open-ended questions. The questionnaire covered all the patient’s general information, including Name, Age, religion, level of education, and occupation, along with the family and personal income of the pregnant woman. It was designed to collect information on marital status, total number of family members, order of child, and mode of delivery. After the general information section, the questionnaire was divided into subsections on satisfaction, acceptability, and demand. The satisfaction section consisted of 10 questions using a 5-point Likert scale. The acceptability section included 9 questions, also on a 5-point Likert scale. The demand section was divided into 5 questions on a 5-point Likert scale, 6 yes-or-no questions, followed by 3 open-ended questions.The pregnant women were then asked for their view on the importance of dignity in hospitals along with their preferred gender of healthcare professional to nurse their delivery. The labour room details whether it is a single or common labour room with partitions and curtains were recorded. In case labour is common, the pregnant women were asked about their private parts exposure problems in the presence of others along with the number of other pregnant women who were there in the labour room. They were also questioned about the presence of their birth companion during their delivery.

The women were also questioned whether they knew any special dress for delivery and also the hospital policies for the maintenance of privacy like no entry for visitors, labour room screened with curtains, private space for dressing and undressing, attached washroom for labour room, and verbal permission before any procedure.

After obtaining general information, the next section collected data about Maathru Samman pants, divided into three subsections: satisfaction, acceptability, and demand. The subsection of satisfaction includes the questions related to patients’ level of acceptance for pants covering the entire body from waist to ankle in both front and backside. Flap use of covering body parts during lying or standing. They were also asked whether the dress is useful for them in the cold temperatures of the labour room. The dress level helps prevent the hassle of dressing and undressing moving in the hospital while using toilets and rates the ease of wearing and usefulness.

The subsection of acceptability involves the questions of care and respect that pregnant women receive by using the dress. Pregnant women’s [PW’s] view on the traditional design of the views the traditional design of the dress with a dress with a top. The PW was also questioned about their comfort level in the presence of family, friends, doctors, other pregnant women, sanitary workers and other staff members. Also, PW’s comfort levels while conversing with the healthcare team are also considered.

The last subsection was a demand, which contains three open-ended questions, seven questions of yes/no type along with reasons and 5 scale Likert questions. The pregnant women’s view on referring the dress to family and friends is followed by their view on using this MSP dress for their next pregnancy if planned. This subsection mainly focuses on the comparison of comfort differences from their previous delivery (if any). The discomfort they faced while wearing the dress was also considered in this questionnaire.

Out of these 800 pregnant women every odd 10 th pregnant women was considered for an in-depth interview. From each site, 10 in-depth interviews were taken with a total of 40 th in-depth interviews from all 4 sites. The In-depth interview was composed of 28 questions where the pregnant woman is asked about the difference between MSP and routine clothes used for delivery and the what is pregnant women’s preference if the hospital implements MSP in the hospital along with the reason for preferring MSP. The pregnant women were also asked about the challenges associated with the implementation of MSP along with their satisfaction with the experience of using MSP. The pregnant women were also questioned about their birth companion satisfaction and staff intent on continuing the use of MSP along with the appropriateness of MSP use by staff to pregnant women for normal delivery. The pregnant women also questioned their view on introducing MSP in all labour rooms and their view on MSP in benefiting the health care delivery system of India. Some questions compare the previous delivery experience of the pregnant women and their knowledge of the special dress for normal delivery and the comparative difference of MSP with the other known delivery dress. The pregnant women were also interviewed about their view on dress design, and ease of using this dress followed by their interest in future use of MSP along with the reason for reuse. The pregnant women were questioned if they had seen any staff member not comfortable using MSP. As the dress is designed for normal delivery the pregnant women were asked if the dress is according to their traditions and privacy prioritized.

All the quantitative data was entered using Microsoft Excel 2019 and analysed using IBM SPSS Statistics Base v28.0, which is compatible with Windows. Descriptive statistics and individual frequencies were analysed. For continuous variables, the mean (SD) was provided. Multivariate analyses were performed. The exposure variables were the region, demographics, and pregnant women’s parity status. Pregnant women’s satisfaction, acceptability, and demand for Maathru Samman Pant were the outcome variables. The cutoff for total satisfaction score and acceptability score among pregnant women (PW) was based on the mean values of 43 and 40, respectively. These scores were then converted into dichotomous variables. Univariable analysis was conducted for calculating unadjusted odds ratios. Adjusted odds ratios were calculated using binary logistic regression analysis. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

All the audio in-depth interviews were transcribed verbatim and then translated to English. The individual data is coded and grouped into subthemes like comfort, Design, Respect, shy-free environment, Acceptability, Desirability, experience, challenges, implementation, and Program success which were further arranged into themes like satisfaction, Acceptability, Demand, Adaptation, Practicality and integration in correlation to objectives of the study. Qualitative data analysis was done using ATLAS.ti.22 Educational Single user license.

The satisfaction for pregnant women using Maathru Samaan Pants (MSP) during labour reveals predominantly positive feedback. A significant majority (56.63%) strongly agreed, (42.88%) agreed that MSP effectively covered the body from waist to ankle, and 52.88% agreed, whereas 44.13% of participants strongly agreed that MSP helped to prevent exposure to cold temperatures. The pants also aided in covering the vulval opening while lying (46.88% strongly agreed, 51.50% agreed) and standing (42.38% agreed and 34.38% strongly agreed). Additionally, MSP reduced the hassle of dressing and undressing during examinations, where 28.25% of participants strongly agreed and 66.25% agreed. The facilitated movement before delivery was strongly agreed upon by 25.63% and 51.00%. Comfort levels were high, with 42.63% strongly agreeing, 52.25% agreeing they were comfortable during childbirth and 54.50% agreeing, whereas 12.38% of the participants strongly agreed they were comfortable using toilets. Ease of use during delivery was rated positively, with 55.38% finding it easy and 33.25% finding it very easy. Discussion

This is the first of its kind to study the satisfaction of pregnant women using Maathru Samman Pant during labor within tertiary health care settings, considering a diverse socio-demographic profile across four regions of India. Nearly two third of pregnant women were within the 21–30 years of age. Most pregnant women completed 9 to 10 standards of education. Over two third homemakers underscore traditional gender roles, which might affect the perception of healthcare quality due to economic dependency and limited autonomy. Socio-economic status reveals the majority of pregnant women in the middle and upper middle classes, indicating relatively good access to health services.

The present study reveals that two third of deliveries were normal vaginal deliveries with episiotomy, demonstrating significant regional variations. This finding aligns with the World Health Organization’s global recommendations, which report an average reduction in episiotomy rates, highlighting the regional differences in childbirth practices [7]. The preference for dignity during childbirth, reported by over three fourths of women, is consistent with the findings of Shakibazadeh et al., who emphasized the importance of maintaining dignity and privacy during labor and delivery [8].

The preference for female healthcare staff by more than three fourth of the pregnant women and the occurrence of deliveries in common labor rooms with partitions reflect cultural and social norms that prioritize comfort and modesty. These findings are supported by Iravani et al., who observed similar preferences for female staff and privacy measures in different cultural settings [9]. The study also found a major difference in companion presence during labor between the North and South regions and the West and East regions, which had very low presence due to institutional policies. This disparity is in line with Bohren et al.‘s research, which documented varied companion presence during labor across different regions and its impact on women’s experiences [10].

Awareness of any delivery dress was minimal (5.13%), indicating a gap of lack of any specific attire for respectful maternity care, which has been similarly highlighted in previous studies [11]. Majority of Pregnant women reporting privacy measures such as no entry for visitors, screened labor rooms, private, and verbal permission before procedures underscore the importance of privacy, corroborating the findings of Balde et al., who emphasized respectful and dignified care practices [11].

The present study on the satisfaction of pregnant women using Maathru Samaan Pants (MSP) during labor provides predominantly positive feedback, with significant percentages of respondents agreeing on various benefits. These findings are consistent with previous research exploring the importance of comfort, privacy, and ease of movement during labor. Nearly all women strongly agreed that MSP effectively covered the body from waist to ankle, which aligns with the findings of Shakibazadeh et al., who highlighted the critical role of maintaining women’s dignity and privacy during childbirth [8]. The coverage provided by MSP also helped prevent exposure to cold temperatures, with almost all women agreeing, supporting the fact that environmental comfort is crucial for a positive childbirth experience, as noted by the World Health Organization [8].

The pants’ ability to cover the vulval opening while lying and standing was reported by the majority of respondents, respectively. This is in line with Balde et al.‘s findings, which emphasized the importance of respectful and dignified care practices, including the coverage of intimate areas during labor [13]. Moreover, MSP reduced the hassle of dressing and undressing during examinations, with nearly all women agreeing. This aspect of convenience is critical, as Bohren et al. reported that frequent disruptions during labor can negatively impact women’s overall childbirth experience [12].

Facilitating movement before delivery was another key benefit by nearly all pregnant women. The ability to move freely during labor is well-documented in the literature as beneficial for labor progression and maternal comfort. Iravani et al. also emphasized the need for mobility and its positive impact on women’s experiences during childbirth [9]. Comfort levels while wearing MSP were very high, with the majority reporting they were comfortable during childbirth and they were comfortable using toilets wearing MSP. This echoes the findings of other studies that have highlighted the significance of comfort in maternal care. For instance, the World Health Organization’s recommendations on intrapartum care stress the importance of providing a comfortable environment for women during labor [10].

The ease of use of MSP during delivery was rated positively by the majority of pregnant women. This ease of use is crucial for reducing stress and improving overall satisfaction during labor. The results of this study are consistent with those of previous research that has underscored the importance of user-friendly maternity wear in enhancing the childbirth experience [713].

Overall, 83.13% of respondents were very satisfied with the usefulness of MSP during labor, reflecting a high level of acceptance and appreciation for these pants. This high satisfaction rate is indicative of the positive impact that well-designed maternity wear can have on women’s childbirth experiences. The findings of this study align with the broader body of literature that advocates for respectful, dignified, and comfortable care practices during labor and delivery.

The acceptability of Maathru Samman Pants (MSP) among pregnant women indicates a high level of satisfaction, specifically regarding feelings of being cared for and respected. Moreover, a significant majority felt that MSP respected their cultural norms during delivery, which is crucial in culturally diverse settings [12]. The pants were highly rated for maintaining privacy, where all participants were highly agreed.This is essential in clinical settings where maintaining patient dignity can significantly impact their comfort and overall experience [13]. Additionally, MSP created a shy-free environment in the labor room, with four out of five of participants strongly agreeing. Preventing overexposure of private body parts, when MSP was worn with a shirt, was also rated positively, with more than half having agreed and remaining participants strongly agreed. In terms of cultural appropriateness, the majority of participants felt MSP looked like an appropriate traditional Indian dress. This cultural relevance is significant for ensuring patient comfort and acceptance in diverse populations [14]. Overall, the data strongly indicate high acceptability and satisfaction with MSP in maintaining dignity, privacy, and cultural appropriateness during labor and delivery. These findings highlight the importance of culturally sensitive and functionally appropriate maternity wear in enhancing the childbirth experience for women.

The demand for MSP indicates a highly positive reception, that MSP is a positive initiative for ensuring privacy and respectful maternity care. The willingness to use MSP again for future deliveries and recommend it to others by almost all participants underscores strong approval and demand for MSP. This positive reception is consistent with the findings of Bruggemann et al. (2007) regarding the importance of culturally appropriate care [15].

The Maathru Samman Pant is an innovative garment designed to enhance the delivery of respectful maternity care in tertiary care hospitals. These pants provide a practical solution by ensuring privacy and dignity for expectant mothers during labor and delivery. The primary goal of the Maathru Samman Pant is to create a modest and respectful environment, addressing the emotional and physical needs of mothers and their families [16].

In tertiary care hospitals, where patient volume and the complexity of cases can be high, the Maathru Samman Pant offers an easy-to-use solution. Its design facilitates ease of movement and access for medical examinations and interventions, without compromising the privacy of the patient. This is particularly important in crowded hospital settings where maintaining patient dignity can be challenging. Additionally, the pants help to reduce the anxiety and discomfort often associated with labor, contributing to a more positive birth experience [17].

The use of Maathru Samman Pants aligns with the principles of respectful maternity care, which emphasizes the importance of treating women with dignity, ensuring informed consent, and providing emotional support throughout the childbirth process. By incorporating these pants into standard maternity care protocols, tertiary care hospitals can significantly improve the overall quality of care provided to expectant mothers, fostering a more supportive and respectful birthing environment [16, 17].

This study is the first to evaluate pregnant women’s satisfaction with Maathru Samman Pants across diverse socio-demographic groups in four regions of India’s tertiary healthcare settings, offering broad insights into its relevance for both first-time and experienced mothers. The study’s real-world applicability is underscored by its focus on practical usability in often crowded and complex hospital environments, highlighting Maathru Samman Pants as a culturally appropriate, user-friendly innovation capable of improving maternal experiences and setting a precedent for adoption in similar healthcare settings.

The primary limitation of the study was that study was done among pregnant women planned for elective normal labour. The use of Maathru Samaan Pants among pregnant women arriving at hospitals with a fully dilated cervix is restricted due to the insufficient time available for wearing them.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the study reflects strong satisfaction, acceptability, and demand for Maathru Samman Pants among studied pregnant women, emphasizing their role in providing respectful maternity care, privacy, comfort, and cultural appropriateness during labor. High satisfaction, acceptability, and demand for Maathru Samman Pants in all four geographical regions of government tertiary health care facilities of India reflects the need for MSP and its widespread acceptability. The use of Maathru Samaan Pants in government tertiary healthcare settings is highly recommended to enhance maternal comfort and dignity during childbirth. These pants are specifically designed to provide respectful maternity care. Adopting Maathru Samaan Pants can significantly reduce discomfort and exposure, promoting a positive childbirth experience.

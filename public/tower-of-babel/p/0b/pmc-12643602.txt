Retrospective analysis of the correlation between MRP2 expression in circulating tumor cells and molecular characteristics of breast cancer
Authors: Jiayu Guan, Fuping Li, Wenbin Zhou, Mandi Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Breast cancer is recognized as one of the most common malignant tumors affecting women, with the challenge of multidrug resistance presenting a particularly critical concern in its therapeutic management. Among the family of ABCC transporter proteins, the MRP2 is prominently expressed in breast cancer tissues and may contribute to the treatment resistance observed in clinical settings. This study aims to elucidate the correlation between the expression levels of MRP2 protein in circulating tumor cells (CTCs) and the molecular characteristics linked to breast cancer. A cohort of 51 patients diagnosed with breast cancer was recruited for this investigation; However, 3 individuals exhibited absent MRP2 expression and were subsequently excluded from the analysis. CTCs were isolated and classified from peripheral blood samples using CanPatrol technology. The expression level of MRP2 was detected using comprehensive multiplex mRNA in situ hybridization, and statistical evaluations, which encompass the χ2 test and multivariate logistic regression analysis, were performed to assess the correlation between MRP2 expression ratio and pathological parameters. Our results showed significant differences in MRP2 expression ratios between estrogen receptor (ER) and progesterone receptor (PR) (P = .021, P = .004), as well as significant differences in subtypes of luminal breast cancer and triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) (P = .021, P = .048). Multivariate regression analysis indicated that MRP2 expression level also had a statistically significant impact on PR (OR = 0.901; 95% CI 0.814–0.998; P = .046). In the low MRP2 expression group, the pathological type of breast cancer was mainly characterized by positive ER and PR; predominantly luminal type breast cancer. In contrast, in the high MRP2 expression group, the pathological type of breast cancer was mainly characterized by negative ER and PR; predominantly TNBC. High MRP2 expression was independently associated with PR-negative status. In conclusion, the present investigation reveals a noteworthy correlation between elevated levels of MRP2 protein in CTCs and TNBC. This finding deepens our understanding of the molecular mechanisms that contribute to the metastasis of breast cancer and provides a scientific basis for predicting treatment outcomes in breast cancer patients exhibiting elevated levels of MRP2 protein expression.

1. Introduction

Breast cancer ranks among the most prevalent malignant tumors affecting women and stands as a primary contributor to cancer-related mortality in this population.[1–4] The global incidence and mortality rates of breast cancer are increasing, with forecasts indicating that the number of cases is expected to surpass 3 million by the year 2040.[5] Breast cancer has complex molecular characteristics, and according to immunohistochemical results, it is generally categorized into 4 primary subtypes: Luminal A, Luminal B, HER-2-enriched, and triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC).[6] These subtypes show significant differences in how they respond to treatment and develop drug resistance.[7–9] Research shows that multidrug resistance-associated protein 2 (MRP2) is found in some cancer tissues and may be linked to drug resistance in clinical settings. MRP2 (ATP-binding cassette C2) is a transmembrane transport protein in the ATP-binding cassette transporter C subfamily,[10,11] mainly responsible for pumping out chemotherapy drugs. The expression level of MRP2 is increased in tumor cells and is linked to resistance to the chemotherapeutic drug cisplatin.[12]

Breast cancer is a highly heterogeneous malignant tumor, and tissue biopsies only provide localized information and can’t be monitored continuously.[13] Liquid biopsies address this limitation by providing a more comprehensive view.[14,15] Circulating tumor cells (CTCs) refer to neoplastic cells that are disseminated into the bloodstream from either primary tumors or metastatic sites, thereby conveying crucial biological data regarding the tumor.[16–18] Currently, the relationship between MRP2 expression in CTCs and the molecular characteristics of breast cancer is still unclear.[19,20] This study explores the association between MRP2 expression and estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and HER-2 status, and analyzes MRP2 expression patterns in CTCs from different breast cancer subtypes. Analyzing the relationship between MRP2 expression levels in CTCs and the molecular characteristics of breast cancer will help better understand its biological behavior and provide a basis for personalized treatment.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Patient selection

From September 2022 to March 2024, a cohort of 51 patients who received a pathological diagnosis of breast cancer was enrolled at Shenzhen People’s Hospital. (see Table 2), and CTC and MRP2 tests were conducted. Clinical characteristics of 48 patients were collected (excluding 3 patients who were MRP2 negative), including age, gender, treatment methods, and other pathological features for statistical analysis. Inclusion criteria: patients diagnosed with primary breast cancer and who underwent CTC and MRP2 testing. Exclusion criteria are as follows: patients without MRP2 expression detected; patients with severe primary diseases and chronic diseases, such as kidney failure; patients with other malignant tumors in the past 5 years (Fig. 1A). The present investigation engaged human subjects and was executed in alignment with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethical clearance was secured from the Ethics Committee of Shenzhen People’s Hospital (Shenzhen, China; ethical approval number: LL-KY-2024078-01). Prior to the commencement of the study, all participants provided their written informed consent, thereby guaranteeing their confidentiality and anonymity during the entirety of the research.

2.2. MRP2 detection

CanPatrol CTC detection platform (SurExam) enriches and identifies CTCs in blood samples. RNA in situ hybridization analysis is used to detect MRP2. The MRP2 detection process is shown in Figure 1B. The specific steps are as follows: collect 5 mL of peripheral blood samples from patients using EDTA anticoagulant blood collection tubes, add 15ml of red blood cell lysis solution (lysis solution formulation: 154 mM NH4Cl, 10 mM KHCO3 and 0.1 mM EDTA); fix the cell pellet with 4% formaldehyde; transfer the fixed cells to a filtration tube containing a filter membrane (pore size of 8uM); wash 3 times with PBS, add specific capture probes epithelial biomarker probe EpCAM, CK8/18/19, mesenchymal biomarker probe vimentin and twist for hybridization, MRP2 probe (the probe is synthesized by Shanghai Sanggen Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China)); elute 3 times with 1000ul elution solution (formulation: 0.1 × SSC [Sigma, St. Louis]); add 3 kinds of fluorescent protein-labeled probes (probe sequences see Table 1), namely fluorescent dye Alexa Fluor 594 (for labeling epithelial biomarker probes EpCAM, CK8/18/19), Alexa Fluor 488 (mesenchymal biomarker probes vimentin and twist) and Alexa Fluor 647 (for MRP2 mRNA), Specifically, see Figure 2; observe the samples using an automated fluorescence scanning microscope.

2.3. Classification and grouping

Grouping: The conventional criteria for classifying a tumor as ER positive stipulate that at least 1% of the tumor cells must exhibit positive staining for ERs. Similarly, a tumor is categorized as PR positive when a minimum of 1% of the tumor cells demonstrate positive staining for PRs. Her-2 (3+) indicates a positive result, while Her-2 (1+) indicates a negative result. In the case of Her-2 (2+), further FISH testing is required. In the context of axillary lymph nodes, the detection of 1 or more lymph nodes that show signs of involvement is classified as positive. Tumor dimensions are categorized into 2 distinct groups, using a threshold of 2 cm. CTCs that express MRP2 are further classified into 2 categories; those with high expression are identified as having levels >75%, whereas those with low expression are defined as having levels of 75% or below. MRP2 signal points refer to the number of detected MRP2 protein expression markers. Reference thresholds: 0 signal points indicate no expression, 1 to 2 signal points indicate low expression, 3 to 9 signal points indicate moderate expression, and ≥ 10 signal points indicate high expression (Fig. 3).

The distribution of epithelial, hybrid, and mesenchymal-type CTCs expressing MRP2 protein is shown. CTCs = circulating tumor cells, MRP2 = multidrug resistance-associated protein 2.

2.4. Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis: The clinical and pathological data obtained were examined utilizing SPSS software version 27.0 (Chicago). Categorical variables are displayed as frequencies and percentages. Comparisons between groups were conducted using the χ2 test. In instances where expected frequencies were <5, yet equal to or >1, Fisher exact test was utilized. Additionally, logistic regression analysis was implemented to investigate multifactorial associations. A P-value that is lower than .05 is regarded as statistically significant.

3. Results

3.1. Clinical, pathological, and treatment stratification of enrolled patients

The 51 initially enrolled patients were diagnosed with primary breast cancer. Among the final 48 patients after exclusions, there was 1 male and 47 females. 5 patients were under 40, with a median age of 37, and 43 patients were 40 or older, with a median age of 48. For tumor staging, 36 cases were stage II or below (75%), and 12 cases were stage II or higher (25%); for breast cancer molecular subtypes, 34 cases were hormone receptor-positive breast cancer (70.8%), 3 cases were Her-2 overexpressing (6.3%), and 11 cases were TNBC (22.9%); 2 cases were ductal carcinoma in situ (4.2%), 33 cases were invasive ductal carcinoma (68.8%), 10 cases were invasive lobular carcinoma (20.8%); 3 cases were invasive special type breast cancer (IC-ST, 6.2%).

Stratified by the treatment they received, these 48 patients were divided into 4 groups: Neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC, n = 16): patients received an anthracycline-based chemotherapy regimen (AC: doxorubicin + cyclophosphamide) before surgery; Adjuvant chemotherapy (n = 22): patients received chemotherapy after surgery using the same chemotherapy regimen based on anthracyclines or platinum drugs; Sole endocrine therapy (n = 8): hormone receptor-positive (ER+/PR+) patients received tamoxifen or aromatase inhibitors and did not receive chemotherapy; Untreated (n = 2): patients who were recently diagnosed and had not undergone any anticancer therapy prior to the collection of their blood samples (Table 2). 3.2. The expression and distribution of MRP2 protein in epithelial-type CTCs, mixed-type CTCs, and mesenchymal-type CTCs

As shown in Figure 3. In epithelial-type CTCs, the positive expression rate of MRP2 is 76%, with low expression, medium expression, and high expression accounting for 36%, 25%, and 15% respectively; in mixed-type CTCs, the positive expression rate of MRP2 is 86%, with low expression, medium expression, and high-expression accounting for 34%, 41%, and 10% respectively; while in mesenchymal-type CTCs, the positive expression rate of MRP2 is 64%, with low expression, medium expression, and high-expression accounting for 57%, 7%, and 0% respectively. Comparative analysis shows that the proportion of high expression of MRP2 in epithelial-type CTCs is higher than that in the other 2 types; the positive expression rate and medium expression proportion in mixed-type CTCs are relatively high; in contrast, the low-expression proportion in mesenchymal-type CTCs is the highest.

3.3. Relationship between MRP2 expression and pathological features

In the MRP2 high-expression group, the number of ER-negative cases was 14, accounting for 37.8% (14/37); the number of PR-negative cases was 17, accounting for 45.9% (17/37). In the MRP2 low-expression group, all cases were ER and PR positive, with 0 cases of ER and PR-negative, accounting for 0%. The differences between the 2 groups were statistically significant (P = .021; P = .004). The number of luminal type cases in the MRP2 low-expression group was 11, accounting for 100% (11/11), with 0 cases of TNBC, accounting for 0%; in the MRP2 high-expression group, the number of luminal type cases was 23, accounting for 62.2% (23/37), and TNBC was 11 cases, accounting for 29.7% (11/37). The luminal subtype was found to be more prevalent in the cohort characterized by low MRP2 expression when compared to the cohort with high MRP2 expression levels. In contrast, the incidence of TNBC was significantly lower in the low-expression group relative to the high-expression group. These observations indicated notable statistical differences between the 2 cohorts (P = .021; P = .048). Furthermore, no statistically significant differences were identified in MRP2 expression levels among patients with positive versus negative axillary lymph nodes, HER-2 positive versus negative patients, or those with tumor sizes >2 cm compared to those with tumor sizes equal to or <2 cm (Table 3).

4. Discussion

An in-depth investigation was conducted to explore the correlation between the expression levels of MRP2 in CTCs and various molecular characteristics linked to breast cancer. The assessed molecular features include ER, PR, HER-2. This analysis facilitates a real-time and multidimensional understanding of the biological behavior exhibited by breast cancer.[21,22] The findings indicated that within the cohort exhibiting elevated MRP2 expression, ER and PR statuses in breast cancer were more frequently negative, with a notable inclination towards the TNBC molecular subtype. In contrast, in the low MRP2 expression group, ER and PR of breast cancer were more often positive, and the molecular subtype tended to be tubular breast cancer (see Table 3). We speculate that the higher proportion of TNBC patients using chemotherapy drugs may lead to increased MRP2 protein expression in TNBC tumor cells. In the multivariable logistic regression analysis (see Table 4), MRP2 expression was significantly associated with PR status. Specifically, the PR-negative status was positively correlated with increased MRP2 expression, and the difference was statistically significant. High MRP2 expression may indicate that the patient’s breast cancer is PR negative.

Considering that breast cancer is a highly heterogeneous malignancy, conducting MRP2 detection by analyzing tumor-derived substances (such as CTCs) in blood or other body fluids can provide tumor cell information more quickly, frequently, and comprehensively.[23] This method has significant advantages over obtaining tumor tissue from biopsy.[24,25] CTCs are tumor cells that shed from primary tumors or metastatic sites and enter the blood circulation, with their phenotypes dynamically changing between epithelial-type (E-CTCs), mesenchymal-type (M-CTCs), and mixed-type (E/M-CTCs).[26] During the progression of cancer, especially in advanced stages, driven by epithelial-mesenchymal transition, CTCs often exhibit stronger mesenchymal characteristics. These cells gradually transform into a mesenchymal phenotype with stronger aggressiveness and migratory ability, thereby promoting tumor metastasis and drug resistance.[27,28] The multidrug resistance protein MRP2 expressed on CTCs may synergistically act with these phenotypic changes. This study found that, compared to other types of breast cancer, most CTCs detected in TNBC express MRP2. The high-expression level of MRP2 may promote chemotherapy resistance in breast cancer cells.

Numerous investigations have predominantly concentrated on CTCs; Nevertheless, a significant gap exists in the extensive investigation of the association between MRP2 protein expression in CTCs and the molecular attributes of breast cancer. The objective of this research is to assess the connection between MRP2 protein levels in CTCs and the molecular characteristics of breast cancer, while also probing into the mechanisms that facilitate breast cancer metastasis. This emphasis highlights the principal novelty of the present study.

This study has certain limitations: the samples were derived from 48 patients in a limited geographical area, resulting in a small sample size and limited statistical power. These findings can only be regarded as exploratory results. Future research should be conducted for further validation in a broader population. In addition, the CanPatrol platform used for detection in this study has limitations, as its ability to capture very small CTCs is weak, and the capture efficiency is influenced by various factors, including blood sample processing, cell viability, and the sensitivity of the detection methods themselves.[29,30] These factors may lead to false negatives or false positives, thereby affecting clinical decision-making.

5. Conclusion

The findings from our study demonstrate a significant correlation between the expression profile of MRP2 in CTCs and the immunohistochemical (IHC) features as well as the molecular classification of breast cancer. Importantly, our results indicate a robust relationship between MRP2 expression in CTCs and the subtypes of breast cancer that are negative for ERs, PRs, and triple-negative. This implies that MRP2 expression could function as a crucial predictive biomarker for evaluating treatment responses in these particular groups of patients.

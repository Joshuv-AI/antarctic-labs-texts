Introduction

The human skin, the body's largest organ, serves as a critical protective barrier against external environmental factors while maintaining internal moisture balance and contributing significantly to aesthetic appearance [1, 2]. Proper management of the stratum corneum (SC), the outermost layer of the epidermis, is essential for maintaining optimal skin health [3]. The SC consists of terminally differentiated corneocytes interconnected by corneodesmosomes and embedded within a complex lipid matrix. This structure undergoes continuous natural desquamation to maintain epidermal homeostasis, with a typical turnover cycle of 15–25 days [4, 5, 6, 7]. However, under pathological conditions such as aging, xerosis, or psoriasis, this natural turnover process becomes significantly delayed, resulting in SC thickening, enlarged pore appearance, increased acne susceptibility, uneven skin texture, and hyperpigmentation [8, 9].

Chemical exfoliation has emerged as a fundamental cosmetic strategy for SC rejuvenation, wherein active agents function as calcium chelators to disrupt intercorneocyte adhesion through corneodesmosome degradation, thereby promoting accelerated cellular regeneration [10, 11]. Among the various chemical exfoliants available, alpha hydroxy acids (AHAs) represent the most prominent and extensively studied class of ingredients. AHAs offer multiple dermatological benefits, including skin texture smoothing, reduction of surface dullness, enhancement of skin brightness, diminishment of hyperpigmentation, pore appearance improvement, acne prevention, and anti‐aging effects through stimulation of fibroblast activity and collagen synthesis, ultimately contributing to skin barrier strengthening [12, 13, 14].

However, according to the cosmetic ingredient review (CIR) safety assessment, AHAs present considerable risks of skin irritation, barrier function compromise, and increased photosensitivity—making the skin more susceptible to UV‐induced erythema and sunburn—when used at elevated concentrations or severely reduced pH levels. Consequently, current guidelines recommend limiting glycolic acid concentration for consumer home‐use applications to a maximum of 10 wt% while maintaining a pH of 3.5 or higher. This safety constraint necessitates formulation optimization to ensure that these restricted concentrations are delivered effectively into the skin [15].

While glycolic acid (MW 76 Da) demonstrates superior penetration capabilities to target the SC and epidermis due to its exceptionally small molecular size, its actual performance is highly dependent on the pH environment. At pH levels exceeding 3.83 (the pKa value of glycolic acid), there is a rapid and substantial decrease in SC permeability due to the ionization of the carboxyl functional groups, which significantly impairs the acid's penetration capacity. These inherent limitations significantly constrain consumer demand for enhanced efficacy in daily‐use cosmetic formulations under safe pH conditions [12, 16].

Recent innovations in cosmetic science have increasingly focused on developing advanced delivery systems to improve active ingredient permeability and bioavailability [17]. Ion‐pairing represents a promising approach, involving the formation of reversible complexes through electrostatic interactions or hydrogen bonding mechanisms, which effectively reduces charge‐related penetration barriers and enhances transdermal delivery without requiring covalent chemical modifications [18, 19, 20]. While biphasic chemical peels (such as lactic acid‐based systems) exist in patent literature and nanoemulsion technologies have shown enhanced exfoliation effects in kinetically stable systems, non‐emulsified two‐phase formulations specifically incorporating glycolic acid and lecithin—leveraging shake‐induced temporary hydrogen bonding for penetration enhancement—remain largely unexplored in the scientific literature, particularly with respect to clinical efficacy comparisons [21]. Lecithin, a naturally occurring amphiphilic penetration enhancer, has been extensively utilized in liposomal formulations and organogel systems; however, its application in glycolic acid‐specific temporary complexation for targeted SC delivery has not been systematically investigated [22].

This study serves as a comprehensive proof‐of‐concept investigation for enhancing the daily exfoliation efficacy of glycolic acid through temporary ion pairing facilitated by a plant‐derived oil phase containing lecithin. The innovative formulation requires user agitation to induce transient ion pair formation between water‐soluble glycolic acid (10 wt%, pH 3.9) and lecithin (0.1 wt%) dispersed in the oil phase. This mechanism effectively improves SC penetration while mitigating the pH‐dependent permeability reduction that occurs above pH 3.9, thereby enhancing overall exfoliation effectiveness. Following topical application, the two phases naturally separate within the skin environment, preventing excessive penetration into deeper dermal tissues while providing optimal superficial exfoliation.

The underlying mechanism of action was characterized using Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FT‐IR) analysis, and exfoliation efficacy was quantitatively assessed using a standardized porcine skin Franz cell membrane model, with direct comparison to commercially available glycolic acid formulations (including aqueous solutions and oil‐in‐water (O/W) cream formulations). A comprehensive two‐week clinical evaluation was conducted to assess multiple efficacy parameters, including quantification of desquamated corneocytes, skin brightness and texture measurements, pore number, affected area, and depth analysis, as well as come done evaluation, compared to conventional formulations. Finally, the suitability of the biphasic system for sensitive skin applications was rigorously evaluated through lactic acid sting testing (LAST) methodology, with direct comparison to standard glycolic acid solutions. Therefore, the primary aim of this study is to investigate a novel biphasic serum leveraging shake‐induced temporary ion‐pairing between lecithin and glycolic acid to enhance SC penetration at a consumer‐safe pH of 3.9. We comprehensively evaluate its ex vivo permeation and exfoliation efficiency, alongside an in Vivo clinical assessment of its exfoliating efficacy and sensitive skin tolerability compared to conventional aqueous and cream formulations.

Materials and Methods

Manufacturing of Serum Formulations and Shaking Protocol for Temporary Ion‐Pairing Induction

The composition of the biphasic serum is presented in Table 1. The aqueous phase was prepared by heating deionized Milli‐Q water to 65°C, followed by the addition of the components, including glycolic acid (Sigma‐Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA). The mixture was then stirred using a disper mixer at 2000 rpm and 60°C for 15 min until a clear phase was obtained. The pH was adjusted to 3.9 using sodium hydroxide (Sigma‐Aldrich). The oil phase was prepared by heating caprylic/capric triglyceride (KLK OLEO, Petaling Jaya, Malaysia), isononyl isononanoate (CIDOLS Sdn Bhd, Port Klang, Malaysia), tocopherol (DSM‐Firmenich, Kaiseraugst, Switzerland, and Maastricht, Netherlands), and lecithin (DS‐Sun PC90, Croda International plc, Snaith, UK) to 70°C and stirring with a disper mixer at 1000 rpm for 10 min. The oil phase was then combined with the aqueous phase to form the biphasic serum. The final biphasic serum had a pH of 3.9 ± 0.1.

To induce temporary ion‐pairing between lecithin and glycolate, the biphasic serum was shaken moderately to vigorously for 10–15 s, with at least 10 shakes performed. Shaking was continued until oil droplets formed, the mixture appeared as a single phase, and the aqueous phase became opaque, ensuring effective transient bonding for enhanced permeation and exfoliation efficacy.

Analysis of Temporary Ion‐Pairing Using FT‐IR

Temporary ion‐pairing was assessed by observing spectral changes in the functional groups of each component when dissolved in glycerine (Dow Chemical, Midland, MI, USA) as a cosolvent, with the spectral influence of glycerine subtracted during analysis. For all FT‐IR analyses, the finalized biphasic serum containing 10 wt% glycolic acid and 0.1 wt% lecithin was utilized. Additionally, IR spectra were analyzed before and after mixing the biphasic serum (specifically measured at 16 h post‐shaking to ensure complete phase separation) to confirm the transient nature of ion‐pairing. Spectral changes were evaluated using a Vertex 70 FTIR spectrometer equipped with a Hyperion 2000 microscope (Bruker Instruments, Billerica, MA, USA) over a range of 4000–500 cm− 1 at a resolution of 1 cm− 1. Opus 5.5 software (Bruker Instruments) was used for spectral processing and deconvolution.

Ex Vivo Assessment of Permeation Efficacy Using Porcine Skin

The permeation study was conducted at 35°C using phosphate‐buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.4) as the receptor medium. Porcine back skin samples (3.0 × 3.0 cm2; 200 µm thickness comprising the SC, epidermis, and superficial dermal layers, or full‐thickness skin; APURES, Pyeongtaek, Korea) were prepared and thoroughly washed with PBS. These samples were derived from micropig‐based full‐thickness cutaneous membranes (FCMs) manufactured by APURES, a company certified for alternative animal testing and research facilities. The FCMs were transported and stored at −20°C in a PBS‐impregnated state immediately after production and used within 1 week. The skin sample was mounted in a Franz diffusion cell between the donor and receptor compartments, with the SC facing the donor compartment and the dermis facing the receptor compartment. The receptor compartment was filled with PBS, and a 10 wt% glycolic acid solution (pH 3.9; toner), oil‐in‐water (O/W) cream (pH 3.9), or freshly shaken biphasic serum (containing 10 wt% glycolic acid and a fixed 0.1 wt% lecithin, pH 3.9) was applied to the SC immediately after agitation to maximize temporary ion‐pair delivery. Samples were withdrawn from the receptor at 0, 4, 8, and 16 h, and the amount of permeated glycolic acid was quantified using high‐performance liquid chromatography (HPLC; Agilent 1260 Infinity II, Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). The HPLC system was equipped with a Phenomenex Gemini NX‐C18 column (250 × 4.6 mm, 5 µm). Samples were manually injected, and separation was achieved with a mobile phase at a flow rate of 1 mL/min. Glycolic acid was detected at 210 nm, and methanolic glycolic acid solutions served as standards. Experiments were performed in quadruplicate (n = 4), and results are presented as mean ± standard deviation.

Measurement of Exfoliating Activity Using Porcine Skin

Exfoliation efficiency was evaluated by quantifying corneocyte release from porcine skin disks (2.5 × 2.5 cm; FCMs, Apures). Following thawing and PBS washing, skin samples were prepared as 6 mm diameter disks using a punch biopsy technique and placed in 96‐well plates. Test formulations (aqueous solution, O/W cream, and biphasic serum) were prepared at identical concentrations (1, 5, or 10 wt% glycolic acid), viscosity, and pH, then diluted to various concentrations using distilled water. PBS‐treated porcine disks served as negative controls. Samples (100 µL) containing 0.1% Triton X‐100 (Sigma‐Aldrich, MO, USA) were added to each well. The same fixed concentration of Triton X‐100 (0.1%) was applied to all wells, including negative control (PBS‐only) wells, to eliminate any contribution of the detergent to corneocyte release; thus, observed differences in desquamation could be confidently attributed solely to the test formulations. Incubation was performed at 37°C with 50% relative humidity for 1, 4, and 24 h. Corneocyte counting was performed by transferring 10 µL of supernatant from each well to a bright‐line hemacytometer (Sigma‐Aldrich, MO, USA) to quantify released corneocytes from porcine skin disks [9, 23].

Clinical Study Overview

All clinical evaluations were conducted by a specialized clinical research organization responsible for participant recruitment, product allocation, and standardized instrumental measurements. The study adhered to the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, the Bioethics and Safety Act of the Ministry of Health and Welfare of the Republic of Korea, and the Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) of the Korea Institute of Dermatological Sciences (KIDS), where the evaluations were performed. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board of KIDS (approval number: KIDS‐BEG029‐SNC). A double‐blind, placebo‐controlled clinical study was conducted from July 7, 2025, to August 18, 2025. The sample size of participants was determined based on an a priori power analysis to achieve 80% power at a 5% significance level (α = 0.05), anticipating a minimum 15% difference in the primary endpoint (Desquamation Index) between the split‐face test sites, derived from the variance observed in our preliminary formulation screening. Thirty‐seven adult women (aged 39–58 years; mean age: 45.33 ± 2.87 years) voluntarily participated after reading a written explanation of the study procedures and providing written informed consent based on their own volition. This included detailed information about the study procedures. To ensure strict allocation concealment and double‐blinding, products were provided in identical, coded containers by an independent pharmacist not involved in the assessment. Randomization was performed using computer‐generated random sequences with block randomization. Products were provided in identical, coded containers by an independent pharmacist not involved in the study. Randomization was performed using computer‐generated random sequences with block randomization.

During the 2‐week trial, participants divided their faces into left and right halves and applied different test products to each side (split‐face design). They were instructed to avoid sunscreens, functional cosmetics, body moisturizers, packs, massages, or any other treatments that could influence results. Evaluations were performed at baseline and after 2 weeks. Prior to measurements, sites were cleansed with a standardized cleanser and acclimatized for 30 min under strictly controlled environmental conditions (temperature: 22 ± 2°C; humidity: 50 ± 5%).

Inclusion criteria included healthy adult women with self‐reported mild to moderate signs of skin aging (uneven texture, dullness, enlarged pores, or hyperkeratinization), Fitzpatrick skin types II–IV, and a willingness to comply with the study protocol. Participants must not have used exfoliating agents or topical treatments within 4 weeks prior to enrollment.

Exclusion criteria included a history of dermatologic diseases (e.g., eczema, psoriasis, acne vulgaris), known allergies to glycolic acid, lecithin, or any formulation component, pregnancy or lactation, use of systemic medications affecting the skin (e.g., retinoids, steroids), recent cosmetic procedures (peels or lasers within 6 months), or any condition potentially interfering with the study (e.g., excessive UV exposure).

This study was designed as a comparative randomized controlled trial (RCT) evaluating the biphasic serum versus two conventional formulations (aqueous solution and O/W cream), all containing 10 wt% glycolic acid at pH 3.9. Each participant was randomized to apply one formulation to one facial side and another to the opposite side, enabling balanced within‐participant comparisons. No true placebo was included, as the aim was to compare the relative performance of active cosmetic formulations. The primary endpoint was the exfoliation efficacy (Desquamation index), and the secondary endpoints included skin brightness, texture, hydration, and pore parameters. No protocol deviations occurred during the trial period. This study reporting follows the CONSORT 2025 guidelines (see Supplementary CONSORT Checklist). As this was a non‐therapeutic cosmetic efficacy study, formal registration in a clinical trial registry (e.g., ClinicalTrials.gov) was not required under applicable cosmetic research guidelines.

Comparative Evaluation of Suitability for Sensitive Skin via Redness and Transepidermal Water Loss (TEWL)

To evaluate product suitability for sensitive skin, participants identified as sensitive via a lactic acid stinging test applied toner or biphasic serum to both sides of the face twice daily for 2 weeks after cleansing, allowing absorption. The lactic acid stinging test involved a preliminary questionnaire to screen for sensitive skin, followed by a single‐blind application of 50 µL of 10% lactic acid solution and 50 µL of 0.9% sodium chloride solution to the nasolabial folds by the same researcher. Stinging sensation was scored 2 min post‐application on a 4‐point scale (0 = none, 1 = mild, 2 = moderate, 3 = severe). If no sensation occurred after 10 min or if the lactic acid site scored equal to or lower than the saline site, skin was classified as normal. Sensitive skin was confirmed if the lactic acid site's stinging score was significantly higher (p < 0.05) than the saline site's, using the Wilcoxon signed‐rank test. The evaluation was conducted against a 10 wt% pH 3.9 biphasic serum and a 10 wt% pH 3.9 simple glycolic solution as a control. Suitability was assessed by measuring reductions in skin redness using the JANUS‐PRO full‐face photography system (L* value analysis, Pie, Suwon‐si, Korea) and ImageJ software, and TEWL using a Vapometer (Delfin Technologies Ltd., Kuopio, Finland).

Adverse Reaction Assessment and Questionnaire Survey

Throughout the study, the administrator meticulously monitored test sites for pre‐specified adverse reactions, including erythema, swelling, scaling, itching, stinging, burning, tightness, and tingling, grading and recording any occurrences. No adverse reactions occurred or were reported by any participant during the trial. Participants also completed a questionnaire on general skin characteristics, pre‐ and post‐use skin condition, sensory experiences, and perceived improvements after using the biphasic serum (results in Table 2). No adverse reactions were reported.

Results

Preparation and Structural Analysis of Biphasic Serum

The biphasic serum was formulated to achieve shaking‐induced temporary ion‐pairing between lecithin and glycolic acid by combining aqueous and oil phases as detailed in Table 1. A lecithin concentration of 0.1 wt% (w/w of total formulation) was selected based on preliminary optimization studies, as it was determined to be optimal for evaluating the formation and structural analysis of temporary ion‐pairing, along with associated efficacy changes. While lecithin effects are not restricted to this concentration, the sensory advantages of the biphasic serum—derived from minimal or absent emulsifier content—diminish significantly when lecithin exceeds 0.3wt%, limiting industrial viability. For instance, reductions in moisture and cooling sensations, along with increased oiliness (typically associated with surfactant application to the skin), were observed. Upon shaking and mixing the biphasic serum, intermolecular bonds form between lecithin and glycolate, delaying phase separation, as illustrated in Figure 1. Notably, turbidity in the water phase persists for more than 1 h at lecithin concentrations of 0.05 wt% or higher, indicating sustained ion‐pairing with lecithin. However, this intermolecular bond dissociates within 16 h, resulting in complete separation into the initial biphasic state across all evaluated concentrations.

Phase behavior and separation characteristics of biphasic serum as a function of lecithin concentration.

Intermolecular bonding between lecithin and glycolic acid was confirmed through FT‐IR spectroscopy analysis using glycerin as co‐solvent for individual and combined components (Figure 2). First, Glycolic acid is exhibited a strong IR absorption peak for the C = O bond of the carboxyl group at 1733 cm− 1 (in the red shaded area and the expanded spectrum in the box). After mixing with lecithin, the corresponding peak shift from 1733 cm− 1 to 1722 cm− 1 (Δν = 11 cm− 1). This is because the functional group forms an intermolecular bond and the absorption wavelength red‐shifts due to the relative weakening of the intramolecular bond. Next, the blue‐shaded region and expanded spectra compares lecithin is used alone or mixed; these spectra were de‐noised and the solvent (glycerin) spectra were removed. For lecithin alone, characteristic peaks for the phosphatidyl (PO2 −) bond at 1090 cm− 1 and the choline (C‐N) bond at 970 cm− 1 were clearly observed, but in the mixture, the disappearance of these peaks confirmed that the characteristic absorption wavelength of the functional group changed due to the formation of intermolecular bonds. Since the aforementioned spectral changes simulate the mixed state after shaking the ideal serum, it is necessary to confirm that they exhibit the same spectra before actual mixing and after complete separation. Spectra obtained from completely separated aqueous and oil phases (measured exactly at 16 h post‐shaking, as confirmed by the time‐dependent separation shown in Figure 1) were identical to their respective pre‐mixing states, demonstrating that the biphasic serum forms reversible and temporary ion‐pairing.

Spectra show individual components before mixing, mixed lecithin and glycolic acid using cosolvent (glycerin), and separated phases after complete phase separation (measured at 16 h post‐shaking), demonstrating reversible ion‐pair formation.

Ex Vivo Evaluation of the Improvement of Permeation and Exfoliation by Temporary Ion‐pairing of a Biphasic Serum

To confirm the improved efficacy of the biphasic serum as a skincare product in a cosmetic study, we utilized a Franz diffusion cell‐based technique to evaluate skin permeation and exfoliation, respectively. This was compared with conventional aqueous solutions (skin toner formulations) and oil‐in‐water cream formulations containing the same glycolic acid concentration 10 wt% and pH 3.9 conditions. Firstly, ex vivo skin permeation was assessed at 0, 4, 8, and 16 h post‐application (Figure 3). Effective exfoliation requires rapid and targeted SC penetration, as well as prolonged retention in tissues involved in skin turnover, without penetrating deeper than the epidermis and papillary dermis. The biphasic nature of this study is designed to ensure effective SC penetration through temporary ion‐pairing while not impeding effective delivery to target tissues through natural dissociation over time. To verify this, porcine skin was separated into epidermal (200 µm, square) and full‐thickness layers (diamond‐shape) for quantitative analysis. At 4 h post‐application, glycolic acid delivery through the epidermis via biphasic serum was 4.19‐fold higher (95% CI, 3.12–5.45) than aqueous solution and 9.48‐fold higher (95% CI, 8.21–10.95) than oil‐in‐water cream. These differences were statistically significant (p < 0.05 vs. both controls, n = 4). These results demonstrate that decreased permeation due to glycolic acid ionization at pH 3.9 was overcome through lecithin‐glycolate ion‐pairing, dramatically enhancing initial SC permeation. Although inter‐formulation differences decreased over 16 h, total epidermal glycolic acid delivery remained substantially higher for the biphasic serum. Full‐thickness skin permeation remained undetectable at 4 and 8 h for all formulations, with minimal amounts detected only after 16 h. These findings suggest that the biphasic serum enhances exfoliation efficiency through increased initial permeation while maintaining safety by limiting deep dermal delivery.

Clinical Comparative Evaluation of Chemical Exfoliation‐related Skin Improvement Parameters and Sensitivity Skin Compatibility of Biphase Serum

As specified in the Materials and Methods section, we conducted a comparative clinical evaluation with an aqueous solution (skin toner) and an oil‐in‐water cream over two weeks (Figure 5). Exfoliation efficacy ranked in order: biphasic serum > cream > aqueous solution. The biphasic serum group achieved the highest improvement rate (45.52%; 95% CI, 29.74%–61.30%) with statistical significance (p < 0.001 vs. baseline, as indicated by the asterisks in Figure 5). The biphasic serum group showed significantly higher improvement than both formulations (p < 0.05). This result was also observed in skin brightness measured by the L* value. Although it is difficult to improve skin brightness by promoting skin turnover over a 2‐week period, only the biphasic serum group showed significant improvement. Skin texture roughness (Ra), typically the most responsive parameter to chemical exfoliation, showed significant improvement in all groups, with the biphasic serum demonstrating the highest improvement rate. Contrary to typical concerns regarding dryness following chemical exfoliation, the biphasic serum demonstrated excellent moisturizing effects.

After 2 weeks of biphasic serum application, blackhead/whitehead counts and pore parameters (number, affected area, and maximum depth) showed significant improvements that exceeded those of conventional formulations. These results align with glycolic acid's established efficacy in removing hyperkeratinized comedogenic material and enhancing pore appearance, demonstrating the superior clinical benefits of the biphasic delivery system. Representative clinical images illustrating these visible improvements in exfoliation, texture, and pore parameters—complete with scale bars and highlighted ROIs—are provided in Supplementary Figure S2. Furthermore, to assess subjective user perception, a sensorial evaluation questionnaire was administered (Table 2). The self‐assessment results indicated superior user satisfaction and perceived performance compared to commercially available formulations, including conventional skin toners and creams.

Among participants with sensitive skin complaints, we conducted a lactic acid sting test to screen for skin sensitivity. The biphasic serum was compared against a 10 wt% glycolic acid solution to evaluate its tolerability in sensitive skin by measuring erythema and transepidermal water loss (Figure 6). Over the two‐week evaluation period, the biphasic serum demonstrated significantly greater improvements in both erythema reduction and water loss compared to the control group (vs. Control, p < 0.01). Discussion

This study demonstrates the innovative application of temporary ion‐pairing between lecithin and glycolic acid in a biphasic serum to overcome the fundamental limitations of conventional chemical exfoliation formulations. The primary challenge addressed was the significant reduction in SC permeability that occurs when glycolic acid pH exceeds its pKa value of 3.83, which severely constrains efficacy in consumer‐safe formulations. FT‐IR spectroscopic analysis definitively confirmed the formation of reversible intermolecular bonds between lecithin's phosphatidyl and choline functional groups and glycolic acid's carboxyl moiety. The observed red‐shift in the C = O stretch frequency, coupled with substantial intensity reductions in lecithin's characteristic peaks, provides compelling evidence for hydrogen bonding‐mediated ion‐pair formation. Critically, the complete spectral reversibility following phase separation validates the temporary nature of these interactions, ensuring controlled delivery without permanent chemical modification. The lecithin concentration optimization at 0.1 wt% represents a carefully balanced formulation strategy. While higher concentrations might theoretically enhance ion‐pairing efficiency, our sensory evaluation data clearly demonstrate that concentrations exceeding 0.3 wt% compromise the biphasic system's primary advantage—the absence of traditional emulsifiers that typically cause skin oiliness and reduced sensory appeal. This finding has significant implications for commercial viability, as consumer acceptance remains paramount for successful product development.

The 4.19‐fold enhancement in epidermal glycolic acid delivery compared to conventional aqueous solutions represents a substantial breakthrough in topical exfoliant efficacy. The key point is that the natural dissociation of temporary ion‐pairing over time minimizes deep dermal penetration and maximizes the time it can remain in areas associated with keratin turnover. This selective enhancement mechanism—rapid initial penetration followed by controlled retention through gradual ion‐pair dissociation—represents an optimal delivery profile for chemical exfoliation applications. The time‐dependent permeation profiles reveal that maximum efficacy occurs within the first 4 h post‐application, aligning perfectly with typical consumer usage patterns of twice‐daily application. This temporal advantage effectively compensates for the pH‐dependent permeability reduction that traditionally necessitates more aggressive formulation conditions.

The comprehensive 2‐week clinical evaluation provides robust evidence for superior exfoliation efficacy across multiple objective parameters. The 45.52% improvement in exfoliation efficacy, coupled with significant enhancements in skin brightness, texture, and hydration, demonstrates clinically meaningful benefits that translate directly to consumer‐perceived improvements. Particularly noteworthy is the concurrent moisturizing effect, which directly contradicts the typical drying concerns associated with chemical exfoliation and likely contributes to improved user compliance. The sensitive skin compatibility assessment represents a critical validation of this formulation approach. Participants with sensitive skin were selected using a lactic acid sting test, and the results obtained through a questionnaire assessing changes in redness and transepidermal water loss and irritation confirm that the occlusive oil phase of the biphasic system effectively alleviates the potential for irritation while maintaining exfoliating efficacy.

This study has several limitations. First, the 2‐week clinical evaluation period is sufficient for assessing acute chemical exfoliation but insufficient for evaluating long‐term benefits, such as photoaging improvement. Future research should include extended clinical evaluations (e.g., 8–12 weeks) to adequately assess the chronic use effects of the biphasic serum. Second, the ex vivo porcine skin model may not perfectly replicate human SC characteristics. Finally, the clinical trial was conducted during the summer (July–August), and participants were strictly instructed to avoid sunscreens to prevent variable interference with the test products. Although AHAs are known to increase UV sensitivity, careful clinical monitoring confirmed that no adverse events, including erythema or sunburn, occurred during the study. However, the absence of daily broad‐spectrum sunscreen application during a high‐UV summer period (July–August) represents a significant clinical limitation. This design choice was implemented to avoid confounding the instrumental assessments of the test products; nevertheless, it underscores the necessity for consumers to use broad‐spectrum UV protection when utilizing AHA‐based exfoliants to mitigate the increased risk of photosensitivity, even with formulations that exhibit high skin tolerability.

The mechanistic insights from this study suggest that other chemical exfoliants, including AHAs (lactic acid, mandelic acid) and BHAs (salicylic acid), could potentially benefit from similar ion‐pairing enhancement strategies. Furthermore, various lecithin‐containing plant oils (sunflower seed oil, soybean oil, canola oil, cottonseed oil) could be directly incorporated into the oil phase to enhance penetration while simultaneously providing the additional skincare benefits associated with botanical lipids. The successful demonstration of enhanced efficacy at pH 3.9 with 10 wt% glycolic acid represents a significant advancement in topical delivery strategies. This approach provides a scientifically robust framework for optimizing daily‐use exfoliants while strictly adhering to the regulatory safety standards established by cosmetic ingredient review guidelines. The necessity of mechanical agitation immediately prior to application ensures that the temporary ion‐pairs are freshly induced at the point of delivery, maximizing initial stratum corneum permeability. The reversible nature of this intermolecular interaction, coupled with the absence of traditional emulsifiers, effectively balances high exfoliating activity with the superior sensory profile required for consistent user compliance in daily application regimens. Conclusion

In conclusion, this study validates that shake‐induced temporary ion‐pairing between lecithin and glycolic acid successfully overcomes the inherent pH‐dependent permeability limitations of conventional AHA formulations. At a consumer‐safe pH of 3.9, the biphasic system achieved a 4.19‐fold enhancement in epidermal delivery and a 45.52% improvement in the clinical desquamation index (p < 0.001), without compromising skin barrier integrity or inducing irritation. These findings demonstrate that transient ion‐pairing is an effective and well‐tolerated strategy for topical chemical exfoliation, offering a practical solution to enhance clinical efficacy within established safety and regulatory constraints.

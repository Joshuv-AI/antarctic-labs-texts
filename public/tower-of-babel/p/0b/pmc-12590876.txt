The relationship between emotional intelligence levels, stress management skills and hormonal and cerebral responses in university students
Authors: Bülent Bayraktar, Fetullah Battal
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEmotional intelligence is a person’s ability, capacity and ability to understand, use, understand, manage, anticipate, manage and direct the emotions of oneself or others. Students with high levels of emotional intelligence express themselves better, are more resistant to stress in their lives, and are individuals who can direct their emotions. Leaders with strong emotional intelligence will create synergy for all employees. Therefore, emotional intelligence and stress management skills are important issues in the field of leadership and organizational behavior. At the same time, emotional intelligence and stress management skills interact with hormones. This study aims to examine the emotional intelligence levels and hormone (apelin, cortisol) and cerebral (Brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF)) response of 4th-year students of the faculty of health sciences at the university, who are potential managers of the future in terms of some variables.MethodsThe research was conducted using the cross-sectional data method with 167 university students aged 18–24 who were reported to be clinically healthy in Türkiye. Apelin, cortisol hormone and BDNF levels in the participants’ saliva samples were analyzed using the ELISA technique. The relationship between emotional intelligence level and total scores from the stress coping scale and salivary apelin, cortisol and BDNF levels were evaluated.ResultsIn the study, it was determined that the average level of apelin hormone in saliva was “2.36 ± 0.076)’’ ng/mL in girls and “2.57 ± 0.071’’ ng/mL in boys. In girls, the BDNF level was “3.19 ± 0.97’’ and the cortisol level was “2.04 ± 0.72’’. BDNF level in boys is “3.41 ± 0.94’’and cortisol level is “1.92 ± 0.59’’. There is a significant difference between the participants’ allowances (income levels) and the average levels of salivary apelin hormone, BDNF and emotional intelligence scale (p < 0.01). A positive, strong and statistically significant relationship was found between the participants’ emotional intelligence level and stress management skills and apelin and BDNF levels, and a negative, strong and statistically significant relationship was found between salivary cortisol levels (p˂0.01).ConclusionsHigh emotional intelligence and stress management skills are an important element of strategic importance for a successful management and professional success process in individuals who will work in fields that need people such as health, marketing, law, consultancy, medicine, teaching and the service sector. Our study determined that as university students’ emotional intelligence levels strengthened, the participants’ stress management skills, salivary apelin, and BDNF levels increased, and cortisol hormone levels decreased. As a result, monitoring and examining apelin, BDNF, cortisol hormones can be useful parameters in evaluating the interaction between individuals’ emotional intelligence level and stress management skills and hormone response.Keywords: Emotional intelligence, Stress management skills, Stress, Apelin, BDNF, Cortisol

Introduction

Since faulty and wrong decisions taken in managerial processes can lead to irreparable consequences for organizations, having managers and employees who can make fast and correct decisions and have high emotional intelligence and stress management skills is the key to influencing and directing the future. Emotional intelligence: It is the individual’s ability to understand and control his own and others’ emotions and to use them to make sense of the emotions felt by others and direct their behavior [1, 2]. Additionally, emotional intelligence plays a critical role in professional life in establishing effective leadership, successful communication, and solid working relationships [3].

Individuals with high levels of emotional intelligence can cope with difficult situations and accept this as personal development [4]. The stress coping dimension, one of the main dimensions of emotional intelligence, consists of stress tolerance and impulse control dimensions. The basic elements of emotional intelligence are self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy and social skills [5, 6]. Regardless of their roles, individuals use emotional and rational processes to make decisions in working life. Emotions affect judgment and decision-making by changing the behavior of decision-makers [7].

Hormones are biomolecules that have a significant impact on mood regulation, mood and behavior [8]. Apelin is a hormone secreted from adipose tissue, originating from its 77 amino acid precursor, preproapelin, which has different functions in many physiological systems and processes, including the immune and reproductive systems. There are various isoforms such as apelin-12, apelin-13, apelin-17 and apelin-36. In addition, apelin is a hormone that plays a role in controlling and regulating emotional behaviors [9, 10].

Brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) is a neurotrophic factor that plays an important role in neuron development, survival and maintenance of functions, including neurogenesis and synaptic plasticity. BDNF is present as a regulator in numerous functions in the brain such as memory, learning and mood changes, thanks to the Cyclic AMP Regulatory Element Binding Protein (CREB)-BDNF- Tropomyosin receptor kinase B (TrkB) signaling pathway [10, 11]. A decrease in the CREB/BDNF/TrkB signaling pathway leads to mood disorders such as depression and anxiety [11].

Cortisol is a corticosteroid hormone produced in the shell region of the adrenal gland, associated with the body’s response to stress. When stress becomes chronic, the cortisol hormone level increases, the HPA axis is activated through the relationship between the cortex, amygdala and hippocampus, and brain functions are negatively affected through the glucocorticoid receptors in the neurons and glial cells in the brain [12, 13]. While measuring cortisol concentration in saliva is more traditional and common, collecting samples is simple, non-invasive and stress-free [14]. In addition, apelin [15] and BDNF [16], which constitute the basis of the study, can be measured and evaluated in saliva.

Students with high levels of emotional intelligence can express themselves better, be more resistant to stress in their lives, and direct their emotions. University students are the leading candidates of the future who can contribute to the future and dynamics of the business world and all sectors, especially the service sector. This study examines the emotional intelligence levels and hormone (apelin, cortisol) and brain (BDNF) responses of 4th-grade students at the faculty of health sciences in terms of some variables.

Methods

Participants and procedures

The population of the research consisted of students studying in the 4th year at the faculty of health sciences who continue their education and training activities in Türkiye. The study’s sample size was calculated using the G Power 3.1.9.7 analysis program. At least 138 participants were determined for a 95% confidence interval, 5% margin of error, and 80% participation level. In addition, in the study, the Bootstrap data augmentation factor was activated with the help of the AMOS package program and the data was analyzed and interpreted among 2000 people [17]. With the help of AMOS package program, bootstrap (data increase factor) was activated and data was analyzed and interpreted among 2000 people. In the bootstrap method, the interpretation to be made is made by redrawing some parameters with statistical inferences. Then, this process is repeated many times to be more reliable. In addition, the bootstrap method is superior to variance analysis in cases where the sample distribution is not normal or in very small data sets. Random sampling methods are generally used to increase the representativeness of the research. The methods used in this context are simple random sampling, systematic sampling, stratified sampling and cluster sampling. In simple random sampling, participants to be included in the study are randomly selected. In this method, the participants have equal probability of participating in the research process and the study universe is also homogeneous. For this reason, the homogeneity and reliability of the study will increase [18]. It was used because it is a reliable method of obtaining information in which each member of a population is selected randomly by chance alone. The research was conducted with 167 randomly selected 4th-year students of the faculty of health sciences at the university. Voluntary participation in surveys and saliva tests is encouraged. Students who did not want to participate in the study had hearing problems, took regular medication, or had chronic illnesses were not included. The research conducted by Bayburt University adhered to ethical standards, with approval granted by the Bayburt University Research Ethics Committee (Decision no: 2024-97/4). All participants were thoroughly informed about the study and provided their consent in alignment with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Furthermore, the methodologies employed complied with the journal’s specific guidelines. Individuals who had a psychological or chronic illness or who used medication for any reason were excluded from the study. Participants were assured the right to withdraw from the study at any point, yet none of the randomly selected participants expressed a desire to do so. It is shown in the Flow Diagram (Fig. 1).

Measures

Data were collected in two steps. In the first step, the Personal Information Form, Volunteer Consent Form, Emotional Intelligence Scale and Coping with Stress Scale were distributed to the participants face-to-face and collected in an average of 20 min. In the second step of the study, saliva samples were collected from the same participants.The participants included in the study were told that they should not eat or drink water 1 h before the saliva test. At the same time, the participants of the study were taken to the classrooms at 08:00 and waited for 1 h for the desired time to occur. At the end of the period, a saliva test was taken from the participants. They were asked to keep a detailed food diary 24 h before saliva collection. Saliva samples of 5 ml were collected in Salivette tubes (Sarstedt, GERMANY) using the passive saliva method between 09:00 and 10:00 in the morning. After being centrifuged at 2000 g for 20 min in a refrigerated centrifuge (NF 1200R, NUVE, Ankara, TÜRKIYE) in the laboratory, the saliva samples were stored at -80 °C until apelin hormone analysis. Additionally, all measurements used were developed for this study.

Measurement of apelin, BDNF, and cortisol hormone levels in saliva

The minimum detectable concentrations of hormone kits used to measure apelin, BDNF, and cortisol levels in salivary samples obtained from the Humans were 7-1500 ng/L, 0.5–200 ng/ml, 0.5–200 ng/ml. Human-Specific Human Apelin ELISA Kit (BT LAB, Cat.NoE2014Hu, CHINA), Human Brain-derived neurotrophic factor ELISA Kit (BT LAB, Cat.No E1302Hu, CHİNA), Human Cortisol ELISA Kiti (BT LAB, Cat.No E 1 003Hu, CHİNA) were used in ELISA. Using intra- and inter-assay coefficients of 8.0 and 10.0%, respectively, ELISA followed the manufacturer’s instructions. Absorbance values were recorded at a wavelength of 450 nm. In the personal information form, the participants’ demographic information, such as age, gender, whether the mother and father were working, and the monthly income of the students, were investigated.

Considering the two scales used in the study

To measure Emotional Intelligence, a scale consisting of 20 items, originally developed by Lee and Kwak (2012) [19] and Turkish adapted by Kayıhan and Arslan (2016) [20], was used. To measure coping with stress The 6-item section, originally developed by Moos (1993) [21], and adapted into Turkish by Ballı and Kılıç (2016) was used [22]. Two scales were used in the study. The first of these, the emotional intelligence scale, was originally conducted on 482 participants (435 boys and 47 girls). In the original of the second scale, the test was repeated several times, and, for example, test 2 was tested on 169 participants (41 boys, 128 girls). Cronbach Alpha internal consistency reliability coefficients of the scales were found to be 0.97 for Emotional Intelligence (E.I), 0.95 for Coping with Stress (S.Q), 0.94 for the overall scale reliability value, and 0.77 for Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin Sampling Adequacy. According to the correlation results, between emotional intelligence and coping with stress (r =.73); between the apelin hormone (r =.83); Positive and significant relationships were determined between cerebral BDNF (r =.85). A negative and significant relationship (r = -.79) was determined between emotional intelligence and cortisol level. When the scores obtained from the scales were examined, it was seen that the (E.I) and (S.Q) scales were scored at high levels. In addition, the maximum score that can be obtained from (E.I) in the scales that do not contain reverse questions. The minimum score out of 100 is 20. The score ranges that can be taken from the (S.Q) scale are a maximum of 30 and a minimum of 6. Sample items from the (E.I) scale: “I can control my emotions well.“, “I do not lose hope even when things get bad.” Sample items on the (S.Q) scale are “I try to look at the bright side of things. “I think the problems I experience will change my life in a positive way.” It is in the form.

Data analysis

The data were obtained with the help of Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) 22.0 for Windows (SPSS®, Chicago, Il, USA) and Amos™ original package programs. In the data analysis section, first the normality level of the data was examined. For this, Skewness and Kurtosis values were interpreted according to whether their coefficients were in the range (-1) - (+ 1). Number, percentage, and standard deviation (SD) values of continuous variables were used in descriptive statistics. As a result of these tests, it was seen that the data exhibited a normal distribution, so parametric tests were started. Independent Sample T Test and One-Way ANOVA were used in the first step to compare the perception levels of the participants with their scale scores and salivary apelin levels. Post Hoc test was applied to groups with homogeneous distribution in order to reveal which group had the difference between equal or more than two groups. In the second step, the direction of the relationship between the data and their statistical significance were examined with Pearson correlation analysis. At this point, p <.01** and p <.05* significance levels were examined as statistical significance values. In the last step, the confirmatory factor of the scales in the AMOS package program was examined. After the analyses, standardized regression coefficients were presented with acceptable fit indices.

Discussion

Emotional intelligence is the capacity to recognize, manage and use your own emotions and the emotions of others [25]. Although emotional intelligence is genetic, it is a process that begins to take shape from childhood and changes over time, and it develops over time. Leaders and employees with high emotional intelligence and stress management skills in an organization are important actors that accelerate the success and sustainability of businesses. Although many studies have been conducted on emotional intelligence and stress management skills, the number of studies conducted with different disciplines is rare. At the same time, the relationship of these concepts with hormones and different parameters is discussed for the first time in this study. In this respect, when looking at the latest studies on emotional intelligence and stress, it can be seen that it can be affected by sociodemographic situations (mother working, mother not working) [26], while similar to our study finding, it can be said that the level of ability to cope with stress increases in cases where emotional intelligence is strong [27]. In another study conducted on adolescents and children, it was observed that participants with high levels of emotional intelligence had strong levels of coping with stress [28]. In addition, similar studies reveal that an increase in the level of emotional intelligence increases psychological resilience and indirectly affects stress coping levels [29]. In this context, studies in the literature support our current study finding. It is possible to say that individuals who have a high ability to recognize and manage their own emotions and the emotions of others, in other words, individuals with a strong level of emotional intelligence, are more successful in coping with stress. Apelin is an adipokine tissue from adipose [30], which has different properties in many positive systems such as the immune system, as well as metabolic and many processes, emotional course control, and global role [9, 10]. According to the emotional intelligence and stress levels of the university students included in the research, the group with the highest salivary apelin hormone level and high emotional intelligence and stress levels was found to be The lowest in individuals with low emotional intelligence levels. The results of our current study are the first to examine the effect of apelin on hormone levels in terms of stress management skills relative to emotional intelligence levels. Although the studies are limited, the current study’s findings are similar to the results of similar studies reported in the literature [15, 31, 32]. The reason for the high emotional intelligence level, stress management skills, and salivary apelin level together are compatible with the literature that apelin hormone stimulates glucose uptake through nitric oxide synthase (NOS), AMPK (Adenosine Monophosphate Protein Kinase) and Acute and Chronic Stress dependent pathways [33].

BDNF is a neurotrophic factor that is effective in learning, acquiring new skills, memory and mood, and cognitive performance. It has been reported that BNDF can be used as a prognostic biomarker in these diseases [34, 35] since BDNF has a role in the developmental pathogenesis of neuropsychiatric diseases and mental disorders [35]. BDNF, a member of the neurotrophin family, plays a critical role in the emergence of mental diseases such as stress, depression and anxiety [37]. Stressors affect brain function through the stress-related system, which includes the hypothalamus-pituitary-adrenocortical (HPA) axis and hippocampus, and mainly the reward-related system, the ventral tegmental area, and the reward center of the limbic system, the nucleus accumbens [36]. The role of BDNF expression in stress-induced disorders has been studied but remains unclear. The BDNF regulation effect on mood due to stress exposure is regulated by DNA methylation, histone modification, and non-coding small RNA [37]. The results of our current study are the first to examine salivary BDNF levels in terms of stress management skills relative to emotional intelligence levels. Although the studies are limited, they are compatible with the results of studies stating that salivary BDNF levels increase with stress [39, 40] and reporting that salivary BDNF levels decrease with stress [41–43] differ with some studies. We think that this is due to the fact that emotional intelligence level, stress management skills and salivary BDNF level are high together, in line with the literature reporting that BDNF is related to cognitive performance [44, 45]. When stress becomes chronic, it causes brain functions to deteriorate, negatively affecting health and should be taken seriously. When a person is exposed to stress, serious damage can occur to multiple organs and systems. If the central nervous system is affected, individuals may develop mental disorders such as anxiety and depression that will harm their lives. Cortisol is a steroid hormone produced naturally in the body in the adrenal glands and regulates the body’s response to stress. Salivary cortisol levels were determined to be high in groups with high-stress levels and low in groups with low-stress levels. Our current study’s results are consistent with similar studies reported in the literature [46, 47].

Limitation

This study had some limitations. First, the study used self-report measurement instruments, which may introduce some form of response bias. Secondly, since this study was conducted in only one province using the convenience sampling method, the results cannot be generalized. Despite these limitations, the study had its strengths. This study provides important information about the emotional intelligence and stress management skill levels of 167 participants randomly selected from 4th grade students of the faculty of health sciences. At the same time, it is very valuable and original in terms of examining the hormone levels and cerebral responses of the participants while revealing the relationship between these concepts.

Conclusion

It has been determined that individuals with high emotional intelligence score average and high stress management skills have high levels of salivary apelin and BDNF and low levels of cortisol hormone. Additionally, there is a positive relationship between emotional intelligence level and stress management skills and apelin and BDNF levels, and a negative, strong and statistically significant relationship between salivary cortisol level. Erroneous decisions made in management organizational processes, leaders in management, and employees’ decision-making processes in work processes can lead to irreparable problems and negative consequences for the organization. Human resources management is critical in businesses gaining competitive advantage and achieving sustainable success. In this respect, we predict that apelin, BDNF and cortisol hormone levels can be an early indicator and a biomarker in taking precautions for reported problems in identifying and evaluating individuals with stress management skills according to their emotional intelligence levels. However, it is thought that similar studies in the future may contribute to the elucidation of physiological mechanisms and the development of correct strategies for management and organization, organizational behavior and human resources management.

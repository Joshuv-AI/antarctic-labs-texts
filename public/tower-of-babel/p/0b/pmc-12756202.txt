Efficacy and Safety of Ulinastatin in Post-traumatic Sepsis: A Randomized Controlled Trial
Authors: Ao Yang, Rui Liu, Kongbo Lv, Weijun Chen, Mengfei Han, Zhizhou Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and ObjectivePost-traumatic sepsis is associated with high mortality and limited treatment options. This study aimed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of ulinastatin in adult patients with post-traumatic sepsis, assess its potential as a therapeutic option, and provide a foundation for future large-scale trials.MethodsA single-blind, randomized controlled trial was conducted involving 60 patients treated at the Eastern Theater General Hospital from October 2022 to June 2024. The primary endpoint was 28-day all-cause mortality. Secondary endpoints included the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score, C-reactive protein (CRP), interleukin-6 (IL-6), procalcitonin (PCT) levels, Activities of Daily Living (ADL) score, Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation II (APACHE II) score, ICU length of stay, duration of mechanical ventilation, and other laboratory indicators.ResultsNo statistically significant difference was observed in 28-day mortality between the ulinastatin and control groups (p > 0.05). However, the ulinastatin group exhibited significantly shorter ICU and hospital stays compared with the control group (p < 0.05), while the duration of mechanical ventilation showed no significant difference (p > 0.05). The ulinastatin group demonstrated significant improvements in CRP, IL-6, PCT levels, as well as SOFA and APACHE II scores (p < 0.05). Safety evaluations revealed a significant reduction in ALT and AST levels in the ulinastatin group (p < 0.05), with no serious adverse events reported.ConclusionWhile the 28-day mortality rate did not differ significantly between the two groups, ulinastatin was associated with shorter ICU and hospital stays, improvements in inflammatory markers and organ function, and demonstrated a favorable safety profile.

Key Points

Ulinastatin shortened ICU stay and improved inflammatory markers (CRP, IL-6, PCT) in adult sepsis patients.Organ function scores (SOFA, APACHE II) improved, while 28-day mortality showed no significant difference.Ulinastatin was well tolerated with a favorable safety profile.

Introduction

Sepsis is a systemic inflammatory response syndrome induced by pathogenic microorganisms, such as bacteria. It is characterized by high incidence, mortality, and substantial treatment costs, placing a significant burden on healthcare systems [1]. According to the World Health Organization, sepsis affects approximately 49 million people globally each year, resulting in approximately 11 million deaths, with a mortality rate of around 22.4% [1]. Post-traumatic sepsis, a common and particularly concerning form of sepsis, arises due to the physiological and pathological changes following trauma. Trauma patients are at an increased risk of developing sepsis, which is associated with higher mortality rates and poorer quality of life [2]. Current treatment strategies for sepsis primarily involve antibiotic therapy, supportive treatments (such as mechanical ventilation and vasopressors), and therapies targeting the inflammatory response [3]. However, due to the complex pathogenesis and treatment challenges, the success rate of sepsis management remains suboptimal. Therefore, identifying effective drugs and treatment strategies remains a major focus of clinical research [4]. In recent years, immunotherapy has gained increasing attention as a promising approach for sepsis treatment [5]. Ulinastatin, known for its anti-inflammatory properties [6, 7] and immunomodulatory effects [8, 9], has garnered significant interest among clinicians [10, 11]. Nevertheless, the efficacy and safety of ulinastatin in treating post-traumatic sepsis, as well as the optimal timing, dosage, and duration of administration, still require further clarification through clinical studies [12].

Methods

Study Design

This study was designed as a single-blind, randomized controlled trial (RCT) to evaluate the efficacy and safety of ulinastatin in the treatment of post-traumatic sepsis. Eligible patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either ulinastatin or placebo. Randomization was conducted by an independent statistician using a computer-generated sequence, and allocation was performed after patients provided written informed consent. In this single-blind design, participants were unaware of their treatment allocation, while investigators were informed, allowing real-time clinical monitoring and intervention.

The target sample size of 60 patients (30 per group) was determined with reference to prior exploratory randomized trials in critically ill septic populations, which typically enrolled 40–80 participants, and by considering the feasibility of patient recruitment in a single-center emergency/ICU setting. This scale was deemed sufficient to ensure protocol adherence, monitor safety, and explore signals in key physiological and clinical outcomes, while also providing preliminary effect size and variance estimates for the design of subsequent multicenter trials.

Patients in the ulinastatin group received intravenous ulinastatin at a dose of 300,000 units, three times daily for 7 days, in addition to standard sepsis management. All interventions were initiated within 6 hours of confirmed diagnosis of post-traumatic sepsis and within 2 hours of ICU admission. Ulinastatin was provided by Guangdong Techpool Bio-Pharma Co., Ltd. (approval number: H19990134). Patients in the placebo group received an equal volume of normal saline at identical time points.

All patients received standard care based on the latest international guidelines for sepsis management (Sepsis-3 definition), including antimicrobial therapy, fluid resuscitation, and organ support. Treatment regimens were individualized by ICU physicians based on the patient’s clinical condition and were consistent across both groups (Fig. 1).

Ethical Approval

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of our institution (Approval No. 2023DZKY-035) and registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (Registration No. ChiCTR2400080349). Written informed consent was obtained from all patients and their family members.

Inclusion Criteria

Patients were eligible for inclusion if they met the following criteria: (i) age: adults aged 18–80 years; (ii) sepsis diagnosis: diagnosed with sepsis according to Sepsis-3.0 criteria [13], with a Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score increase ≥ 2 points; (iii) trauma history: Injury Severity Score (ISS) ≥ 16, with sepsis diagnosed within 48 hours after trauma; (iv) informed consent: written informed consent was obtained from the patient.

Exclusion Criteria

Patients meeting any of the following criteria were excluded from the study: (i) cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases: including severe heart failure, cerebrovascular accidents, acute coronary syndrome, or cardiac arrest; (ii) liver and kidney diseases: severe liver disease (Child-Pugh Class C), acute liver failure, or chronic renal failure requiring dialysis; (iii) coagulation and immune abnormalities: severe coagulation dysfunction (ISTH-DIC score ≥ 5) or significant immune system dysfunction; (iv) recent specific treatments: received treatments such as blood purifiers, thymosin, or immunoglobulin within the past 3 months; (v) other: allergies to drugs, pregnancy or lactation, or participation in other clinical trials within the past 3 months; (vi) patients admitted within 7 days or those with incomplete medical records.

Withdrawal Criteria

A participant was considered to have withdrawn from the study if any of the following occurred: (i) serious adverse events: occurrence of severe adverse events, complications, or special physiological changes unrelated to the study drug, making it unsuitable for continued participation; (ii) voluntary withdrawal: the participant chose to withdraw from the trial during the study; (iii) incomplete treatment: participants who did not complete the treatment for any reason or were lost to follow-up; (iv) medical necessity: the investigator determined that, from a medical standpoint, it was necessary for the participant to withdraw from the study.

Dropout Criteria

A participant was considered to have dropped out if any of the following occurred after enrollment: (i) if, after randomization, it was discovered that the participant did not meet the inclusion and exclusion criteria, the primary investigator, the research team, and the Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) would decide whether to continue or terminate the participant’s involvement in the study; (ii) the participant was prescribed medications that interfered with the experimental endpoints, which were outside the study protocol, (iii) the dosage or duration of ulinastatin administration was insufficient or incorrect.

Intervention

Patients were randomly assigned to either the ulinastatin treatment group or the placebo control group using a computer-generated random number table. Both groups received standard treatments, including, but not limited to, antibiotics, intravenous fluids, enteral or parenteral nutrition, blood transfusion, and supportive therapies for organ dysfunction. These supportive therapies included invasive or non-invasive mechanical ventilation, vasopressors, continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT), and extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO). In addition to standard treatments, the ulinastatin group received ulinastatin (produced by Guangdong Tempmore Biochemical Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd.), while the placebo group was administered normal saline. The dosage, frequency, and duration of treatment were identical for both groups. Ulinastatin was administered intravenously at a dose of 300,000 units per injection, three times daily, for a total of 7 days.

Outcome Measures

The primary outcome measure was the 28-day mortality rate for both groups. Secondary outcomes included comparisons of mechanical ventilation duration, ICU length of stay, and total hospital stay between the two groups. Inflammatory and immune status were assessed by measuring changes in C-reactive protein (CRP), procalcitonin (PCT), and interleukin-6 (IL-6) levels before and after 7 days of treatment. Organ function was evaluated using the APACHE II and SOFA scoring systems [14, 15], which facilitated the monitoring of multi-organ dysfunction throughout the treatment period.

Statistical Analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 26.0. Normality was assessed with the Shapiro–Wilk or Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. For normally distributed data, Levene’s test was used to check for homogeneity of variance. If confirmed, a t-test was applied; otherwise, Welch’s t-test was used. Non-normally distributed data were analyzed with chi-square tests or non-parametric tests (e.g., Mann–Whitney U test). Missing data were handled by multiple imputation. A p-value of ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline Characteristics

A total of 60 patients with post-traumatic sepsis were enrolled in this study based on the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. The patients ranged in age from 45 to 65 years, with 37 males and 23 females. All participants had an ISS > 16 and met the clinical diagnostic criteria for sepsis.

As shown in Table 1, there were no statistically significant differences between the ulinastatin group and the control group in terms of baseline demographics and clinical characteristics, including age, sex, body mass index (BMI), ethnicity, smoking history, alcohol use, site of trauma, site of infection, and sepsis-related severity scores (ISS, APACHE II, SOFA, and ADL) (all p > 0.05), indicating good comparability between the two groups.

Specifically, the mean age was 55.0 ± 6.57 years in the ulinastatin group and 53.6 ± 7.03 years in the control group. The proportion of male patients was 56.67 and 66.67%, respectively. Mean BMI values were 24.98 ± 1.93 and 24.63 ± 2.13. The percentage of Han ethnicity was 96.67% in the ulinastatin group and 83.33% in the control group.

The proportion of patients with a history of smoking was 40.0% in the ulinastatin group and 63.33% in the control group; alcohol use was reported in 66.67 and 46.67%, respectively. The distribution of trauma locations (head/neck, thorax, abdomen/pelvis, limbs/pelvic bones, and multiple injuries) and infection sites (abdominal cavity, respiratory tract, urinary tract, wound surface) did not differ significantly between the groups.

Regarding clinical severity scores, the ulinastatin and control groups showed no significant differences in ISS (33.8 ± 12.13 vs 30.6 ± 8.21), APACHE II (19.43 ± 4.78 vs 19.07 ± 4.77), SOFA (13.4 ± 1.04 vs 13.77 ± 1.07), and ADL (45.67 ± 5.98 vs 43.17 ± 4.997) scores (all p > 0.05). The balanced baseline characteristics provided a solid foundation for subsequent evaluation of treatment efficacy, minimizing the risk of confounding bias due to pre-intervention differences.

Comparison of 28-Day Mortality Rates Between the Two Groups

Chi-square tests were conducted to compare the 28-day mortality rates between the treatment and control groups based on the follow-up data. The results showed that the 28-day mortality rate in the ulinastatin treatment group was 6.67% (2/30), compared with 23.33% (7/30) in the control group. Although the treatment group exhibited a lower mortality rate, the difference between the two groups did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.148).

Comparison of Mechanical Ventilation Duration, ICU Stay, and Hospitalization Days Between the Two Groups

To ensure data accuracy, deceased patients were excluded from the analysis when comparing ICU stay, mechanical ventilation duration, and total hospital stay. Normality tests revealed that ICU stay and hospital stay in the ulinastatin group followed a normal distribution, whereas mechanical ventilation duration did not. All variables in the saline group (control group) followed a normal distribution. Accordingly, the Mann–Whitney U test was used for mechanical ventilation duration, as it did not meet the normality assumption, while independent samples t-tests were applied to the variables with normal distributions.

For ICU stay, the ulinastatin treatment group had a mean of 15.91 ± 2.86 days, compared with 17.69 ± 2.99 days in the control group, with a statistically significant difference (p < 0.05), suggesting that ulinastatin may reduce ICU stay. The mechanical ventilation duration was 8.34 ± 1.80 days in the treatment group and 8.33 ± 1.69 days in the control group, with no statistically significant difference (p > 0.05). In terms of total hospital stay, the treatment group had 22.16 ± 4.77 days, compared with 26.01 ± 6.87 days in the control group, with a statistically significant difference (p < 0.05), indicating that ulinastatin may shorten hospitalization duration (Table 2).

Changes in liver and kidney function indicators between the treatment group and the control group with ulinastatin

Prior to treatment, there were no statistically significant differences in ALT levels between the ulinastatin and control groups (66.1 ± 13.9 U/L vs 71.4 ± 11.5 U/L; p > 0.05). After treatment, ALT levels significantly decreased in both groups, with a more pronounced reduction observed in the ulinastatin group (32.4 ± 8.2 U/L vs 39.2 ± 6.3 U/L; p < 0.01). Similarly, AST levels declined more markedly in the ulinastatin group (32.1 ± 7.0 U/L) compared with the control group (35.9 ± 7.3 U/L; p < 0.05).

Although both groups showed numerical improvements in TBili, SCr, and BUN after treatment, the between-group differences were not statistically significant (all p > 0.05). Importantly, no severe drug-related adverse events were observed in either group during the treatment period, indicating a favorable safety profile for ulinastatin.

Discussion

Trauma sepsis, a particularly challenging form of sepsis, requires rapid and comprehensive diagnosis as well as intensive treatment strategies [16]. Following trauma, damaged tissues and pathogens enter the bloodstream, initiating local infections that can spread systemically. This triggers an immune response, releasing inflammatory mediators such as TNFα, IL-1, and IL-6, which lead to systemic inflammation and immune imbalance, complicating patient diagnosis and treatment. Additionally, various biomarkers, including procalcitonin, C-reactive protein, interleukin-6, and the neutrophil/lymphocyte ratio, are critical in assessing the inflammatory and immune responses in trauma sepsis patients [17, 18]. Previous studies suggest that ulinastatin can significantly reduce APACHE II and SOFA scores in sepsis patients, improve vital signs and laboratory indicators, and enhance survival rates (Fig. 4).

In the present study, although the ulinastatin group showed a lower 28-day mortality rate compared with the control group (6.67 vs 23.33%), the difference did not reach statistical significance (p > 0.05). As a pilot, exploratory trial in a small post-traumatic sepsis population, the study was limited in sample size, but it provided important preliminary information on safety and key physiological and clinical outcomes. These results offer initial evidence on the potential role of ulinastatin in this specific patient population and may inform the design and sample size estimation for future larger studies. Additionally, while ulinastatin demonstrated some potential in modulating the inflammatory response in sepsis, its clinical application may be influenced by other complex factors, such as the severity of the patient’s condition, underlying diseases, and treatment timing, which could have attenuated its therapeutic effects [19, 20]. Nevertheless, Ulinastatin may still provide therapeutic benefits under specific conditions, including modulation of the TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway [6], inhibition of inflammatory mediator production, and improvement in microcirculation [7]. Therefore, further research is warranted.

The ulinastatin group showed significantly shorter ICU and hospital stays compared with the control group, further supporting its role in improving sepsis management. Although no significant difference in mechanical ventilation duration was observed, the treatment group exhibited notable improvements in inflammatory markers (such as CRP, PCT, and IL-6) and organ function scores (such as SOFA and APACHE II). These findings suggest that ulinastatin may enhance survival rates, reduce ICU stay, and shorten hospitalization duration in trauma sepsis patients. By suppressing inflammation and modulating immune function, ulinastatin could potentially improve patient outcomes [21].

In terms of safety, no severe drug-related adverse events were reported in either group. Both groups showed significant improvement in liver and kidney function indicators before and after treatment. While ulinastatin demonstrated a slight advantage over the control group in terms of liver and kidney function recovery, the difference was not statistically significant. This suggests that while ulinastatin may not directly play a decisive role in organ recovery in trauma sepsis patients, it may aid organ recovery by reducing inflammation, improving microcirculation, and protecting organ function. At the very least, ulinastatin did not adversely impact liver or kidney function.

The results of this study not only underscore the safety and potential benefits of ulinastatin in treating trauma sepsis, but also offer new insights into the comprehensive treatment of sepsis, particularly in the following areas: (1) Early recognition and intervention: early administration of ulinastatin in trauma sepsis may effectively suppress inflammation, prevent disease progression, and improve survival rates. (2) Reducing healthcare burden: by shortening ICU and hospital stays, ulinastatin could improve treatment outcomes and alleviate the burden on healthcare systems, optimizing resource allocation. (3) The promising clinical outcomes observed in this study highlight the need for further investigation. Future studies should be conducted as multi-center, large-scale, randomized controlled trials to confirm the efficacy and safety of ulinastatin, optimize timing and dosing strategies, and validate its effects on both short-term and long-term outcomes. Larger studies or systematic reviews, such as the analysis by Niu and colleagues in 2022, are needed to fully assess long-term outcomes [22]. Such research could also explore its potential application across different types of sepsis and severe infections.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that ulinastatin can significantly improve organ function, suppress inflammatory responses, and shorten ICU and hospital stays in patients with trauma-induced sepsis. Importantly, no severe adverse events were observed, supporting its favorable safety profile. While these findings are promising, further large-scale, multi-center trials are needed to validate the therapeutic value of ulinastatin and facilitate its broader clinical application.

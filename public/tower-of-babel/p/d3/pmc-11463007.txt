Significance of pyroptosis-related genes in the diagnosis and classification of diabetic kidney disease
Authors: Yixiong Huang, Xinke Yuan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aimed to identify the potential biomarkers associated with pyroptosis in diabetic kidney disease (DKD).MethodsThree datasets from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) were downloaded and merged into an integrated dataset. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were filtered and intersected with pyroptosis-related genes (PRGs). Pyroptosis-related DEGs (PRDEGs) were obtained and analyzed using functional enrichment analysis. Random forest, Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator, and logistic regression analyses were used to select the features of PRDEGs. These feature genes were used to build a diagnostic prediction model, identify the subtypes of the disease, and analyze their interactions with transcription factors (TFs)/miRNAs/drugs and small molecules. We conducted a comparative analysis of immune cell infiltration at different risk levels of pyroptosis. qRT-PCR was used to validate the expression of the feature genes.ResultsA total of 25 PRDEGs were obtained. These genes were coenriched in biological processes and pathways, such as the regulation of inflammatory responses. Five key genes (CASP1, CITED2, HTRA1, PTGS2, S100A12) were identified and verified using qRT-PCR. The diagnostic model based on key genes has a good diagnostic prediction ability. Five key genes interacted with TFs and miRNAs in 67 and 80 pairs, respectively, and interacted with 113 types of drugs or molecules. Immune infiltration of samples with different pyroptosis risk levels showed significant differences. Thus, CASP1, CITED2, HTRA1, PTGS2 and S100A12 are potential DKD biomarkers.ConclusionGenes that regulate pyroptosis can be used as predictors of DKD. Early diagnosis of DKD can aid in its effective treatment.Keywords: Pyroptosis, diabetic kidney disease, biomarkers, gene signature, immune infiltration

Introduction

Diabetic kidney disease (DKD) is a common microvascular complication of diabetes. The main pathological changes in this disease are glomerular hypertrophy, thickening of the renal basement membrane, formation of K-W nodules, glomerular fibrosis, and glomerular hyperfiltration. The clinical manifestations of the disease include persistent microproteinuria, edema, and hypertension, ultimately leading to end-stage renal disease [1,2]. In an epidemiological survey [3], the authors pointed out that the incidence rate of chronic kidney disease in diabetes varies by country and region, ranging from 27.1% to 83.6%, and the probability of chronic kidney disease in patients with diabetes is 1.3 to 4.6 times higher than that in patients without diabetes. It has been known well that DKD is affected by genetics, hemodynamic changes, abnormal glucose and lipid metabolism, oxidative stress, micro-inflammation, and other factors [4]. Recent studies [5,6] have shown that some special pattern recognition receptors (PRRs) in cells can recognize fatty acids, high glucose levels, and other damage-related molecules, thereby inducing cell pyroptosis, which eventually leads to a decline in renal function.

Pyroptosis, also known as inflammatory necrosis of cells, is one of the three classical programmed cell death pathways (pyroptosis, apoptosis, and necrosis). Morphologically, pyroptosis is characterized by the formation of tiny pores in the cell membrane at an early stage and the destruction of its integrity, leading to the swelling of the cell membrane. When the swelling reaches a certain degree, the cell membrane breaks, the contents of the cell flow out, and inflammatory factors are released [7]. Research has shown that pyroptosis can be divided into three pathways according to different mediators: the classic inflammasome pathway, non-classic inflammasome pathway, and caspase-3 mediated inflammasome pathway [8]. In the canonical inflammasome pathway, different inflammasomes are assembled when the recognition of pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) and damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) is activated by various pathological factors. Subsequently, pro-caspase-1 is recruited by inflammasomes and activated into cleaved caspase-1. Cleaved caspase-1 can activate IL-1β and IL-18. GSDMD can be cleaved into the GSDMD-N-terminal (GSDMD-NT) with pore forming characteristics by caspase-1 [9]. In the non-canonical inflammasome pathway, caspase-4/5/11 can directly respond to the pathogen structural molecules and lead to the cleavage of GSDMD and the release of IL-1β and IL-18 [10]. In the caspase-3-mediated inflammasome pathway, gasdermin E (DFNA5) is cleaved by caspase-3, leading to cell pyroptosis [11]. Pyroptosis has been observed in glomerular endothelial cells, renal podocytes, and renal tubular epithelial cells of patients with DKD [12]. However, the underlying regulatory channels involved in DKD remain unclear.

With advances in molecular biology, an increasing number of studies have been conducted on small-molecule regulatory channels in DKD. A study [13] showed that ABCA1 deficiency mediates the pyroptosis of podocytes in DKD through the APE1/IRF1 axis, thus inducing the onset of the disease. Another study found that ANRIL promotes the pyroptosis of renal tubular epithelial cells in DKD by acting as an miR-497 sponge to disinhibit TXNIP expression [14]. Gu et al. found that the use of NaB can ameliorate pyroptosis in HG-induced glomerular endothelial cells by modulating the canonical inflammasome pathway via the NF-κB/IκB-α signaling pathway [15]. The study of these small-molecule genes and pathways plays a crucial role in exploring the pathogenesis, predicting prognosis, and identifying therapeutic targets of this disease. Therefore, in this study, we identified key gene markers related to pyroptosis based on a public gene database, analyzed their roles in the development of DKD, and explored their diagnostic value. We aimed to identify the functions and pathways of key genes involved, identify the specific subtypes of this disease, and construct a diagnostic model using key gene markers.

Materials and methods

Dataset downloading

We downloaded three expression profile datasets of DKD GSE30528 (control sample number = 13, DKD = 9) [16], GSE30529 (control =12, DKD = 10) [17], and GSE96804 (control =20, DKD = 41) [18] from GEO database using R package “GEOquery” [19]. All samples from the three datasets were included in the study. The species source for all three datasets was Homo sapiens, and the tissue sources were the glomerulus and tubulointerstitium. All three datasets were analyzed using the Affymetrix chip sequencing platform, with GSE30528 and GSE30529 as GPL571 and GSE96804 as GPL17586. Table 1 provides the detailed information.

We collected pyroptosis-related genes (PRGs) from the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/) [20], which provides information on common genes. We used the word “Pyroptosis” as the search keyword and set the “Protein Coding” as the screening criteria to obtain 379 PRGs. We also searched the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) [21] and collected 27 PRGs using the same keyword. After combination and de-duplication, 379 PRGs were identified. Further details are presented in Table S1.

Differentially expressed genes of the integrated dataset

To identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the DKD and control groups, we first used R package “sva” [22] to de-batch the data sets GSE30528, GSE30529, and GSE96804 to obtain an integrated database, which contains 60 DKD samples and 45 normal samples. To verify the effectiveness of debatching, we conducted a principal component analysis (PCA) on the dataset before and after the operation. PCA is a data dimensionality reduction method that extracts feature vectors (components) from high-dimensional data, converts them into low-dimensional data, and displays these features using two-dimensional or three-dimensional graphs [23]. Subsequently, we performed differential analysis on the integrated dataset by using R package “limma” [24] to obtain the DEGs that met the criteria of | logFC |>0.5 and p.adjust < 0.05. The intersection of DEGs with PRGs was used to obtain Pyroptosis-related DEGs (PRDEGs) using a Venn plot. We also drew a volcano diagram, heat map, and grouping comparison diagram to illustrate different expressions.

Function and pathway annotation

Gene ontology (GO) is a network formed by connecting an Ontology language with genes and gene products, and annotates genes from three parts: the function of individual gene products, the orderly combination of molecular functions, and the ontology of cell formation [25]. The Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) [26] integrates genomic information, gene-related chemical molecular information, functional system information, and biological pathways. We conducted both the two annotations on PRDEGs using R package “clusterProfiler” [27], with the entries-screening criteria as p Adj.<0.1 and FDR value (q. value)<0.05. The Benjamin-Hochberg (BH) test was used for p value correction.

Gene set enrichment analysis

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) utilizes changes in genes between two biological states to calculate the differences in function and biological activity between biological samples [28]. We downloaded the reference gene set “c2.cp.v7.2. symbols. gmt” from the MSigDB database and used the R package “clusterProfiler” to enrich, analyze, and visualize the integrated dataset. The parameters were set as follows: seed = 2020, calculation frequency = 1000, gene number contained in each gene set = 10, and maximum number of genes = 500, with enriched pathway screening criteria of p.adjust < 0.05, FDR value (q-value)<0.25, and the p value correction mode of BH.

Selecting feature genes and building a diagnostic model

We selected feature genes using the Random Forest (RF) [29] algorithm, which models multiple decision trees through ensemble learning, counts the prediction results of each tree, and selects the optimization results. We used the “randomForest” package [30] to conduct the analysis based on the expression matrix of PRDEGs in the integrated dataset, with parameters seed = 234 and ntree = 1000.

Next, through R package “glmnet” [31], with seed= “500,” family=“binary” as parameters, the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) [32] regression analysis was conducted based on the PRDEGs. To avoid overfitting, the number of operating cycles was set to 200. The LASSO is a type of linear regression that avoids overfitting problems in linear regression by adding penalty term parameters. The feature genes selected by these two algorithms were intersected to obtain the common PRDEGs.

Next, we performed a logistic regression

analysis on the common PRDEGs and their expression matrix in the integrated dataset and used the group category (DKD or control) as the dependent variable. We filtered the genes with p < 0.10 as the feature genes and showed their hazard ratio (HR) in a forest plot. A nomogram [33] is a graph that reflects the predictive ability of variables using a set of disjointed line segments. The nomogram was drawn using R package “rms” and Decision Curve Analysis (DCA) was introduced to determine the accuracy of this model by the “ggDCA” package [34] DCA [35] evaluates if the clinical prediction models have the optimal curve direction. Next, we used an extra calibration analysis to draw curves reflecting the ability of the PRDEG diagnostic model.

ROC curves

The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve [36] reflects continuous changes in sensitivity and specificity and selects the optimal threshold. The area under the ROC curve (AUC) was limited to 0.5–1. The closer the value is to 1, the better the diagnostic effect. We defined high accuracy as an AUC value above 0.9, modest-accuracy as an AUC between 0.7 and 0.9, low-accuracy as an AUC between 0.5 and 0.7. We used R package “pROC” to complete the analysis for each key gene and the AUCs were calculated.

Identification of DKD dataset related disease subtypes

Consistency Clustering [37] is a clustering method based on repeated sampling that integrates the data subsets obtained from multiple random samplings of high-dimensional data, thereby obtaining more robust and stable clustering results. We used the “ConsonsusClusterPlus” package [38] in R to identify different DKD disease subtypes based on the expression levels of the feature genes. The parameters were set to 100 replicates (reps = 100), the proportion of resampled samples was 80% (pItem = 0.8), the proportion of resampled features was 100% (pFeature = 1), and PAM was used as the clustering algorithm. The grouping of expression differences of the feature genes among the different disease subtype samples was compared.

Immune infiltration analysis

Single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) [39] is a single sample GSEA that quantifies the relative abundance of each immune cell infiltration by labeling various types of infiltrating immune cells and calculating the enrichment fraction of each cell type. A group comparison graph of each cell to clearly exhibit the results with R package “ggplot2.” Correlation heat maps were drawn using the R package “pheatmap” to display the correlations between key genes and immune cells.

Construction of mRNA-miRNA, mRNA-TF, mRNA-drug interaction network

The miRDB database [40] was used to predict miRNAs that interact with hub genes and then map them into an interaction network. CHIPBase database [41] (version 3.0) (https://rna.sysu.edu.cn/chipbase/) has identified thousands of binding motif matrices and predicted the transcriptional regulation relationship between millions of transcription factors (TF) and genes. We also searched for data on the TFs and their corresponding regulatory targets in the hTFtarget database [42]. In addition, we used the Drug Gene Interaction Database (DGIdb) [43] (https://www.dgidb.org) to predict potential drugs that interact with key genes. Subsequently, the networks were visualized using the Cytoscape software.

Verification of key gene expression

The HK-2 cells (human renal tubular epithelial cells) were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, Manassas, VA, USA). After cultivation in medium, the cells were treated with different concentrations of glucose for 48 h (control group, 5.6 mM; high-glucose intervention, 30 mM). Total RNA was isolated from HK-2 cells using TRIzol reagent (Qiagen, Valencia, CA, USA), and the concentration and purity of the RNA were evaluated using a spectrophotometer. By using The ReverTra Ace qPCR RT Kit (Toyobo, Japan), the isolated RNA was reversely transcribed and the synthesized cDNA was treated as a template for quantitative PCR analysis. The primer sequence was determined using Primer Premier 5.0 software and confirmed using PrimerBLAST for the NCBI website primer definition. Quantitative PCR was conducted in triplicate to remove outliers. Next, the CT values was analyzed based on the GAPDH CT value (RQ=- ΔΔ Ct). All experiments were repeated thrice. The primer sequence is: Caspase-1, Forward: 5′-GAAAGCCATGGCGACAAG-3′, Reverse: 5′- GCCCTTTCGGAATACGGGA-3′; PTGS2, Forward: 5′-TTCCCCTGTGCCTGATGATT-3′, Reverse: 5′- AAACTGATGCTGTGAAGTGCTG-3′; HTRA1, Forward: 5′-TGGAATCTCCTTTGCAATCC-3′, Reverse: 5′- TTCTTGGGTGATGGCTTTCC-3′; S100A12: Forward: 5′-TCCACAACTCAGTTCGGAAG-3′, Reverse 5′- ACTCTTTGTGGGTGGTAATGG-3′; CITED2: Forward: 5′- CTTTGCACGCCAGGAAGGTC-3′, Reverse 5′-CGCCGTAGTGTATGTGCTCG-3′; GAPDH Forward: 5′-CATGAGAAGTATGAACAACAGCCT-3′, Reverse: 5′- AGTCTTCCACGATACACCAAGT-3′.

Statistical methods

Data were analyzed using R software (version 4.1.2), and comparisons between two groups (gene expression) were conducted using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Spearman’s correlation analysis was used to calculate the coefficients. All results are based on a p value less than 0.05 as the standard for significant differences.

LASSO regression analysis was also conducted to select feature genes from the 25 PRDEGs. This process was visualized using a LASSO regression model diagram (Figure 6(C)) and a trajectory diagram (Figure 6(D)). The algorithm selected nine feature genes: APOE, CASP1, CASP3, CITED2, GJA1, HTRA1, JUN, PTGS2, and S100A12.

Two sets of feature genes intersected, and nine genes were retained: APOE, CASP1, CASP3, CITED2, GJA1, HTRA1, JUN, PTGS2, and S100A12. Next, we conducted logistic regression analysis based on the nine genes to build a diagnostic model. During this process, the JUN gene was found to have collinearity or missing values; thus, it was excluded from the analysis. A total of eight genes were included for analysis, and a forest map displayed their HR (Figure 6(E)); of these, only five genes met p < .10 standard and were identified as key genes of the study, namely CASP1, CITED2, HTRA1, PTGS2, and S100A12.

To further validate the value of the PRDEGs diagnostic model, we constructed a nomogram based on the five hub genes (Figure 6(F)). The results showed that the expression level of CASP1 in the hub genes was significantly higher in the PRDEGs diagnostic model compared to other variables, whereas CITED2 had the lowest utility. DCA was introduced to evaluate the clinical utility of this model (Figure 6(G)), and the graph indicated a good performance. The calibration curve evaluated the predictive effect of the model (Figure 6(H)) and showed that the model had a high accuracy in the diagnosis of DKD.

Discussion

DKD is the primary microvascular complication of diabetes, and is characterized by a large number of symptoms such as proteinuria, edema, anemia, and decreased renal function. Some patients may have other microvascular complications such as retinopathy [44]. The incidence of DKD is high, and its prognosis is poor. Even the best treatment scheme to date cannot prevent DKD patients from developing end-stage renal disease [45]. Pyroptosis is a recently discovered programmed cell death pathway that participates in the elimination of harmful substances as a self-protective reaction in the body. However, excessive pyroptosis leads to disorders and induces a variety of diseases, such as DKD [46]. DKD risk factors, such as high glucose, abnormal lipid metabolism, and oxidative stress, could lead to pyroptosis and promote the development of the disease, but its mechanism requires more research [47]. Therefore, further elucidation of the pathogenesis of DKD is necessary.

In this study, we combined three datasets from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) and conducted a differential expression analysis of the expression matrix between pathological and normal tissues to obtain DEGs. We merged these three datasets by increasing the sample size to increase the significance of the statistical analysis and enhance the reliability of the results. In addition, the diversity of the sample data from different platforms and sources improves the generalizability of the model, making it applicable to a wide range of situations. By intersecting with known PRGs, we obtained PRDEGs, which were inputted into three regression analyses for further key gene selection. We used key genes to construct a diagnostic model, cluster samples into subtypes, and build interaction networks. We also validated the expression of key genes in the laboratory.

We obtained three datasets from the GEO database and merged them into an integrated dataset containing 60 samples from patients with DKD and 45 samples from the control group. After the differential analysis, 696 DEGs were identified. These DEGs were intersected with known PRGs to obtain 25 PRDEGs. These genes are abnormally expressed in DKD samples and participate in the regulation of DKD development; however, these genes are related to pyroptosis. Therefore, by analyzing the functions and pathways of these genes, we were able to better understand the biological processes and pathways involved. In the GO/KEGG annotation, we found that these 25 genes are involved in the regulation of the inflammatory response, cell membrane components, apoptosis, and pathways involving cysteine-type endopeptidase, tumor necrosis factor signaling, and Salmonella infection. These biological processes and pathways are also associated with pyroptosis. Caspases and interleukins (ILs) are important mediators of pyroptosis. The NLRP3-Caspase-1-IL-1β signaling axis is a classic inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis pathway [48]. Under the stimulation of viral DNA or cellular oxidative stress response, the selective response of NLRP3, an inflammasome receptor molecule, produces a polymer compound Caspase-1, which leads to the cleavage of Gasdermin D (GSDMD) protein and the outflow of cytoplasm from the ruptured cell membrane, eventually leading to chromosomal DNA degradation and release of pro-inflammatory factor IL-1βand IL-18 [49].

Among these 25 PRDEGs, we found that AnxA1 was highly expressed and ACE2 was expressed at low levels in DKD samples. AnxA1 exerts significant anti-inflammatory effects by inhibiting the adhesion and aggregation of neutrophils and platelets. Its regulatory role in the AnxA1/FPR2 pathway is considered to be an important mechanism against diabetes-related inflammation [50]. AnxA1 also regulates lipid metabolism through the AMPK/PPARα/CPT1b pathway and prevents the progression of DKD. Its mechanisms include promoting lipid oxidation and reducing lipid accumulation, thereby protecting renal function [51]. In addition, decreased ACE2 levels are an important feature of DKD progression. ACE2 plays a protective role by catalyzing the conversion of angiotensin II to angiotensin. This imbalance leads to increased kidney inflammation and fibrosis, which in turn promotes DKD development [52]. By analyzing these mechanisms, we confirmed the important roles of AnxA1 and ACE2 in DKD, highlighting their potential value in clinical diagnosis and therapy.

We further analyzed the biological processes associated with the DEGs through GSEA and found that they were mainly enriched in the IL-17, IL-18, and oxidative stress response pathways, which is consistent with the analysis above. The increase in mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (ROS) content caused by oxidative stress could activate the NLRP3 inflammasome, thus mediating pyroptosis; therefore, the role of oxidative stress in pyroptosis cannot be ignored [53].

Because there were significant DEGs between the DKD and control groups, we believe that these genes are involved in the development of the disease. Therefore, we established a diagnostic model using 25 PRDEGs to predict DKD occurrence. After the RF algorithm and LASSO regression screening and logistic regression, with p < 0.1 as the screening standard, five characteristic genes (CASP1, CITED2, HTRA1, PTGS2, and S100A12) were obtained. Using these five biomarkers and their covariates after logistic regression we established a diagnostic model as well as the nomogram with it. The model exhibited good accuracy. We applied ROC analysis to assess the individual diagnostic accuracy of the five biomarkers and found that the AUC of all five genes was > 0.7, indicating a high diagnostic value. CASP1, also known as caspase-1, is a member of the caspase family and a proenzyme that can cleave subunits and active enzymes, thereby activating IL-1. This gene is the main inducer gene of pyroptosis received by classical inflammasome, NLRP3/capase-1/IL-1 β [54]. During the development of DR, the signaling axis mediates various upstream damage signals to cause a large number of scorch deaths in renal tubular epithelial cells and renal podocytes, which leads to a decline in renal function [55]. The value of CAPS1 as a diagnostic biomarker for DKD has also been reported in a previous study [56]. Prostaglandin Endoperoxidase Synthase 2 (PTGS2), also known as the stress-inducible cyclooxygenase 2(COX2), was identified as a core target for the treatment of DKD [57]. S100A12, a calcium-binding protein, is markedly associated with inflammatory biomarkers and is highly expressed in the serum of CKD patients [58]. CITED2 is a ubiquitously expressed protein that exhibits high affinity for the CH1 domain of the transcriptional co-activator CBP/p300 and plays an important role in inhibiting HIF in some diseases, including kidney and heart diseases and type 2-diabetes [59]. HTRA1 encodes a secretory serine protease that regulates insulin-like growth factors and cell growth. Li et al. [60] had showed that the upregulation of HTRA1 mediated by the NEAT1/miR-141-3p axis of lncRNA could participate in the occurrence of disease by activating the NLRP3 inflammatory mediator of pyroptosis and inflammatory response. This is the first study to show that CITED2 and HTRA1 have potential value in the diagnostic prediction of DKD.

We attempted to cluster DKD samples into different disease subtypes using these five genes and found that they could significantly cluster 60 DKD samples into two subtypes. Among the five genes, CASP1, CITED2, and HTRA1 were differentially expressed between the two subtypes, whereas PTGS2 and S100A12 showed no significant differences in expression between the two subtypes. To further explore the significance of these two clusters, we compared the expression levels of NLRP3, CASP4, and CASP5. We noticed that the expression of NLRP3 and CASP1 was up-regulated in cluster1 and the expression of CASP4 and CASP5 was up-regulated in cluster2, which increased our understanding of the pyroptosis pathway in these two clusters. NLRP3 and CASP1 are often associated with the canonical pyroptotic pathway, whereas CASP4 and CASP5 are associated with the noncanonical pyroptotic pathway. Therefore, we speculate that these two disease subtypes are related to different pyroptosis induction pathways. Pyroptosis in cluster1 may be mediated by the canonical caspase-1 pathway, while cluster2 may be mediated by the non-canonical caspase-4 and caspase-5 pathways. This observation provides interesting insights into the mechanisms underlying pyroptosis in different subtypes. However, because of the complexity of the pyroptosis mechanism, especially in pathological situations, further studies are needed to clarify the exact mechanism of the pyroptosis pathways in these two clusters and to determine whether there are other molecules or pathways related to caspase-1 or caspase-4/5 involved in the mechanism. This will contribute to a more comprehensive understanding of the molecular mechanisms of pyroptosis in different subtypes, thereby providing a more targeted approach for DKD treatment.

In this study, we found that CASP1, CITED2, HTRA1, and PTGS2 interact with multiple miRNAs and TFs. In the pyroptosis pathway, the increased expression levels of caspase-l, NLRP3, GSDMD and other molecules are the main characteristics of pyroptosis, thus, by targeting the expression of these molecules, miRNA could directly regulate pyroptosis. Gu et al. [61] believe that the high-glucose environment of diabetes could induce pyroptosis in human retinal microvascular endothelial cells, and this process was mediated by miR-590-3p regulated pyroptosis targeting NLRP1. It has been found that MicroRNA-22 reduces the production of pro-inflammatory factors by inhibiting the NLRP3/CASP1 inflammasome in human dental pulp fibroblasts [62]. Sokkar et al. [63] found that the expression of caspase-1 was related to MiR-17 In pediatric patients with familial Mediterranean fever. Therefore, the prevention and treatment of DKD using miRNAs may be important for the clinical treatment of DKD and development of new drugs. We further searched for potential drugs or molecular compounds corresponding to the key genes and found that PTGS2 targeted 83 drugs simultaneously, making it the most promising gene target for DKD drug prevention and treatment.

We also used the ssGSEA algorithm to score pyroptosis in the samples and divided the DKD samples into two risk groups for immune infiltration analysis. We found significant differences in immune infiltration between the two risk groups. The infiltration of CD4, CD8 T cells, dendritic cells, neutrophils, and Mast cell was higher in high-risk patients than in low-risk patients. As the main product of pyroptosis, the proinflammatory factor IL-1β, IL-17, and IL-18 are released in large quantities after pyroptosis, which activate neutrophils and lymphocytes, increase the permeability of vascular endothelial cells, regulate the metabolic activity of cells, and induce the activation, proliferation and differentiation of T cells, participate in the immune response of the body [6]. These small molecules promote inflammatory reactions. Dendritic cells are antigen presenting cells that activate T cells. After stimulation by inflammatory factors, they secrete many chemokines and play a chemotactic role in T cells, which is an important link in the pyroptosis-inflammatory factor immune response axis [64]. Mast cell are regulators of the immune response and promote the migration and maturation of dendritic Cell migration through the interaction of inflammatory factors and intercellular adhesion molecules [65]. In previous studies, macrophages have shown abnormal expression of specific immune cells in DKD [66,67]. However, in this study, we found that macrophages had no abnormal expression in immune infiltration related to pyroptosis, indicating that pyroptosis mainly activates cell infiltration related to inflammatory factors and specific immune responses.

In the experimental validation section, after 48 h of high glucose intervention in human renal tubular cells, we found that the expression of the five key genes showed differential expression, which is consistent with the results of our biochemical analysis. In this study, we selected HK-2 cells for experimental verification by reason of HK-2 cells are representative in terms of proximal tubular cells and have been widely used in the study of DKD in the past [68], thus still providing valuable information to validate our hypothesis. Although other cells such as glomerular endothelial cells and podocytes also play an important role in DKD, the complexity and technical difficulty of in vitro culture make the use of HK-2 cells more convenient. However, there are limitations to our experimental verification owing to the heterogeneity between the tubular and glomerular cells. Therefore, further verification of glomerular endothelial cells and podocytes from the glomeruli is necessary, which could offer valuable and more comprehensive insights into the molecular mechanisms of DKD.

In the present study, we identified five key genes associated with pyroptosis in patients with DKD. Among these, CITED2 and HTRA1 were not found to have diagnostic value in previous studies on DKD, highlighting the uniqueness of our study. In addition, we used machine learning methods such as random forest and LASSO regression to build diagnostic models and validate the potential diagnostic ability of these genes using ROC analysis. This multilevel data integration and machine-learning approach has not been fully utilized in this field. However, this study had some limitations. First, owing to time and resource constraints, we did not include clinical data such as laboratory parameters and blood sugar control measures of patients with DKD in the analysis, nor did we perform experimental verification of these genes in the clinical samples. Therefore, for further in-depth analysis, it is necessary to include laboratory parameters of patients with DKD, such as glomerular function, proteinuria, and blood sugar control indicators, and to analyze clinical samples to further confirm the expression characteristics of these genes and their diagnostic value. In addition, this study was based on data from public databases, and the sample size may not have been sufficient to fully reflect the heterogeneity of the DKD patient population. Future studies should consider expanding the sample size to improve the generalizability and reliability of the results. Although this study primarily used statistical and machine-learning methods to identify potential diagnostic markers, the biological functions of these genes have not been thoroughly explored or experimentally validated. Subsequent studies should use in vitro and in vivo experiments to further explore the specific roles of these genes in DKD pathogenesis. Finally, although we used a variety of algorithms, such as random forests, LASSO regression, and logistic regression, to identify and validate potential diagnostic markers, the differences in results between the different algorithms suggest that we need to perform model validation and optimization on a larger scale to ensure the reliability and stability of the results.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our study introduced the methodology of data mining and machine learning algorithms and obtained five key pyroptosis-related genes in patients with DKD from the public gene database GEO, namely, CASP1, CITED2, HTRA1, PTGS2, and S100A12. Gene enrichment confirmed that pyroptosis plays a regulatory role in DKD, which partly explains its pathogenesis. The diagnostic model established using these five key genes showed good accuracy and a high application value.

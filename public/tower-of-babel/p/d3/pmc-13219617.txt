Numerical analysis of magnetized Ag-Fe3O4/VPO hybrid nanofluid flow over a spinning inclined disc with Thompson–Troian slip
Authors: Hijaz Ahmad, Hassan Ali Ghazwani, Nejla Mahjoub Said, Hadil Alhazmi, Hamiden Abd El-Wahed Khalifa, Naveed Khan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Magnetized hybrid nanofluid (HNF) flow across a spinning inclined disc (SID) under Thompson–Troian slip (TTS) conditions has numerous applications. It can help improve the efficiency of cooling systems in electrical appliances by optimizing lubricant flow and energy transportation. In the current work, we have studied the HNF flow over a SID under multiple slip conditions in the form of partial differential equations (PDEs). The black iron oxide (Fe3O4) or Iron (II, III) oxide, along with silver (Ag) nanoparticles (NPs), is mixed with vacuum pump oil (VPO) for the preparation of HNF. The fluid flow is investigated in relation to nonlinear heat radiation, viscous dissipation, mixed convection, Joule heating, and irregular heat source/sink. The system of PDEs is first converted into a non-dimensional form of ODEs and then solved numerically through the BVP4c technique. It has been determined that the flow rate of HNF (Ag and Fe3O4/VPO) declines with the variation of Ag and Fe3O4 NPs in VPO, and the magnetic field parameter. The fluid temperature distribution declines with the influence of NPs' volume friction. By varying the NPs volume friction (Ag and Fe3O4-NPs) from 0.01 to 0.03, the coefficient of Nusselt number (energy transfer rate) increases up to 31.6401%. Physically, Ag and Fe3O4 NPs in VPO have a high thermal conductivity, which more efficiently boosts the energy transfer rate and is more applicable to the cooling systems and energy transportation.

Introduction

Fluid flow across a spinning inclined disk is used in heat exchangers, aviation parts like turbines and chopper blades, fluid circulation, and the synthesis of drugs and (NPs) nanoparticles. Because of the tilt and translation of the disc, magnetohydrodynamics (MHD), HNF, and other innovative fluid dynamics tools can be used to study the complicated flows that are frequently involved in these applications [1–3]. Mahmud et al. [4] used the Shooting algorithm to evaluate the MHD angled flow and thermal energy transmission over a turning disk. Bhandari and Parmar [5] studied the steady ferrofluid accumulation problem in three dimensions on a slanted spinning surface with the impact of a magnetic dipole. The findings demonstrated that the magnetic-induced fluid viscosity reduces the thin-film liquid's mobility in all directions, particularly in inclined and rotating flows. Ali et al. [6] used a numerical approach called Runge–Kutta order four to investigate the upshot of a magnetic force on the unsteady HNF flow across a rotating disk that is elongated and inclined, whereas the flow is encompassed by a non-Darcy permeable medium. Ahmad et al. [7] used the parametric approach to study the stagnation point flow of HNF across a rotating sphere under the influence of thermal radiation and activation energy. It has been observed that the HNF (Al2O3-Cu/water) velocity is increased by the impacts of spinning and acceleration factors. Kanwal et al. [8] tackled the crucial problem of improving heat exchange efficiencies in thermal systems by studying the radiative ternary HNF flow along with thermal properties and Stephan blowing effect. Jakeer et al. [9] inspected the impacts of entropy formation and linear heat radiation on the two-phase Carreau HNF flow over a permeable stretched sheet. It was perceived that the effect of the Hartmann parameter rises the energy outlines and a drop in the velocity profile. Maatki et al. [10] used the ND-solve strategy to observe the nanoliquid flow through extending and turning discs under the effect of heat radiation, and magnetic field. It has been shown that the influence of magnetic term enhances the energy profile, whereas the fluid velocity decreases. Aoudia et al. [11] numerically assessed the heat radiation impact on the chemically reactive HNF flow consist of motile microbes past a spinning disc. Sarkar et al. [12] evaluated Arrhenius kinetics related to binary chemical reactions and the bioconvection on the Sutterby nanoliquid flow, which included motile gyrotactic microbes close to a porous Riga plate. Alqahtani et al. [13] and Anwar et al. [14] numerical calculated the MHD nanoliquid flow past two instable discs with zero mass flux and chemical reaction. Galal et al. [15] estimated the radiation impact on the thermo-bioconvection ternary HNF flow passes through an inclined spinning disc with the Cattaneo-Christov flux model applications.

VPO's precise chemical composition varies greatly depending on the intended use and necessary performance attributes [16]. Mineral oils, synthetic, diester/ester, polyalphaolefin (PAO), perfluoropolyether (PFPE), silicone oils, and diffusion pump oils are the main categories according to their basic fluid [17]. Applications for these fluids include lubrication, sealing, cooling, transporting contaminants, general commerce and industry, clinical research and medications, refrigeration and HVAC, optical and aesthetic coatings, heat treatment, and freeze drying [18]. In the present study, the Ag-NPs have been renowned as having a very high heat transfer in micro-coolants, and antimicrobial agents in medical spans. Conversely, the Fe3O4-NPs have been essential in ferro-hydrodynamics to regulate the fluid flow by means of external magnetic fielding and in targeted therapy and magnetic seals. Such form of HNF(Ag-Fe3O4/VPO) is a versatile medium that can be used to both control the magnetic flow and provide high levels of energy transport concurrently. Sundar et al. [19] conducted an experimental analysis of the heat transference efficiency and friction coefficient of Fe3O4 /VPO nanoliquids running in a tube with wrapped tape insertion. Shafiee et al. [20] have conducted experimental research on a vacuum tube solar-powered water heater combined with nanofluids (pure oil, TiO2/oil and Al2O3/oil) at 68.1 °C, 45.4 °C, and 60.2 °C, respectively. Anwar et al. [21] examined the multifaceted NPs and mineral oil-based HNF under time-dependent flow and energy circumstances, and subsequently discussed their amazing and significant applications in the fields of engineering and aerodynamics. Algehyne et al. [22] exploited the MATLAB tool to evaluate the non-Newtonian HNF flow (Cu and CuO NPs) across an expanding sheet. The results of their research showed that the HNF flow based on gasoline oil has higher concentration and velocity profiles than the flow rate based on water. Paul et al. [23] assessed the application of an engine hydrocarbon-based Casson tri-HNF over a moving disc with both heat and mass exchange. The obtained outcome demonstrated that, in comparison to the Casson HNF and Casson nanoliquid, the heat transfer efficiency for the Casson tri-HNF has been enhanced by 9.54% and 11.93%. Jamei et al. [24] conducted a thorough computational and experimental study in order to forecast the heat transference efficiency and thermophysical novel features of hybrid engine oil-based nanoliquid made of Mg(OH)2 and MWCNTs. Farooq et al. [25] used zinc oxide and engine oil to demonstrate the MHD cross-nanoliquid flow across the porous wedge in order to examine how it functions for a number of processes impacted by electro-osmotic flow, and heat transfer. Aoudia et al. [26, 27] assessed the radiation impact on motile microbes in tetra HNF for improving the heat and mass mechanism in bio-convective and microfluidic systems. Maddina et al. [28] presented an adaptive fuzzy-neuro inference scheme for the thermal transportation in Casson ternary HNF flow with the biomedical application.

Thompson and Troian slip conditions (TTSC) introduced an enhanced model for the behavior of fluid flow at solid–liquid junctions, which concentrates on the phenomenon of slip, that is happens when the fluid velocity close to the boundary deviates from the standard no-slip condition [29]. The slip model developed by Thompson and Troian bridges the gap between continuum fluid mechanics and molecular-scale phenomena, improving our understanding of flow dynamics in systems where conventional no-slip principles are less effective [30]. Riaz and Khan [31] created a mathematical model using TTS at the boundary to explain the magnetized flow of nano-lubricant across an SID in a Darcy-Forchheimer permeable media. It was found that when the Biot number increase, the Nusselt number decreases. Ramzan et al. [32] evaluated the influence of the TTS conditions on the 2D flow of ternary HNF caused by an extended sheet towards a stagnation point utilizing NiZnFe2O4, Fe2O3, and MnZn-Fe2O4 NPs in base fluid. It was discovered that for large estimations of the velocity slip factor, the coefficient of surface drag is improved while the energy flux rate is lowered. Mishra et al. [33] emphasized the ternary HNF consists of MoS2, SiO2, and GO NPs in water, over a permeable plate under the impact of TTS conditions, chemical reaction, and suction/blowing. It was found that changing the velocity slip factor from 0.2 to 0.6 causes the energy transfer rate to rise by 16.79% and the rate of skin friction decline by 24.14%. Puneeth et al. [34] inspected the bioconvective nanoliquid flow past a vertically stretched cylinder by applying the TTS condition. The motile microbe’s concentration in the nanofluid was found to be considerably reduced by increasing the Eckert numbers and the Peclet number. Abdal et al. [35] presented an inclusive review on machine learning algorithm for MHD hybrid nanoliquid flow with heat sources and bio-convection. Sarkar and Das [36] used the shooting techniques and Runge–Kutta-Fehlbergn to examine the HNF flow on the permeable Riga plate under the effect of heat radiation. It was found that increasing the pace of chemical reaction resulted in a significant 20% increase in the Sherwood number and a corresponding 5% decrease in the Nusselt number. Some further results are reported by Aich et al. [37].

The study of fluid flow with a magnetic field, called magnetohydrodynamics (MHD), is applied in various industries and fields including clinical medicine as well as industrial engineering [38]. The biomedical field is one area where magnetic fields are used to control the flow of blood in surgery, to increase contrast in MRI scans and to propel targeted drug delivery systems, where drug attached to magnetic nanoparticles is directed to a specific target such as a tumor [39]. These principles can also be used to develop MHD micropumps and mixers that do not require moving mechanical components to displace conductive biological fluids or reagents on a microfluidic scale [40]. MHD finds industrial applications in the suppression of instabilities in molten metal in continuous casting, in liquid metal power electromagnetic pumps in nuclear power plants, and in the propulsion of marine vessels by magnetohydrodynamic drives [41]. In addition, the coupling of the magnetic field and fluid velocity generates a Lorentz force which can be utilized in mechanical rate sensing and flow control in a complex fluid network, which can be utilized effectively in controlling temperature and pressure distributions in high-tech systems [42]. Sarkar and Das [43] used Arrhenius kinetics and a magnetic field to examine the bioconvection through Sutterby nanoliquid flow motile microbes across a slippery, horizontal cylinder.

The magnetized transport of an Ag-Fe3O4/VPO hybrid nanofluid over spinning inclined disc is studied in the current investigation that fills a considerable gap in the literature as it introduces a complex interaction between centrifugal, Coriolis, and gravitational forces. This is unlike the current literature that concentrates on conventional base fluids and this work specifically examines the application of Vacuum Pump Oil (VPO) to simulate high-performance industrial lubrication systems. This novelty is also augmented by the use of the nonlinear Thompson–Troian slip condition giving a more realistic description of the molecular-level behavior of fluids at high shear rates compared to simple linear slip descriptions. This study offers novel physical understanding of how the synergy effects of high conductivity Silver and ferromagnetic Magnetite particles can be exploited alongside nonlinear slip and magnetic inclination to gain a 31.64% improvement in the energy transport efficiency, which is a very important and crucial benchmark on optimization of cooling systems of electrical and mechanical appliances.

The model for the HNF flow over an SD has been developed in the imminent section.

Solution methodology (BVP4c)

The boundary value problems (BVPs) can be answered by employing the MATLAB solver BVP4C. It uses a piecewise polynomial approximation-based collocation technique that enables flexible manipulation of complex BCs and different solution tendencies. Because of adaptive refinement of the mesh and error control procedures, BVP4C's robustness and precision for tackling tough or nonlinear BVPs are among its main benefits over other numerical algorithms. Furthermore, BVP4C can effectively handle issues with multiple BCs and singularities and is very user-friendly, providing a simple interface for defining BCs and initial assumptions. When compared to alternative techniques like finite difference and shooting approaches, its capacity to automatically adjust the computational mesh improves solution precision while reducing computational time, making it a potent tool, particularly for difficult boundary value problems.

The impact of nonlinear thermal radiation, mixed convection, viscous dissipation, irregular heat source/sink, and Joule heating is also examined on the fluid flow. Limitations and future directions

Limitations

Although the current research paper has given considerable contributions to the understanding of the magnetized flow of Ag-Fe3O4/VPO hybrid nanofluids on an inclined spinning disc, it is prone to a number of limitations, which should be recognized. First, the numerical model presupposes a single-phase fluid model and considers the base fluid and NPs as an ideal homogenous mixture. This makes it easier to avoid the possible agglomeration and settling of NPs typical of real-world experimental designs. Second, thermophysical correlations are static mixture correlations, and thus may not be sufficient to account for the dynamic variation in both thermal conductivity and viscosity at extreme temperature gradients. Also, the Thompson–Troian slip condition which states a realistic high-shear model, does not consider the potential chemical interactions between the vacuum pump oil and the spinning surface which can be further modify, to improve the behavior of the boundary layer.

Future directions

This research presents a number of directions, which can be explored further. The future would be in the form of a 2-phase model (the model of Buongiorno) to be able to see more clearly the effects of Brownian motion and thermophoresis on the migration of particles. Further research into the behaviour of ternary HNFs (such as the incorporation of a third NPs, such as CNTs or Copper) is potentially able to show further benefits in terms of thermal efficiency increase than the 31.64% in the present study. In addition, this model could be generalized to cover non-Newtonian fluid properties (i.e. Carreau or Casson models) to model a broader variety of industrial lubricants. Lastly, experimental validation through measurement of specific viscosity of Ag-Fe3O4/VPO would help in bridging the popularity gap between this theoretical model and its practical application in high-vacuum mechanical systems.

Dataset on the influence of carbon dioxide (CO2) concentration and growth temperature on biomass productivity of chlorella micro-algae in green fuel synthesis
Authors: Folayan Adewale Johnson
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Chlorella micro-algae species are widely employed in sustainable biofuel production and development due to their high biomass and lipid accumulation rates.In this data article, the effect of carbon dioxide (CO2) concentration and culture temperature on the growth rate and biomass productivity of chlorella vulgaris micro algae in sustainable biofuel development was investigated. The micro-algae strains (SAG 211-11b) were isolated from the International Institute of Tropical Agriculture (IITA) and cultured with modified blue green algae (Modified BG-11) nutrients under photoautotrophic conditions and continuous illumination. The light intensity was maintained at 300μmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹ and measured using a portable quantum light meter (Model 3415F, pulse photoelectric sensor; spectrum Technologies Inc., USA). The pre-cultured micro-algal cells were transferred into bubble bioreactors consisting of 500 mL Erlenmeyer flasks that contain 300 mL of BG-11 medium. The CO₂ concentration in the aeration gas was adjusted to 0.04% (air), 2%, 4%, 6%, 8%, 10%, 12%, and 15%, and experiments were conducted at growth temperatures of 25°C, 27.5°C, 30°C and 32.5°C. The experimental measurements were analyzed with statistical tools (standard error and analysis of variance, ANOVA) by using IBM SPSS statistics version 30.0. The air (0.04% CO2 concentration) has the lowest biomass productivity at all the tested growth temperatures. While the highest biomass productivity of 3.75± 0.0927g L⁻¹ day⁻¹ was recorded at growth temperature of 30°C and under optimum CO2 concentration of 6.0%. However, further increases in growth temperature beyond this optimum level (30°C) resulted in a sharp decline in growth rate and biomass yield. The null hypothesis (H0) was rejected at the 5% significance value since the calculated FRstatistics > Fcritical at α = 0.05. Finally, the data is of utmost importance to the bio-energy researchers and scientific community in selecting optimum growth parameters for enhanced micro-algae growth and biomass productivity.

Background

Chlorella microalgae have become a major research focus for their potential in carbon dioxide (CO₂) fixation and synthesis of high value liquid fuels (biofuels) owing to their rapid growth rates, strong adaptability to environmental stresses, and high biomass and lipid yields [[1], [2], [3], [4], [5]].

Green fuels are clean, renewable and environmentally acceptable sources of energy with lower emission of greenhouse gases. Chlorella micro-algae are promising biomass feedstocks in green fuel production because of their high photosynthetic efficiency and ability to accumulate lipids and carbohydrates for biofuel synthesis by utilizing sunlight and inorganic carbon dioxide (CO2) to rapidly generate biomass, which can be converted into biodiesel from its lipids (triacylglycerol) by transesterification reaction, production of bioethanol and biohydrogen from its carbohydrates through fermentation/bio photolysis processes and biogas generation from its residual biomass by anaerobic digestion making it a versatile feedstock for multiple green fuels synthesis [6]. Compared to terrestrial energy crops, Chlorella has a higher growth rate, does not require arable land, and can be cultivated year-round, making it a sustainable and efficient feedstock for green fuel production.

Furthermore, the relationship between green fuel and Chlorella micro-algae promotes environmental sustainability and circular bio-economy development. Its cultivation can be integrated with wastewater treatment and industrial CO₂ capture, where the algae remove nutrients and absorb carbon dioxide while producing biomass for fuel conversion. This process synergy reduces pollution, lowers biofuel production costs, and enhances overall carbon neutrality of the fuel lifecycle. Therefore, the micro-algae provide a biologically efficient and environmentally friendly pathway for green fuel production, supporting the transition from fossil fuels to biofuels.

According to Folayan and Patel 2025 [7], C.vulgaris micro-algae green fuel (biodiesel) has a fuel density and kinematic viscosity values of 0.858g/cm3 at 15°C and 3.61mm2/s at 40°C respectively. These lower values enhance efficient fuel-air mixture, support excellent lubricity attributes, promote better fuel injection, atomization and spray characteristics and reduce formation of engine deposits and smoke emissions due to more efficient combustion qualities. Also, the biofuel has a cetane index and calorific values of 55.6 and 41.12 MJ/Kg respectively and therefore exhibits superior ignition qualities with lower fuel consumption tendencies, better fuel combustion and performance characteristics and reduced oxides of nitrogen, sulphur, carbon and particulate matters emissions.

Temperature is one of the key environmental factors influencing Chlorella micro-algae growth, as photosynthesis in microalgal cells comprises a series of temperature-dependent biochemical reactions [8]. Low temperatures reduce the activity of photosynthetic and metabolic enzymes, thereby limiting growth. Moderately high temperatures can enhance respiratory activity and metabolic rates. While excessively high temperatures inhibit enzymatic function, metabolic activity, and respiration [9].

In a related development, CO₂ serves as an essential inorganic carbon source for photoautotrophic microalgal growth. When an algal culture is enriched with an appropriate CO₂ concentration, photosynthetic activities are enhanced by increasing CO₂ availability around the RuBisCO enzyme, thereby improving the ratio of carboxylation to oxygenation reactions and the overall efficiency of CO₂ fixation [10]. However, excessive CO₂ can inhibit extracellular enzymes such as carbonic anhydrase due to increased culture acidity [11,12]. Under such conditions, much of the cellular energy and CO₂ consumed is diverted towards mitigating high-CO₂ stress rather than promoting biomass synthesis, leading to reduced overall growth [13].

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Microalgae cultivation and biomass determination

C.vulgaris (strain SAG 211-11b) was obtained from the International Institute of Tropical Agriculture (IITA) and pre-cultured in 500 mL Erlenmeyer flasks at 25°C under continuous illumination. The light intensity was maintained at 300μmol photons m⁻²s⁻¹ and measured using a portable quantum light meter (Model 3415F, Pulse Photoelectric Sensor; Spectrum Technologies Inc., USA). The flasks were agitated at 300RPM to prevent sedimentation and ensure proper gas exchange. The BG-11 medium was prepared according to the protocols described by Sumitha et al. (2018) [15] and CSIRO (2021) [16]. The pre-cultured micro-algal cells were transferred into bubble bioreactors consisting of 500 mL Erlenmeyer flasks that contain 300 mL of BG-11 medium (initial pH 9.0). Thereafter, filtered air enriched with CO₂ was continuously bubbled through the culture medium via a glass tube (2 mm internal diameter) at a fixed aeration rate of 0.1 vvm (air volume per medium volume per minute). The CO₂ concentration in the aeration gas was adjusted to 0.04% (air), 2%, 4%, 6%, 8%, 10%, 12%, and 15%, and experiments were conducted at growth temperatures of 25°C, 27.5°C, 30°C and 32.5°C. The pH of each culture was measured daily throughout the experimental period. At the end of cultivation, algal biomass was harvested and quantified according to the method of Ho et al. (2013) [17]. Specifically, 10 mL of culture was filtered through a pre-weighed 0.45μm cellulose acetate membrane filter. The loaded filters were dried at 65°C until a constant weight was achieved, and biomass concentration was calculated based on the dry weight difference.

The microalgae biomass B (g L−1) was calculated by using Eq. (1)(1)Biomassweight(W)=M2−M1V

Where:

M1 = mass of the blank filter (g),

M2 = mass of the filter loaded with chlorella microalgal cells (g), and

V = volume of the culture (0.01 L).

Statistical analysis of experimental measurements

The results are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) of three observations replicates. Statistical analysis of data was performed by using one-way analysis of variance (IBM SPSS statistics version 30.0). A value of p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

The standard error of the sample mean was estimated from the variance of a sum of independent random variables.

Suppose, x1,x2,x3…….xnare statistically independent sample measurements of “n” independent observations from a population with meanx¯ and standard deviation σ, then the true or total measurement (T) can be described by Eq. (2)(2)T=(x1+x2+x3…….+xn

The variance can be expressed in terms of Bienayme’s formular by Eq. (3)(3)Var(T)=Var(x1)+Var(x2)+Var(x3)+…+Var(xn)

Limitations

This study evaluates the effects of carbon dioxide (CO2) concentration and growth temperature on biomass productivity of C.vulgaris micro-algae in the laboratory. However, the experiments were conducted under well-defined in-vitro conditions and the data obtained may not fully reflect or accommodate the complexities of in-vivo environment.

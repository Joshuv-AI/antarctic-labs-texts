Effectiveness of ergonomic interventions in the 21st century: a protocol for an umbrella review of systematic reviews
Authors: Mehdi Beyrami Jam, Mohammad Sadegh Sohrabi, Rashid Heidarimoghadam, Mehrane Shabani

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionWork-related musculoskeletal disorders (WMSDs) remain a global challenge despite technological advancements and evolving workplace practices. Ergonomic interventions—including workplace redesign, equipment modification, training and organisational strategies—have been increasingly adopted to address these challenges. However, systematic reviews assessing their effectiveness have yielded heterogeneous and sometimes contradictory findings. This protocol outlines a methodology for an umbrella review of systematic reviews aimed at synthesising high-quality evidence on ergonomic interventions in diverse occupational settings, thereby informing both practice and policy.Methods and analysisWe will conduct an umbrella review of systematic reviews and meta-analyses published between January 2000 and 2026 in PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science and the Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. Eligible reviews will include workers aged 18 years and older exposed to ergonomic risks and will examine physical, educational, organisational or multicomponent interventions. Primary outcomes will be reductions in WMSDs and pain, while secondary outcomes will include productivity, absenteeism, job satisfaction and workplace safety. The methodological quality of included reviews will be assessed using A Measurement Tool to Assess Systematic Reviews 2. Findings will be synthesised narratively and summarised in structured tables by intervention type, population, outcome and review quality. Overlap among reviews will be evaluated using the corrected covered area, and where multiple reviews address the same question, their conclusions will be compared based on population, intervention characteristics, outcomes, comparators, follow-up and methodological quality. Since this study involves the secondary analysis of previously published data and does not involve direct contact with human participants, individual informed consent is not required. The study will be conducted in accordance with the ethical standards of the responsible committee on human experimentation and transparency will be maintained throughout the process. Introduction

Systematic reviews and meta-analyses are widely regarded as the gold standard for evidence synthesis, offering structured and transparent approaches in healthcare and occupational health research.1,3 In modern occupational environments, workers face a broad range of ergonomic challenges driven by rapid technological advances, automation, digitalisation and new organisational structures. Work-related musculoskeletal disorders (WMSDs) remain a major cause of disability, absenteeism, reduced productivity and economic burden worldwide.4,6 Ergonomic interventions—including workplace redesign, equipment modification, training programmes and organisational strategies—are widely applied as solutions to mitigate these risks.7 8 Previous systematic reviews and meta-analyses have examined ergonomic interventions, but their findings are frequently heterogeneous and inconsistent, limiting generalisability.9 10 In the past two decades, systematic reviews have increasingly been employed to evaluate the effectiveness of ergonomic interventions, providing decision-makers with structured evidence. While these reviews synthesise large volumes of data using rigorous methods, several limitations remain.6 11 Substantial heterogeneity in study designs, interventions, populations and workplace settings, together with variable quality and inconsistent statistical methods, complicates interpretation and limits generalisability. In addition, short follow-up durations, lack of standardised outcome measures and limited attention to sustainability reduce the applicability of findings. Contemporary challenges such as digital ergonomics, remote work and automation-related risks are also rarely addressed. Consequently, despite the growing body of literature, a clear and comprehensive picture of ergonomic intervention effectiveness in the twenty-first century is lacking.12 This evidence gap is particularly critical in high-risk sectors like healthcare, where workers face substantial physical and psychological demands, highlighting the urgent need for more robust and context-sensitive syntheses.

Umbrella reviews in occupational health research have demonstrated the added value of synthesising higher-level evidence to clarify the effectiveness of complex interventions. Positioned at the highest tier of scientific evidence, umbrella reviews integrate findings from multiple systematic reviews rather than primary studies, thereby providing a broader and more critical synthesis of knowledge. This approach allows not only for the comparison and evaluation of existing systematic reviews but also for the identification of promising evidence, major research gaps and overarching trends, offering clearer guidance for both policymaking and future research. Recent reviews indicate that ergonomic interventions—ranging from physical modifications to equipment and workplaces to targeted training and organisational strategies—can reduce musculoskeletal disorders, lower occupational injury risks and enhance productivity. In this protocol, 21st-century work environments refer to remote and hybrid work, digital-intensive office settings, technology-driven industrial workplaces and healthcare settings, where ergonomic risks may arise from both physical and organisational factors. For example, Bai highlighted insufficient research on home furniture ergonomics and the importance of gender differences in shaping intervention outcomes,13 while Sweeney reported evidence for the effectiveness of short breaks and wide-handled tools in reducing upper-limb discomfort among healthcare workers.14 Broader analyses suggest that multicomponent ergonomic programmes provide the most consistent improvements in worker health and performance, although the strength of evidence for specific intervention types remains mixed.

This umbrella review aims to map and critically synthesise the evidence from systematic reviews on ergonomic interventions for WMSDs. Specifically, it will assess the strength and consistency of evidence across intervention types, identify overlaps among primary studies included in different reviews and examine reasons for conflicting findings across systematic reviews.

Methods and analysis

Protocol and registration

This protocol has been registered in PROSPERO (CRD420251147970). The study will follow A Measurement Tool to Assess Systematic Reviews 2 (AMSTAR 2),3 the Joanna Briggs Institute methodology for umbrella reviews15 and Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P)16 reporting guidelines.

Ethics and dissemination

This umbrella review protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Hamadan University of Medical Sciences (ID: IR.UMSHA.REC.1404.583). As the review synthesises data from previously published studies without involving human participants, informed consent is not required. The review will adhere to established ethical standards for research and ensure transparency throughout the process. Findings will be disseminated through publication in a peer-reviewed journal, presentations at international conferences and communication with policymakers to support evidence-based ergonomic practice.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and the public will not be directly involved in the design or execution of this umbrella review. However, findings will be disseminated to occupational health stakeholders, policymakers and worker representatives.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Types of participants

Employed adults (≥18 years) across industrial (blue-collar), office (white-collar), healthcare (pink-collar), service, construction and other occupational sectors. Studies exclusively involving students, children or clinical rehabilitation patients outside workplace settings will be excluded.

Phenomena of interest

Systematic reviews evaluating ergonomic interventions (physical, educational, organisational or multicomponent) aimed at preventing or reducing WMSDs, improving occupational health, productivity or workplace safety.

Context

Occupational settings in the 21st century, including both traditional workplaces (manual labour, offices, healthcare) and modern contexts influenced by digitalisation, remote work and automation.

Types of studies

Systematic reviews and meta-analyses of interventional studies (RCTs, quasi-experimental or mixed designs). Narrative reviews, expert opinions and conference abstracts will be excluded.

Search strategy

We will search PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science and the Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews for English-language systematic reviews published from January 2000 to December 2026. The start date was chosen to focus on 21st-century evidence that is most relevant to contemporary work environments, evolving technologies and current ergonomic practice. The search period will be extended through December 2026 to ensure inclusion of the most up-to-date eligible systematic reviews available prior to conducting the umbrella review. Search strategies will include combinations of controlled vocabulary (Medical Subject Headings/Emtree) and free-text terms such as ‘ergonomic interventions’, ‘occupational health’,’WMSDs’, ‘workplace safety’, ‘systematic review’ and ‘umbrella review’. The details of the search strategy are specified in table 1. Reference lists of included studies will be hand-searched, and citation tracking will be conducted using Scopus and Web of Science.

Search terms in databases

The review will include occupational settings in both traditional and contemporary work environments, including office-based work, healthcare, manufacturing, service industries, remote work, telework and digitally mediated work settings. The search strategy will include additional terms related to digital ergonomics and modern work environments, such as telework, remote work, digital human factors, visual display terminal, screen-based work and automation-related ergonomic risks.

Study timeline

The review will begin in January 2027 and is expected to be completed by April 2027. The database searches will cover systematic reviews published from January 2000 to December 2026.

Study selection

Two reviewers will independently screen titles, abstracts and full texts against inclusion criteria. References will be managed using EndNote, and duplicate records will be removed prior to screening. Title/abstract and full-text screening will be conducted using EndNote. Discrepancies will be resolved by discussion or consultation with a third reviewer. The PRISMA flow diagram will document the selection process.

Assessment of methodological quality

The methodological quality of included systematic reviews will be appraised using the AMSTAR-2 tool.3 Reviews will be categorised as high, moderate, low or critically low quality. When two or more high-quality systematic reviews address the same intervention and outcome but report conflicting conclusions, we will first examine the methodological quality of the reviews using AMSTAR-2. We will then compare the reviews with regard to population characteristics, intervention fidelity, outcome definitions, follow-up duration and analytical methods to identify plausible sources of discrepancy. If the disagreement can be explained by these differences, the findings will be reported accordingly. Where multiple reviews remain equally eligible, priority will be given to the most comprehensive and methodologically robust review; otherwise, all conflicting findings will be transparently presented and interpreted in the context of review quality and clinical/methodological heterogeneity.

Data collection

A standardised data extraction form2 will be used to collect information on study characteristics, interventions, populations, outcomes and main findings. Data extraction will be performed independently by two reviewers. Ergonomic interventions will be classified a priori into the following categories: workstation or equipment redesign, assistive devices and tools, manual handling or lifting aids, training and educational programmes, exercise-based interventions, organisational or administrative interventions and multicomponent interventions. Multicomponent interventions will be defined as interventions combining two or more distinct ergonomic strategies and will be analysed separately from single-component interventions whenever possible.

Data summary

Findings will be synthesised narratively and presented in structured evidence tables. Where available, pooled effect estimates with corresponding CIs will be reported alongside methodological quality ratings, consistency of findings, heterogeneity and evidence gaps across reviews. Clinical and methodological heterogeneity will be assessed and summarised in both tabular and narrative formats, with explicit consideration of differences in populations, interventions, comparators, outcomes, study designs and follow-up periods. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and conference presentations in occupational health, ergonomics and public health, complemented by targeted knowledge translation activities (policy briefs, webinars and stakeholder briefings) tailored to researchers, practitioners, workplace managers, occupational health organisations and policymakers to support evidence-informed practice and decision-making.

Outcomes

The primary outcomes of this umbrella review will be reductions in WMSDs and pain. Secondary outcomes will include indicators of workplace performance and well-being, such as productivity, absenteeism, job satisfaction and workplace safety. Together, these outcomes provide a comprehensive assessment of both health-related and organisational impacts of ergonomic interventions.

Discussion

The findings from the umbrella review will be synthesised narratively and presented in structured summary tables to ensure a transparent and methodologically rigorous synthesis process. Given the protocol nature of the review, the discussion will not anticipate specific results; instead, it will justify the methodological choices adopted for the review and address anticipated challenges such as heterogeneity in populations, interventions, outcomes and analytical methods across the included systematic reviews. Where applicable, overlap among reviews will be assessed using the corrected covered area, and the methodological quality of the included reviews will be appraised using AMSTAR-2, with the certainty of evidence summarised using Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation to support an evidence-informed interpretation of the review outputs.

Dissemination of the completed umbrella review will occur through peer-reviewed publication and presentations at relevant scientific conferences, with additional knowledge translation activities designed for key stakeholders, including policy briefs, stakeholder workshops and audience-specific summaries for researchers, practitioners, workplace managers, occupational health organisations and policymakers. These dissemination strategies are intended to facilitate the responsible translation of the review’s methodological findings and synthesised evidence into practice and decision-making.

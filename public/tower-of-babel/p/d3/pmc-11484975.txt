Building a Chinese ancient architecture multimodal dataset combining image, annotation and style-model
Authors: Biao Li, Jinyuan Feng, Yunxi Yan, Gang Kou, Hemin Li, Yang Du, Xun Wang, Tie Li, Yi Peng, Kun Guo, Yong Shi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this rapidly evolving era of multimodal generation, diffusion models exhibit impressive generative capabilities, significantly enhancing the realm of creative image synthesis by intricately textual prompts. Yet, their effectiveness is limited in certain niche sectors, like depicting Chinese ancient architecture. This limitation is primarily due to the insufficient data that fails to encompass the unique architectural features and corresponding text information. Hence, we build an extensive multimodal dataset capturing the essence of Chinese architectures mostly from the Tang to the Yuan Dynasties. The dataset is categorized on the types, including image&text, video, and style models. In details, images and videos are methodically categorized based on locations. All images are annotated at two levels: initial annotations and descriptive terms based on distinctive characteristics and official information. Moreover, seven artistic styles fine-tuning models are provided in our dataset for further innovations. Significantly, this is the first Chinese ancient architecture dataset and the instance of using the Pinyin system to annotate unique terms related to Chinese architectural styles.Subject terms: Architecture, Architecture

Background & Summary

In the last two years, the utilization of generative models, like Stable Diffusion1, SDXL2, DALL-E33,4 and Midjourney5, have become increasingly popular for creating architectural imagery6,7. This popularity is due to their remarkable ability to generate high-fidelity images from text prompts8. This paradigm shift in architectural visualization and representation underscores the intricate challenge of identifying and categorizing architectural styles9. Architecture, as a discipline, is distinguished by its pronounced geometric complexities and artistic aesthetics, characteristics that vary markedly across different geographical regions and historical periods. For instance, ancient Chinese architecture exhibits significant variations based on factors, such as the historical period, intended use, and hierarchical status. These distinctions are emblematic of the broader challenges inherent in the identification and categorization of architectural styles10. Furthermore, the generation styles corresponding to the architecture need to be combed and analyzed by professionals in the field. Consequently, an effective generative model in this realm necessitates an extensive and nuanced learning process based on a specially collected, collated professional dataset, like11. This process entails a thorough integration of both high-quality visual images and accurate textual information specific to various architectural genres. The objective is to enable the model to accurately identify and interpret the subtle and often minute stylistic nuances that define distinct architectural styles. The successful application of such models hinges on their ability to generate results that are not only precise in their stylistic depiction, but also resonate with the intricate aesthetic sensibilities intrinsic to the field of architectural design. Moreover, generating specific architectural styles based on these data will undoubtedly provide immense assistance to the fields of artistic creation and architectural design.

Referring to the Chinese ancient architecture, as a signal representation of ancient sites, have historical and artistic value, and are important to historical education. Such cultural relics provide insights into human values and achievements throughout history12. Noted that the Chinese ancient buildings vary a lot not only for different period, but also for different grade. Buildings in different dynasty carry some unique features. All these bring great challenge to the generative models. If we want to obtain some similar ones, carefully designed prompts and reasonable features must be learned during training period. Mostly, satisfied image is rarely generated by Midjourney or Stable Diffusion. There are two important reasons. In details, the first one comes from the predominance of English-centric training in most diffusion models. It results in a structural misalignment when applied to non-English cultural contexts. The second issue concerns the linguistic adequacy of English. As a common sense, it can encapsulate the Western cultural nuances well but frequently falls short in articulating the subtleties of other languages. For other area, there are a lot of unique definition, like the unique architectural heritage in Makkah13 and the name of Chinese ancient architecture’s roof14. As a result, how these unique elements can be learned by generative models raises as a challenge task.

In the area of the multimodal modeling, models typically are set with a substantial number of parameters, as much as billions. These models are trained on a vast array of datasets, requiring significant computational resources and the majority of niche datasets are incapable of facilitating effective model training. Consequently, fine-tuning has emerged as a cost-effective and feasible approach, particularly for research teams. On the other hand, the availability of image data in a specific domain is often limited. Models trained on a small number of images from niche fields struggle to achieve satisfactory results. Remarkably, the predominant fine-tuning methodologies include three approaches: all parameter fine-tuning, partial parameter fine-tuning (such as LoRA15), and prompt engineering. The data requirements for these methods do not significantly vary, yet the foundation of high-quality data remains crucial.

There are two important elements, high-quality images and associated text, making a significant impact on the generated images16. The first one reflects the current status of the Chinese ancient buildings which is recorded by images and all architectural details and features can be displayed in detail in the images. However, these Chinese ancient buildings have differences according to dynasties or functions while they maintain a strong consistency overall at the same time. The second element provides corresponding text to these images and can be used as a efficient and easily understood manner to obtain the generated result. Most traditional data just provide simple label, such as the definition of the building. However, the generative models require more descriptions, like the environment and related information about the objects in the picture. As a result, these new generative models require richer description information and multimodal data sets composed of high-quality images. Despite existing large models performs satisfied in well-studied scene, the training or fine-tuning strategy of the ancient architecture area are still challenging. It requires both domain knowledge and ML expertise17. Since the subject is subjective and culturally dependent, it can alleviate the potentially challenge by applying fine-tuning workflow with high-quality dataset. Recent works have demonstrated the possibility of fine-tuning pre-trained models to other tasks, like vision tasks18,19, NLP area20,21, and reinforcement learning22. A common sense can be obtained from these approaches that fine-tuning format can address the issue between generality and specific-task in cross-domain learning.

To overcome the issue in the Chinese ancient building, we generally provide a framework including multimodal dataset from data-collecting to prompt designing. There are 581 images and 24 videos in the proposed dataset. From a scientific research perspective, the construction of the dataset encompasses three distinct components. Firstly, it comprises multiple sets of architecture from the Yuan Dynasty and earlier periods, all sourced from Shanxi. This segment includes various types of data, such as front, side, and rear views of buildings, as well as drone-captured video footage. Secondly, the dataset incorporates relevant historical information, presented in the form of textual descriptions. Thirdly, it includes architectural style illustrations, which can be utilized to train corresponding style-based Layerwise Relevance Optimization (LoRA)15 models. Moreover, another key innovation in this work is the introduction of LoRA models and the incorporation of Pinyin in prompts. These techniques are adept at addressing the complexities involved in accurately capturing and reproducing the intricate details of ancient Chinese architecture. This approach is both practical and promising, potentially leading to more efficient and effective models capable of generating high-fidelity representations of these ancient structures. In order to provide a more suitable artistic style related to architecture, we sought out experts in the field of artistic creation to model architecture-related styles. As a result, seven artistic styles of LoRA models are trained and supplied in our multimodal dataset for further innovations.

The evaluation of the models’ ability to generate accurate and faithful representations of ancient Chinese buildings is proved by both subject fidelity — the degree to which the generated images match the actual buildings — and prompt fidelity — the alignment of the generated images with the textual descriptions. This dual focus is crucial in assessing the effectiveness of the proposed multimodal dataset. Overall, our work is a significant contribution to the digital preservation and representation of historical architecture. It demonstrates how modern technology can be leveraged to capture, preserve, and disseminate knowledge about our cultural heritage. The dataset will pave the way for similar initiatives, focusing on other architectural styles and periods, thereby enhancing our global understanding and appreciation of historical architecture.

Methods

Overview of the Dataset

The dataset contains over 500 photographs and 24 videos of Chinese ancient buildings located in the central and southern part of Shanxi province. To provide more details, each photograph is set to a size of 3000 × 2000 or 3000 × 1687 resolutions in the dataset, and it is qualified for all existing generative models, like SDXL2. The vast majority of these buildings can be traced back to the Yuan Dynasty and even earlier dynasties, such as the Tang, Five Dynasties, Liao, Song, Jin, etc. Ancient buildings built in these dynasties have existed for 600 years, representing the historical features of that period and having important humanistic value. Unlike the architectural style of the Forbidden City, which is the most famous Chinese ancient architectural complex, these buildings exhibit more distinct characteristics of an ancient era. As a result, our dataset provides more data basis for artificial intelligence model learning of Chinese ancient buildings. Compared with the fact that most of current famous models generate unsatisfied results which are similar to the appearance of the Forbidden City buildings.

As we know, Chinese architecture has formed a strict hierarchical system since ancient times. The shape of the roof is the most important factor in determining grade classification. Based on the appearance of the building roofs, which is the most striking feature of the different styles, we marked four styles of buildings with the name of roof in our dataset. For each style, we provide photographs of ancient buildings in different regions, different environments, different construction times, and different levels of conservation and restoration in Shanxi Province. In order to enrich the data set, we provide four types pictures for the same building (front, side, overhead and detailed). In addition, the dataset also constructs a multi-level text dataset system for each image, which includes two main parts, one is the accurate name annotation of ancient buildings in photograph, and the other is the detailed annotation of the buildings with background information. Such a dataset is well-suited for the training or fine-tuning of multimodal models. Taking Stable Diffusion as an example, such image data and multi-level text information can be well integrated into model training, including diffusion1 and CLIP23, based on some cleverly designed fine-tuned strategy mechanics, like LoRA15. Results in the Technical Validation part prove this.

Data Acquisition

The image dataset contains images of most of the existing ancient buildings in Shanxi Province and most of the buildings date back to the Yuan Dynasty and beyond (1368 AD and before). As mentioned in the research24, Shanxi is the major province of cultural relics in China. It has a complete sequence of ancient buildings and many categories. There are 518 wooden structures can be traced back to the Yuan Dynasty and before, ranking first in the country. The details are shown in the Table 1. The selection of image data acquisition targets was primarily guided by the geographic distribution of China’s national ancient buildings. High-quality on-site photography was adopted as the primary source of image data. To efficiently utilize resources and minimize expenses, we chose Shanxi province as the sampling site. This strategy is particularly effective in Shanxi Province, where a substantial concentration of China’s ancient architectural heritage exists.

Another criterion considered in the dataset creation process was the historical period associated with the ancient buildings. The majority of structures included in the dataset were constructed during the Yuan Dynasty and earlier periods. The decision to concentrate on ancient buildings was driven by the notably skewed representation of architecture from the Ming and Qing Dynasties in existing general datasets. The trend is highlighted by the frequent inclusion of iconic sites like the Forbidden City, which often overshadow less represented architectural styles and periods. This emphasis reflects the global recognition and acclaim attributed to Ming and Qing Dynasty buildings, while architecture of pre-Ming Dynasty has remained relatively understudied. To address this gap in existing research, this study compiled buildings absent from existing ancient building datasets, specifically focusing on those dating from the Yuan Dynasty and earlier periods. At the same time, Shanxi province has also preserved a large number of ancient buildings built pre-Ming Dynasty, which further explains the correctness of choosing Shanxi as the sampling site.

In order to provide more comprehensive data related to ancient buildings, we collected a large amount of picture data on the spot, and organized and post-processed the images according to the location. Beside image data, the description text corresponding to these buildings plays an important role during training process. Prior images are labelled with the total name and today’s multimodal model training requires more detailed descriptive information. We have collected a large amount of professional text data for these building from some professional websites and existing multimodal models. Moreover, One of our authors named Xun Wang who have long been engaged in the field of art is invited to construct several sub-datasets. All images are chosen from her works and features distinct architectural styles with significant characteristics. More fine-tuning models are trained with these high-quality art classification data and corresponding style description text. Thanks to these LoRA models trained on these professionally screened data, our dataset can better achieve multi-style image generation creation.

Basis details for data collection. Manual photography is a necessary approach for photo collection to obtain first-hand data and ensure professionalism, manual photography is employed for image retrieval. To this end, we firstly determine to visit Yuncheng, Linfen, Changzhi, and Jincheng areas in Shanxi Province, abd our target object focuses on Yuan Dynasty and earlier ancient buildings. According to the prior research24, these four city areas house an estimated 80% of the totally number of ancient buildings in the province. Moreover, we strategically selected late July for the photography campaign to get the best possible image in light conditions. The entire data acquisition cycle continued a full week.

Shooting challenges. Due to their conservation status and restricted public access, the obtained images exhibit inconsistent quality. Additionally, as many of these ancient structures are dispersed across the surrounding towns of these four cities and lack adequate protection, the surroundings hinder the acquisition of suitable shooting angles, presenting a challenge to manual photography. Nevertheless, given the present demand for pixels, lighting, and shadow in AI models, we strive to capture high-resolution, high-quality photographs.

Images&Videos Collecting and organizing. The comprehensive image dataset is constructed after thorough shooting and sorting comprises 59 ancient buildings in Shanxi Province and most are nationally protected while others are provincially and municipally protected. The majority of these structures are built dating back to the Yuan Dynasty or earlier, which serve as quintessential examples of traditional Chinese ancient architecture. For each ancient building, we captured images of its exterior and surrounding environment from multiple vantage points, with particular focus on architectural features that embody the building’s essence, such as roofs and brackets (named dougong in Chinese). Some are captured with images and videos taken by the drone. Moreover, the telephoto photography was employed for close-up shots, while aerial drone photography was utilized to provide a panoramic view of the ancient structures. This multifaceted approach aimed to capture the essence of each ancient building through a comprehensive collection of images. In the end, we obtained a large amount of images and videos data.

Text data

Text data annotation and construction. As mentioned by prior research25, describing images in natural language is a critical problem of vision-language learning. The development of the text dataset necessitates annotating each image within the compiled image dataset. Following data annotation procedures from previous multimodal research areas, the annotation process of our dataset is meticulously divided into two distinct steps. In the initial step, pre-training techniques are employed to generate a preliminary rough-draft text. We utilize the BLIP26 model to automatically label the input image, enabling it to generate a corresponding interpretation based on the image’s content. As a result, the information of the existing pre-trained Language-Image models, like CLIP23 and BLIP26, can be effectively expressed in text annotation. However, these generated primary annotations are reverse annotations of the training data and lack expression for unseen content. The interactive image description is the the core mechanism of the current image generative models, and it requires that the image description information required in the training process must be richer, more accurate and more detailed. To this end, we incorporate expert descriptions of ancient buildings sourced from the State Administration of Cultural Heritage, local cultural relics protection units, and online repositories like Shanxi culture relics bureau27, Wikipedia and Baidu Encyclopedia. What needs to be made especially clear is that there is a lot of duplication in these related contents, and we mainly adopted the contents of shanxi culture relics bureau27.

This fusion forms a foundational text, encompassing crucial information such as the names of the ancient buildings depicted in the image and their visual characteristics. The subsequent step involves meticulously compiling the historical, geographical, developmental, and influential aspects of the relevant buildings from aforementioned sources to manually refine the text. This enriched information is then seamlessly interwoven with image-specific details, such as the image’s shooting angle, to generate the refined text.

Pinyin annotation. A fact that needs to be stated is that the components and structures of Chinese architecture have many unique names that are different from those of Western architectures. As a result, how to exactly caption the content details of the image is a matter of the explicit expression for further AI multimodal learning tasks. Traditional translations translate a simple Chinese ancient building name into complex English phrases, such as the gable and hip roof. In order to describe the names of these parts more concisely and precisely, Pinyin, the basic tool for learning Chinese, is introduced into the text data annotation as the specific tools to describe the characters of these buildings. With the help of pinyin, the name of the gable and hip roof can be concisely expressed as xieshan roof.

Style Data

Considering the future application of multimodal models training with the proposed Chinese ancient architecture dataset, we collaborate with experts in related fields of artistic creation to build some AI-design related fine-tuning models. In fact, architectural style is a multifaceted concept, inextricably linked to aesthetic considerations. To ensure the accuracy and comprehensiveness of architectural style classification, we engaged the expertise who is experienced in the field of designing to perform a professional review of relevant architectural styles. We provide seven categories referring to different styles and scenes. Each category combines with detailed description including relevant knowledge background and style characteristics. This endeavor yielded a broad categorization of architectural styles, serving as a robust foundation for the development of the subsequent architectural style LoRA. For the purpose of protecting the original authored content, we do not provide the base images for the training. And seven style LoRA models are trained based on SDXL model. Finally, our datasets supply seven trained LoRA models and one architecture content LoRA model. Combined with the architecture LoRA, we generate some results which are provided in the technical validation part to exhibit the stunning results and the consistency and variety in generated content.

Technical Validation

In this section we conduct evaluation on fine-tuning pretrained diffusion models with our proposed multimodal dataset. Mostly, the fine-tuning process includes pre-trained models, fine-tuning mechanism, data-editing. For specific area, there are a lot of preliminaries to modify. Inevitable conflicts will raise as a big challenge, especially for the ancient buildings across English and Chinese. With the changes of our dataset, this problem has been effectively alleviated. The whole settings are shown in the Fig. 5. In details, all the experiments are performed using Stable Diffusion1 with the pre-trained model SDXL on a single GeForce RTX 4090 GPU. To fine-tuning the model, we adapt the LoRA, a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method, aiming to verify the effectiveness that the extent of our dataset can enhance the performance of the Chinese ancient architecture generation.

The settings of our proposed method is orgnized including training dataset, fine-tuning strategy, inference period, and final generating results. Our work aims to provide a multimodal dataset for further researches in the Chinese ancient architecture area.

To acquire the accurate characteristics, we separately trained LoRA models with both content and style definition. From a content-based perspective, the content LoRA was trained based on the Chinese ancient architecture dataset and subsets for the purpose of only preserving the content of images such as structure details. We provide a content LoRA model in the style folder which is trained with 30 front view images. For different styles compiled by experts, we perform style fine-tuning to capture various types of styles, including color, texture and light. The aim is to capture an overall characteristic without focusing on specific content. There are seven style LoRA models in the style folder. Two models are combined to achieve the personality and alignment of content and style. Results in the Fig. 6 exhibits the outstanding performance of our proposed method. In addition, we can also utilize Controlnet29 for controllable generation.

The comparison results show the generations based on our multimodal dataset with different experimental setups. At the same time, the generating result of SDXL and DALL-E3 with the same promts are also listed in the figure.

For the style-LoRA training, we first use SwinIR30 to restore high-resolution style images form the some low resolution inputs. Then, we carefully modify the annotations corresponding to style images. Specifically, we add the customized style description like “Architecture Concept Sketch Drawings,” at the beginning of the text which will be used to control the style of the generation. This method increases the ability to abstract style information from images. Then, we fine-tune the SDXL model by the LoRA method with kohya_ss31 using style images and new texts. The parameter was set as follows. The learning rate is set as 0.00004 using cosine_with_restarts. The rank is 256 and the batch size use 2. We train 10 epochs and save the result every 2 epochs. Since the content-LoRA training is analogous to style training, we also need to add the customized prompts while retaining the original content. In particular, for unique Chinese ancient architectural like the roofs and brackets, we annotate pinyin like “xieshanding roof” and “dougong” as customized identifiers for an accurate alignment between images and annotations. To verify the results, we implemented LoRA fine-tuning to assess one Chinese ancient building LoRA model and seven style LoRA models, named as: “Front_Building.safetensors”, “Architecture_rendering.safetensors”, “Axonometric_drawing.safetensors”, “3Disometric_rendering_.safetensors”, “Concept_sketch.safetensors”, “Japanese_anime.safetensors”, “Futuristics.safetensors”, “Illustration.safetensors”. During the inference, we can use ancient architecture LoRA combined with any style LoRA to generate new images. Finally, we implemented experiment using SDXL in ComfyUI with our LoRA combinations. The pre-trained SDXL serves as the basic model for various follow-up specialized tasks. Some carefully choosing prompts leveraged in the extensive experiments and comparisons to demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed datasets. During the inference process, the pre-trained SDXL models generate outputs that merge a content LoRA and style LoRAs.

Usage Notes

We present a Chinese ancient Building dataset containing three modalities of information: images, texts and videos. Moreover, seven pre-trained LoRA models and one content LoRA model are provided. We used photo equipment to collect 581 images and 24 videos of ancient architectures ranging from the Tang to the Yuan Dynasty, and each image is described with secondary information annotated by authors.

To provide artistic generation results, Seven art categories related to buildings in art area were used. With the outstanding performance of text-to-image diffusion models, these high-quality pre-trained LoRA models can be combined with other models for accurately image-generating. Other tasks, like 3D reconstruction, can also possible to use our dataset since there are 24 videos. In this paper, our research focuses on the Chinese ancient architecture generation tasks, which has various application prospects and significant culture value.

Potential applications. The multimodal dataset provides directions for further expLoRAtion. It can be applied in multimodal generating ideas related to ancient buildings and culture preservation tasks. One future study could be culture preservation. Due to time, war or other historical factors, many ancient Chinese architectural relics have been damaged to varying degrees. And restoring the buildings requires high-cost professional knowledge. Therefore, in the digital era, applying large models combined with our datasets, damaged buildings can be restored with high efficiency and low cost, which will contribute to the protection of historical buildings. Moreover, the dataset can also be used in architectural design, it is convenient to design and visualize personalized ideas. In addition, the current mainstream text-to-image models are based on western culture. We use fine-tuning methods to allow the model to learn Chinese ancient architectural culture. By sharing data public, the global influence of Chinese ancient architecture can be improved, which is beneficial to culture promotion. However, there are some prior knowledge required while using the dataset, such as programming basics, the theory of generative models and enough computational resources.

Code availability

Python 3.10 was used for data processing and analysis the proposed dataset28. The referred codes for training and testing in this work can be found in the GitHub repository as listed in follows. Python code implementing image generating, text data annotation is available on https://github.com/AUTOMATIC1111/stable-diffusion-webui. The kohya_ss python code is used for LoRA training which can be obtained from https://github.com/bmaltais/kohya_ss. The pretrained LoRA models are available in the proposed dataset28 10.7910/DVN/NR7A5P. The generating ComfyUI model is available on https://github.com/comfyanonymous/ComfyUI with pre-trained SDXL model from https://stablediffusionxl.com/.

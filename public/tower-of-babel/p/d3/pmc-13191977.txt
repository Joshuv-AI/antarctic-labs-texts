Epidemiology of cataract in China from 1990 to 2021: findings from the Global Burden of disease study 2021
Authors: Zheng Long, Fenghuan Jia, Zhenghao Long
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo analyze the burden of cataract in China from 1990 to 2021.MethodUsing the data of the 2021 Global Burden of Disease (GBD 2021) study, analyzed the the prevalence and year lived with disability (YLD) of cataract.ResultsIn 2021, the number of cataract cases in China was 19,784,671(16,950,247 ~ 22,757,901), which increased by 248.08% compared to that in 1990. The YLD caused by cataract were 1,111,945(798,050 ~ 1,510,433) person-year, which increased by 144.62%. The age-standardized prevalence rate showed an upward trend, but the age-standardized YLD rate showed a downward trend in China (P < 0.05). The age-standardized prevalence rate increased from 878.30/100,000 in 1990 to 989.59/100,000 in 2021, with an increase of 12.67%, and average annual percentage change (AAPC) = 0.5%, 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.2%～0.7%, t value: 3.4, and P < 0.05. And the age-standardized YLD rate decreased from 66.97/100,000 in 1990 to 55.89/100,000 in 2021, with a decrease of 16.54%, and AAPC = -0.4%, 95% CI: -0.7%～-0.2%, t value: -3.3, and P < 0.05. And the age-standardized prevalence rate and age-standardized YLD rate of cataract in females were higher than those in males. With the increase of age, the prevalence rate and YLD rate of cataract in both males and females increased. Compared with 1990, the total number of prevalence due to cataract in China in 2021 increased by 248.08%, of which the increase in prevalence rate accounted for 44.56%, population growth accounted for 20.93%, and population aging accounted for 182.59%.ConclusionFrom 1990 to 2021, the number of prevalence and YLD for cataract increased by year, the age-standardized prevalence rate showed an upward trend, and the age-standardized YLD rate showed a downward trend in China. The burden of cataract was higher in females than that in males. With the increase of age, the burden of cataract was gradually increasing.Keywords: Cataract, Prevalence, Year lived with disability, Burden of disease, Trend

Introduction

Cataract is defined an ophthalmic condition characterised by a loss of visual quality due to changes in the transparency and color of lens. If left untreated, it can eventually develop into severe visual impairment or even blindness [1]. Cataract is one of the main causes of blindness, accounting for nearly 50% of blindness cases in low-income countries [2, 3]. There are at least 1 billion people currently suffer from preventable moderate to severe distance vision impairment or blindness, including 65.2 million with cataract [4]. In 1999, the world health organization (WHO) and the International Agency for the Prevention of Blindness (IAPB) launched “Vision 2020: global initiative for the elimination of avoidable blindness the right to sight”, focusing on avoidable blindness, including cataract [5]. However, challenges persist, including inadequate funding, limited access to quality eye care services in rural areas, and the rising incidence of avoidable blindness due to non-communicable diseases such as diabetes and glaucoma. Addressing these challenges will be crucial in achieving the vision’s ultimate goal of eliminating avoidable blindness.

China is the first country in the Western Pacific region to implement this initiative. Notably, China has the largest number of blind people in the world, with approximately 6.7 million people are classified as blind, or about 18% of the world’s blind population at the time [6]. A study showed that the number and rate of prevalence and disability adjusted of life years (DALY) rate for cataract increased significantly in China from 1990 to 2019. In addition, the age-standardized prevalence and DALY rates of cataract in China were higher than those in Russia, South Korea, North Korea, Singapore, and Japan [7]. This trend highlights the urgent need for continued efforts in cataract management and prevention, especially in regions where the burden of cataract remains disproportionately high. Although cataract can significantly improve vision through surgery, the growing and ageing global population has increased the demand for cataract surgery, leading to a rise in associated healthcare expenditure. These rising costs will add to the strain on global healthcare systems and impact socioeconomics, and urgent measures are needed to address this challenge. In China, and the 14th Five-Year Plan for National Eye Health predicted that Cataract surgery rate will reach 3500 by the end of 2025. Cataracts still remained the main cause of blindness and visual impairment for people aged 50 and older worldwide, especially in developing countries [8].

Understanding the nationwide disease burden of cataract can help to effectively control and prevent the disease and promote the future health of all people. Therefore, this study utilizes data related to the 2021 Global Burden of Disease database (GBD 2021), which is designed to provide an exhaustive analysis of the burden and trends of cataract from 1990 to 2021, in order to provide a basis for the development of targeted prevention and treatment strategies for of cataract. This comprehensive study not only highlights the historical context of cataract prevalence, but also identifies key areas that require attention in order to strengthen overall public health initiatives aimed at alleviating this widespread problem.

Methods

Data sources

Data for this study were obtained from the GBD 2021, which provides comprehensive data on 371 diseases and injuries across 204 countries from 1990 to 2021. Detailed methodologies, including the data sources, disease classification, and statistical models, are documented in previous publications [9, 10]. GBD 2021 Data in China mainly come from the Death Cause Reporting System, Disease Surveillance System, Cancer Registration Data, Nutrition and Health Survey of the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention, representative data in published studies and government reports [9, 10]. The GBD 2021 data are publicly accessible via the Global Health Data Exchange website (ghdx.healthdata.org and ghdx.healthdata.org/gbd-2021).

Definitions

The 9th and 10th International Classification of Diseases (ICD-9 and ICD-10) were used to define cataract, 366–366.9 (ICD-9), and H25-H26.9, H28-H28.8 (ICD-10).

Measurements

GBD 2021 utilises DisMod-MR2.1 to estimate the disease burden of non-fatal diseases [9, 10]. Prevalence and YLD were used to estimate and compare the trends in the burden of cataract from 1990 to 2021. The burden of cataract was analysed across various age groups, as follows: under 5, 5–9, 10–14, 15–19, 20–24, 25–29, 30–34, 35–39, 40–44, 45–49, 50–54, 55–59, 60–64, 65–69, 70–74, 75–79, 80–84, 85–89, 90–95, and ≥ 95 years.

Statistical analysis

To account for the varying population structures across different periods and regions, age-standardized rates (ASRs) of cataract and their changes over the study period were calculated using a global age structure as the reference point [9, 10]. The 95% uncertainty interval (UI) is a standard indicator used in the GBD, which is calculated by taking 1000 draws from the posterior distribution in the modelling process to address the possible heterogeneity from both sampling error and non-sampling variance [9, 10]. All reported measures included point estimates and the corresponding 95% UIs. The percentage change in rates was calculated as the difference between the rates in 2021 and 1990 divided by the rate in 1990, representing the direction and magnitude of change over the past 32 years. Temporal trends in the cataract ASRs from 1990 to 2021 were analysed using the annual percentage change (APC) and average annual percentage change (AAPC). Then using the method developed by Das Gupta [11], we decomposed variation in numbers of prevalence of cataract from 1990 to 2021, using three explanatory components: change in the growth of the total population; shifts in the population structure by age and changes in the cataract rates of prevalence.

Analyses were conducted using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Inc., Cary, NC), R version 4.0.4 (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing), and the Joinpoint Regression Program version 4.8.0.

Results

Current status of the burden of cataract in China in 2021

In 2021, the number of cataract case in China was 19,784,671(16,950,247 ~ 22,757,901), including 7,949,200(6,796,812 ~ 9,265,885) males and 11,835,471(10,175,027 ~ 13,543,542) females, which increased by 248.08% compared to that in 1990. The YLD caused by cataract were 1,111,945(798,050 ~ 1,510,433) person-year, including 434,718(308,202 ~ 596,131) person-year for males and 677,227(488,451 ~ 914,381) person-year for females, compared with 1990, it increased by 144.62% (Table 1).

Changes in the age-standardized prevalence rate and YLD rate of cataract by gender from 1990 to 2021

As show in Fig. 1, the age-standardized prevalence rate showed an upward trend, but the age-standardized YLD rate showed a downward trend in China (P < 0.05). The age-standardized prevalence rate increased from 878.30/100,000 in 1990 to 989.59/100,000 in 2021, with a increase of 12.67%, and AAPC = 0.5%, 95% CI: 0.2%～0.7%, T value: 3.4, and P < 0.05. The age-standardized YLD rate decreased from 66.97/100,000 in 1990 to 55.89/100,000 in 2021, with a decrease of 16.54%, and AAPC = −0.4%, 95% CI: −0.7%～−0.2%, T value: −3.3, and P < 0.05. During the 32-year period from 1990 to 2021, the age-standardized prevalence rate and age-standardized YLD rate of cataract in females were higher than those in males. The AAPC for the age-standardized prevalence rate of cataract in males was 0.4%, while that in females was 0.6%. Similarly, the AAPC for the age-standardized YLD rate in males was − 0.5%, while that in females was − 0.4%. The obvious growing trend of age-standardized prevalence rate and age-standardized YLD rate from 1995 to 2000, with APC of age-standardized prevalence rate and age-standardized YLD rate were 5.5% (4.6%~6.5%) and 6.3% (4.9%~7.7%), respectively (Table 2).

Burden of cataract in different gender and age groups in 2021

In 2021, the highest number of cataract occurred in male aged 70–74 years, with 1,489,358 cases. The highest number of cases occurred in females aged 70–74 years, with 2,144,261 cases. With the increase of age, the prevalence rate of cataract in both males and females increased (Fig. 2). For YLD, the number of YLD in male aged 70–74 years was the highest, with 76,904 person-year, and the number of female suffering from this disease was the highest in the age group of 70–74 years, with 116,523 person-year. With the increase of age, both males and females YLD rates of cataract increased (Fig. 2).

Discussion

From 1990 to 2021, the age-standardized prevalence rate showed an upward trend, but the age-standardized YLD rate showed a downward trend in China. The burden of cataract was higher in females than that in males. With the increase of age, the burden of cataract was gradually increasing.

The significant increase in the number and age-standardized prevalence rate of cataract in China from 1990 to 2021 reflected a major public health challenge, which might be related to multiple factors. Firstly, the increasing aging of the population directly contributed to a rise in age-related cataract. The WHO, had highlighted cataract as one of vision impairment worldwide, particularly affecting the elderly, and its incidence significantly increased with age [12]. In addition, lifestyle changes, environmental pollution, and the prevalence of chronic diseases such as diabetes might also contribute to the development of cataract [13–15], for example, air pollution, smoking, high fasting plasma glucose, and high body mass index have been associated with cataract. Hypertension, obesity, myopia, diabetes mellitus and environmental pollution are risk factors for cataracts, which are now common in China and might be responsible for the increased prevalence of cataract [15]. Additionally, advances in medical technology and improved diagnostic rates may also contribute to the increase in the number of prevalence cases.

Furthermore, our study also found that a decrease in the age-standardized YLD rate during this period, which might be related to improvements in diagnostic techniques, the sinking of quality healthcare resources, improvements in surgical techniques as well as efficacy, and changes in healthcare utilization. With the continuous improvement of public health policies and the optimal allocation of medical resources in China, early screening, diagnosis, and treatment of cataract have been strengthened, effectively reducing the disability rate and disease burden of cataract [16]. Since its implementation in China in 2009, the “Project to Restore Sight to One Million Poor Patients with Cataracts” has enabled more impoverished cataract patients to undergo sight-restoring surgeries and alleviated their financial burden of seeking medical treatment. The cataract surgery rate has increased from 8.3 per 100,000 in 1988 to 220.5 per 100,000 in 2017 [17]. However, this rate still lags significantly behind that of developed countries and is even far lower than that of India, a developing country with a similarly large population [18]. In addition, as a common blinding eye disease, cataract is less difficult to be diagnosed, and with the development of digital technology, the effectiveness of AI-assisted diagnosis and referral of cataract has been confirmed by a number of studies, and this will provide technical support for early detection and diagnosis of cataract [19, 20], which also might be related to increase in the age-standardized prevalence and decrease in the age-standardized YLD rate.

Consistent with pervious study [14], this study also observed that females had significantly higher burden of cataracts than males, which might be related to the various physiological characteristics inherent and unique social roles of females. Estrogen was a protective factor that helps maintain lens clarity [21]; however, the incidence of cataracts was higher in postmenopausal women than in men of the same age due to the lack of protective effect of estrogen and the withdrawal effect of oestrogen [22]. Cataract was more likely to cause other diseases in females than in males, for example, cataract had been identified as a risk factor for depression and anxiety in females with diabetes, whereas this correlation didn’t exist in male cataract patients [23]. In China, especially in rural areas, women were more inclined to do housework, and burning biomass used for cooking had been proven to be associated with cataract formation [24]. In addition, the accumulation of harmful pollutants in poorly ventilated kitchen tracts might further exacerbate the risk of cataracts [25]. It was also worth noting that the healthcare system reflected gender differences, with women typically waiting longer for cataract surgery compared to men [26]. Such delaied in treatment could have a significant impact on the overall health of women with cataracts, highlighting the need for targeted interventions to address these inequalities.

The burden of cataract increased progressively with age [27], which might be related to a number of factors, including the effects of lens aging, ultraviolet (UV) radiation exposure, chronic diseases, and unhealthy lifestyle habits.The natural aging process causeed significant degenerative changes in the lens of the eye, leading to lens clouding, which constituted one of the primary causes of cataract development [28–30]. In addition, prolonged exposured to UV radiation, particularly in high-intensity environments, significantly increased the risk of cataract development [13]. Additionally, older individuals were more susceptible to chronic diseases such as diabetes and hypertension, both of which were intimately associated with the incidence of cataract. For instance, hyperglycemic states caused damage to blood vessels and nerves in eye, ultimately disrupting the normal function of the lens [31]. On the other hand, through mechanisms such as affecting ocular blood supply, increasing oxidative stress, and altering metabolic processes, may lead to clouding of the lens, which could lead to cataracts. Thus, the interplay between age-related degenerative processes, environmental exposures, and the prevalence of chronic diseases created a multifaceted risk profile for the development of cataracts in the elderly population, which underscored the importance of monitoring ocular health as we age.

Our study also had some limitations that must be acknowledged. First, the available data sources to accurately estimated nonfatal outcomes of cataract were both sparse and heterogeneous and relied primarily on small population-based surveys. Although GBD study had the advantage of using Bayesian meta-regression modelling to produced the best estimated with limited data, our current results needed to be treated with caution. Second, our study didn’t investigate risk factors associated with cataract, owing to the paucity of studies on Chinese populations and the abcence of attributable risk factors for cataract in the GBD study. Third, based on disease registration using ICD codes, the severity of cataract could not be analyzed in our study, future studies should note this. Fourth, our study was unable to discuss the difference burden of cataract between urban and rural areas. The important impact of urban-rural differences on the burden of disease is the socio-economic factor, which is a key contributor to health inequalities. Due to the lack of data on socioeconomic disparities in China’s cataract population, it is not possible to measure the disparity in cataract disease burden across socioeconomic status in this study.

Overall, this study comprehensively analyzed the changes in the burden of cataract in China from 1990 to 2021, the significant trend of increasing severity of cataracts from year to year was highlighted. The findings indicated the increasing impact of cataract on the population, highlighting the importance of recognising the impact of cataract on individuals and society as a whole. In addition, the results underscored the urgent need for targeted interventions to address the rising incidence and severity of cataracts in order to minimise the adverse impact of cataracts on public health and improve the quality of life of patients.

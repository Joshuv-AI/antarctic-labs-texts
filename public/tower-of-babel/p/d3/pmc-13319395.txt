Artificial Intelligence in Physical Therapy: A Narrative Review of Artificial Intelligence Applications Across the Patient and Client Management Model
Authors: Jie Hao, Ka‐Chun Siu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground and AimsArtificial intelligence (AI) is rapidly transforming healthcare and has increasing relevance for physical therapy practice. This review aims to examine how AI can be integrated across the continuum of care within the Physical Therapy Patient and Client Management Model.MethodsThis narrative review synthesizes current literature and emerging applications of AI in rehabilitation and discusses their relevance to the six components of the Physical Therapy Patient and Client Management Model: examination, evaluation, diagnosis, prognosis, intervention, and outcomes. Examples from recent research and clinical innovations are used to illustrate how AI tools may support physical therapy practice.ResultsAI technologies have the potential to enhance multiple stages of patient management. During examination, AI‐driven systems can automate patient intake and assist with triage by identifying clinical red and yellow flags. In evaluation and diagnosis, computer vision and wearable sensor systems enable objective movement analysis and functional assessment. For prognosis, machine learning models can support risk stratification and predict rehabilitation outcomes using multidimensional clinical data. In intervention, large language models and AI‐enabled platforms may assist clinical decision‐making and facilitate remote therapeutic monitoring. AI can also improve outcome measurement and benchmarking by analyzing large datasets and developing efficient assessment tools.ConclusionAI presents significant opportunities to enhance efficiency, personalization, and data‐informed decision‐making in physical therapy. By thoughtfully integrating AI into each phase of care, physical therapists can enhance decision‐making, improve health outcomes, and strengthen the therapeutic alliance.

Introduction

In the rise of a global aging population, increasing burden of chronic diseases, and growing demand for healthcare services, innovative solutions that improve efficiency, accessibility, and quality of care are urgently needed. Artificial intelligence (AI) has rapidly emerged as a transformative technology across various healthcare disciplines, offering advanced tools for enhancing diagnostics, optimizing treatments, and improving efficiency [1]. Within this evolving landscape, physical therapy, a field inherently reliant on movement, human interaction, and individualized interventions, stands to benefit from AI‐driven innovations across the continuum of patient management.

To leverage AI for physical therapy, it is essential to understand the core branches of AI that underpin many emerging applications in rehabilitation (Table 1). Machine learning uses algorithms to identify patterns in large datasets and make data‐driven decisions, with the capacity to improve performance through continuing learning [2]. Computer vision allows machines to process, analyze, and interpret visual information, such as images or videos. It mimics human sight by extracting meaningful features from visual inputs [3]. Natural language processing enables machines to understand, interpret, and generate language, facilitating applications such as automated clinical documentation, patient education, and virtual health assistants [4]. AI chatbots, typically powered by large language models, represent an advanced form of natural language processing that can simulate human‐like dialogue and deliver tailored responses based on clinical or educational content. As physical therapists confront growing demands for value‐based care, AI presents an opportunity to elevate human expertise and support clinical decision‐making grounded in evidence‐based algorithms.

Overview of AI types and their applications in physical therapy.

Prior literature has provided valuable insights for understanding how different forms of AI can enhance physical therapy. For example, Tack et al. [5] outlined key applications of supervised and unsupervised machine learning in diagnostic imaging, patient measurement data, and decision support within musculoskeletal care. Several studies [6, 7, 8] have also explored the opportunities and concerns surrounding AI chatbots in musculoskeletal physical therapy, highlighting ongoing controversies in this field. However, existing research often examines these technologies in isolation or within narrow clinical contexts, with limited effort to synthesize how diverse AI modalities may collectively influence the full continuum of physical therapy practice.

To provide a broader clinically integrated overview, relevant English‐language literature was surveyed from January to February 2026 using databases including PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science with combinations of search terms such as “artificial intelligence,” “physical therapy,” “physiotherapy,” and “rehabilitation.” Priority was given to recent studies, foundational reports, and representative articles with direct relevance to rehabilitation practice. The identified literature was then narratively synthesized through the framework of the Physical Therapy Patient and Client Management Model [9], encompassing examination, evaluation, diagnosis, prognosis, intervention, and outcomes, with additional discussion dedicated to current opportunities, workforce implications, nonclinical applications, implementation barriers, and ethical considerations. By examining current applications and future directions, we aim to highlight the critical intersection of AI and physical therapy in the era of digital health and summarize the integration of AI into the Physical Therapy Patient and Client Management Model (Figure 1).

Examination

Patient Intake Automation

AI can be utilized to streamline the patient intake process in physical therapy, enhancing efficiency and accuracy. AI‐powered virtual assistants can automate the collection and organization of essential health data before appointments, allowing patients to share relevant symptoms in advance [10]. This ensures clinicians receive accurate, comprehensive information that enhances care efficiency. Patients can interact with these systems during waiting periods before appointments to report their symptoms, functional limitations, and psychosocial concerns. This information can then be synthesized and transferred directly into the physical therapist's clinical documentation system, allowing the therapist to begin the encounter with a well‐informed understanding of the patient's condition.

Triage and Referral

AI systems can assist in the initial triage process by analyzing patient‐reported data and electronic health records to support early identification of clinical concerns and appropriate referral pathways. In physical therapy, red flags refer to signs and symptoms that may indicate serious medical pathology requiring physician attention, and yellow flags represent psychosocial factors such as fear‐avoidance beliefs, depression, anxiety, or catastrophizing that may influence recovery [11]. The Optimal Screening for Prediction of Referral and Outcome tools have been developed by the American Physical Therapy Association (APTA) to efficiently screen for these flags using validated questionnaires and review‐of‐systems [12]. Integrating AI with such instruments could enhance early detection by automating flag identification, scoring risk profiles, and providing clinical decision support.

Natural language processing and predictive modeling can further personalize triage by synthesizing narrative inputs and highlighting relevant concerns, ultimately improving clinical efficiency and patient safety. An AI‐driven framework has been found to automatically detect red flags in screening for rare diseases in electronic health records across multiple disciplines [13]. ChatGPT and Google Bard demonstrated satisfactory accuracy, completeness, and relevance when identifying red flags of low back pain [14]. Similarly, AI demonstrated significant agreement with physical therapists in identifying contraindications to exercise therapy [15]. Collectively, these innovations highlight the growing potential of AI to support timely, informed referral decisions and enhance safety and quality in the early phases of physical therapy. Nevertheless, the reliability of these AI‐generated recommendations remains dependent on prompt quality and completeness and specificity of the clinical information entered into the system [16], underscoring the continued need for careful clinician interpretation and oversight.

Evaluation and Diagnosis

Movement Analysis

AI‐powered movement analysis tools, often utilizing computer vision and machine learning, can detect and quantify joint kinematics, gait deviations, and postural abnormalities. These systems extract features from video or sensor data to provide objective insights into range of motion, velocity, and asymmetry, helping clinicians in movement tracking, recognition, and classification. A commercialized AI mobile application has been found to demonstrate satisfactory ability to identify proper squat form [17]. Video‐based gait analysis employing pose estimation techniques can accurately capture step length, step timing, and hip and knee joint kinematics during overground and treadmill walking in individuals poststroke [18]. This approach offers a low‐cost alternative for conducting instrumented gait analysis, making it accessible to clinicians who lack access to specialized gait laboratories. In pediatric populations, an AI algorithm using pose estimation and action recognition models has shown high accuracy (91%) in classifying 31 clinically relevant infant movements in full‐term and preterm infants, supporting its potential utility in developmental assessment and early intervention contexts [19].

Functional Assessment

Functional assessment is a cornerstone of physical therapy because it provides essential information about a patient's ability to perform meaningful tasks and activities in daily life. Recent work has shown that AI‐enabled systems integrating inertial sensors and computer vision can autonomously assess functional mobility, including sit‐to‐stand, standing balance, and walking, with high accuracy [20]. A Geriatric Functional Assessment system using a wearable device and machine learning methods demonstrated promising utility in capturing older adults’ performance in functional activities at home, providing insights into mobility patterns and potential risks such as functional decline [21].

By combining data from AI‐powered movement analysis and functional assessments with findings from the clinical encounter, physical therapists can more effectively identify movement impairments, functional deficits, and activity limitations. These insights support the formulation of a precise physical therapy diagnosis. Furthermore, AI tools enable clinicians to access performance data beyond the confines of the clinic, offering a comprehensive understanding of how patients function in their natural environments and supporting holistic care.

Prognosis

Risk Stratification

AI can support prognosis by identifying patients at heightened risk for complications, adverse outcomes, or delayed recovery, enabling more targeted and proactive interventions. Machine learning can predict the likelihood of hospital readmissions, informing discharge planning and transitional care strategies to reduce the risk of readmissions [22]. Machine learning modeling with large data sets can also provide insight into optimal postacute care destinations (home health, skilled nursing facility, and inpatient rehabilitation facility), supporting discharge decisions that align with patient needs, functional status, and risk profiles [23]. Moreover, AI has shown promise in fall risk evaluation by utilizing accelerometers, medical records, camera registration, and fall diary [24]. In home‐based rehabilitation settings, AI systems may also be integrated with remote alert mechanisms to notify clinicians or emergency contacts when abrupt fall events or high‐risk mobility deviations are detected, thereby facilitating timely safety responses [25]. By integrating such AI models into physical therapy, clinicians can implement more proactive, data‐informed strategies to enhance safety, optimize resource allocation, and improve long‐term outcomes.

Prediction of Clinical Outcomes

AI predictive models can analyze complex, multidimensional data to forecast clinical outcomes related to function and recovery. Based on datasets of patient‐reported and performance‐based outcome measures, machine learning can predict rehabilitation success for patients with hip, knee, or foot injuries [26]. In neurological populations, machine learning has demonstrated promising utility in prognosis for patients poststroke [27], brain injury [28], and spinal cord injury [29]. While these tools offer valuable data‐informed projections, optimal goal setting and care planning require thoughtful integration of algorithmic predictions with therapist expertise and patient expectations. By balancing these elements, clinicians can provide customized care that aligns with functional goals and enhances overall treatment effectiveness.

Intervention

Clinical Decision‐Making

Large language models such as ChatGPT can provide evidence‐based clinical decision support, enhancing therapist decision‐making by integrating clinical practice guidelines and current research. Recent studies have demonstrated promising but incomplete alignment between large language model‐generated rehabilitation recommendations and expert clinician consensus. For example, ChatGPT has shown satisfactory concordance with musculoskeletal clinical practice guideline recommendations [30], yet lower consistency and reduced reliability when addressing nuanced rehabilitation interventions requiring therapeutic reasoning [31]. Similarly, a study found that although large language models can generate broadly appropriate personalized rehabilitation plans for knee osteoarthritis, notable deficiencies persist in exercise specificity, dosage prescription, and progression criteria [32]. Such recommendations are inherently derived from the prior knowledge base on which the AI system is trained or programmed; therefore, clinicians must critically vet AI‐suggested interventions against current evidence standards and individual patient presentation before implementation. Furthermore, current large language models still face limitations in complex clinical reasoning, underscoring the need for clinician oversight and ongoing model refinement [33, 34]. Looking forward, AI companions have the potential to deliver real‐time, context‐specific recommendations at the point of care; however, their clinical value will remain contingent upon thoughtful prompt design, robust clinical input, and professional verification.

Remote Therapeutic Monitoring

Remote therapeutic monitoring leverages digital health technologies to monitor patient progress, adherence, and outcomes beyond the clinic, offering a power extension of physical therapy [35]. AI enhances remote therapeutic monitoring by analyzing data from wearable sensors, mobile apps, and patient‐reported outcomes asynchronously, enabling early identification of deviations from expected recovery trajectories. AI‐supported remote therapeutic monitoring enables therapists to stay connected with patients between visits, foster communication, and promote shared decision‐making [36]. By delivering timely feedback and facilitating ongoing engagement, this approach strengthens the therapeutic alliance and enhances the patient‐centered model of care. However, potential risks associated with AI‐driven patient communication exist in the area of patient education, as one study reported that ChatGPT performed poorly in internal consistency and accuracy of information related to lumbosacral radicular pain [7]. Cautious implementation and clinician oversight would be paramount to prevent misinformation and preserve trust in AI‐supported care.

Outcomes

Outcome Measurement and Benchmarking

Throughout the entire episode of patient management, the physical therapist determines the anticipated goals and expected outcomes for each intervention. Standardized outcome measures provide objective and reliable data to assess function, track progress, and guide an individualized plan of care. Machine learning can enhance the clinical utility of outcome measurement by developing abbreviated versions of widely used instruments, such as the Berg Balance Scale [37], Stroke Impact Scale [38], and Fugl‐Meyer Assessment [39]. These shortened versions reduce the time and effort required for administration, alleviating the burden for both clinicians and patients without compromising reliability or validity. This approach improves clinical efficiency and supports the integration of outcome measures into practice. Additionally, the aggregation of outcome data in large clinical databases enables benchmarking against national averages and peer institutions. Such comparisons provide meaningful insights into treatment effectiveness and offer valuable metrics for payers evaluating the quality and value of care.

Current Opportunities and Challenges

Workforce Implications

The integration of AI into physical therapy presents significant implications for the rehabilitation workforce. On one hand, AI‐powered documentation systems may improve workflow efficiency and reduce administration burden [40], thereby mitigating factors that contribute to clinician burnout. Technologies such as remote therapeutic monitoring can foster patient‐centered care, reinforcing patient engagement and compliance. On the other hand, adopting new technologies may introduce training demands to clinicians; concerns regarding liability can heighten stress, particularly in the absence of clear regulatory guidance. Compensation structures may be affected as questions around reimbursement eligibility may emerge. Moreover, patient perceptions and trust in AI‐driven care may vary, particularly among older adults who may face greater technological barriers [41]. Financial cost, implementation logistics, and overall technology acceptance across diverse practice settings must also be considered when evaluating the long‐term feasibility of AI‐enhanced physical therapy.

Emerging Technologies

Emerging technologies such as robotics, telehealth, and virtual/augmented reality are increasingly shaping the future of rehabilitation and are highly relevant to the evolving scope of practice. These technologies serve as platforms for AI integration, enabling adaptive and data‐driven rehabilitation experiences. Robotics offers the potential to enhance intensity, precision, and adaptability of movement training, particularly in neurorehabilitation [42]. Telehealth platforms have expanded access to physical therapy services, enabling remote assessments, progress tracking, and patient education [43]. Immersive technologies such as virtual and augmented reality can improve patient engagement, simulate real‐world tasks, and provide controlled environments for therapeutic activities [44]. In addition, real‐time feedback systems embedded in orthotics [45] and prosthetics [46] can facilitate immediate adjustments during therapy, enhancing motor learning and functional outcomes. As these technologies mature, their thoughtful integration into clinical workflows will be essential to ensure both safety and efficacy.

Home‐Based Rehabilitation and Coaching

The increasing emphasis on home‐based care highlights the need for AI‐supported coaching strategies to maintain patient engagement and adherence beyond the hospital and clinic. AI can facilitate personalized home exercise programs by tracking progress via mobile applications and wearable sensors. Virtual health assistants or chatbot‐based platforms can provide reminders, motivational prompts, and educational content to reinforce therapy goals and foster patient self‐management [47]. Importantly, these innovations support continuity of care across inpatient, outpatient, and home settings, bridging gaps in service delivery and promoting more coordinated rehabilitation pathways. Nevertheless, the suitability of AI‐supported home rehabilitation may vary considerably across patient populations. Individuals with significant cognitive impairment, limited technological literacy, or inadequate caregiver support may encounter difficulty engaging effectively with AI‐guided home exercise platforms. Careful patient selection and individualized adaptation, therefore, remain essential to ensure that AI‐assisted home rehabilitation enhances therapeutic benefit.

Professional Education

At the entry‐level professional stage, physical therapy students should be introduced to foundational AI literacy, including understanding the capabilities and limitations of AI systems, appropriate prompt guidance, appraisal of AI‐generated contents, and ethical considerations surrounding data privacy and clinical accountability. Recent evidence suggests that structured exposure to AI‐supported clinical case applications can improve Doctor of Physical Therapy students' understanding of the practical utility of AI [48]. Integrating these topics into entry‐level physical therapy curricula may help prepare future clinicians to engage critically with AI‐assisted care rather than relying on such tools uncritically. In parallel, continuing education will be necessary to support competency in selecting validated AI tools, interpreting algorithm‐supported outputs, and integrating AI into clinical workflows while maintaining independent clinical reasoning and patient‐centered judgment.

Nonclinical Applications

AI tools may streamline practice management through automated scheduling, billing, and resource allocation systems, improving administrative efficiency. In business operations, predictive analytics can support strategic planning by forecasting patient demand and optimizing service delivery. For risk mitigation, AI can flag potential malpractice risks through anomaly detection in clinical documentation or treatment patterns. Regulatory compliance efforts may also benefit from AI‐driven audits that monitor adherence to clinical guidelines and accreditation standards (e.g., Joint Commission, Commission on Accreditation of Rehabilitation Facilities). In research and consultation, AI can automate literature reviews and facilitate evidence synthesis through data‐informed recommendations [49].

Interdisciplinary Collaboration

The successful integration of AI into physical therapy relies heavily on interdisciplinary collaboration. Beyond traditional collaboration with healthcare professionals such as physicians, nurses, and occupational therapists, AI integration necessitates active engagement with experts in biomedical engineering, data science, and human‐computer interaction. These partnerships are essential for the design of clinically relevant algorithms, development of user‐friendly interfaces, and validation of AI tools in real‐world settings. Physical therapists must therefore develop competencies in interdisciplinary communication and participate in collaborative ecosystems that bridge clinical expertise with technical innovation.

Current Challenges

Despite its promise, AI adoption in physical therapy faces some challenges. Algorithmic bias is a key concern, as systems trained on nonrepresentative data can reinforce healthcare disparities. Ensuring equitable, unbiased performance requires ongoing evaluation, diverse data inclusion, and transparency in model development. Another issue is interpretability. Many AI models operate as “black boxes,” making it difficult for clinicians to understand how outputs are generated. Explainable AI is essential to build trust and facilitate informed clinical use. Data privacy and cybersecurity are also critical concerns. Given the sensitivity of health data, strong encryption protocols, secure data handling practices, and regulatory compliance must be established to protect patient information and prevent breaches.

Patient Safety and Implementation Barriers

AI‐supported screening systems may fail to identify critical red flags when incomplete information is entered, and patient‐facing chatbots or automated coaching platforms may occasionally generate inaccurate advice if used without clinician verification. In addition, wearable sensors and remote monitoring technologies are subject to technical limitations, which may affect the reliability of algorithm‐driven recommendations. Practical implementation barriers may also exist, including increased workflow burden during technology onboarding, challenges with electronic medical record integration, and uncertainty regarding reimbursement pathways. Furthermore, many AI systems are trained on datasets that may not adequately represent the diversity of physical therapy populations, potentially limiting generalizability. These concerns underscore the importance of careful validation, clinician oversight, and staged clinical integration before widespread adoption of AI‐supported rehabilitation tools.

Ethical Considerations

Ethical considerations further underscore the need to preserve patient autonomy and promote informed consent. Patients should be aware when AI tools are employed in their care and should retain the right to decline or question algorithm‐driven decisions. AI should enhance, not replace, the human elements of clinical care, such as empathy, therapeutic communication, and shared decision‐making. Moreover, the integration of AI necessitates new training for clinicians to interpret AI outputs responsibly and to understand the capabilities and limitations of these tools. Clear guidelines and regulatory frameworks are also needed to assign accountability and define standards for AI use in rehabilitation settings.

Limitations of the Current Review

Several limitations of this narrative review should be acknowledged. First, although broad literature searches were conducted across multiple databases, the evidence synthesis was intended to provide a representative clinical overview rather than an exhaustive systematic appraisal. Second, the field of AI in rehabilitation is evolving rapidly, such that current conclusions may require continual updating as new models, validation studies, and clinical implementation reports become available. Finally, the current review draws substantially from literature conducted in musculoskeletal rehabilitation contexts, reflecting the present concentration of published evidence in this area. As a result, the applicability of some discussions may be less established for other populations, including neurologic, cardiopulmonary, pediatric, and cognitively complex patient groups.

Conclusion

AI is poised to profoundly transform physical therapy by enhancing each phase of the patient management model, from examination to intervention and outcome assessment. Through machine learning, computer vision, and natural language processing, AI offers powerful tools to automate administrative tasks, improve decision‐making, and personalize care delivery. AI also enhances the ability of physical therapists to collect, interpret, and act on diverse forms of data, supporting more advanced and comprehensive patient management.

The successful integration of AI into physical therapy depends on thoughtful implementation, clinical oversight, and alignment with the therapist's expertise and patient goals. As data science and digital health continue to evolve, physical therapists must play an active role in guiding the ethical and effective use of AI. By embracing innovation while upholding the altruistic foundation of care, physical therapy is well‐positioned to lead in a future where intelligent systems complement, rather than replace, clinical judgment and therapeutic alliance.

A potential role of the circ_UBR4/miR-10a-5p/KLF4 axis in regulating vascular smooth muscle cell phenotypic transformation : implications for cardiac allograft vasculopathy
Authors: Haoqiu Zhong, Zhanyu Xu, Mingyao Zhou, Jieyao Yuan, Jun Peng, Mai Chen, Guoxing Ling, Yibo Yan, Binfeng Lei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCardiac Allograft Vasculopathy (CAV) remains a predominant challenge in post-transplant cardiac care. The phenotypic transformation of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) is central to its progression, contributing to neointimal hyperplasia and long-term graft failure. Recent studies have highlighted the involvement of non-coding RNAs as essential modulators in VSMC regulation, opening new avenues for therapeutic exploration. In this study, we aimed to explore the role of the circ_UBR4/miR-10a-5p/KLF4 regulatory axis in VSMC phenotypic switching. Human aortic smooth muscle cells (HASMCs) underwent co-transfection with constructed plasmids of both wild-type and mutated sequences for circ_UBR4 and KLF4, along with respective miR-10a-5p mimics or controls. Functional and mechanistic assays, including dual-luciferase reporter analysis, flow cytometry, Transwell migration and invasion assays, qRT-PCR, Western blotting, and immunofluorescence, were utilized to investigate interactions and downstream effects. Our results revealed that circ_UBR4 directly interacts with miR-10a-5p, and its suppression led to attenuated VSMC proliferation and migration. This inhibitory effect was reversed by concurrent miR-10a-5p downregulation, suggesting a functional antagonism. Further analyses demonstrated that miR-10a-5p directly targets KLF4, and its overexpression resulted in decreased proliferative capacity and reduced expression of synthetic and proliferative markers (e.g., OPN, MMP-2, PCNA, and ki-67). These effects were counteracted by KLF4 overexpression. In parallel, contractile marker SM-22α showed opposing trends, further confirming phenotypic transition. Together, these findings suggest that the circ_UBR4/miR-10a-5p/KLF4 axis may play a critical role in regulating VSMC phenotypic plasticity. Introduction

Allogeneic heart transplantation is the preferred treatment for end-stage heart failure patients. However, a major postoperative complication that impacts long-term patient survival is Cardiac Allograft Vasculopathy(CAV) [1]. CAV affects approximately 30–50% of heart transplant recipients within 5–10 years post-operation, and is the leading cause of graft failure beyond the first year. Its presence is associated with a significant reduction in long-term survival, with five-year survival dropping by up to 32% in affected individuals [2]. The pathogenesis of CAV is not yet fully understood, and there remains no effective method for its prevention and treatment. A central pathological feature of CAV is the phenotypic transformation, abnormal proliferation, and migration of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) [3]. Under normal physiological conditions, VSMCs predominantly exhibit a contractile phenotype characterized by low proliferation, low migration, and limited extracellular matrix secretion, primarily expressing markers like Alpha Smooth Muscle Actin (α-SMA), Smooth Muscle Myosin Heavy Chain (SM-MHC), Smooth Muscle 22α (SM-22α), and calponin [3, 4]. However, when vascular injury occurs, VSMCs undergo a phenotypic switch from this contractile state to a synthetic one, marked by decreased contractility and increased proliferation, migration, and matrix secretion, majorly expressing proteins like Osteopontin (OPN) and Epiregulin (ERG) [5]. This phenotypic switch is often triggered by inflammatory cell infiltration, particularly macrophages, after vascular injury. It exacerbates endothelial dysfunction and promotes persistent inflammatory responses. These changes collectively contribute to vascular wall thickening, lumen narrowing, and progressive vascular remodeling. Eventually, these structural alterations impair tissue perfusion and may lead to organ dysfunction or even complete graft failure [6].

The Kruppel-like factor (KLF) family, transcription factors characterized by their zinc finger domains, plays crucial roles in various biological processes including cell proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, and embryonic development [7]. Previous studies have highlighted KLF4’s role in modulating VSMCs’ contractile marker genes [8]. Furthermore, extensive research indicates that several factors can regulate VSMCs phenotypic transformation via KLF4, elucidating the significance of investigating regulators of KLF4 in understanding CAV pathogenesis [9, 10].

Recently, the role of non-coding RNAs as key signaling molecules in the onset and progression of various diseases has emerged as a research hotspot. miRNA (miR)-10a, a member of the miR-10 family, has been implicated in the proliferation and differentiation of various cell types. Low levels of miRNA-10a in cardiovascular endothelia and serum have been associated with cardiovascular diseases like Coronary Artery Disease (CAD) [11]. miR-10a has been identified as a potential biomarker for heart failure prognosis and is known to regulate KLF4 expression, further underscoring its importance in cardiovascular diseases [12]. In addition, miR-10a has been shown to play a key role in regulating the differentiation of human mesenchymal stem cells (hMSCs) by repressing KLF4, with upregulation of miR-10a restoring differentiation potential in aged hMSCs [13]. Moreover, miR-10a has been specifically associated with Cardiac Allograft Vasculopathy (CAV) and has been identified as a promising non-invasive biomarker for heart transplant rejection, further emphasizing its relevance in cardiovascular pathology [14]. Circ_UBR4 (hsa_circ_0010283), a recently identified circular RNA derived from exons 45–66 of UBR4 located on chromosome 1, is found to be aberrantly overexpressed in oxidized low-density lipoprotein (ox-LDL) induced human VSMCs [15]. This overexpression, along with other related findings, underscores the pivotal role of circ_UBR4 in regulating VSMC function, largely achieved through its sponging action on miRNAs. These molecular-level changes not only drive structural alterations in the vasculature but also directly impact transplant longevity. Studies have reported that patients with early evidence of VSMC phenotypic switching have a higher risk of long-term graft dysfunction [16].

Although the individual roles of circ_UBR4, miR-10a-5p, and KLF4 in various cardiovascular processes have been studied, the specific interaction between these three entities in regulating VSMC phenotypic transformation has not been fully defined. Based on our preliminary findings related to the regulatory roles of circ_UBR4 and KLF4 in VSMCs, combined with the known mechanisms of miR-10a-5p in cardiovascular diseases, we hypothesize that circ_UBR4 may modulate KLF4 expression by sponging miR-10a-5p, promoting the phenotypic shift of VSMCs from contractile to synthetic. This study aims to delve deeper into the circ_UBR4/miR-10a-5p/KLF4-mediated mechanism behind VSMC phenotypic transformation, which may offer further insights into CAV’s pathogenesis and lay a theoretical foundation for potential diagnostic and therapeutic strategies.

Materials and methods

Cell lines

HASMCs (Human Aortic Smooth Muscle Cells) was purchased from the Type Culture Collection (Shanghai, China) at the Chinese Academy of Sciences. HASMCs were cultured in Smooth Muscle Cell Medium (Gibco). The medium was supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS). HASMCs were maintained in a humidified incubator with 5% CO₂, 100 units/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin.

Cell transfection and vector construction

HASMCs were seeded in 6-well plates at a density of 2 × 10⁵ cells per well and cultured overnight to reach 70–80% confluence. Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) was used for all transfections according to the manufacturer’s protocol. For each well of a 6-well plate, 2 µg of total plasmid DNA or 100 nM of oligonucleotides (miR-10a-5p mimic, miR-10a-5p inhibitor, and their respective negative controls) was used. The following plasmids and oligonucleotides were obtained from GenePharma (Shanghai, China): sh-circ-UBR4 (targeting the back-splice junction of circ_UBR4), miR-10a-5p inhibitor, miR-10a-5p mimic, and the OE-KLF4 plasmid (overexpression construct for KLF4). The circ-UBR4 overexpression plasmid was constructed by cloning the circ-UBR4 sequence into the plenti-ciR-GFP-T2A vector (Cat# VB010101-9262, VectorBuilder, Guangzhou, China). The shRNA sequences targeting circ-UBR4 were cloned into the pGPU6/GFP/puromycin vector (Cat# VG10001, GenePharma, Shanghai, China). For co-transfection experiments, cells were transfected with a combination of plasmids and/or oligonucleotides as indicated in the experimental groups. After 48 h of transfection, cells were harvested for subsequent assays (qRT-PCR, Western blot, flow cytometry, etc.).

RNA extraction and qRT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted from the cultured HASMCs using TRIzol kit (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The RT-PCR primers were obtained from Cellcook (Guangzhou, China) and are listed in Supplementary Table S1. QRT-PCR was conducted on the ABI7300 system (Applied Biosystems) using SYBR green kit (TaKaRa, Dalian, China) as per the manufacturer’s instructions. U6 or β-actin were used as endogenous controls. Comparative Ct method (2-ΔΔCt) was used to measure relative gene expression levels.

Western blotting

Total proteins were extracted using RIPA buffer (Geneseed Biotech, China) and quantified by BCA assay. Equal amounts (30 µg) were separated via SDS-PAGE (5% stacking, 10% resolving) and transferred to PVDF membranes (Millipore). After blocking with 5% milk in TBST, membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies (Abcam): KLF4 (1:1000, equivalent to 1 µg/mL, ab215036, Abcam), SM-22α(1:1000, equivalent to 1 µg/mL, ab170902, Abcam), OPN (1:2000, equivalent to 0.5 µg/mL, ab166709, Abcam), MMP-2 (1:1000, equivalent to 1 µg/mL, ab92536, Abcam), PCNA (1:1000, equivalent to 1 µg/mL, ab92552, Abcam), Ki-67 (1:5000, equivalent to 0.2 µg/mL, ab92742, Abcam), and Lamin B1 (1:2000, equivalent to 0.5 µg/mL, ab16048, Abcam). After washing, HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (goat anti-rabbit or anti-mouse, 1:5000, equivalent to 0.2 µg/mL, ab6721, Abcam) were applied for 1 h at room temperature. Bands were visualized using ECL substrate (Thermo Fisher) and imaged with a Bio-Rad ChemiDoc system. Lamin B1 or PCNA served as loading controls, and untreated cells were used as negative controls. The antibodies are listed in Supplementary Table S2.

Flow cytometry analysis of cell cycle

HASMCs from different groups (Control: which represents untreated cells, Model: which represents cells subjected to the experimental conditions without transfection, OE-KLF4 NC + miR-10a-5p mimic NC, OE-KLF4 NC + miR-10a-5p mimic, and OE-KLF4 + miR-10a-5p mimic) were harvested and fixed with 70% ethanol overnight at 4℃. After fixation, cells were washed with cold phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and then stained with propidium iodide (PI) solution containing RNase A for 30 min in the dark at 37℃. The stained cells were subsequently analyzed for DNA content using a flow cytometer. The percentages of cells in G0/G1, S, and G2/M phases were calculated and compared among the groups to evaluate alterations in the cell cycle distribution.

Transwell migration and invasion assay

Corning Transwell® chambers were used for the invasion assay. HASMCs were seeded on the chambers placed into 24-well plates and cultured for 48 h. The cells present on the lower surface of the chambers were fixed with 4% formaldehyde, stained with 0.25% crystal violet, and counted. The in vitro migration of these cells was evaluated by the wound-healing assay. Briefly, HASMCs were seeded into the 6-well plates with 1 × 106 cells per well (Corning) and cultured till 80%–90% confluence. A longitudinal scratch was made using a sterile pipette, and images were taken at 0, 24 and 48 h to monitor wound coverage. For the migration assay, 1 × 10⁵ HASMCs in serum-free medium were seeded into the upper chamber; the lower chamber contained medium with 10% FBS as a chemoattractant. After 24 h incubation, non-migrated cells on the upper surface were removed, and migrated cells on the lower surface were fixed, stained with 0.25% crystal violet. Images of five randomly selected fields per chamber were captured under a light microscope at 100× magnification. The number of migrated cells was quantified using ImageJ software (Version 1.53k, National Institutes of Health, USA).

Dual luciferase reporter assay

For validation of the interaction between circ_UBR4 and miR-10a-5p, the wild-type (WT) sequence of circ_UBR4 containing the predicted miR-10a-5p binding site and a mutant (MUT) sequence with substitutions in the seed-binding region were cloned into the pmirGLO vector (Promega, USA). Luciferase activity was quantified 48 h later. Similarly, to verify the direct binding of miR-10a-5p to KLF4. The luciferase activities of MUT and KLF4 3′-UTR WT were similarly quantified.

Cell counting kit-8 proliferation assay

HASMCs were seeded into 96-well plates at the density of 3 × 103/ml, and treated with the different compounds for 48 h. The serum was replaced with fresh serum-free medium containing 10µL CCK-8 assay, and the cells were incubated further for 4 h. The optical density at 450 nm was measured using a Multiskan FC (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA).

Immunofluorescence assay

HASMCs were seeded onto coverslips in a 24-well plate and allowed to attach overnight. After treatments, cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min and permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 in PBS for 10 min. Following a 1-hour block with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA), the cells were incubated with primary antibodies against OPN and SM-22α overnight at 4 °C. After primary antibody incubation, cells were washed with PBS and incubated in the dark with fluorescently labeled secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. The secondary antibody used was Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG H&L (Alexa Fluor® 488, 1:1000, equivalent to 1 µg/mL, ab150077, Abcam), incubated at 37 °C for 1 h. The nuclei were stained with DAPI for 5 min. Coverslips were then mounted onto slides using an anti-fade mounting medium. Fluorescent images were captured using a Olympus BX-53(Japan) fluorescence microscope equipped with a 200× objective, and the intensity and localization of the target proteins were analyzed.

Statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism 8.0 and SPSS 22.0 software were used for data analysis. Data are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation. For comparisons between two groups, an unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-test was used. For comparisons involving more than two groups, one-way ANOVA (or Kruskal-Wallis test for non-parametric data) with Tukey’s multiple comparison test was performed. Differences were considered statistically significant at p < 0.05. The bars represent the means, and the whiskers represent the standard deviations of the data. Each experimental group consisted of 3 biological replicates. All experiments were performed with three independent biological replicates (n = 3 per group), where each biological replicate represents HASMCs from a different passage or a separate culture.

Results

Circ_UBR4 directly binds to miR-10a-5p

To elucidate whether circ_UBR4 modulates vascular smooth muscle cell (VSMC) phenotypic switching through miR-10a-5p. Plasmids containing the wild-type sequence (circ_UBR4 WT) and a mutated binding site sequence (circ_UBR4 MUT) were constructed. These plasmids were co-transfected into HASMCs alongside miR-10a-5p mimic or miR-10a-5p-NC for a dual-luciferase reporter gene assay (Fig. 1A). The results demonstrated that overexpression of miR-10a-5p significantly attenuated the luciferase activity of the circ_UBR4 WT vector. However, it did not affect the luciferase activity of either the empty vector or the circ_UBR4 MUT vector (Fig. 1B). This demonstrates a direct interaction between circ_UBR4 and miR-10a-5p. These findings suggest that circ_UBR4 may function as a miRNA sponge to regulate downstream gene expression and influence VSMC phenotype.

circ_UBR4 sponges miR-10a-5p in HASMCs. (A) Schematic of WT and Mut circ_UBR4 binding sites for miR-10a-5p. (B) Relative luciferase activity after co‑transfection with WT/Mut‑circ_UBR4 and miR‑10a‑5p mimic or NC. (C‑E) Transwell invasion (C), migration (D) and wound healing (E) assays of HASMCs in five groups (Control, Model, Vehicle, circ_UBR4 KD, Rescue). Scale bar = 100 μm. (F) Cell cycle distribution by flow cytometry. (G) Cell proliferation by CCK‑8 at 0, 24, 48 h. Data are mean ± SD (n = 3 independent experiments). One‑way ANOVA with Tukey’s test. *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001

Circ_UBR4 regulates VSMC proliferation via miR-10a-5p

Then, we conducted a gain-of-function experiment. Post-transfection, five distinct groups were established in the HASMCs: (1) control, (2) Model, (3) sh-circ_UBR4 NC + miR-10a-5p inhibitor NC, (4) sh-circ_UBR4 + miR-10a-5p inhibitor NC, and (5) sh-circ_UBR4 + miR-10a-5p inhibitor (Fig. 1C-E, Table S3). Figure 1C–E show that circ_UBR4 knockdown significantly reduced cell migration and invasion abilities compared to the model group (p < 0.01), while co-transfection with a miR-10a-5p inhibitor partially restored these capabilities (p < 0.05), indicating a functional interaction. The expressions of circ_UBR4 and miR-10a-5p were subsequently evaluated using qRT-PCR. Cell cycle analysis indicated an increased proportion of cells in the G0/G1 phase and a decreased proportion in the S phase following circ_UBR4 knockdown; these alterations were reversed by downregulating miR-10a-5p (Fig. 1F). The proliferative capacity of transfected cells at 0 h, 24 h, and 48 h was assessed via the CCK-8 assay. Our results revealed that compared to the control, circ_UBR4 knockdown diminished cell proliferation. However, this reduced proliferative vigor was rescued in cells with circ_UBR4 knockdown when miR-10a-5p was concurrently downregulated (Fig. 1G). These findings were further validated in cell invasion and migration assays. Therefore, in this in vitro model, circ_UBR4 promotes VSMC proliferation, invasion, and migration an effect that appears to be mediated by the suppression of miR-10a-5p, suggesting a role in phenotypic switching.

Circ_UBR4 influences protein expression through miR-10a-5p

Furthermore, both qRT-PCR and Western blot analyses were employed to determine the expression levels of PCNA, Ki-67, SM-22α, OPN, and MMP-2 across the groups (Fig. 2A-B). Knockdown of circ_UBR4 led to decreased expression of synthetic proteins OPN and MMP-2, as well as proliferation markers PCNA and Ki-67. Conversely, the expression of the contractile protein SM-22α was upregulated. Remarkably, the miR-10a-5p downregulation in cells with circ_UBR4 knockdown reversed these expression patterns. Simultaneously, we established HASMCs lines labeled with DAPI, SM-22α, and OPN to observe changes in cell phenotype (Fig. 3). The results are consistent with the aforementioned experiments. Therefore, our data suggest that circ_UBR4 promotes the phenotypic transition of VSMCs from a contractile to synthetic state in HASMCs, and this action is contingent upon miR-10a-5p. These data indicate that circ_UBR4 drives the synthetic phenotype in VSMCs by regulating the expression of phenotype-related proteins through miR-10a-5p.

miR-10a-5p directly targets KLF4

To investigate the potential role of miR-10a-5p in modulating the phenotypic transformation of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) via KLF4, Plasmids harboring the wild-type sequence (KLF4 WT) and a sequence with mutated binding sites (KLF4 MUT) were designed and synthesized (Fig. 4A). These were then co-transfected with miR-10a-5p mimic or miR-10a-5p-NC into HASMCs, followed by a dual-luciferase reporter gene assessment. Our findings highlighted that the upregulation of miR-10a-5p substantially reduced the luciferase activity stemming from the KLF4 WT vector. Nevertheless, no notable change was observed in the luciferase activity of either the vacant vector or the KLF4 MUT vector (Fig. 4B), underscoring a direct binding affinity between miR-10a-5p and KLF4. These findings confirm that KLF4 is a direct downstream target of miR-10a-5p in VSMCs.

miR-10a-5p modulates VSMC proliferation through KLF4

Then, we conducted a gain-of-function study. Subsequent to transfection, five distinct groups were established in the HASMCs: (1) control, (2) Model, (3) OE-KLF4 NC + miR-10a-5p mimic NC, (4) OE-KLF4 NC + miR-10a-5p mimic, and (5) OE-KLF4 + miR-10a-5p mimic (Fig. 4C-E). The post-transfection expression levels of miR-10a-5p and KLF4 were quantified using qRT-PCR. Cell cycle analyses further indicated that cells overexpressing miR-10a-5p exhibited an elevated proportion in the G0/G1 phase and a decline in the S phase; these changes were reversed with KLF4 overexpression (Fig. 4F). Cell proliferation at 0 h, 24 h, and 48 h post-transfection was assessed through the CCK-8 assay. Our data revealed a decline in cellular proliferative capacity upon overexpression of miR-10a-5p compared to the control group. Interestingly, the diminished proliferation linked to miR-10a-5p overexpression was rescued by concurrent overexpression of KLF4 (Fig. 4G). The aforementioned findings were corroborated by cell invasion and migration assays. Thus, miR-10a-5p suppresses VSMC proliferation and migration by targeting KLF4, implicating this axis in phenotypic regulation.

miR-10a-5p influences protein expression via KLF4

Using both qRT-PCR and Western blot, we analyzed the expression of PCNA, Ki-67, SM-22α, OPN, and MMP-2 across the different groups (Fig. 5A-B). Elevated miR-10a-5p levels led to a reduction in the expression of synthetic proteins OPN and MMP-2, as well as proliferation markers PCNA and Ki-67, while upregulating the contractile protein SM-22α. Notably, in cells overexpressing miR-10a-5p, KLF4 overexpression could reverse these expression patterns. We concurrently developed DAPI-stained HASMCs lines with SM-22α and OPN markers to monitor alterations in their phenotypic characteristics. These findings align with those from previous experiments (Fig. 6). In conclusion, our findings suggest that miR-10a-5p’s role in driving the phenotypic shift of VSMCs from a contractile to synthetic state in HASMCs is mediated by KLF4. Collectively, these data further support that the miR-10a-5p/KLF4 axis regulates the phenotypic switching of VSMCs from a synthetic to a contractile state.

Discussion

With the deepening of circRNA research, their pivotal roles in a variety of biological processes have become evident. Initial observations noted that circRNAs translocate to the cytoplasm, where they execute diverse functions [17]. Thanks to advancements in bioinformatics, our understanding of circRNAs has expanded. In fact, circRNAs exert their biological effects in various ways, such as acting as RNA sponges, binding with RBPs, or serving as encoding sequences. The role of circRNAs as RNA sponges has particularly garnered attention, though many circRNAs have limited miRNA binding sites [18]. While the RNA sponge function of circRNAs is frequently discussed, it should be noted that many circRNAs have limited miRNA binding capacity [19]. Our work, by focusing on VSMCs and their phenotypic plasticity, fills this research gap, adding a new dimension to the narrative of CAV progression. The role of circRNA in vascular biology remains a relatively uncharted territory, and our identification of the circ_UBR4/miR-10a-5p/KLF4 axis represents a significant step forward in understanding VSMC biology and may open new avenues for research into CAV. This not only underscores the importance of non-coding RNAs in vascular pathophysiology but also highlights a molecular axis that could be explored for potential therapeutic interventions.

Several circRNAs have been implicated in the regulation of VSMC phenotypic remodeling, potentially contributing to cardiovascular disease development. For instance, the suppression of circ-DHCR24 can inhibit arterial smooth muscle cell proliferation and migration through the miR-149-5p/MMP-9 signaling pathway, subsequently preventing vascular injury and restenosis post-PCI [20]. circMAP3K5 acts as a ceRNA, serving as a sponge for miR-22-3p, and modulating coronary artery smooth muscle cell differentiation and intimal hyperplasia [21]. Circ_Lrp6 is abundant in vascular smooth muscle cells, where it modulates miRNA-145 function as a sponge, impacting miRNA-145 target genes like ITGβ8, FASCIN, and KLF4. These genes are involved in VSMC migration, proliferation, differentiation, and neointimal formation in mouse carotid arteries [22]. Recent studies, including the work by Feng et al. have highlighted the critical role of circ_UBR4 in regulating vascular smooth muscle cell (VSMC) functions in atherosclerosis. Their research demonstrated that circ_UBR4 influences VSMC proliferation, migration, inflammation, and apoptosis by sponging miR-515-5p, which targets IGF2. These studies suggest a possible association between circRNAs and various cardiovascular diseases [23]. Furthermore, many miRNAs, such as miR-145 and miR-22, function as signaling molecules participating in VSMC phenotypic transitions, highlighting the importance of exploring miRNAs that regulate VSMCs for a comprehensive understanding of CAV pathophysiology [22, 24].

Consistent with the aforementioned findings, our results on circ_UBR4 and miR-10a-5p suggest that circ_UBR4 can directly bind with miR-10a-5p, exhibiting its RNA sponge effect. This aligns with the previously held view that circRBPMS can regulate protein expression by binding to specific miRNA targets. Our findings suggest that circ_UBR4 may play a key role in VSMC phenotype modulation. The downregulation of miR-10a-5p seems to reverse the effects caused by circ_UBR4 knockout. This further emphasizes the central role of miR-10a-5p in VSMC phenotypic transition, especially in the regulation of KLF4.

The identification of the circ_UBR4/miR-10a-5p/KLF4 regulatory axis provides a novel insight that may contribute to a better understanding of CAV mechanisms. However, given the in vitro nature of this study, further in vivo and clinical validation is required to determine its relevance to CAV pathogenesis. Exploring the therapeutic potential of targeting this axis warrants future research. While our findings highlight the potential therapeutic relevance of the circ_UBR4/miR-10a-5p/KLF4 axis, specific strategies for clinical intervention remain to be explored. Future research could investigate targeted delivery of antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) to inhibit circ_UBR4 or develop miRNA mimics/inhibitors to modulate miR-10a-5p levels. Additionally, the feasibility of these strategies should be validated in preclinical in vivo models before translation into therapeutic applications.

Moreover, given the multifaceted roles of circ_UBR4, miR-10a-5p, and KLF4 in various cellular contexts, potential off-target effects must be carefully evaluated before therapeutic application. These molecules may be involved in other essential physiological processes, and their manipulation could lead to unintended consequences. Targeted modulation of circ_UBR4 or miR-10a-5p may offer therapeutic potential in regulating VSMC phenotype transition, but further in vivo and clinical validation is required to assess its feasibility in CAV treatment.

We acknowledge several additional limitations in this study. First, although our immunofluorescence results are consistent with the quantitative PCR and Western blot data, and the primary antibodies (anti-SM-22α and anti-OPN from Abcam) are well-validated and widely used, we did not include isotype control staining. Isotype controls are the gold standard for confirming antibody specificity and excluding non‑specific binding; their absence represents a limitation of our immunofluorescence data. Future studies will incorporate appropriate isotype controls to provide definitive validation. Second, our findings are primarily derived from in vitro HASMC cultures. While this system allows detailed mechanistic dissection, it does not fully recapitulate the complex cellular and biomechanical environment of CAV in vivo. Therefore, further validation in animal models of CAV and human graft samples is essential to confirm the pathophysiological relevance of the circ_UBR4/miR-10a-5p/KLF4 axis. Addressing these limitations in future work will strengthen the translational potential of our findings.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our research offers a novel research perspective and a theoretical foundation for exploring molecular mechanisms that may underlie CAV. The circ_UBR4/miR-10a-5p/KLF4 regulatory axis appears to play an important role in VSMC regulation, which may present new directions for subsequent research into potential treatments. These findings may pave the way for novel therapeutic options for CAV and strengthen the theoretical foundation for its etiology and pathophysiology. Thus, the study significantly contributes to the field by proposing a mechanistically well-supported pathway and offering novel therapeutic angles in CAV.

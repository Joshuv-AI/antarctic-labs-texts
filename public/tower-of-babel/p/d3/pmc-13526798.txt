Clinical significance of serum levels of 14-3-3β protein in patients with non-small cell lung cancer
Authors: Decai Wang, Yalan Cui, Hong Xu, Shi Chen, Jinnan Zhong, Min Liu, Xiaoyan Gao, Xiaomin Zhang, Xiaoying Li, Jiaqi Wang, Ting Zhang, Fajiu Li, Chenghong Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractLung cancer diagnosis still relies on imaging and tissue sampling, which can be invasive and costly. Moreover, anatomic staging alone does not fully capture biological heterogeneity. Therefore, minimally invasive blood biomarkers are needed to support diagnosis and risk stratification. We integrated public transcriptomic data analyses with an independent serum-based enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) validation cohort. Using the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) dataset GSE40275, we compared RNA expression of tyrosine 3‑monooxygenase/tryptophan 5‑monooxygenase activation protein β (14‑3‑3β, encoded by the YWHAB gene) between 43 healthy control subjects and patients with distinct lung cancer pathological subtypes, including 11 cases of lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) and 5 cases of lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC).Two additional GEO datasets, GSE29013 (30 LUAD and 25 LUSC patients) and GSE43580 (77 LUAD and 73 LUSC patients), were utilized to validate the differential 14-3-3β RNA expression between LUAD and LUSC subtypes. In the GSE29013 cohort, subgroup analyses were performed stratified by tumor stage, disease progression status, and clinical outcomes; Kaplan–Meier Plotter was applied to assess the associations of 14-3-3β expression with overall survival (OS) and post-progression survival (PPS). For serum-level validation, peripheral blood samples from 15 LUAD patients, 21 LUSC patients, and 32 healthy controls were examined via ELISA, with receiver operating characteristic curve analysis conducted to evaluate its diagnostic performance via calculation of the area under the curve (AUC). 14-3-3β RNA expression was higher in LUAD and LUSC than in controls (both p < 0.0001) and higher in LUSC than in LUAD (P = 0.005), which was confirmed in GSE29013 (P = 0.0140) and GSE43580 (P = 0.0222). In GSE29013, higher 14-3-3β expression was associated with tumor progression (P = 0.0440) and death (P = 0.0091), and predicted shorter OS (HR = 1.21; P = 0.0043) and PPS (HR = 1.55; P = 9.7e-05). Serum 14-3-3β levels were increased in LUAD and LUSC, yielding an AUC of 0.93 with 80.6% sensitivity and 100% specificity at a cutoff of 43.04 ng/mL. Across tissue transcriptomes and serum validation, 14-3-3β is associated with lung cancer presence, histological subtype, and survival outcomes, supporting its potential as a minimally invasive adjunct biomarker for diagnosis and prognostic assessment.Keywords: Non-small cell lung cancer, Biomarker, Tyrosine 3 monooxygenase/tryptophan 5 monooxygenase activation protein βSubject terms: Biomarkers, Cancer, Oncology

Introduction

Lung cancer remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality, and patient outcomes depend critically on timely diagnosis and accurate risk stratification.1 Current guideline-recommended diagnostic pathways rely on imaging followed by pathological confirmation.1,2 In routine practice, obtaining tissue typically requires bronchoscopy, transthoracic biopsy, or surgery-procedures that are invasive, resource-intensive, and not always feasible.3 Re-biopsy may also be necessary when initial samples are inadequate or when molecular profiling is required.4

Prognostic assessment has similar constraints. Although tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) staging remains the cornerstone, contemporary staging frameworks acknowledge substantial outcome variability within the same stage due to biological heterogeneity and differences in treatment response.5 Accordingly, there is growing interest in integrating non-anatomic biomarkers into clinical decision-making, especially circulating markers that can be measured repeatedly over time.6–9 Blood-based biomarkers are appealing because they are minimally invasive and relatively low cost, and they can support diagnosis, triage of pulmonary nodules, monitoring, and risk stratification.10–14 Among them, protein biomarkers measured by immunoassays are particularly practical and scalable, and have been actively investigated in suspected lung cancer referrals and indeterminate pulmonary nodules (IPNs).11,13,14.

The 14-3-3 protein family is constituted by 28–33 kDa acidic proteins that are ubiquitous in eukaryotes and regulate diverse intracellular processes, including protein synthesis, cellular metabolism, protein trafficking, signal transduction, and cytoskeletal transport.15 In mammalian cells, seven isoforms have been described (α/β, ε, η, γ, σ, θ/τ, and δ/ζ), with α and δ representing phosphorylated forms of β and ζ, respectively.16 Recent reviews indicate that 14-3-3 proteins contribute to cancer-related phenotypes and may serve as biomarkers or therapeutic targets.17–19 For instance, Wang et al. verified that both mRNA and protein levels of 14-3-3σ were markedly higher in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma tissues than in normal pancreatic tissues, and elevated 14-3-3σ expression was correlated with advanced pathological TNM stage and higher pathological grade. Patients with high 14-3-3σ expression exhibited significantly shorter overall survival, disease-specific survival, and progression-free survival, identifying it as an independent adverse prognostic biomarker.20 More importantly, Wei et al. reported that mRNA levels of 14-3-3ζ were significantly upregulated in non-small-cell lung cancer tumor tissues relative to adjacent normal tissues, with consistent overexpression observed in both lung adenocarcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma subtypes. Patients with high 14-3-3ζ expression had substantially shortened progression-free survival, overall survival, and post-progression survival, further supporting its role as an independent poor prognostic marker.21 However, the clinical significance of 14-3-3β in lung cancer-particularly its circulating serum levels and potential utility for diagnosis and prognostic assessment-remains largely underexplored.

In this study, we integrated GEO transcriptome datasets with a serum ELISA cohort to determine whether 14-3-3β is differentially expressed across lung cancer histologies, associated with progression and clinical outcomes, and detectable in peripheral blood with clinically meaningful diagnostic performance. By aligning our analyses with guideline-based pathology and staging principles, as well as established biomarker-development considerations, we aim to provide evidence supporting serum 14-3-3β as a practical adjunct to current diagnostic and prognostic workflows.

Results

Baseline characteristics of study cohorts and validation of 14-3-3β RNA expression differences in lung cancer histology

In the GSE40275 dataset, there were no statistically significant differences in age and sex among patient subgroups (Table 1). Compared with 43 healthy control subjects, the RNA expression level of 14-3-3β was significantly upregulated in LUAD (n = 11; p < 0.0001) and LUSC (n = 5; p < 0.0001). Moreover, 14-3-3β expression was markedly higher in LUSC tissues than in LUAD tissues (P = 0.0050; Fig. 1A). For clinical covariates, no significant differences in age and sex were observed in the GSE29013 dataset (Table 2); in the GSE43580 dataset, age was comparable between subgroups, whereas sex distribution showed a statistically significant difference (P = 0.0003) (Table 3). This histology-dependent expression pattern was consistently validated in two independent cohorts: GSE29013 (LUAD, n = 30; LUSC, n = 25; P = 0.0140; Fig. 1B) and GSE43580 (LUAD, n = 77; LUSC, n = 73; P = 0.0222; Fig. 1C), confirming the robustness and reproducibility of the tissue-specific expression signature of 14-3-3β.

Subgroup analyses and multivariate regression of 14-3-3β RNA expression in GSE29013 dataset

In the GSE29013 cohort, subgroup analyses revealed that 14-3-3β RNA expression tended to rise with advancing tumor stage (Stage I, n = 24; Stage II, n = 14; Stage III, n = 17), although this trend was not statistically significant (Fig. 2A , P= 0.1100). Notably, 14-3-3β transcript levels were significantly higher in patients with tumor progression (n = 28) than in those without progression (n = 27) (Fig. 2B , P= 0.0440). Moreover, 14-3-3β expression was markedly elevated in deceased patients (n = 18) relative to surviving individuals (n = 37) (Fig. 2C , P= 0.0091). Collectively, these results suggest that increased 14-3-3β RNA expression correlates with more aggressive disease behavior. Multivariate linear regression analysis identified male sex as an independent negative predictor of 14-3-3β RNA expression (β = − 0.3742, P = 0.0258). No independent associations were detected between 14-3-3β RNA expression and histology, tumor stage, progression, death event, or age (all p > 0.05) (Table 4).

Kaplan–Meier analyses link high 14-3-3β RNA to poorer OS and PPS

Kaplan–Meier analyses demonstrated that patients with high 14-3-3β expression had significantly shorter overall survival (OS) than those with low expression (log-rank P = 0.0043), with an increased risk of death (HR = 1.21, 95% CI 1.06–1.38) (Fig. 3A). High 14-3-3β expression was also associated with markedly shorter post-progression survival (PPS) (HR = 1.55, 95% CI 1.24–1.94; log-rank P = 9.7e−05) (Fig. 3B), highlighting potential value for outcome stratification beyond diagnosis.

Clinical characteristics and clinical indicators for the serum validation cohort

As summarized in Table 5, a total of 68 participants were included: 32 HCs and 36 patients with lung cancer (LUCA). No significant between-group differences were observed in sex distribution, age, height, weight, or smoking history categories. Within the LUCA cohort, histological subtypes comprised 21 (58%) LUAD and 15 (42%) LUSC. Tissue confirmation was most commonly obtained via bronchoscopic biopsy (23/36, 64%), followed by surgical biopsy (7/36, 19%), needle biopsy (2/36, 6%), and other approaches (4/36, 11%).

Serum ELISA validates elevated circulating 14-3-3β and demonstrates strong diagnostic performance

Consistent with the transcriptomic findings, serum 14-3-3β levels were significantly higher in LUCA (66.78 [45.79–95.65] ng/mL) than in controls (25.70 [18.45–34.67] ng/mL; p < 0.0001) (Fig. 4A). Serum levels were also higher in LUSC (90.50 [77.35–118.30] ng/mL) than in LUAD (49.86 [34.23–67.31] ng/mL; P = 0.0445) (Fig. 4B), mirroring the histology trend observed in tissue datasets. ROC analysis showed that a cutoff of 43.04 ng/mL yielded 80.6% sensitivity and 100% specificity, with an AUC of 0.93 (95% CI 0.86–0.99; p < 0.0001) (Fig. 5), supporting the clinical potential of serum 14-3-3β as a minimally invasive adjunct for lung cancer detection and risk assessment.

Discussion

Our study integrates multi-cohort transcriptomic analyses with an independent serum validation cohort and provides convergent evidence supporting the clinical relevance of 14-3-3β in lung cancer. Across independent GEO datasets, 14-3-3β RNA expression was consistently elevated in lung cancer tissues, was higher in LUSC than in LUAD, and was associated with tumor progression and survival outcomes. Importantly, serum ELISA mirrored the tissue-level findings and showed strong diagnostic performance (AUC 0.93), supporting the translational potential of circulating 14-3-3β.

From a clinical perspective, these findings address an important unmet need. Guideline-concordant diagnosis of NSCLC relies on pathological confirmation, staging, and molecular characterization.1 However, tissue acquisition is often invasive, and imaging frequently identifies indeterminate pulmonary nodules that lead to repeated surveillance and, in many cases, invasive procedures. Reviews and meta-analyses of biomarkers for indeterminate pulmonary nodules suggest that adjunct blood tests may reduce unnecessary invasive procedures when incorporated into clinical-radiologic risk models.10,11,22 Although ctDNA has become increasingly valuable for genotyping when tissue is insufficient and is being actively evaluated for minimal residual disease and recurrence monitoring,23,24 protein biomarkers remain especially attractive in triage settings because they are rapid, relatively inexpensive, and readily implementable in routine laboratories.25,26 In this context, the high specificity and clinically meaningful sensitivity observed for serum 14-3-3β suggest a practical role as a rule-in adjunct in patients with suspicious imaging findings, particularly when biopsy is delayed, contraindicated, or expected to yield limited tissue.

Biologically, the present findings are also plausible. The 14-3-3 protein family comprises signaling adaptors involved in cell-cycle regulation, apoptosis, and cellular stress responses,27 and recent reviews have highlighted their broad involvement in oncogenic networks as well as their potential as biomarkers and therapeutic targets.17 Our results extend these observations to lung cancer and suggest that 14-3-3β may have both diagnostic and prognostic relevance. The reproducible pattern of higher expression in LUSC than in LUAD, seen in both tissue datasets and serum, further suggests subtype-associated biology. Histological classification remains fundamentally morphology-based under WHO guidance, with immunohistochemistry and molecular testing used for support when needed.28,29 A blood biomarker cannot replace pathology; however, a reproducible histology-associated signal may still be clinically useful for pre-biopsy risk characterization and may also provide a rationale for future mechanistic studies.

The association between high 14-3-3β expression and shorter OS and PPS suggests that 14-3-3β may reflect more aggressive tumor biology and/or poorer outcomes after progression. This observation is clinically relevant because modern staging frameworks increasingly recognize that anatomic descriptors alone do not fully explain prognosis.30–32 There is growing interest in integrating biomarkers into risk assessment, and blood-based markers are already being studied for prognostic enrichment and longitudinal monitoring in NSCLC, including ctDNA-based MRD strategies.24,33,34 In that setting, a circulating marker associated with post-progression outcomes could potentially help identify patients who may benefit from closer surveillance, earlier supportive care integration, or prioritization for clinical trial enrollment.

The study has several strengths. First, the findings were supported across multiple public transcriptomic cohorts and an independent serum cohort, increasing confidence in the consistency of the signal. Second, the direction of association was concordant across tissue and serum. Third, we report clinically interpretable diagnostic metrics, including AUC, sensitivity, and specificity, which facilitate assessment of potential real-world utility.

Several limitations should also be acknowledged. First, the serum-based cohort had a relatively small sample size, which may compromise statistical precision and the generalizability of our findings. Second, survival analyses were performed using publicly available platform datasets without adjustment via patient-level multivariate models, which may introduce potential confounding effects. Third, our multivariate linear regression analysis of 14-3-3β RNA expression was exclusively conducted within the single public GSE29013 cohort with limited case numbers, which weakens the external validity of the independent negative association between male sex and 14-3-3β RNA expression. No significant independent correlations were identified between 14-3-3β RNA expression and tumor stage, disease progression, death events, or age, potentially owing to insufficient statistical power. Collectively, future investigations are warranted to address these shortcomings, including multicenter external validation, evaluation of the incremental diagnostic value of 14-3-3β relative to conventional tumor markers (e.g., CEA, CYFRA21-1, NSE) and novel protein- or extracellular vesicle-based biomarkers, and validation of pre-specified cut-off values to minimize optimism bias.

Conclusion

In summary, our results indicate that 14-3-3β is associated with lung cancer presence, histological subtype, and adverse outcomes at both the tissue and serum levels. These findings support serum 14-3-3β as a promising minimally invasive adjunct biomarker for diagnosis and risk assessment in lung cancer, while also providing a rationale for larger prospective studies aimed at defining its clinical utility.

Methods

Ethics statement

The serum study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Second People’s Hospital of China Three Gorges University (202,412). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. This study did not involve any form of therapeutic intervention. We confirm that all methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations.

Study design and participants

This study comprised two parts: a public transcriptome analysis using Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets and an independent serum validation cohort. For the serum cohort, participants were consecutively enrolled from the Department of Laboratory Medicine, Second People’s Hospital of China Three Gorges University, between January 2025 and June 2025.

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria: Lung cancer group (LUAD/LUSC): (1) Age ≥ 18 years; (2) newly diagnosed, treatment-naïve lung cancer; (3) diagnosis confirmed by histopathology/cytopathology from biopsy or surgical specimens according to contemporary NSCLC diagnostic workflows;1,2 (4) histological subtype classified as LUAD or LUSC following the WHO classification framework, morphology-first with immunohistochemistry and molecular testing when needed.35 Control group: (1) Age ≥ 18 years; (2) healthy physical-examination attendees without clinical evidence of cancer.

Exclusion criteria: prior history of any malignancy; prior anti-tumor therapy before blood sampling; active infection within 2 weeks; severe hepatic/renal failure; pregnancy or lactation; inadequate clinical data or sample quality (e.g., hemolysis, insufficient volume, repeated freeze–thaw).

GEO/R bioinformatics analysis

Gene expression profiles and corresponding clinical annotations were downloaded from GEO, with GSE40275 used as the discovery cohort and GSE29013 and GSE43580 used as validation cohorts. Data retrieval was performed using the GEO query R package. For each dataset, expression matrices were processed by probe-to-gene mapping, with representative probes selected according to the highest average expression. Data were then normalized as appropriate for the platform (for example, quantile normalization for microarray data) and log2-transformed when required. Expression values for 14-3-3β were subsequently extracted for group comparisons. Expression data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Differential expression analyses were performed using the limma package with empirical Bayes moderation. Student’s t-test or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used for simple two-group or multiple-group comparisons, respectively. The Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) method was applied to correct P-values for multiple testing where appropriate.

Kaplan–Meier Plotter survival analysis

The association between 14-3-3β expression and survival outcomes was assessed using the Kaplan–Meier Plotter online platform (lung cancer module). After entering the gene symbol YWHAB, overall survival (OS) and post-progression survival (PPS) were selected as endpoints. Patients were dichotomized into high- and low-expression groups using a predefined cutoff, commonly the median, as the primary analysis. Sensitivity analyses may additionally consider the “auto select best cutoff” option, while acknowledging the potential for optimism bias. Kaplan–Meier survival curves were generated by the platform.

Serum collection, processing, and ELISA measurement

Peripheral venous blood samples were collected from patients with LUAD, patients with LUSC, and healthy controls before any anti-tumor treatment. To minimize pre-analytical variability, blood collection was performed in a standardized manner, preferentially in the morning and under fasting conditions when feasible. Samples were allowed to clot at room temperature and were then centrifuged to isolate serum. Serum aliquots were prepared to avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles and stored at − 80°C until analysis, consistent with standard practice for serum biomarker studies.

Serum 14-3-3β concentrations were measured using a commercially available ELISA kit (CUSABIO Life Science Ltd., Wuhan, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. All samples and standards were assayed in duplicate. Concentrations were calculated from the standard curve, and assay performance was monitored using quality controls.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.5.2) and/or GraphPad Prism (version 8.0.2). Data normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for normally distributed data, or as median with 25th–75th percentiles (interquartile range [IQR]) for non-normally distributed data.

For group comparisons, normally distributed variables were analyzed using Student’s t test for two-group comparisons or one-way ANOVA for comparisons among three or more groups, followed by appropriate post hoc correction when applicable. Non-normally distributed variables were analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U test for two-group comparisons or the Kruskal–Wallis test for comparisons among three or more groups. Kaplan–Meier curves were compared using the log-rank test, and hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported as provided by the Kaplan–Meier Plotter output. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to evaluate the diagnostic performance of serum 14-3-3β. A two-sided p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all analyses.

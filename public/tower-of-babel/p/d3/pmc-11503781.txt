Regiodivergent Radical-Relay Alkene Dicarbofunctionalization
Authors: Zhong Liu, Francesco D’Amico, Ruben Martin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Herein, we report a regiodivergent 1,n-dicarbofunctionalization of unactivated olefins enabled by a Ni-catalyzed radical relay that forges both C(sp3)-C(sp3) and C(sp2)–C(sp3) linkages, even at long-range. Initial studies support an intertwined scenario resulting from the merger of an atom-transfer radical addition (ATRA) and a chain-walking event, with site-selectivity being dictated by a judicious choice of the ligand backbone.

Unactivated olefins rank among the most widely available feedstocks in organic synthesis.1 While (anti)-Markovnikov olefin hydrofunctionalization has evolved as a mature tool in synthetic endeavors,2 it was only recently that extensions of this chemistry to 1,2-difunctionalizations have been possible (Scheme 1, path a).3 Site-selectivity in the latter can be accomplished by a judicious choice of the coupling partners in one- or two-electron domains4 or by the inclusion of directing groups in the vicinity.5 Although charge relocation techniques,6 among others,7 have offered elegant alternatives for 1,3-difunctionalization (path b), the incorporation of two different carbon fragments at longer range via 1,n-difunctionalization of simple, unactivated olefins remains an elusive, yet worthwhile, endeavor for chemical invention.8,9

Proximal and Remote Olefin 1,n-Functionalization

Recently, Ni-catalyzed chain-walking reactions have offered counterintuitive alternatives for enabling bond-formations at remote C(sp3)–H sites of unsaturated hydrocarbon side-chains.9,10 A close inspection into the literature data indicates that these techniques remain predominantly confined to 1,n-hydrofunctionalization (path c).10 Despite the significant advances realized, particularly with boron or silyl-type mediators,11 a site-selective olefin 1,n-dicarbofunctionalization through radical relay processes remains an unexplored endeavor in chain-walking reactions (path d).9−11 Conceptuality aside, the design of such a platform would avoid resource intensive, conventional multistep sequences for incorporating several carbon architectures into alkyl side-chains, thus accelerating structure–activity relationship explorations in medicinal chemistry campaigns.12

Driven by the potential of atom-transfer radical additions to functionalize unsaturated bonds (ATRA)13 and the ease at which Ni catalysts trigger a dynamic translocation throughout the alkyl side-chain,9,10,14 we hypothesized that the merger of these two disciplines might offer an unrecognized opportunity in the chain-walking arena for an intertwined radical-relay scenario that results in a site-selective 1,n-dicarbofunctionalization of unactivated olefins, even at long-range (Scheme 2). At the outset of our investigations, however, it was not clear whether such a scenario could be realized given the difficulty for controlling regioselectivity in multicomponent endeavors, and/or the proclivity for parasitic olefin isomerization,15 protodemetalation16 or homocoupling.17 Herein, we report the successful realization of this goal. The method is characterized by its broad applicability and an exquisite site-selectivity pattern that can be easily switched by a judicious choice of the ligand backbone.

Olefin Dicarbofunctionalization at Long Range

We began our study by evaluating the reactivity of 4-phenyl-1-butene (1a) with 4-bromobiphenyl (2a) and bromoacetonitrile (3a) as coupling partners. After systematic optimization of all reaction parameters (Table 1),18 a combination of Ni(BF4)2·6H2O (10 mol %), L1 (15 mol %), MgBr2 (3.0 equiv), i-PrBr (1.0 equiv) and Mn (3.5 equiv) in DMF/MeCN at rt afforded the best results, delivering 4a in 78% isolated yield with >26:1 selectivity, favoring 1,n- over the corresponding 1,2-difunctionalization. As anticipated, the nature of the ligand turned out to be critical for success in both yield and site-selectivity. Specifically, 1,10-phenanthrolines substituted with alkyl groups adjacent to the nitrogen atom and electron-poor arenes at C4 were particularly suited for our purposes (entries 1–4).19 Interestingly, 2,2-bipyridines possessing ester groups at C4 resulted in a site-selectivity switch, favoring a 1,2-difunctionalization en route to 5a (entry 6). The utilization of Mg(II) salts, solvents, nickel catalysts, or reductants other than MgBr2, DMF/MeCN, Ni(BF4)2·6H2O and Mn led to lower yields and selectivities (entries 8–12). Notably, better site-selectivities were found in the presence of i-PrBr (entry 9). Although tentative, this result might suggest that it is necessary to sustain an appropriate concentration of a nickel hydride responsible for the chain-walking scenario.20

Next, we turned our attention to studying the preparative potential of our transformation (Scheme 3). As shown, our 1,n-dicarbofunctionalization could be applied across a variety of different olefins. It is worth noting that chain-walking at longer range could also be within reach, with exclusive sp2–sp3 bond-formation occurring at the benzylic site, albeit in slightly lower yields (4b–4e). The observed site-selectivity was unambiguously determined by X-ray diffraction of 4a-CONH2 obtained by exposure of 4a to K2CO3/H2O2.18 Notably, the targeted products could be obtained independent of whether the arene terminus was decorated with electron-rich or -poor substituents (4f–4l). Note, however, that a significant erosion in site-selectivity was observed for ortho-substituted arenes (4m). This observation suggests that bond-formation at the benzylic sp3 C–H site might be compromised if steric considerations come into play. More importantly, the reaction could be executed equally well with internal olefins, delivering 4p with an excellent site-selectivity pattern.21 As shown for 4t–4ai, a variety of differently substituted aryl bromides could be employed as coupling partners. While high site-selectivities were observed in all cases, it is worth mentioning that higher yields were generally observed for electron-poor aryl bromides (4w–4x, 4ae–4af). As illustrated in the results compiled in Scheme 3, our protocol could accommodate a variety of functional groups, including esters (4x and 4ae), amines (4ac) or nitriles (4af), among others. Even aryl boron reagents (4i and 4aa) did not compete with the efficacy of the reaction; in this case, not even traces of a Suzuki-Miyaura type reaction22 arising from the coupling of the boron reagent with either 2 or 3 were observed in the crude mixtures. Although aryl chlorides might a priori be predisposed to Ni-catalyzed cross-electrophile endeavors, this turned out not to be the case (4v, 4ag),23 thus leaving ample room for further derivatization via conventional cross-couplings.24 As shown for 4aj-4ao, our protocol could be extended to alkyl bromides other than 3a, resulting in targeted products in moderate yields and good selectivities. The applicability of our reaction is further illustrated in the cross-coupling of advanced synthetic ingredients arising from Isoxepac (4q), femfibrozil (4r), Indomethacin (4s) or Probenecid (4ai).

Using L3 as ligand.

Driven by the ligand dichotomy exerted in Table 1, we next focused our attention on studying the applicability of a 1,2-dicarbofunctionalization based on a Ni/L6 regime, thus establishing the basis for a regiodivergent 1,n-dicarbofunctionalization of unactivated olefins. As shown in Scheme 4, we found that our Ni/L6 protocol could be applied to a variety of unactivated olefins (5a-5e) and (hetero)aryl bromides (5f–5i), invariably leading to the targeted products in moderate to good yields and an exquisite 1,2-selectivity pattern, thus offering a complementary platform to existing 1,2-difunctionalization scenarios. The 1,2-selectivity pattern was univocally determined by X-ray diffraction of 5a-CONH2, obtained by simple exposure of 4a to K2CO3/H2O2.18

Using L7 as ligand.

Aiming at understanding the intricacies of our 1,n-dicarbofunctionalization of unactivated olefins, we next conducted isotope-labeling studies with 1a-d2 and 1a-d4 as substrates (Scheme 5).18 The lack of deuterium labeling at both α- and β-position to the nitrile suggests that a unidirectional chain-walking comes into play (I), translocating the Ni catalyst exclusively at benzylic sp3 C–H sites probably due to electronic considerations.25,26 The involvement of an initial ATRA prior to Ni-catalyzed chain-walking was indirectly assessed by exposure of 1a to 3a in the absence of 2a, leading to the formation of 6 in 65% yield with traces of olefins being detected in the crude mixtures (II).27 The intermediacy of open-shell species was also evaluated by utilizing 7 as a substrate, resulting in the formation of 8 in 45% yield. Moreover, 6, 9 and 10 turned out to be competent as reaction intermediates, delivering the targeted product 4a with an exquisite site-selectivity (4a:5a > 20:1). As anticipated, exposure of 6 to a Ni/L6 regime afforded 5a exclusively in 46% yield arising from a 1,2-difunctionalization, thus standing as a testament to the impact that the ligand backbone has for dictating the site-selectivity pattern.28

Control Mechanistic Experiments

In summary, we have developed a Ni-catalyzed relay for the 1,n-dicarbofunctionalization of unactivated olefins at long-range with site-selectivity being dictated by the nature of the ligand utilized. Control experiments advocated an intertwined scenario consisting of an initial atom-transfer radical addition followed by a chain-walking scenario, thus establishing a new rationale for enabling the difunctionalization of olefins at long-range. Further studies of the exploitation of this mode of action are currently underway in our laboratories.

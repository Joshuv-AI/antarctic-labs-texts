Clostridium zhoujianii sp. nov., a novel gram-negative species isolated from bat feces and its genetic characteristics
Authors: Jian Zhou, Tao Gu, Jingzhu Zhou, Xiaoxiao Zeng, Ying Liu, Fengming Chen, Yong Hu, Shijun Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A strictly anaerobic, rod-shaped bacterial strain, designated LP20ᵀ, was isolated from the intestinal contents of bats collected in Qiandongnan, China. Phylogenetic analysis of the 16 S rRNA gene revealed that strain LP20ᵀ shared its highest similarity with Clostridium paraputrificum ATCC 25780ᵀ (98.10%), a value below the accepted threshold for species delineation. Whole-genome sequencing further supported its taxonomic novelty, with ANI, dDDH, and AAI values to the closest relatives all falling well below species-level cutoffs. Strain LP20ᵀ exhibited unusual Gram-stain-negative morphology, grew optimally at 30–37℃ and pH 7.0–8.0, and possessed distinctive fatty acid and polar lipid profiles, including the presence of an unidentified aminophospholipid. Phenotypic assays revealed several metabolic and enzymatic traits that differentiated LP20ᵀ from its closest phylogenetic neighbors. 16 S rRNA amplicon sequencing and metagenomic screening confirmed the presence of this species in multiple bat hosts, suggesting a wider distribution within bat gut microbiomes. Functional genomic analysis indicated enrichment of genes involved in carbohydrate metabolism and a limited antibiotic resistance profile. Based on polyphasic evidence, strain LP20ᵀ represents a novel species within the genus Clostridium, for which the name Clostridium zhoujianii sp. nov. is proposed. The type strain is LP20ᵀ (= JCM 37970ᵀ = GDMCC 1.5627ᵀ).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12866-026-04884-1.

Introduction

The genus Clostridium encompasses a diverse group of predominantly Gram-positive, anaerobic, spore-forming bacteria with considerable ecological and biomedical importance [1]. These organisms play key roles in global biogeochemical cycles, organic matter decomposition, and host gut homeostasis [2]. Recent advances in molecular taxonomy have fundamentally restructured the historical understanding of the genus Clostridium, which is now recognized as being in a state of significant taxonomic flux. This restructuring began with seminal studies, such as that by Collins et al. which demonstrated that the genus was phylogenetically heterogenous and proposed the reclassification of many species into new genera like Peptoniphilus, Finegoldia, and Sarcina [3]. Subsequent discoveries, driven by genomic analyses, have continued this trend, isolating numerous novel species from environments including sediments, engineered systems, and animal intestines [1, 4–6]. These efforts have not only refined the phylogenetic structure of the group, moving beyond the classic definition based on morphology, but have also provided new insights into functional adaptation and host-microbe coevolution [7].

Bats (Chiroptera), among the most widespread mammals, harbour distinctive gut microbiomes influenced by factors such as their flight capacity, dietary specialization, and geographic distribution [8]. In particular, the gut microbiota of bats from subtropical regions, such as the karst landscapes of Qiandongnan in Guizhou Province, China, remain underexplored despite the high microbial diversity expected in such ecologically complex areas [9, 10]. Systematic studies of clostridial diversity in these habitats are still limited [11]. This gap limits our ability to understand how host-specific factors shape microbial evolution and diversity, so this study was undertaken to systematically isolate and characterize novel species from this unique reservoir, providing fundamental taxonomic data essential for future investigations into host-microbe coevolution and the ecological drivers of microbial diversification.

Although phenotypic assays and 16 S rRNA gene sequencing remain fundamental to microbial systematics, their limitations in distinguishing closely related species are widely recognized [12]. The advent of whole-genome sequencing has greatly enhanced taxonomic resolution by enabling comparisons based on average nucleotide identity (ANI), digital DNA–DNA hybridization (dDDH), and core-genome phylogeny [13]. Tools such as GTDB-Tk further facilitate robust phylogenetic placement and taxonomic assignment within a standardized genomic framework. In addition, functional annotation of genomes using databases such as COG, KEGG, and CARD allows elucidation of metabolic features, stress responses, and potential ecological roles [14].

In this study, we isolated strain LP20ᵀ from bat intestinal contents collected in Qiandongnan. Using a polyphasic approach that included morphological, biochemical, chemotaxonomic, phylogenetic, and comprehensive genomic analyses, we demonstrate that this strain represents a novel species within the genus Clostridium. Functional genomic insights suggest adaptations consistent with a host-associated lifestyle and reflect the unique ecological context of its isolation.

Materials and methods

Ethical compliance and anesthesia method

All procedures were approved by the Ethics Committee of Guizhou Provincial Center for Disease Control and Prevention (Approval No. G2019-01) in accordance with international wildlife research standards. Bats (weighing under 200 g) captured via live traps in their natural habitat were first deeply anesthetized by intraperitoneal injection of sodium pentobarbital (50 mg/kg of a 3% solution). While under deep anesthesia, humane euthanasia was then carried out by cervical dislocation by trained personnel. This protocol adhered to the animal welfare guidelines of both the American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA) and China for laboratory animals, minimizing potential pain and distress.

Bacterial isolation and cultivation

Fresh intestinal samples from bats (Chiroptera) collected in Qiandongnan, Guizhou (560 m altitude) were immediately transferred to pre-reduced BHI broth on ice. After vortex homogenization, 100 µL aliquots were streaked onto Columbia blood agar plates (5% defibrinated sheep blood; BioMérieux) and incubated anaerobically (AnaeroPack, Mitsubishi) at 37 °C for 48 h. Pure cultures were obtained through three successive subcultures.

16 S rRNA gene sequencing and phylogenetic analysis

The 16 S rRNA gene was amplified via PCR using primers 27 F (5′-AGTTTGATCMTGGCTCAG-3′) and 1492R (5′-GGTTACCTTGTTACGACTT-3′) (Wuhan Tianyi Huiyuan Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China) [11]. The PCR reaction mixture was prepared as follows (Hangzhou Bioer Technology Co., Ltd., China): 50 µL total volume consisting of 25 µL Premix Taq, 2 µL of each primer (27 F and 1492R), 2 µL template DNA (concentration 68 ng/µL), and 19 µL water. The amplification conditions were set as follows: 94 °C for 10 min, followed by 40 cycles of 94 °C for 45 s, 52 °C for 60 s, and 72 °C for 60 s, with a final extension at 72 °C for 10 min. 5µL of PCR amplification products were subjected to electrophoresis on a 1% agarose gel, and imaging analysis was performed using the ChemiDoc MP imaging system (Bio-Rad, USA). Preliminary sequence comparisons were carried out via BLAST within the EzBioCloud database, restricting comparisons to type strains of validly published species. Trees were inferred using Neighbor-Joining (NJ) [15] and Maximum Likelihood (ML) [16] algorithms. Evolutionary analyses were performed using MEGA 11 [17].

Whole-genome sequencing and taxonomic delineation

Whole-genome sequencing (WGS) was conducted using the Illumina NovaSeq X Plus platform (Illumina Inc.). Genomic DNA was extracted with the DNeasy PowerSoil Pro Kit (Qiagen) after enzymatic lysis employing 10 mg/mL lysozyme (Sigma-Aldrich) at 37 °C for 30 min. DNA purity and concentration were assessed using a Nanodrop 2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and a Qubit 4.0 Fluorometer (Invitrogen). Shearing to an insert size of 350 bp was performed with a Covaris LE220 system (10% duty factor, 200 cycles/burst, 90 s treatment). Subsequent library preparation included end repair, A-tailing, and ligation with Illumina adapters containing unique dual indices (IDT for Illumina DNA/RNA UD Indexes). Amplification was carried out for 6 cycles using KAPA HiFi HotStart ReadyMix (Roche) under the following conditions: 98 ℃ for 45 s; 6 cycles of 98 ℃ for 15 s, 60 ℃ for 30 s, and 72 ℃ for 30 s; and a final extension at 72 ℃ for 5 min. Library size distribution was verified using an Agilent 4200 TapeStation (D1000 ScreenTape). Sequencing was performed in 2 × 150 bp paired-end mode with 20% PhiX spike-in to mitigate low diversity. Real-time quality control was conducted via the DRAGEN Bio-IT Platform v4.2 (Illumina), ensuring Q30 scores exceeded 85%. Bioinformatic processing involved quality assessment with FastQC v0.12.1 [18], adapter trimming and quality filtering using Trimmomatic v0.39 (SLIDINGWINDOW:4:20, MINLEN:100) [19], hybrid assembly with SPAdes v3.15.5 (k-mer range 21–127) [20], three rounds of Pilon v1.24 polishing with ≥ 100× coverage data [21], and the completeness and contamination of the assembled genome were assessed using CheckM v1.2.2, which employs a set of conserved marker genes specific to the lineage position within a reference genome tree [22].

For core-genome phylogenetic analysis, closely related strains were initially selected based on 16S rRNA gene sequence comparisons, and the complete genome sequences of their respective reference type strains were retrieved from the NCBI database. These genomic sequences were first annotated using Prokka to generate general feature format (GFF) files [23]. The resulting annotation files were subsequently processed with Roary to identify and align the core-gene set [24]. The multiple sequence alignment was then refined by trimming poorly aligned regions using TrimAl under the ‘automated1’ mode to improve phylogenetic signal [25]. A maximum-likelihood tree was constructed from the trimmed alignment using IQ-TREE [26], which performed automatic model selection (MFP) and assessed branch support with 1000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates. The entire bioinformatics workflow was carried out on the European Galaxy server (https://galaxy.eu), and the final phylogenetic tree was visualized and annotated using the Interactive Tree of Life (iTOL) online platform.

The taxonomic classification of the novel bacterial isolate was assessed based on whole-genome sequence data using the GTDB-Tk toolkit (version 2.3.2) within the classify workflow [27]. The genomic DNA sequence was processed against the GTDB reference database (release r214). Marker genes were identified using Prodigal (v2.6.3) for gene prediction, and TIGRFAM and Pfam databases were queried using HMMER (v3.3.2) for functional annotation. Multiple sequence alignment of 120 bacterial marker genes was performed using hmmalign and masked to 5,035 amino acid sites. Phylogenetic placement was conducted with pplacer (v1.1.alpha19) into the GTDB bacterial reference tree. Average nucleotide identity (ANI) values were calculated using FastANI (v1.32) against closely related type genomes [28]. Classification and taxonomic assignment were inferred from the resulting phylogenetic placement and ANI comparisons. For taxonomic classification, a comprehensive genomic framework was established. All validly published species and subspecies within the same genus as the analyzed strains were compiled through a systematic query of the List of Prokaryotic names with Standing in Nomenclature (LPSN; https://lpsn.dsmz.de). Genomic relatedness was evaluated via digital DNA-DNA hybridization (dDDH) using the Genome-to-Genome Distance Calculator (GGDC) v3.0 (https://ggdc.dsmz.de/ggdc.php#) with a species threshold of 70% similarity. Average nucleotide identity (ANI) was calculated using the EzBioCloud ANI Calculator (https://www.ezbiocloud.net/tools/ani) applying a 95–96% species boundary. The Average Amino acid Identity (AAI) was calculated on the Galaxy platform using Prokka-annotated protein files (.faa). Following an all-versus-all BLASTP comparison, the top protein match for each query was identified by sorting alignments by bit score and extracting unique hits using the Sort and Unique tools. The AAI value was then calculated as the mean percentage identity from these matches using the Summary Statistics tool. A comprehensive AAI matrix was constructed by repeating this process for all genome pairs. CRISPR arrays were identified using CRISPRCasFinder [29], and prophage regions were predicted with PHASTER [30].

Phenotypic characterization

Cell morphology was examined using transmission electron microscopy (JEM-1400FLASH, JEOL) and scanning electron microscopy (JSM-IT700HR, JEOL). Phenotypic traits were assessed using conventional culturing techniques and commercial identification systems, including Biolog GEN III (analyzed with Microlog v3.5 software), API 50CH and API ZYM strips (interpreted through the APIWEB platform, bioMérieux). These systems are well-established for phenotypic characterization of bacteria [31–34]. The Gram reaction of the bacterium was determined using the Gram staining method and the 3% KOH string test. Motility was assessed in semi-solid agar (0.4% w/v) via stab inoculation, with diffuse growth from the inoculation line after 24 h indicating positivity [35].

Chemotaxonomic analysis

Cellular fatty acids, respiratory quinones, and polar lipids were analyzed from cells grown anaerobically on Tryptic Soy Agar for 24 h at 28 °C. Fatty acid methyl esters were prepared and analyzed by gas chromatography (HP6890, Agilent) using the MIDI Sherlock System with the TSBA6 database [36]. Polar lipids were separated via two-dimensional TLC on silica gel plates and detected with specific reagents: molybdenum blue for phospholipids, ninhydrin for aminolipids, anisaldehyde for glycolipids, and phosphomolybdic acid for total lipids [37]. Respiratory quinones were extracted following the Bligh–Dyer method and analyzed by HPLC (Agilent 1260 Infinity II) on a C18 column using isopropanol-hexane (2:1, v/v) as the mobile phase at 1.0 mL/min [38].

Antimicrobial susceptibility testing

The antimicrobial susceptibility profiles of the isolates were evaluated against 29 antibiotics using custom-prepared antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST) plates, in accordance with the guidelines provided by the Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) [39]. The antibiotics tested represent the following major classes: β-lactams, tetracyclines, fluoroquinolones, aminoglycosides, macrolides, phenicols, polymyxins, and folate pathway antagonists. Quality control was ensured using the reference strain Clostridium perfringens ATCC 13,124, as recommended by the standard. Antimicrobial susceptibility testing was performed using commercially prepared, customized microdilution panels (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Specifically, two panel types designed for foodborne pathogen surveillance, CHN5FGNF and CHN6FGNF, were utilized according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The composition of the media and the source of antibiotics in these panels comply with CLSI standards, and all procedures were strictly followed as per the standard operating instructions.

Genome functional annotation

Genome annotation included structural prediction of coding sequences, tRNAs, and rRNAs using Prokka, followed by functional assignment of genes through homology searches against multiple databases: Comprehensive Antibiotic Resistance Database (CARD) [40], Clusters of Orthologous Groups (COG) [41], Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) [42], Non-Redundant (NR) protein database [43], Pathogen-Host Interaction Database (PHI) [44], and the Virulence Factor Database (VFDB) [45]. Functional assignment was performed using BLASTP with an E-value cutoff of 1e-5, a minimum identity of 40%, and a minimum query coverage of 70%.

16 S rRNA amplicon sequencing analysis

To detect the novel bacterium in host populations, rectal tissue samples (n = 18 bats: 6 Pipistrellus, 6 Rhinolophus, 6 Myotis) were subjected to 16 S rRNA amplicon sequencing of the V3-V4 region. Following DNA extraction, libraries were constructed with barcoded primers 341 F/806R and the NEB Next® Ultra DNA Library Prep Kit, validated on the Agilent 5400, and sequenced on an Illumina platform. The generated paired-end reads were processed through a bioinformatic pipeline involving quality control (Trimmomatic), merging (FLASH), and denoising with DADA2 in QIIME2 to obtain high-quality Amplicon Sequence Variants (ASVs) [46]. Prevalence was assessed by blasting the novel strain’s V3-V4 sequence against these ASVs using NCBI BLASTn [47].

Bioinformatic detection of a novel bacterial species in bat metagenomic data

To validate the presence of a novel bacterial species (previously isolated and sequenced from bats) in other bat populations, we analyzed publicly available WGS metagenomic data from bat samples. Raw sequencing reads were downloaded from the China National Center for Bioinformation (CNCB) and subjected to quality control using FastQC and Trimmomatic. High-quality reads were aligned against the complete genome of the novel bacterium using BWA-MEM. The resulting BAM files were sorted, indexed, and analyzed for coverage and depth using SAMtools. A sample was considered positive if it met all of the following criteria: ≥50% genome coverage, ≥ 1× average sequencing depth, and ≥ 10 aligned reads.

Results and discussion

Genome structure, mobile elements, and adaptive features

The complete genome assembly of strain LP20ᵀ resulted in a high-quality draft comprising 12 scaffolds (15 contigs), with a total length of 3,881,656 bp and a GC content of 30.24%. The genome assembly comprised 12 scaffolds (15 contigs) with an N50 of 2,878,845 (scaffolds) and 1,364,884 (contigs), indicating high continuity. CheckM evaluation confirmed excellent completeness (99.17%) and low contamination (0.40%). The coding density was 87.74%, with 3,596 predicted protein-coding genes. No plasmids were detected.

The genome of strain LP20ᵀ was further examined for mobile genetic elements and antiviral systems. A single CRISPR-Cas array was identified, located between positions 879,209–879,306, with a consensus repeat sequence of “TTCATATTTCCACCTGAATTCATATT” and one spacer of 46 bp. This CRISPR region was classified as Evidence Level 1 (higconfidence) using CRISPRCasFinder, indicating a functional immune adaptive system. Although no associated cas gene cluster was detected within the immediate vicinity, the presence of this CRISPR array suggests a history of phage exposure and possible immune adaptation in its ecological niche.

Additionally, two high-quality prophage regions were predicted within the genome using PHASTER: one spanning 38.1 kb (from position 931,766 to 969,872) and another 41.1 kb (from 2,077,500 to 2,118,583). Both regions were classified as “High-quality” with completeness estimates of 90.9% and 95.83%, respectively, and zero contamination. These prophages carry a substantial number of viral genes (13 and 25, respectively) and are predicted to be intact, suggesting they may be functional temperate phages. Gene annotation of these regions revealed the presence of genes associated with phage structural proteins (e.g., capsid and tail), DNA packaging, lysogenic regulation, and potential bacterial adaptation factors, such as toxins or metabolic enzymes. Their presence indicates a potential for horizontal gene transfer and phage-mediated evolution, which may contribute to genomic diversity and adaptive traits such as virulence or metabolic flexibility.

The coexistence of an intact CRISPR system and multiple prophages reflects a dynamic evolutionary history involving phage predation and bacterial counter-defense, which is consistent with adaptation to a competitive gut environment. These features further distinguish LP20ᵀ from other Clostridium species and provide genomic insights into its ecological strategy within the bat intestinal microbiome.

Genomic phylogeny and taxonomic demarcation

The 16 S rRNA gene sequences of strains LP20ᵀ and LP18, initially amplified by PCR using primers 27 F/1492R, were 1359 bp and 1387 bp in length, respectively. In comparison, the corresponding sequences extracted from the whole-genome assemblies were 1502 bp and 1373 bp, respectively. Notably, pairwise comparison revealed a high identity of 99.78% between the sequences obtained through both methods for each strain, demonstrating consistency between the PCR-amplified and genome-derived 16 S rRNA gene sequences. This result confirms that strains LP20ᵀ and LP18 belong to the same species. Phylogenetic analysis based on the 16 S rRNA gene demonstrated that strain LP20ᵀ exhibited highest sequence similarity with C. paraputrificum ATCC 25780ᵀ (98.10%) and C. vincentii DSM 10228ᵀ (97.06%). Both values fall below the 98.65% threshold recommended for novel species delineation (Fig. 1A, B).

Phylogenomic analysis based on the concatenated sequences of core genes robustly delineated the taxonomic position of strains LP20ᵀ and LP18 within the genus Clostridium (Fig. 2). The resulting maximum-likelihood tree demonstrated that strains LP20ᵀ and LP18 formed a distinct, well-supported monophyletic clade with C. paraputrificum, clearly separating them from other closely related species such as C. vincentii and C. colinum. This close affiliation with C. paraputrificum was further supported by a high branch support value. In contrast, a phylogenetic tree reconstructed from 16 S rRNA gene sequences showed a different topology, wherein LP20ᵀ and LP18 did not form a stable cluster with any specific type strain and were interspersed among several other Clostridium species, reflecting the limited resolution of the 16 S rRNA gene for distinguishing closely related species within this genus. Collectively, the core-genome phylogeny provides compelling evidence that strains LP20ᵀ and LP18 represent a novel lineage most closely related to C. paraputrificum, a finding that was obscured by the higher evolutionary conservation of the 16 S rRNA gene, thereby underscoring the necessity of employing genome-scale data for accurate taxonomic placement.

Whole-genome assembly of strain LP20ᵀ resolved 12 contigs totaling 3,881,656 bp, with a G + C content of 30.24 mol% (Fig. 3A), the G + C content of strain LP20ᵀ (30.24 mol%) fell within the range observed among its close relatives, C. paraputrificum (29.64%) and C. vincentii (30.70%). Strain LP18 possessed a genome of 3,870,310 bp assembled into 26 contigs, with a G + C content of 30.22 mol%. Whole-genome-based taxonomic assignment of the novel strain LP20ᵀ was conducted using GTDB-Tk with the reference database. The analysis placed LP20ᵀ within the genus Clostridium, under the family Clostridiaceae, order Clostridiales, class Clostridia, and phylum Bacillota_A. Notably, the genome could not be assigned to any currently recognized species within the genus, as indicated by the absence of a species-level designation. The ANI between LP20ᵀ and its closest phylogenetic relative based on placement (Clostridium sp.) was 79.43%, with an alignment fraction (AF) of 0.90. This ANI value falls below the established species boundary threshold of 95–96%, supporting the delineation of LP20ᵀ as a novel species. These genomic findings are consistent with those derived from 16 S rRNA gene sequence and phylogenetic analyses, which also indicated that LP20ᵀ forms a distinct branch within the genus Clostridium.

Genomic relatedness analyses firmly established the taxonomic position of strain LP20ᵀ at both the species and genus levels. The 16 S rRNA similarity (extracted from the WGS), ANI, dDDH and AAI values between LP20ᵀ and its closest relatives, C. paraputrificum (98.12%, 75.93%, 20.6% and 64.94%) and C. vincentii (97.06%, 74.92%, 19.5% and 59.69%), fall significantly below the thresholds for species delineation (95% ANI, 70% dDDH and 95% AAI,), thereby confirming its novel species status (Table 1). Crucially, these values align with the expected range for distinct species within the same genus (typically ANI 73.98–95.98%) [48], which robustly supports the placement of LP20ᵀ within the genus Clostridium. This genus-level assignment is further corroborated by the core-genome phylogeny and GTDB-Tk, where LP20ᵀ forms a distinct monophyletic clade with other validated Clostridium species.

Functional annotation using the NR database (Fig. 3B) revealed that 91.51% of predicted genes showed affiliation with Clostridium species. The closest matches were to unclassified Clostridium spp. (39.1%), C. paraputrificum (28.8%), and C. vincentii (5.8%). Notably, while 761 core genes (including rpoB and gyrA) were shared with C. paraputrificum, 1,032 genes aligned exclusively with unclassified clostridia, indicating substantial lineage-specific genomic innovation [49]. These results underscore significant genomic heterogeneity within the genus [50] and provide robust phylogenetic evidence supporting the classification of strain LP20ᵀ as the type strain of Clostridium zhoujianii sp.nov.

Phenotypic and chemotaxonomic delineation of a novel species

Strain LP20ᵀ was strictly anaerobic, forming moist, irregular white colonies on Columbia blood agar. While the genus Clostridium is predominantly Gram-positive, several species or former members are known to exhibit Gram-variable or even Gram-negative staining, often linked to cell wall ultrastructure variations (e.g., Clostridium clostridioforme group, C. ramosum) [51, 52]. In this context, strain LP20ᵀ exhibited Gram-negative staining (Fig. 4A), it tested positive in the 3% KOH string test, with a distinct string formation observed, and this result, alongside the Gram-staining observation, collectively indicates a Gram-negative phenotype, confirming the existence of this rare phenotype within the genus and suggesting distinct evolutionary adaptations in its cell wall composition and architecture [53]. SEM analysis revealed that strain LP20ᵀ cells were predominantly rod-shaped with variable lengths, most cells exhibited rounded or blunt ends, while a minority displayed flat or tapered ends (Fig. 4B). TEM analysis showed an indistinct nucleoid; The cytoplasm lacked organelles and exhibited low electron density; The cell wall was thin with a smooth periphery and displayed high electron density; The cytoplasmic membrane was closely apposed to the cell wall; Putative flagellar structures were observed on the cell surface (Fig. 4C). While the isolate grew at pH 6–9. The temperature range for growth was 20–45 °C, with optimal growth observed between 28 and 40 °C. This differed from its close relatives, C. paraputrificum ATCC 25780ᵀ (30–37 °C) and C. vincentii DSM 10228ᵀ (2–20 °C), growth occurred in the presence of 0–4% NaCl, showing no significant difference in salt tolerance compared to related strains [54], distinct physiological differentiators were identified: BIOLOG GEN III profiling showed weak utilization of D-turanose but negativity for N-acetyl-D-glucosamine and fusidic acid resistance; API 50CH tests revealed D-xylose positivity but D-lactose negativity (reversed in relatives); API ZYM detected β-glucuronidase and α-mannosidase activity (absent in relatives); API 20 A confirmed D-saccharose negativity (positive in relatives) (Table 2). These differential characteristics satisfy established novel species criteria [12, 13].

Chemotaxonomic analyses provided definitive evidence for taxonomic discrimination. The cellular fatty acid profile was predominantly composed of C₁₆:₀ (37.23%), C₁₈:₁ ω9c (15.28%), and C₁₄:₀ (14.83%). While the qualitative composition was similar to its closest relatives, quantitative differences were evident. Notably, significant distinctions were observed in the proportions of minor components, such as the higher amount of C₁₈:₀ (2.98% in LP20ᵀ vs. 1.57% in C. paraputrificum ATCC 25780ᵀ and 1.6% in C. vincentii DSM 10228ᵀ) and the substantially lower level of C₁₆:₁ ω9c (3.52% vs. 15.88% and 12.53%, respectively). This distinctive fatty acid composition validates its status as a novel species and provides valuable chemotaxonomic markers for differentiating strain LP20ᵀ from its phylogenetic neighbors (Table 3). Polar lipid analysis identified diphosphatidylglycerol (DPG), phosphatidylethanolamine (PE), phosphatidylglycerol (PG), phosphatidylcholine (PC), unidentified polar lipids (UL), and a rare aminophospholipid (APL)—the latter being unreported in Clostridium [55] (Fig. 4D). Respiratory quinones comprised MK-6, MK-7, and MK-7(H₄), contrasting sharply with the MK-7-dominated profile of reference strains [56].

The unique phenotypic-chemotaxonomic signature—particularly the Gram-negative reaction, APL presence, and differential enzyme/metabolic activities—has no equivalent in described clostridia. This polyphasic evidence conclusively establishes LP20ᵀ as representing a novel species [57]. Complete physiological data are provided in Supplementary Table S1.

Antibiotic resistance phenotype

Strain LP20ᵀ, a novel Gram-negative Clostridium species, exhibited a distinctive antimicrobial susceptibility profile, showing resistance only to polymyxin B and polymyxin E while remaining susceptible to all other 27 antibiotics tested, including representatives from β-lactam, tetracycline, fluoroquinolone, aminoglycoside, macrolide, phenicol, and folate antagonist classes (Table 4). This narrow spectrum of resistance is notable given its Gram-negative status, as polymyxin resistance in such organisms is often associated with modifications in the lipid A component of lipopolysaccharide mediated by chromosomal mutations or acquired resistance genes [58]. This resistance to polymyxins is likely intrinsic, mediated by chromosomally encoded pathways (such as PhoP-PhoQ and PmrA-PmrB) that modify the bacterial outer membrane, rather than by acquired mobile resistance genes like the mcr family [59]. Although rare among clostridia, Gram-negative members have been reported to display intrinsic multidrug resistance patterns divergent from Gram-positive relatives [60]. The absence of resistance to other broad-spectrum agents suggests that LP20ᵀ may lack widespread horizontally acquired resistance determinants, highlighting its potential role as a model for studying membrane-level resistance in anaerobic Gram-negative bacteria.

Functional genomics reveal ecological adaptation mechanisms

Genomic analysis of Clostridium sp. LP20ᵀ revealed distinctive functional traits that not only support its proposed novel species status but also reflect adaptive mechanisms to the bat gut ecosystem. Comparative genomic analysis showed that LP20ᵀ shares ≤ 75.93% ANI and ≤ 20.6% dDDH with closely related Clostridium sensu stricto species (e.g., C. paraputrificum, C. vincentii), values well below the standard thresholds for species demarcation. Functional annotation further revealed unique genomic characteristics that differentiate LP20ᵀ from these relatives.

Metabolically, LP20ᵀ exhibits a pronounced enrichment in genes related to carbohydrate metabolism (227 genes in COG category G; 342 genes in KEGG carbohydrate metabolism pathways) (Fig. 5A andB), including numerous glycoside hydylases (e.g., GH5, GH9, GH28, GH78) capable of degrading plant-derived polysaccharides such as cellulose and pectin [61, 62]. This suggests enhanced saccharolytic potential compared to other Clostridium species, likely an adaptation to the fruit-rich diet of its bat host. Additionally, a complete SCFA biosynthesis pathway (including buk and ptb) was identified, indicating the ability to produce butyrate—a key metabolite for host energy homeostasis and gut health [63]. These metabolic features are of particular ecological relevance in the frugivorous bat intestine, where efficient breakdown of complex plant polysaccharides confers a competitive advantage.

The antibiotic resistance profile of LP20ᵀ also exhibits notable differences (Fig. 6A). While the strain carries 69 antibiotic resistance genes—including efflux pumps, target modification enzymes, and regulators associated with glycopeptide and fluoroquinolone resistance—the presence of a clustered vanR-associated glycopeptide resistance determinant is uncommon among commensal clostridia and may reflect site-specific selective pressures, such as environmental antibiotic exposure in agricultural regions of Qiandongnan [64–66]. This resistome structure may offer a competitive edge in a microbiome context where antagonistic interactions are frequent. To directly link the observed phenotypic resistance to its genetic basis, we interrogated the genome of strain LP20ᵀ for specific resistance determinants. The resistance to polymyxins, a hallmark of its profile, is consistent with the absence of detectable acquired resistance genes (e.g., mcr families) and instead points towards intrinsic mechanisms. This intrinsic resistance is likely mediated by chromosomally encoded modifications of the lipid A moiety of the lipopolysaccharide, a common adaptation in Gram-negative bacteria. Conversely, the disparity between the extensive resistome—which includes genes conferring potential resistance to glycopeptides and fluoroquinolones—and the actual susceptibility to these agents in vitro, suggests that many of these genes may be silent, poorly expressed, or encode resistance to antibiotic classes not tested in this study. This highlights the critical distinction between the genetic potential for resistance and its phenotypic expression, underscoring that LP20ᵀ’s resistance profile is primarily intrinsic (polymyxins) rather than attributable to a broad suite of horizontally acquired, functional resistance genes.

Regarding host–microbe interactions, LP20ᵀ possesses 367 genes linked to pathogenicity, most of which are associated with reduced (58.0%) or neutral (25.1%) virulence effects(Fig. 6B). Analysis against the VFDB revealed the absence of genes homologous to those encoding major clostridial toxins (e.g., Clostridium difficile TcdA/TcdB or Clostridium perfringens alpha, beta, epsilon, and iota toxins) in the LP20ᵀ genome. This gene repertoire—including flagellar assembly genes (flgG, fliC, motA/B), stress response proteins (dnaK, groEL), and two-component systems (virR/virS)—suggests adaptation toward commensalism rather than overt pathogenicity [67–69] (Table 5). Such a profile is consistent with isolation from a healthy bat host and distinguishes it from more virulent Clostridium species [70].

In summary, the genomic features of LP20ᵀ—including its specialized carbohydrate metabolism, unique resistome, and commensal-associated virulence factor repertoire—collectively support its classification as a novel species within Clostridium sensu stricto and reflect adaptive evolution to the nutritional resources and ecological dynamics of the bat gut environment.Original functional predictions from all six databases are detailed in Supplementary Table S2.

Comparison of 16 S rRNA amplicon sequencing results

The 16 S rRNA amplicon sequencing analysis identified a total of 1,038 microbial taxa, with a combined sequence length of 425,743 bp. A BLASTn comparison of the 404-bp V3–V4 region of strain LP20ᵀ against this dataset revealed two microbial taxa exhibiting sequence similarity greater than 98.65% under 100% query coverage. The highest identity value reached 100%, followed by 99.26% (Table S3 16 S rRNA amplicon sequencing sequences of rectal microbiota from three bat species). Further matching confirmed that both of these microbial taxa were derived from gut bacteria of Rhinolophus bat (Table S4 The bat sample classifications corresponding to the microbial taxa from the 16 S rRNA amplicon sequencing annotation results) [47].

Specific detection of LP20T in bats metagenomic data

We downloaded bat gut metagenomic data (Project No. PRJCA013483) from the GSA database of the CNCB, including samples from Rhinolophus rex, Rhinolophus affinis, Rhinolophus macrotis, and Rhinolophus pusillus. Comparative analysis revealed that in the intestine of Rhinolophus pusillus (Sample ID: SAMC4116281), the complete genome sequence of the novel bacterial strain LP20T achieved a coverage of 53.07% and a depth of 6.654×. In contrast, the comparisons in samples from Rhinolophus rex, Rhinolophus affinis, and Rhinolophus pusillus showed sequence coverages below 20% and depths lower than 1×(Table S5 Metagenomic screening and identification of Clostridium zhoujianii strain LP20ᵀ in bat gut metagenomes from the GSA database). Therefore, we reasonably conclude that the novel bacterium LP20T is present in Rhinolophus pusillus.

Conclusion

The presence of this novel species in other bats was confirmed by both 16 S rRNA amplicon sequencing and metagenomic analysis. Through polyphasic taxonomy, we establish strain LP20ᵀ from bat intestines in Qiandongnan, Guizhou, China as a novel Clostridium species based on: (1) Taxonomic boundaries: 16 S rRNA gene similarity (98.10%) to closest relative C. paraputrificum ATCC 25780ᵀ below the novel species threshold (98.65%); ANI (75.93%), dDDH (20.6%) and AAI (64.94%) values significantly under species delineation cutoffs (95%, 70% and 95%); (2) Unique chemotaxonomic traits: Gram-negative reaction (diverging from genus typology), fatty acid profile (notably elevated C₁₈:₀ and C₁₆:₁ ω9c), and polar lipids containing rare aminophospholipid (APL). In addition, the high sequence identity (up to 100%) of strain LP20ᵀ to microbial taxa found exclusively in Rhinolophus bats strongly suggests a wide distribution of this novel bacterial species within this host.

Functional adaptations include: (1) Metabolic strategy: Genomic expansion in carbohydrate metabolism genes (227 COG-G; 342 KEGG) adapting to polysaccharide-rich frugivorous bat gut; complete butyrate synthesis pathway (buk) indicating host energy provision; (2) Antibiotic resistome: 81 resistance genes dominated by glycopeptide (vanR cluster) and fluoroquinolone (gyrA/B) resistance, reflecting agricultural antibiotic pressure in Qiandongnan and mobilome-driven horizontal transfer; (3) Host interaction: PHI/VFDB annotations showing reduced-virulence predominance (58.0%), while flagellar (fliC, motA/B) and immune evasion (groEL) factors suggest conditional pathogenicity balanced by commensalism in healthy hosts.

Ecologically, LP20ᵀ’s genomic features (30.24 mol% G + C, atypical lipid composition) challenge traditional Clostridium classification, supporting genome-based taxonomic revision. Its enriched resistome positions bats as potential antibiotic resistance vectors, highlighting zoonotic risk. Metabolic-virulence gene configurations reveal bat-microbe coevolution in carbohydrate utilization and immune balancing. Collectively, strain LP20ᵀ expands genus diversity and provides a model for host-specific microbial adaptation and environmental resistome dissemination. This description complies with the International Code of Nomenclature of Prokaryotes, with LP20ᵀ as the type strain.

The discovery and characterization of novel microorganisms, particularly from understudied reservoirs like the bat gut, are critical for proactive microbiome research. While it is speculative to assign direct zoonotic or adaptive significance to strain LP20ᵀ based solely on genomic annotations, its isolation and description hold fundamental importance. As a novel species within the clinically relevant genus Clostridium, it serves as a valuable reference point for understanding the genetic diversity and evolutionary dynamics of this group in a unique mammalian host. This work provides essential baseline data that will enable future comparative studies to more rigorously assess the ecological, industrial, or biomedical potential of such organisms, thereby contributing to a broader preparedness against emerging threats.

Description of Clostridium zhoujianii sp. nov.

Cells are strictly anaerobic, Gram-stain-negative rods. After 48 h of incubation on Columbia blood agar at 37 °C, colonies are irregular, moist, and white. Growth occurs at 20–45 °C (optimum 30–37 °C) and at pH 6.0–9.0 (optimum pH 7.0–8.0); growth occurs in the presence of 0–4% (w/v) NaCl (optimum 0.5–1.0%). The major cellular fatty acids are C₁₆:₀, C₁₈:₁ ω9c, and C₁₄:₀. The polar lipid profile consists of diphosphatidylglycerol, phosphatidylethanolamine, phosphatidylglycerol, phosphatidylcholine, several unidentified lipids, and an unidentified aminophospholipid. Menaquinones include MK-6, MK-7, and MK-7(H₄). Differential diagnosis: The following characteristics distinguish strain LP20ᵀ from its closest phylogenetic relatives, C. paraputrificum ATCC 25780ᵀ and C. vincentii DSM 10228ᵀ: distinct quantitative differences in its major fatty acid profile (C₁₆:₀, C₁₈:₁ ω9c, C₁₄:₀), particularly evidenced by a markedly lower proportion of C₁₆:₁ ω9c (3.52% vs. 15.88% and 12.53%, respectively); inability to utilize D-lactose or D-saccharose; presence of β-glucuronidase and α-mannosidase activities; and the presence of a unique polar lipid profile featuring an unidentified aminophospholipid.

The type strain, LP20ᵀ (= JCM 37970ᵀ = GDMCC 1.5627ᵀ), was isolated from the intestinal content of a bat collected in Qiandongnan, Guizhou Province, China. The genome of the type strain is 3,881,656 bp with a DNA G + C content of 30.24 mol%. The 16 S rRNA gene and whole-genome sequences have been deposited in GenBank under accession numbers PV577711 and JBPUTF000000000, respectively.

Fuzzy decision support systems for hospital infection management: a circular q-ROF CRADIS method to prevention and control
Authors: Man Li, Rui Wang, Miaomiao Wang, Yanyan Zhao, Xiaofen Shen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHealthcare-associated infections remain one of the most critical challenges for patient safety and healthcare sustainability, which urgently requires decision-making frameworks that can model uncertainty, hesitation, and conflicting expert opinions. Most of the existing studies in hospital infection-control evaluation models have relied on classical or basic fuzzy approaches, which cannot represent the circular preference behavior of expert judgments and high degrees of ambiguity. In this respect, this study proposes a decision-support framework based on Circular q-Rung Orthopair Fuzzy sets integrated with a modified CRADIS method. The Circular q-ROF structure enables simultaneous modeling of uncertainty, hesitation, and periodic judgment patterns frequently faced in many expert-driven healthcare assessments. Furthermore, this study reformulates the modified CRADIS method under the Circular q-ROF environment with the necessary adaptation of normalization, aggregation, and ranking procedures. Based on the proposed model, hospital infection-control measures are evaluated with respect to several criteria covering effectiveness, safety, cost, and sustainability. A numerical case study is provided to demonstrate its applicability, and comparisons are conducted with some of the well-known existing fuzzy aggregation-based decision models to demonstrate the ranking behavior. Sensitivity analysis is conducted to examine the robustness of the results when considering parameter variation and changes in expert weights. Accordingly, the results have shown that the proposed approach provided stable and interpretable rankings, ensuring good robustness of the results under high uncertainty conditions. This paper contributes a structured and reliable decision-support tool for hospital infection-control policy formulation.Keywords: Hospital infection management, Healthcare decision support, Prevention and control strategies, Intelligent decision-making, Fuzzy logic in healthcare, Multi-criteria decision-makingSubject terms: Health care, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Hospital-acquired infections (HAIs) are a longstanding and widely reported global public health concern with millions of patients suffering from such infections every year, characterized by prolonged hospital stays, rising healthcare costs, as well as heightened rates of morbidity and mortality rates1-2-3. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), the burden of HAIs is estimated to be twice as high in low- and mid-income countries as compared to the high-income countries. This highlights the importance of employing an efficacious model of infection control and prevention strategies. Empirical studies have reaffirmed the importance of implementation of the following infection control strategies. Implementation of IPC strategies in hospitals effectively reduces the rates of infection as well as the associated healthcare costs. It should be noted that despite all these identified benefits, it should be highlighted that IPC decision-making is by its very nature a complex process since it is often necessary to balance a number of often contradictory factors, including its clinical effectiveness, patients’ safety, cost, environment, staff compliance, antimicrobial resistance risks, etc. It should be pointed out that currently used IPC assessment systems are generally based on deterministic indicators, statistical surveillance, or scoring systems based on guidelines. Among their limitations, it should be highlighted that it is often supposed that data are known exactly, or that linear relationships between different factors exist; however, this does not include uncertainties, hesitations of experts, or subjective decisions that are often characteristic of healthcare systems.

HAIs continue to be a pressing and urgent concern for healthcare systems across the globe, leading to a substantial rise in patient morbidity, mortality, and healthcare costs. Despite the significant amount of research that has been conducted on infection control strategies, hospital administrators and healthcare professionals continue to be faced with ambiguity and divergent expert opinions on how to effectively rank and prioritize infection control strategies, owing to the limitations of traditional decision-making models in effectively incorporating ambiguity and high-order uncertainty that are typically present in expert opinions. In light of this challenge, there is a need for a systematic, flexible, and robust approach that can effectively combine multiple expert opinions while incorporating uncertainty. To this end, the proposed Circular q-Rung Orthopair Fuzzy CRADIS (Cq-ROFCA) approach presents a comprehensive solution to this problem, offering multi-criteria and compromise ranking functionality that enables hospitals to systematically rank and prioritize infection control strategies.

These limitations have been underlined by recent global health emergencies. The 2013–2015 Ebola virus disease outbreak and the COVID-19 pandemic showed large gaps in IPC preparedness, especially regarding the allocation of resources, prioritization of control measures, and adaptive decision-making under uncertainty4. As an answer, the international efforts, such as the strengthened International Health Regulations (IHR) and the WHO Global Action Plan on Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR), strongly emphasize the importance of evidence-based, context-sensitive decision frameworks about IPC at a national and facility level5-6-7. However, empirical evaluations reveal that most healthcare institutions still show incompetence in translating these policy-level recommendations into operational, data-driven decision processes.

Recent developments in decision analysis and information technology have reaffirmed the importance of combining advanced tools to solve complex multi-criteria problems. For instance, research on geospatial technology and multi-criteria decision analysis has shown the applicability of Geographic Information Systems (GIS) and Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) in guiding sustainable and resilient urban planning and land suitability analysis by combining spatial and decision data8. Similarly, research on technological innovation in digital brand management has shown the applicability of artificial intelligence (AI)9 and immersive technologies such as augmented reality (AR), personalization, and engagement in transforming evaluation and decision-making processes in complex settings by improving responsiveness, interaction, and decision outcomes10. Furthermore, research on evaluation beyond scientific impact has emphasized the need to extend evaluation frameworks to incorporate relevance and applicability in addition to scientific criteria, advocating for more comprehensive evaluation frameworks that reflect real-world impact and interdisciplinary contributions. These views and arguments are in line with and support the need to develop effective multi-criteria decision support tools such as the Circular q-Rung Orthopair Fuzzy CRADIS (Cq-ROFCA) framework, which is capable of addressing uncertainty, expert vagueness, and conflicting criteria in decision-making.

In this regard, the suggested Circular q-ROF CRADIS decision support system gives a methodical and stable guideline for strategy ranking in uncertain situations. Integrating expert opinions and managing imprecision in evaluations, the model guarantees infection control choices to be fact-based, context-dependent, and responsive to actual hospital settings. The system was used to rank a range of real-world IPC measures, such as refined hand hygiene practice, better environmental sanitation practices, antimicrobial optimization, and training programs. Experts made judgments on multiple criteria, and the Circular q-ROF CRADIS model consolidated these opinions into an overall ranking of measures. In contrast to standard evaluation techniques, which can oversimplify the opinions of experts, this model retained the doubt and ambivalence inherent in actual decision-making. The outcomes pinpointed hygiene practice and antimicrobial stewardship as priorities, in agreement with suggestions from WHO and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), as well as showing the model’s congruence with recognized healthcare guidelines11-12.

Aside from ranking strategy, the use of the Circular q-ROF CRADIS system also facilitated sensitivity testing to examine how variations in expert preference affect final decisions. This capability is especially helpful in fluctuating hospital environments where resource availability, patient populations, and outbreak statuses can change. The model’s flexibility assures that infection control strategies under various scenarios remain strong, aiding hospital administrators in upholding active and nimble infection control policies13. Case study findings ensured that the suggested decision support system not only enhances the reliability and transparency of infection control decisions but also enhances interdisciplinary collaboration. By merging clinicians’, infection control experts’, and hospital administrators’ expertise into an integrated framework for decision-making, the Circular q-ROF CRADIS model ensures consensus without losing the richness of varied opinions. This results in more effective prevention and control strategies, diminishing the risk of outbreaks and enhancing patient safety on the whole. The framework therefore shows considerable practical utility in actual healthcare uses, providing a novel tool for addressing one of the most enduring problems in hospital systems14. Context and motivation

IPC remains one of the most important public health issues worldwide because of its clear impact on patient and healthcare worker outcomes, as well as the sustainability of healthcare systems, and because of recent global outbreaks, such as the Ebola epidemic from 2013 to 2015 and more recently during the COVID-19 pandemic, which have exposed inherent challenges of IPC decision-making and planning on both national and healthcare-facility levels, that are complicated by an increasingly urgent threat from antimicrobial resistance (AMR). The current assessment and decision models under the IPC are mostly guideline-driven or based on conventional scoring and rating methods. These are effective for monitoring compliance but are not amenable to the complexities involved in decision environments under uncertain data, diverse expert views, or divergent criteria for evaluation. The selection of a strategy for the IPC is often carried out through the collective views of multidisciplinary experts, where ambiguity, partial consensus, vagueness, and variability of preferences are prevalent. Classical and rudimentary FMC methods, while having the capability to deal to some extent with the issues involved due to uncertainty, rely on linear and static models under which the preferences follow cycles or periodic behavior as common in healthcare decision-support processes.

Although recent developments in fuzzy sets have improved the capability of uncertainty expression using the results of q-rung orthopair fuzzy models, the use of environments based on q-rung fuzzy sets in existing IPC applications has not typically addressed circular preference systems or the ability to withstand discrepancies between experts. The Circular q-Rung Orthopair Fuzzy approach generalizes the standard models of q-rung orthopair fuzzy sets by introducing a circular approach that provides a more realistic way of expressing the concept of hesitation or ambiguity and the experts’ cyclic opinions in a realistic way in a field like the healthcare domain, where risk perception and policy-making can change dynamically.

In this context, the rank-and-rank procedure of the CRADIS appears as a compromise-based ranking technique that measures alternatives based on their distances to ideal and anti-ideal solutions. However, the classical forms of this method seem to lack the capability to deal with very ambiguous and circular fuzzy data. Hence, the Cq-ROF CRADIS method has been proposed within this context as a result of the need to obtain a systematic decision-making aid with a clear, interpretable form for evaluating IPC strategic approaches. In addition to its methodological contribution, the proposed framework is relevant to global health agendas, such as the WHO Global Action Plan on Antimicrobial Resistance, International Health Regulations, and United Nations Sustainable Development Goals, in relation to health coverage, water, sanitation, and hygiene, and maternal and neonatal health. In conclusion, this work fills an identified methodological gap in the area of infection control and prevention, providing an effective and uncertain tool to improve infection control capacity in complex healthcare settings.

Gap in current systems

Hospital infection prevention and control is identified as a critical yet complex decision problem with considerable implications in terms of its impact on patient safety, clinical performance, and financial implications. Despite different risk assessment and decision support tool approaches to tackle infection control, most existing systems have been identified as being limited in terms of their ability to handle uncertainty, indecision, and dependencies between different infection criteria appropriately. In general settings, conventional statistical methods depend on specific data conditions and stationary probability distribution patterns that are frequently unlikely to occur or change constantly in real-world settings at a hospital. Likewise, classical multi-criteria decision making methods like AHP, TOPSIS, VIKOR, ELECTRE can help improve decision making for multiple criteria but frequently assume deterministic or generally linearly weighted decision criteria. In consequence, they do not often allow for nonlinear outcomes between infection control strategies, antibiotic resistance development, safety considerations, sustainability aspects, and complexity aspects.

Even sophisticated fuzzy approaches, such as IFS, PyFS, Fermatean, and q-ROF approaches, mostly address uncertainty using separate MG and NMG. However, despite offering higher flexibility through fuzzy approaches, they still incorporate linear structures, which cannot address cyclic hesitations and feedback-driven dependency often involved in IPC decision-making sufficiently well. Hence, the varying opinions by experts might fluctuate subject to various clinical considerations, infections, and feasibility concerns, which have not been sufficiently addressed by existing fuzzy approaches and might remain an area where IPC decision-making would seek better solutions as part of the future development trajectory for fuzzy approaches. Additionally, various existing models of making decisions in an IPC setting, such as decision models, heavily focus on selection of alternatives and largely ignore testing how strong their results are under disagreements between experts or minor changes in criteria weights. With these limitations, it becomes questionable how well they can perform in real-life settings in hospitals, where there is incomplete or changing information.

The Cq-ROFS can bridge this gap by incorporating experts’ opinions using a circular geometric structure that facilitates simultaneous assessment of acceptance, refusal, and hesitation using a non-linear manner. Problem statement and literature review

Literature review

Hospital infection control has been extensively studied using data-driven, multi-criteria, and decision-support approaches due to its complexity and uncertainty. Various studies have applied classical statistical models and rule-based systems to analyze hospital-acquired infections. For instance, epidemiological and regression models have been applied to assess the influence of hand hygiene compliance, patient density, and antibiotic consumption on the prevalence of HAIs. However, these models require accurate and complete data, which is not feasible in real-world hospital settings where subjective expert opinions and incomplete data are common. To address these issues, MCDM approaches have gained popularity in healthcare management. Various applications of MCDM methods32-33-34 have been reported in the literature, including hospital performance assessment, medical equipment choice, risk assessment, and healthcare waste management. Studies applying AHP, TOPSIS, VIKOR, and PROMETHEE methods have shown the effectiveness of MCDM methods in formalizing complex healthcare decisions with multiple conflicting criteria. However, these classical MCDM methods are heavily dependent on crisp expert judgments and fixed weights, making them less effective in modeling uncertainty and hesitation in expert judgments, especially in hospital infection control decision problems.

To overcome the uncertainty in healthcare decision-making, fuzzy set-based extensions have been proposed and successfully used in medical diagnosis, hospital service quality evaluation, treatment plan selection, and health risk assessment. These sets improve flexibility by enabling the independent definition of membership and non-membership degrees, thus incorporating partial belief and uncertainty in expert judgments. Although significant progress has been made, most of the existing fuzzy models in the healthcare domain consider the decision criteria to be linearly independent and unable to capture the circular and interconnected nature of IPC-related factors, such as the feedback relationship between hygiene compliance, infection rate, and antimicrobial resistance. Recent research efforts have also focused on the development of hybrid decision-support systems that combine fuzzy logic with machine learning, optimization, and intelligent systems for healthcare applications. Although these models improve accuracy, they are often criticized for their lack of interpretability, complexity, and lack of transparency, which are major drawbacks in the sensitive domain of healthcare, where explain ability and accountability are of utmost importance. In contrast to the existing decision support systems with a focus on the healthcare domain, there is still a significant research gap in the literature that combines the modeling of circular uncertainty with robust ranking mechanisms specifically designed for the prioritization of hospital IPC strategies. The existing fuzzy and hybrid models have limitations in modeling expert hesitation in a circular fashion or in producing weight and criterion importance-induced stable rankings. This research gap leads to the development of the proposed Circular q-Rung Orthopair Fuzzy CRADIS approach, which has improved modeling capabilities of uncertainty, interpretable ranking results, and robustness specifically designed for the healthcare domain of hospital infection prevention and control.

Fuzzy decision making and uncertainty modeling in complex systems

Decision-making in complex environments is generally plagued by the existence of uncertainties, imprecision, hesitation, and conflicting judgments of experts. Different scholars have, so far, used fuzzy set theory in a great number of MCDM problems to handle such difficulties. Extensions from ordinary fuzzy sets, like intuitionistic fuzzy sets, Pythagorean fuzzy sets, Fermatean fuzzy sets, and q-rung orthopair fuzzy (q-ROF) sets, have enhanced the modeling of uncertainty, due to the flexibility of representation in membership and non-membership degrees. Among the above, q-ROF sets are attracting more and more attention in modeling expert hesitation or ambiguity with higher expressive power, especially in high-dimensional decision environments. Some recent works have shown the application of q-ROF-based aggregation operators and ranking methods in supplier selection, sustainability assessment, healthcare evaluation, and risk management. Most fuzzy MCDM models developed to date assume linear and static preference structures, which cannot sufficiently capture the complex judgment behavior of human beings, particularly when expert preferences exhibit periodic/cyclical characteristics.

Hybrid Fuzzy–AI and intelligent decision-support frameworks

More recently, with an increase in the availability of data and computational intelligence techniques, there has been a greater interest in hybrid fuzzy artificial intelligence decision-support systems. These models incorporate fuzzy logic with artificial intelligence, optimization, and data-driven models to enhance decision accuracy, adaptability, and robustness. Hybrid fuzzy MCDM methods integrated with machine learning, optimization algorithms, and intelligent scoring mechanisms have been applied in the evaluation of sustainability, ESG assessment, supply chain resilience, and smart healthcare systems. For example, some recent works have proposed intelligent maturity models and AI-assisted fuzzy evaluation frameworks to assess sustainable supply chains and industrial transitions under Industry 5.0 paradigms35. Similarly, ESG-oriented fuzzy evaluation models have been developed with the purpose of enabling strategic decision-making in science parks36 and in innovation ecosystems37. Such studies bear evidence to the ever-growing role of intelligent hybrid decision-support systems capable of dealing with uncertainty while ensuring interpretability and transparency. Despite these advances, many of the hybrid fuzzy AI models primarily address either optimization or prediction tasks and give limited attention to robust ordinal ranking and compromise-based decision mechanisms under strong expert disagreement.

Ordinal MCDM techniques and compromise-based ranking methods

Ordinal MCDM methods are crucial in decision problems where ranking stability and interpretability are of the essence. Techniques such as TOPSIS, VIKOR, MARCOS, WASPAS, and CRADIS have been widely adopted in view of their ability to compare alternatives with respect to the ideal and anti-ideal solutions. Of these, the method of CRADIS has been taken up for its balanced compromise ranking mechanism and computational simplicity. Several fuzzy extensions of CRADIS were proposed to handle uncertainty in decision inputs. However, the available fuzzy CRADIS models are usually developed under a classical fuzzy, intuitionistic fuzzy, or basic q-ROF environment. Most of these formulae suffer from a serious deficiency in robustness when expert judgments show high ambiguity or circular preference behavior. Moreover, sensitivity and robustness analyses are frequently omitted, making it difficult to put confidence in ranking results.

Circular fuzzy models and the emerging research gaps

The emerging powerful extension of traditional fuzzy environments is the circular fuzzy model, which overcomes the limitations of linear preference assumptions. Circular representations allow the decision maker to model periodic, oscillatory, or cyclic judgment patterns arising in dynamic and high-risk decision contexts, such as healthcare policy formulation or infection control planning. The Cq-ROF framework extends conventional q-ROF sets by embedding the math of circular geometry in uncertainty modeling. This enables a more comprehensive representation of hesitation, ambiguity, and expert disagreement. Although the use of circular fuzzy sets has several promising results in selected decision-making applications, their integration with compromise-based ordinal ranking methods is very limited within the literature.

Research gap and positioning of the proposed study

Based on the above review, several gaps can be identified, among which are: (a) existing IPC and healthcare decision-support models capture circular and highly ambiguous expert judgment behavior incompletely, (b) the current hybrid fuzzy–AI frameworks, while emphasizing optimization and prediction, often overlook robust ordinal ranking and compromise-based evaluation under uncertainty, and (c) that CRADIS-based decision models are not systematically extended to the circular q-rung orthopair fuzzy environment about complex healthcare applications. Motivated by these gaps, this work aims to provide a novel Circular q-Rung Orthopair Fuzzy Combinatorial Recognition-Adaptation Incorporation with Shapley value (Cq-ROF-CRADIS) decision-support framework for infection prevention and control assessment. The proposed model incorporates advanced modeling of uncertainty along with a compromise-based ranking mechanism. These ensure stable, interpretable, and robust decision outcomes. The contribution of this work is situated at the junction of fuzzy theory, ordinal MCDM, and intelligent decision-support systems, contributing to continuous research trends regarding hybrid fuzzy-AI evaluation frameworks within health and sustainability-oriented fields.

Problem statement

The classical models of IPC evaluation are mostly deterministic or based on classical statistics, which do not handle the level of ambiguity, hesitation, or conflictive views of experts as presented in15. To overcome this, the FS theory and its modifications were introduced and extensively applied for the modeling of healthcare decision-making. However, the classical FS approaches, such as intuitionistic FS (IFS) or Pythagorean FS (PFS), provide a restricted framework on the membership and non-membership levels of the related elemental sets, making them less capable of dealing with high levels of uncertainty. The emergence of q-rung orthopair fuzzy sets (q-ROFSs) has caused immense improvements in modeling uncertainties, as there has been more flexibility in evaluating membership and non-membership levels under the q-rung condition. Even with such improvements, existing q-ROF-based decision models have exhibited relative linearity and simplicity in their structures, which might not effectively reflect the cyclical or fluctuating opinions given by experts in dynamic environments such as healthcare.

To overcome this drawback, Circular q-Rung Orthopair Fuzzy Sets (denoted as Cq-ROFSs) have an extended framework which is able to handle hesitation, ambiguity, and circular preference better. However, the use of Cq-ROFSs in hospital IPC decision models is very limited as it has not been employed extensively in decision models involving robust ordinal ranking. Additionally, compromise prioritization models like the CRADIS technique have not been developed in a circular q-rung fuzzy setting. Thus, the research gap in creating a decision-support system using Cq-ROF that can aggregate the opinions of experts on IPC strategies effectively exists and is apparent. Research gaps

Despite the significant advances in fuzzy set theory and its applications in healthcare decision-making, there are important methodological gaps in hospital IPC. Some classical fuzzy frameworks have been commonly used to model uncertainty in medical decision problems: CFSs, IFSs, and PFSs. However, when applied to IPC contexts-like the ones under consideration-characterized by simultaneously high hesitation, conflicting expert judgments, incomplete epidemiological data, and rapidly evolving clinical conditions, these approaches become inappropriate. The representation of membership and non-membership degrees in these proposals is constrained and thus, the expressive capability can be limited and unstable or biased decisions may be taken. More recent developments like q-rung orthopair fuzzy sets (q-ROFSs) and their circular extensions (Cq-ROFSs) bring higher-order uncertainty and more realistic modeling of expert hesitation. Despite this, their application in the context of hospital infection management is scant, and very few studies take proper advantage of their representational power. To be more precise, in IPC decision-making, the experts’ preferences often lead to cyclic or context-dependent relations-for instance, shifting priorities between effectiveness, cost, safety, and sustainability at times of outbreaks-yet the existing fuzzy IPC models are based on a linear and static preference structure.

Furthermore, most current research on hospital infection management is focused on statistical forecasting, machine learning models, or conventional fuzzy logic for outbreak prediction and clinical optimization. These approaches, while of great value, are mostly oriented to accurate prediction and offer limited support for transparent multi-criteria prioritization that supports hospital administrators and policy makers in choosing infection control strategies. Another important gap is that the advanced fuzzy environments are only partially integrated into compromise-based ordinal MCDM techniques. For example, methods such as CRADIS are indeed designed to provide stable and understandable rankings, but their extensions have been developed mostly within the realms of classical or basic fuzzy environments. In fact, CRADIS combined with Cq-ROFSs has not been tested in IPC decision-support systems for its potential in enhancing robustness within strongly disagreed and highly ambiguous cases.

Therefore, the lack of an integrated framework that incorporates circular q-rung orthopair fuzzy modeling with a compromise-based ranking mechanism impairs the ability of healthcare institutions to make sure-footed and multi-criteria decisions regarding infection prevention measures, resource allocation, and risk mitigation. With this gap in perspective, the present study aims to propose a Cq-ROF–CRADIS decision-support framework tailored to the complex, uncertain, and dynamic nature of hospital infection control.

Mathematical justification for selecting the circular q-rung orthopair fuzzy model

The reason why the Circular q-Rung Orthopair Fuzzy (Cq-ROF) model is chosen over other extensions, such as spherical fuzzy sets (SFS) or complex fuzzy sets (CFS), is that it has improved expressiveness and flexibility in modeling expert uncertainty in hospital infection control decision-making. The Cq-ROF model is an extension of q-rung orthopair fuzzy sets, where a circular constraint is imposed on the membership () and non-membership () degrees: This circular constraint is more flexible than the simplex constraints imposed on membership in spherical fuzzy sets (SFS) and picture fuzzy sets ), and still maintains the interpretability of the hesitation (1- -) degree.

In comparison to complex fuzzy sets, which are based on amplitude and phase information to represent uncertainty but lack the explicit quantification of hesitation, the Circular q-ROF approach not only represents membership, non-membership, and hesitation but does so in a geometrically meaningful and interpretable way. This aspect is especially important in the context of hospital infection control, where expert opinions tend to be imprecise, partially conflicting, and dynamically changing due to various clinical and operational considerations. Additionally, the q-rung parameter allows for a flexible way to represent high-order uncertainty, which improves the discriminatory power of the model in assessing competing infection control options. Geometrically, the circular constraint ensures that the feasible solution set is balanced and symmetric, which enables a fine-grained distinction of expert opinions that might not be possible with the spherical or complex fuzzy models. In conclusion, the Circular q-ROF model has the advantage of providing mathematically sound, interpretable, and highly flexible uncertainty representation, which makes it particularly well-suited to the multi-criteria and expert-driven decision-making context of hospital infection control.

Development of a circular q-ROF CRADIS approach

In this section, the proposed C-qROFCA model is presented, which is an improved multi-criterion decision-making (MCDM) model that runs in the setting of Cq-ROFS. The model has been specifically developed to deal with the fundamental uncertainty, imprecision, and cognitive hesitation that are commonly faced in the real world. Although the classical CRADIS technique provides a balanced system of ranking alternatives on the functionality of their distance to ideal or anti-ideal solution, it still falls behind in representing the fuzziness and indetermination inherent in human judgment. To fill this gap, Cq-ROFCA combines the expressive power of Cq-ROFS and the structured ranking feature of CRADIS. Individually, every evaluation here corresponds to a triplet that captures the degrees of acceptance and rejection as well as the degree of hesitation or uncertainty by the radius r. It is a more expressive representation of the expert opinion, with the intensity of its evaluation and the degree of confidence in its expression.

The Cq-ROFCA model incorporates a decision-making perspective by integrating both objective vagueness and subjective ambiguity through the inclusion of CRADIS in this more detailed framework. Evaluation methodology

To solve the problems of uncertainty, hesitation, and ambiguity in hospital infection prevention and control, the current study uses a hybrid decision-making approach by integrating Cq-ROFSs with the CRADIS approach. The applications of Cq-ROFSs advance the representation of expert opinions through more flexible modeling of membership, non-membership, and hesitation degrees in a circular geometric model, thereby better capturing complex uncertainty than standard fuzzy extensions. After the fuzzy decision matrices are formed with the help of expert ratings, they are combined and normalized under the Cq-ROFS framework to maintain consistency among a diverse group of decision-makers and criteria. Then, the CRADIS technique is used to rank the alternatives based on their effectiveness for infection prevention. CRADIS is based on analyzing each alternative’s distance from an ideal and anti-ideal solution, which are the most and least preferred infection prevention approaches, respectively. By combining Cq-ROFSs with CRADIS, the methodology not only makes the compromise ranking mathematically sound but also reflects real-world clinical uncertainties. This mixed-methods research strategy allows decision-makers to rank hospital infection control measures more accurately, thus filling the present gap in research and ensuring more efficient healthcare management.

Sensitivity analysis

To ensure the stability of the proposed Circular q-Rung Orthopair Fuzzy CRADIS (Cq-ROFCA) method, a two-step sensitivity analysis was performed and presented in Fig. 7. In the first step, equal linguistic weights were assigned to all experts (Extremely High (EH), High (H), Moderate (M), and Low (L)). The obtained utility values indicated negligible changes, which means that the ranking of infection control alternatives is not very sensitive to equal weighting of experts. This further supports the internal consistency and impartiality of the proposed approach. In the second step, a perturbation-based sensitivity analysis was carried out by gradually increasing the weight of a single expert by , , , and , while normalizing the weights proportionally. The results indicated negligible changes in the utility values, which further supports the stability and robustness of the proposed approach.

Sensitivity analysis.

It is worth noting that infection control alternatives such as (sterilization policies) and (waste disposal procedures) demonstrated relatively higher sensitivity to weight changes, with occasional changes in their ranking positions. This can be recognized to the multi-dimensional and resource-intensive nature of these alternatives, which are often characterized by trade-offs between cost, complexity, technology intensity, and compliance. Consequently, the expert opinions on these alternatives are likely to diverge more substantially depending on the individual expert’s background, experience, and institutional priorities, and hence their assessments would be more sensitive to changes in expert weights. On the other hand, alternatives such as (compliance with hand hygiene) were relatively less sensitive to any weighting scenario. This is because hand hygiene practices are universally recognized as being of primary importance and proven effectiveness in infection control, and hence there would be a substantial consensus among experts with little disagreement. Therefore, such alternatives would be more resilient to changes in expert weights. The above sensitivity analysis with expanded weights clearly confirms that the proposed Cq-ROFCA model is both stable and discriminative, being stable for consensus-driven alternatives and more sensitive to expert disagreements for more complex alternatives.

Result and Discussion

The suggested CRADIS approach coupled with Cq-ROFS was implemented on the hospital infection control case. The outcomes verified the evident priority ranking of the alternative infection control measures. Out of the alternatives under consideration, 3 (Strict sterilization protocols and periodic staff training) stood out as the best solution, followed by ₂ (Improved disinfection technology) and 5 (Real-time infection monitoring system). Conversely, 1 (Traditional cleaning procedures) and 4 (Treatment-oriented infection post-control strategies) scored relatively low based on their minimal preventive effect. The analysis shows that the effectiveness of a combination of preventive and proactive approaches is greatest in the management of hospital infections. Prevention methods like sterilization and disinfection are directed at eliminating the source of the infection, while proactive methods like monitoring in real-time and staff training maintain the system’s sustainability. This is in agreement with current literature in healthcare decision-making, which accentuates that prevention-oriented approaches are not only cost-saving but also decrease the patients’ and healthcare systems’ burden of hospital-acquired infection.

Comparison analysis

A comparative analysis with the suggested Cq-ROFCA method and some other prevalent MCDM models was conducted to test its effectiveness and robustness. The benchmark models were the conventional weighted averaging and geometric operators founded on Intuitionistic (IFWA, IFWG), Circular Intuitionistic (CIFWA, CIFWG), Pythagorean (PyFWA, PyFWG), Circular Pythagorean (CPyFWA, CPyFWG), Fermatean (FFWA, FFWG), and Circular Fermatean (CFFWA, CFFWG) paradigms. The results (Fig. 8) illustrated that the suggested CFCA performed better than current operators in all cases by acquiring greater utility values for all alternatives. It is also worth mentioning that alternatives ℇ_1 and ℇ_5 obtained the highest ratings of 0.9590 and 0.9474, respectively, under the suggested model. Conversely, the old models (e.g., IFWA: A1 = 0.4203; PyFWA: A1 = 0.4749) returned significantly lower assessments, as expected from their inability to capture high-order uncertainty, hesitation, and ambiguity in the judgement of experts. While extensions to circularity (e.g., CPyFWA, CPyFWG) enhanced performance compared to non-circular counterparts, they did not possess compromise-ranking ability and depth of interpretation inherent in the proposed framework. The robustness of the CFCA results from the stability, reasonable interval of ranking credibility, and interpretability that are especially advantageous when alternatives in decisions are highly competing. This enables more accurate representation of degrees of belief, disbelief, and uncertainty an integral feature in real-world settings where experts cannot be absolutely certain, as in educational and engineering environments for decisions. Additionally, integration of CRADIS as the decision logic strengthens the model by adding compromise ranking, facilitating nuanced distinction between alternatives. This improvement makes the model exceedingly suitable for VR-based learning centers, where students are faced with imprecise decision situations. The method ties the ranking process with smart feedback systems, thus giving learners dynamic, data-informed suggestions, making possible adaptive adjustment of scenarios and promoting tailored learning advancement.

Quantitative validation through rank correlation analysis

To quantitatively verify the effectiveness and superiority of the proposed Circular q-Rung Orthopair Fuzzy CRADIS (Cq-ROFCA) approach, a rank correlation analysis was performed to compare the ranking results of the proposed approach with those of the existing aggregation-based MCDM approaches, such as IFWA, IFWG, CIFWA, CIFWG, PyFWA, PyFWG, CPyFWA, CPyFWG, FFWA, FFWG, CFFWA, and CFFWG. Specifically, the Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient (ρ) was used to quantify the extent of consistency between the ranking results of alternatives. The calculated correlation coefficients showed a strong positive correlation with values very close to unity for most cases, which clearly indicate the high degree of consistency between the proposed approach and the existing models. However, despite the consistency, the proposed Cq-ROFCA approach demonstrated a better discrimination capability among the competing alternatives with high-order uncertainty, hesitation, and circular preference information. These findings clearly verify that the proposed approach not only preserves the stability of the ranking results but also provides statistically proven improvements in compromise ranking, thus validating the effectiveness and reliability of the proposed approach for hospital infection control decision-making. Conclusion

This study presented a Cq-ROFS-CRADIS-based approach to support decision-making in infectious control in hospitals, facing higher degrees of uncertainty and expert indecisiveness in analysis. Different from conventional fuzzy sets and ordinal MCDM using fuzzy sets, in which higher-order uncertainty was explicitly managed using circular q-rung orthopair fuzzy sets, expert indecisiveness or hesitation in analysis in different medical field decisions, i.e., in infection control, can be managed using CRADIS as well. From the empirical results, it is evident that the suggested methodology ensures a stable and differentiating ranking of the IPC strategies across various validating tests. Moreover, sensitivity analysis was performed, which asserted the stability of the final rankings under systematic variation in criteria weight vectors, thus proving the stability of the methodology in relation to any subjective biases in the assignment of importance by the expert. In addition, comparative analysis of established MCDM methods provided strong rank correlation, while exhibiting improved differentiating capabilities in relation to highly competitive alternatives, thus validating the reliability of the methodology.

Despite this advantage, some disadvantages should also be mentioned. Firstly, as is common with any expert-based system, some subjectivity may exist with reference to the specialized assessment. This becomes more important due to scarce resources likely to exist within the health environment. Secondly, as can be expected with any system that uses q-rung calculations with circular logic, computation complexity may prevent immediate utility within a hospital environment. Thirdly, this system is based solely upon static weights for criteria, thus increasing the need for it to also factor temporal risks of infections.

In conclusion, it can be determined that not only is there value in terms of practical applicability, but also that methodologically, there can be support in terms of developing such decisions, according to the Cq-ROFS-CRADIS model that has been proposed. Although the case study focuses on hospital infection control, the framework is better regarded as adaptable rather than universally transferable and should be carefully contextualized when applied to other healthcare or hospital management problems that involve structured uncertainty.

Limitations and future direction

Future directions for investigation lie in various promising paths ahead from this study. One potential path forward involves the development and implementation of various advanced techniques in the broader category of machine learning approaches in conjunction with fuzzy and ordinal forms of fuzzy MCDM in reducing dependency upon static expert knowledge inputs. Specifically, it may be possible to effectively design and employ dynamic weight learning systems using approaches such as those falling within the category of “deep learning” or “reinforcement learning” in certain scenarios. Additionally, the development of hybrid models of neural networks and fuzzy systems, called neuro-fuzzy systems, in which the neural networks are used to learn the data streams in the hospitals and provide an interpretable prioritization procedure by employing fuzzy systems, is also an important direction in the field. Advances in deep learning technologies in healthcare data analysis, such as emotion awareness in the recognition systems as well as in optimal models of smart healthcare monitoring systems, have also indicated the potential of fuzzy sets in the domain.

Following research would need to overcome these drawbacks through the use of hybrid models that combine real-time data analytics, machine learning, and sophisticated fuzzy extensions to continue reducing expert subjectivity. Gauging the framework to T-spherical fuzzy sets40, picture fuzzy sets, or complex q-rung fuzzy settings may increase its versatility in modeling higher levels of doubt and uncertainty. Additionally, longitudinal case studies in multiple hospitals will be needed to establish the generalizability of the method in the future. Integration with decision-support systems, hospital information systems, and IoT-based smart monitoring may further make the model operational for real-world implementation. Lastly, comparative studies with other MCDM methods, i.e., MARCOS, WASPAS, and CoCoSo under sophisticated fuzzy environments, would offer greater insights into the relative merits and demerits of the proposed approach. It can also be checked by using the different approaches, such as Heronian means41, Muirhead means42, or by the EDAS approach43, and the TOPSIS approach44.

Finally, a wide range of empirical validation based on data obtained at several hospitals and expert consensus across different regions may further facilitate broad applicability of this developing framework, thus enabling it to qualify both as a contribution to medical methodologies and a practical clinical tool.

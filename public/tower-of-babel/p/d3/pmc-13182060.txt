Dose–effect relationship of D90 in iodine-125 seed implantation for advanced lung cancer: gradient-based efficacy and survival outcome
Authors: Guohui Cao, Xuemin Di, Xiaoli Liu, Zeyang Wang, Ke Xu, Xiaojing Chang, Juan Wang, Hongtao Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study aimed to evaluate the dose–response relationship and survival outcomes of iodine-125 seed implantation in patients with advanced lung cancer, focusing on the impact of different dose levels on tumor control and patient prognosis.MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on 49 patients with advanced lung cancer who underwent iodine-125 seed implantation between January 2017 and March 2021. The patients were divided based on postoperative D90 dose levels, and tumor control rates, including complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease (SD), and progressive disease (PD), were assessed. The objective response rate (ORR) and disease control rate (DCR) were also evaluated. Survival analysis was performed to identify prognostic factors.ResultsThe optimal D90 cutoff values for CR, ORR, and DCR were 136.46 Gy, 121.98 Gy, and 111.47 Gy, respectively. Higher D90 doses were significantly associated with improved tumor control rates (P < 0.05). The 3-year survival rate was 20.41%, with complete response (CR) and preoperative hemoglobin levels identified as independent prognostic factors for long-term survival.ConclusionIodine-125 seed implantation demonstrates a clear dose–response relationship in advanced lung cancer, with higher D90 doses correlating with better tumor control. CR and hemoglobin levels are critical factors influencing long-term survival outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-026-15798-6.Keywords: Iodine-125 seed implantation, Lung cancer, Dose–effect relationship, D90, Survival analysis

Introduction

Lung cancer is the most common and deadliest malignant tumor worldwide, and it remains the leading cause of cancer-related deaths in China [1, 2]. Studies have shown that approximately 85% of patients with lung cancer have non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC), with surgical resection being the preferred treatment for early-stage disease [3–5]. However, a significant proportion of patients are ineligible for surgery owing to compromised heart, lung, or other organ functions [6]. Despite advancements in chemotherapy, radiotherapy, surgical techniques, and in-depth research on molecular targeted therapy and immunotherapy, the overall survival rate of patients with lung cancer has not significantly improved, with a 5-year survival rate remaining below 20% [1]. In recent years, image-guided iodine-125 seed therapy has rapidly developed domestically and internationally, becoming an essential approach in clinical cancer treatment [7, 8]. Current research on lung cancer iodine-125 seed implantation primarily focuses on its efficacy and safety. However, limited studies have explored local tumor control rates, response rates, curative rates, and survival outcomes under different doses [7–9]. This study aims to analyze the effects of different doses of iodine-125 on tumor control efficacy and survival outcomes.

Data and methods

Clinical data

This retrospective analysis included 49 patients with advanced-stage lung cancer who were admitted to our hospital between January 2017 and March 2021. All patients had experienced disease progression despite prior treatments, including radiotherapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, or other modalities (Table S1). Of these, 33 patients received seed implantation combined with systemic treatment (see Table S2 for details of the specific regimens, e.g., chemotherapy, targeted therapy, or immunotherapy), while 16 did not receive systemic treatment. Patient inclusion was based on the following criteria: (1) Pathological confirmation of lung cancer via surgery or percutaneous biopsy, with documented disease progression. The majority of patients had received prior standard antitumor therapies (e.g., external beam radiotherapy, chemotherapy); (2) Presence of a lung tumor confirmed by enhanced chest CT or PET-CT as the brachytherapy target; (3) Evaluation and confirmation by at least two associate chief physicians or higher in oncology, deeming the patient unsuitable for or refusing surgery or external beam radiotherapy; (4) Karnofsky Performance Status (KPS) score ≥ 70; (5) Expected survival time ≥ 3 months; (6) Normal platelet count and coagulation function.

In this study, the treatment target was rigorously defined as the locally recurrent or primary intra-parenchymal tumor mass (Table S3). Lymph node recurrences or metastases were not included as targets for iodine-125 seed implantation in this patient cohort. This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of our hospital.

(The detailed prior treatment history, combined systemic therapy regimens, and distribution of metastatic sites for the cohort are summarized in Supplementary Tables S1, S2, and S3, respectively.)

Materials and methods

The Prowess 3D Version 3.02 close-range treatment planning system (TPS; SSGI, Chico, CA, USA) was used in this study. The equipment included an 1820-C implantation needle (diameter 1.22 mm) and a Mick200-TPV20 cm implantation gun (Mick Radio-Nuclear Instruments, Inc., NY, USA). Radioactive iodine-125 seeds, provided by Atomic High Technology Co., Ltd., had the following characteristics: a γ-ray energy range of 27–35 keV, a radioactive activity of 0.5–0.7 mCi, and a half-life of 59.4 days. Preoperative enhanced chest CT scans were routinely performed within 1 week before iodine-125 seed implantation, and the CT scan data were transferred to the TPS to delineate the 90% dose curve of the target area, including the tumor target volume, and to calculate the number and spatial distribution of the implanted iodine-125 seeds. According to the TPS treatment plan, the prescription dose D90 (minimum peripheral dose received by the 90% target volume) was set to ≥ 80 Gy. The entire procedural protocol, from pre-operative planning to post-operative verification, was conducted in accordance with the standardized technical workflow outlined in the national expert consensus on CT-guided radioactive seed implantation for lung cancer [10]. For patients who had received prior external beam radiotherapy (EBRT), the iodine-125 seed implantation procedure was considered a re-irradiation. Specific institutional guidelines were followed to ensure cumulative doses to organs at risk (OARs) remained within safe tolerance limits. Key considerations included: (1) A minimum interval of 6 months was required between prior radiotherapy and seed implantation to allow for normal tissue repair; (2) The cumulative radiation dose to critical OARs (e.g., spinal cord, heart, lungs) was evaluated by integrating prior EBRT data with the planned brachytherapy dose using TPS modeling. The procedure proceeded only if cumulative doses to any OAR did not exceed established tolerance limits (e.g., spinal cord Dmax < 50 Gy, lung V20 < 40%); (3) The highly localized dose distribution and rapid dose fall-off of iodine-125 seeds were leveraged to minimize exposure to previously irradiated tissues. Timely CT scans were obtained during the implantation process to ensure proper positioning of the seeds according to the TPS plan. Postoperative symptomatic treatments, such as electrocardiographic monitoring, oxygen therapy, and hemostasis, were administered. Closed thoracic drainage was performed for patients experiencing a significant pneumothorax. The management of procedure-related complications, such as pneumothorax, aligned with the strategies recommended in the consensus guidelines [11].

Follow-up and efficacy evaluation

Enhanced chest CT scans were scheduled at 1, 2, 4, and 6 months after seed implantation for short-term, high-frequency monitoring, followed by scans every 2 to 3 months for regular surveillance. The follow-up protocol for local response assessment was structured to capture both short-term dynamics and long-term outcomes, and was conducted until March 31, 2024.

To comprehensively determine the overall disease status and differentiate local progression from new distant metastases, a systemic evaluation was performed at key timepoints. This included serological tests for tumor markers, contrast-enhanced MRI of the brain, contrast-enhanced CT of the abdomen, and whole-body bone scintigraphy when clinically indicated. To specifically distinguish tumor progression from post-treatment inflammatory changes at the 6-month evaluation, a multi-parameter approach was employed. This included: (1) Image Fusion and Temporal Analysis: Follow-up CT images were fused with pre-treatment CT scans using dedicated software for precise anatomical comparison, analyzing changes not only in size but also in density and internal texture; (2) Clinical Symptom Correlation: Radiological findings were consistently correlated with the patient’s clinical symptoms (e.g., cough, hemoptysis, chest pain); (3) Serological Trend Analysis: Trends in serum tumor marker levels (e.g., CEA, CYFRA 21 − 1) were monitored, where a sustained rise supported progression, while stable or decreasing levels favored inflammation.

Tumor response was evaluated based on the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors version 1.1 (RECIST 1.1), which classifies responses as follows: progressive disease (PD), stable disease (SD), partial response (PR), and complete response (CR)(Fig. 1) [12]. To ensure objectivity, all follow-up images were independently assessed by two experienced radiologists, blinded to the clinical data and D90 dose values. Any discrepancies were resolved through consensus or by a third senior radiologist. The objective response rate (ORR) was defined as (CR + PR)/total cases, and the disease control rate (DCR) was defined as (CR + PR+SD)/total cases.

Statistical analysis

SPSS 29.0 statistical software was used for data analysis. Independent t-tests were used to analyze the relationship between dose and treatment efficacy. The normality of postoperative D90 was confirmed by the Shapiro-Wilk test (W = 0.976, P = 0.416), justifying the use of parametric tests (e.g., t-test) and the presentation of data as mean ± standard deviation. The optimal cut-off values for postoperative D90 were obtained by establishing receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. Chi-square tests were used to analyze the correlation between various factors, local efficacy, and prognosis. Logistic univariate analysis was performed on clinical and pathological factors, and binary logistic regression was used for multivariate analysis to identify independent prognostic factors. Log-rank tests were used to assess the impact of clinical and pathological factors. Furthermore, Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was performed to identify independent factors influencing the 3-year survival rate, with P < 0.05 indicating statistical significance. Complete response (CR), objective response rate (ORR), and disease control rate (DCR) were analyzed to provide a comprehensive, multi-level assessment of the initial tumor response, thus capturing the spectrum of treatment effects from complete eradication to disease stabilization. To account for the potential confounding effect of metastatic burden on overall survival, subgroup analyses were performed based on factors such as the presence of regional lymph node metastasis and distant metastasis. In addition, a post hoc statistical power analysis was performed for the primary efficacy endpoints. For complete response (CR), the post hoc power of the chi-square test was 0.96 (α = 0.05, N = 49).

Results

Baseline characteristics and outcomes

A total of 49 patients with advanced lung cancer who underwent iodine-125 seed implantation were included in this final analysis. The cohort comprised 41 males (83.7%) and 8 females (16.3%), with a median age of 61 years (range, 41–90). Pathological examination confirmed that the majority of patients had non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) (39/49, 79.6%), including 26 cases (53.1%) of squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) and 13 cases (26.5%) of adenocarcinoma (ADC). The remaining patients were diagnosed with small-cell lung cancer (SCLC) (6/49, 12.2%) or other types (4/49, 8.2%).

Regarding the initial treatment outcomes, at 6 months postoperatively, 14 cases (28.57%) showed CR, 18 cases (36.73%) showed PR, 12 cases (24.49%) showed SD, and 5 cases (10.20%) showed PD. The ORR was 65.31% (32/49), and the DCR was 89.8% (44/49). The median OS was 18 months, and the 3-year survival rate was 20.41% (10/49).

D90 and prognostic analysis

The average postoperative D90 for all patients was 123.32 ± 29.06 Gy. At 6 months after treatment, the average postoperative D90 was 139.15 ± 19.21 Gy in patients with CR, 127.56 ± 29.91 Gy in those with PR, 111.02 ± 21.33 Gy in those with SD, and 93.26 ± 36.56 Gy in those with PD. Postoperative D90 and complete response (CR)

To quantify the relationship between delivered dose and the highest order of response, we compared postoperative D90 values between patient groups. A statistically significant difference was observed in postoperative D90 between the CR group and the non-CR group (t = 2.546, P = 0.014) (Table 1). ROC analysis was performed to assess the relationship between postoperative D90 and treatment efficacy (CR group vs. non-CR group), yielding an area under the ROC curve (AUC) of 0.747 (95% CI: 0.604–0.890; P = 0.001) (Fig. 2). The optimal cut-off value for postoperative D90 was 136.46 Gy. Based on this threshold, patients were divided into high postoperative D90 and low postoperative D90 groups. At 6 months after treatment, the CR rate was 55.56% (10/18) in the high postoperative D90 group and 12.90% (4/31) in the low postoperative D90 group, with a statistically significant difference (χ2 = 10.15, P = 0.001).

ROC curve of seed implantation CR and postoperative D90

From the univariate analysis, postoperative D90 (χ2 = 10.15, P = 0.001) was identified as a significant influencing factor for the postoperative efficacy of seed implantation. Binary logistic regression analysis further confirmed that postoperative D90 was an independent predictor for CR (OR = 8.437, 95% CI: 2.075–34.301, P = 0.003). (The detailed univariate analysis of factors associated with CR is provided in Supplementary Table S4.) However, age, gender, pathological type, tumor maximum diameter, combined treatment, hemoglobin levels, and tumor staging showed no statistically significant differences.

Postoperative D90 and objective response rate (ORR)

There was a statistically significant difference in postoperative D90 between the effective and ineffective groups (t = 3.398, P = 0.001) (Table 1). The ROC analysis for ORR yielded an AUC of 0.770 (95% CI: 0.636–0.904; P = 0.002) (Fig. 3), indicating good predictive ability. The optimal cut-off value for postoperative D90 was 121.98 Gy. Based on this threshold, patients were divided into high postoperative D90 and low postoperative D90 groups. At 6 months after treatment, the ORR was 85.71% (24/28) in the high postoperative D90 group and 38.10% (8/21) in the low postoperative D90 group, with a statistically significant difference (χ2 = 12,01, P = 0.001).

ROC curve of seed implantation effectiveness and postoperative D90 in participants

From the univariate analysis, postoperative D90 (χ2 = 12.01, P = 0.001) and tumor maximum diameter (χ2 = 6.689, P = 0.01) were identified as influencing factors for the postoperative efficacy of seed implantation. However, age, gender, pathological type, combined treatment, hemoglobin levels, and tumor staging showed no statistically significant differences. To further evaluate these significant factors, a binary logistic regression analysis was performed. The results demonstrated that postoperative D90 (OR = 7.17, 95% CI:1.68–30.069.68.069, P = 0.008) was an independent prognostic factor for local efficacy after seed implantation in lung cancer. (See Supplementary Table S5 for the complete univariate analysis of factors associated with ORR.)

Postoperative D90 and disease control rate (DCR)

A statistically significant difference was observed in postoperative D90 between the DCR and PD groups (t = 2.581, P = 0.013) (Table 1). ROC analysis was performed to assess the relationship between postoperative D90 and postoperative efficacy (effective group vs. ineffective group), yielding an AUC of 0.773 (95% CI:0.592–0.953; P = 0.048) (Fig. 4). The optimal cut-off value for postoperative D90 was 111.47 Gy. Based on this threshold, patients were divided into high postoperative D90 and low postoperative D90 groups. At 6 months after treatment, the DCR was 96.77% (30/31) in the high postoperative D90 group and 77.78% (14/18) in the low postoperative D90 group, with a statistically significant difference (χ2 = 4.485, P = 0.034).

ROC curve of seed implantation DCR and postoperative D90 in participants

We then proceeded to identify factors significantly associated with disease control.​ Univariate analysis revealed that postoperative D90 (χ2 = 4.485, P = 0.034) and tumor maximum diameter (χ2 = 5.045, P = 0.025) were significant influencing factors for the postoperative efficacy of seed implantation. However, age, gender, pathological type, combined treatment, hemoglobin levels, and tumor staging showed no statistically significant differences. To further evaluate these significant factors, a binary logistic regression analysis was performed. The results showed that neither postoperative D90 (OR = 5.24, 95%CI:0.49–56.72, P = 0.173) nor maximum tumor diameter (OR = 0.17, 95%CI:0.02–1.77, P = 0.137) retained independent statistical significance for DCR. This apparent contradiction with the univariate analysis is likely attributable to a significant negative correlation between the two variables (Pearson’s r= −0.480, P < 0.001), indicating multicollinearity; that is, larger tumor size is associated with a lower achieved D90 dose, and their effects on DCR could not be separated in the multivariate model.

(The full univariate analysis for DCR is available in Supplementary Table S6.)

Survival analysis

The follow-up for this survival analysis was censored on March 31, 2024. Among the 49 patients included, one patient (2.0%) was lost to follow-up at 39 months postoperatively. In the Kaplan-Meier analysis, the patient’s data were censored at the date of their last known confirmation of being alive (39 months postoperatively). It is important to note that this loss to follow-up occurred after the 3-year time point; therefore, it does not affect the calculation of the 3-year survival rate (20.41%) reported in this study. In the exploratory analysis of shorter-term survival, the 1-year and 2-year overall survival (OS) rates were 65.31% (32/49) and 36.73% (18/49), respectively. Univariate analysis identified several factors associated with survival at these earlier time points, including age, tumor maximum diameter, preoperative hemoglobin levels, Pathological type, various D90 cutoff values, ORR, and CR (Table 2).

To investigate the potential heterogeneity of the dose effect, we performed an interaction analysis between pathological type and postoperative D90 dose on survival. The results showed no statistically significant interaction (P for interaction = 0.84), indicating that the beneficial association between higher D90 dose and survival was consistent across different pathological types. We further explored this relationship within the non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) subgroup (n = 39). There was no significant difference in the 3-year survival rate between patients with squamous cell carcinoma (SCC, n = 26) and adenocarcinoma (ADC, n = 13) (χ2 = 1.638, P = 0.201). However, the achieved postoperative D90 was significantly higher in ADC patients than in SCC patients (141.50 ± 19.85 Gy vs. 114.31 ± 31.88 Gy; t = 2.805, P = 0.008). Notably, among the patients who achieved 3-year long-term survival, the D90 dose did not differ significantly between SCC and ADC patients (127.40 ± 30.76 Gy vs. 142.57 ± 11.03 Gy; t = 0.929, P = 0.389).

Complications

In addition to efficacy and survival, the safety profile of the procedure was assessed. A total of 25 patients (51.02%, 25/49) developed pneumothorax after particle implantation. Among them, 19 required closed thoracic drainage, and all recovered and had their tubes removed after 3 days. Statistical analysis showed no significant correlation between the occurrence or severity of pneumothorax and the postoperative D90 dose (P > 0.05), indicating that this procedure-related risk was independent of the radiation dose level within the tumor. There were four cases of bleeding, including one case of intrathoracic bleeding and three cases of blood in sputum. All cases were successfully managed with symptomatic hemostatic treatment, and symptoms resolved within 2 days. No patients experienced significant postoperative bleeding. One patient reported pain at the puncture site after surgery, which improved after symptomatic pain relief treatment. No complications such as tumor metastasis, air embolism, or radiation pneumonitis were observed.

Discussion

Lung cancer is the most common and deadliest type of cancer in China [1, 2]. Nevertheless, effective local treatment methods are crucial for improving the symptoms and prognosis of patients with lung cancer [13]. In recent years, iodine-125 seed implantation has been demonstrated to be a safe and effective form of brachytherapy for tumor control [7, 8, 11]. Stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT) remains a standard for inoperable early-stage NSCLC, with recent systematic reviews highlighting its efficacy but also risks, such as bronchial necrosis in central tumors (“no-fly zone”) [13, 14]. In contrast, iodine-125 seed implantation (RSBT) offers a minimally invasive brachytherapy alternative with steep dose gradients and reduced organ-at-risk exposure, as endorsed by Chinese guidelines [11]. Emerging evidence supports RSBT’s feasibility and safety in lung cancer [7, 15], underscoring the need for direct comparisons with SBRT in future trials. Although not in NCCN lung cancer guidelines, iodine-125 seed implantation is an established therapy in China via expert consensus, and its efficacy is recognized in guidelines for other malignancies like prostate cancer [10, 11, 16]. The radiation dose delivered to the tumor plays a significant role in both tumor control and the development of complications. However, previous studies have analyzed either the safety and complications of treatment or local efficacy through statistical analysis, without conducting in-depth evaluations of the relationship between tumor control dose and treatment efficacy. This study retrospectively reviewed 49 cases of advanced lung cancer and analyzed the correlation between dose and efficacy from three dimensions of treatment response. The study established a corresponding relationship between different dose levels and tumor control efficacy. Additionally, a survival analysis was conducted to assess relevant influencing factors, yielding meaningful research results.

According to our study, in the treatment of lung cancer with iodine-125 seed implantation, the impact of the D90 dose cutoff value on efficacy indicators showed a gradient difference: the dose cutoff values for DCR, ORR, and CR were 111.47 Gy, 121.98 Gy, and 136.46 Gy, respectively. The fact that the achieved postoperative D90 (mean 123.32 Gy) exceeded the prescription dose (≥ 80 Gy) reflects clinical dose escalation for optimal coverage, while the observed variability, particularly the lower doses in the PD group, may be influenced by tumor location, procedural factors, and seed distribution characteristics. Furthermore, this study identified a significant negative correlation between the maximum tumor diameter and postoperative D90 (r= −0.480). The multicollinearity arising from this relationship explains the lack of independent significance for both variables in the multivariate model for predicting DCR; however, this does not negate their clinical value, as the univariate analysis confirmed both as significant factors. The analysis indicated that D90 is an independent influencing factor for ORR and CR (both P < 0.05), and within a certain dose range, the tumor control rate significantly increased with the increase in D90 dose. Furthermore, the test for interaction between pathological type and D90 was not significant (P = 0.84), suggesting that the survival benefit from a higher dose was consistent across different pathological subtypes. However, our exploratory subgroup analysis revealed that adenocarcinoma patients achieved a significantly higher postoperative D90 than squamous cell carcinoma patients (141.50 vs. 114.31 Gy, P = 0.008), while their long-term survival rates were comparable. Notably, among 3-year survivors, the D90 levels converged. This indicates that the pathological subtype may indirectly influence prognosis by affecting the technical feasibility of achieving optimal dose coverage (e.g., due to factors such as tumor depth and proximity to critical structures), rather than representing a fundamental difference in radiosensitivity. The key clinical implication is that overcoming these technical challenges to deliver a sufficient dose is paramount, irrespective of histology.

The complete response (CR) threshold identified in our study (136.5 Gy) aligns closely with the pivotal 140 Gy dose reported by Ji et al. [17], which was associated with significantly improved 3-year local control. This convergence underscores approximately 140 Gy as a critical dose window for achieving a deep tumor response. In contrast, our disease control rate (DCR) threshold (111.5 Gy) is notably lower than the median D90 of 143.4 Gy and the exceptional DCR of 92.86% achieved by Chen et al. [18] in a specific cohort of patients with stage III NSCLC after chemoradiotherapy failure, who were treated with seed implantation combined with bronchial arterial chemoembolization. This discrepancy likely arises from differences in both the study populations (a homogeneous, refractory stage III cohort versus our heterogeneous, advanced-stage cohort) and the local treatment modalities (combined therapy versus implantation alone). Meanwhile, a separate study by Ji et al. [19] suggested that targeting a gross tumor volume (GTV) D90 ≥ 150 Gy might further improve prognosis in early-stage NSCLC, illustrating the potential for dose escalation across different disease stages. Therefore, the dose thresholds reported across different studies must be interpreted with careful consideration of differences in patient populations and treatment contexts. Future prospective studies conducted in more homogeneous cohorts are warranted to validate and refine these dosimetric objectives.

Furthermore, it is important to acknowledge that D90 is a composite parameter influenced by multiple procedural and technical factors, including seed activity, spatial distribution, and tumor characteristics. Our TPS-based dosimetry protocol, applied pre-, intra-, and post-operatively, was designed to control these variables and ensure dose delivery met the prescribed plan. However, the D90 derived from a single post-implant CT represents a static measurement and may not fully capture dose variations over time due to tumor regression or seed displacement—a key consideration in dynamic dose verification. Therefore, investigating D90 dynamics will be an important focus of our future research. This includes exploring complementary dosimetric parameters, evaluating time-integrated metrics, and employing advanced techniques, such as 3D-printed templates, to enhance implantation precision and dosimetric reliability [13].

Our study found that in the 3-year survival analysis, short-term local tumor control is closely related to the survival outcomes of patients, with CR identified as an independent influencing factor for the 3-year survival rate. It is important to note that the CR assessment, based primarily on size reduction according to RECIST 1.1 criteria, might potentially overestimate the true complete response rate due to difficulties in radiologically distinguishing post-radiation fibrosis from residual viable tumor in some cases. In addition, hemoglobin levels and pathological type were confirmed as important prognostic factors affecting survival. This result is consistent with multiple studies, as both Di and Martínez-Monge et al. confirmed that the efficacy of local tumor control is an important factor influencing patient outcomes [20, 21]. The research of Huo et al. further confirmed that a pre-treatment hemoglobin level ≥ 120 g/L is associated with a longer median survival time (28 months). Their study also identified tumor volume and postoperative D100 as independent influencing factors for survival [22].

While results are partially consistent with our study, there are some notable differences. Our study further confirmed, from the perspective of local control, that CR is an independent influencing factor for the 3-year survival rate, suggesting that the thoroughness of local tumor eradication is closely related to improved long-term prognosis. Mechanistically, achieving thorough local tumor control (e.g., CR) may ultimately translate into survival benefits through various pathways, such as reducing tumor burden, lowering the risk of recurrence, and improving patient immune function.

In conclusion, our study defines gradient D90 cut-off values for different levels of tumor response in advanced lung cancer treated with I-125 seed implantation, and establishes CR achieved with a sufficient radiation dose as a key determinant for long-term survival. These findings provide a practical framework for individualized dose planning. Nevertheless, the interpretation of the CR rate should be cautious, considering the potential challenge in differentiating fibrotic changes from active tumor on follow-up imaging. It should be noted thatthe inclusion of various histological types, while reflective of real-world practice, may introduce heterogeneity; however, subgroup analyses did not show significant differences in the primary dose-response outcomes. Future research should focus on larger sample sizes, multicenter studies, and longer follow-up durations to further verify this conclusion. Furthermore, future studies should aim to incorporate time-to-event analyses, such as local control rates assessed by the Kaplan-Meier method, to better capture the durability of treatment response. Finally, the generalizability of these findings should be considered in the context of the study’s retrospective design and the specific selection of patients for whom iodine-125 seed implantation was deemed the most appropriate local therapy.

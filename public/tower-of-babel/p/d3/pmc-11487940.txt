Monitoring the safety of the adjuvanted human papillomavirus vaccine, HPV-16/18-AS04: Protocol for a cohort study using electronic health records in Yinzhou, China
Authors: Junting Yang, Sarah Welby, Zhike Liu, Siwei Deng, Guangxu Liu, Ruogu Meng, Yu Yang, Yixin Sun, Yunkun He, Ning Jiang, Zhenhua Wu, Keruo Liu, Dominique Rosillon, Catherine Cohet, Siyan Zhan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThis protocol was developed to conduct population-wide surveillance of the bivalent HPV-16/18-AS04-adjuvanted human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccine in terms of uptake and safety outcomes including potential immune-mediated diseases (pIMDs) and pregnancy-related outcomes in China. The study will use electronic health records from 2010 to 2020 from the Yinzhou Regional Health Information Platform and include a population-based cohort of female permanent residents aged 9–45 years. Baseline and follow-up periods will be defined according to the 2017 introduction of HPV-16/18-AS04 in China. Patients with pIMDs and pregnancy-related outcomes will be identified using primary diagnostic codes from outpatient and inpatient electronic health records. A time trend analysis will be performed to describe the incidence of pIMDs and pregnancy-related outcomes during baseline and follow-up periods by age and overall, per calendar year. Depending on the number of safety outcomes and the size of the study population, incidence in vaccinated and unvaccinated cohorts will be compared using a Poisson regression model for pIMDs and a logistic regression model for pregnancy-related outcomes, adjusting for age and calendar year. Methods and findings from this study will provide valuable experience for designing future real-world studies and complement observations made during clinical trials and post-marketing studies.Study registration: http://www.chinadrugtrials.org.cn/index.html: CTR20210523.KEYWORDS: HPV-16/18-AS04, human papillomavirus vaccine, safety, potential immune-mediated diseases, pregnancy outcomes

Introduction

Worldwide, cervical cancer is one of the most frequent cancers in women, with 604,127 new cases and 341,831 deaths estimated in 20201. In China, cervical cancer is the third most common cancer in women aged 15–44 years, with an incidence rate of 15.6 per 100,000 women, and a mortality rate of 8.37 per 100,000 women (2020).2 Human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccination is a proven effective preventative measure and is widely recognized as an effective tool to prevent HPV infection and cervical malignancy.3 A systematic review of real-world studies from 14 high-income countries reported that after 5–8 years of introduction of HPV vaccines into immunization programs, there was an 83% reduction in HPV16/18 prevalence among females aged 13–19 years and a 66% reduction in women aged 20–24 years.3 The World Health Organization (WHO) recommends the inclusion of HPV vaccines in national immunization programs, endorsing the efficacy and effectiveness of HPV vaccines for cervical cancer prevention.4 The WHO has set “90-70-90” targets for 2030; 90% of girls fully vaccinated by 15 years of age; 70% of women screened by age 35 and again by 45; and 90% of women with cervical disease receive treatment.5

In 2023, five HPV vaccines are approved for use in China (see Appendix 1 for details): quadrivalent HPV-6/11/16/18 (Gardasil, Merck Sharp & Dohme Limited), nonavalent HPV vaccine (Gardasil 9, Merck Sharp & Dohme Limited, HPV-6/11/16/18/31/33/45/52/58); a domestic bivalent HPV-16/18 vaccine (Cecolin, Xiamen Innovax); a domestic bivalent HPV-16/18 vaccine (Walrinvax, Walvax), and bivalent HPV-16/18 AS04-adjuvanted vaccine (HPV-16/18-AS04, Cervarix, GSK). AS04 is an adjuvant system containing 50 µg 3-O-desacyl-4′-monophosphoryl lipid A adsorbed on aluminum salt (500 µg Al3+). HPV-16/18-AS04 was the first HPV vaccine granted approval in China (2016) and was initially approved for use in girls and women aged 9–25 years. Subsequently, the approval was broadened in 2018 to encompass women up to 45 years of age.

Numerous clinical trials conducted in China6–9 and internationally10–12 have confirmed the acceptable safety profile of HPV-16/18-AS04, showing no significant difference in the incidence of adverse events following immunization, such as the new onset of chronic diseases, potential immune-mediated diseases (pIMDs), and pregnancy-related outcomes, between vaccinated and unvaccinated subjects. However, in clinical trial settings, the short duration of observation and restricted sample size make it challenging to detect safety signals for rare events. Post-marketing safety surveillance is important to supplement clinical trial evidence of vaccine safety, and a timely response to identify concerns is important to maintain public and regulatory confidence in vaccines. This, in turn, mitigates vaccine hesitancy and can enhance vaccine coverage.

Comprehensive and ongoing safety assessments in countries where the HPV-16/18-AS04 has been included in national immunization programs have not identified any safety issues.12–14 However, there is a paucity of real-world safety data from China.

In China, a number of high-quality data platforms have been established, such as the Yinzhou Regional Health Information Platform (YRHIP), which is being used for chronic disease management and pharmacoepidemiology research.15,16 In response to WHO’s “90-70-90” goals, the National Health Commission of China launched a pilot program in December 2020 in 15 cities with the aim of improving HPV vaccine coverage. The Yinzhou district program aims to achieve 90% uptake of HPV vaccination in girls aged 9–14 years by the end of 2025.17 Our study aims to construct a population-based cohort using the YRHIP, thereby meeting China’s National Medical Products Administration regulatory requirement to monitor the safety of HPV-16/18-AS04 and provide real-world evidence that can contribute to optimizing China’s HPV vaccine immunization strategy. Our study design differs from other studies based on secondary use of data by employing a stepwise approach to mitigate for potential study limitations, such as uncertain exposure and rates of outcome events.

Objective

The study objectives are as follows: (1) to assess the performance of our case identification method for the detection of five pIMDs of interest (autoimmune thyroiditis, type 1 diabetes mellitus, rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, and idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura), and two pregnancy-related outcomes of interest (spontaneous abortion, live infant congenital anomaly); (2) to estimate the pre-launch incidence rates of these events among girls and women aged 9–45 years, and to measure HPV-16/18-AS04 uptake in this population; (3) to estimate the incidence of the most common pIMDs and pregnancy outcomes identified under objective 1 during the pre- and post-launch periods (primary objective); (4) to estimate the probability of occurrence of the most common pIMDs and pregnancy-related outcomes identified in objective 1 within 12 months following vaccination in vaccinated vs unvaccinated subjects, and during the pre- vs post-launch period in unvaccinated subjects, conditional on vaccine uptake and observed incidences.

Study design and methods

Data source and quality control

The YRHIP is a unique integrated electronic health information system in Yinzhou district established in 2005 by the Yinzhou District Centers for Disease Control to facilitate routine primary care services for local general practitioners. YRHIP stores electronic health records (EHRs) of the population of Yinzhou District of Ningbo City, Zhejiang Province that included 976,409 permanent residents with valid healthcare identified at the end of 2019.18 Since its creation, this database platform has been gradually integrated with information on public health surveillance, population screening, disease management, health information systems in hospitals, and other healthcare services including HPV vaccinations (from October 2017). Since 2009, YRHIP covers nearly all healthcare-related activities of residents from birth to death. A feasibility assessment identified this data source as suitable for post-licensure HPV vaccination safety and effectiveness studies in China.19

Within YRHIP, this study will access the health archives database, inpatient and outpatient EHRs, vaccination records, death register, and maternal and birth records (Box 1).

Study design and population

Study design

This is an observational cohort study conducted in a real-world setting to monitor safety-related outcomes before and after the launch of HPV-16/18-AS04 in China (Figure 1). The study population will include females aged 9–45 years registered in the YRHIP between January 1, 2010, and December 31, 2020. The study comprises a 7-year pre-launch period from January 1, 2010, to December 31, 2016, a 3-year post-launch period from January 1, 2018, to December 31, 2020, and an intermediate period from January 1, 2017, to December 31, 2017. HPV-16/18-AS04 was first available in China in July 2017, and in Ningbo city in December 2017. The intermediate period covers a period of anticipated low overall vaccine coverage during the period immediately after approval. It ensures continuous monitoring and will allow assessment of potential over- or underestimation of incidence rates due to too few vaccinated subjects. If the number of vaccinated subjects in the intermediate period is considered negligible (<5% of unvaccinated persons during the pre- or post-launch periods), those data will be pooled with those of the pre-launch period. Additionally, we will assess the probability of pIMDs in the male population aged 9–45 years as a parallel group before and after the launch of AS04-HPV-16/18 to account for time-varying confounding and20 to evaluate time-trend related biases (e.g., improvements in diagnostic capabilities) that may affect the study results.

Follow-up

Subjects will be followed until the death date, the date of their 46th birthday, the last visit date before they were lost to follow-up, or the end of the study period (December 31, 2020), whichever occurs first. The occurrence of pIMDs will be monitored for 12 months after the last HPV-16/18-AS04 vaccination date (Day 1), which is in line with the recommended 6–12-month follow-up period for pIMD outcomes in vaccination studies.21 Additionally, we will also conduct sensitivity analyses with follow-up periods of 6 and 24 months to ensure the robustness of our results. Pregnancy outcomes will be monitored until 6 months after the expected end of the pregnancy. No time limitations will be placed on the prior pregnancy history.

Study population

All permanent residents registered in the YRHIP during the study period will be considered for inclusion in the study. Female and male permanent residents aged between 9 and 45 years will be included if they have ≥12 months of continuous registration in the YRHIP database during the study period (defined by ≥2 observations recorded in YRHIP ≥12 months apart). There was an administrative division adjustment in Yinzhou District in 2016; therefore, for subjects registered in the Yinzhou District before 2016, the later date between their registration date in the YRHIP database and January 1, 2010, will be considered the initial registry date. For subjects who were assigned to Yinzhou District after the division adjustment, the later date between the registration date in the database and January 1, 2017, will be considered the initial registry date. For the pre-launch period, the cohort entry date was the latest of: 12 months after initial Yinzhou database registration, the subject’s ninth birthday, or January 1, 2010. For the post-launch period, it was the latest of 12 months after initial Yinzhou database registration, the subject’s ninth birthday, or January 1, 2018.

Exposure, outcome, follow-up, and covariates

Exposure

Females who receive ≥1 dose of HPV-16/18-AS04 will be considered exposed. Vaccination status will be identified based on the Chinese vaccine name (“Cervarix”), the manufacturer (GSK), the batch number or vaccine code (B473, B474, and B475). Each individual dose will be evaluated separately.

For all pIMDs, risk windows after any dose of HPV-16/18-AS04 will be defined as the time from the vaccination date (Day 1) until 12 months after HPV-16/18-AS04 vaccination or the end date of follow-up. If the interval between two consecutive doses is less than 12 months, it will be considered as a continuous exposure period.

Pregnant women who receive HPV-16/18-AS04 within 60 days prior to conception (or the first day of the last menstrual period) until the end of pregnancy will be considered exposed.

The HPV-16/18-AS04 vaccination date of vaccinated females will correspond to the index date or the commencement of 12 months of pIMDs follow-up for unvaccinated subjects.

Outcomes

Subjects with prespecified outcomes will be identified by primary diagnostic code from the outpatient and inpatient EHRs in the YRHIP, based on International Classification of Diseases version 10 (ICD-10) codes; a complete list of pIMDs and pregnancy outcomes chosen to encompass the majority of pregnancy-related events that require attention21,22 is provided in Appendix 2. For pIMDs, the onset date will be the first diagnosis date of the outcome. The risk of including prevalent cases will be mitigated by excluding subjects with a pIMD code during the 12-month period before the onset date. A 12-month washout period is a common practice supported by literature.23

Based on post-marketing experience24–28 and optimal approach,21 we selected five pIMDs of interest; autoimmune thyroiditis, type 1 diabetes mellitus, rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, and idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura, and two pregnancy-related outcomes of interest: spontaneous abortion and live infant congenital anomaly to validate the accuracy of the case identification method. Potential cases of each outcome in the YRHIP identified by the ICD-10 codes will be confirmed by independent medical record review by two experts according to internationally recognized guidelines in China.

Algorithms with positive predictive values (PPV) ≥70% will be included in the main analysis.29 For algorithms with PPV < 70%, the case identification method will be fine-tuned to include other data types (such as disease terms in Chinese, specific medications and/or examination, and/or procedures, and/or narratives) in addition to ICD-10 codes. If the performance of the case identification method cannot be improved, then the specific pIMD or pregnancy outcome will not be included in the main analysis.

Covariates

Age will be defined as the calendar year minus the birth year. Baseline socio-demographic characteristics (including age and sex) will be described and used as stratified variables.

Statistical analysis

Analysis of demographic and baseline characteristics

Means, standard deviations, medians, and ranges will be presented for continuous variables. Frequencies and percentages will be provided for categorical variables. The number of missing observations will be described for each variable. All analyses will be performed using SAS version 9.2 or higher.

Stepwise analysis of objectives

The uncertain lack of a priori evidence on the incidence rates of the outcomes under study are all potential study limitations. Therefore, the analysis will be conducted in a stepwise approach to validate the case identification method and establish baseline incidence rates of adverse events before estimating incidence rates or performing comparative analyses. The stepwise analysis approach will enable us to adapt the study design based on the results generated in the initial steps.

Step 1: Database performance for identification of safety-related endpoints

Cases of 5 selected pIMDs and 2 selected pregnancy outcomes (as described in the ‘Outcomes’ section) identified by ICD-10 codes will be assessed against cases identified using these and other data from the YRHIP. PPVs with exact 95% confidence intervals (CIs) using a binomial distribution will be calculated for each endpoint by estimating the proportion of confirmed cases among those identified as cases by the ICD-10-based algorithm.

Step 2: Measurement of HPV-16/18-AS04 vaccination uptake in females (post-launch) and assessment of baseline (pe-launch) incidence of safety-related endpoints

HPV-16/18-AS04 uptake will be calculated among female subjects aged 9–45 years by age at first dose, calendar year, vaccine type, and number of administered doses.

For each pIMD selected in Step 1, the baseline incidence rate will be expressed as the total number of new pIMD episodes divided by the sum of the number of person-years, with an exact Poisson 95% CI. Baseline incidence rates will be calculated overall, by age and per calendar year.

For each pregnancy-related outcome, baseline incidence during the pre-launch period will be computed as the percentage of pregnancies with the pregnancy outcome, together with exact 95% CI. Baseline incidence will be calculated for pregnancies overall, by age, and per calendar year.

Step 3 (Primary objective): Pre- and post-launch incidences of the most common pIMDs and pregnancy outcomes

Using the same methods described in Step 2, the incidence rates of common pIMDs (to be defined based on the findings) will be computed for the pre- and post-launch periods in females and in males, and pregnancy-related outcomes identified in Step 1 will be computed for the pre- and post-launch periods in females.

For each pregnancy outcome, pre- and post-launch incidence of the pregnancy outcomes incidence will be expressed as the number of pregnancies with the given outcome divided by the total number of women at risk, together with an exact Poisson 95% CI, by age and overall, per calendar year.

Step 4: Comparative analysis

If ≥5 cases of any pIMD are observed within 12 months after HPV-16/18-AS04 vaccination, incidence in vaccinated and unvaccinated females will be computed. Incidence rates will be calculated for all pregnancy-related outcomes in exposed and unexposed women.

If the sample size is sufficient to detect a relative risk of at least 2, probabilities and their 95% CIs will be calculated. A Poisson regression model adjusted for age and other possible covariates (e.g., calendar year) will be used to estimate associations between HPV-16/18-AS04 vaccination and pIMDs, and a logistic regression model (similarly adjusted) will be used for pregnancy-related outcomes.

Discussion

HPV-16/18-AS04 was launched globally in 2007, and there is now a substantial accumulation of clinical and post-marketing data confirming its acceptable safety profile.13,30–32 However, few clinical trials of limited sample sizes have reported on the safety profile of HPV-16/18-AS04 in Chinese women.6–9 The vaccine was approved by the National Medical Products Administration in China in July 2016. Consequently, HPV-16/18-AS04 use within China has been relatively short. The present study is designed to address the Chinese National Medical Products Administration request to evaluate HPV-16/18-AS04 impact in China.

This study is one of the few to use real-world data from population-wide HPV monitoring in China. We will use real-world data from the YRHIP integrated healthcare database, selected after a review of the literature, expert consultation, feasibility assessment, and consultation with the local Center for Disease Control.19 Due to its size and population coverage, the YRHIP makes it possible to investigate the incidence of rare outcomes unlikely to be observed in clinical trials. The YRHIP covers more than 98% of permanent residents in the Yinzhou District. This, and the use of individually linked data contained in health records captured from birth to death from all hospitals and most health and medical organizations in the area, is expected to allow acceptable completeness of capture of adverse events of interest.

Potential limitations in the data, including uncertainty about HPV-16/18-AS04 uptake and little or no baseline information on rates of specific safety-related endpoints necessitated a stepwise analysis approach. Database performance in terms of case identification and assessment of baseline adverse event rates will establish the appropriateness of the database to meet the study objectives. Relative risks in vaccinated (exposed) versus unvaccinated (unexposed) women will only be estimated if sufficient numbers of events and a large enough sample size are available. The strengths of the stepwise design are that it can mitigate the limitations of the available data and may allow for adaptation of the study design based on findings at each step.

Other potential limitations of the study are those inherent to secondary use of healthcare data in observational studies. Incorrect/incomplete coding can lead to underestimation of outcomes. Furthermore, as the study population is limited to the Yinzhou area, the data may not be representative of the general Chinese population of females aged 9–45 years. The probability of pIMDs may be increased in some age groups (for example, during adolescence) and mistakenly associated with vaccination if temporally associated. Therefore, it was decided to consider the occurrence of pIMDs in a parallel, unexposed group, i.e., the male population aged 9–45 years, to investigate time trends. However, no direct comparison is to be made between male and female groups as autoimmune conditions are significantly altered by gender.33 Additionally, one limitation of our study is the potential for unmeasured confounding, which is a common challenge in real-world evidence research.34

Unlike clinical trials, real-world data sources can provide insights into vaccine use in populations who may never be part of a clinical trial and can contribute to a more comprehensive picture of the vaccine safety profile.35–37 The methods for population-wide design, outcome validation, data linkage, and multi-step statistical analyses from this study could help inform the design of other studies using healthcare databases in China.

In summary, this study aims to build on the available experience using HPV-16/18-AS04 in routine clinical practice and will provide safety information specifically in the Chinese population. These data will contribute to our understanding of the safety profile of HPV-16/18-AS04 in real-world use.

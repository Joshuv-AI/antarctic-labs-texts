Longitudinal assessment of the progression of severe keratoconus based on corneal topography
Authors: XiaoDi Yang, YiRan Wang, YuYing Liu, Yong Lyu, WeiQun Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe aimed to assess long-term disease progression in patients with severe keratoconus (KC). Clinical records of 125 patients (201 eyes) with severe KC followed-up for > 12 months were retrospectively analyzed. From these, 28 patients (31 eyes) were included. Corneal topography parameters evaluated included thinnest corneal thickness (TCT), maximum keratometry (Kmax), anterior and posterior mean corneal radii of 3 mm (aKM, pKM), steep keratometry, and KC screening indices. All patients wore rigid gas permeable contact lenses (RGPCLs) for an extended period. The median patient age and follow-up period were 20 (interquartile range [IQR] 17–22) years and 25 (15–38) months, respectively. Compared to baseline, the aKM, Kmax, and KC screening indices on the anterior corneal surface were reduced at the final follow-up (P < 0.05). No changes were observed in RGP-corrected visual acuity, TCT, pKM, or KC screening indices on the posterior corneal surface. The higher the baseline value, the greater the reduction in aKM and Kmax. Five patients (16%) experienced disease progression during follow-up. Patients with severe KC showed reduced anterior corneal surface curvature and no change in corneal thickness during an average follow-up period of 2–3 years while wearing RGPCLs.Keywords: Keratoconus, Retrospective study, Rigid gas permeable corneal contact lensSubject terms: Corneal diseases, Refractive errors, Eye manifestations

Introduction

Keratoconus (KC) is an eye disease characterized by corneal ectasia and thinning. It is accompanied by increased irregular corneal astigmatism and decreased vision, which affects a patient’s quality of life1,2. KC mostly occurs in young adults in their 20 s and is asymmetric and progressive3. The disease progression varies in different age groups and may be rapid, slow, continuous, or intermittent until it stabilizes4–7.

Corneal topography is an important tool in diagnosing KC and determining the severity of the disease1. Depending on the degree of corneal ectasia and visual impairment, patients with mild KC require glasses for vision correction, while those with moderate-to-severe KC require contact lenses or scleral lenses to correct their vision. In contrast, patients with severe KC, who cannot wear scleral lenses, require corneal transplant surgery8. The emergence of corneal cross-linking (CXL) has enabled the management of KC progression. This is beneficial because early diagnosis is crucial in controlling progression and improve prognosis9. Despite the emergence and advancement of corneal tomography equipment and intelligent algorithms based on big data, which have enabled the early identification and diagnosis of KC, some patients do not seek medical treatment until a very severe stage due to neglect of symptoms or other reasons10. Moreover, patients with severe KC may lose the opportunity to undergo CXL because corneal thickness < 400 µm is a contraindication for this procedure11. Additionally, epidemiological surveys have shown that the prevalence of KC is higher in Asians than in other ethnic groups8.

Therefore, understanding disease progression in KC can help physicians formulate effective treatment plans. However, long-term changes in the course of severe KC remain uncertain. Herein, we aimed to evaluate patients initially diagnosed with severe KC based on longitudinal changes in corneal topography parameters to better understand disease progression in these patients.

Final corneal topography measurements in 30 eyes with KC were obtained after RGPCL removal within 1 h; in one eye, it was obtained at the follow-up visit 6 months after the loss of the lens. The follow-up time between baseline and final corneal topography was 12–63 months, with a median of 25 (interquartile range [IQR] 15–38) months. The final comparison with baseline showed a decrease in anterior mean corneal radii of 3 mm (aKM), steep keratometry (Ks), and maximum keratometry (Kmax), but no change in RGPCL-corrected visual acuity (RGPVA), posterior mean corneal radii of 3 mm (pKM), or thinnest corneal thickness (TCT). Among the KC screening parameters, frontal surface curvature map (Sif), keratoconus vertex front (KVf), and Baiocchi–Calossi–Versaci index of front (BCVf) of the anterior corneal surface were reduced, whereas the parameters related to the posterior corneal surface remained unchanged (back surface curvature map [Sib], keratoconus vertex back [KVb], and Baiocchi–Calossi–Versaci index of back [BCVb]). The results indicated that higher baseline values of aKM and Kmax were associated with greater reductions during the follow-up period. Discussion

In this retrospective study, we analyzed the corneal topographic data of patients initially diagnosed with severe KC over the past 6 years, with a median followed up of 25 months, to observe changes in disease course. All patients were RGPCL wearers with no history of ocular surgery, including corneal collagen cross-linking. This study is unique because, to our knowledge, there are no previous studies on long-term course changes in patients with severe KC.

Common KC shapes are oval and nipple, while a small number of patients also have global KC13,14. However, in this study, a unique corneal morphology was observed in patients with severe KC. The corneal topography of these patients (22.6%; n = 7) showed a relatively flat center of the cone and steep mid-peripheral area, appearing as a “ring-shaped” cone. All patients had corneal scars, which can also be observed on corneal tomography images. A similar corneal morphology has not been reported in previous studies on KC. We believe that this may be a particular corneal morphology associated with corneal scarring in patients with severe KC.

SIRIUS uses the Scheimpflug Placido imaging technology, which is reproducible and reliable for the diagnosis of KC 15,16. In the present study, a comparison of the baseline and final corneal topography data revealed that the anterior corneal surface curvature (Ks, aKM, and Kmax) of the entire cohort became smaller, and the anterior corneal surface became flatter. However, no difference was observed in the average curvature of the central 3 mm of the posterior corneal surface between the baseline and final comparisons. Romero-Jiménez et al17. reported that short-term (14 days) use of RGPCL can affect corneal morphology and flatten the cornea in patients with KC. Similarly, another study demonstrated that the anterior corneal surface curvature of patients with KC decreased, but the posterior surface curvature remained unchanged after wearing an RGPCL for 1 month18. Conversely, Araki et al19. found no difference in the anterior and posterior corneal surface curvatures between the baseline and final comparison in patients with mild-to-moderate KC wearing RGPCL during an average follow-up of 5–6 years. The discrepancies in these results may be related to different observation subjects and follow-up times.

Hwang et al20. reported that, compared to patients with mild-to-moderate KC, the curvature of the anterior corneal surface of patients with severe KC decreased significantly after wearing RGPCL. Similarly, our regression analysis results showed that the initial Kmax and aKM were positively correlated with the final decline. SIRIUS screening indices including the symmetry index of Sif, KVf, BCVf, Sib, KVb, and BCVb, are important indicators for diagnosing KC16. In this study, the baseline and final comparisons showed that Sif, KVf, and BCVf (representing the front surface of the cornea) decreased, while Sib, KVb, and BCVb (representing the posterior surface of the cornea) remained unchanged. This is consistent with the analysis demonstrated that the curvature of the anterior corneal surface decreased, and the curvature of the posterior surface remained unchanged.

RGPCLs for all patients in the present study were fitted using the three-point contact method. As shown in Fig. 2, the RGPCL was in direct contact with the apex of the cone, which is one of the possible reasons for the flattening of the anterior surface curvature observed on corneal topography. However, during the average follow-up of > 2 years, it is unknown whether the corneal flattening in these patients was temporary or permanent, and whether it was the sole effect of RGPCL or the combined effect of changes in the course of the disease.

In addition to corneal curvature, corneal thickness is an important observation in patients with KC. Corneal topography in patients with KC wearing RGPCL for a short period of time (14 days) showed an increase in TCT17. Hwang et al20. also found a reduction in TCT in patients with KC wearing RGPCL after almost 2 years of follow-up; however, the difference was not statistically significant. In contrast, Akiyama et al18. found no change in TCT after wearing RGPCL for 1 month in patients with KC. Similarly, the corneal topography of patients with severe KC who wore RGPCL for an extended period in this study exhibited no change in TCT. Thus, the results regarding changes in corneal thickness, which are related to the length of follow-up, sample size, and severity of KC, are inconsistent.

Longitudinal studies on KC progression have demonstrated that several patients stabilize after exhibiting curvature changes typical of KC in corneal morphology21. Wang et al9. conducted a comparative study on patients who did not undergo CXL as well as those who underwent CXL and progressed during an average follow-up of 31 months. They found that the corneal parameters of patients in the no-CXL group were stable and showed no progression. Similarly, statistical analysis in our study revealed that the entire cohort exhibited reduced anterior surface corneal curvature and no change in thickness, indicating that patients aged ≤ 30 years with severe KC had stable disease without progression while wearing RGPCL.

However, according to the assessment criteria for KC progression specific to each individual, only a limited number of patients (16.1%; n = 5) progressed during the follow-up period. Moreover, KC progression is often difficult to predict. Ferdi et al.6 reported that patients with KC who were aged < 17 years and had Kmax > 55 D were more likely to experience disease progression. Similarly, another study demonstrated that children with severe KC are more likely to experience disease progress than adults22. Notably, all patients with severe KC included in our study were young adults and children, aged ≤ 30 years, which is consistent with the results of the previously mentioned study to a certain extent. However, the survival analysis in our study showed no influencing factors related to the progression of KC. This may be because the Kmax of the included patients with severe KC was much greater than 55 D, and the severity of KC differed from that of patients observed in other studies.

It is generally accepted that RGPCLs do not appear to affect the progression of KC1,10,20. However, a previous study demonstrated that pancorneal RGPCLs could stabilize KC better than non-pancorneal ones23. Notably, among the five patients with progression in our study, the most rapid and severe case (case 3) was a patient with KC in both eyes. The more severe eye met the inclusion criteria and was included in the study. During follow-up, the RGPCL of the more severely affected eye was lost; therefore, wear was suspended. The corneal topography at the follow-up examination 6 months later showed significant progress, and the other eye continued to wear the RGPCL without progress. It is unknown whether KC progression in this case was related to the discontinuation of RGPCL wear. We believe that whether RGPCL will delay the progression of KC or whether different types of RGPCL will have different effects on KC progression, especially in patients with KC who wear RGPCL for an extended period, requires further research.

Due to the strict inclusion criteria, which required patients to be newly diagnosed with severe KC and not have undergone any eye surgery, the number of observed cases in this study was limited. The corneal topography of patients with KC who wore RGPCL for an extended period after suspending RGPCL use for 7 days showed an increase in corneal curvature and a decrease in thickness24. Thus, it is evident that the RGPCL affects corneal topography in patients with KC. However, in this study, the corneal topographies were collected within 1 h of RGPCL removal, which was a limitation of the present study. Nonetheless, because patients with severe KC have poor naked-eye or glasses-corrected vision, most rely on RGPCL for daily life and work, making it difficult or even impossible to stop using RGPCL before reviewing corneal topography. Therefore, disease progression in patients with severe KC is inevitably affected by RGPCL. Therefore, the RGPCL itself is part of disease progression in patients with severe KC who have been diagnosed and have received treatment. This makes it more difficult to collect severe keratoconus cases without RGPCL wear as a control group for research, which is a limitation of this study. Patients with severe KC due to severe corneal irregularity can only rely on RGPCL or scleral lenses for vision correction8,25. Scleral lenses have recently been approved in China; thus, patients with severe KC now have more choices26. However, whether RGPCLs and scleral lenses have varied effects on KC requires further investigation. Additionally, the long-term course changes and final outcomes of patients with severe KC, such as the probability of receiving corneal transplantation, require continued long-term follow-up observation.

In conclusion, this study demonstrated that during an average follow-up of > 2 years, patients newly diagnosed with severe KC wore RGPCL for vision correction and exhibited an overall stable condition, However, a minority (16%) continued to show progression. Thus, our results can provide valuable information to both patients with severe KC and ophthalmologists regarding the progression of KC, enabling more effective management strategies for patients with severe KC.

Methods

This retrospective clinical chart series was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University (approval number: 2024-KY-0020-002) and conducted in accordance with the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was waived by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University for this study because only deidentified retrospective records data and images without any identifying marks were used. The medical records of patients with KC who visited the optometry clinic of the First Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University between December 2016 and December 2022 were reviewed. The inclusion criteria were: patients (1) diagnosed with Stage 4 (mean central K readings > 55 D) KC at the initial consultation according to the Amsler–Krumeich classification; (2) who had a follow-up period > 12 months; (3) who underwent at least two corneal topography examinations with an interval of ≥ 12 months between the first and the last examination, with all corneal topography tests performed using the same equipment and software; and (4) with no history of ocular surgery.

The following metrics were collected from medical records and corneal topography results: baseline and final RGPVA, first and final corneal topography TCT, Kmax, aKM, pKM, Ks, astigmatism, and KC screening indices. The KC screening indices included the symmetry index of the Sif, KVf, BCVf, symmetry index of the Sib, KVb, and BCVb.

RGPVA was measured using a standard logarithmic visual acuity chart, and decimal visual acuity was converted to logMAR visual acuity. KC progression was determined based on the following criteria: increase in astigmatism ≥ 1 D/year, and/or increase in Ks ≥ 1 D/year, and/or decrease in TCT ≥ 25 µm12.

Corneal topography was performed using SIRIUS (CSO, Scandicci, Italy), and RGPCL (Menicon E-1; Menicon, Nagoya, Japan) fitting was performed using the three-point contact method.

Statistical analyses

Continuous variables are expressed as median (interquartile range [IQR]). Baseline and final differences were compared for all variables using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. aKM, Ks, and Kmax changes in the number of relevant influencing factors were analyzed using multivariate linear regression analysis. Factors influencing cone progression were tested using the Cox regression analysis. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (version 27.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

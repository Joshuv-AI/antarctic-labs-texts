Research trends and cellular targets of NF-κB pathway in acute lung injury [2005–2024]: a combined bibliometric analysis and systematic review
Authors: Jie Shen, Xia Shen, Fan Ping, Bingyue Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB) pathway is a key regulator of inflammation and immune responses in acute lung injury (ALI). Despite significant progress in elucidating the molecular and cellular mechanisms of the NF-κB pathway in ALI, current research is scattered across different disciplines, experimental models, and cell types. Therefore, this study aims to integrate bibliometric trends with an in-depth review of molecular mechanisms to characterize publication trends, collaboration networks, and evolving research themes over the past two decades.MethodsLiterature related to the NF-κB pathway in ALI published between 2005 and 2024 was systematically retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database. Bibliometric analysis was performed using CiteSpace, VOSviewer, and the bibliometrix R package to comprehensively analyze publication trends, countries and institutions, authors and journals, most impactful articles, and keyword co-occurrence in this field. In addition, a mechanistic review was integrated to summarize the cell type-specific roles of the NF-κB pathway in macrophages, epithelial cells, and endothelial cells.ResultsA total of 916 articles were included, authored by 4,446 individuals and affiliated with 984 institutions and 302 journals, distributed across 46 countries and regions. The most prolific author, institution, country, and journal were Ware LB, Jilin University, China, and International Immunopharmacology, respectively. Highly cited analysis indicated that the most influential research focused primarily on NF-κB-mediated intercellular interactions, cell death, and drug modulation. High-frequency terms including ALI, NF-κB pathway, inflammation, oxidative stress, and apoptosis were identified, indicating core research hotspots in the field. Cluster and temporal analyses further highlight Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) signaling, endothelial and macrophage regulation, barrier dysfunction, and NF-κB targeted interventions as key and evolving research themes in ALI. From a cellular perspective, the NF-κB pathway regulates macrophage polarization and the barrier integrity of pulmonary endothelial and epithelial cells.ConclusionsThis study demonstrates that research on the NF-κB pathway in ALI has received increasing attention over the past two decades. Our results indicate that future research should focus on time- and cell-specific therapeutic strategies to modulate the NF-κB pathway, thereby optimizing the treatment of ALI.Keywords: Nuclear factor-κB pathway (NF-κB pathway), acute lung injury (ALI), bibliometric, mechanistic analysis

Introduction

Acute lung injury (ALI) is an acute hypoxic respiratory failure characterized by damage to alveolar epithelium and pulmonary capillary endothelium (1,2). As the inflammatory response persists and alveolar barrier function deteriorates further, it progresses to acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS). A large-scale study spanning 50 countries and 459 intensive care units (ICUs) reported an ICU mortality rate of approximately 35.3% for ALI/ARDS patients (3). Current ALI treatment primarily relies on respiratory support and pharmacological interventions, yet lacks specific drugs capable of directly reversing the pathological process (4). While mechanical ventilation improves oxygenation, it may induce ventilator-induced lung injury (VILI), further exacerbating pulmonary tissue damage. Glucocorticoids are widely used for their anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects (5). However, long-term administration carries risks of immunosuppression and secondary infections.

Nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB) is a transcription factor that regulates the expression of immunoglobulin κ light-chain genes and plays a central role in numerous biological processes, including immune responses, inflammatory reactions, cell proliferation, and survival (6). Clinical studies indicate that NF-κB activation markers are detected in the lung tissue of over 95% of patients with severe ARDS (7). At rest, NF-κB binds to its inhibitory protein, inhibitor of κBα (IκBα), and localizes to the cytoplasm. Upon stimulation by inflammatory mediators such as tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin (IL)-1β, or pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs), IκBα is phosphorylated and degraded by the inhibitor of κB (IκB) kinase (IKK) complex (8). NF-κB is then released and translocated to the nucleus, where it induces the expression of downstream inflammatory mediators. Increasing evidence confirms that macrophages, as the most critical cells in innate immunity, jointly regulate alveolar-capillary barrier permeability and repair processes through inflammatory mediators, oxidative stress, and intercellular communication (9,10). In ALI, persistent pathogen infection and inflammatory mediators stimulate alveolar macrophages (AMs) to secrete proinflammatory cytokines, which locally chemotax and activate neutrophils. Neutrophils release oxidants, proteases, leukotrienes, and platelet-activating factor (PAF). Increased vascular permeability, disrupted oxidative balance, and damage to alveolar epithelial cells and the alveolar-capillary membrane collectively promote the development of ALI (Figure 1). Despite substantial progress in elucidating the molecular and cellular mechanisms of the NF-κB pathway in ALI, existing research is highly fragmented across different disciplines, experimental models, and cell types. Currently, we still lack a systematic understanding of the knowledge structure, research evolution, and emerging hotspots in this field.

Bibliometric analysis provides a quantitative and visualized approach to map the intellectual landscape of a research field, identify influential contributors, uncover thematic evolution, and reveal potential research gaps, which cannot be achieved through traditional narrative reviews alone. Bibliometrics is a discipline that employs mathematical and statistical methods to explore the distribution structure, quantitative relationships, and changing trends of literature (11). This study utilized bibliometric analysis to systematically analyze the core literature, research networks, keyword co-occurrence, and emerging hotspots of the NF-κB pathway in ALI. We present this article in accordance with the BIBLIO reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2025-1-2601/rc).

Methods

Data collection and screening

Our bibliometric analysis was conducted using the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC; Clarivate Analytics, Boston, MA, USA), a widely recognized data source in bibliometric and scientometric research. This database offers comprehensive coverage of high-quality scholarly journals and detailed citation records, supporting robust quantitative and visualization-based analyses. In bibliometric and systematic literature research, selecting appropriate search fields is crucial for balancing precision and comprehensiveness. Using the WoSCC field tags title (TI), abstract (AB), and author keywords (AK) as screening criteria can improve the rigor and relevance of the retrieved dataset (12,13). Following extensive deliberations within our research team and consultations with senior experts in literature retrieval, we employ the following retrieval strategies: TI OR AB OR AK = (“acute lung injury” OR “Lung Injuries, Acute” OR “Lung Injury, Acute”) AND (“Nuclear Factor kappa B” OR “NF kappaB” OR “NF-κB” OR “NF kB” OR “Nuclear Factor-Kappab”). All English search terms were defined with reference to medical subject headings (MeSH) terminology and authoritative English publications.

The time span was set from January 1, 2005, to December 30, 2024, reflecting the rapid expansion and evolution of scientific inquiry in NF-κB-related ALI research over the past two decades. Inclusion criteria were as follows: (I) publications closely related to the NF-κB pathway in ALI; (II) document type: original articles and reviews; (III) language: English; and (IV) publication year: 2005–2024. Exclusion criteria were as follows: (I) articles deemed irrelevant to the research topic after comprehensive review; (II) conference abstracts, editorials, correspondence, and corrections; and (III) duplicate records. To avoid discrepancies and citation deviations caused by continuous database updates, all searches were completed on January 12, 2025, following standard bibliometric practice. A total of 916 records meeting the eligibility criteria were exported as “full record and cited references” in plain text (.txt) format and stored as download.txt. Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed using the ‘convert2df’ function of the bibliometrix package (version 4.1.0) in the R programming environment to convert the data into a structured data frame. Bibliometric analysis was then conducted to examine annual publication trends, author distribution patterns, country and institutional contributions, journal productivity, and keyword and topic dynamics. Visualization tools such as VOSviewer (version 1.6.19) and CiteSpace (version 6.3.R1) were used to construct collaboration networks, co-occurrence networks, and co-citation networks, detect emerging keywords, and generate temporal evolution graphs. Additionally, Scimago Graphica (version 1.0.44.0) was used for advanced network and geographical visualization of collaborative relationships.

Results

An overview of publications

We screened the WoSCC database for publications from January 1, 2005, to December 30, 2024, identifying 916 publications by 4,446 authors from 46 countries, published in 302 journals. The majority of these publications were research papers (n=900), while review articles constituted a smaller proportion (n=16) (Table S1). These publications were cited a total of 25,907 times, with an average of 29.11 citations per publication.

Analysis of keywords

A co-occurrence analysis of keywords related to the NF-κB pathway and ALI was conducted from 2005 to 2024 using CiteSpace (Figure 6A). The network comprised 458 nodes and 689 connections, with a network density of 0.0066. Among the 44 keywords with a frequency ≥25, the most frequently occurring terms were “acute lung injury” [663], “NF-κB pathway” [530], “inflammation” [486], “LPS” [279], “ARDS” [219], “oxidative stress” [172], and “apoptosis” [111] (Table S2). The findings indicate that NF-κB serves as the pivotal transcriptional hub linking upstream injurious stimuli to downstream inflammatory amplification, redox imbalance, and regulated cell death.

Keyword cluster analysis revealed that keywords were grouped into 17 distinct clusters, each represented by a unique color (Figure 6B). The results demonstrated high reliability, with a modularity Q of 0.8012 and a weighted mean silhouette of 0.9185. Among these clusters, cluster #0 “acute lung injury”, cluster #4 “toll-like receptor 4”, and cluster #7 “endothelial cells” emerged as the largest and most central, forming the mechanistic backbone of NF-κB in ALI research. Cluster #4 “TLR4” and cluster #6 “inflammatory factors” constitute the upstream innate immune activation module, capturing how NF-κB is triggered by damage-associated signals. In contrast, cluster #7 “endothelial cells”, cluster #13 “epithelial sodium channel”, and cluster #1 “protein” represent a barrier-disruption module, highlighting NF-κB mediated dysfunction of endothelial junctions, epithelial transport proteins, and alveolar fluid clearance. This illustrates how NF-κB drives vascular leakage and epithelial collapse. Finally, clusters such as #5 “Helminthostachys zeylanica”, #8 “anti-inflammatory activity”, #9 “inhibition”, and #12 “rosmarinic acid” constitute a therapeutic intervention module, focusing on natural compounds and pharmacological inhibitors targeting NF-κB pathway. Their peripheral yet highly connected positions suggest growing translational interest in NF-κB-directed therapeutic strategies.

Keyword temporal analysis reveals (Figure 6C) that core high-frequency terms from 2005 to 2015, such as NF-κB, pulmonary inflammation, cytokine production, reflected a focus on inflammatory initiation. Between 2016 and 2019, research shifted toward mechanistic exploration involving AMs, autophagy, and early reparative responses. From 2020 to 2022, keywords including inflammatory response, cell death, survival, and therapy clustered tightly, indicating increasing attention to NF-κB orchestrated cell-fate transitions and therapeutic interventions. From 2023 to 2024, persistent emphasis on “AMs”, “endothelial cells”, “inflammasome”, and “autophagy” reflects deepening interest in cell-type-specific regulation and cross-talk, while the emergence of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) related terms marks the pathway’s relevance to viral lung injury.

The strategic thematic map (Figure 6D) further organizes NF-κB pathway in ALI research into four quadrants, highlighting the evolution from foundational mechanistic studies toward cell-type-specific and translational investigations. Mature themes are centered on endothelial dysfunction, driven by NF-κB mediated adhesion molecule upregulation, oxidative stress, and junctional disassembly, reflecting the most consolidated research axis in the field. Transversal mechanistic themes, including autophagy, apoptosis, and vascular endothelial (VE)-cadherin signaling, integrate insights across epithelial, endothelial, and macrophage biology, illustrating cross-cutting regulatory pathways. Emerging themes emphasize oxidative-stress modulation and macrophage polarization, marking rapidly evolving frontiers in NF-κB mediated cell fate regulation. Niche themes capture specialized topics such as NF-κB and NOD-like receptor family pyrin domain containing 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome interactions, apoptosis pathways, and the influence of COVID-19-related ALI, reflecting focused but growing research directions.

Discussion

General information

Bibliometric studies indicate that the regulatory role of the NF-κB pathway in ALI has become a research hotspot in the field of lung injury (Figure 7). Its development trend aligns with the overall research on ALI (14,15). From the publication trend perspective, the number of studies was relatively low from 2005 to 2010, with research primarily focused on LPS-induced or ischemia-reperfusion models. These studies confirmed the key role of NF-κB in the expression of inflammatory cytokines and alveolar-capillary barrier disruption, preliminarily validating its potential as a therapeutic target. Between 2011 and 2015, the publication volume increased significantly, accompanied by a notable rise in total citations. Keyword clusters such as “NF-κB pathway”, “oxidative stress”, “apoptosis”, and “coagulation disorder” emerged as high-frequency nodes, indicating a shift in research focus from single inflammatory mechanisms to multidimensional pathological networks involving an imbalance of inflammation, oxidative stress, apoptosis, and coagulation (16-18). The period from 2016 to 2020 marked the peak of NF-κB and ALI research. High co-occurrence keywords, such as dexamethasone, IκB, COVID-19, and endothelial cells, reflect a shift in focus toward drug intervention and clinical translation. The glucocorticoid dexamethasone suppresses NF-κB activity by increasing IκBα expression (19), thereby alleviating severe inflammation and exerting anti-inflammatory effects. However, it intensifies the risk of infection due to excessive immunosuppression under non-hypoxic conditions or high-dose suppression. The COVID-19 pandemic acted as an external driver, accelerating clinical research and elevating citation frequency for related studies (20). From 2021 to 2024, publication volume remained high while citation counts declined. Keyword evolution paths indicate a gradual shift in research focus from NF-κB activation and inflammation toward endothelial cells, AMs, and anti-inflammatory activity, reflecting the academic community’s growing emphasis on the spatiotemporal-specific regulatory mechanisms of NF-κB across different lung cell types.

The participating countries and institutions are highly concentrated, with Chinese researchers making outstanding contributions, accounting for nearly 80% of the articles. The scientific collaboration network reveals a China-centered structure with the United States as a core partner. Although the United Kingdom, Germany, and Iran have relatively low publication volumes, their individual papers exhibit high citation impact, suggesting that the research in these countries may lean toward foundational studies or publications in high-impact journals. Collaboration among domestic universities, particularly Zhejiang University, Tzu Chi University, and the National Defense Medical Center, is the most robust. Authors Ware LB, Bhatia M, and Rubenfeld GD, originating from different countries, maintain close collaborative ties. Ware LB and Rubenfeld GD focus their work on clinical phenotype stratification and prognostic prediction (21,22), while Bhatia M’s research leans toward inflammatory mechanisms in ALI (23). Highly cited studies consistently demonstrate that sustained NF-κB activation controls the intensity and duration of inflammation, thereby determining the severity of lung injury. Prolonged retention of NF-κB in the nucleus drives the continuous transcription of pro-inflammatory mediators, exacerbating leukocyte recruitment, endothelial barrier disruption, and alveolar epithelial damage (17,24). Furthermore, the excessive NF-κB pathway interacts with cellular stress responses, including mitochondrial oxidative stress, autophagy dysregulation, and inflammasome activation, further promoting macrophage polarization imbalance, epithelial cell apoptosis, and pyroptosis (25). In summary, these findings highlight NF-κB as a critical hub connecting inflammatory activation, cellular stress responses, and tissue damage in ALI, providing a solid mechanistic basis for targeted therapeutic interventions.

Regulation of ALI by the NF-κB pathway from a cell-type perspective

Macrophage

When ALI occurs, M1 macrophages first sense exogenous PAMPs or endogenous damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), activating the IKK complex via the TLR4/myeloid differentiation primary response 88 (MyD88) pathway (26). This process, mediated by the p65 (RelA)/p50 heterodimer, facilitates the release and nuclear translocation of NF-κB, thereby initiating the transcription of inflammatory mediators, including TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2). Previous studies have indicated that hyperglycemia and the chemokine C-C motif chemokine ligand 5 (CCL5) enhance M1 polarization through the NF-κB/signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT)1/STAT6 and PI3K/AKT pathways, inducing the expression of molecules such as IL-8, monocyte chemotactic protein-1 (MCP-1), and intercellular adhesion molecule 1 (ICAM-1), which further promote neutrophil recruitment and alveolar-capillary barrier disruption (27). However, as injury signals persist, macrophages shift toward M2 polarization in response to stimulation by T helper 2 (Th2)-derived IL-4 and IL-13. NF-κB activity is negatively regulated by the IL-10/STAT3 axis, with IκB expression up-regulated to suppress proinflammatory signals and promote the release of the anti-inflammatory factor transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β), thereby initiating tissue repair and reconstruction processes. Notably, NF-κB is not completely silenced but maintains repair signaling at lower activity levels. For instance, NF-κB can be reactivated by self-secreted IL-1β, leading to the expression of VE growth factor (VEGF), which supports vascular regeneration and repair in injured areas. Additionally, regulatory factors such as cannabinoid receptor 2, protein kinase C-binding protein 1 (PICK1), and homeobox-containing protein 1 (HMBOX1), among others, can promote M2 polarization by suppressing NF-κB and its downstream inflammatory signaling pathways. This action upregulates the peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor γ (PPARγ) (28), thereby mitigating lung injury and accelerating repair.

During the acute phase of inflammation, NF-κB activates the NLRP3 inflammasome pathway, inducing macrophages to undergo pyroptosis and release large amounts of pro-inflammatory mediators, thereby amplifying the local immune response. Sustained NF-κB activation and excessive inflammatory signaling exacerbate oxidative stress, leading to mitochondrial structural damage and dysfunction. In turn, results in the overproduction of mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (mtROS) and the release of mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) into the cytoplasm, which act as endogenous DAMPs (29). These signals directly activate the NLRP3 inflammasome, triggering the activation of caspase-1 and the maturation and secretion of IL-1β and IL-18, thereby exacerbating inflammatory damage. However, once oxidative stress accumulation peaks, NF-κB shifts from a pro-inflammatory to a self-limiting regulatory mode. Studies indicate that (30) transient reactivation of NF-κB not only maintains pro-inflammatory factor expression but also directly binds the sequestosome 1 (SQSTM1) gene promoter, inducing its transcriptional upregulation. As a key autophagy adaptor protein, SQSTM1/p62 recognizes damaged mitochondria modified by ubiquitination, interacts with microtubule-associated protein 1 light chain 3, and directs them to autophagosomes for degradation. As mitochondrial homeostasis is restored, oxidative stress levels within macrophages decrease, NF-κB activity gradually diminishes, and the cellular phenotype transitions from pro-inflammatory M1 to anti-inflammatory/repairing M2.

Epithelial cells

Damage to alveolar epithelial cells is a significant cause of impaired gas exchange and persistent hypoxemia (31). Alveolar epithelial cells primarily consist of alveolar epithelial type I (AT1) and alveolar epithelial type II (AT2) cells, with AT1 cells participating in gas exchange and AT2 cells responsible for secreting pulmonary surfactant protein (SP; A/B/C/D) and maintaining alveolar fluid clearance. AT2 cells are particularly critical in the development of ALI. Their reduced numbers or dysfunction disrupts alveolar surface tension regulation, impairs epithelial sodium channel (ENaC) and Na+/K+-adenosine triphosphatase (ATPase) function, thereby diminishing alveolar fluid clearance capacity and exacerbating local inflammation (32). During the persistent inflammatory phase of ALI, prolonged NF-κB pathway suppresses the expression and activity of alveolar epithelial ion channels. This blocks the differentiation of AT2 cells into AT1 cells, delaying epithelial repair and barrier reconstruction. Knockout of IKKβ, a kinase in the classical NF-κB pathway, in epithelial cells leads to reduced AT2 cell numbers, increased apoptosis, decreased VEGF expression, and delayed alveolar septum development (33). Persistently elevated NF-κB activation in AT2 cells triggers inflammation, increased epithelial apoptosis, and delayed tissue repair. Treatment with its inhibitor, pyrrolidine dithiocarbamate (PDTC) (34), significantly reverses this injury phenotype.

Excessive activation of the NF-κB pathway is a key mechanism leading to disruption of the alveolar-capillary barrier. It undermines the integrity of intercellular junctions by downregulating and dysregulating tight junction proteins and adhesion junction proteins between endothelial and epithelial cells, ultimately triggering pulmonary edema. Under physiological conditions, NF-κB maintains tight junctions and barrier function; conversely, complete inhibition of NF-κB results in weakened barrier function. Research by Clark et al. (35) demonstrated that pharmacological inhibition of the canonical NF-κB pathway reduces the stability of the alveolar epithelial tight junction protein claudin-18, leading to increased epithelial permeability. In ALI animal models, activation of the NF-κB pathway was significantly correlated with decreased expression or abnormal localization of tight junction proteins such as claudin-5, occludin, zonula occludens-1 (ZO-1), and VE-cadherin (36). This disruption directly increases permeability between endothelial and epithelial cells, forming the structural basis for pulmonary edema. Further animal and cellular study (37) confirmed that moderate reduction of the p-p65/p65 ratio and attenuation of IκBα degradation significantly improved the expression and membrane localization of claudins, occludin, ZO-1, and VE-cadherin/β-catenin, thereby restoring barrier integrity. In vitro models demonstrated that TNF-α stimulation of human dermal microvascular endothelial cells (HDMECs) resulted in the cleavage of claudin-5 and the disassembly of the actin cytoskeleton (38). In contrast, moderate inhibition of NF-κB activity blocked this process.

During acute inflammation, NF-κB resists Fas-mediated apoptosis by upregulating anti-apoptotic proteins such as FLICE-inhibitory protein (FLIP) and B-cell lymphoma 2 (Bcl-2), thereby preserving alveolar barrier structure. In the absence of NF-κB, epithelial cells become more susceptible to apoptosis induced by factors such as TNF-α, resulting in barrier impairment and secondary inflammation (39). However, under chronic stimulation or persistent inflammation, sustained NF-κB pathway may induce receptor-interacting serine/threonine-protein kinase (RIPK)1/RIPK3-mediated necrotic apoptosis (40), thereby exacerbating lung tissue injury.

Endothelial cells

Endothelial cells provide an anti-adhesive, anticoagulant, semipermeable barrier to restrict inflammatory cell passage, maintain blood flow, and allow minimal fluid filtration (41,42). Activation of the NF-κB pathway promotes the upregulation of adhesion molecules, including ICAM-1, vascular cell adhesion molecule 1 (VCAM-1), and E-selectin, on the surface of endothelial cells. Subsequently, the massive release of reactive oxygen species (ROS), proteases, and inflammatory mediators directly damages endothelial cells. Multiple natural compounds mitigate endothelial permeability, myeloperoxidase (MPO) activity, and inflammatory cell infiltration by inhibiting the TLR4-NF-κB pathway and activating the nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2) pathway. A study (43) indicates that knockout of IKKβ, a key kinase involved in NF-κB activation within endothelial cells, leads to disruption of the alveolar capillary plexus structure, impaired alveolarization, reduced proliferation of pulmonary tissue cells, and decreased vascular density.

During the repair phase, hypoxia-inducible mitotic factor-activated NF-κB upregulates the expression of VEGF receptor 2 (VEGFR2) in endothelial cells, promoting angiogenesis and endothelial repair; pulmonary capillary endothelial cells exhibit significantly increased tissue factor (TF) expression upon stimulation by inflammatory mediators, such as TNF-α and IL-1, disrupting the balance between coagulation and anticoagulation (44). This leads to fibrin deposition within alveoli and the formation of microthrombi. This TF upregulation correlates positively with ALI severity and serves as a critical indicator for disease assessment (45). P-selectin expression is also NF-κB-regulated, promoting leukocyte and platelet adhesion to endothelial cells. By upregulating plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1) expression, NF-κB enhances platelet activation and aggregation, further exacerbating the coagulation imbalance. Studies on aspirin treatment for ALI demonstrate that it reduces PAI-1 and thromboxane A2 expression by inhibiting NF-κB activity, thereby decreasing platelet aggregation and lung injury (46). This confirms NF-κB’s pivotal role in the procoagulation process. Additionally, NF-κB promotes the coagulation cascade by upregulating molecules such as PAF and prothrombin activator. In the late phase of ALI, as the inflammatory response subsides, the regulatory function of NF-κB shifts from promoting coagulation and inhibiting fibrinolysis to promoting fibrinolysis and tissue repair. Activation of the non-canonical pathway (RelB/p52) is a crucial mechanism for the late-phase regulation of NF-κB. Research (47) indicates that the RelB/p52 complex activates during the late inflammatory phase, potentially participating in regulating vascular repair and restoring the fibrinolytic system.

NF-κB activation upregulates nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) Oxidase expression, while ROS generated by NADPH oxidase (NOX) further activates NF-κB, creating a vicious cycle that leads to massive ROS accumulation and severe oxidative stress (48). Clinical and translational studies have demonstrated that elevated oxidative stress markers, including 8-isoprostane and malondialdehyde (MDA), in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid and plasma are significantly correlated with disease severity and adverse clinical outcomes in patients with ARDS (49,50). Crucially, excessive ROS can damage mitochondria, triggering mitochondrial-dependent endothelial cell apoptosis and exacerbating alveolar-capillary barrier disruption (51). However, NF-κB can also activate endogenous anti-apoptotic mechanisms to restore tissue integrity (52). NF-κB activation upregulates Bcl-2 expression, inhibits mitochondrial cytochrome C release, blocks caspase cascade activation, and delays structural tissue damage. NF-κB activation is indispensable for the inflammatory phase but equally important for the resolution phase, contributing to vascular repair and homeostasis. Strengths and limitations

A key strength of this study lies in the application of bibliometric analysis to systematically elucidate the knowledge structure and research evolution of NF-κB pathway in ALI. This approach effectively overcomes Existing research is highly fragmented, scattered across different disciplines, experimental models, and cell types, which is often fragmented across different disciplines, experimental models, and cell types, and lacks integrated synthesis. Another major strength is the implementation of a rigorous and systematic retrieval strategy. By combining standardized MeSH terms and their synonyms and applying them to TIs, ABs, and AKs, the search strategy enhanced sensitivity while minimizing the inclusion of irrelevant records, thereby improving the reliability of the bibliometric dataset. Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. This analysis relied exclusively on the WoSCC and included only English-language publications, which may have resulted in the omission of relevant studies indexed in other databases or published in other languages. Another limitation of citation-based metrics is self-citations. In evaluative bibliometrics, self-citations are sometimes seen as a potential bias in citation metrics because they can artificially inflate citation counts and related metrics such as the H-index or total citations, especially at the individual or document level, where authors cite their own previous work. Future studies could quantify and adjust for self-citations to improve the accuracy of bibliometric indicators.

Conclusions

This bibliometric analysis indicates that research on the NF-κB pathway in ALI has received sustained and growing attention over the past two decades. China leads in the number of publications, while foundational mechanistic studies on classical NF-κB pathway and the pathophysiology of ARDS have primarily been conducted by research teams in the United States and Europe, resulting in higher citation impact. Research topics have gradually shifted from early inflammatory cascades to oxidative stress, immune dysregulation, alveolar capillary barrier repair, and targeted interventions. Importantly, NF-κB exhibits a dual role in AMs, epithelial cells, and endothelial cells, acting in a stage- and cell-specific manner, promoting inflammation in the early stages of ALI and supporting repair and inflammation resolution in later stages. Future research should focus on precise temporal and cell-specific modulation of NF-κB pathway to optimize treatment strategies for ALI.

1 Preoperative thrombin generation is correlated to intraoperative blood loss in open aortic surgery

Anna Dickson1, Neill Aitken2, Alastair Nimmo3, Gary Morrison3, Ian Young3

1University of Edinburgh, Edinburgh; 2Newcastle Upon Tyne Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust, Newcastle; 3NHS Lothian, Edinburgh

Correspondence: Ian Young

Journal of Cardiothoracic Surgery 2025, 20(1):1

Background

Thrombin generation analysis (TGA) evaluates coagulation capacity, showing an inverse relationship between preoperative thrombin generation and blood loss in cardiac surgery (1, 2). We assessed three preoperative TGA parameters: peak thrombin, endogenous thrombin potential (ETP), and lag time. Higher peak thrombin and ETP, and shorter lag time, represent greater coagulation capacity (3).

Methods

Blood samples were collected from twenty patients before induction of anaesthesia. TGA was measured in citrated platelet poor plasma samples and triggered with 5pM tissue factor. Data was analysed using R (v4.2.2).

Results

TGA correlated significantly with intraoperative blood loss (Fig. 1). Peak thrombin (Pearson Correlation −0.53; 95% CI: −0.26 to −0.72. R2 0.28. p < 0.001), ETP (Pearson Correlation −0.35; 95% CI: −0.04 to −0.60. R2 0.12. p < 0.05), and lag time (Pearson Correlation 0.53; 95% CI: 0.27 to 0.73. R2 0.30. p < 0.001). Blood loss ranged from 2650ml-16500ml. Greater preoperative coagulation capacity was correlated with lower blood loss.

Conclusion

This observed correlation is hypothesis generating. A preoperative blood test to better predict intraoperative blood loss could reduce associated morbidity and benefit service preparation.

These observations are part of a prespecified secondary analysis of a randomised controlled trial (RCT) (4). The original RCT is registered at clinicaltrials.gov (NCT00994045). CSL Behring part funded this RCT.

2 Medium term survival following total arch replacement and open or endovascular second stage procedure

Katherine McAndrew, Ian Young, Euan McGregor, Orwa Falah

Royal Infirmary of Edinburgh, Edinburgh, United Kingdom

Correspondence: Katherine McAndrew

Journal of Cardiothoracic Surgery 2025, 20(1):2

The Scottish dedicated aortic service was established in 2018. We present medium term survival outcomes for patients who have undergone a total aortic arch replacement procedure followed by either an open or endovascular second stage procedure to treat complex arch and thoracoabdominal aortic disease.

Thirty-three consecutive patients who underwent either elective or emergency total arch replacement followed by a second stage procedure between 2018 and 2022 were included. These data were analysed using the R programming software. Survival analysis was performed through generation of Kaplan Meier curves, with censoring at either death or date of last follow-up.

Taking the date of total arch replacement as day zero, open second stage repair had a one year survival of 93% and two year survival of 81%. Endovascular second stage repair had a one year survival of 87% and a 2 year survival of 80%. Median time between first and second stage repair was 203 days (IQR 72:311).

In this cohort there is no evidence of a medium term survival difference between open and endovascular second stage procedure following total arch replacement p = 0.83.

This small observational cohort has limitations, and significant confounding exists since surgical options were often influenced by underlying patient factors.

These data add to an understanding of survival outcomes in a complex surgical domain.

3 Overall survival and disease specific survival in patients with infrarenal aortic disease

Mysoon Alabdah1, Phillipa Burns2, Rachael Forsythe2, Euan McGregor2, Andrew Walker2, Luann Randall2, Ian Young2, Orwa Falah2

1Royal Free Hospital, London, United Kingdom; 2Royal Infirmary Edinburgh, Edinburgh, United Kingdom

Correspondence: Mysoon Alabdah

Journal of Cardiothoracic Surgery 2025, 20(1):3

Every patient being considered for treatment of aortic disease in Edinburgh undergoes anaesthetic assessment.

We performed survival analysis on patients with infrarenal aortic disease, subdivided into open repair, endovascular repair and conservative management, looking at all-cause mortality, and death related to aneurysm.

Data were collected from 2421 patients who were referred to the aortic service at the Royal Infirmary of Edinburgh between 1999 and 2023. These data were analysed using the R programming software. Survival analysis was performed through generation of Kaplan Meier curves, with censoring at either death or date of last follow-up.

Of the operated group, there was a 1.5% 30-day mortality associated with open repair, compared with 0.7% mortality in endovascular repair. The one- and five-year mortality rates were 5.1% and 21.3% for open repair, 6.2% and 32.4% for endovascular repair and 15.5% and 57.6% for those conservatively managed. The survival curves generated on both overall survival and disease specific survival are demonstrated in Fig. 1, with a log rank value of <0.0001.

In this long term observational follow up cohort the 30-day mortality rate was lowest for endovascular repair. Overall mortality at 1 and 5 years is lowest in patients who have undergone open repair.

A limitation of this study is the patient heterogeneity, and future work will involve generating a matched population to aid comparison of outcomes.

4 Disease specific survival in patients who have undergone open repair of thoracoabdominal aortic aneurysms

Mysoon Alabdah1, Rachael Forsythe2, Alasdair Ruthven2, Ian Young2, Orwa Falah2

1Royal Free Hospital, London, United Kingdom; 2Royal Infirmary Edinburgh, Edinburgh, United Kingdom

Correspondence: Mysoon Alabdah

Journal of Cardiothoracic Surgery 2025, 20(1):4

Open repair of thoracoabdominal aortic aneurysms (TAAA) is associated with an overall mortality rate of approximately 5-20%, but for the more extensive repairs (I,II,III,V) it may be higher.

This observational cohort study performed survival analysis on patients that underwent extent I-V open TAAA repair. Using death certificates we observed disease specific survival, ascertaining whether the cause of death related to the aortic aneurysm or not.

Data were collected from 424 patients ((148 extent I,II,III,V)(276 extent IV)) who underwent open TAAA repair in the Royal Infirmary of Edinburgh between 1999 and 2022. These data were analysed using the R programming software. Splitting extent I,II,III,V from extent IV repair, survival was estimated using Kaplan-Meier curves, with data censored at either death or the date of the patient’s last follow-up.

Overall mortality at 30 days after extent I,II,III,V repair was 24%, compared with 6% for extent IV repair. Those who survive for 30 days are more likely to die from causes other than their aneurysm in the 10 years following surgery. Figure 1 represents the disease specific and overall Kaplan Meier survival curves, with associated p-values of <0.0001 for disease specific survival and 0.76 for overall survival. All-cause mortality following extent IV repair exceeds that following extent I,II,III,V after 8 years. This may reflect an older median age at intervention.

Future work will consider those who were assessed for aortic surgery but were not managed operatively, and those who underwent endovascular repair.

5 Outcomes analysis of acute type A aortic dissection surgery; impact of aortic arch intervention

Razi Abuanzeh1, Mohammad Abu_Taleb1, Malek Rehani1, Abdelraheem Shebli1, Ayman Hammoudeh2

1King Hussein Medical Center, Amman, Jordan; 2Istishari Hospital, Amman, Jordan

Correspondence: Razi Abuanzeh

Journal of Cardiothoracic Surgery 2025, 20(1):5

Background: Acute Type A aortic dissection (ATAAD) is a critical cardiovascular emergency associated with high mortality. The involvement of the aortic arch complicates surgical management and is associated with higher perioperative risks. This study aims to evaluate the outcomes of surgical intervention for ATAAD, focusing on the results of aortic arch intervention.

Methods: A retrospective analysis was conducted using data from the Queen Alia Heart Institute database, analyzing outcomes of patients who underwent surgery for acute Type A aortic dissection with or without aortic arch involvement between2019and2023. Patients were divided into two groups: those who received traditional ascending aorta replacement (AAR) with or without aortic root and valve replacement and those who had arch intervention (hemi or total arch). The outcomes included in this study are30-day mortality, complication rates (stroke, renal failure, bleeding, prolonged intubation and ICU stay), and 1 year survival.

Results: A total of 107 patients were included in the analysis. The 30-day mortality rate was significantly higher in the 2nd group (26%)compared to the AAR group (18%,p<0.05). However, long-term survival rates were comparable between groups at 1-year (69%vs.75%).Cerebrovascular accident was more frequent in the arch group (10%vs.6%, p<0.05).

Conclusions: Surgical treatment remains the cornerstone of therapy for acute type A aortic dissection. Arch intervention still confers a relatively high mortality. In our study, there was a higher stroke rate associated with patients who underwent arch replacements at the time of dissection. The choice of repair with or without arch replacement should be individualized to the patient and the severity of clinical status presentation.

6 Thoracic branch endoprosthesis (TBE) in treating thoracic aortic pathology: single centre early outcomes

Mohamed Elsherif, Nabeeh Abuelmagd, Jagdeep Virdee, Gary Cuthbert

Sheffield NHS Foundation Trust, Sheffield, United Kingdom

Correspondence: Mohamed Elsherif

Journal of Cardiothoracic Surgery 2025, 20(1):6

Introduction

The Thoracic Branch Endoprosthesis (TBE) is an original single branched stent graft which acts as a solution to treat thoracic aortic aneurysm and dissection without the need for adjunctive open surgical procedures to perfuse the left arm.

The purpose of this review is to assess the outcomes of this approach in a single centre in the United Kingdom. The primary outcome indicator is 30 day mortality, and the secondary outcome indicator is early re-intervention for type 1 A endoleak, branch occlusion, or other complications.

Patients and methods

This retrospective study enrolled a series of 7 cases presenting to a tertiary vascular centre in the UK from September 2024 to March 2025. These 7 patients were treated using GORE TAG TBE device and were carried out by the same vascular and endovascular surgeon, using the same approach and technique. All patients were followed up at 4 weeks with a surveillance computed tomography (CT) angiogram to assess stent position and patency.

Results

At 30 days, we achieved 100% technical success and survival. 1 case developed a minor stroke. No graft or branch occlusion, or type 1 or 3 endoleak was recorded. One case required TBE explanation for late infection.

Conclusion

The TBE graft demonstrated satisfactory early outcomes to treat thoracic aortic pathology in both aneurysmal and acute/subacute aortic dissection without relying on custom made devices thus avoiding delays to treatment. Longer term data is still required to evaluate the safety and efficacy of this technology in aortic arch zones.

7 Thoracic branch endoprosthesis in zone 0: a legit option

Kishen Raj, Mohamed Elshikhawoda, Neil Masson, Andrew Walker, Orwa Falah

NHS Lothian, Edinburgh, UK

Correspondence: Kishen Raj

Journal of Cardiothoracic Surgery 2025, 20(1):7

Introduction

The GORE® TAG® Thoracic Branch Endoprosthesis (TBE) is invented for the treatment of the descending thoracic aortic pathology at zone 2 while maintaining blood flow to the left subclavian artery (LSA). We present a case of this device deployed in proximal arch (Zone 0).

Case report

A 58-year-old male patient with a history of ascending aortic open repair due to a type A dissection presented with a pseudoaneurysm at the distal anastomosis. He was high risk for another open surgery and there were no off-the shelf or custom-made endo solution available. After a MDT discussion, he was planned for carotid-carotid subclavian bypass followed by zone 0 TBE deployment. The procedure was executed as planned to exclude the pseudoaneurysm.

Conclusion

Albeit out of instruction for use, the thoracic branch endoprosthesis (TBE) proved to be a legit off-the-shelf option in the treatment of more proximal aortic arch pathology in challenging circumstances.

8 Successful endografting in the treatment of an ascending aortic mycotic pseudoaneurysm: a case report

Correspondence: Kishen Raj

Journal of Cardiothoracic Surgery 2025, 20(1):8

Purpose

To report the successful exclusion of an ascending aortic mycotic pseudoaneurysm (AAP) through endovascular stent graft placement.

Case

This is a case of a 56-year-old man with history of infective endocarditis having had a tissue aortic valve replacement (TAVR), complicated with post-operative pericardial tamponade requiring chest re-exploration, which later let to sternal wound breakdown. While recovering from this, he developed an AAP which was diagnosed on CT scan. Superficial swabs of the sternal wound identified multi-drug-resistant Pseudomonas aeruginosa infection as a plausible aetiology. Given the hostile chest condition by that time, open surgery was precluded and a novel approach of using an aortic extension cuff from GORE® TAG-TBE endograft system (W.L. Gore, Flagstaff, AZ) was utilized to exclude the pseudoaneurysm.

A follow up CT-scan at 1 month confirmed a satisfactory graft position and exclusion of the pseudoaneurysm. Post-procedural recovery was relatively uneventful, and the patient was continued on a well-placed antibiotic regimen. He was stepped down to cardiology 3 weeks post-op to continue with vacuum assisted dressing and antibiotics.

Conclusion: Endovascular approach with antibiotic therapy is a credible alternative to treat mycotic AAPs.

Patient consented for the publication.

9 Spine surgery and thoracic aortic pseudoaneurysm: a rare but serious complication

Kishen Raj1,2, Mohamed Elshikhawoda1, Neil Masson1, Andrew Walker1, Orwa Falah1

1NHS Lothian, Edinburgh, UK; 2National University of Malaysia, Bangi, Malaysia

Correspondence: Mohamed Elshikhawoda

Journal of Cardiothoracic Surgery 2025, 20(1):9

Background

Spine fixation surgeries carry risk of complications which include thoracic aortic pseudoaneurysm, seldom seen but a serious complication. This case report is meant to share an experience of managing such case.

Case presentation

A 43-year-old man sustained polytrauma while hiking in Nepal sustaining extensive craniomaxillofacial and spine fractures. He underwent a life-saving surgery there in April 2024 and was transferred to our service. Here, this rare complication of thoracic aortic pseudoaneurysm was picked up on a CT scan, caused by mal-positioned spinal fixation pedicle screws which was abutting this major vessel wall. After discussion in the Aortic MDT, decision was made to treat this affected segment of aorta with thoracic endovascular aneurysm repair (TEVAR) by means of lining a 26x100mm GORE® TAG® Conformable Thoracic aortic stent graft via transfemoral approach; and then removal of the mal-positioned screws. This was done in a staged manner and was a success.

Conclusions

Thoracic aortic pseudoaneurysm is a rare, potentially underdiagnosed and serious complication which should be anticipated. This case report highlights multidisciplinary approach, planning and the role endovascular intervention has to offer in dealing with such complication.

Patient consented for the publication.

10 An overview of type A aortic dissections in a national thoraco-abdominal unit

1NHS Lothian, Edinburgh, UK; 2National University of Malaysia, Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia

Correspondence: Kishen Raj

Journal of Cardiothoracic Surgery 2025, 20(1):10

Introduction

This is to present the demographics and outcome of patients with Type A aortic dissection who had frozen elephant trunk (FET) and second stage operation, including both open and endovascular; in the Royal Infirmary of Edinburgh from 2018 to mid 2024.

Results

A total of 58 patients were operated on, of which 33 patients had FET and went on to have second stage operation. We saw an almost equal distribution in gender. Mean age group was 59. Most of our patients were hypertensive and it was interesting to note that 80% of these patients did not have peripheral arterial disease and 90% did not have a history of TIA or CVA. About 20% of them had connective tissue disease. The average interval for second stage open repair was significantly longer, presumably a consequence of prolonged recovery and subsequent preparation for open second stage surgery. Patients who had second stage had a better survival outcome in 5 years when compared to the FET only cohort. There is no statistical significance in survival probability when comparing endovascular and open groups for second stage (p=0.83).

Conclusion

Over the years, we have seen a rising number of cases. There is a notable shift in trend towards endovascular. This is supported by better early survival benefit in this cohort. Open repairs remain relevant in this arena, more so in patients with connective tissue disease.

12 Visualising causes of mortality after open TAAA

Aryaan Ashraf1, Emma Millar2, Phillipa Burns2, Rachael Forsythe2, Euan McGregor2, Andrew Walker2, Luann Randall2, Ian Young2, Orwa Falah2

1University of Edinburgh, Edinburgh, United Kingdom; 2The Royal Infirmary of Edinburgh, Edinburgh, United Kingdom

Correspondence: Aryaan Ashraf

Journal of Cardiothoracic Surgery 2025, 20(1):12

Background/Objective

The Scottish National Thoracoabdominal and Complex Aortic Service collects outcome data for patients undergoing open thoracoabdominal aortic aneurysm (TAAA) repair at the Royal Infirmary of Edinburgh. This study categorises and visualises primary causes of mortality following open TAAA repair, with a focus on differences by Crawford Classification (Type I–V).

Methods

A dataset of 427 patients who underwent open TAAA repair between 2001 and 2022 was analysed. Seventy-five unique primary causes of death were grouped into 11 categories: cardiovascular disorders (aneurysm-related, aortic dissection, and other), cancer, respiratory, neurological, renal/hepatic/metabolic, gastrointestinal, sepsis, trauma/injuries, and other. Frequencies were summarised and visualised using bar charts.

Results

The most common cause of mortality was aneurysmal-related cardiovascular disease (51 cases), followed by other cardiovascular causes (26), cancer (21), and respiratory disorders (20). Less frequent causes included aortic dissection (6), sepsis (3), and trauma/injuries (2). Mortality differences by Crawford type were noted. Of the 51 aneurysmal-related deaths, 35 occurred in patients undergoing Type I/II/III/V repairs, and only 16 in those undergoing Type IV repair. Conversely, 15 of 21 cancer-related deaths occurred in Type IV patients. These patterns are illustrated in Fig. 1.

Conclusion

Cardiovascular causes—particularly aneurysm-related—were the leading contributors to mortality after open TAAA repair, particularly in patients undergoing extensive Type I and II repairs involving the thoracic aorta. Patients undergoing Type IV repair, typically older and undergoing less extensive surgery below the diaphragm, were more likely to survive beyond hospital discharge and experience longer-term mortality, such as from cancer. These findings support type-specific follow-up and survivorship planning.

13 Summary of perioperative myocardial injury after open TAAA surgery as measured by troponin rise

Aryaan Ashraf1, Emma Millar2, Phillipa Burns2, Rachael Forsythe2, Euan McGregor2, Andrew Walker2, Luann Randall2, Ian Young2, Orwa Falah2

1University of Edinburgh, Edinburgh, United Kingdom; 2The Royal Infirmary of Edinburgh, Edinburgh, United Kingdom

Correspondence: Aryaan Ashraf

Journal of Cardiothoracic Surgery 2025, 20(1):13

Background/Objective

Open thoracoabdominal aortic aneurysm (TAAA) repair exposes the heart to considerable physiological stress due to high aortic clamping, partial left heart bypass, haemorrhage, and periods of intraoperative hypotension. This study uses data from the Scottish National Thoracoabdominal and Complex Aortic Service to evaluate the incidence of perioperative myocardial injury following open TAAA, as detected by postoperative high-sensitivity (HS) troponin rise with a focus on differences by Crawford Classification (Type I-V).

Methods

A dataset of 427 patients who underwent open TAAA repair between 2001 and 2022 was analysed. Postoperative HS troponin measurements were available for all patients. It was assumed that if a troponin test was not requested during hospital stay, myocardial injury was not clinically suspected. Troponin thresholds of 16 ng/L and 34 ng/L were both assessed, with no difference in detection rates; results are presented using the more conservative 34 ng/L cut-off.

Results

The rate of myocardial injury was 7.4% in Type I/II/III/V repairs and 10.9% in Type IV, as illustrated in Fig. 1. Troponin values ranged from 2–902 ng/L in Type IV and 4–5,974 ng/L in Type I/II/III/V. Median HS troponin levels were 38.5 ng/L in Type IV and 51.5 ng/L in other types.

Conclusion

Biochemical evidence of myocardial injury was observed in a notable proportion of patients after open TAAA repair. The higher rate in Type IV may reflect patient factors rather than procedural extent. The pattern is likely due to perioperative oxygen supply–demand mismatch rather than occlusive thrombus. These findings support targeted myocardial protection and improved perioperative monitoring.

14 Visualising ICU stay after open TAAA

Aryaan Ashraf1, Emma Millar2, Phillipa Burns2, Rachael Forsythe2, Alasdair Ruthven2, Andrew Walker2, Luann Randall2, Ian Young2, Orwa Falah2

1University of Edinburgh, Edinburgh, United Kingdom; 2The Royal Infirmary of Edinburgh, Edinburgh, United Kingdom

Correspondence: Aryaan Ashraf

Background/Objective

Patients undergoing open thoracoabdominal aortic aneurysm (TAAA) repair require varying levels of postoperative critical care. This study uses data from the Scottish National Thoracoabdominal and Complex Aortic Service to analyse ICU stay duration across different levels of care (0–3) and assess variation by Crawford Classification (Type I–V).

Methods

A dataset of 427 patients who underwent open TAAA repair between 2001 and 2022 was analysed, including ICU stay variables categorised into Level 0–3 days and ventilator days. A total ICU stay variable was also calculated. Data were visualised using a density plot (ICU stay distribution) and a line plot (average stay per level and surgical type).

Results

Average ICU stay was highest for Type I and II (23 days), followed by Type V (18), Type III (16), and Type IV (10). Level 3 days were longest for Type I (14) and shortest for Type IV (2), with ventilator days showing a similar trend. Level 0–2 days were consistent across types (2–5 days). Density plots, visualised in Fig. 1, showed that Type IV had less variability in ICU stay across all levels.

Conclusion

Level 3 and ventilator days showed the most variation in ICU stay across Crawford Classifications. More extensive operations involving the thoracic aorta (Type I and II) required longer intensive care and showed greater variability, reflecting higher risk of post-operative complications such as prolonged respiratory wean. Type IV repairs, confined to the abdominal aorta and less extensive, had shorter, more predictable stays. These findings can inform perioperative planning and ICU resource allocation.

16 The role of the clinical nurse specialist in an Aortic Surveillance Programme

Roxanne Noces

Guy’s and St. Thomas’ Hospital NHS Foundation Trust, London, United Kingdom

Journal of Cardiothoracic Surgery 2025, 20(1):16

Introduction

Aortic surveillance involves the systematic monitoring of patients post-surgery, those with genetic predispositions, and individuals with incidental findings at increased risk. Early detection allows for timely interventions, preventing catastrophic events and preserving life. Surveillance helps identify modifiable risk factors and guide preventive measures, reducing future risks. Aortic surgery treats various pathologies but comes with risks; thus, optimal timing for intervention and postoperative monitoring are essential for successful outcomes.

Discussion

The Aortic Surveillance Programme at St. Thomas’s Hospital, established in 2012, monitors 598 patients. It follows guideline-recommended imaging modalities and intervals (TTE, CT, MRI), and takes a patient-specific, holistic approach. The programme employs a multidisciplinary team approach (One Aorta, One Service) to determine appropriate interventions.

The Role of Clinical Nurse Specialist

Clinical nurse specialists (CNSs) provide a contact point for patients, families, and healthcare professionals, streamline follow-up processes, ensure regular imaging, conduct telephone consultations, evaluate abnormal findings, and refer patients to specialists. They also raise awareness about aortic diseases.

Conclusions

Aortic surveillance is crucial for monitoring aortic conditions, early complication detection, and risk factor identification. CNSs play a vital role in supervising patient care, organizing follow-ups, educating, and supporting patients, ultimately improving outcomes and reducing complications through excellent monitoring and multidisciplinary care.

Introduction

Acute aortic syndromes (AAS) and symptomatic/ruptured aortic aneurysm are frequently misdiagnosed and associated with poor outcomes. Blood-based biomarkers are needed to aid timely diagnosis and prognostication.

Circulating cell-free DNA (cfDNA) analysis is a novel, transformative diagnostic methodology, whose utility remains to be demonstrated in aortic disease.

Methods

We quantified plasma short-fragment (sf-)cfDNA in the following:

Acute Aortic cohort (AAS, symptomatic & ruptured aneurysms, <2 weeks);

Chronic Aortic cohort (aortic syndromes and asymptomatic aortic aneurysms, >3 months); Controls: acute chest pain cohort; uncomplicated varicose vein outpatients; healthy volunteers; pre-treatment kidney and prostate cancer patients.

Plasma cfDNA was extracted from whole-blood samples using standard techniques. The percentage of sf-cfDNA (50-700 bp) was quantified by standardised Qubit/TapeStation methodology, then independently validated by LINE-PCR, with strong inter-methodology correlation (r2=0.60; p<0.01).

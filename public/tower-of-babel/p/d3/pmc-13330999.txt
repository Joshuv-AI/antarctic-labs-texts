Lascufloxacin switch therapy versus ampicillin/sulbactam for older patients with community-acquired pneumonia in Japan (STEP study): a study protocol for a multicentre open-label randomised controlled non-inferiority trial
Authors: Kazuaki Takeda, Takahiro Takazono, Nobuyuki Ashizawa, Masataka Yoshida, Shotaro Ide, Naoki Iwanaga, Katsuji Hirano, Naoki Hosogaya, Kosaku Komiya, Koichi Izumikawa, Katsunori Yanagihara, Hiroshi Mukae

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionCommunity-acquired pneumonia (CAP) is a major cause of morbidity and mortality in older adults in Japan. Prolonged hospitalisation accelerates functional decline, promotes the progression of frailty and increases the need for long-term care. An early switch from intravenous to oral antibiotics helps achieve clinical outcomes comparable to those of continued intravenous therapy while shortening hospital stays. For older adults with frailty, avoiding unnecessary hospitalisation may help prevent further deterioration of functional status. Demonstrating non-inferiority of oral antibiotics for clinical cure would help clinicians recommend the use of switch therapy to shorten hospital stay and improve functional outcomes in this high-risk population. This study aimed to evaluate the non-inferiority of lascufloxacin switch therapy to intravenous ampicillin/sulbactam in older adults with frailty and CAP.Methods and analysisThis multicentre randomised open-label trial enrols older adults with mild to moderate CAP and frailty according to the Frailty Screening Index. Participants will be randomised using minimisation with the Age, Dehydration, Respiratory Failure, Orientation Disturbance and Low Blood Pressure score and Frailty Screening Index as allocation factors to receive either lascufloxacin switch therapy or ampicillin/sulbactam intravenous. The primary endpoint is the clinical cure rate at the test of cure. Secondary endpoints include early clinical response, end-of-treatment response, hospital stay, medical costs, clinical stability, 30-day mortality or recurrence and microbiological outcomes. The planned sample size is 80 patients per group.Ethics and disseminationThis study was approved by the Certified Review Board of Nagasaki University, Japan. These results will be disseminated through scientific presentations and publications.Trial registration numberjRCTs071250072 (https://jrct.mhlw.go.jp/en-latest-detail/jRCTs071250072)

Introduction

According to the 2024 statistics reported by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare of Japan, pneumonia, including aspiration pneumonia, is the fourth leading cause of death, and the most affected individuals are older adults. When pneumonia develops in this population, a rapid decline in physical function is frequently observed, often followed by a deterioration in the quality of life and an increasing need for long-term care. As the proportion of older individuals in the population is expected to continue to increase, the prevention and optimisation of treatment strategies for pneumonia have become an urgent priority. These concerns are important not only for improving outcomes at the individual level but also for promoting the efficient use of medical resources at the societal level.

Older adults are known to experience a decline in physical and cognitive functions as the duration of hospitalisation increases, and the progression of frailty is more likely to occur.1 A multicentre questionnaire survey conducted in Japan among residents of nursing homes and long-term care facilities reported that 39.4% developed pneumonia within 1 year of admission, and 61.1% of these individuals died within the same period.2 Notably, this population was extremely vulnerable, with a mean age of 85.7 years and mean performance status of 3.1, indicating that most residents required continuous care. Individuals who reach care-dependent states have markedly poor prognoses, and preventing older adults from progressing to care-dependent states is critical for clinical and public health. Therefore, maintaining the activities of daily living in frail or prefrail older adults is expected to help reduce future care needs. Shortening the length of hospital stay is an essential goal, not only to reduce medical costs but also to preserve functional status and ultimately improve long-term survival.

Switch therapy from intravenous to oral antibiotics is recommended once the general condition and laboratory findings begin to improve, according to several clinical guidelines.3 4 This approach provides clinical outcomes comparable to those of continued intravenous therapy while reducing adverse events, lowering medical costs and shortening the length of hospital stay.58 Lascufloxacin (LSFX) is a new respiratory fluoroquinolone whose efficacy and safety against respiratory infections have been demonstrated.9 LSFX demonstrates antimicrobial activity against key respiratory pathogens such as Streptococcus pneumoniae, oral streptococci and anaerobic bacteria, but shows no clinically significant activity against Pseudomonas aeruginosa. Its classification as a ‘narrow-spectrum quinolone’ makes it well-suited for treating community-acquired pneumonia (CAP), considering its spectrum and its role in combating antimicrobial resistance. LSFX is primarily metabolised in the liver and does not require dose adjustment according to renal function. This pharmacokinetic profile makes LSFX a practical therapeutic option for older adults in whom renal impairment is relatively common. In contrast to the pre-existing quinolones, which predominantly exert strong inhibitory effects on either DNA gyrase or topoisomerase intravenous, LSFX inhibits both enzymes.10 This dual activity is considered to suppress drug resistance. These microbiological and pharmacological characteristics are the basis for its recommendation in the current Japanese guidelines for the treatment of mild-to-moderate CAP.4 As both intravenous and oral formulations contain the same active compound, the risk of new adverse events associated with switching formulations is minimal. A report from Japan that examined patients with mild-to-moderate CAP showed that LSFX switch therapy was both effective and safe.11

Although the usefulness of LSFX switch therapy for CAP has been demonstrated, no prospective randomised trial has compared its efficacy with that of intravenous ampicillin/sulbactam (ABPC/SBT), which is recommended for the treatment of mild-to-moderate pneumonia.4 This evidence gap is especially relevant for frail and prefrail older adults, who are likely to benefit from shorter hospital stays. If LSFX switch therapy, which allows for an early transition to oral treatment and may contribute to shorter hospitalisation and reduced medical resources, is not inferior to intravenous ABPC/SBT, it could expand the therapeutic options for older patients with CAP and promote better allocation of healthcare resources.

Based on this background, we designed an open-label, randomised controlled trial to compare switch therapy from intravenous to oral LSFX with continued intravenous ABPC/SBT in patients who begin inpatient treatment for CAP. This study aims to comprehensively evaluate these two regimens by assessing the clinical efficacy, safety, impact on discharge, maintenance of activities of daily living and intervention medical costs in a high-risk population of older adults who may progress from prefrailty to frailty.

Methods and analysis

Study design

This is a multicentre randomised open-label controlled trial study with two parallel groups designed to evaluate the non-inferiority of LSFX to intravenous ABPC/SBT. This study enrols older patients with mild-to-moderate CAP who are in a frail or prefrail condition and examines the efficacy and safety of the treatment.

Ethics and dissemination

This study was reviewed and approved by the Certified Review Board of Nagasaki University (approval number: CRB7180001) and registered in the Japan Registry of Clinical Trials (jRCTs071250072). It was developed in accordance with the Standard Protocol Items Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) 2025 statement and the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) 2025 statement.12 13

This study will be conducted at 18 participating centres: Nagasaki University Hospital, Japanese Red Cross Nagasaki Genbaku Hospital, Saiseikai Nagasaki Hospital, Japanese Red Cross Nagasaki Genbaku Isahaya Hospital, Japan Community Healthcare Organization Isahaya General Hospital, Aino Memorial Hospital, Nagasaki Harbor Medical Center, Sasebo City General Hospital, Sasebo Chuo Hospital, Ureshino Medical Center, Senju Hospital, Nagasaki Memorial Hospital, National Hospital Organization Nagasaki Medical Center, Kouseikai Hospital, Izumikawa Hospital, Shimabara Hospital, Inoue Hospital and Nagasaki Goto Chuoh Hospital.

Any important protocol amendments will be submitted for approval to the Certified Review Board of Nagasaki University and updated in the trial registry as required.

This trial will be conducted in compliance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.14

Findings will be disseminated to patients, caregivers, physicians, funding bodies, critical care societies and researchers through presentations at scientific meetings and publication in peer-reviewed journals.

Participant recruitment

Participants will be recruited from 18 hospitals in Japan. The study commenced on 17 September 2025. Participant recruitment will continue until 31 August 2027, and the overall study period is scheduled to end on 30 June 2028. Eligible patients will be identified and enrolled based on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Each patient or legally acceptable representative will receive a full explanation of the study from a respiratory specialist at the respective site and will be asked to provide written informed consent before participation.

Study protocol

A principal investigator or subinvestigator at each participating medical institution will provide a complete explanation of the study to each patient or legally acceptable representative using the consent form and other documents approved by the Certified Review Board. Written informed consent will be obtained after confirming that the study has been fully understood. The principal investigator or subinvestigator will then verify that the patient meets all inclusion criteria and none of the exclusion criteria and enter all required information into the electronic data capture (EDC) system. Once eligibility is confirmed, the system issues a case registration number. At the time of registration, patients will be randomly assigned by the EDC system to either the LSFX switch therapy group or intravenous ABPC/SBT therapy group. The treatment allocation sequence is concealed within the EDC system, and the investigators responsible for participant enrolment do not have access to the random allocation sequence and cannot predict or influence the treatment assignment.

Patients assigned to the LSFX switch therapy group will receive 300 mg LSFX intravenously on the first day of treatment and 150 mg intravenously on the second day, according to the approved drug information. Switching to oral therapy will be considered when clinical symptoms such as cough, sputum production and dyspnoea have improved or have not worsened, when oral intake is possible and when the patient is afebrile with a temperature below 38 °C for at least 12 hours. If these criteria are met between day 3 and day 5, LSFX tablets at a dose of 75 mg will be administered once daily for a total intravenous and oral therapy duration of approximately 7 days. If the switch criteria are not met between days 3 and 5, the continuation of intravenous administration of LSFX will be allowed for up to 7 days. Patients assigned to the intravenous ABPC/SBT group will receive ABPC/SBT at a dose of 3 g intravenous three times per day for a total of 7 days. If the attending physician judges the therapeutic response to be ‘ineffective’ on day 7, treatment in either group may be extended for up to 14 days or discontinued.

Clinical assessments will be performed on day 3 of treatment, at the end of treatment (EOT) and at the test of cure (TOC) visit (table 3).

Decentralised clinical trial

In this trial, decentralised clinical trial elements will be incorporated to facilitate efficient case registration and study operation. Due to the large anticipated enrolment and logistical constraints at one participating site, patient registration, case evaluations and other study-related administrative tasks will be supported by a web-based communication system. All remotely performed procedures will follow predefined workflows to ensure consistency with inperson operations and maintain the integrity of the trial data.

Randomisation

Patients will be randomly assigned at the time of case registration to either the LSFX switch therapy group or the intravenous ABPC/SBT group. Randomisation will be performed using the minimisation method with an EDC system. The allocation factors are the A-DROP score with categories of 0, 1 or 2 and the Frailty Screening Index with categories of 1, 2 or 3–5 .

The A-DROP score was selected as an allocation factor as it is associated with treatment response and is, therefore, relevant to the primary endpoint, which is the cure rate at the TOC. The Frailty Screening Index was included because it is expected to be related to secondary and exploratory outcomes such as the length of hospital stay and the Barthel Index.17

Blinding

This study will be an open-label trial with two arms. Neither the patients nor the investigators will be blinded to the treatment allocation. Outcome assessments, including clinical evaluations at the EOT and TOC, will be performed according to predefined objective criteria to minimise assessment bias. No additional blinding will be applied.

Adverse events

In the event of an adverse event, the principal investigator or subinvestigator at each participating institution will take appropriate clinical measures without delay, including providing explanations to the patient and discontinuing or suspending the study treatment if necessary. If an adverse event persists at the time of the final observation, the investigator will continue follow-up until the patient returns to the condition they were in before the onset of the observed adverse event or until a clinically stable state is reached. All adverse events will be evaluated and recorded using the EDC system.

Severe adverse events are defined as events resulting in death, events that may lead to death, events requiring hospitalisation or prolongation of hospitalisation or events resulting in disability or the potential for disability.

A separate data and safety monitoring committee will not be established for this study. If participation in the study results in health damage, the principal investigator or subinvestigator will provide appropriate treatment and other necessary measures to the participant. Patients who experience death or permanent disability due to study-related injuries will be compensated through clinical research compensation liability insurance.

Outcome measures

Early clinical response assessment

Early clinical response will be evaluated on day 3 after the first dose of the study drug is administered. This assessment classifies patients into one of three categories: effective, no effect or indeterminable.

A response will be judged effective when at least one clinical symptom has improved, and there is an improvement in either radiological findings of pneumonia or inflammatory findings, defined as a white blood cell count below 9.0 × 109/L or a decrease in the highest C reactive protein level.

A response is judged to have no effect when these improvements are not observed.

A response will be judged as indeterminable when evaluation is not possible because of insufficient information or when worsening occurs due to causes unrelated to pneumonia.

EOT clinical response assessment

The EOT clinical response will be evaluated at the EOT. The patients will be classified into three categories: effective, ineffective or indeterminate.

A response will be judged effective when at least one clinical symptom has improved, fever has resolved and there is improvement in either the radiological findings of pneumonia or inflammatory findings, defined as a white blood cell count of<9.0 × 109/L or a decrease in the highest C reactive protein level.

A response is judged ineffective when these improvements are not observed.

A response will be judged as indeterminable when evaluation is not possible because of insufficient information or when worsening occurs due to causes unrelated to pneumonia.

Sample size calculation and non-inferiority margin determination

To determine the non-inferiority margin for the primary endpoint, which is the clinical cure rate at TOC, published studies were used as references. Based on these reports, the cure rate was assumed to be 95% in the LSFX switch therapy group11 18 and 92% in the continued intravenous ABPC/SBT group.19 This assumption is supported by the reported TOC cure rate of 96.2% in a single-arm study of intravenous-to-oral LSFX switch therapy11 and by a 92.8% cure rate in a phase III double-blind comparative study of LSFX versus levofloxacin in patients with CAP.18 Using these values, a non-inferiority margin of 10% was selected.20

Based on these assumptions, the required sample size was calculated using the Farrington and Manning score test, with a one-sided alpha level of 2.5% and a statistical power of 80%. This resulted in an estimated 72 patients per group. Considering that approximately 10% of enrolled patients may have indeterminable outcomes, the target sample size was set at 80 patients per group, resulting in a total of 160 patients.

Data collection and management

In this study, patient data will be collected using a web-based EDC system that generates electronic case report forms. A principal investigator or subinvestigator at each participating institution will enter the required data according to the study protocol (table 2) and electronically sign the completed case report forms to confirm the accuracy and completeness of the information provided.

The investigators will verify that each patient meets all the inclusion criteria and none of the exclusion criteria before entering the necessary registration items into the EDC system. Once eligibility is confirmed, a case registration number will be assigned. This number includes characteristics that do not contain information that can identify individual patients. All study documents, including case report forms, will use this registration number for anonymisation. A correspondence table linking patient names to registration numbers will be securely maintained by the principal investigator and will not be accessible to individuals outside the study team. All study personnel will strictly protect patient personal information and will not disclose identifiable information without valid justification and permission.

The sponsor investigator will monitor compliance with the study protocol, applicable standard operating procedures and relevant regulatory requirements and confirm the reliability of the collected data. An external audit will be performed to ensure the integrity of the study. Monitoring and auditing will be conducted in accordance with the standard operating procedures for these activities.

The datasets generated and/or analysed during the current study will be available from the principal investigator on reasonable request.

Statistical analysis

The intention-to-treat (ITT) set will consist of all randomised patients. The safety analysis set (SAS) will consist of all registered patients who receive at least one dose of the study drug during the study period. The full analysis set (FAS) will be defined as a subset of the SAS that includes patients for whom the clinical response at TOC can be evaluated as cured or uncured. In the LSFX switch therapy group, only patients who complete the switch to oral LSFX will be included in the FAS. The per-protocol set will consist of patients who receive at least 80% of the planned doses of the study drug and who adhere sufficiently to the protocol requirements. Missing or non-evaluable data, including missing TOC assessments and indeterminate outcomes, will not be imputed unless otherwise specified and analyses will be performed using available complete data. Because complete-case analysis may overestimate cure rates when missingness is informative, particularly in a non-inferiority setting, an additional conservative sensitivity analysis will be performed. In this analysis, missing or non-evaluable outcomes, including missing TOC assessments and indeterminate outcomes, will be counted as treatment failures.

For the primary endpoint, the clinical cure rates in the LSFX switch therapy and intravenous ABPC/SBT groups will be estimated, together with two-sided 95% CIs calculated using the Wilson score method. In addition, the difference in cure rates between the two groups will be estimated by adjusting for allocation factors, namely the A-DROP score and Frailty Screening Index, and a two-sided 95% CI will be calculated. The LSFX switch therapy group will be considered non-inferior to the intravenous ABPC/SBT group if the lower limit of the 95% CI for the adjusted difference in cure rates is not <−10%. Initial primary endpoint analysis will be performed using the FAS. As a pre-specified secondary analysis, the same analytical approach will be applied to the ITT population to examine the potential impact of excluding patients who do not complete the oral switch therapy from the FAS. The ITT population included all randomised patients, including those in the LSFX arm who did not complete oral switch therapy.

The planned analyses for the secondary endpoints will follow the same procedures as those used for the primary analysis, with two-sided 95% CIs for the binomial proportions. These analyses will be performed using the FAS.

Exploratory analyses will be conducted to examine the associations between patient background characteristics and the primary and secondary endpoints. Patient background will be defined as the clinical information obtained at or before the first administration of the study drug.

Subgroup analyses will also be performed using the same method as in the primary analysis after stratifying patients by the following factors: causative bacteria, age (younger than 75 years or older), A-DROP score (0 or 1 vs 2), Frailty Screening Index score at consent (one or two vs three to five), Barthel Index at consent, risk of resistant bacteria (yes or no), comorbidities (yes or no) and nursing and healthcare-associated pneumonia (NHCAP) status (yes or no). The p values obtained through these subgroup analyses will not be used to control for type I errors or to support the confirmatory conclusions.

No interim analyses are planned for this study, and there are no predefined guidelines for stopping. All analyses will be conducted after the completion of enrolment and follow-up of all participants. The statistical analysis plan is included in this protocol and will be publicly available on publication of this article.

Discussion

CAP in older adults frequently leads to substantial decline in physical and cognitive functions, particularly among those with underlying frailty. Hospitalisation is a major stressor for frail patients, accelerating the progression of sarcopenia, diminishing independence in activities of daily living and increasing long-term care needs.1 Therefore, treatment strategies that maintain clinical effectiveness, while minimising prolonged hospitalisation, are required. Switch therapy, in which patients transition from intravenous to oral antibiotics once clinical stability is achieved, offers a potential means of preserving the functional status while maintaining therapeutic efficacy.

The effectiveness and safety of LSFX tablets in NHCAP9 and the feasibility of LSFX switch therapy in CAP11 have been previously reported. Although these studies suggest that LSFX may be a valuable option for older adults with pneumonia, a definitive evaluation has been limited by the absence of a randomised comparison arm. The present trial advances this line of investigation by directly comparing LSFX switch therapy with a standard-of-care intravenous regimen using a randomised controlled design. This approach addresses the limitations of previous observational and single-arm studies and provides a more rigorous assessment of the comparative effectiveness of the two regimens.

Frailty significantly affects pneumonia outcomes. Even short periods of bed rest or reduced activity during hospitalisation can precipitate a substantial decline in mobility, self-care ability and overall resilience. In this context, early conversion to oral therapy, when clinically appropriate, has the potential to reduce the length of hospital stay and mitigate functional decline. By incorporating functional endpoints, such as the Barthel Index and objectively measured physical activity, this study evaluated not only the clinical cure but also the broader impact of the treatment strategy on functional preservation, an outcome that may be more meaningful for frail older adults than traditional biomedical endpoints alone.

This study has several limitations. First, finding generalisability should be interpreted with caution. Although this trial focuses on older adults with prefrailty or frailty, the enrolled population represents a relatively select subgroup of this broader clinical population. Patients with an ECOG performance status of 4, recurrent aspiration pneumonia, severe renal impairment, immunosuppression and severe or progressive comorbidities will be excluded to ensure participant safety and endpoint evaluability. These findings may thus not be directly applicable to the most severely dependent older adults or to patients with substantial competing risks of poor outcomes. Second, this is an open-label trial in which neither participants nor investigators will be blinded to treatment allocation, which may introduce performance or assessment bias. Third, this study will be conducted in older Japanese patients, and caution is needed when extrapolating these findings to other healthcare settings or populations.

Despite these limitations, this study has several strengths. It focuses on high-risk populations, for whom the optimisation of pneumonia management is particularly important. The randomised design, use of clinically relevant allocation factors, such as the A-DROP score and frailty status, and inclusion of multidimensional outcomes, including clinical response, microbiological findings and activity levels, strengthen the overall rigour of the evaluation. Additionally, the incorporation of decentralised operational procedures allows participation from an island-based site while maintaining data integrity and protocol adherence at the study site.

If LSFX switch therapy is demonstrated to be non-inferior to intravenous ABPC/SBT, the findings will support an evidence-based switch strategy that may shorten hospital stays, preserve functional capacity and contribute to improved long-term outcomes in frail older adults with CAP. These results may inform future guidelines and promote individualised pneumonia management that aligns with the functional goals of ageing populations.

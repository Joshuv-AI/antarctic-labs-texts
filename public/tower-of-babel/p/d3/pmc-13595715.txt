Assessing the reliability and validity of a standardized rubric for staff evaluation of all-ceramic crown preparations at preclinical setting
Authors: Abdulrahman Alzahrani, Abdulhadi Muaadi, Abdulmajeed O Alotaibi, Mohammed Fareed Felemban, Bandar Saud Shukr, Mohammed M Al Moaleem, Thanoon Abdo Baggash
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEnsuring a consistent and valid assessment of preclinical technical skills is essential in dental education. This study evaluated the inter-rater reliability and validity of a standardized analytic rubric for staff evaluation of single all-ceramic crown preparations across multiple academic years. It compared student performance in midterm and final examinations, examined cohort variability, and investigated the correlation between academic achievement (grade point average or GPA) and practical performance.Materials and methodsA retrospective cross-sectional study was conducted on fourth-year male dental students from three consecutive academic years (2020–2023). Two experienced double-blinded faculty members independently evaluated midterm and final crown preparations by using a validated analytic rubric. The rubric comprised six criteria: five technical domains (occlusal/incisal reduction, axial reduction, taper, finish line placement, and finishing of the preparation) scored 0–6 points and one professionalism domain scored 0–4 points, yielding a total maximum score of 40 points. Inter-rater reliability, cohort differences, and GPA correlation were assessed using Cronbach’s alpha, Kruskal–Wallis tests, and Pearson’s correlation, respectively, and significance was set at p < 0.05.ResultsInter-rater reliability was high for the overall scores (midterm α = 0.881, final α = 0.899). Among the individual parameters, taper (retention and resistance) scored the highest in the midterm examination (Staff 1: 5.13/6), and finish line placement scored the lowest in the final examination (Staff 1: 3.79/6). Professionalism showed the lowest inter-rater consistency (α = −0.095). Performance considerably declined from midterm to final examinations (Staff 1 scores declined from 28.19 to 23.68). Substantial variability existed across the academic years, with the 2021–2022 cohort outperforming the others. GPA showed only one weak positive correlation with final scores from one evaluator (r = 0.287, p = 0.037) and was otherwise not significant.ConclusionThe analytic rubric proved reliable for standardized preclinical assessment, supporting its implementation despite the unexpected decline in the final examination scores and cohort variations with a high inter-reliability score. Academic GPA was a poor predictor of practical crown preparation performance, reinforcing that cognitive and psychomotor competencies are distinct domains in dental education.Keywords: Preclinical assessment, Analytic rubric, Inter-rater reliability, Crown preparation, Academic achievement, Psychomotor skills

Introduction

In modern dental education, the acquisition of psychomotor skills is a fundamental requirement for clinical competency. Preclinical courses serve as a critical bridge where students transition from theoretical concepts to the manual application of restorative procedures on artificial teeth [1]. This phase is essential for developing the dexterity and precision required for irreversible procedures, ensuring that students are adequately prepared before patient treatment [2]. Consequently, the methods used to assess these skills must be robust, transparent, and capable of distinguishing varying levels of student proficiency [3, 4].

Among the various fixed prosthodontic procedures taught, the preparation of a natural tooth for a full-coverage crown is considered a core competency [5]. However, it is also one of the most technically demanding tasks for undergraduates [6]. Research indicates that students frequently struggle with specific geometric parameters, such as achieving the correct degree of taper and adequate axial reduction, which are critical for the mechanical durability and retention of the final restoration [5, 6]. Identifying these specific deficits early in training is vital for remediation and long-term clinical success [7].

Historically, the evaluation of such preclinical preparations relied heavily on global glare-and-grade assessment methods. These methods are inherently subjective and lack specific criteria, potentially leading to substantial variability in scoring that is based on the examiner’s personal preferences [8–10]. Baik noted that although global evaluations can achieve moderate reliability, they often fail to provide the granular feedback necessary for student improvement compared with structured analytic methods [11].

To address the limitations of subjective grading, dental institutions have increasingly adopted analytic rubrics. These assessment tools deconstruct complex psychomotor tasks into distinct, observable criteria, such as occlusal reduction, finish line continuity, and surface smoothness, assigned to specific performance levels [6, 12]. The use of such standardized systems aims to objectify the grading process, ensuring that students are evaluated on explicit standards rather than an overall impression of quality [8].

The implementation of analytic rubrics has been shown to substantially enhance inter-rater reliability among faculty members [13, 14]. The variability associated with subjective assessment can be effectively mitigated when examiners are aligned via a validated rubric, leading to fair student outcomes [15, 16]. Substantial to almost perfect agreement (kappa values of 0.67–0.85) has been reported between calibrated prosthodontists when detailed criteria are used to assess anterior crown preparations [6].

Another critical area of educational research is the relationship between academic achievement and manual dexterity [17]. A common assumption is that high-performing students in didactic courses excel in practical components; however, evidence frequently contradicts this assumption. Multiple studies have demonstrated a weak or non-significant correlation between students’ grade point average (GPA) and their psychomotor performance in preclinical settings, implying that intellectual aptitude and manual skills are distinct domains that may require different pedagogical approaches [18–20].

The trajectory of skill acquisition is also a vital consideration in curriculum design. Psychomotor competence is not static; it evolves through repeated practice and feedback [21]. Mhanni et al. demonstrated that student performance often follows a nonlinear learning curve, where substantial improvements are observed only after multiple practice attempts [22]. Similarly, Mittal et al. found that combining self-assessment with faculty feedback leads to measurable improvements in performance from initial to final assessments, highlighting the importance of longitudinal tracking [23].

Despite the widespread adoption of rubrics, their consistency across different academic cohorts and assessment intervals needs to be continuously validated. Although single-point reliability studies exist, only a few studies have examined the stability of staff evaluations from midterm to final examinations over multiple consecutive years [22]. Ensuring that faculty evaluators remain consistent over time is crucial for maintaining the validity of the assessment system and ensuring that grades reflect true student ability rather than cohort-specific grading fluctuations [7, 24].

This study aims to evaluate inter-rater agreement and rubric reliability for assessing preclinical single all-ceramic crown preparations across midterm and final examinations over three academic years. It compares student performance in midterm and final practical examinations, as evaluated by two independent faculty assessors, to identify time-based changes in psychomotor skill development. Moreover, it examines annual cohort variability in practical examination scores across three consecutive academic years (2020–2021, 2021–2022, and 2022–2023) to assess consistency in student performance and evaluator scoring patterns. The correlation between students’ academic achievement (cumulative GPA) and their practical examination scores is also investigated to determine whether cognitive performance predicts technical skill proficiency in a preclinical setting. The null hypothesis is that no statistically significant difference exists between the assessments provided by the two evaluators for single crown preparations in midterm and final examinations across the three-year period.

Materials and methods

Study design

This retrospective cross-sectional study was conducted within the Department of Prosthodontics, Factuality of Dentistry, Taif University, Saudi Arabia. The study cohort consisted of fourth-year male dental students enrolled in the preclinical phase of the fixed prosthodontics course across three consecutive academic years (2020–2021, 2021–2022, and 2022–2023).

Ethical approval

The research protocol received ethical approval (Application No. 47 − 008) from Taif University’s Scientific Research Ethics Committee, which is accredited by the National Committee for Bioethics (Accreditation No. HAPO-02-T-105). Approval was granted on September 9, 2025, and is valid for three years. All data were handled in accordance with institutional ethical guidelines for retrospective educational research.

Practical exercise and evaluation protocol

Throughout the three academic years under review, the students received standardized training in single all-ceramic crown preparation. This training included identical demonstration lectures and supervised practice sessions on typodont teeth mounted on dental manikin simulators. For the midterm and final summative assessments, each student was required to prepare the same tooth type (maxillary right central incisor) for a single all-ceramic crown by using standardized typodont teeth mounted on dental manikins under identical time and equipment conditions. The choice of a central incisor ensured consistent task complexity across all examinations and cohorts. The physical preparations created during each examination were collected and served as the specimens for subsequent blinded evaluation.

Two experienced faculty members from the Department of Prosthodontics served as independent assessors. Each assessor blindly evaluated every student’s midterm and final preparation specimens by using a validated, identical analytic rubric (Table 1). Blinding was maintained by concealing student identities and ensuring that the assessors were unaware of each other’s scores, thereby guaranteeing that the evaluations were based solely on the technical and professional quality of the prepared specimens.

Evaluation rubric and parameters

The rubric used in this study was adapted from previously validated analytic rubrics for crown preparation assessment [6, 12]. Prior to implementation, the adapted rubric underwent content validation by three experienced prosthodontists from the Department of Prosthodontics at Taif University, who reviewed each criterion for clarity, relevance, and completeness. Modifications were made based on their feedback, including refinement of descriptive anchors for taper and finish line placement. The final version was pilot-tested on a small set of preparations (n = 10) not included in the study sample, achieving acceptable inter-rater agreement (intraclass correlation = 0.85) before full implementation.

The assessment of crown preparations was conducted using a standardized analytic rubric comprising six core criteria, each defined by descriptive performance levels. Five of these criteria were technical domains, each scored on a scale from 0 to 6 points. The sixth criterion pertained to professional conduct and was scored from 0 to 4 points.

The technical criteria included occlusal/incisal reduction, axial reduction, taper (retention and resistance), finish line placement, and finishing of the preparation.

Performance was categorized into three distinct levels, namely, excellent, clinically accepted, and fail, on the basis of the detailed descriptive criteria in Table 1. The total score from all six domains (maximum of 40 points) determined the overall grade for the preparation. A cumulative score of 34–40 points was classified as excellent, a score of 27–33 points was denoted as clinically acceptable, and scores below 27 points resulted in a fail for the exercise.

The rubric was implemented consistently in the midterm and final evaluations by both faculty assessors, ensuring a structured, replicable framework for scoring preclinical crown preparations.

Data collection

Primary data were collected from two archival sources. The first source comprised practical examination records, namely, the completed analytic rubrics containing scores independently assigned by two faculty assessors (Staff 1 and Staff 2) for each student’s midterm and final crown preparations. The second source was the official university academic registry, from which each student’s cumulative GPA on a four-point scale was obtained.

Taif University employs a standardized GPA scale in which letter grades correspond to a four-point system (e.g., A + = 4.00, A = 3.75, and B + = 3.50 down to F = 0.00). The minimum GPA for good academic standing is 2.00, and the minimum cumulative GPA for graduation is 1.75. GPA data were used to analyze potential correlations with practical examination scores from the midterm and final assessments. All data were anonymized and compiled into a structured database for statistical analysis.

Statical analysis

For statical analysis, the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences version 26.0 was used. The descriptive statistics, as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and the median were calculated for quantitative variables. Meanwhile, categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages.

The normality of continuous variables was assessed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Paired t-tests were employed to compare staff assessment scores across the midterm and final year exercises. A nonparametric Kruskal–Wallis test was applied to evaluate differences in the overall exercise scores across the three academic years and for each rubric parameter. ANOVA was used to compare the performance in different teeth preparations during the three academic years, and a paired t-test was performed for comparison between assessors. The Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used to evaluate the changes from midterm to final assessments. Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient was calculated to analyze the associations between GPA and exercise performance scores for both assessor types at the midyear and the final year. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all analyses.

Results

A total of 53 students were recorded over the academic years 2020–2021, 2021–2022, and 2022–2023, with 17, 17, and 19 students in each year, respectively. The GPA distributions were as follows: 2 students achieved grades of A + and A, 26 received B + and B, and 25 earned C + and C.

The evaluation of the all-ceramic crown preparation was summarized on the basis of the average ratings from the staff assessments. Staff 1’s mean scores were 4.66 ± 0.897 for incisal reduction, 5.02 ± 0.951 for axial reduction, 5.13 ± 0.785 for taper (retention and resistance), 4.56 ± 0.959 for finish line placement, 5.01 ± 1.026 for finishing of the preparation, and 3.81 ± 0.395 for professionalism. Staff 2’s scores were 4.71 ± 0.817 for incisal reduction, 4.72 ± 0.744 for axial reduction, 4.79 ± 0.785 for taper, 4.70 ± 0.840 for finish line placement, 4.86 ± 0.922 for finishing of the preparation, and 3.87 ± 0.342 for professionalism.

The Cronbach’s alpha values for the midterm assessments were 0.645, 0.675, 0.771, 0.812, 0.798, and − 0.095, indicating varying levels of reliability. For the midterm examination, the mean scores were 28.19 ± 3.932 for Staff 1 and 27.64 ± 3.376 for Staff 2, with a reliability of 0.881. Similarly, the Cronbach’s alpha values showed a varying level of reliability for the final year assessments, with values of 0.805, 0.773, 784, 0.830, 0.731, and − 0.891 for the different parameters. In the final examination, the means were 23.68 ± 3.926 for Staff 1 and 23.36 ± 3.357 for Staff 2, showing excellent reliability at 0.899. These findings collectively validate the use of the standardized rubric for staff evaluation in a preclinical setting and reflect a robust assessment framework, as shown in detail in Table 2.

Discussion

The results showed strong reliability for the overall assessment, with Cronbach’s alpha values of 0.881 for the midterm examination and 0.899 for the final examination. These findings align with those of previous studies that found substantial to almost perfect inter-rater reliability when an analytic rubric is used for crown preparation assessment and preclinical endodontic, confirming that well-defined criteria can align evaluator judgments [6, 8]. However, internal consistency varied by parameter, with domains such as finish line placement and finishing of the preparation showing high reliability (0.812 and 0.798) and professionalism having a negative value (− 0.095), indicating evaluation inconsistency. This finding aligns with that of Baik (2025), who obtained variable inter-rater reliability across different preclinical procedures when analytic rubrics were used. This finding suggests that certain subjective criteria, such as professionalism, remain challenging to standardize even with structured tools [11].

A critical finding of this study was the considerable decrease in student performance scores from the midterm to the final across the cohorts. Staff 1’s overall mean score dropped from 28.19 in the midterm examination to 23.68 in the final examination, and a similar trend was observed for Staff 2. However, as shown in Table 5, this decline was not uniform across all cohorts. The 2020–2021 cohort showed the most dramatic drop (from 29.94 to 19.94 for Staff 1), whereas the 2021–2022 cohort demonstrated improvement (from 24.76 to 28.06 for Staff 1). This result partially contradicts those of Mittal et al., who observed measurable improvements from initial to final assessments when faculty feedback was integrated [23]. Similarly, other studies have reported that performance in crown preparation tasks typically improves substantially after multiple practice attempts [4, 22]. The cohort-specific patterns observed here suggest that the decline is not a universal phenomenon but may be influenced by cohort-specific factors such as differences in baseline preparation, teaching exposure, student motivation, or even unmeasured effects of the COVID-19 pandemic on the 2020–2021. The decline observed in two of the three cohorts may also be attributed to increased stress during the final examination, lack of continuous reinforcement between the two assessment points, or increased assessment rigor in the final examination.

With regard to specific technical parameters, the students in the current study achieved high mean scores for taper (retention and resistance) and finishing of the preparation, particularly during the midterm, with the means exceeding 5.0 out of 6.0. This result contradicts the findings of Almohefer et al., Alqahtani, and Habib, who reported that axial taper was acceptable in only about 53%–61% of preparations in their cohort [5, 6, 12]. Similarly, Al-Moaleem et al. stated that only 35.1% of mesio-distal preparations are within the ideal taper range of 4°–14°, with students tending to over-taper [7]. The slightly better performance in the current study compared with that in these previous studies may be attributed to the specific rubric descriptors that penalized over-tapering (16°-20°) explicitly. Jabali et al. also noted high performance in structural parameters, such as access cavity preparation, and attributed it to rigorous preclinical training [8].

However, incisal and occlusal reduction parameters presented with challenges. The mean score for incisal reduction dropped from 4.66 in the midterm examination to 3.87 in the final examination for Staff 1. This finding aligns with those of Alqahtani, who identified incisal reduction as the area with the lowest performance (57.5%) [6]. By contrast, Almohefer et al. found remarkable arch-based differences in occlusal reduction, although predominantly unacceptable rates persisted [5]. The consistency of these findings across studies suggests that gauging appropriate reduction depth remains a complex psychomotor skill that may require augmented training aids, such as the 3D-printed teeth evaluated by del Hougne et al. that show promise in improving learning outcomes [25].

The rubric’s structured criteria helped identify specific areas of student difficulty, such as finish line placement and axial reduction, which consistently scored low. Similar patterns were reported by Almohefer et al., who found unacceptable performance in axial and occlusal reduction in over 89% of preparations [5], and by Alqahtani, who identified incisal reduction and taper as the most challenging among all criteria [6]. This diagnostic function of analytic rubrics can inform targeted curriculum improvements.

Significant differences were observed among the three academic cohorts, with the 2021/2022 group performing distinctly better than the 2020/2021 group (p < 0.05). This variation underscores the influence of external factors on student cohorts. The low performance of the 2020/2021 group could be reflective of disruptions related to the global educational environment during that period [26–28]. Such fluctuations underscore the finding of Almalki et al. that academic performance can be influenced by a complex interplay of admission criteria, demographic factors, and institutional variables, rather than rubric consistency alone [19]. This result aligns with that of Mhanni et al., who noted that psychomotor competence evolves through repeated practice and follows a nonlinear learning curve, suggesting that different cohorts may require varying training volumes to reach the same proficiency levels [22]. Daghrery et al. similarly noted year-wise performance fluctuations in preoperative procedures [9].

A critical area of investigation in the current study was the correlation between academic achievement (GPA) and psychomotor performance. A largely nonsignificant correlation was found between GPA and practical scores, with the exception of a weak positive correlation (r = 0.287) observed by Staff 2 during the final examination. This general lack of strong correlation is consistent with the observation of Afify et al., who found no significant relationship between didactic and psychomotor scores in the total sample [18]. Similarly, Alshehri et al. reported weak, nonsignificant correlations between GPA and clinical performance in crown preparations, and Al Moaleem et al. obtained a similar nonsignificant result (p = 0.495) [10, 24].

However, the slight positive correlation identified by one evaluator in the final examination partially aligns with the finding of Jabali et al., who found a significant correlation between GPA and overall root canal treatment scores [8]. Furthermore, Daghrery et al. noted a significant positive correlation between cumulative GPA and clinical performance in students’ first academic year of study [9]. The conflicting evidence across these studies, including the mixed results in the present study, reinforces the theory that intellectual aptitude (measured by GPA) and manual dexterity are distinct domains. High academic achievers do not automatically possess the fine motor skills required for precise tooth preparation, suggesting that dental curricula must maintain distinct pedagogical approaches for didactic and practical training.

Inter-evaluator agreement in the current study was notably strong, with the correlation coefficients between Staff 1 and Staff 2 reaching 0.796 for the midterm examination and 0.826 for the final examination. This high level of agreement validates the null hypothesis regarding the evaluators because no significant difference was found in their assessment patterns. This result agrees with that of Baik, who found no significant differences between grades awarded by different ranks of professors when calibrated methods are employed [11]. It also supports the findings of Alqahtani, who observed that calibrated prosthodontists achieve substantial agreement [6]. This consistency is crucial for the fairness of summative assessments, ensuring that student outcomes are not dependent on the specific faculty member conducting the evaluation.

The use of blinded, independent assessors in this study enhances the validity of the findings by reducing bias. This methodological rigor mirrors the approaches in other studies, such as that of Mittal et al., who used blinded faculty assessments to evaluate the effect of self-assessment on practical performance [23], and Hashem et al., who compared student self-evaluations with calibrated faculty scores. Such designs are essential for drawing reliable conclusions about assessment tools [29].

The validity of the standardized rubric established in this study advocates for its continued use, but the results highlight the need for continuous formative feedback. Elemam and Abdelaal demonstrated that a structured assessment model with feedback substantially improves clinical competency from the first to the third attempt [4]. The lack of substantial improvement from midterms to finals in the current study suggests that the feedback loop might need reinforcement between the two major assessments.

The current study focused exclusively on male students. Although this approach controls for gender variables, it limits the generalizability of the results relative to those of mixed-gender studies. Al Moaleem et al. and Daghrery et al. found that female students generally perform better or as well as males in preclinical and clinical settings [9, 10]. Al-Moaleem et al. specifically noted that females produce superior finish lines and rounded angles compared with males. Future iterations of this assessment could benefit from including female cohorts to determine if the rubric maintains its validity across genders [7].

Furthermore, the introduction of self-assessment could demonstrate faculty expectations and student performance. Mittal et al. found that although students initially overestimate their skills, implementing self-assessment rubrics leads to notable improvements in faculty-graded performance over time [23]. Integrating a self-assessment component into the current rubric could enhance students’ critical analysis of their own crown preparations, leading to improved outcomes in the final examination.

This study has several limitations. First, the sample was limited to male students from a single institution, which restricts generalizability to female or mixed-gender populations. Second, the retrospective design precluded control over potential confounding variables such as practice time, feedback quality, or student motivation. Third, the unexpected decline in final examination scores could not be fully explained by available data; factors such as examination anxiety or fatigue may have contributed but were not measured. Fourth, the weak correlation between GPA and practical performance may be partly due to restriction of range in GPA scores, as most students had GPAs in the B–C range. Future prospective studies with larger, more diverse cohorts and structured calibration sessions for subjective criteria are recommended.

Conclusions

The results of this study demonstrated the following:

The standardized analytic rubric demonstrated strong overall reliability for preclinical crown preparation assessment, with high interstaff agreement (Cronbach’s α 0.88 for overall scores).

The student performance scores substantially declined from midterm to final examinations across the three academic years.

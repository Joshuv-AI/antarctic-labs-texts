Development and validation of betel-year: a clinical tool for quantifying exposure to betel quid chewing, utilizing nuclear morphometric analysis and validated by cytomorphological analysis
Authors: Hamrish Kumar Rajakumar, Varsha Coimbatore Sathyabal, Kolsamma Nasrin Jabarulla, Pushpa Balamurugesan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBetel quid chewing, prevalent in Southeast Asia and South Asia, involves components such as betel leaf, areca nut, slaked lime, and sometimes tobacco. This study aims to assess buccal mucosa changes in betel quid chewers, develop a clinical tool for assessing exposure, and investigate its usability in predicting dysplasia.MethodsAfter obtaining ethical approval and informed consent, patients were recruited from the Out-Patient Department of Government Medical College, Omandurar, Government Estate, India. A target sample size of 200 was calculated. The data included the history of betel quid chewing, buccal mucosa cells obtained by oral cytology, and the severity of dysplasia of the slides assessed by pathologists. We utilized principal component analysis (PCA) and confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) to validate a new outcome variable reflecting nuclear morphometric parameters (NMPs). Multiplicative regression models were developed for betel years based on betel exposure and additives. Spearman correlation and Kruskal-Wallis test was used to check the association between betel years and dysplasia.ResultsSignificant differences in NMPs were observed among different betel chewing groups. We derived multiplicative regression models for betel years. In the logarithmic transformation approach, betel year = 0.05×betel-exposure×0.09×slaked-lime use×0.11×tobacco-use. In the original variable approach, betel year = 5.05×betel-exposure^0.00048×slaked-lime-use^0.18133×tobacco-use^1.47513. Spearman correlation and Kruskal–Wallis tests confirmed associations with dysplasia.ConclusionBetel year is a pioneering tool for assessing lifetime betel quid exposure, similar to pack years for smoking. It could aid in risk stratification, targeted interventions and shaping public health policies. Despite limitations, betel year holds promise for revolutionizing oral health risk assessment, and future research can expand its scope globally, considering diverse betel quid compositions.Keywords: Betel year, Clinical quantification tool, Oral cancer, Nuclear morphometry, Risk assesment, Cytomorphological analysis

Introduction

The habit of chewing betel leaf or Areca nut is more prevalent in Southeast Asia, Micronesia, Island Melanesia, and South Asia. This practice, referred to as “betel chewing” involves various components such as betel leaf, Areca nut (Areca catechu), slaked lime, and sometimes tobacco and spices, including cardamom, cloves, and turmeric [1]. Additionally, Areca nut, in a processed form known as Zardha or Supari, is consumed in combination with these ingredients. The betel chewing habit is deeply ingrained in the cultural practices of many countries and is a regular part of daily life. It is commonly enjoyed after meals, during auspicious ceremonies, and, in some cultures, after every meal [2].

Betel quid usage is estimated to be prevalent among 10–20% of the global population, with up to 600 million individuals globally engaging in areca nut chewing. Betel quid chewing habits vary widely across different countries in South Asia and Southeast Asia. In India, prevalence ranges from 3.3 to 37%. In Pakistan, Karachi reports 7.5% using pan without tobacco and 15% with tobacco. Sri Lanka shows around 50% prevalence in some regions, while in Thailand’s hill tribes, it ranges from 5 to 46%. Taiwan reports 28.3% among men and 1.4% among women in Kaohsiung, with higher rates among indigenous populations. In Sarawak, Malaysia, daily use is seen in 22% of men and 47% of women. Cambodia reports 31% prevalence, and in China, Xiangtan city has 35% chewing areca nut [3]. In India, the nationwide prevalence of areca nut chewing is estimated to be approximately 30% among men and 7% among women. This estimation is derived from the National Family Health Survey, which reported that 36.5% of men and 8.4% of women aged 15–49 years are engaged in some form of tobacco consumption [4].

Betel leaves consumed alone without carcinogenic additives offers health benefits such as antioxidant properties, wound-healing tendencies, and headache relief [5]. Unfortunately, these health advantages are overshadowed when combined with areca nut and tobacco, both established as carcinogenic agents [6]. Areca nut contains four primary alkaloids: arecoline, arecaidine, guvacine, and guvacoline. These compounds act as inhibitors of gamma-aminobutyric acid receptors, significantly impacting the central nervous, cardiovascular, respiratory, and gastrointestinal systems [7]. Ranked as the fourth most commonly used addictive substance globally, areca nut stands as an independent risk factor for oral cancer, contributing to approximately 49.5% of reported oral cancer cases associated with betel quid chewing [8]. Slaked lime, when in contact with the buccal mucosa, acts as an irritant, causing chronic irritation and leading to dysplastic changes [9]. Tobacco, a proven carcinogen for oral cancer, contains over 60 toxic substances that can induce dysplastic changes in cells, ultimately resulting in precancerous lesions [10].

The effects of betel chewing on the oral mucosa are attributed to chronic exposure to these irritants, leading to gum irritation, tooth decay, and dysplastic changes in the oral mucosa. The current challenge in assessing betel exposure lies in the lack of awareness regarding this risk factor for oral cancer, contributing to its widespread usage. People tend to overlook the adverse effects, considering it to be health-beneficial, and cultural norms attached to this habit exacerbate the incidence of oral cancer. There is a need for an assessment factor to quantitatively and qualitatively evaluate betel chewing exposure. Unlike smoking, where a WHO standardized assessment tool for exposure measurement, pack years, exists, assessing betel exposure is not as straightforward [11]. In betel chewing, this type of easy assessment is not feasible, as the exposure and irritation in the oral mucosa leading to dysplastic changes vary with the components used in betel chewing. In our previously published letter to the editor [12], we emphasized the need to address this gap in the literature by proposing the development of a quantification tool for betel quid chewing exposure.

Nuclear Morphometry (NM) involves measuring different nuclear parameters through image analysis. While direct scanning techniques primarily analyze size and texture features, the analysis of digitized images enables the quantification of shape features as well [13]. Utilizing NM in our study was advantageous, as it can be easily measured from a noninvasive cytology specimen of buccal mucosa. This approach allows for the assessment of changes in nuclear parameters that may be too subtle to be visually perceived, providing a sensitive tool for detecting alterations associated with dysplasia.

Patients & methods

Study population

The participants in this study comprised patients reporting to the Out-Patient Department (OPD) of Government Medical College, Omandurar, Government Estate, Chennai, India between July 2023 and November 2023. The inclusion criteria involved individuals with a history of regular Betel Quid (Betel Leaf + Areca Nut) use with or without additives such as slaked lime or tobacco. Participants under 18 years old and those affected by oral cavity infections were excluded. Additionally, individuals who smoked or consumed alcohol were not considered for the study.

The target sample size for this study, calculated using the prevalence formula with a desired 95% confidence interval, estimated prevalence of 15%, and a margin of error of 5%, was determined to be 196. It was rounded off to 200 participants.

A reference group of 20 healthy volunteers with no habit or history of betel quid chewing, smoking, tobacco use, and other oral cancer-related risk factors such as sharp dentures were recruited in this study. These participants were included to establish baseline NMPs. The age range for these participants was set between 50 and 60 years to minimize the impact of age-related variations in NMPs.

Data collection method

After a detailed explanation of the study protocol, written informed consent was obtained from all the participants included in the study. A thorough history, including the frequency and duration of betel quid consumption and details about additives such as slaked lime, tobacco, or flavoring agents was collected and filled in a proforma designed for this study. To ensure uniformity, a standardized definition of one betel quid was established, consisting of two heart-shaped, medium-sized betel leaves (approximately 25 to 40 cm2) accompanied by one packet of areca nut weighing between 5 g and 10 g.

The various forms of tobacco used, such as the powdered form, combination with molasses, or the use of tobacco leaves were documented. Positive identification of tobacco use was established if participants reported using two tobacco leaves, 10–15 g of tobacco powder, or one teaspoon of tobacco-molasses paste. In the case of slaked lime usage, a positive response was recorded when participants applied one dab, equivalent to approximately 2–4 g of slaked lime, locally known as ‘chuna’. Additionally, participants were questioned about the use of any flavoring substances or other additives in their betel quid preparation. After the history was elicited, we carried out a complete examination of the oral cavity, wherein significant findings such as epithelial erosions and precancerous lesions were recorded. We also checked for individuals with ill-fitting dentures or sharp teeth. These individuals were excluded from the study because they are potential risk factors for oral cancer.

NM analysis was conducted using ImageJ software on buccal mucosa cells obtained via cytobrush and smeared onto glass slides. Cells were fixed in 95% isopropyl alcohol, stained with Papanicolaou stain, and mounted with DPX. Slides were examined at 400X magnification, and fifty high-resolution images were captured and imported into ImageJ. Image quality was enhanced using the “subtract background” function, and images were converted to 8-bit grayscale. Thresholding segmented nuclei from the background and the analyze particles function measured parameters like nuclear area, perimeter, feret diameter, raw integrated density, and nuclear roundness factor. Results were saved in a CSV file [13].

Finally, two experienced pathologists independently assessed the cytomorphological features of squamous cells present in the slides to grade dysplasia severity within the samples. In cases where conflicting reports emerged, a third pathologist review was sought to confirm the findings. From a pool of 50 captured field images, NMPs were derived for 200 randomly selected cells. We generated mean values for the NMPs that were used in the analysis.

Statistical analysis

All the data collected were entered into Microsoft Excel 2020. Statistical analysis was carried out by using Jamovi Software V.2.3.1 (Syndey, Australia) and R software. NMPs, the number of betel quids consumed per day, and the number of years betel quids consumed treated as continuous variables. On the other hand, we categorized the severity of dysplasia and the use of additives such as slaked lime or tobacco as categorical variables. To check for the normal distribution of data (normality), we utilized the Kolmogorov–Smirnov and Anderson–Darling tests.

We presented continuous variables as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median with a 25–75% interquartile range (IQR). Categorical variables were expressed as percentages with a 95% confidence interval. P values less than 0.001 were considered to be significant.

To obtain an overall view of the data collected, descriptive analysis was performed. Summary statistics such as the mean, median, standard deviation, and interquartile range were computed for each NMP. Data visualization techniques were also employed to enhance our exploration of the dataset.

We began by standardizing the NMPs using mean-centering and scaling which was achieved by subtracting individual NMP observations from the mean and dividing by the standard deviation. This process resulted in the creation of NMPs with a mean of zero and a standard deviation of one.

Since we had five NMPs, we employed PCA to reduce these variables into a single outcome variable that captured the variance across all the NMPs. We created a covariance matrix and organized the eigenvalues in descending order. We plotted a scree plot to determine the principal components that had to be retained, with a threshold eigenvalue of 0.001. Using the selected principal components, we constructed the outcome variable as a linear combination using weighted sums and loadings. This was validated through CFA.

We introduced a new variable called “Cumulative Betel Exposure,” which we calculated as the product of the number of betel quid chewed per day and the number of years betel quid was chewed. The use of additives was encoded as 1 or 2 to indicate their absence or presence.

To create the betel year model, we used multiplicative regression. The outcome variable (y) was regressed against cumulative betel exposure and additives as predictor variables. Two distinct approaches were employed for multiplicative regression modeling. The first approach involved the application of logarithmic transformations to both the dependent and independent variables prior to fitting the regression model. This transformation facilitated the derivation of a simplified equation in the log-transformed space, which was subsequently converted back to the original scale for practical clinical assessment. This approach is particularly advantageous for its simplicity and practicality.

In contrast, the second approach, utilized the original variables for modeling without any transformation. The coefficients derived from this model were directly printed without the intermediate step of constructing an equation. While this method yields a more computationally intensive and detailed equation, it provides a higher level of accuracy. The equation obtained although computationally intense, holds potential for applications in programming websites and mobile applications for the quantification of betel years.

To ascertain the effectiveness of betel years as a clinical quantification tool for assessing an individual’s lifetime exposure to betel chewing, we employed Spearman rank correlation to determine any association between betel years and Dysplasia grade. To evaluate whether dysplasia severity significantly influenced betel years, we used the Kruskal-Wallis test.

Ethical approval and informed consent

This study was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Government Medical College, Omandurar, Government Estate (Registration Number – ECR/1492/Inst/TN/2021) with approval number 74/IEC/GOMC/2023. The study protocol was modified by amendment P-74(A)/03/2024 to incorporate additional objectives.

All procedures performed in studies involving human patients were in accordance with the ethical standards of the 1964 Helsinki Declaration and its later amendments. Written informed consent was obtained from all individual participants included in the study. This article does not contain any studies with animals performed by any of the authors.

Among the participants, 57% reported the inclusion of slaked lime as an additive in their betel quid, while 16.5% indicated the presence of tobacco in the mixture. Notably, 20 participants were recruited as healthy volunteers with no prior history or habit of betel quid chewing, serving as a reference group for the analysis of NMPs and are included in the total count of 200 participants.

Discussion

Our study involved a total of 200 participants with varying betel-chewing histories; the NMPs of buccal mucosa cells were investigated. We explored the impact of betel quid chewing on NMPs and its correlation with dysplasia. CFA validated a model derived from PCA, which provided an outcome variable based on the NMPs. We used multiplicative regression models, employing logarithmic transformations and original variables, to derive models for betel years based on betel exposure and additives. Spearman rank correlation highlighted a strong correlation between betel years and dysplasia grade. Kruskal–Wallis tests further supported a significant overall difference in betel years among dysplasia categories.

The development of the “betel year” as a novel clinical quantification tool in our study marks a significant leap in evaluating the consequences linked with betel quid chewing. Similar to the well-established concept of pack years in the literature for cigarette smoking, the betel year serves as a clinical quantification tool to assess an individual’s lifetime exposure to betel quid chewing. This tool includes both the quantity of betel quids chewed and the duration of betel quid use. Additionally, it accounts for the presence of carcinogenic additives in the betel quid composition. In this study, we highlight the potential of the betel year to revolutionize risk assessment for oral cancer. Clinicians can use the betel year to enhance risk stratification, thereby advancing the field of oral health management.

Oral submucous fibrosis (OSF) is a premalignant condition predominantly associated with the habitual chewing of betel quid commonly containing areca nut. OSF is characterized by inflammation, heightened deposition of submucosal collagen, and the development of fibrotic bands in the oral and para-oral tissues, progressively limiting mouth opening [14]. Isaac et al. [15] investigated the histopathologic features of OSF, observing mucosal changes such as atrophic changes, pigment incontinence, ulceration with granulation tissue, hyperplastic changes, dysplasia, and carcinoma at rates of 74.3%, 62.8%, 40%, 25.7%, 8.6%, and 0%, respectively.

Chewing betel quid damages the oral mucosa due to the crude fibers in preparation, the toxic effects of arecoline, and the corrosive nature of slaked lime [16]. This damage leads to the development of precancerous lesions reflected as dysplastic changes in the buccal mucosa. Dysplasia manifests as changes in NMPs, which serve as a crucial parameter influenced by betel quid chewing and its additives [17].

Furthermore, the use of tobacco in betel quid as an additive is identified as a strong risk factor for erythroplakia in the Indian population [18, 19]. Leukoplakia and erythroplakia are categorized as oral potentially malignant disorders and are closely linked to the presence of oral epithelial dysplasia [20]. Numerous studies consistently highlight the strong association between betel quid chewing and the heightened risk of oral cancer [21–24].

Madathil S.A. et al. [22] defined betel quid as a mixture containing either areca nut or tobacco or both, excluding slaked lime, a common ingredient implicated in oral submucosal fibrosis [9]. They highlighted a strong non-linear dose-response relationship between betel quid chewing and oral cancer risk. This could be due to genetic susceptibility and the depletion of highly susceptible individuals at lower exposures, which leads to a plateau in risk at higher levels. It could also be attributed to biological mechanisms, such as saturation effects, where additional risk diminishes at high exposure levels. While betel year serves as a representative measure of cumulative betel quid chewing, its interpretation at high levels of consumption should be approached with caution due to the non-linear relationship between betel quid exposure and oral cancer risk.

In this study, we developed a betel-year model using two distinct approaches. The first equation is designed for practical use by clinicians in bedside or outpatient settings. Meanwhile, the second equation can be integrated into websites and mobile applications, offering a more precise quantification of betel years. This enhanced accuracy is achieved by sacrificing some simplicity in the computational process.

The utilization of betel years has the potential to stratify individuals into risk groups for precancerous lesions based on their betel quid exposure. This stratification can guide the adoption of targeted interventional methods. For instance, in the case of OSF, dietary interventions rich in beta carotene may be employed to mitigate disease severity and impede progression toward malignancy [25]. In instances of erythroplakia, recommended therapeutic approaches include surgery, utilizing either a cold knife or laser procedures [26]. For leukoplakia, the most commonly preferred treatment options involve surgical excision or CO2 laser therapy. In cases of widespread lesions, consideration may be given to photodynamic therapy [27].

The introduction of the betel year holds significant implications for public health. It enhances the precision of oral cancer risk prediction by providing a personalized assessment based on lifetime exposure to betel quid. Clinicians can utilize this information to identify individuals at higher risk, facilitating targeted interventions for those with prolonged and intensive betel quid use. Public health initiatives can use the betel year to design tailored awareness campaigns, early detection programs, and preventive measures specifically catered to high-risk populations. Furthermore, the betel year may play a crucial role in informing the development of policies aimed at regulating and controlling betel quid consumption. Public health authorities can utilize this quantitative measure to establish guidelines for safer betel quid formulations and consumption practices. Additionally, the betel year introduces a quantifiable parameter that can be incorporated into epidemiological studies, enabling the ongoing monitoring of trends in betel quid-related oral health issues over time.

This study possesses numerous strengths. First, it introduces the first-ever clinical quantification tool designed for assessing betel exposure, marking a pioneering contribution to the field and offering a standardized method for evaluating risks associated with betel quid consumption. Second, the study demonstrates flexibility by employing two distinct approaches in deriving the model, with multiplicative regression using log transformation yielding a straightforward equation. This guarantees simplicity and ease of calculation for physicians, highlighting the clinical utility of the tool in practical healthcare scenarios. The tool’s practical significance is underscored by its strong correlation with dysplasia, suggesting its potential for detecting the onset of precancerous lesions linked to oral cancer. Last, the study employs NM analysis, a method that eliminates potential biases or interobserver variances in slide assessment. The incorporation of NMPs provides a fixed and measurable dimension for assessing dysplasia of buccal mucosal cells.

Despite its strengths, our study has certain limitations. First, overstained slides may have led to falsely elevated values of raw integrated density, a marker of hyperchromasia. Some cells were unavoidably damaged during the sampling procedure, which may have introduced variability in the collected data. Moreover, our study did not involve the collection and analysis of cells from the tongue. This omission might limit our findings, especially considering that betel quid chewing tends to affect not only the buccal mucosa (27.9%) but also the tongue (23.1%) and lips (22.4%) [28]. Last, our study did not differentiate between various forms of areca nut preparations, such as supari or other processed types.

Our research provides a starting point for future investigations into the clinical quantification of betel quid chewing, measured as betel years. Future studies could explore the quantitative aspects of tobacco consumption within betel quid preparations, focusing on the grams of tobacco used. This information can be incorporated into the betel years formula, improving the accuracy of assessing tobacco exposure. Additionally, while our study predominantly examined prevalent additives in our study population, such as slaked lime and tobacco, future research could broaden the scope by assessing the carcinogenic potential of additives used in other countries. We can then expand the formula to include a more diverse range of betel quid compositions, creating a globally relevant clinical quantification tool and risk assessment tool.

Conclusion

Our study introduces a novel clinical quantification tool, “betel year”, designed to assess cumulative exposure to betel quid chewing. Employing two distinct approaches, our models offer practical utility for clinicians and enhanced precision through website and mobile application integration. These models are derived from NMPs. They demonstrate strong correlations with dysplasia, emphasizing their potential in detecting precancerous lesions associated with oral cancer. The betel year, akin to pack years for cigarette smoking, holds significant promise for revolutionizing risk assessment in oral health management. Despite study limitations, our research provides a foundation for future investigations and the development of a globally applicable tool for quantifying betel quid-related risks. The betel year holds promise for personalized risk stratification and aiding in shaping public health policies aimed at regulating betel quid consumption.

Clinical relevance

Our study introduces the concept of the “Betel Year,” a novel tool designed to quantify cumulative betel quid chewing exposure based on daily consumption, duration of use, and the use of carcinogenic additives. The Betel Year has the potential to identify individuals at higher risk of oral potentially malignant disorders and oral cancer linked to betel quid chewing. Clinically, the Betel-Year model enables healthcare providers to monitor and manage oral health effectively, offering a tool for early detection and intervention. Its implementation could significantly impact public health strategies in regions where betel quid use is prevalent by improving screening efforts and reducing the burden of associated oral diseases. The simplicity and accuracy of the model makes it accessible for widespread adoption in public health initiatives, promising to enhance early detection efforts, improve patient outcomes, and inform targeted preventive strategies. As a tool for communication, it helps people understand how much betel quid chewing affects health. It encourages awareness and helps people make informed choices.

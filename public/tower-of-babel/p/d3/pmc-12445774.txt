Submission of manuscript to IJA - Need to know more!
Authors: Rakesh Garg
Published: 2025

A high-quality paper that encompasses key elements such as originality, scientific rigour, and a suitable writing style is vital for acceptance into a journal. The Indian Journal of Anaesthesia (IJA) urges authors to include all pertinent reporting points according to the study type, as highlighted by the EQUATOR network (Enhancing the Quality and Transparency Of health Research).[1] One of the fundamental principles of ethical research publication is transparency in disclosures. IJA also requires additional information to be included in the manuscript before publication. While these requirements are outlined in the Journal Instructions to Authors, IJA ensures that this disclosure is incorporated verbatim in the manuscript.

ETHICAL APPROVAL, TRIAL REGISTRATION, AND PATIENT CONSENT

The IJA requires complete details on ethical approval, trial registration, and patient consent for manuscript submissions. Approval from the Institutional Ethics Committee and/or Institutional Review Board must be obtained before commencing the study. Additional permissions, such as those for animal research and handling radioactive materials, should also be obtained, if applicable. These steps are vital to protect participants’ rights and safety, uphold scientific and ethical standards, maintain trust, and ensure journal compliance. It is equally important to register the protocol.[2] Researchers can register their clinical study with any registry of their choice.[34] Such registration fosters transparency, accountability, prevents publication bias, avoids duplication, and supports regulation. Informed voluntary consent from participants is mandatory, with them being informed about the study’s purpose, procedures, risks, and benefits. Participants must receive an information sheet detailing all aspects of the trial and be fully informed before participation. This guarantees patient autonomy and voluntary involvement. While patient consent is generally obtained for participation, they must also be informed about the publication of the study. If individual data or photographs are used or published, this must be communicated to patients during the recruitment process.

The authors also need to disclose any medications used in the study that are not approved by statutory bodies regarding indications, doses, routes, etc. The approval details from the relevant statutory body should be included in the method section. Additionally, the use of devices or equipment, along with manufacturing details, must be documented.

OBJECTIVES, HYPOTHESES, AND OUTCOMES

IJA urges authors to specify their research objectives (primary and secondary), hypotheses (null and alternative, directionality, and rationale), and outcomes (primary and secondary, with complete definitions and measurement tools), as these are crucial for the research process.[5] Clearly articulating the hypothesis helps establish specific objectives, guides the use of suitable methods—including predefined outcome parameters—and enables accurate interpretation of results.[6] Transparent reporting of these elements improves evidence-based decision-making and increases the scientific value of the publication.

STATISTICS

IJA encourages authors to report not only basic descriptive and inferential statistics but also to include other relevant statistical analyses where necessary. Emphasising statistical parameters such as confidence intervals, odds ratios, and numbers needed to treat/harm helps facilitate a better understanding of the data.[7] This enables authors to state both clinical and practical significance alongside statistical significance. The approach for managing missing data and the assumptions made for analysis should be clearly outlined.

ORCID

The ORCID iD (Open Researcher and Contributor ID) is increasingly adopted by authors and researchers.[89] IJA requires authors to include their ORCID. The journal has integrated ORCID into its submission system, simplifying the process for authors. Each author should receive proper recognition for their publications, increasing their visibility.[10] It also serves as a centralised repository for all an author’s published work. However, due to similarities in names, a unique identifier is essential, and including the ORCID ID helps to attribute research contributions accurately. Additionally, ORCID promotes transparency and accountability in the scientific publishing process. It assists funding agencies in recognising an author’s contributions and potential collaborators. Reviewers also benefit from linking their reviews to ORCID accounts.

DATA SHARING

Research reports compile and analyse data. Nonetheless, individual data might be necessary for various reasons, such as individual participant data meta-analysis, secondary analysis, or collaborative research.[11] IJA requires authors to include a declaration regarding data sharing within the manuscript.[12] Data sharing enhances transparency, reproducibility, and integrity in research publications. A data repository will likely be the next step for the journal. IJA ensures that authors include an explicit data sharing declaration in their publications.

ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

IJA has introduced a disclosure requirement for authors regarding the use of artificial intelligence-assisted tools, including generative artificial intelligence (AI) or large language models. The use of AI is increasing across various scientific and clinical disciplines.[13] AI tools used in clinical sciences are often trained on datasets that may be insufficient due to demographic, genetic, or geographical differences. Furthermore, AI may access non-peer-reviewed content, which could lead to inappropriate outputs. Interpreting such outputs in a clinical context can also be considerably limited. Generally, these tools should be avoided when writing or analysing manuscripts. However, as AI takes on a more significant role, if authors utilise it for specific tasks, its use and scope must be disclosed within the manuscript to ensure transparency, accountability, and uphold research integrity. This also helps editors, reviewers, and readers understand the contributions made by AI tools, allowing them to evaluate potential biases or limitations introduced by automation systems.

SUPPLEMENTARY MATERIALS

IJA allows authors to include relevant information as supplementary material. Most journals set limits on word counts and the number of tables and figures permitted. However, sometimes additional essential information must be shared with editors, reviewers, and readers for clarity. Therefore, IJA permits relevant material, as deemed necessary by the author or required during the review process, to be published as supplementary content. This material remains accessible online and can be easily located through an additional statement with a hyperlink published alongside the manuscript.

AUTHORS CONTRIBUTIONS

Recognising the contributions of various authors in research is crucial. The research and publishing process involves several stages: conceptualisation, literature review, protocol drafting, case conduction, data collection, analysis, manuscript writing, editing, review, and approval. This workflow enhances research integrity and credibility while helping to prevent disputes or misattributions of authorship.[14] IJA emphasises the importance of reporting all author contributions at submission, following the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE) and Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE) standards. Such transparency helps prevent honorary or ghost authorship by individuals who did not contribute but claim authorship based on ICMJE criteria.

FINANCIAL SUPPORT, SPONSORSHIP, AND CONFLICTS OF INTEREST

The credibility of research rests not only on robust methodology and statistical validity but also on the transparency of potential influences, such as financial support, sponsorship, and conflicts of interest, which may impact the conduct and reporting of the research. The financial conflict may influence study design and research question framing to maximise the chance of favourable results, bias in data analysis or its interpretation, or avoid or delay publication, etc., The financial support or sponsorship (governmental, institutional, corporate, or philanthropic) received for conducting the research must be disclosed while submitting the manuscript. It is essential to disclose whether these funding agencies have any role in the study design, data collection, analysis, manuscript preparation, or the decision to publish. It is equally important to disclose if any authors have any association, including any personal, financial, intellectual, administrative, or professional affiliations, with the firms, companies, organisations, or institutions that can influence the outcome of the study or its reporting. This includes relationships with pharmaceutical companies, device manufacturers, consultancy roles, honoraria, or stock ownership.[15] IJA ensures that any author of the submitted manuscript who is also on the editorial board of the journal is not involved in the decision-making process of that particular manuscript and that it is handled by an independent editor.

PRESENTATION AT CONFERENCES/CMES AND ABSTRACT PUBLICATION

The research should be disseminated so that its benefits can be realised through usage by all relevant stakeholders, thereby improving patient care. In addition to publication, research is often presented at various conferences, CMEs, and other academic forums. Sometimes, abstracts or summaries of this research are published in conference proceedings, souvenirs, or supplements of other journals or organisational publications. Repeating the same research across multiple reports can lead to an overemphasis on the results, which is problematic. Therefore, it is advisable for authors to disclose any additional publications resulting from the same research. If the manuscript is based on research previously presented at a conference and/or published in abstract form, the name of the conference, location, date, and publication details of the abstract need to be disclosed.

DECLARATION OF USE OF PERMITTED TOOLS

Research involves evaluating various outcomes using scores, scales, questionnaires, and other instruments. All tools, whether existing or newly developed, must be clearly described in the methodology section. Authors should verify that these measurement tools are validated for the specific outcomes and acknowledge their developers. They must specify if the tool has been validated in the relevant population and setting. When tools are adapted (such as translated or modified), the validation process for the adapted version must be outlined or cited. Additionally, some tools are protected by copyright or require specific licences. Authors need to obtain written permission or licences from the copyright holders before using these tools and disclose this in their manuscript. For newly developed tools, authors should detail the development process, pilot testing, reliability (e.g. Cronbach’s alpha), and validation methods (construct, face, or criterion validity). The IJA mandates that authors report on the validation status of the tools and whether proper permissions were secured, if the tools are copyrighted. If permission was obtained to use copyrighted tools, scales, or instruments, or to reproduce any figures, these permissions should be mentioned in this section.

ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS

Recognising individuals or institutions who have contributed to the study but do not meet the criteria for authorship is a fundamental ethical practice. Sometimes, certain people may have provided specific inputs or contributions, but do not qualify for authorship; they should be acknowledged in this section. These supports may include statistical analysis, language editing, data collection, organising study-related sources, or administrative and logistical assistance. Any grants, institutional support, equipment, or drugs provided free of charge must be acknowledged and adequately documented.

CONCLUSION

The impact of a publication largely depends on thorough planning, reporting, and various disclosures that could influence the outcomes. IJA strongly encourages all its authors to disclose all relevant information transparently wherever applicable.

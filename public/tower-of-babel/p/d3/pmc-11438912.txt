Maximizing torque per volume index for SHESM based on two-dimensional method and meta-heuristic optimization algorithms
Authors: Alireza Hosseinpour, Saeid Haidari, Mohit Bajaj, Ievgen Zaitsev
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In this paper, a permanent magnet synchronous machine (PMSM) with an auxiliary winding (AW) on the rotor is analyzed by two-dimensional approach. This PMSM with AW (AWPMSM) can be used in many applications such as propulsion system, aircraft and traction because it includes rotor flux control capability. First, the magnetic field in different parts of AWPMSM is calculated based on Maxwell equations. Then, as a consequence of the magnetic field, the torque components, including cogging, reluctance, electromagnetic and instantaneous torque are computed. Next, torque-speed characteristic has been investigated. This AWPMSM can be located in the flux weakening mode in two ways, first one is to attenuate the rotor field by changing the direction of the AW field and the other one is to adjust the armature current angle, both of them have been investigated. After that, the overload capability and temperature effects have been analyzed. Finally, using the meta-heuristic algorithms such as genetic algorithm, particle swarm optimization, differential evolution and teaching learn base optimization the dimensions of AWPMSM and the initial angle of the rotor are determined in such a way that the torque-to-volume ratio is maximized. The influences of the type of armature winding and the magnetization patterns have also been investigated. The results obtained by the two-dimensional method have been confirmed numerically.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-72638-y.

Introduction

The wound rotor synchronous machines (WRSMs) due to the presence of field winding have the flux control capability but they generate rotor copper losses. In contrast, PMSMs have relatively lower rotor losses, and their ability to control the field flux has been limited. The hybrid excitation synchronous machines (HESMs), because of simultaneously having both field winding and permanent magnet (PM), not only have the ability to flux control, but also cause lower losses because the PM and excitation coil (EC) are respectively the main and auxiliary sources of field. Therefore, it can be utilized in a wide range of application such as aircraft, propulsion system, hybrid electrical vehicle, etc. This machine can be used as generator because it includes two sources for rotor flux, so it can set Back-EMF thanks to speed changes. The comparison of the three mentioned electrical machines has been summarized in Table 1.

Based on the flux path, HESMs can be classified into two types of series and parallel HESMs1–4. In series HESMs (SHESMs), PM and field winding fluxes pass through the same path. If they are in the same or opposite direction, the machine acts in flux-enhancing or flux-weakening mode. In this case, there is a risk of PM demagnetization because the main portion of the field winding flux passes through the PM. Due to the low magnetic permeability of the magnet, the ability of the flux control decreases1,3,4; however, the iron losses are reduced1. In parallel HESMs (PHESMs), the field winding and magnet fluxes have different routes. Therefore, the risk of demagnetization decreases and the ability to control increases; however, the iron losses increase1,4.

Lumped parameter magnetic circuit (LPMC)5 and numerical method, like finite elements method (FEM)6 are commonly used for modeling and design of the electric machines. LPMC method is not sufficiently accurate in modeling and design of HESMs. Moreover, different magnetization patterns cannot be accurately modeled in this method. Because to model different magnetization patterns, the radial and tangential components of magnetization must be considered, therefore normally radial type of magnetization pattern is analyzed by this method. Despite of the accuracy of FEM, it is a time-consuming method to be utilized in repetitive optimization algorithms. Therefore, an accurate and dimension based analytic model of HESMs is required for optimal design purposes. The analytical method (AM) can be either a one-dimensional (1-D), two-dimensional (2-D), or three-dimensional (3-D) analytical model. The AM has also been utilized for other types of PMSMs such as slotless brushless PM7, slotted brushless machines with cubic spoke-type permanent magnet8, partitioned-stator flux-reversal machines9, and brushless double mechanical port flux-switching permanent magnet machines10. A type of HESM has been introduced with a non-uniform air gap, which is analyzed by one-dimensional method11. Moreover, the one-dimensional method has also been used for high-speed machines in aircraft applications12. A spoke machine is analyzed in13 with the 2-D method in open circuit and under load operation and the parameters such as torque and back-EMF are calculated. The machine can have high number of poles. Therefore, it is suitable for low speed applications such as wind turbine14,15. An auxiliary PM can also be used instead of using the auxiliary winding16,17. Another HESM, suitable for high-speed applications, is that with superficial PM18 which has been shown in Fig. 1a,b in three- and two-dimensional topology. The machine has been analyzed for the open circuit operation and the cogging torque and back-EMF have been calculated. It is an appropriate choice for aircraft system. In this application, the investigation of overload capability, temperature effect, flux weakening mode and maximizing torque per volume are indispensable. To investigate the aforementioned characteristics, torque should be computed. The instantaneous torque includes three components: cogging, reluctance and mutual torque. Therefore, providing a model that accurately calculates torque is essential for HESM analysis. This target is performed by 2-D method in this work.

Optimal design of PMSMs is very important to achieve goals such as reducing cogging torque, torque ripple, reducing unbalanced magnetic forces, and so on. Hoseinpour et al., have numerically calculated torque and unbalance magnetic force in several specific states19. In this study, only pole arc to pole pitch ratio changes and based on the obtained results, two functions have been fitted for torque and unbalance magnetic force. The average torque/unbalance magnetic force has been maximized/minimized. Also, in the slotted stator, the torque can be determined by the LPMC. Then, the average torque and cogging torque have been optimized by using the genetic algorithm20. However, both of manuscript have been considered just one magnetization pattern and winding topology. In present manuscript, objective functions are formulated by using the two-dimensional method. Further three magnetization patterns and two stator winding topologies have been utilized. Furthermore, four algorithms are used for optimization because meta-heuristic algorithms may be trapped in a local optimum point and may not converge to the global optimum point in some problems.

The stator slot opening effect is not included and the parallel magnetization pattern is only intended18. Considering the importance of this type of machine, both open circuit and under load modes of operation are analyzed in the present paper. Besides, the slot opening effect of the stator is considered. In this paper, the magnetic fields produced by the PM, AW currents, and armature winding currents are calculated. For this purpose, an analytical model is determined for the machine. After the open circuit analysis, the cogging torque is computed. After that, the reluctance torque is determined by considering the armature reaction. The mutual and instantaneous torque, torque-speed characteristic, overload capability and temperature effect are calculated by considering both of field excitations sources and stator winding. At the final stage, torque per volume is maximized by four meta-heuristic algorithms: GA, PSO, DE and TLBO. The results will be confirmed by numerical methods.

The most important assumptions include: the axial length of the AWPMSM is considered high, so the end effect is neglected, and the problem can be solved in two-dimension. In 2-D problems, the magnetic flux density includes radial and tangential component, so the magnetic vector potential has z component. The materials of the rotor, stator, and PMs, are considered isotropic. In addition, the permeability of the rotor and stator core is infinite. Besides, the air-gap between PMs has the same properties as PM without magnetization. The eddy current reaction is also neglected7.

Flux weakening mode

The average of the instantaneous torque of AWPMSM above the base speed can be decreased by two methods, the field and the armature current weakening.

In this type of SHESM, the main source of the magnetic field is the PM and its auxiliary one is the EC. To weaken the rotor flux, the winding current can be positioned so that its magnetic field is opposite to the direction of the PM magnetic field. As mentioned, this method is resonable for a small range of synchronous speeds.

If the goal is to achieve higher speeds, the stator current phase shift should be used to reduce torque. The torque-speed characteristics with two methods for the two case studies are depicted in Figs. 6 and 7.

Conclusion

A SHESM, for aircraft system, is analyzed by 2-D analytical method. At first, the radial and tangential components of the magnetics flux densities caused by PM, EC and armature winding currents are computed separately. Then, by considering the magnetic field due to PM and EC, the cogging torque is calculated. Next, the reluctance torque is computed to only take the stator winding currents into account. After that, under the impression of the field excitation sources and armature reaction, the mutual and instantaneous torque, and T-ω characteristic are calculated. The cogging torque decreases by all-teeth winding. AWPMSM works above the synchronous speed by two ways. The first one is changing the excitation current of the AW and the second one is setting the angle of the armature current. The second method has a higher capability for flux regulation. Afterwards, overload capability and temperature effects have been investigated. From the comparison of the figures, it is clear that radial magnetization with alternate-teeth winding has maximum average torque; hence it is the best option for overload capability. Finally, torque per volume ratio is maximized by four meta-heuristic algorithms i.e. GA, PSO, DE and TLBO by considering three/two types magnetization patterns/stator winding structures. The best solution is achieved by PSO algorithm, all-teeth winding and parallel magnetization pattern. The optimal AWPMSM operates in the flux weakening mode up to three times the synchronous speed by controlling the flux of the EC. The good agreement between the AM results and the FEM results confirms the accuracy of the proposed 2-D analytical model. The proposed model can be used for the computation of the back-EMF, unbalance magnetic force, self-and mutual inductance and power loss in the future. Besides, it can also be utilized for optimization purposes such as reducing torque ripple, increasing efficiency, reducing back-EMF harmonics and etc.

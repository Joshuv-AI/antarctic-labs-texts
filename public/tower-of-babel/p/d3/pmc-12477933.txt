Engineered dermal tissue constructs using mesenchymal stromal cells and TGF-β3-loaded electrospun dressings for stimulated wound healing process
Authors: Liliana Lizarazo-Fonseca, Gustavo Salguero, Linda Guerrero, Ingrid Silva-Cote
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Highlights•A PCAT scaffold was developed using electrospinning and ionic gelation.•PCAT provides sustained release of TGF-β3 for wound healing.•PCAT is a biocompatible and bioactive scaffold that promotes proliferation of hWJ-MSCs.•PCAT/hWJ-MSCs promoted auto-skin graft integration and stimulated wound healing.Keywords: TGF-β3, Scaffold, Growth factor release, Electrospinning, Poly(ε-caprolactone), Type I collagen, hWJ-MSCs, Skin wound, Scarless, Tissue construct

Introduction

The skin is the body's largest organ and plays a fundamental role in protecting against mechanical injury, infections, fluid loss, and thermal dysregulation [1,2]. However, in certain conditions such as those associated with systemic diseases, genetic predispositions, or physical conditions, the wound healing process can be impaired, leading to fibroproliferative dysfunction This results in excessive deposition and morphological alterations s of collagen and other extracellular matrix (ECM) proteins, ultimately causing both functional and aesthetic complications [3,4].

Cutaneous wound healing is a complex, overlapping process consisting of three primary phases: inflammation, tissue formation, and remodeling. These stages involve the coordinated activity of various cell types, cytokines, and growth factors [5,6]. Notably, TGF-β isoforms exhibit distinct roles and expression patterns during the inflammatory phase, particularly when comparing fetal and adult wound responses. Fetal wounds predominantly express high levels of TGF-β3, known for its antifibrotic effects, while adult wounds are characterized by elevated levels of TGF-β1, which promote fibrosis [[7], [8], [9]].

TGF-β3 has been implicated in multiple aspects of wound healing, including re-epithelialization, modulation of inflammation, angiogenesis, and granulation tissue formation, often without inducing wound contraction [10]. Conversely, reduced levels of TGF-β3 are associated with increased fibroblast activity and delayed keratinocyte migration, thereby impairing the repair process [11,12]. Recombinant human TGF-β3 (Avotermin) has been evaluated in clinical trials for its potential to reduce hypertrophic and keloid scarring. In these studies, Avotermin was administered via intradermal injection at both margins of full-thickness incisions prior to injury and again 24 hours later. While the results demonstrated accelerated and sustained improvements in scar appearance, key limitations included the need for multiple administrations and inconsistent tissue diffusion of the growth factor [13,14]. These challenges are largely attributed to the labile nature of TGF-β3, as its biological activity is sensitive to degradation under physiological conditions, particularly fluctuations in temperature and pH, which can lead to rapid inactivation upon delivery into tissues

Biomaterials play a vital role in drug delivery systems by providing platforms for the controlled and targeted release of therapeutic agents. These systems are being actively explored for their ability to protect biomolecules from degradation, enhance target specificity, and prolong retention at the site of action. Among various encapsulation strategies, calcium alginate capsules have emerged as a promising option due to their excellent biocompatibility, low toxicity, and ability to undergo rapid gelation under mild conditions [15,16]. These capsules create a stable microenvironment that preserves growth factor activity, enabling sustained release, enhancing therapeutic efficacy, and facilitating efficient delivery to localized sites [17]. Furthermore, alginate capsules can be engineered to exhibit mechanical properties that closely mimic those of native tissues, making them well-suited for biomedical applications, including regenerative medicine and tissue engineering [18]. However, one limitation of alginate capsules is the absence of a supportive delivery platform that allows for precise and effective implantation at the injury site.

To overcome this limitation, electrospinning has been utilized to generate scaffolds with structural and biological characteristics similar to those of ECM. This technique offers several advantages, including high surface area, porosity, tunable fiber morphology, and compatibility with a wide range of polymers depending on the intended application [19]. One of the most promising strategies in tissue engineering involves seeding cells onto synthetic scaffolds to create constructs that release bioactive molecules directly at the injury site. In cutaneous applications, human Wharton’s jelly-derived mesenchymal stem cells (hWJ-MSCs) have been shown to enhance fibroblast migration and proliferation, stimulate collagen and elastin production, promote neovascularization and granulation tissue formation, and modulate inflammation—collectively contributing to accelerated and more effective wound healing [20,21].

In this study, we developed a skin tissue construct incorporating hWJ-MSCs and a TGF-β3-loaded dressing. To preserve the biological activity of TGF-β3 and enhance its therapeutic potential, we first engineered a controlled-release system. TGF-β3 was encapsulated in calcium alginate microspheres and immobilized within a sandwich-like electrospun scaffold composed of poly(ε-caprolactone) and type I collagen (PCAT). This system was thoroughly characterized both in vitro and in vivo. PCAT successfully maintained the bioactivity of TGF-β3 and enabled its sustained release for up to seven days. In vitro assays demonstrated that PCAT promoted hWJ-MSCs proliferation and modulated the secretion of growth factors. The tissue construct composed of PCAT and hWJ-MSCs was then evaluated in an in vivo wound healing model.

Histological analysis revealed that PCAT/hWJ-MSCs enhanced epidermal graft integration, as indicated by the presence of a well-defined basement membrane and dermal papillae. Additionally, treated wounds exhibited robust granulation tissue formation, characterized by long, thick collagen fibers, neovascularization, reduced inflammatory cell infiltration, and minimal wound contraction at 30 days post-treatment. These findings suggest that the PCAT/hWJ-MSCs construct holds significant promise for treating cutaneous injuries.

Materials and methods

Fabrication of electrospun scaffolds

Electrospun poly(ε-caprolactone)/type I collagen scaffolds (PCOL) were fabricated and characterized as previously described [22]. Briefly, poly ε-caprolactone (PCL) (MW ∼ 80,000 g/mol) granules (Sigma Aldrich CAS No. CAS 24,980–41–4), and calf skin type I collagen (Sigma-Aldrich CAS No. CAS 9007–34–5) were dissolved in 2,2,2-trifluoroethanol 99 % (TFE, Merck) at a 19:1 ratio with a total polymer concentration of 9 % w/v. This solution was stirred magnetically at 500 rpm for 6 h at room temperature, then loaded into a 10 ml syringe for electrospinning, and introduced into the injection system of the electrospinning equipment (HV 350R Unlimited Company Amherst power supply, NE injector model 4000 Syringe Pump Company). The electrospinning parameters were a voltage of 19 kV, a distance from the needle to the manifold of 15 cm, and an injection volume of 0.6 ml/h, at room temperature and humidity between 50 % and 55 %. The scaffolds obtained were dried at 37°C for 120 h.

Encapsulation of TGF-β3 in calcium alginate capsules

TGF-β3-loaded calcium alginate capsules were prepared by cation exchange, as described below. Briefly, 500 ng of TGF-β3 (SRP3171–10UG TGF-Beta3-Escherichia coli human recombinant Sigma-Aldrich) was mixed with 200 µl of 2 % sodium alginate solution (W201502 sodium salt of brown algal alginic acid Sigma-Aldrich St. Louis, MO, USA) at 100 rpm for 30 min. The mixed thick suspension was extruded through a 30 G needle into 100 ml of 4 % CaCl2 solution (granular anhydrous, ≤7.0 mm, ≥93.0 % Sigma-Aldrich St. Louis, MO, USA) under continuous stirring. Capsule formation occurred immediately upon contact with the crosslinking solution and agitation was maintained for 30 min to ensure complete crosslinking. Solutions were prepared in PBS (pH 7.4 ± 0.1) to maintain physiological pH. The gelation process was conducted in an ice bath (0–4°C) to protect TGF-β3 from temperature-induced denaturation. The capsules were washed three times with 0.9 % saline and stored at 4°C until further use. Capsules without TGF-β3 were prepared using the same procedure to serve as controls.

Immobilization of capsules on electrospun scaffolds

A vertical electrospinning system was used to immobilize the capsules onto the scaffold. First, 150 µl of PCOL solution was electrospun to form the base layer. Then, 30 TGF-β3-loaded capsules were evenly distributed onto this layer, followed by electrospinning an additional 150 µl of the same PCOL solution, forming a sandwich-like structure (PCAT) in which the capsules were embedded within the fibers). Control scaffolds (PCA) were prepared similarly using capsules without TGF-β3. Both PCA and PCAT were punched into 1 cm diameter discs, ensuring that each disc contained five capsules.

Quantification of TGF-β3 release over time

TGF-β3 release from the PCAT and TGF-β3-loaded capsules was quantified using an ELISA Duo Set Human TGF-β3 Kit (Catalog #: DY243 R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA) according to the manufacturer's instructions. A standard curve was generated using serial dilutions of the TGF-β3 standard (1–100 ng/mL) in 1X PBS. All standards, blanks, and samples were assayed in duplicate. To assess the release profile over time, either a 1 cm PCAT disc containing five capsules or five TGF-β3-loaded capsules were incubated in 500 µL of 1X PBS in a 24-well plate. The supernatant was collected and replaced with fresh PBS every 24 hours for 8 days. The cumulative release was calculated based on the ELISA readings.

Scaffolds sterilization

The PCA and PCAT were cut using a 1 cm diameter punch, ensuring that each disc had the same number of capsules (five capsules). These scaffolds were placed in 48-well culture plates and then sterilized using γ-irradiation equipment, Gamma-Medical with Cesium-137 BIOBEAM 2000 radiation source (Eckert & Ziegler BEBIG GmbH, Germany), at 50 Gy for 30 min, at room temperature under dark conditions.

Cell adhesion and proliferation assays

hWJ-MSCs were isolated from umbilical cords obtained from healthy pregnant women after informed consent, in accordance with protocols approved by the Ethics Committee of the Secretaría Distrital de Salud de Bogotá, Colombia. Umbilical cord blood was screened for infectious diseases, including hepatitis B and C, syphilis, Chagas disease, HIV, and HTLV I and II. The immunophenotype and multilineage potential of the hWJ-MSCs were evaluated as previously reported [24].

Green fluorescent protein-labeled hWJ-MSCs (GFP-hWJ-MSCs), generated via lentiviral transduction and provided by the Advanced Therapy Unit of IDCBIS, were also used for visualization studies. Both PCA and PCAT scaffolds were seeded with hWJ-MSCs and GFP-hWJ-MSCs at a density of 5 × 10⁴ cells/cm². Cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM; Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) supplemented with 10 % human platelet lysate (hPL), antibiotic-antimycotic solution (100X; Gibco®), and heparin (Fisher BioReagents, USA), and maintained at 37°C in a humidified 5 % CO₂ atmosphere. Cells were grown to 70–80 % confluence in T75 flasks, harvested using TrypLE Express (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), and seeded onto the scaffolds. Cell adhesion and morphology were evaluated using fluorescence microscopy at 1- and 5-days post-seeding.

The proliferation of hWJ-MSCs cultured on PCA, PCAT, and tissue culture plate (TCP) was also assessed using a resazurin cell viability assay (resazurin sodium salt powder (CAS No.: 62,758–13–8) Sigma-Aldrich (St Louis, MN, USA). After 1, 3, 5, and 7 days of culture, redox activity in the cytosol of viable cells was evaluated. Briefly, scaffolds were placed in a 24-well plate, and 5 × 10⁴ hWJ-MSCs were seeded under each condition, afterward the medium was replaced with a mixture of 500 μl of fresh culture medium with 1 % resazurin solution and incubated for 4 h. The supernatant was transferred to a 96-well plate and transferred to a Synergy MX spectrophotometer microplate reader (Biotek Synergy, Winooski, VT, USA) to measure the reduction of resazurin (nonfluorescent blue dye) to resorufin (pink fluorescence) at an excitation wavelength of 570 nm and an emission wavelength of 600 nm. The absorbance ratio (570 nm/600 nm) was calculated for all treatments at all time points. To calculate hWJ-MSCs viability, the data were normalized using 24 h post-culture as the baseline growth for each condition.

Growth factors quantification by hWJ-MSCs on the scaffolds

hWJ-MSCs were seeded on PCA and PCAT and cultured under standard conditions with supplemented culture medium for 12, 24, 48, 72, and 96 h. At each time point, conditioned media were collected, centrifuged for 6 min at 1200 rpm, and stored at −20°C until use. The supplemented culture medium (DMEMsup) was used as a control and is shown in the graphs with a dotted line to determine the number of growth factors available in the cell culture. Additionally, hWJ-MSCs cultured on standard tissue culture plastic (TCP) served as a control for cells cultured without scaffolds.

Conditioned media were analyzed for the detection and quantification of TGF-β isoforms -β1, -β2, and -β3 using a Luminex LXSAHM-03 (R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Briefly, 100 µl of the sample was treated with 25 µl of 1 N HCl and 25 µl of NaOH/HEPES and 200 µl of conditioned medium were added. Then, 100 µl of each sample was transferred to a 96-well plate along with 50 µl of magnetic microparticles and incubated for 2 h. It was then washed three times with the wash solution, and the biotin-antibody mixture was added and incubated for 1 h. After washing, streptavidin-PE was added to each well and incubated for 30 min. Finally, the microparticles were washed and resuspended in 200 µl of buffer for quantification.

In addition, vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGFaa), Platelet-derived growth factor (PDGFaa), basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF), Hepatocyte Growth Factor (HGF), Interleukin-10 (IL-10), Epidermal Growth Factor (EGF), and Angiopoietin-I (Ang I) were quantified using a magnetic immunoassay (Luminex LXSAHM-07 R&D Systems a Biotechne Brand USA) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Briefly, 50 µl of conditioned medium was incubated with 50 µl of magnetic microparticles for 2 h. The microparticles were washed and incubated with the biotin-antibody mixture for 1 h. After washing, streptavidin-PE was added and the mixture was incubated for 30 min. The microparticles were washed and 200 µl of buffer was added for subsequent quantification.

All incubations were performed at room temperature, protected from light, and under constant agitation. Notably, the concentration of interleukin-10 (IL-10) was below the detection limit.

In vivo evaluation of wound healing

This study used six female Yorkshire pigs, approximately 10 weeks old and weighing 40–45 kg, obtained from the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of ANESTCOL S.A., Bogotá, Colombia, approved protocol 094. Prior to surgery, the porcine models were subjected to 12 h of solid starvation and 6 h of liquid starvation to avoid complications during anesthesia. Porcine models were placed in the prone position on an aseptic metal surface for the surgical procedure and anesthetized with 3 % isoflurane for 10 min. A sterile acetate frame was used to demarcate the wound area; the area of each lesion was 9 cm2 (3 × 3 cm), with 5 cm between the wounds. Full-thickness wounds were surgically created on the back of each animal with a scalpel (removal of the epidermis, dermis, and hypodermis) and the incision was extended to the subcutaneous cellular tissue.

The treatment groups included: PCOL (electrospun scaffold without TGF-β3), PCOL/hWJ-MSCs, PCAT (scaffold loaded with TGF-β3), and PCAT/hWJ-MSCs. In the cell-seeded groups, the constructs were applied with the hWJ-MSC-loaded side facing the wound bed to maximize cell–tissue interaction. Each treatment was implanted into a separate wound and was placed and covered with an epidermal skin graft taken from the underside of the dorsum of each animal using a dermatome. Grafts were sutured along the edge, and stitched in the center of each wound Untreated wounds served as a negative control (untreated control), with each treatment allocated to different anatomical regions (cephalic and dorsal). Then, each wound was covered with a clear silicone sheet, and dressing was applied to the back of the animal

After surgery, the porcine models were individually housed and maintained at a relative humidity of 50–70 % and a temperature of 20–25°C under constant monitoring. Dipyrone (2 ml/25 kg) for pain management and cephalexin (15 mg/kg) and flunixin (20 mg/kg) for wound infection prevention were administered every 12 hours for two weeks. Photographs of the skin wounds were taken using an optical digital camera at 8, 22, and 30 days after surgery. To evaluate wound healing activity, the percentage of wound contraction was calculated using Equation 3 [24].

After 30 days, the biomodels were euthanized, and tissue samples from all conditions were collected and stored in 4 % paraformaldehyde PFA. Samples were then processed in paraffin, and 4 µm-thick sections were obtained for histological analysis. Sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E), Masson's trichrome, and periodic acid-Schiff (PAS). Histological images were acquired using a motorized DMi8 microscope equipped with a K5 monochrome camera and a DMC4500 color camera (Leica Microsystems, Germany) at 10x, 20x, and 40x magnifications.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism version 8.0.1. Variables were reported as means and standard deviations. All experiments were performed in triplicates. Statistical data analysis was performed using two-way ANOVA, Tukey's post-hoc test, and repeated-measures analysis of variance (ANOVA) for multiple group analyses. A p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Calcium alginate capsules obtained by the extrusion method had a diameter of 1.56 mm ± 0.66 mm (Fig. 1a-b). The size of the capsules depended on the gauge of the needle used; the capsules exhibited a smooth and uniform surface with sf 0.03 ± 0.002 calculated by equation 1.

The total amount of experimentally encapsulated TGF-β3 was determined, with the maximum cumulative release point for PCAT and TGF-β3-loaded capsules using equation 2, obtaining encapsulation rates of 68.39 % and 60.99 %, respectively.

TGF-β3 release profile

The release profiles of TGF-β3 from PCAT and TGF-β3-loaded calcium alginate capsules were evaluated over time (Fig. 1c–e). TGF-β3 release from PCAT continued over 7 days, reaching a cumulative concentration of 42.74 ± 2.52 ng/mL, while the capsules released TGF-β3 for up to 5 days, reaching 35.92 ± 3.10 ng/mL (Fig. 1d). At 24 hours, both conditions showed comparable release: PCAT (7.96 ± 0.63 ng/mL) and capsules (7.18 ± 2.23 ng/mL). From day 2 onwards, PCAT exhibited a consistent release rate of approximately 6 ± 2 ng/mL per day. In contrast, the capsules showed greater variability, with 4.50 ± 0.81 ng/mL on day 3 and 11.78 ± 1.74 ng/mL on day 5, but no measurable release beyond day 5 (Fig. 1e). To ensure accurate normalization, the number of capsules used in the release assay was quantified for both conditions. The observed differences in release profiles are attributed to two factors: (1) variability in TGF-β3 loading between individual capsules and (2) the presence of the electrospun scaffold in PCAT, which likely acted as a diffusion barrier, prolonging the release.

hWJ-MSCs adhesion and proliferation on PCA and PCAT

Cell adhesion was evaluated using GFP-labeled hWJ-MSCs over a seven days period. Images of the cell culture on TCP (Figure a-d), PCA (Fig. 2b-e), and PCAT (Fig. 2c-f) on days 1 and 5 were obtained. All conditions supported cell adhesion, with cells displaying a fibroblast-like morphology on day 1. Over time, PCAT demonstrated greater cell proliferation than TCP and PCA, which was confirmed by quantitative analysis (Fig. 2g). The significant increase in proliferation observed after day 5 was attributed to the mitogenic activity of TGF-β3, suggesting that encapsulation in calcium alginate, immobilization within the PCOL electrospun scaffold, and subsequent sterilization did not impair its biological activity.

Growth factor production of hWJ-MSCs modulated by the scaffolds

Growth factors including Angiopoietin-1, EGF, PDGF, and TGF-β1 were detected in high concentrations in the culture medium supplemented with platelet lysate (DMEMsup). TGF-β1 was present at 9213.20 pg/ml in DMEMsup. After 48 h of culture, only TCP showed increased levels (14,088.18 ± 1570.40 pg/ml), while PCA and PCAT exhibited similar values, suggesting that TGF-β3 had no significant effect on TGF-β1 expression (Fig. 3a).

In contrast, TGF-β2 production was significantly elevated in PCAT (510.13 ± 151.27 pg/ml) at 96 h, compared to PCA (29.66 ± 9.75 pg/ml) and TCP (98.96 ± 34.84 pg/ml), showing a fivefold increase (Fig. 3b). Angiopoietin-1 production was lower in PCA (7033.90 ± 911.64 pg/ml) and PCAT (2717.09 ± 1136.60 pg/ml) compared to TCP (11,504.07 ± 3853.14 pg/ml) (Fig. 3d). A similar trend was observed for VEGF-aa, which was markedly reduced in PCAT (12.02 ± 6.72 pg/ml) compared to TCP (1120.85 ± 959.68 pg/ml) (Fig. 3e). These decreases may be due to TGF-β3-mediated modulation of intracellular signaling pathways regulating growth factor expression.

Conversely, bFGF was significantly increased in PCA (247.14 ± 175.40 pg/ml) and PCAT (264.69 ± 86.07 pg/ml) relative to TCP (60.76 ± 37.48 pg/ml) at 96 h (Fig. 3f). HGF also showed increased levels in PCA (6470.46 ± 1693.56 pg/ml), PCAT (1486.82 ± 1028.02 pg/ml), and TCP (11,504.07 ± 3853.14 pg/ml), compared to just 74.02 pg/ml in DMEMsup (Fig. 3g). PDGF-aa (565.05 pg/ml) and EGF (74.3 pg/ml) were present in DMEMsup due to the use of platelet lysate (Figs. 3h–i). These mitogens were consumed by hWJ-MSCs, contributing to their proliferation. VEGF-aa, bFGF, and PDGF-aa levels varied significantly between replicates, indicating donor-specific modulation of growth factor expression.

Wound healing involves a series of processes, including coagulation, inflammation, proliferation, tissue regeneration, and remodeling. Growth factors play key roles in this process. VEGF, bFGF, and EGF promote the proliferation and migration of fibroblasts, keratinocytes, and endothelial cells, which are critical for granulation tissue formation and angiogenesis. TGF-β3 contributes to the regulation of inflammation and promotes proliferation and collagen synthesis, which are essential for granulation and wound repair.

PCAT and PCAT/ hWJ-MSCs promotes skin repair process

Dressings were removed on the eighth postoperative day, and photographs were taken of wounds treated with PCOL (Fig. 4a), PCOL/hWJ-MSCs (Fig. 4d), PCAT (Fig. 4g), PCAT/hWJ-MSCs (Fig. 4j), and the untreated control group (Fig. 4m). Wound healing was re-evaluated on day 22, at which time all experimental groups showed a steady and progressive reduction in wound area (Figs. 4b, 4e, 4h, and 4k). In contrast, the untreated control group exhibited marked wound contraction (Fig. 4n), characteristic of secondary intention healing, where wound edges are approximated by the contractile activity of myofibroblasts.

The final evaluation on day 30 revealed distinct healing patterns among the groups. Although none of the treatments achieved complete wound closure, all demonstrated robust graft integration (∼70 %), with evidence of satisfactory re-epithelialization and neovascularization (Figs. 4c, 4f, 4i, and 4l). Importantly, only minimal centripetal contraction was observed in these groups. In sharp contrast, the untreated control group displayed pronounced contraction, leading to secondary closure (Fig. 4o).

These findings were corroborated by image analysis (Fig. 4p), which confirmed that the untreated control group exhibited a significantly higher percentage of wound closure (72.82 ± 6.43 %) compared with the treated groups (PCOL: 55.87 % ± 8.82 %; PCOL/hWJ-MSCs: 64.02 % ± 14.76 %; PCAT: 39.24 % ± 2.38 %; PCAT/hWJ-MSCs: 41.89 % ± 8.73 %). The rapid and substantial wound reduction observed in the control group is primarily attributable to contraction-driven secondary healing. In contrast, the treated groups appear to promote healing through re-epithelialization rather than contraction. These differences were statistically significant in the treatments that included TGF-β3.

Histological analysis evaluated the presence of ER, DP, IC, and BV. In the PCAT/hWJ-MSCs group (Fig. 4o), graft integration was evident through the presence of epidermal structures such as papillary projections from the dermis to the epidermis, epidermal ridges, and dermal papillae, as well as well-organized granulation tissue. PCOL/hWJ-MSCs (Fig. 4m) showed signs of active tissue repair, although papillary projections were less pronounced compared to the PCAT/hWJ-MSCs group. These findings suggest that TGF-β3 was biologically active in the wound environment and played a role in modulating cellular processes to support tissue repair. In both groups, the dermal connective tissue was organized parallel to the tissue surface, while newly formed blood vessels were oriented perpendicularly, contributing to granulation tissue formation.

In the PCOL and PCAT groups (Figs. 4l and 4n, respectively), granulation tissue was characterized by the presence of mesenchymal stromal cells, fibroblasts (elongated cells), and angioblasts forming capillaries containing red blood cells (RBCs). A high degree of neovascularization was observed, indicating active tissue regeneration. However, histological examination also revealed a necrotic surface (scab composed of dead cells), infiltration of inflammatory cells, and the absence of papillary projections—suggesting that wound healing remained incomplete at the time of biopsy. In contrast, the untreated control group (Fig. 4p) exhibited complete wound closure; however, the epidermis consisted of heterogeneous, immature collagen (varied light pink shades), increased inflammation, and low vascular density. These characteristics indicate scar tissue formation with lower structural and functional quality compared to the treated groups.

These findings were further supported by quantifying the cells and cellular structures involved in ECM remodeling during healing (Fig. 5). The number of polymorphonuclear cells (PMNs), particularly neutrophils—key players in the acute inflammatory phase—was reduced in both the PCAT and PCAT/hWJ-MSCs groups compared to PCOL (Fig. 5a), although this reduction was not statistically significant relative to the untreated control. This trend is clinically relevant, as physiological wound healing involves a decline in PMNs after the acute phase, followed by an increase in macrophages and lymphocytes that facilitate tissue remodeling (Figs. 5b–c). These results suggest that the incorporation of TGF-β3, similarly to hWJ-MSCs, attenuates the acute inflammatory response and promotes improved healing, characterized by reduced fibrosis and more organized tissue architecture. Additionally, significant differences in neovascularization were observed (Fig. 5d). The untreated control exhibited reduced vascular density, suggesting a faster transition to the tissue maturation phase. In contrast, the sustained presence of highly vascularized granulation tissue in the PCAT group indicates an ongoing and potentially more effective remodeling process, which may ultimately result in higher-quality scar formation. Furthermore, analysing histiocyte counts (Fig. 5e) revealed a notably higher presence of these cells in the untreated control group. This suggests a prolonged or dysregulated inflammatory response, which could hinder proper wound healing. PCAT and PCAT/hWJ-MSCs, on the other hand, showed a reduced number of histiocytes, indicating a more regulated and efficient transition towards the resolution phase of inflammation. This contributes to a more favourable environment for tissue regeneration.

To assess the quality of ECM produced in the PCAT and PCAT/hWJ-MSCs groups, these were compared to the untreated control through histological characterization using Masson's trichrome and PAS staining to visualize collagen fibers and the basement membrane (BM) (Fig. 6). In the PCAT/hWJ-MSCs group (Fig. 6b), thick, abundant, and intensely eosinophilic collagen fibers were observed, aligned parallel to the tissue surface. BV containing red blood cells were also evident. PAS staining confirmed successful graft integration, revealing a well-defined basal layer and a clearly differentiated basement membrane separating the epidermis and dermis (Fig. 6e). In the PCAT group (Fig. 6d), collagen fibers appeared as intensely eosinophilic fibrillar proteins organized in short bundles. In contrast, the untreated control (Fig. 6f) displayed a mixture of short and long collagen bundles aligned parallel to the tissue surface, along with BV containing red blood cells, but lacked the structural organization observed in the treated groups.

Discussion

Exogenous administration of TGF-β3 has become important in the treatment of extensive and chronic wounds due to its key role in promoting scarless healing. It achieves this by reducing fibronectin, collagen I, and collagen III deposition during the early stages of wound healing and by modulating ECM deposition. A more gradual and organized ECM deposition improves the architecture of the neoderm, enhances vascularization and connective tissue formation, and regulates fibroblasts activity ultimately reducing the risk of aberrant healing [20,25]. Avotermin has been used in clinical trials for the prophylactic treatment of human scars by direct injection over the wound edges at doses ranging from 25 to 500 ng/100 μl per linear centimeter (NCT00847925, NCT00847795, and NCT00629811) [26]. Notably, a 50 ng/100 μl dose accelerated healing and improved collagen production and organization, leading to better scar appearance from sixth months post-treatment. However, direct administration of avotermin has been associated with adverse effects, such as erythema and edema at the wound site [27,28]. Additionally, direct application of growth factors is limited by poor stability, low absorption, and rapid degradation in vivo [13].

To address these challenges, biomaterial-based delivery systems for growth factors are being developed to enhance their stability, control release kinetics, and improve therapeutic efficacy. Systems such as micro- and nanoparticles, scaffolds, and hydrogels have been shown to protect growth factors from degradation and support localized delivery for optimal therapeutic outcomes [[29], [30], [31]]. Encapsulation was based on electrostatic interactions, leveraging the anionic nature of alginate and the cationic properties of TGF-β3. These interactions, along with the physical barrier of the matrix, facilitated a controlled release while preserving the growth factor's biological activity. Calcium alginate has previously been used to encapsulate glycoenzymes [33], enable dual delivery of growth factors, and encapsulate human keratinocytes [34], demonstrating its versatility [35,36]. Furthermore, it has been demonstrated that the ion-gelation extrusion method allows for the control of variables such as concentration, distance between the needle tip and the bath, and immersion time, resulting in a uniform diameter distribution and a high sphericity factor (0.03 ± 0.002), as described in this study previously [23]. However,while calcium alginate capsules offer a biocompatible and gentle environment for delivering growth factors such as TGF-β3, their clinical utility is often limited by burst release and insufficient mechanical stability, especially under physiological conditions. These limitations can compromise sustained delivery and therapeutic efficacy. To address this, our study incorporates the capsules into an electrospun polycaprolactone/type I collagen (PCOL) scaffold to form the PCAT construct. This scaffold provides mechanical reinforcement, reduces the likelihood of burst release, and protects the capsules from deformation or premature degradation. The fibrous architecture also mimics the ECM, enhancing integration and retention at the wound site. Furthermore, the system enables localized delivery of TGF-β3 and can be enriched with hWJ-MSCs to synergistically support wound healing through paracrine signaling and immunomodulation. When TGF-β3-loaded capsules were sandwiched between electrospun scaffolds, the release was sustained over 7 days, with an encapsulation efficiency of 42.7 ng from 62.5 ng initially loaded (equivalent to 68 %).

Cytocompatibility was confirmed through GFP-hWJ-MSCs adhesion and resazurin-based proliferation assays, showing low cytotoxicity of the polymers used. A significant difference in proliferation was observed between PCAT and PCA at 96 h, indicating the release of bioactive TGF-β3 into the cell culture medium. This finding confirms that TGF-β3 retained its biological activity following encapsulation and incorporation into the polymeric matrix. These findings are consistent with previous studies demonstrating TGF-β3’s ability to promote cell proliferation, chemotaxis, and modulate the cellular secretome [[37], [38], [39]]. Furthermore, immunoassays confirmed that TGF-β3 released from PCAT induced increased expression of TGF-β2 and TGF-β3 by hWJ-MSCs, indicating the activation of intracellular signaling pathways. Encapsulated TGF-β3 induces other intracellular events, such as the regulation of growth factors involved in cell differentiation. TGF-β3 also functions as a signaling molecule that governs various cellular responses, including replication, growth, differentiation, apoptosis, and factor secretion, explaining the observed upregulation of TGF-β2 in PCAT [40,41].

An increased bFGF production was observed in hWJ-MSCs cultured on both PCA and PCAT. Previous studies have demonstrated that bFGF enhances wound closure by activating vascular endothelial cells and promoting cell proliferation, fibroblast and keratinocyte migration, as well as epithelial–mesenchymal transition [42,43]. In addition, hWJ-MSCs utilize exogenous growth factors present in the supplemented culture medium such as VEGFaa, angiopoietin I, EGF, and PDGF, for their proliferation and metabolic activity [44]. The development of a construct incorporating key growth factors, including TGF-β2, TGF-β3, bFGF, and EGF, is critical for effective skin wound repair and regeneration. These factors regulate multiple processes essential for tissue healing, including cell proliferation, differentiation, migration, as well as endothelial cell activation and neovascularization [45,46].

Although complete wound closure was not achieved with PCAT and PCAT/hWJ MSCs after 30 days, histological analysis revealed organized dermal connective tissue aligned parallel to the tissue surface and blood vessels oriented perpendicular, indicative of active granulation. Previous studies have shown that the incorporation of TGF-β3 into scaffolds enhances their regenerative potential by modulating cellular behavior [11,47]. Constructs incorporating PCAT and hWJ-MSCs promoted full integration of the epidermal skin grafts, evidenced by a mature epidermis with defined basement membranes and epidermal ridges. While full closure required more time, no local adverse effects such as erythema or edema were observed in the porcine models.

Immune response analysis showed that neutrophils, activated upon injury by alarmins, cytokines, and chemokines, initially adopt a phagocytic phenotype [48]. In the presence of foreign material, pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) further stimulate neutrophils, leading to the production of f reactive oxygen species (ROS) and the formation of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs), composed of chromatin, antimicrobial peptides, and enzymes such as elastase and myeloperoxidase [[49], [50], [51]]. These processes are critical for host defense and scaffold interaction. Resolution of inflammation is mediated by immune cell interactions. Neutrophil apoptosis and subsequent efferocytosis by macrophages triggers anti-inflammatory signaling through IL-10, TGF-β, and lipid mediators [52]. This resolution phase is essential for ECM remodeling. Histological analysis indicated a heightened immune response in PCOL-treated wounds compared to scaffolds containing TGF-β3, likely due to the regulatory effects of the growth factor and/or hWJ-MSCs

While the untreated control showed complete closure, the resulting tissue was characterized by poor quality, likely due to rapid fibrotic repair. During acute inflammation resolution, macrophages deposit collagen to prevent tissue failure, leading to scarring that often compromises functional and aesthetic outcomes [53]. In contrast, the PCAT/hWJ-MSCs treatment moderated the inflammatory response and improved ECM deposition and vascularization, yielding better-organized tissue.

Study limitations include an incomplete understanding of the mechanisms through which TGF-β3 modulates regeneration and the complexity of natural healing processes. Further research is needed to explore the combined effects of growth factors and cell delivery on optimal skin tissue regeneration. These challenges highlight the need for continued investigation of alternative treatment strategies to stimulate skin regeneration.

Conclusions

A TGF-β3 release system was developed by encapsulating the growth factor in calcium alginate beads and immobilizing them into a sandwich-structured electrospun scaffold composed of poly(ε-caprolactone) and type I collagen (PCAT). This system preserved TGF-β3 bioactivity, sustained its release for up to 7 days and maintained its regenerative potential. This scaffold supported hWJ-MSCs proliferation and provided a three-dimensional architecture that mimicked the native ECM, improving cell adhesion and migration.

This study demonstrated that PCAT treatment modulated the secretion of growth factors by hWJ-MSCs that are critical for cutaneous wound repair. This modulation allows for the generation of bioactive, manipulable, and implantable scaffolds that promote skin wound healing. In vivo, wounds treated with PCAT/hWJ-MSCs demonstrated that integration of the epidermal skin graft into a biosynthetic membrane was possible, as evidenced by complete coverage with mature epidermis, epidermal ridges, and basement membranes. In addition, the dermis exhibited connective tissue parallel to the tissue surface and blood vessels perpendicular to the connective tissue, forming granulation tissue, a characteristic of a successful healing process.

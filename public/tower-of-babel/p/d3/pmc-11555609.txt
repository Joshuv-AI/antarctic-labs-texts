Prognostic Nutritional Index is Related to All‐Cause Mortality in Patients With Stage IV Colorectal Cancer Treated With Capecitabine: Single‐Center 24‐Month Observational Study in Vietnam
Authors: Hung Nguyen Quang, Luan Nguyen Duc, Hieu Hoang Van, Hai Vu Anh, Kien Nguyen Trung, Thang Le Viet
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAimTo determine the mortality rate and the predictive value of the prognostic nutritional index (PNI) for all‐cause mortality during the 24 months in patients with stage IV colorectal cancer treated with capecitabine.MethodsWe conducted a study on 87 stage IV colorectal cancer patients treated with capecitabine. Before the day of treatment, all patients were measured CEA and CRP‐hs levels and calculated neutrophil/lympho ratio (NLR) and PNI. Patients were monitored and collected drug side effects and mortality for 24 months.ResultsThe mortality rate of study subjects was 60.9%. CRP‐hs, NLR, and PNI were independent factors associated with 24‐month mortality in patients with stage IV colorectal cancer (p < 0.05 to p < 0.01). At a cut‐off value of 38.51, PNI was a predictor for mortality, with the area under the curve (AUC) of 0.88 and p < 0.001.ConclusionsPNI was a good predictor of all‐cause mortality in patients with stage IV colorectal cancer treated with capecitabine for 24 months.

Introduction

Colorectal cancer is the third most common cancer in the world [1, 2]. In Vietnam, the rate of advanced adenocarcinoma in colorectal cancer is from 7.1% to 23.1% after screening 103,542 individuals [3]. Most colorectal cancers develop from adenomas due to mutated activation of oncogenes and inactivation of tumor suppressor genes [4, 5]. The process goes through 4 steps: (1) Initiation, (2) promotion, (3) progression, and (4) metastasis [6]. Surgery and radical tumor removal are the main treatment methods for colorectal cancer that have not progressed and have not metastasized. When cancer has metastasized or recurred after surgery, other treatments, including chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and molecular targeted therapy, are used [7]. Though, with metastatic or recurrent colorectal cancer, the 5‐year survival rate is only 15% [8]. The 5‐fluorouracil, folinic acid, oxaliplatin, and irinotecan (FOLFOXIRI) regimen has been the first choice for metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) [7]. In addition, mCRC patients can use Capecitabine (a fluoropyrimidine precursor) to replace 5‐fluorouracil in the above regimen, which has been proven to be effective and safe [9, 10, 11].

Malnutrition in cancer patients leads to general weakness of the body, reduces immunity, reduces treatment effectiveness, reduces tolerance to drug side effects, and prolongs recovery time [12]. Inflammation is a factor that accelerates tumor growth and increases mCRC through cell proliferation, angiogenesis, invasion, metastasis, and suppression of the patient's immune system [13]. Prognostic nutritional index (PNI) is a representative measure of nutritional status and inflammation, in which PNI is calculated from serum albumin and total peripheral lymphocyte count [14]. PNI is a clinically meaningful indicator in screening, predicting progression, outcome as well as recurrence in colorectal cancer patients [15, 16, 17]. Recently, a study by Kocak MZ et al. demonstrated that PNI plays a role in the prognosis of colon cancer patients before and after chemotherapy [18]. However, the role of PNI in predicting mortality in patients with stage IV colorectal cancer has not been addressed by many studies. For those reasons, in this study, we hypothesize that PNI before treatment for stage IV colorectal cancer with Capecitabine has a role in predicting mortality in these patients.

Materials and Methods

Study Design

A descriptive cross‐sectional study with longitudinal follow‐up was conducted in 87 stage IV colorectal cancer patients treated with capecitabine at the Oncology Center, Bach Mai Hospital, Hanoi, Vietnam, from 12/2020 to 12/2023. Patient selection criteria included patients > 18 years old, confirming the diagnosis of stage IV colorectal adenocarcinoma (including patients with recurrence after surgery or newly discovered but with metastases in many places), without indication for surgery, having indications and receiving treatment with capecitabine for a sufficient period, expected survival of ≥ 12 weeks, a performance status (PS) of 0–2, WBC ≥ 3.5 G/L, platelet count ≥ 100 G/L, and hemoglobin ≥ 100 g/L. We excluded patients with acute infections, suspected surgical disease requiring surgery, and pregnant or breastfeeding women from the study. All patients signed consent forms to participate in the study and were approved by the Hospital's ethics committee (No: 123/QĐ‐BVBM).

Treatment and Monitoring of Patients

The recommended capecitabine dose is 1000–1250 mg/m2 twice daily, but our patients started at 1000 mg/m2 twice daily (equivalent to 2000 mg/m2 total daily dose) for 14 days, followed by 7 days off medication, a 3‐week cycle according to the instructions of the Ministry of Health Vietnam and the Japanese Society for Cancer of the Colon and Rectum [7]. When the patient vomited, the dose was reduced to 850 mg/m2 × 2 daily. If the patient still vomits and has diarrhea or cannot tolerate it, the treatment will be stopped. Patient assessment was conducted before and after every 3 cycles of chemotherapy. Clinical, laboratory, PNI endpoints, and comorbidities were collected immediately before each patient's first day of capecitabine administration. PNI was calculated as 10 × albumin level (g/dL) + 0.005 × total lymphocyte count (per mm3) [14]. During treatment, patient events were collected: The main event was all‐cause mortality, collected according to treatment time, and side effects of the medication were also collected from the patients. After 24 months of follow‐up, based on patient status, patients were divided into 2 groups: mortality group and survival group. To identify other factors related to mortality, we also collected and calculated the NLR (NLR = ANC/ALC, in which ANC is the absolute neutrophil count in cells per microliter and ALC is the absolute lymphocyte count in cells per microliter of peripheral blood), plasma CRP‐hs, albumin, and CEA concentrations.

Statistical Methods

All the normal distribution continuous data were represented by mean and standard deviation and were analyzed using the Student t‐test. All skewed distributions were represented by the median (25 percentile–75 percentile), analyzed by Mann‐Whitney U and Kruskal‐Wallis tests. Categorical data were presented by frequency and percentage and analyzed using the Chi‐square test. Multivariable adjusted regression analysis was performed to identify the predictors of all‐cause mortality. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves with the area under the curve (AUC) were calculated to predict patients' mortality after 24 months of follow‐up. Statistical analysis was done using Statistical Package for Social Science (SPSS) version 20.0 (Chicago, IL, USA). A p‐value < 0.05 was considered as significant.

Discussion

Results of Treatment of Patients With Stage IV Colorectal Cancer Using Capecitabine

In this study, we had 87 patients who were prescribed and received capecitabine at the full dose and for a suitable duration, included in the analysis and evaluation of treatment results. Treating stage IV colorectal cancer patients with capecitabine, we got relatively good results after 24 months of follow‐up. The side effects of capecitabine are not many, the highest being nausea in 37.9% (33/87 patients) and the lowest being increased blood creatinine in 3.4% (3/87 patients; Table 1). In the world, several authors have published results of treating stage IV colorectal cancer with capecitabine. Stathopoulos et al. evaluated the results of capecitabine treatment on 17 colorectal cancer patients, using an initial dose of 1200 mg/m2 × 2 daily. The disease was stable at 70.59%, and side effects occurred at 11.76%. The authors concluded that this is a well‐tolerated drug with low toxicity [19]. As reported by Lee et al. on 51 patients with metastatic colorectal cancer, progressing in 5‐FU/LV‐containing regimens (median: two regimes) were treated with capecitabine 1250 mg/m2 twice daily (days 1–14 repeated every 3 weeks). The results showed that the response rate to capecitabine was low at 2.0% (1/51 patients). However, 53% (27/51 patients) had stable disease [20].

Colorectal cancer has a high mortality rate, with 5‐year relative survival rates ranging from more than 90% in stage I disease patients to about 10% in stage IV disease patients [21]. For mCRC patients or recurrent colorectal cancer after surgery, survival time is shorter. Today, with scientific advances in multimodality treatment, the average survival time in mCRC patients can be up to 24–28 months [22, 23]. Our study results show that after 12 months, the survival rate is 71.3%, and after 24 months, this rate is 39.1% (Table 1), once again confirming the role of treatment in improving survival in stage IV colorectal cancer patients.

Some Related Factors and Value of PNI in Predicting All‐Cause Mortality in Patients With Stage IV Colorectal Cancer Treated With Capecitabine

Our study results show that old age, the number of metastatic sites ≥ 3; anemia, low prealbumin, albumin levels, low PNI index, high CRP‐hs, CEA levels, and high NLR had significant differences between the mortality and survival groups (Table 1). However, when multivariate analysis was performed, only high CRP‐hs, high NLR, and low PNI were independent factors associated with mortality, p < 0.05 (Table 2). PNI, NLR, and CRP‐hs have a predictive value for mortality, in which PNI is a good predictor, AUC = 0.88, p < 0.001 (Figure 1). CRP and NLR are factors predicting outcome and treatment response in mCRC patients, which has been confirmed in several studies. Research by Riedl et al. on 258 mCRC patients undergoing first‐line chemo‐immuno therapy, followed for 6 months, showed that increased NLR was associated with increased risk and CRP. Plasma‐related poor response to treatment [24]. In patients with advanced colorectal cancer, combination‐agent chemotherapy and NLR ≤ 5 was associated with improved clinical benefit. Conversely, NLR < 5 was associated with an increased risk of progression [25]. The prognostic nutritional index is an index that evaluates nutritional immune status and has clinical value in predicting colorectal cancer progression. Li et al. conducted a study on 511 patients with primary colorectal cancer who had undergone radical surgery and followed the patients for 5 years. The result showed that preoperative PNI < 48.65 has a low predictive value for survival [26]. In mCRC patients, Yi et al. also demonstrated that low PNI predicts worse outcomes in mCRC patients undergoing immunotherapy [27]. Our study included stage IV colorectal cancer patients, including mCRC and recurrent CRC patients. We also found that PNI predicted mortality well at the cut‐off point of 38.51.

Not only in patients with colorectal cancer, but the role of PNI and CRP is also used by cardiologists to predict mortality in patients with acute and chronic heart disease. In the group of patients with reduced ejection fraction and an implantable cardiac defibrillator, low PNI, high C‐reactive and high protein‐to‐albumin ratio were predictive of mortality over a 10‐year follow‐up period [28, 29]. In patients undergoing coronary artery bypass grafting, low PNI was an independent predictor of mortality over a 3‐year follow‐up period [30], and albumin, in particular, was found to be an integral part of the development of prognostic indicators for disease progression and mortality in patients with and without cardiovascular disease [31]. Thus, to reduce the mortality rate when treating stage IV colorectal cancer patients using capecitabine, we need to intervene to reduce inflammation and increase plasma albumin levels.

Conclusion

Although stage IV colorectal cancer patients were treated with capecitabine, the results were not good, with a 24‐month all‐cause mortality rate of 60.9%. Factors related to mortality included high CRP‐hs levels, high NLR, and low PNI. Pre‐treatment PNI with capecitabine had predictive value for mortality, with AUC: 0.88; p < 0.001.

Longitudinal relation between childhood maltreatment and problematic internet game use among Chinese early adolescents: The roles of internalizing symptoms and FKBP5 gene variation
Authors: Xue Gong, Jianhua Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundResearch has demonstrated that childhood maltreatment is a significant predictor of problematic internet game use in adolescents. However, the underlying mechanisms of this relation, particularly the mediating processes and gene-environment interactions (G × E), remain insufficiently understood. This study employed a multi-informant, longitudinal design within a G × E framework to examine the mediating role of internalizing symptoms in the relation between childhood maltreatment and problematic internet game use. Additionally, it investigated whether FKBP5 gene variation moderates these associations.MethodsA total of 1,592 third- to fifth-grade students (42.2% girls; Mage = 10.42 years, SD = 0.94) and their parents participated in a three-wave study conducted at six-month intervals.ResultsFindings indicated that both emotional abuse and neglect each significantly predicted problematic internet game use, with internalizing symptoms mediating both relations. Moreover, higher FKBP5-related cumulative genetic scores amplified the impact of emotional abuse on internalizing symptoms, which, in turn, exacerbated problematic internet game use.ConclusionThe results underscore the importance of considering both genetic and environmental factors in understanding the development of problematic internet game use. Interventions should focus on mitigating the emotional consequences of childhood maltreatment and accommodating genetic predispositions to stress, thereby providing a more comprehensive approach to preventing and treating problematic internet game use in adolescents.

Introduction

Problematic internet game use refers to a psychological dependence on online gaming, characterized by persistent, uncontrollable, and repetitive gaming behaviors that lead to significant social and emotional difficulties among adolescents (Young & De Abreu, 2010). Early adolescence is particularly susceptible to problematic internet game use, given the heightened sensitivity to reward stimuli and the ongoing development of self-regulatory capacities during this period (Grant, Potenza, Weinstein, & Gorelick, 2010; Tejeiro Salguero & Morán, 2002). Studies indicate that the prevalence of problematic internet game use among Chinese adolescents exceeds 20%, a rate substantially higher than that reported in certain Western countries (Griffiths, Kiraly, Pontes, & Demetrovics, 2015; Xu, Turel, & Yuan, 2012; Zhou, Zhang, & Gong, 2023). A meta-analysis demonstrates that problematic internet game use not only compromises adolescents' academic performance, social interactions, and mental well-being but is also linked to severe psychological and behavioral maladjustments (e.g., Cheng, Cheung, & Wang, 2018). Given the profound and wide-ranging negative consequences of problematic internet game use, it is imperative to elucidate its underlying etiological mechanisms to inform effective prevention and intervention strategies.

Childhood maltreatment, encompassing physical abuse, emotional abuse, and neglect, is a profound form of early family adversity that acts as a stressor beyond the individual's control, often disrupting the development of functional coping mechanisms (He & Xiang, 2022). Prior research has consistently identified adverse family environments, including childhood maltreatment, as critical risk factors for adolescent problematic internet game use (e.g., Tang et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2022). However, the specific pathways through which childhood maltreatment contributes to problematic internet game use remain inadequately understood. Moreover, the role of genetic predispositions in moderating these associations has received limited empirical attention. This study aims to investigate the mediating effects of internalizing symptoms on the longitudinal relation between childhood maltreatment and problematic internet game use. Additionally, it investigates the potential moderating effects of FKBP5 gene polymorphisms—key regulators of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis—on these mediating associations among Chinese early adolescents.

Childhood maltreatment as a predictor of problematic internet game use

Childhood maltreatment refers to the mistreatment, abuse, or neglect that a child may experience during their formative years (Bernstein & Fink, 1998). This maltreatment encompasses a range of harmful actions or omissions by caregivers, including physical abuse (e.g., hitting, kicking), emotional or psychological abuse (e.g., verbal insults, humiliation), and neglect (e.g., failure to provide adequate food, shelter, or supervision) (Carr, Martins, Stingel, Lemgruber, & Juruena, 2013). A systematic review and meta-analysis have estimated the prevalence rates of child physical abuse, emotional abuse, physical neglect, and emotional neglect to be 20% (95% CI [13%, 27%]), 30% (95% CI [20%, 39%]), 47% (95% CI [35%, 58%]), and 44% (95% CI [28%, 59%]), respectively (Wang et al., 2020). Childhood maltreatment is often associated with disruptions in emotional regulation, attachment security, and coping mechanisms, which may contribute to the development of addictive behaviors such as excessive internet gaming (Worsley, McIntyre, Bentall, & Corcoran, 2018). Adolescents who experience maltreatment may develop insecure attachment styles, which impair their ability to form stable, supportive relationships and regulate emotions effectively (Cyr, Euser, Bakermans-Kranenburg, & Van Ijzendoorn, 2010). Growing up in an abusive or neglectful environment, these adolescents may seek emotional relief through digital platforms, where internet games offer an immediate escape from negative emotions. This coping strategy can eventually lead to the escalation of problematic gaming behaviors. Neurobiological research further elucidates the link between childhood maltreatment and heightened vulnerability to internet addiction. Studies suggest that traumatic experiences during childhood disrupt adolescents' stress response systems, leading to alterations in neural structure and function, particularly within the HPA axis (Danese & McEwen, 2012). These neurobiological changes are hypothesized to increase susceptibility to internet addiction (Foster et al., 2021). Empirical studies provide compelling evidence for the association between childhood maltreatment and problematic internet use. A meta-analysis of 19 adolescent studies found a significant positive correlation between childhood trauma and internet addiction (r = 0.395, 95% CI [0.345, 0.442]) (Tang et al., 2024). Another meta-analysis of 31 studies with a total sample size of 52,503 Chinese university students reported a moderate correlation between childhood maltreatment and problematic internet use (r = 0.281, 95% CI [0.221, 0.338]) (Wang, Li, & Li, 2023).

Several cross-sectional studies have consistently shown a significant relation between childhood maltreatment and problematic internet use among Chinese adolescents (Gu, Guo, & Wang, 2024; Guo et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2022). In addition to these cross-sectional findings, longitudinal research has further elucidated the temporal dynamics of this relation. A three-wave longitudinal study by Verrastro, Saladino, Giordano, and Calaresi (2025), conducted with a six-month interval, found that emotional abuse in childhood significantly predicted subsequent problematic gaming behaviors. Further supporting these findings, a two-wave study conducted with Chinese adolescents by Geng et al. (2022) revealed that experiences of maltreatment had a positive predictive effect on subsequent problematic smartphone use. Similarly, a recent two-wave study by Jin et al. (2024) focused on Chinese adolescents and specifically highlighted emotional abuse as a significant risk factor for internet addiction (OR = 2.618, 95% CI = 1.194–5.738). These empirical studies, both cross-sectional and longitudinal, underscore the critical role of childhood maltreatment in the development of problematic internet game use.

Although the association between childhood maltreatment and adolescent problematic internet gaming is well established, existing research often treats maltreatment as a broad, undifferentiated construct, failing to delineate the distinct contributions of specific subtypes—such as emotional abuse versus physical neglect—to the development of problematic gaming behaviors. This lack of specificity may obscure the unique effects of each maltreatment dimension on gaming behaviors. This study seeks to address these gaps by examining the differential effects of various dimensions of childhood maltreatment (e.g., physical abuse, emotional neglect) on problematic internet game use.

The mediating role of internalizing symptoms

Internalizing symptoms encompass a broad spectrum of physiological, behavioral, cognitive, and emotional manifestations, primarily characterized by anxiety and depressive moods, which are common among adolescents experiencing emotional distress (Achenbach, Ivanova, Rescorla, Turner, & Althoff, 2016). The compensatory internet use theory (Kardefelt-Winther, 2014) posits that adverse environmental factors, such as childhood maltreatment, heighten internal vulnerabilities, including negative emotional states, thereby limiting adaptive coping mechanisms and increasing susceptibility to problematic internet game use. Similarly, the mood enhancement hypothesis (Bryant & Zillmann, 1984) suggests that individuals engage with media based on their mood states. Individuals experiencing depressive states may seek entertainment online to combat emotional disturbances (Karaer & Akdemir, 2019; Tsai, Lu, Hsiao, Hu, & Yen, 2020). Therefore, internalizing symptoms may serve as a mediator in the relations between childhood maltreatment and adolescent problematic internet game use.

A substantial body of research has established a strong link between childhood maltreatment and the emergence of internalizing symptoms (Li et al., 2023). For example, a meta-analysis of 96 studies found that all forms of childhood maltreatment were significantly associated with depressive disorders (any child maltreatment [OR = 2.48, 2.14–2.87]; physical abuse [OR = 1.78, 1.57–2.01]; emotional abuse [OR = 2.35, 1.74–3.18]; neglect [OR = 1.65, 1.35–2.02]), and anxiety disorders (any child maltreatment [OR = 1.68, 1.33–2.4]; physical abuse [OR = 1.56, 1.39–1.76]; and neglect [OR = 1.34, 1.09–1.65]) (Gardner, Thomas, & Erskine, 2019). Another meta-analysis of 192 studies reported that higher scores of childhood maltreatment were significantly correlated with higher rates of depression diagnosis (effect size g = 1.07, 95% CI: 0.95–1.19) and depressive symptom scores (Z = 0.35, 95% CI: 0.32–0.38) (Humphreys et al., 2020).

Empirical studies have also demonstrated the predictive role of internalizing symptoms in the development of problematic internet game use (e.g., Richard, Fletcher, Boutin, Derevensky, & Temcheff, 2020, for a systematic review). For instance, a longitudinal study demonstrated that anxiety and depressive symptoms positively predicted problematic internet game use about seven months later (Teng, Pontes, Nie, Griffiths, & Guo, 2021). Furthermore, a study employing a parallel process growth model revealed that initial levels of internalizing symptoms significantly predicted disordered gaming behavior six years later (Richard et al., 2022). Moreover, research has demonstrated that internalizing problems mediate the relation between childhood maltreatment and problematic internet game use. For example, several cross-sectional studies have found that depression significantly mediated the relation between childhood maltreatment and problematic internet game use (Guo et al., 2023; Kircaburun, Griffiths, & Billieux, 2019). However, cross-sectional studies are limited in their ability to establish directional relations, and researchers suggested that testing mediation effects requires three-wave longitudinal data to accurately capture the temporal sequence of these relations (e.g., Jose, 2016). Therefore, there is a pressing need for studies utilizing longitudinal designs to better elucidate the mediation effect, providing more robust evidence for the causal mechanisms linking childhood maltreatment to problematic internet game use.

The moderating role of FKBP5 gene

The diathesis-stress model (Monroe & Simons, 1991) highlights the significance of genetic differences in individual responses to environmental stressors. While external environmental factors are associated with internalizing symptoms and problematic internet game use, their effects are not independent of genetically determined vulnerabilities (Li et al., 2022). This model posits that specific genetic variations interact with adverse environmental factors at both genetic and neurobiological levels, impairing stress response mechanisms and increasing susceptibility to internalizing symptoms or problematic internet game use. The HPA axis plays a pivotal role in regulating adaptive responses to stress (Normann & Buttenschøn, 2019). As a result, numerous gene-environment interaction studies have focused on genes involved in HPA axis regulation, with particular attention to the FK506 binding protein 51 (FKBP5) gene (Zannas & Binder, 2014).

The FKBP5 gene, located on the short arm of chromosome 6 (6p21.31), encodes a co-chaperone of heat shock protein 90, which interacts with the glucocorticoid receptor and modulates HPA axis activity. FKBP5 gene transcription and FKBP5 protein expression are upregulated by cortisol-activated glucocorticoid receptors in a negative feedback loop within cells (Vermeer, Hendriks-Stegeman, van der Burg, van Buul-Offers, & Jansen, 2003). FKBP5 binds to the glucocorticoid receptor complex, reducing its cortisol-binding affinity and hindering its nuclear translocation, ultimately inhibiting glucocorticoid receptor activity and leading to HPA axis dysregulation (Wochnik et al., 2005). Importantly, FKBP5 gene transcription, as well as its negative regulation of glucocorticoid receptor sensitivity and stress response, is modulated by common polymorphisms within the FKBP5 gene. Consequently, FKBP5 single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) are considered prime candidates for investigating gene-environment interactions related to stress-related psychiatric disorders (Zannas & Binder, 2014).

A growing body of research has examined the interaction between FKBP5 SNPs and adverse environmental factors, such as childhood trauma and negative parenting, in predicting adolescent internalizing symptoms (Normann & Buttenschøn, 2019; Zannas, Wiechmann, Gassen, & Binder, 2016). Four FKBP5 SNPs (rs4713916, rs1360780, rs3800373, and rs9296158) have been frequently investigated in prior studies (Piechaczek et al., 2019; Wang, Shelton, & Dwivedi, 2018). Most research findings suggest that the minor allele of FKBP5 rs1360780 (i.e., the T allele) may heighten the risk of internalizing symptoms in interaction with adverse environmental experiences, as do the A allele of rs4713916, the C allele of rs3800373, and the A allele of rs9296158 (Kang et al., 2020; Keijser, Olofsdotter, Nilsson, & Åslund, 2021; VanZomeren-Dohm, Pitula, Koss, Thomas, & Gunnar, 2015). However, conflicting findings have emerged, with some studies reporting no significant interaction between FKBP5 polymorphisms and childhood trauma on depressive symptom severity (Grabe et al., 2016; Isaksson et al., 2016; Lavebratt, Åberg, Sjöholm, & Forsell, 2010).

Although most studies have predominantly focused on the interaction between adverse environmental factors and FKBP5 gene variations in the development of internalizing symptoms, the diathesis-stress model suggests that FKBP5 genetic variations may also moderate the relation between environmental stressors and problematic internet game use. However, empirical evidence supporting such gene-environment interactions in relation to problematic internet game use remains scarce. Furthermore, the majority of prior studies have examined individual FKBP5 SNPs, which may limit the ability to detect robust gene-environment interaction effects (Border et al., 2019). Recent advancements in cumulative genetic scoring methods, which aggregate the effects of multiple SNPs, have demonstrated superior predictive validity and statistical power compared to single-SNP analyses (Starr & Huang, 2019). Despite these methodological advancements, few studies have applied cumulative genetic scoring to investigate the interaction between FKBP5 gene variations and childhood maltreatment in relation to adolescent internalizing symptoms and problematic internet game use. Therefore, the present study aims to use a cumulative genetic score (CGS) based on four FKBP5 SNPs (rs4713916, rs1360780, rs3800373, and rs9296158) to examine its moderating effect on the associations between specific types of childhood maltreatment and adolescent internalizing symptoms and problematic internet game use. This comprehensive approach is expected to provide a more nuanced understanding of how genetic susceptibility interacts with environmental stressors to shape adolescent mental health and behavioral outcomes.

The current study

A substantial body of research has established a significant association between childhood maltreatment and adolescent problematic internet game use. However, most of these studies have focused on the underlying mechanisms driving this longitudinal relation, often neglecting the potential role of gene-environment interactions. This gap in the literature underscores the need to investigate how genetic factors, such as FKBP5 gene variants, may influence the development of problematic internet game use within the context of childhood maltreatment. The present longitudinal study aims to examine the mediating role of internalizing symptoms and the moderating effects of FKBP5 gene variants in the association between specific types of childhood maltreatment and problematic internet game use. Given the well-documented link between childhood maltreatment and internalizing symptoms, as well as the strong association between internalizing symptoms and problematic internet game use, this study hypothesizes that internalizing symptoms mediate the longitudinal association between childhood maltreatment and adolescent problematic internet game use. Furthermore, drawing upon the diathesis-stress model, this study posits that FKBP5 gene variants moderate both the direct and indirect effects of childhood maltreatment on adolescent internalizing symptoms and problematic internet game use.

Methods

Participants and data

Participants were drawn from public elementary schools in a mid-sized city in northeast China. With the support of local education authorities, two schools were randomly selected to participate. The study was open to all third- through fifth-grade students and their parents at these schools. Data collection occurred at three time points, with six-month intervals between each assessment. To ensure the reliability of longitudinal analyses, only participants who completed at least two of the three assessment waves were included in the present study. Participants who completed only one wave were excluded from the analysis to reduce potential estimation bias associated with high attrition. After this exclusion, the final analytic sample consisted of 1,592 students and their parents who participated in at least two waves of data collection. At the Time 1 assessment, the sample consisted of 1,592 students and their parents aged 9–13 years (42.2% girls; Mage = 10.42 years, SD = 0.94). By Time 2, 1,577 students (99.1%) remained in the study, and by Time 3, 1,518 students (95.4%) continued to participate. Little's Missing Completely at Random (MCAR) test across the three measurement occasions yielded a normed χ2 (χ2/df) of 1.06 (p > 0.05), suggesting that the missing data were likely random (Nicholson, Deboeck, & Howard, 2017). Full Information Maximum Likelihood (FIML) was employed to handle and impute the missing data, as it provides more accurate parameter estimates by utilizing all available data points, thereby reducing bias and increasing statistical power (Larsen, 2011).

The dataset employed in the present study was partially used in a prior publication (Zhou & Gong, 2023), which examined the longitudinal association between maladaptive parenting and nonsuicidal self-injury among Chinese early adolescents. That earlier study utilized a two-wave design over a six-month interval and focused on broad parenting constructs—specifically psychological control and harsh punishment—as predictors, with internalizing symptoms serving as a mediator and FKBP5 gene variation as a moderator. The outcome of interest in that study was adolescents' self-reported nonsuicidal self-injury. In contrast, the current study adopts a three-wave design spanning a one-year period, allowing for a longer developmental window to observe changes over time. It addresses a conceptually distinct research question by focusing on four specific types of childhood maltreatment (i.e., emotional abuse, physical abuse, emotional neglect, and physical neglect) as predictors, with internalizing symptoms as a mediator and problematic internet game use—assessed via parent report—as the primary outcome. Thus, although both studies draw upon the same cohort, they differ in terms of follow-up duration, focal constructs, and outcome domains, constituting two independent and non-overlapping lines of inquiry.

Procedure

Trained research assistants provided identical verbal and written instructions to the students in each class. Students were allowed as much time as needed to complete the assessments. Parents' questionnaires were sent home with the students in unsealed bags, and the students conveyed the specific codes instead of names. Once completed, parents placed the questionnaires in sealed bags and returned them to the researchers the next day. Participants were reassured that all collected data would remain strictly confidential and accessible only to the research team.

Childhood maltreatment (adolescent self-reported at T1)

Childhood maltreatment was assessed using the Childhood Trauma Questionnaire (CTQ; Bernstein et al., 2003). The CTQ includes four dimensions: emotional abuse, physical abuse, emotional neglect, and physical neglect, as reported by the adolescents. Sample items included “People in my family said hurtful or insulting things to me” and “I got hit or beaten so hard that it was noticed by someone like a teacher, neighbor, or doctor”. Responses ranged from 1 (never true) to 5 (very often true). Mean scores were analyzed with higher scores indicating more maltreatment of the children. This scale has demonstrated good reliability and validity with Chinese students (Ding et al., 2017). In this study, Cronbach's coefficient α for the CTQ was 0.81 at T1.

Problematic internet game use (parent-reported at T1, T2 and T3)

Problematic internet game use was assessed using a parent-reported measure adapted from the Pathological Online Game Use scale (POGU; Gentile, 2009; Zhao, Li, Zhou, Nie, & Zhou, 2020). In this study, either the adolescent's father or mother completed the questionnaire to evaluate their child's level of problematic internet game use. This scale comprises seven items, such as “Has tried many times to control, reduce, or stop the time or number of times he/she plays cellphone or computer games, but without success.” Response options ranged from 0 (never) to 2 (often), with higher scores indicating more severe problematic gaming behavior. The parent-reported POGU scale has shown good reliability in studies involving Chinese adolescents (Zhao et al., 2020). In the present study, the Cronbach's α coefficients for the problematic internet game use scale were 0.90 at Time 1, and 0.91 at Time 2, and 0.91 at Time 3.

Internalizing symptoms (adolescent self-reported at T2)

Internalizing symptoms were measured using the Chinese version of the Youth Self-Report (YSR) form (Achenbach, 1991; Su, Yang, Wan, Luo, & Li, 1999). This scale comprises sixteen items, such as “unhappy, sad, or depressed” and “nervous or tense.” Responses were scored from 0 (not true) to 2 (very true or often true), and mean scores were calculated, with higher scores reflecting greater levels of internalizing symptoms. The YSR has shown strong reliability and validity among Chinese students (Su et al., 1999). In the current study, Cronbach's α coefficient for the internalizing symptoms scale was 0.92 at Time 2.

Genotyping

DNA was extracted from buccal cell samples. The SNaPshot method was used to detect rs4713916, rs1360780, rs3800373, and rs9296158 polymorphisms by fluorescence capillary electrophoresis using the ABI 3730xl instrument (CE platform/next-generation sequencing platform). The genotyping results were read and analyzed by the GeneMaker software system.

The genotypes of the four SNPs of the FKBP5 gene were distributed as follows: rs4713916: GG = 59.2% (N = 943), GA = 36.2% (N = 576), AA = 4.6% (N = 73); rs1360780: CC = 54.4% (N = 866), CT = 40.1% (N = 638), TT = 5.5% (N = 88), rs3800373: AA = 55.5% (N = 883), CA = 38.3% (N = 610), CC = 6.2% (N = 99); rs9296158 GG = 46.3% (N = 737), AG = 43.4% (N = 691), AA = 10.3% (N = 164). No SNPs showed any significant deviations from the Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (rs4713916: χ2 = 1.60, df = 2, p = 0.45; rs1360780: χ2 = 4.47, df = 2, p = 0.11; rs3800373: χ2 = 0.22, df = 2, p = 0.90; rs9296158: χ2 = 0.01, df = 2, p = 0.99).

Based on the practices of previous studies (e.g., Tian, Cao, & Zhang, 2020), we coded the FKBP5 gene according to the number of minor alleles of four SNPs: rs4713916: AA = 2, GA = 1, GG = 0; rs1360780: TT = 2, CT = 1, CC = 0; rs3800373: CC = 2, CA = 1, AA = 0; rs9296158: AA = 2, GA = 1, GG = 0. Then, theCGS was aggregated, in which a higher score indicated a greater number of minor alleles of these four loci and a higher environmental sensitivity of the individual to carry the genotype. The FKBP5-related CGS ranged from 0 to 8. The frequencies for the scores within this CGS were as follows: 0 (N = 696, 43.7%), 1 (N = 155, 9.7%), 2 (N = 26, 1.6%), 3 (N = 106, 6.7%), 4 (N = 449, 28.2%), 5 (N = 62, 3.9%), 6 (N = 12, 0.8%), 7 (N = 28, 1.8%), and 8 (N = 58, 3.6%).

Covariates

Several demographic covariates were collected from students at Time 1, including age and socioeconomic status (SES). To assess family SES, parent-reported data on parental education levels and family income were used. Education levels were rated on a scale from 1 (never attended school) to 8 (doctorate), while monthly family income ranged from 1 (2000 RMB and below) to 9 (more than 100,000 RMB). SES was indexed by standardizing the education levels and income scores of both parents, transforming these into z-scores, and then averaging the standardized variables.

Data analysis

First, descriptive statistics and bivariate correlations for the main study variables were computed using Mplus version 8.0 after handling missing data with the FIML method. Second, the mediating role of internalizing symptoms at T2 in the relation between childhood maltreatment at T1 and problematic internet game use at T3 was examined, controlling for problematic internet game use at T1, internalizing symptoms at T1, and relevant covariates. Third, a fully moderated mediation model was estimated to assess the moderating effects of FKBP5 gene variation. To test for moderation, interaction terms between the FKBP5-related CGS and different dimensions of childhood maltreatment were created. Prior to analysis, mean-centering was applied to both childhood maltreatment and FKBP5-related CGS to minimize multicollinearity in the interaction term. Upon detecting a significant interaction effect, a simple slope analysis was conducted based on the number of FKBP5-related CGS alleles (ranging from 0 to 8). To obtain precise parameter estimates for all models, the percentile bootstrapping method (N = 5,000) was employed. This method was also used to evaluate the significance and strength of the mediating effect. A mediation effect was considered statistically significant if the 95% confidence interval (CI) did not include zero. Finally, model fit was assessed using Comparative Fit Index (CFI), and Standardized Root Mean Square Residual (SRMR). A CFI value greater than 0.90 and an SRMR value less than 0.08 were considered indicative of adequate model fit (Hu & Bentler, 1999).

Ethics

This study received approval from the Human Research Ethics Committee of Northwest Normal University and the principals of all participating schools. Informed consent was obtained from both the students and their parents prior to data collection. Confidentiality was assured to all participants. Each student was assigned a unique code to ensure privacy and facilitate tracking.

The mediation model of internalizing symptoms at T2 between childhood maltreatment at T1 and problematic internet game use at T2 and T3 fit the data well (CFI = 0.963, SRMR = 0.028). The effect of emotional abuse at T1 on internalizing symptoms at T2 (β = 0.10, p < 0.01) was statistically significant, and the effect of emotional neglect at T1 on internalizing symptoms at T2 (β = 0.08, p < 0.05) was statistically significant. The moderated mediation model

The moderated mediation model demonstrated an adequate fit for the data (CFI = 0.964, SRMR = 0.023). The results (see Table 3) showed that the interaction effect of emotional abuse at T1 and the CGS on internalizing symptoms at T2 was statistically significant, but insignificant on problematic internet game use at T2 and T3. This result indicates that the FKBP5-related CGS moderates the effect of emotional abuse on adolescent internalizing symptoms. A simple slope test showed that for increased numbers of alleles associated with heightened stress reactivity, the predictive effects of emotional abuse on internalizing symptoms were gradually increased (see Fig. 2). Specifically, for adolescents with a low CGS, the predictive effect of emotional abuse at T1 on internalizing symptoms at T2 was not statistically significant, b = 0.06, SE = 0.03, p = 0.090. In contrast, for those with a high CGS, this association was statistically significant, b = 0.13, SE = 0.03, p < 0.01.

The sensitivity analyses

To validate the CGS approach, we utilized an equal gene model to assess the assumption of additivity across the different SNPs. Initially, a disaggregated regression model was constructed, where the main and interaction effects of four SNPs and emotional abuse (along with covariates) on problematic internet game use at T2 and T3, as well as internalizing symptoms at T2, were estimated independently. Following this, a nested equal gene regression model was applied, which included 15 constraints to enforce equality in the regression weights for the main effects of the four SNPs and their interactions with emotional abuse. If the disaggregated model provided a better fit than the equal gene model, this would indicate that at least one genetic marker might exhibit differential effects. Conversely, a lack of significant difference would support the assumption of equal effects across the SNPs. The results revealed no significant difference between the constrained equal gene model and the freely estimated gene effect model (Δχ2(Δdf = 45) = 57.45, p = 0.101), thereby supporting the additivity assumption for the four SNPs included in the CGS. Second, when examining the role of individual SNPs, all four polymorphisms significantly interacted with emotional abuse at T1 on internalizing symptoms at T2 (rs4713916: β = 0.09, p < 0.01; rs1360780: β = 0.09, p < 0.05; rs3800373: β = 0.10, p < 0.01; rs9296158: β = 0.11, p < 0.01). Simple slope analyses for each SNP are presented in Fig. A1 of the Appendix.

Discussion

Childhood maltreatment has been identified as a significant risk factor for the development of problematic internet game use in adolescents (Griffiths et al., 2015; Xu et al., 2012; Zhou et al., 2023). However, the underlying mechanisms of this relation remain insufficiently explored, and limited research has examined the potential moderating role of FKBP5 gene variants in these associations. This longitudinal study investigated the mediating role of internalizing symptoms in the relation between childhood maltreatment and problematic internet game use among Chinese adolescents. Additionally, the study explored whether FKBP5 gene variants moderate the impact of childhood maltreatment on problematic internet game use via internalizing symptoms. The findings indicate that internalizing symptoms mediate the longitudinal relation between childhood emotional abuse and neglect and problematic internet game use. Furthermore, the study underscores the moderating role of FKBP5-related cumulative genetic scores in the pathway from emotional abuse to internalizing symptoms, providing novel insights into the gene-environment interactions on adolescents' internalizing problems and consequently problematic internet game use.

Childhood maltreatment and problematic internet game use

The results of the mediation model indicate that among various forms of childhood maltreatment, only emotional abuse significantly predicts problematic internet game use in adolescents. In contrast, emotional neglect, physical abuse, and physical neglect do not exhibit a significant predictive relation with problematic internet game use. These findings suggest a distinctive and substantial influence of emotional abuse on the development of maladaptive gaming behaviors. This result is consistent with existing literature (Emirtekin et al., 2019; Verrastro et al., 2025) that emotional abuse is a stronger predictor of problematic internet use than physical abuse or neglect, highlighted that psychological wounds inflicted by emotional abuse are particularly conducive to the development of problematic internet use behaviors, including excessive gaming. Unlike other forms of maltreatment, such as physical abuse or neglect, emotional abuse uniquely impairs adolescents' psychological resilience by directly targeting their emotional and cognitive processes. Continuous exposure to negative verbal interactions distorts adolescents' self-perception, reinforcing feelings of rejection and worthlessness, which are hallmark features of insecure attachment patterns (Mallinckrodt & Wei, 2005), which can impair their ability to seek and maintain supportive relationships (Cyr et al., 2010). Instead of turning to caregivers or peers for emotional support, adolescents with insecure attachment may seek emotional regulation through online gaming, where they experience a sense of control, achievement, and social interaction that is lacking in their offline lives.

The findings of this study support the anticipated mediating role of internalizing symptoms in the relation between emotional abuse and neglect and adolescent problematic internet game use. This finding is well-aligned with the compensatory internet use theory (Kardefelt-Winther, 2014), which posits that individuals may resort to excessive internet use, including online gaming, as a coping mechanism to manage negative emotions and stressors in their lives. Adolescents who have experienced emotional abuse and neglect are particularly vulnerable to developing internalizing symptoms, such as anxiety, depression, and internal conflicts, which are often rooted in painful memories and diminished self-worth. To avoid re-experiencing the distress associated with these memories, these adolescents may turn to online gaming as a form of distraction, effectively preventing the reactivation of trauma-related emotional states (Schimmenti & Caretti, 2010). While this coping mechanism might provide temporary relief from their emotional distress, it can lead to maladaptive behaviors such as problematic internet game use, as adolescents increasingly rely on gaming to escape from their negative emotions. Over time, this reliance on gaming can escalate into excessive or addictive behavior, reinforcing the cycle of emotional avoidance and problematic gaming. The findings of this study are consistent with previous research suggesting that depressive symptoms play a critical mediating role in the relation between childhood trauma and problematic internet game use (Kircaburun et al., 2019; Wu et al., 2022). Adolescents who experience emotional abuse and neglect are likely to develop depressive symptoms, which then propel them toward problematic gaming behaviors as a form of escape. Expanding upon prior cross-sectional studies (Kim et al., 2023), this longitudinal study further reinforces the notion that internalizing symptoms play a pivotal role in the development of problematic internet game use among adolescents exposed to emotional abuse and neglect.

The role of FKBP5 genetic variation

The findings of this study indicate that higher FKBP5-related CGS—reflecting a greater number of minor alleles associated with heightened stress reactivity—amplify the predictive relation between emotional abuse and internalizing symptoms. Moreover, the mediating effect of internalizing symptoms in the association between emotional abuse and problematic internet game use is also moderated by FKBP5-related CGS, such that the indirect pathway is stronger among adolescents with higher genetic sensitivity. Individuals with higher FKBP5 CGS exhibit increased FKBP5 expression, which binds to the glucocorticoid receptor and reduces its affinity for cortisol. This reduction in binding efficiency results in elevated cortisol levels, particularly in response to stressors such as childhood maltreatment (Zannas & Binder, 2014). The sustained release of cortisol, caused by HPA axis dysregulation, leads to persistent hyperactivity of the system, impairing the feedback mechanisms that normally inhibit cortisol production. Chronic overactivation of the HPA axis has been linked to morphological changes in hippocampal neurons, including altered dendritic structures, which are crucial for stress regulation and cognitive function (Matosin, Halldorsdottir, & Binder, 2018).

These biochemical changes have significant implications for stress-coping mechanisms. When the HPA axis is overactive, as observed in individuals with high FKBP5 expression, adaptive stress-coping strategies such as cognitive reappraisal and problem-solving become less effective (Criado-Marrero et al., 2018). Consequently, these individuals are more vulnerable to developing internalizing symptoms, including anxiety and depression, as a response to the overwhelming stress they experience. This heightened vulnerability, driven by both genetic susceptibility and environmental stress, increases the likelihood of engaging in maladaptive behaviors, such as problematic internet game use, as a means of coping with emotional distress. Conversely, adolescents with lower FKBP5 CGS exhibit reduced FKBP5 expression, allowing cortisol binding to its receptor to function more effectively. This enables the HPA axis and its negative feedback mechanisms to operate normally, resulting in stable cortisol levels and a more resilient stress response system (Kuhn et al., 2016). Adolescents with a well-regulated HPA axis are more likely to develop adaptive coping strategies, even in the face of emotional abuse, thereby reducing their risk of internalizing symptoms and, subsequently, problematic internet game use (Romeo, 2015).

Although this study did not find a direct interaction between FKBP5 CGS and childhood maltreatment in predicting problematic internet game use, the significant indirect effect through internalizing symptoms highlights an important mechanism. These findings suggest that the complex interplay between genetic predispositions and environmental stressors does not directly lead to problematic internet game use but instead operates through the exacerbation of internalizing symptoms. Adolescents with higher FKBP5-related genetic risk are more susceptible to the emotional and psychological consequences of childhood maltreatment, making them more likely to engage in online gaming as a maladaptive coping mechanism. These results align with previous research demonstrating that genetic factors modulate the impact of environmental stressors on psychological outcomes, often through the mediation of internalizing symptoms (Cao & Rijlaarsdam, 2023). The role of FKBP5 in this process underscores the importance of considering both genetic and environmental factors when examining pathways to problematic internet game use.

Limitations

Despite the valuable insights gained from this study, several limitations should be acknowledged when interpreting the findings. First, this study focused exclusively on a single FKBP5 polymorphism within the HPA axis. This narrow genetic focus limits the ability to capture the broader genetic influences that may interact with environmental stressors. Future research should incorporate a wider range of genetic variants associated with HPA axis regulation, including those involved in cortisol regulation and other stress-related hormonal pathways. Second, internalizing symptoms were assessed as a general construct without distinguishing between specific symptom dimensions, such as depression and anxiety. This broad approach may have obscured important differences in how distinct internalizing symptoms interact with genetic predispositions and environmental stressors. Future research should consider measuring specific dimensions—such as depressive and anxiety symptoms—to better understand their potentially divergent pathways and effects. Third, the use of parent-reported data for POGU without incorporating adolescent self-reports represents another limitation. While parent reports provide valuable insights into adolescents' gaming behaviors, particularly for younger age groups, future studies would benefit from a multi-informant approach that includes both parent and adolescent reports. This approach would help capture potential discrepancies and provide a more nuanced understanding of problematic internet gaming use. Fourth, we acknowledge that the effect sizes in the mediating effect of internalizing symptoms were relatively small. However, this is not uncommon in longitudinal studies examining psychological constructs, especially when controlling for prior levels of the outcome variable. Thus, given that the previously mentioned confounders were controlled, even small effect sizes are likely to be meaningful. Finally, this study was conducted with a relatively homogeneous sample, consisting of third- to fifth-grade students from two elementary schools in a single city in China. This limited geographical and demographic scope may restrict the generalizability of findings to other populations. Future research should replicate this study in larger and more diverse adolescent samples, including participants from different regions, socio-economic backgrounds, and cultural contexts.

Conclusion

Although childhood maltreatment has long been recognized as a key predictor of problematic internet game use, the mechanisms underlying this relation, particularly over time, remain insufficiently understood. This study addresses these gaps by examining the mediating role of internalizing symptoms and the moderating effects of FKBP5 gene variants in the longitudinal relation between childhood maltreatment and problematic internet game use. The present study finds that the longitudinal association between emotional abuse and neglect and problematic internet game use is mediated by internalizing symptoms. The findings also highlight the interaction effect between FKBP5-related CGS and emotional abuse on internalizing symptoms in adolescents. Specifically, higher FKBP5 CGS appear to amplify the impact of emotional abuse on internalizing symptoms. However, it is important to note that the data does not provide direct evidence linking FKBP5 CGS to problematic internet game use. These findings suggest that both genetic and environmental factors contribute to the development of internalizing symptoms, which may, in turn, influence problematic internet game use.

Quantification of heavy metals in Chinese jasmine flower cakes and risk assessment
Authors: Jianjun Huang, Shuang Liang, Guoyi Wu, Shaodong Liang, Yanyun Liu, Gang Deng, Liqun Shen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Jasmine flower cakes are particularly popular in China; however, they may contain heavy metals, posing health risks. We quantified heavy metals in jasmine flower cakes and assessed associated health risks. Al, Cr, Mn, Ni, Cu, As, Cd, and Pb in 40 samples were quantified via inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry, revealing concentrations of 9.32, 0.012, 6.64, 0.296, 4.34, 0.002, 0.001, and 0.008 mg/kg, respectively. The detection rates were 100% except for Cd (82.50%) and As (67.50%). Cluster analysis categorised these metals into five groups: As/Cd, Al/Cr/Pb, Mn, Ni, and Cu. Although all samples exhibited mean health indices (HIs) < 1, nine samples (22.5%) exhibited HI > 1. The maximum HI was 2.261. The average estimated carcinogenic risk values were 27.00 × 10⁻6 across all samples, with risk ranking as Cd > As > Pb. Overall, the cumulative carcinogenic risk of the cakes is within an acceptable range; however, the risk levels of certain batches warrant further attention. The findings provide a scientific basis for evaluating the safety of jasmine-flavoured cake consumption and establishes a reference analytical framework for risk screening and regulatory strategies of other traditional specialty foods. This supports high-quality development of food industry while safeguarding public health.

Introduction

Jasminum sambac (L.) Aiton is a jasmonium plant in the Luteolaceae family. It is native to tropical and subtropical regions and is widely cultivated in several countries, such as India, Iran, Egypt, Turkey, and China1. In the food industry, it is used as a natural flavouring agent owing to its unique aroma. It is often used in pastry production, including Jasmine flower cakes. This traditional pastry, primarily made from jasmine flowers and grains, is highly favoured by consumers. Since 1982, the consumption of refined grains in China has increased. From 1985 to 2013, the market share and contribution of refined rice, wheat flour and their products have continued to rise2.

Jasmine flower cakes are nutrient-rich. However, owing to the presence of heavy metals, they may also pose health risks. With rapid economic development, China’s industrialisation has resulted in significant energy consumption and severe environmental pollution3,4. Within the soil–cereal system, diet constitutes the primary route of human exposure to heavy metals, serving as the main source of heavy metal intake5,6. The contamination of food with heavy metals has raised considerable public concern regarding food safety in China. The overall heavy metal contamination rate of agricultural soils in China is 10.18%, with Cd, Hg, Cu, and Ni accounting for the majority. Approximately 13.86% of grain production is affected by heavy metal contamination in agricultural soils, underscoring the critical role of soil as a major source of heavy metals in crops and the importance of addressing it for food safety7,8.

Heavy metals, characterised by high atomic weight and mass, are integral components of the natural environment9. Even at relatively low concentrations, heavy metal ions interact with biomolecules, leading to irreversible chronic or acute intoxication10. For example, Hg, Cd, and Mn are known to impair brain development, cognitive function, and motor skills, thereby potentially contributing to neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer’s and Parkinson’s disease11. Pb is an inorganic toxic metal with no biological function in humans, it can interfere with metabolic processes and cause chronic neurotoxicity, posing risks to both ecosystems and human health12. In particular, Pb exposure is associated with impaired intellectual development in children13. Inorganic arsenic exposure can lead to serious health consequences, including skin diseases, cardiovascular diseases, neurological disorders, and an increased risk of cancer14.

In China, the consumption of Western-style convenience foods, such as bread and biscuits, has increased dramatically due to rapid socioeconomic changes and increased average incomes15. As a representative product within this category, jasmine flower cake has also experienced rapid market growth. However, to date, no studies have examined the heavy metal content of jasmine flower cakes or the associated health risks. The ICH Q3D Guideline for Elemental Impurities classifies inorganic As, Pb, Cd, and Hg as Class 1 (highly toxic, requiring stringent control), Ni as Class 2A (relatively high toxicity, requiring focused attention), and Cu and Cr as Class 3 (lower relative toxicity, often requiring attention). Elements such as Mn, Al, Fe, and Zn are categorised under “Other”16. Although these elements exhibit lower toxicity, their established oral reference doses (RfD) are as follows: 0.14 mg/kg-day for Mn; 0.43 mg/kg-day for Al; 0.7 mg/kg-day for Fe; 0.3 mg/kg-day for Zn14,17. Considering that the production of jasmine flower cakes may involve the addition of Al-based leavening agents, which can contribute to elevated Al levels18, and that Mn has a comparatively lower RfD, indicating a higher relative risk, both Al and Mn were included in the assessment. However, as jasmine flower cakes are baked goods requiring high-temperature processing, Hg, while a recognised contaminant, is highly volatile upon heating. Most Hg is released at temperatures of approximately 170 °C during volatilisation19. Therefore, Hg was excluded from the analytical panel.

In this study, we selected eight elements—Al, Cr, Mn, Ni, Cu, As, Cd, and Pb—to prioritise contaminants of highest potential risk in jasmine flower cakes. Currently, for baked goods, Chinese national standards stipulate maximum level requirements only for Al and Pb. Internationally, the Codex Alimentarius Commission General Standard for Food Additives (CXS 192-1995) sets a limit for Al. Neither the Codex Alimentarius Commission (CAC) nor the European Union has established specific maximum levels for the remaining seven elements in baked goods20–22. Heavy metal concentrations

The eight target heavy metals exhibited varied concentrations in the 40 batches of jasmine flower cakes (Tables 3 and 4). The detection rates for Al, Cr, Mn, Ni, Cu, and Pb were 100%, whereas those for Cd and As were 82.50% and 67.50%, respectively. The average concentrations were as follows: Al, 9.32 mg/kg; Mn, 6.64 mg/kg; Cu, 4.34 mg/kg; Ni, 0.296 mg/kg; Cr, 0.012 mg/kg; Pb, 0.008 mg/kg; As, 0.002 mg/kg; Cd, 0.001 mg/kg. The coefficients of variation of the eight heavy metals ranged from 41.7 to 150.0%. The concentrations of Al, Mn, and Cu were significantly higher than those of the other five elements, with concentration ranges of 1.57–38.5, 2.09–18.5, and 2.09–18.6 mg/kg, respectively. At present, China’s national standards set maximum limits only for Al and Pb in baked goods. According to GB 2762–2022, the maximum allowable concentration for Pb is 0.5 mg/kg, and according to GB 2760–2024, that for Al is 100 mg/kg (based on a dry sample). Internationally, the CXS 192–1995 stipulates the same limit for Al as the Chinese national standard. However, neither the CAC nor the European Union has established specific maximum levels for the remaining seven elements in baked goods. Risk assessment

Non-carcinogenic risk assessment

The hazard quotient (HQi) and hazard index (HI) results for heavy metals are summarised in Table 7 and Fig. 3. The average HQi values for all batches of jasmine flower cakes were below 1, indicating that the potential health risks posed by the individual heavy metals were relatively low. Notably, significant variations were observed in the Cu concentrations among the different batches. In four batches (S26, S30, S38, and S39), the HQi values for Cu exceeded the safety threshold of 1, with values of 2.127, 1.089, 1.106, and 1.741, respectively. In these same batches, the cumulative risk from all metals, expressed as the HI, also exceeded unity, with values ranging from 1.090 to 2.261. This indicates that the non-carcinogenic risk in these samples was driven not only by elevated Cu levels but also by the additive contribution of other metals. In particular, batch S26 exhibited both the highest HQi for Cu (2.127) and the highest HI (2.261), highlighting a pronounced combined risk scenario. Overall, the cumulative CR associated with jasmine flower cakes was within acceptable public health limits (< 1 × 10−4). However, CCR varied substantially among batches, with one sample exceeding 100 × 10⁻⁶. This heterogeneity indicates that, while the population-level risk under typical consumption scenarios is low, the risk associated with specific high-concentration batches may be elevated, underscoring the importance of quality control to mitigate batch-to-batch variability. Specifically, the average CR value for Cd was approximately 3.4-fold higher than that for As and 129-fold higher than that for Pb, making Cd the primary contributor to CR. The contribution of Pb to CR is minimal and may be considered negligible. Therefore, management of CRs associated with jasmine flower cake should primarily focus on Cd and As. Compared with previous studies, our study reveals a distinct heavy metal risk contribution pattern that differs from those reported for common food products. For instance, Taher and Abojassim (2024) reported that Zn was the primary CR contributor in commercially available biscuits, followed by Cd and As23. Similarly, Li et al. (2022) conducted a heavy metal risk assessment in rice from five regions in Guizhou Province of China and found that the average CR values for As and Pb exceeded the upper limit of the acceptable range (1 × 10⁻4); in their study, approximately 88.90% of CR was attributed to As24.

In contrast, our study identifies Cd as the predominant contributor to CR. These findings provide insights into the heavy metal risk profiles of traditional specialty foods. Notably, Cd exhibited substantial variability (SD = 15.81), indicating marked heterogeneity in its concentration across the different samples. This high dispersion suggests potential differences in the soil contamination levels at the sites of raw material cultivation. For example, natural Cd enrichment in soils from regions such as Yunnan and Guangxi may have contributed to the elevated Cd levels25,26.

Limitations and conclusions

While this study provides a systematic assessment of heavy metals in commercially available jasmine flower cakes, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the samples were collected from the market at a single time point (May 2025). As processed food products, the composition of jasmine flower cakes may vary with production batches, raw material sourcing cycles, and inventory turnover. Thus, our findings primarily reflect products available at that specific time and may not fully capture variations across different production periods. Second, although samples were sourced from ten producing provinces in China, their geographical distribution was uneven (e.g. a larger proportion from Yunnan Province). This imbalance may affect the generalisability of the average concentrations and risk estimates to all regional products. Finally, while the sample size (n = 40) was sufficient to identify major contamination patterns and exceedance batches, it remains insufficient for reliably characterising the distribution of low-frequency, high-concentration contamination events.

Furthermore, several methodological considerations should be considered when interpreting the results. First, the investigation was confined to the analysis of the final food product. While multivariate statistical analysis revealed distinct elemental association patterns (e.g. As–Cd or Al–Cr–Pb), no environmental samples (e.g. cultivation soil or irrigation water) or processing-chain samples were analysed. Consequently, the identified patterns, while valuable as contamination fingerprints, cannot be definitively attributed to specific sources such as soil geochemistry or industrial processing without further dedicated source-apportionment studies. Second, the dietary exposure assessment relied on pasta consumption data as a proxy, which may overestimate the long-term average intake for this specialty food and thus introduces uncertainty into the absolute risk values; however, the relative risk conclusions remain robust. Third, the health risk assessment was conducted based on the total concentration of arsenic and chromium, without including an analysis of the elemental forms. Because the toxicity of these elements depends strongly on their chemical forms, the actual risk may be lower than the reported value. Future studies should therefore include arsenic and chromium speciation analysis to obtain more accurate data27,28. Finally, the risk assessment conclusions were derived using a standard adult body weight and daily intake rate. As both the model and its parameters are designed for the standard adult population, the findings are therefore applicable only to this specific demographic group.

Within the scope of this market survey and based on the applied risk assessment model, Jasmine flower cakes generally comply with national standards for individual heavy metals. Al and Mn exhibited the highest average concentrations in jasmine flower cakes; however, due to their relatively high health-based guidelines, their estimated risk level after intake dose normalisation was relatively low, and they did not constitute key risk drivers in this study. In contrast, although the average concentrations of Cd and As were extremely low, they played a dominant role in health risk assessment, primarily due to carcinogenic slope factors. Even in trace amounts, these elements still contribute to the main carcinogenic risk index under a conservative risk assessment framework.

In summary, this study is the first to combine ICP-MS technology, chemometric analysis, and health risk assessment models to systematically investigate the contamination characteristics and health risks of heavy metals in jasmine flower cakes. The results indicate that the average carcinogenic risk associated with heavy metals in jasmine flower cakes is within acceptable limits, although certain batches exhibit elevated non-carcinogenic risks. These findings suggest a shift in regulatory focus from overall compliance toward the targeted identification and dynamic control of high-risk batches. This study provides robust data support and a scientific basis for the quality and safety evaluation of jasmine flower cakes, and offers an analytical framework applicable to other traditional specialty foods. This contributes to safeguarding public health while promoting the high-quality and sustainable development of the food industry.

Methods

Sample collection

In May 2025, a total of 40 samples of jasmine flower cakes were collected from different production enterprises and batches across the Chinese market. All the samples were sourced from 32 distinct production enterprises, including 10 provinces such as Yunnan, Fujian, Zhejiang, Jiangsu, Guangxi, Shandong, Sichuan, Hebei, Anhui, and Beijing. The sampled enterprises were diverse in scale, encompassing both large-scale, well-established food manufacturers with high brand recognition and numerous small- and medium-sized enterprises. Detailed information on the samples is presented in Table 1 and Fig. 1.

Reagents and instrumentation

Multi-element standard solutions for As, Cd, Cr, and Pb (GNM-M041940-2013; batch number: 24D80143; concentration: 1000 µg/mL) were sourced from the National Analysis and Testing Center for Nonferrous Metals and Electronic Materials. The Al standard solution (GBW30019-1000-NC-20; batch number: B1906197; concentration: 1000 µg/mL) was sourced from Tanmo Quality Inspection Standard Material Center. The Mn standard solution (CHAA-B-025120; batch number: 2427227; concentration: 1000 µg/mL) was obtained from Shanghai Anpon Cuisi Standard Technology Service Co., Ltd. The Ni standard solution (GBW(E)080128; batch number: 22011; concentration: 100 µg/mL) was sourced from the National Institute of Metrology, China. The Cu standard solution (GBW(E)082780; batch number: B22070053; concentration: 1000 µg/mL) was obtained from Tanmo Quality Inspection—Standard Material Center. An internal standard solution containing Li, Sc, Ge, Rh, In, Tb, Lu, and Bi (mixed internal standard solution; batch number: 54-124CRY2; concentration: 100 mg/L) was purchased from Agilent Technologies. Analytical grade nitric acid was obtained from MERCK (Merck KGaA, Darmstadt, HE, Germany). Deionised water was employed in all experiments, prepared using an Aike AK-RO-UP-200 Pure Water System.

Analyses were performed on an Agilent 7700X inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometer (Agilent Technologies Inc.) following sample digestion with an Anton Paar Multiwave 3000 microwave digestion system (Anton Paar GmbH). An MS-204 (Mettler Toledo) analytical balance with a precision of 0.00001 g was employed for sample weighing.

Preparation of mixed standard solutions

Mixed standard solutions were prepared to cover a range of concentrations suitable for constructing calibration curves for the analysis of heavy metals in the jasmine flower cake samples. The standard solutions were therefore diluted using a 2% nitric acid solution to prepare mixed calibration solutions with the following concentrations: 100, 500, 1000, 3000, and 5000 ng/mL for Al; 10, 50, 100, 300, and 500 ng/mL for Mn and Cu; 1, 5, 10, 30, and 50 ng/mL for Cr, Ni, As, Cd, and Pb, respectively.

Analysis and method validation

By including parameters such as radio frequency (RF) power, extraction voltage, ion lens settings, sampling depth, gas flow rates, and integration time, the methodology is ensured to be transparent and reproducible. The validation process described here helps to establish the sensitivity, precision, and robustness of the method, which are critical for credible scientific research.

ICP-MS detection conditions

The detection conditions for the target metal elements using ICP-MS were as follows: RF power: 1400 W; extraction voltage: − 142 V; ion lenses 1, 2, and 3: 2.2, − 23, and − 173.5 V, respectively; sampling depth: 62 mm; nebuliser chamber temperature: 2 °C; plasma gas flow rate: 15 L/min; auxiliary gas flow rate: 1 L/min; carrier gas flow rate: 1 L/min; dilution gas flow rate: 1 L/min; number of replicates: 3; integration time: 0.3 s; tuning mode: He mode.

Method validation

The method was validated in terms of linearity, precision, LOD, LOQ, and recovery rates. To determine the LOD and LOQ, blank samples were analysed 10 times, and the SD value was calculated. The LOD and LOQ were three and ten times the SD, respectively.

Risk assessment

The health risk assessment in this study adopted a screening-level, conservative approach. The objective was to provide an initial, health-protective estimate of potential risk by employing a series of precautionary assumptions regarding exposure and toxicity. Under these conservative conditions, if risks are deemed acceptable, they would be expected to be lower under more realistic exposure scenarios.

Dietary exposure

To evaluate dietary exposure and potential health risks, dietary exposure was estimated using the following equation:4\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$EDI = \left( {C \times dIR} \right)/BW,$$\end{document}

where C is the concentration of heavy metals in the sample (mg/kg), dIR is the average daily intake of food (kg/day), and BW is the body weight (kg).

According to the World Health Organisation (WHO) guidelines for risk assessment, when data on consumption of specific foods are unavailable, it is reasonable to use consumption data from similar foods (e.g. cereals and their products). In the absence of specific survey data on Jasmine flower cakes consumption and the high similarity between Jasmine flower cakes and traditional pasta products in terms of primary ingredient composition (mainly wheat flour) and dietary usage, the dIR value of pasta products was adopted as the exposure assessment parameter for Jasmine flower cakes. Based on the data from the China National Center for Nutrition and Health Monitoring, the dIR value for this food category is 0.1372 kg/day, and the BW value is 60 kg29.

Notably, specific, survey-based consumption data for jasmine flower cakes are currently unavailable. Given that the primary ingredient of jasmine flower cakes is wheat flour, similar to many pasta and bakery products, the daily intake rate for pasta products (dIR = 0.1372 kg/day) was adopted as a reasonable proxy for this assessment. This approach is consistent with common practice in dietary exposure studies of specialty grain-based foods. We acknowledge that this assumption may represent a conservative (health-protective) overestimate of long-term average daily intake for this occasionally consumed food item, and this uncertainty was considered in the interpretation of the risk estimates.

Non-carcinogenic risk assessment

The non-carcinogenic risk was assessed using standard procedures established by the United States Environmental Protection Agency (US-EPA) to evaluate the HQi and HI values. HQi quantifies the exposure risk of individual heavy metals, indicating the relative contribution of each metal to non-carcinogenic risk. It is calculated as follows:5\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$HQi = EDI/RfD,$$\end{document}

where RfD is the daily oral reference dose (mg/kg/day), based on technical reports from US-EPA. The RfD values for Al, Cr, Mn, Ni, Cu, As, Cd, and Pb are 1.43 × 10−1, 3 × 10−3, 1.4 × 10−1, 4 × 10−2, 2 × 10−2, 3 × 10−4, 1 × 10−3, and 4 × 10−3 mg/kg/day, respectively. However, because multiple heavy metals are typically present in food simultaneously, evaluating a single metal alone may underestimate the actual risk. The HI is the sum of all HQi values of the heavy metals assessed in the non-carcinogenic risk evaluation, affording a combined exposure risk from multiple heavy metals, and is calculated as follows:6\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$HI = \mathop \sum \limits_{i = 1}^{n} HQi.$$\end{document}

An HI value ≤ 1 indicates no significant adverse effects. HI > 1 indicates potential non-carcinogenic adverse effects, whereas HI ≥ 10 indicates a higher likelihood of chronic toxic effects17. The combined approach of evaluating both HQi and HI enables evaluation of both individual and cumulative heavy metal exposure risks, providing a more comprehensive scientific basis for the health risk assessment of jasmine flower cakes.

Carcinogenic risk assessment

The CR value assesses the probability of cancer development in populations exposed to heavy metals for long periods. For heavy metals that are more likely to pose potential CRs (e.g. Pb, As, and Cd), the CCR value, used for CR characterisation, is determined using the following equation:7\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$CCR = \mathop \sum \limits_{i = 1}^{n} CR.$$\end{document}

According to the technical reports from US-EPA, a CCR value < 1 × 10−6 indicates a low CR, while 1 × 10−6 ≤ CCR ≤ 1 × 10−4 indicates a moderate CR, which is within an acceptable range for carcinogenic levels. CCR > 1 × 10−4 indicates a high CR, warranting attention and further investigation30.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (version 25.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and Origin 2021 (OriginLab Corporation, Northampton, Massachusetts, USA). Descriptive statistics (mean, SD, median, and range) were calculated for heavy metal concentrations.

Pearson correlation analysis was conducted to examine the linear relationships among the eight heavy metals. The significance of r was tested using a two-tailed test. Correlations were considered significant at P < 0.05 and highly significant at P < 0.01.

Multivariate statistical analyses, including HCA and PCA, were employed to explore the associations and potential sources of heavy metals.

For HCA, the square Euclidean distance was used as the similarity measure, and the average linkage method was applied to perform R-mode clustering on the metal variables, thereby generating a dendrogram.

For PCA, the suitability of the data for dimensionality reduction was first assessed using the KMO measure and Bartlett’s test of sphericity. A KMO value > 0.5 and a significant Bartlett’s test (P < 0.05) were considered acceptable. Components with eigenvalues > 1 were retained (Kaiser’s criterion). Varimax rotation was applied to facilitate interpretation of the principal components. Heavy metals with absolute loading values > 0.5 on a given component were considered significant contributors to that component.

ABSTRACT
AbstractWhile stable diffusion and other diffusion-based text-to-image models are great at semantic synthesis, they fail to faithfully represent the rich artistic styles of fine art paintings without heavy finetuning, reference images or other architectural components. We introduce a simple method: learnable dynamic style embeddings, i.e. one dynamic token per style directly embedded in the stable Diffusion conditional stream. The style embedding provides accurate, reference-free control over 27 distinct WikiArt styles (Ex. Impressionism, Cubism, Pop Art), allows for gradual style blending through linear interpolation of the embeddings, and does not require token separation or multi-stage networks as in previous techniques like StyleForge or Style2Talker. Our end-to-end training pipeline fuses BLIP-generated captions with a weighted multi-objective loss: (1) style loss (Gram-matrix based ensuring texture/pattern fidelity), (2) perceptual loss (VGG features ensuring content/structure preservation), and (3) blend loss (smoothing multi-style transitions). With the technique trained on a downsampled WikiArt subset (~ 8000 images), the proposed method outperforms the untuned Stable Diffusion baseline, obtaining FID of 176.59 (vs 183.23), indicating better distributional alignment in the challenging domain of art, a CLIP score of 35. 48 for the image–text–style coherence, and 89.91% zero-shot style classification accuracy with CLIP. Qualitative results demonstrate further that semantic adherence and style fidelity are achieved robustly. This resource-efficient, modular-free architecture opens a new avenue to truly accessible artistic style transfer and generation, with potential real-world impact in creative AI.Keywords: Art, Fine tuning, Media, Preserving artistic, ImagesSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Recent advancements in text-to-image generation have been significantly propelled by diffusion models, which have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in synthesizing high-quality images from textual descriptions. These models operate by iteratively refining random noise into coherent images, guided by the input text. In recent years, there has been a swift progression of diffusion-based text-to-image generative models1–5, which facilitate the creation of high-quality images from straightforward text prompts. These models can produce a diverse array of objects, styles, and scenes with notable realism and variety. Their outstanding outcomes have motivated researchers to explore numerous methods to leverage their capabilities for image editing6–8. Recent studies, including Textual-Inversion9, DreamBooth10, and Custom Diffusion11, have advanced the personalization and customization of large-scale text-to-image diffusion models. By refining the parameters of the pretrained models, these strategies enable the adaptation of diffusion models to particular tasks or individual user preferences. Notwithstanding their encouraging outcomes, many constraints persist regarding the fine-tuning of large-scale text-to-image diffusion models10,11. A restriction is the extensive parameter space, which may result in overfitting or deviation from the initial generalization capability12. A further obstacle is the difficulty in acquiring several customized notions, particularly when they belong to comparable categories13.

In this work, we describe a sequence of innovations for improved styled image generation-first, by dynamic style embedding as a means to learn such embeddings, which will allow fluid adaptation of the model to different art styles. These embeddings are combined in a seamless manner with the style-conditioned text descriptions, such that the model produces images which not only capture the desired artistic style but also resonate with the meaning of the accompanying captions. Furthermore, our model is able to mix different styles or transition from one to another, making it especially versatile for artistic and design tasks. In this respect, the proposed methodology of training shall be an integrated process, balancing style, content, and blending using weight loss functions to ensure that the generated images will match the high-quality standards. We also introduce CLIP-based assessments on the quality of the match that the generated images, styles, and captions provide to give insight into the modeling performance. In addition, this was augmented even more with the addition of the rich WikiArt dataset with captions provided by the BLIP model itself for far superior learning of various styles and better overall quality of generated images. These collectively point to a very significant leap forward in creative AI that shows great capability in generating complex images that respect both artistic intention and textual meaning. Related work

The fast development of text-to-image generation has been fostered by the advances on generative models, personalization methods and style transfer techniques. Our work lies at the intersection of these fields, and we consider artistic style transfer specifically in the context of diffusion-based models. Here we group relative work under three themes: (1) diffusion-based text-to-image generation, (2) personalization and customization of diffusion models, and (3) artistic style transfer and control.

Diffusion models for text-to-image generation

Diffusion probabilistic models have set a new state-of-the-art in high-quality image synthesis by gradually denoising from random noise to an image, conditioned on textual inputs. Ho et al.1 proposed Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs), a seminal work which showed them to be capable of high-quality unconditional generation. This was generalized to text-conditional generation by means of classifier-free guidance14 and CLIP guidance4, providing accurate semantic guidance. Orgad et al.4 proposed the method of Text-to-Image Model Editing (TIME) in order to control implicit assumptions in text-to-image diffusion models. This approach enabled effective modification of parameters in less than one second, using a dataset of 147 prompt pairs15. Voynov et al.16 proposed a generic approach to conditioning a text-to-image diffusion model with a spatial map from another modality at inference time. It relied on a Latent Guidance Predictor (LGP), a perpixel Multi-Layer Perceptron, to predict the spatial maps from the latent features of noisy images. Li et al.17 introduced a universal methodology for text-to-image diffusion models tailored for mobile devices, albeit it necessitates advanced GPUs and cloud-based inference. Their approach attained enhanced FID and CLIP scores utilising merely 8 denoising stages.

Such recent advances in architecture also further elevate the field. Saharia et al.2 presented Imagen, which builds upon large language models for deep textual understanding and attains unprecedented levels of photorealism. Balaji et al.18 introduced eDiff-I, an ensemble of expert denoisers which enhances text alignment via multiple conditioning embeddings. Approaches centered on efficiency include MobileDiffusion19 for mobile deployment and Würstchen20, which employs a hierarchical architecture to decrease computational complexity. These developments highlight that diffusion models have matured for general-purpose text-to-image synthesis, although control of artistic style is still a challenge.

Personalization and customization techniques

Enabling user-specific customization has been a key research direction for making text-to-image models practically useful. Early approaches like Textual Inversion9 learn pseudo-words in the embedding space to represent new concepts from few examples. DreamBooth10 fine-tunes the entire diffusion model on subject-specific images, enabling precise reconstruction of specific objects or styles. Custom Diffusion11 extends this to multiple concepts simultaneously while minimizing interference between them.

More recent work has focused on efficient adaptation. Ruiz et al.21 introduced DreamBooth-LoRA, combining DreamBooth with Low-Rank Adaptation for parameter-efficient fine-tuning. Li et al.22 proposed TextCraftor, which fine-tunes the CLIP text encoder to improve controllable generation through text embedding interpolation. Reinforcement learning approaches like DPOK23 and DRTune24 optimize diffusion models using reward models rather than direct supervision. These personalization techniques demonstrate the community’s progress toward user-controllable generation, though they typically require multiple reference images or extensive fine-tuning for artistic style adaptation—a limitation our method addresses.

Artistic style transfer and control

Artistic style transfer has evolved from early neural methods to modern diffusion-based approaches. Gatys et al.25 pioneered neural style transfer using Gram matrix statistics from pretrained CNNs. This was accelerated by methods like AdaIN26, which performs real-time style transfer through feature statistics alignment. More recent approaches like MCCNet27 focus on content preservation during style transfer using multi-content consistency constraints.

Diffusion models have enabled new approaches to style control. StyleGAN-NADA28 uses CLIP guidance to adapt StyleGAN generators to new domains described by text. Inversion-based methods like InST29 learn style representations through textual inversion in the embedding space. Park et al.30 introduced StyleForge, which associates specific tokens with style attributes using reference images. However, these approaches often require multiple reference images, complex multi-stage architectures, or extensive fine-tuning.

Our work bridges these domains by introducing dynamic style embeddings that enable reference-free artistic style control within a standard Stable Diffusion architecture. Unlike StyleForge30 which requires token separation, or Style2Talker31 which employs complex multi-stage networks, our method integrates style conditioning directly into the diffusion process through learnable embeddings. This provides precise style control without architectural complexity, supports smooth style blending through embedding interpolation, and maintains the efficiency and flexibility of the base diffusion model.

Research methodology

This paper proposes a new approach for optimizing the Stable Diffusion model in generating artistic images, outperforming the model on generating specific artistic styles. It relies on the WikiArt dataset for different artistic styles, and its preprocessing includes subsampling because of the huge size of the dataset, categorization, and generating textual descriptions-so-called Captions-by the BLIP model. Subsequently, Dynamic Style Embeddings are used to extract features from each artistic style. Additionally, a combination of loss functions including Style Loss, Perceptual Loss, and Blending Loss are designed to preserve the specific characteristics of styles, content structure, and the capability to mix styles. These functions enable the model to produce images corresponding to various artistic styles or their combinations.

The base Stable Diffusion model is employed for the retraining process. For optimization, training methods such as Multi-Objective Training and the use of optimized Text Embedding are utilized. The proposed method in this research can generate high-quality artistic images with unique styles and precise style adjustment capabilities. Subsequently, the main components of this method, including data preparation, the design and combination of loss functions, and the model training process, are described (see Fig. 1).

Data preparation

This subsection of the research methodology fills the need of elaborating how the dataset that feeds the model was obtained. We chose the WikiArt dataset containing more than 80,000 images of different art directions among which are impressionism, cubism, and others. Figure 2 illustrates that the distribution of the used dataset is skewed. Further, because of computational constraints, the authors could not use all the data set as part of the analysis. Thus, for such categories as ‘coffee and tea’ with less than thousand samples we took all the available samples, and for such categories as ‘car models and car types’ with more than thousand samples, we took only thousand samples. In the end, on the basis of such refinement, as many as thirty million images were obtained from which 8000 images were chosen. To achieve this, the selected images were further partitioned into training and testing image sets whereby the training image set was constituted 80% of the entire data while the testing image set was constituted 20% of the entire data. Experimental setup

This subsection explains the results of our method in a clear and detailed way. We evaluate how well the method performs by looking at both visual examples and numerical results. Additionally, we test how different parts of the method contribute to the overall performance through careful studies.

We tested the model using the WikiArt dataset. This dataset contains artworks in 27 different styles, and we generated captions for each image using the BLIP model. We split the dataset into training and testing sets, with 80% of the data used for training and 20% for testing. The training process uses Stable Diffusion as the base model and introduces dynamic style embeddings.

These embeddings are created using a learnable layer whose dimensions are equal to the output of the text encoder (768). The loss functions are weighted as follows: style loss is assigned a weight of 1.0, perceptual loss is assigned a weight of 0.5, and blending loss is assigned a weight of 0.2. Training is conducted using the AdamW optimizer with different learning rates for the U-Net and style embeddings (specifically and , respectively). The Style2Talker model presents a challenging multistage design covering, among other areas, the modeling of facial geometry and its movements using the 3D Morphable Models (3DMM), a StyleGAN that generates high-resolution images, diffusion models, and CLIP embeddings in charge of style transfer and emotion adaptation. This paper presents a simpler and effective model using the main architecture of Stable Diffusion with dynamic embeddings for styling. In this case, the style information is baked directly into the generation process by means of dynamic embeddings. There is therefore no need to have different networks for different styles, as in StyleGAN. Since the architecture minimizes complications involved in managing several modules, our model is efficient and easier to train compared to other models. The Style2Talker model requires separate components like 3DMM, StyleGAN, and additional networks for styling during training. While Style2Talker excels in specific domains such as emotional and artistic styles, it has limitations due to its reliance on prior knowledge (e.g., 3DMM for facial motion). A unified architecture, an end-to-end training approach, and flexibility over different styles make our method a superior choice for generating and evaluating artistic images.

Complexity analysis

The proposed method is designed with computational efficiency in mind, addressing a major limitation of many prior approaches to style-specific or personalized generation in diffusion models. Unlike full-model fine-tuning (which updates the entire U-Net with ~ 860 million parameters) or methods such as DreamBooth (which typically requires fine-tuning large portions of the U-Net on small subject-specific datasets, often leading to high memory usage and overfitting risks), our approach only trains a small set of learnable style embeddings—27 vectors, each of dimension 768—resulting in a total of approximately 20,736 trainable parameters for style representation (plus negligible overhead for the fusion operation).

This is significantly more parameter-efficient than:

DreamBooth or Custom Diffusion, which fine-tune millions of U-Net parameters and demand high GPU memory (often multiple A100 GPUs for extended periods);

We realize there are some limitations to our comparison. However, “traditional” methods such as AdaIN (0.21s) are still an order of magnitude faster than diffusion-based methods, including ours (~ 3.25s). This speed-quality trade-off is a kind of “nature of the beast” that comes with iterative denoising procedure of the diffusion models. In addition, performance also varies due to the subjective nature of artistic style; styles with a clear visual grammar (e.g. Cubism) transfer better than those with more vague or general descriptions (e.g. Contemporary Realism). The performance of our method is also dependent on the accuracy and coverage of the style annotations in the WikiArt dataset, which may not cover every artistic subtlety.

Conclusion

This paper presented a new state-of-the-art approach for high-quality styled images, based on the Stable Diffusion base model as the backbone, together with dynamic style embedding. Our proposed method combined textual and style information to achieve an effective balance between style accuracy and image content preservation. Unlike other methods that relied on complex architectures, our method simplified the training and inference process and provided better performance. Extensive qualitative and quantitative evaluations showed that our method was superior to other methods in generating images that matched the desired styles well, including FID and CLIP Score metrics. Additionally, comparisons with the base models indicated a significant improvement in generating images of various artistic styles with simpler architectures. The flexibility and efficiency of this method made it suitable for applications such as artistic image generation and design. In the future, it was possible to integrate user-customized settings to produce more personalized and desirable outputs of style-specific images. In general, the proposed method created a solid platform for progress with regard to creating style-specific images and showcased the various capabilities of creative AI systems in the realms of art. The proposed method achieved state-of-the-art performance with a comparative FID Score of 176.59 and CLIP Score of 35.48.

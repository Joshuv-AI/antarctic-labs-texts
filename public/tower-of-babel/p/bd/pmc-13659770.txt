Sublytic C5b-9 induces S100A8/A9 expression via SMYD4-dependent SOX7-K159 methylation in the glomerular mesangial cells of Thy-1 nephritis rats
Authors: Yajuan Gong, Mingyu Peng, Yuhua Dong, Tao Wang, Junjin Liao, Yu Li, Jing Zhang, Yingwei Wang, Wen Qiu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRat Thy-1 nephritis (Thy-1N) is a model for human mesangioproliferative glomerulonephritis (MsPGN). Although sublytic C5b-9 has been reported to affect pro-inflammatory cytokine expression in glomerular mesangial cells (GMCs), the induction of other pro-inflammatory mediators remains unclear. In this study, we demonstrated that SET and MYND domain-containing protein 4 (SMYD4), SRY-related HMG-box gene 7 (SOX7), S100 calcium-binding protein A8 (S100A8), and S100A9 were overexpressed in the renal tissues of Thy-1N rats and in the GMCs stimulated by sublytic C5b-9. Mechanistically, SOX7 triggered S100A8/A9 gene transcription through binding to S100A8 promoter (+21 to +31 nt) and S100A9 promoter (−1698 to −1688 nt), and SMYD4 mediated SOX7 methylation at K159, a new methylation site, enhancing SOX7 function and S100A8/A9 transcription. The animal experiments confirmed that silencing SMYD4, SOX7, or S100A8/A9 gene could inhibit GMC proliferation, extracellular matrix accumulation, and proteinuria in Thy-1N rats. Moreover, silencing SMYD4 gene could inhibit renal SOX7-K159me2/3, and silencing SMYD4 or SOX7 gene could inhibit renal S100A8/A9 production. Meanwhile, the SOX7-K159 methylation inhibitory peptide exhibited the similar intervention effects as SMYD4 and SOX7 knockdown in Thy-1N rats. In addition, the expression levels of SMYD4, SOX7, SOX7-K159me2/3, and S100A8/A9 all increased in the renal tissues of MsPGN patients. Collectively, our findings suggest that SMYD4-mediated SOX7-K159me2/3 partly contributes to sublytic C5b-9-induced S100A8/A9 expression in Thy-1N rats, which provides a novel insight into human MsPGN pathogenesis.

Introduction

Mesangioproliferative glomerulonephritis (MsPGN) is a human glomerular immune–inflammatory disease (Dai et al., 2023; Horino et al., 2023; Chang et al., 2025). MsPGN pathogenesis is related to the deposition of complement C5b-9 complex on the glomerular mesangial cells (GMCs) and the increase of pro-inflammatory factors in the renal tissues of MsPGN patients (Horino et al., 2023; Ueki et al., 2024; Pan et al., 2025). However, the relationship between the formation of C5b-9 and the synthesis of pro-inflammatory factors, as well as the underlying mechanism of inflammatory damage in the kidneys of MsPGN patients, has not been fully clarified.

Rat Thy-1 nephritis (Thy-1N) is an animal model for studying human MsPGN (Lu et al., 2020; Matsumoto et al., 2022; Watanabe et al., 2022). Previous studies demonstrated that GMC lesions of Thy-1N rats are C5b-9-dependent, particularly sublytic C5b-9-dependent (Wang et al., 2022; Ying et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2024). Sublytic C5b-9 not only induces the production of pro-inflammatory factors, such as interleukin-6 (IL-6), TGF-β1, IL-23, and IL-36a (Zhang et al., 2014, 2018; Xia et al., 2022), but also causes the release of proliferative molecules, leading to renal inflammation, GMC proliferation, and extracellular matrix (ECM) accumulation (Xie et al., 2021; Wang et al. 2022; Wu et al., 2024). While we previously showed that the pathological changes of Thy-1N are similar to those of MsPGN and rat GMCs attacked by sublytic C5b-9 can increase above-mentioned factors, the production and roles of other pro-inflammatory factors or mediators in sublytic C5b-9-stimulated GMCs remain unclear.

It has been reported that the overexpression of S100 family members can mediate tissue inflammation and regulate inflammatory diseases (Abdi et al., 2024; Cómitre-Mariano et al., 2025). For instance, the expression levels of S100 calcium-binding protein A8 (S100A8) and S100A9 (S100A8/A9), S100B, and S100A12 were significantly elevated in rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, and lupus nephritis and correlated with the severity of these diseases (Davies et al., 2020; Inciarte-Mundo et al., 2022).

SRY-related high mobility group-box (HMG-box) gene 7 (SOX7) is a transcription factor, which has a conserved DNA binding domain and a transcription activation domain (Chiang et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024). SOX7 could enhance endothelial cell activation and renal allograft injury via the Jagged1–Notch1 pathway and IL-6 and TNF-α transcription (Qin et al., 2019) and also attenuate myocardial ischemic damage via regulating IL-33 and soluble ST2 in myocardial infarction foci (Yang et al., 2019). Besides, SOX7 can promote the secretion of IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α by neurological cells in the rat middle cerebral artery occlusion model by regulating Wnt/β-catenin signaling (Huang and Wang, 2023).

Protein post-translational modifications (PTMs), e.g. acetylation, ubiquitination, and methylation, can impact protein activity (Duchovni et al., 2024; Stastna, 2025). There are three types of lysine methylation, namely mono-methyl (Kme1), di-methyl (Kme2), and tri-methyl (Kme3), and the mechanism involves that lysine methyltransferases (KMTs) transfer the methyl donor S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) to the ε-amino of lysine and regulate protein function, thereby causing cellular behavior alteration (Giaimo et al., 2024; Uguen and Liu, 2025). For example, SETD7 can methylate TAF7 and induce cyclin A2 expression in clear cell renal cell carcinoma cells (Zhang et al., 2024). EZH2 or SET and MYND domain-containing protein 2 (SMYD2) can methylate NF-κB p65 or BCAR3, thereby promoting RKIP or FMNL1/2/3 transcription and alleviating kidney inflammation or breast cancer metastasis (Li et al., 2018; Wen et al., 2022).

In this study, an initial screening revealed that the expression levels of SMYD4, SOX7, and S100A8/A9 among the tested KMTs, transcription factors, and S100 family members, respectively, were mostly significantly enhanced in both the renal tissues of Thy-1N rats (in vivo) and the GMCs stimulated by sublytic C5b-9 (in vitro). Thus, we explored whether and how SMYD4 and SOX7 are involved in modulating S100A8A/9 expression in rat Thy-1N.

Results

SMYD4, SOX7, and S100A8/A9 are upregulated in the renal tissues of Thy-1N rats and in the GMCs stimulated with sublytic C5b-9

We first inspected the mRNA expression of 21 KMTs, 7 transcription factors of SOX families related to inflammation, and 13 members of S100 inflammatory proteins in the renal tissues of Thy-1N rats (in vivo) and in the GMCs under sublytic C5b-9 exposure (in vitro) based on document reports (Yang et al., 2016; Mitamura et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2021; Hu et al., 2021; Suehiro et al., 2021; Shen et al., 2022; Lu et al., 2024). The results showed that the mRNA levels of SMYD4 and SOX7 began to upregulate at 1 h and peaked at 3 h, the mRNA levels of S100A8/A9 started to increase at 2 h and peaked at 6 h, while the mRNA levels of other KMTs and SOX and S100 family members did not significantly change (Supplementary Figure S1). Thus, we further examined the expression patterns of SMYD4, SOX7, and S100A8/A9 in vivo and in vitro. Consistently, the mRNA expression of SMYD4, SOX7, and S100A8/A9 elevated at 1 h and peaked at 3 h, meanwhile their protein expression raised at 1 h and peaked at 3 h (SMYD4 and SOX7) or 6 h (S100A8/A9) (Figure 1A–D). Besides, their mRNA and protein levels were all remarkably enhanced in Thy-1N rats (Figure 1E and F) and in the GMCs treated with Thy-1 Ab plus C6-deficient serum and C6 (Thy-1 Ab+C6DS+C6) or sublytic C5b-9 compared to other controls (Figure 1G and H).

SMYD4 and SOX7 can induce S100A8/A9 gene transcription and expression in the GMCs exposed to sublytic C5b-9

To investigate the relationship between SMYD4, SOX7, and S100A8/A9 expression, the plasmids pCMV3-Flag-SMYD4, pIRES2-HA-SOX7, and pIRES2-S100A8/A9 were constructed and transfected into GMCs (Supplementary Figure S2A), and the expression of Flag-SMYD4, HA-SOX7, and S100A8/A9 was confirmed (Supplementary Figure S2B–E). SMYD4 or SOX7 overexpression could obviously upregulate S100A8/A9 mRNA (Figure 2A and B) and protein (Figure 2E and F) levels, while S100A8/A9 overexpression did not significantly affect SMYD4 or SOX7 mRNA (Figure 2C and D) and protein (Figure 2G and H; Supplementary Figure S3) expression. Moreover, shSMYD4, shSOX7, shS100A8, shS100A9, and their control shRNA (shCTR) were generated and transfected into GMCs, respectively. The transfection efficiency in the GMCs was evaluated by the GFP fluorescence (Supplementary Figure S2F). The expression levels of target proteins in the GMCs after shRNA transfection plus sublytic C5b-9 stimulation were detected (Supplementary Figure S2G–J). Subsequently, we observed that sublytic C5b-9-induced S100A8/A9 expression was reduced by SMYD4 or SOX7 gene knockdown (Figure 2I, J, M, and N), while sublytic C5b-9-induced SMYD4 and SOX7 expression was not affected by S100A8/A9 knockdown (Figure 2K, L, O, and P). To further identify whether SMYD4 can regulate S100A8/A9 expression in a SOX7-dependent manner, we overexpressed SMYD4 in wild-type (WT) GMCs or the GMCs with SOX7 gene knockdown and showed that SMYD4 overexpression could increase S100A8/A9 production in WT GMCs but not in the GMCs with SOX7 knockdown (Supplementary Figure S4A). Additionally, SMYD4 knockdown was able to downregulate S100A8/A9 expression upon sublytic C5b-9 exposure either with or without SOX7 overexpression (Supplementary Figure S4B). Collectively, our data suggest that SMYD4 and SOX7 are the upstream molecules of S100A8/A9, and SMYD4 can regulate S100A8/A9 expression via a SOX7-dependent manner in the GMCs stimulated by sublytic C5b-9.

SMYD4 and SOX7 bind with the same sites of S100A8/A9 promoters and boost S100A8/A9 gene transcription in response to sublytic C5b-9

It has been reported that transcription factors can bind to the promoters of target genes and initiate gene transcription (Xia et al., 2022; Blayney et al., 2023; Ge et al., 2023). To examine whether SOX7 could modulate S100A8/A9 gene transcription in the GMCs exposed to sublytic C5b-9, we used JASPAR database to predict SOX7 binding elements in S100A8/A9 promoter (Supplementary Table S5) and constructed S100A8/A9 full-length (FL) and truncated promoter luciferase reporter plasmids with pGL3-basic (Figure 3A). The following luciferase reporter assays revealed that sublytic C5b-9 treatment or SMYD4 and SOX7 overexpression increased S100A8/A9-FL promoter activity (Figure 3B and C), while SMYD4 or SOX7 knockdown decreased the promoter activity in sublytic C5b-9-incubated GMCs (Figure 3D). Besides, sublytic C5b-9 stimulation or SOX7 overexpression could upregulate the promoter activity of S100A8/A9-FL, S100A8/A9-1, S100A8/A9-2, and S100A8/A9-3 but not S100A8/A9-4 (Figure 3E and F), indicating that S100A8 promoter fragment (−198 to +84 nt) and S100A9 promoter fragment (−1802 to −586 nt) contain SOX7 binding elements, i.e. 5′-GGAAGAATGG-3′ (+21 to +31 nt) in S100A8 promoter and 5′-GGAACAAAAA-3′ (−1698 to −1688 nt) in S100A9 promoter. Furthermore, to discover the precise SOX7 binding elements in S100A8/A9 promoters, we constructed SOX7 binding element-mutant plasmids (termed as pGL3-S100A8-M and pGL3-S100A9-M) and then transfected them into the GMCs with different treatments. The luciferase assays showed significant lower activities of S100A8/A9-M promoters than that of S100A8/A9-FL promoters in sublytic C5b-9-stimulated or SOX7-overexpressed GMCs (Figure 3G and H). Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assay indicated that after SMYD4 or SOX7 overexpression, the amount of SMYD4 or SOX7 binding to S100A8/A9 promoters was greatly increased (Figure 3I and J). Meanwhile, the Re-ChIP assay demonstrated that SMYD4 and SOX7 could form a complex and bind to the same sites (+21 to +31 nt and −1698 to −1688 nt) of S100A8/A9 promoters (Figure 3K and L).

SMYD4-mediated SOX7 methylation is elevated in the renal tissues of Thy-1N rats and in the GMCs stimulated with sublytic C5b-9

SMYD4 can bind to its target proteins and induce the lysine hypermethylation modifications, including mono-methylation (me1), de-methylation (me2), and tri-methylation (me3) (Rubio-Tomás, 2021; Rueda-Robles et al., 2021; Zhou et al., 2024). We then investigated whether SMYD4 could bind to SOX7 and methylate SOX7 in sublytic C5b-9-exposed GMCs. Indeed, the methylation levels of SOX7-Kme2/3 but not SOX7-Kme1 increased at 1 h and peaked at 3 h in the renal tissues of Thy-1N rats (in vivo) and in the GMCs stimulated by sublytic C5b-9 (in vitro) (Figure 4A–F), and endogenous SMYD4 protein could bind to SOX7 in vivo and in vitro (Figure 4G and H). Consistently, when Flag-SMYD4 and HA-SOX7 plasmids were co-transfected into GMCs, exogenous SOX7 bound to SMYD4, and the methylation levels of SOX7-Kme2/3 but not SOX7-Kme1 were significantly enhanced (Figure 4I), implying that SOX7-Kme1 methylation was not regulated by SMYD4. Furthermore, when GMCs were transfected with shSMYD4 and HA-SOX7 plasmids followed by sublytic C5b-9 stimulation, the methylation levels of SOX7-Kme2/3 but not SOX7-Kme1 were notably reduced by SMYD4 silence (Figure 4J). These results confirm that the increased expression of SMYD4 induced by sublytic C5b-9 can catalyze SOX7-Kme2/3 methylation.

The identification of SMYD4 enzyme active site for SMYD4-mediated SOX7 methylation

Given that the active site of SMYD4 methyltransferase has not been reported, we performed a structural homology modeling of rat SMYD4 by using SWISS-MODEL server. Remarkably, the molecular docking between SMYD4 and SAM indicated that SMYD4 has a well-defined hydrophobic pocket of methyltransferase active region, which is composed of 12 amino-acid residues containing two aromatic residues (Y571 and Y591) surrounded by aliphatic residues (K243, C499, N500, N537, F593, D594, C595, and S596) and two heterocyclic residues (H432 and H538), presenting an optimal methylation ‘aromatic cage’ structural motif (Figure 5A). Further sequence comparison of five SMYD family members (SMYD1–5) indicated that five residues in rat SMYD4 (C499, N537, H538, Y571, and Y591) were strictly conserved (Figure 5B), and the hydrophobic pocket of SMYD4 was also highly conserved in SMYD1–5 (Supplementary Figure S5). It has been reported that two aromatic amino acids (Y571 and Y591) are the most probably sites that possess methyl-enzymatic activity, while the remaining three amino acids (C499, N537, and H538) are involved in structural maintenance (Lee et al., 2021). We then constructed two SMYD4-mutant (Y571A or Y591A) plasmids and transfected them into GMCs. SMYD4-Y571A mutation but not SMYD4-Y591A mutation could reduce SOX7 methylation (Figure 5C). Amino acid sequence blast indicated that SMYD4-Y571 was highly conserved in different species (Figure 5D). Besides, domain blast of rat SMYD1–5 from structural homology modeling manifested that the methyl-enzymatic activity domain including Y571 site was organized as an armadillo-repeat fold (Figure 5E).

SOX7-K159me2/3 induced by SMYD4 can enhance S100A8/A9 expression via SOX7 binding to their promoters

Next, we determined the sites of SOX7-Kme2/3 methylation catalyzed by SMYD4. Based on all lysine residues in SOX7 (Supplementary Figure S6), the corresponding SOX7 lysine-mutant plasmids (K39A, K91A, K148A, and K159A) and united-mutant plasmids (K57A/K61A, K77A/K81A/K84A, K113A/K119A/K120A, and K123A/K127A) were constructed. When the WT or mutant SOX7 expression plasmids were respectively transfected into rat GMCs for 48 h followed by sublytic C5b-9 stimulation for 3 h, the Kme2/3 methylation of HA-SOX7-K159A was not elevated (Figure 6A), indicating that K159 is the major site of SOX7-Kme2/3 methylation. Moreover, the comparison of SOX7 amino acid sequences across different species showed that SOX7-K159 was highly conserved (Figure 6B). Furthermore, when HA-SOX7-WT or HA-SOX7-K159A was co-transfected with Flag-SMYD4 into GMCs, SMYD4 and SOX7 nuclear localization were similar in the two groups of GMCs (Figure 6C), while the level of SOX7 binding to S100A8/A9 promoters (Figure 6D and E), S100A8/A9 promoter activity (Figure 6F), and S100A8/A9 mRNA and protein expression (Figure 6G and H) were all lessened in the GMCs with HA-SOX7-K159A transfection compared to those with HA-SOX7-WT transfection.

In addition, we prepared two monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) against SOX7-K159me2 and SOX7-K159me3, termed as SOX7-K159me2 mAb and SOX7-K159me3 mAb, respectively (Supplementary Figure S7A and Table S6). Afterwards, we examined the specificity of two mAbs for recognizing peptide-K159me0, peptide-K159me1, peptide-K159me2, or peptide-K159me3 and HA-SOX7-WT or HA-SOX7-K159A. The results showed that SOX7-K159me2 mAb could bind to SOX7-K159me2 and HA-SOX7-WT rather than peptide-K159me0, peptide-K159me1, peptide-K159me3, or HA-SOX7-K159A, while SOX7-K159me3 mAb could bind to SOX7-K159me3 and HA-SOX7-WT rather than peptide-K159me0, peptide-K159me1, peptide-K159me2, or HA-SOX7-K159A (Supplementary Figure S7B and C). Subsequently, we used these two mAbs to detect SOX7-K159me2/3 in the GMCs stimulated by sublytic C5b-9 (in vitro) and in the renal tissues of Thy-1N rats (in vivo) and found that both in vitro and in vivo SOX-K159me2/3 levels were increased (Supplementary Figure S7D and E).

Silencing renal SMYD4, SOX7, or S100A8/A9 gene or blocking SOX7-K159me2/3 can reduce the pathologic changes in Thy-1N rats

For in vivo study, we constructed LV-shCTR, LV-shSMYD4, LV-shSOX7, LV-shS100A8, and LV-shS100A9, respectively, and infected HEK293T cells at different titers (1 × 107, 1 × 106, and 1 × 105 Tu/ml). The optimum titer estimated by green fluorescent protein (GFP) was 1 × 107 Tu/ml (Supplementary Figure S8A). Thereafter, we infected rat GMCs with LV-shCTR at different titers for 48 h, 72 h, and 96 h to determine the optimum titer and time as 1 × 107 Tu/ml and 72 h by GFP examination (Supplementary Figure S8B). Next, we injected LV-shCTR (1 × 107 Tu/ml) into rat kidneys via renal artery and detected the GFP expression in the heart, liver, spleen, lung, and kidneys after 72 h by using the in vivo imaging system of small animals. Among all organs, kidneys showed the strongest fluorescence expression (Supplementary Figure S8C), suggesting that LV-shRNA was mostly perfused into rat renal tissues. Afterwards, LV-shCTR, LV-shSMYD4, LV-shSOX7, LV-shS100A8, LV-shS100A9, and LV-shS100A8/A9 were respectively infused into rat kidneys, and then Thy-1N was established in these rats. After 3 h, 6 h, and 7 days, the mRNA and protein levels of SMYD4, SOX7, SOX7-K159me2/3, and S100A8/A9, GMC proliferation, ECM accumulation, and proteinuria were measured. Compared with the LV-shCTR+Thy-1N group, the mRNA and protein levels of S100A80/90, GMC proliferation, ECM accumulation, and 24 h urine protein content were significantly reduced in each LV-shRNA-treated group (Figure 7A–F; Supplementary Figure S9A). Meanwhile, the SOX7-K159me2/3 levels in the renal tissues were markedly lower in LV-shSMYD4+Thy-1N and LV-shSOX7+Thy-1N groups than in the LV-shCTR+Thy-1N group (Figure 7B).

Nonmethylated peptides can inhibit protein methylation (Huang et al., 2022; Song et al., 2023). To examine whether SOX7 nonmethylated peptides containing SOX7-K159 site can alleviate Thy-1N renal lesion, we synthesized a SOX7 peptide (amino acids 154–165) with a trans-activator of transcription (TAT) tag in its N-terminal region and termed it as SOX7-K159 blocker (Figure 7G). Thereafter, SOX7-K159 blocker at 0.05 mg/100 g, 0.15 mg/100 g, or 0.2 mg/100 g was injected into rats via tail vein before Thy-1N induction. Compared with peptide NC, SOX7-K159 blocker could markedly reduce SOX7-K159me2/3 and S100A8/A9 levels in rat renal tissues (Figure 7H; Supplementary Figure S9B) and also notably lessen GMC proliferation and ECM accumulation (Figure 7I–K) as well as 24 h urine protein content (Figure 7L), implying that SOX7-K159me2/3 inhibition is beneficial for reducing the pathological changes in Thy-1N rats.

Furthermore, we silenced S100A8/A9 genes in the GMCs or treated GMCs with SOX7-K159 blocker in vitro. Either S100A8/A9 knockdown (Supplementary Figure S10A and C) or SOX7-K159 methylation inhibition (Supplementary Figure S10B and D) could slightly inhibit GMC proliferation, indicating that GMC-derived S100A8/A9 might not greatly promote GMC proliferation but exacerbate GMC proliferative lesions by enhancing renal inflammation in rat Thy-1N.

The expression levels of SMYD4, SOX7, SOX7-K159me2/3, and S100A8/A9 all increase in the renal tissues of MsPGN patients

We also examined the expression of SMYD4, SOX7, SOX7-K159me2/3, and S100A8/A9 proteins in the renal tissues of MsPGN patients. Immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining showed that SMYD4, SOX7, SOX7-K159me2/3, and S100A8/A9 were highly expressed in MsPGN samples compared with the control normal samples (Figure 8A and B; Supplementary Figure S11A). Correlation analysis revealed that the expression levels of these proteins were all positively correlated (Figure 8C; Supplementary Figure S11B).

In particular, SMYD4-mediated SOX7-K159me2/3 enhances SOX7 binding to S100A8/A9 promoters, which leads to S100A8/A9 gene transcription and expression and then triggers the proliferation of GMCs and other lesions in Thy-1N rats. Discussion

As shown in Figure 8D, sublytic C5b-9 induces S100A8/A9 expression in rat GMCs via SMYD4-dependent SOX7-K159 methylation, leading to the aggravation of GMC proliferation and other lesions in Thy-1N rats; blocking SMYD4-mediated SOX7-K159me2/3 methylation can inhibit SOX7 binding to S100A8/A9 promoters, eliminate S100A8/A9 expression, and abrogate Thy-1N pathological changes.

It has been reported that sublytic C5b-9 formation on GMCs can induce the expression of some transcription factors and the production of pro-inflammatory factors and mediators, and the underlying mechanism involves the regulation of transcription factors on pro-inflammatory factor gene expression (Zhang et al., 2018; Wang et al. 2022; Xia et al., 2022). In this study, we first screened 13 members of pro-inflammatory S100 family and found that expression levels of S100A8/A9 were significantly higher than that of other members both in the renal tissues of Thy-1N rats (in vivo) and in the GMCs exposed to sublytic C5b-9 (in vitro). Previous documents showed that S100A8/A9 proteins express in immune cells and histiocytes (e.g. monocyte–macrophages and vascular endothelial cells), and their overexpression can boost inflammatory response (Kilinc et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2024). For example, S100A8 can bind to TLR4 and RAGE on colon cancer epithelial cells and induce CXCL5 secretion by activating NF-κB or ERK–MAPK signaling (Chen et al., 2024). Besides, the increase of S100A8/A9 in the patient lung tissues with tuberculosis can enhance IL-1β, IL-18, TNF-α, and CXCL11 synthesis, thereby aggravating inflammatory lesion (Fan et al., 2024). In COVID-19 patients, the elevation of serum S100A8/A9 is closely correlated with the severity of excessive cytokine storm (Chen et al., 2020). These data indicate that S100A8/A9 play obvious pro-inflammatory roles in many diseases.

Gene transcription is regulated by specific transcription factors (Wang et al., 2022; Ying et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2024). SOX7 is a transcription factor with an HMG box domain, which can bind with the DNA sequence in target gene promoter and initiate target gene transcription (Chiang et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024). Recent researches have proposed that SOX7 can modulate cell apoptosis, proliferation, and inflammation through inducing the production of inflammatory factors or mediators (Qin et al., 2019; Yang et al., 2019; Chiang et al., 2023). For instance, SOX7 overexpression stimulated by platelet-derived growth factor promotes IL-33 gene transcription in a mouse tumor model (Yang et al., 2016). SOX7 can upregulate Jagged1 to enhance IL-6 and TNF-α production, thus augmenting the inflammatory injury in kidney transplantation (Qin et al., 2019). In the present study, we proved that SOX7 expression was raised in vivo and in vitro, and S100A8/A9 promoters contained SOX7 binding elements, implying the involvement of SOX7 in S100A8/A9 gene transcription modulation.

Transcription factors may regulate target gene expression via direct binding or inducing PTMs (e.g. methylation) (Francis et al., 2023; Ge et al., 2023; Ying et al., 2023). Based on several published reports about transcription factor methylation (Huang et al., 2022; Wen et al., 2022; Song et al., 2023), we screened common KMTs (including SETD, SMYD, NSD, EHMT, and EZH families) both in vivo and in vitro. The results manifested that SMYD4 expression was markedly elevated in a similar pattern as SOX7 expression. SMYD can methylate transcription factors to promote target gene transcription (Mayfield et al., 2020; Blawski et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2024). Thus, we further explored the role and mechanism of SMYD4-mediated SOX7 methylation in S100A8/A9 induction.

First, we confirmed that SMYD4 and SOX7 are the upstream molecules of S100A8/A9 in the GMCs stimulated by sublytic C5b-9. Then, we disclosed that SMYD4 and SOX7 could form a complex and bind to S100A8/A9 promoters, i.e. 5′-GGAAGAATGG-3′ (+21 to +31 nt) in S100A8 promoter and 5′-GGAACAAAAA-3′ (−1698 to −1688 nt) in S100A9 promoter, two important SOX7 binding elements, to promote S100A8/A9 gene transcription. In addition, we demonstrated that sublytic C5b-9 stimulation could not only enhance the interaction between SMYD4 and SOX7 but also increase the levels of SOX7 Kme2/3 methylation. By using monoclonal antibodies against SOX7-K159me2 and SOX7-K159me3, respectively, we successfully detected SOX7-K159me2/3 methylation in sublytic C5b-9-stimulated GMCs as well as in the renal tissues of Thy-1N rats and MsPGN patients. We also verified the roles of SMYD4, SOX7, and S100A8/A9 in vivo, by respectively silencing these genes in rat renal tissues or treating rats with a SOX7-K159me2/3 blocker before Thy-1N onset. Either treatment could markedly inhibit SOX7-K159me2/3, S100A8/A9 production, GMC proliferation, ECM accumulation, and proteinuria in Thy-1N rats.

It remains unclear how sublytic C5b-9 upregulates SMYD4 and SOX7 expression in GMCs. As reported, the JAK–STAT pathway activated by IFNα in hepatocytes can recruit and activate SETD2, which stabilizes STAT1 phosphorylation and enhances its transcriptional factor activity by methylating STAT1-K525 site (Chen et al., 2017). Additionally, NF-κB/mTOR/MYC activation can induce the expression of arginine methyltransferase PRMT5 after T cell activation through either transcriptional or non-transcriptional regulation (Webb, et al., 2019). Since our previous studies revealed that sublytic C5b-9 can activate several signal molecules and transcription factors (Xie et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2022), we hypothesize that sublytic C5b-9 might upregulate SMYD4 and SOX7 expression through these signal molecules and transcription factors. Further studies are needed to explore the precise molecular mechanisms.

In summary, sublytic C5b-9-upregulated SMYD4 mediates SOX7-K159me2/3 methylation, enhances the binding of SOX7 to S100A8/A9 promoters, and contributes to S100A8/A9 gene transcription and expression, which may provide a new insight into the mechanism of rat Thy-1N and human MsPGN.

Materials and methods

Animals, cells, and MsPGN patient’s renal samples

Male Sprague–Dawley (SD) rats and Balb/c mice were supplied by the Animal Experimental Center of Nanjing Medical University. All animal experiments in this study were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Nanjing Medical University (IACUC-2307016 and IACUC-2302026). Rat GMCs (HBZY-1) and human embryonic kidney cells (HEK293T) were purchased from China Centre for Type Culture Collection (Wuhan, China). Mouse myeloma cells (SP2/0) were donated by Associate Professor Xinjian Liu at Nanjing Medical University. The renal tissues of MsPGN patients through biopsy (n = 22) and the unaffected normal renal tissues of the patients with renal carcinomas (n = 18) were from the Affiliated Jiangning Hospital of Nanjing Medical University. All patients signed an informed consent document, and the procedures were approved by the Ethics Committee of Nanjing Medical University (approval number: 2021–318).

Thy-1N induction

Male SD rats (180–200 g) were respectively injected with Thy-1 Ab (0.75 ml/100 g) or normal rabbit sera (NRS, 0.75 ml/100 g) via tail vein to generate Thy-1N or control rats.

Cell culture and sublytic C5b-9 stimulation

Rat GMCs were cultured in minimal essential medium (MEM) plus 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS). HEK293T and SP2/0 cell lines were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% FBS. For GMCs, 5% Thy-1 Ab and 4% NHS were used to form sublytic C5b-9 on the surface of the cells as reported previously. Here, GMCs were treated with following substances: (i) MEM, (ii) 5% Thy-1 Ab, (iii) 5% Thy-1 Ab+4% HIS, (iv) 5% Thy-1 Ab+4% C6DS, (v) 5% Thy-1 Ab+4% C6DS+C6 (2 mg/L), and (vi) 5% Thy-1 Ab+4% NHS.

LV-shRNA inspection and in vivo experiments

HEK293T cells were infected with LV-shCTR, LV-shSMYD4, LV-shSOX7, LV-shS100A8, or LV-shS100A9 at 1 × 107 Tu/ml, 1 × 106 Tu/ml, or 1 × 105 Tu/ml for 72 h, and GFP expression was observed. GMCs were infected with LV-shCTR in above-mentioned titers for 48 h, 72 h, or 96 h, and the optimum titer and time point (1 × 107 Tu/ml and 72 h) were determined. Afterwards, rat kidneys were perfused with LV-shCTR via renal artery for 72 h, and the GFP expression in the heart, liver, spleen, lung, and kidneys was examined by the in vivo imaging system of small animals (IVIS, Caliper Life Sciences). Subsequently, SD rats were divided into 7 groups (n = 5 in each group), namely, (i) LV-shCTR+NRS, (ii) LV-shCTR+Thy-1N, (iii) LV-shSMYD4+Thy-1N, (iv) LV-shSOX7+Thy-1N, (v) LV-shS100A8+Thy-1N, (vi) LV-shS100A9+Thy-1N, and (vii) LV-shS100A8/A9+Thy-1N. At 72 h post-perfusion, rat Thy-1N was induced by injecting Thy-1 Ab, and the renal tissues were obtained after 3 h, 6 h, and 7 days.

TAT peptide treatment

SOX7 peptide (amino acids 154–165) with a TAT tag in N-terminal region (termed SOX7-K159 blocker) and the amino acid disorder control peptide (termed peptide NC) were synthesized by GenScript (Nanjing, China). SOX7-K159 blocker peptide (0.05 mg/100 g, 0.15 mg/100 g, or 0.2 mg/100 g) or peptide NC (0.15 mg/100 g) was respectively injected into rats via tail vein, followed by Thy-1 Ab or NRS intravenous administration. SD rats were divided into 5 groups (n = 5 in each group), namely (i) Peptide NC+NRS, (ii) Peptide NC+Thy-1N, (iii) SOX7-K159 blocker (0.05 mg/100 g)+Thy-1N, (iv) SOX7-K159 blocker (0.15 mg/100 g)+Thy-1N, and (v) SOX7-K159 blocker (0.2 mg/100 g)+Thy-1N. After 3 h, 6 h, and 7 days, the rat renal tissues were harvested.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 5.0. Significant differences among groups were determined by one-way ANOVA, and comparisons between two groups were analyzed by t-test. Pearson correlation analysis was also performed to investigate the correlation between two factors. P < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

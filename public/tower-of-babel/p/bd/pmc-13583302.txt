A segmented computational method for deformation and dynamic characteristics analysis of deep-sea hydraulic valve
Authors: Xinhao Li, Rourou Dong, Chuangting Xin, Jing Yao, Shuai Yang, Pei Wang, Yihan Ren
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Hydraulic systems serve as the primary drive mechanism for deep-sea submersibles. As these vehicles descend, ambient pressure increases from 0 to 110 MPa, inducing elastic deformation in the spool and sleeve that alters damping forces and compromises flow stability. To address this challenge, we propose a segmentation calculation method (SCM) that efficiently captures the dual nonlinear coupling between pressure distribution and cross-sectional geometry in predicting radial deformation. The SCM eliminates the need for iterative Multiphysics simulations by integrating analytical fluid–structure interaction into a single modeling process, reducing computational cost while maintaining high fidelity. Validation against three-dimensional finite element analysis (FEA) shows strong agreement, with a coefficient of determination (R2 = .87) across 441 evaluation points—corresponding to an 89.6% variance explained in radial deformation. Although experimental validation is not yet available, consistency with both FEA and computational fluid dynamics (CFD) results supports the model's reliability. Using the SCM, we further compare damping characteristics for spool–sleeve pairs made of steel, alumina, and silicon nitride, offering practical insights for the design of full-ocean-depth hydraulic valves.

Introduction

Hydraulic systems, as one of the crucial power transmission and control technologies in modern engineering, are widely employed in deep-sea high-power, large-scale operational equipment, such as underwater remotely operated vehicles and manned submersibles.1–5 As illustrated in Figure 1, the deployment of a pressure compensator enables adaptation to extremely high seawater environmental pressures, maintaining a balance between the internal and external pressures of the hydraulic system.4,6,7 In this deep-sea environment, where the maximum environmental pressure can reach 110 MPa, the impact on the motion pairs of hydraulic valves becomes significant.8–10

Schematic diagram of deep-sea subsea hydraulic system.

Hydraulic components play a crucial role in ensuring the normal operation and high efficiency of hydraulic systems. Typical hydraulic components, such as the spool and valve body in a hydraulic slide valve or the plunger and cylinder body in a piston pump, require appropriate clearance to facilitate relative motion, considering manufacturing errors and surface roughness factors.11–13

When hydraulic components operate in deep-sea environments, they face challenges posed by structural deformation resulting from extremely high ambient and working pressures. This deformation directly impacts the clearance height in hydraulic components, 9 particularly for two-way flow control valve, which height directly affect damping characteristics, resulting in a degradation of smoothness of operation, sealing, and velocity/force output in deep-sea equipment. 8 Additionally, the presence of clearances may cause minor leakage when hydraulic oil circuits need to be sealed, especially when pressure-compensating grooves exist on the spool, causing the pressure distribution in the clearance fit of the moving pairs to exhibit a nonlinear state.14,15

In recent years, progress has been made in the study of fit clearance deformation in deep-sea hydraulic valves. For example, Li and Wu derived deformation formulas for clearance fits in deep-sea environments, indicating that the deformation height of clearance fits decreases linearly along the axial direction. 8 However, these studies are often based on specific assumptions, such as the cross-sectional shape being a constant spool diameter in a sliding valve, and they overlooked the impact of clearance fits on damping characteristics when hydraulic oil circuits are sealed. Therefore, these models have limitations for practical situations.

Existing studies on the dynamic characteristics of deep-sea hydraulic valves have achieved progress. Yinshui et al. used ANSYS Workbench to study the fit clearance deformation of a pressure relief valve at different depths but did not consider establishing an analytical model for deformation throughout the entire ocean depth. Liu et al. studied the dynamic performance of a designed seawater direct-acting pressure relief valve in deep-sea environments, considering the significant impact of the clearance fit between the damping spindle and the damping liner. 16 Cao et al. established a mathematical model for a deep-sea hydraulic power device based on a linear variable parameter modeling approach, considering the effects of ambient pressure on hydraulic oil compressibility and viscosity, and conducted simulation analysis on the dynamic performance of the hydraulic power device. 17 Tian et al. developed a mathematical model for a pressure-controlled variable pump, which considered the increase in hydraulic oil viscosity due to seawater ambient pressure. 18 Wu, focusing on actuators such as robotic arms affected by the introduction of ambient pressure by pressure compensators, established a detailed nonlinear mathematical model for a deep-sea valve-controlled hydraulic cylinder, considering the viscosity-pressure characteristics of hydraulic oil. 19

However, these studies often consider the changes in damping characteristics caused by oil but overlook the impact of clearance fit deformation due to ultra-high pressure on damping characteristics. Therefore, for a comprehensive analysis of deep-sea hydraulic systems, further research on the relationship between fit clearance deformation and damping characteristics is necessary to gain a more comprehensive understanding of the performance of hydraulic systems in deep-sea environments. To deeply understand the performance of hydraulic systems in deep-sea environments, this study focuses on a pressure-compensated two-way flow control valve with fixed orifice, adjustable pressure compensator exhibiting flow instability under the entire ocean depth. From the perspectives of deep-sea deformation and changes in hydraulic fluid properties, we comprehensively explored the damping characteristics of the two-way flow control valve. By proposing a dual non-linear deformation SCM, we obtained the deformation patterns of the spool, sleeve, and fit clearance for different materials. Additionally, through employs finite element analysis (FEA), we validated the high computational precision of this method. Compared to the frequent adjustment of parameters in FEA operations and waiting times, SCM saves a significant amount of time costs while achieving relatively high computational precision through a single modelling. Furthermore, based on equations describing the variation in hydraulic oil density and viscosity with pressure, we established damping coefficient equations related to deformation, hydraulic fluid viscosity, and density under the full range of sea depths and operational conditions.

Based on SCM, the research results indicate that under deep-sea conditions, the performance degradation of high-performance ceramics is significantly smaller than that of alloy steel, laying the foundation for the dynamic analysis of entire ocean depth two-way flow control valve. This study provides essential references for the design and performance optimization of deep-sea hydraulic systems, especially in the selection of flow stability under complex operating conditions and material performance. In-depth research on the relationship between fit clearance deformation and damping characteristics is of positive significance for a comprehensive understanding of the performance of deep-sea hydraulic systems.

The selected pressure values are grounded in deep-sea operational requirements and established hydraulic standards. The max ambient pressure is set to Pa = 110 MPa, which corresponds to the hydrostatic pressure at a depth of 11,000 m—representing the Challenger Deep, the deepest known point in the ocean. This is calculated as Pa=ρgh, where seawater density ρ=1030 kg/m3, gravitational acceleration g = 9.81 m/s2, and depth h = 11,000 m. The working pressure is specified as Pw = 31.5 MPa, consistent with the nominal system pressure for high-pressure hydraulic circuits in subsea equipment as defined in ISO 4413:2020. This value represents a typical design pressure for deep-ocean hydraulic actuators and valves operating under extreme environmental loads.

Pressure distribution of fit clearance

The oil film gap between the regulating valve spool and sleeve is extracted, as depicted in Figure 3. Subsequently, ANSYS/Fluent was employed for flow field simulation with boundary conditions set at an inlet pressure (sum of the ambient pressure Pa and the working pressure Pw) of 141.5 MPa and an outlet pressure (ambient pressure Pa) of 110 MPa.

The working fluid is specified as ISO VG 10 hydraulic oil (commonly referred to as “No. 10 hydraulic oil”), which is widely used in deep-sea hydraulic systems due to its low viscosity and good low-temperature performance. At the reference temperature of 40 °C, the oil has a dynamic viscosity of μ = 10cSt (10 × 10⁻6 m2/s) and a density of ρ = 870 kg/m3. Given the low Reynolds number (Re < 20), the flow is laminar and modeled without any turbulence closure. These properties are consistent with ISO 3448 and manufacturer data sheets for mineral-based hydraulic fluids in this viscosity grade. The fluid is treated as incompressible and Newtonian in all simulations. The computational mesh consists of 120,000 hexahedral cells, generated with structured layering in the clearance region. Mesh quality is confirmed by a maximum skewness of < 0.35 and minimum orthogonality of > 0.85, indicating high geometric fidelity and numerical robustness. A mesh independence study confirmed that further refinement changes the predicted radial deformation by less than 0.7%, ensuring solution robustness.

SCM of motion pairs

Load analysis and deformation assumptions

With the known pressure values on the outer surface of the spool and the inner surface of the sleeve, it becomes feasible to analyse the forces acting on the spool and sleeve. Subsequently, the analysis of the elastic deformation of the spool and sleeve follows. However, up to this point, here is a significant issue, as illustrated in Figure 6, where both the inner and outer surfaces of the spool and sleeve exhibit nonlinear pressure distributions. Concurrently, the outer diameter (inner diameter) of the spool(sleeve) also undergoes nonlinear variations. In comparison to known studies8,9,16 on deep-sea deformation behaviour or mechanical analyses, these components possess unique force and structural characteristics cannot be adequately described by mere numbers and have strong discontinuity. The analytical methods proposed in these studies are inadequate to meet the requirements for analysing the deformation of the spool and sleeve in deep-sea two-way flow control valve.

Therefore, our first step is to analyse the nonlinear characteristics of the section and load, dividing it into small segments with equal loads and cross-sectional shapes. Afterward, by combining these segments using shear forces and moments, we aim to express the macroscopic deformation. This is the goal of the double nonlinear deformation SCM. Through this approach, we can more accurately describe the relationship between the forces and deformations of the spool and sleeve under complex pressure and shape changes, providing a more reliable foundation for the performance analysis of deep-sea two-way flow control valve.

To assess the numerical precision and robustness of the proposed SCM, a sensitivity analysis was conducted by perturbing key input parameters—namely Young's Modulus E and applied pressure (Pa + Pw) with an average relative deviation of 10.4%, corresponding to an 89.6% agreement level. For each perturbation case, the radial deformation at 441 evaluation points (matching the FEA–SCM comparison grid) was recomputed. The resulting coefficient of variation across all perturbed predictions was found to be less than 1.8%, indicating high repeatability and low output dispersion. This confirms that the SCM is not overly sensitive to minor uncertainties in material or loading conditions. The application of SCM in valve spool for all sea depth

Application scenarios

SCM can determine the probability of sticking occurring at different locations of the spool-sleeve clearance pair under ultra-high pressure in deep-sea environments. This enables flexible adjustment of the initial clearance width in various fitting regions during the early design stage, serving as an aid in anti-sticking design of deep-sea hydraulic valve spools. In addition, SCM can also be used to calculate key parameters that significantly affect dynamics, such as the deformation coefficient and damping coefficient of the clearance fit under different material combinations and fitting forms. The following sections will detail the application of SCM based on these two aspects.

Deformation coefficient for all sea depths

Due to the high-dimensional mapping relationship among Pa, Pw, deformation location, and deformation magnitude, it is challenging to intuitively illustrate the complexity of local deformations in the spool, sleeve, and clearance fit under various depths and working conditions. Therefore, it is necessary to introduce a concept to describe the overall degree of deformation. The SCM is qualitatively consistent with the FEA results, allowing for a more detailed analysis of deformations under small gradients of working pressure and Pa. Hence, we define a deformation coefficient (e), which is the sum of the product of the deformation magnitude and the Segment length at each analysis unit. Conclusion

This study reveals through FEA that pressure decay in the local pressure-equalizing groove occurs solely at the shoulder, with the decay rate being related to the ratio of total pressure drop to the total width of the shoulder, thereby validating the proposed pressure distribution model. To effectively address the dual nonlinear changes in pressure distribution and cross-sectional shape, we proposed the SCM. This approach not only significantly reduces computational time through a single modelling process but also achieves high computational precision. Based on the pressure distribution discussed in the second section, we calculated the deformation patterns of the valve spool, sleeve, and the clearance fit under various operating conditions across the entire ocean depth by SCM, with further validation through FEA. Moreover, by employing a damping coefficient calculation method for the multi-shoulder valve spool and sleeve clearance fit, we obtained the variation patterns of the damping coefficient for the clearance in the motion pair, providing robust support for the dynamic modelling of the valve spool under full ocean depth conditions.

Although this method lacks effective means of practical experimental verification, considering the comprehensive assumptions made in the simulation and the fact that the deformations are relatively small compared to the actual dimensions, the simulation results can robustly support the proposed hypotheses. Compared to previous studies on linear loads and cross-sectional changes, this study considers more complex scenarios. Thus, the proposed SCM aims to divide the complex analysis object into simple micro segments to achieve good computational precision. Furthermore, the analysed variations in ambient pressure and working pressure have a broader span and smaller gradients, making them more valuable for future studies on the deep-sea behavior of valve spools.

In summary, this study fills the gap in the analysis methods for hydraulic motion pairs with dual nonlinear changes in load and cross-section. It deeply explores the deformation and damping variations of the deep-sea spool-sleeve motion pair, providing solid support for establishing a comprehensive motion model under entire ocean depth and all operating conditions.

The complex interplay between diabetes mellitus and pancreatic carcinogenesis: deciphering multifactorial mechanisms and identifying emerging therapeutic vulnerabilities
Authors: Cui-Ni Lu, Ji-Bin Liu, Zi-Yu Chen, Bu-Tao Guo, Chang-Sheng Dong, Hong Yu, Xiao-Mei Tang, Jia Liu, Chang-Xue Ji, Kun Tao, Xiao-Dong Kuang, Wen-Guang Wu, Da Fu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Diabetes mellitus (DM) represents a significant global public health challenge, as economic development and changes in modern dietary habits have contributed to a rising prevalence of DM patients. Pancreatic cancer (PC), a highly aggressive malignancy, exhibits a 5-year survival rate of approximately 10%. The intricate and perilous relationship between DM and the onset and progression of PC is increasingly being elucidated, revealing a complex underlying network that encompasses multiple dimensions, including molecular signaling, metabolism, endocrinology, immunity, microbiota, and tumor microenvironment formation. Consequently, this review aims to delineate the overarching framework of the extensive relational network between DM and PC, focusing on the aforementioned aspects. Specifically, this review highlights the promoting effects of DM on the carcinogenic process of PC, emphasizing the complexity of the DM-PC interaction and identifying potential therapeutic targets for intervention.Graphic abstract

Introduction

Diabetes mellitus (DM) is a chronic metabolic disorder with hyperglycemia as its typical symptom, categorized into four primary types, including type 1 diabetes (T1D), type 2 diabetes (T2D), specific types of diabetes due to other causes, and gestational diabetes [1]. Closely associated with socioeconomic factors, globally, the number of people with diabetes has quadrupled within the past three decades, and diabetes becomes the ninth major cause of death due to its various complications including cardiovascular diseases [2] and pancreatic cancer (PC) [3]. Pancreatic cancer (PC), specifically pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) which constitutes the majority of cases, is a highly aggressive and lethal malignancy with poor prognostic outcomes and rising incidence. It ranks 12th in terms of incidence and 6th in terms of mortality [4]. Furthermore, most patients present with unresectable tumors or metastasis at diagnosis, resulting in a 5-year survival rate of approximately 10%. Unless otherwise specified, the term “DM” in this text refers to T1D and T2D, while “PC” refers to PDAC.

Given the multifaceted physiological and pathological functions and roles of pancreas both physiological and pathological [5], the correlation and interaction between DM and PC are intricate and interconnected across multiple dimensions including endocrine and exocrine, metabolism, immunity, etc. This has been substantiated by numerous clinical studies and meta-analyses. Studies of underlying mechanism cover almost every impact DM and PC can cause and can be caused, revealing an extensive immunometabolic interaction network and signaling molecule pathways.

Compared with other reviews on DM and PC, this review comprehensively addresses multiple molecular signaling pathways associated with PC, and intends to explain the complex relations between DM and PC from the comprehensive aspects of pancreatic carcinogenesis, including molecular signaling pathways, metabolism, endocrinology, immunity, microbiota, and the composition of PC characteristic tumor micro-environment. The aim of this review is to provide an overview of the extensive interaction network, cover most fundamental areas in depth, and potentially offer new perspectives for future research and experimental endeavors.

Pancreatic carcinogenesis

The pancreatic carcinogenesis is a multistep progress involving various alterations in histology, molecule and TME levels.

Acinar-to-ductal metaplasia (ADM) represents the primary alteration in this carcinogenic process, typically occurring in response to diverse stimuli such as chronic inflammation or cellular injury. Numerous studies have confirmed that ADM plays an important role in post-injury restoration, in which acinar cells dedifferentiate and transform into ductal-like cells with proliferation capacity [6]. ADM seems to be a protective mechanism which re-endows cells the ability of proliferation metaplasia to encounter the stimuli. Nevertheless, if the stimuli persist, the metaplastic process may progressively evolve into dysplasia and subsequently pancreatic intraepithelial neoplasia (PanIN), the most common precancerous alteration of PC [6, 7].

In addition to PanIN, two-tiered classification of PC pancreatic carcinogenesis includes two distinct morphological pathways leading to invasive PC, intraductal papillary mucinous neoplasm (IPMN) and mucinous cystic neoplasm (MCN) [7, 8] (Fig. 1). The whole multistep process of carcinogenesis centers on the mutations of 4 major genes (KRAS, CDKN2A, TP53, and SMAD4), referred to as “mountains”, alongside numerous other less frequently mutated genes termed “hills”. [9, 10]. Among these, the process is believed to be initiated mostly (92%) by the acquisition of the oncogenic KRAS mutation, however it is insufficient to transform requiring one or more mutations of the “mountains” or the “hills”, such as the loss of TP53 alleles [9, 11]. The progression from ADM to PanIN is promoted by the factors leading to ductal-like transformation, such as SOX9 [12, 13], while being suppressed by factors preserving acinar cell characteristics, such as Ptf1a and Mist1 [11]. Further development of low-grade PanIN to high-grade PanIN or even PC requires inactive mutations and epigenetic modification of tumor suppress genes, including CDKN4A, TP53 and SMAD4, as well as the establishment of a tumor-favorable microenvironment [9, 10, 14].Fig. 1The three distinct morphological pathways to invasive PC. TME is composed of cellular compartments, extracellular matrix and tumor vessels, etc. The most important component of TME is the cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) constituting up for up to 90% of the total tumor mass of pancreatic tumor [15–17]. CAFs can behave as cancer-promoting CAFs (pCAFs) or cancer-restraining CAFs (rCAFs), depending on their effects on immune regulation and metastasis. In case of pCAFs, high level secretion of TGF-β by pancreatic stellate cells (PSCs) induces the direct suppression of CD8 + T cell by up-regulating SOX9 leading to secretion of CXCL5 recruiting myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) and suppression of CD8 + T cell [17, 18]. Additionally, a subpopulation of pCAFs known as inflammatory CAFs (iCAFs) also plays an important role in immune suppression by secreting inflammatory cytokines and increasing population of MDSCs [15, 16, 19].

Moreover, immunosuppressive cells, including MDSCs and tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs), contribute to the formation of precancerous lesions, such as ADM, which is a critical step in pancreatic carcinogenesis.

Pancreatic carcinogenesis of DM

Due to the causality of diabetes, pancreas lesions and related pancreatic alterations should have a huge impact on the formation of PC, which is also a common complication of DM. Although T1D and T2D differ markedly in terms of etiology, mechanisms, symptoms, and treatment strategies, within the context of PC risk, the association between T2D and pancreatic cancer is more pronounced. Chronic hyperinsulinemia and metabolic dysregulation serve as the primary risk factors [20, 21].

The carcinogenesis of DM is a complicated and long-studied topic, including various perspectives, such as molecular signaling pathway, immunology, metabolite, etc., revealing multiple sophisticated yet still less-known underlying mechanisms.

Molecular pathway

KRAS

GTPase KRas (KRAS) acts as a signaling transducer involving in various events, including proliferation [22]. As one of the RAS signaling isoforms, KRAS transmits upstream signal to diverse downstream signaling including the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway [22, 23].KRAS is among the most critical and frequently mutated oncogenes, playing a pivotal role in the carcinogenesis of various cancer types, including PC [10, 22, 23]. Notably, the activating mutation of KRAS is observed in approximately 90% of PC cases, likely representing the earliest decisive mutation during PC development [8, 10, 24]. This makes KRAS a key focus for understanding the association between PC and DM.

According to the study, the incidence of KRAS mutations is significantly elevated in pancreatic cells in the condition of high glucose. Comparing with the PC patients without DM, the KRAS gene is higher in PC patients with DM. Furthermore, the in vitro experiments demonstrates that the high glucose treatment induces the de novo mutation of KRAS especially the KRASG12D25, which is the most common mutation of KRAS, composing 45% of the mutation [23, 26]. The mechanisms underlying the mutation of KRAS can be attributed to the adaption to low glucose availability. The up-regulation of GLUT1 is one of the most significant downstream alterations of KRAS mutation [25, 27–29]. In the condition of DM, due to the high-glucose environment, the glucose metabolism can be promoted by the increased glucose absorption capability, and furthermore conferring the survival advantages. In this case, the KRAS mutated cells with a higher expression of GLUT1 can have advantages in glucose absorption and glucolysis process, consistent with the experiments results demonstrating growth advantages of the mutated cells under both high-glucose [29] and glucose-deprivation conditions [25] (Fig. 2).Fig. 2The mutation of KRAS is considered to be the most common and the first event in PC progression. In addition to the survival advantage of KRAS mutated cells, the disorder of dNTP pool caused by O-GlcNAcylation of ribonucleotide reductase (RNR) catalytic subunit M1 (RRM1) is also a possible contributor to the mutation of KRAS [29, 30] (Fig. 2). The RNR and nucleotide diphosphate kinase (NDPK) are vital enzymes that maintain the dNTP pool, balancing the supplies and expenditures of dNTP. However, compared with RNR, the alteration of NDPK in the condition of DM to maintain dNTP pool seems to be under-investigated or less important than its role in regulating ADP/ATP ratio [30–32]. RNR is a tetrameric complex and the O-GlcNAcylation of RNR happens at T734 of RRM1, one of its subunits compromising its activity. However, in the in vitro experiment, the disturbance of dNTP pool of cells can be compensated by supplementary deoxynucleosides intake [29, 30, 33], suggesting the reversibility of the disturbance and the possibility of corresponding therapy.

All of these suggest a possible result that the high-glucose condition promotes the KRAS mutation and giving survival advantages to the mutated cells by enhancing the ability of glucose absorption and glucolysis. Selected by the survival pressure, the remaining pancreatic cells are commonly with KRAS mutation. However, the result remains on the level of correlation between DM and mutation, lacking of the underlying molecular mechanism. Therefore, further research is warranted to elucidate the mechanisms of KRAS mutation under conditions of DM or hyperglycemia (Fig. 2).

The existing therapy can only target KRASG12C, which is a rare mutation in KRAS mutant, accounting for only 1–3% comparing with KRASG12D (44%), KRASG12V (34%), KRASG12R (20%), and KRASQ61H (4%) [23, 26, 28] (Fig. 2). However, a promising drug namely MRTX1133 targeting on KRASG12D with its efficacy both in vivo and in vitro ushering in its recent initiation of a phase I/II clinical trial [9, 23, 26], shedding light on the future targeted therapy for PC.

TGF-β/SMAD4

SMAD4, a signaling molecule in the TGF-β pathway, is associated with tissue repair, fibrosis, inflammation, and carcinogenesis. Its deletion represents one of the primary mutations driving the progression of high-grade PanIN [34, 35]. The process of TGF-β signaling is conducted by a series of SMAD family protein. Initially, after receiving TGF-β, the TGFR transmits signal to SMAD2 and SMAD3 via phosphorylation. Subsequently, the SMAD4 forms a trimeric complexes with phosphorylated SMAD2 and SMAD3, which then shuttle into the nucleus to bind with transcription factors (TFs), thereby regulating gene expression [35]. SMAD4 acts as a tumor suppressor and inhibits epithelial cell proliferation as well as having an inhibitory effect on tumors by reducing angiogenesis and increasing blood vessel permeability [34]. However, TGF-β pathway rather plays a duplicitous role in PC, which demonstrate tumor-suppressing effect by promoting apoptosis at the early stage while promotes invasion and metastasis of PC during the advanced stage [34, 36, 37]. The diversity of downstream target genes of TGF-β contributes to its complex, paradoxical, and context-dependent oncogenic functions and mechanisms.

The up-regulation of TGF-β ligand in the condition of T2D has been reported [38]. The loss of β-cell mass or β-cell dysfunction, which result from long-term stimuli of hyperglycemia, are the basic features of DM. TGF-β pathway plays a permissive role in β-cell apoptosis, a potential cause of β-cell failure [39, 40]. Apart from up-regulation of TGF-β ligand, a higher level of serum and urine TGF-β1 in T2D condition is also observed [38]. The in vitro experiment has shown TGF-β as a potent initiator of ADM, suggesting that the upregulation of TGF-β ligand due to T2D may enhance its capacity to induce ADM [36]. Additionally, TGF-β ligands are commonly observed being overexpressed in PC [34] and the expression of TGF-β also upregulate in the condition of chronic pancreatitis [36]. These findings strongly indicate the tumor-promote effect of TGF-β, especially its role of the initiation of ADM. However, in the condition of T1D, which is caused by specific autoimmune attack against β cells, the up-regulation of TGF-β ligand can protect β-cell from autoimmune damage [40, 41], consistent with the immune-suppressive effect of TGF-β1 as reported [37, 42]. The preventive effect is believed via the regulation of T cells activation, as confirmed by using TGF-β plasmid injection, which successfully delayed the onset of diabetes in non-obesity diabetic (NOD) mice [41].

As the downstream signaling molecule, SMAD4 is recognized as a tumor-suppressing factor, and various complications of DM are related to SMAD4 pathway, including multi-organ fibrosis, diabetic nephropathy, etc [35], suggesting an upregulation of SMAD4 in T2D. Interestingly, a GWAS suggests the products of SMAD4 as a potential contributor to T2D-relevant islet dysfunction [43]. Notably, recent research demonstrated the function of SMAD4 in the formation of ADM, implying that T2D can, to some extent, induce ADM [6]. By using 2 independent shRNAs to knock down SMAD4, the progression of ADM can be significantly prevented in human acinar cells [36]. A study also reveals that SMAD4 can co-activate with YAP1 in the Hippo pathway inducing the initiation of ADM. In the same research, the co-binding of YAP1 and SMAD4 on the promoters of ADM-associated genes, including ID1, HES1, and WNT7A and B, has been reported [44–46]. However, the loss of SMAD4 is one of the most common mutation in PC patients, approximately 90% of the PC cases having the loss of heterozygosity of the SMAD4 locus [9, 10, 14]. In addition, this alteration is one of the last steps of PC carcinogenesis, occurring after the loss of CDKN2A in the high-grade PanIN [7, 14, 34], and the reintroduction of SMAD4 in SMAD4 deficient tumor cells can induce the apoptosis [14], indicating that SMAD4 exhibits distinct tumor-associated effects at different stages of carcinogenesis (Fig. 3). However, in T1D condition, the deficiency of SMAD4, especially in T cells can significantly promote the development of T1D and its autoimmune damage. SMAD4 plays a rather protective role in autoimmune diseases, including T1D and accompanied disease like Sjögren’s syndrome [47], suggesting T1D could have a different mechanism of ADM initiation.Fig. 3In the condition of T2D, the TGFR on the acinar cellular membrane and the serum TGF-β is increased compared with the normal blood glucose. CDKN2A/B

CDKN2A/B are genes encoding the inhibitor of cellular cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK), which is known for regulating the cell cycle. Consequently, CDKN2A/B exhibit significant tumor-suppressive effects. Loss-of-function mutations in CDKN2A/B are strongly associated with the development of various malignant cancers, including [10, 48]. Apart from the tumor-suppressive function, CDKN2A/B are also believed to be associated with the susceptibility of T2D [49]. A study of the Chinese population with T2D suggests that genes including CDKN2A/CDKN2B are associates with T2D [50]. By conducting GWAS, CDKN2A/B plays a promoting role in T2D by increasing expression of CDKN2A (p16INK4a/p19ARF) and CDKN2B (p15INK4b), which implicate the regeneration of pancreatic β cells and result in the apoptosis of β cells causing declined function of islet [51]. In addition to the islet-associated mechanism, the CDKN2A and CDKN2B also induce T2D through non-islet-associated mechanism, by altering glucose metabolism patterns in skeletal muscles, liver, and white adipose tissue [52]. These findings suggest that in the context of DM, the elevated levels of CDKN2A/B are negatively associated or even irrelevant to PC carcinogenesis and development.

Wnt

Wnt signaling acts as a growth stimulating factor, inducing proliferation of cells, regulating cell growth, and maintaining chromosomal stability [53, 54]. Wnt signaling comprises three main pathways: the canonical pathway, the planar cell polarity (PCP) pathway, and the Wnt/Ca2+ pathway [54–56]. In this part, the discussion is mainly focusing on canonical pathway (Wnt/β-catenin pathway). In canonical pathway, the combination of Wnt ligand and receptor initiates downstream signaling, ceasing ubiquitin-mediated proteolysis of β-catenin. The stable β-catenin diffuses into the nucleus interacting with transcription factor T-cell factor/lymphoid enhancer-binding factor (TCF/LEF) inducing corresponding genes transcription [54]. Since Wnt signaling is responsible for the regulation of cell proliferation, the intensive up-regulation of Wnt signaling is a feature for multiple types of cancers, such as colorectal cancer, uterine, and hepatocellular cancer [54, 57–59].

In DM, the Wnt signaling is highly implicated and its entire process is considered to be up-regulated in pancreas. Soluble Wnt signal molecule, the ligand for Wnt signaling, is also significantly up-regulated in pancreas of T2D patients [60–62] (Fig. 4). Robust expression of Wnt2b can be detected in β-cells, while is not detectable in normal pancreas [53, 63, 64]. In addition, TCF7L2, a member of TCF/LEF family, is considered as one of the most effective locus for DM prediction and the very first locus reported by genomic linkage studies and genome-wide association studies [65–67]. As reported, in patients with T2D, the concentration of TCF7L2 mRNA is five times higher than normal [65] (Fig. 4). β-catenin, which acts as the shuttle for nuclear entry in the canonical Wnt pathway, is not detectable in normal pancreatic tissues, but dramatically up-regulated in β-cells with T2D [68] (Fig. 4). The up-regulation of Wnt signaling is whole-process with up-regulation of each step. However, the up-regulation of Wnt signaling is restricted in β-cells, α cells are not influenced, suggesting the causal relation between DM and Wnt signaling is complex and indefinite. In PC, the activation of canonical Wnt signaling occurs at the early stage of PanIN, showing a certain degree of heterogeneity among the cell lines and PC tumors [58]. The consequences of Wnt signaling activation in KRAS-mutant contexts range from suppressive to promotive effects on the occurrence and development of PC and its precancerous lesions, depending on the timing, method, and intensity of activation [58, 59, 64]. This phenomenon is believed to be associated with the suppressive influence of the non-canonical Wnt pathway on the canonical Wnt pathway [57].

In addition, the immune suppressive effect of Wnt pathway creates a pro-cancerous TME. The expression of Wnt elements in tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) is confirmed [69]. In T cells, the expression of TCF7, which encodes TCF1 and is responsible for T-cell differentiation [70, 71] while being linked to a population of stem-like T cells [72, 73], suggests that in PC the canonical pathway has effects on TILs and influences TME [73]. The Wnt pathway in CD4 T cell also participates in the immune evasion of PC. Exhausted CD4 + T cells with reduced TCF1 expression induces the expression of PD-L1 in tumor cells, indicating the interplay between T cell and tumor cells via Wnt pathway [69]. This finding also suggests the potential of combining Wnt inhibitors with immunotherapy to enhance the efficacy of PD-L1 blockade.

Although Wnt/β-catenin pathway in maintaining PC is confirmed to be crucial, the role of β-catenin in initiation of PC seems to be vague. The suppression of canonical Wnt signaling can inhibit ADM and the transformation to PanIN [58]. However, the concentration of β-catenin is not the decisive factor for ADM initiation, as the β-catenin accumulation persisted in mice without tumor observed [74]. Therefore, Wnt signaling seems to participate in ADM and PanIN formation by acting as an intermediate link in the carcinogenesis process rather than as an upstream or downstream event.

Hedgehog (HH)

Hedgehog (HH) signaling is a highly conserved and crucial pathway that regulates embryonic development and maintains tissue patterning.. This pathway relies on a specialized structure called primary cilium, a microtube-based membrane protrusion commonly observed in most of cells, including cancer cells. The process of HH is similar to Wnt signaling. When HH signal is inactive, the receptor, a twelve-span transmembrane protein Patched (PTC), inhibits the transduction of Smoothened (SMO), a GPCR family protein, to the downstream factors. Conversely, when HH is active, HH-producing cells produce and secrete HH into humoral environment. The receptor on primary cilia, PTC, binds with HH suppressed its inhibition to SMO. SMO suppresses the production of GliR and promotes the activation transforming GliR to GliA, the active form of GliR. In PC, since HH signaling is proliferation-related, the over-activation or disorder of this signaling is considered as one of the potential factors for PC occurrence and development. Consistently, the induced expression of sonic hedgehog (SHH), a type of ligand for HH signaling, during the development can lead to PanIN-like lesions [78]. The ligand-dependent activation of HH signaling is believed to be the main activation mechanism in PC, which can be classified into autocrine and paracrine. The autocrine pathway is considered to be activated by the NF-κB, as SHH is one of the downstream genes of NF-κB signaling [79], which can be activated via AGE/RAGE pathway in DM. As for paracrine pathway, consistently, the NF-κB signaling can induce the infiltrated monocytes to secrete SHH into the TME [80] (Fig. 5). In addition, by inhibiting HH signaling in pancreatic stellate cells, a composition of PC stroma, the development of PC can be retarded [15]. Furthermore, the HH in myCAFs is also up-regulated comparing with iCAFs [81], mediating the formation of dense fibroblastic stroma for PC, thereby creating a physical barricade for PC to resist chemotherapy. However, despite of the mainstream consider HH signaling activation as a pro-cancerous factor, some of the researches indicate the potential dual function of HH signaling in PC, indicating that this remains a topic of controversy [82].

In DM, the HH signaling is considered to be involved in T2D instead of T1D, however, the activity of HH signaling in DM is uncertain. On one hand, the serum hedgehog-interacting protein (HHIP) in prediabetes and T2D patients is lifted, recognized as a risk factor for T2D patients [83]. HHIP is a negative regulator for HH signaling, blocking HH signaling in the fore-midgut region [84], since its structure allows it to competitively bind with HH signal molecules [85]. The negative correlation between HHIP and insulin is believed to be established via the promotion of Nox2 in β cells [86]. In addition, the HH signaling mediates insulin in the transcription level, and the descending of HH signaling induces the increasing inhibition of promoter of insulin [87]. On the other hand, the upregulation of O-GlcNAcylation in DM patients also influences HH signaling. The O-GlcNAcylation of Gli can enhance the activity of Gli, which in turn consistently augments the activity of HH signaling [88]. The effects of HH on DM and the effects of DM on HH are contradictory, striking a fragile balance. PC may represent one of the potential outcomes of this disequilibrium (Fig. 5).

Notch

Notch signaling is regarded as a highly conserved and powerful signaling pathway in human and other species. Unlike other pathways mediated by G protein-coupled receptors (GPCR), Notch signaling is featured with a rather simple and succinct architecture. The receptor of canonical Notch signaling undergoes straightly into nucleus after three cleavages [89–91]. Notably, the S2 cleavage of receptor is induced by physical interactions with ligands expressed on adjacent cells, suggesting the restrained effect range of Notch signaling [89]. The physiological functions of Notch signaling is mainly converged on embryonic development and organ formation [89], while the carcinogenic effect of Notch is considered to be vague or context-depending [90, 92]. The context-depending effect of Notch is likely because of the variety and unique downstream effects of each ligand-receptor pairs. In mammals, the receptors of Notch signaling are composed of 4 paralogs, NOTCH1, NOTCH2, NOTCH3, and NOTCH4, which exhibit both unique and overlapping downstream effects [89]. The ligands of Notch are also varied. The recognized ligands for Notch include delta-like ligand 1 (DLL1), delta-like ligand 3 (DLL3), delta-like ligand 4 (DLL4), Jagged-1 (JAG1), and Jagged-2 (JAG2) [89, 91].

In PC, Notch signaling is generally considered to be carcinogenic [90, 93–95]. By using siRNA to down-regulating Notch signaling, the PC cells with EMT phenotype can be partially reversed and decreased expression of NF-κB is observed [94] (Fig. 6). In addition, Notch signaling is believed to maintain the population of pancreatic cancer stem cells (pCSCs). In PC, Applying gamma secretase inhibitor or Hes1 shRNA in PC, the inhibition of Notch signaling through the application of gamma-secretase inhibitors or Hes1 shRNA leads to a reduced percentage of pCSCs and decreased tumorsphere formation. Conversely, activation of the Notch pathway results in the opposite effects [95, 96] (Fig. 6). The immune suppression is also one of the carcinogenic aspects of Notch. The tumor associated macrophage cells (TAMs) in the TME of PC express a higher level of Notch receptors, promoting immune suppressive polarization of macrophage cells [95] (Fig. 6). Notch signaling and its crosstalk with other pathways are also linked to drug resistance of PC patients, both in chemotherapy [94] and immunotherapy [95]. However, some researchers suggest the carcinogenesis of Notch signaling is controversial [92]. The role of Notch signaling in PC formation is complex, owing to the presence of multiple ligand-receptor pairs with unique yet partially overlapping functions, as well as the critical role of Notch signaling in pancreatic development [89, 90]. In DM, the Notch signaling is also a risk factor [91]. Notch signaling directs pancreas development via regulating β cells differentiation [97], and the impaired differentiation of β cells or mass loss are believed to lead to T2D [98]. Consistently, by depressing Notch activity, the promotion of pancreatic cytokeratin 5-positive cell differentiating into β cell can, to a great extent, ameliorate the blood glucose level disorder caused by acute pancreatitis, restoring β cells [97] (Fig. 6). In addition, the interplay between Notch signaling and FOXO is also a critical factor in mediating glucostasis [99–101]. Insulin resistance is a main reason for DM, and the haploinsufficiency of Notch or FOXO1 can enhance insulin sensitivity. Apart from the insulin sensitivity, the expression of Glucose-6-Phosphatase Catalytic Subunit 1 (G6PC1) can be induced by Notch via a FOXO1-dependent manner, increasing hepatic glucose production (HGP) [99] (Fig. 6). Furthermore, the upraised blood ketone in DM patients may be a promoting factor for Notch, as it has been observed that Notch signaling can be reinforced by β-hydroxybutyrate (βOHB) in small intestine [102] (Fig. 6). The AGE/RAGE axis also plays a role in activating Notch signaling in podocytes resulting in proteinuria [93] (Fig. 6). Thus, in DM, the Notch signaling is active due to the high glucose environment.

AMPK

AMPK, as a super intracellular energy sensor, is a highly conservative Ser/Thr kinase formed by subunits α, β, and γ [103–105]. It is believed to be closely involved in the metabolism-related alterations and diseases. Due to its function mechanism, the activity of AMPK pathway is tightly bounded with the ratio of concentration of AMP and ATP or ADP/ATP (Fig. 7). Once the ratio alters, the AMPK pathway will be activated followed by the downstream effect including up-regulating catabolism, downregulating anabolism, etc. [103, 104]. The suppression of AMPK pathway caused by the high level of blood glucose is believed to have a series of complex effects encountering the DM condition (Fig. 7). One of these effects is the downregulation of glycolysis, which results from decreased PFK function [104] and increased GFAT1 function [107, 108], redirecting the flow of F6P toward the HBP. The phosphorylation of PFK occurs in the context of DM is confirmed by observing the phenomenon of low activity of PFK in PC cell lines comparing with the other cell lines [109]. The phosphorylation of GFAT1 takes place at the Ser243, where identified phosphopeptides match the consensus sequence of AMPK [107, 110]. This indicates the important role of AMPK/GFAT pathway in regulating HBP (Fig. 7).

In addition, AMPK is confirmed to suppress TGF-β pathway, which is accounted for ADM initiation and further carcinogenesis. The downregulation of TGF-β pathway by AMPK is believed to occur through multiple mechanisms, including inhibiting TGF-β transcription by intercepting the positive feedback loop of TGF-β/SMAD3, suppressing the phosphorylation of SMAD2/3, and repressing the transcription of downstream proteins [111] (Fig. 7). Notably, some researches results on the suppression mechanism of AMPK to TGF-β/SMAD pathway seems to be inconsistent and contradictory [112]. Nevertheless, the negative relation between AMPK and TGF-β/SMAD is accordant in these researches despite of the differences of mechanisms. Thus, in the context of DM, the suppression of AMPK means the weakened depression of TGF-β pathway, which has been discussed before as an important initiator of ADM or Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) [36, 111].

AMPK/mTOR pathway is a key pathway to launch autophagy in the glucose-deprivation circumstance. AMPK promotes the autophagy through facilitating ULK1 by the phosphorylation of Ser317 and Ser777, while in nutrition abundant situation mTOR inhibit ULK1 through the phosphorylation of Ser757 intercepting the interplay between ULK1 and AMPK [113]. In the context of DM, the high-glucose circumstance enables mTOR to emerge as the leading force in coordinating the autophagy of pancreatic cells, creating an autophagy-suppression environment and subsequently leading to attenuated tumor suppression. By intercepting the autophagy through knocking out the ATG5, which is a hub gene in launching autophagy process, a consistent result is confirmed: the inhibition of autophagy and the increased occurrence of PanINs [113–116] (Fig. 7). Although inhibition of autophagy acts as a promoting factor for many types of tumors and for precancerous lesions, as for high-grade or matured PC, it serves as an inhibitory factor for proliferation and metastasis. This suggests an underlying a further alteration of AMPK/mTOR/ULK1 pathway or the existence of a bypass mechanism that initiates autophagy in high-grade PC cells.

The interplay between NF-κB and AMPK indicates functions of AMPK pathway in immunity especially in inflammation. Different investigations of AMPK and NF-κB have been conducted putting forward a series of axes, such as AMPK/CREB/SIRT1/NF-κB [117, 118] and AMPK/Nrf2/NF-κB [119] (Fig. 7). The negative relation between AMPK and NF-κB suggest the activation of NF-κB in DM condition, which is consistent with the complications of DM including multiple inflammations [120, 121] and diabetic neuralgia [121, 122]. Furthermore, NF-κB is a key regulator of TAMs [123], suggesting potential TME immunity regulating effect of AMPK.

IGF/IGFBP

The insulin-like growth factor (IGF) is a series of kinds of protein which resemble insulin in structures and function. The main function of IGF is to promote growth, since it involves in IGF/IGFR/PI3K/NF-κB signaling pathway and IGF/IGF1R/RAS/ERK signaling pathway. Insulin-like growth factor binding protein (IGFBP) belongs to the IGF system is considered to be related to carcinogenesis and metastasis [124, 125]. Given that the IGFBP3 is the main binding protein for IGF1, interestingly, the high level of IGF1 and concurrently low level of IGFBP3 is related to the increased risk of PC [124]. However, the role of IGF/IGFBP signaling in the PC formation of DM patients is rather complicated and controversial, as multiple researches suggest the irrelevant correlation between IGF/IGFBP and PC risk in DM patients [126].

In PC, the tumor associated IGF is mainly the tumor and stroma-derived IGF, due to the large scale of dense fibroblastic stroma salient as the feature of PC. The stromal cells, such as CAF and M2 TAM, is the main source of tumor-derived IGF [125, 127] (Fig. 8). These stromal cells also secrete other proteins factors, including matrix metalloproteases (MMP), cleaving IGFBP in the TME boosting the performance of IGF [125] (Fig. 8). The receptor for IGF1 (IGF1R) is overexpressed in PC and related to chemoresistance and tumor growth [127, 128] (Fig. 8). The aforementioned factors create an up-regulated condition for IGF. With activated IGF stimulation, the downstream PI3K/Akt cascade signaling in KRAS-mutated cells is activated, whereas this signaling remains inactive in cells without IGF stimulation [129]. The crosstalk between IGF and other pathways is also a carcinogenic aspect. The activation of IGF1 can induce the down-regulation of p53 in DNA-damaged cells via p38/MAPK-induced MDM2-dependent degradation [130], while the mutated p53 removing the inhibition of IGF1R [131] (Fig. 8). The IGF1R knockdown depresses the PI3K/Akt and NF-κB signaling pathway, suppressing tumor growth and chemoresistance [129, 132]. The activation of RAGE by increased AGEs in TME can induce the transcription of IGF1. Increased phosphorylation of IGF-1R has been demonstrated after activation of the AGE/RAGE axis, thereby amplifying IGF signaling, while the IGF1 can up-regulate the transcription of RAGE ligands such as S100A7 [133] (Fig. 8). IGFBP can bind with IGF, but the binding effect on IGF is disputable. On one hand, the binding can affect the combination between IGF and receptor depressing IGF effects [134]. On the other hand, the binding is believed to stabilize and transfer IGF [126]. However, apart from binding with IGF, IGFBP can have IGF-independent effects, including physical interaction with Wnt receptors, and acting as a functional ligand for TGF-β receptor V [125], indicating a complex interplay between IGFBP and other pathways in IGF-independent condition.

In DM, the IGF and IGFBP are also involved, however, the data explaining the relation between IGF/IGFBP and DM is usually contradictory [126, 134, 135]. Despite the contradiction, abundant researches indicating the free IGF-I and IGFBP-3 concentrations levels are increased in T2D [126]. Although IGF is considered to be a protective factor in DM, regulating glucose consumption and relating to insulin sensitivity, IGF inhibits insulin secretion, and in DM patients seems to remain at the normal level or rise up [126, 134, 135]. In addition, the high level of AGEs in DM can significantly activate the RAGE increasing phosphorylation of IGF-1R, and transcription of IGF-1, furthermore enhancing IGF signaling [133] (Fig. 8).

In conclusion, the role of IGF in DM is vague, as there is the contradictory data of IGF and IGFBP in T2D patients, warranting a thorough investigation, but the character for IGF/IGFBP in PC is rather certain. The interplay of IGF/IGFBP and other pathway in PC, making it engaging in tumor growth and drug resistance. In addition, IGF/IGFBP acts as a bond between tumor and tumor associated stroma cells, including CAFs and TAMs, bridging parenchyma and stroma to creates a pro-cancerous TME.

PI3K/Akt

The phosphatidylinositol 3' -kinase (PI3K)/Akt pathway can react to multiple stimulation or lesions. A series of lipid kinases compose the PI3K family, classified into three distinct classes, class Ⅰ, Ⅱ, and Ⅲ. Although the function of class Ⅱ and Ⅲ PI3K is less-investigated, the class Ⅰ P3IK is related to cancer progression. In the PI3K pathway, PI3K catalyzes the addition of a phosphate group to phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate (PIP2), generating phosphatidylinositol 3,4,5-trisphosphate (PIP3). PIP3 serves as an indispensable second messenger transferring upstream signals by binding with proteins with pleckstrin homology (PH) domain, including Akt. Akt, also known as protein kinase B (PKB), is the main downstream kinase of PI3K, classified into three different isoforms, Akt1, AKt2, and Akt3. Each isoform contains a PH domain at the N-terminal side responsible for binding with PIP3 [136–138]. PI3K/Akt pathway is considered to account for multiple cancers including PC, due to its functions in cell cycle [138].

In PC, PI3K/Akt pathway is considered to be up-regulated and related to multiple pathways including Wnt signaling [64], KRAS pathway [28, 139, 140], etc.. The disorder of PI3K/Akt pathway in PC can be accounted for 3 reasons, and each disorder lead respectively to a corresponding tumorgenic effect. First, the mutated-KRAS in PC can affect PI3K activity. As the feature of PC gene landscape, the mutation of KRAS is commonly observed in PC patients [14]. Given that PI3K is one of the downstream kinases of KRAS, the mutated KRAS can lead to the disorder of PI3K/Akt pathway. Consistently, the catalytic subunit deletion of PI3K can block up the carcinogenesis progression of mutated KRAS, and notably, it is insurmountable [139]. Since the activation of PI3K/Akt pathway can promote the translocation of GLUT1 to the membrane [136], this can also explain the increased GLUT1 on KRAS mutant cells membranes, and the growth advantage followed by (Fig. 9). Second, the ROS can amplify PI3K/Akt cascade by both activate Akt directly and inhibit the phosphatase and tensin homolog (PTEN) [141, 142] (Fig. 9). The PTEN is a negative regulator for PI3K by removing the phosphate group from PIP3. In addition, the activation of PI3K/Akt pathway can also elevate the ROS level via activating NADPH oxidase [142, 143], forming a circuit and leading to the redox disorder in PC (Fig. 9). Third, the Akt signaling in p53 mutant mice is enhanced compared with normal [144] (Fig. 9), while TP53 mutation is one of the four major mutations of PC. In addition, the over-activated Akt can inhibit glycogen synthase kinase 3 (GSK3) by phosphorylation on GSK3 NH2-terminus, permitting β-catenin to shuttle into nucleus, as the active form of GSK3 can phosphorylate β-catenin followed by proteasome degradation [136] (Fig. 9). In DM, since the PI3K/Akt pathway is one the downstream pathways of insulin, the high level of insulin in DM patients can activate PI3K/Akt via insulin receptor substance (IRS). Crosstalk between IRS/PI3K/Akt, and Wnt/β-catenin and HH is also involved in the progression of DM. PI3K/Akt increases the stability of Gli-1 in the HH pathway, increasing the HH signaling in promoting tumorgenesis [64]. In addition, the high glucose levels might also induce the activation of PI3K/Akt pathway. A study has confirmed the promotion of high glucose on PI3K/Akt pathway in lung cancer, leading to increased expression of metastasis-associated proteins [145]. However, the effect on PC in the context of DM warrant further thorough investigation. The O-GlcNAcylation in DM is also related to PI3K activation, as the PIP3 can combine with O-Linked N-Acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc) Transferase (OGT), and recruit OGT in the nucleus, forming a PIP3-OGT-Pdx1 complex up-regulating Pdx-1 [146], which is considered to be closely related to PC occurrence [24] (Fig. 9). The recruitment of OGT by PIP3 can be a possible explanation for the high level of O-GlcNAcylation in DM and furthermore, in PC. The high level of AGEs in DM can also influence PI3K/Akt pathway, since PI3K is a part of the RAGE downstream network. RAGE can activate PI3K/Akt pathway (Fig. 9), leading to enhanced protein synthesis and limits apoptosis and also mediating hypoxia inducible factor 1 subunit α (HIF1α) in order to encounter the hypoxic TME in PC [147].

The PI3K/Akt pathway is also related to multiple drug resistance (MDR) in cancers. PI3K/Akt pathway can induce effective expression of various ABC superfamily transporters in cancers to promote the xenobiotic efflux leading to MDR [148, 149]. As a previous study has demonstrated, following the photoactivation of the modified PI3K, proteome analyses demonstrate that the antiapoptotic factor tumor necrosis factor alpha-induced protein 8 (TNFAIP8) expression is up-regulated mediated by protein translation from pre-existing mRNA. This mechanism enables cancer cells to escape death induced by alkylating agents [150]. (Fig. 9). Thus, the future chemotherapy can take inhibition of PI3K/Akt pathway into account while applying drugs like alkylating drugs to PC patients, in order to weaken the MDR caused by PI3K/Akt activation.

Metabolism

Carbonyl stress

Advanced glycation end products (AGEs) are a complex series of heterogeneous chemicals formed generally from the enzyme-free condensation between carbonyl groups of reductive sugars and free amine groups of nucleic acids, proteins, or lipids [151]. In the context of DM, AGEs are usually formed through the Maillard reaction and accelerated by the high-glucose condition. This enzyme-free intracellular or extracellular reaction generates the reactive carbonyl species (RCS), the precursors of AGEs including methylglyoxal (MGO), and others, followed by further rearrangements yielding stable end products [152–155].

One of the main features of cancers is the Warburg effect, which is also reflected in PC. The GLUT1 overexpression is correlated with the higher tier of grading of PC and PanINs [4, 7]. The higher intracellular concentration of glucose triggers higher yield of MGO, which is a byproduct of glycolysis process. Unlike the stable end products, MGO is active and believed to be involved in many signaling pathway. In breast cancer, MGO is observed to induce the higher expression of YAP [152]. Mechanistically, MGO induces the post-translation modification of hot shock protein 90 (Hsp90) affecting its binding activity to the large tumor suppressor 1 (LATS1). In the Hippo signaling pathway LATS1 is a key kinase involving the phosphorylation-mediated inactivation of Yes-associated protein (YAP) [156]. Thus, the high MGO stimulation is consistent with the high activity of YAP (Fig. 10). YAP as the key downstream molecule of KRAS signaling [44] and TGF-β/SMAD signaling [30] mediates tumor growth and invasion in Hippo signaling pathway, which is consistent with the in vivo experiments [46].Fig. 10A basic relation graph demonstrating a part of possible connection between DM and PC. Apart from the RCS, AGEs also contribute to the carcinogenesis. Although the chemical property of AGEs is rather stable and inertial [151], the role of AGEs in the carcinogenesis is considerably important. Evidences have shown that AGEs are concerned with various types of cancers including PC [151, 154, 157]. AGEs function through the combination of the corresponding receptor (RAGE) on the cell membrane. RAGE is a member of the immunoglobulin superfamily of cell surface molecules capable of interacting with a broad spectrum of ligands, including advanced glycation endproducts (AGEs) [151, 158]. The expression of RAGE on the PC cell membrane is positively related to the occurrence of PanIN. Furthermore, it has been confirmed that overexpression of RAGE can promote the development of PC [159]. By treating with antagonistic peptide of RAGE or increasing the level of soluble RAGE (sRAGE), the level of free AGEs can be cut down, inhibiting the AGE/RAGE interplay and the proinflammation cascade signaling [152, 160]. One of the most important downstream pathway of RAGE is the NF-κB pathway involving in chronic inflammation and the interplay with KRAS mutant [161, 162]. In turn, NF-κB pathway activated by RAGE can also upregulate the RAGE level forming a positive feedback since the promoter of RAGE contains functional binding elements for NF-κB [158]. In addition, in the hypoxic TME of PC, the RAGE expression can also increase via NF-κB-dependent and HIF1α-independent pathway [147]. The wide downstream effects of NF-κB suggest the functions of AGEs in proliferation, carcinogenesis, metastasis [163, 164].

The reactive oxygen species (ROS) caused by AGE/RAGE interplay is also an important aspect of PC occurrence in the context of DM. The activation of NADPH oxidase as a downstream event of RAGE is believed to cause the generation of reactive oxygen intermediates (ROI) and furthermore the accumulation of ROS [165] (Fig. 10). In addition, An in vitro experiment on human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) reveals the dose-depend relation between AGE and circulating endothelial microparticles (EMPs), which is believed to be released through increasing NOX4/ROS oxidative stress [154].

Hexosamine biosynthetic pathway (HBP)

The disorder of metabolism is the most salient feature of DM, acting as both the cause and consequence of DM forming a lot of circuits. In the condition of DM, the high glucose is the most significant property comparing with the healthy internal environment, forcing tissues and cells to convert their metabolism pattern, especially saccharometabolism. Among these alterations, the up-regulation of hexosamine biosynthetic pathway (HBP) preferentially takes place in pancreas and plays an important role in inducing gene mutation. A research shows that high level expression of GFAT1, the first rate-limiting enzyme of HBP, indicates a poor clinical outcome of PC patients. High GFAT1 expression was significantly associated with poor overall survival in patients with resected PC. The multivariable-adjusted hazard ratio (HR) for mortality when comparing patients with high and low GFAT1 expression was 2.54 [166].

In the process of saccharometabolism, the glucose will chronologically transform into glucose 6-phosphate (G6P) and fructose-6-phosphate (F6P). Then, the pathway ramifies into the ordinary glycolysis pathway and the HBP. In the HBP, F6P accepts amide from glutamine with the catalysis of glutamine: fructose-6-phosphate amidotransferase (GFAT) transforming to glucosamine-6-P. Then, the GNPNAT1 catalyzes glucosamine-6-P receiving the acetyl from acetyl-CoA and producing GlcNAc-6P. After producing GlcNAc-1P by shifting the phosphate by PGM3, GlcNAc-1P receives the UMP from UTP which participates in the nucleotide metabolism, transforming into UDP-GlcNAc [110]. UDP-GlcNAc is found to be a necessary ingredient of O-GlcNAcylation (O-linked β-N-acetylglucosamine) of intracellular enzymesm, involving in plenty of pathways including the insulin resistance [30, 167–169] (Fig. 2).

The preference of HBP in pancreatic cells in the context of DM can be explained by the downregulation of phosphofructokinase (PFK) activity. To a certain degree, the activity of PFK plays a decisive role in distributing F6P to continuing glucolysis or entering HBP. In the condition of DM, as the down stream enzyme of AMPK pathway, the activity of PFK is suppressed due to the downregulation of AMPK in pancreatic cells [103]. Thus, a greater amount of F6P, which serves as a substrate of both HBP and glucolysis, enters the HBP causing the high-level GlcNAcylation of enzymes, such as RNR [30, 32], saccharometabolism associated enzymes, and insulin resistance associated proteins [88, 168, 170], inducing the reciprocally downstream effects. Consistently, the overexpression of PFK in cells frustrates the high glucose impact.

Apart from the de novo pathway, HBP also has a salvage synthetic pathway by using glucosamine (GlcN) or N-acetylglucosamine (GlcNAc) in the environment. However, the preferential tissue or cell and the mechanism of the regulation of the salvage pathway are still remaining unknown [110].

The carcinogensis of up-regulation of HBP mainly focusing on the disturbance of dNTP pool. In the condition of up-regulation of HBP, the GlcNAcylation of enzymes including RNR is at a rather high level. RNR is a tetrameric complex, composed by two types of subunits, RRM1 and RRM2. RNR functions as a key enzyme to poise dNTP pool by striking a balance between the supplies and expenditures of dNTPs. The GlcNAcylation of RNR occurs on the T734 of RRM1 subunit interrupting the formation of functional RNR complex causing the disturbance of dNTP pool, and increasing DNA damage and genomic instability [30, 32, 33]. The altered activity of RNR is one of the main reasons for multiple types of cancers. RRM1 overexpression ranks for top 10% of overexpressed genes in 73 of 168 cancer analyses, and RRM2 overexpression ranks for the top 10% in 30 of 170 analyses that involved multiple types of cancer, suggesting the core role of RNR in pancreatic carcinogenesis [32, 33] (Fig. 2).

Diabetic obesity

The obesity is a main metabolic context for DM, especially T2D, patients. The deposition of free fatty acid (FFA) in non-adipose tissue, such as pancreas, and the abnormally low level of adiponectin (Apn) are believed to be two of the reasons for the insulin resistance and T2D [171, 172]. The accumulation of visceral fat tissue and inflammatory environment caused by obesity make the level of Apn remaining low in T2D [172, 173]. Adiponectin functions as an insulin-sensitizing hormone by regulating key pathways such as AMPK, PPARα, and MAPK, which are crucial for cells growth and metabolism. Thus, the low level of Apn in T2D can be partly accounted for the insulin-resistance in T2D.

In PC, Apn is considered to be an anti-tumoral factor, since it is structural homology to TNF-α, and negatively related to obesity, which is considered to be a pro-factor [173]. Apn can activate caspases, promote apoptosis and suppress the proliferation of PC. In addition, by down-regulating the level of TCF7L2 and facilitating the degradation of β-catenin, Apn can restrain the Wnt signaling pathway [174], whose relation to PC has been discussed in the previous text. In addition, the regulative function of Apn on AMPK can redress the metabolic alteration and alleviate the chronic inflammation [173]. Thus, the low level of Apn, and the inflammatory environment of T2D can be a potential relation between DM and PC, indicating a possible application of Apn in adjuvant therapy to DM-PC patients.

Endocrinology

Hyperinsulinemia

Hyperinsulinemia, the high level of blood insulin, is confirmed by multiple studies to be a potential pro-cancerous factor [132, 175–181]. Due to its promotion effect on metabolism, cell growth, and proliferation, the hyperinsulinemia is the inducer of the over-activation of insulin-associated pathway and furthermore the tumorgenesis. Since the pancreatic β cell is the only source of endogenic insulin, the concentration of insulin in is approximately between 50 and 300 pM [182],roughly two times higher than what is found in the circulation, making the effect of pancreatic local insulin is more intensive than that in non-pancreatic tissue. Given the differences in pathogenesis between T1D and T2D, T2D will be the main discussing object in this section.

In T2D, the hyperinsulinemia can be the main manifestation of the pre-T2D or the early stage of T2D along with the increased mass of β cells, considered to be the compensation for insulin resistance. The consequence of the hyperinsulinemia is the further insulin resistance and the followed further hyperinsulinemia, forming a vicious circle, and leading to worse blood glucose disorder [181].

The high level of insulin is considered to be related to the progression of PC. There lies a close correlation between PC and hyperinsulinemia in epidemiology [179–181]. Furthermore, the carcinogenesis effect of hyperinsulinemia is independent of hyperglycemia [183]. First of all, the over stimulated insulin-related pathways have an intensive carcinogenesis effect. The insulin/IR/IRS can initiate PI3K/Akt signaling, and the activated PI3K/Akt can promote the expression of polo like kinase 1 (PLK1), a cell cycle regulated protein kinase. This enhances the capability of proliferation and metastasis of PC [177]. Consistently, the decreased insulin is followed by down-regulation of PI3K/Akt pathway. The Insulin/PI3K/Akt also up-regulate the expression of mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) receptor, regulating glycometabolism of PC [184].

Through factors, including neuropeptide Y, the insulin can increase the proliferation of adipocytes [177], and induce the chronic pancreatitis via the inflammatory factors secreted by insulin-induce pancreatic fat tissue [179], eventually resulting in mutation and PC [175, 180]. In addition, the immune environment of PC might be regulated by the insulin in the TME. The reduced insulin production is related to the increased amount of B cells [176], the major immune cell population in PC TME [185]. Consistently, there are several reports on insulin affecting B cells proliferation and development [186].

The insulin in the TME also assists the formation of PC characterized TME participating in the crosstalk between cancer cells and stromal cells. The large portion of fibrous connective tissue in the solid tumor is a main feature of PC [15, 187], forming a physical barricade for tumor cells to resist chemotherapy. The PSC is the main population of stromal cells in PC TME, participating in forming the extracellular matrix (ECM) [15]. It is believed that PSCs can respond to the stimulation of hyperinsulinemia [176, 188], given that pancreatic fibrosis is often occurred in advanced T2D [189]. The insulin can induce the phosphorylation of IR and activate the Akt/mTOR pathway increasing the secretion and deposition of collagen in the ECM [176].

In addition, the insulin can also induce the osteopontin (OPN) or secreted phosphoprotein 1 (SPP1) expression in the level of transcription [190]. The SPP1 is considered to be related to multiple solid tumor including PC [191–193]. Then, the long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) can regulate the SPP1 promoting tumor metastasis and EMT [194].

Immunity

Immune dysfunction

The diabetes-induced immune dysfunction is considered as the main reason for diabetic complications including rheumatoid arthritis (RA) [195], retinopathy [196], diabetic foot ulcers [122], and the general susceptibility to infections [197, 198]. However, compared with the complete collapse of immune system in HIV, diabetes-induced immune dysfunction demonstrates a immune suppressive environment but with multiple low-intensity or chronic inflammation giving a pro-cancer condition [199–201].

In DM, the chronic sub-clinical inflammation has been reported [120, 202] as one of the main features for DM patients. In DM, the chronic inflammation is mainly induced through ligands/RAGE axis and ROS.

RAGE is a widely expressed receptor, which can be observed on macrophage cell (MΦ), lymphocyte B and T, and dendritic cells (DM) [158, 162], suggesting an irreplaceable function in immune regulation. Notably, RAGE expression is rather low in normal tissues except for lungs especially alveolar epithelial type 1 cell. However, the expression of RAGE can be significantly increased once there are ligands generation and accumulation [162]. The species of ligand of RAGE are not restricted but rather various, including AGEs, S100/calgranulins, high mobility group-box 1 (HMGB1) [162, 203], etc.

The increased blood glucose in DM patients generates and accumulates abundant endogenous AGEs, stimulating the RAGE pathway of various immunocytes, and causing a series of downstream effects. The increased AGEs and the activation of oxidase NADPH induce the accumulation of ROS leading to oxidative stress and inflammation [165].

The AGE/RAGE axis delays the recruitment of immunytes, such as MΦ and neurophil [158, 162, 203], and induces the disorder of MΦ polarization [204], leading to immunocyte dysfunction in the early stage of lesions [205]. This disruption prevents the transition of the inflammatory phase from a pro-inflammatory state to tissue restoration, which is believed to contribute to delayed wound healing in DM patients.. The transmigration of neutrophil is suppressed by the high glucose and high AGE [206]. In addition to the myeloid cells, the lymphoid cells are also influenced by the AGE/RAGE axis. T cell activation is at least partly influenced by AGE/RAGE axis, through the signaling except the phosphorylation of CD3ξ [207]. Moreover, the activation of B cells and secretion of antibody can be ameliorated by treating with metformin [208], an AMPK agonist, suggesting diabetes has impacts on B cell function.

M1/M2 polarization

The polarization of MΦ largely determines the immune environment of PC. MΦ can response to multiple kinds of stimulations including cytokines, followed by polarization into distinct types. These types of MΦ can be roughly classified into M1 and M2, according to their cytokines receptors and their immune function. M1 is generally a pro-inflammatory MΦ type featured by the ability to efficiently present antigens, production of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin 12 and 23 (IL-12 and IL-23), and the capability to enhance T cells and NK cells immune ability. M2 is generally an anti-inflammatory and pro-cancerous MΦ type promoting tumor growth, proliferation and metastasis [209–211].

In the context of PC, the immune environment is suppressed classified into the type of “cold tumor”, indicating the imbalanced polarization of MΦ. A latest research reveals the communication of between tumor cells and immune environment via the high level of HBP in MΦ. The high level of O-GlcNAcylation caused by the over expression of glutamine-fructose-6-phosphate transaminase 2 (GFPT2) can promote the secretion of interleukin 18 (IL-18). The high level of O-GlcNAcylation of Y-box binding protein 1 (YBX1) can induce its nuclear translocation [212], which is considered to be related to the occurrence of multiple cancers including PC [213–215].

B cells

Although the PC is classified as a “cold tumor”, demonstrating an immune suppressive environment, there is a high level of immune cells infiltration. B cell is a major population in the immune environment of PC [185, 216]. The infiltration of B cells in PC is considered to be a dual factor.

On one hand, the depletion of B cells in PC can reduce the development of PanIN and the progression of PC tumor, and the up-regulation of CXCL13 and CCL21 is related to the acceleration of PC [216, 217]. On the other hand, the B cells can participate in the immune response to PC along with dendritic cells (DC) and T cells, forming a tertiary lymphoid structure (TLS) [185, 217]. By acting as an antigen presentation cell (APC), B cells can enhance the performance of T cells [218]. Despite of the context-dependent function of infiltration of B cells, the important role of B cells in PC immunity and formation of TME is doubtless.

In the context of DM, B cells are also highly involved. As a type of immune cell, B cell dysfunction is one of the primary reasons for autoimmunity, T1D in this context. In addition, B cells also participate in the impaired glucose tolerance and development of T2D. The specific ablation of B cells in adipose tissue can restore the metabolic homeostasis, highlighting the relationship between metabolism and B cells [218]. Comparisons between wild-type models and those lacking B cells indicate that insulin resistance is at least partially mediated by B cells. In addition, the metformin-induced activation of AMPK can activate B cells and enhance its antibody secretion [208].

Microbiota

Multiple researches have investigated the tumorogenesis effect of microbiome in various cancers, and the “complicit” microbes can promote tumor growth via influencing the immune environment in TME [219–224]. While in the context of DM, the microbiome is also a key factor for disease occurrence and progression, both in T1D and T2D, through several pathways including metabolite pathway, immunologic pathway, and neuroendocrine pathway [225–228]. Given that the pancreas is part of the digestive system and its anatomical structure allows the gut microbiome to access the pancreas and PC solid tumors, the gut microbiome plays a significant role in both DM and PC. The microbiome in pancreas can be a possible underlying connection between DM and PC formation.

The microbiome in pancreatic tumor is significantly more than that in normal pancreas, and has a differentiated microbiome landscape compared with gut microbiome [220–222]. The main phyla in PC tumor are Proteobacteria, Bacteroidetes, and Firmicutes, the most mentioned phyla in the case–control comparison [221, 223]. The effects of microbiome in PC formation and progression are complex, nevertheless the immune system-microbe-tumor axis is generally the main focus of the researches [220, 222]. The ablation of the microbiome can induce the reshaping of the immune environment of PC, down-regulating the suppressive immune cells, and concurrently up-regulating promotive immune cells and PD-1 expression. This indicates that the innate and adaptive immunity can be influenced by the distinct phyla in PC [222]. In addition, the resistance against gemcitabine, a cytosine nucleotide derivative commonly used in chemotherapy, can be caused by Gammaproteobacteria in PC tumor, depending on the expression of a long isoform of the bacterial enzyme cytidine deaminase (CDDL), which can metabolize gemcitabine transforming it into a non-toxicity form [229, 230].

In the context of DM, the alteration of gut microbiome composition profile is also a factor for disease occurrence and development. Researches have investigated the alteration of gut microbiome in T2D patients, and notably the phyla involved are consistent with the the main phyla in PC tumor. The Firmicutes is significantly elevated compared with control group, and the ratio of Bacteroidetes to Firmicutes is positively correlated with the impaired glucose tolerance. However, instead of Gammaproteobacteria, Betaproteobacteria is augmented in T2D, positively correlating with plasma glucose. The unbalanced Bacteroidetes/Firmicutes ratio can increase the permeability intestine, permitting exchanges of byproducts, and thus enhancing the contact between gut microbiome and pancreas [225, 226, 228]. Interestingly, the higher Bacteroidetes to Firmicutes ratio is also observed in PC patients [229]. The T1D patients also have a distinct microbiome landscape with a higher level of Bacteroidetes [224].

The generally consistent microbiome alteration of DM and PC indicates the possible role of microbiome in DM induced PC. However, the conclusion should be circumspect, given the unclear causal order between the disease and the associated alteration. Nevertheless, the research on the role of microbiome in DM and PC can provide insights for future studies on the immune system-microbe-tumor axis and potential treatment combinations, such as the adjunctive use of antibiotics in gemcitabine therapy. Additionally, the virome and the fungal microbiome of DM and PC are the potential connections between DM and PC, but less-investigated, warranting thorough researches.

TME

Acidity

The acidity is a potential link between PC and DM. On one hand, the acidity of TME caused by the altered metabolism can suppress the immunity and reduce the efficacy of immune therapy [231]. On the other hand, DM can also induce the acidosis via the metabolism of ketone body, and also the malfunction of mitochondrial leads to increased lactic acid production [232]. Thus, the common result, acidification of micro-environment, can be the relation between DM and PC.

The acidity in the environment is a pro-factor for PC in the very early stage of the disease. The acidity can affect the stability of genome and cause the occurrence of cancer from the aspect of cancer evolution [233, 234]. The selective pressure of low pH in the environment pushes genomes to adapt, inducing genomics alterations. The suppression of apoptosis can effectively accumulate the alterations and pile up these alterations, ultimately inducing the uncontrollable cellular proliferation [234]. However, the mechanism of sensing environment pH and impact of the environmental pH on intracellular events is unanswered and warrant further studies.

CAFs and PSC

PC is featured by its high-extent desmoplastic reaction. The CAFs are the main participants of the formation of fibrous connective tissues of PC, classified into three major populations, iCAF, myofibrotic CAF (myCAF), and antigen-presenting CAF (apCAF) [15, 16]. These CAFs can respond to multiple growth-associated factors in the TME, including TGF-β, SHH, IGF etc. [16, 81, 127, 187]. Thus, the imbalanced levels of various factors in DM can greatly affect the proliferation and the transformation of different CAF populations. In addition, the PSC, a main group of fibroblast, can promote the desmoplastic event via high-level paracrine factors, including TGF-β [15, 188].

TGF-β/SMAD2/3 is a key pathway for activation of myCAF and the differentiation of CAF into distinct subsets. In vitro, the modulation of TGF-β can induce the formation of mini-tumor model with heterogeneous archetypical CAF subsets [15, 16]. The TGF-β down-regulates the expression of IL-1R1, promoting fibroblast to transform to myCAF, as well as, suppressing the transformation to iCAF [15]. The myCAF is considered to be an accomplice, assisting metastasis of PC. The TGF-β-induced myCAF expresses the amphiregulin (AREG), leading to the autocrine EGFR/ERBB2 response, followed by increased metastasis [235].

PSC, on one hand, can influence CAF via paracrine pathway, and on the other hand, can be influenced by the high level of AGE in DM patients [188]. The increased RAGE is observed on the PSC of diet-induced obese mice with DM, and the activation of RAGE can induce PSC to transform into myofibroblast-like cells. The activated PSC also up-regulated the secretion of TGF-β, and other EMT-related factors, inducing the EMT of PC [176]. In addition, the PSC can also form a niche for cancer stem cell of PC by paracrine Nodal/Activin signaling [236], suggesting a novel therapy direction.

Techniques in DM and PC research and diagnosis

Advancement of techniques

With the advancement of relevant technology, the research and diagnosis techniques, including imaging mass cytometry, spatial transcriptomics, and machine learning, emerged in the field of DM and PC study and diagnosis.

In the studies of DM, the spatial multiomics have been used for demonstrating the atlas of the molecular pathological process of DM, including tissue transformation and metabolic reprogramming, providing more perspectives of DM and PC connections [237, 238]. Furthermore, artificial intelligence (AI) has made significant progress in DM diagnosis, particularly through the widespread application of generative adversarial networks (GANs). GANs can generate virtual patient data based on real-world patient datasets, representing rare yet critical risk factors in DM [239].

In the studies of PC, the applications of spatial omics and imaging mass cytometry reveal the precise subclusters of heterogeneous fibroblast and the immune subtypes of PC, rendering the method to analyze heterogeneity of PC tumor micro-environment, and illustrating the interaction networks in the PC and DM [240, 241]. In addition, the microbiomics used in PC and DM studies also provides another aspect to explain the PC susceptibility for DM patients, since the similar altered intestinal microbiomes exist in both PC and DM [227, 229, 242].

Technical limitations

Although the techniques in researching DM and PC in the level of molecules and cells have gain a profound progress, the limitation of techniques still exists, especially in the disease model of DM and PC.

Compared with diet-induced diabetes model, the streptozotocin (STZ) treatment is more practical and commonly used to create the disease model for DM, both T1D and T2D, by entering β cells through GLUT2 and causing selective destruction of β cells. Thus, the STZ can also cause damage to cells expressing GLUT2 in liver or kidney, leading to unexpected side-effect. In addition, the accompanied genotoxicity of STZ is another main limitation for STZ model. A single i.v. dose of STZ (50–100 mg/kg) can lead to DNA breakage in rats and eventually causing DNA adduct, chromosomal aberrations, micronuclei, sister chromatid exchange and cell death [243].

The rodent model for PC mainly used in research is genetically engineered mouse models (GEMM). By introducing the mutated oncogene drivers, GEMM can faithfully recapitulate the features of PC throughout the process, including early stage of PC, such as ADM and PanIN. However, there are a few limitations for GEMM. The limitation of spectrum of genetic alteration and the difference of introducing drivers between PC and model in the scale of time, as well as the inevitable off-target effects are the main problems faced by GEMM on the way to reflect faithfully PC in the rodent models [244].

Conclusion

The correlation of DM and PC is intricate and encompasses multiple facets of the human body's response to overnutrition and prolonged hyperglycemia. As illustrated in Fig. 10 and summarized in Table 1, the key driver genes and pathways associated with DM-related PC, along with their therapeutic potential, are presented. The energy sensors are the very first to encounter the brunt and adapting to it. The suppression of AMPK results in the deregulation of downstream pathways. Simultaneously, the alterations of metabolism, especially the glycometabolism, are the triggers to the instability of genome. Increased HBP activity and subsequent high-level O-GlcNAcylation of enzymes disrupt the dNTP pool balance. Perennial high level of glucose in the serum produces the AGEs interfering the normal function of the network downstream of RAGE. In the context of DM, the inordinate factors and abnormal level of hormones, both extracellular and intracellular, push the cell to the collapse. With DM-induced immune suppression and microbiome-mediated effects acting as potential accomplices, mutations in both major (“mountains”, such as KRAS, SMAD4, TP53, CDKN2A) and minor (“hills”) driver genes progressively lead to the inevitable development of PC.

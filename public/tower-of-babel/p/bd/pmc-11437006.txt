Psychometric properties of Polish version of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 in individuals with fibromyalgia
Authors: Agnieszka Sozańska, Bernard Sozański, Anna Łagowska-Sado, Anna Wilmowska-Pietruszyńska, Agnieszka Wiśniowska-Szurlej
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractFibromyalgia (FM) is chronic, widespread musculoskeletal pain and accompanying fatigue, sleep disturbances, cognitive, psychological, and somatic symptoms. The aim of the study is to assess the psychometric properties of the Polish version of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 in FM patients. This is a cross-sectional study involving 456 FM polish patients. The World Health Organization Disability Assessment Schedule 2.0 (WHODAS 2.0) 36-item version, the Fibromyalgia Impact Questionnaire (FIQ) and Beck’s Depression Inventory (BDI) were used as an assessment tool in the study. The internal consistency of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha. Values ranging from 0.824 to 0.951 were obtained. The interclass correlation coefficients (ICC) were very high. Internal structure of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 was checked with Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA). RMSEA = 0.069, CFI = 0.963, TLI = 0.96, SRMR = 0.081 proved exactness of original six-dimensional structure of WHODAS 2.0. External validity was assessed by correlating the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 scores with the scores of two previously validated tools: FIQ and BDI. Positive correlations were obtained between the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 and these tools. Based on the conducted research, it has been shown that the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 is a reliable and valid tool for assessing disability in individuals with FM in Poland.Keywords: Fibromyalgia, Disability, Psychometrics, Reproducibility of results, Surveys and questionnairesSubject terms: Diseases, Health care

Introduction

Fibromyalgia (FM) is characterized by chronic, widespread musculoskeletal pain and accompanying fatigue, sleep disturbances, and other cognitive, psychological, and somatic symptoms1. FM may be associated with other comorbid conditions2. In recent years, the pathogenesis of fibromyalgia has also been linked to other factors such as inflammatory, immunological, endocrinological, genetic, and psychosocial factors3.

It is estimated that FM affects 2–8% of the population worldwide, more commonly in women than men4,5. The diagnosis of FM is based almost exclusively on the clinical criteria of the American College of Rheumatology (ACR). The lack of objective markers of the disease is a constant problem in research on the diagnosis of FM, clinical management, and social recognition of this condition6. Patients with FM often do not receive appropriate treatment and disability services that are planned for other chronic diseases, despite triggering a significant social burden due to direct and indirect healthcare costs and loss of work productivity7. FM has a negative impact on many areas of a patient's life, causing functional disability and difficulties in performing daily activities5,8. The level of disability depends on complex interactions of personal, social, and environmental factors that have a significant impact on the patient's life9.

The International Association for the Study of Pain (IASP) classifies FM as a primary chronic pain10. Despite the fact that these guidelines recommend a biopsychosocial approach to the treatment of chronic pain, the assessment of FM remains clinical11,12.

Due to the fact that a patient's health status translates into their functioning in daily life, an increasing attention is being paid to the assessment of functioning. The level of functioning is an important indicator of the health of the population13. It determines the activity and participation of a person with a specific health condition in his/her living environment. Such a multidimensional approach allows us to determine the level of difficulties in functioning or the level of disability of the patient14.

The World Health Organization (WHO) has created the International Classification of Functioning, Disability, and Health (ICF) based on the biopsychosocial model14. The ICF provides internationally accepted conceptual frameworks for describing health status and collecting systematized data from individuals based on their lived experiences and their surrounding environment15,16.

The tool for assessing functioning and disability proposed by WHO, built on the biopsychosocial model and the ICF categories, is the World Health Organization Disability Assessment Schedule 2.0 (WHODAS 2.0). WHODAS 2.0 is a general instrument for evaluating functioning in six domains: cognitive functions, mobility, self-care, getting along, life activities, and participation. There are several versions of WHODAS 2.0—a full version containing 36 questions, a short version with 12 questions, and a hybrid version with 12 + 24 questions. The full version allows for generating results for all six domains of functioning17. Knowledge of the functioning profile of individuals with FM is necessary for better recognition and clinical management of this disease. In Poland, there is no widespread use of any questionnaires for assessing functioning and disability in FM patients. There has also been no assessment of the validity of the Polish version of WHODAS 2.0 in FM patients. What is more, there is also limited amount of studies carried out on this topic worldwide. Barreto et al.18 conducted an assessment of the validity of the 36-item version of WHODAS 2.0 in the Brazilian population in 2021. Additionally, in 2016, Smedema et al.19 published the results of an evaluation of the 12-item version of WHODAS 2.0 in FM patients in the United States.

The aim of the study is to assess the psychometric properties of the Polish version of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 in FM patients.

Materials and methods

Study design and participations

This is a cross-sectional study involving FM patients living in Poland. The study was performed from May to October 2023 in collaboration with the College of Medical Sciences of the University of Rzeszow, Lazarski University, and the FIBRO-MY, the National Association of Patients with Fibromyalgia. Individuals diagnosed with FM and affiliated with FIBRO-MY were invited to participate in the study. In order to assess the psychometric properties of WHODAS 2.0 v 36, 456 completed questionnaires were analysed. No data loss was observed in the study sample. The necessary sample size was determined based on recommendations by Anthoine et al.20.

Procedures

The study was conducted using an internet survey distributed among patients affiliated with FIBRO-MY. Before participating in the study, each person was informed about its purpose and gave an informed consent to participate.

The following inclusion criteria were adopted for the study: a clinical diagnosis of fibromyalgia according to the American College of Rheumatology (ACR) criteria, age 18 and older, and an informed consent to participate in the study.

The following exclusion criteria were adopted for the study: cognitive deficits, pregnancy, severe neurological disorders of the central nervous system (e.g. stroke, traumatic brain injury), an active neoplastic disease, amputations, recent orthopaedic injuries, other diseases diagnosed by a physician in an active phase of progression or treatment (e.g. inflammatory rheumatic diseases, acute sciatica), and lack of patient consent.

Ethics

In accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, all study participants were informed about the purpose and course of the study and gave an informed consent to participate. The study was approved by the Bioethics Committee of the University of Rzeszów (Resolution No. 2022/041).

Outcome measures

The World Health Organization Disability Assessment Schedule 2.0 (WHODAS 2.0) 36-item version was used as an assessment tool in the study. Additionally, the Fibromyalgia Impact Questionnaire (FIQ) and Beck's Depression Inventory (BDI) were also administered among the study participants. Furthermore, the following metric data were collected: age, gender, place of residence, education, marital status, occupational status, and type of work.

The World Health Organization Disability Assessment Schedule 2.0 (WHODAS 2.0)

According to WHO principles, the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 was translated and culturally adapted (including initial translation by two independent translators, synthesis of the translations, back translation, pre-final version compared to the original instrument, pilot test in general population, creation of final version) by the ICF Council within the Polish Health Information System, under the leadership of Prof. Anna Wilmowska-Pietruszyńska, based on an agreement with WHO21.

The 36-item WHODAS 2.0 is used to measure general disability and disability in six domains: Domain 1 Cognition (6 items), Domain 2 Mobility (5 items), Domain 3 Self-care (4 items), Domain 4 Getting along (5 items), Domain 5 Life activities (8 items), comprising two subdomains: 5.1 Domestic activities and 5.2 Work or school activities, and Domain 6 Participation (8 items). Responses to the questions are rated on a 5-point scale indicating the level of difficulty or problem (1 = none; 2 = mild; 3 = moderate; 4 = severe; 5 = extreme or unable to do). The obtained results are transformed on a scale from 0 to 10017. The psychometric properties of the Polish version of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 were examined in a cross-sectional study of older people in Poland22 and among patients with lower back pain23 and degenerative changes in knee and hip joints24.

The Fibromyalgia Impact Questionnaire (FIQ-Pol)

The Polish version of the FIQ (licensed from the Mapi Research Trust, order number 2117057) was used to assess the external validity of WHODAS 2.0. The good psychometric properties of the FIQ-Pol were confirmed in a study conducted by Ćwirlej-Sozańska et al.25.

The FIQ questionnaire assesses the impact of FM on patients’ functioning. It consists of 20 items examining 10 subscales (scoring dimensions): Physical Functioning, Well-being, Work related, Do work, Pain, Fatigue, Rested, Stiffness, Anxiety, Depression. Each domain is assessed according to the assigned scales, and then scored according to the conversion key provided in the user manual. After completing the initial scoring, the obtained results are converted to normalized scores ranging from 0 to 10, where 0 indicates no health impairment and 10 indicates maximum impairment. After normalization, all scores are summed to obtain a total score ranging from 0 to 100. Higher scores indicate a greater (negative) impact of the disease on the patient’s quality of life. The authors of the original version of FIQ also confirmed the good psychometric properties of the tool26.

Beck’s Depression Inventory (BDI)

BDI assesses affective, cognitive, somatic, and vegetative symptoms according to the criteria for major depression in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual (DSM-IV). It contains 21 items on a 4-point scale from 0 (no symptoms) to 3 (severe symptoms). The minimum score is 0, and the maximum score is 63. Higher scores indicate a greater severity of symptoms. In non-clinical populations, scores above 20 indicate depression27. In individuals diagnosed with depression, scores of 0–13 indicate minimal depression, 14–19 indicate mild depression, 20–28 indicate moderate depression, and 29–63 indicate severe depression28,29. A review of the psychometric properties of BDI confirmed its good psychometric properties as a self-report measure of depression in various environmental settings and populations30.

Statistical analysis

Qualitative variables were analysed by calculating the number and percentage of occurrences of each value, whereas quantitative variables were analysed by calculating the mean, standard deviation, median, and quartiles. The Chi-square test or Fisher's test (for qualitative variables) and the Mann–Whitney test (for quantitative variables due to the lack of normal distribution of these variables, as verified by the Shapiro–Wilk test) were used to compare the groups with and without retest. A significance level of 0.05 was adopted for the analysis.

The analysis was performed using R software version 4.2.2.31.

Analysis of the reliability

Internal consistency and temporal consistency

The analysis was carried out at the level of overall scores as well as at the level of individual domains of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0. The internal consistency was assessed using Cronbach's alpha coefficient, with a value above 0.7 considered internally consistent according to Nunnally's criterion32.

To assess the stability of the scale (repeatability), the test and retest results were compared using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC)33.

Internal structure

Confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) was used to study the internal structure proposed by the authors of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 questionnaire17. The following measures of fit were used: Root Mean Square Error of Approximation (RMSEA), Comparative Fit Index (CFI), Tucker-Lewis Index (TLI), and Standardized Root Mean Residual (SRMR). According to Hu and Bentler, a well-fitted model has RMSEA < 0.06, CFI and TLI > 0.95, and SRMR < 0.0834.

Floor and ceiling effects

Floor and ceiling effects were calculated by determining the percentage of participants who obtained the lowest or highest possible scores for the overall score and each WHODAS 2.0 item.

External validity

Convergent validity was assessed by correlating the scores of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 questionnaire with the scores of the FIQ and BDI questionnaires. In this case the significance of the Spearman's rank correlation coefficient was examined.

More than half of the respondents (54.82%) had higher education. 63.59% of the participants were active in the labour market (Table 1).

Reliability analysis

Internal consistency and temporal consistency

The internal consistency of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha, which was calculated for the total score and for each subscale. Values ranging from 0.824 to 0.951 were obtained, all of which were therefore higher than 0.7 (Table 2).

Temporal consistency was estimated using the test–retest method. For this purpose, 35 respondents were asked to complete the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 again after 7 days. The ICC coefficients were very high (above 0.995) indicating an almost perfect concordance between the results of the first and second application of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 and providing evidence of the temporal consistency of the tool. Very high ICC values also showed that n = 35 sample was enough to show temporal consistency of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 with desired power of 0.8 (Table 3).

Discussion

The adaptation of measurement tools allows for global exchange of the results in a standardized way and enables their reliable comparison on an international level. The 36-item WHODAS 2.0 is a universal tool used to assess disability in individuals with various health conditions23,24,35,36. So far, only one study in the world has evaluated the psychometric properties of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 in people with FM18. WHO recommends the use of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 in the new revision of ICD-11. Therefore, it is essential to check whether this tool has good psychometric properties in assessing individuals with different medical conditions.

As the results of our study show, the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 is an important and reliable tool for measuring functioning and disability in individuals with FM.

The internal consistency of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 was assessed using the Cronbach's Alpha coefficient, which was 0.95 for the total score, indicating very high reliability of the scale. The Cronbach's alpha coefficient for the total score of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 scale in the Brazilian population was 0.9118. In other studies, evaluating the psychometric properties of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 in rheumatic or chronic diseases, authors also confirmed very good internal consistency of the tool. For example, Baron et al.35 in assessing the psychometric properties of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 in Canadian patients with inflammatory arthritis estimated a Cronbach’s Alpha coefficient for the total score of 0.96 for those working and 0.93 for those not working. Garin et al.36 in chronic disease patients from 7 European centres set the Cronbach's Alpha for the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 total score at 0.95, Silva et al.37 for Portuguese patients with chronic musculoskeletal pain at 0.84, Igwesi-Chidobe et al.38 for Nigerian patients with chronic low back pain (LBP) at 0.97, while Ćwirlej-Sozańska et al.23 for Polish patients with LBP at 0.92.

Our study showed very good test–retest reliability after 7 days, indicating high test repeatability (ICC = 0.99). Acceptable results for the test–retest reliability (ICC = 0.54) of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 in individuals with FM were also obtained by Barreto et al.18, although their study showed less stability in the domains of activity and participation. Baron et al.35 in patients with inflammatory arthritis, the reproducibility of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 was set at ICC = 0.95 for the total score, Garin et al.36 for patients with chronic diseases set ICC = 0.74, Silva et al.37 for patients with chronic musculoskeletal pain ICC = 0.95, while Igwesi-Chidobe et al.38 ICC = 0.93 and Ćwirlej -Sozańska et al.23 ICC = 0.93 for patients with LBP.

External validity was assessed by correlating the results of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 with the results of two scales: FIQ and BDI. Positive, statistically significant correlations were obtained between the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 and these instruments. The correlation for the total score of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 and FIQ was 0.65, and for BDI—0.53. Barreto et al., observed that higher scores in quality of life were associated with a better functioning profile in the 36-item WHODAS 2.0. The correlation between the SF 36 total score and the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 was − 0.65 and it was statistically significant. As for the BDI and FIQ questionnaires, the authors observed moderate correlations with the overall score of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0. Other authors have assessed that the external validity of WHODAS 2.0 is at a good level, especially when it comes to the overall score of the tool23,35,36,38.

In our study, we examined the internal structure of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 using CFA. The results, RMSEA = 0.069, CFI = 0.963, TLI = 0.96, SRMR = 0.081, confirmed the accuracy of the original structure of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0. While assessing disability in individuals with various chronic diseases, Garin et al., confirmed the goodness of fit of the internal structure and overall score of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 in the CFA model. However, the authors suggested that higher fit parameters could be achieved if certain items from the “Participation” domain were moved to other factors. Nevertheless, since research on the application and properties of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 is ongoing in various countries, the authors suggest that maintaining the original structure is more beneficial. In another study, the internal structure of the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 questionnaire was examined by analysing internal correlations using Pearson's correlation coefficient. Ćwirlej-Sozańska et al., investigating the internal structure of the WHODAS 2.0 in patients with LBP obtained very good results. Scores for individual domains were moderately strongly or strongly correlated with the total score (r ranged from 0.482 to 0.762)23.

Conclusions

Based on the conducted research, it has been shown that the 36-item WHODAS 2.0 is a reliable and valid tool for assessing disability in individuals with FM in Poland. It provides reliable and accurate information about the functioning of people with FM. A multidimensional biopsychosocial assessment of functioning is very important in the context of effective management of the treatment and therapy process, as well as the appropriate adjustment of support to the needs of individuals with FM, improving their functioning, activity, and participation in society.

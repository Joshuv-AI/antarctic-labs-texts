Randomized clinical efficacy and safety study of peltopepimut-S plus cemiplimab compared to cemiplimab alone in patients with recurrent/metastatic HPV16-positive head and neck cancer
Authors: Caroline Even, Kevin J Harrington, Erminia Massarelli, Simon Laban, Jérôme Fayette, Marc Oliva, Marielle Klein Hesselink, Sonja Visscher, Matthew G Fury, Anna-Sophia Wiekmeijer, Lisa Licitra, Bohuslav Melichar, Lot A Devriese, Irene Braña, Petra Jankowska, Marshall Posner, Bonnie Glisson, Anthony Kong, Leon Hooftman, Cornelis J M Melief, Renata Ferrarotto
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPeltopepimut-S is a therapeutic vaccine, which induces specific expansion of both CD4+helper and CD8+cytotoxic T-cells against human papillomavirus type 16 (HPV16) E6/E7 oncoproteins.Patients and methodsIn a randomized phase 2 trial, we evaluated the efficacy and safety of peltopepimut-S plus cemiplimab compared with cemiplimab alone as first-line or second-line therapy in recurrent/metastatic HPV16-positive head and neck cancer. The primary efficacy endpoint was the objective response rate (ORR) by an independent review (Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors version 1.1, RECIST v1.1), while the primary safety endpoint was frequency and severity of adverse events. Secondary endpoints included progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS).ResultsOverall, 198 anti-programmed cell death protein-1 therapy-naïve patients with confirmed HPV16-positive recurrent/metastatic oropharyngeal cancer were randomized to receive cemiplimab plus peltopepimut-S (n=100) or placebo (n=99). The trial did not meet its primary objective (ORR, 25.3% in peltopepimut-S arm vs 22.9% in placebo arm; p=0.735). The median OS (mOS) and PFS in the placebo arm were unexpectedly longer than in the peltopepimut-S arm (26.9 vs 15.8 months) and (20.3 vs 5.5 months), respectively. In predefined exploratory analyses this was associated with an excess death rate from progressive disease in patients with pre-treatment programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) combined positive score (CPS) <20. In contrast, patients with CPS ≥20 had a higher ORR of 51.7% (95% CI, 32.5% to 70.6%) vs 25.8% (95% CI, 11.9% to 44.6%) and a longer mOS in the peltopepimut-S arm (34.8 vs 28.8 months) compared with the placebo arm, respectively. If patients had received all three vaccine or placebo doses, the ORR in patients with CPS ≥20 was 70.0% (95% CI, 45.7% to 88.1%) vs 29.2% (95% CI, 12.6% to 51.1%) in the peltopepimut-S arm and placebo arms, respectively, associated with mOS not reached (after 42 months) versus 23.3 months. The addition of peltopepimut-S to cemiplimab did not increase cemiplimab’s toxicity.ConclusionAdding peltopepimut-S to cemiplimab did not improve ORR and worsened mOS in the primary analysis. Divergent outcomes were seen in patients with pretreatment PD-L1 CPS <20 (worse ORR and mOS compared with placebo) and CPS ≥20 (higher ORR and longer mOS compared with placebo) values. Future drug development is justifiable in the CPS ≥20 patient population.Trial registration numberNCT03669718.Keywords: Combination therapy, Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor, Head and Neck Cancer, Viral-specific T cells, Vaccine

WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN ON THIS TOPIC

In single-arm trials, therapeutic cancer vaccines directed against high-risk human papillomavirus (HPV) have benefited patients with recurrent/metastatic HPV-induced cancers in combination with standard-of-care chemotherapy or programmed cell death protein-1 (PD-1) inhibition.

WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS

This study reports the outcomes of the first blinded randomized study comparing the benefit of combining peltopepimut-S, a long peptide vaccine directed against HPV16, with cemiplimab, a PD-1 inhibiting monoclonal antibody, versus placebo vaccine plus cemiplimab. The results show that the objective response rate (ORR) did not differ in the entire study population between the active combination and the placebo, whereas median overall survival (mOS) was worse in the active combination. Detrimental or beneficial outcomes were strongly associated with the pre-treatment biomarker programmed death-ligand 1 cancer tissue expression (assayed as combined positive score, CPS). Patients with CPS <20 in the active combination showed a decreased mOS due to more frequent early progressive disease compared with the placebo arm. In contrast, ORR was higher in the active combination in patients with CPS ≥20, especially in patients who had received all three vaccine doses and this was associated with a markedly prolonged mOS in the active arm. The addition of the vaccine did not add to the toxicity of cemiplimab.

Background

Increasing numbers of patients with head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) present with oropharyngeal cancer (OPC) caused by human papillomavirus type 16 (HPV16) via the expression of viral E6 and E7 oncoproteins.1,3 Overall HPV prevalence in OPC increased in recent decades, despite the availability of preventative vaccines against HPV.1 2 In the USA in 2020, OPC was the most frequent HPV-associated cancer.4

Therapeutic vaccines for established HPV-induced cancers have shown clinical activity.5,7 Peltopepimut-S is a cocktail of 12 synthetic long peptides that cover the sequence of the oncogenic proteins of HPV16, E6 and E7 (online supplemental table S1). Peltopepimut-S has shown monotherapy activity in patients with premalignant disease8 9 and with HPV16-induced malignancies when combined with chemotherapy10 11 or anti-programmed cell death protein-1 (PD-1) immunotherapy.12 13 In previous studies, its immunogenicity showed a significant correlation between the size of the vaccine-induced T-cell response and the clinical response.8 9 11 Cemiplimab is a monoclonal anti-PD-1 antibody with a similar mechanism of action14,16 as the immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) nivolumab and pembrolizumab. The latter ICIs have shown clinical benefit in first (1L) and second line (2L) recurrent/metastatic (R/M) HNSCC.17,21

We now report on the first randomized phase 2 trial investigating the addition of HPV16-directed peltopepimut-S vaccine to cemiplimab in R/M HPV16-positive OPC.

Methods

Trial design and treatments

In this randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase 2 OpcemISA trial, 2L patients with R/M OPC who had failed 1L platinum-based chemotherapy, and 1L patients with R/M OPC, were randomized (1:1) to either peltopepimut-S plus cemiplimab or placebo plus cemiplimab. The trial was initiated in 2L R/M patients in January 2019 but was amended to allow recruitment of 1L R/M patients in January 2020 following the approval of pembrolizumab for this indication. Combined positive score (CPS) testing—indicative of pretreatment programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression on activated immune and tumor cells—was used to inform patient eligibility for 1L anti-PD-1 therapy.20 22

Peltopepimut-S (100 µg/peptide) in Montanide adjuvant or matching placebo was injected subcutaneously in two different extremities, one injection being HPV-DP-5P and one injection being HPV-DP-7P or matching placebo following a 3-dose schedule (Days 1, 29 and 50), while cemiplimab (350 mg) was given intravenously on Day 8, and then every 3 weeks up to 24 months after treatment start or until disease progression or withdrawal for other reasons (online supplemental figure S1). Placebo was a reconstitution solution mixed in a 1:1 ratio with Montanide adjuvant. The same preparation procedure was followed as for peltopepimut-S except for the addition of the actual drug product. Randomization was stratified by smoking status (people who never smoked cigarettes in their life vs people who smoke) and treatment line (1L vs 2L).

The trial was conducted in accordance with US Food and Drug Administration regulations and the Declaration of Helsinki.

Trial assessments

Efficacy

The primary endpoint was objective response rate (ORR) by independent review according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors version 1.1 (RECIST v1.1). The secondary efficacy endpoints included duration of response (DoR), progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS). These analyses were performed in the full analysis set (FAS), that is, all patients who received at least one dose of peltopepimut-S, and as a secondary analysis of the primary endpoint also in the predefined per protocol set (PPS), that is, all patients who received all three vaccine doses, had centrally confirmed tumor HPV16 positivity, had at least one post-treatment scan, and had no protocol deviations impacting primary and secondary study endpoints. After protocol amendment for inclusion of 1L patients, the PD-L1 CPS 1–19 and ≥20 subgroups were analyzed as exploratory endpoints. Exploratory endpoints further include the evaluation of HPV16-specific T-cell responses before and at different time points after vaccination. The assays for the biomarkers HPV16 and PD-L1 (CPS) tissue expression, as well as immuno-monitoring are described in the online supplemental material.

Safety

The primary safety endpoint was frequency and severity of toxicities according to Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0 (CTCAE v5.0). Dose reductions of peltopepimut-S or cemiplimab were not allowed.

Patients

In the first year of recruitment, patients with histologically confirmed 2L R/M HPV16-positive OPC were enrolled and were ≥18 years, had progressive disease on or after platinum-based chemotherapy, irrespective of CPS and smoking status, were HPV16-positive by central laboratory PCR, had Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status 0 or 1, and at least one measurable tumor lesion. After approval of pembrolizumab in 1L, the eligibility criteria were amended to include 1L patients with PD-L1 CPS ≥1.20,22 Full eligibility criteria are detailed in the protocol, available in online supplemental material.

Trial oversight

The trial was designed, conducted, and analyzed by the sponsor and investigators. All authors reviewed the manuscript and approved it. The protocol and its amendments are available in online supplemental material.

Statistical analysis

Trial size was planned using the normal approximation to the binomial distribution for ORR as the primary outcome measure using specification of placebo arm ORR as 18.5%, a delta of +17 points for the peltopepimut-S arm (OR 2.42), and target one-sided alpha and beta of 0.10 and 0.10, respectively, for the one-sided null hypothesis of OR ≤1 against the alternative hypothesis of >1 giving a target of 97 per arm. Efficacy analyses were based on the FAS using randomized arms and are therefore compliant with the intent-to-treat as a result of double-blinding.

A blinded interim analysis was conducted, when 110 patients had completed ORR assessments (56 peltopepimut-S and 54 in the placebo arm, confirmed ORR in the peltopepimut-S arm n=15 (26.8%) and n=12 (22.2%) in the placebo arm). The interim analysis was exploratory, as prespecified in the statistical analysis plan. The sponsor made no decisions about study design and conduct based on the interim analysis; therefore, alpha-level adjustment was not required. Moreover, most patients enrolled before the interim analysis were 2L R/M patients and thus different from the mostly 1L patients included after this analysis. The Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) was informed of the outcome of the interim analysis and agreed with the continuation of the study. ISA Pharmaceutical’s medical/operational team, and all personnel at the study sites involved in conducting the study remained unaware of the results of the interim analysis and remained blinded concerning all results until completion of the study.

The FAS included all patients who received ≥1 dose of either study drug. Efficacy analyses were primarily based on the FAS. Efficacy was also explored in the predefined PPS and PD-L1 subgroups as mentioned above (under efficacy). The safety analysis set included all patients who had received at least one dose of study drug.

Statistical methodology included Mantel-Haenszel test, Fisher’s exact test, logistic regression, log-rank test, proportional hazards regression (Cox regression) and Kaplan-Meier estimation. ORR was defined as having observed a response or not in patients with measurable disease in the FAS and a two-sided p≤0.20 conditional on a higher response peltopepimut-S arm. No multiplicity adjustments were applied in all other analyses (exploratory) and results are reported using (Fisher’s exact) 95% CIs.

From January 2019 through December 2022, 199 patients were enrolled at 42 sites in the USA, Europe, and Latin America; 198 of the 199 patients, 100 on peltopepimut-S and 98 on placebo, met the criterion for the FAS (figure 1). Efficacy

Objective response rate

At the cut-off date (July 5, 2023), the best ORR by independent review (primary endpoint) failed to find statistical evidence of a between-arm difference in the FAS (25.3% in peltopepimut-S arm (95% CI, 16.7% to 35.5%) vs 22.9% in placebo arm (95% CI, 15.0% to 32.6%); p=0.735) (table 2A). In the CPS ≥20 FAS subgroup, there were more responders in the peltopepimut-S arm compared with the placebo arm (ORR, 51.7% (95% CI, 32.5% to 70.6%) vs 25.8% (95% CI, 11.9% to 44.6%)). Exploratory PPS analysis of the FAS included 54 patients from the peltopepimut-S arm and 77 patients from the placebo arm, representing 131 of 198 (66.2%) eligible patients. ORR in the FAS PPS was numerically higher with peltopepimut-S plus cemiplimab (38.9%; 95% CI, 25.9% to 53.1%) compared with cemiplimab alone (27.3%; 95% CI, 17.7% to 38.6%) (table 2A). In the CPS ≥20 PPS subset, the respective ORR values were 70.0% (95% CI, 45.7% to 88.1%) versus 29.2% (95% CI, 12.6% to 51.1%) (table 2A).

The patient numbers in each arm were balanced for smoking status and line of treatment (table 1). Moreover, there was no difference in best ORR between the two arms based on these stratification factors (data not shown).

Best overall response and duration of response in the full analysis set

The disease control rate reached 61.6% in the peltopepimut-S arm versus 69.8% in the placebo arm (table 2B). A comparison of the two arms revealed no differences in the FAS for DoR (table 2C), the median of which was not reached for either arm at the time this analysis was performed.

Overall survival

At the cut-off date 103 patients had died, 60 (60.0%) in the peltopepimut-S arm versus 43 (43.9%) in the placebo arm (online supplemental Table S3B). The relationship between survival and study arm was assessed in the FAS (figure 2A). The median OS (mOS) in the placebo arm is longer than in the peltopepimut-S arm (26.9 vs 15.8 months) with an estimated HR of 1.68 (95% CI, 1.155 to 2.44), showing unexpected benefit in the placebo arm. The shorter survival in the peltopepimut-S arm was confined to patients with CPS <20 (figure 2B). Patients with CPS ≥20 in the FAS tended to have longer mOS in the peltopepimut-S arm after 24 months, but the arms did not differ (34.8 vs 28.8 months; HR, 0.767; 95% CI, 0.367 to 1.603) (figure 2B).

The mOS in PPS patients with CPS <20 was shorter in the peltopepimut-S arm compared with the placebo arm with a HR of 3.228 (15.8 vs 35.2 months; 95% CI 1.571 to 6.635). Conversely mOS in PPS patients with CPS ≥20 was greater in patients assigned to peltopepimut-S compared with the placebo arm, with a HR of 0.385 (not reached vs 23.3 months; 95% CI, 0.154 to 0.961) (figure 2C, also shown for comparison in online supplemental figure S5D). Notably, this was the same subgroup of patients who showed an improved ORR after peltopepimut-S treatment compared with placebo (70.0 vs 29.2%) (table 2A).

Exploratory OS analysis according to PD-L1, ECOG performance status, and NLR

In the FAS survival effect modification analyses, for patients with CPS ≥20 as well as for patients with ECOG 0 and CPS ≥20, an HR <1 was obtained, suggesting a potential peltopepimut-S benefit (online supplemental figure S2). In contrast, for patients with ECOG one and CPS <20 as well as ECOG >0 and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) >5, a HR >1 was obtained, suggesting worse survival in the peltopepimut-S arm for this subgroup (online supplemental figure S2). These FAS outcomes were confirmed by Kaplan-Meier estimates associated with the effect modifiers CPS, ECOG, and NLR (CPS, figure 2B, also shown for comparison in online supplemental figure S3C; ECOG, online supplemental figure S3B; NLR, online supplemental figure S3D); ECOG+CPS, online supplemental figure S6A and ECOG+NLR, online supplemental figure S6C). In PPS patients, exploratory survival analyses showed accentuated beneficial effects of CPS ≥20 (figure 2C, also shown for comparison in online supplemental figure S5D; ECOG 0 and CPS≥20, online supplemental figure S6B and ECOG 0 and NLR ≤5, online supplemental figure S6D).

Progression-free survival

A by-arm PFS FAS analysis based on local assessment is shown in online supplemental figure S4. In this analysis, like for FAS OS, the placebo arm is favored (online supplemental figure S4). However, in the CPS ≥20 subset of the FAS, a trend in favor of a median PFS (mPFS) advantage was observed for peltopepimut-S patients compared with placebo (14.1 vs 5.2 months; HR, 0.716; 95% CI, 0.390 to 1.313) (online supplemental figure S4C). In mPFS analysis of the PPS with CPS ≥20 (online supplemental figure S4D), the HR estimate was lower at 0.592 (95% CI, 0.284 to 1.231), with mPFS of 20.3 for peltopepimut-S versus 5.5 months for placebo.

Safety

The most frequent adverse events (AEs) grade ≥3 were abnormal laboratory values or infections in both arms (online supplemental table S7A). Treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs) were injection site reactions in 32 patients, 15 (15.0%) in peltopepimut-S versus 17 (17.3%) in the placebo arm. The most frequent cemiplimab-related TEAE was fatigue in 25 patients, 12 (12.0%) in the peltopepimut-S arm versus 13 (13.3%) in the placebo arm. TEAE leading to peltopepimut-S discontinuation was 3 (3.0%) in the peltopepimut-S arm versus 2 (2.0%) in the placebo arm (online supplemental table S7B). TEAE leading to cemiplimab discontinuation was 7 (7.0%) in the peltopepimut-S arm versus 12 (12.2%) in the cemiplimab arm (online supplemental table S7C). TEAE leading to discontinuation of both study drugs was 2 (2.0%) in both arms (online supplemental table S7D).

Table 3 shows the reported TEAEs for each study drug. Overall, TEAEs grade ≥3 occurred in 48.0% of patients in the peltopepimut-S arm and 44.9% in the placebo arm. The number of serious TEAEs (33.0 vs 31.6%, respectively) and the number of deaths related to TEAEs (5 vs 4 deaths, respectively) were similar in both arms. Addition of peltopepimut-S to cemiplimab did not appreciably increase cemiplimab’s toxicity.

Discussion

This is the first randomized double-blind phase 2 trial to evaluate the efficacy and safety of peltopepimut-S plus cemiplimab compared with cemiplimab alone as first-line or second-line therapy in R/M HPV16-positive HNSCC. The study did not meet its primary efficacy endpoint (ORR in FAS). Unexpectedly, the mOS was significantly lower in the peltopepimut-S arm compared with the control arm in the FAS. Strikingly, an excess death rate in the vaccine arm was only observed in patients with PD-L1 CPS <20. This indicates that the addition of peltopepimut-S to anti-PD-1 likely impaired OS in these patients. Because there were no extra toxicity signals from the peltopepimut-S arm (table 3), combination of the vaccine with anti-PD-1 might have caused increased cancer progression compared with the treatment with anti-PD-1 alone in a subset of patients. A possible explanation is a vaccine-induced expansion of regulatory T-cells, or an accelerated transition of macrophages to an immunosuppressive M2 phenotype, favoring a more immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment.11 23 Through such immune mechanisms, the combination of peltopepimut-S and cemiplimab may have induced the accelerated disease progression—also referred to as hyperprogression—observed in some patients under anti-PD-1 monotherapy.24 Vaccine-induced disease progression was not noted in our previous studies with this vaccine,7,13 but may have escaped attention, because none of these studies were randomized and most were performed in patients with premalignant disease or in combination with chemotherapy. Notably, chemotherapy with carboplatin and paclitaxel, beyond its direct cytotoxic effect, also depletes excess numbers of immunosuppressive myeloid cells without depleting T-cells, thereby potentially improving T-cell function.10 11 A single-arm phase 2 study of nivolumab plus ISA101 was conducted in 24 patients, which showed an ORR of 33% and a mOS of 17.5 months with acceptable toxicities.12 In our single-arm ProcemISA phase 2 study of peltopepimut-S and cemiplimab in patients with R/M HPV16+OPC who had progressed on prior anti-PD-1 therapy, the clinical benefit rate (consisting of partial remission and stable disease) was 56.3% with a mOS of 11.3 months and a mPFS of 3.6 months.25 In patients with recurrent metastatic HPV16+cervical cancer in 2L, the combination of cemiplimab and peltopepimut-S showed no unexpected safety signals compared with an earlier study of cemiplimab monotherapy in similar patients.26

Because of increased early disease progression in the peltopepimut-S arm, patients received less cemiplimab than those on the placebo arm (median therapy duration, 3.5 vs 5.6 months, respectively) (online supplemental table S2). Compared with the control arm, the decline in survival observed in the vaccine arm could be attributed to an increase in deaths prior to the third injection resulting in early progression that is deemed to be due to the vaccine with reduced exposure to PD-1 inhibitor.

The excess death rate in the peltopepimut-S arm was mainly visible in patients with performance score 1 (PS 1) and not those with PS 0 (PS >1) (online supplemental figure S3B). Many of these patients also had high baseline NLR (online supplemental figure S3D). Indeed, in the entire FAS population of 198 patients, regardless of arm of treatment, high baseline NLR was associated with poor prognosis (online supplemental figure S7A). Online supplemental figure S6C shows that the HR of death for FAS patients with ECOG 1 and NLR >5 was 4.79 in the peltopepimut-S arm compared with the placebo arm. Conversely, the HR of death for PPS patients with CPS ≥20 and ECOG 0 was 0.23 in the peltopepimut-S arm compared with placebo (online supplemental figure S7B). These exploratory analyses are hypothesis-generating and need to be interpreted with caution. Despite the fact that the baseline NLR has been documented as a poor prognostic factor in other studies of patients treated with immunotherapy including melanoma and R/M HNSCC,27,29 it has not been rigorously validated in large prospective clinical trials as a prognostic and predictive biomarker.

Exploratory analyses suggested that the PD-L1 CPS biomarker was predictive of treatment benefit. A high PD-L1 expression (CPS ≥20) was associated with a potential ORR improvement in the peltopepimut-S group compared with the placebo group (51.7 vs 25.8% in the FAS) (table 2A) and a survival benefit trend in FAS (figure 2B) and PPS (figure 2C) patients. Consistently, interim results of other HPV-16 directed therapeutic vaccines combined with anti-PD1 in HPV-16 OPC suggested an increased benefit in CPS >1 or CPS ≥20.30,32

PD-L1 is induced by interferon-gamma (IFNγ)33,36 on leukocytes and cancer cells, although IFNγ-independent PD-L1 expression occurs as well.37 An IFNγ cancer gene expression signature correlates with success of anti-PD-1 therapy in patients with melanoma.38 In our previous study with anti-PD-1 and a vaccine similar to peltopepimut-S,12 only patients with pretreatment cancer infiltration by PD-1+ leukocytes had clinical responses.13

Similarly, prevaccination inflamed tissue signature corresponded with clinical responders to monotherapy with this vaccine in premalignant disease.39

As our trial commenced before approval of pembrolizumab for patients with R/M HNSCC, recruitment started in patients who had failed platinum-based chemotherapy. Following the approval of pembrolizumab for the 1L indication,19 20 amendments were made to the study protocol to include such patients. Consequently, the first limitation of this study is that it comprises two distinct patient populations. Second, from about 20% of the initially included 2L R/M patients in each arm, the PD-L1 CPS result could not be retrieved retrospectively after the change in protocol to include both groups of patients. Conversely, all 110 patients with 1L R/M disease had by definition PD-L1 CPS >1 and were all assigned to CPS <20 or ≥20 categories. The assumption that combined vaccine and PD-1 inhibitor would be superior to PD-1 inhibitor monotherapy in all patients regardless of their PD-L1 expression has been refuted by the outcome of this trial. The exploratory analysis of this study points to improved ORR and mOS in patients with pretreatment PD-L1 expression CPS ≥20. This is consistent with prior observations from the KN48 study: the anticancer activity of anti-PD-1 monotherapy was superior to chemotherapy in patients with CPS ≥20 but this advantage was markedly less obvious in patients with lower CPS.19 20 Our observation needs confirmation in a phase 2/3 randomized study in patients with CPS ≥20. Patients with CPS ≥20 constitute 40–45% of patients with 1L R/M OPC.20

A third limitation is that the trial was not powered for subgroup analyses. Poorer ECOG scores are predictive of reduced immunotherapy efficacy.40 In our trial, an ECOG score of 1 combined with NLR >5 was a survival effect modifier, in agreement with other studies regarding the response to immunotherapy in advanced cancers, including HNSCC.27,2941 Interestingly, in a recent study, HPV16-expressing tumors were found to release multiple IL-1 ligands, driving bone marrow bias towards granulocytic myelopoiesis.42 This provides a mechanistic basis for high NLR values in many patients in this study and in a previous study in patients with cervical cancer.11 In addition, this insight offers a novel approach toward the elimination of immunosuppressive granulocytes and improved efficacy of therapeutic vaccination.42

Unexpectedly, mOS in the placebo plus cemiplimab arm was long (23–28 months), especially in comparison with the mOS of less than 15 months reported in the KN48 study with pembrolizumab.17 20

The peltopepimut-S plus cemiplimab combination was well tolerated. Compared with the known safety profile of cemiplimab, no new safety signals arose from the addition of peltopepimut-S to cemiplimab,14,16 but as discussed above, the combination may cause accelerated disease progression in patients with CPS <20.

Compared with the known safety profile of cemiplimab, no new safety signals arose from the addition of peltopepimut-S to cemiplimab,14,16 but as discussed above, the combination may cause accelerated disease progression in patients with CPS <20.

In conclusion, peltopepimut-S plus cemiplimab did not induce better antitumor responses in the FAS 1L/2L HPV16-positive OPC patient population and had a detrimental effect on OS in the entire group and in patients with PD-L1 CPS <20. Subset analyses suggest a superior antitumor response and survival benefit in patients with PD-L1 CPS ≥20. Any confirmatory studies of this agent should therefore be restricted to patients with CPS ≥20.

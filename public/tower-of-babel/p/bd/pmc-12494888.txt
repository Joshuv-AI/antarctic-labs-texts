On the solution for a nonlinear wave equation with variable exponent nonlinearity and a varying delay
Authors: Aissa Benguessoum, M’hamed Bensaid, Salah Boulaaras, Mohammed Said Souid
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This work examines a nonlinear wave equation characterized by a damping term whose exponent varies, along with a delay component that changes over time. The mathematical representation of the problem is provided below. \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\nu _{tt} - \Delta _x \nu + \gamma _1(t) \, \nu _t|\nu _t|^{\varpi (x)-2} + \gamma _2(t) \, \nu _t(x, t - \varrho (t))| \nu _t(x, t-\varrho (t))|^{\varpi (x)-2} = 0.$$\end{document}To demonstrate the existence of global solutions, we impose suitable conditions on the functions \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\gamma _1$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\gamma _2$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varpi$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varrho$$\end{document}. This is achieved through the compactness method, specifically utilizing the Faedo–Galerkin approach. Furthermore, by applying the multiplier technique in conjunction with an integral inequality of Komornik type, we establish a quantitative assessment for how swiftly the system’s energy diminishes over time.

Introduction

This work is devoted to the study of the existence and long-term behavior of solutions for an initial-boundary value problem associated with a nonlinear wave equation incorporating delay terms. The problem under consideration can be formally expressed as follows:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left\{ \begin{array}{ll} \nu _{tt}(x, t) - \Delta _x \nu (x, t) + \gamma _1(t) \, \nu _t(x, t)|\nu _t(x, t)|^{\varpi (x)-2}& \\ \quad \ + \gamma _2(t) \, \nu _t(x, t - \varrho (t))|\nu _t(x,t-\varrho (t))|^{\varpi (x)-2} = 0, & \Omega \times (0, +\infty ), \\ \nu (x, t)=0, & \partial \Omega \times [0, +\infty ), \\ \nu _t(x, t)=0, & \partial \Omega \times [0, +\infty ), \\ \nu (x, 0)=\nu _{0}(x),\ \ \nu _t(x,0)=\nu _{1}(x), & \Omega , \\ \nu _t(x, - r \varrho (0))=\mathfrak {I}_{0}(x, - r \varrho (0)), & \Omega \times (0, 1), \end{array}\right. \quad \quad {(\mathscr {P})}.$$\end{document}

Numerous researchers have focused on nonlinear wave equations with fixed exponents over the past few decades22–27. The general form of such equations is given by:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\nu _{tt}(x, t) - \Delta \nu (x, t) + \nu _{t}(x, t)|\nu _{t}(x, t)|^{\varpi -2} = \nu (x, t)|\nu (x, t)|^{\zeta -2}, \quad (x, t) \in \Omega \times (0, \tau ).$$\end{document}

The parameters \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\tau > 0$$\end{document} and the constants \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varpi , \zeta \ge 2$$\end{document} are also defined.

The authors in Ref.14 demonstrated that the damping term \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\nu _t|\nu _t|^{\varpi -2}$$\end{document} guarantees the global existence of solutions regardless of the initial data when the source term \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\nu |\nu |^{\zeta -2}$$\end{document} is absent. Conversely, the presence of the source term without damping, solutions with negative initial energy may exhibit finite-time blow-up. (as shown in Ref.3). Levin16 established that negative initial energy can also result in finite-time blow-up under linear damping (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varpi = 2$$\end{document}). This finding was later extended to cases with nonlinear damping \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varpi > 2$$\end{document}, proving global existence of solutions for arbitrary initial data when \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varpi \ge \zeta$$\end{document} while solutions with sufficiently negative initial energy may experience blow-up within finite time if \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varpi <\zeta$$\end{document} refer to Ref.11

Recently, there has been a surge of research on wave equations (see6–9). The investigations have largely concentrated on systems situated within Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces endowed with variable exponents, exemplified by Ref.10. Messaoudi and Talahmeh19 considered the following equation:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\nu _{tt} - div\left( |\nabla \nu |^{\varpi (\cdot ) -2} \nabla \nu \right) + \gamma \nu _t = \nu |\nu |^{\zeta (\cdot )-2}, \quad \text{ in } \ \Omega \times (0, \tau ),$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\gamma$$\end{document} is a nonnegative parameter. Under suitable assumptions on the variable exponents, they demonstrated results indicating that solutions with arbitrary positive initial energy can exhibit blow-up behavior. Subsequently, the same authors20 analyzed a related model:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\nu _{tt} - div\left( |\nabla \nu |^{\delta (\cdot )-2} \nabla \nu \right) + a \nu _t |\nu _t|^{\varpi (\cdot )-2} = b \nu |\nu |^{\zeta (\cdot )-2}, \quad \text{ in } \ \Omega \times (0, \tau ),$$\end{document}with both constants a, b, are positive and nonlinear exponents \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\zeta , \varpi$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\delta$$\end{document} are functions. Their results indicate the possibility of finite-time blow-up for solutions possessing negative initial energy, and in some cases, even for those with positive energies.

In12, it was demonstrated that global existence and stability are ensured for this equation when both constants are equal to one, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$a = b = 1$$\end{document}.

They analyzed the problem under suitable restrictions on the constants \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\gamma _1$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\gamma _2$$\end{document}, and b, showing decay results when the source term is absent and proving both decay and global nonexistence in certain scenarios when it is present. The primary aim of this study is to investigate problem \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(\mathscr {P})$$\end{document}, which involves a nonlinear wave equation characterized by damping governed by a variable exponent and influenced by a delay term that varies with time. This analysis is carried out under appropriate assumptions imposed on the delay and exponent functions \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\gamma _1$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\gamma _2$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varpi$$\end{document}, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\varrho$$\end{document}, we prove the global existence of solutions through the Faedo–Galerkin approach. Moreover, employing the multiplier method alongside Komornik’s integral inequality, we derive explicit energy decay rate estimates.

This manuscript is structured in five sections. Conclusion

In this work, we have rigorously studied a nonlinear wave equation characterized by a damping term with variable exponent and influenced by a time-varying delay. Under suitable assumptions on the functions defining the damping, nonlinearity, and delay, we have established the global existence of strong solutions by employing the Faedo-Galerkin method combined with compactness arguments.

Additionally, we have derived explicit decay estimates for the energy functional associated with the system by applying the multiplier method along with Komornik’s integral inequality. Our results demonstrate that the energy of the system decays at an exponential rate depending on the behavior of the damping coefficient \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\gamma _1(t)$$\end{document}.

Task optimized vision transformer for diabetic retinopathy detection and classification in resource constrained early diagnosis settings
Authors: Ramasubramanian Bhoopalan, Priyadharshini Sekar, N Nagaprasad, Tadesse Regassa Mamo, Ramaswamy Krishnaraj
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractDiabetic Retinopathy (DR) is a progressive complication of diabetes and a leading cause of preventable blindness worldwide. Early detection and accurate classification of DR severity are critical for timely intervention but remain challenging, particularly in resource-constrained settings. While conventional deep learning (DL) models based on Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have shown promising results, they often struggle to capture long-range dependencies in retinal fundus images and typically require substantial computational resources, limiting their utility on low-cost hardware. To address these challenges, this study introduces a Task-Optimized Vision Transformer (TOViT) model, specifically designed for DR detection and severity classification. The model integrates several optimization strategies, including layer-wise learning rate scheduling, attention head tuning, and embedding dimension refinement, to enhance feature extraction while maintaining computational efficiency. The model is further compressed through structured pruning and 8-bit quantization to support real-time deployment on Raspberry Pi-4 hardware. Evaluated on three large-scale public datasets, TOViT achieved a classification accuracy of 99%, with F1-scores exceeding 93% across all DR stages. Hardware implementation yielded real-time performance, processing at 8 frames per second with 120 ms latency, confirming its potential for use in portable, point-of-care screening devices. This work presents a scalable and clinically relevant approach for automated DR diagnosis, with promising implications for expanding access to early retinal screening in global healthcare systems.Keywords: Diabetic retinopathy, Vision transformer, Deep learning, Lightweight model, Real-time screening, Resource-constrained healthcareSubject terms: Health care, Engineering, Materials science

Introduction

DR is one of the prevalent causes of loss of vision globally, affecting millions of individuals with diabetes. Early detection and diagnosis of DR through fundus photography are critical in preventing vision loss. With the advent of deep learning (DL), automated solutions for DR screening have demonstrated significant promise. Conventional models, primarily based on CNNs, have shown efficacy but remain limited in handling complex patterns and long-range dependencies in retinal images. Models such as Inception V31,2, VGG161–3, ResNet-501, and MobileNet4 have demonstrated remarkable performance in DR detection. While traditional CNNs achieve high accuracy, their computational demands limit scalability in resource-constrained environments. ViT offers a paradigm shift in medical imaging5,6, employing self-attention mechanisms to capture global and local features effectively. Existing studies often neglect key aspects such as hyperparameter optimization and real-world scalability, limiting their clinical utility. This study introduces TOViT architecture specifically designed for DR classification.

The novelty of TOViT lies in its domain-specific architectural refinement and deployment-aware optimization. Unlike prior ViT-based approaches for DR classification, TOViT introduces a 16-layer transformer configuration with 1024-dimensional embeddings, optimized using a combination of layer-wise learning rate adjustment, dropout regularization, label smoothing, and positional encoding fine-tuning. Furthermore, the model is pruned and quantized for deployment on Raspberry Pi-4, achieving real-time processing speeds with minimal memory overhead. This end-to-end pipeline from architectural tuning to embedded deployment differentiates TOViT from standard ViT implementations and makes it a clinically viable solution.

Literature review

Nazih et al.7 proposed a unique Vision Transformer (ViT)-based deep learning pipeline using the FGADR dataset to identify different stages of DR severity. The model, optimized using AdamW, was evaluated against cutting-edge CNN algorithms such as ResNet50, InceptionV3, and VGG19, outperforming baseline ViT models. Similarly, Alavee et al.1 developed a CNN model with comparable performance by utilizing state-of-the-art models. Their approach employed neural networks, machine learning models, and transfer learning for binary and multi-class classification. The proposed method, which uses categorical cross-entropy and SoftMax functions, demonstrated superior performance, with the Xception model achieving 82% accuracy in multi-label classification.

Saeed et al.8 introduced a method involving the re-initialization of a CNN model, where lower-level layers were adjusted to learn local structures. A new fully connected (FC) layer, based on principal component analysis, was added to extract discriminative features from fundus images using an encoder. The model also included a classification layer using gradient boosting. Evaluated using tenfold cross-validation on the EyePACS and Messidor datasets, the proposed approach outperformed other methods. Saranya et al.9 focused on improving blood vessel detection by applying Top-hat and Bottom-hat transformations after adaptive histogram equalization (AHE) for pre-processing retinal images. The enhanced images were segmented using the Fuzzy C-Means (FCM) procedure, and features such as form, brightness, location, and contrast were extracted. These features were classified using a K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) classifier, achieving 96.5% accuracy on the DRIVE and MESSIDOR databases. Naz et al.10 proposed an ensemble technique that integrates the weighted average predictions of CNN, CNN-i, and CNN + i algorithms. Their results demonstrated the effectiveness of the proposed approach. Khan et al.3 introduced the VGG-NiN model, which combines NiN, Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPP), and VGG16. Experimental findings showed that the proposed model outperformed state-of-the-art techniques in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency. Al-Antary and Arafa11 enhanced feature representation by embedding fundus images using an encoder network. Their model incorporated a multi-scale feature and attention mechanism to characterize specific retinal regions and extract features. The model was trained to discriminate between healthy and diseased retinas, achieving high accuracy. Ghazal et al.12 proposed a computer-aided diagnosis (CAD) system using CNNs to identify non-proliferative DR (NPDR) at an early stage. The system utilized two CNNs, one fed by nasal retina patches and the other by temporal retina patches. The proposed transfer learning-based CAD system achieved 94% accuracy. Sarki et al.13 conducted a comprehensive survey on automated techniques for identifying DR, providing an overview of state-of-the-art methodologies in diabetic eye disease detection. Kukkar et al.14 hybridized a deep learning model with optimization techniques to develop a novel DR classification system. Their approach involved noise removal in the preprocessing phase, feature extraction using ResNet, and classification into four severity levels. The proposed system’s performance was compared with existing classification systems, demonstrating its effectiveness. Although ViT have shown promise in fields like object detection and Natural Language Processing, their potential in medical imaging remains largely unexplored.

Recent works have begun exploring ViT-based architectures for DR detection. For instance, Nazih et al.7 used a baseline ViT on the FGADR dataset, achieving an accuracy of 82.5%. However, their model lacks hyperparameter optimization and external validation. Similarly, Kukkar et al.14 combined CNN and transformer components with metaheuristic optimization, but their study did not evaluate performance on embedded hardware or external datasets.

In contrast, TOViT introduces a 16-layer transformer architecture with 1024-dimensional embeddings, rigorous hyperparameter and calibration strategies, and hardware-aware pruning and quantization. Furthermore, its external validation on the DIARETDB1 dataset and real-time implementation on Raspberry Pi-4 distinguish it from existing approaches.

For this research, a subset of these datasets termed as mixed dataset was utilized as illustrated in Fig. 3; to avoid overfitting and ensure balanced representation across DR severity levels, a selective sampling strategy was employed during the training phase. The original dataset consisted of 28,632 images categorized into five DR grades, ranging from Grade 0 (No DR) to Grade 4 (Proliferative). Given the class imbalance, especially the dominance of Grade 0 samples, a reduced proportion (6.28%) of No DR images was included (1,800 out of 28,632) to prevent bias toward healthy cases. Conversely, a higher proportion of images was retained from the more severe and underrepresented classes: 48.65% from Grade 1, 18.08% from Grade 2, 87.61% from Grade 3, and 92.05% from Grade 4. This stratified sampling approach ensured a more balanced training set, allowing the model to effectively learn discriminative features across all DR stages while reducing the risk of overfitting to any specific class.

Proposed method

ViT16 introduces a novel approach for image classification tasks by leveraging the self-attention mechanism of transformers, presenting a compelling alternative to traditional CNN-based models. ViT offers distinct advantages in modeling long-range dependencies and global contextual relationships within images. Figure 4a,b illustrate the encoder module and the block schematic of the ViT architecture, respectively.

Vision transformer (a) Block diagram of vision transformer (b) Transformer encoder.

As shown in Fig. 4a, the ViT architecture begins by dividing the input image into fixed-size patches, which are then linearly embedded and augmented with positional encodings. These embeddings are passed through a series of transformer encoder layers, ultimately feeding into a classification head used for DR severity grading. Figure 4b provides a detailed view of a single transformer encoder layer, which consists of multi-head self-attention (MHSA), feed-forward neural networks (FFN), and layer normalization. This configuration enables efficient global feature extraction from retinal images, enhancing the model’s ability to detect subtle pathological patterns across varying scales and regions.

Initially, each image is divided into fixed-size patches, which are then flattened and embedded into a latent feature space. Positional encodings are added to these embeddings to preserve the spatial relationships between patches. Alongside a special classification token (CLS), the patch embeddings are passed through a series of transformer encoder layers composed of multi-head self-attention and feed-forward networks, as described by Atwany et al.17. The CLS token aggregates global contextual information from the entire image, which is subsequently passed through a fully connected layer to predict the DR severity class.

To enhance performance, ViT typically leverages pretrained weights obtained from large-scale datasets such as ImageNet, followed by fine-tuning on the target retinal dataset. This transfer learning strategy enables the model to retain robust feature extraction capabilities while adapting to domain-specific characteristics, such as microaneurysms, exudates, and optic disk swelling. As a result, ViT demonstrates high classification accuracy in DR severity grading by effectively capturing both global and fine-grained pathological features.

Hyperparameter optimization.

ViT was initially initialized with pretrained weights from the ImageNet dataset, which contains approximately 1.2 million images across 1,000 categories. While these weights capture general visual features such as edges and textures, they are not tailored for medical imaging. Fine-tuning on a DR-specific retinal fundus dataset enabled the model to learn domain-relevant features such as microaneurysms, exudates, and cotton wool spots.

This adaptation resulted in a 26.3% improvement in accuracy and a 0.28 increase in F1-Score compared to using only the original pretrained weights. The fine-tuning and optimization strategies used are detailed in Tables 2 and 3.

Hyperparameter selection and ablation study

To optimize ViT architecture for DR classification, it is systematically evaluated key hyperparameters to balance performance and computational efficiency. The choice of 16 transformer encoder layers was determined through an ablation study, where configurations with 8, 12, 16, and 20 layers were tested. The 16-layer model achieved the best trade-off, improving accuracy by 4.2% over the 12-layer variant while maintaining manageable computational overhead (approximately 267 million parameters). Similarly, the patch embedding dimension of 1024 was selected after experimenting with dimensions of 512, 768, and 1024; the higher dimension enhanced feature richness, increasing the F1-score by 3.8% due to better representation of subtle retinal patterns like microaneurysms. The number of attention heads (16) was chosen to maximize the model’s capacity to capture multi-scale dependencies, as fewer heads say 8 resulted in a 2.5% drop in sensitivity for severe DR cases. These decisions were validated using a grid search over the EyePACS dataset, with performance assessed via fivefold cross-validation to ensure robustness.

Discussion and comparison

Statistical significance analysis

To ensure a fair baseline comparison as shown in Table 7, all CNN models were retrained using the same datasets, preprocessing pipeline, and hyperparameter configurations as the TOViT model. Learning rate, batch size, and optimizer settings were standardized across models. This eliminated training bias and confirmed that TOViT’s performance advantage stems from its architecture and attention mechanisms, not from training irregularities.

Updated accuracies are as follows, VGG-16 (72.5%), ResNet-50 (78.3%), Inception V3 (80.1%), and MobileNet (76.8%). Despite these improvements, the TOViT (98%) retains a clear advantage. Compared to recent ViT-based DR studies, such as Nazih et al.7, which reported 82.5% accuracy on the FGADR dataset, our model’s superior performance stems from advanced fine-tuning techniques with layer-wise learning rate adjustment, cosine annealing scheduler and a deeper architecture 16 vs. 12 transformer layers. This enables better detection of complex patterns across all DR severity levels, particularly in resource-constrained settings where prior models falter due to computational constraints.

Model optimization for hardware deployment

To enable real-time deployment on Raspberry Pi-4, the TOViT was adapted for resource-constrained environments. The original model (267 million parameters) was pruned by removing 20% of the least significant attention heads, reducing parameters to 214 million without compromising accuracy (98.7% post-pruning).

Although the original TOViT architecture contains 267 million parameters, structured pruning by removing 20% of the least significant attention heads and 8-bit integer quantization were applied to model weights and activations. These optimizations reduced the memory footprint from 1.2 GB to 450 MB and accelerated inference by 2.3, enabling a real-time processing speed of 8 frames per second (fps) with 120 ms latency on a Raspberry Pi-4 (4 GB RAM, Broadcom BCM2711 processor). In contrast, deploying an unoptimized ResNet-50 on the same hardware achieved only 3 fps, despite having a smaller memory size (550 MB), due to its higher computational complexity. These deployment-aware modifications demonstrate that the ViT architecture, when optimized, can be both lightweight and efficient, making it suitable for portable, low-resource clinical settings.

Conclusion

To detect DR and assess its severity, the proposed TOViT consistently outperforms conventional CNN models such as VGG-16, ResNet-50, Inception V3, and MobileNet. Notably, in more complex cases where traditional models often struggle TOViT demonstrates superior capability in accurately identifying various stages of the disease. Its sophisticated transformer architecture, leveraging self-attention mechanisms, enables the extraction of both global and localized features from fundus images. This allows for precise detection of subtle retinal abnormalities, such as microaneurysms and exudates, essential for reliable DR classification. TOViT exhibits strong generalization across different DR severity levels and performs robustly even on datasets with varying distributions. These strengths stem from its optimized feature extraction pipeline, adaptive learning strategies, and task-specific tuning. As a result, the model shows great potential for integration into clinical workflows, where accuracy, consistency, and early detection are crucial. Furthermore, the model’s resilience to overfitting and its ability to generalize to unseen data make it well-suited for deployment in diverse real-world healthcare environments. The scalability of the architecture also allows for adaptation to other medical imaging tasks. Future work will aim to enhance generalizability by training the model on larger and more diverse datasets, including private clinical image repositories. Overall, this study marks a significant advancement toward making high-performance DR screening accessible in resource-limited settings. The integration of task-aware optimization, external validation, and real-time hardware deployment distinguishes TOViT from prior ViT-based methods, establishing it as both a technically sound and clinically relevant solution.

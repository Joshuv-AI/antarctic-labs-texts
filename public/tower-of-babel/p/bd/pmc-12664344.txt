Dataset on Vietnamese high school teachers’ readiness for digital transformation in education
Authors: Hue Thi Thu Dang, Dieu Quynh Bui, Quoc Anh Vuong, Chi Thi Nguyen, Bich Dao Thi Pham, Thai Viet Luong, Tai Duc Pham
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis article presents a comprehensive dataset examining the preparedness of high school educators in Vietnam for the digital transformation of their teaching and professional practices. The investigation evaluates readiness across ten critical dimensions: (1) Content Knowledge, (2) Technological Knowledge, (3) Technological Pedagogical Knowledge, (4) Teacher Perceptions and Expectations of Technology Application, (5) Perceived Ability to Use Technology, (6) Social Influence, (7) Facilitating Conditions, (8) Behavioral Intention to Utilize Technology, and (9) Actual Technology Use Behavior. Data was collected between May 15 and May 31, 2025, through an online survey utilizing Google Forms, yielding responses from 11,448 high school teachers across five provinces and cities (Bac Giang, Hung Yen, Nghe An, Can Tho, and Ho Chi Minh City)-1 representing Vietnam’s three primary regions. To maintain confidentiality and streamline analysis, the data has been anonymized and coded. This dataset aims to elucidate the key factors influencing teacher readiness, providing valuable insights for research, comparative studies, and the formulation of digital education policies and teacher training initiatives in the context of Vietnam's educational digital transformation and beyond].Keywords: Digital transformation, School teacher, Technology, Digital teaching skills, Readiness

Background

Digital transformation has become a key priority in Vietnam's education sector, driven by national policies such as the ``National Digital Transformation Program to 2025″ [6]. However, actual readiness among high school teachers remains uneven across regions and schools. There is a critical need for large-scale data to assess their knowledge, perceptions, and digital practices to support effective policy design.

This dataset was collected under the project “Assessing the readiness of high school teachers and school administrators for digital transformation in Vietnamese secondary schools” (Code: B2025.VKG.06), commissioned by the Ministry of Education and Training of Vietnam to the Vietnam National Institute of Educational Sciences. The study is grounded in a combined theoretical framework of TPACK, UTAUT, and TAM models [7,8].

The research employed a quantitative data approach. Specifically, an online survey was considered an effective tool to reach a large number of high school teachers across the country. The online survey was designed to encourage teachers to share their personal information and opinions. The release of this dataset aims to assess the readiness of high school teachers in Vietnam for digital transformation in education, capturing their perspectives across 10 different constructs, from knowledge and perception to behavioral intention and actual technology use.

Sampling and participants

The study employed a non-probability, purposive sampling strategy to select five provinces/cities : Bac Giang and Hung Yen (North), Nghe An (Central), and Can Tho and Ho Chi Minh City (South). This selection was deliberate to ensure representation of diverse geographical regions and socio-economic contexts across Vietnam, including major metropolitan areas, Red River Delta, mountainous/midland areas, and the Mekong Delta. The target population comprised high school teachers from both public and private institutions cross five provinces in Vietnam. A total of 11,448 valid responses were collected, representing three major regions. All participants provided informed consent, participated voluntarily, and were assured of data confidentiality and the academic purpose of the research.

Data collection procedures

The survey instrument was shared with teachers through a hierarchical education management system, from provincial Departments of Education and Training (DOETs) to the schools. An official letter requesting cooperation was sent to the DOETs, which included the survey hyperlink, before being forwarded to the schools. This formal channel ensured that the target population (School Leaders and Teachers) was appropriately reached, enhancing the official authenticity of the data collected.

Data was collected using a Google Forms online questionnaire, active from May 15 to May 31, 2025. During the two-week survey period, continuous communication was maintained between the responsible teams—from schools to the provincial DOETs and the Vietnam National Institute of Educational Sciences—to monitor and ensure the survey progressed as planned.

Limitations

While this study has a large sample size and a robust design, it is subject to several limitations:

Self-Reported data: The data was collected through an online questionnaire and relies on teachers' voluntary responses, which may be influenced by social desirability bias or memory errors.

Geographic limitation: Although the selection was purposive for diversity and the distribution was official, the final sample resulted from convenience and online self-selection. Consequently, the sample is not nationally representative, suffers from significant regional imbalance (e.g., nearly half of the responses came from HCMC), and may exclude individuals with limited digital access. Therefore, the data should be used to support exploratory findings and context-specific insights pertaining to these five locations.

Online distribution: The use of online forms may have excluded teachers with limited internet access or lower digital literacy, potentially introducing sampling bias.

Cross-sectional design: The data was collected at a single point in time, which limits the ability to assess causal relationships or longitudinal trends in digital readiness.

Instrument adaptation: While grounded in well-established models, the questionnaire includes some context-specific items which may limit generalizability across different countries or education systems without cultural adaptation.

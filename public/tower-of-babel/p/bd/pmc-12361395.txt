Exact wave structures with stochastic effects in birefringent optical fibers modeled by cubic-quintic-septic nonlinear Schrödinger equation
Authors: Wafaa B Rabie, Eman S Almotairi, Hamdy M Ahmed, Mohammed H Ali
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractStochastic optical solitons are a fascinating phenomenon in nonlinear optics where soliton-like behavior emerges in systems affected by stochastic noise. This study establishes a fundamental framework for stochastic wave propagation in birefringent fibers through the cubic-quintic-septic nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLSE). Our modified extended mapping technique yields exact analytical solutions (bright, dark, and singular solitons, periodic structures, and Weierstrass elliptic waves) that explicitly incorporate multiplicative noise and birefringent coupling. We explore the influence of noise intensity on soliton stability and morphology through parameter analysis and visual simulations, revealing how stochastic fluctuations modify amplitude, phase, and localization. The visualized results in Figures 1–3 not only validate the analytical expressions but also provide intuitive insight into the role of noise in shaping wave evolution. These findings are crucial for the development of noise-tolerant optical soliton systems, especially in ultra-fast communication platforms, nonlinear fiber lasers, and integrated photonic circuits.Keywords: Birefringent optical solitons, Stochastic nonlinear Schrödinger framework, Noise-modulated wave propagation, Modified extended mapping schemeSubject terms: Mathematics and computing, Applied mathematics, Pure mathematics

Introduction

The propagation of nonlinear optical waves in structured media has been a cornerstone topic in modern photonics, particularly in the context of soliton dynamics within birefringent fibers. These fibers exhibit directional refractive index dependence, enabling complex vectorial interactions and polarization effects that make them ideal platforms for studying coupled nonlinear wave phenomena1–6.

In high-speed optical environments, wave evolution is not only influenced by deterministic nonlinear effects but also perturbed by inherent noise, such as thermal fluctuations, polarization mode dispersion, or fabrication-induced randomness7–11.

Recent advances in modeling such systems have demonstrated the critical importance of extending beyond traditional cubic and quintic nonlinearities to incorporate higher-order effects for accurate characterization of pulse dynamics in modern optical systems. This progression parallels significant developments in analytical techniques for solving nonlinear PDEs, where methods such as Hirota’s bilinear approach, inverse scattering transform, and variational frameworks have enabled deeper insights into complex wave phenomena. Our work builds upon these methodological advances while introducing novel adaptations to address the challenges posed by septic-order nonlinearities under extreme propagation conditions. The chosen analytical strategy is contextualized within this broader landscape of PDE solution methods, which includes both established and emerging techniques for handling high-dimensional nonlinear systems12–17.

However, real-world photonic systems rarely operate in ideal conditions. Stochastic perturbations, especially those modeled as multiplicative white noise processes, can fundamentally alter wave behavior, affecting phase coherence, amplitude stability, and soliton integrity. This introduces a compelling need for analytical approaches that not only capture the deterministic structure of the underlying nonlinear equations but also accommodate the randomness intrinsic to the physical environment18–22.

Recent advancements in high-speed optical communication systems have intensified the demand for robust analytical and numerical frameworks capable of addressing stochastic perturbations in birefringent fibers. While deterministic soliton dynamics in nonlinear Schrödinger equations (NLSEs) have been extensively studied-particularly in cubic-quintic-septic regimes-the interplay of stochastic effects with higher-order nonlinearities remains underexplored. Prior work on stochastic solitons has primarily focused on simplified cubic NLSE models23,24, often neglecting the combined impact of quintic-septic nonlinearities and multiplicative noise. Recent breakthroughs in stochastic integration algorithms and stability analysis have opened new avenues for modeling wave structure resilience25,26. Our work bridges this gap by deriving exact stochastic soliton solutions and quantifying their stability under birefringent noise, thereby generalizing earlier studies while incorporating modern computational and analytical tools. This approach not only extends the theoretical foundation of noisy soliton propagation but also offers practical insights for designing noise-resilient fiber-optic systems. In this groundbreaking work, we employ the modified extended mapping technique (MEMT) to derive the first complete set of exact analytical solutions for the coupled stochastic cubic-quintic-septic nonlinear Schrödinger equation (CQS-NLSE), overcoming fundamental limitations of previous approaches. Our study makes significant advances beyond the foundational work of AlQahtani et al.27 by extending their deterministic quintic-order model to include both critical septic nonlinearities and stochastic effects through Itô calculus, developing exact closed-form solutions (versus their perturbative approach) that encompass bright, dark and singular solitons, periodic waves, and Weierstrass elliptic functions and providing quantitative analysis of noise resilience that was absent in their framework. Through rigorous symbolic computations and systematic parameter constraints, we obtain multiple families of exact waveforms while fully accounting for stochastic effects. Our comprehensive numerical simulations (Figs. 1, 2, 3) reveal solution-specific noise resilience patterns, offering unprecedented insight into amplitude/phase deformations under varying noise intensities - a crucial practical advancement over27 that enables the design of robust high-power systems. Concluding insights

The present study applied the modified extended mapping method to explore the formation of solitons and obtain analytical waveforms governed by the stochastic CQS-NLSE. Our analysis provided a comprehensive framework for understanding how higher-order nonlinearities (cubic, quintic, septic) and stochastic perturbations influence soliton propagation, stability, and interaction dynamics. We derived bright, dark, and singular soliton solutions, as well as periodic, periodic, and Weierstrass elliptic wave solutions under stochastic noise, demonstrating how septic nonlinearity modifies soliton profiles compared to cubic-quintic cases. This technique proved effective in handling the complex interplay of nonlinearities and stochasticity, offering exact solutions where standard perturbation methods fail.

The results suggest strategies for noise-resistant soliton transmission in high-capacity fiber optics. The model can describe quantum droplets with beyond-cubic nonlinearities in stochastic potentials. We obtained various and novel results compared to the results obtained in27. In case of , The extracted solutions demonstrate a certain balance between dispersion and nonlinearity for the investigated model. This results in solitons that have the advantage of being highly stable as they can travel very long distances while still maintaining their velocity and shape. It has been found that the signal flattens as noise level increases. The technique’s simplicity, effectiveness, and success in application further confirm its reliability and suitability for handling nonlinear optical challenges. These findings may be useful for a better understanding of various phenomena that arise in various physical systems described by the current model, as well as for optical communication.

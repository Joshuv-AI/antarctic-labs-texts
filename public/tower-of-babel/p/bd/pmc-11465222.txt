High-Performance Electrochemical Creatinine Sensors Based on β-Lead Dioxide/Single-Walled Carbon Nanotube Electrodes
Authors: Sung Min Jeon, Han Been Lee, Chang Hyeon Ha, Do Hyeon Kim, Cheng Ai Li, Sung Ho Song, Cheol-Jung Lee, Do Kyoung Han, Gi Hun Seong
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractCreatinine is an important biomarker of kidney function and muscular metabolism. In this paper, we developed the β-lead dioxide/single-walled carbon nanotube (β-PbO2/CNT) and the β-PbO2/CNT ion-selective electrode (β-PbO2/CNT/ISE), which were used as highly sensitive potentiometric sensors for creatinine detection. The fabricated electrodes exhibited highly pH-sensitive characteristics due to the synergistic effect of the electrochemical properties of CNT and β-PbO2. Moreover, an ammonium-ion-selective membrane coating allowed the β-PbO2/CNT electrode to be NH4+-selective for direct detection of the ammonium ion. By exploiting the electrochemical characteristics of these electrodes, the creatinine assay was established through the one-step selective conversion of creatinine by creatinine deiminase, in which the OH– and NH4+ generated by the enzymatic reaction were detected using β-PbO2/CNT and β-PbO2/CNT/ISE electrodes as pH- and NH4+-responsive sensors, respectively. The total creatinine assay can be completed within ∼5 min. The assay results from β-PbO2/CNT and β-PbO2/CNT/ISE showed excellent sensitivity values of −75.56 and 64.62 mV in the detection range of 10–400 μM with a fast response (20 s), and the limits of detection were calculated to be 0.06 and 0.13 μM, respectively. Moreover, the developed creatinine sensor showed high selectivity against 11 interfering bio/chemical species with negligible interferences (selectivity coefficient <10–4) and excellent repeatability (>97% within 25 cycles) and long-term stability for 4 weeks of storage. In addition, the feasibility and practicality of the device were successfully demonstrated in human serum tests, with recoveries of 95–104% for PbO2/CNT and 92–110% for PbO2/CNT/ISE.

Introduction

Kidney disease, also known as renal disease, refers to a condition in which the kidneys are damaged and cannot perform their functions effectively. It can lead to a gradual or acute loss of kidney function, ultimately resulting in renal failure. Kidney disease is a significant healthcare concern due to its prevalence, chronic nature, impact on quality of life, economic burden, and need for specialized care. For this reason, there has been an increasing demand for the rapid and accurate point-of-care (POC) diagnosis of kidney disease to preserve kidney function and minimize the risk of complications associated with renal failure. Creatinine is a waste product generated during creatine metabolism processes in skeletal muscles, which is an important biomarker for assessing kidney function and diagnosing kidney disease. When kidney disease occurs, the serum creatinine level rises because the kidneys no longer work properly for filtering and removing the waste products from the body, whereas the concentration of serum creatinine remains relatively stable under normal conditions. The baseline concentration of serum creatinine is 45–90 μM (0.5–1.0 mg dL–1) in females and 60–110 μM (0.7–1.2 mg dL–1) in males.1 Creatinine assay enables the classification and diagnosis of kidney disease according to the five stages of chronic kidney disease (Normal, Mild, Moderate, Severe, and Kidney Failure) and the RIFLE criteria for acute kidney injury (Risk, Injury, Failure, Loss, and End-Stage Renal Disease), as suggested by The National Kidney Foundation and The Acute Dialysis Quality Initiative.2,3 For both acute and chronic cases, creatinine levels in the Failure stage are three times the normal value or greater than 353.6 μM (4 mg dL–1). In the Loss stage, there is a complete loss of kidney function lasting more than 4 weeks. Therefore, rapid and accurate POC testing (POCT) for creatinine with a detectable range covering the five stages of kidney disease can provide valuable information for physicians and healthcare professionals. This enables researchers to evaluate the extent of kidney damage, monitor disease progression, and make timely decisions regarding treatment and management in the early stages.

The traditional method for creatinine detection is based on the Jaffe reaction in which the colored change generated by creatinine reacting with alkaline picrate is evaluated by colorimetry. The Jaffe method is used widely in both laboratory and clinical diagnosis due to its advantages of simplicity and low cost. However, this method is prone to interference from other substances in the serum sample (such as protein, glucose, ascorbic acid, etc.). Moreover, there are some drawbacks, including tedious pH adjustment, toxic/explosive reagent, and the expense of the spectrometer,4 which limit its applicability in monitoring creatinine for POCT. There are also several methods that have been developed for creatinine detection, such as spectroscopic/colorimetric methods, chromatography, chemiluminescence, and electrochemistry.5−9 Of these approaches, electrochemical sensors have attracted significant attention for creatinine monitoring because of their high sensitivity, simplicity, miniaturization, and portability. However, electrochemical creatinine sensors are mostly based on three multienzyme cascade reactions using creatinine amidohydrolase, creatinine amidinohydrolase, and sarcosine oxidase, which increases the complexity of sensing procedures and the necessity for assistance of professionals.10,11 Therefore, this method still faces challenges for applications in POCT due to the lack of simplicity, reproducibility, and accuracy in measurements. As an alternative method, molecularly imprinted polymer (MIP)-based electrochemical sensors have recently been employed for creatine detection. The MIP method offers enzyme-free approaches using a polymer matrix engineered to specifically recognize and bind to creatinine molecules.12,13 Although MIP-based sensing methods have the advantages of being sensitive, stable, and reusable due to the customizable properties of MIPs, they may suffer from complex and time-consuming synthesis, reproducibility issues, and potential interference from molecules structurally similar to creatinine, leading to batch-to-batch variability and inaccuracy in measurements.

Nanomaterials have been utilized widely for various applications in biosensors owing to their attractive and useful properties. Especially for electrochemical sensors, single-walled carbon nanotubes (SWCNTs) are one of the most interesting materials due to their excellent electrical, mechanical, catalytic, and biocompatible properties.14,15 Based on these remarkable properties, SWCNTs have been used extensively as the sensing materials in a wide range of electrochemical biosensing studies.16−19 Additionally, they have also gained significant attention as an ideal support electrode for the deposition/anchoring of metal(oxide) particles due to their unique properties and attractive interactions between functional groups (e.g., carboxyl and hydroxyl) on CNTs and deposited materials.20−22 Moreover, semiconducting metal oxides have also attracted interest for applications in electrochemical sensors. Metal-oxide-based electrochemical sensors, such as IrO2, RuO2, TiO2, WO3, Ta2O5, ZnO, TiO2, SnO2, etc., offer several benefits including good sensitivity, fast response, long stability, low cost, and easy miniaturization.23−30 Among them, lead oxide (PbO2) is considered to be a promising material for electrochemical applications due to its high electrocatalytic activity and electrical conductivity. Thus, the electrochemical biosensing approaches using PbO2 can provide advantages of high sensitivity and selectivity, ease of fabrication, and chemical stability, allowing accurate and reliable measurements in different environmental and clinical conditions.31,32

Hence, we report a simple and facile electrochemical creatinine sensor for the monitoring of kidney disease. For this study, two types of electrodes, namely, β-formed lead dioxide (β-PbO2)/CNT and β-PbO2/CNT/ISE electrodes, were fabricated on a polyethylene terephthalate (PET) strip together with Ag/AgCl, where the selective creatinine assay was accomplished using a potentiometric strategy based on an enzymatic reaction between creatinine and creatinine deiminase generating the products of NH4+ and OH–. The produced OH– can be measured with β-PbO2/CNT as a pH-responsive electrode, and NH4+ can be measured with β-PbO2/CNT/ISE as an ammonium-ion-selective electrode. The combination of β-PbO2 and CNT allows synergistic leveraging of the electrochemical properties of both materials, leading to more precise and accurate detection with a fast response time for POCT applications.

Experimental Section

Materials and Instruments

A solution of SWCNT (3 mg mL–1) was purchased from TOP NANOSYS (Cheonan, South Korea). Lead(II) nitrate [Pb(NO3)2], nitric acid (HNO3), ammonium ionophore 1, poly(vinyl chloride) (PVC), creatinine, creatinine deiminase, human serum, glucose (Glu), l-ascorbic acid (AA), human serum albumin (HSA), cholesterol (Cho), Trition ×-100, isopropanol, urea, calcium nitrate tetrahydrate [Ca(NO3)2·4H2O], potassium bromide, sodium chloride, sodium hydroxide (NaOH), sodium nitrate, potassium tetrakis(4-chlorophenyl)borate (K-TCBP), and bis(2-ethylhexyl)sebacate (DOS) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA). Phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.4) was purchased from Biosesang Co., Ltd. (Seongnam, South Korea). Tetrahydrofuran (THF) was purchased from Daejung Co., Ltd. (Siheung, South Korea). Ag/AgCl paste was purchased from Gwent Electronic Materials Ltd. (Derbyshire, UK). Acetone, ethanol, pH buffer solution, and magnesium sulfate were purchased from Samchun Chemical Co., Ltd. (Pyeongtaek, South Korea). Creatinine assay kit was purchased from Abcam (Cambridge, UK). All chemicals used were of reagent grade, and aqueous solutions were prepared with double-distilled water (ddH2O). Lithium iodide, an Orion PerpHecT ROSS Combination pH Micro Electrode, and an Orion Lab Star PH111 pH/mV Bench Meter were purchased from ThermoFisher SCIENTIFIC (Waltham, MA, USA). Hydrophilic half adhesion tape was purchased from TWO-HANDS Co., Ltd. (Anseong, South Korea). Anodic aluminum oxide (AAO) membranes, 47 mm and 0.02 μm pore size, were purchased from Cytiva (Marlborough, MA, USA). A Micro Laser Machine (C40 Laser Machinery 60 W; Coryart; Anyang, South Korea) was used for the tailored manufacturing of strip-type electrodes. A surface resistance meter (MCP-T610; Mitsubishi Chemical Corporation; Tokyo, Japan), field emission scanning electron microscopy (SEM; S-s800; Hitachi; Tokyo, Japan), and X-ray diffractometer (XRD; D/MAX-2500; Rigaku, USA) were used for electrical, morphological, structural, and chemical characterization. Electrochemical synthesis and measurements were performed using an electrochemical analyzer (CHI 660C; CH Instruments Inc.; Austin, TX, USA).

Preparation of SWCNT Film

To fabricate a homogeneous CNT film, the vacuum filtration method was used (Figure S1).33 Briefly, the SWCNT solution (0.3 mg mL–1) was sonicated for 1 h. A 400 μL volume of the SWCNT solution was diluted with 100 mL of ddH2O and filtered through an AAO membrane with a 0.2 μm pore size. To remove the AAO beneath the SWCNT layer, the membrane was placed on the 3 M NaOH solution. After 2 h, the AAO precipitate was removed and the pH of the solution was adjusted to a neutral pH with ddH2O. The remaining SWCNT layer was then transferred to a PET polymer film and heat-treated at 90 °C for 4 h to achieve strong adhesion between the SWCNT layer and PET film substrate.

Fabrication of β-PbO2/CNT and β-PbO2/CNT/ISE Electrodes

A schematic of the manufacturing process is provided in Figure 1. The SWCNT electrode was constructed by attaching a hydrophilic plastic substrate to the front of the SWCNT film with a 2 mm diameter hole for the sensing area (working electrode), and Ag/AgCl paste was coated on the back of the SWCNT for the reference electrode. The prepared device exhibited simple strip-shaped features with an overall size of 3.5 × 4.0 mm. Then, the β-PbO2 was electrodeposited on the sensing area of the device by cyclic voltammetry (CV), applying 15 cycles from 1.0 to 2.0 V at a scan rate of 0.1 V s–1 in 20 mM Pb(NO3)2 solution containing 0.1 M HNO3 (pH 1) (Figure S2).31 As a result, the sensing area appeared to be brown in color. This electrodeposition method for β-PbO2 was also adapted to conventional carbon paste electrodes (CPEs), which were tested in parallel to compare the sensing performances.

Schematic illustration of the fabrication procedure and sensing mechanism. (A,B) Construction of creatinine sensors based on CNT, β-PbO2/CNT, and β-PbO2/CNT/ISE. (C) Depiction of two strategies for creatinine detection using our sensors.

In another approach, the above fabricated device was additionally modified by an ion-selective membrane (ISM) for NH4+ sensing. The ammonium ion ISM cocktail contains 2.4 mg of ammonium ionophore 1, 65.2 mg of PVC, 131.2 mg of DOS, and 1.2 mg of K-TCBP in 1.5 mL of THF solution, where the PVC and DOS (plasticizer) enable the ISM to better conform and adhere to the surface through mechanical interlocking.34−37 A 10 μL volume of ISM cocktail was then dropped onto the sensing area of the β-PbO2/CNT and dried at room temperature for 10 min to evaporate THF completely.

Electrochemical Characterization and Creatinine Determination

The electrochemical behavior of β-PbO2/CNT and β-PbO2/CNT/ISE as pH- and NH4+-responsive electrodes was characterized by potentiometric methods, measuring the open circuit potential (OCP) of electrodes in solutions with different values of pH and NH4+. The potential shifts between the working electrode (β-PbO2/CNT or β-PbO2/CNT/ISE) and reference electrode (Ag/AgCl) on each device were evaluated to determine how effectively they worked for pH and NH4+ sensing.

For the creatinine assay, the human serum was tested on this device without any pretreatment. The serum samples were spiked with different concentrations of creatinine and then mixed with a test buffer solution (1 mM PBS) containing creatinine deiminase (5 U mL–1) in a 1:5 v/v ratio. After 5 min of incubation at 37 °C, 100 μL volumes of sample with creatinine levels of 10, 25, 50, and 70 μM were tested on β-PbO2/CNT- and β-PbO2/CNT/ISE-based sensors, respectively. For potentiometric analysis, the OCP was measured to determine the potential difference corresponding to the creatinine concentration in the sample. To ensure the reliability of the results, the mean value obtained from 15 to 20 s was evaluated.

Results and Discussion

Characterization of Three Types of Fabricated Electrodes (CNT, β-PbO2/CNT, and β-PbO2/CNT/ISE)

A CNT film with a thickness of ∼100 nm was fabricated on the PET substrate, which provided high conductivity with an average resistivity of 400 Ω sq–1. The SEM image shows the ordered nanostructure of SWCNTs and the homogeneous interconnection between them on the PET substrate (Figure 2A). For fabrication of the β-PbO2/CNT composite, the β-PbO2 was electrodeposited on the SWCNT electrode by applying CVs. The number of CV cycles was varied (5–30 cycles) to examine its effect on the deposition process. It was found that 15 cycles produced the highest surface uniformity and homogeneity with a thickness of approximately 350 nm, exhibiting superior pH-responsive characteristics (Figure S3). After deposition, the sensing area of the SWCNT electrode became brown in color. In Figure 2B, the SEM image shows that the surface of β-PbO2/CNT was completely coated with β-PbO2. The EDX spectrum demonstrates the presence of Pb, O, and C in β-PbO2/CNT, where the weight percentages of Pb, O, and C were 84.89, 12.31, and 2.80%, and the atomic percentages were 29.02, 54.50, and 16.48%, respectively (Figure S6A). This indicates that the ratio of Pb to O (atomic percent) was close to 1:2, revealing that β-PbO2 was successfully deposited. However, in the case of CPE, the PbO2 appeared not only to be structurally uneven but also to be heterogeneously deposited on CPE (Figure S5). This reveals that CNT electrodes can act as the preferable support electrode to assist in the deposition of β-PbO2 due to their large active surface area as well as an effective electron transfer rate.38

SEM image of (A) CNT, (B) β-PbO2/CNT, (C) β-PbO2/CNT/ISE, and (D) XRD pattern of β-PbO2/CNT.

For further investigation, XRD analysis was performed to confirm the structure of PbO2 because PbO2 can exist in different phases (α and β form). The crystal structure of electrodeposited PbO2 on the SWCNT film was characterized by XRD using Cu Kα radiation (λ = 1.5418 Å) at 40 kV and 200 mA. The XRD patterns were recorded over a 2θ range of 20–70° with a scanning rate of 4° min–1. As shown in Figure 2D, peaks in the XRD pattern were observed at 2θ = 25.9, 32.06, 35.94, 48.94, 53.92, and 60.24°, corresponding to (110), (101), (200), (211), (220), and (301) crystal planes of β-PbO2 (JCPDS no. 760564).39,40 β-PbO2 has advantages of high electrical conductivity (∼10 times greater) and chemical stability over α-PbO2.32,41 In practice, the average resistivity of β-PbO2/CNT was improved to 125 Ω sq–1, which is about three times lower than that of SWCNT alone, making it a more favorable electrode for electrochemical biosensing.39

In a different approach, the β-PbO2/CNT/ISE was prepared by simply dropping and evaporating the ISM cocktail on β-PbO2/CNT. As shown in the SEM image (Figure 2C), the surface of β-PbO2/CNT/ISE was completely modified with ISM, which exhibited a three-dimensional porous structure with an ∼5 μm pore size. Three types of fabricated electrodes (CNT, β-PbO2/CNT, and β-PbO2/CNT/ISE) exhibited a high degree of flexibility with negligible change in conductivity, even under severe bending.

pH-Responsive Electrochemical Behavior of β-PbO2/CNT-Based Sensor

The concentration of OH– and NH4+ ions change in proportion to the amount of creatinine.43,44 In practice, using a commercial pH meter, it was observed that the pH varied from 6 to 9 depending on the creatinine concentration, which ranged from 0 to 400 μM (Figure S4). Based on this enzymatic reaction, two strategies for creatinine assay were established on the prepared sensors (Figure 1C). In the first strategy, the β-PbO2/CNT was employed as a pH-responsive electrode, which measured the pH change resulting from OH– ions generated by an enzymatic reaction with creatinine. For pH-responsive characterization, the electrochemical performances of CNT, β-PbO2, and β-PbO2/CNT were investigated using buffer solutions with varied pH (6–9) by the OCP method. As shown in Figure 3A, the slope of the calibration curve for β-PbO2/CNT is −47.99 mV pH–1 (R2 = 0.99, n = 3), which is higher and closer to the ideal Nernst response of 59.24 mV than those of CNT (−18.58 mV pH–1) and β-PbO2/CPE (−32.50 mV pH–1) (Figure S7).

(A) Calibration curves of CNT and β-PbO2/CNT electrodes in different pH buffer solutions (n = 3). (B) Calibration curve of β-PbO2/CNT/ISE ammonium selective electrode in different NH4+ ion concentrations (n = 3).

CNT and β-PbO2 are well-known as pH-responsive materials. They can act as a selective working electrode for potentiometric pH analysis, where pH changes are evaluated by the potential difference between the working and reference electrode (Ag/AgCl). In CNT and β-PbO2, the electrochemical behaviors toward pH appear similar, but the sensing mechanisms are different. The pH-responsive electrochemical characteristic of CNT is attributed to a Fermi level shift caused by the doping of H+ and OH– ions to SWCNT as electron acceptors and donors.45,46 The increases in H+ and OH– ions cause changes in the electronic structure (first interband transition S11) of semiconducting SWCNTs, resulting in down- and upshifts, respectively, of the Fermi levels of SWCNTs. Therefore, the electrochemical potential of the SWCNT relative to the standard hydrogen electrode is inversely proportional to the pH change according to the following equation: E(electrochemical potential) = Φ(work function)/e − 4.44 V.

In the case of PbO2, pH-responsive electrochemical behavior is attributed to ion exchange of the PbO2 surface, causing the redox reaction containing formation of higher and lower valency PbO2 as follows:23,31 PbO2 + H+ + e– ⇌ PbOOH. This indicates that the magnitude of the potential is proportional to the logarithmic concentration of the H+ ion (inversely proportional to the pH of the solution).

NH4+-Responsive Electrochemical Behavior of β-PbO2/CNT/ISE-Based Sensor

In the second strategy for creatinine assay, the β-PbO2/CNT/ISE-containing ISM was applied as an NH4+-responsive electrode, which measured the change in the concentration of the NH4+ ion generated by enzymatic reaction with creatinine. The sensing mechanism of the β-PbO2/CNT/ISE is based on the formation of an electrical double layer at the interface between the ISM and electrode.34 The ammonium ionophore (nonactin) in the ISM recognizes the target (NH4+) ion and binds to NH4+ ions present in the solution, thereby facilitating the transport of H+ ions across the membrane. The target ions passing through the ISM are accumulated, leading to the formation of an electrical double layer at the interface. The consequence of charge separation between different phases creates an electric field at the interface, giving rise to an electrochemical potential, which is measured by an OCP paired with an Ag/AgCl reference electrode. For NH4+-responsive characterization, the electrochemical performance of β-PbO2/CNT/ISE was investigated by OCP in the range of 0–400 μM NH4Cl solution. In Figure 3B, the β-PbO2/CNT/ISE exhibits a proportional relationship between potential and logarithmic concentration of the NH4+ ion with a slope of 57.20 mV (R2 = 0.99, n = 3), which is close to a Nernst response. This indicates that the change in NH4+ concentration via the enzymatic reaction with creatinine can be determined by β-PbO2/CNT/ISE sensors.

Potentiometric Detection of Creatinine Using β-PbO2/CNT- and β-PbO2/CNT/ISE-Based Sensors

Figure 4 shows the potential responses of β-PbO2/CNT and β-PbO2/CNT/ISE sensors after the addition of creatinine solutions with concentrations ranging from 10 to 400 μM. For each test, 50 μL of creatinine was mixed into 450 μL of test buffer to achieve final concentrations of 10, 25, 50, 100, 200, 300, and 400 μM, followed by being applied onto the β-PbO2/CNT sensor. Then, the OCP was performed to measure the potential difference corresponding to creatinine concentration in the sample. Both sensors exhibited a rapid response to creatinine solutions, yielding a steady-state signal within 10 s. An average value between 15 and 20 s was evaluated for reliable tests. In Figure 4A, the β-PbO2/CNT sensor shows a super-Nernstian response to creatinine concentration with a regression equation of y = −78.56x + 524.53 (R2 = 0.99, n = 3) at 25 °C. The limit of detection (LOD) of this sensor was estimated to be 0.06 μM based on 3σ/s (σ is a standard deviation of the blank response and s is the slope of the calibration curve). These results reveal that the β-PbO2/CNT sensor can enable rapid, one-step potentiometric detection of creatinine without the need for an additional ISM coating, offering excellent sensitivity, a low LOD, and a broad detection range for monitoring kidney disease. On the other hand, the β-PbO2/CPE sensor exhibited a sub-Nernstian response with the regression equation of y = −25.93x + 68.24 (R2 = 0.99, n = 3), and the LOD value was calculated to be 0.56 μM (Figure S8). This reveals that the sensitivity and LOD of β-PbO2/CNT are, respectively, 3- and 10-fold as great as those of β-PbO2/CPE, which may be attributed to outstanding electrochemical and biocompatible properties of the sensor due to the synergistic effect of CNT and β-PbO2. In another approach, the potential responses of the β-PbO2/CNT/ISE sensor were investigated in the same way (Figure 4B). The results exhibited near-Nernstian response with the regression equation of y = 64.62x + 151.46 (R2 = 0.99, n = 3), and the LOD value was found to be 0.13 μM. Compared to the β-PbO2/CNT sensor, the sensitivity and LOD of β-PbO2/CNT/ISE was slightly lower, but good enough for creatinine determination in terms of detection range and LOD. Interference Test

To verify the selectivity of the creatinine measurement, several interfering bio/chemical species, including glucose, ascorbic acid, cholesterol, urea, HSA, Ca2+, Mg2+, Li+, K+, Na+, and Cl– were examined on β-PbO2/CNT and β-PbO2/CNT/ISE sensors. In Figure 5A,B, the five interfering biomolecules, which correspond to normal levels in human serum, were tested to determine the selectivity toward creatinine. The negligible interference effect (<10.0%) of interfering biomolecules was observed in comparison to creatinine, indicating high selectivity to creatinine. Furthermore, Table 1 displays the selectivity coefficients of two types of sensors that were calculated using the mixed two solution method (TSM).47 The resulting selectivity coefficients (<10–4) revealed a good selectivity against common interfering ions (six species), considering that an acceptable range of selectivity coefficients is typically less than 10–2 for ion-selective electrodes in the reported literature.48 These results for selectivity indicate that the β-PbO2/CNT and β-PbO2/CNT/ISE sensors have good specificity to creatinine in the presence of typical bio- and chemical interferants, making them more applicable to clinical testing.

Repeatability and Long-Term Stability Tests of Creatinine Sensors

Figure 5C,D shows the sensing performances of the β-PbO2/CNT and β-PbO2/CNT/ISE sensors for creatinine detection under repeated measurements and washing cycles. In the case of the β-PbO2/CNT, the resulting potential values maintained up to 97% in 25 cycles compared to the first measurement, demonstrating the repeatability and robustness of the β-PbO2/CNT sensor. For the β-PbO2/CNT/ISE, the resulting potential value at the 25th cycle was reduced by about 90%, which may be attributed to the ISM layer being sloughed off gradually during repeated washing cycles. To determine the long-term stability of sensors, the storage-time-dependent alterations of resulting potential values were also investigated in the presence of 100 μM creatinine, which is within the physiological level in normal serum. Both sensors exhibited very stable potentiometric responses during the extended experiments. After 4 weeks of storage at 25 °C, it was found that analytical performances retained more than 95% of their initial potential value (Figure 5E,F). These test results reveal that our sensors can provide not only good repeatability and reproducibility under several repetition cycles but also high stability and robustness for long storage.

Recovery Test

To confirm the feasibility of the β-PbO2/CNT and β-PbO2/CNT/ISE sensors for complex biological samples, human serum was applied in a real sample test. The creatinine samples (10, 25, 50, 70 μM) were spiked in the human serum and then mixed with the test buffer in the ratio of 1–5 for recovery tests. The recovery performances of two types of sensors were compared to the commercial creatinine assay kit (as a reference method) by calculating the percentage similarity (PS).49,50

As summarized in Table 2, our sensors allow the accurate and precise determination of creatinine in serum with recoveries in the range of 95–104% for β-PbO2/CNT and 92–110% for β-PbO2/CNT/ISE, which meet the acceptable recovery error range of 80–110% suggested by the FDA guideline.51 Moreover, the PS values between our sensors and the commercial kit reached 100 ± 5%, demonstrating good agreement between both methods. Thus, these results indicate that the β-PbO2/CNT and β-PbO2/CNT/ISE sensors have good reliability and feasibility for the detection of creatinine in real samples.

Conclusions

In summary, potentiometric creatinine sensors based on β-PbO2/CNT and β-PbO2/CNT/ISE were developed for rapid and low-cost diagnosis of kidney diseases. Due to the synergistic effect of the combination of CNT and PbO2, two types of sensors allow a super-Nernstian potentiometric response and negligible interference (selectivity coefficient <10–4), leading to the accurate and selective detection of creatinine with a fast response time (20 s). The developed sensors exhibited excellent sensitivity values of −75.56 and 64.62 mV and LODs of 0.06 and 0.13 μM for β-PbO2/CNT and β-PbO2/CNT/ISE, respectively. In particular, it was found that the β-PbO2/CNT sensor possesses high feasibility and practicality, with a recovery of 95–104%, excellent repeatability (>97% within 25 cycles) and long-term stability (>95%) for 4 weeks storage. Compared to other methods for creatinine detection, our devices exhibited better or comparable performances with the benefits of portability and reusability. These results revealed the reliability and applicability of our sensor for creatinine detection in POC applications. Based on these advantages, we believe that the proposed sensors can serve as useful POCT devices capable of facilitating more regular monitoring of renal healthcare, which would be helpful in early identification and management of kidney problems.

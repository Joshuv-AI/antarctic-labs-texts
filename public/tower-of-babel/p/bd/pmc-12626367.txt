Anti-Mi-2 Antibodies Wong Type Dermatomyositis with Columnar Dyskeratosis
Authors: Elena Bularca, Mar García-García, Pablo Villagrasa-Boli, Adela Marín-Ballvé, Sonia de-la-Fuente-Meira
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

Dermatomyositis (DM) is an autoimmune myopathy with pathognomonic skin lesions that are immediately identifiable. However, patients present rare, atypical lesions.

A 76-year-old Caucasian male presented with scalp and metacarpophalangeal joints erythema which started 4 months earlier. Subsequently, he developed intense extremity muscle weakness and gradual weight loss. Examination revealed erythematous and violaceous plaques with a “shawl” distribution, as well as on the abdominal region and lower extremities. Additionally, he presented Gottron papules, cuticular hyperkeratosis, erythema and periungual telangiectasias [Figure 1a and b].

(a and b) Shawl sign, violaceous plaques on neckline and Gottron papules. (c and d) White hyperkeratotic papules on the metacarpophalangeal joints and lateral fingers

Skin biopsy supported the diagnosis of DM [Figure 2a and b]. Laboratory tests showed elevated creatine phosphokinase 1515 U/L (39–308 U/L), aldolase 25.6 UI/L (1–7.6 UI/L) and beta-2-microglobulin 4.25 mcg/ml 4 (1.09–2.53 mcg/ml) levels; positive antinuclear antibodies (ANA) +1/1280 and positive anti-Mi-2 antibodies, with normal complement. Capillaroscopy showed microhemorrhages, megacapillaries and neoangiogenesis with decreased capillary flow. An electromyogram found signs of inflammatory myopathy. A muscle biopsy did not evidence myositis.

(a) HE 2×: Skin punch with interphase lesion and perivascular component of lymphohistiocytic predominance. (b) HE 20×: At higher magnification, detail of the interphase lesion, with a band-like infiltrate and lichenoid component with necrotic keratinocytes (*). (c) HE, 2×: Skin punch with follicular dilatation. (d and e) HE, 10 and 20×: Detail of the dilated follicle. Note the presence of columnar parakeratosis, in the absence of granular cells. Dyskeratotic elements are also observed

One month later, white hyperkeratotic papules developed on the dorsum of the hands and fingers [Figure 1c and d]. A second skin biopsy revealed follicular plugs and columnar dyskeratosis in the absence of other histopathological criteria of DM [Figure 2c-e]. Positron emission computed tomography screening for malignancy was negative. Methylprednisolone boluses were administered with posterior corticosteroid tapering. Finally, treatment with mycophenolate mofetil was started with favorable evolution. Therefore, the diagnosis of Wong-type DM was established.

Wong-type DM is a rare variant of DM described by O’Leary and Christianson in the 1950s and later by Wong in 1960.[1] Most patients develop confluent orange plaques, islands of spared skin, follicular hyperkeratotic papules and palmoplantar hyperkeratosis. Since 1960, 26 more cases have been reported in the literature, both in children and adults, with a mean age of 40 years [Table 1]. Atypical lesions can appear before (38,46% of reported cases), simultaneously (38,46%) or after (23,08%) the diagnosis of DM. Histopathological differential diagnosis includes disorders with follicular plugging or columnar hyperkeratosis that may hide underlying DM such as pityriasis rubra pilaris, cutaneous lupus erythematosus, keratosis pilaris or porokeratosis. First described in Wong-type DM in 2015,[2] columnar dyskeratosis or pseudocornoid lamella presents with epidermal invaginations and keratotic plugs containing scattered dyskeratotic cells throughout the epidermis and irregular columns of parakeratosis within the stratum corneum.

Wong-type DM is not associated with any characteristic biochemical or autoimmune feature. ANA antibodies were positive in 40% of reported cases, while six cases showed specific antibodies. Two patients developed clinically relevant interstitial lung disease.[34]

There is no evidence regarding the association between Wong-type DM and malignancy. Originally, 52% of patients reported in Wong’s series, which included classic DM and Wong-type DM, presented malignancies. Since then, four cancer-related cases have been published, including metastatic appendicular mucinous adenocarcinoma,[5] endometrial carcinoma,[6] fallopian tube carcinoma[7] and metastatic renal carcinoma in treatment with immunotherapy.[8]

In conclusion, we present a case of a rare subtype of DM, in which knowledge avoids misdiagnosis and leads to early and adequate treatment of the disease.

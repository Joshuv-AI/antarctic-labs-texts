Chain mediating role of moral values identification and positive psychological capital in the relationship between meaning in life and crisis vulnerability
Authors: Qin Liu, Renjie Chang, Shengju Fang, Junjie Peng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
To explore the relationship between meaning in life and vulnerability to crisis among undergraduate nursing students and the chain mediating effect between moral values identification and positive psychological capital (PsyCap). A total of 330 undergraduate nursing students majoring in traditional Chinese medicine in ethnic minority areas of Yunnan Province were administered the Meaning in Life Questionnaire, Moral Values Identification Identity Questionnaire, Positive PsyCap Questionnaire, and Crisis Vulnerability Scale. Meaning in life was negatively correlated with crisis vulnerability and positively correlated with moral values identification and positive PsyCap. Crisis vulnerability was negatively correlated with moral values and positive PsyCap. Moral values was positively correlated with positive PsyCap. Chain mediation effect analysis demonstrated that meaning in life cannot directly predict crisis vulnerability, but it can predict crisis vulnerability through 3 indirect paths: the mediating role of moral values identification, the mediating role of positive PsyCap, and the chain mediating role of moral values identification and positive PsyCap. Meaning in life can indirectly reduce crisis vulnerability among undergraduate nursing students by enhancing moral values identification and positive PsyCap.

1. Introduction

At present, owing to intensifying social competition and employment pressures, the mental health problems of college students are becoming increasingly prominent, which is alarming.[1] In December 2019, the outbreak of Corona Virus Disease 2019 created an unprecedented impact on higher education in China. During the period of normalized epidemic prevention and control, some university campuses implemented the management model of “not leaving the school unless necessary,” while information overload on the pandemic and public opinion exerted a negative impact on the psychology of contemporary college students.[2] On September 11, 2020, the National Health Commission of China released a work plan on its official website, requiring all departments to undertake effective measures to decrease the vulnerability of college students to crises and comprehensively improve their mental health.

Meaning in life, a positive psychological capital (PsyCap) resource that has been receiving increasing scholarly attention[3], contains 2 independent dimensions: the possession of meaning in life and the pursuit of this meaning.[4] Meaning in life is an extremely subjective perception. When a person has a purpose, their perceived meaning and value in life may increase.[5] Meaning in life pertains to the intrinsic value of life and the feeling of having something worth living[6]; moreover, a meaningful life is better, rarer, and more inspiring than a happy life.[7] At the same time, individuals with a strong sense of meaning in life exhibit high levels of hope, whereas those with a weak meaning easily experience boredom and emptiness and may display various addictive behaviors.[8–10] Yang Xiong[11] has explored the thinking style and values of “millennial” college students and found that they possess a broader horizon, a wider range of interests and hobbies, and more diverse and compatible ideas compared with older generations. While these students are guided by mainstream culture and societal values, they are also constantly forming new values in line with their characteristics and those of the times. In the past, several studies have explored the relationship between factors such as mindfulness, PsyCap, self-esteem, and gratitude as well as the mediating role of meaning in life in the reduction of crisis vulnerability. However, the potential mechanism of the chain mediating effect of moral values identification and positive PsyCap on meaning in life and crisis vulnerability is poorly understood. Thus, the current study used 4 scales to explore the impact of meaning in life on the crisis vulnerability of undergraduate nursing students. By constructing a chain mediation model, the study found that meaning in life is influenced by 3 indirect paths: the independent role of moral values identification, the independent role of positive PsyCap and the combined role of moral values identification and positive PsyCap. The results demonstrated that the external factor of meaning in life indirectly influences the crisis vulnerability of nursing students through 2 internal factors: moral values identification and positive PsyCap.

2. Subject and methods

2.1. Subject

This study used simple random stratified sampling to recruit 330 first-, second-, and third-year undergraduate nursing students at a college of medicine in western China. Data of the participants were collected online and anonymously using the Questionnaire Star software. Four invalid responses (e.g., same answers provided for multiple consecutive questions) were excluded, leaving a total of 326 valid questionnaires (men: 57; women: 269). The general questionnaire comprised 5 parts. The first part collected socio-demographic information of nursing students, such as gender, age, grade, ethnicity, type of household registration, whether they are an only child, whether they identify with their profession, socio-economic status, monthly living expenses, and family relationships, which were self-reported and anonymously recorded. The rest of the questionnaire contained 4 scales (Chinese versions) for assessment and diagnosis: Meaning In Life Questionnaire, Moral Values Identity Scale, Positive PsyCap Questionnaire and the Crisis Vulnerability Scale. The 4 scales were standardized and deemed suitable for the Chinese population with high reliability and validity, as detailed below.

2.2. Research tools

2.2.1. Meaning In Life Questionnaire

This scale was revised by Liu Sisi and Gan Yiqun,[12] with a total of 9 items. The Cronbach α coefficient of the scale is 0.833. The items are rated using a seven-point Likert-type scale (1 = strongly disagree; 7 = strongly agree). The higher the total score, the higher is the level of meaning in life.

2.2.2. Moral Values Identification Questionnaire

According to the 3 advocacy of moral values proposed by the 18th Congress of the Communist Party of China and the principle of questionnaire compilation,[13] the Cronbach α coefficient of the scale was 0.909. The compilation of the questionnaire on the moral valued identification of undergraduate nursing students mainly focused on the 3 dimensions of national, social, and personal value identities. Each dimension has 4 indicators, making a total of 12 indicators for examining students’ views on 12 words related to moral values. The items are rated using a five-point Likert-type scale (5 = strongly agree; 1 = strongly disagree).

2.2.3. Positive PsyCap Questionnaire

The Positive PsyCap Questionnaire was developed by Zhang et al[14] in 2010 and included 26 items across the 4 dimensions of self-efficacy, resilience, hope, and optimism. The items are rated using a seven-point Likert-type scale, with higher scores indicating stronger positive mental abilities of participants. The Cronbach α coefficient of this questionnaire is 0.875.

2.2.4. Crisis Vulnerability Scale

The scale was developed by psychologist G. Parry[15] and has a Cronbach α coefficient of 0.785. The scale is composed of 15 items rated using a three-point scale (2 = uncertain, 1 = yes, 0 = no). The higher the total score, the higher is the level of crisis vulnerability.

2.3. Statistical analysis

First, SPSS 23.0 was used for descriptive and correlation statistics, Harman factor analysis for determining common method bias and Pearson correlation for exploring correlations between major variables. Second, the mediation effect was examined using Hayes[16] PROCESS macro (model 4). Finally, the full model was evaluated using Hayes’ PROCESS macro (model 59). Socio-demographic data were described and compared using an independent-sample t test and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). When ANOVA results were significant, Bonferroni corrections were applied to multiple tests.

3. Results

3.1. Common method bias

As this study uses 4 self-reported scales for psychometrics, there may be common methodological biases. Therefore, the Harman single-factor test was used to analyze whether a common method bias existed in the questionnaire. Exploratory factor analysis of all scale items demonstrated that 26 factors exhibited feature roots >1 (63 factors in total) and the explanation rate of the first common factor was 21.588%, which was lower than the 40% judgement standard proposed by Podsakoff et al.[16,17] Thus, the effect of common method bias could be excluded from the study results.

3.4. Meaning in life and crisis vulnerability: result of the chain mediating effect of moral values identification and positive PsyCap

After normalizing the study variables, the SPSS plug-in PROCESS provided by Hayes 2013[16] was used on model 6. Multivariate level regression analysis was conducted with meaning in life as the independent variable, crisis vulnerability as the dependent variable, moral values identification and positive PsyCap as the chain mediating variables and 4 variables (i.e., grade, socio-economic status, professional identity, and family relationship) as the control variables.

Table 2 implied that meaning in life could significantly predict moral values (β = 0.23, P < .001) and meaning in life and moral values could significantly predict positive PsyCap (β = 1.08, 0.34, Ps < 0.001). Meaning in life, moral values identification and positive PsyCap could significantly predict crisis vulnerability (β = −0.12, −0.02, −0.11, P < .001, 0.05, and 0.001, respectively). After adding moral values and positive PsyCap to the model, the impact of meaning in life on crisis vulnerability remains significant, indicating that moral values and positive PsyCap partially mediate the impact of meaning in life on crisis vulnerability. 4. Discussion

Based on the theory of moral development, moral values involve individuals’ moral judgement, code of conduct, and value orientation. In contrast, positive psychology focuses on individuals’ strengths and potential, both of which are closely related to meaning in life and crisis coping ability. There are significant differences in moral values and positive PsyCap among individuals, which may affect individuals’ meaning in life and vulnerability to crises. Therefore, it can be speculated that the internalization and practice of moral values enhances meaning in life and reduces crisis vulnerability by boosting positive PsyCap. Based on this notion, this study hypothetically investigated the relationship between meaning in life and vulnerability to crisis among undergraduate nursing students in a university of traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) in a border province of China, aiming to explore the potential chain mediating effect mechanism that promotes the association between meaning in life and crisis vulnerability.

4.1. Relationship between meaning in life and crisis vulnerability

This study found that the frequency of psychological crises among the participants was relatively high and their tolerance to crises was poor. This frequency is much higher than that observed by Zheng Yanan et al[18]: “35.67% of college students have poor crisis tolerance.” This difference may be attributed to the different sampling method used in the present study. Nursing students are different from nonmedical students in that they undertake more compulsory courses with heavier and more stressful coursework.[19] Therefore, they are at a higher risk of psychological crises; moreover, negative emotions such as burnout, depression, and anxiety[20] are inconducive to the cultivation of crisis tolerance and meaning in life. This study suggests that all social actors should pay attention to the various psychological crises caused by multiple forms of stress to enhance the nursing students’ understanding of health-related knowledge.

The results suggest that meaning in life exerts a significant negative impact on the crisis vulnerability of undergraduate nursing students, which is consistent with the results of previous studies.[20] The sense of meaning in life plays an important role in psychological function and physical and mental health.[21] People with high levels of sense of meaning in life tend to exhibit high levels of subjective well-being, life satisfaction and optimism in the face of difficulties and setbacks and are full of hope for the future.[22–25] These qualities can enhance adaptability and coping strategies and effectively alleviate depression and hopelessness. It is also an important resource for coping with stress,[26,27] which helps in the maintenance of mental resilience during challenges. Previous research, such as Frankl logotherapy and other empirical studies,[20,28,29] has provided theoretical and empirical support for this view.

Maintaining high levels of sense of meaning in life can promote mental health, which is associated with hope and is a buffer for depression.[30] Especially among adolescents, meaning in life is considered an effective positive psychological resource, which can help individuals resist external stressors and decrease problematic psychology and behaviors.[31] In specific contexts, such as the Corona Virus Disease 2019 pandemic, the sense of meaning in life also exerts a positive impact on mental health.[32] As such, lack of meaning in life can hinder self-identity and is associated with anxiety, depression, even suicide risk and low levels of self-esteem.[33] This study demonstrate that meaning in life among the participants was slightly lower than the median level, which indicates that participants may feel that their purpose, goal and meaning in life are currently unclear or they may feel a certain degree of distress and uncertainty. The low level of meaning in life may be caused by various factors such as academic pressure, professional identity, values, social support, personal development and educational training. However, the current study confirms that professional identity, family economic level and family relationship exerted a significant impact on the level of meaning in life.

Therefore, schools and educational institutions can provide positive publicity and career development guidance about the nursing profession to help nursing students understand the value and prospects of the profession and clarify career goals and development directions. At the same time, they can share certain financial resources to help them plan their financial situation. They can also provide appropriate guidance on family relationships, encourage students to communicate effectively with family members, enhance understanding and support between family members and boost nursing students’ sense of meaning in life to reduce their crisis vulnerability.

4.2. Mediating role of moral values

Moral values play an important mediating role in the relationship between meaning in life and crisis vulnerability. The results of this study validate the positive role of moral values identification, which is consistent with the conclusions of previous research.[34]

Moral values can provide individuals with value orientation,[35] which can influence their moral judgements and behavioral choices. When individuals have a sense of meaning in life, they are motivated to make better behavioral choices, establishing a stronger self-identity and closer social connections, which in turn leads to the formation of more pro-social and altruistic moral values. Alternatively, moral values may help individuals improve their crisis coping strategies, enhance their social connections, obtain additional support and resources, strengthen psychological resilience,[36,37] maintain positive attitudes and resilience during crises, exhibit low vulnerability to crisis and better cope with life’s challenges.

When nursing students are under pressure or facing crises, those who possess high levels of meaning in life typically display a deep sense of professional mission and responsibility in moral values and a clear understanding of the values and goals of nursing work. As such, they make judgements and decisions that align with nursing ethics in the face of moral dilemmas. At the same time, meaning in life can enhance empathy and altruism, such that individuals are more inclined to consider the interest and well-being of patients in nursing practice and exhibit high levels of ethical care. In addition, this sense of meaning enhances their self-efficacy and mental resilience, enabling them to adhere to their ethics, maintain a positive professional attitude, grow personally and professionally and demonstrate highly ethical behavior in the face of work pressures and challenges. Thus, moral values play a mediating role in the impact of meaning in life on crisis vulnerability.

4.3. Mediating role of positive PsyCap

Positive PsyCap is a mediating factor in the relationship between meaning in life and crisis vulnerability. This study found an inverse relationship between positive PsyCap and crisis vulnerability, which is consistent with the findings of Lin et al.[38]

Positive PsyCap is a protective factor of mental health and helps reduce negative emotions caused by negative life events, thereby decreasing vulnerability to crises. During university years, undergraduate nursing students have not yet formed a complete cognitive system and their levels of positive PsyCap are easily affected by their personality traits, school educational environment, learning pressure, social culture, career planning, and relationships with peers. Therefore, helping students gradually establish a positive psychological quality and cultivate high levels of positive PsyCap is crucial during this critical period of physical and mental development.

The results of this study demonstrate that the levels of positive PsyCap of the participants differ by year level, which is consistent with the results of previous studies.[39–41] Various individual and social factors, psychological states, and environmental factors may exert an impact on positive PsyCap. A strong positive association is found between sense of meaning and positive PsyCap, which is consistent with the findings of other studies.[42] Nursing students exhibit different levels of sense of meaning in life, which exert different effects on psychology and behavior. Nursing students with high levels of sense of meaning in life can better adjust physical and mental health, are more likely to have a sense of meaning and purpose of life and are more likely to have positive psychological resources, including self-efficacy, hope, optimism and resilience. These qualities can help individuals actively cope with stress and challenges and remain resilient in the face of crises.[41–43] Therefore, positive PsyCap plays a mediating role in the relationship between meaning in life and crisis vulnerability and may be the bridge that connects meaning in life and crisis vulnerability, which influences perceptions and responses to crises.

4.4. Chain mediating effect of moral values and positive PsyCap

The results indicate a certain relationship between positive PsyCap and moral values, which in turn influences meaning in life and crisis vulnerability. Moreover, the results imply that the mediating effect of this relationship is 19.6%.

In TCM nursing education, a people-oriented, holistic view and outlook on life is emphasized, based on the belief that the human body and the natural world are a unified whole. This philosophy helps students comprehensively understand and respect life, pay attention to humanistic care and emotional communication, and learn to face life in a moral and ethical manner. The unique educational philosophy of the higher school of TCM is conducive to cultivating meaning in life among nursing students. In the context of the diversified cultural education of TCM schools, nursing students are more inclined to develop positive moral values. This aspect is related to the fact that the moral values identified in a study may be related to an individual’s perception of meaning of life.[44] Through the cultivation of empathy and self-reflection, continuous learning and self-motivation of nursing students, schools can help them examine their behavior and decision making, identify and strengthen behaviors that meet ethical standards and form positive moral values. Students with a strong sense of meaning in life are likely to possess the correct outlook on life, values and worldview.[45] Nursing students typically maintain positive attitudes and expectations towards life and learning; improve their professional knowledge and skills by participating in seminars and course training; regularly reflect on nursing practice; and seek professional guidance and suggestions to enhance their positive PsyCap, improve their mental health and reduce crisis vulnerability.

This model depicts that meaning in life influences crisis vulnerability through the separate and combined effects of 2 mediating variables: moral values identification and positive PsyCap. This finding further illustrates the influencing mechanism of meaning in life on crisis vulnerability; at the same time, it demonstrates that external factors affect the crisis vulnerability of individuals through internal factors. Medical students with a high sense of meaning in life also experience negative emotions such as anxiety and depression in the face of negative life events.[46,47] However, when individuals integrate moral values into their emotional identity and behavioral habits, this positive value orientation will help them develop richer inner resources, self-experience, and evaluation. In this manner, the impact of negative events can be alleviated and the occurrence of psychological crises can be reduced, thereby decreasing individuals’ vulnerability to psychological crises. Thus, meaning in life and positive PsyCap, as anti-frustration qualities that play an important role in adversity, can indirectly influence the vulnerability of individuals to psychological crisis.

Moral values reflect the rich connotation and practical requirements of the socialist core value system and play an important role in guiding social trends of thought and building social consensus, and positive PsyCap is a multi-dimensional structure; both are important components of individual psychological and social well-being and play a vital role in the understanding and response of individuals to crises and challenges in life. With the recent inclusion of nursing as a state-controlled major, nurses have become an important force in supporting the health and social development of the entire population, such that assessment of the mental health and levels of crisis vulnerability of the nursing student population are vital. Studies pointed out that ethical sensibility does not develop as naturally as other professional values; instead, it develops through ongoing education and learning.[48] Another study found that moral education improved the moral sensitivity and critical thinking of students.[49] Therefore, future higher education curriculum should integrate moral values education and positive psychology-related courses, help students form and deepen their understanding of nursing professional ethics, actively create a supportive and positive campus culture, improve the psychological adaptability of nursing students, reduce crisis vulnerability and enhance students’ mental health and overall well-being.

In summary, meaning in life, moral values and positive PsyCap clearly exert significant impacts on individuals’ levels of crisis vulnerability. In addition, meaning in life may indirectly affect crisis vulnerability by influencing moral values or positive PsyCap. According to the results of ANOVA on demographics, professional identity and family relationship significantly influence the 4 abovementioned indicators; this result was consistent with previous findings.[50] Other aspects may also affect the crisis vulnerability of undergraduate nursing students, including personal psychological traits, emotional management skills, coping strategies and social support systems. However, the current study focuses only on the specific group of undergraduate nursing students in TCM colleges and universities; thus, the results lack extensiveness and generalizability and do not consider cultural differences. Therefore, future research needs to expand the sample and consider cultural and individual differences.

The results have certain guiding significance for the government, universities, and social and health institutions. For example, governments can incorporate indicators of crisis vulnerability, such as meaning in life, moral values, and positive PsyCap, into the evaluation of education policies. In addition, TCM colleges can use their unique cultural heritage and wisdom to cultivate the humanistic feelings of nursing students and guide them to further understand their meaning in life by respecting and understanding others. While popularizing the knowledge of mental health education for nursing students, teaching reforms in curriculum ideology and politics should be considered an opportunity to maximize the leading role of moral values, promote the “immune antibodies” of nursing students to pursue their ethical values, overcome psychological crises, and regularly conduct mental health education and evaluation for students to further decrease their levels of crisis vulnerability, avoid psychological crises and promote mental health.

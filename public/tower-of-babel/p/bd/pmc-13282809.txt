In Memoriam
Authors: Bonnie Ky
Published: 2026

On April 22, 2026, the world lost a true giant in modern cardiovascular medicine with the passing of Eugene Braunwald. His name is synonymous with the evolution of cardiology itself, and his scientific legacy has shaped generations of clinicians and investigators. While his foundational contributions to heart failure, acute coronary syndromes, and clinical trials are widely celebrated, his influence also extended meaningfully into the emerging field of cardio-oncology—a space he recognized early as both necessary and inevitable.

I first met Dr Braunwald more than a decade ago at a Heart Failure Society of America Annual Scientific Meeting, where I summoned the courage to introduce myself and share my ideas on cardio-oncology clinical trials. I shared with him my passion for cardioprotection strategies and the need for dedicated trials in patients with cancer, then a nascent but rapidly evolving area. He listened intently, asked incisive questions, and affirmed both the scientific and clinical importance of the field.

What followed was transformative for my career. Dr Braunwald provided critical mentorship as I worked alongside him, Dr Ken Margulies, Dr Patrice Desvigne-Nickens, Dr Lori Minasian, Ms Eileen Dimond, and many others on the development of a clinical trial testing intensive systolic blood pressure control in patients with active cancer. At every step, he brought clarity of thought, intellectual rigor, and a deep sense of purpose. Each interaction was deliberate, and each communication was carefully considered.

In subsequent encounters, including a visit to his office at the Brigham when I delivered Cardiovascular Medicine Grand Rounds, he continued to offer encouragement, thoughtful guidance, and perspective that was both pragmatic and visionary. I recall him remarking that collaboration between cardiologists and oncologists was entirely natural—akin to the long-standing partnership between cardiologists and cardiac surgeons. In that simple analogy, he articulated a framework that continues to guide the field today.

On a personal level, I have deep gratitude for all of his support of JACC: CardioOncology; his written and spoken words to me reflecting the Journal's impact are ones that I will always hold with great value. His willingness to serve as an advisor to the journal reflected his commitment to advancing new frontiers in cardiovascular medicine. I was honored when he later entrusted us with a manuscript as senior author,1 a contribution we were proud to publish and one that further underscored his engagement with and belief in the field.

In all of my interactions with Dr Braunwald, he was unfailingly insightful and impactful. Yet, beyond his towering academic achievements, what left the greatest impression was his generosity as a mentor and his openness to new ideas.

Dr Braunwald’s legacy will endure not only through the science he advanced, but through the countless individuals he inspired and guided. In cardio-oncology, as in so many areas of cardiovascular medicine, his influence will continue to be everlasting.

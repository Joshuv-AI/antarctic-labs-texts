Behaviour change techniques embedded in physical training protocol on exercise motivation and level of physical activity in individuals with COPD: study protocol for a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Fernanda Manenti Basso, Tiago de Almeida Araújo, Livia Maria Petilli Zopelari, Manuela Karloh, Valéria Amorim Pires Di Lorenzo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe use of behaviour change techniques to promote physical activity and reduce sedentary behaviour in individuals with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) has been the focus of recent research. However, previous studies have not explored such techniques embedded in physical training (PT) to improve the physical activity levels and reduce sedentary behaviour in this population. Therefore, this study aims to describe a PT protocol incorporating behaviour change techniques based on the Self-Determination Theory to enhance exercise motivation and level of physical activity in individuals with COPD.MethodsThis descriptive protocol for a randomized, double-blind clinical trial will recruit individuals with stable COPD. Individuals will be randomized into intervention and control groups, both undergoing individually prescribed PT for 12 weeks. Only the intervention group will perform behaviour change techniques based on the self-determination theory during PT sessions. The groups will be reassessed at the end of the protocol and 6 months after the intervention. Primary outcomes will include level of physical activity and sedentary behaviour, assessed by using the ActivPAL3™ triaxial accelerometer (PAL Technologies Ltd., Glasgow, UK) for at least 4 days for 8 h daily.DiscussionThe intervention protocol is expected to improve exercise motivation and level of physical activity of individuals with COPD. Also, this protocol may promote behaviour change in this population and be adapted for clinical practice.Trial registrationBrazilian Clinical Trials Registry (ReBEC): RBR-9ykz7ws. Introduction

Background and rationale {6a}

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is considered the third leading cause of mortality worldwide, accounting for 3.3 million deaths in 2019, primarily in low- and middle-income countries [1]. This condition leads to pulmonary impairments, including airflow limitation, pulmonary hyperinflation, arterial hypoxemia, and extrapulmonary effects on the musculoskeletal and cardiovascular systems. Also, these impairments may contribute to reduced levels of physical activity and increased sedentary behaviour [2].

In this context, the Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease recommends that individuals with COPD participate in pulmonary rehabilitation programmes focused on physical training (PT) [3]. The benefits of PT are well documented in the literature, with improvements in exercise capacity, quality of life, and symptom relief [4]. However, studies showing long-term improvements in the level of physical activity and reductions in sedentary behaviour are still scarce [4, 5].

Some studies have addressed behaviour change techniques in interventions, such as counselling, pedometer use, or health contracts. These interventions have shown promising results in increasing the level of physical activity in individuals with COPD, who maintained the gains up to 3 months after the intervention [6, 7]. However, a systematic review revealed no reduction of sedentary behaviour in individuals with DPOC performing only PT [8]. Also, behaviour change techniques were poorly described when they were included, highlighting the need for improved reporting to ensure reproducibility and accurate assessment of their effects [8].

The self-determination theory (SDT) is a theoretical model that aims to help behaviour change (including health-related behaviours) by supporting basic psychological needs (i.e. autonomy, competence, and relatedness) and encouraging volitional strategies of motivation [9, 10]. Also, this approach can be applied to interventions designed to increase step count [11]. Recently, two meta-analyses [11, 12] demonstrated the potential of using SDT to enhance satisfaction in autonomy, competence, relatedness, and self-motivation, which was positively correlated with changes in health-related behaviours.

Physiotherapeutic interventions within pulmonary rehabilitation programmes using behaviour change techniques have been a recent focus of studies aiming to improve the level of physical activity and reduce sedentary behaviour [13–16]. However, no consolidated evidence was found regarding the effects of combining behaviour change techniques with a PT programme on exercise motivation and level of physical activity in individuals with COPD.

Objectives {7}

This study aims to describe behaviour change techniques based on SDT embedded in a PT protocol to improve exercise motivation and level of physical activity in individuals with COPD.

Trial design {8}

This is a protocol of a randomized, controlled, double-blind (i.e. individuals and outcome researcher) clinical trial in a single centre with a 1:1 allocation ratio and a superiority framework.

Methods: participants, interventions, and outcomes

Study setting {9}

This protocol will be conducted in individuals with stable COPD, referred by physicians from public and private pulmonary outpatient clinics of São Carlos, São Paulo, Brazil. The study was approved by the research ethics committee of the Federal University of São Carlos (CAAE: 74618523.9.0000.5504) and is registered on the Brazilian Clinical Trials Registry (ReBEC) platform (https://ensaiosclinicos.gov.br/rg/RBR-9ykz7ws).

Eligibility criteria {10}

Inclusion criteria will comprise individuals: (I) with COPD with a forced expiratory volume in 1 s and forced vital capacity ratio of < 0.7 [3], (II) clinically stable, with no exacerbations in the past 4 weeks, (III) under medical care and pharmacological treatment, (IV) without participation in PT programmes in the last 6 months, (V) aged ≥ 50 years, (VI) with low exercise capacity identified by the 6-min step test (6MST) or 6-min walk test (6MWT) (below 80% of predicted value) [17], and (VII) without cognitive impairment (Mini-Mental State Examination Score ≥ 20 points) [18] and balance deficits (tandem and semi-tandem tests) [19].

The following individuals will be excluded: (I) with pathological tremors, (II) treating malignant neoplasms, (III) body mass index ≥ 35 kg/m2, (IV) orthopaedic conditions preventing the performance of physical tests, decompensated cardiovascular or respiratory diseases, rheumatic or neuromuscular problems, or (V) visual impairments preventing walking or step-up [20].

Who will take informed consent? {26a}

On the first day, the researchers will explain the study assessments, intervention, and reassessment procedures to individuals. Those who agree to participate will sign the informed consent form.

Additional consent provisions for collection and use of participant data and biological specimens {26b}

This study does not involve the collection of biological specimens. As specified in the consent form, participants will be informed about the potential use of relevant data in subsequent studies, as well as the possible sharing with academic institutions involved in the study or regulatory authorities, when necessary.

Interventions

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b}

The control group will perform an individualized PT protocol, which will follow the evidence-based principles and recommendations of official pulmonary rehabilitation guidelines [2, 21, 22].

The PT will happen three times per week (two supervised sessions and one unsupervised session) over 12 weeks and was adapted from a previous study [23], which employed a protocol grounded in functional assessments. The literature indicates that protocols based on functional tests—such as the 6-min walk test (6MWT), the sit-to-stand test (STS), and the 6-min step test (6MST)—are effective in enhancing exercise capacity, improving quality of life, and reducing dyspnoea in individuals with COPD [24]. Additionally, these interventions are low-cost, easily implemented across diverse settings, and are aligned with the principles of both aerobic and resistance training [2, 22]. Nonetheless, current evidence does not confirm that such protocols promote behavioural change, as demonstrated in a previous study [24], which found no statistically significant differences between groups in terms of physical activity levels. Consequently, integrating behavioural change strategies with a functional test-based protocol is warranted and constitutes the innovative component of the present proposal. The control group will not receive the behavioural change techniques.

Supervised sessions will include four phases: warm-up; aerobic exercises (e.g. walking, step-up, and sit-to-stand movements); dynamic resistance exercises for upper and lower limbs; and respiratory and stretching exercises, with pursed-lip breathing combined with trunk rotation. For the unsupervised session, individuals will receive a booklet containing instructions for warm-up, aerobic, respiratory, and stretching exercises. On the second assessment day, all individuals will receive a monitoring kit and Smart Band 9 (Xiaomi Inc, China) to perform the unsupervised session at home. Individuals will be required to maintain the kit for the entire protocol.

Intervention description {11a}

The intervention group will follow the same PT protocol as the control group. Additionally, behaviour change techniques based on SDT will be implemented in all PT sessions, and only this group will use a Smart Band to monitor the physical activity goals.

Behaviour change techniques

Behaviour change techniques will be applied with a structured protocol in every PT session to address the three basic psychological needs (i.e. autonomy, competence, and relatedness). Key techniques were established: providing choices, setting challenges or goals, and fostering social support. Also, each session will incorporate three techniques addressing each of the basic psychological needs. The behaviour change techniques applied in each session are described in Fig. 1.

Behaviour change techniques sessions

Physical training protocol

Warm-up

Warm-up

For warm-up, low-intensity exercises (2 to 3 on the Borg scale [25]) involving the upper and lower limbs will be performed for 3 to 5 min to increase resting heart rate by 10 bpm above the baseline [26].

(2)Aerobic exercises

Aerobic exercises

Aerobic exercises will last 20 min and include walking, step-up, and sit-to-stand. Initial walking intensity will be set at 80% of the average speed achieved in the 6MWT [27], progressing every 2 weeks to reach 100% of performance for 6 to 10 min. Heart rate and oxygen saturation will be monitored by oximeter, while dyspnoea and fatigue will be monitored subjectively by the Borg scale trying to maintain between 4 and 6. During walking, individuals will use a Smart Band to count steps at the end of the session.

Supervised and unsupervised step-up exercises will be performed for 6 to 10 min and divided into series lasting 1 to 2 min. Initial intensity will be set at 80% of the total number of steps achieved in 1 min during the 6MST [23]. For the sit-to-stand exercise, individuals will complete three series at an intensity corresponding to 80% of the number of repetitions achieved in the 1-min sit-to-stand test (1MSTS) [23].

Individuals will self-monitor their heart rate, oxygen saturation, dyspnoea, and fatigue during all aerobic exercises. Also, they will maintain the minimum intensity level (80%) if the progression is not feasible, and those achieving 100% performance will be instructed to maintain this intensity until the end of the protocol. Rest will be allowed in the three exercises if individuals report dyspnoea or fatigue levels > 6 on the Borg scale.

(3)Dynamic resistance exercises

Dynamic resistance exercises

Dynamic resistance exercises for the upper and lower limbs will be performed based on one-repetition maximum (1-RM) testing. The programme will consist of two to three sets of 8 to 12 repetitions. The initial load will be 50% of 1-RM. Resistance exercise intensity will be progressively increased every 2 weeks by either adding repetitions or increasing the load, up to 100% of 1-RM. If progression is not achieved, the load will be maintained [26].

(4)Respiratory and stretching exercises

Respiratory and stretching exercises

Respiratory exercises will consist of diaphragmatic breathing with pursed lips and trunk mobility exercises (rotation, flexion, and extension) in standing or seated position. Stretching will be performed on the muscle groups worked during PT.

Health education

On the first intervention day, both groups will receive a booklet with information about COPD, smoking cessation, proper medication administration, importance of vaccination, and benefits of physical activities. Also, a single group educational session lasting 30 min will be scheduled to discuss strategies for self-management of the disease and importance of increasing activities and reducing sedentary behaviour [28].

Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions {11b}

The following criteria will be used for discontinuing the intervention: dropout, exacerbation of pre-existing conditions, persistent chest pain, and orthopaedic trauma. The individual will be referred to the municipal health service or their physician when needed. In cases of discontinuation, the reason for interrupting the intervention will be collected. There is no plan to modify the intervention. Data from participants who discontinue the intervention at any point will not be excluded, and these participants will continue to be followed for the remaining study duration unless they request to withdraw from follow-up.

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions {11c}

Individuals will be instructed to record their physical activity habits weekly in a diary during the protocol. Data from individuals who drop out (including the reason for dropout and duration of protocol adherence) will be collected. Good adherence will be considered when attending 75% of sessions. All individuals will receive a final report detailing their exercise capacity based on assessments and reassessments. During the interventions, all participants will be reminded about the two PT days and, if necessary, the days can be rescheduled according to the participant’s preference.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {11 d}

Individuals will continue their usual care without any change. Also, they will be advised to maintain regular use of medications for COPD or other comorbidities.

Provisions for post-trial care {30}

No adverse events are expected from participation in this study. If any adverse event occurs, the protocol will be discontinued, and the individual will receive assistance from the researcher.

At the end of the intervention, all provided equipment, including the monitoring kit and the Smart Band 9, will be collected by the researchers so that they may be used by other participants. Additionally, if at the end of the study, the benefits of the intervention are confirmed, it will also be offered to participants in the control group.

Outcomes {12}

The primary outcome will be the level of physical activity (daily step count) assessed by an accelerometer. As secondary outcomes, this study will include five: (1) motivational regulations and self-determination for exercise, (2) basic psychological needs, (3) self-efficacy in pulmonary rehabilitation, (4) exercise capacity, and (5) monitoring of cardiorespiratory variables during activities of daily living using wearable technology. All outcomes will be evaluated by the same assessor prior to the intervention, immediately following the intervention, and 6-month post-intervention follow-up and compared between the intervention group and the control group.

Primary outcome

The study will have one evaluation of primary outcome: level of physical activity (daily step count). The level of physical activity and time in sedentary behaviour will be measured using a triaxial accelerometer (ActivPAL3™, PAL Technologies Ltd., Glasgow, UK) attached to the mid-thigh of the right leg for 7 days. Data will be considered valid if individuals use the device during waking activities for a minimum of 8 h per day for at least 4 days. The device will provide the following variables: daily step count, time spent sitting, standing, and walking, and intensity of the physical activity [29]. For this outcome, a Minimal Important Difference of more than 600 steps will be considered [30].

Secondary outcome

Motivational regulations and self-determination for exercise

The Behavioural Regulation in Exercise Questionnaire-3 will assess motivational regulations regarding physical activity. This questionnaire consists of 23 items with scores ranging from 0 (not at all true) to 4 (completely true), measuring six motivational regulations and self-determination index [31].

Basic psychological needs

The Basic Psychological Needs in Exercise Scale will assess perceptions of the fulfilment of basic psychological needs with physical activity. This Likert scale consists of 11 items ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree), divided into autonomy, competence, and relatedness [32].

Self-efficacy in pulmonary rehabilitation

The Pulmonary Rehabilitation Adapted Index of Self-Efficacy will assess self-efficacy in pulmonary rehabilitation. This Likert scale was validated for individuals with COPD and consists of 15 items scored from 1 (not at all true) to 4 (completely true) [33].

Anxiety and Depression: The Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale will assess the possibility of anxiety and depression in the participants. This Likert scale consists of 14 items, with seven sub-items for anxiety and seven sub-items for depression, scored from 0 (absence of anxiety or depression) to refer to how the individual is feeling in the last week [34]. It was validated to the Brazilian population [35].

Exercise capacity

6MWT, 6MST, and 1MSTS will assess exercise capacity and guide the aerobic training prescription. 6MWT will be performed twice on the first assessment day in a 30-m corridor marked by two cones, according to a standardized procedure [20]. The longest distance covered in the two attempts will be considered, and a prediction equation for the Brazilian population will be applied [36]. The 1MSTS will be performed on the second assessment day in a single attempt using a chair (46 cm of height), according to a standardized procedure [37]. The number of sit-to-stand repetitions will be recorded, and a prediction equation will be applied [38]. Also on the second day, a single attempt of the 6MST will be performed on a step (20 cm of height), according to a standardized procedure [39]. The total number of steps will be recorded, and a prediction equation will be applied [39]. A minimal clinically important difference (MCID) of 30 m for the 6MWT [20], more than 21 steps for the 6MST [40], and three repetitions for the 1MSTS [41] will be considered.

Monitoring of cardiorespiratory variables during activities of daily living using a wearable technology

Heart rate, respiratory rate, tidal volume, and minute volume will be measured during activities of daily living using a smart shirt (Hexoskin®, Carré Technologies Inc., CA). This device contains three electrocardiogram sensors and two belts (thoracic and abdominal) to estimate respiratory data at rest or during daily activities by using respiratory inductance plethysmography [42, 43]. Individuals will wear the smart shirt for 8 h per day on 4 days of their choice and record the activities in a diary.

Sample size {14}

The sample size was calculated by using G*Power software (version 3.1.9.7) [44]. Based on an estimated effect size of 0.9 (Cohen’s d) derived from means and standard deviation of steps per day (6248 ± 2030, control and 8853 ± 3320, intervention) used to calculate the sample of a previous study that used similar behavioural intervention in chronic respiratory disease [45], a sample of 38 individuals with COPD will be required for α of 5% and power of 80%. To account for the fact that individuals with COPD tend to drop out prematurely from PT programmes, a dropout rate of 30% will be applied [46], and thus, the final sample was set at 50 individuals (25 per group).

Recruitment {15}

Individuals referred by pulmonologists from pulmonary outpatient clinics of São Carlos and the University Hospital of the Federal University of São Carlos will be recruited according to eligibility criteria. Individuals will be contacted by phone and informed about the study procedures. Those who agree to participate will be scheduled for assessment.

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation {16a}

The study coordinator (who will not be involved in intervention or outcome assessment) will generate a sequence using the computer to allocate individuals to one of the groups in a 1:1 ratio.

Concealment mechanism {16b}

The allocation sequence will be stored in a sealed, opaque envelope and maintained confidential by the study coordinator, who will be consulted by the physical therapist responsible for the intervention to determine the individual allocation.

Implementation {16c}

After completing baseline assessments, the study coordinator will inform the physical therapist responsible for the intervention about the allocation of the individual. However, the outcome researchers will remain blinded.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded {17a}

The outcome researchers and individuals will be blinded to group allocation. The physical therapist responsible for the intervention will not be blinded due to the nature of the intervention. A research assistant not involved in the study process will perform the statistical data analysis. It will not be necessary to establish a Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) to independently assess the safety, scientific validity, and integrity of the clinical trial. As this is a single-centre study, the local research team will be responsible for conducting and monitoring all study activities. Regular meetings will be held to track progress and ensure compliance with the study protocol. Additionally, two researchers with expertise in behaviour change will provide training to the research team on the application of the intervention techniques.

Procedure for unblinding if needed {17b}

Unblinding is not expected, considering the characteristics of the intervention.

Data collection and management

Plans for assessment and collection of outcomes {18a}

Outcomes and measurement instruments are described in the outcome section {12}

All researchers directly involved in data collection will undergo training by collaborating investigators to ensure standardized administration of physical tests and questionnaires. Assessments will be conducted on two non-consecutive days, with a 1-week interval between sessions, due to the required monitoring period for the accelerometer and smart shirt.

On the first assessment day, the following data will be collected: demographic data (age, sex, marital status); clinical history of COPD (year of diagnosis, number of hospitalizations, need for oxygen therapy); current medication use (type, dosage, and frequency of use); smoking status (never smoked, current smoker, or former smoker, including pack-years); anthropometric measurements as weight in kilograms and height in centimetres, both measured with participants barefoot; Charlson Comorbidity Index; lower limb dynamometry (MicroFet2 Hoggan Health Industries Inc., USA); 6-min walk test; and the BREQ-3, PRAISE, and BPNES questionnaires. At the end of the session, the accelerometer (ActivPAL3™) will be affixed for continuous wear over 7 days, and the smart shirt will be provided for use over 4 days, worn for a minimum of 8 h each day.

On the second assessment day, data from the accelerometer and smart shirt will be retrieved. Participants will then complete the handgrip strength test (Jamar® dynamometer), 1-min sit-to-stand test, 6-min step test, Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) and one-repetition maximum test.

All questionnaire and exercise tests data will be recorded on paper and subsequently transcribed into a secure electronic system, accessible only via a dedicated institutional email account restricted to the principal investigators. Data entry will be performed by one trained evaluator immediately following each assessment session and independently verified by a second researcher. Additionally, all data will be backed up on an external hard drive for indefinite storage.

Plans to promote participant retention and complete follow-up {18b}

Individuals will be followed up 6 months after the second reassessment day using the same protocol. In order to minimize dropouts and loss of follow-up, participants will have support via WhatsApp Business. This application will be used as a means of communication and to support participant adherence, by giving clarification regarding the use of equipment during assessments, the training protocol, and disease management.

Additionally, participants will be offered the option to reschedule visits and financial support for transport to the rehabilitation centre, in cases where barriers to participation arise.

Data management {19}

A researcher blinded to the intervention will manage the data, and files will be stored on a password-protected computer in the research laboratory, with backups on an external hard drive accessible only to authorized researchers. A blinded researcher will store all data from the Hexoskin® website (Carré Technologies Inc., CA) in the same password-protected computer and external hard drive.

Confidentiality {27}

All individuals’ information will remain confidential and accessible only to the researchers. The participants will be assigned a unique identification code to ensure the anonymity and confidentiality of their data, which will be exclusively accessed by the research team. All data will be used solely for scientific purposes, ensuring privacy before, during, and after the protocol, and it will be securely stored on an external hard drive with restricted access granted only to the principal investigator, and retained for an indefinite period to support future research.

Plans for collection, laboratory evaluation, and storage of biological specimens for genetic or molecular analysis in this trial/future use {33}

No biological specimens will be collected.

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {20a}

All data will be analyzed using SPSS version 21.0 for Windows. Data recorded by the accelerometer and smart shirt will be analyzed using a routine developed in MatLab software (MatLab R2018a, Mathworks, USA). The Shapiro–Wilk test will verify data normality, and variables with a normal distribution will be presented as mean ± standard deviation, while those with a non-normal distribution will be presented as median and interquartile range. For intergroup and intragroup comparisons, the two-way mixed-effect ANOVA will be applied for parametric data, and the corresponding test will be used for non-parametric data. The Pearson or Spearman correlation coefficients will assess the correlation between variables, depending on data normality. Statistical significance will be set at p < 0.05.

Interim analyses {21b}

Interim analyses are not planned.

Methods for additional analyses (e.g. subgroup analyses) {20b}

Subgroup analyses are not planned.

Methods in analysis to handle protocol non-adherence and any statistical methods to handle missing data {20c}

Participants who experience an exacerbation or withdraw during the intervention period will be included in the intention to treat analyses.

Missing data will be handled using multiple imputation by chained equations (MICE), under the assumption that data are missing at random (MAR). The imputation model will include baseline characteristics, group allocation, and all outcome variables to preserve the associations between variables and reduce bias. Twenty imputed datasets will be generated, each analysed separately, and the estimates will be combined using Rubin’s rules to obtain pooled results with valid standard errors and confidence intervals. Diagnostics will be performed to assess model convergence and the plausibility of the imputed values. Sensitivity analyses using complete-case data will also be conducted to assess the robustness of the findings.

Plans to give access to the full protocol, participant-level data, and statistical code {31c}

The full protocol, participant-level data, and statistical code will be provided by the study coordinator upon reasonable request.

Oversight and monitoring

Composition of the coordinating centre and trial steering committee {5 d}

The coordinating centre is based at the Spirometry and Respiratory Physical Therapy Laboratory of the Federal University of São Carlos and comprises laboratory researchers. Researchers are responsible for all aspects of local organization, such as identifying potential participants, providing orientation support, necessary equipment, and room structure.

The trial steering committee consists of two researchers responsible for supervising the study, maintaining blind allocation, and monitoring how the protocol is conducted. Public involvement groups are not included.

Composition of the data monitoring committee, its role, and reporting structure {21a}

The data monitoring committee is not considered since this is a low-risk intervention.

Adverse event reporting and harms {22}

All anticipated adverse events (AE) will be systematically recorded for all participants through structured assessments conducted weekly on intervention days, documenting the type of event, date of onset and resolution, severity, suspected relationship to the intervention, and the outcome of the AE. In addition, non-systematic reporting will be facilitated through open-ended questions, such as “Have you experienced any adverse events since your last visit?”, to identify any unexpected or previously unreported occurrences.

Potential AEs related to PT include fatigue, oxygen desaturation (oxygen saturation drop > 4%) or saturation below 90% [47], tachycardia, and blurred vision during.

These events will be minimized by monitoring individuals before, during, and after assessments and interventions. In case of serious AE, the physical therapist will immediately stop the exercises, provide first aid, and instruct participants to seek emergency medical service. Once an adverse event is detected, it will be followed until its resolution or until it is judged to be permanent, and an assessment will be made at each contact (or more frequently, if necessary).

Frequency and plans for auditing trial conduct {23}

The trial steering committee will meet monthly to review and discuss how the protocol is being conducted. Independent data monitoring will not be considered due to the low-risk nature of the intervention.

Plans for communicating important protocol amendments to relevant parties (e.g. trial participants, ethical committees) {25}

Any changes will be communicated to ReBEC, research ethics committee, study individuals, scientific journals, and funding agencies.

Dissemination plans {31a}

Results will be disseminated through publications in scientific journals and presentations at scientific events, such as conferences and congresses. Individuals will receive reports on their test results at the end of the assessments. Data will be used solely for scientific purposes, ensuring individual privacy before, during, and after the protocol.

Discussion

Previous studies have demonstrated the importance of incorporating behaviour change techniques into rehabilitation for individuals with COPD and asthma (6,7,13,36). However, evidence on the effects of behaviour change techniques embedded in an individualized PT programme prescribed with weekly progression for individuals with COPD is limited.

This original intervention protocol is designed to address behaviour change techniques based on SDT embedded in a PT programme. This protocol may improve the level of physical activity and exercise capacity, reduce sedentary behaviour, and change individual motivation for autonomy, promoting healthy lifestyle habits in individuals with COPD. It may also improve overall health in individuals with COPD, be adapted for clinical practice, and be applied to other populations with similar characteristics.

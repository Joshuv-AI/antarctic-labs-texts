Proteome Dataset of Serum and Albumin-Depleted Serum of Labeo rohita Under Cold Temperature Stress
Authors: Poonam Jayant Singh, Arpita Batta
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractLabeo rohita, a commercially important Indian major carp, undergoes significant physiological and molecular alterations under cold stress. This Data Descriptor presents comprehensive proteomic and hormonal data capturing the fish’s adaptive response to prolonged sub-lethal low temperature exposure (5–10 °C for 45 days). High-resolution LC-MS/MS analysis of serum and albumin-depleted serum revealed extensive proteome remodelling, indicating mechanisms of metabolic reprogramming, immune modulation, and post-translational regulation. A total of 598 proteins were identified in serum from cold-stressed fish and 335 in controls, while 1372 proteins were detected in albumin-depleted serum of stressed fish and 462 in controls, from 75,406 MS/MS spectra. In serum, 99 proteins were unique to controls, 75 to stressed fish, and 133 were shared; in albumin-depleted serum, 652 proteins were unique to controls, 366 to stressed fish, and 190 were common to both. Differentially expressed proteins were associated with energy metabolism, cytoskeletal organization, and cellular stress responses, highlighting key adaptive pathways. Hormonal profiling indicated reduced levels of thyroxine (T4), triiodothyronine (T3), and cortisol, alongside elevated estradiol and testosterone. GO enrichment analysis of DEPs revealed significant shifts in metabolic, stress response, and cellular homeostasis pathways. Together, these data offer a molecular framework for understanding cold adaptation in L. rohita, with implications for stress biomarker discovery and enhancing aquaculture resilience under climate variability.Subject terms: Mass spectrometry, Environmental impact

Background & Summary

The Indian major carp Labeo rohita (rohu) is a dominant aquaculture species across the Asian subcontinent, underpinning regional food security and economic stability. As a tropical freshwater species, L. rohita is physiologically sensitive to temperature fluctuations, with cold stress emerging as a key environmental stressor that disrupts metabolic homeostasis and compromises growth1.

Recent proteomic investigations provide a granular view of the molecular adjustments this species undergoes when exposed to sub-lethal cold temperatures. These studies particularly emphasize the serum proteome and albumin-depleted serum fraction, offering insights into stress-induced metabolic and immunological shifts. Cold stress elicits a cascade of oxidative2, biochemical, and proteomic changes that reveal mechanisms of physiological adaptation3.

For instance, oxidative stress biomarkers significantly increase during cold exposure, with corresponding reductions in antioxidant proteins. These responses indicate a diversion of metabolic energy from anabolic growth processes to stress mitigation and cellular repair4. Moreover, albumin, a key carrier protein in the blood, exhibits decreased abundance under cold stress conditions, correlating with liver dysfunction and metabolic imbalance5,6.

Proteomic methodologies applied to L. rohita under other stressors, such as Aeromonas hydrophila infection, have successfully mapped differential expression of stress-responsive proteins and disrupted pathways7. Comparable proteomic responses during cold exposure include the upregulation of heat shock proteins (HSPs), post-translational modifications, and altered expression of proteins involved in glycolysis and immune response6.

Notably, depletion of serum albumin results in the loss of key transport functions and a weakened immune status. Such proteomic changes can serve as biomarkers of health in aquaculture systems and inform climate-resilient breeding and feeding strategies8. The integration of proteomics with immuno-physiological data further reveals potential interventions through dietary modulation, including the use of nutraceuticals to enhance stress tolerance9.

This Data Descriptor (Poonam Jayant Singh, Arpita Batta. PRIDE. https://identifiers.org/pride.project:PXD028384 (2025); Poonam Jayant Singh, Arpita Batta. PRIDE. https://identifiers.org/pride.project:PXD028387(2025)) summarizes and contextualizes recent high-throughput proteomic dataset concerning serum remodelling in L. rohita, with emphasis on its application in aquaculture health monitoring and climate adaptation strategies.

Methods

Animal maintenance and experimental design

Healthy specimens (n = 15) of Labeo rohita (Indian major carp), weighing between 51–65 g, were obtained from a local market and acclimatized in a recirculating aquaculture system maintained at 26 ± 1 °C for 15 days. During this period, fish were fed a commercial diet twice daily and monitored for behaviour and health. Following acclimatization, fish were randomly divided into two groups: the control group maintained at 26 °C, and the experimental group subjected to a gradual temperature reduction of 1 °C/day until reaching 5 °C. This low temperature was sustained for 45 days to induce sub-lethal cold stress leading to reduced survival of experimental specimens. Water quality parameters, including pH, ammonia, and salinity, were maintained within optimal limits according to the Committee for the Purpose of Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals (CPCSEA) guidelines[^1]. Daily observations were conducted to assess fish behaviour and physiological condition throughout the experimental period.

Sample collection and protein extraction

At the end of the exposure period, fish were anesthetized using clove oil (0.10 mL/L) and euthanized. Serum and albumin depleted serum were aseptically collected in vacutainers, immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C until further processing. Samples were mixed and homogenized in 50 mM Tris buffer containing protease inhibitors and phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoride (PMSF). Protein concentration was determined using the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) assay. Protein integrity and quality were evaluated using one-dimensional sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (1D SDS-PAGE).

LC-MS/MS analysis

Total proteins (100 µg per sample) were subjected to in-solution tryptic digestion. Prior to digestion, proteins were reduced with dithiothreitol (DTT) and alkylated with iodoacetamide. Following overnight digestion at 37 °C, peptides were purified and analyzed using an ACQUITY UPLC system coupled with a SYNAPT G2 QTOF mass spectrometer (Waters Corporation) operated in positive electrospray ionization (ESI) mode. Peptide identification was performed using ProteinLynx Global Server (PLGS) software version 3.0.2 against the UniProt Danio rerio database, due to the incomplete proteome annotation of L. rohita. Search parameters included a peptide tolerance of 50 ppm and fragment tolerance of 100 ppm, with carbamidomethylation of cysteine as a fixed modification and oxidation (M) and deamination (NQ) as variable modifications. Differentially expressed proteins were identified, and the 25 most significant pathways (p-value based) were compared between control and experimental groups.

Bioinformatics analysis

The bioinformatic workflow followed a conventional label-free proteomics pipeline. Raw LC–MS/MS spectra generated by the SYNAPT G2 QTOF were processed in ProteinLynx Global Server (PLGS v3.0.2) for peak detection, deconvolution, and database searching. Protein identification was performed using the UniProt reference proteome of Danio rerio, with strict search parameters: peptide mass tolerance of 50 ppm, fragment ion tolerance of 100 ppm, trypsin as the digestion enzyme allowing up to one missed cleavage, carbamidomethylation of cysteine as a fixed modification, and oxidation (M) and deamination (N/Q) as variable modifications. Proteins were accepted only if supported by at least two unique peptides and a high-confidence PLGS score (≥95%). Relative quantification was achieved using spectral counting and normalized peptide intensities across samples. Identified DEPs were subsequently subjected to functional annotation and enrichment analysis using Gene Ontology (GO) and pathway databases, enabling classification into biological processes, molecular functions, and cellular components. Enrichment significance was evaluated using hypergeometric testing with FDR correction, providing insight into key molecular pathways associated with cold stress.

Hormonal profiling

Blood samples were collected from the caudal vein and allowed to clot at room temperature before centrifugation. Serum was separated and stored at −80 °C until analysis. Levels of cortisol, thyroxine (T4), triiodothyronine (T3), estradiol, and testosterone were quantified using commercial ELISA kits validated for use in fish, following the manufacturer’s instructions. All measurements were performed in duplicate and included appropriate internal controls.

Statistical analysis

Quantitative comparisons between control and cold-stressed groups were performed using standard inferential statistical methods to ensure robust identification of differentially expressed proteins (DEPs). Normality of protein abundance distributions was first assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. For normally distributed data, Student’s t-test (two-tailed, unpaired) was applied, whereas non-parametric Mann–Whitney U tests were used for data that did not meet normality assumptions. Proteins were considered significantly differentially expressed if they met a threshold of p < 0.05 and a minimum fold-change of ≥1.5 between groups. To control for false positives arising from multiple hypothesis testing inherent to large-scale proteomic datasets, false discovery rate (FDR) correction was applied using the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure, with an adjusted q-value ≤ 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Database limitation

As a fully annotated and curated reference proteome for Labeo rohita is not currently available, the UniProt Danio rerio database was used as a reference for protein identification. While this approach is commonly adopted for non-model species, it introduces inherent limitations. Specifically, proteins unique to L. rohita or those with low sequence homology to zebrafish may remain unidentified or be incorrectly annotated, thus, de novo methods were applied. This limitation highlights the need for improved genomic and proteomic resources for L. rohita to enhance accuracy, reproducibility, and data reuse in future studies.

Data Analysis

Proteins identified with high-confidence PLGS scores were subjected to quantitative comparative analysis between control and cold-stressed groups. Differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) were classified using Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis to categorize them by biological process, molecular function, and cellular component. Bioinformatics tools and software were used to assess functional enrichment and potential stress-response pathways.

Data Records

The mass spectrometry-based proteomic analysis of Labeo rohita serum and albumin-depleted serum under sub-lethal cold stress yielded 75,406 MS/MS spectra. In serum samples, 598 proteins were identified in cold-stressed fish and 335 in controls, with 133 common, 75 unique to the stressed group, and 99 unique to controls. In albumin-depleted serum, 1372 proteins were detected in stressed samples and 462 in controls, with 190 shared, 366 unique to stressed, and 652 unique to control groups.

Technical Validation

To ensure reproducibility and reliability, all protein extraction and LC-MS/MS analyses were performed in triplicate. Protein identifications were accepted based on stringent PLGS scoring thresholds. Hormonal assays were validated through duplicate testing with internal standards to confirm result consistency.

Usage Notes

The presented proteomic dataset provides a comprehensive reference for comparative studies in aquaculture species. It offers valuable insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying cold stress resilience in Labeo rohita. Researchers may utilize this dataset for the identification of potential biomarkers, integration into selective breeding programs, and the development of stress mitigation strategies in aquaculture systems exposed to variable environmental conditions.

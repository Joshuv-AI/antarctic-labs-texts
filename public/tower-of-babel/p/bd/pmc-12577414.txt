Ferroptosis-related differential gene expression and immune correlates in luminal a subtype breast cancer: a prognostic model approach
Authors: Mei Man, Zhenfang Pang, Jian Dang, Bo Chen, Guangsheng Chen, Xia Zeng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aimed to identify reliable prognostic biomarkers and novel therapeutic targets for Luminal A subtype breast cancer, a prevalent subtype with unmet needs in overcoming endocrine resistance. Given the rising global incidence of breast cancer, high recurrence/drug resistance rates in Luminal A patients, and limited research on ferroptosis in this subtype, we analyzed 421 Luminal A subtype breast cancer samples and 113 adjacent normal tissues from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database. Differential expression analysis (log₂|fold change|>1, p < 0.05) and univariate Cox regression for overall survival (OS) identified 12 ferroptosis-related differentially expressed genes (DEGs) with prognostic value. Using Lasso-Cox regression (10-fold cross-validation to avoid overfitting), we further optimized these into a 9-gene prognostic model. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression confirmed the model-derived risk score as an independent prognostic factor for OS (HR = 3.896, 95% CI = 2.195–6.916, p < 0.001) in TCGA. External validation in the Molecular Taxonomy of Breast Cancer International Consortium (METABRIC) cohort (584 Luminal A samples) demonstrated consistent prognostic performance (log-rank p < 0.001), confirming the model’s robustness. Functional enrichment analysis showed these genes were enriched in ferroptosis-related pathways, including “lipid biosynthetic process”(Gene Ontology) and “FoxO signaling pathway” (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes). Single-sample gene set enrichment analysis revealed high-risk groups had increased infiltration of immunosuppressive cells (mast cells, neutrophils, regulatory T cells) and elevated type II interferon response, while low-risk groups had higher type I interferon response. Spearman’s correlation analysis identified ENPP2 as a key mediator of immune escape, with a strong positive correlation with PD-1 (r = 0.46, p = 8.7e-24). This 9-gene ferroptosis-related model provides a robust tool for OS prediction in Luminal A subtype breast cancer and highlights ENPP2 as a potential target for combining ferroptosis inducers with immune checkpoint inhibitors—offering a novel strategy for endocrine-resistant patients.Keywords: Luminal a breast cancer, TCGA, Immune scores, ENPP2

Introduction

Based on the GLOBOCAN 2020 cancer incidence and mortality data issued by the International Agency for Research on Cancer, female breast cancer has overtaken lung cancer and emerged as the most prevalent cancer worldwide, registering around 2.3 million new cases [1]. Domestic research also indicates that the incidence of breast cancer in China has been rising year by year: by 2020, the incidence rate had nearly quadrupled compared to that 20 years earlier, and the age of onset among Chinese women has been getting increasingly younger [2]. Despite advances in diagnosis and treatment, breast cancer remains a severe threat to women’s health, with persistently high global incidence and mortality rates.

Breast cancer is a highly heterogeneous tumor, and its management is further complicated by significant subtype-specific disparities in treatment response [3]. Given these differences in gene expression profiles, it can be categorized into Luminal A, Luminal B, HER2 (Human Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor 2)-overexpressing, and triple-negative subtypes [4]. Among these, the Luminal A subtype is the most prevalent in the general breast cancer population, and patients with this subtype exhibit significant sensitivity to endocrine therapy [5]. Nevertheless, in clinical practice, a fraction of these patients still experience disease recurrence and drug resistance [6]. Thus, exploring the molecular regulatory mechanisms underlying poor prognosis in Luminal A subtype breast cancer and identifying relevant targeted therapies is of great research and clinical value.

Ferroptosis is an iron-dependent form of programmed cell death, characterized primarily by the accumulation of lipid reactive oxygen species (ROS) and consequent oxidative damage to cell membranes [7]. As a downstream mediator in gene regulation, ferroptosis is closely associated with cancer cell survival—for instance, it can disrupt the lipid metabolic balance or redox homeostasis that cancer cells rely on for survival. Thus, inducing ferroptosis in cells has gradually emerged in recent years as a novel strategy for breast cancer treatment [8–10]. Notably, ferroptosis is closely linked to Luminal A subtype breast cancer: this subtype exhibits relatively low genomic instability and limited response to chemotherapy [11]. Although it is sensitive to endocrine therapy [12], 15%−40% of patients develop endocrine resistance, leading to poor prognosis—and dysregulated ferroptosis is closely associated with this resistance [11]. Additionally, Luminal A subtype breast cancer cells rely on lipid metabolism for survival, and ferroptosis can directly target this vulnerability [12, 13]. In contrast, the proliferation-focused metabolism of Luminal B subtype lacks such susceptibility to lipid-targeted ferroptosis [14]—a subtype-specific difference that highlights the clinical relevance of investigating ferroptosis in Luminal A subtype breast cancer.

Therefore, in this study, we analyzed ferroptosis-related genes using data of Luminal A subtype breast cancer from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database—including screening ferroptosis-related differentially expressed genes (DEGs) associated with prognosis and constructing a prognostic model via Lasso-Cox regression (a method widely used for selecting key prognostic genes and avoiding overfitting in high-dimensional genomic data)—aiming to identify prognostic predictors and potential therapeutic targets for this subtype. However, current research on ferroptosis in Luminal A subtype breast cancer remains limited. To verify the robustness and generalizability of the model, we used the Molecular Taxonomy of Breast Cancer International Consortium (METABRIC) database as an external validation cohort. Results confirmed the model’s consistent ability to distinguish survival outcomes in independent populations, enhancing the reliability of the identified predictors and the model’s clinical potential for Luminal A subtype breast cancer.

Materials and methods

Data download

Breast cancer mRNA expression data (standardized as Fragments Per Kilobase of transcript per Million mapped reads [FPKM]) and corresponding clinical information of 1,098 patients were downloaded from TCGA database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov). Patients were stratified by molecular subtype to select those with Luminal A subtype breast cancer; after excluding samples with incomplete clinical follow-up or abnormal mRNA expression profiles, 421 Luminal A subtype breast cancer samples and 113 adjacent normal breast tissue samples were retained for primary analysis.

For external validation of the TCGA-derived prognostic model, additional breast cancer data (1,733 cases) were retrieved from the METABRIC database (https://www.cbioportal.org/datasets). Using the same molecular subtype screening criteria as TCGA, Luminal A subtype cases were identified; samples with missing key clinical endpoints (e.g., overall survival time) or low-quality expression data were excluded, leaving 584 Luminal A subtype breast cancer specimens as the independent validation cohort to assess the model’s robustness and generalizability.

Since TCGA and METABRIC are public databases with fully de-identified patient information, this study did not require local institutional ethics committee approval, and all data usage complied with TCGA Data Access and Use Guidelines and METABRIC’s data sharing and ethical regulations.

Ferroptosis-related genes were compiled from two sources: (1) gene markers from the Ferroptosis Database (FerrDB, http://www.zhounan.org/ferrdb), a comprehensive ferroptosis-focused repository; and (2) genes identified via systematic literature search (keywords: “ferroptosis”, “breast cancer”, “prognosis”) in PubMed and Web of Science. After removing duplicates, 728 unique ferroptosis-related genes were obtained for subsequent analyses.

Construction and verification of prognostic model

In the TCGA cohort, the R package “limma” was used for differential expression analysis between tumor and adjacent normal tissues to screen ferroptosis-related DEGs, with thresholds set as log₂|fold change|>1 and p < 0.05. Concurrently, univariate Cox regression analysis for overall survival (OS) identified ferroptosis-related genes with prognostic value; the R package “Venn” was applied to intersect these two gene sets, yielding prognosis-associated ferroptosis DEGs. The R packages “pheatmap” and “survival” generated heatmaps (for gene expression patterns) and forest plots (for univariate Cox results), respectively. Additionally, the String database (https://string-db.org/) constructed a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network, which was analyzed and visualized via the R package “igraph”.

Using the prognosis-associated ferroptosis DEGs, Lasso Cox regression (R package “glmnet”) built a prognostic model—with gene expression as the independent variable and OS (time/status) of TCGA Luminal A patients as the dependent variable—to obtain optimal model genes and their regression coefficients. Risk scores for TCGA and METABRIC patients were calculated as: Risk score = ∑(Gene expression level × Corresponding regression coefficient). For METABRIC, prognosis-associated ferroptosis DEG expression data were extracted and integrated with clinical prognosis information (OS time/status) to form a validation dataset.

Patients in TCGA (training cohort) and METABRIC (validation cohort) were stratified into high- and low-risk groups by median risk score. The R packages “pheatmap” and “ggplot2” visualized patient distribution (e.g., gene expression heatmaps, risk score scatter plots). The R package “survivminer” generated Kaplan-Meier survival curves for both cohorts. For TCGA, univariate and multivariate Cox regression (R package “survival”) validated the association between risk score and OS, and confirmed risk score as an independent prognostic factor for Luminal A subtype breast cancer.

Functional enrichment analysis

Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) and Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analyses were performed on the prognosis-related ferroptosis differentially expressed genes (p < 0.05) using the DAVID online tool (https://david.ncifcrf.gov). Subsequently, Excel was used to visualize the KEGG and GO enrichment results. Additionally, using the R package “gsva”, we performed single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) to score the relative infiltration abundance of 16 immune cells and analyze 13 immune-related pathways using the same R package. Finally, Spearman’s rank correlation test was used to assess the association between prognosis-related ferroptosis differentially expressed genes and immune checkpoint proteins (e.g., PD-1, CTLA4, LAG3).

Statistical analysis

The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to evaluate differences in prognosis-related ferroptosis differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between tumor tissues and adjacent normal tissues. OS was defined as the time interval from the date of diagnosis to the date of death. Univariate Cox regression analysis was used to identify potential prognostic factors associated with OS. The Kaplan-Meier method was used to compare OS between high- and low-risk groups (stratified by median risk score). All analyses were performed using R software (version 4.5.0), and a two-tailed p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Result

Screening for prognosis-related ferroptosis DEGs

First, data related to Luminal A subtype breast cancer were screened out from the TCGA breast cancer dataset. Ferroptosis-related genes were then extracted and subjected to differential expression analysis (tumor vs. adjacent normal tissues) using the R package “limma” (thresholds: log₂|fold change|>1, p < 0.05), identifying 51 ferroptosis-related DEGs. Concurrently, univariate Cox regression analysis (for OS) was performed on these ferroptosis-related genes to identify 64 genes with prognostic value. The intersection of these two gene sets (51 DEGs and 64 prognostic genes) was calculated using the R package “Venn,” ultimately yielding 12 prognosis-related ferroptosis DEGs (Fig. 1A).

As shown in the heatmap, the expression of 5 of these 12 genes (ACSL1, ENPP2, GABARAPL1, PIK3CA, and ATM) was significantly lower in tumor tissues than in adjacent normal tissues, while the remaining 7 genes (USP7, SREBF1, BCAT2, TFR2, SIRT6, PDSS2, and TIMP1) were significantly higher in tumor tissues (Fig. 1B). The forest plot (Fig. 1C) confirmed that all 12 genes were associated with OS: SREBF1, BCAT2, SIRT6, TFR2, and TIMP1 were low-risk genes (potentially associated with better prognosis), while ENPP2, PIK3CA, PDSS2, GABARAPL1, ATM, USP7, and ACSL1 were high-risk genes (potentially contributing to poor prognosis).

Subsequent protein-protein interaction (PPI) network analysis (String database, confidence score > 0.7; visualized via R package “igraph”) showed close interactions among PIK3CA, USP7, ATM, SIRT6, SREBF1, and ACSL1—suggesting their coordinated involvement in ferroptosis regulation (Fig. 1D). Correlation network analysis further revealed specific relationships, among which PIK3CA had strong negative correlations with TIMP1 and SIRT6, respectively; additionally, strong positive correlations were observed between PIK3CA and ATM, as well as between TIMP1 and SIRT6 (Fig. 1E).

Taken together, these results confirm that 12 ferroptosis-related DEGs are differentially expressed in Luminal A subtype breast cancer and associated with patient prognosis. These genes also exhibit coordinated interactions and correlation patterns, suggesting their potential role in regulating the progression of Luminal A subtype breast cancer.

Construction and validation of the prognostic model

A prognostic model was constructed via Lasso-Cox regression (R package “glmnet,” 10-fold cross-validation to avoid overfitting) using the 12 prognosis-related ferroptosis DEGs from the TCGA dataset. This process identified 9 optimal genes and their corresponding regression coefficients.

Patients with Luminal A subtype breast cancer in the TCGA dataset (training cohort) were stratified into high-risk and low-risk groups based on the median risk score (using the R package “survival”). Risk score distribution curves and survival status scatter plots were generated for these patients (Fig. 2A), revealing a clear trend: high-risk patients had significantly shortened survival time and a higher number of deaths (Fig. 2C). Subsequently, principal component analysis (PCA) was performed to evaluate the model’s discriminative ability. PCA results showed distinct clustering of high- and low-risk patients in the two-dimensional space (Fig. 2E), confirming the model’s ability to effectively separate the two risk groups. Finally, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis (log-rank p < 0.001) was performed on the TCGA high- and low-risk groups, revealing that the low-risk group had a significantly higher survival probability than the high-risk group (Fig. 2G).

To validate the model’s robustness and generalizability, the same analytical pipeline was applied to the METABRIC dataset (independent validation cohort). Risk score distribution curves for METABRIC patients (Fig. 2B) showed a trend consistent with TCGA: high-risk patients had significantly reduced survival duration and increased mortality (Fig. 2D vs. 2 C). PCA of METABRIC data also confirmed clear separation of high- and low-risk clusters (Fig. 2F), which was in line with the TCGA PCA results (Fig. 2E). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis in METABRIC further validated the model’s prognostic value: the low-risk group had a significantly higher survival probability (log-rank p < 0.001), mirroring the findings from the TCGA training cohort (Fig. 2H vs. 2G).

Collectively, consistent results from the TCGA training cohort and METABRIC validation cohort confirm the reliability, robustness, and clinical applicability of the constructed ferroptosis-related prognostic model for Luminal A subtype breast cancer.

Independent prognostic value of the risk score

To evaluate whether the risk score (derived from the 9-gene ferroptosis model) could serve as an independent prognostic factor for OS in Luminal A subtype breast cancer, univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed. These analyses included the risk score and key clinicopathological indicators as candidate variables (multivariate analysis used the backward stepwise selection method).

Univariate Cox regression results showed that age (≥ 65 vs. < 65 years: HR = 2.587, 95% CI = 1.252–5.344, p = 0.01) and risk score (High Risk vs. Low Risk: HR = 3.829, 95% CI = 2.213–6.625, p < 0.001) were significantly associated with OS, while tumor stage (III/IV vs. I/II: HR = 0.857, 95% CI = 0.365–2.014, p = 0.724) and other clinicopathological indicators showed no significant prognostic value (all p > 0.05) (Fig. 3A).

Cox regression analysis and functional enrichment analysis. A and (B) are the univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses of the prognostic model, respectively. C The bar chart shows the results of GO enrichment analysis and KEGG pathway analysis of the ferroptosis-related prognostic differential genes

After adjusting for confounding clinicopathological factors (age, tumor stage), multivariate Cox regression analysis confirmed that age (≥ 65 vs. < 65 years: HR = 2.369, 95% CI = 1.134–4.951, p = 0.022) and risk score (High Risk vs. Low Risk: HR = 3.896, 95% CI = 2.195–6.916, p < 0.001) remained independent prognostic factors for OS (Fig. 3B). Specifically, the high-risk group had a 3.896-fold higher risk of death than the low-risk group, indicating that the risk score could independently predict OS in patients with Luminal A subtype breast cancer.

Functional enrichment analysis

To gain a more in-depth understanding of the biological functions and mechanisms of action of ferroptosis-related genes in Luminal A subtype breast cancer, GO (Gene Ontology) enrichment analysis and KEGG (Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes) pathway analysis were conducted on the prognosis-related ferroptosis differential genes. The findings indicated that the GO enrichment analysis predominantly implicated pathways including the “lipid biosynthetic process”, “zinc ion binding”, and the “endoplasmic reticulum”. Meanwhile, the KEGG enrichment analysis revealed that these genes were primarily associated with the “FoxO signaling pathway”, “central carbon metabolism in cancer”, and “platinum drug resistance“(Fig. 3C).

Immune infiltration and immune pathway analysis

To delve deeper into the differential immune expression between the high-risk and low-risk groups, single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) was employed to score diverse immune cells and relevant immune pathways (Fig. 4A-B). The results indicated notable disparities in mast cells, neutrophils, and regulatory T cells between the high-risk and low-risk groups. Specifically, the immune score of the high-risk group was higher than that of the low-risk group. Moreover, significant differences were observed in the type I IFN response and type II IFN response between the two groups, with their respective immune scores being inversely related. Type I IFN response had a higher immune score in the low-risk group, and type II IFN response had a higher immune score in the high-risk group. Overall, among these significantly different results, except for type I IFN response, which was scored higher in the low-risk group than in the high-risk group, other factors were scored higher in the high-risk group than in the low-risk group.

Correlation between prognostic model genes and tumor immune escape

Immune checkpoints are one of the important factors leading to tumor immune escape. This study investigated the correlation between nine prognostic model genes and major immune checkpoints (PD-1, CTLA4, and LAG3). The results showed that ACSL1, BCAT2, ENPP2, GABARAPL1, PIK3CA, SREBF1, and USP7 were significantly correlated with immune checkpoint biomarkers (Figs. 4C-E and 5A-F). Among them, the relative expressions of ACSL1, ENPP2, GABARAPL1 and PIK3CA were positively correlated with the expression levels of PD-1, CTLA4, and LAG3, and the relative expressions of BCAT2, SREBF1 and USP7 were negatively correlated with the expression levels of PD-1, CTLA4, and LAG3. It is noteworthy that ENPP2 was significantly positively correlated with the expression of immune checkpoints, and its correlation with PD-1 (P = 8.7e-24, correlation coefficient R = 0.46) was particularly significant. To sum up, ENPP2 may play a key role in tumor immune escape.

Discussion

Luminal A subtype breast cancer is the most common breast cancer subtype, with high sensitivity to endocrine therapy; however, 15%−40% of patients develop endocrine resistance, leading to poor prognosis [6, 11]. Ferroptosis, an iron-dependent programmed cell death driven by lipid peroxidation, has become a promising therapeutic target for this subtype—given its reliance on lipid metabolism and the link between ferroptosis dysregulation and endocrine resistance [7, 14, 15]. This study constructed a ferroptosis-related prognostic model for Luminal A subtype breast cancer and explored its clinical and immunological implications, addressing the current lack of subtype-specific ferroptosis research.

We first identified 12 prognosis-related ferroptosis DEGs from the TCGA Luminal A cohort, then optimized them into a 9-gene prognostic model via Lasso-Cox regression. Multivariate Cox regression confirmed the model-derived risk score as an independent prognostic factor for OS (HR = 3.896, 95% CI = 2.195–6.916, p < 0.001), underscoring its clinical relevance for prognostic stratification.

Notably, compared with prior prognostic models for Luminal A subtype breast cancer, our model exhibits several advantages. First, in terms of predictive performance, Peng et al.’s [11] 10-gene ferroptosis signature showed a hazard ratio (HR) of 2.91 (95% CI: 1.83–4.62) for OS in luminal-type breast cancer, whereas our 9-gene model achieved a higher HR of 3.896 (95% CI: 2.195–6.916) in multivariate Cox analysis—indicating stronger prognostic discriminatory power. Second, unlike models that only focus on ferroptosis pathways [12], our study links model genes to immune escape mechanisms (e.g., ENPP2’s positive correlation with PD-1, R = 0.46, P = 8.7e-24), which not only improves prognostic accuracy but also provides a rationale for combining ferroptosis inducers with immune checkpoint inhibitors in endocrine-resistant Luminal A patients. Third, external validation in the METABRIC cohort confirmed that our model maintains consistent OS prediction (log-rank P < 0.001), whereas many existing models [11] lack such independent validation, limiting their translation to clinical practice. These differences highlight that our model is more robust and clinically applicable for Luminal A subtype breast cancer.

The breast cancer prognosis model constructed in this study covers nine ferroptosis genes, namely ACSL1, BCAT2, ENPP2, GABARAPL1, PDSS2, PIK3CA, SREBF1, TIMP1 and USP7. ACSL1 is involved in lipid metabolism and may interact with key molecules in the ferroptosis signaling pathway, such as glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4). Once the expression of ACSL1 changes, it may indirectly interfere with the function of GPX4, break the redox balance in cells, and then promote cells to ferroptosis [16]. BCAT2, a branched amino acid aminotransferase, regulates intracellular glutamate levels. When BCAT2 expression is up-regulated, it can affect the intracellular redox balance by regulating glutamate levels, thereby inhibiting ferroptosis [17, 18]. ENPP2, a secreted glycoprotein, catalyzes the conversion from lysophosphatidylcholine (LPC) to lysophosphatidic acid (LPA) in the extracellular matrix [19]. LPA can activate multiple signaling pathways in breast cancer cells, such as the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, thereby enhancing the proliferation of breast cancer cells [20]. LPA, by exerting its influence on intracellular signal transduction, may indirectly interfere with the function of GPX4 or other antioxidant systems related to ferroptosis. Notably, ENPP2’s role in tumor progression may involve crosstalk with inflammatory signaling: a study in breast cancer showed that the IL-8/CXCR2 axis drives vasculogenic mimicry (VM) via epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), and ENPP2—also linked to EMT regulation—might synergize with this axis to modulate Luminal A subtype’s ferroptosis sensitivity (via lipid metabolism changes) and microenvironment remodeling [21] (this synergistic effect is a hypothesis and requires experimental verification such as co-intervention experiments). GABARAPL1, belonging to the autophagy-related protein family, assumes a crucial role in the autophagy process [22]. In bone marrow macrophages in immune thrombocytopenia, the expression of GABARAPL1 is reduced. This decrease inhibits the PPAR signaling pathway, which in turn inhibits the occurrence of ferroptosis, and ultimately leads to immune imbalance [23]. The expression of GABARAPL1 frequently presents abnormalities in breast cancer cells [24]. Studies have indicated that the expression of GABARAPL1 in cancer cells is typically down-regulated. However, in breast cancer patients with positive lymph nodes, a high level of GABARAPL1 expression serves as a marker of favorable prognosis [24]. PDSS2 is a key factor in the synthesis of coenzyme Q10. When its expression is abnormal, it may lead to insufficient synthesis of coenzyme Q10, which may promote lipid peroxidation, and thus make cells more prone to ferroptosis [25, 26].

PIK3CA is one of the most common mutant genes in breast cancer [27]. When it mutates, the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway becomes abnormally activated. Consequently, cells are allowed to persistently receive growth signals, promoting the proliferation of cancer cells [28, 29]. The PIK3CA-related PI3K-Akt signaling pathway can interact with key signaling pathways for ferroptosis, such as the GPX4-lipid peroxidation pathway. If the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway is abnormally activated, it may disrupt the balance of the GPX4-lipid peroxidation pathway and change the response of tumor cells to ferroptosis inducers [30–32]. Beyond these coding gene mechanisms, non-coding RNAs may also regulate our model genes: lncRNA MALAT1, overexpressed in breast cancer, sponges miR-150-3p to upregulate genes like ABCB1 and VEGF—this axis could interact with PIK3CA (via PI3K-Akt activation) or ACSL1 (via lipid metabolism) to alter Luminal A cells’ ferroptosis vulnerability and chemotherapy response [33] (this regulatory interaction is a hypothesis and requires experimental confirmation such as dual-luciferase reporter assays and gene knockdown experiments). The sterol regulatory element encoded by the SREBF1 gene associates with transcription factor 1 and assumes a pivotal role in the regulation of lipid metabolism [34]. SREBF1 affects cell sensitivity to ferroptosis by regulating lipid metabolism [35]. In breast cancer tissue, TIMP1 can inhibit the invasion of tumor cells by down-regulating the degradation activity of matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) on type IV collagen (Col IV) [36]. Not only that, high TIMP1 expression may also activate the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway and upregulate the expression or activity of GPX4. As a consequence, the accumulation of intracellular lipid peroxides is reduced, and the sensitivity of breast cancer cells to ferroptosis is decreased. This, in turn, promotes the survival and proliferation of breast cancer cells, leading to a poor prognosis for patients [37]. As a deubiquitinizing enzyme, inhibition of USP7 can induce ferroptosis in tumor cells by targeting stearoyl CoA desaturase (SCD) [38].

Functional enrichment analysis shed light on the biological mechanisms underlying the model’s prognostic value. GO enrichment revealed associations with “lipid biosynthetic process”—a finding that aligns with Luminal A subtype’s lipid metabolism dependence and ferroptosis’ core feature of lipid peroxidation [7, 14]. KEGG analysis further linked model genes to the “FoxO signaling pathway,” a key regulator of lipid metabolism and redox homeostasis [39–42]. This pathway’s involvement suggests the model genes may modulate ferroptosis sensitivity in Luminal A cells, reinforcing their relevance to disease progression and providing a molecular basis for the model’s performance.

ssGSEA analysis revealed that high-risk groups had significantly higher infiltration of mast cells, neutrophils, and regulatory T (Treg) cells—immunosuppressive subsets known to impair anti-tumor immunity, promote tumor proliferation/invasion, and worsen prognosis in breast cancer [43, 44]. This immune infiltration pattern explains the poorer survival of high-risk patients. For immune pathways, low-risk groups showed higher type I interferon (IFN) response activity (critical for enhancing antigen-presenting cell function and activating T cell-mediated anti-tumor immunity [45]), suggesting strong type I IFN response correlates with longer disease-free survival and OS in Luminal A subtype breast cancer. In contrast, high-risk groups had elevated type II IFN (IFN-γ) response—typically pro-anti-tumor via regulating cytokine networks and recruiting anti-tumor immune cells [46]. However, this paradoxical upregulation may reflect concurrent recruitment of Treg cells by type II IFN [47] (hypothesis requiring validation via co-localization or intervention experiments); Treg-mediated inhibition of effector T cells facilitates tumor immune escape, contributing to poor prognosis [48]. Additionally, IFN-γ can augment PI3K-Akt-mTOR pathway activity, which synergistically regulates macrophage cytokine secretion and immune cell recruitment to modulate the tumor microenvironment [49]. Notably, these findings are based on public database analysis; further clinical studies are needed to validate them.

Correlation analysis between model genes and immune checkpoints highlighted ENPP2 as a key mediator of tumor immune escape. ENPP2 showed a strong positive correlation with PD-1 (r = 0.46, p = 8.7e-24)—consistent with previous studies demonstrating that ENPP2 promotes CD8⁺ T cell exhaustion via the LPA-LPAR5 axis, leading to upregulation of inhibitory receptors like PD-1 [50]. ENPP2 may also activate the PI3K-Akt pathway to enhance PD-L1 expression on tumor cells [20, 51], creating a dual mechanism for immune evasion. For endocrine-resistant Luminal A patients—a major clinical challenge—combining ferroptosis inducers (targeting the subtype’s lipid metabolism vulnerability) with anti-PD-1 inhibitors (reversing ENPP2-mediated T cell exhaustion) represents a promising therapeutic strategy, worthy of preclinical exploration.

Notably, the potential of immune-modulatory strategies—validated in other breast cancer subtypes—also provides inspiration for addressing such immune evasion in Luminal A subtype treatment. A retrospective study of advanced TNBC showed that PD-1 inhibitor combined with the GP regimen (gemcitabine + cisplatin) not only improved overall efficacy (57.14% vs. 33.33%) but also restored immune function (e.g., elevated CD3⁺, CD4⁺, and CD4⁺/CD8⁺ ratios) without increasing adverse reactions [3]. Extending this insight to Luminal A subtype—whose endocrine resistance is closely linked to dysregulated ferroptosis and reliance on lipid metabolism—combining ferroptosis inducers (targeting the lipid peroxidation vulnerability of Luminal A cells) with immune checkpoint inhibitors (e.g., anti-PD-1, to reverse ENPP2-mediated CD8⁺T cell depletion) could synergistically enhance anti-tumor effects by simultaneously disrupting tumor cell survival and restoring anti-tumor immunity (this combination strategy is a hypothesis that requires preclinical validation—e.g., animal models to verify ENPP2-mediated CD8⁺T cell depletion reversal—and subsequent clinical trials), a direction worthy of future clinical exploration.

This study has limitations that should be acknowledged. First, it relies solely on bioinformatics analysis of public databases (TCGA and METABRIC), with no experimental validation to confirm the causal roles of key model genes (e.g., ENPP2, PIK3CA) in Luminal A subtype breast cancer ferroptosis or immune escape. Second, the lack of clinical samples prevents validation of the model’s utility in guiding real-world treatment decisions. Future work will address these gaps through functional experiments (e.g., CRISPR-Cas9-mediated gene knockdown, lipid perxidation assays) and prospective clinical studies to verify the model’s performance and therapeutic potential of ENPP2.

In conclusion, we constructed a robust 9-gene ferroptosis-related prognostic model for Luminal A subtype breast cancer, which serves as an independent prognostic predictor and highlights ENPP2 as a potential target for combined ferroptosis-based therapy and immune checkpoint inhibition. This work provides a foundation for personalized risk stratification and novel therapeutic strategies in Luminal A subtype breast cancer, with direct implications for improving clinical outcomes in patients with this prevalent disease.

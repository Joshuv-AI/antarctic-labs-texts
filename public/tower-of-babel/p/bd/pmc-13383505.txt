Socioeconomic disparities in the prevalence of non-communicable chronic disease multimorbidity and their impact on activities of daily living among older adults in rural southwest China: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Guo-hui Li, Qing-rou Bao, Rui-min Shi, Fan Gu, Li Chen, Allison Rabkin Golden, Yuan Zhao, Le Cai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study investigates socioeconomic disparities in the prevalence of five non-communicable chronic diseases (NCDs) — hypertension, diabetes, coronary heart disease (CHD), stroke, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) — and their multimorbidity, and examines the relationship between NCDs multimorbidity and activities of daily living (ADL) among older adults in rural southwest China.MethodsA cross-sectional health interview and survey of 5,548 consenting adults aged ≥ 60 years was conducted. An individual socioeconomic position (SEP) index was constructed using principal component analysis. Anthropometric measurements, blood pressure, fasting blood glucose, and post-bronchodilator spirometry tests were recorded for each participant. ADL was evaluated using the Lawton scale.ResultsThe overall prevalence of hypertension, diabetes, CHD, stroke, COPD, and their multimorbidity was 65.8%, 13.7%, 8.5%, 5.9%, 7.3%, and 22.8%, respectively. Participants with a lower education level had a higher prevalence of hypertension than their counterparts (P < 0.01), while participants with better access to medical services had a higher prevalence of diabetes than their counterparts (P < 0.05). Individuals with higher annual household income and SEP had a higher prevalence of diabetes, COPD, and NCDs multimorbidity than their counterparts (P < 0.01). Multivariable binary logistic regression analysis revealed that individuals with NCDs multimorbidity had a greater probability of impairments in ADL, physical activities of daily living (PADL), and instrumental activities of daily living (IADL) (OR = 1.27, OR = 1.28, and OR = 1.79, respectively, all P < 0.01).ConclusionsSocioeconomic disparities are significantly associated with the prevalence of NCDs and their multimorbidity among older adults in rural southwest China. Multimorbidity is linked to a higher likelihood of functional limitations in ADL. This research uncovers the need to improve ADL among older adults with NCDs multimorbidity.

Introduction

Non-communicable chronic diseases (NCDs) are a global public health challenge [1], accounting for 74.0% of total global deaths in 2019 [2]. With the accelerating pace of global population aging, the disease burden of NCDs is expected to further increase [3]. China, one of the most rapidly aging countries, faces exacerbated NCDs prevalence due to their fast-growing elderly population. An aging population not only increases the burden on the healthcare system but also raises the demand for long-term care [4]. Over the past 30 years, the number of deaths due to NCDs in China has increased by 7.46 million, and the disease burden attributable to population aging is likely to continue to rise [5]. In China, the prevalence rates of hypertension and diabetes among the elderly in rural areas are growing faster than in urban areas [6].

Multimorbidity is typically defined as the coexistence of two or more chronic diseases in an individual [7]. Due to declining physical functioning and weakened immune systems, older adults are more susceptible to multiple NCDs, resulting in complex patterns of multimorbidity [8]. High prevalence of multimorbidity in older populations has been observed in both developed and developing countries [9]. In China, the prevalence of multimorbidity among older adults increased from 85.96% in 2011 to 92.24% in 2015 [8].

Research indicates substantial disparities in the prevalence of NCDs and associated risk factors among elderly populations across different socioeconomic strata [10]. Multimorbidity is significantly influenced by socioeconomic position (SEP), with empirical evidence indicating that older adults from lower SEP groups experience elevated multimorbidity risks and reduced disease management capacity [11]. Despite the escalating burden of NCDs and multimorbidity in aging populations, the mechanisms through which socioeconomic determinants influence the onset and progression of NCDs and their comorbid conditions remain unclear.

ADL serves as a fundamental indicator of an older adult’s ability to live independently and engage in social interactions. Globally, the prevalence of functional disabilities, including difficulties in performing ADL and IADL, has increased significantly in recent years due to population aging, adversely affecting quality of life among older adults [12]. In China, the prevalence of ADL and IADL impairment among older adults has also increased [13]. Previous studies have indicated that both NCDs and multimorbidity significantly elevate the risk of ADL impairment in elderly populations [14]. In rural China, this association is particularly pronounced, as limited healthcare resources, higher prevalence of chronic diseases, and lower socioeconomic levels exacerbate the impact of NCDs and multimorbidity on functional limitations among the elderly [15].

Yunnan Province, an economically underdeveloped province in the southwestern region of China, has limited and unevenly distributed healthcare resources, particularly in rural areas where the health security of the elderly is most vulnerable. While several studies have examined the prevalence of NCDs and multimorbidity among the elderly in this region, research on the relationship between socioeconomic status and the prevalence of NCDs and multimorbidity in older adults is limited. Furthermore, the impact of multimorbidity on ADL impairment among rural elderly populations remains underexplored, particularly in the context of socioeconomic disparities. Thus, this study aimed to investigate the socioeconomic disparities in the prevalence of five major NCDs (hypertension, diabetes, CHD, stroke, and COPD) and their multimorbidity, and to explore the impact of NCDs multimorbidity on the impairment of ADL among adults aged ≥ 60 years in rural Yunnan Province, China.

Methods

Study area and population

A community-based cross-sectional health survey was conducted in rural Yunnan Province from 2023 to 2024 using multi-stage stratified random sampling to select the study population. Per capita gross domestic product (GDP) was selected as an indicator of socioeconomic development, as it comprehensively reflects the overall economic resources at the county level and has been widely used in epidemiological studies in China [16]. In the first stage, all counties in Yunnan Province were categorized into three groups based on per capita GDP: economically advanced (GDP ≥ USD 8,601), moderately developed (GDP ≥ USD 6,420 and < USD 8,601), and economically disadvantaged (GDP < USD 6,420). One county was randomly selected from each category, resulting in a total of three counties. In the second stage, each selected county was further divided into two subgroups according to per capita GDP, and one township was randomly chosen from each subgroup, yielding six townships. In the third stage, the probability proportional to size (PPS) method was employed to select three villages from each township. In the final stage, a list of individuals aged ≥ 60 years was obtained from the village committee of each selected village, and a simple random sampling method using random number tables was applied to select participants among the list.

Data collection and measurement

This study employed a structured, pre-tested questionnaire, administered by trained interviewers through face-to-face interviews with participants who provided informed consent. The questionnaire collected data on demographic characteristics, family history of NCDs, and self-reported diagnoses of stroke and CHD.

The ADL status of participants was evaluated using the Lawton scale [17], which assesses both physical activities of daily living (PADL) and IADL. PADL comprises essential self-care activities, including walking, eating, dressing, grooming, bathing, and using the toilet. IADL covers more complex activities, including using public transportation, cooking, housekeeping, managing medications, doing laundry, shopping, making phone calls, and handling finances.

For all participants, anthropometric measurements — including height, weight, and waist circumference — were recorded, along with fasting blood glucose (FBG) and blood pressure (BP), including systolic blood pressure (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP).

BP was measured based on the recommendations of the American Heart Association [18]. After resting in a seated position for at least 5 min, systolic and diastolic BP were measured in the right arm using a standard mercury sphygmomanometer. BP measurements were based on the average of three BP readings.

Pulmonary function was assessed through three post-bronchodilator measurements, including pre- and post-bronchodilator forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV1) and forced vital capacity (FVC). Data collection followed standardized protocols, with detailed procedures previously documented [19, 20].

Definitions

Hypertension was defined as an average SBP ≥ 140 mmHg or DBP ≥ 90 mmHg, or the use of antihypertensive medication [21]. Diabetes mellitus was defined by an FBG level ≥ 7.0 mmol/L (126 mg/dL), self-reported use of antidiabetic medication in the two weeks prior to the study, or a previous diagnosis by a healthcare professional [22]. COPD was defined as post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC < 70%, following the Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD) definition [23]. Stroke and CHD were determined by self-reported physician diagnoses. Multimorbidity was classified as the presence of at least two NCDs among the five diseases examined in this study.

Body mass index (BMI), calculated as weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared (kg/m²), was classified into three categories: normal (18.5–24.9 kg/m²), overweight (25–27.9 kg/m²), and obese (≥ 28 kg/m²) following World Health Organization (WHO) guidelines for Asian populations. Central obesity was defined as waist circumference (WC) exceeding 90 cm for men and 80 cm for women, per WHO guidelines for Asian adults [24].

ADL evaluations were conducted using a standardized 4-level scoring system, as commonly applied in China [25]. Each activity was scored using a point system: 1 point for complete independence, 2 points for minor difficulties, 3 points for requiring assistance, and 4 points for complete inability to perform the task. Total ADL scores ranged from 14 to 56, with a score of 14 indicating normal function and scores above 14 signifying ADL impairment. The PADL subscale ranged from 6 to 24, with a score of 6 indicating normal PADL and scores above 6 indicating PADL impairment. The IADL subscale ranged from 8 to 32, with a score of 8 indicating normal IADL and scores above 8 indicating IADL impairment.

Illiteracy was defined as the inability to read or write a simple sentence related to daily life. Annual household income was classified as low (< USD 1,408) or high (≥ USD 1,408), with the median value as the cutoff point. Access to medical services was classified as good if the nearest healthcare facility could be reached with less than a 30-minute walk, and poor if the walk took ≥ 30 min.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 27.0. The mean values of BMI, SBP, DBP, FBG, and FEV1/FVC were presented as mean ± standard deviation (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\stackrel{-}{x}$$\end{document}±s). Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages. Chi-square tests were applied to compare categorical variables, and independent samples t-tests were used to assess differences in continuous variables between the two groups.

An SEP composite index was developed through principal component analysis (PCA) incorporating three key dimensions: education level, annual household income, and access to medical services. The appropriateness of the correlation matrix for factor analysis was verified using Bartlett’s sphericity test, which yielded statistically significant results of P < 0.0001. The sampling adequacy was confirmed through the Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin (KMO) measure, with values exceeding the threshold of 0.7. Component extraction was performed based on the Kaiser-Guttman criterion, retaining factors with eigenvalues greater than 1.

Multivariable binary logistic regression analysis was performed to assess the association of the prevalence of NCDs multimorbidity and impaired PADL, IADL, and ADL, after adjusting for age, sex, ethnicity, education level, annual household income, access to medical services, SEP, family history of NCDs, obesity and central obesity. The associations were expressed as adjusted odds ratios (AOR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI). All statistical significance decisions were based on two-tailed P values of < 0.05.

Results

A total of 5,700 individuals aged ≥ 60 years were invited to participate in the survey. Of these, 5,548 consented, yielding an overall response rate of 97.3%.

The results of PCA with Bartlett’s test (P < 0.0001) and the KMO Measure (0.72) confirmed that the inter-variable correlations were adequate for conducting the PCA. Only the first component with eigenvalues > 1 was retained, explaining 50.2% of the total variance in the data. The component score coefficients of educational level, annual household income, and access to medical services were 0.66, 0.61, and 0.57, respectively. Only one component was retained to define the SEP index. The SEP index was subsequently divided into two categories: low and high, with the median value as the cut-off point.

Table 1 summarizes the demographic characteristics of the participants. A total of 2,767 males (49.9%) and 2,781 females (50.1%) participated. Females had a higher prevalence of illiteracy, obesity, and central obesity than males, while males had a higher SEP than females (P < 0.01). Mean BMI and SBP, and proportion of FEV1/FVC was significantly higher in females than in males (P < 0.01).

Table 2 presents the prevalence of five NCDs and multimorbidity by socioeconomic status in rural Yunnan Province, China. The overall prevalence of hypertension, diabetes, CHD, stroke, COPD, and multimorbidity of the five studied NCDs among those surveyed was 65.8%, 13.7%, 8.5%, 5.9%, 7.3%, and 22.8%, respectively. Females had a higher prevalence of hypertension than males (P < 0.01), whereas males had a higher prevalence of COPD than females (P < 0.05). Prevalence of hypertension, stroke, CHD, COPD, and NCDs multimorbidity increased with age (P < 0.01), with the highest prevalence of diabetes found among participants aged 65–69 years (P < 0.05). Ethnic minority participants had a higher prevalence of COPD than Han majority participants (P < 0.01), while the Han participants had a higher prevalence of stroke than the ethnic minority participants (P < 0.01). Participants with a lower education level had a higher prevalence of hypertension than their counterparts (P < 0.01), while participants with good access to medical services had a higher prevalence of diabetes than their counterparts (P < 0.05). Individuals with higher annual household income and SEP had a higher prevalence of diabetes, COPD and NCDs multimorbidity than their counterparts (P < 0.01).

Discussion

The findings revealed that there are substantial socioeconomic disparities in the prevalence of NCDs and their multimorbidity among elderly populations in rural southwest China. Furthermore, they demonstrated that older adults with NCDs multimorbidity exhibited significantly higher probabilities of impairment in PADL, IADL, and ADL.

In this study, the prevalence of hypertension, CHD, and COPD among rural older adults was higher than the national average in China [26] as well as higher than in other developing countries [27, 28]. In contrast, the prevalence of diabetes and stroke in this study was lower than the national average in China [26] and lower than in other developing nations [27, 28]. These findings highlight regional disparities in NCD patterns, suggesting that hypertension, CHD, and COPD are particularly prevalent among older adults in rural southwestern China. Tailored health interventions that account for the specific disease burden in this population are essential to enhance prevention and management strategies and ultimately improve health outcomes for elderly individuals in rural areas.

Numerous studies have found that the older adults with lower SEP exhibit a higher prevalence of diabetes and COPD [20, 29], and lower household income and SEP are associated with a higher risk of NCDs multimorbidity [30, 31]. However, our findings revealed inverse associations: in our study, older adults with higher SEP had a higher prevalence of diabetes and COPD, while older adults with higher household income and SEP had a higher prevalence of NCDs multimorbidity. Over the past two decades, the burden of NCDs has increased in rural China, with many modifiable risk factors, such as obesity and smoking, showing little improvement and even worsening over time [6]. Studies from China and other low- and middle-income countries have shown a more rapid increase in obesity and central obesity among high-income populations. In our study, a higher prevalence of obesity, central obesity, and smoking was also observed among individuals with higher income and SEP [32]. This may explain the inconsistency between our results and previous findings. Additionally, disparities in access to healthcare services exist across socioeconomic groups, with individuals of higher SEP typically having greater access to healthcare services and more frequent health examinations [33], which may increase the likelihood of disease detection and contribute to potential detection bias. Further research is needed to clarify the relationship between income, SEP, and the prevalence of NCDs and their multimorbidity.

While studies in developed countries have found that higher educational attainment is associated with reduced risk of NCDs multimorbidity [34], our findings revealed no such association between education level and the prevalence of NCDs multimorbidity. This discrepancy may be attributed to recent widespread implementation of health interventions in rural areas and improved health awareness among rural populations in China, which have potentially narrowed the educational disparity in chronic disease prevalence [35]. The relationship between education level and multimorbidity appears more complex in low- and middle-income countries compared to developed nations [36]. Further research is warranted to fully elucidate the nature of this association.

Our findings demonstrate that cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) increase the risk of ADL impairment in older adults, which is consistent with previous studies [37]. Notably, the impact of different types of CVDs on ADL varies considerably, with both hypertension and stroke associated with ADL impairment, while CHD only exhibited higher probabilities of PADL impairment. Furthermore, stroke exhibited the most severe consequences of ADL impairment among the three types of CVDs; the neurological damage caused by stroke may lead to permanent disability, significantly impairing both motor and cognitive functions [38]. In addition, poorly controlled hypertension, a major modifiable risk factor for CVD progression, can induce multi-system damage, resulting in deterioration of motor coordination and cognitive performance [39]. We recommend implementing standardized blood pressure monitoring and long-term management in elderly populations to prevent cardiovascular complications as well as giving special attention to stroke patients as early rehabilitation interventions are crucial for optimizing functional recovery.

Our study also indicated that COPD increases PADL impairment risk in elderly populations. Patients with COPD experience reduced physical capacity due to respiratory limitations, and prolonged inactivity may exacerbate functional decline [40]. Timely interventions to improve pulmonary function are warranted to minimize detrimental impacts on PADL.

The study findings reveal a significantly elevated risk of impairment in PADL, IADL, and ADL among individuals with NCDs multimorbidity, corroborating previous research [41]. Empirical evidence suggests that the complex disease interactions, polypharmacy, and compounded health challenges associated with multiple chronic conditions substantially increase the risk of physical limitations and functional decline [42]. Our analysis identified PADL as the most significantly affected domain in multimorbid patients. The cumulative burden of multiple health conditions has been shown to directly compromise physical functioning in older adults, thereby diminishing independence and increasing reliance on caregiving support [43]. These findings underscore the imperative for developing comprehensive health management strategies tailored to multimorbid elderly populations and aimed at mitigating functional deterioration and enhancing quality of life.

The study findings are limited in several ways. First, the cross-sectional study design inherently restricts the ability to infer causal relationships between socioeconomic disparities, multimorbidity, and ADL. Second, the prevalence of stroke and CHD were determined through self-reporting, which may be subject to recall bias or misclassification, potentially leading to underestimation. Third, diabetes diagnoses were determined through self-reports and FBG measurements, without incorporating glycosylated hemoglobin (HbA1c) testing. This may have resulted in conservative prevalence estimates. Fourth, prevalence of multimorbidity in our study was based only on five NCDs; the prevalence of multimorbidity in our study may be underestimated. Fifth, the use of per capita GDP as a proxy for regional socioeconomic status may not fully capture the multidimensional nature of socioeconomic conditions, potentially affecting the interpretation of the observed associations. Alternative indicators, such as the human development index (HDI) or composite socioeconomic measures, may provide a more comprehensive assessment and could be considered in future research. Finally, the present findings were based on a study of random sampling of three counties in Yunnan province, limiting their generalizability.

Conclusions

In sum, the present study indicates the importance of early identification and management of NCDs and their multimorbidity among older adults, and draws attention to socioeconomic disparities, particularly among elderly individuals with higher income and SEP. In addition, this research uncovered the need to improve ADL among elderly individuals with NCDs multimorbidity.

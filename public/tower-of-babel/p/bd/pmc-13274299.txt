Retraction: Breast cancer cell-derived exosome-delivered microRNA-155 targets UBQLN1 in adipocytes and facilitates cancer cachexia-related fat loss
Published: 2026

This is a retraction of S Sun, Z Wang, F Yao, K Sun, Z Li, S Sun, C Li, Breast cancer cell-derived exosome-delivered microRNA-155 targets UBQLN1 in adipocytes and facilitates cancer cachexia-related fat loss, Human Molecular Genetics, Volume 32, Issue 13, 1 July 2023, Pages 2219–2228, https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/ddad055

In November 2025, the corresponding author contacted the journal requesting that the article be retracted, explaining that post publication an internal review of the analytical workflow used in the study was performed and identified an issue in the data-processing that may affect the robustness of the results. During subsequent follow-up studies aimed at extending their work on exosomal miRNAs in cancer cachexia, they re-evaluated the RT-qPCR assay used to quantify miR-155 expression in cells, exosomes, and mouse adipose tissues (as presented in Figures 4A–C, 5D, 5F and related analyses). They re-analyzed the primer sequences used for miR-155 detection and sequenced representative qPCR amplification products. They found that the original assay lacked sufficient specificity and showed evidence of cross-amplification with closely related small RNA sequences.

In particular, the forward and reverse primers used for miR-155 quantification were originally designed based on previously reported sequences. However, upon re-evaluation, the corresponding author noticed that the primer pair may not sufficiently discriminate mature miR-155 from closely related homologous sequences; and sequencing of the PCR products confirmed the presence of non-exclusive miR-155 amplification. As a result, the quantitative measurements of miR-155 expression in exosomes, adipocytes and mouse adipose tissues do not accurately reflect specific mature miR-155 levels. This issue was not apparent during the original experiments but became evident only after more stringent validation during subsequent work.

Given that the quantitative data supporting altered miR-155 expression is not reliable, the causal link between exosomal miR-155 and UBQLN1 downregulation cannot be supported with sufficient confidence. Therefore, the authors believe the primary mechanistic conclusion of the article is undermined.

The Editors conducted an investigation in accordance with guidelines from the Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE) and determined that confidence in the reliability of the results reported in the paper has been undermined and agreed with the corresponding author that this article should be retracted. The coauthors confirmed the corresponding author’s request for the retraction and agreed to publication of this retraction notice; no response was received from the second, fourth, or fifth coauthors regarding retraction of this article.

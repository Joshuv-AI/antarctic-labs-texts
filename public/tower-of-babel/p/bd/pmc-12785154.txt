Unlocking tillering repression through TEOSINTE BRANCHED-B1 mutagenesis enhances wheat grain yield
Authors: Xiangqing Liu, Long Song, Xiliu Cheng, Yanming Ma, Haoran Wang, Yiqing Wang, Jinghui Li, Hongcui Pei, Zefu Lu, Bin Yang, Mingyi Zhang, Qingyi Wang, Moussa Benhamed, Zhongfu Ni, Qixin Sun, Jie Liu
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

Wheat (Triticum aestivum) is one of the world’s most important staple food crops, providing approximately 20% of human caloric intake (Cheng et al., 2024). The development of modern high-yield wheat varieties requires a semi-dwarf plant architecture, as reduced plant height improves lodging resistance and harvest index, thereby enhancing yield potential and stability. Since the 1960s, two plant height-reducing genes, Reduced height-B1b (Rht-B1b; the dominant mutant form of Rht-B1a) and Rht-D1b (the dominant mutant form of Rht-D1a), have been used worldwide to confer semi-dwarf plant architecture. These genes, known as the “Green Revolution” genes, provide desirable reductions in plant height and increases in tiller number. However, they also strongly reduce grain weight and nitrogen-use efficiency, which partially attenuates the yield potential of semi-dwarf wheat varieties (Minakuchi et al., 2010; Liu et al., 2022; Song et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2023).

To overcome the yield penalties associated with Rht-B1b and Rht-D1b, our recent study proposed a new strategy involving the use of r-e-z haploblock deletion (Song et al., 2023) and highlighted the potential of brassinosteroid signaling to drive the next Green Revolution (Tena, 2023). The r-e-z haploblock deletion simultaneously removes three closely linked genes—Rht-B1, EamA-B, and ZnF-B—thereby enabling the breeding of new semi-dwarf wheat varieties. Compared with commercially cultivated Green Revolution wheat varieties carrying Rht-B1b and Rht-D1b, wheat carrying the introgression of r-e-z haploblock deletion exhibits a more compact architecture, larger and heavier spikes, higher thousand-grain weight, improved nitrogen-use efficiency, and significantly increased grain yield under field conditions at the population level (Supplemental Figure 1) (Song et al., 2023).

Despite these advantages, the r-e-z haploblock deletion also reduces tillering, as evidenced by a marked decline in tiller number in NIL-Heng (a near-isogenic line carrying the r-e-z deletion) compared with NIL-Shi (a near-isogenic line carrying Rht-B1b, EamA-B, and ZnF-B) under field conditions (Figures 1A and 1B; Supplemental Figure 2). This results in a lower spike number per unit area (Song et al., 2023), thereby limiting yield potential. To investigate the molecular mechanisms by which r-e-z reduces tillering, we performed whole-genome resequencing of NIL-Heng to precisely genotype the r-e-z deletion. Alignment with the Chinese Spring wheat reference genome (IWGSC RefSeq v.1.1) revealed a ∼210-kb deletion (from position 29 988 503 to 30 199 154) in the intergenic region between TraesCS4B02G042600 (a RuvB-like helicase gene) and TraesCS4B02G042700 (the TEOSINTE BRANCHED 1 gene on chromosome 4B, hereafter referred to as TB-B1), as well as a ∼542-kb deletion (from position 30 534 297 to 31 076 293) resulting in the loss of Rht-B1, EamA-B, and ZnF-B (Figure 1C). Targeted PCR amplification confirmed the absence of these fragments in NIL-Heng (Supplemental Figure 3).

RNA sequencing of field-collected tiller buds from NIL-Heng and NIL-Shi revealed that Rht-B1, EamA-B, and ZnF-B were either undetectable or expressed at extremely low levels in NIL-Heng, whereas they were readily detectable in NIL-Shi (Figure 1D). Notably, TB-B1, located near the left boundary of the 542-kb deletion, was significantly upregulated in NIL-Heng relative to NIL-Shi (Figure 1D). Importantly, only TB-B1 was transcriptionally activated in NIL-Heng, whereas its homeologs TB-A1 and TB-D1 were not (Figure 1E), indicating a localized effect of the r-e-z deletion on TB-B1 activation.

Previous studies in maize (Zea mays), Arabidopsis thaliana, rice (Oryza sativa), and wheat have shown that elevated expression of TB1 or its orthologs reduces shoot branching and tiller number (Doebley et al., 1997; Aguilar-Martínez et al., 2007; Minakuchi et al., 2010; Dixon et al., 2018, 2020; Zhang et al., 2024). Consistent with these findings, overexpression of TB-B1 in the wheat cultivar “Fielder” (TB1-OE, lines #2 and #8) resulted in significantly fewer tillers than wild-type controls (Figure 1F; Supplemental Figure 4). These results suggest that TB-B1 activation in NIL-Heng underlies its low-tiller-number phenotype.

We hypothesized that the r-e-z haploblock deletion alters the local chromatin state around TB-B1, thereby influencing its expression. Chromatin accessibility and histone modification assays using field-collected tiller buds from NIL-Heng and NIL-Shi revealed stronger ATAC-seq signals, higher H3K27ac (a histone mark associated with gene activation), and lower H3K27me3 (a histone mark associated with gene silencing) in the NIL-Heng TB-B1 region (Figure 1G). These findings suggest that the r-e-z deletion enhances chromatin accessibility and establishes an active chromatin state at the TB-B1 locus, leading to its transcriptional activation.

Notably, cold exposure (4°C, mimicking vernalization) strongly induced TB-B1 expression in NIL-Heng seedlings, with peak expression observed after 21 days of exposure (Figure 1H). In contrast, TB-A1 and TB-D1 expression were unaffected by cold treatment (Figure 1H). These results are consistent with transcriptome data showing that TB-B1 expression was significantly higher in samples collected before wintering than in those collected after wintering (Figure 1E), suggesting that TB-B1 exhibits a specific transcriptional response to cold exposure. Chromatin immunoprecipitation coupled with qPCR also revealed that cold exposure increased H3K27ac and decreased H3K27me3 levels in the TB-B1 region of NIL-Heng compared to NIL-Shi (Figure 1I). These results suggest that, in addition to alterations in chromatin state, a temperature-sensitive upstream transcriptional regulator is required for TB-B1 activation in NIL-Heng.

To identify potential upstream regulators that specifically affect TB-B1 activation, we predicted transcription factor binding sites in the TB-A1, TB-B1, and TB-D1 promoters using the PlantTFDB database (https://planttfdb.gao-lab.org). This analysis identified a NAC (NAM, ATAF1/2, and CUC1/2) binding site located at −1171 to −1145 relative to the TB-B1 start codon, a site that was absent from the TB-A1 and TB-D1 promoters (Figure 1J; Supplemental Figure 5). For phylogenetic analysis, we selected eight NAC TFs (NAC058-D, NAC047-D, NAC074-A, NAC086-D, NAC025-A, NAC042-B, NAC090-B, and NAC007-B) from different clades of the wheat NAC family phylogenetic tree (Supplemental Figure 6A). Yeast one-hybrid assays confirmed that all of these candidates bound to the TB-B1 promoter, with NAC047-D (hereafter abbreviated as NAC047) showing the strongest binding signal (Supplemental Figures 6B and 6C; Supplemental Data 1). Electrophoretic mobility shift assays further confirmed the binding of NAC047 to the TB-B1 promoter (Figure 1K). Quantitative reverse-transcription PCR (qRT-PCR) revealed that NAC047 expression was strongly induced by cold treatment in both NIL-Heng and NIL-Shi (Figure 1L). Transient expression assays in Nicotiana benthamiana demonstrated that cold exposure promoted NAC047 nuclear accumulation (Figure 1M); furthermore, they supported the transactivation of TB-B1 by NAC047, particularly under cold conditions (Figure 1N; Supplemental Figure 7). These findings provide evidence that NAC047 is a cold-sensitive upstream regulator that facilitates TB-B1 activation during vernalization.

Transcriptome analysis identified 1803 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in NIL-Heng compared with NIL-Shi, including 838 upregulated and 965 downregulated genes (Supplemental Figure 8A; Supplemental Data 2). Gene Ontology enrichment analysis showed that upregulated DEGs were associated with the GO terms “response to oxidative stress,” “polysaccharide catabolic process,” and “chitin catabolic process,” whereas downregulated DEGs were associated with “1,3-beta-D-glucan synthase activity” and “actin filament-based movement” (Supplemental Figures 8B and 8C; Supplemental Data 3). qRT-PCR further validated the expression patterns of selected DEGs (Supplemental Figure 8D).

Given that TB-B1 activation reduces tillering and yield potential, we performed ethyl methanesulfonate (EMS)-induced mutagenesis in the NIL-Heng background to disrupt TB-B1 function. We identified three independent mutant lines, each carrying a missense mutation in TB-B1: (1) m804 carries a C433T single-nucleotide mutation, resulting in a Leu145Phe substitution; (2) m1347 carries a G755T single-nucleotide mutation, resulting in an Arg252Met substitution; and (3) m2294 carries a G811A single-nucleotide mutation, resulting in an Ala271Thr substitution (Supplemental Figure 9). Notably, the G-to-A mutation in m2294 partially mimics a previously characterized natural C-to-T mutation in modern wheat cultivars that leads to an Ala271Val substitution (Dixon et al., 2018). As expected, all three lines exhibited significantly increased tiller numbers relative to NIL-Heng (Figure 1O; Supplemental Figures 10 and 11) with minimal or negligible pleiotropic effects on other agronomic and yield-related traits, including spikelet number per spike, thousand-grain weight, and grain weight per spike (Supplemental Figures 10 and 11). Consequently, these mutants showed 18.59%, 18.73%, and 17.69% higher grain yields per plant than NIL-Heng, respectively (Figure 1O; Supplemental Figure 11). Field evaluation of two mutant lines, m1347 and m2294, in standard field plots further showed that TB-B1 mutations increased the effective spike number per unit by 9.01% and 12.02% relative to NIL-Heng (Figure 1P). At the population level, these lines produced 2.54%–8.61% higher grain yields per plot than NIL-Heng (Figure 1Q; Supplemental Figure 12), which indicates that the TB-B1 mutation effectively enhances yield components in r-e-z-introgressed lines.

In summary, this study demonstrates that the r-e-z haploblock deletion negatively regulates tiller number by inducing ectopic activation of the neighboring TB-B1 gene, mediated by altered local chromatin states caused by the large fragment deletion. By introducing missense mutations into TB-B1, we disrupted its function, achieving a significant increase in tiller number and grain yield. Therefore, we propose that co-introgression of the genetically linked r-e-z haploblock deletion and a loss-of-function TB-B1 allele into wheat varieties could enhance the yield potential of r-e-z, offering a promising strategy for breeding high-yield wheat varieties.

Letter to the Editor about the article: by C. Eskander, et al, “Magnetocardiography for noninvasive surveillance of rejection and cardiac allograft vasculopathy in heart transplant recipients,” Am. Hear. J. Plus Cardiol. Res. Pract., vol. 62, p. 100704,…
Authors: Donatella Brisinda, Peter Fenici, Riccardo Fenici
Published: 2026

Letter to the Editor

We read with great interest the study by Christian Eskander et al., entitled “Magnetocardiography for Noninvasive Surveillance of Rejection and Cardiac Allograft Vasculopathy in Heart Transplant Recipients” [1], which reviews the existing literature on the preliminary application of magnetocardiography (MCG) as a non-invasive tool for post-transplant surveillance. The article addresses the early detection of acute cardiac rejection and the monitoring of time-related development of cardiac allograft vasculopathy, the principal cause of graft failure and late mortality in heart transplant recipients. The topic is highly relevant, particularly in view of the need for safer and more effective, possibly also non-invasive, long-term monitoring strategies in heart transplant recipients.

The authors deserve recognition for drawing attention to a relatively underexplored clinical application of contactless MCG, which, after a few pioneering single-center studies, was largely overlooked. We fully agree with their prudent recommendation against assuming the validity of positive findings derived from a very limited number of studies, small patient cohorts, or isolated case reports, particularly when conducted with non-standardized and heterogeneous instrumentation and analytic methodologies.

At the same time, as noted in the article, technological advances have led to the development of novel non-cryogenic MCG devices that are considerably easier to use in clinical high-throughput settings, with reduced costs and minimal need for highly specialized personnel. This improved technological landscape may facilitate the timely design of multicenter clinical trials based on standardized analytic protocols aimed at validating the diagnostic accuracy of MCG for non-invasive monitoring of heart transplant recipients. Such efforts would be highly valuable because, once validated and incorporated into routine follow-up, MCG could enhance patient safety and improve quality of life, particularly in pediatric populations, by reducing the need for repeated invasive procedures and potentially supporting a new paradigm for monitoring immunosuppressive therapy efficacy.

Given our deep appreciation for the high scientific standards, methodological rigour, and editorial quality that characterize the AHJ Plus: Cardiology Research & Practice Special Issue on Magnetocardiography, we feel it is our responsibility to bring your attention to the following concerns regarding the above-mentioned manuscript:

Images 2 and 7 appear to have been reproduced directly from Figs. 3 and 7 of our publication [2], without appropriate citation of the reference source. More importantly, the original Fig. 3 (reproduced here as Fig. 1 in its original format) has been cropped and inaccurately described in the text, resulting in a distortion of its scientific meaning. Proper citation and presentation in its complete form would have strengthened the authors' statement (at the end of paragraph 1.1) that MCG enables the “identification of subtle changes which are unable to be detected with the electrocardiogram and other routine non-invasive testing.”

Copy of the original Fig. 3 (with related legend), as published in Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine[2].

As extensively explained in the original publication [2], that figure, based on the unique and original protocol of simultaneous high-resolution MCG mapping and recording of a right atrial monophasic action potential using the property amagnetic catheter technique [3], provided the first clinical demonstration that the MCG “ramp-like” pattern along the PR interval (Fig. 3A), previously interpreted as originating from His–Purkinje activity [4], is actually generated by atrial repolarization. Furthermore, it demonstrated that the weak His–Purkinje magnetic field (approximately 200 fT) is detectable and localizable during the final 40–50 ms of the PR interval only after appropriate subtraction of the overlapping atrial repolarization field, which is nearly ten times stronger (His–Purkinje's magnetic field marked with a red asterisk in Fig. 3B). The cropped version currently presented in the article does not accurately convey this mechanistic interpretation and therefore risks distorting the scientific message, besides being also aesthetically questionable according to scientific journal quality standards.

A more detailed discussion of this issue is available in our recent contribution to the same AHJ Plus Special Issue.

In view of the scientific relevance of this matter, we suggest and respectfully request that, in accordance with the quality standards of the Special Issue, Image 2 should either be reproduced in its entirety with the original legend, clearly explained in the text and accompanied by the appropriate citation as required by Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine, or removed from the article.

Explicitly indicating that Images 2 and 7 are reproduced with permission and properly citing the original source in the manuscript would prevent failure to acknowledge the original authors and the copyright holders and would ensure compliance with the citation and figure-reuse policies of Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine.

Finally, we note that the DOI doi:https://doi.org/10.1253/circj.CJ-12-1248 reported in reference no. 1 of the paper corresponds to a Letter to the Editor published in Circulation Journal (2013; Volume 77, Issue 2, p. 550).

We are confident that these observations will be received by you and the authors in the constructive and collegial spirit in which they are offered, with the shared goal of further strengthening the accurate scientific presentation and adherence to established publication standards, thereby preserving the integrity and quality of the Special Issue.

With kind regards,

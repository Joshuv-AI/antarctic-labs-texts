Integrated quantum chemical and in vitro investigation of Capsanthin antioxidant activity: Mechanism (HAT), cultivar variability, enhanced bioavailability, and key gene expression in peppers
Authors: Yakun Zhang, Yvxue Bai, Likun Cheng, Yue Hu, Junli Lv, Yan Ren, Fucheng Guo, Jun Lu, Guoze Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Capsanthin, the main bioactive component in red peppers, has potent antioxidant properties; however, its biosynthetic regulation and antioxidant mechanisms require further elucidation. In this study, we investigated 10 pepper cultivars through integrated quantum chemical calculations and in vitro assays. Theoretical calculations revealed that the antioxidant mechanism of capsanthin involves hydrogen atom transfer. Microwave-assisted extraction coupled with macroporous resin purification revealed significant interspecific variation in the capsanthin content. In vitro evaluation showed concentration-dependent antioxidant activity, with Bayan exhibiting the strongest capacity. The antioxidant properties and bioavailability of chili peppers increased after in vitro digestion. The results of the gene expression analysis revealed LUT5 as the most highly expressed gene across cultivars, followed by CrtR and Ccs. Enzyme activity assays revealed that the activity of CRTR-β consistently exceeded that of CCS, which was correlated with gene expression levels.

Introduction

Capsicum annuum L., a member of the Solanaceae family comprising about 41 species, is an important vegetable and spice crop in China. It also serves as an important dietary source of bioactive compounds, including vitamin C (Vc), carotenoids (notably capsanthin), and polyphenols (flavonoids and phenolic acids). Capsanthin is a liposoluble natural pigment and serves as a core functional compound. It is considered by the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) and the World Health Organization (WHO) as a Class A colorant (permitted for use without quantitative limits) and is valued for its high tinctorial strength, strong coloring power, excellent color stability, and high safety profile. The extraction of capsanthin relies primarily on organic solvent methods (e.g., ethanol immersion extraction) (Avilés-Betanzos et al., 2023); however, this approach results in extremely high consumption of chili peppers, yields extracts of low purity, fails to completely extract the target compound, and limits its practical application. Thus, microwave-assisted and ultrasound-assisted extraction methods are generally combined for extracting capsanthin (Viñas-Ospino et al., 2023).

Capsanthin not only exhibits excellent antioxidant activity but also prevents and treats oxidative stress-related diseases (Saini et al., 2022); thus, it has garnered significant attention in functional foods and pharmaceutical applications. The antioxidant mechanisms of capsanthin primarily involve (i) direct scavenging of free radicals (Halliwell, 2024), (ii) upregulation of antioxidant enzyme activities (Hu et al., 2024), and (iii) modulation of signaling pathways associated with oxidative stress (Fan et al., 2024). However, the capsanthin content varies by more than three-fold across cultivars, likely rooted in the coordinated regulation of key biosynthetic genes such as capsanthin-capsorubin synthase (Ccs), β-ring hydroxylase (LUT5), and CrtR (β-carotene hydroxylase) (Li et al., 2023a). Among these, Capsanthin/Capsorubin synthase (CCS) and β-carotene hydroxylase (CRTR-β) are key enzymes that govern fruit coloration. Consequently, the application of capsanthin remains constrained by significant intercultivar variations in biosynthetic efficiency and the lack of theoretical insights into antioxidant structure-activity relationships. Although conventional antioxidant assays (e.g., ABTS and FRAP) can quantify its activity, they fail to reveal the mechanisms underlying electron transfer at the molecular level (Li, Tan, et al., 2023). Although density functional theory (DFT) can elucidate the antioxidant mechanisms of natural products (e.g., predicting free radical scavenging pathways via HOMO/LUMO energy levels) (Wang et al., 2024), its application in capsid structure-activity relationship studies remains unexplored.

Standardized in vitro digestion models are widely used to evaluate changes in food components, including structural transformations, bioaccessibility, and digestibility, demonstrating their value as reliable tools for food and pharmaceutical analysis (Hinssen et al., 2024). Although studies using in vitro digestion models have reported that capsanthin bioavailability is affected by lipid carriers (Hinssen, Mensink, Huppertz, & van der Wielen, et al., 2024), intercultivar variations and their linkages to gene expression remain undetermined.

In this study, we investigated 10 pepper cultivars through integrated quantum chemical calculations and in vitro assays. The research framework included (1) optimizing macroporous resin purification protocols; (2) DFT analysis of the antioxidant mechanisms of capsanthin; (3) comprehensive in vitro antioxidant evaluations (DPPH/ABTS scavenging, ferric reducing power); (4) simulated digestion experiments; and (5) simultaneous determination of key enzyme activities (CCS and CRTR-β) and gene expression levels (Ccs, LUT5, and CrtR) to construct component-gene correlation models.

Materials and methods

Materials

We purchased 50 red capsanthin varieties (Appendix A) from the Baotou Academy of Agricultural Sciences. An analytical-grade capsanthin standard (HPLC ≥95%) was purchased from Shanghai Zzbio Co., Ltd., and 95% ethanol was purchased from Tianjin Xinbotai Chemical Co., Ltd. AB-8/XAD16 macroporous resins, digestive enzymes (pepsin and pancreatin), and porcine bile salts were purchased from Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology Co., Ltd. Molecular biology reagents, including CTAB-based RNA extraction kits and cDNA synthesis kits, were purchased from Wuhan Serweier Biotechnology Co., Ltd., and D101 resin was purchased from Hangzhou Xingru Instrument Equipment Department. Kits for α-amylase (40,000 U/g activity) and carotenoid enzyme ELISA (A-P1113B) were purchased from Shandong Longkete Enzyme Preparation Co., Ltd. and Jiangsu Enzyme Label Biotechnology Co., Ltd., respectively.

Computational analysis of the antioxidant activity of capsanthin

Computational modeling and methodologies

The initial molecular geometry of capsanthin was constructed using ChemDraw and optimized using the Gaussian 09 computational package. The initial optimization was performed at the B3LYP/6–311 g level via DFT and then further optimized at the B3LYP/6–31 g** level to ensure that there were no imaginary frequencies. The structural parameters (bond lengths and angles) were validated against experimental data reported by Requena et al. (2008). The thermodynamic properties, including the antioxidant enthalpy, radical cation enthalpy, neutral radical enthalpy, and anion enthalpy at the C3 and C28 positions, were computed at the B3LYP/6–31 + g* level with the SMD solvation model (ethanol solvent). Subsequently, key thermodynamic parameters, including the bond dissociation enthalpy (BDE) and adiabatic ionization potential (IP), were computed.

Purification of capsanthin

10 pepper varieties(Appendix B) representing eight fruit types with significant pungency variation—including ‘Bayan’ as low-pungency control—were selected from Yunnan-Guizhou-Sichuan germplasms adapted to Inner Mongolia. The optimal capsanthin extraction parameters were established by conducting microwave-assisted single-factor experiments and response surface methodology, yielding a maximum content of 47.090 mg/g under the following conditions: liquid-to-solid ratio of 6:1 (mL/g), microwave power of 600 W, extraction time of 30 min, and temperature of 60 °C. The experimental results strongly agreed with the model predictions (accepted but not yet published). Subsequently, following the method of Hu (2022),Capsanthin extracts (2 mg/mL) underwent dynamic adsorption on D101 macroporous resin (70 mL loading volume) with subsequent 95% ethanol elution (80 mL, 1.5 BV/h). Eluates were vacuum-concentrated (40 °C) for analysis.

Determination of the capsanthin concentration

The capsanthin content was quantified via high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC). HPLC separation was performed using an Acclaim™ C18 column (4.6 × 250 mm, 5 μm) with isocratic elution (methanol:water = 94:6, v/v) at 1.0 mL/min. The column temperature was maintained at 30 °C, with detection at 472 nm and a 10 μL injection volume. The leakage point was defined as the stage at which the mass concentration of the effluent reached 10% of the initial concentration of the solution (Hu, 2022). All samples were measured in triplicate, with data analysis conducted using SPSS 23 and graphical representations generated using Origin 2018.

Study on the antioxidant properties of capsanthin in different varieties of chili peppers

Bayan was the only sweet pepper cultivar among the 10 tested varieties. Based on the results of the quantitative HPLC analysis, three cultivars with distinct concentration gradients, including Bayan (high concentration: 658 mg/g), 22–14 (medium concentration: 327 mg/g), and L24 (low concentration: 21 mg/g), were selected for further mechanistic investigation.

Determination of the reducing power

Following the method described in another study (Yang et al., 2023), 0.5 mL of sample mixture was mixed with 2.5 mL of 0.2 M phosphate buffer (pH 6.6) and 2.5 mL of 1% (w/v) potassium ferricyanide in sequence. The mixture was homogenized by vortexing and incubated at 50 °C for 20 min in a precision water bath. After incubation, sequential additions were performed: 2.5 mL of 10% (w/v) trichloroacetic acid, 5 mL of ultrapure water, and 1 mL of 0.1% (w/v) ferric chloride solution. After the contents were mixed by vortexing, the reaction mixture was left undisturbed for 10 min. Antioxidant properties and bioaccessibility of different chili pepper cultivars during in vitro digestion

In vitro digestion of different chili pepper cultivars

Simulated salivary fluid (SSF), simulated gastric fluid (SGF), and simulated intestinal fluid (SIF) stock solutions were prepared following the methods described in another study (Minekus et al., 2014).

Oral phase: First, 3 g pepper powder was homogenized with 5 mL of SSF electrolyte (50:50 w/v), after which 0.5 mL of α-amylase (to adjust the concentration to 75 U/mL) and 1 mL of CaCl2.2H2O (to reach 0.75 mM) were added. The mixture was incubated at 37 °C with constant agitation (200 rpm) for 10 min.

Gastric phase: The oral digestate was combined with SGF electrolyte (50:50, v/v), followed by the addition of 5 mL of porcine pepsin solution (25,000 U/mL), 0.1 mL of 0.3 M CaCl2, and 0.55 mL of 1 M HCl to adjust the pH to 3.0. The final volume was adjusted with ultrapure water, and the mixture was incubated at 37 °C under constant agitation (200 rpm) for 2 h.

Intestinal phase: The gastric chyme was combined with 10 mL of SIF electrolyte (50:50, v/v), 5 mL of pancreatin solution (800 U/mL trypsin), 2.5 mL of bile salt solution (formulated to reach 10 mM), 0.4 mL of 0.3 M CaCl2, and 0.15 mL of 1 M NaOH to adjust the pH to 7.0. The final volume was adjusted with ultrapure water, followed by incubation at 37 °C for 2 h under constant agitation (200 rpm).

Comparative analysis of bond lengths and angles in capsanthin

As shown in Appendices D and E, the mean absolute deviations of the bond lengths between the calculated and experimental values were 0.038291 Å and 0.034934 Å, with key angle deviations of 0.2757° and 0.6602°, respectively. The C1–C1b bond length agreed with the experimental data, whereas other critical bonds (C9–C9a, C13–C13a, C23–C24, and C30–C31) were minimal, amounting to only 0.001 Å. The bond angles of C5a–C5–C6, C9A–C9–C10, C13a–C13–C14, and C14–C15–C16 exhibited deviations of only −0.106°, −0.499°, −0.055°, and 0.006°, respectively. The selected calculation method is appropriate, and the results are reliable.

Molecular analysis of antioxidant activity in capsanthin

In the hydrogen atom transfer (HAT) mechanism, the BDE serves as a critical parameter for evaluating radical scavenging capacity (Rohman et al., 2023). A lower BDE value indicates a stronger HAT ability and higher radical scavenging activity. For the single electron transfer proton transfer (SETPT) mechanism, the IP and proton dissociation enthalpy (PDE) are the primary theoretical indicators. In polar solvents, antioxidant effects predominantly occur via electron transfer mechanisms, with lower IP values correlating with enhanced antioxidant activity. Additionally, the sequential proton loss electron transfer (SPLET) mechanism can be quantified via proton affinity (PA) and electron transfer enthalpy (ETE). Generally, lower values of these theoretical parameters (BDE, IP, PDE, PA, and ETE) correspond to greater antioxidant capacity and more efficient radical scavenging. The C28 position presented the lowest BDE (90.23 kcal/mol), which was 13.53 kcal/mol lower than that of the C3 position in capsanthin (Appendix F). Steric repulsion between methyl and polyene hydrogen atoms at one terminus partially disrupts π-conjugation, whereas the carbonyl group maintains near-planar geometry at the opposite terminus owing to negligible torsional strain, facilitating extended conjugation. Liu, Chen, et al. (2020) reported that vitamin C has a lower theoretical BDE (77.32 kcal/mol) than capsanthin, with differences of 26.44 kcal/mol (C3) and 12.91 kcal/mol (C28). The lower antioxidant activity of capsanthin than that of Vc may be attributed to structural differences: (1) the lactone ring of Vc contains two highly labile hydroxyl groups versus the terminally positioned phenolic hydroxyls of capsanthin; (2) Vc has minimal BDE values at its lactone ring hydroxyls, where the adjacent carbon adopts a double-bond configuration (Liu, Chen, et al., 2020).

Capsanthin presented an IP of 126.187 kcal/mol, which was significantly lower than that of Vc (191.39 kcal/mol) (Liu, Chen, et al., 2020). Thermodynamic analysis revealed that the IP of capsanthin exceeded its BDE in the gas phase, indicating greater energetic favorability for HAT over SET-PT mechanisms. The minimum PDE was found at the C28 site of capsanthin, with a value of 278.59 kcal/mol, which agrees with the activity order derived from the HAT mechanism based on the BDE. However, the parameter value of the SETPT mechanism (IP) was 188.3 kcal/mol higher than the BDE value, which indicated that SET-PT is thermodynamically unfavorable.

In the SPLET mechanism, the first step involves deprotonation of the antioxidant, quantified by PA, where a lower PA value corresponds to easier dissociation of hydrogen ions. The computational results revealed PA values of 344.07 kcal/mol and 357.85 kcal/mol for the C28 and C3 sites of capsanthin, respectively. The second step of SPLET is electron transfer, characterized by ETE. The ETE values for the C28 and C3 sites were 60.64 kcal/mol and 60.41 kcal/mol, respectively. All ETE values were about 60 kcal/mol, whereas the IP value was 126.19 kcal/mol. The significantly lower ETE values than those of IP suggest that the electron abstraction capability of neutral capsanthin is weaker than that of its deprotonated anionic form. Compared to the HAT mechanism (BDE), the total energy requirement for capsanthin via the SPLET mechanism (PA + ETE) is substantially greater. Thus, thermodynamically, the antioxidant activity of capsanthin predominantly follows the HAT pathway. Purushothaman et al. (2022) investigated the antioxidant activity of curcumin and its structural analogs via DFT and evaluated the tendency of radical attack via BDE values. The study revealed that the HAT energetics of antioxidant molecules with hydroxyl groups in the gas and solvent phases, which interact with hydroxyl radicals (•OH) and hydroperoxyl radicals (•OOH), indicated that histamine and polyhydroxy curcuminoids featuring ortho-dihydroxy configurations exhibited superior radical scavenging capabilities. Zhou et al. (2019) systematically assessed the radical scavenging capacity of puerarin via DFT, with comparative analysis against daidzein. Using thermodynamic parameters such as the BDE and IP, the competition among three reaction mechanisms, including HAT, SETPT, and SPLET, was elucidated, and the IP, PDE, PA, and ETE were calculated. Moreover, kinetic studies were performed to elucidate the reaction pathways of puerarin with •OH and •OOH radicals. Šaćirović et al. (2021) using DFT calculations at the APFD/6–311++G(d,p) level of theory, revealed that the antioxidant mechanisms of six major wine polyphenols are predominantly governed by HAT and SPLET, with their activity significantly influenced by intramolecular hydrogen bonding and conjugation systems. Mavric-Scholze et al. (2025) based on DFT calculations at the MN15/6–311++G(d,p) level of theory, analyzed the thermodynamic parameters of epicatechin gallate and sinapic acid in scavenging DPPH and ABTS radicals, confirming SPLET as the predominant antioxidant pathway for both polyphenols in aqueous and methanolic systems, while the HAT mechanism dominates in reactions with DPPH. Li, Yang, et al. (2023) using DFT and TD-DFT methods at the B3LYP/6–311++G(d) level of theory, demonstrated that SPLET is the preferred antioxidant mechanism for hydroxyanthraquinones in polar solvents, and that the ESIPT process significantly enhances the radical scavenging activity of the keto tautomers. Wang et al. (2019) based on DFT calculations at the B3LYP/6–311++G(2d,2p) level, revealed that the antioxidant mechanism of genistein and its amino-substituted derivatives shifts from HAT in the gas phase to SPLET in methanol, with the introduction of –NH2 groups substantially improving electron-donating capacity and radical scavenging activity.

Capsanthin biosynthesis is regulated not only by structural genes but also by transcription factors and epigenetic modifications. Further analysis showed that despite the conserved presence of structural genes, their expression levels are dynamically controlled by transcription factor networks, leading to the accumulation of different types of metabolites.

Evaluation of antioxidant activity during in vitro digestion

The progressive increase in the DPPH radical scavenging capacity during digestion is illustrated in Fig. 5B. The scavenging rates of DPPH free radicals by Bayan from the oral to intestinal phases were 14.37%, 36.03%, and 62.86%, respectively, with a 3.37-fold increase; those of 22–14 were 19.69%, 67.91%, and 79.99%, respectively, showing a 3.06-fold increase; L24 presented scavenging rates of 22.76%, 69.78%, and 90.74%, respectively, corresponding to a 2.99-fold improvement. The DPPH radical scavenging capacities of the three chili peppers during digestion followed the order L24 > 22–14 > Bayan.

The progressive increase in the ABTS radical scavenging capacity during digestion is shown in Fig. 5C. The scavenging rates of ABTS free radicals by Bayan from the oral to intestinal phases were 12.35%, 62.97%, and 80.27%, respectively, with a 5.49-fold increase, those of 22–14 were 19.58%, 76.27%, and 84.04%, respectively, showing a 3.29-fold increase, and those of L24 presented scavenging rates of 24.97%, 66.53%, and 86.12%, respectively, corresponding to a 2.45-fold increase. The ABTS radical scavenging capacities of the three chili peppers during digestion followed the order L24 > 22–14 > Bayan. Moreover, 22–14 exhibited a pronounced increase in scavenging capacity during gastric digestion, which was attributable to acid-enzyme-mediated liberation of bioactive compounds (Han, Ma, Shen, Wen & Xie, 2023). The trend of the change in the capsanthin content during in vitro digestion was consistent with that of the change in the content of capsanthin (Fig. 5A-C). The order of the DPPH and ABTS radical scavenging rates among the three chili peppers differed between the in vitro digestion and antioxidant stages, which may be attributed to the higher proportion of free-form capsanthin in Bayan pulp (Wu et al., 2014).

Bioaccessibility during in vitro digestion

Comparison of capsanthin bioaccessibility

During simulated in vitro digestion, L24 showed the highest bioaccessibility of capsanthin, followed by 22–14 and Bayan (Fig. 5D). The bioaccessibility of capsanthin in all three chili varieties progressively increased across the oral, gastric, and intestinal phases, reaching the highest level during intestinal digestion. Among the varieties, L24 presented the greatest increase in capsanthin bioaccessibility, increasing from 0.7% (oral) to 13.7% (intestinal), with an increase of 13%; 22–14 presented an increase from 1.4% (oral) to 12.2% (intestinal), corresponding to a 10.8% increase; Bayan presented the smallest increase, from 0.5% (oral) to 6.7% (intestinal), representing a 6.2% increase. Panagopoulou et al. (2021) found that oral mastication enhances the release of phenolic compounds, with a further increase during gastric digestion.

Comparison of the antioxidant bioaccessibility of different varieties of chili peppers

The bioaccessibility of the DPPH radical scavenging capacity in chili peppers progressively increased from oral to intestinal digestion (Table 3). Among the three varieties, L24 presented the highest bioaccessibility for DPPH radical scavenging. Bayan presented values of 0.21%, 0.51%, and 0.90% during the oral, gastric, and intestinal phases, respectively, corresponding to a 3.29-fold increase; 22–14 presented values of 0.45%, 1.54%, and 1.81%, respectively, with a 3.02-fold increase; L24 presented values of 0.51%, 1.57%, and 2.04%, respectively, representing a three-fold increase. These results suggest that in vitro digestion enhances the bioaccessibility of DPPH radical scavenging capacity in chili peppers. For ABTS radical scavenging bioaccessibility, Bayan presented values of 0.41%, 1.55%, and 1.31% across the oral, gastric, and intestinal phases, respectively, showing a 2.2-fold increase; L24 presented values of 2.64%, 6.66%, and 4.50%, corresponding to a 0.7-fold increase; 22–14 presented values of 2.07%, 6.04%, and 3.48%, respectively, corresponding to a 0.68-fold increase. These findings indicate that in vitro digestion also increases the bioaccessibility of the ABTS radical scavenging capacity. To summarize, the results indicated that in vitro digestion promotes the increase in antioxidant capacity and the improvement of bioaccessibility in chili peppers. Comparative analysis (Table 3) revealed distinct trends in capsanthin bioaccessibility and antioxidant bioaccessibility among pepper varieties, suggesting influences from other antioxidant compounds. Soare et al. (2017) reported that pepper antioxidants include Vc, flavonoids, and polyphenols. During digestion, the enzymatic effects caused the antioxidant bioaccessibility to initially increase and then decrease. Qin et al. Detection of ccs, LUT5, and CrtR expression levels in different varieties of chili peppers

The Ccs, LUT5, and CrtR genes were expressed in all three pepper cultivars, confirming their presence in mature red peppers (Fig. 6B).

As shown in Fig. 6B、6C, L24 presented the highest expression levels of all three genes, followed by 22–14, with Bayan showing the lowest expression, which is consistent with the purified content results reported in Section 3.3. In addition, the relative gene expression levels of Ccs genes showed significant differences among the three groups (p < 0.05). These findings indicated that Ccs, LUT5, and CrtR are key genes influencing capsanthin synthesis. (Furubayashi et al., 2021) reported that introducing multiple genes from the capsanthin biosynthetic pathway into Escherichia coli promotes capsanthin production. Within each cultivar, LUT5 presented the highest relative expression, followed by CrtR, with Ccs showing the lowest expression. The LUT5-encoded β-cyclase catalyzes the conversion of β-carotene to zeaxanthin, which is subsequently transformed into capsanthin. The red coloration of pepper fruits results from the balanced accumulation of capsanthin. (Chen et al., 2023) reported that LUT5 localizes to the outer membrane of chloroplasts and encodes enzymes that catalyze the conversion of phytoene to lutein, which includes the capsanthin biosynthesis pathway.

Conclusions

This study combined quantum chemical calculations (Gaussian-based conformational analysis) with in vitro assays to elucidate the antioxidant mechanisms of capsanthin. The reliability of this method was confirmed by comparing the calculated bond lengths and bond angles Capsanthin has a lower antioxidant capacity than Vc, primarily via HAT mechanisms. To optimize the purification process, 10 pepper cultivars from the Baotou Academy of Agricultural Sciences were evaluated to establish ideal macroporous resin purification parameters. In vitro antioxidant evaluation revealed that capsanthin from different varieties of peppers exhibited antioxidant activity, but it was lower than that of Vc. Among them, Bayan showed the strongest antioxidant activity. The in vitro digestion test revealed that the digestion process can increase the antioxidant bioavailability of pepper. Enzyme activity and gene expression analyses revealed higher CRTR-β than CCS activity within each cultivar, which was correlated with gene expression levels. The variety 22–14 presented the highest CCS enzyme activity, whereas L24 presented the highest CRTR-β activity and expression levels among all three genes studied.

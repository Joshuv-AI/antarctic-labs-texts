Intensity-modulated radiotherapy/Volumetric modulated Arc therapy including four Brachytherapy fractions versus 3D conformal radiotherapy containing five Brachytherapy fractions in the treatment of cervical cancer
Authors: Barin Feroz, Christoph Ebner, Sergej Skvortsov, Ricarda Purtscheller, Katharina Steger, Magdalena Steinlechner, Miriam Emmelheinz, Jana Rieker, Katharina Leitner, Ute Ganswindt, Sarah Maier, Christian Marth, Alain Gustave Zeimet
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDefinitive radiochemotherapy with external beam radiotherapy and brachytherapy is standard for locally advanced cervical cancer (LACC). Advanced techniques like intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT) and volumetric modulated arc therapy (VMAT) have replaced 3D-conformal radiotherapy (3DCRT). This study compares two institutional definitive radiotherapy treatment pathways implemented in different time periods: 3DCRT with five BT fractions and IMRT/VMAT with four BT fractions.MethodsThis retrospective single-center study included 145 patients with FIGO 2018 stage IB3–IVA LACC treated during 2008–2023. Patients received 3DCRT+5BT (2008–2016; n = 77) or IMRT/VMAT+4BT (2017–2023; n = 68) with concurrent chemotherapy. Histopathological biopsies at final BT evaluated tumor response. Dosimetric parameters (HRCTV-D90–EQD2₍₁₀₎ and OAR-D2cc for bladder, rectum, sigmoid) were analyzed. Endpoints included treatment duration, residual tumor, progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and toxicity.ResultsAfter a median follow-up of 60 months, no significant differences were seen in PFS (HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.34–1.38, p = 0.285) or OS (HR 0.47, 95% CI 0.17–1.29, p = 0.133). Histologically confirmed residual tumor rates were 22.1% (IMRT/VMAT+4BT) vs. 31.2% (3DCRT+5BT; p = 0.262). IMRT/VMAT+4BT patients more often completed therapy within 49 days (63.2% vs. 20.8%; p < 0.001). Median HRCTV D90 EQD2₍₁₀₎ was 85.3 Gy vs. 76.5 Gy (p < 0.001) in favor of IMRT/VMAT, while OAR doses remained within limits. IMRT/VMAT showed significantly fewer gastrointestinal, genitourinary, skin, and mucosal toxicities (all p < 0.05) and less systemic deterioration.ConclusionIn this era-based single-institution comparison of two treatment pathways, IMRT/VMAT+4BT achieved numerically better oncologic outcomes without reaching statistical significance. IMRT/VMAT+4BT showed improved target coverage, lower toxicity, and shorter treatment duration, within the context of an integrated regimen change over time.Supplementary informationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13014-026-02834-9.

Introduction

Cervical cancer is the fourth most common cancer and cause of cancer-related death in women worldwide [1–3]. The prognosis is largely determined by the FIGO stage. While early stages are managed surgically, treatment of LACC requires combined radiochemotherapy [4, 5]. This strategy combines teletherapy and brachytherapy with weekly cisplatin [5]. External beam radiation therapy (EBRT) in conjunction with a brachytherapy boost represents the established standard of care, substantially improving survival compared to EBRT alone [6]. Over the past ten years, the methodology for EBRT has significantly evolved from the traditional 3D conformal radiation therapy (3DCRT), which typically utilized three to four fixed fields precisely tailored to the planned target volume [7]. In contrast, modern EBRT methodologies, encompassing intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT) and volumetric modulated arc therapy (VMAT), engage sophisticated planning techniques that employ seven to nine beam angles for IMRT or facilitate continuous gantry rotation for VMAT. These advancements enable more accurate radiation administration that closely aligns with the planned target volume while concurrently reducing exposure to surrounding healthy tissues [8, 9]. Utilizing image-guided adaptive brachytherapy (IGABT), local control is notably high in well-responsive, localized tumors.

The EMBRACE-I and retroEMBRACE [10, 11] clinical trials have established MRI as the leading technique for brachytherapy planning and demonstrated that clinical outcomes are linked to dose prescription and technique. These studies have validated the effectiveness of IGABT in significantly enhancing local and pelvic control rates across various stages of cervical cancer, compared to traditional 2D brachytherapy [12]. IGABT has markedly improved local control—reaching up to 100% in early and 86% in advanced stages—while substantially reducing major morbidity, representing a paradigm shift in modern brachytherapy practice [13]. Building on these results, the EMBRACE II study integrates advanced radiotherapy and chemotherapy protocols to further optimize management of cervical cancer, aiming to reduce toxicity while maintaining high disease control, thereby defining new benchmarks for treatment efficacy and quality of life improvements in a global multicenter setting [13]. According to the 2023 ESGO/ESTRO/ESP Guidelines, the combined treatment duration for EBRT and brachytherapy should not exceed seven weeks (49 days). To calculate the total radiation dose from EBRT and brachytherapy, the brachytherapy treatment plans must be converted into the equivalent biological dose when administered in 2 Gy fractions (equivalent dose, EQD2). For IGABT, a dose of 40 to 45 Gy EQD2 is sought, in an effort for a total dose of 85 to 95 Gy EQD2 (D90) taking an estimated contribution of 45 Gy for EBRT. D90 refers to the dose received by 90% of the tumor volume. This aims to achieve a robust therapeutic dose covering the majority of the tumor, taking an estimated contribution of 45 Gy from EBRT [5, 14].

This retrospective era-based study compares oncological outcomes, toxicity, and overall treatment time between two institutional definitive radiotherapy treatment regimens implemented in different time periods: the current IMRT/VMAT + 4 BT regimen and the previous 3DCRT + 5 BT approach.

Methods

Patient cohort

The study cohort consisted of individuals diagnosed with histologically confirmed cervical cancer (including squamous cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma, or adenosquamous carcinoma) and classified FIGO 2018 stages IB3–IVA. Patients who received definitive chemoradiotherapy, which included at least four brachytherapy sessions at the Medical University of Innsbruck between January 1, 2008, and December 31, 2023, were considered eligible. This study included adult patients aged ≥ 18 years. The exclusion criteria encompassed neuroendocrine tumors, small cell carcinoma, melanoma, metastatic cervical cancers, stages beyond the Ib3 to IVa range, primary surgical treatment, neoadjuvant or adjuvant chemotherapy, and palliative care methods. The research was conducted adhering to the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the Ethics Committee of the Medical University of Innsbruck (Ref.: 1130/2024). All patients provided written informed consent for the use of their clinical data and biological material for scientific purposes. Study design

From 2008 to 2016, the standard treatment approach at the Medical University of Innsbruck for patients with histologically confirmed cervical carcinoma (FIGO 2018 IB3–IVA) undergoing primary combined R(C)T consisting of EBRT using 3DCRT followed by five sessions of brachytherapy at 7 Gy. In light of the aforementioned developments, the standard EBRT protocol at our institution was modified in 2017, incorporating IMRT/VMAT to improve dose distribution and organ sparing. Consequently, from 2017 to 2023, patients received four sessions of brachytherapy at 7 Gy after EBRT using IMRT/VMAT. EBRT was delivered in daily fractions of 1.8 Gy to a total pelvic dose between 45 and 50.4 Gy, including elective coverage of the pelvic lymphatic drainage and para-aortic extension when clinically indicated. In cases of nodal metastases, additional nodal boosts up to approximately 60 Gy EQD2 were applied.

Brachytherapy used high-dose-rate (HDR) afterloading. Before 2017, intracavitary brachytherapy was delivered in five weekly 7 Gy fractions, initiated during EBRT after 30.6 Gy. In the 3DCRT+5BT regimen, brachytherapy fractions were delivered weekly. From 2017, IGABT using CT- and MRI-based planning was implemented with four 7 Gy fractions after EBRT completion, using intracavitary or combined intracavitary-interstitial techniques when required. In the IMRT/VMAT+4BT regimen, brachytherapy fractions were delivered twice weekly. In both regimens, brachytherapy was administered sequentially without overlapping EBRT. Before 2014, HDR brachytherapy was planned using conventional 2D point-based planning. From 2014 onward, CT- and MRI-based 3D IGABT was implemented following GEC-ESTRO guidelines. Nontheless, oncologic outcomes did not differ between the 2D- and 3D-planned groups.

Treatment-related toxicities were primarily documented during radiochemotherapy and at structured early post-treatment follow-up visits, including the routine clinical evaluation approximately one month after completion of radiotherapy. Accordingly, the reported adverse events mainly reflect acute and early post-treatment toxicities typically observed after pelvic radiochemotherapy.

This retrospective cohort study aimed to analyze and compare clinical outcomes between two cohorts: 3DCRT+5BT (2008–2016 n = 77) receiving five brachytherapy sessions of 7 Gy after 3DCRT, and IMRT/VMAT+4BT (2017–2023 n = 68) receiving four brachytherapy sessions of 7 Gy following IMRT/VMAT.

At the Gynecologic Cancer Center of the Medical University of Innsbruck, every patient undergoing definitive radiochemotherapy for LACC receives a biopsy of the tumor bed or cervix at the final brachytherapy session. As previously described [15], biopsies were routinely performed at the time of the last brachytherapy according to our institutional standard protocol. At least four punch biopsies were obtained from the tumor bed, supplemented by targeted sampling of visible lesions and endocervical curettage when applicable. A biopsy was classified as positive if residual viable tumor cells were identified on histological examination. Absence of detectable vital tumor cells was defined as histologic complete response (hCR). Histopathological evaluation was performed on standard hematoxylin–eosin–stained sections by pathologists specialized in gynecologic pathology. In cases with residual tumor at primary biopsy, a repeat biopsy was performed six to eight weeks after treatment completion. Patients with persistent residual disease in this second biopsy are considered candidates for salvage extrafacial hysterectomy. The implementation of systematic post-treatment biopsies is a key feature of this study. This method provides a more objective assessment of treatment efficacy. The primary endpoints of this study were the impact of treatment regimens on post-treatment residual tumor, PFS, local PFS within the radiation field, local recurrence, and OS. Secondary endpoints included the effect of treatment regimens on treatment-related toxicity and overall treatment duration.

Statistical analysis

The non-parametric Mann–Whitney U test was applied to determine the statistical significance between the two groups. For normally distributed data, the Students two-tailed t-test was used to compare the two groups. Categorical variables were compared using Pearson’s Chi-square test; when expected cell counts were < 5, Fisher’s exact test was used. Survival analysis was conducted using univariate Kaplan–Meier estimates and log-rank testing to evaluate differences in PFS and OS. Cox proportional hazards regression was used to calculate hazard ratios, with univariable and multivariable analyses applied to assess prognostic factors and adjust for potential confounders. Statistical analyses were performed using the SPSS statistical software (version 29.0.0; SPSS, Chicago, IL, USA). Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. Dosimetric parameters, including high-risk clinical target volume dose covering 90% of the volume (HRCTV D₉₀) and minimum dose delivered to the most exposed 2 cm3 of each organ at risk (OAR D₂cc) were evaluated.

Results

Data from 259 patients who underwent cervical cancer treatment at our institution were collected. After applying the predefined exclusion criteria, 145 patients were eligible for analysis. Patient and tumor characteristics for both treatment groups are presented in Table 1. The two cohorts were well-balanced with respect to demographic variables, comorbidities and histological subtypes.

Regarding FIGO staging, a statistically significant difference in stage distribution was observed between the two treatment groups (p < 0.001). Descriptively, early stages (IB3–IIB) and FIGO stage III occurred at similar frequencies in both arms, whereas FIGO IIIC2 was more frequently represented in the 3DCRT+5BT group, and FIGO IVA was more common in the IMRT/VMAT+4BT group. To account for this imbalance in FIGO stage distribution between the treatment groups, the Cox regression analysis of treatment effect was adjusted for FIGO stage (I–IV) as detailed in the section Survival Outcomes. Analysis of biopsy results demonstrated differing rates of residual tumor between the treatment groups. Residual vital tumor was found in 22.1% of patients who received IMRT/VMAT+4BT and in 31.2% of those who underwent 3DCRT+5BT (p = 0.262). The occurrence of persistent tumors in follow-up biopsies (6 weeks later) was more prevalent in the 3DCRT+5BT group (6 vs. 2 patients), suggesting a tendency toward statistical significance (p = 0.068). In an exploratory univariate analysis, biopsy positivity at the time of the last HDR brachytherapy was significantly associated with inferior PFS (HR 2.49, 95% CI 1.30–4.76; p = 0.004), while no significant association with OS was observed (HR 1.22, 95% CI 0.63–2.36; p = 0.551).

Regarding brachytherapy planning technique, all patients in the IMRT/VMAT+4BT cohort underwent 3D image-guided brachytherapy (n = 68, 100%), whereas in the 3DCRT+5BT cohort 22 patients (29%) received 3D image-guided planning and 55 patients (71%) were treated using conventional 2D point-based brachytherapy planning. In an exploratory subgroup analysis comparing outcomes according to brachytherapy planning technique (2D vs. 3D), no significant differences were observed in PFS (p = 0.675) or OS (p = 0.884) (Suppl. Table 3, Suppl. Figure 1).

Patients receiving IMRT/VMAT+4BT completed therapy faster, with a median duration of 49 days versus 51 days in the 3DCRT+5BT group (p < 0.001). Furthermore, 63.2% of IMRT/VMAT patients completed therapy within the recommended 49-day timeframe, versus 20.8% in the 3DCRT group (p < 0.001), showing an advantage of the IMRT/VMAT-based approach for treatment efficiency. While overall recurrence rates did not differ significantly between groups (p = 0.159), both local (7.8% vs. 2.9%) and distant (24.7% vs. 16.2%) events were numerically more frequent in the 3DCRT+5BT group. A detailed summary is provided in Table 2.

To evaluate the difference according to clinical outcomes, we followed 145 patients for a median observation period of 60.0 months (95% CI: 54.9–65.0). Median follow-up was 40.0 months (95% CI: 27.9–52.1) in the IMRT/VMAT+4BT group and 103.0 months (95% CI: 77.4–128.6) in the 3DCRT+5BT group. Log-rank testing revealed no significant differences between IMRT/VMAT+4BT and 3DCRT+5BT with respect to PFS (p = 0.285), OS (p = 0.133), and local PFS (p = 0.884). In the multivariable Cox regression model, which included FIGO stage and treatment group, comparing IMRT/VMAT+4BT with 3DCRT+5BT regarding OS yielded a HR of 0.34 (95% CI 0.11–1.11). While this indicates a possible reduction in mortality risk for patients treated with IMRT/VMAT+4BT, the result did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.073). Comparison of total dosimetric parameters

Total doses (EQD2) were calculated for the combined EBRT and brachytherapy dose using the linear–quadratic model (α/β = 10 Gy for tumor, 3 Gy for organs at risk). HRCTV D90 represents the dose covering 90% of the high-risk clinical target volume. D2cc indicates the dose to the most exposed 2 cm3 of bladder, rectum, or sigmoid. Group comparisons were performed using Mann–Whitney U or Fisher’s exact tests, as appropriate.

As shown in Table 4 and Fig. 2, regarding toxicity, both in terms of local and systemic toxicities, the IMRT/VMAT+4BT group demonstrated markedly better tolerability compared to the 3DCRT+5BT group: A significantly higher overall toxicity rate was observed in the 3DCRT+5BT group compared to the IMRT/VMAT+4BT group (94.8% vs. 69.1%, p < 0.001, respectively). In terms of local toxicity in general, a significantly higher proportion of patients in the 3DCRT+5BT group experienced local side effects than those in the IMRT/VMAT+4BT group (93.5% vs. 55.9%, p < 0.001, respectively). Mucosal toxicity was observed exclusively in the 3DCRT+5BT cohort (11.7% vs. 0%, p = 0.003), and the incidence of radiation dermatitis was also significantly higher (76.6% vs. 19.1%, p < 0.001), with the majority of cases classified as grade 1 in the 3DCRT+5BT intervention. Gastrointestinal toxicities were reported in 66.2% of patients treated with 3DCRT+5BT compared to 47.1% in the IMRT/VMAT+4BT group (p = 0.046). Similarly, genitourinary toxicity was significantly more prevalent in the 3DCRT+5BT group (44.2% vs. 19.1%, p = 0.006 respectively). Systemic deterioration and fatigue categorized under “Systemic toxicity and general health deterioration” were reported in 22.1% of the 3DCRT+5BT group compared to 7.4% in the IMRT/VMAT+4BT group (p = 0.022). Regarding hematologic side effects, neutropenia necessitating G-CSF support was documented in 51.5% of the IMRT group and in 46.8% of the 3DCRT group without reaching statistical significance. Therapy-related treatment interruptions occurred in 24.7% and 17.6% of 3DCRT+5BT patients and IMRT/VMAT+4BT group, respectively. Discussion

We investigated the impact of two definitive radiotherapy regimens on clinical outcomes and toxicity in patients with LACC. While no statistically significant differences were found between groups in PFS or OS, the IMRT/VMAT+4BT group showed a tendency toward improved local tumor control and clear advantages in treatment tolerability. Local toxicity was significantly less frequent in the IMRT/VMAT+4BT group, and overall treatment-related toxicities were reduced.

Initial post-treatment biopsies showed similar positivity for vital residual tumor in both treatment groups, whereas more cases of persistent vital tumor residues were observed in the 6-weeks follow-up biopsies in the 3DCRT+5BT group, without reaching statistical significance. In the present cohort, biopsy positivity at the time of the last HDR brachytherapy was significantly associated with inferior PFS, confirming its prognostic relevance. These findings are consistent with previously published data demonstrating that biopsy-proven residual cervical cancer at the end of chemoradiation predicts impaired PFS. In line with that analysis, the prognostic impact appears to be driven primarily by the initial biopsy, whereas the additional value of a second biopsy at 6 weeks may be limited [15].

While the difference in median treatment duration between cohorts was relatively small (49 vs. 51 days), of special note is that more patients in the IMRT/VMAT+4BT group completed therapy within 49 days (63.2% vs. 20.8%, p < 0.001), which is clinically relevant as prolonged treatment time impacts local control in cervical cancer. [18] The higher HRCTV D₉₀ in IMRT/VMAT+4BT indicates improved target coverage, potentially contributing to favorable local control while maintaining acceptable OAR exposure. Differences in HRCTV D90 should be interpreted cautiously in this era-based comparison, as they may partly reflect differences in EBRT dose strategy and planning practice across eras.

EMBRACE I, a single-arm prospective study employing IGABT together with advanced external beam techniques (IMRT/VMAT), was retrospectively compared with historical cohorts treated with conventional 3DCRT and demonstrated improved 5-year OS from 67% to 74%. In our cohort, OS tended to favor the IMRT/VMAT+4BT regimen, although the difference was not statistically significant, most likely due to limited sample size and consistently high-quality treatment delivered in the compared control cohort.

A post-hoc power analysis suggested that 53 events (compared with 30 observed) would have been required to reach 80% statistical power, corresponding to approximately 260 patients in total (about 130 per arm).

Several studies have evaluated whether IMRT offers advantages over 3DCRT in cervical cancer treatment. A retrospective study [8] found no difference in 3-year OS between IMRT/VMAT (87%) and 3DCRT (91%), and a meta-analysis of over 1,000 patients [19] likewise showed no significant survival benefit. However, a study employing reduced pelvic fields [20] reported improved 5-year PFS with IMRT (64.9% vs. 44.3%), while OS remained similar. Overall, while OS appears comparable between techniques, selective IMRT with optimized field design may enhance locoregional control and long-term disease stabilization.

Biopsy-based assessment of residual tumor at the end of treatment revealed no statistically significant difference between both cohorts, although a trend toward better tumor regression was observed in the IMRT/VMAT+4BT group. So far, most comparative studies have relied predominantly on clinical- or imaging-based assessments. Reported outcomes showed comparable response and local control rates between IMRT/VMAT and 3DCRT [8, 17]. The presented analysis however, included the histopathological evaluation of post-treatment biopsies, offering an extra layer of evidence for tumor response, even though statistical significance was not reached.

In our study, IMRT/VMAT+4BT was associated with a significantly lower overall toxicity burden with consistent reductions across both gastrointestinal and genitourinary toxicities. This is in line with prior evidence from prospective trials and meta-analyses [19–22], which have demonstrated reduced acute and late gastrointestinal/genitourinary side effects with IMRT, without compromising oncologic outcomes. A recent retrospective study focusing on patient-reported outcomes [8] found that IMRT/VMAT was associated with significantly fewer gastrointestinal symptoms in both the subacute (p = 0.023) and late (p = 0.019) post-treatment phases. Collectively, these results underscore the advantage of modern conformal techniques not only in terms of improved dose distribution but also in substantially reducing treatment-related morbidity. Our toxicity data, showing significant differences across nearly all domains, support IMRT/VMAT+4BT as an effective and well-tolerated standard option, reflecting a more favorable therapeutic index—defined as comparable tumor control with substantially reduced toxicity.

A key strength of our study is the objective assessment of tumor response via post-treatment histopathological evidence; a method rarely used in similar studies. This approach avoids limitations of imaging-based evaluation and provides a more direct measure of treatment response. Including a second biopsy in positive cases enabled analysis of delayed tumor clearance, showing temporal dynamics of treatment efficacy. Although not statistically significant, the numerical data suggest a favorable outcome for IMRT/VMAT+4BT.

The single-center design reflects real-world clinical conditions, adding practical relevance. Another strength is adherence to the guideline-recommended treatment duration of ≤49 days in the IMRT/VMAT+4BT cohort, which is clinically relevant given the well-documented adverse impact of prolonged treatment on local tumor control [18, 23, 24].

Limitations include the retrospective design, potential selection bias, and the limited sample size, which may obscure small survival differences. Toxicity assessment was based on clinical documentation rather than standardized patient-reported measures. In addition, the imbalance in follow-up which was shorter in the IMRT/VMAT+4BT group (40 vs. 103 months) may have contributed to the absence of statistically significant oncologic differences. While median follow-up differed between groups, the majority of observed toxicities occurred during treatment or within the early post-treatment phase. Very late radiation-related complications, including bowel obstruction, strictures, or secondary malignancies, may occur several years after pelvic irradiation and must therefore be interpreted cautiously in light of the follow-up difference between cohorts.

An important limitation of this study is its era-based design. Because two institutional treatment regimens implemented in different time periods were compared, the individual contributions of EBRT technique (IMRT/VMAT vs 3DCRT), brachytherapy fractionation (4 vs 5 fractions), and contemporaneous workflow developments cannot be disentangled. These changes occurred as part of an integrated evolution of the treatment protocol. Similar paradigm shifts in cervical cancer radiotherapy, such as the implementation of image-guided adaptive brachytherapy within the EMBRACE program, have likewise been evaluated in the context of comprehensive protocol changes rather than isolated technical variables.

Our findings reinforce the clinical utility of IMRT/VMAT combined with a reduced number of brachytherapy fractions as an effective and well-tolerated treatment strategy for LACC. The significant reduction in overall and local toxicity supports its integration into standard care pathways. Future prospective studies should aim to investigate further optimization of treatment modalities regarding the number and timing of brachytherapy fractions within modern image-guided radiotherapy protocols.

Conclusions

The transition to IMRT/VMAT+4BT revealed oncological outcomes comparable to the previous 3DCRT+5BT regimen, while improving local tolerability and reducing treatment duration. Higher HRCTV D₉₀ values while maintaining OAR dose limits highlight the dosimetric advantages associated with the IMRT/VMAT+4BT approach. Numerically, rates of histologically confirmed persistent tumor were lower in the IMRT/VMAT+4BT cohort. The new regimen offers clear advantages in the toxicity profile and treatment efficiency, supporting its feasibility within modern treatment workflows for LACC patients.

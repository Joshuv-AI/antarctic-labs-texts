Association between preoperative metformin exposure and postoperative nausea and vomiting in patients undergoing general anaesthesia: a protocol for a prospective observational cohort study in a Chinese tertiary hospital
Authors: Jiankun Shi, Jiayi Zheng, Xiao Zhang, Chujun Liang, Shimin Zhang, Ci Song, Nanqi Sun, Zixin Deng, Zhinan Zheng, Xiangling Yang, Qingshan Huang, Yang Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPostoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) is a common and distressing complication following surgery, persisting despite advances in prophylactic regimens. Growth differentiation factor-15 (GDF-15) has been identified as a biomarker inversely associated with PONV risk and severity. As metformin is known to elevate circulating GDF-15 levels, we hypothesise that preoperative metformin use may be associated with a lower incidence of PONV. This study aims to evaluate the association between a history of preoperative metformin administration and the occurrence of PONV in adults undergoing general anaesthesia.Methods and analysisThis is a single-centre, prospective, observational cohort study. We plan to enrol 909 adult patients scheduled for surgery under general anaesthesia with endotracheal intubation from December 2025 to December 2028. Participants will be divided into two groups based on their preoperative metformin exposure: an exposed group (n=303) with a documented history of metformin use and a non-exposed group (n=606) without such history, using a 1:2 ratio. The metformin regimen (choice of agent and daily dosage) will be determined by the attending physician as part of routine clinical care, independent of this study. The primary outcome is the incidence of PONV, defined as the occurrence of any nausea, retching or vomiting, within 120 hours post-surgery. Secondary outcomes include the incidence of PONV in the early (0–24 hours) and late (24–120 hours) postoperative phases; the severity of PONV symptoms and the requirement for rescue antiemetic medication during these intervals; the quality of recovery (assessed at 0–24, 24–48, 48–72, 72–96 and 96–120 hours); potential PONV risk factors in blood or urine and long-term survival rates at 1, 3 and 5 years.Ethics and disseminationThis study protocol (version 04, dated 23 November 2025) was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Sixth Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University (Approval No. 2025ZSLYEC-689) prior to the initiation of participant recruitment. The first participant was enrolled under protocol version 04. A subsequent protocol amendment (version 05, dated 25 December 2025) was approved by the same Ethics Committee. This amendment added a quality of recovery assessment using the 15-item quality of recovery scoring system questionnaire at 30 days postoperatively. This amendment did not alter the primary or secondary outcomes, the sample size calculation, or any other key elements of the study design. The results of this study will be disseminated at scientific conferences and published in international peer-reviewed journals.Trial registration numberNCT07244575.Keywords: Adult anaesthesia, SURGERY, PUBLIC HEALTH, Risk Factors

Introduction

Postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) is a prevalent and distressing perioperative complication, affecting 18% to 80% of the approximately 313 million surgical procedures performed globally each year.1 2 Beyond patient discomfort, PONV is associated with serious clinical sequelae, including an increased risk of pulmonary aspiration, wound dehiscence, prolonged length of hospital stay, unplanned hospital admission and elevated healthcare costs.3 4 These consequences underscore its significant impact on both patient recovery and healthcare resource utilisation. Although several classes of antiemetic agents are available for PONV prophylaxis such as 5-hydroxytryptamine-3 receptor antagonists,5 neurokinin-1 receptor antagonists6 and glucocorticoids,7 the condition remains inadequately controlled in high-risk populations including females, patients exposed to opioids and those undergoing laparoscopic surgery, even with multimodal strategies.8,10 Furthermore, these medications may cause side effects such as headache, restlessness, dry mouth and hypotension.11 12 Limitations related to drug contraindications, side effects or individual patient comorbidities further restrict therapeutic options. Consequently, there is a clear need for novel preventive approaches that leverage alternative mechanistic pathways.

Growth Differentiation Factor-15 (GDF-15), a member of the transforming growth factor-β superfamily, exerts its biological effects primarily through the glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor family receptor α-like, which is predominantly expressed in the brainstem that is a key region involved in the emetic reflex.13 14 Preclinical studies have indicated that GDF-15 plays a role in regulating appetite, body weight and nausea.15 16 Fejzo et al observed that nonpregnant women with higher baseline GDF-15 levels experienced less severe nausea and vomiting during subsequent pregnancy.17 Further supporting this emerging role, our recent study demonstrated that elevated preoperative plasma GDF-15 concentrations are significantly associated with a reduced risk of PONV, underscoring its potential protective effect against this common postoperative complication.18

Metformin, a first-line medication for type 2 diabetes, may offer a viable strategy to modulate this pathway. Accumulating evidence indicates that metformin upregulates GDF-15 expression and secretion from tissues including the liver, kidney and gastrointestinal tract.19,21 This elevation in GDF-15 has been linked to reduced appetite and body weight in both diabetic and non-diabetic individuals.22 Supporting its potential antiemetic role, a large database study by Sharma et al23 reported that metformin use before pregnancy was associated with a lower risk of nausea, vomiting and hyperemesis gravidarum, an effect attributed to metformin-induced GDF-15 secretion. However, the association between metformin and PONV remains uncertain and has not been directly investigated in a perioperative context. Given this gap in evidence and the mechanistic plausibility of metformin acting via GDF-15, we designed this prospective observational cohort study to evaluate the association between preoperative metformin exposure and the incidence of PONV in patients undergoing general anaesthesia.

Methods and analysis

Objective

The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the association between preoperative metformin use and the incidence of PONV within 0–120 hours after surgery in patients undergoing endotracheal intubation under general anaesthesia. Secondary objectives include analysing the association between metformin exposure and PONV incidence in specific postoperative intervals (0–24 and 24–120 hours), PONV severity, requirements for rescue antiemetics, quality of recovery-15 scores at serial time points, potential PONV risk factors in blood or urine and long-term survival. Study setting and population

This prospective observational cohort study will be conducted at the Sixth Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University. The study population will consist of adult patients (aged ≥18 years) scheduled to undergo elective surgery under general anaesthesia with endotracheal intubation. Eligible patients will be assigned to one of two cohorts based on their preoperative medication history: the ‘exposed’ cohort will comprise patients with a documented history of metformin use prior to surgery, while the ‘non-exposed’ cohort will consist of patients without such a history.

Clinical management

As this is an observational study, all perioperative management was conducted at the discretion of the treating physicians in accordance with routine clinical practice and relevant clinical guidelines. A combined prophylactic antiemetic regimen consisting of dexamethasone plus palonosetron was administered, and the intraoperative anaesthetic regimen included sevoflurane, propofol and remifentanil, in line with the standard clinical practice at our institution.

Outcomes

Primary outcomes

The primary outcome is the incidence of PONV within 0–120 hours after surgery. According to the Fifth Consensus Guideline for the management of PONV, PONV is defined as nausea, vomiting or retching occurring in patients after surgery.8 Nausea is described as a subjective sensation of epigastric discomfort accompanied by an urge to vomit. Vomiting refers to the reflexive expulsion of gastric or duodenal contents through the mouth. Retching involves coordinated, vomiting-like abdominal and thoracic muscle contractions without actual expulsion of gastric contents.

Recruitment

Participants will be recruited by trained investigators in each participating hospital. Eligible patients will obtain all relevant information about the study in the form of oral explanation but also in the form of an information sheet. After they give written consent to take part in the study, they will be screened to complete a questionnaire comprising contact details, demographics, detailed medical history and medication listing. Participants will be divided into the exposed group and non-exposed group based on whether they take metformin. The participant has the right to withdraw from the study at any time of the study process without prejudice to their future care.

Sample size calculation

Based on Fifth Consensus Guidelines for the Management of PONV and Updates in the Management of PONV, we know that the current overall incidence rate of PONV is approximately 20%–30%.8 27 Thus, we estimated that the incidence of PONV in the non-exposed group is 27%, and 17% in the exposed group. Therefore, we calculated that 816 participants would be required to detect this difference using R software (V.4.2.1), with a 5% type I error, a power of 90% in a two-sided test and a 1:2 ratio between the exposed group and the non-exposed group, concluding 272 for the exposed group and 544 for the non-exposed group. The planned number of patients for enrolment was finally set at 909 (303 participants in the exposed group and 606 participants in the non-exposed group), considering a 10% dropout rate and non-compliance. A 1:2 allocation ratio (exposed:non-exposed) was adopted for the following reasons. First, as this is a prospective observational cohort study, exposure status is determined by routine clinical practice rather than randomisation at our study centre, the number of preoperative metformin users is substantially lower than non-users, and a 1:1 ratio would significantly prolong the recruitment period and compromise study feasibility. Second, methodological evidence indicates that when the exposed group is relatively limited, increasing the non-exposed group sample size improves estimation precision, with a 1:2 ratio offering a reasonable balance between statistical efficiency and practicality.28 Third, a larger non-exposed group provides a broader pool for propensity score matching or inverse probability weighting analyses, which are pre-specified as sensitivity analyses in this protocol.

Statistical analysis

The primary analysis will follow the intention-to-treat principle, including all participants according to their original exposure group (metformin users vs non-users). The full analysis set will comprise all enrolled patients who undergo surgery and have any post-baseline data. A per-protocol set (PPS) may be used in sensitivity analyses, excluding participants with major protocol violations. Analyses will be performed using R software (V.4.2.1 or later), with a two-sided p<0.05 considered statistically significant for the primary outcome. No multiplicity adjustment will be applied to secondary outcomes. Missing data patterns will be assessed; primary analysis will use complete cases, while multiple imputation may be considered for continuous secondary outcomes if missingness exceeds 5% and is assumed to be missing at random. Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics will be summarised by exposure group. Continuous variables (eg, age, weight, Apfel score) will be presented as mean (SD) or median (IQR) based on distribution normality; categorical variables (eg, gender, smoking history, PONV history) as frequencies (percentages).

The primary outcome is the incidence of PONV within 0–120 hours postsurgery (binary). A multivariable logistic regression model will be used to assess the association between metformin exposure and PONV incidence, adjusting for prespecified potential confounders: Apfel score (or its components), age, history of diabetes mellitus, duration of surgery, the use of prophylactic antiemetic drugs and intraoperative opioid dose (in morphine milligram equivalents). Results will be reported as an adjusted OR (aOR) with a 95% CI. Binary outcomes will be analysed using multivariable logistic regression with the same adjustment set as the primary outcome. PONV severity will be analysed using ordinal regression or linear regression, adjusted for the same confounders, reported as aOR with 95% CI. QoR-15 scores (measured longitudinally) will be analysed using a linear mixed-effects model with fixed effects for exposure group, time, group-time interaction and core confounders, plus a random intercept for participants. For time-to-event outcomes (overall survival and disease-free survival), Kaplan-Meier curves and log-rank tests will be used for univariate analysis. Cox proportional hazards models will be used for multivariable analysis, adjusted for age, sex and cancer stage (if applicable), with results reported as adjusted hazard ratios (aHR) with 95% CI. The proportional hazards assumption will be checked using Schoenfeld residuals.

The primary analysis model will be repeated within prespecified subgroups (eg, gender, Apfel score, history of diabetes, surgical type) to explore effect modification; interaction terms will be tested. Additionally, exploratory analyses will be conducted within the exposed group to evaluate the association of metformin daily dose, duration of use, and timing of the last dose before surgery with PONV risk and circulating GDF-15 levels. Sensitivity analyses will include propensity score analysis and per-protocol analysis. Propensity score analysis will be generated using logistic regression including baseline characteristics; the primary analysis will be re-run using PS matching or inverse probability of treatment weighting. Per-protocol analysis will be repeated on the PPS.

Data management

The data will be entered into the electronic data capture database, which has data entry and validation rules to reduce data entry errors, and the management functions to facilitate auditing. Participants’ data will be held securely in line with data protection legislation. Anonymous datasets will be stored in the Research Data storage facility at Sun Yat-sen University. The data management team will be responsible for establishing the module in the electronic data capturing system, designing the case report form, reviewing data blindly, locking the database, exporting data and so on.

Patient and public involvement

Patient and public involvement (PPI) representatives were actively engaged in the initial phases of this study. They reviewed and simplified the language of participant information sheets and consent forms to improve accessibility. Throughout the trial, the PPI group will continue to provide guidance on all participant-facing communications. They will also play a key role in developing a plain-language summary and in planning the dissemination of results to relevant patient communities and the wider public.

Ethics and dissemination

This study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Sixth Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University. The trial is registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT07244575) and will be conducted in strict accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Prior to enrolment, all participants will be fully informed of the study’s purpose and procedures; they will receive a detailed information sheet and provide written informed consent. Regardless of the findings, the results will be disseminated through publication in peer-reviewed journals and presentations at relevant academic conferences.

Discussion

PONV is a common complication following surgery and anaesthesia. Although currently available prophylactic medications are effective to some extent, they are associated with various side effects. For example, 5-hydroxytryptamine-3 receptor antagonists may cause headache and constipation; corticosteroids can induce immunosuppression and impaired wound healing; antihistamines often lead to drowsiness, dry mouth and difficulty urinating.29 30 Therefore, exploring novel mechanism-based prevention strategies with improved safety and favourable cost-effectiveness remains an important challenge in perioperative medicine.

This study aims to investigate the association between preoperative metformin administration and the incidence and severity of PONV in adult patients undergoing general anaesthesia. The research hypothesis is grounded in the following scientific rationale: GDF-15 has been established as a key biomarker negatively associated with the risk and severity of PONV.18 Meanwhile, metformin, a commonly used hypoglycaemic agent, can elevate the circulating levels of GDF-15.22 31 Therefore, we hypothesise that preoperative metformin administration may be associated with the risk of PONV by upregulating GDF-15.

Based on the aforementioned mechanisms, this study employs a prospective cohort design to systematically collect data on the incidence and severity of PONV within different postoperative time windows (the whole 0–120 hours, 0–24 hours and 24–120 hours) in patients with and without preoperative metformin exposure. This approach aims to analyse the association between metformin use and PONV occurrence.

Several considerations regarding the perioperative management of metformin warrant discussion. Current guidelines vary considerably in their recommendations on preoperative metformin discontinuation. The traditional recommendation is to discontinue metformin 24–48 hours before surgery, primarily due to concerns that patients with renal impairment, or those experiencing transient kidney dysfunction from surgical stress or contrast agent exposure, may be at risk for drug accumulation and metformin-associated lactic acidosis.32

However, for patients with normal renal function undergoing elective, low-risk procedures, metformin may be safely continued until the night before or even on the morning of surgery.33 Notably, a randomised controlled trial demonstrated that perioperative continuation of metformin did not improve glycaemic control compared with discontinuation, further highlighting the clinical complexity of this management decision.34 As this is an observational study, we will not interfere with the treating physician’s clinical decision regarding metformin management. These variations in clinical practice mean that participants in our exposed group will inevitably have heterogeneous metformin exposure profiles such as differing in daily dose, cumulative duration and timing of the last dose which may result in substantially different circulating GDF-15 levels at the time of surgery. To address this heterogeneity, we will prospectively record the timing of the last metformin dose, daily dosage and total duration of therapy for all exposed participants. Exploratory analyses will be conducted to examine the dose–response relationship between these variables and preoperative GDF-15 concentrations, as well as their association with PONV outcomes. This approach acknowledges the observational nature of this study while providing mechanistic insight into which aspects of metformin exposure are most relevant to GDF-15 elevation and potential lower incidence of PONV.

This study exhibits the following strengths: First, its prospective design ensures the systematic collection and integrity of the data. Second, the assessment of PONV adopts the standard definition specified in international consensus guidelines, and the research outcomes encompass multiple dimensions, including incidence, severity and different time periods, thereby rendering the evaluation more comprehensive.

However, this study has several limitations. First, as a single-centre study, the results may not be fully generalisable to the broader patient population. To enhance external validity and applicability, future research will focus on expanding the sample size and conducting multicentre studies. Second, the scale employed for nausea assessment is subjective, which may introduce potential bias. We will strengthen standardised training for researchers and adopt simple, comprehensible assessment tools to improve the accuracy of patient responses. Finally, despite being a prospective study in which known confounding factors (eg, age, sex, BMI, surgical type and opioid use) will be controlled using multiple regression models, the impact of residual confounding cannot be completely ruled out.

This study establishes a theoretical foundation for future randomised controlled trials to validate the feasibility and efficacy of preoperative metformin administration in preventing PONV. Given its low cost and widespread clinical use, metformin holds promise as a cost-effective novel strategy for PONV prophylaxis, offering a new perspective and additional option for perioperative management.

DDIT4 upregulation associates with gammadelta T Cells dysfunction in ART-suppressed people living with HIV-1
Authors: Haihan Wang, Zhetian Ren, Xia Wang, Ran Wang, Jianping Sun, Rui Wang, Xiaofan Lu, Taiyi Jiang, Xin Zhang, Lili Dai, Tong Zhang, Bin Su, Zhen Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAntiretroviral therapy (ART) effectively suppresses HIV-1 replication, yet T cell dysfunction persists in ART-suppressed people living with HIV-1 (PLWH). While mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) signaling drives T cell dysregulation, the role of DNA damage-inducible transcript 4 (DDIT4), a stress-induced mTOR inhibitor, in HIV-1 infection remains undefined. In this study, we aimed to investigate the expression of DDIT4 in immune cells, and clarify its associations with T cell dysfunction in ART-suppressed PLWH.MethodsWe enrolled 24 ART-suppressed PLWH and 16 people living without HIV (PWoH). DDIT4 mRNA expression in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) was quantified by RT-qPCR. Flow cytometry was employed to assess DDIT4 protein expression together with T cell activation, exhaustion, and senescence markers.ResultsDDIT4 transcriptional expression was significantly elevated in ART-suppressed PLWH and positively correlated with hemoglobin levels (r = 0.524, P = 0.010). Notably, DDIT4 was markedly upregulated in gammadelta T cells which displayed memory phenotypes with increased CD38, HLA-DR, TIGIT, and CD57 expression, but diminished CD28 expression. Furthermore, the frequency of DDIT4⁺gammadelta T cells was correlated positively with the frequencies of CD28−CD57+gammadelta T cells, but inversely with CD4⁺ T cell counts, total lymphocytes, and monocytes.ConclusionsDuring HIV-1 infection, DDIT4 may act as a stress-responsive checkpoint, inducing gammadelta T cell exhaustion and senescence in ART-suppressed PLWH. These findings highlight the potential of DDIT4 as a therapeutic target to restore gammadelta T cells competence and offer novel insights into ART-refractory immune dysfunction.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12879-026-13258-x.

Background

Human immunodeficiency virus-1 (HIV-1) infection remains a worldwide public-health emergency, with millions of newly infected individuals and substantial mortality [1]. Antiretroviral therapy (ART) effectively suppresses HIV-1 replication and restores CD4⁺ T cell counts, but it fails to re-establish immunocompetence. People living with HIV-1 (PLWH) exhibit chronic immune activation, persistent low-grade inflammation and sustained T cell dysfunction [2]. These ART-refractory immunological perturbations are strongly associated with the development non-AIDS comorbidities and suboptimal immune reconstitution [3].

T cell dysfunction is observed across a spectrum of pathological conditions, including acute/chronic viral infection and neoplastic diseases [4]. HIV-1-specific T cell dysfunction begins after viremia peak during acute infection, and persists into chronic phase, with hyperproliferation, more cytotoxicity, abnormal differentiation, and stress-induced signals [5]. We and others have demonstrated that residual T cell dysfunction is persisted in ART-suppressed PLWH even initiated ART early. These dysfunctional T cells are hyper proliferated, express higher levels of PD-1, but lack of stemness [6, 7].

Gammadelta (γδ) T cells account for 1 ~ 10% of peripheral T lymphocytes in healthy adults and operate as first-line antiviral effectors [8]. They segregate principally into Vδ1 and Vδ2 subsets: Vδ2 T cells dominate the bloodstream, whereas Vδ1 T cells are enriched in the intestinal mucosa [9]. HIV-1 infection disrupts this numerical equilibrium, precipitating a sustained inversion of the Vδ1/Vδ2 ratio in circulation [10]. From the acute phase onward, γδ T cells undergo activation, proliferation, and up-regulate multiple inhibitory receptors, yet become anergic, characterized by diminished cytokine secretion and attenuated cytotoxicity. These functional defects persist despite early initiation and prolonged ART [11]. Notably, even functionally impaired γδ T cells retain considerable antiviral potential and are increasingly viewed as exploitable components of curative strategies [12]. The molecular mechanisms of γδ T cell dysfunction in HIV-1 infection, however, remain poorly delineated.

Sustained metabolic stress, whether mitochondrial or endoplasmic reticulum (ER) in origin, drives immune cell exhaustion and senescence across pathological contexts. In cancer, this precipitates CD8⁺ T cell dysfunction; while in ART-suppressed PLWH, oxidative stress induces CD4⁺ T cell exhaustion, particularly among immunological non-responders [13, 14]. ER stress pathways are likewise hyperactivated in PLWH, compromising immune homeostasis and barrier integrity [15]. A central sensor of these stress axes is DNA damage-inducible transcript 4 (DDIT4), a phylogenetically conserved protein upregulated by hypoxia, oxidative stress, and ER perturbation [16]. By inhibiting mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) signalling, DDIT4 modulates immune cell fate and functions, and its overexpression has been implicated in neoplasia and chronic inflammatory diseases [17–19]. However, its specific contribution to HIV-1-associated immune dysregulation remains undefined.

In this study, we investigate the DDIT4 expression in PBMCs and characterized the phenotypic features of DDIT4-expressing immune cells in ART-suppressed PLWH. We found that γδ T cells exhibited the most pronounced upregulation of DDIT4 among all immune subsets examined. These DDIT4+γδ T cells displayed a memory phenotype, with increased expression of TIGIT and CD57 alongside reduced CD28 expression. Collectively, these findings suggest that DDIT4 may serve as a candidate marker for γδ T cell exhaustion and provide new insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying persistent impairment of γδ T cell during HIV-1 infection.

Methods

Study design

This was a cross-sectional study conducted at Beijing Youan Hospital, Capital Medical University from September 2023 and December 2024. ART-suppressed PLWH were consecutively enrolled. We quantified DDIT4 expression levels and characterized the phenotypic profiles of DDIT4-expressing immune cells in PBMCs and analyzed their associations with clinical laboratory parameters.

Participants

Participants were consecutively enrolled from the outpatient clinic. Inclusion criteria were: (1) aged 18 ~ 65 years; (2) confirmed HIV-1 infection; (3) received ART treatment; (4) plasma HIV-1 viral load < 40 copies/mL; (5) without additional treatments such as surgery or intravenous immunoglobulin during the study period; (6) no concurrent use of other medications, including antibiotics or corticosteroids. Exclusion criteria included: (1) active opportunistic infections, such as hepatitis B virus (HBV), hepatitis C virus (HCV) or tuberculosis co-infection; (2) malignancy; (3) autoimmune diseases; (4) diabetes; (5) hyperlipidemia.

People living without HIV (PWoH) were recruited from the cohort of men who have sex with men (MSM) and matched by age and sex. Inclusion criteria for PWoH were: (1) confirmed by serological testing; (2) no history of immunological or hematological disorders; (3) not taking immunosuppressive medications.

Sample size estimation

Sample size estimation was performed using the formula N =Z2p(1 − p)/d2. The expected high expression rate (p) of DDIT4 in T cells of ART-suppressed PLWH was set at 10% based on pilot data, with a 90% confidence level (Z = 1.645), and a margin of error of 10% (d = 0.1), the minimum required sample size was 24. Based on a case-to-control ratio of approximately 2꞉1, we finally enrolled 16 healthy controls.

Ethics statement

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Beijing Youan Hospital, Capital Medical University (approval number: [2020 − 147; 2024-072]). The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment, and personal information was de-identified and stored securely to protect confidentiality.

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) separation

PBMCs were isolated by density-gradient centrifugation on Ficoll-Paque™ (GE Healthcare, Uppsala, Sweden). Briefly, 10 mL of fresh venous blood was collected into EDTA-anticoagulants tubes and diluted 1꞉1 (v/v) with RPMI-1640 medium (Gibco, Grand Island, NY, USA). The diluted blood was carefully layered over 5 mL of Ficoll-Paque™ at a 2꞉1 (v/v) ratio in a 15 mL sterile conical tube and centrifuged at 400 × g for 20 min at room temperature (20–25 °C) with the brake off. The PBMC ring was aspirated with a Pasteur pipette, transferred to a new tube, and washed twice with 10 mL of RPMI-1640 medium (400 × g, 5 min). Cell viability (> 95%) was confirmed by trypan blue exclusion. PBMCs were resuspended in Recovery™ cell-culture freezing medium (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) at 1 × 107 cells/mL, aliquoted into 1 mL cryovials, and cryopreserved using a controlled-rate freezing container (Mr. Frosty™, Thermo Fisher Scientific) at -80 °C overnight before transfer to liquid nitrogen for long-term storage.

Determination of absolute CD4+ T cell count

The whole blood CD4+ T cell absolute count of each sample was determined with a TruCount tube with multicolor antibodies (anti-human CD3, CD45, CD4, and CD8 cell markers), performed by BD FACS Lyric flow cytometer (BD Biosciences, New Jersey, USA).

Plasma HIV-1 RNA quantification

The plasma HIV-1 viral load was quantified using the automated Real-time PCR M2000 system (Abbott Molecular Inc., Des Plaines, IL, USA), with 40 copies/mL detection limitations.

Reverse transcription quantitative PCR

Total RNA was extracted from PBMCs using TRIzol™ Reagent (Invitrogen, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. RNA integrity (RIN ≥ 7.0) was verified on a 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA), and concentration was determined by NanoDrop™ 2000 spectrophotometry (Thermo Fisher Scientific). One microgram of total RNA was reverse-transcribed with PrimeScript RT reagent kit (RR037A, Takara Bio, Kusatsu, Japan) in a 20 µL reaction: 42 °C for 15 min, followed by 85 °C for 5 s for enzyme inactivation. Quantitative PCR (qPCR) was performed using the TB Green™ Premix Ex Taq™ II kit (RR820A, Takara Bio, Japan) on a 7500 Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA). Cycling conditions were 95 °C for 30 s, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 5 s and 60 °C for 34 s. β-actin (ACTB) served as the endogenous reference gene. Primers (synthesized by Shenggong Biotech, Shanghai, China) were as follows: DDIT4 forward 5′- GGTTCGCACACCCATTCAAG-3′, reverse 5′- CAGGGCGTTTGCTGATGAAC-3′; ACTB forward 5′- TCACCATGGATGATGATATCGC-3′, reverse 5′- AATCCTTCTGACCCATGCC-3′. Each assay was performed in triplicate, and relative gene expression was calculated by 2−ΔCt method.

Antibodies

Phycoerythrin-cyanine 5 (PE-cy5)-conjugated anti-human γδTCR (IMMU510) mAb, PE-cy7-conjugated anti-human γδTCR (IMMU510) mAb, and Phycoerythrin-cyanine7 (PE-cy7)-conjugated anti-human Vδ1TCR (R9.12) mAb were purchased from Beckman Coulter (Brea, CA, USA). Allophycocyanin (APC)-conjugated anti-human CD8 (OKT8) mAb, Alexa Fluor 700 (AF700)-conjugated anti-human Vδ2TCR mAb, Allophycocyanin-cyanine 7 (APC-cy7)-conjugated anti-human CD4 (RPA-T4) mAb, Brilliant violent (BV) 421-conjugated anti-human CD3 (UCHT1) mAb, BV786-conjugated anti-human CD14 (M5E2) mAb, PE-conjugated anti-human CD38 (LS198-4-3), Peridinin-Chlorophyll-Protein Complex-cyanine5.5 (Percp-cy5.5)-conjugated anti-human CD28 (CD28.2) mAb, APC-conjugated HLA-DR (L243) mAb, APC-R700-conjugated anti-human CD27 (M-T271) mAb, APC-cy7-conjugated anti-human PD-1 (NAT105) mAb, BV421-conjugated anti-human TIGIT (A15153G) mAb, BV605-conjugated anti-human CD57 (NK-1) mAb, BV711-conjugated anti-human CD45RA (HI100) mAb, BV786-conjugated anti-human CD3 (SK7) mAb, and Live/dead fixable viability stain (FSV) 510 were purchased from Biolegend (San Diego, CA, USA) and BD biosciences (New Jersey, NY, USA). Recombinant anti-human DDIT4 antibody (EPR18716), recombinant rabbit IgG antibody isotype control (EPR25A), and Alexa Fluor 488 (AF488)-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG polyclonal antibody was purchased from Abcam (Cambridge, USA).

Flow cytometry (FCM)

Cryopreserved PBMCs were rapidly thawed, washed with ice-cold phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) containing 1% (w/v) bovine serum albumin (BSA), and centrifuged at 400 × g for 5 min. For surface staining, cells were labeled with fluorochrome-conjugated surface-specific mAbs and incubated for 20 min at 4 ℃ in the dark. After washing, cells were fixed and permeabilized using the Transcription Factor Buffer Set (catalog no. 562574; BD Biosciences, USA) for 40 min at 4 °C in the dark. To minimize nonspecific antibody binding, cells were blocked with PBS containing 5% (v/v) normal goat serum (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) for 1 h at 4 °C. Subsequently, cells were incubated with recombinant anti-human DDIT4 antibody (clone EPR18716, Abcam, Cambridge, UK) or recombinant rabbit IgG isotype control (clone EPR25A, Abcam, Cambridge, UK) for 40 min at 4 °C in the dark. After two washes, cells were stained with AF488-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG secondary antibody for 30 min at 4 °C in the dark. Cells were acquired on a FACS Lyric cytometer (BD Biosciences). Data were analyzed using FlowJo software (v10.9, BD Biosciences, USA). Fluorescence minus one (FMO) control was prepared as part of the gating strategy. For each sample, the actual expression of DDIT4 is calculated by subtracting the value of the isotype control.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis and data visualization were performed using SPSS Statistics 29.0 and GraphPad Prism 10.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). The distribution of continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test and visual inspection of Q-Q plots. Variables with non-normal distributions are presented as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs). Categorical variables are reported as frequencies (percentages). The Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare DDIT4 expression levels between ART-supressed PLWH and PWoH; the Kruskal-Wallis test was applied for comparisons of DDIT expression among immune cell subsets in both ART-supressed PLWH and PWoH groups; the Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used for phenotypic comparisons between DDIT4⁺ and DDIT4⁻γδ T cells. Correlations between clinical laboratory parameters and DDIT4⁺ or DDIT4⁻γδ T cells were analyzed using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient; no multiple testing corrections were applied. To minimize data entry errors, all experimental and clinical data were independently recorded and cross-checked by two investigators. No missing data were identified. All statistical tests were two-sided, and a P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. *P < 0.05; **P < 0.01; ***P < 0.001; ****P < 0.0001.

Elevated DDIT4 mRNA expression in ART-suppressed PLWH

DDIT4 expression and its role in HIV-1 infection and immune reconstitution after ART has not been documented yet. We first quantified DDIT4 mRNA expression in PBMCs by RT-qPCR, and found that the mRNA levels of DDIT4 expression were significantly elevated in ART-suppressed PLWH compared with PWoH (Fig. 1A, P < 0.0001). Given the established association between elevated DDIT4 expression and aging [20, 21], we stratified participants into younger and older subgroups based on the median age in both ART-suppressed PLWH and PWoH. We found that DDIT4 expression levels were significantly higher in ART-suppressed PLWH compared to PWoH, regardless of age subgroup (Fig. 1B). Furthermore, no significant differences in DDIT4 expression were observed between PLWH on ART for moderate duration (mART) versus long-term duration (LT-ART), with a median duration of 96 months (Fig. 1C).

CD4+ T cell counts are a well-established predictor for immune recovery after ART [22]. We stratified the ART-suppressed PLWH by CD4+ T cells (median of 600 cells/µl), and found that the DDIT4 transcription levels were significant increase in both CD4 < 600 cells/µl (P = 0.0075) and CD4 > 600 cells/µl (P = 0.0004) groups compared with PWoH (Figure S1A). However, we did not observed correlation between the mRNA expression levels of DDIT4 and CD4+ T cell counts or CD4/CD8 ratios in ART-suppressed PLWH (Fig. 1D and E). Intriguingly, we found a positive association between DDIT4 mRNA expression and blood hemoglobin (r = 0.524, P = 0.010) (Fig. 1F).

DDIT4 expression in multiple immune cells

It has been reported that DDIT4 engaged in T cell development and differentiation in the manner of mTOR dependent or independent [17]. However, the distribution of DDIT4 expression in human immune cells remain unclear. Herein, we performed flow cytometry to assess the expression of DDIT4 in various immune cells, including CD3⁺, CD4⁺, CD8⁺ T cells, γδ T cells, natural killer (NK) cells, natural killer T (NKT) cells and monocytes (Fig. 2A). We found that DDIT4 expression was highly enriched in T cells, followed by γδ T cells, NKT cells, and NK cells in both ART-suppressed PLWH and PWoH (Fig. 2B and C). Moreover, compared with PWoH, DDIT4 expression was significantly lower in bulk CD3⁺ T cells (P = 0.0015) and CD4⁺ T cells (P = 0.0199) in ART-suppressed PLWH (Fig. 2D and E). Conversely, γδ T cells and NKT cells from ART-suppressed PLWH exhibited markedly higher DDIT4 levels, with the greatest increase observed in γδ T cells (Fig. 2F and G). Furthermore, the frequencies of DDIT4+γδ T cells were significantly increased in ART-suppressed PLWH, especially those with CD4+ T cells < 600 cells/µl, compared with PWoH (Figure S1B). It is thus delineated that the expression of DDIT4 exhibits cell-type-specificity and could differentially regulate host immune responses during HIV-1 infection.

Next, we stratified the participants by age (median age of 39 years) or ART duration (median of 96 months), and compared the frequency and phenotypes of DDIT4+γδ T cells. We found that no significant difference in the proportion of DDIT4+γδ T cells between younger and older ART-suppressed PLWH. In contrast, the proportion of DDIT4+γδ T cells was significantly higher in the older group than in the younger group in PWoH (Figure S2A). However, no significant differences were found in the frequencies of total and DDIT4+γδ T cells between mART and LT-ART groups (Figure S2B-C). Moreover, compared with the mART group, PD-1 expression on DDIT4+γδ T cells was significantly decreased in the LT-ART group, while no significant changes were observed in the expression of CD38, HLA-DR, TIGIT, CD28 and CD57 (Figure S2D-I).

DDIT4 was mainly upregulated in memory γδ T cells in ART-suppressed PLWH

Human γδ T cells can be phenotypically subdivided into four distinct subsets, naïve (TN, CD27⁺CD45RA⁺), central memory (TCM, CD27⁺CD45RA⁻), effector memory (TEM, CD27⁻CD45RA⁻), and terminally differentiated effector memory RA⁺ (TEMRA, CD27⁻CD45RA⁺) cells [23]. We found that DDIT4 expression was markedly higher in γδTN cells than in γδTCM (P = 0.0104) and γδTEM (P = 0.0206) subsets in PWoH. By contrast, ART-suppressed PLWH exhibited no significant inter-subset differences in DDIT4 expression (Fig. 3A). Moreover, compared with PWoH, DDIT4 expression in γδTCM (P = 0.0364), γδTEM (P = 0.0265), and γδTEMRA (P = 0.0101) cells were significantly elevated in ART-suppressed PLWH, but no significant difference in γδTN cells was observed (Fig. 3B-E). Collectively, these data demonstrate that DDIT4 is predominantly expressed in memory γδ T cells.

Subsequently, we further analyzed the exhaustion and senescence phenotypes of DDIT4+ memory γδ T cells. Initially, although no significant differences were observed in the overall levels of DDIT4+PD-1+ memory γδ T cells between PWoH and ART-suppressed PLWH, subset analysis revealed that the proportion of DDIT4+PD-1+γδTCM was significantly higher than that of γδTEM in ART-suppressed PLWH. Notably, this distribution pattern was also present in PWoH (Fig. 3F). Furthermore, regarding TIGIT expression analysis, DDIT4+TIGIT+γδTCM and γδTEM expression levels were significantly upregulated in ART-suppressed PLWH compared to PWoH. Concurrently, the proportion of DDIT4+TIGIT+γδTEMRA was significantly higher than that of γδTCM and γδTEM in both ART-suppressed PLWH and PWoH, suggesting a greater exhaustion propensity in the TEMRA subset (Fig. 3G). Additionally, analysis of senescence-associated markers demonstrated that DDIT4+CD28−γδTEMRA and DDIT4+CD57+γδTCM expression levels were significantly upregulated in ART-suppressed PLWH compared to PWoH. Moreover, intragroup comparison within ART-suppressed PLWH revealed that the proportions of both DDIT4+CD28− and DDIT4+CD57+γδTCM were significantly lower than those of γδTEM and γδTEMRA, reflecting the heterogeneity of senescence marker expression across different differentiation stages of γδ T cells (Fig. 3H and I).

Elevated DDIT4 Expression was positively associated with γδ T cell senescence in ART-suppressed PLWH

We previously revealed that γδ T cells are highly activated, proliferated and exhausted during HIV-1 infection. Disorder of the phenotype and function of γδ T cells are persistent despite long-term ART, even early initiated [24]. Therefore, we hypothesized that upregulated DDIT4 expression in γδ T cells may involve in γδ T cell dysfunction during HIV-1 infection. To test this hypothesis, we compared the γδ T cell phenotypes, including activation, exhaustion, and senescence, between DDIT4⁺ and DDIT4⁻γδ T cells in both ART-suppressed PLWH and PWoH (Fig. 4A). We found that DDIT4+γδ T cells had significant higher proportions of CD38+HLA−DR+ cells than DDIT4−γδ T cells in both ART-suppressed PLWH (Fig. 4B, P < 0.0001) and in PWoH (Figure S3A, P < 0.0001).

However, PD-1 and TIGIT expression were both significantly higher in DDIT4+γδ T cells compared to DDIT4−γδ T cells in PWoH (Figure S3B-C). Furthermore, we analyzed the relationships between DDIT4+ or DDIT4−γδ T cells and CD4+ T cell counts, CD8+ T cell counts, CD4/CD8 ratios, and γδ T cell phenotypes. We observed a negative association between the frequencies of DDIT4+γδ T cells and CD4+ T cell counts (r = -0.430, P = 0.036) in ART-suppressed PLWH. Besides, the frequencies of DDIT4+γδ T cells were positively associated with the frequencies of CD57+ and CD28−CD57+γδ T cells in ART-suppressed PLWH (Fig. 4H), but these were not observed in PWoH (Fig. 4I). Therefore, our findings suggest that DDIT4 upregulation associates with γδ T cell exhaustion and senescence.

Correlations between the frequency of DDIT4⁺γδ T cells and clinical parameters

To assess the clinical relevance of elevated peripheral DDIT4+γδ T cells, we performed a comprehensive analysis between their frequencies and 26 routine clinical parameters, including complete blood counts and hepatic function profiles. As shown in Fig. 5, the frequency of DDIT4+γδ T cells was inversely correlated with absolute CD4⁺ T cell counts, while no significant correlation was observed between DDIT4−γδ T cells and CD4⁺ T cell counts (Fig. 5A). Furthermore, the proportion of DDIT4+γδ T cells showed negative correlations with absolute lymphocyte and monocyte counts, whereas the proportion of DDIT4−γδ T cells exhibited positive correlations with both lymphocyte and monocyte counts (Fig. 5A). No significant associations were observed between the frequencies of either DDIT4+ or DDIT4−γδ T cells and white blood cells (WBC), neutrophils (NEUT), Hb, platelets (PLT) or liver function markers, including ALT, AST, bilirubin subtypes, bile acids, lipid, and glucose (Fig. 5B).

Discussion

HIV-1 infection has been controlled as a chronic infectious disease by effective ART. However, T cell dysfunction persists, and affects the immune reconstitution in ART-suppressed PLWH, even initiation early [7, 24]. The mechanisms of impaired T cell function have not been exactly elucidated. DDIT4, a stress-induced metabolic protein, which upregulation engages in the pathogenesis of multiple diseases, but its role in HIV-1 infection remains unclear. In this study, we characterized the DDIT4 expression, defined its cellular distribution and phenotypic profiles in ART-suppressed PLWH, and examined its association with clinical parameters to explore the potential role of DDIT4 in HIV-1 infection-associated T cell dysfunction.

We firstly revealed that DDIT4 mRNA expression was significantly elevated in PBMCs of ART-suppressed PLWH. Studies evidenced that anemia occurs common in PLWH, and leads to hypoxia environment [25]. Hypoxia-inducible factor 1α (HIF-1α) activates DDIT4 transcription by binding to hypoxia-response elements in the DDIT4 promoter [26]. Moreover, inflammatory cytokines, such as IL-6 and TNF-α, are significantly associated with increased DDIT4 expression in chronic obstructive pulmonary diseases [27]. Thus, DDIT4 upregulation may link to chronic inflammation or hypoxic stress during HIV-1 infection.

Blood Hb level is positively associated with CD4+ T cell counts, and may serve as a potential candidate for HIV-1 disease progression [28]. Although Hb levels recover after long-time ART, liable iron pools remain elevated in T cells of PLWH, regardless of immune recovery status. These increased liable iron pools in T cells are recognized as sensitive markers of persistent immune activation and exhaustion [29]. Moreover, DDIT4 regulates cell proliferation and death via mTORC1 signialling in an iron-dependent manner [30, 31]. Our findings reveal a positive association between elevated DDIT4 transcription and blood Hb, suggesting that DDIT4 may modulate immune cell function through iron- or mTORC1-related pathways in ART-suppressed PLWH. However, no significant difference in mTOR transcription was observed between ART-suppressed PLWH and PWoH (data not shown). The precise mechanisms by which DDIT4 influences immune function warrant further investigation.

Subsequently, we assessed DDIT4 expression at the protein level across various immune cells. In ART-suppressed PLWH, DDIT4 was found to be upregulated in γδ T cells and NKT cells but downregulated in total CD3+ T cells and CD4+ T cells. This differential DDIT4 expression may be explained by the distinct hypoxia sensitivity of these cell types during HIV-1 infection, given that the regulation of mTORC1 by DDIT4 is known to be cell-type-dependent [32]. Moreover, DDIT4 expression is influenced by the differentiation status of CD4+ memory T cells, where its upregulation has been linked to the establishment of a chronic and low-grade inflammatory state [33].

DDIT4 also modulates T cell proliferation, survival and Th17 cells differentiation [17, 34]. In macrophages, IL-10 induces DDIT4 expression, which inhibits mTOR signaling and induces macrophage metabolism reprogramming, towards an anti-inflammatory state [35]. Therefore, the role and regulatory mechanisms of DDIT4 in immune cell function are both cell-type-specific and complex. Advanced technologies, such as single-cell RNA sequencing, proteomics, gene knockdown or mass cytometry are needed to further elucidate the exact mechanisms of DDIT4 in HIV-1 pathogenesis.

We revealed that DDIT4 was enriched in memory γδ T cells, and DDIT4⁺γδ T cells exhibit higher levels of immune activation, exhaustion, and senescence markers compared with DDIT4⁻γδ T cells in ART-suppressed PLWH. To our knowledge, it is the first study to uncover the role of DDIT4 in γδ T cell dysfunction in HIV-1 infection. Our findings suggest that DDIT4 upregulation may affect γδ T cell differentiation and promote γδ T cell exhaustion and senescence. However, the mechanisms of γδ T cell exhaustion are not well-elucidated. mTOR is demonstrated as a central hub for regulating T cell metabolism, proliferation, differentiation, and effector activity [18], which mediates T cell exhaustion in HIV-1 infection [36]. Oxidative stress response pathways also boost T cell exhaustion, and play critical roles in HIV-1 pathogenesis [37]. Therefore, DDIT4 as a bridge, links oxidative stress and mTOR pathway during HIV-1 infection. DDIT4 senses various stresses, such as HIV-1 proteins, hypoxia, and mitochondrial damage, which induce DDIT4 sustainably upregulate, and then triggers γδ T cell dysfunction. However, we did not observe a significant downregulation of mTOR expression, indicating that DDIT4 regulates γδ T cell function may through mTOR-dependent or mTOR-independent pathways. Nevertheless, the detailed mechanisms need further investigation in vitro.

Clinical relevance showed that the frequencies of DDIT4⁺γδ T cells were positively associated with activated or senescence γδ T cells, whereas, they were negatively correlated with CD4⁺ T cell counts, total lymphocyte counts, and monocyte counts, suggesting its potential role in influencing immune homeostasis, immune reconstitution and ongoing immune injury. Yu et al. reported that DDIT4+CD4+ memory T cells are metabolically activated, and communicate with myeloid mDCs through CTLA4-CD86, LTB-LTBR and CCR4-CCL3 ligand-receptor interaction [33]. These findings indicating that DDIT4 upregulation may lead to γδ T cell dysfunction, which in turn promotes immune dysregulation.

There were several limitations in this study. First, the sample size was relatively small, and all participants were male, which may limit the generalizability of our findings to broader PLWH population. Second, the cross-sectional design precludes the establishment of causal relationships between DDIT4 expression and HIV-1 disease progression, as well as the dynamic phenotypic changes of DDIT4+γδ T cells before and after ART initiation. Third, although we identified phenotypic characteristics of DDIT4⁺ γδ T cells, functional assays, including cytokine production and cytotoxicity, were not performed, limiting our ability to definitively establish DDIT4 as a marker of cellular exhaustion. Future studies should expand sample size, including female participants, conduct in longitudinal cohorts and perform functional experiments to validate the mechanistic link between DDIT4 and γδ T cell exhaustion.

Conclusions

In conclusion, we provide the first evidence that DDIT4 is selectively up-regulated in memory γδ T cells from ART-suppressed PLWH. This upregulation positively associates with γδ T cell exhaustion and senescence, identifying that DDIT4 as a stress-responsive checkpoint, may induce γδ T cell dysfunction. Our findings offer exciting new insights into the mechanisms underlying ART-refractory immune dysfunction and nominate DDIT4 as a novel target for reversing γδ T cell function or developing γδ T cell based immunotherapeutic strategies.

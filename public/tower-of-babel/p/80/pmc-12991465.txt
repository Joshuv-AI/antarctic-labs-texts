Evaluation of the Role of Artificial Intelligence-Assisted Diagnosis of Early-Stage Oral Cancer: A Clinical Study
Authors: Sanjay K. Sahoo, Meenal Jethlia, Jagjeewan Ram, Mudita Chaturvedi, Harveen Kaur, Pankaj Kukreja, M C Prashant, Rahul Tiwari
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Early detection of oral cancer significantly enhances treatment outcomes and survival rates. This research evaluates the role of artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted diagnosis in identifying early-stage oral cancer.Methods:A clinical research involving 100 patients with suspected oral lesions was conducted. Participants underwent conventional visual examination and AI-assisted diagnostic imaging. Diagnostic accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity were compared.Results:AI-assisted diagnostics demonstrated a sensitivity of 92% and specificity of 88%, significantly outperforming conventional methods. Time to diagnosis was reduced by 40%.Conclusion:AI-assisted diagnosis is a promising tool for early-stage oral cancer detection, improving diagnostic accuracy and reducing time to intervention. Further research is recommended to validate these findings in broader clinical settings.

INTRODUCTION

Oral cancer is among the most common malignancies worldwide, with a high prevalence in countries like India due to tobacco use, betel quid chewing, and alcohol consumption.[1] Early detection is critical for effective management, yet diagnostic delays are common due to reliance on conventional visual examination methods, which often lack sensitivity and specificity.[2]

Artificial intelligence (AI) has emerged as a transformative tool in health care, offering enhanced diagnostic capabilities through advanced image analysis and machine learning algorithms. In oral oncology, AI has shown potential in identifying subtle lesions that might elude human examination.[345] This research evaluates the role of AI-assisted diagnosis in detecting early-stage oral cancer, comparing its efficacy with conventional diagnostic methods.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Research design and setting

This prospective clinical research was conducted at a tertiary care hospital over one year. The research adhered to ethical guidelines, with approval from the institutional ethics committee and informed consent obtained from all participants.

Participants

A total of 100 patients presenting with suspicious oral lesions, aged 25–70 years, were included. Exclusion criteria were prior treatment for oral cancer or other systemic malignancies.

Methodology

Conventional Diagnosis: All participants underwent a thorough clinical examination by an experienced oral pathologist, including visual inspection and palpation.AI-Assisted Diagnosis: The AI system utilized a convolutional neural network (CNN) algorithm pretrained on a dataset of 10,000 oral lesion images. Lesion images were captured using high-resolution intraoral cameras. The AI system provided a probability score for malignancy, with a threshold of ≥0.7 considered indicative of malignancy.

Data collection

Parameters, such as diagnostic accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and time to diagnosis, were recorded for both methods. Histopathological analysis served as the gold standard for confirming malignancy.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS software (version 25.0). Comparative analysis employed Chi-square tests for categorical variables and independent t-tests for continuous variables, with significance set at P < 0.05.

Diagnostic Accuracy of Conventional vs. AI-Assisted Methods

The findings reveal that the AI-assisted diagnostic method achieved a sensitivity of 92%, significantly higher than the 78% sensitivity of the conventional method (P = 0.01). Similarly, the specificity of AI-assisted diagnostics was 88%, compared to 70% for conventional diagnostics (P = 0.02). Positive predictive value (PPV) and negative predictive value (NPV) also showed marked improvements with AI assistance, reaching 90% and 89%, respectively, compared to 75% and 72% for conventional methods (P = 0.03 and P = 0.04, respectively). These findings indicate that AI-assisted diagnostics are not only more accurate but also more reliable in detecting true positive and true negative cases of early-stage oral cancer.

AI-assisted diagnostics demonstrated a substantial improvement in time efficiency. The mean time to diagnosis using AI was 27 minutes (±4 minutes), significantly shorter than the 45 minutes (±5 minutes) required for conventional methods (P < 0.001). This represents a 40% reduction in diagnostic time, highlighting the practicality of AI integration in clinical workflows where timely intervention is crucial. The reduction in time was consistent across various patient demographics and lesion complexities, further validating the robustness of the AI system [Table 2].

DISCUSSION

Key contributions and clinical implications

This research demonstrates the transformative potential of artificial intelligence (AI) in diagnosing early-stage oral cancer, addressing a critical gap in timely and accurate detection. The findings revealed significantly higher sensitivity (92%) and specificity (88%) of AI-assisted diagnostics compared to conventional methods, aligning with the World Health Organization’s call for innovative strategies to reduce oral cancer morbidity and mortality.[1] Early diagnosis is essential as it directly correlates with treatment outcomes, survival rates, and quality of life.[2] AI’s ability to identify subtle patterns in lesion morphology, often overlooked by human experts, underscores its role as a valuable adjunct to clinical decision-making.

Efficiency and resource optimization

The marked reduction in diagnostic time (by approximately 40%) further underscores AI’s operational efficiency. This time saving is particularly impactful in resource-constrained healthcare systems where timely intervention can significantly alter prognoses.[3] The research supports existing literature emphasizing the integration of deep learning algorithms in clinical workflows to expedite diagnostic processes, as seen in other fields, such as dermatology[3] and lung cancer imaging.[4] These parallels highlight AI’s adaptability across specialties, with oral oncology standing to benefit significantly from its application.

Comparative analysis with existing studies

The results of this research are consistent with the findings of Hameed and Krishnan[5] and Saremi et al.,[6] who reported comparable diagnostic accuracies in AI-driven detection of pancreatic and other cancers. The robust performance of AI in this research is also supported by its application in cracked tooth imaging and odontogenic tumor diagnoses.[78] Smartphone-based AI applications in oral cancer detection have similarly reported high diagnostic accuracies, reinforcing the potential for integrating AI even in remote or underserved settings.[9] These advancements collectively pave the way for democratizing access to early cancer detection tools.

Limitations and ethical considerations

Despite the promising findings, challenges remain. The research’s single-center design and relatively small sample size limit the generalizability of its outcomes. Moreover, the reliance on pretrained models necessitates further validation across diverse demographics and clinical environments.[10] Ethical considerations, including data privacy and algorithmic transparency, must also be addressed to foster trust among practitioners and patients.[11]

Future directions

Future research should focus on multicenter trials with larger and more diverse populations to validate these findings. Expanding AI applications to include real-time diagnostic support in telemedicine settings could bridge access gaps in rural areas. Additionally, integrating AI with existing radiological and histopathological workflows could enhance its utility in comprehensive patient care.[12]

CONCLUSION

AI-assisted diagnosis is a promising tool for early detection of oral cancer, demonstrating superior sensitivity, specificity, and efficiency compared to conventional methods. Its integration into clinical workflows could revolutionize oral oncology, improving patient outcomes and reducing healthcare burdens. Continued research and validation are essential to realize its full potential in diverse clinical settings.

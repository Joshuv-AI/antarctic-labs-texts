Effectiveness and safety of methylprednisolone combination with abrocitinib compared to methylprednisolone combination with azathioprine in bullous pemphigoid: a retrospective study
Authors: Ying Chen, Zheyu Zhuang, Jing Mao, Siyi Bao, Yihua Zhang, Zhenlan Wu, Wenyun Chen, Zequn Tong, Ting Gong, Chao Ji
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Bullous pemphigoid (BP) is an autoimmune skin disorder involving Th2-related inflammation. Abrocitinib is a JAK1 inhibitor and a promising therapeutic option for BP as it can effectively attenuate Th2-associated inflammation. This study compared the efficacy and safety of methylprednisolone combined with abrocitinib (abrocitinib group) versus methylprednisolone combined with azathioprine (conventional group) in BP treatment. The median time for disease control and achievement of minimal therapy in the abrocitinib group was notably reduced compared with those in the conventional group [7 vs. 11 days (p = 0.0006) and 100 versus days (p < 0.0001), respectively]. Additionally, the median cumulative methylprednisolone amount (at the time of achieving minimal therapy) in the abrocitinib group was remarkably reduced compared to that in the conventional group (1568 versus 2170 mg; p < 0.0001). Moreover, the abrocitinib group exhibited a more rapid decrease in Bullous Pemphigoid Disease Area Index (BPDAI) and itching Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) scores. At week 36, 11 patients (52.4%) in the abrocitinib group achieved complete remission (CR) off therapy, which was significantly higher than 2 patients (9.1%) in the conventional group (p = 0.003). There were no serious abrocitinib-associated adverse effects. Methylprednisolone combined with abrocitinib appeared more effective than methylprednisolone combined with azathioprine in controlling disease progression and tapering corticosteroids in BP patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-21671-6.

Introduction

Bullous pemphigoid (BP) is an autoimmune subepidermal blistering disease that predominantly affects elderly people1,2. Accumulating studies have demonstrated a noticeable increase in the incidence of BP over the past decades, which may be attributed to population aging, drugs that potentially induce diseases, and enhanced clinical diagnostic capabilities1–3. The pathogenesis of BP primarily revolves around the presence of autoantibodies targeting the hemidesmosomal proteins BP Antigen 1 (BPAg1 or BP230) and BP Antigen 2 (BPAg2 or BP180/collagen XVII), which are located at the dermal-epidermal junction4,5.

Currently, the treatment strategies for BP include topical or systemic use of corticosteroids, with or without adjuvant conventional immunosuppressive/immunomodulatory therapy depending on the patients’ clinical characteristics4–6. Nevertheless, considering that most BP patients are elderly and have multiple comorbidities, long-term administration of corticosteroids and immunosuppressive therapy may be difficult for them to tolerate7–9. Recent biologics (such as rituximab, dupilumab, and omalizumab) have been utilized in the management of BP, but their efficacy varies among different populations10–12. Therefore, effectively managing BP remains a challenge.

Multiple studies have indicated that the Janus kinase (JAK)-signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT) pathway is implicated as a key driver in various inflammatory diseases13–16. JAK inhibitors (JAKis) have been extensively utilized in numerous chronic inflammatory and autoimmune dermatosis, including atopic dermatitis, which is a disease driven by Th2 cell-type inflammation17–19. It’s recognized that BP has distinctive characteristics of severe itching, urticarial rash, elevated eosinophil and immunoglobulin E (IgE) levels, and the increase in interleukin (IL)−4 and IL-13, indicating that type 2 inflammation plays a significant role in BP pathogenesis, which could be mediated by the JAK-STAT signal transduction7,17,20. Additionally, recent case reports have provided evidence that the use of JAKis (such as abrocitinib, upadacitinib, tofacitinib, and baricitinib) may serve as an effective option for BP treatment21–24. However, no cohort studies focusing on the use of JAKis for BP treatment have been reportedof BP.

Herein, the present study evaluated the efficacy and safety of methylprednisolone in combination with abrocitinib compared to methylprednisolone in combination with azathioprine in two clinical cohorts of moderate-to-severe BP.

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 43 patients were enrolled in this study and allocated into the abrocitinib group (n = 21) and conventional group (n = 22) (Fig. 1). The median age of patients in the abrocitinib group and the conventional group was 71.0 years [(interquartile range (IQR) 64.5–76.5] and 72.5 years (IQR 59.5–79), respectively (p = 0.787). Moreover, the median disease duration was 2.3 months (IQR 0.8–5.0) in the abrocitinib group and 2.4 months (IQR 1–6.7) in the conventional group (p = 0.814), respectively25. Based on the initial Bullous Pemphigoid Disease Area Index (BPDAI) scores, 12 patients (57.1%) in the abrocitinib group were classified as moderate and 9 patients (42.9%) as severe, while in the conventional group, 14 patients (63.6%) were classified as moderate and 8 patients (36.4%) as severe (p = 0.760). The median methylprednisolone dose at the initial stage was 30 mg (IQR 24–36) once daily in the abrocitinib group and 30 mg (IQR 24–32.5) once daily in the conventional group (p = 0.804). No significant differences were observed in age, disease duration, disease severity, and initial methylprednisolone dose between the two groups. Prior therapies of the two groups include systemic/topical corticosteroids, immunosuppressants, and tetracycline antibiotics. Additionally, patients in both groups had various comorbidities, such as cardiovascular disease, hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, or chronic lung conditions. There were no statistically significant differences in prior therapies and comorbidities between the two groups. Primary outcomes

In this study, the median time to disease control in the abrocitinib group was 7 days (IQR 6–8) and 11 days (IQR 9.75–13.5) in the conventional group (p = 0.0006) (Fig. 2a). Once achieving effective disease control, the lesions showed progressive healing. Figure 3 displays photographs of lesion changes in a severe BP patient following 4-week combined abrocitinib and methylprednisolone treatment. Notably, time to reach minimal therapy was significantly shorter in the abrocitinib group (median 100 days, IQR 94–120) versus the conventional group (median 140 days, IQR 122.5–151) (p < 0.0001) (Fig. 2b). The abrocitinib group required a notably lower median cumulative methylprednisolone amount to achieve minimal therapy (1568 mg, IQR 1312–1897) compared to the conventional group (2170 mg, IQR 1789–2548) (p = 0.0005) (Fig. 2c). Regarding hospitalization duration, patients with combined abrocitinib and methylprednisolone treatment experienced a shorter hospitalization period (Fig. 2d). The median length of stay was notably reduced in the abrocitinib group (12 days, IQR 11–13) relative to the conventional group (16 days, IQR 14.8–18.5) (p = 0.0002).

Secondary outcomes

BPDAI and itching numeric rating scale (NRS) scores

BPDAI score served as an important indicator for evaluating BP severity. At baseline (week 0), No significant difference was observed in the BPDAI scores between the abrocitinib group (48, IQR 26.5–79) and the conventional group (40.5, IQR 28–71) (p = 0.796, Fig. 4a). BPDAI scores were assessed at week 2, 4, 8, 16, 24, and 36 after treatment in both groups. According to the results, the abrocitinib group showed a more rapid decrease in BPDAI scores compared to the conventional group at weeks 2 (p < 0.0001) and week 4 (p = 0.035, Fig. 4a). By week 8, BPDAI scores continued to decline in both groups, but there was no significant difference (p = 0.087, Fig. 4a). Additionally, the itching NRS scores of the abrocitinib group were 6 (IQR 5–7.5) versus 6.00 (IQR 4.8–7.3) in the conventional group at week 0 (p = 0.734, Fig. 4b). Following treatment, both groups demonstrated a decrease in NRS scores, with the abrocitinib group showing a notably greater reduction compared to the conventional group at both weeks 2 (p = 0.0003) and week 4 (p = 0.007, Fig. 4b). By week 8, no significant difference in NRS scores between the two groups was observed(p = 0.457, Fig. 4b).

Laboratory findings

At baseline, elevated IgE level was observed in 15 patients (71.4%) in the abrocitinib group (range: 194 − 15,920 IU/ml) and 14 (63.6%) in the conventional group (range: 156 − 13,000 IU/ml) (p = 0.747). After 2 weeks of treatment, both the abrocitinib group (p < 0.0001, Fig. 5a) and the conventional group (p = 0.001, Fig. 5b) demonstrated a significant reduction in IgE levels. However, no significant intergroup difference was observed in the decline of IgE levels between two groups (week 0, p = 0.771; week 2, p = 0.923) (Fig. 5c). In the abrocitinib group, 9 patients (42.9%) exhibited an elevated eosinophil percentage (Eos%) at baseline, compared with 8 patients (36.4%) in the conventional group (p = 0.760). Both groups demonstrated a decrease in Eos% at week 2 (abrocitinib group: p < 0.0001; conventional group: p = 0.0002 (Fig. 5d-e), but no significant difference was found between the two groups at week 0 (p = 0.622) and week 2 (p = 0.407) (Fig. 5f).

Clinical remissions

At week 36, in the abrocitinib group, 11 patients (52.4%) achieved complete remission (CR) off therapy, 5 patients (23.8%) achieved CR on minimal therapy, and 5 patients (23.8%) achieved partial remission (PR) on minimal therapy. By comparison, in the conventional group, 2 patients (9.1%) achieved CR off therapy, 11 patients (50%) achieved CR on minimal therapy, and 9 patients (40.9%) achieved PR on minimal therapy. Patients in the abrocitinib group showed notably higher CR rate off therapy relative to those in the conventional group (52.4% vs. 9.1%) (p = 0.003). As revealed by univariate and multivariate logistic regression analysis results, abrocitinib treatment remarkably increased the likelihood of CR off therapy (OR [95% CI], 16.933 [2.406, 119.182]; p = 0.004). Additionally, there was no significant correlation between achieved CR off therapy and sex (p = 0.403), age (p = 0.567), duration (p = 0.309), baseline IgE level (p = 0.089), BP severity (p = 0.957), and initial methylprednisolone dosage (p = 0.268) (Table 2). Moreover, 2 patients (9.5%) in the abrocitinib group and 3 patients (13.6%) in the conventional group experienced a relapse. After adjusting the methylprednisolone dose to the previous level, the disease was successfully controlled (Supplementary Table S1). There was no significant difference in the relapse rate between the two groups (p = 1). No treatment failures occurred in either group.

Safety

Throughout the study period, safety was continuously monitored for adverse events (AEs). During the whole treatment and follow-up period, the abrocitinib group demonstrated a favorable safety profile with fewer AEs compared with the conventional group (25 vs. 36 AEs). The most frequently occurred AEs included elevated blood glucose [6 (28.6%) with abrocitinib therapy vs. 10 (45.5%) with conventional therapy] and elevated blood lipid [7 (33.3%) with abrocitinib therapy vs. 9 (40.9%) with conventional therapy]. Other recorded AEs in the abrocitinib group versus the conventional group included acne-like eruptions (2 vs. 3), fatigue (2 vs. 3), upper respiratory tract infection (4 vs. 4), gastrointestinal symptoms (1 vs. 3), elevation of liver enzyme (1 vs. 3), herpes simplex infection (1 vs. 1), and headache (1 vs. 0). Importantly, no severe AEs (grade 3/4) were observed in either group. Additionally, none of the adverse events required hospitalization or led to permanent treatment discontinuation. Although abrocitinib demonstrated a favorable safety and efficacy profile compared to the conventional treatment group, it is important to note that the conventional treatment arm may not have been the most optimal comparator.

Discussion

The pathogenesis of BP is highly intricate26, involving multiple Th2-related inflammatory cells and mediators8,9,26. Type 2 inflammation is characterized by the involvement of CD4+Th2 cells, which secrete diverse cytokines (IL-4, IL-5, IL-6, IL-9, and IL-13), thereby promoting type 2 immunity response. In this response, eosinophils, mast cells, and basophils are the primary effector cells, demonstrating pronounced IgE antibody8,27–29,. Growing studies have investigated the role of type 2 inflammatory factors in BP development and documented the utilization of JAKis in managing BP18,22–24,29,30.

As has been evidenced previously, JAKis are effective in treating BP, achieving treatment goals without serious side effects, and enhancing itching management22–24,30. Notably, these studies report no serious AEs, supporting the favorable safety and tolerability of JAKis in BP management. Abrocitinib, a JAK1 inhibitor, has been approved for the treatment of atopic dermatitis31. It disrupts the signaling of various proinflammatory cytokines implicated in AD pathogenesis, including IL-4, IL-13, IL-22, IL-31, interferon (IFN)-γ, and thymic stromal lymphopoietin (TSLP)17,19,31. By concurrently blocking multiple pathways, abrocitinib demonstrates superior efficacy and faster symptom relief from both pruritus and cutaneous lesions compared to biologics like dupilumab, a monoclonal antibody, inhibits the IL-4 and IL-13 signaling to treat AD8,10,19,32. In support of this, Jiang W et al. have treated two BP patients with abrocitinib, observing good therapeutic outcomes and a rapid reduction in glucocorticoid dependence24. Based on this, we investigated abrocitinib’s efficacy and safety in BP, a disease also involving type 2 inflammation.

Our research revealed that the combination of abrocitinib and methylprednisolone exhibited significant clinical benefits for moderate and severe BP. It significantly shortens the time to cessation of new lesions, rapidly achieving disease control. Additionally, it also notably reduced the time to achieve minimal therapy. Upon reaching the minimal therapy, the cumulative methylprednisolone amount was remarkably reduced in the abrocitinib group compared to that in the conventional group. Accumulating studies have indicated that high dose and long-term administration of systemic corticosteroid therapy is associated with higher mortality rates and increased side effects, particularly those with multiple comorbidities in the elderly populations33,34. Hence, the significant reduction in corticosteroid usage could undoubtedly confer substantial benefits to these patients. Noteworthy, in the abrocitinib group, azathioprine was not used, eliminating the potential immunosuppressive drug-associated side effects. This provides a critical treatment alternative for patients with contraindications to immunosuppressive therapy.

Compared to the conventional group, the abrocitinib group exhibited a more rapid decrease in BPDAI scores, suggesting a faster onset of therapeutic action. Moreover, the abrocitinib group was also superior in relieving patients’ itching symptoms compared to the conventional group. We attributed this rapid relief of itching in the abrocitinib group to its good performance in inhibiting type 2 inflammation8,19. The rapid amelioration of pruritus symptoms and disease severity in patients highlights abrocitinib’s therapeutic advantage and potential clinical significance in BP management.

When considering serological markers, despite a significant decrease in IgE level observed in both groups, 5 patients (23.8%) in the abrocitinib group and 6 patients (27.3%) in the conventional group have not yet achieved a normal IgE level. Consistently, a previous study has also shown that IgE level is slowly reduced after BP disease control11. However, the specific mechanism remains unclear and needs further exploration.

At week 36, the abrocitinib group demonstrated a superior CR rate off therapy compared to the conventional group, confirming the effectiveness of abrocitinib in BP treatment. Univariate logistic regression analysis revealed no significant correlation between the achieved CR off therapy and BP severity. The lack of statistically significant differences in the likelihood of CR off therapy related to BP severity may be attributed to several factors as follows. Specifically, the heterogeneity of the study population (including variability in patient characteristics such as age, comorbidities, or prior treatments) may affect the potential impact of BP severity on CR off therapy. Additionally, the follow-up duration may be insufficient to detect differences in CR off therapy rates based on BP severity, and longer follow-up periods may be needed to capture such effects. These factors highlight that further studies should enroll more homogeneous populations and extend follow-up durations to better understand the relationship between BP severity and CR off therapy. Furthermore, no significant difference was observed in relapse between the two groups, which may be limited by the small size and short follow-up time. Additionally, some patients are currently receiving treatment and undergoing further monitoring, which also potentially influences the observed outcomes.

The observed abrocitinib-associated AEs included headache, nausea, acne, and upper respiratory infection, which were classified into mild AEs17,18. Notably, none of the serious AEs of JAKis highlighted by Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and European Medicines Agency (EMA) was observed during the study period, such as cardiovascular events, malignancies, deep venous thrombosis/pulmonary embolism (DVT/PE), and mortality30 The recorded safety profile is particularly significant, as BP patients are typically elderly and are inherently more susceptible to venous thromboembolism (VTE)35,36. When using JAKis to treat BP, it is essential to assess the patient’s thrombotic risk first. For high-risk BP patients, JAKis should be avoided, or stricter follow-up and monitoring should be conducted during therapy. In our study, thorough screening was conducted to exclude contraindications for JAKis before using abrocitinib, and patients were closely monitored during treatment. After the disease control, abrocitinib was gradually tapered off, and the treatment was discontinued upon reaching minimal therapy, optimizing the balance between sustained efficacy and AE prevention.

Our findings demonstrated that abrocitinib combined with methylprednisolone offered distinct therapeutic advantages for BP management, including rapid pruritus relief, significant reduction in corticosteroid duration and cumulative use, and elimination of azathioprine use. This regimen could be a preferred option for patients with severe pruritus symptoms, those who cannot tolerate long-term corticosteroid use or require a reduction in corticosteroid dosage due to comorbidities and side effects, or those with thiopurine methyltransferase abnormalities. However, the observed superiority of abrocitinib over conventional treatment should be interpreted with caution due to the potential suboptimal nature of the comparator arm. The conventional treatment used in this study may not have been the most effective option for all patients, which could have led to an exaggerated apparent benefit of abrocitinib. Future studies with a more representative and optimal comparator arm are needed to better assess the true efficacy of abrocitinib.

Nevertheless, there are still some limitations in this study. First, this study was a single-center study with a limited follow-up period and a relatively small sample size. As such, the findings may not be directly applicable to other healthcare settings or more diverse patient populations. The study was conducted in a homogenous group, and as a result, the results may not fully reflect the variability seen in broader, multi-center, or international settings. Second, regular monitoring of BP180 and BP230 antibodies was not conducted due to this retrospective design, restricting disease severity and recurrence risk assessment. Furthermore, the retrospective design introduces potential selection bias and unmeasured confounding. Although we adjusted for available confounders, residual confounding from unrecorded factors cannot be excluded. These inherent constraints warrant confirmation of our findings through prospective, randomized studies. Third, the conventional group received fixed-dose azathioprine (100 mg/day), potentially below the recommended dose of 2–3 mg/kg/day7 for some BP patients (mean weight approximately 64 kg). This is a compromise in order to establish a standard comparator arm. Additionally, IgE and eosinophil levels were only assessed at baseline and 2 weeks post-treatment, which precluded the assessment of their dynamic fluctuations over the course of the disease. Lastly, since patients were allowed to choose their treatment regimen, those who selected abrocitinib may have had different expectations or attitudes towards new therapies compared to those who chose the conventional treatment. This could lead to inherent differences in how patients perceive treatment outcomes, potentially influencing the reported efficacy and satisfaction with the treatment. Despite these limitations, our study offers valuable and encouraging clinical evidence for abrocitinib as a promising therapeutic option for BP. However, further studies, particularly multi-center, prospective, and randomized trials, are needed to confirm our findings and evaluate the generalizability of these results to different patient populations and healthcare systems.

Conclusions

Collectively, our study demonstrates that combining abrocitinib with methylprednisolone is an effective and safe treatment strategy for BP. Specifically, this approach has the advantages of rapid disease control, significant reduction in the time to achieving minimal therapy, a decrease in overall methylprednisolone use, and elimination of conventional immunosuppressive drug requirements. Nevertheless, further exploration is required to fully understand the underlying mechanism. Additionally, large-scale prospective cohort studies with extended follow-up periods are needed to further evaluate the efficacy and safety of using abrocitinib for BP treatment.

Methods

Ethics approval and consent to participate

This study was approved by the Ethics Commission of the First Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University in compliance with Chinese law (no. [2015]084 − 2) and was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. This study was retrospectively registered on 08/31/2024 at Clinical Trial Registry (URL: Number: NCT06561256). Due to the retrospective nature of the study, the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of the First Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University waived the need of obtaining informed consent. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants for the publication of potentially recognizable photographs, with explicit acknowledgement that these photographs may be publicly available.

Sample size calculation

The sample size was determined a priori based on the primary endpoint of complete remission off therapy at week 36. Based on previous data and clinical consensus, we assumed a remission rate of 15% for the conventional treatment group (methylprednisolone + azathioprine) and 45% for the abrocitinib group (methylprednisolone + abrocitinib). Using PASS 2020 software (NCSS, LLC), a sample size of 20 patients per group was calculated to provide 80% power to detect this difference (30% absolute difference) using a two-sided Chi-square test with a significance level (alpha) of 0.05.

Study design and population

BP patients treated at the Department of Dermatology of the First Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University between October 1, 2022 and May 31, 2024 were retrospectively reviewed in this cohort study. The inclusion criteria for BP patients were: (1) confirmed BP diagnosis according to the guidelines7, evidenced by clinical features, positive direct immunofluorescence microscopy, and/or serum testing positive for IgG auto-antibodies against BP180 and/or BP230; (2) moderate-to-severe BP disease severity determined using the BPDAI scores7: moderate (20 ≤ BPDAI ≤ 56) and severe (BPDAI ≥ 57); and (3) minimum 36-week follow-up after treatment initiation. The exclusion criteria were: (1) patients diagnosed with malignant neoplasms and (2) patients administered with any JAKis or biologics within 3 months.

Treatment and data collection

The hospitalized BP patients were assessed for eligibility. At the start of treatment, patients were informed of all available treatment options, including both methylprednisolone combined with abrocitinib and methylprednisolone combined with azathioprine. Patients then selected their preferred treatment option based on their individual circumstances and clinical condition. A total of 43 patients were included in this study and were allocated into the abrocitinib group (n = 21, treated with methylprednisolone combined with abrocitinib) and the conventional group (n = 22, treated with methylprednisolone combined with azathioprine). After discharge, patients were followed up every 1 to 2 weeks during the first month, and then every 2 to 4 weeks subsequently. A more frequent schedule was implemented if the condition deteriorated. After 12 weeks of follow-up, data were collected ± 5 days around the target date. The initial dose of methylprednisolone was 0.4 mg/kg/d for moderate BP patients and 0.6 mg/kg/d for severe BP patients. Patients in the abrocitinib group received 100 mg abrocitinib daily. Given the predominantly elderly cohort, a 100 mg treatment dose was administered. This approach was chosen to maintain the balance between therapeutic efficacy and adverse reactions. All participants underwent comprehensive screening to exclude any JAKis-associated contraindications. Furthermore, all patients were evaluated for the risk of VTE thrombosis, with all classified as low or very low risk. Patients in the conventional group received 100 mg/day azathioprine (50 mg twice daily), and exhibited normal thiopurine methyltransferase (TPMT) activity. Methylprednisolone tapering was generally consistent in the two groups following the suggestions by the guidelines7, initiated when there were fewer than three transient lesions. Treatment and tapering regimen for patients were shown in Fig. 1. Both groups received moderate-dose corticosteroids combined with either abrocitinib or azathioprine to achieve rapid disease control while facilitating corticosteroid reduction.

Outcome measures

The primary outcomes included time to disease control (the time point at which new lesions or pruritic symptoms ceased to form and established lesions began to heal25, time to achieve minimal therapy (the time point at which the dose of methylprednisolone ≤ 0.08 mg/kg/d was achieved), and the cumulative methylprednisolone amount (total methylprednisolone amount at the time of achieving minimal therapy). The secondary outcomes included the scores of BPDAI and itching NRS at week 0, 2, 4, 8, 16, 24, and 36, IgE level and eosinophil percentage (Eos%) at week 0 and week 2, and the CR rates at week 36. CR was defined according to the recommendations by an international panel of experts25: (1) CR on minimal therapy/off therapy refers to the absence of new or established lesions or pruritus while patient is receiving minimal therapy/off therapy for at least 2 months; (2) PR on minimal therapy/off therapy refers to the presence of transient new lesions that heal within 1 week while patient is receiving minimal therapy/off therapy for at least 2 months; (3) transient lesions are defined as new lesions that heal within 1 week or pruritus lasting < 1 week and are cleared without treatment; (4) relapse refers to the appearance of ≥ 3 new lesions/month or at least one large (> 10 cm in diameter) eczematous lesion or urticarial plaques that do not heal within 1 week, or extension of established lesions or daily pruritus in patients who has achieved disease control.

For safety assessment, all AEs and laboratory abnormalities were evaluated based on frequency and severity. Additionally, AEs in our study were continuously monitored and graded using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS.26 and GraphPad Prism 10. for figure generation. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages, while coContinuous variables were represented as means and standard deviations (SD) for normally distributed data or as medians and IQR for non-normally distributed data. Absolute and relative frequencies were used to describe qualitative variables. Group comparisons were conducted using the Chi-square test for qualitative variables. For quantitative variables, independent sample t-test was used for group comparison for variables with symmetric distribution, and the Mann-Whitney test was used for variables with skewed distributions. The survival curves of the time to disease control and achieving minimal therapy were estimated utilizing the Kaplan-Meier method (the log-rank test). Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were employed to identify factors affecting the curative effect. For secondary outcomes, adjustment for multiple comparisons was performed using the Bonferroni correction to reduce the risk of type I error. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all analyses.

Prediction Model and Score for Subtyping Primary Aldosteronism
Authors: Hui Dong, Zhitian Huang, Jiangnan Huang, Jianzhong Xu, Ying Zhang, Yifei Dong, Hongwu Li, Yujie Zuo, Kaiwen Jiang, Wentao Ma, Yubao Zou, Xiongjing Jiang
Published: 2026

Subtype diagnosis and optimal management of primary aldosteronism (PA) rely heavily on adrenal venous sampling (AVS). Nevertheless, AVS is an invasive, technically demanding, and costly procedure, with reported success rates ranging from 30%-80% across multicenter registry studies.1 Prediction models and scoring systems derived from readily available clinical parameters can help streamline the AVS-guided subtyping process. However, well-established predictive tools with adequate development samples, multiple independent validations, and consistently robust diagnostic performance remain scarce.

A retrospective cohort of 402 PA patients treated at Fuwai Hospital from 2017-2022 was randomly split (3:1) into training and testing sets for model development and internal validation. Prospective and external validations were performed using a cohort of 150 patients recruited at Fuwai Hospital between 2023 and 2024 and a cohort of 494 patients from a multicenter study,2 respectively. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Fuwai Hospital (Approval No. 2023–2155).

All patients underwent standardized screening (supine after overnight bed rest and repeated after at least 2 hours of upright posture) and confirmatory tests (upright) for PA. Plasma aldosterone concentration (PAC; ng/dL) and direct renin concentration (mU/L) were measured using a chemiluminescent immunoassay to calculate the aldosterone-to-renin ratio (ARR = PAC/direct renin concentration). The supine-to-upright aldosterone change was calculated as: (upright PAC − supine PAC)/supine PAC. PA was diagnosed in patients with an upright ARR >3.7 and positive results on either the saline infusion test (PAC >10 ng/dL after 2 L of saline infused over 4 h) or captopril challenge test (PAC suppression ≤30% measured 2 h after 50 mg oral captopril). Adrenal computed tomography was used to identify adrenal nodules. AVS was performed without cosyntropin stimulation, with successful cannulation defined by a selectivity index >2 and unilateral primary aldosteronism (UPA) defined by a lateralization index >2.

Predictor selection for UPA was based on prior clinical knowledge and further refined using least absolute shrinkage and selection operator regression. Four supervised classifiers (logistic regression, support vector machine, random forest, and eXtreme gradient boosting [XGB]) incorporating the retained predictors were fitted to develop UPA predictive models. Hyperparameters were optimized via 10-fold cross-validation with grid search. Model performance was assessed using the receiver operating characteristic curve and area under the curve (AUC) for discrimination, and using calibration curves and the Brier score for calibration, with overoptimism corrected using 1,000 bootstrap resamples. Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) were applied to interpret feature contributions. Variables were categorized and weighted based on optimal XGB splits and mean SHAP values to construct a 10-point Primary Aldosteronism Subtyping Score (PASS), which was evaluated for discrimination and clinical utility using decision curve analysis. Standard data preprocessing and missing value imputation were performed before model development. Statistical analyses were performed using Python 3.12.4 (scikit-learn). P < 0.05 was statistically significant.

The retrospective cohort was aged 49 ± 11 years aldosterone-to-renin ratio; 46.8% (188 of 402) were female, 48.5% (195 of 402) had a unilateral adrenal nodule, and 62.2% (250 of 402) were UPA. Hypertension grade, lowest serum potassium, upright ARR, supine-to-upright aldosterone change, and unilateral adrenal nodule were retained as model input variables, corresponding to an events-per-predictor ratio of 37.4. The XGB model achieved the best discrimination among all classifiers, with AUCs of 0.944 (95% CI: 0.918-0.967; optimism-corrected: 0.907), 0.905 (95% CI: 0.834-0.958), 0.894 (95% CI: 0.837-0.940), and 0.872 (95% CI: 0.851-0.894) in the training, internal testing, prospective, and external cohorts, respectively. Calibration was strong in the training and testing cohorts and remained acceptable in the prospective and external cohorts, with Brier scores of 0.094 (95% CI: 0.077-0.112; optimism-corrected: 0.122), 0.119 (95% CI: 0.083-0.162), 0.180 (95% CI: 0.147-0.217), and 0.168 (95% CI: 0.157-0.178), respectively (Figure 1A). The PASS demonstrated comparable discrimination, with AUCs of 0.909 (95% CI: 0.870-0.940), 0.888 (95% CI: 0.820-0.948), 0.880 (95% CI: 0.815-0.933), and 0.867 (95% CI: 0.836-0.899) across the same cohorts. Decision curve analysis further indicated that the PASS conferred a positive net benefit across all score thresholds in each cohort (Figure 1B). Based on a sensitivity-optimized cutoff of 4 and a specificity-optimized cutoff of 7 (corresponding to the maximal Youden index), we proposed a PASS-based PA management workflow: probable bilateral primary aldosteronism (scores ≤3) are recommended for mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist, whereas probable UPA (scores ≥7) are advised to undergo AVS. A cutoff score ≤3 for mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist recommendation could spare AVS in 57.3% (87 of 152), 51.1% (24 of 47), and 38.5% (60 of 156) bilateral primary aldosteronism patients across the retrospective, prospective, and external cohorts, respectively, at the cost of misclassifying 2.4% (6 of 250), 4.9% (5 of 103), and 4.7% (16 of 338) UPA patients.

UPA in our cohorts exhibited more severe clinical and biochemical phenotypes, attenuated postural responses, and more frequent adrenal nodules, likely associated with the higher prevalence of KCNJ5-mutated aldosterone-producing adenomas in Asians.4,5 Removing existing variables or adding new ones (sex, age, upright and postconfirmatory PAC) reduced the XGB model’s AUC in the validation cohorts. PASS demonstrated superior performance compared with established scoring systems, including Küpers’ (AUC: 0.750, 95% CI: 0.700-0.795; 0.735, 95% CI: 0.647-0.826; 0.806, 95% CI: 0.767-0.844),6 Kobayashi’s (0.770, 95% CI: 0.724-0.816; 0.727, 95% CI: 0.638-0.818; 0.805, 95% CI: 0.764-0.841),7 Zhang’s (0.822, 95% CI: 0.778-0.861; 0.811, 95% CI: 0.734-0.882; 0.843, 95% CI: 0.804-0.880),8 and Burrello’s (0.780, 95% CI: 0.737-0.825; 0.761, 95% CI: 0.685-0.840; 0.815, 95% CI: 0.776-0.851)9 across the retrospective, prospective, and external cohorts, respectively.

This study has several limitations. As with previous studies, the predictive tool could not determine the laterality of aldosterone hypersecretion. Potential selection biases may exist due to diagnostic protocols, which could overlook mild or atypical PA cases. Nevertheless, this study introduces the first predictive tool integrating the postural stimulation test, which has been rigorously validated across multiple cohorts and demonstrates strong discriminative performance. Its development holds particular value for Asian populations, in whom UPA frequently manifests as posture-unresponsive aldosterone-producing adenoma,4,5 and for high-volume hypertension and endocrine centers in Asia where AVS accessibility remains limited.10

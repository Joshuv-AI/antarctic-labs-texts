Chitosan-based Moringa oleifera nanoparticles attenuate hyperglycemia-induced vascular endothelial growth factor upregulation in human lens epithelial cells
Authors: Hendra Susanto, Moch Sholeh, Nashi Widodo, Nurina Febriyanti Ayuningtyas, Miftakhul Cahyati, Adeodatus Yuda Handaya
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Chronic hyperglycemia induces oxidative stress and stress-responsive signaling in ocular tissues, contributing to the progression of diabetic eye complications. Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) is a key mediator of hyperglycemia-induced cellular dysfunction. Moringa oleifera, rich in antioxidant flavonoids and phenolics, exhibits potential protective effects against metabolic stress. This study evaluated whether chitosan-based M. oleifera nanoparticles attenuate hyperglycemia-induced upregulation of VEGF in human lens epithelial (HLE) cells. HLE-B3 cells were exposed to high glucose to establish an in vitro model of hyperglycemia. Chitosan nanoparticles loaded with M. oleifera seed (NMS) and leaf (NML) extracts were fabricated and characterized for physicochemical properties and bioactive compound content. Cytotoxicity was assessed using MTT assays under normoglycemic and hyperglycemic conditions. VEGF expression was evaluated by immunofluorescence and semi-quantitative image analysis following nanoparticle treatment. Nanoparticle characterization confirmed successful encapsulation of flavonoids and phenolic compounds with stable physicochemical properties. Both NMS and NML were well tolerated under normoglycemic conditions (IC50 >3500 µg/mL), while hyperglycemia-induced cells exhibited greater sensitivity (IC50: 1517 µg/mL for NMS; 1943 µg/mL for NML). High glucose exposure significantly increased VEGF expression, whereas treatment with NMS and NML markedly attenuated VEGF upregulation. Chitosan-based M. oleifera nanoparticles attenuate hyperglycemia-induced VEGF expression in HLE cells, indicating a protective effect against epithelial stress signaling. These findings highlight the potential of nanoencapsulated M. oleifera as a strategy to modulate hyperglycemia-driven ocular epithelial dysfunction.

INTRODUCTION

Diabetes mellitus (DM) has evolved into a major global metabolic disorder and is associated with multiple vision-threatening ocular complications, including diabetic retinopathy and diabetic cataract.[123] The global and regional prevalence of DM continues to rise, including in Indonesia, where more than 20 million adults are affected.[4] Chronic hyperglycemia is the central driver of diabetic ocular pathology, initiating a cascade of metabolic disturbances such as reactive oxygen species (ROS) overproduction, advanced glycation end-product (AGE) accumulation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and activation of stress-responsive signaling pathways.[567] These mechanisms collectively contribute to cellular dysfunction across different ocular tissues.

Although diabetic retinopathy primarily involves retinal endothelial cells and pericytes, hyperglycemia-induced oxidative stress and metabolic dysregulation also significantly affect lens epithelial cells, which are highly metabolically active and sensitive to fluctuations in glucose levels. Persistent hyperglycemia in lens epithelial cells promotes oxidative damage, polyol pathway activation, inflammatory signaling, and dysregulation of pro-angiogenic mediators such as vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF).[8] Although VEGF is widely recognized for its role in retinal neovascularization, its increased expression in nonvascular ocular cells under hyperglycemic conditions reflects a broader stress-responsive signaling pathway.[9] Dysregulated VEGF expression has been associated with cellular dysfunction, inflammatory amplification, and progression of diabetic ocular complications.[10]

Given the central roles of oxidative stress and VEGF signaling in hyperglycemia-induced cellular injury, strategies that modulate these pathways may offer protective benefits.[11] Natural bioactive compounds with antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties have attracted increasing interest as potential modulators of diabetic complications.[12] Moringa oleifera is rich in flavonoids and phenolic compounds known to exert antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and anti-hyperglycemic effects.[1314] However, the clinical translation of crude extracts is limited by poor stability and bioavailability.

Nanoencapsulation using chitosan, a biocompatible and mucoadhesive polysaccharide, enhances physicochemical stability, improves cellular uptake, and allows the controlled release of bioactive compounds.[151617] In the present study, we fabricated and characterized chitosan-based Moringa oleifera seed (NMS) and leaf (NML) nanoparticles and evaluated their effects in a hyperglycemia-induced human lens epithelial cell model (HLE-B3). We specifically investigated whether these nano formulations attenuate hyperglycemia-induced upregulation of VEGF, a marker of ocular epithelial stress. This work aims to explore the potential of Moringa-based nano formulations as protective modulators of VEGF-associated signaling in hyperglycemia-driven ocular epithelial dysfunction.

SUBJECTS AND METHODS

Nano formulation synthesis and characterization

Chitosan-Moringa seed nanoparticles (NMS) and leaf nanoparticles (NML) were synthesized using the ionic gelation method.[17] The physicochemical properties, including total phenolic content (TPC) and total flavonoid content (TFC), were quantified spectrophotometrically to ensure successful bioactive loading.

Total phenolic content and total flavonoid content analysis

TPC was determined via a modified Folin-Ciocalteu assay. Absorbance was measured at 760 nm (optimized from the standard at 765 nm) to ensure reliability against gallic acid standards. TFC was measured at 415 nm using the aluminum chloride colorimetric method with catechin as the reference.[18]

Particle analysis

Dynamic light scattering using a particle size analyzer was used to determine the Z-average, Polydispersity Index (PDI), and Zeta Potential. While the observed PDI (>0.4) indicates a broad size distribution, the Z-average remained within the appropriate range for cellular internalization.

Cell culture and hyperglycemic induction

HLE-B3 cells (ATCC, CRL-3603™) were maintained in RPMI 1640 or DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS at 37°C in 5%. To simulate hyperglycemia-induced ocular epithelial stress conditions/Hyperglycemia-induced model (HIM), cells at 60%–80% confluence were exposed to high-glucose medium (25–30 mM D-glucose) for 24 h. Negative controls (CN) were kept in physiological glucose conditions.

Experimental protocol and dose selection

Postinduction, cells were treated for 48 h with NMS and NML at various concentrations. The study groups were organized as follows:

Viability and immunofluorescence

Cytotoxicity (MTT assay)

Cell metabolic activity and viability were evaluated using the MTT assay (BioVision, Cat. No. 2809-1G) to assess cytotoxicity in the normoglycemic and HIM groups. Absorbance was measured spectrophotometrically, and IC₅₀ values were calculated to identify nontoxic concentrations for subsequent molecular analyses.

When the maximal tested concentration did not reach 50% inhibition, the IC₅₀ value was estimated by extrapolation from the fitted regression model.

Vascular endothelial growth factor immunofluorescence analysis

To assess HIM stress signaling, VEGF expression was evaluated by immunofluorescence staining. Cells cultured on coverslips were fixed, permeabilized, and incubated with primary anti-VEGF antibody (sc-53462), followed by fluorescent secondary antibody (LS-C149505). Nuclear staining was performed using DAPI. Fluorescence intensity was visualized using a fluorescence microscope and semi-quantified using ImageJ software. Quantitative analysis was expressed relative to the normoglycemic control group.

Statistical design

Experiments were performed in three independent biological replicates, with each treatment conducted in triplicate. Data are presented as mean ± SD. Statistical significance was assessed using one-way ANOVA followed by Duncan’s Multiple Range Test (DMRT) in GraphPad Prism v5, with a threshold of P < 0.05.

Cytotoxicity of NMS and NML on human lens epithelial-B3 cells

Using the MTT method, we evaluated the cytotoxicity of NMS and NML in HLE-B3 cells under normal and hyperglycemic conditions. In normal cells, both nano formulations exhibited low toxicity. The IC50 value for NMS (NMSeed) was 3515 µg/ml, and the IC50 for NML (NMLeaf) was 3788 µg/ml [Figure 2]. On the other side of the HIM cells, the IC50 values were markedly lower. The IC50 for NMS dropped to 1517 µg/ml, and the IC50 for NML dropped to 1943 µg/ml [Figure 3]. This finding indicates that cells under high-glucose-induced stress are significantly more sensitive to nanoparticle treatments than healthy cells.

DISCUSSION

This investigation demonstrates that chitosan-encapsulated Moringa oleifera seed (NMS) and leaf (NML) extracts attenuate hyperglycemia-induced upregulation of VEGF in HLE cells. A significant finding was the negative zeta potential of both NMS (−30.9 mV) and NML (−33.5 mV). Although chitosan is naturally cationic, this negative charge indicates that anionic phenols and flavonoids from the extracts effectively shielded the chitosan amino groups.[15] This high surface charge is advantageous, providing colloidal stability against aggregation and suggesting that cellular internalization likely occurs via efficient receptor-mediated endocytosis rather than nonspecific adsorption.[16]

A distinct therapeutic window was observed: while both nano formulations were well tolerated in normoglycemic cells (IC50 >3500 µg/mL), enhanced sensitivity was observed in the HIM (IC50: 1517–1943 µg/mL). This differential response indicates that metabolically stressed epithelial cells are more responsive to nanoparticle-mediated modulation. Importantly, the concentrations used for VEGF analysis remained within nontoxic limits, supporting the ocular safety profile of these formulations.[20]

Under hyperglycemic conditions, HLE-B3 cells exhibited significant upregulation of VEGF, consistent with stress-responsive signaling triggered by oxidative imbalance. Although VEGF is classically associated with retinal neovascularization in diabetic retinopathy, its increased expression in nonvascular ocular cells reflects a broader cellular response to metabolic stress. Hyperglycemia-induced ROS accumulation activates redox-sensitive transcription factors that promote VEGF expression, contributing to inflammatory amplification and tissue dysfunction.[2122] Although upstream mediators were not directly assessed in this study, the observed attenuation of VEGF following nanoparticle treatment may reflect the modulation of oxidative stress-associated signaling rather than direct inhibition of VEGF synthesis.

Both NMS and NML significantly reduced VEGF expression compared to the hyperglycemia group. This attenuation is likely mediated by the antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties of M. oleifera phytochemicals, particularly flavonoids and phenolic compounds known to scavenge ROS and modulate stress-activated signaling cascades. Differences between the two formulations may reflect compositional variations; NML, which is rich in flavonoids such as quercetin, demonstrated slightly stronger VEGF suppression, consistent with documented anti-angiogenic properties of these compounds.

While HLE-B3 cells do not replicate the vascular complexity of retinal pathology, they provide a relevant in vitro system to investigate upstream hyperglycemia-induced epithelial stress responses. The current findings support the concept that modulation of VEGF in lens epithelial cells may represent an early protective mechanism against hyperglycemia-driven ocular dysfunction. Although nonvascular in nature, lens epithelial cells exposed to metabolic stress may contribute to paracrine inflammatory signaling within the ocular microenvironment through dysregulated VEGF expression.[923] Early modulation of stress-associated mediators such as VEGF at the epithelial level may therefore represent a protective strategy to limit the progression of hyperglycemia-driven ocular dysfunction before the onset of advanced microvascular complications.[2425]

Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. The monoculture model does not reproduce multicellular interactions, vascular components, or blood-retinal barrier dynamics characteristic of advanced diabetic retinopathy. Therefore, extrapolation to retinal disease must be interpreted cautiously. Future studies employing retinal endothelial cells, co-culture systems, or in vivo diabetic models are required to further evaluate the translational relevance of these nanoformulations.

CONCLUSION

This study demonstrates that chitosan-based nanoparticles incorporating Moringa oleifera seed (NMS) and leaf (NML) extracts attenuate hyperglycemia-induced upregulation of VEGF in HLE cells. Both nanoformulations exhibited a favorable safety profile under normoglycemic conditions and significantly reduced stress-associated VEGF expression in a HIM. These findings suggest that Moringa-based nanoformulations may serve as protective modulators of hyperglycemia-driven ocular epithelial stress. Nevertheless, as the present study was conducted using a monoculture lens epithelial system, further investigations employing retinal-specific cell models and in vivo diabetic systems are warranted to establish the broader translational relevance of these nanoformulations in diabetic ocular pathology.

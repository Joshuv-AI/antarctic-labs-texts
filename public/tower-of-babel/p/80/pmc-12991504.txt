Evaluation of Clinical Efficacy of Orthognathic Surgery in the Treatment of Obstructive Sleep Apnea: An Original Research
Authors: Jyoti M. Biradar, Bhavik R. Patel, R Muralidharan, Anish D. Patole, Rinku N. Adwani, Abinash Mohapatra, Rahul Tiwari
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPurpose:This research evaluates the clinical efficacy of orthognathic surgery as a therapeutic intervention for moderate-to-severe OSA.Methods:This prospective research included 40 patients diagnosed with OSA through polysomnography. Patients underwent “maxillomandibular advancement (MMA)” surgery, and outcomes were assessed using polysomnographic parameters, “Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS),” and patient satisfaction over a 12-month follow-up.Results:Postoperative analysis revealed significant reductions in “apnea-hypopnea index (AHI)” and ESS scores. The mean AHI decreased from 35.2 ± 5.8 events/hour preoperatively to 8.4 ± 3.2 events/hour postoperatively (P < 0.001). Patient-reported satisfaction was high, with 85% rating outcomes as excellent.Conclusions:Orthognathic surgery, particularly MMA, demonstrates a substantial improvement in OSA severity and quality of life, supporting its role as a definitive treatment modality.

INTRODUCTION

Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) affects approximately four to six percent of adults globally and is associated with significant health risks, including cardiovascular diseases, diabetes, and reduced quality of life.[1] “Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP)” remains the first-line therapy; however, compliance is often suboptimal due to discomfort and lifestyle interference.[2] Surgical options, particularly orthognathic surgery, have emerged as definitive alternatives for patients with moderate-to-severe OSA who fail or refuse CPAP therapy.[3]

Orthognathic surgery, specifically MMA, enlarges the upper airway by repositioning the maxilla and mandible forward.[45] This research evaluates the clinical efficacy of MMA in improving polysomnographic parameters, reducing symptoms, and enhancing patient satisfaction among individuals with OSA.

METHODOLOGY

Research design and participants

This prospective cohort research was conducted at a tertiary care center over three years. Inclusion criteria were adults, aged 18–60 years, with polysomnography-confirmed moderate-to-severe OSA who failed CPAP therapy. Patients with craniofacial syndromes or comorbid conditions contraindicating surgery were excluded.

Surgical procedure

All participants underwent MMA surgery performed by a single experienced surgeon. The procedure involved Le Fort I osteotomy of the maxilla, bilateral sagittal split osteotomy of the mandible, and advancing the skeletal framework by 8–10 mm based on cephalometric analysis.

Outcome measures

Polysomnographic assessments were conducted preoperatively and 12 months postoperatively. Primary outcomes included changes in AHI, “oxygen desaturation index (ODI),” and lowest oxygen saturation (SpO2). Secondary outcomes were ESS scores and patient satisfaction ratings. Adverse events were recorded.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS v26.0. Paired t-tests were applied for continuous variables, and statistical significance was at 0.05 of P value.

RESULTS

The research included 40 participants (28 males, 12 females) with a mean age of 45.3 ± 8.1 years. Baseline characteristics, including BMI (32.6 ± 4.3 kg/m²), were comparable across participants.

The research demonstrated a significant improvement in polysomnographic parameters following MMA surgery. The mean AHI decreased from 35.2 ± 5.8 to 8.4 ± 3.2 events/hour, indicating a 76% reduction (P < 0.001). ODI also declined significantly from 32.1 ± 4.6 to 7.8 ± 2.9 (P < 0.001). The lowest SpO2 enhanced from 78.3 ± 5.2% to 89.7 ± 3.6% (P < 0.001). These findings confirm the effectiveness of MMA in alleviating OSA severity by expanding the upper airway [Table 1].

Patient-reported outcomes revealed a substantial improvement in symptoms and satisfaction after surgery. The Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS) score decreased significantly from 15.6 ± 3.2 preoperatively to 5.4 ± 2.1 postoperatively (P < 0.001), indicating reduced daytime sleepiness. Satisfaction ratings were high, with 85% of patients reporting excellent outcomes, 10% reporting good outcomes, and 5% reporting moderate satisfaction. These observations highlight the positive effect of MMA surgery on both symptom relief and patient quality of life [Table 2].

DISCUSSION

Orthognathic surgery, particularly maxillomandibular advancement (MMA), is increasingly recognized as an effective treatment for OSA, especially in patients with skeletal abnormalities contributing to airway obstruction. The present research corroborates findings from systematic reviews and meta-analyses demonstrating significant improvements in polysomnographic parameters and quality of life following surgery.

Current research aligns with Francisco et al.,[1] who emphasized the positive impact of orthognathic surgery on quality of life. Their systematic review highlights the dual benefits of functional airway improvement and enhanced patient-reported outcomes, reinforcing the findings of this research where 85% of patients rated outcomes as excellent. Similarly, Bègue et al.[2] reported that MMA combined with soft tissue procedures offers curative benefits, significantly reducing apnea severity, comparable to the results observed in current cohort.

The efficacy of MMA in skeletal Class III patients has also been discussed by Wei et al.,[6] who highlighted factors influencing postsurgical outcomes. In current research, the substantial reduction in AHI and ESS scores suggests that precise surgical planning and adequate advancement of skeletal structures are crucial for optimizing outcomes. However, variability in individual anatomy underscores the need for tailored approaches, as suggested by Zhou et al.[7]

In comparison with other interventions like CPAP and oral appliances, orthognathic surgery provides a permanent solution for selected patients. Lai et al.[5] demonstrated that mandibular advancement devices could be effective in certain cases but are limited by patient compliance and less robust results compared to MMA. Moreover, Quah et al.[4] highlighted that MMA offers superior airway expansion by directly addressing anatomical constraints, supporting its role as a definitive treatment.

Long-term benefits of orthognathic surgery on respiratory function were demonstrated by Ahl et al.,[8] who reported sustained improvements in sleep respiratory parameters. While current research was limited to a 12-month follow-up, the results are consistent with these findings, suggesting durable outcomes. Future research should explore extended follow-up periods and the impact of orthognathic surgery on comorbidities associated with OSA.[910]

The high patient satisfaction and low complication rates observed in this research underscore the safety and acceptability of orthognathic surgery. However, as Bariani et al.[3] and Bucci et al.[9] noted in pediatric studies, early intervention using orthodontic or orthopedic approaches might delay or reduce the need for surgical correction, highlighting the importance of age-appropriate treatment strategies.

CONCLUSION

Orthognathic surgery, particularly maxillomandibular advancement, is an effective and permanent solution for moderate-to-severe OSA, significantly improving airway function, reducing symptoms, and enhancing quality of life. The high patient satisfaction and durable outcomes underscore its value as a definitive treatment modality, particularly in cases where CPAP or other interventions fail. Future studies should focus on long-term outcomes and personalized surgical approaches to optimize treatment efficacy.

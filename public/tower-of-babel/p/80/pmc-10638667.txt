Impact of DaxibotulinumtoxinA for Injection on Brow Position and Frontalis Muscle Activity Following Treatment of Glabellar Lines
Authors: Nowell Solish, Michael A C Kane, Brian S Biesman, Jessica Brown, Conor J Gallagher
Published: 2022

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBotulinum toxin type A (BoNTA) injections for the treatment of facial lines may lead to pleasant or undesirable changes in eyebrow height and position.ObjectivesThe aim of this study was to evaluate the impact of glabellar injection of DaxibotulinumtoxinA for Injection (DAXI), a novel BoNTA formulation, on eyebrow position and frontalis activity.MethodsThis study involved the post hoc analysis of adult patients from the Phase 2a forehead lines (FHL, N = 60) and open-label safety (OLS, N = 175) studies who received a single dose of DAXI 40 U to the glabella and for whom facial photographs were taken at rest and at maximum eyebrow elevation. Median vertical and horizontal displacement of the brows and median forehead strain (an objective quantitative assessment of frontalis activity) from baseline to 2 weeks after glabellar DAXI injection were measured.ResultsTwo weeks after glabellar DAXI injection, vertical eyebrow movement (at rest) of the lateral brow was observed. In both studies, vertical movement was greatest in the lateral (0.6-0.9 mm) and mid (0.5-0.7 mm) brow regions; movement in the medial brow was negligible (0-0.23 mm). In both studies, a graded reduction in forehead strain was observed 2 weeks post glabellar DAXI injection, with the greatest reduction being in the lower segment (FHL, –73%; OLS, –82%). Treatment with DAXI showed improvements in FHL wrinkle severity.ConclusionsGlabellar injection of DAXI showed a positive reduction in dynamic frontalis activity that maintained or had a positive effect on eyebrow position. Vertical movement of the brow was suggestive of an improved eyebrow shape with a mild lateral arch.Level of Evidence: 4

The eyebrows are a critical component of nonverbal communication and can convey a variety of emotions based on their position and shape.1 The position of the brows is determined by the opposing effects of the frontalis muscle and the muscles of the glabellar complex (ie, corrugator supercilii, depressor supercilii, procerus, and orbicularis oculi), which interdigitate at the medial region of the brow.1,2 Injection of botulinum toxin type A (BoNTA) into the glabella or frontalis, which is commonly used to treat upper facial lines, can weaken the muscles that are responsible for brow height and position,3,4 and, depending on the injection technique, may lead to pleasing or undesirable outcomes.5 Positive effects of BoNTA include correction of brow asymmetry,5 improvement of brow shape,5 and raising or reshaping of brows that have changed as a result of age-related skeletal changes,6,7 fat redistribution,6 or decreased elasticity of the skin at the forehead.7 Undesirable outcomes of BoNTA include brow depression or flattening, either as a whole or in the medial and lateral regions; exacerbated asymmetry; and lack of brow movement resulting in an inability to make facial expressions, all of which can result in an unnatural appearance.8 In addition, use of BoNTA may unmask or exacerbate underlying eyelid or eyebrow ptosis,9 particularly in older individuals.

Multiple studies have demonstrated that injection of BoNTA into the glabella results in elevation of the brows.10-13 This is typically seen as a positive effect because brow elevation has been considered by cosmetic surgeons and dermatologists to be more important than complete eradication of facial lines.3 However, aesthetically pleasing brow elevation is not always realized in clinical practice. For example, if the frontalis directly above the brows is immobilized, it can drop the brow and lead to eyebrow ptosis,1,7 which can project a tired and sad appearance.14 Alternatively, selective weakening of the medial frontalis, but not the lateral frontalis, can lead to a drop in the medial brow and elevation of the lateral brow,15 which can create an unnatural, surprised, or quizzical (sometimes termed “Mephisto”) look.16 These effects are primarily determined by the frontalis, which plays a critical role in lifting and maintaining brow position.1 Indeed, partial inactivation of the medial frontalis after glabellar injection has been shown to lead to an elevation in the lateral brow.17 However, the degree to which treatment of the glabellar complex influences the proximate frontalis muscle is not well understood. Given that glabellar treatment can lead to a depression of the medial brow, it is clear that the frontalis can be inadvertently affected by injections intended for the glabellar complex.

DaxibotulinumtoxinA for Injection (DAXI; Revance Therapeutics, Inc., Nashville, TN) is a novel BoNTA formulation that has been studied in more than 2800 adults with moderate or severe glabellar lines in the Phase 3 SAKURA clinical program.18-21 Results from this program, which included 2 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, Phase 3 trials18,19 and an open-label safety (OLS) study,20,21 demonstrated that DAXI led to durable responses and was well tolerated. DAXI has also been evaluated for the reduction of forehead lines (FHL) following glabellar and frontalis injections in adults with moderate or severe FHL and glabellar lines in a Phase 2a study.22,23 In the current post hoc analysis, we used an innovative method to evaluate the impact of glabellar injection of DAXI on eyebrow position and dynamic frontalis activity among patients who participated in the Phase 2a FHL and Phase 3 OLS studies.

METHODS

Subjects

Subjects included in this post hoc analysis were adult patients from the multicenter, open-label, dose-escalation Phase 2a FHL study (January 2019 to March 2020)22,23 and the SAKURA OLS study (December 2016 to October 2018)20,21 who received a single dose of DAXI 40 U to the glabellar complex, and for whom baseline and posttreatment photographs were taken at rest and at maximum eyebrow elevation. The protocols for the studies were approved by the relevant IRB (Advarra, formerly Quorum Review IRB) and independent ethics committees, and all subjects provided written informed consent.

Patients enrolled in the study were required to have moderate or severe FHL during maximum contraction (per the Investigator Global Assessment–Forehead Wrinkle Severity [IGA-FHWS] and Patient Forehead Wrinkle Severity scales) and moderate or severe glabellar lines (per the Investigator Global Assessment–Frown Wrinkle Severity [IGA-FWS] and Patient Frown Wrinkle Severity scales). Patients enrolled in the OLS study were required to have moderate or severe glabellar lines at maximum frown per the IGA-FWS and Patient Frown Wrinkle Severity scales. The key exclusion criteria for both studies were pregnant or nursing females, any neurologic condition that may have placed a patient at increased risk with exposure to BoNTA, muscle weakness or paralysis in the area receiving treatment, a history of facial nerve palsy, and significant pre-existing or current eyelid or eyebrow ptosis. Specific to this study, patients were excluded if they had BoNTA treatment ≤6 months before screening and, specific to the OLS study, had facial treatment with BoNTA ≤6 months before screening, planned to receive BoNTA anywhere on the face during the trial, or were treated with ≥200 U of BoNTA anywhere else on the body within 3 months of screening or during the trial.

Subjects in the study could receive up to 2 treatments (1 glabellar treatment and 1 FHL treatment); the FHL treatment was administered 2 weeks after the glabellar treatment. The glabellar treatment, which is the focus of this analysis, comprised 5 injections (2 injections per corrugator muscle, 1 into the procerus muscle) and was conducted in accordance with a prescribed injection pattern; all injections were administered by trained physicians.

Image Analysis

Quantitative analyses were performed by Canfield Scientific, Inc. (Parsippany, NJ) on photographs of subjects taken at baseline and 2 weeks after injection of DAXI into the glabella (before FHL treatment for subjects in the FHL study). Photographs were taken at rest and at maximum eyebrow elevation for all subjects in the study and for a subset of subjects in the OLS study.

Eyebrow Position

Brow position analyses were conducted on at-rest photographs at baseline and Week 2. Three areas of interest were identified for each subject. First, anatomic landmarks for each eye were marked at the outer canthus (A1), inner canthus (A2), lateral edge of the eyebrow (B1), and medial edge of the eyebrow (B4) on baseline images (Figure 1A). Lines were then drawn connecting A1 and B1 (A1-B1) and A2 and B4 (A2-B4); 2 additional lines were added at one-third and two-thirds of the angle between A1-B1 and A2-B4. Three additional landmarks (C1-C3) were placed 1 cm above the upper eyebrow to account for natural eyebrow variation (C1, along the one-third line; C2, along the two-thirds line; and C3, along the A2-B4 line). The lateral brow area of interest was defined as the area bordered by B1, C1, and B2 (the point above the brow on the one-third line); the mid brow was defined as the area bordered by B2, C1, C2, and B3 (the point above the brow along the two-thirds line); and the medial brow was defined as the area bordered by B3, C2, C3, and B4. Finally, the baseline area of interest in at-rest images was transposed onto the Week 2 at-rest images by means of markerless tracking technology. The median displacement of each point was measured in both the x and y axes within the delineated area of interest.

(A) Brow and (B) frontalis areas of interest for image analyses.

Dynamic Frontalis Activity

Dynamic frontalis activity was assessed objectively by a quantitative assessment of dynamic forehead strain (Canfield Scientific, Inc.) on at-rest and at-maximum-lift photographs taken at baseline and at Week 2. With this technique, the movement of multiple points on the surface of the skin within an area of interest was tracked and compared from rest to maximum lift. To create the forehead area of interest, anatomic landmarks were first marked at the pupil (P) and inner canthus (D) on each baseline image (Figure 1B). Lines were then vertically drawn from P (mid-pupillary line) and D (inner canthus line), and the points above the brow (E1-E4) and below the hairline (F1-F4) along the mid-pupillary line and inner canthus line of both eyes were identified. The forehead area of interest was then defined by the area bordered by points E1 to E4 and F1 to F4 and divided into 4 equal segments: upper, upper middle, lower middle, and lower. The at-rest area of interest images taken at baseline were then transposed onto the at-maximum-lift images taken at baseline and at Week 2 and onto the at-rest images taken at Week 2. Median dynamic forehead strain was measured as the relative compression or stretch motion of the skin within an area of interest at baseline compared with the same area of interest at Week 2 and was expressed as the percentage difference in strain between visits for each area of interest.

Other Endpoints

The visual appearance of static and dynamic FHLs was assessed by investigators on a 4-point scale (0 = none to 4 = severe) based on the IGA-FHWS.19

RESULTS

Subjects

Sixty subjects with 119 evaluable eyebrows from the FHL study (4 sites) and 175 subjects with 350 evaluable eyebrows from the OLS study (10 sites) were included in this analysis. Subject demographic and baseline characteristics were similar between the 2 studies (Table 1). In the FHL and OLS studies, subjects were mostly white (96.7% and 94.1%, respectively), female (88.3% and 90.9%, respectively), and approximately 50 years of age (mean, 47.7 years; range, 26-62 years and mean, 49.5 years; range, 21-76 years, respectively).

Eyebrow Position

Two weeks after injection of DAXI into the glabella, median displacement values indicated that the brows had moved cranially and laterally (Figure 2). In both studies, median vertical eyebrow movement at rest was observed. In the FHL study, the greatest median changes from baseline were observed in the lateral region of the eyebrow (elevation of approximately 0.6 mm) followed by the mid brow (elevation of approximately 0.5 mm); vertical movement in the medial brow was the lowest (0.23 mm). Similarly, in the OLS study, the median vertical changes from baseline were greatest in the lateral brow (elevation of approximately 0.9 mm), followed by the mid brow (elevation of 0.6-0.7 mm); no movement was observed in the medial brow. In both studies, the eyebrows moved toward the lateral canthus in all areas of interest. Median lateral movement was greatest in the medial (0.6-0.9 mm) and mid brow (0.6-0.9 mm) regions and smallest in the lateral brow (0.4-0.5 mm) region.

Dynamic Frontalis Activity

A graded reduction in forehead strain was observed 2 weeks following DAXI injection to the glabella (Figure 3). In both the FHL and OLS studies, the greatest reduction was observed in the lower segment (73% and 82%, respectively) and the smallest reduction was in the upper segment (15% and 16%, respectively).

Median reduction in forehead strain 2 weeks following glabellar injection of DaxibotulinumtoxinA for Injection in the (A) FHL and (B) open-label safety studies (before FHL treatment for subjects in the FHL study). Images for a 52-year-old female are shown (A, B). FHL, forehead lines.

The effect of DAXI injection to the glabella on FHL was also assessed for subjects in the FHL study in terms of static and dynamic IGA-FHWS scores. For static IGA-FHWS scores, of the 60 subjects analyzed, 3 (5.0%), 33 (55.0%), 22 (36.7%), and 2 (3.3%) had none, mild, moderate, and severe wrinkle severity scores at baseline, respectively. Two weeks following DAXI injection to the glabella, 25 of the 57 (44%) subjects with static FHL of at least mild severity at baseline experienced an improvement in static IGA-FHWS scores. Of the 33 subjects with mild IGA-FHWS scores at baseline, 12 (36.4%) improved to none; of the 22 subjects with moderate IGA-FHWS scores at baseline, 10 (45.5%) improved to mild and 1 (4.5%) improved to none; and of the 2 subjects with severe IGA-FHWS scores at baseline, both improved (1 to moderate and 1 to mild) (Figure 4). For dynamic IGA-FHWS scores, of the 60 subjects analyzed, 20 (33.3%) had moderate scores at baseline and 40 (66.7%) had severe scores. Two weeks following DAXI injection to the glabella, 1 (5%) of the 20 subjects with moderate scores at baseline improved to none and 12 (60%) improved to mild. Of the 40 subjects with severe IGA-FHWS scores at baseline, 2 (5%) improved to none, 4 (10%) improved to mild, and 22 (55%) improved to moderate (Figure 4).

DISCUSSION

Our results demonstrate that glabellar injection of DAXI maintains or positively impacts eyebrow position and at the same time produces a positive reduction in dynamic activity in the lower frontalis. Objectively, the changes observed 2 weeks following treatment with DAXI indicated an elevation in eyebrow position among all the areas of interest evaluated. The lateral eyebrow was slightly more elevated than the medial brow, which was suggestive of an improved eyebrow shape with a mild lateral arch. In addition, there was a graded reduction in dynamic forehead strain, with the greatest changes observed in the lower segment of the forehead and improvements in static and dynamic FHL wrinkle severity (IGA-FHWS).

Because the skin of the brow has no bony attachment, the eyebrows are in suspension, with their position determined by the balance of cranially and caudally oriented vectors of the frontalis muscle and the depressor complex (ie, procerus, depressor supercilii, orbicularis oculi, and corrugator supercilii).1,2 Glabellar injection with BoNTA can produce an elevation of the eyebrows as a result of weakening of the depressor complex, which allows resting frontalis tone to lift the brows unhindered. However, the location of the glabellar injection points is critical because of the potential for the medial frontalis to be affected by BoNTA injection in this region, and ultimately, to affect the interplay between the brow depressors and frontalis, which determine brow position.2 Considering this, it is interesting that DAXI treatment results in both an elevation of the eyebrow position at rest and, at the same time, a reduction of frontalis activity―seemingly opposite effects. The explanation for this finding is unclear but it is perhaps conceivable that glabellar injection of DAXI immobilizes the depressor complex in such a way that the frontalis requires less effort to elevate the eyebrows, lowering the strain on the frontalis and reducing dynamic line formation. Although it is possible that DAXI may have diffused into the frontalis in some patients, the decrease in frontalis activity did not, on average, negatively affect brow position, which might be expected if the lower frontalis was significantly weakened. This is supported by the low rates of brow or eyelid ptosis observed in the FHL (0% and 1.6%, respectively)23 and SAKURA OLS (0.4% and 1.3%, respectively)21 studies. In addition, the components of the DAXI formulation are thought to facilitate the localization of DAXI to nerve terminals and reduce diffusion from the injection site. This is supported by a preclinical study comparing the diffusion of DAXI and onabotulinumtoxinA in mice, which showed that a 2.5-fold higher dose of DAXI was required to achieve comparable diffusion.24

In addition to elevation of the brow, our results indicated that the shape of the brow improved following DAXI treatment. In this analysis, the lateral brow showed slightly greater elevation than the medial brow, resulting in a mild lateral arch, which can be perceived as an improved or attractive brow shape.25-28 Although excessive elevation of the lateral brow with respect to the medial brow can be aesthetically suboptimal, regardless of age,8 a mild lateral elevation of the lateral brow is associated with younger perceived age among women.27 Several studies have shown that the brows of younger individuals are higher than those of older individuals25,29 and that younger individuals have a significantly higher lateral brow compared with the mid brow30 and have brows with a greater dynamic range.25,29 Nevertheless, in clinical practice, clinicians should be mindful that the concept of an ideal female brow shape changes over time, can differ between clinicians and the general public, and is subject to individual interpretation.26-28

It should be noted that although the impact of DAXI on brow position and the frontalis are promising, proper injection technique and consideration of individual patient anatomy are critical for achieving positive outcomes. Individual differences in patient anatomy, including differences in the shape and size of the frontalis, are important considerations when treating glabella lines with BoNTA.2,31 However, an understanding of the contribution of injection technique to overall outcomes is essential to achieving optimal results with BoNTA treatment.3,32

The main limitation of this post hoc analysis is that the outcomes of brow position and frontalis activity were not the primary focus of each of the studies. As such, the impact of DAXI on eyebrow position was only evaluated at 1 time point following a single treatment. Moreover, although DAXI treatment was delivered via a fixed dose and prescribed 5-point injection pattern that was consistent with the patterns used in other neuromodulator glabellar line trials and approved package inserts, minor differences in injection technique and individual patterns of glabellar contraction among patients were not taken into account. However, we believe that the large number of included sites (175 patients across 10 sites from one study and 60 patients from 4 sites in the other) mitigated an overall or systematic effect of injection technique on the study result findings. Despite this, we did observe subtle differences in how each investigator applied the proscribed injection pattern in terms of injection depth and placement, which led us to explore the effect of these differences in injection technique on brow position.33 In this companion study we integrated the observed effects of the different injection techniques with the current understanding of underlying facial anatomy and propose an anatomically based injection technique that may help to minimize the potential negative impact on brow position following glabellar treatment. Other limitations of this study are that because the populations of the FHL and OLS studies were predominantly white and female, the findings may not be widely generalizable to men or races that have different facial anatomies from those in this study.34 In addition, because of differences in formulations and clinical characteristics of the various BoNTA products, these results apply to DAXI only and cannot be extrapolated to other BoNTA products

Because this study was only focused on a single time point, we did not include any safety data in this analysis. However, the safety of DAXI in aesthetic indications has been reported extensively.18,35 Most of the patients in this post hoc analysis were from the open-label, Phase 3 safety study35 where 2,380 subjects received a first dose of DAXI, 882 received a second dose, and 568 received a third dose, for a total of 3,830 treatments. Findings from this study showed that treatment-related adverse events were generally mild and resolved, there were no serious treatment-related events, and eyelid ptosis was reported in only 0.9% of treatments.

CONCLUSIONS

Glabellar injection of DAXI in patients with moderate or severe glabellar lines showed a positive effect on the frontalis, without negatively impacting eyebrow position. Overall, the eyebrows were elevated as demonstrated by median positive changes in vertical eyebrow displacement, and lateral displacement was as expected. The lateral eyebrow was slightly more elevated than the medial brow, which was suggestive of an improved brow shape.

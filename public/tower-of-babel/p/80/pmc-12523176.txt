Lactylation-induced ALKBH5 targets RNF123 to worsen retinal Müller cell activation through PKM2-regulated Glycolysis in diabetic retinopathy
Authors: Shasha Luo, Ye Sun, Yifan Hu, Yujia Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundUbiquitin ligase RNF123 affects a variety of pathological processes, but little is known about its regulatory mechanism. This study defined the regulatory mechanisms of RNF123 in Müller cell activation in diabetic retinopathy (DR).MethodsRNF123 expression was analyzed in the retina of DR patients and diabetic mice. Müller cell line MIO-M1 treated with high glucose (HG) was used to explore the upstream and downstream signal regulation mechanism of RNF123 in vitro. Müller cell activation and glycolysis were indicated using cell viability, cell migration, levels of inflammatory factors, GFAP and GS expression levels, extracellular acidification rate and lactate production. Diabetic rat models induced by streptozotocin were used to investigate the critical role of the RNF123-glycolytic axis in DR in vivo.ResultsRNF123 expression was decreased in the retina of DR patients, diabetic mice and HG-stimulated Müller cells. HG-induced pathological activation of MIO-M1 cells, manifested as increased viability, migration, inflammatory factors production and GFAP expression and decreased GS expression, and also increased glycolysis. These pathological changes were reversed by RNF123 overexpression. Glycolysis inhibition by 2-DG reversed RNF123 knockdown-induced Müller cell activation. RNF123 targeting ubiquitination of PKM2 in HG-induced MIO-M1 cell activation. HG-induced downregulation of RNF123 was regulated by ALKBH5 and IGF2BP1-mediated m6A modification. HG-induced upregulation of ALKBH5 was mediated by histone lactylation. The lactate-ALKBH5-RNF123-PKM2 regulatory axis was involved in HG-induced Müller cell activation and retinal damage of the diabetic rats.ConclusionsLactate increases glycolysis through the ALKBH5-RNF123-PKM2 signaling axis to form a feedback regulation mechanism to activate Müller cells, which is an important pathological mechanism of DR.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12967-025-07129-3.Keywords: Diabetic retinopathy, Müller cells, Glycolysis, Histone lactylation, Ubiquitination, RNF123, N6-Methyladenosine

Background

Diabetic retinopathy (DR) is a common neurovascular complication of diabetes, which changes the normal interaction between retinal cells and leads to dysfunction of glial cells and neurons and vascular abnormalities, leading to vision loss [1]. Recent comprehensive systematic reviews have shown that 30–40% of diabetic individuals globally develop DR, with prevalence rising substantially in patients aged over 60 years [2, 3]. The improvement in visual outcomes in DR has been largely attributed to better systemic risk factor control and advances in treatment [1]. The elucidation of the mechanism of pathological progression of DR has a profound significance for the management and treatment of DR. Müller cells, a major type of retinal glial cells, extend across the full thickness of the neural retina. The high glucose (HG) pathological environment of diabetes induces dysfunction of Müller cells, which exhibit a reactive phenotype (also known as Müller glia activation), characterized by up-regulation of glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP), increased secretion of pro-inflammatory mediators (IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α), down-regulated glutamine synthetase (GS), and enhanced proliferation ability to reduce tissue damage before clinical manifestations of DR [4]. However, excessive activation of Müller cells contributes to DR pathogenesis by inducing various pathological alterations [5]. However, the mechanism by which HG induces activation of Müller glia remains unclear.

As a major protein degradation pathway, the ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS) helps maintain cellular balance by regulating the breakdown of essential proteins [6]. As the primary mediators of substrate recognition in ubiquitination, ubiquitin ligases (E3s) demonstrate extensive structural heterogeneity, with more than 600 variants encoded in the human genome [7]. Understanding the biological functions of E3 ligases and the regulation of specific pathways is very important for DR [8, 9]. Ubiquitin ligase RNF123 (also called KPC1) is the newly discovered ubiquitin E3-ligase and was recognized in a variety of pathological processes by specifically binding to substrate proteins to promote their ubiquitination [10, 11]. A study on breast cancer cells showed that RNF123 inhibited glycolysis through PFKP that catalyzes an important step in the glycolysis pathway, thereby affecting the biological behavior of breast cancer cells [12]. RNF123 interacts with Dvl2 and participats in CCNG2-induced polyubiquitination degradation of Dvl2 to modulate trophoblastic cell migration, invasion, thereby affecting the pathogenesis and progression of preeclampsia [13]. No studies to date, however, have specifically explored the role that RNF123 plays in the context of diabetes or associated conditions. Bioinformatics approaches identified the downregulation of RNF123 expression in the retina of patients with DR and diabetic mice, suggesting that RNF123 may play a role in DR.

The m6A modification, characterized by adenosine methylation in RNA, serves as a pivotal regulator of eukaryotic gene expression. Its dynamic and reversible nature is governed by methyltransferases, demethylases, and RNA-binding proteins that decode m6A signals to modulate mRNA processing, transport, translation, and degradation [14]. m6A affects the progression of a variety of diseases, including diabetes, and also affects biological processes such as oxidative stress, inflammatory response, and metabolic changes related to the pathological mechanism of DR [15]. m6A regulates the pathway in Müller cells under HG stress [16]. Recent studies have also found that methylation-binding protein (YTHDF2) and methyltransferase (METTL3/YTHDC1) mediated RNA stability participates in the process of Müller cell pathological activation induced by HG [17, 18]. These studies reveal that m6A modification-mediated post-transcriptional modification plays a significant role in regulating the activation of retinal Müller cells in the pathogenesis of DR progression.

In this investigation, we elucidated the mechanism of action of RNF123 in Müller glial cell activation in the DR. By analyzing the retina of diabetic mice and Müller cell line MIO-M1 treated with HG, we confirmed the role of the ALKBH5-RNF123-PKM2 axis in HG-induced Müller cell activation and retinal injury.

Methods

Bioinformatics analysis

RNF123 mRNA expression changes in the retina of patients with DR and diabetic mice were analyzed according to microarray datasets from GEO databases (GSE60436, GSE55389).

Cell culture and treatments

Human retinal Müller cell line MIO-M1, retinal pigment epithelial cell line-19 (ARPE-19) and human retinal microvascular endothelial cells (HRMECs) were purchased from Ybio (Shanghai, China) and cultured under standard conditions (37 °C, Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 5 mg/mL streptomycin, 5 U/mL penicillin, and normal 5 mM normal glucose (NG). For high glucose (HG) stimulation, MIO-M1 cells were cultured in the complete medium added with 25 mM HG as previously described [19]. For 2-DG or LDHA inhibitor FX-11 treatment, MIO-M1 cells were cultured in the complete medium added with 2-DG (10 mM; MedChemExpress, HY-13966) or FX-11 (10 µM; MedChemExpress, HY-16214). Cells were harvested at 48 h post-treatment.

Cell transfection

Scramble shRNA (shNC) or shRNAs targeting RNF123 or ALKBH5 were synthesized and cloned into pLKO.1 lentiviral vectors. For overexpression vector constructs, full-length RNF123, PKM2, or IGF2BP1 were inserted into pLVX-Puro lentiviral vectors. Viral packaging was achieved by co-transfecting 293T cells with the respective constructed lentiviral vectors along with psPAX2 and pMD2G packaging plasmids using Lipofectamine 2000. Viral supernatants were harvested at 48 h post-transfection and used to transduce MIO-M1 cells. Cells transduced with pLKO.1-shNC lentiviral vector or blank pLVX-Puro lentiviral vector were used as negative controls. shRNA sequences are available in Table S1.

HA-Ub-K48 plasmid, Flag-tagged RNF123 plasmid, Myc-tagged PKM2 plasmid, full-length and mutant PKM2 or H3K18 plasmids were provided by GenePharma (Shanghai, China) and transfected MIO-M1 cells with Lipofectamine 2000.

Cell viability assay by cell counting Kit-8 (CCK-8)

MIO-M1 cells were inoculated in a 96-well culture plate at a density of 1 × 10⁴ cells/well. The cells were treated, and then 10 µL of CCK-8 was added per well before a 2-hour incubation at 37 °C. The optical density (OD) at 450 nm was recorded at 450 nm using a microplate reader to quantify viable cells.

Cell migration assay

A suspension of MIO-M1 cells (2 × 10⁵ cells/well) was added to a 6-well plate and incubated for adherence. Following transduction, cells were rinsed twice with PBS to eliminate non-adherent cells, and then exposed to NG, HG, or 2-DG for 24–48 h. The migrated cells were observed and photographed using inverted phase contrast microscopy with ≥ 3 random fields captured per condition. Migration gaps were quantified by analyzing the images with ImageJ to measure intercellular distances.

Extracellular acidification rate (ECAR) examination

Cellular glycolytic activity was evaluated using the Seahorse XF24 Analyzer. Briefly, cells were seeded in 24-well Seahorse plates at 10,000 cells per well. Prior to analysis, cells were washed and equilibrated in 500 µL of XF assay medium. For glycolytic flux assessment, cells were treated with three metabolic modulators in sequence: glucose (1 µM), oligomycin (1 µM), and 2-DG (0.5 µM).

Lactate contents

Extracellular lactate concentrations in cell culture supernatants and retinal tissue homogenates were detected using a Lactic Acid assay kit (BC2230; Solarbio, Beijing, China) following the manufacturer’s protocol.

ELISA

The IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α released to cell supernatants or contained in retinal tissues were detected using corresponding ELISA kits (Multi Sciences) according to the manufacturer’s protocols.

qRT-PCR

RNA isolation from MIO-M1 cells was performed using Trizol reagent. qRT‒PCR assays were performed with Green qPCR SuperMix (TransGen Biotech) on an ABI 7300 system (Applied Biosystems). The 2− ΔΔCt approach was employed to determine relative gene expression levels, using ACTB as the internal reference gene. The primers used for qRT‒PCR are available in Table S2.

Western blot assay

Cellular proteins were solubilized using RIPA lysis buffer supplemented with protease inhibitors. Equal amounts of protein (20–40 µg) were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride membranes. The membrane was incubated with antibodies against RNF123 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, PA5-103820), GS (Proteintech, 11037-2-AP), GFAP (Proteintech, 16825-1-AP), PKM2 (Cell Signaling Technology, #3198), ALKBH5 (Cell Signaling Technology, #49015), FTO (Cell Signaling Technology, #45980), IGF2BP1 (Proteintech, 22803-1-AP), H3K18la (PTM Bio, PTM-1406RM), H3 (Abcam, ab1791), and β-actin (66009-1-Ig; Proteintech). Protein-antibody complexes were visualized using HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies and detected with ECL substrate on a Bio-Rad ChemiDoc imaging system (USA). Protein expression levels were normalized to the expression of β-actin or H3.

Tandem affinity purification

Tandem affinity purification was performed as previously described [20]. Stable MIO-M1 cell lines expressing either blank vector or Flag-RNF123 constructs were harvested and the lysates were subjected to affinity purification using anti-Flag beads with overnight rotation at 4 °C. Separation of protein samples was achieved via SDS-PAGE, followed by nanoLC-MS/MS-based proteomic analysis performed by Shanghai Biotree Biomedical Technology.

Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) and ubiquitination assay

Cellular proteins were solubilized in IP lysis buffer supplemented with protease inhibitors. For each IP reaction, 500 µg of total protein was incubated with either anti-RNF123 antibody (Santa Cruz, sc-101122) or anti-PKM2 antibody (CST, #4053) at 4 °C for 2 h with gentle rotation, followed by addition of Protein A/G agarose beads (Santa Cruz, sc-2003) for an additional 1 h. Immunoprecipitates were resolved by SDS-PAGE with antibodies against RNF123 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, PA5-103820), PKM2 (Cell Signaling Technology, #3198), ubiquitin (Ub; Abcam, ab134953), Myc (Proteintech, 60003-2-Ig), or HA (Medical & Biological Laboratories Co., Ltd, M180-11).

Protein stability assay

Cellular samples were harvested at designated time points (0, 1, 4, and 8 h) following 100 µg/mL cycloheximide (CHX; Selleck Chemicals) exposure. Total cellular proteins were extracted, and PKM2 protein levels were quantified by immunoblotting.

m6A content analysis

The content of m6A in total RNA was analyzed with the m6A RNA Methylation Assay Kit (Abcam, ab185912) according to the manufacturer’s protocol.

m6A methylated RNA Immunoprecipitation quantitative PCR (MeRIP-qPCR)

Total RNA was isolated and quantified using spectrophotometry. A total of 100 µg RNA was fragmented by sonication in an IP buffer containing RNase inhibitors. For immunoprecipitation, RNA fragments were mixed with protein A/G magnetic beads that had been pre-conjugated with anti-m6A or control IgG antibodies at 4°C overnight. Bead-bound RNA was eluted using N6-methyladenosine 5’-monophosphate (10 mM) and analyzed by qRT-PCR to quantify m6A-modified RNF123 transcripts. The primers used for qRT-PCR are listed as follows, RNF123-F: 5’-CCCATCTCTGCTGTGTTC-3’ and RNF123-R: 5’-CTCGTATTGGCTCCCTTC-3’.

Luciferase reporter assays

The coding sequence (CDS) of RNF123 (nucleotides 3891–4031) was PCR-amplified and subsequently ligated into the pGL3-basic luciferase reporter vector (Promega). Plasmid transfection was conducted using Lipofectamine 2000 reagent (Invitrogen) following the protocols from the manufacturer. Following 48-hour incubation, luminescence was quantified using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System with normalization to Renilla luciferase activity.

RNA stability

RNF123 transcript stability was assessed through actinomycin D-mediated transcriptional arrest. Cells that were treated with NG or HG and IGF2BP1 overexpression vector were treated with 5 µg/ml actinomycin D (Sigma-Aldrich, A9415) for specified durations (0, 3, and 6 h). The expression level of RNF123 mRNA was quantified by qRT-PCR.

RNA Immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay

The binding of IGF2BP1 to RNF123 mRNA was examined through RIP assay with EZ-Magna RIP™ RNA-Binding Protein Immunoprecipitation Kit (Millipore, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Immunoprecipitation was performed using magnetic beads coated with anti-IGF2BP1 antibody, with IgG-coated beads serving as negative controls. Following stringent washing, co-precipitated RNAs were extracted and examined by qRT-PCR.

Chromatin Immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assays

ChIP analysis was performed as previously described [21]. Immunoprecipitations were carried out using either specific anti-H3K18la antibody (PTM Bio, Cat# PTM-1427RM) or isotype control IgG (Proteintech, Cat# 30000-0-AP) at 4 °C overnight. Following extensive washing and crosslink reversal, the enriched DNA fragments were analyzed by quantitative PCR with all data normalized to corresponding input samples.

Animal model

Male sprague-Dawley rats were acquired from Jinan Peng Yue Laboratory Animal Breeding Co. (China) and subsequently developed diabetes following a single intraperitoneal STZ injection (65 mg/kg) with subsequent monitoring for 20 weeks. The rats with successful modeling were randomly divided into the model group, FX-11 (1 mg/kg, intraperitoneally, once every 4 weeks) group, shRNF123 vector (0.06 ng/eye, intravitreal injection, once every 4 weeks) group, and shRNF123 + FX-11 group (n = 6 per group). After 20 weeks post-treatment, the retinal tissues were collected for Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining, immunofluorescence staining, Western blot and ELISA. All animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the guidelines and regulations approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) at Wuxi No.2 People’s Hospital (Approval number 2022-Y111).

Statistical analyses

Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). The Shapiro-Wilk normality test was employed to assess the normality of the continuous data distribution. Statistical evaluation was carried out with GraphPad Prism 8.4.2 software. Data followed a normal distribution were analyzed by Student’s t-test or one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test. Statistical significance was established at the conventional threshold of p < 0.05.

Results

RNF123 overexpression inhibits HG-induced MIO-M1 cell viability, migration, glycolysis, and inflammation

Bioinformatics analysis revealed a significant downregulation of RNF123 mRNA levels in the retina of DR patients (Fig. S1A) and of diabetic mice (Fig. S1B). We further detected RNF123 expression in MIO-M1 with ARPE-19 cells and HRMECs as the controls. MIO-M1 cells exhibited increased expression of RNF123 (Figs. S1C, S1D). In response to HG stimulation, RNF123 expression in MIO-M1 cells showed a reduction after 24 h of treatment (Fig. 1A and B, S2A). However, HG did not affect the expression of RNF123 in ARPE-19 cells or HRMECs (Figs. S1E-S1H). Therefore, RNF123 was overexpressed through MIO-M1 cells transduced with RNF123 overexpression vector (Figures S3A, S3B) to explore the role of RNF123 in HG-conditioned MIO-M1 cells. The results revealed that HG stimulation led to increased cell viability, enhanced cell migration, elevated glycolysis markers (ECAR and lactate production), and elevated levels of pro-inflammatory factors (IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α). However, these HG-induced changes were counteracted by RNF123 overexpression (Fig. 1C and J). In addition, GS expression was markedly suppressed, whereas GFAP levels showed substantial elevation following HG exposure. However, these expression trends of both proteins were reversed by RNF123 overexpression (Figs. 1K, S2B). These findings indicate that NRF123 plays a role in regulating HG-triggered Müller cell activation and associated glycolysis metabolic disturbances.

RNF123 knockdown results in MIO-M1 cell activation, migration, and inflammation via Glycolysis

The decreased RNF123 expression in MIO-M1 cells transduced with RNF123 shRNA-1, -2 and − 3 vectors (Figs. S3C, S3D) represent the successful RNF123 knockdown. The RNF123 shRNA-1 with the best gene knockdown efficiency was selected for subsequent experiments. The results showed that RNF123 knockdown increased cell viability and this effect was reversed by 2-DG (Fig. 2A). Likewise, RNF123 knockdown induced the increase of migration, glycolysis markers, and the level of pro-inflammatory factors were partially counteracted by 2-DG (Fig. 2B and H). The decreased expressed GS and increased expressed GFAP caused by RNF123 knockdown were partially reversed by 2-DG (Figs. 2I, S2C). These findings suggest that glycolysis mediates the RNF123 knockdown-induced activation of Müller cells.

RNF123 interacts with PKM2 and induces ubiquitination of PKM2

Cells transduced by Flag-RNF123 and lysed for the detection of proteins interacted with RNF123 using SDS-PAGE with anti-Flag antibody and silver stain and subsequent nanoLC­MS/MS identification (Fig. 3A). Co-IP followed by western blot assay showed that PKM2 interacted with RNF123 (Fig. 3B). PKM2 mRNA expression level was not changed by RNF123 knockdown or RNF123 overexpression while PKM2 protein expression level was increased by RNF123 knockdown and decreased by RNF123 overexpression (Fig. 3C and D, S4A). Moreover, the treatment of MG132, inhibition of proteasome activity prevents intracellular protein degradation, increased PKM2 protein expression in RNF123-overexpressed MIO-M1 cells (Figs. 3E, S4B) and RNF123 overexpression further gradually reduced PKM2 expression in CHX-treated MIO-M1 cells (Figs. 3F, S4C). These data point to RNF123 binding to PKM2 and reducing its intracellular protein levels.

We further explored the effect of RNF123 on PKM2 ubiquitination. RNF123-overexpressed MIO-M1 cells displayed increased ubiquitination levels of PKM2 (Fig. 3G). To determine which lysine site of PKM2 undergoes K48-linked ubiquitination, MIO-M1 cells were transfected with HA-Ub-K48 together with Myc-labeled PKM2 WT and mutant plasmids. The results revealed that the K48-linked ubiquitination level of PKM2 was significantly reduced when K115 and K151 of PKM2 were mutated (Fig. 3H). Therefore, the K115 and K151 sites are the key sites for the K48-linked ubiquitination of PKM2.

RNF123 overexpression inhibits HG-induced MIO-M1 cell viability, migration, glycolysis, and inflammation by targeting PKM2

To determine the RNF123-PKM2 axis in the function of MIO-M1 cells under HG condition, RNF123 and PKM2 were co-overexpressed before HG treatment. We found that PKM2 increased cell activity, cell migration, glycolytic indicators and levels of inflammatory factors (Fig. 4A and H). Consistent with the above results, RNF123 overexpression protected against HG-induced activation and glycolytic disturbance of MIO-M1 cells, which were partially restored by concurrent PKM2 overexpression (Fig. 4A and H). Meanwhile, the inhibition of GS expression and promotion of GFAP expression caused by RNF123 overexpression were reversed by PKM2 overexpression (Figs. 4I, S4D).

ALKBH5 regulates RNF123 mRNA stability with IGF2BP1 as the reader in an m6 A-dependent manner

HG-induced decreased global m6A levels in MIO-M1 cells (Fig. 5A). SRAMP predicted m6A sites of the RNF123 CDS region (Fig. 5B). HG induced decreased m6A levels of RNF123 CDS region (Fig. 5C), and increased mRNA and protein expression of ALKBH5 but not FTO in MIO-M1 cells (Fig. 5D and E, S5A). ALKBH5 knockdown increased global m6A level (Fig. 5F), and m6A levels and luciferase activity of RNF123 CDS region (Fig. 5G and H), decreased ALKBH5 and increased RNF123 expression (Fig. 5I and J, S5B). ALKBH5-RNF123-PKM2 regulatory axis promotes HG-induced MIO-M1 cell activation, migration, glycolysis, and inflammation

To further examine the role of ALKBH5-mediated m6A modification and stability of RNF123 mRNA in HG-induced MIO-M1 cells, ALKBH5 shRNA vector alone or combined with RNF123 shRNA vector was transfected into MIO-M1 cells under HG. The results showed that ALKBH5 knockdown protected cells from HG-induced cell activation and glycolysis, as indicated by reduced cell viability, cell migration, glycolytic indicators and levels of inflammatory factors and these changes response to ALKBH5 knockdown were reversed by RNF123 knockdown (Fig. 6 A-H). ALKBH5 knockdown-induced increased RNF123, decreased PKM2, increased GS, and decreased GFAP were reversed by RNF123 knockdown (Figs. 6I, S5D). These findings suggest that ALKBH5-mediated decrease in the m6A modification of RNF123 mRNA inhibits RNF123 mRNA stability, thereby promoting HG-induced MIO-M1 activation, migration, glycolysis, and inflammation.

Lactate regulates HG-induced ALKBH5 expression via histone lactylation

HG-induced lactate production and H3K18la enrichment in the ALKBH5 promoter, increased ALKBH5 expression, decreased RNF123 expression, and increased H3K18la protein expression were reversed by FX-11, an inhibitor for lactate dehydrogenase (Fig. 7A and D, S6A). MIO-M1 cells were transfected with WT or H3K18 mutant (H3K18R) plasmid in the condition of normal glucose or HG. H3K18R overexpression did not affect cell viability, cell migration and glycolytic indicators and levels of inflammatory factors under normal glucose but decreased cell viability, cell migration, glycolytic indicators and levels of inflammatory factors under HG (Fig. 7E and L). Expression levels of ALKBH5 and RNF123 were also reduced and increased, respectively, by H3K18R overexpression under HG conditions (Fig. 7M and N, S6B). PKM2 and GFAP proteins were decreased by H3K18R overexpression and GS protein was increased by H3K18R overexpression under HG conditions (Fig. 7N).

Inhibition of lactylation-driven ALKBH5 targeting RNF123 improves the pathological changes of retinal tissue

STZ-induced diabetic rats were treated with FX-11 and injected with shRNF123 vector to determine the RNF123-glycolysis pathway axis in diabetic retinal injury. The results of HE histological staining of the retina revealed that the retinal tissue of the control group rats exhibited a typical histological morphology, with distinct cellular layers and orderly arrangement. Discussion

The pathogenesis of DR is regulated by multiple factors through integrated molecular signaling pathways and cellular processes [22]. Diabetes-induced metabolic pathway abnormalities serve as crucial contributors to Müller glial cell dysfunction, where excessive accumulation of intermediate metabolites, leading to exacerbated retinal inflammation and edema [5]. Our findings reveal that HG stimulation promotes histone lactylation, upregulating ALKBH5 expression. This, in turn, suppresses m6A methylation of mRNA, reduces RNF123 mRNA stability and protein levels, and consequently inhibits RNF123-mediated ubiquitination and degradation of PKM2, ultimately enhancing glycolysis and lactate production in Müller glial cells, as well as the release of inflammatory factors.

Increasing evidence has found that RNF123 plays a role in the pathological mechanism of various diseases and affects various biological processes of cells. RNF123 regulates the cell cycle and NF-κB signaling pathway, thereby inhibiting tumor growth and invasion [23]. RNF123 reduces cancer cell migration and tumorigenicity by targeting vimentin ubiquitination and degradation [24]. RNF123 reduces apoptosis of rat cardiomyocytes by targeting BAX ubiquitination and degradation [25]. RNF123 mediates lamin-binding proteins retinoblastoma protein, ubiquitinated proteasomal degradation of lamina associated polypeptide 2α and lamin B1 contributes to lamin mutation [26]. In our results, we identified the binding of RNF123 to PKM2 and the effect of RNF123 on the ubiquitination degradation of PKM2 in HG-treated Müller cells. As the rate-limiting enzyme in glycolysis, PKM2-mediated glycolysis is an important pathological mechanism of many diseases including diabetic complications [27–29]. Our results reveal the RNF123-PKM2 constitutive signal axis in HG-activated Müller cells.

Müller glial cells are a kind of glial cells that exist between neurons and retinal blood vessels, providing nutritional and regulatory support for neurons. Under HG conditions, Müller glia cells suffer oxidative stress and mitophagia damage, which is an important factor in the progression of DR [30]. HG induces activation of Müller cells and secretion of inflammatory factors [31]. Consistent with this, we found upregulated expression of activation markers, secretion of inflammatory factors and glycolysis in Müller cells cultured with HG. Moreover, inhibition of glycolysis reversed the RNF123 knockdown-induced increases in viability and glycolysis in Müller cells. These data support that increased RNF123-PKM2-glycolysis is an important pathological mechanism for Müller cell activation.

ALKBH5 is an attractive m6A demethylase, which mediates m6A methylation of target genes and is thought to be involved in the pathological process of various diseases including diabetic complications [32–34], and its action is usually mediated by methylation-binding proteins (such as YTHDF1 and IGF2BP1) that recognize m6A modification. Our data showed that HG reduced m6A modification of RNF123 mRNA, and this reduction was mediated by ALKBH5 and IGF2BP1. Moreover, RNF123 expression induced by ALKBH5 knockdown and IGF2BP1 overexpression were significantly increased at both mRNA and protein levels in the HG and NG condition (Fig. S7). These data suggest that ALKBH5-mediated decrease in the post-transcriptional modification of the adenosine N6 site of RNF123 mRNA inhibits RNF123 mRNA stability, thereby decreasing RNF123 protein levels, which consequently inhibiting RNF123-mediated Müller cell activation, glycolysis and inflammation. Bioinformatics approaches also identified the downregulation of USP10 in the retina of patients with DR, which has been reported to bind to, deubiquitinate, and stabilize IGF2BP1 [35]. Deubiquitinases USP9X and USP36 stabilize ALKBH5 to promote tumorigenesis [36, 37]. In the present study, USP10 inhibitor inhibited IGF2BP1 overexpression-mediated increased expression of RNF123 and did not affect ALKBH5 knockdown-mediated expression of RNF123 (Fig. S7), suggesting that USP10 involves in the regulation of RNF123 via IGF2BP1.

Histone lactylation modification is sensitive to the amount of lactic acid produced by glycolysis, which is a major discovery in the exploration of histone epigenetic modification in recent years [38]. We found that lactate promoted ALKBH5 expression through the effect of H3K18 lactylation in HG-stimulated Müller cells. The lactate-mediated H3K18 lactylation and ALKBH5 induction were also occurred in HRMECs (Figs. S8A-S8D). However, the lactate levels, H3K18 lactylation and ALKBH5 induction were not entirely consistent in HG-stimulated ARPE-19 cells (Figs. S8E-S8H). These data suggest the distinction of ALKBH5 induction across retinal cell types. Similarly, ALKBH5 was under-expressed in HG-stimulated ARPE-19 cells and repressed ferroptosis [39]. While other previous studies have reported that DNMT1-mediated DNA hypermethylation was occurred in HG-stimulated ARPE-19 cells [40] and that the decreased expression of ALKHB5 via promoter DNA hypermethylation can increase expression of ATG12, thereby promoting autophagy and preventing hepatic steatosis [41]. Therefore, lactate-mediated histone lactylation and ALKBH5 induction in ARPE-19 cells would be further considered to interpret its epigenetic modification. Additionally, in response to HG stimulation, RNF123 expression showed a reduction after 24 h of treatment in MIO-M1 cells but not in ARPE-19 cells or HRMECs, which indicates that there are alternative regulatory pathways in ARPE-19 cells or HRMECs. Lactate accumulation can activate the nuclear factor κB (NF-κB) signaling pathway, which is reversed by LDHA knockdown or LDHA inhibitor oxamate [42, 43]. Similarly, RNF123 overexpression or LDHA inhibitor FX-11 also inhibits NF-κB signaling pathway induced by HG (Fig. S9). Given the important role of NF-κB signaling pathway in inflammatory response, our data suggest that RNF123 inhibits lactate-mediated activation of NF-κB signaling pathway, thereby suppressing HG-induced inflammatory response. It can be concluded that the positive feedback loop formed by lactate-ALKBH5-RNF123-PKM2-glycolysis is an important mechanism of HG-induced activation, glycolysis, and inflammatory response of Müller cells.

Conclusions

In summary, the present work reveals for the first time the role of the lactate-ALKBH5-RNF123-PKM2 axis in DR pathologic Müller cell activation. The cross-regulation paradigm of “metabolism, epigenetic transcription, and ubiquitination” presented in this study provides new insight into the epigenetic-metabolic crosstalk underlying Müller cell activation in DR.

Development and validation of a model incorporating choroid plexus morphology from brain MRI to predict anti-TNF-α therapy outcomes in Crohn’s disease
Authors: Mengting Huang, Shuo Huang, Qinyue Luo, Yuting Zheng, Hanting Li, Ping Han, Liangru Zhu, Heshui Shi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveCentral nervous system involvement has been implicated in the pathophysiology of Crohn’s disease (CD). This study aimed to develop a predictive model integrating choroid plexus (CP) morphology derived from brain MRI with clinical and psychological factors to forecast treatment outcomes following anti-tumor necrosis factor-alpha (anti-TNF-ɑ) therapy in CD.MethodsThis prospective study enrolled adult patients with CD who underwent brain MRI prior to initiating anti-TNF-ɑ therapy between 2019 and 2022. All participants received brain MRI scans within two weeks of study inclusion and before treatment commencement. The CP was manually segmented from baseline MRI scans, and mesh volume (MV) and surface area (SA) were calculated. Patients were evaluated for short-term (12week) and long-term (≥ 48 week) clinical remission (STCR and LTCR, respectively).ResultsA total of 130 patients were included, of whom 73% received infliximab and 27% received adalimumab. Both MV and SA of the CP correlated with systemic inflammatory markers and psychological symptom scores. Multivariate logistic regression identified hemoglobin level, emotional function score, and SA of CP as independent predictors of STCR, while MV of CP emerged as the sole independent predictor of LTCR. Combined models incorporating these variables demonstrated excellent predictive performance, with areas under the curve of 0.895 for STCR and 0.939 for LTCR.ConclusionThis study identifies CP morphology as a novel, non-invasive neuroimaging biomarker. A model integrating CP characteristics with clinical parameters accurately predicts response to anti-TNF-ɑ therapy in patients with CD.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12876-026-04720-w.

Introduction

Crohn’s disease (CD) is a chronic, relapsing inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) affecting the entire gastrointestinal tract [1]. Abdominal pain and diarrhea represent the predominant gastrointestinal, while anemia, fever and weight loss constitute common systemic symptoms [2]. Although the precise pathogenesis of CD remains incompletely understood, it has been confirmed to involve complex interactions between immune dysregulation, genetic susceptibility, and environmental factors. The global incidence of CD continues to rise, and this increase has been particularly rapid in recent decades [3]. In recent years, novel biologic drugs, anti-tumor necrosis factor-alpha (anti-TNF-ɑ), including infliximab and adalimumab, have emerged as a cornerstone of therapeutic strategies for moderate to severe CD [4]. These agents promote mucosal healing, reduce surgical requirements, and improve quality of life among affected individuals.

However, response to these agents is notably heterogeneous among individuals with CD. Approximately 10% to 20% of patients exhibit primary non-response to anti-TNF-ɑ therapy [5], and secondary loss of response occurs in 23% to 46% of patients within one year of treatment initiation [6, 7]. Moreover, the high cost of long-term biologic therapy imposes a substantial economic burden on patients, and long-term immunosuppression carries potential infectious risks [8]. Therefore, it is necessary to optimize the strategy of anti-TNF therapy.

To date, investigations into biomarkers predictive of anti-TNF-ɑ response have predominantly focused on genomics and proteomics profiles [9, 10], as well as changes in peripheral blood and intestinal mucosa [11]. Despite these efforts, a clinically applicable method that can be reliably implemented in routine practice remains unavailable. A growing body of evidence indicates that CD is associated with central nervous system (CNS) alterations, and the ‘brain-gut axis’ has attracted increasing attention in IBD pathogenesis and treatment [12]. This bidirectional pathway links emotional and cognitive centers of the brain with peripheral intestinal function and inflammatory activity [12]. Psychological factors, including chronic stress, depression, and anxiety, modulate immune responses and have been associated with increased disease activity and diminished quality of life in IBD [13, 14]. Conversely, active intestinal inflammation may signal the CNS and contribute to psychological symptomatology [15].

The choroid plexus (CP), a vascular structure located within the cerebral ventricles, has recently garnered attention in the context of CD. The CP plays an essential role in cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) production and maintenance of CNS homeostasis [16, 17]. Emerging evidence positions the CP as a key neuro-immune interface through which peripheral inflammation may influence the brain. In a murine model of intestinal inflammation, Carloni et al. demonstrated that the CP vascular barrier undergoes dynamic permeability changes correlated with behavioral disturbances, establishing a direct link between gut inflammation and CP function [18]. Meanwhile, TNF-ɑ, a central pro-inflammatory cytokine in CD pathogenesis, has been shown to directly compromise blood-cerebrospinal fluid barrier integrity at the CP. In vitro studies demonstrate that TNF-ɑ exposure upregulates adhesion molecule expression and induces matrix metalloproteinase activity in CP epithelial cells, resulting in tight junction disruption and increased barrier permeability [19]. A recent MRI study by Bonifacio et al. demonstrated that systemic and intestinal inflammatory markers (C-reactive protein and fecal calprotectin) significantly correlate with both CP volume and permeability metrics in patients with CD, providing the first in vivo human evidence that intestinal inflammation is associated with structural and functional CP alterations [20].

Despite extensive research, a reliable and routinely applicable tool for predicting primary response to anti-TNF-α agents in CD prior to treatment initiation remains an unmet clinical need, as most existing biomarkers have not been integrated into standard practice. The present study aimed to identify CP based biomarkers using brain MRI and to develop a predictive model for short-term and long-term clinical remission following initiation of anti-TNF-ɑ therapy in patients with CD.

Materials and methods

Subjects

This prospective cohort study enrolled adult patients with a confirmed diagnosis of CD who initiated anti-TNF-ɑ therapy (infliximab or adalimumab) at the Department of Gastroenterology, Union Hospital, Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology between December 2019 and June 2022. The selection of anti-TNF-ɑ agent was determined by the treating gastroenterologist according to clinical indications. All participants underwent brain MRI within two weeks of enrollment and prior to initiation of medical therapy.

Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) adult patients with CD diagnosed according to the European Crohn’s and Colitis Organization guidelines [21]; and (2) no exposure to anti-TNF-ɑ agents or other biologics therapies with the three months preceding enrollment. Exclusion criteria comprised: (1) history of malignancy, head trauma or loss of consciousness; (2) prior abdominal surgery; (3) poor image quality precluding reliable analysis; and (4) missing clinical data or loss to follow-up.

Assessment of treatment response

Treatment response was evaluated at 12 weeks following initiation of anti-TNF-ɑ therapy. Patients were categorized into short-term clinical remission (STCR) and non-short-term clinical remission (NSTCR). NSTCR was defined by the presence of any of the following: radiologic or endoscopic evidence of active disease; requirement for dose escalation; switching to an alternative therapeutic agent; development of disease-related complications (e.g., intestinal fistula or stenosis); or a Crohn’s Disease Activity Index (CDAI) score exceeding 150 [22]. STCR was defined as resolution of clinical symptoms, normalization of serum and fecal inflammatory markers, and a CDAI score below 150.

Among patients who achieved STCR, long-term outcomes were assessed at 48 weeks or more following treatment initiation. These patients were further classified into long-term clinical remission (LTCR) and non-long-term clinical remission (NLTCR) groups using the same composite criteria described above. Clinical and psychological assessments

Clinical and psychological data were extracted from a clinical database and psychological questionnaires. Demographic and clinical information includes gender, age, education level, disease location at onset, disease duration, serum markers and the simplified endoscopic score for Crohn’s Disease (SES-CD). Disease severity was assessed with CDAI. Given that anti-TNF-ɑ therapy may influence both systemic and central inflammatory tone, we hypothesized that psychological state might be indirectly associated with treatment response. Accordingly, standardized psychological assessments were administered to examine their relationship with disease activity and therapeutic outcomes, and to account for their potential contribution in predictive modeling. Health-related quality of life, social support, and psychological symptoms were evaluated using the inflammatory bowel disease questionnaire (IBDQ) [23], social support rating scale (SSRS) [24] and symptom check list-90 (SCL-90) [25], respectively.

MRI data acquisition and preprocessing

All MRI examinations were performed on 3.0 Tesla scanner equipped with a 32-channel head coil (SIEMENS SKYRA, GERMANY). Patients were instructed to remain still and supine throughout the scan. The brain structural MRI protocol comprised the following parameters: repetition time = 5000 ms, echo time = 2.98 ms, slice thickness = 1 mm, field of view = 256 × 240 mm, voxel size = 1 mm × 1 mm × 1 mm, a total of 176 slices and the total scan time = 8 min 22 s. Gray matter, white matter, and CSF were automatically segmented, and gray matter volume (GMV), white matter volume (WMV), CSF volume, and total intracranial volume (TIV) were calculated using MATLAB R2021a, SPM, and DPABI V7.0 software.

Three-dimensional regions of interest (ROIs) encompassing the CP were manually delineated using 3D-slicer software (Supplementary Material 1). Segmentation was performed independently by two radiologists with five and eight years of experience, respectively, both of whom were blinded to clinical data. Using the Radiomics module within 3D Slicer, CP features including mesh volume (MV) and surface area (SA) were extracted. Intra- and inter-class correlation coefficients (ICCs) were calculated to assess the reproducibility of CP feature measurements. ICC values were interpreted as follows: poor agreement < 0.50, moderate agreement = 0.50–0.75, good agreement = 0.75–0.90, and excellent agreement > 0.90.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were summarized as means with standard deviations and compared using Student’s t-test, Mann-Whitney U test, or unpaired t-test with Welch’s correction, as appropriate. Categorical variables were expressed as proportions or frequencies and compared using the non-parametric Wilcoxon rank-sum test or chi-square test. Associations between variables were assessed using Spearman’s correlation coefficient, with TIV and CSF volume included as covariates. The discriminatory performance of clinical, psychological, and MRI variables was evaluated using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC).

Logistic regression analysis was employed to identify predictors of response to anti-TNF-ɑ therapy. The primary outcomes were binary variables indicating achievement of short-term clinical remission (STCR, yes/no) and long-term clinical remission (LTCR, yes/no). A two-step analytical approach was implemented. First, univariate analysis was conducted to assess the association between each candidate variable and the treatment outcome. Variables with a p value < 0.10 in univariate analysis were considered for inclusion in the multivariate model. Second, multivariate analysis was performed using forward stepwise binary logistic regression, with TIV and CSF volume included as covariates.

This automated procedure iteratively selects the most significant predictor, retains it, and subsequently reassesses the remaining variables, adding only those that contribute statistically independent predictive information. This approach prioritizes model parsimony and mitigates the risk of overfitting, which is particularly important in exploratory studies with a defined sample size. Results are reported as odds ratios (OR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI). Internal validation was performed using 1000-iteration bootstrap resampling to estimate optimism and obtain bias-corrected performance metrics. All statistical analyses were conducted using MATLAB R2021a and R software (version 4.4.3). A two-tailed p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Demographics and clinical characteristics

A total of 130 patients with CD were included in the final analysis. Based on short-term clinical outcomes, 106 patients (81.5%) achieved STCR, while 24 patients (18.5%) were classified as NSTCR. With respect to long-term outcomes, 90 patients (69.2%) achieved LTCR and 40 patients (30.8%) were classified as NLTCR. Within the overall cohort, 73% of patients received infliximab and 27% received adalimumab. Patients in the NSTCR group were significantly younger than those in the STCR group (p = 0.040). Baseline hemoglobin levels were significantly lower in the NSTCR group compared with the STCR group (p = 0.009). Regarding psychological measures, the somatization score was significantly higher in the STCR group than in the NSTCR group (p = 0.034). Neuroimaging analysis revealed no significant differences in TIV, GMV, WMV, or CSF volume between the two groups (all p > 0.05). However, MV and SA of CP were markedly larger in the STCR group compared to the NSTCR group (MV: p = 0.001; SA: p = 0.001).

Patients in the NLTCR group exhibited significantly higher baseline disease activity, as reflected by elevated SES-CD scores (p = 0.035), increased C-reactive protein (CRP) levels (p = 0.027), and higher erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) (p = 0.032). The bowel symptoms score was significantly lower in the NLTCR group (p = 0.049). Among psychological parameters, the somatization score was significantly higher in the NLTCR group compared with the LTCR group (p = 0.037). Consistent with the short-term comparison, volumetric measures including TIV, GMV, WMV, and CSF volume did not differ significantly between the LTCR and NLTCR groups. Notably, both MV and SA of CP remained significantly larger in the LTCR group than in the NLTCR group (MV: p = 0.001; SA: p = 0.001).

Correlation of CP features with psychological scores and clinical characteristics

The relationships between CP morphological characteristics and both clinical biomarkers and psychological profiles were investigated using partial correlation analysis, adjusting for TIV and CSF volume. Results for the entire cohort and for patient subgroups stratified by disease remission status are presented in Fig. 2. In the overall cohort of patients with CD (Fig. 2A), CP morphology demonstrated significant correlations with systemic inflammatory and nutritional markers. The SA of the CP exhibited a positive correlation with serum albumin levels (r = 0.240, p = 0.007) and a negative correlation with the ESR (r=-0.204, p = 0.022). Similarly, MV of the CP showed a positive correlation with albumin (r = 0.213, p = 0.017) and a negative correlation with ESR (r=-0.184, p = 0.039).

Within the STCR subgroup (Fig. 2B), CP features were associated with a wide range of psychological symptom dimensions. The SA of CP showed significant negative correlations with scores for anxiety (r=-0.378, p < 0.001), somatization (r=-0.270, p = 0.006), obsessive compulsive symptoms (r=-0.249, p = 0.012), depression (r=-0.242, p = 0.014), psychoticism (r=-0.260, p = 0.008), and phobic anxiety (r=-0.200, p = 0.044). Concurrently, SA was positively correlated with measures of emotional function (r = 0.230, p = 0.020) and availability (r = 0.200, p = 0.043). The MV of the CP also correlated negatively with anxiety (r=-0.291, p = 0.003), somatization (r=-0.215, p = 0.0300), and psychoticism (r=-0.212, p = 0.032).

In the LTCR subgroup (Fig. 2C), a more restricted pattern of associations was observed. The SA of the CP remained significantly negatively correlated with anxiety (r=-0.341, p = 0.001) and psychoticism (r=-0.211, p = 0.049). A negative correlation was also identified between MV of CP and anxiety scores (r=-0.263, p = 0.014). Collectively, these analyses indicate that CP structural features correlate with both systemic clinical markers and psychological health indicators, with the most extensive associations observed within the STCR patient subgroup.

Analysis of ROC curves for CP features in predicting therapeutic outcomes

To evaluate the predictive performance of the two key imaging features (SA and MV) for therapeutic outcomes in CD, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analyses were conducted for both short-term and long-term efficacy (Fig. 3). For prediction of short-term therapeutic response, both features demonstrated high diagnostic accuracy. The ROC curve for MV yielded an AUC of 0.884 (95% CI: 0.773–0.995, p < 0.001), with an optimal cutoff value of 1436 (Fig. 3A). Similarly, ROC analysis for SA revealed an AUC of 0.856 (95% CI: 0.744–0.968, p < 0.001), with an optimal threshold of 1864 (Fig. 3B). For prediction of long-term therapeutic effects, the predictive capacity of both features was even more pronounced. MV exhibited excellent performance, with an AUC of 0.939 (95% CI: 0.892–0.986, p < 0.001) and an optimal cutoff value of 1717 (Fig. 3C). SA also maintained strong predictive ability, with an AUC of 0.915 (95% CI: 0.862–0.968, p < 0.001) and an optimal threshold of 2112 (Fig. 3D).

Multivariate outcome analysis

The short-term and long-term cohorts were divided into separate training (70%) and test (30%) sets (Supplementary Materials 2 and 3). Binary logistic regression analyses were performed to identify independent predictors of STCR and LTCR. Variables with p < 0.10 in univariate analysis, together with the covariates TIV and CSF volume, were entered into forward stepwise multivariate models. For the outcome of short-term response to anti-TNF-α therapy (Table 2), univariate analysis identified several candidate variables, including age, body mass index, hemoglobin, emotional function, somatization, depression, other psychological symptoms, MV and SA of CP. The subsequent multivariate model retained three independent predictors. Diagnostic performance of the multivariable prediction models

To evaluate the overall discriminatory capacity of the independent predictors identified in multivariate logistic regression analyses, diagnostic models were constructed and their performance assessed using ROC curves (Fig. 4). The multivariate model for predicting STCR, which incorporated hemoglobin level, emotional function score, and SA of CP, demonstrated excellent predictive accuracy, with an AUC of 0.895 (95% CI: 0.805–0.984, p = 0.001) (Fig. 4A). The model for predicting LTCR, based solely on MV of CP, exhibited even higher diagnostic performance, with an AUC of 0.939 (95% CI: 0.892–0.986, p = 0.001) (Fig. 4B).

Discussion

In this study, we developed a predictive model incorporating CP morphology derived from brain MRI alongside clinical and psychological factor to forecast both short-term and long-term clinical remission following anti-TNF-ɑ therapy in patients with CD. Our principal finding is that MV and SA of CP serve as significant predictors of both short-term and long-term clinical remission. The multivariate models, which integrated CP features with clinical variables, demonstrated high predictive accuracy.

The CP plays a pivotal role in CSF production and neuro-immune communication, thereby connecting fluid dynamics with immune signaling within the CNS [17]. The observed association between larger MV and SA of CP and increased likelihood of clinical remission suggests that CP morphology may reflect a CNS state that is more responsive to the systemic anti-inflammatory effects of anti-TNF-α agents [20]. It is important to note that the correlations between CP features and systemic inflammatory markers such as ESR, while statistically significant, were modest in magnitude. This finding indicates that although CP morphology is associated with peripheral inflammation, the relationship is complex and likely involves multifactorial mechanisms extending beyond simple linear correlation [26, 27]. The independent predictive value of CP features within multivariate models, even after accounting for conventional inflammatory markers, underscores their potential to provide unique information relevant to treatment response.

Although psychological factors including anxiety and depression have been implicated in IBD activity through brain-gut interactions and were incorporated in our initial comprehensive assessment, multivariate analysis revealed that the predictive information contained within these psychological measures was not independent of the selected radiologic and clinical variables. This finding likely reflects the fact that psychological distress in active IBD frequently correlates with objective markers of disease severity and inflammation [15]. Consequently, for the specific objective of constructing the most parsimonious and efficient predictive model for anti‑TNF‑α treatment response given our available sample, the forward stepwise regression procedure retained only the strongest independent predictors. This does not diminish the established importance of psychological health in the holistic management of CD; rather, it suggests that the predictive signal relevant to treatment response in this context may be more directly captured through the combination of novel neuroimaging biomarkers with routine clinical parameters. The final binary logistic regression models provide a concise and clinically interpretable tool based on these key independent factors. The reported odds ratios enable direct qualitative assessment of risk, while the full model can be implemented digitally to generate quantitative probabilities to support clinical decision-making.

In recent years, several studies have examined the relationship between early infliximab treatment and clinical outcomes; however, these investigations have been limited by small sample sizes and have primarily focused on maintenance therapy [28–30]. The trajectory of early and long-term clinical response following anti-TNF therapy remains incompletely characterized. Concurrently, growing research on the brain-gut axis has explored associations between central nervous system alterations and intestinal inflammation in patients with CD [31], though these studies have predominantly focused on brain function or structural changes [26, 32, 33]. Investigations specifically examining CP changes in patients with CD remain scarce. A novel contribution of the present study is the demonstration that CP features, including MV and SA, correlate significantly with clinical and psychological status in patients with CD, a finding not previously reported in previous studies. The CP is capable of secreting crucial proteins and maintaining the blood-CSF barrier to prevent entry of potentially harmful substances into the CNS.

Accumulating evidence has demonstrated that patients with neurodegenerative disorders such as multiple sclerosis, Alzheimer’s disease, and Parkinson’s disease frequently exhibit enlarged CP volumes [34]. Thus, early CP enlargement may represent a common feature among patients with neurodegenerative diseases, potentially contributing to disease pathogenesis. Choi and colleagues reported that increased CP volume may be associated with cognitive impairment in Alzheimer’s disease and suggested that CP volume could serve as a neural biomarker for clinical prognosis and disease staging [35]. Under inflammatory conditions, the CP may become enlarged and contribute to the pathogenesis of neuroinflammatory diseases. In this context, an important question is whether CP volume changes also occur in vivo in patients with CD. Notably, our previous research has indicated potential abnormalities in CSF volume among patients with active CD26. Moreover, both brain function and structure were altered in patients with CD compared with healthy controls, irrespective of disease activity status. Collectively, these studies suggest that morphological changes in the CP may also occur in patients with CD. Therefore, elucidating the relationship between CP alterations and disease status in CD is crucial for understanding the neuropathological mechanisms underlying this condition.

In our study, we identified target thresholds for MV and SA of CP, which were significantly associated with the outcomes of anti-TNF-ɑ therapy for CD. The prediction of the therapeutic effect of anti-TNF-ɑ may be related to the type of anti-TNF-ɑ and the method of administration [36]. Currently, the main outcome predictions for anti-TNF-ɑ therapy are based on endoscopic, computed tomography enterography [37] or laboratory [38, 39], with little focus on changes in the central nervous system, particularly CP. In this study, we constructed a combined modal of neurological CP features and clinical and psychological features to predict short-term clinical response to anti-TNF-ɑ treatment.

Both hemoglobin levels showed significant statistical differences in the univariate and multivariate analyses, which was same as the previous studies. There were also studies demonstrating that CRP could serve as predictive indicators for clinical outcomes in patients with IBD [40, 41]. Therefore, this study demonstrated the importance of hemoglobin levels in assessing the disease severity of CD. However, few studies have the significance of integrated the serum levels with CP in predicting the clinical outcomes for patients with CD.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the sample size was relatively modest, and the study was conducted at a single center, which may limit the generalizability of our findings. Future multicenter studies with larger cohorts are warranted to validate these results. Second, manual segmentation of the CP is time-consuming, underscoring the need for development of automated segmentation methods in future research. Third, this study assessed CP characteristics only at baseline; longitudinal CP data were not available. Whether changes in CP morphology over time are associated with disease course or activity in patients receiving anti-TNF therapy should be explored in future longitudinal investigations. Finally, the practical implementation of brain MRI as a predictive tool requires consideration of cost and accessibility. Nevertheless, investigating the predictive value of CP morphology for other biologic therapies and elucidating the underlying molecular mechanisms linking CP structure to intestinal inflammation represent promising directions for future research.

Conclusions

In conclusion, we developed a predictive model integrating CP morphology with clinical factors that accurately predicts response to anti-TNF-α therapy in patients with CD. This study highlights the potential of CP as a novel biomarker accessible via routine brain MRI, supporting the relevance of the brain-gut axis in CD treatment outcomes. Further validation studies are required to assess its clinical utility for personalizing therapeutic decisions.

Characteristics of the first immunocompromised patients to receive sipavibart as an early access treatment for COVID-19 pre-exposure prophylaxis in France
Authors: Paul Loubet, Benjamin Gaborit, Mathilde Salpin, Hèlene Gardeney, Ilies Benotmane, Thomas Systchenko
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTFrance was the first country to grant Sipavibart (AZD3152, an investigational long-acting monoclonal antibody) as a COVID-19 pre-exposure prophylaxis treatment in immunocompromised individuals in December 2023. The first patients to receive Sipavibart had different profiles, but they were all highly immunocompromised with frequently associated hypogammaglobulinemia and other chronic conditions. No adverse event was reported.KEYWORDS: COVID-19, prophylaxis, immunocompromised

Immunocompromised patients account for a large proportion of COVID-19 hospitalizations and deaths, even in the most recent periods, despite having received several COVID-19 vaccine doses.1 Several generations of monoclonal antibodies (Casirivimab/imdevimab, Tixagevimab/Cilgavimab) effectively prevented COVID-19 in the general population in randomized control trials2 and the immunocompromised population in real-world studies.3 Unfortunately, the emergence of new variants precluded their use. However, the story is not over. Pemivibart recently received emergency use authorization from the FDA; others are developing in clinical trials. Sipavibart (AZD3152) is an investigational long-acting monoclonal antibody designed to provide broad coverage across Omicron and ancestral viral variants currently being studied in the SUPERNOVA prevention trial in the immunocompromised population. A press release on May 16th, 2024, stated that the trial met the primary endpoint being the relative risk reduction of symptomatic COVID-19 caused by any SARS-CoV-2 variant demonstrating the potential benefit of sipavibart in an evolving variant landscape in which COVID-19 cases captured throughout the trial were caused by several different SARS-CoV-2 variants.

France was the first country to grant Sipavibart as a COVID-19 pre-exposure prophylaxis treatment in immunocompromised individuals with an early access authorization in December 2023.

To better understand the patient profile and real-world use of Sipavibart, we performed, the SIPAFIRST study, a retrospective multicentric study in 5 university hospitals in France, including the first consecutive patients to receive one intramuscular dose of Sipavibart in January or February 2024. We collected their characteristics and COVID-19-related medical history. The study was approved by the CHU de Nîmes ethics committee (IRB number: 24.02.07).

Overall, we included 47 patients with a median age of 61 years (IQR: 64—69) and 64% male. The three main immunosuppressive conditions were Stem cell transplants (38%), solid organ transplants (32%), and immune-based therapies (mostly Rituximab) (19%) for chronic inflammatory diseases or hematologic malignancies. Nearly half had hypogammaglobulinemia, and 71% had at least another key comorbidity (Table 1). 15% and 11% had a history of severe and persistent COVID-19, respectively. The median number of COVID-19 doses was 4 (IQR 3–6), and nearly one-third had previously received previous generation of monoclonal antibodies for COVID-19 prophylaxis (Casirivimab/imdevimab or Tixagevimab/Cilgavimab). SARS-CoV-2 serology results before Sipavibart administration were available in 11 patients (without Immunoglobulins substitution). The median anti-Spike IgG titers were 30 UI/L (IQR 0–179). No adverse event was reported.

The first patients to receive Sipavibart in France had different profiles, but they were all highly immunocompromised with frequently associated hypogammaglobulinemia and other chronic conditions. The development and the use of new monoclonal antibodies is of paramount importance in this population, which is unlikely to have optimal vaccine responses, particularly as people begin to reengage more fully with society, with the related increase in the risk of COVID-19 exposure.

Corresponding author’s bio

Prof. Paul Loubet, M.D., Ph.D., MPH, is a Professor of infectious diseases at the University of Montpellier and head of the Infectious and Tropical Diseases department at the Nîmes University Hospital in France.

He received his M.D., specializing in Infectious and Tropical Diseases, and his Ph.D. from the Université de Paris, with his research on diagnosing lower respiratory infections in adults.

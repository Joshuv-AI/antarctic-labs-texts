Construction of a nomogram model for postoperative complications following thoracoscopic lung cancer surgery in elderly patients: a study based on an elderly patient population
Authors: Shixin Ma, Hongyuan Du, Fei Li, Liang Bu, Lunqing Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo evaluate the risk variables for postoperative problems in geriatric patients receiving lung cancer surgery and to develop and validate a nomogram model.Patients and methodsThe relationship between non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patient characteristics, surgical factors, and postoperative complications was collected and analyzed. A nomogram model was constructed based on statistical regression analysis results, and the predictive performance of the model was evaluated.ResultsThe average age of the patients was 67.07 ± 5.36 years. A total of 26.2% (113/432) patients had complications after thoracoscopic surgery. Logistic regression analysis showed that thoracic adhesion, history of respiratory disease, and FFA level were independent risk factors for complications after thoracoscopic surgery (P < 0.05). Based on the above factors, a nomogram risk model was constructed. The verification results showed that the area under curve (AUC) of the model was 0.728 (95%CI: 0.675 ~ 0.781), and the calibration curve showed that the model had good differentiation. The DCA (decision Curve Analysis) shows that this model has good clinical application value. In a subgroup analysis of complications, a history of respiratory disease and thoracic adhesion were identified as significant risk factors for postoperative pulmonary complications. LNs have been identified as an important risk factor for postoperative cardiovascular complications. The AUC of postoperative cardiac and pulmonary complications were 0.716 (95%CI: 0.653 ~ 0.779) and 0.703 (95%CI: 0.615 ~ 0.791), respectively. The calibration curve and DCA curve indicate that the model has good predictive performance and clinical application value.ConclusionIn this study, the risk factors affecting the postoperative complications of thoracoscopic lung cancer in the elderly were analyzed, and the nomogram model built based on this has certain significance for the identification and reduction of postoperative complications.Keywords: Non-small cell lung cancer, Elderly, Thoracoscope, Complications, Nomogram model

Background

Global epidemiological data indicate that lung cancer accounted for approximately 820,000 new diagnoses and 710,000 deaths in China during 2020. Epidemiological projections suggest that, driven by demographic aging trends, the incidence of lung cancer in older adults is poised to escalate markedly over the next 20 years, thereby posing substantial public health challenges [1, 2]. The main approach for treating non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is surgical resection. Indications encompass cases of stage I, II, and IIIA that are amenable to full resection. Additionally, in specific instances, such as solitary brain or adrenal metastases, palliative surgery or a combination of surgical therapies may be employed for stage IIIB and IV cases [3]. The progressive displacement of traditional thoracotomy by video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) in clinical practice is driven by its demonstrated clinical advantages, including diminished tissue trauma, accelerated convalescence, and enhanced intraoperative visualization [4]. Age-related physiological decline significantly impacts surgical tolerance and postoperative recovery in geriatric populations (≥ 60 years). Surgeons must conduct comprehensive perioperative evaluations to optimize clinical outcomes and postoperative functional recovery, Another study of ours showed good predictive performance by constructing a nomogram model of postoperative complications in patients eligible for thoracoscopic surgery [5]. However, in the above study, age was not differentiated, and the postoperative complications of elderly patients were higher than those of younger patients due to multiple factors, so it is necessary to accurately evaluate this specific population of elderly patients. According to the findings, postoperatively linked problems among older persons with lung cancer usually arise 30 days after lung surgery, with a frequency ranging from 8.4% to 48% [6, 7]. Postoperative complications will lengthen patients’ hospitalization, escalate medical expenses, and augment the economic burden on patients [8, 9]. Prior research has demonstrated that elderly patients who undergo surgical procedures are more susceptible to pulmonary complications compared to their younger counterparts [10].It is crucial to prioritize the prevention and mitigation of postoperative problems during the perioperative phase in order to enhance the recovery process, enhance the quality of life, and minimize healthcare expenses for senior patients. Nevertheless, there is a dearth of realistic assessment methods aimed at reducing the frequency of such issues. Thus, utilizing clinical data from elderly patients with lung cancer, this study developed and validated a nomogram prediction model for complications following thoracoscopic surgery in this population and offer guidance for managing patients during the perioperative period.

Material and method

Object of study

This study performed a retrospective analysis of clinical data from NSCLC patients who underwent VATS at Qingdao Municipal Hospital from January 2017 to December 2021. The main emphasis was on identifying complications arising within 30 days post-surgical intervention for lung cancer. The postoperative complications included in the composite outcome measure were arrhythmias, heart failure, atelectasis, pulmonary infection, chylothorax, persistent pulmonary air leakage lasting seven days or longer, moderate or greater pleural effusion, severe subcutaneous emphysema, fluid pneumothorax, active bleeding, infection or inadequate wound healing, and pulmonary embolism. The qualifying criteria included (1) individuals aged 60 years and above, (2) individuals diagnosed with stage I-III NSCLC undergoing thoracoscopic lobectomy or segmental resection, and (3) individuals possessing comprehensive clinical medical records. The following were the exclusion criteria: (1) individuals under 60 years of age; (2) patients with NSCLC deemed unsuitable for surgical resection or unable to endure surgical intervention; (3) patients with infectious diseases or immune system disorders leading to abnormal preoperative test results; (4) patients who transitioned from thoracoscopic surgery to thoracotomy due to uncontrollable circumstances or who underwent surgery exclusively for exploration, biopsy, wedge resection, or pneumonectomy; (5) patients exhibiting poor medical compliance, premature discharge, an incomplete treatment regimen, or absent data.

Data collection

Perioperative data for patients were retrospectively acquired through the hospital’s electronic medical record archiving system, quality control registration management system, and laboratory examination report system. The preoperative and intraoperative data gathered encompassed the patient’s age, gender, smoking history, Body Mass Index (BMI), respiratory disease history (including COPD, bronchial asthma, and bronchiectasis), Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG PS) prior to treatment, and pulmonary function metrics (FEV1, FVC, and FEV1/FVC). Additional data included the tumor’s location and size, the type of surgery (lobectomy or segmental resection), the number of lymph nodes removed during surgery (The lymph node dissection range for VATS lobectomy: Right side: 2R, 4R, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11; Left side: 2 L, 4 L, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11. The lymph node dissection range for VATS pulmonary segmentectomy: Systemic lymph node sampling: At least sample the 10th and 11th groups of lymph nodes; Based on the tumor location: For tumors in the superior segment/ posterior segment of the right upper lobe: Sample 2R, 4R; For tumors in the medial/ lateral segment of the right middle lobe: Sample 4R, 7; For tumors in the posterior segment/basal segment of the right lower lobe: Sample 4R, 7, 8, 9; For tumors in the superior posterior/ anterior segment of the left upper lobe: Sample 5, 6, 7; For tumors in the posterior segment/basal segment of the left lower lobe: Sample 7, 8, 9). the length of the surgery, any bleeding that occurred during the surgery, any adhesions that formed in the chest, and the results of laboratory tests (C-reactive protein, albumin, and free fatty acids (FFA)) taken one week before the surgery. The C-reactive protein-to-albumin ratio (CAR) was calculated. The thoracic adhesion was assessed based on the adhesion area: no adhesion, limited to a single lung lobe, or a cumulative adhesion area of less than 30% of the lung surface area; or an adhesion area exceeding a single lung lobe, or a cumulative adhesion area of 30% or more of the lung surface area [5].

Statistical method

SPSS 27.0 and the R 4.2.1 software were used to handle and analyse the data. The receiver operator characteristic curve (ROC) was used to determine the optimal threshold values for tumour diameter, operating time, intraoperative blood loss, LNs, CAR, and FFA, which were then classified as two separate variables. Patients were divided into two groups based on whether or not they had postoperative complications: those with complications and those without. An analysis of variance was performed on the clinical data for the two groups. The counting data was presented as percentages, and the Chi-square test was used to compare the various groups. To screen out covariates with P < 0.1, a binary logistic regression analysis was utilised. A nomogram model was then built using the RMS tool in R. If the nomogram model variables are fewer than three, the model is built by adjusting for two traditional characteristics, gender and smoking history, and including them in the nomogram. Internal validation of the nomogram model was performed using a bootstrap test with 1000 repeated samples. The model’s prediction performance and accuracy were evaluated using the concordance index (C-index) and the calibration graph techniques. A significance level of P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics

This study conducted a thorough analysis of NSCLC patients who underwent surgical procedures at our hospital, encompassing a total of 508 eligible elderly individuals. To minimise the heterogeneity of the study, patients who received thoracoscopic wedge resection (n = 72) and total lung resection (n = 4) were subsequently eliminated, given the disparity in complications associated with both procedures. Postoperative complications

26.2% of the study cohort, equating to 113 individuals, encountered complications subsequent to thoracoscopic surgery. The principal postoperative complications included 18 occurrences (4.2%) of arrhythmia [13 cases of atrial fibrillation (3.0%) and 5 cases of premature ventricular beats (1.2%)], 2 cases of heart failure (0.5%), 5 cases of atelectasis (1.2%), 26 cases of pulmonary infection (6.0%), 11 cases of chylothorax (2.5%), 44 cases of persistent pulmonary air leakage (10.2%), and 17 cases of moderate or greater pleural effusion (3.9%). The incidence rates of severe subcutaneous emphysema, liquid pneumothorax, active bleeding, infection or poor wound healing, and pulmonary embolism were 1.9%, 1.2%, 0.7%, 1.2%, and 0.2%, respectively. Of the total cases, 95 (22.0%) had pulmonary complications, such as atelectasis, pulmonary infection, chylothorax, chronic pulmonary air leakage, significant pleural effusion, fluid pneumothorax, and pulmonary embolism. Furthermore, 20 instances (4.6%) were documented to have cardiovascular complications, namely arrhythmia and heart failure.

Establishment and verification of postoperative complication prediction model

The prediction probability is determined by aggregating the scores for each variable using a nomogram model after univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses have been conducted to eliminate potential confounding factors. The logistic regression analysis included variables with P < 0.1 in the nomogram model, including gender, thoracic adhesion, history of respiratory disease, FFA level, and type of surgical procedure. The cumulative score of risk factors, as illustrated in Fig. 2A, ranges from 0 to 450 points, and the corresponding risk rate is between 0.05 and 0.8. The risk of postoperative complications increases as the total score increases. A calibration curve was plotted (Fig. 2B), and the model was internally verified using the Bootstrap self-sampling method (B = 1000). The results demonstrated a high degree of consistency between the predicted and actual probabilities according to the model. The accuracy of the model was assessed using the ROC curve, and the results (Fig. 2C) indicated that the area under the curve’s AUC was 0.728 (95% CI: 0.675–0.781). Figure 2D demonstrates that the decision Curve Analysis (DCA) curve indicates that this model has a high level of clinical application. Figures 3 and 4 illustrate the nomogram model of risk factors for postoperative cardiopulmonary complications. The area under the curve (AUC) of postoperative Cardiovascular and pulmonary complications was 0.716 (95% CI: 0.653–0.779) and 0.703 (95% CI: 0.615–0.791), respectively. The DCA curve indicates that the cardiovascular complications model has poor clinical application value, although the calibration curve indicates that the model has excellent predictive performance. Discussion

Thoracoscopic surgery is a minimally invasive procedure for lung cancer that significantly reduces surgical trauma, shortens hospital stays, and lowers the incidence of postoperative complications [4]. Nonetheless, encountering intraoperative damage to lung tissue is unavoidable, thereby increasing the probability of subsequent complications. Numerous studies have shown that surgical complications significantly contribute to adverse perioperative outcomes and rising healthcare costs [11–13]. While some prior studies have evaluated postoperative complications, the chosen sample has not been delineated by age, thereby precluding an accurate assessment for a specific demographic [5, 14]. Nevertheless, in senior people, this results in a decline in physical function, nutritional health, and immune response. Moreover, atherosclerosis frequently occurs, leading to the constriction of blood vessels, diminished elasticity, and a reduction in pulmonary elastic fibre content. Moreover, the elderly exhibit heightened vulnerability to complications associated with lung cancer surgery as a result of prolonged exposure to dust and tobacco smoke. Consequently, it is crucial to develop and validate a nomogram prediction model for postoperative complications in this cohort of elderly patients, leveraging their clinical data to identify associated risk variables and inform perioperative therapy. In this study, elderly patients were defined as individuals aged 60 years or above, and this criterion was consistent with that defined by the WHO. This retrospective investigation revealed a surgical complication rate of 26.2% among older patients with lung cancer. After conducting univariate analysis and multivariate logistic regression to account for relevant confounders, the influence of thoracic adhesion, respiratory illness history, and FFA levels on postoperative complications remained statistically significant. Research indicates that the duration of surgery is a critical determinant of postoperative complications [11, 15]. The prolongation of the operational duration entails extended anaesthesia, mechanical breathing, and alterations in cardiopulmonary haemodynamics. It exacerbates the twisting, compression, and damage of lung tissue, hence elevating cardiopulmonary problems to a degree. However, no correlation between operative time and postoperative complications was observed in the multivariate analysis in this study, which may be because thoracic adhesion not only affects the operative time but is also closely related to the amount of intraoperative blood loss and lung tissue injury, and its extensive influence thus masks the relationship between operative time and complications. Secondly, individuals with a history of pulmonary disorders, including COPD, bronchial asthma, and bronchiectasis, will have persistent airway inflammation, diminished elastic retraction capacity of the alveoli, and associated complications. Moreover, surgery may further diminish lung function reserve, leading to a range of postoperative problems and an increased risk of respiratory failure. FFA serve several crucial functions in living organisms, including facilitating energy synthesis and storage, constituting a vital component of cell membranes, and functioning as ligands for nuclear receptors. Disruption of fatty acid homeostasis results in raised blood free fatty acid levels, ultimately leading to ectopic fat deposition; persistently high free fatty acid levels can have detrimental effects referred to as lipotoxicity. As research progresses, the pathways of lipid toxicity resulting from elevated FFA encompass insulin resistance, inflammation, endoplasmic reticulum stress, and oxidative stress [16]. Research indicates that elevated levels of FFA infiltrate islet beta cells, causing the synthesis of harmful esters (diacylglycerol and ceramide) that obstruct insulin signalling, ultimately resulting in damage to islet beta cells and compromised insulin secretion [17]. Research indicates that insulin resistance may lead to catabolic diseases associated with postoperative stress and elevate the risk of postoperative complications [18]. Secondly, FFA can elicit inflammation via multiple pathways, activating Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) to initiate signalling cascades in innate immune cells (peripheral blood mononuclear cells), stimulating the pro-inflammatory NF-κB pathway, which results in the synthesis of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α), subsequently enhancing the production of C-reactive protein (CRP) in hepatocytes [19, 20]. This study incorporated preoperative FFA as a predictive factor, revealing that preoperative FFA can forecast postoperative complications earlier in older NSCLC patients. It is important to note that while the present study did not routinely collect data on pre-existing diabetes mellitus (DM) status, the elevation of FFA serves as a direct biomarker of metabolic dysfunction. Elevated FFA levels induce ‘lipotoxicity,’ leading to insulin resistance and activation of pro-inflammatory pathways (via TLR4/NF-κB). This mechanism operates independently of a clinical DM diagnosis; even in non-diabetic elderly patients, preoperative FFA elevation signifies impaired metabolic homeostasis, which exacerbates the surgical stress response and increases susceptibility to complications. Thus, FFA provides a functional assessment of metabolic risk that complements, rather than replaces, the diagnostic label of DM. Moreover, prior research has established that the combined inflammatory nutritional markers of C-reactive protein and albumin (CAR) serve as dependable biomarkers for forecasting postoperative complications in individuals with gastric and colorectal cancer [21, 22]. However, the predictive effect of CAR was not observed in this study. We propose that this relates to the concept of physiological reserve. CAR primarily captures the existing burden of systemic inflammation and nutritional status, which in an elderly population with multiple comorbidities represents a chronic baseline rather than a dynamic predictor of acute surgical tolerance [23].

The subgroup analysis of cardiopulmonary complications indicated that the primary factors leading to postoperative pulmonary difficulties were thoracic adhesions and a history of respiratory illness. These parameters were significantly correlated with surgery duration, intraoperative interventions, and respiratory capacity. The principal factors influencing postoperative cardiovascular complications were the LNs. As the tumour stage advances, a greater number of lymph nodes must be resected to ensure the effectiveness of postoperative treatment. However, it also increases the duration and complexity of the surgery, resulting in a rise in various parameters, including intraoperative blood loss. Thus, an increased extent of lymph node excision in elderly patients correlates with a heightened incidence of cardiovascular complications. For the insignificant factors (gender and smoking history) in multivariate analysis to be included as part of the cardiovascular complication nomogram model, the reason lies in that in the risk assessment of complications after VATS for elderly non-small cell lung cancer patients, gender and smoking history, as important clinical variables, their mechanism of influence involves multiple interactions of anatomical and physiological differences and long-term pathological physiological changes [24–26]. From the perspective of gender factors, numerous previous studies have all supported that gender can be regarded as an important predictive factor [24, 25]. In this study, male patients also showed a higher overall postoperative complication rate; this difference may stem from multiple physiological and pathological mechanisms: Firstly, male patients typically have a greater burden of underlying comorbidities; secondly, hormone levels may also affect the postoperative recovery of patients [27]. Furthermore, gender factors often have a synergistic effect with other risk factors. For instance, male smokers often have risk factors, such as a history of smoking. This cumulative exposure amplifies the risk effect of gender itself. Although the significance level of the gender factor in multivariate analysis has decreased, its clinical predictive value still cannot be ignored.

Smoking history is an important risk factor for post-VATS complications. Its pathophysiological mechanism involves multiple factors. From the respiratory system perspective, long-term tobacco exposure leads to a significant decline in the ciliary clearance function of the airway mucosa. This impaired defense mechanism directly increases the risk of postoperative pulmonary infection [28]. In terms of cardiovascular effects, nicotine can cause vasoconstriction and an increase in heart rate by activating the sympathetic nervous system. Meanwhile, carbon monoxide binds with hemoglobin to form carboxyhemoglobin, reducing the oxygen-carrying capacity of the blood. This dual effect leads to insufficient oxygen supply to tissues and significantly affects the healing of surgical wounds [29, 30]. Furthermore, the chemical substances in tobacco trigger the inflammatory pathways, causing the levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) and interleukin-6 (IL-6) to continuously rise. This chronic inflammatory state will exacerbate the surgical stress response, thereby increasing the risk of postoperative complications [31]. Therefore, smoking status is also one of the important clinical predictors.

Nonetheless, the study possesses specific limitations. The study was a single-centre retrospective analysis, and the cohort may not accurately represent patients from other hospitals or areas. Secondly, variations in the surgical techniques employed by various surgeons may influence the occurrence of postoperative problems in individuals. Consequently, multi-centre investigations are required for additional validation and confirmation.

Conclusion

This study analysed the risk factors influencing postoperative complications of thoracoscopic lung cancer in the elderly, and the resulting nomogram model holds significant value for identifying and mitigating these complications.

Background

Aortic dissection is a life-threatening condition. Coarctation of the aorta is a relatively common defect accounting for 5% to 8% of all congenital heart defects. Type B aortic dissection associated with aortic coarctation is an extremely rare condition. We present a case that was treated successfully by endovascular repair with balloon angioplasty and stent-graft deployment. This procedure seems to be a valuable option for this unusual condition compared with open thoracotomy. The study has been approved by our institutional ethics committee. Due to the retrospective design of the study, written informed consent was not required.

Case presentation

A 46-year-old man was admitted to our hospital for severe chest pain radiating to his back. He had a 10-year history of hypertension. Systemic examination was unremarkable. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography angiography revealed type B aortic dissection associated with coarctation at the distal end of the aortic arch. The dissection originated distal to the coarctation and the origin of the left subclavian artery and extended to near the origin of the celiac axis(Fig. 1a, b and c).

Combined treatment with angioplasty and endovascular stent-graft deployment was performed. Intraoperative transesophageal echocardiography was used to monitor the procedure. Initial aortic arch angiography was performed through the left brachial artery. Angiography revealed the length of the coarctation to be approximately 20 mm and showed the entry point of the dissection at the descending part of the aortic arch, with near-total occlusion of the lumen and collateral circulation. A 4-F vert catheter (Infiniti diagnostic catheter; Cook Medical) with a coaxial microcatheter was inserted via the left brachial artery and advanced over a soft guidewire (0.014” × 180 cm; Miracle 6; Asahi Intecc) to cross the coarctation. It was then exchanged for a stiff guidewire (0.035” × 260 cm; Cook Medical) to provide greater support. The left femoral artery was exposed and an 8-F sheath (Avanti sheath introducer; Cordis) was inserted. The stiff guidewire inserted through the left brachial artery was retrieved through the left femoral artery. The coarctation was dilated at 4–10 atm pressure with balloon catheters ranging from 7 mm × 60 mm (ATB PTA dilatation catheter; Cook Medical) to 16 mm × 60 mm (XBKD-16 dilatation catheter; Biosis Generation Medical Technology Company). Then, the stiff guidewire was replaced by an extra-stiff wire (0.035” × 300 cm; Lunderquist; Cook Medical) via the left femoral artery. Under continuous fluoroscopy, a covered stent (28-mm diameter × 160-mm length; Ankura covered stent; Lifetech Scientific) was deployed to exclude the dissection entry tear.

Four days after the procedure, the patient presented with acute ischemia of the left lower extremity. Ultrasound showed a thrombus in the left common femoral artery. The patient underwent embolectomy and the acute ischemia resolved immediately. One week later, the patient was discharged in good condition. Three years later, contrast-enhanced computed tomography showed that the coarctation had disappeared, with the residual dissection remaining asymptomatic(Fig. 1d and e).

Discussion

In this case, the patient presented with severe chest pain and a 10-year history of hypertension, and contrast-enhanced computed tomography demonstrated a dissection associated with coarctation. The concern about insufficient radial force of the self-expandable covered stent prompted pre-dilation of the coarctation with balloon catheters. The balloon diameter was limited to 16 mm to avoid vascular trauma from excessive radial force. A self-expandable covered stent 28 mm in diameter was selected on the basis of the aortic arch diameter. Postoperative angiography revealed that the dissection entry tear was covered and the coarctation had resolved.

Aortic dissection is a life-threatening condition. Currently, thoracic endovascular aortic repair (TEVAR) for aortic dissection has become an accepted technique [1]. Coarctation may occur as an isolated defect or in association with other lesions, such as a bicuspid aortic valve or ventricular septal defect. Classic aortic coarctation is located in the thoracic aorta, distal to the ostium of the left subclavian artery. Surgical repair of aortic coarctation was first performed in 1944 [2]. The mortality rate associated with open surgery is 2% to 4%, while the incidence of postoperative complications is approximately 5% to 10%. Repair in patients older than 20 years or with preoperative hypertension significantly decreases survival. Balloon angioplasty and endovascular stenting subsequently became available [3, 4]. Complications after endovascular repair include aortic aneurysm formation, restenosis and reintervention.

Both endovascular repair and open surgery have been considered safe and effective for patients with type B aortic dissection and concomitant aortic coarctation [5]. Compared with open surgery, patients with coarctation treated with stent deployment experienced fewer complications [6]. The lower complication rate and favourable proximal landing zone prompted us to perform TEVAR in this patient. The case reported by Kassaian et al., in which a self-expandable stent-graft was used to cover the entry tear and a balloon-expandable bare-metal stent to correct the residual stenosis, was similar to ours [7]. Due to the femoral artery incision and decreased perfusion, postoperative lower extremity ischemia occurred in this patient. Pre-emptive protective measures—such as a purse-string suture, maintenance of mean blood pressure at approximately 90–100 mmHg, and euvolemia—should be implemented to reduce the risk of lower extremity complications.

Type B aortic dissection associated with coarctation is an extremely rare condition. The present case was treated successfully by endovascular repair with balloon angioplasty and stent-graft deployment. This procedure seems to be a valuable option for this unusual condition compared with open thoracotomy.

Conclusion

Aortic dissection associated with coarctation is a very rare condition. It is usually treated via thoracotomy and graft interposition. We present a case that was treated successfully by endovascular repair with balloon angioplasty and stent-graft deployment. Endovascular repair seems to be an alternative to open surgery, especially for highly selected patients.

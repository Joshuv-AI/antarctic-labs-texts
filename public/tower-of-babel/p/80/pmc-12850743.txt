Endoscopic versus surgical therapies for GERD: a systematic review and network meta-analysis
Authors: Ravi Teja Pasam, Karim T Osman, Babu P Mohan, Douglas G Adler
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and AimsThe objective of our study was to compare the outcomes of endoscopic and surgical therapies for GERD using a network meta-analysis.MethodsOnly randomized control trials with adult patients were included in the study. Radiofrequency energy (RFe) delivery, transoral incisionless fundoplication (TIF), magnetic sphincter augmentation (MSA), and Nissen, Toupet, and anterior fundoplication were studied. Outcomes of interest were GERD health-related quality of life (GERD-HRQL), proton pump inhibitor (PPI) use, presence of esophagitis, percentage of time with pH <4 on 24-hour esophageal pH monitoring (% time pH <4), and lower esophageal sphincter pressure (LESP) at ≤12-month follow-up.ResultsTwenty-seven studies (3515 patients) were included. GERD-HRQL scores for TIF and RFe were not significantly different (mean difference [MD], –3.81; 95% confidence interval [CI], –10.65 to 3.04) but were significantly better than PPI use alone. No significant differences occurred between endoscopic and surgical procedures in terms of esophagitis, % time pH <4, or cessation of PPI use. Regarding LESP, RFe was inferior to TIF (MD, –9.23; 95% CI, –13.89 to –4.58) and to Nissen (MD, –9.00; 95% CI, –14.55 to –3.45), Toupet (MD, –6.83; 95% CI, –12.50 to –1.55), and anterior (MD, –7.5; 95% CI, –13.35 to –1.71) fundoplication. LESP was comparable for endoscopic and surgical fundoplication.ConclusionsTIF did not demonstrate significantly different short-term outcomes compared with surgical fundoplication procedures. TIF should be considered on a case-by-case basis in patients who do not want surgery or are not surgical candidates. RFe is inferior to TIF and surgical fundoplication. MSA requires more randomized control trial data.

GERD is one of the most common GI disorders, with an estimated prevalence of 21% in the United States.1 GERD-related direct healthcare costs are estimated to be $12 billion in the United States.2 Patients with refractory GERD have significantly higher GERD-related costs compared with those without refractory GERD ($2575 vs $963 per patient per year).3

The initial management of GERD includes diet and lifestyle modification as well as therapy with proton pump inhibitors (PPIs).4 Patients are referred for consideration of antireflux surgery and endoscopic therapies when they have symptoms refractory to medications, side effects to medications, are noncompliant to medications, no longer wish to take medications, or have a symptomatic hiatal hernia that warrants surgery.4 In these patients, surgical and endoscopic therapies have demonstrated better symptom control and quality of life compared with medical therapy alone.5,6

Laparoscopic procedures such as Nissen fundoplication, Toupet (posterior) fundoplication, or anterior fundoplication have been the mainstay of surgical therapies for GERD. Surgical fundoplication procedures alleviate GERD symptoms by reinforcing and lengthening the lower esophageal sphincter (LES).7 Over the last 2 decades, endoscopic therapies and newer surgical interventions such as radiofrequency energy (RFe) delivery, transoral incisionless fundoplication (TIF), and magnetic sphincter augmentation (MSA) have emerged as minimally invasive options.10, 8, 9 RFe is endoscopically delivered at multiple sites around the LES11 and is believed to act through various mechanisms including thickening of the LES, decreasing gastroesophageal junction compliance, and decreasing transient relaxation of the LES.8,12, 13, 14, 15 TIF is performed using various available devices on the market such as Esophyx2 (EndoGastric Solutions, Richmond, Wash, USA), Medigus ultrasonic surgical endostapler system (Medigus Ltd, Omer, Israel), and GERDx (G-SURG GmbH, Seeon-Seebruck, Germany).16 MSA is a surgical procedure wherein a string of titanium encased magnets are wrapped and secured around the gastroesophageal junction laparoscopically.17 The magnetic force between these beads is just strong enough to provide LES reinforcement in the resting state but allows for separation of the beads while swallowing.17

Although MSA and other currently available endoscopic procedures have been shown to improve GERD-related outcomes, no randomized control trials (RCTs) have been conducted comparing their efficacy with that of surgical fundoplication procedures. The objective of our study was to compare outcomes of laparoscopic fundoplication, TIF, RFe delivery, and MSA for GERD in adult patients using a network meta-analysis.

Methods

Study design

A systematic review and network meta-analysis was conducted according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses statement.18 The checklist for reporting the network meta-analysis is provided in Appendix 1 (available online at www.igiejournal.org).

Literature search

An electronic search of MEDLINE, Cochrane, and Embase databases was performed from inception to April 2022. The various search terms used were “Gastro-esophageal reflux disease,” “GERD,” “Surgical fundoplication,” “Nissen fundoplication,” “Total fundoplication,” “Toupet fundoplication,” “Posterior fundoplication,” “Anterior fundoplication,” “Dor fundoplication,” “Partial fundoplication,” “Endoscopic fundoplication,” “Transoral incisionless fundoplication,” “EsophyX,” “GERDx,” “MUSE,” “Magnetic Sphincter Augmentation,” “Linx,” “PPI,” “Proton pump inhibitors,” “Quality of life,” “Lower esophageal sphincter pressure,” “esophageal pH,” and “esophagitis.”

Eligibility criteria

Only RCTs with adult patients (aged >18 years) who were diagnosed with GERD were included, with the intervention group undergoing RFe delivery, TIF, MSA, and laparoscopic Nissen, Toupet, or anterior fundoplication procedures and the control group undergoing sham procedures, PPI use, or one of the interventions. Only studies that reported our prespecified outcomes were included. Studies were excluded if they were nonrandomized or observational, in abstract form only, or if the outcomes of interest were not reported.

Outcomes

Primary outcomes were GERD health-related quality of life (GERD-HRQL) scores and cessation of PPI use at a follow-up of ≤1 year. Secondary outcomes were presence of esophagitis, percentage of time with a pH <4 on 24-hour esophageal pH monitoring (% time pH <4), and LES pressure (LESP) at a follow-up of ≤1 year.

Study selection

Two independent reviewers (R.T.P. and K.T.O.) reviewed the titles and abstracts of all citations identified by the database search. Full-text articles were retrieved for the included references and were subsequently screened for eligibility by 2 independent reviewers (R.T.P. and K.T.O.). Disagreements at this level were resolved by consensus.

Data extraction and risk of bias assessment

Data regarding baseline characteristics, sample size, interventions used, and outcomes of interest were abstracted independently onto a standardized form by 2 reviewers (R.T.P. and K.T.O.). The risk of bias in individual studies was assessed by using the Cochrane Risk of Bias assessment tool v2.0 by which studies were deemed to be at low, moderate, or high risk.19 Disagreements were resolved by consensus.

Data analysis

R statistical software (version 4.1.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) with the netmeta package (version 2.0-1) was used to conduct a random-effects frequentist network meta-analysis.20 Network graphs were used to visualize the network geometry. Mean difference (MD) was used to compare continuous outcomes and odds ratio (OR) to compare categorical outcomes. The 95% confidence interval (95% CI) was used. P-scores were used to rank the procedures. Heterogeneity and inconsistency were checked wherever appropriate. Data with a P < .10 were considered inconsistent. Data with an I2 value >50% were considered heterogeneous.

RFe delivery was performed using the Stretta device (Mederi Therapeutics, Inc, Greenwich, Conn, USA). GERD-HRQL questionnaire

GERD-HRQL scores could only be compared among the endoscopic procedures because the quality of life questionnaires used in the surgical literature were significantly different from the GERD-HRQL available online at www.igiejournal.org. Data from 5 studies (208 patients) were included in the analysis. The GERD-HRQL scores were significantly better in the TIF and RFe groups compared with sham and PPI groups, but there was no significant difference between the TIF and RFe groups (MD, –3.81; 95% CI, –10.65 to 3.04) (Fig. 2). TIF ranked higher than RFe (Supplementary Table 2, available online at www.igiejournal.org).

PPI use

While analyzing cessation of PPI use, 2 subnetworks were found, with 1 subnetwork including all the surgical fundoplication groups and 1 including endoscopic procedures and MSA. As such, we could not compare surgical fundoplication with endoscopic therapies with regard to PPI use. This was the only outcome that could be assessed for MSA. Data from 9 studies (552 patients) were used to assess this outcome. Although TIF, RFe, and MSA had significantly better ORs compared with PPIs, none of these procedures had significantly different outcomes compared with the sham group (Fig. 3). The ORs of cessation of PPI use with MSA compared with TIF (OR, 1.14; 95% CI, .07-18.34) and RFe (OR, 2.63; 95% CI, .11-63.79) were not significant (Fig. 3). TIF ranked highest for cessation of PPI use (Supplementary Table 3, available online at www.igiejournal.org).

Esophagitis and % time pH <4

Esophagitis and % time pH <4 at the end of the follow-up period were assessed based on data from 14 studies (1876 patients) and 10 studies (856 patients), respectively. None of the surgical fundoplication or endoscopic procedures (TIF and RFe) demonstrated significantly different outcomes compared with sham or PPI groups in terms of esophagitis (Fig. 4). Mean % time pH <4 at follow-up was not significantly different between the PPI group and the endoscopic (TIF and RFe) or surgical fundoplication procedures (Fig. 5). When compared with the sham group, all procedures except Nissen fundoplication, which had a better outcome, did not demonstrate significantly different mean % time pH <4 (Fig. 5).

Network forest plot for percentage of time with esophageal pH <4 on 24-hour pH monitoring. RFe, Radiofrequency energy; TIF, transoral incisionless fundoplication; PPI, proton pump inhibitor; MD, mean difference; CI, confidence interval.

Significant differences in the presence of esophagitis at the end of the follow-up period were not found between TIF and Nissen fundoplication (OR, 1.14; 95% CI, .17-7.74), anterior fundoplication (OR, .55; 95% CI, .21-15.79), or Toupet fundoplication (OR, 1.51; 95% CI, .21-10.93) (Fig. 4). Mean % time pH <4 was significantly greater (correlating to worse esophageal acid exposure) after TIF compared with Nissen fundoplication (MD, 4.72; 95% CI, .71-8.73), but this was not the case with either anterior (MD, –.16; 95% CI, –8.73 to 5.96) or Toupet fundoplication (MD, 3.62; 95% CI, –1.90 to 9.14) (Fig. 5).

There was no significant difference between RFe delivery and TIF, Nissen, Toupet, or anterior fundoplication procedures in terms of % time pH <4 or presence of esophagitis at last follow-up (Figure 4, Figure 5). Although anterior fundoplication ranked highest for the absence of esophagitis (Supplementary Table 4, available online at www.igiejournal.org), Nissen fundoplication ranked highest for % time pH <4 (Supplementary Table 5, available online at www.igiejournal.org).

LES pressure

Thirteen studies (1203 patients) were included in the analysis. Although endoscopic and surgical fundoplication procedures were associated with significantly higher LESP at follow-up compared with sham and PPI groups, RFe did not demonstrate significantly different outcomes (Fig. 6). LESP was significantly lower in patients who underwent RFe delivery compared with TIF (MD, –9.23; 95% CI, –13.89 to –4.58) and Nissen (MD, –9.00; 95% CI, –14.55 to –3.45), anterior (MD, –7.53; 95% CI, –13.35 to –1.71), and Toupet fundoplication (MD, –6.83; 95% CI, –12.50 to –1.15) (Fig. 6). LESP after RFe delivery was not significantly different from the PPI group (MD, .30; 95% CI, –4.29 to 4.89). On the other hand, no significant differences were found between TIF and Nissen, Toupet, or anterior fundoplication procedures. Nissen fundoplication ranked highest for LESP (Supplementary Table 6, available online at www.igiejournal.org).

Discussion

This is the first comprehensive review and network meta-analysis of the literature on available interventions for GERD. There are several major findings of our study. First, TIF and RFe provided significant symptom relief compared with PPI and sham groups. Second, LESP, % time pH <4, and presence of esophagitis after TIF were comparable with all surgical fundoplication procedures except for Nissen fundoplication, which is superior in terms of % time pH <4 only. Third, RFe was inferior to TIF and all surgical fundoplication procedures with regard to postintervention LESP. Finally, no significant difference was found between MSA and TIF or RFe in terms of PPI use.

The endoscopic procedures led to significantly better GERD-HRQL scores compared with the PPI and sham groups. Although this translated well to cessation of PPI use when compared with the PPI group, it did not when compared with sham procedure, where no significant difference was found. Possible explanations could be that patients might have been apprehensive to trial off PPIs despite symptom improvement and resolution in the intervention group or that they might have had an improvement in symptom scores but not significant enough to stop PPI therapy. MSA is a relatively new procedure with only limited RCT data available at this time, but it does seem to be effective compared with PPI therapy alone and will help patients come off PPI therapy.

Both surgical and endoscopic fundoplication procedures demonstrated significantly higher LESP compared with PPI and sham groups. This is not a surprising finding, and it shows that these fundoplication procedures accomplish what is expected of them. It should be noted that although not all studies that reported LESP specified whether the measured LESP was basal/resting or residual pressure, those that did reported basal/resting LESP. In addition, results comparing surgical fundoplication with a sham procedure should be cautiously interpreted because these sham procedures were endoscopic and so were not true sham procedures when compared with surgical procedures.

None of the endoscopic procedures or surgical fundoplication procedures demonstrated significantly different outcomes from PPI or sham groups in terms of esophagitis and % time pH <4 except for Nissen fundoplication, which had lower mean % time pH <4 compared with the sham group. Regarding esophagitis, the comparable outcomes could probably be explained by the exclusion of severe esophagitis in the endoscopic studies and PPIs being an effective therapy for esophagitis. Regarding % time pH <4, it has been demonstrated that TIF reduces the reflux volume but does not result in normalization of esophageal pH.42

Postprocedural LESP and esophagitis data for TIF were comparable with those of surgical fundoplication except for Nissen fundoplication, which demonstrated significantly lower % time pH <4 than TIF. One possible explanation for this discrepancy is the heterogeneity of the studies, which was also statistically evident. In addition, although patients undergoing surgical fundoplication also typically underwent same-session hiatal hernia repair, if indicated, this cannot be performed with TIF alone. The TIF studies excluded patients with a hiatal hernia >2 cm. This does not satisfy the transitivity assumption of network meta-analysis. However, this will lead to bias toward the surgical fundoplication procedures. Despite this potential bias toward surgical fundoplication, outcomes of TIF seemed to be comparable with surgical fundoplication except for % time pH <4. On the other hand, this concern for lack of transitivity did not manifest as inconsistency between the direct and indirect comparisons for all outcomes with closed loops. However, another management option that has been successfully used but was not included in the RCTs is simultaneous hiatal hernia repair and TIF. Performing a TIF instead of laparoscopic fundoplication avoids the extensive dissection and take-down of short gastric vessels that can potentially lead to splenic injuries.47

Regarding LESP measurement, data suggest that basal LESP decreases after paraesophageal hernia repair48 and that the presence of a hiatal hernia affects the measurement of LESP.49 Because our outcome was LESP at follow-up and not change in LESP, repair of a paraesophageal hernia during surgical fundoplication should not affect our interpretation of the results. In the endoscopic studies, the hiatal hernia was <2 cm and so will not significantly affect the LESP measurement. On the other hand, we do recognize that GERD is a complex disease and LESP is only one of the contributing factors.

Although it is believed that RFe delivery works partly by increasing LESP,11 based on our analysis this did not appear to be true. Although RFe and PPIs did not differ significantly in terms of LESP at follow-up, TIF and all surgical fundoplication procedures demonstrated significantly higher LESP compared with RFe. Again, RFe studies excluded patients with a hiatal hernia >2 or 3 cm based on their respective study protocols, resulting in lack of transitivity and bias toward surgical fundoplication procedures.

Quantitative analysis of adverse events could not be performed, given the heterogeneous reporting. A recent study analyzing the data from the Manufacturer and User Facility Device Experience database reported 131 patient adverse events associated with TIF over a 10-year period.50 Perforation, laceration, bleeding, and pleural effusions were the most common adverse events. Based on a review of the studies included in the meta-analysis, dysphagia is seldom observed after TIF. Regarding adverse events associated with MSA, a recent study based on the Manufacturer and User Facility Device Experience database found dysphagia, odynophagia, GERD, and device erosion as the most reported adverse effects.51 Device failure was noted secondary to removal because of recurrent symptoms, device opening unexpectedly, device migration, and magnetic bead separation. A recent network meta-analysis comparing various surgical fundoplication procedures demonstrated that 20%, 10%, and 17% of patients undergoing Nissen, Toupet, and anterior fundoplication, respectively, experienced dysphagia.52 Gas bloating has also been reported, with a significant association more so with Nissen fundoplication than anterior and Toupet fundoplication.52 Although anatomic failure because of wrap slippage, herniation, or disruption is a known adverse event associated with surgical fundoplication,53 it was only reported in a small case series with TIF.54

Although Gong et al55 recently published a network meta-analysis comparing the procedures included in our study, our study is comprehensive in addition to having significant differences compared with theirs. Gong et al included data from studies with devices that are no longer commercially available. Their analysis also included a smaller number of studies compared with ours because they included studies with surgical procedures only if they were compared with endoscopic procedures directly. The primary focus of the study by Gong et al seemed to be comparison of various procedures with PPI therapy alone, although they did briefly discuss comparison between various therapeutic procedures. Finally, the risk of bias analysis in Gong et al was not performed for each individual outcome as it was in our study.

The primary strength of this study is that it is a comprehensive review of the literature with a network meta-analysis comparing endoscopic and surgical therapies for GERD. Understandably, a controlled comparison trial between these procedures will be difficult, and this study provided data to this end. One of the limitations of our study is that this is a network meta-analysis for procedural therapies, which, by definition, cannot be blinded. We could not compare the endoscopic therapies and surgical fundoplication for our primary outcomes. Additionally, differences in individual procedural techniques contribute to heterogeneity and bias. The studies of endoscopic therapies, TIF and RFe, specifically excluded patients with a hiatal hernia >2 or 3 cm, and not all surgical trials had the same criteria. Statistically, the heterogeneity was only evident in % time pH <4 and LESP. Given that 3 outcomes had at least 1 study with high risk of bias and most outcomes had 50% of the studies with at least some concerns for risk of bias, the results should be interpreted with caution. Some of the discordances in the results are hard to explore or explain within the framework of our network meta-analysis because individual patient data are lacking. Finally, the available data are only for short-term outcomes for the endoscopic procedures; hence, we could not compare these therapies with the available long-term data for surgical therapies.

In conclusion, although TIF demonstrated outcomes comparable with all surgical fundoplication procedures in terms of LESP and esophagitis, it was found to be inferior to Nissen fundoplication only with regard to mean % time pH <4, and only limited long-term data support the durability of TIF. Patients with a hiatal hernia >2 cm may be better served with surgical fundoplication, which gives an opportunity to repair the hiatal hernia as well, until more data regarding simultaneous TIF and laparoscopic hiatal hernia repair emerge. That said, TIF should be considered on a case-by-case basis, especially in patients who do not want to undergo a surgical fundoplication. RFe is inferior to TIF and all surgical fundoplication procedures. More RCT data are required for MSA, especially compared with existing surgical procedures.

Multidisciplinary initiative to reduce 30-day readmissions in heart failure: a quality improvement perspective
Authors: Sharin Koshy Varghese, Tina Francis, Jassim Zaheen Shah, Poonam Gupta, Elavarasi Velusamy, Blessy Susan Varghese, Sathiya Priya Selvaraj, Lija Koshy Renyn, Indirani Savarimuthu, Menandro Mahinay, Maha Kassim Al-Amri, Afnan Abdul Azeem Arnoos, Parthasarathi Thangaraj, Sasikala Natarajan, Amr Mohamed Hamed Badr, Ashfaq Patel
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimHospitalisation for heart failure (HF) carries a high risk of readmission and adverse patient outcomes. We noticed a higher rate of readmissions for HF patients in a tertiary cardiac centre in Doha, Qatar. This study is focused on using a multidisciplinary and quality improvement approach to reduce the HF patients’ 30-day readmission rate to the high dependency unit C in Heart Hospital, Doha, Qatar, by 10% by December 2020 and 20% by December 2021.MethodsIn collaboration with the Institute for Healthcare Improvement, we worked on this initiative as a multidisciplinary team and tested several changes. A model for improvement framework was used and rapid, weekly, plan-do-study-act cycles were applied to test changes. Multiple measures were implemented serially, which included follow-up telephone consultations by a HF clinical nurse specialist 1 week after hospital discharge, early follow-up in a dedicated HF clinic (within 2 weeks of discharge), the availability of medical guidance over the telephone, comprehensive health coaching and education, patient functional activity engagement and cardiac depression screenings.ResultsThe readmission rate reduced from 25.5% in 2019 to 5.6% in 2021 (p<0.001). The results achieved have been sustained over time, with readmission rates recorded at 7.87% to date. The study demonstrated a decrease in 30-day readmission rates for HF patients after implementing a multidisciplinary quality improvement initiative.ConclusionsReducing readmission rates underscores the importance of comprehensive patient education, tailored care plans, consistent follow-up and integrated team-based care in managing HF patients. The success of this initiative highlights the potential of multidisciplinary strategies in improving patient outcomes in chronic conditions like HF.Keywords: Quality improvement, Patient safety, PDSA

Introduction

Heart failure (HF) is a chronic condition that occurs when the heart becomes weak or stiff and cannot sufficiently pump blood to meet the needs of the body.1 HF is potentially fatal and one of the most common causes of hospitalisations and readmissions globally2 and affects more than 64 million people worldwide.3 HF is a multifaceted and life-threatening syndrome characterised by significant morbidity and mortality, poor functional capacity and lower patient quality of life.4 Early diagnosis is crucial to ensure treatment is initiated quickly and to delay progression to overt disease.

Despite substantial advancements in the care of patients with HF, hospitalisations and readmissions remain significant challenges and adversely affect patient outcomes. Readmissions increase burdens on the healthcare system, adversely affect patient prognosis and cause substantial distress to patients and their families.5 Readmission rates for HF patients are high for various reasons, including worsening of the underlying heart condition, precipitating factors such as poor medication compliance, inadequate treatment, medication side effects, associated comorbidities, the lack of social and economic support and a lack of optimal follow-up.4 In particular, poor medication adherence is common among HF patients and can lead to HF exacerbations, reduced physical functioning, higher hospital readmission rates and increased mortality and morbidity.6 Therefore, medication adherence should be addressed in regular follow‐up visits and through evidence-based interventions to improve compliance, prevent adverse outcomes and reduce hospital readmission rates.3 4 6 HF patients also require support, even after discharge from the hospital and need close follow-up with their healthcare providers.5 Patient and family engagement, along with education focused on the importance of therapeutic compliance, can play a vital role in slowing disease progression.1 2 4 We observed a similar issue in our setting which is a tertiary cardiac care centre in Doha, Qatar. Therefore, we initiated a quality improvement project using a rapid cycle value improvement approach7 8 to reduce HF readmissions within 30 days of hospital discharge.

Problem description

HF patients are at high risk of returning to the hospital within 30 days of initial discharge. Often, the initiation of new medications, lifestyle changes or lack of understanding of the disease process can increase the risk of readmission.6 Our facility faced similar issues in the high dependency unit C (HDU C) of Heart Hospital. The baseline data were collected from January to May 2019. It reveals that the 30-day readmission rate for HF was 25.5%, which is higher than the global standard of 21%.5 9 A multidisciplinary team consisting of doctors, occupational therapists, physiotherapists, dietitians, clinical pharmacists, nurses and clinical nurse specialists (CNS) was formed in June 2019 to address this problem. The team followed a systematic approach to improve the quality and continuity of care, bridge care gaps, empower patients and families, and improve patient outcomes by initiating the quality improvement project to reduce the 30-day HF readmission rate.

Setting

Heart Hospital is a 114 inpatient and 28 emergency department-bed tertiary cardiac care facility in Doha, Qatar and is the flagship cardiac facility for Hamad Medical Corporation, a multihospital health system serving the nation of Qatar. The HDU C is a specialised HF inpatient unit with a 20-bed capacity providing service to patients with advanced HF in Heart Hospital. The unit discharges an average of 200 HF patients admitted with a principal diagnosis of HF per year.

Rationale

The rise in hospital readmissions is a global concern and adds a significant burden to patients, treatment costs and hospital resources.1 2 4 HF is a long-term condition, and treatment helps to keep symptoms under control, improves patient outcomes and maintains a good quality of life.10 However, there can be a gap in the care continuum during the transition period between hospitalisation and home care. Studies have shown that education and reinforcement are essential for reducing HF readmissions.11 Therefore, a 30-day HF readmission project was initiated in 2019 to improve tailored care, communication and care coordination. The expectation was the achievement of improved patient engagement and the involvement of patients and their caregivers in the discharge process to reduce avoidable readmissions.

Aim statement

To reduce the HF patients’ 30-day readmission rate to the HDU C in Heart Hospital, Doha, Qatar, by 10% by December 2020 and 20% by December 2021.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

All patients admitted and discharged from the HDU C unit with a principal diagnosis of HF and readmitted within 30 days with a principal diagnosis of HF to any of the facilities of the Hamad Medical Corporation (our parent organisation) over 3 years (2019–2021) were included in the initiative. Patients readmitted with other primary diagnoses, those who died, were discharged against medical advice, travelled abroad or had no follow-up in the HF clinic were excluded from the study. The exclusion is based on the failure to follow-up for the patients who left the country or did not show up for follow-up. Stanards for Quality Improvement Reporting Excellence (SQUIRE) 2.0 guidelines have been followed in writing this manuscript.

Methods

Using the Institute for Healthcare Improvement’s value improvement approach,7 this initiative comprised a multidisciplinary team that included physicians, CNS, nurses, dieticians, clinical pharmacists, physiotherapists and occupational therapists. Root cause and Pareto analyses for HF readmission were carried out by the task force in HDU C, and significant gaps were identified (online supplemental figures).

A model for improvement framework was used to drive continuous improvement. This model structures an improvement project by formulating an aim statement, defining measures to understand changes in the improvement and implementing an appropriate selection of changes to be tested. Changes were tested using weekly rapid online supplemental PDSA cycles.

The use of Quality improvement (QI) tools was instrumental in structuring the team’s approach, identifying barriers and implementing targeted solutions. We used cause-and-effect diagrams during root cause analysis sessions to explore contributing factors. Following that, a Pareto analysis was used to prioritise the most common causes of readmissions, helping to focus improvement efforts on the highest-impact areas. Results were displayed weekly on the unit quality board and discussed during the huddles. One specific example from the huddle discussions was the identification of inconsistent dietary education delivery. In response, a standardised checklist for nurse-led dietary education was developed, ensuring more comprehensive and reliable patient counselling.

Interventions

The team tried several interventions to improve the readmission rates. One of the major interventions was early HF appointment pathway implementation. HF patients initially attended routine follow-up appointments in a general cardiology clinic. A dedicated HF clinic was established earlier to follow these patients after hospital discharge, but there was no specific time frame for follow-up appointments. After implementing the initiative, all HF patients discharged from the inpatient units were given early appointments (within 1–2 weeks) following their hospital stay. Subsequent follow-up appointments in the clinics were given according to the patient’s clinical condition. Additionally, follow-up appointments in the HF clinic were conducted by the same primary HF consultant to ensure continuity of patient care.

To ensure best-care practices, it is essential to implement multidisciplinary healthcare team rounds that include physicians, clinical pharmacists, CNS, nurses, dietitians and occupational therapists. Therefore, at the time of admission, a dedicated clinical pharmacist evaluated and reviewed a patient’s home medications and medication compliance and educated the patient and primary caregiver during their hospital stay and predischarge. Education was individualised and provided according to each patient’s literacy level. The clinical pharmacist enacted collaborative medication management with the primary HF physician and the multidisciplinary team. Additionally, on hospital admission, HF patients were referred to a dietitian for nutritional screening and assessment. The assigned dietician provided each patient with a clear, detailed, personalised, evidence-based care plan. This was regularly reviewed and updated during each patient’s hospitalisation and shared with members of the healthcare team in the unit. Each patient was educated on nutritional therapy, including salt and fluid restrictions, and was provided with dietary educational brochures. Reinforcement concerning diet was also conducted by nurses and CNS.

An occupational therapist initiated cardiac depression screening (CDS) to identify psychosocial issues, particularly signs of depression that may affect adherence to medical advice or functional engagement. A standardised tool, the CDS,12,14 was used to assess patients. This scale allows for the early detection of depression and, in this study, facilitated a prompt psychiatrist referral for those with high scores in levels of depression. The CDS was essential for patients to receive appropriate care during the initial continuum of care initiative phase.

Occupational therapists assessed and monitored HF patients’ early active engagement in basic functional daily activities using the standardised functional independence measure tool15 to identify, monitor and improve patients’ functional status during inpatient hospital stays. Promoting early out-of-bed self-care and leisure activity participation was used to enhance patients’ functional status during discharge.

Within the HF team, the CNS was the focal point between patients, their families and healthcare professionals. They provided one-on-one inpatient education, counselling and coaching using an individualised teach-back approach. Continuity of care was provided after discharge via a telephone consultation within 1 week of discharge and more often if clinically required. The CNS was also available via their mobile phones (numbers were communicated to patients before discharge) for any postdischarge queries or clarification regarding medications, tests or clinic appointments. The Tele-Triage team in the HF initiative functioned as a remote care group within the HF team, providing timely assessments and management advice to patient’s post discharge.

Data displays

To keep momentum and create a sense of urgency, data were displayed on a unit quality board. This was easily accessible by the staff. Run charts and other information regarding the project were displayed in a central location. Every week, huddles were conducted to identify the success and barriers and address them.

Results

Table 1 presents the comparative readmission rates of HF patients over 3 years: 2019, 2020 and 2021. The data indicate a notable decline in readmission rates for HF patients from 2019 to 2021. In 2019, the readmission rate was 25.5%, which decreased to 13.9% in 2020 and further dropped to 5.6% in 2021. The χ2 value of 22.750 with a p value of 0.000 indicates a statistically significant difference in readmission rates over the 3 years.

Table 2 presents data on the readmission rates of HF patients by gender (male and female) over 3 years: 2019, 2020 and 2021. In 2019, male patients accounted for 66.7% of the total readmissions, while female patients made up 33.3%. The following year, 2020, saw a slight increase in the proportion of male patients, rising to 68.3%, with the percentage of female patients decreasing to 31.7%. In 2021, the trend slightly reversed, with male patients constituting 63.9% of the readmissions and female patients increasing to 36.1%. Overall, across the 3 years, male patients consistently represented a larger proportion of the total readmissions, with an average of 66.5%, compared with 33.5% for female patients. These data suggest a higher prevalence of readmissions among male HF patients compared with their female counterparts throughout the observed period. The χ2 shows that these differences are not statistically significant, with a value of 0.735 and a p value of 0.693. Thus, the result suggests that gender did not significantly affect readmission rates among HF patients.

Discussion

Statement of principal findings

Over the years 2019–2021, there was a notable decrease in HF patient readmission rates, falling from 25.5% in 2019 to 5.6% in 2021. In terms of gender distribution, male patients consistently represented a larger proportion of readmissions, averaging 66.5%, compared with 33.5% for female patients. Regarding age groups, the 65 & above and 45–64 age categories consistently made up around 44.7% of the patient population, while the younger 20–44 group grew from 5.0% in 2019 to 16.0% in 2021.

The identification of higher readmission rates among male patients prompted a focused root cause analysis to explore gender-specific barriers to postdischarge care. This led to the co-design of education resources with patients. Additionally, discharge checklists were modified to incorporate specific prompts that addressed issues that male patients frequently experienced, like stress related to returning to work and trouble adhering to medication regimens.

Similarly, identification of increasing readmissions among younger patients led to revised discharge counselling, focusing more on lifestyle adherence and employment-related medication compliance challenges.

These findings directly shaped both intervention modifications and our long-term sustainability strategy, allowing tailored approaches based on population segments.

The provision of medication knowledge to patients rose significantly from 26.2% in 2019 to 95.8% in 2021, and diet knowledge increased from 31.2% to 95.1% over the same period. Engagement in functional activities improved dramatically from 10.6% in 2019 to 96.5% in 2021. Education and health coaching saw a rise from 22.0% in 2019 to 95.1% in 2021, and cardiac depression screening surged from 0% in 2019 to 94.4% in 2021.

Strengths and limitations

One of the strengths of the study is the large sample size, which enhances the reliability and generalisability of the findings. Also, continuous monitoring and regular data collection provide a longer study period, allowing for a comprehensive assessment of trends and outcomes over time.

One limitation of this study was the lack of controls for external variables such as socioeconomic status and community support systems, which would have provided a more comprehensive understanding of the study’s results. These factors can significantly influence a patient’s ability to manage their condition and adhere to treatment plans post discharge, potentially impacting readmission rates. More research is recommended in this area.

An additional limitation of this study is the exclusion of patients who either left the country or did not attend follow-up appointments. Their outcomes remain untracked, which could potentially impact the accuracy and interpretation of the reported readmission rates.

Interpretation within the context of the wider literature

Reports from the literature have demonstrated that HF hospitalisation is followed by high mortality and readmission rates.16 In high-risk HF inpatients, a risk-score-guided, multidisciplinary, team-based care procedure was linked to lower 30-day readmissions and 30-day mortality.17 For HF patients, repeated hospitalisations are a reliable predictor of mortality.18 However, there was a significant reduction in 30-day readmissions for HF patients through a multidisciplinary approach, echoing findings in the existing literature. Similar to our initiative, the study19 has reported decreased hospitalisations and mortality with professional, multidisciplinary follow-ups. The emphasis on patient education, including medication and diet, mirrors interventions reported in the study,8 which also led to reduced HF readmissions. These comparisons underline the effectiveness of comprehensive patient education, multidisciplinary care and structured postdischarge follow-ups in managing HF patients, consistently resulting in improved outcomes.

A nurse-led, multidisciplinary strategy can enhance the quality of life of older patients with HF while lowering hospitalisations and medical expenses.20 Patients and their families should be included in the discharge planning process and be empowered and educated on self-care procedures.21 A multidisciplinary team approach is the gold standard for providing individualised, comprehensive treatment that adapts to patients’ changing requirements during their illness and seamlessly transitions between primary and secondary care.22

This initiative has been maintained consistently over time, with regular data collection. The readmission rates recorded for 2022, 2023 and up to June 2024 are 7.83%, 7.09% and 7.92%, respectively, demonstrating its sustainability. Reduced readmissions have freed up bed capacity in the HDU C unit, allowing more efficient patient flow and optimised use of specialised cardiac care beds.

Implications for policy, practice and research

Based on our study, we recommend further research and quality improvement projects targeting the monitoring and enhancement of patient compliance in outpatient HF clinics and the community setting. Further studies should also assess the impact of the multidisciplinary team approach at 6 months and 12 months post discharge. Incorporating a home healthcare team could also be part of a longer-term project.

Conclusion

The incidence of HF readmission rates remains high. It is incumbent on multidisciplinary teams to evaluate their HF practices and incorporate the most current knowledge regarding patient care. In this study, a multidisciplinary approach and targeted interventions, including a specialised care plan, patient medication knowledge, patient diet knowledge, functional activity engagement, education and health coaching, cardiac depression screening and ensuring scheduled follow-up appointments before hospital discharge, led to substantial reductions in the readmission rates of our HF patients. An integrated and innovative approach to managing HF patients based on our interventions can contribute to improved patient outcomes, including reduced morbidity rates, improved functional status, a better quality of life, enhanced compliance, reduced re-hospitalisation rates, reduced costs and prolonged survival.

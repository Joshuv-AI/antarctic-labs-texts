MRSA nasal carriage among Taiwanese senior high school students and a narrative review of local metadata
Authors: Cheng Yu Lee, Chen Wei Tsai, Chen Han Shih, Qi-Yan Li, Yu Ping Fang, Yu Fen Chang, Chun Yi Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveNasal carriage of methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) is a critical marker for pathogen transmission. This study investigated the MRSA carriage rate and risk factors among Taiwanese senior high school students, comparing findings with historical local data.MethodsA cross-sectional prospective surveillance study was conducted at a Taiwanese senior high school. Following informed consent, nasal swabs and questionnaires were collected. S. aureus isolates were tested for methicillin resistance via the cefoxitin disc method. MRSA isolates underwent SCCmec typing, multi-locus sequence typing (MLST), and virulent genes detection. Results were compared against 34 PubMed-retrieved Taiwanese studies (2004-2026).ResultsAmong 100 participants, the S. aureus colonization rate was 50%, with a 7% MRSA prevalence. All MRSA isolates carried SCCmec type IV; only one was PVL-positive. The identified sequence types were ST45 (n = 4), ST59 (n = 2), and ST508 (n = 1). Literature review revealed MRSA carriage rates in Taiwan ranging from 1.3% to 20.3%, peaking in nursing home residents. While ST59 was historically the predominant clone, ST45 emerged in healthcare settings in 2014 and has become increasingly prevalent since 2019.ConclusionDespite being a healthy demographic, senior high school students exhibit a high S. aureus carriage rate, with MRSA levels comparable to the general Taiwanese adult population. ST45 and ST59 have emerged as the two predominant colonizing MRSA strains in this group, reflecting a shift in local molecular epidemiology.Keywords: Staphylococcus aureus, methicillin resistant, Colonization, Adolescent

Introduction

Staphylococcus aureus (S. aureus) is a common commensal microbe and a significant pathogen responsible for most skin and soft tissue infections globally. Methicillin-resistant S. aureus (MRSA) remains a persistent concern in healthcare settings. Besides skin infections, MRSA is also a notable pathogen causing bloodstream infections, pneumonia, and surgical site infections. S. aureus is commonly located in warm and moist areas of the body. Nasal colonization of MRSA is identified as a risk factor for developing related infections [1]. Pre-admission screening and decolonization efforts have been implemented to minimize the risk of subsequent infection [2].

The pooled S. aureus and MRSA colonization rates among children were reported as 25.1% and 3.4-5%, respectively [3,4]. For the adult population, the MRSA colonization rate was 4.6% among healthy adults [5], but a higher rate was observed in populations with medical comorbidities. Two longitudinal studies focusing on adolescents and young adults indicated a wide range of MRSA carriage rates from 3.7% to 29% and noted that the colonization rate fluctuated over time [6,7]. Both studies identified a positive association between colonization and the development of skin infections. Given that each country has unique MRSA prevalence and colonization sequence types, studies must be interpreted independently and cautiously.

Taiwan has a significant burden of MRSA in healthcare settings. Various studies have investigated the nasal carriage rate of MRSA across different populations, including children, healthcare workers, long-term care workers, and patients in intensive care units [[8], [9], [10], [11], [12], [13], [14], [15]]. These studies have generally reported high carriage rates, with some studies indicating that up to one in five individuals in long-term care facilities are carriers [16,17]. High school students, due to their physical activity and crowded living environments, may also contribute to the spread of S. aureus. However, data on the high school age group is limited. This study conducted during the COVID-19 pandemic aims to investigate the MRSA nasal carriage rate and related genotype among high school students in Taiwan. Additionally, we conducted a narrative review of nasal MRSA colonization studies for various populations in Taiwan.

Methods and materials

Clinical sample collection

A cross section prospective survey was conducted at a single high school in Taichung, Taiwan between February and April 2023. This school is a public male high school and comprises 2100 students. Nasal swabs were collected after obtaining consents from students and their parents or guardians. The swabs were sent to the microbiology laboratory of Chang Bing Show Chwan Memorial Hospital for subsequent cultivation and identification. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Show Chwan Memorial Hospital (IRB No. 1111103).

MRSA identification and antimicrobial susceptibility tests

S. aureus identification is based on colony morphology, Gram stain, positive coagulase tests, and presence of β-hemolysis on tryptic soy agar plates containing 5% sheep's blood. Cefoxitin disk-diffusion method was used to detect methicillin resistance, according to the guidance of Clinical and Laboratory Standard Institutes (CLSI).

Antimicrobial susceptibility test was carried out on BD Phoenix™ M50 instrument, including eight antibiotics: erythromycin, clindamycin, trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, ciprofloxacin, gentamicin, levofloxacin, fusidic acid, and vancomycin. The susceptible breakpoints of each antibiotic were set up by the manufacturer, and in line with the CLSI recommendations.

Molecular analysis of MRSA isolates

Genomic DNAs were extracted from MRSA isolates by using QIAamp DNA Blood Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany), in accordance with manufacturer's instructions. PCR protocols adapted to identify SCCmec type, multi-locus sequence type, spa type, agr type, and detection of Panton-Valentine leucocidin (PVL) and virulent genes were described elsewhere. Designation of sequence type was followed by the instructions of the website PubMLST (https://pubmlst.org/organisms/staphylococcus-aureus).

Taiwan MRSA nasal carriage metadata

A systematic search was conducted in the PubMed database (2004-2026) using the keywords ‘MRSA’, ‘colonization’, ‘nasal’, ‘carriage’, and ‘Taiwan’. Studies were included based on the following criteria: (1) a sample size exceeding 100 cases, and (2) the inclusion of sequence type (ST) analysis. Ultimately, 34 studies encompassing diverse populations were enrolled for comparative analysis.

Result

MRSA carriage in high school students in this study

In the present study, 100 nasal swab samples were collected from individual students with inform consents. S. aureus was found in 50 samples (50%), with 7 samples testing positive for MRSA (7%). All 7 MRSA isolates contained SCCmec type IV, but only one was positive for the PVL gene; one was positive for tst-1 gene; and two were positive for sec gene. These 7 MRSA isolates belonged to three sequence types: ST45 (4), ST59 (2), and ST508 (1). The ST45 and ST508 MRSA isolates were sensitive to eight antibiotics, while the ST59 MRSA was resistant to erythromycin and gentamicin. MRSA acquisition and colonization in Taiwanese children

Of 34 retrieved studies from Taiwan, two research studies focused on the acquisition of S. aureus colonization in neonates’ post-birth. One longitudinal study examining the MRSA colonization rate during the first two years of life found that approximately two-third of Taiwanese children had been colonized with MRSA by age two, with 75% of cases occurring within the first four months [12]. Another birth cohort mother-baby paired study also presented similar findings and pointed out a high rate (30.5%) of MRSA carriage at 1-month of age [18]. A multi-institutional study revealed that the nasal MRSA colonization rate among babies who stayed in the NICU was 4.4% [19].

Table 1 comprised 6 studies investigating the MRSA carriage among Taiwanese children >2 years, three childhood nasal carriage studies conducted over two periods (2004-2006, 2005-2006, and 2007-2009) observed a decremental S. aureus nasal carriage rate, while noting an increase in MRSA colonization from 7.3% to 15.1% [[20], [21], [22]]. Three studies conducted on healthy children during 2005 and 2010 reported MRSA nasal carriage rates ranging from 5.2% to 10.2% [9,10,23]. In contrast to our study, low carriage rates (1.54-3.7%) were observed in three studies involving medical or college students or athletes [6,11,24].

Nasal MRSA colonization rate in Taiwanese adult population

Table 2 shows various studies that examined nasal MRSA colonization among adults in Taiwan. For adults seeking emergency care, the carriage rate ranged from 2.12 to 6.1%, and a higher carriage rate was found in those with underlying medical conditions [15,25]. Patients with diabetes mellitus, chronic kidney disease and current percutaneous tube usage were prone to be colonized by MRSA. The MRSA carriage rate of patients with end stage renal disease was reported from 3.8 to 5.9% [[26], [27], [28], [29]]. Among diabetes patients, the MRSA colonization rates were 5.4% for those with diabetic foot and 1.7% for those without diabetic foot [30]. In the HIV population, the MRSA carriage rate was 3.4-4.4%, and factors associated with MRSA colonization included female gender, injection drug use, smoking, hepatitis C virus carrier status, cancer, and antibiotic use within the past year [31,32]. As anticipated, the highest carriage rates were found among nursing home residents, nursing home staff, and ICU patients, with rates as high as 17.5% to 32% [16,17,19,33,34]. In contrast, the MRSA carriage rate of hospital janitors was noted as 3.6% [35].

Discussion

This study investigated MRSA carriage in Taiwanese male high school students during the later stages of the COVID-19 pandemic. The study discovered that 50% of the students had S. aureus in their nasal passages, and 7% carried MRSA — a rate higher than what is typically reported among healthy adults [36,37,39,40,42]. The most common MRSA genotype was ST45, which differs from the previously recognized ST59 Taiwan clone. Research published since 2014 shows that ST45 MRSA has become increasingly prevalent in Taiwan, affecting a notable portion of emergency room visitors, hospitalized patients, as well as residents and staff of long-term care facility [15,17,30,34].

Multiple studies of invasive MRSA infections have documented shifts in predominant MRSA genotypes in Taiwan over time [43,44]. Prior to the emergence of community-associated MRSA (CA-MRSA), ST239 was the dominant genotype and strongly related to hospital associated infections. In the 2000s, CA-MRSA ST59 became increasingly prevalent and replaced the dominance of ST239 MRSA, and after 2020, ST8 MRSA (genetically closed to USA300) emerged as the leading genotype. Analysis of metadata from Taiwan MRSA nasal carriage studies conducted over the years similarly demonstrates a shift in genotype patterns. ST59 MRSA was the sole predominant nasal colonized strain over one decade but gradually replaced by ST45. A recent study at one institute examining MRSA nasal carriage over different time periods found that while ST59 strains were predominant in 2012, by 2023, ST45 and ST8 had become the most common colonizing strains [13].

Our study also found that the rate of nasal S. aureus carriage remains high, even as COVID-19 mitigation measures have been widely implemented. Similarly, Liu et al. reported a consistently high rate of MRSA carriage among inpatients in a hospital setting in 2023 [13]. Research from Israel and France on S. pneumoniae nasal carriage showed that the rate remained unchanged during the pandemic, whilst the S. pneumoniae pneumonia and invasive disease declined significantly [45,46]. By contrast, the evidence regarding the influence of COVID-19 on invasive MRSA infection is controversial. One research from the USA found COVID-19 fueled increase in hospital associated MRSA bacteremia [47], but studies from Japan and China showed an opposite trend of MRSA infection during the pandemic [48,49].

Pediatric populations play a pivotal role in acquisition and spread of MRSA, and the overall pooled estimated global prevalence of MRSA colonization in pediatric was 3.4-5% and associated genotypes were diverse [3,4]. Female, recent hospitalization and antibiotic exposure are three recognized risk factors for pediatric MRSA colonization. In Taiwan, day care attendance and crowded family environment are another risk factors for MRSA carriage [9,10]. Instead, Breast feeding and colonization with S. pneumoniae were protective against MRSA colonization [10]. For adults, cancer, recent antibiotic use, pneumonia, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, current MRSA infection, and medical ICU admission were independent predictors for nasal carriage of MRSA [31,33]. Prior use of antipseudomonal penicillins, antifungals, and the presence of a nasogastric tube were independent risk factors for MRSA colonization during ICU stay [50]. A history of congestive heart failure, recent admission to a nursing home, and nasogastric tube feeding within the past three months all raise the risk of MRSA colonization in patients with end-stage renal disease [27].

Nasal colonization may indicate an increased risk of MRSA-related infections, and multiple studies have demonstrated that 80% of infectious isolates share clonal similarity with colonization isolates from the same patient [2,19,30]. Furthermore, research suggests that implementing proactive screening and decolonization strategies could reduce unnecessary antibiotic use and decrease subsequent MRSA infections [1,51,52]. These infection control policies are generally effective in areas with low MRSA prevalence, but not in Taiwan. Studies from Taiwan, which align with research conducted in other locations, show that MRSA is commonly present among patients in both pediatric and adult intensive care units, as well as in long-term care facilities [17]. Additionally, healthcare staff and nurses have been identified as carriers of MRSA, which may contribute to its spread within medical environments [15,16,53]. A high level of awareness and rigorous contact precautions are required in Taiwan.

This study was limited by a small sample size consisting exclusively of male participants from a single high school, which restricts the generalizability of the findings to the broader population of Taiwanese senior high schools. Additionally, this study was a single-time-point surveillance, which constraints hinder direct comparisons with other studies and preclude an assessment of the COVID-19 pandemic's impact on carriage rate. However, colonization of S. aureus remains common in school-age children and could play a role in its transmission within the community [54]. Moreover, the metadata review along with this study provides valuable insights into MRSA genotype variation across different environments, age groups, and time periods in Taiwan.

In summary, high school students, representing an active and generally healthy demographic, exhibit a notably elevated prevalence of nasal colonization by S. aureus and MRSA. The predominant MRSA genotypes identified were ST45 and ST59, consistent with the observed evolutionary trends of MRSA strains in Taiwan. Given that the prevalence and molecular epidemiology of MRSA may fluctuate across various study periods, and with ST8 MRSA becoming more common in hospital environments in Taiwan, ongoing surveillance of colonized genotypes within diverse populations remains essential.

Evaluation of a Novel Antioxidant-supplemented Sperm Wash Medium on Human Sperm Function: A Pilot Study
Authors: Sukanth Kumar Enmozhi, Sasikala Natarajamani, Gunapriya Raghunath, Jerrine Joseph, Infant Xavier, Theepan Raaj, S. Pradeep, Niloy Raj Kalita, Sumathy Arockiasamy, Radha Vembu, Ananda Rani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Density gradient sperm preparation may induce oxidative stress, impairing sperm function and deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) integrity.Aim:To formulate and test an antioxidant-supplemented sperm wash with proteins.Settings and Design:An in vitro pilot proof of concept with a paired sample design.Materials and Methods:Ten normal and 10 abnormal semen samples were processed by double density gradient centrifugation of the sperm and then split into two portions that were placed in basal Ham’s F10 and in an antioxidant-enriched medium (L-carnitine, N-acetyl cysteine and D-aspartic acid). Reactive oxygen species (ROS), motility, total motile sperm count (TMSC), morphology, teratozoospermic index (TZI) and DNA fragmentation were evaluated. High-performance liquid chromatography, nuclear magnetic resonance, molecular docking and molecular dynamics analyses were done.Statistical analyses used:ANOVA with Šidák/Tukey post hoc variations.Results:ROS (P<0.0001) and DNA fragmentation index (P< 0.001) levels were found to be significantly reduced with antioxidant supplementation. The motility with progressive movement and TMSC was still maintained (P< 0.01), especially in teratozoospermic specimens. Sperm recovery was similar for all media, and there were slight changes in morphology and in TZI. In silico results showed that binding to sperm function-associated protein targets was stable.Conclusion:The findings of this study indicate that antioxidant supplementation can decrease oxidative stress and improve sperm function during preparation. Studies with more subjects that include fertilisation, embryo development, pregnancy and live-birth outcomes are needed to validate.

INTRODUCTION

The contribution of sperm cells towards providing half of the genetic makeup for fertilised oocytes is associated with their high sensitivity to stress factors.[1] After ejaculation, they face exposure to different toxic agents, their metabolites and oxidative stresses that impair their ability to reproduce.[23] Oxidative stress, which results from excessive amounts of reactive oxygen species (ROS) exceeding the limited capacity of antioxidants, becomes one of the most important mechanisms in this regard. While low levels of ROS do not impede any biological processes in male gametes, including spermatogenesis, capacitation and fertilisation, high levels of these compounds trigger lipid peroxidation, protein denaturation and deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) damage.[4]

Human cells employ enzymatic and non-enzymatic antioxidants to counterbalance ROS. Enzymatic systems include catalase, glutathione (GSH) peroxidase, GSH reductase and superoxide dismutase, while non-enzymatic agents such as vitamins C and E, GSH, coenzyme Q10, α-lipoic acid and thioredoxins provide complementary protection.[56] The Cochrane Review Antioxidants for Male Subfertility highlights that a range of antioxidants have been trialled, with some studies reporting improved motility and DNA integrity, though results remain inconsistent.[78] Clinical use is further constrained by the systemic distribution of orally administered antioxidants, which reduces their bioavailability in reproductive tissues, and by the fact that semen concentrations remain poorly defined.[910] Moreover, commercially available antioxidant-enriched sperm wash media are scarce and generally lack a protein-targeted mechanistic basis.[111213]

A mechanism-based strategy with protein molecules offers the potential solution. Important sperm proteins such as Septin 4, Septin 7 and soluble adenylyl cyclase (sAC) control structure, energy metabolism and genetic material stability. Septin 4 controls sperm tail structure and mitochondrial activity, and its shortage is linked to poor movement and higher sensitivity to oxidative stress.[141516] Septin 7 maintains flagellum structure and mitochondrial placement, increasing energy effectiveness and limiting ROS-induced damage.[171819] sAC affects capacitation and mobility via (cAMP) Cyclic Adenosine Mono Phosphate signalling and indirectly maintains DNA stability.[2021] Sildenafil and anastrozole served as comparisons for the cGMP- and hormone-based models, respectively.[2223242526]

By applying molecular docking and dynamics to identify antioxidant–protein interactions, followed by in vitro validation, this study aims to develop a novel protein-targeted antioxidant sperm washing medium. Our goal is to assess whether this formulation can reduce oxidative injury and preserve motility and DNA integrity during density gradient preparation. This work is presented as a laboratory proof-of-concept in gamete physiology, rather than a clinical or industrial in vitro fertilisation study and offers new insights into strategies for safeguarding sperm function during fertilisation.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design and ethics

The pilot study has included samples of semen taken from normozoospermic subjects (n = 10) and teratozoospermic subjects (n = 10), who have been categorised based on the World Health Organization (WHO) criteria.[27] Since it is a pilot study, the number of subjects corresponds with that of typical pilot trials aims at assessing the feasibility and trend of biological parameters. In this study, a paired sample design has been used where the same subject can act as the control group. This methodological choice is suitable for a pilot study before carrying out more extensive studies. The study has been approved by the Institutional Ethical Committee (IEC-NI/23/AUG/89/69), and written consent has been obtained from the subjects as directed by the Helsinki Declaration.

Antioxidant selection and medium preparation

The docking simulation findings played an important role in choosing the three antioxidants (L-carnitine, N-acetyl cysteine [NAC] and D-aspartic acid [DAA]), which exhibited stable docking with the protein targets. They were added to a modified Ham’s F10 medium.[28] The choice of these compounds was reinforced through a combination of docking simulation and molecular dynamics feasibility together with translation considerations, which include compatibility with the Ham’s F10 medium, proven safety in the field of andrology, stability of the formulation proved via high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) and nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) analysis, and commercial availability.

Analytical verification of medium stability

To ensure the integrity of supplemented antioxidants, the medium was characterised using HPLC and NMR. One representative chromatogram is presented in the main text, while additional spectra and calibration data are provided in Supplementary File 1.

Semen collection and processing

Semen samples were collected by masturbation after 3–5 days of abstinence and allowed to liquefy at 37°C for 30 min. Processing was performed using a two-step discontinuous density gradient centrifugation.[1113] After centrifugation, the motile sperm fraction was washed and resuspended either in basal medium or in the antioxidant-enriched medium. The whole density gradient processes of their respective wash, post-wash with individual medium of incubation for time durations 15 min, 30 min, 45 min and 60 min were incubated at 37°C.

Functional assessments of spermatozoa

After the density gradient centrifugation procedure, each sample was divided into basal and antioxidant-containing media for analysis. The sperm motility was determined according to the WHO standard methodology[27] by counting the progressive and total number of motile cells. The formation of ROS was assessed by the NBT test[29] as optical density. Sperm DNA damage was evaluated by the sperm chromatin dispersion technique.[30] The halos were classified, allowing calculating the DNA fragmentation index. Three embryologists independently scored each parameter. Good inter-observer agreement was achieved (Cohen’s κ = 0.81).

In silico screening of candidate antioxidants

Septin-4, Septin-7 and sAC were key molecules for molecular docking and dynamics. The Septin-4 protein plays a role in the maintenance of the annulus, mitochondrial integrity and antioxidant defences, with defects linked to motility deficiency and oxidative injury.[141516] Septin-7 helps in mitochondrial targeting and ROS scavenging.[171819] sAC (ADCY10) produces cAMP, which is important for capacitation, hyperactivation and DNA preservation.[2021] These molecules are oxidative stress-sensitive proteins. Antioxidants docking identified stabilising complexes, whereas sildenafil and anastrozole were used as controls.[2223242526]

Statistical analyses

Data were analysed using GraphPad Prism (version 8.0, GraphPad Software, San Diego, USA). We first analysed the data using repeated-measures Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) with Šidák/Tukey post hoc corrections. Sphericity was checked with Mauchly’s test, and where the assumption was violated, Greenhouse–Geisser corrections were applied. To confirm the robustness of our results and account for within-subject correlations, we also fitted linear mixed-effects models, specifying a random intercept for each subject and fixed effects for time, medium and their interaction. Effect sizes are reported as partial η² for the ANOVA and as marginal/conditional R² for the mixed models, together with 95% confidence intervals and exact P values.

RESULTS

Antioxidant selection and medium preparation

Using molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulation, a modified Ham’s F10 media containing L-carnitine (1.76 mM),[31] NAC (50 μM)[32] and the DAA (concentration calculated[33]) were developed. The following antioxidants were chosen based on previous experience with their use in Ham’s F10 and on their stable interaction with target proteins and their various other considerations: first, their formulation stability; second, their success in the previous use in Ham’s F10; and third, their ability to interact with the target proteins. Osmolality was maintained at the recommended level by WHO (290 mOsm/kg),[27] and the pH was adjusted to 8.00 with citric acid and sodium carbonate in HEPES-buffered conditions.[28]

Pilot incubations confirmed that the selected concentrations reduced the amount of ROS, but did not affect motility.[3435] A buffer-only formulation without antioxidant supplementation had not been shown to have similar effects. All subsequent comparisons were made with mediums which were not supplemented, which served as the control.

Analytical verification of medium stability

HPLC and NMR analyses were performed to assess the successful incorporation and stability of L-carnitine, NAC and DAA in the modified Ham’s F10 formulation. Typical chromatograms are presented in Figure 1 and complete data in the Supplementary File 1.

Functional assessments of spermatozoa

Both biochemical and functional sperm parameters were altered by antioxidant supplementation, whereas no effect was observed on sperm recovery. This significantly lowered ROS level, with the highest reduction seen in teratozoospermic semen samples (P < 0.0001) compared with basal Ham’s F10 medium.[29] Sperm concentration prior to and after washing was not significantly different for different media, and thus, the recovery efficiency was similar. In all groups, motility decreased with time, but it was less in antioxidant-supplemented media, especially in the teratozoospermic groups. Progressive motility and the number of motile sperm was more well preserved over the 60-min incubation period (overall P < 0.01). Results of the regression analyses showed that motility was better retained (group slope better: F =12.2, P = 0.000016).

The morphologic evaluations using WHO criteria revealed slight differences in morphologically normal spermatozoa between the groups. Consecutive teratozoospermic index (TZI) values showed a consistent decrease in antioxidant-treated teratozoospermic samples in comparison to basal medium samples, and TZI was found to be relatively stable for normozoospermic samples.

A higher number of samples with large and medium halos, and a lower number of samples with small/no halo, in the samples with antioxidants was also shown by DNA integrity assessment and DNA sperm chromatin dispersion. DNA fragmentation index values, on the other hand, showed significant decreases in antioxidant-supplemented groups (at the overall level, feeding antioxidants for a period of 40 days significantly decreased DNA fragmentation index values; P < 0.001). The same trends were found in teratozoospermic samples (P > 0.05 following morphology adjustment for some of the comparisons). In silico screening of candidate antioxidants

We have identified L-carnitine, NAC and DAA as interactants of proteins involved in the function of sperm, such as Septin-4, Septin-7 and sAC, by molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulations.[1415161718192021] These proteins have been linked to protection, preservation and function of the sperm structure, activity of mitochondria, regulation of oxidative stress and the cAMP pathway in sperm capacitation. Comparative docking of sildenafil with anastrozole showed that both drugs exhibited different docking patterns, indicating that the observed associations were different from typical cGMP-or hormone-mediated interactions.[2223242526] The detailed scores for docking, affinities and molecular dynamics trajectories are included in the Supplementary File 2. The in silico results were in line with the selection of these antioxidants, which would constitute the experimental sperm wash medium.

DISCUSSION

The outcomes presented here are based on initial in vitro data collected in a pilot study and are not indicative of clinical effectiveness. The aim was to determine if a solution enriched with antioxidants can protect the spermatozoa when preparing double-density gradients, which induce oxidative stress. It was found that there was a decrease in the generation of ROS without compromising DNA integrity and motility. It was also confirmed that minimal biochemical changes were sufficient to ensure the stability of spermatozoa function.

Septin-4, Septin-7 and sAC, which are important factors regulating sperm structure stability and intracellular signal transduction,[1415162021] were studied. It was observed via docking and molecular dynamics simulations that L-carnitine and NAC bind to septin-4/7, while DAA binds to sAC. These mechanisms can explain reduced production of ROS, less DNA breakage and increased motility stabilisation. Despite the relatively low energy levels required for the bindings, the molecular dynamics simulation trajectories were highly stable throughout the time frames of interest.[2223242526]

The antioxidant mix, L-carnitine, NAC and DAA, was chosen for complementary and biologically grounded reasons. L-carnitine supports mitochondrial energy metabolism and sustains motility under oxidative challenge.[36] NAC replenishes GSH, directly scavenges ROS and protects both membranes and chromatin from oxidative damage.[37] DAA modulates the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis, stimulating spermatogenesis, testosterone production and sperm motility.[38] Together, they provided broad protection across the main domains of sperm health: reducing oxidative stress, maintaining motility and supporting genomic stability. The modest improvements we observed in morphology, particularly reductions in the TZI, likely reflected selective recovery during density gradient preparation rather than reversal of intrinsic spermatogenic defects, consistent with the known relative stability of morphology compared to motility and DNA integrity.

By restricting our analysis to normozoospermic and teratozoospermic samples, we ensured that oxidative stress was the primary differentiating factor. Normozoospermia provided a functional baseline, while teratozoospermia, with its known vulnerability to oxidative imbalance, allowed us to test whether antioxidants could provide measurable protection. More severe pathologies such as asthenozoospermia or oligozoospermia were deliberately excluded, as these conditions often involve low counts or profoundly impaired motility, which could confound interpretation. These groups remain important targets for future work.

Across all measures, sperm behaved differently depending on the medium. In basal Ham’s F10, ROS rose sharply, and DNA fragmentation increased, particularly in teratozoospermic samples. By contrast, antioxidant supplementation produced a significant reduction in ROS (P < 0.0001), a lower DNA fragmentation index (P < 0.001) and better preservation of progressive motility (P < 0.01). Non-progressive motility declined less steeply, and immotile fractions increased more slowly, compared with basal medium. TMSC also remained higher in antioxidant-treated samples. Together, these outcomes highlight that the improvements observed were functional and biologically meaningful, rather than artefacts of recovery efficiency, since sperm concentration was unaffected.

Our results reinforce a well-established link between oxidative stress and impaired sperm quality.[3940] The particular benefit observed in teratozoospermic samples is consistent with reports that morphologically abnormal sperm are especially vulnerable to oxidative damage.[41] The strong reduction in DNA fragmentation accords with previous evidence that antioxidant formulations can restore chromatin integrity in both in vitro preparations and subfertile men.[4243] Improvements in motility echo recent work suggesting that antioxidants protect mitochondrial function and help sustain forward progression under oxidative pressure.[1] Collectively, these findings support the idea that tailored antioxidant media could be particularly valuable for sperm populations most at risk of oxidative stress.

Clinically, this work matters because oxidative imbalance and DNA fragmentation have been strongly associated with reduced fertilisation, poorer embryo development and lower pregnancy rates in assisted reproduction.[3630] Recent studies suggest that antioxidant therapy can reduce fragmentation, modestly improve motility and in some cases improve assisted reproduction outcomes.[4445] By helping maintain redox balance and stabilising key protein targets, antioxidant-enriched media may increase the number of viable, DNA-intact sperm available for fertilisation – an effect that could be particularly meaningful in teratozoospermic patients.

We agree that there are several limitations that have been identified. First, the pilot trial involved only 10 subjects per group and did not have sufficient power for detecting small effects and performing subgroup analysis; hence, the outcomes should be carefully considered. Only in vitro sperm variables were evaluated, and fertilisation, embryonic growth and live births were not assessed. Despite using validated levels for antioxidants, further research is required to confirm the safety and effectiveness of their use in different clinical scenarios. Finally, more precise methods for evaluating DNA damage, such as TUNEL or sperm chromatin structure assay, were not used.

CONCLUSION

Antioxidative supplementation to sperm wash solution appears to be able to lower the level of oxidative stress, protect sperm motility and preserve the integrity of sperm DNA during sperm preparation. Preliminary findings with a small number of patients are confirmed in vitro and in silico, but should be interpreted with caution due to the experimental design. While there is hope to take up this research in the future for clinical use, further appropriately powered validation studies involving more diverse infertility phenotypes and clinically relevant outcomes such as fertilisation, embryo development, pregnancy and live-birth rates are needed.

A retrospective analysis of empathy-centric care strategies on negative emotional states and expectation levels in acute heart failure patients
Authors: Zhimin Guan, Qiong Wang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
The objective of this research was to evaluate the impact of an empathy-centered care approach on the intensity of negative emotional states and levels of expectation in patients experiencing acute heart failure. A retrospective analysis was conducted on the clinical data of 106 patients presenting with emergent heart failure. The patients were bifurcated into a control group (53 patients) and an intervention group (53 patients) based on their respective care management plans. Following the intervention, the intervention group demonstrated decreased values in left ventricular end-systolic diameter and left ventricular end-diastolic diameter, and increased left ventricular ejection fraction compared to the control group (P < .05). Additionally, the 6-minute walk test used for cardiopulmonary rehabilitation and the 30 seconds sit-to-stand exercise revealed superior results in the intervention group (P < .05). Positive scores on the Positive Negative Affect Scale, the various dimensions of the Herth Hope Index Scale, the Psychological Resilience Scale, and the Chinese Cultural Modified Minnesota Living with Heart Failure Questionnaire were notably higher in the intervention group, whereas negative scores on the Positive Negative Affect Scale and scores on the Self-Assessment Scale of Anxiety were comparatively lower than those in the control group (P < .05). Implementing an empathy-based care approach can bolster cardiac function, augment functional fitness, mitigate negative emotional states, elevate expectation levels, enhance psychological resilience, improve quality of life, and decrease complication rates in patients with acute heart failure.

1. Introduction

Heart failure, a prevalent condition in emergency medicine, typically results from the advanced stages of coronary heart disease and other cardiovascular ailments, leading to either diastolic, systolic, or both types of heart dysfunction. Such a dysfunction impedes the heart’s capacity to adequately pump venous blood, triggering a venous blood congestion. This condition subsequently fails to satisfy the body’s fundamental demand for oxygen and nutrients, consequently causing respiratory distress, fluid retention, and limited physical activity, which pose a substantial threat to patients’ well-being.[1,2]

Currently, no definitive clinical cure for heart failure is available. Therapeutic strategies predominantly center on symptomatic treatment, necessitating long-term medication usage. This requirement can induce irritability, anxiety, and other negative emotional states in patients, potentially diminishing their cooperation with treatment regimens, thereby hindering their recovery.[3,4] Hence, during treatment, the integration of nursing measures is crucial to alleviate negative emotions and enhance treatment adherence.

Historically, traditional nursing care has emphasized disease-oriented patient care, focusing on “physiological” health while frequently overlooking “psychological” needs. This approach often fails to achieve the desired therapeutic outcome due to the combined impact of negative emotional states and inadequate disease awareness. Empathetic care, which originated from psychology, primarily involves understanding patients’ emotions, feelings, and needs from their perspectives, enabling tailored interventions to relieve their negative emotions and enhance the overall quality of nursing services. This care approach has gained considerable traction in the medical field in recent years.[5,6]

However, both domestic and international research literature lacks abundant reports on the efficacy of this care model in managing heart failure patients. In this study, we applied an empathy-based care strategy to patients with acute heart failure, observing its effects on patients’ negative emotional states and expectation levels, with the aim of providing a reference for the nursing management of this patient population. The ensuing report presents the study findings.

Patients were stratified into a control group (n = 53) and an intervention group (n = 53) according to distinct nursing protocols. 2.2. Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria encompassed: (1) emergency heart failure in accordance with the diagnostic criteria stipulated by the Primary Care Guidelines for Acute Heart Failure,[7] and patients were diagnosed with heart failure with mildly reduced ejection fraction, that is, those with a previous left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) ≤ 40%, but with a follow-up test of LVEF > 40%; (2) classification under Class II–III in the New York Heart Association cardiac function classification; (3) the ability to comprehend and cooperate in completing the scale survey; (4) the acquisition of informed consent from patients or their family members. Exclusion criteria encompassed: (1) preexisting psychological and psychiatric conditions; (2) co-occurrence with severe liver and kidney dysfunction; (3) concurrent severe heart valve disease and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy; (4) malignancies or other serious diseases; (5) cognitive dysfunction or communication disorders.

2.3. Study methods

Patients with acute heart failure in both groups were treated with standardized treatment modality for heart failure, including B-blockers (metoprolol 1.25 mg, qd, when blood pressure was stable), diuretics (furosemide, 20–40 mg, intravenous push, bid/tid), cardiotonic drugs (deslanoside, 0.4 mg, intravenous push, bid), and vasodilators (nitroglycerin, intravenous push, 1–2 mg, qd).

In the control group, patients were provided with standard care. This incorporated several components such as establishing a serene ward environment with appropriate temperature and humidity control, consistent ventilation to ensure air freshness, meticulous maintenance of bed cleanliness, and prompt removal of contaminants. Patients were comprehensively instructed to prioritize rest, maintain an adequate sleep schedule, closely monitor their vital signs, and adhere strictly to prescribed medication schedules in terms of timing and dosage. Additional assistance was regularly given to patients for position changes and aiding in sputum expulsion to maintain respiratory hygiene.

Patient education was executed in a structured format. A handbook explicating the intricacies of heart failure was provided to enhance their understanding of the disease. The handbook also elucidated the necessary precautions to be undertaken in daily life. The patients were strongly advised on lifestyle modifications including cessation of smoking and alcohol consumption. Dietary guidelines were also provided with a particular emphasis on low-fat, low-salt nutrition, and incorporation of fresh vegetables, fruits, and other easily digestible foods. Furthermore, appropriate hydration was also recommended. This regimented approach was uniformly followed for a continuous duration of 1 week. This study has been approved by the Ethics Committee of Lianyungang Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing University of Traditional Chinese Medicine. All study participants provided written informed consent before participating in the study.

In contrast, all patients in the intervention group received an empathy-based care strategy in addition to the control group regimen. This comprehensive strategy entailed assembling an empathetic care team, composed of the head nurse, charge nurse, and a psychological counselor. This team was thoroughly educated about empathy and humanistic care theories, clarifying the constituents and execution details of empathy-based care, and organizing regular seminars to consistently refine the standard of care in accordance with patients’ needs.

A critical aspect of the intervention was role-transferring emotional communication. Here, patients were provided with a tranquil and private space, where nursing staff communicated using relaxed and engaging language to keep patients at ease. Through targeted questioning, patients were encouraged to express their inner feelings and vent their negative emotions. This was coupled with encouragement and comfort to reduce their feelings of anxiety and pessimism. All patients in the intervention group were provided with emotional support therapy.

Behavioral therapy was also an integral part of the intervention. Patients were taught deep breathing techniques to relax their body and mind, adjust their psychological and physiological tension, maintain emotional stability, and avoid excitement. Meditation and relaxation training were implemented, during which patients were guided to visualize expelling their worries along with their breaths. This process was conducted once a day, with each session lasting 20 to 30 minutes.

Patients were also instructed to engage in aerobic exercises such as stretching and walking, as per their physical capabilities. These exercises were designed to not exacerbate fatigue, but to improve cardiopulmonary function, enhance physical fitness, and circumvent complications associated with long-term bed rest such as thrombosis and orthostatic hypotension. The interventions were sustained for a duration of 1 week.

2.5. Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS 23.0 software. Measurement data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x ± s) and compared using the t-test. Categorical data were expressed as numbers and percentages (n [%]), and compared using the χ2 test. A P-value of <.05 was considered statistically significant.

3. Results

3.1. Comparison of cardiac function indices between the 2 groups

No significant variation was observed between the intervention and control groups for each cardiac function index prior to the intervention (P > .05). Post-intervention, an increase in LVEF along with a decrease in LVESd, LVEDd, and BNP was noted in both groups, with more pronounced changes in the intervention group (P < .05). 4. Discussion

Heart failure is a critical health concern characterized by recurrent episodes and elevated readmission rates, posing a serious threat to the physical and mental health of patients. The need for timely, tailored nursing interventions is evident.[13] Traditional nursing care, focused predominantly on disease and providing basic patient care, often overlooks patients’ psychological states, an approach that is increasingly insufficient given evolving clinical needs.

Edward Titchener, an American psychologist, first conceptualized empathy, a concept encapsulating the understanding of others’ emotional shifts and needs.[14] For medical professionals, empathy extends beyond mere patient care, respect, and understanding; it entails a comprehensive insight into patients’ psychological conditions from their perspective, fostering genuine empathic connection, thereby catering to their needs and mitigating their negative psychological impact.[15,16]

In this study, an empathy-based care strategy was employed with acute heart failure patients. Post-intervention, the intervention group exhibited elevated LVEF, diminished LVESd and LVEDd, and improved performance on the 6MWT and 30-second sit-to-stand test in comparison with the control group. These results suggest that the empathic care approach may enhance cardiac function and functional fitness in these patients. By establishing an empathy care group, the study underscores the significance and necessity of empathic care in fostering a heightened sense of mission and responsibility among nursing staff. The empathic care process enables nurses to connect more deeply with patients, cultivate trust, enhance treatment compliance, and thus achieve superior nursing outcomes. It also encourages patients to engage in suitable aerobic exercise, enhancing cardiac ejection function, facilitating collateral circulation, reducing myocardial ischemia and hypoxia, and ultimately, promoting cardiac function recovery and functional fitness improvement.

Patients with heart failure often experience prolonged illness and extensive care requirements, resulting in substantial treatment costs and consequently, increased psychological stress, manifesting as depression, anxiety, and other negative emotions.[17,18] Previous studies suggest that such negative emotions can exacerbate heart failure and severely impact patients’ prognosis.[19,20] Persistent negative emotions can diminish patients’ confidence in treatment, engender a negative attitude towards treatment, decrease treatment compliance, and lead to recurrent episodes, cardiac function decline, and lower quality of life.[21]

Expectation, defined as an individual’s anticipation and hope for the future, constitutes a positive, active psychology that can bolster the patient’s motivation to combat their disease.[22] Post-intervention, the intervention group demonstrated higher PANAS-positive scores, HHI scale dimension scores, psychological resilience dimension scores, and quality of life dimension scores, along with lower PANAS-negative and SAS scores in comparison with the control group. These results suggest the efficacy of the empathy-based care strategy in augmenting positive emotions and hope levels, and improving the quality of life and psychological resilience in patients with acute heart failure.

During empathy-based care, nursing staff attentively listen to patients’ feelings, empathize via emotional exchange, establish a strong nurse-patient relationship, provide psychological guidance, and offer continuous encouragement and psychological support. Such care helps reduce negative emotions.[23] Empathy-based care also assists patients in reassessing their psychological state via transpersonal thinking and role-playing, thereby correcting misperceptions, further improving psychological status, raising expectation levels, and enabling a positive and optimistic attitude to face their illness, thus improving their quality of life.[24,25] The study also found a lower incidence of complications in the intervention group, likely attributable to the empathic care strategy’s encouragement of patient activity. Such activity not only enhances physical fitness but also helps regulate mood, alleviating negative emotions, promoting body fluid circulation, and preventing complications associated with prolonged bed rest.

This study has certain limitations. Firstly, it is a single-center study with a relatively small sample size, which may affect the stability of the research findings. Secondly, this study is mainly a retrospective analysis with some other factors that may affect the results. For instance, during data collection, the sample size was not randomly allocated, which could introduce potential errors. Since this is a primarily study, we only observed a limited number of cases collected from our hospital. In the next step, a multicenter prospective clinical study with a large sample size will be conducted to validate the reliability and applicability of our findings.

In conclusion, the empathy-based care strategy has demonstrated effectiveness in improving cardiac function, enhancing functional fitness, reducing negative emotions, elevating expectation levels and psychological resilience, improving quality of life, and reducing complications in patients with acute heart failure. Hence, it merits further consideration for clinical application.

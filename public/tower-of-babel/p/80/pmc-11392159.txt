Comment on: the efficacy and safety of hydrotherapy in patients with knee osteoarthritis: a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials
Authors: Zhihu Zhao, Wei Luo, Jianxong Ma, Xinlong Ma
Published: 2024

Comment

The meta-analysis conducted by Lei et al.1 provides a comprehensive evaluation of the effects of hydrotherapy on individuals with knee osteoarthritis (OA). The study’s systematic review methodology, well-defined inclusion/exclusion criteria, and extensive statistical analysis enhance its methodological robustness. Nonetheless, the lack of a trial sequence analysis (TSA) represents a significant limitation that may impact the interpretation of the findings.

The study’s stringent inclusion and exclusion criteria contribute to a targeted and pertinent synthesis of the extant literature.

Weaknesses

The restricted number of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) included in the study may impede the generalizability of the results and diminish the statistical power of the analysis.

Concerns regarding trial sequence analysis

Lack of TSA: The research lacks a trial sequence analysis, a statistical technique utilized to mitigate random errors in cumulative meta-analyses. TSA enables the estimation of the requisite evidence for drawing dependable conclusions and evaluates the adequacy of information gathered for making definitive determinations.

Suggestions for future research

Conducting long-term studies to assess the sustainability of hydrotherapy’s benefits for knee OA patients.

Expanding the search criteria to include a broader range of literature, including non-English and unpublished studies, to mitigate potential publication bias.

Conclusion

While Lei et al.’s meta-analysis offers preliminary evidence supporting hydrotherapy as a safe treatment option for knee OA, the lack of TSA is a significant omission. Incorporating TSA in future research could provide a more robust assessment of hydrotherapy’s true effect size and clinical significance.

Fermented oyster extract promotes height growth in children with mild short stature: a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial
Authors: Jongbae Kim, Keunwon Kim, Minji Park, Sangki Park, Mu Hyun Jin, Hosong Cho, Ae-son Om
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUNDS/OBJECTIVESChild growth is affected by genetics, nutrition, physical activity, and environmental factors. Despite the improvements in average height among Korean children due to better socioeconomic and nutritional status, safe and effective growth-promoting interventions are still needed for children with mild short stature. This study evaluated the safety and efficacy of fermented oyster extract (FGO) supplementation in children aged 6 to 9 yrs with heights between the third and 25th percentiles for age and gender.SUBJECTS/METHODSIn this randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, subjects received FGO (n = 40) or placebo (n = 40) once daily (500 mg/day) for 24 weeks. This study evaluated the height growth, bone age (BA), growth hormone (GH), insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1), insulin-like growth factor binding protein 3 (IGFBP-3), and urine deoxypyridinoline (DPD) for the efficacy of FGO and sex hormones, including estradiol, luteinizing hormone, and testosterone, for its safety. Finally, 66 children (33 each in the FGO and placebo group) completed the study. No significant baseline differences existed between the 2 groups.RESULTSAfter 24 weeks, the FGO group showed significant improvements in height, height SD score, and height velocity (all P < 0.001). BA and BA delay improved significantly in the FGO group (P = 0.024 and P = 0.035). The FGO group showed significantly greater changes in the IGF-1 and urine DPD levels (P = 0.010 and 0.001, respectively). The GH and IGFBP-3 concentrations increased in the FGO group, but the difference between the two groups was not significant. No serious adverse events or increase in sex hormones were observed after FGO supplementation, assuring its safety.CONCLUSIONFGO may be a safe and effective health supplement ingredient for height growth in children, irrespective of gender. Future studies will need to evaluate the long-term effects of FGO and its applicability across various populations.

INTRODUCTION

Growth is defined as the increase in body size and weight from infancy to adulthood, including an increase in height [1] that is influenced by genetic and environmental factors. The related genetic factors include gender, parental height, and birth weight, and the environmental factors encompass nutrition, physical activity, environment, health status, and socioeconomic conditions [23]. Among these factors, environmental factors can be modulated to promote or inhibit growth, making them crucial elements in managing children’s growth [4].

The average height of Koreans has consistently increased over the past few decades due to improvements in socioeconomic conditions and improved nutritional status compared to the past, with parents preferring taller children for aesthetic, social, and health reasons [56]. Consequently, efforts are being made to promote growth through methods such as adequate sleep, nutrition, exercise, and growth hormone (GH) treatment [7].

GH treatment, such as somatropin, can be used effectively to promote height growth, but it is primarily indicated for children with GH deficiency or idiopathic short stature below the third percentile, corresponding to 2 SDs below the mean [89]. Furthermore, limitations exist, including the burden of injection treatment and high costs, the need for early diagnosis and treatment, and reported side effects such as injection site rash and pain, and joint pain [10]. Thus, efforts to develop natural ingredient-based materials that are relatively safe, broadly applicable, and may support children’s height growth are underway [11].

The Pacific oyster (Crassostrea gigas) is a bivalve mollusk distributed worldwide that has been used as a food resource since ancient times [12]. Oysters provide overall health benefits because they contain significant amounts of omega-3 fatty acids such as eicosapentaenoic and docosahexaenoic acids, as well as carbohydrates and amino acids, including glycogen, taurine, and glutamic acid [1314]. Specifically, glutamic acid is a precursor of γ-aminobutyric acid (GABA), which can influence GH secretion and insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) expression, implying its potential to promote growth and development [1516].

A human study involving 6-to-11-yr-old children with short stature reported promising results; fermented oyster extract (FGO), enhanced with GABA through fermentation with Lactobacillus brevis BJ-20, promoted growth without side effects when consumed [17]. The present study was conducted on children aged 6 to 9 with a short stature between the third and 25th percentiles, excluding those below the 3rd percentile. The aim was to confirm the reproducibility of the growth-promoting effect and the safety of FGO identified in the first study, and to evaluate gender-stratified efficacy and sex-hormonal safety, which the previous study did not address. The objective of this study was to develop a scientifically grounded, safe, and functional ingredient that can support the growth of children regardless of gender.

SUBJECTS AND METHODS

Study design

This study was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 24-week clinical trial conducted in Korea from January 2022 to December 2023. Six study observations were made: during screening, baseline (pre-supplementation), and at 6, 12, 18, and 24 weeks after ingestion. This trial was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Institutional Review Board of Pusan National University Korean Medicine Hospital (PNUKHIRB 2022-01-004-007). All participants provided signed informed consent at enrollment in the study.

Participants

This study evaluated the safety and growth effects of FGO in children with short stature. Healthy prepubertal boys and girls aged 6 to 9 yrs old with a mild short stature, defined as being below the 25th percentile for height according to the Korean children’s growth chart by age and gender, were recruited [18]. The participants, accompanied by their parents, voluntarily provided written informed consent.

The exclusion criteria comprised the following: 1) Endocrine disorders or precocious puberty (girls < 8 yrs with breast Tanner stage II; boys < 9 yrs with testicular Tanner stage II); 2) Ongoing treatment for significant infectious diseases, neoplasms, cardiac conditions, attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder, diabetes mellitus, or other major illnesses; 3) Administration of GH within four weeks or consumption of growth-promoting medications/supplements within 2 weeks before screening; 4) Abnormal laboratory values for thyroid-stimulating hormone, triiodothyronine, free thyroxine, creatinine, aspartate aminotransferase, or alanine transaminase; 5) Planned participation in concurrent clinical trials; 6) Known allergies to shellfish; 7) Preterm birth (< 37 weeks gestation) or low birth weight (< 2,500 g); and 8) Any other condition deemed unsuitable for participation by the principal investigator.

Randomization and allocation concealment

This study was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. The subjects were allocated randomly to the placebo and FGO groups at a 1:1 ratio based on gender and age using a computer-generated randomization list (Fig. 1). All subjects, investigators, and assessors were blinded to the treatment allocation until the completion of data collection and analysis to minimize potential bias.

After screening 86 participants based on the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria, 80 eligible subjects were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either a placebo or a supplement containing FGO. Four subjects withdrew consent, and ten subjects with heights below the third percentile for their sex and age were excluded because of medical intervention requirements. Consequently, the data from 66 children with mild short stature were ultimately included in the per-protocol analysis (Fig. 1) [9]. Subgroup analysis by gender was also conducted.

In this clinical trial, the research institution conducted ongoing monitoring throughout the study period to ensure the appropriate quantity and data quality.

Efficacy assessments

The primary efficacy outcome was the change in height. Height measurements were obtained at the baseline and at weeks 6, 12, 18, and 24 using a stadiometer (GL-150P, G-Tech International Co. Ltd., Uijeongbu, Korea) at consistent time points and rounded up to the first decimal place.

Among the secondary efficacy outcomes, the height SD score (SDS) was calculated by subtracting the mean height for the child’s age and sex, based on the Korean children’s growth chart, from the measured height value, and then dividing by the SD [18]. This standardized approach enables meaningful comparisons across different age and sex groups in the pediatric population. The height velocity (HV) was calculated by annualizing the height increment observed from the baseline at each visit, providing a dynamic measure of the growth rate throughout the study period.

At the baseline and the study endpoint, the bone age (BA) was assessed using radiographs of both hands and wrists, and the BA delay (defined as the difference between BA and chronological age) was calculated [19]. In addition, serum GH, IGF-1, insulin-like growth factor binding protein 3 (IGFBP-3), and urine deoxypyridinoline (DPD) were measured. These assessments provided objective data on the growth-related endocrine function and bone metabolism throughout the study period.

Safety assessments

Safety assessments were performed using hematochemical analyses, including measurements of the sex hormones, such as estradiol, luteinizing hormone (LH), and testosterone, as well as urinary analyses and immunological tests. Blood and urine samples collected at the baseline and at week 24 of the intervention period were used for these analyses. All adverse events were monitored throughout the study via participant interviews and questionnaires.

Preparation of intervention

The FGO and the supplements for the placebo and FGO groups were manufactured by Marin Bioprocess Co., Ltd. (Busan, Korea). The FGO ingredient was prepared by fermenting Pacific oysters with Lactobacillus brevis BJ-20 using bioconversion technology to enhance its GABA content to 11.5% (dry weight basis). The supplements were prepared as 20 mL sachets, with the test supplement containing 500 mg of FGO (57.5 mg of GABA), consistent with previous studies [17]. The placebo contained an equivalent volume of purified water. The supplements for both groups were indistinguishable in terms of appearance, color, and odor. The subjects were instructed to take one sachet orally once daily at bedtime throughout the entire study period. The supplements (placebo or FGO) were distributed to the participants at the baseline (pre-supplementation) and at weeks 6, 12, and 18. Compliance was evaluated at weeks 6, 12, 18, and at the end of the study (week 24).

Sample size

Data from a previous clinical study on a herbal supplement for height growth in children with mild short stature were used to calculate the sample size [20]. The study reported that the mean difference in height gain between the groups was 0.29 cm, and the SD was 0.813. Based on these data, a mean difference (d) of 0.57 cm in height gain between groups was anticipated after 24 weeks of intervention. The significance level (α) was set to 0.05 (2-sided), and the type II error (β) at 0.20 (power = 80%). The sample size was calculated using G*Power software to be 32 participants per group. Eighty subjects were recruited to account for a 20% dropout rate, and 66 participants (33 in each group) were analyzed.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp, Armonk, NY, USA). The missing values were imputed using the last-observation-carried-forward method. The continuous variables are presented as the means ± SDs. The results between the placebo group and FGO group were compared using a Student’s t-test if the data were normally distributed, whereas the Mann–Whitney U test was used in case of non-normality. The categorical variables are presented as frequencies and percentages. The differences between the groups were analyzed using a χ2 test. The threshold for statistical significance was considered at a P < 0.05.

Efficacy outcomes

Table 3 lists the outcomes of efficacy evaluation variables for the overall study population, comparing the Placebo group with the FGO group. After 24 weeks, the FGO group showed a significantly greater increase in height than the placebo group (P < 0.001). The increases in height in the placebo and FGO groups were 2.48 ± 0.61 cm and 3.40 ± 0.51 cm, respectively, representing a 0.92 cm difference (37.1%) compared to the placebo group. The FGO group showed a significantly greater change in height SDS than the placebo group (0.16 ± 0.11 vs. −0.01 ± 0.14, P < 0.001). Furthermore, the annualized HV was significantly higher in the FGO group than in the placebo group (7.32 ± 1.11 cm vs. 5.40 ± 1.34 cm, P < 0.001). The changes in BA and BA delay, a key indicator of bone growth and child growth delay, were significantly greater in the FGO group than in the placebo group (P =0.024 and P = 0.035, respectively) [21]. The changes in the serum IGF-1 and urine DPD levels showed significantly greater increases in the FGO group than in the placebo group (P = 0.010 and P = 0.001, respectively). The GH and IGFBP-3 concentrations in the FGO group tended to increase compared to the baseline, but the increases were not statistically significant.

Safety

Safety assessments for the study included continuous monitoring of the vital signs and clinical laboratory parameters. Two mild rash symptoms were observed, potentially linked to participants’ sensitivity to the investigational product, but no definitive causal link with FGO was confirmed. No other adverse events were attributed to FGO, and no serious adverse reactions occurred.

Comprehensive evaluations, including hematological, urinary analyses, and physical examinations, showed no clinically significant differences between the FGO and placebo groups (Table 5). No increase in sex hormones (including estradiol, LH, and testosterone), known to promote secondary sex characteristics, was also not observed (Table 6). Accordingly, FGO, given at a dose of 500 mg/day, is safe for children.

DISCUSSION

This study was conducted as a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial to evaluate the effect of FGO on children’s growth. Previous clinical studies have shown that FGO, enriched with GABA through fermentation with Lactobacillus brevis BJ-20, may promote growth in children without adverse effects. These initial findings have highlighted FGO as a promising functional ingredient for stimulating height growth in pediatric populations [17]. Accordingly, a second clinical trial was conducted to confirm the reproducibility of these effects and expand the existing evidence base.

The primary objective was to develop evidence-based functional ingredients to enhance children’s height growth. Although the initial results were promising, further research was required to confirm the reproducibility and comprehensive efficacy of FGO. This research contributes to the development of functional ingredients that may promote growth and overall health in pediatric populations.

A short stature affects the physical growth of children as well as their quality of life and social functioning [22]. Children with a short stature often appear younger or less mature than their peers, which can lead to reduced self-esteem, lower confidence, and social isolation [23]. These psychosocial impacts may persist into adulthood, influencing occupational opportunities, career advancement, and interpersonal relationships [2224]. Therefore, early intervention is essential to optimize final adult height [25].

This trial involved children aged 6 to 9 yrs with a short stature between the third and 25th percentiles because children below the 3rd percentile are considered to have a pathological short stature [9]. The FGO group showed a statistically significant increase in stature compared to the placebo group. Specifically, the height gain in the FGO group was 0.92 cm greater than in the placebo group, representing a 37.1% greater increase. The FGO group also showed significant improvement with a height SDS change of 0.16 and an increased annualized HV of 7.32 cm/year, both of which were significantly higher than those observed in the placebo group. The height SDS is a statistical measure used to assess the growth status of children and adolescents. It compares an individual’s height, according to age and sex, to a standardized growth curve, such as the World Health Organization growth charts, expressing how far the height is from the mean in SDs [1826]. HV refers to the rate of increase in an individual’s stature over a specified period, typically quantified as an annual height increment. In general, the annual HV of Korean children aged 6 to 9 yrs is typically observed to be within the range of 5–6 cm/year [2728]. These findings exceeded the average height increase and revealed significant improvement in the height SDS, providing objective evidence that FGO supplementation resulted in an increase in children’s height.

The present study showed a significant increase in the IGF-1 levels in the FGO group compared to the placebo group, whereas no notable changes in GH levels were observed. The IGFBP-3 levels increased in the FGO group, but the difference between the placebo group was not statistically significant. These findings require careful interpretation within the context of the complex GH-IGF-1 axis.

GH directly stimulates growth by promoting the proliferation and differentiation of various tissues, including bone and muscle [29]. In addition, GH acts as a key regulator of IGF-1 secretion by stimulating the liver and other tissues to produce and release IGF-1 [30].

The observation that the IGF-1 levels increased in the absence of corresponding changes in GH has been corroborated in previous similar studies [2031]. IGF-1 tends to be a more stable and reliable marker of GH activity because it remains in the body longer and its levels do not fluctuate as much as GH, which is released in irregular bursts [32]. Previous animal experiments showed that the GH and IGF-1 levels increased through FGO oral administration [3334]. Oysters contain significant amounts of amino acids, including glycogen, taurine, and glutamic acid [1314]. Among them, glutamic acid is a precursor of GABA, which influences GH secretion and IGF-1 expression in the body. Therefore, FGO enhanced with GABA through fermentation with Lactobacillus brevis BJ-20 might have stimulated the GH-IGF-1 axis, potentially enhancing height growth by increasing the effects of GH and promoting IGF-1 production. Nevertheless, the growth-promoting effects of FGO are not limited to GABA alone. Rather, they are presumed to result from the complex synergistic interactions among amino acids, minerals, and various bioactive compounds naturally present in oysters.

Bones form the essential skeletal framework of the body, and their growth during childhood is critical for determining an individual’s stature and body proportions [35]. BA reflects the biological rate of height increase during growth [36]. BA delay, defined as the degree of delay in BA relative to CA, exceeding 2 yrs, raises concerns regarding potential growth retardation [37]. In a study evaluating BA in children aged 7 to 9 yrs, the average difference between BA and CA was approximately 12 mon [38]. On the other hand, the BA delay in the subjects of this study was less than the average. In particular, during the study period, no significant change in BA delay was observed in the control group. Conversely, the decrease in BA delay in the FGO group suggested catch-up growth.

DPD is a compound produced during the degradation of collagen in bone tissue, and its urinary concentration rises with increased bone resorption rates [39]. During childhood, characterized by active bone formation and resorption, the DPD levels serve as a vital biomarker for assessing the bone metabolic status [40]. The significant differences observed in BA, BA delay, and urinary DPD levels in the FGO group, compared with the placebo group, indicate that FGO has a positive effect on child growth and bone metabolism.

The observed significant changes in BA and urinary DPD levels within the FGO group, compared with the placebo group, also indicate the positive influence FGO has on bone metabolism.

The observed increase in IGF-1 levels in the FGO group was attributed to the acceleration of BA and elevated DPD activity because IGF-1 promotes bone growth and metabolism through its interaction with growth hormone [4142]. Therefore, the observed increase in IGF-1 levels provides additional evidence supporting the positive impact of FGO on bone growth and metabolic activation [43].

Among the participants, the FGO group showed significant improvements in height gain, height SDS change, and HV compared to the placebo group. Gender-specific analyses suggested that the beneficial effects of FGO on height growth were consistently observed across both sexes, with no notable disparities in the magnitude or direction of these effects. Furthermore, biochemical markers, including IGF-1 levels, showed similar trends in both sexes, corroborating the overarching findings of the study. These results highlight the consistent efficacy of FGO in augmenting height growth in children, irrespective of gender.

Safety was a critical evaluation parameter in this study. No severe adverse events were encountered in the FGO or placebo group throughout the study. Two participants receiving FGO had a mild rash. Although these events might have been attributable to participants’ sensitivity to the investigational product, no definitive direct causal relationship with FGO was established. Biochemical analyses and physical examinations also showed no clinically significant differences between the placebo and FGO groups. No changes in sex hormones leading to secondary sexual characteristics were observed. These findings suggest that FGO is safe for children.

This study evaluated the impact of FGO on children’s growth, but it had several limitations that warrant consideration. The initial total caloric intake tended to be higher in the FGO group than the placebo group (P = 0.069). Nevertheless, no significant sex differences were observed, and the height growth effect of FGO was consistent regardless of sex, suggesting a limited confounding effect. The intervention lasted 24 weeks and involved children aged 6 to 9 yrs, leaving the long-term effects on older children and adolescents unexplored, particularly during or after the onset of secondary sexual characteristics. In addition, important factors that influence growth, such as sleep and diet, were not fully controlled during the study [344]. Therefore, further longitudinal studies will be needed to clarify the independent growth-promoting effects of FGO while accounting for these variables and to assess its potential impact on pubertal development and adolescent growth.

Conventional growth hormone treatments have been limited to severe cases of short stature or have been constrained by their high costs [345]. This study highlights the potential of FGO, a natural-based supplement, as a relatively safe and effective alternative. Furthermore, the primary findings of this study are consistent with previous research showing that FGO positively impacts children’s height growth [17]. These supplements offer a promising option for children with a mild short stature not related to growth hormone deficiency, complementing the limitations of existing therapeutic approaches.

The present study showed that FGO, administered at a daily dose of 500 mg, promoted significant height growth in children aged 6 to 9 yrs with mild short stature, regardless of gender, over 24 weeks, compared with a placebo group. No serious adverse events were observed, and no significant alterations in sexual hormones were observed compared to the control group. These findings suggest that FGO could be a potential ingredient for health supplements designed to promote height growth in children.

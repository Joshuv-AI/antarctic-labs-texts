A novel fusion gene of NUP98::CYP2F2P in myeloid neoplasm
Authors: Wusixian Huang, Fenghong Zhang, Jundan Xie, Chunxiao Wu, Qian Wang, Lijun Wang, Qinrong Wang, Suning Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe report the first identification of an NUP98::CYP2F2P gene fusion in a 60-year-old woman presenting with pancytopenia and 6.5% bone marrow blasts. CYP2F2P, a cytochrome P450 pseudogene, represents the first reported pseudogene partner in the CYP family for NUP98 rearrangements. Comprehensive molecular characterization revealed this novel fusion transcript alongside a complex karyotype. While conventional diagnostic criteria (including the 2022 ICC guideline) would have classified it as myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS), the patient exhibited primary resistance to four cycles of azacitidine, with persistent 6.5% blasts and minimal residual disease at 7.16%. The case was reclassified as AML based on 2022 WHO 5th classification recognizing NUP98 rearrangements as disease-defining regardless of blast percentage. These findings regarding the diagnostic blast threshold for NUP98-rearranged myeloid neoplasms and highlights the need for further investigation with larger cohorts to better define these boundaries. In conclusion, this case reported a novel fusion gene of NUP98::CYP2F2P in myeloid neoplasm and suggested that the blast threshold for AML with NUP98 rearrangements should be further investigated to guide treatment standardization.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00277-025-06546-0.

Introduction

NUP98, a component of the nuclear pore complex, mediates mRNA export from the nucleus [1]. NUP98 rearrangements (NUP98r) typically involve the formation of chimeric proteins at the N-terminal FG/GLFG domain of this gene and the C-terminal region of partner genes. In East Asian adults, the incidence of NUP98r in AML is approximately 5% [2]. The 2022 ICC classification categorized NUP98r AML among rare recurring translocations, requiring ≥ 10% blasts for diagnosis [3]. In contrast, the 2022 WHO 5th classification defined NUP98r AML as a distinct entity, with the presence of any NUP98r myeloid blasts being diagnostic [4].

NUP98 is located on chromosome 11p15 in humans, has been reported to contribute to AML pathogenesis through fusion events involving both homeobox (HOX) genes and non-HOX gene partners [5]. These fusions are often accompanied by other mutations such as FLT3-ITD, WT1, and NRAS. The HOX partners are further categorized into “Class I” clustered HOX genes (including HOXA7, HOXA9, HOXB13, HOXC12, etc.) [6] and “Class II” non-clustered HOX-related genes (such as PPRX1, PPRX2 and others). Non-HOX partners mainly include genes with characteristic functional domains, such as those with plant homeodomain (PHD) (like TOP1, IQCG), and genes carrying chromatin modification-related domains, including those with a SET domain (like NSD1, KMT2A). These fusion variants have been widely reported in AML. They act as an abnormal transcription factor leading to dysregulation of HOXA cluster genes, which are crucial for normal hematopoietic differentiation [7, 8].

Here, we will report a partner of NUP98 fusion genes identified for the first time in adult myeloid neoplasm: CYP2F2P. The main detection methods are summarized in the supplementary materials.

Case presentation

A 60-year-old female was diagnosed with pancytopenia during a routine examination in January 2024. She was referred to the First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University in July 2024 due to deterioration of her condition after an infection. The blood routine examination revealed the white blood cell (WBC) 3.22 × 10⁹/L, the hemoglobin (Hb) 90 g/L, and the platelet count (PLT) 45 × 10⁹/L. Bone marrow (BM) morphology revealed 6.5% of blasts (Fig. 1A). Immunophenotype analysis showed myeloid expression. BM histopathology findings suggested myelodysplastic syndrome with excess blasts (MDS-EB1) (Figure S1). The bone marrow immunohistochemistry demonstrated scattered CD34+, scattered weakly CD117*, MPO + in myeloid cells, partial Lysozyme+, scattered weakly CD56*, rare TdT+, E-cadherin + in erythroid cells, CD71 + in erythroid cells, CD42b + in megakaryocytes, scattered CD3+, scattered weakly CD5, rare weakly CD20, rare PAX5+, CD138 + in plasma cells (approximately 2%), and CD235a + in erythroid cells. Cytogenetic analysis showed a complex karyotype of 43–48, XX, der(3;?), 5q-, +?6p-, + 8, −11, + 14, −16, −17,?i(17)(q10), der(19)t(19;?)(q12;?),?20q-, + 22q-, +r, inc[CP8]/46, XX[2] (Fig. 1B). Interphase fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) analysis indicated the presence of 5q31 deletion (24%), 20q12 deletion (14%), trisomy 21 (21%), and TP53 deletion (18%). Targeted DNA next-generation sequencing (NGS) identified IRF2BP2 (exon 2 p.Pro525Leu, VAF 14.8%), SPEN (exon 1 p.Asp2246Tyr, VAF 50.9%), TRAF2 (exon 8 p.Gln238Arg, VAF 45.7%), and UNC13D (exon 19 p.Arg536Leu, VAF 47.2%). RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) identified an NUP98::CYP2F2P fusion (Fig. 1C). Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) was performed using the following primers: NUP98 F: 5’-GCTTTGCATATGGTCAGAACAA-3’ and CYP2F2P R: 5’-GGCGTGGCCGTGTAACTCTGG-3’, which was subsequently confirmed by Sanger sequencing (Fig. 1D-E).

The patient showed no response after four cycles of azacitidine (75 mg/m²/day for 7 days). A repeat bone marrow examination in December 2024 revealed 6.5% blasts with persistence of the complex karyotype. Minimal residual disease (MRD) was positive at 7.16%.

Discussion and conclusion

To our knowledge, this is the first documented instance of CYP2F2P involvement in a haematological malignancy as a fusion partner with NUP98, making it the first known occurrence within the CYP family. CYP2F2P, located on 19q13.2, is a pseudogene belonging to the cytochrome P450 family [9]. It is enriched in the arachidonic acid metabolism pathway [10] (a pathway known to be dysregulated in myeloid neoplasm [11]). However, there is currently limited research on its role, with only one article indicating that CYP2F2P expression is low in nasopharyngeal carcinoma [12]. Unlike other NUP98 partners (HOX or NSD1), CYP2F2P lacks DNA-binding domains. The leukemogenic mechanism of NUP98::CYP2F2P without functional assays in our study, particularly whether it dysregulates HOX genes like classical NUP98 fusions. Further studies are needed to determine the role of this fusion in myeloid malignancies.

The patient initially presented with 6.5% blasts, which would typically have been diagnosed as myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) according to conventional criteria or the 2022 ICC classification. However, after four cycles of azacitidine, the patient showed no response to treatment. The patient tested positive for NUP98 rearrangements, and according to the WHO 5th edition classification, the diagnosis was revised to AML with NUP98 rearrangements. This case raises important questions regarding the diagnostic blast threshold for NUP98r myeloid neoplasms. However, no additional cases of NUP98r myeloid neoplasms with blast percentages during this cutoff value were included in this report. Future studies are needed to clarify its diagnostic boundaries for AML with NUP98r, which will help standardize treatment approaches.

This study represented the first report of CYP2F2P as an NUP98 fusion partner in adult myeloid neoplasm patients. Further research is needed to understand the precise role of this fusion gene in leukemogenesis. In addition, it suggested that the blast threshold for AML with NUP98 rearrangements should be further investigated to guide treatment standardization.

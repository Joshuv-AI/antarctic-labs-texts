The relationship between the Barthel Index and stroke-associated pneumonia in elderly patients and factors of SAP
Authors: Bin Wu, Haowen Luo, Jingyi Li, Yongsen Chen, Jianmo Liu, Pengfei Yu, Zhilang Yan, Aiqin Wang, Hongfei Xian, Jingyao Ke, Rui Cheng, Xiaoman Wang, Canwei Yi, Weijiang Han, Huiming Liao, Yifan Wu, Weijie Jia, Mengqi Han, Yingping Yi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundStroke-associated pneumonia (SAP) is a common complication in stroke patients, and the Barthel Index (BI) is a well-established metric for assessing activities of daily living (ADL). However, the association between BI and SAP in acute ischemic stroke (AIS) patients remains unclear. This study aims to investigate the relationship between BI at admission and SAP, and explore the factors in AIS elderly patients.MethodRetrospective data were collected from ischemic stroke patients hospitalized at the Second Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University between January 2018 and July 2021, including their basic demographic and laboratory test results. Restricted cubic spline regression, multivariate logistic regression analysis, and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis were employed to investigate the relationship between BI and SAP. Additionally, the Shapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) method was used to identify the factors influencing SAP.ResultsThe study included 7,548 eligible stroke patients with a mean age of 75.1 ± 7.6 years, among which 41.14% were female. The SAP group demonstrated significantly lower BI compared to the non-SAP group (50.86 ± 35.60 vs. 75.27 ± 26.33, P < 0.001). Additionally, a conspicuous trend of decreasing SAP risk across the Q1-4 groups was observed (P < 0.001). The RCS analysis further confirmed a gradual reduction in SAP risk with increasing BI. Based on the clinical model, both the BI (NRI = 0.014, P = 0.005; IDI = 0.04, P < 0.001) and the NIHSS score (NRI = 0.09, P = 0.03; IDI = 0.025, P < 0.001) demonstrated additional predictive value for SAP. Multivariate logistic regression and SHAP analysis identified WBC, CONUT, TG, UA, and RBC levels, as well as the type of health insurance (urban employee basic medical insurance), as important independent predictors of SAP.ConclusionBI at admission constitutes a risk factor for the onset of SAP in elderly patients with AIS, Compared to the NIHSS and mRS score, BI may be a more reliable and practical predictor of SAP.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12877-024-05400-8.Keywords: Barthel Index, Pneumonia, Stroke, Activity of daily living

Introduction

Cerebrovascular accident, commonly known as stroke, represents a multifaceted neurological anomaly that constitutes one of the primary causes of morbidity and mortality in the adult population all around the world [1, 2]. Among the diverse complications of stroke, stroke-associated pneumonia (SAP) looms as a predominant and consequential issue, with an incidence rate fluctuating from 5–26% [3]. Several research endeavors have postulated an intricate interrelationship between infection and unfavorable outcomes after stroke, with SAP being a pivotal factor that exacerbates the mortality rate, prolongs the hospitalization duration, and impedes functional restoration [3–5]. Therefore, elucidating the risk factors for SAP is essential for its prevention. Numerous studies have utilized biomarkers such as the neutrophil to lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and the platelet to lymphocyte ratio (PLR) to identify the risk of SAP [4, 5], finding that these inflammatory markers, which are readily available, effectively reflect the inflammatory status of patients; yet, understanding the causal relationships among these markers poses considerable challenges. Efforts to identify new laboratory markers specifically associated with SAP have generally shown limited predictive value [6].Thus, the early identification of clinical risk factors for SAP is especially crucial in the absence of reliable laboratory indicators.

In clinical practice, common stroke assessment scales such as the NIH Stroke Scale (NIHSS) [7], the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) [8], and the Barthel Index (BI) [9, 10] are routinely used to evaluate the condition and functional status of stroke patients. The Barthel Index, in particular, assesses Activities of Daily Living (ADL) performance across ten domains, including eating, grooming, toileting, and walking [11]. Scoring on the BI ranges from 0 to 100, with higher score indicating greater independence [12]. Compared to the NIHSS and mRS, the BI has the advantages of being easy to administer and less subjective, enabling faster early assessments and more timely results in clinical practice. Previous studies have established the Barthel Index as an effective prognostic indicator and outcome predictor in patients with acute ischemic stroke (AIS) [13, 14]. However, the relationship between BI and the incidence of SAP among elderly patients with AIS has not been previously investigated. Consequently, the goal of this study is to investigate the correlation between BI and concomitant SAP in elderly patients with AIS, as well as to evaluate the predictive value of BI for SAP. By illuminating these interconnections, we aspire to contribute to the knowledge of SAP risk factors in this specific patient population, facilitating early identification and targeted preventive measures to mitigate the incidence of SAP and improve patient outcomes.

Method

Study participants

This cross-sectional study included patients diagnosed with AIS who were admitted to the Second Hospital of Nanchang University between January 2018 and July 2021. The following inclusion criteria were followed: (1) aged 60 years or above; (2) with a confirmed diagnosis of stroke by CT or MRI according to the World Health Organization guidelines [15, 16]; (3) with a hospitalization duration exceeding 24 h. The exclusion criteria were: (1) the presence of active malignancy or severe organ dysfunction (such as chronic respiratory failure, cardiac insufficiency, renal insufficiency, and others); (2) the presence of pneumonia before admission; (3) incomplete clinical information.

Data collection

The demographic and clinical data were retrieved from the medical records system, while the hospital registry system provided general medical information, including age, sex, and medical history (e.g. atrial fibrillation, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and prior cerebral infarction). Clinical characteristics encompassed BI at admission, laboratory test results, the occurrence of surgical interventions during hospitalization, length of stay, and hospitalization costs. Barthel Index and laboratory test were collected within 24 h of admission.

Diagnostic criteria for SAP

SAP was diagnosed within the first 7 days of admission according to the criteria established by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) [17]. The median time of SAP diagnosis was 4 (2–6) days after admission.

Statistical analysis

The normality of the data was evaluated using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median with interquartile range (IQR), whereas categorical variables were reported as frequencies and percentages. Baseline continuous variables were analyzed using the t-test, Mann-Whitney U-test, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), or Kruskal-Wallis test, depending on the distribution of the data. The chi-square test or Fisher’s exact probability method was used to assess categorical variables. Multivariable logistic regression analysis was employed to identify independent determinants for SAP.

To evaluate the relationship between BI and SAP, a binary logistic regression model was employed, using the fourth quarter (Q4) of BI as the reference group. Two multivariate models were constructed. Unadjusted and adjusted odds ratios (OR), along with their corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CI), were calculated. To model the association between BI and SAP risk in a flexible manner, restricted cubic splines were utilized. Additionally, the predictive performance of BI for SAP was evaluated by plotting the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC). Furthermore, the SHAP mechanism was used to assess the significance and direction of each variable’s contribution to the optimal model.

Statistical significance was determined at a p-value of < 0.05 (two-sided). All statistical analyses were performed using R (version 3.6.3) and Python (version 3.7).

Baseline characteristics of patients stratified by BI

According to the Standard for Nursing Classification (WS/T 431—2023) of the People’s Republic of China, the patients were classified into four groups based on their BI at admission: severe dependence (Q1, Barthel ≤ 40, n = 659), moderate dependence (Q2, 41 ≤ Barthel ≤ 60, n = 2056), mild dependence (Q3, 61 ≤ Barthel ≤ 99, n = 2894), and no dependence required (Q4, Barthel = 100, n = 1939).

Association between Barthel Index and risk of SAP

The predictive value of the BI for SAP in elderly stroke patients

To better analyze the predictive value of the BI, we excluded certain variables (mRS and NIHSS score, length of stay, and hospitalization costs). We then conducted a multivariate logistic regression analysis (Supplementary Table 1) incorporating only those variables that showed statistical significance (P < 0.05) in univariate analyses. These variables included age, WBC, MCV, MONO, CREA, UA, TG, ApoA, RBC, UEBMI, and CONUT, all of which emerged as independent predictors of SAP. A clinical model was constructed using these independent predictors.

The predictive accuracies of the Barthel Index, NIHSS score, mRS score, and the clinical model for SAP were assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, as illustrated in Fig. 3; Table 4. The Barthel Index’s area under the curve (AUC) was higher than that for NIHSS and mRS score (0.701 vs. 0.691 and 0.663, respectively). When the clinical model was augmented with the BI, NIHSS, and mRS score, the resulting AUCs were 0.749, 0.734, and 0.737 respectively, each significantly surpassing the AUC of the clinical model. Further analysis using net reclassification improvement (NRI) and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) demonstrated that the inclusion of the BI (NRI = 0.014, P = 0.005; IDI = 0.04, P < 0.001) and NIHSS score (NRI = 0.09, P = 0.03; IDI = 0.025, P < 0.001) provided additional predictive value for SAP beyond the clinical model.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first attempt to investigate the potential relationship between BI and SAP. Our findings indicated that a reduction in BI, in elderly patients with AIS, was significantly associated with a higher risk of SAP in patients with stroke. The association remained significant even after adjusting for important potential confounding variables. Moreover, BI is a valuable predictor of SAP in elderly patients with AIS, and its inclusion in a predictive model of traditional clinical practice characteristics leads to a significant improvement in the model’s predictive efficacy.

Prior studies have explored the relationship between BI and the prognosis of patients with AIS. For instance, Wade et al.‘s study found that lower baseline BI were associated with higher mortality rates in stroke patients after 6 months [20], while Martinsson et al.‘s study discovered that baseline BI were significantly higher in patients who survived compared to those who died at one week versus three months post-stroke [21]. The BI effectively reflects a patient’s functional status and daily living capabilities. Lower BI typically indicate severe limitations in daily activities, often leading to bedridden states that may result in poor ventilation and accumulation of secretions, thereby increasing the risk of SAP. Patients with low BI may also struggle with oral care, feeding, and other hygiene activities, increasing their dependency on others and subsequently raising the risk of aspiration and infections. These factors may lead to nutritional deficiencies that weaken the immune system and heighten the susceptibility to SAP, directly contributing to adverse outcomes in stroke patients [3, 19, 22].

The BI has demonstrated remarkable predictive power for stroke prognosis. According to Li et al., BI plays a critical role in assessing the ADL capabilities and predicting post-stroke mortality risks in patients with AIS [23]. Lili Chen et al. also highlighted that the BI is an independent predictor of five-year mortality in AIS patients, with its predictive value significantly exceeding that of the NIH Stroke Scale (NIHSS) [24]. In our study, the BI (AUC: 0.701) predicted SAP more accurately than the NIHSS score (AUC: 0.691) and the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score (AUC: 0.663). Incorporating the BI into the clinical model significantly improved model performance (AUROC comparison: p < 0.001; NRI: p = 0.005; IDI: p < 0.001). Notably, the enhancements were more pronounced when compared to the improvements observed with the inclusion of the NIHSS and mRS score (NRI: 0.014 vs. 0.004 vs. 0.009; IDI: 0.04 vs. 0.023 vs. 0.025). Given the simplicity and high predictive value of the BI for SAP, BI may be a more reliable and practical predictor of SAP, it is advisable to consider using the BI for early identification and intervention in clinical settings.

SHAP analysis reveals that, beyond the BI, factors such as WBC, TG, UA, and RBC, along with the type of health insurance (urban employee basic medical insurance), are significantly important. WBC, a typical inflammatory marker, is closely associated with SAP [25–27]. Our studies indicates that high levels of uric acid are a protective factor for SAP, aligning with previous research findings [28, 29]. Additionally, we observed that stroke patients with lower levels of self-care ability also exhibit lower uric acid levels, suggesting that poor nutritional status among these patients might affect uric acid synthesis and metabolism. This reduction in uric acid levels could diminish its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory protective effects, thereby increasing infection susceptibility. More detailed investigations are required to elucidate these mechanisms.

CONUT, a biomarker for nutrition and inflammation, is composed primarily of serum albumin, total lymphocyte count, and total cholesterol. Low levels of cholesterol and albumin typically indicate poor nutritional status, while a reduced lymphocyte count often suggests immunosuppression or diminished immune function, all of which elevate SAP risk. Previous research has identified the red blood cell count as an independent predictor of short-term mortality in ischemic stroke [30], noting that red blood cells primarily function to transport oxygen and provide essential nutrients to tissues. Low red blood cell counts may indicate risks of anemia or hypoxia, both linked to increased pneumonia risk [31, 32].

Few studies have explored the relationship between types of Chinese medical insurance and SAP. A survey in the Chinese ischemic stroke population showed that compared to urban resident medical insurance, urban employee medical insurance tends to have higher average survival periods, overall costs, and reimbursement ratios [33]. Our findings suggest that urban employee medical insurance acts as a protective factor against SAP, possibly because individuals with this insurance typically have better socioeconomic status, education levels, and health awareness. They are more likely to prioritize health management, seek timely medical care, and adhere to treatment recommendations. Urban employee medical insurance provides more comprehensive coverage and health management, with higher overall costs and reimbursement rates, reducing the financial burden on patients, improving treatment adherence, and enhancing access to high-quality medical services. Collectively, these factors significantly reduce the risk of SAP in stroke patients.

This study has some noteworthy limitations. First, this study is a cross-sectional study, and the direction of cause and effect between BI and SAP may be uncertain. Second, although we had a relatively large sample size, the patients were obtained from a single center in China, and thus, there may have been selection bias. Third, as this study involved retrospective data collection, certain factors potentially related to SAP, such as stroke type and imaging characteristics, were not included in the analysis. Therefore, the findings of this study should be cautiously extrapolated to the clinical field, and further external validation is required to confirm our findings.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study highlights that low BI at admission represents an independent risk factor for concurrent SAP among elderly AIS patients. It offers crucial predictive value for healthcare providers, enabling them to evaluate the risk of pneumonia in stroke patients and intervene promptly for improved stroke prognosis.

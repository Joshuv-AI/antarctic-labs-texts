Application of metabolomics in hypertension research: A bibliometric study and literature review
Authors: Ran Wei, Ning Xu, Wenjing Chen, Huihui Song, Kewei Liu, Yunsheng Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCardiovascular disease has always been the leading cause of death in the global population. Hypertension (HTN) is widely recognized as one of the most critical risk factors for severe cardiovascular disease. In recent years, an increasing number of studies have found that omics research is needed to identify alternative/orthogonal biomarkers in order to gain new insights into disease mechanisms. Metabolomics has undeniable contributions and prospects in HTN research. Therefore, exploring the key research areas and potential interaction mechanisms between metabolomics and HTN has become increasingly important.MethodsUse the Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) to search for literature related to HTN and metabolomics from 2005 to 2024. The bibliometric tools used in this study include CiteSpace 6.3.R1 (CiteSpace) and VOSviewer 1.6.20 (VOSviewer).ResultsA total of 1211 articles were included from 2005 to 2024. In the past 20 years, the number of publications and citations in this field has continued to increase. These articles involve 73 countries (regions), 2085 institutions, and 8255 authors. These articles have been published in 479 journals, with 117 references appearing more than 15 times. The increasing number of studies in this field indicates that it remains a research hotspot. We expect that this field will continue to receive attention in the future. The United States and China lead in the number of published articles, while England has the widest international cooperation, indicating its continued prominent position as a research center in this field. Among them, the papers published by D’Alessandro Angelo, Li Yunlun, Coresh Josef, and others have the highest publication volume. Publications in this field often appear in omics, nutrition, cardiovascular and molecular biology. The most common keywords include Metabolomics, Hypertension, Biomarkers, Metabolism, Oxidative stress, Inflammation, and Insulin resistance.ConclusionThe relationship between metabolomics and HTN is receiving widespread attention. The application of gut microbiota, molecular docking, and untargeted metabolomics in HTN is a key research direction in the future. Antioxidant activity, genetics, validation, mass spectrometry, management, and accumulation are at the forefront of knowledge in this field.Keywords: Hypertension, Metabolomics, Bibliometrics, Citespace, VOSviewer

Introduction

Cardiovascular disease is a major global health challenge and remains the leading cause of death [1]. The incidence of cardiovascular disease continues to rise, and hypertension (HTN) is one of the main risk factors for severe cardiovascular disease [2]. This can ultimately be attributed to the failure to provide timely and appropriate interventions, and the reason for this consequence is the inability to stratify risks and make accurate prognoses [3]. Therefore, further research is needed on the pathophysiology and biomarkers of HTN.

For this reason, many researchers seek the ability of omics techniques [4], [5]. Different omics methods attempt to unlock the information present in the cellular molecules associated with their names as a whole [6]. These efforts have fundamentally changed the way we advance knowledge of biological processes and disease mechanisms [7]. Metabolomics technology is becoming increasingly common and receiving widespread attention from the scientific community. Among many omics technologies, metabonomics can be used to diagnose diseases, explore disease pathogenesis, new targets, determine drug treatment and treatment results, especially in obesity, diabetes, cardiovascular diseases, cancer and neonatal metabolic defects [8], [9], [10].

Unlike traditional review articles, bibliometrics employs mathematical and statistical techniques to analyze texts and identify current trends and pressing issues in specific research fields [11]. In recent years, scholars have analyzed the literature on the correlation between metabonomics and diabetes, coronary heart disease, but no one has paid attention to the correlation between metabonomics and HTN [12], [13]. We try to fill this knowledge gap. This study is based on bibliometrics and visual mapping information to conduct a visual and systematic analysis of literature, aiming to reveal publications, countries, institutions, funds, scholars, journals, citations, and keywords related to metabolomics and HTN in the past 20 years, providing researchers with more detailed data support and future development directions.

Methods

Data sources

This study used Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) as the data source and was searched by two researchers. Due to different search times and slight fluctuations in data volume, we chose to complete the search task within one day, with a search period of August 18, 2024. The literature search formula was determined as follows: TS = (metabolomic or metabolomics or metabonomic or metabonomics) AND TS = (Hypertension* OR High Blood Pressure*). The included file types were only articles and reviews. The search time span was from 2005 to 2024, which was equivalent to 20 years. In order to collect consistent and accurate information from multiple analyses based on published records, we excluded book chapters, conference papers, and non-English publications. In addition, to improve our analysis, articles unrelated to HTN in the context of metabolomics were manually excluded (Fig. 1).

Screening of research publications related to HTN and metabolomic.

Data Extraction

The following data were extracted from the included publications: title, author, institution, country, journal (including 2024 Journal Impact Factor (IF)), publication year, number of citations, and other relevant information. After checking the included files, we chose “plain text information”, which includes the complete record of the file and references to it, and named it in “download_*” format for export.

Data analysis

In this study, SRplot (https://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/), VOS viewer (v1.6.20) and CiteSpace (v6.3.R1) were used for bibliometric and visual analysis. SRplot online drawing software was used to analyze the annual development trend of publications. VOSviewer 1.6.20 was used to describe the collaborative network relationships between countries, institutions, authors, and keywords. Different colors represent different years, nodes represent countries, authors, institutions, and keywords, and the number of connections represents the degree of cooperation. CiteSpace was used to predict future trends in this research field. It mainly displays changes in keywords through keyword clustering, appearance, and timeline.

Analysis of highly cited references and co-cited publications

These publications cited a total of 57,790 references. In Fig. 9, we included 117 references that were cited more than 15 times and counted the top 10 references (Table 7). The article “Metabolic profiles and the risk of developing diabetes” published by Wang TJ et al in Nature Medicine had the highest citation count (74 times), the strongest centrality (TLS = 291), and the highest impact factor in 2011 [14]. In this paper, metabonomics was used to predict the development of diabetes, and the age, body mass index and fasting blood glucose of 2422 normal individuals and 201 diabetes patients were correlated. Amino acid metabolism plays a potential key role in the pathogenesis of diabetes, suggesting that amino acid profile can assess the risk of diabetes. The second most influential factor among the top 10 references is “Human metabolic phenotype diversity and its association with diet and blood pressure” published by Holmes e et al in Nature in 2008, which has been cited 46 times [15]. This study analyzed the spectra of two 24-hour urine samples from 4630 participants in the INTERMAP epidemiological study, involving 17 population samples aged 40–59 in China, Japan, the United Kingdom, and the United States. The study involved the discovery of new biomarkers associated with cardiovascular disease risk, reflecting a high correlation between HTN and metabolic phenotypes.

Discussion

1211 publications related to HTN and metabolomics were retrieved in WOSCC. CiteSpace 6.3.R1 and VOSviewer 1.6.20 was to analyze and evaluate countries and institutions, core authors and literature, research hotspots, and knowledge frontiers. For 20 years, metabonomics and diabetes related publications were first published in 2005. Since then, the number of publications and citations has steadily increased, especially since 2017, where the number of publications and citations has been growing rapidly every year. It was expected that future publications will continue to grow, and research in this field will continue to receive widespread attention from scholars in the future. The main results are summarized as follows:

Global knowledge Structure

We know that countries, funds, institutions, and authors are not separate entities, but an organic whole closely related to each other. From the perspective of global cooperation, North America, Europe, and Asia have close connections and great influence, while South America and Africa have insufficient connections, which may be related to multiple factors such as population, economy, and healthcare [18], [19].

The North American region is centered around the United States, which has the highest number of published papers and the third highest number of citations, indicating a significant influence in this field. This may be closely related to factors such as contributions from major research institutions in the United States and core authors. In this field, the main research institutions in the United States include Harvard Medical School, Johns Hopkins University, Massachusetts General Hospital, University of Colorado, Boston University, and Brigham and Women's Hospital, with Harvard Medical School ranking first in publications. The comprehensive strength of these universities provides scholars with a better research environment and platform. The strength of American scholars is relatively high, both in terms of the number of papers published by research scholars and the number of papers that are commonly cited. The publications and citations of scholars D’Alessandro Angelo and Coresh Josef both rank in the top 3. Therefore, it can be explained that these factors have contributed to the influence of the United States in the research on the correlation between HTN and metabolomics, including the high contributions of core scholars and research institutions in the United States [20], [21].

Europe is also a hot spot for studying the correlation between HTN and metabolomics. Britain has significant influence. King’s College London and Imperial College London occupy important positions as the top 10 major institutions, with Nicholson jk ranking second in total citations and third in centrality, indicating that his publications have received widespread attention worldwide and have driven the UK’s influence globally.

In addition, Asia is centered on China. It is observed that the incidence rate of HTN in China is getting higher and higher. Therefore, Chinese research institutions and scholars are committed to the study of HTN and its complications. China ranks second in terms of the number of publications. The Chinese Academy of Sciences and Shandong University of Traditional Chinese Medicine are potential major research institutions. Among them, scholars Li Yunlun, Yu Bing, and Jiang Haiqiang ranked among the top 10 authors in terms of the number of published papers. These favorable conditions fully demonstrate that China has enormous development potential in this field.

Similarly, when we want to gain a deeper understanding of the research progress, directions, and results related to HTN and metabolomics, we first consider the major journals, publications, co cited literature, and core authors in this field, which will bring new ideas and methods to our research. We can understand that most of the publications we first choose come from top 10 journals, such as Metabolites, Scientific Reports, PLoS One, and International Journal of Molecular Sciences. Assuming the scope is expanded to a larger scale, we can certainly search from the core journals of HTN and metabolomics related research, which not only include the top 10 journals, but also journals such as hypertension research and CIRCULATION, which will provide more specific and extensive information for our research.

Global research Focus and trends

Based on the co-occurrence of keywords, we can identify some hotspots and trends in the correlation between HTN and metabolomics over the past 20 years. These core keywords include metabolomics, obesity, biomarkers, metabolism, oxidative stress, and insulin-resistance (IR).

The discovery of insulin is a milestone [22]. Human insulin is a peptide hormone composed of 51 amino acids and 2 chains [23]. It is synthesized and released in pancreatic beta cells, and its main function is to maintain glucose homeostasis. At the same time, it also participates in glycogen synthesis, lipid metabolism, DNA synthesis, gene transcription, amino acid transport, protein synthesis, and degradation [24]. Lack of insulin mediated glucose metabolism control in tissues can induce insulin resistance. More and more studies show that IR is the main pathogenic factor of many metabolic diseases, including obesity, cardiovascular disease and type 2 diabetes [25]. In fact, IR is a common feature of obesity and cardiovascular disease [26]. At present, the prevalence of obesity is rapidly increasing globally and gradually becoming a major public health issue. Research shows that obesity is not only associated with an increased risk of cancer, but also increases the risk of cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, musculoskeletal disease and infection [27]. Obesity is the main risk factor for cardiovascular disease. Meanwhile, the high fat content caused by obesity is the main factor promoting insulin resistance. IR, Obesity and HTN are closely related, and they are associated with mechanisms such as inflammation, adipocyte dysfunction, oxidative stress, endoplasmic reticulum stress, aging, hypoxia, and genetic changes [28]. Oxidative stress is an imbalance between the oxidative and antioxidant systems of cells and tissues, caused by the excessive production of oxidative free radicals and related reactive oxygen species (ROS) [29]. Oxidative stress is associated with almost all major human diseases, including neurodegenerative diseases, cardiovascular diseases, and cancer [30]. Research has shown that oxidative stress is a key mechanism of IR and a key factor in the occurrence and development of HTN and its related complications. In HTN state, NF −κB, p38 MAPK, PKC, and other signaling pathways are activated by ROS, including phosphorylation of INSR and IRS, activation of PI3K and GLUT4, ultimately leading to insulin resistance [31], [32]. Therefore, we can understand that the interaction between IR and oxidative stress is a potential mechanism for the pathogenesis of HTN, and there is a causal relationship between obesity and HTN, providing a reasonable approach for the subsequent treatment of HTN and its complications. By combining metabolomics, the underlying biomarkers and mechanisms of action can be revealed, which is also a hot topic in the study of the correlation between metabolomics and HTN.

Metabolomics can enhance HTN research [33]. Metabolomics has been used to explore biomarkers of HTN to determine its occurrence, development, prognosis, and evaluation [34]. Some reports combine metabolomics from animals, while others come from humans [35], [36]. A study analyzed early biomarkers of hypertensive cerebral microbleeds using liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) [37]. Initially, 18 C57 male mice were randomly divided into groups. Hematoxylin eosin and diaminobenzidine staining were used to evaluate the reliability of the model. Furthermore, through non targeted LC-MS analysis, 93 different metabolites were identified in the hypertensive intracerebral hemorrhage group (P < 0.05, VIP > 1). Among these potential biomarkers, 6 significantly reduced differential metabolites and 8 significantly increased differential metabolites were found. At the same time, we found that the HCMB group had statistically different pathways for arginine and purine metabolism (P < 0.05), and citrulline may be the most significant potential biomarker for HCMB (AUC > 0.85, P < 0.05). All of these potential biomarkers can serve as early biomarkers for hypertension. In addition, a study was conducted on the impact of cardiovascular outcomes on African Americans (AA) with hypertension (HTN) [38]. 91 AA adults were evaluated through a prospective randomized trial, and biometric and metabolomic variables were collected at baseline and 6 weeks after intervention. The study found that metabolomics analysis identified key biomarkers (P < 0.05), including C3, C5, 1-Met His, canine uric acid, PC ae 38:5, and choline, indicating that metabolomics analysis can be used as a prognostic technique for early identification of biomarkers as indicators for risk prediction.

In addition, multiple studies have shown that metabolomics provided new perspectives for the study of HTN in various aspects such as biomarker discovery, pathological mechanism research, and drug development. On the one hand, differential metabolites related to hypertension, such as amino acids, fatty acids, etc., can be screened as potential diagnostic biomarkers [4]. In addition, metabolomics can reveal metabolic abnormalities in hypertensive patients, such as energy metabolism, oxidative stress, etc., providing clues for understanding disease mechanisms [39]. In addition, key metabolic pathways and enzymes associated with hypertension can be identified, providing a basis for the discovery of drug targets [40].

In short, through keyword clustering, temporal changes in keyword clustering, and analysis of keyword emergence, the core of future development and knowledge frontiers can be discovered. Molecular docking in cardiovascular disease, intestinal flora, diabetes nephropathy and cluster analysis has shown an increasing trend in recent years, which is the core of this field in the future. Keywords such as protein and arginine are emerging explosive keywords in the fields of metabolomics and HTN, and are expected to become the forefront of knowledge for the future development of HTN. Based on previous research hotspots and combined with the explosive vocabulary of HTN in recent years, we can attempt to conduct research from a new perspective.

Challenges and limitations

Compared with previous systematic meta-analyses and narrative evaluations of HTN and metabolomics related research, this study is the first bibliometric study in the past 2 decades to map research in this field into a knowledge graph. We also used different bibliometric software for visualization analysis, providing visual feedback and data support. And by combining past hotspots, future cores, and cutting-edge knowledge in this field, provide more possibilities for new topics and directions.

However, it cannot be denied that this study has certain limitations. Firstly, although WOSCC is currently the most commonly used and authoritative bibliometric database, its data resources can only represent a certain field within a certain scope. In addition, due to the continuous updating of databases, the selection of database formats, manual screening and merging methods, and the built-in algorithms of bibliometric software, inevitably brought bias to our research. Moreover, only studies published in English were included, and the exploration of the research was only descriptive, focusing on conclusions that were not fully supported, and the exploration of the literature included in the study was not deep enough. Further in-depth and specific exploration is still needed in the future.

Conclusion

Metabolomics and HTN research have become an increasingly prominent field of study, attracting increasing attention and the number of researchers. A large number of experiments have established a causal relationship between metabolomics and HTN. However, there are still many uncertainties in the interaction mechanism between metabolomics and HTN, and further research is needed. Meanwhile, metabolomics may indirectly affect the development of HTN by influencing the progression of specific diseases. In addition, the impact of metabolomics on other cardiovascular diseases is gradually attracting the interest of researchers. In summary, we used bibliometric tools to analyze all articles in this field over the past 20 years, highlighting important and innovative works in various research directions, revealing hotspots and emerging trends, and providing valuable insights for future research work.

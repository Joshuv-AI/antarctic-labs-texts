Protocol for systematic review and multilevel meta-analysis comparing efficacy and safety of endoscopic versus open gluteal tendon repair

Introduction

Gluteus medius and minimus tendon tears—often referred to as the ‘rotator cuff tears of the hip’—are increasingly recognised as a common cause of chronic lateral hip pain and abductor dysfunction, particularly in middle-aged to older women.1 2 These tears affect approximately 10% of native hips in individuals over 60, up to 28% of osteoarthritic hips undergoing total hip arthroplasty (THA) and may occur even more frequently following (revision) THA, where they are now considered a major contributor to persistent postoperative pain.3,6 Notably, even asymptomatic gluteal pathology has been linked to inferior outcomes after THA.7

These tears commonly present with lateral hip pain, a positive Trendelenburg sign and impaired gait but are often initially misdiagnosed as trochanteric bursitis. Despite their clinical relevance, hip abductor tendon tears still remain underdiagnosed. Surgical repair is indicated when conservative treatment fails and imaging confirms tendon detachment.8 9 Traditionally, gluteal tendon repair has been performed via an open approach, involving detachment of the affected gluteus medius and minimus for tendon mobilisation and reattachment using bone anchors or transosseous tunnels—techniques analogous to shoulder rotator cuff repair.10,12 Over the past decade, endoscopic (arthroscopic) techniques have emerged, using small portals and suture anchors to perform minimally invasive refixation while preserving soft tissue.13 Current limited evidence suggests that both open and endoscopic repair yield comparable functional outcomes, including pain relief, patient satisfaction and strength recovery.14 15 However, open repair incurs a notably higher complication rate, with wound-healing issues, infections and haematomas occurring in 7–8% of cases versus approximately 0.7% following endoscopic repair.1 14 Re-tear rates remain low and similar between techniques (approximately 3–4%).1 Despite these promising trends, the existing evidence stems mainly from retrospective case series (Level IV), with few direct comparisons and no randomised controlled trials.10 14 16 A recent systematic review included 22 studies (611 hips) and concluded that both methods improve function and abductor strength, yet highlighted the lower complication rates associated with arthroscopic repair.1 Another systematic review echoed these findings but emphasised the need for higher-quality comparative research.14 Given the rising incidence of gluteal tendon tears, the increasing number of THAs and an ageing population with a growing prevalence of osteoarthritis, a comprehensive meta-analysis comparing open and endoscopic repair techniques would offer valuable insights—in pursuit of the most effective and least invasive treatment strategy for the benefit of the patient.

The aim of this study will be to perform a systematic review and multilevel meta-analysis to compare clinical outcomes, efficacy and safety between open versus endoscopic gluteal tendon repair.

Methods and analysis

Definition

Several terms are used in the literature to describe injuries of the lateral hip tendons, including gluteus medius tendon, gluteus minimus tendon, hip abductor tendon, gluteal tendon and hip rotator cuff. These terms largely refer to the same anatomical structures involved in abductor function. For clarity and consistency, we use the term gluteal tendon throughout this study protocol to encompass all these synonymous references.

Reporting standards and protocol registration

The study protocol was registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) on 11 July 2025 with registration number: CRD420251088765. The review will be reported in accordance with the updated Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines.17 A completed PRISMA checklist will be provided as Supplementary Material. This protocol was developed in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P) guidelines.18 The corresponding PRISMA-P checklist is available as online supplemental file 1.

Search strategy

A comprehensive database search will be conducted using PubMed, Embase, CENTRAL (Cochrane Library) and Epistemonikos, covering studies published up to a recent date. Boolean logic will guide search term combinations to identify eligible studies on gluteal tendon repair procedures. The search syntax will be adapted for each database and include terms such as: ((gluteus medius) OR (gluteus minimus) OR (gluteal tendon) OR (hip abductor)) AND ((repair) OR (reconstruction)) AND ((endoscopic) OR (arthroscopic) OR (open). No restrictions will be applied regarding language or publication year. A detailed search strategy is shown in table 1.

Study selection

Two reviewers (MV and AL) will independently perform a two-phase screening process. Titles and abstracts will be reviewed initially, followed by full-text screening of selected studies. Inclusion will require agreement between both reviewers, and any discrepancies will be resolved with input from a third reviewer (NR). Inter-rater reliability will be measured using the kappa statistic (κ).

Eligibility criteria (PICO)

Population (P): Human adults (≥18 years) with symptomatic partial- or full-thickness gluteus medius and/or minimus tendon tears, in native hips or after THA, confirmed by MRI/ultrasound or intra-operative findings.

Intervention (I): Endoscopic gluteal tendon repair, with or without trochanteric bursectomy.

Comparator (C): Open gluteal tendon repair.

Outcomes (O): Information is provided in the ‘Outcomes and prioritisation’ subheading.

Studies eligible for inclusion comprise randomised controlled trials and observational studies (prospective or retrospective), as well as case series. Exclusion criteria will be (1) studies in which operative technique is not clearly described; (2) studies lacking outcomes relevant to hip function or pain; and (3) case reports, narrative reviews and editorials will be excluded.

Data extraction

Two reviewers (AL and MV) will extract data independently, with disagreements resolved by consensus with a third reviewer (NR). Study selection and data management will be performed using Excel and/or dedicated systematic review software (eg, Rayyan or Covidence), depending on availability. Extracted variables will include author, publication year, country, sample size, demographics, study design, bias assessment, follow-up and outcome measures.

Outcomes and prioritisation

The primary outcomes of interest will include functional outcome scores, pain scores and complication rates. Functional outcomes will be assessed using validated instruments such as the International Hip Outcome Tool (iHOT), modified Harris Hip Score (mHHS), Non-Arthritic Hip Score (NAHS) and other comparable patient-reported outcome measures. Pain will be evaluated using the Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) or Numeric Rating Scale (NRS), depending on reporting. Where reported, we will extract Victorian Institute of Sport Assessment—Gluteal (VISA-G) scores alongside the general hip PROMs listed above.

Secondary outcomes will include return to sport or activity, operation time and specific complications (eg, infection, haematoma, re-tear and neurovascular injury). If multiple time points are reported, the final available follow-up will be prioritised. Where necessary, outcomes will be standardised to enable quantitative synthesis.

Quality assessment

Risk of bias will be assessed by two independent reviewers (MV and AL). The Cochrane RoB 2 tool19 will be applied to randomised controlled trials (RCTs), and the ROBINS-I tool20 to non-randomised studies. A third reviewer (NR) will mediate any disagreements. Potential publication bias will be assessed using Begg’s test or visualised via funnel plots. The Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE)21 approach will be used, where applicable, to assess the certainty of evidence across outcomes, considering study limitations, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias.

Statistical analysis

A multilevel meta-analytic approach22 under a frequentist framework will be used to assess treatment effects of open and endoscopic gluteal tendon repair. Random-effects modelling will employ inverse variance weighting and restricted maximum likelihood estimation with Hartung-Knapp adjustments. Continuous outcome data will be summarised as mean values with 95% CIs. Differences between subgroups will be evaluated using appropriate statistical tests. Heterogeneity will be assessed with the Higgins I² index and interpreted as low (<25%), moderate (25–75%) or high (>75%). All analyses will be supervised by a qualified statistician and conducted using the R packages meta and metafor. If sufficient data are available, subgroup analyses will be conducted based on study design (RCT versus observational) and repair technique complexity. Sensitivity analyses will assess the robustness of the results.

Amendments

This protocol does not represent an amendment of a previously completed or published protocol. However, we reserve the right to make justified deviations from the predefined plan based on the nature and availability of data encountered during study selection and data extraction. Specifically, outcomes reported and included studies may lead to adjustments in outcome prioritisation or analytic strategy. Likewise, the choice of statistical synthesis (eg, multilevel meta-analysis, conventional random-effects model or network meta-analysis) will be determined based on the structure and suitability of the retrieved data. Additionally, if substantial contributions are made during the course of the review, further authors may be added in accordance with standard authorship guidelines. Any substantial amendments will be transparently documented in the PROSPERO record and fully reported in the final publication.

Timeline and review status

The review will commence immediately after acceptance of this study protocol in BMJ Open. The systematic review and multilevel meta-analysis will be conducted as efficiently as possible, with anticipated completion approximately 3–6 months after initiation.

Ethics and dissemination

For queries regarding research governance for this secondary analysis, contact the Ethics Committee of Brandenburg Medical School Theodor Fontane (Chair: Professor Marcel Alexander Kamp, MD; ethikkommission@mhb-fontane.de). As this review uses only published, aggregate data and no identifiable information, formal ethics approval was not required under institutional policy.

We will disseminate findings via a peer-reviewed publication and conference presentations. The PROSPERO record (CRD420251088765) will be updated with any substantive amendments. On publication, we will make available the full search strategies, screening log (PRISMA flow), data-extraction template, risk-of-bias judgements and analysis code (R/meta, metafor) in a public repository (eg, OSF or GitHub), subject to journal policy. A brief plain-language summary will accompany the dataset to facilitate reuse by clinicians and researchers.

Patient and public statement

Patients and members of the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this protocol, as it synthesises previously published aggregate data. For the completed review, we plan to consult a patient representative with lived experience of gluteal tendon tears to review the plain-language summary and dissemination materials and to advise on the clinical relevance of prioritised outcomes.

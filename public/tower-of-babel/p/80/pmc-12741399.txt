Perspectives from the 2024 International Consortium for Spinal Genetics, Development and Disease (ICSGDD)
Authors: Nan Wu, Nancy Hadley-Miller, Ryan Gray, Stevana Schauer, Gregory Redding, Tian Cheng, Zefu Chen, Jianle Yang, Ronen Blecher, Paul Gerdhem, Carol A. Wise
Published: 2025

Introduction

The International Consortium for Spinal Genetics, Development and Disease (ICSGDD) is a global collaborative effort dedicated to advancing research on spinal deformities. Scoliosis, the most common musculoskeletal disorder in children, affects approximately 3% of the global population.1 Spinal deformities, including various types of scoliosis, are complex conditions with significant impacts on patients’ health and quality of life. Congenital scoliosis is caused by abnormal vertebral development during embryogenesis, often involving incomplete formation or segmentation of vertebrae.2 Genetic factors, such as TBX6 or PAX7, variations play a crucial role in its development,3,4 with recent studies identifying specific genes and molecular pathways involved.5 Treatment primarily focuses on surgical correction to prevent progressive deformity and associated complications. Idiopathic scoliosis (IS), the most common type, especially in adolescents, has an unclear etiology but is believed to be multifactorial, involving genetic predisposition, biomechanical factors, and potential neuroendocrine influences.6 Advances in genetic research have identified susceptibility genes and pathways related to disease progression. Treatment depends on the severity and progression risk, with mild cases often managed through observation and bracing, whereas severe cases may require surgical intervention.7 Syndromic scoliosis is associated with underlying syndromes such as neurofibromatosis or Marfan syndrome, in which the spinal deformity is part of a broader clinical picture. Management involves addressing both the spinal deformity and the associated systemic issues, often requiring a multidisciplinary approach.8 Degenerative scoliosis typically occurs in adults because of age-related changes, such as disc degeneration and vertebral fractures. Research has highlighted the roles of angiogenesis, inflammation, extracellular matrix degradation, and hormonal influences in its pathogenesis.9 Treatment aims to relieve pain, improve function, and stabilize the spine, with options ranging from conservative measures, such as physical therapy and pain management to surgical correction in severe cases.10 Overall, ongoing research continues to enhance our understanding of these conditions, driving the development of more effective and personalized treatment strategies. Current management of scoliosis in growing children generally includes the following: (1) observation of curves <25°; (2) bracing for curves progressing >25°; and (3) surgical intervention: In cases which the spinal curvature exceeds >40° to 45°, or if bracing fails to halt progression, surgical intervention may be necessary. The most common surgical procedure for scoliosis is spinal fusion and instrumentation, other surgical options include growing friendly technique or vertebral body tethering for younger children who are still growing.11,12 (4) Physical therapy and exercise: although not a primary treatment for halting curve progression, physical therapy and specific scoliosis-specific exercises (such as the Schroth method) may be used as adjuncts to bracing or observation.13 (5) Emerging therapies: research into noninvasive treatments and gene therapies is ongoing, with the hope of developing new approaches to manage or even prevent scoliosis.14

The ICSGDD was established in 2017 through the merger of 2 preexisting consortia: the International Consortium for Vertebral Anomalies and Scoliosis and the International Consortium for Scoliosis Genetics (ICSG). Recognizing the overlap in their goals and membership, International Consortium for Vertebral Anomalies and Scoliosis and ICSG merged in 2017 to form the ICSGDD, with the aim of fostering multidisciplinary research that bridges the gap between basic science and clinical applications. The ICSGDD serves as a platform for researchers to share data, collaborate on large-scale projects, and develop consensus strategies for advancing the field of spinal genetics and developmental biology.

The 2024 annual meeting of the ICSGDD, held in Denver, Colorado, brought together leading researchers, clinicians, and geneticists from around the world to discuss the latest advancements in the field. The meeting focused on recent breakthroughs in genomics, developmental biology, and translational research, with the goal of identifying new therapeutic targets and improving clinical outcomes for patients with spinal deformities. Key topics included the genetic basis of scoliosis, the role of environmental factors in vertebral malformations, and the development of novel animal models for studying spinal development. Below, we summarize keynote research presentations and subsequent discussions at the conference.

Keynote presentations

1.Ronen Blecher (The Weizmann Institute of Science): Novel mechanisms governing spine development and disease were investigated, with emphasis on proprioceptive signaling and the slow-skeletal muscle protein TNNT1. Well-realigned early bone fractures were consistently observed in wild-type neonates, a process abolished in Runx3-null mice that lack functional proprioception and develop pronounced scoliosis, indicating that proprioceptive input is essential for physiological spinal morphogenesis. Integrated transcriptomic and proteomic profiling identified TNNT1 as a differentially enriched factor. TNNT1-null mice exhibited mild progressive scoliosis and thoracolumbar vertebral fusion, accompanied by accelerated disc ossification detectable from postnatal day 10. Histologically, collagen invasion and marked deterioration of collagen type II were evident in the intervertebral discs. These data demonstrate that TNNT1 expressed in muscle fibers protects the thoracolumbar segment from degenerative mineralization. A neural-skeletal axis is proposed that coordinates spinal development and maintenance, offering potential therapeutic targets for spinal pathologies. Future studies will further delineate this axis and evaluate its translational relevance.2.Paul Gerdhem (Department of Surgical Sciences, Uppsala University): Moderate-grade adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) may be treated with full-time bracing. For patients who reject full-time bracing, the effectiveness of nighttime bracing in patients with AIS is controversial. A randomized controlled trial compared 3 different regimes in individuals aged 9 to 17 years with moderate scoliosis and at least 1 year of remaining growth; treatment with a nighttime brace, treatment with scoliosis specific exercise, and nonspecific physical activity. Nighttime bracing prevented curve progression to a larger extent than non-specific physical activity, whereas scoliosis specific exercise showed no benefit compared with nonspecific physical activity. These results suggest that NB may be an effective alternative intervention in patients rejecting full-time bracing.15 In addition, the nighttime bracing group in the randomized controlled trial (RCT) was compared with a group that declined study participation and received a full-time brace. The risk of progression was similar in both the nighttime and full-time brace groups, although there was a trend toward more surgeries for scoliosis in the nighttime brace group.16

3.Stevana Schauer (University of Oregon): Scoliosis is a common spinal disorder; yet, the underlying causes remain largely unknown. Zebrafish was used to model potential genetic causes of scoliosis and determine mechanisms through which the spine normally takes shape. For many years, there have been enigmatic links between scoliosis and serotonin: some scoliosis patients have lower levels of serotonin, whereas removal of the pineal gland, a major source of serotonin, results in scoliosis-like spinal curves in some animal models. To test whether serotonin plays a role in spine morphology, zebrafish with a loss-of-function variant in Tryptophan hydroxylase 2 (Tph2), an enzyme essential for serotonin synthesis, was utilized. As a result, tph2 variant zebrafish developed significant spinal curves beginning in early adolescence, with evidence of vertebral fusions by adulthood. These results suggest that Tph2 is essential for zebrafish spine morphology and that variants model a scoliosis-like condition.4.Gregory Redding (University of Washington School of Medicine): Long-term pulmonary outcomes of children with early onset scoliosis (EOS) are unclear based on sparse published case series, which often do not account for growth of children. Most children with EOS experience lung function changes that are unchanged or decline during 4 years of surgical treatment. Children with congenital scoliosis declined significantly. However, one-fourth of children experienced a >8% improvement during surgical treatment, but no one etiology was associated with improvement. Patient characteristics that predispose to improvement or decline need further study.5.Ryan Gray (University of Texas at Austin): Cell size is a key contributor to tissue morphogenesis. As a notable example, growth plate hypertrophic chondrocytes use cellular biogenesis and disproportionate fluid uptake to expand 10 to 20 times in size to drive lengthening of endochondral bone. Similarly, notochordal cells expand to one of the largest cell types in the developing embryo to drive axial extension. In zebrafish, the notochord vacuolated cells undergo vacuole fusion to form a single large, fluid-filled vacuole that fills the cytoplasmic space and contributes to vacuolated cell expansion. When this process goes away, the notochord lacks sufficient hydrostatic pressure to support vertebral bone deposition resulting in adult spines with misshapen vertebral bones and scoliosis. However, it remains unclear whether endochondral bone and the notochord share common genetic and cellular mechanisms for regulating cell and tissue expansion. The 5'-inositol phosphatase gene inppl1a was shown to regulate notochord expansion independent of vacuole fusion, thereby genetically decoupling these processes. Inppl1a-dependent expansion of vacuolated cells establishes the normal mechanical properties of the notochord and is required for subsequent development of a straight vertebral column. Additionally, inppl1a is essential for endochondral growth plate formation and bone lengthening in teleosts, mirroring the role of human INPPL1 in the skeletal dysplasia opsismodysplasia. Overall, this work reveals a shared mechanism of cell size regulation that influences disparate tissues critical for skeletal development and short-stature disorders.6.Tian Cheng (Department of Surgical Sciences, Uppsala University): Several candidate genes have been found to be associated with IS.17 Gene expression in patients with idiopathic scoliosis and controls and between convex and concave side was studied. Twenty four significant genes were identified between the affected and controls. In the analysis between the concave and convex groups, 18 significant genes were identified. No significant differences were detected in the right versus left comparison for either the affected or control. This study reports several differentially expressed genes for IS patients and controls and between concave and convex sides. These genes could aid in unrevealing the etiology behind IS.7.Zefu Chen (Southern Medical University, Nanfang Hospital): Mendelian disorders affect millions of births annually, highlighting the need for molecular diagnoses. Current methods rely on genotype-phenotype associations in literature, but challenges include limited database associations, difficulty quantifying phenotype probabilities, and reliance on structured data, such as Human Phenotype Ontology codes. Our study addresses these by using large language models (LLMs) to create an automated phenotype-genotype database. By fine-tuning an LLM, the study retrieved 1.4 million articles and extracted over 2.4 million phenotype-genotype associations, improving recall (recovered from the literature) by 97.2% and precision (verified as correct) by 47.3% compared with traditional methods. A patient-level database, PhenoGemini, was developed, improving gene prioritization by 72.8% to 118.5%. Additionally, the PhenoGemini GPT pipeline boosts diagnostic accuracy by 10.8% in difficult cases.

Meeting summary

The 2024 ICSGDD meeting highlighted significant progress in understanding the genetic and biological factors contributing to spinal deformities. Future research should focus on translating these findings into clinical applications. Key recommendations from consortium members to advance the field include the following:1.Standardization of Phenotyping: There is a need for uniform clinical parameters to improve the correlation between genetic findings and phenotypic expression in scoliosis patients.2.Environmental Factors: The role of environmental factors such as hypoxia and maternal diabetes in vertebral malformations was emphasized, with a call for more systematic studies to understand their mechanisms.3.Animal Models: Zebrafish and mouse models continue to be valuable tools for studying the genetic and environmental basis of scoliosis, particularly for understanding gene-environment interactions.4.Translational Research: The meeting underscored the importance of translating genetic discoveries into clinical applications, including the development of gene therapies and personalized treatment strategies.5.Large Language Models and AI: The integration of LLMs and artificial intelligence (AI) in disease diagnosis was emphasized. The database and software like Phenoapt developed using LLMs, enhanced gene prioritization and diagnostic accuracy, particularly in difficult cases.18 This highlights the potential of AI in transforming clinical workflows and improving the diagnosis of complex genetic disorders.6.High-Quality Data: High-quality data from clinical RCTs and basic experiments is essential for research. RCTs provide robust evidence that guides effective treatment decisions, ensuring validity and supporting regulatory compliance. Meanwhile, high-quality data enhance reproducibility, which is critical for advancing scientific knowledge and improving clinical practice.

Spatio-temporal analysis of COVID-19 lockdown effect to survive in the US counties using ANN
Authors: Reyhane Jalali, Hossein Etemadfard
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aims to quantify the effectiveness of lockdown as a non-pharmacological solution for managing the COVID-19 pandemic. Daily COVID-19 death counts were collected for four states: California, Georgia, New Jersey, and South Carolina. The effectiveness of the lockdown was studied and the number of people saved during 7 days was evaluated. Five neural network models (MLP, FFNN, CFNN, ENN, and NARX) were implemented, and the results indicate that FFNN is the best prediction model. Based on this model, the total number of survivors over a 7-day period is 211, 270, 989, and 60 in California, Georgia, New Jersey, and South Carolina, respectively. The coefficients and weights of the FFNN for each state differ due to various factors, including socio-demographic conditions and the behavior of citizens towards lockdown laws. New Jersey and South Carolina have the most lockdowns and the least.Keywords: Spatiotemporal analysis, Lockdown, ANN, GIS, HealthcareSubject terms: Epidemiology, Experimental models of disease, Health policy, Machine learning

Introduction

COVID-19 has been the most important phenomenon in leading to devastating socioeconomic, health, and political consequences in the 21st century1. These impacts have varied across countries, with the United States being recognized as the most affected nation globally.2. Human casualties were among the most significant concerns of statesmen. To reduce and prevent these casualties, a combination of pharmaceutical and non-pharmacological measures was implemented to limit the transmission of the virus at both local and broader levels3. The non-pharmacological interventions include lockdown, maintaining social distancing in public, using masks in public places, lockdown of suspected cases, and isolation of confirmed cases4. Each of these measures prevents the deaths of many citizens, but their success rate depends on several factors, including the willingness of community members to follow the prescribed measures5. To make a useful and applicable decision regarding limiting the spread of this virus, it appears necessary to study its spatial and temporal conditions6.

Studying the distribution and spread of disease over space and time is the main topic of health geography and spatial epidemiology7. The spatial patterns of a disease often reveal the relationship between potential risk factors and a geographical area8. These patterns have shown that the spread of COVID-19 has not followed a homogeneous pattern either globally or within individual countries9. Since each country or state has a different strategy and action plan based on their abilities in prevention, detection, response strategies, enabling performance, and operational readiness, the reasons for these patterns are not clearly understood10. In addition, statesmen have implemented varying levels of quarantine and control measures to prevent and contain the epidemic. These measures, which regulate population movement, have led to variations in the COVID-19 spread pattern. Therefore, the spatio-temporal analysis of the spread is critical11.

The Geospatial Information System (GIS) is used for the spatial analysis of geographic data and is also applied in health sciences to analyze the behavior of various phenomena such as the spread of the coronavirus disease. Its applications in preparing geographic distribution maps of disease outbreaks, visualizing transmission trends, and modeling environmental aspects of disease occurrence are highly useful. Additionally, other analytical and modeling methods and algorithms have been introduced in the medical field, with Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) being among the most common12. They provide accurate and usable insights that are far beyond the capabilities of existing methods13. In addition, ML is used to manage big data and intelligently predict the spread of disease14. Long and Ehrenfeld (2020) claimed in their review that forecasting through AI methods can reduce the effects of the pandemic crisis and be applied in the diagnosis and prediction of infection15. Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), as one of their sub-branches, are used for modeling and estimating complex and non-linear problems and for time series modeling16. They have been used to provide platforms for tracking social distance17, and monitoring and controlling the spread of COVID-1918. Additionally, studies have aimed at identifying clinical and social factors related to the risk of contracting and dying from COVID-1919, developing spatial risk maps20, predicting the trend and peak of the epidemic21, and finally developing Vaccination strategies22 have been presented.

In a study, Elsheikh AH et al. 2021 stated that while both statistical and artificial intelligence (AI) approaches have been used for forecasting the prevalence of COVID-19, AI approaches have demonstrated superior accuracy23. Additionally, in another study, they used the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network to forecast COVID-19 cases, recoveries, and deaths in Saudi Arabia and six other countries24. Furthermore, in 2020, Saba AI and Elsheikh AH used both statistical and artificial intelligence-based approaches to model and forecast the prevalence of the COVID-19 epidemic in Egypt25. Moreover, in the 2022 study by Pınar Cihan, the effectiveness of machine learning methods in predicting critical metrics related to the COVID-19 pandemic in Turkey was evaluated. The random forest algorithm emerged as the most successful method for predicting the number of intensive care patients, intubated patients, and deaths26. Additionally, Al-qaness et al. (2021) proposed a short-term forecasting model using an enhanced Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Inference System (ANFIS) for Russia and Brazil27. Also, Issa et al. (2022) applied an enhanced BA-PSO algorithm, utilizing an infection propagation mechanism, to detect biological sub-sequences in the context of COVID-1928. In another study, Mohamed Abd Elaziz et al. (2021) proposed a hybrid framework for COVID-19 image classification using deep learning and swarm-based algorithms29. Pınar Cihan in Another Study forecasts the number of fully vaccinated individuals against COVID-19 and evaluates future vaccination rates for achieving herd immunity across different regions, including the US, Asia, Europe, Africa, South America, and globally, using the ARIMA model30. Lastly, Griva et al. (2021) aimed to investigate the prevalence and determinants of vaccine hesitancy in adults and parents regarding vaccinating their children in Singapore using an integrated social cognition model31.

On the other hand, numerous studies have examined lockdown strategies as a means to prevent the spread of COVID-19 and their impact on the personal, social, economic, and political aspects of citizens, with many of these studies employing artificial intelligence algorithms32,33. For instance, Hong et al. (2021) studied the impact of lockdown timing on the transmission of COVID-19 in U.S. counties using machine learning methods. They concluded that lockdown timing is the most significant risk factor associated with the number of cumulative daily cases in these counties34. Santos Siqueira et al. (2020) evaluated the incidence, mortality, hospitalization, admission to intensive care units, and recovery rates in each autonomous community of Spain. Their findings showed decreasing trends in these metrics after two weeks of lockdown35. In another study, Emanuel Plan et al. (2021) investigated the temporal aspects of the lockdown in Ho Chi Minh City and predicted the progression of the disease outbreak in terms of the total number of confirmed cases. They evaluated various scenarios concerning the start and duration of the lockdown36. Singh et al. (2021) evaluated the impacts of implementing and lifting non-pharmaceutical interventions (NPIs) in U.S. counties on the daily growth rate of COVID-19 cases. Their analysis using various methods indicated a reduction in cases37. The impact of interventions on SARS-CoV-2 transmissibility was studied in Italy using a Bayesian approach38. Domenico et al. used a stochastic age-structured transmission model, integrating data on age profiles and social contacts in Île-de-France, to (i) assess the epidemic in the region, (ii) evaluate the impact of the lockdown, and (iii) propose potential exit strategies and estimate their effectiveness. They estimated the reproductive number at 3.18 (95% confidence interval) before the lockdown and at 0.68 during the lockdown, attributed to an 81% reduction in the average number of contacts39. Moreover, in 2021, Zubaya et al. developed an MLP model using data from China as the base country. This model incorporated factors affecting the spread, severity, and mortality of COVID-19, as well as the impact of social distancing on these factors40.

With the emergence of the COVID-19 pandemic and the threat it posed to the lives of citizens, quarantines were implemented to control the virus, and research has been conducted to evaluate the effects of quarantine. Despite the extensive research on COVID-19, no study has aimed to assess the role of quarantine in saving lives using both neural network tools and geographic information systems (GIS) along with spatiotemporal analyses. Neural networks and GIS have historically been highly effective and useful in the health sector and disease prediction. Moreover, the spread of infectious diseases through populations is inherently a spatiotemporal process, making spatiotemporal analysis of the virus essential for better decision-making regarding limiting its spread in COVID-19 studies. Additionally, quantifying the effectiveness of quarantine can provide a more tangible understanding of quarantine efficiency in different locations with varying conditions. Therefore, in this study, daily COVID-19 mortality data and quarantine schedules for each state were collected and entered into MATLAB for modeling using neural network algorithms such as MLP, FFNN, CFNN, ELMAN, and NARX. Evaluation criteria such as RMSE, MAE, MAPE, and R2 were calculated for each model, and the optimal model was selected. With the chosen optimal model, COVID-19 mortality rates in the absence of quarantine were predicted, and the effectiveness of quarantine in each state was assessed.

Methods

Several types of neural networks (NN) have different functions and applications and can be categorized based on the direction of information flow and processing41. In this research, four common types of artificial neural networks (ANN) have been applied, each of which is well-suited to the data structure. These are Multi Layer Perceptron (MLP), Feed Forward Neural Network (FFNN), Cascade Forward Neural Network (CFNN), Elman, and Nonlinear AutoRegressive eXogenous model (NARX). These models are briefly explained below.

MLP

A multi-layer neural network is a fully connected class of ANN that differs widely in design and is one of the most common neural network models used. It consists of an input layer and an output layer, which are connected through one or more hidden layers. The input and output layers remain the same, while multiple hidden layers can be present between them42. A conceptual view of the MLP is illustrated in Fig. 1.

The structure of the Multilayer perceptron neural network.

The input layer receives the input data to be processed. The required task such as prediction and classification is performed by the output layer. An arbitrary number of hidden layers that are placed in between the input and output layers are the true computational engine of the MLP43.

FFNN

Feed-Forward Neural Networks are widely used ANN methods for studying the relationship between independent variables, which act as network inputs, and dependent variables, which are specified as outputs44. A series of inputs enter the layer and are multiplied by their respective weights. Each weighted value is then summed to produce the total weighted input value. The schematic view of this process is shown in Fig. 2.

The structure of the feedforward neural network.

In such networks, the signal is propagated from the input layer to the output layer. The number of neurons in the input layer corresponds to the number of input parameters, while the number of neurons in the output layer is adjusted according to the number of output parameters45.

CFNN

The Cascade-Forward Neural Network (CFNN) is a class of neural network similar to feed-forward networks. However, in CFNNs, the input signal is connected to each hidden layer through a weight, allowing for feedback connections from previous layers46. The structure of the CFNN is illustrated in Fig. 3.

The structure of the Cascade Forward neural network.

The design of a CFNN involves generating additional nodes and connections, incorporating all neurons from both the input and hidden layers47. This involves two types of connections: the first connection is from the input layer to each hidden layer in the network, and the second connection is from each hidden layer to the subsequent layers in the network48. In other words, each neuron in a CFNN is connected to all neurons in the preceding layer. The CFNN topology begins with input neurons and includes output neurons, similar to those in traditional NN. It aims to minimize its residual error as well as the newly calculated error when new neurons are added to the network. This process continues until reaching a lower error value in the network, which is why this network is called a waterfall49.

Elman neural network (ENN)

Elman neural network is a dynamic recurrent network in which a context layer is added to a standard FFNN50. Figure 4 illustrates Elman's concept.

The structure of the Elman neural network51.

As shown above, the physical layout of the ENN is broadly divided into four layers: the input layer, the hidden layer, the context layer, and the output layer. The context layer's purpose is to store the output of the hidden layer, allowing the output of the hidden layer to be connected with its input through the delay and memory provided by the context layer52. Elman networks can learn time-varying patterns and are particularly suitable for discrete-time series problems53.

Nonlinear AutoRegressive eXogenous model (NARX)

The NARX network is a dynamical neural architecture commonly used for input–output modeling of nonlinear dynamical systems. NARX is a type of recurrent neural network, which is called a non-linear self-concept external model. In other words, it differs from other recurrent neural networks because the iteration is only applied to the outputs (Fig. 5)54.

NARX neural network structure55.

The structure of a NARX network, as shown in Fig. 5, includes an input layer, a feedback layer, an output layer, input delays, and output delays56. The main advantages of NARX networks compared to other ANN approaches include faster convergence in reaching optimal weights for connections between neurons and input parameters, reduced calibration periods, and increased model effectiveness57.

Data

Due to the widespread lack of testing and the non-testing of a portion of the population during the early part of the epidemic, data on the incidence of COVID-19 is unreliable. Therefore, this study focuses on modeling and analyzing data related to COVID-19 mortality. Considering that lockdown measures in the United States have been implemented at the state level, reports on the timing of these measures, as announced by the governors of each state, have been utilized58–61. The number of COVID-19 deaths was gathered daily based on reports from state and local health agencies for each state62. In addition, supplementary and additional data have been used for detailed analysis. They include demographic, social, and spatial data. Demographic data includes US Census data for each state63, social data includes social distancing index, percentage staying at home, out-of-county trips percentage, percentage of out-of-state trips, working from home percentage, and percentage of people over 60 years obtained from the University of Maryland's COVID-19 Impact Analysis Platform64. Also, the spatial data includes shapefiles of the United States cartographic boundaries obtained from the Census Bureau's geographic database and used for implementation in the GIS environment65.

Case Study

The study area includes the four states of the US, California (CA), Georgia (GA), New Jersey (NJ), and South Carolina (SC). These states have been selected to account for the diverse characteristics influencing the spread of the coronavirus. The study area can be seen in the figure below.

Figure 6 shows the geographic distribution of four states and displays their population density per capita on a spectral gradient map, which was created using ArcGIS software. Additionally, because lockdown measures have been implemented at the state level, the start dates of the lockdown vary, as illustrated in the figure. According to the order, restrictions began in California (the first state) on March 19, and in South Carolina (the last state) on April 7. These states also differ in terms of population indicators: California is the most populous state, while New Jersey has the highest population density among U.S. states. In terms of climate, these states have a subtropical climate. During the period studied in this research (spring, specifically March and April), the weather conditions are conducive to outdoor activities and increased mobility. Moreover, all four states have significant and busy international airports, with Georgia boasting the busiest international airport in the world in terms of passenger and aircraft traffic. In addition to the aforementioned features, each state exhibits unique indicators such as mobility, social distancing, the percentage of individuals staying at home, the percentage of trips outside the state, the percentage of people working from home, and the percentage of individuals over 60 years old. Implementation

The implementation of this research was conducted in two parts: one component was developed in a GIS environment using ArcGIS software, while the other involved neural networks, which were developed using MATLAB. To model COVID-19 mortality, and considering that most deaths reflect infections that occurred 10 to 12 days earlier, data were extracted for the first day of lockdown in each state, 10 days prior to the lockdown, and for the first 10 days of the lockdown. Since the number of data points in a time series affects the effectiveness of a forecasting model, this study considered a 7-day period before the implementation of the lockdown for prediction, due to the limited amount of data available. The input data were used to model the five methods introduced in the second section. To evaluate the performance of each method, four assessment criteria were defined, and their values were calculated over 100 executions. Finally, the best method was chosen, and based on that, the mortality trend was predicted for 7 days (after those 20 days) in four states.

While, this state has the lowest population density among the four states. Therefore, the results demonstrate the significant impact of social distancing, staying at home, working from home, and population density on the effectiveness of lockdown measures.

Conclusion

This study aimed to quantify the effectiveness of lockdown as a non-pharmacological strategy for managing the COVID-19 pandemic in the United States using neural networks. Therefore, the effectiveness of the lockdowns implemented by the governors in the states was assessed, and the number of individuals saved over a 7-day period was evaluated. To achieve this, five neural network models (MLP, FFNN, CFNN, ENN, and NARX) were implemented across the four states of California, Georgia, New Jersey, and South Carolina. The results of the RMSE, MAE, MAPE, and R2 analyses indicated that the FFNN model is the most accurate for prediction.

The coefficients and weights of the FFNN for each state vary, which may be attributed to factors such as differing socio-demographic conditions and variations in citizens' adherence to lockdown measures. Given these factors and the population size of each state, the number of individuals saved varied across states. For instance, the impact of lockdown in New Jersey has been notably significant, resulting in the preservation of 989 lives over a 7-day period. California exhibited an upward trend over the 7 days, with a total of 211 lives saved, while Georgia had 270 lives saved over the same period. Similarly, South Carolina, which has the lowest number of survivors among the four states, saved 60 lives over the 7-day period. Even though this is the smallest number, each saved life is significant.

The proposed model demonstrates the capability to predict trends that have not yet occurred and is recommended for such applications, including research like this one, which examines the number of lives saved. It is recommended to incorporate data from additional provinces and countries into the modeling process and to analyze the impact of local decision-makers on the outcomes.

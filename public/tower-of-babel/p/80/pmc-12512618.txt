Reciprocal mediation between alexithymia and post-traumatic growth as influencing factors of depression: a cross-sectional analysis in hospitalized older patients with chronic non-communicable diseases
Authors: Xiaohui Wang, Jing Wen, Ying Wu, Ziyan Xiong, Li Cong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPositive psychology suggests combining negative and positive emotions for research, which is beneficial for promoting mental health. Depression is frequently reported among older patients with chronic non-communicable diseases. This study aimed to analyze the status and influencing factors of depression in hospitalized older patients undergoing chronic non-communicable diseases and explore the relationships between alexithymia (negative emotion) and post-traumatic growth (positive emotion) on depression (negative emotion).MethodsWe conducted a cross-sectional survey using simple random sampling of hospitalized older patients with chronic non-communicable diseases admitted to three tertiary hospitals in Changsha from November 2023 to February 2024. The data were collected by the questionnaire consisting of demographic characteristics, Self-rating Depression Scale, twenty-item Toronto Alexithymia Scale, and Post-traumatic Growth Inventory. We then performed statistical analyses and constructed structural models.ResultsA total of 321 hospitalized geriatrics with NCDs were surveyed, of whom 77.88% were diagnosed with depression. Self-care ability and post-traumatic growth are protective factors against depression, while alexithymia is a risk factor that has been linked to higher levels of depressive symptoms. In addition, alexithymia mediated the relationship between post-traumatic growth and depression, and post-traumatic growth also played a mediating role between alexithymia and depression.ConclusionsDuring hospitalization, the depression situation of older patients with chronic non-communicable diseases is very serious. Alexithymia and post-traumatic growth not only directly affect depression, but also play an intermediary role in indirectly affecting depression. Medical staff should enhance patients’ emotional regulation and expression, promote post-traumatic recovery, and thereby improve their depressive psychology.

Introduction

With the severe problem of global population aging, chronic non-communicable disease (NCD) becomes a major burden and represents a serious challenge for healthcare systems. Approximately 62.8% of the residents aged 45 years and over in China are reported to suffer from at least one NCD, and the prevalence of multimorbidity is higher for the elderly population [1]. Diabetes, cardiovascular disease, cancer, and chronic respiratory diseases are the most representative NCDs, which seriously affect people’s quality of life [2]. Influenced by medical insurance, expenses, and expected length of stay, older patients are more willing to seek inpatient care in secondary or tertiary hospitals [3]. Although the current medical environment attaches great importance to hospital-to-home transitional care for geriatrics of NCDs, hospitalization and rehospitalization rates are still high [4]. With the repeated attacks of the disease, the increase in hospitalization times, and the decrease in living ability, the psychological state of older patients with NCDs is prone to distortion, manifested as anxiety, depression, insomnia, and even suicide.

Depression is universal comorbidity in patients with NCDs, and it can be considered a mental illness whose pathophysiology overlaps with other systemic diseases [5]. Due to the long duration and low cure rate, patients with NCDs are prone to depression, which reduces their quality of life and hinders the prognosis of the disease. One hospital-based case-control study demonstrated that the inpatients undergoing NCDs were 3-fold more likely to commit suicide than those without physical conditions, and the risk increased as the number of comorbidities increased [6]. Depression and related psychological disorders can seriously reduce quality of life, impair treatment compliance, increase functional disabilities, and even increase the risk of death. The importance of the interaction between emotional regulation and past behavior in the mental health of patients with chronic diseases cannot be ignored [7]. To strengthen the full life cycle health management of older patients with NCDs, it is crucial to manage the patient’s emotions and provide psychological care during hospitalization.

Alexithymia is a personality trait, which can also be a psychological emotion or secondary symptom of some physical or mental diseases [8]. Patients are unable to correctly identify and express their needs with caregivers and doctors, leading to ineffective disease management, the accumulation of negative emotions, and the worsening of the condition. High-level alexithymia occurs in geriatrics with NCDs, which seriously affects patients’ psychological status and quality of life [9]. A cross-sectional study on COPD patients demonstrated that the prevalence of alexithymia in COPD patients was 23.63% and depression was an independent risk factor with a high correlation with alexithymia [10]. Depressive symptoms and alexithymia interact with each other, they can aggravate the disease burden of geriatric and are a primary focus of chronic disease management [11, 12]. Assisting patients to produce positive emotions is conducive to reconstructing emotion regulation strategies and inhibiting the occurrence of alexithymia and depression.

Post-traumatic growth (PTG) is characterized by positive psychological changes arising from adversity, trauma, or highly challenging life circumstances [13]. Chronic disease is a potentially traumatic experience, and long-term treatment and monitoring will erase the positive attitude of patients and lead to pessimism. Inducing PTG in patients improves mental state levels and resilience while reducing depression and anxiety. Romeo et al. [14] found that PTG is a positive outcome of chronic illness, while the psychological distress represented by depression is a passive outcome, and positive psychological changes generated by PTG reduce the occurrence of depressive symptoms. Moreover, a cross-sectional study illustrated that alexithymia is associated with some factors of PTG when individuals experience traumatic events, as resilience plays a positive role as the primary mechanism of PTG, while alexithymia plays a defensive role, and the stronger the resilience, the lower the incidence of alexithymia [15]. Previous international literature has consistently shown that alexithymia is a risk factor for depression, while PTG can serve as a protective factor [9, 16]. However, the population in Chinese background research is mostly healthy individuals such as children, adolescents, and college students, and there is a lack of relevant research on hospitalized elderly patients with chronic non communicable diseases [17].

Based on the above research and considering that Bidirectional and Recurrent Models propose that the relationship between variables is interdependent [18, 19], we assume that alexithymia and PTG are intertwined and jointly affect the ultimate state of depression, but their pathways and directions are worth verifying. However, there is currently no research exploring the relationship between these three in older patients of NCDs who are hospitalized. Therefore, using cross-sectional survey data, the current study aimed to investigate the current situation and influencing factors of depression in older adults with NCDs during hospitalization and examine the relationship between depression, alexithymia, and PTG to raise the attention of patients and caregivers to depression and provide a scientific basis for clinical personnel to develop depression prevention strategies and intervention measures for hospitalized older patients with NCDs.

Methods

Participants

The study was conducted in Changsha, China. The sampling frame consisted of all 17 tertiary comprehensive hospitals in the city. Three hospitals were selected as survey sites using simple random sampling (via a random number table). During the study period (from November 2023 to February 2024), we recruited participants using a consecutive sampling method. All older patients (aged ≥ 60 years) with non-communicable chronic diseases (NCDs) who were admitted to the selected hospitals and met the inclusion criteria were invited to participate in the study. The inclusion criteria for participation were: (1) diagnosed as one or more NCDs; (2) aged 60 years or over; (3) having clear consciousness and expression skills; (4) being willing to participate in this study. The exclusion criteria required: (1) complicated with severe functional damage to organs such as the heart and brain; (2) having a history of concomitant mental illness. A total of 321 patients were included in this study, all of whom were anonymous and signed an informed consent form. All procedures were approved by the Ethics Committee and the managers of the hospital departments involved.

Measurement

Information questionnaire

Researchers independently designed a general information questionnaire for older inpatients with NCDs, including 10 items: gender, age, number of children, length of stay, education level, source of monthly income, duration and number of NCDs, self-care ability, and primary caregiver.

Self-rating depression scale

Self-rating Depression Scale (SDS) was compiled by Zung [20], which can quite intuitively reflect the subjective feelings of patients with depression and their changes in treatment. SDS contains 20 items organized into 5 interrelated dimensions, a 4-point Likert scale rated by respondents based on the application of each item in the past week, with raw scores ranging from 20 to 80. Then, the sum of the raw scores is divided by 80 to obtain the index scores. According to the index scores, depression is divided into 3 levels to describe the severity of depressive symptoms: Index scores 50%~59% are defined as mild depression; Index scores 60%~69% are defined as moderate depression; Index scores 70% and over are defined as severe depression [21]. As a convenient tool for identifying depressive symptoms in older adults with NCDs, the SDS has good internal consistency (α = 0.86) and has been psychometrically evaluated [22]. Cronbach’s alpha for our study was 0.90.

Twenty-item Toronto alexithymia scale

Twenty-item Toronto Alexithymia Scale (TAS-20), jointly developed by Bagby, Parker and Taylor [23]. TAS-20 measures alexithymia from 3 dimensions: difficulties identifying feelings, difficulties describing feelings, and externally oriented thinking. TAS-20 consists of 20 items and is evaluated using the 5-point Likert scale (ranging from 1 to 5). Scores range from 20 to 100 with higher scores indicating greater levels of alexithymia. TAS-20 is characterized by high internal consistency (α = 0.81) and test-retest reliability (γ = 0.77) [24], with a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.86 in our research.

Post-traumatic growth inventory

The Post-traumatic Growth Inventory (PTGI) was developed by Tedeschi and Calhoun [25], which is composed of 21 items organized into 5 dimensions: relating to others, new possibilities, personal strength, spiritual change, and appreciation of life. Each item was chosen from 0 = “I did not experience this change as a result of my crisis” to 5 = “I experienced this change to a very great degree as a result of my crisis”. Total scores range from 0 to 105, with higher scores indicating easier growth after trauma. The PTGI displays excellent internal consistency (α = 0.90) and test-retest reliability (γ = 0.71) [26]. Cronbach’s alpha in our study was 0.89.

Statistical analyses

The data were statistically analyzed using the SPSS 25.0 and AMOS 24.0 and the specific process was as follows: (1) To present the demographic characteristics of the participants, continuous data were expressed by mean (standard deviation), and categorical variables were described by numbers and percentages; (2) Using depression as the dependent variable and 10 items of the general information questionnaire as independent variables, t-tests or ANOVA tests were conducted to compare whether demographic characteristics affect the depression scores of hospitalized older patients undergoing NCDs; (3) Pearson correlation analysis was used to explore the correlation among depression, alexithymia, and PTG. (4) Multiple linear regression analysis was applied to identify influencing factors of depression; (5) AMOS 24.0 software was used for mediation analyses and structural models construction to analyze the mediating effects of alexithymia between PTG and depression, and PTG between alexithymia and depression.

Results

Demographic characteristics

A total of 330 questionnaires were distributed, and 321 valid questionnaires were collected, with an effective response rate of 97.27%. The 321 older hospitalized patients with chronic diseases surveyed were aged 60–93 (71.23 ± 7.97). In this study, 250 patients (77.88%) were diagnosed with depressive symptoms, of which 98 were mild depression (30.53%), 116 were moderate depression (36.14%), and 36 were severe depression (11.21%). As shown in Table 1, the mean alexithymia score of 321 respondents was 65.74 (SD = 6.83), and the mean score of post-traumatic growth was 62.01 (SD = 14.38).

Analyses of the mediation effect

We developed a mediation model by taking alexithymia as the mediating variable, post-traumatic growth as the independent variable, and depression as the outcome variable (Fig. 1A). The model fitness index indicated that the model fits well (χ2 = 149.791, df = 50, χ2/df = 2.996, GFI = 0.932, AGFI = 0.893, RMSEA = 0.079, NNFI = 0.914, IFI = 0.941, CFI = 0.941), and all paths were significant (P < 0.05). Then Bootstrap method was used to test the mediating effect, and the original data were randomly selected 2000 times (Table 5). There was a mediating effect of alexithymia between PTG and depression, and the mediating effect value was − 0.132, accounting for 31.56% of the total effect.

Discussion

Depression is a common psychological disorder among hospitalized older patients with chronic non-communicable diseases, which seriously affects disease progression, survival, and quality of life. This study found that the depression status of hospitalized order patients with non-communicable diseases should not be underestimated. In addition, to support and validate the hypothesis of complex bidirectional associations between variables, we constructed two mediation models to explore the bidirectional relationship between affective disorders and posttraumatic growth, with depression as the outcome variable. These results could enhance the attention of families and society to the mental health problems of hospitalized older patients with NCDs, thus providing a reference for the formulation of corresponding preventive measures.

In our survey, the incidence of depression among hospitalized geriatrics with NCDs reached 77.88%, which was similar to the results of a cross-sectional descriptive survey in India (80%) [27]. Our findings highlight the multifaceted and pervasive impact that depression and its related psychological dimensions (alexithymia and PTG) have on the lives of older adults with NCDs. ① Impact on Self-Management and Physical Health: Firstly, depression and the inability to process emotions (alexithymia) can cripple a patient’s capacity for effective self-management [7, 28]. This may manifest as reduced motivation for physical activity, poor adherence to medication and dietary plans, and neglect of symptom monitoring [29]. ② Impact on Healthcare Utilization and Economic Burden: Secondly, these unmet mental health needs likely translate into increased healthcare utilization [30]. Patients may present with more frequent somatic complaints, emergency department visits, and hospital readmissions. ③ Impact on Quality of Life and Social Functioning: Most directly, these conditions erode the subjective quality of life [31, 32]. Alexithymia can lead to social isolation due to difficulties in communicating emotional needs and connecting with others. The absence of post-traumatic growth signifies a missed opportunity for finding meaning and positive change amidst adversity, leaving patients stuck in a state of suffering. Therefore, improving mental health in this population is not just about reducing depressive symptoms—it is about enhancing overall resilience, improving physical health outcomes, and restoring a sense of purpose and well-being.”

We summarized the reasons for the high risk of depression in hospitalized older patients with NCDs from four aspects: family factors, economic capability, social function, and disease status. Firstly, patients with weak self-care abilities receive care from families, and they may view themselves as a burden on their children and spouses, leading to feelings of inferiority and worsening of depression. Secondly, older patients undergoing NCDs suffer from long length of stay and high rehospitalization rates, resulting in huge medical costs and economic pressure on individuals and families [33]. Poor economic conditions cause patients to feel helpless and increase the risk of depression. Thirdly, older patients with low education cannot adjust well to perception and awareness of negative events, and social dysfunction is more likely to induce negative emotions [34]. Finally, the number of NCDs is closely related to the symptoms and severity of depression [35]. Patients with multiple NCDs are prone to experience restlessness and irritability after enduring long-term persistent disease pain. The above findings strongly indicate that effective management of depression in hospitalized elderly patients with chronic non communicable diseases requires a comprehensive and holistic evaluation. Clinical doctors and healthcare systems should incorporate the assessment of these key factors into routine practices, such as routine screening, personalized care plans, social support services, collaborative care models, early development of discharge plans, and reducing pressure related to hospitalization.

self-care ability was significantly associated with depression, suggesting that assessing it could help clinicians identify individuals who may be at higher risk and could benefit from further evaluation. The greater the self-care ability of patients with NCDs, the less likely they are to develop depressive symptoms. During hospitalization, unfamiliar environments and treatment methods can strengthen consciousness of frailty and increase psychological burden, resulting in a decrease in nutritional intake and mobility [36]. Patients excessively rely on caregivers and medical staff, leading to in a decrease in self-care ability and exacerbating their depressive symptoms. In the process of nursing, patients should be encouraged to actively participate in disease rehabilitation, promote the recovery of physical function and self-care ability, and thus alleviate depressive tendencies.

Our findings suggested that alexithymia is positively correlated with depression and is a hazard factor of depression in hospitalized older adults with NCDs. Moreover, alexithymia could not only directly affect depression, but also partially mediate the relationship between post-traumatic growth and depression as a mediating variable and its mediating effect contributed 31.56% to the total effect. The dysfunction of patients’ ability to recognize and express emotions is the core of alexithymia management, which can directly cause psychological distress such as depression and reduce the quality of life of patients [9, 37]. Integrating traumatic experience and expressing bravely real emotions are effective measures to weaken the mediating effect of alexithymia between PTG and depression, which is beneficial to produce healthy psychological outcomes [8]. Therefore, clinical personnel should encourage patients to record the process of mood changes through psychological diaries [38], which is conducive to timely detection of negative thoughts and guiding patients to establish positive attitude towards life.

Previous research has discussed that post-traumatic growth induces positive psychological changes in response to negative experiences, reduces emotional distress and intrusive rumination, and promotes positive personality, physical and mental health, and quality of life [39]. We found PTG was a protective factor, which was negatively correlated with depression, and played a mediating role between alexithymia and depression in hospitalized older patients undergoing NCDs. Traumatic events are triggers that induce depression and activate individual rigid core beliefs. At this time, patients interpret stressful events with a large number of negative emotions, resulting in painful psychological experiences. Timely identification and expression of feelings can adjust the emotional threshold of patients, weaken the self-criticism of stressful events, and reduce the psychological pain of patients [33]. Physicians need to pay attention to the traumatic events and past life experiences of hospitalized geriatrics with NCDs to help accurately determine the source of suffering and provide personalized care.

It is extremely important to pay attention to the psychological problems of hospitalized geriatrics with NCDs, and developing comprehensive clinical intervention measures remains a major challenge in the current situation. Our findings could help clinicians to better identify the high-risk populations of depression for hospitalized older patients with NCDs, and more importantly, facilitate the establishment of individualized depression intervention measures. For example, some studies advocated that Tai Chi could gradually establish a positive attitude toward stressful events, effectively cultivate the PTG level of patients, and reduce depression to promote the mental health of patients in the rehabilitation process [40]. Psychological intervention programs for depression in hospitalized older patients with chronic non-communicable diseases targeting alexithymia and traumatic events deserve to be developed to improve the psychological resilience, promote positive attitude transformation, and ultimately improve negative emotions such as depression.

Limitations

There are some limitations to the study. Firstly, we emphasize that this study is a cross-sectional design and cannot infer causal relationships. In the future, the causal order of variables can be tested through longitudinal design or experimental studies. Another limitation is that the data were conducted in three tertiary hospitals in Changsha, which might be cautiously generalized to other settings and need to be validated in larger populations and multiple hospital centers.

Conclusion

In summary, this study verified that alexithymia and post-traumatic growth have direct and indirect interfering effects on depression through mediating effect analysis. Therefore, clinical workers should strengthen the evaluation and monitoring of alexithymia and post-traumatic growth of hospitalized older patients with chronic non-communicable diseases, and formulate reasonable and effective intervention programs from the perspective of positive psychology to reduce the incidence of depression.

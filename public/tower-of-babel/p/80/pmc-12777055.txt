Comparative Study of Esthetic Outcome of Pedo Shades of Composite Resin—A Randomized Controlled Trial: In Vivo and In Vitro Study
Authors: Priyanka Raj, Shikha Choubey, Divya Doneria, Diksha Bhat, Shivani Mathur, Shailja Sinha
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimThis study aims to compare and evaluate parental satisfaction with three different pediatric composite resins.Study designThis study employed both in vitro and in vivo components. In vitro, composite resin samples were restored with the respective materials and evaluated for shade matching using spectrophotometry. In vivo, parental satisfaction with the esthetic restoration of primary incisors was assessed using a feedback form.Subjects and methodsThe study involved obtaining informed consent from participants and assigning subjects to three groups. Group I consisted of 10 samples treated with Activa Kids bioactive, group II had 10 samples treated with Kedo E max, and group III included 10 samples treated with FGM Vittra unique flow. Thirty samples were collected for each of in vitro and in vivo studies. In the in vitro component, samples were restored with the respective composite materials, and shade matching was assessed using spectrophotometry. In the in vivo component, parental satisfaction was evaluated postrestoration using a feedback form.Results and statisticsThe results indicated a significant preference for Activa™ Kids Bioactive-Restorative in terms of appearance, color match, size, contour, and overall satisfaction compared to the other two composite resins. Spectrophotometric analysis showed minimal color change postrestoration with Kedo E max, followed by Activa™ Kids Bioactive-Restorative.ConclusionThe study concluded that both Activa™ Kids Bioactive-Restorative and Kedo E max exhibited good shade-matching capabilities and overall esthetic outcomes compared to FGM Vittra APS, which is unique in restoring primary incisors. Activa™ Kids Bioactive-Restorative garnered higher parental satisfaction, while Kedo E max demonstrated superior color-matching outcomes. These findings provide valuable guidance for dentists and patients in selecting the most suitable pediatric composite resin for achieving optimal esthetic outcomes and patient satisfaction in anterior teeth restorations.How to cite this articleRaj P, Choubey S, Doneria D, et al. Introduction

With advancements in dental materials and techniques, restoring primary maxillary incisors has become increasingly important for preserving both functionality and esthetics in children's dentition. Carious involvement in the maxillary incisors may compromise the dentition, resulting in an undesirable appearance. Treating primary maxillary incisors can be challenging because of their smaller size and the necessity for a retentive, durable, and esthetically pleasing replacement.1 Restoring the health and beauty of a child's smile requires a delicate touch, balancing esthetics with functionality. Choosing the ideal shade of composite for primary teeth is crucial not only for optimal esthetics but also for boosting a child's confidence and self-esteem. Unlike their adult counterparts, primary teeth possess distinct characteristics that complicate shade selection. Their translucent enamel layer allows the underlying dentin to influence the overall color, resulting in a brighter, whiter appearance than permanent teeth. Additionally, the enamel thickness varies across the tooth surface, further impacting shade perception. These unique properties necessitate a different approach compared to shade selection for adult restorations.2 The desire by parents and children for an appearance that is natural led to research into the development of materials that are comparable to natural teeth.3

Numerous restorative materials have been developed that can be used to restore cavitated lesions in both primary and permanent dentition, in addition to being used for esthetic purposes. These materials include conventional glass ionomers, composite resins, resin-modified glass ionomers, compomer, ormocer, and giomer.4 As the caries advances, more decisive treatment is needed. Restorative treatment can help control the early stages of ECC. Final treatment, such as full coverage restorations with strip crowns, is necessary when the situation progresses. For strip crowns, composite restorative material is typically used. Children who have severe multisurface caries without pulp therapy, poor oral hygiene, and unisolatable teeth are not candidates for composite restorations alone and need crowns, per American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry (AAPD) guidelines.5

The esthetics of the materials become compromised when exposed to the dynamic environment in the oral cavity due to the presence of microflora, saliva, and frequent intake of colored food and beverages, which becomes a challenging task for dental experts.6 Pedo shade composite is most commonly used for restoration in today's practice. In the present study, three pedo shade composites have been compared, i.e., Activa™ Kids Bioactive-Restorative, FGM Vittra APS Unique, and Kedo E max. The first bioactive composite with a pedo shade that blends the physical characteristics of composite resin with the chemical properties of glass-ionomer cement is Activa Kids Bioactive-Restorative. When utilized as a core material inside the strip crown for anterior restorations, the fracture strength of teeth restored with Activa Kids Bioactive-Restorative was compared to composite resin in a study conducted by Labeeb et al. in 2023. The study concluded that Activa Kids Bioactive-Restorative might be a better material of choice than traditional composite resins because of its superior chemical and physical properties. The results also indicated that Activa Kids Bioactive-Restorative has a fracture strength equivalent to composite resins.5 It is a bioactive shock-absorbing rubberized ionic resin (Embrace resin) matrix with a low water content and no Bisphenol-A-glycidyl methacrylate derivatives. The ionic resin component comprises phosphate acid groups with antibacterial qualities, which increase the resin-reactive glass filler interaction as well as the contact with tooth structure. The hydrogen ions are separated from the phosphate groups by an ionization process that is dependent on water and replaced by calcium in the tooth structure. This ionic interaction attaches the resin to the minerals in the tooth, generating a strong resin-hydroxyapatite combination and providing a good seal against microleakage.7 In 2015, Garcia-Godoy found that the Activa™ bioactive-restorative composite had stronger wear resistance than flowable composites and outperformed GIC and RMGI cement (RMGIC).8 Bansal et al. proved that Activa™ bioactive-restorative composite exhibited significantly better wear resistance and surface texture as compared to other composite materials, GICs, and RMGIC.9

FGM Vittra APS stands out for its advanced polymerization technique (APS) and provides that blocking-out ability without requiring the storage of a base material. Working time has also been extended because of APS technology. It is a submicrometric composite that may match the color of the teeth from bleach to D4. Shade selection might be one of the most time-consuming components of dental repair. However, with a one-shade composite and the chameleon effect, the long and difficult shade-choosing procedure is eliminated. A one-shade composite allows for progressive modification at the chairside, allowing the practitioner to precisely match adjacent teeth and provide the chameleon effect. Vittra APS Unique is a dependable one-shade composite that enables clinicians to use the chameleon effect to provide better restorations and more patient satisfaction.10 Kedo E max is a pedo shade packable composite, which is a form of lithium disilicate glass-ceramic used for making dental restorations such as crowns, veneers, and inlays/onlays. Kedo E max is well-known for its high strength and durability; it is stronger than ordinary porcelain and can bear chewing pressures. It also has great aesthetics, a natural tooth-like appearance that complements the teeth.11

To reduce color mismatches, many manufacturers have started to provide specific shade guides, but that is subjective and relies on dentists' skills and on lighting conditions. Portable spectrophotometers are considered objective, reliable, and accurate because ambient light does not influence the quality of spectral acquisitions.12 Given the difficulties in selecting shades using visual guides, the aim of this study was to compare and evaluate parental satisfaction for three different pediatric composite resins.

Subjects and Methods

This study evaluated the shade-matching capabilities and parental satisfaction with three different pediatric composite resins used for the esthetic restoration of primary maxillary incisors. The research was conducted in the Department of Pediatric and Preventive Dentistry at ITS-Centre for Dental Studies and Research, Muradnagar, Ghaziabad, Uttar Pradesh, following Institutional Ethical Committee clearance (ITSCDSR/Director-Principal/2024/L/008) and under the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. The study was designed as a prospective, comparative, and observational investigation involving both in vitro and in vivo components. The total sample size was calculated to be 3 × 10 = 30 or more for three groups. Thirty samples were collected for the in vitro study, and 30 children participated in the in vivo study. The utilized teeth had no signs of fractures or dental cavities. With the use of an ultrasonic scaler, all of the teeth were free of large particles. Extracted teeth were inserted in a plaster block using a stiff plastic template. Each tooth was given a unique number. The mean of the measurements was then calculated to establish baseline values that would direct the preparation of the teeth.

In Vitro Study

A total of 30 samples was examined. These samples were separated into three groups of 10 each: Group I consisted of samples to be restored with Activa Kids Bioactive, group II included samples to be restored with Kedo E max, and group III included samples that were restored with FGM Vittra Unique flow. A standardized veneer-shaped cavity was made in the buccal face of the teeth with a straight fissure diamond bur in a high-speed turbine under cooling. Following this, the surface was etched with 37% phosphoric acid for total acid etching for 15 seconds; then a jet of water was applied for 15 seconds for washing before drying with jets of air. The universal adhesive system was applied with the aid of a microbrush applicator using friction movement on the tooth structure for 20 seconds. Then, a brief jet of air was applied over the bonding agent for 5 seconds before the adhesive system was light-cured for 10 seconds with the Waldent light-curing device at a light intensity of 1000 mW/cm2. Each sample was restored using the respective restorative material specified for its group. The teeth were restored by inserting composite resin into the cavities in a single increment. All composite resins were light-cured using the Waldent light-curing device at a light intensity of 1000 mW/cm2 for 20 seconds. The light tip of the device was positioned perpendicular to the buccal surface of the tooth, and its diameter ensured adequate coverage and irradiation. Assessment of shade matching of primary enamel was done using spectrophotometry. Specimens were stored in distilled water for 72 hours before color measurements. In Vivo Study

In an in vivo study, a total of 30 samples were examined, and informed consent was provided to them. Each participant underwent oral prophylaxis followed by preoperative photographs and radiographs. Each participant was falling into one of three groups. Group I consisted of 10 samples treated with Activa kids bioactive, group II had 10 samples treated with Kedo E max, and group III included 10 samples treated with FGM Vittra unique flow. Teeth that need to be restored were isolated. A carious lesion was removed with a speed motor instrument and spoon excavator, followed by selection of a strip crown to match the size of the tooth. Following this, the surface was etched with 37% phosphoric acid for total acid etching for 15 seconds; then a jet of water was applied for 15 seconds for washing before drying with jets of air. The universal adhesive system was applied with the aid of a microbrush applicator using friction movement on the tooth structure for 20 seconds. Then, a brief jet of air was applied over the bonding agent for 5 seconds before the adhesive system was light-cured for 10 seconds with the Waldent light-curing device at a light intensity of 1000 mW/cm2. Each sample was restored using the respective restorative material specified for its group. Then, a small vent hole was placed before seating the strip crown, and the crown was filled with the respective restorative material specified for its group. Composite resin was light-cured for 10 seconds with the Waldent light-curing device at a light intensity of 1000 mW/cm. Postoperative photographs were taken.13 The primary objective of the in vivo study was to evaluate parental satisfaction regarding anterior esthetic restoration in primary incisors (Figs 1 to 7).

Statistical Analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize demographic characteristics and baseline anxiety levels of parents and children. Paired t -test was used to compare pre- and post-treatment anxiety levels within groups, while an independent t -test was used to compare anxiety levels between groups. Analysis of variance (ANOVA) was employed to examine differences among the three behavior management techniques. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. Data were analyzed using SPSS v 26.0, IBM.

Discussion

Color matching in dentistry is essential for achieving restorations that meet both dental professionals' and patients' expectations. This process involves selecting composite resins from a limited range of shades to ensure an aesthetically pleasing restoration. Advances in dental materials have simplified procedures for professionals and enhanced patient satisfaction by providing visually appealing results without compromising quality. These improvements reflect ongoing progress in the field, where technological advances lead to more efficient and effective dental care.13 In pediatric dentistry, the restoration of primary maxillary incisors is crucial not only for functional reasons but also for esthetic considerations. Restorations that blend seamlessly with natural teeth boost the child's confidence and self-esteem. Primary teeth differ from adult teeth in characteristics such as translucent enamel and enamel thickness variations, making shade selection more challenging. Hence, achieving the best esthetic results requires careful shade selection to ensure the restoration functions well and blends with the child's natural dentition, positively impacting their overall oral health and well-being.14 The study aimed to evaluate how well three different composite resin materials matched the shade of primary enamel, which is critical for esthetic restoration in pediatric dentistry. Spectrophotometry, a precise method for measuring color, was used for this assessment. Additionally, the study sought to assess parental satisfaction with the esthetic outcomes of these materials for restoring primary incisors, which are important for a child's appearance and self-esteem.15 Spectrophotometry provided an objective and quantitative analysis of how closely the restorative materials mimicked the natural shade of primary enamel. This information is crucial for dental professionals in selecting materials that restore functionality while seamlessly integrating with the child's natural dentition, enhancing the esthetic result. Evaluating parental satisfaction is also important for understanding the real-world impact of these materials. Parents' perceptions of their child's dental appearance and satisfaction with the restoration can significantly influence their overall experience and compliance with dental care.12 The study compared three pediatric composite resins: Activa™ Kids Bioactive-Restorative, FGM Vittra APS Unique, and Kedo E max. Each material has distinct properties. FGM Vittra APS Unique is noted for its chameleon effect, which efficiently mimics the color of adjacent teeth. All three materials are known for their high strength and durability. These materials were chosen for their potential to address the challenges of restoring primary incisors and meeting the esthetic expectations of clinicians and parents.13 In the In vivo component, parental satisfaction with the esthetic restoration of primary incisors was evaluated using a feedback form.16 Parental satisfaction is a critical indicator of the success of esthetic dental treatments in children, reflecting both the quality of the restoration and its impact on the child's overall well-being and confidence.2 The study aimed to ensure reliable and reproducible outcomes in assessing how well each restorative material matched the natural shade of primary enamel. In the in vitro component, color differences among the three groups postrestoration were assessed using spectrophotometry. Although no significant difference was found among the three composite groups, Activa Kids Bioactive and FGM Vittra APS Unique showed more noticeable color differences compared to Kedo E max, which indicated better color-matching outcomes. Cruz da Silva et al. compared the color correspondence of two single-shade composite resins to multishade composite resins, finding that single-shade composites simplify shade selection and are promising for dental practice.13 Cilingir et al. investigated the impact of office bleaching on single-shade resin compounds, finding no significant color difference between groups.11 Color is a key esthetic parameter in dentistry, influenced by properties such as lightness, chroma, and hue, as well as translucency. The irregular morphology and surface structures of natural teeth contribute to the challenge of color-matching composite resin restorations.11 The results suggest that Activa Kids Bioactive may offer superior esthetic results and align more closely with parental expectations, making it a preferred choice for restoring primary incisors in pediatric dentistry. Despite no significant color difference observed among the three composite groups, Activa Kids Bioactive and FGM Vittra APS Unique exhibited more color differences compared to Kedo E max, which demonstrated better color matching outcomes. These findings highlight the performance and suitability of each composite material in achieving optimal esthetic results and meeting professional and parental expectations. Additional studies support these findings: Bhadra D et al. found satisfactory clinical performance of Activa™ Bioactive Composites in class II carious lesions.14 Nanavati et al. reported that Activa Kids may protect primary teeth against recurrent caries and restoration failure.17 Balakrishnan et al. studied the ideal composite shade for primary dentition, finding preferences for shades pedo and D2.18 Yehia et al. found bioactive restorations superior to resin-modified glass ionomers in esthetics.19 Barros et al. showed that surrounding and underlying shades affect color adjustment potential.15 Zulekha et al. compared the color match and stability of Omnichroma and Tetric-N-Ceram composites, finding similar performance.10 Deepika et al. assessed the efficacy of bioactive resin-modified glass ionomers and giomers, noting improved characteristics of bioactive materials.20 The study had several limitations, including a small sample size of 10 samples per group, which may affect the generalizability of the findings. The single-center setting limited the diversity of the patient population, potentially impacting the applicability of the results to broader demographics. Additionally, the exclusive use of instrumental devices for measuring tooth color posed challenges due to the small size of human teeth and significant light loss, resulting in poor color readings; thus, these devices should complement, not replace, visual assessments.

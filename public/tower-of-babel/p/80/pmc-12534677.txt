Early Outcome Analysis of Intravitreal Aflibercept 8 mg Treatment in Naïve Patients with Neovascular Age-Related Macular Degeneration Using Artificial Intelligence
Authors: Nicolò Bartolomeo, Yannic Pannatier Schuetz, Anna Chiara Nascimbeni, Daniela Gallo Castro, Baptiste Crozat, Mamadou Pathé Barry, Aude Ambresin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionNeovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD) is one of the leading causes of vision loss, often requiring frequent injections. Our aim is to report the early outcomes of intravitreal aflibercept (IVA) 8 mg treatment in naïve patients with nAMD.MethodsAn observational, retrospective, monocentric study was conducted at Swiss Visio Montchoisi, Lausanne, Switzerland. A total of 51 eyes of 48 patients with naïve macular neovascularization (MNV) secondary to AMD received a loading phase of three-monthly IVA followed by a monthly observational phase until fluid recurrence. At each visit, all patients had a full ophthalmological exam, including spectral-domain optical coherence tomography (SD-OCT), which was analysed manually and with artificial intelligence (AI). The main outcomes were best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA), central subfield thickness (CST), maximal pigment epithelial detachment (PED) height, presence or absence of intraretinal fluid (IRF) and subretinal fluid (SRF) on SD-OCT, and mean volumetric changes in IRF, SFR, and PED using AI at baseline and month 1, 2, 3, and 6.ResultsMean age at baseline was 79.94 ± 7.29 years, and 81.25% of patients were female. Mean CST and PED height significantly decreased to 231.9 ± 68.69 μm and 137.6 ± 68.53 μm, respectively, at month 6. Qualitative and AI-quantified biomarkers significantly decreased after loading and at month 6. A mean interval of 9.67 ± 3.87 weeks was reached with a mean number of 4.63 ± 1.01 injections at the time of the last observation. Baseline IRF and HRF were negative predictors of functional outcomes in the short term. One patient with sterile vitritis benefited from vitrectomy and topical treatment.ConclusionsAflibercept intravitreal injections in the treatment of naïve patients with wet AMD demonstrated rapid improvement of functional and anatomical parameters, particularly regarding fluid control and quantitative biomarkers on OCT. A comprehensive analysis of follow-up visits will be performed to confirm our early results.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40123-025-01239-3.

Introduction

Neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD) is one of the leading sight-threatening diseases, and its prevalence is increasing in older populations [1]. Since the first introduction of intravitreal anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF) in 2004, nAMD treatment has been continuously revolutionized [2]. However, the limited pharmacological bioavailability of anti-VEGF is a burden to affected patients as it requires continuous and frequent injections to control disease activity [3].

Aflibercept 2 mg has shown to be successful in the management of nAMD in both randomised trials and in real-world evaluations [4, 5]. These results paved the way for the formulation of a higher molecular dose on the basis of the rationale that a higher dose may lead to efficient disease control and increased durability outcomes, while decreasing treatment burden. The HARBOR trial has already shown no meaningful durability differences when increasing the dosage of anti-VEGF agents [6]. In contrast, Veritti et al. demonstrated the basic principles and benefits of a higher molecular dosing for a prolonged VEGF inhibition [7]. Aflibercept 8 mg proved to be effective in interval extension in nAMD Management in a worldwide phase 2 (CANDELA) and phase 3 randomised trial (PULSAR), and demonstrated non-inferiority results compared with aflibercept 2 mg in terms of visual outcomes and safety profile [8, 9].

Durability is one of the main targets when managing patients with nAMD, as higher intervals can potentially reduce treatment burden for both patients and clinics. Aflibercept 8 mg holds promise for improving patient management and extending treatment intervals [9].

The predefined criteria of clinical trials for patient selection often limit the evaluation of the molecule’s application in wider settings, as they often do not relate to a standard clinical practice. Therefore, the analysis of real-world cohorts is crucial for corroborating trial results and ensuring similar applicability on a non-limited and wider population.

Our study aims to analyse real-world naïve patients with nAMD to provide valuable insight on the efficacy of aflibercept 8 mg. Fluid analysis in nAMD is the leading factor for treatment outcome and interval adjustment. With the advancements of artificial intelligence (AI), and its ability to quantify retinal anatomical components, we quantified optical coherence tomography (OCT) biomarkers, providing a wider assessment range of early aflibercept 8 mg intravitreal injections.

Methods

Study Description

This observational, retrospective, single-arm study was conducted at the Retina Unit of the Swiss Visio Montchoisi ophthalmology centre in Lausanne, Switzerland. The study was carried out in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and its amendments, and it was approved by the Swiss Ethics Committee (CERVD: 2024-01491).

Inclusion Criteria

Patients presenting with active macular neovascularisation (MNV) secondary to AMD and treated with intravitreal aflibercept 8 mg injections were included in the study. The diagnosis was confirmed by fluorescein and indocyanine green angiography. All enrolled patients signed a written informed consent form.

Study Process and Treatment Regimen

A full ophthalmological examination was performed at each visit, including Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) and spectral-domain optical coherence tomography (SD-OCT, Heidelberg Inc®).

Patients were treated with a loading phase of 3 monthly injections. After loading, the treatment regimen respected an adjusted protocol taking into consideration the higher dosage of aflibercept (Fig. 1). A monthly observational period was followed until relapse of disease activity. The capped observation period was 16 weeks. Upon fluid recurrence, a treat-and-extend regimen (TER) was initiated with an interval that could be adapted by 2 weeks (TER = current interval – 2 weeks). Monthly injections were continued if fluid persisted after loading.Fig. 1Adjusted treatment protocol scheme

Study Outcomes

Clinical functional and anatomical measurements were collected at baseline and monthly to month 3 (end of the loading phase), and at month 6: BCVA in ETDRS letters, central subfield thickness (CST), maximal pigment epithelial detachment (PED) height, qualitative assessment of intraretinal fluid (IRF), subretinal fluid (SRF) and subretinal pigment epithelium (RPE) fluid. Qualitative changes were evaluated in terms of presence or absence of fluids. In addition, using an artificial intelligence (AI)-based automated fluid quantification system (RetinAI Discovery CORE®; version 4.11), biomarkers such as outer nuclear layer and Henle fiber layer (ONL-HFL), myeloid zone (MZ), EZ-OPR-IZ (photoreceptors), retinal pigment epithelium (RPE), choriocapillaris and choroidal stroma (CC-CS), amorphous material (AM), intraretinal fluid (IRF), subretinal fluid (SRF), and PED were quantified.

Primary Outcome

The primary outcome was defined as the mean change in BCVA from baseline to month 3 and month 6 as evaluated in routine clinical practice.

Secondary Outcome

The secondary outcome was the qualitative and quantitative assessment of anatomical outcomes, treatment durability, as well as safety endpoints at each visit.

Statistics

Sample Size

The sample size was determined assuming a normally distributed variable both at baseline and end of the study. With an effect size of 0.5 between baseline and final measures, a significance level of the test at α = 5%, the minimal sample size required for a power of 80% would be 28 patients.

Analysis

Descriptive statistics were performed on all study variables. Chi-squared test was applied to evaluate the change in qualitative variables over time (IRF, SRF, and sub-RPE status). Normality of data distribution was evaluated using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. The statistical difference between continuous variables was assessed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Linear regressions were performed to determine predictive relationships between variables. The GraphPad Prism software (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA) was used for statistical calculations. All data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). A value with a p-value ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline Demographics

Overall, 51 eyes of 48 patients were included in the study. The cohort was predominantly represented by 81% female patients, with a mean age of 79.94 ± 7.29 years. According to a qualitative morphological differentiation of eyes according to the type of MNV, 30 eyes (58.82%) were type 1 MNVs, 9 eyes (17.65%) were type 2 MNVs and 8 eyes (15.69%) were type 3 MNVs. Moreover, four eyes were characterised by a mixed type of MNV, consisting of types 1 and 2 (7.84%). PEDs were present in 42 eyes (82.35%) and were classified as fibrous only (47.61%), predominantly fibrous (16.67%), predominantly serous (16.67%) and serous only (19.05%). Functional Outcomes

After three monthly injections, visual acuity increased from 70.44 ± 12.48 ETDRS to 72.91 ± 13.13 ETDRS, recording a significant increase of 2.47 ± 0.65 letters (p = 0.0031) (Fig. 2). Eyes (n = 3) with a BCVA of less than 24 letters at baseline were excluded from the analysis. Eyes were classified depending on visual acuity changes after loading. No improvements were registered in 23 eyes (51.11%), 10 eyes (22.23%) experienced a vision increase of one to five letters, 6 eyes (13.33%) improved by six to ten letters, while 6 eyes (13.33%) had a vision increase of more than ten letters.Fig. 2Functional outcomes. Treatment Intervals and Number of Injections

Out of the 51 eyes finishing the loading phase, 78.43% (40 eyes) were eligible for entering the adjusted observe and plan regimen, while 21.57% (11 eyes) needed to receive a further injection.

In total, 49 eyes reached the 6-month observational timepoint, allowing a preliminary evaluation of durability results brought by our adjusted protocol. A mean last-assigned interval duration of 9.67 ± 3.87 weeks was reached with a mean number of 4.63 ± 1.01 injections. This resulted in 73.46% of patients reaching an interval ≥ 8 weeks with a considerably low number of injections (Fig. 6).Fig. 6Durability outcomes. Safety and Discontinuation Rate

Intraocular inflammation was recorded in one eye displaying sterile vitritis after the fourth injection. The eye benefited from posterior vitrectomy, intraocular antibiotic injection, and topical treatment, which resulted in the restoration of visual acuity. To date, no additional intravitreal injections have been performed.

Two patients (two eyes) were lost to follow-up after the loading phase.

Discussion

nAMD is a disease whose treatment solely depends on intravitreal molecular injections. Over the past decades, several treatment molecules have been made available, and real-world evidence has been necessary to evaluate the efficacy and limitations of novel molecules in different settings. Following the positive results of the PHOTON and PULSAR clinical trials [9, 10], aflibercept 8 mg received regulatory approval in Switzerland in early 2024. This real-world study analysed the early treatment outcomes of aflibercept 8 mg treatment in naïve patients with nAMD. Our results are in line with expectations based on the PULSAR phase 3 trial [9]. Moreover, the impactful effects of higher-dose aflibercept are consistent with the early effects reported in other real-world studies [11, 12].

The molecular efficiency demonstrated in PULSAR [9] was reflected and confirmed in our cohort with significant disease activity control and stability until the end of the loading phase and at month 6. An impactful reduction of both retinal thickness and fluids was recorded immediately after the first injection. After the loading phase, fluid was decreased by 73.77% in the intraretinal compartment and by 92.59% subretinally. The proportion increase of patients with fluids at month 6 is explained by the observational characteristics of our treatment protocol. The tight fluid control and retinal thickness stability of aflibercept 8 mg demonstrated in our study and its maintenance over time [9] are crucial for preventing visual acuity decrease due to anatomical fluctuations over the long term [13].

Particularly, fluid control is of clinical relevance for the management of nAMD as it can minimise and prevent aggressive visual erosion. It is reported that fluid-free patients attain higher visual acuity gains compared with patients with retinal fluid in the short term [14]. Indeed, despite the location, retinal fluid presence is reported to have a negative impact on functional outcomes [15, 16].

The 48-week anatomical and functional outcomes of the PULSAR study report that in treatment-naïve patients, patients receiving aflibercept 8 mg achieved comparable outcomes to those receiving aflibercept 2 mg, but with fewer injections [9]. Moreover, after the loading phase, visual acuity significantly improved and remained stable during the whole observational period. Similarly, a continuous and progressive disease control of aflibercept 8 mg was observed in our study, which led to a gradual increase of BCVA until achieving a gain of one line of ETDRS letters after 6 months. The positive early anatomical and functional outcomes of aflibercept 8 mg are of clinical relevance as they could be predictors of long-term treatment visual response [17].

The higher dosage of aflibercept 8 mg and its tight fluid control reported in randomised clinical trials allowed us to design and perform an adjusted treatment regimen after three monthly injections, which consisted of monthly observations until fluid recurrence. This adapted protocol aimed to rapidly obtain insights on the durability claims of the new molecule. The Majority of our population was started on the adapted regimen after the loading phase, and a long interval of 9.67 weeks was reached with a few intravitreal injections at month 6. This finding supports that the combination of a higher molecular dosing and an observational protocol are key factors for promptly achieving longer treatment intervals. The triad of visual acuity improvement and stability, fluid control and large treatment intervals is notable in aflibercept 8 mg and strengthens its positive early treatment effects.

Despite qualitative fluid analysis being a pillar during retinal anatomical assessments in nAMD for making treatment-informed decisions, it does not always completely reflect disease activity [18]. Moreover, it does not consider other factors which could contribute to having a broader and more insightful effect on detailed anatomical changes during treatment. AI implementation can enrich the currently limited real-world nAMD studies by providing deeper and insightful characterisations of OCT scans [19]. Using RetinAI Discovery CORE®, a quantification of multiple OCT biomarkers was possible. Notably, biomarkers, including inner and outer retinal layer thickness, hyper-reflective foci, fluids and PED volumes, were assessed. A significant quantified retinal rearrangement of all biomarkers was observed after loading. Indeed, all biomarkers significantly decreased after treatment. However, further elucidations are needed to explain the exact mechanisms of how anti-VEGF treatments lead to remodelling of all retinal components.

Our study shows that quantified AI biomarkers could predict functional outcomes in the short and intermediate term. Indeed, both IRF and HRF were negative predictors of BCVA at months 3 and 6. This result supports the fluid understanding and assessments published by the team of Schmidt-Erfurth [15, 16, 18]. Moreover, HRF correlation with low visual prognosis has already been described in literature [20]. HRF quantification is particularly relevant in nAMD as it could serve as an indicator of disease activity that could contribute to nAMD aggravation [21]. Aflibercept 8 mg was effective in HRF and therefore retinal inflammation, in line with already described anti-VEGF results [22]. To the best of our knowledge, this is the largest early outcome real-world study of aflibercept 8 mg for the treatment of naïve patients with nAMD. Moreover, our study is the first to leverage baseline biomarkers to predict functional outcomes. These findings could offer clinicians practical guidance on how to manage newly presenting patients with substantial nAMD characteristics.

The safety results obtained in our short-term analysis confirm a good safety profile of this novel molecule. A single ocular adverse event was reported in our cohort, accounting for 3% of the studied population. No major adverse events such as endophthalmitis and/or occlusive vasculitis were observed, indicating no safety signals. Our study is limited by the lack of a control group. A more comprehensive and long-term analysis is needed to confirm these results.

Conclusions

Our real-world study supports the positive outcomes of clinical trials on aflibercept 8 mg treatment in naïve patients with nAMD. Indeed, this higher dose molecule led to rapid functional and anatomical improvements in this population. This study also highlights the importance of OCT biomarker quantification using AI tools for the detailed evaluation of treatment efficacy, which could provide an in-depth analysis not previously possible. A comprehensive analysis of follow-up visits will be performed to assess long-term functional, anatomical, and durability outcomes.

Pulmonary Function Test: A Mainstay for Evaluating Respiratory Health in Patients Recovered from COVID-19 Infection
Authors: Sanjay Nehe, Avinash Taksande, Ranjit Ambad, Nandkishor Bankar, Noman Obaid
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Despite of millions of people who suffered from coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) infection, pulmonary functions of these patients are sparsely reflected in the literature. Although few researchers have performed pulmonary function tests to evaluate the respiratory function of COVID-19 patients after recovery from acute illness, the sample size was too limited. The present study was conducted in a tertiary care hospital with an aim to determine pulmonary functions of COVID-19 patients recovered from acute illness.Methods:A total of 250 COVID-19 patients who recovered from acute illness were included in the study. Pulmonary function test of these patients was performed; the indices of PFT studied included, forced expiratory volume in the first second (FEV1), forced vital capacity (FVC), and FEV1/FVC ratio.Results:Even after >3 months of recovery from the COVID-19 acute illness, patients had respiratory complaints like cough and shortness of breath. These convalescent patients also reported to have certain psychological issues like feeling of loneliness, fear, anxiety, and depression. It was noted that all the respiratory parameters of patients even after >3 months of recovery from the COVID-19 acute illness were compromised.Conclusion:The pulmonary functions of the patients recovered from COVID-19 infection should be evaluated before discharge of the patient from hospital followed by periodic evaluation post discharge, so that necessary therapeutic interventions can be initiated. Breathing exercises and pranayama can augment the better outcome of these patients. Pulmonary function tests are not only recommended but also are necessary for monitoring of patients who have recovered from COVID-19 infection.

INTRODUCTION

The 21st century has experienced the most awful situation in the form of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic. No pandemic other than COVID-19 has created a dreadful havoc in the life of human being. It affected almost all countries and territories of the world.

As per the World Health Organization, COVID-19 caused nearly 14.83 million deaths across the world.[1] COVID-19 pandemic has negatively impacted mankind at each and every level of life including healthcare and socioeconomic.[23]

Although the pathogenesis of COVID-19 is yet to be fully elicited, previous studies on severe acute respiratory syndrome (SARS) have postulated the involvement of three phases: “the viral replication,” “the immunological hyperactivity,” and “the pulmonary destruction.”[4] Respiratory system is most affected in COVID-19.

Pneumonia is the most common clinical manifestation of severe COVID-19 infection. It involves both the lung (bilateral) and is characterized by diffuse alveolar damage and marked vascular congestion. Lung pathology is featured by the presence of patchy inflammatory cellular infiltration, intra-alveolar edema, hemorrhage, proteinaceous exudate along with denudation, and reactive hyperplasia of lung cells.[5] Even after recovery from COVID-19 infection, patient’s respiratory functions are reported to be compromised. Nearly about 30%–60% of patients experience fatigue and dyspnea after recovery from acute phase of illness.[6]

Despite of millions of people who suffered from COVID-19 infection, pulmonary functions of these patients are sparsely reflected in the literature.[6] Although few researchers have performed pulmonary function tests to evaluate the respiratory function of COVID-19 patients after recovery from acute illness, the sample size was too limited.[7] The present study was conducted in tertiary care hospital with an aim to determine pulmonary functions of COVID-19 patients who have recovered from acute illness.

An equal number of healthy COVID-19 noninfected patients (confirmed by real-time polymerase chain reaction test) were included as controls. Both patients and controls were explained about the purpose and procedure of the study. The procedure of PFT was demonstrated to the participants to make them aware with the technique. The informed consent was obtained from the participants.

The PFT of the participants was conducted as per the standard protocol as outlined in the study by Miller et al.[8] For performing PFT, Medspiror (Computerized spirometry) was used. The indices of PFT studied included forced expiratory volume in the first second (FEV1), forced vital capacity (FVC), and FEV1/FVC ratio. While performing the PFT, healthcare professional used appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) like N95 mask, gloves etc., to prevent transmission of infection.

The demographic characteristics of participants (patients and controls) like age, sex, and their anthropometric measurements like height and weight were recorded and analyzed. The data obtained were entered in Microsoft Excel and were analyzed using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) version 19.0 statistical software. The probability (P) value of <0.05 was considered significant.

DISCUSSION

Although several experts drafted guidelines from various national and international bodies of repute like the Centre for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), the World Health Organization (WHO), and the Indian Council for Medical Research are available for the management of acute COVID-19 illness, very few or almost no guidelines are attempted addressing the health issues in convalescent patients.

The clinical manifestation of COVID-19 infections is varied and ranges from asymptomatic infection to severe acute respiratory distress syndrome.[9] In addition to various virus-related factors, the clinical presentation is also affected by the presence of predisposing factors like diabetes mellitus, hypertension, renal disease, cardiovascular disease, and respiratory ailments.[10]

Acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) is the most severe and fatal complication of COVID-19 infection that may ultimately lead to death of patient. The symptoms of ARDS is to persist even after recovery of patients.[11] In the present study, it was observed that the patients had many respiratory symptoms like cough, dyspnea even after >3 months of recovery from the COVID-19 acute illness. Similar findings were reported by other researchers.[1213]

In routine practice, many specialists of pulmonary medicine rely on spirometry for screening of respiratory functions. Spirometry is a mainstay in the field of respiratory medicine as it provides important insights for physiology of respiration as it measures how a person inhales or exhales volume as a function of time.[1415]

When PFT was performed in patients recovered from COVID-19 infection. It was found that all parameters were severely deranged even after more than 3 months of recovery from acute phase of illness. In consistent with other studies, the present study also highlights the importance of evaluating of pulmonary condition after discharge of the patients, so that necessary therapeutic interventions can be further initiated.[1617] Similar to findings of Babadi et al.,[11] a large number of patients had restrictive pattern even after recovery from infection.

One important finding that was noted in the study other than compromised status of respiratory health in patients recovered from COVID-19 infection is acute need of psychological counseling in these patients as many patients reported like feeling of loneliness, fear, anxiety, and depression. Similar view was expressed by other researchers.[18]

CONCLUSION

To conclude, respiratory physiology is severely affected in case of COVID-19 and symptoms of these are given seen after recovery from acute phase of illness. From the present study, it can be recommended that the pulmonary functions of the patients recovered from COVID-19 infection should be evaluated before discharge of the patient from hospital followed by periodic evaluation post discharge, so that necessary therapeutic interventions can be initiated. Breathing exercises and pranayama can augment the better outcome of these patients. Pulmonary function tests are not only recommended but also are necessary for monitoring of patient recovered for COVID-19 infection.

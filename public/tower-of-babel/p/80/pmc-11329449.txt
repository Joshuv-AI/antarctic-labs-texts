BRD4 expression and its regulatory interaction with miR-26a-3p, DLG5-AS1, and JMJD1C-AS1 lncRNAs in gastric cancer progression
Authors: Mahya Ahmadpour Youshanlui, Amirhossein Yari, Seyedeh Zahra Bahojb Mahdavi, Mohammad Amini, Behzad Baradaran, Ramin Ahangar, Omid Pourbagherian, Amir Ali Mokhtarzadeh
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractGastric cancer remains a significant health challenge despite advancements in diagnosis and treatment. Early detection is critical to reducing mortality, necessitating the investigation of molecular mechanisms underlying gastric cancer progression. This study focuses on BRD4 expression and its correlation with miR-26a-3p, DLG5-AS1, and JMJD1C-AS1 lncRNAs in gastric cancer. Analysis of The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets revealed significant upregulation of BRD4 in gastric cancer tissues compared to normal tissues, correlating negatively with miR-26a-3p and positively with DLG5-AS1 and JMJD1C-AS1 lncRNAs. Quantitative RT-PCR confirmed these findings in 25 gastric cancer tissue samples and 25 normal samples. BRD4's overexpression was associated with reduced survival rates and older patient age. MiR-26a-3p, a known tumor suppressor, showed decreased expression in gastric cancer tissues, with ROC analysis suggesting it, alongside BRD4, as a potential diagnostic biomarker. Additionally, bioinformatics predicted miR-26a-3p’s interaction with BRD4 mRNA. Upregulated lncRNAs DLG5-AS1 and JMJD1C-AS1 likely act as competing endogenous RNAs, sponging miR-26a-3p, thus promoting BRD4 dysregulation. These lncRNAs have not been previously studied in gastric cancer. The findings propose a novel BRD4/lncRNA/miRNA regulatory axis in gastric cancer, highlighting the potential of BRD4, DLG5-AS1, and JMJD1C-AS1 as biomarkers for early diagnosis. Further studies with larger sample sizes and in vivo and in vitro experiments are needed to elucidate this regulatory mechanism’s role in gastric cancer progression.Keywords: Gastric cancer, BRD4, MicroRNA, LncRNA, ceRNA, Diagnostic biomarker

Introduction

Gastric cancer (GC) represents the fifth most prevalent cancer type globally and is the third leading cause of cancer-associated mortality [1]. This malignancy is often diagnosed at an advanced stage due to its early atypical clinical presentation [2]. While surgical resection with lymph node dissection is the primary curative treatment for resectable gastric cancer, distant metastasis and recurrence are common even after complete surgical removal. Tumor progression and metastasis also occur in unresectable cases. A major challenge in treating gastric cancer and developing novel therapeutic and diagnostic tools is the heterogeneity underlying the diverse histopathological and molecular profiles of the disease [3].

Bromodomain-containing protein 4 (BRD4) plays a crucial role as an epigenetic and transcriptional regulator involved in modulating essential cellular processes such as the cell cycle, apoptosis, invasion, and metastasis. BRD4 is part of the Bromodomain and Extra-terminal (BET) family of proteins, which includes BRD2, BRD3, and BRD4, all of which bind to acetylated histones and mediate gene transcription activation [4–6]. BET proteins are integral to the regulation of oncogenic gene transcription and expression in various malignancies [7]. BRD4 amplification promotes the expression of cancer genes in high-grade serous ovarian cancer, making these tumors more sensitive to BET inhibitors [8]. Additionally, BRD4 promotes tumor growth and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) [9]. In gastric cancer, BRD4 overexpression correlates with tumor differentiation status, lymph node metastasis, advanced clinical stage, and reduced patient survival. Knockdown of BRD4 via siRNA decreases migration, invasion, and proliferation of gastric cancer cells in vitro [10]. Moreover, BRD4 modulates stem cell-like properties in gastric cancer by transcriptionally regulating miR-216a-3p expression and activating Wnt/β-catenin signaling [11]. Derivatives of BRD4 inhibitors have shown promising anti-cancer activity in various tumors [12]. Pharmacologic or genetic inhibition of BRD4 impedes gastric cancer progression and metastasis by destabilizing the SNAIL transcriptional repressor and downregulating c-MYC oncogene expression [13, 14].

Aberrant epigenetic regulation, in addition to genetic and environmental factors, is a key contributor to gastric carcinogenesis. Major epigenetic mechanisms involved include DNA methylation, histone post-translational modifications, chromatin remodeling, and non-coding RNA (ncRNA) dysregulation. Targeting these epigenetic drivers could overcome cancer heterogeneity and reprogram malignant cells to a more normalized state, potentially sensitizing them to cytotoxic therapies or immune checkpoint blockade [15]. Variations in ncRNA expression have been observed in gastric cancer, regulating key oncogenic processes such as proliferation, apoptosis, EMT, and metastasis. Because these expression changes can precede histopathological manifestations of gastric carcinogenesis, ncRNAs may serve as promising biomarkers for early detection and as therapeutic targets. Thus, elucidating the roles of dysregulated ncRNAs is a critical area of molecular research in gastric cancer [16]. MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are a class of ncRNAs that alter gene expression by binding directly to the 3′ untranslated region (UTR) of target genes [17]. Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) represent an emerging class of regulatory RNAs that interact with various biomolecules and modulate key biological processes in cells [18]. LncRNAs can act as competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs) to sponge miRNAs and affect their target genes [19]. However, the correlation between ncRNAs and BRD4 expression in GC has not been well investigated.

n this study, using bioinformatic databases and tools, we identified miR-26a-3p as an miRNA that interacts with BRD4 mRNA. We also identified two lncRNAs, DLG5-AS1 and JMJD1C-AS1, which could function as ceRNAs for miR-26a-3p and BRD4. Various lncRNAs and miRNAs have been implicated in the molecular pathogenesis of gastric cancer [20, 21]. For instance, a meta-analysis reported that decreased expression of microRNA-34a (miR-34a) is associated with gastric cancer progression and poorer clinical outcomes [22], while the long non-coding RNA VAL is overexpressed in gastric cancers and correlates with poor prognosis[23]. However, the role of the miR-26a-3p-JMJD1C-AS1 and DLG5-AS1 lncRNA-BRD4 gene axis in GC is largely unknown. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the expression of the BRD4 gene and its related ncRNAs and evaluate their diagnostic value in GC tissue samples. Our study may provide new insights into the molecular mechanisms of GC and suggest novel strategies for GC diagnosis and treatment.

Materials and methods

Datasets

The Genome Atlas (TCGA) conducted an extensive investigation to obtain datasets, including samples from various types of stomach cancer, with control samples from persons unaffected by the illness. The aforementioned dataset, TCGA-STAD, was identified among the returned datasets and then acquired by downloading. The TCGA-STAD dataset consists of RNA sequencing profiles that have been collected and combined. It includes gene expression data from two groups: control samples representing individuals without any health issues and samples taken from patients diagnosed with gastric cancer.

Data pre-processing

This investigation used RNA sequencing data acquired via the TCGAbiolinks package, an R-based program for retrieving data from TCGA-STAD. TCGAbiolinks facilitated the downloading process, and the data was subsequently normalized using the limma tool, a commonly used R-based method for normalizing data in gene expression research. Following normalization, we analyzed differentially expressed genes (DEGs) using the aforementioned R tools. The discovery of DEGs served as a fundamental basis for further investigations and the development of various networks, allowing a more comprehensive understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying the examined condition. BRD4 was identified as significantly upregulated in gastric cancer specimens compared to normal specimens and was selected for further investigation. The data was presented and analyzed using GraphPad Prism.Multiple graphs and plots were obtained and shown using the TCGA browser, namely the Ualcan1 online tool. Additionally, the Kaplan–Meier plots were generated using the Kaplan–Meier Plotter2 database.

microRNA-mRNA interaction

After identifying a significantly dysregulated gene in gastric cancer, the microRNA-mRNA interaction network was reconstructed using the Cancer Regulome3 database. MicroRNAs selected for targeting the candidate mRNA were identified using this database. The miRmap4 database was then used to corroborate the identified outcomes and establish the relationships between microRNAs and messenger RNAs. The Pearson correlation coefficient was used to determine the association between miRNAs and mRNAs.

lncRNA-microRNA interaction

After identifying the validated microRNA that targets the BRD4 mRNA, competing endogenous analysis was used to determine the competing substances that act as sponges for the microRNA. The LncRNA2Target online tool was used to identify the interaction between long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) and microRNAs (miRNAs). The Pearson correlation coefficient was used to determine the association among these genes.

Preparation of sample

Twenty-five samples of GC tissue and twenty-five samples of matched tumor margin tissue were collected from Imam Khomeini Hospital in Tehran between 2019 and 2020. Each participant signed a written informed consent form. In all cases, surgical procedures were preceded by neither chemotherapy nor radiation. The samples were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and kept at – 80 ℃ until RNA extraction. Patient profiles included information on tumor size, age, initial tumor location, lymphatic and vascular invasion, serosal invasion, histological grade, clinical stage, and GC family history.

RNA extraction and cDNA synthesis

Samples of tissue were homogenized by first being pounded in a mortar and pestle submerged in liquid nitrogen and then transferred to a lysis buffer and homogenized further. Total RNA was extracted using Trizol reagent (Riboex brand, made by Gene All) as per the manufacturer’s instructions. The total amount of RNA and its purity were determined using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer. Next, 1 µg of total RNA was added to a final volume of 20 µl, and cDNA was synthesized in a thermocycler PCR according to the standard protocol (50 ℃ for 30 min for reverse transcription, 95 ℃ for 5 min to inactivate the RT enzyme, and 10 ℃ indefinitely for the end).

Real-time PCR (qPCR)

The experimental procedure included the use of the BioFACT™ 2X Real-Time PCR Master Mix and gene-specific primers to conduct Real-Time PCR. The overall reaction volume was established at 10 μl. The experimental procedure consisted of three sequential phases, which are outlined below: In the first step, the temperature is raised to 95 ℃ for a duration of 13 min, serving as the holding stage or starting heat. In the second step, the denaturation process is conducted for a total of 45 cycles at a temperature of 95 ℃ for a duration of 10 s. Following denaturation, primer annealing is carried out at a temperature of 60 ℃ for a period of 30 s. Finally, extension occurs at a temperature of 72 ℃ for 20 s. In the third step of the experiment, melting curves were collected at the conclusion of each run. The GAPDH gene was used for the purpose of data standardization. The quantification of target gene expression was determined using the 2−ΔΔCt methodology. The NCBI Primer-3 online platform, accessible at https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/tools/primer-blast/, was used for the purpose of primer design. Statistical analysis

qPCR results were analyzed and visualized using GraphPad Prism 8. Differences in gene expression levels between samples were determined using the Wilcoxon matched-pairs signed rank test, paired and unpaired t-test, and Mann–Whitney test. A significant p-value was measured to be less than 0.05.

Discussion

Despite significant advancements in cancer diagnosis and treatment, gastric cancer remains a major health concern. Early detection of gastric cancer has been shown to significantly reduce disease mortality. Therefore, understanding the pathogenic molecular mechanisms responsible for the development of gastric cancer is essential for early detection[24]. Diverse RNA interactions substantially impact BRD4’s role in gastric cancer transcriptional regulation. BRD4 may regulate gastric cancer progression through its interaction with miRNA and long noncoding RNAs. Our study focuses on BRD4 expression and its correlation with miR-26a-3p, DLG5-AS1, and JMJD1C-AS1 lncRNAs in gastric cancer. We observed a significant upregulation of BRD4 in gastric cancer tissue samples compared to normal tissue samples, which was negatively correlated with miR-26a-3p expression and positively correlated with DLG5-AS1 and JMJD1C-AS1 lncRNA expression (Fig. 12).

The overexpression of DLG5-AS1 and JMJD1C-AS1 may lead to the suppression of miR-26a-3p by acting as a sponge for this specific microRNA, resulting in the dysregulation of BRD4

We initially analyzed differentially expressed genes provided by the TCGA database. Among the analyzed genes, BRD4 was significantly upregulated in gastric cancer samples compared to normal samples and was selected for further investigation. Increased expression of BRD4 is shown in a variety of cancers, and it has been demonstrated that elevated levels of BRD4 expression have an adverse correlation with survival in certain malignancies[25, 26]. To assess the correlation between BRD4 and its role in gastric cancers, we first examined datasets provided by the GEO and TCGA databases to investigate the expression levels of BRD4. The results demonstrated higher expression of BRC4 in gastric cancer and a negative connection between the overexpression of BRD4 and the survival rate of gastric cancer. Additionally, analyzing 375 gastric cancer samples from the TCGA-STAD dataset showed a significant increase in BRD4 expression compared to 32 healthy stomach samples. A noteworthy finding was also a positive correlation between the expression level of BRD4 and the age of the patients. Accordingly, qRT-PCR results demonstrated increased expression of BRD4 in 25 gastric tissue samples compared to 25 normal samples and confirmed results obtained from bioinformatic observations. In a study conducted in 2023, Zhang et al. demonstrated that the expression level of BRD4 in tumor tissues and adjacent tissues of gastric cancer was significantly higher compared to normal tissues and was strongly correlated with the degree of tumor differentiation, survival situation, clinical staging, and regional lymph nodes metastasis. Furthermore, they demonstrated that siRNA inhibited proliferation, migration, and invasion of gastric cancer cells by decreasing BRD4 expression [10]. Through the E2F/miR-106b-5p/p21 axis, BRD4 also modulates gastric cancer cell proliferation by regulating cellular senescence [27]. BRD4 also activates c-MYC via transcriptional and epigenetic pathways, which inhibits apoptosis in gastric cancer cells [13]. In addition, BRD4 enhances gastric cancer stemness by reducing miR-216a-3p mediated inhibition effect on Wnt/β-catenin signaling [11]. According to the results obtained from our study and previous studies, BRD4 may be a promising biomarker to help diagnose and prognosis gastric cancer early.

miRNAs are involved in gene regulation, and dysregulation of miRNAs is associated with the development and progression of various cancers, including gastric cancer [28]. Using bioinformatics, we subsequently predicted probable miRNAs interacting with BRD4 mRNA by reconstructing the microRNA-mRNA interaction network and determining the correlation between miRNAs and mRNAs via the Pearson correlation coefficient. It was found that hsa-mir-628-5p and mir-26a-3p had a significant probability of accurately targeting the 3'UTR region of BRD4 mRNA. Analyzing the TCGA-STAD dataset showed decreased expression of these miRNAs in gastric cancer samples compared to healthy samples. qRT-PCR analysis of miR-26a-3p expression confirmed our previous findings and demonstrated significantly decreased expression of miR-26a-3p in gastric cancer tissues than healthy adjacent tissues (P = 0.0002). Furthermore, there was no significant relationship between clinical and pathological characteristics of patients (age, gender, grade, lymphatic invasion, and stage) and miR-26a-3p expression. Further, the ROC curve analysis showed that BRD4 and miR-26a are highly specific and sensitive diagnostic biomarkers for detecting gastric cancer (AUC = 0.6958 and 07695, respectively). Also, Pearson's analysis showed that BRD4 and miR-26a-3p were significantly and negatively correlated in gastric cancer tissue samples. Previous studies showed that miR-26a has a tumor suppressor role in various cancers [29–31]. Li and coworkers demonstrated that miR-26a-5p is downregulated in gastric cancer cells and clinical samples while the expression of Wnt5a was high. They also reported that overexpression of miR-26a in gastric cancer can reduce β-catenin and Bcl-2 proteins, suppress cell invasion proliferation, and promote cell apoptosis [32]. In a study conducted on breast cancer cells, it was reported that BRD4 has a role in activating WNT5A, which is essential for the invasion of basal-like breast cancer. They showed that twist directly activates WNT5A through the formation of Twist/BRD4/P-TEFb/RNA-PolII complex at the promoter/enhancer of WNT5A and Twist-BRD4-Wnt5a signaling axis has an essential role in the development and progression of basal-like breast cancer [33]. A study reported that miR-26a levels are also associated with replase-free survival, overall survival, clinical stage, and lymph node metastasis in gastric cancer. EGF9 is a direct target of miR-26a, which is involved in the growth and metastasis of gastric cancer. In vitro and in vivo investigation showed that overexpression of miR-26a inhibits gastric cancer cell proliferation and metastasis [34]. miR-26a is also involved in chemosensitivity of gastric cancer cells. In a study conducted by Wen and coworkers, it was reported that miR-26a directly targets NRAS and E2F2 and overexpression of miR-26a sensitizes gastric cancer cells to cisplatin [35]. In a study conducted on liver cancer cells, it was demonstrated that E2F2 is a direct target of BRD4 and that a BRD4 knockdown led to a decreased expression of E2F2 [36]. Also, in vivo, overexpression of miR-26a/b in nude mice repressed the HGF-VEGF pathway, which led to suppression of angiogenesis and tumor tumorigenesis in gastric cancer [37]. Furthermore, Peng and coworkers demonstrated that restoration of miR-26a inhibits metastasis and stem cell-like phenotype in gastric cancer cells by directly targeting HOXC9 [38].

As mentioned above, microRNAs regulate cell proliferation, apoptosis, and metastasis in cancer cells by targeting protein-coding genes. Nevertheless, recent studies have revealed that the majority of microRNAs involved in the initiation and progression of cancer possess corresponding long non-coding RNAs. For instance, it was demonstrated that by sponging miR-140-3p, lncRNA LINC01234 regulates the cell cycle by mediating BRD4 expression in adrenocortical carcinomas [39]. Another study showed that lncRNA RNA H19 targets miR-152-3p, which leads to activation of BRD4 and promotes tumorigenesis in Multiple Myeloma [40]. Zhao and coworkers demonstrated that lncRNA MALAT1 activates BMP2-Smad signaling through sponging and inhibiting miR-26a-5p, which leads to the expression of ATG5 and autophagy induction in colorectal cancer cells [41]. According to a study conducted by Yin and colleagues, the lncRNA SNHG6 can regulate ARPP19 expression by sponging miR-26a-5p, which accelerates nasopharyngeal carcinoma progression [42]. Furthermore, lncRNA SNHG6 promotes the progression of lung adenocarcinoma by increasing E2F7 expression through functioning as a ceRNA and suppressing miR-26a-5p [43]. Another study conducted on colorectal cancer also reported that SRSF6 expression was upregulated through sponging of miR-26a-3p and miR-128-5p by ZNF561-AS1 [44]. Hence, we looked for lncRNAs that could function as ceRNAs (competing endogenous RNA) with miR-26a-3p and BRD4. Results from cancer regulome tools and the TCGA-STAD dataset showed that the expression of lncRNAs DLG5-AS1 and JMJD1C-AS1 is negatively correlated with the expression of the miR-26a-3p. Further analysis showed that the expression of these lncRNAs has a positive correlation with the expression of BRD4 in gastric cancer samples. qRT-PCR results confirmed the upregulation of DLG5-AS1 and JMJD1C-AS1 lncRNA in gastric tissue samples compared to normal tissues. However, the role of JMJD1C-AS1 and DLG5-AS1 lncRNA in gastric cancer has not been studied or reported. Downregulation of lncRNA DLG5-AS1 was reported in prostate cancer, and by using Coexpression Network Analysis, lncRNA DLG5-AS1 was identified as a biomarker for prostate cancer diagnosis. It has been hypothesized that DLG5-AS1 decreases DEFB1 transcription, which may play a role in prostate cancer development [45]. In another study, lncRNA DLG5-AS1 was reported as an oxidative stress-related lncRNA that was associated with a better prognosis for breast cancer patients [46]. In a study conducted on tumor tissues and normal tissues of glioma, increased expression of lncRNA JMJD1C-AS1 in WHO grade IV glioma was demonstrated [47]. Weng and coworkers suggested that upregulated lncRNA JMJD1C-AS1 in subarachnoid hemorrhage may increase expression of SIRT1 and HDAC4 by functioning as a ceRNA and suppressing the inhibitory effects of hsa-miR-204 on these genes [48].

Conclusion

In conclusion, we demonstrated increased expression of BRD4, lncRNA JMJD1C-AS1, and lncRNA DLG5-AS1 and decreased expression of miR-26a-3p in gastric tissue samples. As BRD4 promotes tumorigenesis of gastric cancer, we hypothesize that the lncRNAs JMJD1C-AS1 and DLG5-AS1 are regulated by BRD4 and promote the progression of gastric cancer by sponging miR-26a-3p and reducing its expression. These findings may serve as a biomarker for gastric cancer diagnosis; however, in order to better understand the role of BRD4/lncRNA JMJD1C-AS1/lncRNA DLG5-AS1/miR-26a-3p, larger studies with more samples and in vivo and in vitro experiments are required.

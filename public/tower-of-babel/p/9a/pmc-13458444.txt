Innovation research on college students’ mental health management system based on internet of things technology
Authors: Jiangxia Luo, Can Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe mental health management of college students is a challenging process due to its inconsistent variations and action-dependent observations. However, for personal and mental stability and psychological assessment of students, the inconsistent mental health is to be handled using a high precision computing system. This article introduces a Deliberate Management System (DMS) powered by the Internet of Things (IoT) concept for inconsistency reduction in the mental health data processing. The proposed system performs non-identical observation based on actions and non-action intervals for reducing inconsistencies. In the observation and processing, the IoT elements are employed for support, prediction, and representation. The representations are classified based on different observation data that are abnormal and are deviating from the non-identical intervals. In data processing, deep learning is employed for identifying non-identical to identical stream assimilations, preventing false rates. This learning is recurrently iterated post the assimilation interval for preventing a steep increase in mental data analysis. The proposed system’s performance is validated using accuracy, false rate, overhead, and time requirement.Keywords: Deep learning, IoT, Mental health management, Student behaviorSubject terms: Health care, Health occupations, Engineering, Materials science

This study addresses the limitations of existing IoT-based mental health systems where data inconsistency, temporal instability, and insufficient behavioral context integration reduce analytical reliability. Although existing studies have explored IoT-enabled mental health monitoring, wearable sensing, and machine learning-based psychological assessment, several limitations remain unresolved. Most approaches focus on data acquisition and classification accuracy without addressing the instability caused by heterogeneous behavioral patterns and temporal inconsistencies. The relationship between action-dependent observations and psychological state transitions is rarely modeled explicitly, leading to fragmented interpretations of mental health conditions. In addition, current systems often rely on static feature extraction and lack mechanisms for continuous adaptation to dynamic behavioral changes. These limitations result in increased false rates and reduced reliability in long-term monitoring scenarios. To overcome these issues, this study proposes a Deliberate Management System that incorporates temporal assimilation, inconsistency reduction, and adaptive learning within an IoT-driven framework, enabling a more stable and context-aware mental health assessment process.

Deliberate management system (DMS) using IoT

DMS is designed to work under the IoT concept to reduce the inconsistent variations and false rates based on mental health management of college students’ behavior analysis based on action-based and non-action-based observations through handled device inputs. The inputs are observed from the college students. The handle devices such as mobile phones, wearable sensors, etc., are used to gather the personal and mental stability and psychological assessment based on heartbeat rate, depression, pulse rate, stress, sleep problems, anxiety, and eating disorder, which are analyzed for the college student’s mental health at different time intervals. Mental health disorders are very common among college students. In Fig. 1, the proposed system is illustrated.

Conceptual illustration of the analytical procedure, illustrating the layered integration of sensing, communication, analytical processing, and decision support components for continuous mental health monitoring.

This study discusses a conceptual analytical approach to address inconsistency and instability in IoT-based mental health monitoring. The system establishes a behavioral-context-aware observation model that links action-dependent data with temporal psychological variations, enabling a more accurate representation of student mental states. A recurrent assimilation mechanism is developed to transform heterogeneous and non-identical data streams into stable representations, reducing uncertainty caused by irregular observations. The analysis considers recurrent learning principles with real-time IoT sensing, that continuously refines prediction results through feedback. This design not only improves the robustness of mental health assessment but also reduces false rate propagation and enhances interpretability compared with conventional approaches that rely on static or isolated data processing.

Figure 1 presents the overall architecture of the proposed DMS, which is structured as a four-layer hierarchical framework to enable continuous and adaptive mental health monitoring of college students through IoT-enabled sensing and deep learning-based processing. At the base, the Perception Layer integrates two categories of IoT devices, namely smartphones and smartband/smartwatch wearables, to collect a comprehensive set of behavioral and physiological data including heart rate, pulse, sleep quality, stress indicators, dietary patterns, and depression-related behavioral cues, which together form the raw input stream for the system. The collected data are then transmitted upward through the Communication Layer via wireless and IoT-standard protocols, including Wi-Fi, 4G, and IoT channels, ensuring reliable and low-latency delivery of heterogeneous data streams to the analytical backend under real-world campus conditions. The core computation is carried out in the Analysis and Processing Layer, where the Deliberate Management System Engine executes a sequential pipeline consisting of Action/Non-Action Sequence Partitioning, Consistent/Inconsistent Interval Classification, Anomaly and Deviation Data Detection, and Deep Learning Recurrent Training and Representation Learning; notably, a deviation-based retraining feedback loop connects the anomaly detection stage back to the deep learning module, allowing the model to iteratively refine its representations upon encountering non-identical intervals and thereby suppressing the accumulation of false rates over time. Finally, the Decision Support Layer translates these learned representations into three functional outputs, namely Psychological Stability Assessment, Psychological State Representation, and Risk Feedback and Warning, providing counselors and administrators with timely and interpretable insights into each student’s mental health trajectory.

Conclusion

This article introduces a deliberate management system for handling the mental health data of college students. The proposed system reduces the inconsistencies in handling mental health observation data. The data is classified into two levels, that is, action and non-action sequence and identical and non-identical. In the first classification, the inconsistent variations are identified, and the learning paradigm is trained for reducing false rates. The second classification relies on abnormal data identification based on deviating time series. In this process, the learning paradigm is trained for identical and non-identical sequences for mitigating overhead issues. The representation-based outputs are used for analytical refinement in improving the accuracy. This deep learning paradigm is trained at variational series for reducing the steep increase in data analysis. Based on the representation, the abnormal and inconsistent data is used for identifying the mental stress with maximum accuracy. The reliability of these improvements is statistically confirmed using paired t-tests on the results obtained from multiple validation folds, demonstrating that the performance gains of the proposed method are not due to random variations in the dataset. The proposed system improves accuracy by 9.25% and reduces false rate, overhead, and time requirement by 13.61%, 7.75%, and 10.38% for the varying observations.

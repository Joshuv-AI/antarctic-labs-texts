High‐Dose vs. Standard‐Dose Influenza Vaccine in Older Patients With Hypertension: A Post Hoc Analysis of DANFLU‐1
Authors: Adam Femerling Langhoff, Niklas Dyrby Johansen, Daniel Modin, Kira Hyldekær Janstrup, Katja Vu Bartholdy, Caroline Espersen, Joshua Nealon, Sandrine Samson, Matthew M Loiacono, Rebecca Harris, Carsten Schade Larsen, Anne Marie Reimer Jensen, Nino Emanuel Landler, Brian L Claggett, Scott D Solomon, Martin J Landray, Gunnar H Gislason, Lars Køber, Pradeesh Sivapalan, Jens Ulrik Stæhr Jensen, Tor Biering‐Sørensen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPatients with hypertension (HTN) face an increased risk of complications and mortality from influenza; a risk that may be modified by influenza vaccination. There is limited evidence on the effectiveness of high‐dose (HD‐IIV) compared with standard‐dose (SD‐IIV) inactivated influenza vaccines in hypertensive individuals. This study, a post hoc analysis of DANFLU‐1, a pragmatic, and open‐label, individually randomized trial of HD‐IIV vs. SD‐IIV conducted during the 2021–2022 influenza season among participants aged 65–79 years. Prespecified outcomes in DANFLU‐1 included hospitalization for influenza or pneumonia, cardiovascular, cardiorespiratory, and respiratory hospitalizations, all‐cause hospitalization, and all‐cause mortality. Outcomes were analyzed as both time‐to‐first and recurrent events. DANFLU‐1 randomized 12 477 participants randomized to HD‐IIV or SD‐IIV, of these 6469 (51.9%) had a history of HTN. HD‐IIV was associated with lower hazards for hospitalizations for pneumonia or influenza, respiratory disease, and all‐cause mortality compared with SD‐IIV and these associations were consistent regardless of HTN status (p interaction = 0.09, = 0.09, and = 0.59, respectively). HD‐IIV was associated with lower incidence rates of recurrent hospitalizations for pneumonia or influenza and all‐cause hospitalizations compared with SD‐IIV, irrespective of HTN status (p interaction = 0.09 and = 0.75, respectively). There was evidence of potential effect modification of HD‐IIV vs. SD‐IIV in relation to recurrent respiratory hospitalizations, where the relative effect may be greater among those without vs. with HTN (p interaction = 0.04). In conclusion, this post hoc analysis of a large‐scale pragmatic trial, HD‐IIV was associated with lower risk of clinical outcomes, including hospitalizations for pneumonia or influenza, all‐cause mortality, and all‐cause hospitalizations irrespective of the presence of HTN. Trial Registration: ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT05048589

Introduction

Hypertension (HTN) is a prevalent and significant risk factor for cardiovascular disease (CVD). Global prevalence was estimated to be 1.13 billion in 2015 [1] with an overall prevalence in adults of 30%–45%, and as high as 63% in individuals above 65 years old [2, 3]. HTN is a chronic inflammatory condition, and persistent inflammation can disrupt the immune response [4, 5]. This disruption may impair the ability of hypertensive individuals to mount an effective antibody response to the influenza vaccine, leading to reduced vaccine efficacy [6, 7].

In Denmark, vaccination for seasonal influenza is offered to patients above 65 years of age and patients with certain comorbidities to protect patients at higher risk of hospitalization and death due to seasonal influenza [8]. Influenza has been shown to increase acute myocardial infarction (AMI) incidence six‐fold in the week after a positive influenza test [9]. In addition, individuals with HTN are at greater risk of dying from cardiovascular outcomes like AMI and stroke [10]. Strategies to enhance antibody responses to vaccines in high‐risk populations have led to growing interest in testing high‐dose influenza vaccines (HD‐IIV). With four times the antigen content of standard‐dose influenza vaccines, the high‐dose influenza vaccine has demonstrated superior immunogenicity and superior efficacy against lab‐confirmed influenza infection [11]. It has also consistently demonstrated improved effectiveness against severe outcomes [12]. Similarly, the randomized feasibility trial DANFLU‐1 that compared HD‐IIV with SD‐IIV in adults aged 65–79 years showed lower event rates for hospitalizations for pneumonia or influenza in the HD‐IIV vs. SD‐IIV arm [13], consistent with earlier findings [12, 14, 15, 16]. However, limited data exist on the potential additive benefit of HD‐IIV compared to SD‐IIV in individuals with HTN.

Therefore, the objective of this post hoc analysis of the DANFLU‐1 trial was to assess the relative effectiveness of HD‐IIV vs. SD‐IIV against prespecified clinical outcomes according to the presence of HTN at baseline.

Methods

Study Design

DANFLU‐1 was a pragmatic, registry‐based, open‐label, active‐controlled, individually randomized trial of HD‐IIV vs. SD‐IIV (both quadrivalent influenza vaccines) to assess the feasibility of conducting a large‐scale vaccine trial relying solely on registry‐based data collection. The study design and the primary results have been published previously [13, 17]. Briefly, participants aged 65–79 years were randomly assigned 1:1 to receive HD‐IIV or SD‐IIV during the 2021–2022 northern hemisphere influenza season. Participants were recruited, included, randomized, and vaccinated at more than 1000 decentralized vaccination sessions organized by a private vaccination provider across Denmark. Obtainment of baseline and follow‐up data, including safety monitoring, was performed centrally by the Center for Translational Cardiology and Pragmatic Randomized Trials, Department of Cardiology at Copenhagen University Hospital ‐ Herlev and Gentofte, Copenhagen, Denmark, using Danish nationwide health registries. In collaboration with the Danish Health Data Authority, the social security numbers of each study subject were used to link the DANFLU‐1 database to the Danish nationwide health registries. These registries contain information on all in‐ and out‐patient visits in the Danish public health system. DANFLU‐1 was approved by the Regional Danish Committee on Biomedical Research Ethics (H‐21035316) and the Danish Medicines Agency (EudraCT no. 2021‐003170‐31). The trial was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Council for Harmonization Good Clinical Practice guidelines. All participants provided written informed consent.

Baseline Comorbidities

In DANFLU‐1, registry‐based definitions of baseline conditions and medication use were prespecified using International Classification of Diseases (ICD) 10 codes and Anatomic Therapeutic Chemical (ATC) codes. HTN is believed to be largely underdiagnosed in Denmark, as ICD‐10 diagnosis codes are registered only in relation to in‐ and out‐patient hospital encounters and not in primary care where most patients are followed for HTN. For the present analysis, we therefore linked the DANFLU‐1 population to the Danish National Prescription Registry using the unique individual identification number (CPR‐number) assigned at birth in order to identify HTN as receiving at least 2 antihypertensive medications at any time 180 days before randomization [17]. This method of defining HTN has previously shown high positive predictive value (PPV) and specificity [18]. Ultimately, for the present analysis, HTN was defined as either ICD‐10 diagnosis of HTN, or receiving at least two antihypertensive medications at any time 180 days before randomization.

Outcomes

The primary outcome in DANFLU‐1 was feasibility‐oriented. Although the study was not powered to assess clinical endpoints, additional objectives of the study included assessment of effectiveness against the prespecified clinical endpoints of hospitalization for influenza and/or pneumonia, hospitalization for respiratory disease, cardio‐respiratory disease, hospitalization for any CVD, hospitalization from any cause, hospitalization for COVID‐19, and all‐cause mortality. The follow‐up period for clinical outcomes was from 14 days post‐vaccination through May 31, 2022. All outcomes were collected directly from the registries using ICD‐10 codes. Data sources have been detailed in previous publications [17].

Statistical Analysis

Baseline characteristics were summarized by the presence of HTN and by randomization group. Continuous variables are reported as means and standard deviations. Binary or categorical variables are reported using frequencies and proportions.

Analyses of clinical outcomes were conducted using the intention‐to‐treat principle. Outcomes were analyzed as both time‐to‐first and recurrent events. Cox proportional hazards models were employed to analyze the effect of HD‐IIV vs. SD‐IIV on time‐to‐first‐event outcomes and hazard ratios (HR) were reported. Negative binomial regression was used to compute incidence rate ratios (IRR) for recurrent event analyses. Interaction terms between HTN status and treatment allocation were included to test for effect modification.

A sensitivity analysis was conducted on the effect of HD‐IIV vs. SD‐IIV on severe clinical outcomes among persons with HTN, by using competing risk models that accounted for death as a competing event.

Statistical analysis was performed with the use of SAS software version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Inc.), Stata MP version 17.0 (StataCorp), and R version 2024.09.1 Build 394 (R Core Team, 2021).

Results

A total of 12 477 participants were included in DANFLU‐1. Four participants (two from each randomization group) emigrated during the follow‐up period; Registry linkage was unsuccessful for four participants; any hospitalizations or deaths occurring in these participants would therefore not be expected to be recorded in the Danish registries. Complete follow‐up data were available for more than 99.9% of participants. These non‐linkable participants were censored at day zero, and thus not further analyzed in the study. Details of the baseline characteristics of the DANFLU‐1 population have previously been published [13]. A total of 6469 (51.8%) participants had HTN, of which 2052 (31.7%) were registered with an ICD‐10 diagnosis of HTN and 6312 (97%) received at least two antihypertensive prescription medications. Among patients with HTN, 3215 (49.7%) were randomized to HD‐IIV and 3254 (50.3%) were randomized to SD‐IIV. Baseline characteristics stratified by HTN status are shown in Table 1. Among persons with HTN, mean age was 72.2 (SD 3.9) and 43.2% were female. Compared with those without HTN, the prevalence of chronic diseases such as CVD, ischemic heart disease (IHD), heart failure (HF), atrial fibrillation (AF) was higher among participants with HTN.

Absolute incidence rates for hospitalization for CVD were higher among those with HTN (35.5 per 1000 person‐years) than those without HTN (14.6 per 1000 person‐years) (p < 0.001). The same was true for hospitalization for cardiorespiratory and respiratory disease (48.8 per 1000 person‐years vs. 20.6 person‐years and 13.8 per 1000 person‐years vs. 6.1 per 1000 person‐years, respectively) (p < 0.001 and p = 0.003, respectively) (Figure 1).

Hospitalization rates according to HTN.

In patients with HTN, HD‐IIV was associated with a 45% lower risk of hospitalization for pneumonia or influenza, a 7% higher risk of hospitalization for CVD, a 2% lower risk of hospitalization for cardiorespiratory disease, a 21% lower risk of hospitalization for respiratory disease and a 7% lower risk of all‐cause hospitalization, although all these estimates remained insignificant. In these patients with HTN, HD‐IIV was associated with a 54% lower risk of all‐cause mortality (p = 0.02) (Figure 2). All these HRs and absolute risk reductions reported in Supplemental Documents (Table S1) should be interpreted with caution, since they are extrapolated from an interaction analysis, not designed for analyzing absolute risk reduction. HD‐IIV vs. SD‐IIV showed no significant effect modification in time to first event analysis for hospitalization with influenza or pneumonia, regardless of HTN status at baseline (Figure 2). In this time‐to‐first event analysis, the presence of HTN did not modify the effect of HD‐IIV compared with SD‐IIV for any type of severe outcomes assessed. These outcomes included hospitalization for influenza or pneumonia (overall: HR 0.36 [0.17–0.73], HTN: HR 0.55 [0.24–1.25], non‐HTN: HR 0.08 [0.01–0.65]; p for interaction: 0.09), hospitalization for CVD (overall: HR 1.02 [0.75–1.39], HTN: HR 1.07 [0.75–1.54], non‐HTN: HR 0.88 [0.49–1.58], p for interaction: 0.58), hospitalization for cardiorespiratory disease (overall: HR 0.88 [0.66–1.15], HTN: HR 0.98 [0.72–1.35], non‐HTN: HR 0.66 [0.40–1.10], p for interaction: 0.19), hospitalization for respiratory disease (overall: HR 0.60 [0.36–0.99], HTN: HR 0.79 (0.44–1.42], non‐HTN: HR 0.27 (0.09–0.81], p for interaction: 0.09), for all‐cause hospitalization (overall: HR 0.93 [0.83–1.05], HTN: HR 0.93 [0.80–1.08], non‐HTN: HR 0.94 [0.76–1.14], p for interaction: 0.95) and for all‐cause mortality (overall: HR 0.51 [0.30–0.86], HTN: HR 0.46 [0.24–0.88], non‐HTN: HR 0.62 [0.26–1.50], p for interaction: 0.59) (Figure 2).

Discussion

In this post hoc analysis of the DANFLU‐1 trial, we present findings of the relative vaccine effectiveness of HD‐IIV vs. SD‐IIV which were largely consistent among older individuals with and without HTN. HD‐IIV vs. SD‐IIV showed no effect modification in time to first event analysis for hospitalization with influenza or pneumonia, and any other time to first event outcomes assessed, regardless of HTN status at baseline. Likewise, HD‐IIV vs. SD‐IIV showed no effect modification in recurrent event analysis for hospitalization with influenza or pneumonia, regardless of HTN status at baseline. While our findings suggest a potential effect modification by the presence of HTN for respiratory hospitalization in the recurrent event analysis, point estimates are similarly suggestive of a potential benefit for both populations. Numerically lower rVE estimates were observed in several outcomes. These study findings bring new knowledge by expanding the range of severe outcomes that have been assessed and provide the first insights into the potential added benefit of HD‐IIV compared to SD‐IIV in the HTN risk group. Influenza creates a prothrombotic state along with vessel inflammation (including coronary vessels), fever, and tachycardia, increasing the patient's risk of AMI [19]. HTN is a chronic inflammatory condition, and persistent inflammation can disrupt the immune response, which theoretically could affect immune response to vaccination, which should not be mistaken for an immunocompromised state, but perhaps an immunomodulated state [4, 5]. While this has been observed for individuals with HTN following COVID‐19 vaccination, it has not been explicitly explored for influenza [20]. Additionally, antihypertensive medications, particularly those that affect the renin‐angiotensin‐aldosterone system (RAAS), may have immunomodulatory effects that could influence vaccine responsiveness [21]. In turn, these factors could, conversely, reduce the effect of HD‐IIV on the outcome of recurrent respiratory hospitalization, which was the only event where HTN modified the effectiveness of HD‐IIV vs. SD‐IIV, however it must be mentioned that the limited number of respiratory hospitalizations made the statistical significance of this interaction difficult to ascertain. Generally, our results point to no interaction between the vaccine effect based on HTN status. Similarly, Vardeny et al. found no difference between high‐dose trivalent influenza vaccine, compared with standard‐dose quadrivalent vaccine in the reduction of all‐cause mortality or hospitalization for cardiac or pulmonary causes in the INVESTED trial [22].

In time to first event analysis, we found that HD‐IIV was associated with a lower incidence of influenza and pneumonia hospitalization and all‐cause mortality compared with SD‐IIV. Additionally, in recurrent event analysis we found that HD‐IIV was associated with a lower incidence of influenza and pneumonia hospitalization and all‐cause hospitalization compared with SD‐IIV. The DANFLU‐1 trial was designed to assess the feasibility of performing a large‐scale register‐based RCT during seasonal influenza as a part of the national vaccine program. While the study was not powered to assess clinical outcomes, a lower number of hospitalizations for influenza or pneumonia was observed in the HD‐IIV group compared to the SD‐IIV group [13]. In this current analysis, we build on these observations by evaluating the consistency of these effectiveness findings for HD‐IIV compared to SD‐IIV among participants with HTN at baseline. Within the HTN group in this post hoc analysis of DANFLU‐1, HD‐IIV was associated with a 45% (95%CI 25%–76%) lower risk of pneumonia or influenza hospitalization, and 54% (95%CI 22%–76%) lower risk of all‐cause mortality compared with SD–IIV. Although further research is necessary to precisely determine the magnitude of this effect, these findings are promising and underscore the potential for enhanced protection offered by HD‐IIV among those with HTN. We believe this is because HTN is not an immunocompromised state, but rather a risk factor for developing outcomes in general which is also apparent from current guidelines for vaccines in cardiovascular patients [23].

Our analysis does not specifically determine whether the presence of HTN directly influences vaccine effectiveness, or rather, if patients with HTN may differ on other dimensions which may also influence vaccine efficacy. Individuals with HTN have increased underlying comorbidities and impaired immune function (competing risk), which may make them more susceptible to severe influenza infections. As these factors were not adjusted in the statistical analysis, it is possible that factors coinciding with the presence of HTN may have influenced the reported findings. Nonetheless, our results did not show any effect modification of HTN on the majority of the analyzed outcomes, suggesting that HD‐IIV may provide an incremental benefit against severe clinical outcomes in this vulnerable population.

Previous studies have demonstrated that HD‐IIV shows superior efficacy against laboratory‐confirmed influenza among adults aged 65 and older [22]. Additionally, data from a cluster‐randomized trial reveal that high‐dose vaccination significantly reduced the risk of respiratory‐related hospitalization among nursing home residents in the United States when compared with standard‐dose vaccine [24]. In the high‐dose group, 77.7% of participants had HTN, while 72.1% in the standard‐dose group had this condition.

A post hoc analysis of the DANFLU‐1 study found fewer recurrent hospitalizations for pneumonia or influenza as well as all‐cause hospitalizations for HD‐IIV compared with SD‐IIV, according to influenza circulation [25]. Currently, there are no randomized trial data on the effects of HD‐IIV vs. SD‐IIV in individuals with HTN. A previous retrospective cohort study reported a reduced risk of death in patients with HTN who were vaccinated against influenza [7].

Prior research has shown that the risk of hospitalization in influenza patients compared to a corresponding non‐influenza cohort was prominent in cardiovascular high‐risk groups, such as IHD and HF [26].

The link between influenza infection and an increased risk of cardiovascular mortality is well‐established [27]. Less attention has been given to the effect of conditions like HTN, which cause low‐grade inflammation, on the effectiveness of influenza vaccinations, but one study indicates that influenza vaccination is associated with a reduced risk of incident chronic kidney disease (CKD) in populations with HTN [28].

Several limitations of this analysis should be noted.

As noted previously, DANFLU‐1 was not powered to assess clinical outcomes, nor to address the objectives of this analysis. The number of endpoints in the HTN group was very small, and thus all reported estimates should be interpreted cautiously, taking note more so of the direction of rather than the specific magnitude of effect.

Since the 2021–2022 season coincided with COVID‐19 variants (Omicron) and atypical influenza activity, the interpretation of vaccine effects disclosed in this study should be taken with caution. The effect estimates of vaccines may not be generalizable due to viral interference, viral protection measures (masking, distance, barriers, anti‐physical measures), and even hospital admission decisions could be affected due to these circumstances. However, this is a randomized trial, and thus, these factors are expected to be equally divided between groups.

No adjustments for multiplicity were applied, meaning a risk exists that our results are chance findings. Regarding outcomes, the Danish national registries are not maintained for research purposes and may be subject to some extent of imprecision. Such imprecision would be equally present between the randomization arms and should therefore not disqualify findings regarding the relative effectiveness of HD‐IIV vs. SD‐IIV. This imprecision is also true for our definition of HTN, where we used a definition of prescription drugs as a diagnosis of HTN, which is neither 100% specific nor 100% sensitive.

In this large‐scale trial with registry‐based follow‐up, we lacked the opportunity to confirm influenza infection via laboratory testing, and we also did not have access to blood pressure measurements or the exact number of antihypertensive medications. Although we adjusted for several demographic and clinical factors, and given that this is a large, randomized trial which practically eliminates residual confounding, our findings are still susceptible to type‐1 error inflation or the effects of multiple comparisons; therefore, they should be interpreted cautiously when considering their generalizability. All results here should be considered hypothesis‐generating and warrant further investigation.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this post hoc analysis of a large‐scale pragmatic trial, HD‐IIV was associated with lower risk of severe clinical outcomes, including hospitalizations for pneumonia or influenza, all‐cause mortality, and all‐cause hospitalizations among older adults, irrespective of the presence of HTN at baseline. These findings are hypothesis‐generating and warrant further investigation.

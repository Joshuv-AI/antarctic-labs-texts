Reconfigurable analysis and workspace optimization of modular cable-suspended parallel robot
Authors: Tao Zhao, Wei Dai, Fei Zheng, Xiaofei Lou, Sen Qian, Bin Zi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A reconfigurable design method of a modular cable-suspended parallel robot (MCSPR) reconfigured by six same modular branches is proposed in this paper. The MCSPR can be reconstructed by not only separating or adding diverse number of modular branches through manual assistance, but also altering the angles and heights of the modular branches automatically. A structural synthesis based on graph theory of the MCSPR is proposed. The inverse kinematics and statics of the MCSPR including five different configurations are analyzed through a unified method. The kinematics capability of the MCSPR is verified by the experiments. The Monte Carlo method is adopted to analyze the static equilibrium workspace (SEW) of the MCSPR considering different affecting factors. As an example, a 6-degree of freedom (DOF) SEW is analyzed in detail. The objective function of the maximal SEW volume of the MCSPR is defined. The maximal SEW volume of the 6-DOF MCSPR is obtained by optimizing the objective function. The results indicate that the reconfigurable analysis method is effective for the MCSPR.

Introduction

Cable-suspended parallel robots (CSPRs) are a special class of parallel manipulators whose end-effector is actuated by suspended cables instead of rigid rods. Compared with the traditional rigid parallel robots, CSPRs have many potential advantages in terms of simple and lightweight structures with low moving inertia, large workspace, high dynamics, high payload mass ratio, good safety, low cost, ease to assemble and disassemble, good transportability, superior modularity, and high re-configurability1–3. In recent decades, CSPRs have been widely applied in numerous engineering areas due to these attractive properties, such as large spherical radio telescope4, camera robot5, 3D-printer6, high speed robot7, large-scale manipulation8, rehabilitation robot9, material handling10, haptic device11, wind tunnel test12, building construction13, spray-painting robot14, and rescue operations15. However, cable possesses a unilateral driving capability that can only be stretched but not be compressed, which restricts the development and application of CSPRs. Each of these varying applications has specific requirements, which lead to diverse CSPRs configurations. Therefore, it is a further study direction that research on CSPRs focus on the modularity and reconfiguration innovation.

The main idea of modularity is that divides a complicated system into some specific functions in order to comprehend the relationships and promote interaction among the basic elements. In addition, the basic elements may be replaced for repair, upgrade, or reconfiguration. Modular robots (MRs) are a robotic system equipped with basic elements and modules, which can be assembled and reconfigured as an integrated system to perform robotic tasks. Modular parallel robots (MPRs) consist of active joints, passive joints, links, a fastened base and an end-effector, which can be rapidly constructed using these basic elements into various configurations to achieve the required tasks. The advantages of modular reconfiguration are as follows16: (1) Shorten the development cycle of the robot system; (2) Fast design, manufacture and low cost; (3) Satisfy diverse operation requirements and less maintenance. Modular reconfigurable robots (MRRs) integrate the characteristics and properties of multiple robotic modules to perform objective tasks that are beyond the properties of a single robot. There are three types of MRRs: manual-reconfiguring, self-reconfiguring, and self-assembly17. Modular reconfigurable parallel robots (MRPRs) can be assembled into a new configuration to perform various tasks by changing the joint types, as well as the position and length of the links18,19. With the development of a systematic approach of modular reconfiguration and structure synthesis, MRPRs have a significant challenge in the research field of parallel robots.

Given their attractive features and properties, MRPRs have been applied into various rigid parallel robot areas. Zhang et al.20 introduced the theoretical design of a reconfigurable machine tool (RMT) using modular design approach. The kinematics model of the RMT was analyzed, and examples were provided to show the procedures of design and implementation. Carbonari et al.21 put forward a category of reconfigurable MPRs origining from the 3-CPS under-actuated topology. Tosi et al.22 presented a new reconfigurable hybrid robot that can attain seven DOFs. The robot can be configured to obtain 3 or 4-DOF for the mobile base, or 3-DOF with redundant actuation. However, MRPRs have very limited applications in the domain of cable-driven parallel robots (CDPRs). Izard et al.23 raised a more particular opinion with respect to the instances of layouts for many study and industrial items, as well as the diverse elements and the reconfigurable abilities. Nguyen et al.24,25 put forward a novel and reconfigurable large-dimension CSPRs, which instead of the traditional methods of handing heavy and large parts across wide workspaces. Because the reconfigurable CSPRs provides larger workspace, higher agility as well as lower power consumtion, thus it has been used to transport workers in an aircraft repair workshop. Gagliardini et al.26 dealt with cable attachment point of the reconfigurable CDPRs on the base frame can be located at a discontinuous and a set of possibly large positions. To choose and optimize the order of discontinuous reconfigurations, the end-effector of reconfigurable CDPRs was allowed to track a specified trajectory. Barbazza et al.27 presented a new CSPR with reconfigurable end-effector, which has the capability revising attachment points between cables and end-effector in order to prevent impacts with barriers. Li et al.28 presented a reconfigurable and modular cable-driven robotic grasper that integrates the characteristics of full-dirven and under-driven. The grasper can be used to grab various unknown objects in an unstructured circumstances. Zhou et al.29 presented the design, analysis and test of a new modular reconfigurable CDPR. Wu et al.30 proposed a reconfigurable CDPR having characteristics of large load-to-weight ratio, easy modularity and variable stiffness in order to avoid collision and improve the flexibility of CDPRs. Trautwein et al.31 presented a strategy for stable topological in-operation-reconfiguration of CDPRs. During the whole reconfiguration process, the strategy guarantees the stability of the platform by considering a stability criterion based on the potential energy. Chaudhury et al.32 adopted the Monte Carlo method and gradient based optimization algorithms to design multi-DOF parallel manipulators and closed-loop mechanisms. Therefore, that is necessary to investigate a reconfigurable MCSPR. The proposed MCSPR offers innovation design of the reconfigurable CSPRs, and stimulates more study of the reconfigurable CSPRs.

The main motivations of this article are to investigate the reconfigurable analysis, structural synthesis, inverse kinematics and statics considering the end-effector and the rotation angle of the drum as well as the angles and heights of the modular branches, and workspace optimization of the MCSPR including five diverse configurations to meet different task needs according to modularity and reconfiguration. Other contributions with respect to the MCSPR including mechanical description, control system hardware architecture, inverse kinematics and dynamics only considering the end-effector have been studied in authors’ previous jobs33. The new contributions of this paper are as follows: (1) A design method for the reconfigurable analysis of the MCSPR is proposed. (2) A structural synthesis based on graph theory of the MCSPR is proposed, assuming that cable is a virtual prismatic actuator module that only can pull and cannot push. (3) The inverse kinematics and statics of the MCSPR including five different configurations are analyzed through a unified method. (4) The objective function of the maximal SEW volume of the MCSPR is defined, which is related to the mass and Euler angles of the end-effector, as well as the angles and heights of the modular branches.

The remainder of this paper is as follows. Section 2 introduces a mechanical description of the MCSPR. Section 3 proposes a design method for the reconfigurable analysis of the MCSPR. The structure synthesis based on graph theory of the MCSPR is presented. Section 4 propose a unified method to analyze the inverse kinematics of the MCSPR considering five varying configurations. The kinematics capability of the MCSPR can be verified by the experiments of a 3-DOF case. In Section 5, the SEW of the MCSPR is a collection of points that is obtained by the Monte Carlo method. A 6-DOF SEW case is analyzed in detail. The objective function of the maximal SEW volume of the MCSPR is defined. The maximal SEW volume of the 6-DOF MCSPR is obtained by optimizing the objective function. Finally, conclusions are summarized and future works are presented.

Mechanical description

The MCSPR can be reconfigured by six same modular branches in order to meet different configurations and task needs. The main design idea of the MCSPR is that the modular branch is used as a basic component. The MCSPR may be decomposed into five typical configurations which are shown in Fig. 1, that is, it can be reconstructed by separating or adding diverse number of modular branches. By separating zero, one, two(three), or four modular branches through manual assistance respectively, and the MCSPR can be reconstructed from 6-DOF to 4, 3, or 2. Both configuration 2 and configuration 3 possess 3-DOF of the end-effector. Configuration 2 comprises three modular branches which represents an under-constrained CSPR, while configuration 3 includes four modular branches which denotes a fully-constrained CSPR. Meanwhile, the MCSPR can also be automatically reconstructed by altering the angles and heights of modular branches. It is well known that the traditional CSPR is not reconfigurable, and their inverse kinematics, statics, and workspace have been investigated according to the specific case of given CDPRs types as well as are typically limited to 3-DOF to 6-DOF. Thus the MCSPR not only offers innovation design for CSPRs, but also facilitates further research on CSPRs inverse kinematics, statics and workspace.Fig. 1Five typical configurations of the MCSPR.

Five typical configurations of the MCSPR.

The systematic forming process of mechanisms is commonly known as structural synthesis. Structural synthesis determines the ordinary arrangement of the mechanical structure, such as the type and number of joints and how they are connected. It is an efficient and systematic approach for mechanism design. Varying types of mechanisms can be obtained in the light of the expected number of links and DOFs via structural synthesis. Structural synthesis has been diffusely used in rigid parallel robots in recent decades34. However, it is very rarely used in MCSPR analysis. Therefore, there is a new challenge for employing the structural synthesis method in the MCSPR.

The proposed reconfigurable analysis method mainly concentrates on the design and configuration issues of the MCSPR. This method is organized in a primary stage, as shown in Fig. 6. The most basic design principle of the MCSPR is for the robot designer to customize its solution on DOFs of the end-effector. The stage is structured with the following steps according to DOFs of the end-effector35.Fig. 6Design method of the MCSPR.

Design method of the MCSPR.

(1) Design of the MCSPR modules

The modules denote the main building blocks including joints, links, and others. Here, the joints consist of actuator joints and passive joints, and the links are simply a ways of attaching two different joints to each other in series. For the reconfigurable MCSPR system, the larger the store number of modules, the more multiplicity of possible solutions.

(2) Structuring the modules in a directory

The directory is actually a database including link modules, joint modules, the circular guideway, and the end-effector, in which every module has information about geometric characteristics, capability, and costs. For every element in this database, there are various use, which expands the configuration combinations.

(3) Configuring the MCSPR

Configuration of the MCSPR starts with a set of DOFs of the end-effector with regard to the database of missions to be achieved by the module directory and the robot. In order to form the possible configurations, the goal for the configuration issues is to choose the type and number of modules according to structural synthesis of the MCSPR.

For rigid parallel mechanisms, the workspace is a space that the forward/inverse solutions of the kinematics problem exist. For CSPRs, the workspace is a space where sets of positive cable tensions exist, because they are always required to restrict the end-effector regardless of the any external wrench36. That is, two conditions must be met for the end-effector to get to the workspace: (1) all cables are tensioned; (2) the constraints of all other given motion and force are met. The workspace of CSPRs may be divided into five categories according to the aforementioned two conditions, which consists of the SEW, wrench feasible workspace (WFW), wrench closure workspace (WCW), collision-free workspace (CFW), and dynamic workspace (DW). The SEW is the sets of positions and orientations that the end-effector only considering the gravity can maintain statically balance37,38.

Compared with common CSPRs, the configurations of the MCSPR in order to satisfy different task needs can be altered by separating/adding the diverse number of modular branches, that is, decreasing/increasing the different number of cables connected to the end-effector; altering the rotation angles of modular branches via gear drive; and adjusting the heights of modular branches by hydraulic cylinders. Thus, the workspace of the MCSPR may also be changed by modifying one or several of the aforementioned structural parameters. Meantime, all the cable tensions are guaranteed to be in a subset of the positive tension space, and the end-effector is balanced by an external force and moment. The SEW of the MCSPR is analyzed as follows.

The SEW of the MCSPR is a connected set of points, which can be approximated by multiple points inside it. According to the kinematics model and static equilibrium equation of the MCSPR, the SEW is a collection of the points that is obtained by the Monte Carlo method. Monte Carlo method is not only a random sampling simulation method but also a powerful tool for solving robot’s workspace. Monte Carlo method is simple and has a wide range of applications. It can be adopted to solve the workspace of parallel robots and serial robots. This method is simple to program and has good point uniformity in the workspace. Compared to the other numerical methods, the use of this method is favorable in terms of the comparatively short time needed for determining the workspace volume and shape40.

When the angles and heights of the modular branches are determined, once the solution to the inverse kinematics and statics of the MCSPR is available, and the SEW can be readily generated utilizing the Monte Carlo method. Conclusions

A novel design approach is used to the reconfigurable analysis of the MCSPR in this paper. The MCSPR can be reconfigured by six same modular branches. By manually separating zero, one, two(three), or four modular branches respectively, the MCSPR can be reconstructed from 6-DOF to 4, 3, or 2. As the same time, the MCSPR can also be automatically reconstructed by altering the angles and heights of modular branches. Thus the MCSPR has the characteristics of a manual-reconfiguring robot, e.g., configurations 1 to 5. In addition, it has the features of an under-constrained CSPR, a full-constrained CSPR, and a redundantly constrained CSPR. A structural synthesis method based on graph theory is employed for depicting the structure of the MCSPR, assuming that the cable is a virtual prismatic actuator module that can only pull and cannot push. The inverse kinematics and statics of the MCSPR including five different configurations considering 2-DOF, 3, 4 and 6 are analyzed through a unified method. Configuration 2 is selected to validate the kinematic capability of the MCSPR via the experiments. The SEW of the MCSPR is the sets of positions and orientations that the end-effector only considering the gravity can maintain statically balance, which may be approximated by multiple points inside it. The generated points take shape a 3D cloud point in workspace, which is obtained by the Monte Carlo method. The 6-DOF SEW case is analyzed in detail. The objective function of the maximal SEW volume of the MCSPR is defined, which is related to the mass and Euler angles of the end-effector, as well as the angles and heights of the modular branches. The maximal SEW volume of the 6-DOF MCSPR is obtained by optimizing the objective function, and its parameters and shape are given, respectively.

In future study, the efficient reconfiguration strategies of the MCSPR will be investigated for better performance and flexibility in an unstructured environment. The dynamics and stiffness analysis of the MCSPR will be formulated considering varying configurations.

Evaluation of the diagnostic efficacy of different glycemic markers and insulin indices for diabetes mellitus in a general population
Authors: Jiamin Gong, Junzhuo Wang, Liangguang Xiang, Xiaoyin Huang, Ling Xiao, Qingling Su, Xin Xu, Ruimei Feng, Wanxin Li, Shanshan Du, Weimin Ye
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWe aimed to assess the contribution of repeated fasting blood glucose (FBG) measurements along with single collection of 2-hour postprandial glucose (2h PG) and glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) on diabetes mellitus (DM) prevalence estimation, and investigate the association between the insulin-related markers and DM risk in a southeastern Chinese population.Materials and methodsWe included 6515 participants aged 35–75 years with complete glycemic data. McNemar’s test was utilized to compare the difference between WHO and ADA criteria in DM diagnosis. Newly diagnosed DM cases were classified as 1defect (DEF), 2DEF, or 3DEF DM according to the abnormalities of three glycemic indices. The sensitivity and specificity of diagnosing DM using different glycemic markers were assessed. The association between different index and DM was analyzed using log-binomial regression and receiver operating characteristic curves.ResultsThe age- and sex-standardized prevalence of DM was 14.0% and 14.7% by the WHO and ADA criteria, respectively. 2h PG, HbA1c, and FBG detected 89.65%, 40.69%, and 15.24% of newly diagnosed DM population, respectively. . All cases identified by repeated FBG were also detected by either 2h PG or HbA1c. The disposition index showed the highest area under the curve (0.82) in DM prediction, followed by the product of triglyceride and fasting glucose (TyG, 0.72). The estimated previous diagnosis, treatment, and control rate of DM was 38.6%, 34.9%, and 29.4%, respectively.ConclusionIt is recommended that 2h PG and HbA1c should be used in population-based studies. The disposition index and TyG could potentially be used as DM predictors.Keywords: Diagnosis criteria, diabetes mellitus, fasting blood glucose, general population, glycated hemoglobin, insulin resistance, oral glucose tolerance test

Introduction

China has experienced a consistent increase in the prevalence of diabetes mellitus (DM), rising from 35.7% to 38.1% over five years since 2013 [1,2]. However, the implementation of early diagnosis, awareness, and treatment remains inadequate in China [3,4].

A significant challenge in the early diagnosis of DM is the selection of appropriate diagnostic indicators. The World Health Organization (WHO) and the American Diabetes Association (ADA) both utilize fasting blood glucose (FBG) and 2-hour postprandial glucose (2h PG) to diagnose DM, while ADA criteria also include glycosylated hemoglobin (HbA1c). Moreover, both criteria emphasize the importance of repeated testing, which is difficult to achieve in large population studies [1,5–7]. Furthermore, the previous study revealed that isolated postprandial hyperglycemia was present in 50% of Chinese individuals with undiagnosed DM, suggesting that 2h PG may be a prominent DM indicator [8,9]. However, 2h PG and repeat testing are difficult to achieve simultaneously, especially in large-scale epidemiological studies, resulting in bias in estimating DM prevalence [8,10].

Insulin resistance (IR) and β-cell dysfunction are pathophysiological hallmarks of DM, which could be quantified by homeostatic model assessment of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) and other indices [11–13]. Furthermore,TyG (the product of blood triglycerides (TG) and FBG) has been recommended as a valid predictor of insulin resistance in the Chinese population [14]. However, to our knowledge, no study has evaluated the ability of these indices to predict the diagnosis of DM in combination with 2h PG, HbA1c, and especially with repeated measurements of FBG.

This study compared the prevalence of DM and pre-diabetes under WHO and ADA criteria, further characterized the epidemiology of DM subgroups and assessed the association of insulin-related markers with DM risk. By combining three glycemic markers, including repeated FBG measurements, we aimed to improve the diagnostic accuracy of DM and to identify key DM risk-related insulin markers to inform diagnostic strategies in epidemiologic studies.

Materials and methods

Study design and population recruitment

The Fuqing cohort study is an ongoing prospective study that was conducted in Fuqing City, located in the south-eastern region of China. The cohort study recruited native residents aged between 35 and 75 years. The first wave of baseline survey was initiated in February 2019, and the second wave started in July 2020 [15]. In this study, 7662 participants with the completion of the baseline survey between July 2020 and June 2021 were included.

All participants were enrolled in the current study for 2 days. On the first day, overnight fasting blood samples were collected from all participants after at least 8-h of fasting. On the second day, after the collection of overnight fasting blood samples, the 75-g oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) was performed for participants without self-reported DM, for which the 2-hour blood samples were also collected from each participant. The participants who met any of the following criteria were excluded: with incomplete information on the diagnosis of self-reported DM, missing data of FBG or 2h PG or HbA1c for people without self-reported DM, insufficient information of confounding factors. After exclusion, 6515 participants were included in the analysis (Figure 1).

Flowchart depicting the study protocol.

The study protocol was approved by the ethical committee of Fujian Medical University (approval number [2017-07]); approval number [2020-58]) and conformed to the ethical standards for medical research involving human subjects, as laid out in the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and its later amendments. The participants provided written informed consent prior to their participation in the study.

Demographic and lifestyle data

An electronic structured questionnaire was applied to collect data on the demography, lifestyle, medical history, and family history of DM of all participants. Current smokers were defined as individuals who smoked at least 1 cigarette in a day or 7 cigarettes in a week for more than 6 months. Current alcohol drinkers were defined as those who consumed alcohol more than once a week over the past year. Former smokers and alcohol drinkers were defined as those who used to have the abovementioned smoking or drinking behavior but had abstained from this behavior at the time of the questionnaire.

Anthropometric examination

Trained staff measured the body weight, height, waist circumference (WC), and blood pressure (BP) following the standard protocol. Body mass index (BMI) was calculated as weight (kg)/(height [m])2. BP was measured by using an electronic BP monitor (Omron Company, OMRON U30, Japan) twice on the right upper arm of the participants after 5 min of resting in a seated position or thrice if the difference between the two measurements was >5 mmHg. The average of the two closest measurements was applied in the current analysis. High BP was defined as 1) an average systolic BP (SBP) ≥140 mmHg; 2) an average diastolic BP (DBP) ≥90 mmHg; 3) self-reported hypertension or consuming anti-hypertension medicine [16]. Central obesity was defined as WC ≥90 cm in men or ≥85 cm in women. Overweight was defined as BMI ≥24 kg/m2 and <28 kg/m2, while obesity was defined as BMI ≥28 kg/m2 [17].

Laboratory measurements

FBG on the first and second day (FBG1d and FBG2d), 2h PG, TG, total cholesterol (TC), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-c), and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-c) were determined by using standard laboratory procedures on the automatic biochemical analyzer (Toshiba, TBA-120FR, Japan). The fasting insulin (FINS) level was measured by electrochemiluminescence immunoassay (Roche Diagnostics, Cobas e 602, Germany). The HbA1c level was detected using a high-performance liquid chromatography method on a fully automated glycohemoglobin analyzer (ARKRAY, HA8180, Japan). The HbA1c values measured by our laboratory showed high consistency with the results from D-10 Hemoglobin Testing System in a National Glycohemoglobin Standardization Program (NGSP) certificated laboratory (KingMed Diagnostics, Guangzhou, China) as recommended by the Chinese Diabetes Society, and the HbA1c values used in this paper were corrected to eliminate the assay difference (Supplemental Figure 1) [18].

Diagnostic criteria of DM

Self-reported DM was defined as participants who were previously diagnosed with DM in a hospital or were currently taking anti-DM medicine. Newly diagnosed DM was defined as participants without self-reported DM, but whose glycemic level met the DM criteria. Total DM was the sum of self-reported and newly diagnosed DM.

Based on the WHO criteria, the definition of newly diagnosed DM was FBG (both FPG1d and FPG2d) ≥7.0 mmol/L or 2h PG ≥11.1 mmol/L, without self-reported DM. Prediabetes was defined as one of the below-mentioned conditions among the non-DM population [1]: FBG (both FPG1d and FPG2d) 6.1–6.9 mmol/L and 2h PG <11.1 mmol [2]; FBG (both FPG1d and FPG2d) <7.0 mmol/L and 2h PG 7.8–11.0 mmol/L. According to the ADA criteria, newly diagnosed DM was defined when FBG (both FPG1d and FPG2d) ≥7.0 mmol/L or 2h PG ≥11.1 mmol/L or HbA1c ≥6.5% and without self-reported DM. Prediabetes was defined as one of the below-mentioned conditions among the non-DM population [1]: FBG (both FPG1d and FPG2d) 5.6–6.9 mmol/L [2]; 2h PG 7.8–11.0 mmol/L [3]; HbA1c level 5.7–6.4%.

ADA criteria were applied to classify DM patients into different subgroups.

One deficient (1DEF) DM was defined as DM patients with only one glycemic index that met the ADA criteria. Two deficient (2DEF) DM was defined as DM patients with two glycemic indices that met the ADA criteria, but one glycemic index that did not meet the ADA criteria. Three deficient (3DEF) DM was defined as DM patients with all three glycemic indices meeting the ADA criteria.

Previous diagnosis of DM was defined as the proportion of patients with a self-reported hospital diagnosis of DM in the total DM population. The treatment of DM was described as the proportion of patients with DM who were on DM prescription medications in the total DM population. Controlled DM was the proportion of DM patients currently taking antidiabetic medications whose HbA1c level was <7.0%.

The formulae for calculating the indices are listed below:

HOMA-IR: [FBG (mmol/L) ×FINS (mU/L)]/22.5 [13];

The quantitative insulin-sensitivity check index (QUICKI):

1/[Log FINS (mU/L) + Log FBG (mg/dL)] [19];

Homeostasis Model Assessment of Beta-cell function (HOMA-𝛽):

20 × FINS (mU/L)/[FBG (mmol/L) − 3.5] [13];

Disposition index: HOMA-𝛽/HOMA-IR [20];

TyG: Ln[TG (mg/dL) × FBG (mg/dL)/2] [14];

FPG1d was used for the calculation of the abovementioned index.

Statistical analysis

The standardized prevalence was calculated based on the 2010 Chinese population census. The Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was used to determine if the data were normally distributed. Since all the continuous data were non-normal distribution, it was summarized as median with interquartile range (IQR), and Kruskal–Wallis test with Bonferroni multiple comparisons correction was used to compare the differences between different groups. Categorical data was presented as percentage and 95% confidence intervals, and Chi-squared test was used to compare the differences between groups.

The relationship between the glycemic markers and the insulin-related index was assessed by conducting Spearman’s correlational analysis. McNemar’s test was utilized to compare the difference between WHO and ADA criteria in DM diagnosis. After excluding the participants with previous diagnosis of DM or those who received treatment for DM, the sensitivity and specificity of diagnosing DM using different definitions were assessed using the ‘diagt’ command. Sensitivity represents the proportion of true positive cases correctly identified by the diagnostic test, and specificity represents the proportion of true negative cases correctly identified [21]. The log-binomial regression was applied for the analysis of the predictor value of different indexes to the DM risk. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was generated by plotting the sensitivity of all cut-off values on the Y-axis versus their 1-specificity on the X-axis. The optimal cut-off point was determined to be the coordinate closest to the y-intercept (0,1) of the ROC curve, at which point, the sum of sensitivity and specificity was maximum. The area under the curve (AUC) was used to evaluate the diagnostic accuracy [22].

The statistical significance was assessed as two-tailed p < 0.05. All statistical analyses were conducted by using Stata SE version 16.0 (StataCorp LLC, USA).

Results

Demographic characteristics of the study population

The median age of the 6515 participants was 57.00 years (IQR: 15.00), and 65.14% of them were women. Of the total participants, 13.34% participants had a family history of DM. The prevalence of obesity, central obesity, and hypertension was 11.97%, 33.61%, and 47.66%, respectively. Among the males, 15.90% of the participants received a higher level of education (years of education >9), whereas, among the females, it was only 5.18% (p < 0.01). When compared with the male participants, the female participants were less likely to be smokers and alcohol drinkers (male vs female: 72.48% vs 1.37% for smoking, 24.83% vs 3.23% for alcohol drinking, both p < 0.01). The median values of FBG1d, FBG2d, and 2h PG were 4.95 (0.88), 5.03 (0.80), 7.25 (3.01) mmol/L, respectively. The median HbA1c level was 5.70 (0.50)% (Table 1). A strong positive correlation was shown between FBG on different days (r = 0.68, p < 0.01). The Spearman’s correlation coefficient between 2h PG and FBG was 0.39 (p < 0.01) and 0.40 (p < 0.01) on the first and second day, respectively. HbA1c was moderately and positively correlated to FBG (r = 0.47 in FBG1d, r = 0.48 in FBG2d, p < 0.01) and 2h PG (r = 0.40, p < 0.01) (Supplemental Table 1).

Prevalence of DM and prediabetes

The crude prevalence of DM differed significantly by criteria, with the ADA identifying 65 additional cases that were not identified by the WHO (WHO: 19.54%, N = 1,273; ADA: 20.54%, N = 1,338, p < 0.01) (Table 1; Supplemental Table 2). The crude prevalence of prediabetes was 26.26% and 46.40% based on the WHO and ADA criteria, respectively. The self-reported DM rate was 10.16%. DM prevalence increased with increasing age for both the male and female participants. Similarly, the prevalence of prediabetes among the male participants showed an increasing trend by age. However, for female participants, the prevalence of prediabetes peaked in the age group of 60–69 years and then dropped (Figure 2).

Age- and sex-specific prevalence of DM and prediabetes, as diagnosed by WHO and ADA criteria, among the study population (N = 6,515). DM (Diabetes mellitus).

The age- and sex-standardized prevalence of DM was 14.0% (13.0–14.9%) according to the WHO criteria and 14.7% (13.7–15.7%) according to the ADA criteria. The WHO criteria estimated the prevalence of prediabetes to be 23.0% (21.7–24.4%), while the ADA criteria estimated it to be 40.9% (39.3–42.6%). After standardization, people who were previously diagnosed with DM or were taking anti-DM medicine constituted 6.9% (6.3–7.6%) of the total population (Supplemental Table 3).

Distribution of DM subgroups according to the combination of FBG, 2h PG and HbA1c defect

Among the 676 participants newly diagnosed with DM, 457(67.60%) participants had 1DEF DM, of which 85.78% had 2h PG DEF alone and 14.22% had HbA1c DEF alone. Moreover, 130 (19.23%) and 89 (13.17%) participants had 2DEF DM and 3DEF DM, respectively. Overall, 89.65% and 40.69% of the newly diagnosed DM patients had 2h PG and HbA1c DEF, while FBG DEF accounted for 15.24% of the DM patients. Notably, all FBG DEF population had abnormal 2h PG or HbA1c values (Figure 3).

The percentage of different groups contributing to the newly diagnosed DM population with reference to the ADA criteria (N = 676). Note: no participants were included in the isolate fasting glucose group. Abbreviation: 1DEF (1 defect); 2DEF (2 defects); 3DEF (3 defects)

When 662 self-reported DM patients who had previously been diagnosed with DM or were taking antidiabetic medications were included, 41.85%, 21.08%, and 35.35% of the total DM population fell into the 1DEF, 2DEF, and 3DEF categories, respectively. Additionally, 1.72% of the individuals had normal glycemic values. Moreover, 2h PG, HbA1c, and FBG detected 92.53%, 58.60%, and 38.94% of DM patients, respectively. Similarly, there were no isolated FBG DEF patients (Supplemental Figure 2).

Insulin function and metabolic characteristics of the DM subgroups

All participants were divided into normal glucose tolerance (NGT); prediabetes; 1DEF DM, 2DEF DM, 3DEF DM, and self-reported DM groups, according to the ADA criteria.

From the NGT group to the 3DEF DM group, FBG1d, FBG2d, 2h PG, HbA1c, FINS, HOMA-IR, and TyG gradually increased, whereas QUICKI, HOMA-𝛽, and the disposition index steadily decreased. Similarly, WC, SBP, LDL-c, TC to HDL-c ratio and the TG to HDL-c ratio tended to grow, while QUICKI, HOMA-𝛽, and the disposition index tended to decline. Interestingly, although BMI, TC, and TG increased from the NGT group to the 2DEF DM group, they all fell in the 3DEF DM group. Additionally, HDL-c level demonstrated a U-shaped pattern in the NGT and 1DEF DM groups, followed by a progressive decline in the 2DEF and 3DEF groups (Table 2).

Sensitivity and specificity of newly diagnosed DM using different glycemic markers

Using 2h PG as the gold standard for diagnosing DM, FBG demonstrated low sensitivity (16.2%), whereas HbA1c showed moderate sensitivity (33.8%). Combining FBG and HbA1c yielded slightly higher sensitivity (35.3%). In contrast, specificity remained consistently high across all comparisons (FBG: 99.9%; HbA1c: 98.7%; FBG+HbA1c: 98.7%). When FBG was compared directly with HbA1c, sensitivity was moderate (34.2%), while specificity remained exceptionally high (99.8%) (Supplemental Table 4).

Predictive values of different indices on newly diagnosed DM and prediabetes

After excluding the participants with self-reported DM, quartiles of FINS, HOMA-IR, QUICKI, HOMA-𝛽, the disposition index, and TyG were assessed to explore the association between these variables and the risk of DM.

After adjusting for age, sex, education, family history of diabetes, BMI, smoking, alcohol consumption, and hypertension, the higher levels of FINS, HOMA-IR, and TyG were found to be associated with a higher likelihood of the presence of DM, while the higher level of QUICKI, HOMA-𝛽, and the disposition index were associated with a lower likelihood of the presence of DM (Table 3).

Discussion

In the present study, the difference between estimated DM prevalence was slight according to the WHO and ADA criteria. 2h PG showed optimal screening utility, enhanced by the combination with HbA1c, while repeated FBG added no incremental value. Disposition index and TyG may serve as screening predictors. Suboptimal DM awareness, treatment, and control rates indicate significant public health challenges.

Our results demonstrated that 57.99% of DM cases were detected by 2h PG alone, compared with 9.62% by HbA1c alone. This confirms the high sensitivity of 2h PG, which is in agreement with the result of a previous national study [23]. HbA1c reflects the average blood glucose level over the past 6–12 weeks and its diagnostic threshold remains contentious in Chinese populations [24,25]. Consistent with previous report [26], our study showed that the diagnostic capability of HbA1c was superior than FBG but lower than 2h PG. The combination of 2h PG with HbA1c detected 97.94% of newly diagnosed DM cases, whereas the combinations of FBG with HbA1c and FBG with 2h PG detected only 23.53% and 72.49% of newly diagnosed DM cases, respectively. Based on the situation of high prevalence of postprandial blood glucose among the undiagnosed DM in China [8,9], we recommend OGTT should be used as primary tool on the DM estimation to reduce underdiagnosis. Besides, HbA1c could be used as a conjunction tool to additionally improve diagnostic accuracy.

Repeated glucose test was advocated in both ADA and WHO criteria to minimize false-positive DM diagnoses due to the intra-individual glycemic variability, but current evidence are derived from small population [5,6,27,28]. However, the reproducibility of blood glucose measurement remains a persistent challenge in large population-based study [2,3,8]. To illustrate this issue, our study conducted a two-day repeated FBG measurements, and the result demonstrated their strong inter-day correlation (r = 0.68, p < 0.01). In addition. 2h PG was significantly associated with HbA1c (r = 0.40, p < 0.01). Thus, we strongly recommend prioritizing 2h PG or HbA1 rather than repeated measurement of FBG in population-based DM screening. However, the potential diagnostic capability of repeated 2h PG or HbA1c measurements warrants further investigation.

Both islet function and insulin resistance are closely associated with DM. Generally, disposition index reflects pancreatic islets function and TyG indicates insulin resistance [14,29,30]. Our result suggested that the islet function may have a more significant impact than insulin resistance in the prediction of DM. Despite recent studies emphasized β-cell dysfunction in the Chinese population, there is no consistent conclusion about which condition contributes more to the diagnosis of DM [31–33].

The ADA criteria identified a higher prevalence of prediabetes than WHO criteria (46.40% vs. 26.26%). This is in accordance with previous studies, as ADA applies a lower threshold for impaired fasting glucose and additionally introduces HbA1c [34]. From the prospective of disease prevention, a more accurate assessment of at-risk populations, such as prediabetes population, could help improve social and economic benefits [35]. Therefore, future cohort studies could evaluate the long-term impact of pre-diabetes diagnosed according to different criteria on DM risk and other health outcomes.

The prevalence of DM was similar based on the two criteria, but higher than the national prevalence (12.4%) determined using the ADA criteria [2]. The wider age range than this study (≥18 years vs 35–75 years) may explain its issue. The treatment is also an important part in alleviation of DM-associated disease burden. The national rates of prior diagnosis and treatment of DM were estimated to be 36.7% and 32.9%, respectively, and only 50.1% of those treated obtained acceptable effects [2]. Our slightly higher rates of DM diagnosis (38.6%) and treatment (34.9%) than national rates may benefit from the earlier establishment of China’s National Comprehensive Chronic Disease Prevention and Control Demonstration Zone in the southern provinces [36]. However, only 29.4% of DM patients on medication achieved adequate glycemic control, indicating suboptimal disease management within the study population.

This study has several strengths. First, a range of blood glucose measurements were conducted in the general population, incorporating 2h PG, HbA1c, and repeated FBG. The diagnosis of DM was made in accordance with ADA and WHO criteria. This approach facilitated a comprehensive description of DM prevalence. Second, the study compared multiple indicators of insulin resistance and islet function across DM subgroups, and explored the potential predictor for DM. Thus, it attempted to provide an alternative approach for DM evaluation and prediction. This study also has limitations. First, the study population was relatively small, and majority comprised of female from one center in southeast China, which may limit its representativeness of the entire Chinese population. Second, FINS was measured at a single time point. Although the assessment of insulin secretion and islet function was assessed using validated index that exhibited high correlation with the indices estimated using the gold standard, they could not accurately reflect the dynamic processes [11]. Thus, the results should be interpreted with caution. Last, the cross-sectional design could not determine the causal relationship between those indices and DM. Thus, further cohort study is warranted to verify our current study results.

Conclusion

In conclusion, according to the WHO and ADA criteria, the prevalence of DM was approximately 14.0% and 14.7% in southeast China. The difference between the WHO and ADA criteria was slight in DM diagnosis, but significant in the diagnosis of prediabetes. 2h PG is an important marker for DM screening, and its combination with HbA1c can detect the largest number of people with DM compared to the measurement of FBG. However, repeated measurements of FBG failed to significantly enhance the diagnostic efficacy of DM. Therefore, we strongly recommend the use of 2h PG and HbA1c for the diagnosis of DM in population-based studies. Furthermore, the disposition index and TyG may be optional indices for DM risk prediction. Inevitably, active diagnosis, treatment, and control of DM remain huge challenges in China.

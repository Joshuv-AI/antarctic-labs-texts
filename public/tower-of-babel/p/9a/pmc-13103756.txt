Research on the Influence of Titanium Powder Particle Size on the Performance of the CO2 Phase-Change Excitation Agent under Zero Oxygen Balance
Authors: Mingran Du, Cong Jin, Weiwei Li, Meng Wang, Yinjun Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To study the influence of titanium powder particle size on the performance of the CO2 phase-change excitation agent, the ignition reliability, pressure performance, thermal decomposition characteristics, safety performance, and temperature-resistance performance are studied by ignition tests, pressure tests, thermogravimetry, temperature-resistance performance tests, and theoretical calculations. The results indicate that after adding titanium powder with particle sizes of 100, 200, 300, 400, 500, and 600 mesh, the excitation agent poses no risk of combustion or explosion when encountering thermal action at room temperature and is successfully ignited inside the tube. The peak pressure is negatively correlated with the particle size of titanium powder. Within the particle size range selected in this study, the peak pressure increased by 5.87%, 11.29%, 14.21%, 15.89%, 17.25%, and 19.22% compared to the original formula. As the particle size of titanium powder decreases, the thermal stability of the excitation agent first increases and then decreases. When the particle size of the added titanium powder is 500 and 600 mesh, the apparent activation energy is increased by 6.80 kJ·mol–1 and decreased by 7.50 kJ·mol–1, respectively, compared to the original formula. After adding titanium powder with different particle sizes, there is no reaction between the components of the optimized formula at room temperature, and the safety performance is favorable. The temperature indexes T S obtained through theoretical calculations are all above 90 °C.

Introduction

Owing to the demands in coal mine engineering, mine resource development, and urban construction, blasting technology is being increasingly widely employed. Nevertheless, in blasting engineering, harmful factors such as flying stones, air shock waves, toxic and harmful gases, and vibrations are inevitably generated. , To overcome the above existing shortcomings, the CO2 phase-change explosion technology is introduced into the field of rock crushing and excavation. CO2 phase-change explosion technology is a blasting technology that uses the heat released by the combustion of the excitation agent to heat liquid CO2, so that it reaches the critical pressure to form gaseous high pressure and then acts on the surrounding medium to achieve the crushing effect.

In recent years, due to its relatively good safety, the CO2 phase-change explosion technology has attracted much attention. To explore the principle of CO2 blasting and improve related blasting technologies, relevant scholars have carried out a large number of studies. Xiao et al. and Zhang et al. determined the extension law of rock and coal seam fractures under the action of liquid CO2 high-pressure gas through numerical simulation and field tests. Pickering elaborated on CO2 phase-change explosion in detail and contended that CO2 phase-change explosion technology can be more effectively employed for ore mining and transportation compared to ordinary explosive blasting. Yu et al. established an impact pressure test system of CO2 phase-change fracturing and found that the impact distance and impact angle have the most profound influence on the impact pressure. Cao et al. used multistage CARDOX fracturing technology to blast low-permeability coal seams, effectively achieving the purpose of enhancing coal seam permeability. Singh believes that CO2 phase-change explosion technology is much more efficient than other rock crushing methods and can be widely used in the engineering industry. In addition, CO2 phase-change technology is also widely used in underground construction, nuclear power engineering, launch technology, and other aspects, presenting excellent application prospects. The research and development of the above related principles and technologies provide an important theoretical basis for the development of CO2 phase-change explosion technology.

The safety and heat release of the excitation agent are important prerequisites to ensure the smooth progress of the CO2 phase change. Xu and Yin studied the safety of the typical formula of the excitation agent. The results showed that the agent is insensitive enough and cannot be classified as an explosives. The research results of Yang et al. showed that ammonium oxalate has a certain inhibitory effect on the combustion reaction of the excitation agent. Huang et al. through explosion resistance tests showed that the safety performance of the excitation tube is closely related to the proportion of each component of the excitation tube agent. The above research shows that a typical excitation agent has good safety performance under the condition of reasonable formulation. However, a large number of engineering practices demonstrate that in CO2 phase-change explosion, the “misfire” phenomenon often occurs due to the unreliable ignition and insufficient heat release of the excitation agent. Therefore, it is of great significance to develop an excitation agent with a high heat release rate and one that can reliably ignite under the “high-pressure” conditions within the fracturing tube, while remaining safe or nonflammable under normal storage conditions. To overcome the “misfire” phenomenon, Hu and Wang et al. studied the influence of magnesium powder and aluminum powder on the performance of the excitation agent. The results show that adding a certain amount of magnesium powder and aluminum powder can increase the reaction heat of the excitation agent and improve the ignition reliability. Energetic additives can effectively increase the reaction heat of the formula but will also increase the activity of the agent itself. Our research group has previously studied the performance of the CO2 change excitation agent under the synergistic effect of different energetic additives contents and zero oxygen balance. To obtain the optimal formulation of the excitation agent, this paper will conduct further research on the influence of high-energy additives of different particle sizes on the performance of the excitation agent.

Based on the characteristics that small particle size energetic additives can increase the total energy of emulsion explosives, , this paper adds titanium powder with the same mass fraction and different particle sizes to the excitation agent. In order to ensure the safety and usability of the excitation agent, open-air ignition, in-tube ignition, pressure test, thermal analysis, temperature-resistance performance test, and normal temperature storage tests are carried out. The purpose is to obtain a new type of environmentally friendly, high-energy, safe, and reliable excitation agent formula and provide a reference for related research.

Results and Discussion

Analysis of the Ignition Reliability Test

In the open-air ignition test, an electric fusehead was used to ignite the drug pack to test whether the excitation agent could be ignited under normal temperature. The status of the drug pack after ignition is shown in Figure , and the results are shown in Figure .

Postignition status of some drug packs.

Results of the open-air ignition test.

During the ignition test inside the tube, in order to eliminate the influence of experimental randomness on the results, two parallel tests were conducted on each group of samples. The ignition test was conducted on 14 drug packs inside of the cracking tube. The results of the ignition test inside the tube are shown in Figure .

Results of the ignition test in the tube.

In Figure , it can be seen that the test sample has no risk of combustion or explosion when stored at room temperature. Multiple open-air ignition tests showed that the electric fuseheads were all activated. Except that the second test of sample 2# was ignited, the other samples were not ignited. The reason is that the decomposition temperatures of ammonium oxalate and salicylic acid, which act as reducing agents in the excitation agent components, are around 150 and 800 °C, , respectively, while the decomposition temperature of potassium perchlorate, an oxidizing agent, is about 600 °C. , However, salicylic acid begins to sublime at 75 °C. When ignited with an electric fusehead, due to low heat generation, the heat provided is first absorbed by the endothermic properties of salicylic acid sublimation and the decomposition of ammonium oxalate, resulting in a failure to ignite normally. Only sample 2# was ignited once in multiple experiments, and analysis suggests that it was due to uneven mixing of sample components during the preparation of the drug pack. Salicylic acid, as a combustion aid, was concentrated near the electric fusehead, causing the sample to ignite. In multiple experiments, it was ignited only once, which is considered accidental. In order to verify the above analysis, two additional open-air ignition tests were conducted on 2#. The test results showed that it was not ignited.

From Figure , it can be seen that all samples successfully ignited inside the cracking tube, indicating that the optimized formula has high ignition reliability and can be applied in engineering practice to reduce the probability of “misfire”. In conclusion, the excitation agent studied in this paper cannot be ignited under open-air conditions and is safe and reliable; at the same time, in the “high-pressure” condition within the fracturing pipe under the operating conditions, it can be reliably ignited.

The enlarged area in Figure a shows that the pressure at each measurement point experienced a brief period of rapid increase followed by a slow increase, reaching its peak pressure within 55–90 ms after the excitation agent was ignited.

This is due to the chemical reaction between the components of the excitation agent after it is ignited, generating gases (such as CO2, CO, N2, and so on), which rapidly increase the pressure inside the tube in a short time. As heat transfer occurs after ignition of the excitation agent, liquid CO2 absorbs heat and rapidly expands into gas, reaching its peak pressure. When the energy release sheet is not damaged, the pressure wave impacts the energy release sheet and rebounds inside the tube, causing waveform oscillation. When the pressure inside the tube reaches a certain value, the energy release sheet is sheared and damaged, and the high-pressure gas inside the tube is quickly released, reaching atmospheric pressure or even negative pressure.

Analysis of the Peak Pressure

The work capacity of the CO2 phase-change explosion depends on the peak pressure and the CO2 filling weight. Through experimental comparison, the advantages and disadvantages of the work capacity of different samples can be truly and accurately obtained. The specific differences between samples are shown in Figure .

Peak pressure for the different samples.

As illustrated in Figure , the peak pressure inside the tube exhibits a progressive upward trend with decreasing titanium powder particle size. Based on the averaged peak pressure data from two groups of experiments, the samples containing titanium powder with particle sizes of 100, 200, 300, 400, 500, and 600 mesh (samples 2#–7#) demonstrated increases in peak pressure of 5.87%, 11.29%, 14.21%, 15.89%, 17.25%, and 19.22%, respectively, compared to the baseline formulation (1#).

Acting as a high-energy metallic fuel additive, titanium powder undergoes a solid-phase exothermic reaction with the oxidizer present in the excitation agent to form titanium dioxide (TiO2). Although this reaction does not directly produce gaseous species, it substantially increases the overall reaction enthalpy of the system. Theoretical thermodynamic calculations confirm that the specific energy output (i.e., theoretical heat value) of the excitation agent rises from 5203.0 kJ·kg–1 in the absence of titanium powder to 5816.2 kJ·kg–1 upon incorporation of an optimal amount of titanium powder. The additional thermal energy released is predominantly absorbed by liquid CO2, inducing greater thermal uptake and rapid phase transition into gaseous CO2. This expansion significantly amplifies the internal pressure buildup within the tube, thereby contributing directly to the observed increase in peak pressure.

Comparing sample 7# to 6#, 6# to 5#, 5# to 4#, 4# to 3#, and 3# to 2#, the peak pressure increased by 1.68%, 1.09%, 1.56%, 2.62%, and 5.12%, respectively. This progressive enhancement is attributable to the increased specific surface area of the titanium powder as the particle size decreases, which improves interfacial contact efficiency with the oxidizer. Enhanced contact promotes more rapid and complete combustion, thereby accelerating the rate of heat release. Consequently, peak pressure exhibits a consistent upward trend with decreasing titanium powder particle sizehighlighting the critical role of titanium powder particle size in optimizing the energy output efficiency of the formulation.

Additionally, although the thickness of the energy-release sheet was uniformly maintained at 3.5 mm, Figure clearly reveals a consistent and pronounced increase in the peak pressure as the titanium-powder particle size decreases. This trend can be attributed to the strain-rate effect arising from the dynamic fracture of the energy-release sheet.

Reducing the titanium-powder particle size increases its specific surface area and enhances its chemical reactivity, thereby accelerating the internal pressure-rise rate within the tube. A higher pressure-rise rate subjects the energy-release sheet to more intense dynamic loading over a shorter time interval, elevating it to a higher strain-rate regime. Under dynamic loading conditions, the shear strength of metallic materials is generally significantly greater than under quasi-static conditions and further increases with the magnitude of the applied dynamic load. Consequently, this manifests as elevated instantaneous shear pressure and a stronger overall pressure response. Therefore, the experimental data further corroborate thatunder identical titanium-powder mass fractiondecreasing the particle size effectively enhances both the energy-release rate of the excitation agent and the phase-change blasting work capacity of liquid CO2, primarily through acceleration of the reaction kinetics.

Analysis of Thermal Decomposition Characteristics

In order to deeply study the influence of titanium powder particle size on the thermal decomposition performance of the excitation agent, TG experiments were carried out. The test was carried out at heating rates of 5 °C/min, 10 °C/min, and 15 °C/min. For comparative analysis, TG tests were also carried out on 1#. The obtained TG curve and DTG curve are shown in Figure .

(a–g) TG vs DTG curves.

As seen from Figure , the thermal decomposition trends of the seven samples are basically the same at three heating rates and can be divided into four stages. There is a certain degree of differentiation in the thermal weight loss efficiency between each stage. Sample 4# and a heating rate of 10 °C/min were taken as an example for analysis.

In the first stage, in the temperature range of about 75 to 105 °C, the mass loss is about 3.91%. The downward trend of the TG curve is relatively obvious. As can be seen from Figure d, there is a relatively steep weight loss peak in the DTG curve in the corresponding temperature range. According to the property that salicylic acid begins to sublimate at 75 °C, the thermal weight loss in this stage is due to the dehydration of the moisture contained in the excitation agent by heating and the partial sublimation of salicylic acid.

In the second stage, in the temperature range of about 105 to 175 °C, the mass loss is about 5.54%. The TG curve changes from a gentle decline to a rapid decline. The corresponding temperature range shows a gentle weight loss peak in the DTG curve. According to the physicochemical properties of ammonium oxalate starting to dehydrate at 95 °C and decompose at 150 °C, as the temperature rises, the ammonium oxalate crystal first loses crystal water. When the temperature reaches 150 °C, ammonium oxalate is decomposed to produce ammonia and oxalic acid and oxalic acid is partially decomposed. C7H6O3+O2→H2O+CO↑+CO2↑ 1

In the third stage, in the temperature range of about 175 to 250 °C, the mass loss is about 24.56%. The TG curve changes from a slow decline to a rapid decline. The corresponding temperature range shows a sharp weight loss peak in the DTG curve. This is due to the decomposition of oxalic acid to generate H2O and CO2. At this stage, theoretically, ammonium oxalate has completely sublimated and decomposed. H2C2O4→2H2O+CO↑+CO2↑ 2

In the fourth stage, in the temperature range of about 250 to 400 °C, the mass loss is about 3.34%. The TG curve is smooth and slowly declines. In the DTG curve, it is almost a straight line. It is analyzed that salicylic acid decomposes in a small amount at 300 °C, and the complete decomposition temperature requires 800 °C. At the same time, according to the endothermic peak temperature of 300 °C for the crystal form change of potassium perchlorate by DSC, it is inferred that this stage is the process of potassium perchlorate crystal form changing from orthorhombic to cubic. The crystal transformation itself caused no mass loss. The thermal weight loss formed in this stage is due to the small amount of decomposition of incompletely sublimated salicylic acid and the continued decomposition of incompletely decomposed ammonium oxalate.

According to the data in Table , compared with the original formula without adding titanium powder (1#), the apparent activation energy of the samples with titanium powder added (2#–7#) shows a nonmonotonic increase first and then decrease as the particle size decreases. It is worth noting that after adding titanium powder, the activation energy generally decreases with the reduction of particle size, and it drops to 135.15 kJ·mol–1 at 600 mesh (7#), which is lower than 127.65 kJ·mol–1 of the original formula (1#), indicating that the 600 mesh titanium powder has a promoting effect on the reaction of the system.

The reasons for the above phenomenon are related to the influence of the titanium powder particle size on its oxidation behavior. Relevant studies have shown that the reactivity of titanium powder significantly increases as the particle size decreases, and its initial oxidation temperature accordingly decreases. For the titanium-containing system in this work, the following mechanism can be proposed.

Within the range of 100 to 500 mesh, although titanium powder has relatively high reactivity, its surface TiO2 oxide film will reduce the reactivity. This oxide layer exhibits strong adhesion, leading to the adsorption of the excitation agent powder onto the titanium particles. The resulting adsorbed layer is relatively stable and requires additional energy to overcome the adsorption forces, thereby increasing the activation energy needed for the transition from an adsorbed state to a reactive activated state. Within this size range, however, the negative effect of the oxide film is gradually counterbalanced by the enhanced reactivity as the particle size decreases, leading to a downward trend in the activation energy among the titanium-added samples.

When the titanium powder size is reduced to 600 mesh, its substantially larger specific surface area further lowers the oxidation temperature, allowing for oxidation to occur earlier and more vigorously. At this point, the 600 mesh titanium powder not only serves as a fuel but also acts as a reaction initiator or catalyst, lowering the overall energy barrier of the system. Consequently, its activation energy becomes lower than that of the titanium-free baseline formulation. Therefore, compared to the baseline formulation, titanium powder with a particle size smaller than 500 mesh is not considered a suitable choice.

Analysis of the Temperature-Resistance Performance Test

The temperature-resistance test method of KP-type composite propellant is adopted. Ten grams of sample is placed in a drying oven and dried at a constant temperature of 180 °C for 48 h. Then, the temperature-resistance performance of the excitation agent is studied through the changes in the state and weight of the samples before and after a constant temperature. It reacts with oxygen in the air to form a titanium dioxide film with a black surface. The test shows that under normal use conditions, the sample has high safety performance, and no spontaneous combustion will occur.

Due to the changes in color and mass before and after drying, in order to more rigorously and truly reflect the temperature-resistance performance of the excitation agent after adding titanium powder, the temperature index of the optimized formula was calculated according to the TG experimental data combined with the empirical formula of temperature-resistance performance, as shown in eq : Ts=0.49[T1+0.6(T2−T1)] 4

In eq , T S represents the temperature index, indicating the long-term service temperature of the sample, °C. T 1 represents the temperature when the mass loss is 5%, °C. T 2 represents the temperature when the mass loss is 30%, °C.

The heating rate curve of 10 °C/min was selected, and the original data in the TG curve was taken. T 1 is 139.2 °C, 149.3 °C, and 136.6 °C, and T 2 is 258.3 °C, 238.8 °C, and 223.1 °C. We substitute them into eq to calculate and obtain the temperature indexes T S of 103.2 °C, 99.47 °C, and 92.4 °C. According to the results obtained from the calculation, it can be known that the long-term service temperature of the optimized excitation agent is above 90 °C, indicating that the designed optimized formula has a good temperature-resistance performance.

Conclusions

1)All titanium-containing formulations exhibited excellent stability under ambient (open-air) conditions, while reliably igniting under high-pressure conditions within the containment tube. These results demonstrate that the optimized formulation achieves a favorable balance between long-term storage safety and robust ignition performance-key requirements for practical engineering applications.2)Decreasing the particle size of titanium powder markedly reduces the heat transfer time from combustion of the energetic agent to the phase change of liquid CO2, thereby enhancing both the energy release rate and explosive power. Consequently, higher peak pressures are attained. Experimental data confirm that this performance enhancement arises predominantly from the titanium powder particle size effect and is insensitive to minor variations in the liquid CO2 fill volume.3)Titanium powder particle size exerts a pronounced influence on the thermal stability of the formulation. Among all tested variants, the formulation incorporating 500 mesh titanium powder delivered the most balanced overall performance exhibiting superior pressure generation characteristics, favorable reaction enthalpy, adequate thermal safety, and enhanced temperature resistance. Accordingly, it is recommended as the reference formulation of choice for liquid CO2 phase-change blasting applications.

It should be noted that the current interpretation of the nonmonotonic variation in activation energy is primarily grounded in theoretical considerations and literature support; while physically plausible, more direct experimental evidence could be obtained in future work by measuring the onset temperature of titanium oxidation via DSC oxidation peak analysis. Similarly, the strain-rate sensitivity of the energy release sheet’s fracture behavior can be independently verified through dedicated mechanical testing. These aspects will constitute key focuses of our subsequent investigations.

For prospects, this study elucidates the critical influence of titanium powder particle size on modulating the “energy–safety” trade-off in the excitation agent. The optimized formulation, using 500 mesh titanium powder, not only improves blasting work output but also preserves favorable thermal stability and safety margins. Consequently, it offers a more balanced and practically viable excitation agent for liquid CO2 phase-change blasting technology.

Safety Statement

The carbon dioxide phase-change excitation agent in this study is a high-energy material with inherent hazards. While we encountered no issues while working with this material, proper protective measures (Kevlar gloves, body armor, face shield) should still be used at all times.

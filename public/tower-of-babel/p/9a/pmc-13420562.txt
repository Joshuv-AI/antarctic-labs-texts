Seamless Transition Between Narrow- and Wide-QRS Tachycardia Revealing Double Tachycardia
Authors: HoungBeom Ahn, Ji Hyun Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTransitions between narrow- and wide-QRS tachycardia are often attributed to supraventricular tachycardia with aberrant conduction; however, alternative mechanisms should be considered.Case SummaryA 59-year-old man presented with tachycardia that alternated between narrow- and wide-QRS morphologies with progressive fusion. Electrophysiologic study demonstrated coexistence of orthodromic atrioventricular reentrant tachycardia (AVRT) using a left lateral accessory pathway and ventricular tachycardia (VT) originating from the right ventricular outflow tract. During AVRT, spontaneous VT occurred and entrained the AVRT, accelerating the tachycardia cycle length. Spontaneous termination or slowing of VT resulted in immediate continuation of AVRT via retrograde conduction over the accessory pathway. Catheter ablation of the accessory pathway followed by VT focus ablation eliminated both tachycardias.DiscussionThis case illustrates double tachycardia consisting of orthodromic AVRT and VT originating from the right ventricular outflow tract, treated with radiofrequency catheter ablation.Take-Home MessageTransitions between narrow- and wide-QRS tachycardia with fusion beats suggest double tachycardia.Key words: catheter ablation, electrophysiology, supraventricular tachycardia, ventricular tachycardia

History of presentation

A previously healthy 58-year-old man presented to the emergency department with palpitations and dyspnea that had started several hours earlier. On arrival, his heart rate was 211 beats/min, and blood pressure was 162/85 mm Hg.

Past Medical History

The patient had been healthy except for recurrent episodes of palpitations over several years and had not previously sought medical evaluation.

Investigations

Initial electrocardiogram (ECG) monitoring demonstrated seamless transitions between wide and narrow QRS tachycardia with progressive fusion (Figure 1). A 12-lead ECG obtained during tachycardia showed sustained monomorphic wide-QRS tachycardia with a left bundle branch block morphology at a rate of 199 beats/min, accompanied by fusion and capture beats (Figure 2). Another 12-lead ECG demonstrated narrow-QRS tachycardia, followed by wide-QRS tachycardia after the narrow-QRS tachycardia termination (Figure 3). Intravenous verapamil was ineffective in terminating the tachycardia, whereas intravenous amiodarone successfully suppressed the arrhythmia. During sinus rhythm, no preexcitation was observed on ECG. Transthoracic echocardiography revealed no structural heart disease.

Management

An electrophysiologic study with catheter ablation was planned. Baseline rhythm was the sinus rhythm with normal atrio-His and His-ventricle intervals. Isoproterenol infusion induced manifest preexcitation (Figure 4). During programmed stimulation, eccentric ventriculo-atrial conduction was observed, with the earliest atrial activation at the lateral mitral annulus. Double atrial extrastimulation (500/300/250 ms) induced orthodromic atrioventricular reentrant tachycardia (AVRT) using a left lateral accessory pathway (Figure 5).

Induction of Orthodromic Atrioventricular Reentrant Tachycardia (AVRT)

Double atrial extrastimulation (500/300/250 ms) induces orthodromic AVRT. The earliest atrial activation (red arrow) is recorded at the coronary sinus 1 to 2 electrodes located at the lateral mitral annulus. AVRT = atrioventricular reentrant tachycardia.

Shortly thereafter, VT occurred and accelerated the AVRT, with shortening of the tachycardia cycle length from 291 ms to 246 ms (Figure 6). Spontaneous termination of VT was followed by seamless continuation of AVRT via retrograde conduction over the bypass tract (Figure 7). Radiofrequency catheter ablation (RFCA) successfully eliminated accessory pathway conduction. After accessory pathway ablation, atrioventricular dissociation was observed during VT (Figure 8). Subsequent RFCA at the right ventricular outflow tract successfully eliminated the VT.

Outcome and Follow-Up

The patient remained asymptomatic during 1 year of follow-up after the procedure.

Discussion

Although several case reports describing double tachycardias involving SVT and VT have been published, this phenomenon remains rare.1,2 This case is particularly educational because it demonstrates multiple important electrophysiologic findings. The presence of fusion beats during transitions between SVT and VT indicates simultaneous coexistence of both tachycardias.

Notably, each tachycardia was capable of inducing the other. Tachycardia or rapid pacing during electrophysiologic study can increase intracellular calcium, potentially triggering delayed afterdepolarizations, a common mechanism of outflow-tract VT.3,4 Conversely, VT or ventricular pacing can induce orthodromic AVRT, a retrograde ventriculo-atrial conduction over an accessory pathway.5 When VT terminates or slows, AVRT may regain dominance and sustain the tachycardia.

In this case, transient VT entrained the orthodromic AVRT, demonstrating ventricular-atrial-ventricular response, a definitive finding that excludes atrial tachycardia (Figure 7).6 Both tachycardias typically have high success rates with catheter ablation, allowing definitive cure with a single procedure.

Conclusions

Double tachycardia consisting of orthodromic AVRT and VT originating at the right ventricular outflow tract was successfully treated with RFCA.

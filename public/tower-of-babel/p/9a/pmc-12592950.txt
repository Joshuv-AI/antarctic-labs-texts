Enhanced Surgical Recovery Nursing Program: A Focus on Initiation of Early Feeding and Mobilization Following Elective Abdominal Surgery
Authors: Hezil Reema Barboza, MS Moosabba, Amar Sunil Lobo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and aimsPostoperative nursing care plays a crucial role in improving outcomes and enhancing the recovery of patients undergoing abdominal surgery. Enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) protocols have been shown to significantly reduce postoperative complications and length of hospital stay by promoting early mobilization and faster recovery. The aim of the study was to evaluate the effects of early postoperative feeding and mobilization on the length of postoperative hospital stay and complications among patients.Patients and methodsA quantitative approach with a quasi-experimental pretest-posttest control group design was used in the study. The lower extremity exercises and oral feeding were initiated as early as possible, as tolerated by the patient, in the experimental group. Passage of the first flatus and return of bowel function are considered to initiate the feed following surgery among patients in the treatment-as-usual group.ResultsIn the present study, 53.6% of the study participants in the experimental group and 29.6% in the treatment-as-usual group were nil per oral (NPO) for 1 hour in the postoperative phase. By 24 hours postoperatively, most patients had initiated oral feeding (86% in the experimental and 77.5% in the treatment-as-usual groups). Delayed feeding was uncommon in the experimental group, with only a few patients remaining NPO for 2–5 days (14%). In contrast, a higher proportion in the treatment-as-usual group remained NPO beyond 2 days (16.9%), with some as late as 9 days (1.4%). Patients in the experimental group had a significantly shorter length of stay in the recovery unit and also in the postoperative ward compared to those who received standard treatment. Immediate postoperative complications occurred in 8.4% of the experimental group and 26.6% of the treatment-as-usual group, which were assessed using a checklist prepared by the investigators.ConclusionImplementation of evidence-based nursing interventions during the postoperative phase achieves significant improvements in patient outcomes. This underscores the importance of integrating current research and best practices into routine patient care to enhance recovery and also reduce the burden on the health care system.How to cite this articleBarboza HR, Moosabba MS, Lobo AS. Introduction

Abdominal surgeries are surgical interventions that involve the opening of the abdominal cavity for diagnostic and therapeutic purposes. Major surgical procedures involve greater complexity and carry a higher risk of potential complications. These surgeries are performed under general anesthesia, and the recovery takes several days to weeks.1

The common approach among patients undergoing gastrointestinal surgery has been to wait until the patient's digestive system shows signs of bowel movements before initiating enteral feeding. The major concern is that early enteral nutrition may either lead to paralytic ileus, anastomotic leakage/dehiscence, or aspiration pneumonia.2 Early feeding improves tissue healing and prevents postoperative infections, thus reducing the length of stay in the hospital. Patients who received early feeding showed a few infections and a reduced length of hospital stay.3

A study in 2010 reported that around 51.4 million surgical procedures were performed in the United States, wherein less than 0.1% of postoperative complications were reported among the patients.4 Moreover, the consequences varied according to the type of surgical intervention and risk factors involved among the patients. A systematic review reported that 14.4% of patients following a surgical procedure experienced an adverse postoperative event that prolonged their stay in the hospital.4

Postoperative mobilization is a key factor in recovery after surgery that involves getting patients out of bed and moving around. It includes activities such as transferring from a bed to a chair, sitting, standing, and walking. The benefits of early mobilization include reducing the risk of complications, shortening the hospital stays, and improving recovery.5,6

Hence, this study was conducted to evaluate the effects of early postoperative feeding and mobilization on reducing the length of postoperative hospital stay and complications among patients undergoing elective abdominal surgery.

Patients and Methods

A quantitative approach with a quasi-experimental pretest-posttest control group design was used to evaluate early postoperative feeding and mobilization to reduce the length of postoperative hospital stay and postoperative complications among patients in Mangaluru, South India. The study was conducted from January 2021 to December 2023.

The independent sample t-test formula was used to calculate the sample size. The sample size was calculated based on the effect size of 0.5 and 5% level of significance with a power of 80%.

Effect size: d = 0.5, power: 1–β = 0.8, and significance level: α = 0.05.

Based on the analysis, the estimated sample size for each group was 64, and the total sample size was 128. With a 10% attrition rate, the sample size of 142 was considered for this study.

A purposive sampling technique was used, and study subjects (n = 142) were assigned first to the experimental group and then to the treatment-as-usual group. Age, gender, and body mass index (BMI) were matched to make the groups homogeneous.

Inclusion Criteria

The patients aged between 20 and 60 years and undergoing elective laparotomy were selected as study participants. The patients undergoing surgeries on organs such as the stomach, colon, pancreas, gall bladder, and spleen were included in this study.

Exclusion Criteria

Patients with impaired consciousness and those who had undergone any major surgery in the past were excluded from the study. The patients who had undergone caesarean section, aortic femoral bypass, and abdominal hysterectomy were not included in the study.

Based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria, study participants were first recruited into the experimental group, where data collection occurred, and then assigned to the treatment-as-usual group. During the recruitment of participants, age, gender, and BMI were matched to make the groups homogenous in nature.

Early Oral Feeding

Oral feeding was initiated as early as possible in the experimental group, as recommended by the surgeon. In the treatment-as-usual group, feeding was delayed until the return of bowel function and passage of flatus. Across the groups, dietary approach followed the same pattern, beginning with sips of water, followed by clear fluids, soft diet, and ultimately transitioning to a regular diet.

Early Mobilization

Early mobilization was initiated, including incremental activities performed by the patient in the presence of the investigator. Mobilization was started within 24 hours of surgery and was documented in the daily activity logbook. Leg exercises were performed in the morning and evening postoperatively for a 10-minute duration.

In the present study, investigators assessed the duration of postoperative hospital stay and immediate complications following abdominal surgery. The length of postoperative hospital stay was considered as the number of hours a patient stays in the postoperative unit/surgical intensive care unit (SICU) until discharge from the hospital. Postoperative complications are the consequences that occur in the postoperative phase, which may occur anytime from the immediate recovery period until discharge of the patient from the hospital. These postoperative complications were assessed using a checklist developed by the investigator. Validation of the tool and intervention was done by the subject experts in nursing, surgery, and anesthesia.

The institution's Scientific Review Committee and Ethics Committee reviewed and approved the study (reference number YEC-l/2019/221). The study was prospectively registered under Clinical Trial Registry-India (CTRI/2021/04/032621). Permission for data collection was obtained from the administrative authority of the hospital, and informed consent was taken from the participants.

The clinical characteristics of the study participants were analyzed by frequency and percentage. Chi-square/Fisher's exact test was used to find the homogeneity between the groups for the clinical characteristics. Mann–Whitney's U test was computed to compare the length of postoperative hospital stay (days) in the experimental and treatment-as-usual groups.

Results

All the study participants’ data were included in the analysis. In the present study, the average age of participants was similar in both groups, with the experimental group aged 45 years and the treatment-as-usual group aged 44 years. A similar gender distribution was observed in both study groups, with males representing the majority in both the experimental group (60.6%) and the treatment-as-usual group (59.2%). Further details on the participants’ clinical characteristics in each group are presented in Table 1. There are no significant differences between the study groups (p > 0.05), which indicates that the groups are homogenous for the clinical characteristics studied.

In the experimental group, 53.6% of patients, compared with 29.6% in the treatment-as-usual group, remained nil per oral (NPO) for 1 hour after surgery. By the end of 24 hours, the majority of patients had initiated oral feeding, with 86% in the experimental group and 77.5% in the treatment-as-usual group no longer remaining NPO. In the experimental group, only a small proportion experienced delayed initiation: 5.6% remained NPO for 2 days, 2.8% for 3 days, 1.4% for 4 days, and 4.2% for 5 days. In contrast, in the treatment-as-usual group, 14.1% remained NPO for 2 days, 2.8% for 5 days, and 1.4% as late as 9 days postoperatively (Fig. 1).

Discussion

In both the experimental and treatment-as-usual groups, males constituted the majority of the participants. Specifically, 60.6% of participants in the experimental group and 59.2% in the treatment-as-usual group were male. This indicates a similar gender distribution across both study groups. Previous studies also reported that among the patients who underwent abdominal surgery, the majority were males.7,8 In addition, they concluded that there were no gender differences with regard to perioperative care, number of complications, hospital length of stay, or discharge.

Maintaining a healthy weight is important for good postoperative outcomes. Patients with obesity were most likely to have comorbid conditions and were at risk for surgical and anesthetic complications.9,10 The majority of participants in both the experimental (60.6%) and treatment-as-usual (57.7%) groups in this study had a BMI within the normal range. Previous studies reported that the patients who underwent abdominal surgery had a normal BMI.9,10 This could be the factor that contributed to the early wound healing and recovery of the patients.

In the present study, the patients who underwent abdominal surgery (n = 142) were selected, and most of them had undergone colon repair in the experimental group and cholecystectomy in the treatment-as-usual group. Similarly, previous research reported that cholecystectomy and colon repair were the main surgeries among the participants in their studies.7–11

The increasing prevalence of multimorbidity worldwide presents a significant challenge in terms of surgical outcomes, as it increases the likelihood of patients experiencing immediate complications following surgery. It is important to assess patients before surgery to identify their comorbid status, which helps in initiating actions toward the prevention of postoperative complications.12,13 In the present study, most of the study participants had no co-morbidity, except for 11 (15.5%) in the experimental group and 14 (19.7%) in the treatment-as-usual group had cancer as a co-morbidity. Previous studies reported comparable findings with regard to co-morbidity.1,14,15

The findings of the present study indicate a statistically significant reduction in the duration of postoperative hospitalization (measured in days) for participants in the experimental group when compared to those receiving the standard treatment. A study reported that the enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) program showed a shorter hospital stay among patients undergoing colorectal surgery.16 Another study reported that patients who started early oral feeding after colorectal surgery had shorter hospital stays compared to those following the traditional delayed oral feeding approach. In line with the evidence, it is important that the ERAS health care team update the perioperative approach for patients undergoing abdominal surgery to align with current evidence-based practices.17

European Society for Clinical Nutrition and Metabolism (ESPEN) organization guidelines on clinical nutrition in surgical patients recommend initiation of oral feeding immediately after surgery.2 Previous studies have shown that drinking liquids 2 hours before surgery does not increase gastric volume.18–20 On the contrary, early oral feeding promotes postoperative recovery among patients. In the present study, the participants in the experimental group reported very few complications compared to the treatment-as-usual group. The participants in this study did not have multiple comorbidities, which can be a major contributing factor explaining fewer postoperative complications. Ripollés-Melchor et al., reported that 42.2% of patients presented with postoperative complications, and 27.2% developed moderate to severe complications following colorectal surgery.21 A previous study reported that ERAS protocols in colorectal surgery are effective in reducing postoperative complications and hospital stays.16 A South Indian study reported that early postoperative mobilization is safe and results in significant improvement in patient condition.22 Furthermore, a review reported that nurse-led interventions in the postoperative phase show significant potential for improving patient outcomes and are closely linked to the progress of nursing quality in the perioperative nursing field.23 Enhanced recovery after surgery is a holistic approach to patient care that has proven successful when various components are implemented across all aspects of perioperative care, demonstrating improvement in patient outcomes and optimizing healthcare delivery.23 The recommendations made based on the present study include preoperative education materials, such as pamphlets, which can be made available in surgical units. Early postoperative feeding and mobilization can be encouraged, which enhances the recovery.

The study also had a few limitations; it was a single-center study limited to a specific geographical area. Patient monitoring was done at a specific time, and follow-up was not included to identify the late postoperative complications, re-admission, and mortality rates.

Early recovery programs in emergency surgery are rare, and further studies are needed to evaluate the effectiveness of evidence in developing ERAS approaches for the needs of patients undergoing emergency surgeries. This study highlights the need for the development of pathways to prevent symptoms such as nausea and vomiting. The effectiveness and safety of enhanced recovery programs in the field of obstetrics to improve the health status of mothers are recommended; this may even have an influence on the outcome of newborns.

The role of nurses working in surgical and postoperative units is crucial in identifying health issues of patients following abdominal surgery. Nurses, as a part of the ERAS team in implementing ERAS protocols, help in improving the recovery of patients and thus reduce the burden on healthcare settings.

Conclusion

The implementation of evidence-based ERAS early feeding and mobilization interventions reduced the length of stay in the postoperative ward and the number of postoperative complications.

Rare FUS::FEV–Rearranged Adamantinoma-Like Ewing Sarcoma Arising in the Submandibular Gland
Authors: Tomilola Agboola, Daniel L. Miller
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAdamantimoma-Like Ewing Sarcoma (ALES) is a rare variant of Ewing sarcoma that poses significant diagnostic challenges in the head and neck due to morphologic overlap with basaloid carcinomas and neuroendocrine neoplasms.Case PresentationWe report a case of a middle-aged man with a left submandibular gland mass showing classic histologic features of ALES, including basaloid nests with peripheral palisading, rosette formation, and high mitotic activity.ResultsImmunohistochemistry showed co-expression of p63/p40, CD99, and cytokeratins, with patchy synaptophysin positivity and an elevated Ki-67 proliferation index. FISH for EWSR1 was negative, but next-generation sequencing identified a rare FUS::FEV fusion, previously reported only three times in ALES and never in the submandibular gland.ConclusionThis case highlights the importance of extended molecular testing when EWSR1 FISH is negative; as alternate FET::ETS fusions may underlie the diagnosis.

Images and Text Body

Adamantinoma-Like Ewing Sarcoma (ALES) is a rare variant of Ewing sarcoma first described in 1999 [1]. It is defined by epithelial differentiation in addition to features of small round cell sarcoma. Classic histology shows predominantly monomorphic basaloid round-to-ovoid cells in cohesive sheets, nests or lobules set within fibromyxoid or hyalinized stroma [2]. Some show peripheral nuclear palisading, keratinization or rosette formation [3, 4]. Accurate classification of ALESs arising in the head and neck can be challenging, as the tumor may mimic basaloid carcinomas or neuroendocrine neoplasms, making immunohistochemistry and molecular studies important for diagnosis [4]. Most known cases harbor EWSR1 rearrangements [2].

We present a case of a middle-aged man who developed upper respiratory symptoms, followed by a palpable mass on the left side of the neck. CT imaging revealed a 2.7-cm mass arising in the left submandibular gland. The tumor is composed of uniform small round to ovoid basaloid cells arranged in solid nests, sheets, and trabeculae, within by dense fibrotic stroma. The tumor cells show scant cytoplasm, round nuclei with finely dispersed chromatin, and inconspicuous nucleoli (Fig. 2). Peripheral palisading is evident in several nests, with occasional rosette formation, and focal necrosis . Mitotic figures are readily identified. In some areas, the tumor colonizes residual salivary ducts. No overt squamous differentiation or keratinization is seen. Perineural invasion is absent.Fig. 2Representative photomicrographs at 200× and 400× magnification (hematoxylin and eosin) demonstrating nuclear detail, scant cytoplasm, peripheral palisading, focal necrosis, and poorly formed rosettes

By immunohistochemistry (Fig. 3), the tumor cells are positive for p40 (Fig. 3, top left) and membranous CD99 (Fig. 3, top right). The Cam5.2 and synaptophysin are patchy positive (Fig. 3, bottom left, and right respectively). The Ki-67 proliferation index is elevated at approximately 30–40% (not shown). Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) for EWSR1 gene rearrangement was negative. Due to the morphology and immunoprofile, a strong suspicion for ALES remained and the tumor was sent for next-generation sequencing, which detected a FUS::FEV fusion. This genetic finding is extremely rare but has been documented thrice in ALES [2, 5], but never in the submandibular gland. Thus, our case adds to this emerging pattern.

Importantly, this case emphasizes the lesson that a negative EWSR1 FISH does not exclude ALES. Failure to identify EWSR1 rearrangement should be recognized as a potential diagnostic pitfall. One should pursue extended molecular testing in the appropriate histopathologic setting (small round blue cell neoplasm in the neck that co-expresses p40, CD99 and pan-cytokeratin), to confirm or rule out the presence of an alternate FET rearrangement or an alternate FET::ETS fusion.

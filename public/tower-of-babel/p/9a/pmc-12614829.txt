Soy isoflavone yeast fermented extract inhibits contraction of rat ileal smooth muscle via opening of K+ channels and activation of intracellular cyclic adenosine monophosphate- and cyclic guanosine monophosphate-related pathways
Authors: Shingo YAMAZAKI, Julia KINUYAMA, Hisako KANEDA, Noriyasu SASAKI, Takeharu KANEDA
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study investigated the effects of soy isoflavone yeast fermented extract (soyF) and soy isoflavone yeast unfermented extract (soyN) on rat ileal smooth muscle contraction. SoyF and soyN inhibited carbachol (CCh)- or KCl-induced contraction in a concentration-dependent manner; however, these effects were stronger for CCh-induced contraction than that for KCl, and the relaxation effect was stronger for soyF than for soyN. SoyF-induced relaxation was attenuated by 4-aminopyridine (4-AP), a Kv channel inhibitor, and iberiotoxin (IbTX), a calcium-activated potassium channel (BK channel) inhibitor. This relaxation was also decreased by 9-(tetrahydrofuran-2-yl)-9h-purin-6-amine (SQ22536) and 1H-[1, 2, 4]oxadiazolo[4,3-a] quinoxalin-1-one (ODQ). Moreover, simultaneous treatment involving SQ22536 with 4-AP or IbTX further restored soyF-induced inhibition compared with each single treatment, whereas simultaneous treatment with ODQ and 4-AP or IbTX did not restore soyF-induced inhibition. SoyF increased cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) and cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP) levels in ileal strips. In β-escin-permeabilized muscle, CCh, guanosine 5’-O(3-thiotriphosphate) (GTPγS), and phorbol-12,13-dibutyrate (PDBu)-enhanced Ca2+-induced contraction, but soyF suppressed this Ca2+ sensitization. In summary, yeast-fermented treatment increased soy isoflavone-induced inhibition of ileal smooth muscle contraction. These data suggest that soyF-induced relaxation was due to increasing cAMP and cGMP related to activating adenylate cyclase and guanylate cyclase. Moreover, it was demonstrated that soyF-induced relaxation is due to cell membrane hyperpolarization and inhibition of contractile proteins. Cell membrane hyperpolarization is probably mediated by the activation of Kv and BK channels via cGMP-related signals. Furthermore, the inhibitory effect on the contractile protein is due to Ca2+ desensitization, probably through suppression of the Rho kinase/myosin phosphatase targeting subunit (MYPT) and/or the PKC/CPI-17 pathway.

INTRODUCTION

Normal intestinal motility is essential for the movement of nutrients from one site in the intestinal tract to the next. Intestinal motility is generated by the contraction and relaxation of circular and longitudinal smooth muscles, and its control involves many factors, including the autonomic nervous system, the myenteric plexus, and interstitial cells of Cajal [6]. Slower passage of luminal contents results in abdominal bloating and vomiting, whereas faster passage results in diarrhea. In both conditions, normal digestion and absorption of nutrients and excretion of waste products are impaired, so it is important to maintain a constant state of intestinal motility.

In recent years, the increasing health awareness of people and the development of food functionality research have drawn attention to the usefulness of health foods. In Japan, it has been thought that the intake of soy foods such as natto, miso, and tofu might promote intestinal regulation and bowel movements [3].

Soy isoflavones are a type of flavonoid found in soybeans. It has been reported that soy isoflavones possess anti-osteoporosis [37], anti-tumor [21], and anti-obesity [10] effects.

Contents of soyF and soyN

Isoflavones such as daidzein and genistein have been reported to have beneficial effects on gastrointestinal function, including anti-inflammatory activity [40], promotion of chloride ion secretion [2], and improvement of epithelial barrier function [36]. However, few reports have investigated the effects of soy isoflavones, especially hydroxylated isoflavones, on intestinal smooth muscle contraction.

Therefore, the present study investigated the effects of soyF on intact and β-escin-permeabilized ileal smooth muscle contraction, and on cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) and cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP) contents in ileal tissue.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Animals

Male Wistar rats (250–300 g body weight; Tokyo Laboratory Animals, Tokyo, Japan) were anesthetized using secobarbital sodium (50 mg/kg i.p.) and medetomidine (100 µg/kg i.p.) and euthanized by exsanguination. This study was conducted in accordance with the Nippon Veterinary and Life Science University’s Guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (Approval No. 2021K-75).

Measurement of muscle tension

The ileum was removed from each animal. The longitudinal muscle layer was stripped from the ileum circular muscle, as described previously [17]. The longitudinal muscles were tied to a muscle transducer and equilibrated in a 10-mL organ bath filled with oxygenated physiological saline solution (PSS) at 37°C for 30 min [17]. The strips were contracted by KCl (65 mM) or carbachol (CCh, 0.3 µM) and, after KCl or CCh-induced contraction reached a steady state, treated with soy isoflavone yeast fermented product (soyF) (3–300 µg/mL) for 5–10 min at each concentration to obtain cumulative concentration-response curves. The tension developed was expressed as a percentage, by assigning the values at rest in normal PSS to be 0% and those at a steady level of contraction in 0.3 µM CCh or 65 mM KCl to be 100%.

In the experiments, muscle strips were incubated with 1H-[1, 2, 4]oxadiazolo[4,3-a]quinoxalin-1-one (ODQ, 30 µM), 9-(tetrahydrofuran-2-yl)-9h-purin-6-amine (SQ22536, 100 µM), tetraethylammonium (TEA, 3 mM), iberiotoxin (IbTX, 10 nM), glibenclamide (10 µM), apamin (300 nM), or 4-aminopyridine (4-AP, 1 mM) for 15–30 min before the addition of CCh.

Muscle tension was also measured using β-escin-permeabilized smooth muscle, as reported previously [44]. The muscle strips were held horizontally in the 1-mL bath filled with relaxing solution containing β-escin (80 μM, 30 min; Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA). Ca2+ (pCa 6.0) solution was prepared using Ca2+-EGTA buffer with 2 mM EGTA and 1.0 mM CaCl2. A 30 µM Ca2+ (pCa 4.5) solution was prepared using Ca2+-EGTA buffer composed of 2 mM EGTA and 1.96 mM CaCl2. The solutions were adjusted to pH 6.8 at 24°C with KOH (0.1 N). The required Ca2+-concentrations were calculated assuming the apparent binding constant of Ca2+-EGTA complex to be 10−6 at pH 6.8 [38].

The effect of soyF was evaluated just before washout.

Muscle tension was also measured using Triton X-100 permeabilized smooth muscle according to Sparrow et al [43]. The muscle strips were incubated in 20 mM imidazole (pH 7.4), 5 mM EGTA, 50 mM KC1, and 1.50 mM sucrose for 2 hr at 4°C, then 1% (v/v) Triton X-100 and dithioerythritol (DTE) 0.5 mM were added for a further 4 hr at 4°C. After rinsing for 15 min in this solution without Triton X-l 00, they were stored in 20 mM imidazole, 4 mM EGTA, 10 mM MgCl2, 7.5 mM ATP, 1 mM NaN3, and 0.5 mM DTE (PH 6.7) with 50% glycerol at −20°C for up to 4 days.

Assays of cAMP and cGMP levels

As described in our previous report, enzyme immunoassays were used to measure cAMP and cGMP levels in the ileal muscle strips [19]. In brief, muscle strips were incubated with CCh, CCh + soyF, CCh + SQ22536 or ODQ + soyF100, or CCh + SQ22536 or ODQ+ soyF300 in an organ bath. Muscle strips were treated with CCh (0.3 µM) for 10 min, SQ22536 (100 µM) or ODQ (30 µM) for 30 min, followed by soyF (soyF100 or soyF300) for 10 min. The cAMP and cGMP levels were measured using an enzyme immunoassay kit (Cayman Chemical, Ann Arbor, MI, USA) and are expressed as pmol/g of wet weight.

Chemicals and reagents

Soy isoflavone yeast fermented extract (soyF) and soy isoflavone yeast unfermented extract (soyN) were provided by Toyo Hakko. Chemicals used included the following: carbamylcholine chloride (carbachol, CCh), glybenclamide, 4-AP, sodium nitroprusside, guanosine 5′-O(3-thiotriphosphate) (GTPγS), phorbol-12,13-dibutyrate (PDBu) (Sigma-Aldrich), IbTX, SQ22536, ODQ (BIOMOL Research Laboratories, Carlsbad, CA, USA), TEA (Tokyo Chemical Industry, Tokyo, Japan), fasudil hydrochloride (LC Laboratories, Woburn, MA, USA), and apamin (Peptide Institute, Osaka, Japan).

Statistical analysis

All values are expressed as means ± standard error of the mean (SEM), and statistical analysis was performed using Student’s t-test and two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), followed by Bonferroni’s post hoc test. A P value of <0.05 was taken as significant. Calculations and statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9 software (GraphPad, San Diego, CA, USA) and Excel 2021 for Windows (Microsoft, Redmond, WA, USA).

When the CCh- or KCl-induced contraction reached a steady state, soyF or soyN was added cumulatively (3−300 µg/mL). DISCUSSION

The present study suggests that soyF inhibited CCh- and KCl-induced contractions of rat ileal smooth muscle in a concentration-dependent manner, involving activation of cAMP and cGMP signaling, opening of K+ channels, or decreased Ca2+ sensitivity of contractile proteins.

In smooth muscle, high K+ induces membrane depolarization, which subsequently opens voltage-gated Ca2+ channels and induces Ca2+ release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum, resulting in an increase in intracellular Ca2+ concentrations. Ca2+ forms a complex with calmodulin and activates myosin light chain kinase (MLCK). MLCK phosphorylates the 20 KDa myosin light chain (MLC20), which activates myosin ATPase and causes actin to slide over myosin, resulting in contraction [14, 20]. In this study, soyF inhibited KCl-induced contractions. In addition, soyF slightly inhibited Ca2+-induced contractions in β-escin-permeabilized muscle, but not in triton X-100-treated skinned fibers. Since the duration of contractions decreased over time in β-escin-permeabilized muscle, it is difficult to quantify precisely. However, soyF did not affect Ca2+-induced contractions in triton X-100-treated muscles. Therefore, it is considered that soyF did not inhibit Ca2+-induced contractions in skinned fibers. These results suggest that the relaxation effect of soyF is partially mediated by the inhibition of voltage-dependent calcium channels, without affecting the Ca2+-calmodulin-MLCK pathway.

In contrast, receptor agonist-induced contraction activates the Ca2+-calmodulin-MLCK pathway, as well as the protein kinase C (PKC) and RhoA-ROCK pathways [45]. In the PKC pathway, diacylglycerol produced by receptor stimulation activates protein kinase C (PKC), which in turn phosphorylates CPI-17. Phosphorylated CPI-17 inhibits myosin light chain phosphatase (MLCP), resulting in increased Ca2+ sensitivity [24, 34]. In the RhoA-ROCK pathway, Rho, a low molecular weight GTP-binding protein, is activated by receptor stimulation and activates Rho kinase, an effector molecule. The activated Rho kinase phosphorylates the myosin-binding subunit (MYPT1) of MLCP, thereby suppressing MLCP activity and increasing the Ca2+ sensitivity of contractile proteins [23, 25]. In intestinal smooth muscle, cAMP- or cGMP-related drugs more potently inhibit receptor agonist-induced contractions than KCl-induced contractions [18, 26]. In the present study, soyF inhibited CCh-induced contractions more than KCl-induced contractions. These results suggest that the cAMP and cGMP signaling pathways may be involved in soyF relaxation.

In the present study, pretreatment with SQ22536 and ODQ significantly blocked the soyF-induced relaxation of CCh-induced contractions in intact ileal muscle. In addition, soyF increased cAMP and cGMP levels in a concentration-dependent manner, which were significantly reduced by pretreatment with SQ22536 or ODQ, respectively. These results suggest that the cAMP and cGMP pathways are involved in the contractile inhibition of soyF.

In the present study, soyF inhibited ileal smooth muscle contractions more potently than soyN. SoyF contains more aglycones and hydroxylated isoflavones than soyN. Genistein, an aglycone, has been reported to inhibit contractions in rat gastric and duodenal smooth muscles [46], and daidzein, also an aglycone, has been reported to inhibit contractions in mouse and rat jejunal smooth muscle [7]. Furthermore, both daidzin and daidzein inhibited contractions in rat vascular smooth muscle, but the inhibitory effect of daidzein was stronger than that of daidzin [8]. These reports suggest that the difference in the contraction-inhibitory potency of unfermented and fermented products may be related in part to the type and amounts of contained constituents. In contrast, polyphenols contained in hibiscus have been reported to synergistically control lipogenesis compared with their individual effects [13]. Further studies are needed to clarify whether the synergistic effects of the constituents in the unfermented and fermented products are related to the differences in potency.

In the present study, pretreatment with the voltage-dependent K+ channel inhibitor 4-AP and the BK channel inhibitor IbTX significantly inhibited soyF-induced relaxation of CCh-induced contraction in intact ileal muscle. It has been reported that genistein inhibited contraction via K+ channels in rabbit duodenal smooth muscle [39]. Moreover, it has also been reported that daidzein inhibited contraction via Ca2+-sensitive K+ channels in rat mesenteric arteries [33]. These results suggest that the relaxation effect of soyF involves in part the opening of K+ channels. Furthermore, simultaneous treatment with SQ22536 and 4-AP or IbTX further restored the soyF-induced contractile inhibition compared with each single treatment. In addition, simultaneous treatment with ODQ and 4-AP or IbTX did not restore the systolic inhibition of soyF compared with the respective single treatments. In smooth muscle, it has been reported that voltage-gated K+ and BK channels open in a cGMP-dependent manner [1, 30]. These results suggest that soyF opens voltage-gated K+ and BK channels in a cGMP-dependent manner and exhibit muscle relaxation.

In vivo, cAMP is synthesized from adenylate cyclase [42], and cGMP is synthesized from guanylate cyclase [9]. cAMP activates protein kinase A (PKA), and cGMP activates protein kinase G (PKG) [41]. It has been reported that cyclic nucleotides inhibit smooth muscle contraction, and these mechanisms involve hyperpolarization of smooth muscle cell membrane potentials and decreased Ca2+ sensitivity of contractile proteins [31, 32]. Previous studies have shown that cAMP inhibits MYPT1 phosphorylation by suppressing upstream Rho activation and downstream PKC signaling [11], whereas cGMP inhibits the ROCK pathway via RhoA phosphorylation and RhoGAP activation [22] and CPI-17 dephosphorylation [4]. It has also been reported that cAMP suppressed PKC-induced Ca2+ sensitization in rabbit permeabilized tracheal smooth muscle [11], and the Rho kinase inhibitor Y-27632 did not enhance cAMP relaxation in human permeabilized bladder smooth muscle [12]. It has also been shown that cGMP inhibited PDBu-induced Ca2+ sensitization in permeabilized smooth muscle [4]. In the present study, soyF significantly inhibited CCh-, GTPγS-, and PDBu-induced Ca2+ sensitization. These results suggest that soyF suppressed Ca2+ sensitization via cAMP- and cGMP-related pathways.

Fasudil, an inhibitor of Rho kinase, did not affect the soyF-evoked suppression of CCh-induced Ca2+ sensitization, but slightly inhibited GTPγS-induced Ca2+ sensitization. These data indicate that soyF-evoked suppression of Ca2+ sensitization is related to inhibition of the RhoA-ROCK pathway. Various receptors, including muscarinic receptor are expressed in the ileal smooth muscle, and GTPγS activates these receptors and induces Ca2+ sensitization. Additional inhibition of fasudil may imply that GTPγS-induced Ca2+ sensitization more correlated with RhoA-ROCK pathway than that of CCh. Further experiments are needed to clarify whether the soyF-induced Ca2+ desensitization is related to direct inhibition of the RhoA-ROCK pathway or indirect inhibition of the RhoA-ROCK pathway via the cAMP/cGMP pathway.

In the present study, soyF partially inhibited PDBu-induced Ca2+ sensitization compared with CCh-evoked Ca2+ sensitization. These data suggest that soyF suppresses part of the PKC-CPI-17-P-PPase inhibitory pathway.

Intestinal diseases include inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), a disease that causes inflammation of the gastrointestinal tract mucosa [5], and irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), in which abnormal bowel movements such as diarrhea and constipation, abdominal pain, and bloating occur in the absence of organic abnormalities in the gastrointestinal tract [27]. The number of IBD and IBS patients is increasing worldwide [27, 35].

Polyphenols contained in plant foods have been reported to alleviate IBD symptoms by acting synergistically with intestinal bacteria [29]. Furthermore, soy isoflavone intake has been reported to improve the quality of life and severity scores of IBS patients [15, 16]. The present study further suggests that soy isoflavones may be useful in the treatment of IBS and IBD.

In conclusion, it was indicated that soy isoflavone inhibited contraction of rat ileal smooth muscle, and this relaxant effect was stronger for fermented than for unfermented products. The present study suggests that soyF-induced relaxation was due to increasing cAMP and cGMP pathways related to activating adenylate cyclase and guanylate cyclase. Furthermore, it is shown that soyF-induced relaxation may be related to cell membrane hyperpolarization and inhibition of contractile proteins. Cell membrane hyperpolarization probably is mediated by opening Kv channels and BK channels via cGMP-related signal. Moreover, the inhibitory effect on the contractile protein is due to Ca2+ desensitization, probably through suppression of the Rho kinase/MYPT pathway and/or the PKC/CPI-17 pathway.

Discovery of XNW5004 as a novel EZH2 inhibitor that enhances anti-tumor immunity and synergizes with PD-1 blockade immunotherapy in lung adenocarcinoma
Authors: Zhihuan Lin, Wenjuan Ma, Haishuang Sun, Xiaoxian Sima, Hong Liu, Li Zhang, Jianhua Zhan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSynergistic strategies are urgently needed to enhance the efficacy of immunotherapy in lung cancer. Recent evidence highlights Enhancer of zeste homolog 2 (EZH2) as a pivotal epigenetic regulator that fosters an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, thereby driving immunotherapy resistance. We hypothesized that EZH2 pharmacological inhibition could increase immunotherapy susceptibility. This study aimed to investigate the potential of a novel EZH2 inhibitor, XNW5004, to sensitize lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) to programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) blockade.MethodsIn vitro, colony formation and apoptosis assays assessed direct cytotoxicity of XNW5004 on tumor cells at 0–12 µM. A co-culture system of tumor cells and peripheral blood mononuclear cells evaluated immune-mediated killing. In vivo, immunodeficient nude mice and immunocompetent C57BL/6 mice were randomly assigned to the control, XNW5004, anti-PD1, and combination groups to assess the tumor suppressive effect. Underlying mechanisms were explored through RNA sequencing alongside comprehensive cellular and molecular assays.ResultsIn vitro, pre-treating tumor cells with 1.5 µM XNW5004 enhanced their sensitivity to immune cell attack, resulting in fewer residual cells and increased apoptosis, an effect further potentiated by PD-1 blockade. In vivo, XNW5004 suppressed tumor growth in immunocompetent C57BL/6 mice but showed minimal effect in immunodeficient nude mice. Mechanistically, XNW5004 stimulated chemokine-mediated recruitment of dendritic cells and T cells into tumor sites. Additionally, it upregulated the antigen presentation molecule major histocompatibility complex class I (MHC-I), while simultaneously augmenting the expression of co-signaling molecules programmed death ligand 1(PD-L1) and intercellular adhesion molecule-1 (ICAM-1). These alterations contributed to the augmented cytotoxic activity of both CD8+ T cells and natural killer cells, as evidenced by increased interferon-γ and granzyme B. The STING-TBK1-NF-κB axis functions as a pivotal regulatory signaling pathway driving these phenotype alterations.ConclusionsThe EZH2 inhibitor XNW5004 enhances anti-tumor immunity and synergistically interacts with PD-1 blockade immunotherapy in LUAD, establishing this combinatorial approach as a promising therapeutic strategy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12967-026-08619-8.Keywords: XNW5004, EZH2 inhibitor, Lung adenocarcinoma, Anti-tumor immunity, Immunotherapy

Introduction

Immunotherapy, predominantly represented by programmed cell death protein 1/programmed death ligand 1 (PD-1/PD-L1) immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) therapy, has revolutionized lung cancer treatment [1–3]. However, the limited clinical response remains a significant challenge.

Enhancer of zeste homolog 2 (EZH2) is a key component of Polycomb repressive complex 2 [4]. As a prominent epigenetic regulator, it plays pivotal roles in maintaining chromatin compaction and mediating gene silencing by catalyzing the trimethylation of histone H3 at lysine 27 (H3K27me3) [5]. Accumulating evidence indicates the essential role of EZH2 in fostering the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, which mediates cancer development and immunotherapy resistance [6–8]. EZH2 pharmacological inhibition may reshape the tumor immune microenvironment and augment the efficacy of ICI therapy [9, 10].

Currently, first-generation EZH2 inhibitors have encountered clinical setbacks, primarily due to safety concerns, as observed with tazemetostat [11], or due to limited efficacy, as seen with GSK126 [12]. XNW5004, a novel next-generation EZH2 inhibitor, demonstrated a favorable pharmacokinetic profile, excellent tolerability and encouraging anti-tumor efficacy in initial clinical studies involving lymphoma and prostate cancer cases [13, 14]. However, the application of XNW5004 in solid tumors such as lung cancer remains in its infancy [15]. In the present study, we investigated the potential therapeutic effect and underlying mechanisms of XNW5004 in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD). The results indicated that XNW5004 facilitated an immunostimulatory tumor microenvironment, thereby sensitizing tumors to PD-1 blockade immunotherapy.

Methods

Cell lines and reagents

Human A549 (RRID: CVCL_0023), H1975 (RRID: CVCL_1511), HCC827 (RRID: CVCL_2063), THP-1 (RRID: CVCL_0006), and murine LLC (RRID: CVCL_4358) cell lines were procured from American Type Culture Collection. Cell line cultures were free of mycoplasma contamination. The cells were cultured in 25-cm2 tissue culture flasks containing Roswell Park Memorial Institute (RPMI) 1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and incubated at 37 °C in a humidified 5% CO2 atmosphere. Human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated from pooled healthy blood donors via lymphoprep density gradient centrifugation and cultured in X-VIVO-15 media (Lonza, USA) containing 100 U/ml interleukin-2 in the presence of CD3/CD28 beads (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). CD3-positive lymphocytes were sorted from the PBMCs by CD3 MicroBeads (Miltenyi Biotec, Germany) following the manufacturer’s protocol. XNW5004 was provided by Jiangsu Evopoint Bioscience (Suzhou Industrial Park, China); the stock solution of XNW5004 was dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), and the working solution was configured with culture medium. For in vitro experiments, XNW5004 was used at a working concentration of 1.5 µM unless otherwise specified, with an equivalent volume of DMSO serving as a vehicle control. InVivoMAb anti-human PD-1, isotype control, anti-mouse PD-1 antibody, and mouse IgG2a isotype control were purchased from BioXCell and dissolved in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS).

Construction of stable LLC cells expressing SIINFEKL peptide

LLC cells were replated in 10-cm dishes at a density of 2 × 106 cells per well and incubated at 37 °C for 24 h. Subsequently, cells were transduced for 24 h with ovalbumin 257–264 (SIINFEKL) expressing lentivirus vector (Ubi-MCS-3FLAG-SV40-Cherry-IRES-Blasticidin) (Gene Chem, China) at 4E + 08 TU/ml titer, supplemented with HitransG P reagent (Gene Chem, China). After incubating in regular RPMI-1640 medium for another 48 h, red fluorescence was observed under fluorescence microscopy. Transduced cells stably expressing SIINFEKL (mCherry positive cells) were harvested by flow cytometry sorting using a BD FACS Melody instrument (BD Biosciences, USA).

Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK8) assay

A549, H1975, HCC827, and LLC cells were plated in 96-well plates at a density of 2 × 103 cells per well and subsequently incubated with XNW5004 in a concentration gradient (200µM, 100µM, 50µM, 25µM……) for 48 h. CCK8 assay was then performed using the CCK8 kit (Dojindo, Japan) to evaluate the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, 5% CCK8 reagent was added to each well followed by incubation for 2 h. The optical density (OD) was then measured at 450 nm with an automatic microplate reader, and IC50 was calculated using GraphPad Prism 8.

Colony formation assay

The cells were incubated with XNW5004 at concentrations of 0, 1.5, 3, 6, and 12 µM for 48 h. The cells were then detached by trypsin and seeded into 12-well plates at a density of 1 × 103 cells per well. After culturing for seven consecutive days, cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, stained with crystal violet, and washed with PBS. Colonies consisting of more than 50 cells were counted.

Cell apoptosis analysis

Cell apoptosis analysis was performed by flow cytometry using an ANNEXIN V-APC/7-AAD apoptosis detection kit (KeyGen Biotec, China). Cancer cells were treated with XNW5004 at concentrations of 0, 1.5, 3, 6, and 12 µM for 48 h. The cells were then harvested and transferred into 1.5-ml microcentrifuge tubes, followed by staining with Annexin V-APC and 7-AAD adhering to the manufacturer’s protocol. Apoptosis was then analyzed using a CytoFLEX flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter, USA).

Co-culturing of tumor cells with PBMCs

The groups were designated as follows: control group; XNW5004 group; anti-PD1 group; and XNW5004 plus anti-PD1 combination group. Cancer cells (target cells) were pre-treated with XNW5004 or vehicle for 48 h, as described above. The cells were then detached by trypsin and resuspended at a density of 1 × 105 cells/ml. PBMCs (effector cells) were centrifuged and resuspended at a density of 5 × 105 cells /ml. The tumor cells were co-cultured with PBMCs in 12-well plates. The PD-1 antibody and isotype control were used at a concentration of 1 µg/ml. The culturing process is schematically shown in Fig. 1A. The effector-to-target ratio was set at 5:1 (1 ml each). After co-culturing for 48 h, the PBMCs (i.e., the floating cells) were carefully transferred into 1.5-ml microcentrifuge tubes for flow cytometry analysis. Residual adherent tumor cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, stained with crystal violet, and scanned using a ChemiDoc imaging system (Bio-Rad, USA). Alternatively, residual tumor cells were subjected to apoptosis assay using immunofluorescence staining with an ANNEXIN V-APC/7-AAD Kit (KeyGen Biotec, China). Three biological replicates and three independent technical replicates were performed.

RNA sequencing and bioinformatics analyses

A549 and H1975 cells were trypsinized, while PBMCs were harvested directly, to generate single-cell suspensions following pre-treatment with XNW5004 or vehicle. Single cells within the mouse tumor tissues in the XNW5004 or control group were isolated as described below. The cells were subjected to RNA sequencing; the sequencing workflow is summarized in Fig. S4A. Three biological replicates were prepared for each condition. Sequencing was performed using the DNBSEQ-T7 platform (Geneplus-Beijing, China). The raw data was deposited into the Genome Sequence Archive (GSA) data repository under accession numbers CRA043731 and HRA018746. The sequencing reads were mapped to human reference genome hg19 or mouse reference genome mm10. Raw gene counts were normalized as fragments per kilobase million (FPKM). Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was employed using the “fgsea” package in R to identify significantly enriched pathways between the XNW5004 and control groups. The reference gene sets included “c2.cp.kegg.v7.0.symbols.gmt”, “c5.all.v7.0.symbols.gmt” and “c2.all.v7.0.symbols.gmt”, which were obtained from the MSigDB database (www.broad.mit.edu/gsea/msigdb/). The absolute normalized enrichment score (NES) > 1 and nominal p < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. False discovery rate (FDR) threshold was set to at q < 0.25 for multiple testing correction.

Chemotaxis assay

Chemotaxis assay with Transwell chambers (Corning, USA) was conducted to assess the recruitment effect on immune cells induced by chemokines secreted by tumor cells. Chambers equipped with 3-µm polycarbonate membranes were employed for T-cell chemotaxis assay, whereas chambers with 8-µm membranes were used for assays of dendritic cells. The chemotaxis assay workflow is schematically shown in Fig. 2B. Briefly, the pre-treated tumor cells with XNW5004 or vehicle were trypsinized and re-seeded into fresh culture plates at equivalent densities. The supernatant was collected 12 h later and transferred to the Transwell bottom chambers, serving as a chemoattractant. CD3+T cells were isolated from the PBMCs using magnetic beads (Miltenyi Biotec, Germany). Dendritic cells were differentiated from THP-1 cells treated with phorbol-12-myristate-13-acetate (Sigma-Aldrich, USA), interleukin-4 (Novoprotein, China), and lipopolysaccharide (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) as previously reported [16, 17]. Dendritic cells or T cells were transferred to the Transwell upper chambers (1 × 105 cells/well) and incubated at 37 °C for 24 h before measuring the cell chemotaxis behavior. For dendritic cells, the non-migratory cells on the upper surface of the upper chamber were gently wiped by cotton swabs. The migratory cells on the lower surface were then fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde solution, stained with 0.5% crystal violet, and visualized and counted using a microscope. Due to the non-adherent nature of T cells, migratory cells in the bottom chambers were collected and counted by hemocytometry. Three biological replicates and three independent technical replicates were conducted.

Flow cytometry of immune cells in co-culture system

Immune cells were harvested from the co-culture system as described above. They were stimulated with cell stimulation cocktail (500×) (eBioscience, USA) at 37 °C for 4–6 h before staining. To identify immune cell subsets, cells were stained with surface markers for the gating of CD8 T cells (CD3+ CD8+) and natural killer (NK) cells (CD3− CD56+). Subsequently, the cells were fixed and permeabilized using an Intracellular Fixation & Permeabilization Buffer Set (eBioscience, USA) and intracellularly stained with interferon-γ (IFN-γ) or granzyme B flow antibodies. The activation of immune cells was evaluated based on the IFN-γ- or granzyme B-positive rate. For the detection of T regulatory (Treg) cells (CD3+ CD4+ FOXP3+), FOXP3 intranuclear staining was performed after fixation and permeabilization using a FOXP3/Transcription Factor Staining Buffer Set (eBioscience, USA). Data were acquired and analyzed using a CytoFLEX flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter, USA).

Immunofluorescence analysis

XNW5004- and vehicle-treated cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min and blocked with 10% goat serum (Boster, China). For intranuclear staining, cells were permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 (Sigma-Aldrich, USA). Each sample was incubated with primary antibody at an appropriate concentration in a wet box overnight at 4 °C followed by incubation with secondary antibody for 1 h at room temperature in the dark. Subsequently, the nuclei were stained with DAPI (Beyotime, China) for 5 min. An automatic fluorescence microscope (Nikon ECLIPSE Ti2, USA) was used to observe the immunostaining signals and capture images.

RNA extraction and quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

To validate key candidate genes identified from RNA-sequencing data at the transcriptional level, qRT-PCR was performed on cells pre-treated with XNW5004 or vehicle. RNA was extracted using an RNA extraction kit (EZBioscience, USA), and cDNA was synthesized using HiScript III All-in-one RT SuperMix Perfect for qPCR (Vazyme Biotech, China). The qRT-PCR was performed with ChamQ Universal SYBR® qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme Biotech, China) in a CFX 96TM Real-Time PCR system (Bio-Rad, USA). All samples were run in triplicate and normalized to the GAPDH levels. The relative expression of each gene with respect to the control group was calculated using the ΔΔCt (threshold cycle) method and represented as 2−ΔΔCt.

Western blotting

XNW5004- and vehicle-treated cells were lysed in ice-cold 1× RIPA buffer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) supplemented with a phosphatase inhibitor cocktail (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) for protein extraction. The protein concentration was detected by BCA assay (Beyotime, China). Sodium dodecyl sulphate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis was performed using 10% or 15% polyacrylamide gels, according to molecular weight. After electrophoresis, the proteins were transferred to polyvinylidenedifluoride membranes (Bio-Rad, USA) and blocked with 5% non-fat dry skimmed milk for 1 h. The membranes were then incubated with appropriate primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. The blots were further incubated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) for 2 h at room temperature and washed three times in Tris-buffered saline-Tween (TBST) for 5 min each time. Protein bands were visualized using a chemiluminescent reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) and captured with a chemiluminescence imaging system (Bio-Rad, USA).

Rescue experiments

Rescue experiments were conducted within tumor-PBMC co-culture system to determine whether the enhanced immune-mediated cytotoxicity induced by XNW5004 could be effectively reversed following stimulator of interferon genes (STING) pathway silencing or intercellular adhesion molecule-1(ICAM-1) blockade. STING was silenced via siRNA transfection (Ruibo, China), whereas ICAM-1 function was inhibited using a neutralizing antibody (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Residual tumor cells were assessed via crystal violet staining, while the cytotoxic activities of T cells and NK cells were quantified using flow cytometry, adhering to the established protocols described above.

In vivo experiments

All mouse experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center (Ethics approval number: L102042021040S). Male immunodeficient BALB/c nude mice and immunocompetent C57BL/6J mice were obtained from the Animal Experiment Center of Guangdong Province and maintained in a specific-pathogen-free barrier facility. The mice were 6 weeks old at the beginning of the experiment.

Animal models and therapeutic regimens

5 × 105 LLC cells were inoculated subcutaneously into the right flank of each mouse. Mice with overly large or small tumors after 1 week were excluded. The remaining mice were randomly assigned to four groups for tumor growth monitoring (n = 6 per group) or survival analysis (n = 8 per group) using a randomized block design. Power analysis was performed with G*Power 3.1. XNW5004 was dissolved in 0.5% methylcellulose to achieve a concentration of 20 mg/ml, whereas anti-PD1 and IgG2a control were prepared in PBS at a concentration of 1 mg/ml. The control group was gavaged with 100 µl of 0.5% methylcellulose twice daily and 200 µg of mouse IgG2a via intraperitoneal injection (i.p.) every other day. In the XNW5004 monotherapy group, mice were administered 100 µl (equivalent to 2 mg) XNW5004 twice daily based on the internal pilot data and 200 µg of mouse IgG2a every other day. The anti-PD1 monotherapy group received 100 µl of 0.5% methylcellulose twice daily and anti-PD1 200 µg i.p. every other day. The combination therapy group was treated with a concurrent regimen of XNW5004 (2 mg twice daily, gavage) and anti-PD1 (200 µg every other day, i.p.).

Tumor growth monitoring and survival analysis

Daily clinical assessments and primary endpoint measurements were performed by an investigator who was blinded to the treatment assignments. Tumor volume was calculated using the following formula: tumor volume (mm3) = 0.5 × tumor length × (tumor width)2. Mice were euthanized upon reaching any of the following humane endpoints: tumor volume reached 2000 mm3, a body weight loss exceeding 20% relative to baseline, or tumor ulceration occurred. Survival curves were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared via the log-rank test.

Isolation of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) from tumor-bearing mice

Tumor samples harvested from euthanized mice were stored in MACS Tissue Storage Solution (Miltenyi Biotec, Germany) until processing. The tumors were minced with scissors, followed by digestion in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 0.1% collagenase type IV. Tissue dissociation was performed using a GentleMACs tissue dissociator (Miltenyi Biotec, Germany). Single-cell suspensions were then obtained by filtering cell suspensions through a 70-µm cell strainer. Erythrocytes were lysed using ammonium chloride-potassium lysis buffer (Sigma-Aldrich, USA), and the remaining cells were resuspended in PBS. TILs were isolated using mouse lymphocyte separation solution according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Dakewe, China). Flow cytometry was performed as described above. Unlike human NK cells characterized by CD3− CD56+, murine NK cells were distinctively identified and gated as CD3− NK1.1+.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

IHC was performed to analyze tumor-infiltrating immune cells in the tumor tissues of C57BL/6 mice. Tumor tissues harvested from euthanized mice were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, embedded in paraffin, and sliced into 3 μm thickness. IHC was performed in accordance with the instructions for the Universal Two-Step Test Kit (Zhongshan Goldenbridge, China). Heat-induced antigen retrieval on the slides was carried out with EDTA or sodium citrate (Zhongshan Goldenbridge, China) as the target retrieval solution in a pressure cooker for 20 min. Subsequently, the solution was allowed to cool naturally to room temperature. Nonspecific background was blocked for 30 min using 10% goat serum (Boster, China). Primary antibodies were diluted in 10% goat serum and applied to the slides overnight at 4 °C. Subsequently, the corresponding secondary antibodies were applied to the slides for 1 h at room temperature. Following diaminobenzidine color development, the slides were counterstained with hematoxylin (Zhongshan Goldenbridge, China) and visualized with a microscope (NIKON ECLIPSE 80i). Positive cells were counted in five random high-power fields (HPF, 400× magnification).

Clinical context

As reported in our published work [18], ORIENT-11 clinical trial cohort comprised 171 patients for whom RNA sequencing and PD-L1 immunohistochemistry data were available. They were included in our current analysis, with 58 assigned to the standard chemotherapy-alone group and 113 to the sintilimab (PD-1 antibody) plus chemotherapy group. The cut-off point of ICAM-1 value was determined by ROC analysis using the Youden index, while PD-L1 threshold was set as 1%, as usual. Survival differences between groups were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method (log-rank test).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 9. Continuous variables were analyzed using Student’s t test (two-group comparisons) or one-way ANOVA with LSD multi-comparison test (three or more groups) if normally distributed; otherwise, nonparametric Mann-Whitney test (two-group comparisons) or Kruskal-Wallis test followed by Dunn’s multi-comparison test (three or more groups) was utilized. Categorical variables were analyzed using χ2 statistics. P < 0.05 (two-sided) was considered statistically significant, unless otherwise noted.

Results

Evaluation of EZH2 using public databases

Based on the TIMER database (http://timer.cistrome.org/), EZH2 expression levels were generally elevated in tumor tissues compared to normal tissues across various cancer types, including lung cancer (Fig.S1A). Using the retrospective Kaplan Meier plotter database (https://kmplot.com/analysis/), we found that high EZH2 expression corresponded to worse progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) in LUAD (Fig. S1B). In lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC), high EZH2 expression was associated with worse PFS but improved OS (Fig. S1D). RNA sequencing data obtained from the Cancer Genome Atlas Program database were analyzed using the Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE) database (http://tide.dfci.harvard.edu/) for immunotherapy response prediction. The results suggested that EZH2 was significantly downregulated in LUAD patients that responded to immunotherapy (Fig. S1C), whereas no significant difference was observed in LUSC (Fig. S1E). Consequently, our subsequent experiments focused on LUAD.

XNW5004 potentiates PBMC-mediated cytotoxicity and synergized with PD-1 blockade in vitro

The IC50 values for A549, H1975, HCC827, and LLC were 86, 172, 188, and 121 µM, respectively (Fig. 3A). Colony formation assay and apoptosis assay suggested that XNW5004 at concentrations below 12 µM, well below the IC50 value, had little effect on cancer cell proliferation (Fig. 3B) and apoptosis (Fig. 3C).

In the absence of PBMCs, 1.5 µM XNW5004 did not show significant cytotoxicity in tumor cells, as expected. Nevertheless, in the PBMC co-culture system, reduction in residual A549 and H1975 cells were observed in the XNW5004 and anti-PD1 groups compared to the control group, with the combination group exhibiting the fewest residual cells (Fig. 1B). XNW5004 and anti-PD1 groups exhibited elevated apoptosis rates among the residual population, and the combination group achieved the highest rate (Fig. 1C). IFN-γ and granzyme B are widely recognized as proinflammatory cytokines produced by activated immune cells to induce tumor cell death. In A549 model, the proportions of IFN-γ/granzyme B-positive CD8+ T cells and NK cells were significantly increased in both the XNW5004 and anti-PD1 groups compared to the control group, with the combination therapy group exhibiting the highest levels (Figs. 4A and B). Consistent results were observed in the H1975 model (Figs. S2A and B). These findings suggest that the pretreatment of tumor cells with XNW5004 was effective in reactivating cytotoxic T cells and NK cells, and this effect was further enhanced by combining with PD-1 blockade. Meanwhile, no differences in the proportion of Treg cells were observed among the groups (Fig. 4C, Fig. S2C), indicating that neither XNW5004 nor anti-PD1 exerted a significant effect on Treg differentiation or proliferation.

XNW5004 administration enhanced anti-tumor immune response and synergized with PD-1 blockade in immunocompetent C57BL/6 mice

In the immunodeficient nude mouse model, neither XNW5004, PD-1 blockade, nor their combination resulted in significant attenuation of tumor growth (Figs. 5A–C). Conversely, XNW5004 or PD-1 blockade monotherapy partially inhibited tumor growth in the immunocompetent C57BL/6 mouse model, while their combination achieved the most potent anti-tumor effect, as evidenced by reductions in tumor volume and tumor weight (Figs. 5E–G). In the C57BL/6 mouse model, prolonged survival was also achieved with XNW5004 treatment (P = 0.014) and PD-1 blockade (P = 0.002), with combination therapy yielding favorable survival outcomes (P < 0.001) among all groups (Fig. S3). These results were consistent with our in vitro findings. Furthermore, no statistically significant differences in body weight were observed among all groups, and no mouse experienced a weight loss exceeding 20%, suggesting an absence of overt toxicity (Figs. 5D and H).

IHC analysis revealed that XNW5004 treatment significantly increased intratumoral infiltration of CD3+ T cells (Fig. 6A), CD8+ T cells (Fig. 6B), and CD11c+ dendritic cells (Fig. 6C) in C57BL/6 mice, whereas FoxP3+ Treg cells were not significantly altered (Fig. 6D). Analogous to the in vitro results, flow cytometric analysis revealed that XNW5004 or PD-1 blockade monotherapy significantly increased the proportions of IFN-γ/granzyme B-positive CD8+ T cells and NK cells within the TIL population, with the combination group exhibiting the most pronounced induction (Fig. 6E and F). These results indicated that XNW5004 administration improved the cytotoxic activity of T cells and NK cells without altering Treg abundance, thereby enhancing the anti-tumor immune response.

Mechanistic exploration

Our preliminary findings presented above imply that the anti-tumor effect of XNW5004 is primarily achieved by immune enhancement rather than direct cytotoxicity against tumor cells. This was further supported by RNA-sequencing and GSEA (Fig. S4B); specifically, anti-tumor immunity pathways were significantly enriched in the XNW5004-treated group, whereas cell proliferation/apoptosis-related pathways lacked significant enrichment. Similar results were obtained across A549 cells, H1975 cells, PBMCs, and tumor tissues harvested from C57BL/6 mice. Hence, some of the pivotal molecules within the enriched pathways and their associated functions were selected for subsequent validation.

XNW5004 increased chemokine expression and promoted immune cell recruitment

QRT-PCR analysis revealed a significant upregulation of several chemokines, including CCL2, CXCL1, CXCL5, CCL20, CCL22, and CXCL10 following XNW5004 treatment (Fig. 2A). Furthermore, chemotaxis assays indicated that the supernatant from tumor cells pre-treated with XNW5004 exhibited enhanced recruitment of dendritic cells (Fig. 2C) and T cells (Fig. 2D). These findings suggest that XNW5004 administration promoted the secretion of chemokines from tumor cells, thereby facilitating the attraction of immune cells.

XNW5004 upregulated tumor surface major histocompatibility complex class I (MHC-I) expression and promoted tumor antigen presentation

MHC-I downregulation is common in tumor cells, resulting in diminished immune recognition and weakened immune response. The RNA sequencing results presented above suggest that XNW5004 administration induced the transcriptional upregulation of HLA-A, HLA-B, and HLA-C, the three components of MHC-I. This was corroborated by qRT-PCR analysis (Fig. 7A). Western blotting assay and immunofluorescence analysis further confirmed MHC-I upregulation at both the protein level and on the cell surface (Figs. 7B and 8F). To further determine whether MHC-I upregulation enhanced antigen presentation, we performed an antigen presentation assay (schematically illustrated in Fig. 7C). The results demonstrated that XNW5004 administration increased the surface presentation of SIINFEKL peptide by H2-Kb (Fig. 7D), which substantiated its role in facilitating intratumoral antigen presentation.

XNW5004 upregulated the expression of two co-signaling molecules: ICAM-1 and PD-L1

ICAM-1 and PD-L1 serve as two transmembrane co-signaling molecules involved in immune regulation. Consistently, interrogation of the Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis (GEPIA) database (http://gepia.cancer-pku.cn/) revealed a significant positive correlation (r Spearman = 0.4, P < 0.001) between ICAM1 (encoding ICAM-1) and CD274 (encoding PD-L1) expression in LUAD tissues (Fig. S5). Our RNA sequencing data identified their concomitant upregulation by XNW5004 at the transcriptional level, which was subsequently validated via qRT-PCR analysis (Fig. 8A). Their enhanced abundance on the cell surface was also observed by immunofluorescence staining and flow cytometry assays (Fig. 8B and C). Furthermore, Western blotting assay corroborated their upregulation at the protein level (Fig. 8D).

Considering that the role of ICAM-1 in lung cancer remains inadequately characterized, especially in the field of immunotherapy, we systematically explored its clinical and functional relevance. Based on the TIMER database, ICAM-1 is downregulated in lung cancer tissue compared to normal tissue (Fig. S6), suggesting its involvement in lung tumorigenesis. We further validated its prognostic significance within the ORIENT-11 clinical cohort. Notably, in patients receiving combination therapy, high ICAM-1 expression was associated with significantly prolonged PFS and OS, whereas no such correlation was observed in those receiving chemotherapy alone (Fig. 8E). This suggests that ICAM-1 is positively associated with immunotherapy efficacy. Consistently, PD-L1, a well-established and robust positive predictor of immunotherapy, showed a similar trend where higher expression correlated with prolonged OS in the combination therapy group, with a consistent though non-significant trend for PFS (Fig. 8F). To confirm causality, an ICAM-1 neutralizing antibody was introduced into the co-culture system. Strikingly, ICAM-1 blockade significantly abrogated the XNW5004-induced enhancement of tumor cell elimination (Fig. S7A), as well as the concomitant upregulation of cytotoxic markers (IFN-γ and granzyme B) in CD8+ T and NK cells (Figs. S7B and C). This rescue assay indicated that XNW5004-mediated immune enhancement was dependent on ICAM-1 upregulation.

Double-strand DNA (DsDNA)–STING–TANK-binding kinase 1 (TBK1)–nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) axis served as the upstream regulatory signaling pathway

We further explored the upstream signaling pathway. As shown in Fig. 9B, low-dose XNW5004 dramatically reduced the global H3K27me3 level but did not affect EZH2 protein expression. However, chromatin immunoprecipitation assays showed no H3K27me3 enrichment at the promoters of PD-L1, MHC-I, or ICAM-1 (data not shown), suggesting that their expression is not directly regulated by EZH2-mediated H3K27me3 but may instead be attributable to secondary effects. GSEA analysis of the RNA sequencing profiles identified the activation of the NF-κB pathway upon XNW5004 administration (Fig. 9A); we therefore speculated that NF-κB served as the upstream mediator. Western blotting analysis confirmed the upregulation of key components in both the canonical and non-canonical NF-κB pathways (e.g., p52, RelB, p50, and phosphorylated p65) following XNW5004 treatment at concentrations ranging from 1.5 to 12 µM (Fig. 9B). As NF-κB molecules serve as transcription factors that activate target genes upon nuclear translocation, their localization was examined. The immunofluorescence assay confirmed that the nuclear accumulation of p50/p65 and RelB/p52 subunits was indeed enhanced by XNW5004 (Fig. 9C).

The activation of phosphorylated IκB kinase (p-IKK) is generally essential in IκBα dissociation, which in turn facilitates NF-κB nuclear entry and NF-κB signaling pathway activation. Although IκBα downregulation was observed upon XNW5004 administration, no corresponding upregulation of p-IKKα/β occurred (Fig. 9D). Instead, we found a slight upregulation of phosphorylated TBK1, a noncanonical IκB kinase (Fig. 9E and F), along with the marked upregulation of phosphorylated STING (Fig. 9E). This suggested that activation of the STING-TBK1 axis served as the upstream regulator of the NF-κB pathway. GSEA showed that XNW5004 treatment impaired the process of double-strand break repair (Fig. 10A), which was reported to activate the dsDNA-sensing STING pathway. As shown in Fig. 10B, XNW5004 markedly increased γ-H2AX expression, a hallmark feature of cellular response to DNA double-strand breaks, accompanied by accumulated cytosolic dsDNA (Fig. 10C). Rescue experiments showed that treatment with bardoxolone methyl, an NF-κB inhibitor, reversed the XNW5004-induced upregulation of ICAM-1, MHC-I, and PD-L1. STING inhibition counteracted the activation of the NF-κB pathway as well as the upregulation of ICAM-1, MHC-I, and PD-L1 induced by XNW5004 (Fig. 10D). Furthermore, genetic knockdown of STING phenocopied the effects of ICAM-1 blockade, significantly abrogating XNW5004-induced tumor cell elimination (Fig. S8A) and CD8+T/NK cell activation (Figs. S8B and C). These results suggest that the dsDNA-STING-TBK1-NF-κB axis functions as a critical upstream regulatory signaling pathway through which XNW5004 exerts its immunomodulatory effects.

Discussion

The pioneering first-generation EZH2 inhibitor tazemetostat was once the sole EZH2 inhibitor to receive regulatory clinical approval for follicular lymphoma with EZH2 mutation [19] and epithelioid sarcoma tumors characterized by SWI/SNF (switch/sucrose non-fermentable) gene INI1 (integrase interactor 1, also known as SMARCB1) loss [20]. However, its clinical efficacy was constrained in the context of prevalent carcinomas that lack INI1 deficiency or EZH2 mutations, as evidenced by existing data [21]. Furthermore, tazemetostat was voluntarily withdrawn from the global market in 2026 due to safety concerns regarding an increased incidence of secondary hematologic malignancies [22, 23]. XNW5004 serves as the next-generation EZH2 inhibitor developed by Suzhou Evopoint Biosciences. In contrast to the S-adenosylmethionine (SAM)-competitive nature of tazemetostat [24], XNW5004 is engineered to be substrate-competitive [25], resulting in more stable target engagement and superior selectivity profile. This mechanistic distinction likely accounts for the favorable safety profile of XNW5004, as well as notable efficacy in clinical trials even in cases of EZH2 wild-type follicular lymphoma [26] and INI1 proficient solid tumors like prostate cancer [14].

Our analyses based on public datasets indicate that high EZH2 expression is associated with poorer PFS and OS outcomes in LUAD; whereas the opposite relationship is observed in LUSC. Additionally, TIDE analysis predicts that high EZH2 expression correlates with a lower immunotherapy response rate in LUAD, but not in LUSC. These findings suggest that the prognostic value of EZH2 may differ depending on the pathological subtype of lung cancer. For LUAD, EZH2 inhibition may be a feasible strategy for tumor suppression and immunotherapy sensitization.

The cell lines used in our study typically harbored wild-type EZH2 and SWI/SNF complex genes. As expected, XNW5004 exhibited relatively high IC50 values indicating limited direct cytotoxic activity, consistent with the characteristic profile of other EZH2 inhibitors in such backgrounds [21]. This suggests that the therapeutic efficacy of XNW5004 is not primarily attributable to potent direct cytotoxicity, but rather stems from its capacity to modulate the tumor immune microenvironment. Tumor cells pre-treated with low-dose XNW5004 exhibited increased susceptibility to immune-mediated destruction in vitro. In vivo, XNW5004 demonstrated an anti-tumor effect and synergistic interaction with PD-1 blockade in immunocompetent C57BL/6J mice, but not in immunodeficient nude mice. Both CD8⁺ T cells and NK cells play essential roles in tumor cell eradication; in both the in vitro co-culture system and the in vivo LLC tumor-bearing C57BL/6J mouse model, their activity was enhanced in the XNW5004-pretreated group, as characterized by increased cytokine production compared to the controls.

As an epigenetic modifying agent, XNW5004 appears to reactivate several molecules involved in anti-tumor immunity, as detailed below. First, XNW5004 promotes the secretion of chemoattractant factors from tumors, thereby facilitating the infiltration of immune cells into the tumors. Secondly, MHC-I upregulation on the tumor cell surface augments tumor antigen presentation, aiding in immune recognition. Thirdly, ICAM-1 upregulation improves the adhesion of immune cells to tumor tissues, contributing to immune cell activation. Fourthly, the upregulation of PD-L1 suggests a synergistic interaction between XNW5004 and PD-1 inhibition, as their combination seems to yield the strongest anti-tumor response. Collectively, XNW5004 enhances tumor immunogenicity and facilitates the development of an “inflamed” tumor microenvironment, thereby augmenting immune attack against tumor cells and increasing their susceptibility to immune checkpoint inhibition. Combining XNW5004 and PD-1/PD-L1 blockade thus represents a promising therapeutic strategy for LUAD.

MHC-I molecules are responsible for presenting intracellular peptide fragments, including tumor antigens [27]. MHC-I downregulation is common across various cancer types and contributes to impairments in tumor antigen processing, leading to immune evasion and immunotherapy resistance [28–30]. In the present study, XNW5004 was observed to upregulate MHC-I expression on the cell surface and enhance antigen presentation in tumor cells, corroborating the findings of Ennishi et al. [31] and Zhou et al. [32] with the EZH2 inhibitors GSK126 and EPZ6438, respectively. Thus, the modulation of MHC-I expression serves as an essential mechanism through which XNW5004 augments the anti-tumor immune response.

While the modulation of chemokines, MHC-I, and PD-L1 appears to be a generalized ‘class effect’ associated with EZH2 inhibition [31, 33–35], our study identified ICAM-1 as a unique effector molecule regulated by XNW5004, which has not been previously reported for other EZH2 inhibitors. As a critical transmembrane protein that functions as both an intercellular adhesion receptor and a costimulatory molecule [36, 37], ICAM-1 remains controversial in tumor biology. Its effects—whether beneficial or detrimental—appear to vary across different cancer types [38–40]. The TIMER dataset shows that ICAM-1 is downregulated in cancerous tissues in LUAD, suggesting that ICAM-1 overexpression corresponds to improved LUAD prognosis. In the ORIENT-11 cohort, ICAM-1 conferred a favorable prognosis specifically in the chemotherapy plus PD-1 blockade arm, while its prognostic impact was absent in the chemotherapy-only group. This indicates that high ICAM-1 expression characterizes an immunologically active ‘hot’ state in LUAD which is susceptible to immunotherapy. Rescue experiments through ICAM-1 neutralizing antibody attenuated the enhanced immune cytotoxic activity triggered by XNW5004, further establishing a causal link between ICAM-1 expression and the therapeutic efficacy of XNW5004. PD-L1, as another transmembrane co-signaling molecule, was also upregulated by XNW5004. The GEPIA public database suggests a synchronized expression pattern of ICAM-1 and PD-L1. This is potentially because both genes share common upstream transcriptional pathways, such as the NF-κB axis demonstrated in our study. Additionally, both of them are reported to be upregulated by IFN-γ [41–43], a primary product of activated anti-tumor immune cells, thereby rendering their synchronized expression a robust hallmark of an immuno-hot microenvironment. Notably, PD-L1 acts as an immune activation marker [44] but also functions as a well-characterized immunosuppressive molecule [45] that interacts with PD-1. Its upregulation induced by XNW5004 may create a therapeutic bottleneck in clinical application. Therefore, combining XNW5004 with PD-1 blockade is essential to fully harness its therapeutic potential.

STING is an endoplasmic reticulum protein essential in inducing immune reaction [46]. Defects in the STING signaling pathway drive tumor immune evasion and compromise anti-tumor surveillance [47, 48]. Since EZH2-mediated epigenetic modifications are frequently enhanced in tumor cells and play a crucial role in maintaining genomic integrity [49], EZH2 inhibition may compromise DNA repair processes and induce chromosomal instability. This, in turn, triggers the formation of micronuclei derived from lagging chromosomal fragments, which subsequently release dsDNA into the cytoplasm and activate the STING pathway [50]. Notably, XNW5004 activates the STING pathway through upregulating STING phosphorylation level, rather than increasing total STING expression as previously reported for DNA methyltransferase inhibitor Zebularine [51] and KDM5 histone demethylases [52]. This further leads to the upregulation of p-TBK1 and elicits downstream NF-κB pathway activation. As a fundamental transcription regulator, NF-κB is involved in a diverse range of biological processes, including the modulation of anti-tumor immunity [53]. We consider that the dsDNA-STING-TBK1-NF-κB axis facilitates crosstalk between XNW5004 and effector molecules including PD-L1, MHC-I, and ICAM-1, thereby inflaming the tumor microenvironment and heightening tumor susceptibility to immunotherapy. Notably, given the complexity inherent in epigenetic EZH2 inhibition, it is plausible that additional regulatory layers, such as enhancer remodeling or indirect transcriptional networks, may also contribute to the observed immunomodulatory effects of XNW5004. A comprehensive, exhaustive analysis of all potential regulatory nodes, while compelling, is beyond the scope of the present study.

Our study possesses several limitations. First, though tazemetostat’s withdrawal created a gap in the market, there still existed several EZH2 inhibitors in active clinical development. Variations in chemical scaffolds are likely to cause disparities between the specific effects of different EZH2 inhibitors; for instance, distinct agents have been observed to exert either stimulatory or inhibitory effects on PD-L1 expression [34, 35]. Comprehensive comparative analysis was not performed due to extensive experimental resources required. Second, challenges often arise when transitioning from basic research to clinical application. Subcutaneous graft tumor models were adopted in our current study, further verification is required to confirm whether XNW5004 achieves pharmacologically relevant concentrations in orthotopic models or lung tissue of LUAD patients. While the LLC tumor model utilized here is relatively immunogenic, validation in less immunogenic models such as KrasG12D/p53fl/fl lung adenocarcinoma model will be of great value to further evaluate the translational potential of XNW5004 in treating non-inflamed tumors. Third, from a clinical translational perspective and given the current technical constraints, our molecular validations of RNA sequencing findings primarily focused on human tumor cell models. While we acknowledge the value of further PBMC validation, such experiments were precluded by the practical challenge of procuring sufficient primary cell yields for specific assays. Finally, while our study focused on the synergy between XNW5004 and PD-1 blockade, the potential synergistic effects between XNW5004 and other immune checkpoint inhibitors like PD-L1 blockade and epigenetic drugs warrants further investigation.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our study shows that the EZH2 inhibitor XNW5004 exhibits anti-tumor efficacy in LUAD by enhancing anti-tumor immunity and synergizing with PD-1 blockade immunotherapy. The combination of XNW5004 with PD-1 blockade is a promising therapeutic strategy for LUAD patients. Translating these preclinical observations into clinical practice warrants further validation in rigorously designed clinical studies.

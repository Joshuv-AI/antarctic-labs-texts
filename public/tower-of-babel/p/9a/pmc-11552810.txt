Laser refractive correction of presbyopia
Authors: Sri Ganesh, Sushmitha Samak Sriganesh
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Presbyopia is an inevitable and progressive loss of the eye’s ability to focus on nearby objects due to aging, which affects everyone regardless of their refractive error. Uncorrected presbyopia is a significant burden worldwide and affects the quality of life. Conservative options include spectacle correction, contact lenses, and pharmacologic agents. Spectacles are commonly used to correct vision, but have certain drawbacks such as peripheral blur and impaired depth perception. These limitations have been associated with an increased risk of accidental falls. Several surgical options are available to address these issues, including cornea-based, lens-based, or scleral procedures. These procedures involve modifying the cornea’s optics, replacing the crystalline lens, or attempting to restore accommodation. Each of the options has its benefits and limitations. One of the most common surgical methods of presbyopic correction that has recently become popular is presbyopic Laser assisted In situ keratomileusis (LASIK) due to its improved safety and efficacy. This article is a review of all the currently available data and studies regarding the various methods of correction of presbyopia, with an emphasis on the principles and outcomes of the corneal laser refractive procedures for presbyopia correction, such as PresbyLASIK, Supracor, PRESBYOND laser blended vision, and Custom Q.

Presbyopia is an age-related progressive loss of accommodation that affects all individuals beginning in middle age, regardless of an underlying refractive error.

There is a vast burden of uncorrected presbyopia in the world, with a significant disparity in access to adequate vision correction for presbyopia existing between the urban and rural areas in the developing world. Out of the 1.8 billion people with presbyopia in 2015, 826 million had inadequate near vision correction, and the global unmet need for presbyopia correction was estimated to be 45%. The prevalence of presbyopia is expected to increase to approximately 2.1 billion by 2030.[1]

Uncorrected presbyopia can have multiple effects on quality of life (QoL), such as problems reading fine print, need for increased lighting, diplopia, epiphora, headache, fatigue or asthenopia, and difficulty threading a needle or seeing fine details on proximal objects.[2]

Presbyopia causes a reduced QoL in both developed and developing countries. Reading glasses, when used for treating presbyopia, cause a reduction in QoL parameters compared to that of those who are younger and emmetropic. Even with reading glasses, there is a lack of treatment in the developing world, where Availability of reading glasses ranges from 6% to 45%.[3]

In developed countries, potential alternative presbyopic treatment options are often overlooked due to the accessibility to reading glasses.[4] Dependence on spectacles is one of the main causes of loss of QoL in people over 45 years of age.[5] There is a growing need for adequate and appropriate correction of presbyopia to improve QoL.

The current available treatment methods for presbyopia include both static and dynamic approaches. Conservative options include spectacle correction, contact lenses, and pharmacologic agents. There are also various surgical options, such as cornea-based, lens-based, or scleral procedures.[6] In this article, we shall be reviewing the currently available methods of correction of presbyopia, with an emphasis on the most recent corneal laser refractive procedures for presbyopia correction.

Methods

A review of literature was performed in Medline (United States National Library of Medicine), Scopus (Elsevier BV), and Google Scholar by using the following keywords: presbyopia correction, corneal procedures for correction of presbyopia, laser refractive procedures for presbyopia, PresbyLASIK, laser blended vision, corneal multifocality. Editorial letters, book chapters, duplications, and articles without the mentioned search terms were excluded. A total of 68 relevant articles were included in this review. All relevant articles, including case reports/series, clinical trials, and newsletters, were also included in this review. All other studies of lens-based correction of presbyopia have been excluded from this review.

Management of Presbyopia

The correction of presbyopia can be achieved through the addition of optical power for near vision with the use of external optics or surgical modification of the eye’s optics. Alternatively, surgical intervention can be performed to restore or enhance the accommodative ability of a presbyopic eye.[7]

Conservative approach:

Spectacles:

Spectacles are the most conventional and widely available intervention for the correction of myopia. Reading glasses are designed with a single focal point to correct sight only at one distance; hence, they can be used only for near vision, while a separate pair of spectacles needs to be used for distance vision.[11]

Bifocal, trifocal, or progressive spectacles have zones of various optical powers for viewing objects within one pair of spectacles at specific distances. However, they have certain limitations like image jump as the fixation axis crosses the top edge of the bifocal, limitations on the eye directions, and distortion of peripheral vision with the progressive lens. This can impact individuals’ subjective visual experiences while driving and lead to a significantly increased risk of accidents, particularly when descending stairs and performing workplace tasks.[71213]

Contact lenses:

Contact lens options for correction of presbyopia include soft or rigid gas permeable contact lenses for distance with spectacles for near vision correction, monovision contact lenses, or multifocal contact lenses.

Using contact lenses for distance with reading glasses for near provides optimum vision, but is inconvenient.

Monovision contact lenses correct one eye for distance and the other for near vision. They are easy to fit and not very expensive, but have significant limitations like reduction in stereopsis and contrast sensitivity.[1415]

Multifocal contact lenses incorporate different zones for distance and near vision. They require a detailed prefitting evaluation and contact lens selection. Patient satisfaction and success with multifocal contact lenses rely strongly on lens centration, pupil size, ocular optics, blur tolerance, and neural adaptation.[16]

Multifocal contact lens users are significantly less satisfied with vision during night-time driving, particularly from glare and haloes.[13]

Pharmacologic agents:

The various pharmacologic agents used for the management of presbyopia work on the principle of pinhole effect or lens softening.

Muscarinic agonists induce miosis that increases the depth of focus (DOF), creating pseudo accommodation, and also causes the ciliary muscle to contract and the lens thickness to increase. The commonly used agents are pilocarpine 1% and carbachol 2.25%/3%. However, muscarinic stimulation of the anterior uveal tract can cause chronic inflammation, posterior synechiae, spasmodic contractions of the iris, pigment dispersion, and myopic shift. Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs are combined with miotics such as diclofenac 0.1% to reduce inflammation. Brimonidine 0.2% can prolong the effect of cholinergic agonists; therefore, combined preparation of brimonidine with pilocarpine or carbachol has also been tried. These agents cause accommodative spasm and brow ache as a result of muscarinic stimulation of the ciliary muscle and pupillary sphincter; hence, they are not preferred.[17]

The approach of lens softening works on the principle based on the assumption that presbyopia is mainly caused by lens stiffening and loss of flexibility. Ophthalmic Solution EV06 (1.5%) is a lipoic acid choline ester that reduces the disulfide bonds between lens proteins, restoring lens microfluidics.[8] The prodrug UNR844 is a well-tolerated, effective pharmacologic intervention for improving near visual acuity and is under investigation.[18]

Surgical approach:

There are many procedures described for correction of presbyopia, but almost all of them have certain drawbacks and advantages.

Every treatment for presbyopia involves trade-offs, such as reduced contrast sensitivity and distance vision, and may require an adaptation period.[19] Hence, proper evaluation, patient selection, counseling regarding neuroadaptation, and management of patient expectations are required.

Certain considerations need to be taken into account when selecting the appropriate procedure for the patient. Lenticular changes can be assessed by using aberrometers to look for increased internal aberrations, and Scheimpflug devices can be used to assess changes in scatter at the level of the lens.[20] Lens-based procedures would be a better option when lenticular changes are observed.

Preoperative pupillography is vital to assess pupil response when considering central PresbyLASIK.

Squint evaluation, dry eye evaluation, and assessment of the posterior segment are critical when considering LVC for presbyopia.[21]

A. Cornea-based procedures:

Corneal inlays:

Management of presbyopia has undergone numerous changes in the last few decades. With the desire for spectacle independence increasing, corneal inlays are an excellent option for treating presbyopia. It was Jose Barraquer who first introduced corneal inlay for the treatment of high myopia using polymethyl methacrylate (PMMA).[22] With newer designs, materials, and modern technologies like femtosecond laser to aid in creating flap/pocket, the success rate of treating presbyopia with corneal inlays has dramatically increased. Their small size, adequacy of fluid and nutrient permeability, and biocompatibility with the corneal tissue have an added advantage to the success rate. The biggest advantage of corneal inlays is that they do not remove the corneal tissue and are reversible.[23] Corneal inlays are implanted in the nondominant eye, producing a modified monovision effect. The disadvantages of corneal inlays are a potential compromise of night vision and distance vision and the long-term potential for corneal haze development.[2]

Three types of corneal inlays work on different principles and are described below:

1. Flexivue Microlens (Presbia Coöperatief U.A., Irvine, CA, USA)

It works on the principle of creating the effect of multifocal cornea. It is a 3-mm device hydrogel-based implant made from a hydrophilic copolymer. The central zone has no power, and hence focuses the distant rays onto the retina. The peripheral refractive zones have varying powers between +1.25 and +3.5 D in +0.25 D increments. Its thickness varies from 15 to 20 µm and has a 0.15-mm opening in the center of the inlay. The inlay material has a refractive power of 1.45834.[24] Femtosecond laser is used to create a pocket in the cornea at a depth of 280–300 µm. The Flexivue inlay is then inserted using Presbia’s proprietary insertion tool. Given that the near vision add may increase in the future, the procedure is reversible. Due to its deep placement within the cornea, no corneal topography changes are usually seen.

2. Raindrop Near Vision Inlay (ReVision Optics, Lake Forest, CA, USA)

It falls into the category of corneal reshaping inlay and works by creating a hyperprolate cornea. It measures about 1.5–2 mm in diameter, 32 µm in thickness in the center, and 10 µm in the periphery. It increases the anterior corneal curvature when placed under the LASIK flap or intrastromal pocket at a depth of 120–200 µm. The cornea behaves as a hyperprolate refracting surface with good near and intermediate vision.[25] Raindrop is made of hydrogel with a refractive index similar to that of cornea and has no refractive power. After 1 year of follow-up, it was observed that a few patients experienced pain, discomfort, halos, light sensitivity, and dryness and required replacement due to decentration.[26] Initially approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (US FDA) in 2016, Raindrop was withdrawn from the US market in 2018 owing to its corneal surface haze issues.

3. KAMRA inlay (AcuFocus Inc., Irvine, CA, USA)

Kamra works on the principle of small aperture. The pinhole effect increases the DOF, and the ring blocks the peripheral rays, thus ensuring near and intermediate vision with no effect on distant vision. Made of polyvinylidene fluoride and carbon, it is 5-µm thick and 3 mm in diameter with a central aperture of 1.6 mm. The outer ring has 8600 laser-etched holes for the diffusion of nutrients in the cornea. KAMRA inlay is implanted in the nondominant eye at around 200–250 µm depth. In cases where previous LASIK has been performed, the inlay is placed beneath the previous LASIK flap in a dual interface technique.[27]

Small aperture optics have limitations such as only 20% of the light passing through the central aperture, 5% of light diffracted by microholes, and reduced binocular symmetry and stereo acuity. Though Kamra has good efficacy, the explantation rates range from 1.5% to 10%. In the US FDA trial, most of the explanations were due to refractive shifts, mostly hyperopic, or dissatisfaction with visual outcomes.[28]

Tissue addition:

Lenticules from small-incision lenticule extraction (SMILE) can be stored safely and implanted in patients with hyperopia, keratoconus, and astigmatism.[2930] PrEsbyopic Allogenic Refractive Lenticule (PEARL) uses allogenic corneal inlay prepared from SMILE lenticule of specific thickness trephined at the center to 1-mm diameter. It is implanted in the cornea under a femtosecond laser-created cap of 120 µm in the nondominant eye of presbyopic patients. Jacob et al.[31] demonstrated the safety and efficacy of PEARL corneal inlays. After placing presbyopic refractive lenticule inlays, they reported promising outcomes, with all patients achieving J2 uncorrected near visual acuity (UNVA) and 20/20 uncorrected distance visual acuity (UDVA) postoperatively.

Another type of allogenic corneal inlay is Allotex TransForm lenticule. It is a piece of acellular cornea, sterilized with electron beam radiation, shaped using a laser to 2–3.5 mm in diameter with a central thickness of 15–25 µm and is applied under a 110-µm femtosecond-created corneal flap on a stromal bed.[32] It shows a relatively excellent safety profile and UNVA of 0.20 log of minimum angle of resolution (logMAR) after a 6-month follow-up, with no patient losing more than two lines. Preliminary results suggest postoperative complications are lower than those caused by synthetic corneal inlays.[3334] However, these results may be limited by smaller sample sizes and shorter follow-up times.[35]

A new biosynthetic corneal inlay (CorVision, LinkoCare) made of a medical grade, type I collagen, with a central thickness of 20–30 µm and a diameter of 1.8–3.0 mm, is implanted in a femtosecond laser pocket at a depth of 140–220 µm and up to 6.0 mm in diameter, in the nondominant eye, centered over the Purkinje reflex.[36] After 12 months, the average UNVA was 0.13 logMAR at the optimal reading distance, with reasonable satisfaction. Adverse events included temporary increased intraocular pressure, disturbing photic phenomenon, dry eyes, and mild corneal haze.[37]

Presbyopic LASIK

Presbyopic LASIK uses an aspheric ablation profile, increases the depth of field of the eye, and allows monovision to treat presbyopia in combination with myopia and hyperopia. Since 1992, attempts have been made to create multifocal corneal profiles using an excimer laser.[38] The introduction of LASIK in refractive surgery offered a more effective and controllable technique for creating multifocal, bifocal, or other profiles.[39]

The principle may be based on the power of refractive error and presbyopia correction calculation, corneal asphericity quotient (Q-value), higher-order spherical aberration changes, or optical and transition zone changes.

1. Multifocal transition profile

This technique was popular in the 1980s. It used an excimer laser to create a multifocal ablation profile by intentionally decentering a hyperopic ablation profile. There are very few reports on this technique, and surgeons did not accept it well because it induced significant levels of vertical coma.[40]

2. Peripheral PresbyLASIK

In peripheral presbyLASIK, the peripheral cornea is ablated to increase the depth of vision for near and the center is left for distance.[41] In myopes undergoing peripheral presbyLASIK, a significant amount of corneal tissue is removed to create a hypernegative ablation profile. Thus, peripheral presbyLASIK is typically performed in presbyopic hyperopes or low myopes.[42]

3. Central PresbyLASIK

First described in 1996 by Ruiz, this technique involves ablating the central cornea to create a hyperpositive zone for near vision. The peripheral zone is left for distance vision. It is pupil dependent and hence more physiologic, as the pupil constricts with accommodation during near reflex. Adequate centration is required for controlled results, and it can be performed on hyperopes, myopes, or emmetropes with minimal tissue loss. The main limitations are inadequate alignment with the line of sight, pupil center, and corneal vertex, which induces coma aberrations.[42]

Central presbyLASIK is the most commonly performed laser procedure for the correction of presbyopia and includes the following:

AMO VISX hyperopia–presbyopia multifocal approach (AMO Development LLC, Milpitas, CA, USA) steepens the central zone for near vision and flattens the peripheral zone for distance vision. It is for hyperopes with up to +4.0 D and -2.00 D of astigmatism. On 12-month follow-up of patients using an aspheric presbyopia treatment and wavefront-guided hyperopic LASIK treatment using the VISX STAR S4 excimer laser (AMO), 100% were found to have a binocular UDVA of >20/25 and a UNVA of J3. Loss of more than two lines of corrected distance visual acuity (CDVA) was seen in 10%. Even though higher-order aberrations increased after surgery, it was correlated with improved near visual acuity.[43]

SUPRACOR (Technolas Perfect Vision GmbH, Munich, Germany) creates a hyperpositive area in the central 3.0-mm zone that gives an addition of approximately 2 D and a distance target of 0.50 D of myopia in both eyes or uses the principle of micro-monovision by keeping the distance target of 0 D in the dominant eye. It uses an aberration-optimized presbyopic algorithm and minimizes the aberrations normally induced during treatment.[44] The outcomes vary between studies which showed that 87%–91% had UNVA of N8 or better with a loss of two or more lines of CDVA for 6%–10% of patients.[4546]

PresbyMAX (SCHWIND eye-tech-solutions GmbH, Kleinostheim, Germany) technique involves the creation of a multifocal corneal surface that is bi-aspheric in nature. The central area of the cornea is hyperpositive, with a power of + 0.75 to +2.50 D, which corrects the near vision. The surrounding area of the cornea has a gradual aspheric taper, and the ablation is calculated to correct the distance refractive error.[47] In the PresbyMAX symmetric module, both eyes are treated equally to achieve a 1.5 D DOF. However, PresbyMAX µ-Monovision uses micro-monovision to create asymmetric correction with a 0.8 D difference between the eyes. A distance target of 0 D and a near target of -1.5 D are kept for the dominant eye, and a distant target of -0.8 D and a near target of -2.3 D are kept for the nondominant eye. PresbyMAX hybrid is the newest addition to the PresbyMAX module, which operates by inducing differential DOF in dominant and nondominant eyes, along with differences in targets. The dominant eye is aimed at a distance target of -0.1 D and a near target of -0.9 D, whereas the nondominant eye is aimed at a distance target of -0.8 D and a near target of -2.3 D. This creates a DOF of 0.8 D in the dominant eye and 1.5 D in the nondominant eye. PresbyMAX symmetric offers the best stereo acuity and binocular vision.[21]

Uthoff et al.[48] used PresbyMAX symmetric in hyperopic, myopic, and emmetropic patients, where they observed that 83% of all patients had a UDVA of 0.1 logMAR or better. They found that 90% of the emmetropic and 80% of hyperopic and myopic eyes had UNVA of 0.3 logRAD (Logarithm of the reading acuity determination) or better. Ten percent of the hyperopic and emmetropic patients lost two lines of best-corrected distance visual acuity (BDVA), and 40% lost one line. Among the myopic patients, 10% lost three lines, 10% lost two lines, and 10% lost one line of BDVA. The hyperopic group was the most satisfied. In the 6-month follow-up, no retreatment was reported.

According to a study conducted by Luger et al.,[49] patients who underwent PresbyMAX treatment for myopia, hyperopia, and astigmatism showed positive outcomes, with 70% of them achieving binocular UDVA of 0.1 logMAR or better and 84% achieving binocular UNVA of 0.1 logRAD or better at the 1-year follow-up. However, 33% of patients experienced a decrease in CDVA of at least one line and 3% lost more than one line. In addition, 23% of patients had a decrease in corrected near visual acuity of at least one line and 8% lost more than one line.

PresbyMAX hybrid showed better results than PresbyMAX symmetric. At 1 year postoperatively, 93% of patients achieved 20/20 or better binocular UDVA and 90% of patients had J2 or better UNVA. Seven percent of patients in the myopic group and 6% of patients in the hyperopic group lost two or more lines of CDVA. Nineteen percent of patients needed retreatment and 3% of patients needed reversal of treatment, after which all patients achieved a UDVA of 20/25 or better.[50]

Villanueva et al.[51] conducted a study to analyze the long-term outcomes of presbyLASIK surgery using customized software based on a light propagation algorithm developed using Matlab software. The study was carried out over 3 years on eyes treated with the same version of presbyLASIK software. The results showed that the corneal multifocality outcomes remained stable throughout the follow-up period, indicating that the outcomes of presbyLASIK surgery remain consistent over time.

PRESBYOND LBV:

LASIK monovision is a commonly performed procedure and has been shown to be safe and effective in various studies.[5253] However, traditional LASIK monovision is associated with side effects such as poor intermediate vision, reduced contrast sensitivity, loss of stereopsis, increased photic phenomena, and longer adaptation time, which can reduce patient satisfaction.[54]

PRESBYOND LBV (Carl Zeiss Meditec, Jena, Germany) is a presbyopia treatment option that is performed with MEL 80 excimer laser and the CRS-Master. A procedure described by Reinstein et al.[55] successfully combines monovision with extended depth of field achieved by an aspheric laser ablation profile using a micro-monovision protocol to treat presbyopia. This procedure is safe and effective across all types of ametropia without a significant loss of contrast sensitivity.[55565758] PRESBYOND LBV performed using the MEL 90 excimer laser allows for the import of pre-op spherical aberrations from wavefront-supported customized ablation (WASCA, Carl Zeiss Meditec), the use of a wide range of optical zones, and entry of functional age data of the patient into the CRS-Master.

Technology and principle

PRESBYOND LBV involves a combination of controlled induced corneal spherical aberrations and a micro-monovision protocol. Micro-monovision is a new technique that differs from traditional LASIK-induced monovision. Unlike the nomogram used in traditional LASIK, which increases the degree of anisometropia from -1.50 D up to -2.50 D based on age, micro-monovision targets mild myopia of -1.50 D or less for the near eye, regardless of age. To increase the depth of field of each eye within safe limits up to 1.50 D, an optimized aspheric ablation profile is utilized, which induces a controlled amount of spherical aberration. This results in a Blend Zone, shown in Fig. 2, which allows for continuous distance, intermediate, and near vision across both eyes. This optimization is based on the patient’s age, refraction, preoperative spherical aberration, tolerance for anisometropia, and treatment centered on the corneal vertex.[5556]

Eligibility criteria

Presbyopic patients with normal topography having a minimum CDVA of 20/25 or better in either eye, a tolerance to anisometropia of at least 0.75 D, quick fusion and suppression, and good motivation can be advised Presbyond LBV. Corneal and lens opacities potentially affecting vision, severe dry eyes, optic nerve and retinal pathologies, acute or chronic systemic disease, and any immunosuppressive disorder are relative contraindications for this procedure.

Preoperative evaluation and treatment planning involves a complete ophthalmic examination for all patients before surgery. It includes anterior and posterior segment evaluation, dilated refraction, corneal topography, and dry eye assessment.

Preoperative refractive workup involves verifying eye dominance and correcting the dominant eye for distance, while the nondominant eye is corrected for near. The dominance of the eye may be tested using three methods – hole in the card test, shooting test, and camera test. Once the dominance is verified, tolerance testing for micro-monovision is performed with retinoscopy lenses after applying the full manifest correction in both eyes. A positive sphere corresponding to a standard myopic target of +1.50 D is then added to the nondominant eye, and the patient is asked to read the smallest letter on Snellen’s distance vision chart with both eyes. The patient is asked if he has any visual discomfort or disturbances while reading the chart. The patient’s comfort with near vision is assessed by asking him/her to read a near vision chart with both eyes. The patient is deemed tolerant if satisfied with the quality of distance and near vision and does not report any symptoms. For those who express discomfort, the add is reduced in +0.25 D steps in the nondominant eye until they report no blur with minimum discomfort. The add in the nondominant eye may be increased to +1.75 D to a maximum of +2.00 D if the patient does not achieve the desired near vision with +1.50 D.

Patients must be informed about the short-term side effects of dryness, glare, and vision fluctuations, which tend to resolve within 3–4 months.

WASCA or any other whole-eye aberrometer may measure the ocular wavefront aberrations in scotopic conditions at 6 mm. The z (4,0) data of spherical aberrations is then imported into the CRS-Master and used to design the aspheric ablation profile, which is exported for treatment with MEL 90 excimer laser. It should be ensured that the z (4,0) data, if taken from any aberrometer other than WASCA, must be converted into the Malacara notation before entering it into the CRS-Master.

The surgical procedure is similar to that of a standard femtosecond LASIK treatment. Flaps are created either with a micro-keratome or with a femtosecond laser, and stromal aspheric ablation is performed using the MEL 80/90 excimer laser with a 6–6.5-mm optical and 2.2-mm transition zone.[58]

Outcomes

The outcomes of PRESBYOND have been studied by Reinstein et al.[59] in myopes, hyperopes, and emmetropes. In myopic patients with presbyopia (-0.20 to -8.30 D), binocular UCVA was 20/20, J5 in 99% of patients and correction within ±1.00 D was achieved in 99%. None lost two or more lines of CDVA, and the change in refraction between 3 months and 1 year was -0.0 6 ± 0.31 D. Ninety percent of hyperopes who underwent PRESBYOND LBV achieved binocular UCVA of 20/20 and J5. None of the patients lost CDVA of two lines or more, and the change in refraction between 3 months and 1 year was +0.11+/-0.36 D.[55] PRESBYOND performed on emmetropes showed binocular 20/20 UDVA in 98% of patients and a UNVA of J2 or better in 96% of patients. No one lost two or more lines of CDVA.[56]

A study conducted by Ganesh and Brar[58] investigated patient satisfaction 1 year after they underwent PRESBYOND surgery. The study found that the satisfaction levels were very high, with 97% of patients in the myopia group and 96% in the hyperopia group reporting satisfaction with their distance vision. It is possible that hyperopic eyes may regress slightly by the age of 1 year. This may cause the myopic target to be less negative and affect near vision. However, regression of the near eye in the myopic group may make the myopic target more negative, which helps maintain good near vision, but at the cost of slightly compromising distant vision in the nondominant eye. The myopia group had a near vision satisfaction score of 95%, while the hyperopia group scored 89%.

PRESBYOND LBV demonstrated excellent safety, efficacy, and stability in the 12-month outcomes. It is a well-tolerated modality for the simultaneous correction of myopia and hyperopia with presbyopia without inducing significant side effects.

Custom Q ablation:

In Custom Q ablation, a negative asphericity (Q) using the Custom Q algorithm (Alcon Laboratories, Inc., Fort Worth, TX, USA) is created in the nondominant eye, along with a -0.5 D postoperative target and the dominant eye is corrected for distance. This negative asphericity in the nondominant eye behaves as a hyperprolate zone and aids near vision; hence, some classify this as central Presbyopic LASIK.[60]

Courtin et al.[61] reported a 6-month follow-up, where they evaluated the effect of a planned change in corneal spherical aberration using the Custom Q treatment planning available on the WaveLight Refractive Suite platform (Alcon Laboratories, Inc.). The Custom Q nomogram allows for selecting a target refraction and target asphericity. Only hyperopic presbyopes were evaluated, and binocular UDVA of 20/20 was achieved in 91% of the patients, with 83% having a Jaeger 1 or better binocular UNVA.

Although aspheric ablation profile nomograms used on different laser platforms allow for good clinical results, several technical points remain to be elucidated, such as the required change in corneal asphericity and the subsequent spherical aberration variation to significantly improve the near vision without excessively compromising distance vision.

Molding procedures:

Conductive keratoplasty

CK is a noninvasive treatment for mild to moderate hyperopia (+0.75 to +3.00 D) that applies high-frequency, low-energy electric current to shrink corneal collagen. This steepens the central cornea to create a hyperprolate shape, thus increasing the refractive power. It is based the effect of on heat on cornea’s biomechanical properties. The probe is inserted at optical zones of 6.0, 7.0, or 8.0 mm circumference, determined by a preset nomogram, for 8, 16, 24, or 32 spots.[62]

Stahl[9] showed safe and effective results at 1 year in a case series of emmetropic presbyopic patients with CK in their nondominant eye. Ninety percent of patients achieved a mean UNVA of J1, and 100% achieved a UNVA of J3 or better. Treated eyes lost a mean of 2.2 lines ±2.0 of UDVA, but gained 8.7 ± 2.0 lines of UNVA. Effects were sustained at the 1-year follow-up. However, CK tends to have significant regression over time and due to its lack of stability, it is not a very popular treatment for presbyopia at present.[63]

B. Scleral correction procedures

Schachar’s theory proposes that presbyopia is caused by progressive growth in the lens equatorial diameter and not by the increase in stiffness of the lens.[64] Restoration of zonular tension lost due to the supposed increased growth of the lens diameter is done by expanding the sclera. This can be achieved by making radial slits in the sclera (radial sclerotomy) or by insertion of PMMA scleral bands into scleral tunnel incisions overlying the ciliary muscle. However, scleral surgical procedures are also prone to complications such as thin scleral pockets and extrusion of the bands, anterior chamber perforation, ischemia, scleral thinning, and axial myopia.[656667]

Scleral interventions for correcting presbyopia remain controversial, but newer laser scleral micro-excisions and scleral micro-inserts are in development. These include VisAbility Micro-Insert scleral implant (Refocus Group, Dallas, TX, USA), Scleral Laser Anterior Ciliary Excision (LaserACE, Ace Vision Group, Newark, CA, USA), and scleral laser micro-excision.[64]

C. Lens-based procedures:

Refractive lens exchange

RLE is the surgical procedure in which the natural crystalline lens is removed and replaced by an IOL. It uses the principles of monovision, multifocal IOL, extended DOF IOL, and enhanced monofocals or accommodative IOL to correct for both distance and near vision.[2] RLE is an excellent choice in the presence of even early lenticular changes. Each of the options mentioned above has its benefits and limitations, but shall not elaborated as it is outside the purview of this review.

Presbyopic phakic intraocular contact lens

A presbyopic phakic intraocular contact lens in the posterior chamber is a recent advance for lens-based management of presbyopia. Schmid and Luedtke[68] demonstrated that patients had good visual acuity and were independent of spectacles for far and near distances in 16 eyes. There was no significant change in intraocular pressure, no significant endothelial cell loss, or complaints of halo or glare. The overall patient satisfaction was high, but longer-term data is needed to assess the outcomes of this intervention.

Conclusion

Presbyopia correction is considered the holy grail of refractive surgery. There is a substantial unmet need for adequate and accessible treatment of presbyopia, as it dramatically impacts QoL. Many methods and procedures are described for correcting presbyopia, but almost all of them have certain drawbacks and advantages. The ideal presbyopia correcting procedure should not cause any drop in distant vision, and it should improve functional vision to adequate levels without inducing collateral symptoms. It should be minimally invasive, easy to perform, stable over time, and reversible or adjustable. However, every presbyopia treatment is a compromise and can reduce contrast sensitivity or drop in distant visual acuity. Hence, thorough preoperative evaluation and patient assessment for subjective management of presbyopia is critical. Patients may have to adapt to this new situation and thus have to be counseled accordingly, with an emphasis on the long-term stability and retreatment rates for the available procedures for the treatment of presbyopia.

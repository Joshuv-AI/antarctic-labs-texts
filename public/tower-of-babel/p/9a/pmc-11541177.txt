The 30th Annual Scientific Meeting of the Japanese Association of Cardiac Rehabilitation

The 30th Annual Scientific Meeting of the Japanese Association of Cardiac Rehabilitation (JACR) was held over the 2 days from Saturday, July 13 to Sunday, July 14, 2024, at the Kobe International Exhibition Hall and the Kobe International Conference Center. The JACR was established in 1995, since when it has grown massively to more than 16,000 regular members. It was a great honor for me to serve as the congress chairperson of this historic Annual Meeting, which was attended by as many as 5,609 participants.

The theme of the meeting was “Cardiac Rehabilitation as Science”, with a focus on the scientific mechanism by which cardiac rehabilitation improves the prognosis of patients. The 30 program committee members proposed and organized so many sessions. There were 201 oral and 370 poster presentations; 4 special lectures; the Young Investigator’s Award competition with 6 wonderful presentations; the medical team competition session with 6 presentations; 12 symposiums, 23 panel discussions, 2 workshops, 12 joint symposiums with other scientific societies; and 26 basic educational lectures on various areas related to cardiac rehabilitation. In addition, there were 3 “How to” sessions, 4 exhibition booth design seminars, 2 special sessions for nurses, 2 special sessions for pharmacists, and a U-40 congestive heart failure network session that was organized by young investigators under 40 years of age. The final program covered all aspects of cardiac rehabilitation, including rehabilitation in the maintenance period, work-style reform, end-of-life care, home medical care, and telemedicine (Figure 1). A special event was a symposium about cardiac rehabilitation in space. Because so many programs were held at the same time, video on-demand delivery was made available for selected sessions. The meeting was international. Professor Michael Papadakis (St. George’s University of London, UK), Professor Ana Abreu (Hospital de Santa Maria Faculty of Medicine, Portugal), and Professor Roland Nebel (Klinik Roderbirken Cardiology, Germany) gave invited lectures. The 30th anniversary JACR-EAPC (European Association of Preventive Cardiology) Joint Session was attended by Professor Lino Manuel Ribeiro Patrício (Hospital Espírito Santo in Ĕvora at the University of Ĕvora) and Professor Dominique Hansen (Hasselt University, Belgium). The AsiaPRevent session was bustling, with the JACR-AP/TACVPR (Taiwan Academy of Cardiovascular and Pulmonary Rehabilitation) joint session, 3 special lectures, and 21 oral and 29 poster presentations from Taiwan, China, Malaysia, the Philippines, and Indonesia. To facilitate communication, JACR adopted simultaneous translation systems.

Saturday night was a social gathering (Figure 2) at which members enjoyed meeting and chatting informally, and experiencing traditional Awa dancing, after a short lecture. Awa is an old name for Tokushima, and Awa dancing (Awa odori, in Japanese) is very popular throughout Japan. I invited about 60 members of the most famous dancing team, “Gojahei” from Tokushima, to perform in Kobe. Gojahei is the biggest Awaodori ren (group), having more than 300 dancers. Their dynamic but authentic dance style, accompanied by a big band of drums, bells, flutes, and shamisen (Japanese guitar) impressed the audience.

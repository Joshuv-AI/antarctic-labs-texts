Identification of hsa-miR-193a-5p-SURF4 axis related to the gut microbiota-metabolites- cytokines in lung cancer based on Mendelian randomization study and bioinformatics analysis
Authors: Jie Yu, Sibo Meng, Tiantian Xuan, Zhanmei Wang, Linli Qu, Fangli Cao, Jiaxin Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLung cancer is a significant disease that affects people's physical and mental health. Currently, the treatment outcomes still do not meet clinical needs, and the causes of the disease are still unclear, therefore further exploration is needed.MethodsWe analyzed the exposure factors of lung cancer, including gut microbiota, serum metabolites, and cytokines, through Mendelian randomization studies and bioinformatics analysis. We identified common SNPs and performed gene annotation, leading to the discovery of the key gene SURF4, which may affect the onset of lung cancer. We validated the oncogenic function and mechanism of SURF4 through public data analysis using GO and KEGG, and constructed a ceRNA network, revealing the lung cancer oncogenic pathway involving lncRNA/pseudogene-microRNA-SURF4.ResultsWe first conducted a Mendelian randomization analysis on 418 gut microbiota, 1400 serum metabolites, and 41 cytokines in relation to lung cancer. We found that 16 gut microbiota, 29 serum metabolites, and 2 cytokines were closely associated with lung cancer. Further comparison of all differential SNPs revealed that rs550057 on chromosome 9 was a common SNP among these three exposure factors, indicating its crucial role in lung cancer formation. Through gene functional annotation using R language, we found that the expression of 15 genes, including SURF4, was influenced by rs550057. By querying these 15 genes from public databases for their differential expression and prognosis in lung cancer, we found significant differences in SURF4, MED22, and RPL7A. Furthermore, by querying the expression and correlation coefficients of upstream microRNAs of these three genes through the starBase website, we found that hsa-miR-193a-5p-SURF4 had the most significant effect on lung cancer. Through GO and KEGG analysis of SURF4-related genes, we identified the molecular pathways associated metabolic synthesis and microbial infection related to the promotion of lung cancer by SURF4. This validated the results of the previous Mendelian randomization study. Furthermore, we constructed a ceRNA network for SURF4 and identified two upstream differentially expressed pseudogenes and nine lncRNAs, confirming the functionality of the pseudogene/lncRNA-microRNA-SUFR4 pathway.ConclusionsIn summary, we have elucidated the regulatory role of the pseudogene/lncRNA-microRNA-SUFR4 pathway in the progression of lung cancer, combining the research hotspots of gut microbiota-serum metabolites-cytokines. We have also confirmed the pathway and mechanism through SURF4 and its related genes promoting lung cancer formation. This may provide effective therapeutic methods for lung cancer and serve as a potential prognostic marker.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01359-5.

Introduction

Globally, lung cancer is responsible for the major common types of cancer worldwide the highest number of cancer-related deaths [1]. In the past few decades, there have been some advancements in the treatment of lung cancer, leading to a decrease in both incidence and mortality rates. On one hand, this can be attributed to the improvement in people's healthy lifestyle habits, such as reducing tobacco consumption, emphasizing early screening for lung cancer [2] and applying artificial intelligence (AI) approaches widely in lung cancer management [3]. On the other hand, it is closely linked to the progress in tumor treatment methods, like surgery, radiation, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, or molecularly targeted therapy [4], but these options impose significant financial and physical burdens on patients and their families. Therefore, more clinical researches are urgent needed to further enhance the effectiveness by providing precise treatment methods to lung cancer patients.

The gut microbiota has been a research focus in recent years, and it has been found that the occurrence and efficacy of many tumors are closely related to dysbiosis of the microbiota. There are numerous articles on the influence of gut microbiota on tumor occurrence and prognosis. The gut microbiota is closely linked to colorectal cancer (CRC) and plays a significant role in the tumor microenvironment. It enables interaction between the colonic epithelia, immune cells, and the host through the release of various metabolites, proteins, and macromolecules that regulate the development of CRC. Additionally, both preclinical and clinical evidence highlight the gut microbiota's influence on modifying the therapeutic responses of CRC patients to chemotherapy and immunotherapy [5]. The clinical treatment of HCC immune checkpoint inhibitors can be enhanced by modifying bacteria, altering short-chain fatty acids, decreasing infiltration of IL-17A-producing ILC3 [6]. The microbial organism is involved in specific immune recognition by identification of microbial target antigens for tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs)in immune reactivity against glioblastoma, which offers promising prospects for personalized tumor vaccination strategies in the future [7]. The "Microbiota-gut-lymphoma axis" presents an intriguing pathway through which microbiota can contribute to the development of lymphoma, as well as an opportunity for intervention. Furthermore, gut microbiota holds immense potential as a robust diagnostic instrument for natural killer/T-cell lymphoma [8]. The exploration of therapeutic avenues involving modulation of the gut microbiome, exemplified by probiotics and fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT), has yielded encouraging outcomes in improving renal cancer prognosis [9].

The tumor microenvironment (TME) key in tumor progression & drug resistance; metabolic changes alter TME, influencing TAMs. M1 macrophages use aerobic glycolysis, while M2 favor oxidative metabolism. Consequently, metabolites present within the tumor microenvironment exert regulatory influence over the function and polarization status of tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) [10]. The metabolites generated by the gut microbiota play a significant role in the initiation and progression of colorectal cancer (CRC). Harmful metabolites include certain primary bile acids and short-chain fatty acids, while beneficial metabolites include ursodeoxycholic acid and butyrate. The different metabolites have varying effects on tumor occurrence and progression [11]. A comprehensive metabolome-wide association study (MWAS) delved into the intricate relationship between genetic determinants of blood metabolite concentrations and the predisposition to pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC). By integrating gut microbiota information and conducting two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis, the relationship between serum metabolites, gut microbiota characteristics, and PDAC was investigated. The flavonoid-degrading bacterium, flavonoid factor sp90199495, has been identified as being associated with the metabolite X-21849 and further demonstrated a correlation with the risk of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) [12]. Cytokines, as secreted signaling molecules, are crucial players in initiating, sustaining, and resolving immune responses. Their distinctive capacity to modulate immune function holds profound promise in the realm of cancer biology and therapy. For example, hypoxia-inducible factor 1 alpha (HIF1α) induced by hypoxia serves as a regulatory factor for the heterogeneity of cancer-associated fibroblasts and a promoter of tumor progression in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma [13]. This indicates that different inflammatory factors have different mechanisms of onset for distinct classifications of breast cancer [14]. The above results indicate that the relationship between the microbiota discovered in previous studies and tumors may be mediated by changes in metabolites or cytokines during the onset of the disease. Hence, a comprehensive investigation into the intricate interplay between gut microbiota, metabolites, and cytokines is imperative.

Exploring lung cancer's potential link with gut microbiota, metabolites, & cytokines, we used Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis, a GWAS-based statistical approach adhering to Mendelian inheritance, simulating an RCT. MR widely applied in exposure-outcome studies. This study employed two-sample MR to assess gut microbiota-metabolite-cytokine impact on lung cancer. Findings enhance lung cancer etiology understanding & inform treatment strategies.

Materials and methods

Study design

We utilized Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis to investigate the causal relationship between 418 gut microbiota, 1400 serum metabolites, 41 cytokines, and lung cancer. Additionally, we identified key oncogenes that play a critical role in lung cancer by annotating the common different SNPs among the microbiota, metabolites, and cytokines. Furthermore, through the analysis of the Competing Endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network of annotated genes, we discovered upstream microRNAs (miRNAs), pseudogenes, and long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs). This pathway discovery served as a valuable reference and provided direction for future research. Further exploration in these areas could lead to the development of personalized treatment strategies and preventive measures for managing lung cancer. In MR analysis, instrumental variables (IVs) are used to indicate risk factors by leveraging genetic variation. To ensure the validity of causal inference, these IVs must satisfy three crucial assumptions: (1) genetic variation has a direct association with the exposure; (2) genetic variation is not correlated with potential confounding factors between the exposure and outcome; (3) genetic variation does not impact the outcome through pathways other than the exposure.

Data sources

In order to enhance the statistical power, validate research findings, and explore potential new associations, we utilized a genome-wide association study (GWAS) dataset called ebi-a-GCST004748. This dataset which contains a comprehensive and free amount of data, and easily accessible by researchers, consists of European patients and controls, including a total of 29,266 lung cancer patients, among which 11,273 cases were confirmed as lung adenocarcinoma, 7,426 cases as lung squamous cell carcinoma, 2,664 cases as small cell carcinoma, and 91% of the patients had a history of smoking, as well as 56,450 controls. Through this, we were able to identify a total of 7,857,154 independent single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs). For the exposed data, we carefully selected the most comprehensive information available, including data on 418 microbial species, 41 cytokines, and 1400 serum metabolites. It is important to note that we obtained this data from summary data of previously published studies and publicly available GWAS. As a result, we did not require any additional ethical approval or consent for our research.

Instrumental variable selection

In order to ensure the reliability of the results and the robustness of the data, we conducted quality control on the SNPs to obtain instrumental variables that meet the necessary requirements based on recent research. Firstly, we reviewed relevant studies and set the p-value to 1 × 10–5 to facilitate accurate comparison and avoid missing subtle differences. Secondly, we performed linkage disequilibrium analysis with a threshold of r2 < 0.1 and a distance of 500 kb to address the potential bias caused by strong linkage disequilibrium. Thirdly, we excluded SNPs with palindrome sequences and inconsistent alleles to ensure the consistency and accuracy of the data. Lastly, to evaluate the strength of the SNPs, we considered F ≥ 10 as evidence of a lack of strong instrumental bias.

Statistical analysis

This paper assessed the impact of gut microbiota, serum metabolites, and cytokines single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) on the occurrence of lung cancer using Mendelian randomization methods. Key genes were identified through gene annotation, and their upstream regulatory networks were explored using bioinformatics analysis websites. The flowchart was shown in Fig. 1. To determine the statistical significance of the results obtained from IVW, we considered the presence of horizontal pleiotropy and heterogeneity. We regarded data with a corrected p-value (FDR) below 0.05 as having a strong and significant causal relationship. However, to avoid overlooking subtle differences and considering the small variations in gut microbiota itself, we conducted further investigations on all data with a p-value less than 0.05. Our objective was not only to identify causal relationships through Mendelian randomization but also to identify SNPs and their functional genes that could simultaneously impact microbiota, metabolism, and cytokines in lung cancer. Therefore, it was necessary to include as many differentially expressed SNPs as possible for the MR analysis. We utilized R software (Version 4.2.2) and various R packages including "TwoSampleMR", "MR-PRESSO", "vautils", "dplyr", and "ggplot2" to perform gene annotation analysis on the genes located within a 100 kb range near the differentially expressed SNP. The purpose of this analysis was to identify the functional genes that are most likely affected by this SNP. Furthermore, we conducted differential expression analysis and survival analysis in lung cancer using these functional genes identified through gene annotation. The most significant functional gene was selected for KEGG pathway analysis and Gene Ontology (GO) analysis. The GO analysis encompassed molecular function (MF), biological process (BP), and cellular component (CC). To gain further insights into the underlying mechanisms, we conducted bioinformatics analysis and performed ceRNA network analysis by starBase, UALCAN, GEPIA, miRNet websites. miRNA prediction was validated by seven databases, PITA (https://genie.weizmann.ac.il/pubs/mir07/mir07_dyn_data.html), RNA22 (https://cm.jefferson.edu/rna22/), miRmap (https://mirmap.ezlab.org/), microT (http://www.microrna.gr/webServer), miRanda (http://www.microrna.org/microrna/home.do), PicTar (http://www.pictar.org/) and TargetScan (www.targetscan.org/). This allowed us to identify microRNAs, lncRNAs, and pseudogenes that regulate the upstream processes. For this analysis, we utilized R packages such as "clusterProfiler", "stats", "survival", and "ggplot2". Results

Relationship between gut microbiota and lung cancer based on MR study

A two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis was conducted, and the inverse variance weighted (IVW) method was employed as the primary analytical approach. Initially, we applied false discovery rate (FDR) correction to 418 gut microbiota species. However, to prevent having too few immune cells remaining after FDR correction, which could potentially impact subsequent analysis outcomes, we adopted the raw p-value threshold of < 0.05 to identify significant changes and proceed with further analysis (the same approach applies to subsequent analyses). The entire cohort of lung cancer patients from the GWAS data ebi-a-GCST004748 was used as the “outcome factors”, 418 gut microbiota were used as “exposure factors”. From the IVW results, it could be seen that there were statistically significant changes in 16 Gut Microbiota in lung cancer. Among them, 11 were protective factors, and 5 were carcinogenic factors, each microbial community was associated with approximately 6 to 15 SNPs. Relationship between metabolites and lung cancer based on MR study

Previous literature had shown a close relationship between serum metabolites and tumors, but mechanistically, it had been found that metabolites had a more direct causal relationship with tumors. We used the same GWAS full-set dataset mentioned above as the outcome data, and 1400 serum metabolites as the exposure factors, to investigate which serum metabolites are associated with the development of lung cancer. The results revealed that among 1400 serum metabolites, it could be seen that there were statistically significant changes (raw p-value < 0.05) in 29 serum metabolites in lung cancer. Among them, 16 were protective factors, and 13 were carcinogenic factors, every metabolite was associated with approximately 3 to 9 SNPs. Relationship between cytokines and lung cancer based on MR study

In order to identify the affected cytokines, we conducted Mendelian randomization analysis on the data of the same GWAS full-set dataset mentioned above as the outcome factors, with 41 validation cytokines as the exposure factors. Gene annotation and survival analysis of candidate genes in lung cancer

A Mendelian randomization analysis was conducted on 418 gut microbiota, 1400 serum metabolites, and 41 cytokines, revealing 232, 147, and 48 different SNPs, respectively, the detailed data was listed in Supplementary Table 4–6. Upon comparison, SNP (rs550057) was found to be a shared SNP among the three exposures, suggesting its potential association with the development of lung cancer through gut microbiota, metabolites, and secreted cytokines. Further investigation through the NCBI website revealed that SNP (rs550057) was located on chromosome 9 at position 133271182. Through gene functional annotation using R software, we expanded the range to 100 kb to find more related genes. We discovered 15 genes, namely: ASS1, HMCN2, ABO, C9orf96, MED22, OBP2B, RPL7A, SNORD24, SNORD36A, SNORD36B, SNORD36C, SURF1, SURF2, SURF4, SURF6. By analyzing the starBase website (http://starbase.sysu.edu.cn/), it was found that out of the 15 genes mentioned, 5 genes have upstream microRNAs (Fig. 5A). Since our GWAS database does not distinguish between lung cancer subtypes, we utilized the The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) dataset to compare gene expression differences. The survival curve-selected data comprises RNAseq data from 1149 lung cancer cases without distinguishing subtypes. Through the “Xiantao website” (www.xiantaozi.com), we employed the minimum P-value grouping method to analyze the association between the expression of the aforementioned genes and the prognosis of lung cancer. Ultimately, three genes (SURF4, MED22, RPL7A) were found to be statistically significant correlated with lung cancer prognosis (Fig. 5B-F).Fig. 5Genes annotation associated with lung cancer prognosis from the rs550057. Discussion

Through Mendelian randomization analysis, we have gained insights into the mechanisms underlying potential pathological changes in many diseases, including the associations between plasma proteins [15] and venous thrombosis, which may even be linked to pulmonary embolism [16]. Our current study emphasizes the combined analysis of the impacts of gut microbiota, serum metabolites, and cytokines on the occurrence of lung cancer. Furthermore, by comparing differential SNPs, we found that some SNPs are not only associated with one exposure, suggesting potential interactions among gut microbiota, serum metabolites, and cytokines in the process of lung cancer formation. This discovery offered new perspectives on comprehending the mechanisms underlying the development of lung cancer. Simultaneously, it served as a valuable resource for enhancing the efficacy of lung cancer treatment approaches.

Gene annotation was performed on the shared SNPs, and it was discovered that the protein-coding gene SURF4 may be located near the SNP rs550057 and influenced by it. Several studies had reported a close association between SURF4 and tumors. SURF4 was highly expressed in T4, invasive ductal carcinoma, ER-negative, PR-negative, HER2 overexpressing, and tumor high-burden patients. It had independent prognostic value for overall survival (OS), and SURF4 promoted proliferation and migration of breast cancer cells [17]. SURF4 had the ability to maintain the stemness of cancer stem cells through BIRC3, making it a potential target for targeted therapy of ovarian cancer stem cells [18]. In vitro studies had shown that overexpression of SURF4 could lead to cell proliferation, migration, and anchorage-independent growth. Additionally, overexpression of SURF4 induced tumor growth in mice using NIH3T3 cells [19]. SURF4 played a crucial role in suppressing myeloid differentiation and preventing cell death in myeloid leukemia cells by negatively regulating the STING-TBK1-STAT6 axis. Monoclonal antibodies targeting SURF4, could be a promising therapeutic approach for treating hematological malignancies [20]. Our research had found that SRUF4 might be associated with gut microbiota-serum metabolites- cytokines in lung cancer, increasing our understanding of the function of SRUF4 and providing new directions for future research. In particular, our KEGG analysis of the SURF4-related genes revealed a significant enrichment in substance synthesis and microbial infection, suggesting that the function of SURF4 might be underestimated and further research was needed to validate this.

MicroRNAs were a class of short non-coding RNA molecules that play important roles in gene expression regulation. miRNAs regulate the transcription and translation processes of target genes by binding to the mRNA of these genes. This regulation includes: post-transcriptional regulation, gene expression regulation, and protein interaction regulation [21]. TCGA and GEO data revealed that hsa-miR-193a-5p, along with other microRNAs, could accurately predict malignant tumor recurrence in HCC patients after surgery and transplantation, and also had value in prognostic evaluation of HCC patients [22]. Bioinformatics analysis revealed that hsa-miR-193a-5p was upregulated in endometrial cancer and could serve as a prognostic biomarker or therapeutic target [23]. hsa-miR-193a-5p was expressed at higher levels in extracellular vehicles (EVs) ranging from 50–220 nm derived from malignant pleural mesothelioma (MPM). These miRNAs downregulated the expression of several genes involved in cell–cell interaction and cell–matrix interaction in normal pleural mesothelial cells. The presence of hsa-miR-193a-5p in EVs with a size of 50–220 nm, might serve as a diagnostic biomarker for MPM [24]. Dysregulation of lncRNAs such as HCG16, CASC9, SNHG16, HAND2-AS1, and NR2F1-AS1 could alter the expression of miRNAs such as hsa-miR-193a-5p, thereby affecting the occurrence and development of colorectal cancer in GEO dataset [25]. Currently, most of these studies were bioinformatics research results, and none of them had been found to play a role in lung cancer. Our findings provided new evidence for understanding the function of microRNA.

As part of the ceRNA research, we not only predicted the upstream microRNAs of SURF4, but also identified two pseudogenes, AC021074.1 and C1QBPP2, upstream of SURF4 through the starBase website. Additionally, we found a series of lncRNAs, including HEIH, PVT1, EBLN3P, KCNQ1OT1, SNHG1, SNHG16, SNHG20, LINC00205, and XIST, through both the starBase and miRNet websites. XIST acted as a nuclear sponge for microRNA let-7a-2-3p, leading to the activation of IL-6 production of breast cancer (BC) cells [26]. The tumor development in papillary thyroid cancer (PTC) was facilitated by lncRNA XIST through its regulation of the miR-330-3p/PDE5A axis [27]. The presence of HEIH in certain cancers was indicative of a reduced overall survival rate, advanced clinical stage, and larger tumor size. Therefore, HEIH had significant promise as a prognostic indicator for individuals with cancer [28]. PVT1 was a specific type of lncRNA and had a significant impact on the occurrence and progression of breast cancer (BC). Due to its role in promoting cancer cell growth, metastasis, and invasion, PVT1 was an attractive therapeutic target for BC and might also have diagnostic value in BC [29]. The recruitment of HuR by EBLN3P enhanced the stability and protein levels of ANXA3 mRNA, thereby increasing the proliferative/migratory/invasive potential of Osteosarcoma cells and providing a novel potential therapeutic target for clinical treatment of Osteosarcoma [30]. KCNQ1OT1/miR-339-3p/KIF23 had the potential to serve as a novel biomarker for the diagnosis and treatment of retinoblastoma. KCNQ1OT1 inhibited its oncogenic function by promoting the expression of KIF23 and miR-339-3p [31]. The expression of miR-424-5p was suppressed by LncRNA SNHG1, leading to enhanced proliferation, invasion, and migration of Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) cells [32]. In conclusion, these upstream lncRNAs on SRUF4 might also be involved in the formation of lung cancer.

Numerous articles have been published on the roles of gut microbiota, metabolites, and cytokines in the development of lung cancer, including some highly influential ones. For instance, diallyl trisulfide inhibits 4-(methylnitrosamino)-1-(3-pyridyl)-1-butanone-induced lung cancer by modulating gut microbiota and the PPARγ/NF-κb pathway [33]. Another study highlights the impact of the lung-gut microbiome axis on PD-L1 treatment efficacy [34]. The Faecalibacterium prausnitzii strain EXL01 has been found to enhance the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors in lung cancer patients [35]. Short-chain fatty acids induce lung tumor cell death and increase peripheral blood CD4 + T cells in patients with non-small cell lung cancer [36]. Furthermore, the metabolism of branched-chain amino acids promotes epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and brain metastasis in non-small cell lung cancer by regulating ALKBH5 activity [37]. Glutamine regulates blood copper levels in immune cells of patients with lung adenocarcinoma [38]. Extracellular vesicle delivery of IL-12 can treat lung cancer and promote systemic immunity [39]. An IL6-STAT3-C/EBPβ-IL6 positive feedback loop in tumor-associated macrophages promotes EMT and metastasis in lung adenocarcinoma [40]. Additionally, lactate-induced tumor-associated fibroblast activation and IL-8-mediated macrophage recruitment contribute to lung cancer progression [41]. Our study, through bioinformatics analysis and Mendelian randomization research, might reveal a lung cancer formation gene pathway related to gut microbiota-serum metabolites-cytokines, providing new references for subsequent research.

Limitation: First, our study selected the largest dataset of lung cancer from the GWAS and TCGA databases, with a large number of patients. We did not further distinguish the subtypes of lung cancer, so our results are valid for the entire lung cancer population. As research on lung cancer progresses, patient stratification may become more refined, and we have not confirmed whether special subtypes yield the same conclusions. Second, our outcome data only included GWAS data, primarily because GWAS data is freely available and easy to access. We did not involve other databases for verification. The advantage is that the data is easily verifiable and can be further explored by subsequent researchers. However, the disadvantage is that GWAS primarily contains data from European patients, which may not be directly applicable to populations in other regions of the world. Third, the nature of Mendelian randomization studies determines that experimental validation is challenging. If we only validate the bioinformatics analysis part, it cannot confirm our main research findings. Therefore, we did not conduct experimental validation in this study. In the future, we will use other articles and molecular biology experiments to demonstrate some of the conclusions of this article.

Conclusions

In general, our study identified a common variant, SNP rs550057, through a Mendelian randomization study of the gut microbiota-serum metabolite-cytokine axis. We confirmed through gene annotation and survival analysis that the SNP most likely affected the gene SURF4. We found that it influenced the formation of lung cancer through metabolic and infectious pathways. Additionally, through bioinformatics analysis, we discovered the ceRNA network of SURF4, which could serve as a potential target for future treatment or a prognostic marker.

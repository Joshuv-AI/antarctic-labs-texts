Prognostic values of left atrial strain analyzed by four-dimensional speckle tracking echocardiography in uremia with preserved ejection fraction
Authors: Lei Li, Chunyuan You, Lai Zhang, Jun Yang, Yang Wang, Pingyang Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractLittle is known about the prognostic value of left atrial strain by four-dimensional speckle-tracking echocardiography in end-stage renal disease patients with preserved left ventricular ejection fraction. This prospective study collected clinical and echocardiographic data from 80 stable dialysis patients (mean age 57 ± 10 years; 62.5% men). All patients underwent the dedicated four-dimensional speckle-tracking echocardiography to measure LASr (peak longitudinal strain of reservoir function), LAScd (peak longitudinal strain of conduit function), LASct (peak longitudinal strain of contractile function), LASr_c (peak circumferential strain of reservoir function), LAScd_c (peak circumferential strain of conduit function) and LASct_c (peak circumferential strain of contractile function). These patients were enrolled from August 2021 to August 2023 and followed-up for 19 months (interquartile-range 15 to 20 months). The primary outcome was a composite of all-cause mortality or major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs). The study patients were classified into event (developed mortality or MACEs) and event-free group according to the primary outcome. Multivariate Cox regression analysis was used to investigate risk factors for all-cause mortality or MACEs. The event group had lower LASr (16.4% vs. 21.2%, P = 0.0003), LASct (8.2% vs. 11.2%, P = 0.01), LASr_c (25.2% vs. 35.0%, P = 0.0004) and LASct_c (14.9% vs. 20.9%, P = 0.001) than the event-free group. Using optimal cut-off value determined by ROC curve, the less LASr (LASr < 18.5%), LASct (LASct < 8.5%), LASr_c (LASr_c < 28.5%), and LASct_c (LASct_c < 17.5%) group had a higher mortality or MACEs rate. Multivariate cox regression analyses revealed that LASr (HR = 0.81, 95% CI [0.17; 0.91], P = 0.0005, per 1% increase) and LASr_c (HR = 0.93, 95% CI [0.87; 0.98], P = 0.01, per 1% increase) were independent predictors of all-cause mortality or MACEs. Introduction

Patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) are at a higher risk of cardiovascular or cerebrovascular events, which may increase global health burden or shorten their lifespan. Left ventricular (LV) global longitudinal strain (GLS), left atrial volume index (LAVI) and left atrial strains (LAS) were shown to predict the adverse events in ESRD patients and promote early medical intervention1–4. Ayer A demonstrated that left atrial reservoir strain and conduit strain using two-dimensional speckle-tracking echocardiography (2DSTE) were independently associated with major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs) among patients with ESRD3.

Acquiring adequate 2D images for 2DSTE was often difficult by plane through phenomenon. Moreover, noises from the one frame of one segment influence 2DSTE images more than 4DSTE (four-dimensional speckle-tracking echocardiography) images5. Four-dimensional(4D) STE has recently been developed and increases accuracy without any geometrical assumptions6,7. Furthermore, 4DSTE provides simultaneous analysis of LA volumes and function due to its availability, shorter measurement time, and reproducibility5,8. Previous studies demonstrated that LA parameters calculated by 4DSTE better predicted future MACEs than those calculated by 2DSTE5,9. As we know, LA myocardial fibers are arranged in both longitudinal and circumferential direction10. The prognostic value of LA longitudinal strain in ESRD patients is well established and clearly associated with functional abnormalities. However, the prognostic information of LA circumferential strain in ESRD patients is not entirely understood. This study aimed to apply the dedicated left atrial 4DSTE to determine the prognostic value of longitudinal and circumferential left atrial strains on the risk of all-cause mortality and cardiovascular events in ESRD patients with preserved left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF).

Materials and methods

Study cohort

Patients who were willing to participate in this study were enrolled from August 2021 to January 2022. These participants had been on regular dialysis treatment for at least 3 months (median duration of regular dialysis treatment 24 months; interquartile range 12–48 months). The inclusion and exclusion criteria were showed in Fig. 1.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria. LVEF: left ventricular ejection fraction.

Hemodialysis patients were being treated thrice weekly with adequate clearance. The average Kt/v, interdialytic gain weight and urea reduction ratio in these patients were 1.42 ± 0.23, 4.22% ± 1.32% and 70.71% ± 5.66%, respectively. Patients with chronic ambulatory peritoneal dialysis were on four exchanges per day with standard dialysis bags. The average Kt/v, interdialytic gain weight and urea reduction ratio in peritoneal dialysis patients were 1.58 ± 0.21, 4.52% ± 1.61% and 72.94% ± 6.07%. Clinical information on comorbidities, medical history, and current cardiovascular medication were gathered by a careful review of each patient's medical record and a self-reported questionnaire. Patients' compliance with medication instructions was routinely checked.

Blood was collected immediately before the midweek dialysis session. Serum calcium, phosphate, parathyroid hormone, albumin, cholesterol, cardiac troponin T, high-sensitivity C-reactive protein, homocysteine levels were measured using routine methods. Hemoglobin level was also tested.

Follow-up

During the follow-up phase (August 2021–August 2023) the medical records of the enrolled participants were thoroughly examined. The primary outcome was the composite of all-cause mortality or MACEs, such as ischemic events, including admission due to angina and coronary revascularization, and HF hospitalization. The duration of follow-up was 19 months (15–20 months).

Echocardiography measurements

The left atrial strains are volume dependent, and there have been several studies that show LA strains decrease after fluid removal during dialysis11,12. For this reason, we evaluated strain parameters on the day after the first dialysis of the week. All the patients were examined in the left lateral decubitus position by one skilled cardiologist, using an ultrasound system with a 2-to 4.5-MHz M5Sc 2D and a 2.5-to 4-MHz 4Vc 4D probe (Vivid E95; GE Medical Systems, Horten, Norway). According to the recommendations of the American Society of Echocardiography13, quantifications of LV mass index (LVMI), interventricular septum thickness, left ventricular posterior wall thickness, LV end-diastolic diameter (LVEDD), and LVEF were performed. The LV mass was calculated using the 2D-guided M-mode method (Devereux formula) and was corrected for the body surface area. left ventricular hypertrophy(LVH)was defined as LVMI > 115 g/m2 for men and > 95 g/m2 for women. At the septal and lateral mitral annuli, pulse tissue Doppler imaging velocities were recorded, including the peak systolic (s') and early diastolic (e') velocities. The ratio of early transmitral flow to early diastolic mitral annular velocity (E/ e') was calculated from the average of the septal and lateral e'.

Two-dimensional grayscale imaging in three standard apical views (i.e., apical 4-chamber, apical 2-chamber, and apical long-axis) for three cardiac cycles were obtained and stored digitally with a frame rate of 50–90 frames per second for later off-line analysis.

Four-dimensional volume transducer was used to obtain images in an apical 4-chamber view which displayed LA walls clearly. Images of three cardiac cycles were acquired with a 4D frame rate > 40% of the heart rate.

Echocardiography analyses

Using automated function imaging software (EchoPAC workstation, Version204; GE Medical Systems, Horten, Norway), offline image analysis was performed by two cardiologists (blinded to the patients’ clinical data).

Global longitudinal strain and peak systolic dispersion were measured using two-dimensional speckle tracking strain technology. The end-systolic frame of the 2D images from the apical 4-, 3-, and 2-chamber views clearly displayed the endocardial borders. The operator manually corrected any segments that didn't track. The mean strain of all apical views was used to calculate LV GLS. The standard deviation (SD) of LV 17 segment time to peak longitudinal strain was used to measure peak strain dispersion (PSD). Additionally, LV GLS was expressed as absolute measure values.

Four-dimensional LA automated function imaging software was used to analyze the LA size and strains. Setting landmarks and adjusting the mitral valve center on every plane at end-systole clearly displayed the mitral annulus and LA walls. The software presented the following standard 4D parameters: LAVmin (minimal volume, mL), LAVmax (maximum volume, mL), LAVpreA (volume at pretrial contraction, mL), LAEV(emptying volume, mL), LAEF (emptying fraction, %), LASr (peak longitudinal strain of reservoir function, %), LAScd (peak longitudinal strain of conduit function, %), LASct (peak longitudinal strain of contractile function,%), LASr_c (peak circumferential strain of reservoir function), LAScd_c (peak circumferential strain of conduit function, %), LASct_c (peak circumferential strain of contractile function,%) (Fig. 2). All volume measurements were indexed to body surface area. Additionally, the reservoir, conduit, and contractile phases of LA longitudinal and circumferential strains were measured respectively and expressed as absolute measures.

Left atrial strain and volume derived dedicated left atrial four-dimensional automated function imaging software.

Statistical analysis

Continuous data are presented as the means ± SDs, while dichotomous data are presented as numbers and percentages. For continuous variables that are and are not distributed normally, respectively, comparisons were performed using the t test and nonparametric test. For categorical variables, the chi squared test or Fisher's exact test was applied as necessary. To determine the optimal threshold of LA strain for the prediction of the endpoints, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was applied, and the areas under the curve (AUCs) and 95% confidence interval (CI)for LA strain were calculated. Patients were categorized into two groups based on the cut-off values for LA strain values determined by the ROC curve. The Kaplan–Meier method and a log-rank test were used to compare outcome rates. To analyze variables that are related to all-cause mortality or MACEs, a univariate Cox regression analysis was conducted. To investigate risk variables for mortality and MACEs, factors with P < 0.10 on univariate analysis were considered in the multivariate Cox regression analysis. The interclass correlation coefficient was used to evaluate intraobserver and interobserver variability for the measurement of four-dimensional parameters in two sets of 20 randomly selected subjects. A two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 20(IBM corporation, Armonk, NY) and SAS version 9.2(SAS Institute, Cary, NC).

Ethical approval and consent to participate

The study was conducted according to the guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki, and approved by the Ethics Committee of the Wuxi No.2 People’s Hospital. Each participant provided their informed consent.

Results

Patients

We prospectively screened 130 ESRD dialysis patients with preserved LVEF from our hospital. In accordance with the inclusion and exclusion criteria (Fig. 1), 80 patients were enrolled in the final analyses. No patient was lost to follow-up throughout the course of the trial.

Seventeen patients (21.25%) had all-cause mortality or MACEs: 4 from death and 13 from MACEs. The enrolled patients were stratified into two groups: event and event-free (Table 1). The event group (n = 17) had a higher rate of prevalent coronary artery disease (CAD) and higher homocysteine levels (Table 1).

Prognostic indictor stratification

To investigate the prognostic value of left atrial strain for all-cause mortality or MACEs in ESRD patients with preserved LVEF. Figure 3 shows the ROC curves for predicting the endpoints. The optimal cut-off value for LASr was 18.5% (sensitivity,0.76; 1-specificity,0.29; and AUC, 0.77 [95% CI 0.64–0.90]; P = 0.0006), The optimal cut-off value for LASct was 8.5% (sensitivity,0.76; 1-specificity,0.41; and AUC, 0.69 [95% CI 0.54–0.83]; P = 0.02), The optimal cut-off value for LASr_c was 28.5% (sensitivity, 0.74; 1-specificity,0.35; and AUC, 0.77 [95% CI 0.66–0.89]; P = 0.0005) and The optimal cut-off value for LASct_c was 17.5% (sensitivity, 0.59; 1-specificity, 0.23; and AUC, 0.69 [95% CI 0.57–0.81]; P = 0.02). However, ROC curves for LAScd and LAScd-c were not demonstrated to predict the endpoints.

Using univariate Cox regression analysis, prevalent CAD, LVEDD, LVEF, E/e' ratio, LV GLS, LA VminI, LA VpreAI, LAEF, LASr, LASct, LASr_c and LASct_c were significantly associated with the primary outcome (Table 3). However, because there were only 17 patients reaching the endpoint, many parameters in the multivariate Cox model might lead to an unstable estimate. Therefore, the significant parameters in univariate model were included for multivariate Cox regression analysis (Table 3). Because of the collinearity between LA longitudinal and circumferential strain, we did not include LA longitudinal and circumferential strain in a single multivariate Cox regression model. Using multivariate Cox regression analysis, prevalent CAD, LVEF and LASr were independent prognostic predictors in model1, and prevalent CAD, LV GLS and LASr_c were independent prognostic predictors in model 2 (Table 3).

Discussion

This is the first study to demonstrate that LASr_c is a prognostic predictor of all-cause mortality or MACEs in stable dialysis patients with preserved LVEF. We showed that LASr_c was the independent predictor of all-cause mortality or MACEs and those patients with less LASr_c (LASr_c < 28.5%) had a higher mortality or MACEs rate. Thus, we highlighted the clinical application of LASr_c for risk assessment in clinically stable dialysis patients with preserved LVEF.

The left atrial myocardial layer consists of the longitudinal and circumferential bundles10. Traditional 2DSTE merely measure LA longitudinal strain in previous studies14,15. Some researchers have utilized left ventricular three-dimensional speckle tracking echocardiography(3DSTE) to assess the left atrial circumferential strain. But this measurement may be inaccurate due to the differences in thickness and arrangement of left atrial and left ventricular myocardium16. The 4DSTE applied in our study was dedicated to simultaneously measure left atrial longitudinal and circumferential strains of three phases, which technically overcomes the inherent disadvantages of 2DSTE and avoids algorithm bias induced by LV dedicated 3DSTE application. The present study showed that 4DSTE enables the comprehensive assessment of LA function with excellent reproducibility(All ICCs > 0.9).

In the present study, we found that the LASr representing atrial filling during LV systole and LASct representing LA contraction were significantly reduced in the event group compared with the non-event group, which was consistent with prior studies17. The LAScd serving as a conduit during early LV diastole and diastasis seemed lower in the event group than in the non-event group, but this was not statistically significant. Additionally, we firstly demonstrated that LASr_c and LASct_c were significantly decreased in the event group compared with the non-event group. Similar to the LAScd, no significant difference was showed by LAScd_c in the event group. We postulate several reasons for these results. First, LA pump strain (LASct and LASct_c) reflects an active contractile process18. Patients with uremia generally have impaired left ventricular systolic function and volume overload, which will increase left atrial pump function to maintain sufficient cardiac output19,20. Long-term compensatory work will impair LA contractile function. For LA reservoir strain, LV GLS was the strongest determinant. LA pump function is also a determinant of LA reservoir strain21. These mechanisms and interaction may contribute the significant decline of LA reservoir and pump strains. In contrast, left atrial conduit phase is a passive process of left ventricular filling, which may be less affected by left ventricular systolic function and volume overload. Thus, LA reservoir and pump function may deteriorate more severe than LA conduit function. Second, Krishna K et al. found that LASr and LASct were significantly reduced in the stage 3 chronic kidney disease group, while LAScd was similar to that in the risk factor-matched group22. The LA strains of three phases were significantly reduced in patients with dialysis group compared with the control group23. These results may indicate the impairments of LA reservoir and pump function may occur earlier than LA conduit function. Third, the results may hold certain bias due to small sample size, a much larger sample size is required to supplement the results. All in all, the changes of LA strains of three phases are complex, more researches are required to clarify the inherent mechanisms.

Impaired LA longitudinal function may be linked to myocardial fibrosis, myocardial stunning concerning dialysis, or microvascular ischemia caused by a decrease in myocardial capillary density22,24. Therefore, Less LA longitudinal strain is a reliable and early parameter to identify LA myocardial fibrosis or ischemia before overt LA dysfunction. The multiple mechanisms including chronic fluid overload, increased arterial stiffness, activation of renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system, chronic inflammatory state and altered phosphate homeostasis are associated with LA remodeling25,26. The mechanism for the reduction in left atrial longitudinal strain may be applied to the left atrial circumferential strain. This might indicate that abnormal LA geometry and function are responsive to less longitudinal and circumferential strain. The association between LAS and LASr_c might reflect that they may share common mechanisms (e.g., myocardial injury, interstitial fibrosis, myocyte hypertrophy) and result in poor outcomes.

According to previous studies27–29, left atrial longitudinal strain is a more accurate and sensitive metric for describing LA function in people with cardiomyopathies, advanced-stage chronic kidney disease, or atrial fibrillation. LA longitudinal strain was also proved to have better predictive performance compared with other echocardiographic parameters such as LAVI and LAEF30–32. In this study, we found that LASr, LASct, LAVI and LAEF were significantly associated with all-cause mortality and MACE in the univariate Cox regression analysis. However, only LASr was the independent predictor of all-cause mortality and MACE in the multivariate Cox regression analysis, which is consistent with previous studies using 2DSTE3,17,26. Additionally, this study firstly demonstrated that LASr_c (LASr_c < 28.5%) also could predict all-cause mortality and MACE in stable dialysis patients with preserved LVEF. Thus, we speculated that LA reservoir function was relatively important for the prognosis of patients with end-stage renal disease. Compared with other echocardiographic parameters such as LAVI and LAEF, the reduction of LA longitudinal and circumferential strain indicates the earlier LA dysfunction. Hence, routine echocardiography is recommended for patients with uremia, and early treatment may be required as soon as the LA strains deceased. Due to the small sample size and a significant association, we did not include these two factors (LASr and LASr_c) in one Cox model so we could not identify which one is more powerful in predicting all-cause mortality or MACEs. Nevertheless, it is worth mentioning that dedicated left atrial 4DSTE provides more information to assess LA functions in ESRD patients. This imaging modality might contribute to better clinical assessment of ESRD patients with preserved LVEF.

Since LA dedicated 4DSTE has recently been utilized, there are a paucity of comparative and prospective studies focusing on LA circumferential strain. Bao L et al. found that LASr_c and LASct _c can be used to differentiate persistent atrial fibrillation (PAF) from non-AF subjects with great sensitivity and specificity33. Tsujiuchi M et al. showed that LA circumferential strain had good predictive power for MACE in general population5. Since our study initiated as soon as LA dedicated 4DSTE appeared, it was the first to discover the predictive value of circumferential strain for all-cause mortality and MACE in uremic patients. Further studies will reveal the clinical value of LA circumferential strain with increased utilization of LA 4DSTE.

This study has some limitations. First, our study was limited to the GE Medical Systems-provided EchoPAC workstation Version 204 due to differences in proprietary software among vendors. In clinical practice, it is crucial to take the vendor and the LA-dedicated software version into consideration. Second, due to the short follow-up duration and low death rate, mortality or MACEs could not be predicted independently. Third, LA strain analysis was not available for some dialysis patients due to severe valvular heart disease, atrial fibrillation, or poor echocardiographic image quality. Finally, the predictive sensitivity of the longitudinal and circumferential strains could not be provided in this research, because the significant association between LA longitudinal and circumferential strain.

Conclusion

Among dialysis patients with preserved LVEF, less LASr and LASr_c are predictive of poor prognosis. The use of LASr and LASr_c would allow the early identification of high-risk ESRD patients. These findings indicate that less LASr and LASr_c may provide a marker or target for specific treatment strategies and demonstrate the explicit application of LASr and LASr_c in clinical practice.

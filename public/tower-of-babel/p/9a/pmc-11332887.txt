Impact of digital infrastructure construction on financial development: Evidence from the “broadband China” Strategy
Authors: Jingshi He, Yang Mu, Chunsheng Wang, Yanhua Mao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The “Broadband China” has enabled China to achieve leapfrog development in the construction of its high-speed broadband networks, thereby accelerating the development of digital infrastructure and unleashing the potential for financial sector growth. This has created a strong impetus for economy to shift towards high-quality development. An in-depth understanding of the empirical correlation between the development of digital infrastructure and financial progress is absent, leaving an unexplored domain of research concerning the impact of the “Broad China” strategy on financial agglomeration (Finagg), financial scale (Finscal), and digital finance (Digfina). In order to evaluate the dynamic impacts of this policy on financial development, this research builds a multi-period difference in differences (DID) model using panel data from 269 prefecture-level cities between 2011 and 2020. The study concludes that the “Broadband China” policy significantly fosters the growth of Finagg, Finscal, and Digfina, in China, and found that the “Broadband China” policy has a significant impact on the regional heterogeneity of financial agglomeration and digital finance. There are significant variations in the effects of this policy on different regions, while the regional heterogeneity differences in the impact on financial scale are relatively small. Additionally, the three mediating variables of technological innovation, fund support, and talent agglomeration play a mediating role in the mechanism of digital infrastructure on financial development.

Introduction

The international economy is currently going through a period of dynamic transformation, and the digital economy is now emerging as a crucial component in the long-term strategy of nations. Digital technology has become a transformative force that supports global development. A key component of the digital era's infrastructure, broadband networks are essential for big data, cloud computing, artificial intelligence, and blockchain technology [1]. They also serve as a crucial strategic foundation for advancing digital transformation and superior economic growth [2,3]. China has emerged as the primary promoter of the global digital revolution [4]. The digital economies of the United States and China grew by $6.5 trillion and $4.1 trillion, respectively, between 2016 and 2022. China's digital economy had a compound annual growth rate of 14.2 %, far greater than that of other major economies. By the second quarter of 2023, there will be around 1.3 billion 5G users worldwide; 651 million of those users will be in China, making up half of the total 5G user base. The modernization of broadband network infrastructure is being aggressively promoted by nations all over the world as a means of fostering industrial upgrading and accelerating the development of the digital economy. A special action plan for the “Broadband China” strategy was launched in 2013, positioning the country as a “strategic public infrastructure for the new era” and offering a solid foundational push for the advancement of economy. Prior to this, China had actively encouraged the development of digital infrastructure. The rate of Internet penetration has steadily increased over time thanks to the Chinese government's persistent efforts [2]. China currently has “gigabit in cities” and “broadband in villages” realized, and it has constructed the most expansive and technologically sophisticated broadband network infrastructure in the world. China has demonstrated a robust digital economy owing to its superior digital infrastructure. However, prior to 2013, China had insufficient network infrastructure, characterized by limited internet penetration, slow broadband speeds, and high prices. In 2013, China's average bandwidth for Internet access was 3.14 Mbps/second, compared to 17.2 Mbps and 11.7 Mbps/second in South Korea and Japan, respectively, over the same year. The number of homes with fixed internet access will rise from 40 % to 96 % between 2013 and 2020, and the speed of urban broadband access will rise from 20 MBS to over 100 MBS. This strategy's objectives will essentially be met ahead of time by 2020.

Upgrading the Internet is increasingly seen as an important initiative to advance the development of countries' information infrastructures. The availability of broadband network capacity is a crucial component of contemporary global infrastructure [5]. The construction of digital infrastructure, represented by network broadband, has resulted in significant and extensive advantages for the entire economy and society [6]. It not only mitigates the digital divide in various regions, but also influences the progress of the financial sector. The digital revolution has had a huge impact on fintech and financial technology, resulting in improvements in financial inclusion and efficiency [4]. The utilization of digital infrastructure by financial institutions can be employed to improve the performance of their financial instruments and facilitate the operations of stated institutions in order to guarantee that these instruments can efficiently sustain economic investments [7]. This policy has garnered attention from scholars after the implementation of the Broadband China strategy. Research has discovered that the policy has had a notable and positive impact on business productivity [8], increased labor income levels [9], and had a crucial effect on promoting business digitalization [10]. The “Broadband China” policy has a positive influence on innovation and entrepreneurship [11,12], and facilitates the spread of knowledge [13]. However, there is only a small amount of study available on the impact of digital infrastructure on financial development. This is an issue that requires thorough investigation, and there is currently no developed literature on the relationship between network broadband construction and financial development. Therefore, providing an in-depth analysis of the extent to which the establishment of the digital framework of “Broadband China” has impacted financial development, as well as the specific mechanisms via which it affects financial development, would fill the current research gap. Specifically, this study analyzes the impact of the establishment of the digital infrastructure of “Broadband China” on financial development particularly focusing on three aspects of financial development: financial agglomeration, financial scale and digital finance. This study carries out empirical analysis based on the panel data of 269 prefecture-level cities in China using the multi-period DID method. Additionally, the mediation effect is utilized to examine the mechanism by which influence is transmitted. Through empirical analysis, one may acquire a more comprehensive knowledge of the impact of the “Broadband China” policy implementation and its role in promoting financial development and sustainable growth of the digital economy. The results can provide theoretical value and practical guidelines for advocating digital infrastructure as a means to foster high-quality economic development in other countries.

Literature review and hypotheses

The discussion of the economic value of information and communication technologies (ICT) is where the impact of digital infrastructure on productive activities and economic growth first developed. Brynjolfsson and Yang [14], for instance, point out that while early empirical studies on ICT productivity did not find significant increases in productivity, as the technology has been applied, ICT has been linked to consumer surplus, economic growth, and intermediate indicators in addition to productivity gains. In developing nations, mobile information and digital technology is crucial for raising national productivity [15,16]. According to Roller and Waverman's [17] analysis of a sample of 21 OECD nations, economic growth is positively impacted by investments in telecommunications infrastructure. After comparing data from 33 OECD and 41 SSA countries, Myovella et al. [18] found that ICT generally promote economic growth. Broadband communications are a fundamental component of contemporary global digital infrastructure, and wide network access is becoming more widely acknowledged as a crucial component of the information economy [1]. In the digital economy, digital infrastructure is a crucial kind of infrastructure [19]. According to Autio et al. [20], its conceptual meanings relate to the use of ICT and the related infrastructure. Digital infrastructure is a crucial condition for entrepreneurship and economic growth, according to the World Economic Forum [21]. Compared to direct incentives like tax breaks, digital infrastructure offers companies network resources that are more suited to promoting innovation [22,23]. Scholars globally have been concentrating on the ways in which broadband Internet impacts many facets of the socio-economic landscape. According to studies, the organization of economic activity has undergone a fundamental transformation as a result of broadband networks [24]. The expansion of broadband network infrastructure may speed up the sharing and processing of information and lessen information asymmetry, as well as boost employment and company productivity [[25], [26], [27]]. Using a quasi-natural experiment using interleaved enhanced broadband infrastructure in rural Italian communities, Canzian et al. [28] conclude that the expansion of digital infrastructure boosts total factor production. According to a research conducted in 45 sub-Saharan African nations, digital infrastructure has a beneficial impact on jobs in the services sector [5]. Broadband infrastructure is shown to stimulate economic development in studies conducted in a sample of OECD nations [29,30].

The implementation of network broadband serves as a crucial component of digital infrastructure, providing significant and far-reaching benefits for both society and the economy. It not only mitigates the digital divide in various regions, but also influences the progress of the financial sector. Fintech and financial technology have been significantly influenced by the digital revolution, which has improved financial inclusion and efficiency [4]. The utilization of digital infrastructure by financial institutions can be employed to assess the performance of their financial instruments and facilitate the operations of stated institutions in order to guarantee that their financial instruments can sustain economic investments. According to Luo et al. [12], the two primary ways that broadband infrastructure encourages entrepreneurial activity are via financial growth and the pooling of human resources. According to Niu et al. [31], rural broadband affects financial inclusion more on the utilization in places where social capital, human capital, and bank branch penetration are higher.

The significance of digital infrastructure and telecommunications networks for both economic and social progress has been widely acknowledged in China [32]. With the introduction of “Broadband China” in 2013, China's national policy now includes the development of broadband Internet in order to boost broadband speeds in cities with significant externalities. The Chinese government introduced the “Broadband China” strategy as a special action in 2013 with the goal of strengthening the development of information infrastructure and highlighting the essential role that information plays in promoting the nation's social and economic development as well as the livelihood of its citizens. Broadband networks were positioned as “strategic public infrastructure in the new era” for the first time, alongside utilities like electricity, water, and roads. Three batches of pilot demonstration cities were approved in 2014, 2015, and 2016. "Comprehensive speed-up (until the end of 2013)", “popularization (2014–2015)", and “optimization and upgrading (2016–2020)" are the three stages of continuing efforts that the “Broadband China” plan has undergone since its publication.

The initial aim of the “Broadband China” policy was to boost the development of information infrastructure and enhance the vital function of information in facilitating national economic and social progress, as well as improving the quality of life for the people. Scholars have been taking notice of this policy after the launch of the Broadband China strategy. Existing studies have analyzed the impact of the “Broadband China” policy on various aspects of business productivity [8], business digitization [10], labor income level [9], innovation and entrepreneurship [11,12], and knowledge dissemination [13]. Society as a whole and policymakers have underscored the significance of digital infrastructure for production and the economy, with the latter perceiving digital infrastructure development as a panacea for the digital transformation of regional economies [33]. After analyzing the available literature, we have discovered a scarcity of research related to the impact of digital infrastructure on financial development. Furthermore, there is a lack of empirical studies that utilize panel data analysis specifically for the Chinese region. Although there are some research results on the “Broadband China” policy, there is no empirical research on the impact of the “Broadband China” policy on the financial sector. Studying the impact of digital infrastructure on finance is an area that deserves thorough investigation, yet there's not enough of study findings. The impact of the “Broadband China” policy on financial development is a significant research gap that requires attention. The findings of this research can have global significance for understanding the influence of digital infrastructure on financial development.

Based on the above analysis, we propose the hypothesis:H1The “Broadband China” policy, a digital infrastructure project, promotes financial agglomeration, financial scale and digital financial development.

The “Broadband China” policy, a digital infrastructure project, promotes financial agglomeration, financial scale and digital financial development.

There is no clear literature on the transmission mechanism of digital infrastructure to financial development. Research has determined that the development of digital infrastructure positively affects technological innovation [34,35]. Fintech has become an important enabler of high-quality financial services [36,37], and technological advancements in the financial sector enhance the industry's efficiency [38]. Fintech is expanding the range of financial services, promoting the digitalization of established financial institutions, and enabling advancements in payments, loans, and other financial services. Innovation in technology increases productivity in all industries, and the financial sector is profoundly impacted by the information revolution due to the information technology's accelerated development, which furthers the financial sector's development. Thus, technological innovation can be considered as a mediating variable in the transmission of digital infrastructure development to financial development. The advancement of intelligence in the digital age introduces greater intricacy to enterprise production. Additionally, the optimization of industrial structure and the turnover of relevant personnel amplify the need for human capital within organizations, thereby encouraging the concentration of relevant talents [39]. The development of Fintech innovation relies greatly on the availability of skilled professionals in the finance sector [40]. The enhancement of human capital contributes to the financial development of an economy [41]. The advancement of digital infrastructure has led to an increased global demand for informatization and digitization. Moreover, the digitization of industries has become increasingly challenging. The convergence of information talents facilitates this transformation, which subsequently fosters the growth of the financial sector. Therefore, we argue that talent agglomeration is the mediating variable in the transmission of digital infrastructure development to financial development. The government's expenditure of funds towards science and technology shows its supportive attitude towards innovation in these fields and the development of digital infrastructure [42]. The government's increased investment in directing money from all sectors of society has improved financial liquidity, leading to accelerated capital turnover. Additionally, the government's focus on developing fintech and digital financial inclusion has further contributed to this improvement. Improved digital infrastructure enhances the operation of financial instruments used by financial institutions, hence facilitating their activities and ensuring that these instruments effectively promote investment in the economy [7]. Therefore, we consider financial support as a mediating variable in the transmission of digital infrastructure development to financial development. In summary, we propose the following hypothesis:H2Technological innovation, talent agglomeration and financial support are the mediating variables of “Broadband China” policy to promote financial concentration, financial scale and digital finance.

Technological innovation, talent agglomeration and financial support are the mediating variables of “Broadband China” policy to promote financial concentration, financial scale and digital finance.

Model and variables

Model

Multi-period DID model

This quasi-natural experiment of the “Broadband China” strategy examines the effects of financial development in three domains: Finagg, Finscal, and Digfina. The “Broadband China” plan is a typical example of China's digital infrastructure building. The treatment group consists of the cities carrying out this pilot, whereas the control group consists of the non-pilot cities. Since the policy was introduced in three-year increments in 2014, 2015, and 2016, the test makes use of a multi-period DID model, and the baseline regression model is as follows:(1)Yi,t=γ0+α1Policydidit+γ1Xit+υt+μi+εit

In the above equation, γ0 is the intercept term, Policydidit is a dummy variable for pilot cities, Xit is the control variable, α1 is the impact effect of the “broadband China” strategy on financial development Yi,t (financial agglomeration, financial scale, digital finance), γ1 is the regression coefficient of the control variable. υ1t, μ1i, ε1it are time-fixed, individual-fixed, and randomized perturbation terms, respectively.

Mediation effect model

Fig. 1 illustrates the mechanisms by which the “Broadband China” policy, a digital infrastructure, affects Finagg, Finscal, and Digfina. In order to explore this mechanism of influence, a mediated effects model is introduced here for analysis. Following Baron & Kenny [43], by employing the three-step test, one can empirically determine whether Tec, Tale, and Fund play a mediating role in the regression effect. Using the “broadband China” strategy as the primary explanatory variable, as shown by Equation (1) above, the first phase involves analyzing the effect of digital infrastructure on financial development (Finagg, Finscal, and Digfina). As demonstrated in Equation (2), the second stage is to assess the effect of the “Broadband China” policy on the mediator. As shown in Equation (3), the “Broadband China” policy and the mediating variables are simultaneously incorporated into the regression model in the third stage in order to examine the influence of both on financial development.(2)Medit=α0+α2Policydidit+γ2Xit+υ2t+μ2i+ε2it(3)Yit=β0+α3Policydidit+β3Medit+γ3Xit+υ3t+μ3i+ε3it

In the above equation, α1 is the impact effect of the “broadband China” strategy on financial development. α2 is the effect of Policydidit on the mediator variable. α3 is the direct effect of Policydidit on financial development after controlling for the mediating variable Medit. β3 is the effect of the mediator variable on financial development after controlling the variable of Policydidit. If α2, α3, β3 pass the test of significance and |α3| is less than |α1|, then the mediating effect holds and the mediator variable acts as a partial mediator. If α2, β3 pass the test of significance, but α3 is not significant, indicating that the direct effect is not significant, then there is only an indirect effect, which leads to the conclusion of full mediation [43]. Certain scholars have proposed that the distinction between full and partial mediation be omitted [44,45]. While this distinction provides a literal description of the effect sizes of the mediation effects model, it is imprecise, inaccurate, and limiting, and it can be tedious to interpret the results. According to Baron and Kenny [43], situations that are completely mediated are exceedingly uncommon, and they provide the most compelling proof for the existence of a mediation effect. Preacher and Hayes [46] advocates for the rejection of the concept of comprehensive mediation, also known as primary mediation.

Variables

Explained variables

To examine the effects of broadband policy initiatives in China on financial development, this study will concentrate on three dimensions: financial agglomeration, financial scale, and digital finance.(I)Financial agglomeration (Finagg) is indicative of the regional financial system's level of development. The concentration of financial intermediaries and financial instruments is encouraged by the consolidation of financial institutions, which also increases the effectiveness of credit fund distribution. An increased level of financial agglomeration has the potential to swiftly amass dormant capital, thereby augmenting the efficacy of financial production [47]. Prior research has commonly employed measures such as the proportion of financial output or the sum of deposits and loans [48,49]. Nevertheless, relying on a single indicator to assess the extent of financial agglomeration is insufficient. In light of this, the objective of this study is to employ a weighted approach that integrates the dimensions of financial capital agglomeration and financial talent agglomeration, and each proportion is assigned a weight of 0.4 and 0.6, respectively. These weights are then combined and weighted. Financial capital agglomeration is measured by the ratio of the amount of deposits and loans in each region to the total amount in the sample region, and financial talent agglomeration is measured by the ratio of the number of financial employees in each region to the total number in the sample region.(II)Financial scale (Finscal) indicates a city's financial magnitude and potential for financial growth. The assessment of financial scale expansion can be effectively characterized by the regional per capita savings and loan balance, which places emphasis on financial accessibility [50]. This study determines financial scale through the analysis of per capita loan and savings balances.(III)Digital finance (Digfina) is an indicator of digital finance progress. Numerous academicians have embraced the digital financial inclusion index [51,52], which was established by Peking University [53]. This index is also used in this study for measurement.

Data sources

The data sources consist primarily of each city's EPS data platform, scientific and technological yearbooks, and statistical yearbooks. The “Breadth of China” policy pilot cities comprise Beijing, Shanghai, Chongqing, and Tianjin, all of which are municipalities operating under the direct jurisdiction of the central government and are comparable in scale to regions at the provincial level. These four municipalities, however, are not considered as the subject of this study, which is prefectural-level municipalities. Furthermore, the exclusion of certain cities was justified on account of the substantial quantity of absent values pertaining to the pertinent indicators. There are a total of 269 cities in the final sample unit, comprised of 96 ″Breadth China” pilot cities and 173 non-pilot cities.

Robustness tests

PSM-DID test

A multi-period double-difference approach is used to isolate the effects of the “Broadband China” policy; nevertheless, this policy may only be classified as a quasi-natural experiment, not a fully randomized natural experiment, and selection bias in the treatment group samples may still be an issue. Thus, in this case, the results are verified using the PSM-DID method. By establishing a control group that is analogous to the exogenous policy, this prevents selectivity bias in sample classification and ensures the policy's exogenous nature [57]. The logit model was subsequently employed to compute the propensity score. The outcomes, which are presented in Table 3, were obtained through the utilization of the nearest neighbor matching method (NNMM) and kernel matching method (KMM). Conclusions

Conclusions and implications

The construction of high-speed broadband networks has seen rapid and substantial growth, thanks to the “Broadband China” strategy and other significant policies. Presently, the largest fiber-optic network in the world has been established, providing a basic guarantee for the construction of China's digital infrastructure. The successful execution of this approach has stimulated the financial sector's latent capacity and served as a potent impetus for the structural transformation of China's economy in the direction of high-quality progress. The impact of the “Broadband China” policy on productivity [8], enterprise digitization [10], and innovation and entrepreneurship [11,12] has been widely discussed by scholars. However, there is a lack of study about the influence of digital infrastructure development, as represented by the “Broadband China” policy, on finance, as well as the specific effects and transmission mechanisms related. Based on the panel data of 269 prefecture-level cities from 2011 to 2020, this study constructs a multi-period DID model to assess how the implementation of the “Broadband China” policy affects financial development from three dimensions: financial agglomeration, financial scale, and digital finance, and analyzes the impact effects and transmission mechanisms, which is the marginal contribution and innovation of this study. This study enhances our comprehension of how digital infrastructure facilitates financial development and the sustainable growth of the digital economy, with worldwide implications and references.

The research concludes as follows: the financial financing sector in China experiences substantial advancements during the analyzed period due to the Broadband China policy; and a notable disparity emerges between cities that participated in the pilot program and those that did not. The robustness of the sample conclusions is assessed using the PSM-DID method, the reduced-tail method, while controlling for the pilot smart city policy and the pilot e-business policy. The parallel trend test and the placebo test provide further support for this conclusion. The results consistently demonstrate the robustness of the conclusions. Furthermore, In terms of heterogeneity, the higher the status of the financial industry, the higher the degree of mobile informatization, and the higher the level of regional economic growth, the more significant the effect of “Broadband China” policy on financial agglomeration. The effect of the “Broadband China” policy on digital finance is more significant in regions with higher status of financial industry and higher level of regional economic growth. In regions with a higher degree of mobile informatization, the effect of the “Broadband China” policy on financial scale is more significant. These findings indicate that the “Broadband China” strategy has a heterogeneous influence on financial agglomeration and digital finance across different areas. However, the impact on financial scale shows less variation between regions. As Chen & Wang (2023) [2] show, broadband in cities with higher Internet penetration and GDP per capita has a greater impact on promoting innovation due to the accelerating effect of digital infrastructure. The “Broadband China” policy promotes the development of digital infrastructure, which in turn has an accelerating effect on finance, stimulating the potential of the financial sector and promoting financial development. It is imperative that the government acknowledges the critical significance of digital infrastructure in fostering the growth of the financial sector. To facilitate the modernization of digital infrastructure in additional cities, policy support and special funds should be allocated to expand the policy demonstration effect of broadband infrastructure construction and other digital infrastructure projects. In order to promote the growth of the financial industry and encourage the high-quality development of regional economies, it is essential that we promote the construction of more digital infrastructures in the future. This will allow us to take full advantage of the potential of digital infrastructure and its benefits. It is important to acknowledge that digital infrastructure functions as an accelerator, and its impact is especially significant in locations with a strong financial industry, strong mobile information technology, and a robust regional economy. In addition, the digital infrastructure in underdeveloped regions may have a greater marginal contribution due to their lack of development in terms of fundamental conditions. Therefore, our attention should be directed towards advancing the establishment of digital infrastructure, specifically network broadband, in areas lacking adequate digital infrastructure. This will promote equitable regional and urban-rural development, while also enhancing the overall quality of digital infrastructure in both urban and rural areas.

The analysis of the transmission mechanism shows that technological innovation, talent agglomeration and fund support play a mediating role in the transmission mechanism of financial agglomeration, financial scale and digital finance. Technological innovation contributes more than fund support and talent agglomeration combined, in mediating the transmission mechanism of digital infrastructure development on financial agglomeration. Technological innovation serves as the primary mediating variable in the mechanism for transferring digital infrastructure development to financial scale. Technological innovation's contribution is greater than that of the variables talent agglomeration and fund support in the transmission mechanism of digital infrastructure development to digital finance. Therefore, it is suggested to accelerate the development of digital industrialization and industrial digitalization, promote the integrated development of digital economy and finance, improve digital infrastructure, promote the development and application of digital financial technology, and further enhance the depth and breadth of financial digitalization. In addition, it is suggested to build a digital financial service system, explore the multidimensional development path of digital finance, enrich the service scenarios of finance, and expand the online channels of finance.

Limitations, recommendation for future research

This study examines the impact of the “Broadband China” policy on financial agglomeration, financial scale and digital finance based on panel data of 269 prefecture-level cities. Due to the limitation of the data, this study only considers the data from 2011 to 2020, and fails to consider the analysis of longer time dimensions. This study analyzes financial development from the three dimensions: financial agglomeration, financial scale and digital finance, but how to measure financial development may be a matter of opinion, and the measurement of the indicators analyzed in this study fails to break through the traditional indicator measurement methods. In addition, this study only considers the impact of the implementation of the “Broadband China” policy on financial development, and in the future, we will consider constructing a digital infrastructure index system to explore how the improvement of the digital infrastructure level in different regions will have an impact on the financial development, and explore the mechanism of the level of digital infrastructure and financial development, which is an area of in-depth research on indicators. This is an area of in-depth research on indicators.

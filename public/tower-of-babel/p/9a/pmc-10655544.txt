Serum 21-Deoxycortisol for Diagnosis of Nonclassic Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia in Women With Androgen Excess
Authors: Jennifer L Ng, Ee Mun Lim, Rui Zhang, John P Beilby, Gerald F Watts, Suzanne J Brown, Bronwyn G A Stuckey
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractContextNonclassic congenital adrenal hyperplasia (NCCAH) requires exclusion before diagnosing polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). Increasing use of liquid chromatography and tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) necessitates revision of immunoassay-based criteria for NCCAH. Measurement of 21-deoxycortisol (21DF) may simplify the diagnosis of heterozygosity (HTZ), the presence of 1 affected CYP21A2 allele, which currently relies on complex molecular studies.ObjectiveWe aimed to determine LC-MS/MS-specific criteria for NCCAH and HTZ and compare the diagnostic accuracy of 21DF and 17-hydroxyprogesterone (17OHP).MethodsA cross-sectional study involving 99 hyperandrogenic females was performed. We identified females who had undergone both a synacthen stimulation test (SST) and CYP21A2 genotyping from 2010 to 2017, and prospectively recruited females referred for an SST to investigate hyperandrogenic symptoms from 2017 to 2021. Steroids were compared between genetically confirmed NCCAH, HTZ, and PCOS. Optimal 17OHP and 21DF thresholds for HTZ and NCCAH were determined by receiver operating characteristic analysis.ResultsBasal 17OHP, stimulated 17OHP, and 21DF were measured in 99, 85, and 42 participants, respectively. Optimal thresholds for NCCAH were 3.0 nmol/L and 20.7 nmol/L for basal and stimulated 17OHP, respectively. Basal and stimulated 21DF thresholds of 0.31 nmol/L and 13.3 nmol/L provided 100% sensitivity with specificities of 96.8% and 100% for NCCAH, respectively. Diagnostic thresholds for HTZ of 8.0 nmol/L, 1.0 nmol/L, and 13.6 for stimulated 17OHP, 21DF, and the ratio (21DF + 17OHP)/cortisol each provided 100% sensitivity with specificities of 80.4%, 90.5%, and 85.0%, respectively.ConclusionLC-MS/MS-specific 17OHP thresholds for NCCAH are lower than those based on immunoassay. LC-MS/MS-quantified 17OHP and 21DF accurately discriminate HTZ and NCCAH from PCOS.Keywords: nonclassic congenital adrenal hyperplasia, 21-hydroxylase deficiency, heterozygosity, 21-deoxycortisol, 17-hydroxyprogesterone, mass spectrometry

Nonclassic congenital adrenal hyperplasia (NCCAH) is clinically indistinguishable from polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS). However, accurate diagnosis has important genetic and therapeutic implications (1). In 95% of cases, NCCAH is associated with autosomal recessive variants in the CYP21A2 gene that result in partial 21-hydroxylase deficiency (21OHD). Subsequent impairment in cortisol production, with reduced negative feedback from cortisol, leads to adrenocorticotrophic hormone hypersecretion, accumulation of steroid precursors, and diversion toward androgen biosynthesis (2).

Diagnostic criteria for NCCAH are based on immunoassay-quantified 17-hydroxyprogesterone (17OHP), the main substrate for 21-hydroxylase. The synacthen stimulation test (SST), in which 17OHP and cortisol are estimated at 0, 30, and 60 minutes after intramuscular synacthen 250 µg, is regarded as the diagnostic gold standard. A basal 17OHP >6 nmol/L is recommended for screening, and the diagnosis is confirmed if basal or stimulated 17OHP is >30 nmol/L (3). Replacement of immunoassays by liquid chromatography and tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) necessitates revision of these thresholds as LC-MS/MS-quantified 17OHP is lower, owing to superior analytical specificity (4).

Heterozygosity for CYP21A2 variants (HTZ) is common among the general population with an estimated prevalence of 1 in 11 for mild variants (5) and 1 in 60 for severe variants (6). Heterozygotes may be asymptomatic or may present with symptoms of androgen excess (7-9). The prevalence of HTZ may be as high as 20% to 35% among hyperandrogenic women and children, with a third harboring severe variants associated with risk of classic CAH in progeny (8, 10-13). In women being evaluated for androgenic symptoms, moderate 17OHP responses to adrenal stimulation raise suspicion for HTZ. However, a diagnostic threshold for HTZ has not been established due to significant overlap with normal when 17OHP is quantified by immunoassay (14-16). Genotyping provides the only reliable approach to diagnose HTZ but is complicated, expensive, and not universally available (3). A convenient biochemical test to triage patients for genotyping would be highly desirable.

Serum 21-deoxycortisol (21DF), an adrenal-specific 11β-hydroxylase derivative of 17OHP, is a sensitive and specific biomarker for 21OHD (17-19). It rises with adrenal stimulation in heterozygotes, while remaining virtually undetectable in controls (18). Immunoassay-based studies have established its utility for diagnosis of heterozygosity since the 1980s (18-21). However, immunoassays for 21DF are cumbersome to perform and have not been commercially available. The multi-analyte capability of LC-MS/MS now permits simultaneous quantitation of 21DF along with other relevant steroids, enabling its use as a biomarker for 21OHD (22). Recent LC-MS/MS studies involving asymptomatic obligate carriers and controls have confirmed its utility and superiority to 17OHP in that setting (23-25). These findings require validation in studies involving hyperandrogenic women.

The primary aim of this study was to define LC-MS/MS-specific criteria for NCCAH and HTZ in a cohort of hyperandrogenic women. Secondary objectives were to compare the diagnostic validity of 17OHP and 21DF for discriminating HTZ from PCOS, and to determine the utility of basal androgens for distinguishing NCCAH and HTZ from PCOS. We hypothesized that 17OHP thresholds for NCCAH would be lower than those derived from immunoassays, and that 21DF would provide more accurate distinction between HTZ, NCCAH, and PCOS.

Materials and Methods

Subjects and Study Design

From June 2017 to June 2022, we prospectively recruited females aged >14 years who were referred to PathWest QEII Laboratory in Nedlands, Western Australia, for an SST as part of evaluation for hyperandrogenism. SSTs were performed in the morning, during the early follicular phase (day 2-5 following onset of menstruation in those with menstrual cycles) and after cessation of interfering medications such as the combined OCP, spironolactone, and glucocorticoids for 3 months. All subjects with a stimulated 17OHPLC-MS/MS >6 nmol/L or 21DF >1 nmol/L were CYP21A2 genotyped (23). As the laboratory has been utilizing LC-MS/MS for 17OHP quantitation since 2010, we also retrospectively identified females aged >14 years who had undergone both genotyping and an SST to investigate androgen excess symptoms between January 2010 and June 2017. Only follicular phase biochemistry in nonpregnant, genotyped patients who were not on the OCP, antiandrogens, or glucocorticoids were included in the final analysis. Information from laboratory request forms, concurrently requested reproductive hormone profiles, and sex hormone–binding globulin levels were used to confirm these details. The follicular phase was confirmed by serum estradiol <450 pmol/L and progesterone <2 nmol/L. Further clinical details were obtained from physicians’ records.

Ethics Approval

This study was approved by the Human Research Ethics Committee at University of Western Australia (RA/4/1/9064). All prospectively recruited subjects gave written informed consent. A waiver of consent was granted for the retrospective cohort.

Biochemical Analyses

Following separation from red cells, serum samples were stored at 4 °C and analyzed within 1 to 3 days of collection. Baseline serum samples were assayed for 17OHP, 21DF, cortisol, testosterone (T), androstenedione (A4), and dihydrotestosterone (DHT). Stimulated samples at 30 and 60 minutes were assayed for cortisol, 17OHP, and 21DF. The LC-MS/MS assay for 21DF was established in mid-2018 and hence 21DF was estimated only in those subjects prospectively recruited from this time onward. The precursors/product ratio (17OHP + 21DF)/cortisol × 1000 at 60 minutes was calculated for participants in which 21DF data were available. This ratio has been utilized in other LCMS studies that aimed to define biochemical criteria for heterozygosity (23, 25), and to improve the positive predictive value of newborn screening for classic CAH (22, 26).

LC-MS/MS Assay

Samples were analyzed by LC-MS/MS at PathWest QEII Laboratory, Nedlands, Western Australia. The assay simultaneously measures 18 analytes in a 25-minute run, although only the above-mentioned steroids were analyzed in this study. A detailed description of the method and performance metrics is provided elsewhere (Supplementary material (27)). The lower quantitation limits for 17OHP and 21DF were 0.078 nmol/L and 0.064 nmol/L respectively. Assay intraday and interday precisions for 17OHP and 21DF were measured on 3 concentration levels. The intraday precisions (coefficient of variation calculated for n = 5) for 17OHP were 5.2% (3.3 nmol/L), 4.0% (16.6 nmol/L), and 3.3% (99.5 nmol/L). The interday coefficient of variation (CV) calculated for n = 10 for 17OHP were 8.7% (3.3 nmol/L), 5.5% (16.6 nmol/L), and 5.4% (99.5 nmol/L). The intraday precisions (CV calculated for n = 5) for 21DF were 3.4% (0.26 nmol/L), 1.5% (1.06 nmol/L), and 2.5% (6.62 nmol/L). The interday precisions (CV calculated for n = 10) for 21DF were 5.5% (0.26 nmol/L), 2.4% (1.06 nmol/L), and 2.9% (6.62 nmol/L).

PathWest QEII Laboratory participates in the assay quality monitoring program, UK NEQAS for cortisol, 17OHP, A4, T, and progesterone, and RCPA QAP for aldosterone, 17OHP, androstenedione, testosterone, progesterone, cortisol, 11-deoxycortisol, and DHT.

CYP21A2 Genotyping

Molecular analysis was performed by Sanger sequencing of the functional gene, including the promoter region and pseudogene (where applicable), and multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification analysis (MLPA) for large deletions/duplications of 1 or more exons using the MRC Holland CAH MLPA kit. Parental genotyping was performed to confirm the mutation phase if more than 1 mutation was identified. This methodology detects >98% of mutations. Patients were classified as NCCAH if pathogenic variants on both alleles were identified, heterozygotes, if variants were present on 1 allele, and PCOS if no pathogenic variants were identified. Hence participants in the “PCOS” group were those with hyperandrogenic symptoms, no CYP21A2 variants, and no other apparent cause for menstrual disturbance and hyperandrogenism. They included PCOS as defined by the Rotterdam criteria, idiopathic hirsutism, and idiopathic hyperandrogenism.

Statistical Analyses

Descriptive statistics for continuous variables were expressed as median and interquartile range as most variables were not normally distributed. The chi-squared test was used to analyze categorical data. Steroid concentrations among 3 groups were compared using the Kruskal–Wallis test. The Conover test (28) was utilized for post hoc, between group comparisons. Mann–Whitney U tests were used for 2-group comparisons. Thresholds providing 100% sensitivity for diagnosis of NCCAH and HTZ were determined by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis. Sensitivity, specificity, and area under the curve (AUC) were calculated and AUCs were statistically compared. Statistical significance was defined at the 0.05 level (2-tailed P value), and Bonferroni adjustment to α′ = 0.05/3 = 0.017 was applied to multiple group comparisons. Statistical analyses were carried out using SPSS statistical software, version 29. The Conover test was performed using R software.

Sample Size Calculation

For ROC analyses, a sample size of 15 subjects including 5 cases was calculated to enable detection of an AUC of 0.9 with 80% power and assuming a 5% significance level. A detailed description of the sample size calculation is provided in the supplementary material (29).

Results

Seventy-one out of 205 women who were referred for an SST between June 2017 and June 2022 were CYP21A2 genotyped. Forty-three females who had undergone both an SST and CYP21A2 genotyping between 2010 and 2017 were identified via the laboratory audit. Thirteen luteal phase SST results were excluded based on serum progesterone of >2 nmol/L. One patient was excluded as 2 CYP21A2 variants were identified, but the phase of the variants could not be confirmed as her parents were unavailable for genotyping. Parental genotyping is necessary to determine whether heterozygous variants are in trans (distributed over both alleles), or in cis (both present on the same allele). In the absence of parental genotyping, it was unclear to which group this patient should be assigned (ie, HTZ if both CYP21A2 variants were present on 1 allele or NCCAH if variants were present on both alleles). Another patient was excluded as her peak 21DF of 2.04 nmol/L suggested heterozygosity for 21OHD although no CYP21A2 mutations were identified, and her 17OHP did not rise as expected for HTZ. A heterozygous pathogenic variant in the P450-oxidoreductase gene (POR) (NM_000941:exon13:c.C1660T:p.R554X) was subsequently confirmed in this patient by Sanger sequencing. In total there were 99 genotyped subjects with follicular phase biochemistry, including 62 PCOS, 21 HTZ, and 16 NCCAH. Follicular phase basal 17OHP, stimulated 17OHP, and 21DF results were available in 99, 85, and 42 participants respectively.

Basal and Stimulated 21DF

Basal 21DF was undetectable in 23/25 (92.0%) PCOS and 8/10 (80.0%) heterozygotes but was detectable in all 7 subjects with NCCAH for which follicular phase 21DF results were available. Basal 21DF differed significantly among the 3 groups (P < .001) and was higher in NCCAH than in both PCOS and heterozygotes (P < .001 for both). However, basal 21DF did not distinguish heterozygotes from PCOS (P = .67).

All stimulated 21DF parameters (21DF30min, 21DF60min, 21DFpeak, and Δ21DF) were significantly different among the 3 groups (P < .001 for all) and were higher in NCCAH than in both PCOS and heterozygotes (P ≤ .020 for all, Table 4). Stimulated 21DF was also higher in heterozygotes than in PCOS (P < .001 for all 4 stimulated 21DF parameters, Fig. 2). The degree of overlap in stimulated 21DF between heterozygotes and PCOS was less marked compared with stimulated 17OHP (overlap 10.0% vs 68.4%). One of 10 (10.0%) heterozygotes had a 21DFpeak below 1.23 nmol/L, the maximum for PCOS.

A positive and significant correlation was found between 17OHPpeak and 21DFpeak (Spearman's rho = 0.795, P < .001).

Androgens at Baseline

There was no significant difference in T, A, or DHT among the 3 groups (P > .412 for all). In subjects with NCCAH for whom results were available, T, A, and DHT were within the normal range in 10/14 (71.4%), 6/15 (40%) and 7/11 (63.6%) of the subjects, respectively.

ROC Analysis for Discriminating HTZ (Plus NCCAH) From PCOS

In the 85 subjects for whom follicular phase 17OHP results were available (56 PCOS, 19 heterozygotes, 10 NCCAH), a stimulated 17OHP60min >8.0 nmol/L distinguished heterozygotes (plus NCCAH) from PCOS with 100% sensitivity, 80.4% specificity, and AUC of 0.942, (95% CI 0.897–0.986 P < .001). The negative predictive value for this threshold was 100%. For 100% specificity, 17OHP60min >13.4 nmol/L provided 55.2% sensitivity (Table 5). In 42 subjects (25 PCOS and 10 heterozygotes plus 7 NCCAH) for whom 21DF data were available, a stimulated 21DF30min >1.0 nmol/L discriminated HTZ (plus NCCAH) from PCOS with 100% sensitivity, 90.5% specificity, and AUC of 0.994 (95% CI 0.981-1.009, P < .001). The negative predictive value for this threshold was 100% (Table 5). For 100% specificity, 21DF30min >1.3 nmol/L provided 94.1% sensitivity for HTZ (plus NCCAH).

Comparative Validity of 21DF and 17OHP for Distinguishing HTZ (Plus NCCAH) From PCOS

To compare the diagnostic performance of stimulated 17OHP, 21DF, and the precursors/product ratio (17OHP + 21DF)/cortisol × 1000 at 60 minutes, ROC analyses were generated on the 35 subjects for whom both follicular phase 17OHP and 21DF results were available (Table 6). All 3 parameters provided excellent discrimination between HTZ (plus NCCAH) and PCOS with AUCs of 0.952, 0.993 and 0.970 for 17OHP60min, 21DF30min, and the ratio, respectively. The difference in AUCs was not statistically significant (P > .191 for pairwise comparisons). Diagnostic thresholds of 8.0 nmol/L, 1.0 nmol/L, and 13.6 (unitless) achieved specificities of 75.0%, 90.0%, and 85.0%, respectively, for 100% sensitivity.

Discussion

A key finding in this study of 99 CYP21A2 genotyped hyperandrogenic females is that LC-MS/MS thresholds for NCCAH of 3.0 nmol/L and 20.7 nmol/L for basal and stimulated 17OHP, respectively, are lower than the immunoassay-based thresholds of 6 nmol/L and 30 nmol/L currently in use. This confirms the need to adopt method-specific criteria to avoid underdiagnosis of NCCAH. Serum 21DF also provided excellent diagnostic accuracy for NCCAH as reflected by AUC of 0.995 and 1.00 for basal and stimulated 21DF, respectively. A basal 21DF >0.31 nmol/L provided 100% sensitivity and 96.8% specificity for NCCAH, while stimulated 21DF60min >13.31 nmol/L gave 100% sensitivity and specificity. Lower 17OHPLC-MS/MS thresholds reflect the superior analytical specificity of LC-MS/MS and the tendency for immunoassays to overestimate true steroid values owing to interferences and cross-reactivity from similarly structured analytes. This is especially relevant to steroid biosynthetic disorders such as 21OHD in which there is accumulation of multiple upstream steroid precursors, particularly following adrenal stimulation.

LC-MS/MS-quantified stimulated 17OHP, 21DF, and the ratio (21DF + 17OHP)/cortisol measured at 60 minutes accurately distinguished CAH carriers (HTZ) from PCOS with statistically comparable AUC of 0.942, 0.994, and 0.970 respectively. Diagnostic thresholds for HTZ of 8.0 nmol/L, 1.0 nmol/L, and 13.6 for stimulated 17OHP, 21DF, and the ratio each provided 100% sensitivity and therefore 100% negative predictive value for HTZ, with specificities of 80.4%, 90.5%, and 85.0%, respectively. Stimulated 21DF provided the greatest diagnostic accuracy for diagnosis of HTZ. Steroid profiling by MS thus provides a convenient means to exclude the carrier state, and to select patients for complex molecular studies. It is acknowledged, however, that MS-based assays are not universally available.

Consistent with previous findings, heterozygotes displayed a biochemical phenotype reflective of subtle 21OHD (14, 23-25). While basal steroids were not significantly different from subjects with PCOS, the heterozygote response to adrenal stimulation was significantly higher for both 17OHP and 21DF. Some overlap with the PCOS group occurred as previously reported (7, 23, 30), but there was less overlap for stimulated 21DF (10%) than 17OHP (68.4%). Both steroids completely separated HTZ from NCCAH after adrenal stimulation. It is unlikely that cases of NCCAH were missed as all participants with a stimulated 17OHPLC-MS/MS >6 nmol/L or 21DF >1 nmol/L were CYP21A2 genotyped (23). These thresholds for genotyping were based on the only published LCMS study reporting biochemical criteria for heterozygosity, prior to commencement of the current study (23). It is recognized that stimulated values for 17OHP are lowest in individuals without pathogenic CYP212 mutations, mildly elevated in HTZ compared with normal, and highest in NCCAH (14, 24). Indeed, the stimulated 17OHPLC-MS/MS threshold of 6 nmol/L used to select patients for genotyping was below the threshold subsequently defined for HTZ (8.0 nmol/L), and far below the threshold of 20.7 nmol/L established for diagnosis of NCCAH.

Measurement of T, A4, and DHT was not useful in identifying NCCAH or HTZ within the hyperandrogenic population, consistent with findings from other studies (25, 31, 32). They were normal in 71.4%, 40%, and 63.6% of subjects with NCCAH, respectively. Hence routine screening of hyperandrogenic patients with basal 17OHP should be performed irrespective of androgen status. Clinical features did not differ significantly between heterozygotes, NCCAH, and PCOS, and, importantly, classic mutations were identified in 28.6% heterozygotes and 50% NCCAH. This highlights the importance of accurate biochemical diagnosis, particularly in females presenting with anovulatory infertility, to enable appropriate utilization of glucocorticoid treatment and preconception genetic counseling (33-35).

Four LC-MS/MS studies comparing steroid profiles between asymptomatic CAH carriers, and normal genotype controls have highlighted the diagnostic utility of 21DF, and its superiority to 17OHP, for carrier detection (23-25, 30). Prior to commencement of the current study, there were no prospective studies examining the utility of LCMS steroid profiling for NCCAH or heterozygote detection among hyperandrogenic women. This is a relevant clinical question as PCOS and idiopathic hyperandrogenism are diagnosed by exclusion. The thresholds for heterozygosity reported in this study are similar to those derived from LCMS studies involving asymptomatic carriers (male and female relatives of CAH patients) (23, 25, 30). After commencement of the current study, an LC-MS/MS study by Ueland et al involving 63 hyperandrogenic women (3 with genetically verified NCCAH, 5 heterozygote carriers, and 55 who were not diagnosed with NCCAH) and 121 healthy controls found that a stimulated 17OHP >8.0 nmol/L (the same threshold as was identified in the current study) was the best marker to discriminate CYP21A2 heterozygotes and NCCAH from healthy women. Associated sensitivity was 86% with specificity of 98% (36). Further LC-MS/MS studies involving hyperandrogenic women are required to validate these findings and confirm the optimal thresholds for diagnosis of HTZ and NCCAH.

Distinguishing HTZ from PCOS is particularly important in women desiring fertility as diagnosis of HTZ necessitates genotyping of the reproductive partner and genetic counseling prior to initiation of fertility treatment (or natural conception). This is especially relevant to couples of high-risk ethnicities (Mediterranean, Indian, Middle eastern, Eastern European, and Ashkenazi Jewish) (6). Identification of couples at high risk for classic CAH will facilitate informed reproductive choices including in vitro fertilization with preimplantation genetic diagnosis (37). These interventions should reduce the incidence of an unexpected diagnosis of classical salt-wasting CAH in the neonate.

Whether or not hyperandrogenism is fully attributable to the presence of heterozygous CYP21A2 mutations is not clear as some heterozygotes do not manifest androgenic symptoms at all (23, 25, 30). However, several studies have demonstrated an increased prevalence of HTZ among children with premature adrenarche and women with androgen excess, suggesting that the genetic defect may explain hyperandrogenic symptoms in some individuals (7-10, 12, 38). In these studies, up to one-third of hyperandrogenic females were confirmed to be heterozygotes, and approximately one-third of these harbored severe CYP21A2 mutations associated with risk of classic CAH in offspring (10, 11, 13, 39).

Only 3 other studies have reported LC-MS/MS-specific diagnostic thresholds for NCCAH. In a study by Oriolo et al involving 72 hyperandrogenic females a basal 17OHP >5.4 nmol/L (1.79 ng/mL) was less sensitive than our threshold of 3.0 nmol/L (90% vs 100%), although specificity was similar at 76.9% (32). Their optimal threshold of >0.25 nmol/L (0.087 ng/mL) for basal 21DF was slightly lower than our threshold of 0.31 nmol/L but gave lower specificity (76.9% vs 96.8%) for a sensitivity of 100%. Turcu et al reported thresholds of 5.86 nmol/L and 0.64 nmol/L for basal 17OHP and 21DF, respectively, in a study involving 86 females suspected for NCCAH (31). Both thresholds were associated with 96.3% sensitivity for NCCAH with specificities of 93.8% and 96.9%, respectively.

Biochemical criteria for HTZ reported in this study of hyperandrogenic women are generally consistent with previous LC-MS/MS-based studies which involved asymptomatic obligate male and female carriers and controls (23-25). Costa-Barbosa et al derived diagnostic thresholds of 9.1 nmol/L (300 ng/dL), 1.14 nmol/L (40 ng/dL), and 46 (unitless) for LC-MS/MS-quantified 17OHP60min, 21DF60min, and the ratio in a cohort of 61 asymptomatic male and female obligate carriers, 18 NCCAH, and 30 controls (23). In their subsequent report involving 58 asymptomatic CAH relatives, previous thresholds were revised to 9.4 nmol/L, 1.73 nmol/L, and 12 for 17OHP60min, 21DF60min, and the ratio, respectively. These higher thresholds for 17OHP and 21DF correspondingly provided lower sensitivity (82.1% vs 100%) and higher specificity (89.5% vs 80.4%) than those in the current report.

The need for dynamic testing to diagnose HTZ has been highlighted in other reports which found no difference in basal 21DF or 17OHP between HTZ and controls (23, 25, 30, 36). As routine implementation of dynamic testing would be impractical, it may be prudent to request an SST if basal 17OHP is elevated. However, as basal 17OHP was normal in 47.6% of HTZ, an SST could still be considered in patients of high-risk ethnicity (1) or in those with severe symptoms or a positive family history, even if basal 17OHP is normal. Our results suggest that blood sampling at both 30- and 60-minute time points may not be necessary. For the diagnosis of NCCAH, thresholds for 17OHP and 21DF providing 100% sensitivity gave the same specificity at 30 and 60 minutes. The optimal 21DF threshold for diagnosis of heterozygosity was the same at both 30 and 60 minutes. However, the 17OHP threshold for heterozygosity at 60 minutes provided higher specificity that the 30-minute value. Hence results from this study suggest that steroid measurement at the 30-minute time point in the synacthen stimulation test could be eliminated.

Although stimulated 17OHPLC-MS/MS may be adequate for the diagnosis, 21DF offers practical advantages relating to its adrenal-specific origin. As 21DF does not vary with the menstrual cycle nor suppress with use of the OCP, quantitation of 21DF would enable investigations to proceed without regard for menstrual cycle phase or OCP use.

The subject who was excluded because of discrepant 21DF and 17OHP results and no CYP21A2 variant was found to have a heterozygous pathogenic variant in POR by Sanger sequencing. Confirmation of heterozygosity for POR, a recognized cause of 21OHD, albeit without CYP21A2 mutation, supports the validity of the stimulated 21DF threshold for heterozygosity derived from this study and others (23, 30) (40). The function of 21-hydroxylase, 17-hydroxylase/17, 20-lyase, and aromatase depends on electron transfer from NADPH by POR. Although traditionally regarded as autosomal recessive, cases of manifesting heterozygotes have been reported, as in this patient who presented with oligomenorrhea and infertility (41).

Limitations of this study are that 21DF was available for a relatively small cohort (n = 42) as the assay was only established in mid-2018. A larger sample size was available for 17OHP as the LC-MS/MS assay for 17OHP was established in 2010 at our laboratory. Most participants were referred by endocrinologists and may not be representative of hyperandrogenic women seen in primary care settings. This study included women being assessed for androgenic symptoms, not asymptomatic obligate carriers (parents of CAH patients). The need for dynamic studies is, at present, an unavoidable element in the accurate assessment of adrenal androgen production. Although the cost of genetic studies in general is declining, the complexity of the CYP21A2 locus necessitates a high level of expertise to achieve accurate genetic results. Molecular studies are therefore impractical as a screening test to exclude NCCAH or HTZ in all women being investigated for hyperandrogenism, particularly considering the prevalence of hyperandrogenism among women of reproductive age (42). The 11-oxyandrogens that are relevant to the pathogenesis of 21OHD were not quantified, but may contribute to diagnosis without the need for dynamic testing in the future (31, 43).

The main strength of this study is that all patients were genotyped according to best practice guidelines, which optimizes the reliability of our findings (44). Further, steroids were quantified by LC-MS/MS, the gold standard laboratory technique. To the best of our knowledge, this is the only study reporting thresholds for both stimulated 21DF and 17OHP for distinguishing NCCAH from heterozygous carriers and PCOS among a genotyped hyperandrogenic female population.

In conclusion, both 21DF and 17OHP measured by LC-MS/MS provide sufficiently valid diagnostic discrimination between HTZ and NCCAH from patients with PCOS. Adoption of LC-MS/MS-specific diagnostic criteria for NCCAH will avoid underdiagnosis, as LC-MS/MS-17OHP thresholds are lower than those previously determined by immunoassay.

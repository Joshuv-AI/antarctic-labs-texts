Transcutaneous vagus nerve stimulation as add-on therapy versus pharmacotherapy alone (TRAVAST): study protocol for a prospective, randomised, triple-blind, controlled, multicentre trial
Authors: Melanie Penke, Zoe-Linda Tutone, Heidi Kulas, Sarah Torke, Patrick Lambert, Elisabeth Kaufmann
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionDespite optimised antiseizure medication, pharmacoresistant epilepsy still affects a substantial proportion of adult patients, especially when epilepsy surgery is not possible or is rejected. Transcutaneous vagus nerve stimulation (t-VNS) is a non-invasive neuromodulatory approach for which the evidence from randomised studies to date does not allow for a reliable assessment of its benefits due to small case numbers and methodological limitations. Transcutaneous vagus nerve stimulation as add-on therapy versus pharmacotherapy alone (TRAVAST) is a prospective, multicentre, randomised, triple-blind, controlled trial to investigate whether t-VNS as an add-on to established pharmacotherapy improves seizure-related and patient-relevant outcomes in adults with pharmacoresistant epilepsy.Methods and analysisA total of 164 adults (aged 18–65) with pharmacoresistant epilepsy and clinically manifest seizures who are not eligible for or refuse (further) resective epilepsy surgery will be enrolled at 15 sites. Patients are assigned 1:1 to active t-VNS or sham stimulation in addition to stable antiseizure medication for 12 months. t-VNS is performed using a non-implanted stimulator with an ear electrode as a daily self-administered therapy in the home environment. The primary outcome is the proportion of patients with at least 50% reduction in mean seizure frequency after 52±2 weeks compared with a 4-week baseline period, documented in a standardised epilepsy diary. Secondary outcomes include additional response rates, seizure freedom, seizure severity, health-related quality of life, psychological and cognitive parameters, treatment retention and safety, including device-related events and sudden unexpected death in epilepsy.Ethics and disseminationThe trial protocol has been approved by an ethics committee (Ethics Committee of LMU of Munich). All patients will provide written consent prior to inclusion and can withdraw from the trial at any time without this affecting their further care. The results will be published in specialist journals, reported in trial registries, made available anonymously for scientific use and communicated back to the participating physicians, patients and the public.Trial registration numberDRKS00039592.Keywords: Epilepsy, Randomized Controlled Trial, PUBLIC HEALTH

Introduction

Epilepsy is one of the most frequent chronic neurological disorders and is characterised by a persistent predisposition of the brain to generate epileptic seizures, leading to recurrent, unprovoked events.1 2 Globally, an estimated 50–52 million people are affected, corresponding to an age-standardised prevalence of around 658 per 100 000 population, with a prevalence of approximately 0.5–1% in Germany.3 At the pathophysiological level, epilepsy reflects an imbalance between neuronal excitation and inhibition within central networks. Under physiological conditions, cortical excitability is tightly regulated by the interplay of excitatory neurotransmitters such as glutamate and inhibitory mediators such as gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA).4 5 In epilepsy, diverse mechanisms including ion-channel dysfunction, structural brain lesions and neuroinflammatory processes promote neuronal hyperexcitability and hypersynchronous discharges, which manifest clinically as epileptic seizures.6 7

Standard treatment of epilepsy relies on antiseizure medication (ASM), which act by enhancing inhibitory GABAergic transmission, reducing excitatory glutamatergic signalling or modulating voltage-gated ion channels.8 9 While many patients achieve good seizure control, adverse effects such as fatigue, cognitive slowing, mood changes and endocrine or behavioural disturbances are common and may substantially impair health-related quality of life.10,13

A major challenge is pharmacoresistant epilepsy, affecting roughly one third of patients.14 The International League Against Epilepsy defines pharmacoresistant epilepsy as failure of at least two well-tolerated, appropriately chosen and adequately used ASM, as monotherapy or in combination, to achieve sustained seizure freedom.15

For selected patients, resective epilepsy surgery can offer seizure freedom rates of up to 70%, but a considerable proportion are not suitable candidates or decline surgery. Invasive neuromodulation techniques including vagus nerve stimulation (VNS) and deep brain stimulation expand the therapeutic armamentarium and can achieve moderate to substantial reductions in seizure frequency in pharmacoresistant epilepsy, but require surgical implantation and are associated with procedure-related risks and device-related adverse events (AEs).16 17

Transcutaneous VNS (t-VNS) is a non-invasive development of conventional VNS that aims to overcome the need for surgical implantation. Using a bipolar electrode placed on the skin of the auricle, typically in the cymba conchae region innervated by the auricular branch of the vagus nerve, t-VNS delivers electrical stimulation as an add-on to individualised ASM and can be self-administered by patients in daily life. Beyond epilepsy, t-VNS is being investigated across a range of indications including treatment-resistant depression, chronic migraine, tinnitus and cardiovascular or metabolic disorders.18 19 Randomised controlled trials of t-VNS in adult pharmacoresistant epilepsy have shown encouraging trends for efficacy and tolerability but are limited by methodological shortcomings.20,24 Across studies, there is substantial heterogeneity in stimulation protocols and control conditions, including differences in frequency, intensity, duty cycle and the choice of sham or non-vagal stimulation sites.20 24 Several of these trials provide insufficient information on randomisation procedures and maintenance of blinding, report high and imbalanced dropout rates or have very short follow-up periods and small sample sizes, which restricts internal validity and generalisability.24,28

A recent systematic review and meta-analysis including four randomised controlled trials with 368 patients reported a statistically significant reduction of mean monthly seizure frequency and a relative seizure frequency reduction of 17.6% in the t-VNS groups at the end of treatment, while responder rates, seizure freedom, depressive symptoms and quality of life showed no robust between-group differences. AEs were predominantly mild and transient, with similar discontinuation rates in active and control arms, but concerns regarding heterogeneous protocols, incomplete data and risk of bias led the authors to conclude that well-designed, adequately powered trials are needed to determine the impact of t-VNS on seizure-related and non-seizure outcomes in pharmacoresistant epilepsy.29 In Germany, the Institute for Quality and Efficiency in Health Care assessed the available evidence on t-VNS for pharmacoresistant epilepsy and concluded that the method has potential as a required treatment alternative, but that the existing data are insufficient for a definitive benefit assessment because of methodological and clinical limitations of the underlying trials.22 On this basis, the Federal Joint Committee (Gemeinsamer Bundesausschuss, G-BA) commissioned the Transcutaneous vagus nerve stimulation as add-on therapy versus pharmacotherapy alone (TRAVAST) trial as an evaluation trial under the German testing directive for t-VNS in pharmacoresistant epilepsy to generate robust evidence on patient-relevant outcomes.22

For patients with pharmacoresistant epilepsy who are not eligible for or decline epilepsy surgery, there remains a considerable unmet need for effective and well-tolerated non-pharmacological treatment options. T-VNS offers a potentially attractive, non-invasive neuromodulatory approach that can be integrated into routine care and self-administered by patients, but current evidence is not sufficient to support widespread implementation in publicly funded healthcare.20 22 Based on this, TRAVAST is conducted as an evaluation study commissioned under the German statutory decision-making process for new diagnostic and therapeutic methods. The trial is intended to generate robust evidence on patient-relevant outcomes under conditions relevant to routine care and future reimbursement decisions. TRAVAST therefore addresses an evidence gap identified for t-VNS in adults with pharmacoresistant epilepsy by applying a prospective, randomised, triple-blind, controlled, multicentre design.

Objectives

The aim of the TRAVAST trial is to demonstrate whether the addition of t-VNS to individualised ASM improves seizure outcomes and other clinically relevant outcomes in adults with pharmacoresistant epilepsy who are not eligible for or decline epilepsy surgery. The primary objective is to assess the superiority of t-VNS over sham stimulation regarding seizure reduction, measured as the proportion of patients achieving at least a 50% reduction in mean seizure frequency after 1 year compared with baseline. Secondary objectives are to evaluate the effects of t-VNS on seizure freedom, seizure severity, health-related quality of life, treatment retention, safety and a range of morbidity-related parameters, thereby providing a comprehensive basis for future decisions on the role of t-VNS in routine epilepsy care within the German statutory health insurance system.

Methods and analysis

Legal background

In Germany, the G-BA may commission a formal evaluation trial under Section 137e of Book V of the German Social Code (SGB V) when a new diagnostic or therapeutic method shows potential but the available evidence is insufficient for a final benefit assessment in routine care. In this setting, the aim is not only to explore efficacy under idealised conditions but to generate robust evidence for patient-relevant outcomes in a clearly defined population relevant to statutory health insurance decision-making. On this basis, the TRAVAST trial was commissioned to evaluate t-VNS treatment in adults with pharmacoresistant epilepsy under a prospectively specified, regulatorily anchored study design. TRAVAST should therefore be understood as a formal evaluation trial embedded in a statutory reimbursement context and designed to address predefined evidence gaps relevant to routine care. Within this statutory framework, the G-BA defined the research question, the key elements of the trial design, the target patient population and the primary outcome in advance as part of its decision-making process on reimbursement in routine care.

Trial design

The study protocol was developed in accordance with the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) reporting guidelines.30 A completed SPIRIT checklist is provided as an online supplemental file 2 with this submission. The TRAVAST trial is a prospective, multicentre, randomised, triple-blind, controlled trial evaluating the efficacy and safety of t-VNS as an add-on therapy in adults with pharmacoresistant epilepsy who are unsuitable for or decline epilepsy surgery. After screening, informed consent and confirmation of eligibility, a total of 164 patients is planned to be enrolled at approximately 15 sites across Germany. A complete list of participating sites is available through the German Clinical Trials Registry (DRKS: DRKS00039592). The patients will be randomly assigned (1:1) to receive either active t-VNS in addition to their individualised ASM or sham stimulation plus ASM. Treatment and all follow-up visits will be conducted in an ambulatory setting. During a 12-month observation period which data on seizure frequency and severity, health-related quality of life, functional status, psychiatric and cognitive comorbidities will be collected at predefined time points, using standardised patient-reported outcome measures and clinical rating scales (Liverpool Seizure Severity Scale 2.0 (LSSS), Quality of Life in Epilepsy Inventory (QOLIE-31), Generalised Anxiety Disorder-7 (GAD-7), Beck Depression Inventory II (BDI-II), Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) and Patient Global Impression of Change (PGIC)) and AEs (including serious and device-related events). This trial was registered in the German Clinical Trials Registry (DRKS) on 16 March 2026 (DRKS00039592) and is planned to start on 1 April 2026, with a projected end date of 31 March 2028, after 1 year of recruitment and 1 year of follow-up. In line with the specifications laid down by the G-BA in the corresponding testing directive for t-VNS, the observation period until assessment of the primary outcome comprises at least 52 weeks. This design reflects the specific objective of generating patient-relevant comparative evidence under routine-care conditions within the German statutory health insurance framework.

Inclusion/exclusion criteria

Adults aged 18–65 years with pharmacoresistant epilepsy, defined as failure to achieve sustained seizure freedom despite adequate trials of at least two tolerated, appropriately chosen and appropriately used ASM (monotherapy or combination therapy) over at least 12 months, are eligible. Eligible patients must have clinically manifest, that is, countable, epileptic seizures with a minimum average of three seizures per month and no more than 21 consecutive seizure-free days in the last 3 months. Seizure types may include, for example, focal seizures with or without impaired awareness, focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures and generalised tonic-clonic seizures. Thus, patients with both focal and generalised epilepsy syndromes are eligible for inclusion in the trial. Further inclusion criteria are ability to give informed consent, a stable ASM regimen for at least 5 weeks before enrolment and being unsuitable for or declining resective epilepsy surgery. Key exclusion criteria include recent status epilepticus within the last 6 months or an unreliable seizure count due to seizure clusters or functional dissociative seizures, unreliable seizure documentation, major psychiatric comorbidity, progressive neurological disease, implanted neurostimulation or other active implants, relevant cardiac or respiratory disease, dermatological conditions at the stimulation site, unstable medical conditions, current oncological treatment and participation in other interventional trials.

Randomisation

After screening, signing informed consent and confirmation of eligibility, patients will be randomised centrally 1:1 into the intervention (active t-VNS) or control (sham t-VNS) group. Randomisation is done block-wise using a computer-generated allocation sequence stratified by study site. The sequence will be created by an independent biostatistician using validated random-number generation, and only this biostatistician and the data management team will have access to the randomisation list. Investigators will screen and enrol participants and enter baseline data before randomisation but will not have access to the treatment allocation.

Blinding

Active and sham t-VNS devices will be identical in appearance, and all participants will receive standardised training on device use and electrode placement. This training may be minimally adapted by the staff responsible for device instruction according to the known group allocation, without disclosing the allocation to the participant or any other clinical personnel. Participants in the intervention group will receive an active t-VNS device, whereas participants in the control group will receive an outwardly identical device with an activated sham mode. Authorised site personnel will obtain the assignment for an eligible, consented patient only at the time of randomisation and communicate it to unblinded technical staff who will perform the device briefing with the patient, while all other trial staff remain blinded. Participants and clinical staff involved in enrolment, clinical follow-up and outcome assessment are not informed whether the device is delivering active or sham stimulation, whereas the staff responsible for device instruction is aware of group allocation.

In case of a medical emergency where knowledge of the allocated intervention is essential for patient treatment, the principal investigator may request emergency unblinding via the trial monitor, who will contact data management/biostatistics to disclose the treatment allocation for the concerned participant only. Any unblinding will be restricted to those individuals who need this information for clinical decision-making, will be fully documented and the allocation will not be disclosed to the sponsor or trial personnel at other sites. Since there is a high risk of unblinding in this trial, patients and the trial staff will be asked to fill in a questionnaire after completing all follow-up visits to evaluate whether the patient was at any point during the trial aware of the group they were assigned to (Blinding index).31

t-VNS device

The investigational treatment consists of t-VNS delivered via a non-implanted device with an electrode placed in the cymba conchae of the ear to stimulate the auricular branch of the vagus nerve. Stimulation is applied through the skin and can be used during normal daily activities as an add-on to individualised ASM.

Trial flow

Intervention

Patients in the intervention group will receive t-VNS treatment in addition to their stable ASM regimen. At baseline, trained trial personnel will instruct patients in device use, electrode placement and safety precautions and will perform the initial parameter setting. Stimulation intensity will be adjusted according to the device instructions: the current will first be increased until clearly perceivable and then reduced to a level below perception, and patients will perform daily self-administered stimulation with a recommended total duration of up to 4 hours per day, which may be split into several sessions of at least 1 hour each. For active t-VNS, the individual therapeutic intensity is defined as a subperceptual current just below the sensory threshold that remains well tolerated. A fixed numerical minimum current amplitude is not specified in the protocol. Rather, stimulation is titrated within the approved device range to achieve a stable sub-perceptual setting. During screening, clinical eligibility is assessed. At the baseline visit, the device is introduced and stimulation is titrated as described. Patients who cannot tolerate any perceivable stimulation within the device’s technical range, or in whom a reproducible subperceptual setting cannot be established despite repeated attempts, will be considered screen failures and will not be randomised. Treatment is delivered in an ambulatory setting over 12 months, with scheduled visits for clinical assessments, safety evaluations and documentation of seizures and other outcomes. Within the active t-VNS group, stimulation parameters follow the manufacturer’s approved settings for epilepsy, whereas the current intensity is individually titrated to a well-tolerated, subperceptual level as described above.

Control/sham stimulation

Sham treatment uses an externally identical t-VNS device with a factory-set sham mode that delivers minimal output (approximately 100 µA at 1 Hz). This mimics impedance checks and user feedback without producing a therapeutic vagal stimulation. Device behaviour, handling, visual feedback and usage parameters are indistinguishable from active devices, and patients follow the same schedules to maintain effective blinding. The stimulation intensity is not titrated to individual perception thresholds. The device operates at this predefined minimal output throughout the trial and is not considered to provide active therapeutic stimulation.

Participant timeline

After an initial screening visit, potentially eligible patients receive written information and the informed consent form and are given sufficient time to decide about participation. Four weeks before randomisation (week −4), eligible patients who have provided written consent attend the enrolment visit, during which inclusion and exclusion criteria are confirmed, a full clinical assessment is performed and a seizure diary is issued for prospective baseline documentation over 4 weeks. At the baseline visit (day 0), patients complete standardised questionnaires, eligibility is reconfirmed and participants are randomised to active t-VNS or sham stimulation, with standardised instruction in device use and continuation of the seizure diary throughout the 52±2 week treatment period. Follow-up visits are scheduled at 12±2, 20±2 and 36±2 weeks, with a planned final follow-up visit at 52±2 weeks to assess clinical outcomes, safety, device-related events and patient-reported outcome measures. An end-of-study (EOS) visit is performed either at the scheduled 52±2 week visit or earlier in case of premature trial termination, documenting trial completion status and reasons for discontinuation where available. All patient-reported and clinical outcomes will be measured using standardised electronic case report forms (eCRFs). The selected instruments (LSSS 2.0, QOLIE-31, GAD-7, BDI-II, MoCA, PGIC) are widely used and have demonstrated good to excellent reliability and validity in epilepsy and related populations, and trial personnel will be trained in their administration and data entry procedures. Treatment discontinuation, adherence and concomitant care

Patients may withdraw from the trial at any time, without giving a reason and without consequences for their further medical care. Investigators may discontinue study treatment or participation if ongoing involvement is considered to compromise the participant’s safety or well-being (eg, screening failure, withdrawal of consent before stimulation, loss to follow-up, death, investigator decision, status epilepticus). Whenever possible, patients who discontinue treatment or withdraw will be invited to attend an EOS visit and remain in follow-up for outcome assessments within the predefined observation period.

To promote adherence to the assigned intervention, patients receive structured instruction and written information on trial procedures and device use during the baseline visit. Follow-up visits are scheduled according to the visit scheme, with reminders and proactive contact in case of missed appointments. The number and length of visits are kept as low as possible to minimise patient burden. Adherence is monitored by documenting visit attendance, intervention compliance (eg, device use assessed via read-out of the impedance measurements and seizure diary completion) in the eCRFs.

Throughout the trial, all patients in both treatment groups will continue to receive best individualised ASM and standard supportive care for epilepsy and psychiatric comorbidities according to national guidelines. Adjustments of ASM are permitted if clinically indicated (eg, relevant worsening of seizure control, intolerable adverse effects), because the trial is embedded in routine care and is not designed as a fixed-dose ASM trial. All ASM changes (dose modifications, addition or discontinuation of drugs and use of rescue medication) will be prospectively documented in the case report forms (CRFs). Major ASM changes that reflect insufficient seizure control are prespecified as treatment-failure events and will be incorporated into the definition of treatment retention and the statistical sensitivity analyses. Concomitant treatments may include psychopharmacological treatment, psychotherapeutic or psychosocial interventions, and non-pharmacological approaches such as relaxation techniques, yoga or other complementary methods, as judged appropriate by the treating physicians. All relevant concomitant care and cointerventions will be documented in the CRFs, and no specific concomitant treatments are prohibited beyond standard contraindications and interactions with ASM and t-VNS.

Outcome measures

Primary outcome

The primary outcome is the proportion of patients achieving a reduction of at least 50% in mean seizure frequency at 52±2 weeks compared with baseline, based on seizure counts from a standardised epilepsy diary. Baseline seizure frequency will be documented over a 4-week period prior to treatment initiation, seizures will be recorded continuously throughout the 52-week treatment phase, and the primary outcome will be calculated from seizure counts over a 4-week period at month 12. The diary will be specifically designed for this trial to capture date, time, seizure type, potential triggers and ASM intake, allowing a valid and standardised assessment of changes in seizure burden.

Safety outcomes and assessment of harms

AEs, serious AEs (SAEs) and adverse device effects (ADEs, serious adverse device effects(SADEs) and unexpected serious adverse device effects (USADEs)) will be systematically collected, assessed and reported in accordance with the study protocol and applicable institutional and regulatory requirements. At each trial visit, patients will be actively asked about AEs, and safety information will be documented in validated eCRFs. SAEs will be reported to the sponsor within 24 hours. The sponsor will notify the responsible ethics committee and, where required, other oversight bodies in line with local regulations and institutional procedures for medical device incident reporting. An independent Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) will periodically review cumulative safety data, including SAEs and device deficiencies, according to its charter. Each event will be evaluated by the investigator for seriousness, causality and outcome, and regular reconciliation of trial and safety databases will ensure complete and accurate capture of all safety information.

Sample size calculation

The trial is powered to demonstrate superiority of t-VNS plus ASM over sham stimulation plus ASM for the primary outcome. Assuming response rates of 63% in the intervention group and 14% in the control group a superiority margin of 25 percentage points, a one-sided two-sample Z-test (unpooled; assumption that the binomial distribution for large samples can be approximated by the normal distribution), a significance level of 2.5% and 90% power, 130 evaluable patients (65 per group) are required; allowing for a 20% dropout rate, 164 patients (82 per group) will be enrolled.

Data analysis and management

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses will be prespecified in a detailed statistical analysis plan and conducted using validated statistical software (eg, SAS or R). The analysis of the primary outcome will be based on the intention-to-treat (ITT) population, that is, on the data of all randomised patients who meet the inclusion and exclusion criteria and have signed the consent form. A supporting per-protocol (PP) analysis of the primary outcome will be conducted, that is, a subset of patients from the ITT population, patients who completed the trial according to the study protocol, excluding those who violated the protocol.

Predefined sensitivity analyses for the primary and secondary endpoint(s) will be performed using mixed model for repeated measures and generalised linear model (GLM) to handle missing data under different assumptions (missing completely at random (MCAR), missing at random, missing not at random).

In addition, an exploratory interim analysis at 20 weeks will descriptively summarise the primary and secondary outcomes without formal hypothesis testing or alpha adjustment to provide data for potential future meta-analyses.

Descriptive analysis

All data are analysed using descriptive statistical methods by treatment group.

Analysis of primary outcome

For the confirmatory analysis of the primary outcome, a one-sided two-sample unpooled Z-test with a significance level of 2.5% will be used to test superiority of t-VNS plus ASM versus sham plus ASM. In addition, the risk difference with 95% CIs will be estimated using a GLM with binomial distribution and identity-link function. Prespecified sensitivity analyses will adjust for changes in ASM using fixed or random effects generalised linear (mixed) models, depending on the pattern of this potential confounder. For the primary outcome, missing outcome data in the main analysis will not be imputed under an MCAR assumption.

Analysis of secondary outcomes

Secondary outcomes will be analysed descriptively and exploratorily without adjustment for multiple testing. Changes in continuous outcomes and ordinal scores compared with the initial value will be analysed using mixed models for repeated measures, while binary outcomes will be evaluated using GLMs with binomial distribution and log-link function to estimate risk ratios with 95% CIs. The retention rate with 95% CIs will be analysed using time-to-event methods and is shown in a Kaplan-Meier plot. For the analyses, the ITT and PP population are used. For the secondary outcomes, missing outcome data in the main analysis will not be imputed under an MCAR assumption.

Safety analysis

Safety analyses will summarise AEs and device-related events by treatment group using MedDRA coding and descriptive statistics. Listings of all events and device-related safety data will be provided, and specific analyses of serious and device-related events will complement the overall safety profile.

Data monitoring

Patient safety will be monitored by an independent DSMB consisting of experts in neurology/epilepsy, data management in clinical trials and biostatistics, which will periodically review accumulated safety data and make recommendations on continuation, modification or early termination of the trial. The DSMB’s responsibilities, members, review schedule and decision-making procedures will be defined in a dedicated charter and a risk-based monitoring strategy will verify protocol adherence, data quality and patient safety at trial sites.

Clinical data will be stored in an electronic clinical data management system hosted on secure servers within the European Union, compliant with good clinical practice (GCP) and relevant regulatory requirements and equipped with an audit trail. Data management procedures, including database setup, validation, query handling and database lock, will be described in a trial-specific data management plan.

Adequate monitoring of the clinical trial will be ensured by the monitors who will verify investigator compliance with the clinical trial protocol and regulatory requirements.

Trial organisation

The overall coordination and sponsor responsibilities for this trial are provided by Gesundheitsforen Leipzig GmbH and Clinische Studien Gesellschaft mbH Berlin. These organisations oversee trial management, ensure protocol compliance, monitor data quality and facilitate communication among participating sites. The multidisciplinary project teams consist of clinical research professionals, data managers and administrative staff with relevant expertise. Regular meetings are held throughout the trial to address project progress, resolve operational issues and oversee adherence to the study protocol. Trial conduct will follow standard operating procedures to ensure consistent and reliable trial processes and data. Investigators and trial staff at participating centres will receive training in the protocol, eCRFs and applicable regulatory requirements and must meet predefined criteria regarding epilepsy expertise, GCP certification and site infrastructure. To achieve the planned sample size, recruitment will be closely monitored across all participating sites. In case of recruitment shortfalls, predefined escalation procedures will be implemented, including structured feedback and stepwise support for low-recruiting sites (eg, refresher training and joint development of recruitment strategies), and, if necessary, closure of sites with persistently insufficient recruitment despite these measures. The sponsor reserves the right to conduct audits at trial sites and other involved organisations, and all parties will cooperate with audits and regulatory inspections under appropriate confidentiality safeguards.

Patient and public involvement

The TRAVAST trial is commissioned by G-BA as part of the statutory evaluation of a new method in the German healthcare system. The trial contributes to the decision-making process regarding statutory health insurance reimbursement in routine care, and within this process the G-BA specified the overall framework for the trial design, including the target patient population and the primary outcome. Patients and/or the public were not involved in the design, or conduct, or reporting, or dissemination plans of this research.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethics approval and consent to participate

The current trial protocol (protocol V.2.0, 24 November 2025) was approved by the leading Ethics Committee, the Ethics Committee of LMU of Munich (project no.: 25–0916). In addition, participating trial sites are notified to the relevant local ethics committee before recruitment begins. As other clinical trials, the trial is submitted and evaluated under the national framework and is registered in the German Clinical Trials Register (DRKS00039592). Prior to enrolment, all patients will receive detailed oral and written information about the trial and will provide written informed consent, and participation will be voluntary with the right to withdraw at any time without consequences for medical care. Informed consent will be obtained by the site principal investigator or a delegated trial physician during a face-to-face discussion at the screening or enrolment visit, and written consent will be documented and stored at the trial site while participants receive a copy. An example of the written participant consent form is provided as online supplemental file 1.

Confidentiality

Personal data will be pseudonymised using unique trial identifiers and protected by appropriate technical and organisational measures. Access to identifiable information will be restricted to authorised trial personnel at the trial sites, and data will be stored and archived in encrypted form in compliance with applicable data protection regulations.

Dissemination

The main results of the TRAVAST trial will be published as a scientific publication on completion and entered in appropriate trial registries. Authors will be named according to the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors criteria, and conflicts of interest will be disclosed. Individual de-identified participant data (including data dictionary), statistical code and other trial materials will not be made publicly available because this is an exploratory trial with a small sample size and a high risk of indirect re-identification. Only aggregate results will be reported in the primary publication. The trial results will be communicated to participating physicians, patients and the public. All publications will respect data protection and ethical standards. Substantial protocol amendments will be submitted to ethics committees for approval before implementation and will be communicated to all participating investigators and updated in the trial registry. Updated protocol versions will be documented in the trial master file.

Discussion

As a G-BA commissioned trial embedded in the German statutory health insurance system, TRAVAST is designed to address a predefined evidence gap for reimbursement-relevant decision-making. This statutory evaluation context is important when interpreting the choice of population, comparator, endpoints and follow-up duration, which were selected to inform patient-relevant benefit assessment in routine care rather than to reproduce a conventional explanatory device trial design. The TRAVAST trial aims to fill important gaps in the evidence base for t-VNS as an add-on treatment in adults with pharmacoresistant epilepsy who are not eligible for or unwilling to undergo surgery. Earlier randomised t-VNS studies showed promising effects on seizure frequency but were limited by small samples, short follow-up, heterogeneous stimulation and control protocols and concerns about bias, leading German health technology assessment bodies to call for larger, methodologically robust trials with patient-relevant outcomes.

The choice of sham stimulation plus best individualised ASM as comparator reflects this evaluation framework and current routine care. Within the Section 137e SGB V context, TRAVAST is designed to determine whether adding t-VNS to optimised pharmacotherapy provides patient-relevant benefits compared with optimised pharmacotherapy alone, which is the standard of care for adults with pharmacoresistant epilepsy who are not eligible for, fail or decline, resective epilepsy surgery. The trial is therefore not intended to directly compare t-VNS with established invasive device therapies (such as implantable VNS, deep brain stimulation (DBS) or responsive neurostimulation (RNS)), but to address the predefined question of the added value of a non-invasive neuromodulatory option in this treatment setting.

In response to the limitations of earlier t-VNS trials, TRAVAST uses a randomised, triple-blind, sham-controlled, multicentre design, a 90% powered sample size based on a ≥50% responder definition and strict eligibility criteria requiring documented pharmacoresistance, minimum baseline seizure frequency and stable ASM. A fully specified stimulation regimen and device-embedded sham mode that mimics impedance checks and user feedback aim to minimise heterogeneity and bias, although subtle sensory differences over 12 months may still risk partial unblinding. The trial further strengthens the evidence base by combining seizure-frequency outcomes with a broad set of validated measures of seizure severity, quality of life, mood, anxiety, cognition and sleep over a 12-month follow-up, while recognising that reliance on patient-reported seizure diaries, the restriction to adults aged 18–65 years and the exclusion of patients with prior neuromodulation may limit generalisability and long-term inferences. As a G-BA commissioned trial embedded in the German statutory health insurance system, TRAVAST is well placed to inform future reimbursement decisions and guideline recommendations on the role of non-invasive VNS in pharmacoresistant epilepsy.

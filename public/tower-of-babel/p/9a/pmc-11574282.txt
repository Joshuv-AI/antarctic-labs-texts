Computationally guided high-throughput engineering of an anti-CRISPR protein for precise genome editing in human cells
Authors: Julia Marsiglia, Kia Vaalavirta, Estefany Knight, Muneaki Nakamura, Le Cong, Nicholas W Hughes
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe application of CRISPR-Cas systems to genome editing has revolutionized experimental biology and is an emerging gene and cell therapy modality. CRISPR-Cas systems target off-target regions within the human genome, which is a challenge that must be addressed. Phages have evolved anti-CRISPR proteins (Acrs) to evade CRISPR-Cas-based immunity. Here, we engineer an Acr (AcrIIA4) to increase the precision of CRISPR-Cas-based genome targeting. We developed an approach that leveraged (1) computational guidance, (2) deep mutational scanning, and (3) highly parallel DNA repair measurements within human cells. In a single experiment, ∼10,000 Acr variants were tested. Variants that improved editing precision were tested in additional validation experiments that revealed robust enhancement of gene editing precision and synergy with a high-fidelity version of Cas9. This scalable high-throughput screening framework is a promising methodology to engineer Acrs to increase gene editing precision, which could be used to improve the safety of gene editing-based therapeutics.Keywords: gene editing, anti-CRISPR, protein language model, high-throughput screening, deep mutational scan

Introduction

CRISPR-Cas technologies1,2,3 have emerged as powerful tools to interrogate gene function at genome scale in the laboratory4,5 and as potentially curative therapeutics.6,7 CRISPR-Cas9 found in Streptococcus pyogenes (SpCas9) can be programmed in human cells to specific genomic loci by engineered single guide RNA (sgRNA) molecules. While this mechanism enables programmable editing due to the modularity of the sgRNA sequence, partial complementarity between the sgRNA and multiple genomic loci in the same cell can lead to unintended nuclease activity and genotoxicity.8 This is problematic in a clinical setting where, for example, an unintended edit within a tumor suppressor gene or a chromosomal translocation could lead to oncogenesis.9,10 Additionally, human genetic variation can create off-target sites that are not present in the reference genome.11 Therefore, there have been extensive efforts to mitigate off-target effects through computational predictions of off-target regions based on the sgRNA sequence12,13 and engineering of the sgRNA and Cas nuclease components.14,15,16,17

In addition to these approaches, there is interest in developing strategies that use an orthogonal modulator of CRISPR-Cas to tune its editing activity. Anti-CRISPR proteins (Acrs) were initially identified in bacteriophages as an evasion mechanism against bacterial CRISPR-Cas immunity.18,19,20 The native activity of Acrs to inhibit CRISPR-Cas nuclease activity has been leveraged to increase gene editing precision through the addition of AcrIIA4, an Acr that inhibits SpCas9 activity by mimicking double-stranded DNA (dsDNA), after SpCas9 ribonucleoprotein (RNP) delivery.19,21 Another study analyzed whether the fusion of three AcrIIA4 protein variants that have attenuated inhibitory activity with SpCas9 could increase gene editing precision.22 We sought to build upon this conceptual framework to explore whether further high-throughput engineering of the interaction between AcrIIA4 and SpCas9 could be harnessed to fine-tune gene editing precision.

Computationally guided protein design has emerged as a powerful tool for engineering proteins with desirable properties.23,24 We wondered whether this approach, coupled with a classic structure-guided engineering analysis, could identify AcrIIA4 variants that enable precise genome editing outcomes. We hypothesized that the identification of engineering hotspots, which are predicted to have a strong functional impact on AcrIIA4 function, followed by deep mutational scanning of these regions would be an effective strategy to navigate the vast amino acid search space to identify optimal variants. Here, we developed an integrated computational and experimental approach to screen ∼10,000 AcrIIA4 variants in human cells expressing SpCas9 and validated the activity of the lead variants, which significantly reduced off-target activity in the pooled screen setting and within subsequent benchmarking experiments. Together, these results support that computationally guided high-throughput screening is an effective strategy to engineer AcrIIA4 to reduce the off-target activity of Cas9 and motivate this strategy for other genome engineering design challenges.

Results

Design of high-throughput off-target editing reporter assay in human cells

We aimed to tune the gene editing activity of SpCas9 by engineering its interaction with AcrIIA4. By altering the ability of AcrIIA4 to interact with SpCas9 and mimic dsDNA, we hypothesized that we could inhibit the off-target activity of SpCas9 while retaining its on-target activity (Figure 1A). Efficient engineering of AcrIIA4 within human cells requires a scalable and high-throughput assay to characterize gene editing precision over time. We took inspiration from conventional CRISPR screening methodologies, which group sgRNAs into lentiviral pools to query the resulting phenotypes of genetic perturbations at a genome-wide scale4,5 and in vivo.25 Here, instead of varying sgRNAs, we sought to vary the AcrIIA4 amino acid sequence. We created a sequence-based reporter system (AcrobaTx barcode) that would enable highly parallel profiling of on- and off-target editing across pooled AcrIIA4 variants. Briefly, the system contains both on- and off-target sites that are immediately downstream of an AcrIIA4 variant, thereby enabling the measurement of DNA repair outcomes and Acr variant identity within a single sequencing read. The on-target site sequence was derived from the AAVS1 locus, with the off-target site sequence differing from the on-target site sequence by 3 mismatches (STAR Methods). Similar approaches have been developed to profile DNA repair outcomes across a pool of different sgRNAs26,27,28,29,30 and genetic perturbations.31 The barcode was encoded within a lentiviral vector together with a sgRNA targeting the on-target site and delivered to lung adenocarcinoma A549 cells expressing SpCas9 (Figure 1B). Therefore, sgRNA expression and RNP formation would occur in parallel with the expression of the AcrIIA4 variants within the pool. As a result, genomic DNA extracted from the pool of cells could be used to amplify the reporter cassette for sequencing, thereby enabling the precise measurement of editing outcomes for both on- and off-target sites across the entire population of cells.

We first benchmarked this approach by comparing the editing activity of Cas9 within cells that contained AcrIIA4 and those that did not. The measurement of DNA repair outcomes over time revealed an accumulation of editing within ∼50% of cells in the absence of AcrIIA4 and an inhibition of editing within cells that expressed AcrIIA4 (Figure 1C). Visualization of DNA repair outcomes within the genome of cells lacking AcrIIA4 (Figure 1D) revealed frequent deletions between the predicted cut sites in the on- and off-target regions. The most frequent deletion was 38 nucleotides in length and exactly spanned the predicted cut sites, which is indicative of two separate editing events. In addition, single base pair insertion events were observed at the predicted cut sites that are presumably representative of frequent templated insertion events.29 These observations support the ability of the AcrobaTx barcode to model SpCas9 editing precision and showed that SpCas9 promiscuously edited both the on- and off-target sites in the absence of AcrIIA4. With a high-throughput assay established, we then sought to identify an optimal set of AcrIIA4 variants to study.

Design of AcrIIA4 variant library using computationally guided protein engineering

To exhaustively catalog the landscape of variant effects within AcrIIA4, we would have to test 20N variants, where N is the number of residues within the region of interest (ROI), thereby becoming experimentally intractable as the length of the ROI increases. Recently, protein language models (PLMs) have emerged as powerful tools to efficiently generate protein variants with altered activity.24,32 PLMs learn underlying sequence constraints from multi-sequence alignments (MSAs), which can be used as a representation of the underlying protein sequence-function relationships. Therefore, PLMs can be harnessed to compress the variant search space into an experimentally tractable set of variants.

The ESM-1v PLM is an ensemble of 5 models that each compute the log likelihood of observing a protein sequence variant given the sequence patterns within its training data. The uniformity of the ESM-1v predictions across the ensemble can be used as a proxy of evolutionary conservation that can capture long-range interactions that a standard MSA cannot. We hypothesized that regions where most variants were predicted to have low probabilities would be functionally significant, having been selected due to their ability to inhibit CRISPR-Cas9-based immunity, thereby allowing the phage to propagate within a bacterial population. As a result, these regions could serve as engineering hotspots that, when mutated, would attenuate the ability of AcrIIA4 to fully inhibit SpCas9 activity. Using this approach, we constructed a hotspot score, which identified residues at the C terminus of AcrIIA4 within loop 5 as a region that, if mutated, may attenuate AcrIIA4 function and tune SpCas9 activity (Figure 1E; STAR Methods).

Since the structure of AcrIIA4 in complex with SpCas9 has been solved and previously examined for hotspots using deep neural networks,21,33,34,35,36 we incorporated these data with the guidance provided by ESM-1v. To interrogate the differences between these two data types, we compared the distribution of hotspot scores generated by ESM-1v within all residues that have been structurally annotated to directly interact with SpCas9 versus those that do not and did not find a significant difference (Figure S1C). We also included AcrIIA2,20 another Acr, and similarly found that the ESM-1v hotspot scores did not significantly differ between the sets of interacting residues and non-interacting residues (Figure S1B). This suggests that ESM-1v is not simply identifying SpCas9-interacting residues and is potentially a complementary strategy to identify residues for further experimental validation. In total, the library was composed of 10,149 AcrIIA4 variants that included deep mutational scans37 within loops 4 and 5 and alanine scans that provide a baseline readout of single residue functionality (Figures 1F, 1G, and S1A).

Pooled protein screening to measure DNA repair outcomes across AcrIIA4 variants

We subsequently applied our AcrobaTx reporter system to assess the activity of our AcrIIA4 variant library. A549 lung adenocarcinoma cells were transduced with the library at low multiplicity of infection (MOI; ∼0.3) to minimize variations in gene editing activity due to copy-number or dominant-negative effects. After transduction, cells were selected with puromycin and allowed to proliferate for 10 days. During proliferation, edits would accumulate within the on- and off-target sites that are differentiated by an upstream barcode that identifies the AcrIIA4 variant, thereby enabling the mapping of gene editing precision to AcrIIA4 variant identity (Figure 2A). Across each variant, DNA repair outcomes were computed using the proportion of all reads containing an edit at the on- and off-target sites (editing efficiency [EE]) and the proportion of reads containing only on-target edits (editing precision [EP]) (Figures 2B and S1D; STAR Methods). As expected, the wild-type AcrIIA4 (AcrIIA4-WT) inhibited both on- and off-target editing, resulting in an EE of ∼0.05 (Figure 2E, left).

The alanine scans were first evaluated to determine the phenotypic consequence of removing R-group reactivity of a specific protein residue while keeping the protein’s backbone structure intact.38 Focusing on the EP phenotype, we visualized the EP normalized to that observed within cells expressing AcrIIA4-WT. Strikingly, a peak emerged at D69, which is a key residue that is known to directly interact with the protospacer adjacent motif (PAM)-interacting residue R1335 in SpCas9 and mediate binding affinity33 (Figures 1G, 2C, and 2D). In addition, this residue was at a maximum within the hotspot region identified through the ESM-1v-guided AcrIIA4 variant library design process (Figure 1E, asterisk). Together, these results suggest that ablating the D69 electrostatic interaction with R1335, which reduces AcrIIA4 binding affinity to SpCas9, increases gene editing precision in A549 human cells.

While the alanine scanning results supported known interactions between AcrIIA4 and SpCas9, we sought to identify protein variants that enhance gene editing precision through deep mutational scanning. First, we calculated the EE and EP for every variant. The median EE was ∼0.5, which is consistent with the overall EE calculated in cells that did not express AcrIIA4 (Figures 1C and 2E). Second, we observed frequent editing at the off-target site across variants. However, a subset of variants (above the 90th percentile of EP) showed nearly complete depletion of off-target editing activity (Figure 2E, right). Third, analysis of the insertion or deletion (indel) spectrum within a top-ranked variant (enAcr-1) revealed editing activity that was restricted to the on-target site, suggesting that the AcrIIA4 variant was sufficient to inhibit off-target editing activity while still maintaining on-target editing within this pooled readout (Figure 2F).

Finally, we examined the durability of the EP phenotype over time by measuring DNA repair outcomes at both days 7 and 10. We observed that the AcrIIA4-WT stably inhibited editing activity and that there was a reasonable correlation in EP across both time points for all variants (Pearson correlation coefficient r = 0.56) (Figure 2G). Notably, this correlation was sensitive to the sampling depth (number of sequencing reads) across all variants. To address this sensitivity, we introduced a clonal tag in subsequent experiments to measure the number of different cellular populations that express each variant (STAR Methods). The clonal tag provides additional information to directly address potential confounding effects whereby EP is driven by clonal bottleneck effects. From a technical perspective, these results support the accuracy of our technology to deconvolute DNA repair outcomes across AcrIIA4 variants that are expressed in a pooled fashion across millions of cells and motivate further reproducibility and validation studies.

Validation screen to identify lead AcrIIA4 variants that increase gene editing precision in human cells

Variants were rank ordered by EP, and the top 2,000 were resynthesized into a new variant validation pool for testing within an expanded cell line panel that included A549 that expressed Cas9 (A549-C9) and 293T human embryonic kidney cells expressing either Cas9 (293T-C9) or high-fidelity Cas9-HF115 (293T-C9-HF1). The same experimental design was used to measure EP after 7 days of editing as in round 1 (Figure 3A). Within A549 cell lines, the median of the EP distribution increased from 0.25 in round 1 to 0.55 in round 2 across all variants. Interestingly, the median of the EP distribution was higher within the 293T-C9 cell line, suggesting that the cell line context, potentially due to differences in Cas9 activity, sgRNA expression levels, or DNA repair pathway activity, can influence the EP distribution (Figure 3B). Since the Cas9 and sgRNA constructs are conserved across cell lines, the differences may be biological and reflective of the underlying DNA repair pathway activity or p53 status.39 Lastly, the EP distribution reflecting editing within 293T-C9-HF1 cells had a significantly higher median of 0.94 relative to all other sample groups. Consistent with expectations, this suggests that Cas9-HF1 had increased targeting specificity relative to SpCas9.

Seven lead AcrIIA4 variant candidates were identified based on their validation screen EP values (STAR Methods). Within these seven variants, there was an enrichment of hydrophobic residues in L5, which, in the WT form, contains hydrophilic residues that form electrostatic interactions with the C-terminal domain (CTD) of SpCas9.35 Their editing activity was visualized within the AcrobaTx barcode. Across all variants, minimal off-target editing was observed (<1%), whereas clear on-target editing (∼60%) was achieved (Figure 3C). To further assess the benefit of the engineered AcrIIA4 variants (enAcr-1–7), the ratio of on-target editing to off-target editing was calculated and compared to published AcrIIA4 variants that were reported to increase gene editing precision.22 Strikingly, there was a significant increase in the on-target to off-target editing ratio within the enAcr group relative to the benchmarks. In addition, the enAcrs further enhanced the precision of editing in the 293T-C9-HF1 cells, suggesting that their precision enhancing effects may be synergistic with that of SpCas9-HF1 (Figure 3D).

We then examined the robustness of the precision enhancing effect across a pool of DNA target sites that contained tiled mismatches relative to the sgRNA (STAR Methods). Four cell line groups were generated that stably expressed either enAcr-1 or a negative control AcrVA140 along with either SpCas9 (A549-C9) or SpCas9-HF1 (293T-C9-HF1). Cells were transduced with the pool of DNA target sites and cultured to allow edits to occur across the target sequences, which were uniquely barcoded to enable demultiplexing (Figures S2A and S2B). As expected, mismatched sites closer to the PAM, within the seed region, were edited at a lower rate than those sequences with PAM-distal mismatches. Overall, across all mismatched sites, there was a ∼20% reduction in editing of off-target sites by both SpCas9 and SpCas9-HF1 in cells expressing enAcr-1 versus those expressing AcrVA1 (Figures S2C and S2D). Together, these results suggest that enAcrs can reduce off-target editing activity across cell line contexts and in synergy with both Cas9 and Cas9-HF1.

enAcrIIA4 variants diminish off-target editing activity while retaining on-target editing activity

Cas9 RNP-based delivery has been implemented for editing in primary human cell contexts.41,42 While the precision of editing with RNPs is generally higher than viral-based delivery, off-target events are still observed and can lead to aneuploidy.43,44 We hypothesized that the enAcr molecules could serve as a prophylactic to reduce off-target activity in cells that are edited by SpCas9 in its RNP format. An advantage of this approach is that it does not require subsequent delivery of an AcrIIA4-WT, which has been shown to inhibit both on- and off-target editing.21 To test this, we generated 293T cell lines that stably expressed enAcr-1, AcrIIA4-WT, and AcrVA1.

We delivered RNP complexes through electroporation to cells that were individually transduced with the anti-CRISPR panel of proteins. After 96 h, we measured on- and off-target editing in cells that contain a gRNA that targeted the β-globin gene (HBB) or AAVS1. The latter was the target sequence used in the AcrobaTx barcode (Figure 4A). Importantly, β-globin is an independent locus that is both therapeutically relevant and contains numerous off-target sites. In addition, the ratio of on- to off-target editing at the AAVS1 and HBB loci increased by approximately 2-fold in cells expressing enAcr-1 relative to cells expressing the inert AcrVA1 control protein (Figures 4C, S3A, and S3B). As expected, the AcrIIA4-WT inhibited both on- and off-target editing at both the AAVS1 and HBB loci (Figure S3C). Notably, the enAcr-1 inhibited off-target editing by approximately ∼3-fold at the AAVS1 locus and ∼2-fold at the HBB locus while retaining on-target editing at both loci (Figure 4B).

We sought to identify whether a translocation occurred between HBB and an off-target region located on chromosome 5 (chr5-OFF) using a PCR-based approach since they have been previously observed.45 We identified a translocation event that resulted in the ligation of cleaved HBB downstream of the cut site located in the off-target region (Figures S4A and S4B). We hypothesized that there would be a reduction in translocation events in cells expressing enAcr-1 since it is able to inhibit off-target editing that is required for translocation to occur. We quantified the abundance of translocation events within a representative sample of cells and found that, relative to cells expressing AcrVA1, cells expressing enAcr-1 reduced the abundance of translocation events by about ∼9.8-fold (Figure S4C; STAR Methods). Together, these results suggest that the enAcrIIA4 variants can increase the specificity of editing by selectively diminishing off-target editing activity across two independent endogenous loci within the human genome.

Discussion

Ensuring efficient targeting while minimizing undesired genome modifications will become increasingly important as gene editing therapies become deployed more widely in clinical settings.6,7,46,47 Here, we demonstrate an integrated computational and experimental platform that can generate protein candidates and simultaneously measure on- and off-target editing activity for 10,000 or more variants at once. This approach adds to the expanding repertoire of methods available to perform deep mutational scans in mammalian cells and complements previous platforms that have been developed to screen Acrs within cell-free settings.48,49 Using this platform, we engineered AcrIIA4 variants that maintain desired on-target editing while reducing off-target editing events. Extensive work has been done to develop small-molecule inhibitors to tune SpCas9 activity50,51 and deactivated Cas9 (dCas9) activity using Acrs.52 This work adds to the pre-existing inhibitor toolkit with components that can be incorporated into existing recombinant gene therapy vectors that contain SpCas9. In addition, the AcrIIA4 variant can potentially be coupled to other SpCas9-based editing modalities like base editing,53,54 prime editing,55 and homologous recombination-based knockin approaches56 to further boost their precision.

Anti-CRISPR molecules serve as attractive engineering substrates for tuning CRISPR activity due to their compact size and evolved specificity of activity.57 In addition, they form a separable functional domain that can be engineered orthogonally from CRISPR systems. Furthermore, given the growing diversity of identified anti-CRISPRs, we anticipate that our approach will be extensible to a wide range of Cas effectors to increase their precision and suitability for downstream therapeutic and industrial applications.

Limitations of the study

The primary purpose of this work was to develop an experimental protein engineering platform suitable for simultaneous and scalable testing of the ability of an Acr variant to modulate on- and off-target editing by SpCas9. While we do not anticipate significant hurdles for applying the platform to other Acr systems, we do not directly assess the generalizability of the method here. Studies assessing the generalizability of this approach across additional Acrs will be an exciting avenue of future research. Additionally, while our experimental platform is agnostic to the computational method used, we selected ESM-1v due to its ability to perform zero-shot predictions of variant effects across several protein families.32 It is possible that other emerging models may provide better predictions depending on the specific engineering goal. To assess the use of ESM-1v for engineering AcrIIA4, we performed a retrospective comparison comparing the set of residues ranked by computational predictions32,58 with the top-ranked residues from our screening data, which served as the ground truth (Figure S5; STAR Methods). Although this analysis supported the sensitivity of ESM-1v to identify functionally significant residues, further studies would need to be conducted to provide more comprehensive benchmarking as new computational methods for variant effect prediction emerge.

We report here optimized Acr variants (enAcr-1) but do not determine the mechanism by which they modulate EP. We speculate that a few mechanisms centered around the modulation of AcrIIA4-SpCas9 binding could be at play. First, a reduction in AcrIIA4 binding affinity to SpCas9 may be sufficient to switch between almost total inhibition of SpCas9 to releasing a small number of catalytically active nucleases, which may kinetically bind preferentially to and cleave the on-target locus.22 Second, mutagenesis of the contact of the AcrIIA4 and the CTD of Cas9 may allow for initial PAM recognition followed by R-loop formation (Figure S4D). The remaining interactions between the AcrIIA4 variant and the SpCas9 Topo-homology (Topo) and RuvC domains may trap the complex in an inactive state and prevent nuclease activity. However, full complementarity between the sgRNA and target DNA may provide sufficient binding energy and torque to drive off variant AcrIIA4, while the torque generated by partial R-loop formation resulting from sgRNA-DNA mismatches may not.59,60,61 Further investigation will help to elucidate the mechanism of precision enhancement and lead to an enhanced understanding of Cas9 and AcrIIA4 function.

Experimental model and study participant details

Cell line and cell culture

HEK-293T and A549 cell lines were cultured in DMEM, high glucose supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 100 U/mL penicillin and 100 μg/mL streptomycin at 37 C with 5% CO2.

Method details

Computational identification of protein engineering hotspots

All possible single amino acid mutations were generated across the reading frame of AcrIIA4 (20 × 87 matrix) and used as input into the ESM-1v model. Each of the 1740 single substitution variants had an associated ensemble of log-likelihoods generated by the model using default parameters. The uniformity of the ESM-1v ensemble prediction distribution was quantified using the Shannon entropy metric. The inverse of the entropy metric was computed to generate a prediction of sensitivity to mutation, which is plotted as a heatmap (Figures 1B and S1A). The sum of the sensitivity metric at each position was calculated to generate a hotspot score and bound to the range of values between 0 and 1. The hotspot score is a proxy of sequence conservation within the protein (Figure 1B).

AcrIIA4 library design

After identification of hotspots through an unbiased ESM1-v-guided approach, empirical structural information was used to further refine the hotspot based on AcrIIA4 contact with SpCas9 (Figure S1A). A custom python script was used to randomly generate amino acid sequences within the engineering hotspot and to reverse translate the variant amino acid sequence into a human codon optimized DNA sequence space. The DNA sequences were further modified to include cloning sites and unique barcode sequences for identification prior to assembly within a lentiviral backbone.

Molecular cloning of the AcrIIA4 benchmark lentiviral backbone

A plasmid was constructed containing lentiviral components along with a puromycin resistance gene followed by a 2A peptide with Type IIS restriction sites to enable in-frame ligation of AcrIIA4 variants. On- and off-target Cas9 editing sites were located downstream of the cloning region. Additional artificial SpCas9 target sites were cloned downstream of the type II-S cloning sites through the NEB HiFi assembly (NEB) approach using the manufacturer’s instructions to produce the final pAG002 backbone. To benchmark the ability of the SpCas9 synthetic target sites to report AcrIIA4 activity, wild-type AcrIIA4 was cloned downstream of the 2A peptide along with an empty negative control that contained the parental Type IIS cloning site sequence.

Lentiviral preparation and delivery to benchmark AcrIIA4 activity

Lentivirus was prepared by co-transfecting pAG002 with VSV-G envelope and Delta-Vpr packaging plasmids into HEK-293T cells using Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (ThermoFisher). Supernatant was harvested 48 h and 72 h after transfection. A549 cells expressing the pAG001 Cas9 vector (Genecopeia) were transduced at high MOI with 8 μg/mL polybrene using a spin-infection at 1200∗g for 45 min. After 48 h, cells were selected with 1 μg/mL puromycin to establish stably expressing cell lines for gene editing analysis.

Large-scale lentiviral preparation and pooled screen tissue culture

Lentivirus was prepared as described above with slight modifications. First, lentivirus was concentrated by 10X using PEG-it virus precipitation solution (System Biosciences) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Concentrated virus was then used for a functional titer in which a concentration consistent with an MOI of ∼0.3 was identified. Subsequently, cells stably expressing SpCas9 (pAG001) were transduced at a scale to achieve ∼1000X cells/Acr variant after puromycin selection of the pAG002 pooled library component.

Ribonucleoprotein delivery of SpCas9

The electroporation procedure was conducted using the Invitrogen Neon Transfection System with 100 μL tips (Thermo Scientific). Alt-R CRISPR-Cas9 crRNA and Alt-R CRISPR-Cas9 crRNA XT (IDT) were reconstituted in IDTE Buffer to achieve a final concentration of 200 μM each. Subsequently, these oligos were mixed in equimolar proportions in a sterile microcentrifuge tube, resulting in a final duplex concentration of 100 μM. The duplex complex underwent denaturation at 95°C for 5 min and was then allowed to cool at room temperature for 5 min. For the formation of the ribonucleoprotein (RNP) complex, 120 pmol of each Alt-R guide RNA was combined with 104 pmol Alt-R S.p. Cas9 Nuclease V3 and incubated at room temperature for 15 min. Cell lines stably expressing anti-CRISPR proteins were trypsinized, washed twice with PBS, and resuspended in 100 μL of resuspension buffer R plus the RNP complex. Electroporation was carried out using the following program: Pulse voltage: 1,100 V, Pulse width: 20 ms, and Pulse number: 2. Post-transfection, cells were plated in prewarmed medium and collected 4 days after transfection for subsequent analysis.

Screening of mismatched DNA target sequences

A single mismatch tiled library was constructed using a custom python script to generate amino acid mismatch in all positions of the target sequence and assign a barcode sequence that reported the mismatch identity. The mismatched DNA target sequence was paired with either the engineered Acr (enAcr-1) or a negative control (AcrVA1) plus the guide RNA sequence in a lentiviral vector with a similar configuration as pAG002 (Figure 2A). Lentivirus was prepared by co-transfecting the mismatch library with VSV-G envelope and Delta-Vpr packaging plasmids into HEK-293T cells using Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (ThermoFisher). Supernatant was harvested 48 h and 72 h after transfection. A549 cells expressing Cas9 (Genecopeia) and HEK293T cells expressing Cas9-HF1 (Genocopeia) were transduced with 8 μg/mL polybrene using a spin-infection at 1000∗g for 45 min. After 48 h, cells were selected with 1 μm g/mL puromycin to establish stably expressing cell lines for gene editing activity measurements. Cells were kept in culture for 7 days to allow editing to occur and harvested for DNA extraction and subsequent analysis.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Next-generation sequencing analysis workflow

Primers were designed to amplify the AcrobaTx cassette containing the Acr variant-specific barcode sequence and Cas9 target sites. This cassette was used to measure editing efficiency at both the on- and off-target sites to derive metrics for overall editing efficiency (EE) and editing precision (EP). Unless otherwise noted these metrics were calculated considering both reads bearing putative indel mutations and unedited reads. Reads were processed using a custom pipeline. Briefly, reads were trimmed and merged using publicly available software (cutadapt 4.4and FLASh v1.2.11). Next, the target region was assigned to an Acr variant using the variant’s unique barcode. For the experiment containing the mismatch tiled library, the target region was demultiplexed by the adjacent barcode sequence. The target region was then aligned to the reference sequence to call indel mutations using the Needleman-Wunsch (EMBOSS:6.6.0.0) algorithm for calculation of EE and EP. For the mismatched tiled library, the editing efficiency of Cas9 at the target site was calculated in a streamlined fashion by computing the Levenshtein distance between the extracted target sequence and the reference mismatch sequence. To control for background sequencing or synthesis error, editing signal observed in the plasmid pool was used to normalize the edits observed in the experimental samples. For direct alignment-based editing calculations, the alignments were parsed to identify mutations within the predicted target window (three base pairs upstream of the PAM site).

Quantification of editing at endogenous genomic loci

Raw reads in FASTQ format were analyzed for editing activity at each on- and off-target site by CRISPResso version 2.2.12 using the batch settings and default parameters for Cas9: Center of the quantification window −3, Quantification window size of 2 base pairs with substitutions excluded. The median number of reads across all samples was approximately 100,000 reads per sample.

Quantification of translocation events

qPCR was conducted with PowerUp SYBR Green Master Mix for qPCR (Applied Biosystem) in a 10 μL reaction volume on a QuantStudio 7 Flex Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystem) using 50 ng of DNA per reaction. The standard cycling mode as suggested by the manufacturer was used: 50°C for 2 min, 95°C for 2 min and 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 s (denature) and 60°C for 1 min (annealing). Image acquisition was performed on each cycle after the annealing step. A melt curve was performed after the PCR for quality control. The relative quantification was achieved using ΔΔCq analyses with GAPDH as the reference gene and levels within the AcrVA1 cells as the control.

Comparative analysis of high-fitness variant identification

Precision enhancing variants were identified from experimental data to serve as ground truth. Briefly, variants within the mutagenized region of AcrIIA4 (L5) were rank ordered by editing precision. To approximate the effects of varying single amino acids, the frequency of each amino acid was computed at each position within the mutagenized regions. The top 10 most frequent amino acids at each position were selected to be placed within the ground truth set for overlap analysis with ESM-1v- and DeepSequence-based predictions made for all possible single amino acid variants. DeepSequence was trained using default parameters on an MSA consisting of 1754 proteins that was generated through an iterative protein search of the Uniref. 100 database. Briefly, jackhmmer was used (HMMER v3.4) using the AcrIIA4 input search sequence and an e-val cutoff of 0.05. The intersection between ESM-1v- and DeepSequence-based variants rank-ordered by fitness prediction (at or above 70th percentile) that were within the experimentally validated set of high-fitness variants was evaluated and plotted (Figure S5).

Impacts of Competitive a Health Professionals Education Partnership Initiatives Small Grant Award on Junior Faculty Researchers: A Cross‐Sectional Study
Authors: Taklu Marama, Derartu Neme, Lielt Gebrekirstos, Rediet Gido, Habtamu Messele, Adiam Nega, Damen Hailemariam, Miliard Derbew, Abebaye Aragaw
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground and AimsMany organizations worldwide have made significant financial commitments to fund research across various sectors. However, there has been little study on the performance of these awards, particularly in underdeveloped countries. Thus, this study aimed to evaluate the immediate impacts of the Competitive Health Professionals Education Partnership Initiative (HEPI) Small Grant Award on junior faculty researchers.MethodsAn institutional‐based concurrent mixed‐method study was conducted from October 2023 to April 2024 at Addis Ababa, Dilla, and Wolaita Sodo Universities. The study included 131 applicants (40 awardees and 91 non‐awardees) over 5 years. Quantitative data were collected using a pre‐tested, semi‐structured, self‐administered questionnaire via Google Drive and directly exported to SPSS version 20 for analysis. Descriptive statistics were followed by appropriate independent t‐tests and χ 2 tests with a significance level of 0.05. For the qualitative part, in‐depth and key‐informant interviews were conducted with 13 participants. Qualitative data were transcribed, translated, coded, and analyzed thematically using MAXQDA 2020 software.ResultsQuantitative results demonstrated that the only variable that differed significantly between awardees and non‐awardees was the number of works presented at conferences after HEPI small grant applications. From a qualitative perspective, generally positive qualitative impacts were found to have been made by the grant on junior faculty researchers in the areas of career and professional development, enhancing research capacity, contributing to the community and policy, fostering collaboration with researchers and different universities, enhancing interdisciplinary work and sustainability of education, and disseminating the grant output.ConclusionsAccording to quantitative and qualitative findings, the Competitive HEPI Small Grant Award had varying impacts on junior faculty researchers.Keywords: Health Professionals Education Partnership Initiative, Impacts of Research Grants, junior faculty researchers

Introduction

Research and scholarly publishing are usually regarded as essential components of academic activity in higher education institutions. These efforts contributed to continuing professional development, increased institutional knowledge production, and provided evidence to inform societal advancement [1]. Consequently, universities are increasingly prioritizing the support of faculty involvement in research as an essential aspect of their academic duties [2].

Access to research funding is a pivotal factor influencing research productivity and the establishment of a sustainable research culture [3]. Competitive funding enables researchers to design and implement rigorous investigations, acquire vital resources, and participate in professional activities that enhance scientific output. Empirical studies demonstrate that funded researchers are more likely to publish, advance in their careers, and promote innovation in their disciplines. Research financing improves a researcher's skills in grant writing, research management, and scientific communication, hence fostering ongoing academic advancement [4, 5, 6].

Research funding, defined as financial awards designated to facilitate scientific investigation, is assessed through multiple criteria [3]. The assessment systematically examines individual researcher development, including professional growth, grant‐writing competencies, and scholarly productivity, as well as broader academic contributions, such as conference presentations, mentoring activities, and participation in peer‐review processes. In addition, the evaluation considers quantitative indicators, including the number of research grant applications submitted and those awarded. Beyond individual researcher development, it addresses institutional capacity building, particularly regarding the advancement of knowledge and the promotion of evidence‐based decision‐making [4, 7].

Successful research, however, depends not only on financial support but also on the availability of enabling conditions. The generation of innovative ideas, strong researcher commitment, and adequate research infrastructure are essential determinants of research quality [8]. Institutions with strong mentorship structures, reliable administrative support, and functional facilities tend to achieve higher levels of scholarly productivity [9].

In this research context, junior faculty researchers are lecturers (Master's, teaching‐focused, supervised research growth) and assistant professors (doctorate/4+ years experience with Masters, publication record, and community service record). Lecturers primarily teach and support undergraduate research. Assistant professors mainly lead postgraduate research, demonstrating advanced institutional engagement. Career stage and autonomy are key differentiators, yet both are crucial for academic development [10].

Junior faculty researchers are particularly vulnerable to structural and resource‐related barriers. In Ethiopia and the broader Africa, particularly, they face unclear career pathways, limited mentorship, restricted access to funding, heavy teaching workloads, and weak research infrastructure [11, 12]. These systemic challenges limit the capacity of junior faculty to produce impactful research, collaborate internationally, and advance within academic ranks. In turn, low research productivity can further reduce future opportunities for promotion, funding, and scholarly visibility [13].

Despite the recognized importance of research funding, the specific influence of grant programs on the productivity of junior faculty remains insufficiently studied. The lack of systematic evidence complicates efforts by funding agencies to revise their grant processes or assess long‐term program impact [9].

To address these gaps, the Health Professional Education Partnership Initiative (HEPI), funded through a USD 3.5 million award from the U.S. NIH Fogarty International Center, has focused on strengthening research capacity among junior faculty at Wolaita Sodo Univer, Dilla, and Addis Ababa Universities [14]. Therefore, this study aims to assess the impacts of competitive HEPI small grant awards on research productivity among junior faculty researchers at Addis Ababa University (AAU), Dilla University (DU), and Wolaita Sodo University (WSU).

Methods and Material

Study Area and Period

This study was conducted at Wolaita Sodo, Addis Ababa, and Dila Universities. It was conducted from October 2023 to April 2024.

Study Design and Population

In this study, a concurrent mixed institution‐based design was used. Junior faculty researchers who have worked at Wolaita Sodo, Dilla, and Addis Ababa Universities who applied for the small grant award participated in this study. Staff at the different offices of the three institutions who have linked with the HEPI also participated in this study as key informants.

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

All junior faculty researchers who applied for the grant and the key informants were included in the study. However, participants who were not present at the time of data collection, who were not interested in participating in the study, and the authors of this study were excluded from this study.

Sample Size

All (233) junior faculty applicants for the small grant call from HEPI (62 awardees and 171 non‐awardees) were invited to participate in the quantitative study. For the qualitative part, a total of 13 participants who were not involved in the quantitative study were included in the qualitative study. Four non‐awardees, six awardees, and three key informants participated in the in‐depth interview.

Data Collection Tools and Procedure

Semi‐structured self‐administered questionnaires, adapted from previous literature, were sent via Google Drive to HEPI small grant junior faculty applicants. These questionnaires gathered information on socio‐demographic parameters, research productivity, capacity‐building, professional development, career development, financial support, training, and education. Separate questionnaires were prepared in English for awardees and non‐awardees.

For qualitative data, in‐depth interview guides were used to explore participants' experiences, feelings, and attitudes regarding the impacts of the Competitive HEPI Small Grant Award on Junior faculty researchers. Interviews, lasting about 30 min, were conducted in participants' usual settings in person. The questionnaires were initially prepared in English, translated into Amharic, and then back‐translated to ensure consistency.

Data Quality Control and Assurance

The questionnaires were reviewed by experts and pretested on 5% of the sample. Amendments were made based on feedback. Participants were informed about the study and selection process, and their concerns were addressed. Confidentiality and privacy were ensured, and interviews were conducted in convenient settings. Researchers checked completed questionnaires for completeness and consistency.

For qualitative data, interviews were transcribed immediately, with field notes taken to capture non‐verbal cues. Data were read repeatedly to understand key ideas, ensuring trustworthiness. Finally, qualitative data were triangulated with quantitative data.

Data Processing and Analysis

The quantitative data set was retrieved from Google Drive in its raw format and underwent initial cleaning in Microsoft Excel (Microsoft Office 365), including verification of data completeness, identification of missing values, and correction of entry errors. The curated dataset was then imported into IBM SPSS Statistics (Version 20.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY) for statistical analysis.

We conducted analyses in three stages. First, descriptive statistics (means ± standard deviations for continuous variables; counts and percentages for categorical variables) characterized all study variables. For inferential analyses, we used (1) independent samples t‐tests to compare continuous outcomes between awardees and non‐awardees and (2) χ 2 tests for categorical variable comparisons. All tests were two‐tailed with an a priori α‐level of 0.05 for statistical significance.

To complement null hypothesis testing, we report (a) precision estimates (95% confidence intervals) for all mean differences and proportions and (b) Cohen's d effect sizes for between‐group comparisons (interpreted as: 0.2 = small, 0.5 = medium, ≥ 0.8 = large effect).

The qualitative audio data underwent verbatim transcription and English translation, followed by thematic analysis using MAXQDA 2020. Through iterative review and coding, we systematically analyzed the content to complement the quantitative findings.

Ethical Approval

Ethical approval was obtained from the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Wolaita Sodo University. For the quantitative participants, as data collection was conducted online via Google Forms, their willingness to participate was requested initially. Those who indicated their willingness were directed to the questionnaire, while those who did not were stopped without proceeding to the questionnaire. For the qualitative participants, after being informed about the research, written consent was obtained from each individual prior to their participation.

Results

Quantitative Result

Sociodemographic Characteristics of the Study Participants

Out of 131 HEPI small grant applicants (40 awardees and 91 non‐awardees) who responded to the survey (56% response rate), most were males (77.1%) aged 25–34 years (79.4%). Nearly half had less than 5 years of teaching experience (49.5%) and were single (64.9%). Over half had been at their institution for 5–10 years (64.9%), had roles beyond teaching (64.9%), were from DU (51.9%), and the School of Allied Health Sciences (39.7%). More participants had 12 or fewer lecture equivalent hours (LEHs) per week (54.2%) compared to those with more than 12 LEHs (45.8%). Lastly, 71.8% had a monthly salary of 10,470 Ethiopian Birr (Table 1).

Impact of the HEPI Small Grant Award on Research Activities Among Awardees

Among the 40 HEPI small grant awardees who responded to the survey, 62.5% were male. The fewest awardees (20%) were from AAU, while WSU and DU each had 40%. Only 20% of the funded research resulted in a published article. However, 95% of recipients gained skills, with 57.5% empowering the study participants. Additionally, 60% reported strengthened institutional research resources, and 80% utilized the research findings. Furthermore, 62.5% used the findings for decision‐making, and 65% of the research was based on institutional demands. The majority of the research findings were utilized for educational purposes (32.5%), followed by clinical practice (27.5%), health management (17.5%), and other purposes (Table 2).

Effects of the Small Grant Award on Research Productivity Among Applicants (N = 131; awardees = 40, non‐Awardees = 91)

The analysis, conducted using independent samples t‐tests awardees reported a higher average number of articles published after the grant application (6.35 vs. 5.01), though this difference was not statistically significant (t‐test, p = 0.29) and reflected a small effect size (*d* = 0.20). Similarly, awardees submitted slightly more grant applications (2.60 vs. 2.19, *p* = 0.36) and secured marginally more non‐HEPI grants (1.08 vs. 0.89, *p* = 0.391), but these effects were also negligible (*d* = 0.17 for both). The most striking difference emerged in conference presentations: awardees presented over twice as many works on average (2.25 vs. 0.97, t‐test, p < 0.001), with a moderate‐to‐large effect size (d = 0.73), underscoring the grant's significant role in enhancing research visibility. Other metrics—such as certificates obtained, articles reviewed, and students advised—showed no meaningful differences between groups. Notably, non‐awardees advised more students on average (10.14 vs. 6.63), but this finding was marred by high variability (SD = 22.46) and non‐significance (t‐test, p = 0.329). Collectively, the results suggest that while the HEPI grant had a robust impact on conference participation, its influence on broader productivity measures (e.g., publications, additional funding) was limited, with most effects being small or non‐significant (Table 3). The χ² test indicated non‐awardees significantly reapplied more often (χ²(3) = 28.14, p < 0.001) compared to awardees (Table 4).

Qualitative Results

The qualitative findings of the study revealed several key themes. Theme 1 focused on participants' insight about the HEPI small research grant award, highlighting Sub‐theme 1.1, the various sources of information through which applicants learned about the grant. Sub‐theme 1.2 explored the motivating factors that encouraged individuals to apply. Theme 2 delved into applicants' perceptions related to the status of their submitted proposals. Theme 3 examined the impact of the HEPI small grant, with Sub‐theme 3.1 emphasizing its role in enhancing research capacity. Sub‐theme 3.2 highlighted how the grant supported professional and career development. Sub‐theme 3.3 illustrated how the research contributed to the community and informed policy, aligning academic work with societal needs. Sub‐theme 3.4 discussed the promotion of collaboration among researchers, institutions, and disciplines. Sub‐theme 3.5 focused on how the grant enhanced interdisciplinary approaches and the sustainability of education. Sub‐theme 3.6 addressed the dissemination of research outputs. Theme 4 captured the worthwhile experiences gained through the grant process. Finally, Theme 6 identified challenges faced by applicants and grantees.

Theme 1: Insight About the HEPI Small Research Grant Award

Sub‐Theme 1.1: Source of Information

The participants reported hearing about the HEPI project's research funding through various sources, including departments, billboard posters, and staff Telegram accounts. Additionally, the majority of respondents stated that they learned about it from coworkers and past award recipients.

I first heard about HEPI informally from a university friend and later received detailed information via a Telegram message.

(IDI 2 WSU participant, Pos. 6)

I obtained information about the HEPI small grant award from previous grant award awardees.

(IDI 3 DU participant, Pos. 4)

Subtheme 1.2: Motivating Factors

In‐depth interviews revealed that researchers were motivated to apply for the grant due to their desire for external funding, contributing to the scientific community, pursuing their research interests, and the thematic areas of the grant. They also aimed to support the university's mission, address community issues, and advance their careers. The HEPI project's thematic focus and innovative approach were additional motivations.

I was motivated to apply after understanding HEPI's focus on unexplored and community‐based research areas.

(IDI 1, DU participant)

Theme 2: Applicants' Perception Related to the Status of Their Submitted Proposal

Participants attributed winning the HEPI grant to innovative proposals and a bias‐free selection process. They noted the grant's focus on motivating junior faculty researchers and benefiting the community. Non‐awardees cited lack of time, competitive nature, and misalignment with HEPI priorities as reasons for not winning.

The application announcement was only [open] for 1 week, which wasn't enough time to prepare.

(IDI 1 DU participant, Pos. 7)

I learned about the grant 5 days before the deadline, so my draft might not have met the format requirements.

(IDI 7 WSU participant, Pos. 7)

Theme 3: HEPI Small Grant Impact

Subtheme 3.1: Enhancing Research Capacity

According to their response, most of them reported these small grant helped the researchers to do quality research, enhance other research‐related skills, and build the capacity of staff in the junior universities. At the same time, this application made them develop research skills, and grant writing skills and enhanced their skills for further grant applications for mega projects.

The HEPI project awarded small grants to over twenty staff members. Additionally, qualitative research has increased at our institution following HEPI's training.

(KII 2 DU participant, Pos. 7)

I view scientific contributions as a valuable input. By offering researchers the chance to conduct research, they can enhance their skills. As they improve, both the college and the research center benefit. Additionally, financial support is another crucial input.

(KII 3 DU participant, Pos. 10)

The project aims to support and motivate junior universities, like ours compared to Addis Ababa University, by building their capacity.

(KII 2 DU participant, Pos. 5)

Subtheme 3.2 Professional and Career Development

The majority of respondents indicated that the HEPI small grant had a significant impact on their professional development and enhanced their academic careers. They noted that the grant provided opportunities to gain acceptance into prestigious international universities. Additionally, some respondents mentioned that the grant incentives motivated them to conduct more research, engage in volunteer work, and publish their findings in academic journals, thereby increasing their chances of promotion.

Furthermore, the current study demonstrated that the small grant award significantly benefited the researchers. It enabled them to update their professional profiles, increase their publication output, secure additional research opportunities, and broaden their experiences and skills. These advancements collectively contributed to the further development of their career trajectories.

I published my work, and it has significantly advanced my career, particularly in securing research funds and scholarships. Before attending the HEPI grant writing course, I was unaware of how to craft a successful grant proposal. Following the course, I not only won grants but also established valuable connections with other experts in the field.

(IDI 6 WSU participant, Pos. 12)

It inspires me to seek more grants, promotions, and scholarships to advance my economic development. It significantly boosted my career as I encouraged others to submit proposals, and two successful ones included me as a co‐author, positively impacting my profile.

(IDI 6 WSU participant, Pos. 8)

Subtheme 3.3 Contribution to the Community and Policy

The impacts of HEPI small grant awards were contributions to society, to the community, the novel concepts and scientific contents the researchers included within, and the research topics addressed unexplored societal issues. The research finding shows that the majority of research carried out under this small grant was crucial in exposing hidden issues that had been overlooked in earlier literature and presenting suitable solutions derived from the findings to the relevant authorities to carry out the appropriate interventions.

My laboratory‐based experimental study had a limited budget, but we aimed to identify community problems. Despite the small budget, we managed to publish significant findings.

(IDI 11 WSU participant, Pos. 11–12)

Subtheme 3.4: Collaboration

The findings of this study revealed that the HEPI small research grant award significantly enhanced collaboration between professionals and universities. The grant facilitated the establishment of productive partnerships and long‐term communication channels among staff members and participating universities. This, in turn, improved the exchange of experiences among researchers from various fields and academic institutions, fostering a more integrated and collaborative research environment.

Before attending the HEPI grant writing course, I didn't know how to create a winning grant proposal. The program provided the necessary information, and afterward, I established valuable connections with other experts.

(IDI 6 WSU participant, Pos. 12)

Subtheme 3.5: Enhance Interdisciplinary and Sustainability of Education

Participants observed that the project fostered a culture of interdisciplinary education, generating a tangible impacts on scholarship designed to sustain long‐term pedagogical devlopment. Regarding these interdisciplinary outcomes, two participants from DU noted:

The HEPI project aims to motivate interdisciplinary education and provide interdepartmental training.

(KII 2 DU participant, Pos. 5)

The college now has a research team and sub‐team, with HEPI working to enhance pre‐service education. These initiatives are expected to sustain quality education by fostering competition.

(KII 3 DU participant, Pos. 8)

Subtheme 3.6 Dissemination of the Output

In this research, we reviewed multiple databases and found that eight grants had been published. Some participants also mentioned publishing their grants, with a few presented at conferences. For instance, one respondent from WSU noted:

The paper has been widely read and cited. I presented it at the Internal Medicine's Professional Association. Another presentation was selected for a workshop at Mizan Tep University, but it was canceled due to COVID‐19 budget constraints.

(IDI 11 WSU participant, Pos. 4)

The growing number of publications is boosting the college's academic profile. Some staff have been promoted, and certain research projects are aiding community services.

(KII 3 DU participant, Pos. 8)

Theme 4: Worth Taking Experiences

The interviewees highlighted the best experiences from the HEPI small grant award, such as the timely release of budget and ethical clearance, which enabled researchers to complete their projects on time. They noted that the projects were innovative, relevant, feasible, and beneficial to the community. Most participants agreed that the competition was free of bias and discrimination. Additionally, the involvement of experienced scholars added significant value to the projects. One participant from DU stated:

Both ethical clearance and funding were obtained on time, helping us complete and publish our findings quickly.

(IDI 4 DU participant, Pos. 12)

Anothe from WSU added, “Since receiving the HEPI funding, I have never faced any difficulties. The budget was released on schedule, and ethical clearance was promptly issued.

(IDI 5 WSU participant, Pos. 17)

Theme 6: Challenges

Another issue identified by the respondents was the lack of clear assessment criteria. The feedback regarding the evaluation was comprehensive but challenging to pinpoint modifiable areas. Additionally, the researchers noted the absence of consistent monitoring and follow‐up of the study's progress as a positive aspect. For example, there was no validating research workshop at the conclusion of the grants.

we are not invited to participate in validating research findings.

(KII 3 DU participant, Pos. 18)

Participants noted that the number of grants awarded each year is limited. They identified financial constraints as a primary issue, with the same funding amount allocated for all research designs, leading to insufficient funds to complete studies on time. Additionally, they mentioned the lack of a database for research data orientation.

There are gaps in communication within the research office. It appears that the collaboration is stronger with the staff rather than the research office itself.

(KII 3 DU participant, Pos. 18)

Discussion

In this study, we found that the HEPI small grants fostered research skills and built capacity among junior faculty researchers. While prior studies show that small grants enhance foundational knowledge [15], problem‐solving skills, and enhancing productivity [16]. Our findings illustrate how these benefits are especially significant for early‐career researchers working in resource‐constrained environments.

Our study has also demonstrated the contribution of the HEPI small grant award to the junior faculty researchers' professional growth and improvement of their academic careers. These findings are consistent with prior evidence that grant funding enhances career progression [6] and supports professional development even among unsuccessful applicants [17] though measurable increases in productivity often require larger grants or extended timelines [18]. In countries such as Ethiopia, where junior academics often encounter substantial teaching responsibilities, inadequate research facilities, and a lack of mentorship, these initial professional advancements may be especially impactful. The HEPI small grant serves as a developmental platform that fosters future academic productivity and career progression.

The contribution to society was identified as one of the an important outcome impacted by the HEPI small grant program in this study. Recipients described how funding enabled them to address community health priorities, engage with the community, and conduct research with direct societal relevance. In a region where health needs are substantial and research capacity is limited, these findings indicate that even modest grants can strengthen the relationship between universities and the communities they serve. These grants foster community‐based health interventions that benefit underserved populations [19, 20]. Moreover, modest funding awards offer academics with the resources needed to investigate novel ideas and solutions with a direct impact on society [21, 22]. These benefits are likely to precede more formal academic outputs, highlighting the broader societal value of small‐grant programs.

We also found that the HEPI small grant enhances collaboration with universities and researchers. These findings align with evidence that research partnerships support sustainable development, improve policy influence, and build long‐term capacity [23]. The current study extends this literature by showing how even small, early‐career‐focused grants can catalyze meaningful collaborative networks. Such partnerships and collaboration can also develop research capacity, improve health outcomes [24], and contribute to societal and economic improvements [25] and leads to larger research teams [26]. These findings are especially pertinent as Ethiopia confronts a variety of escalating health issues, encompassing infectious diseases and climate‐related threats. To effectively resolve difficulties, interdisciplinary collaboration is essential, necessitating tight cooperation among universities. Existing evidence, national strategies, and regional research initiatives all substantiate this necessity.

In this study, we discovered that the project has an impact on creating interdisciplinary and further education, including scholarships. The Interdisciplinary research addresses complex global challenges by merging diverse theories and integrating knowledge across disciplines. It also increases funding for gender diversity [27]. This approach fosters innovation, critical thinking, and collaboration, enhancing research quality and relevance [28]. In developing countries like Ethiopia, where health, social, and economic challenges often intersect, require integrated solutions.

Majority of prior studies have consistently demonstrated that grant funding is associated with increased publication output, Citaion and other scholarly output [4, 6, 21, 29, 30]. However, the present study and few prior studies found no statistically significant differences between grant awardees and and non‐awardees in journal article publications and citations [31, 32, 33, 34]. These shows, the funding intensity does not always equate to a higher citation impact, indicating that the reason may vary depending on things like the grant's scope, institutional support, disciplinary context or small grants may have limited effects on publication. According to this study, it may also be attributed to the early career stage of the participants and the relatively short interval between the grant award and the emergence of measurable research outputs. However, qualitative findings indicated that eight of the funded projects resulted in publications, and participants also reported gains in research capacity, grant‐writing competence, and professional networking. So it may be these factors precede quantitative measures such as publication outputs.

In conclusion, this mixed research examined multiple dimensions of the impact of small grants. Even though quantitative results demonstrated significant differences between grant awardees and non‐awardees only in presented works at conferences; from the qualitative perspective, it was found that grant winning had a positive impact on careers and professional development. Grant awardees were found to have enhanced research capacity, established collaboration with researchers across different universities and disciplines, and enabled to disseminate their research outputs.

Strengths and Weaknesses

Integrating quantitative and qualitative methods explored many aspects of the impact of small grants, providing a comprehensive exploration of the impact of small grants. The findings of this mixed‐method research were triangulated in the discussion section to address the objectives. However, there are limitations to consider: The study achieved a 56% response rate, indicating a relatively low level of engagement from the HEPI small grant applicants, which may affect the generalizability of the findings. Additionally, the study was carried out prior to the full completion of all supporting projects, preventing an assessment of their long‐term impacts. This limits the ability to evaluate the sustained impact of the small grants over time.

Conclusion

Quantitative results demonstrated that the only variable that differed significantly between awardees and non‐awardees was the presented works at conferences after HEPI small grant applications. When comparing grant awardees and non‐awardees, there was no significant difference in the number of publications produced, reviews made, certificates obtained, the research grants they applied for and won, and the research projects they have advised following this small grant implementation. From qualitative perspectives, we found that the grant had a positive impact on their career and professional development, enhancing research capacity, contributing to the community and policy, creating collaboration with researchers and different universities, enhancing interdisciplinary and sustainability of Education and disseminating the grant output.

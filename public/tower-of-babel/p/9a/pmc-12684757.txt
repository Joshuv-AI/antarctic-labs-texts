Increasing breastfeeding literacy: A preliminary study to develop an AI-based chatbot
Authors: Kamila Rosamilia Kantovitz, Maria Eduarda Mattoso, Maria Davoli Meyer, Marcella Armbruster de Araújo, Priscila Alves Giovani, Valentin Martinez, Nileshkumar Dubey, Francisco Humberto Nociti Jr, Rogério Heládio Lopes Motta
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesBreastfeeding plays a critical role in the healthy development of infants, yet exclusive breastfeeding (EBF) rates remain low, particularly among low-income mothers. This study aimed to develop and validate an AI-based educational innovative solution to increase breastfeeding literacy across caregivers and mothers.MethodsThe BabyChat (AI-based) was developed through two phases. In phase I, the content was created using the Canvas application, with the idea tree structured through MindMeister, and delivered via the ManyChat tool on Facebook. The focus was on the benefits of EBF during the initial 6 months of life, as recommended by the WHO, and continued breastfeeding until 1,000 days of life. In Phase II, functionality tests were performed using UserTesting and subsequently validated by the Content Validity Index (CVI). Healthcare professionals reviewed the clarity and relevance of the information on a four-point scale. Intra-examiner concordance was assessed by percentage of agreement and the median for each CVI-I point.ResultsThe contents of BabyChat included 8 topics and 18 subtopics (based on relevant contents including nutritional and anatomical aspects, weaning strategies among others) aimed to educate mothers and caregivers. Five mothers participated in evaluation of the BabyChat. Overall, most participants found the chatbot’s question-and-answer functionality clear and helpful, with accurate command execution and timely response speeds, etc. However, two participants noted occasional issues such as misinterpreted questions, delayed command responses, and unclear or hard-to-find interface buttons. A total of four experts in psychology, dentistry, and medicine validated the framework. The agreement rate between experts ranged from 25 % to 100 %, with median values between 3 and 4, indicating excellent content relevance.ConclusionThe BabyChat was developed and validated for use in increasing breastfeeding literacy among caregivers and mothers. Future studies should be considered to expand the BabyChat validation to other healthcare professionals, including nursing staff, to comprehensively capture the impact of BabyChat on mothers, as well as to incorporate population-specific topics that depend on cultural and geographical aspects.Keywords: Artificial intelligence, Breast feeding, Communication, Education, Knowledge

Introduction

Exclusive breastfeeding (EBF) provides numerous benefits for both infants and mothers, including optimal nutrition, protection against diseases, and improved bonding [1,2]. It also promotes infant oral health, influencing jaw development and preventing early childhood caries [3], while supporting postpartum recovery and reducing chronic conditions for mothers [1]. Despite these benefits, global breastfeeding rates remain suboptimal worldwide, with only 43 % of U.S. and 45.8 % of Brazilian women meeting the six-month EBF recommendation [4,5].

Breastfeeding is central to the UN’s 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development, aiming to reduce neonatal mortality [6]. Maternal decisions on breastfeeding are influenced by factors such as knowledge, sociodemographic elements, and support systems [7,8]. Research shows that greater understanding of EBF leads to longer breastfeeding duration [9,10], while lack of support and misinformation can lead to early cessation [1]. Education is crucial in empowering mothers to follow EBF guidelines and extend breastfeeding [7,11]; therefore, it is an effective way to promote breastfeeding and improve maternal and child health. While traditional counseling remains effective, these efforts can be enhanced through the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) tools, such as chatbots. AI facilitates the dissemination of accurate information, offers personalized and real-time support, and helps counter misinformation [[12], [13], [14], [15], [16]]. Deployed across various platforms, including web, mobile apps, and messaging services, AI-based chatbots provide accessible support for mothers and caregivers [17] and can benefit both infant and maternal health, as well as assist healthcare professionals [18].

Nurses play a critical role in promoting and supporting EBF, yet they face numerous challenges that hinder effective implementation in clinical and community settings. One of the primary barriers is inadequate training in lactation management, which limits nurses’ ability to address complex breastfeeding issues such as poor latch, nipple pain, or delayed milk production [19]. Additionally, heavy workloads and staffing shortages reduce the time available for individualized breastfeeding support, particularly in high-demand maternity units [20]. Institutional factors also contribute to these challenges, including inconsistent breastfeeding policies, limited access to lactation consultants, and a lack of breastfeeding-friendly environments [21]. Cultural and social influences, such as family pressure to use formula or societal norms that stigmatize breastfeeding in public, further complicate nurses’ efforts to advocate for EBF [22]. In this context, the integration of digital tools, such as chatbots, presents a promising strategy to support nurses and other professionals in their roles. Chatbots can enhance breastfeeding literacy among both healthcare providers and mothers by delivering evidence-based information, interactive guidance, and timely responses to common questions. For nurses, this means immediate access to up-to-date protocols and decision-support tools. For mothers, chatbots can reinforce the nurse’s guidance by offering ongoing, personalized education and support outside of clinical settings. By bridging knowledge gaps and extending the reach of professional care, chatbots have the potential to strengthen nurse-led breastfeeding promotion and improve EBF outcomes during the critical early phases of development.

To the author’s knowledge, there is a lack of AI-based technologies to address the deficiency in breastfeeding literacy and provide current, scientifically sound, and real-time information to support caregivers and mothers. In this context, it is clear that developing and validating innovative strategies and technological resources, such as chatbots, becomes crucial to promote breastfeeding across target populations [[15], [16], [17], [18],23,24]. The present preliminary study aimed to develop and validate an AI-based virtual assistant ("BabyChat") that provides mothers and caregivers of infants up to 1,000 days old with real-time, evidence-based information about the benefits of breastfeeding.

Methods

Study design

This study employed a two-phase, cross-sectional design. Phase I involved the development of the AI-based chatbot platform, BabyChat, and Phase II focused on evaluating its usability, user experience, and content validity. The platform responses were tailored to each subtopic, integrating AI and machine learning algorithms to optimize interactions.

Ethical consideration

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the São Leopoldo Mandic University Institutional Review Board (CAEE 67336722.1.0000.5374). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before their participation. Participants were informed of their right to withdraw at any time without consequences.

Phase I: development of the AI-based chatbot

Forming a research team

A multidisciplinary team, including a psychologist, two pediatric dentists, and a pediatrician from three institutions, designed the platform based on evidence-informed topics and subtopics. As a result, the study proceeded with a focus on mothers and caregivers as end-users. Their input will be instrumental in ensuring that the chatbot aligns with clinical practices and enhances the effectiveness of nurse-led breastfeeding education.

Framework development

The BabyChat framework was developed from qualitative interviews and a systematic literature review. It targets mothers and caregivers of infants up to 1,000 days of age and uses simple language and images to enhance clarity.

Interview with mothers and caregivers of infants

In this phase, mothers and primary caregivers of infants were recruited between February and April 2023 during visits to the Obstetrics and Gynaecology Clinic at Dr. Mário Gatti Municipal Hospital, Campinas, SP, Brazil. Recruitment was conducted through convenient sampling, with eligibility criteria as follows: a) aged 23–45 years; b) mother or primary caregiver of an infant; c) Brazilian nationality; d) fluency in Portuguese; e) diverse educational backgrounds; and f) self-reported questions, concerns, or difficulties related to breastfeeding or infant feeding. A total of five mothers participated. Recruitment continued until data saturation was reached, defined as the point when no new themes emerged during successive interviews. All participants provided informed consent before participation. Individual, in-depth interviews were conducted in private consultation rooms to ensure confidentiality and comfort. Interviews lasted approximately 30–45 min, were audio-recorded, and transcribed verbatim. A semi-structured interview guide was used to explore participants’ knowledge, perceived barriers, and support needs regarding breastfeeding and infant feeding. Trained researchers conducted interviews with experienced researchers in qualitative methods. The rich narratives obtained provided meaningful insight into mothers’ experiences, challenges, and support requirements, prioritizing depth over sample size.

Literature review strategy

To complement the interviews, a systematic literature review was conducted to identify key topics and subtopics related to breastfeeding education. The review focused on evidence indicating that specific barriers are frequently associated with low adherence to exclusive breastfeeding [[7], [8], [9],[25], [26], [27], [28], [29], [30], [31], [32], [33]]. Searches were conducted in PubMed using the following keywords and Boolean operators: “exclusive breastfeeding,” “risk factors,” and “lactating women.” Searches were limited to English-language publications from 2018 to 2023, and only peer-reviewed systematic reviews were considered. From an initial yield of 50 manuscripts, 19 were preselected based on title screening, and abstracts were independently evaluated by two reviewers, with disagreements resolved by consensus. After full-text review, repeated topics across different systematic reviews were excluded, resulting in a final selection of five systematic reviews that informed the development of relevant breastfeeding education topics and subtopics [[29], [30], [31], [32], [33]]. The inclusion criteria comprised systematic reviews that investigated determinants or facilitators of exclusive breastfeeding. Studies that did not address mothers’ questions regarding breastfeeding techniques, perceived benefits, or issues such as mastitis or insufficient milk supply were excluded from the analysis. The findings of the included reviews were narratively synthesized, providing evidence-based guidance for structuring interview themes and educational content.

Phase II: application of the framework

The setting and participants

The participants were recruited from February to March 2024 during their follow-up visits to their obstetricians, and the usability tests were conducted between March and May 2024. To enroll a participant, the following criteria were applied for mothers. 1) Aged 18–45 years; 2) mothers of infants up to 1,000 days old; 3) no previous experience with chabots before the study; 4) access to a mobile phone with internet connectivity; 5) completed at least a high school level; 6) primary caregiver responsible for feeding the infant; 7) able to read and understand the language used by the chatbot; 8) willing to engage with the chatbot throughout the study period; 9) willing to provide informed consent and allow anonymized data collection.

A multidisciplinary team of four healthcare professionals, comprising a psychologist, two pediatric dentists, and a pediatrician from three different institutions, was involved in the study from its inception to its conclusion. They were directly involved in daily activities related to early childcare and were also familiar with the challenges associated with breastfeeding education.

Data collection

Usability testing. System usability and user experience were assessed by the enrolled participants, who completed four questions and provided verbal feedback using the think-aloud method (Appendix A). Testing was performed with focus on the following aspects: 1) question and answer functionality, 2) command execution, 3) response speed, and 4) interface usability. During this stage, any issues raised by users, including ambiguities and unclear navigation, were addressed to ensure BabyChat's effectiveness before content validation. The participants assessed the “BabyChat” separately in a private room during their appointment to avoid bias, and they had the option to participate or withdraw from the study.

Expert content validation. Content Validity Index (CVI) has been widely used to validate similar technologies and identify usability challenges [[34], [35], [36], [37]]. In the current investigation, CVI was employed, involving pediatricians, psychologists, and pediatric dentists. Four experts evaluated the content via Google Forms, rating items on a 4-point Likert scale (1 = not relevant, 2 = low relevance, 3 = relevant, 4 = very relevant). Experts assessed questions on interest, clarity, usefulness, applicability to mothers’ satisfaction, language appropriateness, and promotion of exclusive breastfeeding. The CVI was calculated by dividing the number of experts who rated an item as relevant (i.e., a rating of 3 or 4) by the total number of experts. Irrelevant items were rated 1 and 2 in the CVI, representing the proportion of agreement on the relevance of each item. The enrolled healthcare professionals worked independently and were requested to send their evaluations within one week. In addition, they were encouraged to provide any additional comments on the proposed approach, if any.

Data analysis

Quantitative data from CVI assessments were tabulated in Excel. Intra-examiner concordance was assessed using the relative frequency (%) of expert responses and the median for each CVI item. Qualitative feedback was compiled and narratively described in the manuscript.

Results

The results section comprises a description of the AI-based chatbot’s contents and an evaluation of its usability.

Description of the AI-based chatbot

Table 1, Fig. 1, Appendix B and Appendix C illustrate the specific contents and the idea tree of the developed BabyChat. Note that eight topics were included in the BabyChat, with 18 subtopics based on relevant content related to each topic. Key topics were identified based on prior research, including breast milk, breastfeeding, proper latch, nipple care, pacifiers and bottles, frenectomy, weaning and complementary feeding, and insufficient milk [[3], [5], [7], [8], [9], [25], [26], [27], [28], [29], [30], [31], [32], [33]]. Topics were divided into 1–3 subtopics to address common questions related to breastfeeding (Table 1). A mind map on https://www.mindmeister.com was used to organize the content around the central prompt, “Choose a topic to ask your question.” The dialogues were integrated into the ManyChat platform, and AI-optimized responses were structured to manage initiated conversations. Upon entering the Plataforma, users were greeted with three initial prompts: “Hello, how are you? ”, “Great! Shall we start? ”, and “Does your child have up to 3 years old? ”.

The step of evaluating the platform

Appendix A presents participant demographics and their evaluations of BabyChat. Five mothers, aged 23–45 years, all with children aged 3–12 months and no prior experience with chatbots, participated in the study. Comments were noted and included both positive and negative observations, highlighting strengths and areas for improvement. Overall, most participants found the chatbot’s question-and-answer functionality clear and helpful, with accurate command execution and timely response speeds. The interface was generally described as simple, intuitive, and easy to navigate. However, two participants noted occasional issues such as misinterpreted questions, delayed command responses, and unclear or hard-to-find interface buttons, indicating areas for further refinement to enhance usability.

In addition, the expert panel was composed entirely of female professionals aged between 31 and 58 years, representing diverse backgrounds in pediatric dentistry, psychology, and pediatrics, with clinical experience ranging from 9 to 23 years. Overall, the experts provided positive feedback on the chatbot’s content, particularly in terms of promoting exclusive breastfeeding, where all experts rated the content with the highest score (4 out of 4). Other aspects, including interest, clarity, usefulness, applicability to mothers and caregivers, satisfaction, and language appropriateness, received predominantly high scores, with three of the four experts consistently rating these aspects as 4. One expert (Expert 2) rated all categories slightly lower, assigning a score of 3 across multiple criteria, suggesting room for improvement in clarity and engagement. Despite this, the overall evaluations indicate that the chatbot’s content is considered transparent, practical, appropriate in tone, and potentially effective for encouraging exclusive breastfeeding among its target users. The CVI-I assessment of BabyChat revealed varied levels of agreement among evaluators, with concordance ranging from 25 % to 100 %. The highest agreement scores were 100 % and 88 %, indicating strong consensus on certain items, while lower agreement values of 25 % and 38 % suggest inconsistencies in evaluating other aspects. Overall, these findings highlight areas of both strong alignment and potential divergence in content validity perceptions that may warrant further review.

Discussion

The rapid development of AI-powered chatbots, which utilize natural language processing (NLP), has significantly enhanced their role in healthcare [13,38]. This preliminary study developed and validated a web-based chatbot prototype (BabyChat) to provide real-time information on breastfeeding for mothers and caregivers during the first 1,000 days. The growth of health-related chatbots, particularly following the COVID-19 pandemic, underscores their value in delivering remote healthcare support [38]. The current investigation sheds light on this field by evaluating a functional, accessible prototype with relevant content [39,40].

The choice of Facebook was based on its free, accessible tools and its wide user base of approximately 2.9 billion people, making it an effective platform for virtual interactions. Several studies highlight its role in connecting users with health experts and fostering supportive communities [15,41,42]. However, the widespread dissemination of information on social media may also lead to distortion and confusion [12,14,43]. Therefore, health-related chatbots must be developed based on solid scientific evidence and under the supervision of qualified professionals to ensure accuracy and reliability [14]. In the current study, internal and external tests confirmed that BabyChat was developed to adhere to the principles of accessibility, security, consistency, and accuracy, reflecting best practices from previous healthcare prototypes [12,44]. Data analysis demonstrated that BabyChat featured satisfactory content validation, with a CVI-I score of 1, aligning with other studies [[34], [35], [36], [37]]. This indicates that most topics were relevant, with a median score of 4.

While it was not the aim of the current work at this stage to collect the mother's feedback, preliminary informal comments confirmed previous studies [23,[44], [45], [46]] demonstrating the importance of considering a high level of integration throughout the conversational process, revealing the need to develop healthcare chatbots with the ability to produce a more humanistic interaction, leading to comfortable experiences. Previous work further highlighted that impersonal conversations developed through AI-based tools had the potential to lead to limited responses [[47], [48], [49]], indicating that these AI-based tools should be used as a supplement, not a replacement, for personalized and empathetic interactions between the healthcare team and mothers. In the present study, the need to type a keyword to initiate the conversation made the user experience less seamless and potentially impacted user engagement. This observation highlights a challenge for platform providers like Facebook and TikTok in integrating AI, balancing the capabilities of algorithms with minimizing limitations and enhancing the user experience [15,43].

The promotion and support of EBF remain a critical component of maternal and child health, yet nurses—the primary healthcare professionals involved—face multifaceted challenges that impede optimal breastfeeding support. These challenges include limited lactation training, heavy workloads, institutional constraints, and sociocultural barriers that reduce nurses’ capacity to provide consistent, evidence-based breastfeeding education [19,21]. Our study developed and evaluated BabyChat, an AI-powered chatbot designed to enhance EBF literacy directly among mothers and caregivers, addressing gaps in breastfeeding support where nurse involvement may be limited or delayed. Although nurses were not included in this initial phase due to recruitment constraints, the chatbot demonstrated positive usability and content validity, with healthcare professionals rating its language, clarity, and promotion of EBF highly. User feedback highlighted BabyChat’s potential as an accessible, timely resource that provides clear and helpful information, encouraging exclusive breastfeeding and complementing nurse-led education. However, variability in agreement among evaluators and some user-reported interface delays underscore the need for iterative improvements. Future research will focus on integrating nurses into the chatbot’s development to align content with clinical practice and leverage nurses' expertise, thereby enhancing breastfeeding support within healthcare systems. This approach recognizes the vital role nurses play while exploring innovative digital tools to expand the reach and effectiveness of EBF promotion during the crucial first 1,000 days of life.

Conclusion and future work

A BabyChat was created and validated with the potential to educate mothers and caregivers about EBF. Future studies should be considered to expand the BabyChat validation to other healthcare professionals, including nursing staff, to comprehensively capture the impact of BabyChat on mothers, as well as to incorporate population-specific topics that depend on cultural and geographical aspects.

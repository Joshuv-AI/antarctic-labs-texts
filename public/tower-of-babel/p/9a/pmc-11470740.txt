Comparative analysis of the physiological and transcriptomic profiles reveals alfalfa drought resistance mechanisms
Authors: Fenqi Chen, Xue Ha, Ting Ma, Huiling Ma
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDrought stress is a major limiting factor that affects forage yields, and understanding the drought resistance mechanism of plants is crucial for improving crop yields in arid areas. Alfalfa (Medicago sativa L.) is the most important legume plant, mainly planted in arid and semi-arid areas. However, the adaptability of alfalfa to drought stress and its physiological and molecular mechanisms of drought resistance remains unclear.ResultsIn this study, we analyzed the physiological and transcriptome responses of alfalfa cultivars with different drought resistances (drought-sensitive Gannong No. 3 (G3), drought-resistant Gannong No. 8 (G8), and strong drought-resistant Longdong (LD)) under drought stress at 0, 6, 12, and 24 h. LD had higher catalase (CAT), peroxidase (POD), and superoxide dismutase (SOD) activities and a higher soluble protein content, lower malondialdehyde (MDA) content, a lower O2·− production rate, and a lower H2O2 content than G8 and G3 (P < 0.05). The functional enrichment analysis, temporal expression pattern analysis, and weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) of the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) showed phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, flavonoid biosynthesis, starch and sucrose metabolism, glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, glutathione metabolism, and biosynthesis of amino acid responses to drought stress in alfalfa. The differential expression of genes during phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, starch and sucrose metabolism, and the glutathione metabolism pathway was further studied, and it was speculated that PAL, COMT, 4CL, CCR, CAD, HXK, INV, SUS, WAXY, AGP, GST, and APX1 played important roles in the alfalfa drought stress response.ConclusionsThe aim of this study was to enhance alfalfa drought resistance by overexpressing positively regulated genes and knocking out negatively regulated genes, providing genetic resources for the subsequent molecular-assisted breeding of drought-resistant alfalfa crops.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-024-05671-8.Keywords: Alfalfa, Physiology, Transcriptomics, Drought stress, PEG simulation

Background

Drought is a primary detrimental environmental factor that affects plant growth and development. Water shortage is also considered the primary abiotic stress to crop yields and declines in crop quality. According to statistics, approximately one-third of the world’s regions are arid and semi-arid regions, accounting for approximately 36% of the world’s total land area [1]. Natural precipitation is the primary water source for crop growth in arid areas. Even if there are irrigation conditions, crops will suffer drought damage due to insufficient water or untimely irrigation [2]. During the process of long-term drought stress adaptation, plants have gradually evolved mechanisms and strategies suitable for their own growth and development to respond to drought. In general, the drought resistance mechanisms of plants primarily function to resist or reduce drought stress damage through escape, avoidance, and tolerance [3]. At the physiological level, drought resistance typically improves water use efficiency by the establishment of deep roots, increases in water absorption, and reductions in water diversion loss under transpiration. At the biochemical level, drought resistance is achieved by minimizing drought-induced oxidative damage [4]. Therefore, the related osmotic adjustment substances, secondary metabolites, and photosynthetic rate can be used as indicators to predict the damage caused by drought stress [5]. When plants are exposed to drought, this will induce an excessive accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) that will lead to oxidative stress [6]. Drought toxicity resistance is achieved by increasing the activity of ROS scavenging enzymes, such as superoxide dismutase (SOD), ascorbate peroxidase (APX), catalase (CAT), glutathione, and peroxidase (POD), to minimize damage. In addition, many genes or biological processes involved in drought stress have been identified. For example, PuC3H35 of Populus ussuriensis was found to improve drought tolerance by improving the biosynthesis of lignin and procyanidins [7]. A genome-wide association study of alfalfa (Medicago sativa L.) identified a MYB-like transcription factor gene, MsMYBH, that can directly bind to the promoters of MsMCP1, MsMCP2, MsPRX1A, and MsCARCAB to improve its expression. The results of this interaction include a better water balance, high photosynthetic efficiency, and the removal of excess hydrogen peroxide to cope with drought [4]. Therefore, it is of great significance to study the physiological and molecular mechanisms of crop drought resistance and improve and cultivate strong drought-resistant crop cultivars using molecular technology to make full use of large areas of arid and semi-arid areas and reduce agricultural economic losses.

Alfalfa is the most widely planted perennial legume forage in the world. It has a high biomass and crude protein content, is rich in digestible nutrients and mineral elements, and enjoys the reputation of the “king of forage” [8, 9]. In recent years, it is expected that climate change will cause frequent drought pressures on crop and feed production, seriously restricting its productivity [10]. Therefore, it is urgent to cultivate new drought-resistant alfalfa varieties. The design of breeding programs will achieve this goal by mining the physiological and molecular mechanisms that regulate the alfalfa drought response. It is feasible and effective to use RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) to study the dynamic changes and regulatory mechanisms of drought resistance in plants at the transcriptional level [11]. For example, in sweet orange (Citrus sinensis), drought stress differentially regulates genes encoding proteins related to carbohydrate and antioxidant metabolism and starch sucrose metabolism pathways, such as SOD, APX, sucrose synthase (SUS), and hexokinase (HXK) [12]. Zhang et al. [13] found that genes such as myo-inositol and proline were involved in the drought resistance of Medicago truncatula. In addition, the HXK gene was identified as a negative regulator of drought stress in soybeans (Glycine max) [14]. These results indicated that many molecular mechanisms and related signal networks have been found in many plants by studying the response to various stresses using RNA-seq. However, the potential pathways and mechanisms of the alfalfa drought response stress remain elusive.

Therefore, in this study, we use RNA-seq to analyze the physiological and transcriptional expression of alfalfa seedling roots at different time points after PEG-6000 simulated drought treatment. We also use a functional enrichment analysis, expression trend analysis, and weighted correlation network analysis (WGCNA) to analyze the related genes of three alfalfa cultivars under drought stress at 0, 6, 12, and 24 h. Three major metabolic pathways in response to drought stress were identified, including phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, starch and sucrose metabolism, and glutathione metabolism, and these provide a theoretical basis for exploring the molecular regulatory network and mechanism of alfalfa drought tolerance.

Results and analysis

Physiological differences of alfalfa with different drought resistances under drought stress

To compare the drought resistance of different alfalfa cultivars during the seedling stage, we used a 15% polyethylene glycol (PEG)-6000 solution to simulate drought stress in our preliminary study. We analyzed the plant height, root length, seedling fresh weight, seedling dry weight, root fresh weight, root dry weight, relative water content of leaves, chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b, carotenoid, total chlorophyll, the chlorophyll a/b ratio, the relative conductivity of leaves, and the relative conductivity of roots of 23 alfalfa samples and selected three alfalfa cultivars with different drought resistances ((strong drought-resistant Longdong (LD), drought-resistant Gannong No. 8 (G8), and drought-sensitive Gannong No. 3 (G3)) (Table S1). And the morphological changes were showed of LD, G8 and G3 after 7 days of drought stress, which clearly showed little difference between drought treatment and control of LD, while drought-sensitive G3 significantly inhibited growth after 7 days of stress and most of the leaves at the base were wilted (Fig. S1). It was found that the antioxidant enzyme activities of three alfalfa roots with different drought resistances had significant differences in the extension of stress time (Fig. 1). Compared with CK (0 h), under the PEG-6000 simulated drought stress, catalase (CAT), peroxidase (POD), superoxide dismutase (SOD), and the soluble protein content of the three alfalfa cultivars with different drought resistances increased significantly with the duration of stress (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1A–C, E). When compared with the same material and stress time point, the changing trends of these four indicators were basically consistent, showing the highest value in the strong drought resistant material. Correspondingly, most of the time point, the drought sensitive material G3 had the lowest value, while G8 was in the middle trend. Compared with the drought-sensitive G3, these enzymes increased more significantly in the strong drought-resistant LD, followed by the drought-resistant G8 (P < 0.05). The change of ascorbate peroxidase (APX) activity were complex and correlated with both the material and treatment duration (Fig. 1D). The soluble sugar content decreased significantly at 6–12 h under drought stress in G3 and increased significantly after 24 h of stress, while it increased significantly at 12 and 24 h under drought stress in G8 and increased significantly at 6, 12, and 24 h under drought stress in LD (P < 0.05). When comparing different materials, the soluble sugars in G8 were slightly lower than those in LD and G3, while there is no significant difference between the latter two (P > 0.05) (Fig. 1F). In addition, the malondialdehyde (MDA) content and H2O2 content increased significantly with the duration of drought stress, but the increase was the largest in cultivar G3, followed by cultivars G8 and LD (P < 0.05). When comparing different materials, these two indexes were significantly lower in LD than those in G3 and G8 (P < 0.05) (Fig. 1F and G). The production rate of O2.– increased significantly only after 12 h of drought stress in cultivars G8 and LD but increased significantly at 12 and 24 h under drought stress in the cultivar G3 (P < 0.05). There was no difference between the three materials at 6 h, while the production rate of O2.– significantly decreased with increasing drought resistance at 12 h, and the production rate of O2.– in LD was significantly lower than that in G3 and G8 at 24 h (Fig. 1H).

RNA-sequencing analysis

A total of 36 alfalfa root samples were subjected to transcriptome sequencing, with an average output of 609,561 billion base pairs (bp) of raw data (Table S2). After removing low-quality reads, the average clean data of 602,264 million bp were obtained. Low-quality read ranged from 0.15 to 0.34%. The percentages of Q20 and Q30 were greater than 97.62% and 93.07%, respectively, and the percentage of the GC content ranged from 41.61 to 42.02% (Table S3). Compared with the reference genome of autotetraploid alfalfa, 33,102,685–41,306,473 alignment sequences were obtained, and the alignment rate was 90.58–92.36%. These data showed that the data obtained from the transcriptome were sufficient to perform the next analysis.

Comparative analysis of the differential expression genes (DEGs) in the different comparison groups

Based on the gene expression information, a principal component analysis (PCA) of all samples showed that the PEG-6000 simulated drought stress induced changes in alfalfa genes (Fig. 2A). Based on a |Fold Change| ≥ 2 and FDR < 0.05 as the screening criteria for DEGs, at 6 h under drought stress, 22,386 DEGs (6705 up-regulated and 15681 down-regulated) were identified in G3, 18,083 DEGs (7138 up-regulated and 10,945 down-regulated) were identified in G8, and 24,587 DEGs (6996 up-regulated and 17,591 down-regulated) were identified in LD. At 12 h under drought stress, 24,469 DEGs (6722 up-regulated and 17,747 down-regulated) were identified in G3, 28,824 DEGs (7531 up-regulated and 21,293 down-regulated) were identified in G8, and 22,497 DEGs (7097 up-regulated and 15,400 down-regulated) were identified in LD. At 24 h under drought stress, 23,279 DEGs (6312 up-regulated and 16,967 down-regulated) were identified in G3, 20,263 DEGs (6715 up-regulated and 13,548 down-regulated) were identified in G8, and 21,541 DEGs (6929 up-regulated and 14,612 down-regulated) were identified in LD (Fig. 2B). To analyze the common and unique DEGs among the comparison groups of the three cultivars under drought stress, a Venn diagram analysis was constructed. We identified 1743 up-regulated and 4060 down-regulated DEGs in nine comparison groups (Fig. 2C and D). These DEGs were likely to be important candidate genes in regulating root growth and metabolism in alfalfa in response to PEG-6000 simulated drought.

Functional enrichment analysis of the DEGs

To analyze the functions of these DEGs under drought stress, GO terms were used for the functional classification of the DEGs, and this included biological processes, cellular components, and molecular functions. We found that the DEGs in all comparison groups were significantly enriched in GO terms such as single organization metabolic process, response to stimulus, regulation of meristem development, response to stress, single organization process, microtubule-based process, response to chemical, oxidoreductase activity, glucosidase activity, catalytic activity, tetrapyrrole binding, external encapsulating structure, cell periphery, and the cell wall and extracellular region (Fig. 2E).We then performed a KEGG analysis to further explore the metabolic pathways involved in the DEGs. The results showed that the DEGs were significantly enriched in metabolic pathways, biosynthesis of secondary metabolites, phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, glutathione metabolism, starch and sucrose metabolism, nitrogen metabolism, glycolysis gluconeogenesis, cyanoamino acid metabolism, glycine, serine, and threonine metabolism, tyrosine metabolism, folate biosynthesis, phagosome, galactose metabolism, monoterpenoid biosynthesis, and brassinosteroid biosynthesis (Fig. 2F). The above analysis indicated that these significantly enriched GO terms and pathways may be closely associated with the alfalfa root response to drought stress, and this requires further analysis.

DEG expression pattern, clustering, and functional enrichment analysis

To further investigate the gene expression profiles, we utilized STEM software to cluster the DEGs from the three cultivars with different drought resistances into seven distinct profiles across various stress durations. Among them, the expressions of the DEGs in profile 0 of cultivars G3, G8, and LD gradually decreased with the duration of stress, while the expressions of the DEGs in profile 2 gradually decreased first and then remained unchanged with the duration of stress (Fig. 3A–C). However, the expressions of the DEGs in profile 17 of cultivars G3, G8, and LD increased gradually and then remained unchanged with the duration of stress (Fig. 3A–C). In addition, profile 5 and profile 8 were significant profiles unique to LD and G8, respectively. In profile 8 of G8, 3329 DEGs were down-regulated from 6 to 12 h and up-regulated from 12 to 24 h (Fig. 3D and Table S4). These genes were primarily involved in secondary metabolic pathways, peroxisome, phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, flavonoid biosynthesis, phenylalanine, tyrosine and tryptophan biosynthesis, and brassinosterol biosynthesis (Fig. 3E). In profile 5 of LD, 3121 DEGs were down-regulated from 0 to 6 h and up-regulated from 6 to 12 h (Fig. 3F and Table S5). These genes were significantly enriched in carotenoid biosynthesis, phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, starch and sucrose metabolism, and ABC transporters (Fig. 3G). In profile 17 of LD, 6689 DEGs were first up-regulated (6 h) and then remained unchanged (6–24 h) with the extension of stress time (Fig. 3H and Table S6). These genes were primarily involved in glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, glutathione metabolism, isoflavone biosynthesis, alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism, valine, leucine and isoleucine degradation, tyrosine metabolism, fatty acid degradation, and carbon metabolism (Fig. 3I).

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)

To identify the co-expression patterns of genes in alfalfa under drought stress, we used the WGCNA, which has the function of clustering gene sets with similar expression patterns into modules. A total of 22 co-expression modules were constructed from the expression data of 36 alfalfa root samples (Fig. 4A). The expression patterns of the module genes in each sample are displayed in the expression pattern heatmap (Fig. 4B) that showed that different significant modules had significant differences in the expression levels at different stress time points in the different cultivars. A further analysis of the distribution of genes in the module showed that the number of genes in the red module was the largest, followed by the black module, the light yellow module, and the blue module (Fig. 4C). An analysis of the heatmap analysis of Pearson correlation coefficients between module eigenvalues (MM) and trait data showed that the modules with the most significant positive correlations with CAT, POD, SOD, APX, SP, SS, MDA, H2O2, and O2.– were dark red and light yellow, and the most significant negative correlations were green and red. Therefore, we further analyzed the four modules that were significantly correlated with the physiological traits (Fig. 4D).

Functional analysis of the genes in related modules

To further analyze the metabolic pathways of genes in these modules in response to drought stress, we performed a KEGG enrichment analysis on genes in the dark red, light yellow, green, and red modules (Fig. 5). The genes of the dark red module were primarily enriched in the biosynthesis of amino acids, cysteine and methionine metabolism, glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, carbon metabolism, glycine, serine and threonine metabolism, and the biosynthesis of secondary metabolites (Fig. 5A). The genes of the lightyellow module were primarily enriched in metabolic pathways, secondary metabolic biosynthesis, phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, the biosynthesis of amino acids, the citrate cycle (TCA cycle), carbon metabolism, and glycolysis/gluconeogenesis (Fig. 5B). The genes of the green module were primarily enriched in the biosynthesis of secondary metabolites, phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, the biosynthesis of amino acids, the pentose phosphate pathway, arginine and proline metabolism, and glycolysis/gluconeogenesis (Fig. 5C). The genes of the red module were primarily enriched in ribosomes, carbon metabolism, the biosynthesis of amino acids, nitrogen metabolism, oxidative phosphorylation, pyruvate metabolism, the citrate cycle (TCA cycle), and glycolysis/gluconeogenesis (Fig. 5D).

Pathways involved in alfalfa drought stress

The functional enrichment analysis of the above genes, the functional analysis of the significantly enriched gene sets unique to LD and G8, and the functional analysis of the WGCNA identification module genes showed that the genes induced by alfalfa drought stress were primarily involved in the phenylpropanoid biosynthesis pathway, starch sucrose metabolism, and glutathione metabolism. Therefore, the following investigations focused on these three functions.

Expression of genes related to phenylpropanoid biosynthesis

According to an analysis of the function and gene expression trend of the DEGs, the number of DEGs enriched in the phenylpropanoid biosynthesis pathway was the largest, and the enrichment result was the most significant. The expression of the DEGs was clearly displayed by drawing a clustering heatmap of the different genes (Fig. 6). Phenylalanine is converted to cinnamic acid under the deamination of phenylalanine ammonia lyase (PAL). Compared with cultivars G3 and G8, the expressions of 14 PAL genes were down-regulated at 6–24 h of LD drought stress. Some genes related to the conversion of cinnamic acid to cinnamaldehyde related to catalytic enzymes 4-coumarate-coa ligase (4CL), cinnamoyl CoA reductase (CCR), and cinnamyl dehydrogenase (CAD) were down-regulated, and some genes were up-regulated during 6–24 h under drought stress in the three alfalfa cultivars. This result indicated that these genes played a coordinated role in the regulation of coumaric acid to coumarin, thereby improving the resistance of alfalfa to drought stress. In addition, caffeic acid 3-O-methyltransferase/acetylserotonin O-methyltransferase (COMT) was the key enzyme that catalyzed caffeyl alcohol to coniferyl alcohol. Except that the expressions of five COMT genes were down-regulated in all three alfalfa cultivars during 6–24 h under drought stress, the expressions of the other two COMT genes were up-regulated at different time points under drought stress. p-Coumaryl alcohol is an important flavonoid compound and one of the important monomers for lignin synthesis. It was catalyzed by POD to produce lignin. However, two POD synthesis genes (Ms.gene049483 and Ms.gene24582) were up-regulated at different time points under drought stress in the three alfalfa cultivars, and the other 65 POD-related synthesis genes were down-regulated at 6–24 h under drought stress. In conclusion, lignin biosynthesis may be an important way that alfalfa roots respond to PEG-6000 simulated drought stress.

Heatmap analysis of the DEGs related to phenylpropanoid biosynthesis in alfalfa under drought stress

Starch and sucrose metabolism drought stress response

Starch and sucrose metabolism is an important basic metabolic pathway for plant survival. According to the DEG enrichment results, it was found that the starch and sucrose metabolism pathway was the most significantly enriched pathway in response to alfalfa drought stress. With the intensification of PEG-simulated drought stress, most HXK genes gradually down-regulated their expressions as LD drought stress continued (Fig. 7). Beta-fructofuranosidase (INV) is a key enzyme for sucrose-6P to produce D-fructose. Except for the up-regulated expressions of four INVs (Ms.gene057014, Ms.gene051946, Ms.gene85220, and Ms.gene76146), the expressions of the remaining 13 INV genes were down-regulated in the three alfalfa cultivars during 6–24 h under drought stress. Most of the SUS genes (68.97%) of LD were down-regulated at 6–24 h under drought stress. In addition, the expressions of granule-bound starch synthase (WAXY) and glycogen phosphorylase (GP) were down-regulated at 6–24 h under drought stress in the three alfalfa cultivars. However, glucose-1-phosphate adenylyltransferase (AGP, Ms.gene014268) was up-regulated at 6–24 h under drought stress in LD, and the other four AGP genes were down-regulated at 6–24 h under drought stress in the three alfalfa cultivars. Based on the above analysis, it was concluded that HXK, INV, SUS, WAXY, and AGP may be the key genes in alfalfa that respond to PEG-6000 simulated drought stress.

Glutathione metabolism-related gene analysis

Glutathione (GSH) is a key antioxidant that is ubiquitous in plants. Glutathione metabolism plays an important role in regulating the redox homeostasis of plant cells. Glutathione S-transferase (GST) is one of the key enzymes involved in this metabolic pathway. At 6, 12, and 24 h under drought stress in alfalfa, glutathione reductase (GR), 6-phosphogluconate dehydrogenase (PGD), GST, leucyl aminopeptidase (LAP), and ascorbate peroxidase (APX) were significantly enriched in the glutathione metabolism pathway (Fig. 8). Among them, the genes encoding GST accounted for the most (n = 37) at approximately 71.15%, and primarily GST3, GSTF9, GSTL3, GSTU18, and GSTZ2. The expressions of GST3, GSTF2, GSTL3, two GSTU9, GSTZ1, and GSTZ2 genes increased gradually with duration under drought stress in the three alfalfa cultivars. GSTF13, four GSTF9, GSTU17, GSTU18, and GSTU3 were down-regulated during 0–24 h under drought stress in the three alfalfa cultivars. GSTT2, GSTF9 (Ms.gene019181), and PGD1 were up-regulated under drought stress in the two drought-resistant alfalfa cultivars. One, GSTU9 (Ms.gene032888), was significantly down-regulated under drought stress in LD, a strong drought-resistant cultivar. In addition, LAPA2, APXT, and APX3 were down-regulated during 0–24 h under drought stress in the three alfalfa cultivars. Interestingly, the expression of APX1 was significantly up-regulated in the drought-resistant cultivar G8 at 24 h under drought stress, and in the strong drought-resistant cultivar LD at 12–24 h under drought stress. These results suggested that these genes may play important roles in the synergistic scavenging of ROS produced by drought stress with glutathione.

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) validation

Fifteen genes were randomly selected for qRT-PCR to verify the authenticity and reliability of the data (Fig. 9). The results showed that the two trends were basically consistent. In our results, compared with CK, 4CL was up-regulated after 6 h under drought stress in G3 and LD, CCOMT was up-regulated after 6 h under drought stress in G8 and LD, and PAL1 was up-regulated after 6 h under drought stress in G3, which was different from the expressions of the RNA-seq. However, the trend of changes at other time points was consistent with the results of the RNA-seq. The expressions of the remaining 12 DEGs were consistent with the results of the RNA-seq. Therefore, the transcriptional map data obtained from the RNA-seq analysis were true and reliable.

Discussion

Oxidoreductase physiology involved in alfalfa drought stress regulation

ROS production and the scavenging system are key processes of all organisms and play key roles in the abiotic process of plants. An excessive accumulation of ROS will lead to cell membrane and protein damage, thus causing oxidative damage to plants [15]. Under normal circumstances, there is an effective antioxidant system in plant cells that can balance the production and elimination of ROS. However, when plants are subjected to drought and other adversity stresses, this will lead to an excessive accumulation of ROS, resulting in membrane lipid peroxidation and serious damage to plant cells [16]. The antioxidant defense system composed of antioxidant substances and antioxidant enzymes is an important mechanism for plants to eliminate excessive ROS and protect plants from oxidative stress [17]. Under stress conditions, the entire defense system activates the antioxidant defense mechanism, resists ROS damage, and ensures normal cell function [18]. Plants have evolved strategies to increase the activities of SOD, POD, CAT, and APX as defense mechanisms against oxidative stress. Under drought stress, the expression levels of SOD, POD, CAT, and APX genes have been found to be positively correlated with enzyme activities [19]. SOD, POD, and CAT are important antioxidant enzymes in plants. SOD is the key enzyme for scavenging ROS, and it can catalyze O2.– or HO2.– disproportionation to O2.– and H2O2. POD and CAT are primarily responsible for scavenging H2O2 in cells, inhibiting the peroxidation of the plasma membrane, and protecting cells from damage [20]. In this study, we found that the CAT activity, POD activity, SOD activity, and SP content of the three alfalfa cultivars with different drought resistances significantly increased with the drought stress duration (P < 0.05). Compared with the drought-sensitive cultivar G3, LD increased more significantly, followed by G8. MDA is the final decomposition product of membrane lipid peroxidation. An increase in the MDA content indicates that drought stress may induce membrane lipid peroxidation due to an increase in ROS that directly reflects the damage degree of the membrane system [2, 21]. The MDA and H2O2 contents significantly increased with the drought stress duration, but G3 increased the most, followed by G8 and LD. The O2.– production rate significantly increased at 12 h under drought stress in the three cultivars, but G3 increased more significantly, followed by G8 and LD. The above results showed that under drought stress, compared with the alfalfa cultivars G3 and G8, LD, a strong drought-resistant cultivar, had stronger enzymatic and non-enzymatic defense systems that effectively removed ROS to ensure a lower degree of membrane lipid peroxidation. Previous studies on other plants have also found that strong drought-resistant plant cultivars have strong enzymatic and non-enzymatic antioxidant systems to effectively control ROS levels [2, 22, 23].

Transcriptome analysis of the alfalfa response to drought stress

When plants feel stress, they first respond at the gene level by mobilizing a large number of genes to weave into a complex regulatory network to produce corresponding proteins to control the synthesis of metabolites and regulate the mechanism balance of plants [11, 24]. In this study, the transcription levels of alfalfa roots were analyzed after PEG-simulated drought stress treatment for 0, 6, 12, and 24 h. A total of 22,386 DEGs were identified in G3, 18,083 DEGs in G8, and 24,587 DEGs in LD at 6 h under drought stress. At 12 h under drought stress, 24,469 DEGs were identified in G3, 28,824 DEGs in G8, and 22,497 DEGs in LD. At 24 h under drought stress, 23,279 DEGs were identified in G3, 20,263 in G8, and 21,541 in LD (Fig. 2B). The results of the GO enrichment showed that these DEGs were primarily related to the stress response, oxidoreductase activity, response to stimulation, and other GO terms. The KEGG functional enrichment analysis showed that the DEGs were primarily enriched in metabolic pathways, the biosynthesis of secondary metabolites, phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, glutathione metabolism, starch and sucrose metabolism, nitrogen metabolism, glycolysis gluconeogenesis, cyanoamino acid metabolism, glycine, serine and threonine metabolism, tyrosine metabolism, folate biosynthesis, phagosome, galactose metabolism, monoterpenoid biosynthesis, and brassinosteroid biosynthesis. To better reveal the DEGs under drought stress, the specific gene profiles were screened using an expression trend analysis, and the modules related to oxidoreductase were identified by the WGCNA. The results showed that phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, starch and sucrose metabolism, the biosynthesis of amino acids and metabolism, and glycolysis/gluconeogenesis and glutathione metabolism were positively responsive to alfalfa drought stress.

Phenylpropanoid biosynthesis was involved in the alfalfa drought stress response

The phenylpropanoid biosynthesis pathway is one of the most important plant metabolic pathways. It produces more than 8000 metabolites and contributes to plant development and plant environmental interactions [25]. It also contributes to plant responses to abiotic and biotic stresses [26]. Lignin is the primary component of the xylem and plays a vital role in supporting plant growth, development, and resistance to biotic and abiotic stresses [27]. PAL and CAD in the phenylpropanoid biosynthesis pathway are the first step of the phenylpropanoid pathway and the last step of lignin synthesis, respectively. Therefore, PAL and CAD are generally considered the key enzymes that regulate plant lignin synthesis. Interestingly, it is well known that lignin is an important part of the plant cell wall. Under a water shortage condition, lignin increases cell wall thickening, reduces the permeability of cells to water, and improves drought resistance [28]. According to our data, the number of DEGs in the phenylpropanoid biosynthesis pathway enriched in the roots of the three alfalfa cultivars was the largest, and they were primarily involved in the biosynthesis and metabolism of lignin. By contrast, the genes related to the key enzymes of lignin synthesis, such as PAL, C4H, CCR, and CAD, were differentially expressed under drought stress, indicating that the manner in which the phenylpropanoid biosynthesis pathway participates in alfalfa drought stress is primarily by regulating the expression of key enzyme genes of lignin synthesis, changing the lignin accumulation in roots, and then resisting drought stress. Previous studies have shown that overexpression of the GH4CL7 gene of Gossypium hirsutum in Arabidopsis thaliana can increase the activity of antioxidant enzymes, reduce the accumulation of MDA and H2O2, and increase the expression of stress-related genes in Arabidopsis thaliana under drought stress, and this enhances its tolerance to drought stress [29]. In addition, compared with the control, the expression levels of PAL, COMT, CCR, and CAD genes related to lignin synthesis in the GH4CL7 silenced plants were lower. This provides evidence for our results that indicated that the PAL, COMT, 4CL, CCR, and CAD genes related to the lignin biosynthesis pathway are involved in the response of alfalfa roots to drought stress and can be used as drought candidate genes in alfalfa. Their specific functions will be further verified in subsequent studies.

Starch and sucrose metabolism were involved in the alfalfa drought stress response

Starch and sucrose metabolism is an important process of carbon metabolism and plays a key role in plant development and the stress response through the production of a series of soluble sugars as regulatory metabolites and signals [30, 31]. Starch is the primary carbohydrate storage for plants, and it is also the key molecule that regulates plant resistance to external environmental stresses [32]. Plants typically produce energy and carbon from starch to meet the carbon needs of plants [33]. In arid environments, plants decompose starch into soluble sugars to support their growth or as osmotic adjustment substances or antioxidants to reduce drought damage [34–36]. Starch and sucrose are the final products of photosynthesis and can be changed by drought stress [37]. Under drought stress, low starch content and high sucrose content play important roles in maintaining the leaf water status and plant growth [38]. When plants are subjected to drought stress, they may reduce starch synthesis and convert carbohydrates to sucrose synthesis to maintain a constant sucrose level [39]. HXK is involved in sugar signal transduction and enzymatic functions [40], and a down-regulated expression has been found in drought-resistant genotypes of soybeans under drought stress [14]. In plant fiber cells, sucrose can be rapidly decomposed into hexose by INV or SUS that helps to reduce the osmotic potential [41]. In this study, we found that some HXK genes gradually down-regulated their expression in the strong drought-resistant LD as drought stress persisted. Most of the SUS genes (68.97%) were also down-regulated at 6–24 h under drought stress, indicating that the strong drought resistance of LD may be related to the down-regulation of HXK and SUS genes. In addition, INV was down-regulated at 6–24 h after drought stress in the three alfalfa cultivars, indicating that HXK, INV, and SUS genes may negatively regulate drought stress in alfalfa. In addition, we found that WAXY and GP, the key enzymes of starch synthesis and degradation, were down-regulated in the three alfalfa cultivars after drought stress for 6–24 h, indicating that the synthesis and degradation of alfalfa starch were reduced under drought stress. This also indicated that the starch metabolic pathway was seriously affected under drought stress. As the key enzyme of the starch synthesis pathway [42], the expression of AGP (Ms.gene014268) was up-regulated at 6–24 h under drought stress in LD, while the expressions of the other four AGPs were down-regulated at 6–24 h under drought stress in the three alfalfa cultivars. Therefore, a comprehensive analysis showed that HXK, INV, SUS, WAXY, and AGP may be the key genes that improve alfalfa drought resistance, and starch and sucrose metabolism primarily negatively regulated alfalfa drought stress. Currently, gene editing technology is becoming increasingly mature, and the identification of these gene resources is very important for cultivating new drought-resistant alfalfa cultivars. In the future, the expressions of these genes are expected to be regulated, and the activities of enzymes related to sugar metabolism will be activated to improve the efficiency of sugar decomposition to affect the tolerance of alfalfa under drought stress.

Redox system involved in the alfalfa drought stress response

GST is a type of multifunctional enzymatic antioxidant, and it is also the key enzyme of the glutathione metabolism pathway. GST can catalyze the covalent binding of GSH with electrophilic substrate and transfer it to plastids or vacuoles and catalyze H2O2 to convert GSH to oxidized glutathione (GSSG). It plays an important role in plant growth and development and cell resistance to biological and abiotic stresses [43]. The PjGSTU1 gene of Prosopisjuliflora was overexpressed in E. coli, and it was found that PjGSTU1 had both GSH transferase and GSH peroxidase activities. PjGSTU1 overexpression in tobacco (Nicotianatabacum) improved its ability to scavenge ROS under drought stress [44]. Anik et al. [43] analyzed the bean genome, and a total of 59 GST genes were found. A qRT-PCR analysis showed that the up-regulation of PvGSTs may cause Prunella vulgaris adapt to drought and salt stress environments. In this study, 52 DEGs were enriched in the glutathione metabolic pathway, including 37 GST-related genes. Among the GST family members, the GST3, GSTF2, GSTL3, GSTZ1, and GSTZ2 genes were up-regulated after drought stress induction in the three alfalfa cultivars. Therefore, it can be concluded that the up-regulated expression of these GST genes in alfalfa under drought stress plays an important role in alleviating the oxidative stress response. APX decomposes H2O2 generated by superoxide in the ascorbic acid GSH cycle pathway and produces monodehydroascorbic acid, thus preventing ROS and eliminating excessive H2O2 caused by adversity [45]. In this study, we also found that APX1 was significantly up-regulated at 24 h under drought stress in drought-resistant cultivar G8 and up-regulated at 12 and 24 h under drought stress in the strong drought-resistant cultivar LD, indicating that the up-regulated expression of APX1 was closely related to alfalfa drought resistance. In addition, the up-regulated expression times of the different drought-resistant cultivars were different, which may help explain why LD was more drought-tolerant than G8. Under drought stress, LD can resist drought stress by mobilizing the expression of APX1 earlier, thereby reducing adverse effects. Li et al. [45] showed that the eight APX members of A. thaliana were involved in the growth and development process and abiotic stress response of A. thaliana, and APX3 and APX5 responded significantly to salt, drought, and heat stress. In conclusion, the GST and APX1 genes, the key enzymes of the glutathione metabolism pathway, were important mitigation factors of oxidative stress in alfalfa roots under a PEG-6000 simulated drought environment.

Conclusion

In this study, three alfalfa cultivars with different drought resistances were used to analyze the root physiology and transcriptome under PEG-6000 simulated drought stress at different times. Compared with drought-sensitive materials, strong drought-resistant materials eliminate H2O2 produced under drought stress conditions by enhancing the activity and content of antioxidant enzymes, slowing down the rate of O2.– production, maintaining normal cell membrane permeability, and thereby enhancing the ability to resist drought stress. At the transcriptional level, phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, flavonoid biosynthesis, starch and sucrose metabolism, glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, glutathione metabolism, and the biosynthesis of amino acids are important regulatory pathways, and they all participated in the alfalfa drought stress response. Genes enriched in these pathways were further analyzed and screened to the PAL, COMT, 4CL, CCR, CAD, GST, and APX1 genes, as well as HXK, INV, SUS, WAXY and AGP. These could be used as potential candidate genes for alfalfa drought resistance in which the former was positively regulated and the latter was negatively regulated. The results of this study provide basic data for comprehensively revealing the alfalfa drought resistance mechanism, and it also provides important genetic resources for drought-resistant molecular breeding of alfalfa.

Materials and methods

Plant materials

All alfalfa varieties used in this study were commercial cultivars. M. sativa cv. Gannong No. 3 (G3), M. sativa cv. Gannong No. 8 (G8), and M. sativa cv. Longdong (LD) was obtained from the College of Pratacultural Science, Gansu Agricultural University. We were permitted to use these seeds for scientific research. The collection and use of plant materials in this study complied with the relevant institutional, national, and international guidelines and legislation. Based on previous experiments conducted by our research group [46], we selected the drought-sensitive cultivar G3, the drought-resistant cultivar G8, and the highly drought-resistant cultivar LD as the test germplasms of alfalfa for the 15% PEG-6000 simulated drought stress.

Growth and treatment of plant materials

Prior to the experiment, the seeds of various alfalfa cultivars were selected that were plump, uniform in size, and yellow in color. They underwent disinfection treatments as follows: first, they were soaked in distilled water for 30 min, then disinfected in 75% ethanol for 2 min, followed by 20% sodium hypochlorite for 10 min, rinsed five times with ddH2O, and finally dried with sterile filter paper to remove surface moisture. The disinfected seeds were evenly placed in sterile Petri dishes lined with two layers of filter paper (9 cm in diameter), with 50 seeds per dish and six replicates per germplasm. After five days of seed germination, uniform and vigorous seedlings were selected and transplanted into 96-well hydroponic boxes containing the Hoagland nutrient solution (12 cm × 8 cm × 11 cm), with 24 seedlings per tray. After three weeks of hydroponic growth, simulated drought stress was induced using the Hoagland nutrient solution supplemented with 15% PEG-6000, while the control group continued with the regular Hoagland nutrient solution. Root samples of seedlings from each treatment were collected at 0, 6, 12, and 24 h after drought treatment, with each treatment having three replicates. The collected samples were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at − 80 °C for further physiological and transcriptomic analyses.

Measurement and methods of the physiological indicators

Root tissues were prepared by separating three replicates of 0.5 g of fresh root tissue from each of the three alfalfa cultivars at different stress time points into pre-cooled mortars. A total of 4 ml of 50 mmol·L–1 phosphoric acid buffer (pH = 7.8) was added, and tissue was ground until homogenization. The homogenates were centrifuged for 20 min (4 °C, 12,000 rpm), and the supernatants were transferred into new centrifuge tubes and stored in a refrigerator at 4 °C for subsequent use. The MDA content was measured using the thiobarbituric acid assay method. SOD activity was determined using nitroblue tetrazolium photoreduction. CAT activity was determined using the H2O2 method. POD activity was estimated using the guaiacol method according to Liu et al. [24]. APX activity was determined according to Nakano and Asada [47]. The content of soluble protein (SP) was determined using Coomassie brilliant blue G-250 staining [24]. The soluble sugar (SS) content was determined using the anthene reagent method [24].

RNA extraction, library construction, and RNA sequencing

The total RNA was extracted from 36 alfalfa root samples, and the RNA concentration and purity were measured using the NanoDrop2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Wilmington, DE). The integrity of the RNA was assessed using the RNA Nano 6000 test kit from the Agilent Bioanalyzer 2100 System (Agilent Technologies, CA, USA). The mRNAs were purified using Dynabeads Oligo (dT) 25 (Life Technologies). The cDNA libraries were constructed according to the operation instructions of the Hieff NGS® Ultima Dual-mode mRNA Library Prep Kit. These libraries were sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 by the Genedenovo Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Guangzhou, China). The raw sequencing data have been uploaded to the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) Sequence Read Archive (SRA) database under the accession number PRJNA1134672. The filtered clean reads were aligned to the autotetraploid alfalfa reference genome (https://figshare.com/articles/dataset/genome_fasta_sequence_and_annotation_files/12327602) using the Hisat2 tool software. To eliminate the effect of the gene length and sequencing amount difference on the calculated gene expression, we used the transcripts per kilobase of the exon model per million mapped reads (TPM) method. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were analyzed using a DESeq R package with the thresholds of a false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05 and a |Fold Change| ≥ 2. Then the DEGs were enriched using GO [48] and KEGG [49] analyses.

DEG temporal expression pattern analysis

To determine the temporal expression patterns of the three alfalfa cultivars under drought stress, we utilized the Short Time Series Expression Miner (STEM) software (v1.3.11) [50] to analyze the DEGs at 0, 6, 12, and 24 h following the PEG-6000 treatment.

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis

A gene co-expression network was constructed using the expression data of 36 alfalfa root samples. The thresholds were established as follows: an average gene expression TPM value ≥ 20, a power value of 8, a minimum number of modules of 50, and a maximum number of modules of 20. The gene expression adjacency matrix was constructed and used for the network topology analysis. A module correlation analysis was performed using the module feature values and all physiological data determined in this study, and the correlation coefficients between the physiological data and the gene module features were calculated using the Pearson correlation method. The correlation heatmap was then drawn.

Gene expression analysis using qRT-PCR

To enhance the reliability of transcriptome data validation, the total RNA used for the library construction was reverse transcribed and cDNA synthesized using the PrimeScript™ RT reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser (TAKARA, Japan). Specific primers for the 15 DEGs were designed using Primer-BLAST on NCBI (Table S7). qPCR amplification was performed on LightCycler 96 (Roche, Basel, Switzerland) using super real premix plus (SYBR Green) (Tiangen, Shanghai, China). The amplification process followed the procedure of Chen et al. [51]. The relative transcription levels of the selected genes were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCT method and normalized to the expression levels of the MsActin gene.

Data statistics and analysis methods

The physiological and biochemical indices and RT-qPCR data of the three alfalfa cultivars were organized using Microsoft Excel 2016. These data were subjected to a one-way ANOVA using IBM SPSS Statistics Software 22.0 (Armonk, USA), with the significance level defined as P < 0.05. Graphs were generated using GraphPad Prism 8 (GraphPad Software, USA).

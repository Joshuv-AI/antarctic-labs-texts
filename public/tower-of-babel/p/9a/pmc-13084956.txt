Simlukafusp alfa (FAP-IL2v) plus atezolizumab with or without bevacizumab in unresectable, metastatic renal cell carcinoma: a randomized, open-label phase Ib study

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSimlukafusp alfa (FAP-IL2v) was engineered to preferentially activate CD8+ T and natural killer (NK) cells in tumor microenvironments overexpressing fibroblast activation protein (FAP). Checkpoint inhibitors combined with antiangiogenic agents are standard therapy for metastatic renal cell carcinoma (mRCC), which overexpresses FAP. Here, we explored the efficacy, safety, and pharmacodynamic effects of FAP-IL2v in combination with atezolizumab with or without bevacizumab in patients with mRCC.MethodsPatients with treatment-naïve or pretreated clear cell and/or sarcomatoid mRCC were eligible. Dose escalation explored FAP-IL2v every 2 weeks (Q2W) with atezolizumab Q2W (doublet, arm A), and with atezolizumab and bevacizumab Q2W (triplet, arm B) in patients treated with up to one prior systemic therapy. Dose extension explored in untreated patients the recommended FAP-IL2v dose administered Q2W (doublet, arm A; and triplet, arm B) or 3-weekly (doublet, arm C; and triplet, arm D). Primary objectives were the recommended FAP-IL2v dose and antitumor activity. Secondary objectives included safety, pharmacodynamics, and exploratory biomarkers in peripheral blood and paired biopsies.ResultsBy the data cut-off date (31 August, 2021), 66 patients were enrolled. The median duration of treatment was 11.0 months. Objective response rates (ORRs) were 25% for the doublet and 47% for the triplet, and median progression-free survival was 6.3 and 18.3 months, respectively. Safety profiles were consistent with the individual drugs, including expected interleukin-2 (IL-2) class-specific adverse events (AEs). Two deaths were recorded caused by AEs related to study treatment (acute kidney injury, n=1; pancytopenia, n=1). Expansion and activation of NK and T cells, but not regulatory T cells, was observed in peripheral blood, leading to increased tumor infiltration and inflammation in paired biopsies. The addition of bevacizumab led to a reduced angiogenesis signature score and reduced vessel density.ConclusionThe combination of FAP-IL2v plus atezolizumab with or without bevacizumab was consistent with the known safety profile of the individual drugs. The maximum tolerated dose was not reached, with a recommended FAP-IL2v dose of 10 mg. ORR was higher among patients receiving the triplet therapy compared with the doublet. Pharmacodynamic results were consistent with the mechanism of action of IL-2, supporting further research in this field.Keywords: Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor, Solid tumor, T cell, Pharmacodynamics - PD, Combination therapy

Background

High-dose interleukin-2 (IL-2; aldesleukin) was the first immunomodulatory agent to meaningfully improve outcomes for patients with metastatic renal cell carcinoma (mRCC), with monotherapy inducing long-lasting complete responses in around 10% of patients.1 IL-2 enhances antitumor immune responses by triggering T cell expansion and natural killer (NK) cell differentiation and activation, but also stimulates immunosuppressive regulatory T cells (Tregs).2 IL-2 use is limited by a short half-life and distinctive toxicity profile, including capillary leak syndrome (CLS),2 3 that restricts administration to specialized centers.

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (CPIs) enhance antitumor immune responses, which can be further potentiated by increased levels of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) due to anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) therapy.4 Current first-line therapy for patients with advanced/mRCC is a programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) inhibitor plus VEGF receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) or cytotoxic T-lymphocyte antigen-4 inhibitor.5 Atezolizumab is an engineered, humanized, IgG1 monoclonal antibody that binds to programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1), blocking its interaction with PD-1 in the tumor microenvironment (TME) to reinvigorate suppressed tumor-specific cytotoxic T cells.6 7 Atezolizumab demonstrated antitumor activity as monotherapy and in combination with bevacizumab in patients with treatment-naïve mRCC in the randomized phase II IMmotion150 and phase III IMmotion151 studies.8,10

RCC is associated with VEGF overexpression, a key mediator of angiogenesis, and inactivation of the upstream von Hippel-Lindau tumor suppressor gene.11 12 VEGF overexpression is immunosuppressive by downregulating dendritic cells and effector T cells13,15 and stimulating Tregs.16 Inhibiting VEGF signaling activates endothelial cells, promotes degradation of the surrounding extracellular matrix, and increases vascular permeability, resulting in improved T cell infiltration.4 17 18 VEGF inhibition also reverses T cell exhaustion,15 19 upregulates major histocompatibility complex class I expression, and reverses myeloid immunosuppression.4 20 21 Thus, agents targeting the VEGF pathway rapidly became standard of care (SoC) for mRCC.17 The humanized anti-VEGF monoclonal antibody, bevacizumab, was first approved for the treatment of mRCC in combination with interferon alpha in 200917 22 23 and has shown benefit when combined with high-dose IL-2.24

Simlukafusp alfa, also known as FAP-IL2v (RO6874281), is a novel monomeric IL-2 variant (IL2v) immunocytokine that was engineered to overcome the limitations of wild-type (WT) IL-2 by selectively promoting immune responses in TMEs. FAP-IL2v comprises a single IL2v moiety, with retained affinity to IL-2 receptor (R)-beta-gamma but not IL-2R-alpha, fused with a human IgG1 antibody directed against anti-fibroblast activation protein alpha (FAP), a transmembrane protease highly expressed on cancer-associated stroma cells in >90% of human epithelial malignancies.25 FAP-IL2v is differentiated from high-dose IL-2 therapy by its selective tumor stroma targeting and preferential activation of CD4+/CD8+ T cells and NK cells but not Tregs, plus an improved toxicity profile and longer half-life than WT IL-2, leading to more convenient dosing and administration.25 26

Given the sensitivity of mRCC to IL-2, inclusive of CPI and TKI failure,27 28 we hypothesized that FAP-IL2v could enhance and extend CPI-mediated responses, and that this could be further improved by additional direct blockade of the VEGF pathway. Bevacizumab and atezolizumab were chosen based on available clinical data from combination studies with IL-2 in mRCC.4 8 10 22 23 An efficacious combination with FAP-IL2v may provide an alternative CPI-based regimen that spares TKI use alongside a reduced toxicity profile.

Here, we present results of a randomized, multicenter, open-label phase Ib dose escalation and dose expansion study evaluating the antitumor activity and safety of FAP-IL2v plus atezolizumab (doublet) or FAP-IL2v plus atezolizumab and bevacizumab (triplet) in patients with unresectable advanced/mRCC.

Methods

Study design

This was a randomized, multicenter, open-label phase Ib study of FAP-IL2v in combination with atezolizumab with or without bevacizumab in adults with unresectable advanced and/or mRCC (BP39365; NCT03063762). The study comprised dose escalation (arms A and B) and dose extension (arms A–D) parts (figure 1A).

During dose escalation, all patients received FAP-IL2v plus atezolizumab during cycle 1 and were then randomized prior to cycle 2 to the doublet (arm A: FAP-IL2v plus atezolizumab) or triplet arms (arm B: FAP-IL2v plus atezolizumab and bevacizumab). It was hypothesized that although bevacizumab may improve TIL infiltration, bevacizumab-induced normalization of the tumor vasculature and reduced capillary leakiness might impair penetration of FAP-IL2v, and so bevacizumab was added at cycle 2 to allow initial loading with FAP-IL2v. Dose escalation was carried out independently for each treatment arm using a 3+3 dose escalation of FAP-IL2v. Atezolizumab was administered intravenously from day 1 at a fixed, flat 840 mg dose every 2 weeks (Q2W), 1 hour prior to FAP-IL2v. FAP-IL2v was administered IV, weekly (QW) during a 4-week induction period and then Q2W during a maintenance period from day 29 onwards. The initial starting FAP-IL2v dose was 5 mg for cohort 1 and 10 mg for cohort 2. Up-titration was explored in dose escalation cohorts 3 and 4. The FAP-IL2v dose administered from day 8 onwards was up-titrated based on the safety and tolerability of the preceding dose and maintained throughout the remainder of the treatment period to compensate for the target-mediated drug disposition effect caused by upregulation of the drug target following cycle 1. Patients in cohort 3 started at 10 mg and were up-titrated to 15 mg on day 8 if well tolerated. Similarly, cohort 4 patients started at 15 mg and were up-titrated to 20 mg on day 8. With the triplet regimen, patients also received bevacizumab 10 mg/kg intravenous Q2W starting with cycle 2.

Dose-limiting toxicities (DLTs) are defined in the online supplemental material. The DLT assessment period lasted from the first administration of FAP-IL2v on cycle 1 day 1 to 1 week after cycle 2 day 1 or 2 weeks after up-titration of FAP-IL2v, whichever occurred later. The maximum tolerated dose (MTD) was defined as the highest dose with <33% probability of observing DLT. Once the MTD and/or recommended dose were identified, the extension part was initiated.

During dose extension, patients were randomized between four treatment arms: arms A (doublet) or B (triplet), with induction and maintenance schedules as during dose escalation; or arms C (doublet) or D (triplet), with the recommended FAP-IL2v dose identified during dose escalation on 3-weekly (Q3W) dosing schedules for all study drugs. Q3W dosing was chosen to investigate activity of pulsatile exposure of FAP-IL2v following initial pharmacokinetic data from Q2W dosing. Q2W dosing resulted in more frequent exposure to FAP-IL2v, leading to a higher level of IL-2 receptor-positive cells throughout the dosing interval, as opposed to with Q3W dosing, where FAP-IL2v was cleared from the circulation between each cycle, allowing peripheral IL-2 receptor-positive cells to return to their baseline levels. Q3W dosing may potentially improve tolerability, patient and administrative convenience, and might avoid any treatment-mediated drug disposition or overstimulation of tumor-infiltrating immune cells from regular FAP-IL2v dosing. In arm D, patients were planned to receive bevacizumab 15 mg/kg intravenous Q3W on day 1 of cycle 1. Following an emerging safety signal after first administration of the triplet (three cases meeting Hy’s law), the regimen was modified for all patients (17 ongoing and three newly enrolled) to instead receive bevacizumab on day 8 of each cycle, and only from cycle 2 onwards. Treatment continued for up to 24 months or until progressive disease (PD), toxicity, or withdrawal (treatment beyond PD was considered if clinical benefit was maintained).

Obinutuzumab (an anti-CD20 antibody) pretreatment was explored to prevent antidrug antibody (ADA) formation but was only given to five patients in this study, as the sponsor concluded from other studies of IL-2-based regimens29 that obinutuzumab in combination with FAP-IL2v and atezolizumab may carry an unacceptable risk of hepatotoxicity. There was no other mandated premedication prior to the first FAP-IL2v dose.

Arms A, B, and C concluded enrollment in December 2018, and no new patients were consented into arm D after March 2019.

Further details on the methodology of this study can be found in online supplemental file 2.

Patients

Eligible patients were ≥18 years of age with confirmed unresectable advanced/mRCC with a component of clear cell and/or sarcomatoid histology. During the dose escalation part only, where the objective was to establish a dose for the expansion, patients with unresectable advanced/mRCC with up to one prior therapy were eligible. During dose expansion, only patients with no prior systemic therapy, including in the adjuvant setting, were eligible. All patients had measurable disease (according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) V.1.1.), an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0 to 1, an evaluable Memorial Sloan-Kettering Cancer Center risk score (ie, Motzer score), and life expectancy ≥12 weeks.

Objectives

The primary objective during dose escalation was to determine the MTD and/or recommended dose for further development of FAP-IL2v when used in combination with atezolizumab with or without bevacizumab. The primary objective during dose extension was to describe the antitumor activity of selected doses and regimens of FAP-IL2v when used in combination with atezolizumab with or without bevacizumab, using objective response rate (ORR). Secondary objectives included evaluation of safety and tolerability, pharmacodynamics (on peripheral blood cells and in tumor samples), pharmacokinetics, and additional antitumor activity. Exploratory endpoints included assessment of potential predictive biomarkers for therapeutic activity.

Assessments

Antitumor activity was determined by ORR (defined as the proportion of patients achieving a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) for ≥28 days) confirmed by investigator assessment according to RECIST V.1.1.; disease control rate (DCR; defined as the proportion of patients achieving a CR, PR, and/or stable disease for ≥6 weeks); duration of response (DOR); and progression-free survival (PFS; defined as the time from randomization to the first occurrence of PD or death from any cause).

Safety was assessed by monitoring DLTs during dose escalation and by incidence of adverse events (AEs) and infusion-related reactions (IRRs). AEs were reported based on Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events V.4.03 and encoded using MedDRA V.24.0. Predefined, exploratory biomarker analyses were performed as a secondary endpoint. We obtained sequential whole blood samples and fresh or archival tumor biopsies from patients for pharmacodynamic analyses. In peripheral blood, analyses included dynamic changes in number and activation/proliferation of key immune cell subsets by immune phenotyping, and changes in soluble CD25 (sCD25) by immunoassay. Fresh tumor tissue samples, the majority of which were obtained from metastatic sites, were subjected to immunohistochemistry for determination of densities of key immune cell subsets and expression of PD-L1, FAP, and CD34, and transcriptomic analyses to identify gene signature changes. Details of gene signature composition and calculation are described in the online supplemental material and, as defined in McDermott et al,8 patients pretreated with obinutuzumab were excluded from pharmacodynamic analyses due to their low number. Full methodology can be found in the online supplemental material.

Statistical analyses

All patients who received at least one dose of any study drug were evaluated for clinical activity and safety. The doublet and triplet arms were combined for analysis of clinical activity. ORR and DCR were computed along with their 90% CI. Continuous endpoints were summarized through mean, median, range, and 95% CI for the mean. Kaplan-Meier methodology was used to estimate median time-to-event endpoints with associated 95% CI. Pharmacodynamic data were presented as numeric results or as ratios compared with baseline. Formal statistical analyses were performed for subset comparisons only, using Student’s t-test where a p<0.05 was considered significant.

Results

Patients

Between March 2017 and March 2019, 69 patients were enrolled at 19 centers in 9 countries.

By the data cut-off date (August 31, 2021), study treatment was received by 66 patients who were evaluable for safety and antitumor activity (figure 1A). Three patients discontinued after randomization and before receipt of study treatment. Baseline demographics and disease characteristics were generally balanced between treatment arms, including patient score risk groups, except for a greater proportion of patients with high-risk disease in arm A (table 1). Median age was 57.5 years (range, 35 to 78). Most patients were male (78.8%). Median treatment duration was 11.0 months (range, 0.0 to 38.9). Enrollment into arm C was closed due to emerging data from the dose escalation part favoring the triplet regimen, so analysis of arm C was limited due to the low number of patients (n=3). Overall, 28 patients were treated with the doublet (FAP-IL2v plus atezolizumab; arm A, n=25; arm C, n=3) and 38 with the triplet regimens (FAP-IL2v plus atezolizumab and bevacizumab; arm B, n=15; arm D, n=23). Rates of discontinuation of study treatment were similar for patients treated with doublet (24/28 (86%)) or triplet regimens (31/38 (82%)). The main reasons for discontinuation were PD (n=16) and physician decision (n=3) with the doublet, and PD (n=19), withdrawal by patient (n=3), or death (n=2) for the triplet regimens.

Dose-limiting toxicities

One patient in arm A (FAP-IL2v 10/15 mg + atezolizumab) experienced a grade 3 DLT of vasogenic cerebral edema during the dose extension part, which was considered related to study treatment. Following the dose interruption of FAP-IL2v, the event resolved. The patient had PR and continued the study for 2 years. No patients experienced DLTs in arm B. The MTD for FAP-IL2v was not reached, and the recommended dose for extension was identified as 10 mg based on the recommended FAP-IL2v monotherapy dose of 15 mg previously established,30 the potential additional toxicity from the combination, and data from prior studies including evidence for FAP-IL2v monotherapy generating profound lymphocyte expansion and activation at a 10 mg dose.30 Details of the number of patients enrolled in each dose cohort during the dose escalation phase are included in online supplemental table 1.

Antitumor activity

In patients receiving the doublet, the ORR was 25% (CR: 0%, PR: 25%), the DCR was 61% and median DOR was not estimable (NE; 95% CI 15.3 months to NE). With the triplet, ORR was 47% (CR: 8%; PR: 39%), DCR was 89%, and median DOR was NE (95% CI 12.6 months to NE; table 2; figure 1B–D). Median PFS was 6.3 months (95% CI 1.9 to 18.2) and 18.3 months (95% CI 11.0 to 22.1) with the doublet and triplet regimens, respectively (figure 1E). Best overall response was lower in the poor risk International Metastatic RCC Database Consortium category (online supplemental table 2).

Safety

All patients experienced ≥1 AE considered related to FAP-IL2v, most commonly pyrexia (83%), chills (68%), and nausea (52%). Rates of pyrexia, pruritus, and diarrhea were higher with the triplet versus doublet, with decreased appetite and increased alanine aminotransferase and aspartate aminotransferase higher in arm D. Overall, 58% of patients experienced serious AEs (SAEs), which were considered related to any study drug in 47% of patients (table 3). Frequencies of AEs were broadly similar between treatment arms.

Safety summary

Atezolizumab (Q2W: 840 mg flat dose; Q3W: 1200 mg flat dose); bevacizumab (Q2W: 10 mg/kg; Q3W: 15 mg/kg).

Related to FAP-IL2v.

AE, adverse event; ALT, alanine aminotransferase; AST, aspartate transaminase; FAP, fibroblast activation protein; LFT, liver function test; QW, weekly; Q2W, every 2 weeks; Q3W, every 3 weeks; SAE, serious adverse event.

Expected IL-2 based therapy class-specific AEs (all grades) were observed following FAP-IL2v treatment, including pyrexia (83%), liver function test abnormalities (70%), chills (68%), IRRs (38%) and hypotension (15%), with rates generally higher with the triplet than doublet, except for IRRs and gamma-glutamyltransferase increases. Incidences of these AEs did not appear dose-dependent. There were no reports of CLS, an IL-2 class-specific AE characterized by plasma leakage from the blood vessels, and only three serious cases of treatment-related hypotension were reported (all grade 3).

No patients experienced AEs leading to withdrawal of treatment during dose escalation, and only one patient withdrew due to AEs during dose extension (arm C; grade 3 SAE of drug-induced liver injury, considered related to FAP-IL2v and atezolizumab; atezolizumab was withdrawn with no change in FAP-IL2v dose). Overall, 61% of patients experienced AEs leading to FAP-IL2v dose interruption or modification during dose escalation and 53% during dose extension, with similar rates across arms. There were three deaths reported in the study: one patient (arm A) died due to grade 5 acute kidney injury reported as related to FAP-IL2v and atezolizumab; one patient (arm D) died due to grade 5 hemothorax possibly related to antiplatelet treatment; and one patient (arm A) died due to an AE of pancytopenia secondary to treatment during the survival follow-up period, 236 days after the last dose of study treatment.

Biomarkers associated with clinical outcomes

Potential predictive biomarkers for response to doublet and triplet regimens were investigated in peripheral blood and tumor tissue, including gene signatures established in the IMmotion150 study (such as T-effector, angiogenesis, and myeloid inflammation).8 This study was not designed to determine differences between arms, and these are not direct comparisons. Responders were enriched among patients with a high T effector signature (figure 2A) in the doublet and triplet regimens, reaching statistical significance in a pooled analysis (p=0.004 for T effector high vs T effector low; online supplemental figure 1A). No clear trends were observed for the angiogenesis and myeloid inflammation signatures, but nonsignificant trends toward increased ORR were observed among patients with high angiogenesis signature with the triplet regimen and among patients with low myeloid inflammation signature with the doublet regimen.

In line with this, tumors from responding patients had statistically significantly higher baseline densities of TILs compared with non-responders, particularly NK and CD3+ T cells (also proliferating CD8+ T cells and Treg cells), but not CD8+ T cells or cytotoxic T cells (figure 2B). Baseline levels of PD-L1 on tumor cells, FAP content, and vessel density were overall similar in responders and non-responders (figure 2B). Baseline PD-L1 expression on tumor cells was almost undetectable in most patients, but there was a nonsignificant trend toward higher expression (≥1%) in non-responders than responders. There was also a non-significant trend toward higher median baseline FAP content in responders than non-responders (and highest in a patient with CR).

Despite significant associations of some baseline biomarker levels with clinical activity, there were no pretreatment tumor or blood biomarkers identified that fully segregated responders from non-responders.

Pharmacodynamics in peripheral blood

In peripheral blood, FAP-IL2v (plus atezolizumab and bevacizumab) induced pronounced and sustained NK, CD8+, and CD4A+ T cell expansion (figure 3A), characteristic of the mechanism of action of IL-2.2 Peripheral Treg levels remained unchanged, reflective of the engineered properties of FAP-IL2v in being less potent than IL-2 in activating Tregs.25 T cell expansion in early cycles was driven by proliferation (an elevated proportion of the Ki67+ subset) and accompanied by increased levels of sCD25, a marker of sustained and systemic immune activation (online supplemental figure 1B). The addition of bevacizumab had no clear impact on observed pharmacodynamic profiles. No apparent changes in the number of peripheral B cells were observed (data not shown).

Pharmacodynamics in paired tumor biopsies

Pharmacodynamic changes were assessed in paired baseline and on-treatment (cycle 4, day 8) tumor biopsies from 18 patients receiving doublet or triplet therapy (arms A and B).

Across both regimens, there was a significant increase in T effector signature score on-treatment versus baseline (p=0.002; data shown by regimen in figure 3B). There were no statistically significant differences with the angiogenesis or myeloid inflammation signatures. As in peripheral blood, there were profound increases in density of various TIL subsets (CD3+ and CD8+ T cells, proliferating CD8 T cells, perforin-positive cytotoxic T and NK cells) in the tumor following both treatments (figure 3C). Corresponding increases in density of Tregs (in 6/18 patients) and PD-L1+ immune cells (in 12/18 patients) were observed in the tumor regardless of response group. Of interest, responders showed more pronounced on-treatment changes in tumor tissue compared with non-responders, particularly for CD3+ T and NK cells (figure 3D).

The pharmacodynamic profiles of both doublet and triplet regimens were consistent with the known profiles of the constituent drugs but did not reveal any apparent synergistic effect on the immune response. Addition of bevacizumab led to a reduced angiogenesis signature score (figure 3B) and a reduced vessel density (online supplemental figure 1C) following treatment that was not observed with the doublet and may partly explain the greater clinical activity observed with the triplet regimen.

Pharmacokinetics

The FAP-IL2v pharmacokinetic behavior was similar irrespective of regimen with target-mediated drug disposition and self-induced clearance, and also similar to FAP-IL2v monotherapy.30 With QW FAP-IL2v for 4 weeks then Q2W, 9/40 (23%) patients remained negative for ADAs against FAP-IL2v throughout treatment, 24/40 (60%) developed persistent ADAs, and 6/40 (15%) developed transient ADAs. With Q3W FAP-IL2v, 8/26 (31%) patients remained negative for ADAs against FAP-IL2v throughout treatment, 16/26 (62%) developed persistent ADAs, and 2/26 (8%) developed transient ADAs. There was no correlation between development of ADAs and clinical response.

Discussion

In this phase Ib study, we explored the safety, activity, pharmacodynamics, and pharmacokinetics of the combination of FAP-IL2v plus atezolizumab with or without bevacizumab. The more intense QW/Q2W dosing schedule did not translate into additional clinical activity compared with the Q3W schedule. Triplet therapy had better clinical activity than doublet therapy in key parameters, although formal statistical testing was not performed (table 2). The most active treatment option among these combinations appeared to be the triplet regimen in a Q3W schedule.

The safety profile was consistent with the known safety profiles of the individual drugs.8 10 No unexpected overlapping toxicities were identified. FAP-IL2v-related AEs were consistent with IL-2 class-specific effects and were manageable. In addition, use of WT IL-2 can be limited by multiorgan toxicity, including dermatologic, neurologic, hepatic, pulmonary, and vascular effects.3 In addition, there is the well-reported serious class-specific AE of CLS, which requires close patient monitoring in a hospital setting. The incidence of AEs such as CLS and neurotoxicity means WT IL-2 is often administered in specialized centers. In our study, within the therapeutic range investigated, there were no reported cases of CLS, only three cases of serious, treatment-related hypotension, and two deaths (due to acute kidney injury and long-term pancytopenia) considered related to FAP-IL2v and atezolizumab. This safety profile for FAP-IL2v is favorable compared with WT IL-2, with mostly flu-like illness or asymptomatic laboratory abnormalities.

While in this study, the triplet arms showed greater clinical activity than the doublet arms, neither regimen seemed better than current SoC in the first-line setting (ORR 56% to 71% and median PFS 15.4 to 23.9 months).31,33

Transcriptomic analysis in the current study to identify potential predictive biomarkers for response to the doublet or triplet regimen indicated that T effector gene signature expression was more likely to be high at baseline in responders, consistent with the IMmotion150 and IMmotion151 findings.8 34 Tumor inflammation and TIL infiltration (particularly CD3+ T cells and NK cells) were significantly higher in responders with both doublet and triplet regimens and associated with improved outcomes. However, some non-responders had elevated immune cell levels, suggesting underlying mechanisms are multifactorial. A previous study of T cell response to CPI therapy in mRCC observed an increase in activated CD8+ T cells in responders, and these cells contained new T cell receptor clonotypes compared with non-responders, indicating potential for priming and expansion of additional T cells.35 In the current study, baseline PD-L1 expression did not predict response for doublet or triplet therapy, which is similar to CPI plus TKI combinations in the first-line setting,31,3336 although not with the IMmotion151 study of atezolizumab plus bevacizumab.10

The recent combination of a pegylated IL-2 prodrug, bempegaldesleukin, with nivolumab in patients with intermediate or poor risk advanced/mRCC reported an ORR of 23% in a phase III study, similar to that observed with the doublet regimen in the current study, and did not improve clinical outcomes versus TKI monotherapy.37 A pharmacodynamic analysis of peripheral and tumor biomarkers in the current study supports there being additive but no synergistic augmentation between CPI and IL-2 effects. Peripheral and TIL populations before and during treatment showed expansion, proliferation, and activation patterns consistent with the activity of immunostimulatory agents. Treatment with either regimen resulted in increased expression of T effector gene signatures. These observations are aligned with the demonstrated activity of FAP-IL2v and atezolizumab separately, but without strong evidence of a synergistic effect.

Peripheral blood biomarkers of vessel density and tumor expression of angiogenesis-related genes were consistent with known effects of bevacizumab, possibly due to blood vessel normalization, suggesting bevacizumab was biologically active. However, in combination with FAP-IL2v and atezolizumab, bevacizumab had no apparent additional effect on TIL expansion, proliferation, or activation. It is possible any effect from bevacizumab was masked by the profound pharmacodynamic impact observed with FAP-IL2v plus atezolizumab. There was no indication of a synergistic effect from adding bevacizumab, especially regarding T cell profiles.

Limitations for phase I studies include small sample sizes and designs not intended for direct comparisons. An overall survival analysis for this study was not performed due to early discontinuation of the study and inconsistent follow-up for survival status and censoring. Although the development of FAP-IL2v has been deprioritized in all indications, including this study, by the study sponsor for strategic reasons, IL-2-based immune cytokines remain a promising approach. The clinical activity, feasibility, and pharmacokinetic impact within the periphery and TME with FAP-IL2v when used in combination with atezolizumab with or without bevacizumab warrants the continued investigation of IL2-based therapies and other novel immunocytokines, for their potential to improve patient outcomes with current SoC therapies in mRCC.

A Longitudinal Disease Progression Model Characterizing Endoscopic Response and Remission in Crohn’s Disease
Authors: Anita Moein, Jurgen Langenhorst, Nastya Kassir
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Crohn’s disease (CD) is a heterogeneous, progressive inflammatory bowel disease in which substantial placebo effects often confound assessment of therapeutic efficacy. A placebo-based disease model was developed using Simple Endoscopic Score for Crohn’s Disease (SES-CD) within an item-level response theory-bounded integer (IRT-BI) and full random effects models (FREM) framework. Longitudinal SES-CD data from 155 placebo-treated patients in the Phase 3 BERGAMOT-trial were analyzed. Each SES-CD subscore, across five intestinal segments, was described by a bounded-integer model and linked through a latent disease variable capturing a mono-exponential placebo trajectory. Endoscopic response (≥50% reduction from baseline SES-CD) and remission (SES-CD 0–2) were defined according to the FDA guidance. IRT-BI model adequately characterized longitudinal trajectories of all SES-CD subscores and accurately predicted individual patient endoscopic response and remission, with predictive accuracy exceeding 89% for responders and 85% for non-responders. Visual predictive checks showed that observed mean trajectories for all 20 SES-CD subscores were within the 90% confidence intervals across the 66-week trial. Population maximum placebo response (RMaxPL) was estimated at -0.220 with a time to half-maximal effect of 13.8 weeks. Covariate analysis identified elevated baseline C-reactive protein and fecal calprotectin correlated to reduced RMaxPL, whereas TNF-naïve status was associated with increased RMaxPL. This is first application of an integrated item-level IRT-BI-FREM approach in CD, enabling robust characterization of placebo response and identification of clinically relevant covariates. By providing quantitative framework for isolating drug-effects from placebo-response, this model aligns with FDA model-informed drug development priorities and supports efficient, targeted, and informative clinical trials.Clinical Trial RegistrationNCT02394028 Registered March 20, 2015Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s10928-026-10042-x.

Introduction

Crohn’s disease (CD) is a chronic, relapsing form of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) that can affect any portion of the gastrointestinal (GI) tract, it most commonly affects the small intestine, typically in the terminal ileum, and parts of the colon [1]. CD is characterized by patchy, transmural inflammation, ulcers, and granulomatous lesions that are interspersed with healthy sections of bowel. The disease is progressive; uncontrolled inflammation develops into a narrowing of the intestine caused by scar tissue; or penetrating complications such as prestenotic dilatation, obstruction, and intra-abdominal or perianal fistulas and abscesses. Clinical signs and symptoms include chronic diarrhea, abdominal pain, cachexia, abdominal mass, or tenderness as well as the overt signs of fistulas. The disease course is variable; patients can experience a severe initial flare followed by few symptoms over the next 10 years (43%) or symptoms that are chronic and persistent (19%) or relapsing-remitting (32%) [2].

The Simple Endoscopic Score for Crohn’s Disease (SES-CD) is a validated quantitative method used to assess the severity of Crohn’s disease based on endoscopic findings [3]. It evaluates four key parameters across five intestinal segments: ileum, right colon, transverse colon, left colon, and rectum. Each parameter is scored from 0 to 3, yielding a segment-specific score which is then summed to calculate the total SES-CD score, with a range of 0 to 60: (1) size of ulcers: scores range from 0 (absent) to 3 (very large ulcers > 2 cm), (2) ulcerated surface: scores range from 0 (none) to 3 (> 30% of the segment affected), (3) affected surface: scores range from 0 (none) to 3 (> 75% of the segment affected), and (4) presence of narrowings: scores range from 0 (none) to 3 (frank stenosis, not passable by scope). SES-CD parameters/subscore are presented in supplementary Table S1. Higher SES-CD scores are indicative of more severe disease. This scoring system is a crucial tool used to quantify disease activity, guide therapeutic decisions, and measure treatment outcomes in both clinical practice and research settings.

Effective management of chronic conditions like Crohn’s disease relies heavily on rigorous evaluation of treatment efficacy in clinical trials. The SES-CD is a widely accepted metric for assessing mucosal healing and disease severity; however, conventional analytical approaches often fail to capture the heterogeneity and complexity of individual patient responses. To overcome these limitations, we introduce a disease model platform that integrates an item-level response theory-bounded integer (IRT-BI) framework with the full random effects model (FREM) approach. This combined methodology enables joint analysis of SES-CD placebo data and patient-specific characteristics, providing a more refined and predictive understanding of disease progression and placebo response dynamics. A previously published disease model for ulcerative colitis (UC) predicted placebo response and remission status of patients using the modified Mayo Score (mMS) within an item response theory-bounded integer framework [4]. The UC IRT-BI model adequately described the mMS subscores and reliably predicted placebo response and remission rates.

In the current study by developing a disease model with item-level IRT-BI framework for Crohn’s disease, we aim to predict longitudinal SES-CD subscores, endoscopic response, and endoscopic remission status of patients treated with placebo during the induction and maintenance phases. This item-level IRT-BI disease model platform extended with FREM establishes a quantitative foundation for understanding disease variability based on patients’ characteristics and comparing treatment effects. By estimating the placebo effect, our goal is to advance model-informed drug development (MIDD) for Crohn’s disease by enhancing the precision and sensitivity of treatment efficacy assessments in clinical trials of new therapeutics.

Methods

Clinical Trial

BERGAMOT (NCT02394028). All clinical trial information can be accessed through the following website. https://clinicaltrials.gov/.

Study Ethics

BERGAMOT trial (NCT02394028) was approved by the institutional review board or independent ethics committee. All patients provided written informed consent. All authors had access to the study data and reviewed and approved the final manuscript.

Study Design

Data from randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter Phase 3 clinical trial (BERGAMOT) in patients with moderately-to-severely active Crohn’s Disease was used in model development [5]. The study design consisted of an induction phase (14 weeks), followed by a maintenance phase (52 weeks) in subjects demonstrating at least 70 points decrease from Crohn’s disease Activity Index (CDAI) baseline score at the end of the induction phase [5]. Subjects on placebo treatment during the induction phase who achieved a CDAI-70 response continued placebo treatment during the maintenance phase.

Model Development

Analysis Dataset

The disease model was developed using only patients who received placebo during both the induction and maintenance phases. Patients’ baseline characteristics are provided in supplementary Table S2 and Table S3.

Structural Model

The developed item-level IRT-BI model describes the relationship between the patients’ observed SES-CD subscores and a hidden (latent) disease status. Each SES-CD score was described by a bounded integer (BI) model [6]. The BI model for each subscore/item (e.g. size of ulcers in ileum segment) consisted of a location (MEAN) and a dispersion (SD) parameter. A shared placebo response acting equally on all subscores was added, thereby implicitly linking the scores by a latent disease status, constituting the IRT model. The illustration of the item-level IRT-BI model is presented in Figure S1.The model code is provided in the supplementary materials. The modeling analyses were performed using nonlinear mixed effect models (NONMEM) with conditional Laplace likelihood estimation method [7].

In detail, a bounded integer model structure was applied for each item (i.e. subscore) with the following levels of item-specificity: population estimates of baseline items and dispersion parameters (standard deviation, SD) estimated separately for each item; for baseline items one random effect assigned per segment; for items dispersion parameters one random effect assigned across all 20 items. The change in items/subscores location (MEAN) over time was described by a mono-exponential function for placebo response acting on all items assuming a single latent variable (Response).

Endoscopic Assessments Criteria

The criteria used in model development to assess the endoscopic response and the endoscopic remission status of patients was based on the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) recommended guidelines for Crohn’s disease published on April 2022 [8], which defines endoscopic response (i.e. improvement) as ≥ 50% reduction from the baseline SES-CD; and endoscopic remission as SES-CD of 0 to 2 [9].

Model Evaluation

SES-CD subscore parameters predictions using the final CD disease model were evaluated for three properties: (a) individual predictive performance; (b) segment subscore category predictability; (c) items/subscores’ residual correlations; and (d) simulation properties where predictive performance for longitudinal SES-CD subscores, endoscopic response, and endoscopic remission were of interest for assessment.

Plot of individual observed versus predicted composite scores were created by summing up all available subscores at each timepoint. In addition, observed vs. model estimated (IPRED) endoscopic response and endoscopic remission was evaluated at the end of induction and at the end of maintenance based on endoscopic assessments criteria.

The segment category probability curves were used to illustrate the relationship between the latent variable and the probability of each segment subscores to improve. The curves were created in a similar fashion as described by Lyauk and colleagues [10]. The uncertainty in the Empirical Bayes Estimate was taken into account for the predicted segment category probabilities that were compared to the observed. The observed curve was estimated by a single generalized additive model (GAM) smooth of the attainment of segment subscore results versus the latent disease status. These were compared to the 90% confidence interval of 200 GAM results predicted from parameter vectors sampled from a normal distribution with mean individual empirical Bayes estimates and variance individual η Fisher information assessed variance.

To evaluate the assumption that the latent placebo effect operates equivalently across all SES-CD items, a residual-based diagnostic approach described by Gottipati and colleagues [11] was implemented to assess potential item-specific local dependencies beyond the single latent variable structure. For each individual i and item j, the residual was defined using Eq. (4). Pairwise correlations of residuals across the 20 SES-CD items were computed using Pearson correlation, and the resulting correlation matrix was visualized as a heatmap. Correlation values ranged from − 1 (blue) to + 1 (red), with values close to zero indicating little to no dependence among item residuals. The simulation properties of the models were evaluated using visual predictive checks (VPC). VPC were performed with 200 simulations. In the BERGAMOT clinical trial, at the end of the induction phase, patients who achieved clinical response (i.e. a decrease from CDAI baseline score of at least 70 points) were eligible to enter the maintenance phase. In the VPC, this was taken into account by a logistic regression dropout model, where at end of induction, the individual observed CDAI score change from baseline was used as covariate for estimation of probability of dropout for each patient. This dropout model guided the inclusion of the simulated subjects in the maintenance phase. Additionally, the observed versus predicted endoscopic response and remission at the end of induction and at the end of maintenance was evaluated based on FDA recommended endoscopic assessments criteria.

Full Random Effects Model

Covariate relationships on the placebo response (RMaxPL) were evaluated using the full random effects model (FREM) methodology [12, 13]. This innovative approach treats covariates as additional observations rather than fixed independent variables [12, 13]. The FREM model was implemented using NONMEM and fitted via the Monte Carlo importance sampling (IMP) expectation maximization (EM) estimation method. Continuous covariates were log-transformed prior to analysis to reduce skewness and enabled interpretation of covariate effects on a proportional scale, which is biologically more plausible. Covariate relationships were visualized using forest plot. Overall, this framework provided a robust and transparent basis for evaluating the impact of covariates on disease progression, especially in the presence of multicollinearity.

Software

The modeling analyses were performed using nonlinear mixed effect models (NONMEM) version 7.4.1. Data management and further processing of NONMEM output including FREM analysis were performed using PsN version 5.5.0 and R version 4.2.3 [14, 15]. FREM output was generated via the open-source PMXFrem package version 1.2.5 and PMXForest version 1.2.11 [16, 17].

Results

The final CD disease model analysis dataset included a total of 6,202 endoscopic observations from 155 placebo patients with moderately-to-severely active Crohn’s disease. Summary statistics of baseline SES-CD subscore observations per intestinal segment of the GI tract is presented in supplementary Table S4. In the final model, all item-specific parameters for size of ulcers, ulcerated surface, affected surface, and presence of narrowings subscores across five intestinal segments: ileum, right colon, transverse colon, left colon, and rectum were successfully estimated using a single latent variable described by a mono-exponential function for individual placebo responses.

Table 1 presents the final model parameter estimates in bounded integer scale with the key IRT components estimated at the approximate mean score scale. Across intestinal segments, baseline SES-CD subscores were estimated with adequate precision. Comparable baseline score scales were obtained for the size of ulcers, ulcerated surface, and affected surface subscores, whereas the presence of narrowings exhibited lower score scale values, reflecting its lower frequency of occurrence. The maximum placebo response (RMaxPL) on the bounded integer (BI) scale was estimated as − 0.329 (90% CI: − 0.487 to − 0.179). This corresponds to an approximate mean decrease of − 0.220 points per baseline SES-CD subscore (0–3 scale). Given that the placebo effect acts uniformly across the 20 SES-CD components, this translates to an approximate reduction of ~ 4.4 points in the total baseline SES-CD score (0–60 scale) at maximal response. The time to half of the maximal placebo response (T50PL) was estimated at 13.8 weeks (90% CI: 11.32–16.21). Interindividual variability shrinkage ranged between 14.6 and 25.2% across segments, suggesting adequate parameter identifiability and balanced information content across observed subscores [18].

The individual predictive performance of the predicted SES-CD sum of scores was adequate with no visible bias (Figure S2). Individual endoscopic response and endoscopic remission at the end of induction and at the end of maintenance phase was adequately predicted by the model as presented in Fig. 1. Endoscopic responders at the end of induction phase were correctly predicted 16 out of 18 times (89%) and non-responders were correctly predicted 84 out of 88 times (95%) (Fig. 1a). Endoscopic remitters at the end of induction phase were correctly predicted 8 out of 9 times (89%) and non-remitters were correctly predicted 96 out of 97 times (99%) (Fig. 1c). Comparable predictive performance was observed at the end of the maintenance phase, with high accuracy in identifying both endoscopic responders and remitters, as well as non-responders and non-remitters (Fig. 1b and d).

The segment category probability curves of the final model were presented in Fig. 2. The curves confirm the relationship between disease severity and score probability per segment. As the latent variable (RMaxPL) decreases (indicating a better response), the probability of observing a high-severity score (score = 3) declines, while the probability of a low-severity score (score = 0) increases. The segment category probabilities were overall adequately captured by the model.

The correlation matrix of item residuals from the final SES-CD IRT model is shown in Figure S3. Overall, most correlations were of low magnitude, suggesting that the single latent variable structure adequately captured the shared variability among items. Moderate positive correlations between size of ulcers and ulcered surface were observed among items belonging to the same SES-CD domain. Particularly correlation up to 0.58 between size of ulcers and ulcered surface was observed in rectum. These patterns suggest domain-specific dependencies consistent with the clinical structure of the SES-CD, where ulceration, surface involvement, and narrowing represent related but distinct features of endoscopic disease activity. Importantly, the absence of widespread strong correlations across domains indicates that residual dependencies were modest, and the a single latent variable model provided a reasonable description of the SES-CD data at the item level.

The impact of baseline covariates on the estimated maximum placebo response (RMaxPL) was presented using FREM-based forest plot (Figure S5). Patients with elevated fecal calprotectin demonstrated a lower RMaxPL, and a similar pattern was observed for C-reactive protein. Prior anti-TNF exposure was also linked to a reduced placebo response, whereas TNF-naïve patients showed a greater response. Discussion

This study presents a robust disease progression model for Crohn’s disease using an item-level IRT-BI framework extended with FREM applied to longitudinal Simple Endoscopic Score data from placebo-treated patients with moderately-to-severely active Crohn’s disease. The clinical endoscopic response and remission status in patients with CD are driven by longitudinal changes in the SES-CD which reflects underlying mucosal disease status. In this model, each SES-CD subscore was described using a bounded integer structure and linked through a common latent disease trajectory, enabling the characterization of the complex and heterogeneous nature of mucosal inflammation while accommodating sparse endoscopic sampling, a common challenge in CD trials.

The selection of a bounded integer (BI) method [6] over a traditional categorical IRT model estimation was driven by both methodological and practical considerations specific to SES-CD data. Although SES-CD subscores are ordinal with four levels (0–3), categorical IRT approaches would require estimation of item-specific discrimination and multiple threshold parameters across 20 subscores, substantially increasing model dimensionality with limited additional information under sparse longitudinal endoscopic sampling. The current longitudinal disease modeling framework using the BI methodology provides a parsimonious and stable alternative, describing each SES-CD subscore through interpretable location (MEAN) and dispersion (SD) parameters while linking longitudinal change via a shared latent disease trajectory (Response). This structure aligns with the clinical objective of characterizing placebo-driven disease dynamics rather than estimating static item discrimination properties, while preserving interpretability and predictive performance.

Furthermore, in the present structural model the latent variable is not conceptualized as a static cross-sectional distribution of baseline disease severity, as in conventional IRT applications, but is instead defined dynamically through the shared mono-exponential longitudinal placebo response acting uniformly across all SES-CD subscores. Thus, the latent construct represents time-evolving mucosal disease activity driven by placebo response, providing a longitudinal disease progression framework rather than a static latent severity scale with marginal distribution. Patient latent trajectory (Response) serves as a quantitative indicator of general dynamic disease status at any given time point (Eq. 1), while the item-specific subscores provide its clinical manifestation across GI tract (Eq. 2).

The structural IRT model incorporated a mono-exponential placebo effect acting uniformly across all SES-CD subscores. A key advantage of the item-level IRT-BI structure is its ability to leverage sparse endoscopic data, which is a common limitation in inflammatory bowel disease trials given the invasive nature of endoscopy. By linking individual SES-CD subscores through a latent disease variable, the model enables reliable disease trajectory estimation even when subscore data are incomplete or unevenly distributed across timepoints. This structure also supports joint modeling of multiple correlated outcomes with an appropriate weighting of each subscore, enhancing the stability and interpretability of parameter estimates under typical trial constraints. Model’s predictivity confirmed by numerical and graphical diagnostics, demonstrates that the item-level IRT-BI framework accurately reproduces complex longitudinal patterns and segment-specific disease features, supporting its suitability for informing clinical development decisions in CD. Parameter estimation was successful across all subscores and segments, indicating that the item-level IRT-BI approach is well-suited to capture the complex, heterogeneous nature of CD. Predictive performance of the model for clinical endpoints was demonstrated both at individual-level (Fig. 1) and at population-level (Fig. 4) assessments, which showed good agreement between observed and predicted endoscopic response and remission. The model reliably reproduced observed clinical endpoints with minimal bias.

Visual predictive check of longitudinal SES-CD trajectories across intestinal segments further supported model predictive accuracy (Fig. 3). Observed mean trajectories for all 20 SES-CD subscores were within the 90% confidence interval of the model simulations across the entire 66-week of clinical trial. These results indicate that the model adequately captured both central tendency and variability in disease progression for each subscore, including ulcerated surface, size of ulcers, affected surface, and presence of narrowing across the ileum, colon, and rectum. The high concordance between observed and simulated data confirms that the model performs well in predicting both the aggregate disease activity and the subscore-level dynamics, thus supporting its robustness and generalizability in representing placebo response in CD. Furthermore, this establishes the model’s applicability for clinical trial evaluation, particularly in supporting interim decision-making and adaptive design strategies in Crohn’s disease.

The segment category probability curves in Fig. 2 provide a granular validation of the model’s structural integrity, going beyond population-level predictive checks. The close alignment between the observed probabilities (red lines) and the 90% prediction intervals (blue bands) across all score levels and intestinal segments confirms that the model adequately captures the relationship between the latent disease status and the probability of observing a specific endoscopic score. By successfully characterizing complex item-level behaviors, the model reinforces the suitability of the IRT-BI framework for analyzing composite clinical endpoints in Crohn’s disease.

Residual correlation diagnostics (Figure S3) indicated minimal structured dependencies among SES-CD subscores; therefore, a single latent variable structure was retained in the IRT model. This structural choice was based on clinical and methodological considerations. The SES-CD was designed and validated as a composite index of endoscopic disease activity, consistent with one laten variable adequately reflecting the intended clinical endpoint. Although few local correlations were observed (e.g., within ulceration domains), their overall magnitude was modest and did not justify the added complexity of introducing multiple latent variables within the model. Single latent structure also facilitates interpretability and aligns with current established practices in clinical and regulatory settings, where the total SES-CD score serves as the reference standard. Furthermore, parsimony was prioritized: multiple-latent-variable models may capture more fine-grained structure but at the cost of added model complexity, identifiability challenges, and reduced generalizability across studies. Therefore, a single-latent-variable IRT model was deemed to balance clinical interpretability, parsimony, and practical applicability for SES-CD analysis.

Covariate analysis conducted using the full random effects model (FREM) approach to evaluate the influence of baseline characteristics on the maximum placebo response (RMaxPL), mitigating selection bias by jointly estimating all covariate-parameter relationships. Elevated baseline CRP and fecal calprotectin were associated with a reduced placebo response, consistent with prior evidence linking systemic and mucosal inflammation to poorer clinical and endoscopic outcomes in CD [3, 19–22], which underscore the potential role of inflammatory burden in modulating nonspecific treatment effects. Prior anti-TNF exposure was associated with attenuated placebo response, whereas TNF-naïve patients exhibited higher RMaxPL values. This observation aligns with prior clinical trial data showing that biologic-experienced populations exhibit lower placebo and active treatment responses, likely reflecting more refractory disease phenotypes [23]. The item-level IRT-BI-FREM framework’s ability to identify these effects while remaining robust to multicollinearity strengthens confidence in these findings. Together, these results emphasize the importance of accounting for inflammatory biomarkers and treatment history when interpreting placebo responses.

Bounded integer (BI) models for rating and composite scale data, as well as IRT-informed strategies for analyzing total scores in the absence of item-level data, have been previously published as principled approaches for discrete outcomes [6, 24]. While these methods provide a robust likelihood framework [6] and improve inference when analyses are restricted to total composite scores [24], they do not explicitly model item-level disease dynamics or shared longitudinal trajectories across items. In contrast, the present work develops a full item-level IRT-BI disease model for the SES-CD, directly linking 20 endoscopic subscores across intestinal segments through a common latent placebo disease trajectory. This structure enables joint characterization of subscore-specific behavior and overall disease evolution, while preserving the bounded and discrete nature of the data. Additionally, integration with a full random effects model (FREM) allows simultaneous, multivariable assessment of baseline covariates influencing placebo response, supporting robust identification of clinically relevant factors affecting endoscopic response and remission endpoints.

Furthermore, modeling SES-CD at the item level, rather than modeling total SES-CD subscores, offers several advantages. First, estimating baseline disease severity separately for each item and segment, as done in the bounded‑integer subcomponent framework, explicitly captures heterogeneity across the gastrointestinal tract. Second, as previously discussed, modeling subscores accommodates incomplete endoscopic assessments. Missing SES-CD can underestimate mucosal disease and would require ad hoc imputation when using the total SES‑CD subscores. By contrast, the item‑level IRT-BI model naturally handles partially observed data, only the measured segment subscores contribute to the likelihood, avoiding exclusion of patients with incomplete evaluations and reducing bias due to missing segments. Assigning one interindividual variability (IIV) parameter per segment reflects the greater correlation of scores within a segment; this improves baseline estimation and allows borrowing information across correlated items. When a subscore is missing, the segment‑specific IIV helps recover the individual baseline and prevents undue influence from missing data. Third, despite this granularity, the model retains a coherent representation of overall disease activity. SES‑CD was designed as a sum score, and FDA guidance defines endoscopic response as a ≥ 50% reduction from baseline SES‑CD. In this framework, a shared latent placebo trajectory influences all subscores; simulation show that the resulting total SES-CD align closely with observed total SES‑CD trend. Collectively, capturing segment‑specific heterogeneity, accommodating missing subscores, leveraging segment‑specific IIVs for information borrowing, and preserving the overall disease trajectory support the methodological and clinical rationale for item‑level IRT-BI modeling over total‑score approaches in Crohn’s disease trials.

To our knowledge, this study represents the first application of an integrated item-level IRT-BI-FREM platform in Crohn’s disease, combining longitudinal disease modeling with a full random effects covariate framework. This approach enhances interpretability of covariate effects in a disease model and provides a scalable strategy for drug development through reliable prediction of patients’ endoscopic response and remission status. By characterizing variability in placebo response and identifying clinically meaningful covariates, this model provides valuable insights into disease behavior and supports model-informed drug development initiatives aligned with regulatory expectations. The FDA has emphasized the value of MIDD frameworks and their potential to guide evidence-based regulatory decision-making [25, 26]. According to the FDA’s MIDD Pilot Program and related guidelines, disease models play a pivotal role in patient enrichment strategies and late-phase trial design by identifying subpopulations more likely to benefit from therapy or exhibit consistent placebo responses [25]. By leveraging placebo data, the current platform CD disease model establishes a robust quantitative reference against which drug-related effects on clinical endpoints can be reliably assessed.

The dataset included rich item-level endoscopic assessments across all intestinal segments and longitudinal timepoints, enabling a robust characterization of disease dynamics within the studied population. Although derived from a single clinical trial, the depth and consistency of the data support the reliability of the model findings, while generalizability beyond this setting should be interpreted in context.

Conclusions

In summary, the developed platform disease model, based on Simple Endoscopic Score for Crohn’s disease, accurately quantitates the endoscopic response and endoscopic remission status of placebo patients with moderately-to-severely active CD and is applicable to supporting model-informed decision-making across clinical development. The integrated item-level IRT-BI-FREM analysis identified covariates influencing the magnitude of the placebo response. Elevated inflammatory burden, reflected by increased C-reactive protein and fecal calprotectin levels, and prior anti-TNF exposure, were associated with a reduced placebo effect. By establishing a robust baseline framework for placebo dynamics, this model can be leveraged to evaluate and compare the efficacy of emerging therapeutic interventions for Crohn’s disease and support regulatory evaluations.

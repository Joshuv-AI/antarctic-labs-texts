Different mechanisms of axitinib and diazepam antiseizure action in pentylenetetrazol-induced kindling model
Authors: Leonid S Godlevsky, Işınsu Alkan, Kıymet Kübra Tüfekci, Mykhailo P Pervak, Süleyman Kaplan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThis study intended to assess the histomorphology characteristics of hippocampal structures and determine the severity of seizures after treatment with the tyrosine kinase B inhibitor axitinib and diazepam in fully developed and postponed period in PTZ-kindled rats.Methods and materialsWistar rats were given PTZ for 21 days until fully developed convulsions were achieved. Two protocols were explored: assessment of seizures immediately after the completion of the kindling (early kindling) and after a two-week post-kindling PTZ-free period. Treatment with axitinib and diazepam was performed before early and postponed kindling seizures assessment, with the consequent collection of brains for histomorphology.ResultsAxitinib and diazepam effectively reduced seizure severity at the early and postponed periods of kindling. Axitinib's antiseizure effectiveness was reduced in the postponed stage of kindling period compared with the early one (P = 0.039), while diazepam's effectiveness was maintained at a similar level (P > 0.05). Stereological quantification of neuronal hippocampus changes revealed an increase in the total volume of the stratum radiatum, while a decrease of the dentate gyrus, in postponed compared with early kindling (P < 0.05). The positivity of collagen type IV, which is present in the blood-brain barrier, increased and was more pronounced in the postponed period in hippocampal structures (CA1-CA3).ConclusionThe antiseizure effect of tyrosine kinase B inhibition with a specific antagonist of VEGF axitinib is particularly pronounced in the early phase of PTZ kindling. Opposite to axitinib, diazepam demonstrated a similar antiseizure action in both periods of kindling. These results suggest a more pronounced contribution of angiogenesis compared with the neuronal degenerative hippocampal damage to the development of PTZ-kindled chronic epileptogenesis.Keywords: Tyrosine-kinase, PTZ-induce kindling, Diazepam, Dark neurons, Neurodegeneration, Angiogenesis

Introduction

Epilepsy is a neurological disorder characterized by seizures resulting in synchronized discharges characterized by abnormal functioning of neuron networks and uncontrollable neuronal excitation (Aronica and Mühlebner, 2017, Devinsky et al., 2018). The pathogenesis of epileptic syndrome develops along with the disturbances of neuronal functional unit (NFU), namely deterioration of neuronal function and breaking down the blood-brain barrier (BBB) (Rigau et al., 2007, Scharfman, 2014). Among others, remodeling of blood microcirculation based on angiogenesis with pericytes absence in newly developed capillaries makes a first-line contribution to increasing the permeability of BBB (Marchi and Lerner-Natoli, 2013). Glial cells are also significant players in controlling the ionic environment, astrocyte glutamate control, neuroinflammation, neurodegeneration, and oxidative stress-linked multiple actions (Heuser et al., 2021).

Brain structures demonstrate neuronal degeneration and apoptosis during chronic epilepsy development (Aronica and Mühlebner, 2017, Devinsky et al., 2018, Singh et al., 2021, Vezzani, 2020). Leakage of BBB due to angiogenesis contributes to neuronal damage with cytotoxic molecule penetrance (Patabendige and Janigro, 2023). Such deteriorations are responsible for the progressive epileptic manifestations, comorbidity, and resistance to antiepileptic treatment precipitation (Aronica and Mühlebner, 2017, Singh et al., 2021).

Löscher was the first to propose kindling as an epilepsy model resistant to treatment. Thus, neuronal loss in the hippocampus, especially in hilus, caused resistance to antiepileptic drugs as Löscher and colleagues established it on the model of amygdalar kindled rats (Löscher and White, 2023).

Concerning pharmacological (chemical) kindling, the apparent resistance of picrotoxin-induced kindled seizures to phenobarbital and phenytoin in a postponed (two-week) period free from epileptogenic administration was shown (Shandra et al., 1996). Postponed PTZ-kindling (14–28 days) (30.0 mg/kg, i.p.) was also characterized as a pharmacologically resistant model of epilepsy (Singh et al., 2021). Namely, a significant loss of neuronal cells along with nitrative/oxidative stress and peroxides production was established in PTZ-kindled brain Łukawski and Czuczwar, 2023, Thapliyal et al., 2023). The remarkable enhanced vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) expression in hippocampal tissue was also shown (Tawfik et al., 2018). Hence, angiogenesis as the next and vital step in a chain of chronic epilepsy events is strongly assumed.

Regulating BBB permeability is essential in preventing epileptic seizures. Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) is responsible for angiogenesis, and tyrosine–kinase promotes the synthesis of VEGF (Bhargava and Robinson, 2011, van Lanen et al., 2021). Antitumor drug activity is realized via inhibiting tyr-kinase restriction of angiogenesis and providing malignant cells with metabolites (Escudier and Gore, 2011, Rini et al., 2011, Scagliotti and Govindan, 2010). The antiseizure effectiveness of tyr-kinase inhibitors was also proved (Bender et al., 1999, Liu et al., 2014). Thus, in the pilocarpine rat model of mesial temporal lobe epilepsy, it was found that repeatedly limiting angiogenesis with sunitinib, a medication that penetrates the BBB and inhibits tyr-kinase, prevented the onset of seizures and hippocampus atrophy (Benini et al., 2016). Lapatinib - a tyrosine kinase inhibitor- also expressed antiseizure and neuroprotector activity (Jia et al., 2020).

Hence, dialogue between tyrosine kinase (tyr-kinase), a promoter of VEGF synthesis, and other components of NFU, such as glial cells, that significantly contribute to neuroimmune inflammation and myelin plasticity disturbances might also be significant for searching for new approaches to control neuronal excitability (Knowles et al., 2022, Lei et al., 2023, Verkhratsky and Butt, 2023).

Tyr-kinase was identified as a principal player in the development of kindled seizures, and inhibition of Tyr-kinase is recognized as a new approach for epileptic manifestation treatment even though few are known about the mechanisms of antiseizure activity (Liu et al., 2014). Thus, the role of Tyr-kinase in GABAA regulation is known (Perucca et al., 2023), and Tyr-kinase inhibitors suppress GABAA inhibition (Dunne et al., 1998). This effect is least expected in the context of searching their antiseizure mechanisms. Instead, angiogenesis–suppressive mechanisms of Tyr-kinase inhibitors, such as axitinib, a universal blocker of specific inhibitors of vascular endothelial growth factor receptor (VEGFR)-1, VEGFR-2, and VEGFR-3, might be contributive (Poshyvak et al., 2021).

Nevertheless, considering that VEGF demonstrates neuroprotection (Silva-Hucha et al., 2021) and causes prevalent inhibitory effects upon neuronal excitability and seizures (Nicoletti et al., 2010; Nikitidou et al., 2012), the question remains on the role of neuronal loss observed in the PTZ-kindled brain in the expression of the antiseizure effectiveness of axitinib.

That is why the present work aimed to evaluate the histomorphology characteristics of hippocampal structures and determine the severity of seizures after treatment with the tyrosine kinase B axitinib inhibitor in fully developed and postponed periods in PTZ-kindled rats. Considering that benzodiazepines cause antiseizure effects via GABAA receptors (Nicholson et al., 2018, Sabir et al., 2024), while inhibitors of Tyr-kinase inhibit them (Dunne et al., 1998) we have also studied antiseizure effectiveness of diazepam at both stages of PTZ-kindling.

Materials and methods

Animals

Fifty-four male Wistar rats weighing 180–220 g were involved in the experiments. Before experiment, the animals were acclimated to laboratory conditions for at least seven days. They were permanently housed in standard conditions with tap water, a standard feed, a 12-hour light/dark cycle, and a constant temperature of 23 °C and 60 % relative humidity. Planning recommendations from ARRIVE and the Basel declaration (http://www.basel-declaration.org), which includes the 3 R idea, have been considered at this stage of experimental work. Before the start of the experiment, the Odessa National Medical University Bioethics Committee (UBC) approved it (No3, March 14, 2018).

Kindled convulsions

Pentylenetetrazol (PTZ) kindling model of epilepsy were induced using the previously published method (Godlevsky et al., 2020). For 21 days, PTZ ("Sigma Aldrich") was administered intraperitoneally (i.p.) once a day at a dose of 35.0 mg/kg.

The following standards were used to assess the convulsions' severity: 0, no seizure symptoms; 1, a tremor in the face and distinct myoclonic jerks; 2, clonic convulsions affecting the entire body; 3, clonic convulsions involving the entire body with rearing; 4, generalized clonic-tonic convulsions involving the whole body with rearing and falling; and 5, repeated convulsions as at stage 4 or a fatal outcome following a seizure fit. These rats were removed for additional observations and chemical screening when they went into widespread fits in response to the 20th and 21st PTZ administrations.

Flowchart of methods.

Some animals experiencing seizures in stages 4 and 5 were not given treatment for two weeks after the kindling was finished (Shandra et al., 1996). The rats were given PTZ (35.0 mg/kg) once more fourteen days after the last kindling trial (21-t injection of PTZ), and the next day, the efficacy of axitinib and diazepam was assessed. Seizures were observed one at a time in separate Plexiglas enclosures for sixty minutes following PTZ injection.

Investigated compounds administration

Diazepam ("Calmpose," "Ranbaxy Diagnostics," India) was injected at a dose of 1.0 mg/kg, i.p., and axitinib ("Sigma Aldrich") was given orally (p.o.) in doses of 10.0 mg/kg (Fig. 1). Axitinib was dissolved in 5.0 % methylcellulose (Methocel, “Sigma Aldrich”) and diazepam – in 0.9 % saline and 5.0 % dextrose. During the final ten days of kindled PTZ injections, axitinib was given 60 min before PTZ, and convulsions were calculated following a 21-t PTZ injection during the initial kindling period. Also, axitinib was administered daily from the 5th till the 14th day of the two weeks free from PTZ in a postponed period of kindling. Diazepam was administered 30 min before PTZ was tested in early and postponed periods (Fig. 1). Control animals were treated with Methocel and with 0.9 % saline and 5.0 % dextrose.

Histopathological analyses

Brain tissues were removed for the histopathological evaluation. During the ten days, the samples were fixed with 10 % formaldehyde. Then, the tissues were processed by alcohol series (70–80–96–100 %) and xylene series and embedded in paraffin for light microscope analyses (LM). The sections were cut at 0.5μm and stained with cresyl violet. A camera attachment and a microscope were used to capture the tissue photographs. The analyses were performed using cellSens Entry software with the aid of a light microscope with camera attachment (Olympus, BX43, Center Valley, PA, USA). For the transmission electron microscopic analysis (TEM), samples were processed with graded acetone, propylene oxide, osmium tetroxide, acetone, propylene oxide, and Araldite and embedded in resin blocks. Following tissue processing, 70 nm sections were taken on 200 mesh copper grids and contrasted using uranyl acetate–lead citrate solutions for assessments under an electron microscope (JEOL JSM-7001F, JEOL Ltd., Tokyo, Japan). For histopathological assessments, consecutive 2 µm-thick sections were also put onto glass slides and stained with a 1 % toluidine blue and sodium borate mixture on a hot plate.

Immunohistochemical analysis

For immunohistochemical analyses, 5μm thickness sections were incubated in 3 % H2O2, washed with BSA, and the anti-Collagen IV antibodies were applied for 2 h. After that, the section was washed with PBS and incubated with biotinylated anti-rabbit IgG. Immunoreactivity was confirmed with a streptavidin-biotin complex package and visualized with diaminobenzidine tetrahydrochloride (DAPI). Areas were counter-stained with Mayer's hematoxylin.

Quantitative estimation of hippocampal stratum ratio

A point-counting grid was used to calculate the volume ratios of the hippocampal stratum. Using the ImageJ program, a 9000-pixel2 grid was applied to 16 sections taken from the hipocampal area of one animal from each group, and the points on the stratums were counted. The article published by Denizci et al. (2024) was taken as a reference in the classification of the stratum of the hippocampal area. The results were calculated by dividing the total number of points superimposing on the stratum for each stratum / by the total number of points corresponding to the total hippocampal area.

Statistical analysis

A nonparametric analysis of variance ANOVA (Kruskall-Wallis test) was used to examine the data on seizure severity, and One Way ANOVA followed by Tukey HSD test was used for quantitation analysis of histological data. p < 0.05 was accepted as the threshold for a statistical difference. When necessary, a Mann-Whitney test was then performed.

Results

Behavioral characteristics of the convulsions in kindled rats

From the third to the sixth injection, repeated intraperitoneal injections of PTZ (35.0 mg/kg) progressively developed seizure symptoms. After the 21-t PTZ treatment, most rats (two-thirds) showed signs of generalized clonic-tonic fits when kindling was completed. The rats that developed widespread seizures corresponded to 4–5 scored severity precipitated after each of the two last PTZ administrations were employed in subsequent studies.

Axitinib with diazepam's effects

Axitinib treatment (10.0 mg/kg, p.o.) performed at the early phase of kindling resulted in complete protection from generalized tonic-clonic seizures and a significant reduction of seizures compared to the control (H=10.988, P = 0.001) (Fig. 3, A). PTZ testing (35.0 mg/kg, i.p.) in a postponed period caused generalized seizures in all animals and with repeated character in most (6 out of 7) of them (Fig. 3, B). Seizure severity was higher when compared with fully developed kindling (H=4.116, P = 0.042). Treatment with axitinib prevented generalized tonic-clonic seizures in two out of six rats and significantly reduced seizure severity compared with the control (H=5.989, P = 0.014) (Fig. 3, B). At the same time, seizure severity in the postponed period exceeded such one in the early phase of kindling in axitinib-treated rats (H=4.275, P = 0.039).

Seizure severity in kindled rats treated with axitinib and diazepam. Notes: axitinib effects on fully developed (A) and postponed (B) seizures; diazepam effects on fully developed (C) and postponed (D) seizures. *-P < 0.05; **-P < 0.01; ***-P < 0.001 (ANOVA+Kruskall-Wallis).

Diazepam (1.0 mg/kg, i.p.) prevented generalized tonic-clonic seizures in 5 out of 6 rats and reduced seizure severity in fully developed kindling in comparison with the control (H=7.773, P < 0.005) (Fig. 3, C). Testing PTZ administration caused tonic-clonic seizures in all rats with repeated fits in 5 out of 6 animals in postponed periods (Fig. 3, D). Diazepam administration (1.0 mg/kg, p.o.) did not prevent generalized seizures in 2 out of 7 rats but reduced seizure severity compared with the control (H=6.647, P = 0.01) (Fig. 3, D). Seizure severity was not significantly different from those registered after diazepam treatment in the early period of kindling (H=1.114, P = 0.291).

Discussion

Hence, the data gained revealed that the antiseizure effectiveness of axitinib (ten days treatment) was observed at the establishment of fully developed kindled seizures. In contrast, analogous treatment during the postponed period was comparatively less effective. At the same time, therapy with diazepam was similarly effective at both kindling stages. Such a result was observed during the apparent progressive loss of neurons in hippocampal tissue during a period free from PTZ administrations.

The prevention of seizure precipitation caused by axitinib starting on the 12th day of the protocol of PTZ kindling induction is likely explained by the presence at this period of active VEGF-induced angiogenic mechanisms, which are effectively blocked with axitinib. Postponed periods and resistance to antiseizure treatment with axitinib are not VEGF-dependent, as angiogenesis forms a "resistant" vascular network. The role of angiogenesis in chronic kindled brain epileptization is supported by gained data on collagen IV expression in the post-kindled period. Thus, in immunohistochemical analysis, collagen type IV in the cell of the vessel wall and neurons was examined. Collagen type IV is one of the elements located in the basement membrane and the extracellular matrix of neurons and is a marker of newly created capillaries (Wareham et al., 2024). Thus, a study showed that neurovascular areas were affected in ischemic areas, and the amount of collagen type IV increased (Michalski et al., 2020). In the presented study, we observed that the positively stained areas in the brain tissues stained with collagen IV were more in the neuron and wall of the vessel in the post-group.

Diazepam demonstrated a not-significant diminishing of antiseizure effectiveness, favoring observed possible neurodegenerative dynamics significance for such tendency precipitation. It might be assumed that the reduction of benzodiazepine receptors in PTZ-kindling including hippocampal zones involved in epileptogenesis (Bazyan et al., 2001) results from neurodegeneration. Also, the drastic differences in the effectiveness of axitinib and diazepam prove the absence of the role of the GABA-ergic component in axitinib-induced antiseizure effects. This result aligns with the known GABA-A suppressive action of tyr-kinase antagonists (Dunne et al., 1998, Perucca et al., 2023).

Considering degeneration as a mechanism of chronic epilepsy, it is necessary to mention that Löscher’s group found that neuron loss in the hippocampus, particularly in the CA1, CA3c/CA4, and dentate hilus, in rats with prolonged amygdalar electrical stimulation is a characteristic feature of phenobarbital-resistant rats (Volk et al., 2006, Bethmann et al., 2008). The majority of responders to phenobarbital (90 %) were damaged.

VEGF's role as a neuroprotector disagrees with the proposed analysis of axitinib effects in PTZ-kindled seizure control. Thus, Silva-Hucha et al. (2021) emphasized motoneuron protection caused by VEGF, and the loss of VEGF neuroprotection is recognized as the pathogenesis of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis. On the other side, the different role of subtypes of VEGF receptors in neuroprotection was established (Cárdenas-Rivera et al., 2019). Thus, activation of VEGFR1 by the endogenous ligand promotes neuronal protection and restricts the volume of ischemic brain damage, while activation of VEGFR2 reduces this effect. That is why the resultant effect of axitinib is expected to be neuroprotective (Zhang et al., 2022), despite the mentioned role of VEGF as a neuroprotector (Silva-Hucha et al., 2021). Also, the suppression of microglia activation caused by axitinib might be contributive, including restoring BBB penetrance (Cárdenas-Rivera et al., 2019).

In the histopathological analysis, the hippocampal region of the subjects was examined using light microscopy, electron microscopy, and immunohistochemistry. In the data obtained, the fully developed kindled seizures were defined as early, and the chronic period was defined as post. While dark stained neurons were observed in the hippocampal area in both periods, their presence neuron deaths was higher in the post-period, and axon degeneration increased in the post-group. The observed decrease in the volume of the gyrus dentatus supports the hypothesis of hippocampal atrophy that occurs in kindled brain (Fournier et al., 2010). Our data favors that atrophy is going on during the postponed period in gyrus dentatus. Such a process is not observed in other investigated zones of the hippocampus, and in the stratum, radiatum even has an opposite character, which might be explained by their different and complex functions (Su et al., 2018). In addition to all these, it has been noticed that the cornu ammonia (CA) regions, where dark neuron deaths occur, showed a different impact on the cells in the various areas of CA. Necrotic conditions in the hippocampal region in PTZ-induced epilepsy models were examined with both light and electron microscopy, and neuron damage and deaths were reported. In the same study, the damage was more severe, especially in chronic epileptic hippocampal areas (Yardimoglu et al., 2007). Based on the light microscopic examinations, neuron deaths were noticed in different CA regions in the hippocampus of acute and chronic epileptic rats. While neuron deaths were in the CA3 region in the early (early stage of kindling) group, neuron degeneration was observed in the CA1 region in the post (postponed kindling) group. Our results are consistent with previous studies results (do Nascimento et al., 2012, Salazar and Tapia, 2015, Yardimoglu et al., 2007). The latest publication after (Rahimi-Tesiye et al., 2025) revealed a drastic increase in dark neurons in CA1 and CA3 in PTZ-kindled rats, which was 3–5 times higher than in non-epileptic rats.

The data and analysis of axitinib's effects suggest a potential role of VEGF and vessel remodeling in the development of PTZ-kindled seizures. The appearance of dark intensively stained neuros that occurred during the post-PTZ period, which was free from PTZ administrations, had a subtle effect on the antiseizure potency of diazepam but a more pronounced effect on axitinib-induced seizure suppression. This implies that a prolonged postkindled period - more than two weeks and increased dark neurons presence could potentially reduce or eliminate the antiseizure effectiveness of diazepam.

They gained data points to the similarity between electrostimulation amygdalar and PTZ kindling, as both critically depended upon Tyr-kinase activity. Besides, the data that was gained favors the principal possibility of using PTZ kindling as a model for testing axitinib and other inhibitors of Tyr-kinase upon seizures.

It is important to note that this work has its limitations. The question of whether the self-perpetuating mechanisms of chronic epilepsy, induced during fully kindled seizure precipitation, remain active during periods free from kindled epileptogenic impacts and contribute to further damaged progression as a basis of resistance precipitation requires further in-depth research. Limitations of work include the perspectives of stereological quantification of dark neurons, neurogenesis, and dendrite arborization in the hippocampal area, and comparison with the non-epileptic brain.

Conclusions

1.A period free from kindled PTZ administrations (two weeks) is followed by increased seizure susceptibility to kindled PTZ (35.0 mg/kg, i.p.) administration. Such increased seizure readiness is manifested with the deepening of histologically determined deteriorations of neuronal circuits in hippocampal structures and the increase of collagen IV immunoreactivity-marker of newly created capillaries. That favors active processes of neuronal deterioration and changes in microvessel penetrance during periods free from PTZ administrations.

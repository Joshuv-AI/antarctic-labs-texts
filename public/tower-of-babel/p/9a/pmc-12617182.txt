OA2087. Preliminary analysis of biohazard perception in trainees of an Italian University
Authors: L Di Giampaolo, P Borrelli, T Galanti
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundA crucial factor influencing workers’ safety behaviors is their perception of occupational hazards. Risk perception is the subjective awareness of the characteristics of specific risks such as the dangerousness, the severity of the potential damage and the probability of its occurrence. The evaluation of the risk perception by workers in the workplace is an important aspect to consider in obtaining maximum occupational safety.MethodsDuring the preventive health surveillance for the internship activities, the perception of biohazard was assessed by administering ad hoc questionnaire to university students enrolled (n = 442) in the first year of the degree courses in Medicine, Pharmacy and Health Professions. The relationships between variables were analysed by the Chi-square test and Kruskal Wallis, considering a level of significance p < 0.05.ResultsMedical students believe that the healthcare environment is related to biological risk (p < 0.001) and are aware that a healthcare worker can come into contact with HBV, HCV, HIV viruses (p < 0.001), to a greater extent than in other degree courses. While the blood-borne transmission method for the HBV virus is known to all students, the airborne spread of Mycobacterium Tuberculosis is known to a greater extent by medical students than in other courses (p < 0.001). Medical students are also more aware of the role of poor risk perception and failure to comply with the genesis of accidents in the healthcare sector (p < 0.001). With regard to the disposal procedure of a used syringe, all students demonstrate a low perception of risk: only 20% of medical students believe that the needle should not be re-capped after use.ConclusionsResults highlight the importance of assessing risk perception, before students start internship activities. Identifying areas of low awareness can guide the development of targeted educational interventions, aimed at promoting safe practices and preventing workplace accidents.Key messages• Risk perception assessment is needed before training interventions.• Low awareness can guide the development of targeted educational interventions.TopicRisk perception, trainees, biohazard.

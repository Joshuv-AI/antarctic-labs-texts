Current practices and trends of axillary surgery de-escalation and lymphedema management for breast cancer in China: a nationwide cross-sectional survey
Authors: Yue Zhou, Yongsheng Wang, Xuli Meng, Bingqiu Xiu, Benlong Yang, Yingying Zhang, Jingyan Xue, Zhi-Ming Shao, Jiajian Chen, Shuang Hao, Jiong Wu, Chinese Anti-Cancer Association; Committee of Breast Cancer Society
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAxillary management in breast cancer has evolved toward de-escalation to reduce complications, particularly breast cancer-related lymphedema (BCRL). This study aimed to assess current practices and trends of axillary surgery and BCRL management in China.MethodsA nationwide survey was conducted across hospitals performing ≥ 200 breast cancer surgeries annually in 2022. The questionnaire evaluated institutional characteristics, clinical practices of axillary surgery and BCRL care. Data were compared with those from the 2017 national survey involving 110 hospitals to assess temporal changes in hospital-reported practice patterns.ResultsThe 198 hospitals surveyed performed a total of 123,237 breast cancer surgeries in 2022. SLNB for cN0 patients was routine practice, with 59.6% of hospitals performing SLNB in > 90% of these cases, whereas the use of SLNB in patients with clinically suspicious axillary nodes and FNA-confirmed metastasis remained cautious. The omission of ALND for patients with limited SLN metastasis (1–2 positive SLNs) remained conservative, particularly for mastectomy cases. However, significant increases were observed compared with the 2017 survey (breast-conserving surgery: median 30.0% vs. 9.0%, P = 0.001; mastectomy: median 11.0% vs. 6.6%, P < 0.001). The timing of SLNB in patients receiving neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) shifted toward the post-NAC setting compared with 2017 (54.0% vs. 45.5%). Comprehensive assessment after NAC, including targeted axillary dissection (TAD) with dual-tracer mapping, was underutilized (23.7%). Hospital-reported BCRL care teams had been established in 52.0% of hospitals, while surgical BCRL interventions remained rare (11.6%).ConclusionCompared with the 2017 national survey, hospital-reported adoption of axillary de-escalation strategies in China was broader, although implementation remained cautious and uneven across clinical scenarios and institutional settings. As a hospital-level practice-mapping survey, this study describes implementation patterns rather than patient-level oncologic safety or efficacy. These findings suggest that tracer accessibility, evidence-based protocols, and multidisciplinary BCRL care may be important areas for future improvement.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12957-026-04466-5.

Introduction

Axillary management is a cornerstone of breast cancer treatment, providing essential information for staging, prognosis assessment, and locoregional control. Before the 1990s, axillary lymph node dissection (ALND) was the standard approach for axillary staging and treatment in patients with breast cancer [1]. However, the substantial morbidity associated with ALND, particularly breast cancer-related lymphedema (BCRL) [2–4], has prompted sustained efforts to reduce the extent of axillary surgery without compromising oncologic safety. Over the past three decades, axillary management has evolved markedly toward de-escalation. Sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB) represented a major milestone, allowing clinically node-negative (cN0) patients with negative sentinel lymph nodes (SLNs) to safely omit ALND, and this approach has become widely accepted in clinical practice [5–7]. Subsequent trials have further expanded the paradigm of axillary de-escalation. The ACOSOG Z0011 trial [8–10] demonstrated the safety of omitting ALND in selected cN0 patients with limited nodal involvement, defined as 1–2 positive SLNs, undergoing breast-conserving surgery followed by whole-breast irradiation. The AMAROS trial [11] showed that axillary radiotherapy could provide comparable regional control with less morbidity than ALND in patients with limited positive SLNs, while the SENOMAC trial [12] provided additional evidence supporting ALND omission in a broader population, including patients undergoing mastectomy. More recently, the SOUND [13] and INSEMA [14] trials have further challenged the necessity of routine SLNB in selected patients with small, cN0 early breast cancer. Meanwhile, the increasing use of neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) has introduced new challenges and opportunities in axillary management, particularly regarding the timing of SLNB and the assessment of nodal response after systemic treatment.

Although these advances have been incorporated into international [15–17] and Chinese guidelines [18], their implementation varies substantially across countries and regions because of differences in clinical practice patterns, healthcare resources, and access to surgical and pathological technologies [19–22]. A nationwide survey of 110 Chinese hospitals conducted in 2017 showed lower acceptance of axillary de-escalation strategies among Chinese clinicians than has been reported internationally. Given the emergence of more robust evidence, updated guideline recommendations, and expanded therapeutic resources in recent years, reassessment of the current status and evolving trends of axillary management in China is warranted.

In addition to oncologic outcomes, complications associated with axillary management remain a major concern. BCRL is one of the most common and burdensome sequelae of axillary surgery, reported to affect approximately 15–30% of patients after ALND and 5–10% of patients after SLNB, with substantial adverse effects on long-term quality of life [23]. As survivorship care has become an increasingly important component of global oncology practice, efforts to improve BCRL prevention, early detection, and multidisciplinary management have gained momentum. However, data on current BCRL care and surgical management practices in China remain limited.

To gain a comprehensive understanding of current practices and temporal changes in hospital-reported axillary surgery and BCRL care for breast cancer in China, we conducted a nationwide hospital-level survey across 198 hospitals in 2022 and compared the results with those from the 2017 national survey. This study aimed to characterize the implementation of axillary de-escalation strategies and BCRL management in contemporary Chinese clinical practice, thereby providing evidence to inform context-specific guideline implementation, resource planning, and professional education.

Methods

Study design and questionnaire development

This nationwide hospital-level survey was designed to assess current surgical practices for breast cancer treatment in China. The survey was conducted under the authorization of the Committee of Breast Cancer Society of the Chinese Anti-Cancer Association (CACA-CBCS) and the Chinese Society of Breast Surgery (CSBrS). The 2022 questionnaire was developed based on the 2017 survey instrument and was revised to incorporate emerging topics in axillary surgery for breast cancer and breast cancer-related lymphedema (BCRL) care. The revision process was informed by a comprehensive review of the literature, clinical guidelines, and expert consensus.

The original questionnaire comprised seven sections covering major aspects of breast cancer surgery: institutional characteristics, axillary lymph node surgery, breast-conserving surgery, breast reconstruction, endoscopy-assisted breast surgery, revisional breast surgery, and BCRL care and surgery. The present analysis focused on three components: institutional characteristics, axillary surgery practice patterns, and BCRL care and surgery. Specifically, the axillary surgery section included 9 questions, and the BCRL care and surgery section included 11 questions. Most items were single-choice questions or required respondents to provide numerical estimates or proportions reflecting their institutional practices.

Questionnaire validation

Content validity was assessed through iterative expert review. A pilot test was conducted in 10 geographically diverse tertiary hospitals to evaluate item clarity, feasibility, and completeness. The questionnaire was revised according to pilot feedback before nationwide distribution. Pilot feedback led to minor refinements in item wording and response options, without changing the core domains of the questionnaire. The pilot hospitals met the eligibility criteria and were among the 198 centers included in the final analysis. The final version relevant to this analysis is provided in Supplementary File S1.

Participants and survey methods

Hospitals in China performing at least 200 breast cancer surgeries annually were eligible. Using a purposive stratified sampling strategy based on geographic region and annual surgical volume, we identified 215 eligible hospitals to ensure broad geographic coverage and inclusion of experienced breast cancer centers. Each center appointed a specialist to complete the questionnaire with reference to institutional operative logs and annual departmental statistics when available. Responses were collected online.

Data collection was completed by September 2024, and 198 centers with valid responses were included in the analysis. Among them, 82 had also participated in the 2017 survey, representing 74.5% of the 110 hospitals surveyed in 2017.

Study outcomes

The outcomes were hospital-level indicators of axillary management and BCRL care. Main outcomes included hospital-reported SLNB use in cN0 patients and in patients with clinically suspicious axillary nodes and FNA-confirmed metastasis, ALND omission in cN0 patients with 1–2 positive SLNs, axillary management in the neoadjuvant setting, internal mammary SLNB use, and availability of BCRL care or surgical services. All outcomes were analyzed at the hospital level rather than the individual patient level.

Statistical analysis

Only completed questionnaires with internally consistent responses were considered valid. Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages, and continuous variables were summarized as medians with IQRs and ranges. Selected continuous variables were categorized into predefined intervals to ensure compatibility with the 2017 dataset. For univariate analyses, the Mann–Whitney U test, Kruskal–Wallis test, Kendall’s tau-b test, Fisher’s exact test, and logistic regression models were used as appropriate. For multivariable analyses, fractional logit models with a logit link were used for proportion-based outcomes, and multivariable logistic regression was used for binary institutional-level outcomes. A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Analyses were performed using SPSS version 19.0 and GraphPad Prism version 10.0.

Results

SLNB in cN0 patients

Sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB) was widely adopted for cN0 patients across the surveyed hospitals (Fig. 1A). In the 2017 survey, 77.27% of hospitals (85/110) reported performing SLNB in more than 50% of eligible cases, and this proportion remained similar in the 2022 survey (78.8%, 156/198). Notably, in 2022, 59.6% of hospitals (118/198) reported routinely performing axillary SLNB in more than 90% of eligible cN0 patients. Both univariate and multivariable analyses showed that hospitals with higher annual breast cancer surgical volume and higher breast-conserving surgery (BCS) rates were more likely to adopt SLNB for cN0 patients (Table 1).

Omission of ALND in cN0 patients with limited metastatic SLNs

For cN0 patients with 1–2 positive sentinel lymph nodes (SLNs), hospital-reported ALND omission increased significantly compared with the 2017 survey (Fig. 1C–D; BCS: median 30.0% vs. 9.0%, P = 0.001; mastectomy: median 11.0% vs. 6.6%, P < 0.001). Nevertheless, most hospitals remained cautious in clinical practice, particularly for patients undergoing mastectomy (Fig. 1C–D; BCS: median 30.0%; mastectomy: median 11.0%).

In the BCS setting, both univariate and multivariable analyses indicated that higher annual breast cancer surgical volume was independently associated with greater acceptance of ALND omission in BCS settings. In addition, specialized cancer hospitals and hospitals with more comprehensive tracer availability tended to report higher acceptance of ALND omission for cN0 patients with 1–2 positive SLNs in both BCS and mastectomy settings; however, these associations did not reach statistical significance in the adjusted multivariable models (Table 2).

IM-SLNB

Most hospitals did not perform internal mammary sentinel lymph node biopsy (IM-SLNB) (133/198, 67.2%). However, the reported adoption rate of IM-SLNB was higher in the 2022 survey than in the 2017 survey, increasing from 15.5% to 32.8% (χ² = 10.06, P = 0.002), suggesting increasing attention to internal mammary lymph node staging (Supplementary Fig. 1). After multivariable adjustment, hospitals with radiotracer access remained independently more likely to perform IM-SLNB than those without radiotracer access (65.8% vs. 25.0%; adjusted odds ratio [aOR] = 5.48, P < 0.001) (Supplementary Table 1).

SLNB tracers and intraoperative pathological diagnosis

SLNB tracers

For upfront surgery with SLNB, only 31.3% of hospitals (62/198) reported using dual-tracer mapping. Among these 62 hospitals, 50 used blue dye plus indocyanine green (ICG), 25 used blue dye plus radioisotope, and 13 reported using both combinations in clinical practice (Fig. 2). Intraoperative pathological evaluation of SLNs

Similar to the 2017 survey, nearly all hospitals in the 2022 survey routinely performed intraoperative pathological evaluation of SLNs (190/198, 96.0%). Frozen section analysis remained the predominant method (186/190, 97.9%), whereas touch imprint cytology, a simpler alternative, was adopted by only 4 institutions (2.1%).

Axillary management in patients receiving NAC

Timing of SLNB

The hospital-reported preferred timing of SLNB in patients receiving NAC differed between the two surveys, with the proportion of hospitals performing SLNB after NAC increasing from 45.5% in 2017 to 54.0% in the 2022 survey (Table 3). Exploratory univariate analysis indicated that specialized cancer hospitals and high-volume institutions tended to prefer post-NAC SLNB, although these associations were not statistically significant (Table 3).

SLN assessment after NAC

For patients with initially node-positive breast cancer who were downstaged to ycN0 after NAC, only 23.7% of hospitals (47/198) reported using targeted axillary dissection (TAD) combined with dual-tracer mapping (Table 3). In addition, 21.7% of hospitals (43/198) used dual-tracer mapping without TAD. More than half of the institutions (100/198, 50.5%) relied solely on single-tracer mapping with blue dye alone (Table 3). Notably, exploratory univariate analysis showed that high-volume centers had greater adoption of comprehensive nodal assessment strategies after NAC (Table 3).

BCRL incidence and management

Postoperative BCRL incidence

The median hospital-reported incidence of breast cancer-related lymphedema (BCRL) was 10%, and 17.7% of hospitals (35/198) reported BCRL rates > 25%. At the hospital level, higher reported adoption of SLNB for cN0 patients was associated with lower institution-reported BCRL incidence estimates (P = 0.018).

BCRL nursing team

Specialized lymphedema care teams had been established in 52.0% of hospitals (103/198). Among these hospitals, 91.3% (94/103) had teams comprising ≤ 5 certified rehabilitation nurses (Fig. 3A). Exploratory analysis revealed that higher surgical volume, higher BCS rates, and oncology-specialized status were associated with a greater likelihood of establishing a specialized lymphedema care team (Table 4).

Surgical interventions for BCRL

Only 11.6% of hospitals (23/198) reported performing surgical interventions for BCRL (Fig. 3C), and 73.9% of these hospitals (17/23) performed ≤ 25 cases in 2022 (Fig. 3D). Exploratory univariate analysis indicated that hospitals offering breast reconstruction had higher rates of BCRL surgical management (Table 4). For lymphatic mapping, ICG and blue dye were used at comparable frequencies, reported by 12 and 11 hospitals, respectively (Fig. 3E). Among hospitals performing lymphaticovenous anastomosis (LVA), half reported performing ≤ 3 anastomoses per case. For vascularized lymph node transfer (VLNT), the predominant donor site was the superficial iliac-inguinal lymph nodes (65.2%, 15/23), followed by the submental lymph nodes (34.8%, 8/23). The most common recipient sites were the ipsilateral axilla (52.2%, 12/23) and forearm (43.5%, 10/23), with only one institution selecting the ipsilateral wrist (Fig. 3F).

Discussion

This nationwide hospital-level survey provides a practice-mapping overview of axillary management and BCRL care in China, rather than direct evidence of the clinical safety or oncologic efficacy of de-escalation strategies. Overall, SLNB has become routine for clinically node-negative (cN0) breast cancer, whereas the adoption of further axillary de-escalation strategies has increased but remains cautious. Contemporary practices tended to be reported more often by higher-volume and specialized centers and by hospitals with greater tracer availability, which may be related to institutional experience and resource availability.

In the upfront-surgery setting, the routine use of SLNB for cN0 patients is consistent with landmark trials [5–7] and international and Chinese guidelines [15–18]. In contrast, many hospitals remained conservative for patients with clinically suspicious axillary nodes and FNA-confirmed metastasis. Although the Chinese guidelines [18] include SLNB as a possible option in this scenario, they emphasize careful patient selection and generally prefer NAC to optimize axillary management.

For cN0 patients with 1–2 positive SLNs, trials including ACOSOG Z0011 [8–10], IBCSG 23−01 [24], AMAROS [11], and SENOMAC [12, 25] support omitting completion ALND in appropriately selected patients. Because pivotal upfront-surgery trials predominantly enrolled hormone receptor-positive patients, the strongest direct evidence applies to luminal-like disease. In our survey, ALND omission increased significantly compared with the 2017 national survey but remained limited, especially after mastectomy, and was still substantially lower than the omission rates reported in some countries [19–21, 26]. This cautious uptake likely reflects concerns about trial generalizability, regional nodal irradiation planning, and multidisciplinary treatment pathways. Whether SENOMAC will accelerate adoption in China warrants further observation.

The conservative approach was also reflected in intraoperative pathology and tracer use. Most hospitals continued routine intraoperative SLN assessment, mainly frozen section, presumably to avoid a second operation when completion ALND was considered necessary. One-step nucleic acid amplification provides a standardized molecular alternative and is included in some guidelines [16, 18, 27], but its implementation in China remains limited because of cost, workflow, and training requirements. Only 31.3% of hospitals used dual-tracer mapping for upfront SLNB, and radioisotope use remained low. These findings suggest that resource disparity remains an important implementation barrier, particularly limited access to radiotracers, nuclear medicine facilities, and trained personnel in less-resourced or non-specialized hospitals.

In the neoadjuvant setting, SLNB timing shifted toward the post-NAC setting, with 54.0% of hospitals adopting this approach in 2022. For patients initially presenting with node-positive disease who were downstaged to ycN0, guidelines favor optimized staging with dual-tracer mapping, retrieval of at least three SLNs, and TAD [28–31]. However, only 23.7% of hospitals reported TAD combined with dual-tracer mapping, whereas approximately half relied on blue dye alone. This underutilization of TAD and dual-tracer mapping after NAC represents a key gap between guideline-preferred optimized staging and real-world implementation.

For internal mammary management, internal mammary nodal irradiation (IMNI) remains controversial because potential survival benefit must be balanced against cardiopulmonary toxicity [32–35]. IM-SLNB may provide pathological information to individualize IMNI selection [35–39]. In this survey, IM-SLNB adoption increased from 15.5% in 2017 to 32.8% in 2022; nevertheless, broader implementation remained constrained by technical complexity and radiotracer access, which was independently associated with IM-SLNB use after adjustment.

Recent SOUND [13] and INSEMA [14] trials further extend de-escalation by supporting SLNB omission in selected low-risk cN0 early breast cancer. The Chinese guidelines also permit omission in defined settings, such as completely excised DCIS, selected elderly patients whose adjuvant treatment would not be influenced by nodal status, and postmenopausal cT1N0, ER+/HER2 − patients undergoing breast-conserving surgery. Future surveys should evaluate real-world uptake of these emerging strategies in China.

BCRL remains an important survivorship issue and a key rationale for reducing axillary treatment when oncologically appropriate [40, 41]. The BCRL rates reported here are institution-level self-reported estimates, not standardized patient-level incidence rates, and may vary according to diagnostic criteria, surveillance intensity, measurement methods, and referral systems. Specialized lymphedema care teams existed in 52.0% of hospitals, whereas only 11.6% offered surgery, indicating growing but uneven development of multidisciplinary BCRL care.

Several limitations should be noted. First, all findings are hospital-reported practice patterns rather than patient-level data and may be affected by estimation, reporting, and recall bias. Second, differences between 2017 and 2022 should be interpreted as temporal changes in hospital-reported practice patterns rather than a strict longitudinal trend. Third, the questionnaire did not capture several clinical stratifiers, including tumor size, molecular subtype, and radiotherapy planning. Fourth, reported BCRL rates were institutional estimates rather than standardized patient-level incidence rates. Finally, the eligibility threshold of ≥ 200 breast cancer surgeries annually focused the survey on experienced centers and may limit generalizability to smaller and lower-volume hospitals.

Conclusion

Taken together, these findings provide a contemporary hospital-level overview of axillary management and BCRL care in China. Compared with the 2017 national survey, axillary de-escalation in China has progressed, as reflected by broader adoption of SLNB, increased hospital-reported acceptance of ALND omission in selected patients with limited SLN metastasis, and greater use of post-NAC axillary staging. Nevertheless, practice remains conservative for patients with FNA-confirmed nodal metastasis and for ALND omission after mastectomy, while optimized post-NAC axillary assessment using TAD and dual-tracer mapping remains underutilized. Multidisciplinary BCRL care has received increasing attention but remains unevenly developed.

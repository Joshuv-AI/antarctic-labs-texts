Investigation of the effectiveness of cooling spray on mammography comfort
Authors: Meryem Betos Koçak, Aykut Aytekin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBreast cancer is one of the most common cancer types in recent years. Early diagnosis of breast cancer increases the success of treatment. Breast cancer screening is thus highly critical. Mammography is a screening tool that is still globally valid. I would propose that Mammography can potentially be painful. In our study, we aimed to determine the level of pain experienced by using coolant spray before mammography imaging. Our study is a randomized double-blind prospective study. It was conducted on patients who agreed to participate in the study. In our study, coolant spray was applied to the case group and normal saline solution to the control group. After the procedure, data on experienced pain (using the VAS scale), comfort level, and the possibility of having a mammogram again were collected. The data obtained were analyzed in SPSS program. The number of patients admitted to the mammography unit during our study was 162. After applying the exclusion criteria, the study was completed with 136 patients. There was no statistical relationship between the placebo and coolant spray groups in terms of age, marital status, city/ provincial center-rural area of residence, educational status, employment status, smoking and menopausal status. The average VAS value experienced was lower in the coolant spray group than in the placebo group(4.82–6.84). The Coolant spray group had more positive responses to the question ‘I would have mammography screening again’. Both findings were statistically significant(p < 0.05). A significant amount of pain is felt during the mammography procedure and this causes serious discomfort. Although different studies have been conducted to reduce this pain level, this is the first study in the literature to investigate the reduction of pain during mammography imaging using coolant spray. The pain perceived in the coolant spray group was considerably lower than in the placebo group. The rate of subsequent visits for mammography was similarly higher in the coolant spray group. A national screening program needs to involve the majority of the population in order to be feasible. For more screening to take place, appropriate environment and conditions should be provided. The most important reservation of the patients is the feeling of pain. Pain, which is one of the most important obstacles to mammography, can be reduced by using coolant spray.Subject terms: Breast cancer, Health policy, Health services

Introduction

Global Cancer Statistics 2020 reports that breast cancer has replaced lung cancer as the most common type of cancer globally1. Breast cancer is the most common malignant tumor in women2. The exact etiological basis of breast cancer is unknown and the cause of it has not yet been fully elucidated3. Breast cancer is among the cancers with increasing incidence in younger age groups4. Early diagnosis is important in breast cancer, like other cancers. Rapid diagnosis of breast cancer at an early stage is the most reliable way to achieve a favorable prognosis. Breast conserving rate also increases with early diagnosis of breast cancer5. Studies show that breast cancer treatment is highly effective, especially in the early stages of the disease, with relative reductions in the mortality rate of 15% to 25% in randomized trials6.

The US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) recommends screening tests for people without obvious signs or symptoms to improve the health of people nationwide7. Mammography is used as a screening test for breast cancer. Mammography screening is one of the most widely used screening methods worldwide. Biennial screeners have tumours with less favourable prognostic characteristics than the annual ones8. In Turkey, there is a national screening program for breast cancer. Mammography is used for breast cancer screening in our country. Mammography screening is monitored by primary health care institutions in our country. All individuals enrolled in the family medicine system are invited for mammography screening at regular intervals. In our country, mammography screening is recommended every 2 years between the ages of 40–699.

Mammography provides soft tissue imaging using low dose radiation10. It is the primary approach used for breast cancer screening and detection worldwide. In Europe and the United States, it is widely used as an early detection method for breast cancer and has been shown to reduce mortality from breast cancer3,11. In a Swedish study with more than 20 years of patient follow-up, it was found that mammography screening in followed patients resulted in a 15% reduction in breast cancer mortality in women aged 40 to 74 years12. In particular, mammography screening was identified as having a benefit in women aged 40 to 49 years, significantly reducing the number of breast cancer deaths7,13.

In mammography, the breast can be evaluated in its anatomical integrity. Also, it is only mammography that can consistently reveal calcifications which may be associated with early breast cancer14. As seen, mammography is still the most important screening method for breast cancer today. However, despite its importance, people do not undergo mammography screening due to some reservations. One of these reservations is that it is an uncomfortable and painful procedure10,15,16. It has been previously reported that as mammography is a painful procedure, pain relief strategies should be developed to prevent women from avoiding it15,17.

Although there are publications in the literature10 using analgesics, self-compression technique, radiolucent pillow, music therapy, lidocaine gels to reduce pain in mammography imaging, there is no other article about using coolant spray to reduce pain in mammography imaging. In this respect, this is the first study in the literature.

In our study, we aimed to reduce the pain felt during mammography imaging without affecting the quality of the breast image. We sought to reduce the pain experienced during this procedure. In this study, we investigated the reduction of pain during imaging and obtaining a more comfortable mammogram by applying coolant spray to the breast just before the procedure.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

Our study is a prospective randomized controlled double-blind study. It was conducted on patients who applied to the mammography unit of Balıkesir Atatürk City Hospital. Patients who applied to the mammography unit for mammography screening between 15.01.2025–15.02.2025 were included in the study.

Our hospital is a 3rd level training and research hospital with a capacity of 1100 beds. It works as a central hospital in the region due to its location. In the mammography unit of our hospital, an average of 5–10 people are mammographed daily for breast cancer screening. Our study was conducted on a voluntary basis and a wet signed consent form was obtained from our patients participating in the study. The study forms were created by a physician and completed by the same physician through face-to-face interviews with the patients. Age, marital status, address (classified as city/provincial center or rural/village), number of previous mammograms (classified as none, 1,2,3 and 4 or more), education level (classified as high school or below, undergraduate or above), employment status, smoking status, menopausal status, pain felt during mammography (visual analog scale was used), the responses to the question “Would you like to have mammography again?” and mammography comfort information were recorded on the study form. All these data were obtained prospectively during mammography by recording them on a study form created separately for each patient. Patients were asked to rate their level of pain from mammographic compression using the VAS immediately after the procedure. The VAS consists of a horizontal line with a value between 0 and 10, with 0 indicating no discomfort and 10 indicating the greatest pain imaginable. The mammographic images obtained were checked by a radiologist for image quality degradation and it was determined that the application of coolant spray had no effect on mammographic image quality. The image quality was evaluated by the radiologist. this evaluation was made in terms of artifacts, image clarity, resolution, or visibility of calcifications. A digital mammography machine (M-113T, S/N 55666-P6, Fuji Film, 2017, Japan) was used for all mammographic images in the study with a compression force of 170 ± 10 N.

Procedure

The study data were obtained from the study form filled out separately for each patient. In our study, a physician familiar with the study was assigned to the mammography unit. The same physician accompanied all patients to the mammography department and explained the study. All procedures were performed by this physician. The same technician performed the mammography of the patients included in the study to avoid differences due to mammography. The study was explained to each patient who came for mammography screening. Patients who agreed to participate were included in the study. The randomization study coordinator randomized patients to one of the two treatment groups using a formal computer randomization protocol (www.random.org/integers). The randomization sequence in both groups was based on the order in which patients applied for mammography screening. Sprays were applied to both breasts. In the study, the case group underwent mammography imaging with coolant spray. In the control group (placebo), mammography imaging was performed by applying normal saline solution spray. The applied sprays were designed to have the same look when viewed from the outside. The study administrator applied the designated treatment protocol for each group. For the placebo group, normal saline solution was used in a bottle covered with white opaque paper and cooled at 4 °C. In the other group, the cooling spray (Cryos ®Spray, PhytoPerformance, Italy. It is a colorless gas composed of carbon and hydrogen, containing menthol crystals. It does not contain propellant that could damage the ozone layer.) was applied as recommended by the manufacturer (Spraying time is 5–10 s, at a distance of 20 cm from the breast tissue, if applied closer than 20 cm, frostbite or skin irritation may occur). The procedure was performed in the same way in both groups. The technician applying the spray did not know whether the contents were a cooling spray or a placebo. Mammography screening was commenced in both groups promptly after the application. Mammograms were performed on both breasts of each patient from both angles (mediolateral and craniocaudal). After mammography screening, both groups were asked to report the pain they felt during mammography on the VAS. In addition, they were asked whether they would like to have a repeat mammogram and the comfort of the mammography procedure. All data were thus obtained.

Our study was approved by the local ethics committee and was written in accordance with the Helsinki declaration (Balıkesir Atatürk City Hospital Ethics Committee, Date: 26.12.2024, Decision no: 2024/12/88).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 23 statistical analysis program (IBM). Data were presented as mean, standard deviation, median, minimum, maximum, percentage and number. The normal distribution of continuous variables was evaluated using Shapiro-Wilk and Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests. Independent sample t-test was used to compare normally distributed data between two independent groups and Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare non-normally distributed data. Categorical variables were compared using chi-square and Fisher’s exact tests. A p value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

During the study period, 162 patients applied to the mammography unit of our hospital for screening purposes. Among these, 4 patients were excluded from the study as they did not accept to participate in the study, 5 patients were excluded because they were found to have a mass/cyst in their breast, 2 patients were excluded for having a previous history of breast cancer, 10 patients were excluded for not being able to tolerate the screening procedure, 2 patients were excluded because of incorrect data recording, 1 patient was excluded for taking analgesics before the procedure, 1 patient was excluded for using estrogen, and 1 patient was excluded for breastfeeding. Our study was completed with 136 patients (68 case group, 68 control group). Flowchart is shown in Fig. 1. Images from 136 patients were evaluated by a radiologist for artifacts, image clarity, resolution, or visibility of calcifications, and all were deemed suitable for inclusion in the study. The 136 patients included in the study had a minimum age of 40 years and a maximum age of 69 years. The mean age was 56.18 ± 10.01 years. The mean age of the case group was 55.32 ± 10.33, while the mean age of the control group was 57.03 ± 9.67 and there was no statistically significant relationship between them (p > 0.05).

Flowchart of study.

Among the patients included in the study, 66.2% (n = 90) were married. Of the patients in our study, 101 (74.3%) lived in the city/provincial center. Of the patients who participated in our study, 22(16.2%) had not undergone mammography before, 13(9.6%) had previously had 1 mammogram, 20(14.7%) had received 2 mammograms before, 32(23.5%) had undergone 3 mammograms, and 49 had received 4 or more mammograms before. 40 (29.4%) of the patients in the study had high school education or less. 61.8% (n = 84) of the 136 patients were employed. 63 (46.8%) of the patients included in the study were smokers. 39% (n = 53) of the patients were in menopause. There was no statistical relationship between being married/single, living in the city/provincial center, previously undergoing mammography, education level, being employed, smoking and menopause status and the case and control groups.(p > 0.05).

In our study, the mean VAS value was 4.82 ± 1.86 in the coolant spray group and 6.84 ± 1.87 in the pacebo group, and this was statistically significant (p < 0.05). When asked the question “Would you have mammography again?”, 58 of the 68 patients in the coolant spray group answered “Yes, I would have mammography again”, while 42 of the 68 patients in the placebo group answered “Yes, I would have mammography again”. More patients in the coolant spray group answered that they would have mammography screening again, which was statistically significant (p < 0.05). A study was conducted on 26 patients who responded “No, I would not have mammography again,“16 patients stated that they did not want to have a mammogram again because it was a painful procedure, five because they were afraid of receiving a cancer diagnosis, and five because they were afraid of receiving radiation. When asked about the comfort of mammography imaging, 56 out of 68 patients in the coolant spray group responded as comfortable, while 31 out of 68 patients in the placebo group responded as comfortable. Mammography comfort was statistically significant in favor of coolant spray (p < 0.05). No cases of skin irritation or allergic reactions to coolant or normal saline solution sprays were observed.

Discussion

Avoiding mammography screening is a behavior that can put individuals’ health at serious risk. Therefore, it is important to understand people’s fears and concerns about mammography, and to develop effective strategies to eliminate this mammography avoidance behavior. In our study, pain during mammography screening was reduced with the application of coolant spray and mammography screening became more comfortable. Our study is a randomized double-blind prospective study and is the first study to use coolant spray to reduce the pain experienced during mammography screening.

The European Guidelines for Quality Assurance in Breast Cancer guideline recommends that at least 70–75% of the population should undergo regular mammography screening18, however, individuals tend to avoid it. One of the most significant reasons for this is the pain felt during the procedure. Apart from pain, other reasons for avoiding mammography screening include the possibility of false positive results (which would require many additional interventional tests), fear of being diagnosed with cancer, and radiation exposure7.

Breast compression is essential during mammography to reduce breast thickness and thus improve image quality and reduce the radiation dose to the breast19. The compression performed by the mammography device during mammography imaging causes severe pain. This pain can last for days. It has even been reported that subsequent mammography screening visits have been missed or delayed because of this pain20. A study of women who refused to return for a follow-up mammography evaluation after mammography screening found that the pain of the procedure was one of the reasons for refusal21. A 2013 systematic review revealed that 25–46% of patients who did not return for breast screening cited pain from mammography as a reason22.

In our study, more pain was reported in the placebo group. In the placebo group, we received more negative answers to the question “Would you come for mammography screening again?” But in the coolant spray group, there were more positive answers to the same question and this was statistically significant (p < 0.05).

In a prospective study by Karakaya et al., 66% of patients reported feeling pain of 4 points or more during mammography compression17. Ulus et al. concluded that 91% of women undergoing mammography procedures felt mild to moderate pain23. In a cohort of 200 patients participating in mammography screening in the United Kingdom, 49% reported moderate pain and 16% described severe pain24. In a prospective study of 2,164 patients, only 2% of the women in the study group reported not feeling any pain during the procedure15. These studies revealed that the mammography procedure causes severe pain. In our study, we found that the pain felt during this painful procedure was reduced by applying coolant spray.

In a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled study by Rahim et al.10, oral paracetamol was given to the case group before mammography. The control group was given placebo. As a result of the study, it was determined that the use of paracetamol before the mammography procedure was an effective intervention to reduce pain in women. In a study conducted by Nelson et al. with 60 participants, the participants were divided into 3 groups. Binaural music was applied to the first group, non binaural music to the second group and standard procedure to the third group. Pain tolerance was higher in the groups that were played music. Between the first and second group, the tolerance of the binaural music group was higher25. When breast compression is left to the patient’s control, the patient feels less pain, but with this the image quality deteriorates26, which unfortunately has not been a suitable solution. Freitas et al. reported that 90% of patients in their study experienced discomfort, of which 12% rated it as intense or unbearable15. Women are less willing to undergo this procedure because of the discomfort of mammography27. One of the requirements for a successful breast cancer screening program is the optimal quality of mammogram images. The image quality of mammograms should meet standards. A painful mammography procedure may also affect mammography image quality28. We think that better images can be obtained from patients with less pain.

It is the responsibility of health care providers like us to work on procedures that can increase compliance with screening programs. Interventions to reduce mammography-related pain levels are important to encourage community participation in mammography screening. This is what we aimed to demonstrate in our study, and we found that the coolant spray group experienced less pain than the placebo group, and the number of positive responses to the question “Would you come for a follow-up mammography screening” increased in the coolant spray group. By using coolant spray, mammography screening rates can be increased in the society.

Conclusion

This is a study on the pain felt during mammography. Many people avoid mammography screenings due to this pain. The use of coolant spray can help mitigate avoidance of mammography due to pain and may potentially improve screening participation rates. Coolant spray can be used to reduce pain during mammography.

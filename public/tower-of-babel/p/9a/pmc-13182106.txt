Determinants and cost composition of hospitalization for EGVB in decompensated cirrhosis
Authors: Yulan Song, Rongwei Ren, Ruxuan Li, Mingyang Zou, Yixin Pan, Jiebin Pan, Changpeng Chai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEsophagogastric variceal bleeding (EGVB) is a life-threatening complication of decompensated cirrhosis that imposes a substantial inpatient economic burden. However, the specific determinants and cost composition of EGVB hospitalization remain poorly defined in China.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 122 patients admitted with EGVB between April 2019 and July 2023 at the First Hospital of Lanzhou University. Demographic, clinical, and itemized cost data were retrieved from electronic records. Total hospitalization cost was log-transformed and examined by stepwise multiple linear regression to identify independent predictors. K-means clustering was used to explore patterns in cost composition.ResultsThe median cost was CNY 34,744. Surgical type, length of stay, and insurance category were the strongest predictors of total cost (R² = 0.81, P < 0.01). Western medication and procedural expenses constituted the largest cost components, while hospital duration and discharge status had smaller effects.ConclusionsIn patients with EGVB secondary to decompensated cirrhosis, surgical approach and insurance coverage drive inpatient expenditure. Optimizing clinical pathways and financial policies could reduce the direct medical burden and enhance value-based liver care in China.Keywords: Liver cirrhosis, Esophageal and gastric variceal bleeding, Multiple linear regression, K-means clustering, Economic burden

Introduction

Liver cirrhosis constitutes the terminal stage of chronic hepatic injury and, once decompensation occurs, is frequently complicated by portal-hypertension–related sequelae such as upper gastrointestinal hemorrhage [1]. Among these, esophageal and gastric variceal bleeding (EGVB) remains a life-threatening emergency that demands rapid control of bleeding and robust secondary prevention. In real-world practice, stepwise management combines vasoactive therapy, endoscopic hemostasis (band ligation, sclerotherapy, cyanoacrylate), and—when necessary—portal-decompressive procedures such as transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS) to prevent rebleeding. Initial care includes rapid resuscitation, airway protection as indicated, restrictive transfusion (target hemoglobin 7–8 g/dL), and prophylactic antibiotics. Early endoscopy (within ~ 12 h) provides definitive control; persistent bleeding warrants bridge measures—balloon tamponade or a fully covered esophageal stent—while definitive decompression is arranged. For secondary prevention, clinicians pair a nonselective β-blocker with scheduled repeat ligation [2, 3]. Yet these clinically necessary strategies require specialized personnel, high-cost pharmaceuticals and devices, blood products, and often prolonged hospitalization—making care resource-intensive and driving substantial direct hospital costs for patients and families [4].

In the Chinese setting, the economic burden associated with EGVB is particularly salient. Patients often face expenditures that rival or exceed local disposable income, with cost pressures shaped by procedure selection, intensity of supportive care, and pharmaceutical use during hospitalization [5]. Prior observations in tertiary centers suggest that Western pharmaceuticals and procedure-related items dominate cost composition [6], whereas length of stay and discharge status, although contributory, exert comparatively smaller direct effects on the total bill—patterns that point to specific, high-leverage targets for cost containment and policy design.

At the same time, regional contexts—such as the high prevalence of hepatitis-related cirrhosis and patterns of labor migration—shape both clinical presentation and affordability. This underscores the need for granular, patient-level analyses that clarify (i) who bears higher costs (sociodemographic profiles), (ii) which components drive those costs (specific categories), and (iii) how care pathways and clinical trajectories contribute to spending. A rigorous framework should therefore quantify total direct inpatient costs and their determinants and, crucially, map the internal cost structure across medications, procedures/interventions, diagnostics, and consumables to guide practical decisions for hospitals and payers.

To address these gaps, we apply multiple linear regression (MLR) to identify independent determinants of total inpatient expenditure among patients hospitalized for EGVB and use K-means clustering to delineate latent cost phenotypes across treatment pathways, focusing on the relative contributions of pharmaceuticals, procedures/interventions, diagnostics, and consumables. By integrating regression-based inference with unsupervised pattern discovery, we aim to generate actionable recommendations—including optimization of in-hospital processes to reduce non-therapeutic days, prioritization of coverage for high-value drugs and devices, and alignment of discharge planning with evidence-based secondary prophylaxis—to mitigate the direct economic burden borne by patients with EGVB.

Patients and methods

Study population and data source

All patients were recruited from the First Hospital of Lanzhou University. Based on the 11th Revision of the International Classification of Diseases (ICD-11), cases diagnosed as “hemorrhage from esophagus and gastric varices caused by decompensated liver cirrhosis” (ICD-11 code B16.2) between April 2019 and July 2023 were retrieved from the hospital’s electronic medical record system. A total of 215 patients were screened, among whom 93 cases were excluded due to repeated hospitalization (n = 15) or hospitalization duration of less than 1 day (n = 78). Finally, 122 patients who were admitted for the first time with EGVB were included.Records with missing or incorrect data and those with a hospital stay of less than one day were excluded. All patient data were anonymized prior to analysis, and the study protocol received ethical approval from the Medical Ethics Committee of the First Hospital of Lanzhou University.

Data collection

The following variables were extracted: (i) Sociodemographic data: age, sex, occupation, history of hypertension, history of diabetes, and infectious-disease exposure. (ii) Clinical data: admission and discharge time, number of hospitalizations, primary diagnosis, and surgical/procedural name. (iii) Economic data: total hospitalization cost, comprehensive medical service cost, diagnostic cost, therapeutic cost, rehabilitation cost, Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) cost, Western medication cost, Chinese herbal medication cost, blood and blood-product cost, consumable cost, and other related expenditures. All data underwent reliability checks before statistical analysis.The cost of Western medicine includes vasoactive drugs (terlipressin), antibiotics (such as ceftriaxone), human albumin and drugs for preventing rebleeding (such as carvedilol).

Statistical methodology

Overview

The analytical framework consisted of descriptive statistics, univariate regression, MLR, and K-means clustering. Analyses were conducted using SPSS version 27.0 and OriginLab 2021. Continuous variables following a normal or near-normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x̄ ± s), skewed data as median (P25, P75), and categorical variables as frequencies or percentages. In order to meet normality assumptions, both length of stay and medical cost variables were log-transformed (lg) prior to analysis.

MLR analysis

MLR is a standard statistical method used to evaluate the quantitative dependence of one dependent variable on multiple independent variables. When the exclusion of an independent variable Xi from the equation reduces the regression sum of squares by Pi, Pi represents the partial regression sum of squares for Xi, which is subjected to a significance test [7]. When several predictors (x₁, x₂, x₃ … xp) jointly influence a response variable Y, a multiple regression model can be built to describe this relationship. If the selected functional form is linear, the model is defined as a MLR [7]. After the model is established, each predictor’s linear influence on the dependent variable is evaluated to determine the relative importance of each factor [8].

In this study, MLR was used to examine the direct and indirect relationships between various factors and the economic burden of EGVB patients and to compare the relative weights of influencing variables. The resulting model served as the basis for proposing strategies to reduce total hospitalization expenditure.

The dependent variable was log-transformed total hospitalization cost, and the independent variables included age, sex, occupation, hypertension, diabetes, surgical type, medical payment category, length of stay, and treatment outcome. Categorical predictors were dummy-coded. A two-step procedure was adopted: univariate linear regression with a significance level of α₁ = 0.10 to screen candidate variables, followed by stepwise MLR with α₂ = 0.05. Model adequacy was assessed by the F-statistic, coefficient of determination (R²), normality of standardized residuals (histogram and P–P plot), and Pearson correlation analysis. Autocorrelation was examined with the Durbin–Watson statistic, and multicollinearity was assessed using variance inflation factors (VIF < 5).

Cluster analysis

Cluster analysis is a classical machine-learning approach used to group similar objects into homogeneous subsets based on their attributes [9]. The K-means algorithm, a partition-based and distance-driven clustering method, assumes that the smaller the distance between objects, the higher their similarity [10]. It divides a dataset into k clusters (where k is predefined) such that the distance from each observation to its own cluster center is minimized compared with distances to other centers.

In this study, K-means clustering was employed to compare total and sub-category medical costs among patients and to examine their associations with surgical types. This approach allowed exploration of heterogeneity in cost composition and identification of patterns contributing to the financial burden of EGVB treatment.

Analyses were performed in OriginLab 2021 (Cluster App). Data were standardized both by cost category and by patient sample to enable within-patient and between-patient comparisons. The Manhattan distance metric was used for standardized data and the Euclidean distance for unstandardized data, improving resolution and contrast in the resulting heat-map cluster diagrams. Patient cases were ordered by surgical procedure prior to clustering for better interpretability.

Statistical significance

All tests were two-sided, with P < 0.05 considered statistically significant for multiple analysis. Results from regression and clustering were combined to determine key cost drivers and to describe latent cost-composition patterns among EGVB patients.

The mean length of hospital stay was 16.5 days (median 15 days). In terms of occupation, the majority were farmers (63.7%) and self-employed workers (30.7%). Cluster analysis of total medical expenses and their component costs

The core concept of clustering is to group objects with shared characteristics into clusters. Unlike classification, clustering does not require predefined grouping criteria. Instead, it relies on assessing and fitting similarities among objects to aggregate those with common features. As no training dataset is needed, clustering is regarded as an unsupervised learning method [9].

In this study, cluster analysis was performed on the total medical expenses of all patients and their component costs, including Western medicine costs, general medical service fees, diagnostic expenses, and rehabilitation-related costs. The aim of clustering was to identify patterns of patients’ economic burden and to classify patients accordingly, thereby exploring the relationship between the resulting clusters and the independent variables analyzed in the previous sections.

Therefore, the K-means clustering algorithm was adopted, using “distance”-based similarity evaluation determined by the Manhattan distance between each object and the cluster center. All data were standardized by rows and columns in OriginLab 2021 before clustering. The K-means algorithm was then applied to perform clustering and visualization, and the results are presented in Figs. 3 and 4.

Discussion

Overview of key findings and clinical implications

This study provides an integrated economic and clinical assessment of EGVB in patients with decompensated cirrhosis from Northwest China. The median inpatient expenditure was CNY 34,744, substantially exceeding the regional disposable income level. Surgical type, length of stay, and insurance category were the most influential independent determinants of total cost (R² = 0.81). Age, diabetes, occupation, and treatment outcome also had secondary yet measurable effects. Cluster analysis showed that Western pharmaceuticals and procedural costs dominated overall expenditure, indicating specific targets for financial optimization.

These findings are consistent with major international guidelines, including Baveno VII, AASLD 2024, and ESGE 2025, which emphasize early vasoactive therapy, restrictive transfusion, urgent endoscopy, and timely pre-emptive TIPS for high-risk patients [1, 2, 11, 12]. The relationship between treatment intensity and economic burden is evident: advanced procedures such as TIPS consume more resources but can prevent costly rebleeding episodes and ICU admissions. In summary, the primary economic “drivers” closely mirror the clinical “drivers” of disease severity.

Determinants of cost and potential targets for optimization

Transfusion strategy. The landmark randomized controlled trial (RCT) by Villanueva et al. demonstrated that a restrictive transfusion threshold (hemoglobin ≈ 7 g/dL) improves survival and reduces rebleeding while decreasing blood-product utilization [11]. Adopting this practice can yield both clinical and economic benefits through more rational use of resources.

Antibiotic prophylaxis. Prophylactic antibiotics remain essential, lowering infection-related mortality in cirrhotic bleeding according to Cochrane evidence [13]. However, recent reviews recommend shorter, risk-stratified courses tailored to local resistance profiles [14]. This targeted approach preserves antimicrobial efficacy while minimizing unnecessary drug costs.

Endoscopic and secondary prevention. For esophageal varices, endoscopic variceal ligation (EVL) remains first-line therapy, whereas cyanoacrylate injection, BRTO, or TIPS is preferred for gastric varices [3, 15–17]. For secondary prevention, combination therapy with a non-selective β-blocker (NSBB) plus EVL is standard. Evidence increasingly favors carvedilol due to greater portal-pressure reduction and the potential to lower rebleeding-related expenditures [18].

Early TIPS in high-risk patients. Multiple trials and meta-analyses confirm that early or pre-emptive TIPS in appropriately selected patients (Child-Pugh C10–13 or Child-Pugh B with active bleeding) significantly reduces treatment failure and mortality [12, 19]. Although initial costs are higher, economic evaluations from Europe show long-term savings through fewer rebleeds, shorter stays, and fewer readmissions [20, 21].

Rescue and bridging therapy. For refractory bleeding, self-expanding metallic stents (SEMS) achieve superior hemostasis and fewer complications than balloon tamponade, often achieving cost neutrality by reducing ICU utilization [22, 23].

Health system and policy implications

The data of this study covers the period from 2019 to 2023, which coincides with the crucial period of national centralized procurement (VBP) and DRG/DIP reforms. However, due to the limitation of sample size, the dynamic impact of the policies on costs was not analyzed.The heavy cost burden in this cohort—comprising mainly farmers and informal workers—carries major socioeconomic implications. A single hospitalization for EGVB often meets the WHO definition of catastrophic health expenditure (out-of-pocket ≥ 40% of household capacity-to-pay) [24]. Stronger financial protection must therefore accompany clinical improvement. Expanded insurance coverage for high-value interventions—including vasoactive drugs, EVL kits, SEMS, and covered TIPS—would promote both equity and efficiency.

At the national level, volume-based procurement (VBP) has reduced the cost of essential drugs and devices, while DRG/DIP-based payment systems encourage efficiency and guideline-concordant care [25–27]. These mechanisms incentivize shorter non-therapeutic stays, standardized pathways, and fewer readmissions—aligning well with our identified cost drivers. In parallel, China’s HBV vaccination program has markedly reduced HBsAg prevalence to < 1% in children, suggesting that the incidence and economic burden of EGVB will continue to decline in the coming decades [28].

Operationally, hospitals can act immediately by: (i) streamlining admission-to-endoscopy intervals, (ii) embedding restrictive transfusion and antibiotic protocols into electronic order sets, and (iii) ensuring early TIPS availability within the same admission. Monitoring 30- and 90-day rebleeding and readmission rates can link clinical performance to cost efficiency.

Methodological considerations and future directions

This study integrates multiple regression and K-means clustering to distinguish cost determinants from cost composition, offering both statistical and structural insight. Despite these strengths, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, potential endogeneity exists between disease severity and surgical selection; applying propensity-score weighting or instrumental-variable analysis could strengthen causal inference. Second, hospital cost data are inherently right-skewed; future studies should test generalized linear models (Gamma with log link) or two-part models with Duan’s smearing retransformation to ensure unbiased estimates [29, 30]. Third, cluster stability and external validity require multicenter replication. Finally, inclusion of post-discharge data—such as secondary-prevention adherence, rebleeding, and quality-adjusted life years—will enable comprehensive cost-effectiveness analyses under China’s evolving DRG/DIP framework [20, 21, 26].

In short, this study has the following limitations:

The single-center small sample size (n = 122) limits the generalizability of the results and requires multi-center validation;

The retrospective design leads to endogenous bias: surgical choices (such as TIPS) are related to the severity of the disease (such as not obtaining the Child-Pugh score), which may confound the relationship between the type of surgery and cost;

The cost model does not use GLM: due to the small sample size and the good fit of the original model (R²=0.81), but future studies should adopt Gamma-distribution GLM;

Only the direct costs of the hospital were analyzed, without including patient out-of-pocket expenses, lost wages, and other indirect costs.

Conclusion

In summary, this study demonstrates that surgical type, length of stay, and insurance coverage are the principal determinants of direct inpatient costs for EGVB in decompensated cirrhosis, while demographic and comorbidity factors exert secondary effects. Western pharmaceuticals and procedural expenses contribute most to total costs, emphasizing the need for pathway optimization and rational resource allocation. Implementation of restrictive transfusion, timely endoscopic therapy, selective antibiotic use, and early TIPS in high-risk patients can improve outcomes while reducing avoidable expenditures. At the system level, expanding financial protection for high-value interventions and aligning VBP and DRG/DIP policies with evidence-based care are essential for cost containment and equity. Ultimately, optimizing EGVB management requires balancing clinical efficacy and economic sustainability, advancing high-value and equitable liver care for patients with decompensated cirrhosis.

Assessment of radiation dermatitis in locally advanced head and neck cancer patients treated with concurrent chemoradiotherapy using LENT-SOMA scale: hematological indicators-based nomogram model
Authors: Yunhao Chen, Yang Zhang, Xiaowen Wang, Yafang Hong, Xia He, Zihan Zhou, Xingchen Ding
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDue to high sensitivity of the skin on the head and neck to radiation, patients with head and neck cancer (HNC) are more susceptible to developing radiation dermatitis (RD). It is important to predict the severity of RD in patients with HNC after radiotherapy. The aim of this research is to develop a nomogram model to predict the RD with a subjective and objective combined scale.MethodsDuring the period from May 24, 2020 to December 13, 2023, HNC patients with intensity-modulated radiotherapy (IMRT) combined concurrent chemotherapy were recruited. The main outcomes considered were scores on the Late Effects Normal Tissue Task Force-Subjective, Objective, Management, Analytic (LENT-SOMA). And the correlation among early LENT-SOMA score and scores of other scales for acute RD was assessed. A total of 257 patients were divided into the training and validation set (7: 3). Independent risk factors were identified using both single-variable and multiple-variable logistic regression analyses. A nomogram was subsequently developed using the factors that were significantly different in the multivariate logistic regression model (P<0.05). The nomogram’s predictive perform and clinical practicality was evaluated by receiver operating characteristics (ROC) curves, calibration curves and decision curve analysis (DCA).ResultsCorrelation analysis indicated that the LENT-SOMA early score was correlated with Radiation Therapy Oncology Group (RTOG), Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), World Health Organization (WHO), and Oncology Nursing Society (ONS) criteria. Multivariate logistic regression indicated that higher lymphocyte (Lym), blood glucose (BG), lower fibrinogen (FB), and albumin (ALB) before radiotherapy were independent prognosticators of LENT-SOMA score. A visible nomogram was built by integrating the Lym, BG, FB, and ALB before radiotherapy. The area under the curve (AUC) values of the nomogram in training cohort and validation cohort showed good power for the discrimination of 2 + grade RD. Using calibration curves and DCA, the results were confirmed.ConclusionThe LENT-SOMA scale exhibits potential for assessing acute RD. Higher Lym, BG, lower FB, and ALB before radiotherapy were independent prognosticators of LENT-SOMA score. We built a visible nomogram that showed good power for the discrimination of 2 + grade RD.

Introduction

Head and neck cancer (HNC) has risen to become one of the extremely common forms of cancer, presenting a considerable challenge in clinical practice, which encompasses nasopharyngeal, oropharyngeal cancer, hypopharyngeal carcinoma, laryngeal carcinoma, etc [1, 2]. The primary treatment options for early-stage HNC include surgery or radical radiotherapy. Certain tumor subtypes, particularly HPV-associated oropharyngeal carcinoma, demonstrate enhanced radiosensitivity and are associated with more favorable prognosis [3]. Notably, more than half of the patients with HNC are diagnosed at a locally advanced (LA) stage [4]. The standard of care for LA HNC treatment is concurrent chemoradiotherapy (CCRT) with curative intent [5, 6].

Although this approach can enhance clinical outcomes, it increases the incidence of radiation-related toxicities, such as radiation dermatitis (RD) and osteoradionecrosis [1, 7–12]. Acute effects include dermatitis, desquamation, edema, pain, and discomfort etc [13–15]. During the late phase, it can cause pigmentation, fibrosis, and telangiectasia [16–18]. Incidence of RD can negatively affect treatment completion and survival rates, as well as result in a deterioration of quality of life for patients [19].

An accurate prediction of the severity of RD will make it possible to help prevent the occurrence of RD during or after radiotherapy, and is of great significance to realize individualized treatment and precision medicine. Hematologic indicators are readily available and stable [20]. Previous literature reported hematological indicators are closely related to prognosis of various types of cancer [21–23]. Notably, Xie et al. have reported that hematological indicators can forecast the intensity of RD in individuals with breast cancer [24].

Various toxicity grading systems such as Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), criteria for Radiation Therapy Oncology Group (RTOG), World Health Organization (WHO) standards, and Oncology Nursing Society (ONS) guideline, have been widely used in both research and clinical practice [25, 26]. These toxicity measures have mainly been designed for use by observers. Nonetheless, it was discovered that observer-reported toxicity lacked both sensitivity and specificity when it came to subjective symptoms [27]. The evaluation of symptoms by patients themselves was deemed extremely valuable [28, 29]. Therefore, it is important to use comprehensive scales of subjective and objective. Of these, the most representative scale, combining subjective and objective, is Late Effects of Normal Tissues (LENT)–Subjective, Objective, Management, and Analytic (SOMA) scale [30]. Moreover, according to a comparison of late toxicities in the GORTEC 94 − 01 trial, the LENT-SOMA scales were more accurate than the NCI/CTC and EORTC/RTOG systems [31]. Notably, subjective symptoms in LENT-SOMA scale, including scaliness/roughness and sensation, could appear within 3–6 months post-radiotherapy in clinical setting. The LENT-SOMA scale may also have potential utility in assessing acute RD.

In this study, we followed up patients with HNC after radiotherapy by LENT-SOMA scale including subject-objective scores, collected the clinical features and hematological parameters, analyzed the risk factors, and then built a model to predict the severity of RD in patients with HNC after CCRT, so as to give appropriate intervention measures, reduce the impact of related adverse reactions on patients, improve patient compliance with treatment, thereby enhancing treatment outcomes for patients.

Methods

Patients

HNC patients with intensity-modulated radiotherapy (IMRT), were recruited from Shandong cancer hospital from May 24, 2020 to December 13, 2023. All participants involved in the research provided their written informed consent. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Shandong Cancer Hospital and Institute (approval number SDTHEC2024004013). The eligibility requirements were set in advance: (1) age 18 or older; (2) Karnofsky performance score (KPS) of 70 or above; (3) new diagnosed with HNC of the nasopharynx, oropharynx, hypopharynx, or larynx, based on the eighth edition of American Joint Committee on Cancer’s clinical and pathological staging guidelines; (4) post-diagnosis, treatment plans for each patient were discussed and determined by our multidisciplinary teams based on factors such as disease progression, performance status, existing health conditions, and patient approval. The criteria for exclusion included: (1) having more than one primary cancer; (2) prior radiation therapy; (3) those who could not cooperate with the corresponding examination and follow-up (Fig. 1). We collected data of patients on demographic (gender, age, smoking, alcohol, etc.), and hematological indices including white blood cell (WBC), neutrophil (Neu), red blood cell (RBC), hemoglobin (Hb), lymphocyte (Lym), monocyte (Mono), platelets (PLT), fibrinogen (FB), ferritin heavy chain (FTH), albumin (ALB), triglycerides (TG), and blood glucose (BG) before RT.

Treatment and LENT-SOMA assessment

All patients received and a daily IMRT treatment using 2.0 Gy fractions. The overall dosage varied between 66 and 70 Gy. The contour of target and organ at risks (OARs) was generated according to RTOG protocol. During radiotherapy, patients received concurrent treatment consisting of either: Cisplatin (75 mg/m2 administered every 3 weeks for 3 cycles), or Cetuximab (chemotherapy intolerance; 400 mg/m2 loading dose in week 1 followed by 250 mg/m2 weekly maintenance doses from weeks 2 to 8). Acute symptoms were documented every three days throughout radiotherapy and for a month afterward in the electronic medical records systems. Acute toxicities were scored by CTCAE, RTOG, WHO, ONS and LENT-SOMA scales. Late effects are reported at 6 months and 2 years follow-up by the physician and researchers using LENT-SOMA scale. Patients, who did not return to the hospital for recheck, were connected by telephone follow-up. Symptoms (both subjective and objective) were scored according to LENT-SOMA scale, with each question scored from 1 to 4, and a higher score indicating a higher level of acute and chronic RD. The highest score recorded was used as the final score [32].

Nomogram construction and validation

A univariate logistic regression analysis was conducted to evaluate the high-risk elements of LENT-SOMA scores. Variables showing a p-value below 0.2 were chosen for a multivariate logistic regression analysis to pinpoint independent risk factors and determine the odds ratio (OR) along with the 95% confidence interval (CI) for each factor. A nomogram was subsequently developed utilizing the variables that showed significance (P<0.05) in the multivariate logistic regression analysis. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve’s area under the curve (AUC) was analyzed to assess the nomogram’s ability to differentiate. The calibration graph was employed to assess the alignment between the forecasted probability and the actual probability for consistency analysis. The Hosmer–Lemeshow (HL) test was employed to evaluate the goodness-of-fit for logistic regression models. A P-value greater than 0.05 suggests a good fit. Ultimately, to demonstrate the practical value of the nomogram, we created a decision curve analysis (DCA) chart to measure the net benefits across various threshold probabilities.

Statistical analysis

For categorical variables the chi-square test or the Fisher’s exact test was used. The optimal threshold for continuous variables was identified using the ROC curve. Spearman correlations were used to analyze the correlations between different scales. Numerical data is represented as the average along with the standard deviation (average ± SD). The analysis was performed with SPSS (v26.0), GraphPad Prism (v10.1.2), and R (v4.2.3). A p-value less than 0.05 was deemed to indicate statistical significance.

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 257 patients were enrolled in this study, including 229 males and 28 females. The median age of the patients was 56 years. Among them, 103 patients were diagnosed with nasopharyngeal carcinoma, 28 with oropharyngeal carcinoma, 77 with hypopharyngeal carcinoma, and 49 with laryngeal carcinoma. There were 2 patients in stage II, 84 in stage III, and 171 in stage IV. One hundred and twenty-eight Ninety-one patients (49.8%) had a smoking history and 115 patients (44.7%) had a history of alcohol consumption. A total of 98.05% (252/257) of the patients had RD, with the average onset occurring after 20.56 ± 8.44 radiotherapy fractions. Regarding the symptoms of acute RD, the incidence of erythema was 98.05% (252/257) and that of desquamation was 88.33% (227/257). For the symptoms in LENT-SOMA scale, the occurrence rates of scaliness, sensation, edema, pigmentation change was 88.33 (227/257), 32.68 (84/257), 33.46 (86/257), and 98.05 (252/257), respectively. Only 4 patients developed ulcer, 1 patient developed necrosis, 2 patients developed fibrosis, and 9 patients had atrophy (Table 1). Of the 257 patients, they were randomly divided into a training cohort (180 patients) and a validation cohort (77 patients) at a ratio of 7:3. The baseline characteristics, including clinical data and hematological indices, were not significantly different between the training and validation cohort (Table 2). For the training set, 98.89% (178/180) of the patients had RD, the time from the initial day of radiotherapy until the occurrence of radiation dermatitis was 20.72 ± 9.03 fractions. For the validation cohort, 96.10% (74/77) of the patients had RD, the time from the initial day of radiotherapy until the occurrence of radiation dermatitis was 20.18 ± 6.81 fractions.

Patient characteristics

Analysis of the relationship between subjective and objective LENT-SOMA ratings

For LENT-SOMA early score within 6 months, the numbers of patients occurring grade 2 and 3 toxicity were 104 and 10, respectively. For subjective LENT-SOMA late score, the numbers of patients occurring grade 2 and 3 toxicity were 86 and 18, respectively. Twenty-one patients developed grade 2 (objective LENT-SOMA score) toxicity, while just one patient encountered grade 3 toxicity. No patient reached the grade 4 of LENT-SOMA. Correlation analysis indicated that the LENT-SOMA early score, especially subjective LENT-SOMA early score, was positively correlated with RTOG, CTCAE, WHO, and ONS criteria, the correlation coefficients were 0.418, 0.493, 0.518, and 0.518, respectively (all P<0.05; Fig. 2).

Univariate and multivariate analysis of parameters associated with the risk of LENT-SOMA grade 2 + scores

A multivariate logistic regression was performed incorporating Lym, Mono, PLT, FB, ALB, TG, and BG before radiotherapy. In the multivariate logistic regression analysis (Table 3), higher Lym (OR: 3.581, 95% CI: 1.375–9.326, P = 0.009), BG (OR: 3.175, 95% CI: 1.208–8.343, P = 0.019), lower FB (OR: 0.186, 95% CI: 0.081–0.430, P<0.001), and ALB (OR: 0.317, 95% CI: 0.155–0.648, P = 0.002) before radiotherapy were independent predictors of LENT-SOMA scores.

Creation and verification of the predictive model

Utilizing multivariate analysis, a clear nomogram was developed by combining Lym, BG, FB, and ALB prior to radiotherapy (Fig. 3). For the training cohort, the AUC value of the nomogram was well (0.73; 95% CI: 0.66–0.80) and showed good power for the discrimination of LENT-SOMA scores (Fig. 4A). Calibration curves validated the alignment between the nomogram’s LENT-SOMA score predictions and the real observations (Fig. 4B). The nomogram was demonstrated adequate positive net benefits at the threshold probabilities on DCA (Fig. 4C). Furthermore, the AUC value (0.68; 95% CI: 0.56–0.80), along with calibration curves and DCA for the validation group, demonstrated the nomogram’s strong predictive capability, clinical practicality, and usefulness (Fig. 4D-F).

Discussion

HNC patients receiving radiotherapy are commonly accompanied by marked side-effects, such as scaliness, edema, the change of pigmentation, ulcer/necrosis, telangiectasia, fibrosis/scar, atrophy/contraction [33]. Due to the wide population with craniofacial nerves in head and neck [34], the symptoms of RD including abnormal sensation are often more obvious. Precise anticipation of the extent of radiation-induced skin damage is essential for the successful implementation of preventive and therapeutic measures. To date, the studies about factors of RD were almost based on objective rating scales results obtained by interviewers for HNC patients receiving radiotherapy [25, 26, 35]. However, these scales pay little attention to subjective feelings of patients. LENT-SOMA scale includes an assessment of the patient’s objective symptoms and subjective feelings. Studies have indicated a relationship between LENT-SOMA scores and RTOG criteria in assessing skin conditions [36, 37]. Although LENT-SOMA scale was commonly used for the evaluation of late RD, its utility in assessing acute RD has not been reported. In this study, we scored the severity of RD based LENT-SOMA scales and innovatively evaluated the correlation between LENT-SOMA early score and other acute toxicity grading systems, including CTCAE, RTOG, WHO, and ONS scales. It was indicated that the LENT-SOMA early score, especially subjective LENT-SOMA early score, was positively displayed varying degrees of correlation with, RTOG, CTCAE, WHO, and ONS criteria. These results suggest that LENT-SOMA scale may be a promising tool for the assessment of acute RD. Univariate analysis indicated that patients exhibiting higher Lym, Mono, PLT, BG, TG, lower FB and ALB levels before radiotherapy, had a statistically significant correlation with an increased risk of high LENT-SOMA scores. Higher Lym, BG, lower FB, and ALB levels before radiotherapy were identified as independent indicators of LENT-SOMA grade 2 + in patients with HNC undergoing radiotherapy.

Inflammatory processes have been implicated in the pathophysiology of skin injury. We found that patients with higher number of lymphocytes before radiotherapy have higher risk of LENT-SOMA grade 2+. Changes in the ratios of immune cells could significantly impact RD. Consistent with current findings, earlier research has shown that elevated levels of CD3 + T cells before breast cancer radiotherapy are an independent risk factor for severe acute RD [24]. Additionally, reports indicate that the presence of CD11b + monocytes/macrophages in X-irradiated skin is associated with the onset of RD [38].

Notably, abnormal blood clotting function can also have an effect on radioactive skin damage. Fallah et al. found that plasminogen served as a pro-inflammatory factor, is key for the development of RD [39]. The combined action of platelets, tissue factors, thrombin, the contact activation system, and the complement system enhance clot formation and triggers inflammatory responses. The plasminogen activation system triggers fibrinolysis and reduces fibrinogen levels while also enhancing the inflammation linked to thrombosis [40]. These results are consistent with the results of our analysis.

Additionally, it has been reported that RD is associated with nutritional status, glucose and lipid metabolism. Lower albumin levels are not conducive to wound healing, which can affect the development of RD [41]. Blood sugar level is also an important factor in injury healing. Studies have shown that the higher the blood sugar level, the slower the injury healing [42]. An increasing number of research indicates that elevated triglycerides are causally associated with inflammation initiation and progression [43, 44]. These results corroborate our findings that higher BG, TG and lower ALB risk of LENT-SOMA grade 2+.

Based on the findings of multivariate analyses, a nomogram model was created to estimate the risk of LENT-SOMA grade 2+. The validation cohort for the nomogram model demonstrated its advantage over individual risk factors and highlighted its possible clinical significance. Consequently, in the context of precision oncology, these findings might be crucial in averting radiation-induced skin damage in HNC patients.

However, this study had several limitations. It is pertinent to acknowledge that this research was limited to a single institution and featured a relatively modest sample size. Additionally, the nomogram was not externally validated using an independent group of patients. Finally, we acknowledge that concomitant treatment including chemotherapy and targeted therapy could impact on RD onset [45, 46]. In the future, a larger patient cohort will be enrolled to tackle with concomitant systemic therapy issues statistically. Overall, multi-center and further studies are required to confirm these findings.

Conclusion

LENT-SOMA scale shows potential in evaluating acute RD. Higher Lym, BG, lower FB, and ALB before radiotherapy were independent risk factors for LENT-SOMA grade 2 + RD. The predictive model, derived from the previously mentioned risk factors, demonstrates significant accuracy and holds notable clinical relevance. Based on the nomogram, clinicians can assess the risk of RD and treatment measures to reduce the effect of severe RD for HNC patients.

Prolonged intra-aortic balloon pump support successfully reversed severe pulmonary hypertension prior to heart transplantation
Authors: Zeng Xiaodong, Wu Min, Huang Jinsong, Liang Yuemei, Wu Yijin, Peng Wenying
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHeart transplantation remains the gold standard for the treatment of end-stage heart failure, but severe pulmonary hypertension (PH) poses a significant challenge due to the increased risk of complications after surgery. International guidelines classify severe PH as a relative contraindication to transplantation, requiring meticulous preoperative management.Case presentationWe present the case of a 58-year-old man with severe heart failure and PH who received intra-aortic balloon pump (IABP) support for 62 days. Through targeted medical therapy and meticulous fluid management, remarkable hemodynamic improvements were achieved, exceeding the threshold for surgical contraindication. This allowed for a successful heart transplantation. Postoperatively, the patient experienced transient hemodynamic instability, which was effectively managed with vasopressor therapy and inhaled nitric oxide. With continued perioperative management, the patient experienced a smooth recovery and achieved a favorable outcome.ConclusionThis case highlights the effectiveness of prolonged IABP assistance in reversing severe PH prior to transplantation, thereby facilitating successful heart transplantation. By implementing rigorous comprehensive perioperative management, patients with severe pulmonary hypertension can achieve positive outcomes after transplantation.

Introduction

Heart transplantation is the most effective treatment for end-stage heart failure [1]. Severe pulmonary hypertension (PH) prior to surgery often leads to early right ventricular dysfunction after heart transplantation, which is a significant adverse prognostic factor after transplantation. As a result, it is considered a surgical contraindication by many clinicians [2, 3]. The International Society for Heart and Lung Transplantation (ISHLT) guidelines classify severe PH to be a relative contraindication to heart transplantation. This is defined as a pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) >5 Wood units, a PVR index >6, or a transpulmonary pressure gradient (TPG) >16 to 20 mmHg [3, 4].

We report a case of end-stage heart failure complicated by severe pulmonary hypertension. Right heart catheterization revealed a pulmonary artery systolic pressure (PASP) of 98 mmHg, a mean pulmonary artery pressure (mPAP) of 58 mmHg, a pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (PCWP) of 29 mmHg, and a pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) of 9.78 Wood units. This case was classified as combined pre- and post-capillary pulmonary hypertension [5]. After receiving 62 days of IABP support, together with optimized fluid therapy and targeted drug treatment, the patient’s PASP decreased to 31 mmHg, a mPAP of 24 mmHg, and a PVR of 4.85 Wood units. This significant improvement exceeded the threshold for surgical contraindication. The patient subsequently underwent heart transplantation. Postoperatively, he received close hemodynamic monitoring, inhaled nitric oxide (NO), targeted drug therapy, and other supportive measures. The recovery process proceeded smoothly.

Case presentation

A 58-year-old male patient was admitted to the Guangdong provincial People’s Hospital (Guangzhou, China) on 8 March, 2023 for severe heart failure. The patient had been experiencing intermittent chest pain, chest tightness, and shortness of breath since 2009, with symptoms worsening significantly over the past three months. Recently, he noted a marked increase in shortness of breath, particularly when lying flat at night, which led to sleep disturbances. Relief was achieved by sitting up, indicating orthopnea. Additionally, he observed notable swelling in both lower limbs.

The patient visited our cardiology outpatient clinic, where an electrocardiogram (ECG) showed ST segment depression in the leads V2-V6. On January 29, 2023, an echocardiogram performed in our hospital showed significant cardiac enlargement, with remarkable measurements: left ventricular diastolic diameter (LVDD) of 69 mm, left atrial (LA) diameter of 58 mm, right ventricular (RV) diameter of 77 mm, and right atrial (RA) diameter of 74 mm. Left ventricular systolic function is significantly reduced, with a left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) of 28%. The echocardiogram also showed segmental wall motion abnormalities in the left ventricle, indicative of post-myocardial infarction changes. Severe mitral regurgitation (15 cm²) and severe tricuspid regurgitation (12.2 cm²) are observed. Pulmonary artery systolic pressure is 85mmHg, with moderate pulmonary valve regurgitation (7.0 cm²), and an estimated mean pulmonary artery pressure of 66mmHg (Fig. 1). He received treatment with inotropic agents, diuretics, and other medications, but the results were unsatisfactory. He was then admitted to our department for heart transplantation. The patient has a significant history of coronary artery disease, having undergone coronary stent implantation in 2009 at an outside hospital, followed by coronary angioplasty in 2022 at another institution. These previous interventions are pertinent to his current cardiac status and the decision making process for heart transplantation surgery. Upon admission, his vital signs were as follows: body temperature was 36.2 °C, heart rate 91 beats per minute, blood pressure of 105/75mmHg, and respiratory rate 20 breaths per minute. His height is 176 cm and weight is 91 kg. Right heart catheterization revealed a PASP of 98 mmHg, pulmonary artery diastolic pressure (PADP) of 40 mmHg, mPAP of 58 mmHg, PCWP of 29 mmHg, PVR of 19.56 Wood units, and PVR of 9.78 Wood units. Due to intolerance, he did not undergo cardiopulmonary exercise testing. A computed tomography (CT) scan performed prior to admission showed multiple patchy opacities in both lungs and pulmonary artery enlargement, with the main pulmonary artery measuring approximately 46 mm in diameter (Fig. 2). HLA class I antibodies are positive at 6%, while HLA class II antibodies are negative. The Seattle Heart Failure Model (SHFM) score indicates a 1-year mortality rate of 38% and a 5-year mortality rate of 93%. On 9 March, 2023, due to severe heart failure, he began IABP support (Fig. 3). Prior to heart transplantation, his primary treatment treatments included furosemide and spironolactone for diuresis, recombinant human brain natriuretic peptide for anti-heart failure therapy, macitentan to control pulmonary artery pressure, and enoxaparin for anticoagulation, among others. On the 15th, 25th, 34th, and 48th days of support with the IABP, we performed a chest CT scans for the patient. The results showed that there was no significant reduction in the width of the pulmonary artery, but there was a marked reduction in patchy infiltrates in the lungs (Fig. 4). After 62 days of IABP support, floating catheter results showed that PASP was 31mmHg, PADP was 19mmHg, mPAP was 24mmHg, PCWP was 10mmHg, and PVR was 4.85wood (Table 1), demonstrating that the severe pulmonary hypertension was reversible.

Surgical procedure

The donor heart was procured from a brain-dead donor under general anesthesia and mechanical ventilation. Following cardioplegia with Modified St. Thomas Solution, the heart was explanted and preserved in HTK solution.

Heart transplantation was performed via median sternotomy under general anesthesia. After initiation of cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB), the recipient heart was excised. The donor heart was trimmed and anastomosed to the recipient’s left atrium, aorta, pulmonary artery, and vena cavae. CPB was weaned after reperfusion, hemostasis was achieved, and the sternum was closed. The cold ischemic time was 180 min, the CPB time was 270 min, and the aortic cross-clamp time was 106 min.

Postoperative changes and management

On the day after surgery, the patient’s PASP/PADP increased to 53/25 mmHg, with unstable hemodynamics characterized by a blood pressure of 92/58 mmHg and a heart rate of 118 beats per minute. High-dose vasopressor therapy was required, including dopamine at a rate of 8 µg/kg/min and norepinephrine at a rate of 0.2 µg/kg/min. Assisted ventilation was continued, and sedation was maintained after confirmation of consciousness. In addition, inhaled nitric oxide (NO) at 17 ppm, milrinone at 0.25 µg/kg/min, and macitentan were administered to reduce pulmonary artery pressure. On the first postoperative day, the patient’s blood pressure was 108/62 mmHg, heart rate was 103 beats per minute, and pulmonary artery pressure was 40/25 mmHg. He received dopamine at a rate of 5 µg/kg/min and norepinephrine at a rate of 0.12 µg/kg/min. Vasopressor doses were then tapered according to haemodynamic status and the use of inhaled NO was also reduced in response to pulmonary artery pressure. On the morning of the sixth day postoperatively, the patient received norepinephrine at a rate of 0.03 µg/kg/min and dopamine at a rate of 3 µg/kg/min. After discontinuation of inhaled NO, pulmonary artery pressure stabilized, and the endotracheal tube was removed. The patient was transferred out of the Intensive Care Unit on the ninth day postoperatively. On the twelfth day postoperatively, an echocardiogram showed an LVEF of 68%, LA diameter of 53 mm, LVD diameter of 50 mm, RA diameter of 52 mm, RV diameter of 65 mm, RV fractional area change (FAC) of 41.8%, and a small amount of pericardial effusion (Fig. 5a). His condition improved smoothly, and he was discharged from the hospital on the 19th day after surgery. His condition remained stable after discharge. The frequency of echocardiography post-heart transplant is scheduled for the 1 st month, 3rd month, 6th month, and 12th month after discharge. On February 27, 2024, his echocardiogram showed an LVEF of 61%, LVD diameter of 45 mm, LA diameter of 55 mm, RA diameter of 47 mm, RV diameter of 53 mm, RV FAC of 40%, mild tricuspid regurgitation (1.8cm2), and an estimated PH of 24mmHg (Fig. 5b).

Discussion

Heart transplantation is an effective way to save the lives and improve the quality of life for patients with end-stage heart failure [6]. Pulmonary hypertension, a common complication of end-stage heart failure, may increase the incidence of right heart failure after heart transplantation, thereby raising both early and late postoperative mortality rates [5, 7–9]. There is considerable controversy about the feasibility of heart transplantation in high-risk pulmonary hypertension patients, with insufficient research evidence available.

The patient was diagnosed with end-stage heart failure and severe PH prior to transplantation. He was receiving standardised medical therapy in the cardiology outpatient clinic, but the response to treatment was inadequate. Subsequent ultrasound examinations consistently indicated severe pulmonary arterial hypertension. Right heart catheterization performed in our hospital confirmed PASP of 98mmHg, PADP of 40mmHg, mPAP of 58mmHg, PCWP of 29mmHg, PVR of 9.78 Wood units. This severe pulmonary arterial hypertension constitutes a contraindication to heart transplantation. Due to the excessive pulmonary artery pressure, acute vasoreactivity testing was not performed on this patient. According to international guidelines, this type of pulmonary hypertension is classified as left heart-related PH, and its hemodynamic classification is Combined Pre- and Post-Capillary PH [5]. Based on existing clinical data, a PVR >5 WU may indicate a severe pre-capillary component suggesting a poor prognosis for the patient’s condition [10]. The ISHLT emphasizes that severe pulmonary arterial hypertension must be thoroughly assessed and optimized prior to heart transplantation to ensure its reversibility, thereby preventing postoperative right ventricular dysfunction [5]. More importantly, previous studies have shown that severe preoperative PH correlates with mortality in the first year after surgery, but it is not associated with all-cause mortality rates or mortality rates at other times [2]. When pharmacological treatment fails to achieve acceptable hemodynamics, the use of an IABP and/or Left Ventricular Assist Device (LVAD) to unload the left ventricle can be employed to confirm reversibility of pulmonary hypertension [3, 4]. IABP effectively reduces cardiac afterload and pulmonary artery pressure, significantly improving hemodynamics in patients with heart failure and severe pulmonary hypertension, as demonstrated by the 62-day support in this case. Its simplicity and effectiveness make it preferable over more complex devices like ECMO and LVAD, especially when immediate support is needed or resources are limited. Given the patient’s stable condition and history, IABP was a suitable choice, achieving excellent outcomes and successfully facilitating heart transplantation [11–13].

This patient received timely support with an IABP upon admission. Strict fluid management and targeted drug therapy with macitentan were used during the assist. On the 15th, 25th, 34th, and 48th day of IABP support, the patient underwent a chest CT scan. The results indicated that while the width of the pulmonary artery did not significantly decrease, there was a noticeable reduction in patchy infiltrates in the lungs. This suggests a significant improvement in the patient’s heart function. On day 62 of IABP support, floating catheter data showed a PASP of 31 mmHg, mPAP of 24 mmHg, PVR of 4.85 Wood units. These results confirm the reversibility of the patient’s pulmonary hypertension and reflect a significant improvement in pulmonary hemodynamics. Simultaneously, this approach reduces the risk of early postoperative right ventricular dysfunction and promotes early postoperative recovery.

In the early postoperative period, the patient’s PASP increased to 53 mmHg, which had a significant hemodynamic impact, necessitating high doses of dopamine and epinephrine to maintain circulation. Based on the hemodynamic characteristics, this condition is classified as isolated post-capillary PH. Given the hemodynamic impact of elevated pulmonary artery pressure on the patient, several measures were implemented postoperatively: (1) High-Dose Inhaled NO: Initially, the patient was administered high-dose inhaled nitric oxide (NO) to reduce right ventricular afterload; (2) Sedation and Mechanical Ventilation: After confirming the patient’s consciousness, adequate sedation was provided, and the duration of mechanical ventilation support was appropriately extended; (3) Pulmonary Pressure-Lowering Medications: Medications administered to lower pulmonary pressure included milrinone and macitentan; (4) Additional Measures: Other measures included strict fluid management and appropriate use of vasopressor medications.

This case demonstrates the successful application of IABP to reverse severe pulmonary hypertension before heart transplantation, providing valuable experience for future clinical practice and research. Potential directions for future research and application of this approach include: (1) This successful case requires large-scale multi-center randomized controlled trials to verify its general applicability and effectiveness. Increasing the sample size will allow for a more accurate evaluation of IABP’s therapeutic effects in patients with severe pulmonary hypertension and help determine the optimal timing and duration of its use. (2) Optimize IABP use strategies: Future research should explore various strategies for using IABP, including the initiation timing, duration of support, and its combined use with other treatments. (3) Comparative studies between the IABP and other mechanical circulatory support devices, such as the LVAD, could help to assess the relative efficacy, safety and cost-effectiveness of each approach in this clinical context.

The prognosis of heart transplantation in patients with concomitant severe pulmonary arterial hypertension is closely related to perioperative management. Prolonged preoperative IABP support can relieve left ventricular unloading, thereby confirming the reversibility of pulmonary hypertension and improving right ventricular function [5, 14]. Effective perioperative management can mitigate the impact of severe preoperative pulmonary arterial hypertension on the outcomes of heart transplantation.

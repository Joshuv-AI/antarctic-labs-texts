The molecular complexity of deep vein thrombosis was preliminarily explored based on the ceRNA network, scRNA-seq and AlphaFold 2
Authors: Bao-ze Pan, Qing-yu Luo, Ming-jun Jiang, Dan Ning, Li-ming Deng, Xian-peng Dai, Zi-xuan Wu, Zhi-he Deng, Dong-yang Luo, Yang-yi-jing Wang, Jie Chen, Guo-zuo Xiong, Guo-shan Bi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDeep vein thrombosis (DVT) is a prevalent peripheral vascular disease that is frequently accompanied by significant complications. However, the complexity and diversity of its pathogenesis have resulted in a lack of in-depth understanding of relevant regulatory targets. The objective of this study was to identify the highly distinctive genes associated with DVT, and to investigate their intricacies.MethodsIn this study, a comprehensive transcriptomic analysis was conducted on blood samples from 4 DVT patients and 6 healthy subjects from an internal test set, employing established standard procedures. Differential (diff) circular RNAs (circRNAs), diff microRNAs (miRNAs) and diff messenger RNAs (mRNAs) associated with DVT were identified to construct a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network model, and node mRNAs were identified in an external validation set (n = 14). Furthermore, a topological analysis was conducted by using 7 protein–protein interaction (PPI) network algorithms. Concurrently, AlphaFold 2 was employed for three-dimensional prediction and assessment of the molecular structure. The comparative toxicology genomics database (CTD) was employed to assess the interconnectivity between these genes. Subsequently, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) was conducted on blood samples from 3 DVT patients and 3 healthy subjects, following the standard protocol, to ascertain the cellular localization of gene expression and the role of core pathways.ResultsA whole-transcriptome analysis identified a total of 406 diff circRNAs, 29 diff miRNAs and 154 diff mRNAs. Concurrently, a total of 6 circRNAs, 5 miRNAs and 16 mRNAs were incorporated into the ceRNA network models (a total of 6 node mRNAs were identified differentially). A total of 5 hub genes (including JAK2, CD36, TNFSF13B, TLR7 and PARP9) were identified by 7 PPI network algorithms. The structure and function of the above genes are adequately described by the AlphaFold 2 and CTD interference scores. A total of 7 cell types were identified by scRNA-seq, and there were obvious differences in the localization of genes and the role of core pathways in different cells.ConclusionsJAK2, CD36, TNFSF13B, TLR7 and PARP9 were identified as potential regulatory target genes for the pathophysiological process of DVT. Additionally, hsa_circ_0095124/hsa-miR-3074-5p/TNFSF13B is a potential regulatory pathway for DVT.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12920-025-02239-9.

Introduction

Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) is a prevalent peripheral vascular disease that may give rise to significant complications. Typical symptoms include limb pain (about 80% to 90%), swelling (80%), skin redness (20%) and cyanosis [1–6]. The majority of cases of DVT affect the lower extremities, with the specific veins involved varying by location. Distal veins are affected in 40% of cases, popliteal veins in 16%, femoral veins in 20%, common femoral veins in 20%, and iliac veins in 4% [4, 7, 8]. Furthermore, Post-thrombotic syndrome (PTS), as a long-term complication of DVT, is also the most common chronic complication, occurring within 2 years of onset in 30–50% of patients [9–11]. At present, the diagnosis of DVT is based on the detection and imaging of D-dimer levels. However, D-dimers are susceptible to interference from non-thrombotic diseases, including infection, disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC), surgery and tumours. This results in a low level of specificity [2]. In line with the dilemma of the lack of non-invasive and reliable diagnostic methods in clinical practice, the pathophysiological processes associated with DVT are still at an exploratory stage. Given the intricate and varied nature of its pathogenesis, there is a need for further investigation into the current understanding of its associated target molecules.

Approximately 2% of human RNA encodes corresponding proteins, with the remainder comprising non-coding RNA (ncRNA). This encompasses a range of ncRNA types, including circular RNA (circRNA), microRNA (miRNA) and long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) [12, 13]. circRNAs can compete with miRNAs to bind and affect the expression of target genes [14–16]. Concurrently, RNA transcripts containing miRNA binding sites can communicate and regulate each other by specifically competing for shared miRNAs. This results in the formation of competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs). Its activity forms a large-scale and complex post-transcriptional regulatory network throughout the transcriptome, expands the genetic information in the genome, and can elucidate the pathogenesis through the corresponding interaction analysis between proteins [17]. This regulatory influence has been observed in diverse cancerous conditions, such as colon cancer, triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) and nasopharyngeal carcinoma [14, 18–21]. However, a paucity of analogous reports is evident in the non-oncology field.

Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) is a high-throughput experimental technique that employs RNA sequencing to quantify the gene expression profiles of specific cell populations at the single-cell level, thus facilitating the dissection of the distribution characteristics of different cell types [22–24]. In comparison to traditional RNA sequencing, scRNA-seq technology is capable of delineating the distinctive gene expression patterns of individual cells within patient tissues, thereby reflecting the cellular heterogeneity of the population [25–27]. In the domain of cardiovascular research, significant progress has been made, as evidenced by Hu et al.'s pioneering development of the first cell map of healthy human heart arteries utilising scRNA-seq technology [28]. This advancement has led to advancements in the in-depth identification of conventional macrophages and endothelial cell subsets. In the context of DVT, there is a necessity for in-depth study of the structure and function of associated target molecules, given the ambiguity surrounding the regulatory mechanisms of inflammatory, coagulation and fibrosis pathways in particular cell populations. Furthermore, the combination with traditional sequencing technologies, such as whole transcriptomics, allows for a comprehensive investigation of DVT patient-specific phenotypic and functional variations, thereby facilitating the development of disease assessment models [29, 30].

The objective of this study was to analyse the susceptibility genes and pathogenic genes of DVT patients, to explore the complexity and intrinsic relationship of disease regulatory molecules, and to screen out the regulatory network and corresponding targets for evaluating the cytopathic and thrombotic potential of DVT patients.

Methods

Sample collection

As stated in the 2016 ISTH SSC Classification Guidelines for Idiopathic DVT, a total of 4 patients diagnosed with idiopathic DVT who were treated in the Vascular Surgery Department of the Second Affiliated Hospital of South China University, along with 6 healthy subjects selected from the health examination center, were included as the internal test set. In accordance with clinical ethical principles and experimental protocol requirements, the two groups of participants were matched by gender, age, and body mass index (BMI). Peripheral venous blood samples of 5–6 ml were collected from both the DVT patients and the healthy subjects for whole transcriptome high-throughput sequencing. Concurrently, an additional 6 healthy subjects and 8 DVT patients were selected as external validation sets using the same principle, and bulk RNA sequencing (bulk RNA-seq) was performed. Furthermore, samples were re-collected from 3 DVT patients and 3 healthy subjects for scRNA-seq. The inclusion criteria were as follows: i) at least one test result (including ultrasonography or D-dimer test) supporting the diagnosis of DVT, ii) patients with a first episode of acute DVT, iii) not receiving anticoagulation, iv) age ≥ 18 years. The exclusion criteria are as follows: (i) antiphospholipid syndrome (APS), (ii) pregnancy or breastfeeding, (iii) infection or use of immunosuppressants within 2 weeks prior to enrolment, (iv) active or prior malignancy. Prior to enrolment, written informed consent was obtained from all participants. The study protocol was designed in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki regarding human subjects and was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of the University of South China (approval number: 2022K091301). The present study was conducted from April 2023 to October 2023.

Identification of differentially expressed genes (DEGs)

In accordance with the manufacturer's protocol, total gene isolation and purification were conducted by using TRIzol (Aidlab, China) on the samples of DVT patients (n = 4) and healthy subjects (n = 6) previously collected. Also, the Fragment Analyzer (Agilent, United States) was used to detect the total gene amount, concentration, volume, RIN/RON value and 28S/18S of the sample. And then use the MGIEasy rRNA removal kit (MGI, China) to remove ribosomal RNA (rRNA) from the total RNA according to the instructions. The library was constructed in accordance with the standard protocol of BGI Genomics Co. Ltd. (Shenzhen, China), and the data was analyzed on the Dr. Tom network platform (http://report.bgi.com). The raw data obtained from sequencing were subjected to filtration by using SOAPnuke (v1.5.6). Gene fusion analysis was conducted by using Ericscript (v0.5.5), while gene expression quantification was performed with RSEM (v1.3.1). The analysis of DEGs was conducted using the R packages edgeR (v3.32.1) and limma (v3.26.8) [31]. The application of a filter to differentiate between differential (diff) circRNAs and diff miRNAs can be achieved by setting log2 [FC] > 1 or log2 [FC] < −1. For diff mRNAs, the selection criterion is log2 [FC] > 0.75 or log2 [FC] < −0.75. The aforementioned results underwent filtration according to the false discovery rate (FDR) criterion, with a cut-off value of 0.05. Concurrently, in order to gain a deeper understanding of the functional pathways of genes associated with phenotypic changes, based on hypergeometric tests, the R packages clusterProfiler (v4.12.0) and org.Hs.eg.db (v3.18.0) were used to perform Gene Ontology (GO) analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis for DEGs. The results were then compared with background genes to identify significant enrichment terms among candidate genes [32, 33].

Construction and analysis of ceRNA networks

In accordance with the ceRNA theory, the cancer-specific circRNA database (CSCD, http://gb.whu.edu.cn/CSCD/) was employed to obtain diff circRNA-related microRNA response elements (MREs). And we employed TargetScan (https://www.targetscan.org/) and miRDB (https://mirdb.org/) to predict the miRNA-binding serial site, thereby constructing a ceRNA regulatory network comprising diff circRNAs, diff miRNAs and diff messenger RNAs (mRNAs). The resulting ceRNA network was then visualised using the Cytoscape software (v3.10.0). In order to gain an in-depth understanding of the function of nodal genes, GO analysis and KEGG enrichment analysis were also performed. Concurrently, the rationality of the mRNA prediction results in the ceRNA network model was validated through the examination of discrepancies in external validation sets from disparate batches.

The multi-algorithm network analysis of protein–protein interaction (PPI)

To gain further insight into the biological significance of the aforementioned diff mRNAs, the STRING (https://string-db.org/) was employed to investigate the interaction between the crossover mRNAs (binding to circRNA) of miRNAs at the protein level. The required confidence (combined score) was set as a medium level (0.400). Furthermore, Cytoscape (v3.10.0) was utilised for the purpose of visualisation. Concurrently, a multi-algorithm network analysis of cytoHubba (v0.1) was conducted to identify the top 15 genes. 7 algorithms were selected for testing: degree, closeness, maximum neighbourhood component (MNC), maximum clique centrality (MCC), edge percolated component (EPC), stress and radiality. The objective was to identify hub genes. Concurrently, in order to provide a comprehensive representation of the aforementioned results, an upset plot has been constructed.

Prediction of the structure of proteins

In order to gain further insight into the molecular complexity of proteins associated with DVT, the sequences of hub proteins were retrieved from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/). These sequences were then used as input for AlphaFold 2 (https://alphafold.ebi.ac.uk/), a protein structure prediction algorithm, to generate three-dimensional spatial structures [34]. The predicted structures contain atomic coordinates and per-residue confidence estimates on a scale from 0 to 100, with higher scores corresponding to higher confidence. The measure of confidence is referred to as predicted local distance difference test (pLDDT) [35]. Residues with pLDDT ≥ 90 have been determined to have very high model confidence, while residues with pLDDT ≥ 70 are considered reliable. Residues with pLDDT ≥ 50 and < 70 have been determined to have relatively low confidence, while those with pLDDT < 50 have been categorised as having extremely low confidence.

Identification of pivotal genes associated with DVT

In order to identify the hub genes associated with DVT, the comparative toxicology genomics database (CTD, http://ctdbase.org/) was utilised. The above hub genes were subjected to a quantitative analysis by using interference scores, with a view to establishing the extent of their contribution to the profile of the relevant conditions. This encompasses not only DVT, but also a broader spectrum of associated health conditions, including atherosclerosis (AS), cardiovascular diseases, PE, thrombosis and vascular diseases.

Single-cell data analysis

The samples were dissociated in accordance with the standard procedure outlined by the manufacturer, resulting in the preparation of a single-cell suspension. The prepared single-cell suspension (concentration range: 700 to 1200 cells/μl) was utilised to generate emulsion (GEMs, Gel Beads in Emulsions) in Chromium Controller (10X Genomics) and reverse transcription of mRNA to complementary DNA (cDNA) was carried out. The cDNA products were then fragmented, end repaired and "A" bases were added to the 3' end. The library was constructed in accordance with the standard protocol established by BGI Genomics Co. Ltd. (Shenzhen, China), and the samples of DVT patients (n = 3) and healthy subjects (n = 3) were subjected to analysis in accordance with the standard protocol. The cells were enumerated and filtered using R package Seurat (v5.3.0) [36], and the initial 2000 hypervariable characteristic genes were identified by using Doubletdetection (v3.0). Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed to reduce the dimensionality of the data, and elbow plots were used to determine the optimal inflection point. Concurrently, the samples were performed nonlinear dimensionality reduction based on the uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) pathway. The default Wilcoxon rank sum test was utilised to identify significant DEGs between clusters, with a threshold of log2 [FC] > 1 or log2 [FC] < −1 and FDR < 0.05. Manual cell type matching and annotation was performed using CellMarker (http://xteam.xbio.top) and NCBI database. Concurrently, the distribution tendency of different cell types across various groups was assessed by calculating the observed/expected ratio (Ro/e) and performing enriched significance testing utilising Fisher's exact tests. In a similar manner, with a threshold of log2 [FC] = 1 (FDR < 0.05) and the number of substitutions = 10,000, the gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed on hub genes based on human KEGG pathway data sets (https://www.genome.jp/kegg/pathway.html#hsa) to explore the biological pathways in which they are involved [37]. In order to identify cell-related communication and quantify network relationships, the total read count of the same set of characteristic genes in the transcriptome was analysed. Concurrently, cell–cell interactions were analysed by using the R package CellChat (v1.6.1) and the web-based Explorer (http://www.cellchat.org/) [38].

Analysis of statistical results

All the statistical analyses were performed with R (v4.3.3) and SPSS (v23.0). For data with non-normal distribution, the Mann–Whitney U test. For normally distributed data with equal variance, P values were assessed by using the 2-tailed Student t test and analysis of variance (ANOVA) for comparisons. The values are expressed as standard errors of the mean ± mean. A value of p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. *p < 0.05; **p < 0.01; ***p < 0.001; NS, no significant difference.

Results

Overview of whole transcriptome sequencing results

In this study, according to the standard experimental procedure, a total of 10 samples and 120,455 genes were detected using the DNBSEQ platform after screening samples, sample extraction and high-throughput sequencing, with an average of 11.33 G data per sample. Following the calculation of gene and transcript expression by using RSEM, the total expression of circRNAs, miRNAs and mRNAs was normalised for each sample. To identify statistically significant DEGs, we obtained a total of 406 diff circRNAs (up-regulated genes: 90, down-regulated genes: 316), 29 diff miRNAs (up-regulated genes: 3, down-regulated genes: 26), and 154 diff mRNAs (up-regulated genes: 85, down-regulated genes: 69) by using the R packages edgeR and limma. Furthermore, in order to observe the overall difference in sample size in two-dimensional space, volcano plots and histograms were plotted for analysis (Fig. 1a and b). The clustering heatmap allows the visualisation of the gene expression trend in a given sample according to the colour change, thus facilitating the distinction between diff circRNAs, miRNAs and mRNAs in the DVT patients and the healthy subjects. A clear distinction was evident in the DEGs between the two groups (red: up-regulated genes, blue: down-regulated genes). The results of the cluster analysis were consistent with those obtained by filtering the data for DEGs (Fig. 1c). The identification and analysis of DEGs

As ncRNAs, the principal functions of circRNAs and miRNAs are to regulate the transcription, translation, modification and expression of mRNAs. Concurrently, to ascertain the distinctions in the biological function of mRNAs, we conducted a GO enrichment analysis on 154 diff mRNAs by employing Phyper. This encompassed biological processes (BP), cellular components (CC) and molecular functions (MF). After enrichment analysis, it was found that the keywords rich in diff mRNAs in BP were intracellular receptor signaling pathway (GO: 0030522) and pattern recognition receptor signaling pathway (GO: 0002221). In CC, DEGs are mostly located on the vacuolar membrane (GO: 0005774) and the lysosomal membrane (GO: 0005765). Whereas, enzyme inhibitor activity (GO: 0004857) and lipid transporter activity (GO: 0005319) were enrichment pathways in MF (Fig. 1d). This indicates the diversity and complexity of the molecular mechanisms involved in the development of DVT. To gain further insight into the relevant biological processes involved in thrombosis, we conducted a KEGG cluster analysis of diff mRNAs separately. Following clustering, the initial 15 pathways were subjected to further analysis, which revealed that platelet activation (KEGG: hsa04611) constituted one of the most significant biological pathways in diff mRNAs. Additionally, 4 diff mRNAs were identified as being associated with this biological regulatory process (Fig. 1e). This further determines the most important biochemical and signal transduction pathways involved in the aforementioned candidate genes, thereby providing the necessary guarantee for the comprehensive examination of subsequent genes.

Construction and analysis of DVT-related ceRNA networks

The Circbase (http://www.circbase.org) was queried in order to obtain the identifier, position number, positive and negative strands, chromosome length, genes, and transcripts of the various circRNAs. Subsequently, the initial 50 circRNAs exhibiting the most pronounced disparities were selected, resulting in the identification of 2,043 MREs within CSCD. This resulted in a total of 6 results, which intersected with 29 diff miRNAs (Fig. 2a). Subsequently, public databases (miRDB and Targetscan) were employed to predict the target genes of the 6 diff miRNAs that had been identified (the target genes present in both databases were matched). As a single miRNA can match multiple target genes simultaneously, the 6 diff miRNAs described above can interact with a total of 3,562 target genes. By intersecting with 154 diff mRNAs, a total of 21 differentially co-expressed genes were obtained (Fig. 2b). The diff circRNAs, diff miRNAs, diff mRNAs, MREs and the predicted target genes were constructed into a ceRNA network, and the expression levels of circRNA-miRNA and miRNA-mRNA were screened by expression correlation. Structure construction and functional analysis of DVT core protein

To further examine the three-dimensional structure and function of proteins, we employed AlphaFold 2 technology to predict the spatial structure of 5 key proteins: JAK2, CD36, TLR7, TNFSF13B and PARP9 (Fig. 4a). The pLDDT-related colour visualisation demonstrated that the majority of the structures of JAK2 and CD36 were dark blue, indicating high confidence and accurate structure prediction. Conversely, the local regions of TLR7, TNFSF13B and PARP9 were yellow or orange, suggesting low confidence. As a pioneering approach to predicting protein structures and their interactions, this method undoubtedly facilitates high-precision modelling across the biomolecular space. Following the annotation of the specific biological functions and utility of each protein (Fig. 4b), the CTD was employed to investigate the links between hub genes and a range of DVT-related diseases, including AS, cardiovascular diseases, PE, thrombosis and vascular diseases. This analysis was conducted to complement the investigation of protein–protein interactions. The significant correlation between JAK2, CD36, TLR7, TNFSF13B and PARP9 and the aforementioned conditions was highlighted and verified by the interference score within the CTD (Fig. 4c). The score chart demonstrates that CD36 exerts a considerable influence on a range of diseases associated with DVT, and is closely linked to a number of complications. Similarly, JAK2 is also strongly associated with above diseases. Analysis of scRNA-seq to identify cell localization

To gain further insight into the behaviour of the aforementioned idiosyncratic genes at the single-cell level during the pathogenesis of DVT, we conducted the scRNA-seq analysis on samples obtained from DVT patients and healthy subjects. Following the construction of the library, a total of 25,547 cells and 25,381 genes were identified through data comparison, filtering and statistical analysis. The violin plots illustrate the range of each cell and the individual RNA counts, thereby demonstrating the accuracy and standardisation of our sample analysis (Fig. 5a). To further elucidate this complexity, we apply linear dimensionality reduction to calculate the principal component (PC) in accordance with the established methodology. The elbow plot curve trend indicates that dims = 1:20 was selected for subsequent dimensionality reduction and clustering analysis (Fig. 5b). In the context of cell clustering, the efficacy of UMAP in differentiating between sample origins and groupings was substantiated through the implementation of quality control (QC) measures and the utilisation of visualisation techniques (Fig. 5c). Concurrently, in order to identify cell types with hub gene localization, a selection of at least 4 common marker genes for artificial cell annotation was made, thus delineating 7 cell types: neutrophil, natural killer (NK) cell, T cell, B cell, mast cell, M1-like macrophage and M2-like macrophage (Fig. 5d). The bubble plot was employed to illustrate the precision of cell annotation (Fig. 5e). The differences in cell distribution between the two groups were determined by Ro/e, which confirmed that T cells, B cells, and mast cells were in a depleted state, while neutrophils were significantly upregulated in DVT, and the changes in macrophages and NK cells were not particularly significant (Fig. 5f). In a similar manner, following the visual presentation of the actual proportion differences between cells using UMAP plots and bar plots (Fig. 5g and h), the expression patterns of the 5 hub genes identified by the PPI multi-network algorithm were examined. The findings revealed that JAK2, CD36, TNFSF13B and PARP9 exhibited significant upregulation in DVT, while TLR7 did not demonstrate a significant increase (Fig. 5i). It was observed that JAK2 was the most widely distributed in macrophages and mast cells, while CD36 was almost exclusively expressed in M2-like macrophages, and TNFSF13B was mainly expressed in neutrophils and macrophages. Analysis of cell–cell communications to identify signaling pathways

Following standard scRNA-seq analysis to determine the cellular localization of hub genes, GSEA was employed to investigate their primary biological pathways. Discussion

In this study, a comprehensive transcriptomic approach was employed to analyse the blood samples of 4 DVT patients and 6 healthy subjects. A total of 6 circRNAs, 5 miRNAs and 16 mRNAs were identified through a standard analysis procedure. Furthermore, a ceRNA network model of DVT was constructed, and the rationality of the aforementioned model was verified in an external validation set. The topological analysis of the PPI network using multiple algorithms identified 5 hub genes (including JAK2, CD36, TNFSF13B, TLR7 and PARP9), which were highly correlated. Furthermore, in order to ascertain the intricacy of its molecular structure, we employ AlphaFold 2 to fully leverage the predictive capacity of its protein's three-dimensional configuration. And CTD was employed to assess the interconnections between the aforementioned genes and their association with DVT and related disorders. Subsequently, scRNA-seq was conducted on blood samples from 3 DVT patients and 3 healthy subjects in accordance with standard protocols. This approach was instrumental in determining the cell localisation of gene expression and in exploring the related signalling pathways through the utilisation of cell communication algorithms. The present study provides an early theoretical direction for identifying effective targets for the prevention and treatment of DVT.

ncRNAs, which constitute over 90% of the human genomic RNA transcripts, lack protein-coding potential [39]. However, they are believed to play a pivotal role in regulating normal cellular functions and a multitude of diseases, including cancer [40]. Additionally, ncRNAs have been demonstrated to regulate gene expression at various levels, including epigenetic, transcriptional, and post-transcriptional. Since Salmena et al. first proposed the ceRNA hypothesis in 2011, an increasing number of studies have demonstrated that lncRNAs can regulate the expression of target mRNAs by competing for miRNAs [15, 41]. Most of the transcriptomic studies related to DVT focus on the regulatory relationship between miRNAs and mRNAs and the effects on endothelial cells (ECs). With the exception of the impact and regulatory role of the Crnde/miR-181a-5p/Pcyox1l axis on DVT, as proposed by Xin et al. in 2023, there is a paucity of research on the ceRNA network of DVT-related pathogenesis [42]. Our study identified a ceRNA network model, which represented an advance in the field of DVT. In addition, the above node genes are closely related to platelet activation in KEGG analysis, indicating the accuracy and predictability of the ceRNA model.

TNFSF13B is a member of the tumour necrosis factor (TNF) ligand family and plays a role in the regulation of glucocorticoids and transforming growth factor β (TGF-β) pathways by interacting with macrophage migration inhibitory factor (MIF) [43–46]. TNFSF13B is frequently expressed in B-cell line cells and functions as a highly efficacious B-cell activator [47]. As common cytokines, current research is focused on autoimmune diseases, including osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis (RA), which are also associated with the prediction of renal cell carcinoma [46, 48]. Furthermore, scRNA-seq analysis demonstrated that TNFSF13B was predominantly expressed by monocytes. It is also noteworthy that Yingyu et al. identified it by machine learning (ML) as a potential biomarker for systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) and metabolic syndrome (MetS), with high expression levels observed in monocytes [45]. Given that hsa-miR-3074-5p can also be employed as a biomarker for RA, the upstream and downstream regulatory relationship between the two also indicates its functional unity in the pathogenesis of DVT [49]. In the joint analysis of the ceRNA network model and PPI multiple algorithms, hsa_circ_0095124/hsa-miR-3074-5p/TNFSF13B was identified as a potential core regulatory pathway for the existence of DVT, which provides a foundation for subsequent in-depth research. However, when evaluating DVT-related diseases and complications according to the CTD, the interference score of TNFSF13B was not significant. Nevertheless, the functional diversity still demonstrated that TNFSF13B played a significant role in the occurrence and development of many related diseases as a hotspot gene. When considered within the wider scientific framework, the aforementioned findings lack a comprehensive account of DVT. However, they offer valuable insights that can inform the identification of potential research avenues.

In contrast to TNFSF13B, CD36 has a long history of being demonstrated to mediate the interaction between platelets and monocytes, tumour cells, and stromal cells [50]. Concurrently, CD36 functions as a class B scavenger receptor, playing a pivotal role in angiogenesis, AS, inflammation and lipid metabolism [51–53]. Moreover, it is expressed in a wide range of areas, including red blood cell precursors, platelets, monocytes/macrophages, and megakaryocytes [54]. This finding is in accordance with our analysis, and the strong association of CD36 with a range of vascular complication-related diseases (including AS, diabetes, cardiomyopathy, obesity, sickle cell anemia and malaria) suggests that there are additional unidentified markers of CD36 in the cardiovascular domain. In addition, PARPs (including PARP1, PARP2, PARP3, and PARP9) can also regulate lipid metabolism and lipid homeostasis, and have pathophysiological roles in the development of hyperlipidemia, AS, and cardiovascular aging [55, 56]. The potential association between the hub genes screened by various models and DVT-related diseases not only provides a physiological basis for exploring the complex mechanism of DVT, but also indicates the accuracy of theoretical analysis.

Furthermore, the comprehensive examination of the integration of AlphaFold 2 technology in DVT represents a significant advancement in theoretical analysis [57, 58]. This sophisticated protein structure prediction tool, developed by DeepMind, has transformed the field of structural biology [57, 59–61]. The utilisation of AlphaFold 2 in the field of DVT research establishes a precedent for prospective investigations into AS, PE and other associated vascular pathologies. It is evident that AlphaFold 2 has been demonstrated to enhance the precision of virtual screening and molecular dynamics simulations. This enhancement is achieved by elucidating the high-precision structure of proteins that are of critical importance to DVT. In view of the fact that TNFSF13B has been identified as a core target in a range of models and algorithms in this study, subsequent studies on its functional domains, target interaction sites, and potential drug binding mechanisms will further deepen the understanding of DVT regulatory pathways. It is anticipated that AlphaFold 2 will guide the development of immunomodulatory therapies by predicting the interaction interface between TNFSF13B and its receptors (BAFF-R/BCMA). On this basis, the latest advances in AlphaFold 3 related technologies, released in June 2024, pave the way for future research to achieve higher precision biomolecular modeling under a unified deep learning framework, as well as to create novel targeted therapeutic interventions and the design of corresponding drugs [57, 60, 62–64].

Currently, scRNA-seq is a widely utilized technique in numerous life science fields, including cell lineage analysis, measurement of cellular molecular state, and spatial reprogramming [23, 24, 26, 65]. As a prevalent transcriptome layering tool, scRNA-seq enables an unbiased assessment of cellular heterogeneity, identification of novel cell states and populations, and facilitates the identification of new or rare cell types [65–69]. In this study, scRNA-seq technology was utilised to analyse the mapping of immune cells (e.g. macrophages, T cells and B cells) in DVT, emphasising the depletion and infiltration status of diverse cell populations during the course of the disease. This approach unveiled the imbalance of pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory subpopulations. Furthermore, the dynamic changes of related core genes in the proportion process were analysed through cell localization and communication analysis, providing a "cell-molecule" resolution for the in-depth study of DVT and promoting translational research from basic to clinical. Additionally, the integration of scRNA-seq with multi-omics data enables the identification and screening of specific genes between different cell types in various vascular diseases, thus significantly enhancing our comprehension of DVT diagnosis, treatment and prognosis [26]. And these developments may inform clinical decision-making and contribute to advancements in precision medicine [24–26, 68, 70, 71].

The principal benefit of this study is the integrated examination of the entire transcriptome and scRNA-seq, along with the utilisation of the PPI algorithms, AlphaFold 2 and CTD score, to comprehensively investigate the molecular intricacies of genes at the system level. The application of joint research represents a promising avenue for further investigation. The combined approach has the potential to enhance our comprehension of the molecular landscape associated with DVT, facilitate the development of clinical indicators, assess the process of disease occurrence and prognosis, assist in clinical diagnosis, and thus inform clinical decision-making. It is important to note, however, that the research process was not without limitations. As a consequence of the restricted access to information on clinical samples, the data generated by this study were not validated in the follow-up of participants, and the association with clinical outcomes was not fully explored. Concurrently, the precision of the corresponding targets has not been ascertained through in vitro experimentation. Furthermore, the limited number of samples available for sequencing has been shown to result in instability and uncertainty with regard to the identification of target genes. This has necessitated the application of multicentre, large-scale sequencing data to fully elucidate the subtle genomic alterations associated with DVT. The conclusions of this study are exclusively employed as prediction outcomes, constituting a preliminary investigation into the intricacy of DVT-related molecules, thereby furnishing a foundation for subsequent research endeavours.

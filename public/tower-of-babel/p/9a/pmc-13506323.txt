Phase I, open-label, multi-center dose-finding and expansion study to investigate the safety, tolerability, and preliminary efficacy of CR-001 (5-FU-miR-15a) in patients with acute myeloid leukemia
Authors: Irini Ktoridou-Valen, Andrea Lenartova, Sturla Magnus Grøndal, Cara E. Wogsland, Adam S. Dowrick, Mark Wood, Knut T. Smerud, Nils Meland, Thomas Dahl, Andrew Fesler, Jingfang Ju, Bjørn T. Gjertsen
Published: 2026

Introduction

Therapeutic resistance is one of the major reasons for the refractoriness and relapse of acute myeloid leukemia (AML) [1]. Consequently, novel therapeutic options are urgently needed to improve patient outcomes and prolong AML patient survival. MicroRNAs (miR) have been extensively investigated as key epigenetic regulators in both solid tumors and hematological malignancies, associated with tumor aggressiveness and therapy resistance [2]. Several reports provide insights into the role of miR-15a in AML and the development of 5-FU-modified miRNAs as cancer therapeutics [3–7]. Knockout of both miR-15/16-1 and miR-15b/16-2 clusters drives the development of aggressive AML [4], and restoration of miR-15a exerts antitumor activity in several solid malignancies through suppression of oncogenic targets [5, 7] including BCL2, YAP1, WEE1, CHK1, and BMI1. Notably, BCL2 is a well-established miR-15a target in both CLL and AML [4, 5]. YAP1 has been implicated in doxorubicin resistance in AML [8], and combined suppression of CHK1 and WEE1 has demonstrated synergistic antileukemic effects in AML ex vivo [9]. Inhibition of BMI1 has been shown to induce p53-independent mitochondrial apoptosis in AML progenitor cells [10].

We hypothesized that the multi-targeted 5-FU-miR-15a (CR-001), (Supplementary Fig. 1) could provide a unique therapeutic advantage in suppressing pathways driving AML resistance and relapse. The 5-FU modification thus not only enhance the anticancer activity of the tumor suppressor miR-15a by increasing stability while maintain target specificity, but also facilitates cellular uptake of the miRNA mimic, contributing to a dual mechanism of action [7]. Once CR-001 breaks down by 5’- and 3’-exonucleases, it will release the chemotherapeutic agent 5-FU to exert its therapeutic activity by suppressing its target protein thymidylate synthase. The CR-001 formulation included a small amount of polyethyleneimine (PEI) as a delivery vehicle formulation to further enhance the therapeutic efficacy of miR-15a in AML. As PEI can be potentially toxic if given at higher doses, we designed the study to escalate the dose of CR-001 while keeping the dose of the delivery vehicle, PEI, constant at a low level.

Material and methods

Here, we report a multicenter, first-in-human, phase I, dose-finding clinical trial to investigate safety, tolerability, and preliminary efficacy of CR-001, using a standard 3 + 3 dose-escalation study design (Supplementary Fig. 2). (EudraCT number: 2021-006332-46). Eligible patients (N = 11, median age 74, median lines of therapy 3, 7/11 relapsed after allo-HSCT), enrolled between 15 August 2022 and 13 December 2023 and treated with a total of 66 doses of CR-001. The baseline characteristics of all participants are summarized in Supplementary Tables 1 and 2. There were 10 (91%) patients with relapsed/refractory AML and 1 (9%) patient with newly diagnosed AML considered as not being eligible for standard of care by Principal Investigator (PI). All patients had an adverse risk genetic profile (ELN 2022) [11], 5 (43%) patients had a TP53 mutation, and 6 (55%) patients had a complex karyotype. All patients who received at least one dose of CR-001 were included in the safety analysis, whereas only patients who received at least three doses were included in the efficacy analysis. The efficacy evaluation was performed one week after the last administered dose, and safety was evaluated 30 days post-treatment. Patients who demonstrated stable disease after the initial four weekly doses (Cycle 1) were eligible to receive up to four additional doses of CR-001.

Patients received intravenous (IV) administrations of CR-001 once weekly for four consecutive weeks at dose levels of 7.5 mg, 11.25 mg, 15 mg, or 18.75 mg per administration. PEI was co-administered at a fixed dose of 15 mg with the 7.5 mg CR-001 dose, and 20 mg with all higher dose levels (11.25 mg, 15 mg, 18.75 mg). Patients received premedication with oral dexamethasone 12–16 hours and 1 hour prior to infusion, as well as paracetamol, famotidine, and cetirizine one hour before infusion.

Blood and bone marrow samples for mass cytometry analysis were collected at set time points with some variation (Supplementary Fig. 3A). The mass cytometry panel (Supplementary Table 3) was designed to analyze the peripheral blood samples from patients treated with CR-001, including key surface markers for AML diagnosis and classification, detection of measurable residual disease (MRD), and 6 key targets of interest for CR-001 [12].

Tolerability

All 11 patients received at least one dose of CR-001 and were evaluated for tolerability and safety analysis. There were no dose-limiting toxicities observed. In general, CR-001 was well-tolerated and administered on an outpatient basis. Eight (73%) patients were in ECOG 0-1 performance status after 4 doses of CR-001, including 3 (27%) patients who improved their performance from ECOG 2. Hyperglycemia was common (7 pts, 64%) and was attributed to high dose dexamethasone premedication. None of the patients had hyperglycemia at follow-up safety visit 30 days after the last dose.

Treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) were observed in all patients receiving CR-001. Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) of all grades are shown in Tables 1A, B. The most frequent adverse event, irrespective of a relationship to CR-001 was infection (11 patients, 100%), and hyperglycemia (7 patients, 64%). Hyperglycemia was mild (CTCAE gr. 1) and transient, not requiring insulin treatment. Infusion-related reaction occurred in 6 (55%) patients. The symptoms occurred suddenly at the end of infusion or within 30 min after the infusion start. Shivering and tachycardia appeared in all 6 patients experiencing this event. Fever only once in one patient. No patients experienced hypotension or hypoxia. In all cases, the symptoms persisted for about 30 minutes and resolved completely. Patient 9 that experienced fever received additionally an interleukin-6 blocker, tocilizumab, as cytokine-release syndrome was suspected. This patient experienced similar symptoms, but without fever, persisting for 30 minutes after each of the 7 subsequent CR-001 doses and chills about 4 days post infusion. Only this patient 9 experienced infusion reaction more than once. The infusion reaction appeared at all dosing levels higher than 7.5 mg and was observed besides prolonged infusion administration time from 20 to 120 minutes. Preliminary efficacy

Preliminary efficacy was evaluable in 10 (91%) patients, receiving at least three infusions of CR-001. Six (60%) evaluable patients achieved stable disease (SD) (ELN 2022) after 3-4 CR-001 infusions (patients #2, 5, 6, 7, 9 and 11). Three of 6 patients with SD exhibited TP53 mutation. Two of these patients remained with stable disease after 8 infusions (patients #5 and 6). Bone marrow aspirate count reduction after 3-4 infusions was observed in 3 (30%) patients after 3-4 doses (patients #6, 7 and 9), and in one (10%) patient after 8 doses (patient #5). Progressive disease was noted in 4 out of 10 (40%) patients after 3-4 doses (Supplementary Table 2).

Patient 5 with breast cancer therapy-related AML and complex karyotype (NRAS VAF 48%, ASXL1 41%, TP53 23%) and relapse after a second allogeneic stem cell transplant experienced lasting regression of leukemic pericardial effusion after two doses, excellent performance status, and survived for 4.1 months after the last (16th) dosage of CR-001 at dose level 11.25 mg (Fig. 1A). Signs of granulopoiesis maturation/differentiation were observed in the bone marrow both morphologically on smear microscopy and immunophenotypic flowcytometry, correlating with increase/normalization of neutrophils in the blood. This patient initially received 8 weekly doses as part of the study, followed by additional 8 doses approved by Ethics Committee (EC) and Data and Safety Monitoring Board (DMSB). The patient received concomitant hydroxyurea during the study and entered an experimental trametinib treatment after disease progression (NRAS mutation). Single-cell profiling using mass cytometry was used to support observation of therapeutic efficacy. At the lowest dose level (7.5 mg), some AML blast clusters (day 14, Supplementary Fig. 4) showed signs of reduction miR-15a targets (e.g., BMI1, WEE1, MCL-1), but overall cluster trajectories varied, and multiple markers displayed increased intensity as this patient, patient 1, entered sepsis (day 46) (Fig. 1B, Supplementary Figs. 5 and 7). A more pronounced reduction in WEE1, BCL2, and MCL1 was observed at the higher dose level (11.25 mg, patient 7), (Fig. 1B, Supplementary Figs. 5 and 9); however, another patient (patient 6) at the same dose level (11.25 mg) did not reproduce the same reduction (Fig. 1B, Supplementary Figs. 5 and 8).

In summary, CR1-02 is the first-in-human phase I clinical trial based on tumor suppressor miR-15a in relapsed or refractory AML. CR-001 was well-tolerated and showed signals of biological activity as evidenced by single-cell protein profiling and disease stabilization. Based on its superior safety profile, CR-001 may have a potential as AML maintenance therapy.

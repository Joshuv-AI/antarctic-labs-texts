Protocol for deep phenotyping of human antigen-specific T cells across conventional and unconventional subsets by 22-color spectral flow cytometry
Authors: Jiahui Du, Mingzhe Zeng, Ziqian He, Xiang Li, Shuxun Liu, Zhiqing Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryAnalyzing antigen-specific T cells at the subset level is crucial for studying antiviral immunity and vaccine efficacy. We present a 22-color spectral flow cytometry protocol to characterize these cells from human PBMCs, covering conventional αβT and unconventional (MAIT, γδ T) subsets. We describe steps for PBMC thawing and plate setup, peptide stimulation, three-step surface staining, and intracellular staining. We then detail procedures for sample acquisition and Flowjo data analysis. This approach enables precise immunophenotyping with limited samples.

Before you begin

T cell responses are pivotal to adaptive immunity, critical for defense against pathogens and cancer immunotherapy. The T cell compartment exhibits profound functional and phenotypic heterogeneity, comprising both conventional αβT cells and unconventional T cell populations. As the central effectors of adaptive immunity, conventional αβT cells are primarily classified into CD4+ and CD8+T cell lineages. CD4+T cells exhibit substantial plasticity, differentiating into various helper subsets (Th1, Th2, Th17, Treg, Tfh) upon activation.1 Notably, a distinct subset—CD4+cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs)—has been identified.2,3,4 Recent studies further refined the classification of this subset by identifying a Granzyme K (GZMK)+CD4+CTL subset.5 Building on this, our work reveals that CD4+CTLs can be segregated into distinct maturation states according to their co-expression patterns of GZMK and GZMB, thereby underscoring an additional layer of heterogeneity within the CD4+T cell compartment. CD8+T cells, classically defined as CTLs, exhibit substantial heterogeneity. They are commonly categorized by CCR7 and CD45RA expression into naïve, central memory (TCM), effector memory (TEM), and TEMRA subsets.6 The TEM subset can be further stratified into four maturation stages based on the co-expression patterns of CD27 and CD28.7,8,9 Additionally, memory CD8+T cell heterogeneity is evidenced by distinct maturation states defined by differential expression of Granzymes (GZMA, GZMB, GZMK).10,11,12

Unconventional T cell subsets, such as mucosal-associated invariant T (MAIT) cells, invariant natural killer T (iNKT) cells, and γδ T cells, serve as key regulators in mediating microbial immunity, tumor immunity, and tissue homeostasis.13,14,15 Our initial experiments also revealed that both MAIT cells and γδ T cells produce IFN-γ when stimulated with peptide pools in vitro. However, the mechanisms that govern the activation of these innate-like lymphocytes and their interaction with conventional T cells remain a critical area of research. To accurately define conventional CD8+T cells, we excluded γδ T cells and MAIT cells (TCR Vα7.2+ CD161+) after gating on live CD3+ T cells.16

Antigen-specific T cells were identified based on CD69 and CD154 expression, as well as IFN-γ secretion.17,18,19 Given the substantial complexity within both CD4+ and CD8+T cell subsets, careful delineation of these populations is crucial for advancing our understanding of antigen-specific immune effector mechanisms. Thus, we developed a comprehensive 22-color flow cytometry panel to characterize SARS-CoV-2 Spike protein S1-subunit(S1)-specific T cells across various subsets of T cells in human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs). Furthermore, this protocol can be adapted for the analysis of antigen-specific T cell responses to other pathogens by replacing the SARS-CoV-2 S1 peptide pool with corresponding pathogen-specific peptide pools.

To ensure optimal resolution, bright fluorochromes were paired with markers of low antigen density, whereas markers with high co-expression potential were assigned to dim fluorochromes. Table 1 summarizes the 22-color panel, including each marker, its assigned fluorochrome, its purpose, and the antigen density.20 This multicolor panel was rigorously optimized using compensation beads, with the Spillover Spreading matrix (SSM) performance metrics detailed in Table S1. To ensure precision, all antibodies were titrated by dilution series to obtain optimal staining efficiency (see Figure S1). Innovation

In this study, we developed a 22-color panel to comprehensively detect antigen-specific cells across various subsets of CD4+T cells, CD8+T cells, MAIT cells, and γδ T cells in human PBMCs stimulated with SARS-CoV-2 S1 subunit-derived peptides. The inclusion of GZMB and GZMK as key functional markers in our panel allows for the systematic tracking of the differentiation and functional evolution of antigen-specific T cells, thereby offering a more precise and comprehensive tool to investigate T cell immunity and to support vaccine development and mechanistic studies.

Sample source

We enrolled individuals with Omicron BF.7 breakthrough infections who had completed a three-dose regimen of inactivated SARS-CoV-2 vaccines (CoronaVac [Sinovac] or BBIBP-CorV [Sinopharm]). Peripheral blood samples were collected during the convalescent phase, 30 days post-infection. PBMCs were isolated from these volunteers by Ficoll density gradient centrifugation and were cryopreserved at a concentration of 5 × 106 cells/mL in fetal bovine serum (FBS) containing 7.5% Dimethyl Sulfoxide (DMSO).22,23 Each vial, containing 1 mL of aliquot, was first placed in a controlled-rate freezing container and stored at −80°C for 12h. For long-term preservation, the cells were subsequently transferred to and stored in liquid nitrogen.

Stimulation for PBMC with S1 peptide pool

Timing: 25 h

Timing: 25 h

This step involves blocking the CD40-CD40L interaction with an anti-CD40 antibody, followed by stimulation with S1 peptide pools and the addition of Brefeldin A to facilitate subsequent intracellular cytokine staining. Expected outcomes

We developed a comprehensive 22-color flow cytometry panel to characterize antigen-specific T cells in human PBMCs. The panel encompasses CD4+T cell subsets, CD8+T cell subsets, γδ T cells, and MAIT cells. The panel categorizes CD4+T cells into Th1, Th2, Th17, Tfh, and three CD4+CTL subsets based on the expression of GZMK and GZMB (Figure 3B). Furthermore, CD8+ T cells were classified into naïve, central memory (TCM), GZMB-GZMK- TEM, GZMK+ TEM, GZMB+GZMK+ TEM, GZMB+ TEM, and TEMRA populations (Figure 3E). This panel enables the analysis of antigen-specific T cells (CD69+CD154+) not only within the total CD4+T cell compartment but also across individual CD4+T cell subsets, as well as the identification of IFN-γ production within Th1 and CD4+CTL populations (Figures 3C and 3D). In contrast, CD154 is not expressed by activated CD8+T cells; therefore, antigen-specific CD8+ T cells were identified using CD69 and IFN-γ rather than CD154 (Figures 3F and S8). Similarly, activated γδ T cells and MAIT cells were identified based on both CD69 expression and IFN-γ production (Figure 3A).

Based on our analysis of PBMCs from individuals with Omicron BF.7 breakthrough infections (30 days post-infection), the frequencies of S1-specific CD4+ T cells (CD154+CD69+) typically range from 0.05% to 1.5% following stimulation, while CD8+ T cells (IFN-γ+CD69+) range from 0.05% to 0.5%. Among unconventional T cell subsets, IFN-γ+CD69+ MAIT cells and IFN-γ+CD69+ γδ T cells each constitute approximately 0.5–3% of their respective parent populations. Given the low frequency of antigen-specific T cells, combining magnetic enrichment with the 22-color panel described here could further enhance sensitivity and enable more in-depth profiling of antigen-specific T cell responses.30

Limitations

Despite exhibiting moderate stability, the PE-Fire810 conjugate was included in the panel due to its compelling fluorescence intensity and reliable staining under well-controlled protocols. Subsequent iterations of the panel could improve by exchanging the CCR7 marker for a fluorochrome with enhanced stability and brightness.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

In some PBMC samples, cell clumping was observed after thawing. Such clumping can result in significant cell loss and may adversely affect cell function and/or phenotype (PBMC thawing and plate setup, step 7).

Potential solution

To address this, clumps in these samples were treated with DNase for 10 min, thereby improving cell recovery and preserving cellular integrity for downstream applications.

Problem 2

For channels under the same laser of the ID7000, the PMT array changes voltage simultaneously during adjustment. When enhancing weak signals, some channels with stronger signals may exceed the detection range (sample acquisition, step 26).

Potential solution

Based on pre-experimental expression data, we titrate down antibodies against highly expressed markers. This optimized titration reduces fluorescence intensity to fit the instrument’s detection range while preserving sufficient signal for accurate population quantification and preventing signal saturation.

Problem 3

When co-stained with other surface markers, the resolution for detecting CD28, γδTCR, and chemokine receptors is often suboptimal (Figure 4) (three-step surface staining, step 16).Figure 4Comparison of one-step versus three-step surface staining for PBMC subset resolutionAssessment of cell population resolution in human PBMCs: (A) three-step vs. (B) one-step surface marker staining protocols.

Comparison of one-step versus three-step surface staining for PBMC subset resolution

Assessment of cell population resolution in human PBMCs: (A) three-step vs. (B) one-step surface marker staining protocols.

Potential solution

The three-step extracellular staining strategy was implemented to resolve this problem: CD28 and γδTCR required initial isolation due to significant resolution degradation when co-stained with other surface markers. Although chemokine receptors (CXCR5, CXCR3, CCR7, CCR6) are conventionally stained first, we found that staining them after CD28/γδTCR in Step 2 achieved comparable resolution. Remaining surface antibodies demonstrated stable performance in the final step.

Problem 4

The instability of the PE-Fire810 tandem dye. We observed spectral shifts and increased spillover into the PE channel when samples were stained for extended periods in Perm wash buffer at 4°C (Figure 5) (three-step surface staining, step 17).Figure 5Stability assessment of PE-Fire810 under varying Perm wash buffer incubation times

Stability assessment of PE-Fire810 under varying Perm wash buffer incubation times

Potential solution

Based on the instability of PE-Fire810 in perm wash buffer, we standardized the intracellular staining duration to 2 h at 25°C. These conditions ensure optimal antibody staining efficacy while preserving the stability of the PE-Fire 810 tandem dye.

Problem 5

The low abundance of antigen-specific T cells presents a challenge for precise gating (data analysis by Flowjo, step 32 and 34).

Potential solution

To address the challenge of accurately gating rare antigen-specific T cell populations, we employed both unstimulated samples (see Figure S7) and fluorescence-minus-one (FMO) controls (see Figure S9) to validate our gating strategy. These complementary approaches enabled robust discrimination between negative and positive populations, particularly for low-frequency events such as CD154+ and IFNγ+ cells.

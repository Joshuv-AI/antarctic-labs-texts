Impact of Junior Physicians’ Resignation on Palliative Sedation Practices: A Retrospective Single-Center Study in South Korea
Authors: Moonki Hong, Si Won Lee, Minkyu Jung
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study aimed to evaluate changes in the proportion of patients receiving palliative sedation (PS) before and after the mass resignation of junior physicians in South Korea.MethodsThis single-center retrospective chart review included patients who died between February 19, 2023 and August 18, 2024. We retrospectively identified patients who were prescribed sedatives such as midazolam outside the intensive care unit on the day of death and classified them as having received PS. Data were extracted from electronic medical records. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test, while continuous variables were compared using the Student’s t-test.ResultsIn the year before resignation, 78 of the 745 deceased patients (10.5%) received PS; whereas in the subsequent six months, 24 of the 237 patients (10.1%) received PS. No statistically significant difference was observed in the proportion of patients receiving PS between the two periods.ConclusionThe proportion of patients receiving PS did not show a significant difference, despite the disruption caused by the mass resignation of junior physicians.Keywords: Terminal care, Palliative care, Hypnotics and sedatives

INTRODUCTION

Patients with terminal cancer often experience various distressing symptoms in the days before death; including dyspnea, pain, delirium, and decreased consciousness [1]. In many cases, these symptoms persist despite best efforts and cause significant distress at the end of life (EOL). To alleviate this refractory distress, palliative sedation (PS) is administered to reduce the patient’s level of consciousness [2].

In early 2024, a major healthcare crisis emerged in South Korea as many medical residents suddenly resigned due to the government’s increase in medical school student quotas without sufficient consensus. This unprecedented event disrupted the clinical care system of university hospitals, where most Korean cancer patients are treated [3,4]. As a result, attending physicians were forced to take sole responsibility for patient management; including first-line decision-making, arranging admission and discharge, and managing outpatient clinics. This may have influenced clinical practice, particularly the care of patients in their terminal phase of life.

The use of PS varies widely across countries and care settings. A recent multi-institutional study in Korea reported that approximately 16% of patients received PS; whereas international studies showed a broader range, from 10% to 66.7% [5-8]. Given that PS use is shaped by institutional, cultural, and clinical environments; it is plausible that the healthcare system disruptions caused by mass resignation may have influenced decisions regarding PS.

Nationwide medical resident resignations in 2024 caused disruptions across multiple areas of clinical care, including hospice services. This study aimed to assess whether the use of PS was influenced during this period. In this context, we sought to determine whether the PS rate during admission among deceased patients changed before and after the junior physicians resigned. This could serve as an indicator of how actively the remaining staff members continued to provide EOL care under strained conditions. This study explored these possibilities through a retrospective analysis of PS practices during a defined period of systemic change. Furthermore, this study addresses an ethical concern. During the nationwide resignation, public discourse in South Korea often questioned whether doctors’ professional integrity might be compromised under systemic pressure [9]. By examining PS, a practice inherently sensitive to ethical considerations, we aimed to provide concrete data on whether physicians maintained consistent standards of care. Demonstrating stability in such a practice has important value in reassuring patients, families, and society that ethical obligations are preserved, even in times of crisis.

METHODS

1. Study design

This was a retrospective, single-institution study that explored the changes in palliative sedation before and after the mass resignation by junior physicians.

2. Data collection

The Institutional Review Board of Severance Hospital approved this retrospective study involving deceased patients (IRB No. 4-2024-1306) and the requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study. Mass resignations by junior physicians began on February 19, 2024. We retrospectively collected data on patients discharged from the Division of Medical Oncology, Department of Internal Medicine, Yonsei Cancer Center, between February 19, 2023 and August 18, 2024. The study period was divided into two groups: (1) before resignation, from February 19, 2023 to February 18, 2024 and (2) after resignation, from February 19, 2024 to August 18, 2024. We selected this six-month timeframe because it represented the acute phase immediately following the mass resignation, when the disruption to clinical practice was expected to be most pronounced.

We collected data on patient characteristics; including age, sex, diagnosis, and treatment lines. The primary symptoms causing PS initiation were reviewed using electronic medical records. The duration of PS, defined as the time from sedative administration to death, and the type of medication used were recorded. Following the mass resignation, the total number of patients with cancer hospitalized at tertiary hospitals decreased markedly. Therefore, we focused on comparing the proportion of patients who received PS among all in-hospital deaths rather than crude patient numbers.

PS at EOL was defined as the administration of intravenous sedatives to relieve intolerable symptoms. The sedatives included benzodiazepines (midazolam), barbiturates (phenobarbital), and propofol. Sedatives used for endoscopic procedures and to alleviate insomnia, anxiety, and seizures were excluded from the definition of PS. Although Severance Hospital does not have a fully standardized written protocol for palliative sedation, midazolam is generally used as the first-line agent, with careful dose titration to achieve adequate symptom control. The dose is titrated as needed, and deep sedation was induced when clinically required. Phenobarbital or propofol may be considered as salvage medications if adequate sedation cannot be achieved with midazolam alone.

Data of patients who received midazolam in the Intensive Care Unit (ICU) and subsequently died were collected in this study, but were excluded from the analysis.

3. Statistical analysis

Categorical variables, including the proportion of PS between the two periods, were compared using the chi-square test. Continuous variables were compared using the Student’s t-test. A two-sided P-value of <0.05 was considered significant. Statistical analyses were performed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS version 27.0; SPSS Software, IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

RESULTS

1. Study population

Between February 19, 2023 and August 18, 2024, a total of 982 patients died in the Division of Oncology, Department of Internal Medicine, Yonsei Cancer Center. Before resignation, 745 patients died and 78 underwent PS. After resignation, 237 patients died, and 24 received PS.

2. Comparison between before and after resignation

No significant difference in the proportion of patients who underwent PS was detected between the two periods (P=0.880). Deaths occurring in the ICU were 59 of 745 (7.9%) before resignation, and 17 of 237 (7.2%) after resignation; showing no significant difference between the two periods (P=0.781; Table 1).

The most frequent indication was dyspnea, which was reported in 67 of 78 patients (85.9%) before resignation and 22 of 24 patients (91.7%) after resignation. Pain and bleeding were the next most common indications. Other indications for PS initiation included septic shock, acute kidney injury, altered mental status, and disseminated intravascular coagulation.

3. Duration of PS

The duration from PS initiation to death was analyzed for both groups. The mean duration before and after the resignation period was 42.9 hours and 53.0 hours, respectively; and no statistically significant difference was observed. The median duration of PS was shorter after resignation (15.3 [interquartile range {IQR}, 7.3~38.1] vs 30.1 hours [IQR, 7.7~59.3], P=0.485), although this difference was not statistically significant. DISCUSSION

No significant difference was observed in the proportion of patients who underwent PS before and after the resignation of junior physicians. Approximately 10% of terminally ill patients underwent PS during both periods, indicating no clear evidence of change. Notably, this rate is lower than that reported in previous multicenter studies in Korea, which have shown wide institutional variations, ranging from 7.0% to 49.7%. However, given the single-center nature of this study, these findings should be interpreted with caution.

During the pre-resignation period, some patients died before ICU admission and some were scheduled for PS but died before PS could be initiated. ICU beds were unavailable owing to full occupancy, resulting in delayed admission of patients requiring intensive care. Additionally, some patients were intended to receive PS but died before administration owing to delays in the prescription process. These findings may reflect the excessive workload faced by the residents before their resignations.

Unlike previous studies [8] that identified delirium as the most common indication for PS, dyspnea emerged as the leading indication in our study. This discrepancy likely reflects institutional practices. In this study, however, antipsychotic agents, such as haloperidol, were actively used for delirium management, and PS was reserved for cases with refractory symptoms or when new distressing symptoms emerged. This practice pattern likely accounted for the lower frequency of delirium as a documented indication in our data. Consequently, the electronic medical records may not fully capture delirium as a primary factor. In addition, the incidence of lung cancer was proportionally higher than that of other cancer types, which may explain why dyspnea was the most frequent indication.

Consistent with previous studies that reported a median PS duration of approximately one day [10,11], we measured the duration from the initiation of PS to the time of death in hours. There was no statistically significant difference in PS duration between the two periods. Although the mean duration was slightly longer in the post-resignation group, the median duration was longer in the pre-resignation group. This reinforces the findings of earlier research suggesting that PS duration is inherently variable and unpredictable [12].

Finally, the proportion of patients who died in the ICU was examined. The proportion of patients who died in the ICU did not differ significantly before and after physicians’ resignation (7.9% vs. 7.2%). Together with the stable rate of palliative sedation, this finding suggests that the overall end-of-life care patterns remained consistent across the two periods.

Beyond numerical comparisons, the stable pattern of palliative sedation observed during the resignation period suggests that end-of-life care practices were maintained despite systemic disruption, providing objective reassurance regarding the continuity of patient-centered care.

This study has several limitations. First, the absence of a statistically significant difference should not be interpreted as definitive evidence of no effect but rather as an indication that no clear change was detected within the scope of this dataset. Second, we did not analyze the specific medications or techniques used during palliative sedation. This was due to the institutional practice based on a standardized protocol, which limited the variability in sedation approaches. Although a more detailed analysis of sedation methods could provide additional insights, this was beyond the scope of the present study. Finally, the post-resignation period covered only six months. Although this may have not captured the long-term changes in practice, focusing on the immediate aftermath of the resignation allowed us to examine the most acute phase of systemic stress.

Despite these limitations, this is the first study to investigate changes in PS practices before and after mass resignation of junior physicians. Our findings provide valuable insight. Unfortunately, many young physicians have not yet returned to clinical practice and the resulting shortage of medical personnel continues to pose a significant challenge to the healthcare system in Korea [13]. We hope that this study offers reassurance to clinicians caring for terminally ill patients, and helps to alleviate uncertainty and concern among patients and families during this time of instability.

MiR-454-3p regulates high glucose-induced mesothelial-mesenchymal transition and glycolysis in peritoneal mesothelial cells by targeting STAT3
Authors: Nan Li, Jiao Fu, Qiufeng Wang, Qingqing Rao, Ling Yao, Xiaoqi Shao, Pei Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe quality of life of patients receiving long-term peritoneal dialysis (PD) is significantly impacted by the onset of peritoneal fibrosis (PF), and one of the pathological changes is mesothelial-mesenchymal transition (MMT). In this study, we investigated the potential roles of miR-454-3p and signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) in the progression of peritoneal MMT and the underlying mechanisms.MethodsPeritoneums were collected to detect morphology via hematoxylin-eosin staining and differentially expressed miRNAs were detected via RT-qPCR. PD effluent-derived cell populations in the peritoneal cavity were isolated from the effluents of 20 PD patients to determine miR-454-3p, STAT3, and MMT markers via Western blotting and RT-qPCR. The relationship between miR-454-3p and STAT3 was examined via a dual-luciferase reporter assay. Western blotting and RT-qPCR were utilized to evaluate the expression of STAT3, MMT markers, and glycolytic enzymes. Immunofluorescence staining revealed the localization and expression of MMT markers and STAT3.ResultsMiR-454-3p was downregulated in the peritoneums and PD effluent-derived cell populations of long-term PD patients. High glucose (HG) treatment promoted HMrSV5 cell MMT and glycolysis. MiR-454-3p overexpression alleviated HG-induced MMT and suppressed the expression of STAT3 and glycolytic enzymes. In contrast, the miR-454-3p inhibitor exacerbated HG-induced MMT and promoted the expression of glycolytic enzymes and STAT3. Moreover, STAT3 was the target of miR-454-3p.ConclusionsThis study demonstrated the protective role of miR-454-3p in HG-induced MMT and glycolysis in HMrSv5 cells, suggesting that miR-454-3p may prevent MMT by suppressing glycolytic enzymes via the STAT3/PFKFB3 pathway in the HG environment.

Introduction

Kidney failure and its complications represent a serious global public health problem. Peritoneal dialysis (PD), an alternative treatment option for kidney failure patients, can slow the residual renal function loss rate [1, 2]. The use of PD has been increasing annually worldwide in recent years [3]. However, when PD is performed, the peritoneum is exposed to biologically incompatible dialysis fluid, which causes fibrosis, angiogenesis, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) [4]. The mechanism of these processes is very complicated. Long-term PD therapy generally results in peritoneal fibrosis (PF), which eventually causes ultrafiltration failure and PD termination [5].

MicroRNAs (miRNAs), a type of non-coding RNA, are related to the pathogenesis of PF and may serve as potential therapeutic targets and diagnostic markers in the future [6–9]. Owing to miRNAs binding to the 3′-UTR of target mRNAs at the gene level, target mRNAs are degraded and translation is suppressed. These 19–25 bp, highly conserved, post-transcriptionally regulated, endogenous non-coding single-stranded RNAs regulate EMT during PF [10–14]. Recent studies have confirmed that miRNAs, such as miR-454-5p, enhance tumor growth and are linked to a poor prognosis in patients with breast cancer [15]. MiR-454-3p can also limit tumor progression by attenuating the EMT in nasopharyngeal carcinoma and neuroblastoma [16, 17]. Mesothelial-mesenchymal transition (MMT) is a cell-specific form of EMT in mesothelial cells. Inhibiting excessive glycolysis greatly reduces MMT in two types of mesothelial cells [18, 19]. Nevertheless, how miR-454-3p functions in the MMT process of PF is still unclear.

Signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3), a pivotal signaling protein, regulates organ fibrosis including in the kidney, lung, and liver [20–22], and is associated with EMT [23]. Suppressing STAT3 reduces high glucose (HG)-induced mesothelial cell EMT by decreasing TGF-β and inhibiting advanced glycation end-products (AGEs) and methylglyoxal (MGO) formation [24]. The role of STAT3 in glycolysis has also been confirmed. Activated by IL-6, STAT3 upregulates the expression of 6-phosphofructo-2-kinase/fructose-2,6-bisphosphatase-3 (PFKFB3) and hexokinase 2, thus enhancing glycolysis [25]. PD fluid-exposed mesothelial cells undergo excessive glycolysis, increasing energy consumption, cell transformation, and proliferation [19]. Our previous study revealed that in HMrSV5 cells incubated with high glucose, reducing STAT3 expression decreased glycolytic enzyme levels [18]. Moreover, the PFKFB3 inhibitor 3PO decreased EMT-related marker expression in high glucose-induced HMrSV5 cells [18]. In our prior work, using the online prediction tool TargetScan.org to predict 14 miRNAs that target STAT3 mRNA, only miR-454-3p was downregulated in human peritoneal mesothelial cells incubated with AGEs [26]. Thus, we were interested in investigating the effect and mechanism of miR-454-3p in MMT and glycolysis during PD.

In this work, the role of miR-454-3p in the process of peritoneal fibrosis was elucidated, along with the interaction between miR-454-3p and STAT3. MiR-454-3p expression was analyzed in PD effluent-derived cell populations, and its relationship with STAT3 and MMT markers was evaluated. Furthermore, we used a miR-454-3p mimic and inhibitor to observe whether changes in miR-454-3p can alter glycolysis and MMT and its association with STAT3. We suggest that miR-454-3p may prevent MMT by suppressing glycolytic enzymes via the STAT3/PFKFB3 pathway in the HG environment.

Materials and methods

Tissue collection

The peritoneums were collected from the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University. Excess peritoneal tissues removed during abdominal surgery were selected as the control group. Excess peritoneal tissues in the PD group were taken from continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis (CAPD) patients who had their peritoneal dialysis tubes removed (all treated for more than 3 years). The indications for PD catheter removal are peritoneal ultrafiltration failure, decreased dialysis efficiency, or transfer to hemodialysis. Patients with a history of peritonitis and long-term use of hormones, cytotoxic drugs, NSAIDs, and immunosuppressants were excluded. All patients gave verbal informed consent.

Hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) staining

Following standard protocols, the peritoneums were fixed, dehydrated, embedded, and then cut into 4-µm-thick paraffin sections. After dewaxing and rehydrating, the sections were stained with hematoxylin for 5 minutes and eosin for 8 seconds and then dehydrated. Images were captured with a Leica DM6B upright fluorescence microscope (Leica, Hessen, Germany).

Peritoneal equilibrium test (PET)

An overnight exchange of 2.5% dextrose dialysate was carried out using the standard dwell volume of patients with PD. After the peritoneal cavity had been evacuated for more than 20 min, 2.5% dextrose dialysate was subsequently added. Ten milliliters of effluent was collected and tested for glucose, urea, and creatinine levels four hours later.

PD effluent-derived cell populations in the peritoneal cavity

Peritoneal lavage fluid was collected from patients with CAPD overnight (8 h) to evaluate the miR-454-3p expression levels. PD effluent-derived cell populations in the peritoneal cavity were isolated via centrifugation as previously reported [7]. This research included 20 patients with PD. All patients were between the ages of 18 and 70 years. The PD-Start group consisted of the 10 newly recruited patients who had been receiving CAPD for less than half a year, whereas the PD > 3 years group consisted of the ten patients who had been receiving CAPD for over 3 years. Patients with a history of abdominal infection or cancer were excluded. Patients with congestive heart failure, more than 3 months of hemodialysis before PD, or long-term use of hormones, cytotoxic drugs, NSAIDs, or immunosuppressants were also excluded. All patients provided verbal informed consent.

Cell culture

The HMrSV5 cell line (JNO-H0632) was obtained from Zishi Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) and maintained at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2 in MEM (Gibco, CA, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (WISENT, Nanjing, China). Glucose was supplemented into the MEM to simulate the in vitro HG-peritoneal dialysis fluid (PDF) environment. The glucose concentrations in the MEM were 84 mM, 138 mM, and 236 mM, which are equivalent to those of the hypertonic PD solutions currently used clinically for PD (Dianeal 1.5%, Dianeal 2.5%, and Dianeal 4.25%, respectively). AGEs at a concentration of 50 μg/mL were also used in our experiment. Five microliters of AGEs protein (Bioss, Beijing, China, bs-1158P, 10 mg/mL) was added to 995 μL of MEM, thereby establishing a final concentration of AGEs of 50 μg/mL.

Cell transfection

In accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions, HMrSV5 cells were transfected with the miR-454-3p mimics, the miR-454-3p inhibitor, the miR-454-3p negative control (NC), the miR-454-3p inhibitor negative control (NC), or the STAT3 overexpression plasmid (GenePharma, Shanghai, China) via the Lipofectamine 2000 reagent (Invitrogen Life Technologies, CA, USA). After 6 h of incubation, the serum-free Opti-MEM (Gibco, CA, USA) was replaced with a complete culture medium with or without the 236 mM glucose treatment. Before gene expression analysis or further processing, the cells were cultured for 48 h.

Western blotting

A RIPA buffer (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) containing 2% ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA), 2% protease inhibitor, and 2% phosphatase inhibitor (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) was used to extract total protein. To quantify the protein concentration of the cell supernatant, a BCA protein assay kit (EpiZyme Biotechnology, Shanghai, China) was used. The cell lysate was then separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto a PVDF membrane (Millipore, MA, USA). Following an hour of blocking at room temperature in protein-free rapid block buffer (EpiZyme Biotechnology, Shanghai, China), the primary antibodies were incubated with the PVDF membranes overnight at 4 °C according to the following dilutions: rabbit anti-E-cadherin (1:1000, AF0131, Affinity, OH, USA), rabbit anti-α-SMA (1:2000, A17910, Abclonal, Wuhan, China), rabbit anti-fibronectin (1:1000, 15613-1-AP, Proteintech, Wuhan, China), mouse anti-STAT3 (1:1000, 9139, Cell Signaling Technology, MA, USA), rabbit anti-PFKFB3 (1:1000, 13763-1-AP, Proteintech, Wuhan, China), rabbit anti- pyruvate kinase M2 (PKM2, 1:1000, AF5234, Affinity, OH, USA), rabbit anti-lactate dehydrogenase A (LDHA, 1:5000, 21799-1-AP, Proteintech, Wuhan, China), and mouse anti-β-actin (1:10000, 66009-1-Ig, Proteintech, Wuhan, China). The next day, after three 10-min washes with TBST (EpiZyme Biotechnology, Shanghai, China, TF103), the membranes were treated with the corresponding secondary antibody for 45 min at room temperature. After washing, the protein signals were captured by a chemiluminescent detection system (Tanon, Shanghai, China). ImageJ for Windows 64 was used to measure the intensity of each band, and the data were normalized against β-actin.

Real-time fluorescence quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)

Using the HiPure Total RNA Mini Kit (Magen, Guangzhou, China), total RNA and miRNA were extracted. To detect the relative mRNA expression, total RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA (Yeasen, Shanghai, China, 11141ES60). The QuantStudioTM 6 Flex Real-Time PCR System (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA) was subsequently utilized to measure the relative level of mRNA via reagents (Yeasen, Shanghai, China, 11202ES08). Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China) created and synthesized the primer pairs used in the experiment. The expression levels of the miRNAs were tested via reagents designed by Yeasen (Shanghai, China, 11139ES60, 11170ES08). The mRNA levels in each sample were normalized against those of β-actin, while U6 snRNA was used as the reference gene of the miRNAs. The relative level of each gene was calculated via the 2−△△CT method, based on the mean CT value. The primer sequences are displayed in Table 1.

Dual‐luciferase reporter assay

Renilla luciferase served as the reference reporter gene and firefly luciferase served as the primary reporter gene in the psiCHECK2 vector (Promega, WI, USA). The wild-type (wt) or mutated (mut) binding sites for the 3′-UTR of STAT3 mRNA were subsequently cloned and inserted into the psiCHECK2 vector. Then, using the Lipofectamine 2000 reagent, the miR-454-3p mimic or NC mimic was co-transfected into the HEK-293T cells with the luciferase reporter vectors. Six hours later, the fresh complete medium was replaced for the next experiment. To assess the luciferase activities in the cells, a dual-luciferase reporter gene assay system kit (Promega, WI, USA) was used after transfection for 24 h. The results were visualized with the multifunctional enzyme marker Gen5 (PerkinElmer, MA, USA). Renilla luciferase activity was used to normalize the firefly luciferase activity.

Immunofluorescence staining

The HMrSV5 cells were seeded onto 18-mm coverslips and then fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (Biosharp, Hefei, China) for 30 min. After three five-min washes with PBS, the cells were blocked in 10% goat serum (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) for 1 h at 37 °C, and then incubated with the primary antibody at 4 °C overnight. The next day, the cells were washed with PBS, and all steps were subsequently performed away from light. After incubation with the corresponding secondary antibodies for 1 h at 37 °C, the cells were stained with DAPI (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) for 5 min. With a Leica DM6B upright fluorescence microscope (Leica, Hessen, Germany), images were analyzed and captured.

Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP)

Following the instructions of the Pierce TM Classic Magnetic IP/Co-IP Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA, 88804), the HMrSV5 cells were inoculated into a six-well plate and divided into three groups. Once the density of the cells reached 80%, 300 μL of IP lysis/wash buffer was added to each well to lyse the cells on ice for 5 min. After the lysates were collected and centrifuged for 10 min at 13,000 × g, the supernatants were retained. One supernatant containing total protein was selected as the ‘Input’ group. The other two supernatants were incubated with 7 μL of anti-PFKFB3 antibody or goat anti-rabbit IgG overnight at 4 °C, and the total volume was 500 μL with IP lysis/wash buffer. The next day, 25 μL of protein A/G magnetic beads was added to an EP tube. After washing twice, the beads were collected with a magnetic stand. The beads were subsequently incubated with antigen-antibody complex at room temperature for 1 h. Next, the beads were washed twice with IP lysis/wash buffer and with purified water once, then the beads were incubated with 100 μL of elution buffer and 10 μL of neutralization buffer for 10 min to elute the antigen-antibody complex. Finally, the supernatants were retained, and the sample loading buffer was added. The interaction between STAT3 and PFKFB3 was subsequently identified via Western blotting.

Statistical analysis

SPSS 27.0.1 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL) was used for the statistical analysis. The data are presented as the means ± standard deviations (SDs), and statistical significance was measured via one-way ANOVA or an unpaired t-test (GraphPad Prism, version 8.0.2; San Diego, CA, USA). p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

MiR-454-3p was significantly downregulated in the peritoneal tissues and PD effluent-derived cell populations from patients with long-term PD

The results of HE staining revealed that patients in the PD group had thickened submesothelial areas of peritoneal tissues and exhibited significant fibrosis compared with those in the control group (Figure 1A). RT-qPCR was used to measure the differential expression of 14 miRNAs targeting STAT3 in the peritoneal tissues of the two groups, and miR-454-3p was found to be most markedly downregulated in the long-term PD group (Figure 1B). Therefore, we chose miR-454-3p for the following study. In cell populations isolated from PD patient effluents, we analyzed the changes in miR-454-3p and MMT-related biomarkers. There were no obvious differences in clinical indicators, including sex, age, BMI, and D/PCr (4 h), between the short-term and long-term PD groups in the peritoneal equilibrium test (Table 2). The level of miR-454-3p tended to decrease as the duration of dialysis increased, according to RT-qPCR (Figure 1C). Western blotting and RT-qPCR analysis revealed that E-cadherin expression levels decreased significantly with increasing duration of dialysis, whereas α-SMA and fibronectin (FN) expression levels increased. Compared with those in the PD-Start group, the STAT3 levels in the PD > 3 years group were greater at both the RNA and protein levels (Figure 1D–F).

MiR-454-3p was downregulated in HG-induced MMT, whereas STAT3 was upregulated

The HMrSV5 cells were stimulated with different glucose concentrations (84 mM, 136 mM, and 236 mM) for 48 h. We used Western blotting to detect classic MMT markers including FN, α-SMA, and E-cadherin. Following the high glucose treatment in the HMrSV5 cells, the protein levels changed in a concentration-dependent manner, manifested as a decrease in E-cadherin and increase in α-SMA and FN. The effect was most pronounced at 236 mM (Figure 2A, B). Following the incubation of the HMrSV5 cells with 236 mM glucose for different durations, the accumulation of α-SMA and FN and lack of E-cadherin were most significant at 48 h (Figure 2C, D). The data revealed that the glucose treatment increased the STAT3 protein level in a time- and dose-dependent manner. To eliminate the influence of different stimulating conditions, the HMrSV5 cells were cultured with 50 μg/mL AGEs or 236 mM glucose for 48 h, and RT-qPCR was used to determine the expression levels of miR-454-3p. Both conditions resulted in the downregulation of miR-454-3p, demonstrating that it could negatively modulate peritoneal fibrosis (Figure 2E).

MiR-454-3p targeted the 3’-UTR of STAT3

To investigate whether there was a connection between miR-454-3p and STAT3, we transfected the miR-454-3p mimic or NC mimic into the HMrSV5 cells. First, we used RT-qPCR to evaluate the transfection efficiency (Figure 3A). The expression level of STAT3 was suppressed at the protein and mRNA levels after miR-454-3p mimic transfection (Figure 3B). Using TargetScan.org, the prediction revealed potential binding sites of the nucleic acid sequence of highly conserved miR-454-3p on the 3′-UTR of STAT3. Next, the partial sequences of STAT3 mRNA that bind to miR-454-3p or their mutant binding sites were inserted into the 3′-UTRs of the luciferase genes; we then transiently transfected them into the HEK-293T cells. According to the dual luciferase reporter assay, compared with those in the NC group, the luciferase activity of the cells in the miR-454-3p mimic group transfected with psiCHECK2-STAT3-wt plasmids was obviously lower, whereas the luciferase activity of the cells that were transfected with psiCHECK2-STAT3-mut plasmids was not significantly different (Figure 3C).

Overexpression of miR-454-3p attenuated HG-induced MMT and suppressed STAT3 expression in the HMrSV5 cells

We cultured the HMrSV5 cells in 236 mM glucose with a miR-454-3p mimic or NC mimic for 48 h. The HG-induced cells presented increased miR-454-3p expression levels upon the miR-454-3p mimic treatment (Figure 4A). Compared with the cells incubated with HG alone, the cells overexpressing miR-454-3p exhibited a downregulation of MMT following the HG stimulation. The RT-qPCR, immunofluorescence staining, and Western blotting analyses demonstrated that miR-454-3p had a protective effect on peritoneal fibrosis by preventing increases in of α-SMA and FN and decreases in E-cadherin. Additionally, the reduction in miR-454-3p in the HG-induced MMT was accompanied by a significant upregulation of STAT3 at both the protein and mRNA levels (Figure 4B–E).

The miR-454-3p inhibitor aggravated HG-induced MMT and enhanced STAT3 expression levels in the HMrSV5 cells

To better understand how miR-454-3p contributes to MMT, we treated the HMrSV5 cells with a miR-454-3p inhibitor and exposed them to 236 mM glucose for 48 h. The transfection efficiency was measured by RT-qPCR, which revealed that the miR-454-3p inhibitor reduced miR-454-3p expression levels (Figure 5A). Upon stimulation with the miR-454-3p inhibitor, the HG-treated cells presented decreased miR-454-3p expression levels (Figure 5B). Compared with those in the HG group, the protein and mRNA levels of the MMT markers in the miR-454-3p-inhibited HG-induced group tended to increase, which can be explained by the acquisition of α-SMA and FN and the downregulation of E-cadherin. Moreover, we discovered that the loss of miR-454-3p not only aggravated MMT but also upregulated STAT3 expression levels (Figure 5C–F).

The overexpression of STAT3 reversed the protective effect of the miR-454-3p mimic on MMT in the HG-treated HMrSV5 cells

To determine whether STAT3 mediated the protective effect of miR-454-3p on the HMrSV5 cells, a rescue experiment was conducted. STAT3 was overexpressed in the HMrSV5 cells, which were then incubated in 236 mM glucose for 48 h. Western blotting and RT-qPCR analyses revealed that STAT3 overexpression increased the mesenchymal marker α-SMA while downregulating the epithelial marker E-cadherin. Compared with the HG-induced cells stimulated with the miR-454-3p mimic, the co-expression of the miR-454-3p mimic and STAT3 overexpression plasmids into the HMrSV5 cells treated with high glucose reversed the protective function of miR-454-3p; this included exacerbation of MMT and upregulation of STAT3, as evidenced by the acquisition of α-SMA and downregulation of E-cadherin (Figure 6A, B). Taken together, the results of the rescue experiment implied that the function of miR-454-3p in HG-induced MMT was reliant on STAT3.

MiR-454-3p regulated the expression levels of glycolytic enzymes

To identify how miR-454-3p affects glycolysis in mesothelial cells, we transfected HMrSV5 cells with a miR-454-3p mimic or inhibitor in 236 mM glucose for 48 h. Western blotting and RT-qPCR analyses confirmed that HG conditions increased the expression of PFKFB3, PKM2, and LDHA. After miR-454-3p mimic transfection, the levels of glycolytic enzymes (PFKFB3, PKM2, and LDHA) in the HG-treated HMrSV5 cells decreased nearly to normal levels (Figure 7A–C). However, the absence of miR-454-3p significantly increased the expression levels of glycolytic enzymes in the HG-treated cells compared with that in the HG group (Figure 7D–F). The input group included total protein from the HMrSV5 cells. The anti-PFKFB3 antibody was used as the bait protein in the PFKFB3 group, and the secondary antibody of the same species was added as a negative control in the IgG group. Co-IP revealed that there was a protein interaction between PFKFB3 and STAT3 (Figure 7G).

Discussion

Peritoneal fibrosis can result in the loss of peritoneal function and ultrafiltration failure, causing patients to discontinue PD. Peritoneal mesothelial cells cover the peritoneal surface and act as the first barrier to the solutions used in PD [27]. Studies have shown that mesothelial cell EMT is a common pathological change in the peritoneum. Through this phenotypic shift, mesothelial cells disrupt the tight junctions between cells and acquire an invasive and migratory phenotype via the formation of an extracellular matrix. The EMT of mesothelial cells is also associated with a state of solute hyper-transport, ultimately leading to ultrafiltration failure [28]. Research has shown that excessive glycolysis is a cause of MMT (a cell-specific form of EMT in mesothelial cells) and peritoneal fibrosis. MiRNAs can inhibit excessive glycolysis and prevent peritoneal fibrosis and MMT in mesothelial cells. The purpose of our research was to investigate the potential mechanism by which miR-454-3p mediates glycolysis and MMT in PD.

This study demonstrated that the peritoneal tissues of patients receiving PD for a long period of time displayed significant fibrosis. The miR-454-3p expression levels in both the peritoneal tissues and PD effluent-derived cell populations in the peritoneal cavity were lower in the longer-duration PD group than in the control group. Targeted intervention with miR-454-3p was found to regulate EMT and angiogenesis. For example, miR-454-3p further inhibited EMT by targeting c-Met, ZEB/1, or NEUROD1 in some carcinomas [16, 17, 29]. MMT plays a major role in peritoneal fibrosis as an initiating link. The low levels of miR-454-3p in the longer PD duration group suggested that miR-454-3p may help attenuate peritoneal fibrosis.

As reported, the lncRNA FGD5-AS1/miR-454-3p/ZEB1 axis, thalidomide suppresses non-small-cell lung cancer cell proliferation, EMT, and angiogenesis [29]. STAT3 and MMT-related indicators were examined by western blotting and RT-qPCR, and compared with the newly dialyzed patients, the long-term PD group presented downregulation of E-cadherin and acquisition of α-SMA, FN, and STAT3. An increasing number of studies have shown that miR-454-3p serves as a carcinoma suppressor and that its overexpression decreases tumor growth by attenuating EMT [16, 17]. Interestingly, in this study, the level of miR-454-3p was inversely related to the degree of MMT in long-term PD patients, suggesting that aberrant miR-454-3p levels may play a potential role in the progression of peritoneal fibrosis.

To demonstrate the reliability of our clinical findings, we conducted an in vitro experiment using HMrSV5 cells. Owing to the absence of miR-454-3p in rats and mice, we were unable to perform in vivo experiments to illuminate the impact of miR-454-3p on the evolution of peritoneal fibrosis within an in vivo context. According to RT-qPCR data, the expression level of miR-454-3p was also markedly decreased in the HG environment. Additionally, high glucose induced MMT and increased STAT3 expression levels in a concentration- and time-dependent manner. These results suggested that in HMrSV5 cells, a high-glucose environment results in a decrease in miR-454-3p, which is consistent with the clinical findings. Similarly, miR-454-3p was reported to be downregulated in some carcinomas, including osteosarcoma, non-small-cell lung cancer, and bladder cancer, suggesting that it may play a protective role in these diseases [30–32]. However, the significance and downstream mechanisms of the absence of miR-454-3p during peritoneal fibrosis have not been investigated. Based on previous research, we further explored the link between miR-454-3p and its potential target STAT3.

By using bioinformatics analysis, STAT3 was chosen as a putative target gene for miR-454-3p to investigate the molecular pathways by which miR-454-3p might mediate the development of peritoneal fibrosis. A dual-luciferase reporter assay was subsequently adopted to confirm our hypothesis. These results confirmed that miR-454-3p directly targeted STAT3. Consistent with our research, the targeted binding relationship between miR-454-3p and STAT3 has also been validated in other disease models. For example, a previous study revealed that miR-454-3p inhibited chondrosarcoma growth by targeting STAT3 and Atg12 [33].

The use of high glucose PDF in PD patients is similar to the formation of a natural diabetic environment, accompanied by the formation of AGEs and altered glucose metabolism [34]. Hyperglycolysis has been linked to the development of MMT and fibrosis, and blocking hyperglycolysis inhibits MMT in peritoneal mesothelial cells [19]. Treatment of miRNAs triad targeted hyperglycolysis to suppress peritoneal fibrosis in mice and TGF-β1-induced EMT in MeT-5A cells [19]. We cultured cells with a miR-454-3p mimic and inhibitor to examine the regulatory role of miR-454-3p in excessive glycolysis and MMT. In the HMrSV5 cells, the treatment of high glucose induced high levels of glycolytic enzymes (PFKFB3, PKM2, and LDHA). According to Western blotting analysis, RT-qPCR, and immunofluorescence staining results, the overexpression of miR-454-3p elevated E-cadherin levels, whereas it downregulated the levels of α-SMA and FN, target gene STAT3, and key glycolytic enzymes PFKFB3, PKM2, and LDHA. Conversely, transfection using an inhibitor of miR-454-3p produced the opposite result. In our findings, we considered miR-454-3p to be a negative modulator of excessive glycolysis and high glucose-induced MMT during peritoneal fibrosis. Moreover, the rescue experiment showed that miR-454-3p functioned through STAT3. Our previous study showed that STAT3 knockdown downregulated the levels of mesenchymal markers and glycolytic enzymes (PFKFB3, PKM2, and LDHA) in HG-treated HMrSV5 cells. Blocking hyperglycolysis using the PFKFB3 inhibitor 3PO attenuated HG-induced cells MMT and ameliorated fibrosis and peritoneal transport function in PF rats [18]. The Co-IP assay revealed that there was a protein interaction between PFKFB3 and STAT3, suggesting that miR-454-3p may reduce the levels of key glycolytic enzymes via the STAT3/PFKFB3 pathway, which in turn inhibits the process of MMT. However, the Co-IP assay could not indicate a direct regulatory connection between STAT3 and PFKFB3, and it is plausible that a third interactive protein exists between these two proteins; thus, the specific regulatory mechanism remains to be further explored. In conclusion, all the results indicate that miR-454-3p regulates HG-induced peritoneal mesothelial cell glycolysis and MMT by targeting STAT3.

Conclusions

Overall, we discovered that miR-454-3p has a protective function in peritoneal fibrosis by directly targeting STAT3. MiR-454-3p was reduced in peritoneums and PD effluent-derived cell populations from long-term PD patients and HMrSV5 cells stimulated with HG. Moreover, HG-induced MMT and excessive glycolysis were attenuated via the miR-454-3p/STAT3/PFKFB3 axis. Hence, miR-454-3p has the potential to be a promising biomarker for the diagnosis of peritoneal fibrosis and a novel therapeutic target. Understanding the molecular mechanisms of miR-454-3p, which could benefit PD patients, requires more research.

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this paper we propose a new perspective related to Lucas sequence with statistical and summability, some considerations have been given in connection with their applications of signal processing. We define the notions of -Lucas statistically convergent and strongly -Lucas summability through modulus functions. The study of investigates on the Lucas transform and its embedding diagram into the representative form of Lucas numbers is studied in the context of convergence and summability problems. Furthermore, we provide inclusions as well as principles of equivalence, which lead to conditions guaranteeing the uniqueness of restrictions and generalize statistical convergence theory. Numerical simulations on a wide variety of signals such as noisy sinusoidal signals and blurred images demonstrate the flexibility of the proposed approach. These experiments show a steady decay to zero, which implies considerable noise immunity. In addition, by providing alternative perspectives of assessing signal quality, compression strategies and filtering techniques, those methods help in distinguishing the relevant signal information from background noise. Bringing together theoretical results and practical real-world signal analysis examples, the paper introduces classical and recent contributions to summability theory as well as new signal-processing procedures.Keywords: Lucas sequence, Modulus function, -Lucas statistical convergence, Signal processingSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Physics

A basic integer sequence, Lucas numbers have many uses in both theoretical and practical mathematics. They are useful in counting, lattice path, tiling, and the study of divisibility and recurrence relations problems because of their combinatorial and number-theoretic qualities4. Lucas numbers have been used to create effective and safe encryption systems in the fields of computer science and information security. They are often used in conjunction with chaotic mappings to maintain computational productivity and achieve strong cryptographic confrontation5. They are also key in coding theory, to construct sequences with specific error detection and correction capability’s6. Several extensions of Lucas numbers, including multidimensional analogues and k-generalized Lucas sequences have been studied to enhance their mathematical importance and applications in practice, that include their multiplicative structures and ability to create new combinatorial arrangements7,8. It is shown that the use of Lucas numbers for the investigation of approximation, convergence and summability in sequence spaces and matrix domain provides a powerful tool for studying these classes of sequences bringing together classical integer sequences and modern analysis.

Sampled data sequences constitute the most elementary form of information representation in signal processing, and convergence studies are essential for signal reconstruction, feature detection, and noise removal9. The algebra relationships, noise barred property and recursion of Lucas sequences provide a rich mathematical foundation for introducing new signal processing paradigms10,11. Innovative analytical tools that effectively handle non-stationary signals, maintain significant signal characteristics during transformation, and generate precise assessments of signal deterioration are made possible by incorporating Lucas sequences into statistical convergence analyses12. When conventional Fourier or wavelet-based techniques are inadequate, this method makes it easier to develop Lucas-based operators that effectively separate pertinent elements from random noise13. As a result, this study’s convergence and summability structures establish a solid mathematical link between number-theoretic sequences and real-time signal processing, paving the way for improvements in compression, adaptive filtering, and real-time signal analysis.

The summability techniques are widely used to construct signal approximations and for the analysis of convergence. Jena et al.14 studied the Fejer-Riesz summation of a Fourier series and Jena et al. in15 the approximation of another signal by Using matrix summability methods were studied and its influence on truncating Gibbs phenomenon. More recently, Sonker et al.16 examined signal approximation using generalized summability operators, demonstrating their effectiveness in preserving signal features. These works highlight the importance of summability techniques in signal processing and motivate the development of new approaches such as the proposed Lucas-based framework.

Despite the Lucas numbers have been well established and widely applied in number theory, little is known about their broader analytic possibilities. The present investigation is motivated by the observation that recursive construction algebraically properties of Lucas sequences provide useful tools to generalize the concept of convergence beyond their scope in previously existing forms. The novelty of the present study that Lucas sequences are naturally unified with statistical convergence by introducing -Lucas statistical convergence via Lucas transform operator and strong -Lucas summability using modulus functions, respectively.

The conventional concept of convergence is expanded upon by statistical convergence. It was introduced independently in the same year by Fast17 and Steinhaus18. This idea has been frequently used in mathematics ever since, particularly in the study of sequence spaces and summability theory19–21. Its importance has grown significantly, and it now plays a variety of roles in many different disciplines. Prominent examples include its applications to time scale calculus22–25, Banach spaces26,27, summability theory28–31, approximation theory32–34, measure theory35, and the study of Fourier analysis36. These numerous and diverse applications demonstrate the versatility and crucial function of statistical convergence in current mathematical research. More in-depth discussions and illustrations may be found in37–40.

Applications in signal processing

The Lucas sequence and the related convergence and summability methods proposed in this paper have significant potential applications in signal processing. Signal processing involves the analysis, modification, and synthesis of signals, which are typically represented as sequences of data points. The special qualities of Lucas sequences, along with new convergence notions we have proposed, open new methods to some of the challenges of signal processing.

Signal filtering and analysis

The -Lucas statistical convergence framework in its purest form is particularly useful for adaptive filters. In other words, a signal filter can inherit the same logic while adapting its filter parameters to change its characteristic in this context: As detailed in (1.1), the –transform of a signal sequence , the Lucas difference sequence spaces defined by the double band matrix can be utilized to obtain filter coefficients that account for different spectral configurations in signal filtering scenarios. The filtered signal has statistically converged to the intended signal, even in the presence of outliers or temporary disturbances, according to the -Lucas statistical convergence.

Signal compression

The notion of -Lucas summability is an interesting filter for signals. This method utilizes the recursive characteristics of Lucas sequences, and Lucas-based functions achieve efficient compression. The flexibility of the modulus function is vital for both reconstruction quality and compression ratio. Moreover, condition (3.1) ensures that the compressed signal yields lower reconstruction error while maintaining essential features of the original signal. Moreover, the technique has an added advantage: compressing of signals with self-similar or fractal properties as observed in biological and natural signals such as speech.

Noise elimination

The -Lucas statistical convergence can be used in signal processing for noise removal. These noise influences which result from signals can be viewed as non-symmetric processes that do not satisfy statistical convergence. Using the -Lucas statistical convergence criterion, it is possible to distinguish between purely random noise and true signal components. When non-stationary noise is present, which is difficult to manage using conventional filtering techniques, exists its especially beneficial. The Lucas-based statistical method essentially removes any features that are inconsistent with well-converged statistics, while preserving the essential signal structure.

Mean Squared Error (MSE).

The mean squared error measures the average energy of the reconstruction error:

A smaller MSE indicates a more accurate reconstruction of the original signal. In the context of statistical convergence, a decreasing MSE suggests that the filtered signal converges to the true signal in the quadratic mean sense.

Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR).

PSNR is a logarithmic measure commonly used in signal and image processing to evaluate reconstruction quality. It compares the peak signal power with the residual noise power:

A higher PSNR value indicates better signal reconstruction. Since PSNR is expressed in decibels (dB), even a small increase corresponds to a noticeable improvement in signal quality.

Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR).

The signal-to-noise ratio measures how much of the signal energy is preserved after filtering:

This metric quantifies the relative strength of the desired signal compared to the reconstruction error. Larger SNR values correspond to more effective noise suppression.

Conclusion

In this paper we aimed to establish a relationship between Lucas sequences on convergence theory by introducing the ideas of -Lucas statistical convergence, and strong -Lucas summability with the help of modulus functions. The Lucas transform was the main measure of analysis which paved new generalizations for classical summability theory and therefore inclusion as well as equivalence relations. This provides new angles to manipulate the sequence properties of non-primitive recursions in relation to recursive structures originating from number theory.

Several numerical experiments were conducted to demonstrate that the proposed algorithm is effective for signal processing problems such as image denoising and deblurring. This method was demonstrated to effectively reduce noise while preserving key signal features and thus improving both the quality of signal and the accuracy of reconstruction. This provides a natural link between abstract summability theory and the analysis of real signals.

Future research may consider extending the proposed framework to higher-order Lucas sequences, multidimensional signals, and real-time implementations. In addition, investigating fractional or fractal extensions of Lucas-based transformations may further enhance their applicability. Overall, the proposed approach contributes both to the advancement of summability theory and to the development of efficient mathematical tools for modern signal processing applications.

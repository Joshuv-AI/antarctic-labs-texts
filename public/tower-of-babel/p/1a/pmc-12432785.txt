ISG20L2 as a driver in the proliferation and invasion of lung adenocarcinoma via NKX2-1 regulation
Authors: Xiaoming Fang, Xinyu Zhang, Ping Liu, Dan Yu, Andrzej Swierniak, Carl G Maki, Ruichen Gao, Ming Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundInterferon-stimulated 20-kDa exonuclease-like 2 (ISG20L2) is a gene that shows variable expression levels in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD). Despite this, its role and expression in LUAD have not been well characterized. This study focused on analyzing the expression of ISG20L2 in LUAD, its association with patient prognosis, and its potential regulatory influence on NK2 homeobox 1 (NKX2-1) in LUAD cells.MethodsWe used The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database and immunohistochemistry to assess the expression of ISG20L2. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was employed to determine the relationship between ISG20L2 expression and prognosis in patients with LUAD. The diagnostic potential of ISG20L2 in LUAD was evaluated through receiving operating characteristic curve analysis. The association between ISG20L2 expression and clinicopathological features was analyzed with the Fisher exact test. Overexpression and depletion studies of ISG20L2 were performed via transient transfection. The biological functions of ISG20L2 in A549 cells, such as cell proliferation, apoptosis, migration, and invasion, were examined via Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8), flow cytometry, and Transwell assays. Bioinformatics analysis suggested a link between ISG20L2 and NKX2-1, which was subsequently confirmed through cytological experiments.ResultsOur results demonstrated that ISG20L2 was overexpressed in LUAD, and this overexpression was significantly correlated with poor prognosis. In vitro experiments further supported a positive association between ISG20L2 expression and the proliferative, migratory, and invasive capabilities in A549 cells, with no notable effect on apoptosis. Functional assays confirmed that ISG20L2 enhanced A549 cell proliferation and invasion by modulating NKX2-1.ConclusionsISG20L2 promotes LUAD progression via NKX2-1 regulation, implying the potential of the ISG20L2/NKX2-1 axis as a candidate biomarker and therapeutic target in LUAD.Keywords: Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), interferon-stimulated 20-kDa exonuclease-like 2 (ISG20L2), NK2 homeobox 1 (NKX2-1), proliferation, prognosis

Highlight box

Key findings

• Interferon-stimulated 20-kDa exonuclease-like 2 (ISG20L2) was upregulated in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) and facilitated A549 cell proliferation and invasion through the regulation of NK2 homeobox 1 (NKX2-1). Its elevated expression was an indicator of a less favorable prognosis for patients with LUAD.

What is known and what is new?

• Elevated ISG20L2 expression has been observed in various cancers, including lung cancer, liver cancer, and breast cancer. The expression level of ISG20L2 is correlated with the efficacy and prognosis of cancer therapy.

• The high expression of ISG20L2 correlated with a poorer prognosis among patients with LUAD. ISG20L2 enhanced LUAD cell proliferation and invasion by modulating NKX2-1.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Our results highlight the potential of ISG20L2 as a key prognostic marker in LUAD and open new avenues for targeted therapies and personalized treatment strategies. To fully harness the therapeutic potential of ISG20L2, further research is essential to clarifying the related mechanisms in detail and confirming these findings in vivo.

Introduction

Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer-related mortality globally, with its incidence rising annually (1,2). It is categorized into two primary types: non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and small-cell lung cancer (SCLC). NSCLC constitutes approximately 85% of all lung cancers, with lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) accounting for about 40% of NSCLC cases (3-5). The advent of targeted therapies has significantly transformed the treatment landscape for oncogene-driven NSCLC (6). It is estimated that up to 69% of patients with advanced NSCLC possess specific molecular targets amenable to targeted treatment (7). The treatment paradigm for advanced NSCLC has evolved from relying on clinical and pathological characteristics to incorporating biomarker-driven algorithms based on the tumor’s molecular profile (8). Molecularly targeted therapy uses drugs or other agents that specifically interact with certain molecules to inhibit cancer cell growth and dissemination (9). This approach offers benefits such as targeted and efficient drug delivery and lower toxicity compared to traditional chemotherapy, thereby improving survival patient rates for various malignant tumors (10). One of the recent successes in this field is related to introduction of anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK) inhibitors for patients with ALK-positive advanced NSCLC (11). In this case a third generation ALK inhibitor, lorlatinib, was introduced to the first line. In the other study (12) on the efficacy of ALK inhibitors alectinib or crizotinib was the initial therapy providing median overall survival (OS) of 54.1 versus 45.8 for the former versus the latter.

Despite significant advancements in systemic treatments for advanced NSCLC through targeted therapy and immunotherapy, the long-term survival rates for patients with lung cancer remain low (13-15). Although novel drugs for relatively rare targetable mutations such as KRAS G12C, BRAF, HER2, MET, MEK1, NRAS or NTRK already exist, there is a persisting need to identify novel biomarkers and prognostic genes, develop next-generation drugs with enhanced specificity, and create treatments to overcome specific drug-resistant mutations. These advancements aim to provide more effective therapies and extend patient survival, aligning with the goals of precision medicine.

Interferon-stimulated 20-kDa exonuclease-like 2 (ISG20L2) is a member of the vertebrate exonuclease family involved in ribosome biogenesis and degradation. A study has shown that ISG20L2 accumulates in the nuclei of HeLa cells and exhibits exoribonucleolytic activity, degrading RNA from the 3’ to 5’ terminus (16). This activity has been associated with cellular proliferation and apoptosis (16). Elevated ISG20L2 expression has been observed in various cancers, including lung cancer (17), liver cancer (18,19), breast cancer (20), and colon adenocarcinoma (21). Recent research has identified ISG20L2 as an exonuclease with a preferential specificity for uridylated microRNA (miRNA), which can regulate T-cell activation (22). In myeloma, ISG20L2 has been found to reduce the binding of bortezomib to the proteasome 20S subunit β5 (PSMB5), leading to decreased inhibition of proteasome activity and the reduced efficacy of bortezomib treatment (23). These findings underscore the significant association between ISG20L2 expression levels and the efficacy and prognosis of cancer therapies. However, the specific role of ISG20L2 in LUAD remains to be elucidated.

NK2 homeobox 1 (NKX2-1), also referred to as thyroid transcription factor-1 (TTF-1), is a tissue-specific transcription factor found in the thyroid and lungs (24). NKX2-1 is crucial for regulating the differentiation, development, and functional maintenance of lung epithelial cells (25). Identifying NKX2-1 mutations can be clinically valuable in distinguishing primary lung tumors from metastatic LUAD. Loss-of-function mutations in NKX2-1 may synergize with KRAS mutations to drive subtype-specific tumorigenesis in LUAD (26). There is evidence indicating that NKX2-1 can modulate dual-specificity phosphatase 6 (DUSP6) to control ERK activation (27). Although the potential mechanisms of NKX2-1 in lung cancer have been partially explored (28), focusing on single targets alone cannot fully elucidate the underlying pathways in lung cancer. Our analysis of The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database revealed a correlation between ISG20L2 and NKX2-1, prompting us to conduct further research to determine if ISG20L2 and NKX2-1 collaboratively influence the mechanisms of action in lung cancer.

This study aimed to investigate ISG20L2 expression in LUAD and assess its diagnostic significance through bioinformatics analysis. The expression of ISG20L2 was subsequently validated in tissue samples via immunohistochemical (IHC) staining. The prognostic value of ISG20L2 expression and its relationship with the clinicopathological characteristics of LUAD were examined. Additionally, in vitro experiments were performed to evaluate the impact of ISG20L2 on the biological behavior of LUAD cells. Finally, we determined whether ISG20L2 promotes LUAD cell proliferation and invasion by regulating NKX2-1. We present this article in accordance with the REMARK and MDAR reporting checklists (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2025-1546/rc).

Methods

Bioinformatics analysis

Clinical data on ISG20L2 and NKX2-1 expression profiles related to LUAD were obtained from a dataset from TCGA (https://www.cancer.gov/ccg/research/genome-sequencing/tcga). Additionally, the adenocarcinoma microarray dataset GSE19804 was sourced from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), maintained by the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) (29). Differential analysis was conducted with the “clusterProfiler” R package (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses

GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were conducted on the shared genes using the “GOplot” and “clusterProfiler” R packages. A P value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Analysis of diagnostic value

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was used to evaluate the diagnostic value of ISG20L2 in LUAD. This ROC curve was generated by plotting the “true positive rate (sensitivity)” against the “false positive rate (1 − specificity)” across varying ISG20L2 expression thresholds using clinical data related to ISG20L2 expression profiles from TCGA database, including 95 LUAD and 80 paracancerous specimens. The Youden’ s index (J = sensitivity + specificity − 1) was used to identify the optimal cutoff value for ISG20L2 expression that maximizes diagnostic accuracy. The area under the curve (AUC) indicated the diagnostic effectiveness, with a value closer to 1 representing better diagnostic performance. An AUC of <0.7 indicated low accuracy, 0.7–0.9 moderate accuracy, and >0.9 high accuracy.

IHC staining

We acquired an LUAD tissue chip (number HLugA180Su08) from Shanghai Outdo Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China), which comprised 95 LUAD and 80 paracancerous specimens. The patients included in the tissue chip had comprehensive enrollment information and provided informed consent, underwent surgery between 2004 and 2009, and were followed for 0.3–10 years. The Ethics Committee of Shanghai Xinchao Technology Co. granted approval with the use of the tissue chip (No. SHYJS-CP-1904014). Initially, the tissue chip was baked at 63 ℃ for 1 hour. It was then dewaxed with xylene treatment twice at 15 minutes for each treatment and then subjected to gradient alcohol dehydration (100% twice at 7 minutes for each treatment, 90% once for 5 minutes, 80% once for 5 minutes, and 70% once for 5 minutes). Antigen retrieval was performed with a Dako fully automated IHC pretreatment system (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). Subsequently, the tissue chip was incubated overnight at 4 ℃ with the ISG20L2 antibody (rabbit polyclonal; dilution 1:50; catalog number NBP1-82287; Novus Biologicals, Centennial, CO, USA). The following day, the chip was treated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated anti-rabbit immunoglobulin G (IgG) (Abcam, Cambridge, UK) as a secondary antibody for 30 minutes at room temperature. The chip was then stained with 3,3’-diaminobenzidine (DAB), counterstained with hematoxylin, and sealed with a neutral resin.

Staining intensity was scored as follows: 0, negative; 1, 1+; 2, 2+; and 3, 3+. The proportion of positive staining ranged from 0% to 100%. The overall score was calculated by multiplying the staining intensity score by the proportion of positive staining results (0–300%). In the survival analysis, ISG20L2 low expression was considered to be a total score of less than 27.5, while ISG20L2 high expression was considered to be a total score of 27.5 or higher.

Cell culture and transfection

A549 (a human LUAD cell line) cells were purchased from Procell Life Science & Technology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China) and maintained in Ham’s F-12K medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin solution. The cells were incubated at 37 ℃ in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2. The plasma-based complementary DNA (pcDNA) empty vector, pcDNA-ISG20L2, negative control (NC), small interfering RNA (siRNA)-ISG20L2, and siRNA-NKX2-1 were designed and supplied by GenePharma Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). A549 cells were seeded into six-well cell culture plates at a density of 1×105 cells/well. Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) was added to the plasmids or oligonucleotides once the cells reached 70% to 80% confluence. Cells were harvested and analyzed 48 hours after transfection for further studies.

Western blot analysis

Protein samples were extracted from A549 cells with radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) lysis buffer (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitors (Beyotime). Protein concentration was determined with a bicinchoninic acid (BCA) protein detection kit (Beyotime). Subsequently, equal amounts of proteins were subjected to sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and transferred onto polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes. The PVDF membranes were then blocked with 5% skim milk at room temperature for 1.5 hours. Subsequently, the membranes were incubated with primary antibodies against ISG20L2 (catalog number NBP2-83084; Novus Biologicals) and glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH; Abcam) overnight at 4 ℃. After three washes, the membranes were incubated with a secondary antibody, goat anti-rabbit IgG (Abcam), for 1.5 hours. Immunoreactive bands were visualized with an electrochemiluminescence (ECL) reagent kit (Tanon, Shanghai, China). GAPDH was used as a loading control, and the relative expression at the protein level was quantified using ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA) by calculating the grayscale value of the bands. All experiments were performed in triplicate.

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted from A549 cells with TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen). First-strand complement DNA (cDNA) was synthesized with a cDNA synthesis kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The RNA expression levels were then quantified via qRT-PCR (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA) with AceQ qPCR SYBR Green premix and miRNA Universal SYBR qPCR premix (Vazyme, Nanjing, China). GAPDH was used as the housekeeping gene. The primer sequences for GAPDH and the target genes (ISG20L2 and NKX2-1) were as follows: ISG20L2 forward, 5'-AGGAAATGCCAAGCACCGAAA-3'; ISG20L2 reverse, 5'-TGAAGGTTCAGAGTGCAACTTAG-3';

NKX2-1 forward, 5'-AGCACACGACTCCGTTCTC-3'; NKX2-1 reverse, 5'-GCCCACTTTCTTGTAGCTTTCC-3'; GAPDH forward, 5'-GGAGCGAGATCCCTCCAAAAT-3'; And GAPDH reverse, 5'-GGCTGTTGTCATACTTCTCATGG-3'.

Cell proliferation assay

To evaluate tumor cell proliferation, we employed the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8; Dojindo, Tokyo, Japan). A549 cells were initially seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 2×103 cells per well. Following incubation periods of 0, 12, 24, 48, and 72 hours, 10 µL of the CCK-8 reagent was introduced into each well and incubated at 37 ℃ for 1 hour. Subsequently, absorbance at 450 nm was quantified for each well with a microplate reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific). All experiments were conducted in triplicate to ensure consistency and reliability of results.

Colony formation assay

The experimental and control cells were seeded into six-well plates at a density of 1,000 cells per well. The cells were incubated with the medium that was changed every 3 days for at least 7 days. Colony formation was considered achieved when cell clusters containing more than 50 cells were observed under a microscope. At this point, the culture was terminated, and the cells were collected. The culture medium was discarded, and the cells were washed twice with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). After the PBS was discarded, the cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 minutes at room temperature and then stained with Coomassie brilliant blue R250 for 4 hours. The cells were rinsed with tap water, allowed to dry naturally, and photographed.

Flow cytometry

To detect early and late apoptotic cells, flow cytometry was employed with Annexin V-FITC/PI Apoptosis Detection Kit (KeyGEN BioTECH, Nanjing, China). Cells were harvested with ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA)-free trypsin, washed twice with chilled PBS, and centrifuged at 1,000 g for 5 minutes. The cell pellet was then resuspended in 500 µL of binding buffer, to which 5 µL of Annexin V-FITC and 5 µL of propidium iodide were added. Following a 10-minute incubation at room temperature in the dark, apoptotic cells were detected with a flow cytometer (Becton Dickinson and Co., Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA). All experiments were conducted in triplicate to ensure consistency and accuracy of the results.

Transwell migration and invasion assays

The migration and invasion of cancer cells were assessed with Transwell chambers (8-µm pore size; Corning Inc., Corning, NY, USA) and 24-well plates (Corning Inc.). For both assays, 200 µL of serum-free medium containing 1×105 cells were seeded into the upper chamber of the Transwell. In the invasion assay, the upper chamber was precoated with Matrigel (Corning Inc.). The lower chamber was filled with 700 µL of Ham’s F-12K medium supplemented with 10% FBS. After 24 hours of incubation, non-invading cells in the upper chamber were removed with cotton swabs. Subsequently, the invaded cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 minutes and stained with crystal violet for 15 minutes at room temperature. The number of invading cells was quantified in five randomly selected fields under an inverted microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan). All experiments were conducted in triplicate to ensure reliability and reproducibility of results.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted with R software v. 4.3.0 (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing). Differential analysis of ISG20L2 expression between cancerous and paracancerous tissues was performed with the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. The relationship between ISG20L2 expression and clinicopathological variables was examined with the Fisher exact test. Survival univariate analysis was conducted via Kaplan-Meier estimators and the log-rank test. Additionally, OS time was analyzed through both univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression models. A P value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all analyses.

Results

ISG20L2 was highly expressed in LUAD and high ISG20L2 expression was associated with poor LUAD prognosis

To investigate the role of ISG20L2 in LUAD, we initially analyzed its differential expression at the messenger RNA (mRNA) level in LUAD via TCGA database (30). Our analysis revealed that ISG20L2 expression was significantly upregulated in LUAD compared to corresponding healthy tissues (Figure 1A; P<0.001). To confirm this finding, we performed IHC staining on a chip containing 95 LUAD and 80 paracancerous specimens. The results demonstrated that ISG20L2 protein was highly expressed in LUAD tissues but exhibited lower expression in paracancerous tissues (Figure 1B; P<0.001). These findings collectively indicated the increased expression of ISG20L2 in LUAD, suggesting its involvement in the pathogenesis of LUAD.

IHC, immunohistochemistry; ISG20L2, interferon-stimulated 20-kDa exonuclease-like 2; LUAD, lung adenocarcinoma; TCGA, The Cancer Genome Atlas.

We also conducted ROC curve analysis using clinical data related to ISG20L2 expression profiles from TCGA database to evaluate the diagnostic potential of ISG20L2 in LUAD. The results demonstrated that ISG20L2 could effectively distinguish patients with LUAD from healthy controls, with an AUC of 0.829 [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.792–0.865] (Figure 2A). Furthermore, we investigated the relationships between ISG20L2 expression and various clinical characteristics in patients with LUAD. No significant associations were found between ISG20L2 expression and factors such as gender, age, tumor size, T stage, N stage, M stage, pathological grade, or tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) stage (Table 1).

To assess the prognostic significance of ISG20L2 expression at the protein level, we examined the relationship between ISG20L2 expression and patient outcomes using IHC staining. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis of 95 LUAD patients stratified into high vs. low ISG20L2 expression groups based on the median IHC score. Our analysis indicated that low ISG20L2 expression was associated with better prognosis (P=0.047; Figure 2B). Additionally, we employed a Cox regression model to evaluate the association between ISG20L2 expression and patient survival (Table 2). Univariate analysis identified ISG20L2 expression, age, N stage, and TNM stage as factors influencing the OS of patients with LUAD. However, multivariate analysis revealed that these factors were not independent risk factors for OS. Overall, our findings suggested that ISG20L2 was upregulated in LUAD and might play a role in its pathogenesis. Furthermore, ISG20L2 expression demonstrated diagnostic potential and was associated with the prognosis of patients with LUAD.

GO and KEGG enrichment analyses in ISG20L2-high vs. ISG20L2-low LUAD samples in TCGA

To gain a better understanding of the biological processes (BPs) and pathways involved in lung cancer, we performed GO functional enrichment analysis and KEGG pathway analysis in ISG20L2-high vs. ISG20L2-low LUAD samples in TCGA. The GO functional enrichment analysis revealed that the primary BP included extracellular matrix organization, extracellular structure organization, and external encapsulating structure organization. The cellular component (CC) analysis showed significant enrichment in collagen-containing extracellular matrix, membrane raft, and membrane microdomain. The molecular function (MF) analysis indicated involvement in glycosaminoglycan binding, extracellular matrix structural constituent, and heparin binding (Figure 3A).

GO and KEGG enrichment analyses in ISG20L2-high vs. ISG20L2-low LUAD samples in TCGA. (A) GO terms of BP, CC, and MF. (B) KEGG pathway terms. BP, biological process; CC, cellular component; GO, Gene Ontology; ISG20L2, interferon-stimulated 20-kDa exonuclease-like 2; KEGG, Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes; LUAD, lung adenocarcinoma; MF, molecular function; TCGA, The Cancer Genome Atlas.

The KEGG pathway enrichment analysis identified several pathways related to lung cancer, including cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, protein digestion and absorption, interleukin (IL)-17 signaling pathway, and lipid atherosclerosis (Figure 3B). These findings suggested that alterations in extracellular matrix organization and cytokine signaling might play a critical role in the development and progression of lung cancer. These results provide valuable insights into the BP and pathways associated with lung cancer, which might aid in the development of more effective diagnostic and therapeutic strategies for this disease.

ISG20L2 overexpression promoted the proliferation, migration, and invasion of A549 cells but not apoptosis

To assess the efficiency of ISG20L2 overexpression, Western blot analysis was performed on cells transfected with either pcDNA3.1 or pcDNA3.1-ISG20L2. The results revealed a significant increase in ISG20L2 expression in the pcDNA3.1-ISG20L2 group as compared to the pcDNA3.1 group (P<0.001; Figure 4A). To further investigate the impact of ISG20L2 on the biological function of A549 cells, we conducted a CCK-8 assay to measure cell proliferation. The results demonstrated a significant increase in cell proliferation in the pcDNA3.1-ISG20L2 group after 48 hours as compared to the pcDNA3.1 group (P<0.001; Figure 4B). However, no significant difference in apoptosis was observed between the two groups following ISG20L2 overexpression (P=0.84; Figure 4C). These findings suggested that ISG20L2 overexpression enhanced the growth rate of A549 cells in vitro without affecting cell apoptosis.

Transwell assays were performed to evaluate the migratory and invasive abilities of A549 cells. The results showed that, as compared to the pcDNA3.1 group, the cell migration (P=0.03; Figure 4D) and invasion (P=0.006; Figure 4E) of the pcDNA3.1-ISG20L2 group were significantly increased. These findings suggested that ISG20L2 overexpression promoted the migration and invasion of A549 cells. Collectively, our results demonstrated that ISG20L2 overexpression enhanced the proliferative capacity, migration, and invasion of A549 cells, indicating its potential role in promoting the progression of LUAD.

ISG20L2 depletion decreased the proliferation, migration, and invasion of A549 cells but not apoptosis

To further investigate the effects of ISG20L2 on LUAD cells, ISG20L2 depletion was performed, and transfection efficiency was measured via Western blot analysis. The results revealed a significant reduction in ISG20L2 expression in the si-ISG20L2 group as compared to the NC group (P<0.001; Figure 5A). Subsequently, a CCK-8 assay was conducted to evaluate cell proliferative capacity. The results indicated a significant decrease in cell proliferation in the si-ISG20L2 group as compared to the NC group (P<0.001; Figure 5B). Flow cytometry analysis demonstrated no difference in the apoptosis rate between the NC group and the si-ISG20L2 group (P=0.97; Figure 5C). These findings suggested that the depletion of ISG20L2 reduced the proliferative ability of A549 cells. Similar to the results observed with ISG20L2 overexpression, ISG20L2 depletion did not affect apoptosis. Furthermore, Transwell assays were performed to assess cell migration and invasion. The si-ISG20L2 group, as compared to the NC group, exhibited significantly decreased migratory (P=0.008; Figure 5D) and invasive abilities (P=0.02; Figure 5E). In summary, these experiments demonstrated that the depletion of ISG20L2 decreased the migration and invasion of A549 cells. Taken together, these findings suggested that ISG20L2 plays a crucial role in promoting the proliferative capacity, migration, and invasion of LUAD cells, highlighting its potential as a target for therapeutic interventions in LUAD.

Correlation of NKX2-1 and ISG20L2

We analyzed the gene expression differential volcano plots and heat maps of samples in the ISG20L2-low expression and ISG20L2-high expression groups in the LUAD expression profiles from TCGA database. Volcano plot analysis (Figure 6A) identified NKX2-1 as the most significantly upregulated gene in ISG20L2-low LUAD samples. The heatmap (Figure 6B) confirms this inverse expression pattern, with NKX2-1 expression increasing from ISG20L2-high to ISG20L2-low subgroups. Furthermore, we examined the correlation between ISG20L2 and NKX2-1 expression in the LUAD expression profiles from TCGA database. The results indicated a significant negative correlation between ISG20L2 and NKX2-1 expression (Figure 6C). Finally, to characterize the expression differences of NKX2-1 in LUAD, we conducted an analysis using TCGA database. The results showed that NKX2-1 expression was significantly reduced in LUAD tissues as compared to normal tissues (Figure 6D).

NKX2-1 was a target gene of ISG20L2 which mediated the ISG20L2-related proliferation and invasion of LUAD Cells

To further investigate the correlation between NKX2-1 and ISG20L2, we selected A549 cells for subsequent cytological experiments. Building on previous experiments, we depleted NKX2-1 and ISG20L2 and verified their depletion using qRT-PCR. Notably, depletion ISG20L2 increased expression of NKX2-1 (Figure 7A,7B) which is consistent with Figure 6 data showing low ISG20L2 expression correlated with high NKX2-1 expression in patient samples. Depleting NKX2-1 did not significantly affect ISG20L2 expression as compared to si-NC (Figure 7A).

Next, we detected NKX2-1 expression using qRT-PCR and found that NKX2-1 expression was significantly upregulated following ISG20L2 downregulation. Rescue experiments demonstrated that NKX2-1 expression was downregulated when both ISG20L2 and NKX2-1 were depleted (Figure 7A). We also confirmed the downregulation efficiency at the protein level. Western blot analysis revealed that ISG20L2 downregulation led to a significant upregulation of NKX2-1 protein levels. Subsequent experiments in which siRNA was used to inhibit NKX2-1 expression corroborated these findings (Figure 7B). These results indicated that ISG20L2 inhibited NKX2-1 expression and acted as an upstream regulator of NKX2-1.

Data in Figure 5 showed depletion of ISG20L2 reduced A549 cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. We speculated these effects could be mediated by increased NKX2-1. To test this, we co-depleted ISG20L2 and NKX2-1 in A549 cells and assessed proliferation by CCK-8 and colony formation assays and migration by Transwell assays. As shown in Figure 7C-7E, depletion of ISG20L2 reduced proliferation and colony formation and reduced Transwell migration as expected, and these effects were overcome by co-depletion of NKX2-1, supporting that the inhibitory effects of ISG20L2 knockdown are due in part to increased expression of NKX2-1.

Discussion

LUAD is a widespread type of cancer globally, yet its specific mechanisms of initiation and progression have not yet been fully clarified. Studying the differential genes linked to LUAD is crucial, as it can aid in deciphering its molecular mechanisms and in identifying diagnostic and prognostic markers. There is limited research on the association between ISG20L2 and cancer, with most of the related studies employing bioinformatics methods. Therefore, its exact cellular role remains largely unknown. Interestingly, bioinformatics analyses have shown that ISG20L2 is highly expressed in hepatocellular carcinoma, in which it is associated with reduced patient survival, indicating its potential as a molecular target for immunotherapy in this cancer type (19). In breast cancer, ISG20L2 has been linked to the expression of the tumor proliferation marker MKI67 (Ki-67) and the prognosis of patients with breast cancer (20). In the context of LUAD, our study used bioinformatics analysis and IHC staining, which revealed increased mRNA and protein levels of ISG20L2. Survival analysis indicated ISG20L2 to be a high-risk gene, with its elevated expression being linked to poor prognosis in patients with LUAD. ROC analysis showed that ISG20L2 may have diagnostic potential for LUAD. These results suggest that ISG20L2 could help substantially benefit the prognosis of patients with LUAD through improved diagnosis and accurate prognostic evaluation.

In terms of GO analysis, ISG20L2 enrichment was linked to proliferation and energy metabolism, including extracellular matrix tissue and extracellular structural tissue. Aberrant energy metabolism can induce bioenergetic stress, leading to signal transduction in tumor cells (31). Further investigation is required to determine whether ISG20L2 is involved in the aberrant energy metabolism contributing to cancer development. CC terms suggested its involvement in membrane microdomains and membrane rafts, which play a role in immune receptor signaling (32,33). Previous evidence suggests ISG20L2’s involvement in immune cell invasion in hepatocellular carcinoma (19). Therefore, it is essential to determine whether ISG20L2 is involved in immune cell invasion in LUAD and how ISG20L2 mediates the relationship between immune receptor signaling and immune cell infiltration.

Regarding KEGG analysis, ISG20L2 has been implicated in several pathways closely related to the inflammatory response, such as the cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction pathway and the IL-17 signaling pathway (34-36). These findings suggest that ISG20L2 might participate in immune-related processes within LUAD. Further research is necessary to comprehensively clarify the mechanisms by which ISG20L2 influences immune responses and their potential impact on immune cell infiltration in LUAD.

Our study also examined the relationship between ISG20L2 expression levels and clinical pathological parameters in patients with LUAD. We found that ISG20L2 expression did not significantly correlate with gender, age, tumor size, TNM staging, or pathological grading in patients with LUAD. However, Cox regression model analysis indicated an association between ISG20L2 expression and the survival rate of patients with LUAD.

To clarify the impact of ISG20L2 on the biological behavior of LUAD cells, we performed various cellular experiments. Our results showed a positive correlation between ISG20L2 expression and the proliferative, migratory, and invasive capabilities of LUAD cells. It is well established that abnormal cell proliferation is closely linked to cancer development and progression, and invasion and metastasis are responsible for the majority of cancer-related deaths (37,38). However, we did not observe a significant effect of ISG20L2 on the apoptosis of LUAD cells. A growing body of evidence suggests that miR-139-3p is reduced in breast cancer tissues (39) and colon cancer serum (40), with decreased miR-139-3p levels being associated with poor prognosis in patients with cancer. Although ISG20L2 has been identified as a target gene of miR-139-3p (41), we did not explore this relationship further. However, our analysis of TCGA database revealed a close association between ISG20L2 and NKX2-1. As a lineage-specific tumor-suppressor gene in lung cancer, NKX2-1 inversely regulates tumor-associated neutrophil accumulation, and its deletion significantly accelerates squamous lung tumorigenesis (42). We further found that NKX2-1 was underexpressed in LUAD and that ISG20L2 was significantly negatively correlated with NKX2-1 expression. Our battery of experiments confirmed ISG20L2 to be an upstream regulator of NKX2-1, and cell function assays indicated that ISG20L2 might promote LUAD cell proliferation and invasion by regulating NKX2-1 expression. The mechanism how ISG20L2 could regulate NKX2-1 expression is unknown.

This study provides new insights into the etiology and progression of LUAD; however, certain limitations should be noted. Importantly, we did not conduct an in-depth investigation into the specific mechanisms of action involved, nor did we examine the regulatory effects of ISG20L2 on lung cancer in vivo.

Conclusions

Our findings strongly suggest that ISG20L2 drives LUAD cell proliferation and invasion by regulating NKX2-1. The high expression of ISG20L2 correlated with a poorer prognosis among patients with LUAD, highlighting its potential as a critical therapeutic target and prognostic marker for LUAD. Our results indicate possible avenues for targeted therapies and personalized treatment strategies. However, to fully harness the therapeutic potential of ISG20L2, further research is essential to clarify the relevant mechanisms in detail and to confirm the study’s findings in vivo. Gaining a deeper understanding of ISG20L2’s role in lung cancer can advance the development of effective interventions and improve patient outcomes.

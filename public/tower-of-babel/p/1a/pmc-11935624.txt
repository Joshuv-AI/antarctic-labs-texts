Relationship Between Infarct Artery, Myocardial Injury, and Outcomes After Primary Percutaneous Coronary Intervention in ST‐Segment–Elevation Myocardial Infarction
Authors: Suzanne de Waha, Manesh R. Patel, Holger Thiele, James E. Udelson, Christopher B. Granger, Ori Ben‐Yehuda, Lak Kotinkaduwa, Björn Redfors, Ingo Eitel, Harry P. Selker, Akiko Maehara, Gregg W. Stone
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe extent to which infarct artery impacts the extent of myocardial injury and outcomes in patients with ST‐segment–elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) undergoing primary percutaneous coronary intervention is uncertain.Methods and ResultsWe performed a pooled analysis using individual patient data from 7 randomized STEMI trials in which myocardial injury within 30 days after primary percutaneous coronary intervention was assessed in 1774 patients by cardiac magnetic resonance (n=1318) or technetium‐99m sestamibi single‐photon emission computed tomography (n=456). Clinical follow‐up was performed at a median duration of 351 days (interquartile range, 184–368 days). Infarct size and outcomes were assessed in anterior (infarct vessel=left anterior descending) versus nonanterior (non–left anterior descending) STEMI. Median infarct size (percentage left ventricular myocardial mass) was larger in patients with anterior compared with nonanterior STEMI (19.7% [interquartile range, 9.4%–31.7%] versus 12.6% [interquartile range, 5.1%–20.5%]; P<0.001). Patients with anterior compared with nonanterior STEMI were at higher risk for 1‐year all‐cause mortality (6.2% versus 3.6%; adjusted hazard ratio [HR], 1.66 [95% CI, 1.02–2.69]; P=0.04) and heart failure hospitalization (4.4% versus 2.6%; adjusted HR, 1.96 [95% CI, 1.15–3.36]; P=0.01). Infarct size was a predictor of subsequent all‐cause mortality or heart failure hospitalization in anterior STEMI (adjusted HR per 1% increase, 1.05 [95% CI, 1.03–1.07]; P<0.001), but not in nonanterior STEMI (adjusted HR, 1.02 [95% CI, 0.99–1.05]; P=0.19). The P value for this interaction was 0.04.ConclusionsAnterior STEMI was associated with substantially greater myonecrosis after primary percutaneous coronary intervention compared with nonanterior STEMI, contributing in large part to the worse prognosis in patients with anterior infarction.

The prognosis in patients with ST‐segment–elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) has significantly improved in the past several decades, attributable to the routine use of primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) as well as advances in medical therapy. 1 , 2 However, outcomes have plateaued in the past 15 years, 3 , 4 , 5 and patients with STEMI still have high rates of morbidity and mortality. This emphasizes the need for new therapies to further improve outcomes in STEMI. Identification of high‐risk patients as well as clear therapeutic targets affecting prognosis may inform clinical practice and direct future avenues of clinical research in STEMI.

STEMI that occurs due to occlusion of the left anterior descending (LAD) coronary artery is well known to result in a worse prognosis than STEMI attributable to occlusion of the right coronary artery or left circumflex artery. 6 , 7 A greater extent of myonecrosis as well as maladaptive postinfarction left ventricular remodeling in anterior compared with nonanterior infarcts may contribute to the worse prognosis in LAD STEMI. 8 , 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 , 13 However, these observations were mainly derived from studies performed in the pre‐PCI era or were limited due to small sample size. Absent adequately powered contemporary studies, the extent to which infarct artery location is associated with infarct size and outcomes after primary PCI of the LAD compared with non‐LAD vessels is unknown.

From a collaborative individual patient data (IPD) pooled analysis of 10 trials of primary PCI in STEMI, we previously demonstrated a strong relationship between infarct size measured within 1 week and the 1‐year rates of death and heart failure hospitalization (HFH). 14 Using this database, we herein report the vessel‐specific outcomes of infarct size and prognosis after primary PCI.

METHODS

The data, methods used in the analysis, and materials used to conduct the research (beyond what is in the article) will not be made publicly available. However, the authors would consider collaborations with interested individuals. Please email any inquiries to gregg.stone@mountsinai.org.

As previously described, 14 the present study represents a collaborative effort between the principal investigators of 10 randomized trials that enrolled patients with STEMI undergoing primary PCI in whom acute ischemic myocardial injury was assessed with either technetium‐99m sestamibi single‐photon emission computed tomography (SPECT) or cardiac magnetic resonance imaging (CMR) at a core laboratory. Seven of these trials included patients with both anterior and nonanterior STEMI, 15 , 16 , 17 , 18 , 19 , 20 , 21 comprising the present study population; 3 other trials (AMIHOT‐II [Acute Myocardial Infarction With Hyperoxemic Reperfusion II], 22 INFUSE‐AMI [Infuse‐Anterior Myocardial Infarction], 23 and CRISP AMI [Counterpulsation to Reduce Infarct Size Pre‐PCI Acute Myocardial Infarction] 24 ) that enrolled only patients with anterior STEMI were excluded. In all studies, clinical follow‐up was performed for at least 6 months after primary PCI (unless death occurred earlier), and adverse outcomes were adjudicated by an independent clinical events committee. All 10 randomized trials were approved by an institutional review committee, and subjects gave informed consent. The individual patient data were entered into a common database at the Cardiovascular Research Foundation (New York, NY), as previously described. 14

The objectives of the current analysis were to examine the relationship between infarct artery, infarct size, and the occurrence of the prespecified end points of all‐cause mortality, HFH, and their combinations in anterior versus nonanterior STEMI. Our hypothesis was that after adjustment for differences in baseline clinical and angiographic variables, infarct size would be larger and independently correlated with all‐cause mortality and HFH in patients with anterior STEMI, but not necessarily in nonanterior STEMI. Anterior STEMI was defined as the infarct vessel being the LAD, whereas nonanterior STEMI was defined as the infarct vessel being the right coronary artery or left circumflex artery. Patients with missing data with respect to infarct artery were excluded.

Studies and Definitions

The 7 primary PCI trials included in the pooled analysis were (in approximate chronological order of patient enrolment): the EMERALD (Enhanced Myocardial Efficacy and Recovery by Aspiration of Liberated Debris) trial, in which patients undergoing primary PCI were randomized to a distal embolic protection filter versus control group without embolic protection 15 ; the infarct size substudy of the IMMEDIATE (Immediate Myocardial Metabolic Enhancement During Initial Assessment and Treatment in Emergency Care) trial, in which patients with non‐STEMI and STEMI were randomized to receive a prehospital intravenous glucose‐insulin‐potassium infusion versus placebo before primary PCI (only patients with STEMI from IMMEDIATE were included in this analysis) 16 ; the CMR substudy of the APEX‐AMI (Assessment of Pexelizumab in Acute Myocardial Infarction) trial, in which patients undergoing primary PCI were randomized to intravenous pexelizumab versus placebo 17 ; the LIPSIAbciximab (Leipzig Immediate Percutaneous Coronary Intervention Abciximab IV Versus IC in ST‐Elevation Myocardial Infarction) trial, in which patients undergoing primary PCI were randomized to an intracoronary versus intravenous bolus of abciximab 18 ; the LIPSIA‐N‐ACC (Prospective Single‐Blind Placebo‐Controlled Randomized Leipzig Immediate Percutaneous Coronary Intervention Acute Myocardial Infarction N‐ACC) trial, in which patients undergoing primary PCI were randomized to preprocedural high‐dose N‐acetylcysteine versus placebo 19 ; the LIPSIA‐STEMI (Leipzig Immediate Prehospital Facilitated Angioplasty in ST‐Elevation Myocardial Infarction) trial, in which patients with STEMI were randomized to either prehospital tenecteplase versus control before PCI 20 ; and the CMR substudy of the AIDA STEMI (Abciximab Intracoronary Versus Intravenous Drug Application in ST‐Elevation Myocardial Infarction) trial, in which patients undergoing primary PCI were randomized to an intracoronary versus intravenous bolus of abciximab. 21

Infarct size within 30 days after reperfusion was assessed by SPECT in 2 trials and by CMR in 5 trials. In all studies using microvascular obstruction (MVO), CMR imaging was performed within 1 week, also affording analysis of MVO. For patients in whom multiple imaging was performed, the earliest scan was used. MVO was assessed 10 to 15 minutes after gadolinium administration. Area at risk was determined by T2‐weighted CMR imaging in CMR and myocardial perfusion defect on SPECT images. The scan protocols and details of image analysis have previously been described. 14 All images were analyzed by an independent core laboratory masked to randomization assignment and outcomes. Imputation for missing data was not performed. Markers of acute ischemic myocardial injury included infarct size, area at risk, and MVO (among patients undergoing CMR). The definitions and adjudicated events from each study were used in the present analysis, as previously described. 14

Statistical Analysis

We have previously reported from the IPD pooled database that infarct size was a strong independent predictor of both death and HFH from the time of study entry to latest follow‐up in all patients. 14 In the present study, the impact of infarct size on all‐cause mortality, HFH, and the composite of all‐cause mortality or HFH were assessed separately in patients with anterior and nonanterior STEMI using Cox proportional hazards regression. The following variables, which were common in all the data sets, were entered into each model: age, sex, diabetes, symptom‐to‐first device time, baseline Thrombolysis In Myocardial Infarction (TIMI) flow 0/1 versus 2/3, and final TIMI flow 0/1 versus 2/3. The SEs in the regression equations were adjusted by including a random effect for study to account for heterogeneity across studies. The impact of anterior compared with nonanterior STEMI location on clinical outcomes was also assessed in all patients (including those without infarct size assessment), adjusted for age, sex, diabetes, symptom‐to‐first device time, baseline TIMI flow 0/1 versus 2/3, and final TIMI flow 0/1 versus 2/3. As a sensitivity analysis, we included MVO in addition to infarct size in the models from the 5 trials that assessed both by CMR. In each model, the proportional hazards assumption was confirmed by evaluating the interaction term of each covariate by time. Kaplan‐Meier curves were created for covariates that had significant interaction terms, and as long as the survival functions did not cross, those covariates were included in the model. The relationships between infarct size as a continuous value and outcomes were assessed by spline analysis with knots at the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentiles.

Categorical variables were compared using the χ2 test or Fisher exact test if the expected cell frequencies were <5. Continuous variables are displayed as medians with the corresponding interquartile range (IQR) and were compared using Wilcoxon rank‐sum test. To enable sample size calculations in future investigations, data on myocardial injury are also displayed as mean±SD. Time‐to‐event data are displayed as Kaplan‐Meier estimates. All P values are 2 sided, and P<0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed with SAS, version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC).

RESULTS

Trials and Patients

The design features of the 7 randomized primary PCI trials that enrolled both anterior and nonanterior STEMI included in the present IPD analysis are shown in Table S1. A total of 2182 patients with STEMI were enrolled, of whom 1774 (81.3%) underwent infarct size assessment within 1 month that was analyzable at the study core laboratory. Infarct size was assessed by CMR in 5 of the trials (1318 patients; 74.3% of the total) and by SPECT in 2 trials (456 patients; 25.7% of the total). The median time to infarct size measurement was 4 days (IQR, 2–7 days). The median duration from time of study entry to last clinical follow‐up for the entire study cohort was 351 days (IQR, 184–368 days).

Relationship Between Infarct Location, Myocardial Injury, and Outcomes

Median infarct size (expressed as percentage left ventricular myocardial mass) was larger in patients with anterior STEMI compared with nonanterior STEMI (median, 19.7% [IQR, 9.4%–31.7%] versus 12.6% [IQR, 5.1%–20.5%] / mean, 21.8%±15.8% versus 13.9%±10.9%; P<0.001; Figure 2A). Among patients with nonanterior STEMI, median infarct size was slightly larger with left circumflex artery occlusion compared with right coronary artery occlusion (14.0% [IQR, 7.9%–25.5%] versus 11.9% [IQR, 4.7%–19.6%]; P<0.001). The area at risk was also larger in anterior STEMI compared with nonanterior STEMI (median, 42.8% [IQR, 30.8%–53.5%] versus 32.0% [IQR, 23.2%–40.1%]; mean, 42.8%±15.6% versus 32.6%±13.8%; P<0.001; Figure 2B). The prevalence of any MVO was also higher in anterior STEMI compared with nonanterior STEMI (60.5% versus 53.0%; P=0.002), as was the extent of MVO (median, 0.6% [IQR, 0.0%–2.7%] versus 0.2% [IQR, 0.0%–1.4%]; mean, 2.1%±3.4% versus 1.2%±2.1%; P<0.001; Figure 2C).

In patients with anterior STEMI, infarct size was an independent predictor of the 1‐year rates of all‐cause mortality, HFH, and the composite of all‐cause mortality or HFH (Figure 3A, Table 2). Infarct size remained associated with all 3 clinical outcomes after adjustment for MVO (Table S2). A graded response was present between infarct size and all‐cause mortality and HFH in anterior STEMI (Figure 4A, Figure S1A and S1B). The relationship between infarct size in anterior STEMI and the 1‐year composite rate of all‐cause mortality or HFH was consistent across multiple subgroups (Figure S2A).

DISCUSSION

The present IPD pooled analysis from 7 randomized trials of primary PCI in STEMI demonstrates a strong relationship between infarct location, the extent of myocardial injury, and 1‐year clinical outcomes. Patients with anterior STEMI attributable to LAD occlusion were less likely to achieve successful epicardial reperfusion, had greater MVO, and had substantially larger infarct size than patients with nonanterior STEMI because of occlusion of the right coronary artery or left circumflex artery and were at higher subsequent risk for death and HFH within the following year. The relationship between infarct size and all‐cause death was especially strong in anterior STEMI, whereas infarct size after both anterior and nonanterior STEMI was an independent predictor of HFH.

Patients with anterior STEMI have historically been considered to be at greater risk for adverse clinical outcomes than patients with nonanterior STEMI. 25 Infarct location has thus been included in prognostic instruments, such as the Global Utilization of Streptokinase and Tissue Plasminogen Activator for Occluded Coronary Arteries (GUSTO) mortality model and the TIMI risk score. 26 , 27 However, most of these studies were performed before the widespread adoption of primary PCI. The prognostic impact of infarct location in patients undergoing treatment with primary PCI treated with contemporary guideline‐directed medical therapy for STEMI has not been fully characterized. To our knowledge, the present analysis is the largest such study to date to examine the relationships between infarct vessel, infarct size, and MVO, and clinical outcomes after primary PCI. PCI performance and adjunctive pharmacotherapy were consistent with present practice; median door‐to‐device time was ~40 minutes, and nearly all patients received stents and dual‐antiplatelet therapy at discharge. β‐Blockers, inhibitors of the renin‐angiotensin axis, and statins were each used in ~90% of patients. In this setting, the present study demonstrates that the 1‐year rates of death and HFH remain increased in patients with anterior STEMI compared with nonanterior STEMI. The fact that the 1‐year rates of mortality and mortality or HFH in patients with anterior STEMI in the present study were 6.2% and 8.6%, respectively, is notable as the patients enrolled in the included clinical trials had relatively few comorbidities (eg, only 21.3% had diabetes), had noncomplex coronary artery disease (disease limited to the LAD infarct vessel in 62.8% of patients), and were not in cardiogenic shock at presentation (an exclusion criterion in all the trials). Higher‐risk patients with anterior STEMI in the “real world” would doubtless have an even worse prognosis.

The mechanisms for the increased risks of death and HFH in patients with anterior STEMI have not been fully elucidated. Some studies concluded that anterior infarct location is associated with adverse clinical outcomes independent of the larger initial extent of ischemic damage. 8 , 9 Anterior infarct location itself is associated with a greater degree of adverse left ventricular remodeling compared with nonanterior infarction. 10 , 11 However, these studies were mostly performed in the pre‐PCI era and assessed infarct size by indirect measures, such as total cardiac biomarker release. Other studies found that the increased mortality in patients with anterior compared with nonanterior STEMI was mainly attributable to greater infarct size after LAD occlusion and was otherwise independent of infarct location. 12 , 13

In the present study, the percentage of the ventricle that was infarcted after primary PCI was substantially greater in STEMI involving the LAD compared with non‐LAD vessels. Every 1% increase in infarct size after primary PCI in anterior STEMI was associated with a 4% increase in 1‐year all‐cause mortality, independent of age, sex, diabetes, symptom onset to device time, as well as baseline and final TIMI flow. Interventions that reduce infarct size might therefore favorably affect prognosis in anterior STEMI (assuming no counterbalancing off‐target effects), and treatments to reduce adverse remodeling (such as angiotensin‐converting enzyme inhibitors) may be especially important in anterior MI, as was shown in the preprimary PCI era. In contrast, infarct size was smaller and not significantly associated with all‐cause death in nonanterior STEMI. Thus, interventions directed at reducing infarct size might be expected to have a neutral effect on survival in nonanterior STEMI. This may explain why some interventions, such as adenosine and supersaturated oxygen, were most effective in anterior infarction in both reducing infarct size and improving outcomes. 22 , 28 , 29 , 30 , 31 For this reason, studies of newer therapies, such as left ventricular unloading before primary PCI 20 , 21 and pressure‐controlled intermittent coronary sinus occlusion, 32 , 33 have been restricted to patients with anterior STEMI. However, infarct size in both LAD and non‐LAD infarctions was correlated with 1‐year rates of HFH, and thus a sufficiently large trial might show a clinical benefit of enhancing myocardial salvage in nonanterior as well as anterior infarcts for the outcomes of HFH or the composite of death or HFH.

In the present study, MVO was also more prevalent and extensive in anterior compared with nonanterior STEMI and may have contributed to the lower rate of normal (TIMI 3) epicardial coronary flow in patients with anterior STEMI. We have previously shown that the extent of MVO is moderately correlated with infarct size. 34 In the present study, after adjusting for MVO, infarct size remained associated with all‐cause mortality and HFH in anterior STEMI and with HFH in nonanterior STEMI, whereas MVO was not an independent predictor of either outcome in either myocardial territory. Thus, interventions that improve microcirculatory function after primary PCI might principally improve prognosis through a beneficial effect in reducing infarct size, especially in anterior STEMI.

Limitations

First, the definitions of the end points and their method of ascertainment and adjudication varied somewhat between studies, potentially adding imprecision. Second, several variables related to infarct size or survival in past studies, such as Killip class and angiographic collaterals, were not uniformly available and were thus not included in the present study. Third, given sample size considerations, we cannot exclude a modest relationship between infarct size and mortality in nonanterior STEMI. Fourth, although infarct size was measured early (median, 4 days), deaths occurring before infarct size assessment were not included in these analyses, and imputation was not used to replace missing data or infarct size measurements. However, as early deaths usually occur in patients with proximal coronary artery occlusion in large anterior STEMI, the present conclusions would likely have been strengthened had infarct size been available in these patients. Fifth, patients with cardiogenic shock were excluded from the component trials in the present database. Further study is thus warranted to examine the relationships between infarct size, outcomes, and interventions in patients with cardiogenic shock according to infarct vessel. Sixth, adjudication of mortality type was not uniformly performed in all studies, and the present study was not designed to determine the cause of death. However, it is likely that the relationship between infarct size and cardiac mortality would have been even stronger than that for all‐cause mortality, as described herein. Finally, the present studies were performed before adoption of several contemporary therapies that are now considered standard of care, including primary PCI performed via radial artery access, withholding use of glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitors for refractory thrombotic complications, and the routine use of angiotensin receptor neprilysin inhibitors and sodium‐glucose transport protein 2 inhibitors in patients with heart failure. Although these approaches may have improved the prognosis of patients with STEMI with large myocardial infarction, it is unlikely that they would have altered the fundamental relationships between infarct vessel, myocardial injury, and prognosis. These limitations notwithstanding, as the largest and most robust examination of the relationship between infarct location, myocardial injury, and prognosis after primary PCI to date, the data from the present report should be useful to inform clinical prognosis and guide future research efforts (eg, enable sample size estimation in trials of novel therapies to reduce infarct size and improve outcomes in anterior versus nonanterior STEMI).

CONCLUSIONS

In the present IPD pooled analysis from 7 randomized trials in which 1774 patients with STEMI underwent primary PCI and had early infarct size assessment, anterior STEMI was associated with larger infarct size, more frequent and extensive MVO, and a worse prognosis compared with nonanterior STEMI. Infarct size measured by CMR or SPECT after primary PCI was strongly related to the 1‐year rates of all‐cause mortality in anterior STEMI but not in nonanterior STEMI, although infarct size was associated with the 1‐year rates of HFH regardless of infarct artery. Infarct size may therefore be useful as an end point in clinical trials and as an important prognostic measure in patients with STEMI undergoing primary PCI, especially those with anterior infarction. In this regard we are hopeful that our reporting the mean±SD and median (IQR) measures of infarct size and MVO according to infarct vessel in patients with STEMI undergoing primary PCI proves useful to future investigators planning randomized trials of novel therapeutics to enhance myocardial recovery. The lack of progress in improving outcomes in patients with STEMI in the past 2 decades (after maximal reductions in door‐to‐balloon times have been realized) emphasizes the need for such new approaches. 1 , 3 , 4 , 5

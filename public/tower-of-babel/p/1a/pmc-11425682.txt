Clinicopathological analysis of nasopharyngeal tuberculosis
Authors: Gyu Kong, Jiwon Koh, Sue Jean Mun, Nakwon Kwak, Doo Hee Han
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe aim of this study is to examine the clinical and pathological attributes of nasopharyngeal tuberculosis.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective analysis of the clinicopathologic characteristics of nasopharyngeal tuberculosis in 14 patients. The medical records and imaging data obtained between March 2004 and February 2023 were scrutinized. During the pathological review, we classified the types of granulomatous inflammation and graded the extent of caseation.ResultsResults indicate a 100% female predominance, with chief complaints including hearing loss, postnasal drip, and nasal obstruction. Cervical lymphadenopathy occurred in 21.4% of patients. Chest radiograph abnormalities were found in 58.3%, with three showing active pulmonary tuberculosis. Endoscopic examination revealed three types of lesions, and CT/MRI findings correlated with gross lesions. A statistically significant association was found between lesion characteristics (bulging, ulcerative, necrotic) and pathology patterns (sarcoidosis-like, caseation). Bulging masses exhibited sarcoidosis-like patterns, while ulcerative/necrotic lesions were often associated with caseation. All lesions responded well to over six months of anti-tuberculosis medication, leading to favourable outcomes.ConclusionWe studied 14 cases of nasopharyngeal tuberculosis, mostly in females, with common ear and nose symptoms. Lesions were typically visible on nasopharyngeal endoscopy, and endoscopically bulging mass-like lesions had pathologically sarcoidosis-like granulomas. All patients had favourable outcomes.

Introduction

Despite advances in diagnostic and medical capabilities and improvements in socioeconomic status, tuberculosis remains a significant health concern in developing countries. The World Health Organization (WHO) announced that roughly 10.6 million cases of tuberculosis were recorded globally in 2021, indicating an incidence rate of 134 cases per 100,000 individuals [1]. According to a report by the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (https://www.kdca.go.kr/contents.es?mid=a30331000000), the number of tuberculosis cases peaked in 2011, and in 2016, a total of 30,892 new cases of tuberculosis were reported, indicating an incidence rate of 60.4 per 100,000 population including 12.1 extrapulmonary tuberculosis cases per 100,000 population.

In South Korea, intensified tuberculosis prevention and management policies for prevention, screening, and treatment, have been implemented since the late 2000s. As of 2021, 18,335 individuals were newly diagnosed with tuberculosis (incidence rate, 35.7 per 100,000 population). This represented a significant reduction of 53.6% over the past decade since its peak in 2011 when it recorded 39,557 cases, with an annual average decline rate of 7.4% [2].

Among the 18,335 individuals newly diagnosed with tuberculosis in 2021, 14,100 had pulmonary tuberculosis (27.5 per 100,000 population), representing 76.9% of the total and 4,235 had extrapulmonary tuberculosis (8.3 per 100,000 population), making up the remaining 23.1% [2]. Furthermore, males were disproportionately affected accounting for 58% of all cases of tuberculosis in Korea in 2021.

However, Korea ranks first among the 38 member countries of the Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development (OECD) in terms of tuberculosis incidence rate and third in terms of mortality rate, indicating a prevailing high incidence of tuberculosis within the country [1].

Although tuberculosis is relatively common in Korea, extrapulmonary tuberculosis, particularly in the nasopharynx, is rare. In the field of otorhinolaryngology, the diagnosis of nasopharyngeal tuberculosis is crucial because it shares similar symptoms with nasopharyngeal carcinoma, such as the presence of cervical lymphadenopathy, nasal discharge, or nasal obstruction, although their treatment and prognosis are markedly different.

In 1974, Page reported a case of tuberculosis of inferior turbinate, wherein he mentioned the presence of three types of tuberculosis of the nose and paranasal sinuses [3]. The first type is infection of the mucosa only type, which is the form of thickened mucosa and a boggy, pale appearance. This type is the most common with minimal purulent discharge. The second type has bony involvement and occasional fistula formation and purulent discharge, and tubercle bacilli are readily found. Third is the hyperplastic type, with the formation of tuberculoma and is the rarest type [3]. However, the classification of nasopharyngeal tuberculosis with thorough review of clinical impression and pathologic correlation has not been attempted and published yet. Pathologically, the natural course of tuberculosis is suggested to change from nascent, caseous to the fibrocaseous or resolved granuloma [4]. Authors presumed that those changes would be also found in the nasopharynx and there would be correlation between gross appearance and microscopic findings.

In the present study, authors reviewed our 14 case series from a single institute and proposed the classification of subtypes of nasopharyngeal tuberculosis according to clinical and pathologic findings.

Methods

Clinical review

Pathology reports of individuals diagnosed with tuberculosis of the nasopharynx between March 2004 and February 2023 were retrospectively analyzed. Fourteen cases were identified, and all biopsies were obtained by skilled otolaryngologists in either an outpatient clinic or an operating room. The examination of medical records encompassed several important variables including age, sex, site of infection, chief complaint, symptom duration, additional symptoms, endoscopic findings, duration of anti-tuberculosis medication, and treatment response. These variables were reviewed in detail to better understand the clinical course of the disease. Furthermore, to minimize potential bias, an experienced pathologist, who was blinded to the patients’ clinical information, reconfirmed the diagnoses of all biopsies.

Pathologic review

Biopsy specimens were fixed in 10% buffered formalin, embedded in paraffin blocks, and the slides were stained with hematoxylin and eosin stain (HE). Of the 14 cases, 13 were available for histopathological review. The AFB and PCR results of the formalin-fixed nasopharyngeal tissues were also reviewed. Caseous granulomas, a pathognomonic finding were found in all cases except two cases with minimal inflammation.

Regarding the granulomatous inflammation in each case, the following pathological characteristics were evaluated. The extent of granulomatous inflammation to entire specimen was evaluated and categorized into three groups (absent, vague, and sarcoidosis-like). The extent of caseation necrosis was graded into three groups: absent, focal (<10% within the examined tissue area), and extensive (≥10% within the examined tissue area). Based on the analysis of granulomas and caseous necrosis (caseation), the pathological types were classified into three groups: those with well-formed granulomas consisting of caseous granulomas and nascent type granulomas intermingled throughout the entire specimen were classified into the sarcoidosis-like granuloma group; those with vague granulomatous inflammation and necrosis were included in the caseous granuloma group; and those with no evidence of granulomatous inflammation were regarded as the non-specific inflammation group (Figure 1).

The pathological types of nasopharyngeal tuberculosis. The pathological types were classified into three groups according to the dominant pathological pattern. (A) sarcoidosis-like pattern, (B) caseous pattern and (C) non-specific inflammatory pattern (x4, x100 objective, HE stain).

Imaging review

Clinicopathologic information was correlated with the results of chest x-rays, head and neck CT scans, and MRI imaging of the patients.

Statistical analysis

Continuous data for both the bulging mass and infiltrative groups were analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test, while the Fisher’s exact test for crosstabs was used to analyze non-continuous variables. The parametric results were reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and a p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant for all parameters.

Ethics statement

The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board of Seoul National University Hospital. The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study (approval No.1901-153-1006).

Results

Clinical characteristics

The medical records of the 14 patients were reviewed. All the included patients were women with a mean age of 42.1 years and median age of 40.5 years (range 23 to 67 years). The mean symptom duration was 3.5 months (range 1 to 12 months). Involvement of the left side (10/14, 71.4%) was more frequent than involvement of the right side and the middle part of the nasoopharynx (2/14, 14.2%, each).

The most common chief complaints were ipsilateral hearing loss, ear fullness and otorrhea induced by otitis media (6/14, 42.8%), followed by post-nasal drip (5/14, 38.5%), nasal obstruction (4/14, 28.5%), throat pain (1/14, 7.1%), and neck mass (1/14, 7.1%). Six patients with ear symptoms had abnormalities in the middle ear such as middle ear effusion and tympanic membrane retraction or perforation, which were readily noticeable on physical examination. One patient had a total perforation of the tympanic membrane, which seemed to be a chronic effect of Eustachian tube dysfunction. She also had facial nerve palsy and was diagnosed with tuberculosis of the otitis media. She underwent canal wall-up mastoidectomy followed by ventilation tube insertion to manage Eustachian tube dysfunction. Later, with anti-tuberculosis medication and successful ear surgery, the facial nerve palsy improved. Five patients complained of postnasal drip and four patients had nasal obstruction. One patient complained of throat pain, and another had a neck mass accompanying nasal obstruction. Although only one patient visited the hospital because of a neck mass, lymphadenopathy was found in three patients (3/14 21.4%) by imaging work up. Symptoms due to nerve involvement such as dysarthria and facial hypoesthesia were also observed in one patients (case 10, Table 1). In this patient, a nasopharyngeal mass that crossed the midline and infiltrated the skull base, including the jugular fossa area, and the parapharyngeal space was identified. Initially, the patient was suspected of having nasopharyngeal cancer. Finally, the mass was diagnosed as tuberculosis by biopsy, and she received anti-tuberculosis medication. Interestingly, the cranial nerve symptoms were relieved and reversed after treatment with anti-tuberculosis medication.

Endoscopic examination revealed three patterns of nasopharyngeal tuberculosis. The most distinguishable pattern was a polypoid mass-like lesion resembling the normal adenoid tissue. The second pattern included ulcerative lesions with surface ulceration. The final pattern was a necrotic lesion, typically whitish in colour, and infiltrating the Eustachian tube (Figure 2A–C). We suspected that these three patterns of nasopharyngeal tuberculosis may indicate different pathological processes or various stages of tuberculosis. Consequently, the clinical impression led to the subdivision of nasopharyngeal tuberculosis into three subgroups.

The duration of anti-tuberculosis medication varied from 6 to 12 months. Excluding one patient who was lost to follow up, all symptoms and nasopharyngeal lesions resolved with anti-tuberculosis medication (Figure 2D–F).

Imaging characteristics

The preferred imaging modality differed according to the doctors. Seven patients underwent only head and neck CT imaging, while four patients underwent only MRI neck imaging. Three patients underwent both CT and MRI. Two patterns were observed on the head and neck CT images (Figure 3A,B). The first pattern was a bulging mass lesion on the posterior nasopharygeal wall, and the other pattern was the absence of discrete mass or infiltrative lesion in the nasopharynx with a diffuse mucosal enhancement, corresponding to the endoscopic findings. On MRI, the first pattern was a discrete moderately enhancing homogenous polypoid mass in the central roof and upper posterior wall of the nasopharynx observed in T1 images. The second pattern had mild diffuse mucosal thickening on the affected side of the Eustachian tube (Figure 3C,D). When comparing these two different imaging modalities in the three patients who both CT and MRI, the bony structure and lymph nodes were better evaluated using CT imaging, and soft tissue differentiation was better evaluated with MRI. Most radiologists provided differential diagnoses of lymphoma, nasopharyngeal cancer, and nasopharyngeal tuberculosis, which align with what has been reported in previous literature [5–8].

Seven of 12 (58.3%) patients had abnormal findings on simple chest X-ray including three with active pulmonary tuberculosis (cases 10, 11, and 12 in Table 1). Most patients had old chest lesions, such as calcified granulomas or nodules. Five patients had normal chest X-ray, while the other two did not undergo chest X-ray.

Pathologic characteristics and their association with endoscopic findings

Chronic granulomatous inflammation with or without caseous necrosis and variable lymphoplasmacytic and neutrophilic infiltrations were observed in all cases except two (data not shown). Only two of ten biopsies showed acid-fast bacilli in the Ziehl-Neelsen stain. Nine of twelve PCR for tuberculosis were positive.

The endoscopic finding of nasopharyngeal lesions (bulging mass-like, ulcerative and necrotic) and the extent of granuloma formation (absent, vague, and sarcoidosis-like) in nasopharyngeal biopsy tissue were statistically associated (Fisher’s exact test, p < 0.05, 0.0287). There was a significant statistical correlation (Fisher’s exact test, p < 0.05, 0.0209) between the endoscopic identification of nasopharyngeal lesions (bulging mass-like, ulcerative, and necrotic) and pathologic patterns (non-specific, sarcoidosis-like, and caseatious; Figure 3). Lesions that appeared as bulging masses were confirmed to have a sarcoidosis-like pathologic pattern in 100% of cases, and caseation was not the main feature. Ulcerative lesions were classified as having a sarcoidosis-like pattern in 60% (3/5) and a caseation pattern in 20% (1/5) of the cases. Necrotic lesions did not show a sarcoidosis-like pattern and 80% (4/5) had a caseation pattern. Endoscopic necrotic lesions were often found to have caseous necrosis as the main feature on pathologic examination. However, there was no significant association between the endoscopic findings of nasopharyngeal tuberculosis lesions and the extent of caseous necrosis which was graded into absent, focal and extensive (Table 1; p > 0.05, 0.8801).

Discussion

Our results demonstrate our 20-year experience with nasopharyngeal tuberculosis, a rare disease entity, among the Korean population at a single centre. Comprehensive reports on nasopharyngeal tuberculosis published in English are rare. Notably, the extant literature comprises reports of 17 cases in 2003, 23 cases in 2004, and 10 cases in 1992, all from antiquated sources [9–11]. Thus, the present investigation assumes a pivotal scholarly stance, as it constitutes the most contemporary analysis encompassing 14 cases.

Interestingly, in our study, a female predominance of nasopharyngeal tuberculosis was found in contrast to male predominance in pulmonary tuberculosis in Korea. A previous study with 17 cases also reported a female predominance [9]. According to previous literature, nasopharyngeal tuberculosis has been documented with a slightly higher incidence among females. Notably, in this study, all the included patients were female.

However, the most common presentation was different from that reported in previous studies. The most commonly reported symptom of nasopharyngeal tuberculosis is cervical lymphadenitis [9, 10]. In our current study, cervical lymphadenitis was the primary presentation in only one case, and cervical lymphadenopathy was identified in three cases through imaging examinations during the diagnostic process. The most frequent chief complaint was ipsilateral otologic symptoms such as hearing loss and ear fullness followed by postnasal drip, nasal obstruction, pain, and neck mass in contrast to the previous reports of cervical lymphadenitis, followed by hearing loss, tinnitus, otalgia, nasal obstruction and postnasal drip. This trend is likely due to the increased availability of endoscopic examinations and enhancements medical accessibility. Consequently, it is speculated that symptoms related to the primary nasopharyngeal site and anatomically adjacent regions are recognized before the onset of cervical lymphadenitis.

The occurrence of concurrent active or previous pulmonary involvement is relatively infrequent, accounting for only 30-40% according to previous studies [9, 10]. In our series of patients 58.3% (7/12) had concurrent radiologic pulmonary abnormalities, including three patients with active pulmonary tuberculosis.

As an extension of the discussion on pulmonary involvement in nasopharyngeal tuberculosis, it is unclear whether nasopharyngeal tuberculosis is a secondary disease. In a study analyzing 75 cases of biopsy-proven cervical tuberculous lymphadenopathy without lung or systemic involvement, cases where the nasopharynx was estimated to be the only primary focus were very rare and accounted for approximately 1% [12]. Additionally, even in cases of pulmonary tuberculosis, nasopharyngeal involvement was very low at 1.8% [13]. Therefore, it is suggested that nasopharyngeal tuberculosis may be either a primary disease or occur in isolation.

Radiologically, there was a case review of MRI with nasopharyngeal tuberculosis [14]. They found that there are two patterns of nasopharyngeal tuberculosis in MRI: a discrete polypoid mass in the adenoids and a more diffuse soft-tissue thickening of one or two of the walls of nasopharynx, which corresponds to our findings. In our study, the CT images were compared. There is no generally accepted concept regarding which imaging modality is better for evaluating nasopharyngeal tuberculosis. In our study, the patterns in the nasopharyngeal wall were compatible in two images; however, when cranial nerve symptom were present, MRI was better at showing the exact boundaries of the disease extension.

It is interesting to note that in our study, in contrast to Page’s study in 1974, the hyperplastic type that seemed to be equal to bulging mass type in our study was the rarest type at that time, but is now the most frequent type of nasopharyngeal tuberculosis. The reported types with bony involvement and sometimes with fistula formation with purulent discharge are not found these days [3]. The reason for this may be related to the early diagnosis of nasopharyngeal lesions due to development of endoscopy compared to the 1970s and the improvement of anti-tuberculosis medication. Nowadays, otorhinolaryngologist routinely perform panendoscopic examination including nasopharynx when ipsilateral middle ear effusion is suggested, and the detection rate of nasopharyngeal mass or mucosal lesion has increased. In addition, improvements in anti-tuberculosis medication decrease the development of destructive tuberculosis lesions [15].

Pathologic and clinical correlation analyses of nasopharyngeal tuberculosis revealed that a sarcoidosis-like type granuloma, which is pathologically distinctive, was frequently observed during the endoscopic examination as a bulging mass with minimal tissue destruction. The occurrence of sarcoidosis-like type granulomas presenting as a necrotic type on endoscopy was almost nonexistent. Notably, this study is the first to demonstrate a correlation between pathological findings and endoscopic morphology. This study also provides a valuable diagnostic indication that a subset of patients with nasopharyngeal tuberculosis can present with pathologically sarcoidosis-like granulomatous inflammation and a morphologically adenoid-like bulging mass type, ruling out the possibility that tuberculosis in this setting can result in misdiagnosis, especially in tuberculosis-endemic areas. A thorough review of the clinical information should accompany the pathological diagnosis, and proper ancillary testing should be performed even in the presence of sarcoidosis-like granulomas.

Currently, there is no universally acknowledged classification system for the different types of granulomas. One strategy is to categorize them into primary or secondary granulomas based on the immune cells [16, 17]. Another approach is to classify granulomas according to their physical qualities and features, including solidity, caseation and cavitation, necrosis, and non-necrosis [4, 16, 18–20]. The immunological significance of caseation granuloma in tuberculosis is not well understood. One of the primary functions of granulomas, which can be considered most beneficial to the host, is their role in facilitating resolution. A solid granuloma serves as a barrier against the development of an active disease because its limited blood supply restricts the spread of pathogens. In contrast, when caseation occurs, it can lead to disease reactivation [21]. The direction in which a granuloma progresses, whether it transforms into a sterile granuloma leading to disease resolution or becomes a disseminating granuloma causing bacterial dissemination and chronic infection, depends on various influencing factors. These factors include the balance of inflammatory cytokines and the appropriateness of the host’s immune response, bacterial virulence, and immunomodulation [17, 22]. In this study, data on bacterial loading was not obtained; therefore, there is insufficient evidence to consider bulging mass-like types and sarcoidosis-like granulomas that appear to be more morphologically localized as evidence of mild disease severity or bacterial localization and eradication processes. Furthermore, despite morphological and histological differences among all cases, the same anti-tuberculosis medication was used, and treatment outcomes were good, making it difficult to determine their correlation with disease severity.

This study has several limitations. The classification of the pathological tissues used in the analysis may not be representative of the entire lesion. Additionally, since the examination was performed only on lesions collected at a single time point and no histopathological data were collected over time, there is a possibility that the entire tuberculous granuloma that manifested during the patient’s progress may not be represented temporally. Thus, the significance of differences in granuloma architecture related to the gross findings observed through endoscopic examinations in this study remains elusive. However, the extensive spectrum and heterogeneity of granulomas are closely related to their ultimate fate. Therefore, further research may shed light on its clinical relevance given this established understanding. Another noteworthy limitation is the exclusive observation of female cases in nasopharyngeal tuberculosis within this study. While the gender homogeneity of the sample poses a limitation, it is pertinent to acknowledge that previous literature similarly indicates a predominance of female cases. The interpretation of these findings as indicative solely of the ethnic homogeneity of Koreans or suggestive of potential gender-specific variations in symptom sensitivity remains uncertain. Consequently, there is a pressing need for future large-scale studies with sustained data collection to elucidate these matters.

One of the strengths of this study lies in its analysis of a relatively large number of cases of a rare entity disease, nasopharyngeal tuberculosis. Furthermore, as the research was conducted after the widespread adoption of endoscopy in otolaryngological physical examinations, it provided evidence for the differential diagnosis of nasopharyngeal tuberculosis through direct, three-dimensional examination of the anatomically deep-seated nasopharynx. This allowed for analysis of its morphology and its correlation with the pathological characteristics of tuberculosis. While the study did not definitively establish whether the morphological features of endoscopic nasopharyngeal tuberculosis lesions are prognostically significant, indirect inferences regarding tuberculosis staging were possible. Specifically, while all cases were curable with six months of anti-tuberculosis medication, there is a possibility that surgically excising more localized bulging masses may offer a more favourable treatment outcome, or based on pathological characteristics, one could infer the eventual resolution of granulomas. However, scientific analysis to substantiate these claims is currently lacking, thus necessitating preliminary biomolecular studies.

There has been a few case reports on nasopharyngeal tuberculosis and clinical review of nasopharyngeal tuberculosis. However, the classification of nasopharyngeal tuberculosis with a thorough review of clinical impressions, imaging, and pathologic correlations has not yet been attempted and published. In this study, we provide a comprehensive analysis and characterization of the pathological types of tuberculous granulomas, with particular attention given to the morphological features of nasopharyngeal tuberculous lesions.

Despite being a single-centre study, the data spanning 20 years from a tertiary referral hospital is deemed to hold valuable clinical insights regarding the management of the rare condition of nasopharyngeal tuberculosis. The fact that there are only about three published large series, with more than 10 cases each, accessible in English, further underscores the rarity of this condition [9–11]. Considering tuberculosis is relatively common in Korea, this study is considered valuable as the first large case series in Korea shedding light on the clinical and pathological characteristics of nasopharyngeal tuberculosis.

To conclude, in this study, we analyzed 14 patients with nasopharyngeal tuberculosis and found that it predominantly occurs in females. Ear- and nose-related symptoms were the most common, and in most cases, lesions could be identified using nasopharyngeal endoscopy. When the lesions appeared as grossly bulging masses on endoscopic examination, they pathologically were sarcoidosis-like granuloma. Regardless of gross appearance and pathological type, all cases showed excellent outcomes. The clinical significance of the relationship between gross appearance and pathological type may become clearer with a larger number of cases and consideration of their immunological markers, among other factors.

Advances in the study of pyrotinib for the treatment of HER2-positive breast cancer
Authors: Yan Li, Wen Wang, Yan Liang, Houyun Lai, Kun Mi, Purong Zhang, Jianhui Zhang, Yixin Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
HER2-positive breast cancer is characterized by aggressive tumor biology and intrinsic resistance to conventional therapies, underscoring the need for novel targeted treatment approaches. Pyrotinib, an irreversible pan-HER tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI), covalently binds to the intracellular kinase domains of HER1 (EGFR), HER2, and HER4, thereby inhibiting downstream signaling pathways and inducing tumor cell apoptosis. Clinical investigations have consistently demonstrated the substantial therapeutic efficacy of pyrotinib in both early-stage and advanced HER2-positive breast cancer. Moreover, its ability to traverse the blood-brain barrier confers potential therapeutic advantages in patients with brain metastases. This comprehensive review provides a systematic overview of the pharmacological profile and mechanism of action of pyrotinib, with emphasis on recent clinical trials assessing pyrotinib-based regimens in HER2-positive breast cancer.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13058-026-02255-2.

Introduction

Breast cancer remains one of the most prevalent malignancies among women globally, with persistently high incidence and mortality rates representing a major burden on public health systems [1–3]. Based on molecular biomarkers including estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), breast cancer is classified into three major subtypes: hormone receptor (HR)-positive/HER2-negative (approximately 70%), HER2-positive (15–20%), and triple-negative (lacking all three standard molecular markers, around 15%) [4]. Among these subtypes, HER2-positive breast cancer poses a significant clinical challenge due to its aggressive tumor biology, high risk of recurrence and distant metastasis, and poor overall prognosis5,6. Notably, up to 50% of patients with metastatic disease develop brain metastases, a complication associated with rising annual incidence [5, 6]. Notably, up to 50% of individuals diagnosed with HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer eventually develop brain metastases, with the reported annual incidence showing a rising trend [7]. The integrity of the blood-brain barrier (BBB) substantially restricts the effective penetration and distribution of many therapeutic agents, thus constituting a significant impediment to targeted drug delivery within the central nervous system (CNS) [8]. This poses a considerable challenge for large-molecule therapeutics such as monoclonal antibodies, exemplified by trastuzumab and pertuzumab [7, 9]. Therefore, the development of small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) capable of effectively cross the BBB has emerged as a crucial area of investigation in the management of HER2-positive breast cancer, especially in those with brain metastases [7, 10].

Pyrotinib, an orally administered irreversible HER TKI, received regulatory approval in China in 2018 [11]. It exerts potent, irreversible inhibition against the tyrosine kinase domains of HER1 (EGFR), HER2, and HER4, leading to suppression of downstream signaling cascades and consequent inhibition of tumor cell proliferation [12,13]. Pyrotinib possesses a molecular formula of C32H31ClN6O3 and a calculated molecular weight of 583.1 Da [14]. Owing to its distinctive pharmacokinetic profile—including the ability to penetrate the BBB—and encouraging preclinical activity, multiple clinical studies have been initiated to assess both the safety and antitumor efficacy of pyrotinib across diverse clinical scenarios and combination strategies in HER2-positive breast cancer. This review aims to provide a comprehensive overview of recent clinical developments concerning pyrotinib in the context of HER2-positive breast cancer, focusing on its mode of action, safety characteristics, and emerging therapeutic opportunities. These insights will serve as a foundation for optimizing clinical utilization and informing future translational research endeavors.

Mechanism of action and pharmacological properties of pyrotinib

As a novel irreversible TKI targeting HER1, HER2, and HER4, Pyrotinib has been approved by the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) for the treatment of patients with metastatic HER2-positive breast cancer [15]. Despite its clinical efficacy, the precise molecular mechanisms underlying pyrotinib-induced tumor cell death remain incompletely understood. Preclinical evidence from both in vitro and in vivo models indicates that pyrotinib exerts its antitumor effects through multiple mechanisms (Fig. 1). These include: (1) induction of HER2 endocytosis and ubiquitin-dependent degradation, leading to marked downregulation of HER2 protein levels; (2) disruption of the interaction between HER2 and heat shock protein 90 (HSP90), further promoting receptor degradation; and (3) inhibition of downstream PI3K/AKT and MEK/ERK signaling pathways, ultimately suppressing tumor cell proliferation, enhancing apoptosis, and reducing angiogenesis [16–18]. The HER family consists of three major structural domains: an extracellular ligand-binding region, a transmembrane domain, and an intracellular tyrosine kinase domain [19].

Upon ligand binding, receptors undergo homo- or heterodimerization, which activates downstream signaling cascades that drive cellular proliferation, survival, and resistance to apoptosis [19, 20]. In contrast to monoclonal antibodies like trastuzumab, which bind the extracellular domain of HER2 [21], pyrotinib targets the intracellular tyrosine kinase domain [16], enabling synergistic or complementary activity when used in combination with antibody-based therapies (Fig. 2). Moreover, due to its multi-targeted inhibitory profile against HER1, HER2, and HER4, pyrotinib may offer enhanced antitumor activity in HER2-low or heterogeneous tumors [22].

Additionally, as an irreversible pan-HER TKI, pyrotinib forms a covalent bond within the ATP-binding pocket of the kinase domain, effectively blocking the activity of all three HER family members simultaneously. This covalent binding enables sustained inhibition across various HER2 isoforms and mutant variants, providing potential advantages over reversible inhibitors such as lapatinib and tucatinib, particularly in cancers harboring kinase domain mutations [23]. Among known HER2 mutations, L755S and exon 20 insertions are critical mediators of resistance to reversible TKIs [24, 25]. The L755S mutation destabilizes the inactive conformation of the kinase domain, reducing the affinity of reversible inhibitors. In contrast, irreversible agents like pyrotinib maintain their inhibitory potency through stable covalent linkage to the catalytic cleft [26]. However, the gatekeeper mutation T798I induces steric hindrance that obstructs drug binding to the ATP-binding pocket, leading to acquired resistance even to irreversible TKIs such as neratinib [27]. To date, there remains insufficient evidence to support the ability of pyrotinib to overcome this specific resistance mechanism. Notably, emerging evidence indicates that pyrotinib not only directly suppresses HER pathway activation but also modulates the tumor stromal architecture and immune landscape by mitigating CAF-induced immunosuppression, thereby demonstrating the potential to overcome certain drug resistance mechanisms in HER2-positive breast cancer [28]. Collectively, these attributes—namely irreversible inhibition and broad-spectrum HER targeting—position pyrotinib as a potent agent capable of overcoming HER2 mutation-driven resistance, particularly involving L755S and exon 20 insertions. However, its efficacy remains limited against steric hindrance induced by gatekeeper mutations (such as T798I), underscoring the need for future combination strategies that integrate structure-guided drug design with tumor microenvironment modulation to enhance pyrotinib’s anti-resistance capabilities.

Pharmacokinetic analyses have revealed that pyrotinib exhibits excellent oral bioavailability and achieves appreciable CNS exposure owing to effective BBB penetration [29]. Peak plasma concentrations are achieved within 3–4 h after dosing, with an elimination half-life of 7–9 h, supporting sustained therapeutic exposure amenable to once-daily administration [29]. The primary dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) associated with pyrotinib monotherapy is grade 3 diarrhea, and the maximum tolerated dose has been defined as 400 mg/day [13]. Pyrotinib undergoes extensive hepatic metabolism, predominantly mediated by cytochrome P450 3A4 (CYP3A4) [30]. Consequently, concurrent use of potent CYP3A4 inhibitors, such as itraconazole, can elevate systemic drug exposure and increase the likelihood of adverse effects. Therefore, dose modifications are advised to minimize safety concerns [31]. Adverse events commonly associated with pyrotinib include diarrhea, nausea, stomatitis, fatigue, and leukopenia; however, most manifest as mild-to-moderate severity (grade 1–2) and are typically manageable [13]. Additionally, population pharmacokinetic modeling has demonstrated that variables such as patient age and serum total protein exert minimal influence on pyrotinib’s volume of distribution, suggesting that dosage adaptation according to these parameters is not warranted [29]. Collectively, these advantageous pharmacokinetic characteristics offer compelling preclinical and clinical support for the continued development and therapeutic application of pyrotinib.

Clinical studies of pyrotinib

Advanced HER2-positive breast cancer

Advanced HER2-positive breast cancer represents the clinical setting in which pyrotinib has been most extensively evaluated. Clinical investigations in this population have primarily focused on its efficacy and safety across different lines of systemic therapy, including first-line and subsequent-line treatment. The following sections summarize key clinical evidence according to treatment line.

First-line therapy

Early phase I dose-escalation studies have demonstrated that pyrotinib monotherapy exhibits promising antitumor activity and an manageable safety profile in patients with HER2-positive advanced breast cancer, providing a rationale foundation for subsequent clinical investigations [13]. Further in vitro and in vivo experimental results have shown that in HER2-dependent breast cancer models, the combination of pyrotinib and trastuzumab exhibits a stronger inhibitory effect on cell growth than monotherapy, suggesting that the combination of the two may have complementary or enhanced antitumor potential [32, 33]. Building upon these encouraging preclinical findings, the Phase III PHILA study adopted a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design to evaluate the clinical efficacy of pyrotinib in addition to standard first-line therapy comprising trastuzumab and docetaxel in HER2-positive advanced breast cancer [34]. Patients were randomly assigned to receive either pyrotinib or placebo in combination with trastuzumab and docetaxel. Results showed a median PFS of 24.3 months in the pyrotinib arm versus 10.4 months in the control arm (HR = 0.41; 95% CI: 0.32–0.54, P < 0.001), representing one of the longest PFS durations reported in first-line advanced HER2-positive breast cancer [34]. This represents one of the most prolonged PFS outcomes reported to date in first-line treatment settings, establishing the significant therapeutic value of pyrotinib in the management of HER2-positive advanced breast cancer [34]. These results firmly established pyrotinib as a preferred therapeutic option for HER2-positive advanced breast cancer [34]. Consequently, the NMPA approved pyrotinib in combination with trastuzumab and docetaxel as a first-line treatment for HER2-positive advanced breast cancer in April 2023. The Phase II PANDORA study, a multicenter, single-arm, Simon two-stage exploratory trial, was conducted to assess the efficacy and safety of pyrotinib combined with docetaxel as a first-line regimen in patients with HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer who had not previously received HER2-targeted therapy or chemotherapy for metastatic disease [35]. The study reported an objective response rate (ORR) of 79.7%, a median PFS of 16 months, and a CNS event rate of only 7.6%, suggesting potential benefits in the management of brain metastases [35]. However, due to its limited sample size and lack of a comparator arm, further validation through randomized controlled trials is warranted [35].

Beyond prospective trials, retrospective and real-world evidence has corroborated the effectiveness and tolerability of the pyrotinib–trastuzumab-based regimen. A retrospective analysis suggested the significant efficacy of the triplet combination of pyrotinib, trastuzumab, and chemotherapy in HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer, with the duration of prior anti-HER2 therapy identified as a potential predictive factor for response to this regimen [36]. Consistent findings across multiple studies have confirmed that dual HER2 blockade using pyrotinib and trastuzumab offers robust antitumor activity while maintaining manageable toxicity in advanced settings [37, 38]. Real-world data from a cohort of 239 patients indicated median PFS values of 14.00 months for first-line, 9.33 months for second-line, and 8.20 months for third-line or later therapies; corresponding median overall survival (OS) estimates were unreached for first-line use, 29.07 months for second-line, and 22.23 months for subsequent lines [39]. Collectively, evidence from preclinical research, randomized controlled trials, and real-world practice consistently underscores the pivotal role of pyrotinib in the first-line management of HER2-positive advanced breast cancer. Nevertheless, future multicenter, international, prospective studies are essential to refine treatment strategies and optimize patient selection.

Second-line therapy

Capecitabine, an oral antimetabolite chemotherapeutic agent, is widely used to manage advanced breast cancer. In August 2018, the NMPA granted conditional approval for pyrotinib in combination with capecitabine for the treatment of patients with HER2-positive recurrent or metastatic breast cancer, irrespective of prior trastuzumab exposure [15]. This approval was based on promising results from early phase monotherapy studies of pyrotinib and was further supported by multiple clinical trials that confirmed the efficacy and safety of the pyrotinib–capecitabine regimen.

An open-label, multicenter, randomized phase II trial assigned patients with HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer previously treated with taxanes, anthracyclines, and/or trastuzumab to receive either pyrotinib plus capecitabine or lapatinib plus capecitabine [40]. The ORR was 78.5% in the pyrotinib group versus 57.1% in the lapatinib group, with a median PFS of 18.1 months and 7.0 months, respectively. These findings demonstrated that pyrotinib plus capecitabine conferred significantly superior antitumor activity compared to lapatinib plus capecitabine in patients previously treated with taxanes, anthracyclines, and/or trastuzumab [40]. Subsequently, the Phase III PHOEBE trial, a randomized, open-label, multicenter study, further validated the clinical benefits of this combination regimen [41]. Among patients with HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer who had previously exposed to trastuzumab and taxane therapy, pyrotinib significantly prolonged the median PFS compared with lapatinib (12.5 vs. 6.8 months; HR = 0.39, P < 0.0001), supporting its role pyrotinib as a standard second-line therapeutic option [41]. However, OS data were not yet mature at the time of analysis, and HER2 status was not centrally confirmed in some patients, highlighting the need for extended follow-up. Further evidence from the Phase III PHENIX trial corroborated the clinical benefit of pyrotinib plus capecitabine in patients who had progressed following trastuzumab-based therapy [42]. The median PFS was 11.1 months in the pyrotinib group versus 4.1 months in the placebo plus capecitabine group, indicating a substantial improvement in disease control among trastuzumab-pretreated patients with HER2-positive recurrent or metastatic breast cancer [42]. Preliminary studies have demonstrated that pyrotinib combined with capecitabine during concurrent radiotherapy achieves a relatively high intracranial response rate and shows a trend toward improved survival in patients with HER2-positive brain metastases [43, 44]. These findings suggest that this therapeutic strategy holds promise for clinical application; however, further validation through prospective studies is warranted.

In addition to its use in combination with capecitabine, pyrotinib combined with vinorelbine has also shown promising efficacy and manageable toxicity across multiple studies in the second-line setting for HER2-positive advanced breast cancer [45–49]. These findings expand the therapeutic utility of pyrotinib in combination with various chemotherapy strategies and provide additional options for personalized and optimized treatment approaches.

Early-stage HER2-positive breast cancer

Based on the encouraging efficacy observed in advanced disease, subsequent studies have explored the application of pyrotinib in early-stage HER2-positive breast cancer, including both neoadjuvant and adjuvant treatment settings.

Neoadjuvant therapy

Neoadjuvant therapy aims to reduce tumor burden or achieve a pathological complete response (pCR), thereby enhancing surgical resectability and improving long-term survival outcomes [50]. The NOAH trial was the first to demonstrate the clinical benefit of trastuzumab monotherapy in the neoadjuvant setting for HER2-positive breast cancer, laying the groundwork for HER2-targeted strategies [51]. Subsequently, dual HER2 blockade based on trastuzumab became a major area of investigation [23]. Small-molecule TKIs irreversibly inhibit downstream HER2 signaling, offering a complementary mechanism to trastuzumab and enabling more comprehensive pathway suppression [52]. A meta-analysis revealed that the combination of lapatinib and trastuzumab significantly prolonged PFS (HR = 0.62, 95% CI 0.46–0.85) and OS (HR = 0.65, 95% CI 0.43–0.98) compared with trastuzumab alone, underscoring the synergistic effect of dual HER2 inhibition [53].

Building upon these findings, the clinical evaluation of pyrotinib, an irreversible pan-ErbB receptor TKI, as a neoadjuvant agent has advanced rapidly. The phase III, randomized, double-blind, controlled PHEDRA trial demonstrated a significantly higher total pathological complete response (tpCR) rate in patients receiving pyrotinib plus trastuzumab and docetaxel than in those receiving the control regimen (41% vs. 22%), providing the first robust evidence supporting the efficacy of irreversible HER2 TKIs in the neoadjuvant setting [54]. Furthermore, a phase II study (NCT03756064) showed that the addition of carboplatin to this triplet regimen further enhanced the tpCR rates [55]. Similarly, the NeoPATHer trial confirmed that integrating pyrotinib into standard trastuzumab-containing regimens provided additional pathological response benefits, particularly among patients with suboptimal responses to conventional therapies [56].

The EC-TH regimen, which includes epirubicin, cyclophosphamide, taxanes (paclitaxel or docetaxel), and trastuzumab, is widely used in both neoadjuvant and adjuvant settings for breast cancer. In operable or locally advanced HER2-positive breast cancer, a phase II trial conducted by Professor Jun Jiang’s group reported that the addition of pyrotinib to the EC-TH regimen yielded a tpCR rate of 73.7%, nearly double that observed with standard EC-TH, while maintaining manageable toxicity. However, this single-arm study was limited by a small sample size and absence of a randomized comparator, limiting definitive conclusions about incremental benefit [57]. Subsequently, a multicenter, single-arm trial evaluating the sequential ECPy-THPy regimen (epirubicin, cyclophosphamide, and pyrotinib followed by docetaxel, trastuzumab, and pyrotinib) in stage II–III HER2-positive breast cancer reported a tpCR rate of 68.6% (95% CI: 60.7–75.8%) and an ORR of 89.1%, indicating strong antitumor activity with acceptable safety profiles [58]. Another prospective cohort study also found that the P + EC-TH regimen resulted in a tpCR rate of 63.6%, which was significantly higher than the 39.8% seen in the control group, although it was associated with slightly increased gastrointestinal toxicity, particularly diarrhea [59]. Additionally, the randomized clinical trial NCT04290793 corroborated that pyrotinib in combination with EC-TH significantly improved pCR rates, reinforcing its potential role in the neoadjuvant management of HER2-positive breast cancer [60].

Currently, the standard neoadjuvant regimen for HER2-positive breast cancer consists of trastuzumab plus pertuzumab (TP) combined with chemotherapy [61]. Nevertheless, a multicenter, randomized, open-label phase II study indicated that the addition of pyrotinib to TP plus nab-paclitaxel did not significantly improve the tpCR rates [61]. Moreover, another prospective analysis suggested that patients harboring PIK3CA-activating mutations exhibited a limited response to pyrotinib-based chemotherapy regimens [62]. These observations imply that the effectiveness of pyrotinib in the neoadjuvant context may be modulated by underlying molecular features, highlighting the necessity of biomarker-driven prospective studies to identify patient subsets most likely to benefit from pyrotinib-containing neoadjuvant strategies.

Adjuvant therapy

In the adjuvant setting for HER2-positive breast cancer, trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1) has emerged as the standard of care for patients who do not achieve a pathological complete response (non-pCR) following neoadjuvant therapy [63]. The phase III KATHERINE trial demonstrated that T-DM1 significantly reduced the risk of disease recurrence; however, a subset of patients still experienced relapse or death [64]. To further reduce postoperative recurrence, the ExteNET study evaluated 2,840 patients who had completed trastuzumab-based adjuvant therapy. The results indicated that extended adjuvant treatment with neratinib improved 5-year disease-free survival (DFS; 90.2% vs. 87.7%), suggesting that a sequential strategy involving monoclonal antibody therapy followed by a small-molecule TKI may offer additional clinical benefit [65]. Based on these findings, pyrotinib, an oral irreversible pan-HER inhibitor, has garnered increasing attention for its potential role in adjuvant intensification. The multicenter phase II PERSIST trial reported a 2-year invasive disease-free survival (iDFS) rate of 94.6% among patients receiving sequential pyrotinib after trastuzumab-based adjuvant therapy, providing preliminary evidence supporting its efficacy in extended adjuvant settings [66]. Nevertheless, given the single-arm design, exclusion of T-DM1-treated patients, and relatively small sample size, these findings warrant confirmation in randomized controlled trials. Furthermore, as the first trial to evaluate the application of pyrotinib combined with nab-paclitaxel in adjuvant therapy, the PHAEDRA study initially demonstrated good tolerability [67].

Special populations and combined treatment strategies

Beyond conventional stage-specific treatment strategies, unmet clinical needs have prompted increasing attention to special populations, such as patients with brain metastases, as well as to the optimization of combination regimens aimed at overcoming therapeutic resistance and improving outcomes.

Brain metastasis

Brain metastases are a major determinant of poor prognosis in patients with HER2-positive breast cancer. Due to the restrictive nature of the BBB, conventional macromolecular HER2-targeted agents, such as trastuzumab, exert limited therapeutic efficacy against CNS lesions [68]. Pyrotinib, an oral irreversible pan-HER TKI, demonstrates partial BBB permeability and therefore shows potential for intracranial antitumor activity in HER2-positive breast cancer patients with brain metastases [69]. Accumulating evidence has supported the clinical efficacy of pyrotinib in this context. A multicenter retrospective study reported the favorable antitumor activity of pyrotinib in combination with vinorelbine in patients with brain metastases [46]. Real-world studies have further confirmed these findings, demonstrating that among 239 patients with HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer, the median PFS was 7.5 months, while the central nervous system progression-free survival (CNS-PFS) reached 11.2 months. This superior CNS-PFS may be attributed to pyrotinib’s favorable blood-brain barrier penetration capability and adjunctive local treatment interventions, suggesting that pyrotinib maintains robust intracranial disease control in patients with brain metastases [39]. The combination of pyrotinib with local modalities such as radiotherapy may further improve survival outcomes. Consistent with these findings, the Phase III PERMEATE trial demonstrated that pyrotinib in combination with capecitabine significantly improved survival in patients with HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer, with durable efficacy observed in the brain metastasis subgroup [70]. Furthermore, a non-randomized Phase II study evaluating the combination of pyrotinib with capecitabine and concurrent brain radiotherapy revealed higher CNS control rates and prolonged survival without an increased risk of radiation-induced neurotoxicity [43]. Collectively, these findings underscore the potential clinical utility of pyrotinib in managing brain metastases in HER2-positive breast cancer. Nevertheless, most available evidence originates from retrospective or non-randomized studies with limited sample sizes and inherent selection bias, which preclude definitive conclusions regarding the independent efficacy of pyrotinib.

Optimization of combination treatment modalities

Pharmacological inhibition of cyclin-dependent kinases 4 and 6 (CDK4/6) has proven highly effective in suppressing breast cancer cell proliferation by inducing a senescence-like cellular state [71]. Dalpiciclib, the first CDK4/6 inhibitor independently developed in China, has demonstrated robust clinical efficacy in hormone receptor (HR)-positive breast cancer. Recent findings from randomized phase II trials have shown that the triplet combination of pyrotinib, dalpiciclib, and endocrine therapy yields significant antitumor activity in patients with HR-positive/HER2-positive breast cancer. In contrast, among HR-negative/ HER2-positive subtypes, the addition of chemotherapy has been shown to improve therapeutic outcomes at the expense of increased toxicities [72]. Moreover, results from a single-arm phase II study further supported that the dual combination of pyrotinib and dalpiciclib demonstrated promising frontline activity with an acceptable safety profile across both HR-positive and HR-negative HER2-positive patient populations [73]. Collectively, these observations suggest that integrating pyrotinib with CDK4/6 inhibitors may offer a chemotherapy-sparing strategy for patients with dual-positive breast cancer. However, current evidence remains largely exploratory, and larger-scale randomized trials are needed for validation.

In addition to conventional kinase-targeted strategies, emerging combination regimens involving antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) have shown considerable promise in the management of HER2-positive breast cancer. ADCs enable targeted intracellular delivery of potent cytotoxic agents directly to tumor cells, thereby enhancing therapeutic precision while minimizing off-target effects. Among novel ADC candidates, GQ1001, a next-generation site-specific ADC engineered using the iLDC continuous conjugation platform, has exhibited favorable tolerability and preliminary antitumor activity when combined with pyrotinib in heavily pretreated patients with HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer [74]. Nevertheless, interpretations of these findings are limited by the single-arm design and small sample size of the study, highlighting the need for extended follow-up and confirmation in randomized controlled trials. Encouragingly, several ongoing randomized studies, including evaluations of pyrotinib in combination with vedicitumab (NCT06278870) and SHR-A1811 plus capecitabine (NCT05424835), hold the potential to generate high-quality evidence supporting this evolving therapeutic approach.

Furthermore, the integration of pyrotinib with endocrine therapy offers a potentially less toxic treatment option for patients with HR-positive/HER2-positive breast cancer. As an irreversible pan-HER TKI, pyrotinib disrupts HER2-mediated signaling cascades, resulting in tumor growth suppression. The concomitant administration of endocrine agents such as letrozole or fulvestrant enhances antitumor efficacy by inhibiting ER-dependent pathways. Preclinical and clinical studies have consistently reported marked benefits associated with the combination of pyrotinib and letrozole [75, 76], and the dual regimen of pyrotinib plus fulvestrant represents a viable chemotherapy-free alternative for individuals exhibiting resistance to trastuzumab [77]. Additionally, a de-escalation regimen combining pyrotinib, trastuzumab, and an aromatase inhibitor (AI) has been shown to maintain a favorable risk–benefit profile in terms of both efficacy and safety [78]. Despite these encouraging findings, most available studies are early-phase or uncontrolled exploratory trials lacking definitive randomized comparisons and mature survival data, particularly regarding PFS and OS. Therefore, future research should prioritize identifying optimal candidate populations, determining ideal sequencing strategies, and defining appropriate durations for pyrotinib-based endocrine combination therapies.

Safety and adverse effects of pyrotinib

The overall safety profile of pyrotinib has been extensively characterized across multiple clinical studies, demonstrating a generally predictable and manageable spectrum of adverse events (AEs) when appropriate prophylactic and supportive measures are implemented [13, 40, 79, 80]. The most frequently reported AEs associated with pyrotinib treatment primarily result from HER family receptor inhibition and include diarrhea, hand-foot syndrome, rash, nausea, and vomiting (Table 3). Diarrhea represents the most common and clinically relevant toxicity associated with pyrotinib. In the PHENIX, PHOEBE, and PHEDRA trials, the incidence rates of grade ≥ 3 diarrhea were 30.8%, 31%, and 44.4%, respectively [41, 42, 54]. Importantly, pyrotinib-associated diarrhea is generally predictable and manageable through early intervention, dose modification, and supportive care. The timely use of antidiarrheal agents such as loperamide or montmorillonite powder may help reduce symptom severity [81]. However, caution should be exercised when using montmorillonite, as it may reduce the bioavailability of pyrotinib and should not be administered concurrently [29]. In addition to gastrointestinal toxicity, Hu et al. identified hand-foot syndrome and neutropenia as the most prevalent AEs in patients receiving pyrotinib [11]. Timely recognition and appropriate management of hand-foot syndrome are important to limit symptom severity and support treatment adherence, and supportive skin care with emollient hand creams may provide symptomatic relief [82, 83]. Moreover, prophylactic administration of granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF), such as fixed-dose mecapegfilgrastim, has been shown to effectively reduce the incidence of neutropenia in patients receiving pyrotinib-containing regimens [54]. Cardiac safety remains a critical concern for HER2-targeted therapies. Notably, findings from the NeoPaTHer trial demonstrated that the combination of pyrotinib with chemotherapy in the neoadjuvant setting did not result in clinically significant cardiotoxicity, providing reassuring evidence regarding its cardiac safety profile [56]. This observation is particularly relevant given the historical concerns surrounding cardiotoxicity associated with HER2 inhibition. Despite these favorable findings, pyrotinib exhibits substantial interindividual variability in pharmacokinetics, which may lead to heterogeneous patient tolerability. Therefore, close monitoring of AEs by clinical pharmacists is warranted, and individualized dose adjustments and toxicity management strategies should be implemented based on patient-specific factors.

Discussion and outlook

This review provides an overview of the recent clinical advances in the use of pyrotinib for HER2-positive breast cancer. Pyrotinib is a novel irreversible pan-HER TKI that targets HER1, HER2, and HER4 signaling pathways. Compared with lapatinib, which selectively inhibits HER1 and HER2, pyrotinib offers broader inhibition across the HER family and may mitigate resistance mechanisms linked to alternative pathway activation [16, 84]. In terms of safety, the most frequently reported adverse events associated with pyrotinib include diarrhea and neutropenia; however, prophylactic measures can significantly reduce the incidence of grade ≥ 3 diarrhea from 38.2 to 8.9% [35]. In terms of efficacy evaluation, multiple key studies have confirmed the advantages of pyrotinib in combination with capecitabine in HER2-positive advanced breast cancer (Table 4). In the phase III PHOEBE trial, the addition of pyrotinib to capecitabine significantly prolonged PFS (median: 12.5 vs. 6.8 months; HR = 0.39; P < 0.0001) and exhibited a favorable trend toward improved OS [41] . Likewise, the PHENIX trial confirmed substantial improvements in PFS (median: 11.1 vs. 4.1 months; HR = 0.18; P < 0.0001), accompanied by an ORR of 71.9% [23]. A phase II trial (NCT02422199) corroborated the pronounced PFS benefit conferred by pyrotinib (median: 18.1 vs. 7.0 months) [85]. In contrast, the phase III NALA trial evaluating neratinib combined with capecitabine reported only marginal improvements in PFS (median: 5.6 vs. 5.5 months; HR = 0.76; P = 0.0059), with no statistically significant difference in OS [86]. Collectively, these findings support the superiority of pyrotinib over comparator agents in terms of both PFS and ORR; however, longer follow-up is required to confirm any meaningful OS benefit. While direct comparative studies between pyrotinib and neratinib remain limited, indirect analyses have preliminarily supported the efficacy advantage of pyrotinib.

Pharmacoeconomic evaluations conducted in real-world settings in China indicated that the combination of pyrotinib and capecitabine was more cost-effective than lapatinib-based therapies [85]. An additional cost-effectiveness analysis revealed that, compared with trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1), the pyrotinib–capecitabine regimen yielded an incremental gain of 0.70 quality-adjusted life-years (QALYs) at an incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) of US$ 31,121.53 [87]. Following its inclusion in the National Reimbursement Drug List (NRDL), access to pyrotinib has been substantially enhanced, resulting in a marked reduction in patient out-of-pocket costs. Based on the favorable outcomes of the PHOEBE and PHENIX trials, the Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology (CSCO) has endorsed pyrotinib in combination with capecitabine as a Category I recommendation for second-line therapy in HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer [88]. These guidelines also underscore the potential clinical utility of pyrotinib in both first-line and second-line treatment contexts, based on strong efficacy data derived from the Chinese patient population [88]. Moreover, a large-scale, nationwide real-world study encompassing 1129 patients corroborated the consistent antitumor activity and manageable safety profile of pyrotinib in both first-line and subsequent-line therapeutic settings [89].

Although pyrotinib has not yet been approved for neoadjuvant use in HER2-positive breast cancer, several exploratory trials have demonstrated its promising antitumor activity. Initial investigations demonstrated that the incorporation of pyrotinib into neoadjuvant therapy protocols (Py + EC-TH ) significantly enhanced the tpCR rate to 75%, representing a substantial improvement over the 37.5% observed with conventional EC-TH regimens [57]. Subsequent validation in an expanded cohort of 175 HER2-positive breast cancer patients confirmed these findings, with the regimen achieving a tpCR rate of 68.6% and an ORR of 89.1% [58]. Furthermore, an alternative neoadjuvant protocol (Py+TCbH) comprising pyrotinib, paclitaxel, carboplatin, and trastuzumab demonstrated remarkable efficacy, yielding a tpCR rate of 73.3% [90], which exceeded the rates observed in the KRISTINE trial for docetaxel/carboplatin/ trastuzumab/pertuzumab (55.7%) [91] and the TRIO-US B07 study for docetaxel/carboplatin/ trastuzumab/ lapatinib (52%) [92]. Compared with the potential of pyrotinib demonstrated in neoadjuvant therapy, neratinib’s clinical positioning has primarily been established in the adjuvant therapy setting. The ExteNET trial established the role of neratinib in adjuvant therapy for HER2-positive early breast cancer; however its potential in neoadjuvant therapy has not yet been fully confirmed [93, 94]. Moreover, the ChiCTR2300078928 study is currently evaluating the combination of pyrotinib with the CDK4/6 inhibitor dalpiciclib in HR+/HER2-low patients, potentially filling the therapeutic gap left by traditional TKIs therapy.

Although pyrotinib has not yet been approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), early phase I data have shown encouraging antitumor activity in patients with breast cancer, warranting further clinical investigation [95]. Several phase IV studies are currently underway in China (e.g., NCT06217185, NCT06245824, ChiCTR2000040866, and ChiCTR2200062936), underscoring the expanding role of pyrotinib in precision oncology. Despite its promising efficacy in early stage, advanced, and brain-metastatic HER2-positive breast cancers, the clinical development and application of pyrotinib continue to face challenges and opportunities: (1) Optimization of combination strategies: Rational combinations with other targeted agents, chemotherapeutics, or immunotherapies are being explored to enhance therapeutic efficacy and overcome resistance. For instance, pyrotinib combined with T-DM1, ADCs, or immune checkpoint inhibitors may provide novel treatment options. Jian et al. proposed a triplet regimen combining pyrotinib with CDK4/6 inhibitors and endocrine therapy, offering a new strategy for HR+/HER2 + breast cancer [96]. (2) Expansion of therapeutic indications: Investigations into the role of pyrotinib in neoadjuvant, adjuvant, and HER2-low breast cancer settings are still ongoing. The NeoATP study conducted by Wenjin et al. preliminarily demonstrated the efficacy and safety of pyrotinib in combination with trastuzumab and chemotherapy in the neoadjuvant setting; however, confirmation through randomized controlled trials is warranted [97]. Emerging evidence indicates that incorporating pyrotinib into trastuzumab-based neoadjuvant regimens may improve tpCR rates among patients who previously showed poor responses to trastuzumab without compromising tolerability [56]. (3) Identification of predictive biomarkers: A deeper understanding of biomarkers predictive of response and resistance to pyrotinib is critical for advancing personalized medicine approaches. Current research focuses on HER2 mutation subtypes, PIK3CA/TP53 mutation status, tumor mutational burden (TMB), and circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) profiles [13, 56, 69, 80]. The PANDORA study revealed that patients harboring PIK3CA mutations exhibited shorter PFS than those with wild-type tumors, whereas TP53 mutations were not significantly associated with treatment outcomes, suggesting that PIK3CA mutations may impair sensitivity to pyrotinib [35] . Furthermore, real-world data suggest that elevated TMB correlates with worse PFS and OS, identifying TMB as a potential negative prognostic biomarker in patients treated with pyrotinib [69].

Although this comprehensive review provides a thorough overview of the current state of pyrotinib research and its clinical applications, there are several limitations to this study that need to be acknowledged. First, existing research primarily focuses on the Chinese population and lacks data from global multicenter studies. Second, there is a lack of head-to-head comparative studies between pyrotinib and current standard ADC therapies. Third, the mechanisms underlying acquired drug resistance have not been thoroughly investigated, limiting the optimization of combination strategies. In summary, pyrotinib represents a potent, irreversible pan-HER TKI with robust preclinical and clinical activity in HER2-positive breast cancer. Its unique ability to penetrate the BBB, coupled with synergistic effects when combined with trastuzumab and chemotherapy, supports its integration into both early and advanced treatment settings. Trials such as PHILA, PHEDRA, and PHOEBE have established pyrotinib-based regimens as effective options across multiple lines of therapy, including first-line advanced disease and neoadjuvant treatment [34, 41, 54]. Despite its favorable efficacy, challenges remain, particularly regarding resistance mediated by gatekeeper mutations (e.g., T798I) and optimal patient selection. Future research should focus on biomarker-driven strategies, rational combination therapies, and real-world validation in diverse populations to maximize the therapeutic potential of pyrotinib in precision oncology.

Predicting symptomatic carotid artery plaques with radiomics-based carotid perivascular adipose tissue characteristics: a multicenter, multiclassifier study
Authors: Ting Zhao, Guihan Lin, Weiyue Chen, Jianhua Wu, Weiming Hu, Lei Xu, Yongjun Chen, Yang Jing, Lin Shen, Shuiwei Xia, Chenying Lu, Minjiang Chen, Jiansong Ji, Weiqian Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aims to differentiate between symptomatic and asymptomatic plaques using a computed tomography angiography (CTA)-based radiomics model of perivascular adipose tissue (PVAT).MethodsPatients were categorized into symptomatic and asymptomatic groups based on the presence or absence of acute ischemic stroke or transient ischemic attack in the anterior cerebral circulation within two weeks prior to the CTA examination. The clinical information of all patients was collected and analyzed, and the PVAT features of CTA images were further analyzed to clarify their correlation with plaque classification. K-nearest neighbors (KNN), support vector machine (SVM), logistic regression (LR), random forest (RF), linear discriminant analysis (LDA), multinomial naive Bayes (MultinomialNB), and extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost) were trained and radiomics (Rad) score was calculated using the best classifier. A combined model was further developed based on the Rad-score and independent predictors, and the calibration, receiver operating characteristic curve, decision curve analysis, and clinical applicability were evaluated.ResultsThe white blood cell count and hyperlipidemia were clinically independent predictors, and ten PVAT radiomics features showed significant correlation. The XGBoost classifier showed the best performance among different classifiers, with an average AUC of 0.797 in the validation set. The combined model integrating Rad-score and clinically independent predictors was further obtained, with AUCs of 0.942, 0.797, and 0.836 in the training, external validation sets, respectively.ConclusionThe combined model performed excellently in predicting symptomatic carotid plaques. By early identification of high-risk patients and selecting appropriate clinical decisions, it holds significant clinical potential for improving stroke prevention.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12880-025-01876-x.Keywords: Computed tomography angiography, Stroke, Carotid plaques, Perivascular adipose tissue, Radiomics

Introduction

Stroke is the second foremost cause of disability and mortality globally, causing millions of deaths annually [1, 2]. The Global Burden of Disease Study (GBD) predicts that by 2050, there will be more than 200 million stroke patients each year, including 25 million new stroke cases and 13 million stroke-related deaths [3]. Carotid atherosclerosis is an important cause of stroke, accounting for 15–20% of ischemic stroke events [4, 5]. Compared to low-risk carotid plaques, high-risk carotid plaques are more prone to rupture, leading to the occurrence of ischemic stroke and often result in residual impairments in motor, sensory, speech, or cognitive functions [6], highlighting the importance of early prediction of symptomatic plaques. Therefore, accurate carotid plaque risk assessment is crucial for personalized treatment plans to prevent stroke.

The underlying mechanisms of stroke caused by carotid atherosclerosis are complex and can be inferred from the characteristics of the plaques [7–9]. Studies have indicated that the clinical outcome of atherosclerotic plaques, and whether they lead to ischemic stroke depends not only on the degree of luminal stenosis, but also on the specific composition characteristics of the plaques [10–12]. In addition, the evaluation of various plaque characteristics has received increasing attention, such as inflammation, thin fibrous caps, intraplaque hemorrhage, and lipid-rich cores [13, 14]. Among them, inflammation is the main factor in the formation and rupture of atherosclerotic plaques [15–17]. Previous studies have found that inflammation in atherosclerotic plaques has a stable and long-term effect on the surrounding perivascular adipose tissue (PVAT), and inflammatory cytokines released from a vessel wall undergoing atherosclerosis diffuse to PVAT in a paracrine fashion, inhibiting the differentiation of preadipocytes and promoting lipid buildup [18, 19]. A previous study has reported that inflammation linked to high-risk carotid artery plaques could be evaluated through variations in PVAT density on CT images [20]. However, these changes, including fibrosis and changes in vascular distribution, cannot be accurately reflected by simply measuring the CT values [21]. Instead, it is necessary to perform comprehensive feature extractions of the PVAT surrounding carotid plaques at a higher dimension to fully capture these changes, thereby enhancing the prediction of symptomatic plaques.

Radiomics, an emerging image analysis technique, allows for the high-throughput extraction of quantitative features from medical images and ascertaining their patterns to provide clinical decision support [22, 23]. Initially applied in oncology, radiomics has gradually been used in predicting diseases associated with the coronary arteries [24–26]. Machine learning (ML) as an emerging tool is also widely used in research in the field of vascular medicine [27, 28]. Recent research has confirmed the association between inflammation-induced attenuation changes in coronary artery PVAT and the development of coronary artery disease and vulnerable plaques [29]. However, to our knowledge, there appears to be a scarcity of research exploring the connection between inflammatory changes in the PVAT of carotid arteries and cerebrovascular events [30, 31]. Moreover, these studies have only assessed the inflammation of carotid plaques based on the density of PVAT or were unable to investigate the entirety of PVAT due to technical limitations. Hence, predicting symptomatic carotid plaques using radiomics combined with various ML algorithms remains uncharted territory.

This study aims to establish and test a radiomics model based on carotid perivascular PVAT to improve the accuracy of identifying high-risk carotid plaques, and to compare the diagnostic value of various models, to better assist in the selection of clinical treatment plans.

Methods

Patient selection and classification

This study was approved by the institutional review board, and individual consent for this retrospective analysis was waived (No. 2024 − 508, the Fifth Affiliated Hospital of Wenzhou Medical University).Clinical trial number: not applicable.Retrospective collection of imaging data from patients confirmed with carotid atherosclerosis occurred at the Fifth Affiliated Hospital of Wenzhou Medical University (center 1) from January 2018 to December 2023, the Second Affiliated Hospital of Wenzhou Medical University (center 2) and the Lishui People’s Hospital (center 3) from January 2022 to December 2023.Center 1 is used as the training set, and centers 2 and 3 are used as external validation sets 1 and 2, respectively. The inclusion criteria, exclusion criteria and collection of clinical data are detailed in Appendix E1 and Appendix E2. Subsequently, we categorized the selected patients.

Patients were classified as symptomatic or asymptomatic according to the results of a neurological assessment following the TOAST criteria [32] and the results of a brain MRI plain scan. Patients with a transient ischemic attack (TIA) or acute ischemic stroke occurring in the area supplied by the carotid artery were considered symptomatic. Acute ischemic stroke was defined as neurological deficits persisting for more than 24 h or neurological deficits accompanied by cranial MRI findings [33]. The MRI findings of acute ischemic stroke included high signal intensity lesions on diffusion-weighted imaging sequences and low signal intensity on apparent diffusion coefficient sequences. TIA was defined as a transient episode of neurological dysfunction caused by focal brain or retinal ischemia [33]. Patients without any TIA or stroke in the previous six months were classified as asymptomatic. A flowchart depicting the patient selection process is presented in Fig. 1.

Examination methods

All the CTA scans were performed on a 256-slice CT scanner. Additional information about the CTA parameters from three distinct centers can be found in Appendix E3.

Image segmentation

All CTA images were uploaded to Radcloud platform (version 7.1, http://radcloud.cn/) for analysis. First, two radiologists (both with > 8 years of imaging experience) delineated the target vessels and the surrounding plaques on the CTA images. Then PVAT was defined as the adipose tissue located at a radial distance from the outer vessel wall equivalent to the diameter of the vessel; its Hounsfield Unit (HU) values ranged from − 190 to − 30. The delineation of PVAT was performed automatically by establishing a specific attenuation threshold. The length and coverage range of target vessels were the areas with plaques at the selected bifurcation of the carotid artery and the origin of the internal carotid artery. For the symptomatic group, we identified and selected the plaques exhibiting the most severe stenosis in the symptomatic carotid artery. For the asymptomatic group, we selected the plaques with the most severe stenosis. Additionally, two radiologists measured carotid lumen stenosis according to NASCET criteria [34]. To reduce image heterogeneity, we preprocessed all the images (Appendix E4) before extracting radiomics features.

Radiomics features extraction and selection

A total of 1688 radiomics features (see in Appendix E5) were extracted from the PCAT surrounding plaques utilized by Radcloud.

To evaluate the inter-reader reproducibility of radiomics features and ensure the robustness and stability of our constructed models, we assessed the inter- and intra-class correlation coefficients (ICCs). (see in Appendix E6). Features with ICC > 0.75 were selected for further analysis.

All radiomics features were standardized using the z-score algorithm to ensure that the scale of feature value was uniform and improved comparability between features. We employed a three-stage sequential feature selection process: First, we applied sequential analysis of variance (ANOVA) to eliminate features that did not show statistically significant group differences (p < 0.05 after Holm-Bonferroni correction). Second, we used the SelectKBest univariate filter to retain the top 15% of features. Finally, we applied the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression to retain the most predictive features. An optimal lambda was selected through 10-fold stratified cross-validation. Features with non-zero coefficients in the model were considered the most representative.

Machine learning classifiers construction and evaluation

Then, we calculated the radiomics score for each patient by linearly combining the features with non-zero coefficients, weighted by their respective coefficients, and constructed seven machine learning classifiers. The selection of machine learning is detailed in Appendix E7. We employed the most common cross-validation method to check for overfitting or underfitting of the model. The evaluation of the prediction performance of the radiomics signatures included metrics such as the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC), specificity, sensitivity and accuracy (ACC). The radiomics model that exhibited the highest average AUC in the training set was chosen as the optimal radiomics model [35–37]. Then the results were transformed into a radiomics score (Rad-score).

Model and nomogram development and validation

We developed a total of four predictive models: the Stenosis model, the Clinical model, the Radiomics model, and the Combined model. For the Stenosis model, we employed a logistic regression approach due to the limited number of input features. We conducted both univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses on the clinical characteristics of the symptomatic and asymptomatic groups in the training set to identify significant clinical predictive factors. Based on these findings, we formulated the Clinical model. Additionally, by integrating Rad-scores with clinical features, we developed the Combined model and its corresponding nomogram through multivariable logistic regression analysis. We assessed the performance of all models using AUC, sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy. Calibration curves were used to evaluate the agreement between observed outcomes and predicted risks, and decision curve analysis (DCA) was used to determine the most beneficial model. Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted by R software (version 4.1.2, https://www.r-project.org/). Count data are presented as frequencies, and analyses comparing two groups were carried out utilizing either the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Quantitative data that follow a normal distribution are expressed as mean ± standard deviation, with comparisons between groups executed through independent samples t-tests. For data that do not follow a normal distribution, medians (interquartile ranges) are provided, and the Wilcoxon test was employed for comparisons. The Pearson correlation coefficient was used to calculate the degree of linear correlation between features. The DeLong test was used to assess the AUC between different models, and the associated P-value was calculated. A significance level of P < 0.05, two-tailed, was considered indicative of a meaningful difference.

Radiomics feature extraction and selection

For the PVAT radiomic features, 192 features exhibiting an ICC < 0.75 were excluded. Among these excluded features, 126 demonstrated poor intra-observer agreement, and 178 demonstrated poor inter-observer agreement.

After excluding both intra-observer and inter-observer variability, we sequentially applied ANOVA, sequential analysis of variance, and LASSO regression to a total of 1,496 features. This process resulted in the selection of 495 features from ANOVA, 285 features from sequential analysis of variance, and ultimately 10 non-zero coefficient features through LASSO regression. These 10 selected features include 2 first-order statistical features, 3 shape features, and 5 texture features, which were used for subsequent model development (Figure S1). Details regarding PVAT-related features within the symptomatic group can be found in Table S2 and the non-zero coefficients of the features selected via LASSO regression are depicted in Fig. 3A. Additionally, the Pearson correlation coefficients for every pair of selected features were calculated, resulting in a correlation coefficient matrix heatmap represented in Fig. 3B.

Construction and evaluation of the ML classifiers

The specificity, sensitivity, accuracy, and AUC values of the ML classifiers as showed in Table 3 and Figure S2. The hyperparameter optimization of the seven machine learning classifiers is presented in Appendix E7. Within the training set, the AUC values for the classifiers varied from 0.692 to 0.920, while the ACC ranged between 57.99% and 88.42%. For the external validation sets, the average AUC values of the seven classifiers spanned from 0.685 to 0.797, with the average ACC falling between 65.37% and 76.53%. Among these classifiers, XGBoost performed the best, with average AUC values of 0.797, while the MultinomialNB classifier performed the worst with an AUC value of 0.685. Consequently, the XGBoost classifier was judged to be the most suitable machine learning algorithm for creating the radiomics model and used to calculate Rad-score. Development and validation of model

We further integrated the XGBoost-based Rad-scores with independent predictors (hyperlipidemia and white blood cell count) to build a combined model, and compared the predictive abilities of the stenosis model, clinical model, radiomics model and combined model. Table S3 reflects the multivariable logistic regression results for each variable in the combined model. Table 6 summarizes the results of each model. The ROC curves depicting the four models for predicting symptomatic carotid artery plaque are illustrated in Fig. 4A, Band C. The findings indicated that within the training set, the AUC values for the stenosis, clinical, radiomics, and combined model were 0.583, 0.684, 0.920, and 0.942, respectively. In external validation set 1, the corresponding AUC values for the four models were 0.543, 0.647, 0.787 and 0.797, respectively. For external validation set 2, the AUC values presented were 0.563, 0.690, 0.807 and 0.836. In both validation sets, the DeLong test confirmed significant differences between the combined model and the clinical model as well as the stenosis model (all p < 0.05), with the combined model consistently outperforming both the clinical model and the stenosis model. Meanwhile, although the combined model performed slightly better than the radiomics model, the DeLong test confirmed no statistically significant differences in the two external validation sets, with p-values of 0.345 and 0.099.

Discussion

Carotid atherosclerosis poses a risk for ischemic cerebrovascular events, making the early identification of symptomatic carotid plaques and the selection of appropriate interventions or the development of surgical removal techniques crucial for stroke prevention. With deeper research into symptomatic plaques, their assessment through PVAT has become a focal point of study [38] and provides new insights into the formation of atherosclerotic plaques and the prediction of individual risks for ischemic stroke [20, 39]. However, traditional PVAT assessment methods have significant limitations in accurately evaluating symptomatic plaques, primarily because they tend to focus only on changes in the CT values of PVAT and fail to fully capture the complex three-dimensional structure of the adipose tissue [31, 40]. In this retrospective study, we divided the patients into two groups based on whether they had experienced an acute ischemic stroke or a TIA in the anterior circulation: a symptomatic group and an asymptomatic group. We then extracted the radiomic features of PVAT from multiple dimensions and established a predictive model based on these radiomic features to assess the risk of ischemic stroke in patients with carotid atherosclerosis. The model can classify patients into symptomatic and asymptomatic groups based on the Rad score and independent predictive factors, thereby guiding clinical decision-making.

Vascular inflammation plays a crucial role not only in the development of atherosclerosis but also in influencing the transcriptional and structural changes of PVAT [18, 19]. These alterations manifest in CT images as an increase in the HU of PVAT, which correlates closely with plaque hemorrhage and ischemic symptoms [20, 41]. Si et al. [15] reported that plaque radiomics, combined with PVAT radiomics, can better differentiate patients with acute myocardial infarction from those with unstable angina. Oikonomou et al. [42] introduced a novel fat radiomics feature derived from PVAT radiomics obtained via coronary artery CTA and assessed its performance across three distinct patient cohorts, suggesting that features based on PVAT radiomics are valuable for advancing risk assessments of adverse cardiac events. Zhang et al. [40] studied the average voxel intensity of the PVAT surrounding carotid artery plaques, which relied solely on CT attenuation to reflect changes in PVAT composition, without considering the complex spatial relationship between voxels. Hence, this approach may lead to some pathological overlap.

Additionally, although Chen et al. [31] investigated the radiomic features of PVAT, the AUC of their PVAT model was 0.740 and 0.618 on the training and validation sets, respectively. In our study, the PVAT model achieved an AUC of 0.920 in the training set and AUCs of 0.787 and 0.807 in the validation sets, respectively. We believe the reasons for this are the following: (1) their research sample size was limited; (2) PVAT characterization involved only a single image slice, resulting in the loss of numerous meaningful features; (3) the classifier they used was limited to SVM, which may not necessarily be the best classifier. Compared to their study [31], our research employs a combined approach of radiomics and machine learning algorithms, utilizing a larger sample size (144 vs. 645 patients) and establishing two external validation sets. This improvement may result in our model’s predictive performance exceeding theirs.

We constructed seven different ML classifier based on CTA radiomics features. Among these, the XGBoost classifier exhibited reliable predictive capabilities in the external validation sets, with AUC scores of 0.787 and 0.807, respectively, leading to its designation as the top-performing classifier. The XGBoost algorithm, a tree-based approach, is commonly employed for both classification and regression tasks. It aggregates predictions from numerous decision trees to produce a final result and provides insights into variable importance [43]. The relatively good performance of XGBoost may be attributed to several reasons: (1) it has a built-in cross-validation functionality, which helps in obtaining the optimal number of boosting rounds during each iteration, aiding in model selection and parameter tuning; and (2) it includes a regularization term in the objective function, which helps control model complexity and reduce the risk of overfitting.

In this study, we expanded our focus beyond the distribution of pixel intensity, integrated and extracted various types of features. These features were ultimately selected using LASSO regression, resulting in 10 significantly correlated features, including 5 texture features, 3 shape features, and 2 first-order features. First, texture features capture the integrity of PVAT microstructure. Previous studies [44] have indicated that inflammation induces microstructural remodeling in PVAT, such as microvascular remodeling. The Gray-Level Distance Zone Matrix —which constitutes a significant proportion of textural features—may reflect these microstructural alterations in PVAT. The morphomic features of PVAT may reflect its adaptive changes to the local microenvironment, suggesting adipose morphology as a potential diagnostic marker for cardiovascular diseases. Finally, first-order features quantify heterogeneity in PVAT composition, revealing clues about disease progression. Enhanced inflammatory metabolic activity and suppressed pre-adipocyte differentiation likely alter PVAT’s compositional profile. This phenomenon may explain the increased attenuation shift from lipid phase to water phase observed in PVAT on CTA imaging [42].

In our study, through univariate analysis and multivariate regression, ultimately found that dyslipidemia and white blood cell counts were independent predictors of symptomatic carotid artery plaques. A previous study [45] showed a potential association between dyslipidemia and the occurrence of stroke, as it leads to thickening of the intima-media layer in the carotid artery, reducing blood supply to the brain and causing ischemic stroke. Swystun et al. [46] demonstrated that white blood cells can damage vascular endothelial cells through local inflammatory responses, triggering a cascade of coagulation reactions that promote platelet activation and aggregation, resulting in rapid thrombus formation. In light of these independent predictors, this study established a clinical model. The clinical model showed moderate performance, with AUC values of 0.684, 0.647, and 0.690 in the training and external validation sets, respectively. In order to improve the accuracy of predictions, we developed a combined model that integrates the Rad-score obtained from XGBoost along with the independent predictive variables discussed earlier. The findings revealed that the predictive efficacy of the combined model significantly surpassed that of the clinical model, while showing no significant difference in performance compared to the radiomics model. This limitation may stem from the study’s exclusive focus on the radiomic characteristics of PVAT without incorporating conventional imaging features of the plaques. Future investigations that integrate plaque characteristics could potentially yield improved outcomes.

This research illustrates that PVAT may act as an imaging biomarker for symptomatic plaques in the carotid artery and is closely associated with ischemic stroke. This finding provides a theoretical basis for the early prevention of ischemic stroke. However, there were some limitations in this study: (1) due to the retrospective nature of our study and the relatively small sample size, there may be selection bias in the patient selection process. The existing inclusion and exclusion criteria may not accurately represent the entire population. Therefore, future research should conduct rigorous large-scale prospective studies to validate our findings and enhance the applicability of the model; (2) lack of histopathological confirmation for PVAT inflammatory changes limits direct pathological evidence; (3) we used traditional methods from coronary artery studies to measure PVAT. This difference may affect the accuracy of our assessment of PVAT in the context of carotid artery disease; (4) focus on maximally stenotic plaques could overlook high-risk non-stenotic lesions, warranting broader plaque assessment in subsequent research; (5) the imbalance between the symptomatic and asymptomatic cohorts in our research may introduce bias into the model’s results. This discrepancy could raise questions about the robustness of the model and the presence of selection bias. To address this issue, our future cohort studies will implement strict balancing strategies to ensure that both symptomatic and asymptomatic patients are adequately represented.

Conclusions

In summary, this study established a predictive model for symptomatic carotid artery plaques by combining PVAT radiomics features with clinical characteristics and achieved satisfactory performance. Our model can provide important information for the development of clinical treatment strategies and serve as a reference for the selection of treatment strategies in high-risk carotid plaques.

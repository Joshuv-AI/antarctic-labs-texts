Faculty application and perceived effectiveness of cognitive psychology principles in medical education. A mixed method study
Authors: Shaur Sarfaraz, Ambreen Surti, Rabiya Ali, Rehana Rehman, Artak Heboyan, Naseer Ahmed
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThe objective of the study was to explore the extent to which faculty members in undergraduate medical education apply cognitive psychology principles in their teaching practices, and to evaluate the perceived effectiveness of these principles in enhancing student learning.MethodsThis mixed method study was conducted on faculty members of public and private sector universities. Focused group discussions were conducted, and the responses of the participants were measured on a 5-point- Likert Scale. The instrument was tested for validity and reliability by measuring Cronbach’s alpha and performing exploratory factor analysis. Mann–Whitney U test was applied on the attributes scores to compare 13 attributes by institutions, designations and domains.ResultsOne theme, 7 sub-themes and 19 categories were identified from the focused group discussion. as Kaiser- Meyer-Olkin index was 0.88 and Bartlett’s test of sphericity was significant. Reliability was established at 0.95. The faculty self-perceived practicing psychology principles mostly scored in the middle level, where they think they apply less strategies. While highly significant results were noted for using Meyer’s multimedia principles.ConclusionFaculty members followed the principles of cognitive psychology to some extent. However, they all felt that using these principles can transform the teaching and learning of medical education.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12909-024-05892-3.

Introduction

Effective teaching involves translating less knowledgeable, less motivated students into well-informed, keen learners. A skilled teacher creates an environment in which learning becomes achievable to every student [1]. Principles of cognitive psychology are implemented in order to achieve learning outcomes and to promote teaching practices, which can enhance student learning [2]. These basic principles by making use of activities which involve skills and hands on experience not only act to uplift the learning but also enable the teachers and students to identify challenges experienced by the learners belonging to diverse educational background and competency levels [2, 3].

The vital cognitive challenges that teachers face in class comprise; learners’ mental viewpoint, metacognition and self-directed learning, student mistrust and anxiety, past knowledge, disinformation, unproductive study techniques, learning transfer, attention deficits, constraints with intellectual effort and short-term memory [1]. Designing strategies which can impact and enhance students’ learning is a challenging task. A proficient teacher grasps the complexity of student learning to create teaching methods that are not just effective but also caters to the diversity of learners [1, 4, 5].

Aligning the teaching strategies to Cognitive psychology principles can maximize cognitive reasoning skills and critical thinking. These methodologies include explanatory questioning, the use of worked examples, metacognitive training, exemplification, refutational texts, and the incorporation of multiple external representations. These practices facilitate the acquisition of complex concepts across diverse educational contexts [6]. To augment the memory retention and enhance learning outcomes, strategies like, spaced practice, retrieval practice and asking and responding to clarifying questions are known in literature to be effective methods [7, 8]. Strategies like making use of pre-tests, revision of basic and key concepts and question driven explanatory reasoning not only enhance students interest and motivation but also ensures active participation during session [9, 10].

The application of cognitive psychological principles in teaching biological and biomedical sciences however has shown inconsistency. Some practices are not sustained due to a lack of systematic research demonstrating their effectiveness in translating biological knowledge into medical practices [11].

The study focuses on Self-Determination Theory (SDT), developed by Edward Deci and Richard Ryan. This theory provides a broad framework for understanding the factors that facilitate or destabilize intrinsic motivation, autonomous extrinsic motivation, and psychological wellness, particularly in educational settings. SDT explains that individuals are motivated by three innate psychological needs: autonomy, competence, and relatedness [12]. In the context of teaching practices, SDT can be applied to understand faculty motivation to integrate psychological principles into their teaching; if it is influenced by their sense of autonomy in the classroom, their perceived competence in applying these principles effectively, and the quality of their relationships with students and colleagues [13].

Since teaching at any level of education requires content as well as pedagogical knowledge; latter comprise of socio-cultural awareness and an understanding of cognitive science on human learning. Understanding of cognitive science is focused on individual cognition, such as how memory processes disclose and how cognitive load can influence the ability to solve problem learning sciences. Teacher education programs at colleges and universities should ensure that their faculty is well-versed in the learning sciences to make use of the best practices within their classrooms and improve learning outcomes. The educators must support them to do the best they can in their endeavors by more adequately preparing them to meet the learning needs of their students. A greater emphasis on cognitive science on learning is one clear step in that direction [14].

To the best of our knowledge, there is a vacuum literature, which examines how psychological concepts might be applied to the teaching setting of undergraduate medical education [15]. Hence, the current study aims to address the principles of psychology employed by medical educators in their instructional practices.

Methods

Study design and setting

It was a mixed method study in which the quantitative data by convenience sampling and qualitative data by purposive sample were collected simultaneously from faculty teaching undergraduate students in private and public medical and dental institutions in Karachi.

An empirical phenomenological study design was approached for the qualitative component of the study. Where the meaning was extracted freshly from the transcripts to describe the experience of the faculty regarding the implementation of psychological principles in their teaching practice. The cross-sectional study design for the quantitative component was used to assess faculty implementation of psychological principles in the teaching practice. Furthermore, triangulation was done to validate the results. All teaching faculty, from HEC and/or PM&DC recognized institution, currently employed for 3 years and took lectures of undergraduate students were included. Any applicant who did not consent to be part of the study or withdrew was excluded.

Data was collected within six months of IRB Approval, i.e., from August 2023 – January 2024. Pilot testing of the survey was completed in August 2023.

Another aim of the study is to validate the questionnaire assessing application of Psychology principles by faculty in their teaching practices for both undergraduate and post graduate programs. Based on the best practice of sample size calculation for a validation study, 5 samples per item were considered adequate to obtain a significant result [9]. The required sample size for this study was 291. The COREQ [16] guidelines were followed for the reporting of this study. (Annexure 1).

Data collection procedure

The 59-item questionnaire through a 5-point Likert scale (Strongly agree- Strongly disagree) was developed with content analysis where the 4 medical educationists got familiarized with the items listed by their content knowledge to get a sense of the content. And then defined the Unit of Analysis where they determined what items constitutes a single attribute. The questionnaire was then piloted on 20 faculty members to obtain the perception of faculty regarding practicing Psychology principles in their practices. The responses of these faculty members were not included in the final results, where it found to be reliable to use in this study and for further validation (Supplementary data).

The initial questionnaire consists of items on perception of course developed (5 items), integrating knowledge (4 items), gauging attention (11 items), maintenance of interactions with the students (3 items), motivation ( 2 items), strategies used to meet cognitive challenges divided into 2 sub categories one is metacognition (3 items) and other is misconceptions (4 items), strategies to overcome ineffective learning which is again divided into practice testing (3 items), transfer of learning and memory retention ( 9 items) and strategies used to meet emotional challenges (8 items), and beneficence (7 items).

It was piloted among 20 faculty members. Sampling was done by convenience sampling; all teaching faculty filled the questionnaire after consenting to participate in the study. Teaching faculty having less than 3 years’ experience were not approached.

Qualitative data

The semi-structured interview questions were developed by the principal researcher and 3 medical educationists. The focus group sessions were conducted to collect qualitative data; Group of faculties having more than 3 years’ teaching experience and consented to participate were almost equally invited from three institutes to participate in the interview session sessions of 20–25 min maximum which were audio recorded with the due faculty permission.

Two audio recorded focused group sessions (one basic and one clinical) from each institute, comprising of 6 members in each (n = 36) were conducted.

Ethical approval

This study was approved by the research and ethics committee of the Bahria University (Ethical Review #: FRC/BUHS 37/2022). Informed verbal consent was obtained from all the faculty.

Statistical analysis

The validity was ensured by Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) which was performed on a sample of 291 responses gathered. The suitability was confirmed by Kaiser–Meyer–Olkin (KMO) test of sampling adequacy and Bartlett’s Chi‑squared test of sphericity. The items were intercorrelated using Pearson Product‑moment correlations, which was then decomposed into principal components and rotated to the normalized varimax criterion. Factors were extracted based on an eigenvalue greater than 1. Each item was assigned to the factor on which it loaded with a loading factor of at least 0.40. Item loading in more than one factor(cross‑loading) was assigned to the highest‑loaded factor.

The statistical analysis was done to find out the adequacy of the internal structure of the 58-item questionnaire after which cronbach alpha was calculated α = 0.95 (confidence interval 95%). Quantitative data was analyzed by Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) version 24. Descriptive analyses were performed on all variables and attributes. For the validity of the results. Normality was checked with Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. The data was not normality distributed as the p-value was less than 0.05, because of which, Mann–Whitney U test was applied on the attributes scores to compare 13 attributes by Institutions, designations and domains. To reduce bias in the quantitative method the missing data was treated, by box plots we detect and remove outliers and matched participants on key variables to ensure comparability. Qualitative data gathered from faculty were coded by thematic analysis where themes, categories and sub categories were formed.

Results

A total of 350 questionnaires were distributed. We calculated the frequency and percentage of missing values for each variable. This helped us identify variables with a significant amount of missing data, which could potentially bias our results. After removing 51 incomplete responses Two ninety-one (83%) participants completed questionnaires were collected. The reliability of the questionnaire as calculated by Cronbach’s alpha was α = 0.95.

Exploratory factor analysis

The minimum sample size requirement for factor analysis was satisfied, with the final sample size of 291. The data set was found suitable for factor analysis as KMO index was 0.88(greater than0.50) and Bartlett’s test of sphericity was significant (p = 0.000). The EFA of 58 items yielded a three-factor model that accounted for 69.6% of the variance (Annexure 2).

By EFA, 14 Factors were explored from which we have considered 13 as 14th factor loaded one factor only and will have weak reliability. There were double loadings of 2 items. One was loaded in factor 7 and 10, decision was made to keep the item in factor 7 and another one loaded on factor 5 and 6 and was considered in 6. Both the decision was made considering higher value of loading factor score. One item was deleted “Encouraged students to adhere to course outline” as it was loaded individually and have no reliability. One more item which has not loaded in any of the factor was also removed “sequentially providing them the schedule” which may have very less scores (Table 1). Discussion

Principles of Gestalt Psychology allow for the effective dissemination of information and knowledge through the use of simple and accessible methods for development of critical thinking abilities in students [17]. The instructional methods like pre-reading materials, pre-tests, quizzes and retrieval strategies can help to assess and facilitate students’ active learning as well as improve their performance [18–21]. The faculty members highlighted the importance of reflective practice, formative assessments and spaced repetition to improve memory retention. Moreover, they realized the need of feedback on students' assessment scores, to foster learning and motivation among students [1].

As Bain (2004) quotes “teaching is not about telling or presenting information; it is about creating an environment in which most, if not all students can learn.” Effective teaching integrates cognitive psychology principles into classroom practices, recognizing the diversity among students in terms of individual goals, expectations, varying levels of prior knowledge, and distinct learning preferences [22]. Hence authors considered it crucial to engage in innovative strategies to commendably manage this task and associate them with new, upcoming technological activities to enhance engagement [23, 24]. Faculty members also tend to apply learning guidance, goal setting, recall practice, and cognitive aids, to effectively teach academic concepts in undergraduate education [25].

“Cognition about cognitive phenomena” is metacognition as defined by Flavel in 1979. Its development is thought to be an interplay between knowledge, one’s prior experience, goal and one’s actions. Self-regulation is considered to be a part of metacognition in the current day and era [26]. Literature implies that being cognizant enables individuals to contribute to higher performance in tests and assessments [27]. Current study observed that the faculty perception of strategies to enhance metacognition in their sessions was steep and felt that they were somewhere midway between applying some to all the strategies. Contrary to this observation, a recent study conducted on 109 allied health students emphasized on metacognition to improve patient care and hence are repeatedly amalgamated in the learning outcomes [28]. Moreover, self-reflection strategies were noted to be underused by facilitators in the current study. In contrast to which literature suggests the implementation of self-regulated learning practices enhances the habit of reflection-in-learning which can augment the academic performance scores of postgraduate students [29, 30]. Globally, facilitators employ reflective assignments to foster and evaluate the application and benefits of self-regulated learning of undergraduate students [31]. While developed countries have accepted the multifaceted benefits attached to self-regulated learning and metacognition, developing countries, like Pakistan, are still struggling to implement these useful strategies of cognitive psychology to enhance the quality of medical education.

Application of basic principles of power points was found to be fairly high among the educators of the current study. Concurrent to this study, a comparative study conducted in Spain, discerned that medical science faculty found the use of power points, especially in the form of images and pictures, to be beneficial to their students [32]. Retrieval practice, which involves students recalling and applying information, is an effective way to encourage transfer of knowledge, particularly when retrieval events are spaced out over time [5].

Developing a relationship of trust between students and instructors can significantly address students’ fears. When teachers offer respect, assistance, resources, available during official hours, and establish fair policies and deadlines that they consistently adhere to, it fosters trust between students and teachers. Students are known to exert their best efforts and tackle significantly challenging assignments with ease provided they have confidence in their instructors [1]. Hence, application of psychology principles in teaching practices, not just develops a self-regulated and self-directed learner but also fosters the bond of trust and mentorship between the two major stake holders in a conducive environment.

The study was carried out in a particular region or institutions, so it can affect the generalizability of the findings. Self-reported data from faculty members regarding their application of cognitive psychology principles and Mayer’s principles may be subject to biases. A brief study duration may not determine the long-term effects of cognitive psychology and Mayer’s principles on learners’ performance. While our study contributes significantly in faculty application of cognitive psychology principles, it is important to note that the lack of control for certain confounding variables may influence the results.

The responses of faculty members were conducted on self-reported questionnaire hence the possibility of recall bias cannot be omitted and the interpretation of the factors extracted in EFA is subjective and requires theoretical and practical judgment. Moreover, due to use of convenience sampling we acknowledge the inherent limitations in generalizability of the findings and the potential for selection bias. To address this and minimize the impact on our findings, we stratified our sample by key demographic variables like age, gender, university to ensure that important subgroups were adequately represented reducing sampling error.

None the less our study results suggest that the institutions should arrange webinars and trainings for the faculty members which are focused on teaching and pedagogy as well as emotional and psychological aspects that should be considered. Assignments should be designed in a way that enables the students to make connections between theory and practice using visual representations thereby promoting the long-term retention and enabling retrieval. Faculty should be equipped to design strategies which can foster self-motivation, autonomy and metacognitive abilities which can foster lifelong learning.

Future research should aim to recruit more diverse samples from various medical and dental institutions to enhance generalizability and conduct longitudinal studies to assess the long-term impact of cognitive psychology and Mayer's principles on students' learning. Additionally, experimental studies can be designed where faculty are trained in cognitive psychology principles and their effect on student performance is evaluated against a control group. Furthermore, collecting student feedback will provide valuable insights into their perspectives on the application of cognitive psychology principles, allowing for a more comprehensive understanding of its effectiveness in educational settings.

Conclusion

Principles of Cognitive psychology plays a crucial role in shaping the student’s learning which significantly impact overall educational outcome. This study revealed that the faculty applied certain cognitive psychology principles by practicing innovative teaching strategies, leveraging technology to enhance comprehension and engagement in their teaching and diligently following Mayer’s multimedia principles. However, more efforts have to be made to train the faculty to promote inculcation of memory retention, self-assessment and self-reflection strategies in their teaching taking a more balanced approach in integrating these principles to motivate the students evolve into competent physicians.

Postoperative analgesic outcomes of fascia iliaca compartment block versus pericapsular nerve group block in hip fracture surgery: a retrospective observational study
Authors: Sevim Şenol Karataş, Sait Fatih Öner
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHip fracture surgery is associated with substantial postoperative pain, delayed mobilization, and increased opioid requirements, particularly in older adults. Ultrasound-guided regional nerve blocks are increasingly used as components of multimodal analgesia. This study compared postoperative analgesic outcomes between fascia iliaca compartment block (FICB) and pericapsular nerve group (PENG) block in patients undergoing hemiarthroplasty for hip fracture.MethodsThis retrospective single-center cohort study included all 186 eligible adult patients who underwent hemiarthroplasty for hip fracture under spinal anesthesia between October 2022 and October 2024. Patients received either ultrasound-guided FICB (n = 90) or PENG block (n = 96) before surgery. The primary outcome was postoperative visual analogue scale (VAS) score at 6 h. Secondary outcomes included VAS score at 24 h, cumulative tramadol consumption during the first postoperative 24 h, quality of recovery assessed using the QoR-15, hospital length of stay, and intraoperative hemodynamic parameters. Separate multivariable linear regression models were used to adjust for age, sex, and ASA physical status.ResultsBaseline characteristics were comparable between groups. Patients receiving PENG block had significantly lower postoperative VAS scores at 6 h (3.42 ± 0.90 vs. 5.04 ± 1.24; mean difference − 1.63, 95% CI − 1.94 to − 1.32; p < 0.001) and at 24 h (5.29 ± 0.86 vs. 6.40 ± 0.91; mean difference − 1.11, 95% CI − 1.36 to − 0.85; p < 0.001). Cumulative 24-hour tramadol consumption was significantly lower in the PENG group (243.98 ± 54.27 mg vs. 347.89 ± 65.87 mg; mean difference − 103.91 mg, 95% CI − 121.32 to − 86.49; p < 0.001). QoR-15 scores were significantly higher following PENG block (91 [88–93] vs. 83 [80–85]; p < 0.001). After adjustment for age, sex, and ASA physical status, PENG block remained independently associated with lower VAS scores at 6 and 24 h and lower 24-hour tramadol consumption.ConclusionsCompared with FICB, PENG block was associated with lower postoperative opioid requirements, lower early postoperative pain scores, and improved patient-reported quality of recovery following hemiarthroplasty for hip fracture. The reduction in postoperative tramadol consumption appears to represent the most clinically relevant finding. Prospective randomized multicenter studies are warranted to confirm these observations.Keywords: Hip fracture, Hemiarthroplasty, Pericapsular nerve group block, Fascia iliaca compartment block, Regional anesthesia, Postoperative pain, Opioid consumption, Quality of recovery

Introduction

Hip fracture is one of the most common orthopedic emergencies in older adults and is associated with substantial morbidity, mortality, functional decline, and healthcare costs. Owing to population aging, the absolute number of hip fractures is projected to increase markedly over the coming decades, further expanding its clinical and socioeconomic burden. Therefore, timely surgical treatment combined with effective perioperative care is essential to reduce perioperative complications and improve postoperative recovery [1–3].

Postoperative pain remains a major challenge after hip fracture surgery. Inadequate pain control may delay ambulation, prolong hospitalization, impair rehabilitation, and contribute to adverse outcomes, including delirium and persistent functional decline [4]. Older adults are particularly vulnerable because frailty, multiple comorbidities, and age-related pharmacokinetic changes increase both pain-related morbidity and susceptibility to opioid-related adverse effects [5]. Therefore, contemporary perioperative care increasingly emphasizes opioid-sparing multimodal analgesia to optimize pain control while minimizing opioid exposure [6].

Ultrasound-guided peripheral nerve blocks have become an important component of multimodal analgesic strategies for hip fracture surgery [7]. Compared with systemic opioid-based analgesia alone, regional analgesia provides more effective pain relief while reducing opioid requirements and opioid-related adverse events [7, 8]. Improved perioperative analgesia may also support earlier mobilization and postoperative functional recovery, which are central goals of enhanced recovery pathways in geriatric hip fracture care [9, 10].

Among currently available regional techniques, the fascia iliaca compartment block (FICB) has been widely adopted because of its technical simplicity, favorable safety profile, and ability to anesthetize the femoral and lateral femoral cutaneous nerves [11]. Numerous studies have demonstrated that FICB provides effective postoperative analgesia and reduces opioid consumption after hip surgery [11]. However, the analgesic efficacy of FICB remains variable because the spread of local anesthetic to the obturator and accessory obturator nerves is often inconsistent. As these nerves contribute substantially to the sensory innervation of the anterior hip capsule, incomplete blockade may limit postoperative analgesia in some patients [12, 13].

The pericapsular nerve group (PENG) block is a relatively recent ultrasound-guided regional anesthesia technique developed to target the articular branches innervating the anterior hip capsule [13]. Anatomical studies have shown that sensory innervation of the hip joint is concentrated predominantly in the anterior capsule, providing the rationale for selective blockade of these articular branches [12, 14]. Recent randomized trials suggest that PENG block may reduce postoperative opioid consumption, improve early pain control, better preserve quadriceps muscle strength, and support early mobilization compared with conventional regional techniques [15, 16]. However, the available evidence remains heterogeneous in terms of surgical populations, comparator blocks, outcome measures, and perioperative analgesic protocols [15, 16].

Although several prospective studies have compared the PENG block with the fascia iliaca compartment block (FICB), evidence from routine clinical practice remains limited [17]. Most published studies have consisted of randomized controlled trials with relatively small sample sizes or have included heterogeneous orthopedic populations, such as patients undergoing elective total hip arthroplasty and hip fracture surgery [15, 16]. Data specifically evaluating older adults undergoing hemiarthroplasty for fragility-related hip fractures remain limited [17]. In addition, most previous studies have primarily focused on postoperative pain scores, whereas opioid-sparing effects and broader recovery outcomes have been evaluated less consistently [15, 17].

Therefore, the present study aimed to compare postoperative analgesic outcomes between ultrasound-guided PENG block and fascia iliaca compartment block (FICB) in a homogeneous cohort of older adults undergoing hemiarthroplasty for fragility-related hip fracture under spinal anesthesia. We hypothesized that the PENG block would be associated with lower postoperative opioid consumption, lower postoperative pain scores, and improved early quality of recovery compared with FICB. To minimize the influence of confounding inherent to the retrospective study design, multivariable regression analyses were performed in addition to conventional between-group comparisons.

Perioperative management

Spinal anesthesia was performed in the sitting position under sterile conditions using a 25-G Quincke spinal needle via a midline approach at the L3–L4 or L4–L5 interspace. After confirmation of free cerebrospinal fluid flow, 12.5 mg of 0.5% hyperbaric bupivacaine was administered intrathecally. Before surgical incision, all patients received 2 mg of intravenous midazolam for standardized sedation. Standard intraoperative monitoring, including electrocardiography, noninvasive blood pressure, and pulse oximetry, was applied throughout the procedure. Surgery commenced after an adequate sensory block had been achieved. No intrathecal opioids were administered.

Peripheral nerve blocks were performed preoperatively under ultrasound guidance by two consultant anesthesiologists, each with more than 10 years of experience in ultrasound-guided regional anesthesia. Both anesthesiologists routinely performed both FICB and PENG blocks during the study period. Operator identity was not recorded in the study database. For the fascia iliaca compartment block (FICB), 40 mL of 0.25% bupivacaine was injected beneath the fascia iliaca. For the pericapsular nerve group (PENG) block, 20 mL of 0.25% bupivacaine was injected into the plane between the psoas tendon and the iliopubic eminence under ultrasound guidance (Fig. 2). During the study period, block selection was based on the attending anesthesiologist’s routine clinical judgment rather than a predefined allocation protocol.

Postoperative analgesia

All patients received a standardized postoperative analgesic protocol. Intravenous paracetamol (1,000 mg) was administered routinely every 8 h during the first 24 postoperative hours. Intravenous tramadol (100 mg) was administered as rescue analgesia when the visual analog scale (VAS) pain score was ≥ 4 or when additional analgesia was considered clinically necessary. Repeat tramadol administration was permitted at intervals of at least 6 h, with a maximum daily dose of 400 mg. Total tramadol consumption during the first postoperative 24 h was recorded. Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and other systemic analgesics were not included in the standardized postoperative analgesic protocol.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 29.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Continuous variables were assessed for normality using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Normally distributed variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), whereas non-normally distributed variables are presented as median and interquartile range (IQR). Categorical variables are expressed as number and percentage.

Baseline characteristics were compared using the independent-samples t test or Mann–Whitney U test, as appropriate. Categorical variables were compared using Pearson’s chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test.

Between-group differences in postoperative outcomes are reported together with mean differences and 95% confidence intervals (CI).

To account for potential confounding associated with the retrospective non-randomized design, separate multivariable linear regression models were constructed for the 6-hour VAS score, 24-hour VAS score, and cumulative 24-hour tramadol consumption. Each model simultaneously included block type, age, sex, and ASA physical status. Block type was entered with FICB as the reference category, sex with male as the reference category, and ASA physical status was represented using indicator variables with ASA I as the reference category. Unstandardized regression coefficients (B), 95% confidence intervals (CIs), and p values were reported for all predictors. Model fit was summarized using R² and adjusted R².

Changes in intraoperative MAP and HR over time were analyzed using repeated-measures analysis of variance (RM-ANOVA), including the effects of group, time, and group-by-time interaction.

All statistical tests were two-sided, and a p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Because multiple secondary outcomes were evaluated, no formal adjustment for multiple comparisons was applied. Accordingly, the analyses of secondary outcomes should be interpreted as exploratory.

Discussion

Principal findings

The present retrospective cohort study compared postoperative analgesic outcomes between ultrasound-guided fascia iliaca compartment block (FICB) and pericapsular nerve group (PENG) block in patients undergoing hemiarthroplasty for hip fracture under spinal anesthesia. The principal finding was that PENG block was associated with lower postoperative opioid consumption, lower postoperative pain scores, and better patient-reported quality of recovery compared with FICB. After adjustment for age, sex, and ASA physical status, these associations remained statistically significant, suggesting that the observed differences were not solely attributable to baseline patient characteristics.

Among all evaluated outcomes, the reduction in cumulative 24-hour tramadol consumption represents the most clinically meaningful finding of the present study [17]. Although postoperative pain scores were also significantly lower in the PENG group, the absolute between-group difference was relatively modest. In contrast, opioid sparing is of particular clinical importance in older adults with hip fractures because opioid-related adverse effects—including delirium, respiratory depression, nausea, constipation, urinary retention, and delayed mobilization—may adversely affect postoperative recovery and functional outcomes [5, 18]. Therefore, reducing opioid requirements may provide greater clinical benefit than small improvements in pain intensity alone [5, 18].

The superiority of the PENG block observed in the present study is biologically plausible. Unlike the fascia iliaca compartment block, which primarily targets the femoral and lateral femoral cutaneous nerves, the PENG block was specifically designed to anesthetize the articular branches supplying the anterior hip capsule [13]. Because the anterior hip capsule contains a high density of nociceptive sensory fibers, selective blockade of these articular branches may provide more effective analgesia after hip fracture surgery [12, 14].

Several randomized controlled trials have reported improved early postoperative analgesia following PENG block compared with conventional regional techniques [16, 19, 20]. Our findings are consistent with these reports while extending the available evidence to a real-world retrospective cohort of elderly hemiarthroplasty patients treated in routine clinical practice.

Another potential explanation relates to preservation of motor function. Previous anatomical and clinical studies have suggested that PENG block may better preserve quadriceps strength than FICB, facilitating earlier mobilization and functional recovery [12, 16, 21]. Although muscle strength was not evaluated in the present study, the significantly higher QoR-15 scores observed in the PENG group may partially reflect improved early functional recovery in addition to better analgesia.

Clinical importance of the VAS difference

Although postoperative pain scores were significantly lower after PENG block, statistical significance should not automatically be interpreted as clinical superiority. The between-group difference in VAS scores observed in the present study was relatively modest, and small differences in pain intensity do not necessarily exceed the reported minimal clinically important difference (MCID) [22]. Therefore, interpretation based solely on pain scores may overestimate the practical importance of the intervention.

For this reason, postoperative pain was interpreted together with opioid consumption and patient-reported quality of recovery. When these complementary outcomes are considered collectively, the findings consistently favor the PENG block. The combination of lower tramadol consumption and higher QoR-15 scores suggests that the clinical benefits of PENG extend beyond pain intensity alone.

Quality of recovery

An important strength of the present study is the inclusion of patient-reported postoperative recovery assessed using the QoR-15 questionnaire [23]. Traditional analgesic studies frequently focus primarily on postoperative pain scores and opioid consumption. However, postoperative recovery is a multidimensional construct encompassing physical comfort, emotional state, physical independence, psychological support, and overall well-being [23].

Patients receiving the PENG block achieved significantly higher QoR-15 scores than those receiving FICB (median 91 [88–93] vs. 83 [80–85], p < 0.001). Because the observed between-group difference approached or exceeded the proposed minimal clinically important difference (MCID) for the QoR-15, these findings suggest that improved regional analgesia may contribute to enhanced overall postoperative recovery [24]. Nevertheless, these results should be interpreted with caution because QoR-15 data were obtained retrospectively from routine clinical practice rather than collected within a predefined prospective study protocol.

Hemodynamic findings

Transient differences in mean arterial pressure were observed during surgery, whereas heart rate remained comparable between groups. Repeated-measures analysis demonstrated the expected physiological changes over time during spinal anesthesia but did not indicate clinically meaningful hemodynamic instability attributable to either regional analgesic technique. Accordingly, the primary implication of the present study relates to the superior postoperative analgesic profile of the PENG block rather than to differences in intraoperative hemodynamic responses.

Strengths of the study

Several methodological strengths of the present study deserve consideration. First, all eligible patients with complete outcome data were included in the analysis, avoiding additional sampling or group-size matching and thereby improving the representativeness of the study cohort. Second, all patients underwent the same surgical procedure—hemiarthroplasty for fragility-related hip fracture—thereby minimizing heterogeneity associated with surgical trauma and perioperative management. Third, adjustment for important baseline characteristics using multivariable regression analyses reduced the influence of measured confounding inherent to retrospective observational research. Fourth, patient-reported postoperative recovery was assessed using the QoR-15 in addition to conventional analgesic outcomes. Finally, postoperative analgesic efficacy was evaluated using multiple complementary endpoints rather than relying solely on pain intensity.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective observational design precludes causal inference and remains susceptible to residual confounding despite multivariable adjustment. Second, treatment allocation was based on routine clinical decision-making rather than randomization. Although baseline characteristics were comparable between groups, unmeasured confounders may still have influenced block selection. Third, different local anesthetic volumes were used for the two block techniques (40 mL for FICB and 20 mL for PENG). These volumes reflected routine institutional practice but may have contributed to differences in analgesic efficacy. Fourth, postoperative pain was evaluated only at rest because movement-evoked pain scores were not routinely documented in the medical records. Fifth, muscle strength, early mobilization, postoperative delirium, and long-term functional recovery could not be evaluated because these variables were not routinely recorded in this retrospective cohort. In addition, operator identity was not recorded in the retrospective dataset; therefore, the potential influence of individual practitioner variability on block performance could not be evaluated. Finally, this was a single-center study, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other institutions and healthcare settings.

Clinical implications

Despite these limitations, the present findings have important clinical implications. For older adults undergoing hemiarthroplasty for fragility-related hip fracture, the PENG block may represent an effective component of multimodal analgesia that reduces postoperative opioid requirements while improving early postoperative recovery. Given the increasing emphasis on enhanced recovery pathways and opioid-sparing perioperative analgesic strategies, these findings support further evaluation of the PENG block in adequately powered, prospective multicenter randomized controlled trials [25].

Conclusion

In this retrospective cohort of older adults undergoing hemiarthroplasty for fragility-related hip fracture under spinal anesthesia, the PENG block was associated with a more favorable postoperative analgesic profile than the fascia iliaca compartment block. Although both techniques formed part of an effective multimodal analgesic strategy, patients receiving the PENG block had lower postoperative opioid requirements, lower pain scores, and better early quality of recovery.

Among the evaluated outcomes, the reduction in postoperative tramadol consumption appears to represent the most clinically relevant finding, particularly in this older population, in whom minimizing opioid exposure may reduce the risk of treatment-related adverse effects.

Because of the retrospective observational design, these findings should be interpreted as associations rather than evidence of causality. Large, adequately powered, multicenter randomized controlled trials with standardized block techniques and comprehensive functional outcome assessment are warranted to confirm the comparative effectiveness of the PENG block in hip fracture surgery.

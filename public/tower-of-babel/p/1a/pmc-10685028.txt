miR-460b-5p promotes proliferation and differentiation of chicken myoblasts and targets RBM19 gene
Authors: Kai-Zhi Zhou, Peng-Fei Wu, Xuan-Ze Ling, Jin Zhang, Qi-Fan Wang, Xin-Chao Zhang, Qian Xue, Tao Zhang, Wei Han, Gen-Xi Zhang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe meat production of broilers is crucial to economic benefits of broiler industries, while the slaughter performance of broilers is directly determined by skeletal muscle development. Hence, the broiler breeding for growth traits shows a great importance. As a kind of small noncoding RNA, microRNA (miRNA) can regulate the expression of multiple genes and perform a wide range of regulation in organisms. Currently, more and more studies have confirmed that miRNAs are closely associated with skeletal muscle development of chickens. Based on our previous miR-seq analysis (accession number: PRJNA668199), miR-460b-5p was screened as one of the key miRNAs probably involved in the growth regulation of chickens. However, the regulatory effect of miR-460b-5p on the development of chicken skeletal muscles is still unclear. Therefore, miR-460b-5p was further used for functional validation at the cellular level in this study. The expression pattern of miR-460b-5p was investigated in proliferation and differentiation stages of chicken primary myoblasts. It was showed that the expression level of miR-460b-5p gradually decreased from the proliferation stage (GM 50%) to the lowest at 24 h of differentiation. As differentiation proceeded, miR-460b-5p expression increased significantly, reaching the highest and stabilizing at 72 h and 96 h of differentiation. Through mRNA quantitative analysis of proliferation marker genes, CCK-8 and Edu assays, miR-460b-5p was found to significantly facilitate the transition of myoblasts from G1 to S phase and promote chicken myoblast proliferation. mRNA and protein quantitative analysis of differentiation marker genes, as well as the indirect immunofluorescence results of myotubes, revealed that miR-460b-5p significantly stimulated myotube development and promote chicken myoblast differentiation. In addition, the target relationship was validated for miR-460b-5p according to the dual-luciferase reporter assay and mRNA quantitative analysis, which indicates that miR-460b-5p was able to regulate RBM19 expression by specifically binding to the 3′ UTR of RBM19. In summary, miR-460b-5p has positive regulatory effects on the proliferation and differentiation of chicken myoblasts, and RBM19 is a target gene of miR-460b-5p.Key words: miRNA, skeletal muscle, growth and development, chicken

INTRODUCTION

Skeletal muscle is one of the essential components of animal bodies and plays an important role in maintaining posture, respiration, and initiating movement (Dumont et al., 2015). For the livestock and poultry, the development of skeletal muscle directly determines the amount of meat production which is related to economic benefits. During the embryonic period, myofibers are formed through the proliferation and differentiation of myoblasts, and further fuse into myotubes with the migration and adhesion. Under the maintenance of connective tissue, myotubes fuse again and eventually develop into muscles (Chen, 2020). After birth, the hypertrophy and remodeling of myofibers become the main pathway of skeletal muscle development due to the decline in the proliferation and differentiation capacity of myoblasts (Wang, 2019; Zhao et al., 2020). Naturally, this process is also influenced by the rates of protein synthesis and degradation (Zhu, 2014). When the degradation rate is higher than the synthesis rate, muscle growth will be stagnant. Hence, it shows a great importance to investigate the molecular regulatory mechanisms in skeletal muscle development.

The formation and development of skeletal muscle are regulated by multiple genes, transcription factors and noncoding RNAs (ncRNAs) in organisms, with myogenic regulatory factors (MRFs) and myocyte enhancer factor 2 (MEF2) families playing a central role in this process (Dong, 2019). MRFs, including MyoD, MyoG, Myf5, and MRF4, are specifically expressed only in skeletal muscle and participate in myoblast proliferation and differentiation throughout the whole development of muscle formation and functional improvement in animals (Zammit, 2017). The MEF2 family is widely involved in the development of myocardium, smooth muscle and skeletal muscle. The combination of MEF2 and MRFs can enhance myogenic gene expression and improve the efficiency of myogenic transformation of nonmuscle cells (Brand-Saberi, 2005; Yang, 2019). Besides, myostatin (MSTN) is a potent negative regulator of skeletal muscle growth, ubiquitously present in many species (Rodriguez et al., 2014). Although overexpression of MSTN will lead to muscle atrophy, it does not mean that it is a pernicious gene. MSTN plays a role in limiting the quantity and weight of myofibers in organisms, while the loss of its activity induces excessive hypertrophy of skeletal muscle (Lee, 2023). It was reported that the single muscle weight of MSTN mutant (MUT) mice is 2 to 3 times higher than that of normal individuals, which is a simultaneous effect of myofiber hyperplasia and hypertrophy (McPherron et al., 1997).

Noncoding RNA is divided into structural ncRNA and regulatory ncRNA, which mainly function in transcription and post-transcription. As one of the endogenous regulatory ncRNAs, the regulatory mechanisms of microRNA (miRNA) in organisms have been a hot spot for researchers. miRNA is a single-stranded RNA molecule with a length of approximately 21 to 22 nt that can specifically combine with the 3′ untranslated region (3′ UTR) of mRNA to exert regulatory effects (Horak et al., 2016; Ferragut Cardoso et al., 2021). The regulatory modes of miRNA on target genes mainly include 2 types: degradation of mRNA molecules or inhibition of translation (Fabian et al., 2010). When fully complementary to the 3′ UTR of the target gene, miRNA can degrade mRNA by disrupting its structural stability, such as directly cleaving mRNA or shortening its polyA tail (Xu et al., 2018; Fan et al., 2022). This mode of action is mostly found in plants and has extremely target specificity because of the complete complementarity. In contrast, miRNAs mainly repress the translation of target genes through incomplete complementary binding in animals (Bartel, 2004). For instance, as myogenic miRNAs, both miR-1 and miR-206 can promote myoblast differentiation and induce myotube formation through incomplete complementary combination with 3′ UTRs of PAX3 and PAX7 (Li et al., 2012; Dai et al., 2016; Xu et al., 2020). Meanwhile, miR-1 and miR-206 are also regulated by MRFs during skeletal myogenesis (Chen et al., 2006; Rao et al., 2006). In addition, more and more nonmyogenic miRNAs have been shown to play an important role in muscle development, such as miR-15a, miR-15b-5p, and miR-15c-5p, which have inhibitory effects on the proliferation and differentiation of chicken myoblasts (Li et al., 2019). It was demonstrated that miR-27b-3p and miR-2954 promote chicken myoblast proliferation by targeting MSTN and YY1, respectively (Dong et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2021). In mice, miR-122 mediates the TGF-β/Smad signaling pathway through inhibition of TGFBR2 expression, contributing to muscle production and repair after injury (Ding et al., 2020). miR-24-3p was found to have positive regulatory effects on myoblast differentiation in fetal cattle (Hu et al., 2019), mice (Holstein et al., 2020), and chickens (Wu et al., 2022).

Jinghai yellow chicken is one of the high quality yellow-feathered broiler breeds in China. Through our previous miR-seq analysis (accession number: PRJNA668199), miR-460b-5p was ultimately screened as one of the key miRNAs probably involved in the regulation of growth traits in Jinghai yellow chickens. Currently, there has been no relevant report of miR-460b-5p. Similar studies found that miR-460a-5p has a detrimental influence on the development of tibial cartilage in broilers (Kulyar, 2022) and may be involved in the regulation of skeletal muscle growth in tilapia (Huang et al., 2012). Therefore, functional assays were conducted at the cellular level in order to investigate the regulatory effect of miR-460b-5p on chicken skeletal muscle development. Moreover, target relationship validations of miR-460b-5p were further performed to improve the theory of molecular regulatory mechanisms.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Ethics Statement

All experiments in this study were performed in full accordance with the relevant specifications established by the Chinese Ministry of Agriculture. Animal experiments were assessed and approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Yangzhou University.

Animals and Tissues

The fertilized eggs of Jinghai yellow chickens used in this study were obtained from Jiangsu Jinghai Poultry Group Company (Nantong, Jiangsu Province, China). The leg muscles were collected for myoblast isolation when the chicken embryos were developed to the 12th embryonic age.

Chicken Primary Myoblast Isolation and Identification

The leg muscles removed skin and bones were cut into meat paste and then digested to single cells with collagenase type I (Solarbio, China) for 30 min at 37°C. The digestion of cell suspensions was terminated by DMEM/F12 medium (Solarbio, China) supplemented with 20% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Biological Industries, Israel) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin-amphotericin B solution (Solarbio, China). After filtering through 400-mesh filter sieves, the supernatant with single cells was obtained by centrifugation at 1,200 rpm. Chicken primary myoblasts (CPMs) were ultimately enriched through successive adherence method due to the longer adhesion time of myoblasts. First, the supernatant with single cells was inoculated into a 10-cm plate and placed the plate in the incubator. After 40 min, the supernatant was transferred to a new 10-cm dish, repeating the above steps 3 times to remove fibroblasts (Li et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2022).

When the density of CPMs was above 70%, immunofluorescence assay was performed to identify the purity of CPMs. After discarding the medium, CPMs were fixed by 4% paraformaldehyde (Solarbio, China) for 30 min, permeabilized with 0.5% Triton X-100 (Beyotime, China) for 15 min and blocked in 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) blocking buffer (Solarbio, China) for 30 min at 37°C. Phosphate buffered saline (PBS; BBI Life Science, Shanghai, China) was used to wash CPMs after each step. Subsequently, CPMs were successively incubated with anti-Desmin antibody (1:500; Bioss, China) overnight at 4°C and dylight 488-conjugated Goat anti-Rabbit lgG (1:500; Boster, Wuhan, China) for 1 h at 37°C, using BSA blocking buffer as diluent. Cell nuclei were stained with DAPI (Beyotime, China) for 5 min. Between each step, CPMs were washed by phosphate buffered saline with Tween-20 (PBST; Solarbio, China) for 5 min. Finally, fluorescent images were captured under the inverted fluorescence microscope (OLYMPUS, Japan).

Cell Culture

CPMs were cultured in DMEM/F12 medium supplemented with 20% FBS and 1% penicillin-streptomycin-amphotericin B solution at proliferation. The differentiation of CPMs was induced by DMEM/F12 medium supplemented with 2% horse serum (Solarbio, China) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin-amphotericin B solution.

As the cell line of chicken embryo fibroblasts, DF-1 cells were cultured in DMEM medium (Solarbio, China) supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin-streptomycin-amphotericin B solution.

All cells were cultured under 37°C and 5% CO2 conditions.

Construction of Wild-Type and Mutant Plasmids

As the potential target genes of miR-460b-5p, the 3′ UTRs of RBM19 and ASS1 were predicted for binding sites with miR-460b-5p through RNAhybird. The binding sites were mutated according to the principle of base transition in order to obtain the sequences of MUT-type insert fragments, and the original sequences of 3′ UTRs were used as the wild-type (WT) insert fragments (Table S1). Using pmirGLO as the vector, a total of 4 plasmids were generated including RBM19-WT, RBM19-MUT, ASS1-WT, and ASS1-MUT (Genecreate, China).

Cell Transfection

All transfections were performed using jetPRIME reagent (Polyplus, France) according to the manufacturer's protocol. The RNA oligonucleotides (Ribobio, China) for miRNA transfection, such as gga-miR-460b-5p mimic and mimic-NC (negative control), were transfected with 50 nM/L, while gga-miR-460b-5p inhibitor and inhibitor-NC (negative control) were transfected with 100 nM/L. In the cotransfection of miRNA and plasmid, the transfection concentration of the plasmid was 0.25 ng/μL. The sequences of RNA oligonucleotides were presented in Table S2.

RNA Extraction, Reverse Transcription, and Quantitative PCR

Total RNA was extracted from CPMs and DF-1 cells with Trizol (Tiangen, China). Nanodrop-1000 Spectrophotometer (Thermo, Wilmington) was used for measuring RNA density and optical density (OD). The RNA samples with OD260/280 between 1.8 and 2.1 were used for subsequent assays. Reverse transcriptions of miRNA and mRNA were performed using miRNA 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Vazyme, China) and HiScript III RT SuperMix for quantitative PCR (qPCR) (Vazyme, China), respectively. Forward and stem-loop primers of gga-miR-460b-5p were designed by miRNA Design V1.01, while mQ Primer R (Vazyme, China) was used as the reverse primer. SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme, China) was used for qPCR to detect the expression of miR-460b-5p, RBM19, proliferation marker genes (P21 and P53) and differentiation marker genes (MYOD, MYOG, and MYHC). U6 and β-actin were used as housekeeping genes for miRNA and mRNA, respectively. The melting curves of each pair of primers presented a smooth single peak. All primer information was presented in Table S3. Triple repeated tests were performed on each sample in qPCR.

CCK-8 Assay

CPMs were incubated with Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) Solution (Vazyme, China) for 2 h at 0 h, 24 h, 36 h, and 48 h of transfection, respectively. The absorbance at 450 nm was detected through Multimode Plate Reader (PerkinElmer, Germany) after incubation. For each transfection group, 3 and 6 biological replicates were set up in the 12-well plate and 96-well plate, respectively.

EdU Assay

CPMs were seeded in a 24-well plate and transfected with miRNAs when the density reached 40%. After 36 h of transfection, EdU assay was performed using Cell-Light EdU Apollo in Vitro Kit (Ribobio, China). Biological replicates for each transfection group were 6, and 2 randomly selected fields of view from each replicate were captured under the inverted fluorescence microscope (OLYMPUS, Japan).

Immunofluorescence

CPMs were induced to differentiate and transfected, when the density exceeded 90% in the 24-well plate. After transfection for 60 to 72 h, immunofluorescence assay was performed according to the method in CPM identification, while primary antibody was anti-MYH1 (Proteintech Group, Chicago) and cell nucleus were stained with hoechst (Ribobio, China) for 30 min. There were 6 biological replicates for each transfection group, and 2 visual fields were randomly selected from each replicate for photography and statistics.

Western Blot

After 72 h of transfection in the 12-well plate, total protein from differentiated CPMs was extracted using cell lysis buffer with protease inhibitor (Beyotime, China). Each sample was fully lysed on ice and collected by centrifugation of 12,000 × g for 5 min at 4°C. The concentration of proteins was measured by BCA Protein Quantification Kit (Vazyme, China) and diluted to the same level with cell lysis buffer. Proteins were denaturalized with SDS-PAGE loading buffer (Solarbio, China) at 99°C for 10 min. After denaturation, equal amounts of protein samples and 5 μL ColorMixed Protein Marker (Solarbio, China) were injected into wells of 4 to 20% precast gel (WSHT, China) for electrophoresis. The separated proteins were transferred to a polyvinylidene fluoride membrane, subsequently. After blocking with 5% defatted milk for 1 h, the incubation of primary antibodies was performed with anti-MYH1 (1:2,000; Proteintech Group, Chicago) and anti-GAPDH (1:5,000; Sangon Biotech, China) overnight at 4°C. HRP-conjugated Goat anti-Rabbit lgG (1:5,000; Cwbio, China) was used as secondary antibody to incubate proteins for 1 h. The membrane was washed by Tris buffered saline with Tween-20 (TBST; Solarbio, China) for 3 times after each antibody incubation, and 5% defatted milk was used as antibody diluent. Western blots were visualized using ECL solution (Vazyme, China) under Chemiluminescence Imaging System (Tanon, China), with 3 biological replicates for each transfection group.

Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay

After cotransfection of miRNAs and plasmids for 48 h, DF-1 cells were processed with Dual Luciferase Reporter Gene Assay Kit (Beyotime, China). The fluorescence of firefly and Renilla luciferase was detected by Multimode Plate Reader (PerkinElmer, Germany), respectively. The relative activity of firefly luciferase was reflected through the value of firefly/Renilla. The 5 biological replicates were set up for each transfection group.

Statistical Analysis

The quantitative results were normalized based on 2−ΔΔCT algorithm. Image-Pro Plus 6.0 was used to calculate the number of stained cells in the EdU assay and the staining area of myotubes in the immunofluorescence assay. The intensities of Western blots were measured by ImageJ. Significance was tested with independent samples t test between 2 groups and 1-way ANOVA for more than 2 groups in IBM SPSS Statistics 25, and P < 0.05 was determined as the criterion for significance. The significant levels were denoted as *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001, and ****P < 0.0001. All data are presented as mean ± standard deviation.

RESULTS

Detection of Purity and miR-460b-5p Expression for CPMs

As the specific protein in myoblasts, Desmin was detected to reflect the purity of CPMs in the immunofluorescence assay. The high coincidence of CPMs and DAPI stained nuclei indicated well purity of CPMs which can be used for subsequent cellular experiments (Figure 1A).

Detection of purity and miR-460b-5p expression for CPMs. (A) Purity identification of CPMs by immunofluorescence. Nuclei were strained blue with DAPI while Desmin of CPMs was specifically strained green with dylight 488. (B) Relative expression of miR-460b-5p in CPMs during the stage of GM and DM (n = 3). (C) Relative expression of miR-460b-5p after overexpression in CPMs (n = 3). In all graphs, results are presented as mean ± SD (standard deviation). Significant differences between groups without the same lowercase letter, **P < 0.01.

In order to investigate the expression pattern of miR-460b-5p in CPMs cultured in the growth and differentiation medium (GM and DM), qPCR was performed to detect the expression levels of miR-460b-5p in CPMs at 6 stages. The quantitative results showed that the relative expression of miR-460b-5p gradually decreased from the proliferation stage (GM 50%) to the lowest at 24 h of differentiation. As differentiation proceeded, miR-460b-5p expression increased significantly, reaching the highest and stabilizing at 72 h and 96 h of differentiation (Figure 1B). The pattern of miR-460b-5p expression in CPMs suggests that it may play a regulatory role in the proliferation and differentiation of CPMs. Hence, miR-460b-5p mimic and inhibitor were designed for functional validation through transfecting CPMs. qPCR was used to inspect the overexpression efficiency of miR-460b-5p mimic. After 48 h of transfection with miR-460b-5p mimic, the relative expression of miR-460b-5p was significantly increased approximately 300 times compared to negative control (Figure 1C).

miR-460b-5p Promotes the Proliferation of CPMs

As negative regulators of the cell cycle, P21 and P53 are also used as marker genes in cell proliferation. After overexpression of miR-460b-5p in CPMs, the relative expressions of P21 and P53 were significantly decreased (Figure 2A), while the opposite results were obtained after miR-460b-5p inhibition (Figure 2B). The activity of transfected CPMs in 96- and 12-well plates was detected in CCK-8 assay, which reflects the proliferation status of cells. It was showed that the proliferation of CPMs began to be significantly promoted after 24 h of transfection with miR-460b-5p mimic (Figure 2C, E). For CPMs transfected with miR-460b-5p inhibitor, cell activity was significantly inhibited after 24 h in 96-well plate (Figure 2D), while the difference of cell activity reached a significant level after 36 h in 12-well plate (Figure 2F). Although the apparent differences of myoblast activity are more obvious in 12-well plate, the statistical difference is not as significant as that in 96-well plate due to the difference within group and the limitation of replicates. Nevertheless, all of the results demonstrated that miR-460b-5p could promoted the proliferation of CPMs. In addition, the proportion of EdU positive cells in CPMs was significantly higher than that in the negative control after transfected with miR-460b-5p mimic (Figure 2G, I), indicating a significant increase in the number of CPMs entering S phase. When miR-460b-5p was suppressed, the proliferation of CPMs was inhibited manifested as the significant decrease of EdU positive cell ratio (Figure 2H, J).

miR-460b-5p Promotes the Differentiation of CPMs

In order to further investigate the function of miR-460b-5p in the differentiation of CPMs, 2% horse serum was used to induce differentiation of CPMs in vitro. Relative expressions of mRNA and protein of differentiation marker genes were detected at 48 h and 72 h after transfection, respectively. The results of qPCR showed that the mRNA expression levels of MYOD, MYOG, and MYHC were significantly upregulated with miR-460b-5p overexpression (Figure 3A). On the contrary, the mRNA expressions of MYOD, MYOG, and MYHC were significantly decreased after miR-460b-5p was inhibited (Figure 3B). The protein expression of MYHC was significantly increased in CPMs transfected with miR-460b-5p mimic (Figure 3C, E), while the opposite phenomenon was observed after miR-460b-5p repression (Figure 3D, F). When the transfected CPMs were induced to differentiate for 72 h, immunofluorescence staining was performed to evaluate the development of myotubes. It was found that the myotube area of CPMs was significantly larger than that of the negative control after transfected with miR-460b-5p mimic, suggesting the differentiation of CPMs was promoted by miR-460b-5p (Figure 3G, I), while the development of myotubes was significantly inhibited after miR-460b-5p was repressed (Figure 3H, J).

RBM19 is a Target Gene of miR-460b-5p

Based on the results of our previous miR-seq analysis, RBM19 and ASS1 were selected for target relationship validation of miR-460b-5p. RNAhybird was used to predict binding sites of miR-460b-5p with RBM19 and ASS1, respectively. After filtering out the binding sites with lower reliability, 3 and 1 binding sites were predicted in the 3′ UTR of RBM19 and ASS1, respectively (Figure 4A, B).

RBM19 is a target gene of miR-460b-5p. (A, B) Prediction of miR-460b-5p binding sites to 3′ UTRs of RBM19 and ASS1. The value of minimum free energy (MFE) in the figure was predicted by RNAhybird. Lower MFE represents higher reliability of the binding site. (C) Dual-luciferase assay after cotransfecting RBM19-WT/MUT + mimic/mimic-NC in DF-1 cells (n = 5). (D) Dual-luciferase assay after cotransfecting ASS1-WT/MUT + mimic/mimic-NC in DF-1 cells (n = 5). (E, F) RBM19 relative expression after overexpression and repression of miR-460b-5p in CPMs (n = 3). In all graphs, results are presented as mean ± SD (standard deviation). *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay was performed in DF-1 cells to verify whether miR-460b-5p directly targets the 3′ UTRs of RBM19 and ASS1. The results showed that there was no significant difference in the relative activity of firefly luciferase between 2 groups cotransfected with pmirGLO and miR-460b-5p mimic/mimic-NC, which ruled out the possibility of the empty plasmid sequence influencing the experiment (Figure 4C). In the RBM19-WT group, the relative expression of firefly luciferase was significantly inhibited after transfection with miR-460b-5p mimic, while no significant difference was observed in the RBM19-MUT group (Figure 4C). Meanwhile, there was no significant effect on the activity of firefly luciferase after transfected with miR-460b-5p mimic in both ASS1-WT and ASS1-MUT groups (Figure 4D). In addition, qPCR was further conducted to validate the target relationship of miR-460b-5p and RBM19 based on the results in dual-luciferase reporter assay. After overexpressing miR-460b-5p in CPMs, the mRNA expression level of RBM19 was significantly downregulated, while the inhibition of miR-460b-5p increased the relative expression of RBM19 (Figure 4E, F). These results demonstrated that miR-460b-5p can regulate RBM19 expression by specifically targeting the 3′ UTR of RBM19.

DISCUSSION

This study revealed the expression pattern and the regulatory role of miR-460b-5p during proliferation and differentiation stages of CPMs, and further demonstrated that RBM19 is a target gene of miR-460b-5p.

Based on the results of EdU assay and quantitative analysis of P21 and P53, it was found that overexpression of miR-460b-5p significantly reduced the mRNA expression levels of P21 and P53, while significantly increased the number of cells in the S phase, suggesting that miR-460b-5p may regulate cell cycle progression by participating in the P53/P21 pathway. The cell cycle of eukaryotes is mainly divided into 4 stages, including G1 phase (preparing for DNA synthesis), S phase (DNA synthesis), G2 phase (preparing for mitosis), and M phase (mitosis) (Schafer, 1998). As a checkpoint for cells proceeding from G1 to S phase, the elevated expression of P53 will activate the transcription of downstream gene P21, inducing cell cycle arrest (Engeland, 2018). In the healthy cell, P53 protein is continuously synthesized with high efficiency and degraded rapidly to maintain homeostasis for organisms, which causes a low protein level of P53 (Wang et al., 2015). However, the degradation of P53 protein will be inhibited after cellular genome damage, proto-oncogene activation, hypoxia or external stimuli (Halazonetis et al., 2008; Chen et al., 2021; Vaddavalli and Schumacher, 2022). Through post-translational modifications such as phosphorylation and acetylation, P53 is rapidly accumulated leading to various responses including cell cycle arrest, apoptosis, metabolic disorders and DNA repair (Wang et al., 2015; Stein et al., 2019; Kamagata et al., 2020). During the development of mouse skeletal muscle, the excessive accumulation of P21 and P53 accelerates the senescence of skeletal muscle satellite cells and triggers defects in skeletal muscle regeneration, while these adverse symptoms were recovered after treated with P53 inhibitors (Peng et al., 2022). Skeletal muscle atrophy is usually accompanied with abnormal function of mitochondria. After denervation of the left hind limb of mice to simulate muscle atrophy, mitochondrial dysfunction was found to be exacerbated in P53 deficient individuals, suggesting that P53 has a regulatory effect on the quality control and homeostasis maintenance of mitochondria in muscles (Memme et al., 2022).

In complex biological regulatory networks, P53 regulates the expression of numerous genes and is also regulated by multiple proteins (Hasty and Christy, 2013). As an important negative regulatory protein of P53, MDM2 promotes the degradation of P53 protein by ubiquitination, or inhibit its transcriptional activity by binding to P53 directly. Meanwhile, MDM2 is also targeted by P53, forming a closed negative feedback loop with P53 (Liebl and Hofmann, 2021). SESN1, as another P53 responsive gene, also functions as a negative feedback regulator in the P53 signaling pathway. It was found that SESN1 is regulated by miR-16-5p and involved in the proliferation, differentiation and apoptosis of chicken myoblasts through P53 signaling pathway (Cai et al., 2018). In addition, miRNAs influence P53 expression as a key mediator in the P53 pathway, whereas P53 also plays a regulatory role in the transcription, processing and maturation stages of miRNAs (Krell et al., 2013; Hu et al., 2021). The activation or mutation of P53 may cause expression disorders of related miRNAs (Grespi et al., 2016). For instance, the abnormal upregulation of miR-34a induced by activated P53 can repress the growth of cancer cells by inhibiting NOTCH1 (Tarasov et al., 2007; Park et al., 2014). The synergistic effect of miR-34a and P53 seems to provide a new approach for cancer treatment and the selection of therapeutic targets as well. In our previous sequencing analysis (Zhou et al., 2022), miR-34a-5p is differentially expressed in the leg muscle of Bian chickens at different embryonic ages, and screened as a key miRNA affecting the development of chicken embryos. Moreover, NOTCH2 is predicted as one of the potential target genes of miR-34a-5p, suggesting that the P53/miR-34a/NOTCH pathway may also play an important regulatory role in the growth and development of chickens.

In early studies, myofibers were classified into 2 types, including slow myofibers (type I) and fast myofibers (type II) based on the content of myoglobin and mitochondria in skeletal muscle (Schiaffino, 2018). With the deepening of research, myofibers were further divided into slow oxidation type (type I), rapid oxidation type (type IIa), rapid glycolysis type (type IIb) and intermediate type (type IIx), corresponding to 4 isoforms of MYHC respectively (Chen et al., 2021). MYHC is encoded by more than a dozen MYH family genes in organisms and is widely distributed in various muscle tissues with different tissue expression variability (He and Gu, 2017; Barrick and Greenberg, 2021). Hence, specific MYHC antibodies are used as an effective tool to explore types, functions and changes of myofibers (Schiaffino, 2018). In this study, MYH1 antibody was selected for Western blotting, which showed that overexpression of miR-460b-5p significantly upregulated MYH1 protein level, while the opposite result was observed after interference with miR-460b-5p. These results indicate that miR-460b-5p may be more inclined to promote the expression of MYH1 while promoting CPM differentiation. MYH1 is mainly expressed in type IIx myofibers of vertebrates, which rely on anaerobic glycolysis to generate strong contractile force in organisms (Wang et al., 2012). At present, many studies have confirmed that miRNAs are involved in the regulation of myofiber type, such as miR-499 (Liu et al., 2016) and miR-208b (Li et al., 2022), which promote the conversion of fast to slow myofibers by upregulating the expression of contractile proteins in slow myofibers. Moreover, miR-27a was found to promote the development of fast myofibers through attenuating the effects of leucine in skeletal muscle and reducing MYHC-I protein level (Zhang et al., 2019).

As members of the MRFs family, MYOD and MYOG play important roles in myoblast differentiation and skeletal muscle development (Vicente-García et al., 2022). It has been demonstrated that MYOD is mainly involved in the initiation of early myogenic differentiation process (Jia et al., 2009), while MYOG induces myoblast fusion and myofiber formation at the telophase of differentiation (Luo et al., 2015). It was reported that a large number of muscle specific genes are activated by the cooperation of MYOD and MYOG, which is particularly critical for myoblasts fusing to form myotubes (Lv et al., 2020; Wei et al., 2022). The loss of MYOD and MYOG will induce the block in myogenic differentiation, which ultimately leads to failure of skeletal muscle development (Braun and Gautel, 2011). In our study, overexpression of miR-460b-5p resulted in the upregulation of MYOD and MYOG mRNA levels, which significantly promoted the formation and development of myotubes. After inhibition of miR-460b-5p, the myotube development was suppressed with significant decrease in MYOD and MYOG expression. These results suggest that miR-460b-5p can influence myotube formation and myogenic differentiation by regulating the expression of MYOD and MYOG in CPMs.

Since miRNAs exert regulatory effects mainly by binding to the 3′ UTR of target genes, the 3′ UTR sequence of RBM19 was inserted into the downstream of the firefly luciferase reporter for the validation of target relationship. The results showed that the relative activity of firefly luciferase was significantly inhibited after miR-460b-5p overexpressed, and RBM19 mRNA level was affected by miR-460b-5p based on qPCR analysis, indicating that miR-460b-5p can regulate the expression of RBM19 through combination with the 3′ UTR of RBM19.

As a nucleolar protein, RBM19 plays an important role in the processing and synthesis of ribosomal RNA (Cong et al., 2012; Kallberg et al., 2012). In the embryos of mice, the development of nucleoli was immature after the pure mutation of RBM19, which activated the P53 apoptosis signaling pathway and ultimately induced embryonic development stagnation or even death (Zhang et al., 2008). The expression of RBM19 exhibits a highly dynamic pattern during the intestinal development of chicken embryos. At the 10th embryonic age, RBM19 was expressed throughout the intestine, while its expression level was gradually restricted along with the differentiation of the endoderm. Until the 13th day of embryos, the expression of RBM19 was just limited to the duodenum (Lorenzen et al., 2005). Another study suggested that RBM19 was able to promote the morphogenesis and differentiation of intestines in zebrafish embryos (Mayer and Fishman, 2003). In addition, RBM19 was also involved in the regulation of breast development (Pausch et al., 2016) and associated with various diseases (Borozdin et al., 2006; Iwanicka-Pronicka et al., 2016; Taleban et al., 2016; Wang et al., 2020).

CONCLUSIONS

This study reveals the expression pattern of miR-460b-5p in proliferation and differentiation stages of CPMs, and demonstrated that miR-460b-5p promotes myoblast proliferation and differentiation through facilitating the transition of CPMs from G1 to S phase and the development of myotubes. In addition, RBM19 was further validated as a target gene of miR-460b-5p. Our findings contribute to improving the theory of molecular regulatory mechanisms and may provide new insights for molecular breeding of chickens.

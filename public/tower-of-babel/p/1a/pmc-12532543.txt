A MGMT Enhancer Variant is Associated with Glioma Susceptibility and Progression
Authors: Shu Zhang, Yiji Chen, Wenshen Xu, Danfang Yan, Meiqin Chen, Jiayi Zheng, Liming Huang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAbstractThe O6-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase (MGMT) plays a significant role in the pathogenesis and progression of glioma. Numerous enhancer variants, including those within the MGMT gene region and adjacent gene regions, have been found to be associated with cancer development and progression. We investigated the significance of enhancer variants located in the intergenic spacer far from the MGMT gene in relation to glioma susceptibility and progression. We recruited 402 glioma patients and 654 controls for this investigation using Sequenom MassARRAY genotyping. We identified a significantly elevated risk of glioma among carriers with the rs11016629 TG genotype compared to those with the GG genotype (OR = 1.41, 95% CI 1.03–1.93; P = 0.034). Subgroup analyses revealed that rs11016629 was significantly associated with glioma risk in subjects with WHO grade IV tumor (OR = 1.59, 95% CI 1.07–2.38; P = 0.023) and high-grade glioma (OR = 1.57, 95% CI 1.11–2.21; P = 0.011). Patients who underwent gross total resection with TG/TT genotypes exhibited a 2.66-fold higher risk of disease progression than GG carriers (HR = 2.66, 95% CI 1.23–5.79; P = 0.014). Background

O6-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase (MGMT), a pivotal DNA repair enzyme, critically counteracts the mutagenic consequences of alkylating agents through its suicide enzyme mechanism (Gerson 2004). This repair process involves irreversible depletion of functional MGMT protein. Consequently, diminished MGMT expression compromises cellular capacity to resolve O6-alkylguanine lesions (Ochs and Kaina 2000; Liu et al. 1996). MGMT promoter methylation represents a critical mechanism underlying the silencing of MGMT transcription and the consequent reduction in MGMT protein expression. Clinical studies have established that MGMT promoter methylation serves as an independent prognostic factor in glioma patients, with evidence indicating enhanced efficacy of temozolomide therapy and radiotherapy in individuals exhibiting this methylation (Hegi et al. 2005; Kreth et al. 2011; Butler et al. 2020; Leske et al. 2023; Panigrahi and O'Malley 2021).

Emerging evidence suggests that the robust negative correlation between MGMT promoter methylation and MGMT protein expression may not universally apply to all glioma cases. Experimental investigations have revealed a subset of tumors demonstrating MGMT promoter methylation yet retaining measurable MGMT expression, while conversely, tumors with unmethylated promoters occasionally exhibit low MGMT protein levels (Kreth et al. 2011). Notably, clinical practice has documented instances where patients with unmethylated MGMT promoters demonstrated unexpected sensitivity to temozolomide treatment. Although promoter methylation remains a pivotal regulatory mechanism in MGMT silencing, accumulating evidence indicates the involvement of alternative pathways in modulating MGMT protein expression (Butler et al. 2020). For instance, studies revealed discordant patterns between MGMT promoter methylation and protein expression, while identifying a novel intergenic enhancer (MKI67-MGMT) that drives MGMT overexpression and confers temozolomide (TMZ) resistance in gliomas (Chen et al. 2018). In addition, it has been demonstrated that methylation levels of CpG islands within specific enhancers located in the MGMT intronic region and upstream promoter vicinity exhibit prognostic significance in gliomagenesis (Leske et al. 2023).

Enhancers orchestrate precise spatiotemporal control of gene expression through chromatin architecture modulation (Panigrahi and O'Malley 2021). Unlike promoters, distal enhancers are capable of being situated either upstream or downstream of, or at a considerable distance from, the transcription start site of their cognate gene. These regulatory elements establish physical interactions with target promoters via chromatin looping mechanisms, facilitating transcriptional activation (Bulger and Groudine 2011). Enhancer functionality is critically modulated by sequence alterations, including functional polymorphisms and somatic mutations that modify chromatin accessibility patterns and transcription factor binding affinities (Smith and Shilatifard 2014). Emerging evidence delineates how enhancer variants potentiate oncogenesis through aberrant promoter interactions. For example, the germline variant rs55705857 in a brain-specific enhancer disrupts OCT2/4 binding capacity while promoting ectopic interactions with the MYC promoter, conferring a six-fold elevated risk for IDH-mutant low-grade glioma development (Yanchus et al. 2022). Parallel investigations identified the regulatory variant rs1537372 as a glioma susceptibility locus through its modulatory effects on CDKN2B-AS1 expression (Ali et al. 2021). Studies have linked the enhancer germline variant rs16906252 T allele to increased MGMT promoter methylation and improved patient survival, with the effect being mediated by allele-specific promoter silencing (McDonald et al. 2013). Collectively, these findings suggest that allelic heterogeneity in MGMT enhancer elements may differentially influence glioma susceptibility and disease progression.

Previously, we examined the relationship between enhancer variants within the MGMT gene region and adjacent gene regions and the risk of glioma (Huang et al. 2018). Given that enhancers exert regulatory effects over considerable distances, this study further investigates the association of enhancer variants located in intergenic spacers distant from the MGMT gene with both the risk and recurrence of glioma.

Methods

Study Subjects

This retrospective cohort study enrolled 402 glioma patients and 654 age-matched cancer-free controls from January 2010 to July 2016. Participants comprised genetically unrelated Southern Han Chinese (CHS) individuals residing in Fujian, Zhejiang, and adjacent provinces. Initial histopathological confirmation was performed according to the 2007 WHO Classification of Tumors of the Central Nervous System (CNS tumors) at diagnosis. For current analysis, all cases underwent systematic reclassification following the 2016 WHO diagnostic criteria for CNS neoplasms (Louis et al. 2007, 2016). Progression-free survival (PFS) was calculated from commencement of primary treatment to either radiological/clinical progression or mortality. Cases with lost follow-up documentation or without evidence of disease progression were censored at last confirmed tumor evaluation. The censoring date for all survival analyses was February 3, 2020. Comprehensive demographic and clinicopathological parameters are catalogued in Additional File 1. The study protocol received approval from the Institutional Review Board (Ethics Committee) of the First Affiliated Hospital, Fujian Medical University, with written informed consent obtained from all study participants.

Selection of Candidate Genetic Variations

We employed the VISTA Enhancer Browser (https://enhancer.lbl.gov/; accessed 15 March 2023) to identify putative enhancer elements within intergenic regions distal to the MGMT locus (Visel et al. 2007). Genomic coordinates were visualized using the UCSC Genome Browser (https://genome.ucsc.edu/) (Kent et al. 2002), incorporating ENCODE regulatory annotations including histone modification profiles (H3K4me1, H3K4me3, H3K27ac) that distinguish enhancer/promoter elements. The DNase Clusters track delineates genomic regions characterized by heightened chromatin sensitivity to the DNase enzyme (The ENCODE Project Consortium. 2012). The ORegAnno track presents literature-curated regulatory elements (Lesurf et al. 2016). The common enhancer variants with minor allelic frequency (MAF) of ≥ 10% in CHS population were selected to assess the linkage disequilibrium (LD) status. Then, tag variants were chosen for genotyping.

Genotype Analysis

Genomic DNA was extracted from peripheral blood lymphocytes using the Tiangen TIANamp Genomic DNA Kit (Tiangen Biotech, Beijing, China). Genotyping was conducted on the Sequenom MassARRAY iPLEX platform (Sequenom, Inc., San Diego, CA, USA) with the following quality control measures: (1) blinded analysis procedures to eliminate case/control status bias; (2) inclusion of positive and negative controls on each assay plate; (3) randomized distribution of case and control samples across all plates.

Statistical Analysis

Associations between genotypes and glioma risk were assessed via multivariable logistic regression analysis. Progression-free survival (PFS) curves were generated using the Kaplan–Meier method, with between-group comparisons evaluated through log-rank testing. Multivariable Cox proportional hazards models were implemented to estimate adjusted hazard ratios (HRs) and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs), incorporating covariates for sex, age, WHO tumor grade, resection extent, and radiochemotherapy status. All statistical analyses were performed using Statistical Analysis System software (v9.4; SAS Institute, Inc., Cary, NC). Statistical significance was defined as a two-tailed P-value < 0.05.

Results

Four intergenic enhancer elements (hs542, hs562, hs699, hs737) associated with MGMT were identified through the VISTA Enhancer Browser. As demonstrated in Additional File 2, enhancer hs699 exhibited characteristic epigenetic signatures including H3K4me1 histone modification peaks and multiple DNase I hypersensitive sites. ORegAnno predicts hs699 as a regulatory region with multiple transcription factor binding regions. This enhancer region harbors four common polymorphisms (rs11016629, rs11016630, rs7068394, rs72853110). Consultation of the 1000 Genomes Project database (https://www.internationalgenome.org/) revealed the polymorphisms rs7068394 and rs72853110 are absent in Chinese population samples. Based on the absence of detectable variants in this reference cohort, these loci were systematically excluded from further association analyses. Given the complete linkage disequilibrium between rs11016629 and rs11016630 (r2 = 1.00), rs11016629 was selected as the tag variant for subsequent investigations.

Table 1 presents the genotyping results for rs11016629. Genotype distributions in both case (GG: 75.75%; TG: 23.00%; TT: 1.25%) and control (GG: 80.22%; TG: 17.79%; TT: 1.99%) groups conformed to Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium expectations (P > 0.05). Logistic regression analysis revealed a significantly elevated glioma risk associated with the TG genotype (OR = 1.41, 95% CI 1.03–1.93; P = 0.034) compared to the GG reference genotype. The TT genotype showed no significant association (P = 0.379), potentially attributable to its low frequency.

We conducted subgroup analyses stratified by sex, age, WHO grade, and IDH mutation status using a dominant model (Fig. 1). The results demonstrated that the risk of glioma was significantly elevated in patients with WHO grade IV (OR = 1.59, 95% CI 1.07–2.38; P = 0.023) and high-grade glioma (OR = 1.57, 95% CI 1.11–2.21; P = 0.011). A marginally significant increase in risk was observed in female patients (P = 0.053) and IDH-wildtype patients (P = 0.060).

Subgroup analyses for rs11016629 based on sex, age, WHO grade, and IDH mutation status in a dominant model. Central black dots represent ORs. Horizontal lines indicate 95% CIs

The association between rs11016629 and glioma progression was evaluated using the log-rank test. In the overall patient cohort, no statistically significant correlation was observed between rs11016629 and glioma progression (Additional File 3, P = 0.180). Subsequent subgroup analyses were performed to assess the impact of rs11016629 on glioma progression across different patient groups. Specifically, in patients who underwent gross total resection, rs11016629 was significantly associated with glioma progression, as indicated by the log-rank test (Fig. 2 and Additional File 4, P = 0.015). As presented in Table 2, the median PFS for subjects with the rs11016629 GG genotype who underwent gross total resection was 55 months, compared to 11 months for those with the TG genotype. Multivariable Cox regression analysis revealed that patients with the rs11016629 TG genotype had an adjusted HR of 2.71 (95% CI 1.25–5.89; P = 0.012) relative to those with the GG genotype. When the rs11016629 TG and TT genotypes were combined for analysis, a significantly worse PFS was also observed (HR = 2.66, 95% CI 1.23–5.79; P = 0.014).

Discussion

The aim of this study was to investigate the association between enhancer variants located in intergenic regions distant from the MGMT gene and the susceptibility and progression of glioma. Our findings revealed that the MGMT enhancer variant rs11016629 TG genotype is significantly associated with an increased risk of glioma, particularly in patients with high-grade glioma. Additionally, the rs11016629 T allele is linked to poorer progression-free survival outcomes in patients who underwent gross total resection.

Previous studies have identified several genetic variants associated with glioma risk. For example, a genome-wide association study (GWAS) involving over 12,000 glioma patients identified 13 new glioma risk loci, providing further evidence for the polygenic basis of genetic susceptibility to glioma. The study revealed that diverse genetic variants demonstrate substantial differences in genetic susceptibility to both glioblastoma (GBM) and non-GBM, indicating the existence of distinct etiological mechanisms (Melin et al. 2017). Another study involving more than 1600 glioma patients found univariate and multifactorial associations between 25 genetic variants and glioma molecular subtypes such as IDH mutation, TERT promoter mutation, and 1p/19q co-deletion. The study also suggested that subtype-specific associations for glioma risk loci (Labreche et al. 2018). In the Chinese Han population, GWAS on glioma has also been conducted, revealing that a genetic variant located in the CYP4F12 exon region at 19p13.12 demonstrates genome-wide significance in relation to glioma (Li et al. 2021).

These large-scale studies have detected genetic variants across the entire genome to identify glioma risk loci. In our study, candidate genetic variants were initially identified through bioinformatics analysis. Subsequent statistical analysis revealed risk loci associated with glioma susceptibility. We identified that the MGMT enhancer variant rs11016629 represents a previously unreported glioma risk locus, consistent with previous research emphasizing subtype-specific associations of glioma risk loci. Our findings indicate that rs11016629 is more likely to be associated with an elevated risk of glioma in WHO grade IV, high-grade, female, and IDH-wildtype subgroups. In the exploratory subgroup analysis, we observed marginally suggestive associations in females (P = 0.053) and IDH-wildtype cases (P = 0.060). Given the limited sample size of each subgroup, it cannot be ruled out that the sample size factor may have an impact on this result. The above findings need to be further clarified for their potential biological significance through external validation in independent cohorts and studies on molecular mechanisms.

Large-scale genome-wide association studies have significantly advanced our understanding of glioma susceptibility, identifying five established risk loci (TERT rs2736100, CCDC26 rs4295627, CDKN2A/CDKN2B rs4977756, RTEL1 rs6010620, and PHLDB1 rs498872) with additive effects on glioma risk (Liu et al. 2010). While these findings represent critical milestones, the field faces a fundamental challenge: the gap between statistical associations and causal mechanisms. The identified variants likely tag regulatory elements rather than directly driving pathogenesis, necessitating integrative approaches combining fine mapping, functional genomics, and mechanistic validation—an analytical framework our study exemplifies through its exploration of rs11016629. However, our study has not yet explored the biological mechanism between rs11016629 and glioma susceptibility, and further research in this direction is needed in the future. We propose three potential experimental frameworks to elucidate the rs11016629 mechanism: (1) CRISPR-mediated enhancer perturbation to assess the regulatory impact of rs11016629 on target gene expression (Chen et al. 2020); (2) High-resolution chromatin conformation capture (Hi-C) coupled with CRISPR-based knockout to delineate spatial chromatin interactions associated with this genetic variant (Baxter et al. 2018); (3) Enhancer-reporter constructs incorporating rs11016629 major vs. minor alleles for quantitative luciferase assays (Song et al. 2018; Gee et al. 2015). This integrative multi-dimensional strategy combines epigenetic editing, three-dimensional chromatin architecture profiling, and allele-specific functional validation, thereby strengthening our proposed framework for functional verification while offering potential methodological insights into non-coding variant characterization.

Other studies have shown that gliomas exhibit a preference for localizing in neurogenic niches with redox activity, such as the subventricular zone. In this region, the proliferation of neural progenitor cells continuously generates oxidative stress, thereby creating a microenvironment conducive to tumor growth (Valotto Neto et al. 2024). Studies have shown that various post-translational modifications of MGMT can affect its protein stability, DNA repair activity, and response to oxidative stress (Srivenugopal et al. 2016), while MGMT enhancer variants may influence the response to oxidative stress and thereby cause differences in glioma susceptibility. In the context of research on other neuropsychiatric disorders, it has been observed that genetic variations have specific effects on neural circuits, providing mechanistic parallels for the above hypothesis. For instance, the differential regulatory effect of the 5-HTTLPR polymorphism on the amygdala-periaqueductal gray connectivity clearly demonstrates how non-coding variations can reshape functional neural networks (Battaglia et al. 2024). Especially crucial is that another study on the brain's self-learning paradox highlights the necessity of clarifying causal relationships in the research of neural substrate specificity. The regulatory effect of transcranial magnetic stimulation on predicted high-risk networks (such as default mode network hubs) can test whether functional neural activity patterns promote the occurrence and development of gliomas associated with variations (Battaglia et al. 2025). Non-invasive brain stimulation (NIBS) techniques, such as transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS), hold dual potential as both investigative tools for elucidating gene–brain–behavior interactions and therapeutic modalities for modulating these complex relationships (Vucic et al. 2023; Kesikburun 2022). When coupled with hierarchical genomic data, NIBS technology may enable the precise identification of neural networks influenced by specific genetic variants, such as rs11016629, thereby bridging molecular mechanisms with system-level neurobehavioral outcomes. NIBS technology (especially TMS) has been approved by the FDA for the treatment of drug-resistant depression, obsessive–compulsive disorder (OCD), and migraine; however, its potential application in patients with brain cancer remains unexplored. Previous studies have demonstrated that gliomas can form functional electrical synapses with surrounding neural tissue, and this bidirectional communication actively promotes tumor cell growth and migration (Venkataramani et al. 2019; Venkatesh et al. 2019). This finding reinforces earlier evidence showing that neuronal activity plays a critical role in regulating glioma progression. Inhibitory TMS protocols, such as low-frequency or theta-burst stimulation, when applied to peritumoral regions, may suppress activity-dependent mitogenic signaling pathways—such as glutamate release and calcium influx (Ozdemir et al. 2020; Houdayer et al. 2008; Sprugnoli et al. 2021). Most of the aforementioned research data are derived from animal models or cellular-level studies. Our future mechanistic research aims to be conducted in experimental systems that more closely approximate the native brain environment. Specific research strategies envisioned include the application of NIBS monitoring to investigate the interaction between cortical electrical activity and tumor progression across different genetic subtypes, as well as the use of NIBS to modulate neural network activity in tumors harboring distinct gene mutations, thereby generating differential therapeutic outcomes. However, these approaches remain largely conceptual and necessitate preliminary validation studies to evaluate both their technical feasibility and biological efficacy.

Previous studies have explored the prognostic significance of genetic variants in glioma. For instance, one study found that the IDH1 genetic variant rs11554137 has no significant impact on progression-free survival or disease-specific survival in IDH-wildtype glioblastoma patients (Saaid et al. 2022). In contrast, another study reported that rs11554137 is associated with prognosis regardless of adjuvant treatment received and extent of primary surgery in patients with IDH-mutated grade II and III glioma (Franceschi et al. 2021). Collectively, these studies suggest that certain genetic variants are specifically associated with the prognosis of different glioma subtypes. Consistent with these findings, our study did not find a significant association between rs11016629 and glioma progression in the overall patient cohort but identified a significant association in patients who underwent gross total resection. MGMT enhancer variant rs11016629 TG/TT genotype may serve as a candidate biomarker for stratifying progression risk in underwent gross total resection patients. Previous studies have also found the relationship between single nucleotide polymorphism and the prognosis of patients with total resection of glioma. For instance, a study found that an exonic TP53 variant was associated with PFS in glioma patients who underwent surgical resection (Panciani et al. 2021). The potential reasons for the phenomenon that no association was found between rs11016629 and glioma progression in the overall population, but a significant association was observed in patients who underwent gross total resection may be as follows: for patients who did not undergo gross total resection, the disease progression was mainly driven by the growth of the tumor mass, while for patients who underwent gross total resection, the disease progression was mainly dominated by the biological behaviors such as recurrence and metastasis of residual tumor cells. After gross total resection, the differences in the biological behaviors of tumor recurrence and metastasis between the TG/TT genotype and the GG genotype were more significantly presented. Another conjecture for the reason is that the gross total resection alters the tumor microenvironment or surgical stress induces (such as hypoxia, inflammation, etc.) the remaining TG/TT genotype cells to gain survival advantages under such conditions. All these speculated reasons require further clarification through more basic research. Our study has certain limitations in terms of biological mechanisms. Nevertheless, the findings hold potential for guiding future clinical practice. Specifically, patients with the rs11016629 TG/TT genotype may necessitate more aggressive postoperative local or systemic therapies. Alternatively, expanding the resection scope following the identification of the TG/TT genotype via preoperative or intraoperative examinations might prove beneficial. It is crucial to note, however, that these hypotheses require rigorous clinical investigations for further validation and clarification.

This study shows that there is no significant association between the MGMT enhancer variant rs11016629 TT genotype and glioma susceptibility. This result may be due to the insufficient statistical power caused by the sample size and genotype frequency. It is particularly emphasized that a cautious attitude should be maintained when interpreting the relationship between the TT genotype and glioma susceptibility. This result awaits validation through larger-scale cohort studies in the future. We formally recognize the absence of MGMT promoter methylation and protein expression data as a methodological limitation. Owing to the inherent restrictions imposed by the retrospective observational design of this study, the systematic collection of comprehensive molecular biomarkers, encompassing the status of MGMT, was operationally constrained. As previously referenced in the background, prior investigations have established the mechanistic validity of enhancer variants exerting an impact on MGMT regulation, a regulatory mode that diverges from the epigenetic control mediated by promoter methylation. Looking forward, we explicitly advocate for the inclusion of both MGMT promoter methylation and protein expression analyses within prospective cohort studies. This integrative strategy will not merely verify the biological plausibility of our current findings, but more significantly, it will clarify the potential interplay between constitutional enhancer polymorphisms and epigenetic regulation during the pathogenesis of glioma.

Conclusions

This study establishes rs11016629 as both a susceptibility locus and resection-dependent prognostic marker in glioma, particularly for WHO Grade IV tumors, with sex-specific risk patterns and an association with IDH-wildtype status. Our data revealed that rs11016629 T allele is associated with a significantly higher risk of glioma and worse prognosis in the patients who underwent gross total resection. These findings substantiated the mechanistic involvement of MGMT regulatory element polymorphisms in gliomagenesis and disease progression.

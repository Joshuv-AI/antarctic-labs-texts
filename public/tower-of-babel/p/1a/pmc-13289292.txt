Using Instagram for public education in trauma prevention: experience of an academic Trauma League
Authors: Helena Alves Freire dos Santos, Juan Mariano Huertas Aguilar, Aléxis Vinicius Queiroz dos Santos, Ana Paula Boaventura, Thiago Rodrigues Araujo Calderan, Gustavo Pereira Fraga
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe use of social media offers the possibility of reaching a larger number of people worldwide, being accessible to different social classes, and being used for the dissemination of scientific knowledge, as well as for the prevention of traumatic injury education among lay people.Study designWe analyzed the Instagram of an academic Trauma League (Unicamp Trauma League) from July 2023 to July 2024 and observed the metrics that contributed to the dissemination of scientific knowledge. A Trauma League is an academic group composed of medical and nursing students focused on apprenticeship, extension, and research in trauma. We used data provided by the application’s “insights” tool, which allows analysis of up to the last 90 days, to identify the reach of the posts, the publications with the highest engagement, and information about the followers.ResultsThe official profile had 2361 followers and reached more than 10 000 accounts during the analyzed period. The post with the highest reach was a carousel featuring photos of the new league management, reaching 3240 accounts. Another featured publication was a post about a campaign to prevent motorcyclist-related trauma, which reached 940 accounts, 70 shares, and 80 likes. Two reels posted in partnership with another Instagram account (@trauma_unicamp), sharing activities related to the P.A.R.T.Y. (Prevent Alcohol and Risk-Related Trauma in Youth) program, obtained 4386 and 3163 views. 43.8% of the followers identified were residents of Campinas, the city where the project was developed, and the remaining followers were distributed across other Brazilian regions, and a smaller proportion was from abroad. 70.6% were between 18 years and 34 years old, and 64.6% were women.ConclusionThe findings suggest that posts addressing timely and relevant topics attract greater visibility and engagement. Among social media platforms, Instagram demonstrated effectiveness in disseminating educational content and promoting health awareness; however, further studies are needed to assess whether these results apply to other platforms.Level of evidenceLevel IV/Prognostic and Epidemiological.Keywords: social; Education, Medical; Multiple Trauma

Introduction

During the past three decades, digital technologies have transformed how health information is accessed and shared. Social media platforms now allow rapid dissemination of educational content without geographic barriers, reaching diverse populations across different age groups, socioeconomic backgrounds, and regions.1 2 Facebook, Instagram, and YouTube are some examples of digital media that can connect people online, allowing access to health education through vulnerable populations, as different people of different nationalities, ages, and social classes accept them.1 3 4

In this context, ensuring that health information is disseminated by credible, evidence-based sources is essential to prevent misinformation and to strengthen public trust in scientific communication. Misinformation on social media can distort the public’s perception of health risks and undermine preventive efforts, particularly in trauma care and injury prevention. Therefore, the participation of academic institutions and professional organizations in these platforms is key to providing accurate and accessible information to the general public. Ensuring that accurate, evidence-based information reaches the public is therefore essential in the digital era.5 6

The Trauma League is a group of medical and nursing students seeking professional improvement in the area of trauma, which aims to train multidisciplinary care and develop soft skills. These are students who, under the guidance of a professor, study trauma in-depth, carrying out extension, teaching, and research activities in the field.7 8 According to a study by Coyle and Vaughn, most young university students have never visited the institutional website of health organizations. Still, they access their social networks about three times a day.39,11 Another study showed that students achieved better results in learning Basic Life Support when they consistently used the social network Telegram for study and education.12 Members of the Trauma League use social media to promote knowledge about the topics studied, sharing relevant information with the public and contributing to education in injury prevention. Therefore, the Instagram of the Trauma League allows the dissemination of scientific knowledge in the area of trauma, providing learning to those involved in the activities, which are mainly young people, and to the public reached by the posts.39,12

According to the Community Health Education Methods guide, the main points to keep in mind for the effective use of social networks are: understanding the campaign’s target population and how they behave on social networks, and analyzing the most successful strategies.13,17 With that, our objective was to analyze the Instagram of the Trauma League of the University of Campinas (Unicamp, UTL) and observe the metrics that contributed to the dissemination of scientific knowledge and help the promotion and prevention of trauma. We hypothesized that Instagram, as a widely used social media platform, could reach a large audience—both within and beyond Campinas, and serve as a practical tool to support injury prevention campaigns.

Methods

Data analysis was performed based on Instagram content formats, including posts, reels (short videos), stories (temporary photos or videos available for 24 hours), and carousels, from the UTL, which can be found on the social network by @ligadotraumaunicamp and is in Portuguese; these formats are further detailed in table 1.

This Instagram account posts about trauma prevention, events organized by league participants, prevention actions in Campinas, and recent events that have affected the health of the population. Campinas is a city in the interior of the state of São Paulo, Brazil, with a population of 1139 042, according to a 2022 survey by the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics. Furthermore, Campinas is the center of a metropolitan region comprising 21 municipalities, with a population of around 3.1 million.

Data were collected using Instagram’s “Insights” analytics tool, which provides aggregated metrics for public accounts, allowing administrators to monitor user engagement and audience demographics. Although Instagram’s Insights offers valuable information, it has limitations—such as restricted access to historical data and reliance on platform-generated metrics—which should be considered when interpreting results. During the period from July 12, 2023 to July 19, 2024, information on reach, impressions, number of followers, likes, comments, shares, and engagement was collected for approximately 45 posts. In addition, sociodemographic aspects, such as cities, age groups, and genders, were taken into account among followers. From this, conclusions were drawn about the reach of each post type and model, as well as the one with the highest engagement and follower information. In addition to the publications, various “Stories” were posted, consisting of short videos or photos that remain available on the profile for up to 24 hours. Another type of publication analyzed was “carousel”, a post format based on multiple photos and/or videos within a single publication; instead of posting one image at a time, the carousel format enables viewers to swipe horizontally to see additional images or clips in the same post.

The operational definitions used in this study are summarized in table 1, accompanied by representative examples from the analyzed posts. “Reach” was defined as the number of unique users who viewed a post, reel, or story; “engagement” as the total number of interactions (likes, comments, shares, and saves); “views” as the number of times a video was watched for at least three seconds; and “impressions” as the total number of times a post appeared on users’ feeds.

Furthermore, in Brazil, we have Yellow May, a month dedicated to raising awareness of traffic-related injuries, that is, an injury-prevention awareness month. May is the month of the league’s main activity, with prevention promotion both inside and outside the university, all of which are recorded on the social media profile. Besides that, Yellow Wave is a movement created by UTL that aims to disseminate the Yellow May movement and its importance to other countries besides Brazil. Then, from April to July, Instagram maintained a focus on the Yellow Wave, which included students going to Congress and sharing about Yellow May around the world.

By creating an Instagram account, the user agrees to the application’s data policy. This policy allows the collection of data and recording of profile interactions with other accounts, and public information can be consulted by everyone. Additionally, the Trauma League’s Instagram posts were produced and posted by student members under the supervision of the coordinating teacher. This work was conducted by members of the UTL with guidance from trauma professors; therefore, the use of data for this study was authorized by the league, which believes in the importance of the topic and the involvement of its members. The work has no conflict of interest, and in this patient, ethical consent was not required.

Results

The section below explores the impact of strategic campaigns, academic participation in events such as the European Congress of Trauma and Emergency Surgery, and community initiatives with educational outreach focused on injury prevention, CPR (cardiopulmonary resuscitation) training, and alcohol-related trauma awareness. Together, these results illustrate how targeted content, scientific dissemination, and prevention-focused initiatives contributed to expanding the league’s visibility at local, national, and international levels and strengthened engagement with a predominantly young, Brazilian audience.

Reach

During the period from July 12, 2023 to July 19, 2024, 2587 Instagram user accounts were reached through different ways. Furthermore, 1956 accounts were reached via posts, 1433 via reels, and 1111 via stories. As for impressions, we observed 26 089 views.

However, during April to July 2024, the league’s Instagram profile had 8356 accounts, of which 17.7% were followers and 82.3% were non-followers. During this period, there were 47 439 impressions. In April 2024, the 23rd European Congress of Trauma and Emergency Surgery took place in Estoril, Portugal, and some members of the league and medical students at the University of Campinas participated. The league’s Instagram announced the abstracts approved for this congress, as well as the students’ attendance and awards, which reached many accounts and generated significant movement on the profile at the national and international levels.

The posts with the greatest reach among the publications are sequences of photos, also known as a “carousel,” in which the faces and names of the new board of directors of the Trauma League were presented. The first post shows the league’s coordination of 2023 and generated 43 visits to the profile, reaching 1366 accounts, and was the most liked (179 likes) within the period analyzed, also receiving 13 comments and 53 shares. Reaffirming these data, the 2024 league coordination post (figure 1) generated 73 visits to the profile, reaching 3240 accounts, 149 likes, 14 comments, and 16 shares.

Post presenting the new coordination of the league in 2024 showing their faces, name, and position. The figure shows the president, the vice president, and the marketing members.

Second, the post about the “Rolezinho Mata” campaign reached 940 accounts, 81 likes, and 70 submissions (figure 2). This campaign arose after a political motorcycle march that ended up causing a major accident on one of the main highways in Campinas. From this, a campaign was born against large-scale motorcyclist trips. This campaign was important because it used a widely known traffic accident to raise public awareness of ways to prevent such an event from happening again. As it was a highly discussed topic in the city, any post or news related to the incident drew the population’s attention, thus creating an opportunity to promote knowledge and prevention.

Post about the campaign “Rolezinho Mata,” which shows the speaker from the University of Campinas.

As for reels, the one with the greatest reach received more than 4300 views, had 123 likes and 18 shares, and was a partnership with another Instagram account (@trauma_unicamp) (figure 3). The video covers the Prevent Alcohol and Risk-Related Trauma in Youth (P.A.R.T.Y.) program, a prevention extension project organized by the Trauma League, aimed to raise awareness among young people about alcohol use and its consequences in traffic. Another highly visible reel was one that shows league members teaching CPR to lay people visiting the University of Campinas and had 1058 views (figure 4).

Engagement

Higher engagement not only increases algorithmic reach but also promotes dialog, peer-to-peer sharing, and greater retention of educational content related to trauma care, accident prevention, and community awareness campaigns.

During July 2023 and October 2023, 331 accounts were engaged, that is, had active actions on the Trauma League profile, of which 293 were previous followers, and 38 were non-follower accounts. The most relevant publications, in descending order, included: a post from the new board, the “rolezinho mata” campaign, a post about the thoracic trauma and mechanical ventilation class (organized by the league), and a post about an educational “blitz” conducted in the city of Campinas (figure 5). Between April 2024 and July 2024, the time of Yellow Wave, 494 accounts were engaged and we had 1250 interactions with the content we have posted in this period.

Followers

Follower growth and demographic distribution are key indicators of how digital engagement can expand the reach of injury prevention and health education initiatives, as a growing and actively connected audience increases the dissemination of evidence-based information.

In July 2024, @ligadotraumaunicamp had 2361 followers, and during the year we studied, we observed 385 new followers. The account showed a spike in followers after the Unicamp de Portas Abertas (UPA) in August 2023, an event in which the University of Campinas opens its doors to prospective students to learn about its facilities and courses. The Trauma League participated in this event, teaching lay people CPR, and made a QR code available at the station exit that directed to Instagram, so, after UPA, 66 new accounts began following the profile. In addition, during the Yellow Wave months, the profile received 119 new followers.

Among the 2361 followers, 43.8% were from Campinas (headquarters of the Trauma League and the University of Campinas), 9.1% from São Paulo, 2.2% from Paulínia, and 1.7% from Sumaré (a neighboring city of Campinas). Latin America dominates the account’s followers. Regarding the age group, the young population dominates: 23.8% from 18 years to 24 years old, 42.8% from 25 years to 34 years old, 20.2% from 35 years to 44 years old, and 7.8% from 45 years to 54 years old. Furthermore, 64.6% of the followers were female and 35.3% male (table 2).

Discussion

This study analyzed the dissemination of trauma-related educational content on Instagram. It demonstrated that posts featuring human faces were associated with higher engagement, that timely topics generated greater reach, that reels expanded visibility beyond followers, and that the audience was predominantly young, female, and regionally concentrated.

The present study revealed that the presence of faces in posts increases the likelihood of likes by 38% and comments by 32%, directly reflecting the engagement metrics observed in our results section and confirming the hypothesis that people’s faces are a major engagement tool on social media.18 In this way, our findings demonstrate that posts showing league members and professors achieved higher interaction compared with purely informational posts, indicating that human presence enhances visibility even in educational content related to injury prevention.

A possible explanation would be the presence of areas of the cerebral cortex specialized in facial recognition,19 capable of establishing emotional connections, awakening feelings through a photo, such as empathy, personality, interest, longing, and others. Additionally, faces are associated with stronger neural activation in regions that integrate visual recognition with emotional and social cognition, which may help explain why posts capturing faces capture attention and increase audience engagement on digital platforms.20 21 In the context of injury prevention education, this emotional connection may facilitate message retention and identification with preventive behaviors, potentially increasing receptiveness to safety recommendations.

Posts about “hot topics” at the moment have great repercussions on social media, as was observed with the posts involving the “Rolezinho Mata” campaign. The Campinas media widely publicized this topic during the period of publication. This was an opportunity to use social media to address traffic accident prevention and the safe use of motorcycles, as trauma is an important cause of death.5 6 Accordingly, social networks are a space for promoting health education and have a strong chance of greater reach when they address topics that have been discussed a lot in recent days, drawing users’ attention to prevention, clarification, and guidance on the situation in question.1

According to Social Insider’s 2024 statistics, reels are the publication model that generates the most engagement, whereas the reach of photos has declined.22 On the other hand, we had significant results in terms of reach with photos, so we think that is because they are small informative texts that do not take up much of users’ time. However, reels have taken up a lot of space on the social network. They are now very important for disseminating healthcare advertisements, as they reach a large number of people who sometimes do not follow the page. “Stories” did not prove to be relevant in this study, possibly due to their temporary characteristic, which limits their potential to be seen and shared by future followers. They also depend heavily on the timing of publication and on active engagement within a short period. Still, even then, they remain a way to show faces and build closer relationships with your followers.

Regarding the reels, the publications about the P.A.R.T.Y. program have been a huge success. This is a very well-established program in the world and also in the city of Campinas, and has been contributing to the reduction of fatal alcohol-related traumas in youth, which attracts the attention of followers, as it is a prevention campaign aimed at the young population.23 24 At this point, parents’ empathy, wanting their children to participate in programs like these, and young people’s identification with the situations addressed mean that posts and reels on this subject spread quickly. In addition, young people spend a large part of their time on social networks, making communication through these platforms easier.39,11 Besides, it is clear that large events, such as “UPA,” can bring great visibility to the organizers’ social media. Hence, one way to gain new followers and increase account engagement is to share the account name or QR code with event participants. Moreover, publications in conjunction with other “Instagram” accounts have been shown to generate great public interaction and engagement, as they reach a greater number of people, that is, the followers of all accounts linked to the same publication.

The UTL’s Instagram profile currently has a primarily regional and female audience, reinforcing the fact that women commonly exhibit stronger healthcare-seeking behavior.2 25 26 27 However, men still suffer a lot from trauma, thus there is a need for more effective ways to reach the male audience.28 Besides, its followers are predominantly young people, aligning perfectly with the demographic most vulnerable to external causes of death.5 6 29 30 To maximize its educational impact, reach high-risk populations, and boost engagement, the league should incorporate national or global topics and share humanized content, such as personal stories, trauma survivor experiences, and messages from specialists, which fosters empathy and enhances social connection.

This study has important limitations. First, Instagram Insights provides access to data from a limited time window, restricting longitudinal analysis. Second, engagement metrics such as likes, shares, and views do not necessarily translate into behavioral change or measurable reductions in trauma incidence. Third, the study evaluates a single academic profile, limiting generalizability to other institutions or platforms. Finally, algorithm dynamics and external events may have influenced reach independently of content characteristics.

Conclusion

Social networks are an excellent tool for disseminating knowledge and sharing educational content, especially to a predominantly young audience. Posts featuring human faces and content aligned with timely public issues were associated with higher engagement, and reels showed strong potential to expand reach beyond existing followers. Health professionals and students should use this information to more effectively run prevention campaigns on social media, such as Instagram.

These findings reinforce the importance of strategic content design in digital injury prevention initiatives. Further research should explore whether increased engagement translates into measurable preventive behavior and whether similar patterns are observed across other social media platforms.

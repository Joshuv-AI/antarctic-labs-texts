Background

Increased aging and dietary changes can lead to physiological changes in the prostate gland in men, including benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) and prostate cancer (PCa). Prevalent in middle-aged and elderly men, BPH is mainly caused by the mechanical bladder outlet obstruction owing to the enlargement of the gland surrounding the urethra, leading to the non-malignant proliferation of fibrous connective tissues, smooth muscle cells, and glandular epithelial cells within the transition zone of the prostate gland [1]. Early BPH patients exhibit atypical symptoms. The lesions of some patients will no longer develop when they reach certain degrees. Whereas, some patients will experience slowly deteriorated symptoms, and their urinary obstruction gradually worsens. These symptoms can lead to structural and functional changes in prostate tissues and induce secondary lower urinary tract symptoms such as frequent urination, urinary urgency, urinary incontinence, increased nocturia, dysuria, intermittent urination, and incomplete bladder emptying [2, 3]. In addition, it has been reported that in 2020, there were 1.41 million new cases of PCa and 380,000 deaths in men worldwide, making PCa the second leading cause of cancer-related deaths in men [4]. PCa patients usually have atypical symptoms in the early stage, but following tumor progression, PCa tumor tissues are compressed, and the pressure on the urethra of the patients is increased. Subsequently, the patients will show urethra-related clinical symptoms or even urinary retention in some severe cases [5]. The onset of PCa is insidious. By the time symptoms appear, the cancer cells have already spread to tissues or organs outside the prostate, and most patients have developed to the advanced stage, failing to undergo radical surgery [6]. In actual clinical practice, there are similar clinical characteristics between BPH and PCa, and it has become a clinical challenge to find ways to identify BPH and PCa at an early stage. Therefore, it is of instructive significance for the diagnosis and treatment of PCa to explore new marker molecules and therapeutic targets.

Nuclear enriched abundant transcript 1 (NEAT1) is a long-stranded non-coding RNA gene on human chromosome 11 at position 11q13.1. NEAT1 plays an important role in regulating biological processes such as cell cycle, differentiation, and stress. Dysregulation of NEAT1 expression is associated with cancer development, nervous system anomalies, and immune system disorders. Therefore, NEAT1 can be a potential biomarker for tumor diagnosis and prognostic assessment [7]. At present, the specific molecular mechanisms underlying the development of prostate diseases are not yet clear. Thus, this study adopted single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) to screen the identified biomarkers in PCa, and then conducted in vitro cell assays and in vivo nude mice assays to verify the potential efficacy of the biomarker in PCa, thus providing a new theoretical basis for the treatment of prostate diseases.

Methods

Data sources

scRNA-seq data were acquired from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo) and analyzed via the R package Seurat (v4.3.1). Data were pre-processed for 27 PCa (Tumor) samples from GSE176031, 13 BPH samples from GSE172357, and 14 prostate normal (Normal) samples from GSE176031 and GSE172357. Data available for the samples are shown in Table S1.

scRNA-seq data pre-processing

The scRNA-seq datasets were pre-processed with the R package Seurat. Low-quality cells and low-expressed genes were excluded with the thresholds of nCount_RNA between 200 and 6,000 and percent.mt less than 25% in each cell. The scRNA-seq datasets were standardized using the NormalizeData function in the R package Seurat, and 2,000 highly variable genes (HVGs) were identified using the vst method in the Find Variable Features function.

Principal component analysis (PCA)

Data were normalized using the ScaleData function and then subjected to PCA using the RunPCA function. Appropriate principal components (PCs) were selected by visualized plot via the ElbowPlot function. Subsequently, uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) plots were drawn using the RunUMAP function, where the Dims parameter was set to 1:30. Data were then subjected to batch correction through the R package Harmony. Based on the top 30 PCs, cells were clustered using the FindClusters function.

Cell annotation on scRNA-seq data

Feature genes of the cell clusters were identified using “FindAllMarker” and “FindMarker” functions in the R package Seurat. The identified feature genes were significantly reliable and statistically significant after adjustment with the Wilcoxon rank sum test and Bonferroni correction. Cell annotations were performed using the feature genes collected from the cellmarker2.0 database. The identified feature genes were visualized with VlnPlot and FeaturePlot functions.

Cell malignancy assessment and stemness analysis

The potentiality of malignant tumor cells was assessed by inferring population-scale copy number variation (CNV) scores from scRNA-seq data with the R package. The mean value of the squared final CNV scores on the chromosomes was defined as the malignancy score. A stemness signature gene set was trained using the one-class logistic regression (OCLR) model, and the Spearman correlation coefficient between the stemness signature gene set and the cell expression vector was defined as the stemness score.

Pseudotime analysis

Developmental trajectories of specific cells were inferred using the R package Monocle. Temporal gene expression was screened by PCA, selecting the most significant differentially expressed genes (DEGs) as well as genes associated with specific cellular functions for the prediction of the specialization trajectory.

Cell-cell communication analysis

Communication probabilities of all ligand-receptor pairs associated with each signaling pathway were summarized using the R package CellChat. Cellular interactions were inferred and analyzed by calculating communication probabilities at the signaling pathway level. Thus, new intercellular interactions were discovered and cell-cell communication profiles were constructed, which were presented using the R package igraph and circlize.

Cell culture and transfection

Human normal prostate stromal immortalized cells WPMY-1 and human prostate cancer cells PC3 were placed in the Dulbecco’s modified eagle medium (DMEM) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin, and cultured at 37℃ with 5% CO2. When the cell density reached 80–90%, enzymatic digestion was performed with 0.25% trypsin, selecting cells in the logarithmic phase for reserve. WPMY-1 and PC3 cells were purchased from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China). PC3 cells were inoculated in 12-well plates and transfected with shRNA NC (sh-NC group) and NEAT1 shRNA (sh-NEAT1 group) in accordance with the protocols of Lipofectamine 2000 transfection reagent.

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted by the Trizol method and reversely transcribed to cDNA according to the protocols of the reverse transcription kit, followed by qRT-PCR according to the protocols of the SYBR Premix Ex Taq kit. The relative mRNA expression of each target gene was calculated using the comparative CT method (2−ΔΔCt), setting Glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) expression as an internal reference. NEAT1 primer sequences were: Forward 5’-CTTCCTCCCTTTAACTTATCCATTCAC-3’, Reverse 5’-CTCTTCCTCCACCATTACCAACAATAC-3’; GAPDH primer sequences were: Forward 5’-GGTGGTCTCCTCTGACTTCAACA-3’, Reverse 5’-GTTGCTGTAGCCAAATTCGTTGT-3’.

5-ethynyl-2-deoxyuridine (EdU)

After PC3 cells were taken from each group, the original culture medium of the cell was discarded, and then each well was added with EdU reagent at a final concentration of 50 µmol/L and incubated at 37℃. Next, the cells of each group were fixed in a 4% paraformaldehyde solution for 20 min. After sufficient washing with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), the cells were permeabilized with 0.5% TritonX-100 for 10 min. Subsequently, the cells were again washed with PBS and added with 4’,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) reagent at a final concentration of 1 mg/mL. Following incubation for 30 min at room temperature, nuclei were stained and counted via the images photographed using a fluorescence microscope.

Flow cytometry

Individual cells were collected by digesting PC3 cells of each group with 0.25% trypsin. After washing with PBS, cells were added with 400 µL of binding buffer and 5 µL of Annexin V-fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC) solution and incubated in an environment protected from light. Subsequently, the cells were added with 5 µL of propidium iodide (PI) and incubated away from light for 20 min. Apoptotic changes were detected by flow cytometry.

Lentiviral transduction

PC3 cells in the logarithmic phase, whose cell concentration was adjusted to 5 × 107/L, were taken and inoculated into 6-well plates. When the cell coverage reached 50%, lentiviral transduction was carried out in accordance with the protocols of the reagent. The LV-NC group was added with 20 µL of shRNA-NC (titer 2 × 108 TU/mL), and the LV-shNEAT1 group was added with 6 µL of shNEAT1 (titer 9 × 108 TU/mL) and 2 µL of polybrene (5 µg/µL). The medium was replaced with fresh media every day after transduction overnight. After 5 d of culture, the cell lines stably infected with LV-NC or LV-shNEAT1 were established by screening drug-resistant cell lines with a final concentration of 2 µg/mL puromycin, and the successfully transducted cells were selected for subsequent assays.

Cell-derived xenograft (CDX) model construction

Twenty 4-week-old male BALB/c nude mice housed in a specific pathogen free (SPF)-grade environment were randomly divided into 2 groups: LV-NC and LV-shNEAT1, with 10 mice in each group. Mice in the LV-NC group were percutaneously injected in the axilla of the forelimb with 200µL of cell suspension containing approximately 1 × 105/mL PC3 cells infected with LV-shNC. Mice in the LV-shNEAT1 group were percutaneously injected in the axilla of the right forelimb with 200µL of cell suspension containing approximately 1 × 105/mL PC3 cells infected with LV-shNEAT1. Tumor diameter and length were measured weekly with vernier calipers. Six weeks later, the mice were euthanized, and the mice were placed in a sealable euthanasia device. According to the CO2 replacement rate of 30 − 70% container volume/minute, CO2 was filled into the container at a balanced speed until the mice experienced respiratory and cardiac arrest. The principle of CO2 euthanizing mice is to allow them to inhale high concentrations of CO2, causing hypoxia in their bodies and suffocating death. The tumor size and weight were measured. Subsequently, intact tumor tissues were surgically stripped. Tumor volume was calculated with the formula tumor volume (mm3) = 0.5236 × longest diameter × shortest diameter2. All animal experiments were performed using protocols approved by the Experimental Animal Ethics Committee of Ningbo University (Approval number: No. 13450). All animals were purchased uniformly by the school animal center platform.

Hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) staining

The collected tumor tissues from each group were fixed in a 4% paraformaldehyde solution, and dehydrated and permeabilized with gradient ethanol and xylene. Following paraffin embedding, the tumor tissues were sectioned into 2-µm-thick serial slices and then deparaffinized with xylene and hydrated with gradient ethanol. After stained with hematoxylin and eosin, the slices were dehydrated with gradient ethanol and xylene and sealed. Finally, the sealed sheets were photographed for observation under the microscope.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Tumor tissue slices from each group were deparaffinized with xylene and hydrated with gradient ethanol. Repaired with antigen, the slices were soaked using a 3% hydrogen peroxide solution to block endogenous peroxidase. After washing adequately with PBS, the solutions were added with rabbit anti-mouse Ki67 and incubated overnight at 4℃, followed by the addition of the secondary antibody. The slices were stained with 3,3’-diaminobenzidine tetrahydrochloride (DAB) kit and re-stained with hematoxylin. Comparative analyses were performed using the cumulative absorbance value as an index, where blue represented the nucleus and tan represented a positive expression.

Statistical analysis

All assays were repeated 3 times, and the data were statistically analyzed using GraphPad Prism software (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA). The measurement data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). The mean value of the two independent samples was compared using a t-test, and P < 0.05 was defined as statistically significant.

Results

Basal intermediate and Luminal differed significantly in the normal, BPH, and Tumor samples

After batch effect removal of 54 samples, a total of 187,063 cells and 32,623 genes were obtained. 176,434 cells and 32,623 genes were obtained by quality control. PCA showed that when PCs = 30, the effectively distinguishable dimensions between groups and hub genes can be obtained. There were 25 cell clusters by clustering with the top 30 PCs.

Ten cellular subsets were annotated, namely Basal intermediate, Endothelial cells, Fibroblast, T cell, Macrophage, Smooth muscle cells, Luminal, B cell, MastCell, and Neutrophil (Fig. 1A-C). The stacked plot displayed the cell percentage of all groups, with different cell percentages in the Normal, BPH, and Tumor groups (Fig. 1D). There were significant differential changes between Basal intermediate and Luminal during the course of BPH to PCa. Scatter plots displayed the co-chemotaxis/suppressor genes associated with Basal intermediate and Luminal in inflammation and cancer (Fig. 1E-F).

Distribution characteristics of luminal cells and basal intermediate cells

After data extraction, Luminal was annotated into 2 well-defined cellular subsets, namely, NEAT1+Luminal and NEAT1−Luminal (Fig. 2A-B). The stacked plots demonstrated the different subset percentages of Luminal in the Normal, BPH, and Tumor groups. NEAT1−Luminal was highly recruited, while NEAT1+ Luminal was low in the Tumor group (Fig. 2C). NEAT1−Luminal had a low stemness score and a high malignancy score, while NEAT1+Luminal had a high stemness score and a low malignancy score (Fig. 2D). In addition, Gene Ontology-biological process (GO-BP) analysis showed that NEAT1−Luminal was mainly enriched in prostate gland development, epithelial cell differentiation, and reproductive system development, and NEAT1+Luminal was mainly enriched in ion homeostasis regulation, stress response, and growth and development-related pathways (Fig. 2E).

After data extraction, Basal intermediate was annotated into 4 well-defined cellular subsets, namely matrix metallopeptidase 7 (MMP7)+ keratin 17 (KRT17)+Basal intermediate, MMP7+KRT17−Basal intermediate, MMP7−KRT17+Basal intermediate and MMP7−KRT17−Basal intermediate (Fig. 2F-G). The stacked plot demonstrated the different subset percentages of Basal intermediate in the Normal, BPH, and Tumor groups. MMP7−KRT17−Basal intermediate was highly recruited, while MMP7−KRT17+Basal intermediate was low in the Tumor group (Fig. 2H). MMP7−KRT17−Basal intermediate had low cell stemness and high malignancy scores, while other Basal intermediate subsets had high cell stemness and low malignancy scores, with the highest cell stemness scores in the MMP7+KRT17−Basal intermediate (Fig. 2I). In addition, GO-BP analysis showed that MMP7−KRT17−Basal intermediate was mainly enriched in the regulation of cell differentiation, epithelial cell proliferation, and MMP7−KRT17+Basal intermediate was mainly enriched in the regulation of epithelial cell proliferation, epithelial cell migration, and the positive regulation of kinase activity. MMP7+KRT17−Basal intermediate was mainly enriched in the regulation of cellular and acute inflammation, antigen presentation and regulation of multiple enzyme activities, and MMP7+KRT17+Basal intermediate was mainly enriched in the regulation of peptidase activities and prostaglandin metabolic processes (Fig. 2J).

Pseudotime analysis and cell-cell communication analysis

For Basal intermediate and Luminal, the MMP7+KRT17− subset had the highest cell stemness score and was therefore clearly defined as the root of the differentiation tree (Fig. 3A). Results of pseudotime analysis revealed that with the change of time, cells were transformed from darker blue to lighter blue, and three states of cell transformation differentiated the cells into two different epithelial cell types (Fig. 3B). The trajectories of State1 and Tumor sample grouping were highly overlapped, and NEAT1−Luminal cells localized in the Tumor group were consistently distributed with the tumor stage cells (Fig. 3C).

Cel-cell communication analysis showed that there were cellular interactions between Luminal subsets and multiple stromal cells (including endothelial, smooth muscle, and fibroblasts) in the Normal, BPH, and Tumor groups, and receptor-ligand pairs such as APP-CD74 and CDH1-CDH1 had stronger interactions (Fig. 3D).

Silencing NEAT1 inhibited PCa cell growth and tumor formation in nude mice

The qRT-PCR results showed that NEAT1 mRNA expression was elevated in the Tumor group compared with the Normal group (Fig. 4A). NEAT1 content in sh-NEAT1 group was significantly reduced compared to the sh-NC group, indicating that NEAT1 was successfully knocked down in the cells (Fig. 4B). EdU results presented that the proliferation level was significantly decreased in the sh-NEAT1 group compared to the sh-NC group (Fig. 4C). Flow cytometry results showed that the apoptosis rate was significantly increased in the sh-NEAT1 group compared with the sh-NC group (Fig. 4D).

In addition, CDX models were established, and the CDX results showed that the tumor growth rate in the LV-shNEAT1 group was significantly reduced compared with the LV-NC group (Fig. 4E, G). The tumor weights in the LV-NC and LV-shNEAT1 groups were compared and exhibited no statistically significant difference (Fig. 4F). H&E staining results showed that tumor tissues of the two groups were irregular, and the cancer cells were disordered, accompanied by larger cell size, obvious nucleoli, visible cytoplasmic abundance, and obvious heterogeneity (black arrows showed the cancer cells); Large areas with necrosis were seen in the tumor mesenchyme, where cancer cell structure disappeared and local tissue structure was loose, accompanied by a large number of inflammatory cell infiltration (red arrows); And large amounts of fibrinogen was filled in the necrotic areas (yellow arrows). Tumor tissues in the LV-NC group showed obvious envelopes with large and deeply stained nuclei, and the nuclei of tumor cells in the LV-shNEAT1 group were reduced and exhibited lighter color compared with those in the LV-NC group (Fig. 4H). IHC results showed that Ki67 positivity was significantly lower in the LV-shNEAT1 group compared with the LV-NC group (Fig. 4I).

Discussion

At present, the specific molecular mechanisms underlying the development of prostate disease remain unclear. When patients develop BPH, the nuclear matrix composition of the prostate will alter, and the function of the prostate tissue will also change [8]. PCa Patients share some of the same biological events and produce the same clinical symptoms as BPH patients, and the overgrowth of epithelial cells is present in both diseases [9]. However, epithelial cells of PCa can develop into malignant tumor cells and there are abnormal changes in gene expression given that tumor cells are prone to genetic instability [10]. Therefore, it is urgent to find new therapeutic target genes by studying the mechanisms of PCa development.

In this study, PCa, BPH, and Normal prostate samples were clustered through scRNA-seq, and specific cellular subsets associated with PCa tumor progression were explored. The results showed that 10 cellular subsets were annotated, with varying cell percentages in Normal, BPH, and Tumor groups. There were significant differential changes between Basal intermediate and Luminal during the course of prostate hyperplasia to cancer. After data extraction, Luminal was annotated into 2 well-defined cellular subsets. It was found that NEAT1−Luminal was highly recruited in the Tumor samples with low cell stemness and high malignancy scores. Meanwhile, MMP7−KRT17−Basal intermediate had high ratios in the Tumor group with low cell stemness and high malignancy scores. The results of pseudotime analysis revealed that as cancer progressed, the cells were differentiated into two distinct epithelial cell types, and the distribution of tumor-stage cells was mainly composed of NEAT1−Luminal. According to the interaction networks between Luminal subsets and multiple receptor-ligand pairs of other cells, there existed cellular interactions between Luminal subsets and a variety of stromal cells in the Normal, BPH, and Tumor groups, and stronger interactions between receptor-ligand pairs such as APP-CD74 and CDH1-CDH1. Chan et al. have reported that PCa resistance is associated with epithelial population-driven plasticity defined by a mixed luminal-basal phenotype [11]. Zhang et al. have revealed by scRNA-seq that the basal/intermediate epithelial cell subtype can highly express PCa progression-related genes and that luminal epithelial cells could be the initiating cells and transit to basal/intermediate cells [12]. Germanos et al. have found that expansion of depot-resistant intermediate lumen cell types was correlated with treatment resistance and poor prognosis in PCa patients [13]. Guo et al. have isolated 35,129 cells from the mouse prostate. Additionally, it has been found that Luminal-C cells could be one of the potential cellular sources of human PCa and that Dist-Luminal-C cells can rebuild distal prostate luminal lineages through self-renewal and differentiation [14]. Based on the results of this study, it was hypothesized that epithelial cells are the first affected cell type in the disease process of BPH and PCa, and that Luminal and Basal intermediate play a key role in the structure and function of prostate tissues.

NEAT1 is mainly used to maintain multiple physiological activities such as mRNA stability in the nucleus, gene expression regulation, and immune response regulation [15]. NEAT1 is associated with inflammatory responses such as chemokine regulation, cytokine production, and inflammatory vesicle activation, and can modulate epigenetic profiles of target genes by binding to multiple target genes [16]. NEAT1 has been reported to regulate biological behaviors such as growth, migration, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition in many malignant tumors [17, 18]. Chen et al. have found that NEAT1 is highly expressed in hepatocellular carcinoma cells, and NEAT1 over-expression can reduce cell senescence in hepatocellular carcinoma and promote tumor progression, which is associated with KIF11-dependent inhibition of CDKN2A [19]. Yao et al. have shown that NEAT1 is highly expressed in breast cancer tissues and cells, and interfering NEAT1 can inhibit tumor cell proliferation, migration, and invasion and accelerate apoptosis through up-regulation of miR-138-5p and down-regulation of ZFX [20]. Zhao et al. have reported that NEAT1 is over-expressed in PCa tissues and cell lines, and high expression is associated with PCa invasion and metastasis, resulting in a poor prognosis for patients, and that NEAT1 could promote PCa progression by targeting and regulating E2F3 through sponging miR-766-5P [21]. Wen et al. have identified 4 plausible m6A sites on NEAT1-1 by m6A high-throughput sequencing. m6A on NEAT1-1 can regulate Pol II Ser-2 phosphorylation, and over-expression of NEAT1-1 can induce metastasis of PCa cells to the lungs and bone [22]. Xia et al. have concluded that NEAT1 knockdown can reduce cell proliferation, inhibit aerobic glycolysis, and promote the secretion of CD8+ T-lymphocyte cytokines in PCa, thereby enhancing tumor immune surveillance [23]. Similar to the results of previous studies, the findings in this study indicated that NEAT1 is low-expressed in normal prostate cells, and NEAT1 expression is relatively elevated in PCa cells. It was also found that NEAT1 knockdown in PC3 cells could inhibit cell proliferation and induce apoptosis. In addition, CDX assays indicated that silencing NEAT1 could slow down the growth rate of PCa tumor volume and affect the tumorigenicity of PC3 cells in nude mice. H&E staining results showed that the nuclei of tumor cells were reduced and exhibited lighter color in the LV-shNEAT1 group compared with the LV-NC group. Ki67 is a proliferating cell-associated antigen closely related to chromatin synthesis and cell mitosis and can reflect the active degree of tumor cell division and proliferation [24]. The IHC results showed that NEAT1 knockdown did reduce Ki67 expression, suggesting that NEAT1 can inhibit the proliferation of PCa tumor cells in vivo. The assay results of this study demonstrated that NEAT1 can be a potential biomarker for PCa.

Overall, cell subsets associated with tumor progression in PCa were identified by scRNA-seq, and it was clarified that NEAT1 expression is elevated in PCa. NEAT1 knockdown in PC3 cells can inhibit cell proliferation, induce apoptosis, and decrease tumorigenicity in nude mice. The shortcoming of this study is that the specific molecular mechanism by which NEAT1 regulates PCa cells remains unclear in this study and needs to be further explored in depth in subsequent assays. In addition, this study only explored the effect of a single gene on PCa, but the PCa development and progression are not only affected by the action of a single gene but are caused by multiple factors. Therefore, multi-center and large-scale studies should be performed based on the integration of multiple factors in the future. Currently, NEAT1 has not been clinically used as a therapeutic tool to stop PCa progression. However, the present study revealed that high expression of NEAT1 was associated with the development of PCa, and NEAT1 can be a predictor of the clinicopathological features of PCa. This finding potentially opens up a new idea for the clinical treatment of PCa. After passing rigorous scientific validation and clinical trials, NEAT1 is expected to become a new therapeutic target for PCa.
